## Questions

**1.** Calculate the pH of 0.0250 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid. **[2]**

**2.** Calculate the pH of 0.0400 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide solution. **[3]**

**3.** Barium hydroxide, Ba(OH)₂, is a strong alkali. Calculate the pH of 0.0150 mol dm⁻³ Ba(OH)₂(aq), assuming that it is completely dissociated. **[3]**

**4.** A sample of rainwater has pH 3.45. Calculate the concentration of H⁺ ions in the sample. **[2]**

**5.** Ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH, is a weak acid. For ethanoic acid at 298 K, Ka = 1.74 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³.

**(a)** Write the expression for Ka of ethanoic acid. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the pH of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid. **[3]**

**(c)** State the two assumptions you made in (b). **[2]**

**6.** A 0.200 mol dm⁻³ solution of a weak monobasic acid, HX, has a pH of 2.85.

**(a)** Calculate Ka for HX. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate pKa for HX. **[1]**

**7.** A student uses a burette to titrate 25.0 cm³ portions of sodium hydroxide solution with 0.100 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid. The titres are 22.40 cm³ (rough), 21.60 cm³, 21.70 cm³ and 21.60 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the concentration of the sodium hydroxide solution. **[4]**

**(b)** Calculate the pH of the sodium hydroxide solution, using your answer to (a). **[3]**

**8.** Calculate the volume of 0.150 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide solution needed to neutralise 25.0 cm³ of 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ sulfuric acid, H₂SO₄. **[3]**

**9.** 25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid is mixed with 40.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide solution. Calculate the pH of the mixture. **[4]**

**10.** A buffer solution contains 0.200 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid and 0.150 mol dm⁻³ sodium ethanoate. Ka for ethanoic acid is 1.74 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³.

**(a)** Calculate the pH of the buffer solution. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain, using an equation, how the buffer solution keeps its pH almost constant when a small amount of hydrochloric acid is added. **[2]**

## Mark scheme and full working

Marking key: **M** = method mark, **A** = answer mark, **B** = independent mark; a bare **[1]** is one point. **ecf** = error carried forward: a later mark can still be awarded for correct working that uses an earlier wrong value. A correct final answer with no working scores full marks unless the question says "show". These conventions are to be confirmed by the teacher.

**1.** [H⁺] = 0.0250 mol dm⁻³ because HCl is a strong acid and fully dissociated [M1]; pH = −lg 0.0250 = **1.60** [A1].

**2.** [OH⁻] = 0.0400 mol dm⁻³ [M1]; [H⁺] = Kw ÷ [OH⁻] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 0.0400 = 2.5 × 10⁻¹³ mol dm⁻³ [M1]; pH = −lg(2.5 × 10⁻¹³) = **12.60** [A1]. (Accept 12.6. The route pOH = 1.40, pH = 14.00 − 1.40 is also accepted.)

**3.** Each mole of Ba(OH)₂ gives 2 mol of OH⁻, so [OH⁻] = 2 × 0.0150 = 0.0300 mol dm⁻³ [M1]; [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 0.0300 = 3.33 × 10⁻¹³ mol dm⁻³ [M1]; pH = **12.48** [A1].

**4.** [H⁺] = 10⁻ᵖᴴ = 10⁻³·⁴⁵ [M1]; = **3.55 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³** [A1].

**5.**

**(a)** Ka = [H⁺][CH₃COO⁻] ÷ [CH₃COOH] [1].

**(b)** [H⁺]² = Ka × [CH₃COOH] = 1.74 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.100 [M1]; [H⁺] = 1.32 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [A1]; pH = **2.88** [A1].

**(c)** [H⁺] = [CH₃COO⁻] (the acid is the only source of H⁺; the dissociation of water is negligible) [1]; the equilibrium concentration of CH₃COOH is equal to its starting concentration, 0.100 mol dm⁻³, because very little of it dissociates [1].

**6.**

**(a)** [H⁺] = 10⁻²·⁸⁵ = 1.41 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [M1]; Ka = [H⁺]² ÷ [HX] = (1.41 × 10⁻³)² ÷ 0.200 [M1]; = **9.98 × 10⁻⁶ mol dm⁻³** [A1]. (Calculate from the unrounded [H⁺]; 9.9 to 10.0 × 10⁻⁶ is accepted if [H⁺] has been rounded.)

**(b)** pKa = −lg Ka = **5.00** [1] (ecf from (a)).

**7.**

**(a)** The rough titre is not used; the three accurate titres 21.60, 21.70 and 21.60 cm³ are concordant (within 0.10 cm³), so the mean = (21.60 + 21.70 + 21.60) ÷ 3 = 21.63 cm³ [M1]; n(HCl) = 0.100 × 21.63 ÷ 1000 = 2.16 × 10⁻³ mol [M1]; NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O, a 1 : 1 ratio, so n(NaOH) = 2.16 × 10⁻³ mol in 25.0 cm³ [M1]; c(NaOH) = 2.16 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = **0.0865 mol dm⁻³** [A1].

**(b)** [OH⁻] = 0.0865 mol dm⁻³ [B1]; [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 0.0865 = 1.16 × 10⁻¹³ mol dm⁻³ [M1]; pH = **12.94** [A1] (ecf from (a)).

**8.** n(H₂SO₄) = 0.0500 × 0.0250 = 1.25 × 10⁻³ mol [M1]; H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O, so n(NaOH) = 2.50 × 10⁻³ mol [M1]; V = 2.50 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.150 = 0.0167 dm³ = **16.7 cm³** [A1].

**9.** n(HCl) = 0.200 × 0.0250 = 5.00 × 10⁻³ mol and n(NaOH) = 0.100 × 0.0400 = 4.00 × 10⁻³ mol [M1]; the acid is in excess by 5.00 × 10⁻³ − 4.00 × 10⁻³ = 1.00 × 10⁻³ mol of H⁺ [M1]; total volume = 65.0 cm³ = 0.0650 dm³, so [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0650 = 1.54 × 10⁻² mol dm⁻³ [M1]; pH = **1.81** [A1].

**10.**

**(a)** [H⁺] = Ka × [CH₃COOH] ÷ [CH₃COO⁻] [M1]; = 1.74 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.200 ÷ 0.150 = 2.32 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ [A1]; pH = **4.63** [A1].

**(b)** Added H⁺ reacts with the ethanoate ions: CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺ → CH₃COOH [1]; there is a large reservoir of CH₃COO⁻, so the ratio [CH₃COOH] ÷ [CH₃COO⁻] changes only slightly and the pH hardly changes [1].

## Where marks are usually lost (student-facing note on the page)

- Using [OH⁻] directly in pH = −lg and getting a pH below 7 for an alkali; convert with Kw first (questions 2, 3).
- Forgetting that Ba(OH)₂ gives two OH⁻ ions per formula unit, or that H₂SO₄ needs two moles of NaOH (questions 3, 8).
- Averaging the rough titre with the accurate ones instead of using only concordant results (question 7).
- Dividing by the volume of acid or alkali alone, rather than the total volume, after mixing (question 9).
- Using [H⁺] = Ka × c without the square root for a weak acid, or the square root of Ka alone (question 5).
- Rounding [H⁺] early and losing the third significant figure in Ka or pH.
- In a buffer explanation, saying the pH "does not change" instead of "changes very little", or giving no equation (question 10(b)).
