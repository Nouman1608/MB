Sociology rewards students who can use evidence and argue both sides. Many students know the
content but lose marks because their answers describe rather than evaluate, or because they
use studies without linking them to the question. A teacher who knows the exact specification
can fix both.

## How the papers are set out

Each course page lists every topic and the official paper structure. In outline:

- **AQA GCSE (8192):** two papers of 1 hour 45 minutes, each 50%: families and education, then
  crime and deviance and social stratification. Research methods and theory run through both.
- **AQA A-level (7192):** three papers of 2 hours, each a third of the grade, with Education
  and Crime and Deviance both examined alongside theory and methods.
- **Cambridge IGCSE (0495) and O Level (2251):** two papers, each 50%: research methods,
  identity and inequality, then family, education and crime.
- **Cambridge AS & A Level (9699):** four papers. Papers 1 and 2 (socialisation and research
  methods, and the family) make up the AS Level; Papers 3 and 4 complete the A Level.

## Where marks are usually lost

1. **Evaluation.** Higher-mark questions ask for a judgement. A paragraph that only explains a
   view, without weighing it against another, rarely reaches the top band.
2. **Using studies.** Naming a sociologist earns little on its own; the mark comes from showing
   how the study supports or challenges the point being made.
3. **Methods in context.** Questions that ask how a method would work for a particular group,
   such as pupils in a school, need answers about that group, not a general list of strengths
   and weaknesses.
4. **Command words.** "Identify", "explain" and "evaluate" need different lengths and kinds of
   answer. The [command words guide](/command-words/) sets out what each one asks for.
