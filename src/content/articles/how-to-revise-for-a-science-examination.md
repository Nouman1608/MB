Rereading notes feels like revision. It rarely is. When a student finishes a
chapter and thinks *I knew all of that*, they have measured familiarity, not
recall — and an examination asks for recall under time pressure. The fix is
unglamorous and well evidenced: close the notes and try to produce the content
from memory before checking it.

## Work from the specification, not the textbook

Every science specification is a list of statements beginning "candidates
should be able to…". That list, not the chapter order, is the revision plan.
Print it. Mark each statement with a symbol: confident, shaky, or not yet
learned. The marks will be uneven and that is the point — revision time should
be spent unevenly.

## Retrieve before you review

For each shaky statement, work in three passes.

1. **Blank page.** Write everything you can recall about the topic without
   looking. Definitions, equations, a labelled diagram, a worked example.
2. **Check and correct.** Open the notes and mark your page in a different
   colour. The corrections are your real weak points; nothing else on the page
   needs further work.
3. **Question.** Answer two or three examination questions on that statement
   only, timed roughly to the marks available — about a minute a mark.

The third pass matters most in science, because knowing a definition and
applying it to an unfamiliar context are different skills, and papers test the
second.

## Treat calculation as its own topic

Students often revise physics content and then lose marks on units, significant
figures and rearrangement. Keep a running list of every calculation error you
make across a fortnight. Patterns appear within days — usually two or three
recurring habits, not general weakness. Drill those specifically.

## Diagrams are answers, not decoration

Biology and chemistry both award marks for labelled diagrams, and both punish
vague ones. Practise drawing the six or seven diagrams a specification really
requires until they can be produced from memory in under two minutes each,
labels included.

## Use past papers late, and properly

A past paper attempted too early tells you little, because you cannot yet do
the content. Attempted at the right time, under timed conditions and marked
against the official mark scheme, it is the single most useful revision hour
available. Do not mark generously. Award the mark only if the answer says what
the scheme requires it to say.

## A realistic weekly shape

Three or four focused sessions of 40–50 minutes a day beats a nine-hour
Saturday. Rotate subjects rather than blocking one for a full day, and finish
each session by writing down the one thing you got wrong. The next session
starts there.
