**BC-only material.** Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/10-13-radius-interval-convergence-power-series-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/10-13-radius-interval-convergence-power-series-practice/). Prerequisites (Topics 10.2, 10.5, 10.7, 10.8 and 10.9) are on the [Calculus BC roadmap](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/#roadmap).

## Understanding

- I can write the general form of a power series and name its centre and coefficients. *(Guide: "What a power series is")*
- I can explain the three possible outcomes: convergence only at the centre, on an interval centred at c, or for every real x. *(Guide: "Only three things can happen"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why the ratio test cannot decide an endpoint. *(Guide: "Endpoint toolkit")*
- I can explain why a power series with positive radius is the Taylor series of its sum. *(Guide: "Power series and Taylor series")*

## Skills

- I can use the ratio test on the whole term to find a radius of convergence, including R = 0 and R = ∞. *(Guide: "Finding the radius with the ratio test"; Practice Q1, Q3)*
- I can rewrite a condition such as |2x − 5| < 1 in the form |x − c| < R before reading off R. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can handle series with only even powers, such as Σ x^(2n)/(4ⁿ(n + 1)). *(Practice Q5)*
- I can test each endpoint with a named test (p-series, alternating series, nth term, comparison) and write the interval with correct brackets. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q5, Q6(b))*
- I can find f⁽ⁿ⁾(c) from a coefficient using f⁽ⁿ⁾(c) = n! · aₙ. *(Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can state that term-by-term differentiation or integration keeps the radius, and retest the endpoints of the new series. *(Guide: "Differentiating and integrating term by term"; Practice Q6(c)–(d))*
- I can decide where a series must converge, must diverge, or might do either, from convergence at one point and divergence at another. *(Guide: "Only three things can happen"; Practice Q7)*
- I can justify each endpoint decision in a sentence that names the test and its conditions. *(Practice Q6, Q7(c))*
