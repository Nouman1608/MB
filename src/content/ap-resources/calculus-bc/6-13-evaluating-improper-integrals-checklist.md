**BC-only material.** Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/6-13-evaluating-improper-integrals-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/6-13-evaluating-improper-integrals-practice/). Prerequisites (limits, L'Hospital's rule, Topics 6.7 to 6.12) are on the [Calculus BC roadmap](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/#roadmap).

## Understanding

- I can say what makes an integral improper: an infinite limit, or an integrand that is unbounded on the interval. *(Guide: "The two kinds of improper integral")*
- I can explain what "converges" and "diverges" mean, including divergence by oscillation. *(Guide: "The two kinds of improper integral"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain how an infinitely long region can have a finite area. *(Guide: "The idea" and Figure 1)*

## Skills

- I can rewrite an improper integral as a limit of a definite integral, with correct notation. *(Guide: definitions table; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can evaluate an integral with an infinite limit, using L'Hospital's rule where needed. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q6(c))*
- I can evaluate an integral whose integrand is unbounded at an endpoint. *(Guide: "Benchmarks"; Practice Q3)*
- I can split an integral at an asymptote inside the interval and test both parts. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can split an integral from −∞ to ∞ into two halves and evaluate each. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can state which p-integrals converge on [1, ∞) and on (0, 1]. *(Guide: "Benchmarks"; Practice Q1)*

## Reasoning

- I can show that an integral diverges by finding the limit of the definite integral. *(Practice Q6(d), Q7(c))*
- I can explain why combining log terms is needed before taking a limit. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
- I can interpret a convergent or divergent improper integral as a total in context, with units. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7)*
