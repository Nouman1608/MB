**BC-only material.** Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/10-15-representing-functions-as-power-series-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/10-15-representing-functions-as-power-series-practice/). Prerequisites (Topics 10.2, 10.13 and 10.14) are on the [Calculus BC roadmap](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/#roadmap).

## Understanding

- I can explain why changing a known series gives the Maclaurin series of the new function, without computing its derivatives. *(Guide: "The idea: change a series you already know")*
- I can explain which operations keep the radius of convergence and why the endpoints must still be tested. *(Guide: "Term-by-term differentiation and integration"; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why a series may fail to represent a function at a point where the function itself exists. *(Guide: Common misconceptions)*

## Skills

- I can rewrite a rational function such as 1/(1 + 4x) or 5/(3 + x) in the form a/(1 − r) and write its series and interval. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5(a))*
- I can substitute an expression such as 2x, −x² or −t⁴ into a known series, including inside every power. *(Guide: "Substituting and multiplying by powers of x"; Practice Q2, Q6(a), Q7(a))*
- I can multiply or divide a series by a power of x and pick out a single coefficient. *(Guide: "Substituting and multiplying by powers of x"; Practice Q2)*
- I can differentiate a power series term by term and start the new sum at the right index. *(Guide: "Term-by-term differentiation and integration"; Practice Q3, Q5(b))*
- I can integrate a power series term by term and find the constant from the value at the centre. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(b), Q7(b))*
- I can find the interval of convergence of a new series, testing each endpoint with a named test. *(Guide: Worked example 2(b); Practice Q4, Q7(c))*

## Reasoning

- I can find f⁽ⁿ⁾(0) from a coefficient of a constructed series. *(Guide: "Reading a derivative from a built series"; Practice Q6(d), Q7(d))*
- I can find the exact sum of a numerical series by recognising it as a power series at a point inside its interval. *(Guide: "Example: differentiating to sum a series"; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can use a few terms of a constructed series to approximate a value and justify an error bound. *(Guide: Worked example 2(c); Practice Q6(c), Q7(e))*
