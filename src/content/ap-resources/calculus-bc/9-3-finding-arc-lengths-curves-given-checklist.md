**BC-only material.** Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/9-3-finding-arc-lengths-curves-given-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/9-3-finding-arc-lengths-curves-given-practice/). Prerequisites (Topics 9.1, 8.13, 6.9) are on the [Calculus BC roadmap](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/#roadmap).

## Understanding

- I can explain, with a sketch of a small right triangle, why the length of a short piece of curve is about √(x′(t)² + y′(t)²) Δt. *(Guide: "The idea: Pythagoras on tiny pieces")*
- I can state the two conditions for the integral to give a curve's length: smooth, and traced exactly once. *(Guide: "When does the integral give the length?"; Practice Q4)*
- I can show that the Topic 8.13 formula for y = f(x) is a special case of the parametric formula. *(Guide: "Link to Topic 8.13")*

## Skills

- I can write the arc length integral for a parametric curve, with the chain rule applied and the limits in t. *(Guide: "A strategy for any arc length question"; Practice Q1)*
- I can find an exact length when (dx/dt)² + (dy/dt)² is a perfect square. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5, Q6)*
- I can find the length of a circular arc from its parametric equations. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can evaluate an arc length integral with a calculator in radian mode, to 3 decimal places. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q7(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why an integral over a curve traced twice is not the curve's length. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can prove a curve is traced once by showing x(t) or y(t) is always increasing or always decreasing. *(Practice Q7(a))*
- I can check an arc length against the straight-line distance between the end points and explain why it must be longer. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q7(c))*
- I can find where a parametric curve crosses itself and the length of the loop it makes. *(Practice Q6)*
