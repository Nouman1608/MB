Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/9-2-second-derivatives-parametric-equations-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/9-2-second-derivatives-parametric-equations-practice/). You need [Topic 9.1](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/9-1-defining-differentiating-parametric-equations-checklist/) to be secure first; other prerequisites are on the [Calculus BC roadmap](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/#roadmap).

## Understanding

- I can explain, using the chain rule, why d²y/dx² = [d/dt (dy/dx)] ÷ (dx/dt). *(Guide: "Deriving the correct method")*
- I can show with an example why dividing d²y/dt² by d²x/dt² does not give d²y/dx². *(Guide: "The tempting shortcut that fails"; Practice Q1(C), Q2(C))*
- I can explain why the sign of d/dt (dy/dx) and the concavity are opposite when dx/dt < 0. *(Guide: "Watch the sign of dx/dt"; Practice Q7(c))*

## Skills

- I can simplify dy/dx as a function of t before differentiating it again. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(a))*
- I can find d²y/dx² in terms of t, using the quotient rule where needed. *(Practice Q2, Q5(b), Q7(b))*
- I can evaluate d²y/dx² at a given t, including when only dx/dt and dy/dx are given. *(Practice Q1, Q3)*
- I can find the values of t for which a parametric curve is concave up or concave down. *(Guide: Worked example 2(b); Practice Q5(c))*
- I can set up d²y/dx² and evaluate it with a calculator when one is allowed. *(Practice Q6(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can decide whether a tangent-line estimate is an overestimate or an underestimate, and justify it with the sign of d²y/dx². *(Guide: Worked example 2(c); Practice Q4, Q6(d))*
- I can find and correct an error in someone else's concavity argument. *(Practice Q7(c))*
- I can check a parametric second derivative by eliminating the parameter. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(d), Q7(d))*
- I can keep d²y/dx² (the shape of the path) separate from the second derivatives with respect to t used later for motion. *(Guide: "Where this leads")*
