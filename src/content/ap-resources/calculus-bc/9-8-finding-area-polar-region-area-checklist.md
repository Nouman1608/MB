**BC-only material.** Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/9-8-finding-area-polar-region-area-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/9-8-finding-area-polar-region-area-practice/). Prerequisites (Topic 9.7 and Riemann sums, Topics 6.2–6.4) are on the [Calculus BC roadmap](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/#roadmap).

## Understanding

- I can explain why polar regions are sliced into sectors rather than rectangles, and where ½ r² Δθ comes from. *(Guide: "Why rectangles do not fit" and "Building the formula from thin sectors"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain the polar area formula as the limit of a Riemann sum of sectors. *(Guide: "Building the formula from thin sectors", Figure 1)*
- I can explain why ½ r² is the rate at which area is swept out as θ increases. *(Guide: "Area as an accumulation"; Worked example 2(b))*

## Skills

- I can find the area between a polar curve and two rays, expanding r² and using the power-reducing identities. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can find the area of one petal of a rose by using consecutive zeros of r as limits. *(Guide: "Choosing the limits"; Practice Q3)*
- I can find the area enclosed by a circle or closed curve by choosing an interval that traces it exactly once. *(Practice Q2, Q4)*
- I can find the area of a loop of a curve given as r² = g(θ). *(Practice Q6(b)–(c))*
- I can use a calculator to find a polar area when the limits are not exact angles, writing the integral first. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why an integral over 0 to 2π may double-count a region or give a meaningless answer. *(Guide: "Choosing the limits"; Practice Q2(A), Q6(d), Q7(c))*
- I can handle an inner loop where r < 0, and separate the area of the inner loop from the area of the outer loop. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can check a polar area against simple bounds, such as discs or sectors with the smallest and largest r. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2)*
