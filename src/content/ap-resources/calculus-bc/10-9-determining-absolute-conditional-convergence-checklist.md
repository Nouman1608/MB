**BC-only material.** Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/10-9-determining-absolute-conditional-convergence-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/10-9-determining-absolute-conditional-convergence-practice/). Prerequisites (Topics 10.3 to 10.8) are on the [Calculus BC roadmap](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/#roadmap).

## Understanding

- I can define absolutely convergent, conditionally convergent and divergent using Σ aₙ and Σ |aₙ|. *(Guide: "Two series hiding inside one"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why a series that converges absolutely must converge, using 0 ≤ aₙ + |aₙ| ≤ 2|aₙ|. *(Guide: "Why absolute convergence implies convergence"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why the converse is false, with a counter-example. *(Practice Q1, Q7(d))*
- I can explain why only an absolutely convergent series can be regrouped or reordered without changing its sum. *(Guide: "Regrouping and reordering terms"; Practice Q4, Q6(c))*

## Skills

- I can classify Σ (−1)ⁿ⁺¹/nᵖ for any value of p. *(Guide: "A reference family"; Practice Q2)*
- I can show that Σ |aₙ| diverges using limit comparison or the integral test. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(a), Q7(b))*
- I can show that Σ |aₙ| converges using comparison or limit comparison with a p-series. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(b))*
- I can use the ratio test on |aₙ| and say what L < 1 and L > 1 mean for Σ aₙ. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3)*
- I can apply the alternating series test, showing that the terms decrease and approach 0. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(b), Q7(c))*

## Reasoning

- I can follow the decision path (nth term test, then Σ |aₙ|, then Σ aₙ) and give a full written classification. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q3, Q5, Q7)*
- I can handle a series whose signs do not alternate by testing its absolute values. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can recognise disguised alternating factors such as cos(πn) = (−1)ⁿ. *(Practice Q6(a))*
