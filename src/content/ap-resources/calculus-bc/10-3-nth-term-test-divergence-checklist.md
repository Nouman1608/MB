**BC-only material.** Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/10-3-nth-term-test-divergence-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/10-3-nth-term-test-divergence-practice/). Prerequisites (Topics 1.15, 4.7, 10.1 and 10.2) are on the [Calculus BC roadmap](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/#roadmap).

## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between the sequence of terms aₙ and the sequence of partial sums Sₙ, and say which one decides convergence. *(Guide: "Two sequences, not one"; Practice Q7(d))*
- I can show, using aₙ = Sₙ − Sₙ₋₁, why the terms of a convergent series must approach 0. *(Guide: "Why the terms of a convergent series must go to 0")*
- I can state the nth term test with both of its outcomes: "diverges" or "no conclusion". *(Practice Q2)*
- I can give an example of a series whose terms approach 0 but which still diverges. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q2)*

## Skills

- I can find the limit of a rational term by comparing the highest powers. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5(a), Q5(c))*
- I can recognise an oscillating term whose limit does not exist, and conclude divergence. *(Guide: Worked example 1(b); Practice Q5(b))*
- I can use logarithms or L'Hospital's Rule to find limits such as (1 + 2/n)ⁿ and n sin(3/n). *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can use the fact that a convergent series has terms approaching 0 to find a limit, such as lim (6aₙ + 1)/(aₙ + 2). *(Guide: "Using the fact forwards"; Practice Q4)*
- I can find the value of a constant that makes the limit of the terms equal to 0, and say what the test then shows. *(Practice Q6(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can write a complete divergence justification: the limit of the terms, a comparison with 0, the name of the test and the conclusion. *(Guide: "How to use the test, step by step"; Practice Q5, Q6(a), Q6(d))*
- I can explain why "the terms approach 0" never proves that a series converges. *(Practice Q2, Q6(c))*
- I can interpret the nth term test in a context, explaining why a total grows without bound when the amounts added level off at a non-zero value. *(Practice Q7(a), Q7(d))*
