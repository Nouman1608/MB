**BC-only material.** Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/10-1-defining-convergent-divergent-infinite-series-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/10-1-defining-convergent-divergent-infinite-series-practice/). Prerequisites (Topics 1.15 and 6.12, and the laws of logarithms) are on the [Calculus BC roadmap](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/#roadmap).

## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between the terms aₙ, the series Σ aₙ and the partial sums Sₙ. *(Guide: "Three objects you must keep apart"; Practice Q1)*
- I can state, in my own words, what it means for a series to converge to S, using the limit of the partial sums. *(Guide: "The definition: convergence is a limit"; Practice Q2)*
- I can describe the two ways a series can diverge: partial sums that grow without bound, and partial sums that never settle. *(Guide: "The definition: convergence is a limit"; Practice Q4)*

## Skills

- I can write out and evaluate a partial sum such as S₃, counting terms from the correct starting index. *(Guide: "Three objects you must keep apart"; Practice Q1, Q7(a))*
- I can find a₁ = S₁ and aₙ = Sₙ − Sₙ₋₁ from a formula for the partial sums. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can find the sum of a series from a formula for Sₙ by taking a limit. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q6(b))*
- I can split a term with partial fractions or log laws, find a telescoping Sₙ and take its limit. *(Guide: Worked examples 2 and 3; Practice Q3, Q5, Q7(b)–(c))*
- I can find the sum of a series that starts at a later index by subtracting the missing terms. *(Guide: "Facts that follow from the definition"; Practice Q6(c))*
- I can find how many terms are needed for Sₙ to be within a given distance of the sum. *(Practice Q7(d))*

## Reasoning

- I can justify a conclusion about convergence by referring to lim Sₙ, not to lim aₙ. *(Practice Q2, Q5(c), Q6(b))*
- I can give a counter-example to show that terms tending to 0 do not guarantee convergence. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6(d))*
- I can explain why a table of partial sums is evidence, not proof. *(Guide: "Seeing convergence on a graph"; Common misconceptions)*
