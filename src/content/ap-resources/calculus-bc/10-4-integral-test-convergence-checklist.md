**BC-only material.** Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/10-4-integral-test-convergence-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/10-4-integral-test-convergence-practice/). Prerequisites (Topics 5.3, 6.9, 6.11, 6.13 and 10.3) are on the [Calculus BC roadmap](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/#roadmap).

## Understanding

- I can state the integral test, with its three conditions and its "both converge or both diverge" conclusion. *(Guide: "The integral test"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain, using rectangles above and below the curve, why a finite integral means a convergent series and an infinite integral means a divergent one. *(Guide: Figure 1 and "Why the two pictures settle the question")*
- I can explain why the value of the integral is not the sum of the series. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q7(d))*
- I can recognise series where the test does not apply: terms that change sign or terms that are not decreasing. *(Guide: "When the integral test does not fit"; Practice Q3)*

## Skills

- I can show that f is decreasing using f′(x) < 0, and find the point N from which it decreases. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(b), Q7(a))*
- I can evaluate an improper integral by substitution and take the limit as b → ∞. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q6(c))*
- I can evaluate an improper integral by parts or with arctan, including limits such as b e^(−b) → 0. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q5, Q7(b))*
- I can find the values of a constant for which a series converges, by treating the cases of the antiderivative separately. *(Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can write a complete integral-test justification: the conditions with reasons, the improper integral as a limit, and the conclusion naming the test. *(Guide: Worked examples 1–3; Practice Q5, Q6)*
- I can use the nth term test first and explain why it is inconclusive before moving to the integral test. *(Practice Q6(a))*
- I can use partial sums to show that a claimed sum equal to the integral is wrong. *(Practice Q7(d))*
