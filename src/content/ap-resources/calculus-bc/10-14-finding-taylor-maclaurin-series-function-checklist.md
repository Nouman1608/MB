**BC-only material.** Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/10-14-finding-taylor-maclaurin-series-function-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/10-14-finding-taylor-maclaurin-series-function-practice/). Prerequisites (Topics 10.2, 10.11 and 10.13) are on the [Calculus BC roadmap](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/#roadmap).

## Understanding

- I can explain how a Taylor polynomial is a partial sum of the Taylor series, and why P₃ and P₄ for sin x are equal. *(Guide: "From Taylor polynomials to Taylor series"; Practice Q5(b))*
- I can state the difference between a Taylor series about x = c and a Maclaurin series. *(Guide: "From Taylor polynomials to Taylor series"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why the Maclaurin series for 1/(1 − x) is a geometric series and why it only works for −1 < x < 1. *(Guide: "The four foundation series")*

## Skills

- I can write the Maclaurin series for eˣ, sin x, cos x and 1/(1 − x), with general terms and intervals of convergence, from memory. *(Guide: "The four foundation series"; Practice Q1)*
- I can derive the eˣ, sin x and cos x series from their derivatives at 0. *(Guide: "The four foundation series")*
- I can build a Taylor series about x = c from a pattern in the derivatives, including the general term. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5, Q6(a), Q7(a))*
- I can write a series for a substituted function such as e^(−2x) from a known series. *(Guide: "Building on the foundation series")*
- I can find the interval of convergence of a Taylor series using the ratio test and endpoint tests. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can find f⁽ⁿ⁾(c) from a coefficient and use it to decide whether f is increasing, decreasing, concave up or down, or has a local extremum at c. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4, Q6(d))*
- I can recognise a numerical series as a known Maclaurin series at a particular x and give its exact sum. *(Guide: "Recognising the sum of a series"; Practice Q2)*
- I can justify that a series converges for all real x using the ratio test. *(Practice Q7(c))*
