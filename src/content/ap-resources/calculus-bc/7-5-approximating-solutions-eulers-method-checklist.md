**BC-only material.** Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/7-5-approximating-solutions-eulers-method-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/7-5-approximating-solutions-eulers-method-practice/). Prerequisites (Topics 4.6 and 7.1–7.4) are on the [Calculus BC roadmap](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/#roadmap).

## Understanding

- I can explain why each Euler step is a tangent-line approximation, and why several short steps usually beat one long one. *(Guide: "The idea: follow tangent lines in short steps"; Practice Q5(a))*
- I can explain how Euler’s method follows a slope field from a starting point. *(Guide: "The idea"; Figure 1)*
- I can say what happens to the error when the step size is made smaller, and why it does not become zero. *(Guide: "How step size affects accuracy"; Practice Q7(d))*

## Skills

- I can carry out one Euler step from a given point, using the slope at that point. *(Guide: "The procedure"; Practice Q1)*
- I can carry out several steps in a table, updating both x and y each time. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q7(b)–(c))*
- I can work out the number of steps from the interval and the step size. *(Guide: "The procedure"; Practice Q2)*
- I can use a negative step to approximate a value to the left of the starting point. *(Guide: "Stepping backwards"; Practice Q4)*
- I can find d²y/dx² from a differential equation by implicit differentiation. *(Guide: "Overestimate or underestimate?"; Practice Q5(c), Q6(c))*
- I can keep full values between steps and round only the final answer. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can decide whether an Euler estimate is an overestimate or an underestimate, using the sign of d²y/dx² and not whether y is increasing. *(Guide: "Overestimate or underestimate?"; Practice Q3, Q5(d))*
- I can predict whether a smaller step will raise or lower an estimate, given its concavity. *(Practice Q6(d))*
- I can state an Euler estimate in context, with units, and say what it means. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
