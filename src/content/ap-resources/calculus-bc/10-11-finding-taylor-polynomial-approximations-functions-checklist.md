**BC-only material.** Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/10-11-finding-taylor-polynomial-approximations-functions-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/10-11-finding-taylor-polynomial-approximations-functions-practice/). Prerequisites (Topics 3.6 and 4.6, and factorials) are on the [Calculus BC roadmap](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/#roadmap).

## Understanding

- I can explain why the coefficient of (x − a)ⁿ is f⁽ⁿ⁾(a)/n!, by differentiating the polynomial n times. *(Guide: "The coefficient formula, and where n! comes from")*
- I can explain that the tangent line is the first-degree Taylor polynomial. *(Guide: "From a tangent line to a better polynomial")*
- I can describe how the approximation usually improves as the degree rises, and why it is best near the centre. *(Guide: "Seeing the approximation improve"; Practice Q6(c))*
- I can tell the difference between "nth-degree" and "first n nonzero terms". *(Guide: "Degree versus number of terms")*

## Skills

- I can find a single coefficient from a derivative value, dividing by the right factorial. *(Practice Q1)*
- I can build a Taylor polynomial for a given function by finding and evaluating its derivatives at the centre. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q5(a))*
- I can build a Taylor polynomial from a table of derivative values. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can use a Taylor polynomial to estimate a function value, taking care with the sign of (x − a). *(Practice Q4, Q5(b), Q6(b), Q7(d))*
- I can read derivative values back from a given Taylor polynomial using f⁽ᵏ⁾(a) = k! × coefficient. *(Guide: "Reading backwards"; Practice Q3, Q6(d), Q7(a))*

## Reasoning

- I can use derivative values read from a Taylor polynomial to justify a relative maximum or minimum. *(Practice Q7(b))*
- I can find the Taylor polynomial for f′ from the one for f. *(Practice Q7(c))*
- I can interpret a coefficient in context, with units. *(Guide: Worked example 2(c))*
