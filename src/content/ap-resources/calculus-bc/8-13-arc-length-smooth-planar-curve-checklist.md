**BC-only material.** Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/8-13-arc-length-smooth-planar-curve-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/8-13-arc-length-smooth-planar-curve-practice/). Prerequisites (chain rule, Mean Value Theorem 5.1, Riemann sums 6.2 to 6.6, FTC 6.7) are on the [Calculus BC roadmap](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/#roadmap).

## Understanding

- I can explain how adding the lengths of many short chords, using Pythagoras and the Mean Value Theorem, leads to the arc length integral. *(Guide: "Building the formula from straight pieces")*
- I can state what "smooth" means for this formula and what to do at a corner. *(Guide: "The arc length formula")*
- I can explain why the distance traveled along a path equals the arc length, and how it differs from the chord, the vertical change and the total distance of Topic 8.2. *(Guide: "Arc length as distance traveled"; Practice Q2(D), Q6(c))*

## Skills

- I can write the arc length integral for a curve y = f(x), squaring the derivative and keeping the 1 and the square root. *(Guide: "The arc length formula"; Practice Q1)*
- I can find an exact length by hand when 1 + (f′)² simplifies, including a chain-rule factor. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can show that 1 + (f′)² is a perfect square and use it to find an exact length. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can find the length of a curve x = g(y), using y-limits and dx/dy. *(Guide: "Curves written as x = g(y)"; Practice Q3)*
- I can set up an arc length integral by hand and evaluate it with a calculator to 3 decimal places. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(b), Q7(c))*

## Reasoning

- I can check an arc length against b − a and the straight-line distance between the end points. *(Guide: "Three quick facts"; Practice Q4)*
- I can justify upper and lower bounds on a length from bounds on the integrand. *(Guide: Worked example 2 checks; Practice Q6(d))*
- I can use arc length to answer a distance-traveled question in context, with units, including time at a given speed. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(c))*
- I can write a distance-along-the-path function s(x) as an integral, find s′(x), and solve s(x) = k with a calculator. *(Practice Q7)*
