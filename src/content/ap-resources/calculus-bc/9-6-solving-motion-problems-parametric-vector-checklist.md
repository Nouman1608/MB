**BC-only material.** Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/9-6-solving-motion-problems-parametric-vector-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/9-6-solving-motion-problems-parametric-vector-practice/). Prerequisites (Topics 4.2, 8.2, 9.3, 9.4 and 9.5) are on the [Calculus BC roadmap](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-bc/#roadmap).

## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between velocity (a vector) and speed (a number), and between displacement (a vector) and distance (a number). *(Guide: "The motion toolkit")*
- I can explain why total distance is never less than the length of the displacement. *(Guide: "Integrals: displacement, position and distance"; Practice Q6(d))*
- I can explain why a particle is at rest only when both velocity components are zero at the same time. *(Guide: "Derivatives: velocity, speed and direction"; Practice Q5(d))*

## Skills

- I can find the velocity vector, acceleration vector and speed from a position function. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q2, Q5(a)–(b))*
- I can use a sign table for x′ and y′ to describe the direction of motion on each interval. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3, Q5(c))*
- I can find a position at a later time by adding the integral of velocity to the starting position. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can find the displacement vector and its length over a time interval. *(Practice Q4, Q6(d), Q7(d))*
- I can find total distance travelled as the integral of speed, exactly when possible and with a calculator otherwise. *(Guide: "An exact example", Worked example 2; Practice Q4, Q6(c), Q7(c))*
- I can find the greatest value of a coordinate using where its rate changes sign and the endpoint values. *(Practice Q7(a))*

## Reasoning

- I can decide whether the speed is increasing or decreasing at a given time and justify it with the sign of x′x″ + y′y″ or the derivative of speed. *(Guide: "Is the speed increasing or decreasing?"; Practice Q5(b), Q7(b))*
- I can choose between a derivative and an integral for each part of a motion problem. *(Guide: "Choosing the right tool"; Practice Q7)*
- I can give answers with correct units and avoid the common traps (adding components, forgetting the starting position, degree mode). *(Guide: Common misconceptions)*
