Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/1-2-displacement-velocity-acceleration-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/1-2-displacement-velocity-acceleration-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can state a coordinate system in words before using any sign, and explain what the point-object model ignores. *(Guide: "Object, axis and displacement")*
- I can explain how an instantaneous velocity is the limit of average velocities as Δt → 0, using a table of shrinking intervals. *(Guide: "From averages to derivatives")*
- I can explain why the constant-acceleration equations fail when a_x depends on time. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5(d))*

## Calculation

- I can differentiate x(t) to find v_x(t) and a_x(t), and give each with the correct unit. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can integrate a_x(t) to find v_x(t), including the initial velocity with its sign. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2)*
- I can find displacement and distance travelled from v_x(t) by splitting the integral where v_x = 0. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can find the greatest velocity of an object by setting a_x = 0. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can derive all three constant-acceleration equations by integration, including the chain-rule step. *(Guide: "Deriving the constant-acceleration equations"; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can decide whether an object is speeding up or slowing down from the signs of v_x and a_x, and build a sign chart over an interval. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can choose what to plot to turn sensor data for a time-dependent acceleration into a straight line, and extract a constant from the slope. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can show when the average velocity equals the mean of the end velocities, and when it does not. *(Practice Q7)*
