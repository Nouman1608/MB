## Understanding

- I can describe the gravitational force between two masses: attractive, along the line of centers, acting at each center of mass, and equal in size on both. *(Guide: "Newton's law of universal gravitation"; Practice Q4(b))*
- I can explain what a gravitational field is and why, with gravity as the only force, the acceleration equals g. *(Guide: "The gravitational field"; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can explain apparent weight as the normal force, and say when a system appears weightless. *(Guide: "Apparent weight and weightlessness"; Practice Q2, Q7(b))*
- I can state the equivalence principle and the difference between inertial and gravitational mass. *(Guide: "Inertial mass and gravitational mass"; Practice Q7(c))*

## Calculation

- I can predict the factor by which F_g or g changes when masses, distance or a planet's radius change. *(Guide: "The gravitational field"; Practice Q1)*
- I can find g at a height above a planet and decide whether g can be treated as constant. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7(a))*
- I can find a scale reading from N = m(g + a_y), including when a_y comes from differentiating v_y(t). *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can find where the fields of two masses cancel. *(Practice Q4(c))*
- I can use partial mass to find the field inside a uniform sphere, and the shell theorem for a thin shell. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3)*

## Reasoning

- I can derive F = (GMm/R³)r inside a uniform sphere and sketch g against r inside and outside a sphere and a shell. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can find a planet's field and mass from free-fall data by choosing a straight-line graph. *(Practice Q6)*
