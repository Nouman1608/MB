Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/3-3-potential-energy-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/3-3-potential-energy-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why potential energy belongs to a system of two or more objects, not to one object. *(Guide: "Potential energy belongs to a system")*
- I can say what makes a force conservative and show with numbers that friction has no potential energy. *(Guide: "Potential energy belongs to a system"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why the choice of zero potential energy changes values of U but never changes ΔU. *(Guide: "Defining U with an integral")*
- I can explain why mgΔy is an approximation and when it fails. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*

## Calculation

- I can derive U(x) from a conservative force with ΔU = −∫F dx, including for a nonideal spring. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4)*
- I can derive U_s = ½k(Δx)² and U_g = −Gm₁m₂/r by integration, stating the zero I used. *(Guide: "Elastic potential energy" and "Gravitational potential energy")*
- I can find F_x = −dU/dx at a point and give its direction. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can add pair energies to find the potential energy of three or more objects. *(Guide: "Systems with three or more objects"; Practice Q3)*

## Reasoning and representations

- I can locate equilibrium positions from dU/dx = 0 and classify them with the second derivative or by nudging. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can sketch U(r) and F(r) on aligned axes and mark the minimum, zero crossings and long-range behaviour. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can estimate a force and an equilibrium position from a table of U(x) values. *(Practice Q6)*
