## Understanding

- I can explain that momentum is conserved in elastic and inelastic collisions alike, and that kinetic energy decides the type. *(Guide: "Two checks for every collision"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why each object's kinetic energy may change in an elastic collision while the total stays the same. *(Guide: "Elastic collisions"; Practice Q1)*
- I can describe what happens to kinetic energy during the contact of an elastic collision. *(Guide: "Elastic collisions"; Practice Q3)*
- I can name the nonconservative processes that transform kinetic energy in an inelastic collision, and where the energy goes. *(Guide: "Inelastic and perfectly inelastic collisions"; Practice Q5(d))*

## Calculation

- I can find the common velocity and the kinetic energy transformed in a perfectly inelastic collision. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can do the same for a perfectly inelastic collision in two dimensions, using components. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can solve a one-dimensional elastic collision using momentum and the reversal of relative velocity, and check with kinetic energy. *(Guide: "Solving a one-dimensional elastic collision"; Practice Q4)*
- I can derive the final velocities for an elastic collision with a target at rest, and predict how they change with the mass ratio. *(Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning and skills

- I can classify collisions from measured velocities, using the kinetic energy fraction and the measurement uncertainty as evidence. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can draw and read a chart comparing kinetic energy before and after different outcomes of the same collision. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
- I can explain why sticking together loses the most kinetic energy and why it does not always lose all of it. *(Guide: "Why sticking loses the most"; Practice Q5(e), Q7)*
