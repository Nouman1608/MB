## Understanding

- I can explain, using Newton's third law, why internal forces give equal and opposite impulses and so cannot change a system's total momentum. *(Guide: "Why internal forces cancel")*
- I can explain that momentum is conserved in every interaction, while a chosen system's momentum changes if a net external force acts on it. *(Guide: "Choosing the system"; Practice Q2)*
- I can choose a system so that unknown forces are internal, and say which components of its momentum are conserved. *(Guide: sandbag example; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why we compare states just before and just after a collision or explosion. *(Guide: "Collisions and explosions"; Practice Q5(e))*

## Calculation

- I can find the total momentum and the center-of-mass velocity of a system, using signed velocities. *(Guide: "Total momentum and the center of mass"; Practice Q1)*
- I can use conservation of momentum in one dimension to find an unknown velocity after an explosion or recoil. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can conserve the x- and y-components separately to find a velocity in two dimensions, with its size and direction. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can find the impulse on each part of a system and the external impulse on the whole system from changes in momentum. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(e))*

## Reasoning and skills

- I can plot total momentum against time from sensor data and find a net external force from the slope. *(Guide: "Testing conservation in the lab"; Practice Q5)*
- I can describe a lab procedure to test whether momentum is conserved in an interaction. *(Guide: "Testing conservation in the lab")*
- I can draw a before-and-after sketch and a momentum vector diagram for a collision. *(Guide: Figure 2)*
- I can argue qualitatively about three-dimensional cases, such as why an object at rest that splits in two sends the pieces in opposite directions. *(Practice Q7)*
