Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/5-2-connecting-linear-rotational-motion-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/5-2-connecting-linear-rotational-motion-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why all points of a rigid system share ω and α, but not v, a_T or a_c. *(Guide: "Comparing points on one rigid system"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why s = rθ, v = rω and a_T = rα need angles in radians. *(Guide: "Arc length and the radian"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why a point rotating at constant ω still accelerates. *(Guide: "The other component: centripetal acceleration"; Practice Q7)*
- I can state the conditions for a string or belt to share the rim's speed and tangential acceleration. *(Guide: "Strings, hoses and belts that do not slip")*

## Calculation

- I can derive v = rω and a_T = rα by differentiating s = rθ with r constant. *(Guide: "Deriving v = rω and a_T = rα")*
- I can find the speed, tangential, centripetal and total acceleration of a point from θ(t) or ω(t). *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7(b))*
- I can find a cord's speed, acceleration and distance moved from a drum's ω(t), and the number of turns. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can derive the total acceleration of a rim point in terms of α, r and θ for a spin-up from rest. *(Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can compare linear quantities for points at different distances from the axis using proportional reasoning. *(Guide: table and Figure 1; Practice Q1, Q7(a))*
- I can predict how a_T and a_c change when ω or α changes. *(Practice Q3)*
- I can sketch v–t, a_T–t and a_c–t graphs for points on a rotating system from its ω–t description. *(Guide: "Graphs against time"; Practice Q5)*
