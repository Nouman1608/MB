Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/7-2-frequency-period-shm-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/7-2-frequency-period-shm-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define period, frequency and angular frequency, give their units, and convert between them. *(Guide: "Period, frequency and angular frequency")*
- I can explain why the period of a spring oscillator depends on m and k but not on g or the amplitude. *(Guide: "The object–spring oscillator"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why the mass cancels for a simple pendulum, and why its formula needs small angles in radians. *(Guide: "The simple pendulum"; Practice Q5(c) and (d))*

## Calculation

- I can read ω from an equation of motion of the form d²x/dt² = −ω²x and find T and f. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can use effective spring constants for springs in series and in parallel to find a period. *(Practice Q2 and Q4)*
- I can derive T = 2π√(ℓ/g) for a simple pendulum from Newton's second law, by torque or along the arc. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can use T = 2π√(d/g) to link a vertical spring's static stretch to its period. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7)*
- I can find g from a pendulum's length and period. *(Practice Q7(a))*

## Reasoning

- I can predict factors of change in T and f when m, k, ℓ or g change, including square roots. *(Guide: "Factors of change"; Practice Q4(c))*
- I can choose what to plot to turn period data into a straight line, and find k or g from the slope. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain what a non-zero intercept on a T²–m graph means, and why the slope is still reliable. *(Guide: Figure 2)*
- I can plan a period measurement that reduces timing error. *(Guide: "Measuring a period well")*
