Short on time? This page is the recap for the **calculus-based** course (separate from Physics 1). For explanations, diagrams and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/2-2-forces-free-body-diagrams-study-guide/).

## Recap

- A **force** is a vector describing an interaction between two objects or systems. Name both: "F_T, cable on crate".
- An object or system **cannot exert a net force on itself**. Internal forces cancel in pairs.
- **Contact forces** (normal, friction, tension, spring, drag) are the large-scale effect of **electric forces between atoms**. **Long-range** forces, such as gravity, need no contact.
- A **free-body diagram** shows only the forces exerted **on** the chosen system **by** its environment.
- If a force changes with time, each diagram is a **snapshot** at one instant.

## Key relationships

| Rule or result | Notes |
|---|---|
| Dot = center of mass | The system is treated as if all its mass were there |
| One straight arrow per force, starting on the dot | Label "by what, on what" |
| Same-direction forces side by side | Never merged, never overlapping |
| No components on the diagram | Split forces in your working |
| ΣF = F₁ + F₂ + … (add by components) | Unit-vector notation: ΣF = (ΣF_x) i + (ΣF_y) j |
| Gravity on a slope of angle θ | mg sin θ down the slope; mg cos θ into the slope |
| F_g = mg | g = 9.8 m/s² near Earth's surface |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- g = 9.8 m/s² near Earth's surface. Strings and cables are light, so they pull along their length.
- "Smooth" means friction is negligible. "Steady velocity" or "at rest" means the forces balance (Topic 2.4).
- Axes are stated each time, with one axis along the motion.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Drawing components** as extra arrows on the diagram.
2. **Merging two same-direction forces** into one arrow.
3. **"Force of motion", "inertia" or "ma" arrows.** None of these has a "by what".
4. **Forces the system exerts** on other objects. They belong on those objects' diagrams.
5. **Internal forces** on a system diagram. They cancel; leave them off.
6. **N = mg every time.** Find N from the diagram; ropes, pushes and slopes change it.
7. **Horizontal axes on a slope**, which makes extra components.

## Quick self-check

1. A hand presses down with 5.0 N on a 2.0 kg book resting on a table. What is the normal force from the table? *(19.6 + 5.0 = 24.6 N, about 25 N)*
2. A 10 kg box sits on a 40° slope. Find the components of gravity along and into the slope. *(63 N down the slope; 75 N into it)*
3. What exerts the forward force on a sprinter leaving the blocks? *(The blocks and ground, pushing on her feet. Her muscles are internal to the system "sprinter".)*
