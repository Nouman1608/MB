Short on time? This page is the recap for the **calculus-based** course (separate from Physics 1). For explanations, the field graph and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/2-6-gravitational-force-study-guide/).

## Recap

- Gravity is **attractive**, acts **along the line of centers**, and can be taken to act at each system's **center of mass**.
- The two forces in a pair are equal in size; the accelerations are not.
- The **field** is force per unit mass. With gravity as the only force, a = g.
- **Weight** = mg. Treat g as constant when r hardly changes (near Earth's surface, g ≈ 9.8 N/kg).
- **Apparent weight** is the normal force. **Weightless** means N = 0: no forces at all, or gravity alone.
- **Equivalence principle:** inside a closed cabin you cannot tell a gravitational field from an acceleration of the cabin.
- **Inertial mass** (resists change in motion) and **gravitational mass** (takes part in attraction) are found by experiment to be equal.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Units | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| F_g = G m₁m₂ / r² | N | G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²; r between centers |
| g = F_g/m = GM/r² | N/kg = m/s² | points toward M |
| W = mg | N | near a planet's surface |
| N = m(g + a_y) | N | +y upward; apparent weight |
| g_surface = (4/3)πGρR | N/kg | uniform planet; same ρ, double R → double g |
| Thin shell: F = 0 inside; GMm/r² outside | N | shell theorem, used without proof |
| Uniform sphere inside: M_partial = Mr³/R³, F = (GMm/R³)r = kr | N | toward center: F = −kr |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Spheres are uniform (or made of uniform shells); other bodies' pulls are ignored unless stated.
- Constant g only over heights that are small compared with the planet's radius (g falls 1% by about 32 km above Earth).
- Air resistance ignored for "free fall".

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Using the altitude as r.** Add the planet's radius.
2. **Forgetting to square r**, or squaring the mass instead.
3. **Saying apparent weight depends on velocity.** It depends on acceleration.
4. **Using the whole mass inside a sphere.** Only the partial mass within r counts.
5. **Thinking the field inside a shell is zero only at the center.** It is zero everywhere inside.
6. **"Weightless means no gravity."** It means nothing pushes back against gravity.

## Quick self-check

1. Two masses move twice as far apart. What happens to F_g? *(It becomes ¼ as large.)*
2. g at a planet's surface is 9.8 N/kg. What is it at r = 2R? *(2.45 N/kg)*
3. A 50 kg person in a lift accelerating upward at 2.0 m/s². What does the scale read? *(50 × 11.8 = 590 N)*
