Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/5-3-torque-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/5-3-torque-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why only the component of a force perpendicular to r exerts a torque, and why a force aimed at the axis exerts none. *(Guide: "Only the perpendicular component turns"; Practice Q3)*
- I can define the lever arm as the perpendicular distance from the axis to the line of action, and show it on a diagram. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q4(c))*
- I can explain why moving a force along its own line of action does not change its torque. *(Practice Q5(e))*
- I can explain why torques must always be stated about a named axis. *(Guide: Worked example 3)*

## Calculation

- I can find the size of a torque three ways, rF sin θ, rF⊥ and r⊥F, and get the same answer. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can work out r × F in unit-vector form and give its direction. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can find the angle between r and F from their components and use it to check a torque. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4(b))*
- I can add the torques of several forces about one axis with a stated sign convention. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q5)*

## Representation and reasoning

- I can draw a force diagram with each force starting where it acts, including weight at the centre of mass. *(Guide: "Force diagrams for rigid systems"; Practice Q5(a))*
- I can use the right-hand rule to find the direction of r × F and link it to clockwise or counterclockwise turning. *(Guide: "Torque as a vector"; Practice Q2)*
- I can linearise data on force and angle to test whether a threshold torque is constant, and justify my conclusion with evidence. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can show that the net torque is the same about every axis only when the net force is zero. *(Practice Q7)*
