## Understanding

- I can define a system and its environment, and say whether mass or energy crosses the boundary in a given situation. *(Guide: "Choosing a system")*
- I can explain how the interactions between the parts of a system give it its properties, with an example. *(Guide: "Choosing a system")*
- I can decide when a system can be modelled as a single object and when its internal structure matters. *(Guide: "When is a system one object?"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain how parts of a system can move differently from the system as a whole, and how outside changes can rearrange a system's inside. *(Guide: "The system as one object at its center of mass"; Practice Q7)*

## Calculation

- I can find the center of mass of particles in one or two dimensions, keeping signs and dividing by the total mass. *(Guide: "Center of mass of particles"; Practice Q1)*
- I can use symmetry to place each uniform piece, then combine pieces, including a hole as negative mass. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can use λ = dm/dx to find the mass and center of mass of a rod whose density changes with x. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can set up a mass integral over an area by choosing strips on which the density is constant. *(Guide: "Center of mass of a continuous object"; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can check a center of mass result with a limiting case, such as a uniform object. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5(d))*
- I can explain why the center of mass may lie outside the material and why it need not split the mass in half. *(Guide: Worked example 1 and "A point to notice"; "Common misconceptions")*
- I can predict which way the center of mass moves when mass is added, removed or rearranged. *(Guide: Worked example 1(b); Practice Q4, Q7)*
