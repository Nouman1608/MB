## Understanding

- I can state Newton’s first law in rotational form and explain why a system spinning at a steady rate is in rotational equilibrium. *(Guide: "Newton’s first law in rotational form"; Practice Q1)*
- I can state the corollary: if the torques are unbalanced, the angular velocity must be changing. *(Guide: "Newton’s first law in rotational form")*
- I can explain, with a couple and a tumbling object as examples, why rotational and translational equilibrium are independent. *(Guide: "Equilibrium of one kind only"; Practice Q7)*

## Calculation

- I can draw an extended force diagram that shows where each force acts, including the weight at the center of mass. *(Guide: Figures 1 and 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can show by integration that a distributed weight has torque Mg x_cm about a pivot. *(Guide: "Extended force diagrams")*
- I can find the mass and center of mass of a rod with a given λ(x) and use them to find support forces. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3, Q5)*
- I can use ΣF = 0 and Στ = 0 together to find a cable tension and the size and direction of a hinge force. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can find the condition for an object to be about to tip, using the fact that one support force falls to zero. *(Guide: Worked example 1(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can derive τ_Q = τ_P − d × ΣF and use it to justify choosing any torque point when ΣF = 0. *(Guide: "Choosing the point for torques"; Practice Q2)*
- I can recognise a statically indeterminate problem and say which forces can and cannot be found. *(Guide: Worked example 2(c))*
- I can predict how a support force changes when a distance changes, and linearise data to extract a mass. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
