Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/1-3-representing-motion-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/1-3-representing-motion-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can name the five ways to represent a motion and say what each one shows well. *(Guide: "One motion, five representations")*
- I can read a motion diagram: decide whether an object speeds up or slows down, and whether its acceleration is constant, from the dot spacing. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why the acceleration at the top of a vertical throw is −g, not zero. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q5(b))*
- I can state the conditions for the free-fall model and the accepted values of g. *(Guide: "Free fall near Earth")*

## Skills

- I can sketch matching x–t, v_x–t and a_x–t graphs for motion in stages, with the correct curvature on x–t. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q5(a))*
- I can find a change in velocity from the area under an a_x–t graph, and displacement and distance from a v_x–t graph. *(Guide: "The graph links"; Practice Q4)*
- I can choose the constant-acceleration equation that leaves out the quantity I do not need, and use it for vertical motion. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(c))*
- I can solve a free-fall quadratic and reject the root that does not fit the motion. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can derive a symbolic expression for a time or distance and check its units. *(Guide: Worked example 2(d); Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict a factor of change from a proportion, such as h ∝ v₀² or t ∝ √h. *(Guide: "Functional dependence"; Practice Q3, Q5(d))*
- I can use motion-sensor data to justify a claim that the acceleration is constant, and use slopes and areas to find values. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can test a claim about distances in two stages by comparing average velocities. *(Practice Q7)*
