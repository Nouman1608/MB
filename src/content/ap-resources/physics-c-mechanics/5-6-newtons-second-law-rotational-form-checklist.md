## Understanding

- I can state when a rigid system’s angular velocity changes, and explain that α has the sense of the net torque, not of the rotation. *(Guide: "Newton’s second law in rotational form"; Practice Q2)*
- I can derive Στ = Iα from F = ma applied to each piece of a rigid system, and explain why internal torques cancel. *(Guide: "Where Στ = Iα comes from")*
- I can explain why a_cm = ΣF/M and α = Στ/I must be written as separate equations. *(Guide: "Linear and rotational analyses are separate"; Practice Q4(c))*

## Calculation

- I can predict the factor by which α changes when the torque, the mass or the size of an object changes. *(Guide: "Using functional dependence"; Practice Q1)*
- I can use the parallel axis theorem to find I for an off-center axle, then find α, a_cm and the axle force. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can integrate a time-dependent net torque to find ω(t) and θ(t), and find when ω is greatest. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can write one equation per object for a pulley with hanging masses and derive α in symbols. *(Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can correct a hanging-mass tension for acceleration and include a friction torque when measuring I from a graph. *(Guide: "Measuring rotational inertia in the lab"; Practice Q6)*
- I can model a torque that depends on ω by separating variables, and explain why constant-α reasoning fails for it. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can check a result with limiting cases, such as an axle at the center of mass giving α = 0. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(c))*
