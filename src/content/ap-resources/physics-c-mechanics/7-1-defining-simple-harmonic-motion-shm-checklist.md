## Understanding

- I can explain why SHM is a special case of periodic motion, and give a periodic motion that is not SHM. *(Guide: "Periodic motion and SHM"; Practice Q7)*
- I can define an equilibrium position as a place of zero net force, and a restoring force as one opposite to the displacement. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
- I can state the SHM condition m a_x = −kΔx (or F_net = −kx with x measured from equilibrium) and explain what both the minus sign and the proportionality mean. *(Guide: "The condition for SHM"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why a constant force such as gravity shifts the equilibrium but does not stop the motion being SHM. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5(d))*

## Skills

- I can find an equilibrium position from a force law or from U(x), and measure displacement from it. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can use F_net = −kx and Newton's second law to find an acceleration with the correct sign and units. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can extract k and the equilibrium position from force–position data. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can derive d²u/dt² = −(k/m)u for a block on a vertical spring. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can find k_eff = d²U/dx² for small oscillations about a stable minimum of U(x). *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can decide whether a force law gives SHM, other periodic motion, or no oscillation at all. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
- I can judge how accurate the SHM model is at a given displacement by comparing the exact force with −k_eff u. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q6(d))*
- I can explain why a small-angle pendulum is close to SHM and why the angle must be in radians. *(Guide: "The pendulum: SHM in an angle")*
