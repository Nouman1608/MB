Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/2-5-newtons-second-law-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/2-5-newtons-second-law-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can state the condition for a system's velocity to change, and explain why a constant velocity, including zero, needs no net force. *(Guide: "When does velocity change?")*
- I can explain why internal forces cannot change the motion of a system's center of mass. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q7)*
- I can explain why the acceleration points along the net force, even when the velocity points another way. *(Guide: "The law and its parts"; Figure 1)*
- I can explain why the velocity keeps the value it has reached when the net force drops to zero. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Figure 2)*

## Calculation

- I can apply ΣF_x = m a_x and ΣF_y = m a_y to forces at angles, and give the size and direction of a. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can predict the factor by which a changes when the net force and the mass both change. *(Practice Q1, Q5(e))*
- I can find an internal force, such as a tension, by applying the law to a smaller system. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5(b))*
- I can integrate a time-dependent net force to find v_x(t), including v_x0, and find when the velocity is greatest. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4)*
- I can derive the acceleration of a connected system in symbols and check it with a limiting case. *(Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can plan a cart experiment that tests a ∝ F or a ∝ 1/m, saying what to keep constant and how to make friction negligible. *(Guide: "Testing the law in the lab"; Practice Q6)*
- I can choose what to plot to get a straight line, and say what its slope means. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can use the center-of-mass velocity to judge a claim about whether a system speeds up. *(Practice Q7)*
