## Recap

- Compare the system **just before** and **just after** the collision.
- **Momentum** is conserved for an isolated system in every type of collision.
- **Elastic:** total kinetic energy after = total before. Individual kinetic energies can change.
- **Inelastic:** total kinetic energy decreases. Nonconservative contact forces transform it into internal energy, sound and permanent deformation.
- **Perfectly inelastic:** the objects stick and share one velocity, v_cm. This is the largest kinetic energy loss momentum allows.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | When it applies |
|---|---|
| Σ mᵢuᵢ = Σ mᵢvᵢ (components) | every collision of an isolated system |
| Σ ½mᵢuᵢ² = Σ ½mᵢvᵢ² | elastic only |
| v₂ − v₁ = −(u₂ − u₁) | elastic, one dimension |
| v₁ = (m₁ − m₂)u₁/(m₁ + m₂), v₂ = 2m₁u₁/(m₁ + m₂) | elastic, one dimension, object 2 at rest |
| v = (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂)/(m₁ + m₂) | perfectly inelastic |
| K_after = P²/(2M) | perfectly inelastic (all mass moves at v_cm) |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- External impulses are negligible during the short contact.
- Masses do not change, and nothing is ejected.
- u means velocity before, v means velocity after; signs follow the stated axis.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Saying momentum is lost in an inelastic collision.** Only kinetic energy decreases.
2. **Conserving each object's kinetic energy.** Only the total, and only if elastic.
3. **Using energy conservation across an inelastic collision.** Use momentum across it.
4. **Adding kinetic energies as vectors.** They are scalars.
5. **Applying elastic formulas to an unclassified collision.**
6. **"Sticking means everything stops."** Only if the total momentum is zero.

## Quick self-check

1. A 1.5 kg cart at 2.0 m/s hits and sticks to a 0.50 kg cart at rest. Find the final speed and the kinetic energy before and after. *(1.5 m/s; 3.0 J before, 2.25 J after)*
2. Two equal masses collide elastically head-on; one was at rest and the other moved at 3.0 m/s. What happens? *(They swap: 0 and 3.0 m/s)*
3. Total kinetic energy is 10 J before and 10 J after, but one object's kinetic energy halved. What type of collision is it? *(Elastic; energy moved to the other object)*
