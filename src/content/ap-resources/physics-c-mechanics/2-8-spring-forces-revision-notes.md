Short on time? This page is the recap for the **calculus-based** course (separate from Physics 1, which does not combine springs). For explanations, diagrams and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/2-8-spring-forces-study-guide/).

## Recap

- An **ideal spring** has negligible mass and exerts a force proportional to its change in length from its **relaxed length**. A spring with real mass, or a curved force–extension graph, is **nonideal**.
- A massless spring pulls with the same size of force at both ends.
- The spring force points back towards the relaxed length: a stretched spring pulls, a compressed spring pushes.
- For a hanging block, equilibrium is at stretch s₀ = mg/k. The **net** force points towards that equilibrium.
- k is the slope of the force–extension graph.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Meaning |
|---|---|
| \|F_s\| = k\|Δx\| | size of an ideal spring force; k in N/m |
| F_s,x = −kx | direction: back towards the relaxed position (x = 0) |
| s₀ = mg/k | stretch of a hanging block at equilibrium |
| 1/k_eq = 1/k₁ + 1/k₂ + … | series (same force); k_eq < smallest k |
| k_eq = k₁ + k₂ + … | parallel (same extension) |
| k_piece = nk | one of n equal pieces of a cut spring |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Springs are ideal unless stated. g = 9.8 m/s².
- Combinations are all series or all parallel; mixed arrangements are not expected.
- In a parallel set-up, the springs have equal extensions (for example, the bar they hold stays level).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Centimetres with N/m.** Convert to metres.
2. **Using the spring's length instead of its change in length.**
3. **Series and parallel swapped.** Ask "same force or same extension?", not "how does it look?"
4. **Thinking series springs are stiffer.** They are softer than the softest one.
5. **Spring force versus net force** for a hanging block.
6. **Assuming F = kx for a curved graph.** Use the local slope instead.

## Quick self-check

1. A spring with k = 400 N/m is compressed by 2.5 cm. What force does it exert? *(400 × 0.025 = 10 N, pushing outwards)*
2. Two 200 N/m springs: what is k_eq in series and in parallel? *(100 N/m; 400 N/m)*
3. A 0.50 kg block hangs from a 98 N/m spring. What is its equilibrium stretch? *(4.9 ÷ 98 = 0.050 m)*
