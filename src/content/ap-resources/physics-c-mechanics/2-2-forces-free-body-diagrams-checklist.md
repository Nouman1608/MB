Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/2-2-forces-free-body-diagrams-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/2-2-forces-free-body-diagrams-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can describe any force as an interaction, naming the object that exerts it and the object it acts on. *(Guide: "A force is an interaction"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why an object or system cannot exert a net force on itself, and say what really pushes a car or a runner forward. *(Guide: "Nothing can push itself"; Practice Q2, Q7)*
- I can explain contact forces as the large-scale effect of electric forces between atoms, and tell them apart from long-range forces. *(Guide: "Contact forces and long-range forces"; Practice Q7(c))*

## Skills

- I can draw a free-body diagram with a dot for the center of mass and one straight, labelled arrow per force. *(Guide: "What a free-body diagram shows"; Practice Q5)*
- I can draw forces in the same direction side by side and leave components off the diagram. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can choose tilted axes on a slope and write the components of gravity as mg sin θ and mg cos θ. *(Guide: "Choosing axes"; Practice Q4)*
- I can turn a free-body diagram into a table of components and component sums. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can add forces written in unit-vector notation. *(Guide: "Forces as vectors")*

## Reasoning

- I can draw diagrams for different choices of system and explain why internal forces disappear from the system diagram. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
- I can draw a free-body diagram as a snapshot when a force changes with time, and say when a force such as the normal force disappears. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can explain why a normal force or tension need not equal the weight. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(c))*
