## Understanding

- I can describe a physical pendulum as a rigid body on a fixed axis, and identify m, d and I about the pivot. *(Guide: "What a physical pendulum is")*
- I can explain, with a diagram, why gravity at the centre of mass gives a restoring torque −mgd sin θ. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5(a))*
- I can explain why sin θ ≈ θ needs radians and small angles, and say how the period changes at large amplitude. *(Guide: "The small-angle approximation"; Practice Q5(e))*
- I can explain why a torsion pendulum's period does not depend on g. *(Guide: "The torsion pendulum"; Practice Q2)*

## Calculation

- I can derive d²θ/dt² = −(mgd/I)θ from τ = Iα and read off ω and T. *(Guide: "From τ = Iα to SHM"; Practice Q5)*
- I can show that the simple pendulum period is the special case I = ml², d = l. *(Guide: "The simple pendulum"; Practice Q5(d))*
- I can find the period of a rod, plank, hoop or rod-and-block system, using the parallel axis theorem. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can use a torsion pendulum to find a torsion constant and an unknown rotational inertia. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can find the greatest angular speed ωθ_max without confusing it with ω. *(Practice Q3)*

## Reasoning and experiment

- I can plan a pendulum experiment that reduces timing and amplitude errors. *(Guide: "Designing a pendulum experiment"; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can linearise pendulum data, plot it with labelled axes and units, and find g and k² from the slope and intercept. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can test a claim about adding mass to a pendulum and find where added mass leaves the period unchanged. *(Practice Q7)*
