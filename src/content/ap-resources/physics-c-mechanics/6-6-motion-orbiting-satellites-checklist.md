## Understanding

- I can explain, using momentum conservation, why a massive central body's motion can be ignored, and estimate how small it is. *(Guide: "The system: a massive body and a light satellite"; Practice Q7(c))*
- I can explain why U_g is negative with the zero at infinity, and what a negative total energy means. *(Guide: "Gravitational potential energy with the zero at infinity")*
- I can say which of E, L, K and U are constant in circular and in elliptical orbits, and justify each with a force or torque argument. *(Guide: "What is conserved, and why"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why a satellite moved to a higher circular orbit has more total energy but less kinetic energy. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(d))*

## Calculation

- I can derive U_g = −GMm/r by integrating the gravitational force from infinity. *(Guide: "Gravitational potential energy with the zero at infinity")*
- I can derive K = −½U and E = ½U = −GMm/(2r) for a circular orbit and use them. *(Guide: "Energy in a circular orbit"; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can predict how speed and energy change when a circular orbit's radius changes by a given factor. *(Practice Q3)*
- I can use r_p v_p = r_a v_a with energy conservation to find unknown speeds or distances in an elliptical orbit. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can derive v_esc = √(2GM/r), use it, and find the speed left over far away for a faster launch. *(Guide: "Escape velocity"; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning and graphs

- I can sketch K, U and E against r for circular orbits, with correct signs and shapes. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can use P = F · v to explain when gravity does work on a satellite and how its speed changes around an ellipse. *(Practice Q7)*
