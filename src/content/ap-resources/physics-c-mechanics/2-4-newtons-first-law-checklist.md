Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/2-4-newtons-first-law-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/2-4-newtons-first-law-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can state Newton’s first law and explain that "constant velocity" includes both speed and direction, with rest as one case. *(Guide: "Newton’s first law"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why equilibrium does not mean being at rest, and why being at rest for an instant does not mean equilibrium. *(Guide: "Translational equilibrium"; Practice Q1)*
- I can describe an inertial reference frame and explain why an accelerating vehicle is not one. *(Guide: "Inertial reference frames"; Practice Q3)*

## Calculation

- I can find the net force on a system by adding forces as vectors, component by component, leaving out third-law partners. *(Guide: "Net force"; Practice Q2)*
- I can write ΣF_x = 0 and ΣF_y = 0 for a system in equilibrium and solve them for two unknown forces. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can differentiate position functions to decide along which axes the forces on a system are balanced. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can use equal displacements in equal times from motion data as evidence that the net force is zero. *(Practice Q6(a))*

## Reasoning

- I can sketch velocity–time graphs that show a balanced direction (flat line) and an unbalanced one (sloping line). *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q5(c), Q6(d))*
- I can explain that the velocity changes only along the direction of an unbalanced force. *(Guide: "Balanced in one direction, unbalanced in another")*
- I can rebut the claims that motion needs a force and that constant speed means equilibrium. *(Practice Q6(b), Q7)*
