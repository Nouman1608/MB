Short on time? This page is the recap for the **calculus-based** course, which uses î, ĵ, k̂ notation in three dimensions (the Physics 1 topic stays in one dimension). For explanations, diagrams and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/1-1-scalars-vectors-study-guide/).

## Recap

- **Scalars** have magnitude only: distance, speed, time, mass.
- **Vectors** have magnitude and direction: position, displacement, velocity, acceleration.
- Draw a vector as an **arrow**: it points the vector's way, and its length is proportional to the magnitude (state the scale).
- In **one dimension**, state the positive direction; opposite directions get opposite signs.
- In two or three dimensions, write vectors with **unit vectors** î, ĵ, k̂ along +x, +y, +z, or as a magnitude and a direction.
- The **resultant** is found by adding components axis by axis.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A = A_x î + A_y ĵ + A_z k̂ | unit vector notation; components are signed scalars |
| \|A\| = √(A_x² + A_y² + A_z²) | magnitude, never negative |
| A_x = \|A\| cos θ, A_y = \|A\| sin θ | θ measured from +x towards +y |
| tan θ = A_y / A_x | direction; check the quadrant with a sketch |
| r = x î + y ĵ + z k̂ | position vector from the origin to the point |
| r̂ = r / \|r\| | unit vector along r: magnitude 1, no unit |
| Δr = r − r₀ | displacement |
| R_x = A_x + B_x, R_y = A_y + B_y, R_z = A_z + B_z | resultant R = A + B |
| \|A\| − \|B\| ≤ \|A + B\| ≤ \|A\| + \|B\| | range of a resultant (\|A\| ≥ \|B\|) |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Axes are perpendicular and right-handed; î, ĵ, k̂ each have magnitude 1.
- Angles are in degrees unless stated. Your calculator must be in degree mode.
- Vectors may be moved on a diagram if their length and direction are unchanged.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Adding magnitudes** instead of components.
2. **Distance = |displacement|** for a path that bends or turns back.
3. **Trusting tan⁻¹** without checking which quadrant the vector is in.
4. **Swapping sine and cosine** when the angle is measured from the y-axis.
5. **Dropping a sign** for a component that points west, south, down or left.
6. **Giving r̂ a unit**, or a magnitude other than 1.
7. **|r₂| − |r₁| for |Δr|.** Subtract the vectors first.

## Quick self-check

1. What is the magnitude of (2.0 î − 6.0 ĵ + 3.0 k̂) m? *(√(4 + 36 + 9) = 7.0 m)*
2. A displacement is 20 m at 30° below the +x axis. Give its components. *(17.3 m and −10.0 m)*
3. Add (4.0 î + 1.0 ĵ) m and (−6.0 î + 3.0 ĵ) m. Give the magnitude and the angle from +x. *(−2.0 î + 4.0 ĵ m; 4.47 m at 116.6°, second quadrant)*
4. With +x to the right, a cart moves 2.5 m/s to the left. What is v_x? *(−2.5 m/s; speed 2.5 m/s)*
