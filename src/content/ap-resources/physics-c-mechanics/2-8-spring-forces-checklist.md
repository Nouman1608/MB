Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/2-8-spring-forces-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/2-8-spring-forces-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can state the two conditions for an ideal spring and give an example of each way a spring can be nonideal. *(Guide: "The ideal spring model"; Practice Q7(a))*
- I can explain why a massless spring exerts the same size of force at both ends. *(Guide: "The ideal spring model"; Practice Q5(a))*
- I can explain why the spring force points towards the relaxed length while the net force on a hanging block points towards its equilibrium. *(Guide: "A hanging block"; Practice Q3)*

## Calculation

- I can use F_s,x = −kx with x in metres and give the direction of the force from the sign. *(Practice Q1)*
- I can find k_eq for springs all in series or all in parallel, and the force and extension of each spring. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can find the spring constant of a piece of a cut spring and use it in a combination. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can find k from the slope of a force–extension graph. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can derive the series and parallel rules and show that series k_eq is below the smallest k. *(Guide: "Combining springs"; Practice Q5)*
- I can decide whether springs act in series or in parallel by asking "same force or same extension?", even when the picture misleads. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can use data to decide whether a spring is ideal over a range, and predict factors of change in extension. *(Practice Q2, Q6(c))*
- I can explain how a spring's own mass changes its stretch. *(Practice Q7)*
