## Understanding

- I can choose a system that includes the spring and explain why the block alone does not have constant energy. *(Guide: "The system and its energy")*
- I can explain where kinetic energy is greatest and where it is zero, and why potential energy is greatest at the turning points. *(Guide: "Trading energy back and forth"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why K and U each repeat every half period. *(Guide: "Energy against time"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why a vertical spring has the same energy pattern when y is measured from the hanging equilibrium. *(Guide: "Vertical springs and pendulums")*

## Calculation

- I can show that dE/dt = 0 from Newton's second law, and that K + U = ½kA² from x(t). *(Guide: "Why the total energy is constant"; Practice Q5)*
- I can use E = ½kA² = ½mv_max² to find the amplitude, maximum speed or spring constant. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can find the speed at any position from K = ½k(A² − x²), and the positions where K and U are in a given ratio. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4(c)–(d))*
- I can find the amplitude from a starting position and velocity using energy. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*

## Reasoning with representations

- I can read turning points, K and U from an energy–position graph. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can sketch K, U and E against time on the same axes. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can linearise speed–position data to find k and E. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can predict how E, v_max and T change when A, m or k changes, including after a sudden push. *(Guide: "Changing the amplitude, mass or spring"; Practice Q3, Q7)*
