Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/3-5-power-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/3-5-power-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can describe power as the rate of energy change, and say whether a given process is a transfer into or out of my chosen system or a conversion inside it. *(Guide: "Power is a rate of energy change"; Practice Q4(c))*
- I can explain why only the component of a force along the velocity delivers power, and give an example of a force that delivers zero power. *(Guide: Figure 1 and "The sign of power"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why constant power does not mean constant force, and why a constant-power model fails at v = 0. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(e))*

## Calculation

- I can find average power from a total energy change or total work and the time taken, with the unit W = J/s. *(Guide: "Average power")*
- I can derive P = F·v from P = dW/dt and dW = F·dr, and calculate it from vector components. *(Guide: "Instantaneous power"; Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can find the net power on an object as dK/dt or as F_net·v, and use it to find how fast the speed is changing. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can find work as W = ∫P dt, or as the area under a power–time graph, and check it against ΔK. *(Guide: Worked example 3 and Figure 2; Practice Q5(b))*
- I can find v(t) and x(t) for an object driven from rest at constant power, and the force at any speed from F = P/v. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3 and Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can split an applied force's power into rates of potential-energy and internal-energy gain for steady motion against friction. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can compare two situations at the same power, for example carts of different mass, and predict ratios of energy and speed. *(Practice Q3)*
- I can linearise speed–time data to test a constant-power claim and extract the power from a slope. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can work out what a power–time relationship says about the force, and explain why average force × average velocity is not the average power. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7)*
