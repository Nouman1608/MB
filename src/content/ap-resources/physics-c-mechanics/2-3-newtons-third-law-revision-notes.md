## Recap

- Every force is one half of an **interaction** between two objects.
- If A pushes or pulls on B, then B pushes or pulls on A **at the same instant**, with **equal size** and **opposite direction**.
- The pair is always the **same type** of force and acts on **two different objects**.
- Show a pair on **two separate** free-body diagrams, with equal-length arrows and matching labels.
- **Internal** forces (between parts of your chosen system) cancel in pairs. They cannot change how the system’s **centre of mass** moves.
- **Tension** is the combined effect of string segments pulling on their neighbours.

## Key relationships

| Idea | Statement | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Third law | F_B on A = −F_A on B | names swapped, same type, same instant |
| System of particles | ΣF (all parts) = ΣF_ext | internal pairs add to zero |
| Hanging string at rest | dT/dy = λg | y measured up from the bottom; λ in kg/m |
| Tension along a hanging string | T(y) = T(0) + ∫₀ʸ λg dy′ | largest at the top |
| Ideal string | λ → 0, so T is the same everywhere | also does not stretch |
| Ideal pulley | same T on both sides | massless, frictionless axle |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- g = 9.8 m/s² near Earth’s surface.
- "Light", "massless" or "ideal" string: ignore its mass and stretch.
- dT/dy = λg holds for a string **hanging at rest**; other situations need the second law (Topic 2.5).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Pairing weight with normal force.** Same object, different types: not a pair.
2. **Bigger mass, bigger force.** The two forces in an interaction are always equal in size.
3. **Cancelling partners on one diagram.** They belong on different diagrams.
4. **Forgetting the system choice.** A force is internal or external only relative to the system you chose.
5. **Assuming equal tension in a heavy rope.** Only an ideal string has the same tension throughout.
6. **Putting the hand’s push on the far block.** A force appears only on the object it touches (or attracts).

## Quick self-check

1. A cyclist’s tyre pushes backwards on the road. What is the partner force? *(The road pushes forwards on the tyre, same size, friction.)*
2. A uniform 4.0 m rope of mass 2.0 kg hangs at rest from a beam. What is the tension 1.0 m below the top? *(It supports the 3.0 m below, mass 1.5 kg: T = 1.5 × 9.8 ≈ 15 N.)*
3. Two skaters, at rest together on smooth ice, push apart. Does their centre of mass move? *(No. The pushes are internal and nothing else acts horizontally.)*
