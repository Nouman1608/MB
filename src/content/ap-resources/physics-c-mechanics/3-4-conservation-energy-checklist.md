Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/3-4-conservation-energy-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/3-4-conservation-energy-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can say which kinds of energy a chosen system can have, and why a one-object system has only kinetic energy. *(Guide: "What energies can a system have?")*
- I can state the two conditions for a system's mechanical energy to be constant. *(Guide: "Choosing the system decides what stays constant"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain that total energy is always conserved, and where mechanical energy goes when friction acts. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7)*

## Calculation

- I can use K + U = constant to find speeds, including with a spring and gravity together. *(Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can find the speed at any point and the turning points from U(x) and a total energy. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can use U_g = −GMm/r to find escape speed and greatest height, and compare with the constant-g answer. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can integrate a position-dependent friction force to find the energy dissipated. *(Guide: Worked example 3)*

## Reasoning and experiment

- I can draw energy bar charts with values for several moments and show the total. *(Guide: Figure 2)*
- I can compare two scenarios with energy, predicting how a speed or distance changes by a factor. *(Guide: "Comparing scenarios"; Worked example 3(d))*
- I can explain which student is right when one confuses constant speed with constant mechanical energy. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can design an experiment to test energy conservation, choose a linearised graph and find the fraction of energy dissipated. *(Practice Q6)*
