Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/6-2-torque-work-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-mechanics/6-2-torque-work-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why a torque on a body that does not turn transfers no energy. *(Guide: "A torque transfers energy only through an angle")*
- I can decide whether the work done by a torque is positive, negative or zero from the senses of the torque and the rotation. *(Guide: "Positive, negative and zero work"; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can explain why torque and work share base units but are different quantities. *(Guide: "From force work to torque work")*

## Calculation

- I can derive dW = τ dθ from the work done by the tangential component of a force, using ds = r dθ. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5(a))*
- I can calculate the work done by a torque that depends on angle by integration. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can find work as the signed area under a torque–angle graph, including estimates from data. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can derive the rotational work–energy theorem from τ_net = Iα with the chain rule. *(Guide: "The rotational work–energy theorem"; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can use net work to find a final angular velocity, including friction's negative work. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can derive symbolic results, such as the angle of greatest ω and the stopping angle, for a body acted on by several torques. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can compare work, kinetic energy and ω between bodies with different rotational inertias. *(Guide: "Comparing scenarios"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why equal angles do not give equal work when the torque changes with angle. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can use measured and predicted energies to estimate a friction torque. *(Practice Q6(d))*
