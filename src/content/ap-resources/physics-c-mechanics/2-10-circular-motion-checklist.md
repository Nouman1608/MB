## Understanding

- I can derive a_c = v²/r, directed to the centre, by differentiating r(t) = r cos(ωt) î + r sin(ωt) ĵ twice. *(Guide: "Why moving in a circle means accelerating")*
- I can explain why "centripetal force" never appears on a free-body diagram, and name the real forces that provide it in a given situation. *(Guide: "Where the inward force comes from"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain when the net force on an object moving in a circle does not point to the centre. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q7)*

## Calculation

- I can use T = 1/f and T = 2πr/v, and predict factors of change in a_c. *(Guide: "Period and frequency"; Practice Q1, Q3)*
- I can find a_t = dv/dt from v(t) or s(t) and combine it with a_c to give the size and direction of the net acceleration. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7)*
- I can derive the minimum speed √(gr) at the top of a vertical loop. *(Guide: "Vertical loops"; Practice Q2)*
- I can derive and use the greatest and least speeds on a banked curve with friction. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can derive Kepler's third law for a circular orbit and use it to find a period, a radius or a central mass. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can decide the direction of friction on a banked curve from the car's speed. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q5(a))*
- I can show that a conical pendulum's period depends only on h and g, and use a T²–h graph to find g. *(Guide: "Conical pendulums"; Practice Q6)*
- I can evaluate a claim about the forces on an object in circular motion using component reasoning. *(Practice Q7(c))*
