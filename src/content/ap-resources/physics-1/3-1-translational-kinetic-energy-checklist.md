## Understanding

- I can explain what translational kinetic energy is and why it uses the speed of the center of mass. *(Guide: "Energy of motion")*
- I can explain why kinetic energy is a scalar that is never negative, and why moving left or right at the same speed gives the same K. *(Guide: "Kinetic energy is a scalar"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why the kinetic energies of objects in a system add, even when their velocities cancel. *(Guide: "Kinetic energy is a scalar"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why two observers in different frames can measure different kinetic energies for the same object. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q7)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate K = ½mv² with correct units, and show that 1 J = 1 kg·m²/s². *(Guide: "Energy of motion"; Practice Q1)*
- I can find an object's speed from its kinetic energy using v = √(2K/m). *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can predict the factor by which K changes when the mass or speed changes. *(Guide: factor-of-change table; Practice Q2)*
- I can calculate a change in kinetic energy by subtracting two kinetic energies, not by using ½m(Δv)². *(Guide: Worked example 3)*

## Reasoning and representations

- I can sketch K against velocity and K against v², and state the slope of the straight-line graph. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
- I can sketch K against time for an object thrown straight up and explain why it touches zero at the top. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can choose what to plot so that kinetic-energy data give a straight line, and use the slope to test a claim. *(Guide: linearising paragraph; Practice Q6)*
- I can use K ∝ v² to explain why halving K does not halve the speed. *(Practice Q4)*
