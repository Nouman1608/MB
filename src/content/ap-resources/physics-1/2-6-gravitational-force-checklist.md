## Understanding

- I can describe the gravitational force between two masses: attractive, along the line of centres, and acting at each centre of mass. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
- I can explain why the forces two bodies exert on each other are equal, even when one is far more massive. *(Guide: "Gravity is an interaction between masses"; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain what a gravitational field is and why N/kg is the same unit as m/s². *(Guide: "The gravitational field")*
- I can explain when the gravitational force near a planet can be treated as constant, and when it cannot. *(Guide: "Weight, and when gravity is constant")*
- I can tell inertial mass from gravitational mass and explain why their equality makes all objects fall with the same acceleration. *(Guide: "Inertial mass and gravitational mass"; Practice Q7)*

## Skills

- I can calculate a gravitational force with G, both masses and the centre-to-centre distance. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can predict the factor of change in force or field strength when masses and distances change. *(Guide: inverse-square table, Worked example 2; Practice Q1, Q2)*
- I can calculate g at a height above a planet by adding the height to the radius. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*
- I can calculate apparent weight in an accelerating lift with F_N = m(g + a_y). *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q5)*
- I can linearise field-strength data by plotting g against 1/r² and find the central mass from the slope. *(Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why a scale reading depends on the lift’s acceleration and not on its velocity. *(Guide: Figure 3; Practice Q3, Q5)*
- I can explain why astronauts in orbit appear weightless even though gravity acts on them. *(Guide: "Weightlessness and the equivalence principle"; Practice Q7)*
- I can state the equivalence principle in my own words, using a closed lift as the example. *(Guide: "Weightlessness and the equivalence principle")*
