Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, graphs and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/1-2-displacement-velocity-acceleration-study-guide/). This is the algebra-based course: no calculus is needed.

## Recap

- Treat the moving thing as a **point object**. Choose an origin and a positive direction, and write them down.
- **Position** x says where the object is. **Displacement** Δx is the change in position. **Distance travelled** is the total path length and is never negative.
- **Average velocity** uses only the start and end of an interval. **Average speed** uses the whole path.
- An object **accelerates** whenever its velocity changes: faster, slower, or reversing direction.
- **Instantaneous** velocity (or acceleration) is the average over a very short interval. On a graph, it is the slope of the tangent line.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Δx = x − x₀ | x, x₀ in m | find displacement (signed) |
| v_avg = Δx / Δt | m/s | find average velocity over an interval |
| average speed = distance / Δt | m/s, never negative | describe the whole path, including turn-arounds |
| a_avg = Δv_x / Δt = (v_x − v_x0) / Δt | m/s² | find average acceleration |
| slope of x–t graph | m/s | velocity (tangent slope = instantaneous) |
| slope of v_x–t graph | m/s² | acceleration |
| area under v_x–t graph | m | displacement (area below the axis is negative) |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The object is a **point**: rotation and size are ignored.
- Motion is along **one straight line**, so a sign fully describes direction.
- The reference frame is at rest relative to the ground (inertial) unless stated.
- Near Earth's surface, free-fall acceleration has size **g = 9.8 m/s²** downward (air resistance ignored). The course also accepts 10 m/s² where stated.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **No axis stated.** A sign means nothing without one.
2. **Negative a = slowing down.** Wrong. Compare the signs of v_x and a_x.
3. **Dropping signs in Δv_x.** A ball that rebounds changes velocity by more than the change in speed.
4. **Mean of two velocities as average velocity.** Use total Δx ÷ total Δt.
5. **Secant slope used as the instantaneous value.** Use a tangent, or shrink the interval.
6. **v = 0 so a = 0.** Not at the top of a throw.
7. **Distance and displacement swapped.** Check whether the object turned around.

## Quick self-check

1. With +x to the right, a cart goes from x = +2.0 m to x = −4.0 m in 3.0 s. What is its average velocity? *(−2.0 m/s)*
2. v_x = −5.0 m/s and a_x = −2.0 m/s². Speeding up or slowing down? *(Speeding up: same signs.)*
3. A ball hits a wall at +3.0 m/s and rebounds at −2.0 m/s after 0.10 s of contact. What is its average acceleration? *(−50 m/s²)*
