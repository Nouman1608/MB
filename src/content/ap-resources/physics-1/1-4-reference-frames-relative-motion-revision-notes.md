Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, diagrams and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/1-4-reference-frames-relative-motion-study-guide/). This is the algebra-based course: no calculus is needed.

## Recap

- A **reference frame** is an origin, an axis and a clock that belong to one observer. Every position and velocity is measured in some frame.
- Changing the observer can change the **size and direction** of a measured velocity or displacement.
- An **inertial frame** is not accelerating. Treat every frame as inertial unless told otherwise.
- In this course, relative velocities are combined **along one line only**, as signed numbers.
- All inertial observers measure the **same acceleration** and the same time interval Δt.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Meaning | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| v_AC = v_AB + v_BC | inner letters match | find what an outside observer measures |
| v_BA = −v_AB | swap subscripts, flip sign | describe the observer as seen by the object |
| v_AB = v_AG − v_BG | object first, observer second | convert two ground-frame velocities |
| x_AB = x_AG − x_BG | position of A measured by B | convert positions |
| Δx_AC = Δx_AB + Δx_BC | displacements chain too | compare how far each observer says A moved |
| a_AB = a_AG (B inertial) | acceleration is frame-independent | skip recalculating acceleration |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Objects and observers are **points** moving on **one straight line**.
- Every frame moves at **constant velocity** relative to the ground, and the ground counts as inertial.
- All observers' clocks agree. (This fails only near the speed of light, which is outside the course.)
- Near Earth's surface, g = 9.8 m/s² downward, the same in every inertial frame.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **No observer named.** "The velocity is 5 m/s" relative to what?
2. **Subtracting speeds instead of signed velocities.** Objects approaching each other close at the **sum** of their speeds.
3. **Subscripts reversed.** v_AG − v_BG gives v_AB, not v_BA.
4. **Recalculating acceleration in a moving frame.** For an inertial observer it is unchanged.
5. **Thinking one observer is wrong.** Both are right in their own frames.
6. **Adding velocities at an angle.** Relative motion here stays on one line.

## Quick self-check

1. Take +x downstream. A river flows at +1.0 m/s. A boat moves at 3.0 m/s relative to the water. What is its velocity relative to the bank going downstream, and going upstream? *(+4.0 m/s; −2.0 m/s)*
2. v_AB = +6.0 m/s. What is v_BA? *(−6.0 m/s)*
3. A car accelerates at 2.5 m/s² relative to the road. What acceleration does a bus driver moving at a steady 12 m/s measure for it? *(2.5 m/s², the same)*
4. With +x east, an object is at x = 40 m and an observer at x = 55 m, both measured from the ground. Where is the object in the observer's frame? *(−15 m: 15 m west of the observer)*
