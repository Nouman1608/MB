Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/1-2-displacement-velocity-acceleration-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/1-2-displacement-velocity-acceleration-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what the object model ignores and why a moving car can be treated as a point. *(Guide: "Start with an object and an axis")*
- I can state a coordinate system in words, such as "+x to the right, origin at the start line", before I write any sign. *(Guide; every practice question)*
- I can explain the difference between displacement and distance travelled, and give a trip where they differ. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why an object whose velocity reverses direction is accelerating even if its speed is the same before and after. *(Guide: "Average acceleration"; Practice Q4)*

## Skills

- I can calculate average velocity and average speed for a trip with a turn-around. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can calculate average acceleration with signed velocities, including a rebound. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can estimate an instantaneous velocity by shrinking the time interval in a data table. *(Guide: "Instantaneous velocity without calculus"; Practice Q3)*
- I can draw a tangent line on a position–time graph and find its slope from two points on the line. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
- I can find acceleration from the slope of a velocity–time graph, and displacement and distance from its areas. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can turn position data into a velocity–time graph using interval midpoints and read off the acceleration. *(Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can decide whether an object is speeding up or slowing down from the signs of v_x and a_x, without assuming negative acceleration means slowing. *(Guide: speeding-up table; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can justify why a ball at the top of its flight has zero velocity but non-zero acceleration. *(Guide: "Limiting cases worth knowing"; Practice Q7)*
- I can use experimental data to support or refute a claim that acceleration is constant. *(Practice Q6)*
