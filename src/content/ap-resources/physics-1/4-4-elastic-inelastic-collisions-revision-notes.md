Short on time? This page is the recap. For graphs, energy bar charts and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/4-4-elastic-inelastic-collisions-study-guide/). This is the algebra-based course: no calculus is needed.

## Recap

- Every collision of a system with no net external force conserves **momentum**. The type of collision depends on **kinetic energy**.
- **Elastic:** total kinetic energy of the system is the same before and after. One object can lose kinetic energy as long as the other gains the same amount.
- **Inelastic:** total kinetic energy decreases. Nonconservative forces transform it into internal (thermal) energy, sound and deformation. Total energy is still conserved.
- **Perfectly inelastic:** the objects stick and move with one velocity. This leaves the least kinetic energy that momentum conservation allows.
- **Explosions** increase kinetic energy, using stored energy (spring, chemical).
- In this course you will not solve for two unknown final velocities. You may be given one and asked for the other, then asked to classify.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Use it to |
|---|---|
| Σ(mv) before = Σ(mv) after | find an unknown velocity (signed) |
| K_sys = Σ ½mv² | compare kinetic energy before and after (never negative) |
| m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v_f | find the common velocity when objects stick |
| fraction of K transformed = m₂ / (m₁ + m₂) | m₁ moving sticks to m₂ at rest |
| ΔK = K_after − K_before | size of the transformation (negative for inelastic) |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- No net external impulse during the short collision.
- Objects are treated as particles; rotation is ignored.
- "Elastic" is a model. Real collisions are at best close to elastic.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Momentum "lost" in a crash.** It is kinetic energy that falls.
2. **Energy conservation across a sticky collision.** Use momentum for the collision; use energy only afterwards.
3. **Judging by individual objects.** Classify by the system total.
4. **Bouncing apart = elastic.** Calculate the totals.
5. **Signs in kinetic energy.** Square speeds; K is never negative.
6. **Early rounding.** Keep extra figures until the comparison.

## Quick self-check

1. A 1.0 kg cart at 4.0 m/s sticks to a 3.0 kg cart at rest. Find the common speed and the kinetic energy before and after. *(1.0 m/s; 8.0 J and 2.0 J)*
2. A moving cart sticks to an identical cart at rest. What fraction of the kinetic energy is transformed? *(One half)*
3. Two balls have 5.0 J of kinetic energy in total before a collision and 5.0 J after, but one ball's kinetic energy changed. Elastic or inelastic? *(Elastic: the system total is unchanged.)*
