Short on time? This page is the recap. For the derivation, diagrams and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/6-2-torque-work-study-guide/). This is the algebra-based course: no calculus is needed.

## Recap

- A torque transfers energy into or out of a rigid system only when the system **turns** while the torque acts.
- A tangential force F at radius r moves its point of application along an arc s = rΔθ, so W = Fs = (rF)Δθ = **τΔθ**.
- A torque in the **same** sense as the rotation does positive work (energy in). An **opposing** torque, such as axle friction, does negative work (energy out).
- A force through the axle exerts no torque and does no work on the rotation.
- The **net** work done by all torques equals the change in rotational kinetic energy.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| W = τΔθ | τ in N·m, Δθ in rad, W in J | work by a constant torque |
| s = rΔθ | s, r in m | arc length moved by the point where a force acts |
| τ = rF⊥ | N·m | torque from the perpendicular force component |
| W = area under τ–θ graph | J | work by a changing torque |
| W_net = ½Iω² − ½Iω₀² | J | find a final angular velocity, or a stopping angle |
| 1 rev = 2π rad; 90° = π/2 rad | rad | convert angles first |

**Factors of change:** τ × 2 at the same Δθ → W × 2. From rest, W × 2 → ω × √2. A stopping angle against a constant friction torque grows with ω₀², so doubling ω₀ multiplies it by 4.

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The object is **rigid** and turns about a **fixed axis** in one plane.
- W = τΔθ needs a **constant** torque; otherwise use the graph area.
- Friction torques are treated as constant unless a graph says otherwise.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Degrees or revolutions in W = τΔθ.** Convert to radians.
2. **Calling torque "energy" because both use N·m.** Write work in J.
3. **Positive work for friction.** It opposes the rotation, so its work is negative.
4. **Largest torque × whole angle** for a changing torque. Use the area.
5. **Work done by a torque that does not turn anything.** It is zero.
6. **Forgetting the starting kinetic energy** when the object is already spinning: W_net is the **change** in K.

## Quick self-check

1. A constant torque of 5.0 N·m turns a wheel through 4.0 rad. How much work is done? *(20 J)*
2. A constant torque of 2.0 N·m acts through 3.0 revolutions. How much work is done? *(3.0 × 2π = 18.8 rad, so 37.7 J)*
3. Torque rises steadily from 0 to 4.0 N·m as a wheel turns from 0 to 3.0 rad. How much work is done? *(Triangle: ½ × 3.0 × 4.0 = 6.0 J)*
