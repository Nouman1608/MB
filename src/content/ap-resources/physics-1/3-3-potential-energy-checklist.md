Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/3-3-potential-energy-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/3-3-potential-energy-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why a single object has no potential energy, and name the system that does (ball–Earth, block–spring). *(Guide: "Potential energy belongs to a system"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain what makes a force conservative and why friction has no potential energy. *(Guide: "Potential energy belongs to a system"; Practice Q1, Q7)*
- I can explain that potential energy is a scalar set by the positions of the objects in a system. *(Guide: "Scalar, and set by position")*
- I can explain why the zero of potential energy is a free choice and why ΔU does not depend on it. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q7)*

## Skills

- I can calculate spring energy with U_s = ½kΔx², converting Δx to metres. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can find the stretch or compression that stores a given energy. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate near-surface changes with ΔU_g = mgΔy, with signs that match my axis. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q7)*
- I can calculate U_g = −Gm₁m₂/r with r measured centre to centre. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
- I can add pair energies for a system of three or more objects. *(Guide: "Systems with more than two objects"; Practice Q6)*
- I can sketch U_s against Δx and U_g against r, and say what each shape means. *(Guide: Figure 1)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict how U_s changes when Δx changes, and how U_g changes when r changes, without substituting numbers. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q4, Q6)*
- I can explain why a less negative U_g is a larger potential energy. *(Guide: "The general form"; Practice Q4)*
- I can justify when ΔU_g = mgΔy is a good approximation and when the general form is needed. *(Guide: Worked example 3)*
- I can argue that ΔU_g does not depend on the path taken, because gravity is conservative. *(Practice Q7)*
