## Recap

- **Angular momentum** is the rotational partner of linear momentum. It is always measured about a stated axis or point.
- A rigid body spinning about a fixed axis has **L = Iω**.
- An object moving in a straight line has angular momentum about any point off its path: **L = rmv sin θ = mvd**, where d is the perpendicular distance from the point to the line of motion. About a point on the path, L = 0.
- **Angular impulse** is torque × time. It has the same sign as the torque.
- The **rotational impulse–momentum theorem** says the net angular impulse equals the change in angular momentum. It follows from τ_net = Iα when I is constant.
- This course uses only a **+ or − sign** for each sense of rotation. Three-dimensional directions of L are not tested.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| L = Iω | kg·m²/s | angular momentum of a rigid body |
| L = rmv sin θ = mvd | kg·m²/s | angular momentum of a moving object about a point |
| angular impulse = τΔt | N·m·s (= kg·m²/s) | effect of a torque acting for a time |
| ΔL = L − L₀ | kg·m²/s | change in angular momentum, with signs |
| τ_net Δt = ΔL | — | link torque, time and change in L |
| slope of L–t graph | N·m | net torque |
| area under τ–t graph | N·m·s | angular impulse (area below the axis is negative) |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The body is **rigid** and turns about a **fixed axis**, so I is constant while torques act.
- θ is the angle between the radial line (point to object) and the velocity, not between the path and some other line.
- Friction and drag are negligible **unless stated**. If they are not, include their torques in τ_net.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **No reference point stated.** L about one point is not L about another.
2. **Dropping sin θ.** L = rmv only when v is perpendicular to r.
3. **Forgetting L₀.** The theorem gives the change, not the final value.
4. **Ignoring signs.** A braking torque gives a negative angular impulse when the spin is positive.
5. **Comparing torques instead of angular impulses.** A small torque for a long time can beat a large torque for a short time.
6. **Force × time instead of torque × time.** Multiply by the lever arm first.
7. **"Straight-line motion has no angular momentum."** It does, about any point off the line.

## Quick self-check

1. A wheel with I = 0.30 kg·m² spins at 8.0 rad/s. What is its angular momentum? *(2.4 kg·m²/s)*
2. A constant net torque of 0.50 N·m acts for 6.0 s. What is the change in angular momentum? *(3.0 kg·m²/s, same sign as the torque)*
3. A 0.10 kg ball moves at 5.0 m/s in a straight line that passes 0.20 m from a post. What is its angular momentum about the post? *(0.10 kg·m²/s, at every point on its path)*
