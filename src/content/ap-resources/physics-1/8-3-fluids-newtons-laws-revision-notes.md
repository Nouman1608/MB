## Recap

- Newton's laws apply to any **small piece of fluid**. Its forces are the pressure forces on its faces, its weight, and pushes from walls.
- A fluid's velocity changes only when the forces on it do not balance. In a level pipe, that means a **pressure difference**: the fluid accelerates from high to low pressure.
- In a fluid at rest, each layer holds up the fluid above it. That is why P = P₀ + ρgh.
- The **buoyant force** is the net upward force from all the fluid's pressure forces. Pressure is greater on the bottom of an object than on its top.
- Its size equals the **weight of the displaced fluid**. It does not depend on the object's mass or material, or on depth once the object is fully under.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| F_b = ρ_fluid V_disp g | ρ in kg/m³, V in m³, F in N | find the buoyant force |
| F_b = (P_bottom − P_top)A | P in Pa, A in m² | derive or check F_b from pressures |
| floating: F_b = mg | N | find a submerged depth or volume |
| V_sub / V = ρ_object / ρ_fluid | no unit | find the fraction under the surface |
| scale under water: T = mg − F_b | N | find F_b, V or ρ from two readings |
| ρ_object / ρ_fluid = mg / F_b | no unit (fully submerged) | density from two scale readings |
| ma_y = F_b − mg (+y up) | m/s² | rising or sinking objects, ignoring drag |
| a = (P₁ − P₂) / (ρL) | m/s² | acceleration of a slug of fluid of length L |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The fluid is **ideal**: incompressible (constant density at all depths) and with no viscosity.
- Drag on a moving object is ignored unless a question gives it.
- The buoyant force from air is ignored for solids and liquids weighed in air.
- g = 9.8 m/s²; density of water 1000 kg/m³.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Using the object's density in F_b.** Use the **fluid's** density.
2. **Using the whole volume for a floating object.** Use only the submerged volume.
3. **Saying F_b grows with depth.** Not for a fully submerged object in an ideal fluid.
4. **Drawing F_b and the pressure forces together.** That counts the fluid twice.
5. **"Floats because it is light."** Compare densities, not masses.
6. **"Weighs less in water."** The weight is the same; the scale reading is less.
7. **"A fluid needs a push to keep moving."** A pressure difference changes velocity; it is not needed for constant velocity.

## Quick self-check

1. A block reads 12.0 N on a spring scale in air and 9.0 N fully under water. What is the buoyant force, and what is the block's volume? *(3.0 N; 3.1 × 10⁻⁴ m³)*
2. A plastic block of density 920 kg/m³ floats in water. What fraction of it is above the surface? *(0.080, or 8%)*
3. A ball of density 400 kg/m³ is held under water and released. What is its acceleration just after release, ignoring drag? *(14.7 m/s² ≈ 15 m/s² upward)*
