## Understanding

- I can state the condition for rotational equilibrium, Στ = 0, and explain that it means constant angular velocity, not necessarily rest. *(Guide: "Newton's first law in rotational form")*
- I can explain why a steadily spinning fan has zero net torque on it. *(Guide: Figure 2)*
- I can state that unbalanced torques always mean the angular velocity is changing, even at an instant when ω = 0. *(Guide: "Unbalanced torques"; Practice Q3)*
- I can give one example of translational equilibrium without rotational equilibrium, and one of the reverse. *(Guide: "The two kinds of equilibrium are independent"; Practice Q2, Q7)*

## Skills

- I can draw a force diagram that puts each force at the point where it acts, with the weight of a uniform object at its centre. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
- I can choose a pivot that removes an unknown force and write Στ = 0 with a stated sign convention. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can use only the perpendicular part of an angled force when finding its torque. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can combine Στ = 0 with ΣF = 0 to find every unknown force on a beam or rod. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can find the condition for an object to start tipping by setting the force at one support to zero. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can derive a symbolic expression for a force in equilibrium and predict how it changes when a distance changes. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can sketch an angular velocity–time graph and mark where the net torque is zero and where it is not. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can linearise balance data and use the slope to support or refute a torque model. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can show that a couple gives the same torque about any point. *(Practice Q7)*
