Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/1-3-representing-motion-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/1-3-representing-motion-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can name five ways to represent a motion and say what each shows most clearly. *(Guide: "One motion, five representations")*
- I can read a motion diagram: gaps for speed, arrows for velocity and acceleration. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can state what the slope and the area of x–t, v_x–t and a_x–t graphs represent. *(Guide: "Graphs: slopes and areas"; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can explain why the three kinematic equations need constant acceleration, and when to split a motion into stages. *(Guide: "Where the constant-acceleration equations come from")*
- I can describe free-fall acceleration near Earth's surface: size, direction and why it stays the same at the top of a throw. *(Guide: "Free fall near Earth's surface"; Practice Q3)*

## Skills

- I can sketch matching x–t, v_x–t and a_x–t graphs from a narrative, with values. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can find displacement from a v_x–t graph and change in velocity from an a_x–t graph, counting area below the axis as negative. *(Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can choose the kinematic equation that leaves out the quantity I do not need, and pick the correct square root. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can solve a vertical throw with a_y = −g, including heights below the launch point. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can derive a symbolic expression, such as a fall time or stopping distance, before putting in numbers. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can use a derived expression to predict how a quantity changes when another is doubled or quadrupled. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
- I can compare two objects in free fall that start with different velocities. *(Practice Q3)*
- I can describe and sketch motion with changing acceleration, and explain why the constant-acceleration equations do not apply. *(Guide: "When acceleration is not constant"; Practice Q7)*
