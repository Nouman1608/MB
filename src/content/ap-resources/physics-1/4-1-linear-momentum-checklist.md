Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/4-1-linear-momentum-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/4-1-linear-momentum-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can state the definition p = mv and give the unit of momentum. *(Guide: "Momentum in everyday speech and in physics")*
- I can explain why momentum always points the same way as velocity. *(Guide: "Momentum is a vector"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain how the physics meaning of momentum differs from its everyday meaning. *(Guide: "Momentum in everyday speech and in physics")*
- I can describe what makes an interaction a collision, and what makes it an explosion. *(Guide: "Collisions and explosions as models"; Practice Q6)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate the momentum of an object with a sign that matches my stated axis. *(Guide: "Momentum is a vector"; Practice Q1)*
- I can find the total momentum of a two-object system by adding signed momenta. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Figure 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can find the components, size and direction of a momentum in two dimensions. *(Guide: "Momentum in two dimensions"; Practice Q4)*
- I can predict the factor by which momentum changes when mass and speed change. *(Guide: "Factors of change"; Practice Q2)*
- I can sketch momentum against velocity and momentum against time, and label key values. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can compare momentum and kinetic energy for two objects and explain why they scale differently with speed. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why a ball that bounces back at the same speed has a different momentum. *(Guide: "Momentum is a vector"; Practice Q4)*
- I can justify why the object model is allowed when analysing a collision. *(Guide: "Collisions and explosions as models"; Practice Q6)*
