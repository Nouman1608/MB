Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, graphs and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/3-3-potential-energy-study-guide/). This is the algebra-based course: no calculus is needed.

## Recap

- **Potential energy (U)** is energy stored in the arrangement of a **system** whose objects interact through **conservative forces** (gravity, ideal springs).
- A **single object** cannot have potential energy. Name the pair: ball–Earth, block–spring.
- A conservative force does the same work on any path between two points. Friction is not conservative, so there is no friction potential energy.
- U is a **scalar** fixed by the **positions** of the objects in the system.
- The **zero** of U is your choice. Pick whatever makes the problem simplest and state it.
- For **three or more** objects, add the potential energy of every pair.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| U_s = ½kΔx² | k in N/m, Δx in m from the relaxed length | find spring energy (never negative) |
| U_g = −Gm₁m₂ / r | r centre to centre in m; zero at infinity | find gravitational energy of two spheres (always negative) |
| ΔU_g = mgΔy | Δy in m, + for upward; g = 9.8 N/kg | find changes near a planet's surface |
| ΔU = −W_conservative | J | link to work by a force inside the system |
| U_total = sum over pairs | J | three or more interacting objects |

**Factors of change:** Δx × 2 → U_s × 4. r × 2 → U_g × ½ (less negative, so larger). Δy × 2 → ΔU_g × 2.

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The spring is **ideal**: it obeys Hooke's law and has negligible mass.
- Planets, moons and stars are treated as **spherical**, so r is measured between centres.
- ΔU_g = mgΔy assumes a **constant field**. It is accurate only when Δy is much smaller than the planet's radius.
- g = 9.8 N/kg at Earth's surface; the course also accepts 10 N/kg where stated.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Giving potential energy to one object.** Write "the ball–Earth system".
2. **Forgetting to square Δx**, or using total spring length instead of the change from relaxed length.
3. **Leaving Δx in centimetres.** Convert to metres first.
4. **Measuring r from the surface** in U_g = −Gm₁m₂/r.
5. **Calling U_g = −5 J "less" than U_g = −10 J.** −5 J is the larger value.
6. **Using mgΔy for satellites.** Use the general form when Δy is comparable with the radius.
7. **Missing a pair** in a three-object system: A, B and C have three pairs.

## Quick self-check

1. A spring with k = 400 N/m is stretched 5.0 cm. How much energy does it store? *(0.50 J)*
2. A 2.0 kg box is lifted 3.0 m. What is ΔU_g for the box–Earth system? *(+59 J)*
3. Two asteroids move to three times their original separation. What happens to U_g? *(It becomes one third of the original value: still negative, but larger.)*
4. Can the ball–Earth system have negative potential energy near the ground? *(Yes, if the ball is below the chosen zero.)*
