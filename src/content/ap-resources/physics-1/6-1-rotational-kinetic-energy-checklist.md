## Understanding

- I can explain why a wheel spinning on a fixed axle has kinetic energy even though its centre of mass is at rest. *(Guide: "A spinning wheel has kinetic energy"; Worked example 3)*
- I can show, by adding ½mv² for each piece with v = rω, that the kinetic energy of a rotating rigid system is ½Iω². *(Guide: "From ½mv² to ½Iω²"; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why mass far from the axis carries more kinetic energy than the same mass near it. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2, Q4)*
- I can explain why rotational kinetic energy is a scalar that is never negative, and add the energies of objects spinning in opposite directions. *(Guide: "Rotational kinetic energy is a scalar"; Practice Q3)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate rotational kinetic energy for a few point objects, or for an object with a given rotational inertia. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can convert rev/min to rad/s before using K = ½Iω². *(Guide: "Units: always rad/s"; Practice Q5)*
- I can find the total kinetic energy of an object that moves and spins as ½Mv_cm² + ½I_cm ω², and show it on an energy bar chart. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Figure 2)*
- I can predict the factor by which K changes when ω or I changes, including hoop against disc. *(Guide: factors-of-change table; Practice Q1, Q2, Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can find the kinetic energy of an object on a pivot either from ½I_pivot ω² or from ½Mv_cm² + ½I_cm ω², and explain why mixing the two double counts. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6)*
- I can choose a graph of spin data that should be a straight line, find I from its slope, and use the data to support or refute a claim about K and ω. *(Practice Q7)*
