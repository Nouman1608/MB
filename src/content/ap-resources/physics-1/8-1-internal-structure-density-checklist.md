## Understanding

- I can explain the shape, volume and compressibility of solids, liquids and gases using how strongly their particles interact and how far apart they are. *(Guide: "Why solids, liquids and gases behave differently", Figure 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can say what makes a substance a fluid and explain why air counts as one. *(Guide: "What counts as a fluid"; Practice Q2, Q7)*
- I can use the words mass, volume, weight and density correctly, each with its unit. *(Guide: vocabulary table; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why cutting a uniform object does not change its density. *(Guide: "Density: mass per unit volume"; Practice Q3)*
- I can state the two properties of an ideal fluid and say when water, honey or air fails to fit the model. *(Guide: "The ideal-fluid model"; Practice Q7)*

## Skills

- I can calculate density, mass or volume from ρ = m / V, including the volume of a box or cylinder. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can convert densities between g/cm³ and kg/m³, and volumes between mL, L and m³. *(Guide: "Converting units"; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can find the average density of an object made of several parts by adding masses and adding volumes. *(Guide: Worked example 3)*
- I can plot mass against volume, draw a best-fit line and use its slope to find a density. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Figure 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can describe how to measure the volume of an irregular object by water displacement. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*

## Reasoning

- I can compare the densities of two objects using ratios of mass and volume. *(Practice Q3)*
- I can design a density experiment where an unknown container mass cancels out, and explain what the graph's intercept means. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can evaluate a claim that a fluid's density cannot change, using the particle picture of gases and liquids. *(Practice Q7)*
