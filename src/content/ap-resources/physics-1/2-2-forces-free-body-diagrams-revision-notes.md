Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, diagrams and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-2-forces-free-body-diagrams-study-guide/). This is the algebra-based course: no calculus is needed.

## Recap

- A **force** is an interaction: a push or pull **on** one object **by** another. It is a vector, measured in newtons (N).
- An object or system **cannot exert a net force on itself**. Only external interactions change the motion of a system as a whole.
- **Contact forces** (normal, friction, tension, applied) are the large-scale result of **electric** forces between atoms at the touching surfaces. **Long-range** forces such as gravity need no contact.
- A **free-body diagram** shows the forces exerted **on** the chosen object or system **by** its environment, drawn from a dot at its center of mass.
- Choose axes with one axis **along the acceleration**. On a ramp, that means axes parallel and perpendicular to the surface.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| F_g = mg | m in kg, g = 9.8 N/kg, F_g in N | find the size of the gravitational force near Earth |
| F_g along a ramp = mg sin θ | θ = ramp angle | find gravity's down-slope component |
| F_g into a ramp = mg cos θ | θ = ramp angle | find gravity's component into the surface |
| F_x = F cos θ, F_y = F sin θ | θ measured from the +x axis | split a force at an angle (in a separate sketch) |

| Force | Direction |
|---|---|
| Gravitational, F_g | straight down (towards Earth's centre) |
| Normal, F_N | perpendicular to the surface, away from it |
| Friction, F_f | along the surface, opposing sliding or the tendency to slide |
| Tension, F_T | along the string, pulling away from the object |

## Diagram conventions

- One **dot** for the object or system (its center of mass).
- One **straight arrow per force**, starting on the dot, pointing the way the force acts, and labelled.
- Same-direction forces **side by side**, never overlapping.
- **No components**, no velocity, no acceleration and no net-force arrow on the diagram.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **A "force of motion"** along the velocity. Nothing exerts it.
2. **Forces the object exerts** on other things. They belong on the other objects' diagrams.
3. **F_N = mg every time.** Not on a ramp, and not when something else pulls up or pushes down.
4. **Gravity drawn perpendicular to a ramp.** Gravity is always vertical.
5. **Internal forces** drawn on a system's diagram.
6. **Sine and cosine swapped** on a ramp. Check with θ = 0: the down-slope part must vanish.

## Quick self-check

1. What is the size of the gravitational force on a 5.0 kg bag? *(49 N, downward)*
2. How many forces act on a stone in flight after it is thrown, with air resistance negligible? *(One: gravity, Earth on stone.)*
3. A 10 kg box sits on a 30° ramp. Find gravity's components along and into the ramp. *(49 N down the slope; 85 N into the surface)*
4. Is the normal force on a book resting on a level desk a contact or a long-range force, and what causes it at the atomic scale? *(Contact; electric repulsion between atoms of the two surfaces.)*
