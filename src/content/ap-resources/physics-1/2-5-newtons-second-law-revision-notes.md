Short on time? This page is the recap. For diagrams, derivations and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-5-newtons-second-law-study-guide/). This is the algebra-based course: no calculus is needed.

## Recap

- **Unbalanced forces:** the net force on the system is not zero. The system’s velocity then changes.
- **Newton’s second law:** the centre-of-mass acceleration has size proportional to the net external force and points the same way.
- Only **external** forces count. Internal forces come in third-law pairs and cancel, so they cannot change the centre-of-mass velocity.
- Forces can balance along one axis and not along another. Velocity changes only along the unbalanced axis.
- Choose the system first. The whole system gives the acceleration; a single part gives an internal force such as a tension.

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The frame of reference is **inertial** (not accelerating) unless stated.
- Strings are **light** and do not stretch; pulleys are light and frictionless. Then the tension is the same all along a string, and joined objects share the same size of acceleration.
- Near Earth’s surface, F_g = mg with g = 9.8 m/s² (10 m/s² where stated).
- Air resistance is negligible unless stated.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Using one force instead of the net force.** Add every external force with signs first.
2. **Drawing ma on the free-body diagram.** It is not a force.
3. **Assuming acceleration is along the velocity.** It is along the net force.
4. **Counting internal forces.** For a whole system, string tensions between its parts cancel.
5. **Setting tension equal to the hanging weight** when the hanging object accelerates.
6. **Using weight in newtons as the mass.** m goes in kg.

## Quick self-check

1. A net force of 15 N acts on a 3.0 kg block. What is its acceleration? *(5.0 m/s², in the direction of the net force)*
2. The same net force acts on a block with three times the mass. By what factor does the acceleration change? *(It becomes one third.)*
3. A 1200 kg car accelerates at 2.5 m/s². What net force acts on it? *(3.0 × 10³ N, forward)*
