## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between periodic motion and simple harmonic motion, with one example of each. *(Guide: "SHM compared with other periodic motion"; Practice Q1, Q7)*
- I can define the equilibrium position as the place where the net force is zero, and locate it for a horizontal spring, a vertical spring and a pendulum. *(Guide: "Equilibrium position and restoring force")*
- I can explain what a restoring force is and why its direction depends on position, not on velocity. *(Guide: "Equilibrium position and restoring force"; Practice Q2)*
- I can state the SHM condition: the restoring force is proportional to the displacement from equilibrium. *(Guide: "The condition for SHM"; Practice Q1)*

## Skills

- I can derive a = −(k/m)x from Hooke's law and Newton's second law, and use it to find an acceleration with its direction. *(Guide: "The condition for SHM"; Practice Q2, Q3)*
- I can sketch F_net against x for an SHM system and for systems that are not SHM. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can test force data for SHM by checking whether F/x is constant. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can find the equilibrium stretch of a vertical spring, d = mg/k, and the net force about it. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate the restoring torque on a pendulum and compare mgℓ sin θ with mgℓθ, using radians. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can show that a vertical spring–mass system is SHM because F_net = −ky when y is measured from equilibrium. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can justify, with numbers, why a pendulum is close to SHM only at small angles. *(Guide: "The pendulum: a restoring torque"; Practice Q6)*
- I can evaluate a claim that a repeating motion is SHM by looking at how the force depends on displacement. *(Practice Q5, Q7)*
