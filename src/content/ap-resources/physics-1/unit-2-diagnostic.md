This diagnostic is for the **algebra-based Physics 1 course**, Unit 2 (Force and Translational Dynamics). It shows you **which topics to revisit**. Allow about 30 minutes, then use the table at the end.

These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. They have not been calibrated against real exam results, so your total does not predict an exam score. Use a scientific calculator in degree mode and g = 9.8 m/s² (9.8 N/kg). Ignore air resistance, and treat strings and springs as ideal.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · 2.1)

Three small weights are glued to a light right-angled bracket. Take the origin at the corner, **+x to the right and +y up**. The weights are 2.0 kg at (0, 0), 1.0 kg at (0.90 m, 0) and 3.0 kg at (0, 0.60 m). Where is the center of mass?

- (A) (0.30 m, 0.60 m)
- (B) (0.30 m, 0.20 m)
- (C) (0.15 m, 0.30 m)
- (D) (0.45 m, 0.10 m)

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** Total mass 6.0 kg. x_cm = (1.0 × 0.90) ÷ 6.0 = 0.15 m. y_cm = (3.0 × 0.60) ÷ 6.0 = 0.30 m.

- (A) divides each weighted sum by 3, the number of weights, instead of 6.0 kg, the total mass.
- (B) is the geometric centre of the triangle. It ignores the masses.
- (D) swaps the positions of the 1.0 kg and 3.0 kg weights.

**If you missed this:** read "The equation" and Worked example 2 in the [Topic 2.1 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-1-systems-center-mass-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · 2.2)

A ball hangs at rest from a string tied to a hook on a smooth vertical wall. The ball rests against the wall, so the string is tilted. Which set of arrows belongs on the ball's free-body diagram?

- (A) Gravity; separate vertical and horizontal arrows for the tension; normal force
- (B) Gravity; tension; the push of the ball on the wall, towards the wall
- (C) Gravity; tension; normal force; friction (wall on ball), up
- (D) Gravity (Earth on ball), down; tension (string on ball), towards the hook; normal force (wall on ball), away from the wall

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (D).** Earth, the string and the wall each exert one force, drawn as one straight arrow from the dot.

- (A) draws components. Only whole forces go on the diagram.
- (B) includes a force the ball exerts. It acts on the wall.
- (C) adds friction, but the wall is smooth.

**If you missed this:** read "What a free-body diagram shows" in the [Topic 2.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-2-forces-free-body-diagrams-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · 2.3)

A camera drone hovers at a fixed point. Its rotors push air downward. Which is a Newton's third-law pair?

- (A) Air on drone (up) and Earth on drone (gravity, down)
- (B) Drone on air (down) and air on drone (up)
- (C) Earth on drone (gravity, down) and drone on air (down)
- (D) Air on drone (up) and drone on Earth (gravity, up)

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** Swap the objects and keep the type: "drone on air" becomes "air on drone". Same type, opposite directions, different objects.

- (A) is the trap. These forces are equal only because the drone is in equilibrium. Both act on the drone, and they are different types.
- (C) and (D) each mix two different interactions (drone–Earth and drone–air). The partner of Earth's pull on the drone is the drone's pull on Earth.

**If you missed this:** read "How to find a third-law pair" in the [Topic 2.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-3-newtons-third-law-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · 2.4)

A 50 N shop sign hangs at rest from two light cords. The left cord rises at 30° above the horizontal; the right cord at 60°. What are the tensions?

- (A) Left (30°) cord 25 N, right (60°) cord 43 N
- (B) Left (30°) cord 43 N, right (60°) cord 25 N
- (C) 25 N in each cord
- (D) 50 N in each cord

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** At rest, so ΣF_x = 0 and ΣF_y = 0. Horizontal: T_L cos 30° = T_R cos 60°. Vertical: T_L sin 30° + T_R sin 60° = 50 N. Solving gives T_L = 25 N and T_R = 43 N. Check: 12.5 + 37.5 = 50 N vertically.

- (B) swaps the values. The steeper cord, closer to vertical, does more of the lifting.
- (C) shares the weight equally, which ignores the angles.
- (D) makes each cord hold the whole weight.

**If you missed this:** read "Translational equilibrium" and Worked example 2 in the [Topic 2.4 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-4-newtons-first-law-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 5 (multiple choice · 2.5)

Take **+x east and +y north**, looking down on frictionless ice. Two horizontal forces act on a 2.0 kg puck: 6.0 N east and 8.0 N north. What is the puck's acceleration?

- (A) 7.0 m/s² at 53° north of east
- (B) 5.0 m/s² at 53° north of east
- (C) 3.0 m/s² east
- (D) 4.0 m/s² north

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** ΣF = √(6.0² + 8.0²) = 10 N at tan⁻¹(8.0 ÷ 6.0) = 53° north of east. a = 10 ÷ 2.0 = 5.0 m/s², along the net force.

- (A) adds the sizes. Perpendicular forces combine by Pythagoras.
- (C) and (D) each use one force only.

**If you missed this:** read "Newton’s second law" in the [Topic 2.5 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-5-newtons-second-law-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 6 (multiple choice · 2.6)

At what height **above Earth's surface** is the gravitational field strength one ninth of its value at the surface? R is Earth's radius.

- (A) 8R
- (B) 3R
- (C) 2R
- (D) 9R

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** g = GM ÷ r², with r from Earth's **centre**. One ninth needs r = 3R, which is 2R above the surface.

- (A) forgets to square r (so r = 9R), then subtracts R.
- (B) is the distance from the centre, not the height.
- (D) forgets to square r and measures from the centre.

**If you missed this:** read "The gravitational field" and Figure 2 in the [Topic 2.6 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-6-gravitational-force-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 7 (multiple choice · 2.7)

Take **+x in the direction of the pull**. A 4.0 kg block rests on a level table. For the block and table, μ_s = 0.50 and μ_k = 0.30. A horizontal pull of 15 N is applied. What is the block's acceleration?

- (A) −1.2 m/s²
- (B) +0.81 m/s²
- (C) +3.8 m/s²
- (D) 0

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (D).** Static friction can supply up to μ_s mg = 0.50 × 39.2 = 19.6 N. The 15 N pull is less, so the block stays at rest, with 15 N of static friction in the −x direction.

- (A) treats μ_s F_n as the actual friction. It is only a maximum.
- (B) uses kinetic friction (11.8 N), which acts only once the surfaces slide.
- (C) ignores friction.

**If you missed this:** read "Static friction" and Worked example 1 in the [Topic 2.7 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-7-kinetic-static-friction-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 8 (multiple choice · 2.8)

A 1.2 kg mass hangs at rest from **two identical springs** side by side. Each spring is stretched 3.0 cm from its relaxed length. What is the spring constant of **one** spring?

- (A) 2.0 × 10² N/m
- (B) 3.9 × 10² N/m
- (C) 2.0 N/m
- (D) 20 N/m

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** At rest, 2kΔx = mg, so k = (1.2 × 9.8) ÷ (2 × 0.030) = 196 N/m.

- (B) lets one spring hold the whole 11.8 N weight.
- (C) leaves the stretch in centimetres.
- (D) uses the mass (1.2) instead of the weight (11.8 N).

**If you missed this:** read "Hooke's law" and "Comparing springs and situations" in the [Topic 2.8 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-8-spring-forces-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 9 (multiple choice · 2.9)

A car turns on a flat, level road along a curve of radius 40 m. For the tyres and road, μ_s = 0.80. What is the greatest speed at which the car can follow the curve without skidding?

- (A) 20 m/s
- (B) 18 m/s
- (C) 22 m/s
- (D) 5.7 m/s

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** On a flat road, static friction supplies the whole inward force. At the limit, μ_s mg = mv² ÷ r, so v = √(μ_s g r) = √(0.80 × 9.8 × 40) = 18 m/s. The mass cancels.

- (A) is √(gr), which leaves out μ_s.
- (C) divides by μ_s instead of multiplying.
- (D) leaves out g.

**If you missed this:** read "Where does the inward force come from?" in the [Topic 2.9 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-9-circular-motion-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 10 (short answer · 2.5)

Take **+y up**. A student pulls up with 60 N on a 2.0 kg block. A light string hangs from it to a 3.0 kg block below.

(a) Find the acceleration of the blocks.
(b) Find the tension in the string.
(c) Another student says the tension must be 3.0 × 9.8 = 29 N, the lower block's weight. Explain the mistake.

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**(a)** System: both blocks (string forces internal). ΣF_y = 60 − 5.0 × 9.8 = 11 N, so a_y = **+2.2 m/s²**.

**(b)** System: the lower block. T − m₂g = m₂a_y, so T = 3.0(9.8 + 2.2) = **36 N**. Check on the top block: 60 − 19.6 − 36 = 4.4 N = 2.0 × 2.2. ✓

**(c)** T = m₂g only if the lower block does not accelerate. It speeds up upward, so the string pulls with more than its weight; the extra 6.6 N gives it 2.2 m/s².

Check yourself: 1 point each for 2.2 m/s² from external forces only, choosing the lower block alone, 36 N, and linking (c) to the acceleration.

**If you missed this:** read "Choosing the system: external and internal forces" and Worked example 2 in the [Topic 2.5 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-5-newtons-second-law-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 11 (short answer · 2.9)

A 30 kg child on a swing moves on a circle of radius 2.5 m (treat the two ropes as one). At the lowest point the child moves at 3.0 m/s.

(a) Find the child's acceleration at the lowest point, with its direction.
(b) Find the tension in the rope there.
(c) A parent says the tension there equals the child's weight, because the child is neither speeding up nor slowing down. Evaluate this claim.

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**(a)** a_c = v² ÷ r = 3.0² ÷ 2.5 = **3.6 m/s²**, upward, towards the centre of the circle.

**(b)** Take "towards the centre" (up) as positive. F_T − mg = ma_c, so F_T = 30(9.8 + 3.6) = **4.0 × 10² N** (402 N). The weight is 294 N.

**(c)** **Incorrect.** The speed is not changing, but the direction is. The acceleration points up, so the net force points up and the tension exceeds the weight by ma_c = 108 N.

Check yourself: 1 point each for 3.6 m/s² towards the centre, tension and weight opposite in the equation, 402 N, and rejecting the claim because of a_c.

**If you missed this:** read "Vertical loops and the minimum speed at the top" (the paragraph on the bottom of the loop) in the [Topic 2.9 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-9-circular-motion-study-guide/).
</details>
