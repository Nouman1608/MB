Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-8-spring-forces-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-8-spring-forces-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can state what makes a spring "ideal" and what the model ignores. *(Guide: "The ideal spring")*
- I can explain the difference between the length of a spring and its change in length, and measure Δx from the relaxed length. *(Guide: "The ideal spring"; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can explain what the spring constant k measures and which of two springs is stiffer. *(Guide: "Hooke's law"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain what the minus sign in F_s = −kΔx tells me about direction. *(Guide: "Hooke's law"; FAQ)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate the size and direction of a spring force, converting centimetres to metres. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q1)*
- I can derive an expression for the stretch of a spring holding an object at rest, vertically or on a smooth slope. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can plot force against change in length, draw a best-fit line and find k from its slope. *(Guide: Worked example 1 and Figure 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can find the relaxed length and k from a graph of total length against force. *(Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why the spring force on a block points back toward the relaxed position whichever way the block is moving. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can use data to decide whether an object obeys Hooke's law, giving a reason. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can design an experiment to measure k and say how to make the result reliable. *(Guide: "Designing the experiment well"; Practice Q6)*
- I can combine a spring force with static friction to decide whether an object stays at rest. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can check an expression with limiting cases, such as a slope angle of 0° or 90°. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
