## Understanding

- I can name the system whose energy I am tracking: spring–object, or pendulum bob–Earth. *(Guide: "The system and its energy")*
- I can explain why the total energy of an ideal oscillator stays constant. *(Guide: "The system and its energy")*
- I can say where kinetic energy is zero and where potential energy is largest, and explain why. *(Guide: "Trading energy back and forth"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why changing the amplitude changes the total energy but not the period. *(Guide: "Changing the amplitude"; Practice Q2)*

## Calculation

- I can use E = ½kA² to find the total energy or the amplitude. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 3; Practice Q4)*
- I can find the maximum speed of a spring oscillator or a pendulum bob from energy. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q4, Q7)*
- I can find the speed at any displacement using ½mv² + ½kx² = ½kA². *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can find the kinetic and potential energy fractions at a given displacement, such as x = A/2. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q3)*

## Reasoning and representations

- I can read k, A, U and K from an energy–position graph and sketch the K curve. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can draw energy bar charts for an oscillator at different positions. *(Guide: "Energy bar charts"; Practice Q7)*
- I can describe how K and U change with time over one period. *(Guide: Figure 2)*
- I can use measured speeds and amplitudes to find k and test a claim about energy. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why a heavier pendulum bob is no faster at the bottom. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*
