Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/4-2-change-momentum-impulse-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/4-2-change-momentum-impulse-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can state that the net external force on a system equals its rate of change of momentum. *(Guide: "Force is the rate of change of momentum")*
- I can define impulse, give its unit, and show that 1 N·s = 1 kg·m/s. *(Guide: "Impulse"; Practice Q1)*
- I can state the impulse–momentum theorem and explain that impulse points the same way as the net force. *(Guide: "The impulse–momentum theorem")*
- I can show that F_net = ma follows from the theorem when mass is constant, and say why changing-mass systems are only described in words. *(Guide: "Newton's second law as a special case")*

## Calculation

- I can calculate a change in momentum with signed velocities, including a rebound. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can find an impulse from the area under a force–time graph, using rectangles and triangles. *(Guide: Figure 1, Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can find the net force from the slope of a momentum–time graph. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can find the force from a surface during an impact by adding the weight to the net force. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4, Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain, with J = F_avg Δt = Δp, why a longer stopping time reduces the average force. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3)*
- I can predict how the average force changes when the drop height or the stopping time changes. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can plan an experiment with force and motion sensors that tests the impulse–momentum theorem. *(Guide: "Testing the theorem in the lab"; Practice Q6)*
- I can linearise impulse and velocity data, find a mass from the slope, and use the graph to support or refute the theorem. *(Practice Q6)*
