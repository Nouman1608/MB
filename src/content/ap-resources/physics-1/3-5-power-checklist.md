Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/3-5-power-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/3-5-power-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain power as the rate of energy transfer into or out of a system, or of conversion inside it, and say which applies once I have chosen the system. *(Guide: "Power is a rate"; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain the difference between energy and power, and why a kilowatt-hour is an energy unit. *(Guide: "Average power"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why a force at right angles to the velocity has zero power and a force against the velocity has negative power. *(Guide: "Instantaneous power"; Worked example 2)*

## Skills

- I can calculate average power from an energy change or from the work done, with time in seconds. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can use P = Fv cos θ with only the force component along the velocity. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2)*
- I can find power from the slope of an energy–time graph and energy from the area under a power–time graph. *(Guide: Figures 1 and 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can find the top speed of a vehicle, or its speed on a hill, from its power and the forces it must balance. *(Guide: "Why a vehicle has a top speed"; Practice Q5)*
- I can plan a power measurement, process the data and choose a graph with sensible axes and scales. *(Guide: "Measuring power in the lab"; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can derive P = F∥v from the definition of work and use it to explain why constant force can give rising power. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7)*
- I can derive a symbolic expression for average power and predict how it changes when one quantity changes. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can account for every watt of input power by tracking where the energy goes. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can use experimental data to support or challenge a claim about power, quoting values as evidence. *(Practice Q6)*
