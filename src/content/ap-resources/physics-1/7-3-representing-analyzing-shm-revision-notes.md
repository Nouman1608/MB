## Recap

- Measure **displacement x from equilibrium**. The largest displacement is the **amplitude A**.
- Released from rest at x = +A: use **x = A cos(2πft)**. Passing through equilibrium moving +x: use **x = A sin(2πft)**.
- The angle 2πft is in **radians**.
- **Velocity** is the slope of the x–t graph: zero at peaks and troughs, steepest at x = 0.
- **Acceleration** comes from Newton's second law with the restoring force: it is opposite to x and proportional to it.
- **Amplitude does not change the period.** Twice the amplitude means twice the force, twice the acceleration and twice the top speed, so the longer path takes the same time.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| x = A cos(2πft) | x, A in m; f in Hz; t in s | position when released from +A at t = 0 |
| x = A sin(2πft) | as above | position when passing x = 0 in +x at t = 0 |
| T = 1/f | s, Hz | switch between period and frequency |
| a_x = −(k/m)x | m/s² | acceleration of a spring–object system at any x |
| a_max = kA/m | m/s², at x = ±A | largest acceleration |
| slope of a–x graph = −k/m | s⁻² | test for SHM and find k/m |

## Where things are zero or largest

| Position | x | Speed | a_x |
|---|---|---|---|
| x = +A | max | 0 | most negative |
| x = 0 | 0 | max | 0 |
| x = −A | min | 0 | most positive |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The restoring force is proportional to displacement (ideal spring, or a pendulum at a **small angle**).
- No friction or air resistance, so the amplitude stays constant.
- x is measured from **equilibrium**. For a hanging spring, that is where the object hangs at rest, not the spring's unstretched end.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Calculator in **degrees**.
2. Saying a = 0 at the turning point because v = 0.
3. Putting the largest acceleration at equilibrium.
4. Reading T from peak to trough (that is T/2).
5. Assuming a bigger amplitude gives a longer period.
6. Using cos when the motion starts at equilibrium.

## Quick self-check

1. x = (0.050 m) cos(2π × 2.0 Hz × t). Where is the object at t = 0.125 s? *(At x = 0: the angle is π/2, a quarter cycle.)*
2. A 0.50 kg object on a 20 N/m spring is at x = +0.030 m. What is a_x? *(−1.2 m/s²)*
3. The amplitude of a spring–object system is tripled. What happens to the period? *(Nothing: it stays the same.)*
