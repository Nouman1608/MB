This diagnostic is for the **algebra-based Physics 1 course**, Unit 5 (Torque and Rotational Dynamics). It shows you **which topics to revisit**. Allow about 30 minutes, then use the table at the end.

These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. They have not been calibrated against real exam results, so your total does not predict an exam score. Use a scientific calculator, g = 9.8 m/s² and 1 rev = 2π rad. Rotation senses are clockwise or counterclockwise about a stated axis. Treat strings as light and axles as frictionless unless told otherwise.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · 5.1)

Take **counterclockwise as positive**. At t = 0 an exercise-bike flywheel turns clockwise at 8.0 rad/s. A motor gives it a constant angular acceleration of +2.0 rad/s². What is its angular displacement from t = 0 to t = 6.0 s?

- (A) −12 rad
- (B) −84 rad
- (C) +12 rad
- (D) +20 rad

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** With ω₀ = −8.0 rad/s, Δθ = ω₀t + ½αt² = −48 + 36 = −12 rad. Check by stages: the wheel stops at 4.0 s after turning 16 rad clockwise, then turns 4.0 rad counterclockwise, so the net is −12 rad.

- (B) subtracts the ½αt² term. Substitute the signed α and let the signs do the work.
- (C) has the right size but loses the sign of ω₀.
- (D) is the **total** angle turned (16 + 4.0). Displacement counts the reverse turn as negative.

**If you missed this:** read "Speeding up or slowing down?" and Worked example 2 in the [Topic 5.1 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/5-1-rotational-kinematics-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · 5.2)

A grinding wheel has radius 0.10 m. For safety, points on its rim must not move faster than 30 m/s. What is the greatest rotation rate allowed?

- (A) 3.0 × 10² rpm
- (B) 48 rpm
- (C) 2.9 × 10³ rpm
- (D) 1.8 × 10⁴ rpm

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** ω = v ÷ r = 30 ÷ 0.10 = 300 rad/s. In rpm: 300 × 60 ÷ 2π = 2865 rpm, about 2.9 × 10³ rpm.

- (A) is the value in rad/s with the wrong unit.
- (B) is 47.7 rev/s, labelled as revolutions per minute.
- (D) multiplies by 60 but does not divide by 2π rad per revolution.

**If you missed this:** read "Linear speed and tangential acceleration" in the [Topic 5.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/5-2-connecting-linear-rotational-motion-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · 5.3)

A vertical mast stands on a hinge at ground level, which is the axis. A rope is tied to the mast 3.0 m above the hinge and runs down to a stake in the ground. The rope makes 30° with the mast and pulls with a tension of 200 N. What is the size of the rope's torque about the hinge?

- (A) 600 N·m
- (B) 520 N·m
- (C) 300 N·m
- (D) 0 N·m

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** τ = rF sin θ = 3.0 × 200 × sin 30° = 300 N·m. Equivalently, the lever arm from the hinge to the rope's line of action is 3.0 sin 30° = 1.5 m.

- (A) leaves out sin θ, as if the rope pulled at right angles to the mast.
- (B) uses cos 30°. The angle is already between r and F, so use sin.
- (D) would be right only if the rope's line of action passed through the hinge. It passes 1.5 m away.

**If you missed this:** read "Two other ways to read the same equation" in the [Topic 5.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/5-3-torque-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · 5.3)

A uniform rod hangs from a pivot at its top end. It is pulled aside and held at 30° from the vertical, then at 60° from the vertical. By what factor does the torque of gravity about the pivot change?

- (A) 2.0
- (B) 1.7
- (C) 0.58
- (D) 1.0

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** The weight acts at the centre, L/2 from the pivot, and the angle between r and the downward weight is the angle from the vertical. So τ = Mg(L/2) sin φ, and the factor is sin 60° ÷ sin 30° = 1.7.

- (A) treats the torque as proportional to the angle itself.
- (C) uses cos φ, which measures the vertical distance, not the lever arm.
- (D) notices that r and F are unchanged but forgets the angle.

**If you missed this:** read "Predicting changes: functional dependence" and Worked example 3 in the [Topic 5.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/5-3-torque-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 5 (multiple choice · 5.4)

Three small masses are fixed to a light flat frame: 2.0 kg at (0, 0), 1.0 kg at (0.30 m, 0) and 1.0 kg at (0, 0.40 m). What is the rotational inertia about an axis **perpendicular to the frame through the 1.0 kg mass at (0.30 m, 0)**?

- (A) 0.25 kg·m²
- (B) 0.43 kg·m²
- (C) 0.27 kg·m²
- (D) 1.1 kg·m²

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** Measure every r from the axis. The 2.0 kg mass is 0.30 m away. The other 1.0 kg mass is √(0.30² + 0.40²) = 0.50 m away. The mass on the axis adds nothing. I = 2.0 × 0.30² + 1.0 × 0.50² = 0.18 + 0.25 = 0.43 kg·m².

- (A) measures from the origin, a different axis.
- (C) uses only the horizontal distance (0.30 m) for the top mass.
- (D) forgets to square the distances.

**If you missed this:** read "Several objects: add them up" in the [Topic 5.4 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/5-4-rotational-inertia-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 6 (multiple choice · 5.5)

A hanging mobile is a uniform rod of mass 0.30 kg and length 0.90 m. A 0.20 kg ornament hangs from the left end and a 0.40 kg ornament from the right end. How far from the **left** end must the supporting string be tied for the rod to hang level?

- (A) 0.45 m
- (B) 0.60 m
- (C) 0.35 m
- (D) 0.55 m

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (D).** Take torques about the string, distance x from the left end. Counterclockwise: 0.20g·x + 0.30g(x − 0.45). Clockwise: 0.40g(0.90 − x). Setting them equal gives 0.90x = 0.495, so x = 0.55 m.

- (A) is the midpoint. It ignores the unequal ornaments.
- (B) forgets the rod's own weight, acting at its centre.
- (C) is the right distance measured from the wrong end.

**If you missed this:** read "Choosing the pivot" and Worked example 1 in the [Topic 5.5 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/5-5-rotational-equilibrium-newtons-first-law-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 7 (multiple choice · 5.6)

A stepped wheel turns on a fixed axle; I = 0.50 kg·m². A rope wound on the outer step (radius 0.30 m) pulls with 20 N, giving a clockwise torque. A rope wound on the inner step (radius 0.10 m) pulls with 30 N, giving a counterclockwise torque. What is the wheel's angular acceleration?

- (A) 6.0 rad/s² clockwise
- (B) 18 rad/s² clockwise
- (C) 6.0 rad/s² counterclockwise
- (D) Zero, because the axle force balances the rope forces

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Clockwise torque 0.30 × 20 = 6.0 N·m; counterclockwise 0.10 × 30 = 3.0 N·m. Net 3.0 N·m clockwise, so α = 3.0 ÷ 0.50 = 6.0 rad/s² clockwise.

- (B) adds the torques instead of letting them oppose.
- (C) lets the larger force decide the sense. Torque, not force, decides.
- (D) mixes the linear and rotational analyses. The axle force acts at the axis, so it exerts no torque.

**If you missed this:** read "Linear and rotational analyses are separate" in the [Topic 5.6 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/5-6-newtons-second-law-rotational-form-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 8 (short answer · 5.5)

A wheelbarrow is held at rest with its legs just off the ground. Measure all distances **horizontally from the wheel's axle**. The 12 kg barrow has its centre of mass 0.50 m behind the axle. It carries a 60 kg load whose centre of mass is 0.40 m behind the axle. The gardener lifts straight up on the handles, 1.4 m behind the axle.

(a) Find the lifting force.
(b) Find the upward force of the ground on the wheel.
(c) The gardener says: "If I move the load to 0.20 m behind the axle, half as far, I will need half the lifting force." Evaluate this claim.

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**(a)** Take torques about the axle, so the ground's force drops out. Clockwise (weights): 12 × 9.8 × 0.50 + 60 × 9.8 × 0.40 = 58.8 + 235.2 = 294 N·m. Counterclockwise (lift): F × 1.4. So F = 294 ÷ 1.4 = **210 N**.

**(b)** ΣF_y = 0: N + 210 = 72 × 9.8 = 705.6 N, so N = **5.0 × 10² N** (496 N).

**(c)** **Incorrect.** Only the load's torque halves, to 117.6 N·m. The barrow's 58.8 N·m stays. F = 176.4 ÷ 1.4 = **126 N**, which is 0.60 of 210 N, not half (105 N).

Check yourself: 1 point each for choosing the axle as pivot, 210 N, 496 N from the force balance, and rejecting the claim because the barrow's own torque does not change.

**If you missed this:** read "Choosing the pivot" and Worked example 1 in the [Topic 5.5 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/5-5-rotational-equilibrium-newtons-first-law-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 9 (short answer · 5.6)

A playground roundabout has rotational inertia 180 kg·m² (given) and radius 1.5 m. Its bearing exerts a constant friction torque of 30 N·m. Starting from rest, a parent pushes tangentially on the rim with a constant 80 N.

(a) Find the net torque and the angular acceleration.
(b) Find how long it takes to reach 2.0 rad/s.
(c) A 20 kg child now sits on the rim (treat the child as a small mass). With the same push and friction, find the new time to reach 2.0 rad/s from rest.

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**(a)** Push: 1.5 × 80 = 120 N·m. Friction opposes it, so τ_net = 120 − 30 = **90 N·m**. α = 90 ÷ 180 = **0.50 rad/s²**.

**(b)** From rest, ω = αt, so t = 2.0 ÷ 0.50 = **4.0 s**.

**(c)** I = 180 + 20 × 1.5² = 225 kg·m². α = 90 ÷ 225 = 0.40 rad/s², so t = **5.0 s**.

Check yourself: 1 point each for subtracting the friction torque, 0.50 rad/s², 4.0 s, adding mr² for the child, and 5.0 s.

**If you missed this:** read "Using functional dependence" and Worked example 1 in the [Topic 5.6 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/5-6-newtons-second-law-rotational-form-study-guide/).
</details>
