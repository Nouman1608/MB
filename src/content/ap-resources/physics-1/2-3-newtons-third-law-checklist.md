Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-3-newtons-third-law-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-3-newtons-third-law-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can state Newton's third law as F_(A on B) = −F_(B on A) and list the five features of a force pair. *(Guide: "Every force is half of an interaction")*
- I can explain why the two forces in a pair never cancel each other. *(Guide: "Why the pair never cancels"; Practice Q5)*
- I can say which force in this course acts between objects that do not touch, and that it also obeys the third law. *(Guide: "Which forces act at a distance?")*
- I can explain what makes a force internal or external, and how that depends on the system I choose. *(Guide: "Internal and external forces"; Practice Q6)*
- I can describe tension as the pull between neighbouring pieces of a string, and define an ideal string and an ideal pulley. *(Guide: "Tension: the string pulls both ways")*

## Skills

- I can name the partner of any force by swapping the two objects and keeping the type. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can draw free-body diagrams for two interacting objects, with each pair split across the two diagrams and same-direction forces drawn side by side. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can find the tension in an ideal rope pulled from both ends or tied to a wall. *(Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can use the centre of mass to predict where two objects that pull on each other will meet. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can compare the forces, and the changes in velocity, when objects of very different mass interact. *(Guide: "Predicting with the third law"; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can explain how a tractor and trailer speed up even though the trailer pulls back on the rope as hard as the rope pulls it. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can explain, in words, why tension varies along a rope or chain with mass but not along an ideal string. *(Guide: "When tension is not the same everywhere"; Practice Q7)*
