Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-2-forces-free-body-diagrams-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-2-forces-free-body-diagrams-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can describe any force as an interaction, naming the object it acts on and the object that exerts it. *(Guide: "A force is an interaction"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why an object or system cannot exert a net force on itself. *(Guide: "No object can push itself"; Practice Q7)*
- I can tell contact forces from long-range forces, and explain that contact forces come from electric forces between atoms. *(Guide: "Contact forces and long-range forces"; Practice Q5)*
- I can state the direction of the gravitational, normal, friction and tension forces in a given situation. *(Guide: forces table; Practice Q2)*

## Skills

- I can draw a free-body diagram with a dot, one labelled straight arrow per force, sensible relative lengths, and same-direction forces side by side. *(Guide: "What a free-body diagram shows", Figure 1; Practice Q5, Q6)*
- I can keep components off the free-body diagram and work them out in a separate sketch. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can choose axes with one axis along the acceleration, including tilted axes on a ramp. *(Guide: "Choosing axes"; Practice Q4)*
- I can resolve gravity into mg sin θ along a ramp and mg cos θ into it, and check the result with θ = 0. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can turn a free-body diagram into one equation per axis when the forces balance. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q4, Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how choosing a different system changes which forces appear on the diagram. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can reject a force that has no object exerting it, such as a "force of the throw". *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q1, Q2)*
- I can explain why the normal force is not always equal to mg. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
