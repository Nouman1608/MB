Short on time? This page is the recap. For graphs and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/3-1-translational-kinetic-energy-study-guide/). This is the algebra-based course: no calculus is needed.

## Recap

- **Translational kinetic energy** is the energy an object has because its center of mass moves.
- It is a **scalar**: it depends on speed, not direction. It is **never negative** and is zero only at rest.
- For a system of objects, add each object's kinetic energy as a plain positive number.
- K is **proportional to m** and **proportional to v²**.
- K against v is a **parabola** with its minimum at the origin. K against v² is a **straight line** through the origin with slope ½m.
- Observers in different frames can measure **different kinetic energies** for the same object. Both are correct in their own frame.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| K = ½mv² | m in kg, v in m/s, K in J | find kinetic energy |
| v = √(2K / m) | m/s | find speed from kinetic energy |
| 1 J = 1 kg·m²/s² | joule | check units |
| ΔK = ½mv² − ½mv₀² | J | find a change in kinetic energy |
| K_system = K₁ + K₂ + … | J | total kinetic energy of several objects |
| slope of K against v² = ½m | kg | find mass from a linearised graph |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The object is modelled as a point at its center of mass; any spinning is ignored (rotational kinetic energy comes in Unit 6).
- All speeds are measured in **one stated frame**, usually the ground.
- g = 9.8 m/s² when gravity sets a speed.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Forgetting to square v**, or forgetting the ½.
2. **Doubling K when the speed doubles.** It goes up by a factor of 4.
3. **Writing ΔK = ½m(Δv)².** Subtract the two kinetic energies instead.
4. **Giving K a sign or a direction.**
5. **Cancelling the kinetic energies of objects moving in opposite directions.**
6. **Mixing frames**, for example a speed relative to a ferry deck with a speed relative to the shore.
7. **Halving the speed when K halves.** The speed falls by a factor of √2.

## Quick self-check

1. What is the kinetic energy of a 4.0 kg trolley at 3.0 m/s? *(18 J)*
2. A runner triples her speed. By what factor does her kinetic energy change? *(× 9)*
3. A 0.10 kg ball hits a wall at +5.0 m/s and rebounds at −5.0 m/s. What is ΔK? *(0 J)*
4. A 4.0 kg object has 50 J of kinetic energy. How fast is it moving? *(√(2 × 50 ÷ 4.0) = 5.0 m/s)*
