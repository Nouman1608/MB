## Recap

- The **net force** ΣF is the vector sum of every force exerted **on** the system. Third-law partners act elsewhere and are not included.
- **Translational equilibrium:** ΣF = 0.
- **Newton's first law:** if ΣF = 0, the velocity stays constant (same speed, same direction). The reverse is also used: constant velocity tells you ΣF = 0.
- Forces can be **balanced along one axis but not the other**. Only the velocity component along the unbalanced direction changes.
- **Inertia** is the tendency of an object to keep its velocity. Objects slow down only because a force such as friction acts.
- An **inertial frame** is one in which the first law is observed to hold. An accelerating frame (a braking bus) is not inertial.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Meaning | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| ΣF = F₁ + F₂ + … | vector sum of forces on the system | find the net force |
| ΣF = 0 | translational equilibrium | test for constant velocity |
| ΣF_x = 0, ΣF_y = 0 | one equation per axis | solve for unknown forces |
| ΣF = 0 ⇔ v constant | first law (with its reverse) | link forces and motion |
| v–t graph flat ⇔ ΣF = 0 | zero slope, zero acceleration | sketch or read graphs |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The ground is an **inertial frame** unless a question says otherwise.
- Strings and cords are **ideal**: one tension along each one.
- Weight near Earth's surface is F_g = mg with g = 9.8 m/s².
- Air resistance is ignored unless it is mentioned.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **"Moving means a force is pushing it forwards."** Constant velocity needs zero net force.
2. **"Equilibrium means at rest."** A lift rising at a steady speed is in equilibrium.
3. **"v = 0 means equilibrium."** A ball at the top of its flight has ΣF = mg downward.
4. **Writing one equation for a 2D problem.** You need ΣF_x = 0 **and** ΣF_y = 0.
5. **Assuming F_N = mg.** An angled pull or push changes the normal force.
6. **Adding a third-law partner into ΣF.** Only forces on the chosen system count.
7. **Expecting velocity to "return" after a force stops.** It stays at its new value.

## Quick self-check

1. Three horizontal forces act on a puck: 5.0 N east, 5.0 N west and 2.0 N north. Is the puck in equilibrium? *(No. ΣF = 2.0 N north; its velocity changes towards the north, while its east–west velocity stays the same.)*
2. A 2.0 kg bag hangs at rest from an ideal string. What is the tension? *(19.6 N, equal to mg.)*
3. In a lift moving down at a steady speed, a coin lies at rest on the floor. Is the lift an inertial frame? *(Yes. It moves at constant velocity, and the coin obeys the first law in it.)*
