Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, diagrams and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-8-spring-forces-study-guide/). This is the algebra-based course: no calculus is needed.

## Recap

- An **ideal spring** has negligible mass and exerts a force proportional to its change in length from its **relaxed length**.
- A stretched spring **pulls** on what is attached to it; a compressed spring **pushes**.
- The spring force always points back toward the position where the spring is relaxed. For a block on a horizontal spring, that is the **equilibrium position**.
- The **spring constant** k (N/m) measures stiffness. Large k = stiff spring.
- The force does not depend on which way the object is moving, only on where it is.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| F_s = −kΔx | k in N/m, Δx in m | find the spring force with direction |
| \|F_s\| = k\|Δx\| | N | find the size of the force |
| k = slope of \|F_s\| against \|Δx\| | N/m | find k from data |
| kΔx = mg | hanging object at rest | find k or the stretch |
| kΔx = mg sin θ | object at rest on a smooth slope | find the stretch on a slope |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The spring is **ideal**: massless, and it obeys Hooke's law over the range used.
- The spring is not stretched so far that it stays permanently longer.
- A hanging object is **at rest** when you read the stretch, so spring force equals weight.
- g = 9.8 m/s².

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Using total length for Δx.** Subtract the relaxed length first.
2. **Centimetres with k in N/m.** Convert to metres.
3. **Spring force drawn in the direction of motion.** It points toward the relaxed position.
4. **Treating −kΔx as a negative size.** The sign is direction only.
5. **Joining the dots on a graph.** Draw one best-fit straight line and take its slope from two points on the line.
6. **Thinking k changes with the load.** The stretch changes; k is fixed for the spring.

## Quick self-check

1. A spring with k = 200 N/m is compressed by 4.0 cm. What is the size of the force it exerts? *(200 × 0.040 = 8.0 N)*
2. A 0.30 kg mass hangs at rest and stretches a spring by 6.0 cm. Find k. *(0.30 × 9.8 ÷ 0.060 = 49 N/m)*
3. A force of 1.2 N stretches a spring by 3.0 cm. What force stretches it by 7.5 cm? *(k = 40 N/m, so 40 × 0.075 = 3.0 N)*
