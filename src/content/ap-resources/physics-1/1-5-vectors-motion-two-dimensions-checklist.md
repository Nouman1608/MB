Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/1-5-vectors-motion-two-dimensions-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/1-5-vectors-motion-two-dimensions-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain how any vector can be replaced by two perpendicular components, and draw the right-angled triangle that links them. *(Guide: "Resolving a vector into components", Figure 1)*
- I can explain why I may choose any pair of perpendicular axes, and pick the pair that makes a problem simplest. *(Guide: "Choosing a coordinate system")*
- I can explain why the horizontal and vertical motions of a projectile are independent, and say what the two share. *(Guide: "Motion in two dimensions", Figure 2)*
- I can state the acceleration components of a projectile with +y up: a_x = 0 and a_y = −g. *(Guide: "Projectile motion"; Practice Q2)*

## Skills

- I can resolve a vector into components with sin and cos, including when the angle is given from the y-axis. *(Guide: the warning about cos and sin; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can find a vector's size and direction from its components and choose the correct quadrant from a sketch. *(Guide: "Rebuilding a vector from its components")*
- I can add two displacements by components and give the resultant as a size and a direction. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can solve a horizontal-launch problem: time from the height, then distance and final velocity. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can solve an angled-launch problem on level ground: time to the top, maximum height, time of flight and range. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7)*
- I can turn launch data into a straight-line graph by plotting distance against √h and use the slope. *(Guide: "Planning a launch experiment"; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict how fall time and range change when the launch speed or height is multiplied by a factor. *(Guide: "Predicting changes without starting again"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why a projectile at the top of its path has non-zero velocity and non-zero acceleration. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q2)*
- I can derive the range on level ground in symbols and use it to explain why complementary angles give the same range. *(Practice Q7)*
