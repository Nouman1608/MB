Short on time? This page is the recap. For graphs, bar charts and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/7-4-energy-simple-harmonic-oscillators-study-guide/). This is the algebra-based course: no calculus is needed.

## Recap

- Name the system: **spring–object** or **pendulum bob–Earth**.
- Total energy **E = K + U**. With no friction and no outside work, it is constant.
- At a **turning point**, K = 0 (the smallest it can be) and U is largest.
- At **equilibrium**, U is smallest and K is largest.
- For a spring, all the energy is ½kA² at the turning point, so **E = ½kA²**.
- Changing the amplitude changes the largest potential energy, and so the total energy. It does **not** change the period.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| E = K + U | J | write the energy at any instant |
| K = ½mv² | J; m in kg; v in m/s | kinetic energy |
| U = ½kx² | J; k in N/m; x in m | spring potential energy |
| U = mgh | J; h above the lowest point | pendulum–Earth potential energy |
| E = ½kA² | J | total energy of a spring oscillator |
| v_max = A√(k/m) | m/s | top speed of a spring oscillator |
| ½mv² + ½kx² = ½kA² | J | speed at any displacement |
| v_max = √(2gh_max) | m/s | top speed of a pendulum bob |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- No friction or air resistance: the total energy stays constant.
- Ideal, massless spring; light, inextensible pendulum string.
- For a pendulum, h is measured from the lowest point; small angles are needed for SHM, but not for energy conservation.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Putting the largest kinetic energy at the turning points.
2. Saying energy is "lost" when the object stops.
3. Assuming half the amplitude means half the energy is kinetic (it is ¾).
4. Doubling the energy when the amplitude doubles (it is × 4).
5. Thinking a heavier pendulum bob is faster at the bottom.
6. Linking more energy to a longer period.

## Quick self-check

1. A spring with k = 128 N/m oscillates with amplitude 0.050 m. What is the total energy? *(0.16 J)*
2. The object on that spring has mass 0.32 kg. What is its maximum speed? *(1.0 m/s)*
3. At x = A/2, what fraction of the energy is potential? *(One quarter)*
