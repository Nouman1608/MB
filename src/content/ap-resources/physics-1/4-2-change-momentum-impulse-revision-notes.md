Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, graphs and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/4-2-change-momentum-impulse-study-guide/). This is the algebra-based course: no calculus is needed.

## Recap

- The **change in momentum** is final minus initial momentum. With signs, a rebound gives a bigger change than a stop.
- The **net external force** on a system equals the rate of change of its momentum. Internal forces cannot change a system's total momentum.
- **Impulse** is average force times the time it acts. It is a vector in the direction of the net force.
- **Impulse–momentum theorem:** the impulse of the net external force equals the change in momentum.
- For constant mass, the theorem becomes Newton's second law, F_net = ma.
- Systems with changing mass (rockets, leaking carts) are described in words only in this course.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Δp = p − p₀ = m(v − v₀) | kg·m/s, signed | find a change in momentum |
| J = F_avg Δt | N·s | find impulse from an average force |
| J = Δp | N·s = kg·m/s | link force and time to momentum change |
| F_net = Δp / Δt | N | find average net force from a momentum change |
| area under F_net–t graph | N·s | find impulse when force varies |
| slope of p–t graph | N | find the net external force |
| F_net = ma | constant mass only | recover Newton's second law |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The **mass** of the system stays constant during the interaction.
- In J = Δp, the impulse must come from the **net external** force. When a surface pushes and gravity also acts, the surface force = net force + weight (for an upward net force).
- During short, hard impacts, small external forces such as gravity and friction are often negligible; say so when you ignore them.
- Areas below the time axis on a force–time graph count as negative impulse.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Unsigned velocities in Δp.** A rebound from +4 m/s to −3 m/s is a change of 7 m/s, not 1 m/s.
2. **Peak force × time** for a force that rises and falls. Use the area.
3. **"Soft landings reduce impulse."** They lengthen the time; the impulse stays the same.
4. **Forgetting the weight** when asked for the force *from the floor*, not the net force.
5. **Treating impulse and momentum as different units.** N·s = kg·m/s.
6. **Reading a flat p–t graph as "at rest".** It means constant momentum.

## Quick self-check

1. A constant 3.0 N net force acts for 0.50 s on a 1.5 kg cart at rest. What is its final speed? *(J = 1.5 N·s, so v = 1.0 m/s.)*
2. A momentum–time graph rises in a straight line from 2.0 to 8.0 kg·m/s over 4.0 s. What is the net force? *(1.5 N)*
3. Take +x towards a wall. A 0.10 kg ball arrives at +5.0 m/s and leaves at −3.0 m/s. What is Δp? *(−0.80 kg·m/s)*
