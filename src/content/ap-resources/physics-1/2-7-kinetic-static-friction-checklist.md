## Understanding

- I can split the contact force from a surface into a normal component and a friction component, and say which way each points. *(Guide: "What a surface does to an object")*
- I can decide whether friction is kinetic or static by asking whether the two surfaces slide relative to each other. *(Guide: table; Practice Q2)*
- I can find the direction of kinetic friction on each of two sliding surfaces, including the third-law partner. *(Guide: "Kinetic friction"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why contact area does not appear in the friction equations, and that μ depends on the materials. *(Guide: "Why contact area does not matter")*
- I can explain why μ_s is usually larger than μ_k and what that means for starting and keeping an object moving. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*

## Calculation

- I can find the normal force from the perpendicular forces, including an angled pull or push and a slope. *(Guide: Worked examples 2 and 3; Practice Q3)*
- I can calculate kinetic friction with F_f,k = μ_k F_n and use it in Newton's second law. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can find static friction from a force balance, and check it against the maximum μ_s F_n. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 3; Practice Q1, Q3)*
- I can find μ_s from the angle at which an object just slips, μ_s = tan θ. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can sketch how friction changes as an applied force grows from zero until the object slides, with the drop from static to kinetic. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why static friction can point in the direction of motion, using walking or an accelerating vehicle. *(Guide: "Friction can point the way you move"; Practice Q7)*
- I can compare two situations, such as pulling up or pushing down at an angle, and explain the difference through the normal force. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can design a procedure to measure μ_s and say how to reduce uncertainty. *(Practice Q6)*
