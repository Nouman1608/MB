## Understanding

- I can state that a non-zero net torque changes angular velocity, with α = τ_net / I. *(Guide: "From 'is ω changing?' to 'how fast?'")*
- I can explain why α has the sense of the net torque, and say when a wheel speeds up or slows down. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can derive τ = Iα for a point mass from F = ma and a_T = rα. *(Guide: "Where the equation comes from")*
- I can explain why an axle force or a force through the centre gives no torque. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q7)*

## Skills

- I can predict the factor by which α changes when the net torque or rotational inertia changes, including I ∝ r² for point masses. *(Guide: "Using functional dependence"; Practice Q1)*
- I can compare α for objects of equal mass but different mass distribution, such as a hoop and a disk. *(Guide: "Comparing scenarios"; Practice Q2)*
- I can find the net torque when a friction torque acts and follow α with rotational kinematics. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can draw separate force diagrams for a drum or pulley and a connected block. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can combine ΣF = ma, Στ = Iα and a = Rα to derive and calculate an acceleration and tensions. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why the tensions on the two sides of a pulley with rotational inertia differ. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can use an α–τ graph to find I from the slope and a friction torque from the intercept, and evaluate a claim of direct proportion. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can justify why the linear and rotational motion of one object are analysed separately. *(Guide: "Linear and rotational analyses are separate"; Practice Q7)*
