Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, graphs and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/3-5-power-study-guide/). This is the algebra-based course: no calculus is needed.

## Recap

- **Choose the system first.** Energy can cross its boundary (work by outside forces) or change form inside it (for example, friction turning kinetic energy into internal energy).
- **Power** is the rate of that transfer or conversion. Energy says *how much*; power says *how fast*.
- **Average power** uses the total energy change over the whole interval.
- **Instantaneous power** of a constant force depends on the speed at that instant, so it changes if the speed changes.
- A force at right angles to the velocity has zero power. A force against the velocity has negative power: it takes energy out.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| P_avg = ΔE / Δt | W = J/s | find average power from an energy change |
| P_avg = W / Δt | W is work in J | find average power from the work done by a force |
| P = F∥v = Fv cos θ | θ between F and v | find instantaneous power of a constant force |
| slope of E–t graph | W | power (flat = zero power) |
| area under P–t graph | J | energy transferred (below axis = energy out) |
| v_max = P_max / F_resistive | m/s | top speed at constant velocity on level ground |
| 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J | energy, not power | convert electricity-bill energy to joules |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The force is **constant** when you use P = F∥v. If the force changes, use total energy ÷ total time for the average power.
- Objects are treated as **point objects**; rotation is ignored.
- Near Earth's surface, g = 9.8 m/s². Air resistance is ignored unless the question gives a resistive force.
- "Constant speed" means the net force is zero, so a driving force equals the forces it balances.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Mixing up energy and power.** Check units: J for energy, W for power.
2. **Using the whole force in P = Fv.** Multiply by cos θ, the angle between force and velocity.
3. **Assuming constant force gives constant power.** P rises as v rises.
4. **Forgetting the sign.** Friction on a sliding object has negative power.
5. **Taking the mean of the start and end power** when the power does not change at a steady rate. Use total energy ÷ total time.
6. **Minutes and hours left unconverted.** Change time to seconds before dividing.
7. **Saying no power is involved at constant speed.** Energy can flow in and out at equal rates.

## Quick self-check

1. A 1.2 kW motor runs for 5.0 min. How much energy does it deliver? *(3.6 × 10⁵ J)*
2. A 200 N force acts at 60° to the velocity of a cart moving at 3.0 m/s. What is its power? *(300 W)*
3. How many joules are in 1 kWh? *(3.6 × 10⁶ J)*
