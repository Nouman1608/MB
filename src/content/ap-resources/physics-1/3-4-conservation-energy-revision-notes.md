Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, bar charts and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/3-4-conservation-energy-study-guide/). This is the algebra-based course: no calculus is needed.

## Recap

- **Energy is conserved in every interaction.** It changes form inside a system or crosses the boundary as work.
- A **single object** can only have kinetic energy. Interacting objects (via gravity or springs) or a shape-changing object can also have potential energy.
- **Mechanical energy** E_mech = K + U.
- A change in one energy type is balanced by an equal and opposite change in other types, or by energy crossing the boundary.
- You can **choose** the system so that its total energy is constant. Putting Earth inside turns gravity's work into ΔU_g.
- Friction and air resistance **dissipate** mechanical energy as thermal energy and sound.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | When to use it |
|---|---|
| E_mech = K + U, with K = ½mv² | any system |
| ΔE_system = W_outside | always: energy in or out as work by outside forces |
| K_i + U_i = K_f + U_f | W_outside = 0 and no friction or air resistance inside |
| K_i + U_i = K_f + U_f + ΔE_thermal | W_outside = 0, friction inside the system |
| v = √(2gh) | from rest, smooth path, drop h (mass cancels) |

**Factors of change:** spring launcher, h ∝ Δx² (double the compression, four times the height). Smooth drop, v ∝ √h (four times the drop, twice the speed).

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- "Smooth" or "frictionless" means no friction; air resistance is ignored unless stated.
- Springs are ideal and massless.
- Normal forces on a fixed track do no work because they act perpendicular to the motion.
- g = 9.8 m/s² at Earth's surface. The course framework says exam questions use 10 m/s², and 9.8 m/s² is also accepted.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **No system named.** "Potential energy turns into kinetic energy" needs the object + Earth system.
2. **Counting gravity twice:** using both ΔU_g and the work done by gravity for the same system.
3. **Changing the zero part-way** through a problem.
4. **Saying energy is "lost" with friction** without saying where it went (thermal energy, sound).
5. **Forgetting a term**, such as U_g as a spring-launched ball rises off the spring.
6. **Expecting mass to matter** on a smooth slope.
7. **Doubling instead of quadrupling** when a spring's compression doubles.

## Quick self-check

1. A stone is dropped from rest and falls 5.0 m. Ignore air resistance. How fast is it moving? *(9.9 m/s)*
2. A 0.50 kg block on a smooth floor is pushed against a spring (k = 200 N/m), compressing it 0.10 m, then released. How fast does it leave the spring? *(2.0 m/s)*
3. A system has 100 J of mechanical energy at the start and 70 J at the end. No outside force did work. Where did 30 J go? *(Into thermal energy and sound, through friction or air resistance inside the system.)*
4. System = ball only. Does the ball's energy stay constant as it falls? *(No. Gravity is an outside force doing positive work, so K increases.)*
