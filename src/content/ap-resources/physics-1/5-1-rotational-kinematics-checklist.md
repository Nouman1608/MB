Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/5-1-rotational-kinematics-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/5-1-rotational-kinematics-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why a spinning wheel is a rigid system and not an object, and say when a spinning body can still be modelled as a single object. *(Guide: "When a point is not enough")*
- I can state an axis, a reference line and a positive rotation sense before I write any angular sign. *(Guide: Figure 1; every practice question)*
- I can explain why every point on a rigid body has the same angular velocity and angular acceleration. *(Guide: "Angular velocity and angular acceleration")*
- I can match each rotational quantity and equation to its linear partner from Unit 1. *(Guide: "The analogy with linear motion")*

## Skills

- I can convert between revolutions, degrees, radians and rpm. *(Guide: "Measure angles in radians"; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can calculate average angular velocity and average angular acceleration with signed values, including a reversal. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2)*
- I can choose and use the right constant-α equation for a spin-up or a slow-down. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can find α from the slope, and Δθ and the total angle from the areas, of an ω–t graph. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can sketch the angle–time graph that matches a given ω–t graph. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can process angle–time data into an ω–t graph using midpoint times and find α and ω₀. *(Guide: "Investigating rotation in the lab"; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can decide whether a rotation is speeding up or slowing down from the signs of ω and α. *(Guide: speeding-up table; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can derive a symbolic expression and use it to predict a factor of change, such as the stopping angle when ω₀ is tripled. *(Guide: "Functional dependence"; Practice Q3, Q7)*
- I can design a procedure to test whether a wheel's angular acceleration is constant, and use data to support or refute that claim. *(Guide: "Investigating rotation in the lab"; Practice Q6)*
