Short on time? This page is the recap. For the particle diagrams, graphs and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/8-1-internal-structure-density-study-guide/). This is the algebra-based course: no calculus is needed.

## Recap

- The properties of solids, liquids and gases come from **how strongly their particles interact** and **how far apart they are**.
- Solid: fixed shape and volume. Liquid: fixed volume, no fixed shape. Gas: neither fixed, and easy to compress.
- A **fluid** has no fixed shape and flows. Liquids **and** gases are fluids.
- **Density** describes a material, not a sample. Cutting a uniform object does not change its density.
- For an object made of several parts, use **average density** = total mass ÷ total volume.
- An **ideal fluid** is incompressible (constant density) and has no viscosity (no internal friction). Treat fluids as ideal unless told otherwise.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| ρ = m / V | ρ in kg/m³, m in kg, V in m³ | find density, mass or volume |
| 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³ | 1 mL = 1 cm³; 1 L = 10⁻³ m³ | convert lab units to SI |
| ρ_avg = m_total / V_total | kg/m³ | describe an object made of parts or with trapped air |
| slope of m–V graph | kg/m³ (or g/cm³) | find density from several samples |
| weight = mg | N | do not confuse with mass or density |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Each sample is **uniform**: the same material all the way through.
- Volume by water displacement assumes the object is fully under water, does not absorb water and has no trapped bubbles.
- Liquids are treated as **incompressible**. Gases are not: their density changes when they are squeezed.
- Approximate densities: air 1.2 kg/m³, fresh water 1000 kg/m³, aluminium 2700 kg/m³.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Swapping mass, weight, volume and density.** Use the exact word, with its unit.
2. **"Heavy means dense."** Check the volume too.
3. **Forgetting to cube the length conversion.** 1 cm³ = 10⁻⁶ m³, not 10⁻² m³.
4. **Saying fluids are only liquids.** Air is a fluid.
5. **Using one data point when a graph is given.** Use the slope of the best-fit line.
6. **Adding densities of parts.** Add masses and add volumes, then divide.

## Quick self-check

1. A 250 cm³ block has mass 0.50 kg. What is its density in kg/m³? *(2.0 × 10³ kg/m³)*
2. A uniform plank is sawn into three equal pieces. What happens to the density of each piece? *(It stays the same.)*
3. What mass of water (1000 kg/m³) fills a 2.0 L bottle? *(2.0 kg)*
4. Name the two properties of an ideal fluid. *(Incompressible; no viscosity.)*
