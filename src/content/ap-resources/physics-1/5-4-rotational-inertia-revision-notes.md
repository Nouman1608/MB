Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, the graph and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/5-4-rotational-inertia-study-guide/). This is the algebra-based course: no calculus is needed.

## Recap

- **Rotational inertia** I measures how hard it is to change a rigid system's rotation. It is the rotational partner of inertial mass.
- It depends on the **mass** and on the **distribution of mass relative to the axis**. Mass far from the axis counts much more than mass near it.
- One object has a **different I for every axis**. Always state the axis.
- You calculate I for up to five small objects in a flat layout. For rods, disks, hoops and spheres, the value is given.
- For any family of parallel axes, I is **smallest through the centre of mass**.
- Compare a **hoop** with a **solid disk** of equal mass and radius: the hoop's I is larger, because all its mass is at the rim.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| I = mr² | m in kg, r = perpendicular distance from axis in m; I in kg·m² | find I for one small object |
| I = Σmᵢrᵢ² | every rᵢ from the same axis | add up a system of small objects |
| I = I_cm + Md² | M = total mass; d = distance between the parallel axes | move from the centre-of-mass axis to a parallel one |
| I_new / I_old = (r_new / r_old)² | same mass | factor-of-change questions |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The system is **rigid**: the masses stay at fixed distances from the axis.
- "Small" objects are treated as **points** at a single distance r.
- Connecting rods or frames described as **light** add nothing to I.
- Formulas for extended objects (such as ½MR² for a disk) are **given**, and apply only to the axis stated with them.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Not squaring r.** I = mr², not mr.
2. **Measuring r from the wrong point.** Use the perpendicular distance from the stated axis.
3. **Assuming one I per object.** Change the axis and I changes.
4. **Using part of the mass in Md².** M is the total mass of the system.
5. **Starting the parallel axis theorem from the wrong axis.** It must start from I_cm.
6. **Thinking more material always means more I.** A hoop beats a disk of the same mass and radius.
7. **Putting the minimum at the geometric centre** when the masses are unequal. It is at the centre of mass.

## Quick self-check

1. A 0.30 kg bead is 0.20 m from an axis. Find I. *(0.012 kg·m²)*
2. A 2.0 kg object has I_cm = 0.050 kg·m². Find I about a parallel axis 0.10 m away. *(0.070 kg·m²)*
3. A small mass is moved from 0.15 m to 0.30 m from the axis. By what factor does its I change? *(4 times as large)*
