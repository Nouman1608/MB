## Recap

- **Pressure** is the perpendicular force on a surface divided by the area it acts on. A force along the surface adds nothing to the pressure.
- Pressure is a **scalar**. At a point in a fluid, it has one value. The force it makes on any surface is perpendicular to that surface.
- A fluid's pressure comes from **countless particle collisions** with a surface. Particles move every way, so a fluid pushes on every surface it touches.
- An **incompressible** liquid keeps the same volume and density however hard it is squeezed.
- **Absolute pressure** is the total. **Gauge pressure** is the amount above a reference, usually the atmosphere.
- Pressure increases with **vertical depth** because each layer supports the weight of the fluid above it.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| P = F⊥ / A | P in Pa (N/m²), F⊥ in N, A in m² | pressure from a force |
| P = P₀ + P_gauge | P₀ often P_atm = 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa | switch between absolute and gauge |
| P_gauge = ρgh | ρ in kg/m³, g = 9.8 m/s², h in m (vertical depth) | pressure under a column of fluid |
| F = PA (perpendicular to surface) | N | force from a pressure on a flat surface |
| slope of P–h graph = ρg | Pa/m | find density from a pressure–depth line |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The fluid is **at rest** and **incompressible**, so ρ is the same at every depth.
- g is uniform: 9.8 m/s².
- The pressure on a horizontal surface is the same everywhere on it. On a tall vertical wall it is not, because depth varies.
- Atmospheric pressure is taken as 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa unless stated.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Calling pressure a force or a vector.** It is force per area, and a scalar.
2. **Using the whole force when it acts at an angle.** Use F⊥ only.
3. **Mixing gauge and absolute pressure.** Check which one the question wants.
4. **Using the slanted length of a tube as h.** Use the vertical depth.
5. **Thinking a wider or fuller container has more pressure at the same depth.** It does not.
6. **Doubling absolute pressure when depth doubles.** Only gauge pressure doubles.
7. **Leaving depth in cm or area in cm².** Convert to m and m².

## Quick self-check

1. A 300 N crate rests on a base of area 0.25 m². Pressure on the floor? *(1.2 × 10³ Pa)*
2. Gauge pressure 2.0 m below the surface of fresh water? *(2.0 × 10⁴ Pa, or 19,600 Pa)*
3. Absolute pressure at the same point? *(1.2 × 10⁵ Pa)*
4. Two tanks of the same liquid, one narrow and one wide, are filled to the same depth. Which has the greater pressure at the bottom? *(Neither: the pressures are equal.)*
