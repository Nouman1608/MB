This diagnostic is for the **algebra-based Physics 1 course**, Unit 7 (Oscillations). It shows you **which topics to revisit**. Allow about 30 minutes, then use the table at the end.

These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. They have not been calibrated against real exam results, so your total does not predict an exam score. Use a scientific calculator, in **radian mode** whenever you work out sin(2πft) or cos(2πft), and g = 9.8 m/s². Springs and strings are ideal, surfaces are frictionless, pendulums swing through small angles, and air resistance is negligible unless stated.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · 7.1)

Take **+x to the right**, with x = 0 at equilibrium. A 0.50 kg block on a level, frictionless surface sits between two springs, each fixed to a wall. The left spring has k = 30 N/m and the right spring has k = 20 N/m. Both springs are relaxed when the block is at x = 0. What is the block's acceleration when it is at x = +2.0 cm?

- (A) −1.2 m/s²
- (B) −0.40 m/s²
- (C) −2.0 m/s²
- (D) +2.0 m/s²

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** At x = +2.0 cm the left spring pulls left and the right spring pushes left, so F_net = −(30 + 20)x = −(50 N/m)x. Then a = −(50 ÷ 0.50) × 0.020 = −2.0 m/s².

- (A) uses the left spring only.
- (B) subtracts the springs. Both act toward equilibrium.
- (D) has the right size but points away from equilibrium. A restoring force points opposite to the displacement.

**If you missed this:** read "The condition for SHM" in the [Topic 7.1 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/7-1-defining-simple-harmonic-motion-shm-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · 7.1)

A pendulum has a 0.40 kg bob on a string 0.50 m long. The string makes an angle of 0.10 rad with the vertical. Using the small-angle model, what is the size of the restoring torque about the pivot?

- (A) 2.0 N·m
- (B) 0.39 N·m
- (C) 0.0034 N·m
- (D) 0.20 N·m

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (D).** The weight has lever arm ℓ sin θ about the pivot, so τ = mgℓ sin θ ≈ mgℓθ = 0.40 × 9.8 × 0.50 × 0.10 = 0.196 N·m. Because the torque is proportional to θ at small angles, the pendulum can be modelled as SHM.

- (A) is mgℓ. It leaves out the angle, which sets the lever arm.
- (B) is mgθ, a force. It leaves out the length of the lever arm.
- (C) treats 0.10 as degrees. The small-angle model needs radians.

**If you missed this:** read "The pendulum: a restoring torque" in the [Topic 7.1 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/7-1-defining-simple-harmonic-motion-shm-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · 7.2)

A block on one spring oscillates with a period of 0.60 s. It is replaced by a block with **four times the mass**, hung from **two of the same springs side by side**, which act like one spring with twice the spring constant. What is the new period?

- (A) 1.2 s
- (B) 0.85 s
- (C) 0.60 s
- (D) 0.42 s

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** T = 2π√(m/k), so T depends on m/k. The ratio becomes 4m ÷ 2k = 2(m/k), so T is multiplied by √2: 0.60 × 1.41 = 0.85 s.

- (A) uses only the mass change (× √4 = 2), or forgets the square root.
- (C) assumes the two changes cancel. The mass rose by more than the stiffness.
- (D) uses only the spring change (÷ √2).

**If you missed this:** read "Factor-of-change reasoning" in the [Topic 7.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/7-2-frequency-period-shm-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · 7.2)

A museum wants a pendulum that swings with a frequency of 0.50 Hz. About how long must it be?

- (A) 0.062 m
- (B) 0.99 m
- (C) 0.50 m
- (D) 39 m

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** First the period: T = 1/f = 2.0 s. Then square T = 2π√(ℓ/g) and rearrange: ℓ = gT² ÷ 4π² = 9.8 × 4.0 ÷ 39.5 = 0.99 m.

- (A) puts the frequency, 0.50, in place of the period.
- (C) forgets to square T.
- (D) leaves out the 4π².

**If you missed this:** read "Period and frequency" and "The simple pendulum" in the [Topic 7.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/7-2-frequency-period-shm-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 5 (multiple choice · 7.3)

Take **+x to the right** of equilibrium. A glider moves so that x = (0.050 m) sin(2πt ÷ 0.80 s). Which statement describes the glider at t = 0.50 s?

- (A) x = −0.035 m, moving left
- (B) x = +0.035 m, moving right
- (C) x = −0.035 m, moving right
- (D) x = +0.0034 m, moving right

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** The angle is 2π × 0.50 ÷ 0.80 = 3.93 rad, and sin 3.93 = −0.707, so x = −0.035 m. The glider passes x = 0 moving left at T/2 = 0.40 s and reaches −A at 3T/4 = 0.60 s, so at 0.50 s it is **moving left**.

- (B) drops the sign of the sine.
- (C) reads the direction from the sign of x. Velocity is the slope of the x–t graph.
- (D) comes from a calculator in degree mode.

**If you missed this:** read "Displacement as a function of time" and Worked example 1 in the [Topic 7.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/7-3-representing-analyzing-shm-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 6 (multiple choice · 7.3)

Two identical spring–block systems sit side by side. Block P is released from rest 2.0 cm from equilibrium. At the same instant, block Q is released from rest 6.0 cm from equilibrium. What is true a quarter of a period later?

- (A) Only P has reached equilibrium, because it has less distance to travel.
- (B) Both blocks are at equilibrium, moving at the same speed.
- (C) Both blocks are at equilibrium, and Q is moving three times as fast as P.
- (D) Q has not yet reached equilibrium, because its period is √3 times longer.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** The period does not depend on the amplitude, so both blocks reach x = 0 together, after T/4. Q's restoring force and acceleration are three times as large at every matching point, so it builds up three times the speed.

- (A) and (D) assume a bigger amplitude takes longer. The larger force makes Q cover its longer path in the same time.
- (B) gets the timing right but forgets that Q covers three times the distance in the same time.

**If you missed this:** read "Amplitude does not change the period" in the [Topic 7.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/7-3-representing-analyzing-shm-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 7 (multiple choice · 7.4)

A pendulum bob passes through its lowest point at 0.70 m/s. Taking the bob–Earth system, how high above the lowest point does the bob rise?

- (A) 0.050 m
- (B) 0.036 m
- (C) 0.071 m
- (D) 0.025 m

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (D).** The total energy is constant: all kinetic at the bottom, all gravitational potential at the top. ½mv² = mgh, so h = v² ÷ 2g = 0.49 ÷ 19.6 = 0.025 m. The mass cancels, which is why it is not given.

- (A) drops the ½ in the kinetic energy.
- (B) uses v instead of v².
- (C) uses v ÷ g, which mixes up the units.

**If you missed this:** read Worked example 2 (a pendulum) in the [Topic 7.4 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/7-4-energy-simple-harmonic-oscillators-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 8 (multiple choice · 7.4)

A block oscillates on a spring with amplitude A. The spring is replaced by one with **four times the spring constant**, and the block is set oscillating with the **same amplitude**. Which row is correct?

- (A) Total energy × 4; maximum speed × 2; period × ½
- (B) Total energy × 4; maximum speed × 4; period unchanged
- (C) Total energy unchanged; maximum speed × 2; period × ½
- (D) Total energy × 2; maximum speed × 2; period × 2

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** E = ½kA², so with the same A the energy is × 4. All of it becomes kinetic at equilibrium: ½mv_max² = ½kA², so v_max = A√(k/m), × √4 = × 2. The period T = 2π√(m/k) is × 1/√4 = × ½.

- (B) gives the speed the same factor as the energy. Kinetic energy goes with v², so the speed only doubles.
- (C) assumes the same amplitude means the same energy. The stiffer spring stores more energy at the same stretch.
- (D) takes a square root for the energy, and has the period growing. A stiffer spring turns the block round faster, so the period gets shorter.

**If you missed this:** read "Trading energy back and forth" and "Quick checks with extreme cases" in the [Topic 7.4 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/7-4-energy-simple-harmonic-oscillators-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 9 (short answer · 7.2)

On the fictional moon Teris, an astronaut builds a pendulum 0.50 m long and times **10 full swings in 31.4 s**.

(a) Find the gravitational field strength on Teris.
(b) A spring–block toy has a period of 0.60 s on Earth. Predict its period on Teris, and explain.
(c) How long would a pendulum on Teris need to be to have the same period as a 0.50 m pendulum on Earth?

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**(a)** T = 31.4 ÷ 10 = 3.14 s. Square T = 2π√(ℓ/g) and rearrange: g = 4π²ℓ ÷ T² = 39.5 × 0.50 ÷ 3.14² = **2.0 m/s²**.

**(b)** **0.60 s, unchanged.** T = 2π√(m/k) contains no g. Weaker gravity only moves the equilibrium position of a hanging spring.

**(c)** On Earth, T = 2π√(0.50 ÷ 9.8) = 1.42 s. For the same period, ℓ ÷ g must be the same: ℓ = 0.50 × (2.0 ÷ 9.8) = **0.10 m**.

Check yourself: 1 point each for dividing by 10 for T, g = 2.0 m/s², the unchanged spring period with the reason "no g in T_s", and 0.10 m from keeping ℓ/g fixed.

**If you missed this:** read "The object–spring oscillator" and "The simple pendulum" in the [Topic 7.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/7-2-frequency-period-shm-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 10 (short answer · 7.4)

Take **+x to the right** of equilibrium. A 0.60 kg cart on a level, frictionless track is attached to a spring with k = 60 N/m. It is pulled to x = +0.10 m and released from rest at t = 0.

(a) Find the total energy of the cart–spring system and the cart's maximum speed.
(b) Find the cart's speed and acceleration when it is at x = −0.060 m.
(c) At which times in the first period is the kinetic energy at its largest?
(d) A student says: "The system has the most energy at equilibrium, because that is where the cart moves fastest." Evaluate this claim.

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**(a)** At release K = 0, so E = ½kA² = ½ × 60 × 0.10² = **0.30 J**. At equilibrium all of it is kinetic: v_max = √(2 × 0.30 ÷ 0.60) = **1.0 m/s**.

**(b)** U = ½ × 60 × 0.060² = 0.108 J, so K = 0.30 − 0.108 = 0.192 J and v = √(2 × 0.192 ÷ 0.60) = **0.80 m/s**. a_x = −(k/m)x = −100 × (−0.060) = **+6.0 m/s²**, to the right, toward equilibrium.

**(c)** T = 2π√(0.60 ÷ 60) = 0.63 s. K is largest each time the cart passes x = 0: at T/4 = **0.16 s** and 3T/4 = **0.47 s**. It peaks twice per period.

**(d)** **Incorrect.** With no friction the **total** energy is 0.30 J everywhere. Only the share changes: all kinetic at equilibrium, all spring potential energy at the turning points.

Check yourself: 1 point each for 0.30 J and 1.0 m/s, 0.80 m/s from E − U, +6.0 m/s² with direction, both times in (c), and rejecting the claim because the total is constant.

**If you missed this:** read Worked example 1 and "Energy against time" in the [Topic 7.4 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/7-4-energy-simple-harmonic-oscillators-study-guide/).
</details>
