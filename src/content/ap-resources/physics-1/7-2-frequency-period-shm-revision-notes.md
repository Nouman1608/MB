## Recap

- **Period** T: time for one full cycle (s). **Frequency** f: cycles per second (Hz). They are reciprocals.
- An object on an ideal spring oscillates faster with a **stiffer spring** and slower with **more mass**. Gravity does not change its period.
- A small-angle pendulum oscillates slower with a **longer string** and faster with **stronger gravity**. The bob's mass does not change its period.
- For SHM, the period does **not** depend on the amplitude.
- Time **many cycles** and divide, starting and stopping as the object passes equilibrium.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Depends on | Does not depend on / use |
|---|---|---|
| T = 1/f | frequency f (Hz) | converts between T and f |
| T_s = 2π√(m/k) | mass m, spring constant k | not g, amplitude, or vertical vs horizontal |
| T_p = 2π√(ℓ/g) | length ℓ (pivot to centre of bob), g | not bob mass or amplitude (small angles) |
| T_s² = (4π²/k) m | — | plot T² against m: straight line, slope 4π²/k |
| T_p² = (4π²/g) ℓ | — | plot T² against ℓ: straight line, slope 4π²/g |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The spring is **ideal** (massless, obeys Hooke's law); the pendulum string is light and does not stretch.
- The pendulum swings through a **small angle**, so the SHM model holds.
- No friction or air resistance, so the amplitude stays constant.
- g = 9.8 m/s² near Earth's surface.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Forgetting the square root.** Double the mass gives √2 times the period, not twice.
2. **Putting g in the spring equation, or m in the pendulum equation.**
3. **Timing one cycle.** Reaction time swamps it. Time ten or more.
4. **Counting "one" as you start the stopwatch.** You then time one cycle fewer than you count.
5. **Plotting T against ℓ and expecting a straight line.** Plot T².
6. **Reading the slope from data points.** Use two points on the best-fit line.
7. **Mixing up T and f.** Check: a long period means a low frequency.

## Quick self-check

1. A vibrating platform has f = 4.0 Hz. What is its period? *(0.25 s)*
2. The mass on a spring is multiplied by 9. What happens to the period? *(It triples.)*
3. What is the period of a pendulum 0.25 m long? *(2π√(0.25 ÷ 9.8) ≈ 1.0 s)*
4. A 0.20 kg mass oscillates on a spring with k = 20 N/m. What is its period? *(2π√(0.010) ≈ 0.63 s)*
5. Which graph is a straight line through the origin for a pendulum: T against ℓ, or T² against ℓ? *(T² against ℓ)*
