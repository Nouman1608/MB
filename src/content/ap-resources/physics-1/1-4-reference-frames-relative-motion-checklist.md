## Understanding

- I can describe an observer's reference frame by naming the observer, the origin and the positive direction. *(Guide: "Every measurement has an observer")*
- I can explain why two observers can both be right when they measure different velocities for the same object. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
- I can say what makes a frame inertial and give an everyday example of a frame that is not. *(Guide: "Inertial frames")*
- I can explain why relative-velocity problems in this course stay on one straight line. *(Guide: "Relative velocity along one line")*

## Skills

- I can use v_AC = v_AB + v_BC and v_BA = −v_AB with signed velocities. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1, Q2)*
- I can convert positions and displacements from the ground frame into a moving observer's frame. *(Guide: "Converting positions and displacements"; Practice Q4)*
- I can find a meeting or overtaking time by working in the frame of one of the moving objects. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can derive a symbolic expression for a trip on a moving walkway or current, and test it with a limiting case. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can sketch a velocity–time graph as seen from a frame moving at constant velocity by shifting the original graph. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q3)*

## Reasoning

- I can show with algebra that an observer moving at constant velocity measures the same acceleration as the ground. *(Guide: "Acceleration is the same for every inertial observer")*
- I can use data from two frames to support or reject a claim about acceleration. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why a falling object takes the same time to land whichever inertial frame I use. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can spot when objects approach each other and use the sum of their speeds, not the difference. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q4)*
