Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, diagrams and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/7-1-defining-simple-harmonic-motion-shm-study-guide/). This is the algebra-based course: no calculus is needed.

## Recap

- **Periodic motion** repeats itself in equal time intervals. **SHM** is a special case of it.
- The **equilibrium position** is where the net force on the object is zero.
- A **restoring force** points opposite to the displacement from equilibrium, whichever way the object is moving.
- **SHM condition:** the size of the restoring force is **proportional** to the size of the displacement. A graph of F_net (or a) against x is a straight line through the origin with a negative slope.
- A **vertical spring** is still SHM. Gravity just moves the equilibrium down by mg/k.
- A **pendulum** has a restoring torque mgℓ sin θ. For small angles (in radians) sin θ ≈ θ, so the torque is proportional to θ and the motion is close to SHM.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| F_net = −kx | k in N/m, x in m from equilibrium | test for SHM; find the restoring force |
| a = −(k/m)x | a in m/s² | find acceleration at any displacement |
| d = mg / k | d in m | find where a vertical spring's equilibrium is |
| τ = mgℓ sin θ ≈ mgℓθ | θ in rad, τ in N·m | restoring torque on a pendulum (small angles) |
| F ≈ (mg/ℓ)s along the arc | s = ℓθ in m | treat a small-angle pendulum like a spring |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Springs and strings are **ideal**: massless, and the spring obeys Hooke's law.
- No friction or air resistance, so nothing but the restoring force changes the motion along the axis.
- A pendulum bob is a point mass on a light string, swinging through a **small angle** (about 15° or less keeps the error near 1%).
- g = 9.8 m/s².

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Calling all periodic motion SHM.** A bouncing ball repeats, but its force is not −kx.
2. **Measuring x from the wrong place.** Use the equilibrium position, not the relaxed length of a hanging spring.
3. **Giving the restoring force the direction of motion.** It points toward equilibrium.
4. **"Acceleration is biggest at equilibrium."** It is zero there.
5. **Using degrees in sin θ ≈ θ.** Radians only.
6. **Forgetting the sign.** a and x always have opposite signs in SHM.

## Quick self-check

1. A 0.50 kg block on a spring with k = 200 N/m is at x = +0.10 m from equilibrium. What is its acceleration? *(−40 m/s², toward equilibrium)*
2. A 0.30 kg mass hangs at rest from a spring with k = 60 N/m. How far is the spring stretched? *(0.049 m)*
3. An object in SHM has an acceleration of size 1.5 m/s² at x = +2.0 cm. What is its acceleration at x = −6.0 cm? *(+4.5 m/s²: three times as far, opposite side)*
4. Is a puck sliding back and forth between two walls in SHM? *(No: the force is zero except at the walls, so it is not proportional to x.)*
