Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/8-3-fluids-newtons-laws-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/8-3-fluids-newtons-laws-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain how Newton's laws apply to a small piece of fluid, and name the forces on it. *(Guide: "Newton's laws inside a fluid")*
- I can state when a fluid's velocity changes, and explain why equal pressures on both ends mean constant velocity, not rest. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why pressure increases with depth using a force balance on a column of fluid. *(Guide: "A fluid at rest")*
- I can explain the buoyant force as the net effect of the fluid's pressure forces, and why I must not draw both. *(Guide: Figure 1)*

## Skills

- I can derive F_b = ρ_fluid V g from the pressures on the top and bottom of a submerged block. *(Guide: "Deriving F_b = ρVg")*
- I can draw free-body diagrams for floating, suspended and tied-down objects. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q7)*
- I can find a buoyant force, volume and density from two spring-scale readings. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can find how deep a floating object sits, and the extra load that would sink it. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can sketch and interpret a graph of scale reading against depth as an object is lowered into a fluid. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can derive and use a = (P₁ − P₂) / (ρL) for a slug of fluid in a level pipe. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict how a submerged fraction, scale reading or acceleration changes when the fluid's density changes. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q4)*
- I can use Newton's third law to explain why a balance under a beaker reads more when an object is lowered into the water. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can justify why the buoyant force on a fully submerged object does not depend on depth or on the object's material. *(Practice Q1, Q7)*
- I can decide whether an object floats or sinks by comparing densities, not masses. *(Guide: "Floating"; Common misconceptions)*
