Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/6-2-torque-work-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/6-2-torque-work-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why a torque transfers energy only when the object turns while the torque acts. *(Guide: "Torque transfers energy only when something turns")*
- I can derive W = τΔθ from the work done by a tangential force moving along an arc s = rΔθ. *(Guide: "Deriving W = τΔθ", Figure 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why torque and work are different quantities even though both can be written with N·m. *(Guide: FAQ and misconceptions)*
- I can decide whether a torque does positive, negative or zero work, including friction and forces through the axle. *(Guide: "Positive, negative and zero work"; Practice Q3)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate the work done by a constant torque, converting revolutions or degrees to radians first. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can find the work done by a changing torque from the area under a torque–angle graph, using rectangles, triangles and trapeziums. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Figure 2; Practice Q4, Q6)*
- I can use W_net = ½Iω² − ½Iω₀² to find a final angular velocity, including an object that is already spinning. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can derive a symbolic expression, such as a stopping angle Iω₀² / (2τ_f), and predict factors of change from it. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q2, Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can plan an experiment that measures torque at known angles, plot the torque–angle graph with suitable scales and find the work from it. *(Guide: "Planning an experiment"; Practice Q6)*
- I can justify a claim comparing two spin-ups by comparing the work done, not just the torque or angular acceleration. *(Practice Q7)*
