Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-9-circular-motion-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/2-9-circular-motion-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why an object moving in a circle at constant speed is accelerating, and say which way the acceleration points. *(Guide: "Why moving in a circle is accelerated motion"; Figure 1(a))*
- I can explain that real forces or their components supply the net inward force, with no separate "centripetal force" arrow. *(Guide: "Where does the inward force come from?")*
- I can tell centripetal acceleration (changes direction) apart from tangential acceleration (changes speed). *(Guide: Figure 1(b); Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why gravity alone provides the inward force at the top of a loop at the minimum speed, and in a circular orbit. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4, Q7)*

## Skills

- I can calculate a_c = v²/r, and convert between period, frequency and speed with T = 1/f and T = 2πr/v. *(Guide: "Period and frequency"; Practice Q1)*
- I can find the net acceleration of an object that is speeding up or slowing down on a circle. *(Guide: "Tangential acceleration and the net acceleration")*
- I can apply Newton's second law towards the centre at the top of a loop or a hump, and find the minimum or maximum speed. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can find the no-friction speed or bank angle for a banked curve, and the tension and period of a conical pendulum. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 3; Practice Q5)*
- I can derive T² = (4π²/GM) r³ for a circular orbit and use it to find a central mass or compare two orbits. *(Guide: "Circular orbits and Kepler's third law"; Practice Q2, Q7)*
- I can choose a linear graph (such as F against v²) to test a circular-motion relationship and use its slope. *(Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict how a_c or T changes when speed, radius or period changes, and say which quantity is being held constant. *(Guide: "Period and frequency"; Practice Q2, Q5(c))*
- I can describe the direction of static friction on a banked curve when a vehicle goes faster or slower than the design speed. *(Guide: "Banked curves"; Practice Q5(b))*
- I can argue that an orbiting object's period does not depend on its own mass. *(Practice Q7)*
