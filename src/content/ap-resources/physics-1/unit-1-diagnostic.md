This diagnostic is for the **algebra-based Physics 1 course**, Unit 1 (Kinematics). It shows you **which topics to revisit**. Allow about 30 minutes, then use the table at the end.

These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. They have not been calibrated against real exam results, so your total does not predict an exam score. Use a scientific calculator in degree mode. Use g = 9.8 m/s² and ignore air resistance. Every question states its axis: use it for every sign.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · 1.1)

Take **+x to the right**. A bead slides along a straight wire. Its three moves are drawn as arrows on a grid with the scale **1 square = 2.0 m**: 4 squares right, then 7 squares left, then 1 square right. Which arrow represents the bead's total displacement?

- (A) 2 squares long, pointing left: −4.0 m
- (B) 2 squares long, pointing right: +4.0 m
- (C) 12 squares long, pointing left: −24 m
- (D) 2 squares long, pointing left: −2.0 m

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Add the arrows tip to tail as signed numbers: (+4) + (−7) + (+1) = −2 squares. With 2.0 m per square, that is −4.0 m: 4.0 m to the left.

- (B) gets the size right but loses the sign. The long leftward move outweighs the two rightward moves.
- (C) adds the lengths of all three arrows. That is the distance travelled (24 m), a scalar, and it has no direction.
- (D) reads the answer in squares and forgets to apply the scale.

**If you missed this:** read "Adding vectors in one dimension" in the [Topic 1.1 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/1-1-scalars-vectors-one-dimension-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · 1.2)

Take **+x forward**. An electric scooter on a straight path has these velocities:

| t (s) | 0 | 1.0 | 5.0 | 6.0 | 8.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v_x (m/s) | +2.0 | +5.0 | −3.0 | −3.0 | −7.0 |

Over which interval is the **size** of the scooter's average acceleration greatest?

- (A) 0 to 1.0 s
- (B) 1.0 to 5.0 s
- (C) 5.0 to 6.0 s
- (D) 6.0 to 8.0 s

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Average acceleration is Δv_x ÷ Δt. The four values are (+3.0) ÷ 1.0 = +3.0 m/s², (−8.0) ÷ 4.0 = −2.0 m/s², 0 ÷ 1.0 = 0 and (−4.0) ÷ 2.0 = −2.0 m/s². The largest size is 3.0 m/s².

- (B) has the largest change in velocity (8.0 m/s), and the scooter reverses. But that change is spread over 4.0 s, so the rate is smaller.
- (C) has the most negative velocity at its start, but the velocity does not change, so the acceleration is zero.
- (D) is where the speed reaches its largest value, 7.0 m/s. A large speed is not a large acceleration.

**If you missed this:** read "Average acceleration" in the [Topic 1.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/1-2-displacement-velocity-acceleration-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · 1.3)

Take **+x to the right**. A cart's velocity–time graph is a straight line from +6.0 m/s at t = 0 to −2.0 m/s at t = 4.0 s. During these 4.0 s, what is the **greatest** distance between the cart and its starting point, and when does it happen?

- (A) 9.0 m, at t = 3.0 s
- (B) 8.0 m, at t = 4.0 s
- (C) 10 m, at t = 4.0 s
- (D) 16 m, at t = 4.0 s

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** The slope is (−2.0 − 6.0) ÷ 4.0 = −2.0 m/s², so v_x reaches zero at t = 3.0 s. Up to then the cart moves right. The area of that triangle is ½ × 3.0 × 6.0 = +9.0 m. After 3.0 s the cart moves back left (area −1.0 m), so it never gets farther than 9.0 m from its start.

- (B) is the net displacement at the end, 9.0 − 1.0 = 8.0 m. The cart was farther away at 3.0 s.
- (C) is the total distance travelled, 9.0 + 1.0 m. Distance is not separation from the start.
- (D) treats the area below the axis as positive and uses the whole trapezium, ½(6.0 + 2.0) × 4.0.

**If you missed this:** read "Graphs: slopes and areas" in the [Topic 1.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/1-3-representing-motion-study-guide/). The cart turns round where the line crosses the time axis.
</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · 1.3)

Take **+x down the slope**. A sled starts from rest and slides with constant acceleration. It covers 2.0 m in the first 1.0 s. How far does it travel during the **next** 1.0 s (from t = 1.0 s to t = 2.0 s)?

- (A) 2.0 m
- (B) 4.0 m
- (C) 6.0 m
- (D) 8.0 m

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** From rest, x = ½a_x t². So 2.0 = ½a_x(1.0)², which gives a_x = 4.0 m/s². At 2.0 s, x = ½(4.0)(2.0)² = 8.0 m. During the second second it travels 8.0 − 2.0 = 6.0 m.

- (A) assumes constant velocity, but the sled is speeding up.
- (B) assumes distance grows in proportion to time. With constant acceleration from rest, it grows with t².
- (D) is the total distance after 2.0 s, not the distance in the second interval alone.

**If you missed this:** read "Where the constant-acceleration equations come from" in the [Topic 1.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/1-3-representing-motion-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 5 (multiple choice · 1.4)

Take **+x east**, positions measured from a road sign. At t = 0 a bus is at x = +40 m moving east at 12 m/s, and a cyclist is at the sign moving east at 8.0 m/s. Both keep constant velocity. At t = 5.0 s, what is the **bus's position measured by the cyclist**?

- (A) +60 m
- (B) +100 m
- (C) +140 m
- (D) −60 m

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** At 5.0 s the bus is at 40 + 12 × 5.0 = 100 m and the cyclist is at 8.0 × 5.0 = 40 m, both measured from the sign. So x_bus,cyclist = 100 − 40 = +60 m: 60 m ahead. In the cyclist's frame the bus gains 4.0 m/s, and 40 + 4.0 × 5.0 = 60 m agrees.

- (B) is the bus's position measured from the sign. It ignores the observer's own motion.
- (C) adds the two velocities, which is only right when the bus and cyclist move in opposite directions.
- (D) is the cyclist's position measured by the bus. The subtraction is the wrong way round.

**If you missed this:** read "Converting positions and displacements" in the [Topic 1.4 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/1-4-reference-frames-relative-motion-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 6 (multiple choice · 1.5)

Take **+x horizontal and +y up**. A ball is launched from level ground with v_x0 = 6.0 m/s and v_y0 = 8.0 m/s. What is its **speed** 1.0 s after launch?

- (A) 10 m/s
- (B) 6.3 m/s
- (C) 6.0 m/s
- (D) 1.8 m/s

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** Treat each axis on its own. v_x stays 6.0 m/s. v_y = 8.0 − 9.8 × 1.0 = −1.8 m/s, so the ball is already falling. Speed = √(6.0² + 1.8²) = 6.3 m/s.

- (A) is the launch speed, √(6.0² + 8.0²). It assumes the speed never changes.
- (C) is the speed at the top, where v_y = 0. The top came earlier, at 8.0 ÷ 9.8 ≈ 0.82 s.
- (D) uses only the vertical component and forgets the horizontal motion.

**If you missed this:** read "Projectile motion" and Worked example 3 in the [Topic 1.5 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/1-5-vectors-motion-two-dimensions-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 7 (multiple choice · 1.5)

Take **+x east and +y north**, looking down on an ice rink. A puck's velocity has components v_x = −4.0 m/s and v_y = −3.0 m/s. Which describes its velocity?

- (A) 5.0 m/s at 37° south of west
- (B) 5.0 m/s at 37° north of east
- (C) 7.0 m/s at 37° south of west
- (D) 5.0 m/s at 53° south of west

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Size: √(4.0² + 3.0²) = 5.0 m/s. Both components are negative, so a sketch puts the vector in the south-west quadrant. The angle below the west direction is tan⁻¹(3.0 ÷ 4.0) = 37°.

- (B) trusts the calculator: tan⁻¹((−3.0) ÷ (−4.0)) also gives 37°, but the two minus signs cancel and hide the quadrant. Always sketch.
- (C) adds the sizes of the components. Perpendicular components combine by Pythagoras.
- (D) uses tan⁻¹(4.0 ÷ 3.0), which measures the angle from the south direction. "53° west of south" would be right; "53° south of west" is not.

**If you missed this:** read "Rebuilding a vector from its components" in the [Topic 1.5 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/1-5-vectors-motion-two-dimensions-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 8 (short answer · 1.3)

Take **+x along the track**. A sprinter starts from rest, speeds up with constant acceleration to 9.0 m/s in 2.5 s, then runs at a steady 9.0 m/s.

(a) Describe the velocity–time and acceleration–time graphs for the first 6.0 s, with values.
(b) Find the time the sprinter takes to run 60 m.
(c) A student uses x = ½a_x t² for the whole 60 m with a_x = 3.6 m/s² and gets 5.8 s. Explain the mistake.

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**(a)** v_x–t: a straight line from 0 to 9.0 m/s at 2.5 s, then a horizontal line at 9.0 m/s. a_x–t: flat at 9.0 ÷ 2.5 = 3.6 m/s² from 0 to 2.5 s, then zero.

**(b)** Stage 1 distance = triangle area = ½ × 2.5 × 9.0 = 11.25 m. The remaining 60 − 11.25 = 48.75 m takes 48.75 ÷ 9.0 = 5.42 s. Total **7.9 s**.

**(c)** The equation needs constant acceleration over the whole interval. Here a_x = 3.6 m/s² only for the first 2.5 s and zero afterwards. Using it for the whole run assumes the sprinter keeps speeding up, which would mean reaching about 21 m/s.

Check yourself: 1 point for each graph with values, 1 for splitting the run into two stages, 1 for 7.9 s, 1 for the constant-acceleration condition in (c).

**If you missed this:** work through Figure 2 and the equations table in the [Topic 1.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/1-3-representing-motion-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 9 (short answer · 1.5)

Take **+x horizontal towards a wall and +y up**, origin at the launch point. A ball is kicked from the ground at 14 m/s, 35° above the horizontal, towards a vertical wall 10 m away.

(a) Find how long the ball takes to reach the wall.
(b) Find the height at which it hits the wall.
(c) State whether the ball is rising or falling when it hits, with a reason.

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**(a)** v_x0 = 14 cos 35° = 11.47 m/s, and v_x never changes. t = 10 ÷ 11.47 = **0.87 s**.

**(b)** v_y0 = 14 sin 35° = 8.03 m/s. y = v_y0 t − ½gt² = 8.03(0.872) − 4.9(0.872)² = 7.00 − 3.73 = **3.3 m**.

**(c)** v_y = 8.03 − 9.8(0.872) = −0.52 m/s. The sign is negative, so the ball is **just falling**: it passed its highest point at 8.03 ÷ 9.8 = 0.82 s, a moment before reaching the wall.

Check yourself: 1 point for using only the horizontal motion to find t, 1 for 3.3 m, 1 for the sign of v_y as the reason in (c). A common error is y = v_y0 t = 7.0 m, which leaves out gravity.

**If you missed this:** read "Motion in two dimensions: one problem becomes two" in the [Topic 1.5 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/1-5-vectors-motion-two-dimensions-study-guide/).
</details>
