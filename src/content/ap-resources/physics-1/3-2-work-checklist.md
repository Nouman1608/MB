Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/3-2-work-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/3-2-work-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can describe work as energy transferred into or out of a system by a force exerted over a distance. *(Guide: "Work is an energy transfer")*
- I can explain why a force perpendicular to the motion does no work but can still change the direction of motion. *(Guide: sign table; Practice Q5)*
- I can tell a conservative force from a nonconservative one, and explain why gravity's work does not depend on the path. *(Guide: "Conservative and nonconservative forces"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain where the energy removed by friction goes, without calculating thermal energy. *(Guide: "Where friction's energy goes")*

## Calculation

- I can calculate the work done by a constant force at an angle using W = Fd cos θ. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can find the work done by each force on an object and add them to get the net work. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can use the work-energy theorem, ΔK = W_net, to find a final speed. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can find work from the area under a force–position graph, counting area below the axis as negative. *(Guide: Figure 2, Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate the energy dissipated by friction using the path length, not the displacement. *(Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning and experiments

- I can predict how stopping distance changes when the initial speed changes. *(Guide: Worked example 3)*
- I can design an experiment to test the work-energy theorem and use a v²–d graph to analyse it. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why a wall does no work on a person who pushes off it, and when a system can be modelled as an object. *(Guide: "Where the force acts"; Practice Q7)*
