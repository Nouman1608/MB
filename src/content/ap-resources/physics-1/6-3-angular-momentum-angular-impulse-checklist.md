## Understanding

- I can explain how angular momentum, angular impulse and the impulse–momentum theorem mirror their linear partners. *(Guide: "The idea: a rotational version of momentum")*
- I can explain why an object moving in a straight line has angular momentum about a point off its path, and none about a point on it. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why the value of angular momentum depends on the axis or point I choose, and I always state it. *(Guide: Figure 1 table)*
- I can state a positive sense of rotation and give every L, τ and angular impulse a sign. *(Guide: "Signs, not directions"; Practice Q4)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate L = Iω for a rigid body. *(Guide: "Angular momentum of a rigid body")*
- I can calculate L = rmv sin θ, or mv times the perpendicular distance, for a moving object about a point. *(Practice Q1, Q7)*
- I can compare angular impulses by multiplying torque by time, building each torque from force × lever arm. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can find an angular impulse from the area under a torque–time graph, including part of the area up to a chosen time. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can use τ_net Δt = L − L₀ to find a final angular speed, remembering to include L₀. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can read the net torque from the slope of an angular momentum–time graph. *(Guide: "Reading an angular momentum–time graph"; Practice Q3)*

## Reasoning

- I can derive the rotational impulse–momentum theorem from τ_net = Iα for constant I. *(Guide: "The rotational impulse–momentum theorem")*
- I can derive a symbolic expression, such as a stopping time or final angular speed, and check its units and limiting cases. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can predict factors of change, such as what happens to ω when R or I is changed. *(Guide: "Factors of change"; Practice Q6)*
- I can justify why an object moving at constant velocity keeps the same angular momentum about any fixed point. *(Practice Q7)*
