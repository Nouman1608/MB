Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, diagrams and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-1/5-1-rotational-kinematics-study-guide/). This is the algebra-based course: no calculus is needed.

## Recap

- A spinning body is a **rigid system**: it keeps its shape, but its points move in different directions. It cannot be treated as one point while its rotation matters.
- If the rotation does not affect the question, you may still model the body as an object at its centre of mass.
- Describe rotation about a stated **axis**, from a stated **reference line**, with **clockwise or counterclockwise** chosen as positive.
- All points on a rigid body share the same Δθ, ω and α.
- Instantaneous ω (or α) is the average over a very short interval, or the tangent slope on the graph.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Δθ = θ − θ₀ | rad | find angular displacement (signed) |
| ω_avg = Δθ / Δt | rad/s | find average angular velocity |
| α_avg = Δω / Δt | rad/s² | find average angular acceleration |
| ω = ω₀ + αt | | constant α, no Δθ |
| θ = θ₀ + ω₀t + ½αt² | | constant α, no final ω |
| ω² = ω₀² + 2α(θ − θ₀) | | constant α, no time |
| slope of θ–t graph | rad/s | angular velocity |
| slope of ω–t graph; area under it | rad/s²; rad | angular acceleration; angular displacement |
| 1 rev = 2π rad = 360° | | converting units |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The body is **rigid** and turns about **one fixed axis**.
- The three equations need **constant α**. If α changes in stages, treat each stage separately.
- Directions are only clockwise or counterclockwise about the stated axis.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **No rotation sense stated.** A negative ω means nothing without one.
2. **rpm or degrees in the equations.** Convert to rad/s and rad first.
3. **Negative α = slowing down.** Compare the signs of ω and α.
4. **Different ω for different points on one body.** ω is shared; only linear speed changes with radius (Topic 5.2).
5. **Resetting θ to zero after each turn.** Count all the turns.
6. **Linear scaling for squared quantities.** Doubling ω₀ makes the stopping angle four times larger.

## Quick self-check

1. A fan blade turns 2.5 rev in 0.50 s. What is its average angular velocity? *(10π ≈ 31 rad/s)*
2. Counterclockwise positive. ω changes from +6.0 rad/s to −3.0 rad/s in 1.5 s. Find α_avg. *(−6.0 rad/s²)*
3. Convert 45 rpm to rad/s. *(about 4.7 rad/s)*
4. ω = −8.0 rad/s, α = +2.0 rad/s². Speeding up or slowing down? *(Slowing down: opposite signs.)*
