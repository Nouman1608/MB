Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/7-5-coordination-and-subordination-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/7-5-coordination-and-subordination-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between coordination and subordination. *(Guide: "Two ways to join ideas"; Practice question 1)*
- I can explain how coordination shows balance or equality and subordination shows imbalance or inequality. *(Guide: "What each structure says about the ideas"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain why swapping a conjunction can change a writer's stance. *(Guide: "Why the choice is part of the argument"; Practice question 4)*

## Skills

- I can find coordinating and subordinating words in a sentence. *(Guide: "Two ways to join ideas"; Practice questions 1 and 5, which also tests joining clauses correctly)*
- I can name the relationship a structure creates: addition, contrast, cause, concession, condition. *(Guide: table in "What each structure says about the ideas"; Practice question 3)*
- I can rewrite a sentence so that coordination or subordination serves a stated purpose. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how a writer's structure shapes the reader's view of the subject. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 6)*
- I can analyse the effect of coordination in a public-domain sentence, such as Lincoln's on the Bible and prayer. *(Guide: "Public-domain examples"; Practice question 8)*
- I can go beyond naming the conjunction and explain what it does for the writer's purpose. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice question 8)*
