## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between an independent clause, a dependent clause and a phrase. *(Guide: "Sentences are made of clauses"; Practice question 1)*
- I can explain why the end of a sentence is usually the most emphatic position. *(Guide: "Why arrangement matters"; Practice question 3)*
- I can explain how loose, periodic and balanced sentences differ in structure and effect. *(Guide: "Three arrangements to recognise"; Practice question 4)*

## Skills

- I can classify a sentence as simple, compound, complex or compound-complex. *(Guide: "Four sentence types"; Practice questions 2 and 5)*
- I can mark the clauses in a sentence by finding verbs and their subjects. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 6)*
- I can rearrange a sentence to put a chosen idea in the strongest position. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain what a writer's arrangement of clauses emphasises and why, in relation to the writer's purpose. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 8)*
- I can use a short public-domain example, such as the Declaration's opening sentence or a line from Gettysburg, to explain periodic or end-weighted structure. *(Guide: "Real examples (public domain)")*
- I can avoid analysis that only labels a sentence's type without stating its effect. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*
