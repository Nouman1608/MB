## Recap

**Exigence** is what prompts a text: an event, a problem, a question or a need. It looks back or around. **Purpose** is what the writer wants the text to achieve: it looks forward and is best written as a verb plus a target ("persuade the council to reverse the cut"). A text can have several purposes, so decide which is primary. If an exigence is not stated, infer it from dates, references and the way the text answers an existing question.

## Key relationships

| Idea | Test sentence | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Exigence | "Because ___, the writer wrote." | Because a leak closed the car park. |
| Purpose | "The writer wants the audience to ___." | Park elsewhere and keep the road clear. |
| Primary purpose | The main effect sought; gets most space and direct language. | Change parking behaviour. |
| Secondary purpose | A supporting effect, often carried by tone. | Keep goodwill. |

## Chain to remember

**Exigence** (what is wrong or has happened) leads to a **purpose** (what should change) which leads to **choices** (the evidence, tone and structure used to bring it about).

## Worked mini-example

Paine, *The American Crisis* (1776). Exigence: the military crisis of late 1776, with defeats and expiring enlistments. Purpose: to rally discouraged soldiers and citizens to keep serving, by shaming those who shrink and honouring those who stand. Evidence in the text: "in this crisis"; "shrink from the service of their country"; "he that stands it now, deserves the love and thanks of man and woman".

Douglass, "What to the Slave Is the Fourth of July?" (5 July 1852). Exigence: an invitation to speak at a gathering for the national holiday. Purpose: to make an audience celebrating liberty face the existence of slavery and be moved against it.

## Quick checks for your own writing

- Can I say my exigence in one sentence that begins with "Because"?
- Does my purpose have a verb and a target audience?
- Have I said which purpose is primary and which is secondary?
- If the exigence is implicit, have I pointed to the clue?

## Mistakes to avoid

- Writing a topic ("to discuss the flood") as a purpose.
- Writing the writer's goal as the exigence, or the trigger as the purpose.
- Listing exigence and purpose but never showing how the text's choices follow from them.
- Using vague verbs such as discuss, talk about, explore.
- Ignoring implicit exigence because the text never says "I am writing because".

## Quick self-check

1. A town votes to close a pool. A resident writes to the paper. What is the exigence? **The vote to close the pool.**
2. What is a better version of "The purpose is to discuss litter"? **"To persuade readers to join the beach clean-up by showing what the beach looked like after the storm."**
3. In the Elm Court notice, which is the primary purpose? **To make residents stop using the car park and keep Elm Road clear. Reassurance is secondary.**
4. A date, a place and a publication name are given before a passage. Which components might they supply? **Context, and often the exigence.**
5. Does "the war was going badly" state an exigence or a purpose? **An exigence.**
