Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/4-4-comparison-contrast-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/4-4-comparison-contrast-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what a category of comparison is and why it is chosen first. *(Guide: "Categories and like categories"; Practice question 1)*
- I can explain the rule of like categories. *(Guide: "Categories and like categories"; Practice question 2)*
- I can describe point-by-point and block organisation and say when each suits. *(Guide: "Two ways to organise"; Practice question 3)*
- I can explain why a comparison must lead to a claim. *(Guide: "Comparison must lead to a claim"; Practice question 5)*

## Skills

- I can identify the category of comparison and the objects in a passage. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 1)*
- I can describe the effect of a contrast in a published text. *(Guide: "Comparison in a published text"; Practice question 4)*
- I can repair a comparison that uses unlike categories. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 4; Practice question 6)*
- I can write a comparison paragraph that uses like categories and ends in a claim. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 7)*

## Reasoning

- I can choose categories that are relevant to my claim. *(Guide: "Categories and like categories"; Practice question 7)*
- I can choose between point-by-point and block and give a reason. *(Guide: "Two ways to organise"; Practice questions 3 and 7)*
- I can explain what a comparison contributes to a writer's purpose. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 3; Practice question 8)*
- I can write a one-sentence analysis of a comparison in a text. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 4; Practice question 8)*
