Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/8-6-modifiers-and-parenthetical-elements-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/8-6-modifiers-and-parenthetical-elements-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain how modifiers qualify, clarify or specify information. *(Guide: "What modifiers do"; Practice question 1)*
- I can explain why a modifier should sit closest to what it modifies. *(Guide: "Keep modifiers close to what they modify"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain what makes an element parenthetical and how commas, dashes and parentheses differ in effect. *(Guide: "Parenthetical elements"; Practice question 3)*

## Skills

- I can repair misplaced, dangling and squinting modifiers. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice questions 4 and 6)*
- I can tell essential from non-essential modifiers and punctuate them correctly. *(Guide: "Restrictive and non-restrictive modifiers"; Practice question 5)*
- I can add a parenthetical element to a sentence to give background, a qualification or a personal voice. *(Guide: "Why writers use parenthetical elements"; Practice question 7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain what a parenthetical element adds and how it serves the audience's needs or the writer's purpose. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 7)*
- I can analyse how modifiers and asides shape tone and credibility in a passage. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 8)*
- I can analyse a short public-domain sentence, such as Lincoln's on the brave men, for modifiers and parenthetical elements. *(Guide: "A public-domain example"; Practice question 8)*
