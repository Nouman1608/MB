## Recap

- A writer's **style** is made up of the **mix of word choice, syntax and conventions** that the writer employs.
- **Tone** is the attitude conveyed toward the subject or audience; style is the means, tone is the result.
- Describe style with the pattern **label + evidence + effect**.
- Style is chosen for a **purpose** and an **audience**; the same content can be written in many styles.
- Words, comparisons and syntax all contribute to a specific tone or style, and writers can use them strategically to convey the tone they want.

## Key relationships

| Part of style | What to look at |
|---|---|
| Word choice | Plain or elaborate; concrete or abstract; formal, informal, technical; connotation |
| Syntax | Sentence length; simple or complex; loose, periodic, balanced; repetition; fragments |
| Conventions | Punctuation habits; person (I, you, they); contractions; paragraph length |

| Label | Typical features |
|---|---|
| Plain | Short common words, direct statements |
| Formal | Abstract vocabulary, complex sentences, no contractions |
| Conversational | Contractions, "you", questions, informal words |
| Aphoristic | Compressed, balanced, maxim-like |
| Ornate | Elaborate vocabulary, long sentences, imagery |
| Restrained | Understatement, controlled emotion |
| Urgent | Short sentences, repetition, imperatives |

## Public-domain anchors

- **Francis Bacon (1625)**: "Reading maketh a full man; conference a ready man; and writing an exact man." Balanced, compressed and aphoristic: the repeated pattern and omitted verbs give it the sound of a maxim.
- **Ralph Waldo Emerson (1841)**: "A foolish consistency is the hobgoblin of little minds, adored by little statesmen and philosophers and divines." Witty metaphor and repeated "little" make the tone ironic and confident.

## Worked mini-example

**Marlbridge passage:** "The lights flickered. Then they died. In the dark hall, forty voices fell silent."

- **Label:** plain and tense.
- **Evidence:** three short sentences; common words; no figurative language; the final sentence extends slightly to widen the scene.
- **Effect:** the short sentences imitate the abruptness of a power cut and build suspense.
- **Analysis sentence:** "By using short, plain sentences, the writer mimics the sudden loss of light, creating a tense and immediate tone."

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Labelling without evidence** ("the style is powerful").
2. **Confusing style with tone.**
3. **Equating style with decoration.**
4. **Ignoring conventions** such as person, contractions and punctuation habits.
5. **Using one adjective for a whole passage** when the style shifts.
6. **Forgetting the effect** on the audience.

## Quick self-check

1. What three things make up style? *(Word choice, syntax and conventions.)*
2. What is the difference between style and tone? *(Style is the manner created by the writer's choices; tone is the attitude it conveys.)*
3. Label and justify: "Right. Here's the plan. Three steps. Easy." *(Conversational and brisk: contractions, fragments and short imperative sentences.)*
