Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/1-4-claims-need-defence-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/1-4-claims-need-defence-practice/); also see the [revision notes](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/1-4-claims-need-defence-revision-notes/).

## Understanding

- ☐ I can define a claim and explain why it requires a defence. *(Guide: "What is a claim?")*
- ☐ I can explain how a claim differs from a fact, a topic and a preference. *(Guide: "Claim, fact, topic, preference"; practice questions 1 and 2)*
- ☐ I can name the five kinds of claim and what each must be supported with. *(Guide: "Kinds of claim"; practice question 5)*

## Skills

- ☐ I can pick the claim out of a short passage and quote it. *(Practice questions 4 and 6)*
- ☐ I can rewrite an obvious statement as a defensible claim. *(Guide: Worked example 2; practice questions 3 and 7)*
- ☐ I can sort statements into fact, topic, preference and claim. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
- ☐ I can state a text's main claim in one sentence and list its sub-claims. *(Guide: "Explicit and implicit claims")*

## Reasoning

- ☐ I can explain why a claim could be disputed by a reasonable reader. *(Practice questions 6 and 8)*
- ☐ I can explain why a true statement can still be a claim. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*
- ☐ I can judge whether a claim is specific and supportable enough. *(Guide: "Making a claim defensible and interesting")*
