Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/7-3-qualifying-claims-with-modifiers-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/7-3-qualifying-claims-with-modifiers-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain how modifiers qualify or limit the scope of a claim. *(Guide: "What a modifier does to a claim"; Practice question 1)*
- I can name the dimensions of scope: frequency, amount, group, place or time, condition, certainty and concession. *(Guide: "The dimensions of scope"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain why the strength of a modifier should match the strength of the evidence. *(Guide: "Strength: matching the modifier to the evidence"; Practice question 4)*

## Skills

- I can pick out words, phrases and clauses that work as modifiers in a claim. *(Guide: "Words, phrases and clauses as modifiers"; Practice questions 1 and 5)*
- I can revise an overstated claim using modifiers while keeping a position. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice questions 3 and 6)*
- I can write three versions of a claim for three levels of evidence. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 7)*
- I can avoid burying a claim under too many modifiers. *(Guide: "Keeping the position visible"; Practice question 3)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain what a particular modifier limits and why the writer chose it. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 8)*
- I can say how modifiers affect the credibility of an argument. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 8)*
- I can explain how a public-domain writer, such as Lincoln in the House Divided passage, uses a verb to signal the strength of a claim. *(Guide: "A public-domain example")*
