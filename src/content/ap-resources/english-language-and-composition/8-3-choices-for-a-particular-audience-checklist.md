Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/8-3-choices-for-a-particular-audience-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/8-3-choices-for-a-particular-audience-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why audiences are unique and dynamic. *(Guide: "Audiences are unique and dynamic"; Practice question 1)*
- I can name the audience features a writer must consider: perspective, context and needs. *(Guide: "Audiences are unique and dynamic"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain why audience affects evidence and organisation as well as tone. *(Guide: "One claim, three audiences"; Practice question 3)*

## Skills

- I can choose evidence that suits a named audience. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 4)*
- I can plan an order of points for a hostile, neutral or supportive audience. *(Guide: "Strategies for hostile, neutral and supportive audiences"; Practice question 5)*
- I can adapt the same claim for two different audiences. *(Guide: "One claim, three audiences"; Practice question 7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how a passage's evidence, organisation and language reflect its intended audience. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 6)*
- I can base claims about an audience on clues in the text rather than invention. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice question 6)*
- I can analyse a short public-domain example, such as Anthony's "we, the whole people", in terms of audience. *(Guide: "A public-domain example"; Practice question 8)*
