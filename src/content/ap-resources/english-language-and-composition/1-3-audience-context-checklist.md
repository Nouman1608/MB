Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/1-3-audience-context-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/1-3-audience-context-practice/); also see the [revision notes](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/1-3-audience-context-revision-notes/).

## Understanding

- ☐ I can define audience and context and give an example of each. *(Guide: "Why writers think about readers first"; "Context: time, place and occasion")*
- ☐ I can explain the difference between a primary and a secondary audience. *(Guide: "Types of audience"; practice question 1)*
- ☐ I can explain why a divided audience complicates a writer's choices. *(Guide: Washington example; practice question 8)*

## Skills

- ☐ I can find clues to the audience in a passage (address, assumed knowledge, pronouns, objections). *(Guide: "Evidence of audience in the text"; practice questions 1 and 3)*
- ☐ I can describe time, place and occasion for a text from its setting. *(Guide: "Context: time, place and occasion"; practice question 2)*
- ☐ I can explain the effect of a pronoun such as "we". *(Practice question 4)*
- ☐ I can choose a revision that suits a named audience. *(Practice question 5)*

## Reasoning

- ☐ I can explain how the same message changes for two audiences. *(Guide: Worked example 1; practice questions 6 and 7)*
- ☐ I can explain how context changes the force of a sentence. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- ☐ I can explain how an ambiguous image can be heard differently by two groups. *(Practice question 8)*
