## Recap

- **Evidence and sources will either support, complement or contradict a writer's thesis.**
- **Supporting** evidence backs the claim directly. **Complementary** evidence adds context, a related example or another angle that extends it. **Contradictory** evidence cuts against it.
- Contradictory evidence is not something to hide: **qualify, concede, rebut or refute**.
- Name the relationship, use evidence with commentary, and keep the source's claim distinct from your own.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Job in the argument | Typical commentary |
|---|---|---|
| Supports | Proves the claim | "This shows that..." |
| Complements | Extends or deepens | "This matters because it also..." |
| Contradicts | Tests the claim | "This is true, but..." or "This overlooks..." |

| Response to contradiction | Move |
|---|---|
| Qualify | Narrow my claim so the evidence no longer contradicts it |
| Concede | Accept all or part of the evidence and say what follows |
| Rebut | Offer a contrasting view or alternative evidence |
| Refute | Demonstrate with evidence that the contradicting claim is invalid |

## Checking a source

1. **What does it claim**, and how strong is its evidence?
2. **Who produced it, and why?**
3. **Does it bear directly, partly or loosely on my thesis?**
4. **Which role does it play?** (support, complement, contradict)

## Public-domain anchor

**Henry David Thoreau, *Walden* (1854)**: "I went to the woods because I wished to live deliberately, to front only the essential facts of life..." First-hand experience offered as evidence for his claim about simple living. It is vivid and specific, though a critic could say that one person's experiment is limited as evidence of what is typical.

## Worked mini-example

**Thesis (Marlbridge):** "Schools should start later."

- **Supports:** "Pupils in a pilot reported 40 more minutes of sleep."
- **Complements:** "Teachers noted that first lessons were calmer."
- **Contradicts:** "Bus operators said later starts raised costs."

**Use:** lead with the sleep data and comment on its effect on learning; use the calmer lessons to broaden the case; concede the cost and qualify the claim ("provided the transport costs can be met").

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Treating all evidence as support.**
2. **Dropping in a quotation without commentary.**
3. **Ignoring strong contradictory evidence.**
4. **Using a complement as if it proved the main claim.**
5. **Using evidence that does not bear on the thesis.**
6. **Mixing the source's claim with your own interpretation.**

## Quick self-check

1. Does "A second study found the same effect in another city" support, complement or contradict the thesis? *(It supports: it bears directly on the claim.)*
2. How is a complementary source different from a supporting one? *(It adds context or a new angle rather than directly proving the claim.)*
3. Name two ways to respond to contradictory evidence. *(Any two: qualify, concede, rebut, refute.)*
