Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/8-5-irony-and-complex-perspectives-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/8-5-irony-and-complex-perspectives-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain how irony can arise from the difference between an argument and readers' expectations or values. *(Guide: "When words mean more than they say"; Practice question 1)*
- I can distinguish verbal irony, understatement, overstatement, situational irony and satire. *(Guide: "Kinds of irony you will meet"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain why irony depends on shared knowledge and can be risky. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 5; Practice question 4)*

## Skills

- I can identify signals of irony: incongruity, exaggeration, understatement, clash with values and mock-formal diction. *(Guide: "Signals of irony"; Practice question 3)*
- I can state the surface meaning and the intended meaning of an ironic passage. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 5)*
- I can write a short ironic passage whose signals are clear. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain the purpose of irony in a passage and its effect on the audience. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 6)*
- I can explain how a writer signals a complex perspective with competing attitudes. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 6)*
- I can analyse a short public-domain example, such as Johnson's definition of oats or Austen's opening line, for irony. *(Guide: "Public-domain examples"; Practice question 8)*
