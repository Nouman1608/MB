Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-4-transitional-elements-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-4-transitional-elements-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what a transitional element is and that it can be a word, phrase, clause, sentence or paragraph. *(Guide: "What a transition does"; Practice question 5)*
- I can name the relationships transitions signal. *(Guide: "Relationships and the words that signal them"; Practice question 1)*
- I can explain why a transition must match the logic of the ideas. *(Guide: "Matching the transition to the logic"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain how transitions introduce evidence and show its relation to other evidence. *(Guide: "Transitions that introduce and relate evidence"; Practice question 5)*

## Skills

- I can identify the relationship a transition signals in a passage. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice questions 1 and 4)*
- I can choose the right transition for a given relationship. *(Guide: "Matching the transition to the logic"; Practice questions 1 and 2)*
- I can add transitions to a paragraph whose ideas are linked but unmarked. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 6)*
- I can write a sentence that links two paragraphs by looking back and forward. *(Guide: "Bigger transitions"; Practice questions 3 and 8)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how transitions guide a reader through a line of reasoning. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 3; Practice question 7)*
- I can spot a transition that misleads and repair it. *(Guide: "Matching the transition to the logic"; Practice question 2)*
- I can judge when a text needs more or fewer transitions. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 5; Practice question 6)*
- I can write a one-sentence analysis of how transitions create coherence. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 4; Practice question 7)*
