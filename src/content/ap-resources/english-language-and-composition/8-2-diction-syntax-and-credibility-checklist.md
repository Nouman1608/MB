Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/8-2-diction-syntax-and-credibility-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/8-2-diction-syntax-and-credibility-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain how diction and syntax influence how a writer is perceived and whether an audience accepts an argument. *(Guide: "How words and sentences build a writer's image"; Practice question 1)*
- I can explain how word choice can reveal a writer's bias. *(Guide: "Bias and credibility"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain why credibility depends on the particular audience. *(Guide: "Bias and credibility"; Practice question 4)*

## Skills

- I can distinguish denotation from connotation and spot loaded words. *(Guide: "Diction: what to look at"; Practice question 3)*
- I can describe a passage's syntax in terms of length, structure, order and repetition. *(Guide: "Syntax: what to look at"; Practice question 5)*
- I can revise loaded or vague wording so that it protects credibility for a named audience. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain the effect of a passage's diction on the writer's credibility with a stated audience. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 6)*
- I can explain how syntax shapes the impression of a speaker's honesty and authority. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 6)*
- I can analyse a short public-domain example, such as Douglass's "yours, not mine", for diction and syntax. *(Guide: "Public-domain examples"; Practice question 8)*
