## Recap

- **Modifiers**, whether words, phrases or clauses, **qualify, clarify or specify** information about the thing they are attached to.
- To reduce ambiguity, place a modifier **closest to the word, phrase or clause it modifies**.
- **Parenthetical elements** are not essential to understanding what they describe; they **interrupt** the sentence to add information that may address an audience's needs and/or advance a writer's purpose.
- They are set off by **commas** (quiet), **dashes** (emphatic) or **parentheses** (quietest).

## Key relationships

| Problem or tool | Example | Repair or effect |
|---|---|---|
| Misplaced modifier | "She served sandwiches to the guests on paper plates." | "She served sandwiches on paper plates to the guests." |
| Dangling modifier | "Walking home, the rain began." | "Walking home, I felt the rain begin." |
| Squinting modifier | "The teacher said on Friday she would resign." | Decide the meaning; move "on Friday" |
| Essential (restrictive) | "Pupils who cheat lose marks." | No commas; identifies which pupils |
| Non-essential | "The pupils, who cheated, lost marks." | Commas; extra information about all of them |
| Appositive | "The mayor, a former teacher, spoke." | Renames or describes |
| Dash aside | "The plan—risky but bold—won." | Emphasis |
| Parenthesis | "The plan (risky) won." | Quiet optional comment |

## What a parenthetical element can do

1. **Add background.**
2. **Qualify** in passing.
3. **Add personal voice or tone.**
4. **Emphasise** by setting something apart.
5. **Concede** quietly ("though imperfect").

## Public-domain anchor

**Abraham Lincoln (1863)**: "The brave men, living and dead, who struggled here, have consecrated it, far above our poor power to add or detract." "Living and dead" widens the tribute to include survivors; "who struggled here" identifies the men; "far above our poor power to add or detract" is a modifier that sets the soldiers' deed against the speakers' modesty.

## Worked mini-example

**Marlbridge sentence:** "The new clinic, which opened in March, already treats two hundred patients a week."

1. **Parenthetical:** "which opened in March" (non-essential clause between commas).
2. **Function:** gives background so the audience sees how quickly the clinic has grown.
3. **Test:** remove it; the sentence still works.
4. **Analysis sentence:** "The aside about the opening month shows how quickly the clinic has become busy, which supports the writer's claim that it meets a real need."

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Leaving a modifier far from its target.**
2. **A dangling modifier** with no clear subject.
3. **Commas around essential information** (or none around non-essential).
4. **Overusing asides** until the main sentence is lost.
5. **Naming the punctuation without the function.**
6. **Treating dashes and parentheses as the same.**

## Quick self-check

1. Fix: "Running for the bus, the bag was left behind." *(For example: "Running for the bus, she left her bag behind.")*
2. Which is the parenthetical element in "The report, published last week, was criticised"? *(published last week)*
3. Which punctuation gives an aside the most emphasis: commas, dashes or parentheses? *(Dashes.)*
