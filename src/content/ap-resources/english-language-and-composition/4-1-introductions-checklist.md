Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/4-1-introductions-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/4-1-introductions-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can state the three jobs of an introduction: orient, engage and focus. *(Guide: "What does an introduction have to do?"; Practice question 1)*
- I can list the main opening strategies and say what each does well and what each risks. *(Guide: "The strategies available to a writer"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain why an introduction may, but need not, contain the thesis. *(Guide: "What does an introduction have to do?"; Practice question 5)*
- I can explain why announcing the essay's plan is weaker than presenting the subject and claim. *(Guide: "Weak and strong introductions side by side"; Practice question 4)*

## Skills

- I can name the strategy in a published introduction and describe its effect on the audience. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice questions 1 and 7)*
- I can diagnose a weak introduction against the three jobs. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 6)*
- I can rewrite an introduction so that it orients, engages and focuses. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 8)*
- I can write an opening sentence that uses a specific, relevant strategy rather than a generalisation. *(Guide: "Weak and strong introductions side by side"; Practice question 3)*

## Reasoning

- I can choose an opening strategy to suit a stated audience and purpose, and justify it. *(Guide: "Choosing by rhetorical situation"; Practice questions 2 and 8)*
- I can explain why a decorative quotation or an over-long anecdote weakens an argument. *(Guide: "The strategies available to a writer", "Common misconceptions"; Practice question 4)*
- I can write a one-sentence analysis of what an introduction does within the whole argument. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 4; Practice question 7)*
- I can check that my opener leads to my thesis rather than sitting beside it. *(Guide: "Where this leads"; Practice question 8)*
