Use this diagnostic to find **which Unit 9 topics to revisit**: at least one short question per topic.

These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. All passages are written by Marlbridge, and all details are invented for practice. The diagnostic is not calibrated and gives **no predicted score**.

**How to take it.** Allow 25 minutes and answer everything before opening any answer. No calculator is needed. The two written questions need only a few sentences each.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · 9.1)

A writer begins an essay on school uniforms without mentioning any of the arguments already made for or against them. What is the likely weakness?

- (A) The essay will be too short.
- (B) The essay does not enter the existing conversation, so it may repeat points already answered.
- (C) The essay will have too many sources.
- (D) The essay will not have a thesis.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** Entering a conversation means engaging the positions already argued.

- (A) Length is not the issue.
- (C) The writer has not used sources.
- (D) The essay may still have a thesis; the problem is that it ignores the debate.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 9.1 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/9-1-entering-an-ongoing-conversation-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · 9.1)

Which signal verb best describes a source that takes a premise for granted without proving it?

- (A) "demonstrates"
- (B) "assumes"
- (C) "concedes"
- (D) "illustrates"

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** To assume is to rely on an unproven premise.

- (A) means to show with evidence.
- (C) means to grant something against one's own side.
- (D) means to give an example.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 9.1 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/9-1-entering-an-ongoing-conversation-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · 9.2)

Thesis: "Free school breakfasts improve attendance." Which evidence is best described as **complementary**?

- (A) "Schools with free breakfasts had 6 per cent higher attendance."
- (B) "Free breakfasts also reduce the number of pupils who report headaches in the morning."
- (C) "In two schools, attendance fell after the scheme began."
- (D) "There are no breakfast schemes in the town."

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** It adds a related benefit that extends the case without directly proving the effect on attendance.

- (A) supports the thesis directly.
- (C) contradicts it, at least in part.
- (D) does not bear on the effect of breakfasts.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 9.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/9-2-evidence-that-supports-complements-or-contradicts-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · 9.3)

"Critics are right that the scheme cost more than planned; even so, it has saved lives." This sentence

- (A) concedes a portion of the opposing claim and then pivots back to the writer's claim.
- (B) refutes the critics with evidence.
- (C) ignores the critics.
- (D) concedes the entire thesis.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** "Critics are right that..." accepts a portion as correct, and "even so" is the pivot.

- (B) No evidence is used to demonstrate that the critics are wrong.
- (C) The critics are named and answered.
- (D) The writer keeps the claim that the scheme saved lives.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 9.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/9-3-conceding-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 5 (multiple choice · 9.4)

Which statement correctly defines **refuting**?

- (A) Demonstrating, using evidence, that all or part of a competing claim is invalid
- (B) Accepting a competing claim
- (C) Offering a new thesis with no reference to the competing claim
- (D) Repeating the writer's own claim more loudly

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** A refutation shows with evidence that a claim does not hold.

- (B) describes conceding.
- (C) ignores the competing claim.
- (D) repetition is not demonstration.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 9.4 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/9-4-rebutting-and-refuting-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 6 (multiple choice · 9.5)

Which statement about counterarguments is consistent with the course?

- (A) Transitions may be used to introduce counterarguments, and not all arguments explicitly address a counterargument.
- (B) Every argument must name an opposing view in its first sentence.
- (C) A counterargument may never appear in the middle of an essay.
- (D) Counterarguments should be invented if none is obvious.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Both statements are in the course framework.

- (B) overstates the requirement.
- (C) A counterargument can appear in the middle.
- (D) An invented objection is a strawman.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 9.5 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/9-5-introducing-counterarguments-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 7 (short answer · 9.3 and 9.4)

Opposing claim: "Online classes are always worse than in-person classes." Write one sentence that **concedes** part of the claim and one that **rebuts** the rest.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Sample.** *Concession:* "It is true that some pupils find online classes harder to concentrate in." *Rebuttal:* "But 'always' ignores that pupils who live far from school or who have health needs often do better with online options, so the question is which pupils the format suits."

**Marking guide.** Full credit for a concession that accepts something true and a rebuttal that offers a contrasting perspective or alternative evidence aimed at the absolute.

**If you missed this:** Topics [9.3](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/9-3-conceding-study-guide/) and [9.4](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/9-4-rebutting-and-refuting-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 8 (short answer · 9.1, 9.2 and 9.5)

Thesis: "Schools should teach financial skills." Name one objection a careful reader would raise, write one transition that introduces it, and say where you would put it in a timed essay.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Sample.** *Objection:* the timetable is already full, so adding financial skills would displace other subjects. *Transition:* "It might be objected that the timetable is already full." *Placement:* in the second body paragraph, after a first case for the thesis, answered briefly by conceding the pressure on time and showing that financial skills can be taught within existing maths lessons.

**Marking guide.** Full credit for a realistic objection, a transition that introduces it as an opposing view and a justified placement.

**If you missed this:** Topics [9.1](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/9-1-entering-an-ongoing-conversation-study-guide/), [9.2](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/9-2-evidence-that-supports-complements-or-contradicts-study-guide/) and [9.5](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/9-5-introducing-counterarguments-study-guide/).
</details>
