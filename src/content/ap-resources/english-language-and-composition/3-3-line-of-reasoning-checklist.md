Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/3-3-line-of-reasoning-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/3-3-line-of-reasoning-practice/); also see the [revision notes](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/3-3-line-of-reasoning-revision-notes/).

## Understanding

- ☐ I can define a line of reasoning and describe its parts. *(Guide: "What a line of reasoning is"; practice question 1)*
- ☐ I can describe claim-then-reasoning and reasoning-then-claim and when each suits an audience. *(Guide: "Two common patterns"; practice question 2)*
- ☐ I can list at least five ordering principles. *(Guide: "Why the order of paragraphs matters"; practice question 4)*

## Skills

- ☐ I can map the line of reasoning of a short argument. *(Guide: Worked example 1; practice question 6)*
- ☐ I can order scrambled claims into a coherent line. *(Guide: Worked example 2; practice question 7)*
- ☐ I can choose a transition that states the real relation between ideas. *(Practice question 5)*
- ☐ I can check an argument for gaps and detours. *(Guide: "Checking whether a line of reasoning supports the thesis")*

## Reasoning

- ☐ I can explain how paragraph order reveals the writer's reasoning. *(Guide: Gettysburg example; practice question 8)*
- ☐ I can explain why commentary between paragraphs matters. *(Guide: "Commentary builds the line, not just the paragraph")*
- ☐ I can judge whether a line of reasoning supports the thesis and name any weakness. *(Practice question 6)*
