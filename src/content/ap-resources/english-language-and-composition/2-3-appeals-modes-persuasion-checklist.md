Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/2-3-appeals-modes-persuasion-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/2-3-appeals-modes-persuasion-practice/); also see the [revision notes](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/2-3-appeals-modes-persuasion-revision-notes/).

## Understanding

- ☐ I can define logos, ethos and pathos and give the question each answers. *(Guide: "Three ways to persuade")*
- ☐ I can explain how a writer builds credibility without a title. *(Guide: "Ethos: the appeal to credibility and character"; practice question 3)*
- ☐ I can explain why one sentence can serve more than one appeal. *(Guide: "The appeals work together")*

## Skills

- ☐ I can identify the primary appeal of a sentence and justify it. *(Practice questions 1, 2 and 6)*
- ☐ I can explain what an appeal does for the audience and purpose. *(Guide: Worked example 1; practice question 6)*
- ☐ I can revise a paragraph to add a missing ethos or logos appeal. *(Guide: Worked example 2; practice question 7)*
- ☐ I can describe how citing an expert works as an appeal. *(Practice question 4)*

## Reasoning

- ☐ I can explain how a single sentence works through two or more appeals. *(Guide: Anthony example; practice questions 5 and 8)*
- ☐ I can avoid the common misclassifications of statistics and expert quotations. *(Guide: "Common appeal confusions")*
- ☐ I can write about an appeal in four steps: point, name, audience effect, purpose. *(Revision notes: "How to write about appeals")*
