Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/8-1-comparisons-for-an-audience-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/8-1-comparisons-for-an-audience-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why writers use similes, metaphors, analogies and anecdotes to relate an argument to an audience. *(Guide: "Why writers compare"; Practice question 1)*
- I can explain why an effective comparison must be shared and understood by the audience. *(Guide: "The condition: shared and understood"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain how a comparison transfers meaning and also hides differences. *(Guide: "What a comparison does"; Practice question 4)*

## Skills

- I can tell a simile, a metaphor, an analogy and an anecdote apart. *(Guide: "The forms a comparison can take"; Practice question 3)*
- I can name the two sides of a comparison and say what is transferred from one to the other. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 6)*
- I can choose or write a comparison for a named audience and purpose. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice questions 5 and 7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why a comparison suits one audience and not another. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 5)*
- I can say what a comparison hides and how that affects its persuasiveness. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 8)*
- I can analyse a short public-domain comparison, such as Washington's fingers and hand, in terms of audience and purpose. *(Guide: "A public-domain example"; Practice question 8)*
