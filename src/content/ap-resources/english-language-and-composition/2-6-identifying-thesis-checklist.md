Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/2-6-identifying-thesis-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/2-6-identifying-thesis-practice/); also see the [revision notes](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/2-6-identifying-thesis-revision-notes/).

## Understanding

- ☐ I can define a thesis and explain why it is not necessarily one sentence or stated outright. *(Guide: "What a thesis is"; "A thesis is not always one sentence, and not always stated")*
- ☐ I can list six things that look like a thesis but are not. *(Guide: "Thesis versus things that are not the thesis"; practice question 2)*
- ☐ I can describe thesis-first, thesis-last and implied structures. *(Guide: the table of patterns; practice question 4)*

## Skills

- ☐ I can find the thesis in a short passage and quote it. *(Guide: Worked example 1; practice questions 3 and 6)*
- ☐ I can distinguish a fact, a counterargument and a sub-claim from the thesis. *(Practice questions 3 and 6)*
- ☐ I can apply seven methods for finding the thesis. *(Guide: "Methods for finding the thesis")*
- ☐ I can describe the structure that leads to the thesis. *(Practice questions 4 and 6)*

## Reasoning

- ☐ I can infer an implied thesis from irony and explain how I know. *(Guide: Worked example 2; practice question 7)*
- ☐ I can explain why Swift's literal proposal is not his thesis. *(Guide: Swift example; practice question 5)*
- ☐ I can explain how a change of wording in a famous opening sentence is the thesis in miniature. *(Guide: Stanton example; practice question 8)*
