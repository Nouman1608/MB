Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/6-3-bias-limitations-study-guide/).

## Recap

- When using sources, the strongest arguments **recognise and acknowledge biases and limitations** and **account for them** in their reasoning.
- **The degree to which a source considers other positions** reflects its degree of bias.
- In reading, identify and explain claims and evidence. In writing, develop a paragraph with a claim and evidence.

## Key relationships

| Sign of bias | Check |
|---|---|
| Vested interest | Who benefits? |
| Loaded language | Are the words emotive or measured? |
| Omission | Are other positions mentioned fairly? |
| Selective evidence | Is contrary evidence missing? |
| Hidden method | Is the source of data stated? |

| Limitation | Check |
|---|---|
| Sample size | How many? |
| Selection | Who was chosen and how? |
| Scope | Where and when? |
| Date | How recent? |
| Method | How measured? |
| Type | Anecdote, opinion, data? |

| Way to account for it | Language |
|---|---|
| Qualify | "suggests", "in this case" |
| Name it | "Although the sample was small..." |
| Add evidence | "A second source, independent of the company, shows..." |

## Assumptions and conventions

- Judge each claim on its evidence, not only the source's label.
- Name the source's interest when it matters.
- Do not call a source "biased" without saying how.
- A biased source can still show what a group claims or believes.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Treating a company's brochure as neutral evidence.**
2. **Calling every source biased** without evidence.
3. **Ignoring sample size or selection.**
4. **Overclaiming from a small survey.**
5. **Dismissing a source entirely because of bias.**
6. **Acknowledging a limitation but not adjusting the claim.**
7. **Judging by tone alone.**

## Worked mini-example

*Source:* "In a survey of 50 visitors to our stand, 9 in 10 said our filter water tasted better."

*Interest:* the company sells filters. *Limitations:* 50 people; chosen from the company's own stand; taste, not safety.

*Responsible use:* "A filter company reports that 45 of 50 visitors to its stand preferred its water, but the sample is small and self-selected, so the figure says little about tap water in general."

## On the exam

When you use a source, say what it can show and what it cannot. When a reading question asks about bias, use the measure of how fully the source considers other positions.

## Sentence frames for use in writing

- "Although the survey was small, it suggests …."
- "As a company that sells …, the author has an interest in …, so the claim should be read with care."
- "The report does not consider …, which limits what it can show about …."
- "A second source, independent of …, supports the claim by …."

These frames name the limitation and then adjust the claim, which is how bias and limits are accounted for.

## Quick self-check

1. What does the degree of consideration of other positions show? *(How biased the source is.)*
2. Name two limitations of "9 in 10 of 50 visitors to our stand preferred it". *(Small sample; self-selected group; measured taste, not safety.)*
3. How can you account for a limitation? *(Qualify the claim, name the limitation, add other evidence.)*
