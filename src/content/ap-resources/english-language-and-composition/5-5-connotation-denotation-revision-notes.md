Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-5-connotation-denotation-study-guide/).

## Recap

- **Denotation**: the literal, dictionary meaning.
- **Connotation**: the associations, feelings and judgements a word carries.
- Word choice contributes to **tone** and **style**.
- In reading, explain how word choice contributes to tone or style. In writing, use words strategically to convey a specific tone or style.

## Key relationships

| Neutral | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| walk | stroll | trudge |
| determined | resolute | stubborn |
| thrifty (admiring) | frugal | stingy |
| crowd | gathering | mob |
| unusual | distinctive | peculiar |

| Analysis step | Question |
|---|---|
| 1 | Which words stand out? |
| 2 | What do they connote? |
| 3 | What might the writer have said instead? |
| 4 | What attitude or purpose does the choice serve? |

## Assumptions and conventions

- Read a word in its sentence; context can alter connotation.
- Consider the audience: connotations vary.
- Quote the word, then explain it.
- Support strong connotations with reasoning.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Listing words without explaining connotation.**
2. **Saying "this has a negative connotation" without saying what it suggests.**
3. **Treating synonyms as identical.**
4. **Using a loaded word the evidence does not support.**
5. **Mixing connotations** so the tone is confused.
6. **Overlooking neutral choices** as a strategy.
7. **Ignoring audience** when choosing words.

## Worked mini-example

Compare "The crowd gathered" and "The mob gathered". Both refer to a large group of people, so the denotation is almost the same. "Gathered crowd" is neutral to warm; "mob" suggests disorder and menace. A writer who chose "mob" invites the reader to fear the group.

## On the exam

In a reading question about word choice, quote the word, name its connotation and link it to tone. In a writing question, choose the word whose connotation matches the audience and purpose, and avoid loaded words that the evidence cannot support.

## Sentence frames for analysis

- "The word '…' connotes …, which suggests the writer sees … as …."
- "By choosing '…' over the more neutral '…', the writer invites the audience to …."
- "The positive connotations of '…' and '…' build an attitude of … toward …, which serves the writer's purpose of …."
- "Although '…' and '…' both mean …, '…' carries a judgement of …."

Use a frame only as a start; always replace the blanks with quoted words and a specific effect.

## Common pairs to practise

| Neutral | Warmer | Cooler or harsher |
|---|---|---|
| old | seasoned | outdated |
| quiet | peaceful | dull |
| cautious | prudent | timid |
| talkative | friendly | chattering |

For each pair, say what a writer gains by choosing the warmer or the harsher word, and for which audience.

## Quick self-check

1. Which has the more negative connotation, "thrifty" or "stingy"? *(Stingy.)*
2. What does "boasted liberty" do that "liberty" alone does not? *(Adds a negative connotation of empty bragging, undercutting the praise.)*
3. Why might a writer choose a neutral word? *(To appear objective or avoid inflaming the audience.)*
