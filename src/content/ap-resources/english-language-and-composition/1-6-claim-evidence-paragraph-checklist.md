Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/1-6-claim-evidence-paragraph-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/1-6-claim-evidence-paragraph-practice/); also see the [revision notes](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/1-6-claim-evidence-paragraph-revision-notes/).

## Understanding

- ☐ I can describe the five parts of a claim-evidence-commentary paragraph. *(Guide: "A reliable paragraph pattern")*
- ☐ I can explain when to quote, paraphrase or summarise. *(Guide: "Three ways to use a source"; practice question 2)*
- ☐ I can explain what makes a quotation dropped rather than embedded. *(Guide: "Embedding"; practice questions 1 and 5)*

## Skills

- ☐ I can embed a short quotation in my own sentence with correct punctuation. *(Guide: Worked example 1; practice question 6)*
- ☐ I can write a paraphrase that changes both words and structure. *(Guide: Worked example 2; practice questions 3 and 7)*
- ☐ I can write a one-sentence summary that adds no opinion. *(Guide: Worked example 2; practice question 7)*
- ☐ I can choose a signal verb that fits a source's stance. *(Guide: "Signal verbs")*

## Reasoning

- ☐ I can revise a paragraph that contains a dropped quotation. *(Practice questions 4 and 8)*
- ☐ I can explain how a single word in a quotation supports a claim. *(Guide: Worked example 1; practice question 6)*
- ☐ I can distinguish commentary from repetition of the evidence. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; practice question 8)*
