Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-2-unity-coherence-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-2-unity-coherence-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define unity and coherence and tell them apart. *(Guide: "Two words, two jobs"; Practice question 5)*
- I can explain how coherence operates between clauses, between sentences and between paragraphs. *(Guide: "Two words, two jobs"; Practice question 7)*
- I can list ways ideas are linked (logic, known-then-new order, repetition, parallelism, order of paragraphs). *(Guide: "How ideas link"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain why transitions cannot repair ideas that do not connect. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice question 3)*

## Skills

- I can find a sentence that breaks the unity of a paragraph. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 1)*
- I can reorder sentences so that each leads to the next. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 3; Practice question 2)*
- I can choose an opening sentence that links a paragraph to the one before. *(Guide: "How ideas link"; Practice question 3)*
- I can revise a paragraph by cutting, reordering and linking. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 6)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how a published passage builds coherence at more than one level. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice questions 4 and 7)*
- I can diagnose the cause of a jolt in a draft (off-topic, order, vocabulary, vague reference). *(Guide: "What breaks unity and coherence"; Practice question 6)*
- I can write a sentence that links two paragraphs so that the argument moves on. *(Guide: "How ideas link"; Practice question 8)*
- I can explain how organisation reflects a line of reasoning. *(Guide: "How ideas link"; Practice question 7)*
