Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/6-2-position-perspective-study-guide/).

## Recap

- A **position** is a stance on an issue; a **perspective** is the vantage point shaped by background, interests and expertise.
- Sources may have the **same position** yet each come from a **different perspective**.
- In reading, identify and explain claims and evidence. In writing, develop paragraphs with claims and evidence.

## Key relationships

| Question | Concept |
|---|---|
| What does it argue? | Position |
| From where does it argue? | Perspective |
| What does it stress? | Evidence chosen |
| What does it leave out? | Limits of the perspective |

| Clue | Example |
|---|---|
| Role or background | "As a nurse..." |
| Interests | What the writer gains or loses |
| Expertise | Data, experience, training |
| Evidence | Classroom time (teacher), safety (parent) |
| Vocabulary | Words that reflect concerns |

## Assumptions and conventions

- Name the perspective in a short phrase ("from a teacher's viewpoint").
- Do not treat a perspective as a flaw by itself.
- Compare what different perspectives stress and omit.
- Use shared positions with different perspectives as strong support.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Treating position and perspective as synonyms.**
2. **Assuming sources that agree give the same reasons.**
3. **Labelling a perspective as bias without evidence.**
4. **Ignoring the writer's role or interests.**
5. **Using one perspective to speak for everyone.**
6. **Listing sources' positions without saying why they differ.**
7. **Describing a source's perspective without linking it to its evidence.**

## Worked mini-example

*Issue:* a school phone ban. A teacher and a parent both support it.

| Source | Position | Perspective |
|---|---|---|
| Teacher | For the ban | Classroom attention |
| Parent | For the ban | Home and social pressure |

Same position, different perspectives, so the two give different reasons. A writer can say that agreement from two different concerns strengthens the case.

## On the exam

In a reading question, identify the position first and then the perspective, using clues such as role, interests and evidence. In writing, name a perspective only when it explains the evidence a source offers.

## Sentence frames for analysis

- "Both sources argue that …, but from different perspectives: the first as …, the second as …."
- "Because the writer speaks as …, the evidence stresses … and leaves out …."
- "Source A takes the position that …, and its perspective as … explains why it cites …."
- "That two sources with different concerns reach the same position strengthens the case because …."

Check each frame against the sources: position first, perspective second, evidence third.

## Quick self-check

1. A teacher and a parent both support a ban for different reasons. Same position or perspective? *(Same position; different perspectives.)*
2. Give two clues to a perspective. *(Role or background, interests, expertise, choice of evidence, vocabulary.)*
3. Why might agreement between different perspectives strengthen an argument? *(Different concerns lead to the same conclusion.)*
