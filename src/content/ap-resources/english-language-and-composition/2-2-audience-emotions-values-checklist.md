Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/2-2-audience-emotions-values-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/2-2-audience-emotions-values-practice/); also see the [revision notes](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/2-2-audience-emotions-values-revision-notes/).

## Understanding

- ☐ I can explain why emotions and values matter in an argument, and how they work with evidence. *(Guide: "Why feelings belong in an argument")*
- ☐ I can list at least five sources of emotion in a text. *(Guide: "Where emotion comes from in a text")*
- ☐ I can explain three ways a writer relates to an audience's values. *(Guide: "Relating to values")*

## Skills

- ☐ I can identify the device behind an emotional effect. *(Practice questions 1 and 3)*
- ☐ I can explain an emotional appeal in terms of device, feeling or value, and effect. *(Guide: Worked example 1; practice question 6)*
- ☐ I can rewrite a flat sentence to add imagery or a values appeal while keeping the facts. *(Guide: Worked example 2; practice questions 5 and 7)*
- ☐ I can distinguish a value appeal from a factual statement. *(Practice question 2)*

## Reasoning

- ☐ I can judge whether an appeal is relevant, proportionate, supported and respectful. *(Guide: "When emotion is persuasive and when it is not"; practice question 5)*
- ☐ I can explain why restraint can be more moving than extravagance. *(Guide: Chief Joseph example; practice questions 4 and 8)*
- ☐ I can explain how framing a proposal through a value targets a particular audience. *(Guide: "Relating to values")*
