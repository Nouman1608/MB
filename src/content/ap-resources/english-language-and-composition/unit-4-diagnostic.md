Use this diagnostic to find **which Unit 4 topics to revisit**: one question per topic, then two short written tasks.

These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. All passages are written by Marlbridge for this resource. The diagnostic is not calibrated and gives **no predicted score**. Rubrics are a suggested Marlbridge rubric.

**How to take it.** Allow 30 minutes. Answer everything before opening any answers. No calculator is needed.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · 4.1)

Read the opening of an essay (Marlbridge passage).

> Every autumn the council spends £12,000 collecting leaves that, left alone, would feed the soil beneath the trees they fell from. The money is not wasted on a luxury; it is spent on a habit. The town should stop raking.

Which choice best describes what the opening does?

- (A) It opens with a specific figure and a mild paradox to engage the reader, then states a clear claim.
- (B) It opens with a dictionary definition and a long history of leaf collection.
- (C) It announces what the essay will discuss without taking a position.
- (D) It quotes an expert to establish authority.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** A figure (£12,000) and a contrast (money spent collecting what would feed the soil) orient and engage, and the last sentence presents the claim.

- (B) The passage contains no definition or history.
- (C) The passage takes a position ("should stop raking").
- (D) No expert is quoted.

**If you missed this:** [4.1 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/4-1-introductions-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · 4.2)

An essay argues that a town should keep its public library open on Sundays. Which closing sentence adds the most beyond repeating the claim?

- (A) In conclusion, libraries are good and should be open on Sundays.
- (B) A town that keeps its library open on Sundays keeps something that cannot be bought back: a place to go on a day with nowhere to be.
- (C) As I have said, libraries should be open.
- (D) Thank you for reading this essay about libraries.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** It explains significance and leaves the reader with an image, so it passes the so-what test.

- (A) and (C) repeat the claim.
- (D) is a courtesy, not a conclusion.

**If you missed this:** [4.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/4-2-conclusions-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · 4.3)

Which thesis best previews a line of reasoning for an argument about school uniforms?

- (A) This essay will discuss school uniforms.
- (B) Schools should drop compulsory uniform, because it costs families money, takes up lesson time in enforcement and does little to improve behaviour.
- (C) Uniforms are an interesting subject.
- (D) Uniform, cost and behaviour are all relevant.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** It states a position and signals three reasons in order.

- (A) is an announcement.
- (C) makes no arguable claim.
- (D) is a bare list of topics with no claim.

**If you missed this:** [4.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/4-3-thesis-previewing-reasoning-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · 4.4)

A writer compares two phones. Which pair of statements compares like categories?

- (A) "Phone X costs £300; Phone Y has a nice colour."
- (B) "Phone X has a battery life of 20 hours; Phone Y has a battery life of 14 hours."
- (C) "Phone X is cheap; Phone Y is popular."
- (D) "Phone X is small; Phone Y has good reviews."

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** Both phones are measured on battery life.

- (A) compares cost with appearance.
- (C) compares price with popularity.
- (D) compares size with reviews.

**If you missed this:** [4.4 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/4-4-comparison-contrast-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 5 (multiple choice · 4.5)

A writer wants to show that a classroom is too hot. Which sentence develops the idea most effectively through description?

- (A) The classroom was very hot and uncomfortable.
- (B) By midday the windows were sticky with condensation, the fan only pushed warm air from one side of the room to the other, and pupils fanned themselves with exercise books.
- (C) A classroom is a room where pupils learn.
- (D) Heat is a form of energy.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** It uses specific, sensory details that let the reader feel the heat.

- (A) uses vague adjectives.
- (C) is a definition that does not address the heat.
- (D) is a general scientific statement with no link to the classroom.

**If you missed this:** [4.5 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/4-5-definition-description-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 6 (constructed response · 4.1, 4.2, 4.3)

The prompt is: *Argue whether schools should teach students how to manage money.* Write (a) a thesis that takes a position and previews the line of reasoning, and (b) a one-sentence closing idea that adds significance without repeating the thesis.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Sample response (Marlbridge).**

(a) *Schools should teach students how to manage money, because the skill is used every day, mistakes in it are costly to correct and no other institution reaches every young person.*

(b) *A school that sends students into adult life without this knowledge leaves one of its most practical lessons to chance.*

**Suggested Marlbridge rubric (4 points).**

| Point | Award it when the response... |
|---|---|
| 1 | The thesis takes a clear, defensible position |
| 1 | The thesis previews two or more reasons in a sequence |
| 1 | The closing idea adds significance or a consequence |
| 1 | The closing idea is not a repetition of the thesis |

**If you missed this:** [4.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/4-2-conclusions-study-guide/) and [4.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/4-3-thesis-previewing-reasoning-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 7 (constructed response · 4.4, 4.5)

Write three or four sentences that compare cycling and bus travel to school, using like categories, and state at the end what the comparison shows. You may invent plausible figures.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Sample response (Marlbridge; invented figures).**

> On cost, cycling is cheaper: a bicycle's yearly upkeep is about £40, against £540 for a bus pass. On time, the two are close, with a cyclist taking 18 minutes and the bus 20 including waiting. On reliability, the bicycle wins again, since it does not depend on a timetable. These categories show that, for a student who lives within three miles, cycling is the better choice unless the weather is severe.

**Suggested Marlbridge rubric (4 points).**

| Point | Award it when the response... |
|---|---|
| 1 | Names at least two categories of comparison |
| 1 | Compares both options on each category (like categories) |
| 1 | Is organised clearly (point by point or block) |
| 1 | Ends with a claim that follows from the comparison |

**If you missed this:** [4.4 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/4-4-comparison-contrast-study-guide/) and [4.5 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/4-5-definition-description-study-guide/).
</details>
