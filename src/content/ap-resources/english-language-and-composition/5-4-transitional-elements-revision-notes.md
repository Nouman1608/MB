Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-4-transitional-elements-study-guide/).

## Recap

- **Transitional elements** are words, phrases, clauses, sentences or paragraphs that show relationships among ideas.
- They help create **coherence** among sentences, paragraphs and sections.
- They can **introduce evidence** or indicate its relationship to other evidence in a paragraph or the text as a whole.
- In reading, explain how they guide the reader through the line of reasoning. In writing, use them to do so.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Examples |
|---|---|
| Addition | also, moreover, in addition |
| Contrast | but, however, in contrast |
| Concession | although, admittedly, granted |
| Cause or result | because, since, therefore, as a result |
| Example | for instance, such as, to illustrate |
| Sequence | first, then, finally |
| Emphasis | indeed, above all, more telling still |
| Similarity | similarly, likewise |
| Summary | in short, in sum |

| Level | Example |
|---|---|
| Word | however |
| Phrase | on the other hand |
| Clause | although the cost is high |
| Sentence | "Cost is only half the story." |
| Paragraph | A short paragraph marking a turn in the argument |

## Assumptions and conventions

- Punctuate transitions correctly: "however" in the middle of a sentence takes commas; after a semicolon it is followed by a comma.
- Do not start every sentence with a transition.
- A transition should reflect the writer's actual reasoning.
- Between paragraphs, "look back, look forward".

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Choosing a word before deciding the relationship.**
2. **"Therefore" where nothing follows from the previous sentence.**
3. **"However" where nothing contrasts.**
4. **Overusing "moreover" and "furthermore"** as filler.
5. **Leaving evidence without a signal** (what kind, how it relates).
6. **Using a transition to cover a gap in logic.**
7. **Naming "transitions" in analysis** without saying what relationship they show.

## Worked mini-example

*No transitions:* "The town built a skate park. Teenagers have somewhere to go. Crime fell. Some residents complained. The council kept the park."

*With transitions:* "The town built a skate park, and as a result teenagers have somewhere to go. Crime fell as well. Some residents, however, complained. Even so, the council kept the park."

Each added phrase names a relationship: result, addition, contrast, concession.

## On the exam

When a question asks which transition fits, decide the relationship between the ideas first and then choose the word. When a question asks about a paragraph-level transition, look for the sentence that looks back and forward.

## Quick self-check

1. Which transition signals a concession? *(Although, admittedly, granted.)*
2. What is wrong with "The shop sells fruit. Therefore, it is on Park Road."? *(The "therefore" names a result that does not exist.)*
3. How can a sentence be a transition between paragraphs? *(It looks back to the last idea and forward to the next.)*
