Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-5-connotation-denotation-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-5-connotation-denotation-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define denotation and connotation and give an example of each. *(Guide: "Two layers of meaning"; Practice question 5)*
- I can explain why two words with the same denotation can have different effects. *(Guide: "Same meaning, different attitude"; Practice question 1)*
- I can explain how connotation depends on context and audience. *(Guide: "Same meaning, different attitude"; Practice question 4)*
- I can explain how connotation contributes to tone. *(Guide: "How connotation builds tone and style"; Practice question 2)*

## Skills

- I can place words on a scale from positive to negative connotation. *(Guide: "Same meaning, different attitude"; Practice questions 1 and 6)*
- I can analyse word choice in a passage by naming connotations and effects. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice questions 2 and 7)*
- I can analyse connotation in a published text. *(Guide: "Connotation in a published text"; Practice question 3)*
- I can rewrite a sentence to change its connotation without changing its facts. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 6)*

## Reasoning

- I can choose words with the connotations that suit a stated audience. *(Guide: "Choosing words strategically"; Practice questions 4 and 8)*
- I can judge whether a loaded word is supported by the evidence. *(Guide: "Choosing words strategically"; Practice question 8)*
- I can explain why a neutral word may be a deliberate choice. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice question 8)*
- I can write a one-sentence analysis linking word choice to tone and purpose. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 5; Practice question 7)*
