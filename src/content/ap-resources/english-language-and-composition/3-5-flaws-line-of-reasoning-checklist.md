Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/3-5-flaws-line-of-reasoning-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/3-5-flaws-line-of-reasoning-practice/); also see the [revision notes](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/3-5-flaws-line-of-reasoning-revision-notes/).

## Understanding

- ☐ I can define specious and illogical reasoning with an example of each. *(Guide: "Specious and illogical: two kinds of failure")*
- ☐ I can describe at least eight common flaws and say what is wrong with each. *(Guide: "Reasoning flaws: what goes wrong"; practice questions 1 to 4)*
- ☐ I can explain why naming a fallacy is not enough. *(Guide: "When the path breaks")*

## Skills

- ☐ I can use nine questions to test an argument for flaws. *(Guide: "Questions that expose flaws")*
- ☐ I can diagnose three flaws in a paragraph and explain each. *(Guide: Worked example 1; practice question 6)*
- ☐ I can repair a flawed argument. *(Guide: Worked example 2; practice question 7)*
- ☐ I can choose the right repair for each flaw. *(Guide: "Fixing a flaw in your own writing")*

## Reasoning

- ☐ I can explain why a satirist might use flawed reasoning on purpose. *(Guide: Swift example; practice questions 5 and 8)*
- ☐ I can distinguish an emotional appeal from an emotional fallacy. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*
- ☐ I can explain why a true conclusion can rest on bad reasoning. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*
