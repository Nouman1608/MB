Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-1-body-paragraphs-reasoning-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-1-body-paragraphs-reasoning-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can name the parts of a body paragraph and say what each does. *(Guide: "From thesis to paragraphs"; Practice question 1)*
- I can explain the difference between evidence and commentary. *(Guide: "Commentary: where the thinking happens"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain what a line of reasoning is. *(Guide: "Line of reasoning"; Practice question 5)*
- I can explain why each body paragraph needs a claim linked to the thesis. *(Guide: "From thesis to paragraphs"; Practice question 3)*

## Skills

- I can label the claim, evidence and commentary in a paragraph. *(Guide: "Weak and strong body paragraphs side by side"; Practice question 1)*
- I can add commentary to a paragraph that only lists evidence. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 6)*
- I can describe the line of reasoning in a short argument. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 7)*
- I can write a body paragraph with a claim, evidence, commentary and a link. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 8)*

## Reasoning

- I can judge whether a line of reasoning supports the thesis. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 3; Practice questions 5 and 7)*
- I can choose a revision that makes a paragraph contribute more to the argument. *(Guide: "Weak and strong body paragraphs side by side"; Practice question 4)*
- I can explain how commentary qualifies as well as supports. *(Guide: "Commentary: where the thinking happens"; Practice question 6)*
- I can write a one-sentence analysis of how a paragraph or argument is developed. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 4; Practice question 7)*
