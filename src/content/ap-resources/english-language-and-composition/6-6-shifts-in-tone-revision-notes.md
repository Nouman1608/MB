## Recap

- A **shift in tone** is a change in the writer's attitude from one part of a text to another.
- Shifts may suggest the writer's **qualification, refinement or reconsideration** of a perspective.
- In reading, explain how word choice, comparisons and syntax contribute to tone. In writing, use them strategically to convey tone.

## Key relationships

| Marker | Examples |
|---|---|
| Turning transition | but, yet, however, still |
| Diction change | formal to casual; gentle to harsh |
| Connotation change | positive to negative |
| Sentence change | long to short |
| Punctuation | dash, colon, question |
| Address change | "they" to "we" |
| Imagery | new comparisons |

| What the shift does | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Qualifies | Limits or adds a condition |
| Refines | Sharpens or develops |
| Reconsiders | Rethinks or reverses |

## Assumptions and conventions

- Name the tone before and after with precise words.
- Quote the words on both sides of the turn.
- Say what the shift does for the argument.
- A cluster of markers is stronger evidence than one.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Naming a shift without naming the tones.**
2. **Saying "the tone shifts" without evidence.**
3. **Treating every "but" as a shift.**
4. **Assuming a shift means a change of mind.**
5. **Describing the content change** (topic) instead of the attitude.
6. **Forgetting the effect on the audience.**
7. **Creating accidental shifts** in your own writing.

## Worked mini-example

*Passage:* "The house was lovely: sunlit rooms, a garden full of roses. We bought it within a week. Then the first winter came, and we learned what a draught could do."

*Shift:* admiring and lyrical, then wry and regretful. *Markers:* "Then", a short plain sentence after the lyrical description.

*Effect:* it qualifies the earlier praise with a dry understatement.

## On the exam

When asked about a shift, locate it, name the tones before and after, and say whether the change qualifies, refines or reconsiders the writer's view and what that does for the argument.

## Sentence frames for analysis

- "The tone shifts from … to … at '…', marked by …."
- "The word '…' signals a turn from … to …, which qualifies the writer's earlier …."
- "After the shift, the sentences become … and the diction …, which conveys …."
- "This shift refines the writer's view by …, so that the audience …."

Each frame needs quoted words on both sides of the turn.

## Quick self-check

1. Name two markers of a tone shift. *(For example: a turning transition; a change in sentence length or diction.)*
2. What might a shift from praise to criticism be doing? *(Qualifying the praise, so the criticism is harder to dismiss.)*
3. What four things should you do when describing a shift? *(Locate it, name the tones, quote evidence, explain what it does.)*
