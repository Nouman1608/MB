Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/1-2-exigence-purpose-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/1-2-exigence-purpose-practice/); also see the [revision notes](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/1-2-exigence-purpose-revision-notes/).

## Understanding

- ☐ I can define exigence and purpose in my own words and give the test sentence for each. *(Guide: "Two questions that look alike")*
- ☐ I can explain why exigence looks back and purpose looks forward. *(Guide: "Two questions that look alike"; practice question 7)*
- ☐ I can explain what makes a purpose strong or weak. *(Guide: "Purpose: use a verb and a target"; practice question 2)*

## Skills

- ☐ I can identify an exigence that is stated outright. *(Practice questions 1 and 3)*
- ☐ I can infer an exigence that is only implied, and point to the clue. *(Guide: "Kinds of exigence"; practice question 5)*
- ☐ I can write a purpose with a verb and a target audience. *(Guide: Worked example 1; practice question 6)*
- ☐ I can identify a primary and a secondary purpose. *(Guide: Worked example 2; practice questions 4 and 6)*

## Reasoning

- ☐ I can explain how an occasion becomes an exigence that sharpens an argument. *(Guide: Douglass example; practice question 8)*
- ☐ I can correct a response that has swapped exigence and purpose. *(Practice question 7)*
- ☐ I can trace how an exigence leads to a purpose and then to a writer's choices. *(Guide: "How exigence leads to purpose"; Worked example 2)*
