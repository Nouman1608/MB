Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/6-4-revising-thesis-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/6-4-revising-thesis-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why new evidence may require revising a thesis or the line of reasoning. *(Guide: "A thesis is a working claim"; Practice question 5)*
- I can name the main ways a thesis can be revised. *(Guide: "Ways a thesis can change"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain why the evidence should shape the claim, not the reverse. *(Guide: "A method for revising"; Practice question 3)*
- I can explain why a revised thesis must still be specific and defensible. *(Guide: "Ways a thesis can change"; Practice question 4)*

## Skills

- I can test a thesis against a set of evidence. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 1; Practice question 1)*
- I can write a revised thesis that fits the evidence. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 4; Practice question 6)*
- I can revise the line of reasoning after revising the thesis. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 5; Practice question 7)*
- I can identify the revision a writer has made in a text. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 8)*

## Reasoning

- I can choose between narrowing, qualifying and reversing a claim and justify the choice. *(Guide: "Ways a thesis can change"; Practice questions 1 and 6)*
- I can explain why ignoring contrary evidence weakens an argument. *(Guide: "Weak and strong responses to new evidence"; Practice question 3)*
- I can decide whether new evidence refines or overturns a thesis. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice question 1)*
- I can write a one-sentence analysis of how a writer revises a position in response to evidence. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 6; Practice question 8)*
