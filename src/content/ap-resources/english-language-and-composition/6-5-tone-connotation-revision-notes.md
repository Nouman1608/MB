Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/6-5-tone-connotation-study-guide/).

## Recap

- **Tone** is the writer's attitude or feeling about a subject, conveyed through word choice and writing style.
- Readers **infer tone** from word choice, especially the positive, negative or other connotations of the words.
- In reading, explain how word choice contributes to tone. In writing, use words strategically to convey tone.

## Key relationships

| Chain | Question |
|---|---|
| Word | Which words stand out? |
| Connotation | What do they suggest? |
| Attitude | What does the writer feel? |
| Purpose | What does the tone do to the audience? |

| Vague | Precise |
|---|---|
| negative | scornful, indignant, bitter, grim |
| positive | admiring, hopeful, affectionate |
| serious | solemn, earnest, urgent |
| funny | wry, sardonic, playful |
| sad | wistful, mournful, resigned |
| neutral | detached, objective, matter-of-fact |

## Assumptions and conventions

- Use two linked words when one is not enough ("wry but affectionate").
- Quote the words that carry the tone.
- Tone is about the writer's attitude, mood is about the reader's feeling.
- Sentence length and rhythm contribute as well as vocabulary.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Naming tone as "negative" or "positive".**
2. **Giving the tone without any words as evidence.**
3. **Confusing tone with mood.**
4. **Describing the topic instead of the attitude.**
5. **Ignoring the purpose.**
6. **Mixing tones in your own writing without meaning to.**
7. **Assuming a neutral passage has no tone.**

## Worked mini-example

*Passage:* "The minister delivered his statement with the confidence of a man who has never been asked to prove anything."

*Words:* "confidence" (mock-praise), "never been asked to prove anything" (a jab).

*Tone:* sardonic and critical. *Purpose:* to suggest the minister has never been held to account, using humour to make the criticism memorable.

## On the exam

When a question asks about tone, choose the answer that names a precise attitude supported by specific words. Beware of options that describe the topic or the mood instead.

## Sentence frames for analysis

- "The writer's tone is …, created by words such as '…' and '…'."
- "The connotation of '…' (…) shows that the writer regards … as …."
- "The … tone suits the writer's purpose of … because the audience …."
- "Although the subject is …, the tone is …, which creates … for the reader."

Always replace "…" with a precise tone word and quoted evidence.

## Tone words to practise

| Tone | Typical signals in the words |
|---|---|
| Earnest | Plain, sincere diction; direct address |
| Wry | Understatement; mock-formal phrases |
| Indignant | Accusing verbs; emphatic adverbs |
| Wistful | Past tense; soft, longing images |
| Detached | Neutral nouns; passive voice; no judgement words |

Pick a sentence from any text you are reading and decide which row it fits.

## Quick self-check

1. Name a more precise word for "negative". *(Scornful, indignant, bitter, disappointed.)*
2. Which words in "limped in at 8:50, yet again, with no apology" convey tone? *(Limped, yet again, no apology: indignant.)*
3. What is the difference between tone and mood? *(Tone is the writer's attitude; mood is the reader's feeling.)*
