Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/2-5-sufficient-evidence-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/2-5-sufficient-evidence-practice/); also see the [revision notes](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/2-5-sufficient-evidence-revision-notes/).

## Understanding

- ☐ I can define sufficiency in terms of quantity and quality. *(Guide: "How much evidence is enough?"; "Two measures")*
- ☐ I can explain how the wording of a claim sets the standard of evidence. *(Guide: "The scope of the claim sets the standard")*
- ☐ I can explain why too much repetitive evidence can weaken a paragraph. *(Guide: "Too much can also be a problem"; practice question 5)*

## Skills

- ☐ I can list at least five ways evidence falls short. *(Guide: "Common ways evidence falls short")*
- ☐ I can narrow a sweeping claim so that the evidence can support it. *(Guide: Worked example 1; practice questions 2 and 6)*
- ☐ I can suggest stronger or different evidence for a claim. *(Practice questions 3, 6 and 7)*
- ☐ I can judge whether a paragraph has sufficient evidence. *(Guide: Worked example 1; practice question 1)*

## Reasoning

- ☐ I can explain why a claim about a pattern needs many related instances. *(Guide: Worked example 2; practice question 4)*
- ☐ I can explain how audience scepticism changes how much evidence is needed. *(Guide: "Sufficiency depends on the audience too")*
- ☐ I can analyse how the Declaration of Independence signals that its claim needs proof. *(Guide: "Let Facts be submitted"; practice question 8)*
