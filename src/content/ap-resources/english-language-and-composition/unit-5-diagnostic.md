Use this diagnostic to find **which Unit 5 topics to revisit**: one question per topic, then a short written task.

These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. All passages are written by Marlbridge for this resource. The diagnostic is not calibrated and gives **no predicted score**. Rubrics are a suggested Marlbridge rubric.

**How to take it.** Allow 30 minutes. Answer everything before opening any answers. No calculator is needed.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · 5.1)

Read this body paragraph (Marlbridge passage). Sentences are numbered.

> (1) A school allotment teaches responsibility. (2) At Hollin Primary, each class tends a bed for a whole term, and pupils who forget to water it find the lettuces wilting by Friday. (3) The wilting makes the cost of neglect visible in a way that a lost worksheet never does. (4) A school that wants responsible pupils should therefore give them something living to look after.

Which sentence is the commentary?

- (A) Sentence 1
- (B) Sentence 2
- (C) Sentence 3
- (D) Sentence 2 and sentence 4 only

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** Sentence 3 explains what the evidence in sentence 2 shows. Sentence 1 is the claim, sentence 2 is evidence, and sentence 4 links the paragraph to the argument.

- (A) is the claim.
- (B) is evidence.
- (D) Sentence 4 links to the thesis, and sentence 2 is evidence; neither alone is the explanatory commentary on the evidence.

**If you missed this:** [5.1 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-1-body-paragraphs-reasoning-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · 5.2)

Read this paragraph (Marlbridge passage).

> (1) A skate park would give teenagers a safe place to practise. (2) A ramp built by the council is inspected for safety, unlike a handrail on a high street. (3) The council meets on the first Tuesday of each month. (4) Skaters who practise in a park are less likely to injure pedestrians.

Which sentence most clearly breaks the unity of the paragraph?

- (A) Sentence 1
- (B) Sentence 2
- (C) Sentence 3
- (D) Sentence 4

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** The council's meeting schedule does not serve the controlling idea (the safety of a skate park).

- (A) states the controlling idea.
- (B) supports it.
- (D) supports it.

**If you missed this:** [5.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-2-unity-coherence-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · 5.3)

Which sentence uses parallel structure correctly?

- (A) The programme will reduce waiting times, to improve safety and save money.
- (B) The programme will reduce waiting times, improve safety and save money.
- (C) The programme will reduce waiting times, improving safety and the saving of money.
- (D) The programme will reduce waiting times, it will improve safety and money saved.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** The three verbs ("reduce", "improve", "save") have the same form.

- (A) mixes a base verb with an infinitive.
- (C) mixes a base verb, a participle and a noun phrase.
- (D) mixes a verb, a clause and a noun phrase.

**If you missed this:** [5.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-3-repetition-synonyms-parallelism-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · 5.4)

Choose the best transition: "The new timetable shortens the school day; [ … ], students have less time for clubs."

- (A) for example
- (B) as a result
- (C) in contrast
- (D) similarly

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** Having a shorter day causes less time for clubs, a result.

- (A) would introduce an example of the shortening.
- (C) would signal contrast, but the second clause follows from the first.
- (D) would signal a parallel idea, not a consequence.

**If you missed this:** [5.4 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-4-transitional-elements-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 5 (multiple choice · 5.5)

A writer wants to describe a person who keeps a tight hold on money in a way that shows admiration. Which word is best?

- (A) stingy
- (B) tight-fisted
- (C) thrifty
- (D) miserly

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** "Thrifty" has a favourable connotation of care and good management.

- (A), (B) and (D) all carry negative connotations of meanness.

**If you missed this:** [5.5 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-5-connotation-denotation-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 6 (multiple choice · 5.6)

Which revision of "The meeting was bad" is most precise?

- (A) The meeting was really bad.
- (B) The meeting was a total disaster.
- (C) The meeting ran for three hours and ended without a decision.
- (D) The meeting was not good at all.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** It replaces a vague judgement with facts that the reader can picture and check.

- (A), (B) and (D) are still vague judgements.

**If you missed this:** [5.6 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-6-precise-word-choice-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 7 (constructed response · 5.1, 5.2, 5.4)

Revise this paragraph (Marlbridge passage) so that it has unity and coherence. You may cut and reorder sentences and add transitions, but do not add new evidence.

> Our town should plant more street trees. The council has a budget of £2 million. Trees give shade. Shade makes pavements cooler in summer. Some trees drop leaves in autumn. Cooler pavements make walking more pleasant. A tree costs about £300 to plant.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Sample response (Marlbridge).**

> Our town should plant more street trees. Trees give shade, and shade makes pavements cooler in summer, so walking becomes more pleasant. A tree costs about £300 to plant, which is a small fraction of the council's £2 million budget.

*Changes:* cut "Some trees drop leaves in autumn" (does not serve the claim); reordered so that shade leads to cooler pavements and then to pleasant walking; joined the sentences with "and" and "so"; placed the cost point last and linked it to the budget with "which".

**Suggested Marlbridge rubric (4 points).**

| Point | Award it when the response... |
|---|---|
| 1 | Cuts the off-topic sentence about leaves |
| 1 | Orders the ideas in a logical chain (shade, cooler pavements, pleasant walking) |
| 1 | Adds at least one accurate transition or link |
| 1 | Connects the cost sentence to the budget without new evidence |

**If you missed this:** [5.1](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-1-body-paragraphs-reasoning-study-guide/), [5.2](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-2-unity-coherence-study-guide/) and [5.4](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/5-4-transitional-elements-study-guide/) study guides.
</details>
