Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/9-3-conceding-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/9-3-conceding-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define concession and name its three forms. *(Guide: "What it means to concede"; Practice questions 1 and 4)*
- I can explain how conceding enhances credibility. *(Guide: "Why concede? What the writer gains"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain why a concession is a strategy and not a surrender. *(Guide: "The pattern: concede, then pivot"; Practice question 3)*

## Skills

- I can write a concession in each of the three forms. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 6)*
- I can use concession language and put the concession in a subordinate position. *(Guide: "The language of concession"; Practice question 5)*
- I can pivot back to my thesis after a concession. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 6)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain what a writer concedes, how, and what the writer gains. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 7)*
- I can judge when a concession goes too far and gives away the thesis. *(Guide: "Limits: do not concede too much"; Practice questions 3 and 6)*
- I can analyse a public-domain concession, such as Douglass's praise of the founders, for its strategy. *(Guide: "A public-domain example"; Practice question 8)*
