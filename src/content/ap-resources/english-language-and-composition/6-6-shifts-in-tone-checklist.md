Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/6-6-shifts-in-tone-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/6-6-shifts-in-tone-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define a shift in tone. *(Guide: "What a shift is"; Practice question 5)*
- I can explain how shifts may suggest qualification, refinement or reconsideration. *(Guide: "What a shift is"; Practice question 2)*
- I can list the markers of a shift. *(Guide: "Markers of a shift"; Practice question 4)*
- I can explain why a cluster of markers is stronger evidence than one. *(Guide: "Markers of a shift"; Practice question 1)*

## Skills

- I can locate a shift in a passage. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 1; Practice questions 1 and 6)*
- I can name the tone before and after a shift with precise terms. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Steps 2 and 3; Practice questions 3 and 6)*
- I can describe a shift in a published text. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 3)*
- I can write a passage that shifts tone deliberately. *(Guide: "Writing a shift on purpose"; Practice question 7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain what a shift does to the writer's perspective and the audience. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 5; Practice questions 2 and 6)*
- I can tell whether a shift qualifies, refines or reconsiders. *(Guide: "What a shift is"; Practice question 8)*
- I can distinguish a shift in tone from a change of topic. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice question 4)*
- I can write a one-sentence analysis of a shift using the sentence frame. *(Guide: "Describing a shift"; Practice questions 6 and 8)*
