Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/4-2-conclusions-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/4-2-conclusions-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what it means for a conclusion to bring an argument to a unified end. *(Guide: "What does a conclusion have to do?"; Practice question 1)*
- I can list the strategies a conclusion may use and say what each does for the reader. *(Guide: "The so-what test"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain why summary alone makes a weak conclusion. *(Guide: "Weak and strong conclusions side by side"; Practice question 3)*
- I can explain why a conclusion should not open a new line of proof. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice question 5)*

## Skills

- I can apply the so-what test to a draft conclusion. *(Guide: "The so-what test"; Practice question 6)*
- I can write a conclusion that returns to the introduction in a new light. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 8)*
- I can name the strategy in a published conclusion and describe its effect. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice questions 4 and 7)*
- I can rewrite a repetitive conclusion so that it adds significance. *(Guide: "Weak and strong conclusions side by side"; Practice question 6)*

## Reasoning

- I can choose a conclusion strategy to suit a purpose (persuade, interpret, reflect) and justify it. *(Guide: "Common conclusion shapes"; Practice questions 2 and 8)*
- I can explain why a call to act must follow from the argument. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 2)*
- I can judge whether a conclusion's wide claim is earned or empty. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice question 6)*
- I can write a one-sentence analysis of a conclusion's function in the whole argument. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 3; Practice question 7)*
