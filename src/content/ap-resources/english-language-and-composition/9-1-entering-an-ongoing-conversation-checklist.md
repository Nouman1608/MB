Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/9-1-entering-an-ongoing-conversation-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-language-and-composition/9-1-entering-an-ongoing-conversation-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what it means to enter an ongoing conversation by engaging positions already argued. *(Guide: "Arguments arrive in a conversation that has already started"; Practice question 1)*
- I can explain why ignoring the conversation weakens an argument. *(Guide: "Arguments arrive in a conversation that has already started"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain why a fair representation of other positions strengthens my own. *(Guide: "Step 2: represent other positions fairly"; Practice question 3)*

## Skills

- I can map positions by claim, reasoning, evidence and assumptions. *(Guide: "Step 1: map the conversation"; Practice question 4)*
- I can choose signal verbs that fit what a source actually does. *(Guide: "Step 2: represent other positions fairly"; Practice question 5)*
- I can place my own claim as agreeing, disagreeing, qualifying or adding something new. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice questions 6 and 7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how a writer's paragraph enters a conversation and what it adds. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 7)*
- I can find where the real disagreement lies among several positions. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 6)*
- I can analyse how a public-domain text, such as the Declaration of Sentiments, enters a conversation by echoing and changing an earlier text. *(Guide: "A public-domain example of entering a conversation"; Practice question 8)*
