## Recap

- **Budget surplus:** tax revenue is greater than government purchases plus transfer payments in a year. **Deficit:** it is smaller. **Balanced budget:** they are equal.
- **Interest on the debt** is not part of G (it buys nothing), but it is government spending in the budget.
- A deficit is financed by **borrowing**: the government sells bonds.
- The **national debt** is the total the government owes: past deficits minus past surpluses.
- **Any deficit adds to the debt.** Only a surplus reduces it.
- **Burden of the debt:** interest must be paid every year; it uses funds that could go to other uses (opportunity cost); borrowed interest makes the debt grow faster; future taxpayers bear the cost; government borrowing may crowd out private investment (Topic 5.5).

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Formula | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Budget balance | T − (G + transfers + interest) | find a surplus (+) or deficit (−) |
| Debt after a deficit | start debt + deficit | update the debt |
| Debt after a surplus | start debt − surplus | update the debt |
| Interest bill | debt × interest rate | find the yearly cost of the debt |
| Interest share of revenue | interest ÷ tax revenue × 100 | measure the burden on the budget |
| Debt when all interest is borrowed | start debt × (1 + interest rate) | see the debt grow by itself |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Figures are for one government over one year (the budget) or at one date (the debt).
- Unless the question says otherwise, the deficit is financed entirely by borrowing, and a surplus is used to repay debt.
- If a question gives total "government spending" as one figure, it already includes everything on the spending side.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Mixing up deficit and debt** (flow v. stock).
2. **Saying a falling deficit lowers the debt.** The debt still rises.
3. **Leaving out transfers or interest** from the spending side.
4. **Wrong sign:** revenue minus spending, so negative = deficit.
5. **Thinking a surplus wipes out the debt.** It reduces it only by the size of the surplus.
6. **Treating borrowed interest as costless.** It adds to the debt and to next year's interest.

## Quick self-check

VD is the valda, the currency of the fictional country Valdoria used in the study guide.

1. T = 250, G = 200, transfers = 60, interest = 15 (VD billion). What is the balance? *(Deficit of VD 25 billion)*
2. The debt is VD 400 billion and the government runs a deficit of VD 30 billion. What is the new debt? *(VD 430 billion)*
3. The deficit falls from VD 50 billion to VD 20 billion. What happens to the debt? *(It still rises, by VD 20 billion)*
4. Debt of VD 300 billion at 4% interest, all borrowed. Debt one year later? *(VD 312 billion)*
