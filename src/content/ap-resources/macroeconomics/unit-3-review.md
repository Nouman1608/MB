Use this review after the [Unit 3 diagnostic](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/unit-3-diagnostic/). It shows how the nine topics fit together, then gives six questions that each combine two or more topics.

## Big ideas of the unit

- **AD is planned spending, C + I + G + NX, at each price level.** The wealth, interest rate and exchange rate effects give its slope; other changes in spending shift it. See [Topic 3.1](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-1-aggregate-demand-ad-study-guide/).
- **Multipliers size the shift.** Spending becomes someone's income, so AD moves by 1 ÷ MPS times a change in spending and MPC ÷ MPS times a change in taxes or transfers. See [Topic 3.2](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-2-multipliers-study-guide/).
- **Sticky wages make SRAS slope up**, so along it more output means more jobs and less unemployment. Changes in costs shift it. See [Topic 3.3](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-3-short-run-aggregate-supply-sras-study-guide/).
- **Flexible wages make LRAS vertical** at full-employment output (Yf), set by resources and technology, like a point on the PPC. See [Topic 3.4](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-4-long-run-aggregate-supply-lras-study-guide/).
- **AD meets SRAS in the short run; all three curves meet in the long run.** Left of LRAS is a recessionary gap, right of it an inflationary gap. See [Topic 3.5](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-5-equilibrium-aggregate-demand-aggregate-supply-study-guide/).
- **The signs tell you the shock.** Price level and output in the same direction: AD (demand-pull if AD rises). Opposite directions: SRAS (cost-push and stagflation if SRAS falls). See [Topic 3.6](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-6-changes-ad-as-model-short-study-guide/).
- **Left alone, the economy returns to Yf.** Wages adjust and SRAS shifts until AD meets LRAS; an AD shock changes only the long-run price level. See [Topic 3.7](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-7-long-run-self-adjustment-study-guide/).
- **Fiscal policy closes gaps sooner, but with lags.** See [Topic 3.8](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-8-fiscal-policy-study-guide/).
- **Automatic stabilizers act at once but only soften shocks:** tax revenue moves with real GDP, transfers against it. See [Topic 3.9](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-9-automatic-stabilizers-study-guide/).

## Key relationships and methods

| Idea | Relationship or method | Topic |
|---|---|---|
| MPC and MPS | ΔC ÷ ΔYd and ΔS ÷ ΔYd; MPC + MPS = 1 | 3.2 |
| Spending multiplier | 1 ÷ MPS, for a change in C, I, G or NX | 3.2 |
| Tax multiplier | −MPC ÷ MPS; transfers work the same way, opposite sign | 3.2, 3.8 |
| Shift or movement? | Price level: along a curve; spending: AD shifts; costs: SRAS shifts | 3.1, 3.3 |
| Equilibrium | Short run: AD = SRAS; long run: also on LRAS (Y = Yf) | 3.5 |
| Output gap | Actual real GDP − Yf (÷ Yf × 100 for a percentage) | 3.5 |
| Shocks | AD: PL and Y same direction; SRAS: opposite directions | 3.6 |
| Self-adjustment | Recessionary gap: wages ↓, SRAS right; inflationary: wages ↑, SRAS left | 3.7 |
| Sizing policy | ΔG = gap ÷ spending multiplier; ΔT = gap ÷ size of tax multiplier | 3.8 |
| Stabilizers | ΔYd = ΔY − ΔT + Δtransfers; ΔC = MPC × ΔYd | 3.9 |

## The questions

These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. The rubrics are a **suggested Marlbridge rubric**, not official scoring. All questions use **Valdoria**, a fictional country whose currency is the **valda (VD)**; data are fictional, in VD billion at base-year prices. Multipliers use the simple model (constant MPC, saving the only leakage).

## Question 1 (multiple choice · mixed)

Valdoria starts in long-run equilibrium with an MPC of **0.75**. Investment falls by **VD 10 billion** at every price level. SRAS slopes upward. What happens in the short run?

- (A) AD shifts left by VD 40 billion; real GDP falls by less than VD 40 billion and the price level falls.
- (B) AD shifts left by VD 40 billion; real GDP falls by exactly VD 40 billion and the price level is unchanged.
- (C) AD shifts left by VD 30 billion, because investment uses the tax multiplier.
- (D) AD shifts left by VD 10 billion; real GDP falls and the price level rises.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Multiplier = 1 ÷ 0.25 = 4, so AD shifts left by **VD 40 billion**. The surplus at the old price level pushes the price level down, which raises the quantity demanded along the new AD, so output falls by less than the shift.

- (B) would need a horizontal SRAS.
- (C) Investment is spending: use the spending multiplier.
- (D) ignores the multiplier, and a fall in AD **lowers** the price level.
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · mixed)

Valdoria is in long-run equilibrium. Workers and firms come to expect higher inflation, and new wage contracts raise costs. AD and resources do not change, and there is no policy. Which describes the short run and the long run?

- (A) Short run: price level up, real GDP down, unemployment up. Long run: falling wages shift SRAS back, and the economy returns to its original price level and Yf.
- (B) Short run: price level up, real GDP down. Long run: LRAS shifts left, and the lower output becomes the new Yf.
- (C) Short run: price level up, real GDP up, unemployment down. Long run: real GDP returns to Yf at a higher price level.
- (D) Short run: price level up, real GDP down. Long run: the higher price level shifts AD left until unemployment is back at the natural rate.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Higher wage costs shift SRAS left: stagflation. Unemployment above the natural rate then pushes nominal wages down, and SRAS shifts back until it meets AD on LRAS, neither of which has moved.

- (B) LRAS shifts only with resources or technology.
- (C) describes a rise in AD.
- (D) Self-adjustment shifts SRAS, not AD.
</details>

## Question 3 (constructed response · mixed)

Full-employment output is **VD 680 billion**, the natural rate of unemployment is **5.0%**, each VD 20 billion change in output moves unemployment by 1 percentage point, and the MPC is **0.8**.

| Price level | 96 | 100 | 104 | 108 | 112 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AD₁ | 720 | 700 | 680 | 660 | 640 |
| SRAS₁ | 640 | 660 | 680 | 700 | 720 |

(a) Show that Valdoria is in long-run equilibrium.
(b) Exports rise by **VD 8 billion** at every price level. Calculate the shift in AD and write the AD₂ schedule.
(c) Find the new short-run equilibrium, the inflation rate and the unemployment rate. Name the type of inflation and output gap.
(d) Explain why real output rises by less than the shift in AD.
(e) Draw a correctly labelled AD–AS graph showing the change.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** AD₁ = SRAS₁ = 680 at a price level of **104**. Output equals Yf, so the crossing is on LRAS and unemployment is 5.0%.

**(b)** Multiplier = 1 ÷ 0.2 = 5; AD shifts right by 5 × 8 = **VD 40 billion**. AD₂: **760, 740, 720, 700, 680**.

**(c)** AD₂ = SRAS₁ = **700** at **108**. Inflation = 4 ÷ 104 × 100 = **3.85%**. Output is 20 above Yf, so unemployment is **4.0%**. **Demand-pull** inflation; **positive (inflationary) gap** of 20.

**(d)** At 104 there is a shortage of 720 − 680 = 40, so the price level rises. Through the wealth, interest rate and exchange rate effects, quantity demanded falls along AD₂ from 720 to 700, so output rises by 20, not 40.

**(e)** Axes "Price level" and "Real GDP"; vertical LRAS at 680 through E₁ (104); AD₂ right of AD₁, crossing SRAS₁ at E₂ (108, 700), right of LRAS.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Equilibrium 104 and 680, on LRAS |
| 1 | Shift of +40 with the AD₂ schedule |
| 1 | New equilibrium 108 and 700 |
| 1 | Inflation 3.85% and unemployment 4.0% |
| 1 | Demand-pull inflation, positive gap |
| 1 | Rising price level lowers quantity demanded along AD₂ |
| 1 | Labelled graph with E₂ above and right of E₁, right of LRAS |

**Total: 7 points.** Accept values carried forward. "AD shifts left as prices rise" earns no point in (d).
</details>

## Question 4 (constructed response · mixed)

Use Question 3. There is no policy. A typical contract pays **VD 20 an hour**.

(a) Calculate the real wage, in base-year valdas, at E₁ and E₂. Explain why firms hired more workers.
(b) Explain what happens to wages and SRAS when contracts are renewed.
(c) Find the long-run price level and the shift in SRAS.
(d) Calculate the nominal wage that restores the original real wage. Compare the long-run outcome with E₁.
(e) Years later, new capital raises Yf to **VD 700 billion**; AD stays at AD₂. Calculate the growth rate of Yf and the new long-run price level. Why is VD 700 billion sustainable now but not at E₂?

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** E₁: 20 ÷ 1.04 = **VD 19.23**; E₂: 20 ÷ 1.08 = **VD 18.52**. Labour became cheaper in real terms, so extra output was profitable.

**(b)** Unemployment (4.0%) is below the natural rate, so workers are scarce and want back lost purchasing power. Nominal wages rise, raising costs: SRAS shifts **left**.

**(c)** AD₂ = 680 at **112**. SRAS₁ gave 720 there, so SRAS shifts **left by VD 40 billion**.

**(d)** 20 × 112 ÷ 104 = **VD 21.54** (+7.69%). Real GDP (680) and unemployment (5.0%) match E₁; the price level is 7.69% higher.

**(e)** Growth = 20 ÷ 680 × 100 = **2.94%**. LRAS moves to 700, which AD₂ reaches at **108**. At E₂, 700 was beyond capacity and relied on a real wage workers would not accept for long; now resources exist to produce it at the natural rate.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Real wages 19.23 and 18.52, linked to cheaper labour |
| 1 | Scarce workers push nominal wages up |
| 1 | Higher wages shift SRAS left |
| 1 | Long-run price level 112; SRAS left by 40 |
| 1 | Wage 21.54; same output and unemployment, higher price level |
| 1 | Growth 2.94% and price level 108 |
| 1 | Separates a temporary boom from a rise in capacity (LRAS shift) |

**Total: 7 points.** "LRAS shifts left to close the gap" earns no point.
</details>

## Question 5 (constructed response · mixed)

Valdoria's real GDP is **VD 760 billion**, full-employment output is **VD 800 billion**, and the MPC is **0.75**.

(a) Find the gap in VD billion and as a percentage of Yf, and compare unemployment with the natural rate.
(b) Calculate the increase in government spending, and the tax cut, that would each close the gap. Why is the tax cut larger?
(c) When the recession began, national income fell by **VD 40 billion** in the first round and **100,000** workers lost their jobs. Income tax takes **20%** of income; each unemployed worker receives **VD 25,000** a year. Calculate the first-round changes in tax revenue, transfers, disposable income and consumption, and compare with no automatic response.
(d) How did the budget balance change in (c)? Was this discretionary?
(e) Why did the stabilizers act sooner than the policy in (b) could?

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** 760 − 800 = **−VD 40 billion** (**−5.00%**): recessionary, with unemployment **above** the natural rate.

**(b)** Multipliers 4 and −3. ΔG = 40 ÷ 4 = **VD 10 billion**; tax cut = 40 ÷ 3 = **VD 13.33 billion**. G is spent in full in round one; a quarter of a tax cut is saved first.

**(c)** Tax: 0.2 × (−40) = **−8**. Transfers: 100,000 × 25,000 = **+VD 2.5 billion**. ΔYd = −40 + 8 + 2.5 = **−29.5**. ΔC = 0.75 × (−29.5) = **−VD 22.125 billion**, against −30 with no automatic response.

**(d)** It worsens by **VD 10.5 billion** with rates and benefit rules unchanged: **not** discretionary.

**(e)** The rules already exist, so they act as soon as incomes change. Discretionary policy must wait for the gap to be recognised, a plan agreed and the change carried out.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Gap −40 (−5.00%), unemployment above the natural rate |
| 1 | ΔG = 10 and tax cut = 13.33 |
| 1 | Part of a tax cut is saved before it is spent |
| 1 | Tax −8 and transfers +2.5 |
| 1 | ΔYd = −29.5 and ΔC = −22.125, compared with −30 |
| 1 | Balance worsens by 10.5 with no new decision |
| 1 | Stabilizers avoid recognition, decision and implementation lags |

**Total: 7 points.** Multiplying the gap by the multiplier earns no point in (b).
</details>

## Question 6 (constructed response · mixed)

Valdoria is in long-run equilibrium at a price level of 100 and full-employment output of **VD 600 billion**. A sharp, temporary rise in world oil prices moves short-run equilibrium to a price level of **106** and real GDP of **VD 580 billion**.

(a) Draw a correctly labelled AD–AS graph showing the initial equilibrium and the effect of the oil price rise. Label PL₁, PL₂, Y₂ and Yf.
(b) Explain why SRAS shifts but LRAS does not.
(c) State the inflation rate, the effect on unemployment and the term for this combination.
(d) The MPC is **0.8**. Treating the price level as constant, calculate the rise in government spending that would close the gap. Why would real GDP rise by less than the shift in AD?
(e) Compare the outcome soon after the policy in (d) with the long run under no policy.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** Axes "Price level" and "Real GDP"; vertical LRAS at 600; AD and SRAS₁ cross on it at PL₁ = 100. SRAS₂ lies left, crossing AD at PL₂ = 106 and Y₂ = 580.

**(b)** Oil is an input for almost every firm, so costs rise at every output level: SRAS shifts left. The rise is temporary and destroys no resources or technology, so capacity and LRAS are unchanged.

**(c)** **6.0%** inflation; unemployment **rises above** the natural rate; **stagflation** (cost-push inflation).

**(d)** Gap = −20; multiplier 5; ΔG = 20 ÷ 5 = **VD 4 billion**. Along SRAS₂ the shift raises the price level, which lowers the quantity demanded, so output rises by less than 20.

**(e)** With policy, output returns towards Yf sooner, but the price level rises **above 106**. With no policy, high unemployment pushes wages down and the oil shock fades, so SRAS shifts back: the economy returns to **100 and VD 600 billion**, after a long spell of high unemployment.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Graph: SRAS left, PL₂ above PL₁, Y₂ left of Yf |
| 1 | Higher input costs shift SRAS left |
| 1 | LRAS unchanged: no change in resources or technology |
| 1 | 6.0%, unemployment up, stagflation |
| 1 | ΔG = 4 |
| 1 | Output rises by less because the price level rises |
| 1 | Higher price level with policy against PL 100 and Yf, after slow adjustment, without it |

**Total: 7 points.** Shifting AD left in (a) earns no graph point.
</details>
