Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/1-1-scarcity-study-guide/).

## Recap

- **Scarcity:** resources are limited, but wants are unlimited. There is never enough to produce everything people want.
- Because most resources are scarce, **individuals and societies must make choices**.
- Every choice is a **trade-off**: using a resource one way means not using it another way.
- **Every** economy and every person faces scarcity, rich or poor. Time is scarce too.
- **Economic resources** (factors of production) are the inputs used to produce goods and services.
- A **free good** uses no scarce resources (air to breathe). A good with a zero price, such as a free school meal, is still scarce.

## Key relationships

| Resource | Test to identify it | Example | Income (background) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Land | Comes from nature, used in its natural state | farmland, river water, oil, fish | rent |
| Labour | Human effort, physical or mental | a teacher's work | wages |
| Capital | Made by people **and** used to produce | machine, van, factory, work laptop | interest |
| Entrepreneurship | Organises the other resources, takes risk, innovates | founder who risks her savings | profit |

| Scarcity | Shortage |
|---|---|
| Wants exceed resources | Buyers want more than sellers offer at the current price |
| Every economy, permanent | One market, usually temporary |

## Assumptions behind the ideas

- Wants are treated as unlimited: when one is satisfied, others appear.
- "Capital" means physical capital, not money.
- The amount of each resource is fixed at a moment in time, even if it can grow over years.
- Measuring opportunity cost and drawing the PPC belong to Topic 1.2.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Calling money capital.** Money buys capital; it produces nothing itself.
2. **Mixing up scarcity and shortage.** A sold-out shop shows a shortage.
3. **Saying rich countries escape scarcity.**
4. **Treating a zero-price good as a free good.**
5. **Limiting land to ground.** Water, minerals and forests are land too.
6. **Calling every boss an entrepreneur.** A salaried manager supplies labour; the entrepreneur bears the risk.

## Quick self-check

VD is the valda, the currency of the fictional country Valdoria used in the study guide.

1. Classify a fishing boat used by a Valdorian fishing firm. *(Capital)*
2. Classify the fish in the lake before they are caught. *(Land)*
3. A student has 10 free hours this week and wants 6 hours for a part-time job, 5 for revision and 3 for football. How many hours short is she, and what does this show? *(4 hours short; scarcity of time forces her to choose)*
4. A bakery's VD 8,000 bank balance: which resource? *(None in itself; money is used to buy resources)*
