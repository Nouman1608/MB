## Recap

- **Financial capital** is money used to buy financial assets (bonds, shares, deposits). It moves between countries in search of the best **real** return.
- Real interest rate = nominal interest rate − expected inflation.
- Funds flow **towards** the country whose real interest rate is relatively **higher**.
- The receiving country's currency is in higher demand, so it **appreciates**; its net exports **fall**.
- The inflow raises the **supply of loanable funds** at home; the outflow lowers it in the other country. The two real rates move closer together.
- A central bank can change the domestic interest rate in the **short run**, and so change net capital inflows.

## Key relationships

| Change in Valdoria (Kestria unchanged) | Capital flow | Valda | Valdoria's NX | Flow's effect on Valdoria's loanable funds | Kestria's real rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Real rate rises (contractionary policy, more government borrowing, more investment demand) | inflow rises | appreciates | fall | supply rises | rises |
| Real rate falls (expansionary policy, more saving) | inflow falls | depreciates | rise | supply falls | falls |

Valdoria (valda, VD) and Kestria (kesta, KS) are the fictional countries used in the study guide.

**Balance of payments:** CA + CFA = 0. A larger capital and financial account surplus (net inflow) goes with a larger current account deficit.

## Assumptions

- Flexible exchange rates set in the foreign exchange market.
- Other things equal: investors respond to real interest rates only (risk and expected currency changes are ignored).
- You predict directions, not sizes.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Comparing nominal rates.** Subtract expected inflation first.
2. **Inflow → depreciation.** It is an **appreciation**.
3. **Shifting loanable funds demand for a capital flow.** Capital flows shift **supply**.
4. **Ignoring the other country.** Its real rate moves the opposite way to the flow.
5. **"Tight money raises net exports."** It lowers them.
6. **Same signs for CA and CFA.** They have opposite signs.

## Quick self-check

1. Valdoria: nominal rate 6%, expected inflation 2.5%. Kestria: nominal 4%, expected inflation 0.5%. Which way does capital flow? *(Neither, other things equal: both real rates are 3.5%)*
2. Valdoria's real rate rises above Kestria's. What happens to the kesta? *(It depreciates)*
3. Valdoria's CFA is +VD 12 billion. What is its CA? *(−VD 12 billion)*
4. Kestria's central bank lowers its interest rate. What happens to Valdoria's net capital inflow? *(It rises)*
