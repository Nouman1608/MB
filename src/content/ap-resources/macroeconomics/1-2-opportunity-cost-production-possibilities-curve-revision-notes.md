## Recap

- Scarcity forces **tradeoffs**. The **opportunity cost** of a choice is the next best alternative given up.
- A **PPC** shows the maximum combinations of two goods an economy can make with **all** its resources fully and efficiently used and fixed technology.
- **On** the curve: efficient, full employment of resources. **Inside**: inefficient, some resources unemployed or badly used. **Outside**: unattainable with current resources and technology.
- **Movement along** the curve = a different choice of output. **Shift** = a change in capacity.
- **Shape:** straight = constant opportunity cost; bowed outward = increasing (specialised resources, the usual case); bowed inward = decreasing.
- **Outward shift (growth):** more or better land, labour, capital or entrepreneurship, or better technology/productivity. **Inward shift (contraction):** resources or productivity lost.
- A change that helps only one good **pivots** the curve outward along that good's axis.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Rule | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Total opportunity cost | amount of the other good given up | describe a move along the PPC |
| Per-unit opportunity cost of X | Y given up ÷ extra X gained | compare costs step by step |
| Opportunity cost of Y | 1 ÷ (opportunity cost of X) | swap the direction |
| Slope of PPC (size) | opportunity cost of the horizontal-axis good | read costs from a graph |
| Moving from inside to the curve | opportunity cost = 0 | explain why idle resources are wasteful |

## Assumptions behind the model

- Two goods (or two groups of goods), one time period.
- Quantity and quality of resources fixed.
- Technology fixed.
- Change any of these and the curve shifts.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Calling unemployment a shift.** It is a point inside the curve.
2. **Giving a total when a per-unit cost is asked.** Divide by the extra units gained.
3. **Inverting the ratio.** For the cost of one X, the good given up goes on top.
4. **Mixing up shapes.** Bowed outward means **increasing** opportunity cost.
5. **Shifting the whole curve** when technology improves only one good.
6. **Unlabelled graphs.** Label both axes with the goods and the curve "PPC".
7. **Dropping the units.** Say "2 tonnes of rice per bicycle", not "2".

## Quick self-check

1. A country can make 80 units of X or 20 units of Y, with constant opportunity cost. What is the opportunity cost of 1 Y? *(4 units of X)*
2. In the study guide, Valdoria moves from 40 to 50 thousand bicycles and rice falls from 60 to 36 thousand tonnes. What is the opportunity cost per bicycle? *(24 ÷ 10 = 2.4 tonnes of rice)*
3. A recession leaves many workers unemployed. Where is the economy on its PPC? *(Inside the curve; the PPC does not shift.)*
4. Valdoria builds many new factories. What happens to its PPC in the future? *(It shifts outward.)*
