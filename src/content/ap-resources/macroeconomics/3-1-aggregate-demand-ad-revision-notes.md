## Recap

- **Aggregate demand** is the relationship between the **price level** and the quantity of **real GDP** that households (C), firms (I), the government (G) and the rest of the world (NX) want to buy.
- Axes: **Price level (PL)** on the vertical axis, **Real GDP (Y)** on the horizontal axis.
- The AD curve **slopes downward** because of three effects, not because buyers switch between goods.
- A change in the price level gives a **movement along** AD. A change in spending for any other reason **shifts** AD.

## Key relationships

| Effect or shifter | Chain of reasoning | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Real wealth effect | PL ↑ → real value of money and savings ↓ → C ↓ | Move up along AD |
| Interest rate effect | PL ↑ → demand for money ↑ → interest rates ↑ → I ↓ | Move up along AD |
| Exchange rate effect | PL ↑ → interest rates ↑ → currency appreciates → exports ↓, imports ↑ → NX ↓ | Move up along AD |
| Consumer wealth or confidence ↑, income taxes ↓, transfers ↑ | C ↑ at every PL | AD shifts right |
| Business expectations ↑, policy interest rates ↓, business taxes ↓ | I ↑ at every PL | AD shifts right |
| Government buys more goods and services | G ↑ at every PL | AD shifts right |
| Foreign incomes ↑, currency depreciates (not caused by PL) | NX ↑ at every PL | AD shifts right |

The opposite change in each shifter shifts AD left.

## Assumptions behind the model

- Real GDP is measured at constant (base-year) prices.
- Along one AD curve, everything except the price level is held constant.
- G is set by government decisions and does not respond to the price level here.
- The **size** of a shift is the subject of Topic 3.2 (multipliers).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Price and Quantity** as axis labels. Write Price level and Real GDP.
2. Saying a higher price level **decreases AD**. It decreases the **quantity** of real GDP demanded.
3. Using **substitution between goods** to explain the slope.
4. Treating a **policy** rise in interest rates as a movement. It shifts AD left.
5. Saying a tax cut raises **G**. It raises disposable income and so **C**.
6. Forgetting that **imports are subtracted**: more imports lower NX.

## Quick self-check

1. Valdoria's price level falls. Shift or movement? *(Movement down along AD; quantity of real GDP demanded rises.)*
2. At one price level, C = 700, I = 150, G = 250 and NX = −30 (VD billion). What is the quantity of real GDP demanded? *(VD 1,070 billion)*
3. Real GDP demanded is VD 20 billion higher at every price level. What happened to AD? *(It shifted right by VD 20 billion.)*
4. Which effect links a higher price level to lower investment? *(The interest rate effect.)*
