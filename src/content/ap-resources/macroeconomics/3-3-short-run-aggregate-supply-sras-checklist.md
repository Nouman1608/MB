Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-3-short-run-aggregate-supply-sras-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-3-short-run-aggregate-supply-sras-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define the SRAS curve and say what "short run" means in macroeconomics. *(Guide: "What the SRAS curve shows")*
- I can explain why sticky wages and prices make the SRAS curve slope upward, without using the law of supply. *(Guide: "Why the SRAS curve slopes upward"; Practice Q1)*
- I can list the determinants of SRAS and say which direction each one shifts the curve. *(Guide: "What shifts the SRAS curve"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why a rise in expected inflation shifts SRAS to the left. *(Guide: "What shifts the SRAS curve"; Practice Q2, Q7(a))*

## Skills

- I can draw an SRAS curve with the axes labelled price level and real GDP, and show a shift with SRAS₁ and SRAS₂. *(Guide: Figures 1 and 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can read a new SRAS schedule after a shift and find the output supplied at a given price level. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate the inflation rate and the unemployment rate for two points on an SRAS curve. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6)*
- I can calculate a real wage when the nominal wage is fixed and the price level rises. *(Guide: Worked example 1(c); Practice Q7(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can tell a movement along SRAS from a shift of SRAS, and tell an SRAS shift from an AD shift. *(Guide: Worked example 2 "Check"; Practice Q2, Q7(a))*
- I can explain why moving up the SRAS curve raises employment and lowers unemployment when the labour force is fixed. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3, Q6(c))*
- I can describe the short-run trade-off between inflation and unemployment. *(Guide: Worked example 1(d); Practice Q6(d))*
