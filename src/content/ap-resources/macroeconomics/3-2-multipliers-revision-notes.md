## Recap

- A change in **autonomous** spending (C, I, G or NX) becomes someone's income, which is partly spent again. Total spending and output change by **more** than the first change.
- The **MPC** is the share of an **extra** unit of disposable income that is spent; the **MPS** is the share saved.
- The **expenditure multiplier** gives the change in AD per VD 1 change in autonomous spending.
- The **tax multiplier** gives the change in AD per VD 1 change in taxes. It is negative and smaller in size, because a tax change works through disposable income and part of it is saved first.
- Both multipliers depend only on the MPC: a higher MPC means bigger multipliers.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Formula | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| MPC | ΔC ÷ ΔDI | find how much of extra income is spent |
| MPS | ΔS ÷ ΔDI = 1 − MPC | find how much of extra income is saved |
| Spending multiplier | 1 ÷ MPS = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC) | multiply a change in C, I, G or NX |
| Tax multiplier | −MPC ÷ MPS | multiply a change in taxes |
| Transfer change | +MPC ÷ MPS | multiply a change in transfer payments |
| Change in AD | multiplier × initial change | find the size of the AD shift |
| Change needed | target change in AD ÷ multiplier | work backwards to a policy |

Quick reference: MPC 0.5 → 2 and −1; MPC 0.75 → 4 and −3; MPC 0.8 → 5 and −4; MPC 0.9 → 10 and −9.

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The MPC is constant in every round.
- Saving is the only leakage (imports and income taxes are ignored).
- The price level does not change, so the shift in AD equals the change in real GDP.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Writing the spending multiplier as **1 ÷ MPC**.
2. Using **totals** (C ÷ DI) instead of **changes** for the MPC.
3. Using the **spending** multiplier for a **tax** change.
4. Losing the **sign**: a tax rise lowers AD.
5. Saying a **balanced-budget** rise in spending and taxes has no effect. It raises AD by the amount of the spending change.
6. Saying a **higher MPS** makes the multiplier bigger. It makes it smaller.

## Quick self-check

VD is the valda, the currency of the fictional country Valdoria used in the study guide.

1. The MPS is 0.2. What are the spending and tax multipliers? *(5 and −4)*
2. With MPS 0.2, government spending falls by VD 10 billion. What is the change in AD? *(−VD 50 billion)*
3. The MPC is 0.6 and taxes rise by VD 20 billion. What is the change in AD? *(−VD 30 billion: tax multiplier −1.5)*
