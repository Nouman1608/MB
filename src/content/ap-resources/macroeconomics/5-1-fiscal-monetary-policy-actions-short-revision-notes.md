## Recap

- **Fiscal policy** (government): government spending changes AD directly; taxes and transfers change it indirectly, through disposable income and consumption.
- **Monetary policy** (central bank): changes the policy rate, by changing administered rates (ample reserves) or the money supply (limited reserves). Interest rates then change investment and consumption.
- To close a **recessionary gap**, use **expansionary** fiscal and monetary policy. To close an **inflationary gap**, use **contractionary** fiscal and monetary policy.
- A combined policy can influence **AD, real output, the price level and interest rates**.

## Key relationships

| Mix | AD, real output, price level | Nominal interest rate |
|---|---|---|
| Both expansionary | Rise | Uncertain |
| Both contractionary | Fall | Uncertain |
| Expansionary fiscal + contractionary monetary | Uncertain | Rises |
| Contractionary fiscal + expansionary monetary | Uncertain | Falls |

| Calculation | Formula |
|---|---|
| Spending multiplier | 1 ÷ MPS |
| Tax multiplier | −MPC ÷ MPS |
| Shift from a change in G or in investment | spending multiplier × change |
| Shift from a change in taxes | tax multiplier × change in taxes |
| Combined shift in AD | fiscal shift + monetary shift |

**Why the interest rate is uncertain when both expand:** monetary policy lowers it, but higher income from fiscal expansion raises money demand and pushes it up.

## Assumptions

- The multiplier gives the **shift in AD**. With upward-sloping SRAS some of it raises the price level, so calculated policy sizes are the **minimum** needed.
- A change in investment caused by interest rates uses the **spending** multiplier.
- Graphs: output and price level on **AD–AS**; the nominal interest rate on a **money market** (or reserve market) graph.
- Out of scope here: crowding out and the national debt (Topics 5.4–5.5); exchange-rate effects (Unit 6).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying two expansionary policies **must** lower interest rates.
2. Adding the actions (VD 6 billion + VD 4 billion) instead of their **multiplied** effects.
3. Using the spending multiplier for a **tax** change.
4. Treating equal-sized opposite actions as cancelling out.
5. Calling a policy "recessionary"; gaps are recessionary or inflationary, policies are expansionary or contractionary.

## Quick self-check

VD is the valda, the currency of the fictional country Valdoria used in the study guide.

1. MPC = 0.8 and there is a recessionary gap of VD 50 billion. What is the minimum rise in government spending that closes it alone? *(VD 10 billion)*
2. MPC = 0.75. The government cuts taxes by VD 3 billion. By how much does AD shift? *(+VD 9 billion)*
3. Government spending rises by VD 5 billion and a rate rise cuts investment by VD 5 billion (MPC 0.75). Net shift in AD, and effect on the interest rate? *(Zero; interest rate rises)*
