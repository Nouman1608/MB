## Recap

- The **nominal interest rate** is the rate paid on a loan, **not adjusted for inflation**. It measures the return in money.
- The **real interest rate** is adjusted for inflation. It measures the return in **purchasing power**.
- When a loan is agreed, lenders and borrowers set the nominal rate as the **expected real rate plus expected inflation**.
- After the event, the **real rate in hindsight** is the nominal rate minus the **actual** inflation rate.
- Unexpected inflation makes the real rate lower than planned (borrowers gain). Unexpectedly low inflation, or deflation, makes it higher (lenders gain).
- The real rate is **negative** whenever inflation is above the nominal rate.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Formula | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Setting the nominal rate | nominal = expected real + expected inflation | find the rate written in a new loan |
| Real rate in hindsight | real = nominal − actual inflation | find what a lender actually earned |
| Expected real rate | expected real = nominal − expected inflation | find what both sides planned |
| Inflation from a price index | (new index − old index) ÷ old index × 100 | get the inflation rate to subtract |
| Expected inflation ↑ (expected real unchanged) | nominal ↑ by the same number of points | predict changes in nominal rates |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The nominal rate is fixed for the whole loan.
- "Inflation" means the percentage change in a price index such as the CPI over the period of the loan.
- The course uses subtraction. The exact division method gives slightly different answers and is background only.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Adding** inflation to get the real rate. Real = nominal **minus** inflation.
2. Using **expected** inflation for a real rate in hindsight. Use **actual** inflation.
3. Subtracting the **CPI level** instead of the inflation rate.
4. Forgetting that subtracting **negative** inflation (deflation) raises the real rate.
5. Saying all inflation hurts lenders. Only **unexpected** inflation does.
6. Judging a savings account by its nominal rate alone.

## Quick self-check

1. Lenders want a real rate of 2% and expect 5% inflation. What nominal rate do they charge? *(7%)*
2. A loan has a nominal rate of 6%. Inflation turns out to be 9%. What is the real rate in hindsight? *(−3%)*
3. A deposit pays 4% nominal and prices fall by 1%. What is the real rate? *(5%)*
4. Expected inflation rises from 3% to 5% and the expected real rate stays at 2%. What happens to the nominal rate? *(It rises from 5% to 7%.)*
