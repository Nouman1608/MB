## Recap

- Inflation that **lasts** is caused by the money supply growing **too fast for too long**. A sustained **fall** in the money supply causes **deflation**.
- **Equation of exchange:** M × V = P × Y. It is always true, because velocity is defined as nominal GDP ÷ M.
- **Quantity theory of money:** velocity is stable and real output is fixed at full employment by real resources, so in the long run the growth rate of M sets the inflation rate.
- **Long-run neutrality:** at full employment, money changes nominal values only. Real GDP is unchanged in the long run.
- **AD–AS story:** more money shifts AD right; output rises above Yf in the short run; nominal wages rise and SRAS shifts left; output returns to Yf at a higher price level.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Formula | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Equation of exchange | M × V = P × Y | link money to spending |
| Velocity | V = nominal GDP ÷ M = (P × Y) ÷ M | find how often money is spent |
| Price level | P = (M × V) ÷ Y | find P from money, velocity and output |
| Real output | Y = (M × V) ÷ P | find real GDP |
| Money supply | M = (P × Y) ÷ V | find the money needed |
| Growth form (approx.) | %ΔM + %ΔV ≈ %ΔP + %ΔY | find long-run inflation from growth rates |
| Constant V | inflation ≈ %ΔM − %ΔY | the most common exam case |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- V is **stable**. If a question gives a change in V, include it.
- Y is at **full employment** in the long run (vertical LRAS).
- P is a price **level** (index ÷ 100), not an inflation rate.
- The growth-rate rule is an approximation; levels give the exact answer.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Treating P as the inflation rate.** Inflation is the % change in P.
2. **Forgetting real growth.** 8% money growth with 3% real growth and constant V gives about 5% inflation, not 8%.
3. **Saying money raises real GDP in the long run.** Only in the short run.
4. **Calling velocity an amount of money.** It is a number of times per year.
5. **Confusing a one-time price rise with inflation.** Continuing inflation needs continuing money growth.
6. **Ignoring a change in V** when the question gives one.

## Quick self-check

VD is the valda, the currency of the fictional country Valdoria used in the study guide.

1. M = VD 150 billion and nominal GDP = VD 900 billion. What is V? *(6)*
2. M = 300, V = 3, Y = 750. What is P? *(1.2, an index of 120)*
3. Money grows 6% a year, V is constant and real GDP grows 2% a year. About what is long-run inflation? *(4%)*
4. The economy is at full employment and M rises 10% once. What happens to real GDP and the price level in the long run? *(Real GDP unchanged; price level 10% higher, if V is constant)*
