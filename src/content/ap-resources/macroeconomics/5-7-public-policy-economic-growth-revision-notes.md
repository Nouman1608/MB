## Recap

- **Economic growth** = growth in **real GDP per capita**. Policy can raise it by raising **productivity** (output per worker) or **labour force participation** (more of the population at work).
- **Productivity** rises with more **physical capital**, more **human capital** and better **technology** per worker.
- **Public investment** in infrastructure (roads, ports, power, broadband), education and research raises productivity.
- **Supply-side fiscal policies** use taxes, spending and benefit rules to change **incentives** to work, save, invest and innovate, raising aggregate supply and potential output.
- Most growth policies raise **AD** in the short run; their lasting effect is a **rightward shift of LRAS** and an **outward shift of the PPC**.

## Key relationships

| Policy | Short run | Long run |
|---|---|---|
| Lower marginal tax rates on work | AD right (more consumption) | Participation and hours rise: SRAS and LRAS right |
| Lower taxes on saving or investment | Small AD effect | More capital per worker: LRAS right |
| Public infrastructure | AD right (G rises) | Productivity rises once in use: LRAS right |
| Funding education, training, research | AD right (G rises) | Human capital and technology rise: LRAS right |
| One-off cash payment to households | AD right | No change in capacity: LRAS unchanged |

**Growth of real GDP per capita ≈ growth of productivity + growth of employed share of population.**

## Assumptions behind the analysis

- The model gives the **direction** of incentive effects, not their size.
- After a supply-side policy, real output rises, but the **price level** depends on the relative sizes of the AD and AS shifts.
- Growth policies take time; training and research pay off years later.
- Borrowing to pay for a policy can **crowd out** private investment (Topic 5.5).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Calling every fiscal stimulus a growth policy. Only a rise in **capacity** gives lasting growth.
2. Drawing a supply-side policy with AS shifts only: show the **AD** shift too.
3. Stating that a supply-side tax cut must lower the price level.
4. Measuring growth by real GDP instead of real GDP **per capita**.
5. Forgetting the **opportunity cost**: more capital goods now means fewer consumer goods now.
6. Shifting SRAS alone for a lasting change in capacity: **LRAS** must shift.

## Quick self-check

VD is the valda, the currency of the fictional country Valdoria used in the study guide.

1. Output per worker is VD 60,000 and 45% of the population is employed. What is real GDP per capita? *(VD 27,000)*
2. Productivity grows 3% and the employed share of the population grows 2%. About how fast does real GDP per capita grow? *(about 5%; exactly 5.06%)*
3. A government gives every household a one-off payment while the economy is at full employment. What happens to LRAS? *(Nothing: it is a demand policy, so the long-run result is a higher price level.)*
