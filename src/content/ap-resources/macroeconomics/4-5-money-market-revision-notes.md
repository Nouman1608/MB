## Recap

- The **money market** compares the money people want to hold with the money that exists. Its "price" is the **nominal interest rate**, the opportunity cost of holding money instead of bonds.
- **Money demand (MD)** slopes **down**: a higher nominal rate makes holding money more costly, so people hold less.
- **Money supply (MS)** is **vertical**: given the monetary base set by the central bank, the quantity of money does not depend on the interest rate.
- **Equilibrium:** the nominal rate where quantity of money demanded = quantity supplied.
- Out of equilibrium, people buy or sell **bonds**, which moves bond prices and so interest rates back to equilibrium.

## Key relationships

| Situation | What happens | Effect on nominal interest rate |
|---|---|---|
| Rate above equilibrium | Surplus of money; people buy bonds; bond prices rise | Falls |
| Rate below equilibrium | Shortage of money; people sell bonds; bond prices fall | Rises |
| Price level rises | MD shifts right | Rises |
| Real GDP rises | MD shifts right | Rises |
| Price level or real GDP falls | MD shifts left | Falls |
| Central bank buys bonds (expansionary) | MS shifts right | Falls |
| Central bank sells bonds (contractionary) | MS shifts left | Rises |

## Assumptions behind the model

- Money earns little or no interest; bonds earn interest.
- The monetary base, and so the money supply, is set by the central bank and does not respond to the interest rate.
- Bond prices and interest rates move in opposite directions (Topic 4.1).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Real** interest rate on the axis. Use the **nominal** rate.
2. MS drawn with an upward slope. It is **vertical**.
3. Shifting MD when the interest rate changes. That is a **movement along** MD.
4. Saying a surplus of money **raises** the rate. It **lowers** it.
5. Saying a higher price level increases the money **supply**. It increases money **demand**.
6. Unlabelled axes or curves. Write "Nominal interest rate", "Quantity of money", MS and MD.

## Quick self-check

VD is the valda, the currency of the fictional country Valdoria used in the study guide.

1. Money demand is Qd = 500 − 25i (VD bn, i in %). The money supply is VD 400 bn. What is the equilibrium nominal interest rate? *(4%)*
2. In the same market, what is the situation at 6%? *(Quantity demanded is VD 350 bn, so there is a surplus of money of VD 50 bn and the rate will fall.)*
3. Real GDP falls. What happens to MD and the nominal interest rate? *(MD shifts left; the rate falls.)*
