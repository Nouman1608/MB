## Understanding

- I can define market equilibrium and explain why the price stays there until something shifts a curve. *(Guide: "What equilibrium means")*
- I can define a surplus and a shortage and say whether each happens above or below the equilibrium price. *(Guide: "Disequilibrium: surpluses and shortages"; Practice Q1)*
- I can tell a movement along a curve from a shift of a curve, and say which one happens while the price adjusts. *(Guide: "How prices restore equilibrium"; Practice Q2)*

## Calculation

- I can find the equilibrium price and quantity from a demand and supply schedule. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(a), Q7(a))*
- I can calculate the size of a surplus or shortage by comparing Qd and Qs at the same price. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5(b), Q7(c))*
- I can build a new schedule after a shift and find the new equilibrium. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5(c), Q7(b))*

## Graphing

- I can draw a correctly labelled supply and demand graph with the equilibrium price and quantity marked on the axes. *(Guide: "Showing a change on a graph"; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can show a shift of demand or supply on a graph and label the new equilibrium P₂ and Q₂. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q6(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how a surplus or shortage makes the price adjust until the market clears. *(Guide: "How prices restore equilibrium"; Practice Q5(d), Q6(c))*
- I can decide which curve a change affects and predict the effect on equilibrium price and quantity. *(Guide: "Changes in equilibrium: when a curve shifts"; Practice Q3)*
- I can analyse two shifts at once, saying which variable is certain and which is indeterminate, and why. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4, Q6(d), Q7(d))*
