## Understanding

- I can explain why inflation that lasts is caused by money growth that is too fast for too long, and why a sustained fall in the money supply causes deflation. *(Guide: "Inflation as a monetary event"; Practice Q7(a), Q7(c))*
- I can state the equation of exchange and say what M, V, P and Y each measure. *(Guide: "The equation of exchange"; Practice Q1)*
- I can define the quantity theory of money and name its two assumptions. *(Guide: "The quantity theory of money"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why money does not change real output in the long run when the economy is at full employment. *(Guide: "The quantity theory of money"; Practice Q2, Q4)*

## Calculation

- I can find velocity from nominal GDP and the money supply. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can rearrange MV = PY to find the price level, real output or the money supply. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5(a)–(c), Q7(b))*
- I can estimate long-run inflation from the growth rates of money, velocity and real GDP. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q7(a))*
- I can calculate an exact inflation rate from price levels and compare it with the growth-rate estimate. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5(b), Q7(b))*

## Reasoning and graphs

- I can draw an AD–AS graph showing the short-run and long-run effects of an increase in the money supply. *(Guide: "Showing it on an AD–AS graph"; Practice Q4, Q6)*
- I can explain how a change in velocity changes inflation for a given rate of money growth. *(Guide: "When velocity changes"; Practice Q5(d))*
- I can explain why a one-time rise in the money supply gives a one-time rise in the price level, not continuing inflation. *(Practice Q7(c))*
