## Recap

- The **SRAS curve** shows the real output all firms supply at each **price level** in the short run.
- **Short run:** nominal wages and some other input prices are fixed.
- It slopes **upward** because wages and prices are **sticky**: when the price level rises, costs lag, profit per unit rises and firms produce more. The real wage falls.
- A change in **production costs** shifts the curve. A change in the **price level** moves the economy along it.
- Moving **up** SRAS: output ↑, employment ↑, unemployment rate ↓ (labour force fixed). This is the **short-run trade-off** between inflation and unemployment.

## Key relationships

| Change | Effect on SRAS |
|---|---|
| Price level rises | Movement up along SRAS |
| Nominal wages or other input prices rise | Shift left (decrease) |
| Expected inflation rises | Shift left: higher wage demands |
| Productivity rises | Shift right (increase) |
| Producer subsidies, lower business taxes | Shift right |
| Bad harvest, natural disaster | Shift left |
| Consumer confidence, household taxes | No SRAS effect: AD shifts |

## Assumptions

- Some input prices, especially nominal wages, are fixed for a period.
- The labour force is constant when linking output to unemployment.
- Left and up describe the same decrease; right and down the same increase.
- The long run, when all prices adjust, is Topic 3.4.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Labelling the axes **"Price" and "Quantity"**.
2. Explaining the slope with the **law of supply** or with AD's wealth or interest-rate effects.
3. Shifting SRAS because the **price level** changed.
4. Shifting SRAS **right** when expected inflation rises.
5. Shifting SRAS for a change in **household** spending.
6. Saying a lower unemployment rate comes from a smaller labour force. Along SRAS it comes from **more jobs**, with the labour force fixed.
7. Comparing two price levels on one curve and calling it a shift. To show a shift, compare the **same** price level on two curves.

## Quick self-check

VD is the valda, the currency of the fictional country Valdoria used in the study guide.

1. The price level rises from 100 to 108 and a contract fixes the nominal wage at VD 24 an hour. What is the real wage in base-period valdas? *(VD 22.22)*
2. Firms supply VD 40 billion less at every price level. Left or right? *(Left: a decrease)*
3. Labour force 25.0 million, employment rises from 23.50 to 24.00 million. New unemployment rate? *(4.0%)*
