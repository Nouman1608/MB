## Recap

- Foreign buyers pay in **their own** currency, so a change in the exchange rate changes what they pay even when producers keep their prices.
- When a currency **appreciates**, the country's goods become **dearer** abroad and foreign goods become **cheaper** at home: exports fall, imports rise, **net exports fall**.
- When a currency **depreciates**, the country's goods become **cheaper** abroad and foreign goods **dearer** at home: exports rise, imports fall, **net exports rise**.
- NX is a component of AD, and this change is not caused by the price level, so **AD shifts**.
- Short run: AD up → real output, employment and the price level rise. AD down → all three fall.

## Key relationships

Exchange rate written as kestas per valda (KS per VD). Valdoria (valda) and Kestria (kesta) are the fictional countries used in the study guide.

| Relationship | Rule | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Home good priced abroad | price in KS = price in VD × (KS per VD) | find what foreign buyers pay |
| Foreign good priced at home | price in VD = price in KS ÷ (KS per VD) | find what home buyers pay |
| Net exports | NX = X − M | find the trade balance |
| AD | AD = C + I + G + NX | see that NX shifts AD |
| Spending multiplier | 1 ÷ (1 − MPC) | size the shift in AD from a change in NX |

## The chain to write out

cause → currency demand or supply shifts → appreciation or depreciation → exports and imports change → NX changes → AD shifts → output, employment and price level change (short run)

## Assumptions

- Other things equal: incomes, tastes and producers' own prices do not change.
- The exchange rate is flexible and set by the foreign exchange market.
- You predict directions, not sizes of trade responses.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **"Stronger currency, more exports."** It is the opposite.
2. **Upside-down conversions.** Multiply by KS per VD to go from VD to KS; divide to go back.
3. **Adding imports to AD.** Imports are subtracted.
4. **Movement instead of shift.** A currency change not caused by the price level shifts AD.
5. **Both countries gaining NX.** If Valdoria's NX with Kestria rise, Kestria's fall.
6. **Stopping at NX** when the question asks about output, unemployment or the price level.

## Quick self-check

1. A Valdorian good costs VD 80. What does it cost in Kestria at 1 VD = 6 KS, and at 1 VD = 5 KS? *(KS 480, then KS 400)*
2. A Kestrian good costs KS 300. What does it cost in Valdoria at 1 VD = 6 KS, and at 1 VD = 5 KS? *(VD 50, then VD 60)*
3. In questions 1 and 2, did the valda appreciate or depreciate, and what happens to Valdoria's NX? *(Depreciated; NX rise)*
4. Exports are VD 90 billion and imports VD 100 billion. What are net exports? *(−VD 10 billion, a trade deficit)*
