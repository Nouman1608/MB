Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/6-6-real-interest-rates-international-capital-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/6-6-real-interest-rates-international-capital-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define financial capital and international capital flows, and tell financial capital apart from physical capital. *(Guide: "Why financial capital moves between countries")*
- I can explain why financial capital flows towards the country with the relatively higher real interest rate. *(Guide: "Why financial capital moves between countries"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain how a central bank's short-run change in the interest rate, extra borrowing or a change in saving changes net capital inflows. *(Guide: "What changes real interest rates", Worked example 2; Practice Q6(a)–(b), Q7(a))*

## Calculation

- I can calculate real interest rates from nominal rates and expected inflation and use them to predict the direction of capital flows. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5(a)–(c))*
- I can use CA + CFA = 0 to find the current account balance from the capital and financial account balance. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4, Q5(d))*

## Reasoning and graphs

- I can show on a foreign exchange market graph how a capital inflow makes a currency appreciate and an outflow makes it depreciate. *(Guide: Figure 1(c); Practice Q2, Q6(c), Q7(b))*
- I can show how a capital flow shifts the supply of loanable funds in both countries and explain why the real rates move closer together. *(Guide: Figure 1(a)–(b); Practice Q3, Q6(e), Q7(d))*
- I can carry the chain from a change in real interest rates through the exchange rate to net exports and aggregate demand. *(Guide: "Writing the chain in an exam answer"; Practice Q6(d), Q7(c))*
- I can explain why the exchange-rate effect of monetary policy works in the same direction as its effect on investment. *(Guide: Worked example 2(d))*
- I can explain why a foreign country's policy can have effects on domestic aggregate demand that pull in opposite directions. *(Practice Q7(e))*
