## Understanding

- I can define nominal GDP as spending on output at current prices and real GDP as output at constant base-year prices. *(Guide: "Two ways GDP can grow"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why real GDP, not nominal GDP, shows whether the economy is producing more. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(c))*
- I can explain why the GDP deflator is 100 in the base year and what a deflator above or below 100 means. *(Guide: "The calculations"; Practice Q4)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate nominal GDP and real GDP from a table of prices and quantities. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate the GDP deflator from nominal and real GDP. *(Practice Q2, Q5(b))*
- I can convert nominal GDP into real GDP using the deflator, dividing rather than multiplying. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q6(a))*
- I can calculate growth rates of real GDP and nominal GDP, and an inflation rate from the deflator. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 4; Practice Q6(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why real GDP is larger than nominal GDP before the base year. *(Guide: Worked example 2(d); Practice Q6(d))*
- I can show, with numbers, that changing the base year changes measured real growth. *(Guide: "Why base-year prices can overstate growth"; Practice Q7(a)–(b))*
- I can explain why fixed base-year prices tend to overstate real growth and how chain weighting responds. *(Guide: "Why base-year prices can overstate growth"; Practice Q7(c)–(d))*
