## Recap

- Banks record their position on a **balance sheet (T-account)**: assets = liabilities + owners' equity.
- **Fractional reserve banking:** banks keep only a fraction of deposits as reserves and lend the rest.
- Reserves split into **required reserves** (set by the required reserve ratio) and **excess reserves** (the rest).
- **Excess reserves are the basis of money creation.** When a bank lends, it creates a new demand deposit: new money.
- Each loan is spent and redeposited, so the process repeats in ever-smaller rounds.
- The **money multiplier** is the money supply ÷ the monetary base. Its maximum is 1 ÷ rr.
- The simple multiplier **overstates** expansion if banks hold excess reserves or the public holds more currency.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Formula | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Balance sheet | Assets = liabilities + owners' equity | check a T-account |
| Required reserves | rr × demand deposits | find what must be held |
| Excess reserves | total reserves − required reserves | find what one bank can lend |
| Excess reserves from a new cash deposit | (1 − rr) × deposit | the quick route |
| Maximum money multiplier | 1 ÷ rr | size the expansion |
| Maximum change in money supply (and loans) | initial excess reserves × multiplier | system-wide effect |
| Maximum change in demand deposits | new reserves × multiplier | system-wide effect on deposits |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Banks lend **all** their excess reserves.
- Every loan is spent and **fully redeposited**: no one holds extra cash.
- The required reserve ratio is positive and given. (Some real central banks, including the US Federal Reserve since 2020, set it at zero.)

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Putting deposits on the assets side.** They are liabilities.
2. **Calculating required reserves from total reserves.** Use demand deposits.
3. **Letting one bank lend the whole deposit.** It lends only excess reserves.
4. **Using the deposit, not the excess reserves, for the money supply change** after a cash deposit.
5. **Saying a cash deposit itself changes M1.** It only changes the form of money.
6. **Thinking a higher rr raises the multiplier.** It lowers it.

## Quick self-check

All figures are fictional, in millions of valdas (VD).

1. What is the maximum money multiplier when rr = 5%? *(20)*
2. A bank has demand deposits of 2,500 and reserves of 300; rr = 10%. What are its excess reserves? *(VD 50 million)*
3. With rr = 10%, what is the maximum change in the money supply from VD 50 million of new excess reserves? *(VD 500 million)*
