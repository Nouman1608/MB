## Recap

- **Automatic stabilizers** are parts of the tax and transfer system that change **by themselves** when real GDP changes. No new law or announcement is needed.
- **Recession:** incomes, spending and profits fall, so **tax revenue falls**; more people qualify for unemployment benefits and income support, so **transfers rise**. Disposable income and consumption fall by **less**, so the recession is milder.
- **Expansion:** **tax revenue rises** and **transfers fall**. Disposable income and consumption rise by **less**, so the economy is less likely to overheat.
- **Discretionary fiscal policy** (Topic 3.8) needs a new decision and suffers decision and implementation lags. Stabilizers avoid these lags.
- Stabilizers **moderate** the business cycle. They do not eliminate output gaps or restore full employment on their own.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Formula or direction | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Disposable income | income − taxes + transfers | see why it falls by less than income |
| Change in tax revenue (proportional tax) | tax rate × change in income | find the automatic change in revenue |
| Change in consumption | MPC × change in disposable income | find the first-round change in spending |
| Tax revenue over the cycle | same direction as real GDP | explain the tax stabilizer |
| Transfers over the cycle | opposite direction to real GDP | explain the transfer stabilizer |
| AD–AS effect | AD shifts by less, in either direction | show smaller changes in real GDP and the price level |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Tax rates and benefit rules **do not change**; only incomes and the number of people who qualify change.
- The calculations look at the **first round** of spending only.
- A progressive tax would make the effect stronger (background only).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Calling a new tax cut or a new spending plan an automatic stabilizer.** If someone decided it, it is discretionary.
2. **Saying the government "cut taxes" when revenue falls** with unchanged rates.
3. **Reversing transfers:** they **rise** in recessions and **fall** in expansions.
4. **Saying stabilizers shift AD right in a recession.** AD still shifts left, by less.
5. **Claiming stabilizers restore full employment.** The gap only shrinks.
6. **Forgetting the expansion side.** Stabilizers also restrain booms.
7. **Treating transfers as government purchases.** They act through disposable income.

## Quick self-check

VD is the valda, the currency of Valdoria, the fictional country used on these pages.

1. Income falls by VD 20 billion and the income tax takes 30% of each valda of income. By how much does tax revenue change? *(It falls by VD 6 billion.)*
2. In the same recession, transfers rise by VD 2 billion. By how much does disposable income fall? *(VD 12 billion: 20 − 6 − 2.)*
3. The MPC is 0.75. By how much does consumption fall in the first round? *(VD 9 billion.)*
4. Is a vote to extend unemployment benefits for an extra six months an automatic stabilizer? *(No. It is a new decision, so it is discretionary.)*
