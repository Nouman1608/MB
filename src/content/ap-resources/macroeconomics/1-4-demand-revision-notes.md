## Recap

- **Demand** is the quantity buyers are **willing and able** to buy at **each** price, in a given period, other things equal.
- **Quantity demanded** is the amount at **one** price.
- **Law of demand:** price and quantity demanded are **inversely** related, so the demand curve **slopes downward**.
- **Market demand** = the sum of all buyers' quantities **at each price** (add sideways).
- A change in the good's **own price** gives a **movement along** the curve. A change in any **determinant** **shifts** the curve.

## Key relationships

| Change | Effect on demand for good X | Graph |
|---|---|---|
| Price of X rises | Quantity demanded falls | Move up along D |
| Price of X falls | Quantity demanded rises | Move down along D |
| Income rises (X normal) | Increases | Shift right |
| Income rises (X inferior) | Decreases | Shift left |
| Price of a substitute rises | Increases | Shift right |
| Price of a complement rises | Decreases | Shift left |
| Tastes move towards X | Increases | Shift right |
| Buyers expect X's price to rise | Increases now | Shift right |
| More buyers | Increases | Shift right |

## Graph checklist

- Vertical axis: **Price**. Horizontal axis: **Quantity**.
- Downward-sloping curve labelled **D** (or D₁).
- For a shift: draw **D₂**, label it, and add an **arrow** showing the direction.
- For a movement: mark two points on the **same** curve.

## Assumptions behind the model

- Only one influence changes at a time (other things equal).
- Buyers are willing **and able** to pay.
- Demand is measured over a stated period (per week, per month).
- In this topic you do not find the new price. That needs supply (Topics 1.5 and 1.6).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying "demand rose" when only the **price fell**.
2. Adding **prices** instead of **quantities** for market demand.
3. Getting substitutes and complements the wrong way round.
4. Assuming higher income always raises demand (not for **inferior** goods).
5. Forgetting that an **expected** price rise increases demand **now**.
6. Leaving axes, curves or shift arrows unlabelled.

## Quick self-check

VD is the valda, the currency of the fictional country Valdoria used in the study guide.

1. At VD 5, group A wants 30 units and group B wants 45. What is market quantity demanded at VD 5? *(75 units)*
2. Coffee becomes more expensive. What happens to the demand for tea, a substitute? *(It increases; the curve shifts right.)*
3. 200 identical buyers each want 4 units at VD 10. The number of buyers rises to 250. What is the new market quantity demanded at VD 10, and is this a shift or a movement? *(1,000 units, up from 800; a shift right, because the number of buyers is a determinant)*
