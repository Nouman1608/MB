Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/5-4-government-deficits-national-debt-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/5-4-government-deficits-national-debt-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define a budget surplus, a budget deficit and a balanced budget. *(Guide: "The government budget"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why transfer payments and interest count as budget spending even though they are not part of G. *(Guide: "The government budget"; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can explain the difference between the deficit (a flow) and the national debt (a stock). *(Guide: "Deficit and debt: flow and stock"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain how a deficit is financed by borrowing and why that adds to the debt. *(Guide: "How a deficit is paid for"; Practice Q2)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate a budget balance from tax revenue, purchases, transfers and interest. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5(a), Q7(a))*
- I can update the national debt through several years of deficits and surpluses. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3, Q5(b), Q7(b))*
- I can calculate the interest bill and its share of tax revenue. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5(c), Q6(a)–(b))*
- I can show how borrowing to pay interest makes the debt grow over several years. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(c))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why a falling deficit still raises the debt, and why only a surplus lowers it. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q5(d))*
- I can explain why interest on the debt uses funds that could go to other uses and why borrowed interest makes the debt grow faster. *(Guide: "The burden of the national debt"; Practice Q4, Q6(d)–(e), Q7(c))*
- I can describe crowding out and future taxes as further issues in the burden of the debt. *(Guide: "The burden of the national debt"; Practice Q7(e))*
