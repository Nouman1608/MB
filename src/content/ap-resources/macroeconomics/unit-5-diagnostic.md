This diagnostic helps you find **which Unit 5 topics to revisit**. It has one or two short questions on each topic, 5.1 to 5.7. Answer every question before opening any answer; allow about 30 minutes.

These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. The set is not calibrated, so it gives **no predicted score**. **Valdoria** and **Kestria** are fictional countries using the **valda (VD)**; all data are fictional. A four-function calculator is enough. Only the stated change happens.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · 5.1)

Valdoria has a recessionary gap, and its central bank works with **ample reserves**. Which combination would help close the gap?

- (A) Raise transfer payments; lower the interest rate the central bank pays on reserves
- (B) Raise transfer payments; raise the interest rate the central bank pays on reserves
- (C) Cut taxes; sell government bonds on the open market
- (D) Cut government spending; lower the interest rate the central bank pays on reserves

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** A recessionary gap needs both policies to be **expansionary**. Higher transfers raise consumption. With ample reserves, the central bank lowers its policy rate mainly by lowering administered rates such as the rate paid on reserves.

- (B) A higher rate on reserves is contractionary.
- (C) A bond sale is a contractionary, limited-reserves tool.
- (D) A spending cut is contractionary.

**If you missed this:** read "Choosing the direction: match the gap" in the [Topic 5.1 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/5-1-fiscal-monetary-policy-actions-short-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · 5.2)

Valdoria's natural rate of unemployment is 4.5%. Its short-run Phillips curve (SRPC) passes through (unemployment 4.5%, inflation 3%) and (5.5%, 1%). A fall in AD has moved the economy to **5.5% and 1%**. With no policy action, what happens in the long run?

- (A) The SRPC shifts left; unemployment returns to 4.5% with inflation below 3%.
- (B) The SRPC shifts right; unemployment returns to 4.5% with inflation above 3%.
- (C) The economy moves back up the same SRPC to 4.5% and 3%.
- (D) The LRPC shifts right to 5.5%, the new long-run equilibrium.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** The point is **right** of the LRPC: a recessionary gap. Nominal wages and expected inflation fall, so SRAS shifts right and the **SRPC shifts left**. Unemployment returns to the natural rate at lower inflation.

- (B) describes adjustment from an **inflationary** gap.
- (C) Moving back up the same SRPC needs AD to rise again, which is not self-adjustment.
- (D) A fall in AD does not change the natural rate.

**If you missed this:** read "Equilibrium and gaps on the graph" and Worked example 1(d) in the [Topic 5.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/5-2-phillips-curve-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · 5.2)

Which event would shift Valdoria's **long-run** Phillips curve to the **left**?

- (A) The central bank lowers its policy rate.
- (B) A programme retrains workers whose skills became obsolete, cutting structural unemployment for good.
- (C) The world price of oil falls sharply for a year.
- (D) Workers and firms come to expect higher inflation.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** Less structural unemployment lowers the **natural rate**, where the LRPC stands. LRAS shifts right too.

- (A) is a demand change: a movement along the SRPC.
- (C) A temporary supply shock shifts the **SRPC** left, not the LRPC.
- (D) Higher expected inflation shifts the **SRPC** right.

**If you missed this:** read "What shifts the LRPC" in the [Topic 5.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/5-2-phillips-curve-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · 5.3)

Over several years Valdoria's money supply grows by **6%** a year, the velocity of money **falls by 1%** a year and full-employment real GDP grows by **3%** a year. Which is the best estimate of the inflation rate?

- (A) 2%
- (B) 3%
- (C) 4%
- (D) 8%

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Inflation ≈ %ΔM + %ΔV − %ΔY = 6 + (−1) − 3 = **2%**.

- (B) ignores velocity: 6 − 3.
- (C) flips the sign of the velocity change: 6 + 1 − 3.
- (D) adds real growth: 6 − 1 + 3.

**If you missed this:** read "The quantity theory in growth rates" and "When velocity changes" in the [Topic 5.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/5-3-money-growth-inflation-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 5 (multiple choice · 5.4)

Kestria starts the year with a **balanced budget** and a debt of **VD 500 billion**. A recession cuts tax revenue by **VD 12 billion** and raises unemployment benefits by **VD 8 billion**; nothing else changes. Deficits are borrowed. What are the budget balance and the debt at the end of the year?

- (A) Deficit VD 20 billion; debt VD 520 billion
- (B) Deficit VD 12 billion; debt VD 512 billion
- (C) Deficit VD 4 billion; debt VD 504 billion
- (D) Deficit VD 20 billion; debt VD 500 billion

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** The balance moves from 0 to 0 − 12 − 8 = **−20**. The deficit is borrowed, so the debt rises to 500 + 20 = **VD 520 billion**. These are automatic stabilizers at work.

- (B) leaves out benefits. Transfers are not in G, but they are budget spending.
- (C) nets the two changes, but both push towards deficit.
- (D) Any deficit, planned or not, adds to the debt.

**If you missed this:** read "Deficit and debt: flow and stock" and "Why deficits happen" in the [Topic 5.4 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/5-4-government-deficits-national-debt-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 6 (multiple choice · 5.5)

A teacher models a new government deficit in the loanable funds market as a **fall in national saving**. Using this method, which is correct?

- (A) Supply shifts left; the real interest rate rises and the equilibrium quantity (private investment) falls.
- (B) Supply shifts left and demand shifts right; private investment falls by the full deficit.
- (C) Supply shifts left; the real interest rate falls and private investment rises.
- (D) Supply shifts right, because savers can buy government bonds; the real interest rate falls.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** A deficit is negative public saving, so supply shifts left. The real interest rate rises and private investment is **crowded out**.

- (B) shows the same deficit twice. Use one method, never both.
- (C) A leftward supply shift raises the real interest rate.
- (D) Selling bonds is how the government borrows; it does not add to supply.

**If you missed this:** read "Another way to draw it" after Worked example 1 in the [Topic 5.5 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/5-5-crowding-out-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 7 (multiple choice · 5.6)

Valdoria's real GDP rises 6% in a year as unemployment falls from 8% to its natural rate of 5%. The labour force, capital and technology do not change. Which statement is correct?

- (A) It is a recovery: the economy moves from inside its PPC to the curve; LRAS does not shift.
- (B) LRAS shifts right by 6%, because real GDP rose 6%.
- (C) The PPC shifts outward, because more workers are employed.
- (D) It is long-run growth, because real GDP removes inflation.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Idle workers went back to work. Output rose, but **capacity** did not, so the PPC and LRAS stay put.

- (B) Real GDP rising to Yf is a move to LRAS, not a shift of it.
- (C) Employing existing workers uses existing capacity.
- (D) Real GDP removes inflation, but long-run growth means more capacity.

**If you missed this:** read "Showing growth on graphs: the PPC and LRAS" in the [Topic 5.6 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/5-6-economic-growth-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 8 (multiple choice · 5.7)

Which policy would raise Valdoria's real GDP per capita **mainly through labour force participation** rather than productivity?

- (A) Research grants for new battery technology
- (B) A tax credit for firms that buy new equipment
- (C) Longer free preschool hours, so more parents can take full-time jobs
- (D) More places at vocational colleges

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** More adults join the labour force, so a larger share of people work, even with output per worker unchanged.

- (A) works through **technology**.
- (B) works through **physical capital** per worker.
- (D) works through **human capital** per worker.

**If you missed this:** read "Policies that raise productivity or participation" in the [Topic 5.7 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/5-7-public-policy-economic-growth-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 9 (short answer · 5.1)

Valdoria's real GDP is VD 780 billion and full-employment output is VD 810 billion. The MPC is 0.8.

(a) Identify the gap and its size, and calculate the spending and tax multipliers.
(b) A cut in the policy rate raises investment by VD 2 billion. Calculate the shift in AD.
(c) Calculate the minimum rise in transfer payments that, with (b), would close the gap.
(d) State the effect of the combined policy on the price level and on the nominal interest rate.

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**(a)** A **recessionary gap** of 810 − 780 = **VD 30 billion**. Spending multiplier = 1 ÷ 0.2 = **5**; tax multiplier = −0.8 ÷ 0.2 = **−4**.

**(b)** 5 × 2 = **+VD 10 billion**.

**(c)** AD must still rise by 30 − 10 = 20. Transfers work like a tax cut (multiplier 4): 20 ÷ 4 = **VD 5 billion**.

**(d)** The **price level rises**. The **nominal interest rate is uncertain**: the central bank lowers it, but higher income raises money demand.

**If you missed this:** read Worked example 1 in the [Topic 5.1 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/5-1-fiscal-monetary-policy-actions-short-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 10 (short answer · 5.3)

Valdoria's money supply is VD 150 billion and nominal GDP is VD 900 billion. Real GDP is at full employment, VD 750 billion.

(a) Calculate velocity and the price level.
(b) The money supply rises to VD 165 billion; velocity is constant. State the short-run effect on real output, then calculate the long-run price level and the percentage changes in M and P.
(c) Next year full-employment output grows 4% and velocity is unchanged. What money supply keeps the price level at its value in (b)?

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**(a)** V = 900 ÷ 150 = **6**. P = 900 ÷ 750 = **1.20** (index 120).

**(b)** In the short run AD rises, so output can rise above VD 750 billion; in the long run wages adjust and it returns to 750. P = (165 × 6) ÷ 750 = **1.32**. M and P both rose **10%**: money is neutral in the long run.

**(c)** Y = 750 × 1.04 = 780. M = (1.32 × 780) ÷ 6 = **VD 171.6 billion**: **4%** growth, matching real growth.

**If you missed this:** read Worked examples 1 and 2 in the [Topic 5.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/5-3-money-growth-inflation-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 11 (short answer · 5.6)

Data for Kestria; capital and technology do not change.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Real GDP (VD billion) | 240 | 249.6 |
| Employed workers (million) | 6.0 | 6.4 |
| Population (million) | 10.0 | 10.4 |

(a) Calculate the growth rates of real GDP and of real GDP per capita.
(b) Calculate labour productivity in each year.
(c) Use the aggregate production function to explain (b). Did living standards rise?

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**(a)** Real GDP: (249.6 − 240) ÷ 240 × 100 = **4.00%**. Per capita: VD 240 billion ÷ 10.0 million and VD 249.6 billion ÷ 10.4 million both give VD 24,000, so growth = **0.00%**.

**(b)** Year 1: 240 billion ÷ 6.0 million = **VD 40,000** per worker. Year 2: 249.6 billion ÷ 6.4 million = **VD 39,000**, a fall of 2.5%.

**(c)** More workers produced more output, but with the same capital, **capital per worker fell** (by 6.25%), so each worker produced less. Living standards did **not** rise: real GDP per capita is unchanged.

**If you missed this:** read "Labour productivity" and Worked example 2 in the [Topic 5.6 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/5-6-economic-growth-study-guide/).
</details>
