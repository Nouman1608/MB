This diagnostic helps you find **which Unit 3 topics to revisit**: one question on each topic, 3.1 to 3.9, and a second on 3.2, 3.6 and 3.8. Answer everything before opening any answer; allow about 30 minutes.

These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. The set is not calibrated and gives **no predicted score**. **Valdoria** and **Kestria** are fictional countries using the **valda (VD)**; data are fictional, in VD billion at base-year prices, and apply only to their own question. A four-function calculator is enough. Multipliers use the simple model (constant MPC, saving the only leakage).

## Question 1 (multiple choice · 3.1)

Which event alone shifts Valdoria's aggregate demand (AD) curve **right**?

- (A) Foreign investors move their savings out of Valdoria, and the valda depreciates.
- (B) Valdoria's price level falls by 2%.
- (C) Valdorian households buy more imported cars at every price level.
- (D) The central bank raises its policy interest rate.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** A depreciation not caused by the price level makes exports cheaper and imports dearer, so net exports rise at every price level.

- (B) is a movement **along** AD.
- (C) Imports are subtracted in NX, so AD shifts **left**.
- (D) A policy rate rise cuts investment: AD shifts **left**.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 3.1 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-1-aggregate-demand-ad-study-guide/), "What shifts the AD curve".
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · 3.2)

When Valdoria's disposable income rises by VD 50 billion, saving rises by VD 10 billion. Exports fall by **VD 6 billion** at every price level. What is the change in AD?

- (A) −VD 30 billion
- (B) −VD 7.5 billion
- (C) −VD 24 billion
- (D) −VD 1.2 billion

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** MPS = 10 ÷ 50 = 0.2, so the spending multiplier = 1 ÷ 0.2 = 5. ΔAD = 5 × (−6) = **−VD 30 billion**.

- (B) divides by the MPC; the multiplier is 1 ÷ MPS.
- (C) uses the tax multiplier (−4) on a spending change.
- (D) multiplies by the MPS instead of dividing.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 3.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-2-multipliers-study-guide/), "The two multipliers".
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · 3.3)

Which **pair** of events would **both** shift Valdoria's short-run aggregate supply (SRAS) curve to the **right**?

- (A) A per-unit subsidy to producers, and lower expected inflation.
- (B) Higher output per worker, and a higher price level.
- (C) Lower taxes on business profits, and higher consumer confidence.
- (D) Lower nominal wages, and higher world oil prices.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** A subsidy lowers costs; lower expected inflation means smaller wage demands. Both shift SRAS right.

- (B) A higher price level is a movement **along** SRAS.
- (C) Consumer confidence shifts **AD**.
- (D) Dearer oil shifts SRAS **left**.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 3.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-3-short-run-aggregate-supply-sras-study-guide/), "What shifts the SRAS curve".
</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · 3.4)

A minister says: "If we accept 6% inflation every year, we can keep unemployment below the natural rate for good." Which response is correct?

- (A) Wrong: once wages and prices adjust fully, unemployment returns to the natural rate whatever the inflation rate.
- (B) Right: along SRAS a higher price level always brings higher output.
- (C) Right, provided LRAS slopes upward like SRAS.
- (D) Wrong: higher inflation keeps unemployment permanently above the natural rate.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** LRAS is vertical at full-employment output (Yf). Renegotiated wages catch up with prices, so inflation buys no lasting output or jobs: **no long-run trade-off**.

- (B) The SRAS trade-off lasts only while wages are sticky.
- (C) LRAS is vertical.
- (D) Long-run unemployment is **at** the natural rate.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 3.4 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-4-long-run-aggregate-supply-lras-study-guide/), "No long-run trade-off between inflation and unemployment".
</details>

## Question 5 (multiple choice · 3.5)

Kestria's full-employment output is **VD 900 billion**.

| Price level | 100 | 104 | 108 | 112 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AD (VD billion) | 930 | 910 | 890 | 870 |
| SRAS (VD billion) | 850 | 870 | 890 | 910 |

Which statement is correct?

- (A) Equilibrium at 108; negative gap of VD 10 billion; unemployment above the natural rate.
- (B) Equilibrium at 108; positive gap of VD 10 billion; unemployment below the natural rate.
- (C) Long-run equilibrium at 108, because AD equals SRAS.
- (D) Equilibrium at 110 and VD 900 billion, where SRAS meets LRAS.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** AD = SRAS = 890 at 108. Gap = 890 − 900 = **−VD 10 billion** (−1.11% of Yf): recessionary, so cyclical unemployment is positive.

- (B) reverses the sign (actual − potential).
- (C) Long-run equilibrium also needs output = Yf.
- (D) Short-run equilibrium is where **AD** crosses SRAS.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 3.5 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-5-equilibrium-aggregate-demand-aggregate-supply-study-guide/), "Output gaps".
</details>

## Question 6 (multiple choice · 3.6)

Valdoria's AD **decreases** and its SRAS **increases**. Which short-run result is certain?

- (A) The price level falls; the change in real output is indeterminate.
- (B) Real output falls; the change in the price level is indeterminate.
- (C) The price level and real output both fall.
- (D) The changes in the price level and in real output are both indeterminate.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Both shifts push the price level **down**. Their effects on output conflict, so that change depends on which shift is larger.

- (B) swaps the two results.
- (C) looks only at the AD shift.
- (D) misses that the price effects agree.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 3.6 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-6-changes-ad-as-model-short-study-guide/), "All four shocks from the same starting point".
</details>

## Question 7 (multiple choice · 3.7)

Kestria has an **inflationary gap** and no policy is used. Compared with this **short-run** position, what happens in the long run?

- (A) Wages rise, SRAS shifts left; the price level rises further, real GDP falls to Yf, unemployment rises to the natural rate.
- (B) Wages rise, SRAS shifts left; the price level and real GDP both fall.
- (C) The high price level shifts AD left until the price level is back where it started.
- (D) LRAS shifts right to the current output; the price level does not change.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Scarce workers push nominal wages up. SRAS shifts left along the unchanged AD: the price level rises again and output falls back to Yf.

- (B) A leftward SRAS shift **raises** the price level.
- (C) Self-adjustment shifts **SRAS**, not AD.
- (D) LRAS moves only with resources or technology.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 3.7 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-7-long-run-self-adjustment-study-guide/), "Closing an inflationary gap".
</details>

## Question 8 (multiple choice · 3.8)

Valdoria has an **inflationary gap of VD 24 billion**; its MPC is 0.8. With a constant price level, which action **alone** closes the gap exactly?

- (A) Cut transfer payments by VD 6 billion
- (B) Cut government spending by VD 6 billion
- (C) Raise taxes by VD 4.8 billion
- (D) Raise government spending by VD 4.8 billion

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Spending multiplier 5, tax multiplier −4. A transfer cut works like a tax rise: 4 × 6 = **VD 24 billion** off AD.

- (B) removes 5 × 6 = 30, overshooting; the right G cut is 4.8.
- (C) removes only 4 × 4.8 = 19.2.
- (D) is expansionary and widens the gap.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 3.8 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-8-fiscal-policy-study-guide/), "Calculating the change needed to close a gap".
</details>

## Question 9 (multiple choice · 3.9)

In a recession, Valdoria's budget deficit grows from VD 20 billion to VD 35 billion, with no new tax or spending laws. What best explains this?

- (A) Tax revenue fell and transfer payments rose automatically as incomes fell.
- (B) The government chose expansionary discretionary policy.
- (C) Tax rates fell automatically.
- (D) The central bank cut interest rates, raising government spending.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** With unchanged rules, lower incomes bring in less tax and more households qualify for benefits. These automatic stabilizers cushion disposable income with no new decision.

- (B) needs a new decision; none was made.
- (C) The **rates** stay the same; **revenue** falls.
- (D) is monetary policy.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 3.9 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-9-automatic-stabilizers-study-guide/), "The idea: a fiscal policy that runs itself".
</details>

## Question 10 (short answer · 3.2)

Valdoria's MPS is **0.4**. Exports fall by **VD 9 billion** at every price level, and the government raises pensions (a transfer payment) by **VD 9 billion**.

(a) Calculate both multipliers.
(b) Calculate the net change in AD.
(c) How large a rise in pensions would have left AD unchanged? Why is it larger than VD 9 billion?

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**(a)** MPC = 1 − 0.4 = 0.6. Spending multiplier = 1 ÷ 0.4 = **2.5**. Tax multiplier = −0.6 ÷ 0.4 = **−1.5**.

**(b)** Exports: 2.5 × (−9) = −22.5. Pensions work like a tax cut: 1.5 × 9 = +13.5. Net ΔAD = **−VD 9 billion**: AD shifts left.

**(c)** 22.5 ÷ 1.5 = **VD 15 billion**. Pensioners spend only 60% of a transfer at first (VD 5.4 billion of 9); lost exports are lost spending in full.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 3.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-2-multipliers-study-guide/), "Why the tax multiplier is smaller".
</details>

## Question 11 (short answer · 3.6)

Valdoria's full-employment output is **VD 900 billion**; the natural rate of unemployment is **5.0%**, and each VD 30 billion of output is 1 percentage point of unemployment. A long port strike raises transport costs: SRAS **decreases by VD 60 billion** at every price level. AD does not change.

| Price level | 100 | 103 | 106 | 109 | 112 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AD (VD billion) | 960 | 930 | 900 | 870 | 840 |
| SRAS₁ (VD billion) | 840 | 870 | 900 | 930 | 960 |

(a) Write SRAS₂ and find the new short-run equilibrium.
(b) Calculate the inflation rate, the change in real output and the new unemployment rate.
(c) Name the type of inflation and the type of output gap.

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**(a)** SRAS₂: **780, 810, 840, 870, 900**. AD = SRAS₂ = VD 870 billion at **109** (from 900 at 106).

**(b)** Inflation = (109 − 106) ÷ 106 × 100 = **2.83%**. Real output falls by **VD 30 billion** (−3.33%). Unemployment = 5.0 + 1.0 = **6.0%**.

**(c)** **Cost-push inflation** (stagflation), with a **negative (recessionary) gap**.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 3.6 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-6-changes-ad-as-model-short-study-guide/), "Supply shocks: shifts of SRAS".
</details>

## Question 12 (short answer · 3.8)

Kestria's real GDP is **VD 864 billion**, full-employment output is **VD 900 billion** and the MPC is **0.8**. The price level is constant.

(a) Identify the gap and the type of fiscal policy needed.
(b) The government will raise spending and cut taxes **by the same amount**. Calculate the amount that closes the gap.
(c) Explain one reason why the policy might not work as planned.

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**(a)** 864 − 900 = **−VD 36 billion**, a recessionary gap. **Expansionary** policy is needed.

**(b)** Spending multiplier = 1 ÷ 0.2 = 5; tax multiplier = −4. Each valda of the pair adds 5 + 4 = 9 to AD. 36 ÷ 9 = **VD 4 billion** of each. Check: 5 × 4 + 4 × 4 = 20 + 16 = 36.

**(c)** **Lags.** Recognising the gap, agreeing a plan and carrying it out take time. If the economy has recovered by then, the spending pushes output above Yf and adds to inflation.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 3.8 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-8-fiscal-policy-study-guide/), Worked example 1 and "Lags in discretionary fiscal policy".
</details>
