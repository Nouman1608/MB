## Recap

- **Demand for valdas** shifts when foreigners want more or fewer Valdorian goods, services or assets: foreign tastes, **foreign** incomes, relative price levels, relative interest rates, expectations.
- **Supply of valdas** shifts when Valdorians want more or fewer foreign goods, services or assets: Valdorian tastes, **Valdorian** incomes, relative price levels, relative interest rates, and **tariffs or quotas** on the other country's goods.
- One event can shift both curves. Higher Valdorian inflation: demand for valdas down **and** supply up, so the valda depreciates.
- **Fiscal policy:** deficit-financed expansion → more government borrowing → real interest rate rises → demand for the currency rises → appreciation. Contraction does the opposite.
- **Monetary policy:** expansion → interest rate falls → demand for the currency falls (and supply rises) → depreciation. Contraction → appreciation.
- When one currency appreciates, the other depreciates.

## Key relationships

| Change in Valdoria (or Ostria) | Market for valdas | Valda |
|---|---|---|
| Ostrian incomes or tastes for Valdorian goods rise | Demand ↑ | Appreciates |
| Valdorian incomes rise | Supply ↑ | Depreciates |
| Valdoria's inflation above Ostria's | Demand ↓, supply ↑ | Depreciates |
| Valdorian interest rates rise relative to Ostria's | Demand ↑ (supply ↓) | Appreciates |
| Valdorian tariff or quota on Ostrian goods | Supply ↓ | Appreciates |
| Expansionary monetary policy in Valdoria | Demand ↓ (supply ↑) | Depreciates |
| Deficit-financed expansionary fiscal policy in Valdoria | Demand ↑ (via higher real interest rate) | Appreciates |

## Assumptions behind the model

- Exchange rates are flexible, and financial capital can move between the two countries.
- Each shift is analysed with everything else held constant.
- Fiscal policy questions use the interest-rate chain unless the question asks about incomes or prices and imports.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying higher **domestic** incomes raise demand for the domestic currency. They raise its **supply** (more imports).
2. Shifting **demand** for a tariff on imports. The tariff cuts the **supply** of the domestic currency.
3. Saying expansionary monetary policy makes the currency appreciate.
4. Forgetting to state the effect on the other currency.
5. Claiming a definite change in quantity when demand and supply shift in opposite directions.
6. Going on to net exports when the question only asks about the exchange rate.

## Quick self-check

Valdoria (currency: valda, VD) and Ostria (currency: ostro, OS) are the fictional countries used in the study guide.

1. Valdoria's central bank raises interest rates. What happens to the valda? *(It appreciates: demand for valdas increases.)*
2. Ostrian incomes fall. Which curve shifts, and what happens to the valda? *(Demand for valdas decreases; the valda depreciates.)*
3. The valda rises from OS 1.5 to OS 1.8. By what percentage do the valda and the ostro change? *(Valda +20%; ostro −16.67%.)*
