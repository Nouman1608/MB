Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/6-1-balance-payments-accounts-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/6-1-balance-payments-accounts-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define the balance of payments and name its two accounts. *(Guide: "What the balance of payments records")*
- I can list the three parts of the current account and give an example of each. *(Guide: "The current account (CA)"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain what the capital and financial account records and what a CFA surplus or deficit means. *(Guide: "The capital and financial account (CFA)"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain the difference between the balance of trade and the current account. *(Guide: "The current account (CA)"; Practice Q2)*

## Calculation

- I can decide whether a transaction is a credit or a debit by asking which way the money flows. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4, Q6(a))*
- I can calculate the balance of trade, net income from abroad and net unilateral transfers from data. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate the CA and the CFA, give each its sign, and check that CA + CFA = 0. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can use CA + CFA = 0 to find a missing value. *(Practice Q3, Q7(a)–(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why total credits equal total debits, so a CA deficit is matched by an equal CFA surplus. *(Guide: "Why CA + CFA = 0"; Practice Q7(c))*
- I can explain how a change in one account, such as more foreign purchases of domestic assets, is matched by an opposite change in the other. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6(c))*
- I can explain why buying an asset abroad goes in the CFA but the income it earns goes in the CA. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(b), Q7(d))*
