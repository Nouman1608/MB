## Recap

- **Monetary policy:** central bank action on interest rates and the money supply to reach goals such as **price stability** and **full employment**.
- **Policy rate:** the target for the overnight interbank lending rate (in the United States, the federal funds rate).
- **Limited reserves:** reserves are scarce; the central bank moves rates by changing the **quantity of reserves** (and so the money supply).
- **Ample reserves:** extra reserves do not move the rate; the central bank changes **administered rates**, mainly the **interest on reserves**. The United States has ample reserves.
- **Open market purchase:** reserves and the monetary base rise. With limited reserves, the money supply rises by **more** than the base because of the money multiplier.
- **Lags:** it takes time to **recognise** a problem and for the economy to **adjust** to the policy.

## Key relationships

| Tool | Expansionary | Contractionary |
|---|---|---|
| Open market operations | Buy bonds | Sell bonds |
| Required reserve ratio | Lower | Raise |
| Discount rate | Lower | Raise |
| Interest on reserves | Lower | Raise |

**Expansionary chain:** policy action → interest rates fall → investment and interest-sensitive consumption rise → AD right → real output up, unemployment down, price level up.

**Contractionary chain:** policy action → interest rates rise → investment and consumption fall → AD left → real output down, price level down.

**Calculation:** maximum money multiplier = 1 ÷ required reserve ratio; maximum change in money supply = new excess reserves × money multiplier (plus any new deposit itself, if the purchase is from the public).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Calling a bond **purchase** contractionary. Buying bonds is expansionary.
2. Giving the change in the **monetary base** as the change in the money supply.
3. Saying OMOs move the policy rate in an **ample**-reserve system.
4. Mixing up the **discount rate** (central bank lends to banks) and the **policy rate** (banks lend to each other).
5. Shifting **SRAS** instead of **AD** after a monetary policy action.
6. Describing government spending or taxes as monetary policy.

## Quick self-check

VD is the valda, the currency of the fictional country Valdoria used in the study guide.

1. The central bank buys VD 12 bn of bonds from banks; the required reserve ratio is 25% and reserves are limited. What are the changes in the monetary base and the maximum change in the money supply? *(+VD 12 bn; +VD 48 bn)*
2. The central bank sells VD 3 bn of bonds to banks; reserves are limited and the ratio is 10%. Maximum change in the money supply? *(−VD 30 bn)*
3. An economy with ample reserves has high inflation. Which tool should the central bank use, and in which direction? *(Raise the interest on reserves.)*
