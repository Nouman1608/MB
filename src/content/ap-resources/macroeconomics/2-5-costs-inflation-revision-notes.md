## Recap

- **Expected inflation** can be written into contracts (higher nominal interest rates, wage rises, indexed pensions).
- **Unexpected inflation** = actual inflation − expected inflation. Contracts already signed cannot adjust.
- Loans, wages and pensions are usually fixed in **money**. When prices rise more than expected, each fixed valda buys less.
- So unexpected inflation **arbitrarily redistributes** purchasing power: from lenders to borrowers, and from people receiving fixed nominal payments to those paying them.
- **Unexpected deflation** reverses this and raises the real burden of debt.
- **Costs to the economy:** unfair, luck-based redistribution; uncertainty that discourages long-term fixed-rate lending and investment; resources spent protecting against inflation.

## Who gains and who loses

| Group | Unexpected inflation | Unexpected deflation |
|---|---|---|
| Lender with a fixed-rate loan | loses | gains |
| Borrower with a fixed-rate loan | gains | loses |
| Pensioner with a fixed, non-indexed pension | loses | gains |
| Saver holding cash | loses | gains |
| Worker on a fixed wage contract | loses | gains (if the job is kept) |
| Employer paying fixed wages | gains | loses |

## Key relationship

| Relationship | Formula | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Purchasing power of a later payment | payment × (CPI at start ÷ CPI at end) | express it in start-of-period money |
| Indexed payment | payment × (CPI at end ÷ CPI at start) | keep purchasing power constant |
| Approximate real return | nominal interest rate − inflation rate | quick check (formalised in Topic 4.2) |

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying **all** inflation hurts lenders: only the unexpected part does.
2. Saying borrowers **lose** from unexpected inflation.
3. Saying deflation helps borrowers.
4. Saying inflation makes **everyone** poorer: it redistributes.
5. Flipping the CPI ratio when finding purchasing power.

## Quick self-check

1. A loan repayment of VD 1,050 is made when the CPI has risen from 100 to 105. What is it worth in start-of-year valdas? *(VD 1,000: the lender earned nothing in real terms)*
2. Prices fall unexpectedly. Does a lender with a fixed-rate loan gain or lose? *(Gain)*
3. A fixed pension of VD 600 a month after prices rise by 20%: purchasing power in old valdas? *(VD 500)*
