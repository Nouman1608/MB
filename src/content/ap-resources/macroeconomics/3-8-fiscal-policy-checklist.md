Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-8-fiscal-policy-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-8-fiscal-policy-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define fiscal policy and name its tools: government spending, taxes and transfer payments. *(Guide: "What fiscal policy is"; Practice Q1)*
- I can tell fiscal policy apart from monetary policy. *(Guide: "What fiscal policy is"; Practice Q1, Q6(b))*
- I can explain why government spending changes AD directly, while taxes and transfers change it indirectly through disposable income. *(Guide: "Direct and indirect effects on AD"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why the spending multiplier is larger than the tax multiplier. *(Guide: "Direct and indirect effects on AD"; Practice Q3, Q5(d))*

## Calculation

- I can calculate the change in government spending needed to close a recessionary or inflationary gap. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q2, Q5(c), Q7(a))*
- I can calculate the change in taxes or transfers needed to close a gap, and state its direction. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q5(d)–(e), Q7(b))*
- I can combine a spending change and a tax change to close a gap. *(Guide: Worked example 2(d); Practice Q7(c))*

## Reasoning and graphs

- I can choose expansionary or contractionary policy for a given gap and state its short-run effects on real GDP, the price level and unemployment. *(Guide: "Matching the policy to the gap"; Practice Q5(a), Q6)*
- I can show the short-run effect of fiscal policy on a correctly labelled AD–AS graph. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why real GDP may rise by less than the shift in AD when SRAS slopes upward. *(Guide: "Calculating the change needed to close a gap", Assumption; Practice Q7(e))*
- I can explain the lags in discretionary fiscal policy and how bad timing can make fluctuations worse. *(Guide: "Lags in discretionary fiscal policy"; Practice Q4, Q6(e))*
