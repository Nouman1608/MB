Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-6-changes-ad-as-model-short-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-6-changes-ad-as-model-short-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what positive and negative shocks to AD and SRAS are, and decide which curve an event shifts. *(Guide: "What a shock is"; Practice Q2)*
- I can state the short-run effects of an AD shock on the price level, output and employment. *(Guide: "Demand shocks: shifts of AD"; Practice Q1)*
- I can state the short-run effects of an SRAS shock on the price level, output and employment. *(Guide: "Supply shocks: shifts of SRAS"; Practice Q4)*
- I can distinguish demand-pull from cost-push inflation and explain stagflation. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q6(d))*

## Skills

- I can use the multiplier to find how far AD shifts after a change in spending. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(a))*
- I can find the new short-run equilibrium from schedules after a shift, and calculate the inflation rate and new unemployment rate. *(Guide: Worked examples 1–2; Practice Q5(b)–(c))*
- I can draw a correctly labelled AD–AS graph showing a shift of AD or SRAS, with both equilibria labelled. *(Guide: Figures 1–2; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why real output rises by less than the horizontal shift of AD. *(Guide: Worked example 1(d); Practice Q5(d))*
- I can identify the type of shock from data on the price level and real output. *(Guide: "All four shocks from the same starting point"; Practice Q3, Q7(a))*
- I can explain why, when AD rises and SRAS falls together, the price level must rise but the change in output depends on the sizes of the shifts. *(Practice Q7(b)–(d))*
