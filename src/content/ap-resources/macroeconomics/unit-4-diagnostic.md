This diagnostic helps you find **which Unit 4 topics to revisit**: one or two short questions on each topic, 4.1 to 4.7. Answer every question before opening any answers (about 30 minutes).

These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. The set is not calibrated, so it gives **no predicted score**. **Valdoria** is a fictional country; its currency is the **valda (VD)** and its central bank is the **Central Bank of Valdoria (CBV)**. All data are fictional. A four-function calculator is enough. Unless stated, checking accounts pay no interest, banks lend all excess reserves and the public holds no extra cash. Only the stated change happens.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · 4.1)

A previously issued Valdorian bond pays a fixed **VD 45** of interest a year. Market interest rates on new bonds fall, and the bond's price rises from **VD 900** to **VD 1,000**. Taking the return as yearly interest ÷ price, what happens to the return a new buyer earns?

- (A) It rises from 4.5% to 5%.
- (B) It stays the same, because the interest payment is fixed.
- (C) It falls from 5% to 4.5%.
- (D) It rises, because the bond is now worth more.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** 45 ÷ 900 = **5%**; 45 ÷ 1,000 = **4.5%**. A higher price for the same fixed payment means a lower return.

- (A) reverses the move.
- (B) The payment is fixed, so the **price** adjusts, and with it the return to a new buyer.
- (D) A higher price is a gain for the current owner; a new buyer gets a lower return.

**If you missed this:** read "Bond prices and interest rates" in the [Topic 4.1 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/4-1-financial-assets-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · 4.2)

A Valdorian savings deposit pays a nominal interest rate of **1%**. Over the year, the price level **falls by 2%**. What real interest rate did the saver earn?

- (A) −1%
- (B) 1%
- (C) 3%
- (D) −3%

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** Real rate = nominal − inflation = 1 − (−2) = **3%**. Falling prices mean each valda buys more.

- (A) adds the inflation rate (1 + (−2)) instead of subtracting it.
- (B) is the nominal rate, ignoring the change in prices.
- (D) subtracts the wrong way round (−2 − 1). Deflation raises the real rate above the nominal rate.

**If you missed this:** read "Calculating a real rate in hindsight" and Worked example 2 in the [Topic 4.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/4-2-nominal-v-real-interest-rates-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · 4.3)

Which transaction **increases M1** but leaves **M2 unchanged**?

- (A) A household deposits VD 500 of banknotes into its checking account.
- (B) A household moves VD 500 from its savings deposit into its checking account.
- (C) A household moves VD 500 from its checking account into a small time deposit.
- (D) A household buys a VD 500 phone with a credit card.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** Savings deposits are in M2 only; checking deposits are in M1 and M2. The money enters M1 but stays inside M2.

- (A) Currency falls and deposits rise by the same amount, so M1 does not change.
- (C) is the reverse of (B): M1 **falls**.
- (D) A credit card is a loan, not money.

**If you missed this:** read Worked example 2 in the [Topic 4.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/4-3-definition-measurement-functions-money-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · 4.4)

A Valdorian bank makes a new loan of **VD 50 million** by adding it to the borrower's checking account. What is the effect at that moment?

- (A) M1 rises by VD 50 million.
- (B) M1 does not change, because the bank's reserves fall by VD 50 million.
- (C) The monetary base rises by VD 50 million.
- (D) M1 rises by VD 50 million multiplied by the money multiplier.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** The borrower has a new deposit and nobody has less money: lending creates money.

- (B) Reserves leave only when the borrower spends the loan, and then they move to another bank.
- (C) Lending creates deposits, not currency or reserves.
- (D) The multiplier describes many rounds across the system, not one loan.

**If you missed this:** read part (c) of Worked example 1 in the [Topic 4.4 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/4-4-banking-expansion-money-supply-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 5 (multiple choice · 4.4)

A Valdorian bank has demand deposits of **VD 500 million** and reserves of **VD 130 million**. The required reserve ratio is **20%**, and every other bank is fully loaned up. If the bank lends all its excess reserves, what is the **maximum** increase in the money supply for the whole banking system?

- (A) VD 30 million
- (B) VD 150 million
- (C) VD 500 million
- (D) VD 650 million

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** Required reserves = 0.2 × 500 = 100. Excess reserves = 130 − 100 = **30**. Multiplier = 1 ÷ 0.2 = 5. Maximum increase = 30 × 5 = **VD 150 million**.

- (A) is the most this one bank can lend, not the total for the system.
- (C) multiplies the **required** reserves, which the bank must keep.
- (D) multiplies **all** the reserves. Only excess reserves can be lent.

**If you missed this:** read "Fractional reserve banking" and "The money multiplier" in the [Topic 4.4 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/4-4-banking-expansion-money-supply-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 6 (multiple choice · 4.5)

A new payment app lets Valdorians keep their wealth in an interest-earning fund until the moment they pay. The CBV takes no action. What happens?

- (A) Money demand shifts left; the nominal interest rate falls; prices of existing bonds fall.
- (B) Money supply shifts right; the nominal interest rate falls; prices of existing bonds rise.
- (C) Money demand shifts right; the nominal interest rate rises; prices of existing bonds fall.
- (D) Money demand shifts left; the nominal interest rate falls; prices of existing bonds rise.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (D).** People hold less money at every rate, so MD shifts left. The surplus of money is used to buy bonds, so bond prices rise and the rate falls.

- (A) gets the rate right but not bond prices, which move opposite to rates.
- (B) The quantity of money has not changed; only how much people want to hold.
- (C) has the shift the wrong way round.

**If you missed this:** read "What shifts the curves" in the [Topic 4.5 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/4-5-money-market-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 7 (multiple choice · 4.6)

Valdoria has **limited reserves** and a recessionary output gap. Which set of actions is **all expansionary**?

- (A) Buy government bonds, raise the required reserve ratio, lower the discount rate
- (B) Sell government bonds, lower the required reserve ratio, lower the discount rate
- (C) Buy government bonds, lower the required reserve ratio, lower the discount rate
- (D) Buy government bonds, lower the required reserve ratio, raise the interest on reserves

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** Each action adds excess reserves or makes reserves cheaper, so interest rates fall.

- (A) A higher reserve ratio cuts excess reserves: contractionary.
- (B) Selling bonds removes reserves: contractionary.
- (D) Higher interest on reserves raises the policy rate: contractionary.

**If you missed this:** read "The tools" in the [Topic 4.6 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/4-6-monetary-policy-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 8 (multiple choice · 4.6)

Valdoria has limited reserves and a required reserve ratio of **25%**. The CBV **sells VD 6 billion** of government bonds to **households**, who pay from their checking accounts. What is the maximum **decrease** in the money supply?

- (A) VD 1.5 billion
- (B) VD 6 billion
- (C) VD 18 billion
- (D) VD 24 billion

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (D).** Deposits and reserves fall by 6 at once. Required reserves fall by only 0.25 × 6 = 1.5, so banks are 4.5 short and cut lending by up to 4.5 × 4 = 18. Total: 6 + 18 = **VD 24 billion** (= 6 × 4).

- (A) is the fall in required reserves.
- (B) is the fall in the monetary base only.
- (C) forgets the 6 of deposits that left at once.

**If you missed this:** read Worked example 1, especially Check 2, in the [Topic 4.6 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/4-6-monetary-policy-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 9 (multiple choice · 4.7)

Valdoria's government moves from a balanced budget to a **budget surplus**. Using the supply-side view of the loanable funds market, what happens?

- (A) Supply shifts right; the real interest rate falls; private investment rises.
- (B) Demand shifts right; the real interest rate rises; private investment falls.
- (C) Supply shifts left; the real interest rate rises; private investment falls.
- (D) The money supply shifts right; the nominal interest rate falls.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** A surplus is positive public saving, so national saving rises, the real rate falls and firms borrow more.

- (B) describes a larger **deficit**.
- (C) treats the surplus as less saving. A surplus adds to national saving.
- (D) uses the wrong model: the budget affects loanable funds, not the money supply.

**If you missed this:** read "What shifts the curves" in the [Topic 4.7 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/4-7-loanable-funds-market-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 10 (short answer · 4.2)

A Valdorian lender and borrower agree a one-year loan at a nominal rate of **9%**. Both expect inflation of **5%**.

(a) Calculate the real interest rate they expect.
(b) Actual inflation is **7.5%**. Calculate the real rate in hindsight and say who gains.
(c) For next year, both now expect 7.5% inflation and want the same expected real rate as in (a). Calculate the nominal rate they will agree.

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**(a)** 9 − 5 = **4%**.

**(b)** 9 − 7.5 = **1.5%**. The **borrower** gains: inflation was higher than expected, so repayments buy less than planned.

**(c)** Nominal = expected real + expected inflation = 4 + 7.5 = **11.5%**.

**If you missed this:** read "How lenders and borrowers set a nominal rate" in the [Topic 4.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/4-2-nominal-v-real-interest-rates-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 11 (short answer · 4.5)

Valdoria's money demand is Qd = 560 − 20i, where Qd is in VD billion and i is the nominal interest rate in per cent. The money supply is **VD 480 billion**.

(a) Calculate the equilibrium nominal interest rate.
(b) At 6%, calculate the shortage or surplus of money and explain how the rate adjusts.
(c) The CBV wants a rate of 3%. By how much must it change the money supply?

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**(a)** 560 − 20i = 480, so 20i = 80 and **i = 4%**.

**(b)** At 6%, Qd = 560 − 120 = 440: a **surplus of VD 40 billion**. People buy bonds, bond prices rise and the rate falls to 4%.

**(c)** At 3%, Qd = 560 − 60 = 500. The money supply must **rise by VD 20 billion**, from 480 to 500.

**If you missed this:** read Worked example 1 in the [Topic 4.5 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/4-5-money-market-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 12 (short answer · 4.7)

In one year Valdoria has Y = 800, C = 520, T = 170 and G = 150 (VD billion).

(a) Calculate private, public and national saving.
(b) Valdorians lend VD 15 billion more abroad than foreigners lend into Valdoria. Calculate domestic investment.

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

**(a)** Private saving = 800 − 170 − 520 = **110**. Public saving = 170 − 150 = **20** (a surplus). National saving = **130** (check: 800 − 520 − 150 = 130).

**(b)** Net capital inflow is −15, so investment = 130 − 15 = **VD 115 billion**. Funds lent abroad cannot finance investment at home.

**If you missed this:** read "National saving and investment" in the [Topic 4.7 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/4-7-loanable-funds-market-study-guide/).
</details>
