Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/1-3-comparative-advantage-gains-trade-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/1-3-comparative-advantage-gains-trade-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define absolute advantage and comparative advantage and explain how they differ. *(Guide: "Absolute advantage and comparative advantage"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why a producer can have an absolute advantage in both goods but not a comparative advantage in both. *(Guide: "Absolute advantage and comparative advantage"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why equal opportunity costs leave no gains from trade. *(Practice Q7(a))*

## Calculation

- I can find absolute advantage from output data, input data or PPC intercepts. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 3; Practice Q1, Q3, Q5(b))*
- I can calculate opportunity costs from an output table or a straight-line PPC. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q6(a))*
- I can calculate opportunity costs from an input (hours per unit) table, putting the right good on top. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3, Q5(c))*
- I can state the range of mutually beneficial terms of trade and test given terms against it. *(Guide: "Specialisation and the terms of trade"; Practice Q4, Q5(d), Q7(b))*
- I can calculate each side's consumption after specialisation and trade, and its gain. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(d))*

## Reasoning and graphs

- I can draw a labelled PPC and show a consumption point beyond it after trade. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6(e))*
- I can explain why a producer would refuse terms of trade outside the range. *(Guide: "Specialisation and the terms of trade"; Practice Q7(c))*
- I can explain that trade lets a country consume beyond its PPC without shifting the PPC. *(Guide: Worked example 2 and "Common misconceptions")*
