Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/2-2-limitations-gdp-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/2-2-limitations-gdp-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why GDP is a useful indicator of a country's economic performance. *(Guide: "What GDP is good for")*
- I can tell exclusions by design (intermediate goods, used goods, financial transactions, transfers) apart from genuine limitations. *(Guide: "Exclusions by design are not limitations"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why nonmarket activities, such as home production and volunteering, are missing from GDP. *(Guide: "Nonmarket transactions"; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can explain what the informal (underground) economy is and why it is mostly missing from GDP. *(Guide: "The informal (underground) economy"; Practice Q5)*
- I can describe how leisure, the environment, the quality of output and the distribution of income limit GDP as a measure of well-being. *(Guide: "The main limitations"; Practice Q3, Q6(c))*

## Calculation

- I can calculate GDP per person, converting billions and millions correctly. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q6(a))*
- I can estimate the change in GDP per person from the growth rates of GDP and population. *(Guide: Worked example 1(c); Practice Q4)*
- I can value a nonmarket activity at market prices and say how much production GDP misses. *(Guide: Worked example 2(b); Practice Q5(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why a rise in measured GDP may not mean people are better off, using an example such as repair spending. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain how moving work from the home into the market overstates measured growth. *(Guide: "Nonmarket transactions"; Practice Q7)*
- I can use data to argue whether a country with higher GDP per person has a higher standard of living. *(Practice Q6)*
