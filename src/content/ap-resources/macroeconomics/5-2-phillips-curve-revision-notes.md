## Recap

- **Axes:** inflation rate (vertical), unemployment rate (horizontal).
- **SRPC:** downward-sloping. The short-run trade-off: lower unemployment comes with higher inflation. The economy is **always on** its current SRPC.
- **LRPC:** vertical at the **natural rate of unemployment**. No long-run trade-off.
- **Long-run equilibrium:** where the SRPC crosses the LRPC.
- **Left of the LRPC** (unemployment below the natural rate): inflationary gap. **Right of the LRPC**: recessionary gap.

## Key relationships

| Event | AD–AS model | Phillips curve model |
|---|---|---|
| AD increases | AD right | Move **up-left** along the SRPC |
| AD decreases | AD left | Move **down-right** along the SRPC |
| Adverse supply shock | SRAS left | SRPC **right** (stagflation) |
| Favourable supply shock | SRAS right | SRPC **left** |
| Long-run adjustment after AD rises | SRAS left | SRPC right; back to LRPC at higher inflation |
| Natural rate falls | LRAS right | LRPC **left** |
| Natural rate rises | LRAS left | LRPC **right** |

## Assumptions

- The SRPC is drawn for given expected inflation and given input prices; when they change, the SRPC shifts.
- In the long run wages and prices adjust fully, so unemployment returns to the natural rate.
- Temporary supply shocks shift the SRPC but not the LRPC.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Shifting the SRPC for a **demand** shock. It is a movement along the curve.
2. Moving the SRPC the **same** way as SRAS. They move in opposite directions.
3. Drawing the LRPC at zero unemployment instead of the natural rate.
4. Calling a point left of the LRPC "recessionary".
5. Swapping the axes or leaving them unlabelled (label both as percentages).
6. Forgetting that in the long run after a demand rise, inflation ends **higher** than it started.

## Quick self-check

1. Valdoria's natural rate is 4.5% and unemployment is 3.8%. Which side of the LRPC is the economy on, and what kind of gap is it? *(Left; inflationary gap)*
2. A government spending cut lowers AD. What happens on the Phillips curve graph in the short run? *(Move down and to the right along the SRPC)*
3. A sharp fall in imported energy prices. Which curve moves, and which way? *(SRPC shifts left)*
4. Better job-matching cuts frictional unemployment. Which curve moves? *(LRPC shifts left)*
