## Recap

- After a shock, the short-run equilibrium can be off LRAS: real GDP is below or above **full-employment output (Yf)**.
- With **no government policy**, nominal wages and other input prices adjust when contracts are renewed. This **shifts SRAS** until real GDP is back at Yf and unemployment is back at the **natural rate**.
- **Recessionary gap** (output < Yf, unemployment above natural rate): nominal wages fall → SRAS shifts **right** → price level falls → output rises to Yf.
- **Inflationary gap** (output > Yf, unemployment below natural rate): nominal wages rise → SRAS shifts **left** → price level rises → output falls to Yf.
- A **shift of LRAS** means full-employment output has changed. Rightward = **economic growth**.

## Key relationships

| Shock, no policy | Long run compared with before the shock |
|---|---|
| AD decreases | Lower price level; real GDP = Yf; natural rate of unemployment |
| AD increases | Higher price level; real GDP = Yf; natural rate of unemployment |
| Temporary SRAS decrease or increase | Back to the original price level, Yf and natural rate |
| LRAS shifts right | Higher Yf: the economy now returns to a larger output |

| Where to look | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| AD crosses SRAS | Short-run equilibrium |
| AD crosses LRAS | Long-run equilibrium price level |
| Horizontal gap between SRAS and AD at the long-run price level | Size of the SRAS shift needed |

## Assumptions

- The government takes no action and no other shock arrives.
- Resources and technology are fixed, so LRAS does not move, unless a question says otherwise.
- Wages and prices are fully flexible in the long run. The model gives no fixed time for this; it can be slow.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Shifting AD back** instead of SRAS.
2. **Wrong wage direction:** high unemployment lowers nominal wages; a labour shortage raises them.
3. **Stopping at the short run:** the long-run point is on LRAS.
4. **Saying the price level returns to its old value after an AD shock.** It ends lower (AD fell) or higher (AD rose).
5. **Moving LRAS to meet the economy.** Only resources and technology move LRAS.
6. **Equating the SRAS shift with the output gap.** The shift is usually larger.

## Quick self-check

1. Real GDP is VD 820 billion and Yf is VD 840 billion. Name the gap and the direction SRAS shifts without policy. *(Recessionary gap of VD 20 billion; SRAS shifts right.)*
2. AD rose last year from long-run equilibrium. Compared with before, what is the long-run unemployment rate? *(The same: the natural rate.)*
3. A drought ends after one year. Where does the economy end up without policy? *(At its original price level and Yf.)*
