## Recap

- A government with a **budget deficit** usually **borrows** to pay for its spending.
- **Crowding out**: extra government borrowing raises the **real interest rate**, which reduces **interest-sensitive private spending** (business investment, new housing, goods bought on credit).
- In the **loanable funds** graph, government borrowing shifts **demand right**. The real interest rate rises; the total quantity of loanable funds rises; **private** borrowing falls.
- **Short run:** the fall in investment offsets part of the fiscal stimulus, so AD rises by **less** than the simple multiplier suggests.
- **Long run:** less private investment means **less physical capital**, lower productivity growth and a **smaller rightward shift of LRAS**.

## Key relationships

| Change | Loanable funds market | Private investment | Long run |
|---|---|---|---|
| Larger deficit (more borrowing) | D shifts right (or S shifts left); r rises | Falls | Slower capital accumulation and growth |
| Smaller deficit (less borrowing) | D shifts left (or S shifts right); r falls | Rises | Faster capital accumulation and growth |

**Accounting check:** government borrowing = extra saving + private investment crowded out.

## Assumptions behind the model

- The interest rate on the graph is the **real** rate.
- Other things are equal: private saving and investment plans do not change for other reasons.
- Saving responds to the interest rate. The less it responds (steeper supply), the bigger the crowding out.
- Use **one** method for the shift: demand right **or** supply left, never both.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Shifting **demand left** for more government borrowing.
2. Saying the **total** quantity of loanable funds falls (with the demand-shift method it rises).
3. Putting the **nominal** interest rate on the loanable funds axis.
4. Claiming private investment falls by the **whole** amount borrowed.
5. Saying crowding out makes fiscal policy have **no** effect on AD.
6. Saying crowding out **stops** growth; it slows it.

## Quick self-check

VD is the valda, the currency of the fictional country Valdoria used in the study guide.

1. Private demand Qd = 240 − 10r, supply Qs = 60 + 20r. Find the equilibrium. *(r = 6%, VD 180 billion)*
2. The government borrows VD 30 billion more. Find the new real interest rate and private investment. *(r = 7%; private investment VD 170 billion)*
3. How much investment is crowded out? *(VD 10 billion; the other VD 20 billion comes from extra saving)*
4. Why can this slow long-run growth? *(Less investment → less physical capital per worker → slower productivity growth → LRAS shifts right by less.)*
