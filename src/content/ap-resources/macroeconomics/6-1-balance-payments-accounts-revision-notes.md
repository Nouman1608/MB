## Recap

- The **balance of payments (BOP)** records all of a country's international transactions over a period. It has two parts: the **current account (CA)** and the **capital and financial account (CFA)**.
- The **CA** records net exports (the **balance of trade**), net income from abroad and net unilateral transfers.
- The **CFA** records purchases and sales of assets between countries (factories, firms, land, shares, bonds, loans, deposits) and transfers of financial capital.
- Each account can be in **surplus** or **deficit**. A CFA surplus is a net financial capital **inflow**; a CFA deficit is a net **outflow**.
- Total credits = total debits, so **CA + CFA = 0**.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Formula or rule | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Balance of trade | exports − imports (goods and services) | find net exports |
| Net income from abroad | income received from abroad − income paid to foreigners | include interest, dividends, profits, wages |
| Net unilateral transfers | transfers received − transfers sent | include remittances, aid, gifts |
| Current account | net exports + net income + net transfers | find the CA balance |
| Capital and financial account | foreign purchases of domestic assets − domestic purchases of foreign assets | find the CFA balance |
| Balance of payments | CA + CFA = 0 | find a missing item; check your work |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- All transactions are recorded, so credits equal debits exactly. (Real statistics need a balancing item for errors.)
- "Domestic" means residents of the country whose accounts you are building, whatever currency they use.
- Central bank purchases and sales of foreign assets are treated as part of the CFA.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Calling the balance of trade "the current account".** It is only one part.
2. **Recording a purchase of a foreign asset as a credit.** Money leaves: debit.
3. **Putting interest or dividends in the CFA.** Income goes in the CA.
4. **Calling remittances exports.** They are transfers.
5. **Saying a CFA surplus means lending abroad.** It is a net inflow.
6. **Dropping signs.** Add signed numbers: deficit (−), surplus (+).

## Quick self-check

All figures are fictional, in billions of valdas (VD bn), for Valdoria.

1. Net exports −15, net income from abroad +4, net unilateral transfers −2. What is the CA? *(−13)*
2. The CA is −13. What is the CFA, and is it an inflow or an outflow? *(+13, a net inflow)*
3. A Valdorian investor buys a flat in Kestria. Which account, credit or debit? *(CFA, debit)*
4. The investor later receives rent from the flat. Which account? *(CA, net income from abroad, credit)*
