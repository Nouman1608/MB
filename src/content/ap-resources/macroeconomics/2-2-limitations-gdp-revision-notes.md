## Recap

- GDP is a **useful** indicator: one number for total output, measured regularly, comparable over time and between countries.
- It measures **market** production. It is **not** a full measure of well-being.
- **Exclusions by design** (intermediate goods, used goods, financial transactions, transfer payments) are correct accounting, **not** limitations.

## The limitations at a glance

| Limitation | Effect on GDP as a guide to well-being |
|---|---|
| Nonmarket transactions (home production, volunteering) | Missing, so GDP **understates** output; moving work from home to market inflates measured growth |
| Informal (underground) economy | Hidden cash work and illegal trade are mostly missing; makes country comparisons less reliable |
| Leisure | Free time has no value in GDP; longer hours raise GDP |
| Environment and resources | Pollution and depletion are not subtracted; clean-up and repair spending are **added** |
| What is produced and its quality | Security spending counts the same as schooling; quality gains and new products are captured poorly |
| Distribution of income | A total says nothing about who receives it |
| Population and prices | Use GDP per person; use real GDP (Topic 2.6) to remove price changes |

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Formula |
|---|---|
| GDP per person | GDP ÷ population |
| % change in GDP per person (approx.) | % change in GDP − % change in population |

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Calling transfer payments or intermediate goods a "limitation".**
2. **Saying clean-up spending lowers GDP.** It raises it.
3. **Comparing living standards with total GDP** instead of GDP per person.
4. **Unit slips:** billions ÷ millions needs a factor of 1,000.
5. **Assuming GDP growth means everyone is better off.**

## Quick self-check

VD is the valda, the currency of the fictional country Valdoria used in the study guide.

1. GDP is VD 240 billion and population is 8 million. What is GDP per person? *(VD 30,000)*
2. A retired nurse volunteers at a clinic every week. Is her work in GDP? *(No: it is a nonmarket activity, so GDP understates output.)*
3. GDP and population both grow by 5%. What happens to GDP per person? *(No change)*
