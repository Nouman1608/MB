## Understanding

- I can explain why an economy with an output gap moves back to full-employment output in the long run when the government takes no action. *(Guide: "The idea: an economy off LRAS will not stay there")*
- I can explain why unemployment above the natural rate pushes nominal wages down, and unemployment below it pushes them up. *(Guide: "Closing a recessionary gap", "Closing an inflationary gap"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain that self-adjustment shifts SRAS, not AD or LRAS. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can explain that a shift of LRAS shows a change in full-employment output, and that a rightward shift is economic growth. *(Guide: "Shifts of the LRAS curve"; Practice Q4, Q7(d))*

## Skills

- I can find the short-run and long-run equilibria from AD and SRAS schedules, and the size of the SRAS shift. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate real wages before and after a change in the price level, and the nominal wage that restores the real wage. *(Guide: Worked example 1(b)–(c); Practice Q7(a)–(b))*
- I can draw an AD–AS graph showing a shock, the short-run equilibrium and the long-run return to LRAS. *(Guide: Figures 1 and 2; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can compare the long-run price level, real output and unemployment with their values before an AD shock. *(Guide: "Summary" table; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why a temporary supply shock leaves the economy where it started in the long run. *(Guide: "Supply shocks and self-adjustment"; Practice Q3, Q6(d))*
- I can tell a temporary output gain from AD apart from lasting growth in capacity. *(Guide: "Shifts of the LRAS curve"; Practice Q7(e))*
