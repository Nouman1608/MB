Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, graphs and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/6-3-foreign-exchange-market-study-guide/).

## Recap

- The **foreign exchange market** is where one currency is traded for another. Its price is the **exchange rate**.
- With a **flexible** rate, the market sets the rate where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
- **Demand for valdas** comes from people buying Valdorian exports, services (such as tourism) and financial assets. It is a derived demand.
- **Supply of valdas** comes from Valdorians paying for imports, travel abroad and foreign assets.
- A cheaper valda makes Valdorian goods cheaper abroad, so **more valdas are demanded** (downward slope).
- A dearer valda makes foreign goods cheaper for Valdorians, so **more valdas are supplied** (upward slope).
- Movements toward equilibrium are **movements along** the curves. Shifts are Topic 6.4.

## Key relationships

| Idea | Rule | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Axes for the market for valdas | vertical: OS per VD; horizontal: quantity of VD | label the graph |
| Equilibrium | quantity demanded = quantity supplied | find the equilibrium rate |
| Surplus | quantity supplied − quantity demanded (rate above equilibrium) | predict depreciation |
| Shortage | quantity demanded − quantity supplied (rate below equilibrium) | predict appreciation |
| Reciprocal rate | VD per OS = 1 ÷ (OS per VD) | switch to the other currency's market |
| Linked markets | supply of VD = demand for OS; demand for VD = supply of OS | answer about either currency |

## Assumptions behind the model

- Two currencies only (valda and ostro), so "the other currency" is clear.
- The exchange rate is flexible: no government sets it.
- Everything except the exchange rate is held constant when you move along a curve.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Labelling the vertical axis just "price" or using the wrong ratio (VD per OS in the valda market).
2. Saying Valdorians demand valdas in this market. They mostly supply them.
3. Saying a surplus raises the rate. It lowers it.
4. Shifting a curve because the exchange rate changed. That is a movement along.
5. Measuring a surplus or shortage vertically instead of as a quantity of currency.
6. Assuming the two currencies change by the same percentage.

## Quick self-check

Valdoria (currency: valda, VD) and Ostria (currency: ostro, OS) are the fictional countries used in the study guide.

1. At OS 1.8 per valda, 70 million valdas are demanded and 95 million supplied. What is in the market, and which way does the rate move? *(A surplus of 25 million VD; the valda depreciates.)*
2. A Valdorian buys Ostrian shares. Does she demand or supply valdas? *(She supplies valdas and demands ostros.)*
3. One valda is worth OS 1.25. What is one ostro worth in valdas? *(VD 0.80)*
