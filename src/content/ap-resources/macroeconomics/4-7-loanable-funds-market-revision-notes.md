## Recap

- The **loanable funds market** models savers lending to borrowers. Its price is the **real interest rate**.
- **Demand** (borrowers: firms investing, households, a government in deficit) slopes **down**: at a lower real rate more investment projects are worth funding.
- **Supply** (savers: households, firms, foreign lenders, a government in surplus) slopes **up**: a higher real rate rewards saving.
- **Equilibrium:** the real rate at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
- Below equilibrium there is a **shortage** and the rate rises; above it there is a **surplus** and the rate falls.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Formula or result |
|---|---|
| Private saving | Y − T − C |
| Public saving | T − G (negative in a deficit) |
| National saving | private + public = Y − C − G |
| Closed economy | investment = national saving |
| Open economy | investment = national saving + net capital inflow |
| Investment tax credit | demand right → real rate up, quantity up |
| Households save more | supply right → real rate down, quantity up |
| More government borrowing | demand right (or supply left) → real rate up, private investment down |
| Net capital inflow rises | supply right → real rate down, quantity up |

## Assumptions

- All kinds of lending (loans, bonds) are combined into one market.
- Amounts are flows per year.
- A change in the real rate moves you **along** a curve; other factors **shift** it.
- A government deficit can be shown as a demand shift or a supply shift. Both give a higher real rate.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Labelling the vertical axis "interest rate" or "nominal interest rate" instead of **real interest rate**.
2. Confusing this graph with the money market, where supply is vertical.
3. Shifting supply for an investment tax credit. It shifts **demand**.
4. Saying more saving raises the interest rate. It **lowers** it.
5. Writing public saving as G − T.
6. Forgetting net capital inflow in an open economy.

## Quick self-check

VD is the valda, the currency of the fictional country Valdoria used in the study guide. Amounts are in VD billion.

1. Y = 400, C = 290, T = 60, G = 75. What are private, public and national saving? *(50, −15, 35)*
2. National saving is 120 and net capital inflow is 15. What is investment in this open economy? *(135)*
3. A new technology raises the expected return on new machines. Which curve shifts, and what happens to the real interest rate? *(Demand right; the real rate rises.)*
