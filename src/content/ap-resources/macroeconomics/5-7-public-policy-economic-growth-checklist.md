## Understanding

- I can explain the two routes by which policy raises real GDP per capita: productivity and labour force participation. *(Guide: "Why policy can change long-run growth")*
- I can give examples of policies that raise physical capital, human capital, technology or participation, and name the channel for each. *(Guide: "Policies that raise productivity or participation")*
- I can define supply-side fiscal policy and explain that it works through incentives to work, save, invest and innovate. *(Guide: "Supply-side fiscal policy"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why public investment in infrastructure shifts AD first and LRAS later. *(Guide: "Government investment in infrastructure and technology"; Practice Q2)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate real GDP per capita from output per worker and the employed share of the population. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can calculate the growth in real GDP per capita after a change in productivity, participation or population, and check it with the shortcut. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*

## Graphs and reasoning

- I can draw an AD–AS graph showing a supply-side tax cut, with the short-run AD shift and the long-run SRAS and LRAS shifts. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6(a)–(c))*
- I can explain why the price-level effect of a supply-side policy is ambiguous while real output rises. *(Guide: "Supply-side fiscal policy"; Practice Q6(d))*
- I can tell a growth policy from a demand-only policy and explain why a demand boost at full employment mainly raises the price level in the long run. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7(b), Q7(d))*
- I can use a PPC with capital and consumer goods to explain the opportunity cost of faster growth. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q4, Q7(c))*
- I can explain how borrowing to fund a growth policy can crowd out private investment. *(Guide: "Trade-offs and limits"; Practice Q6(e))*
