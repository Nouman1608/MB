Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-2-multipliers-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-2-multipliers-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define the MPC and the MPS and explain why they add up to 1. *(Guide: "Marginal propensities: MPC and MPS")*
- I can define the expenditure multiplier and the tax multiplier and say which changes each one applies to. *(Guide: "The two multipliers")*
- I can explain why a change in autonomous spending changes total spending and output by more than the first change. *(Guide: "The idea"; Worked example 1(c))*

## Calculation

- I can calculate the MPC and MPS from changes in disposable income, consumption or saving. *(Guide: Worked example 1(a); Practice Q3)*
- I can calculate the spending and tax multipliers from the MPC or MPS. *(Practice Q1, Q6(a))*
- I can calculate the change in AD from a change in C, I, G or net exports, including a fall in spending. *(Guide: Worked example 1(c); Practice Q5, Q7(a))*
- I can calculate the change in AD from a change in taxes or transfers, with the correct sign. *(Guide: Worked example 2(b); Practice Q2, Q7(c))*
- I can work backwards from a target change in AD to the spending change or tax change needed. *(Guide: Worked example 2(d); Practice Q6(b)–(c))*
- I can calculate the combined effect of equal changes in spending and taxes. *(Guide: Worked example 2(c); Practice Q6(e))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why the spending multiplier is larger than the tax multiplier. *(Guide: "Why the tax multiplier is smaller"; Practice Q4, Q6(d))*
- I can explain how a higher MPC leads to a larger multiplier, using rounds of spending. *(Practice Q5(d), Q7(b))*
