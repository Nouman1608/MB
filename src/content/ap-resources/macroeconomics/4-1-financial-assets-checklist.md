Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/4-1-financial-assets-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/4-1-financial-assets-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can describe money, bonds and stocks, and explain the difference between a loan (bond) and ownership (stock). *(Guide: "What a financial asset is")*
- I can define liquidity, rate of return and risk in my own words. *(Guide: "The three attributes"; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can explain why cash and demand deposits are the most liquid assets, and rank other assets by liquidity. *(Practice Q1, Q6(b))*
- I can explain why higher expected returns usually come with higher risk. *(Guide: "The three attributes"; Practice Q6(c)–(d))*

## Calculation

- I can calculate the price of a one-year bond from its payment at maturity and the market interest rate. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3, Q7(a))*
- I can calculate a bondholder's capital gain or loss when market rates change. *(Guide: Worked example 1(d); Practice Q7(b))*
- I can calculate the yearly opportunity cost of holding money, including when deposits pay some interest. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain, step by step, why the price of a previously issued bond falls when market interest rates rise. *(Guide: "Bond prices and interest rates"; Practice Q2, Q7(c))*
- I can explain why a lower bond price means a higher rate of return for a new buyer. *(Practice Q2, Q7(d))*
- I can explain why people hold less money when interest rates rise, using the idea of opportunity cost. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4, Q5(c))*
- I can choose a suitable asset for a given purpose and justify it with liquidity, return and risk. *(Practice Q6)*
