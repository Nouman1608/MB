Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/1-1-scarcity-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/1-1-scarcity-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define scarcity as limited resources compared with unlimited wants. *(Guide: "What scarcity means"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why every economy and every person faces scarcity, including rich countries. *(Guide: "What scarcity means"; Practice Q3, Q7(b))*
- I can define economic resources and name the four factors of production. *(Guide: "Economic resources: the four factors of production")*
- I can tell a free good apart from a good that simply has a zero price. *(Guide: "Scarce goods and free goods"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain the difference between scarcity and a shortage. *(Guide: "Scarcity and shortage are different"; Practice Q1, Q7(c))*

## Skills

- I can classify any item as land, labour, capital or entrepreneurship, or say that it is not an economic resource. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q4, Q6(a))*
- I can explain why money is not capital in economics. *(Guide: "Economic resources: the four factors of production"; Practice Q2, Q6(b))*
- I can use simple data to show that wants are greater than the resources available. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5(a)–(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how scarcity forces individuals, firms and governments to make choices. *(Guide: "How scarcity forces choices")*
- I can describe the trade-off in a given choice: what is gained and what is given up. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can explain why finding more resources reduces scarcity but does not end it. *(Guide: the Check in Worked example 2; Practice Q7(b))*
