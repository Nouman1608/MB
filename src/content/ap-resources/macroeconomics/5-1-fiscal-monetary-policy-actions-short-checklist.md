## Understanding

- I can name the tools of fiscal policy and of monetary policy, and say who uses each. *(Guide: "Two levers on one economy")*
- I can choose a fiscal action and a monetary action that together close a recessionary gap, and a pair that closes an inflationary gap. *(Guide: "Choosing the direction: match the gap"; Practice Q1, Q6(b))*
- I can explain why policies that push in the same direction have a certain effect on AD but an uncertain effect on the interest rate. *(Guide: "What a combined policy does"; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why policies that push in opposite directions have a certain effect on the interest rate but an uncertain effect on AD. *(Guide: "What a combined policy does"; Practice Q2, Q7(b))*

## Calculation

- I can calculate the combined shift in AD by multiplying each action by its own multiplier and adding the results. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q7(a))*
- I can calculate the minimum fiscal action needed to close a gap when a monetary action already shifts AD by a known amount. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain, with numbers, why a tax change must be larger than a spending change to have the same effect on AD. *(Guide: Worked example 2(c); Practice Q5(d), Q7(c))*

## Graphs and reasoning

- I can show a combined policy closing a gap on a correctly labelled AD–AS graph, with the new price level. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6(a), (c))*
- I can show on a money market graph how fiscal expansion changes money demand and the nominal interest rate. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q6(d))*
- I can identify the open market operation a central bank would use to hold the interest rate steady during a fiscal expansion. *(Guide: "Showing the interest rate on a money market graph"; Practice Q6(e))*
