## Recap

- **Short run:** some input prices, especially nominal wages, are fixed. **Long run:** all wages and prices have adjusted fully. Neither is a fixed number of years.
- The **LRAS curve** is **vertical** at **full-employment output (Yf)**, also called potential output.
- Why vertical: in the long run, input prices rise or fall with the price level, so real profit per unit does not change. Output depends only on **resources and technology**.
- At Yf, unemployment equals the **natural rate**; cyclical unemployment is zero.
- **No long-run trade-off** between inflation and unemployment: any price level gives Yf and the natural rate in the long run.
- LRAS and the **PPC** both show **maximum sustainable capacity**.

## Key relationships

| Situation | PPC | AD–AS model |
|---|---|---|
| Full employment | On the curve | Real GDP = Yf, on LRAS |
| Unemployed resources | Inside the curve | Real GDP < Yf, left of LRAS |
| Resources over-used for a time | Beyond the curve: not sustainable | Real GDP > Yf, right of LRAS |
| More resources or better technology | Shifts out | LRAS shifts right (Topic 3.7) |

| Curve | Slope | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| SRAS | Upward | Sticky wages and prices |
| LRAS | Vertical | Fully flexible wages and prices |

## Assumptions

- In the long run every input price changes in proportion to the price level.
- Resources and technology are fixed unless the question says otherwise.
- How the economy returns to Yf after a shock is Topic 3.7.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying LRAS is vertical because wages are **sticky** (that explains SRAS).
2. Equating full employment with **zero** unemployment.
3. Saying output **cannot** exceed Yf. It can, but not sustainably.
4. Shifting LRAS left when the economy is merely **inside** its PPC.
5. Defining the long run as a **number of years**.
6. Claiming a higher price level raises **long-run** real output. It raises nominal values only; real output stays at Yf.
7. Shifting LRAS for a **temporary** rise in input prices. That shifts SRAS only; LRAS moves when capacity changes.

## Quick self-check

VD is the valda, the currency of the fictional country Valdoria used in the study guide.

1. Labour force 25.0 million, natural rate 5.0%, unemployment 6.0%. How many people are cyclically unemployed? *(0.25 million)*
2. A firm's price rises 10% from VD 50; costs of VD 40 also rise 10%. Real profit per unit? *(VD 10, unchanged)*
3. Unemployment is below the natural rate. Left or right of LRAS? *(Right)*
