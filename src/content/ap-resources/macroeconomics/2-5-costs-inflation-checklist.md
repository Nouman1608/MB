Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/2-5-costs-inflation-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/2-5-costs-inflation-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between expected and unexpected inflation. *(Guide: "Expected and unexpected inflation"; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why contracts fixed in money terms allow unexpected inflation to move purchasing power from one group to another. *(Guide: "Expected and unexpected inflation")*
- I can explain why fully expected inflation does not redistribute wealth between a lender and a borrower. *(Guide: "What if the inflation had been expected?"; Practice Q4)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate what a fixed loan repayment is worth in start-of-year money after inflation or deflation. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3, Q6(b)–(c))*
- I can calculate the real value of a fixed wage or pension and the percentage change in purchasing power. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5, Q7(a))*
- I can calculate the indexed payment that keeps purchasing power constant. *(Guide: Worked example 2(d); Practice Q5(c), Q7(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can identify who gains and who loses from unexpected inflation: lenders, borrowers, savers, pensioners, workers and employers. *(Guide: "Other winners and losers"; Practice Q1, Q5(d))*
- I can explain how unexpected deflation reverses these effects and raises the real burden of debt. *(Guide: "Lenders and borrowers"; Practice Q2, Q6(c))*
- I can explain why the redistribution is called arbitrary. *(Practice Q6(d))*
- I can explain at least one cost that unexpected inflation imposes on the whole economy, such as less long-term lending. *(Guide: "Costs to the economy as a whole"; Practice Q7(c))*
