## Understanding

- I can define short-run equilibrium as the point where the aggregate quantity of output demanded equals the quantity supplied, where AD crosses SRAS. *(Guide: "Short-run equilibrium"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain how unplanned changes in inventories push the price level and output towards equilibrium from above or below. *(Guide: "Short-run equilibrium"; Practice Q2, Q5(b))*
- I can state the extra condition for long-run equilibrium: AD and SRAS cross on LRAS, at Yf. *(Guide: "Long-run equilibrium"; Practice Q7(c))*
- I can explain why a short-run equilibrium need not be at full employment. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*

## Skills

- I can find the equilibrium price level and real output from AD and SRAS schedules. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(a))*
- I can calculate a surplus or shortage of output at a price level away from equilibrium. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(b))*
- I can calculate an output gap in money terms and as a percentage of Yf, with the correct sign. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5(c), Q7(a)–(b))*
- I can draw a correctly labelled AD–AS graph showing long-run equilibrium or a negative or positive output gap. *(Guide: Figures 1–2 and "How to draw an AD–AS equilibrium graph"; Practice Q6(a))*

## Reasoning

- I can link the position of short-run equilibrium relative to LRAS to unemployment above, at or below the natural rate. *(Guide: "Output gaps"; Practice Q3, Q5(d), Q7(b))*
- I can work out full-employment output from unemployment data and the natural rate. *(Practice Q7(a))*
- I can explain why a positive output gap is called inflationary and why it cannot last. *(Guide: "Output gaps"; Practice Q6(c))*
