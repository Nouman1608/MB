Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/6-3-foreign-exchange-market-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/6-3-foreign-exchange-market-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can describe the foreign exchange market and say what its price, the exchange rate, measures. *(Guide: "What the foreign exchange market is")*
- I can explain that the demand for a currency comes from the demand for that country's goods, services and financial assets. *(Guide: "Who demands valdas?"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain that the supply of a currency comes from that country's residents paying for things in other currencies. *(Guide: "Who supplies valdas?"; Practice Q1, Q7(a))*
- I can explain why a change in the exchange rate is a movement along the curves, not a shift. *(Guide: "Equilibrium, surplus and shortage"; Practice Q3)*

## Graphing and calculation

- I can draw a graph of the market for a currency with both axes labelled correctly. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can find the equilibrium exchange rate and quantity from a table or from equations. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q5(a))*
- I can calculate a surplus or shortage of a currency at a given exchange rate. *(Practice Q5(b)–(c))*
- I can convert an exchange rate into its reciprocal and find the value of trades in the other currency. *(Guide: Worked example 1(d); Practice Q5(d), Q7(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why the demand curve for a currency slopes downward and the supply curve slopes upward, using prices of real goods. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q6(d))*
- I can explain, with a graph, how a surplus or shortage moves a flexible exchange rate back to equilibrium. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2, Q6(b)–(c))*
- I can link the market for one currency to the market for the other, including opposite percentage changes. *(Guide: "Two markets, one set of trades"; Practice Q7(c)–(d))*
