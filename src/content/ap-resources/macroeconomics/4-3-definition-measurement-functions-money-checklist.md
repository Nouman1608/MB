## Understanding

- I can define money as any asset accepted as a means of payment, and explain why bonds, houses and credit cards are not money. *(Guide: "What money is"; Practice Q7(c))*
- I can explain how money solves the double coincidence of wants problem of barter. *(Guide: "What money is"; Practice Q6(b))*
- I can name the three functions of money and identify which one an everyday example shows. *(Guide: "The three functions of money"; Practice Q1, Q6(a))*
- I can explain why money is an imperfect store of value. *(Guide: "The three functions of money"; Practice Q6(c))*
- I can list what is in M1, what M2 adds, and what is in the monetary base. *(Guide: "Measuring the money supply: M1 and M2", "The monetary base", Figure 1)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate M1 and M2 from a table, leaving out reserves, large time deposits, bonds and credit limits. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can calculate the monetary base from currency in circulation and bank reserves. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can find the value of near-money as M2 − M1. *(Practice Q5(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can work out how a transfer between accounts changes M1, M2 and the monetary base. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q7)*
- I can explain why depositing or withdrawing cash leaves M2 and the monetary base unchanged. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7(a))*
- I can tell which items are in the monetary base but not in M1. *(Practice Q4)*
