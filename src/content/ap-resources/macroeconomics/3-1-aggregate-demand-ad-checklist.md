Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-1-aggregate-demand-ad-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/3-1-aggregate-demand-ad-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define aggregate demand and name the four groups of buyers and their components (C, I, G, NX). *(Guide: "What aggregate demand means")*
- I can explain the difference between aggregate demand and the quantity of real GDP demanded. *(Guide: "What aggregate demand means"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why the AD curve does not slope down because of substitution between goods. *(Guide: "Why the AD curve slopes downward")*

## Graphing

- I can draw an AD curve with the axes labelled Price level and Real GDP. *(Guide: "The axes", Figure 1; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can show a shift of AD on a graph and label the old and new curves. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q6(b))*
- I can describe a shift of AD from a schedule, giving its direction and size at every price level. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain the real wealth, interest rate and exchange rate effects, linking each to the right component. *(Guide: "Why the AD curve slopes downward"; Practice Q1, Q4, Q6(d))*
- I can decide whether an event causes a movement along AD or a shift of AD. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q3)*
- I can name the component a news event affects and state whether AD shifts right or left. *(Guide: "What shifts the AD curve"; Practice Q2, Q6(c))*
- I can explain why a policy change in interest rates shifts AD but a price-level-driven change does not. *(Guide: "What shifts the AD curve"; Practice Q7(a))*
- I can combine two opposing changes in spending and state the overall direction of the shift. *(Practice Q7(c))*
