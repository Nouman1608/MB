Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/4-4-banking-expansion-money-supply-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/4-4-banking-expansion-money-supply-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can place reserves, loans, securities, demand deposits and owners' equity on the correct side of a bank's balance sheet. *(Guide: "What a bank does"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain fractional reserve banking and why a single bank can lend only its excess reserves. *(Guide: "Fractional reserve banking"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain how one bank's loan becomes another bank's deposit, so lending creates new money in rounds. *(Guide: "How the banking system multiplies deposits", Figure 1)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate required reserves (rr × demand deposits) and excess reserves (total − required). *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q6(a), Q6(c))*
- I can update a balance sheet after a cash deposit, a loan or a withdrawal and check that it balances. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6(b), Q6(e), Q7(b))*
- I can calculate the money multiplier as money supply ÷ monetary base, and its maximum as 1 ÷ rr. *(Guide: "The money multiplier"; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can calculate the maximum change in loans, demand deposits and the money supply, and say which uses the excess reserves and which uses the whole deposit. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q6(f))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why a cash deposit or withdrawal does not change M1 at first, but changes how much banks can lend. *(Guide: Worked example 1 "Check"; Practice Q7(a))*
- I can work out a multiple contraction of the money supply after a withdrawal. *(Practice Q7(b)–(d))*
- I can explain why excess reserves held by banks and currency held by the public make the actual expansion smaller than the simple multiplier predicts. *(Guide: "Why the simple multiplier overstates expansion"; Practice Q4, Q5(d), Q7(e))*
