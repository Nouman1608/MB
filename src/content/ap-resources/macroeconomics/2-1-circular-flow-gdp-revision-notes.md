## Recap

- **GDP:** the market value of all **final** goods and services **produced within** a country in a given period.
- **Nominal GDP** uses the prices of the same year: add price × quantity for every final good.
- **Circular flow:** households sell resources to firms in the **factor market** and buy goods and services in the **product market**. Money flows one way; goods, services and resources flow the other way.
- One person's spending is another person's income, so **total spending = total income = value of output**.
- **Leakages:** saving, taxes, imports. **Injections:** investment, government purchases, exports.

## Key relationships

| Approach | Formula | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Expenditure | GDP = C + I + G + Xn | Xn = exports − imports; can be negative |
| Investment (I) | new capital goods + new housing + change in inventories | shares and bonds are not investment |
| Government (G) | purchases of goods and services only | transfer payments are excluded |
| Income | GDP = wages + rent + interest + profit | profit is income too |
| Value added | value of output − intermediate goods bought | sum of value added = price of final good |
| Nominal GDP | Σ (price × quantity) of final goods | leave out intermediate goods |

## Not counted in GDP

| Excluded | Why |
|---|---|
| Intermediate goods | already inside the final price (double counting) |
| Used goods | produced in an earlier year |
| Shares, bonds, bank deposits | financial transactions, not production |
| Transfer payments | nothing produced in return |
| Nonmarket activity (Topic 2.2) | no market transaction to record |

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Calling share purchases "investment".**
2. **Putting new houses in C.** They belong in I.
3. **Adding transfer payments to G.**
4. **Adding imports** instead of subtracting them.
5. **Adding every sale in a supply chain.** Use final goods or value added.
6. **Forgetting a fall in inventories** is a negative part of I.
