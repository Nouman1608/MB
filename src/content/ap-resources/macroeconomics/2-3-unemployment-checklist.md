Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/2-3-unemployment-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/2-3-unemployment-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can sort any adult into employed, unemployed or not in the labor force, and explain the test for being unemployed. *(Guide: "Sorting the adult population into three groups"; Practice Q1)*
- I can define the labor force, the unemployment rate and the labor force participation rate, and say which denominator each rate uses. *(Guide: "Sorting the adult population into three groups"; Practice Q2)*
- I can define frictional, structural and cyclical unemployment and classify an example of each. *(Guide: "Three types of unemployment"; Practice Q4)*
- I can define the natural rate and explain why full employment does not mean zero unemployment. *(Guide: "The natural rate of unemployment"; Practice Q6(c))*

## Calculation

- I can calculate the labor force, the unemployment rate and the participation rate from survey data. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q7(a))*
- I can work backwards from the two rates to the number employed and unemployed. *(Practice Q5(a))*
- I can calculate the natural rate and the cyclical rate, including a negative cyclical rate. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6(a)–(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how a person moving between groups changes each rate, including why discouraged workers lower the unemployment rate. *(Guide: "How changes in the labor market move the rates"; Practice Q3, Q5(b))*
- I can explain why the measured rate can understate joblessness, using discouraged workers and part-time workers who want full-time work. *(Guide: "Limitations of the unemployment rate"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain how a change in labor force characteristics or job-search methods changes the natural rate. *(Guide: "The natural rate of unemployment"; Practice Q6(d)–(e))*
