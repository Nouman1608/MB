## Understanding

- I can explain why a change in the exchange rate changes what foreign buyers pay, even when producers keep their own prices. *(Guide: "Why the exchange rate matters for trade"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why an appreciation lowers exports, raises imports and lowers net exports. *(Guide: "How an appreciation changes trade"; Practice Q1, Q7(a))*
- I can explain why a depreciation raises exports, lowers imports and raises net exports. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(b))*
- I can explain that one currency's appreciation is the other currency's depreciation, so the two countries' net exports move in opposite directions. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q4, Q6(e))*

## Calculation

- I can convert a price from one currency into another, before and after a change in the exchange rate. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q5(b))*
- I can calculate the percentage change in the value of each currency and explain why the two figures differ. *(Guide: Worked example 2 "Check"; Practice Q5(a))*
- I can calculate net exports and use the spending multiplier to find the shift in AD from a change in NX. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q5(c), Q7(b))*

## Reasoning and graphs

- I can draw the foreign exchange market for a currency and show the shift that causes it to appreciate or depreciate. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can carry the chain from a currency change through net exports to a shift of AD on a correctly labelled AD–AS graph. *(Guide: "From net exports to aggregate demand"; Practice Q6(c))*
- I can state the short-run effects on real output, unemployment and the price level. *(Practice Q3, Q6(d))*
- I can explain why a currency change shifts AD rather than moving the economy along it. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q7(c))*
