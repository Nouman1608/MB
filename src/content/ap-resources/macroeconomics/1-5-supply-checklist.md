Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/1-5-supply-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/macroeconomics/1-5-supply-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can state the law of supply and say why the supply curve slopes upward. *(Guide: "The law of supply"; Practice Q1, Q7(e))*
- I can explain the difference between quantity supplied and supply. *(Guide: "What supply means")*
- I can tell a change in quantity supplied from a change in supply, and say which one a change in the good's own price causes. *(Guide: "Change in quantity supplied v. change in supply"; Practice Q2, Q6(e))*
- I can list the determinants of supply and give the direction each one shifts the curve. *(Guide: "Determinants of supply"; Practice Q3)*

## Skills

- I can build a market supply schedule by adding producers' quantities at each price. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(a))*
- I can calculate a change in quantity supplied from a schedule or a supply equation. *(Practice Q2, Q5(b), Q7(a))*
- I can draw a supply graph with price on the vertical axis, quantity on the horizontal axis and every curve labelled. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can show an increase or a decrease in supply on a graph with a labelled new curve and an arrow. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q6(b))*
- I can work out a new supply schedule after a per-unit tax or subsidy. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7(b)–(d))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how a change in input prices, technology or the number of sellers shifts supply. *(Guide: Worked examples 2 and 3; Practice Q5(c), Q6(c))*
- I can explain how a change in the price of an alternative product, or in producers' expectations, shifts current supply. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4, Q6(d))*
- I can explain why a change in demand does not shift the supply curve. *(Guide: Worked example 3(f); Practice Q3(D), Q6(e))*
