## Recap

- A **shock** shifts AD or SRAS. **Positive** = right, **negative** = left. In the short run, LRAS does not move.
- **Spending** changes (C, I, G, NX) shift **AD**. **Cost** changes (wages, input prices, expected inflation, productivity, supply shocks) shift **SRAS**.
- **AD shock:** the price level, real output and employment move in the **same** direction.
- **SRAS shock:** output and employment move one way, the price level the **other** way.
- **Demand-pull inflation:** AD shifts right. **Cost-push inflation:** SRAS shifts left.
- **Stagflation:** a higher price level with lower output and higher unemployment, after a negative SRAS shock.
- After an AD shift, output changes by **less** than the horizontal shift, because the price level also changes.

## Key relationships

| Shock | Price level | Real output | Employment | Unemployment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AD ↑ (positive) | ↑ | ↑ | ↑ | ↓ |
| AD ↓ (negative) | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ | ↑ |
| SRAS ↑ (positive) | ↓ | ↑ | ↑ | ↓ |
| SRAS ↓ (negative) | ↑ | ↓ | ↓ | ↑ |

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Size of AD shift from a spending change | spending change × 1 ÷ (1 − MPC) |
| Inflation rate | (PL₂ − PL₁) ÷ PL₁ × 100 |

## Assumptions

- Short run only: nominal wages are sticky and LRAS is fixed.
- One curve shifts at a time unless the question says otherwise.
- What happens after the short run, with no policy, is Topic 3.7.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Shifting a curve because the **price level** changed. That is a movement along it.
2. Treating an **energy price** rise as an AD shift. It raises costs: SRAS left.
3. Calling every price rise **demand-pull**. Check whether output rose or fell.
4. Saying output rises by the **full** AD shift.
5. Moving **LRAS** for a temporary cost shock.
6. Saying unemployment and the price level **always** move in opposite directions. Not after a negative SRAS shock.
7. Leaving out the **old curve** or the new equilibrium labels on a graph.

## Quick self-check

VD is the valda, the currency of the fictional country Valdoria used in the study guide.

1. After a shock the price level rises from 104 to 108 and real GDP falls. Which shock, and what type of inflation? *(SRAS decreased; cost-push, 3.85%)*
2. Government spending rises by VD 10 billion and the MPC is 0.8. How far does AD shift, and does output rise by more or less than this? *(VD 50 billion; less)*
3. Productivity rises across the economy. What happens to unemployment in the short run? *(It falls: SRAS shifts right and output rises)*
