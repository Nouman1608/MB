## Recap

- Eukaryotic cells contain **membrane-bound organelles**: nucleus, rough and smooth ER, Golgi complex, vesicles, lysosomes, vacuoles, mitochondria and (in plants and algae) chloroplasts. The plasma membrane bounds the whole cell.
- Each compartment holds its own enzymes, substrates and conditions. This **compartmentalization** lets the cell run many reactions at once.
- **Minimizing competing interactions:** opposing pathways run in different places; each space keeps its own pH (lysosome about 4.5–5, cytosol about 7.2); harmful enzymes stay contained; enzymes and substrates are concentrated.
- **Increasing surface area:** many reactions happen on membrane proteins, so folds such as cristae (mitochondria), stacked thylakoids (chloroplasts) and the ER network add room for them without adding volume.

## Key relationships

| Feature | How it helps | Example |
|---|---|---|
| separate membrane-bound space | keeps opposing reactions apart | building and breaking down molecules at the same time |
| proton pumps in a membrane | keep a different pH inside | acidic lysosome for digestive enzymes |
| enzymes kept inside | protects the rest of the cell | lysosomal enzymes work poorly in the cytosol |
| small volume | concentrates enzymes and substrates | faster reactions |
| folded inner membrane | more area for embedded proteins | cristae hold more ATP synthase |
| two membranes, two spaces | allows a difference across a membrane | H⁺ difference across the inner mitochondrial membrane (Unit 3) |

| Calculation | Rule |
|---|---|
| folded membrane area | unfolded area **+** area added by all folds |
| pH change | each 1 unit = factor of 10 in H⁺; higher pH = fewer H⁺ |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Box and fold models ignore curvature; they compare areas, not exact sizes.
- "More membrane means more activity" assumes the same number of proteins per µm².
- Enzyme activity measured in a test tube may differ inside a cell.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying folding makes the organelle bigger; it adds area, not volume.
2. Forgetting the unfolded part of the membrane when totalling area.
3. Saying higher pH means more H⁺ ions.
4. Treating compartments as sealed; vesicles and transport proteins move material between them.
5. Saying the nucleus makes proteins; ribosomes do, outside it.
6. Describing an organelle's job without saying **why** a separate compartment helps.

## Quick self-check

1. How many times more H⁺ is there at pH 5 than at pH 7? *(10² = 100 times)*
2. A membrane of 2.0 µm² gains 10 folds of 0.30 µm² each. New total? *(2.0 + 10 × 0.30 = 5.0 µm², 2.5 times the original)*
3. Predict one effect of blocking lysosome proton pumps. *(Lysosome pH rises, digestive enzymes slow down, undigested material builds up.)*
