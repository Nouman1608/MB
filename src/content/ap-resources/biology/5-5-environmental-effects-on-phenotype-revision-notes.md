Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, figures and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/5-5-environmental-effects-on-phenotype-study-guide/).

## Recap

- A **phenotype** depends on the **genotype** and on the **environment** in which the organism develops.
- Environmental signals (temperature, day length, light, diet, soil chemistry, other organisms) change **which genes are expressed** and **how much**, or change **how well a gene product works**.
- **Phenotypic plasticity** is the ability of one genotype to produce different phenotypes in different environments.
- The DNA sequence is not changed, so these responses are **not inherited** as such. Offspring inherit the alleles, including the ability to respond.
- To show an effect is environmental, hold the genotype constant: **clones**, inbred lines or identical twins.

## Key relationships

| Example | Signal | Result |
|---|---|---|
| sex in many reptiles | egg incubation temperature | in most such turtles, warm eggs give females (more aromatase, more oestrogen); cool eggs give males |
| arctic fox, snowshoe hare | shorter days | moult into a white winter coat |
| hydrangea | soil pH | acidic soil: aluminium taken up, flowers blue; alkaline soil: pink |
| skin | UV light | more melanin (tanning) |
| human height and weight | nutrition, health | where a person falls within a genetically set range |
| yeast | pheromone of the opposite mating type | mating genes switched on, including pheromone production |
| Siamese cat | cool skin | heat-sensitive tyrosinase works, dark fur |
| phenylketonuria | low-phenylalanine diet | prevents brain damage in people with two recessive alleles |

| Reaction norm shape | Meaning |
|---|---|
| flat line | trait not plastic for this factor |
| steep line | very plastic genotype |
| parallel lines | genotypes differ by a constant amount |
| crossing lines, different slopes | genotype-by-environment interaction |

| Calculation | How |
|---|---|
| percentage change | (new − original) ÷ original × 100 |
| pivotal temperature | interpolate to 50% between the two nearest points |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Comparisons only isolate the environment if the genotypes are the same and other variables are controlled.
- Linear interpolation assumes a straight line between two neighbouring points.
- Small samples **support** a conclusion; they do not prove it.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying the environment "changes the genes" or "causes mutations" when it changes gene expression.
2. Saying a tan or a winter coat is passed to offspring.
3. Calling a trait "genetic **or** environmental" when it is both.
4. Using seedlings, not clones, and then claiming a difference is environmental.
5. Dividing a percentage change by the new value instead of the original.
6. Forgetting that crossing reaction norms mean the "better" genotype depends on the environment.

## Quick self-check

1. Cuttings of one clone grow to 20 cm in shade and 30 cm in sun. Percentage increase? *((30 − 20) ÷ 20 × 100 = 50%)*
2. Why are warm turtle eggs more likely to hatch as females? *(Temperature raises expression of aromatase in the gonad; more testosterone becomes oestrogen, so ovaries develop.)*
3. Why do Siamese cats have dark paws? *(Their tyrosinase is heat-sensitive; it only makes melanin in the cooler skin of the extremities.)*
