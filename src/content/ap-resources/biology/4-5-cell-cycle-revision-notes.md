## Recap

- The **cell cycle** is a tightly regulated sequence that controls how eukaryotic cells grow and divide: **interphase** (G1, S, G2), then **mitosis**, then **cytokinesis**.
- **G1:** the cell is metabolically active, grows and copies its organelles and cytoplasm.
- **S:** DNA (as chromatin) replicates. Each chromosome becomes two **sister chromatids** joined at a **centromere**.
- **G2:** protein synthesis, lots of ATP made, centrosome duplication completed.
- **G0:** a non-dividing state outside the cycle. Many G0 cells can re-enter with the right cues; other non-dividing cells are held at one stage.
- **Mitosis** passes a complete genome to two **genetically identical** daughter cells. Used for growth, tissue repair and asexual reproduction.

## Key relationships

| Step | Main events |
|---|---|
| Prophase | chromosomes condense; spindle forms; centrosomes move to opposite poles; nuclear envelope breaks down |
| Metaphase | spindle fibres line chromosomes up on the equator |
| Anaphase | sister chromatids separate and are pulled to opposite poles |
| Telophase | spindle breaks down; nuclear envelopes re-form; cytoplasm starts to divide |
| Cytokinesis | animal: **cleavage furrow**; plant: **cell plate** from Golgi vesicles |

| Stage (cell with 2n chromosomes) | Chromosomes | Chromatids | DNA |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | 2n | 2n | x |
| G2 to metaphase | 2n | 4n | 2x |
| Anaphase (whole cell) | 4n | 4n | 2x |
| Each daughter | 2n | 2n | x |

| Calculation | How |
|---|---|
| Mitotic index | (cells in mitosis ÷ total cells) × 100 |
| Time in a stage | (cells in stage ÷ total cells) × cycle length |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Timing from cell counts assumes cells are spread at random through the cycle, not dividing in step.
- Large counts give better estimates; misidentifying early prophase as interphase lowers the mitotic index.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying the chromosome number doubles in S phase (the DNA doubles).
2. Placing DNA replication in prophase.
3. Calling interphase a resting stage.
4. Giving plant cells a cleavage furrow.
5. Saying mitosis halves the chromosome number.
6. Forgetting to include interphase cells in the total when calculating a mitotic index.

## Quick self-check

1. A cell has 12 chromosomes in G1. How many chromatids in G2, and how many chromosomes in anaphase? *(24 chromatids; 24 chromosomes in anaphase, 12 going to each pole.)*
2. 30 of 240 cells counted are in mitosis. Mitotic index? *(30 ÷ 240 × 100 = 12.5%)*
3. Why are the two daughter cells genetically identical? *(S phase makes exact copies of every chromosome, and anaphase sends one sister chromatid of each chromosome to each pole.)*
