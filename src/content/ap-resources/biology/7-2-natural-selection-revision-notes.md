Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, figures and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/7-2-natural-selection-study-guide/).

## Recap

- **Phenotype** = observable or measurable traits; **genotype** = alleles carried. Natural selection acts on phenotypes; genotypes are affected only through the phenotypes they produce.
- Variation is **genetic** (mutation, new combinations from meiosis and fertilisation) or **environmental**. Only the genetic part reaches the next generation.
- A **selective pressure** is a feature of the environment that makes some phenotypes survive or reproduce better. Changing environments create new pressures.
- The **same phenotype** can raise fitness in one environment and lower it, or make no difference, in another.
- Differences in the **types** and **numbers** of molecules in cells can give some individuals a better chance of surviving and reproducing in a given environment.

## Key relationships

| Example | Variation | Environment where it is favoured |
|---|---|---|
| Sickle-cell trait (one Hb S allele) | one amino acid change in β-globin | regions where malaria is common |
| Insecticide resistance | altered target protein, or more detoxifying enzyme | where the insecticide is used |
| Earlier flowering | heritable flowering date | warmer, earlier springs |
| Unsaturated membrane lipids | kinked fatty acid tails | cold conditions |
| Enzyme variants | different temperature optima | each in its own temperature range |

**Chain to state in answers:** molecule → cell function → phenotype → survival and reproduction in **this** environment.

| Calculation | Method |
|---|---|
| Relative fitness | mean success of phenotype ÷ mean success of best phenotype |
| Frequency after selection | survivors (or offspring) of a phenotype ÷ total survivors (or offspring) |

## Assumptions

- Using survival as fitness assumes survivors of all phenotypes have equal numbers of offspring.
- Frequency projections assume offspring share their parent's phenotype.
- All numbers in the guide and practice are fictional model values.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying the environment or a chemical **causes** the helpful variation.
2. Calling a trait "advantageous" without naming the environment.
3. Treating environmentally caused differences as heritable.
4. Calling insecticide resistance artificial selection (humans do not choose the parents).
5. Skipping links in the molecule-to-fitness chain.

## Quick self-check

1. A phenotype has survival 0.45; the best phenotype has 0.90. Relative fitness? *(0.45 ÷ 0.90 = 0.50)*
2. Why is the Hb S allele common in some regions despite causing disease? *(Carriers resist severe malaria, so they have the highest fitness where malaria is common.)*
3. A bacterium is moved from 37 °C to 10 °C. What membrane change would help? *(More unsaturated fatty acid tails, which keep the membrane fluid.)*
