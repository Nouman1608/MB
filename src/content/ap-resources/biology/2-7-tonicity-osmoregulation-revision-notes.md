## Recap

- **Osmosis** is the passive net movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane. Water moves; the solutes that set up the gradient cannot cross.
- **Tonicity** compares two solutions. A solution **hypotonic** to a cell has fewer solute particles, so water enters the cell. **Hypertonic**: more solute particles, water leaves. **Isotonic**: no net movement.
- Animal cells burst (lyse) in hypotonic and shrivel in hypertonic solutions. Plant cells become **turgid** in hypotonic solutions (the wall stops them bursting), **flaccid** in isotonic and **plasmolysed** in hypertonic.
- **Water potential (Ψ)** predicts direction: water moves from higher to lower Ψ.
- **Osmoregulation** keeps water and solute balance within limits: contractile vacuoles pump out water in freshwater protists; the plant central vacuole keeps cells turgid.

## Key relationships

| Equation | Symbols and units |
|---|---|
| **Ψ = Ψp + Ψs** | all in bars; Ψp = 0 in an open container |
| **Ψs = −iCRT** | i = particles per unit (sucrose 1, NaCl 2, CaCl₂ 3); C in mol L⁻¹; R = 0.0831 L bar mol⁻¹ K⁻¹; T in K (°C + 273) |

| Solution compared with cell | Solute particles | Water potential | Net water flow |
|---|---|---|---|
| hypotonic | fewer | higher than cell | into cell |
| isotonic | equal | equal | none |
| hypertonic | more | lower than cell | out of cell |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Salts are assumed to separate completely (i = 2 for NaCl), which is close enough for dilute solutions.
- In an open container Ψp = 0, so Ψ = Ψs.
- An isotonic point from mass-change data assumes the solute does not enter the cells and equilibrium was reached.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Using tonicity words without saying what is being compared.
2. Saying water moves toward higher water concentration and then drawing the arrow the wrong way.
3. Forgetting the minus sign, forgetting i, or using °C instead of kelvin.
4. Saying plant cells burst in pure water.
5. Saying water stops moving in an isotonic solution (only *net* movement stops).
6. Plotting raw mass change instead of percentage change when starting masses differ.

## Quick self-check

1. Ψs of 0.10 M sucrose at 27 °C? *(−(1)(0.10)(0.0831)(300) = −2.49 bar)*
2. A cell has Ψs = −6.0 bar and Ψp = +2.5 bar. Will it gain water from the solution in question 1? *(Cell Ψ = −3.5 bar, lower than −2.49 bar, so yes: water moves into the cell.)*
3. Why does *Paramecium* need a contractile vacuole but a plant cell does not? *(It has no wall and lives in hypotonic water, so it must expel the water that keeps entering; a plant cell's wall stops it bursting.)*
