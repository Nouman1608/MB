Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/2-10-origins-cell-compartmentalization-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/biology/2-10-origins-cell-compartmentalization-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can describe the specialized internal regions of a prokaryotic cell, such as the nucleoid and the plasma membrane's role in respiration, and explain why they are not membrane-bound organelles. *(Guide: "Inside a prokaryotic cell"; Practice Q5)*
- I can name the structures that prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells share and those only eukaryotes have. *(Guide: comparison table; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can explain how the internal membranes of a eukaryotic cell divide it into regions with specialized jobs. *(Guide: "Inside a eukaryotic cell")*
- I can outline the steps by which mitochondria and then chloroplasts arose by endosymbiosis, and explain why plants have both. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
- I can explain why modern mitochondria and chloroplasts cannot live on their own. *(Guide: "Since then: dependence"; Practice Q6)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can calculate percentage sequence identity and say which organism is the closest relative. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3, Q4)*
- I can calculate the percentage of protein synthesis remaining after a drug treatment. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can calculate surface area to volume ratios and total membrane area per unit volume for model cells. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can list at least four independent lines of evidence for endosymbiosis and explain how each supports it. *(Guide: "The evidence"; Practice Q2, Q4)*
- I can build a claim–evidence–reasoning argument from data on an unfamiliar structure. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can describe a result that would weaken the endosymbiosis claim and explain why. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can evaluate an argument against endosymbiosis by separating an organelle's origin from its present-day dependence. *(Practice Q6)*
