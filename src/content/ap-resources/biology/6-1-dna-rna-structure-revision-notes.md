## Recap

- Genetic information is stored in, and passed on through, **DNA**. Some viruses (for example influenza viruses and HIV) use **RNA** instead.
- **Prokaryotes** typically have **one circular chromosome** in the nucleoid region, with no nuclear envelope. It is packed by supercoiling.
- **Eukaryotes** typically have **several linear chromosomes** in the nucleus. DNA wraps around **histone** proteins (nucleosomes, "beads on a string") and is folded further.
- Histones are positively charged; the DNA backbone is negatively charged. The attraction holds DNA on the histones.
- **Plasmids** are small, circular, extra-chromosomal DNA molecules. They are common in bacteria and also occur in some eukaryotes, such as yeasts. They often carry genes such as antibiotic resistance and can pass between cells.
- Pairing rules are the same in all known cellular life: evidence of common ancestry.

## Key relationships

| Feature | Prokaryote | Eukaryote |
|---|---|---|
| Chromosome shape | typically circular | typically linear |
| Number of chromosomes | typically one | several |
| Location | nucleoid (cytoplasm) | nucleus |
| Packing | supercoiling and proteins | histones (nucleosomes) and further folding |
| Plasmids | common | in some (for example yeasts) |

| Base group | Rings | Bases | Pairs with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purines | double | A, G | a pyrimidine |
| Pyrimidines | single | C, T (DNA), U (RNA) | a purine |

Pairs: **A–T** (DNA), **A–U** (RNA), **G–C**. One purine + one pyrimidine = three rings = constant helix width.

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- "Purines = pyrimidines = 50%" applies only to **double-stranded** molecules, where every base is paired.
- Length calculations use a model value of about 0.34 nm per base pair.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying prokaryotes have no chromosome.
2. Saying all genetic material is DNA.
3. Swapping purines and pyrimidines: purines are the **double**-ringed A and G.
4. Pairing two purines (A–G) or two pyrimidines (C–T).
5. Calling histones DNA; they are proteins.
6. Applying the 50% purine rule to a single strand.

## Quick self-check

1. A double-stranded DNA molecule has 2 000 base pairs. How many purines does it contain? *(4 000 nucleotides, half are purines: 2 000)*
2. Double-stranded DNA is 22% C. What are G, A and T? *(G = 22%; purines total 50%, so A = 50 − 22 = 28% and T = 28%)*
3. Give two ways a bacterial chromosome differs from a human chromosome. *(circular, not linear; in the cytoplasm, not a nucleus; not wound around histones; usually only one)*
