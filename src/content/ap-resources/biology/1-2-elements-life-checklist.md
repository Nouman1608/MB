Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/1-2-elements-life-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/biology/1-2-elements-life-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why organisms must take in atoms and molecules from their environment to build new molecules. *(Guide: "Where the atoms in your body come from")*
- I can name carbon, hydrogen and oxygen as the most common elements in biological molecules, and say why carbon makes a good backbone. *(Guide: "The core elements")*
- I can state where sulfur is found in proteins, naming the two amino acids. *(Guide: "Sulfur: proteins"; Practice Q2)*
- I can state where phosphorus is found in phospholipids and in nucleic acids. *(Guide: "Phosphorus"; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can state where nitrogen is found in nucleic acids and in proteins. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q1)*
- I can describe how plants and animals obtain carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus and sulfur. *(Guide: "How organisms obtain these elements"; Practice Q7)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can identify the likely class of a molecule from its formula or a table of elements. *(Guide: Figure 1, Worked example 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can calculate the percentage by mass of an element in a molecule. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
- I can correct tracer readings for background and calculate the percentage of a label in each fraction. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can convert percentages by mass into a ratio of atoms. *(Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict which molecules a ³⁵S or ³²P label will enter, and explain why. *(Guide: "Using elements as labels"; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can explain how a shortage of nitrate or phosphate limits growth, using data. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can use elemental data as evidence to argue which class an unknown molecule belongs to. *(Practice Q6)*
