Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, figures and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/3-1-enzymes-study-guide/).

## Recap

- **Enzymes** are proteins that act as **biological catalysts**. They speed up reactions in cells and are not used up.
- **Activation energy (Eₐ)** is the energy needed to start a reaction. Enzymes **lower Eₐ**, so many more encounters lead to products.
- Enzymes do **not** change ΔG, do not supply energy and do not change the amount of product finally made.
- The **active site** is a pocket lined by R groups. It has a particular **shape** and a particular pattern of **charge and polarity**.
- A substrate binds only if it is **compatible in shape and charge**. Binding forms the **enzyme–substrate complex**; products are then released and the enzyme is reused.
- Cells **regulate** processes by controlling which enzymes they make and how active those enzymes are.

## Key relationships

| Idea | What it means | Exam signal |
|---|---|---|
| lower Eₐ | smaller energy hill on an energy profile | "explain how the enzyme speeds up the reaction" |
| same ΔG | start and end levels unchanged | energy diagram with two curves |
| shape fit | substrate size and outline match the pocket | "why does the enzyme act on only one substrate?" |
| charge fit | opposite charges attract; like charges repel | substitution of a charged R group in the active site |
| enzyme reused | E appears on both sides of E + S → ES → E + P | "why does the rate fall?" (substrate used up, not enzyme) |

| Equation | Notes |
|---|---|
| **rate = change in amount ÷ time** | units, e.g. cm³ s⁻¹ or mmol L⁻¹ min⁻¹ |
| **relative rate = 1 ÷ time** | when you time an end point, such as a colour change |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The **initial rate** assumes the substrate concentration has not yet fallen much.
- Comparing rates assumes temperature, pH and volumes were the same in every tube.
- Small trials **support** or **do not support** a claim; they do not prove it.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying the enzyme is "used up" when the rate falls.
2. Saying the enzyme "gives energy" to the reaction or makes ΔG more negative.
3. Explaining specificity by shape only, and forgetting charge.
4. Confusing negative controls (no effect expected) with positive controls (effect expected).
5. Reading the final height of a product–time curve as the rate; the rate is the **gradient**.

## Quick self-check

1. 18 cm³ of gas is produced in the first 60 s. Initial rate? *(18 ÷ 60 = 0.30 cm³ s⁻¹)*
2. An enzyme lowers Eₐ from 70 to 25 kJ mol⁻¹. By how much is it lowered, and what happens to ΔG? *(45 kJ mol⁻¹; ΔG is unchanged)*
3. Why add a boiled-enzyme tube to an experiment? *(It shows whether the effect needs an intact, heat-sensitive protein, ruling out other substances in the extract.)*
