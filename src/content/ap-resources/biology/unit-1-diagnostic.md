## What this diagnostic is for

Use this page **before** you revise Unit 1, or right after you finish it. It has one short question for each topic and a second one for the three largest topics (lipids, nucleic acids and proteins). Each answer tells you which study guide to read if you got it wrong.

These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, written for this site. They are not past exam questions. They are not calibrated against real exam results, so the number you get right does **not** predict an exam score. Treat your result as a map of what to revisit.

**Calculator and data.** A calculator is allowed on both sections of the Biology exam. Here a basic calculator is enough, and every number you need is given in the question. All data and organisms are fictional.

**How to work.** Answer every question before you open any answer. Write down a short reason for each choice. A right answer for the wrong reason still counts as a gap.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · 1.1)

A drop of water placed on a waxy leaf stays as a round bead. The same drop placed on a clean glass slide spreads out into a thin film. Which statement best explains the difference?

- (A) Water forms hydrogen bonds with the wax, and these pull the drop into a sphere.
- (B) The wax is nonpolar, so water molecules stay attracted mainly to each other (cohesion); the glass surface is polar, so water also hydrogen-bonds to it (adhesion) and spreads.
- (C) Glass has a higher specific heat than wax, so the water on glass evaporates into a film.
- (D) Water forms covalent bonds with glass but not with wax.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** Water is polar. On a nonpolar surface it has nothing to attract, so cohesion between water molecules wins and the drop pulls into a bead. A polar surface lets water form hydrogen bonds with it, so adhesion spreads the drop.

- (A) is tempting because hydrogen bonds do shape the drop, but they form between water molecules, not with nonpolar wax.
- (C) mixes up two separate properties. Specific heat is about temperature change, not spreading.
- (D) confuses attraction with bonding. Adhesion is a weak attraction; no covalent bonds form.

**If you missed this:** read "From mechanism to biological outcome" in the [water and hydrogen bonding study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/1-1-water-and-hydrogen-bonding-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · 1.2)

Bacteria are grown for several generations with ammonium containing the heavy isotope ¹⁵N as their only nitrogen source. Which molecule in the cells would you expect to contain **no** ¹⁵N?

- (A) DNA
- (B) Enzymes
- (C) Glycogen
- (D) RNA

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** Glycogen is a carbohydrate made only of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, so there is nowhere for nitrogen to go.

- (A) and (D) are tempting if you think of nucleic acids only as "the phosphorus molecules". Their bases are rich in nitrogen.
- (B) is tempting if you link proteins only with sulfur. Every amino acid has an amine group, so enzymes contain nitrogen.

**If you missed this:** read "Three more elements" in the [elements of life study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/1-2-elements-life-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · 1.3)

A tripeptide, a disaccharide and one fat (triglyceride) molecule are each broken down completely into their building blocks. How many water molecules are used in total?

- (A) 3
- (B) 5
- (C) 6
- (D) 9

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** Hydrolysis uses one water molecule per covalent link broken. Tripeptide: 3 amino acids, 2 links. Disaccharide: 1 link. Fat: glycerol joined to 3 fatty acids, 3 links. Total 2 + 1 + 3 = 6.

- (A) counts one water per molecule, not per link.
- (B) treats the fat as a chain of three parts (3 − 1 = 2). A fat has **four** parts, glycerol plus three fatty acids, each fatty acid joined to glycerol.
- (D) counts one water per building block (3 + 2 + 4). The n − 1 rule applies to each molecule.

**If you missed this:** read "Counting monomers, bonds and water" in the [introduction to macromolecules study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/1-3-introduction-macromolecules-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · 1.4)

Liver cells store glucose as granules of glycogen rather than as the same amount of free glucose. Which is the best explanation?

- (A) Each glucose unit in glycogen releases much more energy than a free glucose molecule.
- (B) One large polymer behaves very differently from thousands of dissolved glucose molecules, so the store stays in place and does not upset the water balance of the cell.
- (C) Glucose cannot be used for energy until it has been joined into glycogen.
- (D) Glycogen is nonpolar, so it can be stored in droplets like fat.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** Joining glucose into one large, branched polymer packs it into a compact store. It does not act like thousands of free sugar molecules dissolved in the cytoplasm, and enzymes can still release glucose quickly from the many chain ends.

- (A) is tempting because glycogen is an energy store, but its value comes from the glucose it holds, not from extra energy per unit.
- (C) reverses the order: cells use free glucose directly. Glycogen is broken down to glucose when energy is needed.
- (D) confuses carbohydrates with lipids. Glycogen has many polar –OH groups.

**If you missed this:** read "Storage polysaccharides: starch and glycogen" in the [carbohydrates study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/1-4-carbohydrates-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 5 (multiple choice · 1.5)

Which lipid is paired with its correct function?

- (A) Cholesterol: gives structural stability to animal cell membranes
- (B) Phospholipids: the main long-term energy store in fat tissue
- (C) Steroid hormones: form the bilayer of the plasma membrane
- (D) Saturated fats: keep membranes fluid in the cold

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Cholesterol sits among the phospholipids of animal cell membranes and helps them keep their shape and stay intact.

- (B) gives the job of fats (triglycerides) to phospholipids. Phospholipids build membranes.
- (C) swaps two lipids. Phospholipids form the bilayer; steroid hormones carry signals.
- (D) is backwards. Kinked **unsaturated** tails keep membranes fluid in the cold; straight saturated tails pack tightly.

**If you missed this:** read "Structure decides function" in the [lipids study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/1-5-lipids-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 6 (multiple choice · 1.6)

Two double-stranded DNA molecules have the same length and exactly the same percentages of A, T, G and C. Yet they carry different genetic information. Which statement explains this?

- (A) The two molecules contain different sugars.
- (B) The bases are joined to each other in a different order along the strands.
- (C) One molecule has more phosphate groups per nucleotide than the other.
- (D) In one molecule the strands are antiparallel, and in the other they are parallel.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** The sugar–phosphate backbone is the same all along any DNA strand. Information is carried in the **sequence** of bases, and the same set of bases can be arranged in many different orders.

- (A) is wrong: all DNA contains deoxyribose.
- (C) is wrong: every nucleotide has one phosphate group.
- (D) is tempting because direction matters, but double-stranded DNA is always antiparallel.

**If you missed this:** read "Information written in a chemical alphabet" in the [nucleic acids study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/1-6-nucleic-acids-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 7 (multiple choice · 1.7)

A chemical breaks the hydrogen bonds between the C=O and N–H groups of a protein's backbone, but leaves its peptide bonds and R-group interactions unchanged. Which level of structure is most directly disrupted?

- (A) Primary
- (B) Secondary
- (C) Tertiary
- (D) Quaternary

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** α-helices and β-pleated sheets are held by hydrogen bonds between atoms of the **backbone**.

- (A) would need peptide bonds to break, and they are intact.
- (C) is tempting because tertiary structure also uses hydrogen bonds, but those form between R groups.
- (D) concerns how separate chains fit together; nothing in the question mentions more than one chain.

**If you missed this:** read "Four levels of protein structure" in the [proteins study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/1-7-proteins-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 8 (multiple choice · 1.7)

In a single-chain protein dissolved in the cytoplasm, which pair of R groups is most likely to form an ionic interaction, and at which level of structure?

- (A) A positively charged R group and a negatively charged R group; tertiary structure
- (B) Two negatively charged R groups; tertiary structure
- (C) A positively charged R group and a negatively charged R group; secondary structure
- (D) Two nonpolar R groups; primary structure

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Opposite charges attract. R-group interactions, wherever the two R groups sit in the sequence, build the tertiary structure.

- (B) has like charges, which repel.
- (C) has the right pair but the wrong level: secondary structure comes from the backbone, not R groups.
- (D) describes hydrophobic clustering, not an ionic interaction, and primary structure is only the sequence.

**If you missed this:** read "Tertiary structure: the whole chain folds, driven by R groups" in the [proteins study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/1-7-proteins-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 9 (short answer · 1.5)

Fatty acid X has the formula C₁₈H₃₆O₂. Fatty acid Y has the formula C₁₈H₃₂O₂. Glycerol is C₃H₈O₃.

(a) How many C=C double bonds does Y have? Show how you know.
(b) A fat is made from one glycerol and three molecules of Y. How many water molecules are released, and what is the formula of the fat?
(c) Predict whether a fat made only from Y or one made only from X is more likely to be liquid at room temperature. Explain.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**(a)** X is saturated: it has the most hydrogen an 18-carbon chain can hold. Each C=C double bond means two fewer hydrogen atoms. Y has 36 − 32 = 4 fewer, so **2 double bonds**.

**(b)** Each fatty acid joins glycerol by dehydration synthesis, so **3 water molecules** are released. Add the atoms: C 3 + 54 = 57; H 8 + 96 = 104; O 3 + 6 = 9. Remove 3 H₂O (6 H, 3 O): **C₅₇H₉₈O₆**.

**(c)** The fat made from **Y**. Its two double bonds kink each tail, so the tails cannot pack closely. With fewer attractions between tails, the fat melts at a lower temperature.

**Watch for:** "double bonds are weaker, so the fat breaks apart" earns nothing. Melting separates whole molecules; no covalent bonds break.

**If you missed this:** read "Fatty acids: saturated and unsaturated" and "Fats (triglycerides)" in the [lipids study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/1-5-lipids-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 10 (short answer · 1.6)

A student builds a model of a short piece of DNA and describes it like this:

- **Left strand**, read from top to bottom: 5′ end, then bases A, T, G, C, then 3′ end. Sugars: deoxyribose.
- **Right strand**, read from top to bottom: 5′ end, then bases U, A, C, G, then 3′ end. Sugars: ribose.
- Each base pair is joined by **two** hydrogen bonds.

(a) Identify **three** errors in the model and correct each one.
(b) Write the correct sequence of the right strand from its 5′ end to its 3′ end.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**(a)** Any three:

1. **Direction.** Both strands start with 5′ at the top. DNA is antiparallel, so the right strand should run 3′ (top) to 5′ (bottom).
2. **Uracil.** U belongs in RNA. Opposite A, DNA has **T**.
3. **Sugar.** DNA contains **deoxyribose** in both strands, not ribose.
4. **Hydrogen bonds.** A–T pairs have two hydrogen bonds, but C–G pairs have **three**. The model should show 2 + 2 + 3 + 3 = 10, not 8.

**(b)** Read top to bottom, the corrected right strand is 3′-TACG-5′. Read from its 5′ end: **5′-GCAT-3′**.

**Watch for:** writing 5′-TACG-3′. Those are the right partner bases with the ends labelled the wrong way round, which describes a different molecule.

**If you missed this:** read "DNA: an antiparallel double helix" and "DNA and RNA compared" in the [nucleic acids study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/1-6-nucleic-acids-study-guide/).
</details>
