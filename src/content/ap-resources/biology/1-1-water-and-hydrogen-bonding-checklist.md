Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/1-1-water-and-hydrogen-bonding-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/biology/1-1-water-and-hydrogen-bonding-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why the O–H bonds in water are polar and why the bent shape makes the molecule polar but not charged. *(Guide: "Polar covalent bonds")*
- I can draw two water molecules with δ⁺ and δ⁻ labels and show a hydrogen bond between them, distinguishing it from a covalent bond. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain how cohesion and adhesion allow water to be pulled up the xylem, and say what provides the pull. *(Guide: "Cohesion, adhesion and transpiration"; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why ice floats and why this matters to organisms in lakes. *(Guide: "Ice floats"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain how water dissolves ions and polar molecules, and why nonpolar substances are hydrophobic. *(Guide: "Water as a solvent"; Practice Q1)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can use q = mcΔT to find energy or temperature change, with units on every line. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can read a heating graph and use its gradients to compare specific heats. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate a mean and a percentage change from repeated trials. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can calculate the cooling effect of evaporation using a heat of vaporization. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can identify the independent variable, dependent variable, control condition and controlled variables in a water-transport experiment. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why a correlation does not prove cause, and name a likely confounding variable. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can link each property of water to its molecular cause **and** to a biological outcome, such as temperature buffering or evaporative cooling. *(Guide: "From mechanism to biological outcome"; Practice Q6, Q7)*
