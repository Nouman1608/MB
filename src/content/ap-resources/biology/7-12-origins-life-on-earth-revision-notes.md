## Recap

- **Geological evidence** (rock ages, signs of early conditions and fossils) supports models of when life began.
- Earth formed about **4.6 bya**. Heat and frequent large impacts kept it **too hostile for life until about 3.9 bya**. The **earliest fossil evidence** of life, such as stromatolites (layered mounds built by microbial mats), is about **3.5 bya**.
- The hostile period sets the earliest plausible date; fossils set a minimum age. Together they give a plausible range.
- The **RNA world hypothesis** proposes that RNA was the earliest genetic material. RNA stores information in its base sequence, is copied by base pairing, and can fold into catalysts (**ribozymes**).
- Later, DNA (more stable) took over information storage and proteins (more varied shapes) took over most catalysis.

## Key relationships

| Evidence or idea | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Earth's age, 4.6 bya | oldest possible date: nothing could live on Earth earlier |
| Hostile conditions until about 3.9 bya | life could not start and survive before then |
| Earliest fossils, 3.5 bya | life existed by then (a minimum age) |
| Ribozymes, including the ribosome's core | RNA can be a catalyst, so proteins are not essential for catalysis |
| Lab RNAs that copy templates | RNA-catalysed, base-pairing replication is chemically possible |

| Assumption of the RNA world | In one line |
|---|---|
| 1 | Genetic continuity came from copying RNA |
| 2 | Copying depended on complementary base pairing (A–U, G–C) |
| 3 | Catalysts were not proteins made from genes |

## Assumptions behind the reasoning

- The dates are approximate, and the course values (4.6, 3.9, 3.5 bya) are the ones to use.
- Experiments in modern laboratories show what is **possible**, not what **did** happen.
- A null hypothesis always states no effect or no difference.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Treating the oldest fossil date as the moment life began.
2. Saying DNA was the first genetic material.
3. Writing "proteins catalysed early RNA replication": that contradicts assumption 3.
4. Stating the expected result as the null hypothesis.
5. Calling the RNA world hypothesis proven.

## Quick self-check

1. How long was the plausible window? *(3.9 − 3.5 = 0.4 billion years = 400 million years.)*
2. Why can RNA, but not DNA alone, explain the first genetic system? *(RNA can both carry information and act as a catalyst; DNA needs protein enzymes to be copied.)*
3. Null hypothesis for "Does RNA X speed up copying?" *(RNA X has no effect on the rate of copying.)*
