## Understanding

- I can name four kinds of cellular response to a signal and give an example of each. *(Guide: "Four kinds of cellular response")*
- I can tell a response that changes existing proteins from one that needs new gene expression, and say which is faster. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain how a transcription factor at the end of a pathway can change a cell's phenotype. *(Guide: "Changing gene expression", "Changing phenotype")*
- I can describe apoptosis and explain how it shapes a developing organism. *(Guide: "Apoptosis"; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain how quorum sensing lets a bacterial population switch genes on together. *(Guide: "Changing phenotype"; Worked example 1)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can describe a switch-like trend in data and quote a fold change. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
- I can calculate a percentage change and the fraction of a response that a drug removes. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can use doubling time to find when a growing population reaches a threshold. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict the effect of a mutation in the ligand-binding domain, the intracellular domain or a later component, as loss or gain of function. *(Guide: "When part of the pathway changes"; Practice Q3)*
- I can predict whether a chemical acting on a named component will activate or inhibit the response. *(Guide: "When chemicals interfere"; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can use results from mutants, inhibitors and bypassing compounds to justify a claim about the order of pathway components. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain competition between a signal and a blocking molecule for the same binding site. *(Practice Q6)*
