## Recap

- **Monosaccharides** (simple sugars) are the monomers. Glucose, fructose and galactose are all C₆H₁₂O₆ but with different arrangements of atoms. Ribose and deoxyribose are five-carbon sugars in RNA and DNA.
- Two monosaccharides join by **dehydration synthesis** (one H₂O removed) to make a **disaccharide**. The link is a **covalent bond**.
- Many monosaccharides joined in this way make a **polysaccharide** (complex carbohydrate). It can be **linear** or **branched**.
- **Hydrolysis** breaks each link using one H₂O. Digestion of starch is hydrolysis.
- Starch, glycogen and cellulose are all polymers of glucose. They differ in branching and in how the units are joined, and so in function.

## Key relationships

| Polymer | Where | Shape | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starch | plants (seeds, tubers) | mix of linear and branched chains | energy storage |
| Glycogen | animals (liver, muscle), fungi | highly branched | energy storage, fast release |
| Cellulose | plant cell walls | linear, chains hydrogen-bonded side by side | structural support |

| Disaccharide | Monomers |
|---|---|
| Maltose | glucose + glucose |
| Sucrose | glucose + fructose |
| Lactose | glucose + galactose |

| Counting rule | Example |
|---|---|
| n monomers → n − 1 links → n − 1 waters | 10 glucose → 9 waters; C₆₀H₁₀₂O₅₁ |
| molar mass = n × 180.16 − (n − 1) × 18.02 (g mol⁻¹) | maltose: 2 × 180.16 − 18.02 = 342.3 g mol⁻¹ |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The counting rule assumes every link joins exactly two monomers and releases one water. It holds for linear and branched chains alike.
- Branching models (rate = number of free ends × rate per end) assume every end is equally reachable. Real molecules release glucose more slowly than such a model predicts.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying dehydration synthesis *adds* water.
2. Counting n waters instead of n − 1.
3. Calling the links in a chain hydrogen bonds; they are covalent. Hydrogen bonds join neighbouring cellulose chains.
4. Saying cellulose is not made of glucose.
5. Saying branching adds monomers; it adds chain ends.
6. Trying to draw detailed structures of starch or cellulose: not required.

## Quick self-check

1. Name the monomer of glycogen and its formula. *(Glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆)*
2. How many water molecules are released in making a chain of 30 glucose units? *(29)*
3. Why does glycogen release glucose faster than an unbranched chain of the same size? *(More branches give more chain ends, and enzymes remove glucose from many ends at once.)*
4. Why does cellulose form strong fibres? *(Straight, unbranched chains lie side by side and form many hydrogen bonds between chains.)*
