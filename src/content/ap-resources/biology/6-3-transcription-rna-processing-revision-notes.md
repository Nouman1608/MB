## Recap

- Information flows **DNA → RNA → protein**. Transcription makes the RNA; translation (Topic 6.4) makes the protein.
- What an RNA does depends on its **base sequence** and on the **shape** it folds into by pairing with itself.
- **RNA polymerase** reads **one** strand, the **template strand**, and builds a complementary RNA. No primer is needed.
- In eukaryotes the first transcript (**pre-mRNA**) is processed in the nucleus before export. Prokaryotic mRNA is generally used without this processing.

## Key relationships

| RNA | Job |
|---|---|
| mRNA | carries a gene's information from DNA in the nucleus to a ribosome in the cytoplasm |
| tRNA | carries a specific amino acid; its **anticodon** pairs with an mRNA **codon** |
| rRNA | structural and working part of the ribosome, with proteins |

| Transcription rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Template read | 3′ → 5′ |
| RNA built | 5′ → 3′ (nucleotides added to the 3′ end) |
| Pairing | template A → U, T → A, G → C, C → G |
| RNA sequence | same as the **coding** strand, with U for T |

| Processing step | Where on the mRNA | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Cap (modified G, "GTP cap") | 5′ end | helps the ribosome recognise the mRNA |
| Poly-A tail | 3′ end | makes the mRNA more stable |
| Splicing | introns removed, exons joined | gives the mature mRNA; **alternative splicing** gives several versions |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Lengths of mature mRNA are sums of the exons kept; cap and tail are left out unless the question includes them.
- Data on mRNA stability compare versions that differ in **one** feature only (for example, tail or no tail).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Writing the mRNA as identical to the template strand.
2. Forgetting to reverse a template written 5′ → 3′, so the ends are labelled the wrong way.
3. Putting T in an RNA sequence.
4. Saying introns are removed from the DNA.
5. Swapping the jobs of the cap (recognition) and the tail (stability).
6. Saying RNA polymerase needs a primer.

## Quick self-check

1. Template 3′-TACGTT-5′. mRNA? *(5′-AUGCAA-3′)*
2. Which strand does the mRNA resemble? *(The coding strand, with U instead of T.)*
3. A gene has three exons of 200, 150 and 90 nt. Mature mRNA length if all are kept, and if the middle exon is skipped? *(440 nt; 290 nt)*
4. An mRNA lacks its poly-A tail. Prediction? *(It is broken down sooner, so less protein is made from it.)*
