Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/7-4-population-genetics-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/biology/7-4-population-genetics-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why a change in allele frequencies across generations is evidence that a population has evolved. *(Guide: "What 'evolution' means in a population")*
- I can explain why mutation is random, why it is the source of new alleles, and why on its own it changes frequencies slowly. *(Guide: "Mutation"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain genetic drift as chance sampling, and why it is stronger in small populations. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can distinguish the bottleneck effect from the founder effect and give an example of each. *(Guide: Figure 2, "The founder effect"; Practice Q5, Q6)*
- I can explain how gene flow adds or removes alleles and why it stops connected populations diverging into separate species. *(Guide: "Gene flow"; Practice Q3)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can calculate allele frequencies from genotype counts, doubling homozygotes, and check that they add to 1. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can calculate the new allele frequency when migrants join a population. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can describe replicate results using ranges and counts of fixed or lost alleles. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate percentage changes in hatching success or allele numbers from a table. *(Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can state a null hypothesis and predict results for an investigation of drift or selection. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can explain how a bottleneck lowers variation and can make harmful recessive alleles more common, and how gene flow can rescue a population. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can explain how drift lets a small population diverge from others of the same species. *(Guide: "Genetic drift"; Practice Q3)*
