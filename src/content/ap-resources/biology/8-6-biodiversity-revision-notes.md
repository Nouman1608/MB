## Recap

- **Biodiversity** works at three levels: genetic, species and ecosystem.
- **Resistance**: how little an ecosystem changes when disturbed. **Resilience**: how fast and how fully it recovers.
- Natural and artificial ecosystems with **few parts** and **little variety among the parts** are often less resilient (a one-variety crop field is the classic artificial case).
- Diversity gives **back-up species** (functional redundancy), **alternative food-web pathways** and a **variety of responses** to stress.
- **Producers**, **keystone species** and **essential abiotic and biotic factors** maintain an ecosystem's diversity.
- A **keystone species** has a **disproportionate** effect relative to its abundance, usually by controlling a dominant competitor or grazer. Examples: the ochre sea star on rocky shores; sea otters in kelp forests.
- Adding or removing any component changes structure in the **short term** (direct partners) and the **long term** (whole community).

## Key relationships

| Change | Short-term effect | Long-term effect |
|---|---|---|
| Keystone predator removed | its prey (often a dominant competitor) increases | dominant species spreads; many others lost; community simplifies |
| Producer lost | herbivores and species that shelter in it decline | habitat and food web collapse; species that depended on them leave or die |
| Essential abiotic factor changed (e.g. water, temperature) | sensitive species decline | community composition shifts to tolerant species |
| Species added to a low-diversity system | depends on its interactions | may fill a gap and add resilience, or out-compete natives (see Topic 8.7) |

| Equation | Use |
|---|---|
| **Percentage change = (new − original) ÷ original × 100** | size of a fall (resistance) |
| **Recovery = after ÷ before × 100** | how completely an ecosystem returns (resilience) |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Comparisons of resilience assume the ecosystems faced the same disturbance and were measured the same way.
- A single disturbance with no replicates can **support** a pattern; it cannot prove it.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Calling the most abundant species the keystone species.
2. Confusing resilience (recovery) with resistance (how little it changes).
3. Giving only the short-term effect when a question asks for the long term.
4. Forgetting abiotic factors when asked what maintains diversity.
5. Saying diverse ecosystems cannot be damaged.

## Quick self-check

1. Biomass falls from 300 to 240 g m⁻² in a drought. Percentage fall? *(60 ÷ 300 × 100 = 20%)*
2. Why does removing a keystone predator reduce diversity? *(Its prey, often a dominant competitor, is released and crowds out other species.)*
3. Why is a single-variety crop field fragile? *(Every plant responds the same way, so one pest or disease can affect them all.)*
