## Understanding

- I can define evolution in terms of heritable change in a population over generations, and explain why individuals do not evolve. *(Guide: "What evolution is"; Practice Q5)*
- I can state that natural selection is a major mechanism of evolution, and name another kind of process that also changes populations. *(Guide: "What evolution is")*
- I can set out Darwin's argument in order: overproduction, competition for limited resources, variation, inheritance, differential survival and reproduction. *(Guide: "Darwin's argument, step by step"; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can explain why fitness is measured by reproductive success and not by strength or lifespan. *(Guide: "Fitness means reproductive success"; Practice Q2)*
- I can give examples of biotic and abiotic factors and explain how a change in them can reverse the direction of selection. *(Guide: "Changing environments change the direction of selection"; Practice Q4)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can calculate mean offspring per individual and relative fitness for each phenotype. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can project the fraction of a phenotype in the next generation, and state the assumptions behind the projection. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6, Q7)*
- I can calculate percentage survival and a mean from a frequency table. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can describe a flowchart, histogram or line graph by naming its axes, groups, units and trend. *(Guide: Figure 1, Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can evaluate a claim that confuses survival with fitness, using data. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why a difference in survival between two groups is not enough on its own to show that a trait will spread. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain how the size of a fitness difference affects the rate of change, and how a changing environment affects its direction. *(Practice Q7)*
