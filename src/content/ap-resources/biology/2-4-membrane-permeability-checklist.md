## Understanding

- I can explain why a membrane is selectively permeable in terms of its hydrophobic interior. *(Guide: "Why the hydrophobic interior decides"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why ions are blocked by their charge, not their size. *(Guide: "Why the hydrophobic interior decides"; Practice Q2, Q7)*
- I can sort substances into those that cross freely (N₂, O₂, CO₂), those that cross in small amounts (H₂O, NH₃) and those that need channel or transport proteins. *(Guide: Figure 1 and table; Practice Q1)*
- I can name the main wall material of bacteria, fungi and plants, and say that archaea walls lack peptidoglycan. *(Guide: "Cell walls")*
- I can describe the three roles of a cell wall: structure, a barrier to some substances, and protection from osmotic lysis. *(Guide: "Cell walls"; Practice Q3, Q6)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can calculate a mean, standard deviation and standard error (SE = s ÷ √n) from repeats. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can draw or read error bars of mean ± 2 SE and decide whether to reject or fail to reject a null hypothesis. *(Guide: "Testing a hypothesis"; Figure 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can compare rates given in powers of ten. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict whether a new substance will cross a bilayer from its size, polarity and charge, and propose how to test it. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why a plant cell becomes turgid but an animal cell bursts in pure water. *(Guide: "Cell walls"; Practice Q3)*
- I can use a control treatment to rule out an alternative explanation, such as direct damage instead of osmotic lysis. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can evaluate a claim that goes beyond the data, such as confusing the wall's role with the membrane's. *(Practice Q6)*
