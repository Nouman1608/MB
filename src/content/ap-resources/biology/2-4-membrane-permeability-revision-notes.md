## Recap

- The plasma membrane separates the cell's internal environment from the outside. It is **selectively permeable** because its interior is **hydrophobic**.
- The nonpolar hydrocarbon tails block **ions** and **polar molecules**. An ion would have to leave its water shell to enter the tails.
- **Small nonpolar** molecules (N₂, O₂, CO₂) cross freely. **Small polar, uncharged** molecules (H₂O, NH₃) cross in small amounts.
- **Hydrophilic** substances, such as ions and large polar molecules (glucose), cross through **embedded channel and transport proteins**.
- **Cell walls** (Bacteria, Archaea, Fungi, plants) are outside the membrane. They are a structural boundary, a barrier to some substances and protection from **osmotic lysis**. Animal cells have no wall.

## Key relationships

| Substance | Crosses the bilayer? | Route |
|---|---|---|
| N₂, O₂, CO₂ | freely | through the tails |
| H₂O, NH₃ | small amounts | through the tails |
| glucose and other large polar molecules | hardly | transport proteins |
| Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, H⁺ | no | channel and transport proteins |

| Group | Wall material |
|---|---|
| Bacteria | peptidoglycan |
| Archaea | varied, not peptidoglycan |
| Fungi | chitin |
| Plants | cellulose |

| Statistics | Rule |
|---|---|
| **SE = s ÷ √n** | s = sample standard deviation, n = number of values |
| **mean ± 2 SE** | roughly a 95% confidence interval |
| bars do not overlap | reject H₀ (no difference) |
| bars overlap | fail to reject H₀ |

## Assumptions behind the conclusions

- Artificial vesicles are a **model**; real membranes contain many proteins and may offer several routes.
- The ± 2 SE rule is an approximation. It works best with several repeats.
- Data **support** a hypothesis or lead you to **reject** or **fail to reject** H₀; they do not prove anything.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying ions are blocked by size. Their **charge** blocks them.
2. Saying water cannot cross a bilayer at all.
3. Saying the cell wall chooses what enters; the membrane does the fine selecting.
4. Saying the wall stops water entering; it stops the cell **expanding** and bursting.
5. Dividing s by n instead of √n for the standard error.
6. Writing "accept the null hypothesis" or "prove the hypothesis".

## Quick self-check

1. Why does CO₂ cross a pure bilayer but Cl⁻ does not? *(CO₂ is small and nonpolar, so it passes through the tails; Cl⁻ is charged and held in its water shell.)*
2. Values 10, 12 and 14: what is the SE? *(Mean 12, s = 2, SE = 2 ÷ √3 = 1.15; interval 12 ± 2.3.)*
3. Why does a plant cell not burst in pure water? *(The wall resists expansion; turgor pressure builds until no more water enters.)*
