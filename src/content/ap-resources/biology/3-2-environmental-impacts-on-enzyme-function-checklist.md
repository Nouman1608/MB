Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/3-2-environmental-impacts-on-enzyme-function-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/biology/3-2-environmental-impacts-on-enzyme-function-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what denaturation is and why it stops an enzyme catalysing, without saying peptide bonds break. *(Guide: "Denaturation: losing the working shape"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain both parts of a temperature–rate curve: more collisions below the optimum, broken hydrogen bonds above it. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain how a change in pH alters R-group charges, the fold and the active site. *(Guide: "pH: changing the charges"; Practice Q4)*
- I can state when denaturation may be reversible and why cold usually does not denature an enzyme. *(Guide: "Can denaturation be reversed?")*
- I can describe competitive and noncompetitive inhibitors, including where each binds. *(Guide: Figure 4; Practice Q3)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can calculate a percentage change in rate, dividing by the starting value. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can calculate an inhibited rate as a percentage of the uninhibited rate at different substrate concentrations. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can estimate an optimum from data and suggest how to improve the estimate. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate the change in rate per unit of substrate and use it to show saturation. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can use a reversibility test to decide whether a loss of activity is permanent. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain how the relative concentrations of substrate and product affect the net rate, and predict the effect of blocking the next enzyme in a pathway. *(Guide: "Substrate and product concentrations"; Practice Q5)*
- I can identify an inhibitor as competitive or noncompetitive from data, and design an experiment to tell them apart. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can predict the effect of more enzyme or a noncompetitive inhibitor on the maximum rate. *(Practice Q7)*
