## Recap

- A molecule's **structure** (size, polarity, charge) decides whether it can cross the hydrophobic core of the bilayer. Charge and polarity matter more than size.
- **Facilitated diffusion** moves ions and large polar molecules through **transport proteins**, **down** their concentration gradient, with **no energy input**.
- **Channel proteins** form a hydrophilic pore; they are fast, specific and often gated. **Carrier proteins** bind a specific molecule and change shape; they are slower.
- **Na⁺ and K⁺** need channel proteins. Because ions carry charge, their movement can make one side of the membrane more negative: the membrane becomes **polarized**.
- **Aquaporins** are channels that move **large quantities of water** quickly. Without them, water crosses the bilayer only slowly.

## Key relationships

| Substance | Route | Example |
|---|---|---|
| small nonpolar | simple diffusion through the bilayer | O₂, CO₂ |
| ions | channel proteins | K⁺ leaving through K⁺ channels |
| large polar | carrier proteins | glucose into red blood cells |
| water in bulk | aquaporins | kidney collecting ducts, root cells |

| Data pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|
| rate ∝ concentration (straight line) | simple diffusion; no proteins to saturate |
| rate levels off at high concentration | carriers or channels saturated: facilitated diffusion (or active transport) |
| uptake not affected by blocking ATP | passive: simple or facilitated diffusion |
| more transport proteins | higher maximum rate; direction unchanged |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- At saturation, the rate is assumed proportional to the number of transport proteins.
- When a protein route is blocked, the remaining rate is assumed to come from the bilayer route.
- Concentration comparisons ignore charge; for ions, the charge across the membrane also matters.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying facilitated diffusion uses ATP because a protein is involved.
2. Saying channels "pump" ions.
3. Explaining ion exclusion by size instead of charge.
4. Saying water can only cross through aquaporins.
5. Expecting facilitated diffusion to keep speeding up as concentration rises.
6. Doubling the **whole** rate when only the protein route is doubled.

## Quick self-check

1. Why does K⁺ leaving a cell make the inside negative? *(K⁺ carries positive charge out; the negative ions it balanced cannot follow.)*
2. Concentration doubles from 20 to 40 units; rate rises from 3.0 to 3.3. Percentage rise and meaning? *((3.3 − 3.0) ÷ 3.0 × 100 = 10%; the carriers are close to saturation.)*
3. Is water movement through aquaporins active? *(No. It is passive; aquaporins only speed it up.)*
