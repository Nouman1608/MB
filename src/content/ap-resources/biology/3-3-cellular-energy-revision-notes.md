## Recap

- **All living systems need a continuous energy input**, from light (photosynthesis) or from food molecules (respiration).
- Life is **highly ordered**. Keeping that order costs energy all the time.
- **First law:** energy is converted, never created or destroyed. **Second law:** every conversion releases some heat, so total disorder (entropy) of organism plus surroundings rises.
- Organisms are **open systems**: they gain order by releasing heat, CO₂ and H₂O, which disorder the surroundings more.
- **Energy coupling:** energy-releasing (exergonic) processes drive energy-requiring (endergonic) ones, usually through ATP + H₂O → ADP + Pᵢ.
- **Pathways are sequential:** the product of one step is the reactant of the next, so energy comes out in small, controllable amounts.
- **Glycolysis and ATP synthesis by a membrane ATP synthase** are found in Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya: evidence of common ancestry.
- The Gibbs free energy equation is not needed.

## Key relationships

| Idea | What to write |
|---|---|
| Staying alive | energy input > energy loss; a large, lasting deficit → loss of order → death |
| Coupling test | overall change = energy required (+) + energy released (−); it must come out negative |
| ATP hydrolysis | about −30 kJ mol⁻¹ in standard lab conditions (more in cells) |
| Number of ATP | smallest whole number whose total release exceeds the energy required |
| Energy budget | intake = losses (faeces, heat) + energy stored |
| Blocked pathway step | substrate of that step builds up; later products fall |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Energy values are per mole of reaction and use the rounded 30 kJ mol⁻¹ for ATP unless a question gives another value.
- An energy budget assumes every route of energy loss has been measured.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying organisms "make" or "create" energy.
2. Saying living things break the second law.
3. Saying energy is released when a bond in ATP breaks; the whole hydrolysis reaction releases it.
4. Using a fraction of an ATP when counting how many are needed.
5. Saying stepwise pathways release *more* energy than burning; the total is the same.
6. Explaining shared pathways only by "all cells need energy" instead of common ancestry.

## Quick self-check

1. A reaction requires +12 kJ mol⁻¹. What is the overall change when coupled to one ATP? *(+12 − 30 = −18 kJ mol⁻¹, so it can proceed.)*
2. A reaction requires +75 kJ mol⁻¹. How many ATP, at minimum? *(Three: 2 × 30 = 60 is too little; 3 × 30 = 90 gives an overall −15 kJ mol⁻¹.)*
3. How can a seedling become more ordered without breaking the second law? *(It releases heat and simple molecules that increase the disorder of its surroundings by more than its own order increases.)*
