Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/6-8-biotechnology-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/biology/6-8-biotechnology-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why DNA moves toward the positive electrode and why short fragments travel farthest in a gel. *(Guide: "Gel electrophoresis"; Practice Q1)*
- I can describe the three steps of a PCR cycle and the purpose of the primers and the heat-stable polymerase. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q2, Q4)*
- I can explain how a plasmid with a resistance marker lets you select transformed bacteria, and what gene cloning means. *(Guide: "Bacterial transformation and gene cloning"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain what DNA sequencing tells you and how DNA fingerprints are used in forensics, kinship and species identification. *(Guide: "DNA sequencing and DNA fingerprints"; Practice Q5)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can estimate fragment sizes from a ladder, remembering the scale is roughly logarithmic. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can use N = N₀ × 2ⁿ to find copy numbers and the minimum number of cycles, rounding up. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate transformation efficiency using only the DNA actually spread. *(Practice Q3)*
- I can calculate percentage identity between DNA sequences. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain the purpose of every control lane or control plate in a biotechnology experiment. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can use band patterns to exclude or fail to exclude an individual, and explain why a match supports but does not prove identity. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can connect sequence differences to common ancestry and state a limitation of the data. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can predict how a mutation in a primer-binding site would affect a PCR result. *(Practice Q4)*
