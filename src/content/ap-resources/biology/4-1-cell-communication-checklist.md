Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/4-1-cell-communication-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/biology/4-1-cell-communication-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can name the parts of any act of cell communication: signalling cell, signal, target cell with receptor, and response. *(Guide: "Why cells need to communicate")*
- I can describe the two kinds of direct contact, junctions and surface molecules, with an example of each. *(Guide: "Communication by direct contact"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain how an antigen-presenting cell activates a helper T cell, and how a killer T cell recognises an infected cell. *(Guide: "Surface molecules that touch"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why a local regulator affects only nearby cells, and why neurotransmitter signalling is short-distance even when the neuron is long. *(Guide: "Neurotransmitters"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain how a morphogen gradient gives cells different fates depending on where they sit. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
- I can describe quorum sensing and explain why bacteria benefit from acting only at high density. *(Guide: "Quorum sensing in bacteria"; Practice Q5)*
- I can name hormones that travel long distances in the blood, with where each is made and what it does. *(Guide: "Communication over long distances")*
- I can explain why a hormone reaches nearly every cell but only target cells with a matching receptor respond. *(Guide: "Specificity comes from receptors"; Practice Q2, Q6)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can use a halving model to find signal concentrations and the positions of fate boundaries. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
- I can calculate means, light per cell and percentage change from experimental data. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can use t = x² ÷ (2D) to compare diffusion times over different distances, with distances in metres. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict how a change in signal production, or a missing receptor, changes which cells respond. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5, Q6)*
- I can judge what an experiment shows about a signal, identify its control and suggest a further control. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can use data to reject a claim that confuses delivery of a signal with response to it. *(Practice Q6)*
