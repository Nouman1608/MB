## Recap

- Lipids are mostly **carbon and hydrogen**. C–H bonds are **nonpolar**, so most lipids are **hydrophobic**. They are grouped by this property, not by a shared monomer.
- **Saturated** fatty acid: only C–C single bonds, straight tail. **Unsaturated**: at least one C=C double bond, which **kinks** the tail. More double bonds = more unsaturated.
- **Fat (triglyceride)** = glycerol + 3 fatty acids, joined by dehydration synthesis (3 water released). Hydrolysis reverses it.
- **Phospholipid** = glycerol + 2 fatty acids + a phosphate group. Hydrophilic head, hydrophobic tails (**amphipathic**). In water they form a **bilayer**, heads out, tails in.
- **Steroids** have four fused carbon rings. Steroid hormones control growth and development, energy metabolism and homeostasis. **Cholesterol** gives animal cell membranes structural stability.
- You need the general shapes only. The exact molecular structure of specific lipids is not tested.

## Key relationships

| Structure | Consequence | Biological outcome |
|---|---|---|
| straight saturated tails | pack tightly, many attractions between tails | solid at room temperature (butter, lard) |
| kinked unsaturated tails | pack loosely, fewer attractions | liquid at room temperature (most plant and fish oils); fluid membranes in the cold |
| many C–H bonds, little oxygen | about 37 kJ g⁻¹, against about 17 kJ g⁻¹ for carbohydrate | light, compact energy store |
| fat layer conducts heat poorly | slows heat loss | blubber insulates whales and seals |
| hydrophilic head + hydrophobic tails | heads face water, tails hide inside | bilayer of plasma and cell membranes |
| four fused rings, hydrophobic | small molecule that suits a signal or a membrane | steroid hormones as signals; cholesterol stabilises animal membranes |

| Equation | Use |
|---|---|
| **mass = energy ÷ energy per gram** | compare fat and carbohydrate stores |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- 37 and 17 kJ g⁻¹ are **average** values for complete respiration; individual fats and sugars differ a little.
- Comparisons of stored mass ignore any water stored with the fuel.
- "Solid" or "liquid" depends on the temperature you choose; compare lipids at the **same** temperature.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Calling lipids polymers.
2. Saying unsaturated tails have weaker *covalent* bonds; it is the packing that changes.
3. Drawing phospholipid heads pointing into the bilayer.
4. Saying cholesterol is in all cell membranes; this course links it to **animal** cells.
5. Forgetting that steroids are lipids.
6. Predicting an effect without the mechanism: always link structure → packing or polarity → function.

## Quick self-check

1. Why does adding double bonds lower the melting point of a fat? *(Each double bond kinks the tail, so tails pack loosely, with fewer attractions between them to overcome.)*
2. Mass of fat that stores 740 kJ? And of carbohydrate? *(740 ÷ 37 = 20 g of fat; 740 ÷ 17 = 43.5 g, about 44 g, of carbohydrate.)*
3. Why do phospholipids, but not fats, form bilayers? *(Phospholipids have a hydrophilic head and hydrophobic tails; fats are hydrophobic all over, so they clump into droplets instead.)*
4. Predict what happens to an animal cell's membrane if its cholesterol is removed. *(It loses structural stability: it is more easily damaged and less able to keep its shape as conditions change.)*
