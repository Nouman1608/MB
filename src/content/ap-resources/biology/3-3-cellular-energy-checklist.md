## Understanding

- I can explain why every living system needs a continuous input of energy, from light or from food. *(Guide: "Why every living thing needs a supply of energy")*
- I can state the first and second laws of thermodynamics and explain why a growing organism does not break them. *(Guide: "Order, energy and the laws of thermodynamics"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why energy input must exceed energy loss, and what happens when it does not for a long time. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can describe how ATP hydrolysis couples an energy-releasing process to an energy-requiring one. *(Guide: "Energy coupling and ATP", Figure 1)*
- I can explain why energy pathways run in steps in which each product is the next reactant. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can name core pathways shared by all three domains. *(Guide: "Shared pathways and common ancestry")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can add energy values with their signs to decide whether a coupled reaction can proceed. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7)*
- I can find the smallest whole number of ATP needed to drive a reaction. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can balance an energy budget and calculate percentages, deficits and how long a reserve lasts. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can use concentration data to find which step of a pathway has been blocked. *(Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can build a claim, evidence and reasoning argument that shared pathways support common ancestry, and rebut "all cells just need energy". *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain where the energy released by a pathway goes, using both laws. *(Practice Q7)*
