## Recap

- The plasma membrane is **selectively permeable**, so a cell can keep different solute concentrations inside and outside: **concentration gradients**. This is how cells and organisms keep their solute and water balance.
- **Diffusion** is net movement from high to low concentration caused by random motion. At equal concentrations, movement continues both ways but there is **no net movement** (dynamic equilibrium).
- **Passive transport** (simple diffusion, facilitated diffusion, osmosis) moves substances down their gradient with **no direct metabolic energy**.
- **Active transport** needs a **direct input of energy** (usually ATP) and a specific membrane protein. It can move substances from **low to high** concentration.
- **Endocytosis** takes material in by folding the membrane inward to form a vesicle. **Exocytosis** releases material when a vesicle fuses with the membrane. Both need energy and move large molecules, particles or large amounts.

## Key relationships

| | Passive | Active | Endocytosis / exocytosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direction | down the gradient only | can go against it | into / out of the cell |
| ATP used directly? | no | yes | yes |
| Protein needed? | only for facilitated diffusion | yes (pump) | cargo travels in vesicles |
| Example | O₂ into a respiring cell | mineral ions into root hairs | phagocytosis of bacteria; insulin release |

| Calculation | How |
|---|---|
| Inside : outside ratio | inside concentration ÷ outside concentration; above 1 for a substance still entering suggests active uptake (uncharged solutes) |
| Rate | change in concentration ÷ time |
| Percentage change | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Comparing concentrations alone works for **uncharged** solutes. For ions, the charge across the membrane also matters (Topic 2.8).
- A large volume of outside solution is assumed to keep the outside concentration constant.
- Inhibitor results assume the inhibitor only blocks ATP production and does not damage the membrane.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying diffusion **stops** at equilibrium; only **net** movement stops.
2. Saying any process with a protein uses energy; facilitated diffusion is passive.
3. Defining active transport as "against the gradient"; the definition is direct energy input.
4. Saying vesicle cargo passes through the bilayer.
5. Treating a change of temperature as a clean test of ATP dependence.
6. Claiming active transport from evidence that a substance merely **entered** a cell.

## Quick self-check

1. Inside 6.0, outside 1.5 mmol L⁻¹ for an uncharged solute that is still entering. Ratio and conclusion? *(6.0 ÷ 1.5 = 4.0; above 1, so active transport is needed.)*
2. An inhibitor cuts uptake from 4.2 to 1.4 units. Percentage change? *((1.4 − 4.2) ÷ 4.2 × 100 ≈ −67%, a fall of about two-thirds.)*
3. Why does a white blood cell need ATP to engulf a bacterium? *(Endocytosis uses energy to fold the membrane, form the vesicle and move it.)*
