## Recap

- **Checkpoints** are internal controls that let a cell continue only when conditions are right.
- **Cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs)** are enzymes that phosphorylate target proteins. Their amount stays about constant, but they are **active only when bound to a cyclin**.
- **Cyclins** are made steadily, then destroyed quickly, so their concentration rises and falls each cycle.
- An active **cyclin-CDK complex** phosphorylates targets that drive the next stage; destroying the cyclin switches the CDK off.
- **Apoptosis** (programmed cell death) is orderly self-destruction. It removes damaged or unneeded cells and shapes organs in development.
- **Cancer**: mutations make "drive" proteins overactive or "stop" proteins fail, so cells divide with damaged DNA and avoid apoptosis. Usually several mutations are needed.
- Not required: specific cyclin-CDK pairs or specific growth factors.

## Key relationships

| Checkpoint | Checks | If not passed |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | cell size, nutrients, signals, DNA damage | pause and repair, G0, or apoptosis |
| G2 | DNA fully replicated and undamaged | pause until fixed, or apoptosis |
| M (spindle) | every chromosome attached to both poles | anaphase delayed |

| Change | Chain of effects |
|---|---|
| Cyclin not made | CDK stays inactive → targets not phosphorylated → cell stops at that checkpoint |
| Cyclin not broken down | CDK stays active → cell cannot leave that stage (for a mitotic cyclin, stuck in mitosis) |
| Damage-checkpoint protein lost | damaged cells keep dividing, little apoptosis → mutations build up → cancer risk |
| Too much apoptosis | healthy cells lost → tissue shrinks or fails |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Concentrations in model graphs are in arbitrary units (au); compare them only with each other.
- Cell-culture results show what can happen; a whole body has extra controls (neighbouring cells, the immune system).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Calling cyclins enzymes (CDKs are the kinases).
2. Saying CDK **amount** rises and falls (CDK **activity** does).
3. Saying a failed checkpoint always kills the cell.
4. Describing apoptosis as harmful in every case.
5. Saying one mutation causes cancer.
6. Skipping links in a prediction: write cyclin → CDK activity → phosphorylation → progress.

## Quick self-check

1. Why does CDK activity peak when cyclin peaks? *(CDK needs bound cyclin to be active; more cyclin means more active complexes.)*
2. UV raises apoptosis in a culture from 4% to 30%. Percentage increase? *((30 − 4) ÷ 4 × 100 = 650%)*
3. Which checkpoint stops a cell entering anaphase with an unattached chromosome? *(The M, or spindle, checkpoint.)*
