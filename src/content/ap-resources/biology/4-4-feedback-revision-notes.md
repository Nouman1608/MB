## Recap

- **Homeostasis** keeps internal conditions near a **set point**. It is dynamic: the value moves and is pulled back.
- A loop has a stimulus, sensor, control centre, effector and response. It is **feedback** because the response acts on the stimulus.
- **Negative feedback** reduces the original change. It works at molecular level (end-product inhibition of the first enzyme in a pathway), cellular level and organism level (blood glucose, body temperature).
- **Positive feedback** amplifies the original change: childbirth (oxytocin), lactation (suckling → oxytocin and prolactin), fruit ripening (ethylene → more ethylene), blood clotting.

## Key relationships

| Variable | Sensor / control | Signal | Effector response | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| glucose rises | pancreas β cells | insulin | cells take up glucose; liver makes glycogen | negative |
| glucose falls | pancreas α cells | glucagon | liver breaks down glycogen, releases glucose | negative |
| end product D builds up | first enzyme in the pathway | D binds allosteric site | enzyme slows, less D made | negative |
| cervix stretched | stretch receptors, brain | oxytocin | stronger uterine contractions, more stretch | positive |
| fruit ripens | fruit cells | ethylene | more ripening, more ethylene | positive |

| Disruption | Effect on blood glucose | Effect on insulin |
|---|---|---|
| β cells destroyed (type 1) | high | very low |
| target cells resist insulin (type 2) | high | normal or high |

## Assumptions behind the reasoning

- A loop works only if every link works; a break anywhere stops the correction.
- If the variable never returns, the stimulus stays, so the control centre usually keeps signalling (often more strongly).
- Simple models (halving a difference each round) show the shape of a response, not real values.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying "negative" means harmful.
2. Saying positive feedback keeps a variable stable.
3. Mixing up glucagon (hormone) and glycogen (storage carbohydrate).
4. Saying type 2 diabetes always means no insulin.
5. Forgetting to say what **ends** a positive feedback loop.

## Quick self-check

1. Why does insulin release fall as glucose returns to normal? *(The stimulus, high glucose, is shrinking: negative feedback switches itself down.)*
2. What stops the positive feedback in childbirth? *(The birth removes the pressure on the cervix, so oxytocin release stops increasing.)*
3. Is fruit ripening controlled by negative or positive feedback? *(Positive: ethylene triggers ripening, and ripening fruit release more ethylene, so the change grows.)*
