## Understanding

- I can name the three stages of cell signalling and say which stage any component belongs to. *(Guide: "From signal to response"; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain how a receptor's ligand-binding domain makes it specific, and that ligands can be peptides, proteins or small molecules. *(Guide: "Reception: ligands and receptors")*
- I can predict whether a receptor is on the surface or in the cytoplasm or nucleus from the type of ligand, and explain why. *(Guide: "Where is the receptor?"; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can explain how ligand binding changes the shape of the receptor's intracellular domain and starts transduction. *(Guide: "Where is the receptor?"; Practice Q5, Q6)*
- I can describe, step by step, a G protein-coupled receptor pathway that uses cAMP. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain how a ligand-gated channel responds by opening or closing, and why its response is fast. *(Guide: "Ligand-gated ion channels"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain the jobs of kinases, phosphatases and second messengers, and name responses such as growth, secretion and gene expression. *(Guide: "Transduction" and "Response")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can calculate the amplification in a model cascade, dividing where several molecules activate one. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7)*
- I can read a log-scale chart of molecule numbers. *(Guide: Figure 2)*
- I can use timing data, with linear interpolation, to propose the order of a kinase cascade. *(Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can use experimental results to locate a receptor and to judge whether a molecule acts as a second messenger. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can tell apart evidence that a molecule is sufficient for a response and evidence that it is necessary. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can predict the behaviour of a receptor built from the domains of two different receptors. *(Practice Q6)*
