## Recap

- **Detecting a deviation:** compare observed counts with the Mendelian prediction using chi-square. If χ² exceeds the critical value, reject the Mendelian null hypothesis and look for a biological cause.
- **Incomplete dominance:** heterozygote is a blend (red × white → pink). **Codominance:** heterozygote shows both phenotypes fully (blood group AB, IᴬIᴮ).
- **Linked genes** are on the same chromosome and tend to be inherited together. **Crossing over** in prophase I makes recombinants. Recombination frequency gives map distance; adding distances orders genes.
- **Sex-linked genes** are on X or Y. XY males have one X, so one recessive X-linked allele shows. Fathers pass X to every daughter and Y to every son. Y-linked traits go father to all sons.
- **Other systems:** birds are ZW (female) and ZZ (male); bees are haplodiploid (haploid males from unfertilized eggs).
- **Pleiotropy:** one gene, several effects (sickle-cell allele: anaemia, organ damage, malaria resistance in heterozygotes). The effects never separate.
- **Non-nuclear inheritance:** mitochondria and chloroplasts are randomly shared out at division and usually come from the egg or ovule, so their traits are typically maternal.

## Key relationships

| Pattern | Data clue |
|---|---|
| Incomplete dominance or codominance | heterozygote × heterozygote gives 1 : 2 : 1 phenotypes |
| Linked genes (test cross) | two large and two small classes instead of 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 |
| X-linked recessive | more affected males; never father to son; can skip through carrier daughters |
| Y-linked | only males; every son of an affected father |
| Pleiotropy | several traits always inherited together |
| Mitochondrial or chloroplast | reciprocal crosses differ; offspring match the mother |

| Equation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Recombination frequency (%) = recombinants ÷ total × 100** | 1% = 1 map unit |
| **χ² = Σ (o − e)² ÷ e** | df = categories − 1 |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Map distances from one cross are reliable for genes fairly close together; recombination frequency never exceeds about 50%.
- The heterozygous parent in a mapping test cross must have a known arrangement of alleles (from known parents) to tell parental from recombinant classes.
- Maternal inheritance of organelles is "typical", not universal.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Calling a pink heterozygote codominant: a blend is incomplete dominance.
2. Dividing recombinants by parental types instead of by the total.
3. Labelling the large classes as recombinants.
4. Saying an affected father can pass an X-linked trait to his son.
5. Assuming the female is always the sex with two identical sex chromosomes (birds are the reverse).
6. Explaining traits that always go together with linkage when no recombinants ever appear (think pleiotropy).

## Quick self-check

1. 45 recombinants among 300 test-cross offspring: map distance? *(45 ÷ 300 × 100 = 15 map units)*
2. Pink × pink (incomplete dominance): proportion pink? *(1/2)*
3. Why are Z-linked recessive traits more common in female birds? *(Females are ZW, so one recessive allele on their single Z is expressed.)*
