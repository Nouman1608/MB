## Recap

- **Genetic diversity** = variety of alleles and genotypes within a population or species. Measure it by alleles per gene, heterozygosity (expected 2pq for two alleles) or the spread of a heritable trait.
- Diversity is **gained** by mutation, meiosis and fertilisation, and gene flow. It is **lost** by selection, chance in small populations and inbreeding.
- With little diversity, one disease or change in conditions can affect almost every individual. Examples: one potato variety and blight in 1840s Ireland; uniform crops and rusts; the few founders of today's condors and black-footed ferrets.
- With high diversity, some individuals are more likely to already carry alleles that let them survive. Not every cell in a varied bacterial population is killed by an antibiotic.
- Adding individuals from another population (**genetic rescue**) restores alleles by gene flow.

## Key relationships

| Situation | Effect on diversity | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Population crash (bottleneck) | many alleles lost | less able to respond to the next pressure |
| Inbreeding | more homozygous individuals | harmful recessive alleles expressed more often |
| Monoculture or clones | almost no variation | one pathogen strain can destroy the whole crop |
| Gene flow from another population | alleles added | resilience restored (genetic rescue) |
| Environment changes | value of each allele changes | an adaptive allele may become harmful |

| Calculation | How |
|---|---|
| Fraction surviving if only rr dies | 1 − q² |
| Fraction surviving if only the recessive homozygote survives | q² |
| Expected heterozygosity, two alleles | 2pq |

## Assumptions behind the reasoning

- Hardy–Weinberg calculations assume random mating and no recent selection at that gene.
- "More diverse means more resilient" is a probability statement: it makes survival more likely, not certain.
- A small trial or survey **supports** a claim; it does not prove it.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying the pressure creates the helpful allele. Selection acts on existing variation.
2. Comparing only averages; the spread of a trait decides who survives an extreme.
3. Calling an allele "good" without naming the environment.
4. Equating a large population with a diverse one.
5. Using q instead of q² for the frequency of the homozygous recessive genotype.

## Quick self-check

1. Why is a field of genetically identical plants at risk? *(Every plant has the same susceptibility, so a pathogen that infects one can infect all.)*
2. If q = 0.9 and only rr individuals die, what fraction survives? *(1 − 0.9² = 1 − 0.81 = 0.19)*
3. Give one way to raise the diversity of a small, inbred population. *(Bring in individuals from another population of the species: gene flow.)*
