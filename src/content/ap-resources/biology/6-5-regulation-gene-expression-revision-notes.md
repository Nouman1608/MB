## Recap

- Cells keep almost all their genes but express only some, at controlled levels. Most control happens at the **start of transcription**.
- **Regulatory sequences** (promoter, operator, control elements) are DNA. **Regulatory proteins** (repressors, activators, transcription factors) bind them.
- **Constitutive** genes are always on. **Inducible** genes switch on when an inducer appears. **Repressible** genes switch off when a corepressor builds up.
- An **operon** = promoter + operator + several structural genes, transcribed as one mRNA. The repressor gene lies outside it.
- The operator lies between the promoter and the genes, so a bound repressor blocks RNA polymerase for **all** the genes at once.
- In eukaryotes, scattered genes that share the **same control sequence** are switched on together by one transcription factor.
- **DNA methylation** usually silences; **histone acetylation** loosens chromatin and usually activates. Both are reversible and can be passed to daughter cells.
- Phenotype = which genes are on **and** how much product they make. Development uses chains of transcription factors (sequential expression).

## Key relationships

| Situation | Repressor state | Operon |
|---|---|---|
| lac, no lactose | active, on operator | off |
| lac, lactose present | allolactose bound, off operator | on (strongly if glucose is scarce: CAP–cAMP helps) |
| trp, little tryptophan | inactive, off operator | on |
| trp, much tryptophan | tryptophan bound, on operator | off |

| Mutation | Likely effect on an inducible operon |
|---|---|
| no working repressor | on all the time (constitutive) |
| operator cannot bind repressor | on all the time (constitutive) |
| promoter cannot bind RNA polymerase | off all the time |

## Assumptions

- Fold change compares a test value with a stated reference value; always say which.
- Drug experiments affect many genes, so effects on one gene may be partly indirect.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Putting the repressor gene inside the operon.
2. Saying lactose (or allolactose) binds the operator.
3. Calling an epigenetic change a mutation.
4. Mixing up the effects of DNA methylation and histone acetylation.
5. Forgetting that the level of expression, not just on/off, shapes phenotype.

## Quick self-check

1. Why does a repressor bound to the operator stop all three lac genes? *(The operator lies upstream of all three genes, in RNA polymerase's path, and they share one mRNA.)*
2. A wild-type operon gives 4 units without inducer and 320 with it. Fold induction? *(320 ÷ 4 = 80)*
3. Which change makes a gene easier to transcribe: adding acetyl groups to histones or adding methyl groups to its promoter DNA? *(Adding acetyl groups to histones.)*
