## Recap

- A **population evolves** when its allele frequencies change between generations. Selection is not the only cause.
- **Mutation:** random change in DNA; the only source of brand-new alleles. Supplies the variation selection acts on, but alone changes frequencies very slowly.
- **Genetic drift:** chance change in allele frequencies because each generation is a random sample of the last. Strongest in **small** populations. Can fix or lose alleles whatever their effect.
- **Bottleneck effect:** a population is reduced to a few individuals for at least one generation; survivors' alleles are a chance sample, and lost alleles stay lost after regrowth.
- **Founder effect:** a few individuals start a separate population; its frequencies reflect the founders, not the source.
- **Gene flow:** migration adds or removes alleles; makes populations more alike and prevents them diverging into separate species.

## Key relationships

| Process | Random? | Variation within a population | Difference between populations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mutation | yes | increases (slowly) | can increase |
| Drift, bottleneck, founder | yes | decreases | increases |
| Gene flow | not selective | usually increases | decreases |
| Natural selection | no | often decreases | can increase |

| Calculation | How |
|---|---|
| total copies of a gene | 2 × number of diploid individuals |
| copies of A | 2 × (AA) + 1 × (Aa) |
| frequency of A | copies of A ÷ total copies |
| check | frequencies of all alleles add to 1 |
| mixing populations | add allele counts from each, divide by combined total |

## Null hypotheses you may need

- "Survival (or reproduction) is independent of genotype": if true, frequency changes are due to chance (drift).
- "Allele frequencies do not change between generations": rejected if a clear, repeatable change is measured.
- Prediction for drift: replicate small populations **spread out** in random directions; large ones stay near the start.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying mutations happen because an organism needs them.
2. Saying drift happens only in small populations (it is just stronger there).
3. Counting individuals or phenotypes instead of alleles.
4. Forgetting to double homozygotes when counting alleles.
5. Confusing bottleneck (whole population shrinks) with founder effect (small group separates).
6. Saying individuals evolve.

## Quick self-check

1. Genotypes 30 BB, 50 Bb, 20 bb. Frequency of B? *((60 + 50) ÷ 200 = 0.55)*
2. Why can a harmful allele become common in a small population? *(Drift is chance sampling; it ignores the allele's effect.)*
3. What does gene flow do to two populations' allele frequencies? *(Pulls them toward each other.)*
