## Recap

- Meiosis turns one **diploid (2n)** cell into four **haploid (n)** cells. In animals these become gametes; fertilization restores 2n.
- **Homologous chromosomes**: one from each parent, same genes in the same order, possibly different alleles. **Sister chromatids**: identical copies made in S phase, joined at the centromere.
- DNA replicates once (S phase before meiosis). There is no S phase between meiosis I and II.
- **Meiosis I**: prophase I (condensing, synapsis of homologues, chiasmata may form, spindle forms, nuclear envelope breaks down); metaphase I (homologous **pairs** at the plate); anaphase I (homologues separate, sister chromatids stay joined); telophase I and cytokinesis (two haploid cells, chromosomes still duplicated).
- **Meiosis II**: prophase II (new spindle); metaphase II (chromosomes in **single file**, the kinetochores of the two sister chromatids attached to microtubules from opposite poles); anaphase II (centromere proteins break down, sister chromatids separate); telophase II and cytokinesis (four haploid cells, single-chromatid chromosomes).

## Key relationships

| Stage (per cell), start 2n | Chromosomes | Chromatids | DNA (G1 = x) |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | 2n | 2n | x |
| Prophase I to metaphase I | 2n | 4n | 2x |
| End of meiosis I | n | 2n | x |
| End of meiosis II | n | n | x ÷ 2 |

| | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Divisions | 1 | 2 |
| Cells made | 2 | 4 |
| Daughter chromosome number | 2n | n |
| Daughter cells | genetically identical | genetically different |
| Shared features | spindle moves chromosomes; same stage names; cytokinesis by furrow or cell plate | |

## Assumptions behind the counts

- The table assumes chromosomes separate correctly. Errors (nondisjunction) are covered in Topic 5.2.
- "DNA content" means nuclear DNA per cell; values are relative.
- The cleavage furrow applies to animal cells; plant cells form a cell plate.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying sister chromatids separate in anaphase I.
2. Calling cells at the end of meiosis I diploid because their chromosomes have two chromatids.
3. Adding an S phase between meiosis I and II.
4. Counting a duplicated chromosome as two chromosomes.
5. Saying homologues are identical copies.
6. Saying meiosis gives two cells.

## Quick self-check

1. A species has 2n = 16. How many chromosomes and chromatids are in a cell at metaphase I? *(16 chromosomes, 32 chromatids)*
2. How many cells, with how many chromosomes each, does that cell give at the end of meiosis II? *(4 cells of 8 chromosomes)*
3. Which stage halves the chromosome number, and why? *(Anaphase I with telophase I: homologues go to different cells, so each cell gets one of each pair.)*
