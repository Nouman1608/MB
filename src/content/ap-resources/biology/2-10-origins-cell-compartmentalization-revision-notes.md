## Recap

- **Prokaryotes** typically have no membrane-bound organelles, but they do have specialized regions: a **nucleoid** (DNA, no membrane), smaller **ribosomes**, a **plasma membrane** that holds the proteins for aerobic respiration, and, in some species, internal thylakoids (cyanobacteria) or protein-shelled compartments (carboxysomes).
- **Eukaryotes** keep many internal membranes that partition the cell: nucleus, ER, Golgi, vesicles, lysosomes, vacuoles, mitochondria and (in plants and algae) chloroplasts.
- **Endosymbiosis:** a host cell took in an aerobic prokaryote that became the mitochondrion. Later, a cell that already had mitochondria took in a cyanobacterium that became the chloroplast. That is why plants have both organelles and animals have only mitochondria.
- Over time most of the guests' genes moved to the nucleus or were lost, so the organelles now depend on the cell.

## Key relationships

| Evidence | What it shows |
|---|---|
| two membranes | prokaryote enclosed by a host membrane |
| own small, usually circular DNA | like a bacterial chromosome |
| own ribosomes, bacteria-like | blocked by some antibiotics that act on bacteria |
| divide by splitting | like binary fission; never made from scratch |
| DNA sequences closest to alphaproteobacteria (mitochondria) or cyanobacteria (chloroplasts) | shared ancestry with specific bacterial groups |

| Calculation | Rule |
|---|---|
| percentage identity | identical positions ÷ positions compared × 100 |
| percentage remaining | treated ÷ control × 100; inhibition = 100 − remaining |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Higher sequence identity means a more recent shared ancestor, assuming sequences change at a similar rate.
- A drug that "blocks bacterial ribosomes" is assumed to act on ribosomes only; a second drug acting on cytosolic ribosomes is the control.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying prokaryotes have no internal organization.
2. Saying prokaryotes cannot respire aerobically without mitochondria.
3. Saying plants have chloroplasts *instead of* mitochondria.
4. Saying one observation proves the theory; the strength is in several lines agreeing.
5. Calling modern mitochondria bacteria; they cannot live alone.
6. Listing evidence without saying **why** it supports a prokaryotic origin.

## Quick self-check

1. 1,020 of 1,500 aligned positions are identical. Percentage identity? *(1,020 ÷ 1,500 × 100 = 68.0%)*
2. Name two specialized regions inside a prokaryote. *(Any two: nucleoid, ribosomes, plasma membrane with respiration proteins, thylakoids in cyanobacteria, carboxysomes.)*
3. Why do almost all eukaryotes have mitochondria but only some have chloroplasts? *(Mitochondria were acquired first, by the common ancestor; chloroplasts later, by one lineage.)*
