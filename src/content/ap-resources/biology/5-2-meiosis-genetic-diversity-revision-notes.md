## Recap

- Correct separation (homologues in meiosis I, sister chromatids in meiosis II) gives each gamete one haploid set that mixes maternal and paternal chromosomes.
- **Crossing over (recombination):** in prophase I, non-sister chromatids of a homologous pair swap matching segments at chiasmata. Result: recombinant chromatids with new combinations of existing alleles, and sister chromatids that are no longer identical.
- **Independent assortment:** each homologous pair lines up at metaphase I independently of the others, so whole chromosomes are shuffled into gametes.
- **Random fertilization:** any gamete from one parent can fuse with any gamete from the other.
- **Nondisjunction:** homologues (meiosis I) or sister chromatids (meiosis II) fail to separate, giving gametes that are not haploid. Fertilization then gives 2n + 1 (trisomy) or 2n − 1 (monosomy) zygotes.

## Key relationships

| Quantity | Rule | Human value (n = 23) |
|---|---|---|
| Gamete combinations (no crossing over) | 2ⁿ | 2²³ ≈ 8.4 million |
| Zygote combinations from one couple | 2ⁿ × 2ⁿ | 2⁴⁶ ≈ 7.0 × 10¹³ |
| Probability a gamete has all maternal chromosomes | (1/2)ⁿ | 1 in 2²³ |

| Nondisjunction in | Gametes from one cell |
|---|---|
| Meiosis I | n + 1, n + 1, n − 1, n − 1 |
| Meiosis II (one cell) | n, n, n + 1, n − 1 |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- 2ⁿ ignores crossing over, so the real number of genetically different gametes is far larger.
- Homologues are assumed to differ in at least some alleles; otherwise shuffling them would change nothing.
- Details of different organisms' sexual life cycles are outside the course; the rules apply to the chromosomes.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying crossing over happens between sister chromatids.
2. Saying crossing over creates new alleles.
3. Using 2n or n² instead of 2ⁿ.
4. Placing independent assortment in metaphase II.
5. Giving the same gamete outcome for nondisjunction in meiosis I and II.
6. Forgetting that fertilization adds variation as well as restoring 2n.

## Quick self-check

1. A species has 2n = 12. How many chromosome combinations can its gametes have, ignoring crossing over? *(n = 6, so 2⁶ = 64)*
2. In the same species, sister chromatids fail to separate in one cell in meiosis II. Chromosome numbers of the four gametes? *(6, 6, 7, 5)*
3. Why can all four products of one meiosis differ genetically? *(Crossing over makes sister chromatids non-identical, so the two cells of meiosis II receive different chromatids.)*
