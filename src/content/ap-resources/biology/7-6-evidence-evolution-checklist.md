## Understanding

- I can name the five kinds of data that support evolution (geographical, geological, physical, biochemical, mathematical) and give an example of each. *(Guide: "Many sciences, one conclusion")*
- I can explain how the order of rock layers gives the relative age of fossils. *(Guide: "The age of the rock layers"; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain how carbon-14 dating works and why it only suits remains up to about 50,000 years old. *(Guide: "Radioactive decay of isotopes"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain how matching layers and fossils between sites, and fossils on separated continents, count as geographical evidence. *(Guide: "Geographical data"; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain how homologous and vestigial structures show common ancestry, and why analogous structures do not. *(Guide: "Morphological evidence"; Practice Q1, Q6)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can find the number of half-lives from the fraction remaining and calculate an age. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can read an age from a decay curve when the fraction is not a whole number of half-lives. *(Guide: Figure 2)*
- I can count sequence differences and calculate percentage identity. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can describe a table or graph using specific values, the trend and the relationship between variables. *(Guide: "Describing data well"; Practice Q4, Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can use the number of sequence differences to decide which species share the most recent common ancestor. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why DNA can show more differences than the protein it codes for. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why independent lines of evidence that agree make a stronger case, and state the assumptions behind a dating method. *(Practice Q5, Q7)*
