## Recap

- **Gel electrophoresis** separates DNA fragments by size. The phosphate backbone makes DNA negative, so it moves toward the **positive** electrode. Short fragments move farthest. A **ladder** of known sizes lets you estimate band sizes.
- **PCR** amplifies one target region between two **primers**, using a **heat-stable DNA polymerase**. Each cycle: **denature** (strands separate), **anneal** (primers bind), **extend** (polymerase adds nucleotides to the primers).
- **Bacterial transformation** introduces foreign DNA, usually a **plasmid** carrying a gene of interest and an **antibiotic-resistance marker**. Only transformed cells grow on antibiotic plates. Dividing bacteria copy the plasmid: **gene cloning**.
- **DNA sequencing** gives the order of nucleotides. Comparing sequences or band patterns gives a **DNA fingerprint** for forensic identification, kinship, species identification and phylogenetics.

## Key relationships

| Equation or rule | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **N = N₀ × 2ⁿ** | maximum copies after n PCR cycles from N₀ starting copies |
| efficiency = colonies ÷ µg DNA spread | transformation efficiency (transformants per µg) |
| distance falls roughly linearly as log(size) rises | ladder bands crowd together near the wells |
| every band in a child is in the mother or the biological father | basis of a kinship test |
| fewer sequence differences → more closely related | basis of molecular phylogenetics |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- 2ⁿ assumes **100% efficiency** in every cycle; real reactions are lower and level off.
- Size estimates from a gel are approximate (2 significant figures).
- A match **supports** identity; a reliable mismatch **excludes**.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying DNA moves toward the negative electrode, or that big fragments go farthest.
2. Saying PCR copies the whole genome; only the region between the primers is copied.
3. Forgetting why the polymerase must be heat-stable.
4. Rounding a number of PCR cycles **down**.
5. Using the total DNA instead of the DNA actually **spread** when calculating transformation efficiency.
6. Saying the antibiotic makes cells resistant; the plasmid gene does.
7. Treating a single matching band as proof of identity.

## Quick self-check

1. What happens at each PCR temperature? *(Hot: strands separate; cooler: primers bind; middle: polymerase extends the primers.)*
2. Three copies, 10 perfect cycles? *(3 × 2¹⁰ = 3072 copies)*
3. A child has a band that neither the mother nor a tested man has. What can you conclude? *(If the test is reliable, that man is excluded as the biological father.)*
