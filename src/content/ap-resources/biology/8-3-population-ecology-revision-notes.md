## Recap

- A **population** is all the individuals of one species in one area at one time. Members interact (competing, mating, spreading disease) and interact with their environment.
- In a closed population, size changes only through **births** and **deaths**. Growth depends on birth rate, death rate **and** population size.
- Births and deaths depend on energy and matter. Adaptations for obtaining and using food (feeding structures, energy budgets, breeding when food peaks, dormancy) change B and D.
- With **no constraints on reproduction**, the per capita growth rate stays at its maximum, rₘₐₓ, and growth is **exponential**. It cannot last: resources eventually run short.

## Key relationships

| Equation | Meaning and units |
|---|---|
| **dN/dt = B − D** | B, D = births and deaths per unit time in the whole population; dN/dt in individuals per unit time |
| b = B ÷ N, d = D ÷ N | per capita birth and death rates (per individual per unit time) |
| **r = (B − D) ÷ N**, so dN/dt = rN | per capita growth rate; compares populations of different sizes |
| **dN/dt = rₘₐₓN** | exponential growth; rₘₐₓ = maximum per capita growth rate |

| Feature of exponential growth | What stays the same | What increases |
|---|---|---|
| shape | per capita rate, doubling time | dN/dt, N |
| graph | straight line on a log y-axis | steeper and steeper J on a linear axis |

## Graphing population data

Line graph of N (or density) against time; time on the x-axis; labels and units on both axes; even scale; plot means with error bars for replicates; log y-axis for exponential data; trend line through the general pattern.

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- dN/dt = B − D assumes no immigration or emigration.
- Exponential growth assumes unlimited resources and a constant rₘₐₓ.
- Month-by-month (step) projections are estimates of a continuous process.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Treating B and D as per capita rates, or forgetting to divide by N when comparing populations.
2. Saying the growth rate is constant in exponential growth (only the per capita rate is).
3. Reporting N after one step when asked for dN/dt.
4. Using a bar chart for a time series, or leaving out units.
5. Calling a group of several species a population.

## Quick self-check

1. N = 500, B = 60 per year, D = 35 per year. dN/dt and r? *(25 per year; 25 ÷ 500 = 0.05 per year)*
2. rₘₐₓ = 0.3 per day, N = 1,000. dN/dt? *(0.3 × 1,000 = 300 per day)*
3. Why does a straight line on a log graph show exponential growth? *(Equal steps on a log axis are equal ratios, so a constant slope means the same multiplication in each time interval.)*
