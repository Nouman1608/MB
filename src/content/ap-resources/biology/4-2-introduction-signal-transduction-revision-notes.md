## Recap

- **Signal transduction** converts a signal at the receptor into a response inside the cell, in three stages: **reception**, **transduction**, **response**.
- A **ligand** (peptide, protein or small molecule) binds the **ligand-binding domain** of a specific receptor. Binding changes the receptor's shape, so its **intracellular domain** changes too and starts transduction.
- Water-soluble ligands → receptors **on the surface**. Small nonpolar ligands (steroids, thyroid hormones) → receptors **in the cytoplasm or nucleus**.
- **GPCRs** (seven passes through the membrane) activate a **G protein** (GDP → GTP), which activates **adenylyl cyclase**: ATP → **cAMP** → protein kinase → targets.
- **Ligand-gated channels** open or close when the ligand binds; ions flow and the cell responds in milliseconds.
- **Kinases** add phosphate (from ATP) to proteins; **phosphatases** remove it. A chain of kinases is a **phosphorylation cascade**.
- Responses: cell growth, secretion, gene expression, enzyme activity, channel opening or closing.

## Key relationships

| Component | Stage | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Ligand (first messenger) | reception | binds a specific receptor |
| Receptor | reception | changes shape when bound; starts transduction |
| G protein | transduction | on with GTP, off with GDP; activates adenylyl cyclase |
| Adenylyl cyclase | transduction | makes many cAMP from ATP (amplifies) |
| cAMP (second messenger) | transduction | diffuses; activates protein kinase |
| Protein kinase | transduction | phosphorylates many proteins (amplifies) |
| Target protein or gene | response | changes cell activity |

| Relationship | Use |
|---|---|
| Overall amplification = product of the factors at each step | e.g. 20 × 100 ÷ 4 × 200 × 1000 = 1 × 10⁸ |
| Step where several molecules activate one | divide; this step lowers the number |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Amplification models use fixed factors per step; real values vary, and signals are switched off over time (GTP broken down, cAMP removed, phosphatases).
- An experiment showing a molecule is **sufficient** for a response does not show it is **necessary**.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying the hormone enters the cell to act when its receptor is on the surface.
2. Calling the hormone the second messenger.
3. Confusing the GPCR with the G protein.
4. Saying kinases remove phosphates.
5. Saying phosphorylation always switches proteins on.
6. Multiplying at every step, including steps where several molecules activate one.

## Quick self-check

1. A small nonpolar hormone: where is its receptor? *(Cytoplasm or nucleus; the complex often changes gene expression.)*
2. Three steps each multiply the signal by 10. Overall amplification? *(10 × 10 × 10 = 1000)*
3. Why does a ligand-gated channel give a faster response than a GPCR pathway? *(Binding opens the channel directly; there is no chain of relay molecules.)*
