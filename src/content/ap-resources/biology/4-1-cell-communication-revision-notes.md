Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, figures and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/4-1-cell-communication-study-guide/).

## Recap

- Communication needs a **signalling cell**, a **signal**, a **target cell with a receptor**, and a **response**.
- **Direct contact, junctions:** gap junctions (animals) and plasmodesmata (plants) join the cytoplasm of neighbouring cells, so ions and small molecules pass straight through.
- **Direct contact, surface molecules:** a molecule fixed on one cell binds a receptor on a touching cell. An antigen-presenting cell shows antigen fragments to a helper T cell; a killer T cell recognises fragments shown by an infected cell.
- **Local regulators** are released into the fluid around a cell and act on cells nearby: neurotransmitters at synapses, morphogens in embryos, quorum-sensing signals in bacteria, and defence signals in plants.
- **Hormones** travel in the blood to distant targets: insulin, human growth hormone, thyroid hormones, testosterone, estrogen.

## Key relationships

| Route | Distance | Example | Why it suits the job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junctions | touching cells | gap junctions in heart muscle | fast spread through a tissue |
| Surface contact | touching cells | APC → helper T cell | precise: only the cell touched |
| Local regulator | nearby cells | neurotransmitter, morphogen | fast; concentration falls with distance |
| Quorum sensing | cells in one population | bacterial glow, biofilms | response only once the population is dense |
| Hormone | whole body | insulin, thyroid hormones | reaches distant organs; long-lasting |

| Idea | What to remember |
|---|---|
| Morphogen gradient | high near source, low far away; different concentrations switch on different genes |
| Quorum threshold | signal concentration rises with cell density; genes switch on together above a threshold |
| Specificity | delivery is wide; **receptors** decide which cells are targets |
| Different responses | the same signal can cause different responses in different target cells |

## Assumptions behind the models

- Model gradients assume the signal spreads evenly from one source and is removed at a steady rate.
- In quorum-sensing experiments, filtered medium may differ from fresh medium in food and waste, not only in signal.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying hormones are carried only to their target organs.
2. Calling neurotransmitter signalling long-distance because the neuron is long.
3. Treating all direct contact as channels between cells.
4. Saying bacteria do not communicate.
5. Assuming every target cell responds to a signal in the same way.
6. Describing a gradient boundary as the place where the signal "stops".

## Quick self-check

1. Why do muscle cells respond to insulin but some other cells do not? *(Only cells with insulin receptors are targets.)*
2. A morphogen halves every 20 µm from a source of 100 units. Concentration at 40 µm? *(100 × 0.5 × 0.5 = 25 units)*
3. Why is quorum sensing useful to bacteria? *(Jobs such as glowing or forming a biofilm work only when many cells do them together.)*
