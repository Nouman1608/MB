## Understanding

- I can define a mutation and explain how it can change the type of protein, the amount of protein, or nothing. *(Guide: "What a mutation is"; Practice Q7)*
- I can tell silent, missense and nonsense point mutations apart from a codon change. *(Guide: "Point mutations"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why insertions or deletions that are not multiples of three shift the reading frame, and why a point mutation cannot. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can name the causes of mutation and explain why mutations are random. *(Guide: "What causes mutations"; Worked example 2)*
- I can explain how nondisjunction leads to aneuploidy and describe the main changes in chromosome structure. *(Guide: "Errors in chromosomes")*
- I can name and describe transformation, transduction, conjugation, transposition and viral recombination. *(Guide: "Variation for natural selection to act on"; Practice Q6)*

## Skills

- I can transcribe a template strand, translate it with a codon table, and show the effect of a mutation on the polypeptide. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can predict the gametes produced when nondisjunction happens in meiosis I or meiosis II. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can calculate percentages and ratios from survival or enzyme data and use them as evidence. *(Practice Q5, Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can argue, with data, that the same mutation can be beneficial in one environment and harmful in another. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why an environment selects existing variants rather than causing the mutations it favours. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can predict whether a mutation's location (active site, surface, promoter, third codon position) makes a large or small effect likely. *(Guide: "Location matters"; Practice Q4, Q7)*
