## Understanding

- I can name the membrane-bound structures of a eukaryotic cell and say what each one keeps in its own space. *(Guide: Figure 1 and the structures table)*
- I can explain how separate compartments let opposing reactions, such as building and breaking down a molecule, run at the same time. *(Guide: "Idea 1"; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain how proton pumps keep the inside of a lysosome acidic, and why that matters for its enzymes. *(Guide: "Idea 1"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why folded membranes, such as cristae and stacked thylakoids, let more membrane-bound reactions happen in the same volume. *(Guide: "Idea 2" and Figure 2; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain that compartments are not sealed: vesicles and transport proteins move material between them. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can total the area of a folded membrane model, including the unfolded part, and compare it with the original. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can convert a pH difference into a factor change in H⁺ concentration. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can estimate a value between two data points in a table. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can turn fractionation data into percentages and identify the compartment that holds an enzyme. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can use sphere volumes to compare the concentration of molecules in a small compartment and in a whole cell. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict and justify the effects of disrupting a compartment, such as blocking its pumps or making its membrane leaky. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q5)*
- I can make a claim linking internal membrane area to the rate of a process, support it with data and suggest another explanation to test. *(Practice Q6)*
