## Recap

- A **community** is the interacting populations of different species in one area. It changes over time as those interactions change.
- **Species composition**: which species are present. **Species richness**: how many. **Evenness**: how equally individuals are shared among them. **Diversity** combines richness and evenness.
- Interactions control how each population gets **energy and matter**, so they drive population dynamics and community structure.
- Predator–prey numbers can cycle, with the **predator peak lagging** behind the prey peak.
- Similar species can coexist through **niche partitioning**; without it, one may exclude the other (**competitive exclusion**).
- A **trophic cascade** passes effects down a food chain, alternating down, up, down.

## Key relationships

| Interaction | Signs | Example of effect |
|---|---|---|
| Competition | − / − | each species gets less of a shared limited resource |
| Predation, herbivory | + / − | consumer gains energy; prey or plant loses |
| Parasitism | + / − | parasite feeds on a living host over time |
| Mutualism | + / + | both gain (pollination, nutrient exchange) |
| Commensalism | + / 0 | one gains; the other is unaffected |

| Equation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Diversity index = 1 − Σ(n/N)²** | n = individuals of one species; N = individuals of all species |
| **SE = s ÷ √n**; error bars = mean ± 2 SE | non-overlapping bars: the means are probably different |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Simpson's index assumes the sample represents the whole community (same method, effort and season at each site).
- Error bars of ± 2 SE give a rough 95% confidence interval; they assume repeated samples were collected independently.
- Comparing indices only makes sense between similar habitats.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Calling a community an ecosystem (an ecosystem includes the non-living parts).
2. Forgetting to square n/N, or forgetting to subtract the sum from 1.
3. Judging diversity by richness alone and ignoring evenness.
4. Swapping mutualism (+/+) and commensalism (+/0).
5. Saying predators peak at the same time as prey.
6. Reading overlapping error bars as proof that there is no difference.

## Quick self-check

1. Two species with 5 individuals each: index? *(1 − (0.25 + 0.25) = 0.50)*
2. Two species with 9 and 1 individuals: index? *(1 − (0.81 + 0.01) = 0.18; same richness, less even, lower index)*
3. Why does the predator peak come after the prey peak? *(Predators need time to turn extra food into extra offspring.)*
4. Name the +/0 interaction. *(Commensalism)*
