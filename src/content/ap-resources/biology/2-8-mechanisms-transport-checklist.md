## Understanding

- I can compare simple diffusion, facilitated diffusion and active transport by direction, energy use and the proteins involved. *(Guide: "Three ways across the membrane")*
- I can explain why active transport needs both a membrane protein and a direct input of metabolic energy such as ATP. *(Guide: "Three ways across the membrane"; Practice Q5)*
- I can describe the steps of the sodium–potassium pump cycle, including where ATP and phosphorylation come in. *(Guide: "The sodium–potassium pump"; Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain what an electrochemical gradient is and why Na⁺ rushes into a resting cell when its channels open. *(Guide: "Electrochemical gradients and membrane potential"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain how the pump helps maintain the membrane potential, directly and by keeping the ion gradients. *(Guide: "Electrochemical gradients and membrane potential"; Practice Q4)*
- I can give examples of other pumps, such as proton and calcium pumps, and say what each one maintains. *(Guide: "Electrochemical gradients and membrane potential"; Practice Q5, Q6)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can use the 3 Na⁺ : 2 K⁺ : 1 ATP ratio to calculate ion movement, ATP use and net charge moved. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q2, Q7)*
- I can calculate concentration ratios across a membrane, including from pH values. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5, Q6)*
- I can describe a graph of membrane potential over time, using numbers for each phase. *(Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can use the concentration push and the electrical push together to predict which way an ion leaks. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can predict the effects of stopping a pump on ion gradients, membrane potential, pH or cell volume. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can explain why cells must keep spending ATP to maintain gradients. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
