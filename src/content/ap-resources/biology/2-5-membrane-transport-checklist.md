Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/2-5-membrane-transport-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/biology/2-5-membrane-transport-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain how selective permeability allows a cell to build and keep concentration gradients. *(Guide: "Why cells keep gradients")*
- I can explain diffusion as net movement caused by random motion, and describe what happens at dynamic equilibrium. *(Guide: "Concentration gradients and diffusion"; Practice Q2)*
- I can define passive transport and name its three types, including the one that uses proteins. *(Guide: "Passive transport"; Practice Q1)*
- I can define active transport by its direct energy input, and explain why it can move substances from low to high concentration. *(Guide: "Active transport"; Practice Q3)*
- I can describe endocytosis and exocytosis, give an example of each, and explain why both need energy. *(Guide: Figure 1 and "Bulk transport"; Practice Q6)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can calculate a rate of uptake and an inside : outside concentration ratio from data. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7)*
- I can calculate a percentage change or a percentage of a maximum from transport data. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate how much membrane exocytosis adds and compare it with the cell's surface area. *(Guide: "Membrane bookkeeping"; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can use two lines of evidence (build-up above the outside concentration, and dependence on ATP) to decide whether uptake is passive or active. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3, Q4)*
- I can explain why a temperature experiment is a weak test of ATP dependence. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can evaluate a transport claim and propose an investigation with variables, controls and predicted results. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can predict how stopping ATP production affects simple diffusion, active transport and exocytosis. *(Practice Q7)*
