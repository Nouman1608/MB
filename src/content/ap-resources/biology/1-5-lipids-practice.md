These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. The mark points are a suggested Marlbridge rubric to help you check your work; they are not an official scoring guideline. Data for every question: energy released by complete respiration, fat 37 kJ g⁻¹ and carbohydrate 17 kJ g⁻¹. A calculator is assumed. Keep full calculator values until the last step, then round to the significant figures of the data. All data sets and organisms' figures are fictional.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · foundation)

Olive oil is liquid at 20 °C, but butter is solid. Which statement best explains this difference?

- (A) The fatty acid tails in olive oil contain more C=C double bonds, so they are kinked and cannot pack closely together.
- (B) The fatty acid tails in olive oil contain more hydrogen atoms, which makes them lighter.
- (C) Butter contains glycerol, but olive oil does not.
- (D) The covalent bonds in olive oil tails are weaker, so the tails break apart at room temperature.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Olive oil is rich in unsaturated fatty acids. Each double bond kinks the tail, so the tails cannot line up closely. With fewer attractions between tails, the oil stays liquid at a lower temperature. Butter is rich in straight saturated tails that pack tightly.

- (B) is backwards: unsaturated tails have *fewer* hydrogen atoms (each double bond means two fewer). Mass is not the reason anyway.
- (C) is false: both are fats, and every fat (triglyceride) contains glycerol.
- (D) confuses melting with breaking covalent bonds. Melting separates whole molecules from each other; the tails stay intact.
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · core)

Phospholipids are added to water and shaken gently. Which arrangement forms?

- (A) A single layer, with the fatty acid tails pointing down into the water and the heads in the air.
- (B) A bilayer, with the phosphate heads facing the water on both sides and the fatty acid tails facing each other in the middle.
- (C) A bilayer, with the fatty acid tails facing the water on both sides and the phosphate heads in the middle.
- (D) The phospholipids dissolve evenly through the water, because the phosphate group is polar.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** The heads are polar and hydrophilic, so they are stable next to water. The tails are nonpolar and hydrophobic, so they gather away from water, next to each other. A bilayer satisfies both.

- (A) puts the hydrophobic tails in the water, which is the least stable position. (At an air–water surface, phospholipids do form a single layer, but with their tails pointing up into the air.)
- (C) reverses the orientation: the hydrophobic tails would be in contact with water.
- (D) ignores the tails. The molecule is only partly polar, so it does not dissolve as single molecules; it gathers into structures that hide the tails.
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · core)

A hibernating mammal needs an extra store of 555 kJ. What mass of fat would store this energy?

- (A) 15.0 g
- (B) 32.6 g
- (C) 61.7 g
- (D) 20.5 kg

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** mass = energy ÷ energy per gram = 555 kJ ÷ 37 kJ g⁻¹ = **15.0 g**.

- (B) uses the carbohydrate value: 555 ÷ 17 = 32.6 g. That is the mass of carbohydrate, more than twice as much.
- (C) divides by 9, which is the energy of fat in kilocalories per gram, not kilojoules. Mixing units gives 61.7 g.
- (D) multiplies instead of dividing: 555 × 37 = 20 535 g. The units would be kJ² g⁻¹, not grams, which shows the error.
</details>

(a) Describe the relationship shown, using values from the table.
(b) State which oils would be liquid at 25 °C.
(c) Estimate the melting point of an oil with a mean of 1.0 double bond per tail, assuming a straight line between Q and R.
(d) Explain the relationship in terms of the structure of the fatty acid tails.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** As the mean number of double bonds per tail increases, the melting point decreases. It falls from 52 °C for P (0.2) to −14 °C for S (2.0): a drop of 66 °C as the double bonds rise by 1.8 per tail.

**(b)** An oil is liquid if 25 °C is above its melting point. **Q, R and S** are liquid (melting points 21, 3 and −14 °C). P (52 °C) is solid.

**(c)** Gradient from Q to R = (3 − 21) ÷ (1.3 − 0.8) = −18 ÷ 0.5 = −36 °C per double bond. From Q: 21 + (−36 × 0.2) = 21 − 7.2 = 13.8, so about **14 °C**. (A value read from a line drawn on the graph, between 12 and 16 °C, is acceptable.)

**(d)** More double bonds mean more kinks in the tails. Kinked tails cannot pack closely, so there are fewer attractions between neighbouring tails. Less energy is needed to let the molecules move past each other, so the oil melts at a lower temperature.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Negative relationship described with at least two quoted values |
| 1 | Q, R and S identified as liquid (with P solid) |
| 1 | Estimate between 12 and 16 °C, with working or a line shown |
| 1 | Double bonds → kinks → looser packing → fewer attractions between tails → lower melting point |

Do not award the last point for "double bonds are weaker". The covalent bonds inside the tails do not break when a fat melts.
</details>

## Question 5 (constructed response · core)

A student draws a single phospholipid and labels it.

(a) Identify the part of the molecule that is hydrophilic and the part that is hydrophobic, and explain each in terms of polarity.
(b) Explain why phospholipids in water form a bilayer without the cell using energy to arrange them.
(c) A cell is given a chemical that makes it replace phospholipids with triglycerides (fats) when it builds new membrane. Predict the effect on the new membrane and justify your prediction.

<details>
<summary>Model answer and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** The phosphate head is hydrophilic: it is charged or polar, so it attracts the δ⁺ and δ⁻ ends of water molecules. The two fatty acid tails are hydrophobic: they are made of nonpolar C–H bonds and cannot form attractions with water.

**(b)** Water molecules are most stable when they can hydrogen-bond with each other or with polar groups. If the heads face the water and the tails face each other, no tail touches water. Water keeps its hydrogen bonds and the heads interact with water. This is the most stable arrangement, so it forms on its own.

**(c)** A stable bilayer would **not** form. A triglyceride has three fatty acids and no polar head, so it is hydrophobic all over. It has no part that is stable facing the water. Triglycerides would gather into oily droplets instead. Without a continuous bilayer, the cell could not keep a barrier between its contents and the outside, so ions and other substances would leak, and the cell would probably die.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Head identified as hydrophilic **and** tails as hydrophobic, each linked to polarity |
| 1 | Bilayer explained by heads facing water and tails hidden from water |
| 1 | Prediction: no stable bilayer, because triglycerides lack a hydrophilic head |
| 1 | Consequence for the cell: loss of the barrier, leakage or cell death |

Accept "the fats would form droplets" for the prediction point if the reason (no hydrophilic part) is given.
</details>

## Question 6 (constructed response · stretch)

In a fictional experiment, cultured animal cells were treated with a compound that removes cholesterol from their plasma membranes. Control cells were given the same solution without the compound. All cells were then exposed to the same mild physical stress. Cells with damaged membranes were counted with a dye that only enters damaged cells.

| Group | Trial 1 | Trial 2 | Trial 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Control / % cells damaged | 6 | 8 | 7 |
| Cholesterol removed / % cells damaged | 21 | 25 | 23 |

(a) Calculate the mean percentage of damaged cells for each group.
(b) Identify the independent variable and explain why the control group is needed.
(c) Using your knowledge of cholesterol, explain the result.
(d) Predict how removing cholesterol would affect the cells if they were also moved to a much warmer temperature, and justify your prediction.

<details>
<summary>Model answer and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** Control: (6 + 8 + 7) ÷ 3 = 21 ÷ 3 = **7%**. Cholesterol removed: (21 + 25 + 23) ÷ 3 = 69 ÷ 3 = **23%**. That is about 3.3 times as many damaged cells (23 ÷ 7 = 3.29), or 16 percentage points more. The ranges (6–8 and 21–25) do not overlap.

**(b)** The independent variable is whether cholesterol was removed from the membrane. The control group shows how many cells are damaged by the stress and the handling alone, so any extra damage in the treated group can be linked to the loss of cholesterol.

**(c)** Cholesterol sits among the phospholipids of animal cell membranes and gives the membrane structural stability. Without it, the membrane is less able to hold together under stress, so more membranes tear or become leaky and let the dye in.

**(d)** Damage would probably **increase further**. At a warmer temperature, phospholipid tails move more and the membrane becomes more fluid. Cholesterol normally limits this movement. Without it, the membrane would become even less stable, so more cells would be damaged. (A good answer also says the prediction should be tested by comparing treated and control cells at both temperatures.)

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Both means correct (7% and 23%) |
| 1 | Independent variable stated **and** purpose of the control explained |
| 1 | Result linked to cholesterol's role in membrane stability |
| 1 | Prediction (more damage) justified by increased fluidity at higher temperature without cholesterol to limit it |

Do not award the third point for "cholesterol stores energy" or "cholesterol is a hormone": cholesterol's role here is structural.
</details>

## Question 7 (calculation · stretch)

A fictional migrating songbird stores 12 g of fat before crossing a sea. In flight it uses energy at 45 kJ per hour.

(a) Calculate the energy stored in the fat.
(b) Calculate how many hours of flight this fat supports.
(c) Calculate the mass of carbohydrate that would store the same energy, and how many extra grams the bird would carry.
(d) Explain, in terms of lipid structure, why fat is the better store for this bird.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

**(a)** Energy = 12 g × 37 kJ g⁻¹ = **444 kJ**.

**(b)** Time = 444 kJ ÷ 45 kJ h⁻¹ = 9.87 h, so about **9.9 hours**.

**(c)** Mass of carbohydrate = 444 kJ ÷ 17 kJ g⁻¹ = **26.1 g**. Extra mass = 26.1 − 12 = **14.1 g**, more than doubling the store. (Equivalently, 12 g of carbohydrate would hold only 12 × 17 = 204 kJ, enough for about 4.5 hours.)

**(d)** Fat tails are long chains of C–H bonds with very little oxygen, so a gram of fat releases more energy when respired. Fat is also hydrophobic, so it is stored in compact droplets without drawing in water. For a flying animal, every extra gram costs energy to carry, so the denser store is a clear advantage.

Suggested mark points (4): 1 for 444 kJ; 1 for about 9.9 h with units; 1 for 26.1 g and about 14 g extra; 1 for linking energy density to C–H-rich, oxygen-poor tails **and** to the cost of carrying mass in flight.

Common errors: multiplying 12 × 45 = 540 mixes up the store with the rate; dividing 45 ÷ 12 = 3.75 has no meaning here. Check that the units work out (kJ ÷ kJ h⁻¹ = h).
</details>
