## Understanding

- I can define population density and carrying capacity, and explain why K depends on the environment's total resources and can change. *(Guide: "Carrying capacity"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain how a rising density reduces each individual's share of resources, lowering births and raising deaths. *(Guide: "Density and resources work in both directions"; Practice Q4)*
- I can distinguish density-dependent from density-independent factors and give two examples of each. *(Guide: "What limits growth"; Practice Q1)*
- I can describe the S-shaped logistic curve and say where growth is fastest and where it stops. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q3)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can use dN/dt = rₘₐₓN(K − N)/K for populations below, at and above K. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can calculate means from replicate data and percentage survival at different densities. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate rates, ratios, percentages and percentage changes, using the original value as the denominator. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can estimate K and per capita growth rates from a table of population counts. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can read a graph of dN/dt against N and identify K/2 and K on it. *(Guide: Figure 2)*

## Reasoning

- I can classify a factor using the fraction of the population affected, not the number. *(Guide: "What limits growth"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why only density-dependent factors can hold a population near K. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can predict how a change in resources changes K and the population's growth. *(Guide: "Carrying capacity"; Practice Q6)*
