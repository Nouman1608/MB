Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, figures and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/6-7-mutations-study-guide/).

## Recap

- A **mutation** is a change in the DNA sequence. It can change the **type** of protein, the **amount** of protein (for example, a promoter change), or nothing detectable.
- Mutations are caused by **replication errors**, **failed repair**, **radiation** and **reactive chemicals**. They are **random**: a mutagen raises the rate, not the direction.
- A mutation is **beneficial, detrimental or neutral** depending on its effect and the **environment**. Mutations are the original source of new alleles.
- **Nondisjunction** in meiosis gives n + 1 or n − 1 gametes, leading to **aneuploidy**. Whole extra sets are **polyploidy**. Structural changes (deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation) can cause disorders.
- **Transformation, transduction, conjugation** (horizontal gene transfer) and **transposition** add variation in prokaryotes; related viruses in one host cell can **recombine**.

## Key relationships

| Change | Frame | Effect on polypeptide |
|---|---|---|
| Substitution → **silent** | kept | same amino acid |
| Substitution → **missense** | kept | one different amino acid |
| Substitution → **nonsense** | kept | early stop: shortened chain |
| Insert/delete 1, 2, 4, 5… bases | **shifted** | most later amino acids change; stop usually moves |
| Insert/delete 3, 6, 9… bases | kept | whole amino acids gained or lost |

| Nondisjunction in… | Gametes from one meiosis |
|---|---|
| Meiosis I | n+1, n+1, n−1, n−1 (all 4 abnormal) |
| Meiosis II (one cell) | n+1, n−1, n, n (2 of 4 abnormal) |

## Assumptions behind the reasoning

- Codon tables apply to the **mRNA**. The **coding** strand matches the mRNA (T for U); the **template** strand is complementary.
- "Neutral" means no effect on fitness in **that** environment; it may matter elsewhere.
- A missense prediction depends on **where** the amino acid is and how different the new R group is.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying a mutation "denatures" a protein.
2. Saying a point mutation causes a frameshift.
3. Calling every mutation harmful, or saying organisms mutate "in order to" adapt.
4. Reading the template strand as if it were the mRNA.
5. Forgetting that a promoter mutation changes the **amount** of protein, not its sequence.
6. Naming a specific disorder instead of explaining the mechanism (specific disorders are not assessed).

## Quick self-check

1. mRNA codon UGG (Trp) becomes UGA. Type? *(Nonsense: UGA is a stop codon.)*
2. Six adjacent bases are deleted from a coding sequence. Is the frame shifted? *(No: 6 is a multiple of 3. Two amino acids are lost; if the gap spans codon boundaries, one amino acid at the join may also change.)*
3. Why can a mutation that helps in one place harm in another? *(Its effect on survival and reproduction depends on the environment, for example a coat colour that hides on dark rock but stands out on pale sand.)*
