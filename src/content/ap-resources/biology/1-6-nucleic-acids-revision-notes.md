## Recap

- DNA and RNA are **polymers of nucleotides**. Each nucleotide has a **five-carbon sugar**, a **phosphate** (on carbon 5′) and a **nitrogenous base** (on carbon 1′).
- Nucleotides are joined by **covalent bonds** between the **3′ –OH** of one nucleotide and the phosphate of the next, forming a sugar–phosphate backbone. Hydrolysis breaks these links.
- A strand has a **5′ end** (free phosphate) and a **3′ end** (free –OH). New nucleotides are added only to the **3′ end**.
- DNA is a **double helix** of two **antiparallel** strands. Bases pair by **hydrogen bonds**: A–T (two) and C–G (three). In RNA, A pairs with **U**.
- The backbone is held by strong covalent bonds; the strands are held together by weak hydrogen bonds, so they can be separated without breaking either strand.

## Key relationships

| Feature | DNA | RNA |
|---|---|---|
| Sugar | deoxyribose (–H on 2′) | ribose (–OH on 2′) |
| Bases | A, G, C, T | A, G, C, U |
| Pairs | A–T, C–G | A–U, C–G |
| Strands | typically two | typically one |

| Rule | Use it to |
|---|---|
| In double-stranded DNA, %A = %T and %G = %C | find all four percentages from one |
| %A + %T + %G + %C = 100 | check your answer |
| H bonds = 2 × (A–T pairs) + 3 × (C–G pairs) | compare how strongly two segments hold together |
| Links in one strand = number of nucleotides − 1 | count covalent bonds in a backbone |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The pairing percentages hold only for **double-stranded** molecules. Single strands need not follow them.
- "Typically" matters: some viruses have single-stranded DNA or double-stranded RNA.
- Counting hydrogen bonds is a model. Real strand separation also depends on other conditions.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Writing a complement without reversing it, so the 5′ and 3′ labels are wrong.
2. Saying hydrogen bonds link nucleotides within one strand.
3. Pairing A with G, or putting T in RNA.
4. Applying A = T to single-stranded DNA or RNA.
5. Forgetting that DNA and RNA differ in sugar as well as base and strand number.

## Quick self-check

1. Complement of 5′-GGATC-3′, written 5′ to 3′? *(5′-GATCC-3′)*
2. Double-stranded DNA is 40% C. Percentage of A? *(G = 40%, so A + T = 20% and A = 10%)*
3. Why can a nucleotide not be added to the 5′ end? *(There is no free 3′ –OH there for the new nucleotide's phosphate to bond to.)*
