## Recap

- An enzyme's shape is held by weak interactions: **hydrogen bonds**, ionic interactions and hydrophobic interactions. Disturb them and the active site changes.
- **Denaturation**: loss of the working 3D shape, so the enzyme can no longer catalyse. Peptide bonds are not broken. Causes: heat, pH, chemicals.
- **Temperature**: faster molecules give more collisions, so the rate rises up to the **optimum**; above it, bonds in the enzyme break and the rate falls steeply.
- **pH**: changes the charges on R groups, disrupting the fold and the active site's charge pattern. Each enzyme has its own optimum pH.
- Denaturation is **sometimes reversible** (small pH changes, removal of a chemical). Strong heating usually is not. Cold slows enzymes without denaturing them.
- **Relative concentrations** of substrate and product set the direction and efficiency of the net reaction. Removing product keeps the forward reaction going.
- **Inhibitors** bind reversibly: **competitive** at the active site; **noncompetitive** at an **allosteric site**.

## Key relationships

| Change | Effect on rate | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| temperature up, below optimum | rises | more frequent, more energetic collisions |
| temperature up, above optimum | falls steeply | hydrogen bonds break; active site changes shape |
| pH away from optimum | falls | R-group charges change; fold and active site disrupted |
| more substrate (low levels) | rises | more active sites occupied |
| more substrate (high levels) | levels off | enzyme saturated; all active sites busy |
| product builds up | net forward rate falls | reverse reaction becomes more frequent |
| competitive inhibitor | falls, but recovers at high substrate | substrate outcompetes inhibitor for the active site |
| noncompetitive inhibitor | falls at all substrate levels | allosteric binding changes the active site |

| Calculation | Formula |
|---|---|
| **percentage change** | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 |
| **percentage of control** | inhibited rate ÷ uninhibited rate × 100 |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Rates compared across conditions are **initial rates**, measured before substrate runs low.
- Only one factor changes at a time; the others are held constant.
- An optimum read from widely spaced points is only an estimate.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying enzymes are "killed" by heat.
2. Saying denaturation breaks peptide bonds.
3. Saying cold denatures enzymes.
4. Explaining the plateau at high substrate as "the enzyme is used up".
5. Saying a noncompetitive inhibitor binds the active site.
6. Dividing by the new value, not the old one, in a percentage change.

## Quick self-check

1. With an inhibitor, the rate falls from 12.0 to 3.0 µmol min⁻¹. What percentage of normal is this? *(3.0 ÷ 12.0 × 100 = 25%)*
2. Adding lots of substrate restores the rate. Which type of inhibitor? *(Competitive)*
3. Why does pH 9 lower the activity of an enzyme with an optimum of pH 2? *(R-group charges change, disrupting hydrogen bonds and ionic interactions; the active site loses its shape and charge pattern.)*
