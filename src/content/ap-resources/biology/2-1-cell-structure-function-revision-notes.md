## Recap

- **Ribosomes**: rRNA and protein, no membrane, found in all cells. They make proteins by reading mRNA. Free ribosomes make proteins for the cytosol; ribosomes on rough ER make proteins for export, membranes and lysosomes.
- **Endomembrane system**: nuclear envelope, ER, Golgi, lysosomes, vacuoles, transport vesicles and plasma membrane. Together they modify, package and transport proteins, lipids and polysaccharides.
- **ER**: gives mechanical support and a route for transport inside the cell. Rough ER = bound ribosomes, protein synthesis, compartmentalization. Smooth ER = lipid synthesis and detoxification.
- **Golgi**: stack of flattened sacs; checks folding, chemically modifies (e.g. adds sugar chains: glycosylation) and packages products for their destinations.
- **Lysosomes**: sacs of hydrolytic enzymes; digest and recycle material; help carry out apoptosis (programmed cell death).
- **Vacuoles**: plants have one large central vacuole that stores water and nutrients and keeps turgor pressure; animal cells have smaller, more numerous storage vacuoles.
- **Mitochondria**: double membrane; smooth outer, folded inner (cristae); two compartments for aerobic respiration.
- **Chloroplasts**: double membrane; in plants and photosynthetic algae; site of photosynthesis.

## Key relationships

| Structure | Feature | Why it helps the cell |
|---|---|---|
| Rough ER | ribosomes on membrane | new export proteins go straight into a separate compartment |
| Golgi | flattened stack with entry and exit faces | products are finished and sorted in order |
| Lysosome | membrane around hydrolytic enzymes | digestion happens without harming the rest of the cell |
| Mitochondrion | folded inner membrane | more area for ATP-making proteins |
| Double membranes | two compartments | different reactions kept apart |
| Plant central vacuole | large water store | turgor pressure supports the plant |

## Assumptions behind claims from data

- A large volume of an organelle **suggests** a heavy use of its function; it does not prove it.
- In a pulse-chase experiment, only molecules made during the pulse carry the label, so the label tracks one batch along the route.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Calling ribosomes membrane-bound or "eukaryote only".
2. Saying the Golgi makes proteins.
3. Saying proteins cross membranes between ER and Golgi; vesicles carry them.
4. Saying cristae hold more fluid; they add membrane area.
5. Forgetting that plant cells have mitochondria as well as chloroplasts.
6. Listing an organelle without linking its structure to its function.

## Quick self-check

1. A cell's mitochondria have an inner membrane area of 30 µm² and an outer area of 10 µm². What does the ratio of 3 tell you? *(The inner membrane is folded, giving three times the outer area for ATP-making proteins.)*
2. A secreted protein is radioactively labelled. Where is the label first, and where last? *(First in the rough ER; last outside the cell, after Golgi and secretory vesicles.)*
3. Why does a wilting plant cell have a smaller central vacuole? *(It has lost water, so turgor pressure has fallen.)*
