## Recap

- **Density** = individuals per unit area or volume. Resources set how dense a population can become; density sets how much of each resource each individual gets.
- **Carrying capacity (K)** is the sustainable population size supported by the ecosystem's total available resources. It is set by the limiting resource and changes when the environment changes.
- As density rises, each individual gets less food, water, light or space, so per capita births fall and per capita deaths rise.
- **Density-dependent factors**: competition, predation, disease and parasites, waste build-up, territorial stress. They can hold N near K.
- **Density-independent factors**: frost, storms, floods, drought, fire. They remove about the same fraction at any density and cannot hold N steady.
- With these limits, growth usually becomes **logistic**: S-shaped, levelling off at K.

## Key relationships

| Equation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **dN/dt = rₘₐₓN(K − N)/K** | logistic growth; (K − N)/K = unused share of K |
| per capita rate = rₘₐₓ(K − N)/K | falls steadily as N rises |
| maximum dN/dt = rₘₐₓK/4 | at N = K/2 |

| Population size | (K − N)/K | Growth |
|---|---|---|
| N much less than K | close to 1 | nearly exponential |
| N = K/2 | 0.5 | fastest |
| N = K | 0 | zero (births balance deaths) |
| N > K | negative | population falls |

## Calculation tips

- **Mean** = sum ÷ number of values. **Percentage change** = (new − original) ÷ original × 100.
- **Ratio**: divide one value by the other and write it as "x : 1".
- In the logistic equation, work out (K − N)/K first, then multiply by rₘₐₓN.

## Assumptions behind the model

- K and rₘₐₓ are constant; no immigration or emigration.
- Births and deaths respond instantly to density (real populations often overshoot or oscillate).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying growth is fastest near K (it is fastest at K/2).
2. Classifying a factor by the **number** killed instead of the **fraction**.
3. Dividing by N instead of K in the logistic term.
4. Saying births and deaths stop at K.
5. Treating K as fixed for a species.

## Quick self-check

1. rₘₐₓ = 0.5 per month, K = 400, N = 100. dN/dt? *(0.5 × 100 × 300 ÷ 400 = 37.5 per month)*
2. At which N is growth fastest when K = 400? *(200)*
3. A frost kills 20% of a population at every density. Which type of factor? *(Density-independent.)*
