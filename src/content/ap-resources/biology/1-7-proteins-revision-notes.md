## Recap

- Proteins are linear chains of **amino acids** joined by covalent **peptide bonds**.
- Every amino acid has a **central carbon** bonded to an **H**, an **amine group (−NH₂)**, a **carboxyl group (−COOH)** and a variable **R group**. You do not need the structures of particular amino acids.
- R groups are **nonpolar (hydrophobic)**, **polar (hydrophilic)** or **ionic (charged)**. Their interactions decide the structure and function of each region of the protein.
- A peptide bond forms by **dehydration synthesis** between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amine group of the next. Hydrolysis breaks it.

## Key relationships

| Level | What it is | What holds it together |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | sequence of amino acids | peptide bonds (covalent) |
| **Secondary** | α-helices and β-pleated sheets | hydrogen bonds between **backbone** atoms (C=O and N–H) |
| **Tertiary** | 3D shape of one chain | **R-group** interactions: hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic interactions, ionic interactions, disulfide bridges (covalent S–S) |
| **Quaternary** | two or more chains together | the same interactions, between chains |

| Rule | Use |
|---|---|
| **peptide bonds = water released = n − 1 per chain** | for several chains: total amino acids − number of chains |
| **mass of protein = (amino acids × mean mass) − (bonds × 18 Da)** | estimating protein mass |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Mean amino acid masses are averages; real proteins differ.
- The mass lost when a disulfide bridge forms (about 2 Da) is ignored.
- A small experiment (few trials) **supports** a prediction about structure; it does not prove it.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying amino acids join through their R groups.
2. Calling a peptide bond a hydrogen bond.
3. Saying secondary structure comes from R groups.
4. Giving every protein a quaternary structure.
5. Counting n peptide bonds instead of n − 1, or subtracting 1 only once for several chains.
6. Saying denaturation breaks peptide bonds.
7. Predicting an effect without the full chain: change in R group → interaction gained or lost → shape → function.

## Quick self-check

1. How many water molecules are released when a chain of 50 amino acids forms? *(49)*
2. A protein has three chains of 40 amino acids each. How many peptide bonds? *(3 × 39 = 117)*
3. A buried nonpolar R group is replaced by an ionic one. Predict the effect. *(The charged group is trapped away from water and the hydrophobic core is disrupted, so the tertiary structure is likely to change and the protein may lose function.)*
