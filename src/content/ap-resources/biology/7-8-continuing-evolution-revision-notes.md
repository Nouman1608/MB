Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, figures and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/7-8-continuing-evolution-study-guide/).

## Recap

- **All species have evolved and are still evolving**, humans included. Populations evolve; individuals do not.
- Evolution continues because new mutations arise each generation, environments change, other species (hosts, parasites, predators) evolve too, and drift acts in every population.
- Fastest change: **large populations with short generations** (bacteria, viruses, tumour cells, insects, weeds).

## Key relationships

| Line of evidence | What you see |
|---|---|
| Genomic change over time | new mutations every generation; long-term lab populations of bacteria keep changing and can gain new abilities |
| Continuous fossil change | a measurable trait shifts gradually through successive rock layers |
| Resistance | antibiotics, pesticides, herbicides and chemotherapy drugs stop working as resistant variants spread |
| Emergent diseases | pathogens jump to new hosts, escape immunity, gain resistance or swap genes |

| Calculation | How |
|---|---|
| New mutations per generation in a population | genome size × rate per base pair × number of cells |
| Expected mutants for one trait | population size × mutation rate for that trait |
| Chance of two independent mutations in one cell | multiply the two rates |
| Frequency of resistant cells | resistant ÷ (resistant + susceptible) |

## How resistance spreads

1. Variation exists **before** the chemical is used.
2. The chemical kills susceptible individuals.
3. Resistant survivors reproduce and pass on resistance alleles (bacteria may also transfer them).
4. Repeated use makes the resistant type common.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying the chemical **causes** resistance mutations.
2. Saying an individual organism evolves or adapts genetically during its life.
3. Treating a single field comparison as proof; look for a baseline, replication and controls.
4. Forgetting that wild-caught organisms may differ because of their environment; rear offspring under common conditions to test inheritance.
5. Calling evolution "progress": it is change that suits the current environment.

## Quick self-check

1. Why do combination treatments slow the spread of resistance? *(A cell needs two independent mutations; the chance is the product of two small rates, so such cells are very unlikely to exist at the start.)*
2. A population of 3 × 10⁸ cells; resistance mutation rate 2 × 10⁻⁸ per cell. Expected resistant cells? *(3 × 10⁸ × 2 × 10⁻⁸ = 6)*
3. Why do flu vaccines need updating? *(The virus's surface proteins keep changing, so antibodies to older forms bind less well.)*
