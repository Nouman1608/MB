## Recap

- The plasma membrane separates the cytosol from the extracellular fluid and keeps the two different.
- Its framework is a **phospholipid bilayer**. Polar, hydrophilic **phosphate heads** face the watery fluid inside and outside the cell. Nonpolar, hydrophobic **fatty acid tails** face each other in the middle.
- The bilayer forms by itself: it is the most stable arrangement in water. No energy from the cell is needed.
- **Membrane proteins** can be hydrophilic, hydrophobic or both. Their **hydrophobic regions** (nonpolar R groups) form the surface that touches the tails. Their **hydrophilic regions** (polar and charged R groups) face the cytosol or outside fluid, or lie inside the protein, for example lining a pore.
- **Integral** proteins pass through the bilayer; **peripheral** proteins sit on one surface.
- **Fluid mosaic model:** phospholipids with proteins, steroids such as cholesterol (vertebrate animals), glycoproteins and glycolipids set into them. All can move sideways within the membrane.

## Key relationships

| Component | Where it sits | Job |
|---|---|---|
| phospholipids | two layers, heads out, tails in | barrier: the hydrophobic interior blocks ions and polar molecules |
| integral proteins | through the bilayer | channels, carriers, receptors |
| peripheral proteins | on one face | enzymes, signalling, links to the cytoskeleton |
| cholesterol (vertebrate animals) | among the tails | keeps the membrane stable as temperature changes |
| glycoproteins, glycolipids | carbohydrate chains on the outer face | cell recognition and adhesion |

| Calculation | Use |
|---|---|
| **helix length = number of amino acids × 0.15 nm** | about 20 or more nonpolar amino acids are needed to cross a 3 to 4 nm hydrophobic interior |

## Assumptions behind diagrams and numbers

- Membrane diagrams are **not to scale** and show a moving structure frozen at one instant.
- The 0.15 nm rule assumes the segment is an α-helix running straight across the membrane.
- A single experiment (one run, no repeats) can **support** an idea about fluidity but cannot settle it.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Drawing tails facing the water or a single layer of phospholipids.
2. Saying a whole membrane protein is hydrophobic.
3. Putting carbohydrate chains on the cytosol side.
4. Calling a protein integral when it only touches one surface.
5. Saying "fluid" means the membrane has no fixed shape; it means components drift sideways.
6. Saying cells must spend energy to keep the bilayer together.

## Quick self-check

1. Which part of a phospholipid faces the cytosol? *(The polar phosphate head of the inner layer.)*
2. How long is a helix of 18 amino acids? *(18 × 0.15 = 2.7 nm, a little short of a 3 to 4 nm interior.)*
3. How can you tell the outside face of a membrane in a diagram? *(The carbohydrate chains of glycoproteins and glycolipids point outwards.)*
4. What does a lack of recovery after photobleaching in fixed cells show? *(It is a control: when movement is blocked, there is no recovery, so recovery in living cells is caused by movement.)*
