Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/8-6-biodiversity-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/biology/8-6-biodiversity-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can name the three levels of biodiversity and give an example of each. *(Guide: "Why diversity matters")*
- I can explain the difference between resistance and resilience. *(Guide: "Diversity and resilience"; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why ecosystems with few components, or little variety among them, are often less resilient, for natural and artificial ecosystems. *(Guide: "Diversity and resilience"; Practice Q1)*
- I can describe how producers, keystone species and essential biotic and abiotic factors maintain diversity. *(Guide: "What keeps an ecosystem diverse"; Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can define a keystone species and describe a real example. *(Guide: "Keystone species"; Practice Q2)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can calculate the percentage fall and the percentage recovery of an ecosystem after a disturbance. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can use removal data to identify a keystone species from its effect relative to its abundance. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2)*
- I can read a line graph comparing several communities and describe which is most resilient. *(Guide: Figure 1)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict the short-term and long-term effects of removing a keystone species, a producer or an abiotic factor, step by step. *(Guide: "Adding or removing a component"; Practice Q3, Q5, Q6)*
- I can explain how a predator can change plant diversity indirectly, through its prey. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*
- I can evaluate whether data from a natural change prove a cause, and suggest a better test. *(Practice Q7)*
