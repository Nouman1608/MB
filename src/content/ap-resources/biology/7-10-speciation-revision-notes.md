## Recap

- **Biological species concept**: a species is a group that can interbreed in nature and produce **viable, fertile** offspring. It applies to sexually reproducing organisms, not to asexual ones or fossils.
- A new species forms when populations become **reproductively isolated**: no gene flow, so mutation, selection and drift change each gene pool separately.
- **Allopatric** speciation needs geographic isolation. **Sympatric** speciation happens in the same area, for example by **polyploidy** (plants) or a **host shift** (apple maggot fly, hawthorn to apple).
- **Gradualism**: slow, steady change over very long times. **Punctuated equilibrium**: long stasis, then rapid change.
- **Divergent evolution**: related populations adapt to different habitats and spread apart; fastest in **adaptive radiation** when new habitats open. **Convergent evolution**: similar pressures give similar traits in unrelated lineages (Caribbean *Anolis* ecomorphs).

## Key relationships

| Barrier | When it acts | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Habitat | pre-zygotic | live in different parts of the same area |
| Temporal | pre-zygotic | breed in different seasons or times of day |
| Behavioural | pre-zygotic | courtship songs or displays not recognised |
| Mechanical | pre-zygotic | reproductive parts do not fit |
| Gametic | pre-zygotic (after mating) | sperm cannot fuse with egg |
| Reduced hybrid viability | post-zygotic | hybrids die young |
| Reduced hybrid fertility | post-zygotic | hybrids sterile (mule: 32 + 31 = 63 chromosomes) |
| Hybrid breakdown | post-zygotic | hybrids fertile, their offspring weak or sterile |

| Polyploidy check | Working |
|---|---|
| Tetraploid × diploid | gametes 2 sets + 1 set = 3 sets (triploid), usually sterile |
| Tetraploid × tetraploid | 2 sets + 2 sets = 4 sets, fertile |

## Assumptions behind the conclusions

- A species boundary is judged by **gene flow in nature**. Hybrids made only in a laboratory do not show that two groups are one species.
- Isolation can be partial. Speciation is a process, so some populations are "in between".

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying two groups are one species just because they can mate or make hybrids.
2. Calling gametic isolation post-zygotic (it acts before the zygote forms).
3. Saying sympatric speciation needs a barrier.
4. Treating "rapid" in punctuated equilibrium as overnight.
5. Saying convergent species share a recent common ancestor.
6. Saying individuals, rather than populations, become new species.

## Quick self-check

1. Two moth species fly on the same nights but one is attracted only to its own pheromone. Barrier? *(Behavioural, pre-zygotic.)*
2. A diploid plant has 2n = 20. How many chromosomes in a triploid hybrid with a tetraploid of the same species? *(10 + 20 = 30.)*
3. Why are radiations fast on new islands? *(Many empty niches and few competitors, so divergent selection acts in many directions at once.)*
