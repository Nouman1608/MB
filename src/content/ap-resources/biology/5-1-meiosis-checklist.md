## Understanding

- I can explain why diploid organisms that reproduce sexually need meiosis, and how fertilization restores the diploid number. *(Guide: "Why sexual reproduction needs a halving division")*
- I can define chromosome, sister chromatid, homologous chromosome, haploid and diploid, and tell homologues apart from sister chromatids. *(Guide: "The vocabulary you need first"; Practice Q3)*
- I can describe what happens to the chromosomes, spindle and nuclear envelope in prophase I, metaphase I, anaphase I and telophase I. *(Guide: "Meiosis I"; Practice Q1)*
- I can describe what happens in prophase II, metaphase II, anaphase II and telophase II, including what holds sister chromatids together until anaphase II. *(Guide: "Meiosis II"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why the cells at the end of meiosis I are already haploid even though their chromosomes still have two chromatids. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can state the number of cells, chromosomes, chromatids and relative DNA content at any stage of meiosis for a given 2n. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can read a DNA-content graph or table and link each change to S phase, cytokinesis I or cytokinesis II. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate percentages from counts of cells in different classes. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can identify mitosis, meiosis I or meiosis II in a diagram from pairing, chromosome number and chromatid number. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can give similarities and differences between the processes and outcomes of mitosis and meiosis. *(Guide: "Mitosis and meiosis compared"; Practice Q6)*
- I can predict what happens if a step of meiosis, such as the separation of sister chromatids, is blocked. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why the four products of meiosis are not identical to the parent cell. *(Guide: "Mitosis and meiosis compared")*
