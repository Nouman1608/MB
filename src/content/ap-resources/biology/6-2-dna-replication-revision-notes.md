## Recap

- Replication ensures each daughter cell receives the same hereditary information.
- It is **semiconservative**: the parent strands separate, each acts as a template, and each new molecule keeps one parent strand.
- Evidence (Meselson and Stahl, 1958): after one round in a new isotope, all DNA was intermediate (not conservative); after two rounds, two bands appeared (not dispersive).
- **DNA polymerase** adds nucleotides only to a 3′ end, so DNA is made **5′ → 3′**. It cannot start a chain, so it needs an **RNA primer**.
- Replication starts at an **origin**, which opens two forks moving apart. Bacteria typically have one origin; each eukaryotic chromosome has many.

## Key relationships

| Enzyme or part | Where | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Topoisomerase | ahead of the fork | relieves supercoiling (overwinding) |
| Helicase | at the fork | separates strands by breaking hydrogen bonds between bases |
| RNA primer | start of each new strand or fragment | provides a 3′ end to build on |
| DNA polymerase | behind the fork | adds complementary nucleotides to the 3′ end; also replaces primers with DNA |
| Ligase | lagging strand | seals the backbone between fragments |

| Strand | Direction relative to the fork | Synthesis | Primers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leading | towards the fork | continuous | one per fork |
| Lagging | away from the fork | discontinuous, in fragments | one per fragment |

| Model | After round 1 | After round 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Conservative | old and new bands, 50 : 50 | old 25%, new 75% |
| Semiconservative | all hybrid | hybrid 50%, all-new 50% |
| Dispersive | one hybrid band | one band, shifted further |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Band predictions assume every molecule replicates exactly once per round.
- Replication-time calculations assume constant fork speed, two forks per origin and all origins starting together.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying the lagging strand is made 3′ → 5′.
2. Saying helicase breaks covalent bonds, or that topoisomerase acts behind the fork.
3. Forgetting that DNA polymerase needs a primer.
4. Saying ligase forms hydrogen bonds.
5. Writing a new strand without reversing it to read 5′ → 3′.
6. Confusing "fraction of molecules containing an old strand" with "fraction of strands that are old".

## Quick self-check

1. A template strand reads 5′-ATTGCC-3′. Write the new strand 5′ → 3′. *(5′-GGCAAT-3′)*
2. DNA with both strands labelled replicates three times in unlabelled medium. What percentage of molecules contain a labelled strand? *(8 molecules, 2 contain one labelled strand: 25%)*
3. A drug blocks ligase. Which strand is affected, and how? *(The lagging strand stays as separate fragments; the leading strand is largely unaffected.)*
