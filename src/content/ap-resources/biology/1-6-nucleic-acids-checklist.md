Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/1-6-nucleic-acids-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/biology/1-6-nucleic-acids-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can name the three parts of a nucleotide and say which carbon of the sugar carries the phosphate and which carries the base. *(Guide: "The nucleotide: three parts", Figure 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why the information in a nucleic acid is carried by the order of its bases, not by the backbone. *(Guide: "Information written in a chemical alphabet")*
- I can explain what the 5′ and 3′ ends of a strand are and why new nucleotides are added only to the 3′ end. *(Guide: "Building a strand"; Practice Q7)*
- I can describe DNA as an antiparallel double helix, with A–T and C–G pairs held by hydrogen bonds and backbones held by covalent bonds. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can state three structural differences between DNA and RNA. *(Guide: "DNA and RNA compared"; Practice Q1)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can write a complementary DNA or RNA sequence from 5′ to 3′ with both ends labelled. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q7)*
- I can find all four base percentages in double-stranded DNA from one of them. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can count hydrogen bonds between strands and covalent links within a strand. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6, Q7)*
- I can identify the parts and the 5′ and 3′ ends on a diagram of a strand, and say whether it shows DNA or RNA. *(Guide: Figures 1 and 2; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can use base composition data to decide whether a sample is DNA or RNA and whether it is likely to be single- or double-stranded. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why strands rich in C–G pairs need more energy to separate, and why heating does not break the backbone. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
- I can predict the effect of a nucleotide that lacks a 3′ –OH on the synthesis of a new strand. *(Practice Q7)*
