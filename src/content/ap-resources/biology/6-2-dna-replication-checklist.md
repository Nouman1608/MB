Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/6-2-dna-replication-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/biology/6-2-dna-replication-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what semiconservative replication means and sketch it for two rounds. *(Guide: "Semiconservative replication", Figure 1)*
- I can explain why new DNA is always made 5′ → 3′ and why DNA polymerase needs an RNA primer. *(Guide: "The direction rule"; Practice Q7)*
- I can state the job and position of helicase, topoisomerase, DNA polymerase and ligase. *(Guide: "At the replication fork"; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can explain why the leading strand is continuous and the lagging strand is made in fragments. *(Guide: "Leading and lagging strands", Figure 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can describe what happens to RNA primers and how the lagging strand is completed. *(Guide: "Leading and lagging strands")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can write the new strand made on a template, 5′ to 3′, and say which new strand at a fork is leading. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can predict the density bands, or the percentage of labelled molecules, for each replication model. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2)*
- I can calculate replication time from fork speed, genome size and number of origins, remembering two forks per origin. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how density data rule out the conservative and dispersive models. *(Guide: "The evidence"; Worked example 2)*
- I can identify a defective enzyme from experimental observations and justify my answer. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can predict the effect of blocking primers, ligase or topoisomerase on each strand. *(Practice Q4, Q5, Q7)*
- I can explain why eukaryotic chromosomes need many origins of replication. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
