Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/biology/6-6-gene-expression-cell-specialization-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/biology/6-6-gene-expression-cell-specialization-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why cells with the same genome make different proteins, using the term differential gene expression. *(Guide: "One genome, many kinds of cell"; Practice Q1)*
- I can describe how general transcription factors and RNA polymerase assemble at a promoter. *(Guide: "Starting transcription at a promoter")*
- I can explain how activators on an enhancer, far upstream or downstream, raise the rate of transcription through DNA looping. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain how a repressor bound to DNA blocks transcription. *(Guide: "Repressors: turning transcription down"; Practice Q5)*
- I can describe how miRNAs and siRNAs reduce gene expression after transcription, without changing the DNA. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q3, Q6)*

## Skills

- I can use reporter-gene deletion data to identify a promoter, an enhancer and a silencer, with percentages. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can compare protein and mRNA changes to decide whether a small RNA blocks translation, causes mRNA breakdown, or both. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can predict which genes a cell type expresses from the combination of activators and repressors it contains. *(Guide: "Combinations of transcription factors"; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can make a claim about how a gene is regulated and support it with two pieces of evidence and a mechanism. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q5, Q6)*
- I can explain the purpose of a control group in an RNA-interference experiment. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can explain how a change in an enhancer can alter the phenotype in one tissue only, and contrast it with a change in the coding sequence. *(Guide: "From regulation to phenotype"; Practice Q7)*
