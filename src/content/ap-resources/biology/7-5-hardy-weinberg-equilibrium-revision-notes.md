## Recap

- The **Hardy–Weinberg model** predicts allele and genotype frequencies in a population that is **not evolving**.
- **p** and **q** are the frequencies of two alleles of one gene. They are proportions (0 to 1) of all the alleles in the population.
- If gametes combine at random, genotype frequencies are **p²**, **2pq** and **q²**.
- Five conditions keep frequencies constant: **large population, no migration, no new mutations, random mating, no natural selection**.
- No real population meets all five. Use the prediction as a **null hypothesis**: a clear mismatch means at least one condition is broken.

## Key relationships

| Equation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **p + q = 1** | all alleles of the gene; true for any population with two alleles |
| **p² + 2pq + q² = 1** | genotype frequencies AA, Aa, aa under equilibrium |
| **p = (2 × AA + Aa) ÷ 2N** | counting alleles; N = number of individuals |
| **q = √(q²)** | from the recessive phenotype; assumes equilibrium |
| **χ² = Σ (o − e)² ÷ e** | tests observed genotype numbers against expected |

| Condition broken | Force acting | Main effect |
|---|---|---|
| large population | genetic drift | random allele-frequency change |
| no migration | gene flow | alleles added or removed |
| no new mutations | mutation | new alleles appear |
| random mating | inbreeding or mate choice | genotype frequencies shift (fewer heterozygotes with inbreeding) |
| no natural selection | selection | fitter alleles rise in frequency |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- One gene, two alleles, diploid organisms, sexual reproduction.
- √(q²) is only valid if the population is in equilibrium. If every genotype can be seen, count alleles instead.
- When expected numbers use an allele frequency known in advance, chi-square df = 3 − 1 = 2 (critical value 5.99 at p = 0.05).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Writing pq instead of **2pq** for heterozygotes.
2. Treating q² (recessive phenotype) as q.
3. Saying dominant alleles become more common on their own.
4. Thinking equilibrium means p = q = 0.5.
5. Saying inbreeding alone changes allele frequencies.
6. Saying "fail to reject" proves the population is not evolving.

## Quick self-check

1. 16% of a population shows a recessive phenotype. Find q, p and the carrier frequency. *(q = √0.16 = 0.4; p = 0.6; 2pq = 0.48)*
2. A sample has AA 45, Aa 40, aa 15. Find p by counting. *((2 × 45 + 40) ÷ 200 = 130 ÷ 200 = 0.65)*
3. Which condition does a tiny island population most clearly break? *(Large population size: drift will act.)*
