## Understanding

- I can define monomer, polymer and polymerization, and say which groups of macromolecules are polymers and why lipids are the exception. *(Guide: "Big molecules from small parts")*
- I can describe dehydration synthesis: which monomer loses H, which loses –OH, what forms and what is released. *(Guide: "Dehydration synthesis"; Practice Q1)*
- I can describe hydrolysis, including where the H and the –OH from water end up. *(Guide: "Hydrolysis"; Practice Q4)*
- I can classify a biological event, such as digestion or glycogen storage, as hydrolysis or dehydration synthesis. *(Guide: "Comparing the two reactions"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why the links between monomers are covalent bonds, not hydrogen bonds. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can use the n − 1 rule to count bonds and water molecules in a straight or branched chain. *(Guide: Figure 2, Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can calculate the mass of a polymer, or the number of monomers in it, from molar masses. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate a rate and a percentage from hydrolysis data. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can describe a diagram of these reactions: its direction, where the water is and how many bonds change. *(Guide: "Reading diagrams of these reactions", Figure 1; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can track a labelled atom through dehydration synthesis or hydrolysis and say where it ends up. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can explain the role of control tubes in an enzyme hydrolysis experiment. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can use a mass balance to explain why the products of hydrolysis weigh more than the polymer, without claiming mass is created. *(Practice Q5, Q7)*
