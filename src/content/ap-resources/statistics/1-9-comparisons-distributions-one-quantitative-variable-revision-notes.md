## Recap

- Compare distributions of the **same variable** for two or more groups, drawn on the **same scale**.
- A full comparison covers **shape, centre, variability** and **unusual features** (outliers, clusters, gaps).
- Each sentence needs a **comparative word**, **values for both groups**, **units** and **context**.
- Any summary statistic can be used as evidence. For skewed data or outliers, prefer the median and IQR.
- A **z-score** tells you how many standard deviations a value is above (+) or below (−) the mean.

## Key relationships

| Idea | What to remember |
|---|---|
| Dot plots, back-to-back stemplots, histograms | Show centre, variability, shape, outliers, clusters and gaps |
| Parallel boxplots | Show centre, variability, outliers and skewness or symmetry; **hide** clusters, gaps and n |
| Groups of different sizes | Compare relative frequencies (proportions), not counts |
| Skewness on a boxplot | Median near one end of the box and a long whisker on the other side |
| z-score with parameters | z = (x − μ) / σ |
| z-score from a sample | z = (x − x̄) / s |
| Value from a z-score | x = μ + zσ |
| Units of z | None, so values on different scales can be compared |

## Assumptions and conventions

- The groups measure the same variable in the same units.
- Use μ and σ when they are given for the whole population; otherwise use x̄ and s.
- A larger z-score means "further above the mean", which is not always "better". For times, a lower value (more negative z) can be the better result.
- A difference between groups describes **these** data. Whether it generalises, or shows cause, depends on how the data were collected.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Listing** two sets of statistics without saying which is greater.
2. **No context or units** ("A is bigger than B").
3. **Reading box width as sample size.** A wider box means more spread.
4. **Claiming a boxplot shows no gaps.** It cannot show gaps at all.
5. **Comparing counts** for groups of different sizes.
6. **Sign errors** in z: always calculate x − μ, not μ − x.
7. **Comparing raw values** from different scales instead of z-scores.

## Quick self-check

1. Population mean 50 kg, σ = 4 kg. What is the z-score of 61 kg? *(z = 11 ÷ 4 = 2.75)*
2. μ = 200 points, σ = 20 points. Which value has z = −1.5? *(x = 200 − 1.5 × 20 = 170 points)*
3. Sana scored 78 in a quiz with mean 70 and σ = 4, and 88 in a test with mean 75 and σ = 10. Where did she do relatively better? *(Quiz: z = 2.00 against 1.30 in the test)*
4. Name two features a boxplot cannot show. *(Any two of: clusters, gaps, number of values)*
