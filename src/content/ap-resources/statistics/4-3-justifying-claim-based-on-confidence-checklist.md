## Understanding

- I can explain why one calculated t-interval may or may not contain the population mean, and why the widths of t-intervals vary. *(Guide: "What a t-interval does and does not tell you"; Figure 1)*
- I can explain why "there is a 95% probability that μ is in this interval" is wrong. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why a plausible value is not a proved value, and why "0 is in the interval" does not mean "no difference". *(Guide: "Using an interval to justify a claim"; Practice Q3)*

## Skills

- I can interpret a t-interval for μ in context, with the level, both endpoints, units and the population. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can interpret a t-interval for μd, stating the order of subtraction. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*
- I can interpret a confidence level as the long-run capture rate in repeated random samples of the same size. *(Guide: "Interpreting the confidence level"; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can predict how raising or lowering the confidence level changes t*, the margin of error and the width. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can predict how changing n changes the standard error, t* and the width, using the 1/√n rule. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4)*
- I can recover x̄, the margin of error and s from a reported interval. *(Practice Q6, Q7(d))*

## Reasoning

- I can decide whether an interval gives convincing evidence for or against a claimed value of μ. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(d))*
- I can use 0 and the sign of a matched-pairs interval to judge a claim of a difference, in context. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q7(c))*
- I can explain why the same data can support different conclusions at different confidence levels. *(Guide: Worked example 1 check; Practice Q6)*
