Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/4-7-constructing-confidence-interval-difference-between-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/4-7-constructing-confidence-interval-difference-between-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can tell when two samples are independent and when the data are paired, and choose the right interval for each. *(Guide: Step 1 and Worked example 3; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why the critical value comes from a t-distribution, not the normal distribution. *(Guide: Step 3; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain what each of the three conditions protects. *(Guide: Step 2)*
- I can explain why the 10% condition is not checked in a randomized experiment. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*

## Skills

- I can define μ₁ − μ₂ in context, with the order of subtraction, the response variable and both populations or treatments. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q5, Q6)*
- I can check the randomization, 10% and sample data conditions for both groups. *(Guide: Step 2; Practice Q3, Q5)*
- I can check small samples for strong skewness and outliers using a dot plot or the 1.5 × IQR rule. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can calculate the standard error √(s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂) without adding standard deviations. *(Practice Q2, Q4)*
- I can find t* with technology df or with the conservative df, and state which I used. *(Guide: Step 3; Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate the margin of error and the interval, with units. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can say why a conservative df gives a slightly wider interval and why n₁ + n₂ − 2 is not conservative. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why large samples (both n ≥ 30) make skewed sample data acceptable. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can find and correct errors in someone else's two-sample interval. *(Practice Q7)*
