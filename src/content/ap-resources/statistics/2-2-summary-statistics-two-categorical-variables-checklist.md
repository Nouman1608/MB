Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/2-2-summary-statistics-two-categorical-variables-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/2-2-summary-statistics-two-categorical-variables-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between a joint, a marginal and a conditional relative frequency. *(Guide: "From pictures to numbers"; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why the same cell gives different conditional relative frequencies when I restrict to its row or to its column. *(Guide: "Conditional relative frequencies"; Practice Q2, Q7)*
- I can explain what a conditional distribution is and why it adds to 1. *(Guide: "Conditional distributions and association")*
- I can explain what "no association" means in terms of conditional distributions. *(Guide: "Conditional distributions and association"; Practice Q3)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate a joint relative frequency and a marginal relative frequency from a two-way table. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can find the right denominator for a conditional relative frequency from the wording of a question. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q4)*
- I can build a two-way table of counts from percentages, then calculate relative frequencies from it. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can use joint = marginal × conditional to check my work. *(Guide: "Conditional relative frequencies")*

## Reasoning

- I can compare conditional distributions to decide whether two categorical variables are associated, quoting values. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5, Q7)*
- I can spot and correct a claim that swaps the direction of a conditional relative frequency. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can judge "most", "more popular" and "more than its share" claims with the correct relative frequency. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why an association in observational data does not show cause and effect. *(Practice Q5, Q7)*
