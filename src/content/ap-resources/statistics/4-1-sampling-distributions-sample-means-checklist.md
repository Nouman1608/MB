## Understanding

- I can tell apart the population distribution, the distribution of one sample and the sampling distribution of x̄. *(Guide: "From proportions to means")*
- I can explain why x̄ is an unbiased estimator of μ and why sample means vary less than individual values. *(Guide: "Mean and standard deviation of x̄"; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain what each condition is for: randomization for independence and no bias, 10% for the standard deviation formula, and population shape or n ≥ 30 for a normal model. *(Guide: "The conditions"; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can explain why a strongly skewed population may need a sample much larger than 30. *(Guide: "The conditions")*

## Calculation

- I can calculate μx̄ and σx̄ = σ/√n for a given population and sample size. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can find how the sample size must change to reach a target σx̄, and round the answer up. *(Guide: Worked example 2(d); Practice Q3)*
- I can calculate a normal probability for x̄ by standardising with σ/√n. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can find a cut-off value for x̄ from a probability with invNorm. *(Practice Q7(d))*

## Reasoning

- I can decide, with numbers and context, whether a normal model for x̄ is appropriate, and name the condition that fails if it is not. *(Guide: Worked example 2(e); Practice Q6)*
- I can interpret μx̄, σx̄ and a probability about x̄ for the specific population and sample size. *(Guide: "Interpreting results in context"; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can use a small probability about x̄ to explain why a claimed population mean is doubtful, without calling it proof. *(Practice Q7(c))*
