Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/1-10-investigative-question-revisited-data-collection-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/1-10-investigative-question-revisited-data-collection-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can name the three components of an investigative question and say what each one guides. *(Guide: "The three components of an investigative question")*
- I can tell whether a question asks to estimate a parameter or to test a claim, and name the direction of the claim. *(Guide: "Component 2"; Practice Q6(e))*
- I can explain the difference between a census and a sample. *(Guide: "Who is measured? Census or sample"; Practice Q1, Q7(c))*
- I can explain why random selection affects generalising, while random assignment affects cause and effect. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q3)*

## Skills

- I can classify a study as an experiment or an observational study, and an observational study as prospective, retrospective or a survey. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can identify the experimental units, factors, levels, treatments and response variable in an experiment. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q5(b))*
- I can count the treatments when there is more than one factor by multiplying the numbers of levels. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q5(d))*
- I can write an investigative question with all three components for an estimate or for a test. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(e), Q7(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can describe a confounding variable and explain its link to both the explanatory and the response variable, in context. *(Guide: "Confounding variables"; Practice Q6(c))*
- I can justify which population a conclusion applies to, using how the units were chosen. *(Guide: Worked example 2(d); Practice Q5(c), Q6(d))*
- I can explain why volunteers, however many, limit the conclusion to individuals like them. *(Practice Q3, Q7(c))*
- I can decide whether a cause-and-effect conclusion is justified and explain why. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q3, Q6(c))*
