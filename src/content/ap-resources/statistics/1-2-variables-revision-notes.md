## Recap

- An **observational unit** is the item or individual a piece of data is about: a person, household, tree, day, photo, review…
- A **variable** is a characteristic that can differ from one observational unit to another.
- Data can come from forms, sensors, **photos, sounds, videos and text**. You record variables from them.
- A **parameter** summarises the variable for the whole **population**. It is usually unknown.
- A **statistic** summarises the variable for a **sample**. It is used to estimate the parameter and is usually not exactly equal to it.

## Key relationships

| Term | Values are… | Test | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Categorical (qualitative) | category names or group labels | an average would be meaningless | heating fuel; yes/no; postcode |
| Quantitative (numerical) | measured or counted amounts, usually with units | an average makes sense | mass (kg); number of siblings |
| Discrete quantitative | countable: 0, 1, 2, … (the list may be finite or go on for ever) | can you list the possible values? | goals scored; emails received |
| Continuous quantitative | any value in an interval | is there always a value between two values? | time (s); length (cm) |
| Parameter | a summary of the population | from every unit of interest? | proportion of all 1,250 households |
| Statistic | a summary of a sample | from only the units observed? | 9 ÷ 40 = 0.225 of sampled households |

## Assumptions and conventions

- Classify a variable by what the characteristic **is**, not by how a display rounds it.
- A number used only as a label (ID, postcode, jersey, phone number) is categorical.
- When age or another quantity is recorded in a rounded form, say how it is measured and give your reason.
- A row number or name that only identifies the unit is not a variable of interest.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Calling every number quantitative.** Labels written in digits are categorical.
2. **Assuming the units are people.** Ask "one row describes one what?"
3. **Confusing observational units with units of measure** (kg, s, kWh).
4. **"No maximum, so continuous."** Counts are discrete even with no upper limit.
5. **"Rounded, so discrete."** Measured times and lengths stay continuous.
6. **Deciding parameter or statistic by the type of summary** (mean, median, proportion). Only population versus sample matters.
7. **Treating sample-to-sample variation as an error.** Different random samples give different statistics.

## Quick self-check

1. A study records the daily rainfall (mm) at one weather station on each of 30 days. What are the observational units, and what type is the variable? *(The 30 days; continuous quantitative.)*
2. A park has 400 trees. A full survey finds 120 oaks. A sample of 20 trees contains 7 oaks. Which proportion is a parameter? *(120 ÷ 400 = 0.3 is the parameter; 7 ÷ 20 = 0.35 is a statistic.)*
3. Is "number of words in a customer review" discrete or continuous? *(Discrete: it is a count.)*
