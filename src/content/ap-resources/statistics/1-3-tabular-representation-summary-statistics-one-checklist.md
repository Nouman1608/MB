Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/1-3-tabular-representation-summary-statistics-one-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/1-3-tabular-representation-summary-statistics-one-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what a frequency table and a relative frequency table each show. *(Guide: "Frequency tables" and "Relative frequency tables")*
- I can explain why the relative frequencies of one categorical variable add to 1, and why a rounded total may be slightly off. *(Guide: "Rounding and totals that are not exactly 1"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why a mean or median makes no sense for a categorical variable, and say what to report instead. *(Guide: "Summarising a categorical variable")*
- I can explain why I need n to turn a relative frequency back into a count. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*

## Skills

- I can build a frequency table from a list of raw answers and check that the counts add to n. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can turn counts into proportions and percentages by dividing by n. *(Practice Q1, Q4, Q6)*
- I can find a missing relative frequency, the proportion for a group of categories, and the proportion "not" in a category. *(Practice Q2, Q4, Q5)*
- I can convert between a proportion, a percentage, a part-to-whole ratio and a part-to-rest ratio. *(Guide: "One piece of information, four forms"; Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can find counts from a relative frequency table when n is given. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5, Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can tell the difference between "the most common category" and "a majority", and use it to judge a claim. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can check a "twice as many" or "1 in 10" claim against the counts or proportions. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can write every conclusion in context, naming the category, the individuals and the group surveyed. *(All worked examples)*
