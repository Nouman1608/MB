Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/5-1-graphical-representations-between-two-quantitative-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/5-1-graphical-representations-between-two-quantitative-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what bivariate quantitative data are and why both values must come from the same individual. *(Guide: "From one variable to two")*
- I can explain the difference between an explanatory and a response variable, and why "explanatory" does not mean "cause". *(Guide: "Explanatory and response variables"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain what a positive or a negative association means, using "tends to". *(Guide: "Describing a scatterplot: four features"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why strength depends on scatter around the pattern, not on steepness. *(Guide: "Describing a scatterplot: four features"; Practice Q3, Q7)*

## Skills

- I can choose which variable goes on each axis in a new context. *(Guide: "Explanatory and response variables"; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can construct a scatterplot with labelled axes, units and equal scale steps, without joining the dots. *(Guide: "Constructing a scatterplot"; Practice Q4)*
- I can describe the form, direction and strength of an association, including a curved one. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q4, Q7)*
- I can identify unusual points and clusters and suggest a reason for them in context. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can use a scatterplot to decide whether a claim is supported, quoting evidence from the data. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
- I can use one counterexample to reject a claim that says "always" or "every". *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why an association shown in a scatterplot does not on its own show that one variable causes changes in the other. *(Guide: Worked example 3)*
- I can write every description in context, naming both variables and the individuals. *(All worked examples)*
