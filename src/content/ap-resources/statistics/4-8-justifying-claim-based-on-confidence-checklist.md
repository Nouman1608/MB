Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/4-8-justifying-claim-based-on-confidence-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/4-8-justifying-claim-based-on-confidence-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why one computed interval may or may not contain μ₁ − μ₂. *(Guide: "What a two-sample interval can and cannot tell you")*
- I can explain that the confidence level describes the method in repeated sampling, not one interval. *(Guide: "Interpreting the confidence level"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why an interval containing 0 does not show the means are equal. *(Guide: Common misconceptions; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain when a difference can be called cause and effect and when results generalise to a population. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*

## Skills

- I can interpret an interval for μ₁ − μ₂ with the level, limits, units, order of subtraction, response and both populations or treatments. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 3; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can interpret a confidence level in context. *(Guide: Worked example 1(b); Practice Q3)*
- I can rewrite an interval when the order of subtraction is reversed. *(Guide: Worked example 2(d); Practice Q4)*
- I can spot and correct faulty interpretations of an interval. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q1)*

## Reasoning

- I can use whether 0 lies in the interval to decide if there is convincing evidence of a difference, and in which direction. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2, Q5, Q6)*
- I can judge a claim about the size of a difference by comparing the claimed value with the whole interval. *(Guide: Worked examples 1(d) and 2(c); Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why intervals at different confidence levels from the same data can lead to different conclusions. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can finish every conclusion with a sentence in context that refers to the interval. *(All worked examples)*
