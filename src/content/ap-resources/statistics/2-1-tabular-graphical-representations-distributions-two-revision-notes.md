## Recap

- A **two-way (contingency) table** shows two categorical variables for the same individuals. Rows are the categories of one variable, columns the categories of the other.
- Each **cell** counts individuals in that row category **and** that column category. Row totals, column totals and the grand total sit at the edges.
- Cells can hold **counts** or **relative frequencies** (proportions).
- To compare groups, find the distribution of the response **within each group**: cell ÷ group total.
- Two variables are **associated** if the distribution of one changes across the categories of the other.

## Key relationships

| Display | What you see | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Two-way table | Exact counts and totals | Starting point for every calculation |
| Side-by-side bar chart | One cluster of bars per group, one bar per response category | Comparing categories; use proportions if group sizes differ |
| Segmented bar chart | One bar of height 1 per group, split into within-group proportions | Seeing at a glance whether distributions differ |
| Mosaic plot | Segmented bars whose **widths** show group sizes | Group sizes, within-group distributions and cell counts (areas) together |
| Mosaic rectangle | width × height = share of all individuals in that cell | Getting counts back: width × height × grand total |

## Assumptions and conventions

- Each individual is in exactly one category of each variable, so each group's proportions add to 1.
- Put the explanatory variable on the axis (one bar per group) and split by the response variable.
- Stack segments in the same order in every bar, and use patterns or labels, not colour alone.
- Small differences between sample proportions can happen by chance; describe their size.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Comparing counts** between groups of different sizes.
2. **Reading group sizes from a segmented bar chart.** Every bar is height 1.
3. **Ignoring column widths** in a mosaic plot. Width tells you group size.
4. **Mixing up directions.** "Proportion of App users who are under 30" is not "proportion of under-30s who use the App".
5. **Claiming cause** from a survey or other observational study.
6. **Totals that do not add up.** Check rows and columns against the grand total first.

## Quick self-check

1. Group A: 40 of 50 said Yes. Group B: 60 of 100 said Yes. Which group was more likely to say Yes? *(A: 0.80 against 0.60, even though B has more Yes answers)*
2. In a mosaic plot of 600 people, a column has width 0.30 and a segment in it has height 0.50. How many people are in that cell? *(0.30 × 0.50 × 600 = 90)*
3. Both bars of a segmented bar chart have their dividing line at 0.45. What does this suggest? *(No association: the response distribution is the same in both groups)*
