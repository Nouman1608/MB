## Understanding

- I can explain what the intersection A ∩ B is and read the symbol as "A and B". *(Guide: "Joint probability and the symbol ∩")*
- I can explain what a joint probability is, in context, for one trial or one randomly chosen individual. *(Guide: "Joint probability and the symbol ∩"; Practice Q7)*
- I can state what "mutually exclusive" (or "disjoint") means and why it gives P(A ∩ B) = 0. *(Guide: "Mutually exclusive (disjoint) events"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why an event and its complement are always mutually exclusive, but mutually exclusive events need not be complements. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3)*

## Skills

- I can list the outcomes in two events and find their intersection from a sample space. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can find a joint probability from a two-way table by dividing a cell by the grand total. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can read a Venn diagram and say whether two events overlap. *(Guide: "Seeing it on a Venn diagram")*
- I can use a joint probability to find an expected count. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can justify whether two events are mutually exclusive using the value of P(A ∩ B) and a conclusion in context. *(Guide: "How to justify a claim"; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can explain why a small joint probability still means the events are not mutually exclusive. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can explain why a zero cell in a sample does not, on its own, prove that two events can never happen together. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why "one event does not affect the other" is not the same as "mutually exclusive". *(Practice Q6)*
