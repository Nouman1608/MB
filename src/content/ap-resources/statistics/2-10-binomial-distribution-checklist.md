## Understanding

- I can state the four conditions for a binomial random variable and explain what X counts. *(Guide: "When is a random variable binomial?")*
- I can justify that a random variable is binomial by checking each condition in context. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain which condition fails when a variable is not binomial, such as drawing without replacement from a small group or counting trials until a success. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain what ₙCₓ counts and why the formula needs it. *(Guide: "The binomial probability function"; Practice Q3)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate P(X = x) with the binomial probability function, showing n, p and x. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can turn "at least", "at most", "more than" and "fewer than" into the right inequality and find the probability with a sum, a complement or a cdf. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can calculate the mean np and the standard deviation √[np(1 − p)] of a binomial variable. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can estimate a binomial probability from simulation results and compare it with the exact value. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can interpret a binomial probability, the mean and the standard deviation in context. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 3; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can use a small binomial probability as evidence against a claim, without saying it proves anything. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why a simulation estimate differs from the exact probability and how to improve it. *(Practice Q7)*
