Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/1-9-comparisons-distributions-one-quantitative-variable-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/1-9-comparisons-distributions-one-quantitative-variable-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can say which features (centre, variability, shape, outliers, clusters, gaps) dot plots, stemplots and histograms show when comparing groups. *(Guide: "Which display shows which feature?")*
- I can explain why parallel boxplots hide clusters, gaps and sample size, and why a wider box does not mean more data. *(Guide: "Parallel boxplots"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why groups of different sizes must be compared with relative frequencies. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can explain what the sign and size of a z-score tell me, and why z-scores have no units. *(Guide: "Z-scores: relative position")*

## Skills

- I can read a back-to-back stemplot, including split stems, and find each group's median and IQR. *(Guide: "A back-to-back stem-and-leaf plot"; Practice Q4)*
- I can judge skewness and spot outliers from parallel boxplots or five-number summaries. *(Guide: "Parallel boxplots"; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate a z-score from μ and σ, or from x̄ and s when the parameters are unknown. *(Guide: Worked examples 2 and 3; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can find the value that has a given z-score using x = μ + zσ. *(Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can write a full comparison of two distributions with comparative words, values for both groups, units and context. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can use several representations together to justify or reject a claim in context. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5, Q7)*
- I can use z-scores to decide which of two values, in the same distribution or in different ones, is more unusual, taking care when a lower value is better. *(Guide: Worked examples 2 and 3; Practice Q3, Q6)*
