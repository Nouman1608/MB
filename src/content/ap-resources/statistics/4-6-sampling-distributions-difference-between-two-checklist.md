## Understanding

- I can explain why x̄₁ − x̄₂ is an unbiased estimator of μ₁ − μ₂. *(Guide: "Mean and standard deviation of x̄₁ − x̄₂")*
- I can explain why the variances add even though the means are subtracted. *(Guide: "Mean and standard deviation of x̄₁ − x̄₂"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why the formula does not apply to paired data from the same individuals. *(Guide: "The conditions and what each one is for"; Practice Q2, Q7)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate the mean and standard deviation of the sampling distribution of x̄₁ − x̄₂, stating the order of subtraction. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can predict how changing one or both sample sizes changes the standard deviation. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3, Q7)*
- I can turn a statement such as "group 2's sample mean is larger" into an inequality and find the probability with a normal model. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 3; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can check the randomization and 10% conditions for two independent samples, and explain why an experiment needs only random assignment. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q5, Q6)*
- I can decide whether the sampling distribution is normal (both populations normal) or approximately normal (both samples of at least 30), or neither. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2, "When the normal model is not justified"; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can interpret the mean, standard deviation and a probability in context, naming both populations and the sample sizes. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can use a probability to judge whether an observed difference casts doubt on stated values, without claiming proof. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
