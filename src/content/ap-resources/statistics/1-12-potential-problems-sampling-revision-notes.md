## Recap

- A **statistic** from a sample estimates a **parameter** of the population.
- **Bias** is a systematic error in the sampling method: the statistic tends to be **consistently** above, or consistently below, the parameter.
- **Chance variation** is different: random samples miss the parameter by small amounts on both sides.
- **Nonrandom** samples (convenience, voluntary response) do not use chance to choose individuals, so they are open to bias.
- A **larger sample does not remove bias**.

## Key relationships

| Kind of bias | Stage | What happens | Quick example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Undercoverage | Who **can** be chosen | Part of the population is left out or less likely to be picked | Online-only survey misses people without internet |
| Voluntary response | How they are chosen | The sample is made up entirely of volunteers; strong opinions are over-represented | Phone-in poll on a radio show |
| Convenience (nonrandom) | How they are chosen | Easiest-to-reach individuals are used | Asking only your own class |
| Nonresponse | Who **answers** | Chosen individuals do not reply, and they differ from those who do | 28% of posted questionnaires returned, mostly by keen families |
| Response | What they **say** | Answers or measurements drift one way from the truth | Leading question; self-reported exercise; a scale that reads heavy |

## Assumptions and conventions

- Bias belongs to the **method**, judged over many samples taken the same way. One sample can land near the parameter by luck.
- Random selection prevents bias in **who is chosen** only. Nonresponse, response bias and an incomplete list can still bias a random sample.
- Nonresponse: the **researcher** chose the people. Voluntary response: the **people** chose themselves.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **No direction.** Say whether the estimate is likely too high or too low, and why.
2. **No context.** Name the people who are missed or over-represented in this study.
3. **Mixing up nonresponse and voluntary response.**
4. **Saying a bigger sample fixes bias.** It only makes a biased estimate more precise.
5. **Calling every difference from the parameter "bias".** Chance variation is not bias.
6. **Stopping at "it could be biased"** without naming the kind of bias.

## Quick self-check

1. A gym emails a questionnaire to 400 randomly chosen members and 90 reply. What is the response rate, and what kind of bias should you worry about? *(90 ÷ 400 = 22.5%; nonresponse bias)*
2. A website asks visitors to rate a new film; 3,000 do. Which bias is most likely? *(Voluntary response bias)*
3. "Wouldn't you agree that homework is a waste of time?" Which bias, and which direction for the proportion who think homework is a waste of time? *(Response bias from question wording; the proportion is likely too high)*
