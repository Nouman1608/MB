## Understanding

- I can explain what P(A | B) means, in context, as a probability within the group where B has happened. *(Guide: "What “given” does to a probability")*
- I can pick out the condition from words such as "given", "of the" and "among". *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why P(A | B) and P(B | A) are usually different, using their denominators. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5, Q7)*
- I can explain why the general multiplication rule works for any two events, not only independent ones. *(Guide: "The general multiplication rule"; Practice Q2)*

## Calculation

- I can find a conditional probability from a two-way table by dividing by the condition's total. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can use P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B) with given probabilities. *(Guide: "Notation and the formula"; Practice Q2)*
- I can use P(A ∩ B) = P(A) · P(B | A), including for two draws without replacement. *(Guide: "The general multiplication rule"; Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can build a tree diagram, multiply along paths and add paths to find the probability of an event. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can reverse a conditional probability, finding P(B | A) from a tree. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can interpret a conditional probability in context, naming the condition. *(Practice Q4, Q7)*
- I can spot a claim that swaps the condition and explain the error. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why a conditional probability from observational data does not show cause and effect. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7)*
