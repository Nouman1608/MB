## Understanding

- I can explain what the sampling distribution of p̂1 − p̂2 is, and why the difference changes from one pair of samples to the next. *(Guide: "Why compare two proportions?")*
- I can explain why p̂1 − p̂2 is an unbiased estimator of p1 − p2. *(Guide: "Mean and standard deviation of p̂1 − p̂2"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why the variances add when the proportions are subtracted, and why the standard deviation of the difference is larger than either one alone. *(Guide: "Mean and standard deviation of p̂1 − p̂2"; Practice Q7)*
- I can say what each condition is for: randomization and 10% for independence, large counts for the normal shape. *(Guide: "The conditions and what each one is for")*

## Skills

- I can calculate the mean and standard deviation of p̂1 − p̂2, stating the order of subtraction. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q4)*
- I can check the randomization and 10% conditions for two independent random samples, with numbers from the context. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can check the randomization condition for an experiment and explain why the 10% condition is not needed. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can check all four expected counts and say when a normal model should not be used. *(Guide: "When the large-counts condition fails"; Practice Q3)*
- I can turn a statement such as "group 2 is at least as high" into an inequality about p̂1 − p̂2 and find its probability with a normal model. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5, Q6)*
- I can find the smallest equal sample size that gives a required standard deviation. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can interpret the mean, the standard deviation and a probability for p̂1 − p̂2 in context, naming both populations. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can use a probability to judge whether an observed difference is surprising, without claiming proof. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can find and correct errors in someone else's standard deviation calculation. *(Practice Q2, Q7)*
