Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/1-6-descriptions-one-quantitative-variable-distributions-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/1-6-descriptions-one-quantitative-variable-distributions-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can list the four parts of a complete description: shape, centre, variability and unusual features. *(Guide: "What 'describe the distribution' means")*
- I can explain why skew is named after the longer tail and not after the peak. *(Guide: "Shape, part 1"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain the difference between an outlier, a gap and a cluster. *(Guide: "Unusual features"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why a gap alone does not make a value an outlier. *(Guide: "Unusual features"; Practice Q6)*

## Skills

- I can name a shape as skewed right, skewed left or approximately symmetric from a dot plot, stem-and-leaf plot, histogram or frequency table. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1, Q3)*
- I can name a shape as unimodal, bimodal or approximately uniform. *(Guide: "Shape, part 2"; Practice Q5, Q7)*
- I can find the median on a dot plot, and the bin that contains it on a histogram. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2)*
- I can describe variability with the smallest and largest values and where most values lie. *(Guide: "Centre and variability from a graph"; Practice Q4)*
- I can predict a likely shape from the context of a variable. *(Guide: "Shape, part 1"; Practice Q2)*

## Reasoning

- I can write a full description of a distribution in context, with the variable, the individuals and the units. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can find and correct errors in someone else's description. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can use counts or percentages from a graph to support or reject a claim. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5, Q7)*
