## Understanding

- I can explain what a parameter is and why it has one fixed value. *(Guide: "Parameters and statistics"; Practice Q2)*
- I can tell a parameter (μ, σ) from a statistic (x̄, s) in a given situation. *(Guide: simulation section; Practice Q2, Q7)*
- I can explain why the expected value need not be a possible value of X, and why it is not the most likely value. *(Guide: "The mean (expected value)"; Practice Q1, Q3)*
- I can explain why the standard deviation of a random variable has no n − 1 in it. *(Guide: FAQ and misconceptions)*

## Skills

- I can check that a table is a valid probability distribution and find a missing probability. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate the expected value of a discrete random variable as a weighted sum. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can calculate the variance and standard deviation of a discrete random variable, showing the deviations, squares and weights. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q4, Q6)*
- I can check my answers with one-variable statistics, using the probabilities as frequencies and reading σx. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*

## Reasoning

- I can interpret the mean as a long-run average, in context with units. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q5)*
- I can interpret the standard deviation as a typical long-run distance from the mean, in context. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can use the mean to estimate a long-run total, such as income over many plays. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can compare two random variables with the same mean by their standard deviations to support a decision. *(Practice Q6)*
