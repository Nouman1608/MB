## Understanding

- I can tell apart the distribution of a population, the distribution of one sample and the sampling distribution of p̂. *(Guide: "Why sample proportions vary")*
- I can explain why μp̂ = p shows that p̂ is an unbiased estimator of p. *(Guide: "Mean and standard deviation of p̂")*
- I can say what each condition is for: random and 10% for independence and the standard deviation formula, large counts for the normal shape. *(Guide: "The conditions and what each one is for"; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can explain how the standard deviation of p̂ changes when the sample size is multiplied by 4. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q2)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate μp̂ = p and σp̂ = √(p(1 − p)/n). *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5(a))*
- I can check the random, 10% and large-counts conditions with numbers from the context. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3, Q4(a))*
- I can find a probability about p̂ by standardising and using the normal distribution. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4, Q6(c))*
- I can find the smallest sample size that makes σp̂ no larger than a target, rounding up. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7(a))*

## Reasoning

- I can interpret the mean and standard deviation of p̂ in context, naming the population and the sample size. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(b))*
- I can use a probability from the sampling distribution to judge whether a sample result gives reason to doubt a claim, without saying the claim is proved false. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(d))*
- I can recognise when a normal model is not appropriate because the expected counts are too small. *(Guide: "When the large-counts condition fails"; Practice Q3)*
