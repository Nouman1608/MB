## Understanding

- I can define bias as a systematic error in the method that makes a statistic consistently too high or too low. *(Guide: "Bias: a fault in the method, not bad luck")*
- I can tell bias apart from chance variation between random samples, using a set of repeated results. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why convenience samples and voluntary response samples are likely to be biased. *(Guide: "Nonrandom samples"; Practice Q1, Q3)*
- I can explain why a larger sample does not remove bias. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q6, Q7)*

## Skills

- I can identify voluntary response bias in a described study. *(Practice Q1, Q6)*
- I can identify undercoverage bias, including undercoverage caused by an incomplete list. *(Guide: "Undercoverage bias"; Practice Q2)*
- I can identify nonresponse bias and calculate a response rate. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can identify response bias from leading or confusing wording, self-reported answers or faulty measurement. *(Guide: "Response bias"; Practice Q4, Q7)*
- I can tell nonresponse apart from voluntary response by asking who decided who is in the sample. *(Guide: "Nonresponse bias")*

## Reasoning

- I can state whether a biased method is likely to overestimate or underestimate the parameter, with a reason in context. *(Guide: "Writing about bias"; Practice Q2, Q5, Q6)*
- I can find more than one source of bias in the same study and give the direction of each. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can compare two sampling plans and justify which is less likely to be biased. *(Practice Q3, Q6)*
