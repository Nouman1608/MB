Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/1-4-graphical-representations-one-categorical-variable-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/statistics/1-4-graphical-representations-one-categorical-variable-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what the height of a bar and the size of a pie slice each represent. *(Guide: "Bar charts" and "Pie charts")*
- I can explain why a frequency bar chart and a relative frequency bar chart of one data set have the same shape. *(Guide: "Bar charts")*
- I can say when a pie chart is not appropriate, for example when individuals can give more than one answer. *(Guide: "Bar chart or pie chart?"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain how a bar chart differs from a histogram, and why shape words such as "skewed" do not apply to categories. *(Guide: "Bar charts"; Practice Q7)*

## Skills

- I can draw a bar chart with separate, equal-width bars, labelled axes and a scale that starts at 0. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate slice angles from counts or proportions and check that they add to 360°. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can read a count back from a bar chart or a pie chart when I know n. *(Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can spot a misleading graph, such as a truncated axis, and say what the data really show. *(Guide: "Graphs that mislead"; Practice Q2)*

## Reasoning

- I can use values read from a graph to support or reject a claim, telling "most common" apart from "a majority". *(Guide: "Using a graph to justify a claim"; Practice Q5)*
- I can compare two groups of different sizes using relative frequencies, and explain why counts can mislead. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can choose a suitable graph to compare groups and justify the choice. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can write every conclusion in context, naming the individuals, the variable and the values. *(All worked examples)*
