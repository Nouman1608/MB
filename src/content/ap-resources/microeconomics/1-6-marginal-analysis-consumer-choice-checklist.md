## Understanding

- I can define marginal benefit, marginal cost and marginal analysis, and state the rule: MB > MC do more, MB < MC do less, MB = MC stop. *(Guide: "Thinking at the margin")*
- I can state the assumptions of the rational consumer choice model: limited income, given prices, known preferences, utility maximization and diminishing marginal utility. *(Guide: "The model of rational consumer choice"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain diminishing marginal utility and why total utility still rises while marginal utility is positive. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why a sunk cost does not change the optimal quantity. *(Guide: "Sunk costs do not change the best quantity"; Practice Q2, Q4)*

## Calculation and graphing

- I can calculate marginal utility from total utility, and total utility from marginal utility. *(Guide: "Total utility and marginal utility"; Practice Q1, Q6)*
- I can find the optimal quantity from an MB table and a price, and check it with total net benefit. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can draw an MB and MC graph with labelled axes, mark the optimum and show the effect of a higher MC. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate MU per dollar and find the utility-maximizing combination of two goods for a given income. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why a consumer should compare MU per dollar, not MU alone, when prices differ. *(Guide: "Spending a budget on two goods"; Practice Q3)*
- I can show that a combination is not optimal because money is left over or the MU-per-dollar ratios differ, and say which way spending should move. *(Practice Q6(d), Q7(b))*
- I can explain how a fall in a good's price changes the utility-maximizing combination. *(Practice Q7)*
