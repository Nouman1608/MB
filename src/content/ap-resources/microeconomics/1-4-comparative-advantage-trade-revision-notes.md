Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, graphs and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/1-4-comparative-advantage-trade-study-guide/).

## Recap

- **Absolute advantage:** a producer can make more of a good than another producer with the same resources (or the same amount with fewer resources).
- **Comparative advantage:** a producer can make a good at a lower opportunity cost than another producer.
- With two goods and two producers, each has the comparative advantage in a different good, unless their opportunity costs are equal (then no one does).
- **Specialisation** by comparative advantage raises total output of both goods. **Trade** shares the extra output.
- After trade, each side can **consume** outside its own PPC. The PPC itself does not move.

## Key relationships

| Data type | Absolute advantage | Opportunity cost of 1 X |
|---|---|---|
| **Output** (units made) | larger number | output of Y ÷ output of X |
| **Input** (hours per unit) | smaller number | hours per X ÷ hours per Y |

| Idea | Rule |
|---|---|
| Two PPCs on one graph | higher intercept = absolute advantage in that good; flatter line = comparative advantage in the good on the horizontal axis |
| Mutually beneficial terms of trade | strictly **between** the two producers' opportunity costs |
| Gain per unit traded | exporter: price received − own cost; importer: own cost − price paid |

## Assumptions behind the model

- Two producers, two goods, fixed resources and technology.
- Constant opportunity cost (straight-line PPCs) in most questions.
- Transport costs and trade barriers are ignored.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Deciding specialisation by absolute advantage.
2. Using the output rule on input data, which gives the reciprocal.
3. Saying one producer has the comparative advantage in both goods.
4. Saying trade shifts the PPC outward.
5. Giving terms of trade outside the range, or exactly at one end, as "beneficial".
6. Forgetting units: "2 sacks of rice per pair of shoes", not just "2".

## Quick self-check

1. Pella can make 10 kites or 20 drums with its resources. What is its opportunity cost of 1 kite? *(20 ÷ 10 = 2 drums; 1 drum costs 1/2 kite.)*
2. A potter needs 4 hours per pot and 2 hours per vase. What is the opportunity cost of 1 pot? *(4 ÷ 2 = 2 vases.)*
3. Producer X's cost of a hat is 2 scarves and Producer Y's is 5 scarves. Who exports hats, and what terms work for both? *(X; 1 hat for more than 2 but fewer than 5 scarves.)*
4. Does a country that is worse at producing both goods gain from trade? *(Yes, if its opportunity costs differ: it still has a comparative advantage in one good.)*
