## Understanding

- I can describe the features of a perfectly competitive labour market and explain why each firm is a wage taker. *(Guide: "What makes a factor market perfectly competitive"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why a wage taker's marginal resource cost equals the market wage at every quantity of labour. *(Guide: "What makes a factor market perfectly competitive"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why a firm can have market power when it sells but still be a wage taker when it hires. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why the firm's downward-sloping MRP curve is its labour demand curve. *(Guide: "The hiring rule")*

## Calculation and graphing

- I can calculate MP and MRP from a total product table, with units. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can find the profit-maximising number of workers where MRP = MRC = wage, and show what happens when the wage changes. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate MRP for a firm with output market power and compare it with VMP = MP × P. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Figure 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can draw side-by-side graphs of the labour market and one wage-taking firm, labelling the market wage, the firm's horizontal S = MRC line, its MRP curve and its quantity of workers. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6)*
- I can use MP per dollar to find the least-cost mix of labour and capital. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3, Q7)*
- I can find the profit-maximising quantities of two inputs where MRP_L = W and MRP_K = r. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain, with numbers, why hiring one worker past the MRP = W point lowers profit. *(Guide: Worked example 1(c))*
- I can explain why least cost does not guarantee maximum profit. *(Guide: Worked example 3(c); Practice Q7(d))*
- I can show how a change in market labour supply changes the market wage and one firm's hiring. *(Practice Q6)*
