## Understanding

- I can explain what a production function shows and why it is measured in units of output, not money. *(Guide: "From inputs to output")*
- I can define the short run and the long run by whether inputs are fixed, not by the calendar. *(Guide: "Short run and long run"; Practice Q2)*
- I can define total product, marginal product and average product in words. *(Guide: "Total, marginal and average product")*
- I can explain why marginal product often rises at first, using specialisation. *(Guide: "How marginal product changes: three stages")*
- I can explain why diminishing marginal returns happen, using the fixed input, and why they are a short-run idea. *(Guide: "How marginal product changes: three stages"; Practice Q6)*

## Calculation and graphing

- I can calculate MP and AP from a total product table, with units. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can rebuild total product from a table of MP (running sum) or of AP (AP × L). *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4, Q7)*
- I can identify the worker with whom diminishing marginal returns begin, and tell it apart from where AP starts to fall and where MP turns negative. *(Guide: Worked example 1(c); Practice Q3, Q4)*
- I can sketch TP above MP and AP, with MP cutting AP at its maximum and TP peaking where MP = 0. *(Guide: Figures 1 and 2; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why AP rises when MP is above it and falls when MP is below it, with a numerical example. *(Guide: "The three curves and how they are linked"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why diminishing marginal returns do not mean total product is falling. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain how more capital or better technology shifts the production function, and why adding capital is a long-run change. *(Guide: "What shifts the production function"; Practice Q6)*
