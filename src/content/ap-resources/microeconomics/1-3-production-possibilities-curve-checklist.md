Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/1-3-production-possibilities-curve-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/1-3-production-possibilities-curve-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define a PPC and state its four assumptions: two goods, fixed resources, fixed technology, full and efficient use. *(Guide: "Assumptions of the model")*
- I can explain how a PPC shows scarcity, trade-offs and opportunity cost. *(Guide: "Reading a PPC")*
- I can classify a point as efficient, inefficient or unattainable, and explain what each means. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why a typical PPC is bowed outward and when a straight line is a better model. *(Guide: "Why the curve bows outward"; Practice Q3, Q7)*

## Calculation and graphing

- I can calculate the opportunity cost of either good between two points on a PPC table, using changes rather than levels, with units. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q7)*
- I can calculate the constant opportunity cost on a linear PPC from its two intercepts. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can show that moving from inside the curve to the curve has zero opportunity cost. *(Guide: Worked example 1(d); Practice Q6)*
- I can draw a PPC with both axes labelled with the good and its unit, and plot points from a table. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can draw an outward shift, an inward shift and a pivot on one axis. *(Guide: Figures 2 and 3; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can tell a shift of the PPC from a movement along it or towards it, and give the cause of each. *(Guide: "Shifts of the curve versus movements along it"; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can explain why re-employing idle resources is not economic growth in this model. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can explain how a technology change in one industry changes the opportunity cost of both goods. *(Guide: Worked example 2(c))*
