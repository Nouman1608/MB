Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, graphs and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/6-2-externalities-study-guide/).

## Recap

- An **externality** is a cost or benefit that falls on a **third party** and is not paid for through the market.
- **Rational agents** respond only to **private** costs and benefits, so the market finds MPB = MPC, not MSB = MSC.
- Causes: **poorly defined property rights** and **high transaction costs**. Non-excludable benefits lead to **free riding**.
- The **deadweight loss** is the triangle between MSB and MSC from Qm to Qs, pointing to where MSB = MSC.

## Key relationships

| Type | Curves | Market vs optimum |
|---|---|---|
| Negative, production | MSC = MPC + MEC lies above MPC (supply) | Qm > Qs; price too low |
| Negative, consumption | MSB = MPB − MEC lies below MPB (demand) | Qm > Qs |
| Positive, production | MSC lies below MPC | Qm < Qs |
| Positive, consumption | MSB = MPB + MEB lies above MPB | Qm < Qs |

| Calculation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Market quantity | MPB = MPC |
| Optimal quantity | MSB = MSC |
| DWL (straight lines) | ½ × (Qm − Qs, as a size) × (gap between MSB and MSC at Qm) |
| Corrective tax or subsidy | MEC or MEB **at Qs** |
| Tax revenue / subsidy cost | per-unit amount × new quantity |

| Policy | Use |
|---|---|
| Per-unit tax | negative externalities; raises revenue |
| Per-unit subsidy | positive externalities; costs the government |
| Regulation | output or emission limits, required methods |
| Public provision | government supplies the good (e.g. free vaccinations) |
| Property rights | assign a right, or let rights be traded or bargained over when transaction costs are low |

## Assumptions

- Competitive markets, so supply = MPC and demand = MPB.
- With low transaction costs and clear rights, bargaining reaches the efficient outcome whoever holds the right.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying a negative externality makes the market price too high (it is too low).
2. Shading the whole band between MPC and MSC as DWL.
3. Setting the tax equal to the MEC at the market quantity when MEC changes with output.
4. Drawing a consumption externality by moving the cost curve (move the benefit curve).
5. Claiming the optimal pollution level is zero.
6. Missing labels: name every curve (D = MPB, S = MPC, MSB, MSC), Qm, Qs and the DWL.

## Quick self-check

1. Demand is P = 40 − Q, MPC = 4 + Q and MEC = $6. Find Qm and Qs. *(40 − Q = 4 + Q gives Qm = 18; 40 − Q = 10 + Q gives Qs = 15.)*
2. What is the DWL in question 1? *(At 18: MSC = 28, MSB = 22. DWL = ½ × 3 × 6 = 9.)*
3. What per-unit tax corrects it? *($6, the MEC. Buyers then pay $25 and sellers keep $19.)*
