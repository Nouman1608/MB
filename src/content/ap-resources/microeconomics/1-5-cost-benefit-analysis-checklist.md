Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/1-5-cost-benefit-analysis-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/1-5-cost-benefit-analysis-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define opportunity cost as the value of the next-best alternative given up, and explain why it is not the sum of all rejected options. *(Guide: "Opportunity cost: the next-best alternative"; Practice Q1)*
- I can tell an explicit cost from an implicit cost and give an example of each. *(Guide: "Explicit and implicit costs"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain that total benefit is utility for a consumer and total revenue for a firm. *(Guide: "What cost-benefit analysis means")*
- I can explain why money already spent and not recoverable, or a cost paid whichever option is chosen, is not a cost of the decision. *(Guide: Worked example 1(a))*

## Calculation and graphing

- I can add explicit and implicit costs to find the total economic cost of a decision. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q6(b))*
- I can calculate net benefit for every option in a table and pick the largest. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can find marginal benefit and marginal cost as changes in the totals, and show they lead to the same choice. *(Guide: Worked example 2(b); Practice Q7(a))*
- I can draw total benefit and total cost curves and show the best choice as the largest vertical gap between them. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can tell a "how much" decision from an all-or-nothing decision and choose the right method for each. *(Guide: "Two kinds of decision"; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can explain why the option with the largest total benefit, or any positive net benefit, is not automatically the best choice. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can explain how leaving out an implicit cost can lead to the wrong decision. *(Guide: Worked example 1(c); Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why a cost that is the same for every quantity affects whether to act but not how much to do. *(Practice Q7(c))*
