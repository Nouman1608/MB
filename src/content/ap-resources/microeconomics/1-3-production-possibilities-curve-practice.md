These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. The mark points are a suggested Marlbridge rubric to help you check your work; they are not an official scoring guideline. All economies and data are fictional. Only subtraction and division are needed, so a four-function calculator is enough. Give opportunity costs as exact values or to 2 decimal places.

Questions 1, 3, 5, 6 and 7 use the production possibilities of **Rosmar**, a fictional economy that produces corn (thousand tonnes per year) and tablets (thousand tablet computers per year):

| Point | P | Q | R | S | T | U |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Corn (thousand tonnes per year) | 0 | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
| Tablets (thousands per year) | 60 | 57 | 51 | 41 | 26 | 0 |

## Question 1 (multiple choice · foundation)

Rosmar moves from point R to point S. What is the opportunity cost of the extra 5 thousand tonnes of corn?

- (A) 10 thousand tablets
- (B) 41 thousand tablets
- (C) 51 thousand tablets
- (D) 5 thousand tonnes of corn

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Tablets fall from 51 thousand to 41 thousand, so 51 − 41 = 10 thousand tablets are given up.

- (B) reads the level of tablets at S instead of the change.
- (C) reads the level of tablets at R, the starting point, instead of the change.
- (D) names what is gained, not what is given up. An opportunity cost must be measured in the *other* good.
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · core)

Which event would shift a country's PPC outward?

- (A) Unemployed workers in the country find jobs.
- (B) Consumers decide they want more corn and fewer tablets.
- (C) A new irrigation method raises the output of every farm, and a new chip design raises the output of every tablet factory.
- (D) Workers move from tablet factories to farms.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** Better technology in both industries means the same resources can produce more of both goods, so the whole curve moves out.

- (A) confuses using existing resources with gaining new ones: the point moves from inside towards the curve, but the curve stays put.
- (B) confuses a change in preferences with a change in productive ability: it is a movement along the curve.
- (D) is a reallocation of the same resources, which is a movement along the curve, not a shift.
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · core)

Rosmar's PPC is bowed outward from the origin. Which statement best explains this shape?

- (A) Resources are not equally suited to producing corn and tablets, so each extra unit of corn costs more tablets than the one before.
- (B) Rosmar has more resources than other economies.
- (C) The price of corn rises as more corn is produced.
- (D) Some of Rosmar's workers are unemployed.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** As Rosmar moves towards corn, it must use resources that are better at making tablets, so the opportunity cost of corn rises: 3, 6, 10, 15 and then 26 thousand tablets for each extra 5 thousand tonnes. Rising opportunity cost gives the bowed-out shape.

- (B) confuses the *position* of a curve with its *shape*. More resources would place the curve farther out, not change how it bends.
- (C) brings in prices and markets. The PPC is about physical production possibilities, not prices.
- (D) confuses inefficiency with shape. Unemployment puts the economy at a point inside the curve.
</details>

## Question 4 (calculation · core)

Tamsin, a fictional economy, uses all its resources to produce either 300 baskets of mangoes per week or 100 chairs per week. Its resources are equally suited to both goods.

(a) Calculate the opportunity cost of one chair, and of one basket of mangoes.
(b) Classify each combination as efficient, inefficient or unattainable: X (40 chairs, 150 baskets), Y (60 chairs, 120 baskets), Z (80 chairs, 80 baskets).
(c) What is the opportunity cost of increasing chair production from 60 to 70 per week?

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

**(a)** One chair costs 300 ÷ 100 = **3 baskets of mangoes**. One basket costs 100 ÷ 300 = **1/3 of a chair** (about 0.33 chairs). These are constant along the whole line.

**(b)** The PPC is: baskets = 300 − 3 × chairs.

- X: at 40 chairs, maximum baskets = 300 − 120 = 180. 150 < 180, so X is **inefficient** (inside).
- Y: at 60 chairs, 300 − 180 = 120. Y is **efficient** (on the curve).
- Z: at 80 chairs, 300 − 240 = 60. 80 > 60, so Z is **unattainable** (outside).

**(c)** 10 more chairs × 3 baskets per chair = **30 baskets of mangoes**. Check: at 70 chairs the line gives 300 − 210 = 90 baskets, and 120 − 90 = 30.

Suggested mark points (3): 1 for both opportunity costs with units; 1 for all three classifications with a supporting calculation; 1 for 30 baskets.

Common error: writing the opportunity cost of a chair as 1/3 basket (dividing the wrong way). Check: if 100 chairs cost 300 baskets, each chair must cost more than one basket.
</details>

## Question 5 (graph · core)

Use the Rosmar table.

(a) Draw a correctly labelled PPC for Rosmar, and plot point R.
(b) An earthquake destroys half of Rosmar's tablet factories. Farms are not damaged. Show the new PPC on your graph and explain its position.
(c) After the earthquake, can Rosmar still produce combination R? Explain.

<details>
<summary>Model answer and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** Horizontal axis: "Corn (thousand tonnes per year)". Vertical axis: "Tablets (thousands per year)". (The axes may be swapped if the labels and points match.) Plot P to U and join them with a smooth curve bowed outward from the origin, meeting the tablet axis at 60 and the corn axis at 25. Label it PPC₁. Plot R at (10, 51), on the curve.

**(b)** Draw PPC₂ meeting the corn axis at the **same point** (25) but the tablet axis **lower** than 60. The curve pivots inward on the tablet axis. Reason: the earthquake reduced the capital used to make tablets, so the most tablets Rosmar can make falls. If all resources go to corn, the factories were not being used anyway, so maximum corn is unchanged.

**(c)** **No.** R was on PPC₁, so it used all of Rosmar's resources efficiently. After the earthquake there are fewer factories, so at 10 thousand tonnes of corn Rosmar can make fewer than 51 thousand tablets. PPC₂ lies inside PPC₁ everywhere except at the corn intercept, so R is now outside PPC₂: **unattainable**.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Both axes labelled with the goods **and** units; curve bowed outward through the table values |
| 1 | New curve pivots inward on the tablet axis with the corn intercept unchanged |
| 1 | Explanation links the pivot to the loss of capital used only for tablets |
| 1 | R identified as unattainable (outside PPC₂) with a reason |

Do not award the second point for a parallel inward shift of the whole curve: the earthquake did not reduce Rosmar's ability to grow corn.
</details>

## Question 6 (constructed response · core)

Rosmar is producing 10 thousand tonnes of corn and 40 thousand tablets per year. A government minister says: "If we put our unemployed workers back to work, our production possibilities curve will shift outward. That is economic growth."

(a) Identify where Rosmar's current combination lies relative to its PPC, with a calculation.
(b) Calculate the largest increase in tablets Rosmar could achieve without producing less corn, and state its opportunity cost.
(c) Evaluate the minister's statement.
(d) Give two changes that **would** shift Rosmar's PPC outward.

<details>
<summary>Model answer and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** At 10 thousand tonnes of corn, Rosmar could make 51 thousand tablets (point R). It makes only 40 thousand, so it is **inside** the PPC: inefficient.

**(b)** 51 − 40 = **11 thousand tablets**, with an opportunity cost of **zero**, since no corn is given up.

**(c)** The minister is **wrong about the shift**. Re-employing workers uses resources Rosmar already has, so the economy moves from inside the curve towards it (to R). The curve does not move. Output rises, which is welcome, but in this model economic growth means an outward shift of the curve itself, caused by more or better resources or technology.

**(d)** Any two: more workers (for example, population growth or immigration); more capital (new factories or machinery); better education and training (higher-quality labour); new technology in either or both industries; discovery of more usable land or raw materials.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Inside the curve, using 51 > 40 |
| 1 | 11 thousand tablets **and** zero opportunity cost |
| 1 | States re-employment is a movement towards the curve, not a shift, **with** the reason (no new resources or technology) |
| 1 | Two valid causes of an outward shift |
</details>

## Question 7 (explanation · stretch)

(a) Using the Rosmar table, calculate the opportunity cost of 1 thousand tonnes of corn for each move from P to U.
(b) Explain why these values rise, using a specific example of a resource.
(c) Describe a situation in which a straight-line PPC would be a reasonable model, and explain why.

<details>
<summary>Model answer and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** Each move adds 5 thousand tonnes of corn.

| Move | Tablets given up (thousand) | Opportunity cost of 1 thousand t of corn |
|---|---|---|
| P → Q | 60 − 57 = 3 | 3 ÷ 5 = 0.6 thousand tablets |
| Q → R | 57 − 51 = 6 | 6 ÷ 5 = 1.2 |
| R → S | 51 − 41 = 10 | 10 ÷ 5 = 2.0 |
| S → T | 41 − 26 = 15 | 15 ÷ 5 = 3.0 |
| T → U | 26 − 0 = 26 | 26 ÷ 5 = 5.2 |

**(b)** Resources are specialised. For example, Rosmar's first extra corn can be grown by moving farm-ready land and farm workers who were making few tablets, so few tablets are lost. Later, Rosmar must turn trained electronics engineers and clean-room factory sites into farming resources. They are very productive at tablets but poor at farming, so each extra tonne of corn costs many more tablets.

**(c)** A straight line fits when resources are **equally suited** to both goods, so moving them does not change their productivity. Example: a workshop whose workers, using the same tools, can make either wooden chairs or wooden tables in fixed times. Each table always costs the same number of chairs, so the opportunity cost is constant and the PPC is linear.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | All five opportunity costs correct, as a change ÷ change |
| 1 | Rising cost explained by resources being unequally suited, with a specific example |
| 1 | Straight line linked to equally suited resources **and** constant opportunity cost, with a plausible example |

Accept answers to (a) given per 5 thousand tonnes (3, 6, 10, 15, 26 thousand tablets) if the unit is stated clearly.
</details>
