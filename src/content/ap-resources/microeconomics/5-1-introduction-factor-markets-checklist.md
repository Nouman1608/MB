## Understanding

- I can explain how a factor market differs from a product market and say who buys and who sells in each. *(Guide: "Two kinds of market", Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can name the factor price for labour, capital and land, and explain how factor prices act as incentives and signals. *(Guide: "Factors and their prices")*
- I can explain why the demand for labour is a derived demand. *(Guide: "Derived demand"; Practice Q6(b))*
- I can name the three things a firm's hiring decision depends on: productivity, output price and the cost of the factor. *(Guide: "Derived demand"; Practice Q7)*

## Calculation and graphing

- I can calculate MRP for a price taker as MP × P and check it as the change in total revenue. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can calculate MRP for a firm that must cut its price to sell more, and explain why it is below MP × P. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5, Q7)*
- I can calculate MRC from a total cost table and explain why it equals the wage for a wage taker. *(Guide: "Marginal resource cost"; Practice Q4)*
- I can draw a labour market graph with the wage on the vertical axis, quantity of labour on the horizontal axis, and the equilibrium marked. *(Guide: Figure 3; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why the quantity of labour demanded falls as the wage rises, using falling MRP. *(Guide: "The labour market: demand and supply"; Practice Q6(b))*
- I can explain why the quantity of labour supplied rises as the wage rises. *(Practice Q3, Q6(c))*
- I can decide whether one more worker adds more to revenue than to cost by comparing MRP with MRC. *(Guide: Worked example 1(d); Practice Q7(c))*
- I can explain why a change in the wage is a movement along the labour demand and supply curves, not a shift. *(Guide: "Movement, not shift"; Practice Q3)*
