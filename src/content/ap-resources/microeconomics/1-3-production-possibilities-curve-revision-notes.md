## Recap

- A **PPC** shows the maximum combinations of two goods an economy can produce with its resources and technology.
- It illustrates **scarcity** (a boundary), **trade-offs** (more of one means less of the other on the curve) and **opportunity cost** (what is given up).
- **On** the curve: efficient. **Inside**: inefficient, with unemployed or misused resources. **Outside**: unattainable now.
- Shape: **straight** = constant opportunity cost; **bowed outward** = increasing opportunity cost (specialised resources); bowed inward = decreasing opportunity cost (rare).
- **Economic growth** = outward shift; **contraction** = inward shift.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | How to use it |
|---|---|
| **Opportunity cost of 1 X = Y given up ÷ X gained** | Use changes between two points, not levels |
| Opportunity cost of Y = 1 ÷ (opportunity cost of X) | Between the same two points |
| Slope of PPC (size) = opportunity cost of the good on the horizontal axis | Constant slope → constant cost |
| Linear PPC: cost of X = (max Y) ÷ (max X) | e.g. 40 bicycles or 20 wheat → 1 wheat costs 2 bicycles |

| Change | Effect on the graph |
|---|---|
| More or better resources; better technology for both goods | outward shift |
| Better technology for one good | pivot out on that good's axis |
| Loss of resources (disaster, shrinking workforce) | inward shift |
| Unemployed resources put back to work | point moves from inside towards the curve |
| Society chooses a different mix | movement along the curve |

## Assumptions behind the model

- Two goods; fixed quantity and quality of resources; fixed technology.
- Points on the curve assume full employment and efficient use of resources.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Calling a point inside the curve "unattainable".
2. Saying lower unemployment is an outward shift.
3. Using the level of a good instead of the change when calculating opportunity cost.
4. Forgetting to state the unit ("2 bicycles per tonne of wheat").
5. Shifting the whole curve when technology improves for one good only.
6. Unlabelled axes: always name both goods and their units.

## Quick self-check

1. A straight PPC runs from 30 laptops to 60 tonnes of rice. What is the opportunity cost of 1 tonne of rice? *(30 ÷ 60 = 0.5 laptops)*
2. Does a fall in unemployment shift the PPC? *(No: the point moves from inside towards the curve.)*
3. Why is a typical PPC bowed outward? *(Resources are not equally suited to both goods, so the opportunity cost rises as you make more of one.)*
