## Recap

- The supply and demand model explains what sets prices and quantities, and why they differ across markets and change over time.
- **Equilibrium**: the price at which the quantity buyers want equals the quantity sellers offer. No shortage, no surplus: the market **clears**.
- Above equilibrium there is a surplus, so the price falls; below it there is a shortage, so the price rises. The equilibrium is stable.
- The equilibrium price is a **signal**: it rations the good to buyers who value it most and gives sellers an incentive to move resources towards what people want.
- **Consumer surplus (CS)**: willingness to pay minus price, summed over units bought.
- **Producer surplus (PS)**: price minus minimum acceptable price, summed over units sold.
- **Total surplus = CS + PS.** With no market failures, it is largest at the competitive equilibrium, so the market is **efficient**.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | How to use it |
|---|---|
| Equilibrium: Qd = Qs | find the row or the crossing point |
| Height of demand = willingness to pay = marginal benefit | for each unit |
| Height of supply = minimum acceptable price = marginal cost | for each unit |
| **CS = ½ × Q* × (demand price intercept − P*)** | straight-line demand |
| **PS = ½ × Q* × (P* − supply price intercept)** | straight-line supply |
| Spending = P* × Q* | not the same as CS |

| Quantity traded | Marginal benefit vs marginal cost | Effect of one more unit on total surplus |
|---|---|---|
| below equilibrium | MB > MC | raises it |
| at equilibrium | MB = MC | none left to gain |
| above equilibrium | MB < MC | lowers it |

## Assumptions

- A perfectly competitive market: many buyers and sellers, an identical good, no one can set the price.
- No market failures: demand and supply reflect all benefits and costs.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Forgetting the ½ in a triangle area.
2. Measuring CS height from zero instead of from the price.
3. Calling total spending "consumer surplus".
4. Assuming CS and PS are equal at equilibrium.
5. Writing "demand equals supply" instead of "quantity demanded equals quantity supplied".
6. Shading surplus beyond the quantity actually traded.

## Quick self-check

1. Demand meets the price axis at $40, supply at $16, and equilibrium is $25 and 300 units. Find CS and PS. *(CS = ½ × 300 × 15 = $2,250; PS = ½ × 300 × 9 = $1,350.)*
2. A buyer would pay up to $18 and the price is $12. What is her consumer surplus? *($6.)*
3. Why does producing beyond equilibrium lower total surplus? *(Each extra unit costs more to make than buyers value it: MC > MB.)*
