## Recap

- **Price discrimination** means charging different buyers different prices for the same product when the cost of serving them is the same.
- The aim is **more profit**: the firm turns some consumer surplus into profit and may sell units it would otherwise lose.
- With **group pricing**, the firm sets MR = MC separately for each group. The **less elastic** group pays the **higher** price.
- With **perfect price discrimination**, each buyer pays their maximum willingness to pay.

## Key relationships

| Idea | What to remember |
|---|---|
| Conditions | market power + identify buyers with different willingness to pay + prevent resale (arbitrage) |
| Group pricing | MR = MC in each group; higher price where demand is less elastic |
| Perfect price discrimination: MR | MR = P, so the MR curve **is** the demand curve |
| Perfect price discrimination: output | where demand meets MC (P = MC): the competitive quantity |
| Perfect price discrimination: areas | CS = 0; DWL = 0; profit (producer surplus) = whole area under demand and above MC |

| Measure | Single price | Perfect price discrimination |
|---|---|---|
| Quantity | MR = MC (lower) | P = MC (higher) |
| Consumer surplus | positive | zero |
| Deadweight loss | positive | zero |
| Allocatively efficient? | no | yes |

## Assumptions

- The firm has a downward-sloping demand curve (it is not a price taker).
- Cost per unit is the same for every buyer, so price differences are not cost differences.
- In the graphs here, MC is constant and there are no fixed costs, so profit equals producer surplus.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Calling a cost-based price difference price discrimination.
2. Saying the group with "more demand" pays more; it is the **less elastic** group.
3. Drawing MR below demand for a perfectly price-discriminating firm.
4. Reading the single-price monopoly price from MR instead of demand.
5. Saying perfect price discrimination is inefficient: it removes deadweight loss, even though consumers keep no surplus.
6. Forgetting that a perfectly competitive firm cannot price discriminate.

## Quick self-check

1. A monopolist faces P = 100 − 2Q with MC = ATC = $20. Find the single-price output, price and profit. *(MR = 100 − 4Q = 20 gives Q = 20, P = $60, profit = 40 × 20 = $800.)*
2. Same firm: find consumer surplus and deadweight loss at the single price. *(CS = ½ × 20 × 40 = $400; efficient Q = 40, DWL = ½ × 20 × 40 = $400.)*
3. Now it perfectly price discriminates. Find output, profit, CS and DWL. *(Q = 40; profit = ½ × 40 × 80 = $1,600; CS = 0; DWL = 0.)*
4. Adults' demand is less elastic than children's. Who pays more? *(Adults.)*
