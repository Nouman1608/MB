## Recap

- **Monopolistic competition**: many small firms selling **differentiated** products (close but not perfect substitutes), with **easy entry and exit**.
- Each firm faces a **downward-sloping, fairly elastic** demand curve, so MR lies below demand.
- Firms compete with **advertising**, branding, quality, location and service as well as price. Advertising aims to raise demand and make it less elastic; it raises ATC, not MC.
- **Short run:** produce where MR = MC, read P from demand, compare P with ATC: profit, loss or zero.
- **Long run:** entry (if profits) or exit (if losses) shifts each firm's demand until it is **tangent to ATC**: P = ATC, zero economic profit.

## Key relationships

| Situation | What happens to each firm's demand | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Short-run profit (P > ATC) | firms enter; demand shifts **left**, becomes more elastic | profit falls to zero |
| Short-run loss (P < ATC) | firms exit; demand shifts **right**, becomes less elastic | loss shrinks to zero |
| Long-run equilibrium | demand tangent to ATC at the MR = MC quantity | P = ATC > MC |

| Measure | How to find it |
|---|---|
| Profit or loss | (P − ATC) × Q at the MR = MC quantity |
| Excess capacity | quantity at minimum ATC − quantity produced |
| Deadweight loss | triangle between demand and MC, from the quantity produced to where they meet |

## Long-run comparison

| | Perfect competition | Monopolistic competition |
|---|---|---|
| Profit | zero | zero |
| P and MC | P = MC | **P > MC** (allocatively inefficient) |
| Output and minimum ATC | at minimum ATC | **below** it (excess capacity) |

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Showing long-run economic profit: free entry removes it.
2. Placing the tangency at minimum ATC: it is on the falling part of ATC.
3. Drawing the tangency where MC crosses ATC.
4. Reading the price from MR instead of demand.
5. Saying advertising raises MC: it is a fixed cost, so it raises ATC.
6. Forgetting the shutdown check: a loss-making firm stays open in the short run if P ≥ AVC.

## Quick self-check

1. A firm produces 50 units where MR = MC; P = $9 and ATC = $7. Profit? What happens next? *((9 − 7) × 50 = $100 profit; firms enter and its demand shifts left until P = ATC.)*
2. In long-run equilibrium, is P greater than, equal to or less than MC? *(Greater than MC.)*
3. Why is there excess capacity in the long run? *(Demand slopes down, so it touches ATC to the left of minimum ATC.)*
4. Which is productively efficient in the long run: perfect competition or monopolistic competition? *(Perfect competition.)*
