## Recap

- **Scarcity:** the resources available are not enough to satisfy all of society's wants and needs.
- Scarcity is **relative** (not enough compared with wants) and **universal** (rich and poor, people and countries).
- **Causes:** resources are limited and have alternative uses; time is limited; wants keep growing.
- Scarcity → **choice** → **trade-off** (more of one thing means less of another) → **opportunity cost** (the next best alternative given up).
- Most factors are **rival**: one user's use stops another using the same unit at the same time. **Established knowledge** is **non-rival**, so it may not be scarce.

## Key relationships

| Factor of production | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Land** | natural resources as found in nature | farmland, water, forests, minerals |
| **Labour** | human effort and skill | a teacher's working hours |
| **Capital** | human-made tools, machines, buildings used in production | ovens, vans, factories |
| **Entrepreneurship** | organising the other factors and taking risk | the founder of a new firm |

| Scarcity | Shortage |
|---|---|
| not enough resources for all wants | in one market, buyers want more than sellers offer at the current price |
| permanent, everywhere | temporary, particular goods |

## Assumptions to keep in mind

- People want more than resources can provide, so wants are treated as unlimited.
- Each resource has more than one possible use.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Calling money "capital". Capital is physical tools, machines and buildings.
2. Saying scarcity means "rare". It means "not enough for what people want".
3. Treating scarcity and a shortage as the same thing.
4. Saying rich countries escape scarcity.
5. Saying knowledge is never scarce: only established, already-known knowledge is non-rival, and using it still needs scarce time and resources.
6. Listing everything given up as the opportunity cost instead of only the next best alternative.

## Quick self-check

1. A hospital's new X-ray machine is which factor of production? *(Capital.)*
2. Sana has 6 free hours. An essay takes 2 hours and a football training session takes 1 hour. What is the opportunity cost of writing one essay? *(2 training sessions.)*
3. Why may a well-known mathematical rule not be scarce? *(It is non-rival: any number of people can use it at once without using it up.)*
4. What are the two sides of scarcity? *(Limited resources with alternative uses, and wants that keep growing.)*
