These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. The mark points are a suggested Marlbridge rubric to help you check your work; they are not an official scoring guideline. All firms and data are fictional. Only addition, subtraction and multiplication are needed, so a four-function calculator is enough.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · foundation)

Ellery Mugs, a fictional pottery, sells mugs at the market price of $8 each and cannot change this price. The marginal cost of each mug it makes per hour is:

| Mug | 1st | 2nd | 3rd | 4th | 5th | 6th |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MC ($) | 5 | 4 | 5 | 7 | 9 | 11 |

How many mugs per hour maximize profit?

- (A) 2
- (B) 4
- (C) 5
- (D) 6

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** MR is $8 for every mug. Mugs 1 to 4 each have MC below $8, so each adds to profit. The 5th mug has MC = $9 > $8, so it would cut profit by $1. Check: revenue minus variable cost is $3, $7, $10, $11, $10 and $7 for 1 to 6 mugs, highest at 4.

- (A) picks the output where MC is lowest. The firm should keep going while MR is above MC, not stop at the cheapest unit.
- (C) includes the 5th mug, whose MC ($9) is above MR ($8).
- (D) maximizes revenue ($48), not profit. The 5th and 6th mugs cost more than they earn.
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · core)

At its current output, a firm's marginal revenue is $20 and its marginal cost is $14. Its marginal cost is rising. To increase its profit, the firm should:

- (A) increase output, because the next unit adds more to revenue than to cost.
- (B) reduce output, because it is producing where MR is not equal to MC.
- (C) keep output the same, because MR > MC shows the firm is already making a profit.
- (D) keep output the same and raise its price until it equals MC.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Each extra unit changes profit by MR − MC = $20 − $14 = $6 at the margin. The firm should expand until MR = MC.

- (B) knows that MR should equal MC but moves the wrong way. Cutting output would remove units that add $6 each to profit.
- (C) confuses MR > MC (about the next unit) with total profit being positive (about all units). It also ignores that profit can still rise.
- (D) mixes up price and MC. Setting price equal to MC is not the profit-maximizing rule, and a price-taking firm cannot change its price.
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · core)

Harlow Print Studio, a fictional shop, must lower the price of its art posters to sell more per day:

| Posters per day | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Price ($) | 22 | 20 | 18 | 16 | 14 | 12 |
| MC of that poster ($) | 6 | 7 | 9 | 11 | 13 | 15 |

Which output and price maximize the studio's profit?

- (A) 3 posters at $18 each
- (B) 3 posters at $14 each
- (C) 5 posters at $14 each
- (D) 6 posters at $12 each

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** TR is $22, $40, $54, $64, $70 and $72, so MR is $22, $18, $14, $10, $6 and $2. Posters 1 to 3 have MR above MC ($22 > $6, $18 > $7, $14 > $9). The 4th has MR = $10 < MC = $11. So the studio sells 3 posters and charges the price at that quantity, $18.

- (B) has the right quantity but uses the MR of the 3rd poster ($14) as the price. Customers pay the price in the price row.
- (C) keeps producing while **price** is above MC. That treats price as if it were MR. The 4th and 5th posters add less to revenue than they cost.
- (D) maximizes total revenue ($72), not profit. The 6th poster even has MR of only $2.
</details>

## Question 4 (calculation · core)

Calloway Honey, a fictional producer, sells jars of honey at the market price of $14 each. Its fixed cost is $20 per day.

| Jars per day (Q) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total cost (TC, $) | 20 | 28 | 34 | 41 | 50 | 61 | 74 | 89 |

(a) Calculate the MC of each jar.
(b) Use MR and MC to find the profit-maximizing output.
(c) Calculate the profit at that output, and confirm it is the highest.
(d) The market price falls to $10. Find the new profit-maximizing output and the profit or loss.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

**(a)** MC = change in TC: **$8, $6, $7, $9, $11, $13, $15** for jars 1 to 7.

**(b)** MR = $14 for every jar. Jars 1 to 6 have MC below $14. The 7th jar has MC = $15 > $14. Output = **6 jars per day**.

**(c)** TR = 14 × 6 = $84; TC = $74; profit = **$10 per day**. Profits at each output are −$20, −$14, −$6, $1, $6, $9, **$10**, $9, so 6 jars is the highest.

**(d)** MR = $10. Jars 1 to 4 have MC of $8, $6, $7 and $9, all below $10. The 5th jar has MC = $11 > $10. Output = **4 jars**. TR = $40; TC = $50; result = **−$10** (a loss). Check: 3 jars gives $30 − $41 = −$11 and 5 jars gives $50 − $61 = −$11, so 4 jars gives the smallest loss.

Suggested mark points (4): 1 for all seven MCs; 1 for 6 jars with the MR and MC comparison; 1 for profit of $10; 1 for 4 jars and a loss of $10.

Common error: stopping at the jar with the lowest MC. The test is MR ≥ MC.
</details>

## Question 5 (graph · core)

Calloway Honey takes the market price, and its MC curve is U-shaped.

(a) Draw a correctly labelled graph showing Calloway's MR curve at a price P₁ and its MC curve. Label the profit-maximizing quantity Q₁.
(b) The market price rises to P₂. Show the new MR curve and label the new profit-maximizing quantity Q₂.
(c) Separately, the rent on Calloway's storage unit doubles. State what happens to the profit-maximizing quantity, and explain.

<details>
<summary>Model answer and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** Vertical axis: "Price, cost, revenue ($ per jar)". Horizontal axis: "Quantity (jars per day)". Draw a horizontal line at P₁ labelled MR₁ = P₁. Draw a U-shaped MC curve. Mark Q₁ where the **rising** part of MC crosses MR₁, with a dashed line down to the quantity axis.

**(b)** Draw a higher horizontal line at P₂, labelled MR₂ = P₂. It crosses the rising part of MC farther to the right, at **Q₂ > Q₁**. A higher price makes extra jars worth producing that were not worth it before.

**(c)** **No change.** Rent is a fixed cost. It does not change with output, so it does not change MC. MR and MC still cross at the same quantity. Calloway's profit falls by the extra rent.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Axes labelled; horizontal MR = P₁; U-shaped MC; Q₁ where rising MC crosses MR₁ |
| 1 | Higher MR₂ = P₂ with Q₂ to the right of Q₁ |
| 1 | Quantity unchanged after the rent rise |
| 1 | Reason: rent is a fixed cost, so MC (and MR) are unchanged; only profit falls |

Do not award the first point if Q₁ is placed where MC crosses MR on its falling part.
</details>

## Question 6 (constructed response · core)

Kittiwake Boat Tours, a fictional company, runs boat tours. To sell more tours per day it must lower the price. Its fixed cost is $60 per day.

| Tours per day (Q) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Price ($) | 90 | 80 | 70 | 60 | 50 | 40 |
| MC of that tour ($) | 20 | 25 | 30 | 35 | 40 | 45 |

(a) Calculate total revenue and marginal revenue for each output.
(b) Identify the profit-maximizing number of tours and the price. Explain using MR and MC.
(c) Calculate the maximum daily profit.
(d) The manager proposes running 5 tours a day "to maximize revenue". Evaluate the proposal with numbers.

<details>
<summary>Model answer and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)**

| Q | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TR ($) | 90 | 160 | 210 | 240 | 250 | 240 |
| MR ($) | 90 | 70 | 50 | 30 | 10 | −10 |

**(b)** Tours 1 to 3 have MR above MC ($90 > $20, $70 > $25, $50 > $30), so each adds to profit. The 4th tour has MR = $30 < MC = $35, so it would cut profit by $5. **3 tours a day at $70 each.**

**(c)** TC = $60 + $20 + $25 + $30 = $135. Profit = $210 − $135 = **$75 per day**.

**(d)** Revenue is highest at 5 tours ($250). But TC at 5 tours is $60 + $150 = $210, so profit is $250 − $210 = **$40**, which is $35 less than at 3 tours. The 4th and 5th tours each add more to cost than to revenue (MR of $30 and $10 against MC of $35 and $40). The proposal raises revenue but lowers profit, so it should be rejected if the goal is profit.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Correct TR **and** MR rows |
| 1 | 3 tours, supported by MR ≥ MC up to the 3rd tour and MC > MR for the 4th |
| 1 | Price of $70 read from the price row (not $50 or $30) |
| 1 | Profit of $75 |
| 1 | Profit at 5 tours ($40) compared with $75, with the reason that MC > MR for the extra tours |

Accept the total approach in (b) if the full profit row is shown ($10, $55, $75, $70, $40, −$15), as long as the MR and MC comparison is also given.
</details>

## Question 7 (explanation · stretch)

Use Calloway Honey from Question 4 (price $14, fixed cost $20, profit-maximizing output 6 jars).

(a) Explain, using marginal reasoning, why a firm producing where MC is greater than MR can raise its profit by producing less.
(b) The city adds a fee of $2 on every jar sold, paid by the firm. Find the new profit-maximizing output and the profit or loss.
(c) Instead of (b), the city charges a yearly licence fee that works out at $30 per day, whatever the output. Find the profit-maximizing output and the profit or loss.
(d) Explain why the two policies have different effects on output.

<details>
<summary>Model answer and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** The last unit produced adds MC to cost but only MR to revenue. If MC > MR, that unit lowers profit by MC − MR. Cutting it out saves more cost than it loses in revenue, so profit rises. The firm should keep cutting until MR = MC.

**(b)** The fee raises every jar's MC by $2: $10, $8, $9, $11, $13, $15, $17. Jars 1 to 5 have MC below $14; the 6th has MC = $15 > $14. New output = **5 jars**. TC = $61 + $10 = $71; TR = $70; result = **−$1** (a loss of $1). Check: 4 jars gives $56 − $58 = −$2 and 6 jars gives $84 − $86 = −$2.

**(c)** The licence is a fixed cost: it does not change MC. Output stays at **6 jars**. Profit = $10 − $30 = **−$20** (a loss of $20).

**(d)** A per-unit fee is a variable cost, so it raises MC. MC now crosses MR at a lower quantity, and output falls. A licence is a fixed cost. It raises total cost by the same amount at every output, so MC and the profit-maximizing output do not change; only profit falls.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | (a) MC > MR means the last unit lowers profit; producing less raises profit |
| 1 | (b) 5 jars, with the new MCs compared with $14 |
| 1 | (b) result of −$1 |
| 1 | (c) 6 jars and −$20 |
| 1 | (d) per-unit fee raises MC; licence is fixed, so MC and output are unchanged |
</details>
