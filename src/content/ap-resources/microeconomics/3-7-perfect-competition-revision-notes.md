## Recap

- **Features:** many small buyers and sellers, an identical product, no barriers to entry or exit, good information. No firm has market power.
- Each firm is a **price taker**: its demand curve is horizontal at the market price, so **P = MR = AR = d**.
- Profit is maximised where **MC = MR = P**, on the rising part of MC.
- **Short run:** price can be above minimum ATC (profit) or below it (loss). The firm keeps producing while P ≥ AVC.
- **Long run:** profit attracts entry (supply right, price falls); losses cause exit (supply left, price rises). This stops when **P = minimum ATC** and economic profit is zero.
- **Prices as signals:** the price tells buyers the marginal cost of production and tells sellers buyers' marginal benefit, and gives both a reason to respond.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | How to use it |
|---|---|
| **Profit = (P − ATC) × q** | Read ATC at the chosen q, not at its minimum |
| TR = P × q; TC = ATC × q | Profit = TR − TC |
| **P = MC** | Allocative efficiency (marginal benefit = marginal cost) |
| **P = minimum ATC** | Productive efficiency (efficient scale); long run only |
| Number of firms = Q ÷ q | Long run: market quantity at P = min ATC ÷ output per firm |

| Industry type | Long-run supply | Long-run price after demand rises |
|---|---|---|
| Constant cost | horizontal | unchanged |
| Increasing cost | upward-sloping | higher |
| Decreasing cost | downward-sloping | lower |

## Assumptions behind the model

- Identical firms with the same cost curves; no barriers, so the number of firms can change in the long run.
- Efficiency conclusions assume no externalities (Unit 6).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Drawing a downward-sloping demand curve for the firm.
2. Choosing output where P − ATC is largest instead of where MC = MR.
3. Measuring the profit rectangle at minimum ATC instead of at the chosen q.
4. Saying entry raises each firm's output (it falls; market output rises).
5. Forgetting to draw the market and firm side by side with the same price.
6. Claiming productive efficiency in the short run.

## Quick self-check

1. P = $14, q = 200 where MC = MR, ATC = $11. Profit? *((14 − 11) × 200 = $600 economic profit.)*
2. What happens in the long run? *(Firms enter, supply shifts right, price falls to minimum ATC, profit falls to zero.)*
3. In an increasing-cost industry, demand rises permanently. Long-run price? *(Higher than before, because each firm's costs rise as the industry grows.)*
