## Understanding

- I can state the profit-maximizing rule (MR = MC, with MC rising) and explain it with marginal reasoning. *(Guide: "Marginal revenue and marginal cost"; Practice Q2, Q7(a))*
- I can explain why MR equals the price for a price-taking firm but is below the price for a firm that must cut its price to sell more. *(Guide: "What MR looks like for different firms"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why the MR = MC output may give a loss, not a profit. *(Guide: Worked example 1(d); Practice Q4(d))*
- I can explain why maximizing revenue or profit per unit is not the same as maximizing profit. *(Guide: Worked example 2(d); Practice Q1, Q6(d))*

## Calculation and graphing

- I can calculate MR and MC from tables of price, TR and TC. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q4, Q6)*
- I can find the profit-maximizing output in whole units by producing every unit with MR ≥ MC. *(Guide: "Using the rule with whole units"; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can confirm the answer with the total approach (largest TR − TC) and state the profit with units. *(Guide: Worked example 1(c); Practice Q4(c), Q6(c))*
- I can read the price of a price-setting firm from the price row or demand curve, not from MR. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q3, Q6(b))*
- I can draw a labelled MR and MC graph and mark the profit-maximizing quantity where rising MC crosses MR. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can show how a change in the market price changes a price taker's output. *(Practice Q5(b))*
- I can explain why a change in fixed cost leaves output unchanged but a change in variable cost does not. *(Guide: "Why fixed costs do not change the output"; Practice Q5(c), Q7)*
- I can reject a crossing where MC is still falling. *(Guide: Worked example 1(d))*
