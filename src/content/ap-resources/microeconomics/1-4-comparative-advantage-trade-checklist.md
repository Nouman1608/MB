Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/1-4-comparative-advantage-trade-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/1-4-comparative-advantage-trade-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define absolute advantage and comparative advantage in one sentence each, and say how they differ. *(Guide: "Absolute advantage" and "Comparative advantage")*
- I can explain why specialisation should follow comparative advantage, not absolute advantage. *(Guide: Worked example 1(c); Practice Q7(a))*
- I can explain why, with two goods and two producers, each producer has the comparative advantage in a different good, and what happens when costs are equal. *(Guide: "Comparative advantage"; Practice Q7(c))*
- I can explain why trade lets a country consume outside its PPC without shifting the PPC. *(Guide: Worked example 3 and Figure 2)*

## Calculation and graphing

- I can find absolute advantage and both opportunity costs from **output** data, with units. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can find absolute advantage and both opportunity costs from **input** data, and check by converting to output. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q4)*
- I can draw two PPCs on one labelled graph and read absolute advantage from the intercepts and comparative advantage from the slopes. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate total output before and after specialisation. *(Guide: "Specialisation and gains from trade"; Practice Q6(a)–(b))*
- I can calculate each side's consumption and gain after trade at given terms. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6(c)–(d))*

## Reasoning

- I can state the range of mutually beneficial terms of trade and explain why it lies between the two opportunity costs. *(Guide: "Terms of trade"; Practice Q3)*
- I can test a proposed rate and say which side would reject it, and why. *(Practice Q4(c)–(d), Q7(b))*
- I can show with a calculation that a point after trade is outside a country's own PPC. *(Practice Q5(c), Q6(d))*
