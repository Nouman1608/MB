## Recap

- **Imperfectly competitive** markets are those where firms have some control over price (market power).
- Product markets: **monopolistic competition** (many sellers, differentiated products, low barriers), **oligopoly** (a few large firms, high barriers), **monopoly** (one seller, no close substitutes, very high barriers).
- Factor markets: **monopsony**, a single buyer (for example, one employer hiring most local workers).
- Every firm still aims to **maximise profit**; the structure changes the outcome.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | What it means |
|---|---|
| Price taker: D = MR = P | every unit sells at the market price |
| Downward-sloping demand: MR < P | to sell one more, the price falls on **all** units |
| **MR = new price − (price cut × units sold before)** | quantity effect minus price effect |
| Straight-line demand: MR has the same price intercept and twice the slope | MR = 0 halfway to the demand curve's quantity intercept |
| MR > 0 ⇔ demand elastic; MR < 0 ⇔ demand inelastic | TR is largest where MR = 0 |
| Efficiency needs P = MC | imperfect competitors stop where P > MC, so output is too low |

| Barrier to entry | Example |
|---|---|
| High fixed or start-up costs | steel mill, power grid |
| Legal barriers | patent, copyright, government licence |
| Exclusive ownership of a key resource | the only deposit of a mineral |

## Assumptions behind the model

- The firm charges the **same price to every buyer** (no price discrimination; that comes in Topic 4.3).
- All else is held constant along the demand curve.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Writing P = MR for a firm with a downward-sloping demand curve.
2. Saying MR is below price because costs rise. It is a revenue idea only.
3. Drawing MR on or above the demand curve.
4. Confusing P > MC (inefficiency) with P > ATC (profit).
5. Mixing up monopoly (one seller) and monopsony (one buyer).
6. Forgetting that profit lasts only if barriers to entry block new firms.

## Quick self-check

1. A firm's demand is P = 30 − 3Q. What is the MR of the 3rd unit? *(TR at 2 = 24 × 2 = 48; TR at 3 = 21 × 3 = 63; MR = 15, below the $21 price.)*
2. At a firm's chosen output, P = $40 and MC = $25. Is output efficient? *(No. The next unit is worth about $40 to a buyer but costs $25, so output is too low.)*
3. Name the barrier: a firm holds the only licence to run buses on an island. *(Legal barrier.)*
