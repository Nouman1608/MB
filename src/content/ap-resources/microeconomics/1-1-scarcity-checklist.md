Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/1-1-scarcity-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/1-1-scarcity-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define scarcity in one sentence, linking limited resources to society's wants and needs. *(Guide: "The basic problem: scarcity"; Practice Q1, Q6)*
- I can explain why scarcity is relative and why it affects rich countries as well as poor ones. *(Guide: "The basic problem: scarcity"; Practice Q6)*
- I can name the four factors of production and give an example of each. *(Guide: "Resources: the factors of production")*
- I can explain the causes of scarcity: limited resources with alternative uses, limited time, and growing wants. *(Guide: "Why most resources are scarce"; Figure 1)*
- I can explain the difference between scarcity and a shortage, with an example of each. *(Guide: "Scarcity is not a shortage"; Practice Q6)*

## Skills

- I can classify a list of real resources as land, labour, capital or entrepreneurship, and spot money as not a factor. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can use a simple calculation to show that wants exceed the resources available. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate the opportunity cost of a choice when a resource can go to two uses, dividing the right way round. *(Guide: Worked example 2(c); Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why a rival resource is scarce, using limited quantity and an alternative use. *(Guide: Worked example 1(b); Practice Q3, Q5)*
- I can explain why established knowledge is non-rival and so may not be scarce, and why using it still needs scarce resources. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q3, Q7)*
- I can explain why adding resources meets some wants but does not end scarcity. *(Guide: Worked example 2(d); Practice Q4(d), Q7(c))*
- I can link scarcity to choice, trade-offs and opportunity cost, naming only the next best alternative as the cost. *(Guide: "Scarcity, choices and trade-offs")*
