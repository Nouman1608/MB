## Recap

- A **monopsony** is the single (or dominant) **buyer** of a type of labour. Workers have few other employers to go to.
- It faces the **upward-sloping market supply of labour**, so it is a **wage setter**: to hire more, it must offer a higher wage.
- With one wage for all, an extra worker costs their wage **plus** the raise to all existing workers, so **MRC > W**.
- It hires where **MRC = MRP** and pays the wage on the **supply curve** at that quantity.
- Compared with a competitive labour market, it hires **fewer** workers at a **lower** wage, and the wage is **below MRP**. The result is **inefficient**: there is a deadweight loss.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | How to use it |
|---|---|
| **TLC = W × L** | Wage from the supply schedule × number hired |
| **MRC = ΔTLC ÷ ΔL** | Always above the wage after the first worker |
| **MRC = new wage + (raise × existing workers)** | Quick check on a table |
| Linear supply **W = a + bL → MRC = a + 2bL** | Same intercept, twice the slope |
| **Hiring rule: MRC = MRP** | Gives Lm |
| **Wage: from S at Lm** | Gives Wm, below MRC and MRP |
| **Competitive outcome: S = MRP** | Gives Lc > Lm and Wc > Wm |
| **DWL = ½ × (MRP − W at Lm) × (Lc − Lm)** | For straight-line curves |

## Graph checklist

- Axes: wage ($ per period) and quantity of labour.
- S upward; MRC above S, steeper, same vertical intercept; MRP = D downward.
- Lm below MRC = MRP; Wm on S at Lm; competitive point where S crosses MRP.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Reading the wage at the MRC = MRP point instead of from the supply curve.
2. Drawing MRC below S, or parallel to it.
3. Saying the monopsony hires more workers than a competitive market.
4. Mixing up monopsony (one buyer) with monopoly (one seller).
5. Forgetting the raise for existing workers when calculating MRC from a table.

## Quick self-check

1. 10 workers will work for $15 an hour; 11 workers need $16. What is the MRC of the 11th worker? *(11 × 16 − 10 × 15 = 176 − 150 = $26.)*
2. Where does a monopsony read its wage? *(From the supply curve, at the quantity where MRC = MRP.)*
3. Why is the monopsony wage below the competitive wage? *(It hires fewer workers, and fewer workers will work for a lower wage on the upward-sloping supply curve.)*
