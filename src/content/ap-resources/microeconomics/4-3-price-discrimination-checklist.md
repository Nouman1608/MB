Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/4-3-price-discrimination-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/4-3-price-discrimination-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define price discrimination and tell it apart from a price difference caused by a cost difference. *(Guide: "What price discrimination is")*
- I can state the three conditions a firm needs: market power, a way to identify buyers, and a way to stop resale. *(Guide: "The three conditions"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why a perfectly competitive firm cannot price discriminate. *(Guide: "The three conditions")*
- I can explain why the group with less elastic demand pays the higher price. *(Guide: "Who pays more"; Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can explain why MR equals demand under perfect price discrimination. *(Guide: "Perfect price discrimination"; Practice Q7)*

## Calculation and graphing

- I can find the single-price monopoly quantity, price and profit, reading the price from demand. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate consumer surplus, profit and deadweight loss for a single-price monopolist and for a perfectly price-discriminating one. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can find the profit-maximising price for each group with MR = MC in each market, and compare total profit with a single price. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can draw a labelled graph showing a single-price monopoly and then perfect price discrimination, with the right areas shaded. *(Guide: Figures 1 and 2; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why perfect price discrimination is allocatively efficient but leaves consumers with no surplus. *(Guide: "Perfect price discrimination"; Practice Q2, Q7)*
- I can explain which buyers gain and which lose when a firm moves from a single price to group pricing. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Interpretation)*
- I can give practical reasons why perfect price discrimination is rare. *(Practice Q7)*
