Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/2-1-demand-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/2-1-demand-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why markets need well-defined property rights. *(Guide: "Markets need property rights, incentives and constraints"; Practice Q6)*
- I can give examples of incentives buyers respond to and of income, time and legal constraints they face. *(Guide: same section; Practice Q6)*
- I can define demand and quantity demanded, and explain "willing and able" and "other things equal". *(Guide: "What demand means")*
- I can state the law of demand and explain the downward slope with the substitution effect, the income effect and diminishing marginal utility. *(Guide: "Why the demand curve slopes downward"; Practice Q3)*

## Calculation and graphing

- I can build an individual demand schedule from marginal benefit values, buying each unit while MB is at least the price. *(Guide: "From marginal benefit to individual demand"; Practice Q4)*
- I can add individual quantities at each price to find market demand. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can draw a demand curve with both axes labelled (price with units; quantity with units and period) and the curve labelled. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can show a change in quantity demanded as a movement along the curve, and a change in demand as a shift. *(Guide: Figures 1 and 2; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can name the determinants of demand and give the direction of the shift for each: income (normal and inferior), substitutes, complements, tastes, expectations and number of buyers. *(Guide: "The determinants of demand"; Practice Q2, Q7)*
- I can tell a change in quantity demanded from a change in demand, and use the right words for each. *(Guide: "Change in quantity demanded versus change in demand"; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can explain how a legal constraint, such as a minimum age, changes market demand. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can read a demand curve as a marginal benefit curve, both across from a price and up from a quantity. *(Guide: Worked example 2, "Interpretation")*
