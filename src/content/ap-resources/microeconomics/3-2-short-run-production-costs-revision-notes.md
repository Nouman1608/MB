Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, graphs and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/3-2-short-run-production-costs-study-guide/).

## Recap

- In the short run some inputs are fixed, so costs split into **fixed** (do not vary with output, paid even at zero output) and **variable** (rise with output, zero at zero output).
- **Specialisation and the division of labour** make MC fall at low output. **Diminishing marginal returns** make MC rise after that.
- **AFC** falls at every output. **AVC** and **ATC** are U-shaped. **MC** cuts both at their minimum points.
- The gap between ATC and AVC is AFC, so it narrows as output rises. Minimum ATC is at a larger output than minimum AVC.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | How to use it |
|---|---|
| **TC = TFC + TVC** | TFC = TC at Q = 0 |
| **MC = ΔTC ÷ ΔQ = ΔTVC ÷ ΔQ** | A change between rows; plot halfway between outputs |
| **AFC = TFC ÷ Q; AVC = TVC ÷ Q; ATC = TC ÷ Q** | Ratios within one row |
| **ATC = AFC + AVC** | Find any one from the other two |
| MC = w ÷ MP; AVC = w ÷ AP (labour the only variable input) | Max MP ↔ min MC; max AP ↔ min AVC |
| MC < average → average falls; MC > average → average rises | So MC crosses AVC and ATC at their minimums |

| Change | Curves that shift |
|---|---|
| Variable input price rises (wages, materials) | MC, AVC, ATC up; AFC unchanged |
| Fixed cost rises (rent, insurance, licence) | AFC, ATC up; MC, AVC unchanged |
| Productivity rises | MC, AVC, ATC down |

## Assumptions behind the model

- At least one input is fixed; input prices and technology are constant along each curve.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying fixed cost is zero at zero output.
2. Shifting MC when only a fixed cost changes.
3. Drawing MC through ATC or AVC anywhere other than their lowest points.
4. Drawing AFC as U-shaped, or letting it touch the axis.
5. Placing the minimum of ATC at the same output as (or to the left of) the minimum of AVC.
6. Unlabelled curves and axes: label "Cost ($ per unit)" and "Output (units per period)".

## Quick self-check

1. TC is $500 at Q = 0 and $740 at Q = 4. Find TFC, TVC, AVC and ATC at Q = 4. *(TFC $500; TVC $240; AVC $60; ATC $185)*
2. A firm's insurance premium rises. What happens to MC? *(Nothing: insurance is a fixed cost.)*
3. MC is $18 and ATC is $22. Is ATC rising or falling? *(Falling, because MC is below ATC.)*
