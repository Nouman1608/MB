Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/4-4-monopolistic-competition-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/4-4-monopolistic-competition-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can list the features of monopolistic competition and tell it apart from perfect competition, oligopoly and monopoly. *(Guide: "A market between two extremes"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why each firm's demand curve slopes down but is fairly elastic. *(Guide: "A market between two extremes")*
- I can explain what advertising is meant to do to demand, and how it affects ATC and MC. *(Guide: "Differentiation and advertising"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why zero economic profit still means a normal return for the owners. *(Guide: FAQ; "The short run")*

## Calculation and graphing

- I can find the profit-maximising output and price from a table or graph, and calculate profit or loss. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q4, Q6)*
- I can decide whether a loss-making firm should stay open in the short run by comparing price with AVC. *(Guide: Worked example 2(b); Practice Q6)*
- I can draw a labelled short-run graph showing an economic profit or loss. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
- I can draw the long-run equilibrium graph with demand tangent to ATC to the left of minimum ATC. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate excess capacity, consumer surplus and the deadweight loss in long-run equilibrium. *(Guide: "Why the long run is inefficient"; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how entry (after profits) or exit (after losses) shifts each firm's demand until P = ATC. *(Guide: "The long run"; Practice Q3, Q4)*
- I can explain why long-run equilibrium is neither allocatively nor productively efficient. *(Guide: "Why the long run is inefficient"; Practice Q2, Q7)*
- I can compare long-run outcomes with perfect competition and monopoly, and weigh excess capacity against product variety. *(Guide: comparison table; Practice Q7)*
