Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/6-2-externalities-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/6-2-externalities-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define an externality and classify one as positive or negative, in production or in consumption. *(Guide: "What an externality is"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why rational buyers and sellers ignore external costs and benefits. *(Guide: "Why markets ignore external effects"; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain how poorly defined property rights and high transaction costs cause externalities. *(Guide: "Why markets ignore external effects"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why people free ride when a benefit is non-excludable. *(Guide: Worked example 3(d); Practice Q6)*

## Calculation and graphing

- I can find the market and socially optimal quantities from equations, using MSC = MPC + MEC or MSB = MPB + MEB. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q4, Q7)*
- I can calculate the DWL of a negative or positive externality. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q4, Q7)*
- I can draw labelled graphs of all four types of externality and shade the DWL on the correct side. *(Guide: types table and Figures 1 and 2; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can calculate a corrective tax or subsidy, its effect on prices and its revenue or cost. *(Guide: Worked examples 1(d) and 2(c); Practice Q3, Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why the corrective tax equals the MEC at the optimal quantity, not at the market quantity. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can compare taxes, subsidies, regulation, public provision and property rights as ways to correct an externality. *(Guide: "Policies that correct externalities")*
- I can explain why private bargaining reaches the efficient outcome when transaction costs are low, whoever holds the right. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
