## Understanding

- I can define market equilibrium as the price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied, and explain what "the market clears" means. *(Guide: "Market equilibrium"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why the price moves back towards equilibrium when it is too high or too low. *(Guide: "Why the market settles there"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain how the equilibrium price rations goods and gives sellers an incentive to move resources. *(Guide: "The price as a signal"; Practice Q6)*
- I can define consumer surplus and producer surplus and link them to willingness to pay and minimum acceptable price. *(Guide: "Consumer surplus", "Producer surplus")*

## Calculation and graphing

- I can find the equilibrium price and quantity from a table. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can find the equilibrium quantity and surplus in a market of individual buyers and sellers. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate consumer, producer and total surplus as triangle areas on a straight-line graph, measuring heights from the price. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can draw a labelled supply and demand graph with equilibrium marked and consumer and producer surplus shaded and labelled. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can tell total spending (price × quantity) apart from consumer surplus. *(Guide: Worked example 1(c); Practice Q2)*

## Reasoning

- I can use marginal benefit and marginal cost to explain why total surplus is largest at the competitive equilibrium. *(Guide: "Why equilibrium maximises total surplus"; Practice Q3, Q7)*
- I can show that trading more or less than the equilibrium quantity lowers total surplus. *(Guide: Worked example 2(c); Practice Q4, Q7)*
- I can state when a market equilibrium would not be efficient. *(Practice Q7)*
