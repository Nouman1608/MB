## Recap

- A change in the **wage** moves the market **along** the labour demand and supply curves.
- **Labour demand shifts** when MRP changes at every quantity: a change in the **output price** (often from product demand), in **worker productivity**, in the **prices of other inputs**, or in the number of firms hiring.
- **Labour supply shifts** with **immigration**, **education and training**, **working conditions**, **age distribution**, **alternative options**, **preferences for leisure** and **cultural expectations**.
- Demand up → wage up, employment up. Supply up → wage down, employment up.

## Key relationships

| Change | Curve | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Output price rises | D<sub>L</sub> | right |
| Worker productivity rises | D<sub>L</sub> | right |
| Price of a substitute input (machines that replace workers) falls | D<sub>L</sub> | usually left |
| Immigration of workers into the market | S<sub>L</sub> | right |
| More people trained for the job | S<sub>L</sub> | right |
| Safer or more pleasant working conditions | S<sub>L</sub> | right |
| Higher wages in an alternative job | S<sub>L</sub> | left |
| Stronger preference for leisure | S<sub>L</sub> | left |

## Assumptions and scope

- The labour market is competitive: the wage settles where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
- "Other things constant" for every single shift.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Shifting a curve because the **wage** changed.
2. Shifting **supply** when the output price changes (it shifts demand).
3. Shifting **demand** for a change in working conditions (it shifts supply).
4. Saying higher pay elsewhere **increases** supply in this market.
5. Claiming a definite wage change when both curves shift and no sizes are given.
6. Unlabelled graphs: name both axes and both curves, and mark old and new equilibria.

## Quick self-check

1. A worker's MP is 9 units. The output price rises from $10 to $12. What happens to the worker's MRP? *(It rises from $90 to $108.)*
2. A country makes it easier for qualified electricians to immigrate. What happens to the electricians' wage and employment? *(Supply increases: wage falls, employment rises.)*
3. Demand for teachers rises while fewer graduates choose teaching. What is certain? *(The wage rises; the change in employment is indeterminate.)*
