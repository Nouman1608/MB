## Recap

- **Price ceiling** = legal maximum price. Binding only **below** equilibrium → shortage; quantity traded = Qs.
- **Price floor** = legal minimum price. Binding only **above** equilibrium → surplus; quantity traded = Qd.
- **Quota** = legal maximum quantity. Buyers pay the demand price at the quota; the gap above the supply price goes to licence holders.
- **Per-unit tax** on sellers: supply shifts **up by t**. **Per-unit subsidy** to sellers: supply shifts **down by s**.
- In an **efficient** competitive market, all of these lower total surplus. The lost surplus is **deadweight loss (DWL)**.

## Key relationships

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Tax revenue | t × Q after the tax |
| Subsidy cost | s × Q after the subsidy |
| Price sellers keep (tax) | price buyers pay − t |
| Price sellers receive (subsidy) | price buyers pay + s |
| Buyers' share of a tax | rise in the price buyers pay |
| Sellers' share of a tax | fall in the price sellers keep |
| DWL of a tax or subsidy (straight lines) | ½ × t (or s) × change in quantity |
| DWL of a price control or quota | ½ × (efficient Q − traded Q) × (demand price − supply price at traded Q) |

| Policy | Q traded | CS | PS | Government |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Binding ceiling | falls | may rise or fall | falls | — |
| Binding floor | falls | falls | may rise or fall | — |
| Tax | falls | falls | falls | gains revenue |
| Subsidy | rises | rises | rises | pays more than CS + PS gain |

**Incidence rule:** the **more inelastic** side bears more of a tax and gains more of a subsidy.

## Assumptions

- A perfectly competitive market with no externalities (so the starting equilibrium is efficient).
- Under shortages, units go to the buyers who value them most (otherwise the loss is larger).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Drawing a ceiling above equilibrium or a floor below it and calling it binding.
2. Shifting a curve for a price ceiling or floor.
3. Using quantity demanded as the amount sold under a ceiling.
4. Counting tax revenue as part of the deadweight loss.
5. Assuming the side that hands over the tax bears it.
6. Forgetting to label both prices (buyers pay, sellers keep) on a tax graph.

## Quick self-check

1. Qd = 100 − 2P and Qs = 3P − 50, with a $5 tax on sellers. Find the prices and quantity. *(Equilibrium was $30 and 40. With the tax: 100 − 2P = 3(P − 5) − 50, so P = $33 for buyers, $28 for sellers, Q = 34.)*
2. Revenue and DWL? *(5 × 34 = 170; DWL = ½ × 5 × 6 = 15.)*
3. Who bears more of the tax, and why? *(Buyers, $3 of $5: demand is less elastic than supply here.)*
