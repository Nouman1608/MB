Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, graphs and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/3-5-profit-maximization-study-guide/).

## Recap

- Firms are assumed to choose the output that makes **economic profit (TR − TC)** as large as possible.
- The change in profit from one more unit is **MR − MC**. If MR > MC, produce more; if MC > MR, produce less.
- So the **profit-maximizing rule** is: produce where **MR = MC**, on the rising part of MC.
- The rule gives the **largest profit or the smallest loss**. It does not promise a profit.
- A price-taking firm has **MR = price**. A firm that must cut its price to sell more has **MR < price**.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | How to use it |
|---|---|
| **Profit = TR − TC** | total approach: pick the output with the biggest gap |
| **MR = change in TR ÷ change in Q** | for a price taker, MR = P |
| **MC = change in TC ÷ change in Q** | fixed cost drops out of every change |
| **Change in profit = MR − MC** | marginal approach: add units while this is ≥ 0 |
| Price of a price-setting firm | read from the demand (price) row at the chosen output, not from MR |

| Change | Profit-maximizing output |
|---|---|
| Fixed cost rises (rent, licence) | no change; profit falls by the same amount |
| Variable input cost rises (MC up) | falls |
| Market price rises (price taker: MR up) | rises |

## Assumptions behind the model

- The firm's goal is maximum economic profit (implicit costs included).
- The firm knows its revenue and costs at each output.
- Output is chosen for a given set of fixed inputs (the short run).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Choosing the output with the highest **profit per unit** or the highest **revenue**.
2. Using price instead of MR for a firm that must lower its price to sell more.
3. Giving the price of a price-setting firm as the value of MR or MC at the crossing.
4. Stopping at a crossing where MC is still **falling**.
5. Saying a rise in fixed cost reduces output.
6. Forgetting that MR = MC can give a loss.

## Quick self-check

1. A price taker sells at $20. The MC of its 9th unit is $18 and of its 10th is $23 (MC rising). How many units? *(9: the 10th unit's MC is above MR.)*
2. A firm sells 5 units at $40 each or 6 units at $37 each. What is the MR of the 6th unit? *(6 × 37 − 5 × 40 = $222 − $200 = $22.)*
3. A firm's yearly licence fee doubles. What happens to its profit-maximizing output? *(Nothing: MC is unchanged. Profit falls.)*
