## Recap

- A **perfectly competitive labour market** has many employers, many workers with the same skills, good information and free movement between jobs.
- **Market** supply and demand for labour set the wage. Each **firm is a wage taker**: it can hire any number of workers at that wage.
- For the firm, **MRC = W** at every quantity, so its **labour supply curve is horizontal** at the market wage.
- The firm hires workers while **MRP > W** and stops where **MRP = MRC = W**. Its downward-sloping MRP curve is its **labour demand curve**.
- A firm can be a **monopoly in its output market** and still a **wage taker** in its labour market.
- With other inputs, the firm also uses the **least-cost** and **profit-maximising** rules below.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | How to use it |
|---|---|
| **MRP = ΔTR ÷ ΔL = MP × MR** | Revenue added by one more worker, in dollars |
| **VMP = MP × P** | Equals MRP only if the output market is perfectly competitive (MR = P) |
| Output market power: MR < P | So MRP < VMP; use MRP to decide |
| **Wage taker: MRC = W** | Constant, however many are hired |
| **Hiring rule: MRP = MRC = W** | Hire the last worker whose MRP ≥ W (on the falling part of MRP) |
| **Least cost: MP_L ÷ W = MP_K ÷ r** | If MP_L ÷ W is larger, shift spending to labour |
| **Profit maximum: MRP_L ÷ W = MRP_K ÷ r = 1** | Implies least cost; least cost does not imply profit maximum |

| Change | Effect for one firm |
|---|---|
| Market wage falls | Movement down along MRP: more workers hired |
| Market wage rises | Movement up along MRP: fewer workers hired |
| Output price or productivity changes | MRP curve shifts (Topic 5.2) |

## Assumptions

- Other inputs are fixed when we study the labour decision on its own.
- Workers are identical, so one wage applies to all.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Comparing MP (units of output) with the wage (dollars).
2. Using VMP for a firm that has to cut its price to sell more.
3. Drawing the firm's labour supply as upward-sloping, or as the market supply curve.
4. Stopping where MP peaks, or where total revenue is largest.
5. Calling equal MP-per-dollar ratios "profit maximising" when MRP is still above the input price.
6. Treating a wage change as a shift of labour demand.

## Quick self-check

1. A worker adds 12 units of output that sell for $5 each in a competitive market. The wage is $50. Should the firm hire them? *(Yes: MRP = 12 × 5 = $60 > $50.)*
2. MP_L = 10, W = $20, MP_K = 30, r = $40. To cut costs, which input should the firm use more of? *(Capital: 30 ÷ 40 = 0.75 per dollar is more than 10 ÷ 20 = 0.5.)*
3. Why is a wage taker's MRC equal to the wage? *(It can hire another worker at the market wage without raising pay for anyone.)*
