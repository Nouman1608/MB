## Recap

- **Explicit costs** are money paid to others. **Implicit costs** are the opportunity costs of the owner's own resources.
- Typical implicit costs: the owner's forgone salary (their time), forgone interest on their own savings (cost of financial capital), forgone rent on property they own, and compensation for risk.
- **Accounting profit** ignores implicit costs. **Economic profit** subtracts them, so it is never larger than accounting profit.
- **Normal profit** is the accounting profit that exactly covers implicit costs. Economic profit is then zero.
- Firms respond to **economic** profit, because it compares this use of resources with the next-best one.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | How to use it |
|---|---|
| **TR = P × Q** | Revenue from sales |
| **Accounting profit = TR − explicit costs** | What the firm's books show |
| **Economic profit = TR − explicit costs − implicit costs** | What the owner gains over the best alternative |
| Economic profit = accounting profit − implicit costs | Quick check between the two |
| **Economic profit = (P − ATC) × Q** | When ATC includes implicit costs |
| Normal profit: accounting profit = implicit costs | Economic profit = 0, P = ATC |

| Economic profit | Signal | Likely response |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | Resources earn more here than elsewhere | Expansion and entry of new firms |
| Zero (normal profit) | Resources earn exactly their next-best return | No entry or exit |
| Negative (loss) | Resources would earn more elsewhere | Exit over time |

## Assumptions behind the model

- Implicit costs are valued at the next-best use of the owner's resources.
- Owners want to earn at least as much as their best alternative.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying economic profit is larger than accounting profit.
2. Calling interest on a bank loan an implicit cost (it is explicit).
3. Treating zero economic profit as a loss.
4. Forgetting compensation for risk or the owner's time when listing implicit costs.
5. Saying firms enter because of high **accounting** profit.
6. Using ATC without checking whether it includes implicit costs.

## Quick self-check

1. TR is $50,000, explicit costs $30,000 and implicit costs $15,000. Find both profits. *(Accounting $20,000; economic $5,000.)*
2. Accounting profit is $24,000 and implicit costs are $24,000. What kind of profit is this? *(Normal profit: economic profit is zero.)*
3. Which is implicit: rent paid to a landlord, or rent the owner could earn on her own shop? *(The rent she could earn on her own shop.)*
