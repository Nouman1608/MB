## Understanding

- I can list the features of a perfectly competitive market and explain why they leave each firm with no market power. *(Guide: "What makes a market perfectly competitive")*
- I can explain why a price-taking firm's demand curve is horizontal and why P = MR = AR = d. *(Guide: "The price taker's demand curve"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain how prices signal marginal cost to buyers and marginal benefit to sellers, and give both a reason to act. *(Guide: "Long-run equilibrium and efficiency"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why long-run equilibrium is allocatively efficient (P = MC) and productively efficient (P = minimum ATC). *(Guide: "Long-run equilibrium and efficiency"; Practice Q6)*

## Calculation and graphing

- I can find the profit-maximising output from a cost table using MC = MR = P. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate economic profit or loss as TR − TC or (P − ATC) × q, using ATC at the chosen output. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q4)*
- I can find the long-run price and the number of firms from minimum ATC and market demand. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*
- I can draw side-by-side market and firm graphs with a shared price axis, showing short-run profit or loss as a shaded rectangle. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can draw a firm in long-run equilibrium where P = MR = MC = minimum ATC. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how short-run profits lead to entry and losses lead to exit, and what this does to market supply, price, firm output and the number of firms. *(Guide: "From short run to long run"; Practice Q5)*
- I can decide whether a loss-making firm should keep producing in the short run by comparing P with AVC. *(Guide: Worked example 1(b); Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can compare long-run supply and long-run price in constant-cost, increasing-cost and decreasing-cost industries. *(Guide: Figure 3; Practice Q3, Q7)*
