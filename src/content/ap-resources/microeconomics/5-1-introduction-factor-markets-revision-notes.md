## Recap

- **Factor markets** are markets for labour, capital and land. **Households sell** factor services; **firms buy** them. This is the reverse of a product market.
- **Factor prices:** labour → **wage**; capital → **interest**; land → **rent**. They act as incentives and signal scarcity.
- Labour demand is a **derived demand**: it comes from the demand for the product the workers make.
- A firm's hiring decision depends on the factor's **productivity** (MP), the **output price** (or MR) and the **cost** of the factor (MRC).
- **Market labour demand** slopes **down**; **market labour supply** slopes **up**. A change in the wage moves along the curves.

## Key relationships

| Measure | Formula | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Marginal product (MP) | ΔTP ÷ ΔL | In units of output; falls with diminishing returns |
| **Marginal revenue product (MRP)** | **ΔTR ÷ ΔL = MP × MR** | In dollars; the firm's demand for labour |
| MRP for a price taker in the output market | MP × P (value of the marginal product) | MR = P |
| MRP for a firm with market power | MP × MR, less than MP × P | Price cut on earlier units lowers MR |
| **Marginal resource cost (MRC)** | **ΔTC ÷ ΔL** | Also called marginal factor cost (MFC) |
| MRC for a wage taker | = wage | Horizontal line on a graph |

| Curve | Slope | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Labour demand | negative | MRP falls as workers are added, so a lower wage makes more workers worth hiring |
| Labour supply | positive | higher wage raises the opportunity cost of leisure and draws workers from other jobs |

## Assumptions and scope

- Other inputs (capital, land) are **fixed** while the firm varies labour.
- "Other things constant": only the wage changes when we move along a curve. Shifts are Topic 5.2.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Calling households the buyers in a factor market.
2. Giving MP (units of output) when the question asks for MRP (dollars).
3. Multiplying **total** product by price instead of **marginal** product.
4. Using MP × P for a firm that must cut its price to sell more.
5. Including fixed cost in MRC.
6. Saying a wage change **shifts** labour demand or supply.

## Quick self-check

1. A worker adds 6 units of output, and each unit sells for $15 in a competitive market. What is the worker's MRP? *(6 × $15 = $90)*
2. Hiring a 6th worker raises total revenue from $400 to $470. What is the MRP of the 6th worker? *($470 − $400 = $70)*
3. A firm hires at a market wage of $80 a day. What is its MRC? *($80 for every worker)*
4. Why is the demand for bakers a derived demand? *(Bakeries want bakers only because people buy bread.)*
