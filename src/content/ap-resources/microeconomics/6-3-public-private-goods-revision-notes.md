## Recap

- **Rival:** one person's use leaves less for others. **Non-rival:** one more user takes nothing away from the rest.
- **Excludable:** non-payers can, in practice, be kept out. **Non-excludable:** they cannot, or only at a prohibitive cost.
- **Private goods** (rival, excludable) are provided well by markets.
- **Public goods** (non-rival, non-excludable) face the **free rider problem**: people can benefit without paying, so private firms cannot cover their costs. Government usually provides them and funds them with taxes.
- **Open-access resources** (rival, non-excludable) are **overused**: each user ignores the cost their use imposes on other users.
- Governments sometimes provide **private goods** such as schooling free of charge. That does not make them public goods.

## Key relationships

| Type | Rival? | Excludable? | Market problem | Typical response |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Private good | yes | yes | usually none | leave to the market |
| Public good | no | no | underprovided (free riding) | government provision, funded by taxes |
| Open-access resource | yes | no | overused | quotas, licences, fees, property rights |
| Club good (background term) | no | yes | not a focus of the course | often sold by firms |

| Test | How to use it |
|---|---|
| Provide a public good? | Provide if **total** benefit (sum over all users) ≥ cost |
| Efficient use of an open-access resource | Add users while the **extra** output each adds ≥ its cost, not while each user's own share ≥ cost |
| Equal tax to fund a public good | Tax per person = cost ÷ number of payers; some may still lose |

## Assumptions

- People and firms are rational: they weigh their own benefits and costs.
- "Excludable" is judged by what is practical, not by what the law allows.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying a good is public **because** the government provides it.
2. Calling an open-access resource a public good. It is rival.
3. Confusing non-rival with free or unlimited.
4. Using each user's own catch (the average) instead of the extra catch (the marginal) when finding the efficient number of users.
5. Forgetting that a tax-funded public good can leave some taxpayers worse off even when society gains.
6. Explaining underprovision without the word **non-excludable** and the free rider incentive.

## Quick self-check

1. Is a seat on a crowded commuter train rival? Is it excludable? *(Rival and excludable: a private good.)*
2. A public weather-warning broadcast costs $2,500 a year. Each of 250 residents values it at $12. Should it be provided? *(Total benefit 250 × $12 = $3,000 > $2,500, so yes; net gain $500.)*
3. Why is groundwater shared by many farms overused? *(It is rival but non-excludable, so each farm ignores the cost its pumping imposes on the others.)*
