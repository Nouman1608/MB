## Recap

- Firms produce where MR = MC (a labour-market buyer where MRP = MRC). A policy changes output only by changing one of these.
- **Per-unit taxes and subsidies** shift MC, so they change the price consumers pay, the net price firms keep, quantity, consumer and producer surplus, deadweight loss and government revenue or cost. The **less elastic** side bears more of a tax.
- **Lump-sum taxes and subsidies** change only fixed cost (and ATC). Price and output stay the same; profit changes.
- **Price controls** cut output in perfect competition but can raise it where firms have market power or a buyer has monopsony power.
- **Natural monopoly** regulation: fair return (P = ATC) or socially optimal (P = MC, plus a lump-sum subsidy).
- **Antitrust policy** aims to make markets more competitive.
- Intervention raises efficiency only when it fixes the incentive behind the market failure.

## Key relationships

| Policy | Perfect competition | Monopoly (or monopolistic competition) |
|---|---|---|
| Per-unit tax | P up, Q down, DWL appears | P up, Q falls further, DWL grows |
| Per-unit subsidy | Q above efficient, DWL appears | Q rises towards efficient, DWL shrinks |
| Lump-sum tax or subsidy | no change in P or Q (short run) | no change in P or Q; profit changes |
| Binding price ceiling | shortage, Q down, DWL | ceiling where D meets MC → efficient Q; between MC at old Q and old P → Q up; below MC at old Q → Q down |

| Labour market | Binding minimum wage |
|---|---|
| Perfectly competitive | employment falls; surplus of labour |
| Monopsony | wage and employment both rise, up to the wage where supply meets MRP |

| Natural monopoly | Price rule | Profit | Deadweight loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unregulated | MR = MC | positive | largest |
| Fair return | P = ATC | zero | smaller |
| Socially optimal | P = MC | loss = lump-sum subsidy needed | zero |

Deadweight loss = ½ × (efficient Q − actual Q) × (price on demand − MC at actual Q), when MC and demand are straight lines.

## Assumptions

- Firms maximise profit; price controls are enforced; curves are straight lines in calculations.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Shifting MC for a lump-sum tax.
2. Saying every price ceiling reduces output.
3. Claiming a natural monopoly breaks even at P = MC.
4. Paying the natural monopoly a per-unit subsidy instead of a lump-sum one.
5. Forgetting that a per-unit tax on a monopoly makes its deadweight loss **bigger**.
6. Drawing a collusion-and-policy graph: it is outside the course.

## Quick self-check

1. A monopoly pays a new $50 thousand annual licence fee. What happens to its price? *(Nothing: a lump-sum fee leaves MR and MC unchanged; only profit falls.)*
2. A natural monopoly has MC = $5 and fixed cost $300 thousand. At P = MC it serves 60 thousand customers. What subsidy does it need? *(ATC = 5 + 300 ÷ 60 = $10; loss = (10 − 5) × 60 = $300 thousand, a lump-sum subsidy.)*
3. Why can a minimum wage raise employment at a monopsony? *(It makes MRC equal the minimum wage, so hiring one more worker no longer raises the wage of all the others.)*
