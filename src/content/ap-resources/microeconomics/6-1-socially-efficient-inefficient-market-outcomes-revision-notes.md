## Recap

- **Social efficiency** (allocative efficiency): produce every unit whose marginal social benefit is at least its marginal social cost, and stop where **MSB = MSC**. Total economic surplus is then as large as it can be.
- A **perfectly competitive** market reaches this quantity when nobody outside the market is affected: demand = MPB = MSB, supply = MPC = MSC, and at equilibrium P = MB = MC.
- **Rational agents** equate **private** marginal benefit and **private** marginal cost. If private and social values differ, or if a firm can use **market power**, the market quantity is not efficient.
- **Policymakers** aim for MSB = MSC, but use **cost-benefit analysis**: a policy is worth it only if the surplus it restores is larger than its cost.

## Key relationships

| Situation | Market quantity vs efficient quantity | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Perfect competition, no external effects | equal | P = MSB = MSC |
| Monopoly, oligopoly, monopolistic competition | too little | P > MC |
| Negative externality | too much | MSC > MPC is ignored |
| Positive externality | too little | MSB > MPB is ignored |
| Asymmetric information | usually too little | some worthwhile trades do not happen |
| Public goods | too little (often none) | free riding |

| Calculation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Efficient quantity (equations) | set MSB = MSC and solve for Q |
| Efficient quantity (table) | last unit with MSB ≥ MSC |
| DWL (table) | add MSB − MSC for every unit between Q and Q\* (use the size) |
| DWL (straight lines) | ½ × (Q\* − Q, as a size) × (MSB − MSC at Q, as a size) |
| Net gain of a policy | DWL removed − cost of the policy |

## Assumptions

- MSB and MSC include everyone affected, not just the buyer and seller.
- Efficiency compares total surplus only; it ignores who receives it.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying more output is always better: overproduction also creates DWL.
2. Calling a monopoly's extra profit a deadweight loss (it is a transfer).
3. Using the MSB − MSC gap at one quantity as the DWL: DWL is an area.
4. Forgetting the ½ in the triangle formula.
5. Shading the DWL triangle on the wrong side: it always points to where MSB = MSC.
6. Judging a policy only by the DWL it removes, without subtracting its cost.

## Quick self-check

1. MSB = 60 − Q and MSC = Q. What is the efficient quantity? *(60 − Q = Q, so Q\* = 30.)*
2. In question 1, a firm with market power produces 20. What is the DWL? *(At 20, MSB = 40 and MSC = 20. DWL = ½ × 10 × 20 = 100.)*
3. Why can a competitive market be inefficient? *(If buyers or sellers ignore costs or benefits that fall on others, private and social values differ.)*
