## Recap

- **Marginal** = one more unit. **Marginal analysis** compares the marginal benefit (MB) of one more unit with its marginal cost (MC).
- The **optimal quantity** is where MB = MC. This is also where total net benefit (total benefit − total cost) is largest.
- **Sunk costs** (already paid, cannot be recovered) and benefits already fixed by past choices do not change MB or MC, so they do not change the optimal quantity.
- **Consumer choice model:** consumers have limited income, face given prices, know their preferences and choose to maximize **total utility**.
- **Diminishing marginal utility:** each extra unit adds less satisfaction than the one before.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | How to use it |
|---|---|
| **MU = change in TU ÷ change in quantity** | With one unit at a time, MU = next TU − this TU |
| **TU = sum of the MUs** | Add the MU of every unit consumed |
| **Net benefit = TB − TC** | Largest where MB = MC |
| **MU per dollar = MU ÷ P** | Compare goods with different prices |
| **Utility maximized: MU_A ÷ P_A = MU_B ÷ P_B, all income spent** | Both conditions are needed |

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| MB of next unit > MC | increase the activity |
| MB of last unit < MC | decrease the activity |
| MU_A ÷ P_A > MU_B ÷ P_B | buy more A and less B |
| Price of a good is zero | consume until MU = 0 (TU at its peak) |
| A sunk cost is added or removed | no change in quantity |

## Assumptions

- Income is limited and prices are given: the consumer faces a constraint.
- The consumer is rational and aims for the greatest total utility.
- MU diminishes as more of a good is consumed.
- Indifference curves are not part of the course.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying total utility falls when marginal utility falls. TU still rises while MU > 0.
2. Comparing MU instead of MU per dollar when prices differ.
3. Forgetting the budget condition: equal ratios must come with all income spent.
4. Letting a sunk cost change the answer ("I've paid, so I'll do more").
5. Stopping where MB = 0 when each unit has a price.
6. Using total or average values where the question asks about the next unit.

## Quick self-check

1. TU is 10, 18 and 24 utils for 1, 2 and 3 cups. What is the MU of the 3rd cup? *(24 − 18 = 6 utils)*
2. The last unit of A gives 40 utils at $5; the last unit of B gives 30 utils at $2. What should the consumer do? *(A gives 8 utils per $, B gives 15: buy more B and less A.)*
3. A non-refundable $25 membership is paid. Does it change how many classes you should attend? *(No. It is sunk, so MB and MC of each class are unchanged.)*
