## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between income (a flow) and wealth (a stock), and say why wealth is usually more unequal. *(Guide: "Why markets can produce unequal outcomes"; Practice Q7)*
- I can describe how income levels and poverty rates vary within and between groups and countries. *(Guide: "Income levels and poverty rates")*
- I can explain what the line of equality, a Lorenz curve and the Gini coefficient each show. *(Guide: Figure 1 and "The Gini coefficient"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why efficient market outcomes can still be unequal. *(Guide: "Why markets can produce unequal outcomes")*

## Calculation and reading data

- I can read a Lorenz curve or its table to find the share of income received by the poorest or richest part of the population. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can calculate a poverty rate and compare rates between groups. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can calculate the value of a factor's marginal product and use it to compare incomes. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate average tax rates and classify a tax as progressive, proportional or regressive. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can find how a tax changes the ratio between high and low after-tax incomes. *(Guide: Worked example 3(b); Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can compare inequality across countries, policies or time periods using Lorenz curves and Gini coefficients, without confusing shares with income levels. *(Guide: Worked example 1(c) and (d); Practice Q1, Q3)*
- I can identify and explain human capital, social capital, inheritance, discrimination, access to financial markets, mobility and bargaining power as sources of inequality. *(Guide: "Other sources"; Practice Q4, Q7)*
- I can explain how owning capital turns wealth into income and can widen inequality over time. *(Guide: Worked example 2(d); Practice Q5(c))*
