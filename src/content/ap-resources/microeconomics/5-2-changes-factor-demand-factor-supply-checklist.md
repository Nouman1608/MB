## Understanding

- I can tell a change in the quantity of labour demanded or supplied (caused by the wage) from a change in demand or supply (a shift). *(Guide: "Movements and shifts"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain how a change in the output price or in worker productivity shifts labour demand, using MRP = MP × P. *(Guide: "What shifts labour demand"; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can explain how the price of an input that substitutes for, or works with, labour affects labour demand. *(Guide: "What shifts labour demand")*
- I can name the seven determinants of labour supply and give the direction of each shift. *(Guide: "What shifts labour supply"; Practice Q2, Q7)*

## Calculation and graphing

- I can recalculate MRP after a change in output price or productivity and find how many workers are worth hiring at a given wage. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can draw a correctly labelled labour market graph showing a shift of demand or supply and the new wage and quantity. *(Guide: Figures 1 and 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can find the equilibrium wage and quantity from linear demand and supply equations before and after a shift. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict the effect of a single shift on the equilibrium wage and employment. *(Practice Q2, Q6(a))*
- I can explain which outcome is indeterminate when both curves shift, and what extra information would settle it. *(Guide: "Two shifts at once: a summary"; Practice Q3, Q6(c))*
- I can trace a change in a product market through to the labour market that produces it (derived demand). *(Guide: Worked example 2(a); Practice Q6)*
