## Recap

- **Shortage** (excess demand): Qd > Qs, at a price **below** equilibrium. Buyers bid the price up.
- **Surplus** (excess supply): Qs > Qd, at a price **above** equilibrium. Sellers cut the price.
- During adjustment, **no curve shifts**: price changes and both quantities move along their curves until Qd = Qs.
- At a disequilibrium price, the quantity **traded** is the smaller of Qd and Qs, and total surplus is lower than at equilibrium.
- A change in a **determinant** shifts a curve, creates a shortage or surplus at the old price, and the market moves to a new equilibrium.

## Key relationships

| Event | Price | Quantity |
|---|---|---|
| Demand increases | rises | rises |
| Demand decreases | falls | falls |
| Supply increases | falls | rises |
| Supply decreases | rises | falls |
| D up and S up | ? | rises |
| D down and S down | ? | falls |
| D up and S down | rises | ? |
| D down and S up | falls | ? |

| Calculation | How |
|---|---|
| Shortage | Qd − Qs at the given price |
| Surplus | Qs − Qd at the given price |
| New equilibrium (straight lines) | set the new Qd = Qs, solve for P, then substitute for Q |
| CS or PS (straight lines) | ½ × Q × height of the triangle; add a rectangle at a disequilibrium price |
| Change in surplus | new area − old area, using the curves that apply after the change |

**Elasticity:** the more elastic the curve that does **not** shift, the more the effect shows up in quantity and the less in price.

## Assumptions

- A competitive market with many buyers and sellers, and a price free to move.
- "Other things equal": only the stated determinant changes.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying a higher price "increases supply" (it increases quantity supplied).
2. Measuring a shortage or surplus in dollars instead of units.
3. Predicting both price and quantity when both curves shift.
4. Shifting a curve back because of the price change it caused.
5. Using quantity demanded as the amount traded when there is a shortage.
6. Assuming consumer surplus must fall whenever price rises.

## Quick self-check

1. Demand is Qd = 90 − 3P and supply is Qs = 6P − 36. Find the equilibrium. *(9P = 126, so P = $14 and Q = 48.)*
2. In the same market, what happens at $10? *(Qd = 60, Qs = 24: a shortage of 36 units. The price rises.)*
3. Incomes rise (normal good) and a new machine cuts production costs. What can you predict? *(Quantity rises; the price change is indeterminate.)*
