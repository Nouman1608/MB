## Recap

- Rational decision makers compare the **benefits** and **costs** of each option and choose the one that leaves them best off.
- **Opportunity cost** is the value of the **next-best** alternative given up: one option, not all of them.
- **Explicit costs** are money paid to others. **Implicit costs** are the value of your own resources used, such as wages you give up or rent you could have earned.
- Total benefit is **utility** for a consumer (often shown in dollars) and **total revenue** for a firm.
- The **optimal choice** maximises **total net benefit**.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | How to use it |
|---|---|
| **Total economic cost = explicit + implicit** | List every payment, then add what your own time and resources could have earned |
| **Net benefit = total benefit − total cost** | Work it out for every option; pick the largest |
| Marginal benefit = change in total benefit; marginal cost = change in total cost | For "how much" decisions, add a unit while MB ≥ MC |
| Accounting view = explicit costs only | Leaves out implicit costs, so it overstates the gain |

| Type of decision | Method |
|---|---|
| "How much" (batches, hours, kilometres) | Totals **or** marginal steps; both give the same answer |
| "All or nothing" (take the job, build the bridge) | Compare **total** benefit with **total** cost |

## Assumptions

- Decision makers are rational and want the largest net benefit.
- Benefits and costs can be put in the same unit (usually dollars) so they can be compared.
- Only costs that the decision changes are counted. Money already spent and not recoverable is ignored.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Adding the values of **all** rejected options to get opportunity cost.
2. Leaving out implicit costs, especially income given up.
3. Choosing the largest **total** benefit instead of the largest **net** benefit.
4. Stopping at "net benefit is positive" without checking the other options.
5. Using marginal steps for an all-or-nothing decision.
6. Counting costs you pay whichever option you choose.

## Quick self-check

1. Ana can go to a concert she values at $40, a match she values at $55, or a film she values at $25. She chooses the match. What is its opportunity cost? *($40, the concert: the next-best option only)*
2. A 4-day coding workshop costs $300. Ari would otherwise earn $80 a day. He values the workshop at $650. Should he go? *(Total cost = 300 + 4 × 80 = $620; net benefit = 650 − 620 = $30, so yes)*
3. A firm's total revenue is highest at 8 units. Is 8 units the best choice? *(Not necessarily: find where total revenue minus total cost is largest)*
