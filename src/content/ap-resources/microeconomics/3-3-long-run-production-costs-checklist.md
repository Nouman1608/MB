Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/3-3-long-run-production-costs-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/3-3-long-run-production-costs-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why every input, and so every cost, is variable in the long run. *(Guide: "Short run and long run"; Practice Q1, Q4(d))*
- I can define increasing, constant and decreasing returns to scale. *(Guide: "Returns to scale: when every input grows"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain how the LRATC curve relates to short-run average total cost curves for different plant sizes. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
- I can give causes of economies of scale and of diseconomies of scale. *(Guide: "The shape of the LRATC curve"; Practice Q7(c))*

## Calculation and graphing

- I can identify returns to scale by comparing the percentage change in all inputs with the percentage change in output. *(Guide: Worked example 1(b); Practice Q2, Q7(b))*
- I can calculate LRATC from long-run total cost and output. *(Guide: Worked example 1(a); Practice Q4)*
- I can find the MES from a table or graph, including when the bottom of LRATC is flat. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q3, Q4(c))*
- I can draw a labelled LRATC curve showing the three regions and the MES. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can show a change in input prices as a shift of LRATC. *(Practice Q5(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain the difference between diseconomies of scale and diminishing marginal returns. *(Guide: "The shape of the LRATC curve"; Practice Q7(a))*
- I can use the MES and market size to estimate how many firms can reach the lowest cost. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why a large MES relative to demand tends to make a market more concentrated. *(Guide: "Minimum efficient scale and market structure"; Practice Q6(b))*
