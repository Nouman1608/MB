Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/6-3-public-private-goods-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/microeconomics/6-3-public-private-goods-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define rival and excludable, and judge excludability by what is practical rather than what is legal. *(Guide: "Two questions about any good"; Practice Q1)*
- I can classify a good as a private good, a public good or an open-access resource, giving a reason for each test. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1, Q6(a), Q7(a))*
- I can explain why government provision does not make a good public, using schooling as an example. *(Guide: "Private goods provided free by government"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why non-rivalry means that, once a public good exists, one more user costs nothing extra. *(Guide: "Public goods and the free rider problem")*

## Calculation and graphing

- I can add up all users' benefits from a public good, compare the total with its cost, and decide whether to provide it. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6(b), Q6(e))*
- I can calculate an equal tax to fund a public good and each group's net gain. *(Guide: Worked example 1(c); Practice Q6(d))*
- I can find the open-access and efficient number of users from a table, using the share per user and the extra output added. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*
- I can draw an open-access resource graph with D = MSB, MPC and MSC, and shade the deadweight loss of overuse. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain the free rider problem and why it stops private firms from providing public goods. *(Guide: "Public goods and the free rider problem"; Practice Q2, Q6(c))*
- I can explain why open-access resources are overused, naming the cost each user imposes on others. *(Guide: "Open-access resources and overuse"; Practice Q4, Q7(d))*
- I can suggest a quota, licence, fee or property right to limit overuse, and explain how it works. *(Guide: Worked example 2(d); Practice Q5(c), Q7(e))*
- I can explain why a tax-funded public good can raise total surplus while some taxpayers lose. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6(d))*
