## Recap

- A **thesis** about a poem is a defensible interpretation of what the poem does and means. It may preview the line of reasoning but need not list devices.
- A **line of reasoning** is the logical sequence of claims that defends the thesis. For a poem, follow the poem's **movement** (its turns, builds and contrasts), not its line order.
- **Commentary** explains how evidence supports a claim and the claim supports the thesis; build a chain: evidence, device, effect, link.
- Strong poetry theses often rest on a **tension, shift or ambiguity** that a first reading passes over.
- Refer to **the speaker**, not the poet, unless the context says otherwise.
- Developing an interpretation is recursive: evidence can suggest a claim, and a claim can lead you to evidence.

## Key relationships

| Part | Test | Example (Westminster Bridge) |
|---|---|---|
| Thesis | Arguable, with a reason? | Beauty depends on stillness; stillness is both peaceful and uncanny |
| Line of reasoning | Follows movement, claims build? | city as natural; openness and clarity; sight to feeling |
| Evidence | Short, accurate, purposeful? | "the very houses seem asleep" |
| Commentary | Device, effect, link? | personification: city a body at rest; supports stillness as life |

| Level of thesis | Looks like |
|---|---|
| Summary | Retells the poem |
| Device list | "uses personification, imagery and a simile" |
| Developing | A claim with a thin "how" |
| Defensible | An idea with a reason, resting on a tension |

## Assumptions

- You quote accurately and keep quotations short.
- Paragraph order follows the poem's movement.
- Each paragraph links back to the thesis.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Summarising the poem line by line.
- Writing a thesis that is only a list of devices.
- Naming a device with no effect.
- Treating the speaker as the poet.
- Ignoring a tension or ambiguity that would sharpen the thesis.

## Quick self-check

1. Why is "The poet uses personification, imagery and a simile" not a thesis?
2. What should the order of paragraphs follow in a poetry essay?
3. Write the four links of a device-to-effect chain.
4. How can an ambiguous ending strengthen a thesis?

**Answers.** (1) It lists devices and makes no claim about meaning or effect. (2) The poem's movement: its turns, builds and contrasts. (3) Evidence, device, effect, link to claim and thesis. (4) By explaining what the competing readings add, it shows the writer has noticed the poem's complexity and can build an argument on it.

## Sentence frames to adapt

- "The poem presents [subject] as [idea], and its movement from [x] to [y] shows [reason]."
- "[Quotation] is a [device] that [effect], which supports the claim that [claim]."
- "Although [apparent meaning], the [word / structure / ambiguity] suggests [complication]."
- "Taken together, [device 1], [device 2] and [device 3] show that [thesis idea]."

The second frame is a device-to-effect chain in one sentence. Use it in every body paragraph at least once.
