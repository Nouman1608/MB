Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/5-6-thesis-and-commentary-poetry-analysis-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/5-6-thesis-and-commentary-poetry-analysis-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what a thesis about a poem must do, and why it is not a device list. *(Guide: "The thesis for a poem"; Practice question 1)*
- I can explain how a line of reasoning for a poem follows its movement, not its line order. *(Guide: "The line of reasoning for a poem"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain what commentary adds to a quotation. *(Guide: "Commentary as a device-to-effect chain"; Practice question 3)*
- I can say why a tension or ambiguity can strengthen a thesis. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 4)*

## Skills

- I can tell a summary, a device list, a developing thesis and a defensible thesis apart. *(Guide: the thesis table; Practice questions 1 and 6)*
- I can plan a three-step line of reasoning from a poem's movement. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice questions 5 and 6)*
- I can build an evidence, device, effect, link chain. *(Guide: "Commentary as a device-to-effect chain"; Practice question 7)*
- I can assess a paragraph with the self-assessment table and name one change. *(Guide: "A self-assessment table"; Practice question 8)*

## Reasoning

- I can find a tension or shift in a poem and turn it into a claim. *(Guide: Worked example 1, steps 2 and 3; Practice question 6)*
- I can say why paragraphs should follow movement, not line-by-line summary. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice question 5)*
- I can use a poem's ambiguity inside an argument. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 4)*
- I can write a paragraph with accurate quotations and device-to-effect commentary. *(Guide: Worked example 1, model paragraph; Practice questions 7 and 8)*
