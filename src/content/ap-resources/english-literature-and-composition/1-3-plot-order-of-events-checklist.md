Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/1-3-plot-order-of-events-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/1-3-plot-order-of-events-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define plot as a sequence of connected events and explain the difference between plot and summary. *(Guide: "Plot is a chain, not a list")*
- I can explain what exposition does for a reader. *(Guide: "Plot is a chain, not a list"; Practice question 4)*
- I can explain why not every story follows exposition, rising action, climax and resolution. *(Guide: "The parts of a plot, as a vocabulary")*
- I can explain how a flashback or late reveal changes what the reader knows. *(Guide: "Order is a choice"; Practice questions 1 and 2)*

## Skills

- I can label the stages of a short plot and say what each does. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 1)*
- I can trace cause-and-effect links between events and say which pair is linked. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 2; Practice question 5)*
- I can describe the order of events in a passage and say what that order makes the reader notice. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 3)*
- I can write a sentence about the function of a flashback using at least two details. *(Practice question 7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how a sequence of events focuses attention on a relationship, not just the action. *(Practice questions 6 and 8)*
- I can identify the climax as the decisive turn, even when it is quiet. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Worked example 2)*
- I can rewrite a plot summary sentence as a sentence about function. *(Guide: "Strong and weak statements about plot")*
