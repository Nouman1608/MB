## Recap

A **significant event** is a moment, or related set of moments, that changes the situation, forces a choice, or reveals what the central conflict is really about. Explain its **function**, not just its **content**. Significant events often put **competing value systems** into collision, and events **collide and accumulate** to create anticipation and suspense, so earlier events matter to the later one. The method: select, say what changes, name the values in collision, trace the build-up, explain the function. The full explanation, with worked examples from *Great Expectations* and *Pride and Prejudice*, is in the [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/9-3-significant-events-study-guide/).

## Key relationships

| Step | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Select | Which event changes something? | Magwitch's revelation (*Great Expectations*, Chapter 39) |
| What changes | What differs before and after? | Pip's benefactor, his expectations, his understanding |
| Values in collision | Which systems meet? | Gentility versus gratitude and labour |
| Build-up | What led here, and what follows? | The convict of Chapter 1; Pip's shame of Joe |
| Function | What does it do for the work? | Reverses an assumption; turns a story of rise into a moral test |

| Content | Function |
|---|---|
| "Darcy proposes and Elizabeth refuses." | "The scene sets class pride against self-respect and prejudice, exposing both flaws." |
| "Magwitch visits Pip." | "The visit forces Pip to measure his gentility against the man who paid for it." |

## Conditions to remember

- An event is significant because of what changes, not because it is dramatic.
- Related scenes can count as one event.
- Anticipation and suspense come from accumulation; name the earlier events.
- A release (catharsis) is a function when the event resolves built-up tension.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Retelling the event.
- Saying it is "important" with no function.
- Ignoring what came before.
- Picking a scene because it is memorable, not because it changes anything.
- Treating the event in isolation from the work's meaning.

## Worked mini-example

Take Chapter 39 of *Great Expectations*. *What changes:* Pip's benefactor, his expectations and his understanding. *Values in collision:* gentility against gratitude and labour. *Build-up:* the convict of Chapter 1 and Pip's growing shame of Joe. *Function:* it reverses an assumption and turns a story of rise into a moral test. Content is one sentence; function is the rest.

## Quick self-check

1. What is the difference between content and function?
2. Why do significant events often show competing values?
3. How do accumulating events create suspense?
4. What changes in Chapter 39 of *Great Expectations*?
5. Why is Darcy's first proposal a turning point?

**Answers.**
1. Content is what happens; function is what it does for the work.
2. Because the central conflict usually sets value systems against each other, and key events are where they meet.
3. Earlier events create expectations that the later event fulfils, complicates or overturns.
4. Pip learns that Magwitch, not Miss Havisham, is his benefactor, which changes his expectations and tests his values.
5. It does not end the relationship; it exposes both flaws and begins the revisions the rest of the novel requires.
