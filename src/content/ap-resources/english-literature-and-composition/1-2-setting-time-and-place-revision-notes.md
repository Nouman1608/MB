## Recap

**Setting** is the time and place of the events, and the details that establish it. Setting details do three jobs: they **situate** the reader (period, landscape), create **mood** (atmosphere) and convey **values** (what a community cares about). Analysis explains what a detail conveys and what difference it makes to character or meaning.

## Key relationships

| Idea | What to look for | Example of a claim |
|---|---|---|
| Time | Objects, season, hour, habits about time | "The gas lamp suggests an earlier period." |
| Place | Landscape, position, condition, sense details | "Mud at the far end marks the edge of the community." |
| Mood | Adjectives of light, sound, weight | "'Soundless' and 'oppressively low' create heaviness." |
| Values | Customs, order, what is tidy or neglected | "Fixed stall positions convey respect for inheritance." |
| Effect on character | What setting limits, allows or signals | "Sandeep's place shows him as an outsider." |

## What you may assume

- A writer selects setting details deliberately, so each detail can be questioned.
- You may date a setting only as far as the details allow.
- The way a setting is described may reflect the narrator's perspective as well as the place itself.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Describing the scenery without saying what it conveys.
- Assuming weather always matches a character's feelings.
- Naming a precise decade or place the text does not state.
- Treating the narrator's description as neutral.
- Listing many details with no explanation instead of explaining a few well.

## Key terms

- **Setting:** the time and place of the events and the details that establish them.
- **Mood:** the feeling a text creates in the reader; setting details are a main way of creating it.
- **Values (of a setting):** what a community or place treats as important, shown by order, custom and condition.
- **Sensory detail:** a detail of sight, sound, smell, touch or temperature.

## Sentence frames

- "The detail '…' conveys a setting that is …, which suggests …."
- "By placing [character] at '…', the setting marks [his or her] position as …."
- "The setting limits [character] because …."

## Mini-example

Detail: "a stove … gave off more smell than heat". Conveys: disappointment and neglect. Effect: the waiting room matches the old man's resigned nod.

## How questions ask about this

- *"The details … most directly convey a setting that is …"*: choose the answer that matches both the time or place and the mood or value.
- *"The function of this detail is to …"*: look for what the detail does to character, mood or meaning, not what it describes.
- *"Which detail suggests an earlier period?"*: pick the detail that fixes time, not custom or place.
- Constructed response: name two details, say what each conveys, then state the effect on a character or on the mood.

## Quick self-check

1. Name the three jobs a setting detail can do.
2. "A stove gave off more smell than heat." What does this detail convey, and what kind of detail is it?
3. Why is "the far end where the cobbles gave way to mud" more than a description of the ground?

**Answers.**

1. It situates the reader in a time and place, creates a mood, and conveys values.
2. It conveys disappointment or neglect; it is a sensory detail (smell and temperature) that creates mood.
3. Because position and ground condition place the character socially: the edge of the square and the worst ground mark an outsider within the community's order.
