Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/5-3-imagery-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/5-3-imagery-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define an image and name the senses an image can appeal to. *(Guide: "What an image is"; Practice question 1)*
- I can distinguish a literal image from an image that works as a comparison. *(Guide: "Literal and comparative images side by side"; Practice question 4)*
- I can explain how adjectives, adverbs and verbs contribute to sensory imagery. *(Guide: "What an image is"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain what imagery is and why the pattern across images matters. *(Guide: "Tracking imagery through a whole poem"; Practice question 5)*

## Skills

- I can work through image, sense, association and effect. *(Guide: "Reading imagery: four moves"; Practice question 6)*
- I can quote an image accurately and name its sense. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice questions 1 and 7)*
- I can build a three-column table of images, senses and suggestions. *(Guide: "Tracking imagery through a whole poem"; Practice question 6)*
- I can describe how images shift in a poem, using the sentence "The images move from... to...". *(Guide: "Tracking imagery through a whole poem"; Practice question 8)*

## Reasoning

- I can say what association an image creates and what that does to the speaker's attitude. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice questions 3 and 7)*
- I can explain how imagery emphasises an idea in part of a poem or throughout. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 8)*
- I can turn "the poem has nature imagery" into an interpretive comment. *(Practice question 7)*
- I can write a paragraph on imagery with accurate quotations and commentary. *(Practice question 8)*
