Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/1-5-claim-and-textual-evidence-paragraph-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/1-5-claim-and-textual-evidence-paragraph-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define a claim and explain why a fact or a summary is not one. *(Guide: "What a literary-analysis paragraph is made of"; Practice questions 1 and 4)*
- I can name the parts of the paragraph: claim, evidence and link. *(Guide: "The shape of a paragraph")*
- I can explain what makes evidence relevant, specific and sufficient. *(Guide: "Choosing evidence"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain why a quotation needs a link to the claim. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*

## Skills

- I can quote exactly and embed a short quotation in my own sentence. *(Guide: "Quoting accurately and smoothly"; Practice question 3)*
- I can write a claim about a character, setting, plot or narrator that a reader could dispute. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 7)*
- I can choose two or three details of different kinds to support a claim. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice questions 2 and 5)*
- I can use the present tense and "the narrator" or "the character" when writing about a text. *(Guide: "Quoting accurately and smoothly")*

## Reasoning

- I can diagnose a weak paragraph sentence by sentence and say what each sentence is missing. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 1)*
- I can rewrite a weak draft into a paragraph with a claim, evidence and a link. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 8)*
- I can decide which of several claims a detail supports best. *(Practice question 5)*
