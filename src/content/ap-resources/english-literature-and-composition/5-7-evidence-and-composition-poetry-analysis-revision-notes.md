## Recap

- Evidence from a poem is **relevant** when it bears on the claim and **sufficient** when its quantity and quality provide apt support.
- Evidence is **effective** when commentary explains the logical relationship between it and the claim.
- Writers use evidence **purposefully**: to illustrate, clarify, exemplify, associate, amplify or qualify.
- **Quote** the smallest accurate phrase, **integrate** it into your own sentence, and show line breaks with a slash.
- **Coherence** works within sentences, within paragraphs and across the essay.
- **Transitional elements** (words, phrases, clauses, sentences, paragraphs) show relationships between ideas.

## Key relationships

| Habit | Do | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Quote | "kindly stopped for me" | A whole stanza copied out |
| Integrate | Death "kindly stopped for me", so the speaker yields politely | The poem says. "He kindly stopped for me." |
| Line break | "kindly stopped for me; / The carriage held but just ourselves" | Quoting as prose with no sign of the break |
| Transition | "however", "consequently", "by contrast" | Short unlinked sentences |
| Reference | "This repetition..." | "This shows..." with no referent |

| Level of coherence | Check |
|---|---|
| Sentence | Does one clause link to the next? |
| Paragraph | Does a topic sentence state a claim, and does each sentence follow? |
| Essay | Does each paragraph link to the thesis and its neighbour? |

## Assumptions

- You copy words, capitals and punctuation exactly from the poem.
- You choose evidence for a claim and can say what each piece does.
- You draw evidence from more than one place when your claim concerns the poem's movement.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Dropping quotations into the essay with no integration.
- Quoting long passages instead of the telling phrase.
- Forgetting the slash for a line break.
- Pointing at a device with no quoted words.
- Paragraphs that list observations with no linking sentences.

## Quick self-check

1. How should you show a line break inside a quotation?
2. What makes evidence sufficient?
3. What is a transitional element?
4. Why should a paragraph cover one claim, not the whole poem?

**Answers.** (1) With a forward slash and a space on each side ( / ); a double slash for a stanza break. (2) Its quantity and quality provide apt support for the line of reasoning. (3) A word, phrase, clause, sentence or paragraph that shows how ideas relate. (4) Because a paragraph's job is to develop one claim and its evidence; the whole poem belongs to the thesis and the line of reasoning.

## Sentence frames to adapt

- "[Speaker or subject] is presented as [idea]: '[phrase] / [phrase]' [illustrates / amplifies / qualifies] [claim]."
- "The line break after '[word]' leaves [word] hanging, so [effect]."
- "While the first stanza [does x], the second [does y], so the poem [claim]."
- "This [repetition / contrast] links [paragraph] to the thesis by [how]."

Mark every line break in a quotation with a slash and copy words and punctuation exactly. Each frame ends with an effect or a link, never with a restatement of the quotation.
