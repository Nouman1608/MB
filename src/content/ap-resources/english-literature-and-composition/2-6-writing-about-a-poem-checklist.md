Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/2-6-writing-about-a-poem-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/2-6-writing-about-a-poem-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can say what a claim about a poem needs: to say what the poem does or shows and to need defence. *(Guide: "Choosing a claim about a poem"; Practice question 1)*
- I can explain what a device-to-effect chain is. *(Guide: "The device-to-effect chain"; Practice question 4)*
- I can explain why naming a device is not analysis. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*
- I can explain why I write about "the speaker" and use the present tense. *(Guide: "Quoting lines of poetry")*

## Skills

- I can quote lines accurately, using a slash at line breaks. *(Guide: "Quoting lines of poetry"; Practice question 3)*
- I can choose the quotation that best supports a claim. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 3; Practice question 2)*
- I can write a device-to-effect chain with the device, quotation, function and effect. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 3; Practice question 5)*
- I can write a paragraph about a poem with a claim, two or more quotations and a link. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 4; Practice question 6)*

## Reasoning

- I can diagnose a weak paragraph about a poem and say what each sentence lacks. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 1)*
- I can revise a weak paragraph into a stronger one. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 4; Practice question 7)*
- I can explain how a final image changes the reader's view of the speaker's situation. *(Practice question 8)*

All ticked? Take the [Unit 2 diagnostic](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/unit-2-diagnostic/), then return to the [revision notes](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/2-6-writing-about-a-poem-revision-notes/).
