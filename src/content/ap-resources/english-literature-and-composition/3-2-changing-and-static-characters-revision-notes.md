## Recap

A **dynamic** character develops over a narrative; a **static** character remains largely unchanged. Change may be **external** (health, wealth, position) or **internal** (attitude, self-knowledge). Show change by comparing early and late details and naming the turning point. A dynamic character's choices often affect the **climax** or **resolution**. A static character may measure others' change or represent a value.

## Key relationships

| Idea | What it means | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Dynamic character | Changes over the work | Early and late details differ |
| Static character | Remains largely the same | Repeated manner, words, values |
| External change | Visible change of circumstance | Wealth, health, position |
| Internal change | Change of understanding or feeling | Speech, self-judgement |
| Turning point | Where change becomes visible | An event or realisation |
| Function | What change or constancy does | Affects climax; measures change |

## What you may assume

- Change can be for better or worse.
- External and internal change may be linked in either direction.
- A text may show change gradually, with one scene making it clear.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Claiming change without quoting an early and a late detail.
- Not saying whether the change is internal or external.
- Treating constancy as a weakness of the writing.
- Naming a turning point without saying what changed.
- Ignoring the effect on the plot.

## Key terms

- **Dynamic character:** one who changes significantly over the work.
- **Static character:** one who remains largely unchanged.
- **Internal / external change:** a change of understanding or feeling / a change of circumstance or position.
- **Turning point:** the event or recognition where change becomes visible.

## Sentence frames

- "At first [character] …; by the end, … , which shows a change from … to …."
- "The turning point is …, after which …."
- "[Static character]'s unchanged … makes [other character]'s change visible."

## Mini-example

Early: "Tomasz studied his boots." Late: "I will speak for the new workers." Kind of change: internal and external. Effect: his choice changes the scene.

## How questions ask about this

- *"Between the early and late passages, [character] changes from … to …"*: find the pair of details that differ.
- *"The change is best described as …"*: decide internal, external or both.
- *"The function of [character]'s remaining unchanged is to …"*: say what the constancy measures or represents.
- Constructed response: quote an early and a late detail, name the turning point and say what the change does to the plot.

## Quick self-check

1. What two details do you need to show a character has changed?
2. Is Elizabeth's "Till this moment, I never knew myself" an external or an internal change? Why?
3. What does Aunt Hester's unchanged rule do in the paired extracts?

**Answers.**

1. An early detail and a later detail of the same kind, such as speech or response.
2. Internal: it is a change in her self-understanding, expressed in words rather than circumstances.
3. It measures Leo's change: the same sentence is met differently.
