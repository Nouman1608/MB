## Recap

- A **thesis statement** expresses an interpretation of a literary text that requires a defence through textual evidence and a line of reasoning.
- A thesis **may preview** the line of reasoning but need not list the literary elements, the points or the quotations.
- A **line of reasoning** is the logical sequence of claims that work together to defend the thesis.
- **Commentary** explains how each piece of evidence supports its claim and how the claim supports the thesis. It is how a line of reasoning is communicated.
- Body paragraphs develop the reasoning, justify claims with evidence and link evidence to the thesis; topic sentences state the claim of a paragraph.
- Developing an interpretation is **recursive**: evidence can lead to a claim, or a claim to evidence.

## Key relationships

| Part | Test it with | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Thesis | Could a reasonable reader disagree? Does it say why? | "Trust rests on being answerable, not on accuracy alone" |
| Line of reasoning | Does each claim build on the one before? | neutrality, then answerability, then care |
| Evidence | Is it short, accurate and chosen for a purpose? | "like a man standing between two quarrelling nations" |
| Commentary | Does it explain, connect and link to the thesis? | simile raises trade to diplomacy; trust is in a person |

| Level of thesis | Looks like |
|---|---|
| Summary | Retells the passage |
| Obvious | "The writer uses description to show..." with a truism |
| Developing | A claim with no reason |
| Defensible | An arguable idea with a reason and a hint of the reasoning |

## Assumptions

- You write about **what the passage suggests**, with accurate quotations.
- You keep to the prompt: the thesis answers the question.
- Commentary is longer than the quotation and adds something the quotation does not say.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Writing a topic or a summary instead of a claim.
- Listing devices without saying what they do.
- Quoting a long passage and explaining nothing.
- Commentary that praises ("a powerful image") or repeats ("this shows he is exact").
- A line of reasoning in which each paragraph repeats the same point.

## Quick self-check

1. Is "The passage is about a weigher and a machine" a thesis? Why or why not?
2. What three moves does strong commentary make after a quotation?
3. Can a thesis exist without any preview of the line of reasoning?
4. Why is the third claim of a good line of reasoning usually not a repeat of the first?

**Answers.** (1) No: it is a summary; it makes no arguable claim. (2) Explain what the evidence shows; connect it to the paragraph's claim; link to the thesis. (3) Yes: a thesis may preview the reasoning but is not required to. (4) Because a line of reasoning is a logical sequence of claims that build; repeating the first claim adds nothing.
