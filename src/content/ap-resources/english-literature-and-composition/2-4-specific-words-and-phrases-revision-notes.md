## Recap

An **antecedent** is the word, phrase or clause that comes before the **referent** that points back to it. A referent is **ambiguous** when it could refer to more than one antecedent. **Repetition** emphasises ideas or associations. **Alliteration** repeats a sound at the start of nearby words, to emphasise those words and their associations. In each case, say what the feature does in *this* poem.

## Key relationships

| Idea | What it is | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Antecedent | The earlier word that a later word refers back to | Where is it, even if it is in the title? |
| Referent | What a pronoun or phrase points to | Is it clear? |
| Ambiguous referent | Could point two ways | What does each reading mean? |
| Repetition | A word or phrase that returns | What changes with each return? |
| Alliteration | The same sound at the start of nearby words | Which words are linked, and why? |
| Word choice | A particular word rather than another | What does it add that another word would not? |

## What you may assume

- Features of words and sounds are chosen by the writer, but their effect depends on context.
- A title can be part of a poem's meaning and can serve as an antecedent.
- A claim about sound is a claim about reinforcement, not about sound alone.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Naming "alliteration" or "repetition" without saying what it does.
- Missing an ambiguous pronoun, or giving only one reading.
- Treating the same letter as the same sound.
- Claiming a sound means something in every poem.
- Forgetting the title when looking for an antecedent.

## Key terms

- **Antecedent:** the earlier word that a later referent points back to.
- **Referent:** what a pronoun or phrase refers to.
- **Repetition:** a word or phrase that returns for emphasis.
- **Alliteration:** repeated sound at the start of nearby words.

## Sentence frames

- "The pronoun '…' could refer to … or …, which leaves …."
- "The repetition of '…' stresses …."
- "The repeated … sound in '…' reinforces …."

## Mini-example

Words: "clasps … crag … crooked". Sound: repeated hard *k*. Reinforces: the tight grip of the eagle on rock.

## How questions ask about this

- *"The pronoun … most likely refers to …"*: find the antecedent, and notice any second possible reading.
- *"The repetition of … serves to …"*: say what it emphasises or what associations it gathers.
- *"The alliteration in … most directly …"*: tie the sound to the meaning of the words.
- Constructed response: name the feature, quote the words and explain what it does in this poem.

## Quick self-check

1. In "Anna told Lucia she had lost it", name the ambiguous referent and its two possible meanings.
2. What do the repeated *k* sounds in "clasps the crag with crooked hands" reinforce?
3. Where does the "He" in the first line of "The Eagle" find its antecedent?

**Answers.**

1. "She" could mean Anna or Lucia.
2. The picture of a tight, hard grip on the rock.
3. In the title, "The Eagle".
