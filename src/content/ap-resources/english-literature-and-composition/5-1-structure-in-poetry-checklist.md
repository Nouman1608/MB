Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/5-1-structure-in-poetry-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/5-1-structure-in-poetry-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can say what structure means in a poem: arrangement, relationship of parts and sequence of information. *(Guide: "What structure is"; Practice question 5)*
- I can explain how closed forms use predictable patterns to develop relationships among ideas. *(Guide: "What structure is"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain how open forms still have structure. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 4)*
- I can explain that I need not label rhyme schemes, meters or forms. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice question 5)*

## Skills

- I can work through pattern, shift, relationship and effect. *(Guide: "A method"; Practice question 6)*
- I can identify parallel, build, shift, contrast, interruption and resolution. *(Guide: the relationships table; Practice questions 1 and 2)*
- I can find the turn in a poem and say what changes across it. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice questions 3 and 6)*
- I can explain the effect of a stanza break. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice questions 4 and 6)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how structures combine to emphasise an idea. *(Guide: "What structure is"; Practice question 8)*
- I can say how the first and last lines of a poem relate. *(Practice question 7)*
- I can tie a pattern and its break to the poem's meaning, not just describe it. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice question 8)*
- I can write a paragraph on structure with accurate quotations and commentary. *(Practice question 8)*
