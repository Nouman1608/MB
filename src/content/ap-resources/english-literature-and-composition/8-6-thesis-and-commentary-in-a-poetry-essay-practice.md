These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. The poem "Night Shift" and the prompt were written by Marlbridge for this page. Mark points are a suggested Marlbridge rubric, not an official scoring guideline. Questions 1 to 6 are multiple choice; Questions 7 and 8 are written responses. Allow about 50 minutes. No calculator is needed.

Read this poem, "Night Shift", written by Marlbridge. Questions 1 to 8 refer to it unless stated otherwise.

> At four, the only light is the vending machine's  
> low hum of orange, and the hall smells of floor-wax.  
> She sits behind the desk and logs the names  
> of those who come, who leave. Her tea goes cold.  
> The ward is quiet. Down a corridor  
> a monitor insists, then stops; a shoe  
> squeaks on the lino. Somewhere, someone's turning  
> to face the wall. She writes the time, and times  
> the pause between two breaths she doesn't hear  
> but knows are there. At six, the windows grey.  
> The day shift comes in laughing, shaking rain  
> from coats. She hands the clipboard over, and  
> for a second can't remember how to leave.

**Prompt (written by Marlbridge).** Write an essay analysing how the poet conveys the nurse's complex relationship to her work.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · foundation)

Which of the following is a **defensible thesis** that responds to the prompt?

- (A) "The poet uses imagery, a quiet tone and free verse."
- (B) "By showing the nurse logging names and timing breaths she cannot hear, the poem presents her work as a vigilance she cannot switch off, so that when the shift ends she is unsettled by having nothing to watch."
- (C) "'Night Shift' is about a nurse who works through the night."
- (D) "The poem conveys the nurse's complex relationship to her work."

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** It makes a claim that could be argued, responds to the prompt, and implies a line of reasoning (vigilance, then the unsettled ending).

- (A) names features but makes no claim about meaning.
- (C) summarises the poem and gives nothing to defend.
- (D) restates the prompt in the poet's name.

</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · core)

A student writes: "In 'Night Shift' the poet uses the details of the ward to show that the nurse is tired." What is the main weakness of this sentence as a thesis?

- (A) It is not a claim.
- (B) It contradicts the poem.
- (C) It is a claim, but thin: it does not engage with the prompt's "complex relationship", so it offers no interpretation of her feelings about the work.
- (D) It is too long.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** Tiredness is a defensible observation, but the prompt asks about a complex relationship to work. A thesis must address the question; this sentence covers only one feeling.

- (A) is wrong because the sentence does assert something.
- (B) is wrong because there is no contradiction; the nurse's tea has gone cold, and the ward is quiet, which suggests fatigue.
- (D) misjudges the problem: length is not the issue.

</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · core)

A student's thesis is: "The nurse's attentive routines show vigilance that she cannot switch off, so the shift's end unsettles her." Which sequence of claims forms the best **line of reasoning**?

- (A) 1. The poem uses a vending machine. 2. The poem uses a clipboard. 3. The poem uses tea.
- (B) 1. She is tired. 2. She is hungry. 3. She is bored.
- (C) 1. The poem is about a hospital. 2. Hospitals are busy. 3. Nurses are important.
- (D) 1. Her routines of logging and timing show attentive care. 2. The details of the quiet ward make that care a solitary, almost obsessive habit. 3. The ending shows the habit continuing after it is no longer needed.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (D).** Each claim advances the thesis in a logical order (care, solitude and habit, the habit outliving its purpose).

- (A) lists objects, not claims.
- (B) lists feelings without evidence or connection to the thesis.
- (C) drifts from the poem to general statements.

</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · core)

For the evidence "a monitor insists, then stops", which sentence is **commentary** rather than summary or device-naming?

- (A) "The poet uses personification here."
- (B) "By giving the monitor the verb 'insists' the poem makes the machine sound demanding, and the abrupt 'stops' leaves a silence the nurse must interpret, which shows how her vigilance depends on small sounds."
- (C) "This line is important."
- (D) "A monitor makes a noise and then stops."

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** It identifies the effect of the specific words and explains how that effect supports the thesis about vigilance.

- (A) names a device without effect or link.
- (C) is an empty evaluation with no explanation.
- (D) is summary: it restates the evidence.

</details>

## Question 5 (multiple choice · core)

A student's paragraph quotes "the pause between two breaths she doesn't hear / but knows are there" and explains well how it shows her sensitivity. The paragraph ends with the sentence "Therefore the poem has a nurse in it." What is the most useful revision?

- (A) Add a summary of the whole poem.
- (B) Delete the paragraph.
- (C) Replace the last sentence with one that links the evidence back to the thesis, such as "Her ability to attend to what she cannot hear shows that her vigilance is as much imagination as procedure, which is why it cannot simply stop at six."
- (D) Add a second quotation about the vending machine.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** The paragraph's analysis is sound; what is missing is the link that ties the evidence to the line of reasoning and thesis.

- (A) adds summary, which earns nothing.
- (B) discards good analysis.
- (D) adds evidence that does not address the missing link.

</details>

## Question 6 (multiple choice · stretch)

Which sentence best shows the **sophistication** that comes from handling a tension in the poem?

- (A) "Some readers might see the poem differently."
- (B) "Although the nurse's routines look like detached procedure, the intimacy of timing breaths she cannot hear shows that care and habit have become inseparable, so her difficulty leaving at the end is both a relief and a loss."
- (C) "The poem is about a nurse and it is sad."
- (D) "The poem is relevant to modern hospitals."

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** It holds two readings in tension (detached procedure and intimate care), uses the poem's evidence, and develops a more complex interpretation of the ending.

- (A) acknowledges an alternative without developing it.
- (C) is generic.
- (D) offers a vague connection to context with no explanation.

</details>

## Question 7 (written response · core)

Write a thesis statement (one or two sentences) for the prompt above, and then list **three claims** that form a line of reasoning for it.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Sample response (written by Marlbridge).**

*Thesis:* "The poem presents the nurse's work as a form of devoted attention that has outgrown its purpose: her small rituals show care, but the unsettled ending shows that she has built her identity around the vigil."

*Claims:*
1. The routines of logging and timing ("logs the names", "times / the pause between two breaths") show careful, devoted attention.
2. The quiet, nearly empty ward ("The ward is quiet", the cold tea) makes the care solitary and slightly obsessive, so the work becomes her whole world.
3. At the end, when the day shift arrives "laughing", she "can't remember how to leave", showing that the vigil is part of who she is.

**Suggested Marlbridge rubric (4 points)**

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | The thesis is defensible and responds to the prompt (not a restatement or summary). |
| 2 | The thesis expresses a complex relationship (care and dependence, or similar), not a single feeling. |
| 3 | Each claim is distinct and can be supported with evidence from the poem. |
| 4 | The claims are ordered logically so that together they defend the thesis. |

</details>

## Question 8 (written response · stretch)

Here is a weak paragraph about "Night Shift".

> The poet says that the nurse is at her desk at four in the morning. She writes the time and times the pauses between breaths. This shows that she is a good nurse. The poet uses a quiet tone.

Rewrite it as a strong body paragraph (about 120 to 150 words) that follows the claim, evidence, commentary, link model, and supports your thesis from Question 7 or another defensible thesis.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**What was wrong.** It summarises, makes a general judgement ("a good nurse"), names a tone without evidence or effect, and does not link to a thesis.

**Sample response (written by Marlbridge).** The nurse's rituals show devoted attention that goes beyond procedure. She "writes the time, and times / the pause between two breaths she doesn't hear / but knows are there". The doubled verbs, "writes" and "times", mimic the routine of recording, but the phrase "doesn't hear / but knows" moves from observation to imagination: she is attending to something she cannot perceive. The line break after "times" suspends the pause, so the reader waits through it as she does. That patience shows care as sensitivity, not just duty, and prepares for the ending, where she "can't remember how to leave". Her skill and her inability to stop are the same quality, so the poem presents her devotion as both her strength and her limit.

**Suggested Marlbridge rubric (4 points)**

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Opens with a claim that advances a line of reasoning. |
| 2 | Uses specific, accurate quotation as evidence. |
| 3 | Commentary explains how the words and form create an effect (not just naming devices). |
| 4 | Ends by linking the paragraph back to the thesis or the next claim. |

</details>
