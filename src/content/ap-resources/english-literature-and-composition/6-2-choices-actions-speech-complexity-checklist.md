Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/6-2-choices-actions-speech-complexity-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/6-2-choices-actions-speech-complexity-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what it means for a character to be complex, using the idea of tension rather than unpredictability. *(Guide: "What makes a character complex?"; Practice Question 5)*
- I can name the three channels of evidence: speech, action and private thought. *(Guide: "Three channels of evidence"; Practice Question 4)*
- I can explain how an inconsistency between private thoughts and behaviour reveals a tension between private and professed values. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Questions 1 and 3)*
- I can explain how competing or conflicting choices contribute to complexity in the whole text. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Questions 2 and 8)*

## Skills

- I can use the six-step method to map a tension: professed, actual, private, gap, meaning, function. *(Guide: "A method: map the tension"; Practice Question 9)*
- I can use stage directions or small gestures as evidence of a private feeling. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Questions 4 and 8)*
- I can write an analytic paragraph that quotes a professed value, a contradicting action and a private detail. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Question 7)*
- I can explain the cost of a choice as evidence of real conflict. *(Guide: Jane Eyre example; Practice Question 2)*

## Reasoning

- I can avoid labelling a character a hypocrite and go on to say what the gap shows. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Question 6)*
- I can decide whether an inconsistency is a deliberate source of meaning before concluding that it is a mistake. *(Guide: Common misconceptions; Practice Question 5)*
- I can state the function of a character's complexity, such as suspense, irony or sympathy. *(Guide: both model sentences; Practice Questions 6 and 9)*
