These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. Passages marked "written by Marlbridge" are original; Hamlet (Shakespeare, about 1600), Macbeth (about 1606) and Jane Eyre (Charlotte Brontë, 1847) are public-domain works. The mark points are a **suggested Marlbridge rubric**, not an official scoring guideline. Allow about 50 minutes.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · core)

Which of the following is the strongest thesis statement about Hamlet's delay in avenging his father?

- (A) By setting Hamlet's reflection against the swift action of Laertes and Fortinbras, Shakespeare presents his delay as both a failure of will and a mark of conscience, so that the play refuses to settle whether thought is a weakness or a virtue.
- (B) Shakespeare uses soliloquy, imagery and foils to depict Hamlet's delay.
- (C) Hamlet delays killing Claudius.
- (D) Hamlet is a tragedy about a prince.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** It makes a claim about meaning, is specific to the play and defensible, previews a line of reasoning (contrast, then the double reading of delay) and says what it means.

- (B) lists devices without saying what they accomplish.
- (C) states a fact that no one disputes.
- (D) is a general classification, not an interpretation.
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · foundation)

A line of reasoning is best described as

- (A) a list of the quotations a student plans to use.
- (B) the logical sequence of claims that work together to defend the thesis.
- (C) the first sentence of an essay.
- (D) a summary of the work's plot in order.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** A line of reasoning organises claims so that each builds on the last and all support the thesis.

- (A) describes evidence, not reasoning.
- (C) confuses a position in the essay with a logical structure.
- (D) describes summary, which is not an argument.
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · core)

In a body paragraph on Macbeth, which sentence is commentary?

- (A) Macbeth asks, "Will all great Neptune's ocean wash this blood / Clean from my hand?"
- (B) Macbeth has just killed Duncan.
- (C) In Act 2, Macbeth returns from Duncan's chamber.
- (D) The exaggeration of a whole ocean failing to cleanse one hand shows that his guilt cannot be removed by any physical remedy, which supports the claim that he has betrayed his own conscience.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (D).** It interprets the evidence, explains how the language creates the meaning and connects it to a claim.

- (A) is evidence (a quotation).
- (B) and (C) are summary of events.
</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · core)

Which statement about a thesis and a line of reasoning is accurate?

- (A) A thesis must list every literary device that will be discussed.
- (B) A thesis should avoid making any claim, so that it cannot be wrong.
- (C) A thesis may preview the development of the argument, but it need not list the points, the devices or the evidence.
- (D) A thesis must be a quotation from the text.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** A thesis can indicate a direction but does not have to list the points, devices or evidence.

- (A) imposes a requirement that does not exist.
- (B) misunderstands the purpose: a thesis must be defensible, which means someone could argue against it.
- (D) confuses evidence with the thesis.
</details>

## Question 5 (multiple choice · core)

**Context written by Marlbridge.** A student's claim is that Mr Ince's gentility covers anxiety about money. The evidence is that, after a supper at which he said that a gentleman never speaks of money and then spoke of nothing else, he "went, candle in hand, to the strong-room door and tried the lock twice." Which commentary is strongest?

- (A) The repeated check reveals an anxiety that contradicts his stated principle, so his physical behaviour undermines his claim to gentility and supports the view that his manners are a cover.
- (B) This shows that he is a man.
- (C) Mr Ince tries the lock twice.
- (D) The lock is a symbol.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** It interprets the detail, links it to the earlier contradiction and connects it to the claim.

- (B) offers no interpretation related to the claim.
- (C) restates the evidence.
- (D) names a technique without evidence or explanation.
</details>

## Question 6 (multiple choice · stretch)

A student's draft thesis reads, "In Jane Eyre, Jane leaves Thornfield after learning Rochester's secret." Which revision best turns this summary into an interpretive thesis?

- (A) "In Jane Eyre, Jane leaves Thornfield, which is a large house."
- (B) "By having Jane leave a home and a love she wants, Brontë argues that self-respect can outweigh desire, and makes independence a moral choice rather than a mood."
- (C) "In Jane Eyre, Jane leaves Thornfield and goes elsewhere."
- (D) "Jane Eyre is a novel with a heroine named Jane."

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** It moves from what happens to what it means, is defensible and gives a reason that the choice matters.

- (A) and (C) are plot detail with no claim.
- (D) is a general description.
</details>

## Question 7 (written response · core)

**Passage written by Marlbridge.**

> Mr Ince told his daughters that a gentleman never speaks of money. He spoke of nothing else through the whole of supper, though always in the tone of a man regretting that he must. When the cloth was drawn he went, candle in hand, to the strong-room door and tried the lock twice.

Write a thesis statement of one or two sentences for an essay on how this character's inconsistency contributes to the meaning of a novel.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Model thesis.** "By contrasting Mr Ince's professed disdain for money with his obsessive talk and his nightly check of the lock, the narrator reveals gentility as a performance covering fear, and so presents the novel's polite society as a world in which manners conceal anxiety about security."

**Suggested Marlbridge rubric (4 points).**

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Makes an interpretive claim about meaning or function, not a summary |
| 2 | Is defensible and specific to the passage's details |
| 3 | Connects the character to the wider meaning of the novel (the significance) |
| 4 | Previews a direction for the argument without merely listing devices |

Other defensible theses are acceptable, for example that Mr Ince's inconsistency makes him pitiable rather than hypocritical.
</details>

## Question 8 (written response · core)

**Claim and evidence written by Marlbridge.** Claim: "The neglected glasshouse represents Mr Wray's unresolved mourning." Evidence: "each Sunday, Mr Wray walked to the end of the path and stood a while with his hat in his hand, as though at a grave." A draft commentary reads: "This shows that Mr Wray is sad."

Rewrite the commentary in three or four sentences, using the four moves: interpret, explain how, connect to the claim, connect to the larger argument.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Model response.** The ritual of standing with his hat in his hand turns an ordinary Sunday walk into an act of mourning, and the simile "as though at a grave" makes the nature of that mourning explicit: the glasshouse has become a memorial. Because he performs the gesture weekly, the grief is not a past event but a continuing practice. This supports the claim that the glasshouse represents mourning he has not resolved. It also advances the larger argument that the garden preserves loss rather than letting it pass, since the building is left to decay and he never clears it.

**Suggested Marlbridge rubric (4 points).** One point for interpreting the gesture beyond "sad" (mourning, memorial); one for explaining how a specific word or choice produces the meaning ("weekly ritual", "as though at a grave", "hat in his hand"); one for connecting to the claim (unresolved mourning); one for connecting to the larger argument (loss preserved, not released).
</details>

## Question 9 (written response · stretch)

Choose a longer work you know well, or use Hamlet. Write a thesis and a three-claim line of reasoning for the prompt: "Analyse how a contrast between two characters contributes to the meaning of the work as a whole." Then explain in a sentence why your claims must appear in that order.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Model response (Hamlet).**

- **Thesis:** "By setting Hamlet's hesitation against the swift action of Laertes and Fortinbras, Shakespeare makes the play an inquiry into whether reflection is a weakness or a form of conscience, and refuses to settle the question."
- **Claim 1:** Laertes' immediate fury ("To hell, allegiance! vows, to the blackest devil!") shows action without scruple, and so exposes what Hamlet's delay is not.
- **Claim 2:** Fortinbras' expedition for a trivial patch of ground shows a different sort of action, honour pursued without reflection, which Hamlet admires and condemns in himself.
- **Claim 3:** Seen against both, Hamlet's delay appears as the price of a conscience that questions the justice of revenge itself.
- **Order:** Claims 1 and 2 establish the two contrasts that Claim 3 needs; only after seeing the alternatives can the reader interpret Hamlet's behaviour as a choice, not a failing.

**Suggested Marlbridge rubric (4 points).** One point for a defensible, specific thesis; one for three claims that each need proof; one for evidence or a precise reference for at least one claim; one for an explanation of the order that shows logical progression rather than a list. Any well-supported work earns credit.
</details>
