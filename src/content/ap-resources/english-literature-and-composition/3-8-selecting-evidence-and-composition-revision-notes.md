## Recap

**Select** evidence that is **relevant** (bears on the claim) and **sufficient** (enough, of good quality, to support the line of reasoning). Use it **strategically**: to illustrate, clarify, exemplify, associate, amplify or qualify. Evidence is **effective** when commentary explains its logical relationship to the claim. **Grammar and mechanics** that follow conventions make your interpretation clear.

## Key relationships

| Idea | What it means | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Relevant | Bears on this claim | Can I explain the link briefly? |
| Sufficient | Enough, in quantity and quality | Two or three varied details with commentary? |
| Illustrate | Shows the claim at work | Does it display the idea? |
| Clarify | Makes a point precise | Does it define a term? |
| Exemplify | Gives an example of a pattern | Is it representative? |
| Associate | Links two ideas | Does it connect them? |
| Amplify | Builds on a point | Does it raise the stakes? |
| Qualify | Limits or complicates | Does it refine the claim? |

## What you may assume

- Evidence can be a short phrase, a detail or a stage direction.
- A relevant detail that complicates your claim can strengthen it.
- Editing for clarity does not change your ideas.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Quoting everything that seems related.
- Choosing evidence of only one kind.
- Leaving quotations without commentary.
- Ignoring comma splices, agreement and tense errors.
- Changing the wording of a quotation.

## Key terms

- **Relevant:** bears directly on this claim.
- **Sufficient:** enough, in quantity and quality, to support the line of reasoning.
- **Purposes of evidence:** illustrate, clarify, exemplify, associate, amplify, qualify.
- **Mechanics:** punctuation, agreement and tense that let the reader follow.

## Sentence frames

- "[Evidence] illustrates / clarifies / qualifies the claim because …."
- "Although …, the detail '…' shows …."
- Edit check: one idea per sentence, a comma plus "and" or "but" between clauses, the present tense.

## Mini-example

Claim: Lady Macbeth redefines courage. Illustrate: "Art thou afeard / To be the same in thine own act and valour / As thou art in desire?" Qualify: Macbeth's "Who dares do more is none".

## How questions ask about this

- *"Which detail is the most relevant evidence for the claim?"*: choose the detail that bears directly on it.
- *"The purpose of this evidence is to …"*: choose among illustrate, clarify, exemplify, associate, amplify or qualify.
- *"Which sentence integrates the quotation correctly?"*: check exactness, grammar and punctuation.
- Constructed response: select two or three varied details, name each purpose and explain the link; edit for errors.

## Quick self-check

1. Name the six purposes of evidence.
2. Why is "He has almost supp'd" not relevant to a claim about courage?
3. Correct: "Its a small detail, it matter."

**Answers.**

1. Illustrate, clarify, exemplify, associate, amplify, qualify.
2. It concerns the dinner and has no bearing on courage.
3. "It is a small detail, but it matters."
