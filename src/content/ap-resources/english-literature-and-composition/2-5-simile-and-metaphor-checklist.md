Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/2-5-simile-and-metaphor-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/2-5-simile-and-metaphor-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define simile and metaphor and say how they differ. *(Guide: "Comparison is how poems make the abstract concrete"; Practice questions 4 and 7)*
- I can name the main subject and the comparison subject in a figure. *(Guide: "The two subjects"; Practice question 1)*
- I can explain that context decides which traits transfer. *(Guide: "What transfers, and who decides")*
- I can explain why a difference between the two things can be revealing. *(Guide: "Where the comparison does not fit"; Practice question 6)*

## Skills

- I can list associations of a comparison subject and choose the ones the poem supports. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 3; Practice question 2)*
- I can track what transfers across an extended metaphor, line by line. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 3)*
- I can explain what an image shows about the main subject. *(Practice questions 3 and 5)*
- I can write a short explanation of a metaphor with two quoted details. *(Practice question 8)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how the weather or setting words in a poem decide which traits transfer. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 4)*
- I can explain what a final line adds to an earlier metaphor. *(Practice question 9)*
- I can replace "the poet uses a metaphor" with a sentence about what transfers and what it shows. *(Guide: "Strong and weak statements about simile and metaphor")*
