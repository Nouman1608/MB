Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/9-5-details-diction-and-syntax-that-reveal-perspective-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/9-5-details-diction-and-syntax-that-reveal-perspective-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what perspective is and why it controls what the reader is given. *(Guide: "Perspective: who is looking, and how can you tell?"; Practice Question 1)*
- I can explain how details, diction and syntax each reveal perspective. *(Guide: "Perspective: who is looking, and how can you tell?"; Practice Questions 2 and 3)*
- I can explain how contrasting perspectives in one text add complexity. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Question 4)*
- I can explain how a change or inconsistency in perspective produces irony. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Question 5)*

## Skills

- I can identify the source of a perspective (first person, close third, omniscient). *(Guide: "A method for perspective"; Practice Question 5)*
- I can collect details, key words and sentence patterns as evidence. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Questions 2 and 3)*
- I can describe a perspective with a precise phrase, not a single word. *(Guide: "Weak and strong versions side by side"; Practice Question 4)*
- I can describe a sentence pattern in plain words and state its effect. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Question 6)*

## Reasoning

- I can say what a perspective lets the reader see or hides from them. *(Guide: "A method for perspective"; Practice Question 7)*
- I can notice when a narrator is implicated in what they observe. *(Practice Questions 4 and 8)*
- I can compare a weak and a strong sentence about perspective and say why the strong one scores. *(Guide: "Weak and strong versions side by side")*
- I can write a paragraph on perspective with a claim, evidence and commentary. *(Practice Questions 7 and 8)*
