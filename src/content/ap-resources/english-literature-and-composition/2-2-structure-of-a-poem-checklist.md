Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/2-2-structure-of-a-poem-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/2-2-structure-of-a-poem-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define structure as the arrangement of lines, stanzas, sentences and ideas. *(Guide: "Structure is arrangement with a purpose")*
- I can explain how line and stanza breaks develop and relate ideas. *(Guide: "Structure is arrangement with a purpose"; Practice question 4)*
- I can explain how the position of an idea shapes the reader's expectation. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 3)*
- I can explain the difference between structure and form. *(Guide: "Form as a frame")*

## Skills

- I can tell end-stopped lines from enjambed lines and say what each does in context. *(Guide: "What to look at"; Practice questions 1, 2 and 5)*
- I can identify what changes across a stanza break. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice questions 4 and 6)*
- I can describe how a sonnet's quatrains and couplet are arranged. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 7)*
- I can link a structural feature to a quoted line. *(Practice questions 8 and 9)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how a poem's structure imitates its subject. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can replace "the poem has three stanzas" with a sentence about what the arrangement does. *(Guide: "Strong and weak statements about structure")*
- I can say where a poem departs from a form and what that suggests. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*
