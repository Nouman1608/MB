Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/5-5-allusion-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/5-5-allusion-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define allusion and say how it differs from a quotation. *(Guide: "What an allusion is"; Practice question 4)*
- I can list the kinds of source an allusion can refer to: literary works including myths and sacred texts, other works of art, and people, places and events. *(Guide: "What an allusion is"; Practice question 1)*
- I can explain that an allusion carries the associations of its source into a poem. *(Guide: "What an allusion is"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain that an allusion may elevate, deepen, contrast or create irony. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 3)*

## Skills

- I can work through reference, associations, link and effect. *(Guide: "What to say when you explain one"; Practice question 6)*
- I can identify a reference from the signals in a poem, even if I do not know the source well. *(Guide: "What to say when you explain one"; Practice question 5)*
- I can link an allusion's associations to the poem's situation. *(Guide: Worked example 1, step 3; Practice question 6)*
- I can quote the words around an allusion accurately. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 8)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how an allusion raises a private experience to a larger scale. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 7)*
- I can explain how a grand allusion set beside a small reality creates irony. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice questions 3 and 5)*
- I can avoid retelling a source and focus on what its associations do. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice question 6)*
- I can write a paragraph on an allusion with accurate quotations and commentary. *(Practice question 8)*
