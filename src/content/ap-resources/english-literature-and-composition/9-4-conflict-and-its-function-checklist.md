Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/9-4-conflict-and-its-function-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/9-4-conflict-and-its-function-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can name the main kinds of conflict: internal, interpersonal, social and circumstantial. *(Guide: "Conflict: the engine and the argument of a plot"; Practice Question 1)*
- I can explain how conflict both drives the plot and develops meaning. *(Guide: "Conflict: the engine and the argument of a plot"; Practice Question 2)*
- I can explain how an unseen character or earlier action can cause conflict. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Questions 4 and 5)*
- I can explain what catharsis is and when it occurs. *(Guide: "What the framework asks you to notice"; Practice Question 6)*

## Skills

- I can identify the opposing forces and what each stands for. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Question 7)*
- I can say what is at stake for each side and for the reader. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Question 7)*
- I can trace how events escalate, complicate or delay a conflict. *(Guide: "A method for conflict")*
- I can quote accurately from a scene of conflict. *(Practice Questions 7 and 8)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain what an unresolved ending contributes to an interpretation. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Question 3)*
- I can explain how a conflict that is never shown still governs the present. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Question 4)*
- I can compare a weak and a strong sentence about conflict and say why the strong one scores. *(Guide: "Weak and strong versions side by side")*
- I can write a paragraph on conflict with a claim, evidence and commentary. *(Practice Questions 7 and 8)*
