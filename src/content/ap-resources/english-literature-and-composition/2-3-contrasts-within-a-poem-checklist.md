Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/2-3-contrasts-within-a-poem-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/2-3-contrasts-within-a-poem-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between a shift and a juxtaposition. *(Guide: "A contrast is a difference that does work")*
- I can name seven things that can be contrasted in a poem. *(Guide: "What can be contrasted")*
- I can name three kinds of signal that mark a shift. *(Guide: "How a shift is signalled"; Practice question 5)*
- I can explain why a signal word does not by itself prove a shift. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*

## Skills

- I can find the shift or juxtaposition in a short poem and quote both sides. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice questions 1 and 5)*
- I can name the word, structural convention or punctuation that signals a shift. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 1; Practice questions 2 and 3)*
- I can name more than one kind of contrast at work in the same poem. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 4)*
- I can write a short explanation of a contrast with details from both sides. *(Practice question 8)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain what a contrast emphasises or reveals, not only what it is. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 5; Practice question 4)*
- I can explain how placing the absent or the ruined last changes the poem's focus. *(Practice questions 4 and 9)*
- I can replace "there is a shift" with a sentence naming what changes and what it shows. *(Guide: "Strong and weak statements about contrast")*
