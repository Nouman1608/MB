Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/6-4-narrator-perspective-reliability-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/6-4-narrator-perspective-reliability-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain how a narrator controls the details and emphases that affect a reader's interpretation. *(Guide: "The narrator as a filter"; Practice Question 3)*
- I can explain how diction, syntax and tone reveal a narrator's perspective. *(Guide: "Four kinds of evidence for perspective"; Practice Questions 1, 2 and 5)*
- I can explain how details a narrator includes or omits can convey bias. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Questions 3 and 7)*
- I can explain how a narrator's reliability affects a reader's understanding of a character's motives. *(Guide: "Reliability: how far should we trust the account?"; Practice Question 8)*

## Skills

- I can analyse the syntax of a sentence, saying what is emphasised and what is subordinated. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Question 1)*
- I can use the reliability toolkit: stake, omission, description of others, contradiction, tone, knowledge. *(Guide: "Reliability" table; Practice Questions 4 and 8)*
- I can quote a loaded word or phrase and explain the attitude it carries. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Question 2)*
- I can write a paragraph that explains perspective and reliability with specific evidence. *(Guide: both model sentences; Practice Question 7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why unreliable does not simply mean lying. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Questions 3 and 6)*
- I can judge reliability as a matter of degree and say what I trust and what I doubt. *(Guide: "Reliability" section; Practice Questions 6 and 7)*
- I can explain how a retrospective narrator's perspective differs from the younger self's. *(Guide: Great Expectations example; Practice Questions 5 and 9)*
