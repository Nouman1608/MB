## Recap

- **Image**: a sensory detail. **Imagery**: the pattern of such details.
- **Motif**: a **unified pattern of recurring objects or images** used to **emphasise a significant idea** in large parts of or throughout a text.
- **Symbolic setting**: a setting that is, or comes to be, associated with **abstractions** such as emotions, ideologies or beliefs. Some settings have near-universal associations, but each text uses them in its own way.

## What images do

| Function | Quick test |
|---|---|
| Create mood | What atmosphere does the detail set? |
| Make the abstract concrete | What idea does it give a physical form? |
| Characterise | What does the character notice? |
| Link scenes | Where else does this detail appear? |

## Five-step method for a motif

1. **Collect** recurring objects or images.
2. **Group** them by what they share.
3. **State the idea** they emphasise.
4. **Track change** across the text.
5. **Explain function**.

## Signals that a setting is symbolic

- Emotive or abstract language around it.
- Characters react as if it meant more than a place.
- It recurs at key moments.
- A boundary or crossing is marked (a fence, a door, a threshold).
- It is linked to ideas such as guilt, freedom, decay or safety.

## Examples (public domain)

- **The Fall of the House of Usher** (Edgar Allan Poe, 1839): the house is building and family at once; the fissure in the wall suggests decay, and the collapse ends both.
- **Young Goodman Brown** (Nathaniel Hawthorne, 1835): the village and forest set faith against doubt; Faith's pink ribbons recur as an image of innocence ("My Faith is gone!").

## Assumptions

- Common associations are a starting point; the text decides the meaning.
- A motif must recur; one image is only a detail.
- Evidence is the quotation, the repetition and the change.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Treating every garden, storm or forest as symbolic without evidence.
- Calling any repeated word a motif.
- Describing images without saying what they do.
- Giving one fixed meaning with no textual support.
- Forgetting function.

## Quick self-check

**1.** A story mentions a stopped clock, a calendar left on one page and a locked bedroom. What motif might these form?

**2.** Name two signs that a setting is symbolic.

**3.** Improve: "The forest is a symbol."

**4.** What is the difference between an image and a motif?

**Answers.**

1. A motif of arrested time or an unprocessed loss: each object records a moment that was not allowed to pass.
2. For example: emotive or abstract language around it; characters' strong reactions; recurrence at key moments; a boundary crossing.
3. For example: "By making the forest the place where Brown's trust in his neighbours is overturned, Hawthorne turns it into a figure for doubt."
4. An image is a single sensory detail. A motif is a pattern of recurring images or objects that emphasises an idea.
