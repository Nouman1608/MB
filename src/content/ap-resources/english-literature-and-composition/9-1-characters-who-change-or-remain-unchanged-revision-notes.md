## Recap

A character **changes** when their values, beliefs, understanding or conduct differ after the pressures of the plot, and the difference can be traced. A character **remains unchanged** when the same pressures leave them as they were. Change is not the same as events; it is not always improvement; and staying the same can be as meaningful as changing. **Minor characters** often remain unchanged because the narrative does not focus on them: they may advance the plot, interact with major characters, or throw a major character's change into relief. Always link the change, or its absence, to the interpretation of the work as a whole. The full explanation, with worked examples from *Macbeth* and *Pride and Prejudice*, is in the [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/9-1-characters-who-change-or-remain-unchanged-study-guide/).

## Key relationships

| Step | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | What are the early traits and values? | Lady Macbeth: "unsex me here"; "A little water clears us of this deed" |
| Pressure | What tests them? | Mounting killings; Macbeth's withdrawal |
| Response | Same or different? | "What, will these hands ne'er be clean?" |
| Meaning | What does it show about values? | Conscience she tried to suppress persists |
| Whole | How does it affect the work? | The play argues that guilt survives will |

| Pattern | What it can suggest |
|---|---|
| Change as growth | The work values self-knowledge (Elizabeth, Darcy) |
| Change as reversal or collapse | The work warns about a belief (Lady Macbeth) |
| Stasis of a minor character | A fixed social type; a foil (Mr Collins) |
| Stasis of a major character | Tragic or satirical refusal to learn |

## Conditions to remember

- You need a before and an after, both evidenced.
- A change may be partial or apparent; name which.
- Link every change to the work as a whole.
- Attribute values to the character from what they say and do, not from your opinion of them.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Summarising events instead of tracing change.
- Assuming all major characters change, or all minor characters are unimportant.
- Calling any mood shift a change in character.
- Quoting one moment as proof of a change.
- Forgetting the "so what" for the whole work.

## Quick self-check

1. What is the difference between an event and a change?
2. Why do minor characters often remain unchanged?
3. Give the five steps of the method.
4. What does the contrast between "A little water clears us" and "will these hands ne'er be clean?" show?
5. What does Collins's lack of change help *Pride and Prejudice* do?

**Answers.**
1. An event happens to a character; a change is a traceable difference in values, beliefs or conduct.
2. The narrative does not focus on them; they serve the plot, interact with major characters or highlight others.
3. Baseline, pressure, response, meaning, whole.
4. Lady Macbeth's belief that guilt can be washed away is reversed; her conscience persists.
5. It gives a fixed point against which Elizabeth's growth and Charlotte's choice are measured.
