Use this diagnostic to find **which Unit 7 topics to revisit**: one short question per topic.

These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. All passages were written by Marlbridge. The diagnostic is not calibrated and gives **no predicted score**.

**How to take it.** Allow 40 minutes; answer everything before opening any answer. Write your answer to the written question before reading the model response.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · 7.1)

**Passage written by Marlbridge.**

> For twenty years Mr Calloway had carried his father's silence like a coat he could not take off. At the funeral, when the vicar asked whether anyone wished to speak, he heard himself say, "He taught me to mend a net," and wept, and knew, as one knows the sea is there, that he had been angry for the wrong reason.

The moment is best described as

- (A) a gradual change over twenty years.
- (B) a description of the funeral service.
- (C) a flashback to his childhood.
- (D) an epiphany: a sudden realisation, tied to the central conflict between him and his father, that changes how he sees his own anger.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (D).** "He heard himself say" and "knew" mark a sudden shift in perception that relates to the long conflict with his father and recasts it.

- (A) misreads the long background ("twenty years") as the time of the change; the change is sudden.
- (B) ignores the character's realisation.
- (C) no earlier time is narrated beyond the single remembered skill.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 7.1 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/7-1-character-change-complex-relationships-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · 7.2)

**Passage written by Marlbridge.**

> In the city Ines kept every window shut. On the island, on the first morning, she opened them all and stood in the draught like a person being forgiven.

What does the change of setting, together with her behaviour, most strongly suggest?

- (A) The island is colder than the city.
- (B) Ines dislikes fresh air.
- (C) The island is associated with release, and her behaviour shows a shift from closed-in caution to relief.
- (D) The city is on an island.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** The contrast between shut windows and open ones shows how she behaves in each setting, and the simile "like a person being forgiven" shows what the island means to her.

- (A) takes "draught" literally and ignores the simile.
- (B) is contradicted by her opening "them all".
- (D) misreads the contrast between the two places.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 7.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/7-2-setting-relationship-character-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · 7.3)

**Passage written by Marlbridge.**

> She counted the seconds after the shot: one, and the window, and the dog turning its head, and the curtain lifting like a slow breath, and two.

The passage most clearly uses

- (A) compression, summarising a long period.
- (B) a flashback to an earlier event.
- (C) expansion, in which a moment of a second or two is stretched across a series of small details.
- (D) foreshadowing of a later event.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** Only two seconds pass, but they hold the window, the dog and the curtain, so time seems to slow.

- (A) is the opposite.
- (B) no earlier time is presented.
- (D) nothing here anticipates later events.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 7.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/7-3-plot-order-sequence-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · 7.4)

**Passage written by Marlbridge.**

> I want it known that I was in perfect command of myself. The bottles on the sideboard were arranged in order of height, as I always arrange them when I am in perfect command of myself.

What most strongly suggests that this narrator may be unreliable?

- (A) The over-insistent repetition of "perfect command of myself" and the odd evidence offered for it, which suggest a need to persuade.
- (B) The mention of bottles.
- (C) The word "sideboard".
- (D) The phrase "I want it known".

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Repeating a claim and supporting it with an irrelevant ritual are signs of over-insistence and anxiety.

- (B) is a detail that gains meaning only through the repeated claim.
- (C) is a neutral word.
- (D) is part of the defensive tone, but the repetition and the odd evidence are the strongest signs.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 7.4 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/7-4-narrator-reliability-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 5 (multiple choice · 7.5)

**Passage written by Marlbridge.**

> The lighthouse stood at the end of the spit, and nobody went out to it, though the path was kept clear.

The lighthouse is most likely

- (A) a setting that is beginning to carry symbolic weight, since the community avoids it yet maintains the path, suggesting respect for something it will not approach.
- (B) a literal building with no further significance.
- (C) a sign that the spit is dangerous to walk on.
- (D) an image of smell.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** The paradox of avoiding the lighthouse while keeping the path clear signals that it is associated with something unspoken.

- (B) ignores the community's contradictory behaviour.
- (C) is contradicted by the clear path.
- (D) mislabels the sensory detail, which is visual.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 7.5 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/7-5-symbol-imagery-fiction-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 6 (multiple choice · 7.6)

**Passage written by Marlbridge.**

> The speech went on like a tap left running in an empty house.

The simile most strongly conveys

- (A) admiration for the speaker's fluency.
- (B) a mocking attitude, since the speech is continuous, wasteful and heard by no one.
- (C) that the speaker is a plumber.
- (D) that the house is flooded.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** The traits of a running tap, unceasing and wasteful, in "an empty house" with no audience, convey scorn.

- (A) is the opposite of the effect.
- (C) and (D) take the simile literally.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 7.6 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/7-6-simile-personification-fiction-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 7 (written response · 7.7)

**Passage written by Marlbridge.**

> Mr Tidy cleaned his shop window every morning, though nobody had bought anything for a month. He spoke to the passers-by as if they were already customers, and when they did not stop, he nodded as though they had.

Write a thesis of one or two sentences on how the narrator presents Mr Tidy.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Model response.** "By letting Mr Tidy treat every passer-by as a customer, and even nod as though they had stopped, the narrator presents his courtesy as a defence against failure that is both comic and quietly dignified, and so suggests that routine can be a form of hope."

**What a good answer does.** It makes a defensible interpretation about the presentation (courtesy as defence), is specific to the passage's details and states a significance. Any other defensible, specific thesis earns credit.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 7.7 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/7-7-thesis-commentary-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 8 (multiple choice · 7.8)

A student's claim is "Mr Tidy's cheerfulness is a defence against fear." The only evidence is that he cleans the window every morning and greets the passers-by. What is the main weakness?

- (A) The evidence is too short.
- (B) It should be paraphrased.
- (C) It supports the cheerfulness but not the fear, so the evidence is insufficient for every part of the claim.
- (D) It should come from the end of the text.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** Sufficiency depends on coverage. The claim has two parts, and nothing yet shows fear.

- (A) confuses length with support.
- (B) paraphrase does not fix the missing part.
- (D) position is not the issue; coverage is.

**If you missed this:** [Topic 7.8 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/7-8-selecting-relevant-sufficient-evidence-study-guide/).
</details>
