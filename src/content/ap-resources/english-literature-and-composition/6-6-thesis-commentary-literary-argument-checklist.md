Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/6-6-thesis-commentary-literary-argument-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/6-6-thesis-commentary-literary-argument-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain that a thesis states an interpretation of a literary text and needs a defence through textual evidence and a line of reasoning. *(Guide: "What a literary argument does"; Practice Question 1)*
- I can explain that a thesis may preview the line of reasoning without listing points, devices or evidence. *(Guide: "What a thesis is"; Practice Question 4)*
- I can define a line of reasoning as a logical sequence of claims, and say why a list is not one. *(Guide: "The line of reasoning"; Practice Questions 2 and 9)*
- I can explain that commentary shows the relationship between evidence, claims and thesis. *(Guide: "Commentary: the part that makes it an argument"; Practice Question 3)*

## Skills

- I can tell a thesis from a summary, a fact, a topic and a list of devices. *(Guide: "What a thesis is not"; Practice Questions 1 and 6)*
- I can revise a weak thesis step by step into a defensible one. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Questions 6 and 7)*
- I can write commentary using four moves: interpret, explain how, connect to the claim, connect to the thesis. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Questions 5 and 8)*
- I can plan a three-claim line of reasoning in which each claim builds on the last. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Question 9)*

## Reasoning

- I can use the self-assessment table to rate my own thesis and paragraph. *(Guide: "Self-assessment table")*
- I can explain why quotation without commentary does not make an argument. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Question 5)*
- I can say why a claim needs to sit in a particular position in a line of reasoning. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Question 9)*
