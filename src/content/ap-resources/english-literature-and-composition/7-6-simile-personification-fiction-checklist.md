Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/7-6-simile-personification-fiction-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/7-6-simile-personification-fiction-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain that the function of a simile depends on the objects chosen and the traits they share. *(Guide: "What figurative comparison does"; Practice Questions 1 and 2)*
- I can explain what personification is, and that it can run in two directions. *(Guide: "Personification: two directions"; Practice Questions 3 and 4)*
- I can explain how assigning non-human qualities to a person communicates an attitude about that person. *(Guide: "Personification: two directions"; Practice Question 4)*
- I can explain why figurative language must be interpreted in context. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Question 5)*

## Skills

- I can analyse a simile in five steps: parts, trait, connotations, attitude, function. *(Guide: "Analysing a simile"; Practice Questions 6 and 7)*
- I can say what a comparison leaves out as well as what it includes. *(Guide: "Analysing a simile"; Practice Question 2)*
- I can identify personification in both directions in a short passage. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Questions 3 and 4)*
- I can write a paragraph that explains two or more figurative comparisons and the attitudes they convey. *(Guide: both model sentences; Practice Question 7)*

## Reasoning

- I can replace vague comments such as "paints a picture" with specific ones about trait and attitude. *(Guide: FAQs; Practice Question 6)*
- I can write an original simile and explain its objects, trait and attitude. *(Practice Question 8)*
- I can use a public-domain example, such as Hard Times, to explain how comparison conveys an attitude to people and systems. *(Guide: "Public-domain examples to learn from"; Practice Questions 5 and 9)*
