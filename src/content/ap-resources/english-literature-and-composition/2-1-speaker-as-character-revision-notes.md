## Recap

The **speaker** is the voice that says a poem; the speaker is not necessarily the poet. Treat the speaker as a character: their **words**, **details**, **organisation of thought**, **decisions** and **actions** reveal their perspective and any bias. Always ask who is speaking, to whom, when and why, and how.

## Key relationships

| Idea | What it means | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Speaker | The voice that says the poem | Pronouns, situation, relationship |
| Addressee | Who the speaker is talking to | "You", titles, form of address |
| Occasion | When and why the speaker speaks | Departure, return, quarrel, memory |
| Tone | The speaker's attitude | Word choice, repetition, corrections |
| Perspective | How the speaker understands their circumstances | What they notice, avoid and assume |
| Self-correction | The speaker revises a thought | Dashes, breaks, "no" or "I mean" |

## What you may assume

- A poem's "I" is a created voice unless the question tells you otherwise.
- You may infer a speaker's situation from details, but state only what the lines support.
- A speaker may be honest, mistaken, evasive or ironic.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Writing "the poet" when you mean "the speaker".
- Labelling the tone with no quoted lines.
- Ignoring the addressee.
- Treating omissions and corrections as unimportant.
- Retelling the poem instead of saying what the voice reveals.

## Key terms

- **Speaker:** the voice that says the poem; not necessarily the poet.
- **Addressee:** the person or thing the speaker talks to.
- **Occasion:** the moment and reason for speaking.
- **Tone:** the speaker's attitude, shown by word choice, detail and corrections.

## Sentence frames

- "The speaker's [repetition / correction / omission] of '…' shows …."
- "By addressing '…', the speaker shows a relationship of …."
- "The tone moves from … to …, signalled by …."

## Mini-example

Line: "Or so / the ticket says." Reveals: doubt about returning. Claim: the speaker hides fear behind practical instructions.

## How questions ask about this

- *"The speaker's phrase … most directly shows that the speaker …"*: find the attitude or perspective the words reveal.
- *"The speaker addresses …"*: identify the addressee from forms of address and pronouns.
- *"The tone of the poem is best described as …"*: choose an answer that fits the whole poem and any shift.
- Constructed response: make a claim about the speaker's perspective and support it with two quoted lines.

## Quick self-check

1. In "I am only going for a week. Or so the ticket says", what does the second sentence reveal?
2. Why is "chartered" repeated in Blake's first stanza worth commenting on?
3. What is the difference between speaker and addressee?

**Answers.**

1. It hands certainty to the ticket, which shows that the speaker doubts she will be back in a week.
2. The repetition applies the idea of legal ownership or control to both the street and the river, which suggests that the speaker sees control everywhere in the city.
3. The speaker is the voice; the addressee is who the voice is speaking to.
