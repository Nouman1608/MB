## Understanding

- I can name six jobs that evidence does: illustrate, clarify, exemplify, associate, amplify and qualify. *(Guide: "What evidence is for"; Practice Question 1)*
- I can explain that evidence is effective when commentary explains how it relates to the claim. *(Guide: "Relevant, effective and sufficient"; Practice Question 6)*
- I can explain that evidence is sufficient when its quantity and quality give apt support for the line of reasoning. *(Guide: "Relevant, effective and sufficient"; Practice Question 2)*
- I can explain that developing an interpretation is a recursive process. *(Guide: "A recursive process"; Practice Question 9)*

## Skills

- I can select the most relevant piece of evidence for a claim. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Question 3)*
- I can integrate a short quotation into my own sentence and follow it with commentary. *(Guide: "Integrating quotations"; Practice Questions 6 and 7)*
- I can use coordination to show balance and subordination to show imbalance between ideas. *(Guide: "Composing with control"; Practice Questions 4 and 8)*
- I can use a colon, a semicolon and a dash to show relationships within a sentence. *(Guide: Punctuation table; Practice Questions 5 and 8)*

## Reasoning

- I can say what a piece of evidence does for a claim and not only what it says. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Question 7)*
- I can revise a claim when the evidence does not support it. *(Guide: "A recursive process"; Practice Question 9)*
- I can choose precise verbs to communicate an interpretation clearly. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Question 8)*
