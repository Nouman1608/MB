Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/4-7-evidence-and-composition-prose-analysis-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/4-7-evidence-and-composition-prose-analysis-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what makes evidence relevant and what makes it sufficient. *(Guide: "Evidence: relevant, sufficient and strategic"; Practice questions 1 and 2)*
- I can name six purposes of evidence: illustrate, exemplify, clarify, associate, amplify, qualify. *(Guide: the purposes table; Practice question 3)*
- I can explain the three levels of coherence: sentence, paragraph, whole essay. *(Guide: "Composition"; Practice question 4)*
- I can explain why developing an interpretation is recursive. *(Guide: "Evidence: relevant, sufficient and strategic")*

## Skills

- I can quote the shortest accurate phrase that proves a point. *(Guide: "Choosing and trimming quotations"; Practice question 6)*
- I can build a quotation into my own sentence. *(Guide: "Choosing and trimming quotations"; Practice question 7)*
- I can use transitions to show contrast, cause or addition. *(Guide: "Composition"; Practice question 4)*
- I can fix unclear pronoun references and unlinked sentences. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 7)*

## Reasoning

- I can test a set of quotations for relevance and cut what does not serve the claim. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 1)*
- I can judge whether the evidence for a claim is sufficient. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 2)*
- I can revise a disjointed paragraph so that it is coherent and economical. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 7)*
- I can write a complete essay with a thesis, a line of reasoning, accurate evidence and commentary. *(Practice question 8)*
