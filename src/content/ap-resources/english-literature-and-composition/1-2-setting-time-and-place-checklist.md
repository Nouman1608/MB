Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/1-2-setting-time-and-place-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/1-2-setting-time-and-place-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define setting as time and place, and say what social and mood effects setting details can have. *(Guide: "Setting is where and when, and what that carries")*
- I can explain how a detail of position or condition can signal social status. *(Guide: "Reading place"; Practice question 3)*
- I can explain why the narrator's word choice may colour how a setting is presented. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 4)*
- I can explain why weather does not always mirror a character's feelings. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*

## Skills

- I can pick out the details that fix a period or season and avoid dating more precisely than the text allows. *(Guide: "Reading time"; Practice question 1)*
- I can identify sensory details of sound, smell and temperature and say what mood they create. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice questions 5 and 7)*
- I can collect setting details, group them by what they convey and state the value or mood. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 2)*
- I can write a short paragraph opening that links two setting details to a value. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 8)*

## Reasoning

- I can show how a setting limits or shapes what a character can do. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 4; Practice questions 4 and 8)*
- I can reject an answer that overgeneralises from one detail to a whole society. *(Practice questions 4 and 6)*
- I can say what difference a setting detail makes to meaning, not just what it describes. *(Guide: "Checking your inference")*
