## Recap

- Writers use evidence **strategically**: to **illustrate**, **clarify**, **exemplify**, **associate**, **amplify** or **qualify** a point.
- Evidence is **effective** when commentary explains its relationship to the claim, and **sufficient** when quantity and quality support the line of reasoning.
- Developing an interpretation is **recursive**: evidence can lead to a line of reasoning, or a line of reasoning to evidence. If the evidence does not fit, revise the claim.
- **Composition** matters: sentence structure, word choice and punctuation make the reasoning clear.

## Jobs of evidence

| Job | In plain words |
|---|---|
| Illustrate | Gives a concrete instance |
| Clarify | Makes an abstract claim visible |
| Exemplify | Offers a typical case |
| Associate | Links two details so each lights the other |
| Amplify | Strengthens or extends the claim |
| Qualify | Complicates or limits the claim |

## Evidence test

1. **Relevant?** Does it bear directly on the claim?
2. **Effective?** Is there commentary linking it to the claim?
3. **Sufficient?** Do quantity and quality cover every part of the claim?

## Sentence control

| Tool | Effect | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Coordination | Balance between ideas | "She smiles, and she weeps." |
| Subordination | One idea gains weight over another | "Although she smiles, she weeps." |
| Semicolon | Joins two close independent clauses | "She says it is nothing; her hands say otherwise." |
| Colon | Introduces an explanation or example | "Her distress is clear: she reads the letter twice." |
| Dash | A sharp pause or emphasis | "The flame cannot change the words—and she knows it." |

## Assumptions

- Quote only the words that carry your point, then comment.
- One claim may need more than one piece of evidence, from different places in the text.
- Precision beats volume: choose the exact verb, noun and quotation.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Dropping quotations into the text without a lead-in or commentary.
- Using only one detail for a broad claim.
- Ignoring evidence that complicates your claim.
- Joining every idea with "and", so nothing is more important than anything else.
- Using vague verbs such as "shows" and "uses" throughout.

## Quick self-check

**1.** A student claims that a character is "generous", with one quotation of a single act. What might be insufficient?

**2.** Rewrite using subordination so that the second idea is emphasised: "She was polite, and she was furious."

**3.** Which job is evidence doing here: "She locked the gate, yet left the key in the lock"?

**4.** Punctuate with a colon: "The narrator gives one reason for her silence she is afraid."

**Answers.**

1. One act may not be enough for a general claim. Add a second example from a different point in the text, and explain how each supports "generous".
2. "Although she was polite, she was furious."
3. Qualifies (it complicates the idea of locking), and possibly associates two details.
4. "The narrator gives one reason for her silence: she is afraid."
