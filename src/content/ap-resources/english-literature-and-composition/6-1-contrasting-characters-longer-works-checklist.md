Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/6-1-contrasting-characters-longer-works-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/6-1-contrasting-characters-longer-works-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define a foil and explain why it is not the same as a villain or an antagonist. *(Guide: "Why writers set characters against each other"; Practice Questions 2 and 4)*
- I can explain why foils work best when the characters share a situation. *(Guide: "The contrast frame"; Practice Question 6)*
- I can explain that characters have different perspectives that reveal different information and support different interpretations. *(Guide: "Perspective: why characters know different things"; Practice Question 3)*
- I can say why a character's description of another person is evidence about the speaker as well as the subject. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Questions 3 and 8)*

## Skills

- I can use the four-step contrast frame (shared ground, point of difference, what it shows, function). *(Guide: "The contrast frame"; Practice Question 9)*
- I can pick out specific details of diction, action and attention that distinguish two characters. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Questions 1 and 7)*
- I can explain what a contrast reveals about each character's values, not only their traits. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Question 7)*
- I can write an analytic sentence that links a contrast to the work's larger conflict or question. *(Guide: both model sentences; Practice Questions 4 and 9)*

## Reasoning

- I can tell a strong claim about function from a mere statement of difference. *(Practice Questions 4 and 5)*
- I can resist claiming that the writer favours one character without textual support, and I can recognise nuance in both. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Question 2)*
- I can use a public-domain example, such as Hamlet with Laertes and Fortinbras, to explain how a foil works over a whole play. *(Guide: "Public-domain examples to learn from"; Practice Question 9)*
