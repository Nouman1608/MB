## Understanding

- I can define pacing as the manipulation of time in a text. *(Guide: "Time is a material"; Practice Question 1)*
- I can name the factors that contribute to pace: arrangement of details, frequency of events, narrative structures, syntax, tempo, and shifts in tense or chronology. *(Guide: "Time is a material" table; Practice Questions 2 and 5)*
- I can explain expansion and compression of time and give an effect of each. *(Guide: pace figure; Practice Questions 1, 2 and 6)*
- I can explain that the order in which information is revealed can evoke an emotional reaction. *(Guide: "Order of information and emotional response"; Practice Question 8)*

## Skills

- I can use the four-step method for the order of information. *(Guide: "Order of information and emotional response"; Practice Questions 7 and 8)*
- I can identify how syntax speeds or slows a passage and quote the evidence. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Questions 5 and 7)*
- I can explain how a late reveal changes the significance of earlier details. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Questions 3 and 4)*
- I can write a paragraph on pacing that names the technique, the evidence and the effect. *(Guide: both model sentences; Practice Question 7)*

## Reasoning

- I can say why a technique has the effect it does, and not only name it. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Questions 7 and 9)*
- I can compare two orderings of the same events and explain the different effects. *(Practice Question 8)*
- I can use a public-domain story, such as Bierce's An Occurrence at Owl Creek Bridge, to show how pace and order shape meaning. *(Guide: "Public-domain examples to learn from"; Practice Questions 3 and 9)*
