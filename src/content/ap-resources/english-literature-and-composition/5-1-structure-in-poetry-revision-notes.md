## Recap

- **Structure** is the arrangement of a text's parts, their relationship to one another, and the sequence in which information is revealed.
- **Closed forms** follow predictable patterns of lines, stanzas, meter and rhyme, which develop relationships among ideas.
- **Open forms** do not follow predictable patterns but still use structure: repetition, parallel phrasing, stanza breaks, changes in line length.
- Structures **combine** to emphasise certain ideas and concepts.
- You do not need to label rhyme schemes, metrical patterns or forms. Explain what the arrangement does.
- Use a four-step method: pattern, shift, relationship, effect.

## Key relationships

| Feature | What to ask | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Repetition or parallel | What is compared? | three "In me thou seest" images |
| Build | Does each unit add or narrow? | year, day, fire |
| Shift (volta) | Where does the thought turn? | couplet turns to the listener's love |
| Stanza break | What changes across it? | spider "it" to soul "you" |
| Interruption | What is stressed by the break? | open ending "somewhere" |
| Resolution | What does a completed pattern suggest? | rhymed couplet as settled judgement |

| Form | Pattern | How it develops ideas |
|---|---|---|
| Closed | Predictable lines, stanzas, rhyme | Each unit compared with the last; interruption stands out |
| Open | Unpredictable; repetition and breaks | Ideas related by parallel, shift and build |

## Assumptions

- You describe what is **on the page**, then say what it does.
- You treat a pattern **and its break** as meaningful.
- You do not rely on naming a form to earn credit.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Naming a rhyme scheme and stopping.
- Calling free verse structureless.
- Describing a stanza break as only a pause.
- Summarising what happens without linking to arrangement.
- Ignoring the pattern before a turn.

## Quick self-check

1. A poem's three stanzas each open with "I remember". What relationship does this set up?
2. In Sonnet 73, what do the three images share, and how do they differ in scale?
3. How does a stanza break between "it" and "you" act in Whitman's spider poem?
4. Why is "The poem has a rhyme scheme" a weak comment?

**Answers.** (1) Parallel: the stanzas are invited to be compared, and any change in the third gains emphasis. (2) They share the idea of decline (autumn, twilight, fire) and shrink from a year to a day to the last moment of a fire. (3) It marks the turn from observation of the spider to an address to the soul, linking them by parallel. (4) It names a feature but does not say what the pattern does to the relationship between ideas.

## Sentence frames to adapt

- "The repeated [phrase or pattern] sets [part A] beside [part B], so the reader compares [what]."
- "The shift at [word / stanza break] turns the poem from [x] to [y], which emphasises [idea]."
- "By [breaking / completing] the pattern at [point], the poem puts weight on [word or idea]."
- "The [open / closed] form [uses repetition / uses a predictable pattern] to relate [idea] to [idea]."

Each frame names a feature and then says what it does. A feature with no effect is a label, not analysis.
