Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/1-4-narrator-point-of-view-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/1-4-narrator-point-of-view-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain that a narrator or speaker is not necessarily the author. *(Guide: "Who is telling this, and from where?"; Practice question 7)*
- I can explain the difference between perspective and point of view. *(Guide: "Who is telling this, and from where?")*
- I can describe what first-person and third-person narrators can and cannot provide. *(Guide: "First person and third person"; Practice question 6)*
- I can explain how a narrator's perspective controls details and emphases. *(Guide: "Perspective controls emphasis")*

## Skills

- I can identify the narrator and point of view of a passage from its pronouns and how close it stays to one mind. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice questions 1 and 4)*
- I can tell what a narrator knows that a character does not. *(Practice questions 1 and 2)*
- I can read words that carry the narrator's or character's judgement. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice questions 3 and 5)*
- I can write a short claim about a narrator's perspective with two details. *(Practice question 8)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why a gap between a narrator's account and the details invites the reader to infer more. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 4)*
- I can compare what two narrators can provide. *(Practice question 9)*
- I can replace "it is first person" with a sentence about what first-person narration does in a passage. *(Guide: "Strong and weak statements about narrators")*
