Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/3-8-selecting-evidence-and-composition-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/3-8-selecting-evidence-and-composition-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what makes evidence relevant and what makes it sufficient. *(Guide: "Choosing evidence is part of the argument"; Practice questions 1 and 2)*
- I can name six purposes for evidence: illustrate, clarify, exemplify, associate, amplify, qualify. *(Guide: "Six purposes for evidence"; Practice question 3)*
- I can explain why evidence that qualifies a claim can strengthen an argument. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 4)*
- I can explain why grammar and mechanics matter to interpretation. *(Guide: "Composition: control of the sentence")*

## Skills

- I can select two or three details of different kinds for a claim. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Steps 1 to 4; Practice question 7)*
- I can assign a purpose to each piece of evidence. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 3; Practice questions 3 and 7)*
- I can quote exactly and fit the quotation to my sentence. *(Guide: "Quoting accurately and smoothly"; Practice question 5)*
- I can find and fix a comma splice, an agreement error and a tense shift. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice questions 6 and 8)*

## Reasoning

- I can reject evidence that is striking but not relevant. *(Practice question 2)*
- I can explain how interpretation can start from evidence or from a line of reasoning and move between them. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*
- I can edit for clarity without changing my ideas. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 4)*
