## Recap

**Conflict** is tension between competing values: **internal** (within a character) or **external** (between a character and outside forces). A text may have several conflicts that **intersect**, and an additional conflict can **heighten** a primary one. **Inconsistencies** (words against actions, clashing accounts) can create contrasts that represent conflicts of values or perspectives.

## Key relationships

| Idea | What it means | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Internal conflict | Competing values within a character | Soliloquy, hesitation, unsent letter |
| External conflict | A character against outside forces | Argument, deadline, rule, society |
| Intersecting conflicts | Two conflicts press on one another | A pressure that makes a doubt worse |
| Competing values | What each side holds important | "Loyalty against ambition" |
| Inconsistency | Words against action, or clashing views | "Don't mind me" and refused dinners |
| Heightening | An added conflict raises the stakes | A deadline on a dilemma |

## What you may assume

- Conflict does not need to involve open fighting.
- Two characters may use the same word to mean different values.
- A conflict may be left open at the end.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Naming the people in conflict without naming the values.
- Treating all conflict as external.
- Ignoring how conflicts intersect.
- Overlooking contradictions as evidence.
- Saying who is right instead of what each side values.

## Key terms

- **Internal conflict:** competing values within a character.
- **External conflict:** a character against outside forces.
- **Intersecting conflicts:** conflicts that press on one another.
- **Competing values:** things held important that cannot all be satisfied.

## Sentence frames

- "The conflict is between [value] and [value]."
- "[Character]'s [action or contradiction] shows an internal conflict between …."
- "The pressure from … heightens …."
- "[Two characters] use the word '…' to mean different things: for one it means …, for the other …."

## Mini-example

Words: "Don't mind me." Behaviour: a week of refused dinners. Values in tension: not burdening her daughter against needing care. Effect: it heightens the daughter's internal conflict.

## How questions ask about this

- *"The conflict in this passage is best described as …"*: name the values in tension and the kind of conflict.
- *"Which detail shows the character's internal conflict?"*: look for hesitation, action in place of speech or contradiction.
- *"The exchange heightens the conflict by …"*: say how a second conflict presses on the first.
- Constructed response: identify two conflicts, name the values and explain how they intersect.

## Quick self-check

1. What is internal conflict?
2. What two values are in tension in Macbeth's "We will proceed no further"?
3. How does an inconsistency such as "Don't mind me" while refusing dinner show conflict?

**Answers.**

1. Tension between competing values within a character.
2. Loyalty and good name against ambition.
3. The contradiction between words and behaviour shows two values pulling against each other in the speaker.
