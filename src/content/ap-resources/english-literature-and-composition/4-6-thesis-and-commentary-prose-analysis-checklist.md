Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/4-6-thesis-and-commentary-prose-analysis-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/4-6-thesis-and-commentary-prose-analysis-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can say what a thesis statement is and what it requires to be defended. *(Guide: "What a thesis is"; Practice question 1)*
- I can say what a line of reasoning is and how it differs from a list of points. *(Guide: "The line of reasoning"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain what commentary does and why it is how a line of reasoning is communicated. *(Guide: "Commentary"; Practice question 3)*
- I can explain why interpretation is recursive. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 5)*

## Skills

- I can tell a summary, an obvious statement and a defensible thesis apart. *(Guide: "What a thesis is"; Practice questions 1 and 4)*
- I can write a thesis that answers a prompt with a reason. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 6)*
- I can plan a three-step line of reasoning in which each claim builds. *(Guide: "The line of reasoning"; Practice question 6)*
- I can rewrite weak commentary so that it explains, connects and links. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 7)*

## Reasoning

- I can find a tension in a passage and turn it into a stronger claim. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 6)*
- I can say why a thesis need not list devices or evidence. *(Guide: "What a thesis is"; Practice question 4)*
- I can assess a thesis and paragraph with the self-assessment table and name one change. *(Guide: "A self-assessment table"; Practice question 8)*
- I can write a body paragraph that opens with a claim, quotes briefly and develops commentary. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 7)*
