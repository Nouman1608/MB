Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow the link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/7-1-character-change-complex-relationships-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/7-1-character-change-complex-relationships-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain that a character's change often emerges from a conflict of values. *(Guide: "Why change matters in a short story"; Practice Question 9)*
- I can explain how a change in circumstances can lead to a change in a character. *(Guide: "Why change matters in a short story"; Practice Question 1)*
- I can explain what an epiphany is, how it relates to the central conflict and how it can affect the plot. *(Guide: "Epiphany"; Practice Questions 2 and 5)*
- I can explain how a group or force can function as a character and what inclusion or exclusion reveals. *(Guide: "Relationships: individuals, groups and forces"; Practice Questions 4 and 8)*

## Skills

- I can use the five-step frame (before, pressure, turn, after, function) to track a character's change. *(Guide: "Tracking change"; Practice Questions 7 and 9)*
- I can tell gradual change from sudden change and name the textual signals of each. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Questions 1, 2 and 7)*
- I can analyse a group's behaviour as I would a character's. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Questions 4 and 8)*
- I can explain the function of a character who remains unchanged. *(Guide: "Why change matters in a short story"; Practice Question 3)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how an ironic ending can qualify the meaning of a change. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Question 6)*
- I can avoid assuming that change is always improvement. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Question 6)*
- I can use a public-domain story, such as Chopin's The Story of an Hour, to illustrate an epiphany. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Question 5)*
