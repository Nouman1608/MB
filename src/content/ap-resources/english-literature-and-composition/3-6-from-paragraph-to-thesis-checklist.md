Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/3-6-from-paragraph-to-thesis-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/3-6-from-paragraph-to-thesis-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define a thesis statement as a defensible interpretation that requires evidence and a line of reasoning. *(Guide: "What a thesis is")*
- I can explain why a topic, a fact, a summary and a list of devices are not theses. *(Guide: "The thesis ladder"; Practice questions 1 to 3)*
- I can explain what it means to preview a line of reasoning. *(Guide: "Previewing a line of reasoning"; Practice question 5)*
- I can explain why a thesis does not have to list points, elements or evidence. *(Guide: "Previewing a line of reasoning")*

## Skills

- I can pick the best thesis from a set of options for a given prompt. *(Practice question 1)*
- I can turn an observation into a claim and then into a thesis by asking "So what?". *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 8)*
- I can revise a weak thesis into a defensible one. *(Guide: "Strong and weak theses"; Practice questions 4 and 7)*
- I can write a one-sentence thesis that answers a prompt. *(Practice question 6)*

## Reasoning

- I can test a draft thesis against the prompt, defensibility and specificity. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 5)*
- I can explain how a thesis can show movement without listing. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 6)*
- I can explain that interpretation is recursive and a thesis may change as I plan. *(Guide: "A drafting routine")*
