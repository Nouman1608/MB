## Recap

**Commentary** explains the relationships among **evidence**, the **line of reasoning** and the **thesis**. A **line of reasoning** is the logical sequence of claims that together defend the thesis, and it is communicated through commentary. Commentary should say **what** the evidence shows, **how** the writer's choice works and **why** it matters to the claim and thesis.

## Key relationships

| Idea | What it is | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence | An exact detail or quotation | Is it short, accurate and relevant? |
| Paraphrase | Restating the evidence | Does it add interpretation? (If not, it is not commentary.) |
| Commentary | Explains what, how and why | Does it name a choice and link to the claim? |
| Line of reasoning | Logical sequence of claims | Does each claim build on the last? |
| Thesis | The overall interpretation | Do all claims support it? |
| Transition | A phrase that shows a link | Does it say how one claim leads to the next? |

## What you may assume

- Commentary usually runs longer than the quotation it discusses.
- A line of reasoning may qualify or complicate an earlier claim.
- You may start from evidence and form a line of reasoning, or start from a line of reasoning and find evidence.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Leaving a quotation without commentary.
- Using paraphrase as commentary.
- Repeating the same claim in different words.
- Ordering claims at random.
- Forgetting to link a claim back to the thesis.

## Key terms

- **Commentary:** explanation of how evidence supports a claim and how the claim supports the thesis.
- **Paraphrase:** restating the evidence, which is not commentary.
- **Line of reasoning:** claims in a logical sequence.
- **Transition:** a phrase that shows how one claim builds on another.

## Sentence frames

- "The [word or image] works by …, so that …."
- "This matters because …, which supports the claim that …."
- "More than …, this shows …, which leads to …."

## Mini-example

Evidence: "Creeps in this petty pace". What: time is slow and trivial. How: the verb "creeps" and the adjective "petty". Why: his actions have emptied his world of meaning.

## How questions ask about this

- *"Which of the following is commentary?"*: choose the sentence that explains how or why, not a paraphrase or quotation.
- *"What is the main weakness of this commentary?"*: it usually lacks the how or the why.
- *"Which order of claims forms the most logical line of reasoning?"*: choose the order in which each claim builds on the last.
- Constructed response: quote briefly, then write at least a how and a why.

## Quick self-check

1. What are the three levels of commentary after paraphrase?
2. Why is "This shows he feels free" thin commentary?
3. What makes claims a line of reasoning and not a list?

**Answers.**

1. What it shows; how it works (the writer's choice); why it matters (link to claim and thesis).
2. It repeats the quotation without naming a choice or linking to the claim.
3. Each claim builds on the last, they do not repeat, and commentary shows the connections.
