Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/1-1-character-perspective-motive-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/1-1-character-perspective-motive-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can name description, dialogue and behaviour as the three main ways a text reveals a character. *(Guide: "Characters are built from details")*
- I can explain the difference between a character's perspective and their motive. *(Guide: "Characters are built from details"; Practice question 9)*
- I can explain why a description from another character reveals that character's perspective as well as the person described. *(Guide: "Whose view is this description?"; Practice question 6)*
- I can explain why a character's view is not the same as the author's. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*

## Skills

- I can list specific details from a passage and group them by what they suggest. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can use the detail, inference, perspective chain to build a claim. *(Guide: "The detail, inference, perspective chain"; Practice questions 1 and 2)*
- I can read an action or habit, such as counting or avoiding a clock, as evidence of an attitude. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 3)*
- I can tell which character, or the narrator, is the source of a description. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 6)*
- I can write a two- or three-sentence claim about a character with two supporting details. *(Practice question 8)*

## Reasoning

- I can choose the claim best supported by all the details in a passage and reject claims that go beyond it. *(Practice question 7)*
- I can explain how a character's perspective changes after a moment of recognition. *(Practice questions 4 and 9)*
- I can say when a text allows two readings of a motive and which one the details favour. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Step 4)*
