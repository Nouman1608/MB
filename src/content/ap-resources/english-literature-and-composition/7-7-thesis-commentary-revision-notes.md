## Recap

- **Thesis**: a defensible interpretation that previews a direction. **Line of reasoning**: claims in a logical sequence. **Commentary**: explains the relationships among evidence, claims and thesis.
- **More sophisticated** arguments may:
  1. explain the **significance or relevance** of an interpretation within a **broader context**;
  2. discuss **alternative interpretations**;
  3. use **relevant analogies**.
- **Revise** the thesis as evidence develops.

## Why sophistication is not decoration

Each move must do work in the argument. A broader context should be linked to a phrase from the text. An alternative interpretation should be answered with a detail that favours your reading. An analogy should mirror the structure of the situation it clarifies. If a move can be deleted without changing the argument, it is probably decorative.

## Moves and starters

| Move | Starter | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Broader context | "This matters because ...", "The story thereby extends beyond ..." | Vague grand claims |
| Alternative interpretation | "A reader might see ...; however, ..." | Listing readings without choosing |
| Analogy | "Like a ..., ..." | A parallel that distracts |

## Commentary chain (with sophistication)

1. Interpret.
2. Explain how (words, choices).
3. Consider an alternative (if relevant) and answer it with evidence.
4. Connect to the claim.
5. Place it in a broader context (if the evidence supports it).
6. Connect to the thesis.

## Weak to strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "Mrs Mallard is sad and then happy." | "By moving Mrs Mallard from conventional grief to a private room where spring presses in, Chopin presents her joy as natural and long suppressed." |
| "Some readers disagree." | "A reader might take her joy as callous; but the narrator's insistence on her husband's tenderness rules out that reading." |
| "This is relevant to society." | "The story's quarrel is with an institution in which a 'private will' is imposed in the name of love." |

## Planning prompt

Before writing, jot four lines: **claim** (one sentence), **three supporting claims** in order, **one piece of evidence per claim** and **one sophistication move** with the evidence that supports it. If any line is empty, the argument is not ready.

## Assumptions

- Sophistication comes from integration, not length.
- An alternative interpretation is credible only if you answer it with evidence.
- A broader context must be linked to the text, not asserted.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Treating sophistication as vocabulary.
- Mentioning an alternative and then dropping it.
- Making broad claims with no textual link.
- Adding an analogy that does not fit.
- Failing to revise a thesis that the evidence has outgrown.

## Quick self-check

**1.** What are the three sophistication moves?

**2.** True or false: acknowledging an alternative reading always weakens a thesis.

**3.** Why might an analogy hurt an essay?

**4.** What should you do if your evidence contradicts your thesis?

**Answers.**

1. Broader context or significance, alternative interpretations and relevant analogies.
2. False. If you answer it with evidence, it shows control and strengthens the thesis.
3. If it does not fit or distracts, it takes attention away from the evidence.
4. Revise the thesis so that it accounts for the evidence; interpretation is recursive.
