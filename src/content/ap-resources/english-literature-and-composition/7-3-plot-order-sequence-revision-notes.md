## Recap

- **Pacing** is the **manipulation of time** in a text.
- **Expansion** gives a short stretch of story time a long stretch of text (slow, tense, vivid). **Compression** gives a long stretch of story time a short stretch of text (fast, summarising).
- The **order in which information is revealed** can evoke an emotional reaction and change how earlier details are read.

## Factors that set pace

| Factor | Slows | Speeds |
|---|---|---|
| Arrangement of details | Accumulating sensory detail | Bare statement |
| Frequency of events | Few events | Many events |
| Structure | Scene with dwelling; reflection; flashback | Summary; time skip |
| Syntax | Long, clause-heavy sentences | Short, plain sentences |
| Tempo | A fast event narrated slowly | A slow event summarised |
| Tense and chronology | Present-tense freeze; return to a moment | Leap forward |

## Four-step method

1. **What is revealed, and when?**
2. **What does the reader feel at each point?**
3. **How does a later detail change an earlier one?**
4. **What does that do to the meaning of the story?**

## Examples (public domain)

- **An Occurrence at Owl Creek Bridge** (Ambrose Bierce, 1890): a fraction of a second is stretched across a long escape; the last sentence, "Peyton Farquhar was dead; his body, with a broken neck, swung gently from side to side beneath the timbers of the Owl Creek bridge", reorders everything before it.
- **The Tell-Tale Heart** (Edgar Allan Poe, 1843): slow, meticulous detail holds the reader in the narrator's calm before the collapse.

## Assumptions

- Pace depends on context; judge sentence length by what surrounds it.
- A late reveal must be justified by how it reshapes earlier details.
- Explain effects with evidence: the word, the clause, the order.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Writing "this creates suspense" without saying what is withheld.
- Equating short sentences with speed in every case.
- Ignoring the effect of compression or ellipsis.
- Treating a twist as separate from the earlier narrative.
- Naming a technique without effect.

## Quick self-check

**1.** "Years went by. Mara grew old." What does this do to pace?

**2.** A story spends a full page on the three seconds before a collision. What is this called and why do writers use it?

**3.** What does a late reveal do to the details that came before it?

**4.** Give two signs that a sentence slows the narrative.

**Answers.**

1. It compresses a long span of time into a few words, hurrying the story and implying that little of note changed.
2. Expansion; it slows time to heighten tension, vividness or the reader's sense of a crisis.
3. It can change their significance, so that ordinary details become heavy with meaning.
4. For example, accumulating details and long clause-heavy syntax, or an interruption by reflection or memory.
