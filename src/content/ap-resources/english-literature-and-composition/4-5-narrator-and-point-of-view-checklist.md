Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/4-5-narrator-and-point-of-view-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/4-5-narrator-and-point-of-view-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between narrator, point of view and author. *(Guide: "Who is telling, and from where"; Practice question 1)*
- I can explain what narrative distance means and name its four kinds. *(Guide: "Who is telling, and from where"; Practice question 2)*
- I can say what stream of consciousness is and what it lets a reader sense. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 4)*
- I can explain how a perspective controls the details a reader receives. *(Guide: "Who is telling, and from where"; Practice question 5)*

## Skills

- I can identify first-person and third-person narration from pronouns and position. *(Practice questions 1 and 5)*
- I can compare the distance of two versions of the same event. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice questions 2 and 7)*
- I can find diction that conveys an attitude and explain how. *(Guide: "How a perspective shows itself"; Practice questions 6 and 8)*
- I can describe what the shape of a sentence (syntax) suggests. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 3)*

## Reasoning

- I can name a tone precisely and support it with quoted words. *(Guide: "How a perspective shows itself"; Practice question 6)*
- I can explain how a narrator's background and position shape the tone. *(Guide: "How a perspective shows itself"; Practice question 8)*
- I can say what a gap between a narrator's claim and the way the narrator speaks lets a reader see. *(Guide: Worked example 1, step 4; Practice question 8)*
- I can write a paragraph on a narrator's perspective with accurate quotations and commentary. *(Practice question 8)*
