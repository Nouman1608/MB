Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/5-4-metaphor-and-personification-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/5-4-metaphor-and-personification-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain that a metaphor focuses on particular traits, not the whole objects compared. *(Guide: "What a metaphor does"; Practice questions 1 and 5)*
- I can explain how comparisons carry literal and figurative meaning and a perspective. *(Guide: "What a metaphor does"; Practice question 7)*
- I can define extended metaphor and say why it must be read in context. *(Guide: "What a metaphor does"; Practice question 8)*
- I can define personification and say what it does to a nonhuman subject. *(Guide: "What personification does"; Practice question 3)*

## Skills

- I can work through sides, traits, left behind and effect. *(Guide: "A method"; Practice question 7)*
- I can list the traits a poem's details transfer. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice questions 1 and 5)*
- I can say which traits are not transferred and why that matters. *(Guide: Worked example 1, step 3; Practice question 2)*
- I can identify personification and name the human trait it gives. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice questions 3, 4 and 6)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how an extended metaphor builds a larger meaning. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 8)*
- I can say how changing one word of a comparison changes the traits transferred. *(Guide: "Practising the trait list on a new comparison"; Practice question 5)*
- I can explain how personification characterises a thing and shapes the reader's response. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 6)*
- I can write a paragraph on metaphor or personification with accurate quotations and commentary. *(Practice question 8)*
