## Recap

- **Story** is chronological; **plot** is the order of presentation. Differences between them are structural choices.
- A sequence has **function**: it shapes what the reader knows and feels at each moment.
- **Contrasts** within a text (of scene, tone, setting, event or outcome) often express contradictions and make the text more complex.

## Techniques and effects

| Technique | Definition | Effect to name |
|---|---|---|
| Flashback | A shift to an earlier time | Explains a present reaction; reveals hidden history |
| Foreshadowing | A hint of a later event | Anticipation; unease; irony |
| In medias res | Opening in the middle of action | Immediate engagement; questions raised |
| Stream of consciousness | A continuous flow of a character's thoughts | Intimacy; blurred past and present |
| Contrast between scenes | Opposed tones, events or settings | Nuance, ambiguity, contradiction |

## A four-step method for any sequence

1. **Identify** the order the text uses (and compare it with chronology if you can).
2. **Name** the technique, with the detail that shows it (a time marker, a hint, an abrupt opening).
3. **Say** what information is revealed or withheld, and when.
4. **Explain** the effect on the reader or on the interpretation of other parts of the work: anticipation, suspense, tension or irony.

## Examples (public domain)

- **Wuthering Heights** (Emily Brontë, 1847): opens in 1801 with Lockwood, then Nelly Dean tells the earlier history. The delayed explanation makes the reader work out the past with an outsider.
- **Frankenstein** (Mary Shelley, 1818): letters, within them Victor's tale, within that the creature's account. Nested order puts the creature's voice at the centre.
- **Macbeth** (Shakespeare, about 1606): "Fair is foul, and foul is fair" opens the play and foreshadows its reversals.

## Assumptions

- Check the whole work for patterns. One flashback or hint is a data point; a pattern is an argument.
- Say how a technique works on this passage. "Flashbacks add background" is true of nearly any story and earns little.
- A contrast must be placed, with its sequence. The same two scenes reversed would mean something different.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Confusing story with plot.
- Naming a technique without its effect.
- Writing "builds suspense" without saying what is withheld.
- Treating foreshadowing as a spoiler instead of a source of expectation.
- Leaving a contrast as "opposites" without stating the nuance it creates.

## Quick self-check

**1.** A novel begins with a woman burying a letter, then moves to her girlhood. Name the technique and one effect.

**2.** An early scene mentions that the river is "unusually quiet this spring"; later the village floods. Name the technique and the feeling it builds.

**3.** Chapter 5 is a wedding feast; Chapter 6 is a funeral. What question should you ask about the contrast?

**4.** True or false: a story told in strict chronological order has no structure to analyse.

**Answers.**

1. In medias res (opening in the middle of the action) followed by a flashback; effect: curiosity about why she buries the letter and what the girlhood explains.
2. Foreshadowing; it creates unease or anticipation, and gives the earlier detail new meaning after the flood.
3. What does the juxtaposition suggest: irony, the fragility of happiness, a threat beneath the feast, a link between the two events?
4. False. Pacing, the order of revelation within scenes, contrasts and parallels still shape meaning.
