Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/3-1-character-in-longer-works-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/3-1-character-in-longer-works-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain how a description creates expectations about a character's behaviour. *(Guide: "Description sets up expectations"; Practice question 2)*
- I can explain how meeting or breaking an expectation affects interpretation. *(Guide: "Description sets up expectations"; Practice question 3)*
- I can explain why a reader's understanding depends on the perspective of the narrator or another character. *(Guide: "Whose perspective shapes the description?"; Practice question 5)*
- I can explain what a comparison reveals about the person making it. *(Guide: "Comparisons and what they reveal")*

## Skills

- I can identify whose view a description gives. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 2; Practice question 1)*
- I can use a soliloquy, dialogue or stage direction as evidence for a character's motive. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice questions 6 and 8)*
- I can infer a motive from an action or inaction. *(Guide: "Motives from actions and inactions"; Practice question 3)*
- I can write a short explanation of how an expectation is created and broken. *(Practice question 9)*

## Reasoning

- I can say what a community's verdict reveals about the community. *(Guide: Worked example 1, Step 4; Practice question 1)*
- I can explain how a character's upbringing or background shapes perspective in dialogue. *(Practice question 7)*
- I can limit a claim about a character to what has been shown so far. *(Guide: "Checking your inference")*
