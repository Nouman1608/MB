Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/6-3-sequence-contrasts-longer-plot-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/6-3-sequence-contrasts-longer-plot-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between story (chronological) and plot (order of presentation). *(Guide: "Story, plot and the gap between them"; Practice Question 8)*
- I can define flashback, foreshadowing, in medias res and stream of consciousness. *(Guide: "Four ways of interrupting chronology"; Practice Questions 1, 2 and 4)*
- I can explain that interrupting chronology affects what the reader knows and when, and so creates anticipation, suspense or tension. *(Guide: "Four ways of interrupting chronology"; Practice Question 3)*
- I can explain how contrasts within a text can represent contradictions and so add nuance, ambiguity or complexity. *(Guide: "Contrasts: where the plot sets two things against each other"; Practice Question 5)*

## Skills

- I can identify a technique from a specific textual detail such as a time marker, an unexplained opening or a hint. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Questions 1, 2 and 4)*
- I can use the four-step method for a sequence: identify, name, say what is revealed or withheld, explain the effect. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Question 7)*
- I can analyse a contrast between two scenes by naming the elements, what differs, what the juxtaposition suggests and how it complicates the work. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Question 5)*
- I can reorder a list of events from telling order to story order and say what the order does. *(Guide: the story-order figure; Practice Question 8)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why a late revelation changes the weight of a later scene. *(Practice Question 3)*
- I can avoid empty phrases such as "it builds suspense" by stating what is withheld and what the reader therefore wants to know. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Questions 7 and 9)*
- I can use a public-domain work, such as Wuthering Heights or Macbeth, to illustrate a structural choice. *(Guide: "Public-domain examples to learn from"; Practice Questions 6 and 9)*
