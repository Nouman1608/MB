Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/5-7-evidence-and-composition-poetry-analysis-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/english-literature-and-composition/5-7-evidence-and-composition-poetry-analysis-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what makes evidence from a poem relevant and what makes it sufficient. *(Guide: "Evidence from a poem"; Practice question 1)*
- I can explain why evidence must be accompanied by commentary that explains the logical relationship. *(Guide: "Evidence from a poem"; Practice question 4)*
- I can name six purposes of evidence: illustrate, clarify, exemplify, associate, amplify, qualify. *(Guide: "Evidence from a poem"; Practice question 6)*
- I can explain the three levels of coherence and the role of transitional elements. *(Guide: "Composition"; Practice question 5)*

## Skills

- I can quote the smallest accurate phrase that proves a point. *(Guide: "Quoting poetry"; Practice question 2)*
- I can integrate a quotation into my own sentence. *(Guide: "Quoting poetry"; Practice questions 2 and 7)*
- I can show line breaks with a slash and keep capitals and punctuation exact. *(Guide: "Quoting poetry"; Practice question 3)*
- I can use topic sentences, transitions, repetition and clear reference. *(Guide: the coherence table; Practice questions 5 and 7)*

## Reasoning

- I can test a set of quotations for relevance and save some for another claim. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 1)*
- I can judge whether the evidence for a claim is sufficient. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice question 6)*
- I can revise a disjointed paragraph so that it flows and links to a thesis. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 7)*
- I can write a complete poetry essay with a thesis, a line of reasoning that follows the poem's movement, accurate evidence and commentary. *(Practice question 8)*
