Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/9-2-ideal-gas-law-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/9-2-ideal-gas-law-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can list the four assumptions of the classical ideal gas model and say what each one means for the atoms. *(Guide: "The ideal gas model"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain when a real gas behaves most like an ideal gas, and which assumptions fail at high pressure or near condensation. *(Guide: "The ideal gas model")*
- I can state PV = nRT = Nk_BT, name every symbol with its SI unit, and explain why R = N_A k_B. *(Guide: "The ideal gas law")*
- I can explain, using moving atoms, why heating a gas at constant volume raises its pressure. *(Guide: "Using the law to predict changes"; Practice Q7b)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can convert °C to K, L to m³ and kPa to Pa before using PV = nRT. *(Guide: "The ideal gas law"; Practice Q3, Q5)*
- I can find n or N for a gas from P, V and T. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can use P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂ to find a new pressure, volume or temperature for a fixed amount of gas. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3, Q5, Q7c)*
- I can predict the factor by which one variable changes when two others change by known factors. *(Guide: "Using the law to predict changes"; Practice Q2)*
- I can plot P against T in °C, draw a best-fit line and extrapolate it to find absolute zero. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can find the amount of gas from the gradient of a P–T or V–T graph. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6c)*

## Reasoning

- I can say which graph of gas data gives a straight line through the origin, and what its gradient means. *(Guide: "Graphs of gas behaviour"; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why absolute zero is found by extrapolation and why different gas samples give the same intercept. *(Guide: "Absolute zero from a graph"; Practice Q6b, Q6d)*
- I can compare the pressures of gas samples using N/V and T, and reject the idea that more atoms always means more pressure. *(Practice Q7)*
