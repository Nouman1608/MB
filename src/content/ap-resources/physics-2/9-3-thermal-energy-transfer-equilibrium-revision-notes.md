## Recap

- Two systems are in **thermal contact** if energy can move between them by thermal processes.
- **Heating** moves energy into a system; **cooling** moves energy out. Both are transfers, not contents.
- The three thermal processes are **conduction**, **convection** and **radiation**.
- Atoms in a hotter system have a larger average kinetic energy. In a collision, energy is most likely, but not certain, to pass from the higher-energy atom to the lower-energy one.
- After very many collisions, the most probable state is equal temperatures.
- **Thermal equilibrium**: no net energy transfer by thermal processes between systems in contact.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| K_avg = (3/2)k_B T | k_B = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K, T in K | link temperature to atomic energy |
| Energy lost by hot system = energy gained by cold system | J; isolated pair of systems | check a transfer |
| T_f = (N₁T₁ + N₂T₂)/(N₁ + N₂) | same monatomic ideal gas, rigid insulated boxes | find a final temperature |

| Process | Carrier | Needs matter? |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | collisions between neighbouring particles | yes |
| Convection | bulk movement of a fluid | yes, a fluid |
| Radiation | electromagnetic waves | no |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The pair of systems is **isolated**: no energy leaks to the surroundings.
- For gas samples: **ideal, monatomic**, in **rigid** boxes (no work done).
- Temperatures are in **kelvin** when you use K_avg = (3/2)k_B T.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **"Cold flows in."** Only energy flows.
2. **Internal energy decides the direction.** Temperature decides it.
3. **"Every collision goes hot to cold."** It is the most likely result, not a certainty.
4. **"Equilibrium means atoms stop."** Exchange continues; the net is zero.
5. **Simple average for T_f.** Weight by the number of atoms.
6. **Convection in a solid.** Solids conduct; only fluids convect.

## Quick self-check

1. A small iron nail at 90 °C is dropped into a large tank of water at 20 °C. Which way does net energy flow? *(Nail to water: the nail is at the higher temperature, even though the water has more internal energy.)*
2. Which thermal process can cross a vacuum? *(Radiation only.)*
3. Equal numbers of atoms of the same gas at 300 K and 500 K reach equilibrium in insulated rigid boxes. Final temperature? *(400 K.)*
4. What is the average kinetic energy of a gas atom at 300 K? *(6.21 × 10⁻²¹ J.)*
