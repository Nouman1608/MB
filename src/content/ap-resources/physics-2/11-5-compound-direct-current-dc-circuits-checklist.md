## Understanding

- I can define series and parallel connections and say which quantity is shared in each (current or potential difference). *(Guide: "From one resistor to many")*
- I can decide whether two elements are in series, in parallel or neither by tracing their connections, not their positions on the page. *(Guide: Figure 3; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why adding a branch in parallel lowers the equivalent resistance. *(Guide: "Equivalent resistance in parallel"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain when the resistance of connecting wires can be ignored and when it cannot. *(Guide: "Wires"; Practice Q7d)*
- I can define emf and model a real battery as an ideal emf in series with an internal resistance. *(Guide: Figure 2)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can derive the series and parallel rules for equivalent resistance from ΔV = IR and conservation of charge and energy. *(Guide: "Equivalent resistance in series" and "in parallel")*
- I can reduce a compound network and find the current in and potential difference across every resistor. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can use ΔV_terminal = ℰ − Ir, including finding ℰ and r from two measurements. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q7)*
- I can sketch terminal voltage against current for a real battery and read ℰ and r from it. *(Practice Q7c)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict how the currents in, and brightness of, bulbs change when a branch is added, removed or short-circuited, and justify each change. *(Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can explain why an extra lamp dims the first one with a real battery but not with an ideal one. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can place ammeters and voltmeters correctly and explain qualitatively how real meters change the readings. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4)*
