Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/10-2-conservation-electric-charge-process-charging-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/10-2-conservation-electric-charge-process-charging-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can state conservation of charge and say what must happen for a system's net charge to change. *(Guide: "Charge is conserved"; Practice Q6c)*
- I can explain why charging a solid means moving electrons, not protons. *(Guide: "Charge is conserved"; Practice Q5c)*
- I can explain charging by rubbing and why the two objects end with equal and opposite charges. *(Guide: "Charging by friction"; Practice Q1)*
- I can describe induced charge separation in a conductor and polarization in an insulator, and say why the net charge does not change. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can describe grounding and why Earth's own charge barely changes. *(Guide: "Grounding"; Practice Q6)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can find the charges on identical conductors after contact by adding with signs, then sharing. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3, Q5b)*
- I can count the electrons moved and state their direction. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q5c, Q7c)*
- I can draw the charge distribution on objects during induction. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q7a)*
- I can combine contact charging with Coulomb's law to compare forces before and after. *(Practice Q5, Q7d)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why a charged object attracts a neutral object, and why only repulsion proves two objects are charged. *(Guide: "Why a charged object attracts a neutral one"; Practice Q2)*
- I can put the steps of charging by induction in order, predict the sign of the result and explain what goes wrong in the wrong order. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can compare what grounding does to a charged conductor and to a charged insulator. *(Practice Q6)*
