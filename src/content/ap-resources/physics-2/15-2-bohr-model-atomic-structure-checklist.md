## Understanding

- I can describe an atom as a small, positive nucleus of protons and neutrons surrounded by electrons. *(Guide: "What is inside an atom")*
- I can explain why the mass of an atom is almost all in its nucleus. *(Guide: "Mass is in the nucleus"; Practice Q6c)*
- I can explain that Z identifies the element, A identifies the isotope, and an ion has a nonzero net charge. *(Guide: "Elements, isotopes and ions"; Practice Q2, Q6d)*
- I can state that the electrons, not the nucleus, mainly decide how an atom interacts with other atoms. *(Practice Q6d)*
- I can describe the Bohr model as a historical, classical-based model that led to discrete energy states, and say what it cannot do. *(Guide: "What the Bohr model can and cannot do")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can read nuclear notation and count the protons, neutrons and electrons in an atom or ion. *(Guide: Figure 1, Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q6a)*
- I can calculate the net charge of an ion in coulombs. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6b)*
- I can set the Coulomb force equal to mv²/r and find the speed of an electron in an orbit. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can find the electron's de Broglie wavelength in an orbit and check how many wavelengths fit the circumference. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5c)*
- I can sketch an electron standing wave around an orbit for a given n. *(Guide: Figure 2)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain, using the standing-wave condition 2πr = nλ, why only certain orbits are allowed. *(Guide: "Why only some orbits"; Practice Q4)*
- I can use functional dependence to compare the radius, speed, force and energy of two orbits. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3, Q7)*
- I can decide whether a proposed orbit is allowed and justify the decision with numbers. *(Practice Q7c)*
