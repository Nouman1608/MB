## Recap

- A charge q moving through a potential difference ΔV changes its electric potential energy by qΔV.
- Batteries turn non-electric energy into electric potential energy of the charges; resistors turn it into thermal energy.
- A point has one value of potential, so going round a closed loop and back to the start gives a total change of zero. That is the **loop rule**, and it is **energy conservation**.
- The rule holds for **every** closed loop, including loops with no battery. Such a loop shows that parallel branches have equal potential differences.
- The direction you go round the loop does not matter, as long as you keep it.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| ΔV = ΔU_E / q | V = J/C | link potential difference to energy per charge |
| ΣΔV = 0 | sum round one closed loop | write a loop equation |
| +ℰ / −ℰ | battery crossed − to + / + to − | sign of a battery term |
| −IR / +IR | resistor crossed with / against the current | sign of a resistor term |
| ΔV_terminal = ℰ − Ir | loop rule applied to the battery alone | terminal voltage of a real battery |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Wires and ammeters are ideal (no potential change across them) unless stated.
- One reference point is chosen as 0 V and kept throughout.
- Resistances are constant, so each resistor term is ±IR.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Wrong resistor sign** when going against the current. It is +IR.
2. **Leaving out the −Ir term** for a real battery.
3. **Thinking position decides the share of voltage.** In one loop, resistance does.
4. **Ignoring loops without a battery.** They are often the shortest route to an unknown.
5. **Graph not closing.** A potential–position graph must end at its starting value.
6. **Saying current is used up.** Potential falls; the current in one loop is the same everywhere.

## Quick self-check

1. A 1.5 V ideal cell drives a current through two resistors in series. The first has 0.90 V across it. What is across the second? *(0.60 V)*
2. You cross a 10 Ω resistor against a current of 0.20 A. What is the term in your loop equation? *(+2.0 V)*
3. 3.0 C of charge passes through a resistor with 4.0 V across it. How much electric potential energy does it lose? *(12 J)*
