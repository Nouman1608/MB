## Recap

- A gas is a huge number of atoms moving randomly. They collide with each other and with the walls, and momentum is conserved in each collision.
- **Pressure** comes from atoms changing momentum when they hit a surface. Many tiny impulses each second add up to a steady force.
- Only the **perpendicular** velocity component reverses in an elastic bounce. The parallel component is unchanged.
- Pressure exists **everywhere** inside a gas, not just at the walls.
- **Temperature** measures the **average kinetic energy** of the atoms. One atom has no temperature.
- The **Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution** shows the spread of atomic speeds. You need its shape, not its formula.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Δp = 2m v cos θ | m in kg, v in m/s, θ from the normal | find the momentum change of one atom in a bounce |
| F⊥ = (collisions per second) × Δp | F in N | turn many impulses into an average force |
| P = F⊥/A | P in Pa = N/m², A in m² | find the pressure on a surface |
| K_avg = (3/2)k_B T = ½m v_rms² | k_B = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K, T in K | link temperature, kinetic energy and speed |
| v_rms = √(3k_B T/m) | m = mass of **one** atom | compare speeds of gases or temperatures |

| Change | Effect on the speed distribution |
|---|---|
| Higher T, same gas | peak moves to higher speed, curve wider, peak lower, area unchanged |
| Lighter atoms, same T | same shifts as a hotter gas; same K_avg |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The gas is **ideal**: atoms are tiny and interact only in collisions.
- Collisions are **elastic** and walls are smooth.
- Temperatures are in **kelvin**.
- "Same direction for every atom" calculations are simplified models of a random gas.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Δp = mv for a bounce.** It is 2mv head-on, because the atom reverses.
2. **Using sin θ for the perpendicular part.** Measure θ from the normal; the perpendicular part is v cos θ.
3. **Forgetting to convert cm² to m².** 1 cm² = 1 × 10⁻⁴ m².
4. **Doubling T doubles v_rms.** It doubles K_avg; v_rms rises by √2.
5. **Heavier atoms have more kinetic energy at the same T.** Same K_avg, lower speed.
6. **Heating makes the curve taller.** The peak gets lower and moves right.
7. **Using °C in (3/2)k_B T.**

## Quick self-check

1. A helium atom (6.65 × 10⁻²⁷ kg) hits a wall head-on at 1200 m/s and rebounds elastically. What is the magnitude of its momentum change? *(1.60 × 10⁻²³ kg·m/s, which is 2mv)*
2. What is the average kinetic energy of an atom in a gas at 400 K? *(8.28 × 10⁻²¹ J)*
3. A gas is heated from 100 K to 900 K. By what factor does v_rms change? *(×3, since √9 = 3)*
