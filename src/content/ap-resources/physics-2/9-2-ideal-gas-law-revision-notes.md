## Recap

- **The ideal gas model** assumes: atomic velocities are random; the atoms' own volume is negligible compared with the gas volume; collisions are elastic; there are no forces on the atoms except during collisions.
- Real gases fit the model best at **low pressure** and **well above their condensation temperature**.
- Graphs of P, V and T test the model and give properties of the gas from gradients and intercepts.
- **Absolute zero** is the temperature at which an ideal gas would have zero pressure. It is found by extrapolating a P–T line, not by measurement.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| PV = nRT | P in Pa, V in m³, n in mol, R = 8.31 J/(mol·K), T in K | find any one variable from the other three |
| PV = Nk_BT | N = number of atoms, k_B = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K | work with numbers of atoms |
| N = nN_A, R = N_A k_B | N_A = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ | convert between moles and atoms |
| P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂ | fixed n; any consistent P and V units; T in K | predict a change without finding n |

| Graph (fixed n) | Shape | Gradient or intercept tells you |
|---|---|---|
| P against V, constant T | curve (isotherm), P ∝ 1/V | further from origin = larger nRT |
| P against 1/V, constant T | straight line through origin | gradient = nRT |
| V against T (K), constant P | straight line through origin | gradient = nR/P |
| P against T (K), constant V | straight line through origin | gradient = nR/V |
| P or V against T (°C) | straight line, not through origin | T-intercept ≈ −273 °C |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The gas is **ideal** and the amount of gas is **fixed** unless the question says otherwise.
- "Rigid" or "sealed rigid" means constant V. "Free piston" usually means constant P.
- Convert °C to K by adding 273; 1 L = 10⁻³ m³; 1 atm ≈ 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Celsius ratios.** Heating from 20 °C to 40 °C does not double P; the factor is 313/293.
2. **Wrong units in PV = nRT.** Use Pa and m³ there; L and kPa only work in the ratio form.
3. **n with k_B, or N with R.** Pair n with R and N with k_B.
4. **Forgetting a second change.** If T and V both change, apply both factors.
5. **Drawing a line through the first and last points only.** Use a best-fit line before extrapolating.
6. **Saying ideal gas atoms never collide.** They collide elastically; there are no forces between collisions.

## Quick self-check

1. How many moles of ideal gas fill 2.0 L at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 300 K? *(0.080 mol)*
2. A fixed amount of gas has its volume halved and its absolute temperature doubled. By what factor does its pressure change? *(× 4)*
3. A P–T(°C) line for a gas meets P = 0 at −275 °C. What is this temperature called, and what is the accepted value? *(Absolute zero; −273 °C, or 0 K)*
