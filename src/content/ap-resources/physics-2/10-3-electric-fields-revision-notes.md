## Recap

- A charged object sets up an **electric field** around it. Another charge placed in the field feels a force.
- The field at a point is the force per unit charge on a **test charge**: a charge small enough not to disturb the field.
- E is a **vector**. Near a lone positive charge it points outward; near a lone negative charge it points inward.
- The net field is the **vector sum** of the fields from each nearby charge.
- **Vector field maps** draw arrows (direction and size). **Field lines** are a simpler model: tangent gives direction, closer lines mean a stronger field, lines never cross.
- **Conductor in equilibrium:** E = 0 inside, excess charge on the surface, E perpendicular to the surface.
- **Insulator:** excess charge can be inside and on the surface; the field inside can be nonzero (qualitative only).

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| E = F_E / q | E in N/C, F in N, q in C | find E from a measured force, or the force from E |
| F_E = qE | keep the sign of q | get the direction of the force (opposite to E if q < 0) |
| E = k\|q\| / r² | k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C², r in m | find the field of a point charge |
| E_net = E₁ + E₂ + … (vectors) | add x- and y-components | combine fields of up to four charges |
| E outside a charged sphere = k\|Q\|/r² | r from the centre, r ≥ R | treat a symmetric sphere as a point charge |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Charges are **point charges** unless a size is given.
- Source charges stay fixed while you measure the field.
- Conductors are in **electrostatic equilibrium** (no charge moving).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Adding magnitudes of fields that point in different directions.** Use components.
2. **Giving an electron a force along E.** Negative charges are pushed opposite to E.
3. **Using the test charge in E = k|q|/r².** The q there is the source charge.
4. **Forgetting to square r**, or measuring r to the surface of a sphere instead of its centre.
5. **Treating field lines as paths.** They show the force direction, not the motion.
6. **Saying E = 0 inside any charged object.** Only inside a conductor in equilibrium.

## Quick self-check

1. A +2.0 μC test charge feels a force of 0.012 N. What is the field there? *(6.0 × 10³ N/C, in the direction of the force)*
2. The field 0.10 m from a point charge is 1200 N/C. What is it 0.20 m from the charge? *(300 N/C)*
3. A metal sphere carries excess negative charge. Where is that charge, and what is the field at its centre? *(On the outer surface; zero)*
