## Recap

- **Current** is the rate at which charge passes through a cross-section of a conductor. It is measured at a place, not stored in a component.
- Charge moves around a circuit because a source keeps a **potential difference** across it. The potential difference a source provides is its **emf** (ℰ), measured in volts. emf is energy per unit charge, not a force.
- The carriers are already in the wire. The source supplies energy, not charge.
- Free electrons move randomly at about 10⁶ m/s. With no current their **net** motion is zero. A potential difference adds a slow **drift** in one direction, and only the drift carries net charge.
- Current is a **scalar with a direction** along the wire. It does not change when a wire bends.
- **Conventional current** = the direction positive charge would move: from + to − outside the source. In metals, electrons carry the current and drift the opposite way.
- Circuit diagrams in this course use conventional current unless told otherwise.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| I = ΔQ / Δt | I in A, ΔQ in C, Δt in s | find current, charge or time for a steady current |
| N = ΔQ / e | e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C | count the electrons (or singly charged ions) that pass |
| ΔQ = area under I–t graph | A × s = C | find charge when the current changes |
| I_average = total ΔQ / total Δt | A | summarise a changing current |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Time is in **seconds**.
- Each electron or singly charged ion carries charge of size e.
- The current is the **net** flow: carriers of opposite sign moving in opposite directions both add to the current in the same direction.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Minutes not converted.** 2 minutes is 120 s.
2. **Electron direction given as the current direction.** Reverse it for conventional current.
3. **Subtracting ion currents** when positive and negative ions move in opposite directions. They add.
4. **"Current is used up."** The same current enters and leaves a component.
5. **Peak × time for a changing current.** Use the area under the I–t graph.
6. **"emf is a force."** It is a potential difference, in volts.

## Quick self-check

1. A current of 2.0 A flows for 30 s. How much charge passes? *(60 C)*
2. Electrons in a wire drift to the left. Which way is the conventional current? *(To the right)*
3. About how many electrons carry 1.0 C of charge? *(6.25 × 10¹⁸)*
