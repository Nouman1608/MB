Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/12-1-magnetic-fields-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/12-1-magnetic-fields-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can describe a magnetic field as a vector field that acts on moving charges, currents and magnetic materials, and give its unit. *(Guide: "What a magnetic field is")*
- I can explain why there are no magnetic monopoles and predict the poles of the pieces of a cut magnet. *(Guide: "Dipoles, poles and field lines"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain that magnetic dipoles come from moving electrons, and that permanent and induced magnetism are both alignment of dipoles. *(Guide: "Where magnetism comes from")*
- I can compare ferromagnetic, paramagnetic and diamagnetic materials and give an example of each. *(Guide: "How different materials respond"; Practice Q4)*
- I can describe permeability, state that μ₀ is a constant, and explain why a material's μ is not. *(Guide: "Magnetic permeability"; Practice Q2)*

## Representations and skills

- I can draw the field lines of a bar magnet as closed loops, with arrows outside and inside the magnet. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
- I can predict which way a compass points at any point near a magnet. *(Practice Q3)*
- I can add two perpendicular magnetic fields to find the direction a compass points. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can sketch Earth's dipole field and label its magnetic poles correctly. *(Guide: "Earth as a magnet"; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can use μ/μ₀ data to classify a material and justify the classification. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can justify the direction of a field inside a magnet or inside Earth using the closed-loop rule. *(Practice Q6c)*
- I can use data to show how a dipole's field decreases with distance and test a claimed relationship. *(Practice Q7)*
