## Recap

- **Power** is the rate at which energy is transferred, converted or dissipated. Unit: watt, 1 W = 1 J/s.
- P = IΔV comes from energy per charge (ΔV) multiplied by charge per second (I). It applies to every element.
- **Into the circuit:** the battery, at a rate Iℰ for an ideal battery. **Out of the circuit:** resistors and bulbs, as thermal energy (and light). **Within:** a capacitor can store energy.
- With an ideal battery, energy in per second = total energy out (and stored) per second.
- Brightness of a bulb increases with its power.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| P = IΔV | W = A × V | any element, I and ΔV known |
| P = I²R | R in Ω | elements share the same current (one loop) |
| P = ΔV²/R | ΔV in V | elements share the same ΔV (each directly across the same battery) |
| ΔE = PΔt | J, with Δt in s | energy transferred in a time |

| Change (constant R) | Effect on P |
|---|---|
| I × 2 | × 4 |
| ΔV × 3 | × 9 |
| ΔV × 1/2 | × 1/4 |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Batteries are ideal and wires have no resistance.
- Resistors and bulbs keep a **constant resistance** unless the question says otherwise.
- A rating such as "6.0 V, 3.0 W" applies **only** at the stated potential difference.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **"Bigger R, more power" without checking what is shared.** It is true only for the same current.
2. **Forgetting the square.** Double I or ΔV gives four times the power.
3. **Unit prefixes.** Convert mA, kΩ and minutes before calculating.
4. **"Current is used up in a bulb."** Energy is transferred; the current in and out is the same.
5. **Using the battery's emf for one element** when it is not the only element in the loop.
6. **Trusting the rating in every circuit.** A 12 W bulb can be dimmer than a 3 W bulb.

## Quick self-check

1. A motor carries 0.50 A with 12 V across it. What is its power? *(6.0 W)*
2. The ΔV across a resistor doubles. What happens to its power? *(× 4)*
3. How much energy does a 60 W lamp transfer in 5.0 minutes? *(1.8 × 10⁴ J)*
4. Two bulbs share one loop. Which is brighter: the one with larger or smaller R? *(Larger R)*
