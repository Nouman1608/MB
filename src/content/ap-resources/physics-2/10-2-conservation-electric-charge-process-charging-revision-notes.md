## Recap

- **Conservation of charge:** a system's net charge is constant unless charge moves in or out. Any change is matched by an opposite change in the surroundings.
- In solids, charging means moving **electrons**. Negative = gained electrons; positive = lost electrons.
- **Friction (rubbing):** electrons move from one material to the other; the two end with equal and opposite charges.
- **Contact:** touching conductors share charge; the total is unchanged. Identical spheres end with equal shares.
- **Induced charge separation:** a nearby charge pushes free electrons across a conductor. The near side takes the opposite sign; net charge is unchanged. It happens in neutral objects too.
- **Polarization in insulators:** electrons shift within each molecule; the near surface takes the opposite sign.
- A charged object **attracts** a neutral one, because the opposite charge is nearer (1/r²). Only **repulsion** proves like charges.
- **Grounding:** connecting to Earth, a huge, nearly neutral system that can give or take electrons.

## Key relationships and procedures

| Idea | Rule | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| q = Ne | e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C | count electrons moved |
| Σq before = Σq after | for an isolated system | check any charging problem |
| q_each = (q₁ + q₂)/2 | identical conductors after contact | find charges after touching |
| Induction | rod near → ground → break ground → remove rod | give a sphere the opposite charge to the rod |

## Assumptions behind the answers

- Conductors are on insulating stands, so charge leaves only through deliberate contact or grounding.
- Equal sharing applies only to **identical** conductors.
- Earth's own change in charge is negligible.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **"Protons moved."** Electrons moved.
2. **Halving each charge separately on contact.** Add the charges first, with signs, then share.
3. **"Attraction proves it is charged."** Neutral objects are attracted too.
4. **Wrong order in induction.** Remove the ground before the rod, or the sphere ends neutral.
5. **"The rod lost charge in induction."** It never touched the sphere.
6. **"Insulators cannot be charged."** They can; the charge just does not spread.

## Quick self-check

1. Identical metal spheres with +6 nC and −2 nC touch and separate. What is the charge on each? *(+2 nC)*
2. An object gains 1.0 × 10¹⁰ electrons. What is its charge? *(−1.6 × 10⁻⁹ C)*
3. A positive rod is used to charge a sphere by induction. What sign does the sphere end with? *(Negative: electrons flow up from the ground.)*
