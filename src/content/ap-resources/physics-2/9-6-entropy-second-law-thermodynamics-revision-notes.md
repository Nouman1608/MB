## Recap

- **Entropy S** describes how spread out a system's energy is. Spread-out energy means high entropy and less energy available to do work.
- Concentrated energy spreads out by itself: hot spots even out, gases fill their containers, moving objects stop.
- **Second law:** the total entropy of an **isolated** system never decreases. It is constant only if every process is reversible.
- Real processes (friction, mixing, free expansion, heating across a temperature difference) are **irreversible** and raise total entropy.
- An isolated system moves spontaneously toward **thermodynamic equilibrium**, where its entropy is at its **maximum**.
- Only qualitative reasoning is needed. You never calculate entropy in this course.

## Key relationships

| Situation | Entropy statement | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Isolated system, real process | ΔS_total > 0 | irreversible |
| Isolated system, ideal reversible process | ΔS_total = 0 | reversible limit |
| Isolated system at equilibrium | S at maximum; no further change | nothing left to spread |
| Closed system cooled (energy leaves by heating) | ΔS_system can be < 0 | surroundings gain at least as much S |
| Closed system heated (energy enters by heating) | ΔS_system > 0 | energy spreads into it |
| System completes a cycle | ΔS_system = 0 | state function |
| Two paths, same end states | same ΔS_system | state function; Q and W differ |

## Assumptions behind the statements

- "Isolated" means no energy and no matter cross the boundary. Check the boundary before you apply the second law.
- "Closed" means energy can cross but matter cannot.
- Reversible processes are idealised: very slow, no friction, no heating across a finite temperature difference.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Applying "entropy never decreases" to a closed system.** It applies to the total for an isolated system.
2. **Thinking the second law breaks energy conservation.** Energy is always conserved; the second law sets the direction.
3. **Saying ΔS depends on the path.** The system's ΔS does not; only Q, W and the surroundings' changes do.
4. **"Q = 0, so ΔS = 0."** A free expansion has Q = 0 but ΔS_gas > 0.
5. **"Cold to hot is impossible."** It is impossible only **spontaneously**. A freezer does it using work.
6. **Calling every slow process reversible.** It also needs no friction and no heating across a temperature difference.

## Quick self-check

1. Hot soup cools in a room. What happens to the entropy of the soup, the room and the total? *(Soup decreases; room increases by more; total increases.)*
2. A gas completes a cycle. What is ΔS for the gas? *(Zero: entropy is a state function.)*
3. A gas is heated with 400 J and does 400 J of work while expanding at constant temperature. What is ΔU? Does its entropy rise or fall? *(ΔU = 0; its entropy rises because it spreads into a larger volume.)*
4. When is the total entropy of an isolated system constant? *(Only if every process is reversible.)*
