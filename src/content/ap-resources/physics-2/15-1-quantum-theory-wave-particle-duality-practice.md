These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. The mark points are a suggested Marlbridge rubric to help you check your work; they are not an official scoring guideline. Data for every question: h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s; c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s; e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; 1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J; electron mass mₑ = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg; proton and neutron mass 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg. All wavelengths of light are vacuum wavelengths unless stated. All speeds are much less than c. A scientific calculator is assumed. Round only at the end.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · foundation)

Lamp X gives out blue light of wavelength 450 nm. Lamp Y gives out red light of wavelength 675 nm. What is the ratio (energy of one photon from X) ÷ (energy of one photon from Y)?

- (A) 1.5
- (B) 0.67
- (C) 2.25
- (D) 1.0, because all photons travel at the same speed

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** E = hc/λ, so E ∝ 1/λ. E_X/E_Y = λ_Y/λ_X = 675 nm ÷ 450 nm = 1.5. (Check: E_X = 2.76 eV and E_Y = 1.84 eV.)

- (B) treats E as proportional to λ. Shorter wavelength means higher frequency and more energy, not less.
- (C) squares the ratio. Photon energy depends on 1/λ, not 1/λ².
- (D) confuses speed with energy. All photons in a vacuum share the speed c, but their energies depend on frequency.
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · core)

Orange light of wavelength 600 nm in air passes into water, which has an index of refraction of 1.33. Which row describes the light in the water?

- (A) Speed 2.26 × 10⁸ m/s, wavelength 451 nm, photon energy unchanged
- (B) Speed 2.26 × 10⁸ m/s, wavelength 600 nm, photon energy smaller
- (C) Speed 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s, wavelength 451 nm, photon energy larger
- (D) Speed 3.99 × 10⁸ m/s, wavelength 798 nm, photon energy unchanged

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** v = c/n = (3.00 × 10⁸ m/s) ÷ 1.33 = 2.26 × 10⁸ m/s. The frequency is set by the source and does not change, so E = hf does not change. From v = fλ, the wavelength falls by the same factor as the speed: 600 nm ÷ 1.33 = 451 nm. (Treating air as n ≈ 1.)

- (B) keeps the wavelength fixed, so it must change the frequency, which would change the energy. In fact the frequency, not the wavelength, stays fixed.
- (C) keeps the vacuum speed in the water. Photons in a medium travel at c/n.
- (D) multiplies by n instead of dividing. A larger index means a slower photon.
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · core)

An electron and a proton move at the same speed. Which statement about their de Broglie wavelengths is correct?

- (A) The electron's wavelength is about 1830 times the proton's.
- (B) The electron's wavelength is about 43 times the proton's.
- (C) The proton's wavelength is about 1830 times the electron's.
- (D) Their wavelengths are equal, because they move at the same speed.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** λ = h/(mv). With the same v, λ ∝ 1/m, so λ_e/λ_p = mₚ/mₑ = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ ÷ 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ = 1.83 × 10³. The lighter particle has the smaller momentum and so the longer wavelength.

- (B) uses √(mₚ/mₑ) = 42.8. That ratio applies when the two particles have the same **kinetic energy**, not the same speed.
- (C) has the ratio upside down: it gives the heavier particle the longer wavelength.
- (D) forgets that λ depends on momentum, which includes the mass.
</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · core)

A very dim beam of electrons is aimed at two narrow slits so that only one electron passes through the apparatus at a time. A screen records where each electron arrives. Which description of the results is correct?

- (A) Each electron is recorded at a single point; after many electrons, the points form bright and dark fringes.
- (B) Each electron is spread out across the screen as a faint copy of the full interference pattern.
- (C) The electrons form two bright bands, one behind each slit, because each electron is a particle.
- (D) No pattern forms, because interference needs two electrons to pass through the slits together.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Each detection happens at one point, which is particle-like behaviour. The wave model gives the probability of arriving at each point, so many detections build up the interference fringes, which is wave-like behaviour.

- (B) is not what detectors record. An electron is never detected as a spread-out smear; it arrives whole at one place.
- (C) is the classical particle prediction. It fails because the electron's wave passes through both slits and interferes.
- (D) is contradicted by experiment: the fringes still appear when the electrons pass one at a time. Each electron's wave interferes with itself.
</details>

## Question 5 (calculation · core)

A sterilising lamp gives out 2.0 W of ultraviolet light, all of wavelength 250 nm.

(a) Calculate the energy of one photon, in joules and in electron-volts.
(b) Calculate the number of photons the lamp emits each second.
(c) An infrared heater gives out 2.0 W of light of wavelength 1000 nm. How many times more photons per second does it emit than the ultraviolet lamp? Explain without a full calculation.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

1. (a) E = hc/λ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s)(3.00 × 10⁸ m/s) ÷ (250 × 10⁻⁹ m) = **7.96 × 10⁻¹⁹ J**.
2. In eV: 7.956 × 10⁻¹⁹ J ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J/eV = **4.97 eV**.
3. (b) N/t = P/E = 2.0 J/s ÷ 7.956 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = **2.5 × 10¹⁸ photons per second**.
4. (c) The infrared wavelength is 4 times longer, so each infrared photon carries 1/4 of the energy (1.24 eV). To deliver the same 2.0 W, the heater must emit **4 times as many** photons per second (1.0 × 10¹⁹ per second).

Suggested mark points (4): 1 for E = 7.96 × 10⁻¹⁹ J; 1 for 4.97 eV; 1 for 2.5 × 10¹⁸ photons per second; 1 for the factor of 4 with the reason (E ∝ 1/λ at equal power).

Common error: writing f = cλ instead of f = c/λ, which gives an energy near 5 × 10⁻³² J. Always check that a visible or ultraviolet photon has a few eV.
</details>

## Question 6 (constructed response · core)

A particle of mass m and charge q starts from rest and is accelerated through a potential difference ΔV.

(a) Derive an expression for its de Broglie wavelength λ in terms of m, q, ΔV and physical constants.
(b) The potential difference is increased to 4ΔV. By what factor does λ change? Justify using your expression.
(c) An electron and a proton are each accelerated from rest through the same potential difference. Determine the ratio λ_electron/λ_proton.
(d) Calculate λ for an electron accelerated through 50 V, and say whether a crystal with atomic spacing about 2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m could diffract it.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** The work done by the field gives the kinetic energy: K = qΔV. Also K = ½mv² = p²/(2m), so p = √(2mK) = √(2mqΔV). Then **λ = h/p = h/√(2mqΔV)**.

**(b)** λ ∝ 1/√ΔV. Multiplying ΔV by 4 multiplies λ by 1/√4, so **λ halves**.

**(c)** The two particles have charges of equal size, so with the same ΔV, λ ∝ 1/√m. λ_e/λ_p = √(mₚ/mₑ) = √(1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ ÷ 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) = **42.8**.

**(d)** λ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴) ÷ √(2 × 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 50) = **1.7 × 10⁻¹⁰ m**. This is comparable to the atomic spacing, so **yes**, the crystal can diffract the electrons.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Uses energy conservation K = qΔV for a particle starting from rest |
| 1 | Correct momentum p = √(2mqΔV) (or v = √(2qΔV/m) then p = mv) |
| 1 | Final expression λ = h/√(2mqΔV) |
| 1 | (b) Factor 1/2, justified by λ ∝ 1/√ΔV from the expression |
| 1 | (c) Ratio 42.8 (accept 43), electron longer |
| 1 | (d) λ ≈ 1.7 × 10⁻¹⁰ m and a conclusion based on λ being comparable to the spacing |

Do not award (b) for "λ quarters" (that treats λ ∝ 1/ΔV). Accept a ratio in (c) written as proton/electron = 0.0234 if the student states clearly which particle has the longer wavelength.
</details>

## Question 7 (constructed response · stretch)

A research team compares two experiments with fictional data.

- Experiment 1: a beam of neutrons moving at 2.0 × 10³ m/s is aimed at a crystal whose rows of atoms are about 2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m apart.
- Experiment 2: a dust grain of mass 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁵ kg and diameter about 1 × 10⁻⁶ m drifts at 1.0 × 10⁻³ m/s towards a pair of slits.

(a) Calculate the de Broglie wavelength in each experiment.
(b) Predict, with reasons, in which experiment a diffraction or interference pattern could be observed.
(c) A student says: "Neutrons have no charge, so they cannot be diffracted. Only charged particles and light have waves." Evaluate this claim.
(d) The neutrons in Experiment 1 have a kinetic energy of about 0.02 eV. Explain, using the de Broglie relation, why physicists use slow neutrons rather than fast ones to study crystals.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** Neutron: λ = h/(mv) = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴) ÷ (1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ × 2.0 × 10³) = **1.99 × 10⁻¹⁰ m**. Dust grain: λ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴) ÷ (1.0 × 10⁻¹⁵ × 1.0 × 10⁻³) = **6.6 × 10⁻¹⁶ m**.

**(b)** **Experiment 1.** The neutron wavelength is about the same as the spacing of the rows of atoms, so the crystal acts as a grating and strong diffraction is expected. The dust grain's wavelength is about 10⁻⁹ of its own size, and far smaller than any slit it could pass through, so no pattern can be seen.

**(c)** The claim is **wrong**. The de Broglie wavelength λ = h/p depends only on momentum, not on charge. Any particle with momentum has a wavelength, so neutral neutrons can be diffracted (as the numbers in (a) and (b) show). Photons are also neutral, and they diffract.

**(d)** Kinetic energy 0.02 eV means a small momentum, so a long wavelength. At this energy λ is close to the atomic spacing. Much faster neutrons would have larger momentum and a much shorter wavelength than the spacing, so the diffraction angles would be too small to measure.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Neutron λ ≈ 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ m |
| 1 | Dust grain λ ≈ 6.6 × 10⁻¹⁶ m |
| 1 | Chooses Experiment 1 **because** λ is comparable to the atomic spacing |
| 1 | Explains no pattern for the dust grain by comparing its λ with its size or any slit width |
| 1 | Rejects the claim: λ = h/p does not involve charge, with neutrons or photons as evidence |
| 1 | (d) Links low energy to low momentum to a wavelength that matches the spacing |

Accept, in (d), reasoning that compares the wavelengths of slow and fast neutrons using λ ∝ 1/v. Carry forward an error from (a) into (b) once.
</details>
