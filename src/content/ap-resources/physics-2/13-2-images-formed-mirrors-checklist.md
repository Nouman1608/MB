Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/13-2-images-formed-mirrors-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/13-2-images-formed-mirrors-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can describe the focal point of a concave, a convex and a plane mirror, and use f ≈ R/2. *(Guide: "Curved mirrors and the focal point"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain the difference between a real and a virtual image in terms of whether reflected rays meet. *(Guide: "Real and virtual images"; Practice Q7c)*
- I can explain why a plane-mirror image is as far behind the mirror as the object is in front. *(Guide: "The mirror equation and sign conventions"; Practice Q1)*
- I can state the sign convention for f and sᵢ, and say what a negative sᵢ means. *(Guide: sign table; Practice Q2, Q3)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can draw the three principal rays for a concave and a convex mirror, using dashed lines for virtual extensions. *(Guide: Figures 1 and 2; Practice Q5a)*
- I can use the mirror equation to locate the image for a concave or convex mirror. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q3, Q5b)*
- I can find the image height from |M| = |sᵢ/sₒ| and describe the image by location, type, orientation and size. *(Guide: "Magnification"; Practice Q5c)*
- I can derive a symbolic expression for magnification and use it to find where to place an object. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
- I can linearise the mirror equation and find f from the intercept of a best-fit line. *(Practice Q7a, Q7b)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict how the image position and size change as an object moves towards a concave mirror. *(Guide: image table; Practice Q4)*
- I can use evidence, such as an image on a screen, to justify whether a mirror is concave or convex. *(Practice Q7c)*
- I can explain why a convex mirror gives a wide field of view. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
