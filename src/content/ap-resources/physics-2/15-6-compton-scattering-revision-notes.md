## Recap

- In **Compton scattering** a photon (usually an X-ray) collides with a free electron at rest and comes out at an angle θ to its original direction.
- The photon hands some energy to the electron, so it leaves with lower energy and a **longer wavelength**.
- The shift grows with the scattering angle and does not depend on the starting wavelength.
- A classical wave model predicts no change in wavelength. Treating the photon as a particle that obeys energy and momentum conservation explains the shift, so the effect is evidence for photons.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| E = hf = hc/λ | h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s | find photon energy |
| p = h/λ = E/c | kg·m/s | find photon momentum |
| hc/λ = hc/λ′ + K_e | J (or eV) | find the electron's kinetic energy |
| Δλ = (h/m_e c)(1 − cos θ) | h/(m_e c) = 2.43 × 10⁻¹² m | relate the shift to the angle |
| Σp_x and Σp_y are each conserved | vector components | find the electron's momentum and direction |

| θ | 0° | 90° | 180° |
|---|---|---|---|
| Δλ | 0 | 2.43 pm | 4.85 pm (the maximum) |

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Saying the photon slows down.** Its speed stays at c.
2. **Using p = mv for a photon.** Use p = h/λ.
3. **Thinking Δλ depends on λ.** Only the fractional change does.
4. **Putting the electron on the same side as the scattered photon.** The y-components of momentum must cancel.
5. **Rounding E and E′ before subtracting** to find K_e.
6. **Calculator in radians mode** when θ is in degrees, or writing 1 + cos θ instead of 1 − cos θ.

## Quick self-check

1. A photon scatters at 90°. What is Δλ? *(2.43 pm)*
2. Which has the larger shift at the same angle: a 30 pm photon or a 60 pm photon? *(Neither: the shift is the same. The 30 pm photon loses the larger fraction of its energy.)*
3. A photon scatters straight back (θ = 180°). In which direction does the electron move? *(Forward, along the photon's original direction.)*
