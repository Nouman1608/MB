## Understanding

- I can explain electric potential as electric potential energy per coulomb at a point, and say why it does not depend on the test charge. *(Guide: "Energy per coulomb")*
- I can explain the difference between V (a property of a point, in volts) and U_E (a property of a system, in joules). *(Guide: misconceptions; Practice Q6d)*
- I can explain how a battery keeps a potential difference between its terminals. *(Guide: "Batteries")*
- I can explain why conductors in contact end up at the same potential, and why that does not mean equal charge. *(Guide: "Conductors in contact"; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why equipotential lines are perpendicular to the field and why the field does no work along them. *(Guide: "Equipotential lines"; Practice Q3)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can find the potential due to up to four point charges by adding signed values of kq/r. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q7a)*
- I can use ΔU_E = qΔV, with the signs of both q and ΔV, to find an energy change or the work an external force does. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6d, Q7b)*
- I can find an average field from |ΔV/Δr| and give its direction toward lower potential. *(Guide: "Potential and field"; Practice Q4, Q6b)*
- I can draw field vectors from an equipotential map, and equipotential lines from a field map. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can find the charge on each of two joined conducting spheres using equal potentials and charge conservation. *(Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict the direction of motion of a positive or negative charge released on an equipotential map, and say how its acceleration changes. *(Guide: "Predicting motion from a map"; Practice Q6c)*
- I can explain why V can be zero where E is not, using scalar versus vector addition. *(Practice Q1, Q7c)*
