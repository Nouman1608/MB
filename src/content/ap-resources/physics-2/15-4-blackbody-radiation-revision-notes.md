## Recap

- All matter spontaneously turns some internal (thermal) energy into electromagnetic radiation.
- A **blackbody** absorbs all radiation falling on it. At constant temperature it must emit at the same rate as it absorbs.
- A blackbody's spectrum is **continuous** and depends **only on temperature**. It is plotted as intensity per unit wavelength against wavelength.
- Curve features: near zero at short λ, steep rise, one peak, long tail. A hotter curve is higher at **every** wavelength and peaks at a **shorter** wavelength. The area under it is the power per unit area.
- Classical physics predicted ever-rising emission at short wavelengths (the "ultraviolet catastrophe"). **Planck's law** fits the data by assuming light energy comes in quanta E = hf.
- Hot objects change colour from dull red to white as T rises; stars that look bluish are hotter than stars that look reddish.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| λ_max = b/T | b = 2.898 × 10⁻³ m·K; T in K; λ in m | link peak wavelength and temperature |
| P = σAT⁴ | σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W/(m²·K⁴); A in m² | find total power emitted |
| P/A = σT⁴ | W/m² | compare surfaces at different T |
| A = 4πr² | sphere | surface area of a star or ball |
| P₂/P₁ = (A₂/A₁)(T₂/T₁)⁴ | ratios | compare two blackbodies without σ |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The object is modelled as an **ideal blackbody**; real surfaces emit somewhat less.
- T is the **absolute** temperature, uniform over the surface.
- P = σAT⁴ is the power **emitted**, not the net power after absorbing from the surroundings.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Using °C.** Convert to kelvin first.
2. **Doubling T and doubling P.** P scales as T⁴.
3. **λ_max ∝ T.** It is inversely proportional.
4. **Crossing curves.** A hotter curve lies above a cooler one everywhere.
5. **Peak only.** A blackbody emits at all wavelengths, not just at λ_max.
6. **Forgetting the area.** Two stars at the same T can emit very different total powers if their radii differ.

## Quick self-check

1. Where is the peak for a blackbody at 2900 K? *(About 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ m, 1.0 µm, infrared)*
2. A blackbody's temperature is tripled. By what factor does its power change? *(3⁴ = 81)*
3. A star's peak wavelength is half that of another. Compare their temperatures. *(The first is twice as hot)*
4. What did Planck assume that classical physics did not? *(Light energy is emitted in quanta, E = hf)*
