## Understanding

- I can describe the field around a long straight wire as circles centred on the wire, with no part towards, away from or along it. *(Guide: "The field around a long straight wire", Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can use the right-hand rule to find the direction of the field at any point near a straight wire. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1, Q6)*
- I can find the direction of the field at the centre of a current loop. *(Guide: "The field at the centre of a loop"; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why a magnetic field exerts a force on a current-carrying wire, starting from the force on moving charges. *(Guide: "The force on a current-carrying wire")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can calculate B = μ₀I/(2πr) and predict how B changes when I or r changes. *(Guide: "The field around a long straight wire"; Practice Q2)*
- I can add the fields of two or more wires as vectors, including fields that are opposite or perpendicular. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6)*
- I can calculate the force F = IℓB sin θ on a wire and find its direction with the right-hand rule. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can find the force per unit length between two parallel wires and say whether they attract or repel. *(Guide: "Two parallel wires"; Practice Q3)*
- I can choose axes that give a straight-line graph (B against 1/r, or F against I) and use the slope to find an unknown. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why two wires exert equal forces on each other even when their currents differ. *(Practice Q3)*
- I can plan an experiment to test how the field of a wire depends on current or distance, including how to deal with Earth's field. *(Guide: "Designing the experiment"; Practice Q7)*
- I can evaluate a claim that adds field magnitudes without considering direction. *(Practice Q6c)*
