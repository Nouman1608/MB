## Recap

- At a boundary between two media a wave is **partly reflected and partly transmitted**. The two new waves share the incident energy, so each has a smaller amplitude.
- The **reflected** pulse is inverted if the wave slows down in the new medium, and upright if it speeds up. The **transmitted** pulse is always upright.
- Limiting cases: a fixed end reflects everything, inverted; a free end reflects everything, upright.
- **Frequency and period do not change** at a boundary. Wavelength and pulse width change in proportion to speed.
- A **polarized** transverse wave oscillates in one direction. **Longitudinal** waves (such as sound) cannot be polarized.
- Waves can be polarized by a filter (or slot), by reflection from a surface and by refraction.
- Polarizing a wave usually reduces its **intensity**, the average power per unit area over one period.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Strings that are tied together have the **same tension**.
- Filters are **ideal** unless the question says otherwise.
- Light falls on a surface **at right angles** when you use I = P/A.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Changing the frequency** at a boundary. Only v and λ change.
2. **Inverting the transmitted pulse.** Only the reflection can flip.
3. **Giving the reflected pulse the new speed.** It is still in the first medium.
4. **Saying sound can be polarized.** It is longitudinal.
5. **Halving the intensity at the second filter.** The ½ rule is only for unpolarized light; after that, the angle between the axes decides.
6. **Confusing intensity and power.** Intensity is power per square metre.

## Quick self-check

1. A pulse goes from a heavy rope into a light rope under the same tension. Is the reflected pulse inverted? *(No. The wave speeds up, so the reflection is upright.)*
2. A 10 Hz wave moves at 40 m/s and then enters a medium where it moves at 15 m/s. What are the two wavelengths? *(4.0 m, then 1.5 m)*
3. Unpolarized light of intensity 120 W/m² passes through one ideal polarizing filter. What intensity leaves? *(60 W/m²)*
