Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/14-5-doppler-effect-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/14-5-doppler-effect-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between the rest frequency of a source and the frequency an observer measures. *(Guide: "Rest frequency and observed frequency")*
- I can explain why the source's own vibration does not change when it moves. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q1)*
- I can use the travel-time argument to explain why a shrinking separation raises the observed frequency and a growing one lowers it. *(Guide: "Why it happens"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why the wave speed in a medium is not changed by the motion of the source. *(Guide: "A moving source"; Practice Q5)*

## Skills

- I can draw a wavefront diagram for a moving source and mark where the observed frequency is higher and lower. *(Guide: Figure 1, Worked example 1)*
- I can use v = fλ with the crest spacing on a wavefront diagram to find the frequency at a fixed point. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can sketch how the frequency heard from a passing source changes with time, relative to f₀. *(Guide: "A source that passes you"; Practice Q6)*
- I can state the direction of the shift in wavelength for light from an approaching or receding source (blue or red shift). *(Guide: "The Doppler effect for light"; Practice Q3)*

## Reasoning

- I can rank observed frequencies for several situations using the direction and size of the relative velocity. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2)*
- I can justify why there is no shift when source and observer share the same velocity, even in a steady wind. *(Guide: "What sets the size of the shift"; Practice Q7)*
- I can predict how a reflected wave's frequency changes when the reflecting object moves toward or away from the detector, and how that depends on its speed. *(Practice Q4)*
