## Understanding

- I can explain resistance as the opposition an object gives to the movement of charge, and define it as R = ΔV/I. *(Guide: "What resistance measures")*
- I can explain the difference between resistivity (a property of a material) and resistance (a property of an object). *(Guide: "Resistance from shape and material")*
- I can explain why the resistivity of a metal usually increases with temperature. *(Guide: "Resistivity and temperature"; Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can say what makes an element ohmic, and explain why a filament bulb is not. *(Guide: "Ohm's law and ohmic elements"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain that a resistor converts electrical energy into thermal energy without using up current. *(Guide: "What resistance measures")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can calculate R from ρ, L and a diameter, converting mm and mm² correctly. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can predict the factor by which R and I change when length, diameter or material change. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q1, Q5c)*
- I can use I = ΔV/R to find any one of the three quantities. *(Practice Q5b)*
- I can find R from the slope of an I–ΔV graph, remembering that the slope is 1/R. *(Guide: "Reading resistance from a graph"; Practice Q2, Q7)*
- I can plot R against L and use the slope to find a resistivity. *(Guide: Worked example 3)*

## Reasoning

- I can plan an experiment to measure the resistance of an element, placing the ammeter and voltmeter correctly. *(Guide: "Measuring resistance in the lab"; Practice Q6a)*
- I can use data or a graph to decide whether an element is ohmic. *(Practice Q6c)*
- I can evaluate a claim that changing ΔV changes the resistance of an ohmic element. *(Practice Q7c)*
