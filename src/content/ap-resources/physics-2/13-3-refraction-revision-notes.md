## Recap

- **Refraction** is the change in direction of a ray as it passes from one medium into another.
- It happens because the **speed of light changes** at the boundary. The frequency does not change.
- A ray that meets the boundary **along the normal** slows down but does not bend.
- Into a **higher n**: bends **toward** the normal. Into a **lower n**: bends **away** from the normal.
- **Total internal reflection** (TIR): going into a lower n, beyond the critical angle, all the light reflects and none is transmitted. At exactly θc the refracted ray runs along the surface.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| n = c / v | c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s; n has no units | find the speed of light in a medium, or n from a speed |
| n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂ | θ measured from the normal, in degrees | find a refracted angle or an unknown n |
| sin θc = n₂ / n₁ | only when n₁ > n₂ | find the critical angle for TIR |
| sin θ_air = n sin θ_material | slope of sin θ_air vs sin θ_material = n | find n from a graph of experimental data |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Air has n = 1.00 (its real value, 1.0003, makes no difference at three significant figures).
- Each medium is uniform, so rays travel in straight lines inside it.
- Light is treated as rays; its wave nature is ignored in this topic.
- Thin container walls with parallel faces do not change the final direction of a ray.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Angle from the surface.** Always use the angle from the normal.
2. **Wrong way round in Snell's law.** Pair each n with the angle in the **same** medium.
3. **Calculator in radians.** Check that sin 30 = 0.5.
4. **TIR in the wrong direction.** It can only happen going into a lower n.
5. **Plotting θ₂ against θ₁ and expecting a straight line.** Plot the sines.
6. **Saying light "speeds up into glass".** Higher n means lower speed.

## Quick self-check

1. Light travels at 2.40 × 10⁸ m/s in a plastic. What is n? *(1.25)*
2. Find the critical angle for glass (n = 1.52) to air. *(41.1°)*
3. A ray in air meets water (n = 1.33) at 45°. What is the angle of refraction? *(32.1°, toward the normal)*
4. Can a ray in air be totally internally reflected at a water surface? *(No: going into a higher n, the refracted angle is always smaller than the incident angle.)*
