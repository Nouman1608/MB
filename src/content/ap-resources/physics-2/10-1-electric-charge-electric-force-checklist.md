Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/10-1-electric-charge-electric-force-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/10-1-electric-charge-electric-force-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can state the charges of the proton, electron and neutron and explain what a neutral object is. *(Guide: "Charge: a property of matter")*
- I can explain why every net charge is a whole-number multiple of e, and spot an impossible charge. *(Guide; Practice Q2)*
- I can say when the point-charge model is reasonable and when it breaks down. *(Guide: "The point-charge model"; Practice Q7d)*
- I can state the direction of the electric force between like and unlike charges and explain why the two forces in a pair are equal in size. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5b)*
- I can describe permittivity and polarization and the difference between a conductor and an insulator. *(Guide: "Permittivity and polarization"; Practice Q4)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can use F = k|q₁q₂|/r² with correct units and prefixes. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can predict a new force from factors of change in charge and distance. *(Guide: "Predicting changes"; Practice Q1)*
- I can find the net force on a charge from two or three others by adding vector components. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can derive the position where the net force from two fixed charges is zero. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can linearise force and distance data and find a charge from the slope. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can compare electric and gravitational forces between particles and explain why gravity dominates at large scales. *(Guide: "Electric force versus gravitational force"; Practice Q5c, Q5d)*
- I can explain that contact forces such as the normal force are electric forces between atoms, modelled in bulk. *(Guide: "Everyday forces are electric underneath")*
