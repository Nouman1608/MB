## Recap

- **Rest frequency f₀:** how often the source vibrates. An observer moving with the source measures f₀.
- **Observed frequency f:** how many wavefronts reach a given observer each second.
- **Doppler effect:** f differs from f₀ because of the relative velocity of source and observer.
- **Travel-time argument:** if the distance between source and observer is shrinking, each crest has a shorter trip than the last, so crests arrive closer together in time (f > f₀). Growing distance gives f < f₀. Constant distance gives f = f₀.
- **Moving source in a medium:** wavefronts bunch up ahead (shorter λ) and spread out behind (longer λ). The wave speed is set by the medium and does not change.
- **Moving observer:** the wavelength in the medium is unchanged, but the observer meets crests more often (approaching) or less often (receding).
- **Light:** no medium is needed; only relative velocity matters.
- In this course the Doppler effect is **qualitative**: predict, compare and justify. No Doppler formula is needed.

## Key relationships

| Situation | Observed frequency | Observed wavelength (sound ahead/behind a source, or light) |
|---|---|---|
| Separation shrinking (approaching) | f > f₀ | shorter |
| Separation growing (receding) | f < f₀ | longer |
| Separation constant (same velocity, or motion across the line of sight) | f = f₀ | unchanged |
| Larger relative speed along the line | larger \|f − f₀\| | larger change |

| Passing source at constant speed | What you hear |
|---|---|
| Approaching | steady pitch above f₀, getting louder |
| Passing | pitch falls quickly through f₀ |
| Receding | steady pitch below f₀, getting quieter |

## Assumptions behind the rules

- Speeds are constant unless the question says otherwise.
- For sound, the medium (air) is still, or any wind is steady.
- Speeds of sources are well below the wave speed.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Saying the source's own frequency changes.** It does not.
2. **Rising pitch on approach.** At constant velocity the pitch is constant while approaching.
3. **Mixing up pitch and loudness.** Pitch ↔ frequency; loudness ↔ amplitude.
4. **Changing the wave speed.** Source motion changes λ in the medium, not v.
5. **Red shift = approaching.** Red shift means receding (longer λ).

## Quick self-check

1. A drone hovers beside you and beeps. Then it flies away from you at constant speed. What happens to the pitch you hear? *(It drops below f₀ and stays at that lower value while the drone recedes at constant speed.)*
2. Two cyclists ride side by side at the same velocity; one rings a bell. Does the other hear a shift? *(No: their separation is constant.)*
3. Wavefronts ahead of a moving source in air are 0.50 m apart and sound travels at 340 m/s. What frequency reaches a still observer there? *(680 Hz, from v = fλ.)*
4. A spectral line from a galaxy is measured at a shorter wavelength than in the laboratory. What does that show? *(The galaxy is approaching: blue shift.)*
