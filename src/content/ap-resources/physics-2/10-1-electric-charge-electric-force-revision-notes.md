## Recap

- **Charge** is a basic property of matter. It is positive or negative.
- Proton +e, electron −e, neutron 0. The **elementary charge** e is the smallest charge that exists on its own, so q = Ne with N a whole number.
- A **neutral** object has equal amounts of positive and negative charge, not zero charges.
- A **point charge** is a model: the object's size is negligible compared with the distances involved.
- The electric force acts **along the line** joining two charges. Like charges repel; unlike charges attract. The two forces in a pair are equal and opposite.
- Several charges: find each pair force, then add **as vectors**.
- Electric forces can attract or repel; gravity only attracts. For particles, gravity is far weaker. Gravity dominates at large scales because large objects are nearly neutral.
- Contact forces (normal, friction, tension) are electric forces between atoms, modelled in bulk.
- **Permittivity** measures how strongly a material polarizes; **polarization** is an induced separation of charge inside a neutral material. Conductors let charge move easily; insulators do not.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| q = Ne | e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; N a whole number | count extra or missing electrons |
| F = k\|q₁q₂\|/r² | k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²; q in C; r in m | find the size of the force between two point charges |
| k = 1/(4πε₀) | ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² C²/(N·m²) | switch between the two forms of Coulomb's law |
| F ∝ \|q₁q₂\|/r² | factors multiply | predict new forces without k |
| F_net = ΣF (vectors) | components with signs from a diagram | combine up to four charges |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Charged objects are treated as **point charges**: small compared with their separation.
- Charges are at rest (electrostatics).
- Only the charges named in the question act; everything else is far away or neutral.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **1/r, not 1/r².** Doubling r quarters F.
2. **Using the sign of the answer as the direction.** Use |q₁q₂|, then decide direction from a diagram.
3. **"Bigger charge, bigger force on it."** Pair forces are always equal in size.
4. **Adding magnitudes of forces at an angle.** Use components.
5. **Charges that are not multiples of e.** 2.4 × 10⁻¹⁹ C (1.5e) is impossible.
6. **Forgetting unit prefixes.** μC = 10⁻⁶ C, nC = 10⁻⁹ C; r in metres.

## Quick self-check

1. The distance between two point charges is halved. By what factor does the force change? *(×4)*
2. A bead has charge +8.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. How many electrons is it missing? *(5)*
3. One charge is tripled and the separation is tripled. What happens to the force? *(It becomes 3/9 = 1/3 as large.)*
4. Why does the Moon orbit Earth because of gravity and not electric force? *(Both are almost exactly neutral, so the electric force between them is close to zero; gravity never cancels.)*
