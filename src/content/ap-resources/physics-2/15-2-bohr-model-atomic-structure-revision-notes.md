## Recap

- An atom has a tiny, positive **nucleus** of protons and neutrons, surrounded by negative **electrons**. Most of the atom is empty space.
- The mass of an atom is almost all in the nucleus: an electron is about 1/1800 of the mass of a proton.
- Each element has its own **atomic number Z** (number of protons). Different **isotopes** of an element have different numbers of neutrons, so different **mass numbers A**.
- An **ion** has a nonzero net charge because its numbers of protons and electrons differ. The electrons decide how an atom interacts with other atoms.
- The **Bohr model** treats the electron as orbiting the nucleus in a circle, held by the Coulomb force. It is a historical model that led to the idea of discrete energy states.
- Only orbits whose circumference holds a **whole number of de Broglie wavelengths** are allowed, so only certain energies are allowed.
- In this course, describe electron structure only by energy levels. Orbitals and their shapes are not needed.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Notation such as ⁵⁶₂₆Fe: N = A − Z | A = mass number, Z = atomic number, N = neutrons | count nucleons |
| net charge = (Z − number of electrons)e | e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C | find an ion's charge |
| k\|q₁q₂\|/r² = mv²/r | k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C² | link speed and radius of an orbit |
| 2πr = nλ, λ = h/(mv) | n = 1, 2, 3, … | pick out the allowed orbits |
| r = n²r₁, v = v₁/n, λ = nλ₁ | hydrogen: r₁ = 5.29 × 10⁻¹¹ m | compare orbits |
| E = −ke²/(2r) = −13.6 eV/n² | zero energy at infinite separation | find the energy of a hydrogen state |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- One electron only (H, He⁺, Li²⁺). For a nucleus of charge +Ze, use kZe²/r².
- The nucleus is treated as fixed because it is so much heavier than the electron.
- Speeds are much less than c.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Mixing up A and Z.** Z is the smaller number (bottom left); A is the larger (top left).
2. **Getting the electron count wrong for an ion.** A 2+ ion has 2 fewer electrons than protons; a 1− ion has 1 more.
3. **Thinking ions or isotopes change the element.** Only Z does.
4. **Expecting faster electrons in bigger orbits.** v = √(ke²/(mr)) falls as r grows.
5. **Allowing any radius.** The standing-wave condition allows only r = n²r₁.
6. **Giving the ground state zero energy.** It is the most negative level.

## Quick self-check

1. How many protons, neutrons and electrons are in ¹⁸₈O²⁻? *(8, 10, 10)*
2. For hydrogen, how do r and v in the n = 2 orbit compare with n = 1? *(r is 4 times larger, 2.12 × 10⁻¹⁰ m; v is half, 1.09 × 10⁶ m/s)*
3. How many de Broglie wavelengths fit around the n = 2 orbit? *(Two)*
