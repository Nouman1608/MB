## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between electric potential (a property of a place, in volts) and electric potential energy (a property of a system, in joules). *(Guide: "From potential to potential energy")*
- I can predict which way a positive or a negative charge moves when released from rest, in terms of potential and field direction. *(Guide: "Which way does a released charge move?"; Figure 1)*
- I can explain why a charge that starts and ends on the same equipotential line has the same speed at both points. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can choose the system (charge alone, or charge plus field) and use either W_field or ΔU_E without counting the energy twice. *(Guide: "Energy conservation for a moving charge")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can calculate ΔU_E = qΔV with the correct sign for a positive or negative charge. *(Practice Q1, Q5a)*
- I can use ΔK + ΔU_E = 0 to find the speed of a particle after it crosses a potential difference, starting from rest or already moving. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5, Q7)*
- I can find the potential difference needed to stop a moving charge, or its distance of closest approach to a fixed charge. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5c)*
- I can derive a symbolic expression for the speed of a charge released near a fixed point charge. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can sketch U_E and K against position for a uniform field and for a fixed point charge. *(Guide: "Energy graphs"; Practice Q3, Q6d, Q7c)*

## Reasoning

- I can compare the kinetic energies and speeds of two particles with different charges and masses crossing the same ΔV. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why changing the plate spacing, with the same potentials, does not change the final speed. *(Practice Q7d)*
- I can check an answer by a second method, such as forces and kinematics in a uniform field. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
