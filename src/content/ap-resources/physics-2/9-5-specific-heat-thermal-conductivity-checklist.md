## Understanding

- I can explain what specific heat means and why a material with a high c changes temperature slowly. *(Guide: "Energy to change temperature"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why specific heat is a property of the material, not of the object, and link it to the atoms and how they interact. *(Guide: "Why materials have different specific heats")*
- I can explain why objects in thermal contact in an insulated container reach one common temperature, and why the total energy stays the same. *(Guide: "Thermal equilibrium and mixing"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why metals conduct far better than wood, glass or still air, and why a metal feels colder than wood at the same temperature. *(Guide: "Why thermal conductivity depends on the material"; Practice Q4)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can use Q = mcΔT to find Q, m, c or ΔT, using a temperature change rather than a temperature. *(Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can combine Q = PΔt with Q = mcΔT to find a heating time or the fraction of energy that reaches an object. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can find c from the slope of a temperature–energy or temperature–time graph. *(Guide: Figure 1 and Worked example 1; Practice Q6b)*
- I can find the final temperature when two objects reach thermal equilibrium, and check that it lies between the starting temperatures. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2)*
- I can calculate a rate of conduction with Q/Δt = kAΔT/L and predict factors of change when A, L or ΔT change. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3)*

## Reasoning

- I can plan an experiment to measure a specific heat, including how to reduce energy losses. *(Practice Q6a)*
- I can explain whether energy losses make a measured c too large or too small. *(Guide: Worked example 1 check; Practice Q6c)*
- I can explain why the rate is the same through each layer of a wall in steady state, and use it to find a boundary temperature. *(Practice Q7)*
