## Understanding

- I can define current as the rate at which charge passes through a cross-section of a conductor, and give its unit. *(Guide: "What current measures"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain that a source keeps a potential difference (emf) across a circuit, and that this is what makes charge move. *(Guide: "What makes charge move")*
- I can explain that the carriers are already in the wire and the source supplies energy, not charge. *(Guide: "What makes charge move"; Practice Q7c)*
- I can explain why zero current does not mean the charge carriers are at rest. *(Guide: "Random motion and drift"; Practice Q2, Q7d)*
- I can explain why current is a scalar even though it has a direction along a wire. *(Guide: "Which way is the current?")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can use I = ΔQ/Δt to find current, charge or time, converting minutes and prefixes correctly. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can find the number of electrons or ions that carry a given charge. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q5d)*
- I can sketch a current–time graph and find the charge from the area under it, and the average current. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can find the total current when positive and negative carriers move in opposite directions. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can give the direction of conventional current from the motion of electrons, and the other way round. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q3, Q7b)*
- I can use the direction carriers move to decide which end of a conductor is at the higher potential. *(Practice Q6a)*
- I can justify, using conservation of charge, why the current is the same on both sides of a lamp. *(Practice Q7a)*
- I can use the drift speed to explain why a lamp lights almost instantly even though electrons drift slowly. *(Guide: "Random motion and drift"; Practice Q7c)*
