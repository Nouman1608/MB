## Understanding

- I can describe entropy as how spread out a system's energy is, and link higher entropy to less energy being available to do work. *(Guide: "Entropy: how spread out the energy is"; Practice Q7d)*
- I can give everyday examples of concentrated energy spreading out by itself. *(Guide: "Which way do things happen?")*
- I can state the second law for an isolated system, including when total entropy stays constant. *(Guide: "The second law"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain what makes a process reversible and why every real process is irreversible. *(Guide: "The second law"; Practice Q7c)*
- I can explain why entropy is a state function, and why ΔS = 0 for a system that completes a cycle. *(Guide: "Entropy is a state function"; Practice Q3, Q7b)*
- I can explain why an isolated system moves toward equilibrium and why its entropy is greatest there. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6c)*

## Skills

- I can sketch how the temperatures and the total entropy of two objects change as they reach equilibrium. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6a, Q6b)*
- I can draw a system boundary and say whether the system is isolated or closed. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q2)*
- I can combine an energy calculation (Q = mcΔT or ΔU = Q + W) with a statement about entropy. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5, Q7a)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain how a closed system's entropy can decrease without breaking the second law. *(Guide: "Isolated systems and closed systems"; Practice Q2, Q6d)*
- I can compare two paths between the same states: same ΔS for the system, different effects on the surroundings. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q4)*
- I can use the second law to explain why a process that conserves energy still never happens in reverse. *(Practice Q5d)*
- Stretch (background): I can explain why no engine can turn all of the energy it takes in by heating into work. *(Guide: background note on engines; Practice Q7d)*
