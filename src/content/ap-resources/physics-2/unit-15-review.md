## Big ideas of the unit

- **Quantum theory was needed.** Spectra, blackbody radiation and the photoelectric effect could not be explained by classical physics ([Topic 15.1](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/15-1-quantum-theory-wave-particle-duality-study-guide/)).
- **Light comes in photons.** Each has E = hf, momentum p = h/λ, no mass and no charge. Brighter light means more photons, not bigger ones.
- **Matter has waves.** λ = h/p. Quantum effects matter when λ is comparable to the size of the system.
- **Atoms have structure.** A small, massive nucleus (Z protons, A − Z neutrons) with electrons around it. In the Bohr model only orbits holding a whole number of electron wavelengths are allowed, so the energy is quantized ([Topic 15.2](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/15-2-bohr-model-atomic-structure-study-guide/)).
- **Spectra are fingerprints.** A photon is absorbed or emitted only if its energy equals a gap between levels ([Topic 15.3](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/15-3-emission-absorption-spectra-study-guide/)).
- **Hot objects glow with a continuous spectrum.** λ_max = b/T and P = σAT⁴; Planck's quantized light explained the curve ([Topic 15.4](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/15-4-blackbody-radiation-study-guide/)).
- **Two collisions prove photons.** In the photoelectric effect one photon is absorbed by one electron ([Topic 15.5](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/15-5-photoelectric-effect-study-guide/)); in Compton scattering a photon bounces off an electron, conserving energy and momentum ([Topic 15.6](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/15-6-compton-scattering-study-guide/)).
- **Nuclei change by the same conservation laws.** Mass converts to energy (E = mc²), decay is random with a fixed half-life ([Topic 15.7](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/15-7-fission-fusion-nuclear-decay-study-guide/)), and each decay type conserves A, charge and lepton number ([Topic 15.8](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/15-8-types-radioactive-decay-study-guide/)).

## Key relationships and methods

| Idea | Relationship or method | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Photon (15.1) | E = hf = hc/λ; p = h/λ; v = c/n in a medium | Using λ in a medium in E = hc/λ |
| Matter wave (15.1) | λ = h/p = h/√(2mK) | K in eV, not J |
| Bohr orbits (15.2) | ke²/r² = mv²/r; 2πr = nλ; r = n²r₁ | Speed falls as r grows |
| Transitions (15.3) | E_photon = \|ΔE\|; λ (nm) = 1240 ÷ E (eV) | Matching a gap exactly; ionization takes any E above the binding energy |
| Blackbody (15.4) | λ_max = b/T; P = σAT⁴ | Kelvin; T⁴ |
| Photoelectric (15.5) | K_max = hf − φ = eV_s | Intensity changes the number of electrons, not K_max |
| Compton (15.6) | Δλ = (h/mₑc)(1 − cos θ); conserve p in x and y | Δλ depends only on θ |
| Mass-energy (15.7) | E = Δmc²; 1 u ↔ 931 MeV | Mass is not conserved; nucleon number is |
| Decay rate (15.7) | N = N₀e^(−λt); λ = ln 2 / t½ | Expected values only |
| Decay types (15.8) | α: A − 4, Z − 2; β⁻: Z + 1 with ν̄; β⁺: Z − 1 with ν; γ: no change | Lepton number |
| Two products from rest | Equal and opposite p; K ∝ 1/m | The lighter product gets more energy |

## Question 1 (multiple choice · mixed)

*Topics 15.1, 15.5, 15.6.* Which pair of observations both give evidence that light is a stream of photons?

- (A) Light spreads out after a narrow slit; the maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons does not depend on intensity.
- (B) The maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons does not depend on intensity; X-rays scattered by electrons have a longer wavelength that grows with the scattering angle.
- (C) Light travels more slowly in glass than in air; scattered X-rays have a longer wavelength than the incident X-rays.
- (D) A thin oil film shows coloured fringes; light can be polarized.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** Both results need a photon of energy hf (and, for Compton scattering, momentum h/λ) interacting with one electron. A wave model predicts that brighter light gives faster electrons, and that scattered waves keep their wavelength.

- (A) Diffraction at a slit is wave behaviour.
- (C) A lower speed in glass fits a wave model; only the Compton half is photon evidence.
- (D) Interference and polarization are both wave behaviour.
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · mixed)

*Topics 15.3, 15.4.* Light from the hot, dense surface of a star passes through the cooler, thinner gas around it. The light reaching Earth has its peak intensity per unit wavelength at 500 nm. Which row is correct?

- (A) A continuous spectrum with no lines, because the gas re-emits what it absorbs; surface temperature about 5.8 × 10³ K
- (B) Bright lines on a dark background; surface temperature about 5.8 × 10³ K
- (C) A continuous spectrum with dark lines; surface temperature about 1.7 × 10⁻⁴ K
- (D) A continuous spectrum with dark lines; surface temperature about 5.8 × 10³ K

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (D).** The surface acts as a blackbody, so its spectrum is continuous, with T = b/λ_max = 2.898 × 10⁻³ ÷ 500 × 10⁻⁹ = 5796 K. Atoms in the cool gas absorb photons that match their energy gaps, leaving dark lines.

- (A) The gas re-emits in all directions, so little of that light continues towards Earth.
- (B) is an emission spectrum from a hot, thin gas viewed against a dark background.
- (C) inverts Wien's law (λ_max ÷ b).
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · mixed)

*Topics 15.2, 15.8.* A neutral atom of carbon-14 (¹⁴₆C) undergoes beta-minus decay. Just after the decay, before it gains or loses any electrons, what is the particle that remains?

- (A) ¹⁴₅B⁻: 5 protons, 9 neutrons, 6 electrons
- (B) ¹⁴₇N: 7 protons, 7 neutrons, 7 electrons
- (C) ¹⁴₇N⁺: 7 protons, 7 neutrons, 6 electrons
- (D) ¹⁴₆C⁺: 6 protons, 8 neutrons, 5 electrons

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** In the nucleus a neutron becomes a proton, emitting an electron and an antineutrino. Z rises to 7 (nitrogen) and A stays 14, so there are 7 neutrons. The 6 original electrons are still around the nucleus, so the charge is +7e − 6e = +e: a positive ion.

- (A) moves Z the wrong way. Beta-minus raises Z.
- (B) assumes the atom stays neutral. No electron was added.
- (D) treats the beta particle as an electron leaving the atom's outer electrons. It is created in the nucleus, and the element changes.
</details>

## Question 4 (constructed response · mixed)

*Topics 15.1, 15.2, 15.3, 15.6.* In the Bohr model of hydrogen, r_n = n²r₁ with r₁ = 5.29 × 10⁻¹¹ m, and E_n = −13.6 eV ÷ n².

(a) Calculate the wavelength of the photon emitted when the atom drops from n = 3 to n = 2. Name the part of the spectrum.
(b) Using the standing-wave condition, calculate the electron's de Broglie wavelength in the n = 2 orbit, and hence its speed.
(c) Calculate the momentum of the photon in (a). The atom (mass 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg) was at rest. Calculate its recoil speed.
(d) A photon of wavelength 300 nm hits an atom in the n = 2 state. Show that the atom is ionized and calculate the de Broglie wavelength of the freed electron.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** E₃ = −1.51 eV and E₂ = −3.40 eV, so E = 1.89 eV. λ = 1240 ÷ 1.889 = **656 nm**, **visible** (red).

**(b)** Two wavelengths fit around the orbit: λ = 2π(4r₁) ÷ 2 = 4πr₁ = **6.65 × 10⁻¹⁰ m**. v = h/(mₑλ) = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ ÷ (9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 6.648 × 10⁻¹⁰) = **1.09 × 10⁶ m/s**, half the n = 1 speed, as v ∝ 1/n requires.

**(c)** p = h/λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ ÷ 656 × 10⁻⁹ = **1.01 × 10⁻²⁷ kg·m/s**. Momentum is conserved, so the atom moves the opposite way with v = p/m = **0.605 m/s**. Its kinetic energy is negligible next to 1.89 eV.

**(d)** E = 1240 ÷ 300 = 4.13 eV, more than the 3.40 eV binding energy of n = 2, so the atom is **ionized**. K = 0.73 eV = 1.17 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. p = √(2mₑK) = 4.62 × 10⁻²⁵ kg·m/s, so λ = h/p = **1.43 × 10⁻⁹ m**.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Energy gap 1.89 eV |
| 1 | 656 nm, visible |
| 1 | λ = 2πr₂/2 with r₂ = 4r₁ |
| 1 | v = 1.09 × 10⁶ m/s |
| 1 | Photon momentum and recoil speed 0.605 m/s, opposite to the photon |
| 1 | 4.13 eV > 3.40 eV, so ionized, with K = 0.73 eV |
| 1 | K converted to joules and λ = 1.43 × 10⁻⁹ m |

Total: 7 points. Carry forward an error in the energy from (a) into (c) once.
</details>

## Question 5 (constructed response · mixed)

*Topics 15.1, 15.4, 15.5.* A lamp filament, modelled as a blackbody, has surface area 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ m² and runs at 2900 K.

(a) Calculate its peak wavelength and the total power it radiates.
(b) A filter passes only 450 nm light. 2.0 mW of it falls on a metal with work function 2.10 eV. Calculate the photons per second reaching the metal, K_max in eV, and the stopping potential.
(c) The filament is turned up to 3300 K. Calculate the factor by which its total power rises. A student says: "The filament is hotter, so the electrons from behind the filter will be faster." Evaluate the claim.
(d) The filter is removed. Explain why K_max now increases, even though the filament's peak wavelength is far longer than the metal's threshold wavelength.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** λ_max = 2.898 × 10⁻³ ÷ 2900 = **999 nm** (infrared). P = σAT⁴ = (5.67 × 10⁻⁸)(2.0 × 10⁻⁵)(2900)⁴ = **80.2 W**.

**(b)** E = hc/λ = 4.42 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = 2.76 eV. Photons per second = 2.0 × 10⁻³ ÷ 4.42 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **4.5 × 10¹⁵**. K_max = 2.76 − 2.10 = **0.66 eV**, so V_s = **0.66 V**.

**(c)** P ∝ T⁴: (3300/2900)⁴ = **1.68**. The claim is **wrong**. The filter fixes the photon energy at 2.76 eV, so K_max stays 0.66 eV. The hotter filament emits more at every wavelength, so more 450 nm photons arrive per second and more electrons are emitted: the current rises, not the speed.

**(d)** The spectrum is **continuous**. Even with its peak in the infrared, the filament emits some violet and near-ultraviolet light, with λ shorter than 450 nm. K_max is set by the most energetic photons present, for example 400 nm photons (3.10 eV) give 1.0 eV. Photons longer than λ₀ = 1240 ÷ 2.10 = 590 nm, including most of the output, eject nothing.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | λ_max = 999 nm |
| 1 | P = 80.2 W |
| 1 | 4.5 × 10¹⁵ photons per second |
| 1 | K_max = 0.66 eV and V_s = 0.66 V |
| 1 | Power factor 1.68 |
| 1 | Claim wrong: photon energy fixed by the filter; more photons give more current, not more K_max |
| 1 | (d) Continuous spectrum includes short wavelengths; K_max set by the most energetic photons |

Total: 7 points.
</details>

## Question 6 (constructed response · mixed)

*Topics 15.1, 15.5, 15.6.* An X-ray photon of wavelength 60.0 pm travels along +x and scatters off a free electron at rest. It is detected at 120° to the +x direction, above the x-axis.

(a) Calculate the wavelength of the scattered photon.
(b) Calculate the kinetic energy of the electron, in joules and in keV.
(c) Use conservation of momentum to find the magnitude and direction of the electron's momentum.
(d) Calculate the electron's de Broglie wavelength. Explain why the electron can be treated as free, when in the photoelectric effect the work function matters.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** Δλ = 2.43 pm × (1 − cos 120°) = 2.43 × 1.5 = 3.65 pm, so λ′ = **63.6 pm**.

**(b)** E = hc/λ = 3.315 × 10⁻¹⁵ J and E′ = hc/λ′ = 3.125 × 10⁻¹⁵ J. K = **1.90 × 10⁻¹⁶ J = 1.19 keV**.

**(c)** p = h/λ = 1.105 × 10⁻²³ kg·m/s; p′ = h/λ′ = 1.042 × 10⁻²³ kg·m/s.

- x: p_ex = p − p′cos 120° = 1.105 × 10⁻²³ + 0.521 × 10⁻²³ = 1.626 × 10⁻²³ kg·m/s.
- y: p_ey = 0 − p′sin 120° = −0.902 × 10⁻²³ kg·m/s.
- p_e = √(1.626² + 0.902²) × 10⁻²³ = **1.86 × 10⁻²³ kg·m/s**, at **29.0° below** the +x axis.

Check: p_e²/(2mₑ) = 1.90 × 10⁻¹⁶ J, which matches (b).

**(d)** λ = h/p_e = **35.7 pm**. The photon carries about 20.7 keV, thousands of times a typical work function of a few eV, so the energy binding an outer electron is negligible. In the photoelectric effect, visible or ultraviolet photons have only a few eV, so the work function is a large part of the energy.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | λ′ = 63.6 pm, using cos 120° = −0.5 |
| 1 | K = 1.90 × 10⁻¹⁶ J = 1.19 keV, from unrounded energies |
| 1 | Photon momenta from p = h/λ |
| 1 | Both components, with the y-component negative |
| 1 | 1.86 × 10⁻²³ kg·m/s at 29° below the +x axis |
| 1 | λ = 35.7 pm |
| 1 | Photon energy far larger than the binding energy, compared with the photoelectric case |

Total: 7 points.
</details>

## Question 7 (constructed response · mixed)

*Topics 15.1, 15.7, 15.8.* Nucleus P (A = 214, Z = 84), at rest, alpha decays to nucleus D. D then undergoes beta-minus decay to nucleus E. Invented atomic masses: P 213.99520 u; D 209.98420 u; ⁴He 4.00260 u. P has a half-life of 2.0 ms. Take each mass as proportional to its nucleon number when sharing energy.

(a) Write both decay equations, giving A and Z for D and E. Show that lepton number is conserved in the second.
(b) Calculate the energy released in the alpha decay, and the kinetic energy of the alpha particle.
(c) Sometimes D is left in an excited state 0.80 MeV above its ground state. Calculate the alpha particle's kinetic energy in this case, and the wavelength of the gamma photon D then emits.
(d) A sample starts with 6.0 × 10⁹ nuclei of P. Calculate the expected number of alpha particles emitted in the first 5.0 ms.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** ²¹⁴₈₄P → ²¹⁰₈₂D + ⁴₂He. Then ²¹⁰₈₂D → ²¹⁰₈₃E + ⁰₋₁e + ν̄. Lepton number: 0 before; (+1) + (−1) = 0 after.

**(b)** Δm = 213.99520 − 209.98420 − 4.00260 = 0.00840 u (electron masses cancel). Q = 0.00840 × 931 = **7.82 MeV**. Equal and opposite momenta, so K ∝ 1/m: K_α = 7.82 × 210/214 = **7.67 MeV** (D gets 0.15 MeV).

**(c)** Only 7.82 − 0.80 = 7.02 MeV is shared, so K_α = 7.02 × 210/214 = **6.89 MeV**. Gamma: λ = 1240 eV·nm ÷ 0.80 × 10⁶ eV = 1.55 × 10⁻³ nm = **1.55 pm**. A and Z of D do not change.

**(d)** 5.0 ms is 2.5 half-lives. Remaining = 6.0 × 10⁹ × (½)^2.5 = 1.06 × 10⁹. Decayed = **4.9 × 10⁹**, so about 4.9 × 10⁹ alpha particles.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Alpha equation with D: A = 210, Z = 82 |
| 1 | Beta-minus equation with E: A = 210, Z = 83, electron and antineutrino |
| 1 | Lepton number 0 = (+1) + (−1) |
| 1 | Q = 7.82 MeV from the mass decrease |
| 1 | K_α = 7.67 MeV, using equal momenta |
| 1 | K_α = 6.89 MeV in the excited case |
| 1 | Gamma wavelength 1.55 pm |
| 1 | 4.9 × 10⁹ decays |

Total: 8 points. Do not award the lepton point for "electron and neutrino".
</details>
