Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/11-4-electric-power-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/11-4-electric-power-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define power as the rate of energy transfer and explain why 1 A × 1 V = 1 W. *(Guide: "Power as a rate of energy transfer")*
- I can describe where energy enters a circuit, where it leaves and where it can be stored. *(Guide: "Energy into, out of and within a circuit")*
- I can explain that a bulb's brightness increases with its power, and that a rating applies only at the stated ΔV. *(Guide: "Bulb brightness"; Worked example 2)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can derive P = IΔV from ΔE = qΔV and I = q/Δt. *(Guide: "Deriving P = IΔV")*
- I can derive P = I²R and P = ΔV²/R using Ohm's law. *(Guide: "Two forms for a resistor")*
- I can calculate power and energy with correct unit prefixes and time in seconds. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can predict the factor by which power changes when I, ΔV or R changes. *(Practice Q2, Q5d)*
- I can derive a symbolic expression for the power of a wire in terms of ρ, L, d and ΔV. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7a)*
- I can sketch P against I, and P against R at fixed ΔV and at fixed I. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can compare the brightness of bulbs that share the same ΔV, or the same current, and justify which is brighter. *(Practice Q3, Q4)*
- I can show that energy is conserved by comparing the power from the battery with the total power dissipated. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7c)*
- I can evaluate a claim such as "larger resistance always means more power". *(Practice Q6c)*
