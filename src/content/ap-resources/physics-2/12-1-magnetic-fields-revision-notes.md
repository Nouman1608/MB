## Recap

- A **magnetic field B** is a vector field (unit: tesla, T). It is used to find forces on moving charges, currents and magnetic materials.
- Fields come from **dipoles**, never from a lone pole. Cutting a magnet gives two smaller dipoles.
- **Like poles repel; unlike poles attract.**
- B points the way the **north end of a compass** points. A compass (a dipole) tends to line up with the field.
- A dipole's field gets **weaker with distance**.
- Dipoles come from **moving charge**: electrons orbiting and spinning in atoms.
- **Permanent** and **induced** magnetism are both alignment of dipoles in a system. Induced alignment disappears when the external field goes.
- **Earth** is modelled as a dipole. The magnetic pole near the geographic North Pole is a magnetic **south** pole.

## Key relationships

| Idea | What to remember |
|---|---|
| Field-line rules | direction = tangent and arrow; closer lines = stronger B; lines never cross; every line is a closed loop |
| Bar magnet | outside: away from N, into S; inside: S to N |
| Adding fields | B_total is the vector sum; for perpendicular parts, B = √(B₁² + B₂²), angle = tan⁻¹(B₁/B₂) |
| Vacuum permeability | μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A ≈ 1.26 × 10⁻⁶ T·m/A (a constant) |
| Materials | ferromagnetic μ ≫ μ₀; paramagnetic μ slightly > μ₀; diamagnetic μ slightly < μ₀ |

| Material type | Examples | In a field | Afterwards |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ferromagnetic | Fe, Ni, Co | domains align, strong attraction | can stay magnetised |
| Paramagnetic | Al, Ti, Mg | weak alignment with B, weak attraction | alignment lost |
| Diamagnetic | all materials (main effect in Cu, water) | weak alignment against B, weak repulsion | nothing left |

## Assumptions behind the answers

- "Direction of B" means the direction the north end of a small compass would point.
- Earth's field is treated as a **dipole field**; real Earth's field is only approximately a dipole.
- A material's permeability μ is **not** a constant: it varies with temperature, orientation and field strength.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Stopping field lines at the poles. Continue them inside, S to N.
2. Saying a cut magnet gives a separate N piece and S piece.
3. Calling Earth's Arctic magnetic pole a north pole.
4. Saying magnets attract all metals.
5. Treating field lines as the paths of moving charges.
6. Giving a magnetic force to a charge at rest.

## Quick self-check

1. Earth's field somewhere is 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ T. What is that in μT? *(50 μT)*
2. Which way does B point inside a bar magnet? *(From the S end to the N end.)*
3. A sample is weakly pushed away from both poles of a strong magnet. What kind of material is it? *(Diamagnetic.)*
