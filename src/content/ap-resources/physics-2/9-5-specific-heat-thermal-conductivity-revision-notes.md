## Recap

- **Specific heat c** is the energy needed to raise 1 kg of a material by 1 K. It is intrinsic: the same for a bead and a block of the same material.
- c depends on the atoms: how many particles are in a kilogram and how many ways they can store energy.
- For solids and liquids W ≈ 0, so the energy supplied by heating becomes internal energy: ΔU ≈ Q.
- **Thermal conductivity k** measures how easily a material conducts energy. Metals (free electrons) have high k; non-metal solids lower; still gases very low.
- In an isolated system, objects in thermal contact reach a common temperature, and the total energy is conserved.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Q = mcΔT | Q in J, m in kg, c in J/(kg·K), ΔT in K or °C | find the energy for a temperature change, or c, m or ΔT |
| Q = PΔt | P in W, Δt in s | find the energy from a heater |
| slope of T against Q = 1/(mc) | K/J | read c from a graph |
| slope of T against t = P/(mc) | K/s | read c from heater data |
| Σ mcΔT = 0 | for an isolated system | find a final (equilibrium) temperature |
| Q/Δt = kAΔT/L | rate in W, k in W/(m·K), A in m², L in m | find the rate of conduction through a slab |

Factor-of-change reminders for conduction: double A → rate × 2; double ΔT → rate × 2; double L → rate × ½.

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- c is **independent of temperature** (the course model).
- **No change of state** during heating or cooling.
- Mixing problems: the container is **insulated**, so no energy leaves the system.
- Conduction: **steady state**, uniform slab, energy flows straight through.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Reading high c as "heats quickly".** High c means slow temperature change.
2. **Adding 273 to ΔT.** A change of 10 °C is a change of 10 K.
3. **Taking the simple average** as the final temperature when the mc values differ.
4. **A final temperature outside the starting range.** That signals a sign error.
5. **Thinking a thicker slab conducts more.** Rate ∝ 1/L.
6. **Calling k the rate.** k is the material property; the rate also needs A, ΔT and L.
7. **Saying metal "is colder".** At the same temperature, metal only *feels* colder because it conducts faster.

## Quick self-check

1. How much energy warms 0.50 kg of aluminium (c = 900 J/(kg·K)) by 40 K? *(18,000 J)*
2. A rod's length is halved; A and ΔT stay the same. What happens to the rate of conduction? *(It doubles)*
3. Equal masses of the same liquid at 60 °C and 20 °C are mixed in an insulated flask. Final temperature? *(40 °C, because the two mc values are equal)*
