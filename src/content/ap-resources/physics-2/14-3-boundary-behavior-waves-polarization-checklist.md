## Understanding

- I can explain that a wave reaching a boundary between two media is partly reflected and partly transmitted, and that the two share the energy. *(Guide: "A wave meets a boundary"; Practice Q7d)*
- I can state when a reflected pulse is inverted and when it is upright, using the change in wave speed. *(Guide: "Inverted or upright?"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why the transmitted pulse is never inverted. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why the frequency of a wave does not change at a boundary. *(Guide: "Frequency stays the same"; Practice Q2)*
- I can describe a polarized and an unpolarized transverse wave, and explain why a longitudinal wave cannot be polarized. *(Guide: "Polarization"; Practice Q3)*
- I can name three ways a wave can be polarized: by a filter or slot, by reflection and by refraction. *(Guide: "Three ways to polarize a wave")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can use v = √(F_T/μ) to compare the speeds on two strings under the same tension. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can find the new wavelength or pulse width after a boundary using λ₂/λ₁ = v₂/v₁. *(Practice Q2, Q5, Q7)*
- I can calculate intensity from I = P/A and power from P = IA. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can find the intensity after one or two ideal filters, for parallel and crossed axes. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q4, Q6)*
- I can sketch the reflected and transmitted pulses at a junction, showing orientation and width. *(Guide: Figure 1, Worked example 1; Practice Q5c, Q7a)*

## Reasoning

- I can use observations of reflected and transmitted pulses to decide which medium is faster. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can use a rotating-filter experiment as evidence that light is transverse and sound is longitudinal. *(Practice Q6b)*
- I can explain why turning a single filter does not change how much unpolarized light gets through. *(Practice Q6c)*
