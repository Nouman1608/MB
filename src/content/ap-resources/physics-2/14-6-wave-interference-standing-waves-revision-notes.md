## Recap

- **Interference** is what happens when two or more pulses or waves overlap. They travel through each other; they do not bounce off.
- **Superposition:** while they overlap, add the displacements, with signs.
- **Constructive:** displacements in the same direction. **Destructive:** opposite directions.
- **Beats:** two waves of slightly different frequency drift in and out of phase, so the loudness rises and falls periodically.
- **Standing wave:** two waves travelling in opposite directions in a confined region. **Nodes** never move; **antinodes** have the largest amplitude. Neighbouring nodes are λ/2 apart.
- **Boundary conditions:** fixed string end or closed pipe end → node; loose string end or open pipe end → antinode.
- **Fundamental (first harmonic):** the longest possible wavelength. Harmonic number n = f/f₁.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | When to use it |
|---|---|
| f_beat = \|f₁ − f₂\| | two nearby frequencies sounding together |
| v = fλ | every standing-wave calculation |
| v = √(F_T/μ) | wave speed on a string |
| λₙ = 2L/n, fₙ = n·v/(2L), n = 1, 2, 3 … | fixed–fixed string, open–open pipe, loose–loose string |
| λₙ = 4L/n, fₙ = n·v/(4L), n = 1, 3, 5 … | pipe closed at one end, string fixed at one end and loose at the other |
| node-to-node = λ/2; node-to-antinode = λ/4 | reading lengths from a diagram |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Waves in a given medium all travel at the same speed, whatever their frequency.
- Pipes are treated as ideal: the antinode sits exactly at the open end.
- The speed of sound is 340 m/s unless the question gives another value (it rises with air temperature).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Adding sizes, not signed displacements.** An upward 3 cm pulse and a downward 1 cm pulse give +2 cm, not 4 cm.
2. **Beat frequency as a sum or an average.** It is the difference.
3. **Closed end as an antinode.** It is a node.
4. **Even harmonics in a closed pipe.** Only odd ones exist.
5. **Nodes a whole λ apart.** They are λ/2 apart.
6. **Forgetting v.** Frequency depends on v as well as on L.

## Quick self-check

1. Two notes of 440 Hz and 437 Hz sound together. What is the beat frequency? *(3 Hz)*
2. A pipe closed at one end is 0.17 m long and v = 340 m/s. Find its two lowest resonant frequencies. *(500 Hz and 1500 Hz)*
3. A string fixed at both ends is 0.80 m long. Give the wavelengths of its first two harmonics. *(1.6 m and 0.80 m)*
4. Two pulses pass through each other. What are their shapes afterwards? *(Exactly as before.)*
