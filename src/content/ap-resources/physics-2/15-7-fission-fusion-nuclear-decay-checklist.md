## Understanding

- I can explain what the strong force does, which particles it acts between and why it only matters inside a nucleus. *(Guide: "What holds a nucleus together")*
- I can name the four quantities conserved in every nuclear reaction and say why mass is not one of them. *(Guide: "Rules that every nuclear reaction obeys"; Practice Q4)*
- I can describe fusion and fission and give one balanced example of each. *(Guide: "Fusion and fission")*
- I can explain binding energy as the energy needed to separate a nucleus into its nucleons. *(Guide: "Binding energy")*
- I can explain why some nuclei fission spontaneously while others need an energy input. *(Guide: "Fusion and fission")*
- I can explain why the decay of one nucleus is unpredictable but a large sample follows a predictable curve. *(Guide: "Radioactive decay is random"; Practice Q3)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can find a missing product in a nuclear equation by balancing A and Z. *(Practice Q1)*
- I can calculate the energy released from a mass change, in joules or using 931 MeV per u. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 3; Practice Q4)*
- I can use λ = ln 2 / t½ and N = N₀e^(−λt) to find the amount left or the time taken, with consistent units. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can plan a half-life measurement that deals with background radiation, and find λ from the gradient of a ln R against t graph. *(Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can use momentum conservation to explain why the lighter product of a reaction gets more kinetic energy, and calculate each share. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7)*
- I can use the binding energy per nucleon curve to decide whether fusion or fission of given nuclei releases energy. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
- I can evaluate a claim that mass increases or is conserved when energy is released. *(Practice Q7d)*
