Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/15-4-blackbody-radiation-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/15-4-blackbody-radiation-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain that all matter turns some of its internal energy into electromagnetic radiation, and why hotter objects radiate more. *(Guide: "Everything glows")*
- I can define a blackbody and explain why a body at constant temperature that absorbs radiation must also emit it. *(Guide: "The blackbody model")*
- I can state that a blackbody's spectrum is continuous and depends only on its temperature. *(Guide: "The blackbody spectrum"; Practice Q7c)*
- I can explain why classical physics failed at short wavelengths and what Planck assumed about light energy. *(Guide: "Why classical physics failed"; Practice Q7d)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can sketch intensity per unit wavelength against wavelength for two temperatures, with the correct shape, order and peak positions. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6)*
- I can use λ_max = b/T to find a temperature or a peak wavelength, with T in kelvin and λ in metres. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5b, Q7a)*
- I can use P = σAT⁴ to find the power, the area or the temperature of a blackbody. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5, Q7b)*
- I can compare two blackbodies using ratios of T⁴ and of area. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3, Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can justify that a hotter blackbody emits more at every wavelength and peaks at a shorter wavelength. *(Practice Q2, Q6b)*
- I can link the total power to the area under the curve. *(Practice Q6c)*
- I can explain the colours of hot objects and stars using where their curves peak. *(Guide: "The blackbody spectrum"; Practice Q7c)*
