## Understanding

- I can explain why a double-slit pattern needs both diffraction and interference. *(Guide: "Two slits, one light source")*
- I can explain why Young's double-slit result is evidence that light behaves as a wave. *(Guide: "Two slits, one light source"; Practice Q1)*
- I can describe a real double-slit pattern as fringes inside a single-slit envelope, and explain a missing fringe. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q7c)*
- I can explain why a grating gives sharper, brighter maxima than two slits with the same d. *(Guide: "Diffraction gratings"; Practice Q7e)*
- I can describe the white-light pattern from a grating: white centre, red farthest out in each order. *(Guide: Figure 3; Practice Q4)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can show that the path difference is d sin θ and use it to say whether a point is bright or dark. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can use y_max = mλL/d to find fringe positions, spacing or d, and check the small-angle condition. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6, Q7a)*
- I can find d from lines per millimetre and use d sin θ = mλ with tan θ = y/L at large angles. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can find the highest order a grating produces. *(Practice Q5d)*
- I can plan a measurement of d or λ and analyse it with a straight-line graph. *(Guide: "Planning a measurement"; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict the factor by which the fringe spacing changes when λ, L or d changes. *(Practice Q3)*
- I can predict what happens to the pattern when one slit is covered. *(Practice Q7d)*
- I can decide whether two grating orders overlap. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
