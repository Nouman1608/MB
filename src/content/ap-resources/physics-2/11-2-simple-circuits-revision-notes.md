## Recap

- A **circuit** is made of electrical **loops** containing elements: wires, batteries, resistors, bulbs, capacitors, switches, ammeters and voltmeters.
- A **closed loop** is a complete path through which charges could flow.
- One element can belong to **several loops**. Count loops without the battery too.
- **Schematics** show connections only. Wires are ideal, so length and shape do not matter. Crossing wires without a dot are not connected.
- Schematics use **conventional current**: out of the long (+) line of the battery, back into the short (−) line.
- **Arrangement matters.** The same parts wired in one loop or in separate branches behave differently.

## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Closed circuit | complete loop with a source | charges could flow |
| Open circuit | a break anywhere in the loop | no current anywhere in that loop |
| Short circuit | path with no change in potential, connected across an element | a bypassed bulb or resistor has zero potential difference and carries no current |
| Variable element | normal symbol with a diagonal arrow through it | its value can be adjusted (for example a dimmer) |

| Meter | How it is connected | Ideal behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Ammeter (circle with A) | in the loop: the current passes through it | acts like a plain wire |
| Voltmeter (circle with V) | across the element: one lead on each end | lets no charge through |

## Assumptions behind the answers

- Wires and closed switches are **ideal**: no change in potential along them.
- Meters are **ideal** unless the question says otherwise.
- Schematics show conventional current unless told otherwise.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Saying current reaches the gap and stops.** An open loop has no current anywhere.
2. **Letting a shorted bulb share the current.** It has no potential difference, so no current.
3. **Forgetting loops without the battery** when counting loops.
4. **Putting an ammeter across an element.** That shorts it.
5. **Putting a voltmeter in the loop.** That opens the circuit.
6. **Reading meaning into wire length or position** on a schematic.

## Quick self-check

1. A switch in a single loop with a battery and two bulbs is opened. Which bulbs carry current? *(Neither)*
2. A battery is connected to two bulbs, each in its own branch. How many closed loops are there? *(3: battery with each bulb, and the two bulbs together)*
3. A plain wire is connected across one of two bulbs in a single loop. What happens to that bulb? *(It goes out: short circuit, no potential difference across it)*
