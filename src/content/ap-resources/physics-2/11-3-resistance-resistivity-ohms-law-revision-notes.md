## Recap

- **Resistance R** measures how strongly an object opposes the movement of charge. R = ΔV/I, unit ohm (1 Ω = 1 V/A).
- **Resistivity ρ** is a property of the **material**, set by its atomic structure. Resistance is a property of one **object** and also depends on its shape.
- For most conductors, resistivity **increases with temperature**: hotter atoms vibrate more and get in the way of drifting electrons more often.
- **Ohmic** elements keep the same R at every current, so I ∝ ΔV. In the course model, an ohmic material's resistivity does not change with temperature.
- A **filament bulb** is non-ohmic: its R rises as it heats up.
- Resistors convert electrical energy into **thermal energy**, warming the resistor and its surroundings.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| R = ρL/A | ρ in Ω·m, L in m, A in m² | find R from material and shape |
| A = π(d/2)² | d = diameter in m | get the area of a round wire |
| I = ΔV/R | I in A, ΔV in V, R in Ω | link current, potential difference and resistance |
| slope of I against ΔV = 1/R | A/V | read R from a graph |
| slope of R against L = ρ/A | Ω/m | find resistivity from lab data |

| Change to a wire | Effect on R |
|---|---|
| Length × 2 | × 2 |
| Area × 2 | ÷ 2 |
| Diameter × 2 | ÷ 4 |
| Higher-resistivity material | larger, in proportion to ρ |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The conductor has **uniform** cross-section along its length.
- "Resistor" means an **ohmic** element unless the question says otherwise.
- Temperature is constant unless the question is about heating.
- Connecting wires have negligible resistance.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Slope = R on an I–ΔV graph.** It is 1/R; steeper means smaller R.
2. **Diameter used as radius**, or forgetting to square it.
3. **mm² → m² with 10⁻³.** The factor is 10⁻⁶.
4. **"Bigger ΔV, bigger R."** For an ohmic element R stays the same.
5. **"Thicker wire, more resistance."** Thicker means less.
6. **Calling a curved graph through the origin "ohmic".** It must be straight.

## Quick self-check

1. A wire is replaced by one of the same metal with twice the length and twice the diameter. By what factor does R change? *(× 1/2)*
2. A resistor carries 0.30 A with 9.0 V across it. What is its resistance? *(30 Ω)*
3. An I–ΔV line through the origin has a slope of 0.050 A/V. What is R? *(20 Ω)*
4. Why does a lamp filament's resistance rise as it glows? *(Its temperature rises, so the resistivity of the metal rises.)*
