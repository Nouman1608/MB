## Understanding

- I can use ΔU = qΔV to describe where charges gain and lose electric potential energy in a circuit. *(Guide: "Energy carried by moving charge"; Practice Q7d)*
- I can explain why the loop rule is a consequence of conservation of energy. *(Guide: "The loop rule")*
- I can explain why the loop rule also applies to loops with no battery, and what that says about parallel branches. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why the share of potential difference in a single loop depends on resistance, not on position. *(Practice Q6b)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can write a loop equation with the correct sign for every battery and resistor, going either way round the loop. *(Guide: "Sign rules"; Practice Q2)*
- I can include internal resistance in a loop equation and find a terminal voltage. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can solve a loop with two opposing batteries and check the energy balance. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can use loops with and without a battery to find unknown resistances, emfs or potentials. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4, Q7)*
- I can derive symbolic expressions, such as the potential at a point between two resistors, from the loop rule. *(Practice Q6c, Q7b)*

## Reasoning

- I can sketch a graph of electric potential against position around a loop, with rises, falls and flat sections in the right places. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q6a)*
- I can read potentials from a graph or table and deduce resistances, the current or the internal resistance. *(Practice Q3)*
- I can evaluate a claim about a circuit using the loop rule and energy conservation. *(Practice Q6b, Q7d)*
