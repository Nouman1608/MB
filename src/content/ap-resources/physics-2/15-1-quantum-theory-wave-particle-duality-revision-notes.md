## Recap

- Quantum theory was developed because classical physics could not explain **atomic spectra**, **blackbody radiation** or the **photoelectric effect**. It is needed at atomic and subatomic scales.
- Light can be modelled as a wave **and** as a stream of **photons**. A photon is massless, neutral and has energy proportional to its frequency.
- Photons travel in straight lines unless they interact with matter. In a vacuum all photons travel at c; in a medium, at c/n.
- **Wave-particle duality:** in a dim two-slit experiment, each photon or electron arrives as one dot, but many dots build up an interference pattern.
- Every particle has a **de Broglie wavelength**. Wave effects show up only when λ is comparable to the size of the system.
- In a **bound system** (such as an electron in an atom), energy and momentum are **quantized**: only certain values are allowed.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| E = hf = hc/λ | h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s = 4.14 × 10⁻¹⁵ eV·s; λ is the vacuum wavelength | find the energy of one photon |
| 1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J | | switch between eV and J |
| N/t = P/E | P in W, E in J | count photons per second in a beam |
| v = c/n, λ_medium = λ/n | f and E unchanged | describe light in a medium |
| λ = h/p = h/(mv) | p in kg·m/s, λ in m | find a particle's de Broglie wavelength |
| λ = h/√(2mqΔV) | particle accelerated from rest through ΔV | link wavelength to accelerating voltage |

Functional dependence to remember: E ∝ f ∝ 1/λ for photons; λ ∝ 1/p for particles; λ ∝ 1/√ΔV for a particle accelerated from rest.

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Speeds are much less than c, so p = mv and K = ½mv² (check v/c if unsure).
- Particles start from rest when accelerated through ΔV.
- Wavelengths in E = hc/λ are vacuum wavelengths.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Thinking intensity sets photon energy.** Frequency sets it; intensity sets the number of photons.
2. **Giving photons mass or charge.**
3. **Using the in-medium wavelength in E = hc/λ.** Frequency, and so photon energy, does not change in a medium.
4. **Inverting the de Broglie relation.** Heavier or faster means shorter λ.
5. **Mixing eV and J.** Convert before using h in J·s.
6. **Saying an electron moves along a wavy path.** The wave describes where it is likely to be detected.

## Quick self-check

1. What is the energy of a 620 nm photon, in eV? *(3.21 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, which is 2.01 eV)*
2. Find the de Broglie wavelength of an electron moving at 2.0 × 10⁶ m/s. *(3.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ m)*
3. A particle's momentum doubles. What happens to its de Broglie wavelength? *(It halves.)*
