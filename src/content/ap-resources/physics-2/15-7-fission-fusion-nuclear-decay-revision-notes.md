## Recap

- The **strong force** acts between nucleons, dominates at nuclear distances (about 10⁻¹⁵ m) and is negligible beyond a few of those distances.
- **Fusion:** small nuclei join into a larger one (plus particles). **Fission:** a nucleus splits into smaller nuclei (plus particles, usually neutrons).
- Either process releases energy when the products have a **higher binding energy per nucleon**. The peak of the curve is near iron.
- Some heavy nuclei fission **spontaneously**; others need an **energy input**, such as absorbing a neutron.
- Released energy appears as **kinetic energy** of the products or as **photons**.
- **Decay is random** for one nucleus. Probability makes a large sample predictable.
- Half-lives range from fractions of a second to billions of years. You are given them; you do not memorise them.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| ΣA before = ΣA after; ΣZ before = ΣZ after | A = nucleon number, Z = charge number | find a missing product |
| E = mc², Q = Δm c² | c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s | find energy released from a mass decrease |
| 1 u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg = 931 MeV/c² | 1 MeV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ J | convert mass in u straight to MeV |
| p_before = p_after | from rest: equal and opposite p | share energy: K ∝ 1/m for two products |
| λ = ln 2 / t½ | λ in s⁻¹ (or h⁻¹, etc.) | link the decay constant and half-life |
| N = N₀e^(−λt) = N₀(½)^(t/t½) | N = undecayed nuclei | predict the amount left or, with logs, an age |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The sample is **large**, so the actual number left is very close to the predicted value.
- Count rates have the **background subtracted** before you use them.
- Reactants start **at rest** unless the question says otherwise.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Calling mass conserved.** Nucleon number is conserved; mass decreases when energy is released.
2. **Treating binding energy as stored energy.** It is the energy needed to pull the nucleus apart.
3. **"Two half-lives = all gone."** It leaves ¼.
4. **Mixing time units.** λ in h⁻¹ needs t in hours.
5. **Sharing energy equally.** The lighter product gets more kinetic energy.
6. **Forgetting neutrons** when balancing a fission equation.

## Quick self-check

1. What fraction of a sample is left after 3 half-lives? *(⅛)*
2. A source has a half-life of 10 s. What is λ? *(0.0693 s⁻¹)*
3. A reaction loses 0.0100 u of mass. How much energy is released? *(9.31 MeV)*
4. Balance: ¹₀n + ²³⁵₉₂U → ¹⁴¹₅₆Ba + ⁹²₃₆Kr + ? *(3 ¹₀n)*
