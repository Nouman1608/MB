## Understanding

- I can describe the three things that can happen to light at a boundary: reflection, transmission and absorption. *(Guide: "Light at a boundary")*
- I can state when a reflected ray has a 180° phase change and when it has none, and explain that refraction never changes the phase. *(Guide: "Phase changes on reflection"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why thin-film colours only appear when the thickness is comparable to the wavelength of light. *(Guide: "Light at a boundary" and FAQ)*
- I can explain that the amount of interference depends on thickness, wavelength, phase changes and viewing angle. *(Guide: "Two reflected rays")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can draw a labelled diagram of the two reflected rays from a film and mark the phase change at each surface. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6a)*
- I can find the wavelength of light inside a film using λ_film = λ/n. *(Guide: "The wavelength inside the film"; Practice Q2)*
- I can find which visible wavelengths a film reflects strongly or weakly at normal incidence. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate the minimum thickness of a quarter-wave antireflection coating. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can use two measured reflection minima to find the thickness of a film. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain the black region and the colour bands of a soap film using thickness and phase changes. *(Guide: "Soap bubbles and oil films", Worked example 3; Practice Q4)*
- I can justify why a coating's index must lie between the indices of air and the lens. *(Guide: Worked example 2c; Practice Q6)*
- I can predict which way a reflection minimum moves when a film gets thinner. *(Practice Q7d)*
- I can explain where the energy goes when reflected light cancels. *(Guide: "Antireflection coatings" and FAQ)*
