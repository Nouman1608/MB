## Understanding

- I can describe Compton scattering as a collision between a photon and a free electron at rest, in which the photon survives with less energy. *(Guide: "A photon that bounces")*
- I can state what happens to a photon's energy, frequency, wavelength, momentum and speed when it is scattered. *(Guide: "The momentum of a photon"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why a classical wave model predicts no wavelength shift, and why the observed shift is evidence for photons. *(Guide: "A photon that bounces"; Practice Q7d)*
- I can explain why the effect is studied with X-rays rather than visible light. *(Guide: "The Compton shift equation")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can use p = h/λ to find the momentum of a photon. *(Guide: "The momentum of a photon"; Practice Q5d)*
- I can use Δλ = (h/m_e c)(1 − cos θ) at any angle, including angles above 90°. *(Guide: table; Practice Q2, Q3)*
- I can use energy conservation to find the kinetic energy of the recoiling electron without rounding too early. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can apply momentum conservation in x and y to find the electron's momentum and direction. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can choose axes that make Compton data linear and read λ and h/(m_e c) from the graph. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can draw before-and-after momentum diagrams for a scattering event, with the electron on the correct side. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6a)*
- I can compare two scattering events by angle or by starting wavelength, using absolute and fractional changes. *(Guide: "Comparing scattering situations"; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why an electron can end up with more momentum than the incoming photon without breaking conservation of momentum. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6c)*
