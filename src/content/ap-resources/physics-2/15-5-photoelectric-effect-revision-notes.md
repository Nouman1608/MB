Short on time? This page is the recap. For the apparatus diagram, the graph and the worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/15-5-photoelectric-effect-study-guide/).

## Recap

- The **photoelectric effect** is the emission of electrons when electromagnetic radiation falls on a photoactive material.
- The **work function φ** is the minimum energy needed to remove an electron from the material. The exam gives you its value.
- Emission needs a minimum frequency, the **threshold frequency f₀**. At or above f₀, electrons are emitted even in dim light.
- K_max depends on frequency, not on the number of photons. This is evidence that light comes in photons.
- In the stopping-potential experiment, the collector is made negative relative to the emitter and the potential difference is raised until the current is zero.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| E = hf = hc/λ | h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s = 4.14 × 10⁻¹⁵ eV·s; hc = 1240 eV·nm | find photon energy |
| K_max = hf − φ | all energies in the same unit (J or eV) | find K_max or φ |
| f₀ = φ/h, λ₀ = hc/φ | Hz, m (or nm) | find the threshold |
| eV_s = K_max | V_s in V; K_max in eV equals V_s in V | link stopping potential to K_max |
| 1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J | | convert units |

| On a graph of K_max against f | It gives |
|---|---|
| Slope | h (the same for every material) |
| Horizontal intercept | f₀ |
| Vertical intercept (extended) | −φ |

For V_s against f, the slope is h/e and the vertical intercept is −φ/e.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Brighter light, faster electrons.** No: brighter light gives more electrons per second at the same K_max.
2. **Saving up energy.** One electron cannot combine energy from two photons. Below f₀ nothing is emitted.
3. **Treating K_max ∝ f.** The line does not go through the origin.
4. **Wavelength the wrong way round.** Emission needs λ shorter than λ₀.
5. **Mixing eV and J** in one equation.
6. **Reading the dashed extension below f₀ as real electrons.**

## Quick self-check

1. Light of 300 nm falls on a metal with φ = 3.10 eV. What is K_max? *(1240 ÷ 300 = 4.13 eV, so K_max = 1.03 eV)*
2. The intensity of that light is tripled. What happens to K_max and to the current? *(K_max unchanged; current about three times larger)*
3. A metal has φ = 2.48 eV. What is its threshold wavelength? *(1240 ÷ 2.48 = 500 nm)*
