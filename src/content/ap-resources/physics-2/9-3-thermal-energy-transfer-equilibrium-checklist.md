## Understanding

- I can say what it means for two systems to be in thermal contact, and use "heating" and "cooling" for energy moving into and out of a system. *(Guide: "Systems in thermal contact"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain the difference between temperature, internal energy and heating. *(Guide: "Systems in thermal contact"; Practice Q3)*
- I can describe conduction, convection and radiation, and say which of them need matter. *(Guide: "Three thermal processes"; Practice Q2)*
- I can state that net energy moves spontaneously from the higher-temperature system to the lower-temperature one, whatever their internal energies. *(Guide: "Why energy flows from hot to cold"; Practice Q3, Q7b)*
- I can describe thermal equilibrium as no net energy transfer, while atoms keep colliding. *(Guide: "Thermal equilibrium", Worked example 3; Practice Q7d)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can check momentum and kinetic energy in a collision between two atoms, and find the energy transferred. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can use K_avg = (3/2)k_B T to compare the average energy of atoms in two systems. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*
- I can find the final temperature and the energy transferred when two samples of the same gas reach equilibrium. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4, Q7c)*
- I can sketch the speed distributions of a gas at two temperatures and at the final equilibrium temperature. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can sketch temperature against time for two systems approaching equilibrium. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q7e)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain, using collisions, why net energy flows from hot to cold even though single collisions can go either way. *(Guide: Worked example 1b; Practice Q5d, Q6b)*
- I can correct the claim that energy flows from the system with more internal energy. *(Practice Q3, Q7b)*
- I can explain why a vacuum gap allows only radiation. *(Guide: "Three thermal processes"; Practice Q2)*
