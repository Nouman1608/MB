## Recap

- **Electric potential V** is the electric potential energy per unit charge at a point. It is a property of the point, not of the test charge. V is zero infinitely far away.
- V is a **scalar**: for several point charges, add each kq/r with its sign.
- **Potential difference** ΔV between two points is the change in U_E per coulomb when a charge moves between them. Batteries keep a potential difference by separating charge chemically.
- A conductor in equilibrium is at **one potential** throughout. Conductors joined together share electrons until their surfaces are at the **same potential**, not until they hold equal charge.
- The **average field** between two points is |ΔV| divided by the distance along the field. E points toward **decreasing** V.
- **Equipotential lines** are perpendicular to the field. Close lines mean a strong field. Moving along an isoline, the field does no work.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| V = U_E / q | V in volts, 1 V = 1 J/C | link potential and potential energy |
| V = Σ kqᵢ / rᵢ | k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C², q with sign, r in m | find V from up to four point charges |
| ΔU_E = qΔV | J; q with sign | find the energy change when a charge moves |
| \|E\| = \|ΔV / Δr\| | V/m = N/C; Δr along the field | find the average field and its size |

| Charge released at rest | Moves toward | Its U_E |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | lower V (along E) | decreases |
| Negative | higher V (against E) | decreases |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- V = 0 infinitely far from all charges.
- Charges are **point charges**, or spheres that act like point charges from outside.
- Conductors are in **electrostatic equilibrium** (no charge is still moving).
- |ΔV/Δr| gives an **average** field; it equals the field everywhere only where the field is uniform.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Treating V as a vector.** No components, no angles.
2. **Losing the sign of q** in kq/r.
3. **Using r²** in the potential. That is the field formula.
4. **"V = 0 so E = 0."** Midway between +q and −q, V = 0 but E ≠ 0.
5. **Field pointing uphill.** E points toward lower V.
6. **"Equal charge after contact."** Equal potential, not equal charge.

## Quick self-check

1. What is the potential 0.30 m from a +2.0 nC point charge? *(60 V)*
2. Two parallel equipotentials at 0 V and 12 V are 4.0 mm apart. What is the field between them, and which way does it point? *(3.0 × 10³ V/m, from the 12 V line toward the 0 V line)*
3. How much work does the field do on a charge carried along an equipotential line? *(Zero)*
