## Recap

- A **junction** is a point where three or more wires meet. A **branch** is the path between two junctions.
- **Junction rule:** the charge entering a junction per second equals the charge leaving it per second.
- It follows from **conservation of charge**. If charge built up at a junction, the currents would change until it stopped, so a steady circuit has no build-up.
- The **loop rule** (Topic 11.6) is energy conservation. The **junction rule** is charge conservation. Most branched circuits need both.
- The rule also works for a closed boundary drawn around any part of a steady circuit.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Meaning | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| ΣI_in = ΣI_out | currents in A; in = out at a junction | find an unknown current at a junction |
| I = ΔQ/Δt | charge per second | link the junction rule to charge |
| same I through every element in a branch | no junction inside a branch | find currents in series elements |
| ΔV the same across parallel branches, so I = ΔV/R | less R, more I | decide how current splits |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Currents are **steady** (constant in time). Topic 11.8 deals with changing currents.
- Batteries, wires and meters are **ideal** unless the question says otherwise.
- Only one battery, or batteries in the same branch. Two batteries with different emfs wired in parallel are outside the course.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Saying current is used up** by a bulb or resistor.
2. **Splitting current equally** between branches that are not identical.
3. **Sending more current through the larger resistance.**
4. **Dropping a minus sign.** A negative answer means the current flows opposite to your arrow.
5. **Writing the same junction equation twice** and thinking you have two equations.
6. **Mixing up the rules:** junctions are about current, loops are about potential difference.

## Quick self-check

1. At a junction, 4.0 A enters along one wire and 2.5 A leaves along a second wire. What does the third wire carry? *(1.5 A, leaving the junction)*
2. An ideal 6.0 V battery is connected across a 4.0 Ω resistor and a 12 Ω resistor in parallel. Find the current in each and in the battery. *(1.5 A, 0.50 A, 2.0 A)*
3. Which conservation law is behind the junction rule? *(Conservation of electric charge)*
