Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/10-3-electric-fields-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-2/10-3-electric-fields-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain how a charged object sets up an electric field and why the field exists whether or not a charge is there to feel it. *(Guide: "What an electric field is")*
- I can define the field as force per unit test charge and say why a test charge must be small. *(Guide: "What an electric field is"; Practice Q1)*
- I can state which way the field points near positive and negative charges, and which way a positive or negative charge is pushed. *(Guide: "Direction of the field and of the force"; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can explain why the field inside a conductor in equilibrium is zero, why its excess charge is on the surface, and why the field at the surface is perpendicular to it. *(Guide: "Charged conductors and insulators"; Practice Q3)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can use E = F_E/q to find a field from a measured force, and the force on a new charge from a known field. *(Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can calculate the field of a point charge and predict how it changes when the distance changes. *(Guide: "Field of a point charge"; Practice Q2)*
- I can add the fields of two to four charges using components and symmetry. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can draw and read vector field maps and field-line diagrams, including what line spacing and line numbers show. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can find the field outside a charged sphere by treating it as a point charge at its centre. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can find where the net field of two charges on a line is zero and explain why it is not between unlike charges. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
- I can explain why fields at an angle cannot be added as plain numbers. *(Practice Q6d)*
- I can compare the field inside a charged conductor with the field inside a charged insulator. *(Practice Q7)*
