## Recap

- A **wave pulse** is a single disturbance. A **wave** is a continuous, periodic disturbance with a definite wavelength and frequency.
- Both move **energy** from one place to another. Each part of the medium only moves around its equilibrium position.
- **Mechanical** waves (strings, water, sound) need a medium. **Electromagnetic** waves (light, radio) do not.
- **Transverse:** disturbance perpendicular to the direction of travel. **Longitudinal:** disturbance parallel to it.
- Sound is a mechanical, longitudinal wave. **Compressions** are high-pressure regions; **rarefactions** are low-pressure regions.
- **Amplitude** = maximum displacement from equilibrium. For sound, it can be the largest pressure change from normal pressure.
- Larger amplitude means **more energy** and, for sound, a **louder** sound.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| v = √(F_T/μ) | F_T tension in N; μ = m/ℓ in kg/m; v in m/s | find or compare pulse speeds on a string |
| μ = m/ℓ | m in kg, ℓ in m | get mass per length from a mass and a length |
| t = d/v | d in m | find a travel time |
| c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s | all EM waves in a vacuum | compare light and sound travel times |

| Change | Effect on speed |
|---|---|
| Tension × 4 | speed × 2 |
| Mass per length × 4 | speed × ½ |
| Amplitude × 2 | no change |
| Warmer air (sound) | faster |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Strings are uniform (μ is the same everywhere) and the tension is constant along the string.
- The speed does not depend on the amplitude or on the shape of the pulse.
- Sound speed figures are for a stated medium and temperature.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Saying the medium travels with the wave.** Only energy travels.
2. **Forgetting the square root.** Double F_T gives √2 times the speed, not twice.
3. **Using grams, or total mass, for μ.** Use kg, and divide by length.
4. **Measuring amplitude crest to trough.** Measure from equilibrium.
5. **Sending sound through a vacuum.** Sound needs a medium.
6. **Calling a wave longitudinal because it is horizontal.** Compare the disturbance with the direction of travel.

## Quick self-check

1. The tension in a string is made four times larger. What happens to the pulse speed? *(It doubles.)*
2. A string has μ = 0.010 kg/m and a tension of 25 N. Find the pulse speed. *(50 m/s)*
3. Which of these can cross empty space: sound, light, a pulse on a spring? *(Only light.)*
4. In a sound wave, is a compression a region of high or low pressure? *(High.)*
