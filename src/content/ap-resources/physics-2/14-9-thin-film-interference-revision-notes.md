## Recap

- At a boundary, light is partly **reflected**, partly **transmitted** and partly **absorbed**.
- A thin film reflects from its **top** and **bottom** surfaces. The two reflected waves overlap and interfere.
- The effect shows when the thickness is **comparable to the wavelength** (hundreds of nm).
- Interference depends on film thickness, wavelength, reflection phase changes and viewing angle. Calculations use **normal incidence** only.
- Soap and oil films show colour bands because their **thickness varies**. A film much thinner than λ with one phase change looks black.
- An **antireflection coating** makes the two reflections cancel, so more light is transmitted.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Meaning | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| reflection from higher n → 180°; from lower n → 0° | phase change on reflection | count phase changes (0, 1 or 2) |
| λ_film = λ / n_film | wavelength inside the film; f unchanged | compare the path with the right wavelength |
| extra path = 2t | normal incidence, down and back | find the path difference |
| 0 or 2 changes: bright 2n t = mλ, dark 2n t = (m + ½)λ | m = 0, 1, 2, … | predict reflection colours |
| 1 change: bright 2n t = (m + ½)λ, dark 2n t = mλ | conditions swap | predict reflection colours |
| t_min = λ / (4n_coating) | quarter-wave antireflection coating, n_air < n_coating < n_lens | design a coating |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Light hits the film at **normal incidence**.
- λ without a label is the wavelength in **air** (n = 1.00).
- Visible light runs from about **400 nm to 700 nm**.
- Absorption in the film is small enough to ignore in the interference conditions.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Flipping every reflection.** Only reflection from a higher n gives 180°.
2. **Adding a phase change for refraction.** Refraction never changes the phase.
3. **Using t instead of 2t.**
4. **Using λ in air inside the film.** Divide by n_film, or write 2n t.
5. **Using one formula for all films.** One phase change swaps bright and dark.
6. **Choosing a coating index above the lens index.** That gives one phase change and makes reflection stronger.

## Quick self-check

1. What is the wavelength of 520 nm light inside a film with n = 1.30? *(400 nm)*
2. Light in glass reflects from the glass–air surface. Is there a phase change? *(No: air has the lower n)*
3. Minimum thickness of a coating (n = 1.25) on glass (n = 1.52) that cancels 600 nm reflection? *(120 nm)*
4. Why does a soap film look black just before it bursts? *(t ≪ λ and one phase change, so the reflections cancel for every colour)*
