Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/computer-science-principles/5-6-safe-computing-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/computer-science-principles/5-6-safe-computing-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define PII and give examples. *(Guide: "Personal data and privacy"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain how collected and aggregated data can risk privacy and safety. *(Guide: "Personal data and privacy"; Practice Q7, question 10)*
- I can explain what strong passwords and multifactor authentication do. *(Guide: "Authentication: proving who you are"; Practice Q2, Q8)*
- I can explain the difference between symmetric key and public key encryption, and what certificate authorities do. *(Guide: "Encryption: keeping messages secret"; Practice Q3, Q9)*

## Skills

- I can identify phishing, keylogging, rogue access points, malicious links and untrustworthy downloads in scenarios. *(Guide: Worked example 2 part B; Practice Q4, Q6)*
- I can say whose key is used at each step of public key encryption. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can calculate the number of possible PINs and the time to try them all. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*

## Reasoning

- I can match a protection to a specific threat. *(Guide: attack table; Practice Q5, Q8)*
- I can explain why a second factor helps even if a password is stolen. *(Guide: Worked example 1 part (c); Practice Q8)*
- I can analyse a scenario for benefits and privacy risks of data collection and suggest actions by the user and the organisation. *(Guide: "Personal data and privacy"; Practice question 10)*
