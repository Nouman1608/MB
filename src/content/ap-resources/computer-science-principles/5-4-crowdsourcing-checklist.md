Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/computer-science-principles/5-4-crowdsourcing-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/computer-science-principles/5-4-crowdsourcing-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define crowdsourcing and citizen science and give an example of each. *(Guide: "Many people, one task" and "Citizen science"; Practice Q1, Q2)*
- I can explain how public data and wide access to information help people solve problems at scale. *(Guide: "Information, public data and problem-solving at scale"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain how crowdsourcing offers new models of collaboration and funding. *(Guide: "Information, public data and problem-solving at scale"; Practice Q3)*
- I can list challenges: quality, uneven participation, privacy and fair credit. *(Guide: "Challenges and limits"; Practice Q5, question 10)*

## Skills

- I can apply a threshold rule to counts of reports and list what is confirmed. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate the share of contributions from each group or area. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q8)*
- I can compare shares of contributions with shares of the population or area. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q8)*

## Reasoning

- I can evaluate a crowdsourced data set for quality and coverage. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7, question 10)*
- I can suggest ways to improve reliability and representation. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q9, question 10)*
- I can link uneven participation to the digital divide and bias. *(Guide: "Challenges and limits"; Practice question 10)*
