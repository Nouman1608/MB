## Recap

- A **computing innovation** includes a program as an essential part of how it works. It can be physical, nonphysical software or a nonphysical concept.
- **Collaboration** improves innovations because different people notice different problems. It includes both teamwork and consulting users.
- **Diverse perspectives** help a product fit more people, avoid bias and serve its real purpose.
- **Consultation with users** (surveys, interviews, watching people try a prototype) shows what the program must do and how people will really use it.
- **Online tools** (shared documents, comments, change tracking) let teams share ideas and give feedback even when apart.
- **Pair programming**: one driver writes, one navigator reviews; they swap roles regularly.
- **Interpersonal skills**: communication, consensus building, conflict resolution, negotiation.

## Key relationships

| Idea | What to say in an answer |
|---|---|
| Diverse team | More blind spots are noticed early, so the product fits more people and carries less bias |
| User consultation | The developers learn the purpose from the users' point of view |
| Pair programming | Two sets of eyes catch errors as the code is written |
| Collaboration tools | Share work and feedback quickly, whether people are in one room or not |
| Interpersonal skills | Keep the team working together when views differ |

## Assumptions and conventions

- Say what perspective is **missing** and what it could change. "Add more people" alone earns nothing.
- Link the benefit to the **purpose** of the innovation, using the details in the scenario.
- Collaboration lowers the chance of bias; it does not remove it.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Treating "diverse" as a vague word. Name the group and the problem.
2. Forgetting that users are part of collaboration.
3. Claiming a diverse team **guarantees** a bias-free product.
4. Describing pair programming as one person typing while the other is idle.
5. Mixing up conflict resolution (settling a disagreement) with negotiation (agreeing a trade).
6. Listing tools without saying **why** they help.

## Exam wording to recognise

Questions often begin "Explain how..." or "Describe one...". A reliable answer pattern is **perspective, problem, method, improvement**: say who is missing, what could go wrong, how the team could involve them and what gets better. For interpersonal skills, name the skill and then say what a team member would **do** (for example, "agree who will build which part before starting"). Avoid answers that only say "more ideas" or "better teamwork"; they name no group and no result.

## Quick self-check

1. A map app is tested only by its developers, all of whom live in the same city. Name one risk. *(It may fail for users in other places or with different needs, because those perspectives were never consulted.)*
2. In pair programming, what does the navigator do? *(Reviews each statement, spots errors and thinks ahead while the driver writes; the two swap roles.)*
3. Two teammates agree to build one feature now and another next week. Which skill is this? *(Negotiation, which can also be seen as consensus building.)*
