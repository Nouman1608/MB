Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/computer-science-principles/1-1-collaboration-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/computer-science-principles/1-1-collaboration-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what a computing innovation is and give a physical, a software and a concept example. *(Guide: "Why does a computing innovation need more than one mind?")*
- I can explain how diverse perspectives help a product fit more people and avoid bias, without claiming a guarantee. *(Guide: "What diverse perspectives actually change"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain that collaboration includes consulting users as well as working with teammates. *(Guide: "Two kinds of collaboration"; Practice Q3)*
- I can describe how pair programming works, including the swapping of roles. *(Guide: "How teams collaborate in practice"; Practice Q2)*

## Skills

- I can name the four interpersonal skills and say what each looks like in a team. *(Guide: skills table; Practice Q5, Q9)*
- I can describe two ways online tools help a team and say why they help. *(Guide: "How teams collaborate in practice"; Practice Q8)*
- I can identify a missing perspective in a scenario and the problem it could cause. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can propose a specific method for gathering a missing perspective and explain the improvement. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7)*
- I can diagnose a team breakdown as a failure of communication or conflict resolution and suggest a fix. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q9)*
- I can judge whether a claim such as "a diverse team means no bias" is supported. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q6)*
