Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/computer-science-principles/4-2-fault-tolerance-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/computer-science-principles/4-2-fault-tolerance-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define fault tolerance and redundancy. *(Guide: "Why do parts fail, and what do we do about it?" and "Redundancy: the idea behind fault tolerance"; Practice Q1, Q2)*
- I can explain how the Internet stays usable when a device or connection fails. *(Guide: "Redundancy: the idea behind fault tolerance"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain that redundancy costs additional resources but improves reliability. *(Guide: "Redundancy: the idea behind fault tolerance"; Practice Q5, Q9)*
- I can explain what a single point of failure is. *(Guide: "Weak points: single points of failure"; Practice Q3)*

## Skills

- I can list the paths between two devices and test the effect of removing a device. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6, Q8)*
- I can identify the devices that lie on every path. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7)*
- I can count the links in a star, ring and full mesh. *(Guide: "Comparing designs"; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can find pairs of failures that disconnect a network. *(Guide: Worked example 1 part (c); Practice Q8)*
- I can choose between designs using a requirement and a budget. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice question 10)*
- I can explain why redundancy does not guarantee protection against every failure. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice question 10)*
