Work through the list without notes or a calculator. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/1-5-determining-limits-algebraic-properties-limits-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/1-5-determining-limits-algebraic-properties-limits-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can state the sum, difference, constant multiple, product, quotient, power and root properties, with the condition each one needs. *(Guide: "The properties of limits"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why the quotient property cannot be used when the bottom limit is 0, and what nonzero/0 tells me. *(Guide: Worked example 1(d); Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why substitution gives the limit of a polynomial, using the properties step by step. *(Guide: "Why direct substitution works for polynomials")*
- I can explain why a limit can differ from the function value at that point. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q5(b))*

## Skills

- I can combine given limit values to find the limit of a more complicated expression, naming each property. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q7(b))*
- I can find the limit of a polynomial or rational function by substitution, after checking the bottom is not 0. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can find the limit of a composite function, and track the side of approach when the outer function jumps. *(Guide: "Limits of composite functions"; Practice Q3, Q5(d))*
- I can find one-sided limits from a piecewise formula and choose a constant that makes the two-sided limit exist. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
- I can read one-sided limits of sums and products from two graphs, treating open and filled circles correctly. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can show that a sum can have a limit even when neither part has one. *(Guide: Worked example 2(c); Practice Q7(d))*
- I can use the difference property to find an unknown limit, or to prove that a limit cannot exist. *(Practice Q7(a), Q7(c))*
