Work through the list without notes or a calculator. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/1-14-connecting-infinite-limits-vertical-asymptotes-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/1-14-connecting-infinite-limits-vertical-asymptotes-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain what lim (x → a) f(x) = ∞ means, and why the limit still does not exist as a number. *(Guide: "What an infinite limit means"; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can explain why 1/x has no two-sided infinite limit at 0 while 1/x² does. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can state the limit-based definition of a vertical asymptote, including that one side is enough. *(Guide: "Vertical asymptotes, defined with limits"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why a vertical asymptote is a discontinuity that cannot be removed. *(Guide: "Vertical asymptotes, defined with limits"; Practice Q6(d))*

## Skills

- I can use a sign check to find each one-sided limit when substitution gives a nonzero number over 0. *(Guide: "Finding one-sided infinite limits: the sign check"; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can predict whether the one-sided limits match from the power of the factor in the bottom. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can factor and cancel to decide whether a zero of the bottom gives a hole or a vertical asymptote, and give the height of a hole. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q6(a))*
- I can find a parameter that turns a vertical asymptote into a hole, or that fixes the sign of an infinite limit. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can recall the infinite limits of ln x at 0 and tan x at π/2. *(Guide: table of standard examples; Revision notes)*

## Reasoning

- I can write a justification sentence that names a one-sided infinite limit as the reason for a vertical asymptote. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6(c))*
- I can read unbounded behaviour from a table and explain why a sign check is still needed to confirm it. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why a point where f is undefined is not always a vertical asymptote. *(Guide: Worked example 3, step 6)*
