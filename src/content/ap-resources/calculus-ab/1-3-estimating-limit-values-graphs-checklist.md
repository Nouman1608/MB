Work through the list without notes or a calculator. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/1-3-estimating-limit-values-graphs-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/1-3-estimating-limit-values-graphs-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain what x → a⁻ and x → a⁺ mean, and why the sign shows direction, not whether x is negative. *(Guide: "A note on notation")*
- I can state the rule linking the two one-sided limits to the two-sided limit. *(Guide: "One-sided and two-sided limits"; Practice Q5(b))*
- I can explain why the value f(a) does not affect lim (x → a) f(x), using a graph with a hole and a separate dot. *(Guide: Worked example 1(a); Practice Q1, Q5(c))*
- I can explain why writing "= ∞" describes a limit that does not exist. *(Guide: "A note on writing ∞"; Practice Q6(c))*

## Skills

- I can read left-hand and right-hand limits from a graph, including at a jump. *(Guide: Worked example 1(b); Practice Q2, Q5(a))*
- I can read a one-sided limit at an endpoint of the domain. *(Guide: "One-sided and two-sided limits"; Practice Q5(d))*
- I can recognise a jump, unbounded behaviour and oscillation on a graph, and name which one stops a limit existing. *(Guide: "Three ways a limit can fail to exist"; Practice Q3, Q6(a))*
- I can find x-values closer and closer to 0 where an oscillating function takes two different values. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q6(b))*
- I can sketch a graph with open and filled circles that matches a list of limit statements. *(Practice Q7(a))*

## Reasoning

- I can justify "the limit does not exist" by quoting the two one-sided limits, not just by saying "there is a jump". *(Practice Q5(b), Q7(b))*
- I can explain how a graph's scale or window can hide a hole or a narrow asymptote, and say how I would check. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4, Q7(d))*
- I can say when my graph reading is only an estimate. *(Guide: "What a graph tells you about a limit")*
