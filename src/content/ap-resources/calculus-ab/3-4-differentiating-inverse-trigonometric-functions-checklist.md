Work through the list without notes or a calculator. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/3-4-differentiating-inverse-trigonometric-functions-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/3-4-differentiating-inverse-trigonometric-functions-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain why sine, cosine and tangent must be restricted before they have inverses, and state the range of arcsin, arccos and arctan. *(Guide: "Why the ranges are restricted"; Practice Q7(d))*
- I can explain the difference between sin⁻¹ x and 1/sin x. *(Guide: "A note on notation")*
- I can explain why arcsin and arccos are not differentiable at x = ±1, while arctan is differentiable everywhere. *(Guide: "Where the derivatives fail"; Practice Q4, Q6(c))*

## Skills

- I can state the derivatives of arcsin x, arccos x and arctan x from memory. *(Revision notes; Practice Q1, Q2)*
- I can derive the derivative of arcsin x or arctan x by implicit differentiation, using a reference triangle or an identity. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can use the chain rule with an inner function, squaring the whole inner function. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q2, Q7)*
- I can combine an inverse trigonometric derivative with the product rule and evaluate it exactly. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can write the tangent line to an inverse trigonometric graph at a given point. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(b), Q7(c))*
- I can find a derivative at a point with the inverse-function rule from Topic 3.3. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3)*

## Reasoning

- I can justify the sign of the square root using the range of the inverse function. *(Guide: arcsin derivation; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can decide where an expression such as arcsin(x − 2) is defined and where it is differentiable. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can use the sign of a derivative to say whether an inverse trigonometric function is increasing or decreasing. *(Practice Q5(c))*
