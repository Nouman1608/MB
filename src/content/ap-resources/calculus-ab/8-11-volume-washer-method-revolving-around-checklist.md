Work through the list without notes. Use a calculator only where a statement mentions one. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/8-11-volume-washer-method-revolving-around-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/8-11-volume-washer-method-revolving-around-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain why a region with a gap between itself and the axis produces ring-shaped cross sections. *(Guide: "From discs to washers"; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can explain why the area of a washer is π(R² − r²) and not π(R − r)². *(Guide: Figure 1 and "The trap"; Practice Q4, Q7(c))*
- I can describe the disc method as the washer method with r = 0. *(Guide: "When does a washer become a disc?")*

## Skills

- I can find R and r as distances from the axis, using a test point to decide which boundary is farther away. *(Guide: "Finding R and r"; Practice Q1, Q5(b))*
- I can write and evaluate a washer integral around the x-axis in terms of x. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q7(a))*
- I can rewrite curves as x in terms of y and write a washer integral around the y-axis with y-limits. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q5, Q7(b))*
- I can use a calculator to find intersection values, store them and evaluate a washer integral. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3, Q6(b))*
- I can write a complete integral, with π, brackets, limits and dx or dy, before evaluating. *(Guide: "The method, step by step")*

## Reasoning

- I can round only the final answer to three decimal places and explain how early rounding changes it. *(Guide: "Rounding on calculator questions"; Practice Q3, Q6(d))*
- I can check a washer volume against a simpler shape, such as a cylinder or cone. *(Guide: Worked example 1 check; Practice Q5(d))*
- I can spot a negative or unreasonably small volume and trace it to a swapped or wrongly squared radius. *(Practice Q1, Q4)*
