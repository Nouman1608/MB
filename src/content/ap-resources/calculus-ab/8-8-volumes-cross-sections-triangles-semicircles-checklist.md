Work through the list without notes. Use a calculator only where a statement says so. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/8-8-volumes-cross-sections-triangles-semicircles-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/8-8-volumes-cross-sections-triangles-semicircles-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain why the volume is still ∫ A dx when the slices are triangles or semicircles. *(Guide: "Same integral, new shapes")*
- I can derive the area of an equilateral triangle of side s, (√3/4)s², using Pythagoras. *(Guide: "The area formulas, derived"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why a semicircle on side s has area (π/8)s², with radius s/2. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can tell the two isosceles right-triangle cases apart: leg in the base gives s²/2, hypotenuse gives s²/4. *(Guide: area table; Practice Q3)*

## Calculation

- I can find a volume with triangular or semicircular slices perpendicular to the x-axis. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can find a volume with slices perpendicular to the y-axis, writing s in terms of y. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q7(c))*
- I can use a calculator for the limits and the integral, storing values and giving three decimal places. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4, Q6(c))*
- I can find a volume from a given area function, or from a triangle with a stated height. *(Guide: "Other shapes and given areas"; Practice Q7(a))*
- I can estimate a volume from a table with a trapezoidal sum of cross-sectional areas. *(Practice Q6(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why the ratio of two volumes on the same base depends only on the shape constants. *(Practice Q5(d))*
- I can find an unknown constant from a given volume. *(Practice Q7(b))*
- I can explain how these solids differ from solids of revolution in Topic 8.9. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*
