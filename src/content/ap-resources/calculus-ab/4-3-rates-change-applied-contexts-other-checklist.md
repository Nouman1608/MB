Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/4-3-rates-change-applied-contexts-other-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/4-3-rates-change-applied-contexts-other-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain that a derivative is a rate of change in any context, not only in motion, and give three examples with units. *(Guide: "One idea, many settings")*
- I can explain the difference between an amount f(a), an average rate over an interval and an instantaneous rate f′(a). *(Guide: Worked example 1(c); Practice Q2, Q5(c))*
- I can explain why f′(a) gives only an approximate change over the next unit of input. *(Guide: "Writing an interpretation"; Practice Q7(d))*
- I can interpret a rate when the input is not time, such as altitude, distance or number of items. *(Guide: "When the input is not time"; Practice Q1, Q5)*

## Calculation

- I can find the units of f′ and f″ from the units of f and of its input. *(Guide: "Units"; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can find a rate of change from a formula, using any differentiation rule I need. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q6, Q7)*
- I can estimate a rate of change from a table using the nearest values either side, and say "approximately". *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3)*

## Reasoning

- I can write a four-part interpretation: when, what, increasing or decreasing, and rate with units. *(Guide: "Writing an interpretation"; Practice Q1, Q5(b), Q7(b))*
- I can avoid double negatives by putting the sign either in the word or in the number, not both. *(Guide: "Writing an interpretation"; Practice Q6(c))*
- I can use the signs of f′ and f″ together to say whether a quantity is changing faster or more slowly. *(Guide: "What the second derivative adds"; Practice Q4, Q6(d))*
