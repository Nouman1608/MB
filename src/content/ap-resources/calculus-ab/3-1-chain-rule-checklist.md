Work through the list without notes or a calculator. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/3-1-chain-rule-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/3-1-chain-rule-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain, with an example like (3x + 1)², why the power rule alone gives the wrong derivative of a composite. *(Guide: "Why a new rule is needed")*
- I can split a composite into its outer and inner functions, including hidden ones such as tan³x and 1/(x² + 1). *(Guide: "Outer and inner functions"; Practice Q5(a))*
- I can explain why rates multiply along a chain, using units such as (points/°C) × (°C/hour). *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q4)*

## Skills

- I can differentiate a power, root, trig, exponential or log of an inner function, multiplying by the derivative of the inside. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q2)*
- I can find the derivative of f(g(x)) at a point from a table, finding g(a) before I look up f′. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q6(a), Q6(b))*
- I can apply the chain rule several times to a function with three or more layers. *(Guide: "More than two layers"; Practice Q5)*
- I can use the chain rule inside the product rule and simplify the result. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6(c))*
- I can use a chain-rule derivative to write a tangent line and to find where a tangent is horizontal. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 3; Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can decide which rule is the outer structure by asking which operation is done last. *(Guide: Worked example 3, "Order of operations")*
- I can find and correct a missing or wrong factor in someone else's chain-rule answer. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q5(b))*
- I can tell sin²x apart from sin(x²), and a power of a function such as [f(x)]³ apart from a function of a power such as g(x²), and differentiate each correctly. *(Revision notes: "Mistakes to avoid"; Practice Q6(a), Q6(b))*
