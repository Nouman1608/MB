Work through the list without notes or a calculator. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/5-3-determining-intervals-on-which-function-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/5-3-determining-intervals-on-which-function-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can state what it means for f to be increasing or decreasing on an interval, by comparing f(x₁) and f(x₂). *(Guide: "What increasing and decreasing mean")*
- I can explain, using the Mean Value Theorem, why f′(x) > 0 on an interval makes f increasing there. *(Guide: "The link to the derivative")*
- I can explain why a single zero of f′, with the same sign on both sides, does not end an interval of increase. *(Guide: the x³ example; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain the difference between f′ being positive and f′ being increasing. *(Guide: Worked example 2 "The trap"; Practice Q4, Q6(d))*

## Skills

- I can differentiate, factor f′ and build a sign chart with test values or factor signs. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q3)*
- I can include every split point: zeros of f′, points where f′ is undefined, and gaps in the domain of f. *(Guide: "When the domain has a gap"; Practice Q7)*
- I can read the intervals where f increases or decreases from a graph of f′. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Figure 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can find where a quantity is increasing in context from the sign of its rate, and give units. *(Guide: "Increasing and decreasing in context"; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can write a justification that names f (or the quantity), the interval and the sign of f′. *(Guide: "Writing a justification"; Practice Q5, Q6)*
- I can compare two values of f, such as f(3) and f(1), using an interval where f is increasing or decreasing. *(Practice Q5(d), Q6(c))*
- I can explain why I must not join intervals across a point where f is undefined. *(Guide: h(x) = x + 4/x)*
