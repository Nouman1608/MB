Work through the list without notes. Use a calculator only where a statement mentions one. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/8-12-volume-washer-method-revolving-around-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/8-12-volume-washer-method-revolving-around-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain why a radius is a distance from the axis, written as larger coordinate minus smaller coordinate. *(Guide: "The same washers, a different axis"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why the lower curve gives the outer radius when the axis is above the region. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2, Q6(b))*
- I can explain why moving the axis changes each radius before squaring, so the volume cannot be adjusted by a simple amount. *(Guide: Worked example 3 sense check)*

## Skills

- I can write radii for an axis y = k above or below a region, including axes such as y = −1. *(Guide: Worked example 1 and "The same region, axis below"; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can set up and evaluate a washer integral around a horizontal line other than the x-axis. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q7(a))*
- I can rewrite curves as x in terms of y and set up a washer integral around a vertical line x = h. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q5, Q7(b))*
- I can find intersection values on a calculator, store them and give a shifted-axis volume to three decimal places. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4, Q6(c))*
- I can use a test point to check that my R and r match the sketch. *(Guide: "A quick test before you integrate")*

## Reasoning

- I can describe how the outer and inner boundaries swap when the axis moves from one side of a region to the other. *(Guide: "The same region, axis below"; Practice Q6(d))*
- I can check a volume against a cylinder, cone or frustum. *(Guide: Worked example 2 check; Practice Q5(d))*
- I can use symmetry to explain why two different axes give the same volume. *(Practice Q7(c))*
