Work through the list without notes. Use a calculator only where the linked question allows one. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/8-5-finding-area-between-curves-expressed-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/8-5-finding-area-between-curves-expressed-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain how thin horizontal strips of length right − left and thickness Δy lead to an area integral in y. *(Guide: "Turning the strips sideways", Figure 1)*
- I can explain why vertical and horizontal strips must give the same area for the same region. *(Guide: Worked example 1 check; Practice Q3, Q7(b))*
- I can explain why a negative result means I subtracted left minus right. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q2)*
- I can look at a region and say whether dx or dy needs fewer integrals, and why. *(Guide: "Choosing dx or dy"; Practice Q5(d))*

## Skills

- I can rewrite a curve given as y = f(x) in the form x = g(y). *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q7)*
- I can find the y-limits by solving R(y) = L(y) algebraically. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q5(a))*
- I can decide which curve is on the right using a test y-value between the limits. *(Guide: "The method, step by step"; Practice Q1, Q5(b))*
- I can find intersections in y with a calculator, store them and use them as limits. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4, Q6(a))*
- I can write an area integral with y-limits, brackets and dy before evaluating it. *(Practice Q5(b), Q6(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can treat the y-axis as the curve x = 0 when it is a boundary. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q1)*
- I can interpret the length of a horizontal strip, R(y) − L(y), in a real context. *(Practice Q6(d))*
- I can use horizontal strips to find an area that would be hard with vertical strips. *(Practice Q7)*
