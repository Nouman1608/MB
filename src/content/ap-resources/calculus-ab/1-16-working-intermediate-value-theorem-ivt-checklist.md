Work through the list without notes or a calculator. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/1-16-working-intermediate-value-theorem-ivt-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/1-16-working-intermediate-value-theorem-ivt-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can state the Intermediate Value Theorem, naming both conditions and the conclusion. *(Guide: "The theorem, part by part"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain, with a sketch, why a continuous graph cannot skip a height between f(a) and f(b). *(Guide: "The idea" and Figure 1)*
- I can explain why the theorem gives "at least one" value of c and never its location. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q3, Q6(c))*
- I can tell "the theorem does not apply" apart from "there is no solution". *(Guide: "When the theorem does not apply"; Practice Q7(b), Q7(c))*

## Skills

- I can check continuity on a closed interval for a polynomial, a quotient or a sum of familiar functions, and spot a point where it fails. *(Guide: "When the theorem does not apply"; Practice Q1)*
- I can find the constant that makes a piecewise function continuous before using the theorem. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4)*
- I can give the least number of solutions of f(x) = d from a table of a continuous function. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q5(b))*
- I can show that an equation such as g(x) = h(x) has a solution by applying the theorem to g(x) − h(x). *(Guide: "Using the theorem to locate solutions"; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can narrow a solution to a smaller interval by testing a point inside it. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can write a complete three-part justification: continuity with a reason, the trapped value with numbers, and the theorem named. *(Guide: "Writing a justification"; Practice Q5(a), Q6(a))*
- I can explain why a value outside the range of the end values is not guaranteed. *(Guide: Worked example 2(c); Practice Q5(c))*
- I can combine the theorem with another fact, such as the degree of a polynomial, to show there are exactly n solutions. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
