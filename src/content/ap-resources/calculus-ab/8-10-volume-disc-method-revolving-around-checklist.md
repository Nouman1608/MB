Work through the list without notes. Use a calculator only where a statement says so. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/8-10-volume-disc-method-revolving-around-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/8-10-volume-disc-method-revolving-around-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain why the radius of a disc is the distance from the axis of revolution to the curve, not from a coordinate axis. *(Guide: "What changes when the axis moves"; Practice Q2, Q3)*
- I can explain why spinning about y = k is like shifting the region down by k and spinning about the x-axis. *(Guide: "A useful way to see it")*
- I can decide whether a region touches the axis along its whole length, and so whether discs apply. *(Guide: "The axis must be part of the boundary"; Practice Q5(b), Q6(a))*

## Skills

- I can write the radius as larger minus smaller for an axis above, below, left or right of a curve. *(Guide: radius table; Practice Q1, Q2)*
- I can handle a negative axis such as y = −1, writing the radius as f(x) + 1. *(Guide: Worked example 3)*
- I can choose dx for a horizontal axis and dy for a vertical axis, rewriting the curve as x = g(y) when needed. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q5(a), Q6(b))*
- I can expand a squared radius such as (2 − y^(1/3))² and evaluate the volume exactly. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can write the integral in full and evaluate it with a calculator to three decimal places. *(Practice Q4, Q6(c))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why π ∫ [k² − f(x)²] dx is not a disc volume about y = k. *(Practice Q1(A), Q4(C))*
- I can find a disc radius from a graph by drawing one strip at right angles to the axis. *(Guide: "Reading the radius from different representations"; Figure 1)*
- I can check a volume against the smallest cylinder that contains the solid. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 to 3; Practice Q7(c))*
- I can explain what is wrong with an integral that uses the wrong variable or leaves out the curve. *(Practice Q6(d))*
