This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can describe a Riemann sum as a total of products, each a function value at a point in a subinterval times that subinterval's width. *(Guide: "What a Riemann sum is, in general" and Figure 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can state the definition of the definite integral as a limit of Riemann sums, including the condition that the largest width shrinks to 0. *(Guide: "From sums to the definite integral"; Practice Q7(b), Q7(c))*
- I can explain why, for a continuous function, the choice of sample points does not change the limit. *(Guide: "From sums to the definite integral"; Practice Q6(d))*
- I can match each part of ∫ (a to b) f(x) dx with a part of a Riemann sum. *(Guide: sum-and-integral table)*

## Skills

- I can read and evaluate a short sum written in sigma notation, including one whose index starts at 0. *(Guide: "Summation notation from first principles")*
- I can write left and right Riemann sums with equal widths in sigma notation, using Δx = (b − a)/n and xᵢ = a + iΔx. *(Guide: "Equal widths"; Practice Q5(a), Q5(b))*
- I can write a Riemann sum with unequal widths from a table in sigma notation, and calculate it with units. *(Practice Q3, Q7(a))*
- I can rewrite the limit of a Riemann sum as a definite integral, in more than one correct form. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q6(a), Q6(b))*
- I can rewrite a definite integral as the limit of a right Riemann sum. *(Guide: Worked example 2(a); Practice Q2, Q5(c))*

## Reasoning

- I can spot a wrong translation: a missing width factor, b mistaken for b − a, or a double shift. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q1, Q6(c))*
- I can find the value of a limit of sums when the integral is a familiar shape, such as a trapezoid or part of a circle. *(Guide: Worked example 2(c); Practice Q4)*
- I can say whether a left or right sum overestimates or underestimates, using whether f is increasing or decreasing. *(Guide: Worked example 2(c); Practice Q5(d))*
