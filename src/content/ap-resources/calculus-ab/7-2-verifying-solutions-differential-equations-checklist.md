Work through the list without notes or a calculator. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/7-2-verifying-solutions-differential-equations-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/7-2-verifying-solutions-differential-equations-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain what it means for a function to be a solution of a differential equation on an interval. *(Guide: "What 'solution' means here")*
- I can explain why agreement at one input value does not prove a solution, but disagreement at one value disproves it. *(Guide: "Why one point is not enough"; Practice Q7(c))*
- I can explain why a differential equation can have infinitely many solutions, using a family with a constant C. *(Guide: "Infinitely many solutions" and Figure 1; Practice Q4, Q5(d))*
- I can explain why a formula is a solution only on intervals where it is defined and differentiable. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6(c))*

## Skills

- I can verify a first-order solution by setting out the left side and right side separately. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q3)*
- I can verify a second-order solution, finding y″ with the chain rule. *(Guide: "Second-order equations"; Practice Q5)*
- I can find the values of r that make y = e^(rx) a solution. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2)*
- I can find unknown constants in a given form by matching coefficients. *(Practice Q7(a))*
- I can show a family with an arbitrary constant solves an equation for every value of the constant. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 3; Practice Q6(b), Q7(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can spot a constant solution and say whether the family includes it. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6(d))*
- I can explain why adding a constant to a solution may give a non-solution. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can check that a solution's shape matches what the equation says about the rate. *(Guide: Worked example 1 and Figure 1)*
