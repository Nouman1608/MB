Work through the list without notes or a calculator. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/2-9-quotient-rule-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/2-9-quotient-rule-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can state the quotient rule in the correct order, including the condition that the bottom is not zero. *(Guide: "The rule"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain, with an example, why the derivative of a quotient is not the quotient of the derivatives. *(Guide: "Why you need a new rule"; Practice Q7(b))*
- I can show where the quotient rule comes from, using the product rule. *(Guide: "Where the rule comes from"; Practice Q7(a))*
- I can say what swapping the two numerator terms does to the answer. *(Guide: table of three features; Practice Q1, Q2)*

## Skills

- I can differentiate a rational function with the quotient rule and simplify the numerator. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q6(a))*
- I can differentiate a quotient that includes eˣ, ln x, sin x or cos x. *(Guide: "When not to use the quotient rule"; Practice Q3, Q5)*
- I can use values of f, f′, g and g′ from a table to find the derivative of a quotient at a point. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q7(c))*
- I can use the reciprocal rule, d/dx [1/g] = −g′/g². *(Guide: "A special case"; Practice Q7(c))*
- I can write the equation of a tangent line to the graph of a quotient. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(c))*

## Reasoning

- I can decide when rewriting or simplifying first is quicker than the quotient rule. *(Guide: "When not to use the quotient rule"; Practice Q4)*
- I can find where the derivative of a quotient is zero by setting its numerator to zero. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q3, Q6(d))*
- I can interpret the value and sign of the derivative of a quotient in context, with units. *(Practice Q6(b), Q6(c))*
