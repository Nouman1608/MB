Work through the list without notes. Use a calculator only where a statement says so. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/8-9-volume-disc-method-revolving-around-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/8-9-volume-disc-method-revolving-around-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain why spinning a region about an axis it touches gives circular slices, and link this to the cross-section method of Topics 8.7 and 8.8. *(Guide: "From known cross sections to solids of revolution"; Figure 1)*
- I can identify the radius of a disc as the distance from the axis to the curve, measured at right angles to the axis. *(Guide: "The disc method"; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can explain why a curve below the x-axis still gives a positive disc volume. *(Guide: "A curve below the axis")*
- I can tell when the disc method does not apply because the region does not touch the axis. *(Guide: "The disc method", point 3; Practice Q7(c))*

## Skills

- I can write π ∫ (a to b) [f(x)]² dx for a rotation about the x-axis, with π, the square and x-limits. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q2)*
- I can rewrite a curve as x = g(y) and write π ∫ (c to d) [g(y)]² dy for a rotation about the y-axis, with y-limits. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q5(b))*
- I can expand a squared radius such as (3x − x²)² before integrating, and evaluate the volume exactly. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(a))*
- I can write the integral in full and then evaluate a disc volume with a calculator to three decimal places. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4, Q6(c))*
- I can estimate a volume from a table of radii with a trapezoidal sum of the areas πr². *(Practice Q6(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can check a volume against a cylinder built from the widest radius and the length of the solid. *(Guide: checks in Worked examples 1 to 3)*
- I can explain why π[∫ f(x) dx]², 2π ∫ [f(x)]² dx and π ∫ f(x) dx are not disc volumes. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q1, Q2)*
- I can find an unknown limit from a given volume. *(Practice Q7(a), Q7(b))*
- I can interpret a volume of revolution in context with cubic units. *(Practice Q6(d))*
