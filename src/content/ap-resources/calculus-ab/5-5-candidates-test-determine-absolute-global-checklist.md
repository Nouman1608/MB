Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/5-5-candidates-test-determine-absolute-global-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/5-5-candidates-test-determine-absolute-global-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between a local maximum and an absolute maximum, using a graph. *(Guide: "Local versus absolute" and Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why an absolute extremum inside a closed interval must be at a critical point. *(Guide: "Where can an absolute extremum be?")*
- I can state the two conditions the Candidates Test needs and say what can go wrong without them. *(Guide: "When the test does not apply"; Practice Q4)*

## Skills

- I can find every critical point inside an interval, including points where f′ does not exist. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q5(b))*
- I can discard critical points that lie outside the interval. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(b))*
- I can build a table of candidates and evaluate f at each, with exact values. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q2, Q5(c))*
- I can compare exact values such as −3∛4 and −6 without a calculator. *(Guide: Worked example 2, step 5)*
- I can use a calculator to evaluate candidates and give answers to 3 decimal places without rounding early. *(Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can state an absolute extremum as a value of f and say where it occurs, including ties. *(Guide: "How to write the answer"; Practice Q3)*
- I can justify an answer by naming continuity on the closed interval and the candidates. *(Practice Q5(c), Q6(b))*
- I can explain why decreasing at an endpoint does not make that endpoint the minimum. *(Practice Q6(c))*
- I can decide whether a function has an absolute maximum on an open interval and explain why. *(Practice Q7(c))*
