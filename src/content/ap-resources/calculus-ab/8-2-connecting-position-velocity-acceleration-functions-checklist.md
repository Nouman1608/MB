Work through the list without notes. Use a calculator only where a statement says so. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/8-2-connecting-position-velocity-acceleration-functions-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/8-2-connecting-position-velocity-acceleration-functions-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain why ∫ (a to b) v(t) dt equals x(b) − x(a), using the Fundamental Theorem. *(Guide: "Displacement: the integral of velocity")*
- I can explain why an integral of velocity needs a starting position before it gives a position. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain the difference between displacement and total distance, and say when they are equal. *(Guide: "Total distance: the integral of speed"; Practice Q4)*
- I can give the units of ∫ v dt and ∫ a dt from the units of v, a and t. *(Guide: "Units" in "Displacement: the integral of velocity")*

## Calculation

- I can find v(b) from v(a) and a(t), and x(b) from x(a) and v(t). *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q3)*
- I can find total distance by hand, splitting at the times where v changes sign. *(Guide: Worked example 1(c); Practice Q1, Q7(d))*
- I can read positions, displacement and distance from a velocity graph using signed areas. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can use a graphing calculator to find a position, a total distance with ∫ |v|, and the zeros of v, to three decimal places. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
- I can find average velocity and average speed over an interval. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q5(d))*

## Reasoning

- I can find the leftmost or rightmost position by comparing positions at the end points and at direction changes. *(Guide: Worked examples 2(c) and 3(c); Practice Q5(c), Q6(d))*
- I can justify a direction change by a sign change in v, not by a = 0 or v = 0 alone. *(Practice Q6(a))*
- I can compare two moving objects by writing each position as a starting value plus an integral. *(Practice Q7)*
