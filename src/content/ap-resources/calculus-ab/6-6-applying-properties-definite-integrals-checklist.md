Work through the list without notes or a calculator. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/6-6-applying-properties-definite-integrals-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/6-6-applying-properties-definite-integrals-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain why a definite integral is a signed area, and why it can be 0 or negative. *(Guide: "Integrals from geometry"; Practice Q7(b))*
- I can explain each property of definite integrals with a picture or a Riemann sum. *(Guide: "The properties of definite integrals")*
- I can explain why a removable discontinuity or a jump does not stop a definite integral from existing. *(Guide: "Integrals of functions with jumps and holes"; Practice Q3, Q5(d))*
- I can name rules that are not properties, such as splitting a product, and give a counterexample. *(Guide: "What is not a property"; Practice Q4)*

## Skills

- I can evaluate an integral by splitting the region into rectangles, triangles, trapezoids and parts of circles. *(Guide: Worked examples 2 and 3; Practice Q2, Q5(a), Q7(a))*
- I can recognise √(r² − x²) as a semicircle of radius r. *(Guide: "Recognise circles"; Practice Q2)*
- I can combine given integral values using reversed limits, constant multiples, sums and adjacent intervals. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q6(a)–(b))*
- I can handle an added constant, using ∫ (a to b) k dx = k(b − a). *(Guide: Worked example 1(d); Practice Q5(c), Q6(c))*
- I can integrate a piecewise function by splitting at each jump. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can solve for an unknown constant or limit from a given integral value. *(Guide: Worked example 3(b); Practice Q6(d), Q7(d))*
- I can tell net area from total area and explain when they differ. *(Guide: Worked example 2, Interpretation; Practice Q7(b))*
- I can state when these properties do not apply, such as across a vertical asymptote. *(Guide: "Boundary")*
