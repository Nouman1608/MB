Work through the list without notes or a calculator. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/3-6-calculating-higher-order-derivatives-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/3-6-calculating-higher-order-derivatives-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain that f″ is the derivative of f′, and that f‴, f⁽⁴⁾ and so on follow the same way. *(Guide: "The idea"; Practice Q1, Q3)*
- I can read and write f″(x), y″, d²y/dx², f⁽ⁿ⁾(x) and dⁿy/dxⁿ, and say why d²y/dx² is not (dy/dx)². *(Guide: "Notation you must recognise"; Practice Q2, Q4)*
- I can explain why f″(a) may fail to exist even when f′(a) exists, with an example. *(Guide: the x^(4/3) example; Practice Q6)*

## Skills

- I can find a second derivative that needs the product, quotient or chain rule, simplifying f′ first. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3, Q7(a))*
- I can find the nth derivative of a polynomial, of e^(kx) and of sin(kx) or cos(kx) using a pattern. *(Guide: "Patterns" and Figure 1; Practice Q2, Q4)*
- I can find d²y/dx² for an implicit curve, substituting dy/dx and using the curve's equation. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can evaluate a higher derivative at a point from its formula. *(Practice Q1, Q4, Q7(d))*

## Reasoning

- I can justify, with one-sided limits or a corner in f′, that a second derivative does not exist at a point. *(Practice Q6(d))*
- I can suggest a formula for g⁽ⁿ⁾(x) from the first few derivatives and check that it carries on to n + 1. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why y cannot be treated as a constant in an implicit second derivative. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q5(c))*
