Work through the list without notes or a calculator. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/1-12-confirming-continuity-over-interval-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/1-12-confirming-continuity-over-interval-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain what it means for a function to be continuous on an interval. *(Guide: "From one point to a whole interval")*
- I can state the extra conditions needed at the endpoints of a closed interval [a, b]. *(Guide: "Open and closed intervals"; Practice Q7(b))*
- I can list the six function families that are continuous on their domains, and say where each common example is continuous. *(Guide: family table; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why "continuous on its domain" does not mean "continuous on every interval", using 1/x as an example. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q7(a))*

## Skills

- I can find the domain of a formula with denominators, even roots and logarithms, and write it as a union of intervals. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q5(a))*
- I can choose the correct bracket at each endpoint of an interval of continuity. *(Guide: Worked example 1, step 5; Practice Q5(b))*
- I can test a piecewise boundary using the left limit, the right limit and the function value. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q6(b), Q6(c))*
- I can find where a trigonometric quotient fails to be defined and compare those values with a given interval. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can justify intervals of continuity by naming the family facts and the combination rules I used. *(Guide: "A procedure you can rely on"; Practice Q5(b), Q6(a))*
- I can decide which side's interval a boundary point belongs to, using one-sided continuity. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q3, Q6(d))*
- I can explain why an asymptote of a formula does not matter outside the interval where that formula is used. *(Guide: Worked example 2, step 1)*
- I can show that one excluded value inside an interval is enough to make the answer "not continuous". *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4, Q5(c))*
