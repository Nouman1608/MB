Work through the list without notes or a calculator. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/1-15-connecting-limits-infinity-horizontal-asymptotes-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/calculus-ab/1-15-connecting-limits-infinity-horizontal-asymptotes-practice/). This topic is shared by Calculus AB and Calculus BC.

## Understanding

- I can explain what lim (x → ∞) f(x) = L means, using a table of large x values. *(Guide: "What x → ∞ means")*
- I can describe the three kinds of end behaviour: levelling off, growing without bound, and oscillating. *(Guide: "End behaviour and horizontal asymptotes"; Practice Q4)*
- I can say when a line y = L is a horizontal asymptote, and why a graph has at most two. *(Guide: "End behaviour and horizontal asymptotes"; Practice Q4, Q7(c))*
- I can explain why a graph may cross its horizontal asymptote, with an example. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5(d))*

## Skills

- I can find a limit at infinity of a rational function by dividing by the highest power of x in the denominator. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5(a))*
- I can predict the limit of a rational function from the degrees and leading coefficients. *(Guide: degree table; Practice Q1, Q3(D))*
- I can handle square roots as x → −∞ using √(x²) = |x|. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2)*
- I can find where a graph meets its horizontal asymptote by solving f(x) = L. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(c), Q7(d))*
- I can use the growth order ln x < xᵖ < bˣ to evaluate a limit at infinity. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3, Q7(a))*

## Reasoning

- I can compare two growing functions by finding the limit of their ratio, and state which dominates. *(Guide: "Comparing how fast functions grow"; Practice Q3)*
- I can interpret a limit at infinity in a context, with units. *(Practice Q6(a), Q6(b))*
- I can tell a horizontal asymptote (x → ±∞) apart from a vertical asymptote (f(x) → ±∞). *(Guide: Worked example 1, "Connection to Topic 1.14")*
