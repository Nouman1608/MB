## Recap

- Light comes in **photons**. Each photon's energy depends only on its frequency.
- **Absorbing** a photon raises the energy of an atom or molecule by exactly the photon's energy. **Emitting** a photon lowers it by exactly that amount.
- Photon energy = **difference** between two levels: E(photon) = E(upper) − E(lower).
- Only photons whose energy matches a gap are absorbed or emitted, so spectra show sharp lines.
- **Brightness** = number of photons. It does not change the energy of each photon.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| c = λν | c = 2.998 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹; λ in m; ν in s⁻¹ (Hz) | convert between wavelength and frequency |
| E = hν | E in J per photon; h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J s | find the energy of one photon from its frequency |
| E = hc/λ | λ in m | go straight from wavelength to energy |
| E(per mol) = E(per photon) × N_A | N_A = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹; ÷ 1000 for kJ | compare with bond energies in kJ mol⁻¹ |

Proportions: λ and ν are **inversely** proportional; E and ν are **directly** proportional; E and λ are **inversely** proportional.

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The light travels at c, its speed in a vacuum (air changes it by less than 0.1%, too little to matter here).
- Each absorption or emission involves **one** photon and **one** atom or molecule.
- The energy levels are fixed values, so each gap gives one sharp wavelength.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Not converting nm to m.** 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m. Do it first.
2. **Per photon vs per mole.** E = hν is per photon; multiply by N_A for per mole.
3. **Forgetting J → kJ.** Divide by 1000 at the end.
4. **Using the level energy instead of the gap.** Always subtract: upper − lower.
5. **Negative photon energy.** If you get one, you subtracted the wrong way round.
6. **Thinking brighter light can reach a bigger gap.** One photon, one transition: energy per photon decides.

## Quick self-check

1. What is the frequency of light with λ = 5.00 × 10⁻⁷ m? *(6.00 × 10¹⁴ s⁻¹)*
2. What is the energy of one photon of frequency 1.00 × 10¹⁵ s⁻¹? *(6.63 × 10⁻¹⁹ J)*
3. An atom drops from a level at −3.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ J to one at −7.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. What is the energy of the emitted photon? *(4.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ J)*
