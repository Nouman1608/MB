Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/4-7-types-chemical-reactions-study-guide/).

## Recap

- The course sorts reactions by what is transferred: **electrons** (redox), **protons** (acid-base), or **ions joining into a solid** (precipitation).
- Classify from the **net ionic equation**; spectator ions hide nothing important.
- **Oxidation** = loss of electrons = oxidation number goes **up**. **Reduction** = gain of electrons = oxidation number goes **down**.
- Electrons move **from** the species oxidised **to** the species reduced. Electrons lost = electrons gained.
- **Combustion** is redox with O₂. Complete combustion of a hydrocarbon gives **CO₂ + H₂O** only.

## Key relationships

| Type | What moves | Evidence in the equation |
|---|---|---|
| Redox | electrons | at least one oxidation number changes |
| Acid-base | a proton, H⁺ | one species loses an H⁺, another gains it; no oxidation number changes |
| Precipitation | ions come together | (aq) ions → ionic (s); no oxidation number changes |

## Oxidation-number rules

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Atom in an element | 0 |
| Monatomic ion | its charge |
| Sum in a formula | overall charge |
| F in compounds | −1 |
| Group 1 / group 2 metals | +1 / +2 |
| H | +1 (−1 in metal hydrides) |
| O | −2 (−1 in peroxides) |

## Assumptions

- The equation is **balanced** for both atoms and charge.
- Oxidation numbers are **bookkeeping**: they assume all bonds are fully ionic. They are not real charges.
- Solubility: you are expected to know only that **Na⁺, K⁺, NH₄⁺ and NO₃⁻ salts are soluble**. Other solubility data will be given.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **"H⁺ present, so acid-base."** Metal + H⁺ → H₂ is redox.
2. **"Solid formed, so precipitation."** A metal coming out of solution is a redox product.
3. **Oxidation number ≠ ion charge.** S in SO₄²⁻ is +6; there is no S⁶⁺ ion.
4. **Electron direction backwards.** From the oxidised species to the reduced one.
5. **Choosing a sodium, potassium, ammonium or nitrate salt as the precipitate.**
6. **Forgetting to multiply by coefficients** when counting electrons lost and gained.

## Quick self-check

1. Oxidation number of Cr in Cr₂O₇²⁻? *(+6)*
2. Oxidation number of P in PO₄³⁻? *(+5)*
3. NH₄NO₃(aq) is mixed with KCl(aq). Could a precipitate form? *(No: every possible product is an ammonium, potassium or nitrate salt, so all stay dissolved.)*
4. In Mg(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + H₂(g), which species is reduced? *(H⁺: hydrogen goes from +1 to 0.)*
