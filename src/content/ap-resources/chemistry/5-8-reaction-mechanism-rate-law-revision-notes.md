## Recap

- In a mechanism, one step is usually much slower than the rest. This **rate-limiting step** sets the rate of the whole reaction.
- When the **first** step is the slow step, the rate law of the reaction is the rate law of that elementary step: each species in it is raised to its coefficient in the step.
- Reactants that react only in **fast steps after** the slow step do not appear in the rate law (zero order).
- **Intermediates** never appear in a rate law compared with experiment. **Catalysts** can, if they react in the slow step.
- A mechanism is consistent with the evidence if it adds up to the overall equation, has reasonable steps, and predicts the experimental rate law. Consistent is not the same as proven.

## Key relationships

| Idea | What to write or do | Example (fictional) |
|---|---|---|
| Elementary step rate law | aA + bB → … gives rate = k[A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ | 2D → D₂ gives rate = k[D]² |
| Slow first step | Rate law of the reaction = rate law of step 1 | Step 1 (slow) X + Y₂ → … gives rate = k[X][Y₂] |
| Overall order | Sum of the exponents | k[A][B] is second order overall |
| Units of k (rate in M s⁻¹) | First order: s⁻¹; second order: M⁻¹ s⁻¹ | k[A][B]: M⁻¹ s⁻¹ |
| Effect of changing [A] by a factor f | Rate changes by fⁿ, where n is the order in A | Order 2, triple [A]: × 9 |

## Assumptions behind the rule

- The steps are **elementary**, so the slow step's coefficients show the particles colliding.
- The slow step is **much** slower than the steps after it, so the later steps do not hold up the reaction.
- The steps go **one way only** (single arrows). A fast reversible step before the slow step needs Topic 5.9.
- The slow step is the **first** step. If it is not, use the approximation in Topic 5.9.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Using the overall coefficients** for the rate law instead of the slow step's.
2. **Including a reactant from a fast later step.** It is zero order.
3. **Putting an intermediate in the final rate law.**
4. **Leaving out a catalyst** that reacts in the slow step.
5. **Saying a consistent mechanism is "proven".**
6. **Forgetting units of k**, which depend on the overall order.

## Quick self-check

1. Step 1 (slow): J₂ + L → J₂L. Step 2 (fast): J₂L + L → J₂ + L₂. What is the rate law? *(rate = k[J₂][L])*
2. In question 1, what role does J₂ play, and what happens to the rate if [L] is doubled? *(J₂ is a catalyst; the rate doubles)*
3. Two mechanisms both predict the experimental rate law and both add up. Is either proven? *(No. Both are consistent; more evidence, such as a detected intermediate, is needed.)*
