Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/9-10-cell-potential-under-nonstandard-conditions-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/9-10-cell-potential-under-nonstandard-conditions-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain that E° is the cell potential when Q = 1, and that E differs from E° when Q ≠ 1. *(Guide: "Standard conditions are just one point on the journey")*
- I can describe the cell potential as a drive towards equilibrium that is larger the further the cell is from equilibrium. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5(b))*
- I can explain why a "dead" cell is at equilibrium (Q = K, E = 0) rather than empty of reactant. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q1, Q5(d))*
- I can explain why Le Châtelier's principle is not used for a working cell. *(Guide: "Why not Le Châtelier's principle?"; Practice Q6(a))*

## Skills

- I can write Q for a cell reaction, leaving out solids, and compare it with 1. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can predict whether a change such as adding an ion, precipitating an ion or diluting makes E larger or smaller than E°. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q6(b))*
- I can read the Nernst equation qualitatively: Q < 1 gives E > E°, Q > 1 gives E < E°. *(Guide: "The Nernst equation, read without a calculator")*
- I can use the Nernst equation to check a prediction, with the correct n. *(Practice Q4(d), Q5(c), Q6(c))*

## Reasoning

- I can identify the anode and the direction of electron flow in a concentration cell from the direction needed to reach equilibrium. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q3, Q7(a))*
- I can explain why a concentration cell gives a voltage although E° = 0, and when it stops. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*
- I can justify a claim about cell potential with the chain: concentration → Q → distance from equilibrium → E. *(Practice Q4(c), Q6)*
