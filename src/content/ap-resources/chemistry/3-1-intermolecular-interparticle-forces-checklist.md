Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/3-1-intermolecular-interparticle-forces-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/3-1-intermolecular-interparticle-forces-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between the bonds inside a molecule and the forces between molecules, and say which ones boiling overcomes. *(Guide: "Forces between particles, not inside them")*
- I can explain how a temporary dipole induces a dipole in a neighbouring particle to produce London dispersion forces. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why "London dispersion forces" and "van der Waals forces" are not the same term. *(Guide: "A naming note")*
- I can state the two conditions for hydrogen bonding and spot molecules that have H and O but cannot hydrogen bond with themselves. *(Guide: "Hydrogen bonding"; Practice Q1)*
- I can describe the noncovalent interactions that hold large biomolecules in shape. *(Guide: "Forces inside large biomolecules"; Practice Q6)*

## Skills

- I can predict how electron count, π bonds and branching change the strength of dispersion forces. *(Guide: "What makes dispersion forces stronger"; Practice Q4)*
- I can draw or describe how partial charges line up in dipole–dipole forces, hydrogen bonds and ion–dipole forces. *(Guide: Figures 2 and 3; Practice Q5(c), Q6(b))*
- I can name the force acting between two different species, such as an ion and water or a polar and a nonpolar molecule. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3)*

## Reasoning

- I can rank substances of similar size by the strength of their intermolecular forces and predict their boiling point order. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why a large nonpolar molecule can have stronger intermolecular forces than a small polar one. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*
- I can evaluate a general claim about intermolecular forces using boiling point data. *(Practice Q7)*
