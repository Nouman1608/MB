## Recap

- A salt's solubility depends on pH when one of its ions is a **weak base**, a **weak acid** or **OH⁻**.
- Acid (H₃O⁺) removes a basic anion by turning it into its conjugate acid. Base (OH⁻) removes a weakly acidic cation by turning it into its conjugate base.
- Removing an ion makes **Q < K_sp**, so the equilibrium shifts towards the dissolved ions and **more solid dissolves**.
- **K_sp does not change.** Only the solubility changes.
- The course tests this **qualitatively**. You will not calculate solubility as a function of pH.

## Key relationships

| Type of salt | Examples | In acid | In base |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anion is a weak base | CaF₂, CaCO₃, Ca₃(PO₄)₂ | more soluble | about the same, or slightly less |
| Hydroxide | Mg(OH)₂, Fe(OH)₃ | more soluble | less soluble (OH⁻ is a common ion) |
| Cation is a weak acid (BH⁺) | fictional (BH)X | about the same | more soluble |
| Anion of a strong acid, neutral cation | AgCl, AgBr, AgI | no change | no change |

Useful equations: F⁻ + H₃O⁺ → HF + H₂O; OH⁻ + H₃O⁺ → 2 H₂O; CO₃²⁻ + 2 H₃O⁺ → CO₂ + 3 H₂O; BH⁺ + OH⁻ → B + H₂O.

**pH versus pK_a.** A basic anion A⁻ is mostly removed once the pH is below the pK_a of HA. A cation BH⁺ is mostly removed once the pH is above the pK_a of BH⁺.

## Assumptions behind the reasoning

- Temperature is constant, so K_sp is constant.
- The added acid or base does not supply a **common ion** (HCl would add Cl⁻; NaOH adds OH⁻).
- In experiments, a buffer holds the pH steady and its ions do not react with the salt.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Saying K_sp increases in acid.** Say the solubility increases.
2. **Making every salt pH-sensitive.** Check the ions first: Cl⁻, Br⁻ and I⁻ do not react with H₃O⁺.
3. **Reacting the acid with the metal ion.** The proton goes to the basic anion or to OH⁻.
4. **Forgetting the common ion.** NaOH lowers the solubility of Mg(OH)₂; HCl lowers the solubility of a chloride.
5. **Skipping the Q step.** A full explanation says which ion is removed, that Q falls below K_sp, and which way the equilibrium shifts.
6. **Claiming a trend inside the error.** Compare differences in the data with the precision of the balance.

## Quick self-check

1. Does BaCO₃ dissolve more in HNO₃(aq) or in water? *(In HNO₃: H₃O⁺ removes CO₃²⁻)*
2. Does adding acid change the solubility of AgI? *(No: I⁻ is the conjugate base of a strong acid)*
3. What does adding NaOH do to the solubility of Fe(OH)₃? *(Lowers it: OH⁻ is a common ion)*
4. Which way does the solubility of a salt with a weakly acidic cation change as the pH rises? *(It increases)*
