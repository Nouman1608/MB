Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/3-6-deviation-ideal-gas-law-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/3-6-deviation-ideal-gas-law-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can name the two assumptions of the kinetic molecular theory that real gases break. *(Guide: "Ideal gases are a model")*
- I can explain why attractions between particles make the measured pressure lower than the ideal value. *(Guide: "Effect 1"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why particle volume makes the measured volume or pressure higher than the ideal value at very high pressure. *(Guide: "Effect 2"; Practice Q4)*
- I can state the conditions that make a gas behave most ideally, and those that make it least ideal. *(Guide; Practice Q1)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate an ideal pressure or volume with PV = nRT and compare it with a measured value. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(a), Q7(a))*
- I can calculate PV/nRT and say what a value below or above 1 means. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5(b), Q7(b))*
- I can show with numbers why particle volume is negligible at ordinary pressure. *(Guide: "Effect 2"; Practice Q7(d))*

## Reasoning

- I can rank gases by how much they deviate, naming the intermolecular forces involved rather than just saying "strong" or "weak". *(Guide: "Predicting which gas deviates more"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why a gas becomes more ideal when it is heated. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(d), Q6(a))*
- I can explain the dip below 1 and the rise above 1 on a graph or table of PV/nRT against pressure. *(Guide: Figure 2, Worked example 2; Practice Q6(b), Q6(c))*
- I can justify which effect dominates with a particle-level argument that links to the measured pressure or volume. *(Practice Q5(c), Q7(c))*
- I can explain why PV/nRT = 1 at one pressure does not prove a gas is ideal. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
