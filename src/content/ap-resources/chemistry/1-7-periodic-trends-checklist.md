Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/1-7-periodic-trends-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/1-7-periodic-trends-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why elements in the same group have similar properties, using their valence electron configurations. *(Guide: "Why the periodic table repeats")*
- I can explain shielding and effective nuclear charge, and make a simple Z_eff estimate for a main-group atom. *(Guide: "The model behind every trend")*
- I can use Coulomb's law to say how nuclear charge and distance change the attraction for an outer electron. *(Guide: "The model behind every trend"; Practice Q6)*
- I can tell electron affinity apart from electronegativity. *(Guide: "Electron affinity" and "Electronegativity")*

## Skills

- I can put atoms in order of atomic radius from their positions on the periodic table. *(Practice Q1)*
- I can compare an atom with its ion, and order an isoelectronic series of ions by size. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7)*
- I can predict the more electronegative of two elements, including diagonal pairs such as O and Cl. *(Practice Q3)*
- I can use successive ionization energies to find the number of valence electrons and the likely ion. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can estimate a missing value from neighbouring elements in a group or period. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain the general rise in first ionization energy across a period and the fall down a group. *(Practice Q5, Q6)*
- I can explain the dips at groups 13 and 16 by naming the subshell or the electron pairing involved. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can explain a trend with a cause (charge, distance, shielding, repulsion), not just describe its direction. *(Practice Q5–Q7)*
- I can evaluate a claim about atomic or ionic size and correct it with evidence. *(Practice Q7)*
