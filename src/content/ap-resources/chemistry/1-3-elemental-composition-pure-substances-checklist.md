Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/1-3-elemental-composition-pure-substances-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/1-3-elemental-composition-pure-substances-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between a substance made of separate molecules, such as H₂O, and one described by a formula unit, such as NaCl. *(Guide: "What a pure substance is made of", Figure 1; Practice Q7)*
- I can state the law of definite proportions in my own words and explain why it follows from a fixed formula. *(Guide: "The law of definite proportions")*
- I can define an empirical formula and reduce a molecular formula to one. *(Guide: "The empirical formula"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why two different compounds can have the same percentage composition. *(Guide: "The empirical formula"; Practice Q7)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate the percentage by mass of each element from a formula, counting every atom. *(Guide: "Percentage by mass from a formula"; Practice Q3)*
- I can use a fixed composition to predict the mass of an element in a sample of any size. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can find an empirical formula from percentage composition by assuming a 100 g sample. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can find an empirical formula from measured masses, finding the second element's mass by subtraction. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can turn a ratio such as 1 : 1.50 or 1 : 1.33 into whole numbers by multiplying, not rounding. *(Guide: "Turning a ratio into whole numbers"; Practice Q4)*
- I can check an empirical formula by working its percentage composition back out. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2)*

## Reasoning

- I can use the law of definite proportions to decide whether a sample could be a stated pure compound. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can write a testable scientific question based on composition data. *(Guide: "Asking a testable question"; Practice Q6)*
