Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/9-9-cell-potential-free-energy-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/9-9-cell-potential-free-energy-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what a standard reduction potential measures and why the hydrogen half-cell is set at 0.00 V. *(Guide: "Standard reduction potentials")*
- I can say which of two half-reactions runs as the reduction in a galvanic cell by comparing their E° values. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can explain that a positive E°cell means a thermodynamically favoured reaction, and a negative E°cell means an applied potential is needed. *(Guide: "The sign of E°cell"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why E° does not change when an equation is multiplied, but ΔG° does. *(Guide: "Do not multiply E° by coefficients"; Practice Q6)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate E°cell = E°(cathode) − E°(anode) without multiplying by coefficients or changing a sign twice. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can find n from the electrons that cancel in a balanced overall equation. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can calculate ΔG° from E° with ΔG° = −nFE° and give the answer in kJ mol⁻¹ with the correct sign. *(Practice Q2, Q4)*
- I can work backwards from ΔG° or a measured cell potential to an unknown reduction potential. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can use E° and ΔG° together to decide whether a proposed redox reaction is thermodynamically favoured. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can explain why recharging a cell needs an applied potential larger than the cell's own E°. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can state that a favourable E° tells me nothing about how fast a reaction goes. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*
