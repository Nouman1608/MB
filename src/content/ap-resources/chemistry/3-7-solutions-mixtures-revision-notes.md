## Recap

- A **solution** (homogeneous mixture) has the same macroscopic properties throughout. It can be a gas (air), a liquid (sugar in water) or a solid (brass).
- A **heterogeneous mixture** has properties that depend on where you sample it: layers, settling solids, visible grains.
- **Molarity** is the lab's main concentration unit: moles of solute per litre of **solution**, written M (mol L⁻¹).
- An ionic solute separates into ions. Each ion's concentration = salt concentration × number of that ion in the formula. Polyatomic ions stay whole.
- Dilution and mixing change the volume; the moles of each solute species are unchanged.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| M = n ÷ V | M in mol L⁻¹, n in mol, V in L of solution | find molarity, or rearrange: n = M × V, V = n ÷ M |
| [ion] = M(salt) × subscript | e.g. 0.25 M CaCl₂ → 0.50 M Cl⁻ | find ion concentrations |
| M₁V₁ = M₂V₂ | moles before = moles after; any volume unit, if the same on both sides | dilution |
| M(mix) = (n₁ + n₂) ÷ (V₁ + V₂) | add moles of the same species | mixing solutions that do not react |
| N = M × V × N_A | N_A = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ | count solute particles |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The solute dissolves **completely** and does not react with anything else present.
- When two dilute aqueous solutions are mixed, the **volumes add**. This is close to true, not exact.
- The solution is homogeneous, so any portion has the same molarity as the whole.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Millilitres in M = n ÷ V.** Convert to litres first.
2. **Litres of water instead of litres of solution.** Make the solution up to the final volume.
3. **Forgetting ion counts.** 0.10 M Al(NO₃)₃ is 0.30 M NO₃⁻.
4. **Averaging concentrations when mixing.** Add moles; divide by total volume.
5. **Thinking a portion is weaker.** Pouring some out changes moles, not molarity.
6. **Calling a coloured liquid heterogeneous.** Colour is not the test; uniformity is.

## Quick self-check

1. How many moles of solute are in 50.0 mL of a 0.120 M solution? *(0.00600 mol)*
2. What is [NO₃⁻] in 0.040 M Ca(NO₃)₂? *(0.080 M)*
3. 20.0 mL of 1.00 M HCl is diluted to 250.0 mL. What is the new concentration? *(0.0800 M)*
4. A salad dressing of oil and vinegar has separated into two layers. Solution or heterogeneous mixture? *(Heterogeneous: a sample from the top layer differs from one taken from the bottom layer.)*
