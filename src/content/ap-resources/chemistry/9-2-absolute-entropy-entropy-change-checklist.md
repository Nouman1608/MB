Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/9-2-absolute-entropy-entropy-change-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/9-2-absolute-entropy-entropy-change-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what a standard molar entropy is and state its unit, J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. *(Guide: "Absolute entropy and standard molar entropy")*
- I can explain why entropy is called absolute and why every substance has a positive S° at 298 K. *(Guide: "Absolute entropy and standard molar entropy"; Practice Q7(c))*
- I can explain why elements in their standard states have S° above zero, unlike their ΔH°f values. *(Practice Q6(d), Q7(c))*
- I can describe how S° compares for the solid, liquid and gas of one substance, and why gases have the largest values. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q7(b))*

## Calculation

- I can calculate ΔS° for a reaction as products minus reactants, multiplying each S° by its coefficient. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q2, Q6(b))*
- I can choose the correct S° for each state, such as H₂O(l) rather than H₂O(g). *(Practice Q3, Q4(b))*
- I can calculate ΔS° for a physical change such as vaporisation. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7(a))*
- I can convert ΔS° from J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ to kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. *(Guide: Worked example 1(b); Practice Q3)*
- I can scale ΔS° to a given mass by finding moles of reaction first. *(Guide: Worked example 1(c); Practice Q4(c), Q6(c))*
- I can work backwards from ΔS° to find an unknown S°. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q5)*
- I can write the new ΔS° when an equation is reversed or multiplied by a factor. *(Revision notes: quick self-check)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict the sign of ΔS° from moles of gas and use it to check a calculated value. *(Practice Q4(a), Q6(a))*
- I can find the error in a calculation that gives a sign that contradicts the prediction, or a negative S°. *(Practice Q5, Q6(d))*
- I can explain a calculated ΔS° at the particle level. *(Practice Q7(b))*
