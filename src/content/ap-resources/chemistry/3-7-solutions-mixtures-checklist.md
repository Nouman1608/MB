Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/3-7-solutions-mixtures-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/3-7-solutions-mixtures-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between a solution (homogeneous mixture) and a heterogeneous mixture in terms of whether properties depend on where you sample. *(Guide: "Homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures"; Practice Q1)*
- I can give an example of a gaseous, a liquid and a solid solution. *(Guide: "Solutions are not only liquids"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why molarity uses the volume of the solution, not the volume of the solvent. *(Guide: "Molarity"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why a portion of a solution has the same molarity as the whole sample. *(Practice Q4, Q6(d))*

## Calculation

- I can calculate molarity from a mass of solute and a volume of solution, converting mL to L. *(Guide: "Molarity"; Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate the mass of solute needed to make a given volume of a solution of known molarity. *(Practice Q6(a))*
- I can find the concentration and number of moles of each ion produced by an ionic solute. *(Practice Q2, Q3)*
- I can count the number of solute particles in a given volume of solution. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4(b))*
- I can carry out single and two-step dilution calculations with M₁V₁ = M₂V₂. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can find ion concentrations after mixing two solutions that do not react, and check the charges balance. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can evaluate a method for making a standard solution and describe the correct use of a volumetric flask. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why averaging concentrations gives the wrong answer when unequal volumes are mixed. *(Practice Q7(b))*
- I can state the assumptions behind a solution calculation, such as complete dissolving and additive volumes. *(Revision notes: "Assumptions")*
