Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/2-4-structure-metals-alloys-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/2-4-structure-metals-alloys-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can describe metallic bonding as the attraction between positive ion cores and a sea of delocalized valence electrons. *(Guide: "A different kind of solid"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why a metal is neutral overall even though its cores are positive. *(Guide: "Drawing the electron-sea model")*
- I can explain the difference between an interstitial alloy and a substitutional alloy, with steel and brass as examples. *(Guide: "Alloys"; Figure 2)*
- I can explain why an alloy is a mixture and not a compound. *(Guide: FAQ; Revision notes)*

## Skills

- I can draw a particulate model of a metal with equal cores in a regular array, the correct core charge and the correct number of delocalized electrons. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6)*
- I can draw particulate models of a substitutional alloy and an interstitial alloy that show where the added atoms sit. *(Guide: Figure 2)*
- I can classify an alloy as interstitial or substitutional from atomic radii, using percent difference. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q7)*
- I can convert between an alloy's mass percent and its atom ratio, and use the result in a model. *(Guide: Worked examples 2 and 3; Practice Q4, Q5, Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why metals conduct electricity and why alloys still conduct. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why metals are malleable while ionic solids are brittle. *(Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why interstitial atoms make an alloy harder and less malleable or ductile. *(Guide: "How alloying changes properties"; Practice Q6)*
- I can evaluate a claim about where added atoms sit in an alloy. *(Practice Q7)*
