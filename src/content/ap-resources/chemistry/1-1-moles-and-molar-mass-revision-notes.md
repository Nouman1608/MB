## Recap

- You cannot count particles directly in the lab, so chemists count them by weighing.
- The **mole** is a counting unit: 1 mol of anything contains **6.022 × 10²³** of its particles (atoms, molecules or formula units).
- **Molar mass, M**, is the mass of one mole, in g mol⁻¹. Find it by adding average atomic masses from the periodic table.
- The average mass of one particle in **amu** has the same number as the molar mass in **grams per mole**.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| n = m / M | n in mol, m in g, M in g mol⁻¹ | turn a mass into moles (or rearrange: m = n × M) |
| N = n × N_A | N particles, N_A = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ | turn moles into a particle count (or n = N / N_A) |
| Atoms of X = N × (subscript of X) | from the formula | count atoms of one element inside a compound |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The sample is **pure**: all of the mass belongs to the substance in the formula.
- Average atomic masses assume the **natural mix of isotopes**.
- Masses are measured accurately; the answer can be no more precise than the least precise measurement (significant figures).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Ignoring brackets.** Ca(NO₃)₂ has 2 N and 6 O.
2. **Equal mass ≠ equal particles.** Compare moles, not grams.
3. **Molecules vs atoms.** 1 mol O₂ = 2 mol O atoms.
4. **Wrong direction with N_A.** Particles → moles: divide. Moles → particles: multiply.
5. **Rounding early.** Keep full calculator values; round once at the end.
6. **Dropping units.** Units that do not cancel to the answer's unit mean the setup is wrong.

## Quick self-check

1. What is the molar mass of CO₂? *(44.01 g mol⁻¹)*
2. How many moles are in 9.01 g of water? *(0.500 mol)*
3. How many atoms are in 0.100 mol of Fe? *(6.02 × 10²² atoms)*
