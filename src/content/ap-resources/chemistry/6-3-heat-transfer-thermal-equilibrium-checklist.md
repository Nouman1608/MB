## Understanding

- I can explain that the particles of a warmer body have a greater average kinetic energy than those of a cooler body, and that the two spreads of energy overlap. *(Guide: "What temperature means for particles"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain the difference between heat (energy in transit) and temperature (a measure of average kinetic energy). *(Guide: "How collisions move energy")*
- I can describe thermal equilibrium as equal average kinetic energy and equal temperature, with collisions continuing but no net transfer. *(Guide: "Reaching thermal equilibrium"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why particles at the same temperature have the same average kinetic energy but not the same average speed. *(Guide: "Same temperature, different particles"; Practice Q3)*

## Skills

- I can compare average kinetic energies at two temperatures by converting to kelvin first. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4, Q7(a))*
- I can use temperature–time data to state the direction of heat transfer. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q5(a))*
- I can decide from data, using the precision of the thermometer, when thermal equilibrium is reached. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q5(d))*
- I can sketch temperature–time curves for two bodies approaching thermal equilibrium. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q7(c))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain, naming the particles, how collisions move energy from a warmer body to a cooler one, including through a wall between them. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(b), Q7(b))*
- I can explain why the net transfer slows as the temperatures get closer. *(Practice Q5(c))*
- I can evaluate a claim that confuses total energy with temperature, or says particles stop at equilibrium. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why the final temperature lies between the two starting temperatures in an insulated system. *(Guide: "Energy is conserved during heat transfer")*
