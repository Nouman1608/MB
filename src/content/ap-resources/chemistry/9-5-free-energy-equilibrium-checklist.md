## Understanding

- I can explain that "thermodynamically favoured" (ΔG° < 0) means products are favoured at equilibrium (K > 1) under standard conditions. *(Guide: "Two ways of saying products are favoured"; Practice Q1)*
- I can state which range of K goes with a negative, zero and positive ΔG°. *(Guide: sign table; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why a positive ΔG° still allows some product to form. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why a favoured reaction is not necessarily fast. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*

## Calculation

- I can calculate K from ΔG° at a given temperature, converting kJ to J and using e, not 10. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can calculate ΔG° from K using ln, and check that the sign of ΔG° matches the size of K. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can find ΔG° and K at a new temperature from ΔH° and ΔS°, and find the temperature at which K = 1. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q5)*
- I can use a value of K to find an equilibrium concentration for a simple reaction. *(Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can estimate whether K is close to 1 or far from 1 by comparing ΔG° with RT (about 2.5 kJ mol⁻¹ at 298 K). *(Guide: "Estimating K"; Practice Q3)*
- I can use the "5.7 kJ mol⁻¹ per factor of 10" rule at 298 K to estimate K or a ratio of K values. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q3, Q7)*
- I can explain why raising the temperature increases or decreases K, using the signs of ΔH° and ΔS°. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can evaluate a claim about favourability or product formation with a calculation as evidence. *(Practice Q6, Q7)*
