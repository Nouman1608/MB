## Recap

- A **galvanic (voltaic) cell** runs a thermodynamically **favourable** redox reaction (ΔG° < 0) and produces electrical energy.
- An **electrolytic cell** uses a **power supply** to drive a thermodynamically **unfavourable** reaction (ΔG° > 0), turning electrical energy into chemical energy.
- In **both**: oxidation at the **anode**, reduction at the **cathode**; electrons move through the external wire from anode to cathode.
- Labelling electrodes as positive or negative is not assessed. The signs swap between the two kinds of cell, so rely on anode = oxidation.

## Key relationships

| Part or observation | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Electrode | Conductor where electrons are lost (anode) or gained (cathode); may react, or be inert (graphite, platinum) |
| Half-cell solution | Holds the ions that react or form; lets ions move |
| Salt bridge (e.g. KNO₃) | Completes the circuit with moving ions; keeps each half-cell neutral; keeps solutions apart |
| Voltmeter / power supply | Measures the potential difference (galvanic) / pushes electrons to drive the reaction (electrolytic) |
| Electrode loses mass | A metal there is being oxidised → anode |
| Electrode gains mass | Metal ions are being reduced and deposited → cathode |
| Gas at an electrode | A half-reaction there makes a gas (e.g. Cl₂ at the anode from Cl⁻) |
| Electron count | Electrons lost at the anode = electrons gained at the cathode; use it to link mass changes |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- All the current goes into the half-reactions you wrote (no side reactions).
- The salt-bridge ions do not react with anything in the half-cells.
- Mass changes are of the pure metal deposited or dissolved.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Electrons in the salt bridge.** Only ions move there.
2. **Thinking the anode is always negative.** True only for galvanic cells; not assessed.
3. **Reversing electron flow for electrolytic cells.** It is still anode → cathode through the external circuit.
4. **Sending ions the wrong way.** Anions → anode; cations → cathode.
5. **Forgetting to balance electrons** before comparing moles at the two electrodes.
6. **Saying bigger electrodes raise the voltage.** They only let the cell run longer.

## Quick self-check

1. In a galvanic cell, the zinc electrode gets thinner. Is zinc the anode or the cathode? *(Anode: zinc is oxidised.)*
2. Which way do K⁺ ions in a KNO₃ salt bridge move? *(Towards the cathode half-cell.)*
3. 0.0200 mol of electrons passes and Cu²⁺ is reduced at the cathode. What mass of copper forms? *(0.0100 mol × 63.55 g mol⁻¹ = 0.635 g)*
4. What happens to the current if the salt bridge is removed? *(It stops: charge builds up in each half-cell.)*
