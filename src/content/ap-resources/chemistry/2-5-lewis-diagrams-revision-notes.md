## Recap

- A **Lewis diagram** shows all the valence electrons in a molecule or ion. Shared pairs are lines; lone pairs are pairs of dots. Core electrons are not drawn.
- Single bond = 2 shared electrons; double = 4; triple = 6.
- C, N, O and F almost always end with **8** electrons around them (octet rule). H ends with **2**.
- B and Be can end with **fewer than 8** (BF₃: B has 6).
- Atoms in **period 3 or beyond** can have **more than 8** (SF₄: S has 10).
- A Lewis diagram shows connectivity, not shape.

## The method

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Total valence electrons. Negative ion: add the charge. Positive ion: subtract it. |
| 2 | Skeleton: least electronegative atom in the centre; H and F outside. |
| 3 | Single bonds to every outer atom (2 electrons each). |
| 4 | Lone pairs to complete each outer atom's octet. |
| 5 | Leftover electrons on the central atom. |
| 6 | Central atom short of 8? Turn outer lone pairs into double or triple bonds. |
| 7 | Recount; brackets and charge for an ion. |

**Shortcut for octet molecules:** bonds = (needed − available) ÷ 2, where needed = 8 per non-H atom + 2 per H.

## Usual bonding patterns (neutral atoms)

| H | C | N | O | Halogen (outer) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 bond, 0 lone pairs | 4 bonds, 0 | 3 bonds, 1 | 2 bonds, 2 | 1 bond, 3 |

## Assumptions behind the model

- Electrons are either shared in pairs or held as lone pairs; the model ignores how they are spread in space.
- The skeleton is known or can be chosen from electronegativity.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Ignoring the charge.** NH₄⁺ has 8 valence electrons; CN⁻ has 10.
2. **H in the centre.** It forms only one bond.
3. **Leftover electrons not drawn.** In SF₄ the last 2 electrons are a lone pair on S.
4. **Expanded octet on a period 2 atom.** N, C, O and F never exceed 8.
5. **Double bond to an outer halogen.** Use O, N or C lone pairs to make multiple bonds.
6. **No brackets on an ion.** Brackets plus the charge show the charge belongs to the whole ion.

## Quick self-check

1. How many valence electrons are in SO₃²⁻? *(6 + 18 + 2 = 26)*
2. How many lone pairs are on the central atom of PCl₃? *(One: 3 bonds + 1 lone pair = 8)*
3. What is the bond between the two carbon atoms in C₂H₂? *(A triple bond: 10 electrons; needed 20, so 5 bonds = 2 C–H + 1 C≡C)*
4. How many electrons surround Xe in XeF₂? *(10: 2 bonds and 3 lone pairs)*
