Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/7-5-magnitude-equilibrium-constant-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/7-5-magnitude-equilibrium-constant-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain, using the shape of the K expression, why a very large K means the equilibrium mixture is mostly products. *(Guide: "What the size of K tells you"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why a very small K means the reaction barely proceeds, and why the product concentration is still not zero. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2)*
- I can say what "essentially to completion" means and why it is different from "complete". *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why K tells me nothing about how fast equilibrium is reached, and what a catalyst does and does not change. *(Guide: "K tells you where, not how fast"; Practice Q6)*

## Calculation

- I can read K from a particle diagram for a 1 : 1 reaction, and draw a diagram that matches a given K. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can use [A] + [B] = total with [B]/[A] = K to find the composition of an A ⇌ B mixture and the percentage converted. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can find the trace concentration of a product (or reactant) when K is very small (or very large). *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can show that a given set of concentrations is an equilibrium mixture by substituting into the K expression. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can use the value of K, with a short calculation, to justify or reject a claim about an equilibrium mixture. *(Practice Q5, Q7)*
- I can explain why K values are only directly comparable as ratios when the expressions have the same shape, and why K must be quoted with a temperature. *(Guide: "Comparing K values fairly"; Practice Q7)*
