## Understanding

- I can say which phase changes increase the energy of a system and which decrease it. *(Guide: "What happens to energy in a phase change"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why the temperature of a pure substance stays constant while it melts or boils. *(Guide: "Why the temperature stays constant"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why ΔH_vap is larger than ΔH_fus for the same substance. *(Guide; Practice Q6)*
- I can state that a phase change and its reverse have molar enthalpies of equal size and opposite sign. *(Guide: "Opposite changes, opposite signs"; Practice Q6)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate the energy absorbed or released in a phase change using q = n × ΔH, converting grams to moles first. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can compare the energy needed to change the phase of equal masses of different substances. *(Practice Q3)*
- I can find a molar enthalpy of fusion or vaporization from a heating curve or heating data. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4, Q6)*
- I can combine q = mcΔT and q = n × ΔH in a calculation with several steps, keeping units consistent. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can identify each sloping and flat section of a heating or cooling curve and say what is happening to the particles. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
- I can use an energy balance that includes a phase change to predict a final temperature. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can explain everyday effects, such as steam burns or ice cooling a drink, using the energy of phase changes. *(Practice Q5, Q7)*
