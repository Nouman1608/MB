## Recap

- A **buffer** resists pH change when small amounts of acid or base are added.
- It contains **large concentrations of both members of a conjugate pair**: HA with A⁻, or B with BH⁺.
- Added **H₃O⁺** is removed by the **base member** (A⁻ or B). Added **OH⁻** is removed by the **acid member** (HA or BH⁺).
- These reactions go essentially to completion. The strong acid or base is swapped for a weak one, so the pH changes only slightly.
- Each addition converts some of one member into the other. The buffer works only while both remain in large amounts.
- A buffer's pH sits near the pK_a of its weak acid, not necessarily near 7.

## Key reactions

| Buffer | Added acid (H₃O⁺) | Added base (OH⁻) |
|---|---|---|
| HA / A⁻ | A⁻ + H₃O⁺ → HA + H₂O | HA + OH⁻ → A⁻ + H₂O |
| B / BH⁺ | B + H₃O⁺ → BH⁺ + H₂O | BH⁺ + OH⁻ → B + H₂O |
| Amounts after adding x mol | base member − x, acid member + x | acid member − x, base member + x |

## Making a buffer

| Mixture | Buffer? |
|---|---|
| Weak acid + salt of its conjugate base | Yes |
| Weak base + salt of its conjugate acid | Yes |
| Excess weak acid + some strong base (or excess weak base + some strong acid) | Yes, after reaction |
| Weak acid + equal moles of strong base | No: only A⁻ remains |
| Strong acid + its salt (HCl + NaCl) | No: Cl⁻ cannot remove H₃O⁺ |
| Weak acid alone | No: almost no A⁻ |

## Assumptions

- The added acid or base is small compared with the amounts of both buffer members.
- Spectator ions (Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, NO₃⁻) take no part.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Swapping the roles.** The base member removes acid; the acid member removes base.
2. **Judging by labels.** Do the strong acid/base reaction first, then list what remains.
3. **"Buffers keep pH at 7."** They keep it near the weak acid's pK_a.
4. **"pH stays perfectly constant."** It changes, just much less than in water.
5. **Writing H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O as the buffer reaction.** Show the buffer member reacting.

## Quick self-check

1. Which buffer member removes added OH⁻ in a CH₃COOH / CH₃COO⁻ buffer? *(CH₃COOH)*
2. 0.30 mol HA and 0.20 mol A⁻; add 0.050 mol HCl. Amounts after? *(HA 0.35 mol, A⁻ 0.15 mol)*
3. Is 0.10 mol HF + 0.10 mol NaOH a buffer? *(No: only F⁻ remains)*
