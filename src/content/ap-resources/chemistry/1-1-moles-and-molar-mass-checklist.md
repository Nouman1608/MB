Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/1-1-moles-and-molar-mass-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/1-1-moles-and-molar-mass-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why chemists need the mole to connect a mass measured on a balance to a number of particles. *(Guide: "Why chemists count in moles"; Practice Q7)*
- I can state Avogadro's number with its unit, N_A = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. *(Guide)*
- I can explain why the molar mass in g mol⁻¹ has the same number as the average particle mass in amu. *(Guide: "Molar mass"; Practice Q7)*
- I can say what "particle" means for an element, a molecular compound and an ionic compound. *(Guide)*

## Calculation

- I can calculate the molar mass of a formula that contains brackets, such as Ca(NO₃)₂. *(Practice Q1)*
- I can convert a mass into moles and a number of particles, writing units at every step. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
- I can count the atoms of one element in a given amount of a compound. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can work backwards from a number of atoms to the mass of the sample. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can find the mass of a single atom or molecule, and use it to identify a substance. *(Practice Q3, Q5)*
- I can give a final answer with the correct number of significant figures. *(All worked examples)*

## Reasoning

- I can compare the numbers of particles in samples of different substances without assuming equal mass means equal particles. *(Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can state an assumption behind a mole calculation, such as sample purity or natural isotope abundance. *(Practice Q7; Revision notes)*
