Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/7-9-introduction-le-ch-teliers-principle-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/7-9-introduction-le-ch-teliers-principle-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can state Le Châtelier's principle and explain that the shift only partly undoes the stress. *(Guide: "What Le Châtelier's principle says", Figure 1)*
- I can explain a shift in terms of the forward and reverse rates becoming unequal and then equal again. *(Guide: "Why it works: rates")*
- I can name the changes that cause no shift: a catalyst, extra solid or pure liquid, and an unreactive gas added at constant volume. *(Guide: "Changes that cause no shift"; Practice Q1, Q6(c))*
- I can say which stress changes the value of K, and which do not. *(Guide: Stress 2; Practice Q3)*

## Skills

- I can predict the shift when a reactant or product is added, or removed by precipitation or reaction. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q6(d))*
- I can write heat into an equation and predict the effect of heating or cooling. *(Guide: Stress 2; Practice Q1, Q6(b))*
- I can count gas molecules to predict the effect of a volume change, including the case of equal numbers. *(Guide: Stress 3; Practice Q4, Q6(a))*
- I can count dissolved particles, leaving out solvent water, to predict the effect of dilution. *(Guide: Stress 4; Practice Q5(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can predict what an observer would see: a colour change, a pH change or a temperature change. *(Guide: "What you would actually see"; Practice Q3, Q5(c))*
- I can separate the instant effect of compressing a gas from the shift that follows. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can deduce the sign of ΔH from an observed effect of temperature. *(Practice Q5(a))*
- I can evaluate a claim about adding a solid to a heterogeneous equilibrium. *(Practice Q7)*
