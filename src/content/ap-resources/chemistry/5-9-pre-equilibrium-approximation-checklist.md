Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-9-pre-equilibrium-approximation-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-9-pre-equilibrium-approximation-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can say when the slow step's rate law is the final answer and when it is not. *(Guide: "When the slow step is not the first step"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why an intermediate must not appear in the rate law of a reaction. *(Guide; Practice Q1, Q7)*
- I can explain, in terms of particles, why a fast reversible first step stays close to equilibrium while a later step is slow. *(Guide: "The idea"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why steps after the slow step do not affect the rate law. *(Guide: "The pre-equilibrium recipe"; Practice Q7)*

## Skills

- I can write the forward and reverse rates of a fast step and set them equal to find the intermediate's concentration, with the forward constant on top. *(Guide: "The algebra in three lines"; Practice Q2)*
- I can derive a rate law with a pre-equilibrium, showing the slow step, the equilibrium expression and the substitution. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can derive a half-order rate law when a molecule splits in the fast step. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can combine step constants into an overall rate constant and give its units. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate a rate, and the intermediate's concentration, from step rate constants and reactant concentrations. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can show how a catalyst can appear in a rate law through a fast first step. *(Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can find orders from initial-rate data and decide which proposed mechanisms are consistent with them. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why a matching rate law supports a mechanism without proving it. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
