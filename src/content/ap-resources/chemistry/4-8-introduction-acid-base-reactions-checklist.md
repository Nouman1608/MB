Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/4-8-introduction-acid-base-reactions-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/4-8-introduction-acid-base-reactions-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can define a Brønsted-Lowry acid as a proton donor and a Brønsted-Lowry base as a proton acceptor. *(Guide: "Acids and bases as proton donors and acceptors")*
- I can explain why a base does not need to contain OH⁻, giving an example. *(Guide: Common misconceptions)*
- I can explain, using lone pairs and polar O–H bonds, why water can act as an acid or a base. *(Guide: "Water: an acid or a base"; Practice Q4(c))*
- I can explain the difference between a strong and a weak acid in terms of how fully it ionises in water. *(Guide: "Strong and weak acids"; Practice Q6(a))*

## Skills

- I can write the conjugate acid and conjugate base of a species with the correct charge. *(Practice Q1, Q5(a))*
- I can label the acid, base, conjugate acid and conjugate base in a proton-transfer equation. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q4)*
- I can write equations showing an amphiprotic species acting as an acid and as a base. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can read a particle model of an acid solution and work out the percentage of acid that has ionised. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3, Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can use "the stronger the acid, the weaker its conjugate base" to compare two anions as bases. *(Practice Q3, Q6(d))*
- I can evaluate a claim about whether an anion makes a solution basic. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why equal concentrations of two acids can give different H₃O⁺ concentrations. *(Guide: Worked example 3(d); Practice Q3)*
