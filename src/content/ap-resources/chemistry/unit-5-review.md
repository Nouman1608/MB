## How to use this review

Use this page after the [Unit 5 diagnostic](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/unit-5-diagnostic/). Answer all seven questions on paper before opening any answer.

These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. Species written with letters are fictional and all data are invented. Each rubric is a **suggested Marlbridge rubric**, not official scoring, and your total gives no predicted score.

## Big ideas of the unit

- **Rates of different species are linked by coefficients** and are always positive ([Topic 5.1](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-1-reaction-rates-study-guide/)).
- **An overall rate law is measured, not read from the equation.** Initial rates give the orders and k ([Topic 5.2](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-2-introduction-rate-law-study-guide/)); so does whichever of [A], ln[A] or 1/[A] against t is straight ([Topic 5.3](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-3-concentration-changes-over-time-study-guide/)).
- **For one elementary step, the coefficients are the orders.** Three-particle collisions are rare ([Topic 5.4](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-4-elementary-reactions-study-guide/)).
- **Rate depends on collision frequency, energy and orientation.** Concentration changes the first; temperature mainly the second ([Topic 5.5](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-5-collision-model-study-guide/)).
- **Eₐ is the climb to the transition state.** A higher barrier gives a slower, more temperature-sensitive step ([Topic 5.6](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-6-reaction-energy-profile-study-guide/)).
- **A mechanism must add up, use reasonable steps and match the rate law.** Intermediates are made then used; catalysts used then given back ([Topics 5.7](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-7-introduction-reaction-mechanisms-study-guide/) and [5.8](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-8-reaction-mechanism-rate-law-study-guide/)).
- **If a fast reversible step comes before the slow step, substitute out the intermediate** using forward rate = reverse rate ([Topic 5.9](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-9-pre-equilibrium-approximation-study-guide/)).
- **A multistep profile has one hump per step**, each barrier measured from where its step starts ([Topic 5.10](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-10-multistep-reaction-energy-profile-study-guide/)). **A catalyst** gives a new path with a lower highest point, leaves ΔH unchanged and can appear in the rate law ([Topic 5.11](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-11-catalysis-study-guide/)).

## Key relationships and methods

| Idea | Relationship or method | Topic |
|---|---|---|
| Rates of species | rate = −(1/a) Δ[A]/Δt = (1/c) Δ[C]/Δt | 5.1 |
| Rate law | rate = k[A]ᵐ[B]ⁿ; rate ratio = (concentration ratio)^order; k in M^(1 − overall order) s⁻¹ | 5.2 |
| Integrated laws | zero: [A] vs t slope −k; first: ln[A] vs t slope −k, constant t½ = 0.693/k; second: 1/[A] vs t slope +k | 5.3 |
| Elementary step | orders = coefficients; molecularity = particles colliding | 5.4 |
| Temperature | k = A e^(−Eₐ/RT): used to explain, never to calculate | 5.5, 5.6 |
| Profile | Eₐ(reverse) = Eₐ(forward) − ΔH; overall ΔH = sum of step ΔH | 5.6, 5.10 |
| Mechanism rate law | slow first step: its own rate law; fast equilibrium first: forward rate = reverse rate, then substitute | 5.8, 5.9 |
| Catalyst | used then regenerated; in the rate law if it reacts at or before the slow step | 5.11 |

## Question 1 (multiple choice · mixed)

The reaction 2A → B is first order in A, with −Δ[A]/Δt = k[A] and a half-life of 150 s. At what rate is B forming when [A] = 0.40 M?

- (A) 3.7 × 10⁻³ M s⁻¹
- (B) 2.7 × 10⁻³ M s⁻¹
- (C) 1.8 × 10⁻³ M s⁻¹
- (D) 9.2 × 10⁻⁴ M s⁻¹

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (D).** k = 0.693 ÷ 150 s = 4.62 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ (Topic 5.3). −Δ[A]/Δt = 4.62 × 10⁻³ × 0.40 = 1.85 × 10⁻³ M s⁻¹ (Topic 5.2). B forms half as fast: 9.2 × 10⁻⁴ M s⁻¹ (Topic 5.1).

- (A) doubles the rate for A instead of halving it.
- (B) is 0.40 ÷ 150.
- (C) is the rate for A.
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · mixed)

A reaction goes by two steps: step 1 (slow, Eₐ = 90 kJ mol⁻¹) then step 2 (fast, Eₐ = 25 kJ mol⁻¹). It is warmed by 10 K at the same concentrations. Why does the rate rise?

- (A) Step 2's rate constant increases by the larger factor.
- (B) A larger fraction of step 1 collisions can reach TS 1; step 1 stays rate-limiting.
- (C) Heating lowers step 1's Eₐ towards step 2's.
- (D) Collisions are more frequent; the fraction with enough energy hardly changes.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** The slow step sets the rate (Topic 5.8). Its barrier lies far out in the Maxwell–Boltzmann tail, so warming multiplies the fraction of step 1 collisions with enough energy (Topics 5.5 and 5.6). k₁ grows by a larger factor than k₂, so step 1 stays the bottleneck (Topic 5.10).

- (A) Step 2 is not the bottleneck, and its lower barrier makes it less temperature-sensitive.
- (C) Temperature does not change Eₐ.
- (D) Collision frequency rises only slightly.
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · mixed)

For 2A + B → A₂B, experiments give rate = k[A]²[B]. Which mechanism adds up to the overall equation, predicts this rate law **and** uses only unimolecular or bimolecular steps?

- (A) One step: 2A + B → A₂B
- (B) A + B → AB (slow); AB + A → A₂B (fast)
- (C) A + A ⇌ A₂ (fast, reversible); A₂ + B → A₂B (slow)
- (D) A + A ⇌ A₂ (fast, reversible); A₂ + B → AB + A (slow)

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** The steps add to 2A + B → A₂B (Topic 5.7). The slow step gives rate = k₂[A₂][B]; the pre-equilibrium gives [A₂] = (k₁/k₋₁)[A]², so rate = k[A]²[B] (Topic 5.9). Both steps are bimolecular.

- (A) fits, but it is termolecular, which is rare (Topic 5.4).
- (B) adds up but predicts rate = k[A][B].
- (D) has the right rate law, but its steps add to A + B → AB.
</details>

## Question 4 (constructed response · mixed)

Initial rates for M₂ + 2N → 2MN at one temperature:

| Experiment | [M₂] (M) | [N] (M) | Initial rate (M s⁻¹) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.040 | 0.050 | 3.0 × 10⁻⁵ |
| 2 | 0.160 | 0.050 | 6.0 × 10⁻⁵ |
| 3 | 0.160 | 0.150 | 1.8 × 10⁻⁴ |

(a) Determine the order with respect to M₂ and to N.
(b) Write the rate law and calculate k with units.
(c) Explain why this reaction cannot be a single elementary step.
(d) Mechanism I: M₂ + N → MN + M (slow); M + N → MN (fast). Mechanism II: M₂ ⇌ 2M (fast, reversible); M + N → MN (slow, happens twice). Which is consistent with the data?
(e) Predict the initial rate when [M₂] = 0.090 M and [N] = 0.20 M.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** Experiments 1 → 2: [M₂] × 4, rate × 2. 4ᵐ = 2, so **m = ½**. Experiments 2 → 3: [N] × 3, rate × 3, so **first order in N**.

**(b)** rate = k[M₂]^½[N]. k = 3.0 × 10⁻⁵ ÷ (0.20 × 0.050) = **3.0 × 10⁻³ M^(−½) s⁻¹** (overall order 1½). All three experiments agree.

**(c)** An elementary step's orders are its whole-number coefficients, so an order of ½ cannot come from one collision.

**(d)** Both mechanisms add up to M₂ + 2N → 2MN.
- Mechanism I: slow first step, so rate = k[M₂][N]. First order in M₂: **rejected**.
- Mechanism II: slow step rate = k₂[M][N], with M an intermediate. k₁[M₂] = k₋₁[M]², so [M] = (K₁[M₂])^½ and rate = k₂K₁^½[M₂]^½[N]. **Consistent** (not proven).

**(e)** rate = 3.0 × 10⁻³ × (0.090)^½ × 0.20 = 3.0 × 10⁻³ × 0.30 × 0.20 = **1.8 × 10⁻⁴ M s⁻¹**.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | (a) Half order in M₂ from experiments 1 and 2 |
| 1 | (a) First order in N from experiments 2 and 3 |
| 1 | (b) Rate law and k = 3.0 × 10⁻³ |
| 1 | (b) Units M^(−½) s⁻¹ |
| 1 | (c) Elementary orders are whole-number coefficients |
| 1 | (d) Mechanism I rejected because it predicts first order in M₂ |
| 1 | (d) Mechanism II: [M] substituted to give [M₂]^½[N] |
| 1 | (e) 1.8 × 10⁻⁴ M s⁻¹ (carry forward from (b)) |

**Total: 8 points.**
</details>

## Question 5 (constructed response · mixed)

Compound A decomposes: A → 2B. Data at 300 K:

| t (s) | 0 | 50 | 100 | 150 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [A] (M) | 0.0800 | 0.0566 | 0.0400 | 0.0283 |

At 320 K, with the same starting concentration, the half-life is 25 s.

(a) Show with calculated values that the reaction is first order in A.
(b) Find k at 300 K, with units.
(c) Calculate the rate of formation of B at t = 100 s at 300 K.
(d) Find k at 320 K, and the time at 320 K for [A] to fall from 0.0800 M to 0.00500 M.
(e) Use a sketch of Maxwell–Boltzmann curves to explain why k is four times larger at 320 K. Evaluate a student's claim that Eₐ is lower at 320 K.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** ln[A]: −2.526, −2.872, −3.219, −3.565, falling by a constant 0.346–0.347 per 50 s, so ln[A] against t is straight. 1/[A] rises by 5.2, 7.3, 10.3 M⁻¹: not constant. **First order.** (Also: [A] halves every 100 s.)

**(b)** Slope = −0.00693 s⁻¹, so **k = 6.93 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹** (0.693 ÷ 100 s).

**(c)** −Δ[A]/Δt = k[A] = 6.93 × 10⁻³ × 0.0400 = 2.77 × 10⁻⁴ M s⁻¹. B forms twice as fast: **5.54 × 10⁻⁴ M s⁻¹**.

**(d)** k = 0.693 ÷ 25 s = **0.0277 s⁻¹**, four times the 300 K value. 0.0800 → 0.00500 M is four half-lives: **100 s**.

**(e)** Sketch number of particles against kinetic energy. The 320 K curve has a lower peak, shifted to higher energy, with a thicker tail and the same area. A larger area lies beyond Eₐ at 320 K, so a larger fraction of collisions reach the transition state; collisions are only slightly more frequent. The student is **wrong**: Eₐ does not change with temperature.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | (a) ln[A] values with constant differences (or constant half-life) |
| 1 | (a) 1/[A] (or [A]) shown not to be linear |
| 1 | (b) k = 6.93 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ with units |
| 1 | (c) 5.54 × 10⁻⁴ M s⁻¹, doubling the rate for A |
| 1 | (d) 0.0277 s⁻¹ and 100 s |
| 1 | (e) Sketch: lower, right-shifted peak at 320 K |
| 1 | (e) Larger fraction beyond Eₐ named as the main cause |
| 1 | (e) Rejects the claim: Eₐ does not change with temperature |

**Total: 8 points.** Do not award the sketch point if the 320 K peak is drawn taller.
</details>

## Question 6 (constructed response · mixed)

Uncatalysed, R + T → RT is one step with Eₐ = 120 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔH = −30 kJ mol⁻¹. A trace of Z makes it much faster, by this proposed mechanism:

Step 1: T + Z ⇌ TZ
Step 2: TZ + R → RTZ
Step 3: RTZ → RT + Z

On the catalysed profile, relative to the reactants at 0 kJ mol⁻¹: TS 1 is at 34, TZ at 20, TS 2 at 72, RTZ at −10, TS 3 at 15 and the products at −30.

(a) Show that the steps add up, and identify the catalyst and the intermediates with reasons.
(b) Calculate Eₐ for each catalysed step and identify the rate-limiting step.
(c) Use the profile to explain why step 1 can be treated as a fast equilibrium, then derive the rate law.
(d) Predict the effect on the rate of doubling [T] while halving [Z].
(e) A student says: "Step 1 is uphill, so it must be the slow step." Evaluate this, and show that ΔH is the same with and without Z.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** Cancel TZ, RTZ and Z from both sides: **R + T → RT**. **Z** is the catalyst: used in step 1, given back in step 3. **TZ** and **RTZ** are intermediates: each made in one step, used in a later one.

**(b)** Step 1: 34 − 0 = **34**; step 2: 72 − 20 = **52**; step 3: 15 − (−10) = **25** kJ mol⁻¹. Step 2 has the largest barrier, and TS 2 is the highest point: **step 2 is rate-limiting**.

**(c)** From TZ, the way back over TS 1 is 34 − 20 = 14 kJ mol⁻¹, far less than the 52 kJ mol⁻¹ climb over TS 2. So TZ usually falls apart before it reacts with R: step 1 stays near equilibrium. Slow step: rate = k₂[TZ][R]. k₁[T][Z] = k₋₁[TZ], so [TZ] = (k₁/k₋₁)[T][Z] and **rate = k[R][T][Z]**.

**(d)** rate ∝ [T][Z]: 2 × ½ = **1**, no change.

**(e)** **Wrong.** Barriers decide speed, not a step's ΔH: step 1's barrier (34) is smaller than step 2's (52). Step ΔH values +20, −30 and −20 kJ mol⁻¹ sum to **−30 kJ mol⁻¹**, the same as without Z.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | (a) Correct sum with cancellations |
| 1 | (a) Z catalyst; TZ and RTZ intermediates, with reasons |
| 1 | (b) 34, 52 and 25 kJ mol⁻¹; step 2 rate-limiting |
| 1 | (c) Small return barrier from TZ (14 against 52) justifies the equilibrium |
| 1 | (c) rate = k[R][T][Z] with TZ substituted |
| 1 | (d) No change (2 × ½) |
| 1 | (e) Rejects the claim (barriers decide) and shows ΔH = −30 kJ mol⁻¹ on both paths |

**Total: 7 points.**
</details>

## Question 7 (constructed response · mixed)

Gas A dimerises: 2A → A₂. The rate law is written −Δ[A]/Δt = k[A]². The times are not equally spaced.

| t (s) | 0 | 40 | 160 | 240 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [A] (M) | 0.0250 | 0.0200 | 0.0125 | 0.0100 |

(a) Show that the data fit a second-order rate law, and find k.
(b) Predict the time at which [A] reaches 0.00500 M.
(c) A student says: "[A] halved in the first 160 s, so it will be 0.00625 M at 320 s." Evaluate this.
(d) If the reaction is one elementary step, explain at the particle level why rate ∝ [A]². What can the data show about this proposal?

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** 1/[A]: 40.0, 50.0, 80.0, 100.0 M⁻¹. Slopes: 10 ÷ 40, 30 ÷ 120, 20 ÷ 80 = **0.250 M⁻¹ s⁻¹** each, so 1/[A] against t is straight. ln[A] slopes are −0.00558, −0.00392, −0.00279 s⁻¹, not constant. Second order; slope = +k, so **k = 0.250 M⁻¹ s⁻¹**.

**(b)** t = (1/0.00500 − 1/0.0250) ÷ 0.250 = (200 − 40) ÷ 0.250 = **640 s**.

**(c)** **Wrong.** Only a first-order half-life is constant. The next half-life (0.0125 → 0.00625 M) is 1 ÷ (0.250 × 0.0125) = 320 s, so [A] reaches 0.00625 M at 480 s. At 320 s, 1/[A] = 40 + 0.250 × 320 = 120 M⁻¹, so [A] = 0.00833 M. Half-lives lengthen as [A] falls.

**(d)** Two A particles must collide. Doubling [A] doubles the A particles **and** the partners each can hit, so A–A collisions per second rise fourfold; at constant temperature the successful fraction is unchanged, so rate ∝ [A]². The data are **consistent** with a bimolecular step but do not prove it: a multistep mechanism could give the same rate law.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | (a) Constant 1/[A] slope shown, and ln[A] slope not constant |
| 1 | (a) k = 0.250 M⁻¹ s⁻¹ |
| 1 | (b) 640 s |
| 1 | (c) Rejects the claim: second-order half-lives get longer |
| 1 | (d) Collision-counting reason for [A]² |
| 1 | (d) Consistent with one step, but not proof |

**Total: 6 points.**
</details>
