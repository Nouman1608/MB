## Recap

- Particles are always moving. At any moment they have a **spread** of kinetic energies.
- Temperature measures the **average** kinetic energy of the particles. The particles in a warmer body have a greater average kinetic energy than those in a cooler body.
- When two bodies are in **thermal contact**, their particles collide. On average, energy passes from the faster particles to the slower ones, so energy moves from the warmer body to the cooler body. This is **heat transfer** (also called heat exchange, or transfer of energy as heat).
- The warmer body cools, the cooler body warms, and the net transfer slows as the gap closes.
- At **thermal equilibrium** the average kinetic energies, and so the temperatures, are equal. Particles still move and collide, but energy passed each way is equal: **no net transfer**.
- In an insulated system, energy lost by the warmer body = energy gained by the cooler body, so the final temperature lies **between** the two starting temperatures.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Meaning | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| KE = ½mv² | kinetic energy of one particle of mass m and speed v | explain why lighter particles move faster at the same temperature |
| average KE ∝ T (in K) | doubling the Kelvin temperature doubles the average kinetic energy | compare two temperatures: KE₂ / KE₁ = T₂ / T₁ |
| T(K) = T(°C) + 273.15 | Kelvin conversion | convert before any ratio |
| net heat flow: higher T → lower T | direction of transfer | say which body gains energy |
| equilibrium: T₁ = T₂ | equal average kinetic energy | say when transfer stops |

## Assumptions behind the model

- The two bodies are **insulated** from everything else, so no energy is lost to the air or the container.
- Energy moves only **as heat**; no work is done, and there is no phase change or reaction.
- Each body has a single, uniform temperature at any moment (stirring helps make this true).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **"Cold moves."** Only energy moves, warmer → cooler.
2. **"Particles stop at equilibrium."** They keep colliding; only the *net* transfer stops.
3. **Same temperature ≠ same speed.** It means the same average kinetic energy.
4. **Same temperature ≠ same total energy.** A bigger body has more particles.
5. **Celsius ratios.** Convert to kelvin before comparing kinetic energies.
6. **Saying "heat" for temperature.** Temperature is a property of a body; heat is energy in transit.

## Quick self-check

1. A gas is heated from 200 K to 400 K. By what factor does its average kinetic energy change? *(It doubles.)*
2. A gas is heated from 100 °C to 200 °C. Does its average kinetic energy double? *(No: 373.15 K → 473.15 K, a factor of 1.268.)*
3. Two blocks at 30 °C touch. Is there heat transfer between them? *(No net transfer: their average kinetic energies are equal, although particles still collide.)*
