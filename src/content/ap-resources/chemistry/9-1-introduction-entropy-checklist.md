## Understanding

- I can describe entropy as a measure of how dispersed the matter and the energy of a system are. *(Guide: "Entropy as dispersal")*
- I can explain why some endothermic processes still happen, using the idea that energy is not the only factor. *(Guide: "Why energy alone is not enough")*
- I can explain, using particle pictures, why entropy increases from solid to liquid to gas. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why vaporisation gives a much larger entropy increase than melting. *(Guide: "Matter dispersal in phase changes"; Practice Q2)*
- I can use the kinetic-energy distribution to explain why the entropy of a substance increases with temperature. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q3)*

## Skills

- I can predict the sign of ΔS for any phase change and its reverse. *(Practice Q1, Q7(a))*
- I can predict the sign of ΔS when a gas expands or is compressed at constant temperature. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5(a))*
- I can predict the sign of ΔS for a reaction by counting moles of gas only, ignoring solids and liquids. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q6(a))*
- I can rank entropy changes for several processes by size, using phase and the change in moles of gas. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain how changing a product's state, such as H₂O(l) to H₂O(g), changes ΔS for a reaction. *(Practice Q6(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can recognise when equal moles of gas mean the sign of ΔS cannot be predicted by counting, and say so. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can say when two effects cannot be ranked without data. *(Practice Q5(c))*
- I can reject the claim that the sign of ΔH fixes the sign of ΔS, with a reason. *(Practice Q6(c))*
- I can support an entropy claim with particle-level evidence, naming whether matter or energy becomes more dispersed. *(Practice Q5, Q7)*
