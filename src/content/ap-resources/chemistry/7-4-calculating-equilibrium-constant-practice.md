These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. The mark points are a suggested Marlbridge rubric to help you check your work; they are not an official scoring guideline. Concentrations are in mol L⁻¹ and partial pressures in atm. All measurements are invented for practice, and every system is at constant temperature. K is written without units.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · foundation)

For N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g), an equilibrium mixture contains [N₂O₄] = 0.0400 mol L⁻¹ and [NO₂] = 0.0120 mol L⁻¹. What is Kc?

- (A) 3.60 × 10⁻³
- (B) 1.44 × 10⁻²
- (C) 0.300
- (D) 278

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Kc = [NO₂]² / [N₂O₄] = (0.0120)² / 0.0400 = 1.44 × 10⁻⁴ / 0.0400 = 3.60 × 10⁻³.

- (B) doubles [NO₂] before squaring, (2 × 0.0120)² ÷ 0.0400, treating the coefficient as a multiplier as well as a power.
- (C) forgets to square [NO₂].
- (D) is the expression upside down.
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · core)

For FeO(s) + CO(g) ⇌ Fe(s) + CO₂(g), the equilibrium partial pressures are P_CO = 0.80 atm and P_CO₂ = 0.32 atm. What is Kp?

- (A) 2.5
- (B) 1.12
- (C) 0.40
- (D) 0.26

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** The solids are left out, so Kp = P_CO₂ / P_CO = 0.32 / 0.80 = 0.40.

- (A) is upside down (0.80 ÷ 0.32).
- (B) adds the two pressures.
- (D) multiplies the two pressures (0.32 × 0.80 = 0.256) instead of dividing, as if CO were a product.
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · core)

A vessel starts with [A] = 0.100 mol L⁻¹ and [B] = 0.100 mol L⁻¹ and no C. The reaction is A(g) + B(g) ⇌ 2C(g). At equilibrium [C] = 0.040 mol L⁻¹. What is Kc?

- (A) 0.16
- (B) 0.25
- (C) 0.44
- (D) 6.25

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** Making 0.040 mol L⁻¹ of C uses 0.020 mol L⁻¹ each of A and B (1 : 1 : 2). So [A] = [B] = 0.080 mol L⁻¹, and Kc = (0.040)² / (0.080 × 0.080) = 0.0016 / 0.0064 = 0.25.

- (A) uses the starting values 0.100 for A and B.
- (C) subtracts 0.040 from A and B, ignoring the 1 : 2 ratio, so [A] = [B] = 0.060.
- (D) forgets to square [C].
</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · core)

For 2A(g) ⇌ B(g), two equilibrium mixtures are made at the same temperature. In mixture 2, [A] is twice as large as in mixture 1. How does [B] in mixture 2 compare with [B] in mixture 1?

- (A) half as large
- (B) the same
- (C) twice as large
- (D) four times as large

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (D).** Kc = [B] / [A]² is the same in both mixtures. Doubling [A] makes [A]² four times larger, so [B] must also be four times larger to keep the ratio equal to Kc.

- (A) has the relationship upside down.
- (B) wrongly assumes [B] is fixed; K is fixed, not the individual concentrations.
- (C) ignores the power of 2 on [A].
</details>

## Question 5 (constructed response · core)

A student mixes 10.0 mL of 0.00500 mol L⁻¹ Fe(NO₃)₃ with 10.0 mL of 0.00300 mol L⁻¹ KSCN. A red complex ion forms:

Fe³⁺(aq) + SCN⁻(aq) ⇌ FeSCN²⁺(aq)

The absorbance of the mixture is measured every minute. After it stops changing, a calibration graph shows [FeSCN²⁺] = 3.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol L⁻¹.

(a) Write the expression for Kc.
(b) Calculate [Fe³⁺] and [SCN⁻] immediately after mixing, before any reaction.
(c) Calculate the equilibrium concentrations of Fe³⁺ and SCN⁻.
(d) Calculate Kc.
(e) Explain why the student waited for the absorbance to stop changing.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** Kc = [FeSCN²⁺] / ([Fe³⁺][SCN⁻]).

**(b)** The total volume is 20.0 mL, so each solution is diluted by a factor of 2.
[Fe³⁺]₀ = 0.00500 × 10.0 / 20.0 = **2.50 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹**; [SCN⁻]₀ = 0.00300 × 10.0 / 20.0 = **1.50 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹**.

**(c)** Forming 3.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol L⁻¹ of FeSCN²⁺ uses the same amount of each reactant (1 : 1 : 1).

| | Fe³⁺ | SCN⁻ | FeSCN²⁺ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial | 2.50 × 10⁻³ | 1.50 × 10⁻³ | 0 |
| Change | −3.00 × 10⁻⁴ | −3.00 × 10⁻⁴ | +3.00 × 10⁻⁴ |
| Equilibrium | 2.20 × 10⁻³ | 1.20 × 10⁻³ | 3.00 × 10⁻⁴ |

**(d)** Kc = 3.00 × 10⁻⁴ / (2.20 × 10⁻³ × 1.20 × 10⁻³) = 3.00 × 10⁻⁴ / 2.64 × 10⁻⁶ = **114**.

**(e)** K must be calculated from equilibrium concentrations. A constant absorbance shows that [FeSCN²⁺] has stopped changing, so the system has reached equilibrium.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Correct Kc expression |
| 1 | Both starting concentrations halved for dilution |
| 1 | Equilibrium concentrations found by subtracting 3.00 × 10⁻⁴ from each reactant |
| 1 | Kc = 114 (accept 113–114) |
| 1 | Constant absorbance means constant concentration, so equilibrium |

Common errors: forgetting the dilution gives 23.6; using the starting concentrations without subtracting gives 80.0. Carry forward an error from (b) into (c) and (d) if the method is otherwise correct.
</details>

## Question 6 (constructed response · stretch)

Pure hydrogen sulfide is sealed in a rigid container and heated to a fixed high temperature. At that temperature, before any reaction, its pressure would be 1.00 atm. It partly decomposes:

2H₂S(g) ⇌ 2H₂(g) + S₂(g)

At equilibrium the total pressure is 1.15 atm.

(a) Explain why the total pressure rises as the reaction proceeds.
(b) Calculate the equilibrium partial pressure of each gas.
(c) Calculate Kp.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** Every 2 mol of gas that react form 3 mol of gas. At constant volume and temperature, pressure is proportional to the amount of gas, so the pressure rises.

**(b)** Let the partial pressure of S₂ formed be y atm.

| | H₂S | H₂ | S₂ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial | 1.00 | 0 | 0 |
| Change | −2y | +2y | +y |
| Equilibrium | 1.00 − 2y | 2y | y |

Total pressure = (1.00 − 2y) + 2y + y = 1.00 + y = 1.15, so y = 0.15 atm.
P_S₂ = **0.15 atm**, P_H₂ = **0.30 atm**, P_H₂S = 1.00 − 0.30 = **0.70 atm**.

**(c)** Kp = (P_H₂)² × P_S₂ / (P_H₂S)² = (0.30)² × 0.15 / (0.70)² = 0.0135 / 0.49 = **0.028** (0.02755 before rounding; two significant figures, because y = 0.15 has two).

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | More moles of gas on the product side, so pressure rises at constant volume and temperature |
| 1 | Change row in the ratio −2y : +2y : +y |
| 1 | Total pressure expression 1.00 + y = 1.15, giving y = 0.15 |
| 1 | Three correct partial pressures |
| 1 | Kp ≈ 0.028 from a correct expression |
</details>

## Question 7 (constructed response · core)

Three experiments study CO(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ CO₂(g) + H₂(g) at the same temperature. The equilibrium concentrations (mol L⁻¹) are:

| Experiment | [CO] | [H₂O] | [CO₂] | [H₂] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.200 | 0.300 | 0.240 | 0.250 |
| 2 | 0.100 | 0.400 | 0.200 | 0.200 |
| 3 | 0.150 | 0.150 | 0.300 | ? |

(a) Calculate Kc from experiment 1 and from experiment 2.
(b) A student claims: "Experiment 2 has more water, so its K should be larger." Evaluate the claim using your answers to (a).
(c) Calculate [H₂] in experiment 3.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** Kc = [CO₂][H₂] / ([CO][H₂O]).
Experiment 1: (0.240 × 0.250) / (0.200 × 0.300) = 0.0600 / 0.0600 = **1.00**.
Experiment 2: (0.200 × 0.200) / (0.100 × 0.400) = 0.0400 / 0.0400 = **1.00**.

**(b)** The claim is **wrong**. Both experiments give Kc = 1.00. The equilibrium concentrations are different, but K depends only on the reaction and the temperature, which are the same.

**(c)** Kc = 1.00 = (0.300 × [H₂]) / (0.150 × 0.150), so [H₂] = 1.00 × 0.0225 / 0.300 = **0.0750 mol L⁻¹**.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Correct expression and Kc = 1.00 for experiment 1 |
| 1 | Kc = 1.00 for experiment 2 |
| 1 | Claim wrong: same K, because K depends only on temperature for a given reaction |
| 1 | [H₂] = 0.0750 mol L⁻¹ using Kc = 1.00 |
</details>
