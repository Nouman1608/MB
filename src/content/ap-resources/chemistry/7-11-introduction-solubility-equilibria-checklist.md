Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/7-11-introduction-solubility-equilibria-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/7-11-introduction-solubility-equilibria-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can describe a saturated solution as a dynamic equilibrium in which dissolving and precipitating happen at equal rates. *(Guide: "Dissolving is an equilibrium too", Figure 1)*
- I can explain why adding more solid to a saturated solution does not change the ion concentrations. *(Guide: Common misconceptions; Practice Q7(b))*
- I can explain the difference between molar solubility (mol L⁻¹) and K_sp. *(Guide: "Molar solubility and how it links to K_sp")*
- I can link a K_sp greater than 1 to a soluble salt, such as any sodium, potassium, ammonium or nitrate salt. *(Guide: "Connecting K_sp to the solubility rules"; Practice Q4)*

## Calculation

- I can write the K_sp expression for any ionic solid from its formula, leaving out the solid. *(Practice Q1)*
- I can write K_sp in terms of s for any ion ratio, using (xs)ˣ(ys)ʸ. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can calculate the molar solubility of a salt from its K_sp and convert it to g L⁻¹ or a mass in a given volume. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate K_sp from a measured solubility in grams per volume. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can calculate K_sp from the measured concentration of just one ion. *(Practice Q6(a), (b))*
- I can use Q and K_sp, after allowing for dilution, to predict whether a precipitate forms when two solutions are mixed. *(Guide: "Will a precipitate form?"; Practice Q6(c))*

## Reasoning

- I can rank salts by solubility, comparing K_sp directly only when their ion ratios match and calculating s when they do not. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3)*
- I can evaluate a claim that equal K_sp values mean equal solubilities. *(Practice Q7(a))*
- I can state the assumptions behind a K_sp calculation. *(Guide: "Assumptions in these calculations"; Revision notes)*
