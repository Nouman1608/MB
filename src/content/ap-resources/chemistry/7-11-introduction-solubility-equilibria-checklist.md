Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/7-11-introduction-solubility-equilibria-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/7-11-introduction-solubility-equilibria-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can describe a saturated solution as a dynamic equilibrium in which dissolving and precipitating happen at equal rates. *(Guide: "Dissolving is an equilibrium too", Figure 1)*
- I can explain why adding more solid to a saturated solution does not change the ion concentrations. *(Guide: Common misconceptions; Practice Q7(b))*
- I can explain the difference between molar solubility (mol L⁻¹) and K<sub>sp</sub>. *(Guide: "Molar solubility and how it links to K<sub>sp</sub>")*
- I can link a K<sub>sp</sub> greater than 1 to a soluble salt, such as any sodium, potassium, ammonium or nitrate salt. *(Guide: "Connecting K<sub>sp</sub> to the solubility rules"; Practice Q4)*

## Calculation

- I can write the K<sub>sp</sub> expression for any ionic solid from its formula, leaving out the solid. *(Practice Q1)*
- I can write K<sub>sp</sub> in terms of s for any ion ratio, using (xs)ˣ(ys)ʸ. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can calculate the molar solubility of a salt from its K<sub>sp</sub> and convert it to g L⁻¹ or a mass in a given volume. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate K<sub>sp</sub> from a measured solubility in grams per volume. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can calculate K<sub>sp</sub> from the measured concentration of just one ion. *(Practice Q6(a), (b))*
- I can use Q and K<sub>sp</sub>, after allowing for dilution, to predict whether a precipitate forms when two solutions are mixed. *(Guide: "Will a precipitate form?"; Practice Q6(c))*

## Reasoning

- I can rank salts by solubility, comparing K<sub>sp</sub> directly only when their ion ratios match and calculating s when they do not. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3)*
- I can evaluate a claim that equal K<sub>sp</sub> values mean equal solubilities. *(Practice Q7(a))*
- I can state the assumptions behind a K<sub>sp</sub> calculation. *(Guide: "Assumptions in these calculations"; Revision notes)*
