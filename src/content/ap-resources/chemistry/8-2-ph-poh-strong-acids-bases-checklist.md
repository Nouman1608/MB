Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/8-2-ph-poh-strong-acids-bases-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/8-2-ph-poh-strong-acids-bases-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can name the six strong acids and the group 1 and group 2 hydroxides as strong bases. *(Guide: "What makes an acid or base strong")*
- I can write a single-arrow ionisation equation for a strong acid and a dissociation equation for a strong base. *(Guide; Figure 1)*
- I can explain the difference between a strong acid and a concentrated acid. *(Guide: "Strong is not the same as concentrated")*
- I can explain why only the first proton of H₂SO₄ counts as fully ionised. *(Guide: FAQ and strong acid table)*

## Calculation

- I can find the pH and pOH of a strong acid solution from its concentration. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can find [OH⁻], pOH and pH for a group 1 or group 2 hydroxide, using the correct factor. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q6(a))*
- I can list every species in a strong acid or strong base solution with its concentration, including the minor ion from K_w. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3, Q6(c))*
- I can calculate pH after a dilution, or work out the dilution needed for a target pH. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4)*
- I can find the pH when two strong acids are mixed, using total moles and total volume. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate the mass of a strong base needed to make a solution with a given pH. *(Practice Q6(d))*

## Reasoning

- I can justify why two strong bases at the same concentration can have different pH values. *(Practice Q6(b))*
- I can explain why pH = −log c fails for a very dilute strong acid, and why its pH stays below 7. *(Guide: "A very dilute strong acid"; Practice Q7)*
