## Understanding

- I can give examples of reversible physical processes (evaporation and condensation, gas absorption and desorption, dissolving and precipitating). *(Guide: "Reversible processes are everywhere"; Practice Q2)*
- I can name proton transfer (acid–base) and electron transfer (redox) as reversible chemical processes. *(Guide: "Reversible processes are everywhere"; Practice Q7)*
- I can list the three things you observe at equilibrium: no visible change, all species present, constant concentrations or partial pressures. *(Guide: "What you see when a system reaches equilibrium"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why equilibrium is dynamic: the forward and reverse processes continue at equal rates. *(Guide: "Equilibrium is dynamic"; Practice Q1, Q2)*
- I can explain why a closed system is needed for equilibrium. *(Guide: Common misconceptions)*

## Skills

- I can find the time at which equilibrium is reached from a table or graph of concentration or partial pressure against time. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can describe how the forward and reverse rates change from the start of a reaction until equilibrium, and sketch the rate–time graph. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can use the coefficients to check graph data or to find equilibrium partial pressures from a measured change. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can refute the claim that equilibrium is where concentration lines cross or where amounts are equal. *(Guide: Worked example 1(b); Practice Q3)*
- I can use labelled-atom evidence to argue that an equilibrium is dynamic. *(Guide: "Equilibrium is dynamic"; Practice Q5)*
- I can use data to show that a reaction has not gone to completion and has not stopped. *(Practice Q7)*
