## Recap

- The phase of a substance depends on a balance between **interparticle forces** (pulling particles together) and **thermal motion** (pushing them apart).
- **Crystalline solid:** particles in a regular, repeating 3-D pattern. **Amorphous solid** (such as glass): no repeating pattern. In both, particles vibrate but do not move past one another.
- **Liquid:** particles touch, move, collide and swap neighbours. Hydrogen bonding or polarity can give short-range order; higher temperature disrupts it.
- **Gas:** particles far apart, moving in straight lines between collisions. Spacing and collision frequency depend on temperature, pressure and volume.
- Particles keep their size and identity in every phase. Melting and boiling overcome forces *between* particles, not bonds *inside* molecules.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Meaning | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Molar volume = M ÷ density | volume of 1 mol of the substance | compare phases quantitatively |
| V(solid) ≈ V(liquid) | particles touch in both | explain why melting changes volume only a little |
| V(gas) ≫ V(liquid) | particles separate completely | explain why boiling changes volume hundreds or thousands of times |
| spacing ratio = ∛(volume ratio) | length scales as the cube root of volume | estimate how far apart gas particles are |

## Assumptions behind the models

- Each circle stands for one whole particle (atom, molecule or ion), drawn the same size in every phase.
- A 2-D drawing stands for a 3-D arrangement.
- Density data are for a stated temperature and pressure; gas densities change a lot with conditions.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Gaps in a liquid.** Liquid particles touch; draw them almost as close as in the solid.
2. **Gas in a heap.** Spread gas particles through the whole box.
3. **Changing the particle.** Same size, same atoms, in all phases.
4. **Splitting molecules.** Boiled Br₂ is still Br₂, not Br atoms.
5. **"Solid particles are still."** They vibrate about fixed positions.
6. **"All solids are crystalline."** Amorphous solids have no long-range order.
7. **Phase diagrams.** Interpreting them is not assessed in this course; spend your time on particle models.

## Quick self-check

1. Name one crystalline and one amorphous solid. *(For example, table salt and glass.)*
2. Why do the solid and liquid of one substance have similar molar volumes? *(Particles are in contact in both phases.)*
3. A gas has 1000 times the molar volume of its liquid. Roughly how many times farther apart are its particles? *(∛1000 = 10 times.)*
4. What happens to the spacing of gas particles when the gas is compressed at constant temperature? *(It decreases, and collisions become more frequent.)*
