Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, figures and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/1-7-periodic-trends-study-guide/).

## Recap

- Properties repeat (**periodicity**) because valence electron configurations repeat. Elements in a group have the same outer pattern, such as ns¹ for group 1.
- Each period fills a new outer shell and ends with a filled shell at a noble gas.
- Every trend is explained with **Coulomb's law** (F ∝ q₁q₂ / r²), the **shell model**, **shielding** by core electrons and **effective nuclear charge**, Z_eff.
- Simple estimate: Z_eff ≈ protons − core electrons (Na +1, Cl +7, K +1).

## Key relationships

| Property | Across a period → | Down a group ↓ | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Atomic radius | decreases | increases | Z_eff rises in the same shell; a new shell is farther out |
| First ionization energy | increases (dips at groups 13 and 16) | decreases | stronger or weaker attraction for the outer electron |
| Electron affinity (energy released) | usually more | usually less | larger Z_eff or a closer shell attracts the added electron more |
| Electronegativity | increases | decreases | bonding pair held closer or by a larger Z_eff |

**Ions:** cations are smaller than their atoms (a shell is lost); anions are larger (extra repulsion, same protons). In an isoelectronic series, more protons means a smaller ion.

**Successive ionization energies:** each one is larger; the big jump shows that the next electron comes from an inner shell. Magnesium: 738, 1451, 7733 kJ mol⁻¹, so it forms Mg²⁺.

## Assumptions behind the model

- Core electrons shield fully and valence electrons barely shield each other (the simple Z_eff estimate).
- An electron in a higher shell is, on average, farther from the nucleus.
- Trends are general patterns. Explain any exception from the configuration.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **"More shielding across a period."** Core shielding is about constant; nuclear charge changes.
2. **"More electrons, bigger atom."** Not across a period.
3. **"Half-filled is stable" as the whole explanation.** Name the paired-electron repulsion.
4. **Describing instead of explaining.** Give the Coulomb's law reason, not just the arrow.
5. **Electron affinity vs electronegativity.** Isolated atom gaining an electron vs atom attracting a shared pair in a bond.
6. **Sign confusion.** Tables use different signs for electron affinity; compare how much energy is released.

## Quick self-check

1. Which is larger, Mg or Mg²⁺? *(Mg: Mg²⁺ has lost its n = 3 shell.)*
2. Why is the first ionization energy of Al lower than that of Mg? *(Al loses a 3p electron, higher in energy and more shielded than Mg's 3s electron.)*
3. Put N³⁻, O²⁻ and F⁻ in order of increasing radius. *(F⁻ < O²⁻ < N³⁻: all have 10 electrons, and fewer protons gives a larger ion.)*
4. Which is more electronegative, P or N? *(N: its bonding electrons are in a shell closer to the nucleus.)*
