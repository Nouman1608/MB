## Recap

- All particles of matter are in **continuous, random motion**. KMT uses this to explain the properties of gases.
- An **ideal gas** in KMT: particles move in straight lines, have negligible volume, do not attract or repel, and collide elastically.
- **Pressure** comes from particles hitting the walls. It rises if the hits are more frequent or harder.
- The **Kelvin temperature** is proportional to the **average kinetic energy** of the particles.
- A **Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution** shows the spread of particle speeds or energies at one temperature.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Meaning | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| KE = ½mv² | kinetic energy of one particle (m mass, v speed) | link energy, mass and speed |
| average KE ∝ T (kelvin) | doubling T doubles average KE | predict the effect of heating |
| v₁ / v₂ = √(M₂ / M₁) at equal T | lighter particles are faster | compare average speeds of two gases |
| average speed ∝ √T for one gas | doubling T raises speed by √2 | compare one gas at two temperatures |

## Assumptions behind the model

- Particle volume is tiny compared with the container volume.
- There are no forces between particles except during collisions.
- Collisions are elastic: total kinetic energy is conserved.
- Average kinetic energy is proportional to the **Kelvin** temperature, so always work in kelvin.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Same T ≠ same speed.** Same average KE, but heavier particles move more slowly.
2. **Speed ratio is a square root.** A particle 4 times heavier is 2 times slower, not 4.
3. **Squeezing at constant T does not speed particles up.** Pressure rises because hits per unit area become more frequent.
4. **Celsius ratios are meaningless.** Convert to kelvin before comparing.
5. **Hot curve is lower, not taller.** Same area, spread wider.
6. **No bell curve.** Start at zero, single peak, long tail to the right.
7. **Energy axis vs speed axis.** At the same T, all gases share one kinetic-energy curve; on a speed axis the heavier gas peaks at lower speed.

## Quick self-check

1. Krypton (83.80 g mol⁻¹) and neon (20.18 g mol⁻¹) are at the same temperature. Which atoms move faster on average, and by what factor? *(Neon, about 2.04 times faster)*
2. A gas is heated from 200 K to 400 K. What happens to the average KE and the average speed? *(KE doubles; speed rises about 1.41 times)*
3. A sample of gas is cooled. Describe the change in its Maxwell–Boltzmann speed curve. *(Peak moves to lower speed and becomes taller and narrower; area unchanged)*
