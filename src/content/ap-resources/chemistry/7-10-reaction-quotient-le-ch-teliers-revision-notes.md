## Recap

- Q has the same form as K but uses the concentrations (or partial pressures) **at any moment**.
- At equilibrium Q = K. A stress makes **Q ≠ K**, so the system is no longer at equilibrium.
- The system reacts until **Q = K again**. The new equilibrium has different concentrations.
- Most stresses change **Q**. A temperature change alters **K**.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Meaning | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Q < K | Too few products | Predict a net **forward** reaction |
| Q > K | Too many products | Predict a net **reverse** reaction |
| new Q = s^Δn × K | Every concentration scaled by s; Δn = product coefficients − reactant coefficients (gases, or dissolved species) | Handle volume changes (halve V: s = 2) and dilution (double V: s = ½) |
| Exothermic: T up → K down | Heating favours the reverse reaction | Predict the effect of temperature on K |
| Endothermic: T up → K up | Heating favours the forward reaction | |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The system was at equilibrium before the stress, so Q = K at the start.
- The temperature is constant unless the question changes it; K is only fixed at a fixed temperature.
- Solids, pure liquids and solvent water are left out of Q, just as they are left out of K.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Reversing the rule.** Q > K means *reverse*, not forward.
2. **Changing K when you add a species.** Only temperature changes K.
3. **Changing Q when you heat a rigid flask.** The concentrations stay the same at that instant; K moves.
4. **Forgetting to raise terms to their coefficients** when you scale concentrations.
5. **Expecting old concentrations to return.** Only the ratio in Q returns to K.

## Quick self-check

1. A mixture has Q = 0.80 and K = 0.25. Which way does it react? *(Reverse: Q > K.)*
2. For N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g) at equilibrium, the volume is halved. What is the new Q in terms of K? *(Q = 2K, so the reaction goes in reverse.)*
3. For A(aq) ⇌ 2B(aq) at equilibrium, the solution is diluted to twice its volume. What is the new Q? *(Q = ½K, so the reaction goes forward.)*
