## Understanding

- I can define a physical change and a chemical change in terms of composition, not reversibility. *(Guide: "What changes and what stays the same"; Practice Q1)*
- I can name phase changes and the making or separating of mixtures as physical changes. *(Guide: "Physical changes"; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can describe a chemical change at the particle level as atoms rearranged into new combinations. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can list the four common signs of a chemical change: heat or light, a gas, a precipitate and a colour change. *(Guide: "Evidence of a chemical change")*

## Skills

- I can classify a described change as physical or chemical and justify it. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can pick out the strongest piece of evidence for a reaction from several observations. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can predict the observations an experiment will give, including whether a balance reading changes. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can plan a test that decides whether a new substance has formed. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can give a physical change that mimics each sign of a reaction, such as bubbles from boiling. *(Guide: "Evidence is a clue, not proof"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why a reaction can occur with no visible sign. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can use mass data from open and closed containers to show that atoms are conserved. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can write a claim, the evidence and the reasoning that links them. *(Guide: "Writing an evidence-based answer"; Practice Q4, Q6)*
