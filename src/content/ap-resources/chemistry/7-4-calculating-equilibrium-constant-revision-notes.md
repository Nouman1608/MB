## Recap

- K is found from **experimental measurements** taken **at equilibrium**, when the concentrations or partial pressures have stopped changing.
- **Method:** balanced equation → expression (no solids or pure liquids) → equilibrium values in the right units → substitute → K with no unit.
- If you know the **starting amounts** and **one equilibrium value**, an **ICE table** gives the rest. The change row follows the coefficients.
- At a fixed temperature, every experiment gives the **same K**, whatever the starting amounts.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Meaning | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| c = n / V | concentration in mol L⁻¹ from moles and volume in L | turn moles into what Kc needs |
| Change ratio = coefficient ratio | if C rises by x and its coefficient is 1, a species with coefficient 4 changes by 4x | fill the C row of an ICE table |
| P_X = (fraction of gas moles that are X) × P_total | partial pressure from a total pressure | turn a total pressure into what Kp needs |
| K = expression with equilibrium values | Kc from [X]ₑ, Kp from P_X at equilibrium | calculate K |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The readings were taken **at equilibrium** (constant over time).
- The **temperature is constant** throughout and is the same in every experiment you compare.
- The species measured is the only source of the signal (for example, only one species absorbs light at the chosen wavelength).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Putting starting values into K.**
2. **Forgetting to divide moles by the volume.**
3. **Ignoring coefficients in the change row** (H₂ in CH₄ + 2H₂S ⇌ CS₂ + 4H₂ changes 4 times as much as CS₂).
4. **Wrong signs:** reactants go down, products go up for a forward reaction.
5. **Splitting a total pressure equally** between gases made in a 2 : 1 ratio.
6. **Including a solid** or giving K a unit.

## Quick self-check

1. For A(g) + B(g) ⇌ C(g), the equilibrium concentrations are [A] = 0.20, [B] = 0.50 and [C] = 0.40 mol L⁻¹. Find Kc. *(0.40 ÷ (0.20 × 0.50) = 4.0)*
2. A 3.00 L vessel holds 0.60 mol of a gas at equilibrium. What concentration goes into Kc? *(0.20 mol L⁻¹)*
3. For 2SO₃(g) ⇌ 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g), [SO₃] starts at 0.100 mol L⁻¹ with no products. At equilibrium [O₂] = 0.020 mol L⁻¹. Find Kc. *([SO₂] = 0.040, [SO₃] = 0.060; Kc = (0.040)² × 0.020 ÷ (0.060)² = 8.9 × 10⁻³)*
