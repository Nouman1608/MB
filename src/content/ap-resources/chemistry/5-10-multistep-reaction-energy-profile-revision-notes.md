## Recap

- A multistep energy profile is the profiles of the elementary steps joined end to end.
- Each **peak** is a transition state. There is one peak for every elementary step.
- Each **valley between peaks** is an intermediate. It is a real species that lasts long enough to react again.
- The curve starts at the overall reactants and ends at the overall products.
- The axes are energy (kJ mol⁻¹) and reaction coordinate. The reaction coordinate is not time.

## Key relationships

| Quantity | How to read it |
|---|---|
| Number of steps | Number of peaks |
| Number of intermediates | Valleys between peaks (steps − 1 for steps in a chain) |
| Ea of a step | Its peak − the valley where the step **starts** |
| ΔH of a step | Energy at its end − energy at its start |
| Overall ΔH | Products − reactants = sum of the steps' ΔH |
| Reverse Ea of a step | Its peak − the valley where the step **ends** = Ea − ΔH |
| Rate-limiting step | The step with the largest Ea |

## Shapes to recognise

- **Slow first step:** the first climb is the largest; the rate law comes from step 1.
- **Fast pre-equilibrium, then slow step:** a low first hump, an intermediate that can easily slide back, then a much larger climb (Topic 5.9).

## Assumptions

- Every hump is one elementary step.
- A larger barrier means a smaller fraction of collisions succeed, so that step is slower.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Counting valleys as steps.** Count peaks.
2. **Measuring every Ea from the reactants.** Use each step's own starting valley.
3. **Calling a transition state an intermediate.** Peaks are transition states; valleys are intermediates.
4. **"Biggest drop = slowest step."** A drop is ΔH, not a barrier.
5. **Changing overall ΔH when the route changes.** It depends only on reactants and products.

## Quick self-check

A two-step reaction has step 1: Ea = 40, ΔH = −10; step 2: Ea = 70, ΔH = +25 (kJ mol⁻¹). Reactants are at 0.

1. What is the energy of the intermediate? *(−10 kJ mol⁻¹)*
2. What is the energy of TS 2? *(−10 + 70 = 60 kJ mol⁻¹)*
3. What is the overall ΔH? *(+15 kJ mol⁻¹)*
4. Which step is rate-limiting? *(Step 2: its barrier, 70, is larger)*
