Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/2-2-intramolecular-force-potential-energy-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/2-2-intramolecular-force-potential-energy-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can name the attractions and repulsions between two approaching atoms and say which dominates at long and at short distance. *(Guide: "Why a bond has a length"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why the zero of potential energy is set at infinite separation and why a bonded pair has negative potential energy. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q4(d))*
- I can state that forming a bond releases energy and breaking a bond absorbs it. *(Guide: "Reading a potential energy graph"; Practice Q1, Q7)*
- I can explain how the size of an atom's core affects bond length and bond energy. *(Guide: Factor 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why higher bond order gives shorter, stronger bonds. *(Guide: Factor 2 and Figure 2; Practice Q5)*

## Skills

- I can read the equilibrium bond length and the bond energy from a potential energy curve. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can plot a potential energy curve from data with labelled axes, units and a sensible scale. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can sketch and compare two potential energy curves on the same axes. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can convert a molar bond energy into the energy for one bond using Avogadro's number. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
- I can calculate q₁q₂ / r² from ion charges and radii and use it to rank ionic attractions. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3, Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can decide whether charge or size has the larger effect when comparing two ion pairs. *(Practice Q6(c))*
- I can correct the claims that bond breaking releases energy and that atoms are most stable when the nuclei are as close as possible. *(Practice Q7)*
