## Recap

- A chemical equation records atoms being **rearranged**. It must conserve atoms (so mass) and charge.
- Balance with **coefficients**, never by changing subscripts.
- Physical changes have equations too: only the state symbol changes, or an ionic solid separates into its ions, e.g. CaCl₂(s) → Ca²⁺(aq) + 2Cl⁻(aq).
- One reaction in solution can be written three ways: **molecular**, **complete ionic** and **net ionic**.

## Key relationships

| Form | What it shows | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Molecular | every substance as a full formula | plan amounts of reagents |
| Complete ionic | strong electrolytes as separate ions | list every species; find spectators |
| Net ionic | only the species that change | show what actually reacts |

| Split into ions | Keep whole |
|---|---|
| soluble ionic compounds (aq) | solids and precipitates (s) |
| strong acids: HCl, HBr, HI, HNO₃, HClO₄, H₂SO₄ | liquids, including H₂O(l) |
| soluble strong bases (NaOH, KOH) | gases (g) |
| | weak acids and molecular substances |

All **sodium, potassium, ammonium and nitrate** salts are soluble. Other solubility data will be given, or shown by a precipitate.

## Assumptions behind the rules

- "Strong" acids and soluble ionic compounds are treated as **fully** separated into ions in water.
- A precipitate is treated as completely insoluble, so it is written as a solid.
- H⁺(aq) and H₃O⁺(aq) mean the same thing here; H₃O⁺ is preferred.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Splitting a solid or a gas** into ions.
2. **Breaking polyatomic ions** such as SO₄²⁻ into atoms.
3. **Losing coefficients** when splitting: 3Ca(NO₃)₂ → 3Ca²⁺ + 6NO₃⁻.
4. **Checking atoms only.** Add up the charges too.
5. **Calling an ion a spectator** when it changes state (Ca²⁺ from solid CaCO₃ is not a spectator).

## Quick self-check

1. Is Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s) balanced for charge? *(Yes: +2 on each side.)*
2. Write the equation for solid AlCl₃ dissolving in water. *(AlCl₃(s) → Al³⁺(aq) + 3Cl⁻(aq))*
3. Mg(s) is added to HCl(aq), giving MgCl₂(aq) and H₂(g). Net ionic equation? *(Mg(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + H₂(g); Cl⁻ is the spectator.)*
