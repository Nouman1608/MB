## Recap

- An **energy diagram** shows the energy of the **system** at each stage of a process. The surroundings are not drawn.
- **Vertical axis:** energy, usually in kJ mol⁻¹ (per mole of the process as written). **Horizontal axis:** progress from start to finish, with no scale and no unit.
- Each stage is a **horizontal level**; the **arrow** runs from the start level to the end level and is labelled ΔH.
- Zero can go anywhere. Only **differences** between levels mean anything.
- Diagrams work for reactions, phase changes and dissolving. A hump (activation energy) can be added, but it does not affect ΔH.

## Key relationships

| Idea | Rule |
|---|---|
| Energy change | ΔH = E(final) − E(start) |
| Exothermic | final level lower, arrow down, ΔH < 0, surroundings warm |
| Endothermic | final level higher, arrow up, ΔH > 0, surroundings cool |
| Reverse process | same two levels, arrow reversed, ΔH changes sign |
| Phase levels for one substance | gas highest, then liquid, then solid |
| Dissolving step diagram | step 1 up, step 2 up, step 3 down; overall = sum of the three |

## Assumptions behind a diagram

- All levels refer to the same conditions (same temperature and pressure), so the differences are meaningful.
- ΔH is per mole of the equation **as written**; a different equation would need different levels.
- Step values for dissolving are a model: the real process does not happen in three separate steps.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Unlabelled or unit-less axis.** Write "Energy (kJ mol⁻¹)".
2. **Uneven ticks** or levels placed by eye instead of by the scale.
3. **Arrow drawn from high to low** whatever the process. It goes from start to end.
4. **Comparing arrow lengths on different axes.** Read the numbers.
5. **Changing ΔH when a catalyst is added.** Only the hump moves.
6. **"Temperature" on the vertical axis.** It is energy.

## Quick self-check

1. Reactants at 20 kJ mol⁻¹, products at 85 kJ mol⁻¹. ΔH? Endo or exo? *(+65 kJ mol⁻¹, endothermic.)*
2. A reaction has ΔH = −184 kJ mol⁻¹. What is ΔH for the reverse, and which way does its arrow point? *(+184 kJ mol⁻¹; up.)*
3. On one diagram for a pure substance, which phase sits lowest? *(The solid.)*
