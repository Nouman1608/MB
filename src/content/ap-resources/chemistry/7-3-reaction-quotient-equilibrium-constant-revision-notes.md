## Recap

- The **reaction quotient, Q**, describes the relative amounts of products and reactants in a mixture **at any moment**.
- As a reaction runs, Q changes and moves towards a fixed value. That value is the **equilibrium constant, K**. At equilibrium, Qc = Kc and Qp = Kp.
- **Kc and Qc** use concentrations in mol L⁻¹. **Kp and Qp** use partial pressures of gases, usually in atm. They are usually different numbers. You do not convert between them, but you must use the one the question asks for.
- **Pure solids and pure liquids** (including water as the solvent) are left out, because their "concentration" does not depend on how much is present.
- At a fixed temperature, K is constant. Changing amounts changes Q, not K.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Meaning | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Qc = [C]^c[D]^d / ([A]^a[B]^b) | for aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD, concentrations at any moment | describe any mixture in solution or gas |
| Qp = (P_C)^c(P_D)^d / ((P_A)^a(P_B)^b) | same shape, partial pressures of gases | describe a gas mixture |
| At equilibrium: Qc = Kc, Qp = Kp | Q stops changing once rates are equal | recognise or test for equilibrium |

## Assumptions behind the expression

- The equation is **balanced**; the powers come from its coefficients.
- The **temperature is fixed**. A different temperature gives a different K.
- Values put into K are **equilibrium** values. Any other values give Q.
- Questions where a dissolved substance is in equilibrium with the same substance as a gas are outside this course.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Coefficients as multipliers.** 2NO gives [NO]², not 2[NO].
2. **Upside down.** Products go on top for the reaction as written.
3. **Adding instead of multiplying** the terms.
4. **Including (s) or (l) species.** Keep H₂O(g); leave out H₂O(l).
5. **Mixing types.** No pressures in Kc, no concentrations in Kp.
6. **Calling any Q value "K".** Only equilibrium values give K.

## Quick self-check

1. Write Kp for CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g). *(Kp = P_CO₂)*
2. Write Kc for Cu²⁺(aq) + 4NH₃(aq) ⇌ Cu(NH₃)₄²⁺(aq). *(Kc = [Cu(NH₃)₄²⁺] / ([Cu²⁺][NH₃]⁴))*
3. For 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), a mixture has P_SO₂ = 0.20 atm, P_O₂ = 0.50 atm and P_SO₃ = 0.40 atm. What is Qp? *(0.40² ÷ (0.20² × 0.50) = 8.0)*
4. A mixture has Qc = 3.0 and Kc = 3.0 at the same temperature. What can you say? *(It is at equilibrium; the concentrations stay constant.)*
