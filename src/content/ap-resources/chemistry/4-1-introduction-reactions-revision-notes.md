## Recap

- A **physical change** alters properties such as state, shape or mixing, but every substance keeps its composition.
- Common physical changes: **phase changes** (melting, boiling, sublimation and their reverses) and **making or separating mixtures** (dissolving, filtering, distilling).
- A **chemical change** forms new substances. Atoms are kept but rearranged into new combinations.
- Four common signs of a chemical change: **heat or light**, **a gas**, **a precipitate**, **a colour change**.

## Key relationships

| Sign | Points to a reaction when… | Can be faked by… |
|---|---|---|
| Heat or light | energy is released as a new substance forms | dissolving NaOH (hot) or NH₄NO₃ (cold) |
| Gas | the gas is a new substance (e.g. CO₂ turns limewater cloudy) | boiling; dissolved gas escaping |
| Precipitate | two solutions mix and an insoluble product forms | crystals forming as a saturated solution cools |
| Colour change | a new colour appears | dilution making a colour paler |

## Assumptions behind the evidence

- One observation is a **clue**. The deciding question is: has a substance with a different composition formed?
- A reaction can give **no visible sign** (strong acid + strong base: only the temperature rises).
- Mass is conserved in a **closed** container. An **open** container loses mass only if a gas escapes.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **"Temperature changed, so it reacted."** Dissolving and phase changes also transfer energy.
2. **Calling dilution a colour change.** A paler shade of the same colour is not a new substance.
3. **Claim with no reasoning.** Link the evidence to a new substance forming.
4. **Using reversibility as the test.** Composition decides, not how easy the change is to undo.
5. **Saying atoms are lost** when an open flask gets lighter. The gas has left the flask.

## Quick self-check

1. Is distilling sea water physical or chemical? *(Physical: it separates a mixture.)*
2. Name a physical change that produces bubbles. *(Boiling, or gas escaping from fizzy water.)*
3. A sealed bag containing baking soda and vinegar weighs 64.30 g before mixing. What does it weigh afterwards? *(64.30 g: no gas can escape.)*
4. Why is a precipitate good evidence? *(The solid is a substance that was not present in either solution.)*
