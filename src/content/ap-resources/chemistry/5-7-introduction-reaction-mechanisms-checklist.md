Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-7-introduction-reaction-mechanisms-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-7-introduction-reaction-mechanisms-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what a reaction mechanism is and why most reactions happen in several elementary steps. *(Guide: "The balanced equation hides the steps"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why the coefficients of an overall equation do not tell me which particles collide. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7)*
- I can describe how the amount of an intermediate changes from the start of a reaction to the end. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q4(d))*
- I can explain the difference between an intermediate and a transition state. *(Guide: "Intermediates exist only while the reaction runs")*

## Skills

- I can add the steps of a mechanism, cancel species, and write the overall equation. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can tell when a step must happen more than once and use a multiplier before adding. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can identify the reactants, products, intermediates and catalysts in a mechanism. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1, Q2)*
- I can state the molecularity of each elementary step. *(Practice Q4(c))*
- I can decide whether a proposed mechanism is consistent with an overall equation, even when every step is balanced. *(Practice Q3, Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can use the order in which a species appears to argue that it is a catalyst rather than an intermediate. *(Practice Q2, Q5(b))*
- I can explain how detecting an intermediate supports one mechanism over another. *(Guide: "Building evidence for a mechanism"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why a mechanism that adds up correctly, or one supported by a detected intermediate, is still not proven. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(c))*
