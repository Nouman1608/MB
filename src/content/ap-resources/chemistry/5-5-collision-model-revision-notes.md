Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, figures and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-5-collision-model-study-guide/).

## Recap

- Bonds can only break and form when particles **collide**. No collision, no reaction.
- A collision produces products only if it has **enough energy** (at least the activation energy, Eₐ) **and** a **suitable orientation** (the atoms that must bond actually meet).
- In most reactions only a **small fraction** of collisions succeed.
- The **Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution** shows the spread of particle kinetic energies: a peak, then a long tail to the right. The area under the curve beyond Eₐ is the share of particles with enough energy.
- Heating the sample makes the curve **lower, wider and shifted right**. The area beyond Eₐ grows a lot. The total area stays the same.

## Key relationships

| Change | Collision frequency | Fraction with energy ≥ Eₐ | Fraction with good orientation | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Increase concentration | increases (in proportion) | no change | no change | increases |
| Increase temperature | increases slightly | **increases a lot** | no change | increases a lot |
| Larger, less symmetrical particles | — | — | smaller | slower if Eₐ is similar (k smaller) |

For an elementary step A + B → products: collision frequency ∝ [A][B], so rate = k[A][B]. The rate constant k holds the energy and orientation fractions.

## Assumptions

- Particles react only during a collision, and only bonds in the colliding particles change.
- Temperature measures the **average** kinetic energy; individual particles have a wide range of energies.
- The orientation requirement depends on the shapes of the particles, not on temperature or concentration.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Saying every collision reacts.** Most fail.
2. **Giving "more collisions" as the main reason for faster rates at higher temperature.** The main reason is the larger fraction with energy ≥ Eₐ.
3. **Saying Eₐ falls when temperature rises.** Eₐ stays the same.
4. **Drawing the higher-temperature peak taller.** It is lower and further right, with the same area.
5. **Linking concentration to collision energy.** Concentration changes frequency only.
6. **Forgetting orientation.** Energy alone is not enough.

## Quick self-check

1. A reaction is first order in A and first order in B. Both concentrations are doubled. By what factor does the rate change? *(× 4: collisions between A and B happen four times as often)*
2. What does the area under a Maxwell–Boltzmann curve to the right of Eₐ represent? *(The particles, and so the share of collisions, with at least the activation energy)*
3. Why does a fast collision between two reactant molecules sometimes fail to react? *(The orientation is wrong: the atoms that must bond do not meet)*
