Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/7-2-direction-reversible-reactions-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/7-2-direction-reversible-reactions-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can state the direction of net change when the forward rate is greater than, less than or equal to the reverse rate. *(Guide: "Two rates, one net direction"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why the faster direction slows down and the slower direction speeds up as a reaction approaches equilibrium. *(Guide: "How the rates change on the way to equilibrium"; Practice Q6(b))*
- I can describe the two rates at the start of a reaction that begins with only reactants or only products. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why the net direction depends on concentrations, not only on the rate constants. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Common misconceptions)*

## Calculation

- I can write the forward and reverse rate laws for an elementary reversible step, including a squared term from a coefficient of 2. *(Guide: "Calculating the two rates for an elementary step"; Practice Q3)*
- I can calculate both rates and the net rate for a given mixture, and state the direction with its sign. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q6(d))*
- I can find the equilibrium concentrations of a simple A ⇌ B reaction by setting the two rates equal. *(Practice Q4(c), Q7(c))*

## Reasoning

- I can use a series of particle snapshots to decide the direction of net change and when equilibrium is reached. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can describe what a particle snapshot model shows well and at least one thing it cannot show. *(Guide: Worked example 2(d); Practice Q5(d))*
- I can use a table or graph of forward and reverse rates to find when equilibrium is reached. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can explain, in terms of rates, what happens when extra product is added to a system at equilibrium. *(Guide: "Preview"; Practice Q7)*
