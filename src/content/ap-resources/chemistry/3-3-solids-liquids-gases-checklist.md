Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/3-3-solids-liquids-gases-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/3-3-solids-liquids-gases-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can describe how particles are arranged and how they move in a solid, a liquid and a gas. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can explain the difference between a crystalline and an amorphous solid, with an example of each. *(Guide: "Solids"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain how interparticle forces and particle packing affect the structure of a solid. *(Guide: "Solids")*
- I can explain why a liquid flows and takes the shape of its container but keeps its volume. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why a gas has no definite shape or volume. *(Guide: "Gases")*

## Skills

- I can draw particulate models of one substance in three phases, keeping the particles identical. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can spot and correct errors in someone else's particulate drawing. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate molar volume from molar mass and density, converting between L and cm³. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can estimate how much farther apart gas particles are than liquid particles using a cube root. *(Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why the solid and liquid phases of a substance have similar molar volumes. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can predict how compressing, heating or expanding a gas changes the spacing and collision frequency of its particles. *(Guide: gas table; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain how hydrogen bonding and temperature change the arrangement of molecules in a liquid. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why a phase change does not break the covalent bonds inside molecules. *(Practice Q5)*
