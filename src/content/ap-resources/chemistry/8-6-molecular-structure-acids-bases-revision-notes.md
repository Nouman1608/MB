## Recap

- Acid strength depends on how stable the **conjugate base** A⁻ is with its negative charge.
- **Acidic hydrogens** are bonded to electronegative atoms (usually O or a halogen). C–H hydrogens are not acidic in water.
- **Strong acids:** HCl, HBr, HI, HClO₄, H₂SO₄, HNO₃. Their conjugate bases are very weak.
- **Strong bases:** group 1 and heavier group 2 hydroxides (NaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)₂, Ba(OH)₂). Their conjugate acid, water, is very weak.
- **Weak acids:** carboxylic acids, R–COOH. **Weak bases:** nitrogen bases (NH₃, amines) and carboxylate ions, R–COO⁻.

## Key relationships

| Structural feature | Effect on the conjugate base | Example (acid strength increases →) |
|---|---|---|
| More electronegative atom bearing the charge (same period) | holds charge better | CH₄ < NH₃ < H₂O < HF |
| More electronegative central atom in an oxoacid | pulls density from O–H | HOI < HOBr < HOCl |
| Electronegative atoms nearby (inductive effect) | charge spread through bonds | CH₃COOH < ClCH₂COOH < Cl₂CHCOOH < Cl₃CCOOH |
| More O atoms on the central atom | inductive pull plus resonance | HClO < HClO₂ < HClO₃ < HClO₄ |
| Resonance in A⁻ | charge shared by several atoms | CH₃CH₂OH ≪ CH₃COOH |
| Larger anion down a group | charge spread over bigger volume, weaker H–X bond | HF < HCl < HBr < HI |
| Conjugate pair | Ka × Kb = Kw (1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25 °C) | weaker acid ↔ stronger conjugate base |

## Assumptions behind the comparisons

- Compare **closely related** acids, changing one feature at a time.
- Strength means extent of ionisation **in water** at the same temperature.
- Electronegativity arguments apply across a period or for atoms near the acidic site, not to the halide ion itself down a group.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Counting every H as acidic.** Check what each H is bonded to.
2. **Talking only about the acid.** Explain using the stability of the **conjugate base**.
3. **Reversing the halogen acid trend.** HF is weak; HI is strongest.
4. **Confusing strong with concentrated.** Strength is fixed by structure, not by concentration.
5. **Saying a strong acid has a strong conjugate base.** It is the reverse.
6. **Naming a feature without its effect.** "Cl is electronegative" earns little; "Cl pulls electron density and stabilises the negative charge on A⁻" earns the point.

## Quick self-check

1. How many acidic hydrogens does HCOOH (methanoic acid) have? *(One: the H on O)*
2. Which is stronger, HOCl or HOI? *(HOCl: Cl is more electronegative)*
3. Ka of HF is 6.8 × 10⁻⁴. What is Kb of F⁻? *(1.5 × 10⁻¹¹)*
4. Why is ethoxide a stronger base than acetate? *(Ethoxide's charge sits on one O; acetate shares its charge over two O atoms by resonance.)*
