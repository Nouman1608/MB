## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between an elementary step and an overall reaction. *(Guide: "One collision, one step")*
- I can explain why the rate of an elementary step is proportional to the product of the reactant concentrations, each raised to its coefficient. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q7)*
- I can define molecularity and classify a step as unimolecular, bimolecular or termolecular. *(Guide: "Molecularity"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why termolecular steps are rare and why steps with four or more particles are not expected. *(Guide: "Why three-particle collisions are rare"; Practice Q7)*

## Skills

- I can write the rate law of an elementary step from its equation, leaving out the products. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can give the units of k for a unimolecular, bimolecular or termolecular step. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate the rate of an elementary step from k and the concentrations. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can predict the factor by which the rate changes when one or more concentrations change. *(Practice Q3, Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can compare an experimental rate law with the one a single elementary step predicts, and say what the comparison shows. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q5, Q6)*
- I can explain why a matching rate law does not prove a reaction is one elementary step. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can spot an overall equation that cannot be a single step because too many particles would have to collide. *(Guide: Worked example 3)*
