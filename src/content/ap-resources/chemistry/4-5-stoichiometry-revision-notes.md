## Recap

- Atoms are conserved in a reaction, so a **balanced equation** lets you calculate any product amount from a reactant amount, or the reverse.
- The **coefficients** give the proportions in which particles, and therefore moles, react. They are **mole ratios**.
- Every calculation passes through moles. The mole ratio is the only step that crosses the equation.
- The **limiting reactant** runs out first and fixes the amount of product. The other reactant is **in excess**.
- **Theoretical yield** is the product calculated from the limiting reactant. **Percent yield** = actual ÷ theoretical × 100.
- Adding more limiting reactant gives more product (in proportion) until it stops being limiting. Adding more excess reactant gives no more product.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| n = m / M | m in g, M in g mol⁻¹ | turn a mass into moles |
| n = PV / RT | P in atm, V in L, T in K, R = 0.08206 L atm mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ | turn gas data into moles (or V = nRT / P) |
| n = M × V | M in mol L⁻¹, V in **L** | turn a solution volume into moles (or V = n / M) |
| n(B) = n(A) × (coefficient of B / coefficient of A) | mol | cross the equation |
| smallest n ÷ coefficient | mol | find the limiting reactant |
| percent yield = actual / theoretical × 100 | same units top and bottom | judge how efficient the reaction was |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The equation is **balanced** and is the **only** reaction taking place.
- The reaction **goes to completion**, so the limiting reactant is fully used.
- Reactants are **pure** and amounts are measured accurately.
- Gases behave **ideally**, so PV = nRT applies.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Using coefficients as mass ratios.** Convert to moles first.
2. **Comparing grams to find the limiting reactant.** Compare moles ÷ coefficient.
3. **Ignoring coefficients.** More moles does not mean "in excess" if the coefficient is larger.
4. **Working from the excess reactant.** The product always comes from the limiting reactant.
5. **mL instead of L, or °C instead of K.** 25.0 mL = 0.0250 L; 25 °C = 298.15 K.
6. **Rounding early.** Keep full values until the last line.

## Quick self-check

1. In C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O, how many moles of O₂ react with 0.20 mol of C₃H₈, and how many moles of CO₂ form? *(1.0 mol O₂; 0.60 mol CO₂)*
2. How many moles of solute are in 30.0 mL of 0.200 M solution? *(0.00600 mol)*
3. For A + 2B → AB₂, you mix 3.0 mol A with 4.0 mol B. Which is limiting and how much AB₂ forms? *(B is limiting, 4.0 ÷ 2 = 2.0 < 3.0 ÷ 1; 2.0 mol AB₂)*
4. In question 3, what happens to the amount of AB₂ if you add another 1.0 mol of A? *(Nothing: A is already in excess)*
