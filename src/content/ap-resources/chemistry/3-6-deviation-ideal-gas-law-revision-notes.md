## Recap

- PV = nRT rests on two KMT assumptions that real gases break: **particles have no volume** and **particles do not attract each other**.
- **Attractions** pull particles back as they approach the walls, so collisions are less frequent and less forceful. The measured pressure is **lower** than ideal.
- **Particle volume** reduces the free space the particles can move in. At very high pressure the measured volume (or pressure) is **higher** than ideal.
- Both effects are always present; the conditions decide which one dominates.

## Key relationships

| Idea | What it tells you | When it matters most |
|---|---|---|
| P_ideal = nRT / V | the pressure an ideal gas would exert | compare with the measured pressure |
| PV/nRT = 1 | ideal behaviour | low pressure, high temperature |
| PV/nRT < 1 | attractions dominate | low T, moderate-to-high P, strong intermolecular forces |
| PV/nRT > 1 | particle volume dominates | extremely high P, large particles |

## Assumptions and conditions

- **Most ideal:** high temperature and low pressure; small, nonpolar particles with few electrons (He is the classic example).
- **Least ideal:** close to condensation (low T, high P), and polar or hydrogen-bonded or large, highly polarisable molecules.
- A higher boiling point signals stronger intermolecular forces, and so a larger deviation from attractions at the same conditions.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **"Real pressure is always lower."** Only when attractions dominate.
2. **High pressure is not "more ideal".** Low pressure is.
3. **Low temperature is not "more ideal".** Slow particles feel attractions more.
4. **Collisions do not cause deviations.** Ideal particles collide too.
5. **Name the force.** Not "strong forces" but "hydrogen bonding between NH₃ molecules".
6. **Intermolecular, not intramolecular.** Bonds inside molecules are irrelevant here.
7. **PV/nRT = 1 at one pressure ≠ ideal.** The effects may simply cancel.

## Quick self-check

1. A gas has PV/nRT = 0.90 at 50 atm. Which effect dominates? *(Attractions)*
2. Which deviates more at 25 °C and 5 atm: N₂ or NH₃? Why? *(NH₃: hydrogen bonding and dipole–dipole forces give stronger attractions)*
3. Why does PV/nRT exceed 1 for every gas at extremely high pressure? *(The particles fill a large share of the volume, so the real volume is larger than ideal)*
