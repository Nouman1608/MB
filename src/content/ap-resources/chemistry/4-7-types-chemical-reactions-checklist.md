## Understanding

- I can say what is transferred in each reaction type: electrons in redox, protons in acid-base, and ions joining into a solid in precipitation. *(Guide: "Three questions that sort most reactions")*
- I can define oxidation and reduction in terms of electrons and in terms of oxidation numbers. *(Guide: "Redox reactions"; Practice Q7)*
- I can state that electrons move from the species oxidised to the species reduced, in equal numbers. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why combustion is a redox reaction, and name the products of complete combustion of a hydrocarbon. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can state which salts are always soluble: those of sodium, potassium, ammonium and nitrate. *(Guide: "Precipitation reactions")*

## Skills

- I can assign oxidation numbers to atoms in elements, compounds and polyatomic ions. *(Guide: oxidation-number table; Practice Q1)*
- I can balance a complete combustion equation and show that electrons lost equal electrons gained. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can find which product is the precipitate when two ionic solutions are mixed, and write its net ionic equation. *(Guide: Worked example 1(c); Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can calculate the mass of a precipitate from a volume and concentration. *(Practice Q6(d))*

## Reasoning

- I can classify a reaction as acid-base, redox or precipitation and give evidence from the equation. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can explain why a reaction involving H⁺, or one that forms a solid, may still be redox. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7)*
- I can use unchanged oxidation numbers to show that a reaction is not redox. *(Practice Q2, Q6(c))*
