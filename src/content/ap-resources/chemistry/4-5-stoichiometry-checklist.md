Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/4-5-stoichiometry-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/4-5-stoichiometry-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why conservation of atoms means a balanced equation predicts product amounts from reactant amounts, and reactant amounts from product amounts. *(Guide: "Why a balanced equation lets you predict amounts"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why coefficients are mole ratios and not mass ratios. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can define the limiting reactant, the excess reactant, theoretical yield and percent yield. *(Guide: "Limiting reactant, excess reactant and yield")*

## Calculation

- I can follow the road map from a given quantity to moles, across the mole ratio, and on to the wanted quantity. *(Guide: Figure 1; Worked example 1)*
- I can use molarity to find moles from a solution volume, and the volume of solution needed for a reaction. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can use PV = nRT to turn moles of a gas into a volume, with T in kelvin. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can identify the limiting reactant by comparing moles divided by coefficients. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can calculate the theoretical yield, the excess left over and the percent yield. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can check an answer with conservation of mass or conservation of atoms. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict how the amount of product changes when the amount of one reactant changes, and say whether that reactant is limiting or in excess. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q3, Q6(e))*
- I can explain a limiting-reactant result at the particle level, describing what is left after the reaction. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can state the assumptions behind a stoichiometry calculation, such as completion and a single reaction. *(Revision notes)*
