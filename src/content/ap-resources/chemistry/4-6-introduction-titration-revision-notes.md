## Recap

- A **titration** finds the amount of an **analyte** by reacting it with a **titrant** of known concentration added from a burette.
- The titration reaction must be **specific** (reacts only with the analyte) and **quantitative** (goes to completion with a known equation).
- The **equivalence point** is reached when the titrant has exactly used up the analyte, in the ratio of the balanced equation.
- The **endpoint** is the observable change (indicator colour, permanganate pink, a jump in pH or a change in conductivity). It should match the equivalence point as closely as possible.
- On a titration curve, the equivalence point is where the measured property changes most sharply (or where two straight-line sections meet).

## Key relationships

| Step | Relationship | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Write the balanced equation | the mole ratio comes from here |
| 2 | n(titrant) = M(titrant) × V(titrant) | V in **litres** |
| 3 | n(analyte) = n(titrant) × (coeff. analyte / coeff. titrant) | ratio the right way up |
| 4 | c(analyte) = n(analyte) ÷ V(sample) | the **sample** volume, not the total |
| Predict | V(equivalence) = n(titrant needed) ÷ M(titrant) | useful for planning |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The reaction **goes to completion** and is the only reaction with the titrant.
- The endpoint **coincides** with the equivalence point.
- Volumes and the titrant concentration are **accurate**.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Calling the endpoint the equivalence point.** One is seen; the other is calculated.
2. **M₁V₁ = M₂V₂ for a non-1:1 reaction.** Go through moles.
3. **Dividing by the total volume** in the flask instead of the original sample volume.
4. **Thinking added water in the flask changes the titre.** Moles of analyte are unchanged.
5. **Ignoring direction of errors.** More titrant recorded than needed → analyte calculated too high.

## Quick self-check

1. What volume of 0.100 M HCl reaches equivalence with 25.00 mL of 0.0800 M NaOH? *(20.0 mL)*
2. What volume of 0.100 M HCl reaches equivalence with 10.00 mL of 0.0500 M Ca(OH)₂? *(10.0 mL, because the ratio is 2 HCl : 1 Ca(OH)₂)*
3. A student overshoots the endpoint. Is the calculated analyte concentration too high or too low? *(Too high)*
