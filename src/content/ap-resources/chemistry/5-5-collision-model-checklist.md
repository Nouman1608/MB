Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-5-collision-model-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-5-collision-model-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why reactant particles must collide before bonds can break and form. *(Guide: "No collision, no reaction")*
- I can state the two conditions a collision must meet to produce products: energy at least Eₐ and a suitable orientation. *(Guide: "What makes a collision successful"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why only a small fraction of collisions succeed in most reactions. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can describe the shape of a Maxwell–Boltzmann curve and say what its total area and the area beyond Eₐ represent. *(Guide: "The Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution"; Practice Q3)*

## Skills

- I can sketch Maxwell–Boltzmann curves for one sample at two temperatures, with the higher-temperature peak lower and further right, and mark Eₐ. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can predict how a change in concentration changes the rate of an elementary step, and calculate the new rate. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can decide which orientation of a collision can form the new bonds a step needs. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain a change in rate with concentration in terms of collision frequency, and say why the fraction of successful collisions is unchanged. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why a temperature rise increases rate, naming the larger fraction of collisions with energy ≥ Eₐ as the main cause. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can evaluate a claim about rate using data on collision frequency and energy fraction. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can explain how a step with a lower Eₐ can still be slower, using orientation. *(Practice Q7)*
