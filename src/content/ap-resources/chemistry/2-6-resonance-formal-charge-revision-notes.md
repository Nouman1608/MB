## Recap

- **Resonance structures** have the same skeleton and electron count; only lone pairs and multiple-bond pairs move. Join them with ↔.
- When the structures are **equivalent** (formate, nitrite, nitrate, carbonate), the real species is a **resonance hybrid**: one structure, not a switch. The resonance bonds are identical in length and energy, and any charge is shared.
- When the structures are **not equivalent** (N₂O), they contribute unequally; formal charge ranks them.
- Species with an **odd number of electrons** (NO₂, ClO₂) cannot give every atom an octet. This is a limitation of the Lewis model.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Use it to |
|---|---|
| FC = V − N − ½B (V valence, N nonbonding electrons, B bonding electrons) | find the formal charge of any atom |
| Shortcut: FC = V − dots − lines | do the same quickly from a drawing |
| Sum of FCs = overall charge | check your diagram |
| Average bond order = bonds to the equivalent atoms ÷ number of positions | compare bond lengths and strengths (higher order → shorter, stronger) |

| Atom (usual pattern) | FC 0 | FC +1 | FC −1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | 4 bonds | 3 bonds, 0 lone pairs | 3 bonds, 1 lone pair |
| N | 3 bonds, 1 lone pair | 4 bonds | 2 bonds, 2 lone pairs |
| O | 2 bonds, 2 lone pairs | 3 bonds, 1 lone pair | 1 bond, 3 lone pairs |

## Choosing the best diagram

1. Octets for C, N, O and F (never more than 8).
2. Formal charges as close to zero as possible.
3. Negative formal charge on the most electronegative atom.
4. No like charges on neighbouring atoms.

If the octet rule and formal charge disagree (for example SO₄²⁻), name the criterion you use.

## Assumptions behind the model

- Formal charge assumes bonding electrons are shared exactly equally.
- Lewis diagrams assume electrons are in fixed pairs; resonance is the patch for electrons that are really spread out.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying the molecule "flips" between structures.
2. Moving atoms between "resonance structures".
3. Assuming every resonance bond order is 1.5 (nitrate is 4/3).
4. Formal charges that do not add up to the ion's charge.
5. Giving N or O more than 8 electrons to lower formal charges.

## Quick self-check

1. Formal charge on S in H₂S? *(6 − 4 − 2 = 0)*
2. Average C–O bond order in CO₃²⁻? *(4 ÷ 3 = 1.33)*
3. Formal charge on N in NH₄⁺? *(+1)*
4. Why can't NO have an octet on both atoms? *(It has 11 valence electrons, an odd number)*
