## Recap

- **Photoelectron spectroscopy (PES)** uses high-energy photons to knock single electrons out of gas-phase atoms or ions, from **every** occupied subshell.
- The **binding energy** of an electron is the energy needed to remove it. Electrons in the same subshell share one binding energy, so they form one peak.
- **Peak height** is ideally proportional to the number of electrons in the subshell, so you read relative heights.
- Spectra often plot binding energy **increasing to the left** on a **log scale**.
- Separate s and p peaks within a shell are the evidence that shells contain subshells.

## Key relationships

| Feature | What it tells you | Coulomb's law link (F ∝ q₁q₂ / r²) |
|---|---|---|
| Number of peaks | Number of occupied subshells | — |
| Relative peak heights | Superscripts of the configuration; total = number of electrons | — |
| Peak far to high binding energy | Core electrons, close to the nucleus | Small r, almost full nuclear charge |
| Lowest-energy peak | Valence electrons; ≈ first ionization energy | Large r, strong shielding |
| Same subshell in a heavier element | Peak moves to higher binding energy | More protons, larger q |
| Isoelectronic species | Same peaks and heights, shifted positions | More protons → every peak higher |

Units: 1 MJ mol⁻¹ = 1000 kJ mol⁻¹.

## Assumptions behind the reading

- Heights are only *ideally* proportional to electron numbers, so round the ratio to the nearest whole number of electrons.
- The sample is a single element (or ion) in the gas phase, in its ground state.
- Subshells fill in Aufbau order; you will not be asked to write configurations for the elements that break this rule.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Tallest = highest energy.** Height is electron count, not energy.
2. **Reading the axis the wrong way.** Check which side is high binding energy.
3. **One peak per electron.** It is one peak per subshell.
4. **Treating peaks as successive ionization energies.** Only the lowest peak matches an ionization energy.
5. **Keeping the valence peak for a cation.** Mg²⁺ has no 3s peak.
6. **Saying "more shells" when comparing the same subshell in two elements.** The difference is nuclear charge.
7. **Ignoring the 2s/2p split** when judging a simple shell model.

## Quick self-check

1. A spectrum has heights 2, 2, 6, 2, 5. Which element? *(Chlorine, 17 electrons)*
2. Which has its 1s peak at higher binding energy, F or Ne? *(Ne: one more proton)*
3. How many peaks does the spectrum of Ca²⁺ have? *(Five: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p)*
4. A valence peak is at 0.74 MJ mol⁻¹. What is that in kJ mol⁻¹? *(740 kJ mol⁻¹)*
