## Recap

- A **stress** upsets an equilibrium: the forward and reverse rates are no longer equal.
- **Le Châtelier's principle:** the system shifts in the direction that partly counteracts the stress, then settles at a new equilibrium.
- The shift is **partial**. Added species are not completely used up; removed species are not completely replaced.
- A shift shows up in measurable properties: **colour**, **pH** and **temperature**.

## Key relationships

| Stress | Shift | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Add a reactant or product | Away from it (uses it up) | Precipitating or reacting a species away counts as removing it |
| Remove a reactant or product | Towards it (replaces it) | |
| Heat | Endothermic direction | Write heat into the equation; K changes |
| Cool | Exothermic direction | K changes |
| Smaller gas volume (higher pressure) | Side with fewer gas molecules | No shift if numbers are equal |
| Larger gas volume | Side with more gas molecules | |
| Dilute a solution | Side with more dissolved particles | Do not count water as the solvent |
| Catalyst, extra solid or pure liquid, unreactive gas at constant volume | No shift | |

## Assumptions behind the predictions

- The system was at equilibrium **before** the stress.
- Only **one** condition changes at a time; the others stay fixed (for example, temperature stays constant when the volume changes).
- You know the sign of ΔH for temperature questions, or can deduce it from an observation.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Saying K changes when you add a species.** Only temperature changes K.
2. **Counting solids, pure liquids or solvent water** when you count particles.
3. **Forgetting the instant effect.** Compressing a coloured gas darkens it at once, before any shift.
4. **Saying the system returns to the old concentrations.** It reaches a new equilibrium.
5. **Assuming heat always shifts right.** Check the sign of ΔH.
6. **Describing the shift but not the observation** when the question asks what you would see.

## Quick self-check

1. For 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), ΔH < 0, which way does the system shift when the volume is decreased? *(Right: 3 gas molecules → 2.)*
2. Same system: which way does it shift when it is heated? *(Left: the reverse reaction is endothermic.)*
3. Does adding solid CaCO₃ to CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g) change the CO₂ pressure at equilibrium? *(No: the solid is not in the expression.)*
