Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/4-2-net-ionic-equations-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/4-2-net-ionic-equations-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why a balanced equation must conserve both atoms and charge. *(Guide: "What a balanced equation tells you")*
- I can explain the difference between molecular, complete ionic and net ionic equations, and say when each is most useful. *(Guide: "Which form should you use?")*
- I can explain what a spectator ion is, and why an ion that changes state is not one. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q2)*
- I can state that all sodium, potassium, ammonium and nitrate salts are soluble, and use it. *(Practice Q1)*

## Skills

- I can balance an equation from a word description, using coefficients only and including state symbols. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can write equations for physical changes, including an ionic solid dissolving into its ions. *(Guide: "Equations for physical changes"; Practice Q5)*
- I can decide which species to split into ions and which to keep whole. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can write the complete ionic and net ionic equations for a precipitation reaction. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can write a net ionic equation for a solid or a carbonate reacting with a strong acid. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can check an equation for charge balance and reject one that balances atoms only. *(Guide: "Atoms balanced is not enough"; Practice Q3)*
- I can find and correct errors in someone else's net ionic equation. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why two different pairs of reagents can share the same net ionic equation. *(Guide: "Which form should you use?")*
