## Recap

- A **reversible** process can go forward (reactants → products) and in reverse (products → reactants). It is written with ⇌.
- Physical examples: evaporation and condensation; a gas dissolving in and escaping from a liquid; a salt dissolving and precipitating.
- Chemical examples: **proton transfer** in acid–base reactions and **electron transfer** in redox reactions.
- In a **closed system** at constant temperature, a reversible process reaches **equilibrium**.
- Equilibrium is **dynamic**: both processes continue, at equal rates, so there is no net change.

## Key relationships

| Idea | What it means | How you see it |
|---|---|---|
| Equilibrium condition | forward rate = reverse rate | rate–time lines meet and stay together |
| Constant composition | each concentration or partial pressure stops changing | concentration–time or pressure–time lines go flat |
| All species present | the reaction does not go to completion | no line falls to zero |
| Stoichiometry still holds | changes are in the ratio of the coefficients | e.g. for A ⇌ 2B, B rises twice as much as A falls |

## Assumptions behind the picture

- The system is **closed**: no matter escapes or enters. An open beaker that loses a gas never settles.
- The **temperature is constant**. A change of temperature moves the system to a new equilibrium (Topic 7.9 onwards).
- Every reaction you meet in this unit is treated as reversible, even if the reverse is very slow.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Constant ≠ equal.** Equilibrium concentrations are usually different from each other.
2. **Crossing ≠ equilibrium.** Two concentration lines can cross while both are still changing.
3. **Not stopped.** The reaction continues both ways; only the net change is zero.
4. **Rates, not rate constants, are equal** at equilibrium.
5. **Reverse rate starts at zero** when only reactants are present, then grows as product builds up.
6. **Use the coefficients** when you read a graph: the changes must match the balanced equation.

## Quick self-check

1. A sealed jar of water at constant temperature has a steady water level. Which two rates are equal? *(The rate of evaporation and the rate of condensation.)*
2. For A ⇌ 2B starting with 1.00 atm of A, P_A levels off at 0.70 atm. What is P_B at equilibrium? *(0.60 atm.)*
3. At equilibrium, is the forward rate zero? *(No. It equals the reverse rate, and both are greater than zero.)*
