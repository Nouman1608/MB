Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. For the first Skills statement, mark your drawing against the guide's "Drawing a profile" list. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-6-reaction-energy-profile-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-6-reaction-energy-profile-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can describe an elementary step as some bonds breaking while new bonds form. *(Guide: "Bonds break, bonds form"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain what the reaction coordinate shows, and why it is not time. *(Guide: "Bonds break, bonds form")*
- I can describe the transition state: the highest-energy arrangement, with partial bonds, which cannot be isolated. *(Guide: "The transition state"; Practice Q3, Q7)*
- I can explain why the energy profile does not change when the temperature changes. *(Practice Q4, Q6)*

## Skills

- I can draw a fully labelled energy profile for one elementary step from Eₐ and ΔH data. *(Guide: Figure 1, Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can read Eₐ(forward), Eₐ(reverse) and ΔH from a profile as differences between levels. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can calculate any one of Eₐ(forward), Eₐ(reverse) and ΔH from the other two. *(Practice Q2, Q5, Q7)*
- I can decide from a profile whether a step is exothermic or endothermic. *(Practice Q2)*

## Reasoning

- I can use the Arrhenius equation, without calculating, to explain why rate rises with temperature. *(Guide: "Temperature and the Arrhenius equation"; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why a step with a larger Eₐ is slower and more sensitive to temperature. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why the reverse of a strongly exothermic step is much slower. *(Practice Q5, Q7)*
