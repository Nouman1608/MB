## Recap

- **Electronegativity** is how strongly an atom in a bond attracts the shared electrons. Pauling scale, no unit; fluorine is highest (3.98).
- **Across a period it increases:** larger effective nuclear charge, same shell. **Down a group it decreases:** extra shell, bonding electrons further away and more shielded.
- **Nonpolar covalent:** electrons shared between atoms of equal or similar electronegativity (H₂, Cl₂; C–H counts as nonpolar).
- **Polar covalent:** unequal sharing. The more electronegative atom is δ−, the other δ+. For single bonds, a bigger difference means a bigger bond dipole.
- **Every polar bond has some ionic character.** Ionic and covalent are ends of a continuum, not separate boxes.
- **Ionic:** usually a metal with a nonmetal. **Covalent:** usually two nonmetals. Properties of the compound give the best evidence.
- **Metallic:** metal cations with **delocalized** valence electrons that belong to no single atom.

## Key relationships

| Situation | Bond type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Same element, or very small difference | nonpolar covalent | Cl–Cl; C–H (0.35) |
| Two nonmetals, clear difference | polar covalent | H–Cl (0.96); O–H (1.24) |
| Metal + nonmetal, large difference | ionic | NaCl (2.23) |
| Metal atoms only | metallic | Cu, brass |

| Property evidence | Points to |
|---|---|
| Conducts as a solid, malleable | metallic |
| Conducts only when molten or dissolved, brittle, high melting point | ionic |
| Low melting point, does not conduct | covalent molecules |

## Assumptions

- Electronegativity values describe atoms in bonds and are averages; they guide, not decide.
- "Bigger difference, bigger dipole" is used for **single** bonds.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Using a fixed cut-off** (such as 1.7) to call a bond ionic. HF (1.78) is covalent.
2. **Writing full charges** (Cl⁻) on atoms in a polar covalent bond. Use δ+ and δ−.
3. **Putting δ− on the wrong atom.** It goes on the more electronegative atom, even if both are nonmetals (in O–F, F is δ−).
4. **Calling C–H polar.**
5. **Describing metals as sharing fixed electron pairs.** Say "delocalized".
6. **Ignoring the properties** when the question gives them.

## Quick self-check

1. Which atom is δ− in a Si–H bond (Si 1.90, H 2.20)? *(H)*
2. Is the bonding in MgO more likely ionic or covalent? *(Ionic: metal + nonmetal, difference 2.13.)*
3. A solid conducts only when molten. Which bonding? *(Ionic.)*
