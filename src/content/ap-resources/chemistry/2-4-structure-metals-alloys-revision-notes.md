Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, diagrams and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/2-4-structure-metals-alloys-study-guide/).

## Recap

- In a metal, each atom releases its valence electrons into a shared **sea of delocalized electrons**.
- The rest of each atom is a **positive ion core** (nucleus + inner electrons). The cores sit in a regular, closely packed array.
- **Metallic bonding** = attraction between the cores and the electron sea, in every direction.
- An **alloy** is a mixture of a metal with other elements. It keeps metallic bonding and has a variable composition (no formula).

## Key relationships

| Idea | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Charge balance in a model | electrons in sea = (number of cores) × (valence electrons per atom) | 16 Mg atoms: 16 Mg²⁺ cores, 32 electrons |
| Size comparison | percent difference = \|r(host) − r(added)\| ÷ r(host) × 100 | Cu/Zn 4.7 %; Fe/C 40.5 % |
| Interstitial alloy | added atoms much smaller; fill gaps; no host atom removed | steel (C in Fe) |
| Substitutional alloy | added atoms of comparable size; replace host atoms | brass (Zn for Cu) |
| Mass % to atom ratio | convert each mass to moles, then compare | 70 % Cu / 30 % Zn brass: 29 % of atoms are Zn |

## Properties from the model

- **Conducts electricity and heat:** mobile delocalized electrons (alloys too).
- **Malleable and ductile:** layers of cores slide; the sea adjusts; no like-charge repulsion.
- **Interstitial alloy:** more rigid lattice, harder, less malleable and ductile.
- **Substitutional alloy:** uneven rows, layers slide less easily, usually harder than the pure metal.

## Assumptions

- Atoms are treated as spheres of fixed radius; tables list slightly different radii.
- Models show a small 2-D section; the real array is 3-D and extends in every direction.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Calling metallic bonding "ionic" because the cores are positive.
2. Drawing electrons attached to particular cores.
3. Saying interstitial atoms **replace** host atoms.
4. Treating mass percent as atom percent when the molar masses are very different (C and Fe).
5. Saying alloys stop conducting.

## Quick self-check

1. Zinc (134 pm) and copper (128 pm): which type of alloy? *(Substitutional: 4.7 % difference.)*
2. How many delocalized electrons in a model of 12 Al atoms? *(36)*
3. Why is steel less ductile than iron? *(Carbon atoms in the gaps make the lattice more rigid, so layers cannot slide as easily.)*
