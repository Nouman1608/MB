## Recap

- **Filtration** traps undissolved solid. It cannot separate a solution, because dissolved ions and molecules are far smaller than the filter's pores.
- To separate the parts of a solution, use a difference in their **intermolecular forces**.
- **Chromatography** (paper, thin-layer, column): a **mobile phase** (moving solvent) carries the mixture over a **stationary phase** (paper, silica gel). Components held more strongly by the stationary phase move more slowly.
- Paper (cellulose) and silica are **polar**: they form hydrogen bonds and dipole–dipole attractions with polar components.
- **Distillation**: heat the mixture, condense the vapor. The component with weaker intermolecular forces has the higher vapor pressure and collects first.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Meaning | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Rf = spot distance ÷ solvent-front distance | both from the origin; 0 ≤ Rf ≤ 1, no unit | compare spots on a chromatogram |
| Polar stationary phase: lower Rf ↔ more polar | the polar component is held back | rank relative polarities |
| More polar solvent → all Rf values rise | solvent competes for the surface | predict the effect of changing solvent |
| First out of a silica column = highest Rf on silica TLC | least attracted to the stationary phase | predict elution order |
| Weaker IMFs → higher vapor pressure → lower boiling point | from Topic 3.2 | predict which liquid distils first |

## Assumptions

- The stationary phase is **polar** (paper or silica). If it is nonpolar, every polarity conclusion reverses.
- Spots are compared on the **same plate with the same solvent**. Rf values from different solvents cannot be compared directly.
- In distillation, the vapor temperature is read with the thermometer bulb **level with the side arm**.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **"Furthest spot = most polar."** Wrong on paper or silica: the furthest spot is the least polar.
2. **Measuring from the paper's edge.** Measure from the origin line, to the centre of the spot.
3. **Inverting the ratio.** Rf can never be more than 1.
4. **Origin line in ink, or below the solvent.** Use pencil, and keep the origin above the solvent level.
5. **"Boiling breaks bonds."** Boiling overcomes intermolecular forces only.
6. **Comparing molar masses instead of IMFs.** Water (18 g mol⁻¹) boils at a higher temperature than hexane (86 g mol⁻¹) because of hydrogen bonding.
7. **"Distillation always gives pure liquids."** Close boiling points need a fractionating column, and some mixtures (ethanol–water) cannot be fully separated.

## Quick self-check

1. A spot moves 2.8 cm while the solvent front moves 7.0 cm. What is its Rf? *(0.40)*
2. On a silica plate, spot X has Rf 0.20 and spot Y has Rf 0.70. Which is more polar? *(X: it is held more strongly by the polar silica)*
3. Why can you not filter the sugar out of sugar water? *(The dissolved sugar molecules are far smaller than the filter's pores, so they pass through with the water.)*
4. Pentane and octane are mixed and distilled. Which collects first, and why? *(Pentane: fewer electrons, weaker London dispersion forces, higher vapor pressure.)*
