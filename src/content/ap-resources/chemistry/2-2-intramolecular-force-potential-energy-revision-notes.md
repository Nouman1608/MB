## Recap

- A **potential energy curve** plots energy (kJ mol⁻¹) against **internuclear distance** (pm). Zero = atoms far apart and not interacting.
- As atoms approach, **attraction** (each nucleus for both atoms' electrons) lowers the energy. Too close, **repulsion** (nucleus–nucleus, electron–electron) raises it steeply.
- The **minimum** gives the **equilibrium bond length**. The **depth** of the minimum below zero is the **bond energy**, the energy needed to separate the atoms.
- **Covalent bonds:** bigger atomic cores give longer bonds; longer bonds are usually weaker. **Higher bond order** gives shorter bonds with larger bond energies.
- **Ionic interactions:** Coulomb's law. Attraction is proportional to each ion's charge and grows as the distance between ion centres shrinks.

## Key relationships

| Change | Bond length | Bond energy | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Larger atoms (down a group) | longer | smaller | C–F 134 pm, 485 → C–I 213 pm, 240 kJ mol⁻¹ |
| Higher bond order | shorter | larger | C–C 154 pm, 347 → C≡C 120 pm, 839 kJ mol⁻¹ |
| Larger ion charges | — | stronger attraction | Mg²⁺O²⁻ > Na⁺F⁻ |
| Smaller ions (same charges) | — | stronger attraction | Na⁺F⁻ > Na⁺Cl⁻ |

Coulomb's law: **F ∝ q₁q₂ / r²**, with r ≈ sum of the ionic radii.

## Assumptions

- Tabulated bond energies are **averages** over many compounds.
- Comparing ions with q₁q₂ / r² treats them as point charges in contact; it ranks, it does not give a measured energy.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Quoting bond energy as negative.** The minimum is negative; the bond energy (energy to break) is positive.
2. **Saying bond breaking releases energy.** It always absorbs energy.
3. **Reading the bond length at the zero crossing** instead of at the minimum.
4. **Drawing curves that do not level off at zero** at large distances.
5. **Assuming a double bond is twice as strong** as a single bond.
6. **Using only charge or only size** when ranking ionic attractions.
7. **Missing units or scale** on a sketched graph.

## Quick self-check

1. A curve has its minimum at 120 pm and −500 kJ mol⁻¹. State the bond length and bond energy. *(120 pm; 500 kJ mol⁻¹)*
2. Which bond is shorter, C=C or C–C? *(C=C)*
3. Which pair attracts more strongly, K⁺ with F⁻ or K⁺ with I⁻? *(K⁺ with F⁻: same charges, but F⁻ is smaller.)*
