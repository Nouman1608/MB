Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/3-13-beer-lambert-law-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/3-13-beer-lambert-law-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can state the Beer-Lambert law, A = εbc, and give the meaning and unit of each symbol. *(Guide: "What the law says")*
- I can explain that ε depends on the species and the wavelength, but not on the concentration. *(Guide: "Molar absorptivity and the choice of wavelength"; Practice Q3, Q4)*
- I can explain why absorbance is proportional to both path length and concentration, using the number of absorbing particles in the light beam. *(Guide: "Why b and c matter"; Practice Q2, Q7)*
- I can explain why the instrument is set to λmax and zeroed with a blank. *(Guide: Figures 1 and 2; Practice Q3, Q6)*

## Calculation

- I can rearrange A = εbc to find any one quantity, with units that cancel correctly. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can find ε from the gradient of a calibration curve and read an unknown concentration from it. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can multiply back by a dilution factor to find the concentration of the original sample. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can predict a new absorbance when the path length or concentration changes. *(Practice Q2, Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can identify sources of error in an absorbance experiment, such as smudges, bubbles, cloudiness, water droplets or a different cuvette. *(Guide: Worked example 3)*
- I can predict whether an error makes the calculated concentration too high or too low, saying first what happens to A. *(Practice Q4, Q6, Q7)*
- I can explain why the unknown's absorbance should lie within the range of the standards. *(Guide: "Calibration curves")*
