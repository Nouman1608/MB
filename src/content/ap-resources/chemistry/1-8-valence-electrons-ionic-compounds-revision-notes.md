## Recap

- **Valence electrons** are the outer-shell electrons beyond the noble-gas core. Main groups: groups 1–2 have 1–2; groups 13–18 have (group − 10).
- Whether two elements bond depends on how strongly each **nucleus attracts valence electrons** (Coulomb's law: charge and distance). A weakly holding metal plus a strongly attracting nonmetal gives electron transfer and an ionic compound.
- **Metals** react by losing electrons, so they get **more** reactive down a group (valence electron further away, more shielded).
- **Halogens** react by gaining electrons, so they get **less** reactive down a group (incoming electron further from the nucleus).
- Elements in one group form **analogous compounds**: Na₂O, K₂O; MgCl₂, CaCl₂; H₂O, H₂S.
- Main-group ions take the electron arrangement of the nearest noble gas. The charge stops there because the next electron would come from (or go into) a different shell, which costs much more energy.

## Key relationships

| Group | Valence e⁻ | Typical ion | Example compound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | +1 | NaCl |
| 2 | 2 | +2 | MgO |
| 13 | 3 | +3 (Al) | Al₂O₃ |
| 15 | 5 | −3 | Ca₃N₂ |
| 16 | 6 | −2 | K₂S |
| 17 | 7 | −1 | CaF₂ |
| 18 | 8 (He 2) | none | — |

**Ionic formula rule:** smallest whole-number ratio of ions with total charge zero. Check: (number × charge) + (number × charge) = 0.

## Assumptions behind the predictions

- Predictions from position apply to **main-group** elements. Transition metals often form more than one ion; use the charge given (iron(III) means Fe³⁺).
- "Typical" charge means the usual one in simple ionic compounds, not every compound the element can form.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Subscripts are not charges.** In Ca₃N₂, calcium is 2+.
2. **Reduce the ratio.** MgO, not Mg₂O₂.
3. **Halogen trend runs the other way.** Fluorine is the most reactive halogen.
4. **Nonmetal charges are negative.** Sulfur forms S²⁻, not S⁶⁺.
5. **"Wants a full shell" is not an explanation.** Use energy: a jump in successive ionization energy, or a new shell for an extra electron.
6. **Analogous ≠ identical.** Same formula type; properties change gradually.

## Quick self-check

1. Formula of lithium nitride? *(Li₃N)*
2. Formula of aluminium sulfide? *(Al₂S₃)*
3. Successive ionization energies show the big jump after the second electron. Typical ion? *(M²⁺)*
4. Which reacts more vigorously with water, Na or K? *(K: its valence electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded)*
