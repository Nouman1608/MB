Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/7-8-representations-equilibrium-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/7-8-representations-equilibrium-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what a box in a particle diagram represents and why counts are proportional to concentration. *(Guide: "What a particle diagram shows")*
- I can tell from a sequence of snapshots when equilibrium has been reached. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6(b))*
- I can explain why particles move between equilibrium snapshots even though the counts stay the same. *(Guide: "Before and at equilibrium"; Practice Q1)*
- I can link a box that is mostly products, mostly reactants or a mixture to a large, small or moderate K. *(Guide: "Linking the picture to the size of K"; Practice Q3)*
- I can connect the particle view (reactions continuing) with the lab view (constant colour and pressure). *(Guide: "Particle level and lab level")*

## Calculation

- I can calculate K directly from counts when both sides of the equation have the same number of particles. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can convert counts to concentrations and calculate Kc when the particle numbers differ on each side. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6(c))*
- I can calculate Q from a box and predict how the box will change. *(Guide: Worked examples 2 and 3; Practice Q4)*
- I can use an ICE table in particle counts to find the equilibrium box. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning and drawing

- I can draw an equilibrium box with a key, the right counts and conserved atoms. *(Guide: "Drawing your own particle diagram"; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can check a sequence of boxes for atom conservation. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6(a))*
- I can find and explain errors in someone else's particle diagram. *(Practice Q3, Q7)*
