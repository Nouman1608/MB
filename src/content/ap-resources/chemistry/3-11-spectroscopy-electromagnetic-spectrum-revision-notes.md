## Recap

- Electromagnetic radiation delivers energy in packets called **photons**.
- In order of **increasing photon energy** (decreasing wavelength): radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma.
- A molecule stores energy in **rotation**, **vibration** and **electron arrangement**. Each is quantized.
- The gaps between levels grow in the order **rotational ≪ vibrational ≪ electronic**.
- A photon is absorbed only if its energy **matches** a gap. So the region a substance absorbs in tells you the type of transition.
- Emission is the reverse: the species drops down a gap and gives out a photon of matching energy.

## Key relationships

| Region | Approximate wavelength | Transition | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Microwave | 1 mm to 1 m | rotational | how fast the molecule spins |
| Infrared | 700 nm to 1 mm | vibrational | how strongly bonds stretch and bend |
| Visible | 400 nm to 700 nm | electronic | an electron moves to a higher level |
| Ultraviolet | 10 nm to 400 nm | electronic | an electron moves up; bonds may break |

Colour: a substance looks roughly the **complementary** colour of the light it absorbs (absorbs orange → looks blue).

## Assumptions behind the model

- Energy levels are **quantized** and the three kinds of gap are well separated in size.
- Region boundaries are **approximate**; near an edge, the region only roughly predicts the transition.
- Single atoms have no bonds, so they cannot vibrate and show no molecular rotational spectrum; they show only electronic transitions.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Reversing the energy order.** Long wavelength means low energy, not high.
2. **"Infrared excites electrons."** No: infrared is vibrational.
3. **"Microwaves cause vibration."** No: microwaves are rotational.
4. **Comparing wavelengths in different units.** Convert mm, μm and nm to metres first.
5. **Naming the absorbed colour as the colour seen.** You see what is left.
6. **Confusing brightness with photon energy.** More photons do not make each photon more energetic.

## Quick self-check

1. Which has more energy per photon, infrared or ultraviolet? *(Ultraviolet)*
2. A gas absorbs at 3.5 μm. What type of transition is this? *(Vibrational: 3.5 μm is infrared)*
3. Why does neon gas show no infrared absorption? *(Single atoms have no bonds to vibrate)*
4. A solution absorbs violet light. Roughly what colour does it look? *(Yellow)*
