## Understanding

- I can describe the four regions of a weak acid titration curve and name the major species in each. *(Guide: "What a pH titration curve tells you")*
- I can explain why a strong and a weak acid with the same moles need the same volume of base. *(Guide: "The equivalence point"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why pH = pKa at the half-equivalence point, using the Ka expression. *(Guide: "The half-equivalence point")*
- I can explain whether the equivalence-point pH is neutral, basic or acidic from the species left in the flask. *(Guide: "What sets the pH"; Practice Q1, Q6)*

## Calculation

- I can find the concentration of a monoprotic analyte from the equivalence volume. *(Practice Q3, Q5)*
- I can read pKa from a curve or data table and convert it to Ka, or to Kb for a weak base. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2)*
- I can calculate the pH at the start, in the buffer region, at equivalence and after equivalence for a monoprotic titration. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can use the total volume of solution whenever I turn moles into a concentration after mixing. *(Practice Q5, Q6)*

## Reasoning with curves

- I can find the number of acidic protons of a polyprotic acid from its titration curve. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q7)*
- I can read pKa₁ and pKa₂ from a diprotic curve at the correct volumes. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can identify the major species at any point along a polyprotic curve. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4, Q7)*
- I can find the moles of a polyprotic acid from its first equivalence volume. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can compare strong and weak acid curves: starting pH, buffer region and equivalence pH. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6)*
