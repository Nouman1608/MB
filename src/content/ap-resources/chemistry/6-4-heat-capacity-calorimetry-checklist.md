Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/6-4-heat-capacity-calorimetry-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/6-4-heat-capacity-calorimetry-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what the sign of q means: positive when a system absorbs heat, negative when it releases heat. *(Guide: "From direction to amount")*
- I can define specific heat capacity, molar heat capacity and the heat capacity of an object, with their units. *(Guide: "Molar heat capacity and the heat capacity of an object")*
- I can explain why equal masses of different substances change temperature by different amounts when they gain the same energy. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can state the first law of thermodynamics and use it to say that energy lost = energy gained in an insulated calorimeter. *(Guide: "Calorimetry: energy lost = energy gained")*
- I can name the three main ways a system changes its energy. *(Guide: "From direction to amount")*

## Calculation

- I can calculate q with q = mcΔT, writing ΔT as final minus initial. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can convert a specific heat capacity into a molar heat capacity. *(Practice Q3)*
- I can find an unknown specific heat capacity or a final temperature from calorimetry data. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can find the heat capacity of a calorimeter from a mixing experiment. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can give an answer to a precision that matches the temperature change. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(c))*

## Reasoning

- I can use a temperature rise or fall in a dissolution calorimeter to decide whether dissolving is exothermic or endothermic, and give q for the process the correct sign. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4, Q7(a))*
- I can calculate the energy per mole of solute dissolved, and explain why it stays the same when the mass changes but ΔT does not. *(Practice Q7(c)–(d))*
- I can state the assumptions behind a calorimetry calculation. *(Guide: "The usual assumptions"; Practice Q6(d))*
