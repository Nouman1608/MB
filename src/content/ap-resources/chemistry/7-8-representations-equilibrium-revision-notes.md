## Recap

- A **particle diagram** is a snapshot of a small, fixed volume. The count of each species is proportional to its concentration or partial pressure.
- In a **sequence** of boxes, the counts change while Q moves towards K. When the counts stop changing, **Q = K** and the system is at equilibrium.
- Particles in later equilibrium boxes sit in different places because reactions continue in both directions at **equal rates**. Equilibrium is **dynamic**.
- The same equilibrium box can be reached from mostly reactants or from mostly products, if the atoms, volume and temperature are the same.
- At the lab scale, colour, pressure and concentration stay constant because the particle-level changes cancel out.

## Key relationships

| Idea | Rule | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Concentration from a box | c = (count × mol per particle) ÷ box volume | turn a picture into numbers for Kc |
| Counts straight into K | allowed only when both sides have the same total number of particles | save a step in reactions such as A₂ + B₂ ⇌ 2 AB |
| Q from a box | same form as K, using the counts (or concentrations) shown | decide which way the box will change |
| Mostly products at equilibrium | K very large | link a picture to Topic 7.5 |
| Mostly reactants at equilibrium | K very small | link a picture to Topic 7.5 |

## Assumptions behind a diagram

- Every box in a sequence has the **same volume** and the same **temperature**.
- A box is a **representative sample**: its ratio matches the whole container.
- Very small amounts (less than one particle's worth) **cannot be drawn**, so they look like zero.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Equal counts ≠ equilibrium.** Equilibrium means counts that no longer change.
2. **Using counts directly when the particle numbers differ on each side.** Convert first, or K comes out wrong by a factor.
3. **Counting a joined pair as two particles.** One J₂ is one particle.
4. **Breaking atom conservation.** Count each element in every box you draw.
5. **Saying reactions stop at equilibrium.** They continue at equal rates.
6. **No key.** Use shapes or shading, not colour alone, and say what each symbol is.

## Quick self-check

1. For X₂ + Y₂ ⇌ 2 XY, an equilibrium box holds 2 X₂, 1 Y₂ and 4 XY. What is K? *(4² ÷ (2 × 1) = 8.0; counts can be used directly)*
2. For 2 N ⇌ N₂, a 1.0 L box at equilibrium holds 2 N and 2 N₂, and each particle is 0.050 mol. What is Kc? *([N] = [N₂] = 0.10 mol L⁻¹, so Kc = 0.10 ÷ 0.10² = 10; raw counts would give 0.5)*
3. What must stay the same in every box of a sequence? *(The number of atoms of each element, and the box volume)*
