Short on time? This page is the recap. For explanations, a particle diagram and worked examples, use the [full study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/7-5-magnitude-equilibrium-constant-study-guide/).

## Recap

- K has **products on top** and **reactants on the bottom**, so its size shows which side the equilibrium mixture favours.
- **Very large K**: the mixture at equilibrium is mostly products. The reaction goes **essentially to completion**.
- **Very small K**: the mixture is mostly reactants. The reaction **barely proceeds**.
- **K near 1**: both reactants and products are present in significant amounts.
- A rough guide (not a rule): above about 10³ is product-favoured, below about 10⁻³ is reactant-favoured.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Meaning | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Kc = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ / ([A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ) | products over reactants, at equilibrium | decide which side is favoured |
| For A ⇌ B: [B] / [A] = K | volume cancels, so also number of B ÷ number of A | read a particle diagram |
| For A ⇌ B: fraction as B = K / (1 + K) | e.g. K = 10² gives 99.0 % B | turn K into a percentage |
| Small K: substitute the reactant concentration into the expression | reactant barely changes | estimate the trace of product |

## Assumptions behind the reasoning

- The system has actually **reached equilibrium**. K says nothing about a mixture that is still changing.
- The **temperature is fixed**. K has one value per temperature.
- You are comparing K values for expressions of the **same shape**. For P ⇌ 2R, K = 1 does not mean equal concentrations.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **"Large K, so fast."** K is about position, not rate. A catalyst changes the rate, not K.
2. **"Small K, so no product."** A zero product concentration would make K = 0.
3. **"Complete" instead of "essentially complete".** A trace of reactant always remains.
4. **Changing K by adding a substance.** Adding reactant changes Q; only temperature changes K.
5. **Forgetting the powers.** Square the concentrations that have a coefficient of 2.
6. **Mixing Kc and Kp.** Use whichever the question gives; do not convert.

## Quick self-check

1. For A ⇌ B, K = 1 × 10⁻⁵. What percentage of the material is B at equilibrium? *(about 0.001 %)*
2. For X ⇌ Y, K = 4.0, and a box holds 25 particles at equilibrium. How many are Y? *(20 Y and 5 X)*
3. A reaction has K = 10³⁰ but no change is seen after a week. Give one reason. *(The rate is very slow, for example because of a high activation energy; K does not describe rate.)*
