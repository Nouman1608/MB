## Recap

- Most reactions do not happen in one collision. They happen as a **reaction mechanism**: a series of **elementary steps**, each one a single molecular event.
- The coefficients of an elementary step tell you how many particles react (its **molecularity**: uni-, bi- or, rarely, termolecular). The coefficients of the overall equation do not.
- When you add the steps and cancel species on both sides, you must get the **overall balanced equation**.
- An **intermediate** is present only while the reaction is running: it starts at zero, builds up a little and returns to zero.
- A mechanism is a model. Detecting an intermediate that only one proposed mechanism predicts is evidence for that mechanism.

## Key relationships

| Species | Pattern in the steps | In the overall equation? |
|---|---|---|
| Reactant | Used, not made back | Yes, left side |
| Product | Made, not used again | Yes, right side |
| Intermediate | Made in an early step, used in a later step | No |
| Catalyst | Used in an early step, made back in a later step | No |

## Assumptions behind the model

- Each step is a genuine **elementary** event, so its coefficients show the particles that collide.
- Steps involving three or more particles colliding at once are treated as unlikely.
- The mechanism describes the *main* route; it is a proposal supported by evidence, not a certainty.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Treating the overall equation as one step.** Its coefficients summarise; they do not describe a collision.
2. **Mixing up intermediates and catalysts.** Check the order: made-then-used, or used-then-given-back.
3. **Forgetting a multiplier.** If an intermediate is made twice but used once, the later step happens twice.
4. **Cancelling unequal amounts.** Only cancel what is equal on both sides.
5. **Calling a transition state an intermediate.** A transition state cannot be isolated.
6. **Thinking "adds up" means "proven".** Several mechanisms can add up to the same equation.

## Quick self-check

1. Steps: A₂ + C → A₂C, then A₂C + C → A₂ + C₂. What is the overall equation? *(2C → C₂)*
2. In question 1, which species is the intermediate and which is the catalyst? *(A₂C is the intermediate; A₂ is the catalyst)*
3. Mechanism X predicts intermediate M; mechanism Y does not. M is detected. What can you conclude? *(The evidence supports X over Y, but does not prove X.)*
