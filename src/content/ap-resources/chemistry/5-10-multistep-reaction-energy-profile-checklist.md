Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-10-multistep-reaction-energy-profile-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-10-multistep-reaction-energy-profile-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain how a multistep profile is built by joining the profiles of the elementary steps. *(Guide: "From one hump to several")*
- I can tell a transition state (peak) from an intermediate (valley) and say why only the intermediate could be detected. *(Guide: "Anatomy of a multistep profile"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain, using collisions, why the step with the largest activation energy is the slowest. *(Guide: "Which step is rate-limiting?"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why the overall ΔH does not depend on the number of steps. *(Guide: "Reading the numbers")*

## Skills

- I can count the steps and intermediates on a profile. *(Practice Q1)*
- I can measure each step's activation energy from the valley where that step starts. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can find each step's ΔH and the overall ΔH, and check that the steps add up. *(Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can find the activation energy of a reverse step. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6)*
- I can turn step data into energies and sketch a labelled profile with the correct relative heights. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can identify the rate-limiting step from a profile and link it to the rate law, including a fast pre-equilibrium. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can describe a profile shape that is consistent with a given rate law and mechanism. *(Practice Q7)*
