## Understanding

- I can explain the difference between Q, which describes a mixture at any moment, and K, the value Q reaches at equilibrium. *(Guide: "Q at any moment, K at equilibrium"; Practice Q5)*
- I can state that at equilibrium Qc = Kc and Qp = Kp, and that Q moves towards K as a reaction runs. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5(d))*
- I can explain that Kc uses concentrations and Kp uses partial pressures, so their values usually differ. *(Guide: "Gases: Qp and Kp"; Practice Q5(b))*
- I can explain why pure solids and pure liquids are left out of Q and K. *(Guide: "What to leave out"; Practice Q7)*

## Skills

- I can write Kc for a reaction with gases, with each concentration raised to its coefficient. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can write Kp for a heterogeneous reaction, leaving out the solids. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q2, Q7)*
- I can write Kc for a reaction in water, leaving out H₂O(l) but keeping H₂O(g) when it appears. *(Practice Q1, Q3)*
- I can substitute concentrations or partial pressures into Q and calculate its value. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4, Q5(c))*
- I can plot Q against time with labelled axes and an even scale, and read K from the flat part. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can decide whether a mixture is at equilibrium by comparing Q with K. *(Guide: Worked examples 2 and 3; Practice Q5(d))*
- I can explain why two mixtures that start differently at the same temperature end with the same value of Q. *(Practice Q6(e))*
- I can explain why adding more of a solid does not change Q. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7(b))*
