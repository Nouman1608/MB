## Recap

- To predict equilibrium concentrations you need the **balanced equation**, the **initial** concentrations or partial pressures, and **K**.
- **Step 1:** calculate Q from the starting values and compare it with K. The mixture always changes so that Q moves towards K.
- **Step 2:** build an **ICE table**, substitute the Equilibrium row into K and solve for x.
- At equilibrium (Q = K) the **forward and reverse rates are equal**. Both reactions continue, but concentrations stay constant. This is **dynamic** equilibrium.

## Key relationships

| Idea | Rule | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Q < K | net forward reaction; reactants fall, products rise | set the signs in the Change row |
| Q > K | net reverse reaction; products fall, reactants rise | set the signs in the Change row |
| Q = K | no net change; forward rate = reverse rate | recognise a mixture already at equilibrium |
| Change row | changes in the ratio of the coefficients (−x, −2x, +3x …) | write every species in terms of one unknown |
| c = n / V | mol L⁻¹ from moles and litres | get concentrations before using Kc |
| Small-x check | x < about 5% of the starting value | decide whether c − x ≈ c is acceptable |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The temperature is **constant**; K only applies at the temperature it was measured at.
- Pure **solids and liquids** are left out of K and out of the ICE table.
- Kc uses **concentrations**; Kp uses **partial pressures**. Use the one the question gives. Converting between them is not assessed.
- In the small-x shortcut, you assume very little reacts. You must check this assumption afterwards.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Putting initial values into K.** They give Q, not K.
2. **Ignoring coefficients.** A coefficient of 2 gives a change of 2x, squared in K as (2x)².
3. **Assuming a forward reaction.** Compare Q with K first.
4. **Using moles in Kc.** Divide by the volume.
5. **Forcing the small-x shortcut.** If x is more than about 5% of the starting value, solve the quadratic.
6. **Keeping a root that gives a negative concentration.** Only one root makes physical sense.
7. **Thinking equilibrium means equal amounts or no reaction.** It means constant amounts and equal rates.

## Quick self-check

1. For A(g) ⇌ 2 B(g), Kc = 0.10. A mixture has [A] = 0.50 mol L⁻¹ and [B] = 0.30 mol L⁻¹. Which way does it react? *(Q = 0.30² ÷ 0.50 = 0.18 > 0.10, so backwards: B falls, A rises)*
2. For 2 G ⇌ H going forwards, [G] falls by 0.30 mol L⁻¹. By how much does [H] rise? *(0.15 mol L⁻¹)*
3. For Z ⇌ Y, Kc = 4.0. A flask starts with [Z] = 1.00 mol L⁻¹ and no Y. Find the equilibrium concentrations. *(x ÷ (1.00 − x) = 4.0, so x = 0.80: [Y] = 0.80 mol L⁻¹, [Z] = 0.20 mol L⁻¹)*
