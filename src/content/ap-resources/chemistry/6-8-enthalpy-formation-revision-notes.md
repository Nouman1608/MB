## Recap

- The **standard enthalpy of formation**, ΔH°f, is the enthalpy change when **1 mol** of a substance forms from its **elements in their standard states**, under standard conditions (1 bar, data usually at 298 K).
- The **standard state** of an element is its most stable form: O₂(g), N₂(g), H₂(g), C(graphite), Br₂(l), Fe(s) and so on. For these, **ΔH°f = 0**.
- Other forms of an element are not zero: O₃(g) and C(diamond) have positive values.
- A table of ΔH°f values lets you calculate ΔH° for any reaction or physical change between listed substances.

## Key relationships

| Idea | Rule |
|---|---|
| Reaction enthalpy | ΔH°rxn = ΣnΔH°f(products) − ΣnΔH°f(reactants) |
| Coefficients | multiply each ΔH°f by its coefficient n |
| Element in standard state | ΔH°f = 0 |
| Formation equation | exactly 1 mol of product, so fractions such as ½N₂ are allowed |
| Physical change | same formula, e.g. H₂O(l) → H₂O(g): +44.0 kJ mol⁻¹ |
| Unknown ΔH°f | rearrange the formula and solve for the missing value |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- All values refer to the **same standard conditions**, so differences between them are meaningful.
- ΔH° is per mole of the reaction **as written**; double the equation and ΔH° doubles.
- Each substance is in the **state shown** in the equation, and the table value for that state is used.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Reactants − products.** Wrong sign.
2. **Ignoring coefficients.** 6H₂O means 6 × ΔH°f.
3. **Wrong state.** H₂O(l) −285.8, H₂O(g) −241.8 kJ mol⁻¹.
4. **Non-zero value for O₂(g), H₂(g) or C(graphite)**, or zero for O₃.
5. **Losing a minus sign** when subtracting a negative total. Use brackets.
6. **Confusing with bond enthalpies**, where it is broken − formed and the reactants come first.

## Quick self-check

1. What is ΔH°f of N₂(g)? *(0, because it is an element in its standard state.)*
2. Using NO(g) +90.3 and NO₂(g) +33.2 kJ mol⁻¹, find ΔH° for 2NO(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO₂(g). *(66.4 − 180.6 = −114.2 kJ mol⁻¹.)*
3. Write the equation whose ΔH° is ΔH°f of CO₂(g). *(C(graphite) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g).)*
