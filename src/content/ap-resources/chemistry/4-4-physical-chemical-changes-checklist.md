## Understanding

- I can tell chemical bonds (covalent, ionic, metallic) apart from intermolecular attractions, with an example of each. *(Guide: "Two kinds of attraction, two kinds of change")*
- I can state the bond-level rule for classifying a process as chemical or physical. *(Guide; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why phase changes keep molecules intact. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1, Q2)*
- I can explain why breaking chemical bonds usually needs much more energy than overcoming intermolecular attractions. *(Guide: "Why the energy scale is so different"; Practice Q4)*

## Skills

- I can classify a process as physical or chemical by comparing the formulas present before and after. *(Guide: "A decision routine"; Worked example 1)*
- I can explain a macroscopic observation, such as a gas or a colour change, in terms of what the particles do. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can pick out the strongest evidence of a chemical change from a set of observations. *(Practice Q3)*
- I can describe what happens to ions and water molecules when an ionic solid dissolves. *(Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can make a claim about the type of change and support it with evidence from experimental data. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can give an argument for each side of the "dissolving a salt" question. *(Guide: "When the line is blurred"; Practice Q6)*
- I can show that a gas or colour change is evidence, not proof, by giving a counterexample. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why melting an ionic or network solid is usually called physical even though chemical bonds break. *(Practice Q7)*
