## Recap

- In a reaction, bonds in the reactants **break** and new bonds in the products **form**. Both change the potential energy of the system.
- **Breaking** a bond is endothermic (energy in). **Forming** the same bond is exothermic and releases the same amount.
- A **bond enthalpy** is the energy to break one mole of a bond in gaseous molecules. It is always positive.
- Tables give **average** bond enthalpies, taken over many molecules. Answers from them are **estimates**.

## Key relationships

| Idea | Rule |
|---|---|
| Estimate of ΔH | ΔH ≈ Σ(bonds broken) − Σ(bonds formed) |
| Exothermic | energy released forming bonds > energy needed to break bonds; ΔH < 0 |
| Endothermic | energy needed > energy released; ΔH > 0 |
| Counting | bonds per molecule (from Lewis structure) × coefficient |
| Shortcut | bonds unchanged on both sides cancel; the answer does not change |
| Unknown bond | rearrange the same equation and solve for the missing value |

## Assumptions behind the method

- All reactants and products are **gases**. Energy from intermolecular forces in liquids or solids is not counted.
- Each bond has its **average** strength, whatever molecule it is in.
- The imaginary path "break everything, then form everything" gives the same ΔH as the real path, because ΔH depends only on the start and the end.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Formed − broken.** Wrong sign. It is broken − formed.
2. **"Breaking bonds releases energy."** Never.
3. **Double bond = two single bonds.** Use the C=C or O=O value itself.
4. **Missing coefficients.** 2NH₃ has 6 N–H bonds.
5. **Wrong bond order.** O₂ is O=O, N₂ is N≡N, CO₂ is O=C=O (two C=O).
6. **Expecting the exact measured value.** It is an estimate.

## Quick self-check

1. Estimate ΔH for H₂(g) + F₂(g) → 2HF(g) using H–H 432, F–F 154, H–F 565 kJ mol⁻¹. *(586 − 1130 = −544 kJ mol⁻¹, exothermic.)*
2. How many C–H bonds are broken when 2 mol of CH₄ react completely? *(8 mol.)*
3. Give one reason a bond-enthalpy estimate differs from the measured ΔH. *(Table values are averages over many molecules; or a substance is not a gas.)*
