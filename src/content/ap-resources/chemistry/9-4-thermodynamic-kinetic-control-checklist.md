Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/9-4-thermodynamic-kinetic-control-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/9-4-thermodynamic-kinetic-control-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why ΔG° tells me whether a process is favoured but not how fast it goes. *(Guide: "Favoured is not the same as fast")*
- I can define kinetic control: favoured (ΔG° < 0) but with no measurable rate. *(Guide: "Kinetic control: what the term means"; Practice Q1)*
- I can name a high activation energy as the usual cause of kinetic control. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can state that a catalyst lowers Eₐ but leaves ΔG° unchanged. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2)*

## Skills

- I can calculate ΔG° from ΔG°f values to show that a slow process is favoured. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can read a schematic energy profile and point to the barrier and the overall drop. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate a reverse activation energy from the forward Eₐ and ΔH°. *(Practice Q5(b))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain, in terms of collisions, why a high Eₐ makes a favoured reaction too slow to observe. *(Guide: "Why a favoured reaction can be slow"; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can argue that a system showing no change is not at equilibrium by comparing Q with K. *(Guide: "Kinetic control is not equilibrium"; Practice Q4, Q6(c))*
- I can describe a test (catalyst or spark) that tells kinetic control apart from equilibrium. *(Guide: table; Practice Q6(d))*
- I can tell a process under kinetic control from one that is not favoured at all. *(Guide: "Kinetic control or not favoured?"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why diamond survives at room temperature even though graphite is more stable. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
