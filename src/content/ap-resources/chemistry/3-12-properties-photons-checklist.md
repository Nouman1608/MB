## Understanding

- I can explain that absorbing a photon raises the energy of an atom or molecule by exactly the photon's energy, and emitting one lowers it by that amount. *(Guide: "A photon is a fixed packet of energy")*
- I can state whether wavelength, frequency and photon energy are directly or inversely proportional to each other. *(Revision notes; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why a brighter lamp delivers more photons but not more energetic ones. *(Guide: "Brightness is not photon energy"; Practice Q7)*

## Calculation

- I can convert a wavelength in cm or nm to metres and use c = λν to find the frequency. *(Practice Q1)*
- I can use E = hν or E = hc/λ to find the energy of one photon, with units cancelling to J. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
- I can convert between energy per photon in J and energy per mole of photons in kJ mol⁻¹. *(Practice Q3)*
- I can find the longest wavelength able to break a bond from its bond energy. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate the number of photons, and moles of photons, in a given amount of light energy. *(Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can use an energy-level diagram to find the wavelength of each emission line and say which region it is in. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can work out an unknown energy level from two measured lines, and explain which lines a ground-state atom can absorb. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can evaluate a claim about light intensity and transitions using calculated photon energies. *(Practice Q7)*
