## Recap

- In an elementary step, some bonds **break** and new bonds **form**, at overlapping times.
- The **reaction coordinate** (x-axis) tracks progress through that rearrangement. It is not time.
- The **energy profile** goes from the reactant level, up to one peak, the **transition state**, then down to the product level.
- The **transition state** is the highest-energy arrangement, with partial bonds. It cannot be isolated.
- **Activation energy** is the energy needed to get from reactants to the transition state. Rate depends on the fraction of collisions that can reach it.

## Key relationships

| Quantity | How to read it | Sign |
|---|---|---|
| Eₐ(forward) | E(transition state) − E(reactants) | always positive |
| Eₐ(reverse) | E(transition state) − E(products) | always positive |
| ΔH (overall energy change) | E(products) − E(reactants) | negative: exothermic; positive: endothermic |
| Link | Eₐ(reverse) = Eₐ(forward) − ΔH | |
| Arrhenius equation | k = A e^(−Eₐ/RT) | qualitative use only |

In the Arrhenius equation, e^(−Eₐ/RT) is linked to the fraction of collisions that can reach the transition state; A holds collision frequency and orientation. T is in kelvin.

## Assumptions

- A single elementary step has **one** peak and no dips between reactants and products.
- The profile is a property of the reaction: it does **not** change with temperature or concentration.
- Only energy differences matter; the zero on the energy axis is arbitrary.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Reading Eₐ as the peak's axis value** instead of the gap from reactants.
2. **Linking ΔH to speed.** An exothermic step can be slow.
3. **Calling the transition state an intermediate.**
4. **Drawing a lower peak at higher temperature.** The peak is fixed.
5. **Getting the sign of ΔH backwards.** Products below reactants: ΔH negative.
6. **Drawing two humps for one elementary step.**

## Quick self-check

1. An elementary step has Eₐ(forward) = 75 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔH = −30 kJ mol⁻¹. What is Eₐ(reverse)? *(105 kJ mol⁻¹)*
2. Where on the profile is the transition state, and what are its bonds like? *(At the single highest point; old bonds partly broken, new bonds partly formed)*
3. Two steps have similar A. Which one's rate rises by the larger factor when heated? *(The one with the larger Eₐ)*
