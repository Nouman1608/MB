## Understanding

- I can list the regions of the electromagnetic spectrum in order of wavelength and of photon energy. *(Guide: "The electromagnetic spectrum"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why a shorter wavelength means a more energetic photon. *(Guide: "Light is a stream of energy packets"; Practice Q6)*
- I can describe the three ways a molecule stores energy: rotation, vibration and electron arrangement. *(Guide: "Three ways a molecule stores energy")*
- I can state that rotational gaps are much smaller than vibrational gaps, which are much smaller than electronic gaps. *(Guide: Figure 2)*

## Skills

- I can match microwave radiation to rotational transitions, infrared to vibrational transitions, and ultraviolet or visible radiation to electronic transitions. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can convert wavelengths given in mm, μm and nm to metres before comparing them. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can assign a set of absorption wavelengths to regions and transition types and rank the energy gaps. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why a single atom shows only electronic transitions, while a molecule can also absorb microwaves and infrared. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain a solution's colour as the light left after an electronic absorption, and explain why a substance that absorbs only in the ultraviolet is colourless. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can evaluate a claim about which radiation can break a bond, using photon energies and bond energies. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why a gas absorbs infrared but lets visible light through, using the idea that a photon must match a gap. *(Practice Q7)*
