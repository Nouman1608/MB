## Recap

- An atom is a small **positive nucleus** (protons and neutrons) surrounded by **negative electrons**. Attraction between them holds the atom together.
- Electrons occupy **shells** (n = 1, 2, 3 …) split into **subshells** s, p, d, f, holding 2, 6, 10 and 14 electrons.
- The **ground-state configuration** fills the lowest-energy subshells first (Aufbau). The periodic table's s, p, d and f blocks show the order.
- **Core electrons** are inside the noble-gas core; **valence electrons** are in the highest occupied shell.
- An electron is harder to remove when it is **closer** to the nucleus and feels a **larger effective nuclear charge**.

## Key relationships

| Idea | Rule | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Coulomb's law | F ∝ q₁q₂ / r² | compare attractions: 2 × charge → 2 × force; 2 × distance → ¼ force |
| Filling order | 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p 5s 4d 5p | write configurations of atoms and anions |
| Cation rule | remove from the largest n first (4s before 3d) | write configurations of cations |
| Core charge | ≈ protons − core electrons | estimate the charge an outer electron feels |

## Assumptions behind the model

- The **core charge** is an estimate: shielding by inner electrons is not perfect. Use it for comparisons, not exact values.
- Configurations are for the **ground state** (lowest energy) of an isolated atom or ion.
- Exceptions such as Cr and Cu exist; writing them is not assessed on the exam.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Removing 3d before 4s.** Fe²⁺ is [Ar]3d⁶, not [Ar]4s²3d⁴.
2. **Forgetting the d block.** Arsenic is [Ar]4s²3d¹⁰4p³, not [Ar]4s²4p³.
3. **Superscripts that do not add up.** Check the total against the electron count.
4. **Treating 1/r² as 1/r.** Doubling the distance quarters the force.
5. **Changing protons in an ion.** Only electrons change.
6. **Ranking without reasons.** Name both charge and distance.

## Quick self-check

1. Noble-gas configuration of K? *([Ar]4s¹)*
2. Configuration of Zn²⁺? *([Ar]3d¹⁰)*
3. If the distance between two charges halves, what happens to the force? *(It becomes 4 times larger.)*
4. Which is harder to remove: a 3s electron from Na or from Mg? *(Mg: core charge about +2 against +1, at a similar distance.)*
