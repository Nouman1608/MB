## Recap

- **q** is the energy transferred as heat, in J or kJ. Positive q: the system absorbs heat (its energy rises). Negative q: it releases heat (its energy falls).
- **Specific heat capacity, c**, is the energy to warm 1 g by 1 °C. Water: 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹, unusually large.
- The same energy given to equal masses of different substances gives **different** temperature changes: the smaller c, the larger ΔT.
- **Molar heat capacity** is per mole; the **heat capacity** of an object is for the whole object.
- **First law of thermodynamics:** energy is conserved. In an insulated calorimeter, energy released by one part is absorbed by the rest.
- A system changes its energy by **heating/cooling**, **phase changes** (Topic 6.5) and **reactions** (Topic 6.6 on).
- **Dissolution calorimeter:** mixture warms → dissolving is exothermic; mixture cools → dissolving is endothermic.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| q = mcΔT | q in J, m in g, c in J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹, ΔT in °C (or K) | heat for a mass of one substance |
| q = nC_mΔT, with C_m = c × M | C_m in J mol⁻¹ °C⁻¹ | heat for an amount in moles |
| q = CΔT, with C = mc | C in J °C⁻¹ | heat for a whole object or a calorimeter |
| ΔT = T(final) − T(initial) | °C or K (same size) | gives q its sign |
| q(lost) + q(gained) = 0 | J | any insulated calorimeter |
| q(process) = −q(solution) | J | energy of dissolving or a reaction in solution |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- No energy is exchanged with the air or bench (perfect insulation).
- The calorimeter absorbs negligible energy, unless its heat capacity is given.
- A dilute solution has c = 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹; its mass is water plus solute.
- The final reading is the thermal-equilibrium temperature, after stirring.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Sign of ΔT.** Always final minus initial.
2. **Sign of q(process).** It is opposite to q(solution).
3. **"High c heats fast."** High c heats *slowly*.
4. **Wrong mass.** Use the mass of the substance whose temperature changes (for a solution, water + solute).
5. **Per gram vs per mole.** Match c with grams and C_m with moles.
6. **Too many significant figures.** ΔT, found by subtraction, often has fewer than the readings.
7. **Halfway final temperature.** Only when the two heat capacities (mc) are equal.

## Quick self-check

1. Energy to warm 10.0 g of water by 5.0 °C? *(209 J)*
2. 100.0 g of water and 100.0 g of copper each absorb 2.00 kJ. Which warms more? *(Copper: about 52 °C against 4.78 °C.)*
3. In a calorimeter the solution's q is −850 J. What is q for the process? Exothermic or endothermic? *(+850 J; endothermic: the solution cooled.)*
