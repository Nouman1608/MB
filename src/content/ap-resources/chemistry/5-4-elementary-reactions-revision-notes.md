## Recap

- An **elementary reaction** is one event: one particle reacts on its own, or two (rarely three) particles collide and react in that collision.
- An **overall reaction** is the sum of one or more elementary steps.
- A step's rate depends on how often the right particles meet, which is proportional to the **product of their concentrations**.
- So, **for an elementary step only**, each reactant's coefficient is its order in the rate law. Products do not appear.
- **Molecularity** is the number of reactant particles in the step.
- Termolecular steps are **rare**: three particles must meet at the same instant with the right energy and orientation.

## Key relationships

| Elementary step | Molecularity | Rate law | Units of k |
|---|---|---|---|
| A → products | unimolecular | k[A] | s⁻¹ |
| A + B → products | bimolecular | k[A][B] | M⁻¹ s⁻¹ |
| 2A → products | bimolecular | k[A]² | M⁻¹ s⁻¹ |
| 2A + B → products | termolecular | k[A]²[B] | M⁻² s⁻¹ |

Testing an overall reaction:

| Experimental rate law vs rate law from coefficients | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| Different | Not a single elementary step |
| Same | One step is possible, but not proved |
| Four or more reactant particles | Cannot be one step |

## Assumptions

- You have been told the step is elementary. You cannot tell from the equation alone.
- Temperature is constant, so k is constant.
- The rate law is for the forward step only.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Writing a rate law from the coefficients of an **overall** equation.
2. Calling 2A → products unimolecular. Count particles, not substances.
3. Putting **products** in the rate law.
4. Saying termolecular steps are **impossible** (they are rare).
5. Treating a matching rate law as **proof** of a single step.

## Quick self-check

1. Write the rate law for the elementary step NO + NO₃ → 2 NO₂. *(rate = k[NO][NO₃]; bimolecular)*
2. For the elementary step A + 2B → products, by what factor does the rate change if [B] doubles? *(× 4)*
3. And if [A] and [B] both double? *(× 8)*
4. What are the units of k for a termolecular step? *(M⁻² s⁻¹)*
5. An overall reaction 2X + Y → Z has rate = k[X][Y]. Can it be one step? *(No: one step would give k[X]²[Y].)*
