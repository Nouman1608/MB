Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-3-concentration-changes-over-time-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/5-3-concentration-changes-over-time-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why the shape of a concentration–time graph depends on the order of the reaction. *(Guide: "From a rate law to a concentration–time graph")*
- I can state which plot is straight for a zero-, first- and second-order reaction. *(Guide: "The three integrated rate laws"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why the half-life of a first-order reaction does not depend on the starting concentration. *(Guide: "Half-life"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why radioactive decay is a first-order process. *(Guide: "Radioactive decay"; Practice Q7)*
- I can describe how the half-life changes as the reaction goes on for zero- and second-order reactions. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 3; Practice Q6)*

## Calculation

- I can make ln[A] and 1/[A] columns from data and use constant differences to find the order. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can find k, with the right sign and units, from the slope of the straight-line plot. *(Practice Q1, Q3, Q5)*
- I can use an integrated rate law to find a concentration at a given time, converting time units first. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can use t½ = 0.693/k, and count half-lives to find the amount left. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2)*
- I can find the time needed to reach a given concentration for any of the three orders. *(Practice Q4, Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can justify a claim about reaction order with values calculated from data, not only a description of the graph. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can evaluate a claim that k or the half-life changes during a reaction. *(Practice Q6, Q7)*
