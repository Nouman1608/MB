## Recap

- **VSEPR:** electron domains around an atom repel (Coulombic repulsion) and get as far apart as possible.
- **Domain count:** each lone pair = 1; each bonded atom = 1, whether the bond is single, double or triple.
- **Shape name** uses atom positions only. Lone pairs change the shape and squeeze angles.
- Repulsion: lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bond > bond–bond. Multiple bonds also push a little harder than single bonds.
- With **5 domains**, lone pairs go **equatorial**. With **6 domains**, a second lone pair goes **opposite** the first.
- **σ bond:** head-on overlap on the axis. **π bond:** side-on, above and below. π is weaker than σ and stops rotation, which allows *cis*/*trans* isomers.

## Key relationships

| Domains | Lone pairs | Shape | Angle | Hybridization |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0 | linear | 180° | sp |
| 3 | 0 / 1 | trigonal planar / bent | 120° / just under 120° | sp² |
| 4 | 0 / 1 / 2 | tetrahedral / trigonal pyramidal / bent | 109.5° / about 107° (NH₃) / 104.5° (H₂O) | sp³ |
| 5 | 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 | trigonal bipyramidal / seesaw / T-shaped / linear | 90°, 120°, 180° | shape only |
| 6 | 0 / 1 / 2 | octahedral / square pyramidal / square planar | 90°, 180° | shape only |

| Bond property | Rule |
|---|---|
| Higher bond order (same two elements) | shorter, stronger |
| Larger atoms | longer, usually weaker |
| C–C 347, C=C 614 kJ mol⁻¹ | π adds about 267 kJ mol⁻¹, less than the σ bond |

**Dipole moment = polar bonds + shape that does not cancel them.**

## Assumptions behind the model

- You start from the **best Lewis diagram**; resonance and expanded octets do not change the domain count.
- Angles quoted are **ideal**; real angles shift with lone pairs, multiple bonds and atom size.
- Hybridization labels are only used for 2–4 domains. How hybrid orbitals form, d-orbital hybridization and molecular orbital diagrams are outside the course.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Counting a double bond as two domains.
2. Naming the electron-domain geometry when the question asks for the molecular geometry.
3. Forgetting lone pairs on the atom when naming its hybridization.
4. Putting trigonal bipyramid lone pairs on the axis.
5. Saying a double bond is twice as strong as a single bond.
6. Assuming polar bonds always mean a polar molecule.

## Quick self-check

1. Shape and angle of SCl₂? *(4 domains, 2 lone pairs: bent, under 109.5°)*
2. σ and π bonds in HCN? *(2 σ, 2 π; carbon is sp, 180°)*
3. Shape of SF₆, and does it have a dipole moment? *(octahedral; no, the six bond dipoles cancel)*
