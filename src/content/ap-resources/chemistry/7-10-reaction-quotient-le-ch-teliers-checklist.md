## Understanding

- I can explain that a stress takes a system out of equilibrium by making Q different from K. *(Guide: "From a rule of thumb to a reason")*
- I can state which way a reaction goes when Q < K and when Q > K, and why. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can say which stresses change Q and which change K. *(Guide: stress table; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why heating a rigid flask leaves Q unchanged at that instant but changes K. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6(b))*

## Calculation

- I can calculate Q after a species is added or removed, and compare it with K. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7(a))*
- I can calculate Q for a mixture that is not at equilibrium, including terms with powers. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can find the new Q after a change in gas volume, using partial pressures or the factor rule. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can find the new Q after diluting a solution. *(Guide: factor rule; Practice Q3)*
- I can use one given concentration at a new equilibrium to find the others and check that Q = K. *(Practice Q7(b))*
- I can find a new equilibrium mixture when the equation gives a perfect square. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6(c))*

## Reasoning

- I can deduce the sign of ΔH from how K changes with temperature. *(Practice Q6(a))*
- I can explain why the new equilibrium concentrations differ from the old ones even though Q returns to the same K. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7(c))*
