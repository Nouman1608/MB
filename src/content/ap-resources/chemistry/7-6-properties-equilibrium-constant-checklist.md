## Understanding

- I can explain why a value of K belongs to one equation written one way, and why rewriting the equation does not change the mixture. *(Guide: "One reaction, many equations"; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain, from the expressions, why reversing an equation gives 1/K. *(Guide: Rule 1)*
- I can explain why multiplying the coefficients by c raises K to the power c, including c = ½. *(Guide: Rule 2; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why adding equations multiplies the K values, and why intermediates cancel. *(Guide: Rule 3; Practice Q6)*

## Calculation

- I can find K for a reversed, doubled, tripled or halved equation. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q2)*
- I can combine the K values of two or more steps to find K for an overall reaction. *(Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can plan a route to a target equation (multiply, add, reverse) and apply the matching change to K at each step. *(Guide: Worked example 2 and Figure 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can apply the same rules to Q, and check one result directly from concentrations. *(Guide: "The same rules apply to Q"; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can prove a manipulation by writing out and combining the expressions, showing which species cancel. *(Practice Q4, Q6)*
- I can decide whether a mixture is at equilibrium by comparing Q and K for the same version of the equation. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can spot and correct the errors "double K", "add the K values" and "make K negative". *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q1–Q3, Q7)*
