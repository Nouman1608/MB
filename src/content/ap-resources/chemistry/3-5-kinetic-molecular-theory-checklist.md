## Understanding

- I can state the five assumptions of the kinetic molecular theory for an ideal gas. *(Guide: "The assumptions of the model")*
- I can explain that all particles of matter, not only gas particles, are in continuous random motion. *(Guide: "From a law to a model")*
- I can explain gas pressure as the force of particle–wall collisions per unit area. *(Guide: "Pressure from collisions"; Practice Q1)*
- I can state that the average kinetic energy of the particles is proportional to the Kelvin temperature, and that it is the same for every gas at the same temperature. *(Guide; Practice Q5(b))*

## Calculation

- I can use KE = ½mv² to find the ratio of the average speeds of two gases at the same temperature. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3, Q7(a))*
- I can find the factor by which the average kinetic energy and the average speed change when a gas is heated, using kelvin. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4, Q6(b))*
- I can combine KMT with PV = nRT to find a new pressure after heating. *(Practice Q6(a))*

## Reasoning

- I can explain, with two separate effects on collisions, why heating a gas at constant volume raises its pressure. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6(c))*
- I can explain why compressing a gas at constant temperature raises the pressure without changing the average speed. *(Guide: "Pressure from collisions")*
- I can draw particle diagrams of a gas at two temperatures with the right features kept the same and changed. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6(d))*
- I can sketch and describe how a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution changes with temperature, keeping the area constant. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q2)*
- I can identify which speed distribution belongs to the lighter gas, and explain why kinetic-energy distributions at the same temperature are identical. *(Guide: Figure 3; Practice Q5, Q7(c))*
- I can evaluate a claim about partial pressures in a gas mixture using KMT. *(Practice Q7(b))*
