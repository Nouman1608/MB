Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/6-2-energy-diagrams-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/6-2-energy-diagrams-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can say what the vertical axis, the horizontal axis, the levels and the arrow on an energy diagram each represent. *(Guide: "What an energy diagram shows")*
- I can tell from the levels whether a process is endothermic or exothermic, and give the sign of ΔH. *(Guide: "Reading endothermic and exothermic"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why the position of zero on the energy axis does not change ΔH. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why a hump or a catalyst does not change whether a process is endothermic or exothermic. *(Guide: "Diagrams with a hump")*

## Skills

- I can calculate ΔH as the final level minus the starting level, with the correct sign. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can label an energy axis with a quantity and units, choose an even scale and place levels on it accurately. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can draw the diagram for the reverse of a process and give its ΔH. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can draw an energy diagram for a phase change, with gas above liquid above solid. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3)*
- I can draw a three-step diagram for dissolving and read the overall energy change from it. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can use a dissolving step diagram to predict whether the water warms or cools, and justify it. *(Practice Q7(b))*
- I can find and correct errors in someone else's energy diagram. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why two arrows of similar length on different diagrams may show very different energy changes. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
