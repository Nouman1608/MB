Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/3-4-ideal-gas-law-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/3-4-ideal-gas-law-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can name the four variables in PV = nRT and give a suitable unit for each. *(Guide: "What the ideal gas law says")*
- I can choose the value of R that matches my pressure and volume units. *(Guide; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why gas-law calculations need temperature in kelvin. *(Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can explain what a partial pressure is and why each gas in a mixture acts independently. *(Guide: "Mixtures of gases"; Practice Q7)*

## Calculation

- I can use PV = nRT to find the amount or mass of a gas. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can find new conditions after a change using P₁V₁ / T₁ = P₂V₂ / T₂. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2)*
- I can calculate mole fractions and partial pressures, and check that they add up. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3, Q7)*
- I can subtract the vapour pressure of water for a gas collected over water. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can find a molar mass from gas density and use it to identify a gas. *(Guide: "Molar mass from gas density"; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict whether a gas property doubles, halves or stays the same when another property changes. *(Guide: relationships table; Practice Q2)*
- I can identify which gas-law graphs are straight lines through the origin and which are curves. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can extrapolate a volume–temperature graph to absolute zero and explain the intercept. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can evaluate a claim about a gas mixture using partial pressures. *(Practice Q7)*
