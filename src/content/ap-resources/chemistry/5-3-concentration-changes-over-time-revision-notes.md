## Recap

- Measure one reactant, A, many times during **one** run of the reaction.
- The order in A shows in the **shape** of the data. Turn it into a straight line to read it.
- Make three columns: [A], ln[A], 1/[A]. With equal time steps, the column with **constant differences** is the straight one.
- The slope of that straight line gives the rate constant k.
- **Half-life** (t½) is the time for [A] to fall to half. It is constant only for first order.
- **Radioactive decay** is first order: each isotope has a fixed half-life.

## Key relationships

| Order | Integrated rate law | Straight plot | Slope | Units of k | Half-life |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | [A]ₜ − [A]₀ = −kt | [A] vs t | −k | M s⁻¹ | gets shorter |
| 1 | ln[A]ₜ − ln[A]₀ = −kt | ln[A] vs t | −k | s⁻¹ | constant: t½ = 0.693/k |
| 2 | 1/[A]ₜ − 1/[A]₀ = kt | 1/[A] vs t | +k | M⁻¹ s⁻¹ | gets longer |

Fraction left after n half-lives (first order): (½)ⁿ.

## Assumptions

- **Temperature is constant**, so k stays the same during the run.
- The rate depends only on the reactant you are watching; any other reactant is absent from the rate law or in large excess.
- The quantity you plot is proportional to concentration (for example absorbance, if Beer's law holds).
- The data cover a large enough fall in [A] to tell the plots apart.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **"Gone after two half-lives."** No: one quarter is left.
2. **Constant half-life for every order.** Only first order.
3. **Wrong sign.** Second-order slope is +k; k is always positive.
4. **log instead of ln.** Use the natural log.
5. **Mixed time units.** Match t to the units of k.
6. **Plotting the product** instead of the reactant.

## Quick self-check

1. A plot of ln[A] against time is straight. What is the order in A? *(first order)*
2. A first-order reaction has t½ = 45 s. What is k? *(0.693 ÷ 45 s = 0.0154 s⁻¹)*
3. What fraction of a first-order reactant is left after three half-lives? *(one eighth, 0.125)*
4. A ln[A] against time plot has slope −0.0350 min⁻¹. What is the half-life? *(0.693 ÷ 0.0350 min⁻¹ = 19.8 min)*
5. Which units of k mean second order? *(M⁻¹ s⁻¹)*
