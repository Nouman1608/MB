## Recap

- **Kinetics** is about how fast reactions go. The **rate** is the change in concentration (or amount) of a species per unit time.
- Reactants **disappear** and products **form**. Rates are always reported as positive numbers.
- The coefficients in the balanced equation fix the ratio of the rates of the species.
- Rates usually **fall** as the reaction goes on, because reactant concentrations fall.
- **Average rate** = slope of the line joining two points on a concentration–time graph. **Instantaneous rate** = slope of the tangent at one time. **Initial rate** = instantaneous rate at t = 0.
- A rate is measured by following something that changes: gas volume, mass, colour (absorbance), pressure, conductivity or pH.
- **Faster:** higher concentration (or gas pressure), higher temperature, larger surface area of a solid, a catalyst.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Meaning | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| rate of disappearance = −Δ[A]/Δt | A is a reactant; Δ[A] is negative | report a reactant's rate as a positive number |
| rate of formation = +Δ[C]/Δt | C is a product | report a product's rate |
| For a A + b B → c C: rate of reaction = −(1/a)Δ[A]/Δt = −(1/b)Δ[B]/Δt = (1/c)Δ[C]/Δt | one value for the whole reaction | convert the rate of one species into another |
| moles per second ÷ volume (L) = M s⁻¹ | convert an amount rate to a concentration rate | use gas or mass data for a solution |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The rates of different species are linked only through the **balanced equation**; there are no side reactions.
- The solution volume stays constant, so a change in moles gives a change in concentration directly.
- For mass-loss data, all the mass lost is the gas product (no splashing or evaporation of water).
- The temperature is constant during the measurement.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Forgetting the minus sign** for a reactant, giving a negative rate.
2. **Inverting a coefficient ratio.** In 2 A → 3 B, B forms 3/2 as fast as A disappears, not 2/3.
3. **Mixing "rate of A" with "rate of reaction".** Read the question.
4. **Treating the rate as constant.** An average rate belongs to one interval.
5. **Saying more solid raises its concentration.** For solids, surface area matters.
6. **Explaining temperature only by more collisions.** Say that more collisions have enough energy.

## Quick self-check

1. For 2 SO₂ + O₂ → 2 SO₃, SO₃ forms at 0.040 M s⁻¹. How fast is O₂ used, and what is the rate of reaction? *(0.020 M s⁻¹; 0.020 M s⁻¹)*
2. [A] falls from 0.500 M to 0.350 M in 25 s. What is the average rate of disappearance of A? *(0.0060 M s⁻¹)*
3. Why does powdered zinc react with acid faster than one lump of the same mass? *(More surface is exposed, so acid particles collide with zinc atoms more often.)*
