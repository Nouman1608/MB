Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/1-6-photoelectron-spectroscopy-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/1-6-photoelectron-spectroscopy-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can describe what photoelectron spectroscopy measures and why it removes electrons from every subshell, not only the outer one. *(Guide: "What photoelectron spectroscopy measures")*
- I can say what the position and the height of a peak each tell me. *(Guide: "How to read a spectrum"; Practice Q1)*
- I can read a spectrum whose binding energy axis increases to the left on a log scale. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
- I can explain why the lowest-energy peak estimates the first ionization energy, and why the other peaks are not successive ionization energies. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q5)*

## Skills

- I can turn a spectrum into a ground-state electron configuration and identify the element. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can convert raw peak heights into numbers of electrons, using the 1s peak to set the scale. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can predict the spectrum of a cation or anion from its configuration. *(Guide: "Atoms, ions and how spectra change"; Practice Q3)*
- I can convert a binding energy between MJ mol⁻¹ and kJ mol⁻¹. *(Practice Q4, Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can use Coulomb's law, distance and shielding to explain why core peaks are at much higher binding energy than valence peaks. *(Guide: "Explaining peak positions"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why the same subshell is at higher binding energy in an element with more protons. *(Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can compare the spectra of isoelectronic species and rank their peak positions. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*
- I can judge whether a model is consistent with a spectrum by citing the number, heights or positions of peaks. *(Guide: "Is the model consistent with the data?"; Practice Q6)*
