Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/2-5-lewis-diagrams-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/2-5-lewis-diagrams-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain what the lines and the dots in a Lewis diagram stand for, and why core electrons are not drawn. *(Guide: "What a Lewis diagram shows")*
- I can explain why hydrogen and fluorine are always outer atoms. *(Guide: "A step-by-step method")*
- I can state which atoms can end with fewer than 8 electrons and which can end with more, and why period 2 atoms cannot exceed 8. *(Guide: "When the central atom does not end with eight"; Practice Q2, Q7)*
- I can explain why one molecular formula can have more than one valid skeleton. *(Guide: "One formula, more than one skeleton")*

## Skills

- I can count the total valence electrons in a molecule or polyatomic ion, adding or subtracting electrons for the charge. *(Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can choose the central atom using electronegativity. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
- I can draw a Lewis diagram that needs a double or triple bond. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can draw a Lewis diagram for a polyatomic ion with brackets and its charge. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can place leftover electrons on a period 3 or larger central atom to give an expanded octet. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q3, Q7)*
- I can use "needed minus available" to predict the number of bonds in an octet molecule. *(Guide: "A quick check for multiple bonds"; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can find the error in someone else's Lewis diagram by recounting the total and each atom's electrons. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can justify why a proposed Lewis diagram is impossible, using the period of the central atom. *(Practice Q7)*
