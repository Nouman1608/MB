Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/8-4-acid-base-reactions-buffers-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/8-4-acid-base-reactions-buffers-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why a strong acid or strong base reacts completely with any base or acid it meets, and write the net ionic equation. *(Guide: "React first"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why neutralising a weak acid gives pH above 7 and neutralising a weak base gives pH below 7. *(Guide: Worked examples 2(b) and 3(b); Practice Q2, Q6(b))*
- I can say what a buffer contains and decide whether a mixture is one. *(Guide: "What makes a buffer"; Practice Q3, Q7)*
- I can write the equilibrium for a weak acid mixed with a weak base and explain why it does not go to completion. *(Guide: "Weak acid with weak base")*

## Calculation

- I can find the pH when a strong acid or strong base is in excess, using the total volume. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q6(c))*
- I can use a before/after mole table to find what is left after mixing. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can calculate the pH of a buffer made by partial neutralisation, with the ratio the right way up. *(Guide: Worked example 2(a); Practice Q5, Q7(c))*
- I can calculate the pH at equal moles using K_b of A⁻ or K_a of HB⁺. *(Guide: Worked example 3(b); Practice Q6(a), Q7(b))*
- I can calculate K for a weak acid with a weak base from two pK_a values. *(Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can evaluate a claim that "equal moles means neutral". *(Practice Q6(b))*
- I can choose the amount of strong base needed to make a buffer at a chosen pH, and explain why a strong acid cannot do the job. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can predict which side a weak acid and weak base equilibrium favours by comparing acid strengths. *(Practice Q4)*
