## Recap

- A particle diagram of a solution shows a small volume of it. It must get two things right: the **relative numbers** of particles (concentration) and the **interactions** between them.
- **Ionic solutes** are drawn as separate ions in the formula ratio. **Polyatomic ions** (SO₄²⁻, NO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻, NH₄⁺) stay whole.
- **Molecular solutes** (glucose, ethanol, methanol) stay as whole molecules.
- Water is polar: O is δ−, H is δ+. Ion–dipole attractions make water point **O towards cations** and **H towards anions**.
- Solutes with O–H or N–H groups form **hydrogen bonds** with water; nonpolar solutes in nonpolar solvents interact by **London dispersion** forces only.
- Use labels, a key or distinct shapes, never colour alone.

## Key relationships

| Idea | How it shows in a diagram |
|---|---|
| Ion ratio | Counts follow the formula, e.g. Ca(NO₃)₂ → 1 Ca²⁺ : 2 NO₃⁻ |
| Charge balance | Total + charge = total − charge in the box |
| Concentration | Particles ÷ volume represented |
| Dilution | Same number of solute particles, larger volume |
| Moles from a diagram | n = (number drawn) × (moles each particle stands for); then M = n ÷ V |

## Assumptions behind the pictures

- Only a few water molecules are drawn; the real solution has far more.
- The solution is homogeneous, so the box is a fair sample of the whole.
- Ionic solutes are assumed to dissolve completely into separate ions.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Ion pairs or lattice pieces** in solution.
2. **Broken polyatomic ions**, such as separate S and O atoms for sulfate.
3. **Molecular solutes drawn as ions.**
4. **Water reversed** around an ion.
5. **Wrong ratio** or charges that do not balance.
6. **Comparing particle counts in boxes of different volumes** without dividing by volume.
7. **Spending time on molality or percent by mass.** They are not assessed in calculations for this course.

## Quick self-check

1. In a diagram of Na₃PO₄(aq), what is the ratio of Na⁺ to PO₄³⁻? *(3 : 1; the PO₄³⁻ ion stays whole.)*
2. Which atom of water faces a Mg²⁺ ion? *(Oxygen.)*
3. Box X (0.10 L) shows 5 solute particles; box Y (0.20 L) shows 8 particles of the same solute. Which is more concentrated? *(X: 50 per L against 40 per L.)*
4. A box of ethanol solution is drawn with C₂H₅O⁻ and H⁺ ions. What is wrong? *(Ethanol is molecular; it dissolves as whole C₂H₅OH molecules.)*
