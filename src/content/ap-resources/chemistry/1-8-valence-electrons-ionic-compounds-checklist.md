Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/1-8-valence-electrons-ionic-compounds-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/1-8-valence-electrons-ionic-compounds-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can state the number of valence electrons of any main-group element from its group number. *(Guide: "Why valence electrons control chemistry")*
- I can explain how the attraction between nuclei and valence electrons decides whether two elements form an ionic bond, share electrons or do not bond. *(Guide; Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why elements in the same group form analogous compounds. *(Guide: "Same group, same kind of compound"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain, without saying "atoms want a full shell", why Mg forms Mg²⁺ and not Mg³⁺. *(Guide: Figure 2 and Worked example 2)*

## Skills

- I can predict the typical ion charge for groups 1, 2, 13, 15, 16 and 17. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can write an ionic formula as the smallest whole-number ratio and check that the charges add to zero. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can predict a formula from an analogous compound, such as Rb₂Se from Li₂O. *(Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can find the number of valence electrons from successive ionization energies and use it to write formulas. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can combine an analogous formula with a molar-mass calculation to identify an unknown metal. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can write the equation for an element's reaction by analogy with a group partner, such as K or Ba with water. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why group 1 and group 2 metals become more reactive down the group, using distance and shielding. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why halogens become less reactive down the group. *(Guide: "Reactivity and periodicity"; Practice Q3)*
- I can correct the claim that more protons means stronger attraction for valence electrons. *(Practice Q6(c))*
- I can link an observation, such as how violently a metal reacts with water, to a particle-level model. *(Guide: Worked example 3)*
