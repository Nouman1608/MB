Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/9-11-electrolysis-faradays-law-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/9-11-electrolysis-faradays-law-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why the amount of product in a cell depends on the number of electrons that pass. *(Guide: "Counting electrons with an ammeter and a clock"; Practice Q1)*
- I can state what the Faraday constant represents and why it works like a molar mass for charge. *(Guide: "The Faraday constant"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why cells joined in series pass the same charge, and why both electrodes of one cell share the same moles of electrons. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q2)*
- I can say which changes alter the amount of product (current, time, ion charge) and which do not (concentration, electrode size). *(Guide: "What changes the amount of product"; Practice Q3)*

## Calculation

- I can use I = q / t to find charge, time or current, with time in seconds. *(Practice Q1, Q4(a))*
- I can convert charge into moles of electrons with F, and back again. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can use a half-reaction to link moles of electrons to moles of metal deposited or removed. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can work backwards from a target mass to the time or current needed. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4(a))*
- I can find the charge on a metal ion from the mass deposited by a measured charge. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q5)*
- I can find the moles and volumes of gases formed at electrodes, using 2 e⁻ per H₂ or Cl₂ and 4 e⁻ per O₂. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can apply the same calculation to the anode of a galvanic cell. *(Guide: "The same counting works in a galvanic cell"; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can evaluate a claim about electrode mass changes by separating equal moles of electrons from unequal masses. *(Practice Q6(c))*
- I can explain why a measured mass may be smaller than the Faraday calculation predicts. *(Guide: "What changes the amount of product"; Practice Q6(d))*
- I can explain a gas volume ratio from the electron counts in the two half-reactions. *(Practice Q7(d))*
