## Recap

- In a redox reaction, electrons pass from the species that is **oxidized** (loses e⁻, oxidation number rises) to the species that is **reduced** (gains e⁻, oxidation number falls).
- A **half-reaction** shows just one of these processes, with the electrons written in.
- A balanced redox equation is built by balancing each half-reaction, making the electrons equal, and adding.
- The coefficients of the balanced equation are mole ratios for stoichiometry and redox titrations.

## Key relationships

| Idea | What it looks like | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Oxidation half-reaction | Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ (e⁻ on the right) | show electrons lost |
| Reduction half-reaction | Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (e⁻ on the left) | show electrons gained |
| Balancing order (acid) | atoms other than O and H → H₂O for O → H⁺ for H → e⁻ for charge | balance any half-reaction in acid |
| Electron count | electrons = change in oxidation number × number of atoms that change | check step 5 |
| Combining | multiply to the lowest common multiple of the electrons, then add | cancel the electrons |
| Basic solution (extension) | balance as in acid, then add OH⁻ to both sides for each H⁺ | turn H⁺ into H₂O |

## Assumptions behind the method

- The reaction is in **aqueous solution**, so H₂O is always available, plus H⁺ (acidic) or OH⁻ (basic).
- You know (or are told) the main reactants and products; the method balances them but does not predict them.
- Spectator ions are left out, so the result is a **net ionic equation**.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Checking atoms only.** Always check total charge on each side too.
2. **Electrons left over.** If e⁻ is in the final equation, the half-reactions were not multiplied correctly.
3. **Electrons on the wrong side.** Confirm with the oxidation-number change.
4. **Adding electron numbers.** A 3e⁻ and a 2e⁻ half-reaction combine with 6e⁻ transferred, not 5.
5. **Not cancelling H⁺ and H₂O** that appear on both sides after adding.
6. **Charges "made to equal zero".** The two sides must have equal charge, which need not be zero.
7. **Using O₂ or O²⁻ to balance oxygen.** Use H₂O.

## Quick self-check

1. Balance: Fe(s) + Ag⁺(aq) → Fe²⁺(aq) + Ag(s). *(Fe + 2Ag⁺ → Fe²⁺ + 2Ag)*
2. Write the oxidation half-reaction for magnesium metal forming Mg²⁺. *(Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻)*
3. Balance: Mg(s) + Au³⁺(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + Au(s). How many electrons are transferred? *(3Mg + 2Au³⁺ → 3Mg²⁺ + 2Au; 6 electrons)*
4. Balance the half-reaction NO₃⁻ → NO in acidic solution. *(NO₃⁻ + 4H⁺ + 3e⁻ → NO + 2H₂O)*
