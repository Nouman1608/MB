## Understanding

- I can state the standard states that the ° in ΔG° refers to: pure solids and liquids, 1 M solutions and gases at 1 bar. *(Guide: "What ΔG° means")*
- I can say what ΔG° < 0 and ΔG° > 0 mean for a process, using the phrase "thermodynamically favoured". *(Guide; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why "thermodynamically favoured" is preferred to "spontaneous", and why ΔG° says nothing about rate. *(Guide: "What ΔG° means"; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why an element in its standard state has ΔG°f = 0. *(Guide: "Method 1")*

## Calculation

- I can calculate ΔG° from free energies of formation, using coefficients and products minus reactants. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can calculate ΔG° from ΔH° and ΔS° at a given temperature, converting J to kJ and °C to K. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can calculate the temperature at which ΔG° = 0 and say on which side the process is favoured. *(Guide: Worked example 2(c); Practice Q5(b), Q7(b))*
- I can check a ΔG° answer for a unit slip by judging its size. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2)*

## Reasoning

- I can state whether a process is favoured at all, high, low or no temperatures from the signs of ΔH° and ΔS° alone. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can justify the sign of ΔS° by describing what happens to the particles. *(Practice Q6(a), Q7(a))*
- I can explain why freezing water and dissolving sodium nitrate need both enthalpy and entropy to predict the outcome. *(Guide: "When enthalpy and entropy pull in opposite directions"; Practice Q4)*
- I can evaluate a claim such as "exothermic means favoured" with a calculation and a reason. *(Practice Q7(c))*
- I can decide whether enthalpy or entropy drives a favoured process. *(Practice Q6(d))*
