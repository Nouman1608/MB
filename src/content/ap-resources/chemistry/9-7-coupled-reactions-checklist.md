Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/9-7-coupled-reactions-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/9-7-coupled-reactions-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why a process with ΔG° > 0 can still be made to happen if free energy is supplied. *(Guide: "Unfavourable does not mean impossible")*
- I can describe how electrical energy drives electrolysis and battery charging, and how light drives photosynthesis. *(Guide: "Driving a process with an outside energy source"; Practice Q3)*
- I can tell the difference between supplying activation energy (a spark, a catalyst) and supplying the free energy an unfavourable process needs. *(Practice Q1, Q3)*
- I can explain what a common intermediate is and why coupled reactions must share one. *(Guide: "Coupled reactions and the common intermediate"; Practice Q1, Q7)*
- I can describe how cells use ATP hydrolysis to drive unfavourable reactions. *(Guide: "Coupling in living things: ATP")*

## Calculation

- I can reverse or scale an equation so that the intermediate cancels, changing ΔG° correctly. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can add coupled reactions and their ΔG° values to find the overall reaction and its ΔG°. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can find how many ATP must be hydrolysed to make an overall process favourable. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can multiply equilibrium constants of coupled steps and convert between K and ΔG°. *(Guide: "What happens to K?"; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can estimate the minimum light energy needed to drive a reaction. *(Guide: Worked example 3)*

## Reasoning

- I can judge whether a proposed coupling will work, using the sign of the overall ΔG°. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why a favourable reaction in a separate container does not drive an unfavourable one. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why a negative overall ΔG° does not mean the reaction is fast. *(Guide: Common misconceptions)*
