## Recap

- **Chemical bonds** (covalent, ionic, metallic) hold atoms together inside particles or lattices. They are strong.
- **Intermolecular attractions** (London dispersion, dipole–dipole, hydrogen bonding, ion–dipole) act between separate particles. They are much weaker.
- A **chemical process** breaks and/or forms chemical bonds, so new substances appear.
- A **physical process** changes only intermolecular attractions. Phase changes, dissolving a molecular solid and separating mixtures are physical.
- Quick test: compare the formulas before and after. A new formula means atoms were rearranged, so the change is chemical. The same formula in a new state or spacing means a physical change.

## Key relationships

| Process | What changes | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| Melting, boiling, subliming, condensing | Attractions between molecules | Physical |
| Dissolving sugar | Sucrose–sucrose hydrogen bonds replaced by sucrose–water ones | Physical |
| Electrolysis of water; metal + acid; burning | Bonds broken and new bonds formed | Chemical |
| Dissolving HCl gas | H–Cl bond breaks, H₃O⁺ forms | Usually chemical |
| Dissolving NaCl | Ionic bonds break, ion–dipole attractions form | Either, with reasoning |

**Energy scale (water):** about 41 kJ mol⁻¹ to boil, about 920 kJ mol⁻¹ to break both O–H bonds. More than 20 times as much.

## Assumptions behind the classification

- The labels "physical" and "chemical" describe **typical** cases. Some processes, such as dissolving a salt, sit on the boundary.
- An observation is interpreted through a particle-level model. The model, not the observation alone, decides.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Saying bubbles in boiling water are H₂ and O₂.
2. Treating any gas or colour change as proof of a reaction.
3. Using reversibility as the test.
4. Saying melting a molecular solid breaks covalent bonds.
5. Giving a label without a bond-level reason.
6. Making a claim from one observation when the data offer several.

## Quick self-check

1. Liquid nitrogen boils away in an open flask. Physical or chemical? *(Physical: N₂ molecules stay intact; only London forces are overcome.)*
2. Which attraction forms when KBr dissolves in water? *(Ion–dipole attractions between K⁺ or Br⁻ and water molecules.)*
3. A heated solid loses mass and gives a gas that turns limewater cloudy. What kind of change? *(Chemical: CO₂ is a new substance made by breaking and forming bonds.)*
