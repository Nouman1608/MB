## Recap

- Reactions are followed over time by spectrophotometry (absorbance ∝ concentration), gas pressure or volume, conductivity or pH, or sampling and titration.
- The **initial rate** is the slope of the concentration–time curve at t = 0, when the concentrations are known exactly.
- A **rate law** gives the rate as k multiplied by each reactant concentration raised to a power: rate = k[A]ᵐ[B]ⁿ.
- Each power is the **order** with respect to that reactant. Their sum is the **overall order**.
- Orders are found **by experiment**. They are not read from the coefficients of an overall equation.
- The **rate constant k** is the same for every experiment at one temperature. It changes when the temperature changes.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Meaning | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| rate = k[A]ᵐ[B]ⁿ | rate law | calculate a rate, or k from one experiment |
| rate ratio = (concentration ratio)ᵐ | one concentration changes, all else fixed | find the order in that reactant |
| order = log(rate ratio) ÷ log(conc. ratio) | same idea with awkward factors | find an order from any ratio |
| units of k = M¹⁻ⁿ s⁻¹ | n = overall order | 0: M s⁻¹; 1: s⁻¹; 2: M⁻¹ s⁻¹; 3: M⁻² s⁻¹ |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- All experiments are at the **same temperature**, so k is the same in each.
- The rates are **initial rates**, so products have not built up and the starting concentrations apply.
- For spectrophotometry, absorbance is proportional to concentration (same wavelength and path length).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Using coefficients as orders.**
2. **Doubling for second order.** Second order: × 4 when the concentration doubles.
3. **Crediting a reactant with a rate change caused by another.** Remove known effects first.
4. **Wrong or missing units for k.** Work them out from the overall order.
5. **Saying k changed** because the rate changed with concentration.
6. **Dropping a zero-order reactant from the reaction** as if it were not used up.

## Quick self-check

1. For rate = k[A][B]², what happens to the rate if [B] triples? If [A] halves and [B] doubles? *(× 9; × 2)*
2. What are the units of k for an overall second-order reaction? *(M⁻¹ s⁻¹)*
3. A first-order reaction has a rate of 8.0 × 10⁻³ M s⁻¹ when [A] = 0.20 M. What is k? *(0.040 s⁻¹)*
