Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/4-3-representations-reactions-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/4-3-representations-reactions-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why atoms and charge are conserved in a particle diagram but the number of molecules need not be. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7)*
- I can list the rules a consistent particle diagram follows, including a key that does not rely on colour. *(Guide: "The rules a consistent diagram follows")*
- I can explain why a soluble ionic compound is drawn as separate ions while a molecular solute stays as whole molecules. *(Guide: "Representing reactions in solution"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why a phase change keeps the same molecules in both boxes. *(Guide: "Representing physical processes"; Practice Q5)*

## Skills

- I can predict the contents of an "after" box from a balanced equation and a "before" box, including any leftover reactant. *(Practice Q1, Q6)*
- I can write a balanced equation from a before-and-after diagram by removing leftovers and reducing the ratio. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can draw the ions in a solution in their correct formula ratio, keeping polyatomic ions whole. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can draw a precipitation reaction with the solid as a packed cluster and spectator ions separate in solution. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can show the states of products correctly in a diagram: packed, close or far apart. *(Practice Q5, Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can use atom counts to show that a particle diagram is wrong, and correct it. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can check a solution diagram by showing the charges add up to zero. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can evaluate a claim about mass or particle numbers using conservation of atoms. *(Practice Q7)*
