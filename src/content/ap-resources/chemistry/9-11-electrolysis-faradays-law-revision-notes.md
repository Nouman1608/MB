## Recap

- In any electrochemical cell, the amount of substance oxidised or reduced is fixed by the **number of electrons** that pass.
- You measure electrons indirectly: **current** (amperes) and **time** (seconds) give the **charge** (coulombs).
- The **Faraday constant**, F = 96,485 C mol⁻¹, is the charge on one mole of electrons. It turns coulombs into moles of electrons.
- The **half-reaction** gives the electrons needed per particle. For a metal ion, this is the size of its charge.
- The same counting works for metal **deposited** at a cathode, metal **removed** from an anode, **gas** formed, and the anode used up in a **galvanic** cell.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| I = q / t | I in A, q in C, t in s | find charge (q = I × t), time (t = q / I) or current |
| n(e⁻) = q / F | F = 96,485 C mol⁻¹ | turn charge into moles of electrons (or q = n(e⁻) × F) |
| n(substance) = n(e⁻) ÷ electrons per particle | from the half-reaction | link electrons to metal or gas |
| m = n × M | M in g mol⁻¹ | turn moles of product into mass |
| electrons per ion = n(e⁻) ÷ n(metal) | from measured mass and charge | find the charge on an unknown metal ion |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- The **current is steady** for the whole time (or you use the average current).
- **All** the charge goes into the reaction you are calculating: no side reactions such as hydrogen forming at the cathode. The answer is therefore a maximum.
- The solution does **not run out** of the ion being reduced.
- Cells joined **in series** share one path, so they pass the same charge.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Time in minutes or hours.** Convert to seconds before q = I × t.
2. **Treating coulombs as moles.** Divide by F.
3. **Ignoring the ion's charge.** Cu²⁺ needs 2 mol e⁻ per mol Cu; Au³⁺ needs 3.
4. **Gas electron counts.** 2 e⁻ per Cl₂ or H₂, but 4 e⁻ per O₂ from water.
5. **Equal masses at both electrodes.** Equal moles of electrons, not equal masses.
6. **Thinking concentration or electrode size changes the yield.** For a fixed charge, they do not.

## Quick self-check

1. How much charge passes when 1.00 A flows for 1.00 h, and how many moles of electrons is that? *(3600 C; 0.0373 mol)*
2. How many moles of electrons deposit 1 mol of aluminium from Al³⁺? *(3 mol)*
3. What mass of copper does 0.100 mol of electrons deposit from Cu²⁺? *(0.0500 mol Cu, so 3.18 g)*
