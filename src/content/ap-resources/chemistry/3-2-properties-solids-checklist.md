Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/3-2-properties-solids-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/chemistry/3-2-properties-solids-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can explain why vapor pressure and boiling point depend directly on the strength of interparticle forces. *(Guide: "Boiling point and vapor pressure"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why melting point is a less reliable guide, because melting only rearranges particles. *(Guide: "Melting point is more subtle"; Practice Q7)*
- I can describe the particles and the interactions in ionic, covalent network, molecular and metallic solids. *(Guide: Figure 1 and summary table)*
- I can explain how noncovalent interactions set the shape, and so the properties, of large biomolecules and polymers. *(Guide: "Large molecules and polymers"; Practice Q6)*

## Skills

- I can draw a particulate model that shows several interacting particles and separates covalent bonds from intermolecular forces. *(Guide: "Drawing interacting particles"; Practice Q5)*
- I can classify an unknown solid from melting point, conductivity, solubility and mechanical behaviour. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why ionic solids are brittle and conduct only when molten or dissolved. *(Guide: "Ionic solids")*
- I can explain why diamond is hard but graphite is soft. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain conductivity, malleability and ductility in metals, and how an interstitial alloy changes them. *(Guide: "Metallic solids"; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain a large difference in melting point by identifying the type of solid, as for SiO₂ and CO₂. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can evaluate a claim that uses melting points as evidence, and use boiling points instead. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7)*
