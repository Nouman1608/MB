## Recap

- **Absolute entropy** is measured from a true zero: a perfect crystal at 0 K. So every substance at 298 K has a positive entropy.
- **Standard molar entropy, S°,** is the absolute entropy of 1 mol of a substance at 1 bar, usually listed at 298 K.
- Gases have much larger S° values than liquids and solids. For one substance, S°(g) > S°(l) > S°(s).
- The entropy change of any process, chemical or physical, comes from the S° values of the species before and after it.

## Key relationships

| Relationship | Symbols and units | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| ΔS° = ΣnS°(products) − ΣnS°(reactants) | S° in J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹; n = coefficients | calculate ΔS° for a reaction or phase change |
| ΔS° (kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹) = ΔS° (J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹) ÷ 1000 | — | prepare ΔS° for combining with ΔH° (Topic 9.3) |
| ΔS for a sample = moles of reaction × ΔS° | result in J K⁻¹ | scale to a real amount |
| Reverse the equation → ΔS° changes sign; double it → ΔS° doubles | — | adapt a given value |

## Assumptions behind the numbers

- Values are for **standard conditions**: 1 bar and the listed temperature (usually 298 K).
- Each species is in the **state shown** in the equation. Changing a state symbol changes the S° value.
- ΔS° is **per mole of reaction** for the equation exactly as written.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Setting S° = 0 for elements** such as O₂(g) or H₂(g). Only ΔH°f is zero for elements.
2. **Forgetting coefficients.** Multiply every S° by its coefficient before adding.
3. **Reactants minus products.** It is always products minus reactants.
4. **Wrong state symbol.** H₂O(l) is 70.0; H₂O(g) is 188.8 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.
5. **J vs kJ.** S° is in joules; divide by 1000 before mixing with ΔH° in kJ.
6. **Skipping the sign check.** If the sign disagrees with the change in moles of gas, recheck.
7. **Accepting a negative S°** when working backwards. That means a sign slip.

## Quick self-check

Use S°: CH₃OH(l) 126.8, O₂(g) 205.2, CO₂(g) 213.8, H₂O(l) 70.0 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

1. ΔS° for 2 CH₃OH(l) + 3 O₂(g) → 2 CO₂(g) + 4 H₂O(l)? *(−161.6 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹)*
2. The same value in kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹? *(−0.1616 kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹)*
3. ΔS° for CO(g) + 2 H₂(g) → CH₃OH(l) is −332.3 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. What is ΔS° for 2 CH₃OH(l) → 2 CO(g) + 4 H₂(g)? *(+664.6 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹: reversed and doubled)*
