## Understanding

- I can define electronegativity and say how it differs from electron affinity. *(Guide: "Electronegativity and its trends")*
- I can explain why electronegativity increases across a period and decreases down a group, using shielding, distance and Coulomb's law. *(Guide; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why C–H bonds are treated as nonpolar even though carbon is slightly more electronegative. *(Guide: "Nonpolar covalent bonds")*
- I can describe metallic bonding as cations surrounded by delocalized valence electrons. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why ionic and covalent bonding form a continuum, and why every polar bond has some ionic character. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q7)*

## Skills

- I can put δ+ and δ− on the correct atoms of a polar bond, including unusual cases such as O–F. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can rank single bonds by bond dipole from electronegativity values or from periodic-table position. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can classify a bond as nonpolar covalent, polar covalent, ionic or metallic. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q4)*
- I can use the metal/nonmetal rule of thumb alongside the electronegativity difference. *(Guide: "Ionic bonds: more than a number")*

## Reasoning

- I can make a claim about the bonding in an unknown solid from its melting point, conductivity and response to hammering, and justify it. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why a fixed cut-off value of electronegativity difference cannot decide bond type. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can explain at the particle level why a metal conducts electricity as a solid. *(Practice Q6(c))*
