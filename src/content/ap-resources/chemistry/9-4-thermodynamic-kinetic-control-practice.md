The mark points are a suggested Marlbridge rubric to help you check your work; they are not an official scoring guideline. Data: ΔG°f at 298 K: CO₂(g) −394.39, H₂O(l) −237.14, H₂O(g) −228.58, C₂H₅OH(l) −174.8, NO(g) +86.60 kJ mol⁻¹; elements in their standard states 0. Reaction J → L and compound W use invented data, made up for practice.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · foundation)

Which observation gives the best evidence that a process is under kinetic control?

- (A) A reaction with ΔG° > 0 produces no detectable product, even with a catalyst present.
- (B) A mixture with ΔG° < 0 shows no change for months, then reacts quickly once a small amount of catalyst is added.
- (C) A reaction with ΔG° < 0 is complete within a few seconds at room temperature.
- (D) In a sealed flask, the concentrations of reactants and products stay constant while both forward and reverse reactions continue.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (B).** The process is favoured (ΔG° < 0) but was not happening at a measurable rate. The catalyst lowered the activation energy and the reaction then went, which shows the obstacle was kinetic.

- (A) describes a process that is **not favoured**. A catalyst cannot make it give much product.
- (C) is favoured **and** fast, so the rate is not limiting anything.
- (D) describes dynamic **equilibrium** (Q = K, equal rates), not kinetic control.
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · foundation)

A catalyst is added to a reaction that is under kinetic control. Which row correctly describes the effect on the activation energy, Eₐ, and on ΔG°?

- (A) Eₐ decreases; ΔG° does not change.
- (B) Eₐ decreases; ΔG° becomes more negative.
- (C) Eₐ does not change; ΔG° becomes more negative.
- (D) Eₐ increases; ΔG° does not change.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** A catalyst provides a new pathway with a lower barrier. ΔG° depends only on the free energies of the reactants and products, which the catalyst does not change.

- (B) and (C) wrongly claim the catalyst changes the thermodynamics.
- (D) has Eₐ moving the wrong way; a higher barrier would slow the reaction further.
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · core)

An open dish of ethanol stands in air at 25 °C. Using the data at the top of the page, which statement about C₂H₅OH(l) + 3O₂(g) → 2CO₂(g) + 3H₂O(l) is correct?

- (A) ΔG° = −1325.4 kJ mol⁻¹; the reaction is favoured, and the ethanol does not burn because the activation energy is high.
- (B) ΔG° = +1325.4 kJ mol⁻¹; the ethanol does not burn because the reaction is not favoured.
- (C) ΔG° = −456.7 kJ mol⁻¹; the reaction is favoured and fast, so the ethanol is burning slowly without a flame.
- (D) ΔG° = −1325.4 kJ mol⁻¹; the ethanol and oxygen are already at equilibrium, so no reaction is seen.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** ΔG° = 2(−394.39) + 3(−237.14) − (−174.8) − 3(0) = −788.78 − 711.42 + 174.8 = −1325.4 kJ mol⁻¹. The reaction is strongly favoured, but it does not happen at room temperature without a flame, so it is under kinetic control.

- (B) subtracts the wrong way round (reactants minus products).
- (C) ignores the coefficients: −394.39 − 237.14 + 174.8 = −456.7 kJ mol⁻¹. It also invents a slow, flameless burning that is not observed.
- (D) has the right ΔG° but the wrong conclusion. With ΔG° so negative, K is enormous. At equilibrium almost all the ethanol would have become CO₂ and H₂O, but the ethanol is still there, so Q ≪ K and the system is far from equilibrium.
</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · core)

A sealed steel cylinder holds a mixture of H₂(g) and O₂(g) at 298 K. After a year, no water can be detected. For 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(g), ΔG° = −457.2 kJ mol⁻¹. Which statement is correct?

- (A) The mixture is at equilibrium, because the amounts are not changing.
- (B) K for the reaction must be very small, because so little water forms.
- (C) Q is much smaller than K, and the rate of reaction is too low to measure.
- (D) ΔG° becomes positive inside a sealed cylinder, so no reaction occurs.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (C).** A large negative ΔG° means K is very large. With essentially no water, Q is close to zero, so Q ≪ K and the system is far from equilibrium. A high activation energy keeps the rate negligible: the mixture is under kinetic control.

- (A) equates "no change" with equilibrium; at equilibrium Q = K, which is not the case here.
- (B) contradicts the data: a very negative ΔG° means a very large K.
- (D) is false; a sealed container does not change the sign of ΔG°.
</details>

## Question 5 (calculation · core)

For an invented reaction J → L, ΔH° = −110 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔG° = −95 kJ mol⁻¹ at 298 K. Without a catalyst, the forward activation energy is 160 kJ mol⁻¹. With a catalyst it is 62 kJ mol⁻¹. At 298 K, no L forms without the catalyst.

(a) State whether J → L is thermodynamically favoured, with a reason.
(b) Calculate the activation energy of the reverse reaction, L → J, with and without the catalyst. (Use enthalpy values, as on an enthalpy profile.)
(c) Explain why the uncatalysed reaction is under kinetic control.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution</summary>

**(a)** ΔG° = −95 kJ mol⁻¹ < 0, so J → L is **favoured**.

**(b)** On an enthalpy profile, the products sit 110 kJ mol⁻¹ below the reactants, so the reverse barrier is the forward barrier plus 110 kJ mol⁻¹.
Uncatalysed: Eₐ(reverse) = 160 + 110 = **270 kJ mol⁻¹**.
Catalysed: Eₐ(reverse) = 62 + 110 = **172 kJ mol⁻¹**.
The catalyst lowers both barriers by the same amount, 98 kJ mol⁻¹.

**(c)** The reaction is favoured but no L forms, so the obstacle is kinetic. With Eₐ = 160 kJ mol⁻¹, almost no J particles have enough energy at 298 K to reach the transition state, so the rate is negligible.

Suggested mark points (4): 1 for "favoured because ΔG° < 0"; 1 for 270 kJ mol⁻¹; 1 for 172 kJ mol⁻¹; 1 for linking the high Eₐ to too few successful collisions while ΔG° < 0.
</details>

## Question 6 (constructed response · core)

Compound W is a white solid that can decompose: W(s) → R(s) + Y(g). For this reaction ΔH° = −35.0 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS° = +120 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. A sample of W kept in a closed jar at 25 °C for a year shows no change.

(a) Calculate ΔG° at 298 K.
(b) Explain why the decomposition is favoured at every temperature, without further calculation.
(c) A student claims: "Because nothing has changed in a year, the jar must be at equilibrium." Evaluate the claim.
(d) Describe an experiment that would support your answer to (c), and state the expected result.

<details>
<summary>Worked solution and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**(a)** ΔG° = −35.0 − 298(0.120) = −35.0 − 35.76 = **−70.8 kJ mol⁻¹**.

**(b)** ΔH° < 0 and ΔS° > 0. Both ΔH° and −TΔS° are negative, so ΔG° < 0 at every temperature.

**(c)** The claim is **incorrect**. ΔG° is negative, so K is large and an equilibrium mixture would contain mainly R and Y. The jar still contains W, so Q ≪ K and the system is far from equilibrium. The decomposition must have a high activation energy, so it is under kinetic control.

**(d)** Add a suitable catalyst to a sample of W (or heat a small sample strongly). Expected result: gas Y is given off and R forms. If the system were already at equilibrium, a catalyst would not change the amounts.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | −70.8 kJ mol⁻¹ with ΔS° converted to kJ |
| 1 | Both terms negative (ΔH° < 0 and −TΔS° < 0), so ΔG° < 0 at all T |
| 1 | Claim rejected: favoured process with no change means Q ≪ K, not equilibrium |
| 1 | High activation energy named as the reason (kinetic control) |
| 1 | Valid test (catalyst or strong heating) with the expected observation |

Accept any valid test that would start a kinetically hindered reaction.
</details>

## Question 7 (explanation · stretch)

Air is mostly N₂ and O₂. At room temperature, no measurable NO forms from them. A mixture of H₂ and O₂ also shows no change at room temperature. A student says: "Both of these are examples of kinetic control."

Use ΔG° values calculated from the data at the top of the page to evaluate the student's statement for each mixture.

<details>
<summary>Model answer and suggested Marlbridge rubric</summary>

**Calculations.**
N₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO(g): ΔG° = 2(+86.60) − 0 = **+173.2 kJ mol⁻¹**.
2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(g): ΔG° = 2(−228.58) − 0 = **−457.2 kJ mol⁻¹**.

**Evaluation.** The statement is **only half right**.
- For H₂ and O₂, ΔG° < 0: water formation is favoured, yet it does not happen at a measurable rate. That is kinetic control, caused by a high activation energy. A spark or catalyst would start it.
- For N₂ and O₂, ΔG° > 0: forming NO is **not favoured** at room temperature. The absence of NO is a thermodynamic result, not a kinetic one. Kinetic control describes only favoured processes, so this mixture is not an example, and a catalyst would not produce a large amount of NO.

| Point | What earns it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Both ΔG° values correct, with coefficients and elements at zero |
| 1 | H₂/O₂ identified as kinetic control: favoured but slow because of a high Eₐ |
| 1 | N₂/O₂ identified as not favoured (ΔG° > 0), so not kinetic control |
| 1 | States that kinetic control applies only when ΔG° < 0 |

Do not award the third point for "no NO forms because it is slow".
</details>
