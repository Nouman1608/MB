## Understanding

- I can explain inductance as opposition to a change in current, using Faraday's and Lenz's laws. *(Guide: "A coil that resists change in its own current")*
- I can explain why straight wires are modelled as having zero inductance while a solenoid is an inductor. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can state and use the units of inductance, including 1 H = 1 V·s/A. *(Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why the current through an inductor cannot change instantly. *(Guide: "The self-induced emf")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can derive L = μ₀N²A/ℓ for a long solenoid from its magnetic field. *(Guide: "The inductance of a long solenoid"; Practice Q6)*
- I can calculate L with and without a core of given permeability. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q6)*
- I can find the size and direction of a self-induced emf from a rate of change of current. *(Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can differentiate a given I(t) to find ℰ(t), and sketch ℰ against t from a graph of I against t. *(Guide: Figure 1, Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can derive U = ½LI² and use it to find stored and released energy. *(Guide: "Energy stored in an inductor"; Practice Q4)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict how L changes when N, ℓ, r or the core changes. *(Guide: scaling table; Practice Q2)*
- I can use energy conservation to follow energy from an inductor into a resistor or a capacitor. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why the emf is zero when the current is at its maximum. *(Practice Q5)*
