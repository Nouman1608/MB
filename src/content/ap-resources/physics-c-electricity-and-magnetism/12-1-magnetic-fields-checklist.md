## Understanding

- I can describe B as a vector field and name the three kinds of object it exerts forces on. *(Guide: "What a magnetic field is")*
- I can explain why there are no magnetic monopoles and what happens when a magnet is cut in half. *(Guide: "No monopoles: Gauss's law for magnetism")*
- I can state Gauss's law for magnetism, ∮B·dA = 0, and explain why it means field lines form closed loops. *(Guide: "No monopoles"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain permanent and induced magnetism as the alignment of dipoles produced by moving electrons. *(Guide: "Where magnetism comes from"; Practice Q5)*
- I can tell ferromagnetic, paramagnetic and diamagnetic materials apart, with examples, and say what happens when the field is removed. *(Guide: "How materials respond to a field"; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain what permeability measures, why μ₀ is a constant and why a material's μ is not. *(Guide: "Magnetic permeability"; Practice Q2)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can draw the field lines of a bar magnet, inside and outside, with correct arrows. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6)*
- I can find the flux through a flat face in a uniform field, using only the component along the normal. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can use ∮B·dA = 0 to find the flux through the last part of a closed surface, with the right sign. *(Practice Q3, Q4)*
- I can derive B_r = −(r/2) dB_z/dz for an axially symmetric field and evaluate it. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict which way a compass points by combining the fields of several poles. *(Practice Q6)*
- I can describe Earth's field as a dipole field and explain why the pole near the geographic North Pole is a magnetic south pole. *(Guide: "Earth as a magnet")*
- I can explain why an unmagnetised iron object is attracted to either pole of a magnet. *(Practice Q5)*
