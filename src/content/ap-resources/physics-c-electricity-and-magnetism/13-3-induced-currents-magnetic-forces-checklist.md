## Understanding

- I can explain why the external field exerts a force on the current it induces in a conductor. *(Guide: "The field pushes on the current it induces")*
- I can identify which segments of a loop feel a magnetic force and which forces cancel. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can use Lenz's law to show that the force opposes the motion, both entering and leaving a field. *(Practice Q1, Q3)*
- I can explain why a loop moving wholly inside a uniform field feels no magnetic force. *(Practice Q3, Q7)*
- I can describe how forces on an induced current produce a torque that opposes rotation. *(Guide: "Turning effects")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can calculate the induced current and the force F = B²L²v/R on a moving rod or loop edge. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can write Newton's second law for a moving conductor and solve it for v(t). *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can find a terminal speed by setting the acceleration to zero. *(Practice Q6, Q7)*
- I can sketch v against t and mark the time constant τ = mR/(B²L²). *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict factors of change in current, force and power when v, B, L or R changes. *(Guide: factor-of-change table; Practice Q2, Q4)*
- I can show that the mechanical power supplied equals the power dissipated, I²R. *(Practice Q4, Q6, Q7)*
- I can explain why constant-acceleration equations do not apply to magnetic braking. *(Practice Q5)*
