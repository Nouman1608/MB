## Understanding

- I can state Ampère's law, ∮B·dℓ = μ₀I_enc, and explain what an Amperian loop is. *(Guide: "From the long-wire field to Ampère's law")*
- I can use the right-hand sign rule to decide which enclosed currents are positive. *(Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why ∮B·dℓ = 0 does not mean B = 0 on the loop. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*
- I can say when Ampère's law gives B directly and when it is true but not helpful. *(Guide: "Choosing an Amperian loop")*
- I can explain that a changing electric field produces a magnetic field (Maxwell's addition). *(Guide: "Maxwell's addition"; Practice Q7)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can derive B inside and outside a long uniform solid conductor and evaluate it with numbers. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
- I can find B in the hollow, in the metal and outside a thick tube, and outside a coaxial cable. *(Practice Q3, Q4)*
- I can set up and evaluate I_enc = ∫J(r′)2πr′ dr′ for a non-uniform current density. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can derive B inside and outside a current-carrying slab. *(Guide: "Slabs and current sheets"; Practice Q5)*
- I can derive B = μ₀nI with a rectangular loop and state the ideal-solenoid assumptions. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q7)*
- I can add fields from a solenoid, wires and cylinders as vectors. *(Guide: Worked example 3)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict a factor of change in B for a solenoid or a wire. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can sketch B(r) for a cylinder and label the value at the surface. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
- I can check a result for continuity at the surface and thin-wire behaviour far away. *(Practice Q6)*
