## Understanding

- I can state Faraday's law, ℰ = −N dΦ_B/dt, and explain why a steady flux induces no emf. *(Guide: "Faraday's law"; Practice Q6(d))*
- I can state Lenz's law and explain that the induced field opposes the change in flux, not the field itself. *(Guide: "Lenz's law: the direction")*
- I can explain that a changing magnetic field creates a non-conservative electric field, ∮E·dl = −dΦ_B/dt, even where there is no wire. *(Guide: "Induced electric fields")*
- I can describe how Maxwell's equations predict electromagnetic waves at c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀), without deriving it. *(Guide: "Maxwell's equations and light")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can find the emf when B changes at constant area, including a B(t) I must differentiate. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can find the emf when the area changes in a constant field, for a sliding rod or a changing loop. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can find the emf of a rotating coil, ℰ = NBAω sin ωt. *(Guide: "Three ways to change the flux"; Practice Q3)*
- I can turn a graph of flux against time into a graph of emf against time. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q2)*
- I can find the induced electric field inside and outside a solenoid whose field is changing. *(Guide: Worked example 3)*
- I can combine a flux integral with Faraday's law for a time-dependent current, and find the total charge that flows. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning and experiments

- I can find the direction of an induced current using Lenz's law and the right-hand rule. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1, Q4, Q5, Q7)*
- I can predict a factor of change in emf when N, B, A or ω changes. *(Practice Q3, Q5(c))*
- I can design a measurement of induced emf, plot a linear graph and use its gradient. *(Guide: "Measuring an induced emf"; Practice Q6)*
