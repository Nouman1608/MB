## Understanding

- I can state conservation of charge for a system and explain what it means for charge to cross the system boundary. *(Guide: "Charge is conserved")*
- I can explain why solids become charged by gaining or losing electrons, not protons. *(Guide: "Charge is conserved"; Practice Q1)*
- I can describe charging by friction, by contact and by induction, and give the sign each method produces. *(Guide: "Three ways to charge an object")*
- I can explain how a neutral conductor and a neutral insulator are each polarized by a nearby charge. *(Guide: "Induced charge separation (polarization)"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain what grounding is and why Earth can supply or absorb electrons. *(Guide: "Grounding")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can find the number of electrons transferred using N = |Δq|/e. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can find the charges on identical conductors after a sequence of contacts and check the total. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q4)*
- I can draw or describe the charge distribution at each stage of charging by induction. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6)*
- I can calculate the net force on a polarized neutral object from a nearby point charge. *(Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can explain why a charged object attracts a neutral one, whatever its sign, and why only repulsion proves an object is charged. *(Guide: "Induced charge separation (polarization)"; Practice Q3, Q5)*
- I can predict the result of changing the order of steps in charging by induction. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can use measured data and its uncertainty to support or challenge a claim about charge conservation. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can choose a larger system so that charge is conserved when charge leaks to the surroundings. *(Guide: "Grounding"; Practice Q7)*
