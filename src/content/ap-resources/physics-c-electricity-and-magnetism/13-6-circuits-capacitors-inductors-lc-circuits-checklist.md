## Understanding

- I can describe one cycle of an LC circuit in terms of where the energy is stored at each quarter-period. *(Guide: "The idea: energy passed back and forth"; Practice Q6(d))*
- I can explain why the current keeps flowing after the capacitor is empty. *(Guide: "The idea: energy passed back and forth")*
- I can explain why the charge and current are a quarter-cycle out of step, and what that means for the inductor voltage at maximum current. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can state the mass–spring analogy: q with x, I with v, L with m and 1/C with k. *(Guide: "The loop rule gives simple harmonic motion")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can use energy conservation to find I_max from Q₀, or Q_max from a starting current. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4, Q7)*
- I can apply the loop rule to get d²q/dt² = −q/(LC) and derive ω = 1/√(LC). *(Practice Q6)*
- I can calculate ω, f and T, keeping ω and f distinct. *(Practice Q1)*
- I can find the charge and current at a stated point in the cycle, such as when the energies are equal. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4, Q6)*
- I can draw quantitative graphs of q, I and the energies against time with numbered scales and units. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q6(d), Q7(e))*

## Reasoning

- I can predict factors of change in T, I_max and energy, stating whether charge or voltage is held fixed. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3)*
- I can plan an experiment to test T = 2π√(LC) and find L from a graph of T² against C. *(Guide: "Designing an experiment"; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why a capacitor in an LC circuit can reach a voltage far larger than the battery that first supplied the energy. *(Practice Q7(f))*
- I can explain why a real LC oscillation dies away. *(Practice Q5(d))*
