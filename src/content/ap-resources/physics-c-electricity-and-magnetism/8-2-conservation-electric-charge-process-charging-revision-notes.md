## Recap

- **Conservation of charge:** the net charge of a system is constant unless charge moves between the system and its surroundings. Charge is moved, never made.
- In solids, **electrons** move. Negative = gained electrons; positive = lost electrons.
- **Friction:** electrons move from one material to the other. Equal and opposite charges result.
- **Contact:** charge flows between touching objects. Both end with the same sign.
- **Polarization (induced charge separation):** a nearby charge rearranges charge inside an object, even a neutral one. Conductors: free electrons move across the object. Insulators: each molecule's electron cloud shifts a little.
- **Grounding:** connecting an object to a huge, nearly neutral system such as Earth, which can supply or absorb electrons.
- **Induction:** polarize, ground, remove the ground, then remove the rod. The object ends with the opposite sign to the rod.

## Key relationships

| Idea | Relationship |
|---|---|
| Charge is quantized | q = Ne, with e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C |
| Electrons moved | N = \|Δq\| / e |
| Conservation (closed system) | Σq before = Σq after |
| Two identical conductors touching | q_each = (q₁ + q₂) / 2 |
| Neutral object near a charge | Net force is attractive (opposite charge is closer; F ∝ 1/r²) |

## Assumptions

- Stands, threads and air are insulating, so no charge leaks away unless a path is stated.
- "Identical" spheres are identical conductors; unequal sizes share unequally (Topic 10.2).
- Earth acts as an unlimited source or sink of electrons.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Saying protons moved.** Say the object lost electrons.
2. **Forgetting the partner.** If the rod gains −q, the cloth gains +q.
3. **Halving each charge separately.** Add the charges first, then share.
4. **Removing the rod before the ground.** The electrons flow back and the sphere ends neutral.
5. **Using attraction as proof of charge.** Neutral objects are attracted too; use repulsion.

## Quick self-check

1. A rod gains −3.2 nC by rubbing. How many electrons did it gain, and what is the cloth's charge? *(2.0 × 10¹⁰ electrons; +3.2 nC)*
2. Identical metal spheres with +5.0 nC and +1.0 nC touch. Final charges? *(+3.0 nC each)*
3. A positive rod is used to charge a sphere by induction. Final sign? *(Negative: electrons come up from Earth)*
