These are **original Marlbridge practice questions**, not past exam questions. They are not calibrated against real exam results, and your result is **not** a predicted score.

**How to take it.** Work without notes for about 30 minutes, and write each answer before you open the explanation. A scientific calculator is assumed. Data for every question: k = 1/(4πε₀) = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C², ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² C²/(N·m²), e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C and g = 9.8 m/s². All data are invented for practice.

## Question 1 (multiple choice · 8.1)

Small bead A carries +6.0 nC and small bead B carries −2.0 nC. The electric force that A exerts on B has magnitude 3.0 mN. What is the electric force that B exerts on A?

- (A) 3.0 mN, directed towards B
- (B) 9.0 mN, directed towards B
- (C) 1.0 mN, directed towards B
- (D) 3.0 mN, directed away from B

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** The two forces are a Newton's third law pair: equal in size, opposite in direction. Coulomb's law uses the product of the charges, the same for both beads. Opposite signs attract, so A is pulled towards B.

- (B) assumes the larger charge feels three times the force.
- (C) makes the opposite assumption.
- (D) has the right size but treats the force as repulsive. Opposite signs attract.

**If you missed this:** read "Coulomb's law" in the [8.1 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-electricity-and-magnetism/8-1-electric-charge-electric-force-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 2 (multiple choice · 8.2)

A plastic rod and a wool cloth start neutral. After rubbing, the rod carries −3.2 nC. The cloth is then laid on a grounded metal table and lifted off again. The rod never touches anything. Which statement about the system "rod + cloth" is now correct?

- (A) Its net charge is −3.2 nC, because about 2.0 × 10¹⁰ electrons flowed from Earth into the cloth.
- (B) Its net charge is still zero, because charge is always conserved.
- (C) Its net charge is −3.2 nC, because protons flowed from the cloth into Earth.
- (D) Its net charge is +3.2 nC, because the cloth gave its electrons to Earth.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** After rubbing, the cloth carries +3.2 nC. Grounding lets electrons flow from Earth into the cloth until it is neutral: N = (3.2 × 10⁻⁹) ÷ (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) = 2.0 × 10¹⁰. Only the rod's −3.2 nC is left in the system.

- (B) Conservation holds only while no charge crosses the boundary. Here charge entered the system from Earth.
- (C) has the right total but the wrong carrier. Protons stay in their nuclei.
- (D) gets the direction wrong. A positive cloth attracts electrons; it does not give them away.

**If you missed this:** read "Charge is conserved" and "Grounding" in the [8.2 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-electricity-and-magnetism/8-2-conservation-electric-charge-process-charging-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 3 (multiple choice · 8.3)

In a field-line diagram of two point charges, X and Y, eighteen lines leave X. Six of them end on Y, and the other twelve run off the edge of the picture. No other lines start or end on Y. Which statement is correct?

- (A) X is positive, Y is negative, and |q_X| = 3|q_Y|.
- (B) X is positive, Y is negative, and |q_X| = 2|q_Y|.
- (C) X is negative, Y is positive, and |q_X| = 3|q_Y|.
- (D) X is positive and Y is negative, but field lines cannot be used to compare the sizes of the charges.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Lines start on positive charges and end on negative ones, so X is positive and Y is negative. The number of lines is proportional to the size of the charge: 18 lines against 6 gives a ratio of 3.

- (B) compares the 12 lines that escape with the 6 that end on Y. The ratio must use all the lines at each charge.
- (C) reverses the direction rule.
- (D) Line diagrams do show relative sizes, through the number of lines at each charge.

**If you missed this:** read "Showing a field: maps and field lines" in the [8.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-electricity-and-magnetism/8-3-electric-fields-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 4 (multiple choice · 8.3)

A solid metal cube carries a net charge +Q and is in electrostatic equilibrium. Which statement is correct?

- (A) E is zero everywhere inside the metal, and just outside each face E is perpendicular to the face.
- (B) E inside the metal is not zero, because the excess charge is spread through the volume of the cube.
- (C) E is zero everywhere inside the metal, and just outside each face E is parallel to the face.
- (D) E is zero at the centre of the cube only, because of symmetry; it is not zero elsewhere inside.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** In equilibrium the free charges have stopped moving, so there is no field inside the metal, and the excess charge sits on the surface. A field component along the surface would push surface charges sideways, so E just outside is perpendicular to it.

- (B) describes a charged insulator, not a conductor.
- (C) A field parallel to the face would make surface charge move, so the cube would not be in equilibrium.
- (D) The zero field comes from equilibrium, not from the shape, so it holds everywhere in the metal.

**If you missed this:** read "Fields of charged conductors and insulators" in the [8.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-electricity-and-magnetism/8-3-electric-fields-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 5 (multiple choice · 8.4)

A thin plastic arc of radius R, centred on point O, spans an angle of 60° at O. It carries positive charge with uniform charge per unit length λ. What is the magnitude of the electric field at O?

- (A) kλ/R
- (B) 2kλ/R
- (C) πkλ/(3R)
- (D) √3 kλ/R

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Measure φ from the arc's line of symmetry, so the arc runs from −30° to +30°. Each piece has dq = λR dφ at distance R from O. Components across the symmetry line cancel in pairs, so:

E = (kλ/R) ∫ cos φ dφ, from −30° to +30°, = (2kλ/R) sin 30° = **kλ/R**, pointing along the symmetry line, away from the arc.

- (B) is the semicircle result. A shorter arc changes the limits.
- (C) adds the magnitudes of all the dE vectors, kQ/R² with Q = λπR/3. It ignores cancellation, so it is about 5% too large.
- (D) uses cos 30° instead of sin 30° after integrating.

**If you missed this:** read "Arcs: a semicircle at its centre" in the [8.4 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-electricity-and-magnetism/8-4-electric-fields-charge-distributions-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 6 (short answer · 8.1 and 8.3)

An oil droplet of mass 3.3 × 10⁻¹⁵ kg carries 5 excess electrons. It sits between two large horizontal plates where the field is uniform and vertical.

(a) Find the magnitude and direction of the field that holds the droplet at rest.
(b) The droplet picks up one more electron. The field does not change. Find the droplet's acceleration.
(c) A student reports a droplet charge of −2.4 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. Explain why this value must be a measurement error.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**(a)** At rest, the electric force balances the weight: (5e)E = mg. E = (3.3 × 10⁻¹⁵)(9.8) ÷ [5(1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)] = **4.0 × 10⁴ N/C**. The force must point up. The droplet is negative, so the force is opposite to E: **E points downward**.

**(b)** The electric force is now 6eE = (6/5)mg, still upward, while the weight is mg. Net force = 0.2mg upward, so **a = 0.2g = 2.0 m/s² upward**.

**(c)** (2.4 × 10⁻¹⁹) ÷ (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) = 1.5. Charge comes only in whole-number multiples of e, so 1.5e cannot exist.

Check yourself: 1 mark each for the size of E, its direction with the reason, the acceleration with direction, and the "whole number of e" argument (4 in total).

**If you missed this:** for (a) and (b), read the [8.3 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-electricity-and-magnetism/8-3-electric-fields-study-guide/); for (c), the [8.1 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-electricity-and-magnetism/8-1-electric-charge-electric-force-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 7 (multiple choice · 8.5)

A closed cube of side a has faces perpendicular to the x-, y- and z-axes. Its two x-faces are at x = a and x = 2a. In this region the field is E = cx î, where c is a positive constant. What is the net electric flux through the cube?

- (A) ca³
- (B) 0
- (C) 3ca³
- (D) 2ca³

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** E is parallel to the four y- and z-faces, so their flux is zero. On the face at x = 2a, the outward normal is +î: Φ = c(2a)(a²) = +2ca³. On the face at x = a, the outward normal is −î: Φ = −c(a)(a²) = −ca³. Net: **ca³**. (By Gauss's law this means the cube encloses a charge ε₀ca³.)

- (B) uses the rule for a **uniform** field. This field is stronger on the right, so more flux leaves than enters.
- (C) adds the sizes of the two fluxes. On a closed surface, entering flux is negative.
- (D) counts only the face where field lines leave.

**If you missed this:** read "Closed surfaces" in the [8.5 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-electricity-and-magnetism/8-5-electric-flux-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 8 (multiple choice · 8.6)

A closed surface encloses charges +3q and −5q. A charge +4q sits outside it. The surface is then stretched to a larger size, and the −5q charge moves to a new place inside it. The +4q charge is still outside. What is the net flux through the surface now?

- (A) −2q/ε₀
- (B) +2q/ε₀
- (C) +8q/ε₀
- (D) Smaller in size than 2q/ε₀, because the surface is now further from the charges

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**Answer: (A).** Gauss's law: Φ = q_enc/ε₀ = (3q − 5q)/ε₀ = −2q/ε₀. The surface's size and shape, and where the charges sit inside, do not matter; outside charges add nothing.

- (B) includes the outside charge: 3q − 5q + 4q = +2q.
- (C) adds the sizes of the enclosed charges and ignores the sign of −5q.
- (D) The field is weaker further out, but the area grows to make up for it exactly.

**If you missed this:** read "From flux to Gauss's law" in the [8.6 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-electricity-and-magnetism/8-6-gauss-law-study-guide/).
</details>

## Question 9 (short answer · 8.6)

A large flat insulating slab has thickness 2b = 0.040 m and a uniform volume charge density ρ = 4.0 × 10⁻⁷ C/m³. Measure x from the slab's middle plane.

(a) Describe a Gaussian surface that makes the integral simple, and say why.
(b) Show that E = ρx/ε₀ inside the slab (|x| < b) and E = ρb/ε₀ outside.
(c) Calculate E at x = 0.010 m and at x = 0.030 m.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**(a)** A pillbox with end area A and its ends at +x and −x. By symmetry E is perpendicular to the slab, points away from the middle plane and has the same size at ±x. So each end has flux EA, and the curved side, parallel to E, has none.

**(b)** Inside: the box encloses a slice of thickness 2x, so q_enc = ρ(2x)A. Gauss's law: 2EA = 2ρxA/ε₀, so **E = ρx/ε₀**. Outside: the enclosed slice is the whole thickness 2b, so 2EA = 2ρbA/ε₀ and **E = ρb/ε₀**, which does not depend on x.

**(c)** At x = 0.010 m (inside): E = (4.0 × 10⁻⁷)(0.010) ÷ (8.85 × 10⁻¹²) = **4.5 × 10² N/C**. At x = 0.030 m (outside): E = (4.0 × 10⁻⁷)(0.020) ÷ (8.85 × 10⁻¹²) = **9.0 × 10² N/C**. Check: viewed from outside, the slab is a sheet with σ = ρ(2b) = 1.6 × 10⁻⁸ C/m², and σ/(2ε₀) gives the same 9.0 × 10² N/C.

Check yourself: 1 mark each for the symmetric pillbox with a reason, the inside result, the outside result and both numbers (4 in total).

**If you missed this:** read "Choosing a Gaussian surface" and "Standard results" in the [8.6 study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-electricity-and-magnetism/8-6-gauss-law-study-guide/).
</details>
