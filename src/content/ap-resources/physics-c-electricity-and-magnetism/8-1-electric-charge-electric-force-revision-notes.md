## Recap

- Charge is a **scalar**, positive or negative. Proton +e, electron −e, neutron 0.
- Objects charge by gaining electrons (negative) or losing them (positive). Protons do not move.
- A **point charge** is an object whose size is negligible compared with the distances involved.
- Like charges repel; unlike charges attract. The forces in a pair are equal and opposite.
- Electric forces can attract or repel; gravity only attracts. Between particles the electric force is far larger, but large bodies are nearly neutral, so gravity dominates at large scales.
- Contact forces (normal force, friction, tension) are electric in origin, but are modelled as non-fundamental forces.
- **Polarisation**: a field shifts electrons relative to nuclei inside a material. **Permittivity** measures how much a material or medium polarises. Free space has ε₀.
- **Conductors**: charge carriers move easily. **Insulators**: they do not.

## Key relationships

| Quantity | Relationship | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Charge on an object | q = ne | n a whole number; e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C |
| Coulomb force (size) | F = (1/(4πε₀)) \|q₁q₂\|/r² | 1/(4πε₀) = 8.99 × 10⁹ N·m²/C² |
| Coulomb force (vector) | F₁₂ = (1/(4πε₀)) (q₁q₂/r²) r̂₁₂ | r̂₁₂ points from 1 to 2 |
| Net force | F_net = F₁ + F₂ + … | vector sum, at most four charges in calculations |
| Electric vs gravity | F_e/F_g = (1/(4πε₀)) \|q₁q₂\|/(Gm₁m₂) | r cancels; proton–electron ≈ 2.3 × 10³⁹ |
| Permittivity of free space | ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² C²/(N·m²) | appears in Coulomb's law |

## Assumptions behind the results

- The charges are point charges (or uniform spheres seen from outside).
- The charges are at rest (electrostatics).
- The charges are in empty space (or air, which behaves almost like empty space).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **F ∝ 1/r.** It is 1/r²: double r, quarter F.
2. **Forgetting SI units.** Convert nC, μC and cm before using the formula.
3. **Adding magnitudes.** Add components of vectors.
4. **Signs in 2D.** Use magnitudes for size; decide each direction by "like repel, unlike attract".
5. **"The bigger charge pushes harder."** The pair forces are equal and opposite.
6. **"Gaining protons."** Only electrons move between objects.

## Quick self-check

1. How many electrons must be removed from a neutral object to give it +1.0 μC? *(6.25 × 10¹²)*
2. The separation of two charges is halved. By what factor does the force change? *(It becomes 4 times larger)*
3. Why does a charged rod attract a neutral piece of paper? *(It polarises the paper; the nearer, opposite charges are attracted more than the farther, like charges are repelled)*
