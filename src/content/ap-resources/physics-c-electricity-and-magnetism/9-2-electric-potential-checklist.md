## Understanding

- I can define electric potential as potential energy per unit charge, and potential difference as the change in it. *(Guide: "From potential energy to potential")*
- I can explain why V can be zero where E is not, and the reverse. *(Practice Q1, Q6)*
- I can explain how a battery keeps a potential difference between its terminals. *(Guide: "Batteries")*
- I can explain why equipotentials cross field lines at right angles and why no work is done along one. *(Guide: "Equipotential maps")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can find V for several point charges by adding scalar terms with their signs. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can integrate k dq/r for a ring on its axis, an arc at its centre and a finite rod on its line or perpendicular bisector. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can find a potential difference near a long wire or cylinder using ΔV = −∫E·dl. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7)*
- I can find E_x from V(x) using E_x = −dV/dx. *(Practice Q4, Q6)*
- I can use ΔU = qΔV to find the work needed to move a charge. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can plot V against position with scaled, labelled axes. *(Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can estimate field strength and direction from the spacing and values of equipotentials. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can decide which of two points is at higher potential from the direction of E. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why V cannot be referenced to infinity for an infinite line of charge. *(Practice Q7)*
