## Recap

- An ideal conductor has electrons that move freely. Positive net charge means missing electrons, modelled as positive carriers on the surface.
- Free charges move until the field inside cancels. This **electrostatic equilibrium** is reached effectively at once.
- E = 0 inside the metal → by Gauss's law, no net charge inside → all excess charge is on the surface.
- E = 0 inside → ΔV = −∫E·dl = 0 → the whole conductor, surface included, is one **equipotential**.
- The field just outside is **perpendicular** to the surface, with E = σ/ε₀.
- σ is **greatest at points and edges**, so the field is strongest there.
- In an external field, a conductor **polarizes**: induced − and + charges appear on opposite sides, the net charge stays the same, and the inside stays field-free.
- A closed conducting shell **shields** its cavity from outside fields.

## Key relationships

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| Inside the metal of any conductor in equilibrium | E = 0; V = constant |
| Just outside a conducting surface | E = σ/ε₀, perpendicular to the surface |
| Thin isolated sheet (for comparison) | E = σ/(2ε₀) |
| Isolated conducting sphere, charge Q | σ = Q/(4πR²); E = Q/(4πε₀r²) for r ≥ R; E = 0 inside |
| Empty cavity in a conductor | no charge on the wall; E = 0 in the cavity |
| Charge q in a cavity of a conductor with net charge Q | wall: −q; outer surface: Q + q |

## Assumptions

- Electrostatic equilibrium: no charge is moving.
- "Ideal" conductor: charges move freely; insulators behave differently.
- Spherical results need spherical symmetry and no nearby charges.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **V = 0 inside because E = 0.** V is constant, not zero.
2. **σ/(2ε₀) next to a conductor.** It is σ/ε₀.
3. **Charge −q on the outer surface.** The induced −q is on the cavity wall.
4. **Uniform σ on every shape.** Only an isolated sphere has uniform σ.
5. **"Shielding works both ways."** A charge inside still produces a field outside unless the shell is grounded.

## Quick self-check

1. Just outside a conductor, σ = 4.0 × 10⁻⁸ C/m². What is E? *(4.5 × 10³ N/C, perpendicular to the surface)*
2. +2.0 nC is placed in the cavity of a neutral metal shell. What are the charges on the inner and outer surfaces? *(−2.0 nC and +2.0 nC)*
3. A metal sphere is at 500 V. What is the potential at its centre? *(500 V)*
