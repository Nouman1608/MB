## Understanding

- I can explain why ΔU = qΔV belongs to the object–field system, and what happens to that energy when only electric forces act. *(Guide: "Energy bookkeeping for a moving charge")*
- I can explain why the path between two points does not change the energy a charge gains. *(Guide: "Energy bookkeeping for a moving charge")*
- I can predict from the signs of q and ΔV whether a charge speeds up or slows down. *(Guide: "Signs"; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can define the electron-volt and convert between eV and joules. *(Guide: "The electron-volt"; Practice Q4)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can find the kinetic energy and speed of a charge accelerated through a potential difference. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q4)*
- I can integrate a non-uniform field to find ΔV and then the energy gained. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can use E_x = −dV/dx and energy conservation together for a given V(x). *(Practice Q6)*
- I can find a distance of closest approach for a charge fired at a fixed charge. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can combine energy and momentum conservation when two charged objects both move. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning and representations

- I can draw energy bar charts and a U(x) graph with a total-energy line, and read off kinetic energy and turning points. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can predict factors of change, such as v ∝ √ΔV or r_min ∝ 1/v². *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2, Q5)*
- I can compare the speeds and energies of different particles in the same potential difference. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can outline an experiment that tests the link between ΔV and kinetic energy. *(Guide: "Designing an experiment"; Practice Q7)*
