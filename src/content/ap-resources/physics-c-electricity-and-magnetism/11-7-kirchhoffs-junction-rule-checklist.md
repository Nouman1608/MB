## Understanding

- I can state the junction rule and explain that it follows from conservation of charge. *(Guide: "Charge cannot pile up at a junction")*
- I can link the rule to charge per unit time, using I = dq/dt. *(Practice Q3)*
- I can explain why the rule still holds while a capacitor charges, and where the charge actually collects. *(Guide: "The calculus view"; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why the same current enters and leaves every element in a branch. *(Guide: "Using the rule")*
- I can say which conservation law each Kirchhoff rule comes from. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can write a junction equation with assumed directions and interpret a negative answer. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1)*
- I can apply the rule to a closed region round several junctions as a check. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can combine N − 1 junction equations with loop equations to find every branch current. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can write the junction rule in terms of node potentials and solve for unknown potentials. *(Guide: "The same circuit with node potentials"; Practice Q7)*
- I can plan a measurement that tests the junction rule, with ammeters placed correctly, and analyse it with a straight-line graph. *(Guide: "Testing the junction rule in the lab"; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict how current divides between branches of different resistance. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can check a circuit solution with an equivalent resistance or an energy balance. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain why a bridge circuit cannot be reduced to series and parallel parts, and find its equivalent resistance from the junction rule. *(Practice Q7)*
