## Understanding

- I can decide whether two elements are in series, in parallel or neither by tracing the paths and junctions, not the layout. *(Guide: "Series and parallel: it is about the paths")*
- I can explain why elements in series carry the same current and why parallel paths share the same potential difference. *(Guide: "Series and parallel: it is about the paths")*
- I can explain why adding a parallel path lowers the equivalent resistance. *(Guide: "Equivalent resistance"; Practice Q6)*
- I can describe a real battery as an ideal emf in series with an internal resistance, and say when its terminal voltage equals its emf. *(Guide: "Real batteries and real wires")*
- I can explain when wire resistance may be neglected and when it may not. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can derive the series and parallel formulas for equivalent resistance. *(Guide: "Equivalent resistance")*
- I can reduce a network step by step and find the current in and potential difference across every resistor. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can use ΔV = ℰ − Ir to find a terminal voltage, an emf or an internal resistance. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q5)*
- I can find ℰ and r from a graph of terminal voltage against current. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can place an ammeter and a voltmeter correctly in a schematic. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q4, Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict how bulb brightness changes when a path is added or removed, following R_eq → I → ΔV → P. *(Guide: "Brightness of bulbs in compound circuits"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain how a real ammeter or voltmeter changes the quantity it measures, and estimate the size of the effect. *(Guide: "Measuring current and potential difference"; Practice Q4)*
- I can compare how an ideal and a real battery respond when more load is connected. *(Practice Q6)*
