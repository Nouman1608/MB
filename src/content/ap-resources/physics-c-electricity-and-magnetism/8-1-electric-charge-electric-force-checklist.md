## Understanding

- I can describe charge as a scalar with two signs, and give the charges of the proton, electron and neutron. *(Guide: "What electric charge is")*
- I can explain that any object's charge is a whole-number multiple of e, and that objects charge by gaining or losing electrons. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can say when an object may be modelled as a point charge. *(Guide: "What electric charge is")*
- I can explain polarisation, permittivity and the difference between conductors and insulators. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why everyday contact forces are electric in origin. *(Practice Q6)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can use Coulomb's law with SI units to find the size of the force between two point charges. *(Practice Q4, Q5)*
- I can decide the direction of each force from the signs and draw it along the line joining the charges. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
- I can add forces from up to four charges using components and give the magnitude and direction of the net force. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can calculate the ratio of electric to gravitational force for two particles. *(Practice Q3, Q5)*
- I can find the point on a line where the net force on a third charge is zero. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict the factor by which the force changes when charges or separation change. *(Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can explain why gravity dominates at large scales even though the electric force is far stronger between particles. *(Guide: "Electric force versus gravitational force"; Practice Q6)*
- I can use the sign of dF/dx, or the forces on each side, to decide whether a balance point is stable. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*
