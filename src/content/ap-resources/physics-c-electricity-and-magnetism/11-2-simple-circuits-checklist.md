Work through the list without notes. If you cannot do a statement, follow its link, then come back. Guide = [study guide](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-electricity-and-magnetism/11-2-simple-circuits-study-guide/); Practice = [practice questions](/advanced-course-resources/physics-c-electricity-and-magnetism/11-2-simple-circuits-practice/).

## Understanding

- I can list the kinds of element a circuit can contain and explain what a closed electrical loop is. *(Guide: "What a circuit is")*
- I can define closed, open and short circuits and give an example of each. *(Guide: "Closed, open and short circuits"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain why a shorted bulb carries no current, using the idea of zero potential difference. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain that a schematic shows connections, not physical layout, and that the arrangement of elements decides how a circuit behaves. *(Guide: "Circuit schematics and symbols")*

## Skills

- I can recognise and draw the symbols for a battery, bulb, switch, capacitor, resistor, inductor, ammeter and voltmeter. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can show that an element is variable by drawing a diagonal arrow through its symbol. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can turn a written description of a circuit into a schematic with junction dots in the right places. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q5)*
- I can list every closed loop in a circuit and name the elements in each. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q3, Q4)*
- I can mark conventional current on a schematic, starting from the battery's positive terminal. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict which bulbs are lit for different switch positions, and justify each answer using loops. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why the current is the same at every point of a single loop. *(Practice Q7)*
- I can explain why a short circuit across a battery is a hazard. *(Guide: "Closed, open and short circuits")*
