## Understanding

- I can explain what resistance measures and state its unit, 1 Ω = 1 V/A. *(Guide: "What resistance measures")*
- I can explain the difference between resistance (an object) and resistivity (a material). *(Guide: "Resistivity is a property of the material"; Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why the resistivity of a metal usually rises with temperature. *(Guide: "Resistivity is a property of the material"; Practice Q5)*
- I can state Ohm's law and say what makes an element ohmic or non-ohmic. *(Guide: "Ohm's law"; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain that a resistor converts electrical energy to thermal energy, which may warm it and its surroundings. *(Guide: "Resistivity is a property of the material")*

## Calculation and skills

- I can derive R = ρℓ/A from E = ρJ and ΔV = Eℓ. *(Guide: "From E = ρJ to R = ρℓ/A")*
- I can calculate the resistance of a round wire, converting the diameter to a radius in metres. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can find the resistance of a wire whose resistivity varies, using R = (1/A)∫ρ(x) dx. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7)*
- I can plot I against ΔV with sensible scales and units, draw a best-fit line and find R = 1/slope. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q1)*
- I can plan an experiment to measure resistivity and find ρ from the slope of R against ℓ. *(Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict the factor by which R changes when a wire's length, diameter or material changes, or when it is stretched. *(Practice Q2, Q3)*
- I can decide from data whether an element is ohmic, and find R at a point on a curved graph from ΔV/I. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can explain how a potential difference is shared along a wire whose resistivity varies, using the same current in every slice. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q7)*
