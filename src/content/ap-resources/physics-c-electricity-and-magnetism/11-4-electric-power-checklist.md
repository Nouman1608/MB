## Understanding

- I can derive P = IΔV from dU = ΔV dq and I = dq/dt, and show that 1 V·A = 1 W. *(Guide: "Where electric power comes from")*
- I can say which elements deliver energy to a circuit and which receive it, and use energy conservation as a check. *(Guide: "Sources and receivers of energy"; Worked example 1)*
- I can explain that a bulb's brightness increases with the power it receives. *(Guide: "Predicting the brightness of bulbs"; Practice Q3)*
- I can explain that a resistor dissipates electrical energy as thermal energy at the rate I²R. *(Guide: "Power in a resistor: three forms"; Practice Q5)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can calculate power using P = IΔV, I²R or ΔV²/R, choosing the most suitable form. *(Practice Q1, Q5)*
- I can find a resistance from a power rating, or a safe maximum ΔV or I. *(Practice Q3, Q5)*
- I can find the energy transferred when power changes with time, using E = ∫P dt. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
- I can draw a power bar chart or sketch a P–t graph and explain what its area means. *(Guide: Figures 2 and 3; Practice Q6)*
- I can convert between joules and kilowatt-hours. *(Guide: "A note on units of energy"; Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict how power changes when R or ΔV changes, stating what is held fixed. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can compare the brightness of two bulbs in series and in parallel, and explain why the order can swap. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7)*
- I can account for the energy into a motor or generator as mechanical power plus thermal dissipation. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can explain why squaring an average current gives the wrong energy. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*
