## Understanding

- I can explain how E = ∫ k dq/r² r̂ comes from adding the fields of many point charges (superposition). *(Guide: "From point charges to continuous charge")*
- I can explain why I must add components, not magnitudes, when the dE vectors point in different directions. *(Guide: "Common misconceptions"; Practice Q1)*
- I can use symmetry to state the direction of E for a ring on its axis, a rod on its bisector and an arc at its centre. *(Guide: "Symmetry does half the work"; Practice Q6)*
- I can say when a point-charge model or an infinite-line model is a good approximation for a finite rod. *(Practice Q5)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can write dq = λ dx or dq = λR dθ, with the correct λ for a rod or an arc. *(Guide: "From point charges to continuous charge")*
- I can derive E for a finite rod at a point on its own line and evaluate it with numbers. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q2)*
- I can derive E for a finite rod on its perpendicular bisector and take the infinite-line limit, λ/(2πε₀r). *(Guide: "The finite rod on its perpendicular bisector"; Practice Q5)*
- I can derive E on the axis of a ring and find where it is greatest. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can derive E at the centre of a semicircle or part of one by integrating over θ. *(Guide: "Arcs"; Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can set up the integral for a rod whose charge density varies along it. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can check a result by showing it becomes kQ/r² far from the object. *(Guide: Worked examples 1 and 2; Practice Q7)*
- I can sketch E along the axis of a ring, showing zero at the centre, the maximum and the sign change. *(Guide: Figure 2)*
- I can compare the fields of two charge arrangements and explain the difference in size or direction. *(Practice Q3, Q6, Q7)*
