## Understanding

- I can define current as the rate at which charge crosses a cross-section, and state that 1 A = 1 C/s. *(Guide: "What current measures")*
- I can explain that a potential difference (emf) drives charge round a circuit by setting up a field in the conductor. *(Guide: "What current measures")*
- I can describe the random motion and the slow drift of free electrons, and explain why zero current does not mean the carriers are at rest. *(Guide: "Drift velocity"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why current has a direction but is not a vector, while current density is a vector. *(Guide: "The direction of current"; Practice Q3)*
- I can state the direction of conventional current and explain why electrons in a wire drift the opposite way. *(Practice Q5, Q7)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can use I = dq/dt, and find charge from q = ∫I dt or the area under an I–t graph. *(Practice Q4)*
- I can derive I = nqv_dA from a sketch of carriers crossing a section. *(Guide: Figure 1; Practice Q7)*
- I can calculate drift speed, current density and the field E = ρJ, with correct units. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5)*
- I can find a total current by integrating a non-uniform J over rings of area 2πr dr. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict how drift speed changes when the wire's diameter changes at fixed current. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can explain why a lamp lights at once even though the drift speed is tiny. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
- I can explain why I = JA gives the wrong answer when J varies across a wire. *(Practice Q6)*
