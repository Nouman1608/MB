## Understanding

- I can define the electric field as E = F/q₀ and give its unit. *(Guide: "What an electric field is")*
- I can explain what makes a charge a good test charge, and why the field does not depend on it. *(Guide: "What an electric field is"; Practice Q2, Q6)*
- I can state the direction of E near isolated positive and negative charges, and the direction of the force on a negative charge. *(Practice Q1)*
- I can read a vector field map and a field-line diagram, and explain why field lines never cross. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
- I can describe where excess charge sits, and what E is inside and at the surface, for a conductor in equilibrium and for an insulator. *(Guide: "Fields of charged conductors and insulators"; Practice Q3)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can use E = kq/r² and F = qE with correct units and signs. *(Guide: Worked example 1)*
- I can add the fields of several point charges by components, using symmetry to cancel components. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4)*
- I can find where the net field of two charges is zero. *(Practice Q5)*
- I can plan a measurement of E with a small charged probe and a force sensor. *(Guide: "Measuring a field"; Practice Q6)*
- I can plot E against 1/r² with labelled axes and find the source charge from the slope. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q6)*

## Reasoning

- I can predict factors of change in E and F when charges or distances change. *(Practice Q2)*
- I can use the point-charge result for the field outside a spherically symmetric sphere. *(Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can derive a symbolic expression for a net field and explain how it behaves far away. *(Practice Q7)*
