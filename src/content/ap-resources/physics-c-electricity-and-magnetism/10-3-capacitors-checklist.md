## Understanding

- I can describe a capacitor as two separated conductors with charges +Q and −Q, and explain why its net charge is zero. *(Guide: "What a capacitor is")*
- I can define capacitance as C = Q/ΔV and explain why it depends only on shape, size and gap material. *(Guide: "What a capacitor is"; Practice Q1)*
- I can explain why the field between parallel plates is uniform, except near the edges, and nearly zero outside. *(Guide: Figure 1)*
- I can explain why the stored energy is ½QΔV and not QΔV. *(Guide: "Energy stored in a capacitor"; Practice Q7)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can use Gauss's law and superposition to show that E = σ/ε₀ between parallel plates. *(Guide: "The parallel-plate capacitor")*
- I can calculate C, Q, E and U for a parallel-plate capacitor with units. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q4)*
- I can derive the capacitance of a concentric spherical capacitor and of a coaxial cylindrical capacitor. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q3, Q6)*
- I can derive U = Q²/(2C) by integrating (q/C) dq, and sketch ΔV against q. *(Guide: Figure 2; Practice Q7)*
- I can find the deflection of a charged particle between plates using constant-acceleration equations. *(Guide: Worked example 3; Practice Q5)*

## Reasoning

- I can decide whether Q or ΔV stays fixed and predict how C, ΔV, E and U change when the plate separation changes. *(Guide: "Fixed charge or fixed potential difference?"; Practice Q2, Q4)*
- I can check a capacitance formula with limiting cases, such as b → ∞ or a thin gap. *(Guide: "A method for any capacitor"; Practice Q6)*
- I can explain where the energy comes from when an isolated capacitor's plates are pulled apart. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q7)*
