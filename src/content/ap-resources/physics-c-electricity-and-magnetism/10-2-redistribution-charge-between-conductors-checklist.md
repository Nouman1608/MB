## Understanding

- I can explain why conductors in contact end at the same potential and why their charges are usually unequal. *(Guide: "Contact means equal potential"; Practice Q1)*
- I can state the direction in which electrons and "positive charge" flow between conductors at different potentials. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q5(c))*
- I can describe ground as a zero-potential reference that can supply or absorb any amount of charge. *(Guide: "Ground: the ideal reference")*
- I can explain why grounding an isolated conductor makes it neutral but grounding one near a charge does not. *(Guide: "Ground: the ideal reference"; Practice Q4)*

## Calculation and skills

- I can derive q₁/R₁ = q₂/R₂ for distant spheres joined by a wire and find each final charge. *(Guide: "Two spheres joined by a long wire"; Practice Q2)*
- I can compare the surface charge densities and surface fields of connected spheres. *(Guide: Worked example 1; Practice Q3)*
- I can find the charge induced on a grounded sphere near a point charge, Q′ = −qR/d. *(Guide: Worked example 2; Practice Q4(c))*
- I can find the potentials of nested conductors and predict the result of joining them. *(Guide: "Conductors inside conductors"; Practice Q5)*
- I can integrate E from infinity to find a grounded conductor's induced charge. *(Practice Q7)*

## Reasoning

- I can use the two-sphere model to explain why charge builds up at sharp points. *(Guide: "Why charge piles up at sharp points")*
- I can explain the order of steps needed to keep a charge induced by grounding. *(Guide: Worked example 2)*
- I can explain why a grounded closed shell shields in both directions but an isolated shell does not. *(Guide: "Grounding a shell around a charge"; Practice Q6)*
