This set covers only topic 3.1.1, **The Pakistan movement (1857–1947)**, the compulsory topic
examined in Section A of Paper 1. It works through the four key developments the specification
names: the War of Independence and its impact (1857–1906); controversy, crises and reaction to
repression (1905–1927); stagnation and stalemate (1927–1939); and the road to independence
(1939–47). The tariffs (1, 2, 4, 6 and 9 marks) follow the pattern of the board's specimen paper,
but this is a topic drill, not a full section, so it has more questions than Section A would.

## Section A — short-answer questions

**1.** In which year was the Indian National Congress founded? **[1]**

A: 1857 &nbsp; B: 1885 &nbsp; C: 1906 &nbsp; D: 1919

**2.** Which Viceroy carried out the partition of Bengal in 1905? **[1]**

A: Lord Minto &nbsp; B: Lord Curzon &nbsp; C: Lord Irwin &nbsp; D: Lord Wavell

**3.** In which city did the massacre at Jallianwala Bagh take place in April 1919? **[1]**

A: Lahore &nbsp; B: Delhi &nbsp; C: Amritsar &nbsp; D: Lucknow

**4.** On which date did Muhammad Ali Jinnah ask Muslims to observe a "Day of Deliverance"? **[1]**

A: 22 December 1939 &nbsp; B: 23 March 1940 &nbsp; C: 16 August 1946 &nbsp; D: 3 June 1947

**5.** State **two** aims of the All-India Muslim League when it was founded in 1906. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** reasons why the War of Independence (1857) failed. **[2]**

**7.** State **two** aims of the Khilafat movement. **[2]**

## Section B — extended questions

**8.** Identify and explain **two** reasons why Congress won the provincial elections of 1937. **[4]**

**9.** Identify and explain **two** features of the Lahore Resolution (1940). **[4]**

**10.** Analyse **two** ways in which the Khilafat movement affected relations between Muslims and
Hindus. **[6]**

**11.** Analyse **two** reasons why Congress rule (1937–39) was important for the Pakistan
movement. **[6]**

**12.** "Direct Action Day (1946) was the main reason why the subcontinent was partitioned in 1947."

How far do you agree with this statement? Explain your answer using the statement above and your
contextual knowledge. **[9]**

**13.** Which was the more important attempt to find a political solution between 1927 and 1935?

- the Nehru Report (1928)
- the three Round Table Conferences (1930–32)

Explain your answer with reference to both bullet points. **[9]**

## Answers

Multiple-choice questions score 1 mark. "State" questions score 1 mark per valid point. "Identify
and explain" questions score 1 mark for each point identified and 1 for its explanation. The 6- and
9-mark questions are marked by levels; the level descriptions follow the answers. Indicative content
is a guide, and other accurate points should be credited.

**1.** **B: 1885** [1]. Congress was founded in Bombay in December 1885, with the support of the
retired British official A. O. Hume. **[1]**

**2.** **B: Lord Curzon** [1]. Lord Minto was Viceroy when the Simla Deputation met him in 1906, so
the two are easily confused. **[1]**

**3.** **C: Amritsar** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** **A: 22 December 1939** [1], after the Congress provincial ministries resigned. **[1]**

**5.** Two of the following, 1 mark each: to protect and advance the political rights of Muslims and
put their needs before the government [1]; to promote loyalty among Muslims towards the British
government [1]. Also credit: to prevent hostility between Muslims and other communities without
harming the League's other aims. **[2]**

**6.** Two reasons, 1 mark each: the uprising had no single, agreed leader or plan, and rebels in
different places fought separately [1]; the British had better weapons and resources and kept
control of the Punjab, where Sikh and other regiments stayed loyal and were used to retake Delhi [1].
Also credit: many princes and large areas of the south and east did not join; the British could move
troops and messages quickly using the telegraph and their control of the sea. **[2]**

**7.** Two aims, 1 mark each: to keep the Ottoman Sultan as Khalifa, the head of the Muslim world [1];
to keep the holy places of Islam under Muslim control [1]. Also credit: to stop the break-up of the
Ottoman Empire after the First World War. **[2]**

**8.** Two reasons, each identified (1) and explained (1):
- Congress was a well-organised, long-established party with branches across India [1], so it could
  run campaigns and put up candidates in most constituencies [1];
- the Muslim League was weak and short of funds after years of inactivity [1], and in
  Muslim-majority provinces regional parties such as the Unionist Party in Punjab won much of the
  Muslim vote instead [1].

Also credit: Congress had gained mass support through earlier campaigns of non-cooperation and civil
disobedience. **[4]**

**9.** Two features, each identified (1) and explained (1):
- it demanded that the Muslim-majority areas in the north-west and east of India be grouped into
  independent states [1], which moved the League from seeking safeguards within India to seeking a
  separate homeland [1];
- it was passed at the League's session in Lahore in March 1940, moved by A. K. Fazlul Haq of
  Bengal [1], so a leader from the eastern zone backed it, showing the demand had support from both
  wings [1].

Also credit: it said minorities in these areas should have adequate safeguards for their rights. **[4]**

**10.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- *Short-term co-operation:* Gandhi linked the Non-Cooperation movement to the Khilafat cause, so for
  a time Hindus and Muslims campaigned together; it was one of the high points of united action
  against British rule.
- *Long-term distrust:* when Gandhi called off Non-Cooperation after the violence at Chauri Chaura in
  1922 without consulting Khilafat leaders, many Muslims felt let down by Congress.
- *Violence:* the Moplah uprising in Malabar (1921) involved attacks on Hindus and damaged trust,
  and communal riots increased in the 1920s, alongside Hindu revival movements such as *shuddhi*.
- *Result:* the collapse of the movement after the caliphate was abolished in Turkey in 1924 left
  Muslims looking for their own political route rather than relying on Congress. **[6]**

**11.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- Congress refused to form coalition ministries with the League, for example in the United
  Provinces, which suggested Muslims would be shut out of power in a Congress-led India.
- Policies such as the singing of *Bande Mataram*, the Wardha scheme of education and the Vidya
  Mandir scheme were seen by many Muslims as promoting Hindu culture.
- The League's reports on Muslim grievances (such as the Pirpur Report) spread the sense of
  discrimination.
- As a result, many Muslims rallied to the League, which grew quickly; the experience helped lead to
  the Lahore Resolution in 1940. **[6]**

**12.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Indicative content:
- *Agree:* the League called Direct Action Day on 16 August 1946 after withdrawing its acceptance of
  the Cabinet Mission Plan; the killings in Calcutta and the violence that spread to places such as
  Noakhali and Bihar convinced many British officials that a united India could not be kept
  together peacefully.
- *Disagree:* the League's victory in the Muslim seats in the 1945–46 elections had already shown
  that it spoke for most Muslims; the breakdown of the Cabinet Mission Plan was itself a cause;
  Britain, weakened by the Second World War, announced in February 1947 that it would leave by June
  1948; Mountbatten's 3 June Plan and the Indian Independence Act turned the decision into law.
- *Judgement:* for example, Direct Action Day sped up partition, but the deeper cause was the
  failure to agree a constitution that both Congress and the League would accept. **[9]**

**13.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Answers must address both bullet points. Indicative
content:
- *Nehru Report:* it proposed dominion status and joint electorates and rejected one-third
  Muslim representation at the centre; Muslims saw it as proof that Congress would not protect
  them, and Jinnah replied with his Fourteen Points (1929), which set out Muslim demands clearly.
  It was important mainly because it failed and hardened Muslim opinion.
- *Round Table Conferences:* they brought British, Congress, League and princely representatives
  together in London; they failed to agree on minority representation, but led to the Communal Award
  (1932), which kept separate electorates, and fed into the Government of India Act (1935).
- *Judgement:* for example, the conferences were more important as a "solution" because they shaped
  the 1935 Act, while the Nehru Report mattered more as a turning point in Muslim opinion. **[9]**

## Levels of response used in these answers

These bands follow the structure of the levels-of-response marking in OxfordAQA's published 9236/1
specimen mark scheme. The wording is Marlbridge's own summary.

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 5–6 | Two points, each explained with accurate, relevant detail and linked to the effect asked about |
| 2 | 3–4 | Clear explanation of at least one point, with some support |
| 1 | 1–2 | Basic points identified, with little development |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing creditable |

**Evaluation (9) — Questions 12 and 13**

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 7–9 | Developed explanation of both sides (or both bullet points); a reasoned judgement that follows from the evidence |
| 2 | 4–6 | Clear explanation with partial support; a judgement that is only partly supported |
| 1 | 1–3 | Basic knowledge with little development; any judgement is asserted |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing creditable |

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing Curzon (partition of Bengal, 1905) with Minto (Simla Deputation, 1906).
- Writing about the 1946 Cabinet Mission Plan when a question asks about the 1942 Cripps Mission.
- Treating the Nehru Report and the Fourteen Points as the same document: the Points were a reply.
- In the 6-mark questions, describing Congress rule without saying why it mattered to the League.
- In the 9-mark questions, arguing only for the factor named in the statement.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Section A really include multiple-choice questions?**
The specification says Sections A and B contain multiple-choice, short and extended response
questions, so be ready for 1-mark items as well as essays.

**How much should I write for a 9-mark answer?**
Enough to explain both sides with dated evidence and reach a judgement, usually four or five
developed paragraphs in about 12 minutes.
