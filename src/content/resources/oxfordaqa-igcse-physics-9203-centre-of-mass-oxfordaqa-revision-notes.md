Condensed recall for **3.1.7 Centre of mass** (P content, full Physics award) in OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203), for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Centre of mass study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-centre-of-mass-oxfordaqa/); exam-style questions are in the [Centre of mass practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-centre-of-mass-oxfordaqa-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Centre of mass | The point at which the mass of an object may be thought to be concentrated; the weight acts there |
| Lamina | A thin, flat sheet of material |
| Uniform | Having its mass spread evenly |
| Axis of symmetry | A line that divides a shape into two mirror-image halves |
| Freely suspended | Hanging from a point so that it can swing without friction stopping it |
| Plumb line | A thread with a small weight, which hangs vertically |
| Line of action | The line along which a force acts |
| Base | The area of contact (or the area enclosed by the contact points) on which an object rests |
| Stability | How hard it is to make an object topple |
| Topple | Fall over because the weight produces a moment that turns the object further |

## Condensed recall

**Definition (a).** The whole mass may be treated as concentrated at one point; draw the weight arrow from there. The point can lie outside the material (a ring, a horseshoe).

**Suspension (b).** A freely suspended object comes to rest with its centre of mass **directly below** the point of suspension. Only then does the weight's line of action pass through the pivot, giving no turning effect.

**Symmetry (c).** The centre of mass of a symmetrical object lies on its axis of symmetry. With two or more axes, it is where they cross. Only valid when the mass is uniform.

**Stability (c).** The position of the centre of mass affects stability. Lower centre of mass and wider base → more stable.

## The lamina method in seven steps

1. Make three holes near the edge, well spread.
2. Hang the lamina from a pin through one hole so it swings freely.
3. Hang a plumb line from the same pin.
4. When still, mark two points along the plumb line.
5. Rule a line through the marks.
6. Repeat for the other holes.
7. The lines cross at the centre of mass.

Third line = check. Lines forming a small triangle → centre of mass is inside it; a smaller triangle means a more precise result.

## Improving the method

- Smooth pin and small holes so the lamina hangs freely.
- Wait for the plumb line to stop swinging.
- Sharp pencil and thin thread for narrow lines.
- Holes far apart so the lines cross at large angles.
- Stiff card so it does not bend.

## Centre of mass of common uniform shapes

| Shape | Centre of mass |
|---|---|
| Rectangle or square | Centre, where the diagonals cross |
| Circle or disc | Centre |
| Ring | Centre of the ring (in the empty space) |
| Metre rule | 50 cm mark |

## Stability in three lines

- Tilt: the line of action of the weight moves towards the edge of the base.
- Inside the base: the weight's moment turns the object back.
- Outside the base: the weight's moment turns it further, and it topples.

## Ways to make an object more stable

| Method | Example |
|---|---|
| Lower the centre of mass | Heavy items stored low in a bus or van |
| Widen the base | Wide wheelbase on a racing car |
| Add mass low down | Heavy base on a desk lamp |

## Exam traps

- **Middle only if uniform and symmetrical.**
- **Below, not beside.** A suspended object hangs with its centre of mass directly below the pivot.
- **Name the plumb line** and the **repeat** in the lamina method.
- **"Top-heavy" is not an explanation.** Use the line of action and the base.
- **Weight acts at the centre of mass**, not at the bottom of the object.

## Self-test

**1.** Define the centre of mass.

**2.** Where does the weight of an object act?

**3.** Where is the centre of mass of a uniform square tile?

**4.** Where is the centre of mass of a uniform ring?

**5.** A picture hangs from a single nail. Where is its centre of mass?

**6.** Explain why a freely suspended object stops with its centre of mass below the pivot.

**7.** In the lamina method, what is the plumb line used for?

**8.** Why are at least two holes needed?

**9.** The three lines form a small triangle. What should you do?

**10.** State two features that make an object stable.

**11.** When does a tilted object topple?

**12.** Why is a symmetry argument not valid for a ruler with a lump of clay stuck at one end?

## Self-test answers

**1.** The point at which the mass of an object may be thought to be concentrated.

**2.** At its centre of mass.

**3.** At its centre, where the diagonals cross.

**4.** At the centre of the ring, in the empty space.

**5.** Directly below the nail.

**6.** In any other position the weight has a moment about the pivot and the object swings; only with the centre of mass directly below is there no turning effect.

**7.** To show the vertical line through the pivot, which passes through the centre of mass.

**8.** One line shows only that the centre of mass is somewhere along it; a second line crossing it fixes the point.

**9.** Take the centre of mass as the middle of the triangle (and repeat more carefully if the triangle is large).

**10.** A low centre of mass and a wide base.

**11.** When the line of action of its weight passes outside its base.

**12.** The mass is no longer uniform, so the centre of mass shifts towards the clay.
