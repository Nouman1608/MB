## Condensed recall

### System view

- Inputs: solar radiation; gases from volcanoes, respiration, decomposition, combustion.
- Storages: gases, water vapour, aerosols, heat.
- Flows: winds, convection, water cycle.
- Outputs: radiation to space; gases taken up by oceans, rocks, organisms.
- Composition stays steady while sources and sinks balance.

### Evolution in five steps

1. Volcanic outgassing: water vapour, CO₂, N₂; almost no free oxygen.
2. Cooling: oceans condense; CO₂ dissolves and is locked in carbonate rocks.
3. Cyanobacteria photosynthesise; oxygen first reacts with iron and other sinks, then builds up (Great Oxidation Event, about 2.4 billion years ago).
4. Ozone forms; UV screened; life spreads onto land.
5. Photosynthesis, respiration, carbon burial and weathering keep gases within life-supporting ranges.

### Variable components

- **Water vapour**: near zero to a few per cent; depends on temperature; acts as a feedback.
- **Aerosols**: dust, sea salt, soot, sulfate; scatter or absorb sunlight; seed clouds; mostly cooling, but soot warms.

### Layers

| Layer | Height (approx.) | Trend | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Troposphere | 0–12 km (higher at equator) | Falls | Heated from ground; rising air cools |
| Stratosphere | 12–50 km | Rises | Ozone absorbs UV |
| Mesosphere | 50–85 km | Falls | Little ozone; thin air |
| Thermosphere | Above 85 km | Rises | Absorption of high-energy solar radiation |

- Boundaries: tropopause, stratopause, mesopause.
- Stratosphere is stably layered, so pollutants there persist for years.

### Lapse rate

- Average about 6.5 °C per km in the troposphere.
- Temperature at height = surface temperature − 6.5 × height (km).
- Short reminder: 18 °C at sea level → 18 − 6.5 × 2 = **5 °C** at 2 km.
- Moist rising air cools more slowly (latent heat released by condensation).

### Pressure

- Highest at the surface; falls with height.
- About half the atmosphere's mass below about 5.5 km; about 90% within the lowest 16 km or so.
- Weather is confined to the troposphere because it holds most mass and water vapour.

### Albedo

| Surface | Albedo |
|---|---|
| Fresh snow | 0.80–0.90 |
| Sea ice | 0.50–0.70 |
| Desert | 0.30–0.40 |
| Grassland | 0.20–0.25 |
| Forest | 0.10–0.20 |
| Open ocean | about 0.06 |

- Mixed surface: overall albedo = Σ(fraction of area × albedo).
- Absorbed fraction = 1 − albedo.

### Infrared absorbers

- Greenhouse gases have three or more atoms (H₂O, CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, O₃): their vibrations change charge distribution, so they absorb infrared.
- N₂ and O₂ (two identical atoms) do not absorb infrared, despite making up about 99% of dry air.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Outgassing | Release of gases from Earth's interior, mainly by volcanoes |
| Great Oxidation Event | Build-up of free oxygen in the atmosphere about 2.4 billion years ago |
| Aerosol | Tiny solid or liquid particles suspended in the air |
| Troposphere | Lowest layer, where weather occurs |
| Stratosphere | Layer above the troposphere containing most ozone |
| Tropopause | Boundary between troposphere and stratosphere |
| Lapse rate | Rate at which temperature falls with height |
| Albedo | Fraction of incoming solar radiation reflected |
| Short-wave radiation | Incoming solar radiation (visible, UV) |
| Long-wave radiation | Infrared emitted by Earth's surface and atmosphere |
| Latent heat | Energy released or absorbed when water changes state |

## Exam traps

- The troposphere is heated from below, not directly by the Sun.
- Stratospheric warming is due to ozone absorbing UV, not to being "closer to the Sun".
- Water vapour is variable and acts as a feedback.
- Abundance does not make a gas a greenhouse gas: N₂ and O₂ are not.
- Albedo is a fraction (0 to 1), not an amount of energy.
- Oxygen accumulated only after the oxygen sinks were filled.

## Self-test

1. Name three gases in Earth's early atmosphere.
2. What removed most early carbon dioxide from the atmosphere?
3. Which organisms first released large amounts of oxygen?
4. Why did the formation of the ozone layer matter for life on land?
5. Why is water vapour described as a feedback rather than a driver of climate change?
6. Give one aerosol that cools and one that warms the atmosphere.
7. Explain why temperature falls with height in the troposphere.
8. Estimate the air temperature at 4 km if it is 30 °C at sea level.
9. Why do pollutants stay in the stratosphere for years?
10. Calculate the overall albedo of a region that is 40% desert (0.35) and 60% grassland (0.20).
11. Why are N₂ and O₂ not greenhouse gases?
12. Why would the thermosphere not feel hot despite its high temperature?

### Answers

1. Any three: water vapour, carbon dioxide, nitrogen.
2. It dissolved in the oceans and was locked into carbonate rocks.
3. Photosynthetic microorganisms, especially cyanobacteria.
4. It screened the surface from harmful UV radiation.
5. Its concentration depends on temperature, so it amplifies changes started by other factors.
6. Cooling: sulfate (or dust, sea salt). Warming: soot (black carbon).
7. The air is heated from below by the ground, and rising air expands and cools.
8. 30 − 6.5 × 4 = 30 − 26 = **4 °C**.
9. Temperature rises with height, so the layer is stable and vertical mixing is limited.
10. (0.40 × 0.35) + (0.60 × 0.20) = 0.14 + 0.12 = **0.26**.
11. Their molecules have two identical atoms, so their vibrations do not change charge distribution and they do not absorb infrared.
12. The air is so thin that there are too few molecules to transfer much heat.
