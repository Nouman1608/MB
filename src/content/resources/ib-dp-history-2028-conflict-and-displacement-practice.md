## Part 1: a paper 1 style set (24 marks)

**Inquiry question: How was displacement experienced by different groups?**

**Source A** (constructed for practice). From the memoir of a survivor of a concentration camp, written in the 1950s, describing life in a displaced persons camp in 1946.

> "We had no country to return to and no family left. The camp gave us bread and a bed, but also a school and a newspaper in our own language. We waited for a ship, and waiting was the hardest part."

**Source B** (constructed for practice). From a report by a relief worker in a refugee camp in Thailand, 1980.

> "The boats arrive at night, overcrowded and short of water. Many families have paid to leave and have nothing left. The mothers say that pirates took what they had. The children are weak but alive."

**Source C** (constructed for practice). From an official statement of a government in a country of first asylum, 1979.

> "We are a small country with limited land. We cannot admit more arrivals unless other countries promise to take them. Our people are generous, but we have our own hardships."

*Context for source C:* The statement was issued before an international conference on refugees and was reported in the national press.

**1.** Explain how the **content** of both source A and source B can be used to answer the inquiry question. **[6]**

**2.** Analyse how the **context** of source C influences how it can be used to answer the inquiry question. **[6]**

**3.** Examine how the **perspectives** in all the sources can be used to answer the inquiry question. **[12]**

## Part 2: short knowledge questions

**4.** Explain one way in which the work of UNRRA affected displaced persons in 1945 and 1946. **[3]**

**5.** Explain one reason why the Hmong left Laos after 1975. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Marking points (6):
- Source A: the survivor had no country or family to return to, showing statelessness and loss **[1]**; the camp provided basic needs and also a school and newspaper in their own language, showing organised community life in a DP camp **[1]**; waiting for a ship shows that emigration depended on others' decisions and was slow **[1]**.
- Source B: boats arrived overcrowded and short of water, showing the dangers of the journey **[1]**; families had paid to leave and lost property, which fits the experience of those who had to buy their escape, such as ethnic Chinese **[1]**; pirates and weak children show the vulnerability of women and children **[1]**.
Markbands: 1 to 2 for description; 3 to 4 for partial explanation (maximum 3 if one source); 5 to 6 for a range of specific content from both sources with developed links.

**2.** Marking points (6):
- Author and audience: a government in a country of first asylum speaking to its own people and to other governments, so it explains limits and seeks help **[1 to 2]**.
- Purpose and timing: issued before a conference, so it was meant to press others to pledge places, and may exaggerate the strain **[1 to 2]**.
- Type: an official statement in the press, so it shows the government's presentation rather than the experience of refugees or the actual numbers **[1 to 2]**.
- Link to the question: it can be used to show how host governments experienced the crisis and how this affected refugees' prospects, but not how refugees themselves experienced displacement **[1]**.
Reward context linked to use.

**3.** Marking points (12):
- Source A: a survivor's perspective in hindsight, stressing loss, community and waiting **[2]**.
- Source B: a relief worker's perspective, observing at first hand, stressing danger and vulnerability, and aiming to move readers **[2]**.
- Source C: a host government's perspective, stressing limited resources and the need for shared responsibility **[2]**.
- Similarities: A and B both show people with little left and dependence on outside help; B and C both refer to hardship (of refugees and of the host) **[2]**.
- Differences: A and B are about refugees' experience, whereas C is about the host's concerns; A is retrospective, and B and C are contemporary; A shows organised community, B shows risk and exploitation **[2]**.
- Combined: displacement was experienced differently according to group and place: as waiting and community in a camp, as perilous flight, and as a burden in the eyes of a host state; a full answer needs all three **[2]**.
Markbands: 1 to 3 minimal; 4 to 6 partial; 7 to 9 at least two sources (maximum 9 for two sources); 10 to 12 insightful understanding of all three sources.

**4.** For example: UNRRA provided food, medical care and shelter in camps **[1]**; it helped to organise the return of many people to their home countries in 1945 **[1]**; it also meant that those who did not wish to return, such as some Jewish survivors, remained in camps awaiting emigration **[1]**.

**5.** For example: many Hmong had fought alongside American-backed forces and feared reprisals after the Pathet Lao took power in 1975 **[1]**; the new government persecuted and attacked highland communities, which led them to flee **[1]**; most crossed the Mekong to Thailand, where camps provided refuge before resettlement **[1]**.
