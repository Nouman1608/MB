## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Where are ordinary dividends paid during the year shown?

A: as an expense in the statement of profit or loss

B: in the statement of changes in equity

C: under current liabilities

D: in an appropriation account

**[1]**

**2.** A company has issued 300,000 ordinary shares of $0.50 each and has called
$0.40 per share. Calls are unpaid on 5,000 shares. What is the paid-up share capital?

A: 150,000

B: 120,000

C: 118,000

D: 148,000

**[1]**

**3.** Define the term "limited liability". **[2]**

**4.** State two differences between ordinary share capital and debentures. **[2]**

**5.** A company has 80,000 of 5% preference shares of $1 each and 400,000 ordinary
shares of $0.50 each. It pays the preference dividend and an ordinary dividend of
$0.04 per share. Calculate the total dividends paid. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why a transfer to the general reserve does not change total equity. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Harlow Tech Ltd provides IT services. At 1 January 2028 its equity was:
ordinary shares of $1 each 300,000; 7% preference shares of $1 each 100,000;
general reserve 25,000; retained earnings 58,000. During 2028:

- On 1 March, 100,000 ordinary shares of $1 were issued at par, fully paid.
- Profit for the year was 96,500.
- The preference dividend for the year was paid.
- An ordinary dividend of $0.03 per share was paid in February, and a further
  $0.05 per share in September.
- 20,000 was transferred to the general reserve.

**(a)** Calculate the total dividends paid in 2028. **[2]**

**(b)** Prepare the statement of changes in equity for the year ended
31 December 2028. **[7]**

**8.** Kirkby Ltd's gross profit for 2028 was 182,000, other income 4,500 and
expenses (including directors' fees) 109,300. On 1 April 2028 it issued 80,000 of 9%
debentures. Debenture interest of 3,600 was paid during the year.

**(a)** Calculate profit from operations. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the debenture interest charge for the year and the amount
accrued. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate profit for the year. **[1]**

**9.** At 31 December 2028 Selby Crafts Ltd had: ordinary shares 150,000; 6%
preference shares 40,000; general reserve 18,000; retained earnings 33,400; 10%
debentures repayable 2034, 50,000; trade payables 21,600; other payables 3,250;
bank overdraft 4,100. Prepare the equity and liabilities section of the statement
of financial position. **[5]**

**10.** Ridgeway Ltd needs 100,000 for new equipment and expects profit from
operations of 60,000 next year. It can issue 100,000 ordinary shares of $1 at par,
or issue 100,000 of 7% debentures.

**(a)** Calculate profit for the year under each option. **[2]**

**(b)** Advise the directors which option to choose. **[4]**

**11.** Broadmere Ltd has issued 600,000 ordinary shares of $0.50 each. It has
called $0.30 per share, and holders of 12,000 shares have not paid the call.
Calculate the issued, called-up and paid-up share capital. **[3]**

**12.** Two partners plan to grow their business by bringing in several new
investors. Explain two advantages to them of forming a limited company. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. Dividends are a distribution of profit to owners, not an
expense. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. 300,000 × 0.40 = 120,000 less 5,000 × 0.40 = 2,000. **[1]**

**3.** A shareholder's liability for the company's debts is limited [1] to the
amount they have paid, or agreed to pay, for their shares, so personal possessions
are protected [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two from: ordinary shareholders are owners, debenture holders are
lenders; dividends vary and may be nil, debenture interest is fixed and must be
paid; ordinary shares are equity, debentures are a liability; debentures are
repaid on an agreed date, ordinary shares are not; ordinary shareholders usually
vote, debenture holders do not [1] [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Preference 80,000 × 5% = 4,000 [1]; ordinary 400,000 × 0.04 = 16,000; total
**20,000** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The transfer reduces retained earnings and increases the general reserve
by the same amount [1]. Both are reserves within equity, and no asset or liability
changes, so total equity stays the same [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Preference 7,000; ordinary: February 300,000 × 0.03 = 9,000 [1] and
September 400,000 × 0.05 = 20,000; total **36,000** [1].

**(b)**

```
Harlow Tech Ltd: Statement of changes in equity for the year ended 31 December 2028
                        Ordinary   Preference   General   Retained      Total
                        shares     shares       reserve   earnings
Balance at 1 Jan 2028   300,000     100,000      25,000     58,000    483,000
Shares issued           100,000                                       100,000
Profit for the year                                         96,500     96,500
Dividends paid                                             (36,000)   (36,000)
Transfer to reserve                              20,000    (20,000)         –
Balance at 31 Dec 2028  400,000     100,000      45,000     98,500    643,500
```

Opening balances and total 483,000 [1]; shares issued 100,000 [1]; profit 96,500 in
retained earnings [1]; dividends 36,000 [1]; transfer shown in both columns [1];
closing component balances [1]; closing total 643,500 [1]. **[9]**

**8. (a)** 182,000 + 4,500 − 109,300 = **77,200** [1].

**(b)** Charge = 80,000 × 9% × 9/12 = **5,400** [1]; accrued = 5,400 − 3,600
= **1,800** [1].

**(c)** 77,200 − 5,400 = **71,800** [1]. **[4]**

**9.**

```

   6% preference shares                    40,000
   General reserve                         18,000

                                          241,400

   10% debentures (2034)                   50,000
```

Share capital lines [1]; reserves and total equity 241,400 [1]; debentures under
non-current liabilities [1]; current liabilities 28,950 [1]; total 320,350 [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** Shares: profit for the year **60,000** [1]. Debentures: interest
100,000 × 7% = 7,000, so profit for the year **53,000** [1].

**(b)** Debentures leave profit 7,000 lower and the interest must be paid even in
a poor year, adding risk [1], but they do not dilute the existing shareholders'
control or share of profit [1]. Shares carry no compulsory return and are never
repaid, but new shareholders vote and share future dividends [1]. If the directors
are confident profit will stay well above 7,000 and want to keep control,
debentures are better; if profit is uncertain, issuing shares is safer [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Issued = 600,000 × 0.50 = **300,000** [1]. Called-up = 600,000 × 0.30
= **180,000** [1]. Paid-up = 180,000 − (12,000 × 0.30) = **176,400** [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Limited liability: the investors' and partners' losses are limited to
their investment [1], so new investors are more willing to put money in [1].
Raising capital: a company can issue shares to many investors [1], providing
larger sums for growth than partners' own savings, and the company continues even
if a shareholder leaves [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Placing dividends or transfers in the statement of profit or loss.
- Calculating an ordinary dividend on the wrong number of shares.
- Forgetting accrued debenture interest.
- Including debentures in equity.
- Confusing called-up and paid-up capital.
