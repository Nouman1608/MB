## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by Kp, and explain why a system described as *homogeneous* has every species in its Kp expression. **[2]**

**2.** Carbon monoxide burns in oxygen: 2CO(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2CO₂(g). Write an expression for Kp and deduce its units when pressures are measured in Pa. **[2]**

**3.** Nitryl chloride decomposes: 2NO₂Cl(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g) + Cl₂(g). An equilibrium mixture contains 0.450 mol NO₂Cl, 0.300 mol NO₂ and 0.150 mol Cl₂ at a total pressure of 120 kPa. Calculate the partial pressure of NO₂Cl and of Cl₂. **[3]**

**4.** Use your answers to question 3, and p(NO₂) = 40.0 kPa, to calculate Kp for the nitryl chloride equilibrium. Give its units. **[3]**

**5.** Propane is dehydrogenated: C₃H₈(g) ⇌ C₃H₆(g) + H₂(g).

**(a)** 2.00 mol of propane is heated in a sealed vessel. At equilibrium, 0.500 mol of hydrogen is present and the total pressure is 150 kPa. Calculate Kp. **[5]**
**(b)** At the same temperature, the total pressure is lowered to 30.0 kPa. Show that a mixture in which half of the original propane has reacted is at equilibrium under these conditions. **[3]**

**6.** A student writes: "Adding a catalyst to a gas-phase equilibrium increases Kp, because more product is made every second." Evaluate this statement. **[3]**

**7.** For 2CO(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2CO₂(g), the forward reaction is exothermic. The temperature is increased at constant total pressure. State and explain the effect on the position of equilibrium and on the value of Kp. **[3]**

**8.** cis-But-2-ene and trans-but-2-ene interconvert in the gas phase:

cis-but-2-ene(g) ⇌ trans-but-2-ene(g)     Kp = 1.50 at temperature T

**(a)** Explain why Kp has no units for this equilibrium. **[1]**
**(b)** 0.800 mol of cis-but-2-ene is sealed in a vessel at temperature T. Calculate the amount of each isomer at equilibrium. **[3]**
**(c)** The vessel is compressed at temperature T. State and explain the effect on the amount of trans-but-2-ene. **[2]**

**9.** Nitrosyl bromide decomposes in a sealed vessel: 2NOBr(g) ⇌ 2NO(g) + Br₂(g). The forward reaction is endothermic. 1.60 mol of NOBr is heated. At equilibrium, 0.400 mol of Br₂ is present and the total pressure is 80.0 kPa.

**(a)** Calculate the amount of NOBr and of NO at equilibrium. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out the partial pressure of every gas in the mixture. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the value of Kp, including units. **[2]**
**(d)** State and explain the effect on Kp of raising the temperature. **[2]**
**(e)** The total pressure is doubled at constant temperature. Use the Kp expression to explain which way the position of equilibrium moves. **[3]**

**10.** Carbon disulfide reacts with hydrogen: CS₂(g) + 4H₂(g) ⇌ CH₄(g) + 2H₂S(g). At a fixed temperature, Kp = 6.25 × 10⁻⁵ kPa⁻². An equilibrium mixture has p(CS₂) = 25.0 kPa, p(H₂) = 40.0 kPa and p(CH₄) = 10.0 kPa.

**(a)** Calculate p(H₂S). **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the total pressure and the mole fraction of H₂S in the mixture. **[2]**
**(c)** Predict how raising the total pressure would change the equilibrium yield of methane. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Kp is the equilibrium constant calculated from the partial pressures of the gases, for a system at constant temperature [1]. Homogeneous means all species are in the same phase, here all gases, so each has a partial pressure that appears in the expression [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* include "at constant temperature" in your definition, because the specification's own wording of Kp contains it.

**2.** **Kp = p(CO₂)² ÷ (p(CO)² × p(O₂))** [1]. Δn = 2 − 3 = −1, so units **Pa⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* work the units out from your own expression; if you write it upside down, the units come out as Pa and both answers are wrong.

**3.** Total amount = 0.450 + 0.300 + 0.150 = 0.900 mol [1]. p(NO₂Cl) = (0.450 ÷ 0.900) × 120 = **60.0 kPa** [1]. p(Cl₂) = (0.150 ÷ 0.900) × 120 = **20.0 kPa** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* a quick check that 60.0 + 40.0 + 20.0 = 120 kPa catches a wrong total before it carries into question 4.

**4.** Kp = p(NO₂)² × p(Cl₂) ÷ p(NO₂Cl)² [1] = 40.0² × 20.0 ÷ 60.0² = 32 000 ÷ 3600 = **8.89** [1]; units **kPa** (Δn = 3 − 2 = +1) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* write the expression in symbols before substituting, so your method is visible even if a value carried from question 3 is wrong.

**5. (a)** Change row from the 1 : 1 : 1 ratio: 0.500 mol of propane reacts, giving 0.500 mol C₃H₆ and 0.500 mol H₂ [1]. Equilibrium: C₃H₈ 1.50 mol, total = 2.50 mol [1]. Partial pressures: p(C₃H₈) = (1.50 ÷ 2.50) × 150 = 90.0 kPa; p(C₃H₆) = p(H₂) = (0.500 ÷ 2.50) × 150 = 30.0 kPa [1]. Kp = p(C₃H₆) × p(H₂) ÷ p(C₃H₈) = 30.0 × 30.0 ÷ 90.0 [1] = **10.0 kPa** [1].
**(b)** Per 1 mol of propane at the start: 0.500 mol C₃H₈, 0.500 mol C₃H₆, 0.500 mol H₂; total 1.500 mol, so each mole fraction is 1/3 [1]. Each partial pressure = (1/3) × 30.0 = 10.0 kPa [1]. 10.0 × 10.0 ÷ 10.0 = 10.0 kPa, which equals Kp, so the mixture is at equilibrium [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* on a "show that", finish by comparing your value with Kp in words; a bare number with no conclusion does not show anything.

**6.** The statement is wrong about Kp: a catalyst does not change the value of Kp [1]. It raises the rates of the forward and reverse reactions by the same factor [1], so the system arrives at equilibrium in less time while the equilibrium partial pressures, and so the yield, are unchanged [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" needs a judgement as well as reasons; agreeing that "more product per second" means a higher yield gains nothing.

**7.** The position of equilibrium moves to the left [1], because raising the temperature favours the endothermic direction, which is the reverse reaction [1]. The value of Kp decreases [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* name the endothermic direction (or say the reverse reaction absorbs heat); "to oppose the change" on its own does not explain which way the equilibrium moves.

**8. (a)** There is 1 mole of gas on each side, so the pressure units cancel [1].
**(b)** Let y mol of cis convert: Kp = y ÷ (0.800 − y) = 1.50 [1]. y = 1.20 − 1.50y, so y = 0.480 [1]. Equilibrium: **0.480 mol trans** and **0.320 mol cis** [1].
**(c)** No change in the amount of trans-but-2-ene [1]. The total pressure cancels from the Kp expression because both sides have equal moles of gas, so compression does not shift the position [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* in part (b) you can work in moles because Δn = 0; say so, or show that the total pressure cancels, so the method is clear.

**9. (a)** 0.400 mol Br₂ forms from 0.800 mol NOBr, which also gives 0.800 mol NO [1]. NOBr left = 1.60 − 0.800 = **0.800 mol**; NO = **0.800 mol** [1].
**(b)** Total = 0.800 + 0.800 + 0.400 = 2.00 mol [1]. p(NOBr) = p(NO) = (0.800 ÷ 2.00) × 80.0 = **32.0 kPa**; p(Br₂) = (0.400 ÷ 2.00) × 80.0 = **16.0 kPa** [1].
**(c)** Kp = p(NO)² × p(Br₂) ÷ p(NOBr)² = 32.0² × 16.0 ÷ 32.0² [1] = **16.0 kPa** [1].
**(d)** Kp increases [1], because the equilibrium moves in the endothermic, forward direction, raising the partial pressures of the products relative to the reactant [1].
**(e)** Writing each p as (mole fraction × P) gives Kp = (mole-fraction terms) × P, because Δn = +1 [1]. If P doubles with the mole fractions unchanged, the right-hand side becomes 32.0 kPa, larger than Kp [1]. The mole fractions of NO and Br₂ must fall and that of NOBr must rise, so the position moves to the left, the side with fewer moles of gas [1]. **[11]**
*Examiner insight:* in part (e), a correct Le Chatelier answer alone may not gain full credit when the question asks you to use the Kp expression; show where the factor of P comes from.

**10. (a)** p(H₂S)² = Kp × p(CS₂) × p(H₂)⁴ ÷ p(CH₄) [1] = 6.25 × 10⁻⁵ × 25.0 × 40.0⁴ ÷ 10.0 = 400 [1]. p(H₂S) = **20.0 kPa** [1].
**(b)** Total pressure = 25.0 + 40.0 + 10.0 + 20.0 = **95.0 kPa** [1]. Mole fraction of H₂S = 20.0 ÷ 95.0 = **0.211** [1].
**(c)** The yield of methane increases, because the position moves right, to the side with fewer moles of gas (3 rather than 5) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* forgetting the square root in part (a) gives 400 kPa, a value larger than the whole mixture; a sense check on size would catch it.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Totalling the starting amounts rather than the equilibrium amounts.
- Applying a 1 : 1 change when the equation's ratio is 2 : 2 : 1 or 1 : 4.
- Leaving a power off a partial pressure, such as p(H₂)⁴.
- Units with the wrong sign when Δn is negative, or units given when Δn = 0.
- Mixing Pa and kPa within one substitution.
- No closing comparison with Kp on a "show that" question.
- Stating that a pressure change alters Kp, or that a catalyst raises the yield.
- Temperature answers that never name the endothermic or exothermic direction.
- Missing the square root when the unknown appears squared.
