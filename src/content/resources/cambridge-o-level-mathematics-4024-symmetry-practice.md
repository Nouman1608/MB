These questions cover section 4.5, Symmetry, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The ideas are explained in the [symmetry study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-symmetry/), and the [symmetry revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-symmetry-revision-notes/) summarise them. Show your working where a calculation or reason is needed.

## Section A

**1.** For each shape, write down the number of lines of symmetry and the order of rotational symmetry.

**(a)** an equilateral triangle

**(b)** a rectangle that is not a square

**(c)** a parallelogram that is not a rhombus

**(d)** a kite that is not a rhombus

**(e)** a regular hexagon **[5]**

**2.** For each capital letter, written in a simple block style, state the number of lines of symmetry and the order of rotational symmetry.

**(a)** H

**(b)** N

**(c)** A **[3]**

**3.** A regular polygon has rotational symmetry of order 8.

**(a)** Find the smallest angle through which it can be turned to fit onto itself.

**(b)** Find the size of one interior angle. **[3]**

**4.** The smallest angle through which a shape can be turned to fit onto itself is 72°. Write down its order of rotational symmetry. **[1]**

**5.** State the number of planes of symmetry of

**(a)** a cuboid measuring 5 cm by 3 cm by 2 cm,

**(b)** a square-based pyramid with its apex above the centre of the base,

**(c)** a cone. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** Triangle ABC is isosceles with AB = AC and angle BAC = 40°. Use the line of symmetry through A to find angle ABC. **[2]**

**7.** The line x = 3 is a line of symmetry of a shape. A, B and C are points on the shape, with A (1, 2), B (2, 5) and C (3, 4). Find the points on the shape that match A, B and C. **[3]**

**8.** A shape has rotational symmetry of order 4 about the origin. The point (2, 1) is on the shape. Find the other three points that must be on the shape. **[3]**

**9.** ABCD is a rhombus with angle DAB = 64°. The diagonals meet at M.

**(a)** Find angle MAB.

**(b)** Find angle ABD.

**(c)** State the size of angle AMB and say which symmetry property of the rhombus explains it. **[4]**

**10.** A prism has a cross-section that is an equilateral triangle.

**(a)** State the number of planes of symmetry of the prism.

**(b)** State the order of rotational symmetry about the axis that runs along its length. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why a parallelogram that is not a rectangle or a rhombus has no line of symmetry but does have rotational symmetry. **[2]**

**12.** Name a quadrilateral that

**(a)** has exactly two lines of symmetry and no right angles,

**(b)** has exactly one line of symmetry, which is one of its diagonals. **[2]**

**13.** A solid cylinder is a circular prism.

**(a)** Describe its planes of symmetry.

**(b)** Describe an axis of symmetry that passes through the centres of both circular faces. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 3 lines, order 3 [1]. **(b)** 2 lines, order 2 [1]. **(c)** 0 lines, order 2 [1]. **(d)** 1 line, order 1 [1]. **(e)** 6 lines, order 6 [1].

**2. (a)** H: 2 lines, order 2 [1]. **(b)** N: 0 lines, order 2 [1]. **(c)** A: 1 line, order 1 [1].

**3. (a)** 360 ÷ 8 = **45°** [1]. **(b)** Exterior angle = 45° [1], so interior angle = 180 − 45 = **135°** [1].

**4.** 360 ÷ 72 = **5** [1].

**5. (a)** **3** [1]. **(b)** **4** [1]. **(c)** **Infinitely many**, all passing through its axis [1].

**6.** The line of symmetry makes angle ABC equal to angle ACB [1]. 2 × ABC = 180 − 40 = 140, so angle ABC = **70°** [1].

**7.** Reflection in x = 3 sends (x, y) to (6 − x, y) [1]. A → **(5, 2)** and B → **(4, 5)** [1]. C is on the line, so it matches itself, **(3, 4)** [1].

**8.** A quarter turn about the origin sends (x, y) to (−y, x), or in the other direction (y, −x) [1]. The three points are **(−1, 2), (−2, −1) and (1, −2)** [1][1].

**9. (a)** AC bisects angle DAB, so angle MAB = 64 ÷ 2 = **32°** [1].

**(b)** Angle ABC = 180 − 64 = 116° because co-interior angles sum to 180° [1], and BD bisects it, so angle ABD = **58°** [1].

**(c)** **90°** [1]. The diagonals are the lines of symmetry of a rhombus, so they meet at right angles. Check: 32 + 58 + 90 = 180.

*Examiner insight:* Since the diagonals are lines of symmetry, either diagonal reflects one half of the rhombus onto the other, and the diagonals cross at 90°.

**10. (a)** **4** (three through the length, one halfway along) [1]. **(b)** **3** [1].

**11.** Reflecting a parallelogram in a diagonal or in a line through the midpoints of opposite sides does not map it onto itself, because adjacent sides have different lengths and the angles are not right angles [1]. Turning it through 180° about the point where the diagonals cross does map it onto itself, so it has rotational symmetry of order 2 [1].

**12. (a)** A **rhombus** [1]. **(b)** A **kite** [1].

**13. (a)** Every plane containing the axis through the centres of the circular faces is a plane of symmetry, so there are infinitely many [1], and there is one more plane halfway along the length, parallel to the circular faces [1].

**(b)** The axis through the centres of the circles: the cylinder fits onto itself after a turn through any angle [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting the diagonals of a rectangle or parallelogram as lines of symmetry.
- Writing order 0 for a shape with no rotational symmetry. The order is 1.
- Leaving out the plane halfway along a prism or cylinder.
- Using 180° ÷ n instead of 360° ÷ n for the smallest turn of a regular polygon.
- Confusing a plane (a surface) with an axis (a line).
