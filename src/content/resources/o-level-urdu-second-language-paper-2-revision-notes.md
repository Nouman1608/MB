## Paper 2 at a glance

**1 hour 30 minutes, 50 marks, 50%** of the qualification. **All four exercises are compulsory** — no
choice. Exercises 1-3: questions and answers in **Urdu**. Exercise 4: question in **English**, answer
in **Urdu**.

## Which spec is this?

Cambridge runs **two** separate O Level Urdu specs: **3247 First Language** (Paper 2 = Texts) and
**3248 Second Language** (Paper 2 = Grammar, Writing and Translation, this resource). Confirm which
one your school has entered you for before using this guide — 3247 candidates need the Texts guide
instead.

## The four exercises, by mark value

| Exercise | Marks | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Sentence transformation | 5 | Rewrite 5 sentences per a given prompt, preserving meaning exactly |
| 2. Multiple-choice cloze | 10 | Fill gaps in a passage from options — tests grammar in context |
| 3. Extended writing | 20 | ~200 words continuous prose, set purpose/format/audience |
| 4. Translation | 15 | ~150-word English passage translated into Urdu |

**Exercises 3 and 4 together carry 35 of 50 marks** — prioritise timed practice on these once
Exercises 1-2's grammar points are secure.

## Exercise 1 — precision over volume

Only 5 sentences, each worth a full mark — no partial credit cushion like longer exercises. Preserve
the **original meaning exactly** while changing the required structure (tense, voice, sentence type).

## Exercise 2 — read beyond the gap

Named grammar points to expect: verb conjugation, subject/verb agreement, comparatives, superlatives,
parts of speech, nouns, prepositions, postpositions, articles, context-appropriate vocabulary. An
option can be **grammatically valid in isolation but wrong for the passage** — always read the full
sentence around each gap before choosing.

## Exercise 3 — match the purpose exactly

Purpose, format and audience are specified on the paper (e.g. a letter, diary entry, description for a
particular reader). Prepare vocabulary and structures across narrative, descriptive, persuasive and
informational purposes — the specific one set varies by series, so don't only prepare one.

## Exercise 4 — natural Urdu, not literal Urdu

A literal, word-for-word translation that produces unnatural Urdu is marked **down** compared with an
accurate translation that also reads naturally. Precision on a short 150-word passage matters more
than speed.

## Why 3248 is a genuinely separate exercise structure

Marlbridge's existing O-Level Urdu resources cover Paper 1 (Reading and Writing, shared between 3247
and 3248) and 3247's own Paper 2 (Texts). This guide is specifically for 3248's own distinct second
paper, which has no equivalent in 3247 — 3247's Paper 2 tests literary set texts, while 3248's Paper 2
tests applied language skills: grammar in context, extended writing, and translation from English into
Urdu. Because the two Paper 2s test such different skills, revision materials genuinely do not
transfer between the two routes even though both share the same Paper 1. Confirm your route before
building a revision plan around this guide.

## Building a personal grammar checklist for Exercise 2

Because the cloze passage draws on a fixed, named list of grammar points — verb conjugation,
subject/verb agreement, comparatives, superlatives, parts of speech, nouns, prepositions,
postpositions, articles and context vocabulary — the most efficient way to prepare is to build a
personal checklist from this exact list and test yourself against each item individually, rather than
revising "Urdu grammar" as one undifferentiated block. Since every cloze gap is designed to probe one
of these named points, working through the list systematically means very little of what appears in
the exercise will be genuinely unfamiliar on exam day.

## Exam traps

- Changing a sentence's meaning while transforming its structure in Exercise 1.
- Choosing a grammatically-possible-but-contextually-wrong cloze option in Exercise 2.
- Ignoring the specified purpose/format/audience in Exercise 3, which caps marks regardless of language
  accuracy.
- Translating word-for-word in Exercise 4 instead of producing natural Urdu.

## Where Paper 2's marks are concentrated

With Exercises 3 and 4 together carrying 35 of the paper's 50 marks, revision time should be weighted
accordingly rather than split evenly across all four exercises. A candidate who has fully secured
Exercises 1 and 2's grammar points but has not practised extended writing and translation under timed
conditions is under-prepared for where most of the marks actually sit — treat Exercises 1-2 as a
foundation to clear quickly and confidently, and Exercises 3-4 as the exercises deserving sustained,
repeated timed practice in the weeks before the exam.

## Common translation-question errors

Candidates lose translation marks less often on vocabulary and more often on structure: writing an
English sentence with an Urdu word order left intact (rather than restructuring for natural Urdu word
order), missing the correct verb agreement with gender and number, and translating idiomatic English
phrases word-for-word instead of using the equivalent Urdu idiom or phrasing that actually carries the
same meaning. Reading your translation aloud as if it were a standalone Urdu sentence — separate from
the English original — is the fastest way to catch this kind of unnatural-sounding structure.

## Self-test

1. How many marks does Paper 2 carry, and what percentage of the qualification?
2. Which exercises use Urdu-only questions, and which uses English?
3. What must Exercise 1's transformations always preserve?
4. Name the specific grammar points listed for Exercise 2.
5. Why might a grammatically correct cloze answer still be wrong?

**Answers:** 1. 50 marks, 50%. 2. Exercises 1-3 are Urdu-only; Exercise 4's question is in English. 3.
The original sentence's meaning. 4. Verb conjugation, subject/verb agreement, comparatives,
superlatives, parts of speech, nouns, prepositions, postpositions, articles, context vocabulary. 5. It
may not fit the specific context of the passage around the gap.
