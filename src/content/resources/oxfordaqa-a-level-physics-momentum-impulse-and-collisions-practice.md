## Questions

**1.** A cyclist and bike of total mass 58 kg move at 6.5 m s⁻¹. Calculate the momentum, and the speed of a 900 kg car with the same momentum. **[3]**

**2.** A 0.60 kg trolley moving at 2.0 m s⁻¹ hits a stationary 0.90 kg trolley. They stick together. Calculate their common speed and the kinetic energy lost. **[4]**

**3.** A dodgem car of mass 210 kg moves right at 2.0 m s⁻¹ and hits a 160 kg car moving left at 1.5 m s⁻¹. The first car rebounds at 0.20 m s⁻¹ to the left.

**(a)** Calculate the velocity of the second car after the collision. **[3]**
**(b)** Show that the collision is inelastic. **[2]**

**4.** A cannon of mass 1200 kg fires an 18 kg ball horizontally at 85 m s⁻¹. The cannon is free to recoil.

**(a)** Calculate the recoil speed. **[2]**
**(b)** Compare the kinetic energies of ball and cannon and explain where the energy came from. **[2]**

**5.** A footballer kicks a stationary 0.44 kg ball, which leaves at 26 m s⁻¹. The boot touches the ball for 8.0 ms.

**(a)** Calculate the impulse on the ball. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the average force. **[2]**

**6.** A 70 kg crash-test dummy travelling at 16 m s⁻¹ is stopped in 0.080 s by a belt and crumple zone.

**(a)** Calculate the average force on the dummy. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the force if the stop took 0.020 s, and explain why crumple zones are used. **[3]**

**7.** A force acts on a 0.35 kg ball, initially at rest. It is recorded every 1.0 ms.

| t / ms | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F / N | 0 | 300 | 700 | 900 | 650 | 250 | 0 |

**(a)** Use the trapezium rule to estimate the impulse. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the final speed of the ball. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the average force. **[1]**

**8.** A 1.8 kg item is dropped 0.50 m onto a foam base and stops in 25 ms. Calculate the force the base exerts on it. **[4]**

**9.** A hose directs 0.45 kg of water per second at 9.0 m s⁻¹ onto a wall. The water stops and runs down. Calculate the force on the wall. **[3]**

**10.** A 0.50 kg puck moving at 4.0 m s⁻¹ hits a stationary 1.5 kg puck head-on. After the collision the first puck moves back at 2.0 m s⁻¹ and the second moves forward at 2.0 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Show that momentum and kinetic energy are both conserved. **[4]**
**(b)** State how the speeds of approach and separation confirm that the collision is elastic. **[1]**
**(c)** The pucks now stick together on impact instead. Calculate their speed and the kinetic energy lost. **[3]**

**11.** A 0.020 kg bullet moving at 300 m s⁻¹ embeds in a 1.98 kg block at rest on a smooth surface.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the block and bullet together. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the kinetic energy lost. **[3]**
**(c)** The block is then stopped by a buffer in 0.050 s. Calculate the average force on the buffer. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the force would be greater if the block rebounded. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** p = 58 × 6.5 = 377 kg m s⁻¹ [1]. v = p/m = 377 / 900 [1] = **0.42 m s⁻¹** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the unit with each answer; a correct number without its unit is incomplete.

**2.** Before: 0.60 × 2.0 = 1.2 kg m s⁻¹ [1]. After: (0.60 + 0.90)v = 1.2, so v = **0.80 m s⁻¹** [1]. KE before = ½ × 0.60 × 2.0² = 1.2 J, after = ½ × 1.5 × 0.80² = 0.48 J [1]. Lost = **0.72 J** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Calculate the two energies separately and subtract; this is easier to credit than a single line of mixed working.

**3. (a)** Right positive. Before: 210 × 2.0 + 160 × (−1.5) = 420 − 240 = 180 [1]. After: 210 × (−0.20) + 160v = −42 + 160v [1]. v = 222 / 160 = **+1.4 m s⁻¹**, to the right [1]
**(b)** KE before = ½ × 210 × 2.0² + ½ × 160 × 1.5² = 420 + 180 = 600 J [1]. KE after = 4.2 + ½ × 160 × 1.3875² = 158 J, less than 600 J, so inelastic [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" needs numerical values for both energies and an explicit comparison; the statement alone is not enough.

**4. (a)** Ball direction positive. 0 = 18 × 85 + 1200v [1]. v = **−1.3 m s⁻¹**, so the cannon recoils at 1.3 m s⁻¹ [1]
**(b)** KE ball = ½ × 18 × 85² = 65 000 J; KE cannon = ½ × 1200 × 1.275² = 980 J [1]. The energy comes from the chemical energy of the propellant, which is why KE increases although momentum is unchanged [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say which store the energy came from. Writing only "energy is conserved" does not explain why kinetic energy was zero before.

**5. (a)** Impulse = Δ(mv) = 0.44 × 26 − 0 [1] = **11 N s** (11.44) [1]
**(b)** F = impulse / Δt = 11.44 / 0.0080 [1] = **1.4 × 10³ N** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The conversion from ms to s is the usual place to lose the answer by a factor of 1000.

**6. (a)** Δp = 70 × 16 = 1120 kg m s⁻¹ [1]. F = 1120 / 0.080 = **1.4 × 10⁴ N** [1]
**(b)** F = 1120 / 0.020 = **5.6 × 10⁴ N** [1]. The momentum change is the same, but a longer stopping time [1] means a smaller rate of change of momentum, so a smaller force on the occupant [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong explanation names both quantities: the change in momentum stays fixed while the time changes.

**7. (a)** Strips of 0.0010 s. Impulse = 0.0010 × [½(0 + 0) + 300 + 700 + 900 + 650 + 250] [1] = 0.0010 × 2800 [1] = **2.8 N s** [1]
**(b)** v = impulse / m = 2.8 / 0.35 [1] = **8.0 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** F = 2.8 / 0.0060 = **470 N** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The average force is much lower than the 900 N peak. Check that your answer is plausible against the data.

**8.** v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.81 × 0.50) = 3.13 m s⁻¹ [1]. Δp = 1.8 × 3.13 = 5.64 kg m s⁻¹ [1]. Resultant force = 5.64 / 0.025 = 226 N upwards [1]. The base also supports the weight 1.8 × 9.81 = 17.7 N, so the force from the base = 226 + 17.7 = **240 N** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* If the question asks for the force from the surface, add the weight; if it asks for the resultant force, do not.

**9.** Momentum lost per second = 0.45 × 9.0 [1] = 4.05 kg m s⁻¹ per second [1]. F = Δ(mv)/Δt = **4.1 N**, acting on the wall away from the hose [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the mass per second explicitly; it shows you are using the rate of change of momentum rather than F = ma.

**10. (a)** Before: 0.50 × 4.0 = 2.0 kg m s⁻¹. After: 0.50 × (−2.0) + 1.5 × 2.0 = 2.0 kg m s⁻¹ [1], so momentum is conserved [1]. KE before = ½ × 0.50 × 16 = 4.0 J [1]. KE after = ½ × 0.50 × 4 + ½ × 1.5 × 4 = 1.0 + 3.0 = 4.0 J, so conserved [1]
**(b)** Approach speed = 4.0 m s⁻¹ and separation speed = 2.0 − (−2.0) = 4.0 m s⁻¹, which are equal [1]
**(c)** 2.0 = 2.0v, so v = **1.0 m s⁻¹** [1]. KE after = ½ × 2.0 × 1.0² = 1.0 J [1]. Lost = 4.0 − 1.0 = **3.0 J** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Show both totals for each conserved quantity. A sign slip on the rebounding puck is the usual reason the totals fail to match.

**11. (a)** 0.020 × 300 = 6.0 kg m s⁻¹ [1]. v = 6.0 / 2.0 = **3.0 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** KE before = ½ × 0.020 × 300² = 900 J [1]. KE after = ½ × 2.0 × 3.0² = 9.0 J [1]. Lost = **891 J** [1]
**(c)** Δp = 2.0 × 3.0 = 6.0 kg m s⁻¹ [1]. F = 6.0 / 0.050 = **120 N** [1]
**(d)** A rebound makes the change in momentum larger (from +mv to −mv), so the same contact time needs a larger force [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In (d) compare the changes in momentum, not just the final speeds.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using one sign for velocities in opposite directions.
- Leaving out the direction of a velocity, or quoting only a speed when direction is wanted.
- Forgetting to convert milliseconds to seconds.
- Using the speed rather than the velocity change when a body rebounds.
- Assuming kinetic energy is conserved in an inelastic collision or explosion.
- Stating the answer without comparing the two kinetic energy totals when asked to show a collision is elastic.
- Using the peak force of a graph as the average force.
- Leaving out weight when a vertical impact asks for the force from the surface.
- Explaining crumple zones in terms of "absorbing" force instead of increasing time.
