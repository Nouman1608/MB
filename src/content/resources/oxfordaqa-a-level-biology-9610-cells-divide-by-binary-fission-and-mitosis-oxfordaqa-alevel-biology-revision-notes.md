These notes condense section 3.2.10 (Cells divide by binary fission and mitosis) of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Biology (9610) specification, Version 5.1, in Unit 2. For worked counting and timing examples, use the [binary fission and mitosis study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-biology-9610-cells-divide-by-binary-fission-and-mitosis-oxfordaqa-alevel-biology/); then try the [binary fission and mitosis practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-biology-9610-cells-divide-by-binary-fission-and-mitosis-oxfordaqa-alevel-biology-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cell cycle | The sequence of interphase and division shown by eukaryotic cells that retain the ability to divide |
| Interphase | The period between mitotic divisions: G1, S and G2 |
| Mitosis | The part of the cell cycle in which a eukaryotic cell divides to produce two daughter cells, each with identical DNA |
| Chromatid | One of the two identical copies of a chromosome after DNA replication |
| Centromere | The region joining two sister chromatids, where spindle fibres attach |
| Spindle fibres | Protein fibres that attach to centromeres and separate chromatids |
| Binary fission | Division of a prokaryotic cell after its circular DNA and plasmids replicate |
| Conjugation | Transfer of DNA from one bacterium to another, possibly of a different species |
| Horizontal gene transmission | Movement of genes between existing cells, not from parent to offspring |

## Interphase

| Phase | Events |
|---|---|
| G1 | Protein content increases; more organelles such as mitochondria and ribosomes; cell grows |
| S | DNA replicated; each chromosome now two chromatids |
| G2 | Rapid cell growth and protein synthesis |

Interphase is not a resting stage, and most of the cycle is spent in it.

## Mitosis stage by stage

| Stage | Chromosomes | Spindle and nucleus |
|---|---|---|
| Prophase | Condense, visible as two chromatids joined at a centromere | Spindle forms; nuclear envelope breaks down |
| Metaphase | Line up at the equator | Fibres attached to centromeres |
| Anaphase | Centromeres divide; chromatids pulled to opposite poles, centromere first | Fibres shorten |
| Telophase | Reach poles; become less distinct | Spindle breaks down; nuclear envelopes reform |

Then the cytoplasm divides: two genetically identical cells.

## Counting rules (2n chromosomes)

| Point in cycle | Chromosomes | Chromatids | DNA |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | 2n | – | x |
| G2 to metaphase | 2n | 4n | 2x |
| Anaphase (whole cell) | 4n separating | – | 2x |
| Each daughter cell | 2n | – | x |

DNA doubles in S phase; chromosome number in the cell doubles at anaphase; DNA per cell halves when the cytoplasm divides.

## Identifying stages from photographs

- Nucleus visible, no chromosomes: interphase.
- Chromosomes visible, scattered: prophase.
- Single line at the middle: metaphase.
- Two V-shaped groups moving apart: anaphase.
- Two groups at the ends, new nuclei or a dividing line forming: telophase.

**Timing stages:** time in a stage = (cells in that stage ÷ total cells counted) × total time.

**Worked reminder.** 200 cells counted in a tissue with a 24-hour cycle; 30 are in mitosis. Mitosis lasts 30 ÷ 200 × 24 = 3.6 hours.

## Binary fission

1. Circular DNA replicates.
2. Plasmids replicate.
3. Cytoplasm divides: two cells, each with one copy of the circular DNA; plasmid numbers may vary.

| | Mitosis | Binary fission |
|---|---|---|
| Cells | Eukaryotic | Prokaryotic |
| DNA | Linear chromosomes in a nucleus | Circular DNA and plasmids |
| Spindle | Present | Absent |
| Result | Identical daughter cells | One circular DNA copy each; plasmids may differ |

## Conjugation

- DNA, often a plasmid copy, passes from one bacterium to another.
- Can cross species boundaries.
- Horizontal gene transmission, unlike vertical transmission in binary fission.
- Can spread genes such as antibiotic resistance rapidly through mixed populations.

## Exam traps

- "DNA replicates in prophase." It replicates in S phase.
- "46 chromatids in metaphase." A human cell has 46 chromosomes and 92 chromatids.
- "Chromosomes separate in metaphase." They line up in metaphase and separate in anaphase.
- "Spindle fibres attach to the chromosome ends." They attach to centromeres.
- "Bacteria divide by mitosis." They divide by binary fission.
- "Each daughter bacterium gets the same plasmids." Plasmid numbers may vary.
- "Conjugation is a form of reproduction." It transfers DNA; no new cells are made.

## Quick self-test

1. In which phase of the cell cycle is DNA replicated?
2. State two events of G1.
3. What attaches spindle fibres to a chromosome?
4. In which stage do sister chromatids separate?
5. A cell has 2n = 20. How many chromatids are present in metaphase?
6. A G1 nucleus contains 3.2 pg of DNA. How much is in a G2 nucleus?
7. Describe how you would recognise metaphase in a photograph.
8. 400 cells counted; 24 in anaphase; cycle 20 hours. How long is anaphase?
9. Give two differences between binary fission and mitosis.
10. Why might two daughter bacteria contain different numbers of a plasmid?
11. What is meant by horizontal gene transmission?
12. Why are daughter cells from mitosis genetically identical?

### Answers

1. S phase.
2. Any two: the cell grows; protein content increases; more organelles such as mitochondria and ribosomes are made.
3. The centromere.
4. Anaphase.
5. **40**.
6. **6.4 pg**.
7. Chromosomes lined up in a single row across the middle of the cell.
8. 24 ÷ 400 × 20 = **1.2 hours**.
9. Any two: binary fission has no spindle; it involves circular DNA not linear chromosomes; there is no nucleus and no stages such as prophase; plasmids replicate and their number may differ between daughter cells.
10. Plasmids are not shared out precisely between the two cells during division.
11. Movement of genes between existing cells, possibly of different species, rather than from parent to offspring.
12. DNA is replicated in S phase, and spindle fibres separate the sister chromatids so each daughter cell receives one copy of every chromosome.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is interphase part of mitosis?**
No. Interphase is the period between mitotic divisions. Mitosis is the division part of the cell cycle.

**What does conjugation have to do with antibiotic resistance?**
A resistance gene on a plasmid can be passed to other bacteria, including other species, by conjugation, so resistance can spread quickly.
