## Questions

**1.** **(a)** State the division of the nervous system (central, somatic or autonomic) to which each belongs: (i) the cerebellum, (ii) a motor neurone to the biceps, (iii) a neurone from the brain stem that slows the heart, (iv) the spinal cord. **[4]**
**(b)** State one structural difference between the motor pathway to a skeletal muscle and the motor pathway to cardiac muscle. **[1]**

**2.** Three patients each have damage to one part of the brain. Name the part most likely to be damaged and explain the symptom.
**(a)** Patient A understands speech and can walk, but staggers and misses a cup when reaching for it. **[2]**
**(b)** Patient B has a body temperature that no longer returns to normal after being in a cold room. **[2]**
**(c)** Patient C stops breathing after a head injury, although the lungs and the diaphragm are undamaged. **[2]**

**3.** A reflex hammer taps the patellar tendon of five volunteers. The time from the tap to the start of muscle activity (the latency) is recorded. In a second test, the same volunteers press a button when a light flashes.

| Volunteer | Knee jerk latency / ms | Button press / ms |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 38 | 212 |
| 2 | 41 | 236 |
| 3 | 36 | 198 |
| 4 | 44 | 241 |
| 5 | 42 | 223 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean latency for each test. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times longer the button press takes than the knee jerk, using the means. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the knee jerk is faster. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain the survival value of reflex actions. **[2]**

**4.** A photomicrograph of skeletal muscle has a scale bar of 2.0 µm that measures 30 mm. A relaxed sarcomere measures 34.5 mm and a contracted one 27 mm.
**(a)** Calculate the magnification. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage decrease in sarcomere length on contraction. **[3]**
**(c)** State two features that would distinguish cardiac muscle from this skeletal muscle in a stained section. **[2]**

**5.** Data supplied for one myofibril are in the table. Each thin filament is 0.9 µm long and the A band is 1.5 µm in every state.

| Sarcomere length / µm | I band / µm | H zone / µm |
|---|---|---|
| 3.2 | | |
| 2.6 | | |
| 2.0 | | |
| 1.8 | | |

**(a)** Complete the table, using sarcomere = A band + I band and H zone = sarcomere − 2 × thin filament length. **[2]**
**(b)** State what the constant A band shows about the filaments. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain the changes in I band and H zone with the sliding filament model. **[2]**

**6.** A motor neurone fires. Give a sequenced account, with reasons, of the events that end with the sarcomeres of a skeletal muscle fibre getting shorter. **[8]**

**7.** A muscle holds 3.6 mmol of ATP and 18.0 mmol of creatine phosphate per kg, and uses ATP at 2.4 mmol per kg per second.
**(a)** Calculate how long the ATP alone would last, and how long ATP plus creatine phosphate would last, assuming one creatine phosphate makes one ATP. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how creatine phosphate maintains the supply of ATP, and why it is not enough for a 400 m race. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how heart rate increases at the start of exercise and returns to resting level afterwards. **[6]**

**9.** Five volunteers had their pulse counted for 30 s at rest, immediately after 3 minutes of exercise, and 5 minutes later.

| Volunteer | Rest | After exercise | 5 minutes later |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 36 | 84 | 46 |
| B | 33 | 79 | 42 |
| C | 39 | 90 | 51 |
| D | 31 | 75 | 40 |
| E | 35 | 83 | 45 |

**(a)** Convert the resting counts to beats per minute and find the mean. **[2]**
**(b)** The mean rate after exercise is 164.4 beats per minute. Calculate the percentage increase from rest. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest two ways to make the investigation more reliable. **[2]**
**(d)** Describe what the 5-minute data show. **[1]**

**10.** In an experiment, liver cell extracts were exposed to adrenaline and the cAMP produced was measured.

| Adrenaline / nmol dm⁻³ | 0 | 1 | 10 | 100 | 1000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cAMP / µmol per g | 0.5 | 2.1 | 7.9 | 15.2 | 15.8 |

**(a)** Outline the steps linking adrenaline arriving at a liver cell to cAMP being made. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe and suggest an explanation for the trend between 100 and 1000 nmol dm⁻³. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why adrenaline needs a second messenger. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** (i) central [1]; (ii) somatic [1]; (iii) autonomic [1]; (iv) central [1]. **(b)** Skeletal: one motor neurone from the CNS to the muscle, whereas cardiac has two neurones with a synapse in a ganglion [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* name the division exactly as asked; "nervous system" or "brain" is too vague to answer a "which division" question.

**2. (a)** Cerebellum [1]; it coordinates movement and balance [1]. **(b)** Hypothalamus [1]; it monitors temperature and coordinates the response [1]. **(c)** Medulla oblongata [1]; it controls the breathing rate through autonomic centres [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* each part needs a named function linked to the symptom; naming the region without the function leaves a mark unclaimed.

**3. (a)** Reflex: 201 ÷ 5 = **40.2 ms** [1]; button: 1110 ÷ 5 = **222 ms** [1]. **(b)** 222 ÷ 40.2 = **5.5 times** [1]. **(c)** Few synapses, with a direct monosynaptic link [1]; no conscious processing in the cerebrum [1]. **(d)** Rapid and automatic, so the body avoids harm before it is consciously aware [1]; innate, so no learning is needed and posture or balance is protected [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* "faster" needs a reason that refers to the pathway; show the sums for the means, so that a slip in arithmetic does not hide a correct method.

**4. (a)** 2.0 µm = 0.0020 mm [1]; 30 ÷ 0.0020 = **×15 000** [1]. **(b)** Relaxed 34.5 ÷ 15 000 = 0.0023 mm = 2.3 µm [1]; contracted 27 ÷ 15 000 = 1.8 µm [1]; (2.3 − 1.8) ÷ 2.3 × 100 = **21.7%** [1]. **(c)** Branched fibres [1]; intercalated discs (and a single central nucleus) [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* convert to one unit before dividing; magnification has no units, so a bare number with a stray unit loses clarity.

**5. (a)** I band: 1.7, 1.1, 0.5, 0.3 [1]; H zone: 1.4, 0.8, 0.2, 0.0 [1]. **(b)** The myosin filaments do not shorten [1]. **(c)** Actin filaments slide towards the centre between the myosin filaments [1]; so the overlap increases, which shortens the I band and H zone [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* say that the filaments slide, not shorten; an answer gains most when it links the change in each band to the movement of actin.

**6.** Calcium channels open in the nerve ending and acetylcholine is released [1]; it diffuses across the cleft to receptors on the sarcolemma, which depolarises [1]; the depolarisation travels into the T tubules [1]; the sarcoplasmic reticulum releases calcium ions [1]; calcium attaches to troponin, shifting tropomyosin off the actin binding sites [1]; myosin heads attach, forming cross-bridges [1]; the heads tilt (power stroke) and drag actin towards the centre, shortening the sarcomere [1]; fresh ATP binds so the heads let go, and its hydrolysis resets them for another cycle [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* in a long answer, present the steps in sequence and use the correct terms; mixing up troponin and tropomyosin is a common way to lose credit.

**7. (a)** ATP alone: 3.6 ÷ 2.4 = **1.5 s** [1]; total 3.6 + 18.0 = 21.6 mmol [1]; 21.6 ÷ 2.4 = **9.0 s** [1]. **(b)** Creatine phosphate donates its phosphate to ADP, making ATP rapidly [1]; the store runs out within seconds, so a long race depends on respiration [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* give the unit with each time; for the explanation, state both what creatine phosphate does and why the store is limited.

**8.** Exercise raises carbon dioxide, which lowers blood pH [1]; chemoreceptors detect this and signal the medulla [1]; the medulla sends impulses along the sympathetic nerve to the SAN, releasing noradrenaline [1]; adrenaline from the adrenal glands also raises the rate [1]; afterwards, carbon dioxide falls and the medulla sends impulses along the vagus nerve [1]; acetylcholine at the SAN slows the heart to resting level [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* name the nerve and the transmitter for each direction of change; the answer must cover both the increase and the return.

**9. (a)** Rates: 72, 66, 78, 62, 70 [1]; mean 348 ÷ 5 = **69.6 beats per minute** [1]. **(b)** (164.4 − 69.6) ÷ 69.6 × 100 [1] = **136%** [1]. **(c)** Same exercise type and duration for all [1]; repeat and take a mean, or use a heart rate monitor instead of counting [1]. **(d)** The rate is still above resting for every volunteer, so recovery is not complete [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* scale the 30 s count to a minute before averaging; a percentage change is divided by the original value.

**10. (a)** The hormone attaches to its receptor on the cell-surface membrane [1]; adenylyl cyclase is switched on [1]; it turns ATP into cAMP [1]. **(b)** cAMP stays almost constant, rising only from 15.2 to 15.8 [1]; receptors or adenylyl cyclase are saturated, so more adrenaline has no effect [1]. **(c)** Adrenaline is water-soluble and cannot cross the membrane [1]; cAMP carries the signal inside and amplifies it [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* quote figures when describing a trend; the explanation needs a limiting factor such as saturation, not the word "plateau" alone.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming "the brain" where a part such as the cerebellum or medulla oblongata is needed.
- Describing a reflex without a link between the short pathway and the speed.
- Using relay neurones in the knee jerk, which is monosynaptic.
- Not converting units (mm to µm) in magnification questions.
- Dividing percentage change by the new value.
- Writing "the filaments shorten", or that the A band changes.
- Missing ATP in cross-bridge detachment.
- Saying adrenaline enters the cell.
