## Biodiversity

- **Definition**: the variety of all the different species of organisms on Earth, or within an ecosystem.
- **Why it matters**: great biodiversity ensures stability by **reducing the dependence of one species on
  another** for food, shelter and the maintenance of the physical environment.
- The future of humans relies on maintaining a good level of biodiversity.
- Many human activities reduce it; measures to stop this are recent.

## Why humans affect ecosystems more

```
rapid population growth + higher standard of living
→ more resources used + more waste produced
→ more pollution (unless waste is handled properly)
```

## Pollution

| Where | Sources |
|---|---|
| Water | Sewage, fertiliser, toxic chemicals |
| Air | Smoke, acidic gases |
| Land | Landfill, toxic chemicals |

Pollution **kills plants and animals**, which can **reduce biodiversity**.

## Land use

- Land is taken by **building, quarrying, farming and dumping waste**.
- **Peat bogs** destroyed for **garden compost**:
  - habitat area falls, so biodiversity falls;
  - decay or burning of peat **releases carbon dioxide**.
- **Conflict**: cheap compost for food production vs conserving peatland habitats and cutting CO₂ emissions.

## Deforestation

- Tropical forests cleared for **cattle and rice fields** and **crops for biofuels**.
- Effects: habitats and species lost; less CO₂ removed by photosynthesis; CO₂ released when trees are burned or
  decay.

## Global warming

- **Carbon dioxide and methane** levels are rising and contribute to global warming.
- Biological consequences: changes in species' distribution (towards the poles, uphill); habitat loss (melting
  ice, flooding); changed migration patterns; extinctions; pests and pathogens spreading to new areas.
- The scientific consensus rests on **systematic reviews of thousands of peer-reviewed publications**.

## Maintaining biodiversity: the five programmes

| Programme | Helps by |
|---|---|
| Breeding programmes for endangered species | Increasing numbers, sometimes for release |
| Protection and regeneration of rare habitats | Keeping homes and resources for species |
| Field margins and hedgerows where one crop is grown | Adding habitats and food for wildlife |
| Some governments reducing deforestation and CO₂ emissions | Protecting forests; slowing global warming |
| Recycling instead of landfill | Saving land and resources |

## Cause-and-effect chains to learn

```
toxic chemicals enter a river → fish and invertebrates are poisoned → fewer species → biodiversity falls
acidic gases dissolve in rain → lakes and soils become more acidic → sensitive species die
hedgerow replanted → more food and shelter → insects, birds and mammals return → biodiversity rises
landfill site built → habitat covered → organisms lose space and food → numbers fall
```

## Evaluation frame

```
benefit to biodiversity → cost or conflict (money, land, food, jobs) → judgement with a reason
```

Worked reminder: a town recycles 40% of its 25 000 tonnes of waste instead of sending it to landfill.
Waste diverted = 0.40 × 25 000 = **10 000 tonnes**; landfill = 25 000 − 10 000 = **15 000 tonnes**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Biodiversity | Variety of all the different species on Earth or in an ecosystem |
| Pollution | Harmful substances released into water, air or land |
| Landfill | Burying waste in the ground |
| Peat bog | Waterlogged habitat where dead plant material builds up as peat |
| Deforestation | Large-scale clearing of forest |
| Biofuel | Fuel made from crops or other living material |
| Global warming | Rise in Earth's average temperature linked to more CO₂ and methane |
| Breeding programme | Breeding endangered species in captivity to increase numbers |
| Field margin | Strip of uncultivated land left around the edge of a crop field |
| Hedgerow | Line of shrubs and trees between fields, a habitat for wildlife |

## Exam traps

- Biodiversity is about **different species**, not numbers of individuals.
- The two gases for global warming in the specification are **carbon dioxide and methane**.
- Peat: give **both** habitat loss and CO₂ release.
- Deforestation reasons in the specification are **cattle, rice and biofuels**.
- Conservation answers must say **how** the method helps, not just name it.
- An evaluation needs a disadvantage and a conclusion.

## Self-test

1. Define biodiversity.
2. Explain why high biodiversity makes an ecosystem more stable.
3. Give two reasons more waste is being produced.
4. Name the pollutants of water listed in the specification.
5. Name two sources of air pollution in the specification.
6. Give four ways humans reduce the land available for other organisms.
7. Why are peat bogs destroyed?
8. Give two harmful effects of destroying peat bogs.
9. Give two reasons for large-scale tropical deforestation.
10. Name the two gases whose rising levels contribute to global warming.
11. Describe two biological consequences of global warming.
12. Give three programmes that help maintain biodiversity.

### Answers

1. The variety of all the different species of organisms on Earth, or within an ecosystem.
2. It reduces the dependence of one species on another for food, shelter and the maintenance of the physical
   environment, so losing one species has less effect.
3. Rapid growth in the human population; an increase in the standard of living.
4. Sewage, fertiliser, toxic chemicals.
5. Smoke; acidic gases.
6. Building, quarrying, farming, dumping waste.
7. To produce garden compost.
8. Habitat and biodiversity are lost; carbon dioxide is released when peat decays or is burned.
9. Any two: land for cattle; land for rice fields; growing crops for biofuels.
10. Carbon dioxide and methane.
11. Any two: species' distribution changes; loss of habitat; changes in migration patterns; extinction;
    spread of pests or pathogens.
12. Any three: breeding programmes; protecting and regenerating rare habitats; field margins and hedgerows;
    reducing deforestation and CO₂ emissions; recycling rather than landfill.
