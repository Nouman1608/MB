These questions cover outcomes 1.1.11 and 1.1.12 of Pearson Edexcel International A Level Accounting (YAC11), Unit 1: capital expenditure and revenue expenditure and their correct treatment with reference to accounting concepts. Review the [capital and revenue expenditure study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-capital-and-revenue-expenditure/) or the [capital and revenue expenditure revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-capital-and-revenue-expenditure-revision-notes/) first. All businesses and figures are invented; amounts are in dollars.

## Section A

**1.** Define capital expenditure and revenue expenditure. **[2]**

**2.** Classify each item as capital or revenue expenditure.

**(a)** Legal fees on buying a warehouse.

**(b)** Insurance of the warehouse for its first year.

**(c)** Installing shelving racks in the warehouse.

**(d)** Repairing a broken warehouse door.

**(e)** Training staff to use a new forklift truck.

**(f)** Delivery charge on the new forklift truck. **[3]**

**3.** A business buys a machine with a list price of $48,000, less 12.5% trade discount. It also pays import duty of $2,100, delivery of $900, first-year insurance of $1,300, installation of $1,600 and staff training of $800. Calculate the cost of the machine. **[3]**

**4.** Explain why the cost of training staff to use a new machine is treated as revenue expenditure. **[2]**

**5.** A business charges a $30 hand tool, expected to last four years, to expenses. State the concept that justifies this and explain it. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Corran Builders has a draft profit of $64,000 for its first year. It depreciates vehicles at 25% of cost a year, with a full year's charge in the year of purchase, and does not depreciate premises. The following errors have been found.

1. A new van costing $22,000 was debited to motor expenses.
2. Delivery of $500 on the new van was debited to carriage inwards.
3. Repairs to the roof of $3,800 were debited to premises.
4. Wages of $2,600 paid to the firm's own staff for building an extension to the premises were left in wages.

**(a)** Calculate the corrected profit. **[6]**

**(b)** Calculate the net effect of the corrections on non-current assets. **[3]**

**7.** A machine costing $24,000, with a useful life of 4 years and no residual value, was charged to repairs in Year 1. The business uses straight line depreciation with a full year's charge in the year of purchase.

**(a)** State the effect of the error on profit in each of Years 1 to 4. **[4]**

**(b)** The errors cancel out over the four years. Explain why the error still matters. **[2]**

**8.** Prepare journal entries, with narratives, to correct the following errors found before the year-end statements were finalised. Depreciation is 20% of cost a year, with a full year's charge in the year of purchase.

**(a)** A machine costing $15,000 was debited to repairs. No depreciation has been charged on it. **[4]**

**(b)** A repair of $1,800 was debited to machinery, and depreciation of $360 has been charged on it. **[4]**

**9.** A haulage firm incurs the following costs on its lorries. Classify each as capital or revenue expenditure, giving a reason.

**(a)** Replacing worn tyres, $2,400. **[2]**

**(b)** Fitting a refrigeration unit so that a lorry can carry chilled food, $11,000. **[2]**

**(c)** Rebuilding an engine to restore its original performance, $6,500. **[2]**

**(d)** Replacing an engine with a model that cuts fuel use by 30%, $14,000. **[2]**

**10.** A builder's invoice for $18,600 covers repainting existing offices ($3,200), building partition walls to create two extra offices ($11,400), and repairing storm damage to the car park ($4,000). State how much is capital expenditure and how much is revenue expenditure, giving reasons. **[3]**

**11.** A business decides to expense all items costing less than $500, even if they will last for several years. Evaluate this policy. **[5]**

**12.** The owner of a shop spends $5,000 on an advertising campaign and wants to record it as a non-current asset, because "it will bring customers in for the next three years". Explain, with reference to accounting concepts, why it should be treated as revenue expenditure. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Capital expenditure is spending to **acquire or improve a non-current asset** [1]. Revenue expenditure is spending on **running the business and maintaining** existing assets [1]. **[2]**

**2.** (a) Capital; (b) Revenue; (c) Capital; (d) Revenue; (e) Revenue; (f) Capital. One mark for each two correct [3]. **[3]**

**3.** Price after discount 48,000 × 0.875 = 42,000 [1]. Add duty 2,100, delivery 900 and installation 1,600 [1]. Cost **$46,600**; insurance and training are revenue [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Training develops the **staff**, not the machine, so it is not needed to bring the machine to working condition [1]; its benefit cannot be controlled by the business as an asset, since trained staff can leave [1]. **[2]**

**5.** **Materiality** [1]: $30 is too small to affect users' decisions, so the cost of recording and depreciating it as an asset is not justified [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)**

| | $ |
|---|---|
| Draft profit | 64,000 |
| Van removed from motor expenses | +22,000 |
| Delivery removed from carriage inwards | +500 |
| Depreciation on van: 25% × 22,500 | −5,625 |
| Roof repairs charged as expense | −3,800 |
| Own wages transferred to premises | +2,600 |
| **Corrected profit** | **79,675** |

Van and delivery [1] [1]; depreciation on $22,500 [1]; roof [1]; wages [1]; total [1].

**(b)** Vehicles: +22,500 − 5,625 = +16,875 [1]. Premises: −3,800 + 2,600 = −1,200 [1]. Net increase **$15,675** [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Delivery is part of the van's cost, so depreciation is on $22,500, not $22,000.

**7. (a)** Year 1: profit **understated by $18,000** (24,000 − 6,000) [2]. Years 2, 3 and 4: profit **overstated by $6,000** each year [2].

**(b)** Each year's profit is wrong, so trends and comparisons are misleading [1], and non-current assets are understated in the statement of financial position until Year 4 [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)**

| | Dr | Cr |
|---|---|---|
| Machinery | 15,000 | |
| Repairs | | 15,000 |
| *Machine wrongly charged to repairs* | | |
| Depreciation expense | 3,000 | |
| Provision for depreciation: machinery | | 3,000 |
| *Depreciation for the year on the machine* | | |

First entry [2]; depreciation entry [2].

**(b)**

| | Dr | Cr |
|---|---|---|
| Repairs | 1,800 | |
| Machinery | | 1,800 |
| *Repair wrongly capitalised* | | |
| Provision for depreciation: machinery | 360 | |
| Depreciation expense | | 360 |
| *Reversal of depreciation charged on the repair* | | |

First entry [2]; reversal [2]. **[8]**

**9. (a)** **Revenue** [1]: tyres wear out regularly and replacing them maintains the lorry [1].

**(b)** **Capital** [1]: it adds a new capability, so the lorry can earn revenue from chilled loads [1].

**(c)** **Revenue** [1]: it only restores the original condition [1].

**(d)** **Capital** [1]: it significantly reduces running costs, improving the asset beyond its original condition [1]. **[8]**

**10.** Capital **$11,400**: the partitions add capacity (extra offices) [1]. Revenue **$7,200**: repainting and storm repairs maintain existing assets [1]. The invoice must be split, not treated as one item [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Up to 5 marks:

- It applies **materiality**: small items do not affect users' decisions [1].
- It **saves time and cost**, as no asset records or depreciation are needed for small items [1].
- Profit in the year of purchase is slightly **understated** and non-current assets are understated [1].
- Many small purchases in one year, such as furnishing a new office, could together be **material**, distorting that year's profit [1].
- **Judgement:** sensible for a business of reasonable size, provided the threshold is applied consistently and reviewed if purchases of small items become large in total [1]. **[5]**

**12.** Under **prudence**, spending is not carried forward as an asset when its future benefit is uncertain [1]; customers attracted by advertising cannot be controlled or measured reliably [1]. Under **accruals**, the cost belongs to the period in which the campaign runs, so it is an expense of this year [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Classifying by size rather than by purpose.
- Using list price instead of the price after trade discount.
- Capitalising training, insurance or advertising.
- Omitting the depreciation effect of a correction.
- Stating the profit effect without the effect on non-current assets.
- Not splitting an invoice that covers both repairs and improvements.
