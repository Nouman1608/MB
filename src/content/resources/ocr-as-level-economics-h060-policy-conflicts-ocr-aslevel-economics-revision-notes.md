These notes condense topic 3.4 **Policy conflicts** from Implementing policy in Component 02, Macroeconomics, of the OCR *AS Level Economics (H060)* specification, Version 3.0 (June 2026), which asks you to evaluate "conflicts and trade-offs between policy objectives". The [policy conflicts study guide](/resources/ocr-as-level-economics-h060-policy-conflicts-ocr-aslevel-economics/) has the full explanations and worked examples, and the [policy conflicts practice questions](/resources/ocr-as-level-economics-h060-policy-conflicts-ocr-aslevel-economics-practice/) give exam-style work. All data is invented.

## The objectives

| Objective | Usual measure |
|---|---|
| Economic growth | Rate of change of real GDP |
| Full employment | Unemployment rate (Labour Force Survey or claimant count) |
| Low and stable inflation | CPI inflation against the 2% target |
| Sustainable balance of payments | Current account balance as % of GDP |
| Sustainable development | Environmental and social indicators alongside GDP |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Policy conflict | Moving towards one objective moves the economy away from another |
| Trade-off | The amount of one objective given up to gain more of another |
| Spare capacity | Unused labour and capital; weakens the growth-inflation conflict |
| Supply shock | A sudden change in costs or capacity that shifts AS |
| Policy mix | The combination of fiscal, monetary and supply side policies used together |
| Fiscal consolidation | Reducing a budget deficit by cutting spending or raising taxes |
| Prioritisation | Choosing which objective takes precedence, such as an inflation target |
| Sustainable development | Meeting present needs without harming future generations' ability to meet theirs |

## The main conflicts in one table

| Conflict | Mechanism | Strongest when | Weakest when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Growth and jobs versus inflation | Higher AD raises output and prices | Near full capacity | Deep recession with spare capacity |
| Growth versus current account | Higher incomes raise imports | Import-heavy spending, weak exports | Export-led growth |
| Inflation versus current account | Higher rates cause appreciation, hurting exports | Large interest rate changes | Rates change little or capital is immobile |
| Growth versus sustainability | More output uses more resources and pollutes | Energy-intensive growth | Growth from services and clean technology |
| Deficit reduction versus growth and jobs | Consolidation cuts AD | Weak economy | Strong private demand |
| Supply shock: inflation versus output | SRAS shifts left | Large, lasting shock | Short-lived shock |

## When objectives are complementary

- In a **recession**, higher AD raises growth and cuts unemployment with little inflation.
- **Supply side** gains raise output and lower inflationary pressure together.
- **Stable inflation** reduces uncertainty, supporting investment and long-run growth.

### Worked reminder: spotting a conflict in data

| Year | Growth (%) | Unemployment (%) | Inflation (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3.2 | 4.1 | 4.5 |
| 2 | 1.1 | 5.0 | 2.3 |

After higher interest rates, inflation fell by **2.2 percentage points** towards target, but growth fell by **2.1 points** and unemployment rose by **0.9 points**: a clear growth-and-jobs versus inflation trade-off.

### Worked reminder: current account

Imports are 30% of GDP. If real income rises 4% and imports rise in proportion, imports grow by 4% of their value: 1.2% of GDP. If exports are flat, the current account worsens by about **1.2% of GDP**.

### Worked reminder: no conflict

Unemployment 9%, inflation 0.5%. A rise in government spending raises growth and cuts unemployment, and inflation moves towards 2%: all three objectives improve.

## Ways to manage conflicts

| Approach | Example | Drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Prioritise | Central bank focuses on 2% inflation | Growth and jobs may suffer |
| Policy mix | Tight monetary policy plus supply side reform | Needs co-ordination |
| Supply side focus | Training, infrastructure | Slow to work |
| Gradualism | Bring inflation down over two years | Inflation above target for longer |
| Accept short-run costs | Consolidation now for lower debt later | Political and social cost |

## Building an evaluative answer

1. **Name** the conflict.
2. **Explain** it with a chain through AD or AS.
3. **Condition** it: spare capacity, type of shock, openness to trade, time horizon.
4. **Judge** which objective should take priority in the context, and why.

## Exam traps

- Growth does not always cause inflation: check for spare capacity.
- A falling inflation rate after rate rises is not free: say what happened to output and jobs.
- The exchange rate links interest rates to the current account.
- Demand-side policies cannot solve a supply shock without a trade-off.
- Not all objectives clash: supply side improvements can help all of them.
- A list of conflicts with no judgement scores poorly on an evaluate question.

## Self-test

1. Define a policy conflict.
2. Explain why faster growth may worsen the current account.
3. When is the conflict between growth and inflation weakest?
4. How can a rise in interest rates worsen the current account?
5. Explain why a supply shock creates a harder trade-off than a demand shock.
6. Give one reason why growth may conflict with sustainable development.
7. Explain how fiscal consolidation can conflict with low unemployment.
8. Name one situation in which growth and low unemployment can be achieved without higher inflation.
9. Inflation falls from 5.1% to 2.9% while unemployment rises from 3.8% to 4.6%. Describe the trade-off in percentage points.
10. What is the policy mix?
11. Give one way of reducing the conflict between growth and inflation in the long run.
12. Why might a government give inflation priority over growth?

### Answers

1. A situation in which moving towards one objective moves the economy away from another.
2. Higher incomes raise spending on imports; if exports do not keep pace, the deficit widens.
3. When there is much spare capacity, such as in a deep recession.
4. Higher rates attract financial inflows, the currency appreciates, exports become dearer and imports cheaper.
5. A supply shock raises inflation and lowers output together, so raising or lowering AD always worsens one of them.
6. Growth uses more resources and energy and increases pollution.
7. Lower spending or higher taxes reduce AD, so firms cut output and jobs.
8. In a recession with spare capacity, or when supply side improvements shift LRAS right.
9. Inflation falls by **2.2 percentage points**; unemployment rises by **0.8 percentage points**.
10. The combination of fiscal, monetary and supply side policies used together.
11. Supply side policies that raise productive capacity, such as training or infrastructure.
12. Low, stable inflation reduces uncertainty and supports investment and long-run growth; high inflation is costly to reduce once expectations rise.
