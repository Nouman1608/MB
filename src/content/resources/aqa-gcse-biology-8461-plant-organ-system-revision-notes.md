These are condensed recall notes for **4.2.3.2 Plant organ system** of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, Version 1.0, for teaching from September 2016 and exams from 2018 onwards. It is Paper 1 content at both tiers; nothing here is marked (HT only). Worked explanations are in the [plant organ system study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-plant-organ-system/); questions are in the [plant organ system practice set](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-plant-organ-system-practice/). All figures below are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Recall |
|---|---|
| Plant organ system | Roots, stem and leaves working together to transport substances |
| Transpiration | Loss of water vapour from the leaves, mainly through stomata |
| Transpiration stream | Movement of water and mineral ions up the xylem from roots to leaves |
| Translocation | Movement of food molecules (dissolved sugars) through phloem |
| Root hair cell | Root cell with a long projection for absorbing water and mineral ions |
| Osmosis | How water enters root hair cells |
| Active transport | How mineral ions enter root hair cells, against a concentration gradient, using energy |
| Guard cells | Cells that open and close each stoma |
| Turgid / flaccid | Swollen with water (stoma open) / lacking water (stoma closed) |
| Potometer | Apparatus that measures water uptake by a shoot |

## Adaptations

| Structure | Adaptation | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Root hair cell | Long projection | Large surface area for uptake |
| Root hair cell | Many mitochondria | Energy from respiration for active transport of ions |
| Xylem | Hollow tubes, lignin | Water flows freely; strong walls support the plant |
| Phloem | Elongated cells, pores in end walls | Cell sap with sugars passes from cell to cell |
| Stomata and guard cells | Pores that open and close | Control gas exchange and water loss |

Detailed phloem structure and the mechanism of transport are **not** required.

## Transpiration in five steps

1. Water evaporates from mesophyll cells into air spaces.
2. Water vapour diffuses out through stomata.
3. Water moves from xylem into mesophyll cells.
4. Water is pulled up the xylem (transpiration stream).
5. Root hair cells take in more water by osmosis.

## Xylem versus phloem

| | Xylem | Phloem |
|---|---|---|
| Carries | Water, mineral ions | Dissolved sugars |
| Direction | Upwards only | To where sugars are used or stored |
| Process | Transpiration stream | Translocation |

## Factors affecting the rate of transpiration

| Increase in | Rate | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | Up | Faster evaporation and diffusion |
| Humidity | Down | Smaller concentration gradient of water vapour |
| Air movement | Up | Vapour removed, gradient kept steep |
| Light intensity | Up | Stomata open wider |

Combined: hot, dry, windy, sunny → highest rate; cool, humid, still, dark → lowest. If the plant runs short of water, guard cells become flaccid, stomata close and the rate falls.

## Calculations

```
rate = distance moved by bubble / time
mean = total of repeats / number of repeats
cross-sectional area of tube = pi x r^2
volume taken up = area x distance
rate per unit leaf area = rate / leaf area
percentage change = (new - old) / old x 100
```

Mini example: a bubble moves 45 mm in 15 minutes. Rate = 3 mm/min.

Mini example: leaf area by squares: 22 whole + 10 part squares (counted as half) = 27 cm².

## Practical reminders

- Potometer: cut and fit the shoot under water; seal joints; keep other factors constant; repeat and take a mean; it measures uptake, which estimates loss.
- Stomata distribution: nail varnish peel from each leaf surface; count in several fields of view; calculate a mean; more stomata on the lower surface.

## Graph skills

- Independent variable on the x-axis; rate on the y-axis.
- Scales use more than half the grid in even steps.
- Line or curve of best fit; describe the trend with data, then explain it.

## Exam traps

- Mineral ions: **active transport**, not osmosis.
- Humidity **decreases** the rate.
- A potometer measures **uptake**.
- Translocation is in **phloem**; the transpiration stream is in **xylem**.
- Light affects transpiration through **stomata**, not by "heating the leaf".
- Divide by leaf area when shoots differ in size.

## Self-test

1. Name the three organs in the plant transport system.
2. How does water enter a root hair cell?
3. Why do root hair cells contain many mitochondria?
4. What is translocation?
5. Describe the first two steps of transpiration.
6. What happens to the stoma when guard cells become turgid?
7. Explain why an increase in humidity slows transpiration.
8. Explain why moving air speeds up transpiration.
9. Why is the rate measured by a potometer only an estimate of transpiration?
10. Repeat bubble distances in 5 minutes are 20, 22 and 18 mm. Calculate the mean rate in mm per minute.
11. A shoot takes up 0.6 cm³ per hour and has a leaf area of 40 cm². Calculate the rate per cm² per hour.
12. Which surface of a leaf usually has more stomata?

### Answers

1. Roots, stem and leaves.
2. By osmosis.
3. To release energy by respiration for the active transport of mineral ions.
4. The movement of dissolved sugars (food molecules) through the phloem.
5. Water evaporates from mesophyll cells into air spaces; water vapour diffuses out through stomata.
6. It opens.
7. The air holds more water vapour, so the concentration gradient from leaf to air is smaller and diffusion is slower.
8. It carries water vapour away from the leaf, keeping the concentration gradient steep.
9. Some of the water taken up is used by the plant (for example in photosynthesis) rather than lost.
10. Mean = 60 ÷ 3 = 20 mm; 20 ÷ 5 = 4 mm per minute.
11. 0.6 ÷ 40 = 0.015 cm³ per cm² per hour.
12. The lower surface.
