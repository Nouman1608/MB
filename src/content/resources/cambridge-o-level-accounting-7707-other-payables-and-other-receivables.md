## Why matching matters

The **matching** (or **accruals**) concept says that the expenses and
incomes in a period's statement of profit or loss are those that **belong to
that period**, whether or not cash has been paid or received. Profit is
revenue earned in the period less the costs of earning it.

If a business simply recorded cash paid and received, profit would move
around with the timing of payments. Paying two electricity bills in one year
and none in the next would make the first year look worse and the second
better, although nothing about the business had changed. Matching makes
profit a fair measure of each year's performance and allows comparison
between years.

## The four adjustments

| Adjustment | Meaning | Statement of financial position | Effect on profit compared with cash |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accrued expense | Expense of this period not yet paid | Other payables (current liability) | Expense higher than cash paid |
| Prepaid expense | Amount paid now for a later period | Other receivables (current asset) | Expense lower than cash paid |
| Accrued income | Income earned this period not yet received | Other receivables (current asset) | Income higher than cash received |
| Income received in advance | Amount received now for a later period | Other payables (current liability) | Income lower than cash received |

A memory aid: **"owed by us" or "received early" → payable; "owed to us" or
"paid early" → receivable.**

## Calculating the amount for the year

For any expense:

```
Expense for the year = cash paid
                       − amount owing at the start (belongs to last year)
                       + amount prepaid at the start (benefits this year)
                       + amount owing at the end
                       − amount prepaid at the end
```

For any income, the same pattern applies with "received" in place of
"paid", "owed to the business" in place of "owing" and "received in advance"
in place of "prepaid".

## Worked example: Chen's shop, year ended 31 December 2027

**1. Insurance (prepaid at both ends).** On 1 January 2027 insurance of $450
was prepaid for January to March. On 1 April 2027 Chen paid $2,400 for the
twelve months to 31 March 2028.

The prepayment at 31 December covers January to March 2028: $2,400 × 3/12 =
$600. Expense = $450 + $2,400 − $600 = **$2,250**.

```
Insurance account

2027                                       2027
1 Jan    Balance b/d                  450  31 Dec   Profit or loss             2,250
1 Apr    Bank                       2,400  31 Dec   Balance c/d                  600
                                    -----                                      -----
                                    2,850                                      2,850
                                    =====                                      =====
2028
1 Jan    Balance b/d                  600
```

**2. Wages (accrued at both ends).** Wages paid in 2027 were $38,600,
including $700 owed at the end of 2026. At 31 December 2027 wages of $950
were owing.

Expense = $38,600 − $700 + $950 = **$38,850**.

```
Wages account
Date     Details                        $  Date     Details                        $
2027                                       2027
Various  Bank                      38,600  1 Jan    Balance b/d                  700
31 Dec   Balance c/d                  950  31 Dec   Profit or loss            38,850
                                   ------                                     ------
                                   39,550                                     39,550
                                   ======                                     ======
                                           2028
                                           1 Jan    Balance b/d                  950
```

**3. Commission receivable (accrued income at both ends).** At 1 January
2027 commission of $320 was owed to Chen. During 2027 he received $4,100. At
31 December 2027 commission of $410 was owed to him.

Income = $4,100 − $320 + $410 = **$4,190**.

```
Commission receivable account
Date     Details                        $  Date     Details                        $
2027                                       2027
1 Jan    Balance b/d                  320  Various  Bank                       4,100
31 Dec   Profit or loss             4,190  31 Dec   Balance c/d                  410
                                    -----                                      -----
                                    4,510                                      4,510
                                    =====                                      =====
2028
1 Jan    Balance b/d                  410
```

**4. Rent receivable (received in advance at both ends).** Chen sublets a
storeroom at $750 a month, $9,000 a year. On 1 January 2027 the tenant had
paid January's rent in advance. During 2027 Chen received $9,750, which
included rent for January and February 2028.

Income = $750 + $9,750 − $1,500 = **$9,000** (exactly twelve months).

```
Rent receivable account
Date     Details                        $  Date     Details                        $
2027                                       2027
31 Dec   Profit or loss             9,000  1 Jan    Balance b/d                  750
31 Dec   Balance c/d                1,500  Various  Bank                       9,750
                                   ------                                     ------
                                   10,500                                     10,500
                                   ======                                     ======
                                           2028
                                           1 Jan    Balance b/d                1,500
```

for each account):

```

        (Insurance prepaid at year end)

        (Wages accrued at year end)

        (Commission accrued at year end)
```

**Statement of financial position extract at 31 December 2027**

```
Current assets
  Other receivables ($600 insurance + $410 commission)       1,010
Current liabilities
  Other payables ($950 wages + $1,500 rent in advance)       2,450
```

## Reading the balances brought down

The side of the balance brought down tells you what it is:

- **Debit balance b/d on an expense account** → prepaid expense (asset).
- **Credit balance b/d on an expense account** → accrued expense
(liability).
- **Debit balance b/d on an income account** → accrued income (asset).
- **Credit balance b/d on an income account** → income received in advance
(liability).

In each example above, the opening balance on 1 January 2027 was last
year's closing adjustment, and it reverses itself during 2027.

## Effect of forgetting an adjustment

| Omitted adjustment | Profit for the year | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Accrued expense | Overstated | Other payables understated |
| Prepaid expense | Understated | Other receivables understated |
| Accrued income | Understated | Other receivables understated |
| Income received in advance | Overstated | Other payables understated |

## Common mistakes

- Counting the wrong number of months, especially when a payment straddles
the year end.
- Bringing an accrual down on the debit side, or a prepayment on the credit
side.
- Ignoring the opening balance on the account.
- Treating income received in advance as an asset because cash has been
received.
- Adding the accrued income to cash received but forgetting to remove the
amount owed at the start of the year.
- Using the cash paid as the expense in the statement of profit or loss.

## Exam technique

- **Calculate**: show cash, then each adjustment with a sign, then the
answer.
- **Prepare** ledger accounts: include the opening balance, the transfer to
the statement of profit or loss, the balance carried down and the balance
brought down on the first day of the next year.
- **Explain** the importance of matching with a consequence: "profit
reflects the costs of earning that year's revenue, so years can be
compared".
- Questions may give dates of payments; draw a quick month line from the
start to the end of the period covered before calculating.
