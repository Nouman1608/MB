These questions cover **3.1.8.2 Gibbs free-energy change, ∆G, and entropy change, ∆S** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content (**International A-level only**), assessed in Unit 3. Use the data given in each question, and treat ∆H and ∆S as independent of temperature. The graph data in Question 8 are invented.

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by *entropy*. **[1]**

**2.** Predict, with a reason, the sign of ∆S for 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2SO₃(g). **[2]**

**3.** State the condition, in terms of ∆G, for a reaction to be feasible. **[1]**

**4.** Dissolving ammonium nitrate in water is endothermic, yet it happens readily at room temperature. Explain why. **[2]**

**5.** A reaction has ∆H = +25 kJ mol⁻¹ and ∆S = +60 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Calculate ∆G at 350 K. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Zinc carbonate decomposes on heating: ZnCO₃(s) → ZnO(s) + CO₂(g), ∆H = +69 kJ mol⁻¹.

Absolute entropies (J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹): ZnCO₃(s) 82; ZnO(s) 44; CO₂(g) 214.

**(a)** Calculate ∆S for the reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate ∆G at 298 K and state whether the reaction is feasible. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the minimum temperature at which the reaction becomes feasible. **[2]**

**7.** 2NO(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO₂(g), ∆H = −114 kJ mol⁻¹.

Absolute entropies (J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹): NO(g) 211; O₂(g) 205; NO₂(g) 240.

**(a)** Calculate ∆S for the reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the temperature above which the reaction is no longer feasible. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain, in terms of ∆G = ∆H − T∆S, why the reaction stops being feasible at high temperature. **[1]**

**8.** A straight-line graph of ∆G against T for a reaction passes through ∆G = −30 kJ mol⁻¹ at 200 K and ∆G = +10 kJ mol⁻¹ at 400 K.

**(a)** Use the gradient to find ∆S, in J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. **[2]**

**(b)** Find ∆H. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the temperature at which ∆G = 0, and state the range of temperatures over which the reaction is feasible. **[2]**

**9.** For H₂(g) + Cl₂(g) → 2HCl(g), ∆S = +20 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. The absolute entropies of H₂(g) and Cl₂(g) are 131 and 223 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Calculate the absolute entropy of HCl(g). **[2]**

**10.** A graph of ∆G against T for a reaction is a straight line that suddenly becomes steeper (more negative gradient) at 350 K. Suggest what happens at 350 K and explain the change in gradient. **[2]**

**11.** The conversion of diamond into graphite has a negative ∆G at 298 K. Explain why diamonds do not visibly turn into graphite. **[2]**

**12.** A reaction is endothermic and has a positive entropy change. Explain, using ∆G = ∆H − T∆S, why it is feasible only above a certain temperature. **[3]**

**13.** A reaction has ∆H = −20 kJ mol⁻¹ and ∆S = −80 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. A student substitutes T = 25 and concludes the reaction is feasible at 25 °C. Show that the student is wrong, and find the temperature below which the reaction is feasible. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A measure of the disorder of a system (the number of ways particles and energy can be arranged) [1]

**2.** Negative [1]; 3 moles of gas form 2 moles of gas, so disorder decreases [1]

**3.** ∆G must be zero or negative [1]

**4.** The entropy change is positive (a solid forms dispersed aqueous ions) [1]; at room temperature T∆S is larger than ∆H, so ∆G = ∆H − T∆S is negative [1]

**5.** ∆G = 25 − 350 × 0.060 [1]; = **+4.0 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]

**6. (a)** ∆S = (44 + 214) − 82 [1]; = **+176 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** ∆G = 69 − 298 × 0.176 = **+16.6 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]; positive, so **not feasible** at 298 K [1]

**(c)** T = ∆H / ∆S = 69 / 0.176 [1]; = **392 K** [1]

**7. (a)** ∆S = 2(240) − [2(211) + 205] [1]; = 480 − 627 = **−147 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** T = −114 / −0.147 [1]; = **776 K** [1]

**(c)** ∆S is negative, so −T∆S is positive and grows with temperature; above 776 K it outweighs the negative ∆H, making ∆G positive [1]

**8. (a)** Gradient = (10 − (−30)) / (400 − 200) = +0.200 kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ [1]; ∆S = −gradient = **−200 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** ∆H = ∆G + T∆S = −30 + 200 × (−0.200) = **−70 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]

**(c)** T = −70 / −0.200 = **350 K** [1]; feasible at all temperatures **below** 350 K (∆G ≤ 0 at and below 350 K) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check: at 200 K, −70 − 200 × (−0.200) = −30 kJ mol⁻¹, matching the graph.

**9.** 2S(HCl) − (131 + 223) = +20 [1]; S(HCl) = 374 ÷ 2 = **187 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1]

**10.** A product boils (or a substance changes to a more disordered state) at 350 K [1]; ∆S becomes more positive, and since the gradient equals −∆S, the line becomes steeper downwards [1]

**11.** ∆G only shows that the change is feasible, not how fast it is [1]; the activation energy is very high, so the rate at room temperature is negligible [1]

**12.** ∆H is positive, so at low temperature ∆G is positive [1]; ∆S is positive, so −T∆S is negative and becomes more negative as T increases [1]; above T = ∆H/∆S, T∆S exceeds ∆H and ∆G becomes negative [1]

**13.** At 25 °C, T = 298 K: ∆G = −20 − 298 × (−0.080) = −20 + 23.8 = +3.8 kJ mol⁻¹ [1]; positive, so the reaction is **not** feasible at 25 °C [1]; T = −20 / −0.080 = **250 K**, so it is feasible below 250 K [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using T = 25 gives ∆G = −18 kJ mol⁻¹ and the wrong conclusion. Always convert to kelvin.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not converting ∆S from J to kJ.
- Using degrees Celsius.
- Forgetting coefficients or element entropies.
- Reading the gradient as ∆S rather than −∆S.
- Stating a temperature without saying above or below.
