These questions cover section 4.1, Geometrical terms, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The terms are explained in the [geometrical terms study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-geometrical-terms/), and the [geometry revision notes](/resources/o-level-mathematics-geometry-revision-notes/) give a condensed list. ## Section A

**1.** Name the quadrilateral that has four equal sides and diagonals that cross at 90°, but no right angles. **[1]**

**2.** State the difference between a chord and a diameter of a circle. **[2]**

**3.** Work out the size of each interior angle of a regular decagon. **[2]**

**4.** Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 30°. Work out the number of sides and the size of each interior angle. **[3]**

**5.** A cuboid is a solid with flat faces. State its number of faces, edges and vertices. **[3]**

**6.** Describe the difference between a sector and a segment of a circle. **[2]**

**7.** Classify each triangle by its sides or angles, using the words equilateral, isosceles, scalene or right-angled.

**(a)** Sides 6 cm, 6 cm, 6 cm.

**(b)** Sides 3 cm, 4 cm, 5 cm.

**(c)** Angles 40°, 70°, 70°. **[3]**

## Section B

**8.** A square-based pyramid has 5 faces and 5 vertices.

**(a)** Use V − E + F = 2 to find the number of edges.

**(b)** A hexagonal prism has 8 faces and 12 vertices. Find its number of edges. **[4]**

**9.** State whether each statement is true or false. Give a reason for each answer.

**(a)** Every rectangle is a parallelogram.

**(b)** Every rhombus is a square.

**(c)** A trapezium has two pairs of parallel sides.

**(d)** Congruent shapes are always similar. **[4]**

**10.** The interior angles of a polygon add up to 1440°.

**(a)** Find the number of sides.

**(b)** Name the polygon.

**(c)** The polygon is regular. Find the size of one exterior angle. **[4]**

**11.** Write down the bearing of

**(a)** due east,

**(b)** south-west. **[2]**

**12.** ABCD is a kite with AB = AD and CB = CD. Angle BAD = 80° and angle ABC = 110°. Find angle BCD, giving a reason. **[3]**

**13.** Triangle P has sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm. Triangle Q has sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm.

**(a)** State the scale factor from P to Q.

**(b)** Are the triangles similar, congruent, both or neither? Explain.

**(c)** Name the longest side of triangle P if it is right-angled. **[4]**

**14.** A cone is cut by a plane parallel to its base, and the small cone at the top is removed. Name the remaining solid. **[1]**

**15.** Name the solid described.

**(a)** Six identical square faces.

**(b)** Two parallel circular faces joined by one curved surface.

**(c)** One square face and four triangular faces that meet at a point.

**(d)** Every point on its surface is the same distance from a fixed centre. **[4]**

**16.** ABCDEF is a regular hexagon. Find angle ABC, and then find angle BAC, giving a reason for each. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A **rhombus** [1].

**2.** A chord is a straight line joining any two points on the circumference [1]. A diameter is a chord that passes through the centre, so it is the longest chord [1].

**3.** (10 − 2) × 180 = 1440 [1]. 1440 ÷ 10 = **144°** [1].

**4.** Sides = 360 ÷ 30 = **12** [1]. Interior angle = 180 − 30 [1] = **150°** [1].

**5.** **6 faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices** [1] for each, 3 marks in total.

**6.** A sector is bounded by two radii and an arc [1]. A segment is bounded by a chord and an arc [1].

**7. (a)** **Equilateral** [1]. **(b)** **Scalene** (and right-angled, since 9 + 16 = 25) [1]. **(c)** **Isosceles** [1].

**8. (a)** 5 − E + 5 = 2 [1], so **E = 8** [1].

**(b)** 12 − E + 8 = 2 [1], so **E = 18** [1].

**9. (a)** **True**: it has two pairs of parallel opposite sides [1].

**(b)** **False**: a rhombus has four equal sides but need not have right angles [1].

**(c)** **False**: it has exactly one pair of parallel sides [1].

**(d)** **True**: congruent shapes have the same shape and are in the ratio 1 : 1 [1].

**10. (a)** (n − 2) × 180 = 1440, so n − 2 = 8 and **n = 10** [1][1].

**(b)** A **decagon** [1].

**(c)** 360 ÷ 10 = **36°** [1].

**11. (a)** **090°** [1]. **(b)** **225°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Bearings use three figures, measured clockwise from north.

**12.** Angles B and D are equal in a kite, so angle ADC = 110° [1]. Angle BCD = 360° − 80° − 110° − 110° [1] = **60°**, because the angles of a quadrilateral sum to 360° [1].

**13. (a)** 6 ÷ 3 = **2** [1].

**(b)** **Similar but not congruent**: the sides are in the same ratio, 3 : 6 = 4 : 8 = 5 : 10, but the sizes differ [1][1].

**(c)** The hypotenuse, the **5 cm side** [1].

**14.** A **frustum** [1].

**15. (a)** A **cube** [1]. **(b)** A **cylinder** [1]. **(c)** A **square-based pyramid** [1]. **(d)** A **sphere** [1].

**16.** Each interior angle of a regular hexagon is 720 ÷ 6 = 120°, so angle ABC = **120°** [1]. Triangle ABC is isosceles because AB = BC [1], so angle BAC = (180 − 120) ÷ 2 = **30°** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "parallelogram" for any slanted shape, or forgetting that a square is also a rhombus and a rectangle.
- Mixing up sector and segment.
- Using the wrong angle fact for exterior angles: they sum to 360°, not 180°.
- Counting hidden edges wrongly in a solid; use V − E + F = 2 as a check.
- Giving bearings with two figures.
