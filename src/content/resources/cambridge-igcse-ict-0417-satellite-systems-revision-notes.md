Section 6.11 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 names five satellite systems: GPS, satellite navigation, GIS, satellite television and satellite phones. For each you must know the characteristics, uses, advantages and disadvantages. These notes are for fast recall; the [satellite systems study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-satellite-systems/) explains how each system works, and the [satellite systems practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-satellite-systems-practice/) test you in exam-style contexts.

## Syllabus coverage

| Syllabus wording | What to be ready for |
|---|---|
| Global Positioning Systems (GPS) | How a receiver fixes its position; uses such as tracking |
| Satellite navigation | GPS plus maps and route software; benefits and drawbacks for drivers and companies |
| Geographic Information Systems (GIS) | Layers of location-linked data; queries; uses in planning, emergencies, utilities, environment |
| Media communication systems | Satellite television and satellite phones; coverage, cost, line of sight, delay |

## One line per system

- **GPS**: satellites broadcast time and position → receiver measures signal travel time to at least three (normally four) satellites → calculates its own position.
- **Sat nav**: GPS position + stored map + route software → spoken and on-screen directions, rerouting, arrival time.
- **GIS**: stores data about places in layers → overlay, zoom and query → supports decisions.
- **Satellite TV**: uplink from broadcaster → satellite → downlink over a wide area → dish and receiver in the home.
- **Satellite phone**: handset connects straight to a satellite → works where there are no mobile masts.

## How the systems connect

GPS is the **input** for sat nav (position) and can be one **data source** for a GIS (where each data item was recorded). Sat nav **uses** position; a GIS **analyses** places. If a question asks "what is the difference between GPS and GIS?", give one function for each: GPS works out a location; GIS combines and analyses layers of geographic data.

## Advantages and disadvantages to recall

### GPS and sat nav

| For | Against |
|---|---|
| Works anywhere with a view of the sky, with no phone signal needed for position | Signal lost or weakened in tunnels, underground car parks, between tall buildings, under thick tree cover |
| Accurate position, typically to within a few metres | Maps can be out of date (new roads, closed roads, changed speed limits) |
| Spoken directions keep the driver's eyes on the road | Can choose unsuitable routes, such as narrow or low-bridge roads for lorries |
| Rerouting around traffic saves time and fuel | Users become over-reliant and stop checking where they are |
| Fleet tracking lets a company see where every vehicle is | Tracking people raises privacy concerns |

### GIS

| For | Against |
|---|---|
| Combines many data sets on one map | Expensive software and data |
| Patterns and relationships become visible | Needs trained staff |
| Easy to update, zoom and query | Needs large storage and processing power |
| Decisions can be tested before money is spent | Only as accurate as the data in each layer |

### Satellite TV and satellite phones

| System | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Satellite TV | Very wide coverage, including rural areas; many channels; no cables to each home | Dish needs clear line of sight; heavy rain or snow can disrupt the picture; dish must be installed and aligned |
| Satellite phone | Works in remote areas, at sea and after disasters damage ground networks | Expensive handsets and calls; usually needs to be outdoors; delay in conversation; slower data |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| GPS receiver | Device that receives satellite signals and calculates its own position |
| Satellite navigation | GPS combined with digital maps and route-finding software |
| GIS | System that captures, stores, analyses and displays geographic data in layers |
| Layer | One set of data about an area, such as roads or flood risk, that can be shown or hidden |
| Geostationary satellite | Satellite that stays above the same point on the equator, so a dish can be fixed |
| Uplink | Signal sent from the ground up to the satellite |
| Downlink | Signal sent from the satellite down to receivers |
| Line of sight | An unblocked straight path between dish or phone and satellite |
| Latency (delay) | Time taken for a signal to travel to the satellite and back |

## Typical contexts and the point to make

| Context | Strongest point |
|---|---|
| Delivery firm | Sat nav saves fuel and time; tracking shows where each van is for customers |
| Hiker or sailor | GPS gives position with no phone signal; batteries and signal loss are the risks |
| Council planner | GIS overlays population, roads and flood-risk layers to choose a site |
| Ambulance service | GIS and GPS find the nearest ambulance and the quickest route |
| Remote farm family | Satellite TV reaches places with no cable network; weather can affect reception |
| Aid workers after an earthquake | Satellite phones work when masts are down; cost and need to be outdoors |

## Exam traps

- **Satellites do not track you.** GPS satellites only send signals; the receiver calculates position.
- **GPS is not sat nav.** Sat nav needs maps and route software as well as GPS.
- **GIS is not a map app.** Its defining feature is layered data that can be analysed and queried.
- **"Works anywhere"** must be qualified: it needs a view of the sky.
- **Vague disadvantages** ("it can go wrong") earn nothing; name the cause, such as signal loss in a tunnel.
- **Disadvantages of the satellite** are not the same as disadvantages for the user; answer for the person in the question.

## Self-test

1. What does a GPS satellite transmit?
2. Why does a receiver need signals from more than one satellite?
3. State two things sat nav adds to GPS.
4. Give two situations in which a sat nav may lose its signal.
5. Define a GIS.
6. Give two uses of a GIS by public services.
7. State one difference between GPS and a GIS.
8. Why must a satellite TV dish be pointed in one fixed direction?
9. Give one weather-related disadvantage of satellite television.
10. Give two groups of people who would benefit from satellite phones.
11. Explain why a satellite phone may be more useful than a mobile phone after a flood.
12. State two disadvantages of satellite phones.

## Self-test answers

1. Its position and the exact time the signal was sent.
2. One distance only gives a sphere of possible positions; distances from at least three satellites meet at one point, and a fourth corrects the receiver's clock.
3. Any two of: digital maps; route calculation; spoken directions; rerouting; estimated arrival time; traffic updates.
4. Any two of: in a tunnel; in an underground car park; among tall buildings; under dense trees.
5. A computer system that captures, stores, analyses and displays data linked to locations, organised in layers.
6. Any two of: emergency services finding the nearest vehicle; planning new buildings or roads; mapping flood risk; recording pipes and cables.
7. GPS works out a position; a GIS analyses layers of geographic data to answer questions about places.
8. The satellite is geostationary, so it stays in the same place in the sky.
9. Heavy rain or snow can weaken the signal so the picture breaks up.
10. Any two of: ships' crews; expedition teams; journalists in remote areas; aid and rescue workers.
11. Floods can damage mobile masts and power to them; a satellite phone connects directly to a satellite, so it still works.
12. Any two of: expensive handsets; expensive calls; needs a clear view of the sky so often not usable indoors; delay; slow data.
