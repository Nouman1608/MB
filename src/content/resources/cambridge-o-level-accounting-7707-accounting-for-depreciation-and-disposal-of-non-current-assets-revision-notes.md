## What the syllabus asks

1. Meaning of depreciation and the need to account for it.
2. Straight-line, reducing balance and revaluation methods.
3. Appropriate methods for different types of non-current asset.
4. Journal entries and ledger accounts for depreciation.
5. Journal entries for buying and selling non-current assets.
6. The non-current asset, provision for depreciation and disposal accounts.
7. Profit or loss on disposal.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Depreciation | Part of the cost of a non-current asset used up in a period |
| Residual value | Expected amount received for the asset at the end of its useful life |
| Useful life | Number of years the asset is expected to be used |
| Accumulated depreciation | Total depreciation charged on an asset since it was bought |
| Net book value | Cost − accumulated depreciation |
| Provision for depreciation account | Ledger account holding accumulated depreciation (credit balance) |
| Disposal account | Account used to find the profit or loss when an asset is sold |
| Obsolescence | Loss of usefulness because better technology is available |

## Why depreciate

- Matching: spreads cost over the years that benefit.
- Prevents overstated profit.
- Prevents overstated assets.
- Not a cash payment; not a replacement fund.

## Formulas

| Method | Formula | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Straight-line | (Cost − residual value) ÷ useful life, or cost × % | Equal each year |
| Reducing balance | Net book value at start of year × % | Falls each year |
| Revaluation | Opening value + additions − closing value | Varies |

**Monthly basis:** annual charge × months owned ÷ 12.
Example: $18,000 asset, 10% on cost, bought 1 August, year end 31 December
→ $18,000 × 10% × 5/12 = $750.

## Which method suits which asset

- Straight-line: fixtures and fittings, evenly used machinery, leases.
- Reducing balance: motor vehicles, computers (lose value fast, repairs rise
later).
- Revaluation: loose tools and other collections of small items.

## Double entries

| Event | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Buy asset by bank | Asset | Bank |
| Buy asset on credit | Asset | Supplier (other payable) |
| Annual depreciation | Statement of profit or loss | Provision for depreciation |
| Sale: transfer cost | Disposal | Asset |
| Sale: transfer accumulated depreciation | Provision for depreciation | Disposal |
| Sale: proceeds | Bank or cash | Disposal |
| Loss on disposal | Statement of profit or loss | Disposal |
| Profit on disposal | Disposal | Statement of profit or loss |

## Disposal account shape

```
Disposal account
Dr  Asset (cost)                      Cr  Provision for depreciation
    Profit on disposal (if any)           Bank/cash (proceeds)
                                          Loss on disposal (if any)
```

Profit or loss on disposal = proceeds − net book value.

## Statement presentation

- Statement of profit or loss: depreciation charge and loss on disposal as
expenses; profit on disposal as other income.
- Statement of financial position: cost, less accumulated depreciation,
equals net book value, for each class of asset.

## Exam traps

- Reducing balance on **net book value**, never on cost after the first
year.
- Deduct residual value only for straight-line on (cost − residual).
- Follow the stated policy on part years exactly.
- The provision for depreciation is a credit balance; never credit the
asset account with depreciation.
- Remove **both** the cost and the accumulated depreciation of the asset
sold.
- Depreciation in the year of sale depends on the policy: check it.

## Quick self-test

1. Define "depreciation".
2. Give **two** reasons why depreciation is charged.
3. A machine costs $30,000, has a residual value of $3,000 and a life of 6
years. Calculate straight-line depreciation.
4. A vehicle costs $20,000. Reducing balance at 30%. Calculate depreciation
for years 1 and 2.
5. Tools valued at $1,600 at the start; bought $450; valued at $1,380 at the
end. Calculate depreciation.
6. Equipment costing $9,600 is bought on 1 May; 25% per year on cost,
monthly basis; year end 31 December. Calculate the charge.
7. Give the journal entry for annual depreciation of $2,500 on fixtures.
8. An asset cost $8,000, accumulated depreciation $5,100, sold for $2,500.
Calculate the profit or loss on disposal.
9. Which side of the disposal account shows the proceeds?
10. Which method suits computers, and why?
11. Give the journal entry for a van bought on credit from Mobi Motors for
$16,000.
12. State **one** cause of depreciation.

### Answers

1. The estimated part of the cost of a non-current asset used up in an
accounting period.
2. Any two: matching cost with the revenue it helps earn; profit not
overstated; assets not overstated.
3. ($30,000 − $3,000) ÷ 6 = $4,500 per year.
4. Year 1: $6,000; Year 2: 30% × $14,000 = $4,200.
5. $1,600 + $450 − $1,380 = $670.
6. $9,600 × 25% × 8/12 = $1,600.
7. Dr Statement of profit or loss $2,500, Cr Provision for depreciation of
fixtures $2,500.
8. Net book value $2,900; loss of $400.
9. Credit side.
10. Reducing balance, because computers lose most of their value in the
early years through obsolescence.
11. Dr Motor vehicles $16,000, Cr Mobi Motors $16,000.
12. Any one: wear and tear; passage of time; obsolescence.
