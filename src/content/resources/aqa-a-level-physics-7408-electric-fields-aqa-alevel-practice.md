These questions cover sub-section 3.7.3 Electric fields of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408, AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards): Coulomb's law, electric field strength and electric potential. It is A-level only and assessed in Paper 2.

Revise from the [electric fields study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-electric-fields-aqa-alevel/) and [electric fields revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-electric-fields-aqa-alevel-revision-notes/) first. Mixed section 3.7 questions are in the [Fields and their consequences practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-fields-and-their-consequences-practice/).

Data: 1/4πε₀ = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻²; e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; electron mass 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg; proton mass 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg. Treat air as a vacuum.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define electric field strength and state its SI unit. **[2]**

**2.** Two point charges 0.040 m apart repel with a force of 2.7 × 10⁻⁴ N. The separation is increased to 0.12 m. Calculate the new force. **[2]**

**3.** State two properties of equipotential surfaces in an electric field. **[2]**

**4.** Show that the unit V m⁻¹ is equivalent to N C⁻¹. **[2]**

**5.** In a hydrogen atom the electron and proton are about 5.3 × 10⁻¹¹ m apart. Calculate the electrostatic force between them. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** Two horizontal plates 3.0 cm apart have a potential difference of 450 V. They are 6.0 cm long. An electron enters midway between them, moving parallel to the plates at 3.0 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the electric field strength between the plates. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the force on the electron. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the time the electron spends between the plates. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the vertical deflection of the electron as it leaves the plates. **[2]**
**(e)** Calculate the angle between the electron's path and its original direction as it leaves. **[2]**

**7.** A proton is accelerated from rest through a potential difference of 5.0 kV.

**(a)** Calculate the kinetic energy gained in joules. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate its final speed. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why an alpha particle accelerated through the same potential difference gains twice this kinetic energy. **[1]**

**8.** An isolated conducting sphere of radius 0.20 m carries a charge of +8.0 nC.

**(a)** Calculate the potential at its surface. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the electric field strength at its surface. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the potential at 0.50 m from its centre. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the work done moving a +2.0 nC charge from 0.50 m to the surface. **[2]**

**9.** Charges of +2.0 nC at A and +8.0 nC at B are 0.30 m apart.

**(a)** Calculate the distance from A of the point between them where the electric field strength is zero. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the electric potential at that point. **[2]**

**10.** Measurements near a charged sphere give V = 450 V at 0.080 m and V = 360 V at 0.100 m from its centre.

**(a)** Estimate the electric field strength at 0.090 m from the centre. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the charge on the sphere from the first reading. **[2]**
**(c)** State what the area under the E–r graph between 0.080 m and 0.100 m represents, and give its value. **[2]**

**11.** A 4.0 MeV alpha particle (charge +2e) travels directly towards a nucleus of charge +50e.

**(a)** Convert the kinetic energy to joules. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the distance of closest approach. **[3]**

**12.** Describe how equipotential lines between two electrodes can be mapped using conducting paper. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Force per unit positive charge acting on a small charge placed in the field, E = F/Q [1]; N C⁻¹ (or V m⁻¹) [1].

**2.** Separation × 3, so force × 1/9 [1]; F = 2.7 × 10⁻⁴/9 = **3.0 × 10⁻⁵ N** [1]

**3.** Any two, one mark each: potential is the same at every point on the surface; no work is done moving a charge along it; it is perpendicular to the field lines [2].

**4.** 1 V = 1 J C⁻¹ and 1 J = 1 N m [1]; V m⁻¹ = N m C⁻¹ m⁻¹ = N C⁻¹ [1]

**5.** F = 8.99 × 10⁹ × (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)²/(5.3 × 10⁻¹¹)² [1] = **8.2 × 10⁻⁸ N** [1]

**6. (a)** E = V/d = 450/0.030 = **1.5 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** F = eE = **2.4 × 10⁻¹⁵ N** [1]
**(c)** t = 0.060/3.0 × 10⁷ = **2.0 × 10⁻⁹ s** [1]
**(d)** a = F/m = 2.63 × 10¹⁵ m s⁻² [1]; y = ½at² = **5.3 × 10⁻³ m** towards the positive plate [1]
**(e)** v_y = at = 5.27 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹ [1]; θ = tan⁻¹(5.27 × 10⁶/3.0 × 10⁷) = **10°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the 6.0 cm plate length for the time and the 3.0 cm separation for E; swapping them is the classic slip.

**7. (a)** E_k = eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 5000 = **8.0 × 10⁻¹⁶ J** [1]
**(b)** v = √(2E_k/m) = √(2 × 8.0 × 10⁻¹⁶/1.67 × 10⁻²⁷) [1] = **9.8 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** Energy gained = QV and the alpha particle has twice the charge of a proton [1].

**8. (a)** V = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 8.0 × 10⁻⁹/0.20 = **360 V** [1]
**(b)** E = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 8.0 × 10⁻⁹/0.20² = **1.8 × 10³ V m⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** V = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 8.0 × 10⁻⁹/0.50 = **144 V** [1]
**(d)** ΔV = 360 − 144 = 216 V [1]; W = QΔV = 2.0 × 10⁻⁹ × 216 = **4.3 × 10⁻⁷ J** [1]

**9. (a)** Fields cancel: 2.0/x² = 8.0/(0.30 − x)² [1]; (0.30 − x)/x = 2 [1]; x = **0.10 m** from A [1]
**(b)** V = 8.99 × 10⁹ × (2.0 × 10⁻⁹/0.10 + 8.0 × 10⁻⁹/0.20) [1] = **540 V** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The field is zero here but the potential is not: like charges give potentials of the same sign, which add.

**10. (a)** E ≈ ΔV/Δr = (450 − 360)/0.020 [1] = **4.5 × 10³ V m⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** Q = 4πε₀rV = 0.080 × 450/8.99 × 10⁹ [1] = **4.0 × 10⁻⁹ C** [1]
**(c)** The potential difference between the two points [1]; **90 V** [1]

**11. (a)** E_k = 4.0 × 10⁶ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **6.4 × 10⁻¹³ J** [1]
**(b)** At closest approach E_k = electric potential energy = (1/4πε₀)Q₁Q₂/r [1]; r = 8.99 × 10⁹ × (2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)(50 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)/6.4 × 10⁻¹³ [1] = **3.6 × 10⁻¹⁴ m** [1]

**12.** Paint two electrodes on conducting paper and connect them to a d.c. supply [1]; connect a voltmeter between one electrode and a probe, and move the probe to find points with the same reading [1]; join points of equal potential to form each equipotential line, repeating for several values [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using plate length for d, or separation for the time between plates.
- Squaring r in the potential equation.
- Treating potentials as vectors or fields as scalars.
- Leaving energies in MeV or kV in an SI calculation.
- Taking zero field to mean zero potential.
