These questions cover **3.1.1 Use of SI units and their prefixes** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1. Section A has short recall and conversion questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [SI units and prefixes study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-use-of-si-units-and-their-prefixes-oxfordaqa/) or the [SI units and prefixes revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-use-of-si-units-and-their-prefixes-oxfordaqa-revision-notes/) first. Use e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C and electron mass 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg where needed.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** State the SI base unit of temperature and the SI base unit of amount of substance. **[2]**

**2.** Which of these is an SI base unit?

A: newton

B: coulomb

C: ampere

D: joule

**[1]**

**3.** Express the joule in SI base units. **[1]**

**4.** Write 0.45 μm in metres and 2.6 GPa in pascals, both in standard form. **[2]**

**5.** A cube has sides of length 3.0 cm. Calculate its volume in m³. **[2]**

**6.** A gamma photon has an energy of 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁴ J. Express this energy in MeV. **[2]**

**7.** Which is equal to 1 mm²?

A: 10⁻³ m²

B: 10⁻⁶ m²

C: 10⁻⁹ m²

D: 10⁻² m²

**[1]**

### Section B

**8.** Units can be built from defining equations.

**(a)** Use the definitions of work and power to show that the watt can be written as kg m² s⁻³. **[2]**

**(b)** Express the unit of momentum in SI base units and show that it is equivalent to the newton second. **[2]**

**(c)** Express the unit of pressure in SI base units. **[1]**

**9.** A copper wire has a diameter of 0.38 mm and a length of 1.50 m.

**(a)** Calculate the cross-sectional area of the wire in m². **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of the wire in m³ and in mm³. **[2]**

**(c)** The density of copper is 8.96 g cm⁻³. Convert this to kg m⁻³ and calculate the mass of the wire in grams. **[2]**

**10.** An electron is accelerated from rest through a potential difference of 2.5 kV.

**(a)** State its kinetic energy in electronvolts. **[1]**

**(b)** Convert this energy to joules. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the speed of the electron. **[2]**

**11.** An electric shower is rated at 2.4 kW. It is used for 12 minutes each day for 30 days.

**(a)** Calculate the energy transferred in kW h. **[2]**

**(b)** Convert your answer to joules. **[1]**

**(c)** A student calculates 2.4 × 12 × 30 = 864 and writes "864 kJ". Explain the student's error and state what unit the number 864 actually has. **[2]**

**12.** A laser pointer emits light of wavelength 650 nm with an output power of 5.0 mW.

**(a)** Write the wavelength in metres in standard form. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy emitted in 2.0 minutes. **[2]**

**(c)** Each photon has an energy of 3.06 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. Express this in eV. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the number of photons emitted per second. **[2]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** Kelvin, **K** [1]; mole, **mol** [1] **[2]**

**2.** **C** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The coulomb is derived (A s). Only the ampere is a base unit among these.

**3.** J = N m = **kg m² s⁻²** [1] **[1]**

**4.** **4.5 × 10⁻⁷ m** [1]; **2.6 × 10⁹ Pa** [1] **[2]**

**5.** Volume = 3.0³ = 27 cm³ [1]; 27 × 10⁻⁶ = **2.7 × 10⁻⁵ m³** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Converting 3.0 cm to 0.030 m first and cubing gives the same answer; cubing 3.0 and then multiplying by 10⁻² does not.

**6.** 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 4.0 × 10⁵ eV [1]; = **0.40 MeV** [1] **[2]**

**7.** **B** [1] **[1]**

### Section B

**8.**

**(a)** W = Fs, so J = N m = kg m s⁻² × m = kg m² s⁻² [1]; P = W/t, so W = J s⁻¹ = **kg m² s⁻³** [1]

**(b)** p = mv: kg × m s⁻¹ = **kg m s⁻¹** [1]; N s = kg m s⁻² × s = kg m s⁻¹, the same [1]

**(c)** Pa = N m⁻² = **kg m⁻¹ s⁻²** [1] **[5]**

**9.**

**(a)** r = 0.19 mm = 1.9 × 10⁻⁴ m [1]; A = π(1.9 × 10⁻⁴)² = **1.13 × 10⁻⁷ m²** [1]

**(b)** V = 1.13 × 10⁻⁷ × 1.50 = **1.70 × 10⁻⁷ m³** [1]; 1.70 × 10⁻⁷ ÷ 10⁻⁹ = **170 mm³** [1]

**(c)** 8.96 g cm⁻³ = **8960 kg m⁻³** [1]; m = 8960 × 1.70 × 10⁻⁷ = 1.52 × 10⁻³ kg = **1.52 g** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Using the diameter in place of the radius gives an area four times too big. Halve before you square.

**10.**

**(a)** **2500 eV** (2.5 keV) [1]

**(b)** 2500 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **4.0 × 10⁻¹⁶ J** [1]

**(c)** ½mv² = 4.0 × 10⁻¹⁶, so v = √(2 × 4.0 × 10⁻¹⁶ ÷ 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) [1] = **3.0 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Substituting 2500 (the eV value) into ½mv² gives a speed about 10⁹ times too large. Energy must be in joules.

**11.**

**(a)** Time = 12 × 30 = 360 minutes = 6.0 h [1]; E = 2.4 × 6.0 = **14.4 kW h** [1]

**(b)** 14.4 × 3.6 × 10⁶ = **5.2 × 10⁷ J** [1]

**(c)** The student multiplied kW by minutes, so the time was not converted to seconds (or hours) [1]; 864 is in **kW min**, which is 864 × 1000 × 60 = 5.2 × 10⁷ J, not 864 kJ [1] **[5]**

**12.**

**(a)** **6.50 × 10⁻⁷ m** [1]

**(b)** E = Pt = 5.0 × 10⁻³ × 120 [1] = **0.60 J** [1]

**(c)** 3.06 × 10⁻¹⁹ ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **1.91 eV** [1]

**(d)** Number per second = power ÷ energy per photon = 5.0 × 10⁻³ ÷ 3.06 × 10⁻¹⁹ [1] = **1.6 × 10¹⁶ s⁻¹** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* 5.0 mW is 5.0 × 10⁻³ W, not 5.0 × 10⁶ W. A photon count of order 10²⁵ should make you check the prefix.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using g or cm in an equation that needs kg or m.
- Converting squared or cubed units with the single power of ten.
- Leaving a named unit in an answer that asks for base units.
- Putting an energy in eV straight into ½mv².
- Mixing kW with minutes or seconds in a kW h calculation.
