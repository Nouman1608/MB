specification, Version 5.2, for exams in May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered and any part of the

You should also be familiar with experiments on diffusion and osmosis and with the terms **isotonic, hypotonic,
hypertonic, turgor and plasmolysis**.

## Diffusion

**Diffusion** is the spreading of the particles of any substance in solution, or particles of a gas, resulting in a
net movement from an area of higher concentration to an area of lower concentration. The greater the difference in
concentration, the faster the rate of diffusion.

- Particles move randomly. Net movement is down the **concentration gradient** because more particles leave the
  crowded region than the empty region.
- Dissolved substances can move into and out of cells by diffusion.
- **Oxygen required for respiration passes through cell membranes by diffusion.** Oxygen is used up in the cell, so
  its concentration inside stays lower than outside and a gradient is kept.

Diffusion needs no energy from the cell. A steeper gradient gives a faster rate, so anything that keeps the gradient
steep (removing the substance on one side, supplying it on the other) speeds up diffusion.

## Osmosis

**Osmosis** is the diffusion of water from a dilute to a more concentrated solution through a partially permeable
membrane that allows the passage of water molecules.

Differences in concentration of the solutions inside and outside a cell cause water to diffuse into or out of the
cell by osmosis. The three terms describe the outside solution compared with the cell.

| Term | Meaning (compared with the cell contents) | Effect on an animal cell | Effect on a plant cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypotonic | Outside solution is more dilute | Water enters; the cell may swell and burst | Water enters; the cell becomes firm (**turgor**) |
| Isotonic | Same concentration | No net movement | No net movement; cell flaccid |
| Hypertonic | Outside solution is more concentrated | Water leaves; the cell shrinks | Water leaves; the membrane pulls away from the wall (**plasmolysis**) |

**Turgor** is the pressure of the cell contents pushing out against the cell wall. A turgid plant cell is firm and
helps support the plant. In **plasmolysis** the cell membrane pulls away from the cell wall because so much water
has left the vacuole and cytoplasm. A plant cell has a cell wall so it does not burst in a dilute solution, but an
animal cell has no wall and may.

### Required practical: concentrations separated by a partially permeable membrane

A typical method uses cylinders of potato placed in sucrose solutions of different concentrations.

1. Cut potato cylinders of the same length and diameter. Dry each lightly with a paper towel and measure its mass.
2. Place one cylinder in each of several solutions (for example 0.0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6 and 0.8 mol/dm³) for the same time.
3. Remove, dry in the same way and measure the final mass.
4. Calculate the **percentage change in mass** for each cylinder and plot a graph of percentage change against
   concentration.

```
percentage change = (final mass − initial mass) ÷ initial mass × 100
```

The independent variable is the concentration. The dependent variable is the percentage change in mass. Control
variables include temperature, time, the volume of solution and the source and size of the potato.

### Worked example 1: the potato practical

Invented data. Each cylinder started at 2.50 g.

| Concentration (mol/dm³) | 0.0 | 0.2 | 0.4 | 0.6 | 0.8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Final mass (g) | 2.90 | 2.68 | 2.52 | 2.27 | 2.10 |

```
0.0: (2.90 − 2.50) ÷ 2.50 × 100 = +16.0%
0.2: (2.68 − 2.50) ÷ 2.50 × 100 = +7.2%
0.4: (2.52 − 2.50) ÷ 2.50 × 100 = +0.8%
0.6: (2.27 − 2.50) ÷ 2.50 × 100 = −9.2%
0.8: (2.10 − 2.50) ÷ 2.50 × 100 = −16.0%
```

On the graph, the line crosses zero between 0.4 and 0.6. Using a straight line between the two points:

```
zero crossing = 0.4 + 0.2 × (0.8 ÷ (0.8 + 9.2)) = 0.4 + 0.2 × 0.08 = 0.416 mol/dm³
```

At that concentration the potato neither gains nor loses water overall, so the solution is **isotonic** with the
potato cells, at about 0.42 mol/dm³. In weaker solutions the solution is hypotonic and the cylinder gains mass; in
stronger solutions it is hypertonic and the cylinder loses mass. Percentage change allows for small differences in
the starting masses.

## Active transport

Substances are sometimes absorbed **against a concentration gradient**, from low to high concentration. This
requires **energy from respiration**; the process is called **active transport**.

| | Diffusion | Osmosis | Active transport |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direction | Down a gradient | Water, dilute to concentrated, partially permeable membrane | Against a gradient |
| Energy from respiration needed? | No | No | **Yes** |
| Example | Oxygen into cells | Water into a root hair cell | Ions into a root hair cell |

Examples from the specification:

- **Plants** absorb ions from very dilute solutions, for example by root hair cells.
- In **animals**, sugar may be absorbed from low concentrations in the intestine and from low concentrations in the
  kidney tubules.

Cells that do a lot of active transport have many mitochondria to supply the energy, which links this idea back to
3.1.1. If the supply of oxygen to a root is cut, ion uptake falls because respiration slows.

## Surface area to volume ratio and exchange

A **single-celled organism** has a relatively large surface area to volume ratio, so all necessary exchanges can
occur across its surface membrane. As an organism becomes larger and more complex, the ratio falls and exchange
becomes more difficult.

### Worked example 2: ratio for cubes

Take cubes with sides of 1 cm, 2 cm and 4 cm.

```
1 cm: surface 6 × 1² = 6 cm²,   volume 1 cm³,  ratio 6.0
2 cm: surface 6 × 2² = 24 cm²,  volume 8 cm³,  ratio 3.0
4 cm: surface 6 × 4² = 96 cm²,  volume 64 cm³, ratio 1.5
```

The ratio halves each time the side doubles. Diffusion into a 4 cm cube would take much longer to reach the centre
than into a 1 cm cube, so a large organism needs specialised exchange surfaces.

## Effective exchange surfaces

In multicellular organisms many organ systems are specialised for exchanging materials. The effectiveness of an
exchange surface is increased by:

- a **large surface area** that is **thin**, to provide a short diffusion path;
- in animals, an **efficient blood supply**;
- in animals, for gaseous exchange, being **ventilated**.

| Exchange surface | How it is adapted |
|---|---|
| Small intestine | Folded into villi for a large surface area; thin lining for a short diffusion path; rich blood supply to carry absorbed food away |
| Lungs | Many alveoli give a large surface area; thin walls; rich blood supply; ventilated by breathing |
| Roots | Root hair cells have a long extension for a large surface area; thin wall |
| Leaves | Thin and flat for a short diffusion path; air spaces in the spongy mesophyll; stomata let gases in and out |

### Worked example 3: effect of folding

A flat piece of lining is 5 cm × 5 cm. It is folded into 10 ridges, each 5 cm long and 0.5 cm high. Treat each ridge as adding two sides of 5 cm × 0.5 cm, and ignore the tops.

```
flat area = 5 × 5 = 25 cm²
one ridge adds 2 × (5 × 0.5) = 5 cm²
10 ridges add 10 × 5 = 50 cm²
total area = 25 + 50 = 75 cm²
increase = 75 ÷ 25 = 3 times the flat area
```

Folding gives three times the surface for the same footprint. The real small intestine has villi and microscopic
folds, so the increase is far larger.

Always link the feature to the reason: "thin walls give a short diffusion path, so diffusion is faster".

## Common mistakes

- Saying osmosis is "the movement of water from low to high water concentration" without stating the partially
  permeable membrane. Link it to the specification wording: dilute to more concentrated solution.
- Saying water moves "towards the area of less water" and not saying which solution is dilute.
- Writing that active transport moves substances "down" a gradient, or that it needs no energy.
- Forgetting that active transport uses energy from respiration, and so needs mitochondria and oxygen.
- Saying plant cells burst in dilute solution. The cell wall resists the swelling, so the cell becomes turgid.
- Giving "large surface area" without "thin" and "blood supply" for animal exchange surfaces.
- Calculating percentage change using the final mass in the denominator.

## Exam technique

- **Describe** the practical as a series of steps and name the variables controlled.
- **Calculate** the percentage change, show the working and keep the sign (+ or −).
- **Explain** a result using the terms hypotonic, isotonic or hypertonic and the direction of water movement.
- For exchange surfaces, give feature, reason and effect together.
