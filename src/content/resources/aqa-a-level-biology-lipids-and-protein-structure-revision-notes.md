## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Condensation | Joining two molecules with the loss of one water molecule |
| Hydrolysis | Breaking a bond by adding one water molecule |
| Ester bond | Forms between an OH of glycerol and the COOH of a fatty acid |
| Saturated | Fatty acid chain with no C=C double bonds |
| Unsaturated | Fatty acid chain with at least one C=C double bond |
| Hydrophilic | Attracted to water; polar or charged |
| Hydrophobic | Repelled by water; non-polar |
| Peptide bond | C-N bond between the carboxyl of one amino acid and the amine of the next |
| Primary structure | Sequence of amino acids in a polypeptide |
| Quaternary structure | Arrangement of two or more polypeptides in one protein |

## Lipids in steps

**Triglyceride formation**

1. One glycerol plus three fatty acids.
2. Three condensation reactions, each releasing one water.
3. Three ester bonds in the product.

**Spotting saturation in a diagram**

1. Follow the hydrocarbon chain from the COOH end.
2. Any C=C means unsaturated. Only single bonds means saturated.
3. A bend in the drawn chain usually marks the double bond.

### Triglyceride versus phospholipid

| Feature | Triglyceride | Phospholipid |
|---|---|---|
| Parts | Glycerol and three fatty acids | Glycerol, two fatty acids and a phosphate-containing group |
| Polarity | Non-polar overall | Hydrophilic head, hydrophobic tails |
| Behaviour in water | Does not mix; forms separate droplets or a layer | Forms a bilayer, heads out and tails in |
| Main roles | Energy store, insulation, protection, metabolic water | Structure of cell-surface membranes |

### Why the properties follow from the structure

- Many C-H bonds in long chains: more energy per gram than carbohydrate.
- Non-polar: insoluble, so no effect on water potential inside a cell.
- High hydrogen content: more metabolic water when oxidised.
- Poor heat conductor and low density: insulation, cushioning and buoyancy.
- Saturated chains are straight and pack closely, so they tend to be solid. Unsaturated chains bend, pack loosely and tend to be liquid at room temperature.
- Phospholipid heads face water, tails face inward: the bilayer core blocks water-soluble substances.

## Proteins in steps

**Amino acid:** one central carbon carrying four attachments: an amine group (NH2), a carboxyl group (COOH), a single hydrogen and an R group. The twenty amino acids found in all organisms differ only in R.

**Peptide formation:** carboxyl of amino acid 1 reacts with amine of amino acid 2, forming a peptide bond and one water molecule. A chain of n amino acids contains one bond fewer than n.

**Counting possibilities:** a chain of length n can be built in 20 to the power n different sequences.

### The four levels

| Level | What it is | Held by |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Order of amino acids | Peptide bonds |
| Secondary | Alpha helix or beta pleated sheet | Hydrogen bonds along the backbone |
| Tertiary | Specific three-dimensional fold of one polypeptide | Hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, disulfide bridges between R groups |
| Quaternary | Two or more polypeptides together | Same types of bond between chains |

### Bonds at a glance

| Bond | Strength | Sensitive to |
|---|---|---|
| Hydrogen | Weak individually | Heat |
| Ionic | Stronger | Changes in pH |
| Disulfide | Strongest; covalent, between cysteine sulfur atoms | Only harsher treatment |

**Structure to function:** the sequence fixes the folding, the folding gives the shape, and the shape allows the job. Globular proteins are compact and soluble. Fibrous proteins, such as collagen, are long, cross-linked, insoluble and strong.

## Food tests

| Test | Method | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emulsion (lipid) | Shake sample with ethanol, pour into water | Cloudy white emulsion | Stays clear |
| Biuret (protein) | Add sodium hydroxide, then dilute copper(II) sulfate | Lilac or purple | Stays blue |

## Small worked reminders

**Formula of a triglyceride.** Oleic acid is C18H34O2 and glycerol is C3H8O3.

1. Add: C3H8O3 + 3 C18H34O2 gives C57H110O9.
2. Subtract three waters (H6O3): C57H104O6.
3. Relative formula mass with Ar values C 12, H 1, O 16: (57 × 12) + 104 + (6 × 16) = 884.
4. Check: reactants 92 + (3 × 282) = 938; products 884 + 54 = 938.

**Peptide bonds in a multi-chain protein.** Three polypeptides have 10, 12 and 14 amino acids.

1. Bonds per chain: 9, 11 and 13.
2. Total: 33 peptide bonds, so 33 water molecules released in synthesis.

**Reading a fatty acid formula.** A saturated acid with 18 carbons is C18H36O2. An unsaturated one with the same chain length and one C=C is C18H34O2. Each double bond removes two hydrogen atoms.

## Interpreting test results

| Tube | Contents | After emulsion test | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Vegetable oil | Cloudy white | Lipid present |
| B | Glucose solution | Clear | No lipid detected |
| C | Egg white solution after biuret test | Purple | Protein present |
| D | Water after biuret test | Blue | No protein; a negative control |

Always say what the result shows, not only the colour. A control tube of water confirms that the reagents themselves do not cause the colour.

## Sentence frames for extended answers

- "The R group contains sulfur, so two of them can form a disulfide bridge, which holds the tertiary structure in place."
- "The chain is straight because it has no C=C, so molecules pack closely and intermolecular forces are stronger."
- "The head is hydrophilic so it faces the water; the tails are hydrophobic so they face inward, forming a bilayer."
- "A change in the sequence changes which R groups meet, so different bonds form and the tertiary shape changes."

## Protein examples to link structure and property

| Protein | Structure feature | Property it explains |
|---|---|---|
| Haemoglobin | Four polypeptides folded compactly (quaternary) | Soluble in cytoplasm, so it can be carried inside red blood cells |
| Collagen | Three long polypeptides wound together with cross-links | Strong and insoluble, so it resists stretching in tissues |
| An enzyme | Tertiary fold gives a specific active site shape | Binds only substrates that fit |
| Any protein | Heat or extreme pH breaks hydrogen and ionic bonds | The shape changes and the protein stops working |

## Command words for this topic

- **State** or **name**: a short fact, such as the name of a bond. No explanation needed.
- **Describe**: say what happens or what the structure is, such as how a triglyceride forms.
- **Explain**: give a reason that links structure to property, using words such as "because" and "so".
- **Compare**: give a similarity or difference for both items, such as triglyceride and phospholipid.
- **Suggest**: apply knowledge to an unfamiliar example, for instance an unfamiliar fatty acid diagram.

## Must-know distinctions

- Ester bond (lipid) versus peptide bond (protein).
- Peptide bonds (primary) versus hydrogen bonds (secondary).
- Disulfide bridge (needs sulfur, tertiary) versus hydrogen bond (any level above primary).
- Hydrophilic head versus hydrophobic tails.
- Saturated (no C=C) versus unsaturated (C=C present).

## Quick self-test

1. How many water molecules are released when one triglyceride forms?
2. State the name of the bond between glycerol and a fatty acid.
3. A fatty acid chain contains one C=C. Is it saturated or unsaturated?
4. Which part of a phospholipid is hydrophobic?
5. How many peptide bonds join the 80 amino acids of one polypeptide?
6. How many different dipeptides can be made from the 20 common amino acids?
7. Name the two types of secondary structure.
8. Which bond forms between the R groups of two cysteine residues?
9. Which bond type is most affected by a change in pH?
10. A sample turns purple after sodium hydroxide and copper(II) sulfate are added. What does this show?
11. Why is ethanol used in the emulsion test?
12. Why does a protein of four polypeptides have quaternary structure while one polypeptide does not?

### Answers

1. Three.
2. An ester bond.
3. Unsaturated.
4. The two fatty acid tails.
5. 79.
6. 20 × 20 = 400.
7. Alpha helix and beta pleated sheet.
8. A disulfide bridge.
9. Ionic bonds, because pH changes alter the charges on R groups.
10. Peptide bonds are present, so protein is present.
11. Lipids do not dissolve in water, so ethanol dissolves the lipid before it is added to water.
12. Quaternary structure is the arrangement of two or more polypeptides; a single chain has only the first three levels.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "fats are insoluble because they are big" instead of "non-polar, so hydrophobic".
- Stating that a phospholipid has three fatty acids.
- Calling any C-H chain "saturated" without checking for a double bond.
- Saying hydrogen bonds hold the primary structure.
- Leaving out the loss of water in condensation, or the use of water in hydrolysis.
- Writing that unsaturated fats pack closely together.
- Describing the biuret test as detecting amino acids rather than peptide bonds.
- Giving the emulsion result as a colour change, when it is cloudiness.
- Writing 80 peptide bonds for a chain of 80 amino acids.
