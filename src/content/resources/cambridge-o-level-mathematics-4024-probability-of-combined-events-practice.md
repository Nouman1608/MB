These questions cover section 8.3, Probability of combined events, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The methods are explained in the [probability of combined events study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-probability-of-combined-events/), and the [probability of combined events revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-probability-of-combined-events-revision-notes/) summarise them. Give answers as fractions in their simplest form or as decimals. Draw tree diagrams with probabilities beside the branches and outcomes at the ends.

## Section A

**1.** Two fair dice are rolled and the scores are added. Find the probability that the total is

**(a)** 8,

**(b)** at least 8,

**(c)** at least one dice shows a 6. **[3]**

**2.** A fair coin is flipped and a fair spinner with sections numbered 1 to 4 is spun. Find the probability of tails and an even number. **[2]**

**3.** A and B are independent events, with P(A) = 0.3 and P(B) = 0.5. Find P(A ∩ B) and P(A ∪ B). **[3]**

**4.** P(A) = 0.6, P(B) = 0.35 and P(A ∪ B) = 0.8. Find P(A ∩ B). **[2]**

**5.** A bag has 3 yellow and 7 green counters. One is chosen, its colour noted, and it is replaced. A second counter is then chosen. Find the probability that

**(a)** both are yellow,

**(b)** one of each colour,

**(c)** at least one is yellow. **[4]**

## Section B

**6.** A bag has 6 red and 4 white balls. Two balls are taken from the bag without replacement.

**(a)** Draw a tree diagram to show the possible outcomes, with probabilities.

**(b)** Find the probability that both are red.

**(c)** Find the probability that both are white.

**(d)** Find the probability that they are different colours. **[5]**

**7.** The probability that it rains is 0.25. If it rains, the probability that the school bus is late is 0.5. If it does not rain, the probability that the bus is late is 0.2. Find the probability that the bus is late. **[4]**

**8.** P(A) = 0.45, P(B) = 0.35 and P(A ∩ B) = 0.1. Find

**(a)** the probability that exactly one of the events happens,

**(b)** the probability that neither happens. **[3]**

**9.** A fair coin is flipped three times. Find the probability of

**(a)** exactly two heads,

**(b)** at least one head. **[3]**

**10.** Two cards are dealt from a pack of 52 cards without replacement. Find the probability that both are aces. **[2]**

**11.** Spinner X has sections 1, 2, 3 and spinner Y has sections 1, 2, 3, 4. Each is spun once and the scores are multiplied. Find the probability that the product is even. **[3]**

**12.** A footballer scores a penalty with probability 0.8, independently each time. He takes three penalties. Find the probability that he

**(a)** scores all three,

**(b)** scores at least one. **[3]**

**13.** A bag has 3 black and 5 white balls. Two are taken without replacement. Find the probability that the second ball is black. **[3]**

**14.** A bag contains the letters A, B, B, C, C, C. Two letters are taken without replacement. Find the probability that

**(a)** both are C,

**(b)** the two letters are different. **[4]**

**15.** Of 100 students, 60 like music (M), 45 like sport (S) and 20 like neither. Find

**(a)** the number who like both,

**(b)** P(M only). **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The sample space has 36 equally likely outcomes. **(a)** Total 8 occurs 5 times, so **5/36** [1]. **(b)** Totals 8 to 12: 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 15, so 15/36 = **5/12** [1]. **(c)** At least one 6: 11 outcomes, so **11/36** [1].

**2.** P(tails) = 1/2 and P(even) = 2/4 = 1/2 [1]. Product = **1/4** [1].

**3.** P(A ∩ B) = 0.3 × 0.5 = **0.15** [1]. P(A ∪ B) = 0.3 + 0.5 − 0.15 [1] = **0.65** [1].

**4.** 0.8 = 0.6 + 0.35 − P(A ∩ B) [1], so P(A ∩ B) = 0.95 − 0.8 = **0.15** [1].

**5.** **(a)** 0.3 × 0.3 = **0.09** [1]. **(b)** 0.3 × 0.7 + 0.7 × 0.3 = **0.42** [1][1]. **(c)** 1 − 0.7 × 0.7 = 1 − 0.49 = **0.51** [1].

**6. (a)** First pick: red 6/10, white 4/10. After red: red 5/9, white 4/9. After white: red 6/9, white 3/9 [1].

**(b)** 6/10 × 5/9 = 30/90 = **1/3** [1].

**(c)** 4/10 × 3/9 = 12/90 = **2/15** [1].

**(d)** 1 − 1/3 − 2/15 = **8/15** [1]. Check: 6/10 × 4/9 + 4/10 × 6/9 = 48/90 = 8/15 [1].

**7.** Rain and late = 0.25 × 0.5 = 0.125 [1]. No rain and late = 0.75 × 0.2 = 0.15 [1][1]. P(late) = 0.125 + 0.15 = **0.275** [1].

**8. (a)** A only = 0.35 and B only = 0.25, so exactly one = **0.6** [1][1]. **(b)** 1 − (0.35 + 0.1 + 0.25) = **0.3** [1].

**9.** There are 8 equally likely outcomes [1]. **(a)** Exactly two heads: HHT, HTH, THH, so **3/8** [1]. **(b)** 1 − P(no heads) = 1 − 1/8 = **7/8** [1].

**10.** P = 4/52 × 3/51 [1] = 12/2652 = **1/221** [1].

**11.** There are 3 × 4 = 12 equally likely outcomes [1]. A product is odd only when both scores are odd: 2 × 2 = 4 outcomes [1]. P(even) = 8/12 = **2/3** [1].

**12. (a)** 0.8 × 0.8 × 0.8 = **0.512** [1]. **(b)** 1 − 0.2³ = 1 − 0.008 [1] = **0.992** [1].

**13.** P(black then black) = 3/8 × 2/7 = 6/56 [1]. P(white then black) = 5/8 × 3/7 = 15/56 [1]. Total = 21/56 = **3/8** [1].

**14. (a)** 3/6 × 2/5 [1] = 6/30 = **1/5** [1]. **(b)** P(same) = P(BB) + P(CC) = 2/6 × 1/5 + 6/30 = 2/30 + 6/30 = 8/30 [1]. P(different) = 22/30 = **11/15** [1].

**15. (a)** Students liking music or sport = 100 − 20 = 80 [1]. Both = 60 + 45 − 80 = **25** [1]. **(b)** M only = 60 − 25 = 35 [1], so P = **35/100 = 7/20** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding along branches or multiplying between paths.
- Using the same probabilities on the second pick when there is no replacement.
- Missing a path, such as RB and BR both being needed.
- Subtracting nothing for the overlap when adding P(A) and P(B).
- Not simplifying fractions.
