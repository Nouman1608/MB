## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Replication | Production of two DNA molecules with base sequences identical to the original |
| Semi-conservative | Each new molecule contains one parental strand and one newly made strand |
| Template strand | Existing strand whose bases determine the order of bases in the new strand |
| Complementary base pairing | A with T (two hydrogen bonds), G with C (three hydrogen bonds) |
| Helicase | Unwinds the helix and breaks hydrogen bonds between the strands |
| DNA polymerase | Joins nucleotides into a new strand complementary to the template |
| Primer | Short single strand that pairs with the template and gives polymerase a starting point |
| Taq polymerase | Heat-stable DNA polymerase from *Thermus aquaticus*, used in PCR |
| Short tandem repeat | Region where a short sequence is repeated a variable number of times between people |
| Okazaki fragment (HL) | Short section of new DNA made on the lagging strand |
| Proofreading (HL) | Removal of a mismatched nucleotide from the 3' end by DNA polymerase III, then replacement |

## D1.1.1 Why replication is needed

- Reproduction: offspring or daughter cells need a full genome.
- Growth: every cell made by mitosis needs every gene.
- Tissue replacement: worn-out cells are replaced by mitosis.
- Happens before division, giving two identical sister chromatids.

## D1.1.2 Semi-conservative replication

- Accuracy: only one base fits each template position, and a purine always pairs with a pyrimidine.
- Evidence: ¹⁵N-labelled *E. coli* DNA copied in ¹⁴N, separated by density in caesium chloride.

| Generation in ¹⁴N | Observed bands | Model ruled out |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One intermediate band | Conservative (predicts heavy plus light) |
| 2 | Intermediate and light, equal amounts | Dispersive (predicts one band) |

- After *n* rounds: 2ⁿ molecules, always 2 hybrids, 2ⁿ − 2 fully light.

**Method: fraction of hybrid molecules**
1. Total molecules = 2ⁿ.
2. Hybrids = 2.
3. Fraction = 2 ÷ 2ⁿ. For n = 4, 2/16 = 1/8.

## D1.1.3 Two enzymes, two bond types

| Bond | Holds together | Acted on by |
|---|---|---|
| Hydrogen bonds | The two strands | Broken by helicase |
| Covalent sugar-phosphate bonds | Each strand | Made by DNA polymerase |

## D1.1.4 PCR and gel electrophoresis

| PCR step | Temperature (typical) | Event |
|---|---|---|
| Denaturation | about 95 °C | Hydrogen bonds break, strands separate |
| Annealing | about 50 to 60 °C | Primers bind complementary sequences |
| Extension | about 72 °C | Taq polymerase extends primers |

- Two primers, one per strand, pointing towards each other; they fix the ends of the copied region.
- Taq survives 95 °C, so it is not replaced each cycle.
- Copies about double per cycle; growth levels off as primers and nucleotides run out.
- Gel: DNA is negative (phosphate groups) and moves to the positive electrode.
- All fragments have the same charge per unit length, so separation is by size; shorter fragments move further.
- Size is read against a ladder of known fragment lengths run in another lane.

## D1.1.5 Applications

- DNA profiling: PCR at several short tandem repeat regions, then sizing on a gel.
- Forensic: one mismatched region excludes a person; a full match is consistent with, not proof of, a common source.
- Paternity: every child band must be present in the mother or the alleged father.
- More regions tested means a lower chance of a coincidental match (probabilities of independent regions multiply).

## HL only: D1.1.6 to D1.1.9

- 5' end: phosphate on carbon 5'. 3' end: hydroxyl on carbon 3'.
- Polymerase adds the 5' phosphate of a new nucleotide to the 3' end of the strand, so synthesis is 5' to 3'.
- Leading strand: continuous, towards the fork, one primer.
- Lagging strand: discontinuous, away from the fork, Okazaki fragments, a primer for each.

| Enzyme (prokaryote) | Job |
|---|---|
| DNA primase | Makes RNA primers |
| DNA polymerase III | Extends primers with DNA; proofreads |
| DNA polymerase I | Removes RNA primers and replaces them with DNA |
| DNA ligase | Seals nicks in the sugar-phosphate backbone between fragments |

**Order on the lagging strand:** helicase, primase, polymerase III, polymerase I, ligase.

## Exam traps

- First-generation Meselson–Stahl data alone do not rule out dispersive replication.
- Gel separation is by size, not by charge.
- PCR heat breaks hydrogen bonds only, not the backbone.
- A profile match gives a probability, not certainty.
- Both new strands are made 5' to 3', including the lagging strand.
- Primase makes RNA, not DNA; polymerase I, not ligase, removes primers.
- Proofreading acts at the 3' end of the growing strand only.

## Self-test

**1.** State the base-pairing rule and the number of hydrogen bonds in each pair.

**2.** What did Meselson and Stahl observe after one generation in ¹⁴N, and which model did it rule out?

**3.** Which result ruled out dispersive replication?

**4.** After 5 rounds of replication in ¹⁴N, what fraction of molecules are hybrid?

**5.** Name the bond broken by helicase and the bond made by DNA polymerase.

**6.** Why are two primers needed in PCR?

**7.** Give the reason for the annealing temperature being lower than the denaturation temperature.

**8.** Why do DNA fragments of different lengths separate on a gel even though they all carry negative charge?

**9.** A scene sample shows one band at a region. What does this tell you about the person's two copies of that region?

**10.** (HL) Why is the lagging strand made in fragments?

**11.** (HL) Put these in order for one Okazaki fragment: ligase, primase, polymerase I, polymerase III.

**12.** (HL) Describe proofreading in one sentence.

## Self-test answers

**1.** A pairs with T (two hydrogen bonds); G pairs with C (three hydrogen bonds).

**2.** A single intermediate band; this ruled out conservative replication.

**3.** The second-generation result: two bands, intermediate and light, rather than one band.

**4.** 2 ÷ 2⁵ = 2/32 = 1/16.

**5.** Helicase breaks hydrogen bonds between bases; DNA polymerase makes covalent bonds between nucleotides in the backbone.

**6.** One primer pairs with each strand at opposite ends of the target, so both strands are copied and the copied region is fixed at both ends.

**7.** At 95 °C hydrogen bonds cannot form; at the lower annealing temperature primers can form hydrogen bonds with their complementary sequences.

**8.** Charge per unit length is the same, so the gel mesh separates them by size: shorter fragments move further.

**9.** Both copies (one from each parent) are the same length.

**10.** Its template runs the opposite way to the fork's movement, and polymerase can only build 5' to 3', so each new length is made as the fork exposes more template.

**11.** Primase, polymerase III, polymerase I, ligase.

**12.** DNA polymerase III removes a nucleotide with a mismatched base from the 3' end of the new strand and replaces it with a correctly paired one.
