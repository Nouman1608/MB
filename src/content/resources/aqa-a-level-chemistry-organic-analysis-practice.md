These questions cover section **3.3.6 Organic analysis** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification
(7404/7405), version 1.1, 1 December 2015, for AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards: 3.3.6.1 test-tube
reactions with Required practical 6, 3.3.6.2 mass spectrometry and 3.3.6.3 infrared spectroscopy. It is AS
content, examined on Paper 2 at AS and A-level, and A-level Paper 3 can test any content. The carbonate test
for carboxylic acids draws on 3.3.9.1, which the specification marks **A-level only**.

Learn the ideas first in the [organic analysis study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-organic-analysis/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-organic-analysis-revision-notes/). The course hub is

**Data for this set.** Precise relative masses: ¹H = 1.0078, ¹²C = 12.0000, ¹⁴N = 14.0031, ¹⁶O = 15.9949.
Infrared ranges (cm⁻¹): O–H (alcohols) 3230–3550; O–H (acids) 2500–3000; N–H (amines) 3300–3500;
C–H 2850–3300; C≡N 2220–2260; C=O 1680–1750; C=C 1620–1680; C–O 1000–1300; C–C 750–1100.

## Questions

**1.** State a reagent you could use to show that cyclohexene is unsaturated, and give the observation. **[2]**

**2.** Describe a test using Fehling's solution to distinguish propanal from propanone. Give the result for
each compound. **[3]**

**3.** Four unlabelled bottles contain cyclohexene, ethanoic acid, ethanal and propanone. Describe a sequence
of three test-tube tests that identifies every bottle. For each test, give the reagent and the observation
that identifies one compound. **[6]**

**4.** Separate samples of butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol and 2-methylpropan-2-ol are warmed with acidified potassium
dichromate(VI). State what you would observe in each tube and explain the results. **[4]**

**5.** A high-resolution mass spectrum of a compound shows the molecular ion at m/z = 72.0573. The compound is
C₅H₁₂, C₄H₈O or C₃H₄O₂. Show by calculation which formula is correct. **[3]**

**6.** Compound P gives a molecular ion at m/z = 88.0522. Its IR spectrum has a very broad absorption from
2500 to 3000 cm⁻¹ and a strong absorption at 1710 cm⁻¹.

**(a)** P is C₄H₈O₂, C₅H₁₂O or C₃H₈N₂O. Show by calculation which is its molecular formula. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the functional group in P, using the IR evidence, and suggest a structure for P. **[3]**

**7.** This question is about propan-1-ol and propanoic acid.

**(a)** Describe how the infrared spectra of the two compounds would differ. Refer to bonds and wavenumber
ranges. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how the fingerprint region could confirm that a sample is pure propanoic acid. **[2]**

**8.** A student makes cyclohexene by the acid-catalysed elimination of water from cyclohexanol. After
purification, the IR spectrum of the product shows an absorption at 1655 cm⁻¹ and a broad absorption at
3380 cm⁻¹. Explain what the spectrum shows about the product. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how the absorption of infrared radiation by carbon dioxide, methane and water vapour is linked to
global warming. **[4]**

**10.** A volatile organic liquid Y gives a molecular ion at m/z = 44.0261.

**(a)** Y is C₃H₈, C₂H₄O or CH₄N₂. Show by calculation which is its molecular formula. **[2]**
**(b)** The IR spectrum of Y has a strong absorption at 1730 cm⁻¹ and no absorption between 3230 and
3550 cm⁻¹. Identify the bond responsible and name Y. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe a test-tube reaction that would confirm the functional group in Y, and write an equation for
the oxidation of Y, using [O] for the oxidising agent. **[3]**
**(d)** Y is heated under reflux with excess acidified potassium dichromate(VI). State one absorption that
appears in the IR spectrum of the organic product but not in that of Y. **[1]**

**11.** Compound Z gives a molecular ion at m/z = 74.0366. These results are recorded:

- IR spectrum: a broad absorption at 3400 cm⁻¹, a strong absorption at 1720 cm⁻¹, and no very broad
  absorption across 2500–3000 cm⁻¹.
- Warming with Tollens' reagent: a silver mirror forms.
- Warming with acidified potassium dichromate(VI): orange to green.
- The O–H group and the C=O group are on the carbon atoms at opposite ends of the chain.

**(a)** Z is C₄H₁₀O, C₃H₆O₂ or C₂H₆N₂O. Show by calculation which is its molecular formula. **[2]**
**(b)** Use all the evidence to deduce the structure of Z. Explain each step. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** Bromine water [1]. It changes from orange to **colourless** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Goes clear" or "decolourised" with no starting colour is weaker than "orange to colourless"; give both colours.

**2.** Warm each compound with Fehling's solution in a water bath [1]. Propanal: the blue solution gives a
**brick-red precipitate** [1]. Propanone: **no change**, the solution stays blue [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* For a "distinguish" question, give the result for both compounds; "no reaction" is a statement about chemistry, while "stays blue" is the observation asked for.

**3.** One correct sequence:
Test 1: add sodium carbonate (or hydrogencarbonate) solution [1]; effervescence, with a gas that turns
limewater cloudy, identifies **ethanoic acid** [1].
Test 2: warm the other three with Tollens' reagent [1]; a silver mirror identifies **ethanal** [1].
Test 3: add bromine water to the last two [1]; orange to colourless identifies **cyclohexene**, and the bottle
that stays orange is **propanone** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Other valid orders can work, but each test must pick out one compound from those left; a test that two remaining compounds both pass identifies neither.

**4.** Butan-1-ol and butan-2-ol: orange to green [1]. 2-methylpropan-2-ol: stays orange [1]. Butan-1-ol is a
primary and butan-2-ol a secondary alcohol, so both are oxidised, and orange Cr₂O₇²⁻ is reduced to green
Cr³⁺ [1]. 2-methylpropan-2-ol is a tertiary alcohol, which is not easily oxidised [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Classify each alcohol; "it is tertiary" with no link to "not easily oxidised" does not explain the observation.

**5.** C₄H₈O: 4(12.0000) + 8(1.0078) + 15.9949 = 72.0573 [1].
C₅H₁₂: 5(12.0000) + 12(1.0078) = 72.0936; C₃H₄O₂: 3(12.0000) + 4(1.0078) + 2(15.9949) = 72.0210 [1].
Only C₄H₈O matches, so the formula is **C₄H₈O** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A "show by calculation" question needs the sum for every candidate; stating the right formula alone gives no evidence.

**6. (a)** C₄H₈O₂: 4(12.0000) + 8(1.0078) + 2(15.9949) = 88.0522 [1]. C₅H₁₂O = 88.0885 and C₃H₈N₂O = 88.0635,
so P is **C₄H₈O₂** [1].
**(b)** The very broad absorption at 2500–3000 cm⁻¹ is O–H in a carboxylic acid [1]. The absorption at
1710 cm⁻¹ is C=O (1680–1750) [1]. P is a carboxylic acid, for example **butanoic acid, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH**
(2-methylpropanoic acid is also correct) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Match each absorption to a named bond and its data range; "1710 shows an acid" skips the bond and is not a full interpretation.

**7. (a)** Propanoic acid shows a C=O absorption at 1680–1750 cm⁻¹, which propan-1-ol does not [1]. Propanoic
acid shows a very broad O–H (acid) absorption at 2500–3000 cm⁻¹ [1]. Propan-1-ol shows a broad O–H (alcohol)
absorption at 3230–3550 cm⁻¹ instead [1].
**(b)** The fingerprint region (below about 1500 cm⁻¹) has a pattern unique to each compound [1]. If the
sample's spectrum matches that of a known pure sample of propanoic acid exactly, the identity is confirmed and
no impurity peaks are present [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Both have O–H" is true but scores nothing here; the difference is the range and shape, so quote both ranges.

**8.** The absorption at 1655 cm⁻¹ is C=C (1620–1680), so cyclohexene has formed [1]. The broad absorption at
3380 cm⁻¹ is O–H (alcohol) (3230–3550) [1]. Cyclohexene has no O–H bond, so the product is not pure [1]. The
impurity is likely to be unreacted cyclohexanol (or water left after drying) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say why the O–H absorption is unexpected (cyclohexene has no O–H); naming an impurity without that reasoning leaves the explanation incomplete.

**9.** The Earth's surface emits infrared radiation [1]. Bonds in these gases (C=O in CO₂, C–H in methane, O–H
in water) absorb infrared radiation [1]. The molecules re-emit the energy in all directions, some back
towards the Earth, so energy is kept in the lower atmosphere [1]. Higher concentrations of the gases
increase the infrared absorbed, which contributes to global warming [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the effect to bonds absorbing infrared; "the gases trap heat" without absorption of infrared by bonds is too vague for this specification point.

**10. (a)** C₂H₄O: 2(12.0000) + 4(1.0078) + 15.9949 = 44.0261 [1]. C₃H₈ = 44.0624 and CH₄N₂ = 44.0374, so Y is
**C₂H₄O** [1].
**(b)** 1730 cm⁻¹ is C=O [1]. Y is **ethanal**, CH₃CHO [1].
**(c)** Warm Y with Tollens' reagent [1]; a silver mirror forms [1] (or Fehling's solution: blue to brick-red
precipitate). CH₃CHO + [O] → CH₃COOH [1].
**(d)** A very broad O–H (acid) absorption at 2500–3000 cm⁻¹ [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In (d), "an O–H absorption" without the acid range does not distinguish the product from an alcohol; give the range.

**11. (a)** C₃H₆O₂: 3(12.0000) + 6(1.0078) + 2(15.9949) = 74.0366 [1]. C₄H₁₀O = 74.0729 and C₂H₆N₂O = 74.0479,
so Z is **C₃H₆O₂** [1].
**(b)** The broad absorption at 3400 cm⁻¹ is O–H in an alcohol (3230–3550) [1]. The strong absorption at
1720 cm⁻¹ is C=O (1680–1750) [1]. There is no very broad absorption at 2500–3000 cm⁻¹, so Z is not a
carboxylic acid [1]. The silver mirror with Tollens' reagent shows the C=O is in an aldehyde, –CHO, which is
on an end carbon [1]. The dichromate result does not show the alcohol type, because the
aldehyde is itself oxidised [1]. The O–H is on the carbon at the other end of the chain, so it is a
primary alcohol [1]. Z is **3-hydroxypropanal, HOCH₂CH₂CHO** [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In a multi-evidence structure question, tie every piece of evidence to a conclusion; a correct structure with no reasoning answers only the last step.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "clear" for colourless, or giving only the final colour of a test.
- Swapping the Tollens' and Fehling's observations.
- Using acidified dichromate(VI) while an aldehyde could still be present, so two compounds both turn green.
- Picking a molecular formula from precise masses without showing the sum for every candidate.
- Assigning a 2500–3000 cm⁻¹ absorption to an alcohol, or 3230–3550 cm⁻¹ to an acid.
- Describing the fingerprint region without saying the spectrum is compared with a known sample.
