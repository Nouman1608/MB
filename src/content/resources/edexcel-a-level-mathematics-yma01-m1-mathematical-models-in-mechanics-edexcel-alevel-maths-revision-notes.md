These notes cover section 1 of Unit M1 in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics (YMA01): "The basic ideas of mathematical modelling as applied in Mechanics", including the specified terms and the assumptions behind them. For worked examples, use the [M1 mathematical models study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m1-mathematical-models-in-mechanics-edexcel-alevel-maths/).

## The modelling cycle

Simplify (choose assumptions) → set up equations → solve → interpret and check against reality → refine the assumptions and repeat.

## The specified terms and what they do

The left column is the word. The right column is what to write when asked how you used it.

| Term | Meaning | Effect on your working |
|---|---|---|
| Particle | Mass at a single point; size ignored | All forces act at one point; no rotation, no moments |
| Light rod | Rod of negligible mass | No weight term for the rod |
| Uniform rod | Mass spread evenly along its length | Weight acts at the midpoint |
| Non-uniform rod | Mass not evenly spread | Weight acts at the centre of mass, not the midpoint |
| Lamina | Flat; thickness negligible | Treated as a two-dimensional shape |
| Rigid body | Does not change shape | Distances within it stay fixed; it does not bend |
| Inextensible string | Does not stretch | Joined particles have equal speeds and equal sizes of acceleration |
| Light string | Negligible mass | Same tension throughout the string |
| Smooth surface | No friction | Only the normal reaction acts |
| Rough surface | Friction can act | Friction F ≤ μR along the surface |
| Light smooth pulley | No mass, no friction | Tension is the same on both sides |
| Bead | Particle threaded on a wire or string | Free to slide along it |
| Wire | Rigid, thin, one-dimensional | The bead's path is fixed; if smooth, the force on the bead is perpendicular to the wire |
| Peg | Fixed support a string passes over or a rod rests on | If smooth, the force is perpendicular to the string or rod, and the tension is unchanged |

## Other standard assumptions

| Assumption | What it ignores | Effect on a typical answer |
|---|---|---|
| No air resistance | Drag opposing motion | Predicted heights, ranges and speeds are too large |
| g = 9.8 m s⁻² constant | Variation of g with height and place | Negligible over everyday distances |
| Constant resistance | Resistance growing with speed | Model is less accurate at high speeds |
| Constant μ | Changes in the surface | Friction may differ from place to place |

## Light versus inextensible

These two are confused more than any others.

- **Light** is about **mass**: no mass, so the **tension** is the same all along.
- **Inextensible** is about **length**: no stretch, so the **acceleration** is the same for both ends.

A question can give one without the other. An elastic string, for example, can be light but stretches, so the joined particles need not move together.

## Good and poor answers

| Question | Poor answer | Good answer |
|---|---|---|
| How did you use "inextensible"? | "The string doesn't stretch" | "A and B have the same magnitude of acceleration" |
| How did you use "uniform"? | "The rod is even" | "The weight of the rod acts at its midpoint" |
| What does "particle" mean here? | "It has no mass" | "Its dimensions are negligible, so its mass acts at a point" |
| Why model the pulley as smooth? | "So it's easier" | "No friction at the pulley, so the tension is the same on both sides" |
| Suggest a refinement | "Make it more realistic" | "Include air resistance acting against the motion" |

## Key words in questions

| Command word | What to write |
|---|---|
| State | A short phrase, with no reasons |
| Explain how you have used | The effect on your equations |
| Suggest | One specific change or reason |
| Comment on | A judgement linked to an assumption, with the direction of the effect |

## Exam traps

- Saying a particle has no mass.
- Swapping the effects of light and inextensible.
- Restating the word instead of giving its effect.
- Calling a rod both light and uniform.
- Naming an assumption that the question has already told you to make.
- Giving a refinement that is vague, such as "consider other forces".
- Rounding answers that use g = 9.8 to more than 3 significant figures without being asked.

## Self-test

1. What does it mean to model a car as a particle?
2. A rod is uniform. Where does its weight act?
3. A string is light. What does that tell you about the tension?
4. A string is inextensible. What does that tell you about two particles joined by it?
5. State what is meant by a smooth surface.
6. A box rests on a rough slope. What extra force must your model include?
7. Why is a pulley modelled as light and smooth?
8. Give one assumption made when a ball is modelled as moving freely under gravity.
9. Under that model a ball reaches 10 m. Would a real ball go higher or lower? Why?
10. What is a lamina?
11. A rod is non-uniform. What changes in a moments calculation?
12. Suggest one refinement to a model of a car accelerating along a road under a constant resistance.

**Answers:**

1. Its dimensions are ignored, so its mass is concentrated at a single point and it cannot rotate.
2. At its midpoint.
3. The tension is the same throughout the string.
4. They have the same speed and the same magnitude of acceleration.
5. There is no friction between the surface and an object in contact with it.
6. Friction, acting along the slope, with F ≤ μR.
7. So that the tension is the same on both sides of the pulley.
8. Any one of: the ball is a particle; air resistance is ignored; g is constant.
9. Lower, because air resistance would act against the motion and increase the deceleration.
10. A flat object whose thickness is negligible, so it is treated as two-dimensional.
11. The weight cannot be placed at the midpoint; it acts at the centre of mass, whose position may be unknown.
12. Let the resistance increase with the car's speed, rather than keeping it constant.
