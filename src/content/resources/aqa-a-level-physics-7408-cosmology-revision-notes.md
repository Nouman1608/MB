## Equations

| Equation | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Δf/f = v/c | Doppler shift in frequency | v ≪ c; v positive for approach |
| z = Δλ/λ = −v/c | red shift | receding: z positive |
| v = Hd | Hubble's law | v in km s⁻¹, d in Mpc → H in km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ |
| age ≈ 1/H | age of Universe | H in s⁻¹; assumes H constant |
| r = vT/2π | binary star orbital radius | circular orbit, plane of orbit |
| I = P/4πd² | quasar power from intensity | d in metres |
| ΔI/I ≈ (r_p/r_s)² | transit dip | fraction, not percentage |

**Converting H**: H (s⁻¹) = H (km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹) × 10³/3.08 × 10²².

## 3.9.3.1 Doppler effect: recall

- Applies to optical and radio frequencies; galaxies and quasars.
- Red shift: wavelength increases, source receding.
- Blue shift: wavelength decreases, source approaching.
- **Binary stars** (edge-on): lines oscillate with the orbital period; with two bright stars the lines split and merge.
- Max shift → orbital speed; cycle time → period; r = vT/2π.

## 3.9.3.2 Hubble's law and the Big Bang: recall

- v ∝ d for distant galaxies: the Universe is **expanding**.
- H is the gradient of a v–d graph.
- Age ≈ 1/H if the expansion rate has been constant; H ≈ 65–75 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ gives about 1.3–1.5 × 10¹⁰ years.
- **Big Bang**: Universe began hot and dense, then expanded and cooled.
- **Evidence 1 – cosmological microwave background radiation**: black-body radiation at about 2.7 K, nearly uniform from all directions; radiation from the early Universe stretched by expansion.
- **Evidence 2 – hydrogen and helium**: about 3 : 1 by mass, matching fusion predicted in the hot early Universe.

## 3.9.3.3 Quasars: recall

- Most distant measurable objects.
- Discovered as **bright radio sources**.
- **Large optical red shifts** → large d by Hubble's law.
- Still detectable at that distance → enormous power (I = P/4πd²).
- Formed from **active supermassive black holes**.

## 3.9.3.4 Exoplanets: recall

- **Direct detection hard**: planet very faint beside its star; tiny angular separation.
- **Radial velocity method**: star orbits common centre of mass → periodic Doppler shift of its lines with the planet's period.
- **Transit method**: planet passes in front of star → small periodic dip in intensity.
- **Typical light curve**: flat → falls → flat-bottomed dip → rises → flat; repeats every orbit.
- Deeper dip → larger planet; dip interval → orbital period. Orbit must be nearly edge-on.

## Methods in steps

**Distance to a galaxy from its spectrum**
1. z = Δλ/λ (use the rest wavelength on the bottom).
2. v = zc, then convert to km s⁻¹.
3. d = v/H gives d in Mpc; multiply by 3.08 × 10²² for metres.

**Power of a quasar**
1. Find d in metres as above.
2. P = 4πd²I.
3. Compare with the Sun's 3.9 × 10²⁶ W to show how extreme it is.

**Binary star orbit**
1. v = cΔλ_max/λ.
2. Read the period from one full cycle of the shift; convert to seconds.
3. r = vT/2π.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Red shift, z | Fractional increase in wavelength due to recession |
| Hubble constant | Recession speed per unit distance |
| Cosmological microwave background | Near-uniform 2.7 K black-body radiation from all directions |
| Quasar | Extremely distant, extremely powerful object, discovered as a radio source |
| Exoplanet | Planet orbiting a star other than the Sun |
| Radial velocity method | Detecting a planet from periodic Doppler shifts of its star |
| Transit method | Detecting a planet from periodic dips in its star's intensity |

## Exam traps

- State the direction separately from the size of v.
- H in km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ needs v in km s⁻¹ for d in Mpc.
- Convert H to s⁻¹ before taking 1/H, then seconds to years.
- 1.0% dip is 0.010 before the square root.
- For quasars, distance in metres before I = P/4πd².
- The Doppler equations need v ≪ c; very large z requires caution.

## Self-test

**1.** A source approaches. Is its light red- or blue-shifted?
**2.** A 500.0 nm line is observed at 505.0 nm. Find z and v.
**3.** What does the period of a binary star's line shifts tell you?
**4.** State Hubble's law.
**5.** v = 7000 km s⁻¹ and H = 70 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹. Find d.
**6.** What assumption is made when estimating the age of the Universe as 1/H?
**7.** Give two pieces of evidence for the Big Bang.
**8.** How were quasars first discovered?
**9.** Why are quasars thought to be extremely powerful?
**10.** Give two reasons exoplanets are hard to detect directly.
**11.** A transit dip is 0.25%. What is r_planet/r_star?
**12.** What causes the Doppler shift in the radial velocity method?

## Self-test answers

**1.** Blue-shifted.
**2.** z = 5.0/500.0 = 0.010; v = zc = 3.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹, receding.
**3.** The orbital period of the stars.
**4.** Recession speed of a galaxy is proportional to its distance, v = Hd.
**5.** d = 7000/70 = 100 Mpc.
**6.** That H has been constant since the Big Bang.
**7.** Cosmological microwave background radiation; relative abundance of hydrogen and helium.
**8.** As bright radio sources.
**9.** Their large red shifts show they are very distant, yet they are still detectable, so their power output must be huge.
**10.** The planet is very faint compared with its star; the angular separation is very small.
**11.** √0.0025 = 0.050.
**12.** The star moves towards and away from us as it orbits the common centre of mass with the planet.
