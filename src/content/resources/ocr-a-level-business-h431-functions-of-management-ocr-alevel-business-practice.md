## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by management. **[2]**

**2.** Explain the function of controlling in management. **[2]**

**3.** Linford Foods planned costs of £350,000 and its actual costs were £371,000. Calculate the variance in pounds and as a percentage of the planned cost, and state whether it is adverse or favourable. **[3]**

**4.** Explain **one** internal constraint on the managers of a small business. **[2]**

**5.** Which **one** of the following is an external constraint on management?

A: A shortage of skilled staff in the business.

B: A new law on workplace safety.

C: The business's culture.

D: A limited internal budget.

**[1]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**6.** Marden Care Homes set the following targets for last year and recorded these results. Revenue last year was £4,000,000.

| Objective | Target | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue growth | 6% | 3% |
| Profit margin | 10.0% | 9.0% |
| Labour turnover (maximum) | 20% | 17% |
| Resident satisfaction | 90% | 93% |

**(a)** Calculate the revenue target and the actual revenue for this year. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the profit target and the actual profit, and the shortfall. **[4]**

**(c)** Evaluate the success of the management of Marden Care Homes. **[9]**

**7.** Marden Care Homes wants to open a new home but has limited funds, a shortage of qualified nurses in the area and strict rules from the care regulator.

Evaluate the constraints on the managers of Marden Care Homes in carrying out this plan. **[8]**

**8.** Explain **two** functions of management using examples from a care home. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Management is the process of getting things done through people and other resources [1] in order to achieve the objectives of the business [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Controlling means monitoring actual results against the plan [1] and taking corrective action where they differ, such as investigating a cost overrun [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Variance = £371,000 − £350,000 = **£21,000** [1]. Percentage = 21,000 ÷ 350,000 × 100 = **6%** [1]. It is **adverse**, because costs were higher than planned [1]. **[3]**

**4.** A small business may have limited finance [1], so managers cannot invest in new equipment or hire extra staff, even where it would help the business grow [1]. Accept skills of staff, or a culture resistant to change, with a consequence. **[2]**

**5.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** Revenue target = £4,000,000 × 1.06 = **£4,240,000** [1]. Actual = £4,000,000 × 1.03 = **£4,120,000** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Profit target = 10.0% × £4,240,000 = **£424,000** [1]. Actual profit = 9.0% × £4,120,000 = **£370,800** [1] [1]. Shortfall = £424,000 − £370,800 = **£53,200** [1]. **[4]**

**(c)** Two of the four objectives were met: labour turnover (17% against 20%) and resident satisfaction (93% against 90%) [1], which shows success in managing people and quality of care [1]. Revenue was £120,000 below target and profit £53,200 below [1], so financial objectives were not met [1].
The causes matter. If the revenue shortfall was due to fewer residents because of local competition or a fall in funding, managers could not control it [1]. But a lower margin (9.0% against 10.0%) suggests costs were higher than planned, which is a failure of control [1].
Were targets realistic? A 6% rise in revenue may have been too ambitious given conditions, and 3% may be good compared with other homes [1]. Success in care is also measured by resident safety and regulator reports, which are not in the table.
The long term: high satisfaction and lower turnover may bring higher occupancy and lower recruitment costs in the future [1].
Judgement: management has been partly successful, strong on people and care but weaker on finance; a fair verdict depends on whether the shortfall was caused by external conditions or poor cost control, so the managers should analyse the margin before judging [1]. **[9]**

**7.** Finance: limited funds restrict how much can be spent on land, buildings and equipment for a new home [1], and managers may need to borrow, which increases risk and cost [1]. Staff: a shortage of qualified nurses means the home may not be able to staff the new home safely, and may need to raise pay or use agency staff at higher cost [1]. Regulation: strict rules on staffing levels, safety and standards must be met before the home can open [1], which adds cost and delay but cannot be avoided.
Overcoming: finance can be eased by a phased opening, a loan or a partner [1]; nurse shortage by training own staff or offering better terms, though both take time [1]. The regulator's rules are fixed, so they must be built into the plan [1].
Judgement: the shortage of nurses is likely to be the most serious constraint, because without staff the home cannot open whatever the funds, so the managers should address recruitment and training first [1]. **[8]**

**8.** Any two functions, 2 marks each. Planning: the manager forecasts the number of residents and sets staffing and budget for next year [1], so the home has enough staff and money for expected demand [1]. Controlling: the manager compares actual spending on food and agency staff with the budget each month [1] and takes action, such as changing supplier or rotas, where costs are above plan [1]. Accept organising (setting rotas and responsibilities), commanding or directing (briefing and motivating care staff) or coordinating (linking kitchen, nursing and cleaning), with an example and an effect. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming functions of management without examples.
- Judging success by profit alone.
- Blaming managers for external causes, or the reverse.
- Listing constraints without saying which is most serious.
- Forgetting to say whether a variance is adverse or favourable.
