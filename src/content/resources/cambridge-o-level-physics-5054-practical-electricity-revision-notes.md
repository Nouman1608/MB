Condensed notes for subtopic 4.4 Practical electricity in Cambridge O Level

## Equations

```
P = I V                         W = A × V
E = I V t                       J = A × V × s
energy (kW h) = P (kW) × t (h)
1 kW h = 3.6 × 10⁶ J
cost = number of kW h × price per kW h
```

Unit pairs: **W with s gives J**; **kW with h gives kW h**.

## Uses of electricity

Heating · lighting · battery charging · powering motors · powering
electronic systems.

## Lamps in parallel — advantages

1. Each lamp has the full supply p.d. → normal brightness.
2. Each can be switched independently.
3. One failing does not switch off the others.

## Hazards

| Hazard | Danger |
|---|---|
| Damaged insulation | Shock from exposed live wire; short circuit and fire |
| Overheating cables | Insulation melts; fire |
| Damp conditions | Water conducts; shock |
| Overloaded plugs, extension leads, sockets | Excess current overheats cables; fire |

## Fuses and trip switches

| Feature | Fuse | Trip switch (circuit breaker) |
|---|---|---|
| How it works | Thin wire heats and melts when current exceeds rating | Switch opens automatically when current exceeds setting |
| After a fault | Must be replaced | Can be reset |
| Speed | Slower | Faster |
| Placed in | Live wire | Live wire |

**Choosing a rating:** I = P ÷ V, then the smallest available rating
**above** that current.

Example: 1150 W on 230 V → 5.0 A → a 5 A fuse would be at its limit, so
choose the next rating up (for example 13 A if only 3 A, 5 A and 13 A are
available). A fuse should not run at exactly its rated current.

## The three wires

| Wire | Role |
|---|---|
| Live (line) | Carries the high alternating p.d. |
| Neutral | Completes the circuit; close to 0 V |
| Earth | Safety wire to the metal case; carries current only in a fault |

## Earthing — the chain of events

Live wire touches earthed metal case → very low resistance path to earth →
very large current → fuse melts or breaker trips → appliance disconnected
from live → case not live, no shock.

**Double insulation:** non-conducting outer case cannot become live, so no
earth wire is needed.

## Switch and fuse in the live wire

- Switch off → appliance disconnected from the high p.d.
- Switch in the neutral → appliance still connected to live when "off":
  shock risk during repair.
- Fuses and breakers in live so that, when they act, the appliance is
  isolated from live.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Kilowatt-hour | Energy transferred by a 1 kW appliance in 1 hour (3.6 × 10⁶ J) |
| Power | Energy transferred per second; P = I V |
| Fuse | A thin wire in the live wire that melts if the current exceeds its rating |
| Trip switch | An automatic switch that opens when the current exceeds its setting; resettable |
| Earth wire | Low-resistance safety connection from a metal case to the ground |
| Double insulation | A non-conducting casing so that the outside cannot become live |
| Overloading | Drawing too much current through one socket, plug or extension lead |

## Exam traps

- Minutes must become hours for kW h, and seconds for joules.
- Do not pick a fuse with a rating equal to or below the working current.
- Do not pick a much larger fuse "for safety" — it would not blow in a
  fault.
- The earth wire does not carry current in normal use.
- Explaining earthing without saying the **fuse melts or breaker trips**
  loses the key mark.
- Switch in the live wire: the reason is that the appliance is
  disconnected from the high p.d., not "so it works".
- In a parallel lighting circuit, every lamp gets the full p.d.

## Self-test

1. State three common uses of electricity.
2. Give two advantages of connecting house lamps in parallel.
3. Find the power of a device taking 0.26 A from 230 V.
4. Find the energy transferred by a 12 V, 2.0 A motor in 5.0 minutes.
5. Define the kilowatt-hour.
6. A 60 W lamp is left on for a week (168 h). Find the energy in kW h.
7. At an invented price of $0.15 per kW h, find the cost in question 6.
8. Calculate the current in a 2.5 kW heater on 230 V and choose a fuse from
   3 A, 5 A and 13 A.
9. State one hazard of damp conditions when using mains appliances.
10. Explain why a metal-cased appliance is earthed.
11. Why does a double-insulated appliance not need an earth wire?
12. Why must the switch be in the live wire?

**Answers**

1. Any three: heating, lighting, battery charging, powering motors,
   powering electronic systems.
2. Each gets the full p.d. (full brightness); each switched independently;
   one failing leaves the others on (any two).
3. P = 0.26 × 230 = **60 W** (59.8 W).
4. E = 2.0 × 12 × 300 = **7200 J**.
5. The energy transferred by a 1 kW appliance in 1 hour.
6. 0.060 × 168 = **10 kW h** (10.08).
7. 10.08 × 0.15 = **$1.51**.
8. I = 2500 ÷ 230 = 10.9 A, so **13 A** (the smallest rating above the
   working current).
9. Water conducts, so current can flow through a person: electric shock.
10. If the live wire touches the case, a large current flows to earth,
    melting the fuse or tripping the breaker so the case cannot stay live.
11. Its casing is non-conducting, so it cannot become live.
12. So that when switched off the appliance is disconnected from the high
    p.d. of the live wire.
