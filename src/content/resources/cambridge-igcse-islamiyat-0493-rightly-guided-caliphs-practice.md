**How to use this set.** In Paper 2, one or two of Questions 3–5 are set on this topic, each worth
14 marks: a knowledge part (a) worth 10 and an understanding part (b) worth 4. The syllabus covers the
caliphs' rule after 632; their lives in the Prophet's (pbuh) time belong to Paper 1. Section A checks
facts; Section B gives full exam-style questions.

## Section A — Short questions

**1.** Give the years of rule of each of the four caliphs. **[4]**

**2.** Name the place where Abu Bakr was chosen as caliph, and state **one** point from his first
address. **[2]**

**3.** Name the commanders who led the Muslims at Qadisiyya and at Yarmuk. **[2]**

**4.** Explain what the diwan was and why Umar set it up. **[3]**

**5.** Name the **six** members of the council Umar appointed to choose his successor. **[3]**

**6.** State **two** achievements of Uthman at sea. **[2]**

**7.** What was the arbitration after Siffin, and why did the Kharijites reject it? **[3]**

## Section B — Exam-style questions

**8. (a)** Describe the expansion of the Muslim state under Umar. **[10]**
**(b)** What can states today learn from Umar's treatment of the Christians of Jerusalem? **[4]**

**9. (a)** Describe the main changes Umar made to the way the state was governed. **[10]**
**(b)** In your opinion, which of these changes best shows his concern for justice? Give reasons. **[4]**

**10. (a)** Write about the achievements and difficulties of Uthman's caliphate. **[10]**
**(b)** What can Muslim leaders today learn from the way Uthman responded to the rebels? **[4]**

**11. (a)** Describe the main difficulties Ali faced during his caliphate. **[10]**
**(b)** How can Ali's treatment of his opponents guide Muslims who are settling disputes today? **[4]**

**12. (a)** Describe how each of the four Rightly Guided Caliphs came to power. **[10]**
**(b)** Explain what these methods show about the importance of consultation (shura) in Muslim
government. **[4]**

## Answers

**How Section B is marked.** Levels of response: part (a) Level 4 = 8–10, Level 3 = 5–7, Level 2 =
3–4, Level 1 = 1–2; part (b) Level 2 = 3–4, Level 1 = 1–2. Each developed point below earns [2];
credit the best five in (a) (maximum 10) and the best two in (b) (maximum 4); in a "your opinion" part
(b), a clear judgement supported by developed reasons reaches the top level. Other accurate points of
the same quality earn the same credit.

**1.** Abu Bakr 632–634 [1]; Umar 634–644 [1]; Uthman 644–656 [1]; Ali 656–661 [1].

**2.** Saqifa Bani Sa'ida in Madina [1]. Any one point [1]: he was not the best of them; obey him only
while he obeys God and His Messenger; correct him if he does wrong; the weak are strong in his eyes
until he secures their rights; truthfulness is a trust.

**3.** Qadisiyya: Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas [1]. Yarmuk: Khalid ibn al-Walid (with Abu Ubayda in overall
command in Syria) [1].

**4.** The diwan was a register of soldiers and other Muslims entitled to payments [1]. With wealth
coming from the conquests, Umar needed a fair, organised system [1] to pay fixed stipends from the
treasury according to a person's early service to Islam and needs [1].

**5.** Uthman, Ali, Talha, al-Zubayr, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf. Six correct [3],
four or five [2], two or three [1].

**6.** Any two, [1] each: the first Muslim navy was built under Mu'awiya, governor of Syria; Cyprus was
taken; the Byzantine fleet was defeated at the Battle of the Masts. Maximum 2.

**7.** After fighting at Siffin, Ali and Mu'awiya agreed that two arbitrators, Abu Musa al-Ash'ari for
Ali and Amr ibn al-As for Mu'awiya, would decide the dispute [2]. The Kharijites said judgement
belongs to God alone, so a human arbitration was wrong, and they left Ali's army [1].

**8. (a)** Credit the best five, [2] each:
- In Iraq, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas defeated the Persian army at Qadisiyya, and the Persian capital
  Ctesiphon (al-Mada'in) was taken [2].
- The victory at Nahavand broke Persian resistance and opened Iran to the Muslims [2].
- In Syria, Damascus was taken, and the Byzantine army was defeated at Yarmuk [2].
- Jerusalem surrendered; Umar travelled there himself, simply dressed, to receive the city [2].
- He gave its Christians a guarantee of safety for their lives, property and churches [2].
- Amr ibn al-As conquered Egypt, including Alexandria [2].
- New garrison cities, Kufa, Basra and Fustat, were built to house the armies and govern the regions [2].
**(b)** Credit the best two, [2] each:
- He protected the churches and their worship, so states should guarantee freedom of religion to
  minorities [2].
- He is reported to have prayed outside the main church so that Muslims would not later claim it,
  so states should protect minorities' places of worship from seizure [2].
- His written guarantee shows that rights should be set down in law, not left to goodwill [2].

**9. (a)** Credit the best five, [2] each:
- He divided the state into provinces, each with a governor (wali), and checked governors' wealth
  when they were appointed and dismissed [2].
- He appointed judges (qadis) separate from governors, so justice did not depend on political power [2].
- He set up the bayt al-mal (treasury) and the diwan register to pay stipends fairly [2].
- He introduced the Islamic calendar, dated from the Hijra [2].
- He founded garrison towns and had canals dug to support farming and supply [2].
- He walked the streets at night to learn of people's needs and gave allowances to the poor, the
  elderly and children [2].
- He arranged for tarawih prayers to be led in congregation in Ramadan [2].
**(b)** A clear judgement [1] supported by reasons [3] (maximum 4). For example: the separate judges [1],
because a governor could no longer decide cases in his own interest [1], because ordinary people could
bring complaints even against officials [1], and because it set a principle that rulers are under
the law [1].

**10. (a)** Credit the best five, [2] each:
- He was chosen by the council of six in 644 [2].
- He had standard copies of the Qur'an made from Hafsa's sheets and sent to the provinces [2].
- The first Muslim navy was built; Cyprus was taken and a Byzantine fleet defeated [2].
- The state expanded in North Africa, Armenia and Khurasan [2].
- He enlarged the Prophet's mosque in Madina [2].
- Complaints grew that governors from his own clan, such as Mu'awiya in Syria, were favoured [2].
- Rebels from Egypt, Kufa and Basra came to Madina, besieged his house, and killed him in 656 while
  he was reading the Qur'an [2].
**(b)** Credit the best two, [2] each:
- He refused to let the Companions fight the rebels for him, to avoid Muslims killing Muslims, so
  leaders should put peace in the community above their own safety [2].
- He listened to complaints and tried to answer them, so leaders should give people a hearing
  before conflict grows [2].
- The crisis shows leaders must be seen to appoint officials fairly, or trust is lost [2].

**11. (a)** Credit the best five, [2] each:
- He took office in 656 in a crisis, with demands for the punishment of Uthman's killers [2].
- Talha, al-Zubayr and A'isha went to Basra; at the Battle of the Camel (656) they were defeated, and
  Ali sent A'isha back to Madina with respect [2].
- He moved the capital from Madina to Kufa [2].
- Mu'awiya, governor of Syria, refused to accept him; at Siffin (657) the Syrians raised copies of
  the Qur'an and Ali accepted arbitration [2].
- The Kharijites rejected the arbitration and turned against him; he defeated them at Nahrawan [2].
- He lost control of Egypt and other regions to Mu'awiya [2].
- In 661 the Kharijite Ibn Muljam attacked him in the mosque of Kufa, and he died of his wounds [2].
**(b)** Credit the best two, [2] each:
- He forbade pursuing those who fled or looting their goods, so disputes should be ended without
  revenge [2].
- He accepted arbitration to stop Muslims killing each other, so Muslims should prefer mediation to
  fighting [2].
- He treated A'isha with honour after defeating her side, so opponents should be treated with
  dignity [2].

**12. (a)** Credit the best five, [2] each:
- After the Prophet's (pbuh) death the Helpers met at Saqifa Bani Sa'ida; Abu Bakr, Umar and Abu Ubayda
  joined them [2].
- Umar gave Abu Bakr his pledge, the others followed, and the community pledged in the mosque [2].
- Abu Bakr, before his death, consulted senior Companions and nominated Umar, who was accepted [2].
- Umar, after being stabbed, appointed a council of six to choose from among themselves [2].
- Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf withdrew his own claim, consulted widely, and the council chose Uthman [2].
- After Uthman's death, the people of Madina urged Ali to take office; he accepted pledges in the
  mosque, though Mu'awiya refused [2].
**(b)** Credit the best two, [2] each:
- In every case leaders were chosen after consultation and public pledge, so government should rest
  on the consent of the people [2].
- No fixed method was used, which shows Muslims may choose suitable ways to consult while keeping
  the principle (Sura 42.38) [2].
- Consultation made leaders answerable, as Abu Bakr said in his first address [2].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing about the caliphs' lives before 632.
- Confusing Qadisiyya (Persians) with Yarmuk (Byzantines).
- Giving achievements but no difficulties when both are asked.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need dates for battles?**
Know the years of each rule and the main battles; exact years for every event are less important
than accurate sequence and detail.
