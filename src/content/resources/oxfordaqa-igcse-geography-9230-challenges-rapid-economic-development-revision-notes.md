These notes cover section 3.2.2.2, Challenges of rapid economic development, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Geography (9230) specification, Version 4.3. Key idea: "Some LICs and NEEs are experiencing rapid economic development which leads to significant social, environmental and cultural change." The specification requires a **case study of an LIC or NEE**; these notes use China as a framework, so replace the facts if you studied a different country. For full explanations, read the [rapid economic development study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-challenges-rapid-economic-development/).

## Specification checklist

For your case-study country, know:

1. **Location and importance** (regional and global).
2. **Political, social, cultural and environmental context.**
3. **Industrial structure** and sector balance; how manufacturing stimulates growth.
4. **TNCs:** advantages and disadvantages.
5. **Political and trading relationships.**
6. **Aid:** types and impacts.
7. **Environmental impacts.**
8. **Quality of life.**

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| NEE | Newly emerging economy |
| TNC | Company operating in more than one country |
| SEZ | Special economic zone with incentives for investment |
| FDI | Foreign direct investment |
| Primary / secondary / tertiary / quaternary | Raw materials / manufacturing / services / knowledge |
| Bilateral / multilateral aid | Government to government / through organisations |
| Tied aid | Must be spent on donor's goods |
| Multiplier effect | Growth in one industry creates jobs in others |

## China at a glance (check against your own notes)

| Theme | Key points |
|---|---|
| Location | East Asia, borders the Pacific; second-largest population (over 1.4 billion) |
| Importance | Second-largest economy by output; largest manufacturer; major trader and investor; UN Security Council member |
| Reforms | "Open door" and market reforms from 1978; special economic zones from 1980 (Shenzhen) |
| Political context | One-party state with strong central planning |
| Social context | Rapid urbanisation; one-child policy (1979 to 2015) leading to an ageing population |
| Cultural context | Ancient culture, many ethnic groups; tension between modern and traditional lifestyles |
| Environmental context | Varied geography; wetter south, drier north; densely populated east coast |

## Industrial structure

- **1978:** most workers (about 70%) in primary industry (farming).
- **Now:** under a quarter in farming; manufacturing grew fast; services now the largest part of output.
- Agriculture: about a third of output in 1980, under a tenth now.
- Quaternary (technology, research) growing.

**How manufacturing stimulated development:**
- millions of jobs and higher incomes;
- exports bring foreign currency for machinery and technology;
- workers move from low-productivity farming to factories;
- multiplier effect: demand for steel, power, transport and services;
- tax revenue pays for infrastructure.

## TNCs

| Advantages to host country | Disadvantages to host country |
|---|---|
| Jobs | Low wages, poor conditions |
| Investment | Profits sent abroad |
| Technology and skills transfer | Factories may relocate when wages rise |
| Exports, foreign currency | Pollution; dependence on foreign firms |
| Tax revenue | Competition for local firms |

Example: Foxconn assembling products for Apple in Shenzhen and Zhengzhou.

## Political and trading relationships

- Closed before 1978; "open door" policy after.
- Joined the **WTO in 2001**; became the largest exporter of goods.
- **Belt and Road Initiative (2013):** funds ports, railways and roads abroad.
- Tensions: trade disputes with the USA, including tariffs from 2018.

## Aid

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bilateral | Government to government |
| Multilateral | Via World Bank, UN, etc. |
| NGO / voluntary | Charities |
| Tied | Money spent on donor's goods |
| Emergency | Short-term relief after a disaster |
| Development | Long-term projects: schools, roads, clinics |

- **Positive impacts:** infrastructure, health, education, jobs, saves lives.
- **Negative impacts:** dependence, conditions, corruption, unsuitable projects.
- China received loans and technical help in the 1980s and after; it is now a major donor.

## Environmental impacts

- **Air pollution:** coal for electricity; smog in cities such as Beijing.
- **Water:** pollution of rivers; shortages in the north.
- **Land:** soil loss, desertification, farmland lost to cities.
- **Climate change:** largest emitter of carbon dioxide.
- **Responses:** large wind, solar and hydropower investment; closing old coal plants; tree planting; stricter laws.

## Quality of life

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| Hundreds of millions out of extreme poverty | Coast-interior and rich-poor inequality |
| Higher life expectancy, better healthcare | Long hours, low pay in some factories |
| More education | Migrant workers lack services; children left behind |
| Better housing and goods | Pollution harms health |
| Better transport | Loss of traditional areas |

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain the advantages and disadvantages of TNCs to a country you have studied. [6 marks]

**Model answer:** "In China, TNCs such as Foxconn, assembling products for Apple, created millions of jobs and brought investment, technology and skills. Their exports earn foreign currency and they pay taxes. However, wages are often low and working conditions can be poor. Much of the profit leaves the country. If wages rise, a company may move its factory to a cheaper country, and factory pollution harms the environment. Overall, TNCs helped China to grow quickly, but the benefits depend on the government setting rules for pay, conditions and pollution."

How it scores: advantages with detail, disadvantages with detail, a named country and TNC, and a concluding judgement.

## Quick recall: five ideas to remember

- Primary is raw materials, secondary is manufacturing, tertiary is services, quaternary is knowledge.
- Manufacturing stimulates growth through jobs, exports and the multiplier effect.
- Always give both sides for TNCs and for aid.
- Environmental costs: air and water pollution, land loss, carbon emissions.
- Quality of life: better for most, but uneven.

## Exam traps

- Name your country and give **facts**, with dates.
- Sectors: primary raw materials, secondary manufacturing, tertiary services.
- TNCs: always give **both** advantages and disadvantages.
- Aid: weigh positives and negatives; distinguish the types.
- Quality of life: give a **balanced** view and a judgement.
- Do not use an HIC as your case study.

## Self-test

1. Name the sector of the economy that includes manufacturing.
2. What is a special economic zone?
3. Give **two** advantages of TNCs for a host country.
4. Give **two** disadvantages of TNCs for a host country.
5. What is tied aid?
6. Explain how manufacturing can stimulate economic development.
7. Give **two** environmental impacts of rapid economic development in your case-study country.
8. Give **one** positive and **one** negative effect on quality of life.
9. Why did China's trade grow after 2001?
10. Why might a country be both an aid recipient and an aid donor over time?

**Answers**

1. The secondary sector.
2. An area with special rules, such as low taxes, designed to attract foreign investment.
3. Any two: jobs; investment; technology transfer; exports; tax revenue.
4. Any two: low wages; profits sent abroad; factories may move away; pollution; competition for local firms.
5. Aid that has to be spent on goods or services from the donor country.
6. It creates jobs and raises incomes, earns export income, moves workers to higher-productivity work, and creates demand in other industries (multiplier effect).
7. For example: air pollution from coal; water pollution; loss of farmland; high carbon emissions.
8. Positive: higher incomes or life expectancy. Negative: pollution or inequality.
9. It joined the World Trade Organization in 2001, which opened more markets to Chinese goods.
10. As its economy grows, it can afford to help others, while earlier it needed outside help for infrastructure.
