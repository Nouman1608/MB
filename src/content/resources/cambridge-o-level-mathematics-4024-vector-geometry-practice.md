These questions cover section 7.4, Vector geometry, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. No diagrams are given, so draw your own sketch for each question and mark the vectors with arrows. The methods are in the [vector geometry study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-vector-geometry/), and the [vector geometry revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-vector-geometry-revision-notes/) list the proof templates. O is the origin throughout.

## Section A

**1.** OA = a and OB = b. Find AB, and the position vector of the midpoint of AB, in terms of a and b. **[2]**

**2.** OA = a and OB = b. P lies on AB with AP : PB = 3 : 2. Find OP in terms of a and b, simplified. **[2]**

**3.** Show that 9a − 6b is parallel to 6a − 4b, and find the ratio of their lengths. **[2]**

**4.** a and b are not parallel. Find p and q if (2p − 1)a + 4b = 7a + (p + q)b. **[2]**

**5.** A is (−3, 4) and B is (9, −2). P lies on AB with AP : PB = 1 : 2. Find the coordinates of P. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** OABC is a parallelogram with OA = a and OC = c. P is the midpoint of AB and Q is the midpoint of CB.

**(a)** Find OP and OQ in terms of a and c.

**(b)** Show that PQ is parallel to AC, and state how their lengths compare. **[5]**

**7.** In triangle OAB, OA = a and OB = b. M is the midpoint of OB. N lies on AB with AN : NB = 1 : 3. X is the point with OX = (3/2)a.

**(a)** Find ON in terms of a and b.

**(b)** Find MN.

**(c)** Find MX, and hence show that M, N and X are collinear.

**(d)** Find MN : NX. **[7]**

**8.** In triangle OAB, OA = a and OB = b. M is the midpoint of OA and N is the midpoint of OB. The lines AN and BM meet at G.

**(a)** Find AN and BM in terms of a and b.

**(b)** Write OG in two ways, using OG = OA + λAN and OG = OB + μBM.

**(c)** Find λ and μ, and hence the ratio AG : GN. **[7]**

**9.** OABC is a trapezium with OA = a, OC = c and CB = (1/2)a. The diagonals OB and AC meet at X.

**(a)** Find OB and AB in terms of a and c.

**(b)** By writing OX = λOB and AX = μAC, find the ratio OX : XB.

**(c)** State the ratio of the areas of triangles OXA and BXC, giving a reason. **[6]**

**10.** ABCDEF is a regular hexagon with AB = a and BC = b. You may use the fact that AD is parallel to BC and twice as long.

**(a)** Find CD in terms of a and b.

**(b)** Write down AD.

**(c)** Find AE and BD in terms of a and b.

**(d)** What do your answers to (c) tell you about the quadrilateral ABDE? **[5]**

**11.** A is (1, 3), B is (4, 5) and C is (10, 9). Use vectors to show that A, B and C are collinear, and find AB : BC. **[4]**

**12.** In triangle OAB, OA = a and OB = b. P lies on OA with OP : PA = 3 : 1, and Q lies on OB with OQ : QB = 3 : 1.

**(a)** Show that PQ is parallel to AB.

**(b)** The area of triangle OAB is 48 cm². Find the area of triangle OPQ and the area of the quadrilateral PABQ. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** **AB = b − a** [1]. Midpoint: **(1/2)(a + b)** [1].

**2.** OP = a + (3/5)(b − a) [1] = **(2/5)a + (3/5)b** [1].

*Examiner insight:* The coefficients 2/5 and 3/5 add to 1, a check that P lies on AB.

**3.** 9a − 6b = 3(3a − 2b) and 6a − 4b = 2(3a − 2b) [1], so 9a − 6b = (3/2)(6a − 4b): parallel, with lengths in the ratio **3 : 2** [1].

**4.** a: 2p − 1 = 7, so **p = 4** [1]. b: 4 = p + q, so **q = 0** [1].

**5.** AB = (12, −6), AP = (1/3)AB = (4, −2) [1], so P = (−3 + 4, 4 − 2) = **(1, 2)** [1].

**6. (a)** OP = a + (1/2)c [1]. OQ = c + (1/2)a [1].

**(b)** PQ = OQ − OP = −(1/2)a + (1/2)c [1] = (1/2)(c − a) [1]. AC = c − a, so PQ = (1/2)AC: **PQ is parallel to AC and half its length** [1].

*Examiner insight:* In a parallelogram AB = OC = c and CB = OA = a; using these is the key to both routes.

**7. (a)** ON = a + (1/4)(b − a) [1] = **(3/4)a + (1/4)b** [1].

**(b)** MN = ON − OM = (3/4)a + (1/4)b − (1/2)b = **(3/4)a − (1/4)b** [1].

**(c)** MX = OX − OM = (3/2)a − (1/2)b [1] = 2((3/4)a − (1/4)b) = 2MN [1]. MX is parallel to MN and M is a common point, so **M, N and X are collinear** [1].

**(d)** MX = 2MN, so N is the midpoint of MX: **MN : NX = 1 : 1** [1].

*Examiner insight:* The collinear mark needs both "parallel" and "common point" in words.

**8. (a)** AN = (1/2)b − a [1]. BM = (1/2)a − b [1].

**(b)** OG = a + λ((1/2)b − a) = (1 − λ)a + (λ/2)b [1]. OG = b + μ((1/2)a − b) = (μ/2)a + (1 − μ)b [1].

**(c)** a: 1 − λ = μ/2. b: λ/2 = 1 − μ [1]. From the second, μ = 1 − λ/2; substituting, 1 − λ = 1/2 − λ/4, so λ = 2/3 and **μ = 2/3** [1]. AG = (2/3)AN, so **AG : GN = 2 : 1** [1].

*Examiner insight:* G is where two medians of the triangle cross, and each is divided 2 : 1. Equating coefficients is valid because a and b are not parallel.

**9. (a)** OB = OC + CB = **c + (1/2)a** [1]. AB = OB − OA = **c − (1/2)a** [1].

**(b)** OX = λ((1/2)a + c). Also OX = a + μ(c − a) = (1 − μ)a + μc [1]. Equate: λ/2 = 1 − μ and λ = μ, so λ/2 = 1 − λ and λ = 2/3 [1]. OX = (2/3)OB, so **OX : XB = 2 : 1** [1].

**(c)** The triangles are similar with length ratio 2 : 1 (OA is parallel to CB and twice as long), so the areas are in the ratio **4 : 1** [1].

**10. (a)** AB + BC + CD = AD = 2b, so CD = 2b − a − b = **b − a** [1].

**(b)** **AD = 2b** [1].

**(c)** DE is parallel to AB in the opposite direction, so DE = −a and AE = AD + DE = **2b − a** [1]. BD = BC + CD = b + b − a = **2b − a** [1].

**(d)** AE = BD, so AE and BD are **equal in length and parallel**, which makes **ABDE a parallelogram** [1].

**11.** AB = (3, 2) [1] and BC = (6, 4) [1]. BC = 2AB, so AB is parallel to BC, and B is a common point, so A, B and C are collinear [1]. **AB : BC = 1 : 2** [1].

**12. (a)** OP = (3/4)a and OQ = (3/4)b [1]. PQ = (3/4)b − (3/4)a = (3/4)(b − a) = (3/4)AB [1], so PQ is a multiple of AB: **parallel** [1].

**(b)** Triangles OPQ and OAB are similar with length ratio 3 : 4, so area ratio 9 : 16 and area of OPQ = (9/16) × 48 = **27 cm²** [1]. Area of PABQ = 48 − 27 = **21 cm²** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using AP : PB = m : n as m/n of AB instead of m/(m + n).
- Writing AB as a − b.
- Collinear proofs with no statement of a common point.
- Forgetting free facts from the shape, such as opposite sides of a parallelogram.
- Algebra slips when equating coefficients, especially with fractions.
- Using the length ratio instead of its square for areas.
