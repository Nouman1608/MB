These questions cover subtopic 1.17 Exponential growth and decay of Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024, 2025 to 2027. Most questions need a calculator, as on Paper 2. Questions 3, 4 and 6 can be done without one. Give money to the nearest cent and other answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise. Show the multiplier you use.

## Section A

**1.** Write down the multiplier for:

**(a)** an increase of 6% **[1]**

**(b)** a decrease of 12% **[1]**

**2.** The population of a town is 12 000 and it grows by 2.5% each year. Work out the population after 10 years, correct to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**3.** A culture contains 500 bacteria. The number of bacteria doubles every 3 hours. Find the number after 12 hours. **[2]**

**4.** A sample of 640 g of a substance halves in mass every 4 days. Find the mass after 12 days. **[2]**

**5.** A car is bought for $18 000. It loses 15% of its value each year. Find its value after 3 years. **[3]**

**6.** State whether each model shows growth or decay, and give the percentage change per period.

**(a)** y = 250 × 1.06ⁿ **[1]**

**(b)** y = 400 × 0.92ⁿ **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** The value of a machine falls from $8 000 to $5 832 over 3 years. The percentage decrease is the same each year. Find this percentage. **[3]**

**8.** $3 000 is invested at 8% compound interest per year. Find the smallest whole number of years for the investment to be worth more than $6 000. Show values to support your answer. **[3]**

**9.** The value of some equipment is $40 000. It loses 20% of its value each year. After how many whole years is its value first less than half of the original value? **[3]**

**10.** The population of a village is 60 000 and it falls by 5% each year. Find the population after 4 years, to the nearest whole number. **[3]**

**11.** A house is worth $250 000. Its value rises by 4% each year. Find its value after 3 years. **[2]**

**12.** $1 200 is increased by $120 each year (linear change), and another $1 200 is increased by 10% of its current value each year (exponential change). Find the difference between the two values after 3 years. **[3]**

**13.** $1 000 is invested at 5% per year compound interest for 2 years. The amount is then reinvested at 3% per year compound interest for 3 years. Work out the final value. **[3]**

**14.** An investment of $P grows at 4% per year. Write down an expression for its value after n years. For $P = 500, calculate the value after 6 years. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **1.06** **[1]**

**(b)** **0.88** **[1]** **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The multiplier for a decrease is 1 minus the rate, not the rate itself.

**2.** multiplier 1.025 **[1]**; 12 000 × 1.025¹⁰ = 15 361.0... **[1]**; **15 400** **[1]** **[3]**

**3.** 12 hours = 4 periods **[1]**; 500 × 2⁴ = **8 000** **[1]** **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The number of periods is 4, not 12. Divide the time by the length of the period.

**4.** 12 days = 3 periods **[1]**; 640 × 0.5³ = **80 g** **[1]** **[2]**

**5.** multiplier 0.85 **[1]**; 18 000 × 0.85³ = 18 000 × 0.614125 **[1]**; **$11 054.25** **[1]** **[3]**

**6. (a)** **growth, 6% increase** **[1]**

**(b)** **decay, 8% decrease** **[1]** **[2]**

**7.** r³ = 5832 ÷ 8000 = 0.729 **[1]**; r = 0.9 since 0.9³ = 0.729 **[1]**; decrease of **10%** per year **[1]** **[3]**

**8.** multiplier 1.08 **[1]**; after 9 years 3000 × 1.08⁹ = 5 997.01, which is under 6 000 **[1]**; after 10 years 6 476.77, so **10 years** **[1]** **[3]**

**9.** half of 40 000 is 20 000 **[1]**; after 3 years 40 000 × 0.8³ = 20 480, which is still above 20 000 **[1]**; after 4 years 16 384, so **4 years** **[1]** **[3]**

**10.** 60 000 × 0.95⁴ **[1]** = 48 870.375 **[1]**; **48 870** **[1]** **[3]**

**11.** 250 000 × 1.04³ **[1]** = **$281 216** **[1]** **[2]**

**12.** linear: 1200 + 3 × 120 = $1 560 **[1]**; exponential: 1200 × 1.1³ = $1 597.20 **[1]**; difference **$37.20** **[1]** **[3]**

**13.** after stage 1: 1000 × 1.05² = 1102.50 **[1]**; stage 2: 1102.50 × 1.03³ **[1]**; **$1 204.73** **[1]** **[3]**

**14.** value = **P × 1.04ⁿ** **[1]**; 500 × 1.04⁶ = 632.65... **[1]**; **$632.66** **[1]** **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using 1.5 or 0.15 as the multiplier in Questions 1, 2 and 5.
- Using 12 as the power instead of 4 in Question 3.
- Using linear working, 18 000 × (1 - 0.15 × 3), in Question 5.
- Not showing the year before the answer in Questions 8 and 9.
- Rounding 15 361 too early, or writing the answer to 2 significant figures, in Question 2.
- Applying the stage 2 interest to the original sum in Question 13.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need logarithms to find the number of years?**
No. The syllabus does not require them. Try whole numbers of years in order and show the values either side of the target.

**What is the difference between growth and decay?**
Growth has a multiplier greater than 1. Decay has a multiplier between 0 and 1.
