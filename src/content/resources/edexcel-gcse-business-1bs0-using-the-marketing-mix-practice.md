## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the term integrated marketing mix. **[2]**

**2.** Which **one** of the following best describes a competitive advantage? **[1]**

A: Having more employees than any other business

B: Something that makes customers choose a business over its rivals and is hard to copy

C: Spending more on advertising than last year

D: Selling the same product in every shop

**3.** A budget supermarket wants an integrated marketing mix for its own-brand range. Which **two** of the following fit that mix? **[2]**

A: Low prices

B: Luxury gift packaging

C: Regular special offers

D: Sponsorship of an opera festival

E: Selling only in exclusive department stores

**4.** Merrow Cosmetics sells 2,000 jars of face cream a month at £15. Each jar costs £6. It switches to a refillable glass jar, raising unit cost to £7.50, and raises the price to £19. Sales fall to 1,800 jars a month.

**(a)** Calculate monthly contribution before the change. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate monthly contribution after the change. **[2]**

**5.** Quickstep Laundry collects customers' washing from their homes and returns it within 24 hours, at prices similar to local launderettes. State **one** element of the marketing mix that gives Quickstep a competitive advantage. **[1]**

**6.** Tindale Bikes cuts the price of last year's models by 30% to clear its stock before new models arrive. Outline **one** way this price decision could affect Tindale's promotion. **[2]**

**7.** Explain **one** reason why a competitive advantage based on a single element of the marketing mix may not last. **[3]**

### Section B: longer questions

**8.** Explain **one** way that a decision about place can influence the product element of the marketing mix. **[3]**

**9.** Sable & Co sells silk scarves at £85, promoted in fashion magazines. To clear stock, it has also started selling them at a discount market stall. Outline **one** way this could weaken Sable & Co's competitive advantage. **[2]**

**10.** Greenleaf Grocers delivers weekly boxes of organic vegetables from farms within thirty miles. Its boxes cost more than supermarket vegetables. It promotes itself through recipe cards in each box, an e-newsletter and posts showing the farmers. Customers order online and choose their delivery day. Analyse how Greenleaf's integrated marketing mix could give it a competitive advantage over supermarkets. **[6]**

**11.** Cobble Coffee Roasters makes speciality coffee at a cost of £3.20 per bag. It markets itself as a small roaster with unusual single-farm beans. It is choosing between two routes for next year.

Option 1: supply a supermarket chain, which would pay £4.50 a bag and take 20,000 bags. The coffee would sit beside many cheaper brands.

Option 2: sell subscriptions through its own website at £9 a bag, forecast at 6,000 bags. Delivery costs £1.80 a bag and online promotion would cost £8,000 a year.

Justify which option Cobble Coffee Roasters should choose. **[9]**

**12.** Rapid Fix repairs phone screens in a shopping centre kiosk. It charges £60, the same as a rival shop nearby, but repairs most screens within 30 minutes while customers shop, uses high-quality replacement screens with a one-year guarantee, and promotes itself through online reviews. The rival takes a day for most repairs.

Evaluate which element of the marketing mix is most important to Rapid Fix's competitive advantage. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** A marketing mix in which product, price, promotion and place are consistent with each other [1] and all aimed at the same target market [1]. **[2]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A [1]. C [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Luxury packaging, opera sponsorship and exclusive stores suggest a premium position, which would contradict the low price.

**4. (a)** (£15 − £6) × 2,000 = **£18,000** [1]

**(b)** (£19 − £7.50) × 1,800 [1] = £11.50 × 1,800 = **£20,700** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The product change only pays because the price was adjusted with it. Linking the two elements is the point of 2.2.5.

**5.** Place: it collects from and returns to customers' homes within 24 hours [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* Its price is similar to rivals', so price is not the source of its advantage here.

**6.** Promotion would need to tell customers about the 30% cut, for example through in-store signs or online adverts [1], so that enough people buy before the new models arrive and the cleared stock frees space and cash [1]. **[2]**

**7.** A single element, such as a lower price or a new feature, can often be copied by rivals quickly [1]. Once rivals match it, customers no longer have a reason to choose the business [1], so the advantage disappears unless the business has built it from several elements, such as a strong brand and convenient place, that are harder to copy together [1]. **[3]**

**8.** If a business moves from shops to selling online [1], the product must be packaged to survive delivery [1], which may mean redesigning the packaging or the product itself, raising costs and possibly the price [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Another valid chain: being stocked by a supermarket may require larger pack sizes or a longer shelf life.

**9.** Customers who paid £85 for a luxury scarf may lose trust in the brand when they see it sold cheaply on a market stall [1], so the premium image built by the fashion magazine adverts is damaged and Sable may struggle to justify its full price in future [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Greenleaf's product (local organic vegetables) and its higher price both signal quality, so customers who value fresh, local food believe the price is worth paying [1]. Its promotion supports this: recipe cards and posts showing farmers tell the story of where the food comes from, something a supermarket finds hard to match [1]. Its place, online ordering and home delivery on a chosen day, adds convenience for busy customers [1]. Because every element points to the same message, customers have a clear reason to choose Greenleaf over cheaper supermarket vegetables [1]. A supermarket could cut prices on organic vegetables, but copying the whole system of local farms, personal promotion and flexible delivery would be much harder [1]. However, the advantage depends on the target market valuing local and organic produce enough to pay more; if incomes fall, some may switch back to supermarkets [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* An Analyse answer should show how the elements support each other, not just describe each one.

**11.** Option 1 contribution = (£4.50 − £3.20) × 20,000 [1] = **£26,000** [1]. Option 2 contribution = (£9 − £3.20 − £1.80) × 6,000 = £4 × 6,000 = £24,000 [1]; minus £8,000 promotion = **£16,000** [1]. Option 1 earns £10,000 more on these forecasts [1]. However, Option 1 puts a speciality coffee beside many cheaper brands, which conflicts with its image as a small roaster with unusual beans, so customers may not see why it is worth more [1]. Option 2 keeps the mix integrated: a premium product, a premium price, direct promotion and a subscription place that builds a loyal customer base [1]. Option 1 also leaves Cobble dependent on one powerful buyer that could cut its price or drop it [1]. **Judgement:** Option 2, because it protects the differentiated position on which Cobble's long-term competitive advantage depends, even though it earns less now; if Cobble needs cash urgently, Option 1 is the safer short-term choice [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* A Justify answer can reject the option with the higher figure if it gives a clear reason. Here the integrated mix provides that reason.

**12.** Place and speed of service stand out: repairing screens in 30 minutes in a shopping centre kiosk means customers do not lose their phone for a day, as they would at the rival [1]. Because convenience matters to people who rely on their phones, this gives a strong reason to choose Rapid Fix at the same £60 price [1]. The product also matters: high-quality screens and a one-year guarantee reduce the risk of a repair failing, which builds trust [1]. Promotion through online reviews spreads this reputation to new customers at little cost [1]. Price is not a source of advantage, because it is the same as the rival's [1]. The elements reinforce each other: fast service earns good reviews, which bring customers to the kiosk [1]. However, the rival could speed up its repairs, which would remove the time advantage unless quality and reviews keep customers loyal [1]. A kiosk in a busy shopping centre is also expensive, so Rapid Fix depends on high volumes [1]. **Conclusion:** place and speed of service is the most important element, because it is the main difference customers notice at the same price; but the advantage lasts only if product quality and reviews keep supporting it, so the integrated mix matters more than any single element [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* The best Evaluate answers reach a judgement about one element and then show how its strength depends on the others.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing elements separately with no link between them.
- Claiming price is an advantage when the case shows prices are the same as rivals'.
- Recommending the option with the higher figure without considering whether it fits the business's position.
- Forgetting to change the unit cost when a product is improved.
- Defining competitive advantage without saying it must be hard to copy.
