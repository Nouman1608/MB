Condensed recall for section 3.4.4 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-training/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- **Benefits of training**: increased productivity, dealing with changes in technology, increased motivation, staff retention, high-quality goods, good customer service.
- **Methods**: **induction, on-the-job, off-the-job**, and the benefits of each.
- **Analyse** the benefits and costs of on-the-job and off-the-job training.
- **Evaluate** which is most appropriate for a variety of businesses.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Training | Teaching employees the skills and knowledge needed for their job |
| Induction | Introduction of new staff to the business, its rules and the job |
| On-the-job training | Learning while doing the job, alongside an experienced worker |
| Off-the-job training | Learning away from the normal workplace, such as at a college or on a course |
| Productivity | Output per worker |
| Retention | Keeping staff in the business |
| Lost output | Production lost while staff are training or training others |

## Benefits of training

| Benefit | Link to business |
|---|---|
| Productivity up | More output per worker; lower unit cost |
| Technology | Staff can use new machines and software |
| Motivation | Staff feel valued; can progress |
| Retention | Fewer leavers; lower recruitment cost |
| Quality | Fewer faults and less waste |
| Customer service | Better handling of customers and complaints |

## Costs and risks

- Course fees, trainers' pay, materials.
- **Lost output** from trainees and trainers.
- Trained staff may **leave for rivals**.
- Benefits are **uncertain** and may take time.

## The three methods

| Method | Meaning | Benefits | Drawbacks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Induction | New staff introduced to business, rules, safety | Settle quickly; fewer mistakes; helps retention | Takes time; information overload |
| On-the-job | Learn while working, alongside experienced staff | Cheaper; specific to the business; starts at once | Trainer may teach badly; trainee slow at first; mistakes |
| Off-the-job | Learn away from work (college, course, training centre) | Specialist teaching; qualifications; new ideas | Expensive; lost output; may not fit the business |

## Choosing a method

| Factor | On-the-job suits | Off-the-job suits |
|---|---|---|
| Skill | Practical skills of this business | Technical, legal or safety knowledge |
| Business size | Small, with experienced staff | Large group of trainees |
| Budget | Limited budget | Enough money for fees |
| Risk | Low-risk tasks | Dangerous tasks (use simulators) |
| Qualifications needed | No | Yes |

Many businesses use a mix: induction, then on-the-job, with off-the-job courses for specialist skills.

## Calculations

- Off-the-job cost = fees + lost output. 8 x $600 + 3 days x 8 x $120 = $4,800 + $2,880 = $7,680.
- On-the-job cost = trainer's time + trainee's lost output. 8 x (5 x $110 + 5 x $60) = 8 x $850 = $6,800.
- Extra output = percentage gain x output per day x days x staff. 10% x $120 x 230 x 8 = $22,080.
- Payback (months) = cost / yearly benefit x 12. $7,680 / $22,080 x 12 = 4.2 months.

## Evaluating training

1. List the benefits, with numbers if you have them.
2. List the costs, including lost output.
3. Say which factors matter most for this business.
4. Judge whether the benefit exceeds the cost, and how likely the benefit is.
5. Suggest a mix of methods if suitable.

## Exam traps

- Induction is only for **new** employees.
- On-the-job training is **not free**: trainer's time and trainee's slow output have a cost.
- Off-the-job is not always best; it may not fit the business's methods.
- Link benefits to **productivity, quality, service or retention**.
- Trained staff can leave; mention it in evaluations.
- Match the method to the business in the question.

## Model answer outline: a 9-mark "evaluate" question

Question style: "Evaluate whether a call centre should use on-the-job or off-the-job training."

- **On the job:** cheaper ($580 against $680 per adviser in an example), specific to the business, starts at once; but trainers lose output and may pass on bad habits.
- **Off the job:** specialist teachers and qualifications; but fees, lost output and skills may not fit the business.
- **Context:** the type of skill, the number of trainees and the budget.
- **Calculation:** compare total cost with the saving from fewer errors.
- **Judgement:** a mix often works best (induction, then on-the-job coaching, then a specialist course). Give a reason for your main choice.

## Revision checklist

- I can define training and induction.
- I can list six benefits of training.
- I can describe on-the-job and off-the-job training with two advantages and two drawbacks each.
- I can work out the cost of each method including lost output.
- I can calculate extra output and a simple payback.
- I can list factors affecting the choice of method.
- I can name the risk of trained staff leaving.

## Self-test

1. Define training.
2. What is induction training?
3. State three benefits of training for a business.
4. Give one benefit and one drawback of on-the-job training.
5. Give one benefit and one drawback of off-the-job training.
6. State one risk to a business of training its employees.
7. Which method would be more suitable for teaching a pilot to handle emergencies? Give a reason.
8. A course costs $400 per person for 10 staff. Calculate the total fees.
9. Each of the 10 staff loses 2 days of work, each worth $100. Calculate the lost output.
10. Using Questions 8 and 9, calculate the total cost of the course.
11. Explain how training can improve staff retention.
12. Explain why on-the-job training may be more suitable for a small cafe than an off-the-job course.

### Answers

1. Teaching employees the skills and knowledge needed to do their job.
2. The introduction of new employees to the business, its rules, safety procedures and the job.
3. Any three: higher productivity; coping with new technology; motivation; retention; quality; customer service.
4. Benefit: cheaper (or specific to the business). Drawback: the trainer's output falls (or trainee works slowly, or bad habits are passed on).
5. Benefit: specialist teaching or qualifications. Drawback: expensive or lost output while staff are away.
6. Trained staff may leave to work for a rival.
7. Off-the-job (a simulator), because mistakes in a real aircraft would be dangerous.
8. 10 x $400 = **$4,000**.
9. 10 x 2 x $100 = **$2,000**.
10. $4,000 + $2,000 = **$6,000**.
11. Training makes staff more skilled and confident, shows the business values them and gives promotion chances, so they are less likely to leave.
12. It is cheaper, requires no fees or travel, can start at once and teaches the cafe's own methods, which suits a business with a small budget.
