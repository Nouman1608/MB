## Hazards of using a mains supply

| Hazard | Danger |
|---|---|
| Damaged insulation | Live wire exposed: **shock**; short circuit: **fire** |
| Overheating cables | Insulation melts: **fire**, then exposed wires |
| Damp conditions | Water conducts: **shock** |
| Overloading plugs, extension leads, single and multiple sockets | Total current too large: cables overheat, **fire** |

- Appliances on one extension lead are in parallel: **their currents add**.

## The mains circuit

- **Live (line) wire:** carries the high alternating p.d.; the dangerous wire.
- **Neutral wire:** completes the circuit; close to zero p.d.
- **Earth wire:** safety wire to the ground; carries current only in a fault.
- **Switch in the live wire:** "off" disconnects the appliance from the high p.d.
- A switch in the neutral would leave the appliance's parts **live** even when off.
- Fuses also go in the live wire, for the same reason.

## Fuses

- Thin wire that **heats and melts** when the current exceeds its rating.
- Breaks the circuit and disconnects the live supply.
- Must be **replaced** after the fault is repaired.

**Method in steps: choosing a fuse rating**

1. Calculate the normal current: I = P / V (power in watts).
2. List the available ratings.
3. Choose the **smallest rating greater than** the normal current.
4. Give the reason: lower would blow in normal use; much higher would not protect against a fault.

## Trip switches

- Automatic switch that **opens** when the current exceeds its setting.
- Can be **reset** once the fault is fixed; nothing to replace.
- Choose the setting just above the normal maximum current of the circuit.

## Earthing and double insulation

**Earthed metal case, in steps**

1. Fault: live wire touches the metal case.
2. Large current flows through the low-resistance earth wire to the ground.
3. The fuse melts (or trip switch opens).
4. The live supply is disconnected; the case is safe to touch.

**Double-insulated appliance**

- Outer casing is **non-conducting** (for example plastic): it cannot become live.
- **No earth wire** is needed.
- Its fuse (with no earth wire) **protects the circuit and the cabling**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Live wire | Wire carrying the high alternating p.d. from the supply |
| Neutral wire | Return wire completing the circuit, near zero p.d. |
| Earth wire | Low-resistance safety path from a metal case to the ground |
| Fuse rating | Current above which the fuse melts |
| Trip switch | Automatic switch that opens on excess current and can be reset |
| Double-insulated | Has a non-conducting outer casing; needs no earth wire |
| Overloading | Drawing too much total current from one plug, lead or socket |

## Exam traps

- Reason for the switch in live: **disconnects from the high p.d.**, not "turns it off".
- A fuse **melts**; it does not "limit" or "reduce" the current.
- The earth wire does not store or absorb charge; it provides a path for a large fault current.
- The fuse rating must be **above** the normal current, not equal to or below it.
- Double-insulated appliances still have fuses.
- "Water and electricity don't mix" scores nothing: say water **conducts**.

## Self-test

1. Name the three wires in a mains circuit.
2. Which wire is at a high p.d.?
3. Explain why a switch is connected in the live wire.
4. State the hazard of using a hair dryer with wet hands.
5. Why is plugging many appliances into one extension lead dangerous?
6. Describe how a fuse protects a circuit.
7. A 230 V television is rated at 150 W. Choose a fuse from 3 A, 5 A and 13 A.
8. A 230 V iron draws a current of 5.2 A. Explain why a 5 A fuse is unsuitable.
9. State one advantage of a trip switch over a fuse.
10. Explain why a metal-cased toaster must be earthed.
11. Why does a double-insulated drill not need an earth wire?
12. What does the fuse protect in a double-insulated appliance?

### Answers

1. **Live** (line), **neutral** and **earth**.
2. The **live** wire.
3. So that when switched off the appliance is **disconnected from the high p.d.**; a switch in the neutral would leave parts live.
4. **Electric shock**: water conducts, so current can flow through the person.
5. The currents add, so the **total current** is too large; the cables **overheat** and could cause a fire.
6. If the current exceeds the rating the fuse wire **melts**, breaking the circuit and disconnecting the live supply.
7. I = 150 ÷ 230 = 0.65 A, so **3 A**.
8. 5 A is **below** the normal current, so the fuse would melt in normal use; a **13 A** fuse is needed.
9. It can be **reset** rather than replaced.
10. If the live wire touches the case, a large current flows to **earth**, the fuse melts and the live supply is cut off, so the case does not stay live.
11. Its outer casing is **non-conducting**, so it cannot become live.
12. The **circuit and the cabling**.
