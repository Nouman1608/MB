This set practises **3.3.9.1 Carboxylic acids and esters** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content, assessed in Unit 4 and in the synoptic Unit 5. If you get stuck, go back to the [carboxylic acids and esters study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-9-1/) or the [carboxylic acids and esters revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-9-1-revision-notes/). Use Ar values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, O = 16.0, Na = 23.0, K = 39.1 and Mg = 24.3.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Name each ester.

**(a)** CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH(CH₃)₂ **[1]**

**(b)** CH₃CH₂OOCCH₂CH₃ **[1]**

**(c)** HCOOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ **[1]**

**2.** Which pair of compounds are functional group isomers of each other?

A: butanoic acid and ethyl ethanoate

B: propanoic acid and methyl propanoate

C: ethyl methanoate and methyl ethanoate

D: ethanoic acid and ethanol

**[1]**

**3.** 2-Methylpropanoic acid, (CH₃)₂CHCOOH, is added to solid magnesium carbonate.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** State two observations. **[1]**

**4.** Esters are used as plasticisers.

**(a)** State one other use of esters. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how a plasticiser makes PVC more flexible. **[2]**

**5.** Ethyl methanoate is heated under reflux with dilute sulfuric acid.

**(a)** Name the two organic products. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the ester is not completely hydrolysed under these conditions. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why methyl ethanoate, CH₃COOCH₃, boils at a much lower temperature than its isomer propanoic acid, CH₃CH₂COOH. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** Butanoic acid reacts with ethanol in the presence of an acid catalyst.

`CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH + CH₃CH₂OH ⇌ CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O`

**(a)** Write an expression for Kc for this reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why Kc has no units for this reaction. **[1]**

**(c)** 0.300 mol of butanoic acid and 0.300 mol of ethanol are mixed with a catalyst. At the temperature used, Kc = 4.00. Calculate the amount of ethyl butanoate present at equilibrium. **[2]**

**(d)** State and explain the effect on the amount of ethyl butanoate of adding more ethanol to the equilibrium mixture. **[1]**

**8.** A monocarboxylic acid, RCOOH, contains only carbon, hydrogen and oxygen. A 0.888 g sample reacts completely with excess sodium carbonate and releases 0.264 g of carbon dioxide.

**(a)** Write a general equation for the reaction, using RCOOH. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the Mr of the acid. **[3]**

**(c)** Identify the acid. **[1]**

**(d)** Name the two esters that are isomers of this acid. **[2]**

**9.** Ester Y, C₅H₁₀O₂, is heated under reflux with aqueous sodium hydroxide. The products are sodium ethanoate and an alcohol, B. When B is warmed with acidified potassium dichromate(VI), the mixture turns green and the organic product is a ketone.

**(a)** Identify B and explain how the information shows this. **[2]**

**(b)** Give the structural formula and name of Y. **[1]**

**(c)** Write an equation for the reaction of Y with sodium hydroxide. **[1]**

**(d)** State how ethanoic acid could be obtained from the reaction mixture. **[1]**

**10.** Glyceryl trihexadecanoate, (C₁₅H₃₁COO)₃C₃H₅ (Mr = 806.0), is boiled with excess aqueous potassium hydroxide to make a soft soap.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** 40.3 g of the triester is used and the yield of potassium hexadecanoate, C₁₅H₃₁COOK, is 80.0%. Calculate the mass of soap obtained. **[3]**

**(c)** Calculate the maximum mass of glycerol (Mr = 92.0) that could form from the same sample. **[1]**

**11.** One triester in a vegetable oil has a dodecanoate, a hexadecanoate and an octadecanoate group on the same glycerol unit. Its formula is C₄₉H₉₄O₆ (Mr = 778.0). The oil is converted into biodiesel.

**(a)** Name the three esters formed from this triester. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why biodiesel is described as a mixture. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the minimum mass of methanol needed to convert 3.89 kg of this triester completely. **[2]**

**(d)** State what else, besides methanol, is needed for the reaction. **[1]**

**12.** A fat is hydrolysed in two separate experiments: in one it is refluxed with dilute hydrochloric acid, in the other with aqueous sodium hydroxide.

**(a)** Compare the organic products of the two experiments. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why only the second experiment can be used to make soap. **[2]**

**13.** A student tests three colourless liquids, ethanoic acid, ethyl ethanoate and ethanol, by adding a small amount of each to sodium carbonate solution. Describe what the student sees with each liquid and explain the result for ethanoic acid. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 1-Methylethyl butanoate [1]

**(b)** Ethyl propanoate [1]

**(c)** Butyl methanoate [1]

*Examiner insight:* in (b) the formula is written "backwards"; the C=O carbon belongs to the CH₂CH₃ end, so the acid part is propanoate.

**2.** **A** [1]. Both have formula C₄H₈O₂ but different functional groups. C is a pair of isomers with the same functional group; B and D have different molecular formulae.

**3. (a)** 2(CH₃)₂CHCOOH + MgCO₃ → ((CH₃)₂CHCOO)₂Mg + CO₂ + H₂O [1]

**(b)** Effervescence (fizzing) and the solid disappears; accept the gas turns limewater cloudy [1]

**4. (a)** Any one of: solvents, perfumes, food flavourings [1]

**(b)** Ester molecules fit between the polymer chains [1]; they weaken the intermolecular forces between the chains, so the chains can slide past each other more easily [1]

**5. (a)** Methanoic acid and ethanol [1]

**(b)** The reaction is reversible; the acid and alcohol formed can react to re-form the ester, so an equilibrium mixture results [1]

**6.** Propanoic acid has an O–H group, so its molecules form hydrogen bonds with each other [1]; methyl ethanoate has no O–H, so only weaker permanent dipole–dipole and van der Waals forces act between its molecules, needing less energy to overcome [1]

**7. (a)** Kc = [CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₂CH₃][H₂O] ÷ ([CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH][CH₃CH₂OH]) [1]

**(b)** There are equal numbers of moles (two) on each side, so the concentration units cancel [1]

**(c)** Let x = amount of ester: x² ÷ (0.300 − x)² = 4.00, so x ÷ (0.300 − x) = 2.00 [1]; x = 0.600 − 2x, so x = **0.200 mol** [1]

**(d)** The amount of ethyl butanoate increases, because the equilibrium shifts to the right to use up the added ethanol [1]

*Examiner insight:* in (c) take the square root of both sides; expanding the quadratic works too but is slower and easier to get wrong.

**8. (a)** 2RCOOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2RCOONa + CO₂ + H₂O [1]

**(b)** n(CO₂) = 0.264 ÷ 44.0 = 0.00600 mol [1]; n(acid) = 2 × 0.00600 = 0.0120 mol [1]; Mr = 0.888 ÷ 0.0120 = **74.0** [1]

**(c)** Propanoic acid, CH₃CH₂COOH (C₃H₆O₂) [1]

**(d)** Methyl ethanoate [1]; ethyl methanoate [1]

*Examiner insight:* using a 1 : 1 ratio gives Mr 148, which matches no simple monocarboxylic acid; let a strange answer send you back to the equation.

**9. (a)** B is **propan-2-ol** [1]. Y has five carbons and the acid part (ethanoate) has two, so B has three carbons; B is oxidised to a ketone, so it is a secondary alcohol [1]

**(b)** CH₃COOCH(CH₃)₂, 1-methylethyl ethanoate [1]

**(c)** CH₃COOCH(CH₃)₂ + NaOH → CH₃COONa + (CH₃)₂CHOH [1]

**(d)** Add a strong acid such as dilute hydrochloric acid to convert the ethanoate ions into ethanoic acid (then distil) [1]

**10. (a)** (C₁₅H₃₁COO)₃C₃H₅ + 3KOH → 3C₁₅H₃₁COOK + C₃H₅(OH)₃. Correct formulae [1]; 3KOH and 3 soap balanced [1]

**(b)** n(triester) = 40.3 ÷ 806.0 = 0.0500 mol [1]; n(soap) = 3 × 0.0500 = 0.150 mol, Mr = 294.1, so theoretical mass = 44.1 g [1]; at 80.0%: 0.800 × 44.1 = **35.3 g** [1]

**(c)** 0.0500 × 92.0 = **4.60 g** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the most common slip is forgetting the factor of 3 in (b), which gives 11.8 g.

**11. (a)** Methyl dodecanoate, methyl hexadecanoate and methyl octadecanoate. All three [2]; two correct [1]

**(b)** Each triester gives three methyl esters, and the oil contains triesters with different acid chains, so several different methyl esters form [1]

**(c)** n(triester) = 3890 ÷ 778.0 = 5.00 mol, so n(methanol) = 15.0 mol [1]; mass = 15.0 × 32.0 = **480 g** [1]

**(d)** A catalyst (for example a strong base such as potassium hydroxide) [1]

**12. (a)** Both give glycerol (propane-1,2,3-triol) [1]; the acid gives long-chain carboxylic acids, the alkali gives their sodium salts [1]

**(b)** Soap is the sodium salt of a long-chain carboxylic acid, which forms only in alkali [1]; in acid the hydrolysis is reversible and gives the free acids, not salts [1]

**13.** Ethanoic acid: effervescence [1]; ethyl ethanoate and ethanol: no effervescence [1]; ethanoic acid releases H⁺ ions, which react with carbonate ions to give carbon dioxide (2H⁺ + CO₃²⁻ → CO₂ + H₂O) [1]

*Examiner insight:* an observation is needed for every liquid; "nothing happens" scores, "no reaction" alone may not.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading the ester formula left to right instead of finding the carbonyl carbon.
- Using 1 : 1 instead of 2 : 1 for acid : carbonate.
- Forgetting the factor of 3 for fats, soaps and methyl esters.
- Giving the free acid as an alkaline hydrolysis product.
- Saying the catalyst shifts the esterification equilibrium.
