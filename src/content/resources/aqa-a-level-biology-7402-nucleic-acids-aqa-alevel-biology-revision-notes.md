## Roles

- **DNA** holds genetic information in all living cells.
- **RNA** transfers genetic information from DNA to the ribosomes.
- **Ribosomes** are formed from RNA and proteins.

## Nucleotides

| | DNA nucleotide | RNA nucleotide |
|---|---|---|
| Pentose | Deoxyribose | Ribose |
| Phosphate | Yes | Yes |
| Bases | A, C, G, T | A, C, G, U |

Nucleotides join by **condensation**, forming **phosphodiester bonds** between the phosphate of one nucleotide and the sugar of the next. This builds the sugar-phosphate backbone.

## DNA and RNA molecules

| | DNA | RNA |
|---|---|---|
| Chains | Two | One |
| Shape | Double helix | Single chain |
| Length | Very long | Relatively short |
| Pairing | A–T, C–G by hydrogen bonds | Not paired as a double helix |

## Structure linked to function

| Feature of DNA | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Sugar-phosphate backbone of covalent phosphodiester bonds | Strong, so the sequence is stable |
| Bases inside the helix | Protected from reaction with other chemicals |
| Many hydrogen bonds between base pairs | Hold the strands together overall |
| Each hydrogen bond weak | Strands separate easily for replication |
| Specific complementary pairing | One strand fixes the sequence of the other, so copies are accurate |
| Very long molecule | Stores a large amount of information |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Polynucleotide | Polymer of nucleotides |
| Complementary base pairing | A with T (or U in RNA), C with G |
| Double helix | Two polynucleotide chains twisted together, joined by hydrogen bonds between bases |
| Template strand | An original strand used to line up new nucleotides |
| DNA helicase | Unwinds the helix and breaks hydrogen bonds between bases |
| DNA polymerase | Catalyses condensation reactions joining adjacent nucleotides |
| Semi-conservative replication | Each new molecule has one original and one new strand |
| Genetic continuity | Daughter cells receive the same genetic information |

## Writing complementary sequences

- DNA partner: A↔T, C↔G.
- RNA from DNA: A in DNA → U in RNA; T → A; C → G; G → C.

Mini reminder with a new sequence: DNA strand G C A T T A → partner DNA C G T A A T → complementary RNA C G U A A U.

## Why DNA's simplicity caused doubt

Only four bases and a repeating backbone, while proteins use twenty amino acids. The code works through **sequence**: 4ⁿ possible sequences of length n (4³ = 64; 4¹⁰ ≈ 1.05 million).

## Replication in order

1. Helicase unwinds the double helix.
2. Helicase breaks hydrogen bonds between complementary bases; strands separate.
3. Free DNA nucleotides are attracted to exposed bases on both template strands; complementary pairs form.
4. DNA polymerase joins adjacent nucleotides by condensation (phosphodiester bonds).
5. Two molecules form, each with one original and one new strand.

## Density bands (¹⁴N and ¹⁵N)

| Model | Heavy to light, generation 1 | Heavy to light, generation 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Semi-conservative | All intermediate | Half intermediate, half light |
| Conservative | Half heavy, half light | Quarter heavy, three-quarters light |
| Dispersive | All intermediate | One band between intermediate and light |

Generation 1 rules out conservative; generation 2 rules out dispersive. Reverse the labels if the bacteria move from light to heavy.

## Exam traps

- Thymine in RNA, or uracil in DNA.
- "DNA polymerase forms hydrogen bonds." It catalyses condensation between nucleotides.
- "Helicase breaks phosphodiester bonds." It breaks hydrogen bonds.
- Forgetting that ribosomes contain RNA.
- Describing RNA as double-stranded.
- Saying results "prove" semi-conservative replication. They rule out alternatives.
- Applying base-pair rules to a single strand on its own.

## Self-test

1. Name the pentose in an RNA nucleotide.
2. Which base is found in DNA but not RNA?
3. Name the bond joining nucleotides in a polynucleotide.
4. State two differences between a DNA molecule and an RNA molecule.
5. What are ribosomes formed from?
6. Write the complementary DNA strand for A A G C T C.
7. Write the RNA sequence complementary to the DNA strand A A G C T C.
8. State the role of DNA helicase.
9. State the role of DNA polymerase.
10. Explain what semi-conservative means.
11. After one generation of moving from ¹⁵N to ¹⁴N, which model is ruled out by a single intermediate band?
12. How many different sequences are possible for a stretch of 5 bases?

### Answers

1. Ribose.
2. Thymine.
3. Phosphodiester bond.
4. Any two: DNA has two chains, RNA one; DNA is very long, RNA relatively short; DNA has deoxyribose, RNA ribose; DNA has thymine, RNA uracil.
5. RNA and proteins.
6. T T C G A G.
7. U U C G A G.
8. Unwinds the double helix and breaks the hydrogen bonds between complementary bases.
9. Catalyses the condensation reactions that join adjacent nucleotides, forming phosphodiester bonds.
10. Each new DNA molecule contains one strand from the original molecule and one newly made strand.
11. The conservative model.
12. 4⁵ = 1,024.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the role of RNA in AQA A-level Biology 3.1.5?**
RNA transfers genetic information from DNA to the ribosomes, and ribosomes themselves are formed from RNA and proteins.

**Which enzyme breaks hydrogen bonds in DNA replication?**
DNA helicase, which also unwinds the double helix.

**What does semi-conservative replication ensure?**
Genetic continuity between generations of cells, because each copy keeps one original strand as a template.
