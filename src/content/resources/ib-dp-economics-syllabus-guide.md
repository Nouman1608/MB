This guide covers all four units of the **IB Diploma Programme Economics**
syllabus, first assessment 2022, for both Standard Level (SL) and Higher
Level (HL) students. Where a sub-topic is HL-only, it is marked as such --
nothing beyond SL content is presented as compulsory for SL students.

Total recommended teaching time is 150 hours at SL and 240 hours at HL,
split across the four units below plus an internal assessment component
(a portfolio of three commentaries, 20 hours at both SL and HL).

## Unit 1 -- Introduction to economics (10 hours, both SL and HL)

- **1.1 What is economics?** -- the nature of the subject, scarcity,
  choice and opportunity cost, the basic economic questions every
  society must answer.
- **1.2 How do economists approach the world?** -- positive and normative
  economics, the role of models and assumptions, rational and irrational
  decision-making.

## Unit 2 -- Microeconomics (35 hours SL / 70 hours HL)

- **2.1 Demand** (includes HL-only sub-topics)
- **2.2 Supply** (includes HL-only sub-topics)
- **2.3 Competitive market equilibrium**
- **2.4 Critique of the maximizing behaviour of consumers and
  producers** (HL only)
- **2.5 Elasticity of demand** (includes HL-only sub-topics)
- **2.6 Elasticity of supply** (includes HL-only sub-topics)
- **2.7 Role of government in microeconomics** (includes an HL-only
  calculation)
- **2.8 Market failure -- externalities and common pool or common access
  resources** (includes an HL-only calculation)
- **2.9 Market failure -- public goods**
- **2.10 Market failure -- asymmetric information** (HL only)
- **2.11 Market failure -- market power** (HL only)
- **2.12 The market's inability to achieve equity** (HL only)

This is the largest unit in the syllabus, and the HL-only sub-topics
(2.4, 2.10, 2.11, 2.12, plus extensions inside 2.1/2.2/2.5/2.6/2.7/2.8)
account for most of the extra 35 hours HL students spend beyond SL.

## Unit 3 -- Macroeconomics (40 hours SL / 75 hours HL)

- **3.1 Measuring economic activity and illustrating its variations**
- **3.2 Variations in economic activity -- aggregate demand and
  aggregate supply**
- **3.3 Macroeconomic objectives** (includes an HL-only calculation)
- **3.4 Economics of inequality and poverty** (includes an HL-only
  calculation)
- **3.5 Demand management (demand-side policies) -- monetary policy**
  (includes HL-only sub-topics)
- **3.6 Demand management -- fiscal policy** (includes HL-only
  sub-topics)
- **3.7 Supply-side policies**

## Unit 4 -- The global economy (45 hours SL / 65 hours HL)

- **4.1 Benefits of international trade** (includes HL-only sub-topics
  and a calculation)
- **4.2 Types of trade protection** (includes HL-only calculations)
- **4.3 Arguments for and against trade control/protection**
- **4.4 Economic integration**
- **4.5 Exchange rates** (includes an HL-only sub-topic)
- **4.6 Balance of payments** (includes HL-only sub-topics)
- **4.7 Sustainable development** (includes an HL-only sub-topic)
- **4.8 Measuring development**
- **4.9 Barriers to economic growth and/or economic development**
- **4.10 Economic growth and/or economic development strategies**

## How the four units connect

Unit 1 sets up the toolkit (scarcity, opportunity cost, models) that
Units 2-4 apply at three different scales: the individual market
(Unit 2), the national economy (Unit 3), and the world economy
(Unit 4). A student who is shaky on Unit 2's supply-and-demand
diagrams will struggle disproportionately in Units 3 and 4, since both
reuse the same diagrammatic language (shifts, equilibrium, elasticity)
applied to aggregate and international markets instead of a single
good.

## Assessment

DP Economics is assessed through three external written papers plus
an internally-assessed portfolio of three commentaries (20 hours of
the total teaching time at both SL and HL). The external papers test
definitions, diagram construction, short-answer application and
extended essay-style evaluation -- Economics is graded heavily on
evaluation (weighing up arguments, not just describing them), which is
worth practising deliberately rather than assuming it follows
automatically from knowing the content.
