These questions cover **Section C: Coordinate geometry in the (x, y) plane** of the AQA A-level
Mathematics (7357) specification, version 1.3 (31 January 2018), for A-level exams from June 2018
onwards: content references C1 to C4. Section C is listed under Paper 1, and Papers 2 and 3 can
also assess it. A calculator is required in every 7357 assessment, but show your algebra: the method
marks are for the working. Questions 11 and 12 are longer problems that combine ideas.

## Questions

**1.** Find the equation of the line through (3, −4) that is parallel to 5x − 2y + 7 = 0. Give your answer in the form ax + by + c = 0, where a, b and c are integers. **[3]**

**2.** The lines L₁ and L₂ have equations 2x − 3y + 13 = 0 and 3x + 2y − 13 = 0.

**(a)** Show that L₁ and L₂ are perpendicular. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of P, the point where L₁ and L₂ meet. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the exact area of the triangle formed by L₁, L₂ and the y-axis. **[3]**

**3.** A plumber charges a fixed call-out fee plus an hourly rate. A 2-hour job costs £130 and a 5-hour job costs £265. Assume the cost £C is a linear function of the time h hours.

**(a)** Find C in terms of h. **[3]**
**(b)** Interpret the gradient and the intercept of your model in context. **[2]**

**4.** A circle has equation x² + y² + 8x − 2y − 8 = 0.

**(a)** Find the centre and radius of the circle. **[3]**
**(b)** Determine whether the point (−1, 4) lies inside, on or outside the circle. **[2]**

**5.** A circle C has equation x² + y² − 10x − 4y + 4 = 0. The points A(1, −1) and B(8, 6) lie on C.

**(a)** Find the centre and radius of C. **[3]**
**(b)** M is the midpoint of AB. Show that the line from the centre to M is perpendicular to AB. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the exact shortest distance from the centre of C to the chord AB. **[2]**

**6.** P(−3, 2) and Q(5, 8) are the ends of a diameter of a circle.

**(a)** Find the equation of the circle. **[3]**
**(b)** Verify that R(4, 9) lies on the circle. **[1]**
**(c)** State the size of angle PRQ, giving a reason, and hence find the area of triangle PQR. **[3]**

**7.** The line y = kx + 5 is a tangent to the circle x² + y² = 5.

**(a)** Find the possible values of k. **[4]**
**(b)** For the positive value of k, find the point of contact. **[2]**

**8.**

**(a)** A curve has parametric equations x = 4cos θ, y = 3sin θ, 0 ≤ θ < 2π. Find its Cartesian equation. **[3]**
**(b)** Write down parametric equations for the circle (x + 1)² + (y − 4)² = 49. **[2]**

**9.** A curve has parametric equations x = 2 + sin θ, y = cos²θ, 0 ≤ θ < 2π. Find its Cartesian equation and state the set of possible values of x. **[4]**

**10.** A curve has parametric equations x = t + 2, y = t² − 1.

**(a)** Find a Cartesian equation of the curve. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of the points where the curve meets the line y = 4x − 12. **[4]**

**11.** A point P on the rim of an observation wheel is modelled by x = 18sin(πt/12), y = 20 − 18cos(πt/12), where t is the time in minutes from when P is at its lowest point, and x and y are in metres, with y the height above the ground.

**(a)** Find a Cartesian equation of the path of P. **[3]**
**(b)** State the radius of the wheel and the height of its centre. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the time for one revolution. **[1]**
**(d)** Find the first time at which P is 29 m above the ground. **[3]**
**(e)** Find how long P spends above 29 m in each revolution. **[1]**
**(f)** Give one limitation of the model. **[1]**

**12.** A circle has centre C(1, 3) and equation (x − 1)² + (y − 3)² = 20.

**(a)** Show that A(5, 5) and B(3, −1) lie on the circle. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the equations of the tangents to the circle at A and at B. **[4]**
**(c)** The tangents meet at T. Find the coordinates of T. **[2]**
**(d)** Show that CATB is a square and find its area. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Gradient of 5x − 2y + 7 = 0 is 5/2 [1]. y + 4 = (5/2)(x − 3) [1], so 2y + 8 = 5x − 15, giving **5x − 2y − 23 = 0** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the final mark needs the exact form asked for; y = (5/2)x − 23/2 is correct but does not have integer a, b, c in ax + by + c = 0, so it loses the last mark.

**2. (a)** Gradients are 2/3 and −3/2 [1]. (2/3)(−3/2) = −1, so perpendicular [1].
**(b)** Eliminate y: 4x − 6y = −26 and 9x + 6y = 39 [1], so 13x = 13 [1]. **P = (1, 5)** [1].
**(c)** y-intercepts: 13/3 (L₁) and 13/2 (L₂) [1]. Base = 13/2 − 13/3 = 13/6, height = 1 (the x-coordinate of P) [1]. Area = (1/2)(13/6)(1) = **13/12** [1].
*Examiner insight:* in a "show that", state the conclusion ("so the lines are perpendicular") after the product; a bare −1 with no statement can lose the final mark.

**3. (a)** Gradient = (265 − 130)/(5 − 2) = 45 [1]. Intercept = 130 − 2 × 45 = 40 [1]. **C = 45h + 40** [1].
**(b)** £45 is the charge for each hour [1]; £40 is the fixed call-out fee [1].
*Examiner insight:* interpretations must use the context and units ("£45 per hour"), not "the gradient is the rate of change".

**4. (a)** (x + 4)² − 16 + (y − 1)² − 1 − 8 = 0 [1]. (x + 4)² + (y − 1)² = 25 [1]. **Centre (−4, 1), radius 5** [1].
**(b)** (−1 + 4)² + (4 − 1)² = 9 + 9 = 18 [1]. 18 < 25, so the point is **inside** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the comparison must be with r² = 25 (or √18 with r = 5); comparing 18 with 5 is a method error and scores nothing for the conclusion.

**5. (a)** (x − 5)² − 25 + (y − 2)² − 4 + 4 = 0 [1]. Centre **(5, 2)** [1], radius **5** [1].
**(b)** M = (9/2, 5/2) [1]. Gradient AB = 7/7 = 1 and gradient from (5, 2) to M = (1/2)/(−1/2) = −1 [1]. Product −1, so the line is perpendicular to AB [1].
**(c)** The perpendicular from the centre meets AB at M, so the distance is √((1/2)² + (1/2)²) [1] = **√2/2** [1].
*Examiner insight:* "exact" means a surd; 0.707 earns the method mark but not the accuracy mark.

**6. (a)** Centre = midpoint of PQ = (1, 5) [1]. PQ = √(8² + 6²) = 10, so r = 5 [1]. **(x − 1)² + (y − 5)² = 25** [1].
**(b)** (4 − 1)² + (9 − 5)² = 9 + 16 = 25, so R lies on the circle [1].
**(c)** **90°**, because the angle in a semicircle is a right angle [1]. PR = √(49 + 49) = 7√2 and QR = √(1 + 1) = √2 [1]. Area = (1/2)(7√2)(√2) = **7** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the reason mark needs the circle property linked to PQ being a diameter; a bare "90°" with no reason does not earn it.

**7. (a)** x² + (kx + 5)² = 5 [1]. (1 + k²)x² + 10kx + 20 = 0 [1]. Tangent, so 100k² − 80(1 + k²) = 0 [1], giving 20k² = 80 and **k = ±2** [1].
**(b)** k = 2: 5x² + 20x + 20 = 0, so x = −2 [1]. y = 2(−2) + 5 = 1: **(−2, 1)** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the method mark for the discriminant needs "= 0" for a tangent; using "> 0" or "≥ 0" loses that mark and everything that follows from it.

**8. (a)** cos θ = x/4 and sin θ = y/3 [1]. cos²θ + sin²θ = 1 [1], so **x²/16 + y²/9 = 1** [1].
**(b)** **x = −1 + 7cos θ** [1], **y = 4 + 7sin θ**, 0 ≤ θ < 2π [1].
*Examiner insight:* writing 49 instead of 7 as the multiplier is a common slip; the coefficient is r, not r².

**9.** sin θ = x − 2 [1]. y = cos²θ = 1 − sin²θ [1], so **y = 1 − (x − 2)²** [1]. Since −1 ≤ sin θ ≤ 1, **1 ≤ x ≤ 3** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the domain is a separate mark; the Cartesian equation alone scores at most 3 out of 4.

**10. (a)** t = x − 2 [1], so **y = (x − 2)² − 1** [1].
**(b)** t² − 1 = 4(t + 2) − 12 [1]. t² − 4t + 3 = 0 [1], so t = 1 or t = 3 [1]. Points **(3, 0) and (5, 8)** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the final mark needs both points as coordinates; stopping at the values of t is incomplete.

**11. (a)** sin(πt/12) = x/18 and cos(πt/12) = (20 − y)/18 [1]. Using sin² + cos² = 1 [1]: **x² + (y − 20)² = 324** [1].
**(b)** Radius **18 m**; centre **20 m** above the ground [1].
**(c)** One revolution when πt/12 = 2π, so **t = 24 minutes** [1].
**(d)** 20 − 18cos(πt/12) = 29 [1]. cos(πt/12) = −1/2 [1]. πt/12 = 2π/3, so **t = 8 minutes** [1].
**(e)** The next solution is πt/12 = 4π/3, t = 16, so P is above 29 m for **8 minutes** [1].
**(f)** For example, the wheel may stop or slow down to let people on and off, so the speed is not constant [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (d), angles in a model like this must be in radians; working in degrees with π in the equation gives a wrong time and loses the accuracy mark.

**12. (a)** A: 4² + 2² = 20 [1]. B: 2² + (−4)² = 20 [1].
**(b)** Gradient CA = 2/4 = 1/2, so tangent gradient −2 [1]: **y = −2x + 15** [1]. Gradient CB = −4/2 = −2, so tangent gradient 1/2 [1]: **x − 2y − 5 = 0** [1].
**(c)** x − 2(−2x + 15) − 5 = 0 [1], so x = 7 and **T = (7, 1)** [1].
**(d)** (−2)(1/2) = −1, so the tangents are perpendicular; each is perpendicular to its radius, so all four angles of CATB are 90° [1]. CA = CB = √20 and TA = √(2² + 4²) = √20, so adjacent sides are equal [1]. Area = (√20)² = **20** [1].
*Examiner insight:* a "show that" for a square needs both right angles and equal adjacent sides; showing only one of them leaves the shape unproved and loses a mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a line in the wrong form, or with fractional a, b, c when integers were asked for.
- Taking the gradient of 5x − 2y + 7 = 0 as 5 or −5/2 instead of 5/2.
- Sign errors in the centre: (x + 4)² means the centre has x = −4.
- Comparing a distance with r² (or a squared distance with r) when testing inside or outside.
- Using the radius gradient as the tangent gradient.
- Not stating the circle property that justifies a right angle or a bisected chord.
- Using the circle equation instead of the line to find y after solving a line-circle quadratic.
- Leaving out the range of x after eliminating a trigonometric parameter.
- Working in degrees when the model is in radians.
- Giving a decimal when the question says "exact".
