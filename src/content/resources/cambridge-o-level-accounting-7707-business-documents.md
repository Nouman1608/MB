This guide covers section **2.2 Business documents** of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for examination in 2027, 2028 and 2029. The syllabus lists eight documents: "invoice, debit note, credit note, cheque counterfoil, paying-in slip, receipt, bank statement, statement of account". You must understand "how these business documents are used as sources of information" and these documents "being produced and recorded manually or digitally". If you sit an earlier series, check the syllabus for your exam year.

Use it with the [business documents revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-business-documents-revision-notes/) and the [business documents practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-business-documents-practice/). The documents are recorded in the books covered in the [books of prime entry study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-books-of-prime-entry/). The [shared guide to business documents and books of prime entry](/resources/igcse-accounting-business-documents-and-books-of-prime-entry/) gives a summary table. This page follows one trading relationship through every document. All businesses and figures are invented; amounts are in dollars.

## Why documents matter

A business document is written evidence that a transaction has taken place. It is the **source** from which the first accounting record is made. Documents also let a business:

- check that it has been charged, or has charged, the right amount
- prove a payment was made or received
- compare its own records with those of a customer, supplier or bank
- trace any figure in the accounts back to the original transaction

## One transaction cycle, eight documents

Halden Office Supplies sells to Pryce Accountants on credit. On 1 March, Pryce already owes Halden 300 for an invoice dated 20 February.

### 1. Invoice

On 2 March Halden sends Pryce an invoice.

| Invoice 7731, 2 March | $ |
|---|---|
| 25 reams of paper at 6.40 | 160.00 |
| 10 toner cartridges at 48.00 | 480.00 |
| List price | 640.00 |
| Less trade discount 20% | (128.00) |
| **Total due** | **512.00** |
| Terms: 2% cash discount if paid within 14 days | |

An **invoice** is issued by the seller to the buyer when goods are sold on credit. It shows both businesses' names, the date, an invoice number, the goods, quantities, unit prices, any trade discount, the total due and the payment terms.

- Halden records 512 in its **sales journal**; Pryce records 512 in its **purchases journal**.
- Trade discount is deducted on the invoice and never recorded separately. Cash discount is only an offer: it is recorded only if payment is made within the terms.

### 2. Debit note

On 5 March Pryce finds two cartridges damaged and returns them. It sends Halden a **debit note** listing the goods returned and asking for credit. The value is the price actually charged: 2 × 48.00 = 96.00, less 20% trade discount = **76.80**.

A debit note may also be sent by a seller to a buyer to charge an extra amount, for example after undercharging on an invoice. Read the question to see which use is meant.

A debit note is a request or notification; the entry in the books is usually made from the credit note that follows (or, for an undercharge, recorded like an extra invoice).

### 3. Credit note

On 7 March Halden accepts the return and issues **credit note CN 214** for 76.80. A **credit note** is sent by the seller to the buyer to reduce the amount owed, because of returned goods or an overcharge. It is often printed in red to distinguish it from an invoice.

- Halden records 76.80 in its **sales returns journal**; Pryce records it in its **purchases returns journal**.

### 4. Cheque counterfoil

On 12 March Pryce pays the 300 owed from February by cheque. When writing the cheque, Pryce's clerk completes the **cheque counterfoil** (the stub that stays in the cheque book) with the date, payee and amount.

- The counterfoil is Pryce's source for the **payments side of the cash book** (bank column), and its evidence of the cheque written.

### 5. Receipt

Halden issues a **receipt** for 300. A receipt is given by the person or business receiving money, as proof of the amount, date and method of payment.

- Pryce keeps it as evidence of payment. For cash sales, the seller's copy of the receipt is a source for the receipts side of the cash book.

### 6. Paying-in slip

Halden pays the cheque, with other cheques and cash, into the bank. Its clerk completes a **paying-in slip**, listing the cash and cheques deposited and the total. The bank stamps the counterfoil as proof.

- The paying-in slip counterfoil is Halden's source for the **receipts side of the cash book** (bank column).

### 7. Statement of account

At the end of March Halden sends Pryce a **statement of account**, summarising the month's transactions and the balance owed.

| Date | Details | Debit $ | Credit $ | Balance $ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Mar | Balance brought forward | | | 300.00 |
| 2 Mar | Invoice 7731 | 512.00 | | 812.00 |
| 7 Mar | Credit note CN 214 | | 76.80 | 735.20 |
| 12 Mar | Cheque received | | 300.00 | 435.20 |
| 31 Mar | Amount due | | | **435.20** |

The statement is not a new transaction, so it is not recorded in a book of prime entry. Pryce **checks** it against Halden's account in its own purchases ledger. If the two do not agree, there is a missing document or an error to investigate. It also reminds Pryce to pay. Pryce did not pay within 14 days of the invoice, so no cash discount applies.

### 8. Bank statement

Halden's bank sends a **bank statement** listing every deposit, payment and charge on its account. It shows the paying-in on 12 March and also bank charges of 15 that Halden had not known about.

- The bank statement is the source for entering items not already in the cash book, such as bank charges, interest, standing orders, direct debits and credit transfers.
- It is also compared with the cash book to find differences, the starting point of a bank reconciliation (section 3.3).

## Summary: who issues each document and where it is recorded

| Document | Issued by | Source for |
|---|---|---|
| Invoice | Seller | Seller: sales journal. Buyer: purchases journal |
| Debit note | Buyer (returns) or seller (undercharge) | Leads to a credit note or an extra charge |
| Credit note | Seller | Seller: sales returns journal. Buyer: purchases returns journal |
| Cheque counterfoil | Payer, from own cheque book | Payer: cash book payments (bank) |
| Paying-in slip | Business depositing money | Cash book receipts (bank) |
| Receipt | Receiver of money | Evidence of payment; cash book for cash sales |
| Statement of account | Seller | Checking against the ledger; no entry |
| Bank statement | Bank | Entering missing items in the cash book; checking |

## Produced and recorded manually or digitally

Every document can exist in either form.

| | Manual | Digital |
|---|---|---|
| Invoices and credit notes | Handwritten or typed, with a carbon or duplicate copy kept | Created by a program and emailed; copy stored automatically |
| Receipts | Paper receipt book with copies | Printed by a till or emailed from a card terminal |
| Cheque counterfoils and paying-in slips | Paper, filled in by hand | Fewer used; online transfers create digital records instead |
| Statements of account | Prepared by hand and posted | Generated from the ledger and emailed |
| Bank statements | Printed and posted | Downloaded through online banking |

**Digital** documents are quick to produce, arithmetic is automatic, copies are easy to store and search, and the data may flow straight into the books. However, they need equipment and security, and an error in the price list or customer details is repeated on every document. **Manual** documents need no equipment and are simple, but they are slower, more prone to arithmetic and copying errors, and take storage space.

## Common mistakes

- Saying a credit note is sent by the buyer. It is sent by the seller.
- Recording trade discount in the accounts. Only the net amount is recorded.
- Treating the statement of account as a source for an entry.
- Valuing returns at list price instead of the price charged after trade discount.
- Confusing a cheque counterfoil (kept by the payer) with a receipt (given by the receiver).

## Exam technique

- **Identify** a document: give its exact name.
- **State** who issues it and to whom.
- **Explain** its use: what information it gives, and which book or check it is used for.
- **Calculate**: invoice totals after trade discount; credit note values at the price charged; statement balances.
