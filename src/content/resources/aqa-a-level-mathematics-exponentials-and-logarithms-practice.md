These questions cover **Section F: Exponentials and logarithms** (F1 to F7) of the AQA A-level
Mathematics (7357) specification, version 1.3 (31 January 2018), for A-level exams from June
2018 onwards. Section F is Paper 1 content, and Papers 2 and 3 can also assess it. A calculator
is required in every 7357 paper, so all questions assume one, but questions marked
**(exact)** need answers in terms of ln, e or logs. Give other answers to 3 significant figures.

Learn the methods first in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-mathematics-exponentials-and-logarithms/)
and review with the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-mathematics-exponentials-and-logarithms-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Write 3 log_a 2 + log_a 5 − ½ log_a 16 as a single logarithm in its simplest form. **[3]**

**2.** Solve 7ˣ = 30. **[2]**

**3.** A curve has equation y = 2 + ln(x − 1), for x > 1.

**(a)** State the equation of the asymptote to the curve. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the exact x-coordinate of the point where the curve crosses the x-axis. **(exact)** **[2]**
**(c)** Find the exact x-coordinate of the point on the curve where y = 4. **(exact)** **[1]**

**4.** Solve log₃(2x + 1) − log₃(x − 1) = 2. **(exact)** **[4]**

**5.** Solve 3^(2x + 1) − 10 × 3ˣ + 3 = 0. **[5]**

**6.** Solve 5^(x + 1) = 3^(2x). Give your answer in the form ln p / ln q, where p and q are
rational, and then as a decimal. **[4]**

**7.** The number of fish in a lake is modelled by P = 500e^(0.04t), where t is the time in years
after the fish were introduced.

**(a)** Show that dP/dt = 0.04P. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the rate of increase of the population when P = 1500. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the time taken for the population to reach 1500. **[2]**

**8.** A scientist believes that two quantities are related by y = axⁿ. She records:

| x | 2 | 5 | 10 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 5.0 | 10.6 | 18.9 | 33.7 |

**(a)** Show that the model gives a straight line when ln y is plotted against ln x, and state
what the gradient and intercept represent. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate ln x and ln y for x = 2 and x = 20, to 3 decimal places. **[2]**
**(c)** Assume the points lie close to a straight line. Use your values from (b) to estimate n
and a. **[3]**
**(d)** Use your model to estimate y when x = 15. **[1]**

**9.** The number of bacteria N in a sample, t hours after the start of an experiment, is
recorded. A graph of log₁₀ N against t is a straight line with intercept 2.3 and gradient 0.12.

**(a)** Show that N = kbᵗ, and find k and b. **[3]**
**(b)** Interpret the value of b in context. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the time at which the model predicts 10 000 bacteria. **[2]**

**10.** After a dose, the concentration of a drug in a patient's blood is modelled by
C = 12e^(−kt) mg/L, where t is in hours. The concentration halves every 4 hours.

**(a)** Show that k = ¼ ln 2. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the time after the dose at which the concentration falls to 1.5 mg/L. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the rate at which the concentration is decreasing when t = 2. **[2]**

**11.** The population of a town was 24 000 in 2010 and 31 000 in 2020. It is modelled by
P = Ae^(kt), where t is the number of years after 2010.

**(a)** Find A and k. **[3]**
**(b)** Use the model to predict the population in 2040. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the year in which the model first predicts a population above 50 000. **[2]**
**(d)** Give one limitation of this model for long-term prediction, and suggest one refinement. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 3 log_a 2 = log_a 8 [1]; ½ log_a 16 = log_a 4 [1]; log_a (8 × 5 / 4) = **log_a 10** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* the power law must be applied to each coefficient before combining; a final answer left as log_a (40/4) usually loses the last mark for "simplest form".

**2.** x ln 7 = ln 30, so x = ln 30 / ln 7 [1] = **1.75** (3 s.f.) [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* a bare 1.75 from a calculator's solve function risks the method mark; show the logarithm step.

**3. (a)** **x = 1** [1]
**(b)** 2 + ln(x − 1) = 0, so ln(x − 1) = −2 [1]; x = **1 + e⁻²** [1]
**(c)** ln(x − 1) = 2, so x = **1 + e²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "exact" means 1 + e⁻², not 1.135; a decimal here gets the method mark but not the accuracy mark.

**4.** log₃ [(2x + 1)/(x − 1)] = 2 [1]; (2x + 1)/(x − 1) = 3² = 9 [1]; 2x + 1 = 9x − 9, so x = **10/7** [1]; check: 10/7 > 1, so both log arguments are positive and the root is valid [1]
*Examiner insight:* removing the logs as if 2 = log₃ 2 (writing (2x + 1)/(x − 1) = 2) is a common slip and loses every mark after the first.

**5.** 3^(2x + 1) = 3 × (3ˣ)², so with u = 3ˣ [1]: 3u² − 10u + 3 = 0 [1]; (3u − 1)(u − 3) = 0, u = 1/3 or u = 3 [1]; 3ˣ = 1/3 gives **x = −1** [1]; 3ˣ = 3 gives **x = 1** [1]
*Examiner insight:* writing 3^(2x + 1) as (3ˣ)² + 3 or 9ˣ alone is the usual error; the factor 3 from 3¹ must stay.

**6.** (x + 1) ln 5 = 2x ln 3 [1]; x ln 5 − 2x ln 3 = −ln 5, so x(2 ln 3 − ln 5) = ln 5 [1]; x = ln 5 / (ln 9 − ln 5) = **ln 5 / ln(9/5)** [1] = **2.74** (3 s.f.) [1]
*Examiner insight:* brackets matter: writing x + 1 ln 5 instead of (x + 1) ln 5 changes the equation, so the working that follows can lose its method marks.

**7. (a)** dP/dt = 500 × 0.04e^(0.04t) [1] = 0.04 × 500e^(0.04t) = 0.04P [1]
**(b)** dP/dt = 0.04 × 1500 = **60 fish per year** [1]
**(c)** e^(0.04t) = 3 [1]; t = ln 3 / 0.04 = **27.5 years** (3 s.f.) [1]
*Examiner insight:* in a "show that" the final line must read dP/dt = 0.04P explicitly; stopping at 20e^(0.04t) does not earn the second mark.

**8. (a)** ln y = ln a + ln xⁿ = ln a + n ln x [1]; this is Y = c + mX with gradient n and intercept ln a [1]
**(b)** x = 2: ln x = 0.693, ln y = 1.609 [1]; x = 20: ln x = 2.996, ln y = 3.517 [1]
**(c)** n = (3.517 − 1.609)/(2.996 − 0.693) [1] = **0.828** [1]; ln a = 1.609 − 0.828 × 0.693 = 1.035, a = e^1.035 = **2.82** [1]
**(d)** y = 2.82 × 15^0.828 = **26.5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* the intercept is ln a, so quoting a = 1.035 loses the accuracy mark; follow-through usually applies to (d) from your own a and n.

**9. (a)** log₁₀ N = 2.3 + 0.12t, so N = 10^2.3 × (10^0.12)ᵗ [1]; k = 10^2.3 = **200** (3 s.f.) [1]; b = 10^0.12 = **1.32** (3 s.f.) [1]
**(b)** The number of bacteria is multiplied by about 1.32 each hour, an increase of about 32% per hour [1]
**(c)** log₁₀ 10 000 = 4, so 4 = 2.3 + 0.12t [1]; t = **14.2 hours** (3 s.f.) [1]
*Examiner insight:* interpreting b needs the context and the time unit ("per hour"); "b is the growth rate" alone is too vague for the mark.

**10. (a)** 12e^(−4k) = 6 [1]; e^(−4k) = ½, so −4k = ln ½ = −ln 2, k = ¼ ln 2 [1]
**(b)** 12e^(−kt) = 1.5, so e^(−kt) = 1/8 [1]; t = ln 8 / k [1] = 3 ln 2 / (¼ ln 2) = **12 hours** [1]
**(c)** dC/dt = −12k e^(−kt), so at t = 2, dC/dt = −12 × ¼ ln 2 × e^(−½ ln 2) [1]; decreasing at **1.47 mg/L per hour** (3 s.f.) [1]
*Examiner insight:* in (b), using the rounded k = 0.173 still gives 12.0, but giving 12.0 without showing ln 8 / k risks the method mark.

**11. (a)** At t = 0, P = A, so **A = 24 000** [1]; 31 000 = 24 000e^(10k) [1]; k = (1/10) ln(31/24) = **0.0256** (3 s.f.) [1]
**(b)** t = 30 [1]; P = 24 000e^(30k) = **51 700** (3 s.f.) [1]
**(c)** e^(kt) = 50 000/24 000, t = ln(25/12)/k = 28.7 [1]; so **2038** [1]
**(d)** Limitation: the model grows without limit, but housing, land and services limit a town's size, so long-term predictions are likely to be too high [1]. Refinement: use a model whose growth rate falls so the population levels off at a maximum, or refit k using newer census data [1]
*Examiner insight:* in (c), t = 28.7 means 28.7 years after 2010, which is during 2038; answering 2039 or 28.7 alone loses the final mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating log(a + b) as log a + log b when combining logs.
- Not checking that every log argument is positive after solving, so an invalid root is kept.
- In hidden quadratics, losing a coefficient (3^(2x + 1) is 3 × 9ˣ, not 9ˣ) or keeping a negative u.
- Missing brackets when bringing a power down: (x + 1) ln 5, not x + 1 ln 5.
- Giving decimals where "exact" is asked, or exact forms with no decimal where 3 s.f. is asked.
- Quoting the intercept of a log graph as the parameter itself (ln a instead of a).
- Using rounded intermediate values so early that the third significant figure changes.
- Answering a "which year" question with a number of years, or the wrong year.
- Stating a generic limitation ("models are not perfect") with no link to the context.
