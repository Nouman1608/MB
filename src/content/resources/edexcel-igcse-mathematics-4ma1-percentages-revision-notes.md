These notes condense **section 1.6, Percentages**, of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2. Statements 1.6 A–G are for both tiers; repeated percentage change and solving compound interest problems are **Higher tier only**. For explanations and worked examples, use the [section 1.6 study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-percentages/).

## Condensed recall: both tiers

**Conversions (1.6 A, C).** p% = p/100. 12.5% = 0.125 = 1/8; 4% = 0.04 = 1/25; 150% = 1.5.

**A as a percentage of B (1.6 B).** (A ÷ B) × 100, same units first. 36 cm of 2.4 m: 36/240 × 100 = 15%.

**Percentage of an amount (1.6 D).** Multiply by the decimal: 17% of 450 = 0.17 × 450 = 76.5.

**Multipliers (1.6 D, E).**

- Increase by p%: × (1 + p/100). +6% → × 1.06.
- Decrease by p%: × (1 − p/100). −6% → × 0.94.

**Percentage change (1.6 E).** (change ÷ original) × 100. Also used for profit, loss, growth and error.

**Reverse percentage (1.6 F).** original = new value ÷ multiplier. After +20%, £84 → 84 ÷ 1.2 = £70.

**Compound interest and depreciation (1.6 G).** value = original × multiplierⁿ.

| Situation | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Compound interest at r% | P × (1 + r/100)ⁿ | £500 at 4% for 3 years: 500 × 1.04³ = £562.43 |
| Depreciation at r% | P × (1 − r/100)ⁿ | £9000 at 15% for 2 years: 9000 × 0.85² = £6502.50 |
| Simple interest at r% | P + n × (r/100) × P | £500 at 4% for 3 years: 500 + 3 × 20 = £560 |

## Condensed recall: Higher tier only

- **Repeated change:** multiply the multipliers. +25% then −20%: 1.25 × 0.8 = 1, no overall change.
- **Overall percentage:** (product − 1) × 100. 0.9 × 0.9 = 0.81 → 19% decrease.
- **Find the original:** final ÷ multiplierⁿ.
- **Find the rate:** multiplier = ⁿ√(final ÷ original); rate = (multiplier − 1) × 100.
- **Find the number of years:** try n = 1, 2, 3, … and show the values either side of the target.

## Worked recall

**Choosing the method from the wording.**

- "Find 15% of …" → multiply by 0.15.
- "Increase/decrease … by 15%" → multiply by 1.15 or 0.85.
- "… after a 15% increase. Find the original" → divide by 1.15.
- "Find the percentage change" → change ÷ original × 100.
- "… each year for n years" → multiply by the multiplier to the power n.

**Multiplier from a percentage change.** If a value goes from 250 to 285, the multiplier is 285 ÷ 250 = 1.14, so the increase is 14%.

**Percentage profit from a sale price.** Bought for £80, sold for £68: loss = £12, and 12 ÷ 80 × 100 = 15% loss.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Per cent | Out of 100 |
| Multiplier | The decimal that a value is multiplied by to apply a percentage change |
| Percentage increase / decrease | The change as a percentage of the original value |
| Reverse percentage | Finding the original value from the value after a change |
| Simple interest | Interest calculated on the original amount only, the same each year |
| Compound interest | Interest added to the balance, so later interest is on a larger amount |
| Depreciation | A fall in value, usually by a fixed percentage each year |
| Principal | The amount first invested or borrowed |
| Per annum | Per year |

## Exam traps

- **Original, not new,** goes on the bottom in percentage change.
- **Reverse percentages divide.** For "after a 15% discount the price is £68", do 68 ÷ 0.85 = £80, not 68 × 1.15.
- **Depreciation multiplier.** 22% off leaves 78%, so × 0.78.
- **Interest vs value.** "How much interest" means final value − principal.
- **Compound vs simple.** Read the word; they give different answers from year 2 onwards.
- **Changes do not add.** −10% then −10% is −19%.
- **Money.** Give 2 decimal places: £6502.5 must be written £6502.50.

## Self-test

1. Write 0.4% as a decimal and as a fraction.
2. Write 27 as a percentage of 60.
3. Find 13.5% of 840.
4. Write down the multiplier for a decrease of 3.5%.
5. A bill of £64 rises by 7.5%. Find the new bill.
6. A town's population rises from 12 500 to 13 250. Find the percentage increase.
7. After a 12% pay rise, Ola earns £30 240. Find her pay before the rise.
8. Find the value of £3000 after 4 years at 2% per year compound interest.
9. A machine worth £24 000 depreciates by 20% per year. Find its value after 3 years.
10. (Higher) Find the overall percentage change when a price rises by 30% and then falls by 30%.
11. (Higher) An amount grows by 10% per year. After 2 years it is £2420. Find the amount at the start.
12. (Higher) £4000 grows to £4410 in 2 years at a fixed compound rate. Find the rate.

**Answers**

1. 0.004 and 4/1000 = 1/250.
2. 27/60 × 100 = 45%.
3. 0.135 × 840 = 113.4.
4. 0.965.
5. 64 × 1.075 = £68.80.
6. 750/12 500 × 100 = 6%.
7. 30 240 ÷ 1.12 = £27 000.
8. 3000 × 1.02⁴ = £3247.30 (to the nearest penny).
9. 24 000 × 0.8³ = £12 288.
10. 1.3 × 0.7 = 0.91, a 9% decrease.
11. 2420 ÷ 1.1² = £2000.
12. 4410 ÷ 4000 = 1.1025; √1.1025 = 1.05, so 5% per year.
