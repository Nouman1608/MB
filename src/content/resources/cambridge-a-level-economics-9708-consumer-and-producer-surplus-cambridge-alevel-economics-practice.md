## Section A

**1.** On a demand and supply diagram, consumer surplus is the area

A: below the supply curve and above the price

B: above the demand curve and below the price

C: below the demand curve and above the price

D: above the supply curve and below the price

**[1]**

**2.** Supply of a good is perfectly elastic. What is the producer surplus?

A: Zero

B: Equal to consumer surplus

C: Equal to total revenue

D: It cannot be calculated

**[1]**

**3.** Define producer surplus. **[2]**

**4.** A consumer is willing to pay 48 cu for a jacket and buys it in a sale for 35 cu. Calculate her consumer surplus and explain what it measures. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why consumer surplus can rise even when the price of a good rises. **[2]**

**6.** Explain one reason why consumer surplus may be a poor measure of the benefit different households receive. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Six buyers and six sellers trade one bicycle each in an invented market. The price is 20 cu.

| Buyer | Most willing to pay (cu) | Seller | Least willing to accept (cu) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 30 | P | 8 |
| B | 25 | Q | 12 |
| C | 22 | R | 16 |
| D | 18 | S | 21 |
| E | 15 | T | 23 |
| F | 11 | U | 26 |

**(a)** State how many bicycles are traded and explain why. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate total consumer surplus and total producer surplus. **[2]**

**8.** In an invented market for melons, Qd = 150 - 5P and Qs = 10P - 30, with P in cu and Q in hundreds a week.

**(a)** Calculate the equilibrium price and quantity. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate consumer surplus and producer surplus at equilibrium. **[2]**

**(c)** A heatwave raises demand to Qd = 180 - 5P. Calculate the new consumer surplus and producer surplus. **[3]**

**(d)** Starting again from the original market, a pest raises costs so that supply becomes Qs = 10P - 60. Calculate the new consumer surplus and producer surplus and the loss of total surplus. **[3]**

**9.** Supply of a service is perfectly elastic at 6 cu. Demand is Qd = 90 - 5P and then rises to Qd = 120 - 5P.

**(a)** Calculate consumer surplus before and after the rise in demand. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why producer surplus does not change. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why the price elasticity of supply determines how a rise in demand affects producer surplus. **[4]**

**11.** A new machine lowers the cost of producing a good with very price-inelastic demand. Explain how consumer surplus and producer surplus are likely to change. **[4]**

**12.** Explain why total surplus is greatest at the competitive equilibrium quantity. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1].

**2.** **A** [1]. Every unit is sold at exactly the minimum price producers will accept.

**3.** The difference between the **price producers receive** [1] and the **minimum price they would be willing to accept**, summed over all units sold [1].

**4.** 48 - 35 = **13 cu** [1]. It measures the **extra benefit** she gains above the amount she pays [1].

**5.** If the price rise is caused by an **increase in demand** [1], consumers now **value the good more**, so their willingness to pay rises by more than the price and the gap can widen [1].

**6.** Willingness to pay depends on **income** [1], so a poorer household with a strong need may show less surplus than a rich household with a weaker need [1].

**7. (a)** **Three** bicycles [1]. Buyers A, B and C value a bicycle above 20 cu and sellers P, Q and R accept less than 20 cu; buyer D (18) and seller S (21) do not trade, so quantity demanded equals quantity supplied at three [1].

**(b)** Consumer surplus = (30 - 20) + (25 - 20) + (22 - 20) = **17 cu** [1]. Producer surplus = (20 - 8) + (20 - 12) + (20 - 16) = **24 cu** [1].

**8. (a)** 150 - 5P = 10P - 30, so 180 = 15P and **P = 12 cu** [1]; **Q = 90 hundred** [1].

**(b)** Demand meets the price axis at 30 cu and supply at 3 cu. Consumer surplus = 1/2 x (30 - 12) x 90 = **810** [1]. Producer surplus = 1/2 x (12 - 3) x 90 = **405** [1] (hundred cu).

**(c)** 180 - 5P = 10P - 30, so P = 14 and Q = 110 [1]. Consumer surplus = 1/2 x (36 - 14) x 110 = **1,210** [1]. Producer surplus = 1/2 x (14 - 3) x 110 = **605** [1].

**(d)** 150 - 5P = 10P - 60, so P = 14 and Q = 80; supply now meets the price axis at 6 cu. Consumer surplus = 1/2 x (30 - 14) x 80 = **640** [1]. Producer surplus = 1/2 x (14 - 6) x 80 = **320** [1]. Total surplus falls from 1,215 to 960, a loss of **255** [1].

**9. (a)** Before: Q = 90 - 30 = 60; consumer surplus = 1/2 x (18 - 6) x 60 = **360 cu** [1]. After: Q = 120 - 30 = 90; consumer surplus = 1/2 x (24 - 6) x 90 = **810 cu** [1].

**(b)** Price stays at 6 cu, which is the **minimum price producers will accept** for every unit [1], so there is no gap between price received and minimum price on any unit and **producer surplus remains zero** [1].

**10.** A rise in demand creates excess demand at the old price [1]. If **supply is inelastic**, output cannot rise much, so the **price rises a lot**, and producers gain a large increase in surplus on existing units [1]. If **supply is elastic**, output rises easily and the **price rises only a little** [1], so the gain to producers is smaller and more of the extra surplus goes to consumers [1].

**11.** Lower costs **shift supply to the right** [1]. With very inelastic demand, the new equilibrium has a **much lower price** and only a small rise in quantity [1]. **Consumer surplus rises substantially**, because buyers pay much less on almost the same number of units [1]. **Producer surplus rises only a little**: costs are lower, but the price falls by almost as much, and few extra units are sold [1].

**12.** For every unit up to the equilibrium quantity, the **maximum a buyer will pay exceeds the minimum a seller will accept** [1], so trading it adds to total surplus [1]. Beyond equilibrium, the minimum supply price exceeds willingness to pay, so extra units would reduce total surplus; at equilibrium all gains from trade are taken [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the old intercept after a shift in Question 8(c) and Question 8(d).
- Forgetting that the supply-side intercept changes when supply shifts.
- Including buyer D or seller S in Question 7.
- Stating "elasticity matters" in Question 10 without naming which elasticity.
