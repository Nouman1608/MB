These notes condense sub-topic 2(b) Group 7 (halogens) – chlorine, bromine and iodine (points 2.5 to 2.8C) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024). Points 2.5 to 2.7 can appear on Paper 1 and Paper 2; point 2.8C is **Paper 2 only**. Model answers and worked examples are in the [Group 7 study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-group-7-halogens/); test yourself with the [Group 7 practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-group-7-halogens-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Halogen | A Group 7 element; exists as diatomic molecules (X₂) |
| Halide | A compound containing a halide ion (Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻), such as potassium bromide |
| Displacement reaction | A more reactive halogen takes the place of a less reactive halogen in a compound |
| Spectator ion | An ion that is unchanged in a reaction, such as K⁺ in a displacement |
| Diatomic | Made of molecules containing two atoms |

## Colours and states (2.5)

| | Chlorine | Bromine | Iodine |
|---|---|---|---|
| State at room temperature | Gas | Liquid | Solid |
| Colour of element | Pale yellow-green | Red-brown (orange-brown vapour) | Grey-black (purple vapour) |
| Colour in water | Very pale green | Orange / yellow | Brown |

Trends down the group: colour darker; melting and boiling points higher; gas → liquid → solid; reactivity lower.

Reason for the physical trend: larger Mr → stronger intermolecular forces between X₂ molecules → more energy needed to overcome them.

## Predicting (2.6)

| | Fluorine (above chlorine) | Astatine (below iodine) |
|---|---|---|
| State | Gas | Solid |
| Colour | Paler than chlorine (pale yellow) | Darker than iodine (black) |
| Reactivity | More reactive than chlorine | Less reactive than iodine |
| Formula | F₂ | At₂ |

## Displacement grid (2.7)

| Added → | to KCl | to KBr | to KI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chlorine water | — | Orange (Br₂ formed) | Brown (I₂ formed) |
| Bromine water | No reaction | — | Brown (I₂ formed) |
| Iodine solution | No reaction | No reaction | — |

Conclusion: chlorine > bromine > iodine.

### Equations

```
Cl2 + 2KBr -> 2KCl + Br2        ionic: Cl2 + 2Br- -> 2Cl- + Br2
Cl2 + 2KI  -> 2KCl + I2         ionic: Cl2 + 2I-  -> 2Cl- + I2
Br2 + 2KI  -> 2KBr + I2         ionic: Br2 + 2I-  -> 2Br- + I2
```

The halogen gains electrons (is reduced); the halide ion loses electrons (is oxidised).

### Identifying an unknown halide

```
chlorine water: no change               -> chloride
chlorine water: orange; bromine: none   -> bromide
chlorine water: brown; bromine: brown   -> iodide
```

## Explaining the trend (2.8C) — Paper 2 only

```
Cl 2,8,7: reacts by gaining 1 electron -> Cl- (2,8,8)
down the group:
  more occupied shells -> outer shell further from the nucleus
  more shielding by inner electrons
  weaker attraction for an incoming electron
  electron gained less easily -> less reactive
```

Contrast: Group 1 metals lose an electron, so the same changes make them **more** reactive down the group.

## Practical notes for displacement

- Use a few cm³ of each halide solution in separate test tubes and add the halogen solution a few drops at a time.
- Keep an untouched tube of each solution for comparison, so a faint colour change is not missed.
- The halide solutions start colourless; bromine water and iodine solution are themselves coloured, so look for a **change** from the reagent's colour.
- Work in a fume cupboard: chlorine and bromine are toxic.

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Halogen vs halide | Element (Cl₂) vs ion in a compound (Cl⁻ in KCl) |
| Chlorine vs chloride | Reacts as X₂ vs the product ion X⁻ |
| Element colour vs solution colour | Bromine red-brown liquid vs orange solution |
| Group 1 trend vs Group 7 trend | Reactivity increases down vs decreases down |

## Answer frames

- **Displacement observation:** "The colourless solution turns [orange/brown] because [bromine/iodine] is formed."
- **Evidence for order:** "Chlorine displaces bromine from bromide solution, so chlorine is more reactive than bromine."
- **No reaction:** "Iodine is less reactive than bromine, so it cannot displace bromine from bromide."

## Exam traps

- Solid iodine is grey-black, not purple.
- Bromine water is orange; the element is red-brown.
- "Br" or "I" in equations instead of Br₂ and I₂.
- Missing the "2" in 2KBr / 2Br⁻.
- Claiming iodine displaces chlorine.
- Explaining boiling points by covalent bonds breaking.
- In the 2.8C explanation, saying "more protons, so more attraction".

## Self-test

1. Give the colour and state of bromine at room temperature.
2. Describe the colour of iodine vapour.
3. Explain why iodine has a higher melting point than chlorine.
4. Predict the state of astatine at room temperature.
5. State the colour seen when chlorine water is added to potassium iodide solution.
6. Write the balanced equation for chlorine reacting with potassium bromide.
7. Write the ionic equation for bromine reacting with iodide ions.
8. Explain why there is no reaction when iodine solution is added to potassium chloride solution.
9. Name the spectator ion when chlorine reacts with sodium bromide solution.
10. Bromine water is added to an unknown halide solution and it turns brown. Identify the halide ion.
11. Predict whether fluorine would displace chlorine from potassium chloride. Give a reason.
12. (Paper 2 only) Explain why iodine is less reactive than bromine.

### Answers

1. Red-brown liquid.
2. Purple.
3. Iodine has a larger Mr, so the intermolecular forces between I₂ molecules are stronger and need more energy to overcome.
4. Solid.
5. Brown (iodine formed).
6. Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂.
7. Br₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Br⁻ + I₂.
8. Iodine is less reactive than chlorine, so it cannot displace it.
9. Na⁺.
10. Iodide, I⁻.
11. Yes: fluorine is above chlorine, so it is more reactive.
12. An iodine atom has more occupied shells, so its outer shell is further from the nucleus and more shielded; the attraction for an extra electron is weaker, so it gains an electron less easily.
