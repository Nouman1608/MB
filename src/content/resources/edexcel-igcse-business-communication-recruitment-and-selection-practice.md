## Questions

**1.** Give two communication methods that use information technology (IT) and state one limitation of one of them. **[3]**

**2.** Explain how jargon can act as a barrier to communication and how this barrier can be removed. **[4]**

**3.** Lakehurst Tours prints 40 copies of a weekly staff memo at 0.12 per copy. It is considering sending the memo by email instead, using a messaging service costing 18 a month. Assume 52 weeks and 12 months in a year.

**(a)** Calculate the yearly cost of printing the memo. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the yearly saving from using email. **[1]**
**(c)** Evaluate whether Lakehurst Tours should replace printed memos with email. **[6]**

**4.** State the type of employment in each case (full-time, part-time, job share or casual/seasonal/temporary).

**(a)** A café hires a student for six weeks over the summer. **[1]**
**(b)** Two nurses share one post, one working Monday to Wednesday and the other Thursday to Friday. **[1]**
**(c)** An assistant works 12 hours a week. **[1]**
**(d)** A manager works the business's standard full working week on a permanent contract. **[1]**

**5.** Seaview Ice Cream hires 5 seasonal workers for 8 weeks. Each works 24 hours a week at 9.00 an hour.

**(a)** Calculate the total wage cost. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one benefit of using seasonal workers rather than permanent staff. **[3]**

**6.** Brindle Logistics is recruiting a warehouse supervisor.

**(a)** Explain how a job description differs from a person specification. **[4]**
**(b)** Outline one advantage to Brindle of asking applicants to complete an application form rather than send a curriculum vitae (CV). **[2]**

**7.** Redfield Library has a full-time post of 36 hours a week at 10.50 an hour. It is offered as a job share between Tam (20 hours) and Uri (16 hours).

**(a)** Calculate the library's weekly wage cost for the post. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse one benefit and one limitation of job share for the library. **[6]**

**8.** Pellam Engineering needs a new supervisor on a salary of 30,000. External recruitment would cost an advertisement of 450 plus an agency fee of 10% of the salary. Promoting a current employee would cost 1,100 in training for the promoted person. Both candidates, one internal and one external, have similar qualifications.

**(a)** Calculate the cost of external recruitment. **[3]**
**(b)** Justify whether Pellam should recruit internally or externally. **[8]**

**9.** A shop receives 80 applications for a sales post and shortlists 10% of them. Each interview lasts 40 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the number of interviews and the total interview time in hours, to 2 decimal places. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain two ways the shop can make shortlisting and interviewing fairer. **[4]**

**10.** An advertisement from Corran Motors says: "Young, energetic man wanted as sales assistant."

**(a)** State two of the grounds named in the specification on which the advertisement may treat people less favourably. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse the effects of equal opportunities laws on how Corran Motors recruits staff. **[6]**

**11.** Marrow Café employs 4 part-time workers, each for 18 hours a week at 7.90 an hour. A new minimum wage law sets 8.50 an hour. Assume 52 weeks a year.

**(a)** Calculate the extra weekly wage cost. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the extra yearly cost. **[1]**
**(c)** Evaluate the likely effects of the law on Marrow Café. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two of email, text/instant messaging, video conferencing, intranet, social media [2]. Limitation, such as email overload or video conferencing needing a reliable connection [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the method and attach a limitation that belongs to that method; a limitation that could apply to any method is weaker.

**2.** Jargon is technical language [1] that the receiver does not know, so the message is misunderstood [1]. It can be removed by using plain language or explaining terms [1] and checking understanding by asking the receiver to repeat the instruction [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A full answer states how the barrier arises and how it is removed; naming the barrier alone does not answer both halves.

**3. (a)** 40 × 0.12 = 4.80 per week [1]; 4.80 × 52 = **249.60** [1].
**(b)** Email 18 × 12 = 216; saving 249.60 − 216 = **33.60** a year [1].
**(c)** Email saves 33.60, a small amount [1]; it is quicker and leaves a record [1]; but staff without regular computer access may miss it [1], and the memo may be skimmed or lost in an inbox [1]. Judgement: email is worth adopting if all staff have access, since it is cheaper and faster [1], perhaps keeping a notice board copy for those without [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* For "evaluate", use the figures from the stem and reach a conclusion with a reason; the saving alone is not enough.

**4. (a)** Casual/seasonal/temporary [1]. **(b)** Job share [1]. **(c)** Part-time [1]. **(d)** Full-time [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the specification's names for the types; "short contract" or "half job" are less precise than the named type.

**5. (a)** Hours each: 24 × 8 = 192 [1]. Workers: 5 × 192 = 960 hours [1]. Cost: 960 × 9.00 = **8,640** [1].
**(b)** Seaview pays only while the summer peak lasts [1], because workers are hired for set weeks [1], so it avoids paying permanent wages in the quiet months [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An explain answer works best as a fact, a reason and a consequence in that order, each building on the last.

**6. (a)** A job description sets out the job: title, duties and responsibilities [1], hours and who the post reports to [1]. A person specification sets out the person wanted: qualifications, skills and experience [1], and personal qualities, often essential and desirable [1]. **(b)** Every applicant answers the same questions [1], so Brindle can compare them directly and fairly [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (a) needs both documents described; writing only about the job description will leave half the marks.

**7. (a)** Total hours 20 + 16 = 36, so the job is covered [1]. Cost 36 × 10.50 = **378** [1].
**(b)** Benefit: the post is covered when one person is away [1], so the library keeps its opening hours without hiring a temporary replacement [1], and it keeps two sets of skills [1]. Limitation: handover between Tam and Uri must be good [1], otherwise tasks are missed or repeated [1], and there is extra admin for two people [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Analyse answers should be set in the library context and develop each point; a bare list of benefits does not analyse.

**8. (a)** Agency fee 0.10 × 30,000 = 3,000 [1]. Add advertisement 450 [1]. Total **3,450** [1].
**(b)** External costs 3,450 against 1,100 for internal, so internal is 2,350 cheaper [1]. Internal is also quicker and the person knows the firm [1], and the promotion may motivate other staff [1]. But internal recruitment creates another vacancy to fill [1], and gives no new ideas [1]. External offers a wider choice and fresh skills [1]. As the candidates have similar qualifications, the extra 2,350 for external buys little [1]. Decision: recruit internally, unless the internal candidate proves weak at interview [1]. **[11]**
*Examiner insight:* A justify answer picks one option and uses the data in the case; here that is the cost gap and the similar qualifications.

**9. (a)** 80 × 0.10 = **8 interviews** [1]. 8 × 40 = 320 minutes [1]. 320 ÷ 60 = **5.33 hours** [1].
**(b)** Compare every application with the same person specification [1], so the choice rests on the job needs [1]. Ask all candidates the same core questions and score the answers [1], so candidates are compared consistently [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the time as the unit asked for and show the conversion step; a rounded hours figure with no working is risky.

**10. (a)** Age [1] and gender [1]. **(b)** The advertisement must describe the real job requirements instead of a preferred age or gender [1], so Corran draws on a wider pool of applicants [1]. Candidates must be chosen for their ability to do the work [1], using the same questions and written records for each candidate [1]. Failing to comply risks legal action and bad publicity [1], while compliance can improve the firm's reputation [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Tie each effect back to a recruitment stage (advert, shortlisting, interview) so the answer stays on Corran's process and not on discrimination in general.

**11. (a)** Current weekly pay 4 × 18 × 7.90 = 568.80 [1]. New weekly pay 4 × 18 × 8.50 = 612 [1]. Extra **43.20** [1].
**(b)** 43.20 × 52 = **2,246.40** [1].
**(c)** Costs rise by 2,246.40 a year [1], so profit falls unless prices rise [1]. Raising prices may lose price-sensitive customers [1]. Workers receive more pay [1], which may raise motivation and cut turnover [1]. The firm may cut hours to save cost [1], but that may weaken service [1]. Judgement: the rise is modest relative to the total wage bill of 568.80 a week, so the café can probably absorb part of it and benefit from staff retention [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluate needs a weighed conclusion; stating the law raises costs and stopping there reads as description.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a method or barrier with no limitation, cause or removal.
- Mixing up the job description and the person specification.
- Calling a job share "part-time" without explaining it is one job split between people.
- Forgetting to multiply by the number of workers or weeks.
- Giving a final cost without units or leaving out one cost (such as the advertisement).
- Answering "justify" or "evaluate" with advantages only.
- Writing generally about discrimination instead of effects on the business's recruitment.
- Ignoring figures given in the case study.
