## What the syllabus asks

1. Purposes of purchases ledger and sales ledger control accounts.
2. Books of prime entry as sources for control account entries.
3. Preparing both accounts with: credit purchases and sales, receipts and
payments, cash discounts, returns, irrecoverable debts, dishonoured cheques,
interest on overdue accounts, contra entries, refunds, and opening and
closing balances (debit and credit in each account).
4. The impact of digital recording on preparing and using control accounts.

Not required: reconciling control account balances with the ledger
balances.

## Core idea in one line

A control account is a **summary account built from totals**, whose balance
should equal the total of the individual accounts in its ledger.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sales ledger | Ledger of individual credit customers' accounts |
| Purchases ledger | Ledger of individual credit suppliers' accounts |
| Sales ledger control account | Summary of all sales ledger accounts; balance = total trade receivables |
| Purchases ledger control account | Summary of all purchases ledger accounts; balance = total trade payables |
| Contra entry | Setting off an amount owed by a customer against an amount owed to the same business as a supplier |
| Dishonoured cheque | Cheque received that the payer's bank refuses to pay; the debt is reinstated |
| Interest on overdue accounts | Charge added to an account that is paid late |
| Refund | Payment that clears a balance on the "wrong" side, such as repaying a customer who overpaid |

## Purposes

- Check the arithmetical accuracy of a ledger.
- Show which ledger contains an error.
- Give total trade receivables and payables quickly.
- Help to prevent and detect fraud when kept by a different person.
- Help managers monitor credit given and received.

## Sales ledger control account

| Debit | Credit |
|---|---|
| Balance b/d (debit balances) | Balance b/d (credit balances) |
| Credit sales (sales journal) | Sales returns (sales returns journal) |
| Dishonoured cheques (cash book) | Receipts from customers (cash book) |
| Interest on overdue accounts (general journal) | Cash discounts allowed (cash book) |
| Refunds to customers (cash book) | Irrecoverable debts (general journal) |
| Balance c/d (credit balances) | Contra with purchases ledger (general journal) |
| | Balance c/d (debit balances) |

## Purchases ledger control account

| Debit | Credit |
|---|---|
| Balance b/d (debit balances) | Balance b/d (credit balances) |
| Purchases returns (purchases returns journal) | Credit purchases (purchases journal) |
| Payments to suppliers (cash book) | Refunds from suppliers (cash book) |
| Cash discounts received (cash book) | Interest charged by suppliers (general journal) |
| Contra with sales ledger (general journal) | Balance c/d (debit balances) |
| Balance c/d (credit balances) | |

## Method

1. Enter the opening debit and credit balances separately.
2. Post each total to the side matching the individual accounts.
3. Enter the closing balance that is given (usually the smaller one).
4. Find the other closing balance as the balancing figure.
5. Total, and bring both balances down on the first day of the next period.

## Never in a control account

- Cash sales and cash purchases.
- Trade discounts (deducted on the invoice before it is recorded).
- The allowance for irrecoverable debts.
- Irrecoverable debts in the purchases ledger control account.

## Digital recording: effects

- Software posts once and updates the individual account and the control
total together, producing the control account automatically.
- Arithmetical errors become rare; the arithmetic-check purpose matters less.
- Input errors (wrong customer, wrong amount) are not revealed, because both
records change together.
- Up-to-date totals and overdue lists support credit control.
- Passwords, permissions and audit trails help prevent fraud.

## Exam traps

- A dishonoured cheque is a **debit** in sales ledger control.
- The contra is a **credit** in sales ledger control and a **debit** in
purchases ledger control.
- A refund to a customer is a debit; a refund from a supplier is a credit.
- Do not net opening debit and credit balances.
- Name sources precisely: "general journal", "sales returns journal".

## Quick self-test

1. State **two** purposes of a sales ledger control account.
2. Name the source of the figure for credit sales.
3. Name the source of the figure for irrecoverable debts written off.
4. On which side of the sales ledger control account is a dishonoured cheque
entered?
5. On which side of the purchases ledger control account is discount
received entered?
6. Explain what a contra entry is.
7. Give the double entry for a contra of $350.
8. Why do cash sales not appear in the sales ledger control account?
9. A sales ledger control account has total debits of $64,200 and credits,
excluding the closing debit balance, of $51,850. What is the closing debit
balance?
10. How can a purchases ledger contain a debit balance?
11. State **one** limitation of control accounts kept by accounting software.
12. Is the allowance for irrecoverable debts entered in the sales ledger
control account?

### Answers

1. Any two: checks the arithmetical accuracy of the sales ledger; locates
errors to that ledger; gives total trade receivables quickly; helps detect
fraud; supports credit control.
2. Sales journal.
3. General journal.
4. Debit.
5. Debit.
6. Setting off the amount a customer owes the business against the amount
the business owes the same person as a supplier.
7. Dr Purchases ledger control $350, Cr Sales ledger control $350.
8. They are paid immediately, so no customer account is debited.
9. $64,200 − $51,850 = $12,350.
10. The business has overpaid a supplier, or returned goods after paying.
11. An input error, such as a receipt posted to the wrong customer, changes
both records equally and is not revealed.
12. No. It is a separate account deducted from trade receivables in the
statement of financial position.
