## Corrosion and rusting

- **Corrosion**: destruction of materials by chemical reactions with substances in the environment.
- **Rusting**: corrosion of iron. **Both air (oxygen) and water are needed.**
- Experiment: nails in (1) air and water: rusts; (2) boiled water with oil: no air, no rust; (3) dry with calcium
  chloride: no water, no rust.

## Prevention

| Method | How | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Barrier | Keeps out air and water | Grease, paint, electroplating |
| Oxide layer | Protective layer | Aluminium oxide on aluminium |
| Sacrificial | **More reactive** metal corrodes instead | Zinc on iron (galvanising), magnesium blocks |

- Barrier fails if **scratched** (except sacrificial coatings).
- Sacrificial protection: explain with **relative reactivity**: zinc is more reactive than iron, so it loses
  electrons first.
- Tin is **less** reactive than iron, so once scratched the iron rusts faster.

## Alloys

| Alloy | Composition | Key property or use |
|---|---|---|
| Bronze | Copper + tin | Hard; statues, medals |
| Brass | Copper + zinc | Hard; taps, door fittings |
| Gold jewellery | Gold + silver, copper, zinc | Harder than pure gold |
| High carbon steel | Iron + more carbon | Strong but brittle |
| Low carbon steel | Iron + little carbon | Softer, easily shaped |
| Stainless steel | Iron + chromium + nickel | Hard, corrosion resistant |
| Aluminium alloys | Aluminium + others | Low density |

**Carats**: 24 carat = 100% gold. 18 carat = 75%. Percentage = carats ÷ 24 × 100.

Example: 50 g of 18 carat gold contains 0.75 × 50 = **37.5 g** of gold.

## Glass and ceramics

| Material | Made from | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Soda-lime glass | Sand + sodium carbonate + limestone | Ordinary glass |
| Borosilicate glass | Sand + boron trioxide | Melts at **higher** temperature |
| Clay ceramics | Wet clay shaped, then heated in a furnace | Pottery, bricks |

## Polymers

| Type | Behaviour on heating | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| **Thermosoftening** | **Melts** | Separate chains, weak forces |
| **Thermosetting** | **Does not melt** | Chains joined by strong cross-links |

- **LD** and **HD poly(ethene)** are both made from **ethene** but under **different conditions**.
- Polymer properties depend on the **monomers** and the **conditions**.

## Composites

- **Matrix (binder)** surrounding and binding **fibres or fragments** (the **reinforcement**).
- Examples: **fibreglass**, **carbon fibre**, **concrete**, **plywood**.
- Combine useful properties, such as strong and light.

## Selecting materials

| Needed property | Possible material |
|---|---|
| Light and strong | Carbon fibre, aluminium alloy |
| Hard and non-rusting | Stainless steel |
| Heat resistant, brittle | Ceramics |
| Flexible and cheap | Thermosoftening polymer |
| Does not melt in heat | Thermosetting polymer |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Corrosion | Destruction of a material by reaction with the environment |
| Alloy | Mixture of a metal with other elements |
| Sacrificial protection | A more reactive metal corrodes instead |
| Galvanising | Coating iron with zinc |
| Composite | Material with a matrix and reinforcement |
| Thermosetting | Polymer that does not melt when heated |
| Carat | Measure of gold proportion, 24 = pure |

## Exam traps

- **Rusting needs only water.** It needs air and water.
- **Sacrificial = barrier.** Sacrificial uses a more reactive metal.
- **Alloy as compound.** It is a mixture.
- **Thermosetting melts.** It does not.
- **Wrong carat maths.** Percentage = carats ÷ 24 × 100.
- **Properties without use.** Link each property to a use.
- **Composites as alloys.** Composites have matrix plus reinforcement.

## Quick self-test

1. What two things are needed for iron to rust?
2. Give two barrier methods of rust prevention.
3. Explain how zinc protects iron when the coating is scratched.
4. Name the metals in bronze and brass.
5. Why is stainless steel used for cutlery?
6. What percentage of gold is 18 carat gold?
7. Name the raw materials for soda-lime glass.
8. How does borosilicate glass differ from soda-lime glass?
9. How are clay ceramics made?
10. What is the difference between thermosoftening and thermosetting polymers?
11. Name the matrix and reinforcement in fibreglass.
12. A ring has a mass of 8.0 g and is 9 carat gold. Calculate the mass of gold.

### Answers

1. Air (oxygen) and water.
2. Any two: greasing, painting, electroplating.
3. Zinc is more reactive than iron, so it loses electrons and corrodes instead of the iron.
4. Bronze: copper and tin. Brass: copper and zinc.
5. It contains chromium and nickel, so it is hard and resists corrosion.
6. 18 ÷ 24 × 100 = 75%.
7. Sand, sodium carbonate and limestone.
8. It melts at a higher temperature (sand and boron trioxide).
9. By shaping wet clay and heating it in a furnace.
10. Thermosoftening melts on heating; thermosetting does not (strong cross-links between chains).
11. Matrix: polymer resin; reinforcement: glass fibres.
12. 9 ÷ 24 × 8.0 = 3.0 g.

## Worked answers to model

**Explain how galvanising protects iron.** "The zinc coating acts as a barrier. If it is scratched, zinc is more
reactive than iron, so it loses electrons in preference and corrodes instead of the iron."

**Explain why steel is used for a car body and stainless steel for a sink.** "Low carbon steel is soft and easily
shaped. Stainless steel contains chromium and nickel, so it is hard and resists corrosion."

**Explain the difference between thermosoftening and thermosetting polymers.** "Thermosoftening polymers have
separate chains with weak forces between them, so they soften and melt on heating. Thermosetting polymers have
strong cross-links between chains, so they do not melt."

## Composite examples

| Composite | Why it is used |
|---|---|
| Carbon fibre | Strong and light for sports equipment and aircraft |
| Fibreglass | Strong, light, does not rust, for boats |
| Reinforced concrete | Strong in compression and tension for buildings |
| Plywood | Strong across the sheet, does not split easily |
