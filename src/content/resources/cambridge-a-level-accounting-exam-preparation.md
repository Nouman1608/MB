Cambridge International A-Level Accounting (9706) is a staged qualification of four papers across AS
and A Level. Paper 1 (Multiple Choice) is 1 hour, 30 marks, 14% of the full A Level. Paper 2
(Fundamentals of Accounting) is 1 hour 45 minutes, 90 marks, 36% -- the single largest paper. Together
Papers 1 and 2 form the AS Level. Paper 3 (Financial Accounting) is 1 hour 30 minutes, 75 marks, 30%, and
Paper 4 (Cost and Management Accounting) is 1 hour, 50 marks, 20%; both are A Level only. These notes
complement the site's guides to
[Business Entities and the Accounting System](/resources/business-entities-and-the-accounting-system/)
and [Accounting for Non-Current Assets](/resources/accounting-non-current-assets/).

## Paper 2 is worth more than Paper 1 and Paper 4 combined

At 36%, Paper 2 alone outweighs Paper 1 (14%) and Paper 4 (20%) added together, and comes close to
matching Paper 3 (30%). **Exam-preparation priority**: give Fundamentals of Accounting proportionally
the largest share of revision time of any single paper, not an equal quarter-share across all four.

## Paper 1's multiple-choice format needs a different skill than the written papers

Unlike Papers 2-4, which are written and workings-based, Paper 1 tests the same content through
multiple-choice questions with no partial credit for method. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise
timed multiple-choice questions specifically, since the skill of eliminating distractors quickly differs
from the extended-answer technique the other three papers reward.

## AS content (Papers 1-2) underpins the A Level-only papers

Financial Accounting (Paper 3) and Cost and Management Accounting (Paper 4) both build directly on the
bookkeeping and statement-preparation foundations set in Paper 2. **Exam-preparation priority**: treat
weak AS-level technique as a warning sign before starting A Level-only revision -- gaps in Paper 2
fundamentals resurface as errors in Papers 3 and 4.

## Cost and Management Accounting introduces genuinely new analytical content

Paper 4 covers costing methods, budgeting and standard costing -- content with only a light foundation
in the AS papers. **Exam-preparation priority**: treat Paper 4 as needing dedicated new-content revision
rather than an extension of Paper 2's financial accounting technique.

## The AS/A Level split lets a staged entry work in your favour

Because Papers 1-2 form a complete AS Level qualification on their own, candidates entering in stages
have already banked 50% of the full A Level's assessment weighting (14% + 36%) before ever sitting Paper
3 or Paper 4. **Exam-preparation priority**: if entered in stages, treat the AS result as a genuine
foundation rather than starting A Level revision as if nothing has been assessed yet -- the technique
tested is the same technique, applied to new content.

## Every paper rewards clearly labelled workings, not just final figures

Across all four papers, marks are available specifically for showing method -- ledger workings, formula
substitutions, variance calculations -- independent of whether the final figure is correct.
**Exam-preparation priority**: practise labelling every working clearly rather than performing
calculations mentally, since an unclear or missing working line forfeits credit even when the final
answer given is right.

## Worked routine: a standard-costing variance calculation

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - state the standard and actual figures being compared:
Write both out before calculating anything.

Step 2 - calculate the variance and label its direction:
Favourable or adverse -- stated explicitly, not left implied by a
sign alone.

Step 3 - show the variance formula used, not just the result:
Method marks depend on the formula being visible, not just the
final figure.

Step 4 - state one plausible cause for the variance:
Ties the number back to a real business explanation.

Step 5 - note what management might do in response:
Connects the calculation to its actual decision-making purpose.
```

Step 2's explicit favourable/adverse labelling is a small habit that avoids a common, cheap mistake --
stating a variance's size correctly but getting its direction backwards.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: weight revision towards Paper 2, the single largest paper at 36%; practise
  timed multiple-choice technique specifically for Paper 1; give Paper 4's costing and budgeting content
  dedicated new-material revision time.
- **During Paper 1**: eliminate implausible options first rather than trying to calculate every option
  in full.
- **During Papers 2-4**: show the formula or method before the final figure, for partial credit.

## Self-test

1. How is revision time best weighted across the four papers, and why?
2. Why does Paper 1 need different exam technique from Papers 2-4?
3. Why should weak Paper 2 technique be treated as a warning sign before revising Papers 3-4?
4. What should always be stated explicitly when calculating a variance?

**Answers:** 1. Towards Paper 2, since at 36% it alone outweighs Paper 1 and Paper 4 combined and is the
single largest paper in the qualification. 2. Because it is multiple-choice with no partial credit for
method, rewarding fast, accurate elimination of distractors rather than the extended written technique
the other papers reward. 3. Because Papers 3 and 4 build directly on the bookkeeping and statement
foundations set in Paper 2, so gaps there resurface as errors at A Level. 4. Whether the variance is
favourable or adverse, stated explicitly rather than left implied by a sign alone.
