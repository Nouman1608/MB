## Reversible reactions with hydrated compounds

| Compound | Hydrated | Anhydrous | Heat | Add water |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Copper(II) sulfate | CuSO₄·5H₂O, blue | CuSO₄, white | Blue → white | White → blue |
| Cobalt(II) chloride | CoCl₂·6H₂O, pink | CoCl₂, blue | Pink → blue | Blue → pink |

- Dehydration (heating) is endothermic; hydration (adding water) is exothermic.
- The "add water" direction is the chemical test for water.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reversible reaction | A reaction that can go in both directions; symbol ⇌ |
| Closed system | Nothing can enter or leave |
| Equilibrium | Forward rate = reverse rate; concentrations no longer changing |
| Position of equilibrium | The proportion of reactants to products at equilibrium |
| Shift to the right | More products form |
| Shift to the left | More reactants form |
| Yield | Amount of product obtained |
| Compromise conditions | Conditions balancing yield, rate, cost and safety |

## Shift rules (equilibrium opposes the change)

- **Temperature up:** shifts in the endothermic direction.
- **Temperature down:** shifts in the exothermic direction.
- **Pressure up:** shifts to the side with fewer gas molecules.
- **Pressure down:** shifts to the side with more gas molecules.
- **Same number of gas molecules each side:** pressure has no effect on position.
- **Add reactant / remove product:** shifts to the right.
- **Catalyst:** no shift; equilibrium reached faster.

Quick method: write the number of gas molecules under each side; note the sign of ΔH for the forward reaction; then apply the rules.

## Mini-examples using information provided

- **2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), forward exothermic.** Cooling shifts it right; raising pressure shifts it right (3 gas molecules → 2).
- **CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g) in a closed container.** Only CO₂ is a gas: 0 gas molecules on the left, 1 on the right. Raising pressure shifts it left.
- **H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g).** 2 gas molecules each side: pressure has no effect on position.
- **A coloured product.** If adding a reactant deepens the product's colour, that is the equilibrium shifting right; removing a reactant fades it.

Always finish with what is **observed** (colour, amount of gas) if the question gives colours.

## Haber process

- Equation: **N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)**
- Sources: nitrogen from **air**; hydrogen from **methane**.
- Conditions: **450 °C; 20 000 kPa / 200 atm; iron catalyst**.
- Forward reaction exothermic; 4 gas molecules → 2.

## Contact process (conversion step)

- Equation: **2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g)**
- Sources: SO₂ from **burning sulfur** or **roasting sulfide ores**; O₂ from **air**.
- Conditions: **450 °C; 200 kPa / 2 atm; vanadium(V) oxide catalyst**.
- Forward reaction exothermic; 3 gas molecules → 2.

## Haber and Contact side by side

| | Haber | Contact |
|---|---|---|
| Product | Ammonia | Sulfur trioxide |
| Temperature | 450 °C | 450 °C |
| Pressure | 200 atm (high) | 2 atm (low) |
| Catalyst | Iron | Vanadium(V) oxide |
| Why that pressure | Yield needs high pressure | Yield already high at low pressure |

## Why these conditions

| Condition | Effect on yield | Effect on rate | Other factors |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lower temperature | Higher (forward exothermic) | Slower | Too slow to be economic |
| 450 °C | Lower than at low temperature | Fast enough | Compromise |
| High pressure (Haber, 200 atm) | Higher (fewer gas molecules on right) | Faster | Expensive equipment and energy; safety risk |
| Low pressure (Contact, 2 atm) | Already high at low pressure | Adequate | Avoids cost and hazard of high pressure |
| Catalyst | No effect | Faster | Equilibrium reached sooner, saving time and money |

## Exam traps

- Reactions do **not** stop at equilibrium.
- Concentrations at equilibrium are **constant**, not **equal**.
- A catalyst never changes the yield.
- 450 °C answers must mention **both** yield and rate.
- Pressure: count **gas** molecules only, from the balanced equation's coefficients.
- Haber: 200 atm; Contact: 2 atm. Learn kPa values too: 20 000 kPa and 200 kPa.
- Contact catalyst is **vanadium(V) oxide**; Haber catalyst is **iron**.
- Equilibrium needs a **closed** system.

## Self-test

1. What does the symbol ⇌ show?
2. Describe the colour change when water is added to anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride.
3. State **two** features of a system at equilibrium.
4. Why must the system be closed for equilibrium to be reached?
5. For A(g) + B(g) ⇌ 2C(g), what is the effect of increasing pressure on the position of equilibrium?
6. For a reaction whose forward direction is exothermic, which way does equilibrium shift if the temperature is raised?
7. What is the effect of a catalyst on the position of equilibrium?
8. State the equation for the Haber process.
9. State the sources of hydrogen and nitrogen in the Haber process.
10. State the conditions used in the Contact process.
11. Why is a temperature lower than 450 °C not used in the Haber process?
12. Why is a high pressure not used in the Contact process?

**Answers**

1. The reaction is reversible.
2. Blue to pink.
3. Forward and reverse rates are equal; concentrations of reactants and products no longer change.
4. Otherwise a product (or reactant) could escape, so the reverse (or forward) reaction could not keep pace.
5. No effect: two gas molecules on each side.
6. To the left (the endothermic direction).
7. None; it only increases the rate at which equilibrium is reached.
8. N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g).
9. Hydrogen from methane; nitrogen from air.
10. 450 °C, 200 kPa / 2 atm, vanadium(V) oxide catalyst.
11. The yield would be higher, but the rate would be too slow to be economic.
12. The position of equilibrium already lies far to the right at low pressure, so the extra yield would not justify the cost and hazards of high-pressure equipment.
