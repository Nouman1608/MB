These questions cover section **3.3.1.2 Reaction mechanisms** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Learn the rules first in the [Reaction mechanisms study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-reaction-mechanisms/) and the [Reaction mechanisms revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-reaction-mechanisms-revision-notes/). Where a question asks for curly arrows, describe each arrow by giving where it starts and where it ends.

## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by each term.

**(a)** radical **[1]**

**(b)** homolytic fission **[1]**

**(c)** heterolytic fission **[1]**

**2.** Write an equation to show the homolytic fission of a bromine molecule. **[1]**

**3.** Write an equation to show the heterolytic fission of the C–Cl bond in chloromethane, CH₃Cl, in which chlorine takes both electrons. **[1]**

**4.** Ethane reacts with chlorine in the presence of ultraviolet light to form chloroethane. Write equations for the following steps in the mechanism.

**(a)** initiation **[1]**

**(b)** the two propagation steps **[2]**

**(c)** two different termination steps **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the propagation steps are described as a chain reaction. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why a small amount of butane is found in the products of the reaction of ethane with chlorine. **[2]**

**7.** State the conditions needed to start a free-radical reaction between an alkane and chlorine. **[1]**

**8.** State where a curly arrow starts and where it ends when it shows the formation of a covalent bond. **[2]**

**9.** State where a curly arrow starts when it shows the breaking of a covalent bond. **[1]**

**10.** Bromoethane reacts with aqueous hydroxide ions. Outline the mechanism by describing the curly arrows, the partial charges and the product. **[4]**

**11.** Hydrogen bromide reacts with ethene.

**(a)** Explain why the C=C double bond attracts the hydrogen atom of HBr. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the curly arrows in the first step of the mechanism. **[2]**

**(c)** Name the type of species formed after the first step and describe the final step. **[2]**

**12.** A student draws a curly arrow in a mechanism starting from the carbon atom of a C–Br bond, pointing to the oxygen of an OH⁻ ion. Explain the two errors in this arrow. **[2]**

**13.** Classify each species as a nucleophile or an electrophile: OH⁻, NH₃, H⁺ and CN⁻. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A species with an unpaired electron [1]

**(b)** A covalent bond breaks and each atom takes one electron from the shared pair, forming two radicals [1]

**(c)** A covalent bond breaks and one atom takes both electrons from the shared pair, forming a positive and a negative ion [1]. **[3]**

**2.** Br–Br → Br• + Br• [1]. **[1]**

**3.** CH₃–Cl → CH₃⁺ + Cl⁻ [1]. **[1]**

**4. (a)** Cl₂ → 2Cl• [1]

**(b)** Cl• + C₂H₆ → •C₂H₅ + HCl [1]; •C₂H₅ + Cl₂ → C₂H₅Cl + Cl• [1]

**(c)** Any two from: Cl• + Cl• → Cl₂; •C₂H₅ + Cl• → C₂H₅Cl; •C₂H₅ + •C₂H₅ → C₄H₁₀ [1 each]. **[5]**

**5.** In each propagation step a radical is used and a new radical is formed [1]; so the chlorine radical is regenerated and can react again, and one initiation can lead to many cycles [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Two ethyl radicals can combine in a termination step [1]: •C₂H₅ + •C₂H₅ → C₄H₁₀ [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Ultraviolet light (or high temperature) [1]. **[1]**

**8.** It starts at a lone pair of electrons on an atom (or at another covalent bond such as C=C) [1] and ends at the atom that becomes bonded [1]. **[2]**

**9.** It starts at the middle of the covalent bond (and ends at the atom that takes both electrons) [1]. **[1]**

**10.** The C–Br bond is polar, so carbon is δ+ and bromine is δ– [1]; one arrow starts at a lone pair on the oxygen of OH⁻ and ends at the carbon atom [1]; a second arrow starts at the C–Br bond and ends at the bromine atom [1]; products are ethanol and a bromide ion [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** The C=C double bond is a region of high electron density [1]; the hydrogen in HBr is δ+ (electron-deficient) because bromine is more electronegative, so it is attracted to the double bond [1]

**(b)** One arrow starts at the C=C double bond and ends at the hydrogen atom of HBr [1]; a second arrow starts at the H–Br bond and ends at the bromine atom [1]

**(c)** A carbocation (CH₃CH₂⁺) [1]; an arrow from a lone pair on the bromide ion to the positively charged carbon forms the C–Br bond, giving bromoethane [1]. **[6]**

**12.** The arrow shows the movement of electrons, so it should start at the lone pair on the oxygen of the OH⁻ and not at the carbon atom [1]; it should end at the carbon, not at the oxygen, because the hydroxide ion attacks the δ+ carbon [1]. **[2]**

**13.** OH⁻, NH₃ and CN⁻ are nucleophiles (electron-pair donors) [1]; H⁺ is an electrophile (electron-pair acceptor) [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* An arrow starts where the electrons are and ends where they go. If your arrow starts at a positive charge or an atom with no lone pair, check it again.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Starting arrows at the wrong place.
- Missing the dot on radicals.
- Writing propagation steps that do not regenerate a radical.
- Leaving out partial charges on polar bonds.
- Mixing up nucleophile and electrophile.
