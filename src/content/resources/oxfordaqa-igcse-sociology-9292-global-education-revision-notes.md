**These notes cover 3.1.2.5 Global education** in OxfordAQA International GCSE Sociology (9292):
"Literacy rates: global rates and comparisons between countries", "Differences in educational
provision around the world", "Effects of poverty on schools and education", "Gender and other
inequalities in access to education" and "Inequalities in access to digital education". They condense
the [global education study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-sociology-9292-global-education/) and
expand the short summary in the [Education revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-sociology-9292-education-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Literacy | The ability to read and write, with understanding, a short simple statement about everyday life (UNESCO) |
| Adult literacy rate | The percentage of people aged 15 and over who are literate |
| Youth literacy rate | The literacy rate among people aged 15–24 |
| Provision | What education is available, to whom, and on what terms |
| Compulsory schooling | The years of education required by law |
| Child labour | Work by children that interferes with their schooling or development |
| Digital divide | The gap between those with access to digital technology and skills, and those without |
| Critical consciousness | Freire: understanding and questioning the conditions of one's life |
| Intersection | Several inequalities combining for the same person |

## Literacy in five points

- Defined by UNESCO; **adult** rate is for ages 15+.
- Near universal in many high-income countries; lowest in parts of **sub-Saharan Africa** and **South
  Asia**.
- Lower in **rural** areas, among **older** people and **poorer** groups.
- **Women** make up the majority of adults lacking basic literacy, according to UNESCO data.
- **Youth** literacy is often higher than adult literacy where schooling has recently expanded.

When comparing: age group, male and female gap, change over time, how literacy was measured.

## Freire

- Adult literacy work in **Brazil**; *Pedagogy of the Oppressed* (1968).
- Literacy should build **critical consciousness**, not passive repetition.
- Literacy is **political**: it can keep people passive or help them change their situation.

## Provision: what varies

Compulsory years · cost (free or fees, uniforms, books) · trained teachers and class sizes ·
buildings, electricity, water, toilets · language of teaching · types of school · access to
pre-school, secondary and tertiary stages.

**Global commitments:** Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948): right to education, elementary
education free and compulsory. Sustainable Development Goal 4 (2015): "inclusive and equitable quality
education" for all.

## Effects of poverty

| Level | Effects |
|---|---|
| Family | Child labour; unaffordable fees and uniforms; hunger and illness; no space or light for study |
| School | Large classes; untrained or absent teachers; few textbooks; poor buildings |
| Country | Low spending; debt; conflict and displacement closing schools |

## Gender and other inequalities

**Barriers to girls' education in some societies:** domestic work and care; early marriage; sons
prioritised; unsafe journeys; no separate toilets or sanitary facilities; legal restrictions, such as
the Taliban's bar on girls' secondary schooling in Afghanistan since 2021.

**Different patterns elsewhere:** boys leaving early to earn; girls outperforming boys in examinations
in many higher-income countries.

**Other inequalities:** disability · rural location · ethnicity, caste and language · refugees and
conflict · social class and private schooling. These **intersect**.

## Digital education

- **Devices**, **connection**, **electricity**, **skills and support**.
- COVID-19 school closures (2020) showed how online learning widened gaps within and between
  countries.
- Online learning can widen access only where the divide is addressed.

## Model paragraph

"In [a named society], poverty is a major barrier to education because many families depend on
children's labour. A child working on the family farm during harvest misses weeks of school, falls
behind and may leave early. This links to material explanations of attainment, applied between
countries rather than only within one. However, gender interacts with poverty: when money is short,
some families keep daughters at home first."

## Exam traps

- "Literacy" with no definition or age group.
- Comparisons without figures or years.
- Gender inequality assumed to be identical everywhere.
- Digital divide reduced to "no internet".
- Effects of poverty listed but not explained.
- No named society in an extended answer.

## Self-test

1. How does UNESCO define a literate person?
2. Which age group does the adult literacy rate cover?
3. In which world regions are literacy rates lowest?
4. Which group makes up the majority of adults lacking basic literacy?
5. What did Freire argue literacy should develop?
6. Name **four** ways in which educational provision varies between societies.
7. What does Sustainable Development Goal 4 aim to do, and when was it agreed?
8. Give **three** effects of poverty at the level of the family.
9. Give **three** reasons why girls may have less access to education in some societies.
10. Name **two** inequalities in access to education other than gender.
11. Name the four elements of the digital divide in education.
12. How did the COVID-19 school closures affect educational inequality?

**Answers:** 1. Someone who can read and write, with understanding, a short simple statement about
their everyday life. 2. People aged 15 and over. 3. Parts of sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia.
4. Women. 5. Critical consciousness: the ability to understand and question the conditions of one's
life. 6. Any four: compulsory years; cost; trained teachers and class sizes; buildings and resources;
language of teaching; types of school; access to different stages. 7. To ensure inclusive and
equitable quality education for all; 2015. 8. Any three: child labour; unaffordable fees or uniforms;
hunger or illness; no space or light for study. 9. Any three: domestic work and care; early marriage;
sons prioritised; unsafe journeys; lack of toilets and sanitary facilities; legal restrictions.
10. Any two: disability; rural location; ethnicity, caste or language; refugee status or conflict;
social class. 11. Devices, connection, electricity, and skills and support. 12. Children with devices
and internet could continue learning online while many without could not, widening gaps within and
between countries.
