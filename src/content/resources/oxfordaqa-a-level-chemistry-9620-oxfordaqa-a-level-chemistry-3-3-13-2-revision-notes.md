It is International A2 content, examined in Unit 4 and able to reappear in Unit 5. For explanations and worked examples read the [proteins study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-13-2/); then try the [proteins practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-13-2-practice/).

## The subtopic in six lines

1. Proteins are chains of amino acids joined by peptide links, –CO–NH–.
2. Hydrolysis of the peptide links gives back the amino acids.
3. Primary structure: sequence. Secondary: α-helix or β-pleated sheet. Tertiary: overall 3D shape.
4. Hydrogen bonds hold secondary structure; side-chain interactions, including S–S bonds, hold tertiary structure.
5. Amino acids are separated by TLC, located with ninhydrin or UV light, and identified by Rf values.
6. Rf = spot distance ÷ solvent front distance, both from the baseline.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Peptide link | –CO–NH– joining two amino acid residues |
| Dipeptide / tripeptide | Two / three amino acids joined by one / two peptide links |
| Primary structure | Order of amino acids in the chain |
| α-helix | Coiled backbone held by hydrogen bonds between peptide links along one chain |
| β-pleated sheet | Sections of chain side by side, hydrogen bonded between neighbouring sections |
| Tertiary structure | Overall folded shape of the whole chain |
| Disulfide bridge | S–S covalent bond between two cysteine side chains |
| Rf value | Ratio of distance moved by a spot to distance moved by the solvent front |
| Ninhydrin | Developing agent that makes amino acid spots visible (usually purple) |

## Drawing peptides

- First-named amino acid has the free –NH₂; last has the free –COOH.
- Join C=O of one residue to N–H of the next.
- n amino acids → (n − 1) peptide links → (n − 1) water molecules lost.
- Mr(peptide) = sum of amino acid Mr − 18.0 × (n − 1).
- Two different amino acids → 2 dipeptides; three different (each once) → 6 tripeptides.

Example: Gly–Ala–Ser = H₂N–CH₂–CO–NH–CH(CH₃)–CO–NH–CH(CH₂OH)–COOH, Mr 75.0 + 89.0 + 105.0 − 36.0 = 233.0.

## Hydrolysis

- Water adds across each C–N bond in the peptide links: C gets –OH, N gets –H.
- Conditions: heat under reflux with concentrated HCl, or with aqueous NaOH.
- Excess acid: every amino group as –NH₃⁺; carboxyl groups as –COOH.
- Excess alkali: every carboxyl group as –COO⁻; amino groups as –NH₂.
- Side chains unchanged (unless they contain –COOH or –NH₂).

## Levels of structure

| Level | Diagram clue | Held by |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Sequence of names or codes | Peptide links (covalent) |
| Secondary: α-helix | Coil with dashed lines along it | H-bonds, C=O···H–N in the same chain |
| Secondary: β-pleated sheet | Parallel strands with dashed lines across | H-bonds, C=O···H–N between neighbouring sections |
| Tertiary | Whole chain folded; S–S bridges | Side-chain interactions: H-bonds, S–S bonds |

## The two bonds

**Hydrogen bond**: δ+ H on N–H of one peptide link attracted to a lone pair on the δ− O of a C=O in another. Weak individually, but there are very many.

**S–S bond**: formed by oxidation of two cysteine –SH groups: –CH₂–SH + HS–CH₂– + [O] → –CH₂–S–S–CH₂– + H₂O. Covalent and strong; links distant parts of the chain.

## TLC checklist

- Pencil baseline; solvent level below it.
- Handle the plate by its edges (fingerprints contain amino acids).
- Lid on the tank so the atmosphere is saturated.
- Mark the solvent front as soon as the plate is removed.
- Ninhydrin spray then warm, or a fluorescent plate under UV light.
- Standards on the same plate, same solvent.
- Rf has no units and is between 0 and 1.

## Rf practice

| Front (cm) | Spot (cm) | Rf |
|---|---|---|
| 7.2 | 1.8 | 0.25 |
| 7.2 | 3.6 | 0.50 |
| 7.2 | 5.4 | 0.75 |

## Identifying amino acids from a chromatogram

1. Measure from the baseline to the centre of each spot, and from the baseline to the solvent front.
2. Divide to get each Rf.
3. Match each value to a standard run on the same plate.
4. Report which amino acids are present; TLC does not give their order.

## Exam traps

- Peptide drawn with C–C or N–O link.
- One water subtracted for a tripeptide.
- Neutral amino acids drawn after acid or alkaline hydrolysis.
- S–S bonds assigned to secondary structure.
- Hydrogen bonds described as "between N and O" without the H.
- Distances measured from the bottom of the plate.
- Thinking TLC gives the sequence of amino acids.

## Self-test

Answer without looking, then check below.

1. Give the structure of a peptide link.
2. How many peptide links are in a tetrapeptide?
3. Draw Ala–Gly (alanine R = CH₃, glycine R = H).
4. Calculate the Mr of Ala–Gly (alanine 89.0, glycine 75.0).
5. Give the products of hydrolysing Ala–Gly with excess hydrochloric acid.
6. What is the primary structure of a protein?
7. Name the two types of secondary structure.
8. Which bonds hold secondary structure in place?
9. Which amino acid forms S–S bonds, and what type of bond is S–S?
10. Why is the baseline drawn in pencil?
11. A spot moves 2.7 cm and the solvent front 6.0 cm. Calculate Rf.
12. Name two ways of locating amino acid spots on a TLC plate.

### Answers

1. –CO–NH– (C=O bonded to N–H).
2. Three.
3. H₂N–CH(CH₃)–CO–NH–CH₂–COOH.
4. 89.0 + 75.0 − 18.0 = 146.0.
5. ⁺H₃N–CH(CH₃)–COOH and ⁺H₃N–CH₂–COOH.
6. The sequence of amino acids in the chain.
7. α-helix and β-pleated sheet.
8. Hydrogen bonds between C=O and N–H groups of peptide links.
9. Cysteine; a covalent bond.
10. Pencil (graphite) does not dissolve in the solvent and move up the plate.
11. 2.7 ÷ 6.0 = 0.45.
12. Spraying with ninhydrin (then warming); ultraviolet light with a fluorescent plate.
