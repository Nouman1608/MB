**Key question 2.2, Cambridge IGCSE History 0470:** "To what extent was the League of Nations a success?" Learn the
structure, aims and membership; the four named disputes of the 1920s; the four areas of humanitarian work; and the
Manchurian and Abyssinian crises. Explanations are in the
[League of Nations study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-league-of-nations-success/); practise with
the [League of Nations practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-league-of-nations-success-practice/).

## Structure and weaknesses

| Feature | Weakness it created |
|---|---|
| Assembly: unanimous decisions | Slow; one member could block action |
| Council: Britain, France, Italy, Japan permanent at first | Depended on great powers with their own interests |
| No army | Military action relied on members' willingness |
| Sanctions: condemnation, economic, military | Economic sanctions weak without the USA |
| Permanent Court of International Justice | Could not enforce its rulings |
| USA never joined; Germany 1926-33; USSR 1934-39 | Never had all the great powers at once |

## The four disputes of the 1920s

| Dispute | Who | Outcome | Success? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vilna, 1920 | Poland and Lithuania | Poland kept Vilna; League did not act | No |
| Aaland Islands, 1920-21 | Sweden and Finland | Islands to Finland, demilitarised, Swedish culture protected; Sweden accepted | Yes |
| Corfu, 1923 | Italy and Greece | Mussolini occupied Corfu; Conference of Ambassadors made Greece pay; Italy withdrew | No |
| Bulgaria, 1925 | Greece and Bulgaria | Greece withdrew and paid compensation | Yes |

**The pattern:** success with small states; failure when a great power or a great power's ally was involved.

## Humanitarian work

| Area | Achievement | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Refugees | Nansen; return of hundreds of thousands of prisoners of war; Nansen passports | Refugee crises continued into the 1930s |
| Health | Fought epidemics such as typhus; work on malaria and leprosy; forerunner of the WHO | Depended on members' funding |
| Working conditions | ILO recommendations on working hours and child labour | Many members did not adopt them |
| Slavery | Slavery Commission; Slavery Convention (1926) | Forced labour continued in many colonies |

## The 1930s

| | Manchuria, 1931-33 | Abyssinia, 1935-36 |
|---|---|---|
| Aggressor | Japan | Italy |
| Motives | Raw materials, land, army influence, Depression | Empire, prestige, revenge for Adowa (1896) |
| Trigger | Mukden incident, September 1931 | Wal Wal clash, December 1934 |
| League response | Lytton Commission; report condemned Japan | Condemnation; sanctions without oil |
| Key failure | No sanctions; distance; no US involvement | Suez Canal left open; Hoare-Laval Pact (December 1935) |
| Result | Manchukuo; Japan leaves League (1933) | Italy conquers Abyssinia (May 1936); sanctions dropped; Italy leaves (1937) |
| Wider effect | League shown powerless against a great power | Collective security collapses; Rome-Berlin Axis; Rhineland remilitarised |

## How the Depression made things harder

- Members would not risk **trade sanctions** that hurt their own economies.
- **Nationalist and militarist** groups gained support in Japan, Italy and Germany.
- Britain and France cut spending, including on **armed forces**, and feared war.
- The **Disarmament Conference** (1932-34) failed; Germany left it and the League in 1933.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Covenant | The League's founding rules, part of the peace treaties |
| Collective security | Members acting together against an aggressor |
| Sanctions | Penalties, especially trade bans, against an aggressor |
| Conference of Ambassadors | Body of Allied ambassadors that settled some post-war disputes, including Corfu |
| Mandate | Former colony run by a League member under League supervision |
| ILO | International Labour Organization, improving working conditions |
| Nansen passport | Identity document for stateless refugees |
| Lytton Commission | League inquiry into Manchuria |
| Hoare-Laval Pact | Secret British and French plan (1935) to give Italy much of Abyssinia |
| Manchukuo | Japanese puppet state in Manchuria |

## One-line answers to the focus points

- **Did weaknesses make failure inevitable?** They made failure likely against great powers, but the League still
  solved disputes between smaller states in the 1920s.
- **How successful was peacekeeping in the 1920s?** Mixed: success in the Aaland Islands and Bulgaria, failure in Vilna
  and Corfu.
- **How important was humanitarian work?** It helped millions and outlived the League through the United Nations, but
  it was not the League's main purpose.
- **How far did the Depression make the League's work harder?** Greatly: it encouraged aggression by Japan and Italy
  and made members unwilling to impose sanctions that hurt their own trade.

## Exam traps

- **Corfu is a failure.** Italy got compensation and the League was bypassed.
- **Abyssinia sanctions existed** but excluded oil.
- **"Inevitable" questions** need both structure and circumstances.
- **Humanitarian work counts** as part of "success", but weigh it against peacekeeping.
- **Dates matter.** Mukden 1931; Japan leaves 1933; invasion of Abyssinia 1935.

## Self-test

1. Where was the League based? *Geneva.*
2. Why did the USA not join? *The Senate refused to ratify the Treaty of Versailles.*
3. What were the three stages of collective security? *Moral condemnation, economic sanctions, military action.*
4. Who kept Vilna, and why did the League not act? *Poland; France saw Poland as an ally and Britain would not act
   alone.*
5. Why is the Aaland Islands case seen as a success? *Both Sweden and Finland accepted the League's ruling.*
6. Who was murdered in 1923, leading to the Corfu crisis? *The Italian general Tellini.*
7. What did the League order in 1925? *Greece to withdraw from Bulgaria and pay compensation.*
8. Who led the League's work for refugees? *Fridtjof Nansen.*
9. What did the Slavery Convention of 1926 aim to do? *End slavery and the slave trade.*
10. What did the Lytton Commission conclude? *That Japan was in the wrong and should withdraw, with Manchuria governed under
    Chinese sovereignty.*
11. Why were sanctions against Italy ineffective? *Oil was excluded and the Suez Canal stayed open.*
12. What was the Hoare-Laval Pact? *A secret plan by Britain and France to give Italy much of Abyssinia.*
