## Questions

**1.** The forces below are: normal contact force, magnetism, friction, gravity, electrostatics. Sort them into contact forces and non-contact forces. **[2]**

**2.** A fridge magnet is held to a fridge door by a magnetic force of 2.5 N.

**(a)** State the size and direction of the force that the door exerts on the magnet, relative to the force of the magnet on the door. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the type of force. **[1]**

**3.** A boy leans on a wall with a horizontal force of 30 N.

**(a)** State the force the wall exerts on the boy. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why these two forces do not cancel each other. **[2]**

**4.** A lamp of weight 18 N hangs at rest from a ceiling by a cable. Describe the free body diagram for the lamp. **[4]**

**5.** A trolley is pushed to the right with 48 N. Friction of 12 N and air resistance of 9 N act to the left. Calculate the resultant force, with its direction. **[3]**

**6.** A drone is pushed by wind with a horizontal force of 24 N east. Its rotors give a force of 7.0 N north. Ignore all other forces.

**(a)** Describe a scale drawing to find the resultant, using 1 cm to represent 2 N. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the size of the resultant force. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the angle of the resultant north of east. **[1]**

**7.** A child pulls a sledge with a rope. The force in the rope is 85 N. Its horizontal and vertical sides in a scale drawing are in the ratio 15 : 8 : 17 (horizontal : vertical : rope). Use a scale of 1 cm to 5 N to find the horizontal and vertical components. **[4]**

**8.** A ring is pulled with 12 N east and 16 N north. A third force acts on it so that it is in equilibrium. Determine the size and direction of the third force, and describe the vector diagram. **[5]**

**9.** A lorry travels at a constant 20 m/s on a level road with a driving force of 5200 N.

**(a)** Explain what this tells you about the resistive forces. **[2]**
**(b)** The driver brakes. Describe the resultant force and the change in velocity. **[2]**

**10.** A parachutist of weight 650 N opens the parachute while falling fast. At that moment drag is 1100 N.

**(a)** Calculate the resultant force, with its direction. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the parachutist reaches a new constant velocity. **[4]**

**11.** A student says: "A book's weight and the table's normal contact force are a Newton's third law pair, because they are equal and opposite." Evaluate this claim. **[4]**

**12.** A child sits on a sledge on level snow. The rope force is 80 N, at an angle such that the horizontal and vertical sides are in the ratio 4 : 3 : 5 (horizontal : vertical : rope). The combined weight is 300 N. Friction is 40 N.

**(a)** Find the horizontal and vertical components of the rope force. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the free body diagram for the sledge. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the horizontal resultant force. **[2]**
**(d)** The vertical forces balance. Calculate the normal contact force. **[1]**
**(e)** Name the interaction pair partner of the normal contact force. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Contact: normal contact force and friction [1]. Non-contact: magnetism, gravity and electrostatics [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Both groups need to be right; one misplaced force usually costs a mark, so check each one against whether touching is needed.

**2. (a)** The force is **2.5 N** [1] in the opposite direction, towards the door [1]. **(b)** **Magnetic** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Equal and opposite" needs both words addressed: give the size and the direction, not just "the same".

**3. (a)** **30 N** [1], horizontal, pushing the boy away from the wall [1]. **(b)** Each force acts on a different object [1]: the 30 N is on the wall and the 30 N is on the boy, so neither cancels the other [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong answers say which object each force acts on. "They are equal and opposite so they cancel" is the usual wrong answer.

**4.** Draw the lamp only [1]. Weight of 18 N down [1]. Tension of 18 N up from the cable [1]. The arrows are the same length because the resultant is zero (the lamp is at rest) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Label each arrow with the object that exerts it and keep equal forces equal in length; unlabelled arrows are hard to credit.

**5.** Total resistance = 12 + 9 = 21 N [1]. Resultant = 48 − 21 = **27 N** [1] to the **right** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Direction is part of a resultant. Writing "27" with no unit or direction gives away marks.

**6. (a)** Draw 12.0 cm east and then 3.5 cm north from its end [1]. Join start to end and measure the line, and the angle with a protractor [1]. **(b)** R = √(24² + 7.0²) [1] = **25 N** [1]. **(c)** tan⁻¹(7.0 / 24) = **16°** north of east [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert with the scale before drawing, and show that the arrows go head to tail.

**7.** Rope arrow = 85 ÷ 5 = 17.0 cm [1]. Construct a right-angled triangle with the rope as the longest side [1]. Horizontal side 15.0 cm = **75 N** [1]. Vertical side 8.0 cm = **40 N** [1]. Check: 75² + 40² = 7225 = 85². **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the scale conversion for both components; answers in centimetres instead of newtons lose the final marks.

**8.** Resultant of the 12 N and 16 N = √(12² + 16²) = 20 N [1]. The third force is also **20 N** [1] in the opposite direction [1]. Angle: tan⁻¹(16 / 12) = 53°, so it points **53° south of west** [1]. Drawn head to tail the three arrows form a **closed triangle** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Equilibrium means the arrows return to their start. A third force that is only the right size, with the wrong direction, does not meet the condition.

**9. (a)** Constant velocity means the resultant force is zero (Newton's first law) [1], so the resistive forces total **5200 N** [1]. **(b)** The braking force makes a resultant that acts **backwards** [1], so the velocity decreases and the lorry slows [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the motion to the resultant. "The forces are balanced" needs the reason, constant velocity.

**10. (a)** 1100 − 650 [1] = **450 N upward** [1]. **(b)** Drag is greater than weight, so the resultant is upward and the parachutist slows [1]. As speed falls, drag falls [1]. This continues until drag equals 650 N, the weight [1]. The resultant is then zero, so velocity is constant, and smaller than before [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A full explanation covers the cause (drag changes with speed) and the end condition (resultant zero). Stating only "terminal velocity" is not an explanation.

**11.** The claim is **not correct** [1]. The weight and the normal contact force both act on the **book**, one object [1]. They are **different types** of force: gravitational and contact [1]. A pair is the book's gravitational pull on the Earth with the Earth's pull on the book, or the book's push on the table with the table's push on the book [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Equal and opposite" is not sufficient to identify a pair. Check the object and the type for both forces.

**12. (a)** Horizontal = 80 × 4/5 = **64 N** [1]; vertical = 80 × 3/5 = **48 N** [1]. **(b)** Sledge only, with arrows for: weight 300 N down, normal contact force up, rope force at an angle (or its components) and friction 40 N backward [1]. Label each force [1]. **(c)** 64 − 40 [1] = **24 N** forward [1]. **(d)** 300 − 48 = **252 N** [1]. **(e)** The sledge's push on the snow (a contact force of 252 N, downward) [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Treat horizontal and vertical separately, and say which direction each resultant is in. Do not draw the force of the sledge on the snow in the sledge's diagram.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not saying which object each force acts on.
- Calling weight and normal contact force a third-law pair.
- Giving a resultant without a direction or a unit.
- Adding perpendicular forces arithmetically.
- Not converting with the scale in a vector diagram.
- Drawing the force of the object on the surroundings in a free body diagram.
- Saying "no forces" when the forces are balanced.
- Treating constant velocity as needing a constant resultant force.
- Stopping the terminal velocity explanation before the final balance.
