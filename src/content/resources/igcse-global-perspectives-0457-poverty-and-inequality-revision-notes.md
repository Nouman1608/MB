These notes follow the Cambridge IGCSE Global Perspectives (0457) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027, Version 2 (published April 2025). Poverty and inequality is one of the topics in the section 3 topic list, used for Component 1 Written Exam, Component 2 Individual Report and Component 3 Team Project. The syllabus says knowledge of content is not assessed. These notes show how to use the topic to practise the assessed skills: research, analysis, evaluation, reflection, communication and collaboration.

## The skills in one table

| Assessment objective (syllabus name) | What it means for this topic | Components |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 Research, analysis and evaluation | Find issues inside the topic; analyse causes and consequences; judge sources, evidence and reasoning; assess actions | 1, 2, 3 |
| AO2 Reflection | Consider perspectives of people in poverty, taxpayers, charities and governments objectively and with empathy; justify your own view | 2, 3 |
| AO3 Communication and collaboration | Present arguments clearly, cite and reference, contribute to the Team Project | 2, 3 |

Component 1 is 100% AO1, so the Written Exam is about using the sources well, not about what you know.

## How each component uses this topic

- **Component 1 Written Exam:** the sources may be about poverty or inequality. You analyse their information and perspectives, evaluate a piece of research and suggest how to test a claim, judge the quality of arguments, and assess actions.
- **Component 2 Individual Report:** you choose a global issue that divides opinion, such as how best to reduce inequality, and research local and/or national and global perspectives before proposing one course of action.
- **Component 3 Team Project:** your team picks a local issue, such as the cost of school uniform, then plans, carries out and measures an action.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition | Why it matters in a source |
|---|---|---|
| Issue | "An important subject or problem for discussion" (syllabus) | Poverty is a topic; "should cash come with conditions?" is an issue |
| Perspective | "A viewpoint on an issue based on evidence and reasoning" (syllabus) | Judge it by its evidence and reasoning |
| Absolute poverty | Not enough to meet basic needs | Fixed line; can fall as an economy grows |
| Relative poverty | Much less than most people in your society | Moves with typical income; can stay high even as living standards rise |
| Inequality | Uneven spread of income, wealth or opportunity | Not the same as poverty |
| Income | Money coming in over time | Can change quickly |
| Wealth | What you own | Builds up across generations |
| Gini coefficient | 0 = total equality, 1 = total inequality | One number hides who gains and who loses |
| Median | The middle value | Less pulled by very high earners than the mean |
| Multidimensional poverty | Poverty measured by health, schooling and living standards as well as money | Gives a different picture from income alone |

## Real examples at a glance

You do not need to learn these. They are here so you can evaluate them if they appear in a source, or use them as starting points for research.

| Who / what | What it is | Key point | One evaluation point |
|---|---|---|---|
| World Bank international poverty line | Global measure of extreme poverty | Updated in June 2025 to $3.00 a day (2021 prices) | A single global line cannot reflect very different costs of living within countries |
| UN Sustainable Development Goals 1 and 10 | Global targets adopted in 2015 | Goal 1: end poverty in all its forms everywhere. Goal 10: reduce inequality within and among countries | Goals set direction but do not force any government to act |
| Multidimensional Poverty Index (UNDP and OPHI) | Counts deprivations in health, education and living standards | Shows poverty that income data can miss | Needs survey data that some countries collect rarely |
| Bolsa Família, Brazil (2003) | Conditional cash transfer | Cash linked to school attendance and health check-ups | Conditions can shut out families who cannot meet them |
| Benazir Income Support Programme, Pakistan (2008) | Cash payments to women in low-income households | Main payment unconditional, paid to women | Depends on accurate data to target the right households |
| Finland basic income experiment (2017–2018) | Unconditional monthly payment to unemployed people | Small employment effects; better reported wellbeing | Short, limited trial; may not scale to a whole country |
| Grameen Bank and Muhammad Yunus (Nobel Peace Prize 2006) | Microfinance: small loans for people without bank access | Widely copied model | Randomised evaluations have generally found modest average effects on income |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Poverty vs inequality.** Poverty is about having too little. Inequality is about the gap. One can fall while the other rises.
- **Absolute vs relative poverty.** Two sources can disagree only because they use different measures.
- **Income share vs income amount.** If the economy grows, the poorest group's share can fall while their actual income stays flat.
- **Percentage vs percentage points.** 50% to 70% is +20 percentage points, but a 40% rise.
- **Mean vs median.** A few very rich people raise the mean but barely move the median.
- **Short-term relief vs long-term cause.** A food bank relieves hunger now; it does not raise incomes.
- **Fact vs claim vs opinion.** "The scheme paid 200 families" (fact) / "the scheme cut hunger" (claim needing evidence) / "the scheme is wasteful" (opinion).
- **Value vs vested interest.** A value is a belief about what matters ("fairness"). A vested interest is something a person stands to gain or lose.

## Mini essay plan

*Typical extended question (invented):* "Using the sources, assess which action would be most effective in reducing child poverty in a town."

1. **Set the criteria.** Say how you will judge: effect on the cause, cost, fairness, how long the effect lasts.
2. **Action 1.** What it does; evidence for it from the sources; how reliable that evidence is; a limitation.
3. **Action 2.** Same structure. Compare directly with Action 1 ("unlike free meals, training targets income").
4. **Action 3 (if given).** Same structure, briefly.
5. **Weigh up.** Which criteria matter most here, and why. Note trade-offs, such as reach against cost.
6. **Judgement.** Name one action (or a combination) and give the deciding reason. Say what evidence would change your mind.

## Quick self-test

1. What does the syllabus say about knowledge of content in 0457?
2. Give the syllabus definition of a perspective.
3. What is the difference between absolute and relative poverty?
4. A Gini coefficient rises from 0.35 to 0.42. What has happened?
5. Why might a source quote the median income rather than the mean?
6. A survey at a food bank finds 80% of users skip meals. Why can you not apply this to the whole town?
7. "No child should go hungry." Is this a fact, a value or a prediction?
8. Give one vested interest a landlords' association might have in a debate about rent caps.
9. State one strength and one limitation of conditional cash transfers.
10. Which assessment objective makes up all of Component 1?
11. Give one reason why a food collection alone may be a weak Team Project action.
12. Write one global research question on this topic that divides opinion.

### Answers

1. It is not assessed; the topics are a context for practising skills.
2. "A viewpoint on an issue based on evidence and reasoning."
3. Absolute poverty is not meeting basic needs; relative poverty is having much less than most people in your society.
4. Income inequality has increased.
5. The median is less affected by a few very high incomes, so it better shows a typical household.
6. Food bank users are a self-selected group more likely to be in need, so the sample is not representative.
7. A value.
8. Rent caps could reduce their members' income, so they gain from arguing against them.
9. Strength: links help to school attendance and health. Limitation: families who cannot meet conditions may lose support.
10. AO1 Research, analysis and evaluation.
11. It meets a short-term need but may not address a cause, and success is hard to measure beyond counting items. Adding a measure of impact makes it stronger.
12. For example: "Should governments give cash to people in poverty with or without conditions?"

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding outside facts about poverty instead of quoting and using the sources.
- Defining "fact" or "vested interest" in general terms without pointing to the exact words in the source.
- Treating a falling share of income as proof that people are getting poorer.
- Saying a survey is "biased" without explaining who was asked and how that skews the result.
- Describing two arguments one after the other without comparing their evidence and reasoning.
- Judging an action as "good" without criteria such as cost, reach, fairness or how long it lasts.
- Ending an extended answer with "it depends" and no final judgement.
- In the Individual Report, giving only global perspectives and no local or national one.
- In the Team Project, choosing an action with no plan for measuring success.
