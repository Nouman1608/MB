## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for explaining it. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

**1.** Sincerity: doing a deed purely for Allah [1].

**2.** Eid al-Adha [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: paying Zakah; giving sadaqah; feeding the poor; sharing time or knowledge; helping neighbours; giving to orphans.

**4. (a)** Any one [1]: whoever hides the faults of a Muslim, Allah will hide his faults in this world and the next; Qur'an 24:19 warns against loving the spread of indecency.

**(b)** One reason identified [1] and explained [2].

- **It protects dignity** [1]: people make mistakes, and exposing them harms their reputation [1], so concealing them shows mercy [1].
- **It earns reward** [1]: the Hadith promises that Allah will hide the faults of those who hide the faults of others [1], so it benefits the person who does it [1].
- **It keeps the community healthy** [1]: spreading faults encourages gossip and distrust [1], while discretion builds trust [1].

**5.** For each of two ways: way identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Cleanliness** [1]: the Hadith says cleanliness is half of faith, and wudu and washing are part of daily worship [1].
- **Moderation in food** [1]: the Qur'an says "eat and drink, but be not excessive" (7:31), which prevents harm from overeating [1].
- **Avoiding harmful substances** [1]: alcohol and drugs are forbidden because they damage health [1].
- **Seeking treatment** [1]: the Hadith says Allah has sent down a cure for every disease, so Muslims are encouraged to see a doctor [1].

**6.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Following the Prophet (pbuh)** [1]: he was known as al-Amin, the trustworthy, and Muslims follow his example [1].
- **A way to Paradise** [1]: the Hadith says truthfulness leads to righteousness and righteousness leads to Paradise [1].
- **Trust in society** [1]: honest dealings in trade and promises let people rely on one another [1].
- **Warning against cheating** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) said that whoever cheats is not one of us [1].

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two ways, way identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Ways (AO1):

- **Self-control** [1]: giving up comfort, such as sleep for Fajr or food while fasting, trains discipline [1].
- **Putting others first** [1]: sacrifice teaches a Muslim to prefer the needs of others to his own [1].
- **Trust in Allah** [1]: Prophet Ibraheem (AS) showed that obedience to Allah comes before even the dearest things [1].
- **Reduces greed** [1]: giving up wealth for Allah loosens attachment to possessions [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- Qur'an 59:9, which praises the Ansar for giving others preference over themselves even though in privation [2].
- Qur'an 3:92: people will not attain righteousness until they spend from what they love [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Ways (AO1):

- **Remembrance of Allah** [1]: prayer and dhikr bring a sense of peace and trust [1].
- **Hope in hardship** [1]: the Qur'an promises that ease follows hardship, which reduces despair [1].
- **Limits on burdens** [1]: Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity, so people are not asked for more than they can bear [1].
- **Community support** [1]: family, friends and the mosque offer help and company [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Verily, with hardship [will be] ease" (Qur'an 94:5-6) [2].
- Qur'an 13:28: hearts find assurance in the remembrance of Allah [2].
- Qur'an 2:286: Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the Hadith says Allah looks at hearts and deeds, not faces and wealth; without sincerity a good deed may be only showing off (riya'); the Hadith that actions are judged by intentions; taqwa keeps a person good even when unseen.
- **Against the statement:** the Hadith also mentions "deeds", so the heart alone is not enough; the Qur'an links belief with righteous action many times; Zakah, prayer and fasting are required acts; a good heart that never acts does not help others.
- **Balanced view:** the heart gives deeds their value, and deeds show what is in the heart; the Hadith joins the two.

*Use of the passage*

- "Does not look to your faces and your wealth" warns against pride in appearance and money.
- "He looks to your heart" supports the priority of sincerity and taqwa.
- "And to your deeds" shows that actions also count.

Level 4 answers use the passage directly, give reasoning on both sides, and reach a reasoned judgement.
