## Section A

**1.** Define the term *niche market*. **[2]**

**2.** Identify **two** factors, other than price, that could increase the supply of fresh fish. **[2]**

**3.** Explain the difference between a movement along a demand curve and a shift in demand. **[3]**

**4.** Explain **one** reason why a marketing objective must be linked to the corporate objective. **[2]**

**5.** Halden Paints had sales of $18m in a market worth $150m. Calculate its market share. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **one** difference between marketing a convenience product and a speciality product. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Rushford Juices had sales of $4.5m in a market worth $30m last year. This year its sales are $4.8m and the market is worth $36m.

**(a)** Calculate the market growth.

**(b)** Calculate the change in Rushford's market share in percentage points.

**(c)** Explain **one** implication of these results for Rushford. **[7]**

**8.** A rise in incomes increases demand for restaurant meals, while higher food costs reduce supply. Analyse the likely effect on the price and quantity of restaurant meals. **[4]**

**9.** Brightwater Valves sells industrial pumps to water companies. Explain **two** ways in which its marketing is likely to differ from that of a business selling garden hoses to households. **[4]**

**10.** A cinema chain wants to segment its market. Analyse how it could use demographic and psychographic segmentation. **[4]**

**11.** Penmore Coffee is considering a loyalty app costing $30 000 a year. It expects the app to keep 400 customers who would otherwise leave, each providing $90 of contribution a year.

**(a)** Calculate the net gain or loss from the app in one year.

**(b)** Explain **one** factor, other than this figure, that Penmore should consider. **[4]**

**12.** Lindqvist Audio makes high-end speakers for a small group of music enthusiasts. Its owner wants to move into the mass market for wireless speakers. Evaluate whether Lindqvist should make this move. **[10]**

---

## Answers

**1.** A small, specialised part of a larger market [1] in which customers have particular needs [1].

**2.** Any two: more fishing boats or producers [1]; better fishing technology [1]; good weather; lower fuel costs.

**3.** A movement along the curve is caused by a change in the product's own price [1]. A shift is caused by a change in any other factor, such as income or tastes [1], so more or less is demanded at every price [1].

**4.** Marketing objectives must support what the whole business is trying to achieve [1]; for example, a growth objective needs a marketing objective such as raising sales in a new region, or marketing effort is wasted on goals the business does not need [1].

**5.** 18 ÷ 150 × 100 [1] = 12% [1].

**6.** A convenience product, such as bread, needs wide distribution so it is always available [1], whereas a speciality product, such as a designer watch, is sold through a few exclusive outlets with strong brand image [1].

**7.** **(a)** (36 − 30) ÷ 30 × 100 [1] = 20% [1].

**(b)** Last year share = 4.5 ÷ 30 × 100 = 15% [1]. This year share = 4.8 ÷ 36 × 100 = 13.3% [1]. Change = a fall of 1.7 percentage points [1].

**(c)** Rushford's sales rose by only 6.7% while the market grew by 20%, so rivals are winning most new customers [1]. Rushford may need to review its product range, price or promotion to avoid losing further share and bargaining power with retailers [1].

**8.** Higher incomes shift demand to the right [1], which on its own raises price and quantity [1]. Higher food costs shift supply to the left [1], which also raises price but reduces quantity. Price is therefore very likely to rise, while the effect on quantity depends on which shift is larger [1].

**9.** Any two, explained. Brightwater sells to a few large buyers, so it will use a direct sales team and technical presentations [1] rather than mass advertising, because each order is large and buyers need detailed specifications [1]. Prices are likely to be negotiated in long-term contracts [1], whereas the hose maker uses fixed retail prices because it sells to many households through shops [1]. Allow several people in the buying decision or importance of after-sales service.

**10.** Demographic: the cinema could target families with children through weekend matinées and family tickets [1], and students with discounted weekday tickets [1]. Psychographic: it could target film enthusiasts who value art-house films with special screenings [1], or people seeking a premium night out with reclining seats and food service [1].

**11.** **(a)** Benefit = 400 × 90 = $36 000 [1]. Net gain = 36 000 − 30 000 = $6 000 [1].

**(b)** Any one, explained. The forecast of 400 retained customers may be optimistic [1], and if only 300 stay the scheme would make a loss of $3 000. Allow data protection duties, customers belonging to rival schemes, or extra benefits such as customer data used for promotions [1].

**12.** For the move: the mass market offers far higher sales volume [1], which could bring economies of scale and lower unit costs [1]; dependence on a small group of enthusiasts is risky if their tastes change [1].
Against the move: mass-market wireless speakers face intense competition from large brands with big promotion budgets [1]; prices and margins are much lower, so Lindqvist would need high volumes to make the same contribution [1]; its brand reputation for high-end quality could be damaged by cheaper products [1]; it may lack the production capacity and distribution needed [1].
Judgement: Lindqvist should not move fully into the mass market [1]; it could instead launch a premium wireless range under its own brand, staying near its niche [1]. The decision depends on its finance and capacity, and on whether its brand can command a price premium in the larger market [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating the change in market share as a percentage instead of percentage points.
- Rounding market shares too early, so the change is wrong.
- Saying a rise in price "shifts demand".
- Describing B2B marketing without contrasting it with B2C.
- Recommending the mass market without considering competition, margins or capacity.
