These questions cover section 3.10.2, Moving charges in a magnetic field, of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), which is International A-level content. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [moving charges study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-moving-charges-in-a-magnetic-field/) or the [moving charges revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-moving-charges-in-a-magnetic-field-revision-notes/) first.

Use e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, electron mass 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg, proton mass 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg and 1 u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg. All velocities are perpendicular to the magnetic field.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Explain why a uniform magnetic field changes the direction of a moving electron but not its speed. **[2]**

**2.** Calculate the force on a proton moving at 4.0 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹ in a field of 0.25 T. **[2]**

**3.** An electron moves to the right across the page. A uniform magnetic field points into the page. In which direction is the force on the electron?

A: up the page

B: down the page

C: into the page

D: out of the page

**[1]**

**4.** Show that the time for one orbit of a charged particle in a uniform magnetic field is independent of its speed. **[3]**

**5.** Calculate the radius of the path of an electron moving at 2.0 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹ in a field of 5.0 mT. **[2]**

**6.** A proton and an alpha particle (charge +2e, mass about four times the proton's) move at the same speed in the same field. Calculate the ratio of the radius of the alpha particle's path to the proton's. **[2]**

### Section B

**7.** Electrons are accelerated from rest through 180 V and enter a uniform field of 0.90 mT, where they move in a circle of radius 5.0 cm.

**(a)** Show that the specific charge of the electron is given by e/m = 2V/(B²r²). **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate e/m from these data. **[1]**

**(c)** The accelerating p.d. is doubled. State the factor by which the radius changes. **[1]**

**8.** A beam of singly charged neon ions passes undeflected through a region where an electric field, from plates 8.0 mm apart with 400 V between them, is perpendicular to a magnetic field of 0.050 T.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the ions that pass through undeflected. **[2]**

**(b)** These ions then enter a uniform field of 0.50 T and travel through semicircles to a detector. Calculate the radius of the path for an ion of mass 20 u. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the distance between the points where ions of mass 20 u and 22 u reach the detector. **[2]**

**9.** A cyclotron accelerates protons. Its magnetic field is 0.60 T and its dees have radius 0.35 m.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency of the alternating p.d. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the maximum kinetic energy of the protons in MeV. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the frequency of the alternating p.d. does not need to change as the protons speed up. **[1]**

**10.** A beam of particles travelling east enters a region where the magnetic field points vertically downwards. The beam curves towards the north. Deduce the sign of the charge on the particles, explaining your reasoning. **[2]**

**11.** Electrons with kinetic energy 3.0 keV are to travel in a circle of radius 0.10 m.

**(a)** Calculate their speed. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the flux density needed. **[2]**

**12.** In a cyclotron, a student suggests doubling the alternating p.d. across the gap to double the final energy of the particles. Evaluate this suggestion. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The force is always perpendicular to the velocity [1], so no work is done on the electron and its kinetic energy (and so speed) is unchanged; only the direction changes [1]. **[2]**

**2.** F = BQv = 0.25 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 4.0 × 10⁵ [1] = **1.6 × 10⁻¹⁴ N** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** **B** [1]. The equivalent conventional current is to the left; with the field into the page the thumb points down the page. **[1]**

**4.** BQv = mv²/r, so r = mv/(BQ) [1]. T = 2πr/v [1] = 2πm/(BQ), in which v does not appear [1]. **[3]**

**5.** r = mv/(BQ) = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 2.0 × 10⁷ / (5.0 × 10⁻³ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) [1] = **0.023 m** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** r ∝ m/Q at the same speed and field [1]; ratio = (4/2) = **2** [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** eV = ½mv², so v² = 2eV/m [1]. r = mv/(eB), so r² = m²v²/(e²B²) = 2mV/(eB²) [1]. Rearranging: e/m = 2V/(B²r²) [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** e/m = 2 × 180 / ((0.90 × 10⁻³)² × 0.050²) = **1.8 × 10¹¹ C kg⁻¹** [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** r ∝ √V, so the radius increases by a factor of **√2** (about 1.4) [1]. **[1]**

**8. (a)** E = 400 / 0.0080 = 5.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹ [1]; v = E/B = 5.0 × 10⁴ / 0.050 = **1.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** r = mv/(BQ) = 20 × 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ × 1.0 × 10⁶ / (0.50 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) [1] = **0.415 m** [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** For 22 u, r = 0.4565 m [1]. The ions travel semicircles, so the landing points are a diameter from the entry point: separation = 2 × (0.4565 − 0.415) = **0.083 m** [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** f = BQ/(2πm) = 0.60 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ / (2π × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷) [1] = **9.1 × 10⁶ Hz** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** v = BQR/m = 0.60 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 0.35 / 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ = 2.01 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹ [1]; Ek = ½mv² = 3.38 × 10⁻¹³ J [1]; ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ J per MeV = **2.1 MeV** [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** The time for each semicircle, πm/(BQ), does not depend on speed, so the protons always return to the gap after the same interval [1]. **[1]**

**10.** Using Fleming's left-hand rule with the field down and the current east, the force on a positive charge is north [1]. The beam curves north, so the particles are **positive** [1]. **[2]**

**11. (a)** Ek = 3.0 × 10³ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 4.8 × 10⁻¹⁶ J [1]; v = √(2Ek/m) = **3.2 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** B = mv/(er) = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 3.25 × 10⁷ / (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 0.10) [1] = **1.8 × 10⁻³ T** [1]. **[2]**

**12.** The maximum kinetic energy is B²Q²R²/(2m), set by the field and the dee radius [1], so doubling the p.d. does not change the final energy [1]. It only halves the number of revolutions needed to reach that energy; to raise the energy, increase B or R [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Following an electron's velocity, rather than the opposite direction, with Fleming's rule.
- Using e for the charge of an alpha particle or a doubly charged ion.
- Using the radius rather than the diameter to find where an ion lands after a semicircle.
- Leaving mT or keV unconverted.
- Stating that the cyclotron frequency rises as particles speed up.
