These questions cover sub-topic 4(c) Alkanes of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024): points 4.19 to 4.22, none of which has a C reference. Use Ar values: H = 1, C = 12, Cl = 35.5. Draw structures on paper, showing every bond in displayed formulae. Revise with the [alkanes study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-alkanes/) and the [organic chemistry revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-organic-chemistry-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** State the general formula of the alkanes. **[1]**

**2.** Give the molecular formula of the alkane with 16 carbon atoms. **[1]**

**3.** Name the alkane with the formula C₅H₁₂ that has an unbranched chain. **[1]**

**4.** State what is meant by a saturated hydrocarbon. **[2]**

**5.** State the condition needed for methane to react with bromine. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** An alkane has a relative molecular mass of 100.

**(a)** Use the general formula to calculate the number of carbon atoms in a molecule. **[2]**
**(b)** Give its molecular formula. **[1]**

**7.** The table shows the boiling points of some alkanes.

| Alkane | Butane | Pentane |
|---|---|---|
| Formula | C₄H₁₀ | C₅H₁₂ |
| Boiling point / °C | -1 | 36 |

**(a)** State the physical state of each alkane at 20 °C. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why pentane has a higher boiling point than butane. **[2]**

**8.** Butane, C₄H₁₀, has an isomer.

**(a)** Draw the displayed formula of butane. **[2]**
**(b)** Draw the structural formula of its branched isomer and name it. **[2]**

**9.** Methane reacts with bromine in ultraviolet radiation.

**(a)** Write the balanced equation for the reaction, naming the products. **[2]**
**(b)** State what type of reaction this is and explain your answer. **[2]**
**(c)** State what you would see happening to the bromine. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain what would be seen in the same mixture kept in the dark. **[1]**

**10.** Propane reacts with chlorine in ultraviolet radiation to form 1-chloropropane (C₃H₇Cl, Mr = 78.5) and hydrogen chloride.

**(a)** Write the balanced equation. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the moles of propane (Mr = 44) in 8.8 g. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the maximum mass of C₃H₇Cl that can be made from 8.8 g of propane. **[2]**
**(d)** The actual yield is 70%. Calculate the mass obtained. **[1]**

**11.** Explain why alkanes do not decolourise bromine water at room temperature in the dark. **[2]**

**12.** A student writes that the reaction between ethane and chlorine in sunlight is an addition reaction giving only chloroethane. Identify the two errors. **[2]**

**13.** 3.0 g of ethane, C₂H₆ (Mr = 30), burns completely in excess oxygen.

**(a)** Write the balanced equation. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the moles of ethane. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide formed at room temperature and pressure (molar volume 24 dm³). **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the mass of water formed. **[1]**

**14.** Hexane, C₆H₁₄, has a longer chain than pentane, which boils at 36 °C. Predict whether hexane is a gas or a liquid at 20 °C and explain your prediction. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ [1].

**2.** C₁₆H₃₄ [1].

**3.** Pentane [1].

**4.** A hydrocarbon in which all the carbon-carbon bonds are single bonds [1] so each carbon is bonded to the maximum number of other atoms (hydrogen) [1].

**5.** Ultraviolet radiation (sunlight) [1].

**6. (a)** 14n + 2 = 100 [1], so n = 98 / 14 = 7 [1].
**(b)** C₇H₁₆ [1].

**7. (a)** Butane is a gas [1]; pentane is a liquid [1].
**(b)** Pentane molecules are longer, so the intermolecular forces between them are stronger [1]; more energy is needed to overcome them [1].

**8. (a)** Four carbon atoms in a chain, with each carbon bonded to hydrogens to give four bonds per carbon (10 C-H bonds, 3 C-C bonds in total) [2].
**(b)** CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃ (a CH carbon bonded to three CH₃ groups) [1]; 2-methylpropane [1].

**9. (a)** CH₄ + Br₂ → CH₃Br + HBr [1]; bromomethane and hydrogen bromide [1].
**(b)** Substitution [1], because a hydrogen atom is replaced by a bromine atom and two products are formed [1].
**(c)** The red-brown colour of bromine fades (becomes colourless) [1].
**(d)** No change; the bromine colour stays, because UV radiation is needed to start the reaction [1].

**10. (a)** C₃H₈ + Cl₂ → C₃H₇Cl + HCl [1].
**(b)** 8.8 / 44 = 0.20 mol [1].
**(c)** Moles of C₃H₇Cl = 0.20 mol (1 : 1) [1]; mass = 0.20 x 78.5 = 15.7 g [1].
**(d)** 70% x 15.7 = 11.0 g [1].

**11.** Alkanes have only single bonds, so they are saturated and cannot undergo addition reactions with bromine [1]; without UV radiation there is no substitution either, so the orange colour remains [1].

**12.** Error 1: the reaction is substitution, not addition [1]. Error 2: hydrogen chloride is also formed, so there are two products [1].

**13. (a)** 2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O [1].
**(b)** 3.0 / 30 = 0.10 mol [1].
**(c)** Moles of CO₂ = 0.20 mol; volume = 0.20 x 24 = 4.8 dm³ [1].
**(d)** Moles of H₂O = 0.30 mol; mass = 0.30 x 18 = 5.4 g [1].

**14.** Liquid [1], because hexane molecules are longer than pentane, so the intermolecular forces are stronger and its boiling point is higher than 36 °C (well above 20 °C) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using CₙH₂ₙ instead of CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.
- Leaving out UV radiation from the conditions.
- Drawing carbon atoms with five bonds, or leaving out hydrogens.
- Writing "addition" for the halogen reaction.
- Forgetting the hydrogen halide product.
- Explaining boiling point trends by "bond strength" instead of intermolecular forces.
