Recall notes for **4.2.4 Energy transfers** (4.2.4.1 to 4.2.4.3) in AQA GCSE Physics (8463). Paper 1, both
tiers.

Worked examples and full explanations are in the [energy transfers study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-energy-transfers-aqa-electricity/).
Practise with the [energy transfers practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-energy-transfers-aqa-electricity-practice/).

## Equations to recall

| Equation | Words | Units |
|---|---|---|
| P = V I | power = potential difference × current | W, V, A |
| P = I² R | power = current² × resistance | W, A, Ω |
| E = P t | energy transferred = power × time | J, W, s |
| E = Q V | energy transferred = charge flow × potential difference | J, C, V |

Prefixes: 1 kW = 1000 W; 1 MW = 1 000 000 W; 1 kJ = 1000 J; 1 kV = 1000 V.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Power | Rate of energy transfer; 1 W = 1 J/s |
| Electrical work | Energy transferred when charge flows in a circuit |
| Power rating | The power of an appliance in normal use: the energy it transfers each second |
| National Grid | System of cables and transformers linking power stations to consumers |
| Step-up transformer | Increases the pd from the power station to the transmission cables |
| Step-down transformer | Decreases the pd to a much lower value for domestic use |

## Core ideas

- Energy transferred by an appliance depends on its **power** and **how long** it is on.
- A device's power depends on the **pd across it** and the **current through it**.
- Work is done when charge flows; pd is the energy transferred per coulomb (from E = QV).
- In P = I²R, heating power depends on current **squared**: double I, four times the heating.
- Appliances transfer energy from **batteries** or the **ac mains** to the **kinetic energy of motors** or to
  **heating devices**.
- Higher power rating → larger change in stored energy each second.

## Picking the equation

- Given V and I → P = VI.
- Given I and R → P = I²R.
- Given P and t → E = Pt.
- Given Q and V → E = QV.
- Given I, V and t → find P or Q first, then E.

## Worked reminders

- 230 V, 13 A appliance: P = **2990 W** (about 3.0 kW).
- 2.0 kW heater for 15 minutes: E = 2000 × 900 = **1 800 000 J** (1.8 MJ).
- 0.25 A through 40 Ω: P = 0.0625 × 40 = **2.5 W**.
- 500 C through 12 V: E = **6000 J**.
- 60 W lamp transferring 54 kJ: t = 54 000 ÷ 60 = **900 s** (15 minutes).

## The National Grid in four links

1. Step-up transformers raise the pd for transmission.
2. For the same power (P = VI), a higher pd means a lower current.
3. Lower current means much less heating of the cables (heating power I²R).
4. Less energy is wasted, so the system is efficient. Step-down transformers then reduce the pd for safe
   domestic use.

Ratio rule: multiply the transmission pd by n and the current falls to 1/n, so the heating loss falls to 1/n².

## Appliance transfers

| Appliance | Main useful transfer |
|---|---|
| Fan, food mixer, washing-machine drum | Mains → kinetic energy of a motor |
| Kettle, toaster, heater | Mains → heating (thermal energy store) |
| Torch, phone, cordless drill | Battery chemical store → light, or the kinetic energy of a motor |

## Exam traps

- Squaring trap: P = I²R squares the **current** only.
- Time trap: E = Pt needs seconds; 1 hour = 3600 s.
- Prefix trap: 2.5 kW is 2500 W, not 2.5 W.
- Transformer trap: step-up **raises pd and lowers current**; never "raises current".
- Vague trap: "less energy lost" earns little without the current and heating links.
- Charge versus energy: Q is in coulombs, E in joules.

## Self-test

1. State the unit of power and what 1 unit means.
2. A 12 V lamp draws 2.5 A. Calculate its power.
3. Calculate the power when 0.40 A flows through a 25 Ω resistor.
4. A 1.2 kW microwave runs for 4.0 minutes. Calculate the energy transferred.
5. 80 C flows through a 9.0 V battery. Calculate the energy transferred.
6. A 2.4 kW kettle runs on 230 V. Calculate the current (2 s.f.).
7. Describe the useful energy transfer in an electric fan.
8. Name the device that increases the pd between a power station and the transmission cables.
9. Explain why transmitting at high pd reduces energy losses.
10. If the current in a resistor is tripled, by what factor does its heating power change?
11. A motor transfers 4500 J in 90 s. Calculate its power.
12. Why is the pd reduced before it reaches homes?

### Answers

1. The watt (W); 1 W is 1 joule transferred per second.
2. P = 12 × 2.5 = **30 W**.
3. P = 0.40² × 25 = 0.16 × 25 = **4.0 W**.
4. E = 1200 × 240 = **288 000 J** (288 kJ).
5. E = 80 × 9.0 = **720 J**.
6. I = 2400 ÷ 230 = **10 A** (2 s.f.).
7. Energy from the mains (or battery) to the kinetic energy of the motor and blades.
8. A step-up transformer.
9. For the same power a higher pd means a smaller current, and a smaller current heats the cables much less, so
   less energy is wasted.
10. It increases nine times (3²).
11. P = 4500 ÷ 90 = **50 W**.
12. A much lower pd is needed for safe domestic use.
