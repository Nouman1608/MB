These questions cover section 3.3.5 Refraction and total internal reflection, statements a to h, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) specification, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Take the speed of light in a vacuum or air as 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s. For help, see the [refraction study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-refraction-and-total-internal-reflection-oxfordaqa/) and the [refraction revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-refraction-and-total-internal-reflection-oxfordaqa-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** A ray of light passes from air into glass along the normal. State what happens to its speed and to its direction. **[2]**

**2.** Which statement about light entering glass from air at an angle is correct?

A: Its frequency decreases.

B: Its speed increases.

C: Its wavelength decreases.

D: It bends away from the normal.

**[1]**

**3.** Light travels at 1.24 × 10⁸ m/s in diamond. Calculate the refractive index of diamond. **[2]**

**4.** A ray in air strikes the surface of water (n = 1.33) with an angle of incidence of 48°. Calculate the angle of refraction. **[3]**

**5.** A transparent plastic has a critical angle of 43.2°. Calculate its refractive index. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why light travelling from air into glass can never be totally internally reflected. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Straight water waves in a ripple tank travel from deep water into shallow water, where they move more slowly. The wavefronts meet the boundary at a slant.

**(a)** Describe how the wavelength and frequency change as the waves enter the shallow water. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain, in terms of the wavefronts, why the direction of the waves changes. **[3]**

**8.** A narrow beam of white light passes through a triangular glass prism and a spectrum is seen on a screen.

**(a)** Name this effect. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the colours separate. **[2]**

**(c)** State which colour is deviated most. **[1]**

**9.** A student investigates refraction in a transparent block. She obtains these results.

| i (°) | 25 | 35 | 45 | 55 | 65 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| r (°) | 17 | 23 | 28 | 33 | 37 |

**(a)** Calculate sin i ÷ sin r for i = 65°. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how she could use a graph to find the refractive index more reliably. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest one way to make her measurements of r more accurate. **[1]**

**10.** A glass has a refractive index of 1.46.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of light in this glass. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the critical angle of the glass. **[2]**

**(c)** Light inside the glass meets the glass–air boundary at 30° to the normal. Calculate the angle of refraction in air. **[2]**

**(d)** State what happens when the angle in part (c) is increased to 50°, and explain why. **[2]**

**11.** Light inside a diamond (n = 2.42) meets the diamond–air surface with an angle of incidence of 30°.

**(a)** Show that the light cannot refract out of the diamond at this angle. **[2]**

**(b)** State what happens to the light instead. **[1]**

**(c)** Use your answer to suggest why a cut diamond appears to sparkle. **[2]**

**12.** Optical fibres are used in endoscopes and in communication networks.

**(a)** Explain how light travels along a curved optical fibre. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe how an endoscope is used and give one advantage of using it. **[2]**

**(c)** Give two benefits of optical fibres over copper cables for carrying communication signals. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Speed decreases [1]; direction does not change [1]. **[2]**

**2.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* Frequency is fixed by the source. The wave slows, so with the same frequency the wavelength must be shorter (v = f λ).

**3.** n = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.24 × 10⁸ [1] = **2.42** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** sin r = sin 48° ÷ 1.33 [1] = 0.7431 ÷ 1.33 = 0.5588 [1]; r = **34.0°** [1]. **[3]**

**5.** n = 1 ÷ sin 43.2° [1] = 1 ÷ 0.6845 = **1.46** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Total internal reflection needs light to travel from a denser to a less dense medium [1]. Light entering glass from air is going into a denser medium, so it always refracts in, bending towards the normal [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Wavelength decreases [1]; frequency stays the same [1].

**(b)** One end of each wavefront reaches the shallow water first [1] and slows down while the rest of the wavefront is still moving faster [1]. The wavefront turns, so the direction of travel bends towards the normal [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** Dispersion [1].

**(b)** Each colour travels at a different speed in glass [1], so each is refracted by a different amount (each has a slightly different refractive index) [1].

**(c)** Violet [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** sin 65° ÷ sin 37° = 0.9063 ÷ 0.6018 [1] = **1.51** [1].

**(b)** Plot sin i on the vertical axis against sin r on the horizontal axis and draw a line of best fit through the origin [1]. The gradient of the line is the refractive index [1].

**(c)** Any one: use a narrower ray; mark the emergent ray with crosses far apart; use a sharp pencil and a protractor with a fine scale; repeat each reading and take a mean [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* The graph method uses all the results at once and shows up any anomalous point, which is why it is more reliable than one ratio.

**10. (a)** v = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.46 [1] = **2.05 × 10⁸ m/s** [1].

**(b)** sin c = 1 ÷ 1.46 = 0.6849 [1]; c = **43.2°** [1].

**(c)** sin(angle in air) = 1.46 × sin 30° = 0.730 [1]; angle = **46.9°** [1].

**(d)** Total internal reflection occurs [1], because 50° is greater than the critical angle of 43.2° [1]. **[8]**

*Examiner insight:* In part (c) the angle in air must be the larger one. If you get an angle smaller than 30°, you have the equation upside down.

**11. (a)** sin(angle in air) would be 2.42 × sin 30° = 1.21 [1]. The sine of an angle cannot exceed 1, so no refracted ray exists (equivalently, the critical angle is 24.4° and 30° is greater) [1].

**(b)** It is totally internally reflected [1].

**(c)** The critical angle of diamond is very small, so much of the light entering it is totally internally reflected several times [1] before leaving through the top faces, and dispersion spreads it into colours, so bright flashes are seen [1]. **[5]**

**12. (a)** Light enters the fibre and strikes the inside surface at an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle [1]. It is totally internally reflected [1], and this repeats many times so the light follows the fibre round the bend [1].

**(b)** The endoscope is passed into the body through a small cut or natural opening; one set of fibres carries light in and another carries an image back so the doctor can see inside [1]. Advantage: the patient avoids major surgery, so recovery is quicker (or less pain or scarring) [1].

**(c)** Any two: can carry much more information; little energy is lost over long distances; signals are not affected by electrical interference; fibres are thinner and lighter [1] [1]. **[7]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the exit equation with the angle inside the glass on top, giving an angle in air that is too small.
- Omitting one of the two conditions for total internal reflection.
- Saying frequency changes on refraction.
- Explaining refraction with "the light is denser" instead of a change in speed.
- Giving a unit for refractive index.
- Describing an optical fibre application without a benefit when the question asks for both.
