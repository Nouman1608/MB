specification (for teaching from September 2016). Paper 2 content for both tiers; nothing is Higher tier only.
For explanations use the
[common atmospheric pollutants study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-common-atmospheric-pollutants-and-their-sources/);

## Core facts

- Burning fuels is a major source of atmospheric pollutants.
- Most fuels contain **carbon and/or hydrogen**, and may contain **sulfur**.
- Gases released may include **carbon dioxide, water vapour, carbon monoxide, sulfur dioxide, oxides of
  nitrogen**.
- Solid particles and **unburned hydrocarbons** form **particulates**.

## Where each pollutant comes from

| Product | Cause |
|---|---|
| Carbon dioxide + water | Complete combustion (plenty of oxygen) |
| Carbon monoxide | Incomplete combustion (limited oxygen) |
| Soot (carbon) | Incomplete combustion (very limited oxygen) |
| Sulfur dioxide | Sulfur impurities in the fuel burn |
| Oxides of nitrogen | Nitrogen and oxygen **from the air** react at high temperature in engines |
| Particulates | Soot, ash, unburned hydrocarbons |

## Equations

| Reaction | Equation |
|---|---|
| Complete combustion of methane | CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O |
| Incomplete combustion (carbon monoxide) | 2CH₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO + 4H₂O |
| Incomplete combustion (soot) | CH₄ + O₂ → C + 2H₂O |
| Sulfur burning | S + O₂ → SO₂ |
| Nitrogen oxide in engines | N₂ + O₂ → 2NO |

## Effects

| Pollutant | Effect |
|---|---|
| Carbon monoxide | **Toxic**; **colourless and odourless**, so hard to detect; reduces oxygen carried in blood |
| Sulfur dioxide | **Respiratory problems**; **acid rain** |
| Oxides of nitrogen | **Respiratory problems**; **acid rain** |
| Particulates | **Global dimming**; **health problems** (lungs) |

## Acid rain

- SO₂ and nitrogen oxides dissolve in rain to form **acidic solutions**.
- Damages **buildings** (limestone, marble), **trees and plants**, and makes **lakes** too acidic for wildlife.

## Predicting products

| Fuel | Conditions | Products |
|---|---|---|
| Natural gas (C, H only) | Plenty of oxygen | CO₂ + H₂O |
| Natural gas | Limited oxygen | CO and/or C + H₂O |
| Coal (C, S) | Plenty of oxygen | CO₂ + SO₂ (+ particulates) |
| Petrol in car engine | Hot engine | CO₂, H₂O, plus possibly CO, NOₓ, unburned hydrocarbons, soot |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pollutant | Substance in the air that causes harm |
| Complete combustion | Burning in plenty of oxygen |
| Incomplete combustion | Burning with too little oxygen |
| Particulates | Tiny solid particles in the air |
| Acid rain | Rain made acidic by dissolved sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides |
| Global dimming | Reduction in sunlight reaching the surface |

## Exam traps

- **Nitrogen oxides from the fuel.** They come from the air.
- **Sulfur dioxide from the air.** It comes from sulfur in the fuel.
- **Carbon dioxide as acid rain gas.** Not in this section's list.
- **Carbon monoxide "smells".** It is odourless.
- **Soot as CO.** Soot is carbon, C.
- **Particulates and the greenhouse effect.** They cause global dimming.
- **No condition stated.** Link the product to the oxygen supply or temperature.

## Quick self-test

1. Name five gases that may be released when a fuel burns.
2. What is formed in complete combustion of a hydrocarbon?
3. Why does carbon monoxide form in some fires and engines?
4. Name the black solid formed by incomplete combustion.
5. Where does the sulfur dioxide from burning coal come from?
6. How are oxides of nitrogen formed in a car engine?
7. Why is carbon monoxide hard to detect?
8. State two problems caused by sulfur dioxide.
9. State two problems caused by particulates.
10. Write the equation for sulfur burning in oxygen.
11. Write the equation for the incomplete combustion of methane to carbon monoxide and water.
12. Coal (carbon and sulfur) is burned in limited oxygen. Predict four products.

### Answers

1. Any five: carbon dioxide, water vapour, carbon monoxide, sulfur dioxide, oxides of nitrogen (also soot, unburned hydrocarbons).
2. Carbon dioxide and water.
3. There is not enough oxygen for complete combustion.
4. Soot (carbon).
5. Sulfur in the coal.
6. Nitrogen and oxygen from the air react at the high temperature of the engine.
7. It is colourless and odourless.
8. Respiratory problems and acid rain.
9. Global dimming and health problems.
10. S + O₂ → SO₂.
11. 2CH₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO + 4H₂O.
12. Any four: carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, soot, sulfur dioxide, particulates.

## Worked answers to model

**Explain why a car engine produces oxides of nitrogen although petrol contains no nitrogen.**
"The temperature in the engine is very high, so nitrogen and oxygen from the air react together to form oxides of
nitrogen, for example N₂ + O₂ → 2NO."

**Explain why a gas fire with a blocked air vent is dangerous.**
"There is not enough oxygen for complete combustion, so carbon monoxide forms. Carbon monoxide is toxic and is
colourless and odourless, so people cannot detect it."

**Explain how burning coal causes acid rain.**
"Coal contains sulfur. The sulfur burns to form sulfur dioxide. This dissolves in rain water and makes it acidic."

## Pollutant, source and effect in one line

| Pollutant | Source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon monoxide | Incomplete combustion | Toxic; reduces oxygen carried by blood |
| Soot (carbon) | Incomplete combustion | Global dimming; lung problems |
| Sulfur dioxide | Sulfur in fuels | Respiratory problems; acid rain |
| Oxides of nitrogen | Air in hot engines | Respiratory problems; acid rain |
| Unburned hydrocarbons | Fuel not burned | Form particulates |

Use this pattern, pollutant then source then effect, in every "describe and explain" answer.
