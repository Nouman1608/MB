Condensed recall for section 4.3 (Symmetry) of the 4MA1 specification. This is Foundation Tier content that the Higher Tier also assumes. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [Symmetry study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-symmetry-edexcel-igcse-maths/); to test yourself under exam conditions, use the [Symmetry practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-symmetry-edexcel-igcse-maths-practice/).

The one statement to know: identify any lines of symmetry and the order of rotational symmetry of a given two-dimensional figure.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Line of symmetry | A line that splits a shape into two mirror-image halves |
| Mirror line | Another name for a line of symmetry |
| Rotational symmetry | The shape fits onto itself more than once in a full turn |
| Order of rotational symmetry | The number of positions in a 360° turn where the shape looks the same |
| Centre of rotation | The fixed point the shape is turned about |
| Angle of turn | The smallest rotation that maps the shape onto itself: 360° ÷ order |
| Regular polygon | All sides equal and all angles equal |

## Core facts

- Lowest possible order is **1**. Order 1 = no rotational symmetry. There is no "order 0".
- Angle of turn = **360° ÷ order**. Order 3 means 120°; order 8 means 45°.
- A regular polygon with **n** sides has **n** lines of symmetry and order **n**.
- Two or more lines of symmetry → rotational symmetry with order equal to the number of lines.
- Rotational symmetry does **not** guarantee a line of symmetry (parallelogram).
- A circle has infinitely many lines of symmetry (every diameter).

## Shape table

| Shape | Lines | Order |
|---|---|---|
| Equilateral triangle | 3 | 3 |
| Isosceles triangle | 1 | 1 |
| Scalene triangle | 0 | 1 |
| Square | 4 | 4 |
| Rectangle | 2 | 2 |
| Rhombus | 2 | 2 |
| Parallelogram | 0 | 2 |
| Kite | 1 | 1 |
| Isosceles trapezium | 1 | 1 |
| Trapezium (not isosceles) | 0 | 1 |
| Regular pentagon | 5 | 5 |
| Regular hexagon | 6 | 6 |
| Regular octagon | 8 | 8 |

Where the lines are:

- **Rectangle**: through the midpoints of opposite sides. Not the diagonals.
- **Rhombus**: the two diagonals. Not through midpoints of sides.
- **Square**: both of the above, so four lines.
- **Kite**: one diagonal only, the one joining the two vertices where equal sides meet.
- **Regular polygon, odd n**: each line joins a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side.
- **Regular polygon, even n**: half the lines join opposite vertices and half join midpoints of opposite sides.

## Quick checks

**Is a line a line of symmetry?** Pick a vertex, move at right angles to the line, and go the same distance beyond it. You must land on another vertex every time.

**What is the order?** Trace the shape, hold the centre still, turn through 360° and count the fits, including the starting position.

**Where is the centre for order 2?** The midpoint of any vertex and the vertex it swaps with. For a parallelogram, it is where the diagonals cross.

## On a coordinate grid

- Reflection in the vertical line x = a sends (x, y) to (2a − x, y).
- Reflection in the horizontal line y = b sends (x, y) to (x, 2b − y).
- A half turn about (p, q) sends (x, y) to (2p − x, 2q − y).

**Mini example.** A shape has vertices (2, 0), (6, 0), (7, 3) and (3, 3). Opposite sides go 4 across and 1 across 3 up, so it is a parallelogram (not a rhombus, since √10 ≠ 4). Lines of symmetry: 0. Order: 2. Centre: midpoint of (2, 0) and (7, 3) = (4.5, 1.5).

## Grid-shading tasks

For a 4 by 4 grid labelled A to D across and 1 to 4 up:

| Symmetry wanted | Swap rule |
|---|---|
| Order 2 about the centre | A ↔ D, B ↔ C and 1 ↔ 4, 2 ↔ 3 together |
| Vertical mirror down the middle | A ↔ D, B ↔ C, row unchanged |
| Horizontal mirror across the middle | 1 ↔ 4, 2 ↔ 3, column unchanged |

So for order 2, B1 pairs with C4; for a vertical mirror, B1 pairs with C1.

## Linking 4.2 and 4.3

```
order n (regular)  ->  n sides
exterior angle     =  360° ÷ n
interior angle     =  180° − exterior angle
```

Order 10 → exterior 36°, interior 144°. Interior 162° → exterior 18° → 20 sides → 20 lines, order 20.

## Exam traps

- Diagonals of a rectangle or parallelogram are **not** lines of symmetry.
- "Order 0" is never correct.
- A kite has **one** line of symmetry, not two.
- Giving the angle of turn (90°) when the question asks for the order (4).
- Forgetting that a point on the mirror line stays fixed when completing a shape.
- Shading an extra square in a grid task, which can break symmetry already present.
- Writing a description instead of a name: "parallelogram", not "a leaning rectangle".

## Self-test

1. How many lines of symmetry does a regular octagon have?
2. Write down the order of rotational symmetry of a kite.
3. Name a quadrilateral with no lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 2.
4. Name a quadrilateral with exactly two lines of symmetry that are its diagonals.
5. A shape has rotational symmetry of order 5. What is the smallest angle of turn that maps it onto itself?
6. A regular polygon has 18 lines of symmetry. Work out its exterior angle.
7. Does an isosceles trapezium have rotational symmetry? Give its order.
8. Reflect the point (1, 4) in the line x = 3.
9. A half turn about (2, 2) maps (0, 1) onto which point?
10. True or false: every shape with rotational symmetry of order 2 has two lines of symmetry.
11. A regular polygon has interior angles of 140°. How many lines of symmetry does it have?
12. In a 4 by 4 grid (A–D across, 1–4 up), which square must be shaded so that A2 is matched under a half turn about the centre?

### Answers

1. 8.
2. Order 1.
3. A parallelogram.
4. A rhombus (a square has four lines).
5. 360° ÷ 5 = 72°.
6. 18 sides, so 360° ÷ 18 = 20°.
7. No rotational symmetry: order 1 (it has one line of symmetry).
8. (2 × 3 − 1, 4) = (5, 4).
9. (2 × 2 − 0, 2 × 2 − 1) = (4, 3).
10. False: a parallelogram has order 2 and no lines of symmetry.
11. Exterior angle 40°, so 9 sides and 9 lines of symmetry.
12. A ↔ D and 2 ↔ 3, so D3.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does order of rotational symmetry 1 mean?**
It means the shape only fits onto itself after a full turn, so it has no rotational symmetry. Every shape has order at least 1.

**Are the diagonals of a rectangle lines of symmetry?**
No. Reflecting a corner in a diagonal of a non-square rectangle lands outside the shape, so a rectangle has only the two lines through the midpoints of opposite sides.

**Which quadrilateral has no lines of symmetry but rotational symmetry of order 2?**
A parallelogram that is not a rectangle or a rhombus. It maps onto itself after a half turn about the point where its diagonals cross.
