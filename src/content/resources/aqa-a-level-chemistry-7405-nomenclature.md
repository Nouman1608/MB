This guide teaches section **3.3.1.1 Nomenclature** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2 at AS and A-level. ## Types of formula

Use the example butan-2-ol.

| Type | What it shows | Butan-2-ol |
|---|---|---|
| Empirical formula | Simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element | C₄H₁₀O (already simplest) |
| Molecular formula | Actual number of atoms of each element | C₄H₁₀O |
| General formula | Algebraic formula for a homologous series | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH for alcohols |
| Structural formula | Shows the arrangement of atoms in groups, without showing every bond | CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ |
| Displayed formula | Shows every atom and every bond | All C–H, C–C, C–O and O–H bonds drawn out |
| Skeletal formula | Shows the carbon skeleton as lines, with functional groups; carbon atoms at the ends and corners, hydrogen on carbon not shown | Zig-zag of four lines with an OH on the second carbon |

Empirical formula example: a compound with molecular formula C₆H₁₂O₂ has empirical formula C₃H₆O, because the ratio 6 : 12 : 2 simplifies to 3 : 6 : 1.

In a skeletal formula, each vertex and each end of a line is a carbon atom. Hydrogen atoms attached to carbon are not drawn, but you must know they are there to count the formula. Atoms other than carbon and hydrogen, and hydrogen on them (such as the H in OH), are drawn.

## Homologous series

A homologous series is a family of compounds with the **same functional group**. Members have these characteristics:

- the same functional group and therefore similar chemical properties;
- the same general formula;
- each member differs from the next by a –CH₂– group;
- gradual change in physical properties (for example, boiling point increases with chain length).

| Series | Functional group | General formula | Suffix or prefix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alkanes | C–C single bonds only | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | -ane |
| Alkenes | C=C | CₙH₂ₙ | -ene |
| Halogenoalkanes | –F, –Cl, –Br, –I | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁X | fluoro-, chloro-, bromo-, iodo- |
| Alcohols | –OH | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH | -ol |
| Aldehydes | –CHO | CₙH₂ₙO | -al |
| Ketones | C=O between carbons | CₙH₂ₙO | -one |
| Carboxylic acids | –COOH | CₙH₂ₙO₂ | -oic acid |

A **functional group** is the atom or group of atoms that is responsible for the characteristic reactions of a compound.

## IUPAC naming

### Stem names

| Carbons | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stem | meth | eth | prop | but | pent | hex |

For a ring, add **cyclo** before the stem: cyclohexane, cyclopentene.

### The rules, in order

1. Find the **longest continuous carbon chain** that includes the functional group. This gives the stem.
2. Identify the **principal functional group** and use its suffix (for example -ol, -oic acid).
3. **Number the chain** from the end that gives the lowest number to the principal functional group. If there is no principal group, give the lowest numbers to the substituents (side chains).
4. Name **substituents** with a prefix (methyl, ethyl, chloro, bromo) and give each a number.
5. Use **di-, tri-, tetra-** when the same substituent occurs more than once, with a number for each.
6. List prefixes in **alphabetical order**, ignoring di-, tri-, and so on.
7. Write numbers with commas between them and hyphens between a number and a letter.

### Order of priority of functional groups

Carboxylic acid, then aldehyde, ketone, alcohol, alkene. Halogens and alkyl groups are always prefixes. The highest-priority group gets the suffix and the lowest number.

### Aldehydes, acids and ketones

- Aldehyde –CHO is always on carbon 1, so no number is needed: propanal, not propan-1-al.
- Carboxylic acid –COOH is always on carbon 1: butanoic acid. The carbon of the –COOH counts in the chain.
- Ketones need a number from pentanone upwards: pentan-2-one and pentan-3-one are different. Propanone and butanone need no number.

## Worked examples: from structure to name

### Worked example 1: branched alkane

CH₃CH(CH₃)CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃

- Longest chain: five carbons, pentane.
- Methyl groups on carbons 2 and 3 (numbering from the left).
- Name: **2,3-dimethylpentane**.

### Worked example 2: halogenoalkane

CH₃CH₂CH(Cl)CH₃

- Longest chain: four carbons, butane.
- Chlorine on carbon 2 (numbering from the right gives 3, so number from the left, giving 2).
- Name: **2-chlorobutane**.

### Worked example 3: alcohol with a side chain

CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₂OH

- Longest chain that includes the –OH: four carbons, butane with -ol: butan-1-ol.
- The –OH carbon is C1; the methyl is on C3.
- Name: **3-methylbutan-1-ol**.

### Worked example 4: alkene

CH₃C(CH₃)=CHCH₃

- Longest chain including the C=C: four carbons.
- Number from the end that gives the double bond the lowest number: from the right, C=C starts at carbon 2.
- Methyl on carbon 2.
- Name: **2-methylbut-2-ene**.

### Worked example 5: two functional groups

CH₃CH(OH)CH(Cl)CH₃

- The alcohol takes priority and gets the suffix.
- Number to give –OH the lowest number: –OH on C2, Cl on C3.
- Name: **3-chlorobutan-2-ol**.

### Worked example 6: ring

A six-membered ring with a –OH on one carbon and a methyl on the next carbon is **2-methylcyclohexanol**. The ring carbon carrying the –OH is C1 (no number needed for the –OH because the ring carbon with the principal group is numbered 1), and the substituent takes the next lowest number.

## Worked examples: from name to structure

### Worked example 7: draw 2,2-dimethylbutane

1. Butane: C–C–C–C.
2. Two methyl groups on C2.
3. Add hydrogens so each carbon has four bonds.
4. Structural formula: (CH₃)₃CCH₂CH₃. Molecular formula C₆H₁₄, which fits CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ with n = 6.

### Worked example 8: draw 3-bromobutanoic acid

1. Butanoic acid: four carbons with –COOH as C1: HOOC–C–C–C.
2. Bromine on C3.
3. Structural formula: CH₃CH(Br)CH₂COOH. Molecular formula C₄H₇BrO₂.

### Worked example 9: draw pent-2-ene

1. Five carbons with C=C between C2 and C3.
2. Structural formula: CH₃CH=CHCH₂CH₃. Molecular formula C₅H₁₀, which fits CₙH₂ₙ.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing the longest chain without the functional group in it.
- Numbering from the wrong end. The principal functional group gets the lowest number.
- Leaving out a position number that is needed, such as writing methylbutane instead of 2-methylbutane.
- Writing prefixes in the wrong order: ethyl before methyl, bromo before chloro, and ignoring "di" when alphabetising.
- Forgetting commas between numbers and hyphens between numbers and letters.
- Adding a number to an aldehyde or carboxylic acid.
- Treating the –OH group as a prefix when an alcohol is the principal group.
- Drawing a skeletal formula with the wrong number of carbon atoms.
- Using a branched alkane's longest chain wrongly when the chain bends round a corner.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Name**: give the full IUPAC name, with numbers and hyphens.
- **Draw**: use the type of formula asked for. A skeletal formula shows no C or H on carbon.
- **State the empirical formula**: simplify the molecular formula to the lowest whole-number ratio.
- **Give the general formula**: write CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH (and similar) for the series.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does the name ending change with the functional group?**
The ending shows the principal functional group. The ending tells you what homologous series the compound belongs to.

**Do I need to number the bromine in bromoethane?**
No. In a two-carbon chain, the positions are equivalent, so no number is needed.

**Why do skeletal formulas not show hydrogen on carbon?**
They are a shorthand. You calculate the number of hydrogen atoms from the fact that every carbon must have four bonds.
