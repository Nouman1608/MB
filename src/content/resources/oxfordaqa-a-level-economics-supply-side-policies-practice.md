## Questions

**1.** A freight firm in Country W installs routing software of its own choice. Deliveries per driver rise 8%. State whether this is a supply-side policy and explain your answer. **[2]**

**2.** In Country W, income tax is 25% on income above a 6,000 cu tax-free allowance. A worker earns 20,000 cu.

**(a)** Calculate the tax paid. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the tax saved under each reform: raising the allowance to 8,000 cu, or cutting the rate to 22%. **[2]**
**(c)** State which reform gives this worker a bigger incentive to earn an extra 1,000 cu, and give the evidence. **[1]**

**3.** Explain how government spending on education and training may shift the long-run aggregate supply curve to the right. **[4]**

**4.** Capital spending on roads is 2.7 billion cu this year, when the price index is 108 (base year = 100). Last year's spending was 2.25 billion cu in base-year prices.

**(a)** Convert this year's spending to real terms. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in real spending. **[2]**
**(c)** Give one reason why the spending will not raise potential output at once. **[1]**

**5.** Country W has a labour force of 4.0 million. Structural unemployment is 160,000. A retraining scheme moves 12.5% of the structurally unemployed into jobs.

**(a)** Calculate the structural unemployment rate before the scheme. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the number and the rate after the scheme. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the scheme may also reduce inflationary pressure. **[3]**

**6.** In a model of Country W, AD is Y = 1,260 − 3P and SRAS is Y = 420 + 5P, where Y is real output in billion cu and P is the price index.

**(a)** Find the equilibrium price level and output. **[2]**
**(b)** Supply-side policies shift SRAS to Y = 468 + 5P. Find the new equilibrium. **[2]**
**(c)** Work out how far the price level and real output have each moved, as percentages. **[2]**

**7.** Country W exports goods worth 520 million cu and imports goods worth 560 million cu. Supply-side policies raise exports by 7% and cut imports by 3%.

**(a)** State the original goods balance. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the new exports, imports and balance. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the current account as a whole might still be in deficit. **[2]**

**8.** A weekly job pays 200 cu. Out-of-work support is withdrawn at 70% of earnings above 50 cu. A reform cuts the withdrawal rate to 50%.

**(a)** Calculate the net gain from taking the job before the reform. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate it after the reform, and the percentage increase. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate whether this reform will reduce unemployment. **[4]**

**9.** The government of Country W gives 150 million cu in subsidies to a new-technology sector, linked to 2,000 jobs. 35% of these jobs would have existed anyway.

**(a)** Calculate the cost per net job. **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate industrial policy as a way to raise potential output. **[6]**

**10.** Country W (illustrative) introduced training schemes, deregulation and a lower corporation tax over five years.

| | Year 1 | Year 5 |
|---|---|---|
| Unemployment rate | 7.5% | 6.0% |
| Inflation rate | 3.8% | 2.6% |
| Current account deficit (% of GDP) | 4.0% | 3.1% |

**(a)** Calculate the change in the unemployment rate in percentage points and as a percentage. **[2]**
**(b)** To what extent do the data support the claim that supply-side policies explain the improvement in all three indicators for Country W? **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** It is not a supply-side policy because the firm chose to buy the software and the government took no action [1]. It is a supply-side improvement, a rise in productivity that started in the private sector [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers usually gain credit by separating who acted from what changed. Calling the rise a policy without naming government action is a common slip.

**2. (a)** Tax = 0.25 × (20,000 − 6,000) = **3,500 cu** [1].
**(b)** Allowance 8,000: 0.25 × 12,000 = 3,000, saving 500 [1]. Rate 22%: 0.22 × 14,000 = 3,080, saving 420 [1].
**(c)** The rate cut [1]. The worker keeps 78% of extra earnings instead of 75%, while a bigger allowance leaves the marginal rate at 25%. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both sets of figures. Stating the rate cut with no marginal-rate evidence is a bare assertion.

**3.** Spending on education and training raises the skills of workers (human capital) [1]. Workers with more skills get more done in each hour, which lifts productivity [1]. Better skills match more people to vacancies, reducing structural unemployment, so more labour is employed [1]. Higher productivity and employment raise potential output, so LRAS shifts right and the trend rate of growth can rise [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A full chain ends at the curve that moves. Answers that stop at "workers are better trained" leave the last link unmade.

**4. (a)** Real spending = 2.7 / 1.08 [1] = **2.5 billion cu** [1].
**(b)** 2.5 / 2.25 − 1 [1] = **11.1%** [1].
**(c)** Construction takes time, so the extra capacity appears only when the road opens [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Real terms means dividing by the price index over 100. Comparing 2.7 with 2.25 directly mixes money values with base-year values.

**5. (a)** 160,000 / 4,000,000 = **4.0%** [1].
**(b)** Moved into jobs = 0.125 × 160,000 = 20,000, leaving **140,000** [1]. Rate = 140,000 / 4,000,000 = **3.5%** [1].
**(c)** Retrained workers fill vacancies that employers could not fill before [1], so employers bid less aggressively for scarce skills, reducing wage pressure [1]. Higher productivity lowers unit labour costs, easing cost pressure on prices [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that explain the link from unemployment to wages or costs gain more than those that only state inflation will fall.

**6. (a)** 1,260 − 3P = 420 + 5P, so 8P = 840 [1]. **P = 105, Y = 945** [1].
**(b)** 1,260 − 3P = 468 + 5P, so 8P = 792 [1]. **P = 99, Y = 963** [1].
**(c)** Price level: 99 / 105 − 1 = **−5.7%** [1]. Output: 963 / 945 − 1 = **+1.9%** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the equation that sets AD equal to SRAS so your working is clear, then check the answer by substituting back into both equations.

**7. (a)** 520 − 560 = **−40 million cu**, a deficit [1].
**(b)** Exports = 520 × 1.07 = **556.4** [1]. Imports = 560 × 0.97 = **543.2** [1]. Balance = 556.4 − 543.2 = **+13.2 million cu**, a surplus [1].
**(c)** The current account also includes trade in services and income flows [1], and transfers. Any of these could be negative by more than 13.2, so the total could remain in deficit [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Relate the answer to the data given. Naming what else is in the current account, and saying its sign could outweigh the goods surplus, shows understanding.

**8. (a)** Benefit lost = 0.70 × (200 − 50) = 105 [1]. Net gain = 200 − 105 = **95 cu** [1].
**(b)** Lost = 0.50 × 150 = 75, so net gain = **125 cu** [1]. Increase = 125 / 95 − 1 = **31.6%** [1].
**(c)** For: a bigger reward for working makes job search and acceptance more attractive, so more unemployed people may take jobs [1], and it may encourage people outside the labour force to enter [1]. Against: a lower withdrawal rate costs the government more, since more households keep some support [1], and it cannot create jobs if there is too little demand or the skills do not match [1]. The effect is likely to be modest and slow unless vacancies exist. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong evaluation ends in a judgement tied to a condition, such as whether vacancies exist, rather than listing points on both sides.

**9. (a)** Gross cost per job = 150m / 2,000 = 75,000 cu [1]. Net jobs = 2,000 × 0.65 = 1,300 [1]. Cost per net job = 150m / 1,300 = **about 115,400 cu** [1].
**(b)** Industrial policy can raise capacity where private firms under-invest, for example in research with spillovers [1], and a young sector may need support while it builds scale [1]. It may attract private investment, raising the capital stock [1]. However, the government may back the wrong sector, as it lacks full information [1], and lobbying may distort decisions [1]. The 35% of jobs that would have existed anyway shows weak value for money. The case depends on spillovers being large and on the subsidy being time-limited [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Using the 115,400 figure in part (b) earns more than general comments. A judgement should say what the result depends on.

**10. (a)** 7.5 − 6.0 = **1.5 percentage points** [1]. 1.5 / 7.5 = **a 20% fall** [1].
**(b)** For: training and deregulation address structural unemployment without adding to demand-pull inflation [1], lower costs and higher productivity ease inflation [1], and better competitiveness narrows the deficit, as the fall from 4.0% to 3.1% suggests [1]. Lower corporation tax can raise investment and the capital stock [1]. Against: the data show association, not proof, as other factors may have changed [1]. Supply-side effects take years and the five-year change may be partly cyclical [1]. Some measures may cost revenue or raise inequality [1]. If unemployment is partly due to weak demand, demand-side policy may work faster [1]. Evidence from one country over five years cannot show which policy mattered [1]. Judgement: the data fit the claim in direction but cannot prove it. Supply-side policies are the likeliest source of lasting gains, since they target capacity, but they work best alongside steady demand [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the table figures in the argument, not only general theory. Judgements gain from naming the condition that decides the outcome.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Labelling private-sector improvements as government policy.
- Giving a calculation answer with no working, so the reasoning cannot be followed.
- Dividing by the price index instead of by the index over 100 in real-terms questions.
- Describing a tax cut as "more incentive" without naming whose incentive or which tax rate.
- Stopping an explanation before LRAS or SRAS moves.
- Treating a deficit in goods trade as the whole current account.
- Giving evaluation as a list, with no weighing and no conclusion.
- Overlooking deadweight, so reading gross costs as net.
- Claiming supply-side policy works fast.
