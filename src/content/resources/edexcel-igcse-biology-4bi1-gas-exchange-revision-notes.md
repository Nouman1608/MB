## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Diffusion | Net movement of particles from a higher to a lower concentration |
| Gas exchange | Taking in one gas and giving out another across a surface, by diffusion |
| Net exchange | The overall change once respiration and photosynthesis are added together |
| Stoma (plural stomata) | Pore in the leaf epidermis between two guard cells |
| Ventilation | Moving air into and out of the lungs |
| Alveolus (plural alveoli) | Tiny air sac at the end of a bronchiole; site of gas exchange |
| Pleural membranes | Membranes around the lungs and lining the thorax; fluid between them reduces friction |
| Emphysema | Breakdown of alveolar walls, reducing surface area |

## Flowering plants (2.40B–2.45B, Paper 2 only)

### 2.40B–2.41B Diffusion, respiration and photosynthesis

- All gas movement into and out of a leaf is by **diffusion** through stomata and air spaces.
- **Respiration**: all the time, all living cells. Takes in oxygen, gives out carbon dioxide.
- **Photosynthesis**: in the light only. Takes in carbon dioxide, gives out oxygen.
- Cells using a gas keep its concentration low inside the leaf, so the gradient is maintained.

### 2.42B Leaf adaptations

| Feature | Benefit |
|---|---|
| Thin | Short diffusion distance |
| Broad and flat | Large surface area |
| Air spaces in spongy mesophyll | Gases reach all cells; large internal surface area |
| Stomata, mostly on lower surface | Gases enter and leave |
| Moist cell walls | Gases dissolve, then diffuse into cells |

### 2.43B Stomata

- Guard cells open and close the pore.
- Usually **open in light** (carbon dioxide in for photosynthesis, oxygen out).
- Usually **closed in the dark** or when water is short (reduces water loss, but limits gas exchange).

### 2.44B Net exchange and light intensity

| Light | Comparison | Net result |
|---|---|---|
| Dark | Respiration only | Oxygen in, carbon dioxide out |
| Dim (balance point) | Photosynthesis rate = respiration rate | No net exchange |
| Bright | Photosynthesis faster | Carbon dioxide in, oxygen out |

### 2.45B Hydrogen-carbonate indicator practical

| Carbon dioxide level | Colour |
|---|---|
| Atmospheric | Red |
| Raised | Orange → yellow |
| Lowered | Magenta → purple |

**Method in steps**

1. Same volume of red indicator in identical sealed tubes.
2. Leaf on gauze above the liquid (not touching it).
3. Vary light: foil (dark), gauze layers (dim), uncovered (bright).
4. No-leaf control tube.
5. Same temperature, same distance from lamp, same time; compare colours.

## Humans (2.46–2.50)

### 2.46 The thorax

- Airway order: **trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli**.
- Ribs protect the lungs; **intercostal muscles** lie between the ribs.
- **Diaphragm**: muscle sheet below the lungs, separating thorax from abdomen.
- **Pleural membranes** with fluid between them reduce friction during breathing.

### 2.47 Ventilation in steps

**Inhalation**

1. External intercostal muscles contract; ribs move up and out.
2. Diaphragm contracts and flattens.
3. Thorax volume increases.
4. Pressure falls below atmospheric.
5. Air flows in.

**Exhalation**

1. External intercostal muscles relax; ribs move down and in (internal intercostals contract in forced exhalation).
2. Diaphragm relaxes and domes upwards.
3. Thorax volume decreases.
4. Pressure rises above atmospheric.
5. Air flows out.

### 2.48 Alveoli adaptations

| Feature | Why it speeds diffusion |
|---|---|
| Huge number of alveoli | Large surface area |
| Alveolar and capillary walls one cell thick | Short diffusion distance |
| Dense capillary network, constant blood flow | Keeps concentration gradient steep |
| Ventilation | Replaces air, keeps gradient steep |
| Moist lining | Gases dissolve before diffusing |

### 2.49 Smoking

| Component | Effect |
|---|---|
| Tar | Carcinogens → lung cancer; irritates airways |
| Cilia damaged, more mucus | Mucus not cleared → smoker's cough, bronchitis, chest infections |
| Emphysema | Alveolar walls break down → smaller surface area → breathlessness |
| Carbon monoxide | Binds to haemoglobin more readily than oxygen → blood carries less oxygen |
| Nicotine | Addictive; raises heart rate and blood pressure |

**Coronary heart disease chain:** fatty deposits narrow coronary arteries → clot blocks artery → heart muscle short of oxygen → cannot respire aerobically → heart attack. Smoking raises the risk.

### 2.50 Breathing practical

- **Limewater** turns cloudy with carbon dioxide. Exhaled air turns it cloudy faster than inhaled air.
- Approximate composition: inhaled air about 21% oxygen, 0.04% carbon dioxide; exhaled air about 16% oxygen, 4% carbon dioxide, warmer and more water vapour.
- **Exercise**: count breaths per minute at rest, exercise for a fixed time, count again; repeat for a mean. Control type, length and intensity of exercise.
- Exercise → faster respiration in muscles → more oxygen needed and more carbon dioxide made → faster, deeper breathing.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Respiration vs gas exchange.** Respiration is a chemical reaction in cells that releases energy. Gas exchange is the diffusion of gases across a surface. Breathing (ventilation) is a third thing: moving air.
- **"No gas exchange" vs "no net gas exchange".** At the balance point both processes still run; only the net change is zero.
- **Yellow vs purple.** Yellow = more carbon dioxide (respiration winning). Purple = less carbon dioxide (photosynthesis winning).
- **Contract vs relax.** Breathing in: intercostals and diaphragm **contract**. Normal breathing out: they **relax**.
- **Volume vs pressure.** Volume goes up, pressure goes down. They always change in opposite directions.
- **Tar vs carbon monoxide.** Tar damages the lungs. Carbon monoxide affects the blood.

## Small worked reminders

**Reading a day-and-night net exchange curve (Paper 2 only).** If a graph shows net carbon dioxide uptake by a plant over 24 hours, the curve is below zero at night (net release, respiration only), crosses zero twice (around dawn and dusk, when the two rates are equal) and peaks around midday, when light is brightest. The two crossing points are where the indicator would stay red.

**Building a six-mark answer on breathing during exercise.** Link each step: muscles contract more → faster respiration → more oxygen needed, more carbon dioxide produced → breathing rate and depth increase → more air ventilates the alveoli → concentration gradients stay steep → faster diffusion of oxygen into the blood and carbon dioxide out of it.

**Converting counts.** Breaths counted over part of a minute must be scaled: multiply a 30-second count by 2, a 15-second count by 4.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the process by which gases enter a leaf.
2. Which gas does a leaf give out in bright light?
3. A sealed tube of hydrogen-carbonate indicator with a leaf is kept in the dark. What colour does it turn, and why?
4. Give two features of a leaf that help gas exchange.
5. What is the function of the fluid between the pleural membranes?
6. What happens to the diaphragm when you breathe in?
7. A student counts 6 breaths in 20 seconds. What is the breathing rate in breaths per minute?
8. Inhaled air is about 21% oxygen and exhaled air about 16%. What percentage of the inhaled oxygen is removed? Give your answer to 1 decimal place.
9. Exhaled air contains about 4% carbon dioxide and inhaled air about 0.04%. How many times higher is the exhaled concentration?
10. Explain why emphysema makes a person breathless.
11. Which substance in tobacco smoke reduces the oxygen-carrying capacity of the blood?
12. Why does a no-leaf tube need to be included in the indicator practical?

### Answers

1. Diffusion (through the stomata).
2. Oxygen (net), because photosynthesis is faster than respiration.
3. Yellow. Only respiration happens, so carbon dioxide concentration rises.
4. Any two: thin; large surface area; air spaces in spongy mesophyll; many stomata; moist cell walls.
5. Reduces friction as the lungs move.
6. It contracts and flattens.
7. 6 × 3 = **18 breaths per minute**.
8. (21 − 16) / 21 × 100 = **23.8%**.
9. 4 / 0.04 = **100 times**.
10. Alveolar walls break down, so the surface area for gas exchange falls; less oxygen diffuses into the blood.
11. Carbon monoxide.
12. It is a control: it shows any colour change is caused by the leaf, not by light or the apparatus.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating that plants respire only at night, or photosynthesise "instead of" respiring during the day.
- Writing "no gas exchange" at the balance point instead of "no **net** gas exchange".
- Reversing indicator colours: yellow is high carbon dioxide, purple is low.
- Saying the leaf in the indicator tube was in the liquid. It sits on gauze above it.
- Describing inhalation without the volume and pressure steps, or putting them in the wrong order.
- Saying the diaphragm relaxes when breathing in.
- Giving an alveolus feature (for example, "thin walls") without the effect ("short diffusion distance").
- Saying carbon monoxide damages the alveoli. It binds to haemoglobin.
- Linking smoking to heart disease without mentioning the coronary arteries or oxygen supply to heart muscle.
- In the exercise practical, forgetting to repeat readings or to keep the exercise the same.

## Frequently asked questions

**What colour does hydrogen-carbonate indicator turn?**
Yellow means more carbon dioxide and purple means less carbon dioxide than in the starting red indicator.

**What happens during inhalation?**
The external intercostal muscles contract and the ribs move up and out, the diaphragm contracts and flattens, thorax volume increases, pressure falls below atmospheric and air flows in.

**What is the difference between respiration and gas exchange?**
Respiration is a chemical reaction in cells that releases energy. Gas exchange is the movement of gases across a surface by diffusion.

**What is the difference between tar and carbon monoxide in cigarette smoke?**
Tar damages the lungs and is linked to cancer. Carbon monoxide binds to haemoglobin, reducing the oxygen the blood can carry.

**Do plants respire only at night?**
No. Plants respire all the time in all living cells; photosynthesis happens only in the light.
