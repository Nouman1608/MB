## The five tests in one table

| Gas | Do this | Observe this | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydrogen, H₂ | Hold a **lighted** splint at the mouth of the tube | A **pop** | Hydrogen and air burn very rapidly |
| Oxygen, O₂ | Put a **glowing** splint into the tube | The splint **relights** | Oxygen supports burning far better than air |
| Carbon dioxide, CO₂ | **Bubble** the gas through limewater | Limewater turns **cloudy white** | Insoluble calcium carbonate forms |
| Ammonia, NH₃ | Hold **damp red** litmus in the gas | Litmus turns **blue** | Dissolved ammonia gives OH⁻ ions |
| Ammonia, NH₃ | Hold hydrogen chloride gas from **concentrated** hydrochloric acid near it | **White smoke** | Solid ammonium chloride forms |
| Chlorine, Cl₂ | Hold **damp blue** litmus in the gas | Turns **red**, then **bleached white** | Acidic in water, then bleaches |

Smell: ammonia and chlorine **both** have a sharp, choking smell. Smell is a property listed in the specification, not a way to tell the two apart.

## Equations to know

- Hydrogen pop: 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l)
- Limewater: Ca(OH)₂(aq) + CO₂(g) → CaCO₃(s) + H₂O(l)
- Ammonia in water: NH₃(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ NH₄⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq)
- White smoke: NH₃(g) + HCl(g) → NH₄Cl(s)

Word equation versions are acceptable when a question asks for a word equation, for example *ammonia + hydrogen chloride → ammonium chloride*.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning in this section |
|---|---|
| Lighted splint | A wooden splint that is burning with a flame |
| Glowing splint | A splint that has been lit and blown out, so it only glows |
| Limewater | Calcium hydroxide solution |
| Damp litmus | Litmus paper wetted with water so a gas can dissolve on it |
| Bleach | Removes colour from a dye; chlorine turns litmus white |
| White smoke | Tiny particles of solid ammonium chloride suspended in air |
| Fume cupboard | Ventilated enclosure for work with toxic gases such as ammonia and chlorine |

## Where each gas turns up

| Gas | Typical source in this course |
|---|---|
| Hydrogen | Metals with dilute acids; Group 1 metals with water; cathode in sodium chloride solution electrolysis |
| Oxygen | Anode in copper sulfate solution electrolysis |
| Carbon dioxide | Carbonates with acids; heating carbonates of magnesium, copper, zinc, calcium or lithium |
| Ammonia | Haber process |
| Chlorine | Anode in sodium chloride solution electrolysis |

## An elimination order for an unknown gas

Use this order when a question gives a set of observations.

1. **Smell?** Sharp and choking: ammonia or chlorine. Go to step 2. No smell: go to step 3.
2. **Damp litmus.** Red litmus turns blue: ammonia. Blue litmus turns red then white: chlorine.
3. **Lighted splint.** Pop: hydrogen.
4. **Glowing splint.** Relights: oxygen.
5. **Limewater.** Cloudy white: carbon dioxide.

A splint going out tells you only what the gas is **not** (not hydrogen, not oxygen). It never confirms carbon dioxide.

## Useful contrasts

- **Hydrogen vs oxygen.** Hydrogen burns (it is the fuel); oxygen helps other things burn. Hence lighted splint for one, glowing for the other.
- **Ammonia vs chlorine on litmus.** Ammonia: alkaline, red to blue, no bleaching. Chlorine: acidic, blue to red, then bleached.
- **Ammonia's two tests.** Litmus shows ammonia is alkaline; the white smoke shows it combines with hydrogen chloride. Either confirms ammonia.
- **Carbon dioxide test vs carbonate test.** 3.4.2 c tests the gas. In 3.4.3 d the same limewater result is used to show a solid contained carbonate ions, after adding dilute acid.

## Exam traps

- "Use a splint" without saying lighted or glowing.
- "Litmus turns blue" without saying the litmus was red and damp.
- "Chlorine turns litmus red": incomplete without the bleaching.
- "White smoke with hydrochloric acid": the smoke comes from hydrogen chloride gas given off by concentrated acid.
- Naming the ion instead of the gas: chlorine, not chloride; ammonia, not ammonium.
- "Limewater goes clear": the change is from clear to cloudy white.
- Describing a test that destroys the evidence, such as smelling a toxic gas.

## Quick self-test

1. Which splint is used to test for oxygen, and what is seen?
2. Give the positive result for hydrogen.
3. Name the solid that makes limewater cloudy.
4. Why must litmus be damp when testing for ammonia?
5. Name the white smoke formed in the second ammonia test.
6. Damp blue litmus turns red and then white. Name the gas.
7. Which two gases in this section have a sharp, choking smell?
8. A gas is made at the anode when copper sulfate solution is electrolysed. Name it and give its test.
9. Write a balanced equation for the reaction that causes the pop.
10. A gas puts out a lighted splint. Can you conclude it is carbon dioxide? Explain.
11. What happens to damp red litmus in chlorine?
12. Name the gas formed when dilute hydrochloric acid is added to zinc, and give its test.

### Answers

1. A glowing splint; it relights.
2. A pop is heard when a lighted splint is held at the mouth of the tube.
3. Calcium carbonate.
4. Ammonia must dissolve in water to form hydroxide ions, which change the litmus colour.
5. Ammonium chloride.
6. Chlorine.
7. Ammonia and chlorine.
8. Oxygen; a glowing splint relights.
9. 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
10. No. Many gases put out a flame; bubble it through limewater and look for cloudy white.
11. It is bleached white (it cannot turn red, as it is already red).
12. Hydrogen; a lighted splint gives a pop.
