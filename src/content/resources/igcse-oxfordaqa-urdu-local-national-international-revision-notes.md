## Why Theme 2 feels harder than Theme 1

Theme 1 draws on personal, familiar vocabulary (yourself, your family).
Theme 2 shifts to more abstract, opinion-heavy subject matter (social
and global issues), which demands vocabulary you cannot simply describe
from direct personal experience. Revision should therefore prioritise
**problem-and-solution language** as core vocabulary, not an optional
extra:

- the issue is...
- a possible solution would be...
- this would help because...

## Four topics, four vocabulary sets

| Topic | Core vocabulary focus |
|---|---|
| **Topic 1: Home, town, neighbourhood and region** | Housing types, rooms/facilities, local amenities, directions |
| **Topic 2: Social issues** (sub-topics: charity/voluntary work; healthy/unhealthy living) | Problem/solution language |
| **Topic 3: Global issues** (sub-topics: the environment; poverty) | Cause/effect language (climate, pollution, recycling) |
| **Topic 4: Travel and tourism** | Transport, booking, holiday recount (past tense), comparison |

## The structures that separate strong from weak answers

- **Conditional** — *if we recycled more, the environment would
improve.* Essential for proposing solutions in Topics 2 and 3.
- **Cause and effect connectives** — *because of this, as a result,
this leads to.* Needed whenever a social or global issue is being
explained, not just named.
- **Comparatives/superlatives** — applied to places (*quieter than the
city*) and travel experiences (*the best holiday I have had*).
- **Past tense narrative + present tense evaluation, together** —
travel tasks typically want both: what happened, and what you think of
it now.

## The same structures in Urdu

| Structure | Urdu example | English |
|---|---|---|
| real condition | اگر ہم زیادہ چیزیں دوبارہ استعمال کریں تو ماحول بہتر ہو جائے گا۔ | If we reuse more things, the environment will improve. |
| unreal condition | اگر ہم زیادہ ری سائیکلنگ کرتے تو ماحول بہتر ہوتا۔ | If we recycled more, the environment would be better. |
| cause and effect | گاڑیوں کا دھواں سانس کی بیماریوں کا باعث بنتا ہے۔ | Car fumes cause breathing illnesses. |
| result | اس کے نتیجے میں ... | as a result of this ... |
| comparative | میرا گاؤں شہر سے زیادہ پرسکون ہے۔ | My village is more peaceful than the city. |
| superlative | یہ میری زندگی کی سب سے اچھی چھٹیاں تھیں۔ | These were the best holidays of my life. |
| past + evaluation | پچھلے سال ہم مری گئے اور وہاں ہم نے برف دیکھی۔ میرے خیال میں یہ ایک یادگار سفر تھا۔ | Last year we went to Murree and saw snow there. I think it was a memorable trip. |

Note that گئے takes no نے (جانا is intransitive), while ہم نے برف دیکھی does, with دیکھی agreeing with
the feminine برف.

## Gender of abstract nouns

The trap below about agreement is easiest to avoid if you learn the gender with the noun.

| Noun | Gender | Example |
|---|---|---|
| مسئلہ (problem) | masculine | یہ ایک بڑا مسئلہ ہے۔ |
| حل (solution) | masculine | یہ ایک اچھا حل ہے۔ |
| تجویز (suggestion) | feminine | یہ ایک اچھی تجویز ہے۔ |
| آلودگی (pollution) | feminine | آلودگی بڑھ رہی ہے۔ |
| غربت (poverty) | feminine | غربت کم ہو رہی ہے۔ |
| تبدیلی (change) | feminine | ایک بڑی تبدیلی آئی ہے۔ |

## Reading: identify the task type first

Before answering, decide whether a text is **describing a problem**,
**proposing a solution**, or **evaluating how effective a solution has
been** — these are different tasks even when the surface topic (an
environmental issue, a social problem) looks the same. Answering the
wrong task type, even with accurate vocabulary, does not answer the
question asked.

## Writing: the reliable four-step structure

For a written task on a social or global issue:

1. Name the specific problem (present tense).
2. Give a cause (*because of this...*).
3. Propose a solution (conditional: *if... this would...*).
4. Give a personal opinion with a reason (*I believe this matters
because...*).

This structure consistently outperforms a description-only response
that never proposes a solution or gives an opinion.

## Worked example

**Task (written for this page):** Write about an environmental problem in your area and suggest a
solution.

> ہمارے شہر میں سب سے بڑا مسئلہ فضائی آلودگی ہے۔ سڑکوں پر گاڑیاں بہت زیادہ ہیں اور ان کے دھوئیں کی
> وجہ سے ہوا گندی ہو جاتی ہے۔ اس کے نتیجے میں بہت سے بچوں اور بزرگوں کو سانس کی تکلیف ہوتی ہے۔ اگر
> لوگ چھوٹے سفر کے لیے پیدل چلیں یا بس استعمال کریں تو سڑکوں پر گاڑیاں کم ہو جائیں گی۔ میرے خیال
> میں یہ بہت اہم ہے کیونکہ صاف ہوا ہر انسان کا حق ہے۔

**English gloss:** "In our city the biggest problem is air pollution. There are far too many cars on
the roads, and because of their fumes the air becomes dirty. As a result, many children and elderly
people have breathing difficulties. If people walk or use the bus for short journeys, there will be
fewer cars on the roads. In my opinion this is very important because clean air is every person's
right."

| Step | Where it appears |
|---|---|
| 1 Problem, present tense | سب سے بڑا مسئلہ فضائی آلودگی ہے |
| 2 Cause | ان کے دھوئیں کی وجہ سے، then a result with اس کے نتیجے میں |
| 3 Solution, conditional | اگر لوگ ... پیدل چلیں ... تو ... کم ہو جائیں گی |
| 4 Opinion with reason | میرے خیال میں ... کیونکہ ... |

The Urdu condition uses the subjunctive in the اگر clause (چلیں، استعمال کریں) and a future in the
result (کم ہو جائیں گی), a common and correct pattern for proposals.

## Signals of the task type in a reading text

| Task type | Typical Urdu signals |
|---|---|
| describing a problem | مسئلہ یہ ہے کہ، کی کمی ہے، بڑھ رہا / رہی ہے |
| proposing a solution | ہونا چاہیے، حکومت کو چاہیے کہ، تجویز ہے کہ، اگر ... تو |
| evaluating a solution | اس منصوبے سے فائدہ ہوا، کامیاب رہا، ناکام رہا، ابھی تک کوئی فرق نہیں پڑا |

## Translation drill

Paper 1 includes translation from English into Urdu, so practise in that direction. Cover the Urdu
column, translate, then check.

| English | Urdu |
|---|---|
| My town is quieter than the city. | میرا قصبہ شہر سے زیادہ پرسکون ہے۔ |
| Many young people do voluntary work. | بہت سے نوجوان رضاکارانہ کام کرتے ہیں۔ |
| Pollution is increasing every year. | آلودگی ہر سال بڑھ رہی ہے۔ |
| Last summer we travelled by train. | پچھلی گرمیوں میں ہم نے ریل گاڑی سے سفر کیا۔ |
| If we help poor people, poverty will decrease. | اگر ہم غریبوں کی مدد کریں تو غربت کم ہو جائے گی۔ |

In the fourth sentence سفر کرنا is transitive in form, so the past takes نے and کیا agrees with the
masculine سفر.

## How Theme 2 is examined

As with every theme in this specification, Theme 2 is not tested on a
dedicated paper of its own — it is assessed across the same Paper 1
(Reading, Grammar and Meaning) and Paper 2 (Writing) that draw on all
three themes, each 1 hour 45 minutes and worth 70 marks (50% of the
GCSE). This means Theme 2 vocabulary and structures need to be
exam-ready alongside Theme 1 and Theme 3 material, not revised as an
isolated block that only appears in one specific section of one paper.

## Exam traps

- Treating "social issues" and "global issues" as interchangeable,
rather than distinguishing local-community from wider-world problems.
- Describing a problem without proposing a solution — missing an easy
opportunity to use conditional structures.
- Writing about travel purely as narrative, without an evaluative
comment.
- Losing gender agreement when adjectives modify abstract nouns (an
issue, a solution) rather than familiar personal nouns.
- Reading a passage without first identifying whether it describes,
proposes, or evaluates.
- Using نے with جانا in a travel recount (ہم نے مری گئے is wrong; ہم مری گئے).
- Translating "the environment" word by word with an article: Urdu has no "the", so ماحول alone is
correct.

## Building genuinely active vocabulary, not passive recognition

Being able to recognise a Theme 2 word when reading is not the same as
being able to produce it correctly in writing under time pressure. For
each of the four topics, practise actively producing full sentences
using the target vocabulary — not just matching words to translations —
since the writing task requires active production, while reading only
requires recognition. Time spent purely on recognition drills builds a
false sense of readiness for the productive task.
