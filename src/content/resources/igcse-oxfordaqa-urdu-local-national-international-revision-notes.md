## Why Theme 2 feels harder than Theme 1

Theme 1 draws on personal, familiar vocabulary (yourself, your family).
Theme 2 shifts to more abstract, opinion-heavy subject matter (social
and global issues), which demands vocabulary you cannot simply describe
from direct personal experience. Revision should therefore prioritise
**problem-and-solution language** as core vocabulary, not an optional
extra:

- the issue is...
- a possible solution would be...
- this would help because...

## Four sub-topics, four vocabulary sets

| Sub-topic | Core vocabulary focus |
|---|---|
| **2.1 Home, town, neighbourhood, region** | Housing types, rooms/facilities, local amenities, directions |
| **2.2 Social issues** | Poverty, inequality, homelessness — problem/solution language |
| **2.3 Global issues** | Environment, climate, pollution, recycling — cause/effect language |
| **2.4 Travel and tourism** | Transport, booking, holiday recount (past tense), comparison |

## The structures that separate strong from weak answers

- **Conditional** — *if we recycled more, the environment would
improve.* Essential for proposing solutions in 2.2 and 2.3.
- **Cause and effect connectives** — *because of this, as a result,
this leads to.* Needed whenever a social or global issue is being
explained, not just named.
- **Comparatives/superlatives** — applied to places (*quieter than the
city*) and travel experiences (*the best holiday I have had*).
- **Past tense narrative + present tense evaluation, together** —
travel tasks typically want both: what happened, and what you think of
it now.

## Reading and listening: identify the task type first

Before answering, decide whether a text is **describing a problem**,
**proposing a solution**, or **evaluating how effective a solution has
been** — these are different tasks even when the surface topic (an
environmental issue, a social problem) looks the same. Answering the
wrong task type, even with accurate vocabulary, does not answer the
question asked.

## Writing and speaking: the reliable four-step structure

For a written or spoken task on a social or global issue:

1. Name the specific problem (present tense).
2. Give a cause (*because of this...*).
3. Propose a solution (conditional: *if... this would...*).
4. Give a personal opinion with a reason (*I believe this matters
because...*).

This structure consistently outperforms a description-only response
that never proposes a solution or gives an opinion.

## How Theme 2 is examined

As with every theme in this specification, Theme 2 is not tested on a
dedicated paper of its own — it is assessed across the same Paper 1
(Reading, Grammar and Meaning) and Paper 2 (Writing) that draw on all
three themes, each 1 hour 45 minutes and worth 70 marks (50% of the
GCSE). This means Theme 2 vocabulary and structures need to be
exam-ready alongside Theme 1 and Theme 3 material, not revised as an
isolated block that only appears in one specific section of one paper.

## Exam traps

- Treating "social issues" and "global issues" as interchangeable,
rather than distinguishing local-community from wider-world problems.
- Describing a problem without proposing a solution — missing an easy
opportunity to use conditional structures.
- Writing about travel purely as narrative, without an evaluative
comment.
- Losing gender agreement when adjectives modify abstract nouns (an
issue, a solution) rather than familiar personal nouns.
- Reading a passage without first identifying whether it describes,
proposes, or evaluates.

## Building genuinely active vocabulary, not passive recognition

Being able to recognise a Theme 2 word when reading is not the same as
being able to produce it correctly in writing or speech under time
pressure. For each of the four sub-topics, practise actively producing
full sentences using the target vocabulary — not just matching words to
translations — since the writing and speaking tasks require active
production, while reading and listening only require recognition. Time
spent purely on recognition drills builds a false sense of readiness
for the productive tasks.

## Listening tasks specifically

Listening tasks on Theme 2 material often move at natural speed through
opinion-heavy content, so practise listening for the specific signal
words that mark a shift from description to opinion or from problem to
solution (for example, equivalents of "however," "in my opinion," or
"a solution would be"), rather than trying to catch every word.
Identifying these structural signal words first makes it far easier to
locate the specific information a question asks for on a second or
third listen.

## Speaking tasks: rehearsing under time pressure

Speaking tasks on this theme often ask candidates to discuss a social
or global issue spontaneously, without the drafting time a written task
allows. Rehearse the four-step structure (problem, cause, solution,
opinion) aloud, at speaking pace, on two or three likely topics from
each sub-topic, so producing it fluently under time pressure feels
familiar rather than something attempted for the first time in the
actual assessment.

## Self-test

1. Why does Theme 2 demand a different kind of vocabulary preparation
than Theme 1?
2. Name the four sub-topics of Theme 2.
3. Give one structure essential for proposing a solution to a problem.
4. What four-step structure works well for a written task on a social
or global issue?
5. Name one common source of lost marks specific to Theme 2's more
abstract vocabulary.

**Answers:** 1. Theme 2's content is more abstract and opinion-based
(social and global issues) rather than personal and familiar, requiring
problem-and-solution language as core vocabulary, not description
alone. 2. Home/town/neighbourhood/region; social issues; global issues;
travel and tourism. 3. Conditional structures (e.g. "if we recycled
more, the environment would improve"). 4. Name the specific problem,
give a cause, propose a conditional solution, give a personal opinion
with a reason. 5. Losing gender agreement when adjectives modify
abstract nouns (an issue, a solution) rather than the familiar personal
nouns practised in Theme 1.
