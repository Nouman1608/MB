## What 4.4 covers

The syllabus lists five points:

1. the meaning of irrecoverable debts and irrecoverable debts recovered
2. journal entries and ledger accounts to record irrecoverable debts
3. journal entries and ledger accounts to record irrecoverable debts recovered
4. the need for maintaining an allowance for irrecoverable debts
5. journal entries and ledger accounts to record the creation of, and adjustments
   to, an allowance for irrecoverable debts

## Two different ideas

| | Irrecoverable debt | Allowance for irrecoverable debts |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | A specific customer's debt that will not be paid | An estimate of the part of the remaining receivables that may not be paid |
| Certainty | Known | Estimated, usually a percentage |
| Customer's account | Closed off (credited) | Not touched: no customer has yet failed to pay |
| Statement of profit or loss | Expense | Only the **change** in the allowance |
| Statement of financial position | Trade receivables are reduced | Deducted from trade receivables |

An **irrecoverable debt recovered** is cash received from a customer whose debt was
written off in an earlier period. It is income of the period in which the cash
arrives.

## Worked example 1: a debt partly paid, then written off

Brightwater Tiles has a 31 December year end. K Okafor owed 1,250. On
18 September 2027 he was declared bankrupt and paid 400 in full and final
settlement. The remaining 850 is irrecoverable.

```
Journal ($)                                         Dr       Cr
18 Sep 27  Bank                                    400
              K Okafor                                      400
           Cash received in final settlement
18 Sep 27  Irrecoverable debts                     850
              K Okafor                                      850
           Balance of debt written off: customer bankrupt
```

```
K Okafor account ($)
Date      Details                Amount  Date      Details                Amount
1 Sep 27  Balance b/d             1,250  18 Sep 27 Bank                      400
                                         18 Sep 27 Irrecoverable debts       850
                                 ------                                   ------
                                  1,250                                    1,250
                                 ======                                   ======
```

Only the 850 is an expense. Recording the whole 1,250 as irrecoverable and the
400 as income would overstate both the expense and the income.

## Worked example 2: a debt recovered in a later year

L Mensah's debt of 610 was written off by Brightwater in 2026. In March 2028 her
business recovered and she paid in full. The debt is first **reinstated**, so
her account shows that she paid, which matters if she asks for credit again.

```
Journal ($)                                         Dr       Cr
12 Mar 28  L Mensah                                610
              Irrecoverable debts recovered                 610
           Debt written off in 2026 reinstated
12 Mar 28  Bank                                    610
              L Mensah                                      610
           Cash received from L Mensah
```

At the year end the irrecoverable debts recovered account is closed by debiting
it and crediting the statement of profit or loss with 610. It is shown as other
income. It is **not** credited to sales: the sale was in a different year.

## Why maintain an allowance?

At any year end some trade receivables will not be collected, but the business
does not yet know which ones. Waiting until a customer fails would put the loss
in a later year than the sale that caused it. An allowance:

- applies **matching**, because the expected loss is charged in the year of the
  credit sales
- applies **prudence**, because trade receivables are not shown at more than the
  business expects to collect
- gives owners and lenders a **realistic** current asset figure, which matters
  for liquidity ratios such as the acid test

## Worked example 3: the allowance over three years

Brightwater's policy is an allowance of 3% of trade receivables, after
irrecoverable debts are written off. In 2029 the rate is cut to 2.5% after a
new credit-control system is introduced.

| Year end | Receivables before write-offs | Written off | Remaining | Rate | Allowance needed | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 31 Dec 2027 | 36,850 | 850 | 36,000 | 3% | 1,080 | Created: +1,080 |
| 31 Dec 2028 | 42,600 | 1,600 | 41,000 | 3% | 1,230 | Increase: +150 |
| 31 Dec 2029 | 30,800 | nil | 30,800 | 2.5% | 770 | Decrease: −460 |

The order matters: **write off first**, then apply the percentage. Applying 3%
to 42,600 would give 1,278 and an allowance against a debt already removed.

```
Allowance for irrecoverable debts account ($), running balance form
Date       Details                     Debit    Credit    Balance
31 Dec 27  Profit or loss                        1,080    1,080 Cr
31 Dec 28  Profit or loss                          150    1,230 Cr
31 Dec 29  Profit or loss                460                770 Cr
```

```
Journal, 31 December 2028 ($)                       Dr       Cr
Profit or loss (increase in allowance)             150
   Allowance for irrecoverable debts                         150

Journal, 31 December 2029 ($)
Allowance for irrecoverable debts                  460
   Profit or loss (decrease in allowance)                    460
```

The allowance is a credit balance that stays open from year to year. Only the
movement passes through the statement of profit or loss.

## Into the financial statements

**2028 statement of profit or loss.** Expenses include irrecoverable debts 1,600
and increase in allowance for irrecoverable debts 150. Other income includes
irrecoverable debts recovered 610. The net effect on profit is
1,600 + 150 − 610 = **1,140** lower.

**2029 statement of profit or loss.** The decrease of 460 is added to gross
profit as other income (or shown as a deduction from expenses). It does not
mean cash was received: it reverses part of an earlier estimate.

**Statement of financial position extracts.**

```
                                           2028       2029
Trade receivables                        41,000     30,800
Less allowance for irrecoverable debts  (1,230)      (770)
                                         39,770     30,030
```

## Effect of errors

| Error | Profit for the year | Net trade receivables |
|---|---|---|
| Irrecoverable debt of 300 not written off (allowance worked out on the correct receivables) | Overstated by 300 | Overstated by 300 |
| Full allowance of 1,230 charged in 2028 instead of the increase of 150 | Understated by 1,080 | Understated by 1,080, because the account would show 2,310 |
| 2029 decrease of 460 not recorded | Understated by 460 | Understated by 460 |
| Debt recovered credited to sales | Correct in total, but revenue and gross profit overstated by 610 | Unaffected |

## Common mistakes

- Debiting the allowance account when a specific customer's debt is written off.
  In section 4.4 the write-off goes to the irrecoverable debts account.
- Charging the whole new allowance as an expense instead of the change.
- Forgetting that a decrease in the allowance increases profit.
- Applying the percentage before removing irrecoverable debts.
- Recording a recovery without reinstating the customer's debt.
- Showing the allowance as a current liability; it is deducted from trade
  receivables in current assets.

## Exam technique

- **Prepare** journals with a narrative and the correct order: reinstate, then
  record cash.
- **Prepare** the allowance account in T-account or running balance form; both
  are allowed.
- **Explain** the need for an allowance using two concepts, prudence and matching,
  and apply them to the business named.
- **Calculate** the change, not just the new balance, and label it increase or
  decrease.
