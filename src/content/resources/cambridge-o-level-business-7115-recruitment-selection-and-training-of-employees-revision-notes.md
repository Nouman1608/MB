Condensed recall for subtopic **2.3 Recruitment, selection and training of employees**. For worked examples and annotated answers, use the [Recruitment, selection and training study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-recruitment-selection-and-training-of-employees/).

## The recruitment and selection stages

1. Vacancy identified (someone leaves, is promoted, or the business expands).
2. Job analysis → **job description** (duties, hours, pay, who the post reports to).
3. **Person specification** (qualifications, experience, skills, qualities; essential and desirable).
4. Advertise internally and/or externally.
5. Applications or CVs received; **shortlist** against the person specification.
6. Interviews, tests and references.
7. Job offer and **contract of employment**.
8. **Induction training**.

## Internal versus external recruitment

| | Internal | External |
|---|---|---|
| Benefits | Cheaper, quicker; candidate already known; motivates staff by showing promotion is possible; little induction needed | Wider choice; new ideas and skills; avoids jealousy between existing staff |
| Limitations | Few candidates; no new ideas; leaves another vacancy | Costly and slower; candidate less well known; longer induction |

## Selection methods

- **Application form / CV**: compare applicants on the same points.
- **Interview**: tests communication and attitude; can be subjective.
- **Skills test or practical task**: shows actual ability.
- **References**: confirm reliability and past performance.

## Part-time versus full-time

- **Part-time**: flexible cover for busy times, lower total wage cost, wider pool of applicants (parents, students); but more staff to train and manage, may be less committed, communication harder.
- **Full-time**: continuity, deeper knowledge, often more loyal; but paid even in quiet times, less flexible.

## Training

| Type | Benefits | Limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Induction | New staff settle quickly, learn rules and safety, fewer early mistakes | Delays productive work; costs trainer time |
| On-the-job | Cheap; directly relevant; output continues | Trainer's work slows; bad habits passed on; mistakes on real work |
| Off-the-job | Specialist trainers; wider skills; no risk to real work | Expensive; employee away from work; may leave for a better job with new skills |

**Importance to the business**: productivity, quality, safety, flexibility, lower labour turnover. **To employees**: skills, confidence, promotion, pay.

## Reducing the workforce

| Dismissal | Redundancy |
|---|---|
| Due to the employee's conduct or ability | Due to the job no longer being needed |
| Example: theft, repeated lateness after warnings | Example: automation, falling demand, branch closure |
| The post is usually filled again | The post is usually removed |

**Choosing who to make redundant**: use fair, job-related criteria applied to everyone: skills needed in future, performance, attendance, length of service. Explain the decision to remaining staff to protect morale.

## Legal controls

| Control | Impact on employers | Impact on employees |
|---|---|---|
| Employment contract | Must set out terms clearly and keep to them | Know pay, hours, duties and rights |
| Unfair dismissal | Need a fair reason and procedure; risk of penalties | Protection from being sacked without cause |
| Discrimination | Job-related criteria only in hiring, pay, promotion | Fair treatment and opportunities |
| Health and safety | Cost of equipment, training, checks; fewer accidents | Safer workplace |
| Legal minimum wage | Higher wage costs for low-paid jobs | Guaranteed minimum income |

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Recruitment | Attracting suitable people to apply for a job |
| Selection | Choosing the most suitable applicant |
| Job description | Document listing the duties and conditions of a job |
| Person specification | Document listing the qualities, skills and qualifications needed |
| Shortlist | The applicants chosen for interview |
| Induction training | Introduction for new employees to the business, its rules and their job |
| On-the-job training | Learning at the workplace while doing the job |
| Off-the-job training | Learning away from the workstation, such as at a college |
| Dismissal | Ending employment because of the employee's conduct or ability |
| Redundancy | Ending employment because the job is no longer needed |

## Exam traps

- **Recruitment ≠ selection.**
- **Job description is about the job; person specification is about the person.**
- **Redundancy is not the employee's fault.**
- **Recommend a candidate against the case criteria**, not just "the most experienced".
- **Legal answers stay general**: describe the control and its impact.
- **Training has costs** (time, money, staff leaving); weigh them.

## Self-test

1. Define recruitment.
2. State two items in a job description.
3. State two items in a person specification.
4. Give one benefit of internal recruitment.
5. Give one limitation of external recruitment.
6. Why might a café employ part-time staff?
7. Which training is best for a worker using a complex new machine? Why?
8. Give one limitation of on-the-job training.
9. Distinguish dismissal from redundancy.
10. Give two situations in which a business may need to reduce its workforce.
11. Give one criterion a business could use when choosing employees for redundancy.
12. Explain one impact of a rise in the legal minimum wage on a supermarket.

## Answers

1. Attracting suitable people to apply for a vacancy.
2. Any two: job title, duties, responsibilities, who the post reports to, hours, pay.
3. Any two: qualifications, experience, skills, personal qualities.
4. Any one: cheaper, quicker, candidate known, motivates staff.
5. Any one: costly, slow, candidate less well known, may upset internal applicants.
6. To cover busy periods such as lunchtimes without paying staff during quiet times.
7. Off-the-job training by specialists, so mistakes do not damage the machine or output.
8. Any one: slows the trainer's own work; bad habits passed on; costly mistakes on real work.
9. Dismissal is due to the employee's conduct or ability; redundancy is because the job is no longer needed.
10. Any two: automation; falling demand; branch closure; cost cutting to survive.
11. Any one: skills needed in future; performance; attendance; length of service.
12. Wage costs rise, so the supermarket may raise prices, cut hours or accept lower profit; staff may be more motivated.
