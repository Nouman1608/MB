These notes condense section **3.1.5.2 Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2 (and Paper 3). The [Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-maxwell-boltzmann-distribution/) explains each feature with worked examples, and the [practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-maxwell-boltzmann-distribution-practice/) give exam-style practice.

## What the specification asks

- The **Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution** of molecular energies in gases.
- **Draw and interpret** distribution curves for different temperatures.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Distribution | How the molecules in a sample are spread across a range of energies |
| Most probable energy | The energy at the peak; more molecules have this energy than any other |
| Mean energy | The average energy, to the right of the peak |
| Activation energy, Eₐ | Minimum energy for a successful collision |
| Tail | The high-energy region of the curve, which does not touch the axis |

## Features of the curve

| Feature | Reason |
|---|---|
| Starts at the origin | No molecules have zero energy |
| One peak | The most probable energy |
| Asymmetric, with a long tail on the right | Energy has a lower limit of zero but no upper limit |
| Does not touch the x-axis | No maximum energy; a few molecules have very high energy |
| Area under the curve = total number of molecules | Fixed for a given sample |
| Area to the right of Eₐ | Molecules with energy equal to or greater than Eₐ |

Axes: x = energy; y = number of molecules (or fraction of molecules) with that energy.

## Effect of raising the temperature

| Change | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Peak moves right | Higher most probable energy |
| Peak is lower | Same area spread over a wider range |
| Curve is broader | Wider range of energies |
| Area under curve unchanged | Same number of molecules |
| Area to the right of Eₐ increases | More molecules have energy of at least Eₐ |
| Eₐ unchanged | Temperature does not change the activation energy |

## Drawing checklist

1. Axes: "energy" (x) and "number of molecules" (y).
2. Curve starts at the origin.
3. Asymmetric peak and tail that approaches, but never touches, the x-axis.
4. Second curve (higher temperature): lower peak, further right, tail above the first.
5. Eₐ drawn as a vertical line on the x-axis.
6. Area to the right of Eₐ shaded on each curve.
7. The two curves cross once to the right of the first peak.

## Worked reminders

**Which curve is the higher temperature?** The one with the lower peak at higher energy and the larger tail.

**Why is the area the same?** The number of molecules is fixed. Raising the temperature moves molecules to different energies but does not change how many there are.

**Numbers.** In 1.00 × 10²² molecules, 0.5% have energy at least Eₐ at 300 K and 4.0% at 340 K.
300 K: 5.0 × 10¹⁹. 340 K: 4.0 × 10²⁰. The higher temperature has **eight times** as many molecules able to react (illustrative figures).

**Why does the curve not touch the axis?** There is no upper limit to energy, so a very few molecules have very high energies.

**Why is the peak to the left of the mean?** The long tail on the right pulls the mean to higher energy than the peak.

## Answer patterns

**Describe the change at a higher temperature.** "The peak is lower and moves to the right. The area under the curve is unchanged. More molecules have energy at least equal to Eₐ."

**Explain why the area is constant.** "The area represents the total number of molecules, which does not change."

## Common mistakes

- A symmetrical curve.
- The curve not starting at the origin, or touching the x-axis at the end.
- A second curve with a different area.
- Moving the peak right without lowering it.
- Eₐ moved on the diagram when the temperature changes.
- Shading the area to the left of Eₐ.
- Using a curve for speed but labelling it as energy.
- Not labelling the axes.

## Self-test

1. State what the y-axis of a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution shows.
2. Explain why the curve starts at the origin.
3. Explain why the curve does not touch the x-axis.
4. State what the area under the curve represents.
5. State what the area to the right of Eₐ represents.
6. List three differences between the curve at 400 K and at 300 K.
7. Explain why the peak is lower at 400 K.
8. Why does the curve have a long tail to the right?
9. State what happens to Eₐ when the temperature rises.
10. At 300 K, 2.0% of the molecules in a sample have energy of at least Eₐ. At 320 K it is 5.0%. How many times more molecules can react at 320 K?
11. Explain why the mean energy is greater than the most probable energy.
12. Describe how you would show the effect of a lower temperature on a diagram already drawn for 300 K.

### Answers

1. The number (or fraction) of molecules with a given energy.
2. No molecule has zero energy; all are moving.
3. There is no maximum energy, so a very few molecules have very high energy.
4. The total number of molecules.
5. The number of molecules with energy equal to or greater than Eₐ, which can react on collision.
6. At 400 K the peak is lower, the peak is at a higher energy and the tail is larger (a larger area lies above Eₐ). The areas under both curves are equal.
7. The area under the curve is fixed, and the molecules are spread over a wider range of energies.
8. Energy has a lower limit of zero but no upper limit, and a few molecules gain large amounts of energy in collisions.
9. Nothing; Eₐ does not change with temperature.
10. 5.0 ÷ 2.0 = **2.5 times** as many.
11. The tail of high-energy molecules pulls the average above the peak.
12. Draw a second curve with a higher and narrower peak, to the left of the first, starting at the origin, and with a smaller area beyond Eₐ.
