## Section A

**1.** State **two** physical properties of transition elements that are different from those of Group I metals. **[2]**

**2.** Which property is typical of transition elements but **not** of Group I metals?

A: they conduct electricity

B: they form coloured compounds

C: they form positive ions

D: they are shiny when freshly cut **[1]**

**3.** Name the transition element used as a catalyst in the Haber process. **[1]**

**4.** State the colour of hydrated copper(II) sulfate crystals. **[1]**

**5.** *(Extended)* Give the formula of the ion present in iron(III) chloride. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** The table shows data for four metals.

| Metal | Melting point / °C | Density / g/cm³ |
|---|---|---|
| W | 1455 | 8.91 |
| X | 63 | 0.86 |
| Y | 1085 | 8.96 |
| Z | 98 | 0.97 |

**(a)** Which **two** metals are transition elements? Explain your choice using the data. **[3]**

**(b)** Predict which metal would float on water. **[1]**

**7.** Transition elements often act as catalysts.

**(a)** State the meaning of the term *catalyst*. **[2]**

**(b)** Give one example of a transition element acting as a catalyst as an element, and one example in a compound. In each case name the process. **[4]**

**8.** A student has two solids, sodium chloride and copper(II) carbonate, but the labels have fallen off.

**(a)** Suggest how the student could tell them apart by looking at them. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain which property of transition elements this uses. **[1]**

**9.** *(Extended)* Iron forms iron(II) and iron(III) compounds.

**(a)** Give the formulae of iron(II) oxide and iron(III) oxide. **[2]**

**(b)** State the property of transition elements shown by the existence of these two oxides. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why a Group I metal such as sodium cannot form two different oxides in this way. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Iron reacts with dilute sulfuric acid and, separately, with chlorine.

**(a)** Name the iron compound formed with dilute sulfuric acid. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the balanced equation for the reaction of iron with chlorine to form iron(III) chloride. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe a test, with results, to show which product contains Fe²⁺ and which contains Fe³⁺. **[3]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Aqueous iron(II) sulfate decolourises acidified potassium manganate(VII).

**(a)** Write the half-equation for the change to the iron ions. **[1]**

**(b)** State, with a reason, whether iron(II) is oxidised or reduced. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why iron, rather than sodium, is used to make bridges and machinery. Refer to **two** properties. **[2]**

**13.** Nickel is a transition element. Using the general properties of transition elements, predict:

**(a)** whether nickel has a high or low melting point, **[1]**

**(b)** whether nickel compounds are likely to be coloured, **[1]**

**(c)** one use of nickel linked to a property of transition elements. **[1]**

**14.** *(Extended)* Copper forms two oxides: a black oxide, CuO, and a red oxide, Cu₂O.

**(a)** Name each oxide, including the oxidation number of copper. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how these two oxides show that copper is a transition element. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two from: higher melting point [1]; higher density [1]; harder / stronger.

**2.** **B** [1].

**3.** Iron [1].

**4.** Blue [1].

**5.** Fe³⁺ [1].

**6. (a)** W and Y [1]; they have high melting points (over 1000 °C) [1]; and high densities (about 9 g/cm³) [1].

**(b)** X (or Z): density less than 1 g/cm³ [1].

**7. (a)** A substance that increases the rate of a reaction [1] and is unchanged at the end of the reaction [1].

**(b)** Element: iron [1] in the Haber process (or nickel in hydrogenation of alkenes) [1]; compound: vanadium(V) oxide [1] in the Contact process [1].

**8. (a)** Copper(II) carbonate is coloured (green); sodium chloride is white [1].

**(b)** Transition elements (copper) form coloured compounds; Group I compounds are white [1].

**9. (a)** FeO [1]; Fe₂O₃ [1].

**(b)** Variable oxidation numbers / ions with different charges [1].

**(c)** Sodium only forms Na⁺ ions (oxidation number +1) [1].

**10. (a)** Iron(II) sulfate [1].

**(b)** 2Fe + 3Cl₂ → 2FeCl₃: correct formulae [1]; balanced [1].

**(c)** Add aqueous sodium hydroxide to a solution of each [1]; iron(II) sulfate gives a green precipitate [1]; iron(III) chloride gives a red-brown precipitate [1].

**11. (a)** Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻ [1].

**(b)** Oxidised [1]; it loses an electron / its oxidation number increases from +2 to +3 [1].

**12.** Iron has a high melting point / is hard and strong, whereas sodium is soft with a low melting point [1]; sodium reacts vigorously with water, while iron does not (or iron has a high density/strength suitable for structures) [1].

**13. (a)** High [1].

**(b)** Yes, likely to be coloured [1].

**(c)** As a catalyst, e.g. for adding hydrogen to alkenes [1].

**14. (a)** CuO: copper(II) oxide [1]; Cu₂O: copper(I) oxide [1].

**(b)** Copper forms ions with different (variable) oxidation numbers, +1 and +2 [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving "conducts electricity" or "shiny" as a transition element property.
- Saying the metals themselves are coloured, rather than their compounds.
- Naming a catalyst without the process it catalyses.
- Giving two element examples when the question asks for one element and one compound.
- Confusing the formulae of iron(II) and iron(III) compounds.
- Reversing the precipitate colours: Fe²⁺ green, Fe³⁺ red-brown.
