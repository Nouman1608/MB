These notes condense section 11.2, Naming organic compounds, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). All three outcomes are required for every candidate and cover unbranched compounds with up to four carbon atoms. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [naming organic compounds study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-naming-organic-compounds/); then try the [naming organic compounds practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-naming-organic-compounds-practice/).

## The three outcomes

1. Name and draw structural and displayed formulae of unbranched alkanes, alkenes, alcohols, carboxylic acids and the products of reactions in 11.4–11.7, up to four carbons.
2. State the type of compound from a name (-ane, -ene, -ol, -oic acid) or from a molecular, structural or displayed formula.
3. Name and draw unbranched esters made from alcohols and acids with up to four carbons each.

## Stems and endings

| Stem | C atoms | Ending | Series |
|---|---|---|---|
| meth- | 1 | -ane | alkane |
| eth- | 2 | -ene | alkene |
| prop- | 3 | -ol | alcohol |
| but- | 4 | -oic acid | carboxylic acid |
| | | -yl ...-oate | ester |

Count every carbon in the chain, including the –COOH carbon.

## The complete list

| C | Alkane | Alkene | Alcohol | Acid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | methane | — | methanol | methanoic acid |
| 2 | ethane | ethene | ethanol | ethanoic acid |
| 3 | propane | propene | propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol | propanoic acid |
| 4 | butane | but-1-ene, but-2-ene | butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol | butanoic acid |

## Numbering rules

- Number from the end **nearer** the functional group; use the lowest number.
- Alkene: number of the first carbon of C=C.
- Alcohol: number of the carbon holding –OH.
- No number needed for: ethene, propene, methanol, ethanol, any carboxylic acid.

| Structural formula | Name |
|---|---|
| CH₃CH₂CH=CH₂ | but-1-ene |
| CH₃CH=CHCH₃ | but-2-ene |
| CH₃CH₂CH₂OH | propan-1-ol |
| CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ | propan-2-ol |
| CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH | butan-1-ol |
| CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₃ | butan-2-ol |

## Naming any compound in four steps

1. **Find the functional group** and choose the ending: C=C gives -ene, –OH gives -ol, –COOH gives -oic acid, only single bonds gives -ane.
2. **Count the carbons** in the longest chain, including any –COOH carbon, and choose the stem.
3. **Number the chain** from the end nearer the functional group, if a position number is needed.
4. **Assemble and check**: stem + position + ending, with hyphens around the number. Read the name back and redraw it; the redrawn molecule should match the one you started with.

Example: CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH. Functional group –COOH, so -oic acid; four carbons, so but-; no number for acids. Name: butanoic acid.

## Reaction products to name

| Reaction | Product type | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Alkane + Cl₂, UV light | chloroalkane | chloromethane CH₃Cl; chloroethane C₂H₅Cl |
| Alkene + Br₂ | dibromoalkane | 1,2-dibromoethane; 1,2-dibromopropane; 2,3-dibromobutane |
| Alkene + H₂, Ni | alkane | ethene → ethane |
| Alkene + steam, acid | alcohol | ethene → ethanol |
| Acid + metal/base/carbonate | salt (-oate) | sodium ethanoate; calcium methanoate |
| Ethanol oxidised | carboxylic acid | ethanoic acid |
| Acid + alcohol | ester | ethyl ethanoate |

## Esters: two-way method

**Name from parents**: alcohol → -yl (first word); acid → -oate (second word).

| Alcohol | Acid | Ester | Formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| methanol | ethanoic acid | methyl ethanoate | CH₃COOCH₃ |
| ethanol | ethanoic acid | ethyl ethanoate | CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ |
| ethanol | methanoic acid | ethyl methanoate | HCOOCH₂CH₃ |
| propan-1-ol | propanoic acid | propyl propanoate | CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ |
| butan-1-ol | ethanoic acid | butyl ethanoate | CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ |
| methanol | butanoic acid | methyl butanoate | CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₃ |

**Parents from name**: first word -yl → alcohol (-anol); second word -oate → acid (-oic acid).

**Reading a formula**: the part before –COO– (including the C of COO) is the acid; the part after –O– is the alcohol.

## Identifying from a formula

| Feature | Type |
|---|---|
| only C–C and C–H single bonds | alkane |
| C=C | alkene |
| –OH, no C=O | alcohol |
| –COOH | carboxylic acid |
| C–COO–C | ester |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stem | first part of a name, showing the number of carbon atoms |
| Ending (suffix) | last part of a name, showing the homologous series |
| Position number | number showing which carbon carries the functional group |
| Unbranched | carbon atoms joined in one continuous chain |
| Ester | compound formed from a carboxylic acid and an alcohol, containing –COO– |
| Carboxylate salt | salt of a carboxylic acid, named with -oate |

## Exam traps

- CH₃COOH is **ethanoic** acid: two carbons.
- Lowest number wins: butan-2-ol, never butan-3-ol.
- Ester names: alcohol part first, but the formula is written acid part first.
- Salt names end -oate, not -oic.
- In a dibromo product, bromine atoms sit on **adjacent** carbons.
- Do not use "propan-1-ene": alkenes and alcohols use different endings.

## Self-test

1. Name CH₃CH₂CH₃.
2. Name CH₃CH=CHCH₃.
3. Write the structural formula of propan-2-ol.
4. How many carbon atoms are in butanoic acid?
5. Name the ester made from ethanol and propanoic acid.
6. Which alcohol and acid make methyl methanoate?
7. Name the product of adding bromine to propene.
8. Name the salt made from ethanoic acid and potassium hydroxide.
9. State the type of compound: C₄H₉OH.
10. Name CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃.
11. Why is "butan-3-ol" not a correct name?
12. Name the product when ethane reacts with chlorine in ultraviolet light (monosubstitution).

## Answers

1. Propane.
2. But-2-ene.
3. CH₃CH(OH)CH₃.
4. Four.
5. Ethyl propanoate.
6. Methanol and methanoic acid.
7. 1,2-dibromopropane.
8. Potassium ethanoate.
9. Alcohol.
10. Propyl ethanoate.
11. The chain must be numbered from the end nearer the –OH; that gives butan-2-ol.
12. Chloroethane.
