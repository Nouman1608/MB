# Applying key concepts: practice

Examples are written in English. Do any task that asks you to produce language in the language your school course uses, and ask your school teacher to check it. MYP has no prescribed content list: schools design their own units, so ask your teacher which concepts and contexts your units use. Real MYP work is judged against criterion level descriptors (levels 1 to 8), so the marks in square brackets are a revision aid, not IB marks.

Study first with the [draft study guide](/resources/ib-myp-language-acquisition-key-concepts-in-unit-tasks-draft/) or the reviewed [key and related concepts guide](/resources/ib-myp-language-acquisition-key-and-related-concepts/). Course hub: [/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-language-acquisition/](/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-language-acquisition/).

## Questions

**1.** State the four key concepts named in the IB brief for language acquisition. **[2]**

**2.** Classify each as factual, conceptual or debatable. (i) "How do people greet a teacher in the community you study?" (ii) "Why do some communities greet strangers more warmly than others?" (iii) "Should schools teach greetings from every culture?" **[3]**

**3.** A student must write an email asking a museum for opening times. Explain how the key concept **communication** guides the choices (audience, purpose, mode). **[3]**

**4.** Read this original text. *"My cousin and I have the same birthday, so every year the two families meet halfway at a lake. Mum says it is the only day when nobody argues about who drives."* Identify which key concept(s) the text shows best and explain. **[3]**

**5.** Write a statement of inquiry for a unit on festivals, naming the key concept, a related concept and a global context. **[3]**

**6.** A classmate wrote: "How do festivals show culture?" as a statement of inquiry. Explain what is wrong and rewrite it. **[3]**

**7.** Plan a short spoken task on a hobby that uses the key concept **creativity**. Give three specific choices you would make. **[3]**

## Model answers

**1.** Communication, connections, creativity, culture [2: all four; 1: two or three].

**2.** (i) Factual. (ii) Conceptual. (iii) Debatable. [1 each]

**3.** Audience: a museum office, so formal and polite [1]. Purpose: to get one piece of information, so short and clear with one direct question [1]. Mode: written, so use a subject line and a closing [1].

**4.** Culture or connections (accept either with reasons) [1]. Families link across places and meet on a shared day [1]. A family custom shapes how people behave [1].

**5.** For example: "Festivals show how communities express shared values." Key concept: culture; related concept: conventions; context: personal and cultural expression [3 for all three named, with a claim not a question].

**6.** It is a question, not a general claim [1]. A statement of inquiry is a sentence joining concepts and a context [1]. Rewrite: "Festivals show how communities express shared values." [1]

**7.** Accept three reasoned choices, for example an unexpected opening, a comparison, a short invented dialogue [1 each].
