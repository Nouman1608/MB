Recall notes for **4.2.3 Domestic uses and safety** (4.2.3.1 and 4.2.3.2) in AQA GCSE Physics (8463). Paper 1,
both tiers.

For explanations and an annotated model answer, read the [domestic uses and safety study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-domestic-uses-and-safety/).
Then practise with the [domestic uses and safety practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-domestic-uses-and-safety-practice/).

## The numbers

| Quantity | UK domestic supply |
|---|---|
| Type | Alternating (ac) |
| Frequency | 50 Hz (50 cycles each second; one cycle every 0.02 s) |
| Potential difference | About 230 V |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Direct pd | Acts in one direction only; current always flows the same way (cells, batteries) |
| Alternating pd | Keeps reversing direction; current keeps changing direction (mains) |
| Frequency | Number of complete cycles each second, in hertz (Hz) |
| Three-core cable | Cable with live, neutral and earth wires, used to connect most appliances to the mains |
| Live wire | Brown; carries the alternating pd from the supply |
| Neutral wire | Blue; completes the circuit |
| Earth wire | Green and yellow stripes; safety wire that stops the appliance becoming live |
| Earth potential | 0 V |

## Potentials of the three wires

- Live to earth: **about 230 V**.
- Neutral: **at, or close to, earth potential (0 V)**.
- Earth: **0 V**; carries a current **only if there is a fault**.
- Insulation is colour coded **for easy identification**.

## Ac and dc on a pd–time graph

- Dc: a flat horizontal line, staying on one side of zero.
- Ac: a repeating wave crossing zero, positive then negative.
- Frequency = 1 ÷ time for one complete cycle.
- Example: one cycle takes 0.020 s, so f = 1 ÷ 0.020 = **50 Hz**.

## The two dangers to explain

**1. Live wire with the switch open**

- The live wire is still at about 230 V on the supply side of the switch.
- The body is at about 0 V.
- Touching the live wire puts about 230 V across the body.
- A current flows through the body to earth: an electric shock.

**2. Any connection between live and earth**

- Earth is a very low-resistance path.
- A pd of about 230 V across a low resistance drives a very large current.
- Result: overheating and fire risk, or a shock if the path is through a person.

## How the earth wire protects

- The earth wire is connected to the metal casing.
- If the live wire touches the casing, current flows down the earth wire.
- The casing stays at 0 V, so it does not become live.
- A person touching the casing does not have a large pd across them.

## Scenario check: what is the pd?

Work these out from the potentials, not from memory of the answer.

| Situation | Potentials | pd |
|---|---|---|
| Live wire to the ground, switch open | About 230 V and 0 V | About 230 V |
| Live wire to neutral wire, appliance on | About 230 V and about 0 V | About 230 V |
| Neutral wire to earth wire | About 0 V and 0 V | About 0 V |
| Earthed metal casing to the ground, during a live-to-case fault | 0 V and 0 V | About 0 V, so touching the case is safe |
| Unearthed metal casing to the ground, during the same fault | About 230 V and 0 V | About 230 V, so touching the case gives a shock |

The last two rows show exactly what the earth wire achieves: it changes the pd between the casing and a person
from about 230 V to about 0 V.

## Exam traps

- Neutral **does** carry current in normal use. Earth does **not**.
- "Switched off" does not mean the live wire is at 0 V.
- Frequency is 50 Hz; pd is about 230 V. Do not swap units.
- Ac means the **direction** reverses, not that the current is weaker or stronger.
- A shock answer needs pd across the body **and** current through it.
- Give the full colour of the earth: green **and** yellow stripes.

## Self-test

1. Is the UK mains supply ac or dc?
2. State the frequency and pd of the UK domestic supply.
3. Explain the difference between direct and alternating pd.
4. What is the colour of the neutral wire?
5. What is the job of the live wire?
6. What is the potential of the neutral wire?
7. When does the earth wire carry a current?
8. A trace shows one cycle every 0.025 s. Calculate the frequency.
9. Why can touching a live wire be dangerous even when the appliance switch is off?
10. Why is a connection between the live wire and earth dangerous?
11. How does the earth wire stop a metal casing becoming live?
12. Why is the insulation on each wire a different colour?

### Answers

1. **Ac.**
2. **50 Hz** and **about 230 V**.
3. A direct pd acts in one direction, so current flows one way; an alternating pd keeps reversing, so the current
   keeps changing direction.
4. **Blue.**
5. It carries the alternating pd from the supply.
6. At, or close to, earth potential: **0 V**.
7. Only when there is a fault.
8. f = 1 ÷ 0.025 = **40 Hz**.
9. The live wire is still at about 230 V, so there is a large pd across the body (at 0 V) and a current flows
   through it.
10. The earth path has very low resistance, so a very large current flows, risking fire or a shock.
11. The casing is connected to the earth wire, so if the live wire touches it the current flows to earth and the
    casing stays at 0 V.
12. For easy identification, so the wires are not connected wrongly.
