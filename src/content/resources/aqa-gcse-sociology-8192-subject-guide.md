AQA GCSE Sociology (8192) asks how society is organised and why people's
lives turn out differently. It studies four major areas of social life
(families, education, crime and deviance, and social stratification)
and teaches the theories and research methods sociologists use to
investigate them. The GCSE is assessed by two equally weighted written
papers, each lasting 1 hour 45 minutes and worth 100 marks, with no
coursework.

## How the course is organised

The seven content areas fall into two kinds: foundations that apply
everywhere, and four substantive topics.

**Foundations**

- *The sociological approach:* what it means to think sociologically
  and how sociologists explain social behaviour.
- *Social structures, social processes and social issues:* the
  framework of institutions, processes and issues that the substantive
  topics are built on.
- *Sociological research methods:* research design, qualitative and
  quantitative methods, different types of data, primary and secondary
  sources, interpretation of data, and practical and ethical issues.

**Substantive topics**

- *Families:* the functions of families, family forms, conjugal role
  relationships, changing relationships within families, criticisms of
  families, and divorce.
- *Education:* the roles and functions of education, the relationship
  between education and capitalism, educational achievement, and
  processes within schools.
- *Crime and deviance:* the social construction of crime and deviance,
  social control, criminal and deviant behaviour, and data on crime.
- *Social stratification:* the functionalist theory of stratification,
  socio-economic class, life chances, poverty as a social issue, power
  and authority, and power relationships.

## How it's assessed

The qualification is linear and not tiered, so everyone sits the same
two papers at the end of the course.

| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | The Sociology of Families and Education | 1 hour 45 minutes | 100 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | The Sociology of Crime and Deviance and Social Stratification | 1 hour 45 minutes | 100 | 50% |

Both papers are externally assessed, and there is no coursework. Each
paper focuses on two substantive topics but also draws on relevant
social theory and research methods content from across the whole
course. The specification was first taught in 2017 and first examined
in May and June 2019.

## How the parts fit together

The paper titles name only the substantive topics, which can make it
look as though the foundations are not examined. In fact they are
examined on both papers, because theory and methods are drawn on
throughout. A question about family forms may ask you to judge how a
piece of research was carried out; a question about crime data may ask
what kind of data it is and how reliable it is likely to be. Research
methods, in other words, is not a topic you revise once for one paper.

The four substantive topics are linked by shared ideas. Social
stratification, with its focus on socio-economic class, life chances
and power, offers explanations for patterns you meet elsewhere:
differences in educational achievement, for instance, or in who is
recorded in data on crime. The relationship between education and
capitalism and the functionalist theory of stratification both ask
how institutions serve society and whose interests they reflect.
Families and education are both agents through which people learn
the norms of society, and social control in the crime topic extends
the same idea.

Seeing these connections pays off in longer answers, where you are
expected to use evidence and theory from more than one area to support
an argument.

## Planning your study

1. **Start with the sociological approach and methods.** An early
   grounding in how sociologists think and how they collect data makes
   every later topic easier to evaluate.
2. **Study families and education.** These complete the Paper 1
   content, and both lend themselves to practising methods questions.
3. **Move to crime and deviance and social stratification.** These
   complete the Paper 2 content and build on ideas about structure and
   inequality from the earlier topics.
4. **Return to methods and theory with every topic.** Add each new study
   or theory to a single set of notes that you can apply anywhere,
   rather than keeping separate notes for each paper.
5. **Keep Paper 1 topics active.** Short reviews of families and
   education while you study Paper 2 stop that material fading.

Short and extended answers on each topic should start as soon as it is
taught. Full papers and the paper-by-paper exam preparation page are
most useful in the final months, once all seven content areas are
covered.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in AQA GCSE Sociology?**
There are two written papers. Each lasts 1 hour 45 minutes, is worth 100 marks and carries 50% of the GCSE.

**What topics are on each AQA GCSE Sociology paper?**
Paper 1 covers the sociology of families and education. Paper 2 covers the sociology of crime and deviance and social stratification. Both also draw on social theory and methods from across the course.

**Is research methods examined in GCSE Sociology 8192?**
Yes. Theory and methods content from across the course is drawn on in both papers, so research methods should be revised alongside every topic.

**Is there coursework in AQA GCSE Sociology?**
No. The GCSE is assessed entirely by the two externally assessed written papers.

**When was AQA GCSE Sociology 8192 first examined?**
It was first taught in 2017 and first examined in May and June 2019.
