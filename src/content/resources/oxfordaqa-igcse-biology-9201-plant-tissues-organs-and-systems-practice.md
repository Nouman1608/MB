These questions cover section **3.1.4 Plant tissues, organs and systems** of the OxfordAQA International GCSE
Biology (9201) specification, Version 5.2, for exams in May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered. All
data are invented for practice. A calculator is useful for questions 6 to 8.

## Section A

**1.** Name the plant tissue that:

**(a)** carries out photosynthesis. **[1]**
**(b)** has air spaces to help gases diffuse. **[1]**
**(c)** covers the plant. **[1]**

**2.** State the job of xylem and phloem in a plant. **[1]**

**3.** Name **three** organs of a plant. **[3]**

**4.** In a cross-section of a primary root of a dicotyledonous plant, state where the xylem is found. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why a leaf is described as an organ. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** The table shows the thickness of the layers in a leaf, measured from a micrograph.

| Layer | Thickness (μm) |
|---|---|
| Upper epidermis | 15 |
| Palisade mesophyll | 80 |
| Spongy mesophyll | 95 |
| Lower epidermis | 15 |

**(a)** Calculate the total thickness of the leaf. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the thickness that is palisade mesophyll. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why palisade mesophyll cells contain many chloroplasts. **[1]**

**7.** A student counts stomata on a leaf using a circular field of view with a diameter of 0.50 mm. The area of the field is 0.196 mm².

| Leaf surface | Number of stomata counted |
|---|---|
| Upper | 4 |
| Lower | 21 |

**(a)** Calculate the number of stomata per mm² on the lower surface. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times more stomata per mm² the lower surface has than the upper surface. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest **one** way to make the student's results more reliable. **[1]**

**8.** A stem of celery is placed in coloured water. After 2.5 hours the dye has moved 15 cm up the stem.

**(a)** Calculate the mean speed of the dye in cm per hour. **[2]**
**(b)** State which tissue the dye is moving in. **[1]**
**(c)** Predict how far the dye would move in 6 hours at the same speed. **[1]**

**9.** A cross-section of a plant stem shows the xylem and phloem in separate bundles arranged in a ring near the outside.

**(a)** Describe the position of the xylem compared with the phloem in each bundle. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how you can tell from the diagram that this is not a primary root. **[1]**

**10.** Describe how the structure of the spongy mesophyll is suited to its function. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why there are more stomata on the lower surface of many leaves than on the upper surface. **[2]**

**12.** Describe the tissues you would see in a section through a leaf, from the top surface downwards, and give the job of each. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Palisade mesophyll [1]
**(b)** Spongy mesophyll [1]
**(c)** Epidermal tissue (epidermis) [1]

**2.** They transport substances around the plant [1]

**3.** Stems, roots and leaves [1 each, 3 marks]

**4.** In the centre of the root [1]

**5.** A leaf is made of several tissues [1]; for example palisade mesophyll, spongy mesophyll, epidermis, xylem and phloem [1]

**6. (a)** 15 + 80 + 95 + 15 = **205 μm** [1]
**(b)** 80 ÷ 205 × 100 [1] = 39.02, so **39%** [1]
**(c)** Chloroplasts absorb light energy to make food (photosynthesis) [1]

**7. (a)** 21 ÷ 0.196 [1] = 107.1, so **107 per mm²** [1]
**(b)** Upper surface: 4 ÷ 0.196 = 20.4 per mm² [1]; 107.1 ÷ 20.4 = **5.25, so about 5 times as many** (equivalent to 21 ÷ 4 = 5.25) [1]
**(c)** Count several fields of view on each surface and calculate a mean (or use more than one leaf) [1]

**8. (a)** speed = distance ÷ time = 15 ÷ 2.5 [1] = **6 cm per hour** [1]
**(b)** Xylem [1]
**(c)** 6 × 6 = **36 cm** [1]

**9. (a)** The xylem is on the inside and the phloem is on the outside of each bundle [1]
**(b)** In a primary root the xylem is in the centre, not in separate bundles in a ring [1]

**10.** It has air spaces [1]; so that gases (carbon dioxide and oxygen) can diffuse through the leaf to and from the cells [1]

**11.** Any two of the following, 1 mark each [2]: the lower surface is usually in shade and cooler, so less water is lost by evaporation through the stomata; gases can still enter and leave through the many lower stomata; the upper surface is exposed to more sunlight and heat, so fewer stomata reduce water loss

**12.** Marking points, 1 mark each [6]:
- upper epidermis covers the top surface
- palisade mesophyll carries out photosynthesis (chloroplasts absorb light)
- spongy mesophyll has air spaces to facilitate diffusion of gases
- lower epidermis covers the bottom surface
- xylem transports substances (water) around the plant
- phloem transports substances (sugars) around the plant

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving photosynthesis to the spongy mesophyll rather than the palisade mesophyll.
- Saying the epidermis "makes" food.
- Putting the xylem on the outside of the phloem in a stem bundle.
- Writing that the root xylem is in a ring near the edge.
- Using the wrong unit for speed, or mixing hours and minutes.
- Using a single field of view as if it were reliable.
