## Section A

**1.** State the general form of a straight line and explain what m and c represent. **[2]**

**2.** State the shape of the graph of: y = x², y = x³, y = 1/x. **[3]**

## Section B

**3.** A line passes through (−2, 7) and (4, −5).

**(a)** Find its gradient. **[2]**
**(b)** Find its equation in the form y = mx + c. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the equation of the line perpendicular to it through (4, −5). **[3]**

**4.** For y = x² − 4x − 5:

**(a)** Find where the curve crosses the x-axis and the y-axis. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of the turning point by completing the square. **[3]**
**(c)** State the equation of the line of symmetry. **[1]**

**5.** Describe the transformation of y = f(x) given by each:

**(a)** y = f(x) + 3 **(b)** y = f(x − 2) **(c)** y = −f(x) **(d)** y = 2f(x) **[4]**

**6.** Explain why the graph of y = 1/x has two asymptotes, and state their equations. **[3]**

**7.** Explain how to find the solutions of x² − 4x − 5 = 2x − 1 graphically, and state how many there are. **[4]**

---

## Answers

**1.** y = mx + c [1]; **m is the gradient** and **c is the y-intercept** [1].

**2.** y = x² — a **parabola** [1]. y = x³ — a **cubic curve with rotational symmetry about the origin** [1]. y = 1/x — a **hyperbola in two separate branches** [1].

**3. (a)** m = (−5 − 7) ÷ (4 − (−2)) [1] = −12 ÷ 6 = **−2** [1].
**(b)** y − 7 = −2(x + 2) [1]; y = −2x − 4 + 7 [1]; **y = −2x + 3** [1].
**(c)** Perpendicular gradient = **½** [1]; y + 5 = ½(x − 4) [1]; **y = ½x − 7** [1].

**4. (a)** y-axis: x = 0 gives **(0, −5)** [1]. x-axis: (x − 5)(x + 1) = 0 [1], so **(5, 0) and (−1, 0)** [1] [1].
**(b)** x² − 4x − 5 = (x − 2)² − 4 − 5 [1] = (x − 2)² − 9 [1]; turning point **(2, −9)** [1].
**(c)** **x = 2** [1].

**5. (a)** Translation **3 units upwards** [1]. **(b)** Translation **2 units to the right** [1]. **(c)** Reflection **in the x-axis** [1]. **(d)** **Vertical stretch, scale factor 2** [1].

**6.** As **x approaches 0 the value of 1/x becomes infinitely large**, so the curve approaches but never meets **x = 0** [1] [1]. As **x becomes very large, 1/x approaches but never reaches zero**, so the curve approaches **y = 0** [1].

**7.** **Draw both graphs on the same axes** — the parabola y = x² − 4x − 5 and the straight line y = 2x − 1 [1] [1]. The solutions are the **x-coordinates of the points where the two graphs intersect** [1]. Algebraically x² − 6x − 4 = 0, which has a positive discriminant, so there are **two solutions** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the reciprocal instead of the negative reciprocal for a perpendicular gradient.
- Giving the turning point as (−2, −9) — the sign inside the bracket reverses.
- Confusing f(x − 2) with a translation to the left.
- Reading off the y-coordinates rather than the x-coordinates at intersection.
