Condensed recall for section 6.2 (Statistical measures) of the 4MA1 specification. Averages, range, the estimated mean for grouped data and the modal class are needed on both tiers; quartiles, the interquartile range and readings from cumulative frequency diagrams are **Higher tier only**. Worked examples are in the [Statistical measures study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-statistical-measures-edexcel-igcse-maths/); exam-style questions are in the [Statistical measures practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-statistical-measures-edexcel-igcse-maths-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Average | A single typical value for a data set |
| Mean | Total ÷ number of values |
| Median | Middle value of the ordered data |
| Mode | Most frequent value |
| Range | Largest − smallest |
| Discrete data | Data that can only take separate values, such as counts |
| Modal class | Class with the highest frequency |
| Measure of spread | A number describing how varied the data are (Higher) |
| Lower / upper quartile | Values one quarter / three quarters of the way through the ordered data (Higher) |
| Interquartile range (IQR) | Upper quartile − lower quartile (Higher) |

## Formulae

```
mean (list) = Σx ÷ n
mean (table) = Σfx ÷ Σf
estimated mean (grouped) = Σ(f × midpoint) ÷ Σf
median position = (n + 1)/2
total = mean × number of values
```

Higher:

```
lower quartile position = (n + 1)/4      upper quartile position = 3(n + 1)/4
IQR = upper quartile − lower quartile
from a cumulative frequency diagram: read at n/2, n/4, 3n/4
```

## Which average?

| Data | Best average | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Contains an extreme value | Median | Not distorted by the extreme |
| Non-numerical (colours, brands) | Mode | The only one that works |
| Numerical, no extremes | Mean | Uses every value |

## Mean problems: work with totals

- **Missing value:** total needed − total known.
- **Combining groups:** add the two totals, divide by the combined number. Never average two means of different-sized groups.
- **Adding a value:** new mean × new count − old total = the new value.
- **Unknown frequency:** write Σfx and Σf in terms of k, set Σfx = mean × Σf, solve.

Mini example: mean of 4 numbers is 10; a fifth number makes the mean 11. Fifth number = 55 − 40 = 15.

## Frequency tables

- Mode = the value with the highest frequency (not the frequency itself).
- Median: running totals of f find the value in the middle position.
- Mean: add an fx column.

Mini example: values 1, 2, 3 with frequencies 5, 8, 7 (Σf = 20). Running totals 5, 13, 20, so the 10th and 11th values are both 2 and the median is 2. Σfx = 5 + 16 + 21 = 42, so the mean is 42 ÷ 20 = 2.1. The mode is 2.

## Grouped data

- Midpoint = (lower boundary + upper boundary) ÷ 2. Work it out for each class: unequal classes have different midpoints.
- The mean is an **estimate** because midpoints replace the actual values.
- The modal class is the class with the highest frequency.

## Higher tier only: spread

- The range uses two values only, so one extreme changes it a lot.
- The IQR covers the middle 50% and ignores extremes.
- Choose n so that positions are whole numbers when possible: n = 11 gives quartiles at the 3rd and 9th values; n = 15 at the 4th and 12th; n = 19 at the 5th and 15th.
- From a cumulative frequency graph: read across from the vertical axis at n/4, n/2, 3n/4; read the answers on the **horizontal** axis.

## Comparing distributions

Two sentences: one comparing averages, one comparing spreads, each with context. "Higher median → greater on average." "Smaller IQR → more consistent."

## Exam traps

- Median from unordered data.
- Median of an even number of values: halfway between the two middle values.
- Σfx ÷ number of classes.
- Mode given as a frequency.
- Upper boundaries instead of midpoints.
- Averaging two group means.
- Quartiles read off the wrong axis.
- Comparisons without context.

## Self-test

1. Find the median of 9, 2, 6, 4, 7, 3.
2. The mean of 8 numbers is 5. What is their total?
3. The mean of 4 numbers is 7. Three are 5, 8 and 9. Find the fourth.
4. 10 people have mean age 30; 15 people have mean age 40. Find the overall mean.
5. Which average suits favourite colours?
6. Find the midpoint of the class 20 < x ≤ 35.
7. Frequencies 3, 5, 2 for values 1, 2, 3. Find the mean.
8. Why is a grouped mean only an estimate?
9. (Higher) 11 ordered values: where are the quartiles?
10. (Higher) Lower quartile 18, upper quartile 31. Find the IQR.
11. (Higher) Total frequency 200 on a cumulative frequency graph. At what cumulative frequencies do you read the quartiles?
12. (Higher) Why is the IQR not affected by one very large value?

### Answers

1. Ordered 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9: median (4 + 6) ÷ 2 = 5.
2. 40.
3. 28 − 22 = 6.
4. (300 + 600) ÷ 25 = 36.
5. The mode.
6. 27.5.
7. (3 + 10 + 6) ÷ 10 = 1.9.
8. Midpoints are used instead of the actual values.
9. The 3rd and 9th values.
10. 13.
11. 50 and 150.
12. It uses only the middle half of the data, so the largest values are left out.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between the mode and the modal class?**
The mode is the single most common value in ungrouped data. For grouped data you cannot see individual values, so you give the modal class: the class with the highest frequency.

**Is the interquartile range on the Foundation tier of 4MA1?**
No. Measures of spread beyond the range, including quartiles and the IQR, are Higher tier only in section 6.2.
