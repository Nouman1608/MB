Condensed notes for subtopic **1.13 Percentages** of Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024, 2025 to 2027. The [Percentages study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-percentages/) explains each method with worked examples; the [Percentages practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-percentages-practice/) give exam-style questions with mark schemes.

## Syllabus outcomes

1. Calculate a given percentage of a quantity.
2. Express one quantity as a percentage of another.
3. Calculate percentage increase or decrease.
4. Calculate with simple and compound interest (formulas are not given).
5. Calculate using reverse percentages.

Contexts include deposit, discount, profit and loss, earnings and percentages over 100%.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Multiplier | number you multiply by to apply a percentage change |
| Principal (P) | original sum of money |
| Rate (R) | interest rate per year, in percent |
| Cost price | price paid by the seller |
| Selling price | price at which the item is sold |
| Deposit | first part-payment |
| Commission | earnings as a percentage of sales |
| Reverse percentage | find the original value from the changed value |

## Multipliers

| Change | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| +15% | 1.15 |
| +5% | 1.05 |
| +120% | 2.2 |
| -12% | 0.88 |
| -35% | 0.65 |
| -7.5% | 0.925 |

Percentage of a quantity: multiplier = p/100. For example 45% of 360 = 0.45 × 360 = 162.

## Core methods

| Task | Method | Example |
|---|---|---|
| p% of a quantity | × p/100 | 35% of 240 = 84 |
| x as a % of y | x/y × 100 | 18 of 24 = 75% |
| Increase | × (1 + p/100) | 60 + 15% = 60 × 1.15 = 69 |
| Decrease | × (1 - p/100) | 80 - 12.5% = 70 |
| % change | (change ÷ original) × 100 | 45 to 54: 9/45 = 20% |
| Reverse | ÷ multiplier | 54 after +20%: 54 ÷ 1.2 = 45 |

## Profit, loss, discount and others

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Profit | selling price - cost price |
| Percentage profit | profit ÷ cost price × 100 |
| Loss | cost price - selling price |
| Sale price after d% discount | original × (1 - d/100) |
| Deposit | percentage × full price |
| Balance still to pay | full price - deposit |
| Commission | percentage × sales |

Examples: bought $400, sold $340: loss 15%. 15% deposit on $1 840 is $276, balance $1 564. Commission 3.5% of $12 400 is $434.

## Simple interest (formula not given)

```
I = P x R x T ÷ 100
```

| P | R | T | Interest | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $2 500 | 4% | 3 years | $300 | $2 800 |
| $3 200 | 3.5% | 4 years | $448 | $3 648 |
| $1 000 | 5% | 2 years | $100 | $1 100 |

Interest is the same each year.

## Compound interest (formula not given)

```
amount = P x (1 + R/100)^n
```

| P | R | n | Amount | Interest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $2 500 | 4% | 3 | $2 812.16 | $312.16 |
| $3 200 | 3.5% | 3 | $3 547.90 | $347.90 |
| $2 800 | 5% | 2 | $3 087.00 | $287.00 |

Compare with simple interest: compound interest is greater after the first year.

## Repeated change

Multiply by the multiplier once for each change.

| Situation | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Car loses 18% per year, $24 000, 2 years | 24 000 × 0.82² | $16 137.60 |
| +20% then -20% | 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96 | 4% decrease overall |
| +10% then +10% | 1.1 × 1.1 = 1.21 | 21% increase overall |
| -50% then +50% | 0.5 × 1.5 = 0.75 | 25% decrease overall |

Percentage changes are never simply added.

## Reverse percentages: method

1. Write the multiplier for the change.
2. Set original × multiplier = new value.
3. Divide the new value by the multiplier.

| Situation | Calculation | Original |
|---|---|---|
| After +20% the price is $54 | 54 ÷ 1.2 | $45 |
| After -12% the price is $66 | 66 ÷ 0.88 | $75 |
| Sold at 10% profit for $330 | 330 ÷ 1.1 | $300 |
| After +8% the price is $91.80 | 91.80 ÷ 1.08 | $85 |

## Percentages over 100

| Statement | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 150% of a number | 1.5 × the number |
| 240% of 50 | 120 |
| 90 as a percentage of 60 | 150% |
| New value is 130% of old | multiplier 1.3 |

## Exam traps

- Divide by the **original** to find percentage change.
- Reverse percentages need **division**, not subtracting a percentage of the new value.
- 5% increase has multiplier 1.05, not 1.5.
- Do not add successive percentage changes.
- Compound interest uses a power; simple interest uses multiplication by years.
- Interest question: say whether the answer is interest or total amount.
- Deposits are a percentage of the full price.
- Round money answers to the nearest cent at the end.

## Self-test

1. Find 12.5% of 480.
2. Express 34 as a percentage of 80.
3. Increase $250 by 8%.
4. A price falls from $80 to $68. Find the percentage decrease.
5. Find the simple interest on $1 500 at 4% for 5 years.
6. Find the compound amount on $1 000 at 5% for 2 years.
7. After a 25% increase, a rent is $750. Find the original rent.
8. A value increases by 10% and then by 20%. Find the overall percentage increase.

**Answers**

1. **60**.
2. **42.5%**.
3. 250 × 1.08 = **$270**.
4. 12 ÷ 80 × 100 = **15%**.
5. 1500 × 4 × 5 ÷ 100 = **$300**.
6. 1000 × 1.05² = **$1 102.50**.
7. 750 ÷ 1.25 = **$600**.
8. 1.1 × 1.2 = 1.32, so **32%**.
