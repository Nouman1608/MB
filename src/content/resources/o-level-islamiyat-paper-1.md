This guide covers **Paper 1**, one of two compulsory papers for
Cambridge O Level Islamiyat 2058, 2024–2025 series. Confirmed
independently against 2058's own specification PDF: it shares an
identical Paper 1 / Paper 2 four-topic structure with Cambridge IGCSE
Islamiyat 0493.

## Where this fits in 2058

Paper 1 covers the Qur'an and the life of the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh),
while Paper 2 (not covered in this guide) covers the Hadiths, the
Rightly Guided Caliphs, and the Articles of Faith and Pillars of Islam.
Together the two papers form the whole of 2058 — there is no separate
topic list beyond these four sub-topics per paper.

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE O LEVEL ISLAMIYAT 2058 — PAPER 1**

- 1.1 Major Themes of the Qur'an — the central themes and teachings
running through the Qur'an
- 1.2 The History and Importance of the Qur'an — how the Qur'an was
revealed and compiled, and its significance
- 1.3 The Life and Importance of the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh) — the key
events of his life and their significance for Muslims
- 1.4 The First Islamic Community — the early Muslim community in
Makkah and Madinah, and its formative events

## How to approach it

As with the IGCSE variant of this syllabus, each paper mixes a
compulsory question with a choice from the remainder, so breadth of
preparation across all four sub-topics matters more than depth in only
one or two — a candidate weak on one sub-topic risks being forced into
unfamiliar territory if that is where the compulsory question falls.
Answers are strongest when they combine accurate factual recall — dates,
names, sequence of events — with an explanation of significance, since
1.2 and 1.3 explicitly ask for an understanding of importance, not just
narrative retelling. Build a simple timeline of the Prophet's life and
the early community (1.3 and 1.4) as a revision anchor, since many exam
questions ask candidates to place events in relation to one another.
For 1.1 and 1.2, practise linking a named theme of the Qur'an to a
specific example of its teaching rather than listing themes as abstract
labels — vague, unsupported statements are a common way marks are lost
even when the underlying knowledge is correct. As with any two-paper
qualification examined across a defined series, confirm your own
centre's exact entry pattern and paper timetable directly with your
school rather than assuming it matches another qualification's.
