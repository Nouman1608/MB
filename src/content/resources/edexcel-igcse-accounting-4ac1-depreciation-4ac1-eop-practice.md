These questions cover section 5.2, Depreciation, in Topic 5 of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Accounting (4AC1) specification, Issue 1, October 2016. Most of them start from a trial balance or year-end note, as Topic 5 work does in Paper 2. Amounts are in dollars, written without a currency sign, and every business is invented. Teaching is in the [depreciation study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-4ac1-depreciation-4ac1-eop/) and the [depreciation revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-4ac1-depreciation-4ac1-eop-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.**
**(a)** A business keeps a provision for depreciation account. State the account credited when the year's depreciation is recorded. **[1]**
**(b)** State how carrying value is calculated. **[1]**

**2.** Explain one cause of depreciation of a fleet of delivery vans. **[2]**

**3.** A trial balance shows fixtures at cost 24,000 and provision for depreciation of fixtures 9,000. Fixtures are depreciated at 15% a year by the reducing balance method.
**(a)** Calculate the depreciation charge for the year. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the carrying value at the year end. **[1]**

**4.** Distinguish between the straight line and reducing balance methods of depreciation. **[2]**

**5.** A business has a year end of 31 March. It buys a machine for 14,400 on 1 August. Machinery is depreciated at 12.5% a year on cost, calculated monthly. Calculate the charge for the year ended 31 March. **[2]**

**6.** A non-current asset cost 20,000. Depreciation to date is 13,800. It is sold for 5,500.
**(a)** Calculate the profit or loss on disposal. **[2]**
**(b)** State the effect on profit for the year. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Fenwick Office Supplies has a year end of 31 December. Its trial balance includes office equipment at cost 32,000, provision for depreciation of office equipment 12,800, motor vehicles at cost 54,000 and provision for depreciation of motor vehicles 21,600. Office equipment is depreciated at 25% by the reducing balance method; motor vehicles at 20% a year on cost.
**(a)** Calculate the depreciation of office equipment for the year. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the depreciation of motor vehicles for the year. **[1]**
**(c)** Prepare the non-current assets section of the statement of financial position. **[3]**

**8.** Bayview Laundry buys a washing machine for 32,000. It expects to use it for four years and sell it for 2,000. Profit before depreciation in Year 1 is 41,000.
**(a)** Calculate the annual straight line charge. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the charge for each of the four years using 50% reducing balance. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate Year 1 profit under each method. **[2]**
**(d)** Advise Bayview Laundry which method to use, giving one reason. **[3]**

**9.** Ashgrove Bakery has a year end of 31 December 2027. On 1 January 2027 ovens at cost were 48,000 and the provision for depreciation of ovens was 19,200. Ovens are depreciated at 12.5% a year on cost, calculated monthly.
- On 31 May 2027 an oven that cost 9,600 on 1 January 2024 was sold for 4,800, received by bank.
- On 1 July 2027 a new oven was bought for 12,000, paid by bank.

**(a)** Calculate the depreciation of ovens for 2027. **[4]**
**(b)** Prepare the ovens at cost account, the provision for depreciation of ovens account and the disposal of ovens account for 2027. **[9]**
**(c)** Show the ovens in the statement of financial position at 31 December 2027. **[2]**

**10.** The draft profit of Linfield Motors is 38,500. These errors are found:
1. Depreciation of fixtures, 2,400, was omitted.
2. Vehicles (cost 30,000, provision at the start of the year 10,800) were depreciated at 20% of cost. The policy is 20% reducing balance.
3. A profit on disposal of 450 was recorded as a loss of 450.

**(a)** Calculate the corrected profit. **[5]**
**(b)** State the effect of error 1 on the statement of financial position. **[1]**

**11.** The owner of Ashgrove Bakery says: "Depreciation is just money we put aside to buy new ovens." Explain why the owner is wrong and why depreciation is still charged. **[4]**

**12.** Prepare journal entries, with narratives, to record:
- depreciation of fixtures for the year of 5,200
- the disposal of a fixture that cost 3,000, with depreciation to date of 2,100, sold for 1,000 cash. For this question, show the cash receipt in the journal.
**[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Provision for depreciation (of that class of asset) [1].
**(b)** Cost less depreciation to date (accumulated depreciation) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The asset account stays at cost; the credit goes to the provision.

**2.** The vans are driven long distances, so engines and bodies wear out through use (wear and tear) [1]; as they wear they are worth less and will need replacing, so part of their cost is charged each year [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the cause to the vans; a definition of depreciation alone does not explain a cause.

**3. (a)** 15% × (24,000 − 9,000) [1] = 15% × 15,000 = **2,250** [1].
**(b)** 15,000 − 2,250 = **12,750** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The trial balance provision is the opening figure, so it reduces the base before the percentage is applied.

**4.** Straight line charges the same amount every year, based on cost (less residual value) [1]; reducing balance applies a fixed percentage to the carrying value at the start of each year, so the charge falls each year [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Distinguish" needs both methods described against the same feature.

**5.** August to March is 8 months [1]. 14,400 × 12.5% × 8/12 = **1,200** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Count the months owned before the year end, including August.

**6. (a)** Carrying value 20,000 − 13,800 = 6,200 [1]; 5,500 − 6,200 = **loss of 700** [1].
**(b)** Profit for the year is reduced by 700 [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** 25% × (32,000 − 12,800) [1] = 25% × 19,200 = **4,800** [1].
**(b)** 20% × 54,000 = **10,800** [1].
**(c)**

| Non-current assets | Cost | Depreciation to date | Carrying value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Office equipment | 32,000 | 17,600 | 14,400 |
| Motor vehicles | 54,000 | 32,400 | 21,600 |
| Total | 86,000 | 50,000 | 36,000 |

Office equipment row [1]; motor vehicles row [1]; totals [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Depreciation to date is opening provision plus this year's charge: 12,800 + 4,800 and 21,600 + 10,800.

**8. (a)** (32,000 − 2,000) ÷ 4 = **7,500** [1].
**(b)** Year 1: 50% × 32,000 = **16,000** [1]. Year 2: 50% × 16,000 = **8,000** [1]. Year 3: **4,000**; Year 4: **2,000** [1].
**(c)** Straight line: 41,000 − 7,500 = **33,500** [1]. Reducing balance: 41,000 − 16,000 = **25,000** [1].
**(d)** Reducing balance [1]: a washing machine in heavy use loses most of its value and efficiency early and needs more repairs later [1], so high early depreciation and lower later depreciation (with rising repair costs) spread the total cost of using it more evenly; both methods charge 30,000 in total [1]. Straight line earns the same marks with a matching reason: the machine gives roughly equal service each year, so equal charges match cost to benefit. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Totals are equal (16,000 + 8,000 + 4,000 + 2,000 = 30,000); say so, because it shows that the choice is about timing.

**9. (a)** Oven sold: 9,600 × 12.5% × 5/12 = 500 [1]. New oven: 12,000 × 12.5% × 6/12 = 750 [1]. Other ovens: (48,000 − 9,600) × 12.5% = 38,400 × 12.5% = 4,800 [1]. Total **6,050** [1].
**(b)** Depreciation on the oven sold: 2024 to 2026 is 3 × 1,200 = 3,600, plus 500, so 4,100. Carrying value 5,500; proceeds 4,800; loss 700.

**Ovens at cost**

| Debit | Amount | Credit | Amount |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1 Jan Balance b/d | 48,000 | 31 May Disposal | 9,600 |
| 1 Jul Bank | 12,000 | 31 Dec Balance c/d | 50,400 |
| | 60,000 | | 60,000 |

Bank 12,000 [1]; disposal 9,600 [1]; balance 50,400 [1].

**Provision for depreciation of ovens**

| Debit | Amount | Credit | Amount |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 31 May Disposal | 4,100 | 1 Jan Balance b/d | 19,200 |
| 31 Dec Balance c/d | 21,150 | 31 Dec Income statement | 6,050 |
| | 25,250 | | 25,250 |

Transfer 4,100 [1]; charge 6,050 [1]; balance 21,150 [1].

**Disposal of ovens**

| Debit | Amount | Credit | Amount |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 31 May Ovens at cost | 9,600 | 31 May Provision for depreciation | 4,100 |
| | | 31 May Bank | 4,800 |
| | | 31 Dec Income statement (loss) | 700 |
| | 9,600 | | 9,600 |

Cost and depreciation entries [1]; bank 4,800 [1]; loss of 700 on the credit side [1].
**(c)** Ovens: cost 50,400 [1], less depreciation to date 21,150, carrying value **29,250** [1]. **[15]**
*Examiner insight:* Charge the year-of-sale depreciation (500) before transferring the sold oven's depreciation, or the transfer and the loss are both wrong.

**10. (a)** Error 1: profit falls by 2,400 [1]. Error 2: charge made 6,000; correct charge 20% × (30,000 − 10,800) = 3,840 [1], so profit rises by 2,160 [1]. Error 3: profit rises by 900 (removing the 450 loss and adding the 450 profit) [1]. Corrected profit = 38,500 − 2,400 + 2,160 + 900 = **39,160** [1].
**(b)** The carrying value of fixtures is overstated by 2,400 (it must be reduced) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An item shown on the wrong side moves profit by twice its value.

**11.** Depreciation is not money put aside: no cash leaves the business or is saved when it is charged [1]; the cash was paid when the ovens were bought [1]. It is charged because the ovens are used to earn revenue over several years [1], so the accruals concept matches part of their cost to each year that benefits, giving a fair profit and a realistic carrying value [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using cost as the reducing balance base when the trial balance gives a provision.
- Leaving out this year's charge from depreciation to date in the statement of financial position.
- Counting months wrongly, or ignoring a monthly policy.
- Forgetting the year-of-sale charge on an asset sold.
- Treating a profit on disposal as a loss, or recording it on the wrong side.
- Writing that depreciation saves cash.
