Condensed notes on section **3.6.3 Making human resource decisions: improving organisational design and managing the human resource flow** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). The full explanations and worked examples are in the [organisational design and HR flow study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-organisational-design-and-human-resource-flow/); exam-style questions are in the [organisational design and HR flow practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-organisational-design-and-human-resource-flow-practice/).

## Specification content

- Models of organisational structure: functional, product-based, regional, matrix.
- Influences on organisational design: authority, span, hierarchy, delegation, centralisation and decentralisation.
- The value of changing organisational design.
- How managing the HR flow helps meet HR objectives: HR plan, recruitment, training, redeployment, redundancy.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organisational structure | How a business is arranged: who is responsible for what, who reports to whom |
| Authority | The right to make decisions and give instructions |
| Hierarchy | The levels of management; the chain of command |
| Span of control | Number of subordinates a manager directly supervises |
| Delegation | Passing authority for a task to a subordinate |
| Centralisation | Decisions made at the top |
| Decentralisation | Decisions pushed down to lower levels or local units |
| Delayering | Removing levels of hierarchy |
| HR flow | Movement of people into, through and out of the business |
| Natural wastage | Not replacing staff who leave |
| Redeployment | Moving staff to different jobs within the business |
| Redundancy | Dismissal because the job is no longer needed |

## Structures compared

| Structure | Organised by | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| Functional | Activity (marketing, operations, finance, HR) | Specialisation; clear careers | Silo thinking; slow coordination; product performance hard to see |
| Product-based | Product or group | Product focus; accountable managers | Duplicated functions; higher cost; rivalry |
| Regional | Area or country | Local knowledge; quick local decisions | Duplication; inconsistency; weak central control |
| Matrix | Function and project or product; two bosses | Flexible use of specialists; teamwork | Unclear authority; conflict; slow decisions |

Suitability: one product, one country suits functional; many products suit product-based; global spread suits regional; project work suits matrix.

## Span, hierarchy and the shape of the organisation

- **Tall:** many levels, narrow spans; close control, slow communication, costly, many promotion steps.
- **Flat:** few levels, wide spans; fast communication, cheaper, more autonomy, fewer promotions.
- Wide spans suit experienced staff on routine work; narrow spans suit complex or risky work.

**Worked example.** 500 operatives, management post costs £40,000.

- Span 10: 50 + 5 + 1 = **56 posts** = £2.24m; 4 levels.
- Span 5: 100 + 20 + 4 + 1 = **125 posts** = £5.0m; 5 levels.
- The wider span saves **£2.76m** and speeds communication, but gives less supervision.

## Centralisation, decentralisation and delegation

| | Centralised | Decentralised |
|---|---|---|
| For | Consistency, control, economies of scale | Speed, local knowledge, motivation, develops managers |
| Against | Slow, overloads senior managers, ignores local needs | Inconsistency, harder to control, risk of poor decisions |

Delegation needs trust, clear instructions, training and real authority. It spreads workload and develops staff.

## Influences on design

Size and growth; products and markets; nature of the work; skills and attitudes of staff; management style; technology (supports flatter, remote and flexible structures); rate of change; cost; external environment.

## Value of changing design

**Benefits:** faster decisions; lower costs; better communication; closer to customers; better use of skills; motivation; flexibility. **Costs:** disruption; resistance; redundancy and retraining; loss of experience; confusion; time lag. Delayering saves money but overloads remaining managers.

## The HR flow

**HR plan:** workforce needed (from forecast output, productivity, technology) against workforce available (current staff minus leavers plus internal moves).

**Worked example.** Need 240; have 250; turnover 12%.

- Leavers = 12% × 250 = **30**; supply = **220**; shortfall = **20**.
- Close by recruiting, retraining, redeploying, raising productivity or using overtime.
- If need were 200: surplus **20**; use natural wastage, redeployment, voluntary redundancy, then compulsory redundancy.

| Recruitment | Internal | External |
|---|---|---|
| For | Cheap, quick, motivating | Wider choice, new ideas |
| Against | Limited choice; creates another vacancy | Costly, slow; fit risk |

- **Training:** keeps skills current; supports retention and promotion.
- **Redeployment:** retains skills, avoids redundancy cost, keeps morale; but may need retraining and staff may resist.
- **Redundancy:** reduces costs and matches demand; but payments, lost skills, low morale, reputation damage, legal process.

**Redundancy example.** 15 posts at £8,000 each = **£120,000** one-off; saving 15 × £28,000 = **£420,000** a year; payback about **3.4 months**. Ignores lost output, effect on morale and rehiring costs.

## Exam traps

- Say when a structure suits the business, not just what it is.
- Delegation is the passing of authority; decentralisation is a structure-wide approach.
- Flat is not always better.
- Redundancy is a last resort; consider wastage, redeployment and voluntary options first.
- Internal recruitment creates another vacancy.

## Self-test

1. Name the four organisational structures in the specification.
2. Give one disadvantage of a matrix structure.
3. Define "span of control".
4. 240 operatives with a span of 8: how many supervisors are needed?
5. Give one advantage of a flat structure and one of a tall structure.
6. Give two advantages of decentralisation.
7. Give two reasons for changing organisational design.
8. Define "natural wastage".
9. A firm needs 180 staff. It has 190 and expects to lose 10% of them. Calculate the shortfall or surplus.
10. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of internal recruitment.
11. Give one advantage of redeployment over redundancy.
12. Give two costs of redundancy to a business.

### Answers

1. Functional, product-based, regional, matrix.
2. Any one: confusion over authority; conflict between managers; more meetings; slower decisions.
3. The number of subordinates a manager directly supervises.
4. 240 ÷ 8 = **30 supervisors**.
5. Flat (any one): faster communication; lower cost. Tall (any one): close control; more promotion steps.
6. Any two: faster local decisions; local knowledge; motivation; develops managers; frees senior managers.
7. Any two: growth; new markets or products; new technology; falling performance; cost cutting.
8. Not replacing employees who leave.
9. Leavers = 10% × 190 = 19; supply = 171; shortfall = 180 − 171 = **9**.
10. Advantage (any one): cheaper; quicker; motivating. Disadvantage (any one): limited choice; creates another vacancy.
11. It keeps skills and knowledge and avoids redundancy and recruitment costs.
12. Any two: redundancy payments; lost skills and experience; low morale; damaged reputation; rehiring costs.
