## Section A

**1.** State the difference between commercial and subsistence
farming. **[2]**

**2.** Sort these into inputs, processes and outputs: fertiliser,
harvesting, milk, labour, ploughing, wool. **[3]**

**3.** Name two natural causes of food shortages. **[2]**

**4.** Define *extensive farming*. **[1]**

**5.** State two effects of malnutrition on people. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Read the description of an illustrative farm.

> Farm F covers 2,000 hectares of semi-arid grassland. One family and
> two hired workers look after 1,500 beef cattle, which graze freely.
> The cattle are sold to a meat-packing company in a city 300 km away.

**(a)** Classify farm F using three terms. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of hectares per animal. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest how physical inputs explain why farm F is pastoral
rather than arable. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain why the farmer needs good transport links. **[2]**

**7.** Explain how the physical inputs of a river delta make it
suitable for wet rice cultivation. **[4]**

**8.** Explain how conflict can cause food shortages. **[4]**

**9.** A government in a drought-prone country is choosing between
two schemes: importing emergency food aid each year, or providing
farmers with drought-resistant seed and small irrigation pumps.
Evaluate the two schemes. **[6]**

**10.** For a named farm or agricultural system you have studied,
describe its inputs, processes and outputs. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** Commercial farming produces crops or animals to sell for
profit [1]; subsistence farming produces food mainly for the farmer's
own family, with little or nothing left to sell [1].

**2.** Inputs: fertiliser, labour [1]. Processes: harvesting,
ploughing [1]. Outputs: milk, wool [1].

**3.** Any two [2]: drought; floods; tropical storms; pests such as
locusts; plant or animal diseases.

**4.** Farming with low inputs of labour and capital per hectare over
a large area, giving low output per hectare [1].

**5.** Any two [2]: stunted growth in children; weakness and tiredness,
so less able to work; lower resistance to disease; poor concentration
at school; in severe cases, death.

**6. (a)** Extensive [1], commercial [1], pastoral [1].
**(b)** 2,000 ÷ 1,500 = 1.3 hectares per animal (to one decimal place)
[1].
**(c)** Up to 3 marks: the semi-arid climate gives too little or
unreliable rainfall for crops without irrigation [1]; grassland can be
grazed even when it would not support a crop [1]; soils may be thin
or the growing season short, so rearing animals that move across large
areas is the most reliable use of the land [1].
**(d)** The cattle must be taken 300 km to the meat-packing company
[1]; good roads or railways reduce journey time and cost and keep the
animals in good condition, so the farmer earns more [1].

**7.** Up to 4 marks: the land is flat, so fields can be flooded evenly
and bunded easily [1]; alluvium deposited by the rivers is fertile
and renewed by floods [1]; high temperatures allow a long growing
season [1]; heavy monsoon rain supplies the water rice needs, and clay
soils hold water in the paddies [1].

**8.** Up to 4 marks: farmers flee or are killed, so fields are not
planted or harvested [1]; crops, stores and animals are destroyed or
stolen [1]; markets close and roads become unsafe, so food cannot be
traded or moved [1]; aid agencies cannot reach people in need [1]; money
is spent on fighting rather than farming [1].

**9.** Up to 6 marks for a balanced evaluation with a conclusion.
Food aid: saves lives quickly in a crisis [1]; but it is expensive
every year, does not increase local output, and free food can lower
prices so local farmers cannot sell their crops [1–2]. Seeds and pumps:
raise output in the long term and reduce future need for aid [1];
but they cost money at the start, pumps need fuel and maintenance, and
irrigation may lower groundwater [1–2]. Conclusion: a combination is
best, with aid kept for emergencies while seeds and pumps build
resilience [1].

**10.** Levels-style marking.
*Level 1 (1–3 marks)*: simple inputs, processes or outputs with no
named place.
*Level 2 (4–5 marks)*: a named farm or system with developed points on
at least two parts of the system.
*Level 3 (6–7 marks)*: a named farm or system with developed,
place-specific detail on inputs (physical and human), processes and
outputs.
Indicative content (wet rice, Ganges–Brahmaputra delta): flat delta,
fertile alluvium, high temperatures, monsoon rain from about June to
September; small family plots, hand labour, draught animals or power
tillers, seed and fertiliser; seedlings raised in a nursery and
transplanted into flooded, bunded fields, weeded and harvested by
hand; rice for the family and local sale, straw as a by-product, with
a second crop in the dry season where irrigation is available.

## A note on exam technique for this subtopic

Classify on all three scales, keep physical and human inputs separate,
and explain *how* each factor affects the farm rather than just naming
it. For solutions, link each one to a specific cause and weigh its
drawbacks.
