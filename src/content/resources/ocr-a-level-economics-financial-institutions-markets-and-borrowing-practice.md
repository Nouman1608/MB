## Questions

**1.** A bond has a face value of 100 cu and pays a coupon of 6 cu a year. Calculate the current yield when its price is 75 cu and when it is 120 cu, and state the relationship between price and yield. **[3]**

**2.** A household buys a house for 150,000 cu with a deposit of 15,000 cu and takes an interest-only mortgage at 4.2%.

**(a)** Calculate the loan-to-value ratio. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the monthly interest payment. **[2]**
**(c)** House prices fall 10%. Calculate the household's equity and explain one effect of falling house prices on the real economy. **[3]**

**3.** A pay day lender lends 400 cu for 20 days and the borrower repays 480 cu.

**(a)** Calculate the cost over the loan period as a percentage. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the simple annualised rate. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why lenders charge such high rates. **[2]**

**4.** An insurer has 2,000 policies. Each has a 1.5% chance of a claim of 8,000 cu, and the premium is 150 cu.

**(a)** Calculate expected claims per policy and in total. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate expected profit. **[1]**
**(c)** The claim chance rises to 2.5% for every policy because of a regional flood. Calculate the result and explain what this shows about pooling. **[2]**

**5.** A hedge fund has capital of 20 and holds assets of 120.

**(a)** Calculate its leverage. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the return on capital if asset values rise 5% and if they fall 5%. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the fall in asset values that wipes out its capital. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain one risk to the real economy of highly leveraged funds. **[2]**

**6.** A lender requires an expected return of 6% and expects 5% of borrowers to default.

**(a)** Calculate the interest rate needed if nothing is recovered on default. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the rate needed if 60% of the loan is recovered on default. **[2]**
**(c)** State what the difference shows. **[1]**

**7.** Bank P lends 50m cu overnight to Bank R at 2.8% a year.

**(a)** Calculate the interest for one night. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate it if the rate were 4.0%. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why inter-bank lending might stop in a crisis and what follows for borrowers. **[2]**

**8.** A credit card balance is 1,200 cu. Interest is 2.5% a month and the minimum payment is 30 cu.

**(a)** Calculate the monthly interest. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how much of the minimum payment reduces the debt. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the effective annual rate to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(d)** An overdraft of 800 cu is used for 45 days at 19.5% a year. Calculate its cost. **[2]**

**9.** Households in Country C hold shares worth 500m cu. Share prices fall 30%.

**(a)** Calculate the loss of wealth. **[1]**
**(b)** Consumption falls by 5% of the lost wealth. Calculate the fall. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain two other ways a fall in the stock market may affect the real economy. **[4]**

**10.** A good costs 60 cu. The exchange rate is 1 cu = 0.50 foreign currency units (fu), then the cu depreciates to 0.45 fu.

**(a)** Calculate the foreign price before and after. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the effect on the trade balance is uncertain. **[2]**

**11.**

**(a)** Distinguish a retail bank from an investment bank. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the investment decisions of a pension fund affect the real economy. **[4]**

**12.** Extract (invented): in Country C, bank lending to firms grew by 12% a year for five years, house prices rose by 60%, and household debt rose faster than incomes. Then banks tightened lending sharply, and investment fell by 18% in a year.

Evaluate the role of the financial sector in the real economy of Country C. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** At 75 cu: 6 ÷ 75 = 8.0% [1]. At 120 cu: 6 ÷ 120 = 5.0% [1]. Price and yield move in opposite directions because the coupon is fixed [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the division. A yield without the price used loses the link to the method.

**2. (a)** Loan = 150,000 − 15,000 = 135,000 cu [1]. LTV = 135,000 ÷ 150,000 = **90%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 135,000 × 0.042 ÷ 12 [1] = **472.50 cu** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Value 135,000 cu, so equity = **0** [1]. Falling prices push borrowers into negative equity, and they cut spending to service debt [1]. Banks face losses if borrowers default, which reduces lending [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that give the number and then follow it to consumption or lending tend to earn more than those that stop at the figure.

**3. (a)** 80 ÷ 400 = **20%** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 20 × 365 ÷ 20 [1] = **365%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Default risk is high because borrowers have few other options [1]. Small, short loans have high admin costs per unit lent [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Put different loans on the same time basis before comparing. A 20% one-period cost is not the same as a 20% annual rate.

**4. (a)** 0.015 × 8,000 = **120 cu** [1]. 2,000 × 120 = **240,000 cu** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Premium income 2,000 × 150 = 300,000. Profit = 300,000 − 240,000 = **60,000 cu** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Claims 2,000 × 0.025 × 8,000 = 400,000, so result = **−100,000 cu** [1]. Pooling relies on claims being independent. A shared event makes them move together, so the insurer cannot rely on averages [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the number to the idea. The loss is the evidence, and the explanation names correlated risk.

**5. (a)** 120 ÷ 20 = **6** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 5% of 120 = 6, and 6 ÷ 20 = **+30%** [1]. A fall gives **−30%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 20 ÷ 120 = **16.7%** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Losses force fund sales, which push asset prices down further [1]. Banks that lent to the fund make losses and cut lending to firms and households [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A chain from forced selling to lending to the real economy shows more than saying leverage is risky.

**6. (a)** (1 − 0.05)(1 + r) = 1.06 [1]. r = 1.06 ÷ 0.95 − 1 = **11.6%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 0.95(1 + r) + 0.05 × 0.60 = 1.06 [1]. 1 + r = 1.03 ÷ 0.95, so r = **8.4%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Security lowers the rate needed by about 3.2 percentage points because the lender's loss on default is smaller [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Use percentage points for a difference between rates. Writing percent suggests a relative change.

**7. (a)** 50,000,000 × 0.028 × 1/365 [1] = **3,835.62 cu** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 50,000,000 × 0.04 ÷ 365 = **5,479.45 cu** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Banks doubt each other's solvency, so they refuse to lend or demand a high rate [1]. Banks then cut lending to firms and households, which lowers consumption and investment [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the lack of trust as the cause, then follow it outside the banking sector.

**8. (a)** 1,200 × 0.025 = **30 cu** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 30 − 30 = **0**, so the debt does not fall [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** (1.025)¹² − 1 [1] = 0.3449, so **34.5%** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** 800 × 0.195 × 45 ÷ 365 [1] = **19.23 cu** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A nominal annual rate of 30% is not the effective rate. Show the compounding step.

**9. (a)** 500 × 0.30 = **150m cu** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 150 × 0.05 = **7.5m cu** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Firms find it harder and dearer to raise funds by issuing shares, so investment may fall [2]. Pension funds hold shares, so asset values fall and future retirement spending is reduced; confidence may also fall [2]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each effect needs its chain. Naming an effect is only the start.

**10. (a)** 60 × 0.50 = **30 fu** [1]; 60 × 0.45 = **27 fu** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (27 − 30) ÷ 30 = **−10%** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The effect depends on how responsive demand for exports and imports is, so spending on exports may rise or fall [1]. Effects take time to appear, so the trade balance may first worsen [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Cheaper exports do not guarantee a better trade balance. Say what it depends on.

**11. (a)** A retail bank serves households and small firms with accounts and loans [1]. An investment bank issues and trades securities and advises large clients [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The fund collects contributions and buys bonds and shares [1]. This supplies long-term funds to firms and governments to finance investment [1]. Falling asset values reduce what the fund can pay out [1], which cuts future spending by retired households [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Define the institution, then explain its effect. Both parts are needed.

**12.** Lending to firms funded investment, which raises AD now and capacity later [1]. Rising house prices raised wealth and borrowing, so consumption rose [1]. Debt rising faster than incomes made households vulnerable [1]. Sharp tightening cut investment by 18% [1], which reduces AD and employment [1]. Evaluation: the effect depends on whether lending funded productive investment or only asset prices [1]. Leverage and household debt made the bust worse than the boom was helpful [1]. Long-run gains in capacity must be set against short-run instability [1]. More data on how much lending went into investment would help [1]. Judgement: the sector was useful but its benefit depended on stable, well-priced lending [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the figures in the extract. A verdict that states what it depends on is stronger than a one-sided or balanced-but-undecided list.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that bond prices rise when interest rates rise.
- Comparing pay day and bank rates on different time bases.
- Stating a result without following it to AD, AS or employment.
- Mixing up the retail, commercial and investment roles.
- Leaving out security when explaining why rates differ.
- Using percent when the question needs percentage points.
- Ignoring the data and giving generic points in the evaluation.
