These questions cover section **3.7.2 Gravitational field strength** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification: force fields, field lines, g = F/m and g = GM/r² in a radial field. Section 3.7 is International A-level only. Marks are shown for each part. The planets, moon and stars in Questions 3, 7, 9 and 11 are invented for practice.

Use G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻², g at the Earth's surface = 9.81 N kg⁻¹, Earth mass 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg and Earth radius 6.37 × 10⁶ m.

Revise first with the [gravitational field strength study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-gravitational-field-strength-oxfordaqa/) and the [gravitational field strength revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-gravitational-field-strength-oxfordaqa-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by a force field. **[1]**

**2.** At a distance 3R from the centre of a planet of radius R, the gravitational field strength is what fraction of its surface value?

A: 1/3

B: 1/6

C: 1/9

D: 1/27 **[1]**

**3.** A planet has mass 6.4 × 10²³ kg and radius 3.4 × 10⁶ m. Calculate the gravitational field strength at its surface. **[2]**

**4.** State the gravitational field strength at a distance of two Earth radii from the Earth's centre. **[1]**

**5.** The direction of the gravitational field strength at a point near a planet is

A: away from the planet's centre

B: towards the planet's centre

C: along the planet's surface

D: in the direction of the planet's motion **[1]**

**6.** Explain why the gravitational field strength at a point does not depend on the mass of the object placed there. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A planet has 64 times the mass of its moon. Their centres are 9.0 × 10⁸ m apart.

**(a)** Calculate the distance from the planet's centre of the point on the line between them where the resultant gravitational field is zero. **[3]**

**(b)** A probe is released from rest on this line, 7.0 × 10⁸ m from the planet's centre. Explain which way it moves. **[2]**

**8.** A space station orbits 4.0 × 10⁵ m above the Earth's surface.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational field strength at the station. **[2]**

**(b)** Express this as a percentage of the value at the Earth's surface. **[1]**

**9.** A moon has radius 2.5 × 10⁶ m and surface gravitational field strength 1.8 N kg⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate its mass. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate its mean density. **[2]**

**10.**

**(a)** Sketch or describe the gravitational field lines around an isolated spherical planet. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how the field lines within a few hundred metres of the planet's surface should be drawn, and explain why. **[2]**

**11.** Two stars, each of mass 2.0 × 10³⁰ kg, have centres 6.0 × 10¹¹ m apart. Point P lies on the line between them, 2.0 × 10¹¹ m from star A.

**(a)** Calculate the field strength at P due to each star. **[2]**

**(b)** Determine the size and direction of the resultant field at P. **[2]**

**12.**

**(a)** Define gravitational field strength. **[1]**

**(b)** Show that the units N kg⁻¹ and m s⁻² are equivalent. **[1]**

**(c)** Derive g = GM/r² from Newton's law of gravitation. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A region in which a body experiences a force [1].

**2.** **C** [1]

**3.** g = GM/R² = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 6.4 × 10²³/(3.4 × 10⁶)² [1] = **3.7 N kg⁻¹** [1]

**4.** 9.81/4 = **2.45 N kg⁻¹** [1]

**5.** **B** [1]

**6.** The force on the object is proportional to its mass, F = GMm/r² [1], so dividing by m gives g = GM/r², which contains only the mass creating the field and the distance [1].

**7.**

**(a)** GM(p)/x² = GM(m)/(d − x)², so x/(d − x) = √64 = 8 [1]; x = 8(9.0 × 10⁸ − x), so 9x = 7.2 × 10⁹ [1]; x = **8.0 × 10⁸ m** from the planet's centre [1]

**(b)** At 7.0 × 10⁸ m the probe is nearer the planet than the neutral point, so the planet's field is stronger than the moon's there [1]; the resultant field points towards the planet, so the probe accelerates **towards the planet** [1].

**8.**

**(a)** r = 6.37 × 10⁶ + 4.0 × 10⁵ = 6.77 × 10⁶ m [1]; g = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.97 × 10²⁴/(6.77 × 10⁶)² = **8.7 N kg⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** 8.69/9.81 × 100 = **89%** [1]

**9.**

**(a)** M = gR²/G [1] = 1.8 × (2.5 × 10⁶)²/6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ = **1.7 × 10²³ kg** [1]

**(b)** Volume = (4/3)π(2.5 × 10⁶)³ = 6.54 × 10¹⁹ m³ [1]; ρ = M/V = 1.69 × 10²³/6.54 × 10¹⁹ = **2.6 × 10³ kg m⁻³** [1]

**10.**

**(a)** Straight radial lines pointing inwards towards the centre of the planet [1], spreading further apart with distance and never crossing [1].

**(b)** Parallel, equally spaced lines pointing vertically down towards the surface [1], because over such a small height compared with the planet's radius the field strength and direction hardly change, so the field is approximately uniform [1].

**11.**

**(a)** g(A) = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 2.0 × 10³⁰/(2.0 × 10¹¹)² = **3.3 × 10⁻³ N kg⁻¹** [1]; g(B) = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 2.0 × 10³⁰/(4.0 × 10¹¹)² = **8.3 × 10⁻⁴ N kg⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** The fields act in opposite directions, so the resultant = 3.34 × 10⁻³ − 0.83 × 10⁻³ = **2.5 × 10⁻³ N kg⁻¹** [1], **towards star A** [1]

**12.**

**(a)** The force per unit mass acting on a small mass placed at that point in the field [1].

**(b)** 1 N = 1 kg m s⁻², so 1 N kg⁻¹ = 1 kg m s⁻² ÷ 1 kg = 1 m s⁻² [1].

**(c)** The force on a mass m at distance r from a point mass M is F = GMm/r² [1]; g = F/m, so the m cancels and g = GM/r² [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the altitude instead of the distance from the centre (Question 8).
- Expanding the neutral-point equation and keeping the wrong root, or forgetting that G cancels (Question 7).
- Adding opposing fields (Question 11).
- Giving a definition of g without "per unit mass" (Question 12).
- Drawing radial lines outwards, or near-surface lines unevenly spaced (Question 10).
- Using the diameter instead of the radius in the volume of a sphere (Question 9).
