These questions cover **3.2.7 Work, energy and power** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [work, energy and power study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-work-energy-and-power-oxfordaqa/) or the [work, energy and power revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-work-energy-and-power-oxfordaqa-revision-notes/) first. Take g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Define the joule. **[1]**

**2.** In which case does the named force do no work?

A: friction on a book sliding across a desk

B: the tension in a rope pulling a sledge along level snow

C: the normal contact force on a car moving along a level road

D: the weight of a ball falling to the ground

**[1]**

**3.** A trolley is pulled 2.5 m along a level floor by a force of 16 N acting at 25° above the horizontal. Calculate the work done by the force. **[2]**

**4.** A climber of mass 65 kg climbs a vertical height of 12 m in 40 s. Calculate the work she does against gravity and her average useful power. **[2]**

**5.** An electric kettle takes 2.2 kW from the mains and transfers 1.9 kW to the water. Calculate its efficiency as a percentage. **[1]**

**6.** Starting from the definition of power, show that the power needed to move an object at constant velocity v against a constant force F is P = Fv. **[2]**

**7.** A cyclist produces a useful power of 180 W while riding at a steady 6.0 m s⁻¹ on level ground. What is the total resistive force?

A: 30 N

B: 1080 N

C: 0.033 N

D: 174 N

**[1]**

### Section B

**8.** A toy catapult launches a 0.050 kg ball horizontally. While the ball is in contact, the force on it rises uniformly from 0 to 60 N over the first 0.15 m and then stays at 60 N for a further 0.05 m before the ball leaves.

**(a)** Calculate the work done on the ball. **[3]**

**(b)** Assuming all of this work becomes kinetic energy of the ball, calculate its launch speed. **[2]**

**9.** A car of mass 1100 kg has a maximum useful power output of 45 kW. The resistive force on it is given by F = 25v, where F is in newtons and v is the speed in m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the top speed of the car on a level road. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the acceleration of the car at 20 m s⁻¹ when the engine delivers its maximum power. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the car's acceleration decreases as its speed increases. **[1]**

**10.** An escalator carries 30 people, of average mass 70 kg, each minute up a vertical height of 6.0 m.

**(a)** Calculate the useful power output of the escalator. **[2]**

**(b)** The escalator motor is 65% efficient. Calculate its input power. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the energy wasted in one hour, in MJ. **[2]**

**11.** A 15 kg box is pulled 6.0 m up a slope inclined at 30° to the horizontal, at constant speed, by a rope parallel to the slope. The frictional force on the box is 40 N.

**(a)** Calculate the work done against gravity. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the work done against friction. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the tension in the rope, and use it to find the total work done by the rope. **[2]**

**(d)** The pull takes 12 s. Calculate the average power of the rope's pull. **[1]**

**12.** A pump lifts 0.50 m³ of water each minute through a vertical height of 8.0 m. The density of water is 1000 kg m⁻³. The pump's input power is 1.2 kW.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of water lifted each second. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the useful power output of the pump. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the efficiency of the pump. **[1]**

**(d)** Suggest two ways in which energy is wasted by the pump. **[2]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** The work done when a force of 1 N moves its point of application through 1 m in the direction of the force [1] **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A does negative work, removing energy. The normal force in C is perpendicular to the motion, so cos 90° = 0.

**3.** W = 16 × 2.5 × cos 25° [1] = **36 J** [1] **[2]**

**4.** W = 65 × 9.81 × 12 = **7.7 × 10³ J** (7650 J) [1]; P = 7650 ÷ 40 = **190 W** [1] **[2]**

**5.** 1.9 ÷ 2.2 × 100 = **86%** [1] **[1]**

**6.** P = W/t and W = Fs [1]; P = Fs/t, and s/t = v at constant velocity, so P = Fv [1] **[2]**

**7.** **A** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* At steady speed, driving force = resistance = P ÷ v = 180 ÷ 6.0. Option B multiplies instead of dividing.

### Section B

**8.**

**(a)** Triangle: ½ × 0.15 × 60 = 4.5 J [1]; rectangle: 0.05 × 60 = 3.0 J [1]; total = **7.5 J** [1]

**(b)** ½ × 0.050 × v² = 7.5 [1]; v = √300 = **17 m s⁻¹** [1] **[5]**

**9.**

**(a)** At top speed, driving force = resistance, so P = 25v × v = 25v² [1]; v = √(45 000 ÷ 25) = **42 m s⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** Driving force = 45 000 ÷ 20 = 2250 N [1]; resistance = 25 × 20 = 500 N [1]; a = (2250 − 500) ÷ 1100 = **1.6 m s⁻²** [1]

**(c)** At fixed power the driving force P/v falls as speed rises while the resistive force rises, so the resultant force (and acceleration) falls [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), writing P = 25 × v without the second v treats the resistance as constant. Substitute F = 25v into P = Fv first.

**10.**

**(a)** Energy per minute = 30 × 70 × 9.81 × 6.0 = 1.24 × 10⁵ J [1]; P = 1.24 × 10⁵ ÷ 60 = **2.1 kW** [1]

**(b)** 2060 ÷ 0.65 = **3.2 kW** [1]

**(c)** Wasted power = 3170 − 2060 = 1110 W [1]; energy = 1110 × 3600 = **4.0 MJ** [1] **[5]**

**11.**

**(a)** Height gained = 6.0 sin 30° = 3.0 m [1]; W = 15 × 9.81 × 3.0 = **441 J** [1]

**(b)** 40 × 6.0 = **240 J** [1]

**(c)** T = mg sin 30° + 40 = 73.6 + 40 = **114 N** [1]; W = 113.6 × 6.0 = **681 J**, which equals 441 + 240 [1]

**(d)** 681 ÷ 12 = **57 W** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The two routes in (c) must agree. If they do not, one of the forces has been resolved the wrong way.

**12.**

**(a)** 0.50 m³ × 1000 = 500 kg per minute = **8.3 kg s⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** P = (m/t)gΔh = 8.33 × 9.81 × 8.0 [1] = **650 W** [1]

**(c)** 654 ÷ 1200 = **0.55 (55%)** [1]

**(d)** Any two: heating of the motor windings (resistance); friction in the bearings and moving parts; turbulence and friction of water in the pipes; sound [2] **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using sin instead of cos for the angle between force and displacement.
- Forgetting that the resistive force can depend on speed.
- Mixing minutes and seconds in power calculations.
- Efficiency answers above 100%, from dividing input by output.
