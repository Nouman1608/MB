## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Outgassing | Release of gases from inside the Earth through volcanoes |
| Greenhouse gas | A gas that absorbs and re-emits infrared radiation, e.g. water vapour, carbon dioxide, methane |
| Enhanced greenhouse effect | Extra warming caused by human increases in greenhouse gases |
| Anthropogenic | Caused by human activity |
| Correlation | Two variables change together; not on its own proof that one causes the other |
| Mitigation | Action that reduces the size of a problem, here by cutting emissions or removing gases |
| Particulates | Tiny solid particles, mainly carbon (soot), suspended in air |
| Potable water | Water that is safe to drink; it still contains small amounts of dissolved substances |
| Desalination | Removing dissolved salts from sea water or salty water |

## C6.3a The first atmosphere

**Thought to be:** mainly carbon dioxide and water vapour, some nitrogen, possibly a little methane and ammonia, almost no oxygen. Source: **volcanic outgassing**.

**How it changed, in order:**

1. Earth cooled; water vapour **condensed** to form oceans.
2. Carbon dioxide **dissolved** in the oceans and was locked into **carbonate rocks**.
3. Photosynthesis removed more carbon dioxide (see C6.3b).
4. Carbon was stored in **fossil fuels** and sedimentary rocks.
5. Nitrogen, being unreactive, slowly built up to become the main gas.

**Air today (dry):** about 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, 0.9% argon, 0.04% carbon dioxide.

**Evidence is indirect.** No direct samples exist. Scientists use volcanic gases today, old rocks and models, so conclusions are "thought" or "suggested", not known.

## C6.3b Oxygen

- **Cyanobacteria** (and later algae and plants) photosynthesise: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂.
- Early oxygen reacted with **dissolved iron**, forming **banded iron formations** (evidence).
- Oxygen built up in the air once the iron was used up; the first big rise was about 2.4 billion years ago.
- An **ozone layer** formed, absorbing ultraviolet radiation.
- The build-up took **billions of years** (orders of magnitude, M2h).

## C6.3c Greenhouse effect: method in steps

1. Sunlight, mostly short-wavelength, travels through the air largely unabsorbed.
2. Land and sea take in this energy and heat up.
3. The surface emits **longer-wavelength infrared** radiation.
4. Greenhouse gas molecules **absorb** infrared and **re-emit** it in all directions.
5. Some returns to the surface, so less energy escapes to space and the surface stays warmer.

The natural effect keeps the Earth habitable. Human activity **enhances** it. Nitrogen and oxygen hardly absorb infrared.

## C6.3d Evidence and uncertainty

**Evidence for a human cause:**

- Carbon dioxide rose from about **280 ppm** before industry to a monthly average over **430 ppm** in 2025.
- The rise **correlates** with fossil fuel burning.
- Average temperatures have risen at the same time.
- Ice cores show carbon dioxide and temperature moving together in the past.
- There is a known **mechanism** (C6.3c).

**Uncertainties:**

- Correlation alone does not prove cause.
- Natural factors also change climate: the Sun's output, volcanoes, ocean cycles.
- Past data come from **proxies** (ice cores, tree rings), with larger error.
- Fewer measuring sites existed in the past.
- Models simplify complex systems such as clouds.

**Reading data (CM6.3i):** for a table or graph, quote values with units, calculate a change or rate (change ÷ time), state the trend, then comment on whether the rate is increasing.

**Converting units:** 1% = 10 000 ppm. So ppm ÷ 10 000 = %, and % × 10 000 = ppm.

## C6.3e Effects and mitigation

| Effect of more CO₂ and CH₄ | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Higher average temperature | Renewable and nuclear electricity |
| Sea-level rise (thermal expansion, melting land ice) | Better insulation and efficient vehicles |
| More extreme weather: heatwaves, storms, floods | Carbon capture and storage |
| Drought and changed rainfall; lower crop yields in some places | Tree planting |
| Species and habitats shift | Capture methane from landfill; cut gas leaks |

**Evaluating a mitigation:** say how it reduces emissions, then give a limitation (cost, scale, land use, reliability, public acceptance), then judge. Scale matters: actions by one person or one country are small against global emissions. Risk: rare but severe outcomes still need planning for.

**Methane sources:** cattle, rice paddies, landfill, gas extraction. Methane traps more heat per molecule than carbon dioxide.

## C6.3f Pollutants

| Pollutant | Formed by | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| CO | Incomplete combustion of carbon fuels | Toxic; reduces oxygen carried by blood; no colour or smell |
| SO₂ | Burning sulfur impurities in fuels: S + O₂ → SO₂ | Acid rain; lung irritation |
| NO, NO₂ | N₂ + O₂ → 2NO at high engine temperatures; 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂ | Acid rain; breathing problems; smog |
| Particulates | Incomplete combustion, especially diesel | Lung and heart damage; blackened buildings; global dimming |

**Small worked reminder.** The products of burning a hydrocarbon depend on the oxygen supply. Plenty of oxygen gives CO₂ and H₂O; less gives CO; very little gives C (soot). Balance each by counting oxygen atoms last.

**Mass reminder.** Sulfur dioxide has Mr 64 and sulfur has Ar 32, so each gram of sulfur burned gives 2 g of sulfur dioxide.

## C6.3g Potable water

| Source | Treatment | Ease |
|---|---|---|
| Ground water (aquifers) | Little needed; sterilise (may filter) | Easiest |
| Fresh surface water | Sedimentation → filtration (sand, gravel) → sterilisation (chlorine, ozone or UV) | Fairly easy |
| Waste water | Screening, settling, biological breakdown of organic matter, then filtration and sterilisation | Harder, more stages |
| Salt water | Desalination: distillation or reverse osmosis | Hardest; needs most energy |

**Distillation** separates water by boiling and condensing. **Reverse osmosis** pushes water through a membrane under high pressure. Distillation and filtration are separation techniques from C2.1 (see [the C2.1 separation methods page](/resources/ocr-gcse-chemistry-purity-and-separating-mixtures/)).

## Must-know distinctions

- **Greenhouse effect vs ozone depletion.** Infrared absorbed by greenhouse gases warms the Earth; ozone loss lets in more ultraviolet. Different problems.
- **Natural vs enhanced greenhouse effect.** The first is essential for life; the second is the human addition.
- **Correlation vs causation.** Data that rise together need a mechanism before you claim a cause.
- **Potable vs pure.** Potable water is safe to drink; pure water contains only water molecules.
- **Sulfur dioxide vs oxides of nitrogen.** SO₂ comes from sulfur in the fuel; NOₓ comes from nitrogen in the air.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the process that released the gases of the early atmosphere.
2. Why did the oceans form as the Earth cooled?
3. Give two ways carbon dioxide was removed from the early atmosphere.
4. Write the balanced equation for photosynthesis.
5. Which type of radiation is absorbed by greenhouse gases?
6. Convert 0.038% carbon dioxide into ppm.
7. Convert 1.9 ppm of methane into a percentage.
8. A 500 cm³ sample of dry air is 78% nitrogen. What volume is nitrogen?
9. What mass of carbon dioxide forms when 6 g of carbon burns completely? (C = 12, O = 16)
10. What mass of sulfur dioxide forms when 16 g of sulfur burns? (S = 32, O = 16)
11. Why do car engines release oxides of nitrogen even though petrol contains no nitrogen?
12. Which source of water needs the most energy to make it potable, and why?

### Answers

1. Volcanic activity (outgassing).
2. Water vapour condensed when the temperature fell below its boiling point.
3. Any two: dissolving in oceans; forming carbonate rocks; photosynthesis; locking into fossil fuels.
4. 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂.
5. Infrared (long wavelength).
6. 0.038 × 10 000 = **380 ppm**.
7. 1.9 ÷ 10 000 = **0.00019%**.
8. 0.78 × 500 = **390 cm³**.
9. 6 g is 0.5 mol of carbon, giving 0.5 mol of CO₂ = 0.5 × 44 = **22 g**.
10. 16 g is 0.5 mol of sulfur, giving 0.5 mol of SO₂ = 0.5 × 64 = **32 g**.
11. Inside the hot engine, atmospheric nitrogen combines with oxygen.
12. Salt water; desalination by distillation or reverse osmosis uses a lot of energy.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "greenhouse gases reflect heat" instead of "absorb and re-emit infrared radiation".
- Missing out the change in wavelength: incoming short wavelength, outgoing infrared.
- Saying the early atmosphere contained oxygen from volcanoes.
- Forgetting that oxygen first reacted with iron before building up in the air.
- Describing a data trend without quoting any values or units.
- Concluding that a correlation proves humans cause climate change, with no mention of mechanism or uncertainty.
- Giving a mitigation with no limitation when the command word is "evaluate".
- Mixing up the sources of SO₂ (fuel) and NOₓ (air).
- Calling potable water "pure" or "clean water with nothing in it".
- Saying the ozone hole causes global warming.
