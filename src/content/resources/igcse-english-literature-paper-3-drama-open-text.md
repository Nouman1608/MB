Cambridge IGCSE Literature in English (0475) gives candidates three
routes through the qualification: Paper 1 plus Paper 2, **or** Paper 1
plus Paper 3 and Paper 4, **or** Paper 1 plus Paper 3 and Component 5
Coursework. This guide covers Paper 3 -- Drama (Open Text), the drama
paper taken by candidates on either of the two routes that don't use
Paper 2, and explains how it differs in both format and demand from
[Paper 2 Drama](/resources/igcse-english-literature-paper-2-drama/).

## The headline difference: open book

Paper 3 is the only externally assessed literature paper on this
syllabus where candidates may take their set text into the exam room.
The syllabus is specific about what "open book" actually permits:

- Candidates may take a **clean copy** of their set text into the
  exam room.
- The text must **not** contain personal annotations, highlighting or
  underlining.
- Candidates may not use editions that include extensive study notes
  or facing-page translations or explanations.
- Editions with brief introductions, conclusions and foot/end notes
  are permitted, but candidates must not use those additional notes
  during the exam.

This is a meaningfully different skill from Paper 2's closed-book
format. Having the text available removes the pressure of recalling
exact wording from memory, but it does not reduce the standard
expected -- all four assessment objectives are still tested in full,
and examiners still expect precise, well-selected quotation rather
than time spent searching the text during the exam. In practice, open
book rewards candidates who know their text well enough to locate
relevant passages quickly, not candidates who have simply not learned
the text.

## Format: one question, not two

Where Paper 2 requires two questions on two different texts, Paper 3
is shorter and narrower:

- **45 minutes, 25 marks** (compared with Paper 2's 1 hour 30 minutes,
  50 marks)
- Candidates answer **one question from a choice of two**, both worth
  equal marks (25 marks each)
- Both questions are set on **the same single text** -- candidates
  study just one play for this paper, not two
- Relevant passages are printed on the question paper

Because there is only one text to prepare rather than two, Paper 3
candidates can go deeper into a single play than Paper 2 candidates
typically manage across two. The trade-off is time pressure: 45
minutes is a tight window for a full response addressing all four
assessment objectives, so efficient planning matters more here than on
the longer papers.

## Set texts for the 2026 series

Paper 3 draws on the same five-play list as Paper 2 for the 2026
series: *Blues for an Alabama Sky* (Pearl Cleage), *A Taste of Honey*
(Shelagh Delaney), *A Midsummer Night's Dream* (Shakespeare), *Antony
and Cleopatra* (Shakespeare) and *A Streetcar Named Desire* (Tennessee
Williams). Set texts rotate by examination series, so always confirm
the current list for the year your candidates are actually sitting the
exam, rather than assuming it matches a previous year's list.

## What the four assessment objectives reward here

As on every paper in this syllabus, all four AOs are tested and
weighted equally:

- **AO1** -- knowledge of the content of the text, shown through
  reference to detail and use of quotations
- **AO2** -- understanding of characters, relationships, situations
  and themes
- **AO3** -- understanding of the writer's intentions and methods,
  responding to their use of language
- **AO4** -- a personal response, communicated either directly
  (questions asking "what do you think?") or by implication (questions
  asking you to "explore the ways in which...")

Open-book access mainly supports AO1 (you can locate and check
quotations rather than relying purely on memory) and, indirectly, AO3
(you can point precisely to specific lines when discussing language
and technique). It does not substitute for AO2 and AO4, which still
require genuine understanding and a considered personal response
worked out in advance of the exam, not assembled on the spot from the
page in front of you.

## How Paper 3 combines with the rest of the qualification

Paper 3 is never taken alone -- it always pairs with either **Paper 4
Unseen** (1 hour 15 minutes, 25 marks, a critical commentary on
unseen poetry or prose) or **Component 5 Coursework** (25 marks, two
internally assessed portfolio assignments of 600-1200 words each, on
different texts). Both routes bring the drama component to the same
25% weighting of the overall qualification; the choice between Paper 4
and Component 5 is a decision your school makes about how the course
is delivered and assessed, not one you make individually as a
candidate, so confirm early which route you are on.

## How to approach it

Because the whole paper rests on one text, use your preparation time
to build genuine familiarity with the entire play, not just the scenes
you expect to be asked about -- both set questions could draw on any
part of it, and the "choice of two" only helps if you're prepared for
either. Practise locating specific, well-chosen quotations quickly
under time pressure, since 45 minutes leaves little room for
page-flipping; a small number of pre-identified key passages per major
theme or character, tagged with sticky notes you're permitted to use
(check current guidance on what markings are allowed on a clean set
text), is more useful than trying to search cold. Because this is the
shortest externally assessed paper on the syllabus, spend a
proportionate few minutes planning your single answer before writing,
since a well-structured response that clearly addresses all four AOs
in 40 minutes of writing will outperform an unplanned answer that runs
out of time before reaching a personal response.
