## Definitions

- **Electrolysis:** the decomposition of an ionic compound, when molten or in aqueous
  solution, by the passage of an electric current.
- **Anode:** the positive electrode. **Cathode:** the negative electrode.
- **Electrolyte:** the molten or aqueous substance that undergoes electrolysis.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Inert electrode | Conducts but does not react: platinum or carbon (graphite) |
| Cation | Positive ion — moves to the cathode |
| Anion | Negative ion — moves to the anode |
| Discharged | An ion gains or loses electrons at an electrode and becomes an atom or molecule |
| Binary compound | Compound of two elements only |
| Oxidation *(Supplement)* | Loss of electrons — at the anode |
| Reduction *(Supplement)* | Gain of electrons — at the cathode |
| Electroplating | Coating an object with a thin layer of metal by electrolysis |

## The general rule (Core)

**Cathode:** metals or hydrogen. **Anode:** non-metals (other than hydrogen).

**Molten binary compounds:** metal at the cathode, non-metal at the anode.
e.g. molten NaCl → sodium + chlorine; molten ZnBr₂ → zinc + bromine.

## Aqueous solutions (Supplement)

```
CATHODE: metal less reactive than hydrogen (Cu, Ag)?  yes → metal    no → hydrogen
ANODE:   concentrated halide?                          yes → halogen  no → oxygen
```

## Core observations to learn

| Electrolyte | Cathode | Anode |
|---|---|---|
| Molten PbBr₂ | lead: silvery-grey liquid | bromine: red-brown fumes |
| Conc. NaCl(aq) | hydrogen: colourless bubbles (pops with lighted splint) | chlorine: pale yellow-green bubbles (bleaches damp litmus) |
| Dilute H₂SO₄ | hydrogen: colourless bubbles, twice the volume | oxygen: colourless bubbles (relights glowing splint) |

## Copper(II) sulfate (Supplement)

- **Carbon electrodes:** cathode — pink-brown copper; anode — oxygen bubbles; blue colour
  **fades**.
- **Copper electrodes:** cathode **gains** copper; anode **dissolves** (loses mass); blue
  colour **unchanged**.

## Half-equations (Supplement)

```
reduction (cathode)            oxidation (anode)
Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu                2Cl⁻ → Cl2 + 2e⁻
2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H2                 2Br⁻ → Br2 + 2e⁻
Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb                4OH⁻ → O2 + 2H2O + 4e⁻
Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag                  Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻
```

Check: atoms balance **and** total charge is equal on both sides.

## Charge transfer (Supplement)

Electrons flow through the **external circuit** from anode to cathode via the power supply;
**ions** carry charge through the **electrolyte**; at the electrodes ions **gain** (cathode)
or **lose** (anode) electrons.

## Electroplating (Core)

| Part | What to use |
|---|---|
| Cathode | The object to be plated |
| Anode | The plating metal |
| Electrolyte | Solution of a salt of the plating metal |
| Reasons | Improve appearance; improve resistance to corrosion |

## Observation phrase bank

| Product | What you see |
|---|---|
| Hydrogen or oxygen | Bubbles (effervescence) of colourless gas |
| Chlorine | Bubbles of pale yellow-green gas |
| Bromine (from molten bromide) | Red-brown fumes |
| Bromine (from aqueous bromide) | Solution near the anode turns orange-brown |
| Copper | Pink-brown solid coating on the cathode |
| Lead (from molten salt) | Silvery-grey liquid collecting under the cathode |

## Quick predictions to test the rules

| Electrolyte (inert electrodes) | Cathode | Anode |
|---|---|---|
| Molten lithium bromide | lithium | bromine |
| Concentrated hydrochloric acid *(Supplement)* | hydrogen | chlorine |
| Concentrated aqueous lithium chloride *(Supplement)* | hydrogen | chlorine |
| Aqueous copper(II) nitrate *(Supplement)* | copper | oxygen |

## Exam traps

- Aqueous NaCl gives **hydrogen**, not sodium, at the cathode.
- **Dilute** halide solutions give **oxygen** at the anode.
- Observations need colours, bubbles or coatings — "chlorine is produced" is a product,
  not an observation.
- Solid lead(II) bromide does not conduct: it must be **heated until molten**.
- Electrons do **not** pass through the electrolyte.
- In electroplating the object is the **cathode**.
- Half-equations at the anode have electrons on the **right**.
- With copper electrodes the **anode** changes; the cathode still gains copper.
- Sulfate and nitrate ions are not discharged: solutions containing them give **oxygen** at the anode.

## Self-test

1. Define electrolysis.
2. Which electrode is positive?
3. Name the products of electrolysing molten potassium iodide.
4. Why must lead(II) bromide be molten?
5. What would you see at the anode during electrolysis of concentrated aqueous sodium
   chloride?
6. *(Supplement)* Predict the products of electrolysing concentrated aqueous potassium
   bromide with inert electrodes.
7. *(Supplement)* Predict the anode product for dilute aqueous sodium chloride.
8. *(Supplement)* Write the half-equation for the formation of bromine.
9. *(Supplement)* Why does the blue colour fade when copper(II) sulfate is electrolysed with
   carbon electrodes?
10. *(Supplement)* Which particles carry charge in the wires, and which in the electrolyte?
11. To nickel-plate a steel spoon, what is used as the anode and the electrolyte?
12. Give two reasons for electroplating.

**Answers**

1. The decomposition of an ionic compound, molten or in aqueous solution, by passing an
   electric current.
2. The anode.
3. Potassium (cathode) and iodine (anode).
4. The ions must be free to move; in the solid they are fixed in the lattice.
5. Bubbles of a pale yellow-green gas (chlorine).
6. Hydrogen at the cathode; bromine at the anode.
7. Oxygen.
8. 2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻.
9. Cu²⁺ ions are removed as copper deposits on the cathode and are not replaced.
10. Electrons in the wires; ions in the electrolyte.
11. Anode: nickel. Electrolyte: a solution of a nickel salt (for example nickel(II)
    sulfate).
12. To improve appearance and to improve resistance to corrosion.
