## Forces between poles

- Every magnet has an **N pole** and an **S pole**.
- **Like poles repel; unlike poles attract.**
- A magnet attracts **unmagnetised magnetic material** with either pole.
- **Attraction** can mean magnet + magnet or magnet + magnetic material.
- **Repulsion** happens only between two magnets: it is the only proof that an object is magnetised.

## Magnetic and non-magnetic materials

| Magnetic (attracted; can be magnetised) | Non-magnetic |
|---|---|
| Iron, steel, nickel, cobalt | Copper, aluminium, brass, wood, plastic, glass |

- Not all metals are magnetic.

## Induced magnetism

- A magnetic material near a magnet **becomes a magnet**.
- The end nearest the magnet becomes the **opposite** pole, so they attract.
- Explains chains of nails or paper clips hanging from a magnet.

## Temporary and permanent magnets

| | Soft iron | Steel |
|---|---|---|
| Magnetises | easily | less easily |
| When field removed | loses magnetism | keeps magnetism |
| Type | temporary | permanent |
| Used for | electromagnet and relay cores | compass needles, motors, loudspeakers |

**Rule:** must the magnetism switch off? Soft iron. Must it stay? Steel.

## Magnetic fields

- **Magnetic field:** a region in which a magnetic pole experiences a force.
- **Direction:** the direction of the force on an **N pole** at that point.
- Bar magnet field lines: leave N, enter S, arrows N → S outside the magnet.
- Lines never cross; closest together at the poles.
- **Plotting compass:** shows direction; mark the needle tip, move the compass, repeat, join the dots.
- **Iron filings:** show the pattern quickly but **not the direction**.

## Extended only

- Magnetic forces are due to **interactions between magnetic fields**.
- **Closer field lines = stronger field.**
- N facing S: lines cross the gap; attraction.
- N facing N: lines bend away; a **neutral point** midway where the fields cancel; repulsion.
- Between the flat poles of a U-shaped magnet: parallel, evenly spaced lines; **uniform field**.

## Uses

| Permanent magnets | Electromagnets |
|---|---|
| Loudspeakers, motors, compasses, door catches | Scrap-metal cranes, relays, electric bells, sorting iron from waste |
| Steady field, no power needed | Can be switched off and its strength varied |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Magnetic vs magnetised:** a magnetic material can be attracted and magnetised; a magnetised object already has its own N and S poles.
- **Temporary vs permanent:** the test is what happens when the magnetising field is removed.
- **Pattern vs direction:** iron filings give the pattern; a compass gives the direction.
- **Attraction vs repulsion:** attraction has two possible causes; repulsion has only one.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Magnetised | Has become a magnet, with N and S poles |
| Unmagnetised | Magnetic material that is not acting as a magnet |
| Induced magnetism | Magnetism produced in a magnetic material by a nearby magnet |
| Temporary magnet | Loses its magnetism when the magnetising field is removed (soft iron) |
| Permanent magnet | Keeps its magnetism (steel) |
| Magnetic field | Region in which a magnetic pole experiences a force |
| Field line | Line showing the direction of the force on an N pole |
| Uniform field (Extended only) | Same strength and direction everywhere; parallel, evenly spaced lines |

## Exam traps

- "It was attracted, so it is a magnet": wrong; repulsion is the test.
- Aluminium cans and copper wire are **not** attracted by magnets.
- Arrows on field lines point **from N to S** outside the magnet.
- Iron filings show shape, not direction.
- Electromagnet cores are soft iron, not steel: steel would stay magnetised when the current stopped.
- Field lines must not cross or start in mid-air.

## Self-test

1. State what happens when two S poles are brought together.
2. A bar is attracted to both ends of a magnet. What can you conclude about the bar?
3. Name two magnetic and two non-magnetic metals.
4. What is induced magnetism?
5. A magnet's N pole is near one end of an iron nail. Which pole is induced at that end?
6. Why are electromagnet cores made of soft iron?
7. Define a magnetic field.
8. What determines the direction of a magnetic field at a point?
9. Give one advantage of a plotting compass over iron filings.
10. Give one use of an electromagnet and explain why an electromagnet is needed for it.
11. *(Extended)* How is the strength of a magnetic field shown on a field diagram?
12. *(Extended)* Describe the field between the poles of a U-shaped magnet with flat, parallel faces.

### Answers

1. They **repel**.
2. It is made of a **magnetic material** but is **unmagnetised** (a magnet would be repelled by one end).
3. Magnetic: any two of iron, nickel, cobalt (and steel, an alloy). Non-magnetic: copper, aluminium (or brass).
4. Magnetism produced in an unmagnetised magnetic material when it is placed near a magnet.
5. An **S pole**.
6. Soft iron magnetises easily and **loses its magnetism** when the current is switched off.
7. A region in which a magnetic pole experiences a force.
8. The direction of the force on an **N pole** placed there.
9. It shows the **direction** of the field.
10. A scrap-yard crane: the magnetism must be **switched off** to drop the load (or a relay: switched on and off by a current).
11. By the **spacing** of the lines: closer lines mean a stronger field.
12. **Uniform**: parallel, evenly spaced lines from N to S.
