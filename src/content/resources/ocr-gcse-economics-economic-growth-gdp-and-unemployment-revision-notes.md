## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Economic growth | A rise in an economy's production over time, shown by a rise in real GDP |
| GDP | The money value of all final output made inside a country's borders in a set period, usually a year |
| GDP per capita | GDP divided by the population; output per person |
| Employment | Having a paid job, including self-employment |
| Unemployment | Having no job but being available for work and looking for one |
| Labour force | The employed plus the unemployed |
| Claimant Count | A measure of unemployment based on the number of people claiming unemployment-related benefits |
| Sustainable growth | Growth that serves people today while leaving later generations able to meet their own needs |

## Formulas

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Growth rate | (new GDP - old GDP) / old GDP x 100 |
| GDP per capita | GDP / population |
| Labour force | Employed + unemployed |
| Unemployment rate | Unemployed / labour force x 100 |
| Change in a rate | New rate - old rate, in percentage points |
| Combined effect of two rises | Multiply the growth factors: 1.02 x 1.03 = 1.0506 |

## Method boxes

**Growth rate in steps**

1. Find the change: new GDP minus old GDP.
2. Divide by the old GDP, not the new.
3. Multiply by 100 and add a % sign. A negative answer means output fell.

**Unemployment rate in steps**

1. Work out the labour force: employed people plus unemployed people.
2. Divide the unemployed by the labour force.
3. Multiply by 100. Give the answer to the accuracy asked.

**Analysing a data table in steps**

1. Quote the start and end values and the overall change.
2. Name the turning points by year.
3. Compare periods (recent against historical).
4. Link to a second series, such as unemployment.
5. State one limit of the data.

## Worked reminders (Country Y, illustrative)

- GDP rises from 2,000 to 2,090 billion units: (2,090 - 2,000) / 2,000 x 100 = 4.5%.
- Population rises from 40 million to 41.2 million, which is 3.0%.
- GDP per capita: 2,000 billion / 40 million = 50,000; 2,090 billion / 41.2 million = 50,728. The rise is 1.5%, much lower than 4.5% because population also grew.
- Employed 18.8 million, unemployed 1.2 million: labour force 20.0 million; rate = 1.2 / 20.0 x 100 = 6.0%.
- Regional check: Region P has a labour force of 5.0 million and 600,000 unemployed, a rate of 12.0%. Region Q has 15.0 million and 600,000, a rate of 4.0%. Together: 1,200,000 / 20.0 million x 100 = 6.0%. Region P holds 25% of the labour force but 50% of the unemployed, so the national 6.0% hides the problem in P.

## The business cycle sketch

Picture a graph with real GDP up the side and time along the bottom. The line rises to a peak, falls to a trough and then recovers, and its long-run upward slope is economic growth. Cyclical unemployment follows the cycle.

| Phase | What happens to real GDP | Unemployment |
|---|---|---|
| Boom | Rises quickly to a peak | Lowest |
| Downturn | Falls | Rises |
| Trough | At its lowest point | Near its highest |
| Recovery | Rises again | Falls, often with a lag |

## Determinants of growth

- **Investment:** more capital per worker, so higher output per worker.
- **Technology:** better methods, so higher productivity and lower costs.
- **Size of workforce:** more workers, if jobs exist for them.
- **Education and training:** skilled workers are more productive.
- **Natural resources:** more inputs, but non-renewable ones can run out.
- **Government policies:** infrastructure, schools, tax incentives and stable rules.

For each, write a chain: cause, then change in resources or productivity, then higher potential output, then growth. Add a cost or limit, such as opportunity cost or a long delay.

## Costs and benefits of growth

| | Benefit | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Economic | Higher incomes, more jobs, more tax revenue | Inflation risk, uneven sharing of gains |
| Social | Better health, education, housing | Inequality, stress, congestion, some regions left behind |
| Environmental | Money and technology for clean production | Pollution, resource depletion, habitat loss |

Evaluation prompts: who gains, what kind of growth, over what time, from what starting point. Finish with a judgement.

## Types of unemployment

| Type | Cause | Lasts | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cyclical | Weak total demand in a downturn | Until recovery | Factory staff laid off in a recession |
| Frictional | Moving between jobs | Short | A person who has left one job and is looking for another |
| Seasonal | Time-of-year demand | Months, recurring | Holiday resort staff in winter |
| Structural | Skills or location mismatch, often after an industry declines | Often long | Shipyard workers when the yard closes |

## Evaluation chains to learn

- **Growth is good:** higher output, so higher incomes and tax revenue, so more can be spent on schools and hospitals. But if the gains go to a few and production pollutes, wellbeing may not rise. Judgement: growth is worth having when it is shared and sustainable.
- **Unemployment is costly:** a firm closes, so workers lose income, so local spending falls, so more firms struggle. But short frictional spells help people find better jobs. Judgement: long structural unemployment in one region is the most damaging.
- **Claimant Count is useful but limited:** it is quick and available by area, yet it misses people who do not claim and changes when benefit rules change.

## Consequences of unemployment

- **Individuals:** lower income, debt, skill loss, poorer health and confidence.
- **Regions:** less local spending, shop closures, falling property prices, out-migration.
- **Government:** more benefit spending, lower tax receipts, and an opportunity cost of what else the money could fund.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Real versus nominal GDP:** use real GDP to measure growth.
- **GDP versus GDP per capita:** total output against output per person. Totals can rise while per capita falls.
- **Number versus rate:** the number is a count of people; the rate expresses that count as a percentage of the labour force.
- **Claimant Count versus all unemployed:** claimants only count those who claim.
- **Percent versus percentage points:** a rate going from 3.6% to 4.4% is up 0.8 percentage points.
- **Cyclical versus structural:** one ends with recovery, the other needs retraining or relocation.

## Quick self-test

1. State what is measured by GDP.
2. Country Y's GDP falls from 2,000 to 1,940. Calculate the growth rate.
3. GDP is 900 billion with a population of 18 million. A year later GDP is 936 billion and the population is 18.3 million. Calculate the percentage change in GDP per capita.
4. Claimants rise from 450,000 to 540,000. Calculate the percentage rise.
5. Employed 24.1 million, unemployed 0.9 million. Calculate the unemployment rate.
6. The rate in question 5 rises to 4.4%. State the change in percentage points.
7. The workforce rises by 2% and output per worker by 3%. Calculate the rise in output.
8. A hotel cleaner is out of work from November to March every year. Name the type of unemployment.
9. Give one environmental cost and one environmental benefit of growth.
10. Explain why a national unemployment rate can hide regional problems.

### Answers

1. The money value of final output made inside a country in a set period.
2. (1,940 - 2,000) / 2,000 x 100 = -3.0%.
3. Per capita starts at 900 billion / 18 million = 50,000. It then becomes 936 billion / 18.3 million = 51,148. The change is 1,148 / 50,000 x 100 = 2.3%.
4. (540,000 - 450,000) / 450,000 x 100 = 20.0%.
5. Labour force = 25.0 million; rate = 0.9 / 25.0 x 100 = 3.6%.
6. 4.4 - 3.6 = 0.8 percentage points.
7. 1.02 x 1.03 = 1.0506, so output rises by about 5.1%.
8. Seasonal unemployment.
9. Cost: pollution or faster use of non-renewable resources. Benefit: higher incomes can fund cleaner technology.
10. One region may have a high rate, and another a low rate, so the average hides the first and understates the hardship there.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the new year's GDP as the divisor in a growth calculation.
- Giving total GDP when asked for GDP per capita.
- Dividing by the employed instead of the labour force.
- Writing percent when the question is about a change in a rate.
- Naming a determinant of growth without a chain of reasoning.
- Giving only costs, or only benefits, in an evaluate answer, and no judgement.
- Writing about unemployment in general when the question asks about individuals, regions or the government.
- Not using the data given, so the answer reads as generic.

## Frequently asked questions

**What determines economic growth?**
The OCR GCSE Economics (J205) notes list investment, technology, the size of the workforce, education and training, natural resources and government policies such as infrastructure and tax incentives.

**What is the difference between GDP and GDP per capita?**
GDP is the total output of an economy, while GDP per capita is output per person. Total GDP can rise while GDP per capita falls if the population grows faster.

**What are the limits of the Claimant Count?**
The Claimant Count is quick and available by area, but it misses unemployed people who do not claim benefits and changes when benefit rules change.

**What are the consequences of unemployment for a region?**
Unemployment reduces local spending, leads to shop closures and falling property prices, and can cause people to move away, so the decline can become self-reinforcing.
