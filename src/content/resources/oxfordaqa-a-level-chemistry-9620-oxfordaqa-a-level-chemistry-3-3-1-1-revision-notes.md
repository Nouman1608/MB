These notes condense **3.3.1.1 Nomenclature** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, examined in Unit 2 and able to reappear in Unit 5. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [nomenclature study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-1-1/); then try the [nomenclature practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-1-1-practice/).

## The subtopic in four lines

1. Six formula types: empirical, molecular, general, structural, displayed, skeletal.
2. A homologous series shares a functional group and a general formula, and successive members differ by CH₂.
3. IUPAC names: longest chain with the functional group, lowest locant for the suffix group, prefixes in alphabetical order.
4. Names and drawings are limited to chains and rings of up to six carbons each.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Empirical formula | Simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element |
| Molecular formula | Actual number of atoms of each element in a molecule |
| General formula | Algebraic formula for any member of a homologous series |
| Structural formula | Minimal detail showing the arrangement of atoms, e.g. CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ |
| Displayed formula | Shows every atom and every bond |
| Skeletal formula | Lines for the carbon skeleton; C and H on C omitted; functional groups shown |
| Homologous series | Compounds with the same functional group, same general formula, differing by CH₂ |
| Functional group | Atom or group of atoms that gives a compound its characteristic reactions |
| Locant | Number showing the position of a group on the chain |
| Stem | Part of the name giving the number of carbons in the main chain |

## Stems and endings

| Carbons | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stem | meth | eth | prop | but | pent | hex |

| Group | How named | Example |
|---|---|---|
| C=C | -ene, with locant | but-2-ene |
| two C=C | -adiene | buta-1,3-diene |
| –F, –Cl, –Br, –I | fluoro-, chloro-, bromo-, iodo- | 2-bromobutane |
| –OH | -ol (hydroxy- if not the main group) | propan-2-ol |
| –CHO | -al (C1, no locant) | butanal |
| C=O in chain | -one (locant from C5 up) | pentan-3-one |
| –COOH | -oic acid (C1, no locant) | 2-methylpropanoic acid |
| –C≡N | -nitrile (CN carbon counted, C1) | propanenitrile |
| ring | cyclo- | cyclohexanol |

**Suffix priority** in this subtopic: carboxylic acid > aldehyde > ketone > alcohol. Halogens are always prefixes.

## General formulas

| Series | General formula |
|---|---|
| Alkanes | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ |
| Alkenes, cycloalkanes | CₙH₂ₙ |
| Halogenoalkanes (one X) | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁X |
| Alcohols | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH |
| Aldehydes, ketones | CₙH₂ₙO |
| Carboxylic acids | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH |

## Six formulas for butan-2-ol

| Formula | Butan-2-ol |
|---|---|
| Molecular | C₄H₁₀O |
| Empirical | C₄H₁₀O |
| General | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH |
| Structural | CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ |
| Displayed | every C–H, C–C, C–O and O–H bond drawn |
| Skeletal | zig-zag of three lines with OH on the second carbon |

## Naming in five steps

1. Identify the functional group(s); choose the suffix.
2. Longest chain containing the suffix group (and C=C).
3. Number to give the suffix group the lowest locant; then C=C; then the set of substituents.
4. Prefixes in alphabetical order, with di-/tri- for repeats (di- and tri- ignored in ordering).
5. Commas between numbers; hyphens between numbers and letters.

**Drawing from a name:** write the stem chain, number it, add each group at its locant, fill each carbon to four bonds with H, then name your drawing to check.

## Homologous series: four features

- Same functional group.
- Same general formula.
- Successive members differ by CH₂ (Mr + 14.0).
- Similar chemical reactions; gradual trend in physical properties (boiling point rises with chain length).

## Exam traps

- Longest chain may bend through a branch: (CH₃)₂CHCH(CH₃)CH₃ is 2,3-dimethylbutane.
- No locant on aldehydes or carboxylic acids: butanal, not butan-1-al.
- Count the nitrile carbon: CH₃CH₂CN is propanenitrile.
- Alphabetical order: bromo before chloro before ethyl before methyl.
- –CHO, not –COH, for an aldehyde.
- Skeletal formulas must show OH, not just O, for an alcohol.
- Empirical and molecular formulas can be the same (C₅H₁₀O).
- Alkenes and cycloalkanes share CₙH₂ₙ, so a general formula alone does not prove the series.

## Self-test

Answer without looking, then check below.

1. Give the empirical formula of hexane, C₆H₁₄.
2. Give the general formula of the carboxylic acids.
3. State two features of a homologous series other than having the same functional group.
4. Name CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂OH.
5. Name CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₃.
6. Name CH₃CHBrCHClCH₃.
7. Give the structural formula of 3-methylbut-1-ene.
8. Give the structural formula of 2-methylbutanoic acid.
9. Name (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CN.
10. Correct the name 1-methylpentane and explain the error.
11. An alcohol in the series CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH has Mr = 74.0. Find n.
12. Name CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CHO.

### Answers

1. C₃H₇.
2. CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH.
3. Any two: same general formula; successive members differ by CH₂; similar chemical properties; gradual trend in physical properties.
4. 2-methylbutan-1-ol.
5. Pentan-3-one.
6. 2-bromo-3-chlorobutane.
7. CH₂=CHCH(CH₃)CH₃.
8. CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)COOH.
9. 3-methylbutanenitrile (the CN carbon is C1).
10. Hexane: the methyl group extends the main chain, so the longest chain is six carbons.
11. 14n + 18 = 74.0, so n = 4 (butanol).
12. 3-hydroxybutanal: the aldehyde is the suffix group and C1, so –OH is a hydroxy prefix on C3.
