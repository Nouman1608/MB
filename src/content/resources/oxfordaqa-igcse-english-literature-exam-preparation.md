OxfordAQA International GCSE English Literature (9275) is linear, with one compulsory component and a
route choice. **All candidates sit Paper 1 (Prose and Drama)** -- 1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks, 40% of
the qualification. **Route A** then adds Paper 2a (Poetry and Unseen Texts), an **open-book**
examination of 2 hours 15 minutes and 90 marks, worth 60%. **Route B** instead adds the shorter Paper
2b (Poetry, 1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks, 40%) **together with** a Non-Exam Assessment -- one extended
response to a prose fiction text of the student's choice, 30 marks and 20%, teacher assessed and AQA
moderated. The specification is being revised for first teaching September 2026 with a refreshed set
of prescribed texts; the board states the paper structure, timings, marks and weightings above are
unchanged by that revision -- only the texts differ. These notes complement the site's guides to
[Prose and Drama](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-english-literature-prose-and-drama/) and
[Poetry and Unseen Texts](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-english-literature-poetry-and-unseen-texts/).

## The two routes are structurally very different, not merely alternative papers

Route A concentrates 60% into a single long open-book examination that includes unseen texts. Route B
splits the same territory into a shorter poetry paper plus a coursework response on a text you choose.
**Exam-preparation priority**: confirm your route before planning revision. Practising unseen-text
technique for a Route A paper you are not sitting, or leaving a Route B coursework response
under-developed, forfeits a large share of the qualification.

## Route A's unseen element cannot be revised by reading set texts

If you are on Route A, part of Paper 2a asks you to respond to poetry you have never seen.
**Exam-preparation priority**: practise on genuinely unseen poems under time -- reading once for sense,
once for method, then writing. This is a transferable skill built only by repetition, and it is the
component that most distinguishes Route A preparation from Route B.

## Open book raises the expectation of precision rather than lowering the workload

Having the texts available in Paper 2a removes the memorisation burden and raises the standard for
accuracy: an answer that quotes loosely or ranges vaguely compares badly with one that goes straight to
the right moment. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise navigating your texts at speed, knowing where
the turning points sit, so that time goes into writing rather than searching.

## Paper 1 is closed to no one and worth 40% -- it is the common foundation

Whatever route you are on, Paper 1 (Prose and Drama) is 40% of the grade in 90 minutes for 60 marks.
**Exam-preparation priority**: give it proportionate attention rather than treating it as the smaller
paper. At 1.5 minutes per mark it also rewards planning: two or three minutes deciding an argument
before writing produces a better structured response than starting immediately.

## Short, flexible quotations do more work than long ones

A four-word phrase containing one striking verb can support analysis of imagery, tone and character; a
three-line quotation usually cannot be analysed within the time available. **Exam-preparation
priority**: build a bank of short phrases and rehearse two different points from each.

## On Route B, choosing the coursework text well is part of the assessment

Route B's non-exam assessment is one extended response to a prose fiction text of the student's
choice. **Exam-preparation priority**: choose a text with enough to say about method -- structure,
narration, imagery -- rather than one you simply enjoyed, and use the redrafting time on the argument
rather than only on expression.

## Worked routine: an analytical paragraph

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper, set text or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - state the point about the writer's METHOD:
Not what happens, but what the writer does and to what end.

Step 2 - embed a short quotation in your own sentence:
Folded in, not dropped in as a standalone line.

Step 3 - analyse one specific word:
Name it, say what it connotes, and say why that matters for the
point in step 1.

Step 4 - name the technique:
Metaphor, caesura, dramatic irony, structural placement -- the
term earns credit when tied to an effect.

Step 5 - link outward to the whole text or the question's theme:
One sentence, so the paragraph contributes to an argument rather
than standing alone.
```

Step 3 is where grade differences usually lie: analysis of a specific word is demonstrable, whereas
comment on a quotation as a whole tends to restate it.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: confirm your route; if on Route A, practise unseen poetry under time and
  practise navigating the open-book texts at speed; if on Route B, choose a coursework text with
  method to discuss and redraft for argument; build a bank of short, flexible quotations for Paper 1.
- **During Paper 1**: spend two or three minutes planning the argument before writing.
- **During Paper 2a**: go straight to the passage you planned to use rather than searching, and resist
  long quotation just because the text is available.
- **In every paragraph**: point about method, embedded short quotation, analysis of a specific word,
  named technique, link outward.

## Self-test

1. What is compulsory, and how do the two routes differ?
2. What does Route A require that Route B does not?
3. Why does open book not reduce the preparation needed?
4. What should govern the choice of Route B's coursework text?

**Answers:** 1. Paper 1 (Prose and Drama, 40%) is compulsory for all; Route A adds a single open-book
Paper 2a (Poetry and Unseen Texts, 60%), while Route B adds a shorter Paper 2b (Poetry, 40%) plus a
non-exam assessment on a prose text of the student's choice (20%). 2. Response to genuinely unseen
poetry, which can only be prepared for by repeated timed practice on unfamiliar poems. 3. Because it
raises the expectation of precision and adds a navigation cost -- searching for a passage consumes
writing time, so knowing where key moments sit is itself preparation. 4. Whether the text offers
enough to discuss about method -- structure, narration, imagery -- rather than personal enjoyment
alone.
