## 1.1.2(a): reading apparatus correctly

Written questions test correct use by asking you to read a scale, record a value, or describe a step. The rule throughout: **record what the instrument can show, no more and no less.**

### Burette

- The scale reads **downwards** from 0.00 at the top.
- Read the **bottom of the meniscus** with your eye level with it, against a white background.
- Record to **two decimal places**, ending in 0 or 5: the scale is marked every 0.1 cm³, and you judge whether the meniscus is on a line or halfway between.

**Worked example 1.** The bottom of the meniscus sits just over halfway between the 23.4 and 23.5 marks. The reading is **23.45 cm³**. It is not 23.5 (too few figures) or 23.46 (more precise than the scale allows).

A titre is final reading minus initial reading. With an initial reading of 0.85 cm³ and a final reading of 23.45 cm³, the titre is **22.60 cm³**: keep the final zero.

### Pipette and volumetric flask

These have one graduation mark. Fill until the bottom of the meniscus sits on the mark at eye level. Record the volume the apparatus is made for, to the precision printed on it, such as 25.0 cm³ or 250.0 cm³.

### Thermometer

Read at eye level, with the bulb fully in the liquid and not touching the container. A thermometer marked every 1 °C can be read to the nearest 0.5 °C, so record 21.0 °C or 21.5 °C, not 21 °C, if that is the resolution you are using.

### Balance

Zero (tare) the balance with nothing on it, or with the empty container if weighing directly. Record every digit the display shows: 1.20 g on a two-decimal-place balance, not 1.2 g. The trailing zero tells the reader the precision.

### Gas syringe

Read the volume at the end of the plunger, against the scale, once the plunger has stopped moving. Make sure the syringe started at zero.

### Correct technique in a sentence

When asked to describe a technique, give the step and the reason in one line. For example: "Rinse the burette with the sodium hydroxide solution it will hold, so the solution is not diluted by water left inside." The [1.2.2 guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-h432-ocr-a-level-chemistry-1-2-2/) lists the steps for each technique.

## 1.1.2(b): appropriate units

M0.0 lists three things you may be tested on: converting between units, giving units for an equilibrium constant or a rate constant, and recognising that different units are used in similar topic areas, so conversions may be needed.

### Conversions you meet in practical work

| Quantity | Common units | Conversion |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | cm³, dm³, m³ | 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³; 1 m³ = 1000 dm³ = 10⁶ cm³ |
| Concentration | mol dm⁻³, g dm⁻³ | g dm⁻³ = mol dm⁻³ × M |
| Energy | J, kJ | 1 kJ = 1000 J |
| Pressure | Pa, kPa | 1 kPa = 1000 Pa |
| Temperature | °C, K | K = °C + 273 |
| Mass | g, kg, mg | 1 g = 1000 mg |

**Worked example 2: the ideal gas equation.** 0.150 g of a volatile liquid is vaporised. The vapour occupies 62.5 cm³ at 100 °C and 101 kPa. Calculate the Mr of the liquid. Use pV = nRT with R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹.

```
p = 101 kPa = 101 000 Pa
V = 62.5 cm³ = 62.5 × 10⁻⁶ m³
T = 100 + 273 = 373 K
n = pV ÷ RT = (101 000 × 62.5 × 10⁻⁶) ÷ (8.314 × 373) = 2.04 × 10⁻³ mol
Mr = 0.150 ÷ 2.04 × 10⁻³ = 73.7
```

The volume must be in m³ and the pressure in Pa, because R is in J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. Using cm³ with Pa gives an answer a million times too large.

**Worked example 3: mixing kJ and J.** For a decomposition, ΔH = +178 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = +161 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Find the temperature above which the reaction is feasible.

```
convert ΔS: 161 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ = 0.161 kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹
feasible when ΔG = ΔH − TΔS < 0, so T > ΔH ÷ ΔS
T = 178 ÷ 0.161 = 1106 K (to 4 significant figures)
```

### Units of Kc and rate constants

Work these out from the expression every time.

**Worked example 4.** For N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g):

```
Kc = [NH₃]² ÷ ([N₂][H₂]³)
units = (mol dm⁻³)² ÷ (mol dm⁻³)⁴ = (mol dm⁻³)⁻² = mol⁻² dm⁶
```

**Worked example 5.** For rate = k[A][B]²:

```
k = rate ÷ ([A][B]²)
units = mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ ÷ (mol dm⁻³)³ = mol⁻² dm⁶ s⁻¹
```

## 1.1.2(c): presenting observations and data

### Results tables

- Put the **independent variable in the first column** and the dependent variable after it.
- Give each column a heading with the **quantity and unit**, separated by a solidus: "time / s", "volume of gas / cm³".
- Write **no units in the body** of the table.
- Record every value in a column to the **same number of decimal places**, matching the resolution of the apparatus.
- Keep **raw data** (each reading) separate from **processed data** (means, 1/time), with the processing column headed by what it is: "1/time / s⁻¹".

### A titration table

| | Rough | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Final burette reading / cm³ | 24.10 | 23.45 | 23.95 | 23.80 |
| Initial burette reading / cm³ | 0.50 | 0.85 | 1.30 | 1.20 |
| Titre / cm³ | 23.60 | 22.60 | 22.65 | 22.60 |

All readings to two decimal places; the rough titre is shown but left out of the mean. The concordant titres 22.60, 22.65 and 22.60 give a mean titre of 22.62 cm³.

### Qualitative observations

An observation records what you see, hear, feel or smell, not what you conclude. Use precise terms.

| Vague | Precise |
|---|---|
| "It went cloudy" | "A white precipitate formed" |
| "It reacted" | "Effervescence; the gas turned limewater milky" |
| "It went green" | "The solution changed from orange to green" |
| "It got hot" | "The temperature rose from 20.5 °C to 27.0 °C" |
| "It dissolved" | "The solid dissolved to give a colourless solution" |

For a colour change, give the starting colour and the final colour. For a precipitate, give its colour and say that it is a precipitate. In tests with "a few drops" and "excess", record both results.

## Common mistakes

- Recording a burette reading to one decimal place, or to a figure not ending in 0 or 5.
- Dropping trailing zeros: 22.6 instead of 22.60, or 1.2 g instead of 1.20 g.
- Using cm³ with Pa in pV = nRT.
- Combining ΔH in kJ with ΔS in J without converting.
- Writing units in each cell of a table instead of in the heading.
- Writing a conclusion ("chloride present") where an observation ("white precipitate") is asked for.
- Giving only the final colour in a colour change.

## Exam technique

- **"Record"** or **"Write down the reading"**: give the value to the resolution of the scale, with its unit.
- **"Describe"** a technique: name the apparatus, the step and the reason.
- **"State the units"**: show the cancelling of units, not just the result.
- **"Suggest improvements to the table"**: name each fault and its fix.
