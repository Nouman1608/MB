This guide teaches **Section R: Forces and Newton's laws (R1 to R6)** of the AQA A-level Mathematics (7357) specification, version 1.3 (31 January 2018), for A-level exams from June 2018 onwards. The specification lists Section R under **Paper 2**, alongside vectors, quantities and units, kinematics and moments. A calculator is required in every 7357 paper. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² throughout unless a question says otherwise, and give answers to 3 significant figures where they are not exact.

## R1: forces and Newton's first law

A **force** is a push or a pull. It has size and direction, so it is a vector, measured in newtons (N). The forces you meet most often are:

- **weight**, W = mg, acting vertically down;
- **normal reaction**, R, from a surface, at right angles to it;
- **friction**, F, along a rough surface, opposing motion or the tendency to move;
- **tension** (pull) or **thrust** (push) in a string or rod;
- **driving force** and **resistances** for vehicles.

**Newton's first law:** a body stays at rest, or moves with constant velocity, unless a resultant force acts on it. So "at rest" or "constant velocity" both mean the resultant force is **zero**: the forces balance in every direction.

**Worked example 1.** A boat moves in a straight line at a constant 4 m s⁻¹. The engine gives a forward thrust of 650 N. Water resistance is 480 N and air resistance is R N. Find R.

```
Constant velocity → resultant force = 0 (first law)
650 = 480 + R
R = 170 N
```

The speed is not needed: any constant velocity means balanced forces.

## R2 and R3: Newton's second law and weight

**Newton's second law:** the **resultant** force on a body equals mass × acceleration, **F = ma**, and the acceleration is in the direction of the resultant. Draw every force, choose a positive direction, then write "resultant force in that direction = ma".

For 2D force vectors, a = F ÷ m component by component.

**Worked example 2.** Forces (5i + 2j) N and (3i − 8j) N act on a particle of mass 4 kg. Find its acceleration and the magnitude of the acceleration.

```
F = (5 + 3)i + (2 − 8)j = (8i − 6j) N
a = F ÷ m = (8i − 6j) ÷ 4 = (2i − 1.5j) m s⁻²
|a| = √(2² + 1.5²) = √6.25 = 2.5 m s⁻²
```

**Weight and g (R3).** Weight is the force of gravity on a body: W = mg. The specification says g may be assumed constant, and its value is used to varying degrees of accuracy, such as 9.8 or 9.81 m s⁻². It also says g is **not** a universal constant: it depends on location. The inverse square law for gravitation is not required. Use exactly the value of g the question gives, and don't give answers more precisely than g allows.

**Worked example 3.** A ball of mass 0.5 kg falls vertically. Air resistance is a constant 1.4 N. Find its acceleration.

```
Down positive: resultant = mg − 1.4 = 0.5 × 9.8 − 1.4 = 3.5 N
a = 3.5 ÷ 0.5 = 7 m s⁻² downwards
```

**Resolving (R2 extended).** When a force acts at an angle θ to your chosen direction, its component in that direction is F cos θ, and its component at right angles is F sin θ. Resolve **along** the motion to use F = ma, and **perpendicular** to the motion, where the acceleration is zero, to find a normal reaction.

**Worked example 4.** A sledge of mass 12 kg is pulled across smooth horizontal ice by a rope at 25° above the horizontal. The tension is 60 N. Find the acceleration and the normal reaction.

```
Horizontally (F = ma):   60 cos 25° = 12a
                         a = 54.378... ÷ 12 = 4.53 m s⁻²
Vertically (no motion):  R + 60 sin 25° = 12 × 9.8
                         R = 117.6 − 25.357... = 92.2 N
```

The rope lifts part of the weight, so R is **less** than mg.

## R4: Newton's third law, connected particles and equilibrium

**Newton's third law:** if body A exerts a force on body B, then B exerts a force on A that is equal in size and opposite in direction. The pair act on **different** bodies, so never put both in one F = ma equation.

**Worked example 5.** A man of mass 70 kg stands in a lift of mass 500 kg. The lift accelerates upwards at 0.6 m s⁻². Find (a) the tension in the lift cable, (b) the force the man exerts on the floor.

```
(a) Lift and man together, up positive:
    T − 570 × 9.8 = 570 × 0.6
    T = 570 × 10.4 = 5928 N
(b) Man alone: R − 70 × 9.8 = 70 × 0.6
    R = 70 × 10.4 = 728 N (floor on man)
    Third law: the man pushes down on the floor with 728 N
```

Treating lift and man as one body removes the internal force pair.

**Connected particles.** For particles joined by a light inextensible string, the tension is the same throughout the string and the particles share the same size of acceleration. Over a **smooth** pulley the tension is the same on both sides. Write a separate F = ma equation for each particle, then solve together. These modelling words (light, inextensible, smooth) are explained in [Overarching themes](/resources/aqa-a-level-mathematics-overarching-themes/).

**Worked example 6.** Particle A, mass 3 kg, lies on a smooth horizontal table. A light inextensible string joins A, over a smooth pulley at the table edge, to particle B, mass 2 kg, which hangs freely. The system is released from rest. Find the acceleration and the tension.

```
A (horizontal):  T = 3a
B (down):        2g − T = 2a
Add:             2g = 5a  →  a = 19.6 ÷ 5 = 3.92 m s⁻²
                 T = 3 × 3.92 = 11.76 = 11.8 N
Check with B:    19.6 − 11.76 = 7.84 = 2 × 3.92 ✓
```

**Worked example 7.** A car of mass 1200 kg tows a trailer of mass 400 kg along a straight horizontal road using a light rigid tow bar. The driving force is 2600 N. Resistances are 500 N on the car and 300 N on the trailer. Find the acceleration and the force in the tow bar.

```
Whole system:  2600 − 500 − 300 = 1600a  →  a = 1.125 m s⁻²
Trailer:       T − 300 = 400 × 1.125
               T = 750 N (tension)
```

**Equilibrium of a particle.** The resultant force is zero, so resolve in two perpendicular directions and set each total to zero.

**Worked example 8.** A particle of mass 5 kg hangs from two light strings. One makes 30° with the horizontal and the other 45° with the horizontal, on opposite sides. Find the tensions.

```
Horizontal:  T₁ cos 30° = T₂ cos 45°
Vertical:    T₁ sin 30° + T₂ sin 45° = 5 × 9.8 = 49
Solve:       T₁ = 35.9 N,  T₂ = 43.9 N
```

Quick check: the steeper string carries more weight, so T₂ > T₁.

## R5: resultant forces and motion in a plane

To add forces given by magnitude and direction, resolve each into perpendicular components, add them, then rebuild the resultant with Pythagoras and tan. Then a = resultant ÷ m, in the same direction.

**Worked example 9.** Three horizontal forces act on a particle of mass 3 kg: 12 N along i, 9 N along j, and 15 N at 135° anticlockwise from i. Find the resultant and the acceleration.

```
i:  12 + 15 cos 135° = 1.393... N
j:  9 + 15 sin 135°  = 19.606... N
|resultant| = √(1.393² + 19.606²) = 19.7 N
direction: tan⁻¹(19.606 ÷ 1.393) = 85.9° anticlockwise from i
|a| = 19.656... ÷ 3 = 6.55 m s⁻², in the same direction
```

## R6: friction

On a rough surface the friction force F satisfies **F ≤ μR**, where R is the normal reaction and μ is the **coefficient of friction**. Friction is only as large as it needs to be, up to its maximum μR.

- If the body is at rest and not about to move, F is whatever balances the other forces, and F < μR.
- If the body is **on the point of moving** (limiting equilibrium), F = μR.
- If the body is **moving**, F = μR, opposing the motion.

A smooth surface is the model μ = 0.

**Worked example 10.** A box of mass 5 kg rests on rough horizontal ground, μ = 0.4. A horizontal force of 15 N acts on it. Does it move?

```
R = 5 × 9.8 = 49 N;  maximum friction = 0.4 × 49 = 19.6 N
15 < 19.6, so the box stays at rest and F = 15 N (not 19.6 N)
```

**Worked example 11 (limiting friction and statics).** A box of mass 20 kg is on rough horizontal ground, μ = 0.35. It is pulled by a rope at 20° above the horizontal and is on the point of moving. Find the tension P.

```
Vertical:    R = 20 × 9.8 − P sin 20° = 196 − 0.342P
Horizontal:  P cos 20° = F = μR (limiting)
             0.9397P = 0.35(196 − 0.342P)
             P(0.9397 + 0.1197) = 68.6
             P = 64.8 N
```

**Worked example 12 (rough inclined plane).** A block of mass 4 kg is released from rest on a rough plane inclined at 30°, μ = 0.25. Find its acceleration.

```
Perpendicular:  R = 4g cos 30° = 33.948... N
Max friction:   F = 0.25 × 33.948 = 8.487... N
Down the slope: 4g sin 30° = 19.6 N > 8.49 N, so it slides
F = ma:         19.6 − 8.487 = 4a  →  a = 2.78 m s⁻² down the plane
```

## Common errors

- Using a single force instead of the **resultant** in F = ma.
- Writing R = mg when another force has a vertical component, or on a slope (R = mg cos θ there).
- Setting F = μR when the body is at rest and not on the point of moving.
- Swapping sin and cos when resolving: draw the angle and check which side it is next to.
- Writing a single F = ma equation for a pulley system whose particles move in different directions. Write one equation per particle.
- Quoting more figures than the value of g supports.
