Condensed recall for **3.1.1 Section A: Living with hazards** (OxfordAQA International AS and A-level
Geography 9635, Unit 1, studied by everyone). For the full explanation, read the
[Physical Geography 1 study guide](/resources/a-level-oxfordaqa-geography-physical-geography-1/).

## Hazard, risk and perception

A **hazard** is a natural event with the potential to cause damage; a **disaster** is when it
overwhelms a community's ability to cope.

```
Risk = hazard x vulnerability / capacity to cope
```

- **Vulnerability:** poverty, building quality, population density, age structure, warning systems.
- **Capacity to cope:** governance, wealth, infrastructure, preparedness.
- **Perception:** people accept risk fatalistically, adapt to it, or seek to dominate it through
  engineering.
- **Park model:** quality of life after a disaster through relief, rehabilitation and
  reconstruction; compare curves for different countries.
- **Hazard management cycle:** preparedness, response, recovery, mitigation.

## Tectonic hazards

| Margin | Process | Hazards |
|---|---|---|
| Constructive | Plates diverge, magma rises | Shield volcanoes, shallow earthquakes |
| Destructive | Oceanic plate subducts | Composite volcanoes, strong earthquakes, tsunami |
| Collision | Continental meets continental | Fold mountains, major earthquakes, no volcanoes |
| Conservative | Plates slide past | Major earthquakes, no volcanoes |

Plate movement is driven by mantle convection, ridge push and slab pull. Collision and conservative
margins make no magma, so have no volcanoes.

- **Magnitude** (moment magnitude scale) is a property of the event; **intensity** (Mercalli scale)
  is the effect at a place. One earthquake, one magnitude, many intensities.
- **Earthquake hazards:** ground shaking, liquefaction, landslides, tsunami.
- **Volcanic hazards:** lava flows, pyroclastic flows, lahars, ash falls, gases. Eruptions can often
  be predicted from precursors (seismicity, ground deformation, gas); earthquakes cannot be reliably
  predicted.

## Storm hazards

Tropical storms need sea temperatures above about 27 degrees Celsius to a depth of 60 m, latitudes
5 to 30 degrees (Coriolis deflection), low wind shear and convergence in the lower atmosphere.
Forms of hazard: high winds, **storm surges** (usually the greatest killer), coastal flooding, river
flooding, landslides.

River flooding responses: **hard** (dams, levees, channelisation: effective but expensive, can shift
the problem downstream) and **soft** (floodplain zoning, afforestation, wetland restoration: cheaper,
more sustainable, slower), plus forecasting and warnings. Risk management is by preparedness,
mitigation, prevention and adaptation.

## Fires in nature

Intense wildfires depend on vegetation type, fuel characteristics, climate and recent weather, and
fire behaviour. Causes are natural (lightning) or human (accidental or deliberate). Impacts are
primary and secondary, and environmental, social, economic and political.

## Required case studies

A recent volcanic event and a recent seismic event; two recent tropical storms in contrasting areas;
a recent wildfire event; a multi-hazardous environment; and a local-scale case study of a place in a
hazardous setting. Know impacts and human responses, with dates and figures you are sure of.

## Exam traps

- Using hazard and disaster as if they meant the same.
- Giving primary effects when secondary are asked for.
- Placing volcanoes at collision or conservative margins.
- Saying earthquakes can be predicted.
- Explaining a difference in impact by magnitude alone, not vulnerability and capacity to cope.

## Self-test

1. Write the risk equation.
2. Why do collision margins have no volcanoes?
3. Distinguish magnitude from intensity.
4. State two conditions needed for a tropical storm.
5. Why do two earthquakes of equal magnitude cause different impacts?

**Answers:** 1. Risk = hazard x vulnerability / capacity to cope. 2. No magma is generated there.
3. Magnitude is the size of the event; intensity is its effect at a specific place. 4. For example
sea temperature above about 27 degrees Celsius and low wind shear. 5. Vulnerability and capacity to
cope differ: building quality, warnings, emergency response and recovery funding.
