## The four specification points

1. Explain what is meant by service marketing.
2. Explain the additional elements used in the marketing of a service: process, people, physical evidence.
3. Evaluate the impact and importance of 'process', 'people' and 'physical evidence' in the marketing of a service.
4. Recommend and justify a marketing mix for a service as part of a marketing strategy.

## Core recall

### What makes services different

| Feature | Meaning | Marketing response |
|---|---|---|
| Intangibility | Cannot be seen or touched before purchase | Reviews, guarantees, tangible clues |
| Inseparability | Produced and consumed together | Staff become part of the product; train them |
| Variability | Quality differs by person and day | Standard processes, training |
| Perishability | Cannot be stored | Off-peak pricing, booking systems, capacity planning |

### The seven Ps

Product, price, place, promotion plus:

| Element | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Process | Systems and procedures for delivering the service | Online booking, check-in, complaints |
| People | Staff (and customers) involved | Trained, friendly staff |
| Physical evidence | Tangible clues of quality | Premises, uniforms, website, receipts, reviews |

### Evaluating the three extra Ps

| Element | Impact | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Process | Speed, reliability, lower cost, fewer complaints | Can feel impersonal; system cost; failure creates new problems |
| People | Staff deliver the experience; affects retention and reputation | Training cost; high turnover; benefit depends on pay and motivation |
| Physical evidence | Builds trust before purchase; reminds after | Fit-out costs; cannot hide poor service |

Importance is highest where the service is costly, high risk or hard for customers to judge (process for waiting-sensitive services, people for personal services, physical evidence for new or trust-based services).

### Managing perishable capacity

- Unused capacity is lost revenue.
- Off-peak discounts or promotions fill spare capacity; compare the discount with the variable cost.
- Risks: regular customers switch to the cheaper times; weaker premium image.
- Limit offers by day, treatment or customer type.

### Recommending a mix

1. Target customer and objective.
2. Product level and guarantee.
3. Price that signals quality and manages demand.
4. Place: location, online booking.
5. Promotion that makes the service visible: reviews, trials.
6. Process that is quick and consistent.
7. People: selection, training, reward.
8. Physical evidence that fits the price.
9. Check consistency; state a risk.

## Quick worked reminder

Linden Day Spa (invented): 36 slots, 75% used, price £48. Used 27; revenue £1,296; 9 empty slots worth £432. A 25% off-peak discount (£36) fills 6 slots: revenue £216; variable cost £9, so contribution £162.

Hotel desk: 2 staff, 9 minutes per check-in gives 13.3 check-ins an hour against 30 arrivals, so 16.7 wait. With 3-minute check-in, capacity is 40.

Training: 25 staff × £800 = £20,000. Turnover 40% to 28% saves 3 leavers × £3,000 = £9,000. Retention up 3 points of 8,000 = 240 customers × £70 = £16,800. Net gain £5,800. If the retention rise were only 1 point, benefit £14,600 and loss £5,400.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Service marketing | Marketing of products that are mainly an activity or experience |
| Intangibility | A service cannot be touched or seen before purchase |
| Perishability | A service cannot be stored |
| Process | The systems used to deliver a service |
| People | The staff and customers involved in the service |
| Physical evidence | Tangible clues to the quality of a service |
| Internal marketing | Recruiting, training and motivating staff to deliver the promise |

## Evaluation prompts

- What evidence is there that a change in process, people or physical evidence changes customer behaviour?
- How does the cost compare with the extra contribution?
- Is the service high risk or costly, so that trust matters most?
- Do the seven elements fit together and with the price?
- Finish with a judgement for the service and customers in the case.

## Exam traps

- Apply each element to the service in the case.
- Do not forget perishability when explaining pricing.
- Physical evidence is more than a building.
- Figures for training or process benefits are estimates; test them.
- Cover all seven Ps in a recommendation, then focus on the key ones.

## Command words

- **Explain:** define the element (process, people or physical evidence) and give a service example.
- **Evaluate:** weigh the benefit of the element against its cost for the service in the case.
- **Recommend and justify:** cover the seven Ps briefly, then justify the two or three that matter most.
- **Calculate:** show the capacity, discount or retention working step by step.

## Self-test

1. Name the three extra Ps for services.
2. Define perishability.
3. Define intangibility.
4. Give one example of physical evidence.
5. 40 slots, 60% used, price £50. Find the lost daily revenue.
6. Give one benefit of an online booking process.
7. Why does staff training matter more in services than in goods?
8. A discount price of £30 on a treatment with variable cost £8 fills 5 empty slots. Find the extra contribution.
9. Give one risk of off-peak discounts.
10. Why is physical evidence important for a new service?
11. Give one limit of an automated process.
12. 2 staff, each check-in 6 minutes. How many check-ins per hour?

### Answers

1. Process, people, physical evidence.
2. A service cannot be stored, so unused capacity is lost.
3. A service cannot be seen, touched or tried before purchase.
4. Any one: premises; uniforms; website; receipts; reviews.
5. 40% unused is 16 slots; 16 × 50 = **£800**.
6. Any one: faster; less staff time; fewer errors; convenience.
7. Staff deliver the service, so they are part of the product the customer experiences.
8. (30 − 8) × 5 = **£110**.
9. Any one: regular customers switch to cheaper times; weaker premium image.
10. Customers cannot judge quality in advance, so tangible clues build trust.
11. Any one: impersonal; cost; breakdown causes new problems.
12. 2 × (60 ÷ 6) = **20**.
