This guide goes deeper into **3.2.7 Work, energy and power** in Section 3.2 Mechanics and materials of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1. It links forces (3.2.5) to energy (3.2.8): work is the way a force transfers energy, and power is how fast it does so.

## What the specification asks for

- energy transferred, **W = Fs cos θ**, where θ is the angle between F and s
- the significance of the area under a force–displacement graph
- power as the rate of doing work, or rate of energy transfer: **P = ΔW/Δt = Fv**
- quantitative questions on variable forces
- **efficiency = useful output power ÷ input power**, which may be given as a percentage.

## Work done

Work is done when a force moves its point of application. The work done equals the energy transferred:

**W = Fs cos θ**

- F is the force (N), s the displacement (m), θ the angle between the force and the displacement.
- F cos θ is the component of the force along the displacement. Only that component does work.
- The unit is the joule: 1 J is the work done when a 1 N force moves its point of application 1 m in the direction of the force.

| Angle θ | cos θ | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 0° | 1 | Force along the motion: W = Fs |
| 90° | 0 | Force perpendicular to the motion: **no work** |
| 180° | −1 | Force opposes the motion: negative work; energy is removed |

**Zero-work forces.** The normal contact force on an object sliding along a level floor does no work; neither does the weight of a box carried horizontally at constant height. Something can be pushed hard and still have no work done on it if it does not move.

### Worked example 1: a lawnmower

**Question.** A gardener pushes a lawnmower 15 m across a level lawn. She pushes along the handle with a force of 90 N, at 40° below the horizontal. Find the work done by her push.

**Method.** The displacement is horizontal; the angle between force and displacement is 40°.

W = 90 × 15 × cos 40° = **1.0 × 10³ J** (1034 J).

**Comment.** The vertical component, 90 sin 40° = 58 N, pushes the mower into the ground. It does no work, but it increases the normal force and so the friction, which is why pushing at a steep angle is tiring.

### Worked example 2: work done by weight on a slope

**Question.** An 8.0 kg box slides 3.0 m down a slope at 20° to the horizontal. Find the work done on it by its weight.

**Method 1 (W = Fs cos θ).** The weight acts vertically; the displacement is along the slope. The angle between them is 90° − 20° = 70°. W = 8.0 × 9.81 × 3.0 × cos 70° = **80.5 J**.

**Method 2 (vertical drop).** The box falls a vertical height of 3.0 sin 20° = 1.03 m. W = mgΔh = 8.0 × 9.81 × 1.03 = **80.5 J**.

**Comment.** The two agree. The work done by gravity depends only on the vertical height change, not on the path.

## Area under a force–displacement graph

When the force varies, W = Fs cannot be used directly. The **area under a force–displacement graph** equals the work done.

- Straight-line sections: split into rectangles and triangles.
- Curves: count squares, find the work represented by one square, and multiply.

### Worked example 3: a variable force

**Question.** A force on a trolley rises steadily from 0 to 40 N as the trolley moves 0.20 m, then stays at 40 N for a further 0.30 m. Find the total work done.

**Method.**

1. Triangle: ½ × 0.20 × 40 = 4.0 J.
2. Rectangle: 0.30 × 40 = 12 J.
3. Total = **16 J**.

**Counting squares.** If one small square on a graph represents 2.0 N by 0.010 m, it is worth 0.020 J. A curved area covering 640 squares (counting part-squares of half or more) represents 640 × 0.020 = 12.8 J.

## Power

**Power** is the rate of doing work: P = ΔW/Δt, measured in watts (1 W = 1 J s⁻¹).

For a force F moving an object at constant velocity v in its own direction, the work done per second is F × (distance per second), so **P = Fv**.

### Worked example 4: top speed

**Question.** A car's engine has a maximum useful power output of 60 kW. At top speed on a level road, the total resistive force is 2000 N. Find the top speed.

**Method.** At top speed the velocity is constant, so the driving force equals the resistive force (first law). v = P ÷ F = 60 000 ÷ 2000 = **30 m s⁻¹**.

**Extension.** At 20 m s⁻¹ the same car, still at full power, has a driving force of 60 000 ÷ 20 = 3000 N. If resistance at that speed is 1000 N and the car's mass is 1200 kg, its acceleration is (3000 − 1000) ÷ 1200 = **1.7 m s⁻²**. As speed rises, the driving force at full power falls and resistance rises, so the acceleration falls to zero at top speed.

## Efficiency

**Efficiency = useful output power ÷ input power** (or useful energy out ÷ energy in). It has no unit and can be given as a decimal or a percentage. It can never exceed 1 (100%).

### Worked example 5: an electric hoist

**Question.** An electric motor takes 200 W from the supply. It lifts a 25 kg load through 3.0 m at constant speed in 6.0 s. Find the efficiency.

**Method.**

1. Useful work = mgΔh = 25 × 9.81 × 3.0 = 736 J.
2. Useful power = 736 ÷ 6.0 = 123 W.
3. Efficiency = 123 ÷ 200 = **0.61**, or **61%**.

**Comment.** The other 39%, about 77 W, is wasted, mostly heating the motor and overcoming friction. An efficiency above 100% always means input and output have been swapped.

## Common mistakes

- Using the distance moved instead of the displacement in the direction of the force.
- Using sin θ when θ is measured from the displacement.
- Saying a force does work when nothing moves, or when the force is perpendicular to the motion.
- Calculating power with time in minutes.
- Using P = Fv with the resultant force when the question asks for the engine's power: use the driving force.
- Writing efficiency with a unit, or giving a value above 100%.

## Exam technique for OxfordAQA command words

- **Define** work done: force multiplied by the distance moved in the direction of the force. **Define** power: rate of doing work, or rate of energy transfer.
- **Calculate**: state which angle you are using and why; show F cos θ before multiplying by s.
- **Show that**: for P = Fv, start from P = W/t and W = Fs, then divide by t.
- **Explain** why a vehicle reaches a top speed: driving force falls (fixed power, P = Fv), resistance rises with speed, and when they are equal the resultant force and acceleration are zero.
- **Estimate** the work from a curved graph: count squares and state the value of one square.
