## Section A

**1.** State the defining equation of simple harmonic motion and explain the significance of the minus sign. **[2]**

**2.** An oscillator completes 45 oscillations in 18.0 s. Calculate its period, frequency and angular frequency. **[3]**

**3.** A trolley tethered between two springs oscillates so that the distance between its two extreme positions is 7.0 cm. State its amplitude. **[1]**

**4.** Two oscillators each have a period of 0.80 s. Oscillator B reaches its maximum positive displacement 0.20 s after oscillator A. Calculate the phase difference between them, in radians. **[2]**

**5.** For each of the following, state whether the motion is simple harmonic, giving a reason.

**(a)** a = −36x

**(b)** a = −4x²

**(c)** a = 9x **[3]**

**6.** The acceleration of a particle is given by a = −490x. Calculate the angular frequency and the frequency of its oscillations. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The displacement x of an oscillator, in metres, is given by x = 0.025 sin(12πt), where t is in seconds.

**(a)** State the amplitude and calculate the frequency. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the displacement at t = 0.020 s. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the velocity at t = 0.020 s. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the maximum acceleration. **[2]**

**8.** The acceleration–displacement graph for an oscillating object is a straight line from (−0.040 m, +6.4 m s⁻²) to (+0.040 m, −6.4 m s⁻²).

**(a)** Explain how the graph shows that the motion is simple harmonic. **[2]**

**(b)** Determine the angular frequency and the period. **[3]**

**(c)** Calculate the maximum speed. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the speed when the displacement is 0.024 m. **[2]**

**9.** An oscillator moves with displacement x = x₀ sin ωt and period 0.60 s. For the first complete cycle (0 ≤ t ≤ 0.60 s), state the time or times at which

**(a)** the speed is maximum

**(b)** the acceleration has its maximum positive value

**(c)** the velocity has its maximum negative value. **[4]**

**10.** A trolley of mass 0.25 kg is attached to a spring of spring constant 40 N m⁻¹ on a smooth horizontal track.

**(a)** Use Hooke's law and Newton's second law to show that the trolley moves with simple harmonic motion, and identify ω². **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate ω. **[1]**

**(c)** The trolley is pulled 0.050 m from equilibrium and released. Calculate the time it takes to first reach the equilibrium position. **[2]**

**11.** Two identical pendulums of period 1.8 s swing with a phase difference of π/3 rad.

**(a)** Calculate the time interval between them passing through their maximum displacements. **[2]**

**(b)** For one pendulum, state the phase difference between its displacement and its velocity, and between its displacement and its acceleration. **[2]**

**12.** A student calculates the displacement of an oscillator with x₀ = 0.040 m and ω = 5.0 rad s⁻¹ at t = 0.30 s, using x = x₀ sin ωt. The answer obtained is 1.05 × 10⁻³ m.

**(a)** Identify the student's error. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the correct displacement. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** a = −ω²x [1]. The minus sign shows that the acceleration is always in the **opposite direction to the displacement**, towards the equilibrium position [1].

**2.** T = 18.0 ÷ 45 = **0.40 s** [1]; f = 1 ÷ 0.40 = **2.5 Hz** [1]; ω = 2πf = **15.7 rad s⁻¹** [1].

**3.** 7.0 ÷ 2 = **3.5 cm** [1].

**4.** Phase difference = (0.20 ÷ 0.80) × 2π [1] = **π/2 rad** (1.57 rad) [1].

**5. (a)** **Yes**: acceleration is proportional to x and opposite in direction (ω = 6.0 rad s⁻¹) [1].

**(b)** **No**: acceleration is not proportional to x [1].

**(c)** **No**: acceleration is in the same direction as the displacement, so it is not a restoring acceleration [1].

**6.** ω = √490 = **22.1 rad s⁻¹** [1]; f = ω ÷ 2π = **3.52 Hz** [1].

**7. (a)** x₀ = **0.025 m** [1]; ω = 12π, so f = 12π ÷ 2π = **6.0 Hz** [1].

**(b)** ωt = 12π × 0.020 = 0.754 rad [1]; x = 0.025 sin(0.754) = 0.025 × 0.685 = **0.017 m** [1].

**(c)** v₀ = ωx₀ = 37.7 × 0.025 = 0.943 m s⁻¹ [1]; v = 0.943 cos(0.754) = 0.943 × 0.729 = **0.69 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(d)** a_max = ω²x₀ = (37.7)² × 0.025 [1] = **36 m s⁻²** [1].

**8. (a)** The graph is a **straight line through the origin**, so a is proportional to x [1]; the gradient is **negative**, so a is opposite in direction to x [1].

**(b)** Gradient = −6.4 ÷ 0.040 = −160 s⁻² = −ω² [1]; ω = √160 = **12.6 rad s⁻¹** [1]; T = 2π ÷ 12.6 = **0.50 s** [1].

**(c)** v₀ = ωx₀ = 12.6 × 0.040 = **0.51 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(d)** v = ω√(x₀² − x²) = 12.6 × √(0.040² − 0.024²) [1] = 12.6 × 0.032 = **0.40 m s⁻¹** [1].

**9. (a)** At equilibrium: t = **0, 0.30 s and 0.60 s** [1].

**(b)** At x = −x₀: t = **0.45 s** [1].

**(c)** Velocity v = v₀ cos ωt is most negative at ωt = π: t = **0.30 s** [1] [1].

**10. (a)** F = −kx, so a = F/m = −(k/m)x; k/m is a positive constant, so a ∝ −x, which is SHM [1], with **ω² = k/m** [1].

**(b)** ω = √(40 ÷ 0.25) = √160 = **12.6 rad s⁻¹** [1].

**(c)** Released at the extreme, it reaches equilibrium after a quarter of a period: T = 2π ÷ 12.6 = 0.497 s [1]; time = T/4 = **0.12 s** [1].

**11. (a)** Δt = (π/3 ÷ 2π) × 1.8 [1] = (1/6) × 1.8 = **0.30 s** [1].

**(b)** Displacement and velocity: **π/2 rad** [1]. Displacement and acceleration: **π rad** [1].

**12. (a)** The calculator was in **degree mode**; ωt = 1.5 must be treated as radians [1].

**(b)** x = 0.040 sin(1.5 rad) [1] = 0.040 × 0.997 = **0.040 m** (0.0399 m) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating sin ωt in degrees.
- Omitting either "proportional to displacement" or "towards a fixed point" when defining SHM.
- Using the extreme-to-extreme distance as the amplitude.
- Using f where ω is required.
- Giving a phase difference as a time, or without units.
- Not stating that an a–x graph is a straight line through the origin before using its gradient.
