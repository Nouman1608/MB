Section 3.4 is extra International A2 content. All countries, firms and figures are invented; "cu" means currency units. ## Questions

**1.** State whether each policy is mainly market-based or interventionist: (a) floating the exchange rate; (b) government spending on rural clinics; (c) deregulation of business start-ups; (d) a managed exchange rate. **[4]**

**2.** Country D imports tractors with a world price of 8,000 cu.

**(a)** Calculate the domestic price with a tariff of 25%, and with a tariff of 10%. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in the domestic price when the tariff falls from 25% to 10%. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain one cost to Country D of this trade liberalisation. **[2]**

**3.** A foreign firm invests 150 cu million in a factory in Country E. It earns 40 cu million profit in a year and sends 75% abroad.

**(a)** Calculate the profit kept in Country E and express it as a percentage of the investment. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one disadvantage of FDI for Country E. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one advantage of FDI for Country E. **[2]**

**4.** In Country F, quantities of a crop are in thousand tonnes and price in cu per tonne. Demand is Qd = 1,500 - 2P. In a normal year supply is Qs = 3P - 1,000. In a bumper year supply is Qs = 3P - 700. A buffer stock authority sets a floor price of 480.

**(a)** Find the free-market price and quantity in the bumper year. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the quantity the authority buys at the floor price and the cost. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate farmers' revenue in the bumper year with the floor price and without it. **[2]**
**(d)** Identify one problem of running the scheme. **[1]**

**5.** A farmer borrows 300 cu from a microfinance lender at a flat 15% interest, repaid in 6 equal monthly instalments. A local moneylender charges 8% a month simple interest on 300 cu for 6 months.

**(a)** Calculate the total repayable and the monthly instalment from the microfinance lender. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the total repayable to the moneylender and the difference. **[2]**
**(c)** State one limitation of microfinance as a development policy. **[1]**

**6.** A cooperative sells 60 tonnes of produce on fair trade terms at a minimum price of 2,000 cu per tonne. The market price is 1,700 cu. The scheme also pays a premium of 120 cu per tonne.

**(a)** Calculate the revenue with and without the fair trade price. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage gain in revenue. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the total premium. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain one limit to the benefit of fair trade for the country as a whole. **[1]**

**7.** In Country G, 40,000 workers abroad each send home 2,400 cu a year. GDP is 3,200 cu million. Transfer fees are 6%.

**(a)** Calculate total remittances and the percentage of GDP. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the annual fee cost, and the saving if fees fall to 3%. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one drawback of relying on remittances. **[1]**

**8.** Country H earns 1,500 cu million from exports and spends 30% on debt service. A donor cancels one third of the debt service.

**(a)** Calculate the money freed and how many schools it pays for at 0.75 cu million each. **[3]**
**(b)** Separately, a donor gives 120 cu million in aid, 45% of it tied. Tied goods cost 25% more than on the open market. Calculate the real value of the aid. **[3]**

**9.** Country J receives 250,000 tourists, each spending 600 cu. Leakage is 55%. The local multiplier is 2. Calculate the final gain to Country J. **[4]**

**10.** Describe the role of (a) the IMF, (b) the World Bank and (c) NGOs in promoting development. **[6]**

**11.** Country K is poor, mostly rural and has weak roads and few skilled workers. Evaluate whether a market-based strategy or an interventionist strategy is better for promoting its development. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Market-based [1]. (b) Interventionist [1]. (c) Market-based [1]. (d) Interventionist [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* give a one-word label for each part, and add a reason if you can, since a bare label gives the marker nothing to reward on a part that is less clear cut.

**2. (a)** 8,000 x 1.25 = 10,000 [1]; 8,000 x 1.10 = 8,800 [1].
**(b)** (8,800 - 10,000) / 10,000 = **-12%** [1].
**(c)** Tariff revenue falls [1], so less government money for services; or domestic tractor makers face cheaper imports and may close, causing job losses [1].
*Examiner insight:* a cost needs a consequence for the country, not only the statement that revenue falls.

**3. (a)** Kept = 40 x 0.25 = **10 cu million** [1]; 10 / 150 = **6.7%** [1].
**(b)** Profits are repatriated, so most of the gain leaves the country [1]; the national income and balance of payments gain is smaller than the output suggests [1].
**(c)** Technology and skills transfer to local workers [1], which raises productivity beyond the foreign firm itself [1].
*Examiner insight:* an advantage or disadvantage scores best as a chain: the feature, why it matters, and the effect on development.

**4. (a)** 1,500 - 2P = 3P - 700 [1]; P = **440**, Q = **620** [1].
**(b)** At 480: Qd = 1,500 - 960 = 540 and Qs = 3 x 480 - 700 = 740 [1]; buys 740 - 540 = **200 thousand tonnes** [1]; cost 200 x 480 = 96,000 thousand = **96 cu million** [1].
**(c)** With floor: 740 x 480 = 355,200 thousand, or **355.2 cu million** [1]; without: 620 x 440 = 272,800 thousand, or **272.8 cu million** [1].
**(d)** Storage cost, or the funds needed to buy the surplus, or the stock cannot be sold if the price stays low [1].
*Examiner insight:* the floor only matters if it lies above the free-market price, so check that first and then use the floor price for both demand and supply.

**5. (a)** 300 x 1.15 = **345 cu** [1]; 345 / 6 = **57.50 cu** a month [1].
**(b)** 300 x 0.08 x 6 = 144 interest, so **444 cu** [1]; difference = 444 - 345 = **99 cu** [1].
**(c)** Interest may still be high, or borrowers may fall into debt, or loans are too small to build large firms [1].
*Examiner insight:* state the difference as a positive amount saved, and keep the units visible.

**6. (a)** With: 60 x 2,000 = **120,000 cu** [1]; without: 60 x 1,700 = **102,000 cu** [1].
**(b)** 18,000 / 102,000 = **17.6%** [1].
**(c)** 60 x 120 = **7,200 cu** [1].
**(d)** Only members of the scheme benefit, and the price may attract extra producers and create a surplus [1].
*Examiner insight:* divide the gain by the original (market-price) revenue, not by the new revenue.

**7. (a)** 40,000 x 2,400 = **96 cu million** [1]; 96 / 3,200 = **3%** [1].
**(b)** 6% fee: 96 x 0.06 = **5.76 cu million** [1]; at 3%, 2.88, so saving = **2.88 cu million** [1].
**(c)** Brain drain, or dependence on conditions in foreign labour markets [1].
*Examiner insight:* always convert units first, since 3,200 cu million and 96 cu million must be in the same units before a percentage is taken.

**8. (a)** Debt service = 1,500 x 0.30 = 450 [1]; freed = 450 / 3 = **150 cu million** [1]; 150 / 0.75 = **200 schools** [1].
**(b)** Tied = 120 x 0.45 = 54 [1]; real value of tied part = 54 / 1.25 = 43.2 [1]; total real value = 120 - 54 + 43.2 = **109.2 cu million** [1].
*Examiner insight:* divide by 1.25 rather than subtracting 25%, because the price is 25% above the open-market price, not 25% below it.

**9.** Total spending = 250,000 x 600 = 150 cu million [1]. Retained = 150 x 0.45 = 67.5 [1]. Multiplier is 2 [1]. Final gain = 67.5 x 2 = **135 cu million** [1].
*Examiner insight:* apply leakage before the multiplier; applying the multiplier to the full spending overstates the gain.

**10. (a)** The IMF monitors the international monetary system [1] and lends to countries with balance of payments problems, often with conditions [1]. **(b)** The World Bank lends for development projects [1] and gives technical advice, with cheaper loans for the poorest countries [1]. **(c)** NGOs run local projects in health and education [1] and campaign for causes, reaching communities directly [1].
*Examiner insight:* describe what each body does and give one specific example of how it helps, rather than a general statement that it helps development.

**11.** Market-based: trade liberalisation or encouraging FDI could bring capital and technology [1]; applied to K, FDI might build rural processing plants [1]. Weakness: low skills and weak roads limit private investment, so the market may not supply infrastructure alone [1]. Interventionist: public spending on roads, education and health builds the capacity K lacks [1]. Chain: roads cut transport costs, farmers reach markets, incomes rise, and demand grows [1]. Weakness: limited tax revenue, risk of waste, corruption and slow results [1]. The strategies overlap: the state builds roads while private firms invest [1]. Short-run, aid or FDI may fund the state's plans [1]. Judgement: interventionist spending on basics should come first, with market-based policies added once infrastructure and skills exist [1], and success depends on institutions and the government's ability to deliver [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* an evaluation gains credit for applying the argument to K's rural, low-skill setting and for reaching a supported judgement, not for listing generic pros and cons.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating a percentage change using the wrong base.
- Forgetting to apply leakage before the multiplier.
- Not showing the floor or ceiling when answering a buffer stock question.
- Mixing units, such as thousand tonnes and cu million.
- Giving an advantage or disadvantage of FDI with no explanation of the effect.
- Treating market-based and interventionist policies as opposites.
- Describing the IMF and World Bank as the same body.
- Concluding with no judgement.
