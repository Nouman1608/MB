Learn the method from the [2.1 study guide](/resources/ib-dp-business-2-1/) and the [2.1 revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-business-2-1-revision-notes/). All businesses and figures are invented.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *human resource planning*. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between demand for labour and supply of labour. **[2]**

**3.** State whether each of these is an internal or an external factor influencing human resource planning: (i) a new government rule on work permits, (ii) a plan to open a second factory, (iii) a fall in the birth rate twenty years ago. **[3]**

**4.** Orvane Opticians employs 64 staff. Next year it expects 5 retirements and 7 other leavers, and its expansion plan needs 72 staff.

**(a)** Calculate the number of staff Orvane must recruit or train next year. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the retirements and other leavers together as a percentage of the current workforce, to one decimal place. **[1]**

**5.** Explain one way in which the gig economy affects human resource planning. **[2]**

**6.** Explain two reasons why a middle manager might resist a plan to delayer the organisation. **[4]**

### Section B: structured questions

Questions 7 to 11 use the case below.

Feldspar Interiors is an invented furniture retailer with 14 stores and 210 staff. Most sales staff have worked for the firm for over ten years. The board plans to close 4 stores and move a large share of sales online within 18 months. Store staff will be offered either online customer-service roles, which need new software skills, or redundancy. A union representative has said staff "heard about the closures from a newspaper first". Local unemployment is low, and two rival retailers are recruiting.

**7.** Identify two external factors in the case that could influence Feldspar's human resource planning. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how low local unemployment affects Feldspar's plan. **[2]**

**9.** Analyse two reasons why Feldspar's staff might resist the change. **[4]**

**10.** Feldspar estimates driving forces for the change of online growth (5), lower rent (4) and rivals already selling online (3). It estimates restraining forces of staff fear of redundancy (5), loss of trust after the newspaper story (4) and training cost (2). This analysis tool is HL only.

**(a)** Calculate the total score for driving and restraining forces. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain what the scores suggest about the change. **[2]**

**11.** Evaluate two HR strategies Feldspar could use to reduce resistance to the change. **[10]**

**12.** Tolbrook Water, an invented utility, must replace paper job sheets with tablets for 300 field engineers within three months because a regulator requires digital safety records. Recommend whether Tolbrook should rely mainly on consultation or mainly on training and communication to manage resistance. **[6]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** Forecasting the number and type of employees a business will need [1] and matching that need to the employees available, inside and outside the business [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Mentioning both the forecast and the matching gains both marks. "Planning staff" alone restates the term.

**2.** Demand for labour is the number and type of workers the business needs to meet its plans [1]; supply of labour is the workers available, from current staff who remain and from the labour market [1]. **[2]**

**3.** (i) External [1]. (ii) Internal [1]. (iii) External, as demographic change [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** Supply = 64 − 5 − 7 = 52 [1]; gap = 72 − 52 = **20 staff** [1]

**(b)** 12 ÷ 64 × 100 = **18.8%** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A common error is to answer 8 (72 − 64), which ignores leavers. Show supply first.

**5.** The gig economy gives access to short-term or freelance workers [1], so the business can plan a smaller permanent workforce and cover peaks flexibly, although gig workers may lack firm-specific skills or loyalty [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two reasons, each identified [1] and explained in context [1]. For example: loss of status, because delayering removes management levels and the manager's role may disappear or be merged [1][1]; fear of a heavier workload, because remaining managers take on a wider span of control [1][1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Tie each reason to delayering. Generic "fear of the unknown" with no link earns less.

### Section B

**7.** Any two: low local unemployment [1]; rival retailers recruiting [1]; growth of online shopping as a social or technological change [1]. Maximum two. **[2]**

**8.** Low unemployment means few people are looking for work, so the supply of new recruits is limited [1]. Feldspar may struggle to fill online roles from outside and may need to retrain its own staff, while rivals may attract staff who choose redundancy [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Reason 1: fear of job loss, because 4 stores will close and some staff face redundancy [1]; this threatens income, so staff may oppose or slow the closures [1]. Reason 2: low trust, because staff learned of the closures from a newspaper [1]; they may doubt any promises about retraining, which makes them less willing to cooperate [1]. Other valid reasons, such as a skills gap with the new software among long-serving staff, can be credited. **[4]**

**10. (a)** Driving = 5 + 4 + 3 = **12** [1]; restraining = 5 + 4 + 2 = **11** [1]

**(b)** Driving forces lead only narrowly, by 1 [1], so the change is fragile; reducing a strong restraint such as fear of redundancy (5) would make it more secure [1] **[4]**

**11.** Strategy 1, communication and consultation: explanation in context, for example meeting staff and the union to explain the reasons for closures and the timescale [1]; benefit, it addresses the loss of trust caused by the newspaper story [1]; limitation, consultation takes time within an 18-month plan and cannot remove the fact of closures [1].
Strategy 2, training plus redeployment: explanation, funded software training for staff moving to online roles [1]; benefit, it addresses the skills gap among long-serving staff and keeps their product knowledge [1]; benefit, low local unemployment makes keeping existing staff cheaper than recruiting [1]; limitation, training costs money and some staff may not want desk-based roles [1].
Use of case evidence across the answer, such as low unemployment, rivals recruiting and the union comment [1].
Judgement: both are needed, but communication must come first because low trust would undermine any training offer [1]; condition, the strategy works only if the redeployment offer is genuine and redundancy terms are fair [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong answers judge which strategy should come first and why. Two separate descriptions with no comparison stay at the lower levels.

**12.** Context: the change is required by a regulator within three months, so it cannot be delayed or rejected [1]. Consultation would let engineers shape how tablets are used and build ownership [1], but full consultation is slow and staff cannot change the decision itself, which may cause frustration [1]. Training addresses the most likely cause, a skills gap with tablets among 300 engineers [1], and clear communication of the legal reason reduces misunderstanding [1]. Recommendation: rely mainly on training and communication, using limited consultation on practical details such as tablet set-up, because the deadline and legal requirement leave little room for wider participation [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Recommend" needs a clear choice. The deadline in the stem is the deciding fact, so use it.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Calculating the staffing gap from growth alone and forgetting leavers.
* Mixing up internal and external factors.
* Listing resistance reasons that do not fit the people in the case.
* Describing strategies without a limitation or a judgement.
* Ignoring time pressure or legal requirements given in the stem.
