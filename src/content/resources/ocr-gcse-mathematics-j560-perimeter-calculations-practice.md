These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 10.02, Perimeter calculations (10.02a to 10.02c). Every question is for **both tiers**. Section A has short questions on single skills; Section B has longer problems. Questions that ask for an answer "in terms of π" can be done without a calculator. Give other answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.

Learn the methods first in the [10.02 study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-perimeter-calculations/) or the [10.02 revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-perimeter-calculations-revision-notes/). The [OCR GCSE Mathematics hub](/boards/ocr/gcse/mathematics/) lists the other topics.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** An L-shaped room has a bottom wall of 16 m, a left wall of 11 m, a top wall of 7 m and an inner wall of 4 m running down from the end of the top wall. All corners are right angles.

**(a)** Find the length of the right-hand wall. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the perimeter of the room. **[2]**

**2.** A circular plate has diameter 13 cm. Find its circumference, giving your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**3.** A circle has radius 7.5 cm. Find its circumference in terms of π. **[1]**

**4.** A circular rug has circumference 75 cm. Find its radius. **[2]**

**5.** A sector has radius 15 cm and angle 48°. Find its arc length in terms of π. **[2]**

**6.** Find the perimeter of a semicircle of diameter 9 cm. **[2]**

**7.** A rectangle has length (3x − 1) cm and width (x + 4) cm.

**(a)** Write an expression for its perimeter. Simplify your answer. **[2]**

**(b)** The perimeter is 54 cm. Find x. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**8.** A bicycle wheel has diameter 66 cm.

**(a)** How far, in metres, does the bicycle travel when the wheel makes one complete turn? **[2]**

**(b)** The wheel turns 400 times. How far does the bicycle travel, in kilometres? **[2]**

**(c)** How many complete turns does the wheel make on a 5 km ride? **[2]**

**9.** A sector has radius 8 cm and angle 135°.

**(a)** Find the arc length in terms of π. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the perimeter of the sector. **[1]**

**10.** Working backwards from an arc.

**(a)** An arc of length 14 cm is part of a circle of radius 10 cm. Find the angle of the sector, to the nearest degree. **[3]**

**(b)** A sector has angle 240° and arc length 20π cm. Find its radius. **[3]**

**11.** A metal plate starts as a 24 cm by 10 cm rectangle. A semicircle of diameter 10 cm is joined to one short end. A semicircle of the same size is cut out of the other short end.

**(a)** Show that the perimeter of the plate is (48 + 10π) cm. **[3]**

**(b)** Write the perimeter correct to 3 significant figures. **[1]**

**12.** A flower bed is part of a ring. It lies between two circles with the same centre and radii 3 m and 5 m, and it has an angle of 120° at the centre.

**(a)** Find the perimeter of the flower bed. Give your answer in the form (aπ + b) m, where a is a fraction. **[3]**

**(b)** Edging is sold in whole metres at £4.50 per metre. Find the cost of enough edging to go all the way round the bed. **[2]**

**13.** A square of side 11 cm and a circle have the same perimeter. Show that the radius of the circle is 7.0 cm, correct to 1 decimal place. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 11 − 4 = **7 m** [1]

**(b)** inner horizontal wall = 16 − 7 = 9 m [1]; perimeter = 16 + 11 + 7 + 4 + 9 + 7 = **54 m** [1]

Total: 3

*Examiner insight:* The notch is at a corner, so 2 × (16 + 11) = 54 m is a useful check.

**2.** C = π × 13 [1]; = 40.84… = **40.8 cm** [1]

Total: 2

**3.** C = 2 × π × 7.5 = **15π cm** [1]

Total: 1

**4.** r = 75 ÷ 2π [1]; = 11.93… = **11.9 cm** [1]

Total: 2

*Examiner insight:* Dividing by π alone gives the diameter, 23.9 cm. Check which length the question asks for.

**5.** 48/360 = 2/15 [1]; 2/15 × 2π × 15 = **4π cm** [1]

Total: 2

**6.** arc = ½ × π × 9 = 14.13… [1]; perimeter = 14.13… + 9 = **23.1 cm** [1]

Total: 2

*Examiner insight:* The straight edge is the diameter. An answer of 14.1 cm is the arc alone.

**7. (a)** 2(3x − 1) + 2(x + 4) [1]; = **8x + 6** [1]

**(b)** 8x + 6 = 54, so 8x = 48 [1]; **x = 6** [1]

Total: 4

*Examiner insight:* Check by substituting: the rectangle is 17 cm by 10 cm, and 2 × 27 = 54.

**8. (a)** 66 cm = 0.66 m; C = π × 0.66 [1]; = 2.073… = **2.07 m** [1]

**(b)** 400 × 2.073… = 829.4 m [1]; = **0.829 km** [1]

**(c)** 5 km = 5000 m; 5000 ÷ 2.073… = 2411.4… [1]; **2411 complete turns** [1]

Total: 6

*Examiner insight:* Use the unrounded circumference in parts (b) and (c). In part (c) the wheel has not finished its 2412th turn, so round down.

**9. (a)** 135/360 = 3/8 [1]; 3/8 × 2π × 8 = **6π cm** [1]

**(b)** 6π + 16 = 34.84… = **34.8 cm** [1]

Total: 3

**10. (a)** θ/360 × 2π × 10 = 14 [1]; θ = 14 × 360 ÷ 20π [1]; = 80.2…, so **80°** [1]

**(b)** 240/360 × 2πr = 20π [1]; 4πr/3 = 20π [1]; **r = 15 cm** [1]

Total: 6

*Examiner insight:* In part (b) cancel π first: 4r/3 = 20 gives r = 15 with no calculator.

**11. (a)** The two long sides give 24 + 24 = 48 cm [1]. Each semicircle has arc ½ × π × 10 = 5π cm, and both arcs are on the outline: the added one replaces one short end and the cut-out one replaces the other [1]. Perimeter = 48 + 5π + 5π = **48 + 10π cm** [1]

**(b)** 48 + 31.41… = 79.41… = **79.4 cm** [1]

Total: 4

*Examiner insight:* Neither short end of 10 cm is on the final outline. Say why each straight end has gone; that is what a "show that" needs.

**12. (a)** outer arc = 1/3 × 2π × 5 = 10π/3; inner arc = 1/3 × 2π × 3 = 2π [1]; straight ends = 2 × (5 − 3) = 4 m [1]; perimeter = **(16π/3 + 4) m** [1]

**(b)** 16π/3 + 4 = 20.75… m, so 21 m must be bought [1]; 21 × 4.50 = **£94.50** [1]

Total: 5

*Examiner insight:* Edging comes in whole metres, so 20.75 m rounds up. Rounding down to 20 m would leave a gap.

**13.** perimeter of square = 4 × 11 = 44 cm [1]; 2πr = 44, so r = 44 ÷ 2π [1]; r = 7.0028…, which is **7.0 cm** to 1 decimal place [1]

Total: 3

*Examiner insight:* In a "show that", write the unrounded value (7.0028…) before rounding, so the examiner can see the given answer is justified rather than assumed.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the diameter in 2πr, or the radius in πd.
- Leaving out unlabelled walls in an L-shape.
- Giving only the arc for a semicircle or sector perimeter.
- Counting the straight end of a semicircle that has been joined on or cut out.
- Rounding a number of complete turns up, or a length of edging down.
- Rounding the circumference before using it again.
- Losing the exact form when the question asks for π in the answer.
