Use these questions to practise right-angled trigonometry, the sine and cosine rules, the area of a triangle, bearings, angles of elevation and a three-dimensional problem. Questions marked *(no calculator)* are in the style of a non-calculator paper, so leave exact values and surds as they are. For the rest, use a calculator in degree mode and give answers to three significant figures unless the question says otherwise. Draw a diagram for every question before you start. Mark each answer against the [1] points, then read the note under it.

## Questions

**1.** *(no calculator)* A right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of 10 cm. One of its angles is 30°. Find the length of the side opposite the 30° angle. **[2]**

**2.** *(no calculator)* The two shorter sides of a right-angled triangle are 8 cm and 15 cm. Find the hypotenuse. **[2]**

**3.** A triangular field ABC has AB = 120 m, BC = 95 m and angle ABC = 104°. **(a)** Find AC. **(b)** Fencing is sold in rolls of 25 m. How many rolls are needed to fence the whole perimeter? **[4]**

**4.** In triangle ABC, angle A = 40°, angle B = 75° and BC = 12 cm. Find AC. **[3]**

**5.** Find the area of a triangle with sides 8 cm and 11 cm and an included angle of 35°. **[2]**

**6.** The bearing of B from A is 060°. **(a)** Write down the bearing of A from B. **(b)** B is 8 km from A. Find how far east of A the point B lies. **[3]**

**7.** *(no calculator)* A ladder 6.5 m long leans against a vertical wall. Its foot is on level ground 2.5 m from the wall. **(a)** Find how far up the wall the ladder reaches. **(b)** The ladder makes an angle x with the ground. Write down the exact value of cos x. **[3]**

**8.** A vertical tower stands on level ground. From a point P, 40 m from the base of the tower, the angle of elevation of the top is 36°. **(a)** Find the height of the tower. **(b)** Point Q is on the opposite side of the tower from P, 25 m from its base. Find the angle of elevation of the top from Q. **[4]**

**9.** Triangle ABC has AB = 7 cm, BC = 12 cm and AC = 9 cm. Find angle BAC. **[4]**

**10.** A boat leaves port P and sails 14 km on a bearing of 070° to a buoy Q. It then sails 9 km on a bearing of 150° to a harbour R. **(a)** Show that angle PQR = 100°. **(b)** Find the distance PR. **(c)** Find angle QPR. **(d)** Find the bearing of P from R, to the nearest degree. **(e)** Find the area of triangle PQR. **[10]**

**11.** In triangle ABC, AB = 9 cm, AC = 14 cm and the area is 50 cm². Angle BAC is acute. **(a)** Find angle BAC. **(b)** Find the length of BC. **[6]**

**12.** ABCDEFGH is a cuboid. The base ABCD is a horizontal rectangle with AB = 12 cm and BC = 5 cm. The vertical edges are 4 cm, with E above A and G above C. **(a)** Find the length of AC. **(b)** Show that AG = √185 cm. **(c)** Find the angle between AG and the base ABCD. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Opposite = 10 × sin 30° [1] = 10 × ½ = **5 cm** [1].

*Examiner insight:* the exact values for 30°, 45° and 60° are the only way to score without a calculator, so write sin 30° = ½ before you multiply.

**2.** Hypotenuse² = 8² + 15² = 64 + 225 = 289 [1], so the hypotenuse is **17 cm** [1].

*Examiner insight:* add the squares when finding the longest side. Subtracting is for a shorter side, and marks go when the two are swapped.

**3.** **(a)** AC² = 120² + 95² − 2 × 120 × 95 × cos 104° [1] = 28 941, so AC = **170 m** (170.1 m) [1]. **(b)** Perimeter = 120 + 95 + 170.1 = 385.1 m [1]. 385.1 ÷ 25 = 15.4, so **16 rolls** [1].

*Examiner insight:* cos 104° is negative, so the term −2bc cos A adds to the total. If your AC is shorter than 120 m, check this first. Round the 15.4 up, never down.

**4.** AC ÷ sin 75° = 12 ÷ sin 40° [1]. AC = 12 × sin 75° ÷ sin 40° [1] = **18.0 cm** [1].

*Examiner insight:* each side is paired with the angle opposite it. BC is opposite A (40°) and AC is opposite B (75°). Pairing BC with 75° is the usual error.

**5.** Area = ½ × 8 × 11 × sin 35° [1] = **25.2 cm²** [1].

*Examiner insight:* the angle must lie between the two sides you use. Put the unit as cm² on the answer line.

**6.** **(a)** 060° + 180° = **240°** [1]. **(b)** East distance = 8 × sin 60° [1] = **6.93 km** [1].

*Examiner insight:* bearings are measured clockwise from north, so the east distance uses sine and the north distance uses cosine. A bearing of 60° needs the leading zero to be written as 060°.

**7.** **(a)** Height² = 6.5² − 2.5² = 42.25 − 6.25 [1] = 36, so the height is **6 m** [1]. **(b)** cos x = 2.5 ÷ 6.5 = **5/13** [1].

*Examiner insight:* the ladder is the hypotenuse, so you subtract. In part (b), adjacent over hypotenuse gives 2.5 ÷ 6.5, and the fraction must be simplified to 5/13 to be exact.

**8.** **(a)** tan 36° = height ÷ 40 [1], so height = 40 × tan 36° = **29.1 m** [1]. **(b)** tan θ = 29.06 ÷ 25 [1], so θ = **49.3°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* use the unrounded height (29.06 m) in part (b). Using 29.1 still gives 49.3° here, but early rounding costs the accuracy mark in other questions.

**9.** The longest side, 12 cm, is opposite angle A. cos A = (7² + 9² − 12²) ÷ (2 × 7 × 9) [1] = (49 + 81 − 144) ÷ 126 [1] = −14 ÷ 126 = −0.111 [1], so angle BAC = **96.4°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* a negative cosine means an obtuse angle, which is what you expect opposite the longest side. Take the inverse cosine of the negative value without dropping the sign.

**10.** **(a)** The bearing of Q from P is 070°, so the bearing of P from Q is 250°. The bearing of R from Q is 150°. Angle PQR = 250° − 150° = **100°** [1].
**(b)** PR² = 14² + 9² − 2 × 14 × 9 × cos 100° [1] = 196 + 81 + 43.76 [1] = 320.8, so PR = **17.9 km** [1].
**(c)** sin(QPR) ÷ 9 = sin 100° ÷ 17.91 [1], so angle QPR = **29.7°** [1].
**(d)** Bearing of R from P = 070° + 29.7° = 99.7° [1]. Bearing of P from R = 99.7° + 180° = 279.7°, which is **280°** [1].
**(e)** Area = ½ × 14 × 9 × sin 100° [1] = **62.0 km²** [1].

*Examiner insight:* in part (a) a "show that" needs the reasoning written down, which is the back bearing of 250°. A bare 100° gains no more than the one mark. A sketch with north lines at P, Q and R makes parts (a) and (d) much easier.

**11.** **(a)** ½ × 9 × 14 × sin A = 50 [1], so sin A = 100 ÷ 126 = 0.794 [1], giving A = **52.5°** [1]. **(b)** BC² = 9² + 14² − 2 × 9 × 14 × cos 52.5° [1] = 277 − 153.4 = 123.6 [1], so BC = **11.1 cm** [1].

*Examiner insight:* the question says the angle is acute, so you reject 127.5°, the other angle with the same sine. Marks in part (b) follow from your part (a) value if the method is right.

**12.** **(a)** AC² = 12² + 5² = 169 [1], so AC = **13 cm** [1].
**(b)** Triangle ACG is right-angled at C, because CG is vertical [1]. AG² = 13² + 4² [1] = 185, so AG = √185 [1].
**(c)** The angle between AG and the base is angle GAC [1]. tan(GAC) = 4 ÷ 13 [1], so angle GAC = **17.1°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* the angle between a line and a plane is measured to its shadow on the plane, here AC. Use AC (13 cm) in the tangent and not the base edge AB. The right angle at C must be stated in part (b).

*Every mark in these answers is for a specific step. Questions 10 and 12 are structured, so marks follow the steps above. Where an exam item is an extended response, it can instead be marked by levels of response. For a multi-part geometry question, state the triangle you are using before each calculation.*

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the sine rule with a side and an angle that are not opposite each other.
- Forgetting that cos of an obtuse angle is negative in the cosine rule.
- Using the area formula with an angle that is not between the two sides.
- Rounding down when a whole number of items must cover a length.
- Writing a bearing with fewer than three figures, or measuring it anticlockwise.
- Measuring a back bearing by adding 180° when the result is over 360°. Subtract 360° in that case.
- Rounding in the middle of a multi-part question and losing the final accuracy mark.
- Leaving the calculator in radian mode.
- Using the wrong edge when finding the angle between a line and a plane in 3D.
