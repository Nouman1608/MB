## Questions

**1.** *(no calculator)* A right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of 10 cm. One of its angles is 30°. Find the length of the side opposite the 30° angle. **[2]**

**2.** *(no calculator)* The two shorter sides of a right-angled triangle are 8 cm and 15 cm. Find the hypotenuse. **[2]**

**3.** A triangular field ABC has AB = 120 m, BC = 95 m and angle ABC = 104°. **(a)** Find AC. **(b)** Fencing is sold in rolls of 25 m. How many rolls are needed to fence the whole perimeter? **[4]**

**4.** In triangle ABC, angle A = 40°, angle B = 75° and BC = 12 cm. Find AC. **[3]**

**5.** Find the area of a triangle with sides 8 cm and 11 cm and an included angle of 35°. **[2]**

**6.** The bearing of B from A is 060°. Find the bearing of A from B. **[1]**

---

## Answers

**1.** opposite = 10 × sin 30° [1] = 10 × ½ = **5 cm** [1].

*Tip:* learn the exact values of sin, cos and tan of 30°, 45° and 60° for the non-calculator paper.

**2.** √(8² + 15²) = √289 [1] = **17 cm** [1].

*Tip:* add the squares of the shorter sides when finding the hypotenuse.

*Try the real question next:* Cambridge O Level Mathematics 4024, June 2025, Paper 12, Question 14.

**3.** **(a)** AC² = 120² + 95² − 2 × 120 × 95 × cos 104° [1] = 28 941, so AC = **170.1 m** [1]. **(b)** Perimeter = 120 + 95 + 170.1 = 385.1 m [1]. 385.1 ÷ 25 = 15.4, so **16 rolls** [1].

*Tip:* round up when you need enough of something to cover a length.

*Try the real question next:* Cambridge O Level Mathematics 4024, June 2025, Paper 22, Question 22.

**4.** AC / sin 75° = 12 / sin 40° [1]. AC = 12 × sin 75° ÷ sin 40° [1] = **18.0 cm** [1].

*Tip:* pair each side with the angle opposite it.

*Try the real question next:* Cambridge O Level Mathematics 4024, June 2025, Paper 22, Question 22.

**5.** ½ × 8 × 11 × sin 35° [1] = **25.2 cm²** [1].

*Tip:* the angle must be the one between the two sides you use.

*Try the real question next:* Cambridge O Level Mathematics 4024, June 2025, Paper 22, Question 22.

**6.** 060° + 180° = **240°** [1].

*Tip:* a back bearing differs by 180°, and bearings are always three figures.

*Try the real question next:* Cambridge O Level Mathematics 4024, June 2025, Paper 12, Question 6.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Rounding down when a whole number of items must cover a length.
- Using the cosine rule with the wrong angle.
- Writing a bearing with fewer than three figures.
