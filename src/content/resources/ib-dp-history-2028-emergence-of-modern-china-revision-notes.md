These revision notes are for **higher level (HL) only**. Emergence of Modern China (1910 to 1949) is a regional study examined on Paper 3, which standard level students do not sit. They condense the IB *History guide*, first assessment 2028. Read the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-emergence-of-modern-china/) first and then try the [practice set](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-emergence-of-modern-china-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Warlord | A military governor who ruled a region with a private army after 1916 |
| Guomindang (GMD) | The Nationalist Party founded by Sun Yixian |
| CCP | The Chinese Communist Party, founded in 1921 |
| United Front | An alliance between the GMD and CCP (1924 to 1927; 1937 to 1945) |
| New Culture Movement | Intellectual attack on Confucian tradition from about 1915 |
| May Fourth Movement | Student-led protest of 1919 against the Versailles decision on Shandong |
| Soviet | A base area run by Communists, as in Jiangxi |
| Encirclement campaign | A GMD offensive to destroy the Communist bases |
| Three Alls Policy | Japanese policy of "kill all, burn all, loot all" in northern China |
| Hyperinflation | Extremely rapid price rises after 1945 |

## Rise of national identity

- **Yuan Shikai:** strongman, dissolved parliament, tried to become emperor, died 1916.
- **Warlords:** regional armies, high taxes, no central authority.
- **Sun Yixian:** Three Principles of the People; Guangzhou base; reorganised GMD.
- **New Culture Movement:** Chen Duxiu, Hu Shi, Lu Xun; "science and democracy"; attack on Confucianism.

## First World War effects

1. China joined the Allies in 1917; about 140,000 labourers in France.
2. The Twenty-One Demands (1915) humiliated China.
3. Versailles gave Shandong to Japan; Chinese delegates refused to sign.
4. May Fourth Movement: students, strikes and boycotts; turned many intellectuals to Marxism.
5. CCP founded in 1921 with Soviet help.

## Guomindang rule: balance sheet

| Achievements | Failures |
|---|---|
| Nominal unification in 1928 | Warlords kept real power in places |
| Tariff control; new currency (1935) | Little help for the rural poor; no land reform |
| Roads, railways and some industry | Corruption and one-party rule |
| Army training with German advisers | Weak response to Japan in 1931 |
| New Life Movement | Seen as conservative and moralising |

## The rise of communism: key steps

1. **1927:** Shanghai massacre; urban strategy fails.
2. **1931:** Jiangxi Soviet; land redistribution; guerrilla methods.
3. **1930 to 1934:** five Encirclement Campaigns; the fifth drives the CCP out.
4. **1934 to 1935:** Long March (about 86,000 set out; a small fraction arrived).
5. **1935:** Zunyi Conference; Mao's rise.
6. **1936 to 1945:** Yan'an; mass line; rectification; expansion in the war.

## The war with Japan: effects

| Area | GMD | CCP |
|---|---|---|
| Politics | Second United Front; Chongqing government | Gains legitimacy by resistance |
| Military | Defeats in 1937 to 1938; "trading space for time" | Guerrilla warfare behind lines |
| Economy | Printing money; hyperinflation | Rent reduction and local self-help |
| Society | Corruption; refugees; famine | Mass line; village organisation |
| Size | Large army but declining morale | Membership over a million by 1945 |

## Civil War: causes of the communist victory

- **Strategy:** GMD overstretch in Manchuria; PLA moved from guerrilla to conventional war; Liaoshen, Huaihai and Pingjin campaigns (autumn 1948 to January 1949).
- **Peasants:** land reform for the CCP; heavy exactions for the GMD.
- **Leaders:** Mao's party discipline and capable generals; Jiang's interference.
- **Foreign powers:** US aid but no troops; Marshall mission failed; Soviet help in Manchuria.
- **Economy:** hyperinflation destroyed urban support for the GMD.

## Perspectives

- Chalmers Johnson: peasant nationalism due to Japanese invasion.
- Mark Selden: social revolution in Yan'an.
- Westad: GMD mistakes and CCP strengths together.
- Mitter and van de Ven: the GMD's wartime effort deserves credit.

## Worked example: a short plan

**Question:** "Evaluate the importance of the May Fourth Movement for the development of nationalism in China."

1. Thesis: it was a turning point in popular nationalism and in political direction, but built on earlier changes.
2. Background: Twenty-One Demands, Versailles, New Culture Movement.
3. Immediate impact: demonstrations, strikes, release of students.
4. Longer impact: Marxism, CCP (1921), GMD reorganisation.
5. Limits: the countryside was barely affected.
6. Judgement with perspectives.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing the First and Second United Fronts.
- Giving the Long March numbers as exact.
- Forgetting that the GMD had some achievements.
- Saying that the CCP won because it was more popular without evidence.

## Self-test

1. What did the Twenty-One Demands ask for?
2. Where was the CCP founded and when?
3. What was the Sun–Joffe Manifesto?
4. Why did the First United Front end?
5. Who led the Jiangxi Soviet with Zhu De?
6. What happened at Zunyi?
7. What was the Xi'an Incident?
8. Name two reasons for the Communist victory in 1949.

**Answers:** 1. Japanese control of Manchuria, Shandong and China's economy (1915). 2. Shanghai, 1921. 3. A 1923 agreement under which the Soviet Union promised help to Sun's unification of China. 4. Jiang's attack on the Communists in 1927. 5. Mao Zedong. 6. Mao took a leading role after criticising earlier military leaders (1935). 7. The kidnapping of Jiang in December 1936 to force an alliance against Japan. 8. Any two: peasant support through land reform, GMD corruption and hyperinflation, GMD overextension, effective PLA strategy, Soviet help.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the Nanjing decade a success or a failure?**
It is a debate. Use evidence on both sides and a clear judgement.

**How important was the Soviet Union?**
Significant at key points, such as the 1920s and 1945 Manchuria, but not decisive on its own.
