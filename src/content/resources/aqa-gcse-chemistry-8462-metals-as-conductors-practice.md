## Section A

**1.** **(a)** Name the particles that carry electrical charge through a metal. **[1]**
**(b)** State whether the positive ions in a solid metal move through the structure. **[1]**

**2.** Which of these solids conducts electricity? Choose **one**. **[1]**

- A Sodium chloride
- B Copper
- C Silicon dioxide
- D Sulfur

**3.** Explain why copper is used to make electrical cables. **[2]**

**4.** A metal saucepan has a plastic handle.

**(a)** Explain why the saucepan base is made from metal. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest why the handle is made from plastic. **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** Explain, in terms of structure and bonding, why a metal conducts electricity. **[3]**

**6.** One end of an iron rod is held in a flame. After a few minutes the other end is too hot to touch.

**(a)** Name the particles that transfer energy along the rod. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how these particles transfer the energy. **[2]**

**7.** A student connects solid samples of four substances into a simple circuit with a bulb.

| Substance | Bulb lights? | Bulb lights when sample is melted? |
|---|---|---|
| W | Yes | Yes |
| X | No | Yes |
| Y | No | No |
| Z | No | No |

**(a)** Which substance is a metal? **[1]**
**(b)** Which substance is ionic? Explain your answer. **[3]**
**(c)** State **one** type of structure that Y or Z could have. **[1]**

**8.** Solid sodium and solid sodium chloride both contain charged particles.

**(a)** Name the charged particles in sodium. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why solid sodium chloride does not conduct electricity although it contains charged particles.
**[2]**
**(c)** State how sodium chloride can be made to conduct. **[1]**

**9.** A student says: "Metals conduct electricity because the metal ions are free to move through the structure."

**(a)** Identify the mistake in this statement. **[1]**
**(b)** Write a correct version of the statement. **[2]**

**10.** Graphite and diamond are both forms of carbon. Graphite conducts electricity but diamond does not.
Compare the conduction in graphite with that in a metal. **[4]**

**11.** Explain how the structure of a metal allows it to conduct both electricity and thermal energy. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Delocalised electrons** [1]
**(b)** **No**: they stay in fixed positions [1]

**2.** **B** [1]

**3.** Copper has **delocalised electrons** that are free to move [1] and **carry the electrical charge** through the
wire [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** Metals are good **thermal conductors** [1] because the delocalised electrons transfer energy from the
cooker to the food [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Plastic is a **poor thermal conductor**, so the handle stays cool [1]

**5.** A metal has positive ions in a regular arrangement with **delocalised electrons** [1]; these electrons are
**free to move** through the structure [1]; they **carry electrical charge** through the metal [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** **Delocalised electrons** [1]
**(b)** The electrons gain energy at the hot end [1] and move through the metal, passing the energy to cooler
parts [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** **W** [1]
**(b)** **X** [1]; it does not conduct as a solid because its ions are fixed in position [1]; it conducts when
molten because the ions are free to move [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Any one: **small molecules**; **giant covalent** [1]

**8. (a)** Positive ions [1] and delocalised electrons [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Sodium chloride contains ions, but in the solid they are **held in fixed positions** in the lattice [1] so
they cannot move and carry charge [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** **Melt it** or **dissolve it in water** [1]

**9. (a)** The **ions** do not move to carry the charge in a solid metal [1]
**(b)** Metals conduct electricity because the **delocalised electrons** [1] are free to move through the
structure and carry the charge [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Marking points, up to 4:
- In graphite, each carbon atom has one electron that is delocalised [1].
- In a metal, outer electrons from the atoms are delocalised [1].
- In both, the delocalised electrons are free to move and carry charge [1].
- Graphite is a giant covalent structure of atoms, whereas a metal is positive ions in a sea of electrons [1].
**[4]**

**11.** Indicative content, one mark per point, up to 6:
- A metal has positive ions in a regular arrangement [1].
- Outer electrons are delocalised, forming a sea of electrons [1].
- The delocalised electrons are free to move through the structure [1].
- When a voltage is applied, the electrons carry electrical charge, so the metal conducts electricity [1].
- When part of the metal is heated, the electrons gain energy [1].
- They move through the metal and transfer the energy to cooler parts, so it conducts thermal energy [1].
**[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "free ions" for a metal.
- Not using the word "delocalised".
- Forgetting that the electrons carry the energy in thermal conduction.
- Saying ionic compounds conduct when solid.
- Describing the electrons as "shared between two atoms".
