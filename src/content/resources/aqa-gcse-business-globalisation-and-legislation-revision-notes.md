## 3.2.4 Globalisation

### Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Globalisation | Standard definition: the growing connection of the world's economies and markets, so goods, services, money and ideas cross borders more easily |
| Exchange rate | How much of one currency buys a unit of another |
| Stronger pound | Each pound buys more foreign currency |
| Weaker pound | Each pound buys less foreign currency |
| Exporter | A business that sells goods or services to customers abroad |
| Importer | A business that buys goods or services from suppliers abroad |

### How UK businesses compete internationally

The specification lists two bullets: better designs, and higher quality products at lower prices. Splitting them into design, quality and price for analysis:

- better designs (differentiation; can support a higher price)
- higher quality products
- lower prices (needs low unit costs)

Quality and price together mean good value, but this squeezes profit margin.

### Benefits and drawbacks of globalisation

| Benefits | Drawbacks |
|---|---|
| Bigger market through exports | More overseas rivals in the UK market |
| Cheaper components and products | Longer supply chains and transport costs |
| Larger output can lower unit costs | Exchange rate risk |
| New ideas, skills and suppliers | Cultural, language and legal differences |
| Less reliance on the UK economy | Reputation risk from unethical overseas suppliers |

### Impact on the four functional areas

| Area | Example effect |
|---|---|
| Operations | Overseas sourcing, longer supply chains |
| Human resources | Competition on labour costs; need for language skills |
| Marketing | Adapting products and promotion for other countries |
| Finance | Shipping costs, foreign income, currency risk |

### Exchange rates: direction of effect

| | Exporter | Importer |
|---|---|---|
| Stronger pound | Exports dearer abroad, sales may fall or profit per item falls | Imports cheaper, costs fall, profit may rise |
| Weaker pound | Exports cheaper abroad, sales may rise | Imports dearer, costs rise, profit falls unless prices rise |

You will not be asked to calculate exchange rate conversions. A firm that both imports and exports is partly protected.

### Method in steps: an exchange rate question

1. Say whether the business imports, exports or does both.
2. Say whether the pound is stronger or weaker.
3. State the effect on price or cost, then on sales, then on profit.
4. Use any figures from the case study to size the effect.
5. For evaluation, say what the firm could do and what could limit the effect.

### Worked reminder

A firm imports parts costing £150,000 a year and the cost rises 4%. Extra cost = £6,000. If profit was £40,000 it falls to £34,000, which is a 15% fall.

## 3.2.5 Legislation

Only brief knowledge of each law is needed. Put most effort into the effects on the business.

### Four examples of impact the specification gives

| Heading | What to discuss |
|---|---|
| Cost | Wages, equipment, training, adaptations, record keeping |
| Training needs | Managers and staff must know what the law requires |
| Recruitment | Fair selection, hiring plans, attracting staff |
| Consequences of failure | Penalties, compensation, legal costs, reputation, lost sales |

### The laws in the specification

| Area | Law named | Brief meaning (general terms) |
|---|---|---|
| Employment law | National minimum wage/living wage | Legal minimum hourly pay; rates change, so check current ones |
| Employment law | The Equality Act (2010) | Unfair treatment because of characteristics such as age, sex, race, disability or religion is unlawful in areas such as hiring, pay and promotion |
| Health and safety law | Health and Safety at Work Act (1974) | Employers must keep the workplace reasonably safe; employees must take care |
| Consumer law | Trade descriptions | What is said about a product must be accurate and not misleading |

### Effects by law

- Minimum wage: higher wage bill, possible price rises or fewer hours, better motivation and lower staff turnover.
- Equality Act: fair recruitment procedures, training for managers, possible workplace adaptations, a wider pool of talent.
- Health and safety: spending on equipment and training against fewer accidents, less absence, better morale and reputation.
- Consumer law: checks on labels and adverts, quality control, refunds or recalls if wrong, trust from customers.

### Benefits of a safe working environment

- fewer accidents and less absence
- better motivation and loyalty
- a stronger reputation, which helps recruitment
- avoiding compensation and legal action

### Worked reminder

8 staff each work 15 hours a week for 52 weeks. The legal rate rises by 40p. Hours = 8 x 15 x 52 = 6,240. Extra cost = 6,240 x £0.40 = £2,496 a year.

### Distinctions to remember

- Importer versus exporter: a stronger pound helps one and hurts the other.
- Cost of compliance versus cost of failure: the second is usually larger and less certain.
- Law versus ethics: law is the minimum a business must do. Ethical behaviour can go beyond it.

### Impact of legislation on the four functional areas

| Area | Example effect |
|---|---|
| Operations | Safer processes, quality checks so products match their descriptions |
| Human resources | Pay rates, fair recruitment, training in safety and equality |
| Marketing | Honest claims in adverts, packaging and online listings |
| Finance | Cost of compliance, and the cost of penalties, refunds or compensation if the firm fails |

### Consequences of failure (general terms)

- financial penalties and compensation claims
- legal costs and management time
- forced changes to how the business operates
- bad publicity, lost customers and difficulty recruiting
- do not quote figures for fines unless the case study gives them

## Case-study skills

### Building an analysis paragraph

1. Point: state the effect, for example "a weaker pound raises the cost of imported parts".
2. Link: tie it to the business in the stem, using its name, product or figures.
3. Explain: follow the chain, from cost to price to sales to profit.
4. Consequence: say what it means for the business, for example lower profit or a need to raise prices.

### Building an evaluation

Weigh both sides. For each, ask how big the effect is, how soon it happens and whether it lasts. Then give a reasoned judgement that answers the question set. A judgement that appears in the final line but is not supported by the earlier points earns little.

### Command words in practice

General exam guidance, not taken from the AQA specification text (the specification does not define command words).

| Command word | What to do |
|---|---|
| State, identify, define | Short answer, no explanation |
| Calculate | Show the working and give units |
| Explain, analyse | Give a reason, then follow the chain of consequences |
| Evaluate, justify | Weigh both sides and reach a judgement. Support it with evidence from the case |
| Recommend | Choose an option and give reasons that fit the business's situation |

### Worked reminder: weighing a cost

A shop spends £600 on safety training for its staff. If the training avoids one accident costing £4,000 in absence cover and lost sales, the net saving is £3,400. A real answer also notes that the accident might never have happened, so the benefit is uncertain, while the training cost is certain.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the ways the specification says UK businesses compete internationally: the design idea and the quality-and-price idea.
2. Give one benefit and one drawback of globalisation for a UK business.
3. A UK exporter faces a stronger pound. Explain the likely effect on sales.
4. A UK importer faces a weaker pound. Explain the likely effect on profit.
5. A firm imports goods for £90,000 a year and the cost rises by 5%. Calculate the extra cost.
6. Name the law that covers equality at work in the specification, and its year.
7. Name the health and safety law in the specification, and its year.
8. Give two benefits to a business of a safe working environment.
9. Give two consequences for a business of failing to follow consumer law.
10. 5 staff each work 20 hours a week for 52 weeks. The legal rate rises by 50p. Calculate the extra annual wage cost.

### Answers

1. Better designs, and higher quality products at lower prices.
2. Benefit, for example a larger market through exports. Drawback, for example more overseas competitors in the UK market.
3. UK goods become dearer for overseas buyers, so sales may fall, or the firm cuts its price and earns less per item.
4. Imports cost more, so costs rise and profit falls unless the firm raises its prices.
5. 5% of £90,000 = £4,500.
6. The Equality Act, 2010.
7. The Health and Safety at Work Act, 1974.
8. Any two: fewer accidents, less absence, better motivation, stronger reputation, avoiding compensation.
9. Any two: penalties, refunds or recalls, legal costs, damaged reputation, lost sales.
10. Hours = 5 x 20 x 52 = 5,200. Extra cost = 5,200 x £0.50 = £2,600.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying a stronger pound is good or bad without saying for whom.
- Trying to convert currencies, which the specification says is not needed.
- Naming a law but not explaining its effect on the business.
- Giving only costs of legislation and ignoring benefits, or the reverse.
- Not using figures or details from the case study.
- Treating the minimum wage as a fixed number. Rates change.
- Writing a list of points for an evaluation question with no judgement.
- Mixing up exports and imports.
