## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for developing it, up to two marks for each of two points (question 5: four marks for a developed explanation). The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA) [1].

**2.** Bay'ah [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA); Hazrat Umar (RA); Hazrat Uthman (RA); Hazrat Ali (RA).

**4.** Any two, [1] each: sent out the army of Usama; fought the Ridda wars against those who left Islam or refused Zakah; fought false prophets such as Musaylima; had the Qur'an collected into one volume; began campaigns towards Iraq and Syria; nominated Umar (RA) as his successor.

**5.** Hazrat Uthman (RA) had standard copies of the Qur'an made and sent to the major cities [1], and asked that other versions be set aside [1]. This made sure that Muslims everywhere recited the same text [1], which protected the Qur'an from differences as Islam spread to people who did not speak Arabic as a first language [1].

**6.** For each of two qualities: quality identified [1] and shown by an example [1].

- **Justice** [1]: he judged fairly, and made officials answer for their conduct [1].
- **Concern for the poor** [1]: he walked the streets at night to check on the needy and set up a register of stipends [1].
- **Organisation** [1]: he created provinces, governors, judges and a treasury to run the growing state [1].
- **Humility** [1]: he lived simply even when he was ruler of a large empire [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **They kept the community united** [1]: after the Prophet's death, Abu Bakr held the Muslims together and defeated those who left Islam [1].
- **They protected the Qur'an** [1]: it was collected under Abu Bakr and standardised under Uthman, so it is preserved [1].
- **They set an example of leadership** [1]: they were just, consulted others and were accountable, which gives Muslims a model for today [1].
- **They spread Islam** [1]: within thirty years, Islam spread beyond Arabia, and its message reached many peoples [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "You must keep to my Sunnah and to the Sunnah of the Khulafa ar-Rashideen" (Hadith 28 of an-Nawawi) [2].
- "Indeed, it is We who sent down the Qur'an and indeed, We will be its guardian" (Qur'an 15:9), used to show that the work of the Caliphs helped to fulfil the promise to preserve it [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Challenges (AO1):

- **Opposition over Uthman's death** [1]: some Companions demanded that his killers be punished, and this led to the Battle of the Camel [1].
- **Conflict with Mu'awiya** [1]: the governor of Syria refused to pledge loyalty until the killers were punished, leading to the Battle of Siffin [1].
- **Division among his followers** [1]: the Khawarij broke away after the arbitration and opposed both sides [1].
- **Disagreement over the best course** [1]: Ali (RA) was trying to restore order before acting, but critics said he should act at once [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Verily he among you who lives long will see great controversy" (Hadith 28 of an-Nawawi) [2].
- "You must keep to my Sunnah" (same Hadith), which Muslims use to explain how they should respond to disagreement [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- The Prophet (pbuh) himself told the Muslims to follow the Sunnah of the Rightly Guided Caliphs.
- Their leadership shows how to apply the Qur'an and Sunnah to new situations, such as governing a large state.
- Their qualities, such as justice, consultation and humility, are relevant today.
- **For the statement:** following their example gives unity and links Muslims to the earliest community, as the Hadith says.
- **Against the statement:** the circumstances of the seventh century differ from today; the Caliphs themselves used consultation and *ijtihad*, so Muslims today may apply the same principles to modern conditions without copying every action; Shi'a Muslims give particular authority to Ali (RA) and the Imams; the Hadith warns against innovation in religion, not against adapting governance.
- **Judgement:** Muslims value the example of the Caliphs, but they follow its principles (taqwa, justice, consultation) more than the details.

*Use of the passage*

- "Taqwa (fear) of Allah" is the base of all leadership.
- "listen and obey [your leader]" supports unity and order.
- "great controversy" shows that the Prophet (pbuh) foresaw disagreement.
- "the Sunnah of the Khulafa ar-Rashideen" directly supports the statement.
- "Beware of newly invented matters" limits what counts as following the Caliphs.
