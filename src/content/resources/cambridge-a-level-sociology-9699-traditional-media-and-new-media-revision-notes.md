These notes cover section 9.1 *Traditional media and the new media* of Cambridge International A
Level Sociology (9699) Paper 4: organisational trends, debates about control, differences between
old and new media, and whether the traditional media has been undermined. The full explanation is
in the [traditional and new media study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-sociology-9699-traditional-media-and-new-media/);
exam-style questions are in the
[traditional and new media practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-sociology-9699-traditional-media-and-new-media-practice/).

## The argument in one paragraph

Ownership has become more concentrated through integration, cross-media ownership and
conglomerates, while digitalisation has merged once separate media. New media are interactive and
global, and platforms now act as gatekeepers through algorithms. Control is contested between
owners, journalists, audiences, states and platforms. The traditional media has been weakened
economically but not replaced: the two now form a hybrid system.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Horizontal integration** | Buying firms at the same stage of the same industry |
| **Vertical integration** | Owning several stages, from production to distribution |
| **Cross-media ownership** | Owning different types of media (press, TV, film, online) |
| **Diversification** | Expanding into non-media businesses |
| **Media conglomerate** | Very large corporation with many media and non-media interests |
| **Synergy** | One part of a conglomerate promoting another's products |
| **Digitalisation** | Converting all content into digital code |
| **Convergence** | Technological, economic and cultural merging of media |
| **Platform** | Company that hosts and ranks others' content (search, social, video sites) |
| **Algorithm** | Software rules that decide which content users see |
| **Gatekeeping** | Deciding what reaches the audience |
| **Gatewatching** | Users selecting and sharing content from many sources (Bruns, 2005) |
| **Hybrid media system** | Old and new media logics operating together (Chadwick, 2013) |

## Trends

- **Concentration:** horizontal and vertical integration; Disney's purchase of most of 21st Century
  Fox (completed 2019).
- **Cross-media ownership and synergy:** content promoted across film, TV, music and merchandise.
- **Digitalisation and convergence:** the smartphone carries every medium; telecoms, broadcasting
  and computing firms compete.
- **Social media and platforms:** Alphabet (Google, YouTube); Meta (bought Instagram 2012,
  WhatsApp 2014); algorithmic gatekeeping.

## Old versus new

| Traditional | New |
|---|---|
| One-to-many | Many-to-many |
| Professional producers | Professional plus user-generated |
| Editors as gatekeepers | Algorithms and users as gatekeepers |
| Scheduled | On demand |
| National | Global |
| Easier to regulate | Harder to regulate |

Lister et al. (2003): new media are digital, interactive, hypertextual and virtual.

**Evaluation:** the boundary is blurred (newspapers online, broadcasters streaming); Chadwick's
hybrid media system.

## Who controls?

| Answer | Argument | Example or source |
|---|---|---|
| Owners | Ownership gives control of content | *News of the World* closure (2011) and scrutiny of Murdoch's influence |
| Managers and journalists | Professional norms limit owners | Editorial independence codes |
| Audiences | Market competition for attention | Pluralist view |
| The state | Licensing, law, censorship | China's internet controls; Ofcom (2003) |
| Platforms | Algorithms rank content | Search and social feeds |
| Users | Gatewatching and sharing | Bruns (2005) |

## Undermined or not?

**Yes:** falling print and scheduled TV audiences; advertising moved to platforms; news reached via
feeds; citizen journalism breaks stories.

**No:** Curran, Fenton and Freedman (2012) — internet revolution overstated; traditional brands
dominate online news; traditional media set the agenda; new media firms are conglomerates too.

**Judgement:** weakened as a business, not replaced as a source; hybrid system.

## Linking 9.1 to theory

| Evidence from 9.1 | Supports | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Conglomerates, cross-media ownership, synergy | Marxist and neo-Marxist views (9.2) | Fewer owners with common commercial interests |
| User-generated content, gatewatching | Pluralism and digital optimism (9.3) | More producers and more audience choice |
| Platform algorithms ranking content | Critical political economy | Control moves to firms that profit from attention data |
| Hybrid media system | A balanced conclusion | Old and new depend on each other |

## Essay plan in five steps

*"The traditional media has been undermined by the new media." Evaluate.*

1. Define traditional and new media; note the blurred boundary.
2. For: advertising shift, falling print and scheduled audiences, citizen journalism.
3. Against: Curran, Fenton and Freedman (2012); traditional brands online; agenda setting.
4. Reframe: concentration moved to platforms; hybrid system (Chadwick, 2013).
5. Judge: weakened economically, still central to news.

## Exam traps

- Cross-media ownership means owning *different types* of media, not just many outlets.
- New media is broader than social media.
- Do not assume more producers means more diversity of attention; audiences still concentrate on
  a few sources.
- Platforms may not create content, but ranking it is a form of control.
- A list of differences is description; evaluate whether each still holds.
- Avoid unsourced audience or advertising figures.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish horizontal from vertical integration.
2. What is cross-media ownership?
3. Define synergy and give an example.
4. Name three senses of convergence.
5. Why are platform companies described as gatekeepers?
6. Give two features of new media identified by Lister et al. (2003).
7. What is gatewatching?
8. What does Chadwick mean by a hybrid media system?
9. Give one argument that the traditional media has been undermined.
10. What did Curran, Fenton and Freedman (2012) argue?
11. Give one way the state controls the media.
12. Which theory of media control does audience choice support?

### Answers

1. Horizontal: buying firms at the same stage of the same industry; vertical: owning several
   stages from production to distribution.
2. Owning different types of media, such as newspapers, television channels and websites.
3. One part of a conglomerate promoting another, such as a film promoted by the same company's
   TV channels, music and merchandise.
4. Technological, economic and cultural.
5. Their algorithms decide which content users see, as editors once did.
6. Any two: digitality, interactivity, hypertextuality, virtuality.
7. Users selecting and sharing content from many sources, taking on part of the gatekeeping role
   (Bruns, 2005).
8. Older and newer media logics operating together, so outlets and platforms depend on each other.
9. Any one: advertising has moved to platforms; print and scheduled audiences have fallen; news is
   reached through feeds; citizen journalism breaks stories first.
10. That predictions of an internet revolution were exaggerated; established media adapted and
    remain dominant online.
11. Any one: licensing broadcasters; laws on defamation or security; censorship; regulators such
    as Ofcom.
12. Pluralism.
