Every business is invented and amounts are in dollars. Show your workings.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.**
**(a)** Define infrastructure. **[1]**
**(b)** Define tariff. **[1]**

**2.** Give two ways a government can affect business activity. **[2]**

**3.** A shop sells a jacket for 40 before tax. A sales tax of 15% is added. Calculate the price the customer pays. **[2]**

**4.** A business has an overdraft of 25,000. The interest rate rises from 7% to 9% a year. Calculate the increase in yearly interest. **[2]**

**5.** Brackwater Quarry sells crushed stone, mostly for road building. Outline one effect on Brackwater Quarry of a government cut in spending on roads. **[2]**

**6.** Arno imports bicycle parts. The government has added a 12% tax to imported parts. State the type of trade policy described. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Explain one way that higher interest rates may reduce consumer spending. **[3]**

**8.** Delmar Footwear makes shoes in its home country at a cost that lets it sell at 32 a pair. Similar imported shoes sell at 30 a pair. The government puts a 15% tariff on imported shoes, and importers pass it on in full.
**(a)** Calculate the price of imported shoes after the tariff. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse how the tariff may affect Delmar Footwear. **[4]**

**9.** Kinross plc makes a profit of 4.8 million a year. The government raises the tax on business profits from 20% to 24%. Kinross retains 40% of its profit after tax and pays the rest as dividends.
**(a)** Calculate profit after tax at each rate. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate retained profit at each rate. **[2]**
**(c)** Analyse how the tax rise may affect Kinross plc. **[4]**

**10.** Sarvan Exports is a small business that ships furniture overseas. A new government-funded port has opened nearby. The cost of moving each container to a port falls from 1,400 to 950. Sarvan ships 220 containers a year.
**(a)** Calculate Sarvan's yearly saving. **[2]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether the new port will benefit Sarvan Exports. **[8]**

**11.** A new law requires every food business to display detailed allergy information and train all staff in food safety. Meeting the law costs about 6,000 a year for any outlet. Bluebell Café has one outlet and a profit of 30,000. Analyse the impact of the law on Bluebell Café. **[6]**

**12.** Harlow Joinery wants to borrow 100,000 to buy machinery, which it expects to add 15,000 a year to profit. Interest rates have been rising. Option A is to borrow now at a fixed rate of 6% for five years. Option B is to wait a year in the hope that rates fall. Justify which option Harlow Joinery should choose. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The roads, railways, ports, airports, energy, water and communications networks that businesses rely on [1].
**(b)** A tax on imported goods [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two of: infrastructure provision [1], legislation [1], trade policy such as tariffs or trading bloc membership, taxation, interest rates. **[2]**

**3.** Tax 15% × 40 = 6 [1]; price **46** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** 25,000 × 2% [1] = **500** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Fewer roads are built, so demand for crushed stone falls [1], and the quarry's sales revenue falls unless it finds other customers such as house builders [1]. **[2]**

**6.** A tariff [1]. **[1]**

**7.** Higher interest rates make borrowing more expensive [1], so people are less willing to buy goods on credit, such as cars or furniture [1]; households with existing variable-rate loans also pay more interest, leaving less income to spend on other things [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** 30 × 15% = 4.50 [1]; price **34.50** [1].
**(b)** Before the tariff, imports were 2 cheaper than Delmar's shoes [1]. Now they are 2.50 dearer, so customers are likely to switch to Delmar [1]. Delmar's sales and revenue should rise [1], and it may be able to raise its own price slightly and still undercut imports, increasing its profit margin [1]. **[6]**

**9. (a)** At 20%: 4.8 × 80% = **3.84 million** [1]. At 24%: 4.8 × 76% = **3.648 million** [1].
**(b)** At 20%: 40% × 3.84 = **1.536 million** [1]. At 24%: 40% × 3.648 = **1.4592 million** [1].
**(c)** Retained profit falls by about 77,000 a year [1], so Kinross has less internal finance for new projects [1]. It may delay expansion or borrow instead, which adds interest costs [1]. Dividends also fall, so shareholders may be less satisfied and the share price could weaken [1]. **[8]**

**10. (a)** Saving per container 1,400 − 950 = 450 [1]; 220 × 450 = **99,000** [1].
**(b)** For: the yearly saving of 99,000 lowers costs and raises profit [1], or lets Sarvan cut its prices overseas and win more orders [1]. A nearby port may also mean faster, more reliable delivery [1], which improves customer satisfaction [1]. Against: competitors near the port gain the same advantage [1], so Sarvan's cost advantage over them does not change [1]; the saving depends on the port's charges and reliability, which may change [1]. Judgement: for example, Sarvan benefits, because 99,000 is a large saving for a small business, but it will need to use it, through lower prices or better service, to gain over rivals [1]. **[10]**

**11.** The cost of 6,000 is 20% of Bluebell's profit of 30,000 [1], so its profit could fall to 24,000 unless it raises prices [1]. A large chain spreads the same cost per outlet over far more sales, so its prices are less affected [1], and Bluebell may find it harder to compete on price [1]. On the other hand, trained staff and clear allergy information may make customers trust the café more [1], which could bring loyal customers and protect it from penalties if it broke the rules [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Option A is the better choice [1]. Interest at 6% is 6,000 a year [1], which is well below the expected 15,000 extra profit, so the machinery pays for itself [1]. A fixed rate protects Harlow if rates keep rising [1]. Waiting means losing a year's extra profit of about 15,000 [1], for a fall in rates that may not happen; even if rates fell by one percentage point, the saving would be only 1,000 a year [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling a tariff a tax on exports.
- Describing a policy without its effect on the business named.
- Confusing a rise of two percentage points with a 2% rise in interest paid.
- Forgetting that competitors may gain from the same policy.
- Ending "evaluate" or "justify" answers without a decision.
