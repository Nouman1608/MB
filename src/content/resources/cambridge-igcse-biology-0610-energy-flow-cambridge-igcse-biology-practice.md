> **Original questions written for Marlbridge.** They are not past-paper questions and do not copy the structure or mark tariffs of the real examination. Use them alongside official past papers.

These questions cover sub-topic **19.1 Energy flow** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Everything in this sub-topic is Core, so all candidates should attempt every question. All data in the questions are invented for practice.

Revise first with the [energy flow study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-energy-flow-cambridge-igcse-biology/) and the [energy flow revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-energy-flow-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** What is the principal source of energy input to biological systems?

A: the soil

B: the Sun

C: water

D: carbon dioxide

**[1]**

**2.** In which form does energy pass from a plant to a herbivore that eats it?

A: chemical energy

B: heat energy

C: light energy

D: sound energy

**[1]**

**3.** State the energy transfer that takes place in photosynthesis. **[2]**

**4.** Name the process that releases energy from glucose in all living organisms, and state the form in which this energy is eventually transferred to the environment. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the heat released by respiration in a rabbit cannot pass to a fox that eats the rabbit. **[2]**

**6.** The temperature inside a compost heap is higher than the temperature of the air around it. Explain why. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A caterpillar eats leaves containing 1 200 kJ of chemical energy. It loses 600 kJ in its faeces and transfers 480 kJ to the environment as heat from respiration. The rest is stored in new body tissue.

**(a)** Calculate the energy stored in new body tissue. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the energy in the leaves that is stored in new body tissue. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe what happens to the energy in the caterpillar's faeces. **[2]**

**8.** In a pond, algae are eaten by water fleas, and water fleas are eaten by small fish.

Describe the flow of energy from the Sun through this food chain and to the environment. **[4]**

**9.** A student sealed some pondweed, two snails and pond water containing bacteria in a glass jar.

**(a)** The jar was placed in a dark cupboard. After several weeks all the organisms had died. Explain why, in terms of energy. **[3]**

**(b)** A second sealed jar was placed on a sunny windowsill. The organisms survived for many months. Explain how energy and nutrients allow the organisms in this jar to survive. **[3]**

**10.** When coal is burned in a power station, energy is released. Explain why this energy originally came from the Sun. **[3]**

**11.** A mouse and a lizard have similar body masses. The mouse eats much more food each day than the lizard. Suggest why, in terms of the use of energy released by respiration. **[2]**

**12.** Only a small proportion of the light energy reaching a field of wheat is transferred into chemical energy in the plants.

**(a)** Suggest **two** reasons why. **[2]**

**(b)** Name **two** substances made by the wheat plants that contain chemical energy and could be eaten by animals. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

**2.** **A** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Energy passes along a food chain only as chemical energy in the organic compounds of food.

**3.** Light energy [1] is transferred to chemical energy (in glucose) [1].

**4.** Respiration [1]; heat / thermal energy [1].

**5.** The heat is transferred to the surroundings / air [1]; it is not stored in the rabbit's body tissues, so it is not in the food the fox eats [1].

**6.** Decomposers / bacteria and fungi in the heap are feeding on dead material [1]; they respire and release energy as heat [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Decomposition makes heat" is not enough. The mark depends on respiration by decomposers releasing energy as heat.

**7. (a)** 1 200 − 600 − 480 = 120 kJ [1].

**(b)** 120 ÷ 1 200 × 100 [1] = 10% [1].

**(c)** Decomposers feed on the faeces [1]; they respire and the energy is transferred to the environment as heat [1].

**8.** Algae absorb light energy from the Sun (using chlorophyll) [1]; photosynthesis transfers it to chemical energy in organic compounds such as glucose [1]; chemical energy passes to water fleas and then to fish when they feed [1]; at each stage energy is transferred to the environment as heat, mainly from respiration [1].

*Examiner insight:* Four separate ideas earn four marks: light absorbed, light to chemical, chemical passed by feeding, heat to the environment. Many answers describe the feeding but never say how energy leaves.

**9. (a)** No light, so the pondweed could not photosynthesise [1]; no new chemical energy entered the jar / no food was made [1]; all organisms kept respiring and transferring energy to the surroundings as heat, so the stored energy ran out [1].

**(b)** Light energy entered the jar and was transferred to chemical energy by photosynthesis in the pondweed [1]; energy passed to the snails and bacteria in food and left as heat [1]; nutrients such as carbon were recycled: decomposers released carbon dioxide and mineral ions that the pondweed reused [1].

*Examiner insight:* Part (b) tests the contrast between energy flow and nutrient cycling. Saying "energy is recycled" in the jar loses the mark.

**10.** Coal formed from the remains of plants / organisms that lived millions of years ago [1]; those plants absorbed light energy from the Sun in photosynthesis [1]; the chemical energy in their organic compounds was stored in the coal and is released when it burns [1].

**11.** The mouse is a mammal and maintains a constant body temperature [1]; much of the energy released in its respiration is used to keep warm and is transferred to the environment as heat, so it needs more food [1].

**12. (a)** Any two from: some light is reflected from the leaves; some light passes through the leaves; some light falls on the ground or on parts of the plant that do not photosynthesise [2].

**(b)** Any two from: glucose / sugars; starch; protein; fat / oil; cellulose [2].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that energy is "made" or "used up".
- Describing energy passing along a chain as light energy.
- Saying decomposers recycle energy.
- Forgetting that respiration releases energy as heat at every stage.
- Leaving out a unit or dividing by the wrong value in a percentage calculation.
