## Questions

**1.** Explain two reasons why the Wall Street Crash of 1929 led to a prolonged depression rather than a short recession. **[4]**

**2.** Read the extract (constructed for practice).

> "Banks are closing every week. The farmers cannot pay the loans, the shops cannot sell, and the men who were let go from the mills now line up for bread. The President says confidence will return. We have heard it many times." (Constructed for practice: a paraphrase of a newspaper editorial from 1931.)

**(a)** Identify two effects of the Depression mentioned in the extract. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the extract can be used to understand attitudes towards President Hoover, and one limitation of using it alone. **[4]**

**3.** Distinguish between the "relief", "recovery" and "reform" aims of the New Deal, giving one example of each. **[3]**

**4.** Analyse the political effects of the Depression in Canada or in one other country of the Americas. **[6]**

**5.** Explain how the Dust Bowl affected farming communities. **[6]**

**6.** Examine the role of the Depression in changing the experiences of women or of marginalised groups in one country. **[8]**

**7.** Discuss the effectiveness of the responses of R. B. Bennett and William Lyon Mackenzie King to the Depression in Canada. **[8]**

**8.** To what extent was Import Substitution Industrialisation an effective response to the Depression in one Latin American country? **[8]**

**9.** Paper 3 style essay question: "The New Deal ended the Great Depression in the United States." To what extent do you agree? **[15]**

## Answers

**1.** Two developed points, 2 marks each. Bank failures: thousands of banks failed between 1930 and 1933 [1], wiping out savings and cutting lending, so spending and investment fell [1]. Weak policy: the Federal Reserve allowed the money supply to shrink [1], deepening deflation and debt [1]. Trade: the Smoot-Hawley Tariff (1930) and retaliation cut world trade [1], hurting farmers and exporters [1]. Underlying weaknesses: unequal incomes, instalment debt and farm overproduction meant that demand was fragile [1], so shocks spread [1]. **[4]**

**2. (a)** Bank closures [1]; farmers' inability to repay loans and falling sales [1]; mass unemployment among mill workers [1]. Any two. **[2]**
**(b)** It shows doubt about official reassurances, suggesting loss of trust in Hoover's approach of urging confidence [1], and gives an example of how a newspaper reflected public frustration [1]. Limitation: an editorial states an opinion, may exaggerate, and reflects one place and one newspaper's political stance [1]; it does not show what Hoover's government actually did, such as the Reconstruction Finance Corporation [1]. **[4]**

**3.** Relief: immediate help to the unemployed and poor, for example the Civilian Conservation Corps or the Works Progress Administration [1]. Recovery: measures to revive the economy, for example the Agricultural Adjustment Act or the National Recovery Administration [1]. Reform: changes to prevent future crises and improve security, for example the FDIC, the SEC or Social Security [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Choose Canada. Governments fell: Bennett's Conservatives replaced the Liberals in 1930, then the Liberals returned in 1935 [1]. New parties: the CCF (1932), Social Credit in Alberta (1935) and the Union Nationale in Quebec (1936) [2]. Protest: the On-to-Ottawa Trek and the Regina Riot (1935) [1]. Federalism: provinces lacked the revenue to provide relief, leading to the Rowell-Sirois Commission [1]. Judgement: the Depression fragmented politics and led to calls for stronger central action [1]. (Equivalent credit for Brazil: Vargas, 1930, 1934, 1937.) **[6]**

**5.** Cause: drought and the ploughing of grassland left soil exposed to wind [1]. Effects: dust storms destroyed crops and made land unproductive [1]; falling prices and debt led to foreclosures [1]; tens of thousands of families moved, many to California, as migrant workers [1]. Social effects: health problems, hardship and prejudice against migrants [1]. Government response: soil conservation programmes and relief [1]. **[6]**

**6.** Choose women in the USA. Job loss and pressure on married women to give up work [2]. Women's employment overall held up as jobs in teaching and clerical work were seen as women's work [1]. Leadership: Frances Perkins and Eleanor Roosevelt [1]. Domestic burden: managing households on very little [1]. Limits: New Deal programmes often favoured men, and many women workers were excluded from Social Security coverage [1]. Judgement on overall change, weighing losses against new roles [2]. (Alternatively, marginalised groups: Black Americans were "last hired, first fired" but moved towards the Democrats; Mexican repatriation; the Indian Reorganization Act.) **[8]**

**7.** Bennett: initial reliance on tariffs and relief camps [1]; anger and the On-to-Ottawa Trek [1]; "New Deal" broadcasts of 1935 introduced regulation and insurance [1]; Bank of Canada and Wheat Board were lasting [1]; courts struck down much of the New Deal legislation [1]. King: cautious, referred laws to the courts [1]; Rowell-Sirois Commission shaped later reforms [1]. Judgement on effectiveness: both limited, with recovery dependent on trade and rearmament [1]. **[8]**

**8.** Choose Brazil. For: coffee exports fell, so industry grew to replace imported goods [2]; the state supported industry and infrastructure, setting a base for later growth [1]; urban jobs grew [1]. Against: dependence on imported machinery and capital remained [1]; control of labour and authoritarian rule under Vargas [1]; rural poverty continued and benefits were unequal [1]. Judgement: ISI was effective in diversifying the economy but created new dependence [1]. **[8]**

**9.** Use the Paper 3 markbands. A top answer (13 to 15 marks) shows in-depth understanding of the argument, evaluates a range of perspectives and reaches a substantiated judgement. Support (about 4 marks): banking stabilised through the Emergency Banking Act and FDIC; relief programmes put millions to work; GDP rose from 1933; confidence recovered. Against (about 4 marks): unemployment remained high through the 1930s; the 1937 to 1938 recession followed cuts; full employment came with wartime production from 1940 to 1941. Perspectives (about 3 marks): liberal historians such as Schlesinger and Leuchtenburg praise the New Deal; some critics argue that it prolonged the slump; the left argues that it did not go far enough; Keynesian explanations say that only large spending ended the slump. Judgement (about 4 marks): for example, that the New Deal began recovery and reform but war ended the Depression, so "ended" is an overstatement. **[15]**
