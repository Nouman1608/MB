## Questions

**1.** Explain why core body temperature must be kept close to 37 °C. **[3]**

**2.** Describe how the body responds when the external temperature falls. **[4]**

**3.** A volunteer rests in rooms at different air temperatures. Blood flow through the skin of the forearm is measured. Core temperature stays at 37.0 °C throughout.

| Air temperature (°C) | 15 | 25 | 35 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Skin blood flow (arbitrary units) | 22 | 60 | 150 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in skin blood flow from 25 °C to 35 °C. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the change in skin blood flow between 15 °C and 35 °C. **[2]**

**4.** On a very humid day, a person finds it hard to stay cool even though they are sweating heavily. Explain why. **[4]**

**5.** A student writes: "In hot weather, blood vessels move closer to the skin so more heat can escape." Correct the statement. **[3]**

**6.** Explain how the body brings blood glucose concentration back to normal after a meal rich in carbohydrate. **[5]**

**7.** A healthy person eats a meal at time 0. Blood glucose and insulin are measured every 20 minutes.

| Time (min) | 0 | 20 | 40 | 60 | 80 | 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glucose (mmol/dm³) | 4.9 | 6.8 | 7.9 | 6.2 | 5.3 | 5.0 |
| Insulin (arbitrary units) | 8 | 24 | 44 | 56 | 34 | 14 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in blood glucose from time 0 to its peak. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean rate of fall of blood glucose from 40 to 80 minutes, in mmol/dm³ per minute. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the insulin peak occurs after the glucose peak. **[2]**

**8.** (Higher Tier only) A runner has been running for several hours and their blood glucose begins to fall. Explain how glucagon and insulin bring about the return towards normal. **[5]**

**9.** (Higher Tier only) A person's blood glucose falls from 5.0 to 4.0 mmol/dm³ after 6 hours without food.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the fall is not larger, referring to glucagon. **[3]**

**10.**
**(a)** Compare the causes of type 1 and type 2 diabetes. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why insulin for type 1 diabetes is injected instead of being taken as a tablet. **[2]**

**11.** Three adults fast overnight, then drink a glucose solution. Their blood glucose (mmol/dm³) is measured.

| Time after drink (h) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Person X | 4.6 | 7.2 | 5.0 | 4.6 |
| Person Y | 11.2 | 18.5 | 20.1 | 19.8 |
| Person Z | 7.8 | 13.9 | 15.1 | 12.4 |

**(a)** State which person has normal glucose regulation, using data to justify your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in Y's glucose from time 0 to its peak. **[2]**
**(c)** Person Z has type 2 diabetes and person Y has type 1. Use the data to support these diagnoses. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest how Z's condition may be treated at first. **[2]**

**12.** A survey records how many adults in each body mass index (BMI) group have type 2 diabetes.

| BMI group | Adults surveyed | Adults with type 2 diabetes |
|---|---|---|
| Under 25 | 2000 | 24 |
| 25 to 29.9 | 1600 | 48 |
| 30 or over | 900 | 72 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of adults with type 2 diabetes in each group. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe the relationship shown, using the data, and state how many times greater the percentage is in the highest BMI group than in the lowest. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the survey does not prove that a high BMI causes type 2 diabetes. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain how type 2 diabetes affects blood glucose and suggest one lifestyle change that lowers the risk. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Metabolic reactions are controlled by enzymes [1]. If the temperature is too high, the enzyme's shape (active site) changes, it denatures and the reaction slows or stops [1]. If the temperature is too low, there are fewer collisions between enzyme and substrate, so reactions are too slow [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The idea is that enzymes work best near an optimum. An answer saying only "the body would be too hot or cold" gives no biology to credit.

**2.** Temperature receptors in the skin detect the fall and the brain responds [1]. Shivering: skeletal muscles contract rapidly and respiration releases heat [1]. Vasoconstriction: the arterioles to the skin narrow, so the surface capillaries receive less blood [1]. Less heat is transferred to the surroundings and less sweat is produced [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Describe" means state what happens. Name each response and say what changes.

**3. (a)** Increase = 150 − 60 = 90 [1]. 90 ÷ 60 × 100 = **150%** [1].
**(b)** Skin blood flow rises as air temperature rises because of vasodilation: the arterioles widen, sending extra blood close to the surface [1]. More heat is transferred to the surroundings, which keeps core temperature constant at 37.0 °C [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the starting value, 60. In (b), link the change in flow to the effect on core temperature.

**4.** Sweat cools the body when it evaporates, which transfers energy from the skin to the surroundings [1]. In humid air the air already holds a lot of water vapour [1]. So sweat evaporates more slowly [1]. Less energy is transferred from the skin, so cooling is less effective and body temperature may rise above 37 °C [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Sweating cools you" alone is only the starting point. Explain why evaporation is slowed.

**5.** Blood vessels do not move [1]. The arterioles supplying the skin capillaries widen (vasodilation) [1]. More blood flows near the surface, so more heat is transferred to the surroundings [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A "correct the statement" question needs the right idea stated, not just the word "wrong". Use the term vasodilation and say what widens.

**6.** The pancreas detects the rise in blood glucose and secretes insulin [1]. Insulin is carried in the blood [1]. Target cells, mainly in the liver and muscles, take up glucose from the blood [1]. The liver converts glucose to glycogen for storage [1]. As blood glucose falls back to normal, less insulin is secreted (negative feedback) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the gland, the hormone, the target and the effect. "Insulin lowers sugar" states a result without explaining it.

**7. (a)** Rise = 7.9 − 4.9 = 3.0 [1]. 3.0 ÷ 4.9 × 100 = **61%** [1].
**(b)** Fall = 7.9 − 5.3 = 2.6 [1]. 2.6 ÷ 40 = **0.065 mmol/dm³ per minute** [1].
**(c)** The pancreas secretes insulin in response to the rise in glucose, so insulin follows glucose [1]. Time is needed for release and transport in the blood, and insulin stays high while glucose is still above normal [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A rate needs units that include time.

**8.** (Higher Tier only) The pancreas detects the low glucose concentration and secretes glucagon [1]. Glucagon travels in the blood to the liver [1]. The liver converts stored glycogen to glucose [1] and releases it into the blood, so glucose concentration rises [1]. Insulin secretion falls, so cells take up less glucose, and as glucose returns to normal glucagon release falls (negative feedback) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say which hormone is released at low glucose and what the liver does, and avoid confusing glucagon with glycogen.

**9. (a)** (Higher Tier only) Fall = 1.0 [1]. 1.0 ÷ 5.0 × 100 = **20%** [1].
**(b)** Falling glucose stimulates the pancreas to release glucagon [1]. Glucagon makes the liver convert glycogen to glucose [1] and release it into the blood, which limits the fall [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Why is the fall not larger" needs a mechanism: link low glucose to the hormone, the organ and the glycogen store.

**10. (a)** In type 1, the pancreas does not make enough insulin (usually because the immune system destroys the insulin-producing cells) [1]. In type 2, body cells do not respond properly to insulin [1]. Type 1 is not caused by diet [1]. Type 2 is linked to obesity, inactivity, poor diet, age or family history [1].
**(b)** Insulin is a protein [1]. A tablet would be digested in the gut so it would not reach the blood intact [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both sides in each point, so use "whereas" and complete the contrast.

**11. (a)** X [1]: glucose peaks at 7.2 at 1 hour and returns to 4.6 by 3 hours, the same as the starting value [1].
**(b)** Rise = 20.1 − 11.2 = 8.9 [1]. 8.9 ÷ 11.2 × 100 = **79%** [1].
**(c)** Y starts high and stays at 19.8 at 3 hours, so almost none is removed, as expected if little insulin is made (type 1) [1]. Z falls from 15.1 to 12.4 between 2 and 3 hours, so some glucose is removed, but slowly, as expected if cells respond poorly to insulin (type 2) [1].
**(d)** Lifestyle change: a healthier diet, regular exercise or weight loss [1]. If that is not enough, medicines (such as metformin) can be used [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Support each claim with a quoted value and a time.

**12. (a)** 24 ÷ 2000 × 100 = **1.2%** [1]; 48 ÷ 1600 × 100 = **3.0%** [1]; 72 ÷ 900 × 100 = **8.0%** [1].
**(b)** The higher the BMI group, the higher the percentage with type 2 diabetes (a positive correlation) [1]. 8.0 ÷ 1.2 = **6.7 times** greater [1].
**(c)** A correlation shows an association only [1]. Other factors, such as diet, inactivity, age or family history, may differ between the groups [1].
**(d)** In type 2, cells respond poorly to insulin, so blood glucose stays too high [1]. Lifestyle change: regular exercise, a healthier diet or weight loss [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert counts to percentages before comparing groups of different sizes.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that blood vessels move towards or away from the skin instead of arterioles widening or narrowing.
- Saying sweating cools the body without saying that evaporation transfers energy.
- Describing a response without its effect, such as vasoconstriction with no mention of heat transfer.
- Saying insulin "breaks down" or "removes" glucose with no mention of cells taking it up or the liver storing glycogen.
- Mixing up glycogen and glucagon.
- Leaving out the pancreas, or leaving out that hormones travel in the blood.
- Giving type 2 diabetes as "no insulin" rather than cells that respond poorly.
- Calculating percentage change by dividing by the final value.
- Forgetting units on a rate, or quoting data with no time.
- Claiming a correlation proves a cause.
