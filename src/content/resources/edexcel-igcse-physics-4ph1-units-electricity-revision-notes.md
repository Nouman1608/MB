Condensed notes on sub-topic **2(a) Units** (statement 2.1) of Pearson
Edexcel International GCSE Physics (4PH1), Issue 4. For full explanations
and worked examples, use the
[Units for electricity study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-4ph1-units-electricity/).

## The list

Statement 2.1: ampere (A), coulomb (C), joule (J), ohm (Ω), second (s),
volt (V), watt (W).

## Key units table

| Unit | Quantity (symbol) | Defined through | Same as |
|---|---|---|---|
| ampere, A | current (I) | Q = I × t | C/s |
| coulomb, C | charge (Q) | Q = I × t | A s |
| joule, J | energy transferred (E) | E = Q × V | C × V, or W × s |
| ohm, Ω | resistance (R) | V = I × R | V/A |
| second, s | time (t) | | base unit |
| volt, V | voltage (V) | E = Q × V | J/C |
| watt, W | power (P) | P = I × V | J/s, or A × V |

## Must-know statements

- Current is the rate of flow of charge: 1 A = 1 C/s.
- The volt is a joule per coulomb: 1 V = 1 J/C (statement 2.20).
- 1 W = 1 J/s, and 1 W = 1 A × 1 V.
- 1 Ω = 1 V/A: a 1 Ω resistor needs 1 V to drive 1 A through it.

## Unit checks

```
E = Q × V        C × J/C           = J
E = I × V × t    C/s × J/C × s     = J
P = I × V        C/s × J/C         = J/s = W
R = V / I        V / A             = Ω
```

If an answer's unit does not match the quantity, the rearrangement is
wrong.

## One circuit, every unit

A 1.5 V cell drives 0.30 A through a resistor for 4.0 minutes (240 s).

```
charge      Q = I × t = 0.30 × 240   = 72 C
energy      E = Q × V = 72 × 1.5     = 108 J  (≈ 110 J)
power       P = I × V = 0.30 × 1.5   = 0.45 W
resistance  R = V / I = 1.5 / 0.30   = 5.0 Ω
check       E = P × t = 0.45 × 240   = 108 J
```

Each line gives a different unit from the same three data values. The
final check uses 1 W = 1 J/s.

## Symbols that double up

- V is the symbol for the quantity voltage and for the unit volt.
- W is the unit watt here, but the symbol for weight in Topic 1.
- In "E = I × V × t", E means energy (in J), not a unit.

## Exam traps

- Substituting 250 for 250 mA. Always convert to A.
- Leaving time in minutes in Q = I × t or E = I × V × t.
- Answering in W when the question asks for kW, or in J when it asks for
  kJ.
- Saying "a volt is a joule" or "a volt is a coulomb". It is a joule
  **per** coulomb.
- Describing current as "the amount of charge". It is the charge flowing
  **per second**.
- Writing kw, Kw or mj. Use kW and MJ: case matters.
- Rounding too early; keep the calculator value until the final answer.

## Self-test

1. State the unit of charge.
2. Express the ampere in terms of the coulomb and the second.
3. State what is meant by 1 volt.
4. Express the watt in terms of the joule and the second.
5. Convert 750 mA to amperes and 3.3 kΩ to ohms.
6. A current of 0.40 A flows for 2.0 minutes. Calculate the charge that
   passes.
7. A resistor has 6.0 V across it and 30 mA through it. Calculate its
   resistance.
8. A 12 V battery passes 50 C of charge. Calculate the energy transferred.
9. A heater draws 8.0 A from 230 V. Calculate its power in kW.
10. Show that A × V is equivalent to J/s.
11. A student calculates resistance using R = I × V and gets an answer in
    W. What has gone wrong?
12. A 2.0 kW heater is switched on for 30 minutes. Calculate the energy
    transferred in MJ.

## Answers

1. Coulomb (C).
2. 1 A = 1 C/s.
3. One joule of energy is transferred for each coulomb of charge that
   passes (1 V = 1 J/C).
4. 1 W = 1 J/s.
5. 0.75 A; 3300 Ω.
6. Q = I × t = 0.40 × 120 = 48 C.
7. R = V ÷ I = 6.0 ÷ 0.030 = 200 Ω.
8. E = Q × V = 50 × 12 = 600 J.
9. P = I × V = 8.0 × 230 = 1840 W = 1.84 kW, about 1.8 kW.
10. A × V = (C/s) × (J/C) = J/s.
11. Multiplying current by voltage gives power, not resistance; the
    correct rearrangement of V = I × R is R = V ÷ I, which gives ohms.
12. 2.0 kW = 2000 W, 30 minutes = 1800 s; since 1 W = 1 J/s, energy =
    2000 × 1800 = 3 600 000 J = 3.6 MJ.
