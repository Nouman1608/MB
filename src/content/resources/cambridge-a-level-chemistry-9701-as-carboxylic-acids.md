This guide covers subtopic **18.1, Carboxylic acids**, from Topic 18, Carboxylic acids and derivatives,
of Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027 series. It is AS Level content. The
[Carboxylic Acids and Esters study guide](/resources/as-carboxylic-acids-and-esters/) lists each reaction
with ethanoic acid; this page works with longer acids, writes the equations you are most likely to
fumble (nitrile hydrolysis, salts of divalent metals, [H] and [O] equations), and adds calculations and
a two-step synthesis.

## What the syllabus asks for

1. recall how carboxylic acids are produced:
   (a) **oxidation** of primary alcohols and aldehydes with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ or acidified KMnO₄ and
   **refluxing**;
   (b) **hydrolysis of nitriles** with dilute acid or dilute alkali followed by acidification;
   (c) **hydrolysis of esters** with dilute acid or dilute alkali and heat followed by acidification
2. describe:
   (a) the **redox** reaction with reactive metals to give a salt and H₂(g);
   (b) **neutralisation** with alkalis to give a salt and H₂O(l);
   (c) the **acid–base** reaction with carbonates to give a salt, H₂O(l) and CO₂(g);
   (d) **esterification** with alcohols, concentrated H₂SO₄ catalyst;
   (e) **reduction** by LiAlH₄ to a primary alcohol

## Naming acids and their salts

The functional group is –COOH (carboxyl). The carboxyl carbon is C1 and is counted in the stem:
CH₃CH₂COOH is **propanoic acid**, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH is **butanoic acid**. Salts replace "-oic acid" with
"-oate": sodium propanoate, CH₃CH₂COONa; magnesium butanoate, (CH₃CH₂CH₂COO)₂Mg. A Group 2 metal ion is
2+, so it needs **two** carboxylate ions.

## Producing carboxylic acids

**1. Oxidation under reflux.** A primary alcohol is oxidised first to an aldehyde and then to the acid.
Heating under reflux keeps the volatile aldehyde in the flask until it is oxidised further. Using [O] for
oxygen from the oxidising agent:

    CH₃CH₂CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃CH₂COOH + H₂O     (propan-1-ol to propanoic acid)
    CH₃CH₂CHO + [O] → CH₃CH₂COOH              (propanal to propanoic acid)

With acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ the colour changes from **orange to green**. With acidified KMnO₄ it changes from
purple to colourless. Secondary alcohols give ketones and tertiary alcohols are not oxidised, so neither
route gives a carboxylic acid.

**2. Hydrolysis of a nitrile.** Heat the nitrile under reflux with dilute acid or dilute alkali.

With dilute acid, the acid forms directly, with an ammonium salt:

    CH₃CH₂CN + 2H₂O + HCl → CH₃CH₂COOH + NH₄Cl

With dilute alkali, the carboxylate salt forms and ammonia gas is given off. Adding a strong acid
afterwards converts the salt into the free acid:

    CH₃CH₂CN + NaOH + H₂O → CH₃CH₂COONa + NH₃
    CH₃CH₂COONa + HCl → CH₃CH₂COOH + NaCl

**3. Hydrolysis of an ester.** Heating with dilute acid gives the acid and alcohol in an equilibrium.
Heating with dilute alkali goes to completion and gives the salt, which is then acidified:

    CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ + NaOH → CH₃CH₂COONa + CH₃OH

The details are in the [Esters study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-as-esters/).

## Reactions of carboxylic acids

Carboxylic acids are **weak acids**: they only partly dissociate in water,
CH₃CH₂COOH ⇌ CH₃CH₂COO⁻ + H⁺. They still show all the typical reactions of acids, just more slowly than
strong acids of the same concentration.

**(a) Reactive metals (redox).** The metal is oxidised and H⁺ is reduced to hydrogen; you see
effervescence and the metal dissolves.

    2CH₃CH₂COOH + Mg → (CH₃CH₂COO)₂Mg + H₂
    2CH₃CH₂COOH + 2Na → 2CH₃CH₂COONa + H₂

**(b) Alkalis (neutralisation).**

    CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH + KOH → CH₃CH₂CH₂COOK + H₂O

**(c) Carbonates and hydrogencarbonates (acid–base).** Effervescence of CO₂, which turns limewater milky.

    2CH₃CH₂COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃CH₂COONa + H₂O + CO₂
    CH₃CH₂COOH + NaHCO₃ → CH₃CH₂COONa + H₂O + CO₂

This is the standard **test** for a carboxylic acid: alcohols react with sodium but not with carbonates,
so the carbonate test separates the two.

**(d) Esterification.** Heat with an alcohol and a few drops of concentrated H₂SO₄ as catalyst. The
reaction is reversible:

    CH₃CH₂COOH + CH₃CH₂OH ⇌ CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O     (ethyl propanoate)

**(e) Reduction by LiAlH₄.** Lithium tetrahydridoaluminate, LiAlH₄, in dry ether reduces the acid to a
**primary alcohol**. Using [H] for hydrogen from the reducing agent:

    CH₃CH₂COOH + 4[H] → CH₃CH₂CH₂OH + H₂O

NaBH₄ is not a strong enough reducing agent for carboxylic acids; it is used for aldehydes and ketones.

## Choosing a route

| Route | Changes the carbon chain? | Best used when |
|---|---|---|
| Oxidation of a primary alcohol or aldehyde | no | the starting material already has the right number of carbons, ending in CH₂OH or CHO |
| Hydrolysis of a nitrile | the nitrile carbon becomes the COOH carbon | you start from a halogenoalkane with one carbon fewer than the acid (via KCN) |
| Hydrolysis of an ester | the ester splits into two smaller molecules | you need to recover the acid (and alcohol) from an ester |

A secondary alcohol such as butan-2-ol cannot be used for the first route, because oxidation stops at the
ketone, butanone. To make butanoic acid you must start from butan-1-ol or butanal.

## Worked example 1: a gas-volume calculation

**Question.** 1.85 g of propanoic acid reacts with excess sodium hydrogencarbonate. Calculate the volume
of carbon dioxide produced at room conditions. (Ar: H = 1.0, C = 12.0, O = 16.0; molar volume
24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹)

**Answer.**

Mr(CH₃CH₂COOH) = 3(12.0) + 6(1.0) + 2(16.0) = 74.0

n(acid) = 1.85 / 74.0 = 0.0250 mol

CH₃CH₂COOH + NaHCO₃ → CH₃CH₂COONa + H₂O + CO₂ is 1 : 1, so n(CO₂) = 0.0250 mol

V = 0.0250 × 24.0 = **0.600 dm³ (600 cm³)**

With sodium carbonate instead, the ratio is 2 acid : 1 CO₂, so the same mass of acid would give only
300 cm³. Read the equation, not just the reagent name.

## Worked example 2: a two-step synthesis

**Question.** Suggest how propanoic acid could be made from bromoethane in two steps.

**Answer.** Bromoethane has two carbons and propanoic acid has three, so a carbon must be added. The
reaction that adds one carbon is substitution by cyanide.

- **Step 1:** heat bromoethane with **KCN in ethanol** to give propanenitrile, CH₃CH₂CN.
- **Step 2:** heat propanenitrile under reflux with **dilute HCl** (or with dilute NaOH, then acidify) to
  give propanoic acid.

Counting carbons is the quickest way to spot that a nitrile route is needed. Oxidation of an alcohol or
aldehyde never changes the number of carbon atoms.

## Common mistakes

- **Using distillation instead of reflux** for oxidation to the acid: distillation removes the aldehyde
  before it is oxidised further.
- **Forgetting acidification** after alkaline hydrolysis: the product before acidification is the salt.
- **Writing (CH₃CH₂COO)Mg** for the magnesium salt. Mg²⁺ needs two carboxylate ions.
- **Using NaBH₄** to reduce a carboxylic acid. Only LiAlH₄ is strong enough.
- **Saying alcohols fizz with carbonates.** Only the acid gives CO₂.
- **Counting the carboxyl carbon wrongly** when naming: CH₃CH₂COOH has three carbons and is propanoic acid.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **State** or **Give** reagents and conditions: include the word "acidified" for dichromate or
  manganate(VII), "reflux" for oxidation, and "followed by acidification" where it applies.
- **Describe** a reaction: give an equation and what you would see (effervescence, colour change).
- **Calculate:** write the equation first and use its mole ratio.
- **Suggest** a route: count carbons, then pick reactions that change or keep the chain length.
