Section 2.3 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 asks for the characteristics, uses, advantages and disadvantages of these output devices: monitors, touch screen (as output), multimedia projector, laser printer, inkjet printer, dot matrix printer, plotter, 3D printers, speaker and actuator.

Condensed for revision. For full explanations and a worked printer choice, use the [output devices study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-output-devices-and-their-uses/), then try the [output devices practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-output-devices-and-their-uses-practice/).

## Three kinds of output

- **Soft copy**: temporary; screen or sound (monitor, touch screen, projector, speaker).
- **Hard copy**: permanent; paper or object (laser, inkjet, dot matrix, plotter, 3D printer).
- **Action**: movement in a control system (actuator).

## Screens and sound

**Monitor**

- LCD with LED backlighting, or OLED (each pixel lights itself; thin, can be flexible).
- For: instant output, no paper, easily updated, low power. Against: temporary, glare in sunlight, eye strain, large screens expensive.

**Touch screen (output)**

- Displays the options exactly where the user touches.
- For: one device for input and output; buttons change to suit the task. Against: smears, small display area, can crack.

**Multimedia projector**

- Projects an enlarged image from a computer onto a screen or wall.
- For: large image for an audience; video and animation. Against: weak in bright rooms; expensive light source; fan noise; image blocked by people.

**Speaker**

- Digital sound data → DAC → amplified analogue signal → sound.
- For: heard by many; supports screen readers; alerts. Against: disturbs others; not private; temporary.

## Printers

| Printer | How | Best for | Main advantage | Main disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Laser | Toner, charged drum, heat fuses | High-volume documents | Fast, low cost per page | Expensive to buy; ozone and toner particles |
| Inkjet | Ink droplets sprayed from nozzles | Photos, low volume | Cheap to buy; good colour | High cost per page; slow; smudges |
| Dot matrix | Pins strike inked ribbon (impact) | Multi-part forms; dirty or damp places | Prints copies at once; robust | Noisy, slow, low quality |
| Plotter | Pen or large-format ink | Plans, maps, CAD drawings | Very large, precise output | Expensive, slow, large |
| 3D printer | Layers of plastic, resin or metal | Prototypes, prosthetics, parts | Custom one-off items without moulds | Slow; costly materials; misuse risks |

## Actuators

- Motors, pumps, valves, solenoids; heaters switched by the system.
- Receive a signal from a computer or microprocessor and produce movement or action.
- Uses: greenhouse windows and sprinklers, heating, robot arms, automatic doors, industrial valves.
- For: automatic action on the physical world; fast and continuous; works in dangerous places. Against: mechanical failure and maintenance; wrong action if the signal or sensor is faulty; cost.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Soft copy | Temporary output on screen or as sound |
| Hard copy | Permanent printed or physical output |
| Pixel | Smallest dot of colour on a screen |
| LCD | Liquid crystal display, usually lit by LEDs |
| OLED | Organic LED display; each pixel produces its own light |
| Toner | Powder used by laser printers |
| Impact printer | Printer that strikes the paper through a ribbon (dot matrix) |
| Multi-part form | Stack of sheets that produces copies with one print |
| Additive manufacturing | Building an object layer by layer (3D printing) |
| Actuator | Output device that creates movement in a control system |

## Exam traps

- Laser printers use **toner**, not ink.
- The dot matrix printer's key advantage is **multi-part copies** and robustness, not quality.
- A plotter is chosen for **size and precision**, not speed.
- An actuator is an **output** device; a sensor is an **input** device.
- Cost to buy and cost per page are different points; say which you mean.
- For speakers, the computer's digital data must be converted to analogue.

## Self-test

1. State the difference between soft copy and hard copy.
2. Give **one** advantage of an OLED screen.
3. Give **two** disadvantages of a multimedia projector.
4. Describe how a laser printer prints a page.
5. Give **two** reasons an office would choose a laser printer over an inkjet.
6. Why might a home user choose an inkjet printer?
7. Give **one** use where a dot matrix printer is better than a laser printer, and explain why.
8. Name the printer used to produce large engineering drawings.
9. Give **two** uses of a 3D printer.
10. Name **two** types of actuator.
11. Explain the role of an actuator in an automatic greenhouse.
12. Give **one** disadvantage of using speakers in an open-plan office.

## Self-test answers

1. Soft copy is temporary output on a screen or as sound; hard copy is permanent, such as a printout.
2. Each pixel produces its own light, so it can be thinner, flexible, or give higher contrast.
3. Any two: hard to see in bright rooms; light source expensive and wears out; fan noise; image blocked by people.
4. A charged drum attracts toner to the areas to be printed, the toner is transferred to the paper, and heat fuses it in place.
5. Any two: faster; lower cost per page; better for high volumes; toner lasts many pages.
6. Cheap to buy and gives good colour or photo quality for small amounts of printing.
7. Multi-part forms such as invoices, because the impact prints through all the layers at once; or dusty or damp environments, because it is robust.
8. Plotter.
9. Any two: prototypes, replacement parts, prosthetic limbs, dental parts, architectural models.
10. Any two: motor, pump, valve, solenoid.
11. When the computer decides from sensor readings that action is needed, it sends a signal to an actuator, such as a motor that opens a window or a pump that waters the plants.
12. The sound disturbs other workers, or the output is not private.
