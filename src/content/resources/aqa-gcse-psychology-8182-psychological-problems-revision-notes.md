## Mental health: why recorded rates change

- **Increased recognition** → more problems diagnosed.
- **Less stigma** → more people seek help.
- **Modern living** (isolation, pressure, insecurity) → possibly more people affected.

The first two raise **recorded** cases; the third suggests a **real** rise. Beliefs about mental
health also **vary between cultures**.

## Effects

| Individual | Society |
|---|---|
| Damaged relationships | More need for care services |
| Difficulty coping day to day | Economic cost (lost work, benefits) |
| Poorer physical health | Possible links with crime |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sadness | Normal, short-lived response to events |
| Unipolar depression | Persistent low mood without mania |
| Bipolar depression | Depression alternating with mania |
| ICD | International Classification of Diseases; grades depression by number and severity of symptoms |
| Negative schema | Mental framework that makes a person view self, world and future negatively |
| Attributional style | Way of explaining events; depressive = internal, stable, global |
| Dependence | Compulsion to use despite harm, with tolerance and withdrawal |
| Substance misuse | Harmful or unintended use, such as on one occasion |
| Substance abuse | Repeated pattern of harmful use |
| Concordance | How often both twins share a characteristic |
| Withdrawal | Unpleasant physical or psychological symptoms when use stops |
| Tolerance | Needing more of a substance for the same effect |
| Negative triad | Beck's negative view of the self, the world and the future |
| Relapse | Return of symptoms after improvement |
| Stigma | Negative attitudes that mark people out as different or shameful |

## Depression

**ICD symptoms:** low mood, loss of interest, reduced energy, changed sleep, changed appetite,
decreased self-confidence.

| | Biological | Psychological |
|---|---|---|
| Explanation | Serotonin imbalance | Negative schemas, attributional style |
| Treatment | Antidepressants (SSRIs) | CBT |
| For | Drugs that raise serotonin help many | Leads to effective therapy |
| Against | Drugs take weeks; cause or effect? | Negative thinking may result from depression |

**How the explanations work:**

- **Biological**: low levels of the neurotransmitter **serotonin**, or an imbalance in it, are linked to low mood. This connects to synaptic transmission: if too little serotonin is active in the synapse, signals that regulate mood are weaker.
- **Psychological**: Beck's **negative schemas** mean a person sees themselves, the world and the future negatively (the **negative triad**) and filters events to fit. A depressive **attributional style** explains bad events as **internal** ("it's my fault"), **stable** ("it will always be like this") and **global** ("it ruins everything").

**How the treatments work:**

- **Antidepressants (SSRIs)**: block the **reuptake** of serotonin into the presynaptic neuron, so more stays active in the synapse. They usually take several weeks to have an effect.
- **CBT**: the therapist helps the client **identify** negative automatic thoughts, **challenge** them by testing them against evidence (for example, "Did everyone really ignore you?"), and **replace** them with more balanced thoughts. Homework, such as keeping a thought diary or planning enjoyable activities, practises the skills between sessions.

**Antidepressants:** easy, little effort; but side effects, slow, relapse when stopped.
**CBT:** tackles thinking, lasting skills; but needs motivation, costly, waiting lists.

**Comparing them in one sentence:** antidepressants treat a possible biological cause and need little effort from the patient, whereas CBT targets thinking, requires active effort, and may give skills that reduce the risk of relapse after treatment ends.

**Wiles (2013):** randomised trial; patients not helped by antidepressants given usual care or usual
care **plus CBT**; Beck Depression Inventory; CBT group improved more at six months, benefit still
present at twelve months. *For:* random allocation, large sample. *Against:* patients knew their
treatment; volunteers may be more motivated.

*Usefulness:* the study suggests CBT is worth adding for people whose depression has not responded to medication, which supports combining biological and psychological treatments.

## Addiction

**ICD dependence features:** strong desire; difficulty controlling use; withdrawal; tolerance;
neglect of other pleasures; persistent use despite harm.

| | Biological | Psychological |
|---|---|---|
| Explanation | Genetic vulnerability | Peer influence |
| Evidence | Kaij (1960): higher alcohol-abuse concordance in identical than non-identical Swedish male twins | Use often starts in friendship groups; imitation and fitting in |
| Against | Concordance not 100%; shared environment | Correlational; people choose similar friends |

**Explanations in more depth:**

- **Genetic vulnerability**: some people may inherit a greater **predisposition** to addiction, for example in how their brain responds to a substance. Twin studies compare **identical (MZ)** twins, who share all their genes, with **non-identical (DZ)** twins, who share about half; a higher MZ concordance suggests a genetic influence. Kaij used Swedish records to find male twin pairs where at least one twin was registered for alcohol abuse.
- **Peer influence**: people may start using a substance because friends do (imitation, as in social learning), to fit in with the group's norms (normative social influence), or because they overestimate how many peers use it.
- **Interactionist view**: a genetic vulnerability may only lead to addiction if the environment, such as friends who drink heavily, triggers it.

**Aversion therapy:** classical conditioning; substance paired with nausea-inducing drug.
Unpleasant, ethical concerns, may not last outside the clinic.

*How the conditioning works:* the drug (unconditioned stimulus) causes nausea (unconditioned response). Alcohol (neutral stimulus) is taken with the drug, so after repeated pairings alcohol alone becomes a **conditioned stimulus** that produces nausea (**conditioned response**). The person then avoids alcohol.

**Self-management:** self-help groups, twelve-step programmes. Free, supportive; but high drop-out,
hard to evaluate.

*How it works:* the person takes responsibility for change, often with group support (such as Alcoholics Anonymous, where members share experiences and may have a sponsor), and uses strategies such as setting goals, monitoring use, and avoiding the people and places that trigger cravings.

## Applying it to a scenario

*Scenario: Sam has felt low for months, has lost interest in football, sleeps badly and says, "I failed one test, so I'm useless at everything and always will be."*

- **Characteristics**: persistent low mood, loss of interest and changed sleep are ICD symptoms, and the long duration separates this from ordinary sadness.
- **Explanation**: "useless at everything and always will be" shows an **internal, stable, global** attributional style and a negative view of self and future.
- **Intervention**: CBT could help Sam test the belief against evidence (other tests he has passed) and replace it with a balanced thought.

Always quote or paraphrase the scenario detail you are using; a correct concept with no link to Sam earns only part of the marks.

## Model evaluation paragraph

*Topic: evaluate the genetic explanation of addiction.*

> Kaij found a higher concordance for alcohol abuse in identical than in non-identical male twins, which suggests that genes play a part, because identical twins share all their genes. However, concordance was not 100%, so genes cannot be the whole explanation. Twins usually grow up together, so a shared environment could also explain their similarity. The sample was also male and Swedish, so the findings may not generalise to women or other cultures. The evidence therefore suggests a genetic vulnerability that interacts with environmental factors such as peer influence.

## Exam traps

- Depression is not "extreme sadness": use ICD features.
- Bipolar includes mania.
- Kaij supports, but does not prove, a genetic cause.
- "Compare" needs a contrast in the same sentence.
- SSRIs block **reuptake**; they do not "add serotonin to the brain".
- Misuse can be a single occasion; abuse is a repeated pattern; dependence adds tolerance and withdrawal.
- In aversion therapy the substance is the **neutral** stimulus that becomes conditioned, not the unconditioned stimulus.
- Peer influence evidence is mostly **correlational**: it shows a link, not a cause.

## Self-test

1. Give two reasons the recorded incidence of mental health problems might rise.
2. How does bipolar depression differ from unipolar depression?
3. Name three ICD symptoms of depression.
4. What does an internal, stable, global attributional style mean?
5. What did Wiles' study compare?
6. What is tolerance?
7. What did Kaij find?
8. Which learning theory underlies aversion therapy?
9. How do SSRIs affect serotonin?
10. Describe two steps in CBT.
11. Give one reason Kaij's findings do not prove addiction is genetic.
12. Give one strength and one weakness of self-management programmes.

**Answers:** 1. Any two: increased recognition, less stigma, challenges of modern living. 2. Bipolar
includes periods of mania as well as depression. 3. Any three: low mood, loss of interest, reduced
energy, changed sleep, changed appetite, decreased self-confidence. 4. Blaming negative events on
oneself, expecting them to last and expecting them to affect everything. 5. Usual care alone versus
usual care plus CBT, for patients whose depression had not responded to antidepressants. 6. Needing
more of a substance to get the same effect. 7. Higher concordance for alcohol abuse in identical than
non-identical twins. 8. Classical conditioning. 9. They block reuptake, so more serotonin stays active in the synapse. 10. Any two: identifying negative automatic thoughts; challenging them against evidence; replacing them with balanced thoughts; homework such as a thought diary. 11. Concordance was not 100%, or twins share an environment. 12. Strength: free, supportive, available long term. Weakness: high drop-out, or hard to evaluate because members are anonymous and self-selected.
