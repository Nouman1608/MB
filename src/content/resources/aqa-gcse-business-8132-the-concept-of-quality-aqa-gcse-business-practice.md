These questions cover section 3.3.3 The concept of quality of AQA GCSE Business (8132). Revise first with the [Concept of quality study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-business-8132-the-concept-of-quality-aqa-gcse-business/) and the [Concept of quality revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-business-8132-the-concept-of-quality-aqa-gcse-business-revision-notes/). All businesses and figures are invented.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *total quality management (TQM)*. **[1]**

**2.** State **two** expectations that customers have of the quality of a service. **[2]**

**3.** Identify **two** ways a business can find out about quality problems. **[2]**

**4.** A factory produces 8,000 kettles in a month. 120 are found to be faulty. Calculate the defect rate. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** consequence for a business of poor-quality products. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** way in which maintaining high quality can allow a business to charge a higher price. **[3]**

### Section B: longer and case-study questions

**7.** Analyse **two** advantages to a manufacturing business of using TQM rather than relying on inspecting products at the end of production. **[6]**

**8.** Moorside Dental has 2,000 patient visits a month. In March it received 70 complaints. In April, after staff training, it received 30 complaints from 2,000 visits.

**(a)** Calculate the complaint rate in March and in April. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain **one** cost and **one** benefit to Moorside Dental of the staff training. **[4]**

**9.** Explain why quality problems may increase when a business grows through franchising. **[4]**

**10.** Bayview Blenders makes 15,000 blenders a year. 3% are returned faulty, and each return costs £18 to deal with. A new testing machine costing £5,000 a year would cut returns to 1%.

**(a)** Calculate the yearly cost of returns before and after the new machine. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the net yearly saving from the machine. **[1]**

**11.** Harbridge Toys outsources the manufacture of its toys to a factory overseas. Analyse the quality risks to Harbridge Toys of this decision. **[6]**

**12.** Copperleaf Coffee runs 12 cafés and is planning to grow by selling franchises. Its reputation is built on consistent, high-quality coffee and friendly service. Evaluate whether Copperleaf Coffee can maintain quality as it grows through franchising. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** An approach in which every employee in every department is responsible for quality at every stage, aiming to get things right first time [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Any two of: reliability, so the service happens when promised [1]; speed, with little waiting [1]; friendly and knowledgeable staff; accuracy; a clean, pleasant environment. **[2]**

**3.** Any two of: inspecting and testing products [1]; monitoring customer complaints and returns [1]; customer surveys and online reviews; mystery shoppers; reports from staff. **[2]**

**4.** 120 ÷ 8,000 [1] × 100 = 1.5% [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Customers who receive faulty products return them for refunds or replacements [1], which adds to the business's costs [1]. They may also post poor reviews and tell others, damaging the business's reputation, so future sales fall [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Consistently high quality builds a reputation for reliability [1]. Customers see less risk in buying from the business and value the product more [1], so they are willing to pay more than for rivals' products, allowing a higher price and a higher profit margin [1]. **[3]**

**7.** **First advantage:** under TQM, each worker checks quality at their own stage [1], so faults are found and fixed as they occur rather than after the product is finished [1], which means fewer faulty products are made and less material is wasted, reducing costs [1]. **Second advantage:** every employee is responsible for quality, so staff are involved in improving the process [1], which can raise motivation and lead to suggestions that improve quality further [1], making quality more consistent and building the business's reputation with customers [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** March: 70 ÷ 2,000 × 100 = 3.5% [1]. April: 30 ÷ 2,000 × 100 = 1.5% [1].
**(b)** **Cost:** training costs money and takes staff away from patients while it happens [1], so revenue may be lost on training days [1]. **Benefit:** fewer complaints mean more satisfied patients [1], who are more likely to stay with the practice and recommend it, increasing revenue [1]. **[6]**

**9.** Franchise outlets are run by separate owners rather than by the business itself [1], so the brand has less direct control over how each outlet operates [1]. Franchisees may cut corners to save costs, or train their staff less well [1], so customers receive different standards in different outlets, which can damage the reputation of the whole brand [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** Before: 3% of 15,000 = 450 returns × £18 = £8,100 [1]. After: 1% of 15,000 = 150 × £18 = £2,700 [1].
**(b)** £8,100 − £2,700 − £5,000 = £400 net saving a year [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Harbridge does not control the factory directly [1], so it cannot easily check that toys are made to its standards every day [1]. If the factory uses cheaper materials to cut its own costs, the toys may break easily or even be unsafe [1], which could force Harbridge to recall products, an expensive process [1]. Because the factory is overseas, problems may not be noticed until the toys reach the UK [1], by which time a whole shipment may be faulty, damaging Harbridge's reputation with parents and retailers [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **Risks:** each franchise will be run by a different owner [1], so Copperleaf will have less direct control over how coffee is made and how customers are treated [1]. Its reputation depends on consistency [1], so one poorly run franchise could damage the image of all its cafés [1]. **Ways to protect quality:** Copperleaf has already run 12 cafés, so it has proven methods it can write into a franchise manual [1]. It can train franchisees and their staff, supply the same coffee beans to every outlet [1], and use inspections and mystery shoppers to check standards [1]. **Judgement:** Copperleaf can maintain quality if it builds strong controls into its franchise agreements [1], but the risk rises as the number of outlets grows, so it should expand gradually and keep the right to end agreements with franchisees who do not meet its standards [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating quality as the same as luxury.
- Forgetting that services have quality expectations.
- Describing TQM as extra inspectors at the end.
- Forgetting to multiply by 100 when calculating a rate.
- Ignoring the specification's link between growth, outsourcing, franchising and quality.
- Ending an evaluation without saying what would make quality more or less likely to be maintained.
