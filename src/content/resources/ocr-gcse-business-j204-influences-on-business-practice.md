This set covers section 6, Influences on business, of OCR GCSE (9-1) Business (J204), Version 2.0
(January 2026), assessed in Business 2 (J204/02). All businesses and figures are invented. Section A
mirrors the paper's multiple-choice style; Section B uses short case studies. Revise first with the
[Influences on business study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-business-j204-influences-on-business/)

## Section A

**1.** Which of the following is an **environmental** consideration named in the specification? **[1]**

A: Sourcing of materials &nbsp; B: Waste disposal &nbsp; C: Treatment of suppliers &nbsp; D: Marketing decisions

**2.** Unemployment rises sharply across the country. Which business is most likely to see the
largest fall in demand? **[1]**

A: A discount supermarket &nbsp; B: A luxury cruise company &nbsp; C: A pharmacy &nbsp; D: A bus operator

**3.** Which statement best describes a multinational company? **[1]**

A: A business that exports some of its goods &nbsp; B: A business with more than 250 employees &nbsp;
C: A business that operates in more than one country &nbsp; D: A business owned by shareholders

**4.** State **two** ways in which a business could treat its workers ethically. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** Fenwold Strawberries grows fruit outdoors and sells it to local shops. Explain **one** way
climate change could affect the business. **[2]**

**6.** Thistle Knitwear sells 1,200 jumpers a year at £45 each. The wool for each jumper costs £12.
The owner is considering wool from a certified sustainable farm, costing £15 per jumper. Assume
cost of sales is the cost of wool only and the price and sales do not change.

**(a)** Calculate the gross profit before and after the change. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the gross profit margin after the change. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain **one** reason why Thistle Knitwear might still decide to switch. **[2]**

**7.** Consumer incomes in the region fall. Wrenfield Furniture's annual revenue falls from
£480,000 to £408,000.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage fall in revenue. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse **one** way Wrenfield Furniture could respond to the fall in revenue. **[3]**

**8.** Calloway Snacks is about to sell its crisps in six new countries. Explain **one** benefit and
**one** drawback of using exactly the same brand name and packaging in every country. **[4]**

**9.** Analyse how a rise in local unemployment could affect a hotel's recruitment of staff. **[3]**

**10.** Hollin Ceramics is a small pottery with ten staff. Explain **two** ways it could compete
with overseas producers. **[4]**

**11.** Discuss whether a fall in consumer incomes is always bad news for a business. **[6]**

**12.** Marrowby Toys buys wooden toys from a supplier whose factory has been criticised in the press
for unsafe working conditions. An alternative supplier with a good safety record charges 20% more.
Marrowby's net profit margin is currently 8%, and most of its customers are parents who shop in
its own high-street stores. Evaluate whether Marrowby Toys should change supplier. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B: Waste disposal** [1]. A, C and D are ethical considerations. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The two lists in 6.1 are easy to blur; learn which four points are
environmental.

**2.** **B: A luxury cruise company** [1], because a cruise is a non-essential that households cut
first. **[1]**

**3.** **C** [1]. Exporting alone does not make a business multinational; it must operate in more
than one country. **[1]**

**4.** Any two from: paying a **fair wage** above the minimum [1]; providing **safe working
conditions** or **reasonable hours** [1]. Also credit: training, fair treatment in promotion. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Paying the minimum wage" is a legal requirement, so it is weak as an
ethical example.

**5.** More frequent heavy rain, drought or heatwaves could **damage or reduce the crop** [1], so
Fenwold would have less fruit to sell to the shops and its revenue would fall [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Revenue = 1,200 × 45 = £54,000. Before: 54,000 − 14,400 = **£39,600** [1]. After:
54,000 − 18,000 = **£36,000** [1].
**(b)** 36,000 ÷ 54,000 × 100 [1] = **66.7%** [1].
**(c)** Customers may value **sustainably sourced wool** [1], so Thistle could use it in its
marketing and attract buyers willing to pay more, protecting its reputation [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the cost of sales (1,200 × 12 and 1,200 × 15) even when the question
does not ask for it, so that method marks are available.

**7. (a)** (480,000 − 408,000) ÷ 480,000 × 100 [1] = **15%** [1].
**(b)** Wrenfield could introduce a **cheaper range** of furniture [1], which appeals to customers
whose incomes have fallen [1], so it wins back some sales and revenue, although the margin on each
item may be lower [1]. **[5]**

**8.** Benefit: one design and one campaign mean **lower marketing and packaging costs** [1], and
the brand becomes recognisable to travellers and online buyers in every country [1]. Drawback: the
name or packaging may **not suit local tastes or language** [1], so the crisps may sell less well
than a locally adapted product [1]. **[4]**

**9.** More people are looking for work, so the hotel receives **more applicants for each job** [1].
It can choose more experienced staff [1], and there is less pressure to raise pay to attract them,
which helps to control costs [1]. **[3]**

**10.** It could compete on **quality and craftsmanship** [1], for example hand-made pieces that mass
producers do not offer, which supports a higher price [1]. It could **sell online** to customers
abroad [1], reaching buyers who want a distinctive product without needing shops overseas [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Bad news: customers spend less on non-essentials, so many businesses lose sales [1]; fixed
costs stay the same, so profit falls faster than revenue [1]; the business may need to cut prices,
reducing margins [1].
Not always: budget sellers may gain as customers **trade down** [1]; a business may find it easier
and cheaper to recruit [1].
Judgement: it depends on what the business sells, so a fall in incomes is bad news for sellers of
luxuries but can help sellers of budget products [1]. **[6]**

**12.** For changing: unsafe conditions are an **ethical** issue in the treatment of workers [1];
parents are likely to care about how toys are made, so press criticism could damage Marrowby's
**reputation** and sales in its stores [1] [1]; a supplier with a good record is also less likely to
suffer disruption [1].
Against changing: a 20% rise in supply costs is large when the net profit margin is only **8%** [1],
so profit could disappear unless prices rise [1]; higher prices could lose price-sensitive
customers [1].
Judgement: Marrowby should change supplier, because its customers are parents and its brand depends
on trust [1], but it should raise prices on some lines or look for savings elsewhere to protect its
thin margin [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Use both numbers in the case (8% and 20%) and the type of customer. A judgement
that only says "ethics are important" is not supported.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing the ethical and environmental lists.
- Calling legal requirements ethical choices.
- Calculating a percentage change from the new figure instead of the original.
- Explaining a downturn's effect on sales but not on the business as an employer.
- Evaluating without using the figures in the case.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will I be asked to calculate in section 6 questions?**
Possibly, because the specification's quantitative skills apply across the paper: revenue,
percentages and profit margins are often used to measure the impact of an influence.

**How long should a 9-mark answer be?**
Usually four or five short paragraphs: arguments for, arguments against, then a judgement with a
reason.
