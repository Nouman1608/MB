These questions cover section **4.6.2 Reversible reactions and dynamic equilibrium** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry
(8462) specification, for teaching from September 2016. The content is assessed on Paper 2. Questions 6 to 10
test the effect of changing conditions on equilibrium, which the specification marks (HT only), and are
labelled **Higher tier only**.

Learn the content first in the
[reversible reactions and dynamic equilibrium study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-reversible-reactions-and-dynamic-equilibrium/)

## Questions

**1.** Blue hydrated copper(II) sulfate crystals are heated in a test tube. They turn white and droplets of water collect near the top of the tube. Adding water to the white solid turns it blue again.

**(a)** Write a word equation, using the reversible reaction symbol, for the reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe what the observations show about the reaction. **[1]**
**(c)** State what happens to the temperature when water is added to the white solid and explain why. **[2]**

**2.** Ammonium chloride is heated in a test tube. It decomposes into ammonia and hydrogen chloride. At the cool top of the tube, a white solid forms again.

**(a)** Write the equation for this reversible reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** The decomposition is endothermic. State whether the re-forming of ammonium chloride is exothermic or endothermic and compare the energy transferred in the two directions. **[2]**

**3.** A reversible reaction A + B ⇌ C + D is carried out in a sealed container.

**(a)** Explain what is meant by dynamic equilibrium. **[3]**
**(b)** A student says, "At equilibrium, the concentrations of A, B, C and D must all be equal." Is the student correct? Explain. **[2]**

**4.** Limestone is heated: CaCO₃ ⇌ CaO + CO₂.

**(a)** Explain why this reaction goes to completion in an open crucible. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what would happen if the same reaction were carried out in a sealed, strong container. **[2]**

**5.** A student sketches a graph of the rates of the forward and reverse reactions against time for a reversible reaction that starts with only reactants.

Describe how the two rates change and where equilibrium is shown. **[4]**

**6.** (Higher tier only) The reaction N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g) is exothermic in the forward direction. A mixture is at equilibrium.

**(a)** State the effect on the amount of ammonia of increasing the pressure and explain your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** State the effect on the amount of ammonia of increasing the temperature. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest why a high temperature is nevertheless used in industry. **[1]**

**7.** (Higher tier only) In the equilibrium 2NO₂(g) ⇌ N₂O₄(g), NO₂ is brown and N₂O₄ is colourless. A sealed syringe of the equilibrium mixture is pulled out to double its volume.

**(a)** State the effect on the pressure. **[1]**
**(b)** Predict how the position of equilibrium and the colour change after the system settles. Explain. **[3]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) The equilibrium H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g) is at equilibrium. Explain why changing the pressure has no effect on the position of equilibrium. **[2]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) In the equilibrium Fe³⁺(aq) + SCN⁻(aq) ⇌ FeSCN²⁺(aq), the right-hand ion is deep red and the others are pale. More Fe³⁺ is added.

**(a)** Predict the change in the position of equilibrium and in the colour. **[2]**
**(b)** The forward reaction is exothermic. State the change in colour when the mixture is cooled, and explain. **[2]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) A table shows the percentage of product at equilibrium for a gaseous reaction at different temperatures and pressures.

| Temperature / °C | Pressure / atm | Product at equilibrium / % |
|---|---|---|
| 300 | 100 | 60 |
| 300 | 200 | 72 |
| 500 | 100 | 25 |
| 500 | 200 | 38 |

**(a)** Deduce whether the forward reaction is exothermic or endothermic. **[2]**
**(b)** Deduce whether the products or reactants have fewer gas molecules. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** hydrated copper(II) sulfate ⇌ anhydrous copper(II) sulfate + water [reactants/products 1; reversible symbol 1] **[2]**
**(b)** The reaction can go **both ways** / is reversible [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The temperature **rises** [1], because the reverse reaction is **exothermic** (the forward reaction is endothermic) [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** NH₄Cl ⇌ NH₃ + HCl [formulae 1; reversible symbol 1] **[2]**
**(b)** **Exothermic** [1]; the **same amount of energy** is transferred in each direction [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** The **forward and reverse reactions** occur at the **same rate** [1]; so the **concentrations of reactants and products stay constant** [1]; both reactions are **still occurring**, in a closed system [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** No [1]. The concentrations are **constant**, not necessarily equal; they depend on the position of equilibrium [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** The **carbon dioxide escapes** [1], so the reverse reaction cannot take place and the reaction goes to completion [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The carbon dioxide cannot escape (a **closed system**) [1]; the reverse reaction occurs and an **equilibrium** is reached [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Forward rate starts **high** and **falls** [1]; reverse rate starts at **zero** and **rises** [1]; the two lines **meet** and become **level** [1]; equilibrium is where the rates are **equal** [1]. **[4]**

**6. (a)** **More ammonia** [1], because the equilibrium shifts to the side with **fewer gas molecules** (2 compared with 4) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** **Less** ammonia (the reverse, endothermic, reaction is favoured) [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** A higher temperature gives a **faster rate**, so equilibrium is reached more quickly [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** The pressure **decreases** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** The position shifts towards the side with **more gas molecules** (to NO₂) [1]; the mixture becomes **browner** [1] to oppose the fall in pressure (2 molecules on the left, 1 on the right) [1]. **[3]**

**8.** There are **2 gas molecules on each side** (1 + 1 = 2 on the left, 2 on the right) [1], so a change in pressure affects both sides equally and does not shift the position [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** The position shifts to the **right** [1] and the colour becomes **deeper red** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The colour becomes **deeper red** [1], because cooling favours the **exothermic** forward direction, so more FeSCN²⁺ forms [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** Increasing the temperature **reduces** the percentage of product (60 to 25 and 72 to 38) [1], so the forward reaction is **exothermic** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Increasing the pressure **increases** the percentage of product [1], so the **products** have fewer gas molecules than the reactants [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the reaction "stops" at equilibrium.
- Saying the concentrations are equal rather than constant.
- Predicting the temperature effect without checking whether the forward reaction is exothermic.
- Counting solids or liquids when working out the effect of pressure.
- Forgetting to say the system must be closed.
