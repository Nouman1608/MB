## Questions

**1.** A furniture workshop buys hardwood timber, employs carpenters and uses saws and a lathe. The owner set up the business and carries the risk of failure. State the factor of production shown by each of these: the timber, the carpenters, the saws and lathe, and the owner. **[4]**

**2.** A clothing factory has 15 workers making 3,000 shirts a week. It hires 5 more workers and output rises to 3,800 shirts.

**(a)** Define productivity. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate output per worker before and after the new workers were hired. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage change in productivity and suggest one reason for it. **[3]**

**3.** A farm of 25 hectares produces 100 tonnes of maize. After irrigation is installed, the same land produces 125 tonnes.

**(a)** Calculate the yield per hectare before and after irrigation. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage rise in yield. **[2]**
**(c)** The farmer then reclaims 5 hectares of marshland, which produce 10 tonnes. Calculate the average yield on the whole farm and explain why it is lower than after irrigation. **[3]**

**4.** A call centre trains 12 workers. Before the training each worker handled 40 calls a day. Afterwards each handles 46.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in productivity. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the extra calls handled each day by the 12 workers. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how education and training can raise the productivity of labour. **[3]**

**5.** A small workshop has 5 workers making 500 bicycle wheels a week. After it buys a wheel-truing machine, it makes 650 a week with the same workers.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in output per worker. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one way in which capital can raise labour productivity. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one drawback to the workshop of buying the machine. **[2]**

**6.** In a town, 60 workers each produce USD 50 of output a day. 15 migrants then arrive and start work, each producing USD 40 a day at first.

**(a)** Calculate the average output per worker after the migrants arrive. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why total output rises while average productivity falls. **[2]**
**(c)** After training, each migrant produces USD 60 a day. Calculate the new average output per worker. **[2]**

**7.** Classify each activity as primary, secondary or tertiary: **(a)** a salt mine, **(b)** a firm turning steel sheets into cooking pots, **(c)** a driving school, **(d)** a business growing tea. **[4]**

**8.** In Country D, total employment rises from 8 million in Year 1 to 10 million in Year 2. The share working in the primary sector falls from 55% to 30%.

**(a)** Calculate the number employed in the primary sector in each year. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in the number of primary jobs. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain two reasons why the primary sector's share of employment tends to fall as a developing economy grows. **[4]**

**9.** In Country A, total employment rises from 12 million to 15 million. Manufacturing's share of employment falls from 35% to 15%, while an index of manufacturing output rises from 100 to 150.

**(a)** Calculate the number employed in manufacturing in each year. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage rise in manufacturing output per worker. **[3]**
**(c)** Evaluate whether the fall in manufacturing's share of employment shows that manufacturing in Country A is in decline. **[5]**

**10.** A jam firm has 6 workers. Each makes whole jars from start to finish, producing 24 jars a day. The firm then divides the work into separate tasks and daily output rises to 216 jars. Each worker is paid USD 108 a day.

**(a)** Define division of labour. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the labour cost per jar before and after the change. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate whether the firm should keep the division of labour. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** Timber is land [1]. Carpenters are labour [1]. Saws and lathe are capital [1]. The owner is enterprise [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that name a factor and link it to the item given, such as "the owner, because they take the risk", leave no doubt. Money is not capital in this unit.

**2. (a)** Output per unit of input [1] in a given time, for example per worker [1]. **(b)** Before: 3,000 / 15 = 200 shirts [1]. After: 3,800 / 20 = 190 shirts [1]. **(c)** (190 - 200) / 200 x 100 [1] = -5% [1]. A reason: the extra workers share the same machines and space, so each adds less [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Output rose 26.7%, which tempts some answers to say productivity rose. Dividing by the number of workers before judging is what separates productivity from production.

**3. (a)** Before: 100 / 25 = 4 tonnes per hectare [1]. After: 125 / 25 = 5 tonnes per hectare [1]. **(b)** (5 - 4) / 4 x 100 [1] = 25% [1]. **(c)** Total output 125 + 10 = 135 tonnes on 30 hectares: 135 / 30 = 4.5 tonnes [1]. Lower because the reclaimed land yields only 2 tonnes per hectare [1], which pulls the average below the irrigated 5 [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Explanations that compare the yield of the new land (2) with the existing yield (5) are stronger than saying only that reclaimed land is "worse".

**4. (a)** (46 - 40) / 40 x 100 [1] = 15% [1]. **(b)** 12 x 6 = 72 extra calls [1]. **(c)** Training gives workers more skills or knowledge, which is human capital [1]. Skilled workers make fewer errors and work faster [1], so output per worker rises [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A chain of reasoning, from training to skills to speed or accuracy to output per worker, is more convincing than "training makes workers better".

**5. (a)** Before: 500 / 5 = 100; after: 650 / 5 = 130 [1]. (130 - 100) / 100 x 100 = 30% [1]. **(b)** The machine does the task faster or more accurately [1], so each worker makes more units every hour [1]. **(c)** The machine costs money to buy and maintain [1], so unit costs may not fall unless output is high enough to cover it [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A drawback needs a consequence for this workshop, not just a name. Explain what the cost or the training need does to profit or to workers.

**6. (a)** Original total 60 x 50 = USD 3,000 [1]. New total 3,000 + 15 x 40 = USD 3,600 [1]. Average 3,600 / 75 = **USD 48** [1]. **(b)** Total output rises because there are more workers [1]. The average falls because the new workers produce less than the existing USD 50 average [1]. **(c)** 3,000 + 15 x 60 = USD 3,900 [1]. 3,900 / 75 = **USD 52** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* State both the total and the average, since the question is about the difference between them. Quote units in the answer.

**7.** (a) Primary [1]. (b) Secondary [1]. (c) Tertiary [1]. (d) Primary [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Classify by what the activity does. Growing tea is primary even though the product is later sold; processing the leaves into packets would be secondary.

**8. (a)** Year 1: 55% of 8 million = 4.4 million [1]. Year 2: 30% of 10 million = 3.0 million [1]. **(b)** (3.0 - 4.4) / 4.4 x 100 [1] = -31.8% [1]. **(c)** Reason 1: machinery and better farming methods raise productivity [1], so fewer workers can grow the food needed [1]. Reason 2: as incomes rise, people spend more on manufactured goods and services [1], and factories and offices draw workers away from farms [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* The share fell 25 percentage points, but the number of jobs fell 31.8%. Use the right unit for what is asked, and give a reason with a link, not a bare statement.

**9. (a)** Year 1: 35% of 12 million = 4.2 million [1]. Year 2: 15% of 15 million = 2.25 million [1]. **(b)** Output per worker: 100 / 4.2 = 23.8 and 150 / 2.25 = 66.7 [1]. Ratio 66.7 / 23.8 = 2.8 [1], so a rise of **180%** [1]. **(c)** Output rose 50% while jobs fell from 4.2 to 2.25 million, so the sector is producing more, not less [1]. Productivity has risen, probably through capital or technology [1]. Total employment rose by 3 million, so displaced workers may find jobs in other sectors [1]. However, the fall in jobs (46.4%) may hurt workers who cannot retrain or live far from new jobs [1]. Judgement: the data show a more productive sector, not a declining one, though the cost to affected workers depends on how easily they move [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation scores best when the data are used in the argument and the final judgement answers the exact question: share of jobs against the sector's output.

**10. (a)** Splitting production into separate tasks [1], each done by a different worker or group [1]. **(b)** Wage bill 6 x 108 = USD 648. Before: 648 / (6 x 24 = 144 jars) = USD 4.50 [1]. After: 648 / 216 = USD 3.00 [1]. **(c)** Output rises 50% and labour cost per jar falls 33.3% [1]. Workers repeat one task, so skill grows and switching time falls [1]. Workers may find it boring and narrow, so motivation falls [1]. The firm may face absence and staff turnover, and the line stops if one worker is missing [1]. It works best for a standard product with a market large enough to sell the extra jars [1]. Job rotation could reduce the boredom [1]. Judgement: keep it if the lower cost outweighs the extra turnover costs, which seems likely on these figures [1]. **[11]**
*Examiner insight:* The data give a clear case for keeping it; the stronger answers still weigh the human costs and end with a decision reached from both sides.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by the wrong year's inputs, or forgetting to divide at all, when finding productivity.
- Quoting the 25 percentage point fall in a share when the number of jobs was asked for.
- Describing a result without saying what it shows: "it fell 5%" with no conclusion about productivity.
- Giving a factor without a link to output, such as naming irrigation but not yield.
- Calling money capital.
- Writing only advantages of division of labour, or no judgement.
- Mixing total output and average output when migration is the topic.
- Leaving out units: tonnes, shirts, dollars.
