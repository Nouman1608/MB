These questions cover section 3.15, Partnership accounts, of the AQA A-level Accounting (7127) specification (version 1.0, 22 December 2016, A-level exams June 2019 onwards). The qualification is not tiered and section 3.15 is assessed on Paper 1. Use a calculator throughout. All businesses and figures are invented.

## Questions

**1.** State **three** terms of the Partnership Act 1890 that apply when partners have no agreement. **[3]**

**2.** The year ends 31 December. Interest on drawings is 8% a year from the date of each drawing. In 2025 partner Harker drew £6,000 on 1 March, £4,000 on 1 July and £3,000 on 1 October. Calculate the interest on drawings. **[3]**

**3.** Patel and Quinn's profit for the year ended 30 June 2026 was £22,000. Their agreement gives interest on capital at 6% a year (capitals: Patel £50,000, Quinn £30,000), a salary to Quinn of £20,000 and residual profits or losses shared Patel 3 : Quinn 2. Interest on drawings was Patel £500 and Quinn £300. Prepare the appropriation account. **[6]**

**4.** West and York keep fixed capital accounts. For the year ended 31 March 2026:

| | West £ | York £ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Current account at 1 April 2025 | 1,500 credit | 900 debit |
| Interest on capital | 2,400 | 1,600 |
| Salary | -- | 12,000 |
| Share of residual profit | 18,600 | 12,400 |
| Interest on drawings | 420 | 380 |
| Drawings | 21,000 | 26,500 |

Prepare the partners' current accounts in columnar form. **[6]**

**5.** Abel (capital £80,000) and Bryce (capital £20,000) have no partnership agreement. Abel wants interest on capital at 10% a year. Bryce, who works full time in the business, wants a salary of £12,000. Profit for the year is £36,000.

**(a)** Calculate the amount credited to each partner, explaining your answer. **[3]**
**(b)** Give **one** reason why the partners should draw up an agreement. **[1]**

**6.** Dunn and Ellis share profits equally. Ford is to be admitted on 1 January 2026. Before admission, land is revalued upward by £10,000, equipment downward by £2,500 and inventory downward by £700. Prepare the revaluation account. **[4]**

**7.** Continuing question 6: goodwill is valued at £30,000 and will not be kept in the books. The new ratio is Dunn 2 : Ellis 1 : Ford 1. Ford pays £40,000 into the bank as capital.

**(a)** Calculate the goodwill entries in each capital account and Ford's capital after admission. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain the net effect of the goodwill entries on each partner. **[2]**

**8.** Ormond and Price's profit before loan interest for the year ended 31 December 2025 was £71,300. Price lent the firm £20,000 on 1 January 2025; the agreement allows interest on it at 4% a year. The agreement also provides:

- interest on fixed capitals at 5% a year (Ormond £80,000, Price £40,000)
- a salary for Price of £15,000
- interest on drawings: Ormond £900, Price £600
- residual profit shared Ormond 3 : Price 2.

Current accounts at 1 January 2025: Ormond £3,500 credit, Price £1,200 credit. Drawings: Ormond £32,000, Price £34,000.

**(a)** Calculate the profit for the year, explaining the treatment of the loan interest. **[2]**
**(b)** Prepare the appropriation account for the year ended 31 December 2025. **[6]**
**(c)** Calculate each partner's current account balance at 31 December 2025 and prepare the equity section of the statement of financial position. **[4]**

**9.** Taylor, Upton and Vale share profits 3 : 2 : 1. Vale retires on 30 September 2026, when capitals were Taylor £50,000, Upton £40,000 and Vale £20,000, and Vale's current account had a **debit** balance of £1,800. On retirement:

- premises are revalued upward by £18,000 and vehicles downward by £3,000
- the provision for doubtful debts is increased by £1,200
- goodwill is valued at £36,000 and will not be kept; Taylor and Upton continue to share 3 : 2
- Vale is paid £8,000 by bank transfer and the rest is left as a loan to the firm.

**(a)** Prepare the revaluation account. **[3]**
**(b)** Prepare the partners' capital accounts. **[5]**
**(c)** State the amount of Vale's loan and where it appears in the statement of financial position. **[2]**

**10.** Using the current accounts from question 4, comment on the partners' balances at 31 March 2026 and evaluate whether West and York should agree a limit on drawings. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three: profits and losses shared equally [1]; no interest on capital [1]; no partnership salaries [1]; also accept: no interest charged on drawings; interest allowed on a partner's loan to the firm. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Repeating one term in different words gains no second mark.

**2.** 6,000 x 8% x 10/12 = £400 and 4,000 x 8% x 6/12 = £160 [1]; 3,000 x 8% x 3/12 = £60 [1]; total **£620** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Months run from the drawing date to the year end; a full year on each drawing loses the method marks.

**3.**

```
Appropriation account for the year ended 30 June 2026
                                        £        £
Profit for the year                           22,000
Add Interest on drawings: Patel        500
                          Quinn        300       800
                                              22,800
Less Interest on capital: Patel      3,000
                          Quinn      1,800    (4,800)
     Salary: Quinn                           (20,000)
Residual loss                                 (2,000)
Share of loss: Patel (3/5)          (1,200)
               Quinn (2/5)            (800)   (2,000)
```

Interest on drawings added [1]; interest on capital [1]; salary [1]; residual **loss £2,000** [1]; shares **£1,200** and **£800** as losses [1]; layout and subtotals [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Interest and salary are credited in full even when profit is too small; scaling the salary down loses most of the marks.

**4.**

```
Current accounts
                     West    York                       West    York
                        £       £                          £       £
Balance b/d             -     900  Balance b/d         1,500       -
Interest on drawings  420     380  Interest on capital 2,400   1,600
Drawings           21,000  26,500  Salary                  -  12,000
Balance c/d         1,080       -  Share of profit    18,600  12,400
                                   Balance c/d             -   1,780
                   22,500  27,780                     22,500  27,780
Balance b/d             -   1,780  Balance b/d         1,080       -
```

Opening balances [1]; interest on capital and salary [1]; profit shares [1]; interest on drawings and drawings [1]; West **£1,080 credit** [1]; York **£1,780 debit** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* York's opening debit balance belongs on the debit side; on the credit side it changes the closing balance, leaving only own-figure marks.

**5. (a)** With no agreement the Partnership Act 1890 applies [1]; neither interest on capital nor a salary is allowed [1]; profit is shared equally, **£18,000** each [1].
**(b)** An agreement lets rewards reflect each partner's capital and work; under the Act Bryce's full-time work earns nothing extra [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Calculating the claimed interest or salary scores nothing; the question tests the Act's defaults.

**6.**

```
Revaluation account
                        £                          £
Equipment           2,500    Land             10,000
Inventory             700
Capital: Dunn       3,400
Capital: Ellis      3,400
                   10,000                     10,000
```

Decreases debited [1]; increase credited [1]; profit **£6,800** [1]; **£3,400** each to capital [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Sharing with Ford, who was not a partner when values changed, loses the final mark.

**7. (a)** Created 1 : 1: Dunn £15,000 and Ellis £15,000 credited [1]; written off 2 : 1 : 1: Dunn £15,000, Ellis £7,500 and Ford £7,500 debited [1]; Ford's capital 40,000 - 7,500 = **£32,500** [1].
**(b)** Dunn's net effect is nil, because his share stays at one half [1]; Ellis gains £7,500 and Ford loses £7,500, so Ford in effect pays Ellis for the profit share Ellis gives up (one half down to one quarter) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each gain or loss to the change in profit share; net figures without the reason earn only part of the credit.

**8. (a)** Loan interest 20,000 x 4% = £800 [1]; it is an expense in the income statement because the loan is a debt of the firm, so profit for the year is 71,300 - 800 = **£70,500** [1].
**(b)**

```
Appropriation account for the year ended 31 December 2025
                                         £        £
Profit for the year                            70,500
Add Interest on drawings: Ormond        900
                          Price         600     1,500
                                               72,000
Less Interest on capital: Ormond      4,000
                          Price       2,000    (6,000)
     Salary: Price                            (15,000)
Residual profit                                51,000
Share of profit: Ormond (3/5)        30,600
                 Price (2/5)         20,400    51,000
```

Interest on drawings added [1]; interest on capital [1]; salary [1]; residual profit **£51,000** [1]; shares **£30,600** and **£20,400** [1]; layout [1].
**(c)** Ormond: 3,500 + 4,000 + 30,600 - 900 - 32,000 = **£5,200 credit** [1]. Price: 1,200 + 2,000 + 15,000 + 20,400 - 600 - 34,000 = **£4,000 credit** [1].

```
                      Ormond     Price      Total
                           £         £          £
Capital accounts      80,000    40,000    120,000
Current accounts       5,200     4,000      9,200
                                          129,200
```

Equity total **£129,200** [1]; Price's loan of £20,000 shown under non-current liabilities, not equity [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Loan interest placed in the appropriation account loses the treatment mark in (a) and the residual mark; own-figure marks can follow.

**9. (a)**

```
Revaluation account
                                   £                       £
Vehicles                       3,000    Premises      18,000
Provision for doubtful debts   1,200
Capital: Taylor                6,900
Capital: Upton                 4,600
Capital: Vale                  2,300
                              18,000                  18,000
```

Decreases debited [1]; increase credited [1]; profit **£13,800** shared 3 : 2 : 1 [1].
**(b)**

```
Capital accounts
                 Taylor    Upton     Vale                 Taylor    Upton     Vale
                      £        £        £                      £        £        £
Current a/c           -        -    1,800  Balance b/d   50,000   40,000   20,000
Goodwill         21,600   14,400        -  Revaluation    6,900    4,600    2,300
Bank                  -        -    8,000  Goodwill      18,000   12,000    6,000
Loan - Vale           -        -   18,500
Balance c/d      53,300   42,200        -
                 74,900   56,600   28,300                 74,900   56,600   28,300
```

Vale's current account debited [1]; revaluation shares [1]; goodwill created 3 : 2 : 1 [1]; goodwill written off 3 : 2 [1]; Vale settled by £8,000 bank and £18,500 loan, Taylor **£53,300**, Upton **£42,200** [1].
**(c)** **£18,500** [1]; a liability, under non-current liabilities unless repayable within 12 months [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Adding Vale's debit balance gives £30,100 due; only own-figure marks then follow.

**10.** York's £1,780 debit balance shows he drew more than his opening balance plus everything credited to him, so he in effect owes the firm [1]. West's balance fell from £1,500 to £1,080: he also drew nearly all his share [1]. This drains cash needed to pay suppliers or fund growth [1]. Against a limit: partners may rely on drawings as income, and interest on drawings already discourages early withdrawals [1]. A limit could match each partner's expected total share [1]. Judgement: a limit is sensible, mainly for York, as repeated debit balances reduce the firm's resources [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the figures; general points about drawings limit the marks, and "evaluate" needs a supported conclusion.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating interest on a partner's loan as an appropriation rather than an expense.
- Subtracting interest on drawings in the appropriation account.
- Ignoring the months in interest on drawings.
- Applying salaries or interest on capital where the Partnership Act 1890 applies.
- Sharing revaluation profit or goodwill created in the new ratio, or including the incoming partner.
- Adding a retiring partner's debit current account balance to the amount due.
