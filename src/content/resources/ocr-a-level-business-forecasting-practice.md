## Questions

**1.** Explain the nature and purpose of forecasting. **[4]**

**2.** Distinguish between qualitative forecasting and quantitative forecasting. **[3]**

**3.** Describe the Delphi technique. **[3]**

**4.** Describe **one** unstructured method of qualitative forecasting. **[2]**

**5.** Gawthorpe Printers expects to sell 5,000 leaflet packs next year at £3.20 each. Variable cost is £1.90 a pack and fixed costs are £4,200.

**(a)** Calculate the forecast profit. **[3]**
**(b)** Sales volume is then forecast to rise by 10%, with price and costs unchanged. Calculate the new forecast profit. **[2]**

**6.** Tanfield Toys recorded annual sales (£000): 2018: 67, 2019: 73, 2020: 80, 2021: 77, 2022: 84, 2023: 91, 2024: 87. Earlier years were 64 (2016) and 70 (2017). The three-year moving average trend values for 2017 to 2023 are 67.0, 70.0, 73.3, 76.7, 80.3, 84.0 and 87.3.

**(a)** Calculate the three-year moving average for 2021 and check it against the value given. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the cyclical variation for 2020. **[2]**
**(c)** Extrapolate the trend to forecast the trend value for 2025. **[2]**

**7.** Lindeth Bakery forecasts the following for January to March (£). Opening cash in January is 3,000.

| | January | February | March |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash in | 14,000 | 15,500 | 13,000 |
| Cash out | 12,800 | 16,900 | 12,400 |

**(a)** Calculate the forecast closing balance for each month. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain **one** reason why this forecast may prove inaccurate. **[2]**

**8.** Rushbeck Garden Centre plotted weekly sales (£000) against average weekly temperature (°C) for 20 weeks. The correlation is +0.88 and the line of best fit is sales = 2.1 + 0.45 × temperature.

**(a)** Interpret the correlation. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the line to forecast sales in a week averaging 18°C. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the correlation does not prove that warm weather causes higher sales. **[2]**

**9.** Tanfield Toys has strong sales each December and its annual sales also follow a pattern of rises and falls over several years. Analyse how seasonal and cyclical variations affect its forecasting. **[6]**

**10.** Gawthorpe Printers plans to launch recycled-paper packaging, a product with no sales history. The owner will rely on intuition. Evaluate whether the owner should use structured qualitative methods instead. **[8]**

**11.** Rushbeck Garden Centre's three-year moving average trend (£000) was 140 (2019), 146, 153, 158 and 165 (2023). The owner wants to build a new greenhouse now, based on this trend.

**(a)** Calculate the average annual change in the trend and extrapolate the trend value for 2025. **[2]**
**(b)** Evaluate the usefulness of time series analysis to Rushbeck in making this decision. **[4]**
**(c)** Recommend and justify how Rushbeck should respond to the forecast, commenting on the importance of accurate forecasting. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** A forecast is an informed estimate of a future variable such as sales, costs or cash flow [1], built from past data or judgement [1]. Its purpose is to reduce uncertainty [1] so the business can plan, for example staffing, stock or finance [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* An explanation usually earns more when it gives a reason for each point (the purpose) rather than only a definition.

**2.** Qualitative forecasting uses opinion, judgement and experience [1]. Quantitative forecasting uses numerical data and calculation [1]. The distinction is the basis: judgement versus past figures, with a clear contrast between the two [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Distinguish" needs both items and a difference; describing each separately without contrast can leave the last point unearned.

**3.** A panel of experts gives forecasts separately, often anonymously [1]. A coordinator circulates a summary of the answers [1]. Experts revise their views over several rounds until opinion converges on a consensus forecast [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the stages (separate answers, feedback, repeated rounds) is what turns a vague description into a full one.

**4.** Brainstorming: a group produces as many ideas or predictions as possible without early criticism [1] and then reviews them to reach a forecast [1]. (Intuition would also be acceptable: a manager relies on gut feeling built from experience, with no formal process or data.) **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Choose one method and stay with it; mixing two methods in one answer wastes time.

**5. (a)** Revenue = 5,000 × 3.20 = £16,000 [1]. Variable costs = 5,000 × 1.90 = £9,500 [1]. Profit = 16,000 − 9,500 − 4,200 = **£2,300** [1].
**(b)** Units 5,500: revenue £17,600 and variable costs £10,450 [1]. Profit = 17,600 − 10,450 − 4,200 = **£2,950** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each stage of the calculation, because a slip in one line is easier to credit if the working is visible.

**6. (a)** (80 + 77 + 84) ÷ 3 [1] = 80.3, which agrees with the value given (about £80,300) [1].
**(b)** Cyclical variation = actual − trend [1] = 80 − 76.7 = **+3.3** (£3,300 above trend) [1].
**(c)** Average annual change in trend = (87.3 − 67.0) ÷ 6 ≈ 3.4 [1]. Trend 2025 = 87.3 + 2 × 3.4 ≈ **94.1** (about £94,100) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Stating the direction (above or below trend) and the unit makes a cyclical figure meaningful and avoids sign confusion.

**7. (a)** January: 3,000 + 14,000 − 12,800 = **4,200** [1]. February: 4,200 + 15,500 − 16,900 = **2,800** [1]. March: 2,800 + 13,000 − 12,400 = **3,400** [1].
**(b)** Customers may pay later than assumed, so cash in falls [1], leaving less cash than forecast and possibly a shortfall because costs are paid on time (or a rise in flour prices raises cash out) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A reason earns more when it is tied to the business and followed by its effect on the figures.

**8. (a)** A strong [1] positive correlation: weeks with higher temperatures tend to have higher sales [1].
**(b)** 2.1 + 0.45 × 18 [1] = **10.2**, so about £10,200 [1].
**(c)** Correlation shows association only [1]. Another factor, such as school holidays or a promotion, may raise both temperature-linked footfall and sales [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming a specific alternative cause gives the "not causation" point substance.

**9.** Seasonal variation is a regular pattern within a year, such as the December peak [1]. Cyclical variation is a longer pattern over several years linked to the economic cycle [1]. If Tanfield divided an annual forecast evenly by month, it would underestimate December and overestimate quiet months [1], causing stock shortages and idle staff [1]. If it extrapolated a rising phase of the cycle, it would overestimate future sales [1], because toy spending may fall in a downturn [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Analysis usually earns credit by linking each variation to a consequence for the forecast, not only by defining it.

**10.** For: structured methods such as Delphi use several experts, so they reduce one person's bias [1]. There is no history, so a documented process gives evidence to lenders [1]. A wrong launch could be costly, so spending on better forecasts may be justified [1]. Against: structured methods take time and cost money, which matters for a small firm [1]. Experts may share the same blind spots about a new product [1]. Intuition is quick and the owner knows the local print market [1]. Judgement: the owner should use a structured method, such as expert opinion from a packaging specialist, alongside intuition, because the launch is novel and the cost of error is high [1], provided the cost of the panel is small compared with the investment [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation questions tend to reward a reasoned conclusion that refers to the business's circumstances, not a list of pros and cons alone.

**11. (a)** Average change = (165 − 140) ÷ 4 = 6.25 per year [1]. 2025 trend = 165 + 2 × 6.25 = **177.5**, about £177,500 [1].
**(b)** For: it is objective and based on actual sales [1], and it shows a steady upward trend that supports expansion [1]. Against: it assumes the past continues and cannot predict shocks such as a recession or a competitor [1], and it gives no trend value for the latest years, so the forecast relies on extrapolation [1].
**(c)** Recommend phasing the greenhouse, building a smaller unit first [1], because the trend may not continue and the project is expensive to reverse [1]. Accurate forecasts matter because over-building leaves idle capacity and debt [1], which affects the owner, lenders and staff [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* A recommendation is stronger when it is justified against an alternative and linked to the cost of a wrong forecast.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining forecasting without saying what it is used for.
- Describing the Delphi technique without mentioning repeated rounds or anonymity.
- Doing a calculation without showing the stages.
- Writing the moving average against the wrong year.
- Quoting cyclical variation without stating whether it is above or below trend.
- Treating correlation as proof of cause.
- Writing a one-sided evaluation, or a conclusion that repeats earlier points.
- Giving a recommendation with no reason or no alternative.
- Answering in general terms when the question names a business and gives figures.
