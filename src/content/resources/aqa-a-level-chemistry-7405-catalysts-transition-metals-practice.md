These questions cover section **3.2.5.6 Catalysts** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [Transition metal catalysts study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-catalysts-transition-metals/) and the [Transition metal catalysts revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-catalysts-transition-metals-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between a heterogeneous catalyst and a homogeneous catalyst, giving one example of each. **[4]**

**2.** Explain how a heterogeneous catalyst such as iron speeds up the Haber process. **[3]**

**3.** Platinum catalysts are often spread on a ceramic support. Explain the advantages of this. **[2]**

**4.** Explain what is meant by catalyst poisoning and why it has a cost implication in the Haber process. **[3]**

**5.** Vanadium(V) oxide is the catalyst in the Contact process.

**(a)** Write equations to show how V₂O₅ acts as a catalyst. **[2]**

**(b)** State the oxidation states of vanadium in the two vanadium oxides in your equations. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why vanadium is able to act as a catalyst in this way. **[1]**

**6.** The reaction S₂O₈²⁻ + 2I⁻ → 2SO₄²⁻ + I₂ is catalysed by Fe²⁺ ions.

**(a)** Explain why the uncatalysed reaction is slow. **[2]**

**(b)** Write two equations to show how Fe²⁺ catalyses the reaction. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the reaction would also be catalysed by Fe³⁺ ions. **[1]**

**7.** Explain what is meant by autocatalysis, using the reaction between acidified manganate(VII) ions and ethanedioate ions as an example. **[3]**

**8.** The overall equation for the reaction in Question 7 is 2MnO₄⁻ + 16H⁺ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 8H₂O + 10CO₂. Write equations to show how Mn²⁺ catalyses the reaction. **[2]**

**9.** Sketch in words a graph of the concentration of MnO₄⁻ against time for the reaction in Question 8, and explain its shape. **[3]**

**10.** A student adds a small amount of manganese(II) sulfate to the starting mixture in Question 8. State and explain the effect on the rate at the start. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why transition metals and their compounds are often good catalysts. **[2]**

**12.** State which of these is a catalyst in the reaction S₂O₈²⁻ + 2I⁻ → 2SO₄²⁻ + I₂ and explain the effect of the catalyst on activation energy and on the position of equilibrium: Fe²⁺, I⁻, I₂. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A heterogeneous catalyst is in a different phase from the reactants [1]; for example, solid iron in the Haber process (or V₂O₅ in the Contact process) [1]; a homogeneous catalyst is in the same phase as the reactants [1]; for example, Fe²⁺(aq) in the reaction between S₂O₈²⁻ and I⁻ (or Mn²⁺(aq)) [1]. **[4]**

**2.** Nitrogen and hydrogen molecules are adsorbed onto active sites on the iron surface [1]; this weakens the bonds in the reactants and holds them close together, so the reaction occurs with a lower activation energy [1]; the ammonia formed is desorbed from the surface, freeing the active sites [1]. **[3]**

**3.** The support increases the surface area of the catalyst exposed to the reactants [1]; it means that less of the expensive metal is needed, so it minimises the cost [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Poisoning is when an impurity is adsorbed onto the active sites and blocks them [1], so the catalyst has reduced efficiency [1]; the cost implication is that the catalyst has to be replaced or regenerated, or the hydrogen feedstock purified (to remove sulfur compounds), which adds to the cost [1]. **[3]**

**5. (a)** SO₂ + V₂O₅ → SO₃ + V₂O₄ [1]; 2V₂O₄ + O₂ → 2V₂O₅ [1]

**(b)** +5 in V₂O₅ and +4 in V₂O₄ [1]

**(c)** Vanadium has variable oxidation states, so it can be reduced and then re-oxidised, regenerating the catalyst [1]. **[4]**

**6. (a)** Both reactants are negatively charged ions [1]; they repel each other, so collisions between them are rare and few have enough energy to react [1]

**(b)** S₂O₈²⁻ + 2Fe²⁺ → 2SO₄²⁻ + 2Fe³⁺ [1]; 2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂ [1]

**(c)** Fe³⁺ would react with iodide ions first, forming Fe²⁺ and iodine, and Fe²⁺ would then react with persulfate to regenerate Fe³⁺ [1]. **[5]**

**7.** Autocatalysis is when one of the products of a reaction acts as a catalyst for that reaction [1]; Mn²⁺ is formed in the reaction between MnO₄⁻ and C₂O₄²⁻ [1]; the reaction is slow at first and speeds up as Mn²⁺ forms [1]. **[3]**

**8.** 4Mn²⁺ + MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ → 5Mn³⁺ + 4H₂O [1]; 2Mn³⁺ + C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 2CO₂ [1]. **[2]**

**9.** The concentration falls slowly at first [1]; the gradient then becomes steeper as Mn²⁺ is produced and catalyses the reaction [1]; finally the curve levels off as the reactants are used up [1]. **[3]**

**10.** The initial rate is faster, with no slow start [1]; Mn²⁺ is already present to act as the catalyst [1]. **[2]**

**11.** They have variable oxidation states, so they can form intermediates by accepting and giving electrons [1]; this provides an alternative route with a lower activation energy, and the catalyst is regenerated (heterogeneous catalysts also have surfaces that adsorb molecules) [1]. **[2]**

**12.** Fe²⁺ is the catalyst [1]; it lowers the activation energy by providing an alternative route [1]; it does not change the position of equilibrium [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* When asked to explain with equations, write the equations, then say in words that the catalyst is regenerated and the steps add up to the overall equation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying a catalyst is not involved in the reaction.
- Mixing up heterogeneous and homogeneous.
- Omitting the reason the S₂O₈²⁻ and I⁻ reaction is slow (like charges repel).
- Not showing that the catalyst's oxidation state changes and is restored.
- Saying a catalyst shifts the equilibrium.
