## The criterion in one table

| Item | Recall |
|---|---|
| Maximum | 8 |
| Bands | 1-2, 3-4, 5-6, 7-8; 0 if no descriptor is reached |
| Strand i | Select appropriate mathematics in familiar and unfamiliar situations |
| Strand ii | Apply the selected mathematics successfully |
| Strand iii | Solve problems correctly in a variety of contexts |
| What rises through the bands | The difficulty of the problems (simple, more complex, challenging) and, at the top, unfamiliar situations |
| Typical task | Tests and examination-style questions |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning for Criterion A |
|---|---|
| Familiar situation | A problem type you have practised, where the method is cued |
| Unfamiliar situation | A problem that does not reveal its method: hidden step, combined topics, reversed question or new context |
| Select | Decide which mathematics fits before calculating |
| Apply successfully | Carry the method through with accurate algebra, arithmetic, units and notation |
| Solve correctly | Reach the right answer in the form the question asks for |
| Variety of contexts | Pure problems and problems set in situations, across different branches |
| Follow-through | Credit for correct method applied to your own earlier (wrong) value |

## Method signals

These links help with strand i. They are starting points, not rules.

| What you notice | Mathematics to consider |
|---|---|
| Quantities that scale together | Unit rate, ratio, direct proportion |
| "Original" price or value after a change | Inverse percentage: divide by the multiplier |
| Repeated percentage change | Multiplier raised to a power |
| A right angle and two known sides | Pythagoras' theorem |
| A right angle, one side and one angle | Sine, cosine or tangent ratio |
| Two unknowns and two facts | Simultaneous equations |
| An unknown that ends up multiplied by itself | A quadratic: rearrange to equal zero |
| "At least", "no more than", a range of values | An inequality |
| Space inside a solid | Volume, then convert units (1 m³ = 1000 litres, 1 cm³ = 1 ml) |
| "Average" with one value missing | Total = mean × number of values |
| Two events in sequence | Tree diagram or multiplying probabilities |

## The checking toolkit

- **Substitute back** into the original equation or condition.
- **Estimate** with rounded figures: π × 0.6² × 0.9 is roughly 3 × 0.36 × 0.9, about 1.
- **Reverse the operation**: after an inverse percentage, apply the percentage to your answer.
- **Second method**: an equation and a unit-rate approach should agree.
- **Context check**: no negative lengths, no probability above 1, whole numbers where items are counted.

## Rounding a count

| Situation | Round | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tins, boxes, buses, packs needed | Up | Rounding down would leave the job unfinished |
| Full days, complete items made, whole people served | Down | The last part-day or part-item is not complete |
| Money | To 2 d.p. (or the currency's smallest unit) | Smaller amounts cannot be paid |
| Measurements from data given to 3 s.f. | 3 s.f. | Do not claim more accuracy than the data has |

## Exam traps

- Adding a percentage to the sale price to undo a discount (80 + 20% gives 96, not the correct 100).
- Dividing a volume in m³ by a daily use in litres without converting.
- Carrying a rounded intermediate value forward, such as writing π × 0.6² = 1.1310 as 1.1 and multiplying 1.1 by the depth.
- Keeping both roots of a quadratic for a length or a time.
- Solving the equation correctly but not answering the question (finding x when the question asks for the perimeter).
- Reaching a "show that" answer by working backwards from it.
- Writing a calculator display (2.5E−3) instead of 2.5 × 10⁻³.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Selecting vs applying**: choosing Pythagoras' theorem is strand i; squaring and square-rooting correctly is strand ii.
- **Familiar vs easy**: an unfamiliar problem can use simple mathematics; it is unfamiliar because the method is not signalled.
- **Correct vs complete**: a correct value of x is not a complete solution if the question asked for something built from x.
- **State vs calculate**: "state" needs no working; "calculate" needs the relevant stages.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the three strands of Criterion A.
2. Which word separates the top band from the band below it?
3. A jacket costs 63 after a 10% discount. Find the original price.
4. A tank holds 2.4 m³. How many 20-litre containers can be filled completely?
5. The mean of four numbers is 15. Three of them are 12, 18 and 11. Find the fourth.
6. A ladder 5.0 m long reaches 4.8 m up a wall. How far is its foot from the wall?
7. A population of 2000 grows by 5% a year. Write an expression for the population after n years.
8. Two numbers have sum 31 and difference 9. Find them.
9. A square has the same perimeter as a 9 cm by 5 cm rectangle. Find the area of the square.
10. A rectangle has width w and length w + 3, and its area is 40 cm². Find w.
11. Paint covers 12 m² per tin. A wall is 50 m². How many tins are needed?
12. What should you do first when a "show that" result is given to 1 d.p.?

### Answers

1. Select appropriate mathematics; apply it successfully; solve problems correctly in a variety of contexts.
2. Unfamiliar: the top band needs challenging problems in familiar and unfamiliar situations.
3. 63 is 90% of the original, so 63 ÷ 0.9 = **70**.
4. 2.4 m³ = 2400 litres; 2400 ÷ 20 = **120** containers.
5. Total = 4 × 15 = 60; 60 − 41 = **19**.
6. √(5.0² − 4.8²) = √(25 − 23.04) = √1.96 = **1.4 m**.
7. **2000 × 1.05ⁿ**.
8. x + y = 31 and x − y = 9 give 2x = 40, so **20 and 11**.
9. Perimeter 28 cm, so the side is 7 cm and the area is **49 cm²**.
10. w² + 3w − 40 = 0, so (w + 8)(w − 5) = 0 and **w = 5 cm** (w = −8 is rejected).
11. 50 ÷ 12 = 4.17, so **5 tins** (round up).
12. Work out the value to more decimal places, then show that it rounds to the given value.

## Frequently asked questions

**How can I make strand i visible in my working?**
Write a short opening line naming the mathematics and why it fits, such as "the sides are linked by Pythagoras' theorem". It shows your selection even if a later slip spoils the answer.

**Do I lose all the credit if an early answer is wrong?**
Not necessarily. Correct method applied to your own earlier value can still be credited, so use it in later parts and keep your working clear.
