## Answers

**1. Tissue** — a group of **similar cells** working together for a shared function [1], e.g. **muscular tissue** [1].
**Organ** — a group of **different tissues** working together [1], e.g. the **stomach** [1].

**2. Fatty acids** [1] and **glycerol** [1].
*Both are required — "fatty acids" alone gets one mark.*

**3.** It does not **catalyse a reaction** [1]; it emulsifies fats physically and neutralises acid, but is not itself a biological catalyst [1].

**4. (a)** rate = 1000 ÷ 60 [1] = **16.7 s⁻¹** (accept 16.7) [1].

**(b)** The time taken **decreases**, so the **rate increases** [1]. The molecules gain **more kinetic energy** and move faster [1], so there are **more successful collisions between enzyme and substrate** per second, forming more enzyme–substrate complexes [1].

**(c)** The enzyme has **denatured** [1]. The high temperature has broken the bonds holding the **tertiary structure** [1], so the **active site has changed shape** and the substrate no longer fits [1].
*"The enzyme is killed" scores nothing — enzymes are not alive.*

**(d)** Any two: **pH**, **concentration of amylase**, **concentration/volume of starch**, **volume of solution** [1] [1].

**5. (a)** Made in the **liver** [1]; stored in the **gall bladder** [1].

**(b)** It **neutralises stomach acid** [1], providing the **alkaline pH** that pancreatic enzymes need to work [1].
It **emulsifies fats** — breaking large droplets into many small ones [1] — which **increases the surface area** for lipase to act on, speeding up digestion [1].

**6. (a)** The left ventricle pumps blood **to the whole body** [1], which requires a **higher pressure** [1], so a thicker muscular wall is needed to generate it. The right ventricle only pumps to the **nearby lungs** [1].

**(b)** They **prevent backflow** of blood [1], ensuring it flows in **one direction only** through the heart [1].

**(c)** Blood is **re-pressurised** after passing through the lungs [1], so it can be pumped around the body **quickly and at high pressure**, delivering oxygen more efficiently [1].
*(Also acceptable: keeps oxygenated and deoxygenated blood separate.)*

**7.** Any three, each with the adaptation [1] and its reason [1]:
**Very large total surface area** from the huge number of alveoli — allows more gas to diffuse at once.
**Walls one cell thick** — gives a **short diffusion distance**.
**Moist lining** — allows gases to **dissolve** before diffusing.
**Dense capillary network** — maintains a **steep concentration gradient**.
**Well ventilated** — continually replaces the air, also maintaining the gradient.

**8. (a)** Substrate: **protein** [1]. Products: **amino acids** [1].

**(b)** Any four: it is **long**, giving a large surface area over its length [1]; it is **folded**, further increasing surface area [1]; it is covered in **villi and microvilli**, increasing surface area further still [1]; it has a **thin, one-cell-thick wall**, giving a short diffusion distance [1]; it has a **rich blood supply**, maintaining a steep concentration gradient [1].

**9. (a)** **Arteries** carry blood **away from the heart** at **high pressure**, so they have **thick, elastic walls** and a **narrow lumen** [2]. **Veins** carry blood **back to the heart** at **low pressure**, so they have **wide lumens** and **valves** to prevent backflow [2].

**(b)** Capillary walls are **one cell thick** to give the **shortest possible diffusion distance** for the exchange of substances between the blood and surrounding tissue [2].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving only one product of lipid digestion.
- Saying enzymes are "killed" rather than denatured.
- Describing bile as digesting fat.
- Stating an alveolar adaptation without saying what it achieves.
- Confusing tissue with organ.
- Explaining an artery's structure without linking it to the high pressure of the blood it carries.
- Naming a small-intestine adaptation without saying what it increases or shortens.
