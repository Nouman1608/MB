Condensed notes for sub-topic 5.4, Sources of finance and commercial performance.

## Four facts to start with

1. **Short-term** finance is repaid in about a year; **long-term** finance is for more than a year.
2. **Gross profit** = revenue − cost of sales. **Net profit** = gross profit − expenses.
3. **Working capital** = current assets − current liabilities.
4. **Mark-up** is based on cost; **margin** is based on revenue.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Entrepreneur | Person who takes the risk of starting a business |
| Revenue | Money from sales |
| Gross profit | Revenue minus cost of sales |
| Net profit | Gross profit minus expenses |
| Working capital | Current assets minus current liabilities |
| Asset | Something the business owns |
| Liability | Money the business owes |
| Capital | Money put in by the owners |
| Cash discount | Reduction for prompt payment |
| Trade discount | Reduction given to another business |
| Debt factoring | Selling invoices to a factor for quick cash |

## Formulas

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Revenue | Selling price × units sold |
| Gross profit | Revenue − cost of sales |
| Gross profit margin | Gross profit ÷ revenue × 100 |
| Net profit | Gross profit − expenses |
| Working capital | Current assets − current liabilities |
| Mark-up % | (Selling price − unit cost) ÷ unit cost × 100 |

## Worked examples

**Profit.** 1,200 units at $25: revenue $30,000. Cost of sales $18,000: gross profit $12,000, margin 40%. Expenses $7,500: net profit $4,500.

**Working capital.** Current assets $14,000 − current liabilities $9,500 = $4,500.

**Mark-up.** Cost $15, selling price $24: (24 − 15) ÷ 15 × 100 = 60%.

**Cash discount.** $800 with 2.5% discount: pay $780.

**Trade discount.** $400 less 25%: $300.

## Choosing finance

| Need | Choose |
|---|---|
| Short cash gap | Overdraft, debt factoring |
| Equipment | Hire purchase, leasing, loan |
| Large long-term sum | Shares, loan, debentures |
| Risky start-up | Venture capital, crowdfunding |
| Expansion without outside control | Retained profit |

## Improving performance

Raise sales: advertise, new products. Raise profit: cut expenses, change prices. Raise mark-up: increase price or lower cost. Improve working capital: collect debts sooner, sell old stock.

## Model answers to learn

**Why is retained profit a good source?** It costs no interest and does not give outsiders control, but it is only available once the business has made profit.

**Why might raising prices not raise profit?** Customers may buy less, so revenue could fall.

## Margin or mark-up?

| | Gross profit margin | Mark-up |
|---|---|---|
| Based on | Revenue (selling price) | Cost |
| Formula | Gross profit ÷ revenue × 100 | (Selling price − cost) ÷ cost × 100 |
| Example: cost $15, price $24 | 9 ÷ 24 = 37.5% | 9 ÷ 15 = 60% |

The same profit gives a larger mark-up than margin, because cost is smaller than the selling price.

## Layout for a calculation

1. Write the formula.
2. Put in the numbers.
3. Work out the answer.
4. Add the unit ($ or %).
5. Interpret if asked ("profit rose", "the business can pay its debts").

## Exam traps

- **Mark-up on revenue.** Use cost.
- **Forgetting ×100.**
- **Gross and net profit confused.**
- **Liabilities added** in working capital.
- **Short-term finance for a factory.**
- **Calculation with no formula shown.**

## Self-test

1. State two sources of short-term finance.
2. State two sources of long-term finance.
3. What is debt factoring?
4. Calculate revenue for 500 units at $12.
5. Revenue $9,000, cost of sales $5,400: find gross profit and margin.
6. Gross profit $3,600, expenses $2,100: find net profit.
7. Current assets $8,000, current liabilities $5,000: find working capital.
8. Cost $40, selling price $50: find mark-up %.
9. Give one way to improve working capital.
10. Why might an entrepreneur use crowdfunding?

**Answers**

1. Any two: trade credit, debt factoring, hire purchase, leasing, sale and leaseback, overdraft, selling assets, owner's savings, retained profit.
2. Any two: ordinary shares, preference shares, loans, debentures, venture capital, crowdfunding.
3. Selling unpaid invoices to a factor, who pays most of the money at once and collects the debts.
4. 500 × 12 = $6,000.
5. Gross profit = $3,600; margin = 3,600 ÷ 9,000 × 100 = 40%.
6. $1,500.
7. $3,000.
8. (50 − 40) ÷ 40 × 100 = 25%.
9. Any one: collect debts sooner, sell old stock, use trade credit, reduce expenses.
10. To raise money from many people, with no interest or loss of control, and to test demand.
