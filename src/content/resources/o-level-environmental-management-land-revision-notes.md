Topic 2 Land of Cambridge O Level Environmental Management (5014) asks how soil supports crops, how
farmers raise yields, what goes wrong when farming is unsustainable, and how soil erosion is caused and
prevented. The examiners expect accurate terms (pore spaces, salinisation, eutrophication) and, in
"discuss" questions, a balance of benefits and limitations.

## 2.1 What is soil made of, and why does it matter?

**Four components**: mineral particles (sand, silt, clay); organic content (living plants, animals, fungi,
bacteria, and decomposed organic matter); gases; water. (Percentages are not required.)

| Soil property | Why it matters for crops |
|---|---|
| Mineral particles | Sand drains fast but holds few nutrients; clay holds water and nutrients but can waterlog |
| Nutrients | Organic content plus ions: nitrate (NO3-), phosphate (PO4 3-), potassium (K+) |
| pH | Acidic, neutral or alkaline soils suit different crops |
| Pore spaces | Hold air and water; let roots grow |
| Gas content | Roots need oxygen for respiration |
| Water and drainage | Enough water for growth without waterlogging |
| Ease of cultivation | Light soils are easier to plough |

**Loam** is the best growing medium because it has an ideal mix of particles and pore spaces, retains
moisture, contains organic content and ions, drains excess water, and is easy to cultivate.

**Weather** = day-to-day conditions of the atmosphere in a location. Some regions have **wet and dry
seasons** that control when crops can grow. Optimum photosynthesis and growth depend on the **length of
the growing season**, **optimum weather conditions** and **daylight hours**.

## 2.2 How is food produced and how can yields rise?

**Types of agriculture**
- Arable (crops), pastoral (livestock), mixed (both).
- Subsistence (feeds the family) vs commercial (for sale).
- Intensive: high inputs of labour, capital or chemicals per hectare.
- Monoculture: a single crop over a large area.

**Sustainable food production** = producing enough food for the present generation using methods that
ensure future generations can grow food from the same land.

**Twelve strategies to raise yield - grouped**

| Group | Strategies | How they help |
|---|---|---|
| Planting patterns | Mixed cropping, intercropping, crop rotation | Different nutrient needs; fewer pests; legumes add nitrogen |
| Water | Trickle/drip irrigation, rainwater harvesting, automated watering | Water goes to roots with less waste |
| Machines | Mechanisation | Faster planting and harvesting over large areas |
| Genetics | GMOs - genes removed, changed or inserted | Pest resistance, drought tolerance, higher yield |
| Controlled environments | Greenhouses, hydroponics, aeroponics | Control of temperature, water and nutrients all year |
| Livestock | Managed grazing, livestock rotation | Pasture recovers; less overgrazing |
| Land use | Urban farming, agroforestry | Food grown in cities; trees shelter crops and add organic matter |
| Nutrients | Inorganic NPK fertilisers; organic fertilisers (crop residue, manure, mulch) | Replace nitrate, phosphate and potassium |
| Pests | Insecticides, herbicides, fungicides; biological control | Reduce crop losses |

**What happens when farming is unsustainable?**
1. Overproduction → food wastage.
2. Cash crops (coffee, cotton, soya beans, palm oil) and biofuel crops replace food crops → food shortages.
3. Poor irrigation → soil erosion, **salinisation** (salt left as water evaporates) and waterlogging.
4. Overuse of pesticides → pest resistance, pest resurgence, harm to pollinators.
5. Overuse of fertilisers → leaching, nutrient enrichment, **eutrophication** of water.
6. Nutrient exhaustion → soils lose organic content and ions.
7. Removal of vegetation → deforestation, overcultivation, overgrazing.
8. Monoculture and intensification → soil and water pollution, loss of biodiversity.
9. Soil erosion → loss of topsoil and nutrients.

## 2.3 Why does soil erode and how is it prevented?

**Causes**: unsustainable farming; deforestation; farming on steep slopes; bare soil; wind and water.

**Impacts**: silting of rivers; desertification; mass movement (landslides, rockslides, mudslides); loss of
habitats and biodiversity; lower crop yields; malnutrition and famine; displacement of people.

| Strategy | What it does |
|---|---|
| Terracing | Flat steps on slopes reduce soil movement and surface run-off |
| Contour ploughing | Furrows follow the contours, reducing run-off |
| Bunds | Low banks reduce run-off and wind speed |
| Wind breaks | Trees or hedges protect crops and reduce wind erosion |
| Vegetation cover | Roots bind soil; plants absorb water and slow run-off |
| Organic matter | Improves soil structure so it holds together |

## Worked example: matching strategy to problem

A farmer on a 20° hillside in a region with heavy wet-season rain loses topsoil every year.

- Problem: water erosion of bare soil on a steep slope.
- Best strategies: terracing (reduces run-off on steep land) and keeping vegetation cover between crops.
- Why not wind breaks? They target wind erosion, which is not the main problem here.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Saying clay soil is best because it holds water: it waterlogs and is hard to cultivate; loam is best.
- Confusing weather (day to day) with climate (many years).
- Writing "fertiliser causes eutrophication" without the steps of leaching and nutrient enrichment.
- Mixing up intercropping (crops in alternate rows) with crop rotation (changing crops each season).
- Naming a strategy without saying how it reduces erosion.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is loam the best soil for crops?**
It has an ideal mix of sand, silt and clay with pore spaces, so it
holds moisture and nutrients but drains excess water and is easy to cultivate.

**What is salinisation?**
A build-up of salts in soil when irrigation water evaporates, which lowers crop
yields. The 5014 syllabus lists it as a result of mismanaged irrigation.

**Is biological control a pesticide?**
No. It uses a natural predator or parasite to reduce a pest, while
chemical control uses insecticides, herbicides or fungicides.
