**Key question 5 of Core Content Option B in Cambridge O Level History 2147 asks how effectively the United States
contained the spread of communism.** Cambridge sets it through three case studies: Korea (1950–53), Cuba (1959–62) and
Vietnam (1955–75). The word "effectively" demands a judgement, so for each case you need to know what the USA was
trying to achieve, what it did, and what the outcome was compared with its aims. The question was the Paper 2
prescribed topic for June 2024 and is again for **November 2026**, so 2147 candidates in that series will meet it on
the document paper as well as Paper 1.

For condensed recall, use the [containment revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-containment-communism-revision-notes/);
for exam questions and mark schemes, use the
[containment practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-containment-communism-practice/).
The [O Level twentieth century study guide](/resources/o-level-world-history-twentieth-century-1919-1989/) gives the
overview of Option B.

## What the specification sets

Focus points, explored through case studies:

- The United States and events in Korea, 1950–53
- The United States and events in Cuba, 1959–62
- American involvement in Vietnam, 1955–75

Specified content: American reactions to North Korea's invasion of South Korea, the involvement of the UN and the
course of the war to 1953; American reactions to the Cuban Revolution, including the Missile Crisis and its aftermath;
American involvement in the Vietnam War, including reasons for involvement, tactics and strategy, and reasons for
withdrawal.

## Criteria for "effectively"

Before the case studies, decide how you will judge them. Four criteria work well:

1. **Territory:** did communism gain or lose ground?
2. **Cost:** what did the USA pay in lives, money and reputation?
3. **Escalation:** was a wider or nuclear war avoided?
4. **Credibility:** did allies and enemies come to believe the USA would act?

Using the same criteria for each case makes comparison easy and gives your conclusion a basis.

## Korea, 1950–53

**Background.** Soviet and American forces accepted the Japanese surrender in Korea in 1945 on either side of the
38th parallel. By 1948 there were two states: a communist North under Kim Il Sung, backed by Moscow, and a South under
Syngman Rhee, backed by Washington. In 1949 the communists won the Chinese Civil War, and the USSR
tested its first atomic bomb. A US policy paper, NSC-68 (April 1950), called for a large military build-up to contain
communism worldwide.

**American reaction.** When North Korea invaded on **25 June 1950**, Truman saw a test of containment. The USA took the
issue to the UN Security Council. The USSR was boycotting the Council because the UN recognised Nationalist China
rather than the communist government, so it could not use its veto. The Council called on members to help South Korea,
and a UN force, mostly American and commanded by General MacArthur, was sent.

**Course of the war.**
- By September 1950 the North had pushed UN and South Korean forces into a small area around Pusan.
- MacArthur's landing at **Inchon** (September 1950) cut North Korean supply lines; UN forces retook Seoul.
- UN forces crossed the 38th parallel and advanced towards the Yalu River on the Chinese border. The aim had shifted
  from containment to rolling back communism.
- **China entered the war** in October–November 1950 with huge numbers of troops and drove UN forces back south of the
  parallel; Seoul fell again in January 1951.
- MacArthur publicly called for attacks on China itself. Truman, fearing a wider war with China and the USSR, **dismissed
  him in April 1951**.
- The war became a stalemate near the 38th parallel. An **armistice** was signed at Panmunjom in **July 1953**.

**Judgement on effectiveness.** South Korea survived, and the USA showed it would fight to defend an ally, so
containment worked in its original form. But the attempt to liberate the North failed, China emerged as a military
power, and the war cost the USA tens of thousands of lives for a border close to where it began.

## Cuba, 1959–62

**The Cuban Revolution.** Fidel Castro overthrew the US-backed dictator Batista in January 1959. When Castro
nationalised American-owned businesses and land, the USA cut its purchases of Cuban sugar and imposed a trade embargo.
Castro turned to the USSR for trade and support and declared himself a Marxist-Leninist in 1961.

**The Bay of Pigs (April 1961).** About 1,400 Cuban exiles trained by the CIA landed at the Bay of Pigs, expecting a
popular rising. It never came; the invaders were killed or captured within days, and Kennedy, who had approved the plan,
refused to send US air support. The failure humiliated the USA and pushed Castro closer to Moscow.

**The Missile Crisis (October 1962).**
- On 14 October a U-2 spy plane photographed Soviet missile sites under construction in Cuba.
- Kennedy's advisers (the ExComm) considered air strikes and invasion. He chose a **naval blockade, called a
  "quarantine"**, announced on 22 October.
- Soviet ships approaching the quarantine line turned back on 24 October.
- Khrushchev sent two letters: the first offered to remove the missiles if the USA promised not to invade Cuba; the
  second added a demand for removal of US missiles from Turkey. On 27 October a U-2 was shot down over Cuba.
- Kennedy answered the first letter and ignored the second in public, while his brother Robert told the Soviet
  ambassador privately that the Jupiter missiles in Turkey would go. On 28 October Khrushchev announced that the Cuban
  sites would be dismantled.

**Aftermath.** Both leaders wanted to avoid another near miss: a direct communication link, the "hotline", opened in
1963, and the Partial Test Ban Treaty followed in August 1963. Castro remained in power, and Cuba stayed communist.

**Judgement on effectiveness.** The USA failed to prevent a communist state 90 miles from Florida and failed to
overthrow it in 1961. But in 1962 it forced the removal of Soviet missiles without war, which was a major success of
containment in its narrower military sense. The secret Turkish deal shows the success was partly a compromise.

## Vietnam, 1955–75

**Reasons for involvement.** France's defeat at Dien Bien Phu ended its rule in Indochina. The **Geneva Accords
(1954)** drew a temporary line at the 17th parallel and promised national elections in 1956. Washington supported Ngo
Dinh Diem in the South, and the vote never took place, partly because Ho Chi Minh was expected to win it. Eisenhower's **domino theory** argued that if
one country fell to communism, its neighbours would follow. Kennedy increased the number of US military advisers to
about 16,000. In August 1964 the **Gulf of Tonkin incident** led Congress to pass a resolution giving President
Johnson wide powers to use force.

**Tactics and strategy.**
- **Operation Rolling Thunder** (from 1965): bombing of North Vietnam.
- **Ground troops** from March 1965, reaching over half a million by 1968.
- **Search and destroy** missions against the Viet Cong, and **chemical weapons** such as napalm and Agent Orange to
  destroy cover and crops.
- **Strategic hamlets** to separate villagers from guerrillas.

The Viet Cong used guerrilla tactics, tunnels and the **Ho Chi Minh Trail** through Laos and Cambodia for supplies. US
methods often killed civilians and turned peasants against the South Vietnamese government, as the My Lai massacre of
1968 showed when it became public in 1969.

**Reasons for withdrawal.**
- The **Tet Offensive** (January 1968) was a military defeat for the communists but convinced many Americans the war
  could not be won; Johnson announced he would not stand for re-election.
- The anti-war movement grew, including the killing of students at **Kent State University** in May 1970.
- The financial cost and casualties mounted; the draft fell unequally on the poor.
- **Nixon's Vietnamisation** handed fighting to South Vietnamese forces while US troops withdrew.
- The **Paris Peace Accords** (January 1973) ended US combat involvement. Saigon fell to the North in April 1975.

**Judgement on effectiveness.** Containment failed: Vietnam was unified under communism, and Laos and Cambodia also
became communist in 1975. The war divided American society and damaged US prestige. Yet the wider dominoes in Southeast
Asia, such as Thailand, did not fall.

## Comparing the cases

| Criterion | Korea | Cuba | Vietnam |
|---|---|---|---|
| Territory | South preserved | Cuba stayed communist | All Vietnam communist |
| Cost | Very high casualties | Low in lives; Bay of Pigs humiliation | Very high in lives, money and division at home |
| Escalation | Kept the war inside Korea by sacking MacArthur; no direct war with the USSR | Nuclear war avoided | Avoided direct war with China or the USSR |
| Credibility | Showed the USA would fight | Showed it could face down the USSR | Showed the limits of US power |

## Common mistakes

- Treating Korea as a pure success. The push to the Yalu failed.
- Forgetting the Turkish missiles in the Cuban settlement.
- Writing a narrative of Vietnam without explaining tactics and withdrawal, which the content names.
- Ending the Vietnam case in 1973; the syllabus runs to 1975.

## Exam technique for 2147

- **Describe**: one part of a case study in sequence, such as the course of the Korean War.
- **Explain**: reasons for a decision, such as US withdrawal from Vietnam.
- **How far / how effectively**: use the four criteria and compare at least two cases.
- **Paper 2, November 2026**: expect cartoons and statements by Kennedy, Khrushchev and Johnson. Each leader spoke to
  domestic and foreign audiences at once; identify which one a source was aimed at.
