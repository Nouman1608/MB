Pearson Edexcel International A Level Economics (YEC11) is a two-stage course
for students who want to understand how markets, businesses, governments and
the world economy work, and to argue about policy using evidence and
diagrams. It is modular: four externally assessed units, each worth 80 marks
and 25% of the full qualification. Units 1 and 2 form the International AS
stage and last 105 minutes each; Units 3 and 4 form the A2 stage and last
120 minutes each. The specification was first taught in September 2018 and
first examined in January 2019.

## How the course is organised

The four units alternate between microeconomics and macroeconomics, with
the AS stage laying foundations and the A2 stage extending them.

**AS stage**

- **Unit 1: Markets in action** begins with introductory concepts, then
  covers consumer behaviour and demand, supply, price determination, market
  failure and government intervention in markets. It is the micro
  foundation for the rest of the course.
- **Unit 2: Macroeconomic performance and policy** moves to the whole
  economy: measures of economic performance, aggregate demand (AD),
  aggregate supply (AS), national income, economic growth, and
  macroeconomic objectives and policies.

**A2 stage**

- **Unit 3: Business behaviour** returns to microeconomics at greater depth:
  types and sizes of businesses, revenue, costs and profits, market
  structures and contestability, labour markets and government intervention.
- **Unit 4: Developments in the global economy** widens the macro picture to
  an international scale: the causes and effects of globalisation, trade and
  the global economy, the balance of payments, exchange rates and
  international competitiveness, poverty and inequality, the role of the
  state in the macroeconomy, and growth and development in developing,
  emerging and developed economies.

## How it's assessed

Every unit is a separate externally assessed paper, and every unit carries
the same weight.

| Unit | Paper code | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unit 1: Markets in Action | WEC11/01 | 105 minutes (1h45) | 80 | 25% |
| Unit 2: Macroeconomic Performance and Policy | WEC12/01 | 105 minutes (1h45) | 80 | 25% |
| Unit 3: Business Behaviour | WEC13/01 | 120 minutes (2h) | 80 | 25% |
| Unit 4: Developments in the Global Economy | WEC14/01 | 120 minutes (2h) | 80 | 25% |

The two stages use different question mixes. Units 1 and 2 each contain six
multiple-choice questions (6 marks), five short-answer questions (20 marks),
a five-part data-response question based on a source booklet (34 marks) and
one 20-mark essay chosen from two. Units 3 and 4 each contain six
multiple-choice questions (6 marks), a five-part data-response question
(34 marks) and two 20-mark essays chosen from three. In other words, the A2
units drop the short-answer section and put more weight on extended
writing.

## How the parts fit together

The pairing of units is the key to the course. Unit 3 depends on Unit 1:
cost curves, market structures and labour markets only make sense if supply,
demand, price determination and market failure are already secure.
Unit 4 depends on Unit 2: globalisation, trade and exchange rates are
analysed with the AD and AS framework, and questions about growth and
development assume you can already measure economic performance and
discuss macroeconomic objectives. Government intervention appears in both
microeconomic units and the role of the state appears again in Unit 4, so
the case for and against intervention is a thread that runs through the
whole qualification.

Three skills are tested in every unit. First, precise definitions and quick
reasoning for the multiple-choice items. Second, data handling: reading a
source, calculating or interpreting a figure and applying a model to an
unfamiliar context, which the 34-mark data-response question rewards in
all four papers. Third, evaluation in extended writing, which grows from one
20-mark essay at AS to two at A2. Practising diagrams until they can be drawn
accurately and explained in a sentence or two supports all three.

## Planning your study

In the first year, teach and revise Unit 1 and Unit 2 side by side or in
sequence, depending on how your centre runs the course. Many students find
it helps to finish price determination and market failure before starting
aggregate demand and aggregate supply, because the logic of shifting curves
carries across. Build a glossary of definitions from the start, since the
multiple-choice and short-answer sections reward exact terminology.

In the second year, Unit 3 and Unit 4 build directly on the first-year
units, so a short review of Unit 1 before starting market structures, and of
Unit 2 before starting globalisation and trade, saves time later. As the A2
units carry two essays each, begin writing timed evaluative paragraphs early
in the year rather than leaving essay practice until the revision period.

Use past papers for each unit once its content has been taught, and confirm
with your teacher which series each unit will be entered for. Detailed advice
on pacing each section of each paper is on the exam-preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many units are in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Economics?**
There are four units. Units 1 and 2 form the International AS stage and Units 3 and 4 form the A2 stage. Each unit is externally assessed, worth 80 marks and 25% of the full qualification.

**How long is each YEC11 exam?**
Units 1 and 2 each last 105 minutes (1 hour 45 minutes). Units 3 and 4 each last 120 minutes (2 hours).

**How do the AS and A2 papers differ?**
Units 1 and 2 have six multiple-choice questions, five short-answer questions, a five-part data-response question and one 20-mark essay from a choice of two. Units 3 and 4 have six multiple-choice questions, a five-part data-response question and two 20-mark essays from a choice of three.

**Which units build on each other?**
Unit 3 (Business behaviour) extends the microeconomics of Unit 1 (Markets in action), and Unit 4 (Developments in the global economy) extends the macroeconomics of Unit 2 (Macroeconomic performance and policy).

**When was YEC11 first examined?**
First teaching was September 2018 and the first examination was in January 2019.
