Condensed notes for subtopic **3.4 Length and midpoint** of Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024, 2025 to 2027. The [Length and midpoint study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-length-and-midpoint/) explains each method with worked examples; the [Length and midpoint practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-length-and-midpoint-practice/) give exam-style questions with mark schemes.

## Syllabus outcomes

1. Calculate the length of a line segment.
2. Find the coordinates of the midpoint of a line segment.

Neither formula is supplied in the exam, so learn them.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Line segment | part of a line between two points | AB |
| Length | distance between the endpoints | 10 units |
| Midpoint | point halfway along a segment | (4, 6) |
| Bisect | cut into two equal parts | diagonals of a parallelogram |
| Hypotenuse | side opposite the right angle | the segment itself |
| Surd | exact root left unsimplified | √13 |

## Formulas

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Length of AB | √[(x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²] |
| Midpoint of AB | ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2) |

Length is Pythagoras: the differences in x and y are the two shorter sides.

## Worked examples: length

| Points | Differences | Squares added | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| (1, 2), (7, 10) | 6, 8 | 36 + 64 = 100 | 10 |
| (-4, 3), (2, -5) | 6, -8 | 36 + 64 = 100 | 10 |
| (-2, -1), (4, 5) | 6, 6 | 36 + 36 = 72 | 6√2 = 8.49 |
| (1, 1), (4, 3) | 3, 2 | 9 + 4 = 13 | √13 = 3.61 |
| (0, 0), (3, 3) | 3, 3 | 9 + 9 = 18 | 3√2 = 4.24 |
| (0, 0), (5, 12) | 5, 12 | 25 + 144 = 169 | 13 |

Remember to take the square root. Squares of negatives are positive.

## Worked examples: midpoint

| Points | x mean | y mean | Midpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| (1, 2), (7, 10) | 4 | 6 | (4, 6) |
| (-4, 3), (2, -5) | -1 | -1 | (-1, -1) |
| (2, -7), (-6, 1) | -2 | -3 | (-2, -3) |
| (-3, 4), (5, -2) | 1 | 1 | (1, 1) |

The midpoint lies between the endpoints in both coordinates.

## Finding an endpoint

M is the midpoint of AB. Then B = 2M - A (coordinate by coordinate).

| M | A | B |
|---|---|---|
| (3, 1) | (-1, 4) | (7, -2) |
| (0, 2) | (5, -3) | (-5, 7) |
| (-2, -1) | (4, 6) | (-8, -8) |

Or use steps: the move from A to M repeated from M reaches B.

Unknown values: midpoint of (a, 3) and (5, b) is (2, 4): (a + 5)/2 = 2 gives a = -1, and (3 + b)/2 = 4 gives b = 5.

## Applications

| Task | Method | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Perimeter | find each side, add | (0, 0), (6, 0), (6, 8): 6 + 8 + 10 = 24 |
| Isosceles? | two sides equal | A(1, 1), B(5, 1), C(3, 5): AC² = BC² = 20 |
| Right-angled? | a² + b² = c² | P(1, 2), Q(4, 6), R(8, 3): 25 + 25 = 50 |
| Circle from diameter | midpoint = centre; half length = radius | (-2, 3), (6, -3): centre (2, 0), radius 5 |
| Parallelogram diagonals | same midpoint | A(1, 2), C(7, 5) and B(5, 2), D(3, 5): midpoint (4, 3.5) |
| Unknown coordinate | square, solve, allow ± | (1, 2) to (k, 6) = 5: k = 4 or -2 |

Compare squared lengths when testing for equal sides or right angles, to avoid roots.

## Horizontal and vertical segments

| Segment | Length |
|---|---|
| (-3, 4) to (5, 4) | 5 - (-3) = 8 |
| (2, -1) to (2, 6) | 6 - (-1) = 7 |

Subtract only the coordinates that differ.

## Exam traps

- Take the square root of the sum at the end.
- (-8)² = 64, not -64.
- Add (not subtract) the coordinates for the midpoint, then halve.
- Halve both coordinates, not only one.
- Two values of k come from (k - 1)² = 9.
- Leave answers as surds if the question says "exact".
- Check the midpoint lies between the endpoints.
- Finish "show that" with a clear conclusion.

## Self-test

1. Find the length of the segment joining (2, 3) and (8, 11).
2. Find the midpoint of (-5, 2) and (3, 8).
3. M(1, -2) is the midpoint of PQ and P is (4, 3). Find Q.
4. Write the distance between (0, 0) and (2, 4) as a simplified surd.
5. The ends of a diameter are (1, 1) and (7, 9). Find the centre and radius.
6. Show that the triangle with vertices (0, 0), (4, 0) and (2, 3) is isosceles.
7. The distance from (0, 0) to (k, 3) is 5. Find k.
8. The midpoint of (m, 6) and (4, n) is (3, 7). Find m and n.

**Answers**

1. √(36 + 64) = **10**.
2. **(-1, 5)**.
3. Q = (2 - 4, -4 - 3) = **(-2, -7)**.
4. √20 = **2√5**.
5. centre **(4, 5)**; diameter √(36 + 64) = 10, so radius **5**.
6. Both slanted sides have length² = 4 + 9 = 13, so they are equal and the triangle is **isosceles**.
7. k² + 9 = 25, k² = 16, so **k = 4 or k = -4**.
8. (m + 4)/2 = 3 gives **m = 2**; (6 + n)/2 = 7 gives **n = 8**.
