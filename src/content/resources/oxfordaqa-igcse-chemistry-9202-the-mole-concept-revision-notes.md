## The essentials

- **One mole** of a substance = its Mᵣ in grams (3.6.3 a).
- **moles = mass ÷ Mᵣ**, and **mass = moles × Mᵣ**.
- **One mole contains 6.02 × 10²³ atoms or molecules**: Avogadro's constant (3.6.3 b).
- **particles = moles × 6.02 × 10²³**.
- Always go mass → moles → particles, or the reverse.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mole (mol) | The amount of substance containing 6.02 × 10²³ particles |
| Avogadro's constant | 6.02 × 10²³, the number of particles in one mole |
| Relative formula mass, Mᵣ | Sum of the Aᵣ values in a formula; one mole has this mass in grams |
| Molar mass | Mass of one mole, numerically equal to Mᵣ, in g |
| Particle | Whatever is being counted: an atom, a molecule, or a formula unit |
| Standard form | A number written as a × 10ⁿ, with a between 1 and 10 |

## Conversions at a glance

| You know | You want | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| Mass | Moles | ÷ Mᵣ |
| Moles | Mass | × Mᵣ |
| Moles | Particles | × 6.02 × 10²³ |
| Particles | Moles | ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ |
| Moles of molecules | Moles of atoms | × atoms per molecule |

## Quick reference values

| Sample | Moles | Molecules | Atoms |
|---|---|---|---|
| 32 g O₂ | 1.00 | 6.02 × 10²³ | 1.20 × 10²⁴ |
| 3.2 g O₂ | 0.100 | 6.02 × 10²² | 1.20 × 10²³ |
| 11 g CO₂ | 0.250 | 1.51 × 10²³ | 4.52 × 10²³ |
| 9.0 g H₂O | 0.500 | 3.01 × 10²³ | 9.03 × 10²³ |

(Aᵣ: H = 1, C = 12, O = 16)

## Equal masses or equal moles?

- **Equal moles** of any substances contain equal numbers of particles.
- **Equal masses** do not: a substance with a smaller Mᵣ has more moles, and so more particles, in the same mass.
- Example: 10 g of hydrogen gas (Mᵣ 2) is 5 mol; 10 g of oxygen gas (Mᵣ 32) is 0.3125 mol. The hydrogen contains 16 times as many molecules.

## Moles and equations

Divide each mass that reacts by its Mᵣ, then simplify the ratio: the result is the set of coefficients in the balanced equation.

Example: 4.8 g Mg reacts with 3.2 g O₂ to form 8.0 g MgO. Moles 0.20 : 0.10 : 0.20 = 2 : 1 : 2, so **2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO**.

## Worked mini-example: particles back to mass

*What is the mass of 1.505 × 10²³ molecules of carbon dioxide?*

1. Moles = 1.505 × 10²³ ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 0.250 mol.
2. Mass = 0.250 × 44 = **11.0 g**.

The route is the conversion table read from the bottom up: particles → moles → mass.

## Calculator and rounding tips

- Use the ×10ˣ (EXP) key for 6.02 × 10²³.
- Give final particle numbers to 3 significant figures.
- Do not round between steps.
- Sense check: a few grams of a common substance is usually less than 1 mol, and contains around 10²² to 10²³ particles.

## Exam traps

- Calculating Mᵣ ÷ mass.
- Using 16 for O₂ instead of 32, or 35.5 for Cl₂ instead of 71.
- Counting molecules when atoms are asked for.
- Giving moles the unit grams.
- Writing 6.02 × 10²³ as 6.02²³.
- Saying 1 g of any element contains Avogadro's number of atoms.

## Quick self-test

1. What is the mass of one mole of carbon dioxide? (Aᵣ: C = 12, O = 16)
2. How many moles are in 20 g of sodium hydroxide, NaOH? (Aᵣ: Na = 23, O = 16, H = 1)
3. Calculate the mass of 0.25 mol of calcium carbonate, CaCO₃. (Aᵣ: Ca = 40)
4. State Avogadro's constant.
5. How many atoms are in 0.50 mol of copper?
6. How many molecules are in 2.2 g of carbon dioxide?
7. How many atoms are in 1 mol of ammonia, NH₃?
8. Which contains more molecules: 1 g of hydrogen gas or 1 g of oxygen gas? Explain.
9. A sample contains 1.204 × 10²⁴ molecules. How many moles is that?
10. Calculate the mass of one molecule of water, in grams.
11. 2.3 g of sodium reacts with 3.55 g of chlorine, Cl₂, to give 5.85 g of sodium chloride. Use moles to find the balanced equation. (Aᵣ: Na = 23, Cl = 35.5)
12. Why is the mole used instead of counting atoms directly?

### Answers

1. 44 g
2. 20 ÷ 40 = 0.50 mol
3. 0.25 × 100 = 25 g
4. 6.02 × 10²³ (particles per mole)
5. 0.50 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 3.01 × 10²³
6. 2.2 ÷ 44 = 0.050 mol; 0.050 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 3.01 × 10²²
7. 4 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 2.41 × 10²⁴
8. Hydrogen: its Mᵣ (2) is smaller than oxygen's (32), so 1 g is more moles and more molecules.
9. 1.204 × 10²⁴ ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 2.00 mol
10. 18 ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 2.99 × 10⁻²³ g
11. Na 0.10 : Cl₂ 0.050 : NaCl 0.10 = 2 : 1 : 2, so 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl.
12. Atoms are so small that any sample contains far too many to count; moles link a measurable mass to a known number of particles.
