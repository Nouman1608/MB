## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Travelling wave | Disturbance that transfers energy with no net transfer of matter |
| Transverse wave | Oscillations perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer |
| Longitudinal wave | Oscillations parallel to the direction of energy transfer |
| Displacement | Distance and direction of a particle from its equilibrium position |
| Amplitude A | Maximum displacement |
| Wavelength λ | Distance between neighbouring points in phase (e.g. crest to crest) |
| Period T | Time for one complete oscillation |
| Frequency f | Number of oscillations per second; f = 1/T |
| Wave speed v | Speed at which the wave pattern (energy) moves |
| Compression | Region of a longitudinal wave where particles are closer together (higher pressure) |
| Rarefaction | Region where particles are further apart (lower pressure) |
| Mechanical wave | Oscillation of particles of a medium; needs a medium |
| Electromagnetic wave | Oscillating electric and magnetic fields; needs no medium |

## Equations

```
v = fλ = λ/T          f = 1/T          c = fλ  (EM waves in vacuum, c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹)
echo: distance to reflector = vt/2
```

*Worked reminder:* f = 250 Hz, λ = 1.36 m → v = 250 × 1.36 = **340 m s⁻¹**.

## Graph reading: method in steps

1. Read the horizontal axis label: position (x) or time (t)?
2. Position graph → λ (crest to crest) and A.
3. Time graph → T (peak to peak) and A; then f = 1/T.
4. Need v? Combine λ from one with f from the other: v = fλ.

| Graph | Gives | Never gives |
|---|---|---|
| Displacement–position | λ, A, instantaneous shape | T, f |
| Displacement–time | T, f, A | λ |

## Particle motion

- Particles oscillate about fixed equilibrium positions; no net movement.
- At a crest or trough: particle momentarily **at rest**.
- At equilibrium: particle moving **fastest**; v_max = 2πfA (from SHM).
- Wave moving right: points ahead of a crest move up; points behind it move down.
- Longitudinal: compression centres and rarefaction centres are at points of **zero displacement**.

## Changing medium

| Quantity | Changes? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | No | Set by the source |
| Speed | Yes | Set by the medium |
| Wavelength | Yes, λ = v/f | Follows speed |

*Worked reminder:* 680 Hz sound, air 340 m s⁻¹ → λ = 0.50 m; in steel at 5100 m s⁻¹ → λ = 7.5 m.

## Sound and EM waves

**Sound**: longitudinal, mechanical, pressure variations; needs a medium; usually faster in solids than liquids, and in liquids than gases.

**EM waves**: transverse electric and magnetic field oscillations, perpendicular to each other and to travel; c in vacuum; slower in matter.

| Region | λ (order of magnitude) |
|---|---|
| Radio | ≥ 10⁻¹ m |
| Microwave | 10⁻³–10⁻¹ m |
| Infrared | 10⁻⁶–10⁻³ m |
| Visible | 4–7 × 10⁻⁷ m |
| Ultraviolet | 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁷ m |
| X-ray | 10⁻¹¹–10⁻⁸ m |
| Gamma | ≤ 10⁻¹² m |

## Must-know distinctions

| Don't confuse | Difference |
|---|---|
| Wave speed vs particle speed | Constant speed of the pattern vs varying speed of one particle |
| λ vs half λ | Crest to crest vs crest to trough |
| Snapshot vs history | Position graph (many particles, one time) vs time graph (one particle, many times) |
| Mechanical vs EM | Needs medium vs does not |
| Compression vs crest | Longitudinal high-pressure region vs transverse maximum displacement |

## Exam traps

- Period from a position graph, or wavelength from a time graph.
- Frequency "changes" in a new medium.
- Echo distance not halved.
- Unit prefixes: MHz = 10⁶ Hz, GHz = 10⁹ Hz, nm = 10⁻⁹ m.
- "Sound travels as particles moving to the listener."
- Missing units or prefixes in v = fλ calculations.
- Describing particle direction without reference to the direction of travel.
- Giving a region of the EM spectrum without calculating λ first.

## Quick self-test

1. Define wavelength.
2. Define a longitudinal wave.
3. A wave has period 4.0 ms. Calculate its frequency.
4. A wave of frequency 120 Hz has wavelength 2.5 m. Calculate its speed.
5. Which graph gives the wavelength of a wave?
6. Where is a particle on a transverse wave momentarily at rest?
7. A 440 Hz sound passes from air (340 m s⁻¹) into water (1500 m s⁻¹). What happens to its frequency and its wavelength?
8. Give two differences between sound and light.
9. Calculate the wavelength of EM radiation of frequency 6.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz and name the region.
10. A sonar echo returns after 0.20 s in water (1500 m s⁻¹). How far away is the reflector?
11. Where on a longitudinal wave is the centre of a compression, in terms of displacement?
12. A particle oscillates with amplitude 2.0 mm at 50 Hz. Calculate its maximum speed.

### Answers

1. The distance between two neighbouring points on a wave that are in phase, e.g. crest to crest.
2. A wave in which the particles oscillate parallel to the direction of energy transfer.
3. f = 1/(4.0 × 10⁻³) = **250 Hz**.
4. v = 120 × 2.5 = **300 m s⁻¹**.
5. The displacement–position graph.
6. At a crest or a trough (maximum displacement).
7. Frequency unchanged at 440 Hz; wavelength increases from 0.77 m to 3.4 m.
8. Any two: sound is longitudinal, light transverse; sound needs a medium, light does not; light is much faster; sound is a mechanical wave, light electromagnetic.
9. λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ / 6.0 × 10¹⁴ = **5.0 × 10⁻⁷ m**: visible.
10. 1500 × 0.20 / 2 = **150 m**.
11. At a point of zero displacement, with neighbours displaced towards it.
12. v_max = 2π × 50 × 2.0 × 10⁻³ = **0.63 m s⁻¹**.
