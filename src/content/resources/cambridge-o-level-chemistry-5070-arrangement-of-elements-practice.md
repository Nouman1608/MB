## Section A: short questions

**1.** State how the elements in the Periodic Table are ordered. **[1]**

**2.** An atom of element J has the electronic configuration 2,8,8,1.

**(a)** State the period and the group of J. **[2]**

**(b)** State the charge on the ion formed by J. **[1]**

**3.** Which statement explains why chlorine and bromine have similar chemical properties?

A: They have the same number of electron shells.

B: They have the same number of electrons in their outer shells.

C: They are both coloured.

D: They have similar relative atomic masses.

**[1]**

**4.** Write the formula of the compound formed between:

**(a)** magnesium and nitrogen **[1]**

**(b)** aluminium and sulfur **[1]**

**5.** Explain why the noble gases are not given an ion charge in a table of group numbers and ion charges. **[1]**

**6.** Iodine has a lower relative atomic mass than tellurium but is placed after it in the Periodic Table. Suggest why. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** The elements of Period 3 are sodium, magnesium, aluminium, silicon, phosphorus, sulfur, chlorine and argon.

**(a)** Describe how the character of the elements changes across Period 3. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the Period 3 element whose oxide is amphoteric. **[1]**

**(c)** Sulfur dioxide dissolves in water to give an acidic solution. Explain how this is consistent with the position of sulfur in Period 3. **[2]**

**8.** Strontium is in Group II, below calcium.

**(a)** Predict **two** properties of strontium, giving a reason for each based on its position. **[4]**

**(b)** Write the formula of strontium oxide and of strontium chloride. **[2]**

**9.** The table shows some data for Group I elements.

| Element | Melting point / °C |
|---|---|
| Lithium | 181 |
| Sodium | 98 |
| Potassium | 63 |
| Rubidium | 39 |
| Caesium | ? |

**(a)** Describe the trend in melting point down Group I. **[1]**

**(b)** Predict the melting point of caesium and justify your prediction. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest the state of rubidium on a hot day when the temperature is 40 °C. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**10.** Element Z forms an ion Z²⁻ that has the electronic configuration 2,8,8.

**(a)** Give the electronic configuration of an atom of Z. **[1]**

**(b)** State the group and period of Z and identify Z. **[3]**

**(c)** Predict whether Z is a good conductor of electricity. Give a reason. **[2]**

**11.** Explain, in terms of electronic configuration, why lithium, sodium and potassium all react with water to form a hydroxide with the same formula type and hydrogen. Include a balanced equation for one of the reactions. **[3]**

**12.** A student says: "Elements in the same group have identical chemical properties because they have the same electrons."

**(a)** Identify **two** errors in the student's statement. **[2]**

**(b)** Rewrite the statement so that it is correct. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** In order of increasing proton number (atomic number) [1].

**2. (a)** Period 4 (four occupied shells) [1]; Group I (one outer electron) [1].

**(b)** 1+ [1].

**3.** B [1].

**4. (a)** Mg₃N₂ [1] (Mg²⁺ and N³⁻ balance as 3 × 2+ = 2 × 3−).

**(b)** Al₂S₃ [1] (Al³⁺ and S²⁻ balance as 2 × 3+ = 3 × 2−).

**5.** They have a full outer shell, so they do not gain or lose electrons / do not form ions [1].

**6.** The table is ordered by proton number, not relative atomic mass [1]; iodine has a higher proton number than tellurium (53 compared with 52) / iodine has the chemical properties of Group VII so must sit with the halogens [1].

**7. (a)** Changes from metals on the left (sodium, magnesium, aluminium) [1] to non-metals on the right (silicon to chlorine), ending with a noble gas [1].

**(b)** Aluminium [1].

**(c)** Sulfur is on the right of the period, so it is a non-metal [1]; non-metal oxides are acidic [1].

**8. (a)** Any two properties, each with a reason [2 + 2]:
- It is a metal [1] because it is in Group II with magnesium and calcium / on the left of the table [1].
- It forms Sr²⁺ ions [1] because it has two outer electrons, like all Group II elements [1].
- Its oxide is basic [1] because it is a metal oxide, like calcium oxide [1].
- It conducts electricity [1] because it is a metal [1].
- It reacts with dilute acids to give a salt and hydrogen [1], as calcium and magnesium do [1].

**(b)** SrO [1]; SrCl₂ [1].

**9. (a)** Melting point decreases down the group [1].

**(b)** Any value below 39 °C [1] (the true value is about 28 °C), because the trend is a decrease and caesium is below rubidium [1].

**(c)** Liquid / molten (or melting) [1], because 40 °C is above its melting point of 39 °C [1].

**10. (a)** 2,8,6 [1].

**(b)** Group VI [1]; Period 3 [1]; sulfur [1].

**(c)** No / it is a poor conductor [1], because it is a non-metal (on the right of Period 3) [1].

**11.** Each atom has one electron in its outer shell [1]; reactions involve the outer electrons, so each atom loses one electron in the same way to form a 1+ ion [1]; for example 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂ (or the lithium or potassium equivalent, correctly balanced) [1].

**12. (a)** "Identical" is wrong: properties are similar, with trends down the group [1]; "the same electrons" is wrong: they have the same number of outer-shell electrons but different total numbers of electrons / shells [1].

**(b)** Elements in the same group have similar chemical properties [1] because they have the same number of electrons in their outer shell [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "same number of electrons" without the word **outer**.
- Swapping period and group when reading a configuration.
- Giving a precise prediction from irregular data with no justification; the direction of the trend is what earns the mark.
- Writing MgN or AlS: always balance the charges.
- Forgetting that a negative ion has gained electrons, so the atom's configuration has fewer electrons than the ion's.
