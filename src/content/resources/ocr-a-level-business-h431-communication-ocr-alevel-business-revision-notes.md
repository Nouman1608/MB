Condensed notes on the Communication content (5.3 on this site) in the area of study Human resource management within a business environment, OCR A Level Business H431, Version 2.1 (January 2026), final assessment summer 2027. ## What the specification lists

- Formal and informal communication; their use.
- Group norms; group and individual objectives; the impact of groups on communication.
- Verbal and non-verbal methods; factors affecting choice; their importance.
- Media: written, face-to-face, oral, social, electronic, mass; factors affecting choice; importance.
- Models of communication: source, message, media, barriers, receiver, feedback; usefulness.
- Impact of poor communication; effect on stakeholders; effectiveness in achieving objectives.

## Core recall

### Formal against informal

| | Formal | Informal |
|---|---|---|
| Channel | Official, through the structure | Personal and social |
| Record | Usually kept | Usually none |
| Speed | Can be slow | Fast |
| Risk | Slow or ignored | Rumour and distortion |
| Benefit | Accuracy; legal safety | Trust; early warning |

### Groups

- **Group norms:** unwritten rules on behaviour, enforced by group pressure.
- **Group objectives:** aims of the group; may differ from **individual objectives**.
- Effects: good norms improve information sharing; informal groups can block or filter management messages; poor norms (such as limiting output) hurt performance.

### Verbal and non-verbal

| Type | Includes | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Verbal | Spoken and written words | Most business messages |
| Non-verbal | Body language, tone, gestures, dress, silence, visual signs | Trusted more if it conflicts with the words |

### Media

| Medium | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Written | Record; detail | Slow; no immediate feedback |
| Face-to-face | Feedback; body language | Time; no record unless minuted |
| Oral (phone, announcement) | Quick; tone | No record; misheard |
| Social | Fast; reaches customers | Hard to control; reputation risk |
| Electronic (email, intranet, video) | Cheap per message; stored | Overload; tone misread |
| Mass (TV, radio, press) | Large audience | Costly; little feedback |

### Factors affecting the choice

Urgency; complexity; need for a record; need for feedback; cost; number and location of receivers; confidentiality; receiver's skills and access; image of the business.

### The communication model

Source → message → media → receiver, with **barriers** on the way and **feedback** returning.

- Barriers: jargon or language; wrong medium; overload; noise or distance; technology failure; status gaps; lack of trust; unclear or too long message.
- Useful for diagnosing failure and showing why feedback matters; simplifies real two-way communication.

### Impact of poor communication

Mistakes and rework; low motivation and trust; conflict; poor service; slow decisions; absence and turnover; damage to reputation.

## Worked reminder

Kestrel Distribution (invented).

- Email to 250 staff: 25 hours × £16 = £400; 8% misread = 20 × £90 = £1,800; total **£2,200**.
- Ten briefings: 125 hours × £16 = £2,000 + managers £140; 2% misread = 5 × £90 = £450; total **£2,590**.
- Email is cheaper by **£390**, but at a 14% misreading rate the email option costs £3,550, more than the briefings.
- Unclear order instructions: 4% of 5,000 orders at £35 = **£7,000** a month; at 1.5%, **£2,625**; saving **£4,375** a month; a £30,000 system pays back in about **6.9 months**.

## Evaluating communication: a checklist

1. What was the objective of the message?
2. Was the medium suited to urgency, complexity, cost and audience?
3. What barriers were present?
4. Was there feedback?
5. How were stakeholders affected?
6. Judgement: what would be best, and what does it depend on?

## Quick case practice

A manager (invented) must tell 60 staff about a change to a safety rule. Staff time costs £15 an hour and manager time £25 an hour.

- Email: each person takes 6 minutes, so 60 × 0.1 × £15 = **£90**.
- Three briefings of 20 staff, 30 minutes each: staff time 3 × 20 × 0.5 × £15 = £450; manager time 3 × 0.5 × £25 = £37.50; total **£487.50**.

Model response outline: email is much cheaper, but a safety change needs understanding, not just delivery; briefings allow questions and show that managers take the change seriously. The extra £397.50 is small compared with the cost of one accident. Recommend briefings, followed by an email as a written record.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Formal communication | Messages through official channels |
| Informal communication | Messages outside official channels |
| Grapevine | The informal network through which rumours spread |
| Group norm | An unwritten rule of behaviour in a group |
| Feedback | The receiver's response to the message |
| Barrier (noise) | Anything that blocks or distorts a message |
| Encoding and decoding | Putting a message into signals and interpreting it |

## Exam traps

- Verbal is not the same as oral; verbal includes written words.
- Both formal and informal communication are needed.
- Feedback completes the process; mention it.
- Do not assume email is always best; choose for the situation.
- Say how a group's norms affect messages, not just that groups exist.
- Quote the case when judging effectiveness.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between formal and informal communication.
2. What is a group norm?
3. Give one example of non-verbal communication.
4. Name the six parts of the communication model.
5. Give two barriers to communication.
6. Give one strength and one weakness of face-to-face communication.
7. A business pays 40 staff to attend a 30-minute briefing at £15 an hour. Calculate the staff time cost.
8. 3% of 800 messages are misunderstood, each costing £50. Calculate the cost.
9. Why might a manager choose a written medium for a safety rule?
10. Give one effect of poor communication on customers.

### Answers

1. Formal communication uses official channels and is often recorded; informal communication happens outside official channels through personal contact.
2. An unwritten rule or expectation about how group members behave.
3. Any one: body language; facial expression; tone of voice; gesture; dress.
4. Source, message, media, barriers, receiver, feedback.
5. Any two: jargon; information overload; poor medium; noise or distance; lack of trust; technology failure.
6. Strength: immediate feedback. Weakness: takes time and no record unless minuted.
7. 40 × 0.5 hours = 20 hours × £15 = **£300**.
8. 3% × 800 = 24; 24 × £50 = **£1,200**.
9. It gives a permanent record, can be detailed and can be checked later, which matters for legal and safety reasons.
10. Any one: wrong orders; slow replies; poor service leading to lost custom.
