**These notes condense key question 2.6 of Cambridge O Level History 2147: how secure the USSR's control over Eastern
Europe was from 1948 to about 1989.** For the full explanation, read the
[USSR control of Eastern Europe study guide](/resources/o-level-world-history-ussr-control-eastern-europe/); for exam
practice, use the [USSR control of Eastern Europe practice questions](/resources/o-level-world-history-ussr-control-eastern-europe-practice/).

## What held the bloc together

| Method | Example |
|---|---|
| Military | Soviet troops stationed in the satellites; Warsaw Pact from 1955 |
| Political | One-party rule by leaders approved in Moscow |
| Police | Secret police, such as the AVH in Hungary and the Stasi in East Germany |
| Economic | Comecon from 1949 tied trade to the USSR |
| Ideological | Censorship and control of education and the media |

## Condensed recall by focus point

**Hungary 1956: why opposition, and how did the USSR react?**
- Resentment of Rákosi's harsh rule and the AVH; poverty; Soviet control of the economy; attacks on the Church.
- Khrushchev's Secret Speech (February 1956) criticising Stalin raised hopes of change; protests in Poland that year
  won concessions.
- Budapest students' demonstration on 23 October; Stalin's statue toppled; Imre Nagy returned as prime minister.
- Nagy promised free elections and, on 1 November, withdrawal from the Warsaw Pact.
- Soviet tanks attacked Budapest on 4 November; Kádár installed; Nagy executed in 1958; mass flight to the West.
- No Western help: the Suez Crisis divided the West.

**Czechoslovakia 1968: why opposition, and how did the USSR react?**
- Economic stagnation and frustration with censorship.
- Dubček became party leader in January 1968: "socialism with a human face".
- The Prague Spring: censorship ended, freer discussion, the April Action Programme of reforms.
- Dubček promised to stay in the Warsaw Pact and Comecon.
- Brezhnev feared reform would spread; Warsaw Pact forces invaded on 20–21 August.
- Mostly non-violent resistance; Jan Palach burned himself to death in protest in January 1969.
- Dubček replaced by Husák in April 1969; the Brezhnev Doctrine justified intervention.

**How similar were 1956 and 1968?**
- Similar: both began with reform-minded communist leaders; both were crushed by force; the West did not intervene;
  both showed the limits Moscow would accept.
- Different: Hungary tried to leave the Warsaw Pact, Czechoslovakia did not; Hungarians fought with weapons, Czechs and
  Slovaks mostly resisted peacefully; Hungary was crushed by Soviet forces, Czechoslovakia by a joint Warsaw Pact
  force; casualties were much higher in Hungary.

**Why was the Berlin Wall built in 1961?**
- Millions of East Germans had left through West Berlin since 1949, many young and skilled.
- The loss damaged the East German economy and embarrassed communism.
- West Berlin was a showcase of Western prosperity inside the Eastern bloc.
- Khrushchev's demands for Western withdrawal from Berlin, including at the Vienna summit with Kennedy (June 1961),
  failed.
- The border was sealed on 13 August 1961; the wall stopped the exodus but became a symbol of repression.

**What was the significance of Solidarity?**
- Strikes at the Lenin Shipyard in Gdańsk in August 1980, led by Lech Wałęsa.
- Gdańsk Agreement (August 1980) recognised the first independent trade union in the bloc.
- Around ten million members; backed by the Catholic Church and Pope John Paul II.
- Martial law under Jaruzelski (December 1981); Solidarity banned but survived underground.
- Round Table talks and partly free elections in 1989, which Solidarity won; first non-communist-led government in the
  bloc.
- Showed a mass movement could survive and defeat a communist government.

**How far was Gorbachev responsible for the collapse?**
- Responsible: glasnost and perestroika encouraged criticism; withdrew from Afghanistan; troop cuts announced at the
  UN in December 1988; abandoned the Brezhnev Doctrine; did not intervene in 1989.
- Other factors: Soviet economic stagnation and the cost of the arms race; earlier opposition such as Solidarity;
  nationalism and religion; Western media and contacts; communist governments lacked legitimacy after 1956 and 1968.
- Judgement: Gorbachev removed the threat of force, which was the system's main support, but he did not plan its
  collapse.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Satellite state | Country formally independent but controlled by the USSR |
| De-Stalinisation | Khrushchev's criticism of Stalin and loosening of some controls |
| AVH | Hungarian secret police |
| Socialism with a human face | Dubček's aim of a freer, reformed communism |
| Action Programme | April 1968 plan of reforms in Czechoslovakia |
| Brezhnev Doctrine | Claim that socialist states could intervene where socialism was threatened |
| Brain drain | Loss of skilled workers, as from East Germany before 1961 |
| Solidarity | Polish independent trade union founded in 1980 |
| Martial law | Rule by the army, suspending normal rights |
| Glasnost | Gorbachev's policy of openness |
| Perestroika | Gorbachev's restructuring of the Soviet economy |
| Velvet Revolution | Peaceful end of communist rule in Czechoslovakia, 1989 |

## Exam traps

- **Saying Czechoslovakia tried to leave the Warsaw Pact.** Only Hungary announced that.
- **Calling the Berlin Wall a Soviet decision alone.** The East German government built it with Soviet approval.
- **Ending the Solidarity story in 1981.** Its significance comes from survival and victory in 1989.
- **Answering "how secure" with only the collapse.** Show strength (1956, 1961, 1968) and weakness (1980–89).

## Self-test

1. Name **three** methods the USSR used to control the satellite states.
2. Why did Khrushchev's Secret Speech encourage opposition in Hungary?
3. What did Nagy announce on 1 November 1956?
4. Why did the West not help Hungary?
5. What did "socialism with a human face" mean?
6. Why did Brezhnev decide to invade Czechoslovakia?
7. Give **one** similarity and **one** difference between 1956 and 1968.
8. Give **two** reasons why the Berlin Wall was built.
9. Why was Solidarity able to survive martial law?
10. Why was the Polish election of June 1989 significant?
11. Give **two** actions by Gorbachev that weakened Soviet control.
12. Give **one** factor other than Gorbachev that explains the collapse of 1989.

**Answers:** 1. Any three: Soviet troops; the Warsaw Pact; secret police; Comecon; approved one-party leaders;
censorship. 2. It criticised Stalin's crimes, suggesting change was now possible. 3. Hungary would leave the Warsaw Pact
and become neutral. 4. Britain and France were involved in the Suez Crisis, and intervening in the Soviet sphere risked
nuclear war. 5. A reformed communism with free speech and less central control, still inside the Warsaw Pact. 6. He
feared reform would spread to other satellites and weaken Soviet control. 7. Similarity: both crushed by force with no
Western help. Difference: Hungary tried to leave the Warsaw Pact;
Czechoslovakia did not. 8. Any two: to stop the brain drain; to protect the East German economy; to end the embarrassment of mass
flight; Khrushchev's failure to remove the West from Berlin. 9. It had mass membership and the support of the Catholic
Church, and it continued underground. 10. Solidarity won, leading to the first non-communist-led government in the bloc.
11. Any two: glasnost; perestroika; troop cuts; withdrawal from Afghanistan; abandoning the Brezhnev Doctrine; refusing
to send troops in 1989. 12. Any one: economic stagnation and the arms race; Solidarity; nationalism and religion; lack of
support for communist governments.
