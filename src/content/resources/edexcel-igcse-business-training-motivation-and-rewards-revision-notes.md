## 1. Training (2.3.1)

| Type | Where and how | Main purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Induction | At the start of a new job; introductions, rules, safety, systems | Settle the new employee in and show what is expected |
| On-the-job | At the workplace, doing real work with a colleague or supervisor | Learn job-specific skills with little lost output |
| Off-the-job | Away from the workplace: course, college, training room | Learn specialist or wider skills from experts |

### Health and safety compliance

- Businesses must follow the health and safety laws where they operate. The specification names no particular law.
- Training helps through induction on emergency routes, safe use of equipment and refreshers when methods change.
- Non-compliance risks injury, fines, legal action and reputation. Say who is protected: workers and the business.

### Benefits and limitations

| | Benefits | Limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Business | Better quality, fewer errors, flexible staff, less supervision, safer working, higher productivity | Fees, lost output while learning, trained staff may leave, wasted if the person is unwilling |
| Worker | New skills, confidence, promotion chances, feeling valued | Time and effort, may not suit them, skills make them attractive to other employers |

Method in steps for a "training worth it?" question:

1. Add the direct costs (fees) and indirect costs (wages and output lost).
2. Estimate the saving or gain, with a time period.
3. Compare, then comment on what the numbers leave out.
4. Reach a judgement if the command word asks for one.

## 2. Why motivation matters (2.4.1)

- Attracting employees: a good reward package widens the pool of applicants.
- Retaining employees: lower turnover, less recruitment and training cost.
- Productivity: output divided by workers; motivated staff tend to produce more per person.

Link these three together in an answer: attractive rewards bring in applicants, good treatment keeps them, and settled, skilled staff then produce more. A weak reward package can damage all three at once.

Standard-form calculations (the specification does not give formulas, so these are the usual forms):

| Measure | Form |
|---|---|
| Productivity | Output ÷ number of workers |
| Percentage change | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 |
| Staff turnover | Leavers ÷ average number of staff × 100 |
| Commission | Percentage × sales |
| Piece-rate pay | Rate per item × items made |
| Time-rate pay | Hourly rate × hours worked |

## 3. Theories at a glance (2.4.1)

The specification names Taylor, Maslow and Herzberg without describing them, so these are the standard forms of each theory.

| Theory | Core idea | What a business should do | Main weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taylor | Money is the main motivator; scientific management with simple tasks and payment by output | Study the task, train for the best method, pay a piece rate | Ignores non-money needs; dull work |
| Maslow | Five needs in a hierarchy; a higher need matters once a lower one is met | Identify the level and reward at that level | People do not always climb in order |
| Herzberg | Hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction; motivators create motivation | Fix hygiene first, then add motivators | Satisfaction is not always output |

Maslow's levels from the bottom: physiological, safety, social, esteem, self-actualisation.

Herzberg: hygiene factors include pay, conditions, job security, policies, supervisor relations. Motivators include achievement, recognition, responsibility, interesting work, advancement.

Must-know distinctions:

- Pay as a hygiene factor (Herzberg) versus pay as the main motivator (Taylor).
- Maslow's security need versus the social need: a permanent contract meets the first, a friendly team the second.
- Recognition is a motivator for Herzberg and sits at esteem for Maslow.

## 4. Financial methods (2.4.2)

| Method | Meaning | Key plus | Key minus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remuneration | Wage or salary for work | Dependable income | Not linked to effort |
| Bonus | Extra payment for hitting a target | Rewards results | Cost; target may seem unfair |
| Commission | Percentage of sales | Pushes sales effort | Pressure on customers; uneven income |
| Promotion | Move to a senior role | Career goal; esteem | Few posts |
| Fringe benefits | Extras such as company car, health cover, discounts | Attracts and retains | Cost; not valued by all |

## 5. Non-financial methods (2.4.2)

| Method | One-line definition | Best for | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job rotation | Moving between similar tasks | Boredom, covering absence | Tasks are no harder |
| Job enrichment | More demanding, responsible work | Higher needs, motivators | Training and perhaps pay |
| Autonomy | Freedom over how work is done | Capable staff, trust | Errors; weaker control |

Link each to theory: enrichment and autonomy match Herzberg's motivators and Maslow's upper levels; commission and piece rate match Taylor.

## 6. Evaluation frame

For "evaluate" or "justify" questions use this pattern.

1. State the option and one clear benefit, using a figure from the case.
2. Give a limitation or risk for the same option.
3. Repeat for the alternative.
4. Weigh them against the business's size, cost, staff and goals.
5. Give a decision and a reason that refers back to the case.

## 7. Worked reminders

### Pay for a month

A sales assistant at Norwick Tiles earns a basic 1,300 plus 3% commission on sales of 41,000, and a 150 bonus for beating a 40,000 target.

1. Commission: 3% × 41,000 = 1,230.
2. Basic plus commission: 1,300 + 1,230 = 2,530.
3. Sales are above 40,000, so add the bonus: 2,530 + 150 = **2,680**.

### Matching a problem to a method

| Problem in the case | Likely theory | Sensible method | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Staff are bored by repeating one task | Herzberg (interesting work) | Job rotation, then job enrichment | Variety first; depth for lasting effect |
| Good staff leave for a rival offering more | Taylor or Herzberg (pay as hygiene) | Raise remuneration or add fringe benefits | Removes dissatisfaction and helps retention |
| Experienced staff want more say | Maslow (esteem, self-actualisation) | Autonomy or promotion | Meets higher needs |
| Sales team lacks drive | Taylor | Commission or bonus | Links pay to results |
| Staff make safety errors | Compliance | Induction and refresher training | Protects staff and the business |

Check for this month: 0.03 × 41,000 = 1,230, then 1,300 + 1,230 + 150 = 2,680.

### Five-second checks before you write

- Is the answer tied to the business in the question?
- Did you give a reason after every point (because, so, which means)?
- Does a number need units or a percentage sign?
- If the command word is "evaluate" or "justify", have you weighed both sides?

## 8. Quick self-test

1. State the main purpose of induction training.
2. Name two hygiene factors in Herzberg's theory.
3. A salesperson sells 24,000 of goods and earns 5% commission. Calculate the commission.
4. A worker makes 1,200 items at a piece rate of 0.45. Calculate pay.
5. A factory makes 2,250 units with 15 workers. Calculate output per worker.
6. Output per worker rises from 150 to 162. Calculate the percentage change.
7. Six staff each attend a course with a fee of 300 and lose 2 days of work, each day costing 80. Calculate the total cost.
8. Six of 40 staff leave in a year. Calculate staff turnover.
9. Which Maslow level does promotion most directly meet?
10. Give the difference between job rotation and job enrichment.
11. State two limitations of off-the-job training for a business.
12. Explain one reason a business might offer fringe benefits.

### Answers

1. To help a new employee settle in and learn what is expected.
2. Any two of pay, working conditions, job security, company policies, relations with supervisors.
3. 5% × 24,000 = **1,200**.
4. 1,200 × 0.45 = **540**.
5. 2,250 ÷ 15 = **150 units**.
6. (162 − 150) ÷ 150 × 100 = **8%**.
7. Fees 6 × 300 = 1,800; time 6 × 2 × 80 = 960; total **2,760**.
8. 6 ÷ 40 × 100 = **15%**.
9. Esteem (status and recognition).
10. Rotation moves staff between tasks of a similar level; enrichment makes the job itself more demanding with more responsibility.
11. Any two of fees, lost output while away, staff may leave afterwards, skills not used at work.
12. They can attract and retain staff, because benefits add to the reward package, which lowers recruitment cost.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing about the topic in general when the question gives a named business.
- Defining a term but not explaining its effect on the business.
- Mixing up bonus and commission, or rotation and enrichment.
- Saying Herzberg treats pay as the best motivator.
- Stating a limitation of training with no link to cost, time or staff turnover.
- Rounding a percentage change without showing the working.
- Giving a one-sided answer to "evaluate" or "justify".
- Using the word "motivated" without saying what it changes, such as output, quality or retention.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between induction, on-the-job and off-the-job training?**
Induction helps a new starter settle in and learn the rules and safety procedures. On-the-job training builds job-specific skills at the workplace, while off-the-job training teaches wider or specialist skills away from the work area.

**What are Herzberg's hygiene factors and motivators?**
Hygiene factors, such as pay, working conditions and job security, cause dissatisfaction if poor but do not motivate when good. Motivators, such as achievement, recognition, responsibility and interesting work, give real motivation.

**What is the difference between a bonus and commission?**
Commission is a percentage of sales made, usually paid on top of basic pay. A bonus is an extra one-off payment for meeting a target, such as a profit or quality target.

**What are the non-financial methods of motivation in Edexcel IGCSE Business?**
The 4BS1 specification lists job rotation, job enrichment and autonomy. Job rotation adds variety, job enrichment adds depth and autonomy adds freedom.
