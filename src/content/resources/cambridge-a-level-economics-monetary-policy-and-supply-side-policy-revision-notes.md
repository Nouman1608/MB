## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Monetary policy (5.3.1) | Changes by the government or central bank to interest rates, the money supply or credit regulations, aimed at aggregate demand and macroeconomic objectives |
| Expansionary monetary policy | Lower interest rates, more money, looser credit rules: AD shifts right |
| Contractionary monetary policy | Higher interest rates, less money, tighter credit rules: AD shifts left |
| Real interest rate | Nominal rate minus inflation rate (approximately) |
| Supply-side policy (5.4.1) | Policy that raises the economy's capacity to produce, shifting the LRAS curve right |
| Productivity | Output per worker (or per unit of input) |
| Productive capacity | The maximum output the economy can produce with its resources |

## Monetary policy at a glance (5.3.2 to 5.3.3)

| Tool | Expansionary | Contractionary | Main channel to AD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interest rates | Cut | Raise | Consumption (borrowing and saving) and investment |
| Money supply | Increase | Reduce | Lower or higher interest rates, more or less bank lending |
| Credit regulations | Relax (higher loan-to-value cap) | Tighten (lower cap) | Fewer or more people can borrow |

### Chain of reasoning: a rate cut

1. The central bank cuts the policy rate.
2. Bank lending rates fall, so borrowing is cheaper and saving is less rewarding.
3. Households spend more and firms invest in more projects.
4. Consumption (C) and investment (I) rise, so AD shifts right.
5. Real output, the price level and employment rise (if there is spare capacity).

Reverse every step for a rate rise.

## Method boxes

**Interest cost.** Interest = loan x rate. Compare the old and new bills.

**Real interest rate.** Real rate = nominal rate - inflation rate. Example: 5.5% - 2.0% = 3.5%.

**Loan-to-value.** Maximum loan = price x cap. Deposit = price - loan. Example: price 400,000 cu, cap 85%, loan 340,000 cu, deposit 60,000 cu.

**Money market.** Draw demand for money sloping down and supply vertical. A rise in supply moves the equilibrium rate down. Example: demand L = 480 - 30r. A supply of 330 gives r = 5%. A supply of 360 gives r = 4%.

**New AD/AS equilibrium.** Set AD equal to SRAS, solve for P, then substitute to find Y. Always give the percentage change in each variable.

## AD/AS analysis (5.3.4 and 5.4.4)

| Policy | Curve that moves | Real output | Price level | Employment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Expansionary monetary | AD right | Up | Up | Up |
| Contractionary monetary | AD left | Down | Down (or rises more slowly) | Down |
| Supply-side | LRAS right (and often SRAS right) | Up | Down (or rises more slowly) | Up |

Label axes as price level and real output. Name each curve (AD1, AD2, SRAS, LRAS) and each equilibrium.

**Worked reminder.** Country R: AD is Y = 900 - 2P and SRAS is Y = 300 + 4P.

- Equilibrium: 6P = 600, so P = 100 and Y = 700.
- Expansionary policy raises AD by 60: Y = 960 - 2P. Then 6P = 660, so P = 110 and Y = 740.
- Output is up 5.7% (40 / 700) and the price level is up 10% (10 / 100).

When there is little spare capacity, the price rise is a larger share of the effect.

## Supply-side policy (5.4.2 and 5.4.3)

**Objectives:** raise productivity and raise productive capacity.

| Tool | How it works | Example of effect |
|---|---|---|
| Training and education | Raises the skill and output of labour | 4 m of 25 m workers gain 10%: output per worker up 1.6% |
| Infrastructure development | Cuts travel time and costs, links markets | Travel falls from 25 to 15 minutes: 12 deliveries a day become 16 |
| Support for technological improvement | Subsidies or grants lower the cost of research and investment | A 30% subsidy on 500,000 cu of research: the firm pays 350,000 cu |

Other valid measures include better health care, tax incentives for investment and lower barriers for new firms. Each must be linked to productivity or capacity to earn credit.

### Chain of reasoning: a training programme

1. Government funds training.
2. Workers' skills improve, so output per worker rises.
3. Unit costs fall, so SRAS shifts right.
4. Capacity increases, so LRAS shifts right.
5. Potential output rises, the price level is lower than it would be, and employment can rise.

**Worked reminder (supply-side).** Country R again: AD is Y = 900 - 2P. After a supply-side policy lowers costs, SRAS moves from Y = 300 + 4P to Y = 360 + 4P.

- New equilibrium: 6P = 540, so P = 90 and Y = 720.
- Output is up 20 (2.9%) and the price level is down 10 (10%).
- Contrast with the expansionary case above, where both rose.

### Percentage change in capacity

If full-capacity output rises from 800 to 840 cu bn, the increase is 40 / 800 = 5%.

## Answering a policy question

1. Name the policy and the tool or measure.
2. Say which curve moves (AD, SRAS or LRAS) and in which direction.
3. State what happens to output, prices and jobs, using numbers if the question gives any.
4. Add a reason why the effect may be weaker, slower or stronger than expected.
5. Finish with a judgement tied to the situation in the question.

## Must-know distinctions

| Monetary policy | Supply-side policy |
|---|---|
| Demand-side: shifts AD | Supply-side: shifts AS |
| Effect is usually quicker, but the price level rises when output rises | Effect is usually slower, but the price level falls when output rises |
| Changes spending | Changes capacity and costs |
| Rates and credit rules can be changed again | Spending on skills and roads cannot be undone |

Also: a price-level change moves the economy along a curve, while a change in any other factor shifts the curve.

## Evaluation bank

- **Monetary:** time lags, low confidence, spare capacity, fixed-rate debts, borrowers versus savers, demand-pull versus cost-push inflation.
- **Supply-side:** long time lags, high cost, uncertain take-up, needs demand to use the extra capacity.
- Judge by size, timing, cause of the problem and who gains and loses. End with a conclusion that depends on stated conditions.

## Quick self-test

1. A nominal interest rate is 5.5% and inflation is 2.0%. What is the real rate?
2. A firm has an 80,000 cu interest-only loan. The rate falls from 7% to 4%. By how much does the annual interest bill change?
3. A house costs 400,000 cu and the loan-to-value cap is 85%. Find the maximum loan and minimum deposit.
4. Demand for money is L = 480 - 30r. The money supply rises from 330 to 360. What happens to the equilibrium interest rate?
5. Name the three tools of monetary policy in the syllabus.
6. In Country R above, what are P and Y after AD rises by 60 at every price?
7. A country has 6 million workers producing 210 bn cu. Find output per worker. If it rises 4%, find new total output with the same workforce.
8. Full-capacity output rises from 800 to 840. Find the percentage change.
9. State which curve moves first when a government funds a road-building programme to cut costs for firms.
10. Give two reasons expansionary monetary policy might fail to raise output.

### Answers

1. 5.5 - 2.0 = **3.5%**.
2. 80,000 x 0.07 = 5,600 and 80,000 x 0.04 = 3,200. The bill **falls by 2,400 cu**.
3. 400,000 x 0.85 = **340,000 cu** loan, so the deposit is **60,000 cu**.
4. At 330: 480 - 30r = 330 gives r = 5. At 360: r = 4. The rate **falls from 5% to 4%**.
5. **Interest rates, money supply, credit regulations.**
6. **P = 110, Y = 740.**
7. 210 bn / 6 m = **35,000 cu**. At 36,400 per worker, output is **218.4 bn cu**.
8. 40 / 800 = **5%**.
9. SRAS shifts right as costs fall; the improvement in capacity then moves **LRAS right**.
10. Any two: low confidence, banks unwilling to lend, fixed-rate debts, time lags, no spare demand from abroad, cost-push causes.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "demand rises" without saying that AD shifts and which component changes.
- Mixing up the effect of a rate cut on savers (less incentive) and on borrowers (more spending).
- Drawing a shift of AD for a supply-side policy.
- Quoting percentage changes without showing the working.
- Stating the tools of monetary policy without explaining the chain to output, prices and jobs.
- Ignoring spare capacity when predicting the price effect.
- Giving an evaluation point with no reason, such as "time lags" with no explanation of why.
- Omitting a conclusion that answers the question set.
