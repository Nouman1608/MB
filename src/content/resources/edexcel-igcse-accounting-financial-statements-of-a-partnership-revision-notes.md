## Nature of a partnership and an LLP (points a and b)

| Term | Remember |
|---|---|
| Partnership | Two or more owners sharing profit or loss, usually under a written agreement |
| Reasons to form | Extra capital, a mix of skills, shared workload and risk, someone to cover absences |
| Ordinary partnership liability | Unlimited. Personal assets can be used to pay the firm's debts |
| Limited liability partnership (LLP) | A separate legal person (general knowledge). Its members have limited liability, so personal assets are generally protected |
| LLP structure | The owners are called members. Profit is shared as the members agree |

An answer that compares the two should state the difference in liability and then say why it matters: an LLP member's personal assets are not at risk in the way an ordinary partner's are, so the LLP form suits owners who want to limit what they could lose.

## Section 24 defaults (point c)

These apply when there is no agreement, or the agreement is silent on a point. An agreement beats them. The table gives general knowledge of the Act and the usual textbook position, not specification wording.

| Item | Default under Section 24 of the Partnership Act 1890 |
|---|---|
| Salaries | None |
| Profit or loss | Shared equally |
| Interest on a partner's loan | 5% a year |
| Interest on capital | None |
| Interest on drawings | None charged unless agreed |

## The appropriation account (points d and e)

**Purpose:** it shows how the profit for the year is divided between partners. It follows the income statement and starts from profit for the year.

Layout to learn:

| Line | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Profit for the year | Starting figure |
| Add interest on drawings | Added, because partners are charged it |
| Less interest on capital | Fixed amount for each partner |
| Less salaries | Fixed amount for each partner |
| = Residual profit or deficit | Shared in the profit-sharing ratio |

Method in steps:

1. Finish the income statement, including interest on any partner's loan as an expense.
2. Write profit for the year at the top of the appropriation account.
3. Add interest on drawings.
4. Deduct interest on capital and salaries, partner by partner.
5. Find the residual and split it in the ratio.
6. Check: total interest on capital, salaries and shares of residual, less interest on drawings, must equal profit for the year.

## Capital and current accounts (point f)

| Account | Typical entries |
|---|---|
| Capital (fixed) | Capital paid in or withdrawn on a long-term basis. The balance carried down is the capital shown in the statement of financial position |
| Current | Credit: interest on capital, salary, share of profit. Debit: drawings, interest on drawings, share of loss. The balance can be credit or debit |

A credit balance on a current account means the firm owes the partner. A debit balance means the partner has taken out more than they have earned from the firm.

## Statement of financial position (point g)

The equity section lists each partner's capital, then each partner's current account. A credit current account balance is added. A debit balance is deducted. Total equity must equal net assets.

## Worked reminder A: three partners

Sundial Row Optics is owned by Dalia Haddad, Kwame Osei and Irina Volkov. Profit for the year is 48,000. The agreement gives:

- interest on capital at 5% (capitals 50,000, 40,000 and 30,000);
- a salary of 9,000 to Kwame;

## Worked reminder B: when the residual is a deficit

Ember Court Catering is owned by Pradeep Nair and Chiara Lund, who share equally. Profit for the year is only 5,500. The agreement gives Chiara a salary of 8,000 and 3,000 of interest on capital (Pradeep 1,800 and Chiara 1,200).

| Line | Pradeep | Chiara | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Profit for the year | | | 5,500 |
| Less interest on capital | 1,800 | 1,200 | (3,000) |
| Less salary | | 8,000 | (8,000) |
| Deficit | | | (5,500) |
| Share of deficit, equally | (2,750) | (2,750) | (5,500) |

Net effect on the current accounts: Pradeep 1,800 - 2,750 = 950 debit and Chiara 1,200 + 8,000 - 2,750 = 6,450 credit. The two net effects add to the 5,500 profit. The firm still honours the agreed salary and interest, and the partners bear the deficit in the ratio.

## Must-know distinctions

Learn these as pairs. Questions can test whether you can tell the two halves apart.

- **Loan interest or interest on capital.** Loan interest is an expense in the income statement. Interest on capital is an appropriation.
- **Drawings or interest on drawings.** Drawings go to the current account only. Interest on drawings is a charge that increases the profit shared.
- **Capital account or current account.** Capital is the long-term investment. The current account is the yearly running balance.
- **Income statement or appropriation account.** The first measures profit. The second shares it.
- **Fixed amounts or the ratio.** Salaries and interest are fixed. Only the residual follows the ratio.

## Quick self-test

1. A partner with no agreement lends 8,000 to the firm for a full year. How much interest is due, and where is it shown?
2. Under Section 24, how is a profit shared when the partners have said nothing?
3. Calculate interest on capital at 8% on a capital of 25,000.
4. Drawings of 7,200 were taken evenly through the year and the agreement charges 6% a year. Calculate interest on drawings.
5. On which side of a current account are drawings recorded?
6. A residual of 12,000 is shared 3 : 1. What does each partner receive?
7. A current account has an opening credit of 900, interest on capital 1,500, a share of profit 4,200 and drawings of 6,000. Find the closing balance.
8. Explain what happens if the agreed salary is larger than the profit for the year.
9. State one difference between an LLP and an ordinary partnership.
10. How is a debit current account balance shown in the statement of financial position?
11. Give two reasons for forming a partnership rather than trading alone.
12. Why is interest on a partner's loan not found in the appropriation account?

### Answers

1. 8,000 x 5% = 400. It is an expense in the income statement.
2. Equally, and losses are shared equally too.
3. 25,000 x 8% = 2,000.
4. 7,200 x 6% x 6/12 = 216.
5. The debit side.
6. 9,000 and 3,000.
7. 900 + 1,500 + 4,200 - 6,000 = 600 credit.
8. The salary is still credited to the partner. The residual becomes a deficit, shared in the ratio.
9. Members of an LLP have limited liability, whereas partners in an ordinary partnership have unlimited liability.
10. It is deducted in the equity section.
11. For example more capital and shared skills.
12. It is a cost of using money borrowed from a partner, so it is charged before the profit for the year is found.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting loan interest in the appropriation account instead of the income statement.
- Subtracting interest on drawings instead of adding it.
- Sharing salary and interest on capital in the ratio, rather than as fixed amounts.
- Forgetting that a debit opening balance on a current account reduces the closing balance.
- Treating a negative residual as a reason to drop salary and interest. They are still credited.
- Misreading "evenly through the year" and charging interest for twelve months.
- Writing "limited liability" for an ordinary partnership.
- Showing a current account in equity but leaving the capital accounts out, so total equity does not match net assets.
