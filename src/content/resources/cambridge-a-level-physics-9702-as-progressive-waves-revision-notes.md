Condensed notes on subtopic 7.1 Progressive waves (Cambridge 9702, AS Topic 7).
For full explanations and worked examples, use the
[progressive waves study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-progressive-waves/);
for the whole topic, see the [Waves revision notes](/resources/as-physics-waves-revision-notes/).

## What 7.1 asks you to do

- Describe wave motion in ropes, springs and ripple tanks
- Use displacement, amplitude, phase difference, period, frequency, wavelength and speed
- Use a CRO's time-base and y-gain to find frequency and amplitude
- Derive v = fλ from the definitions; recall and use it
- Understand that a progressive wave transfers energy
- Use intensity = power/area and intensity ∝ (amplitude)²

## Wave motion in one sentence

Particles of the medium **oscillate about fixed equilibrium positions**, each slightly behind the previous one; **energy** travels along the wave, but the medium does not.

| Demonstration | What you see | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Rope flicked sideways | Ribbon on the rope moves up and down only | Transverse oscillation, energy travels along |
| Spring pushed and pulled along its axis | Coils bunch and spread; a marked coil moves to and fro | Longitudinal oscillation |
| Ripple tank | Cork bobs in place as ripples pass | Medium oscillates; energy moves outwards |

## Equations

```
f = 1 / T
v = f lambda
phase difference = (dx / lambda) x 360 deg  = (dx / lambda) x 2 pi rad
phase difference = (dt / T) x 360 deg       (same point, two times)
intensity = power / area       W m^-2
intensity proportional to amplitude^2
point source:  intensity = P / (4 pi r^2)   (spreading equally in all directions)
```

## Derivation of v = fλ

1. In one period T, the wave advances one wavelength λ.
2. Speed = distance ÷ time = λ/T.
3. f = 1/T, so v = fλ.

## Phase difference quick values

| Separation | Phase difference |
|---|---|
| λ (or nλ) | 0°, in phase |
| λ/2 | 180° (π rad), antiphase |
| λ/4 | 90° (π/2 rad) |
| 3λ/4 | 270° (3π/2 rad) |

**Example.** λ = 0.60 m; points 0.15 m apart: 0.15/0.60 = ¼ cycle = **90°** (π/2 rad).

## CRO checklist

1. Count divisions (peak to centre for amplitude, or peak to peak ÷ 2).
2. Multiply by the y-gain (V div⁻¹) → amplitude in volts.
3. Count divisions for several whole cycles, divide → divisions per cycle.
4. Multiply by the time-base (s div⁻¹) → period T; then f = 1/T.
5. Two traces: offset in divisions ÷ divisions per cycle × 360° = phase difference.

## Intensity scaling

- Amplitude × 2 → intensity × 4; amplitude ÷ 2 → intensity ÷ 4.
- Point source, distance × 2 → intensity ÷ 4 → amplitude ÷ 2.
- Intensity always measured on an area **perpendicular** to the direction of travel.

**Example — CRO.** Time-base 2.0 ms div⁻¹, y-gain 0.50 V div⁻¹. Four complete cycles span 10 divisions; peak to trough is 6.4 divisions.

```
period    = (10 / 4) x 2.0 ms = 5.0 ms     f = 1 / 0.0050 = 200 Hz
amplitude = (6.4 / 2) x 0.50 V = 1.6 V
```

**Example — intensity and amplitude.** A wave of amplitude 3.0 mm has intensity 0.36 W m⁻². At amplitude 1.0 mm the intensity is 0.36 × (1.0/3.0)² = **0.040 W m⁻²**.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition to recall |
|---|---|
| Progressive wave | Wave that transfers energy from one place to another |
| Displacement | Distance of a particle from its equilibrium position in a given direction |
| Amplitude | Maximum displacement from equilibrium |
| Period | Time for one complete oscillation |
| Frequency | Number of oscillations per unit time |
| Wavelength | Distance between adjacent points oscillating in phase |
| Wave speed | Distance travelled by the wave per unit time |
| Phase difference | Fraction of a cycle between two oscillations, as an angle |
| Intensity | Power per unit area, perpendicular to the direction of travel |

## Exam traps

- Amplitude taken as crest-to-trough height.
- Wavelength read from a displacement–time graph (that gives the period).
- Time-base in ms div⁻¹ not converted to seconds.
- Intensity ∝ amplitude instead of amplitude².
- Changing frequency "changes the speed": in the same medium only λ changes.
- Wavelength defined as "length of one wave" (not credited).
- Phase difference given without a unit, or in metres (that is path difference).

## Self-test

1. Define wavelength.
2. State the relationship between frequency and period.
3. Sound of frequency 680 Hz travels at 340 m s⁻¹. Find the wavelength.
4. Find the period of a 2.5 kHz signal.
5. Two points on a wave of wavelength 1.2 m are 0.30 m apart. Find their phase difference.
6. A point on a wave of period 20 ms is observed twice, 5.0 ms apart. Find the phase difference between the two observations.
7. On a CRO with y-gain 2.0 V div⁻¹, a trace peaks 3.5 divisions above the centre line. Find the amplitude.
8. With the time-base at 10 ms div⁻¹, one cycle spans 4.0 divisions. Find the frequency.
9. The amplitude of a wave halves. What happens to its intensity?
10. A wave of intensity 0.20 W m⁻² falls normally on an area of 0.050 m². Find the power received.
11. Derive v = fλ.
12. The distance from a point source triples. By what factor does the intensity change?

**Answers:**
1. The distance between two adjacent points oscillating in phase.
2. f = 1/T.
3. λ = 340 ÷ 680 = **0.50 m**.
4. T = 1 ÷ 2500 = **4.0 × 10⁻⁴ s**.
5. 0.30/1.2 = ¼ cycle = **90°** (π/2 rad).
6. 5.0/20 = ¼ cycle = **90°**.
7. 3.5 × 2.0 = **7.0 V**.
8. T = 4.0 × 10 ms = 0.040 s; f = **25 Hz**.
9. It falls to **one quarter**.
10. 0.20 × 0.050 = **0.010 W**.
11. In one period T the wave travels one wavelength λ, so v = λ/T; f = 1/T, so v = fλ.
12. Intensity ∝ 1/r², so it falls to **1/9**.
