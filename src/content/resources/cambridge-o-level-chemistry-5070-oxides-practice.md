## Section A

**1.** Define an amphoteric oxide. **[2]**

**2.** Classify each oxide as acidic, basic or amphoteric: magnesium oxide; sulfur dioxide; aluminium oxide; carbon dioxide. **[4]**

**3.** State the type of element that usually forms basic oxides. **[1]**

**4.** Which oxide reacts with aqueous sodium hydroxide but not with dilute hydrochloric acid?

A: calcium oxide

B: carbon dioxide

C: zinc oxide

D: copper(II) oxide **[1]**

**5.** Construct the balanced equation for the reaction of copper(II) oxide with dilute sulfuric acid, and name the salt formed. **[2]**

**6.** Carbon dioxide is bubbled through limewater, aqueous calcium hydroxide. State what is observed, and name the type of reaction between an acidic oxide and an alkali. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Zinc oxide is amphoteric. Construct balanced equations for its reactions with dilute hydrochloric acid and with aqueous sodium hydroxide (which forms sodium zincate, Na₂ZnO₂), and explain how they show it is amphoteric. **[4]**

**8.** Four oxides are tested.

| Oxide | Dilute HCl | Dilute NaOH | Water + universal indicator |
|---|---|---|---|
| E | Dissolves | No reaction | Insoluble |
| F | No reaction | Reacts | Insoluble |
| G | Dissolves | Dissolves | Insoluble |
| H | No reaction | Reacts | Dissolves; pH 2 |

Classify each oxide as acidic, basic or amphoteric. **[4]**

**9.** Describe and explain the change in the type of oxide formed by the elements across Period 3, from sodium to sulfur. **[3]**

**10.** Calcium oxide is added to water.

**(a)** Construct the equation for the reaction.

**(b)** State the colour of universal indicator in the resulting solution, and whether the pH is above or below 7.

**(c)** Explain why calcium oxide is used to remove sulfur dioxide from power-station waste gases. **[5]**

**11.** In the blast furnace, calcium oxide reacts with silicon(IV) oxide to form slag: CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃. Classify each oxide and explain why they react with each other. **[3]**

**12.** A student plans to classify aluminium oxide by adding it to water containing universal indicator. Explain why this will not work, and describe a better method, with the expected results. **[3]**

**13.** Element X is a metal in Group II. Predict the type of oxide it forms, and construct a general equation for its oxide, XO, reacting with hydrochloric acid. **[3]**

**14.** Sulfur dioxide from burning fuels dissolves in rainwater.

**(a)** State the effect of dissolved sulfur dioxide on the pH of rainwater.

**(b)** Use the type of oxide to explain your answer. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** An oxide that reacts with both acids and bases [1] to produce a salt and water [1].

**2.** Magnesium oxide: basic [1]. Sulfur dioxide: acidic [1]. Aluminium oxide: amphoteric [1]. Carbon dioxide: acidic [1].

**3.** A metal [1].

**4.** **B** [1].

**5.** CuO + H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + H₂O [1]; copper(II) sulfate [1].

**6.** The limewater turns milky / a white precipitate forms [1]; neutralisation [1].

**7.** ZnO + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂O [1]. ZnO + 2NaOH → Na₂ZnO₂ + H₂O [1]. Zinc oxide reacts with both an acid and a base [1], and each reaction produces a salt and water, which is the definition of amphoteric [1].

**8.** E: basic [1]. F: acidic [1]. G: amphoteric [1]. H: acidic [1].

**9.** Sodium and magnesium form basic oxides, aluminium forms an amphoteric oxide, and silicon, phosphorus and sulfur form acidic oxides [1]. The elements change from metals to non-metals across the period [1]; metals form basic oxides and non-metals form acidic oxides [1].

**10. (a)** CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ [1].

**(b)** Blue / purple [1]; above 7 [1].

**(c)** Calcium oxide is a basic oxide and sulfur dioxide is an acidic oxide [1], so they react together (forming a salt), and the sulfur dioxide is not released into the air, reducing acid rain [1].

**11.** Calcium oxide is basic (metal oxide) [1]. Silicon(IV) oxide is acidic (non-metal oxide) [1]. A basic oxide reacts with an acidic oxide, forming a salt, calcium silicate [1].

**12.** Aluminium oxide is insoluble in water, so the indicator will not change [1]. Instead, add samples separately to dilute acid and to dilute sodium hydroxide [1]; it reacts with / dissolves in both, showing it is amphoteric [1].

**13.** Basic oxide, because X is a metal [1]. XO + 2HCl → XCl₂ + H₂O: correct products [1]; balanced [1].

**14. (a)** The pH falls / goes below 7 [1].

**(b)** Sulfur is a non-metal, so sulfur dioxide is an acidic oxide, which forms an acidic solution in water [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: writing "reacts with acids and bases" without "to produce a salt and water".
- Question 2: classifying aluminium oxide as basic because aluminium is a metal.
- Question 5: naming the salt "copper sulfate" without the (II). Copper forms more than one oxidation state, so the numeral is expected.
- Question 7: writing ZnO + NaOH → NaZnO + H₂O. Use the formula given for sodium zincate and balance with 2NaOH.
- Question 8: classifying H as basic because it dissolves in water. pH 2 shows an acidic solution.
- Question 9: describing the pattern without explaining it using metallic and non-metallic character.
- Question 10(c): saying calcium oxide "absorbs" sulfur dioxide without explaining the acid–base reaction.
- Question 13: writing XCl for the chloride. A Group II metal forms X²⁺ ions, so the formula is XCl₂.
- Question 14(a): writing "the pH rises" because the rain "becomes more acidic". More acidic means a lower pH.
- Question 12: suggesting a stronger indicator or more water. No amount of water dissolves aluminium oxide; the acid and alkali tests are needed.
