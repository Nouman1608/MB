## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by *bycatch*. **[1]**

**2.** Name **two** types of animal that are often caught as bycatch. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** impacts of marine aquaculture that benefit people. **[2]**

**4.** State what is meant by a *fishing quota*. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why pole and line is described as a sustainable fishing method. **[2]**

**6.** Name the harvesting strategy that bans fishing during the breeding period of a species. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** The table shows the catch of one fish species and the number of boats fishing for it. The figures are invented for practice.

| Year | 1990 | 1995 | 2000 | 2005 | 2010 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Catch (tonnes) | 52,000 | 61,000 | 58,000 | 39,000 | 18,000 |
| Number of boats | 120 | 150 | 190 | 230 | 240 |

(a) Describe the change in the catch between 1990 and 2010. **[2]**
(b) Calculate the percentage decrease in the catch between 2000 and 2010, to one decimal place. **[2]**
(c) Explain why the catch fell even though the number of boats increased. **[2]**

**8.** In a sea area, small shoaling fish are eaten by larger fish and by seabirds. A new industry catches very large numbers of the small fish to make animal feed. Explain how this could affect the food chain. **[3]**

**9.** A fish farm produces 2,400 tonnes of fish a year using 3,000 tonnes of feed. 40% of the feed is made from wild-caught fish.
(a) Calculate the mass of wild-caught fish used in the feed each year. **[1]**
(b) Explain whether this farm reduces the pressure on wild fish stocks. **[2]**

**10.** A company clears an area of coastal mangrove forest to build ponds for farming prawns. Explain **two** negative impacts of the prawn farm on the local marine ecosystem. **[4]**

**11.** Compare the environmental impacts of a seaweed farm and a fish farm of the same size. **[3]**

**12.** Several neighbouring countries share a fishing ground where stocks are falling. Discuss the benefits and limitations of protected areas, quotas and international agreements for managing the stock. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Species caught unintentionally when fishing for a target species **[1]**.

**2.** Any two of turtles, dolphins, sharks, seabirds or young fish of other species: first **[1]**; second **[1]**.

**3.** Increased food supply for humans **[1]**; reduced exploitation of natural fisheries, or jobs and income **[1]**.

**4.** The maximum amount of a species that may be caught in a set period **[1]**.

**5.** Fish are caught one at a time, so there is very little bycatch **[1]**, and unwanted or undersized fish can be released alive, so the stock is not overexploited **[1]**.

**6.** Closed season **[1]**.

**7.** (a) The catch rose from 52,000 tonnes in 1990 to a peak of 61,000 tonnes in 1995 **[1]**, then fell steadily to 18,000 tonnes in 2010 **[1]**.
(b) 58,000 − 18,000 = 40,000 tonnes **[1]**; 40,000 ÷ 58,000 × 100 = 69.0% **[1]**.
(c) More boats caught fish faster than the population could breed and replace them, so the stock was overfished **[1]**; with fewer breeding adults, fewer young were produced each year, so there were fewer fish to catch however many boats were used **[1]**.

**8.** The larger fish and seabirds lose a major food source **[1]**, so their numbers fall through starvation or poor breeding success **[1]**. Organisms eaten by the small fish, such as plankton, may increase because they are no longer being eaten **[1]**.

**9.** (a) 3,000 × 0.40 = 1,200 tonnes **[1]**.
(b) It produces 2,400 tonnes of farmed fish while using 1,200 tonnes of wild fish, so it supplies more fish than it removes and partly reduces pressure on wild stocks **[1]**; however, 1,200 tonnes of wild fish are still caught, so pressure is reduced by much less than the farm's whole output **[1]**.

**10.** Any two explained:
- Clearing mangroves destroys a habitat and nursery area for young fish and other organisms, changing the local food web **[1]**; this can reduce wild fish populations offshore **[1]**.
- Waste feed and prawn faeces released from the ponds cause nutrient enrichment of coastal water **[1]**, leading to algal growth and oxygen loss when bacteria decompose the algae **[1]**.

Also accept disease spreading from farmed to wild crustaceans, or escaped prawns competing with wild species, each with explanation.

**11.** Seaweeds need no added feed, so they do not use wild-caught fish, whereas many farmed fish are fed fishmeal **[1]**. Seaweed farms take up nutrients from the water, whereas fish farms add nutrients through waste feed and faeces **[1]**. Fish farms carry greater risk of disease spreading and escapes affecting wild populations **[1]**.

**12.** One mark per point; at least one benefit and one limitation for two strategies and a judgement for full marks.
- Protected areas benefit: closing breeding grounds to all fishing lets fish grow, breed and spread into surrounding waters **[1]**.
- Protected areas limitation: they need patrols, and fishing effort is pushed into the remaining areas **[1]**.
- Quotas benefit: a cap on the total catch, shared between the countries, prevents more fish being taken than the stock can replace **[1]**.
- Quotas limitation: fish caught over quota may be discarded dead, and quotas may be set too high under pressure from the fishing industry **[1]**.
- International agreements: needed because the stock moves between the countries' waters, but they depend on every country signing and on implementation and monitoring, such as port inspections and tracking boats **[1]**.
- Judgement: for example, quotas and protected areas will only work if an international agreement with monitoring makes all the countries follow them **[1]**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 5**: saying pole and line is "better for the environment" without mentioning bycatch.
- **Question 7(a)**: describing only the fall and missing the earlier peak.
- **Question 7(c)**: writing "overfishing" without explaining the effect on breeding.
- **Question 9(b)**: giving a yes or no with no use of the figures.
- **Question 11**: describing the two farms separately instead of comparing.
