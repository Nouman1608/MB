Condensed notes for sub-topic 4.2 of Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680). The [climate change study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-environmental-management-0680-climate-change/) explains each list in full and works through a carbon footprint calculation.

## Definitions to learn exactly

- **Greenhouse gas**: a gas that absorbs radiation and emits the energy as thermal (heat) energy.
- **Named greenhouse gases**: carbon dioxide (CO₂), water vapour (H₂O), methane (CH₄).
- **Climate change adaptation**: altering behaviour, practices and infrastructure to adapt to the impacts of climate change.

## Six causes (4.2.3): activity → gas

| Activity | Gas | Key words |
|---|---|---|
| Combustion of fossil fuels | CO₂ | Power, transport, industry |
| Agriculture | CH₄ | Cattle digestion, manure decomposition, rice fields |
| Deforestation | CO₂ | Less removal by photosynthesis |
| Changes in land use | CH₄ | Wetland drainage |
| Cement manufacture | CO₂ | Calcium carbonate → calcium oxide + carbon dioxide |
| Population and energy use | CO₂ | More people, more energy |

## Enhanced greenhouse effect (4.2.4)

More greenhouse gases → more radiation absorbed (syllabus: "greenhouse gases absorb solar radiation") → **global warming** → can result in **climate change**.

## Twelve impacts (4.2.5)

Physical: land surface warms; ocean surface warms; ice sheets, glaciers and permafrost melt → sea level rises; ocean acidification from more dissolved CO₂; more frequent and severe extreme weather → flooding and loss of land, drought, wildfires.

Living things: food chains disrupted; biodiversity changes; habitat lost; forced migration of humans and other animals; more pest outbreaks and invasive species.

Food: crop yields change; food shortages.

Not required: ENSO, El Niño, La Niña.

## Twelve footprint strategies (4.2.6)

1. Reduced use of fossil fuels.
2. Sustainable food choices: plant-based, seasonal, local.
3. Less livestock farming.
4. Fewer children per woman.
5. More energy efficiency.
6. Reforestation and afforestation.
7. Carbon sequestration: capture, remove, store CO₂.
8. Transport policies.
9. International agreements and policies.
10. Taxation.
11. Research and climate models.
12. AI to predict impacts and develop strategies.

## Four adaptation strategies (4.2.8)

- Crops resistant to warmer, drier or wetter conditions.
- Improved flood defences.
- Legislation on building near coasts and on floodplains.
- Building design and materials.

## Benefits and limitations to recall (4.2.9)

| Strategy | + | – |
|---|---|---|
| Renewables replacing fossil fuels | Large cut in CO₂ | Cost; intermittent supply |
| Plant-based, local food | Less methane and transport | Behaviour change; some foods cannot be grown locally |
| Fewer livestock | Less methane | Farmers' livelihoods; cultural diets |
| Fewer children per woman | Lower long-term demand | Slow; sensitive personal choice |
| Energy efficiency | Saves money as well as emissions | Up-front cost |
| Reforestation | Stores carbon; habitat | Slow; land competition; fire risk |
| Carbon sequestration | Captures emissions at source | Expensive; storage must not leak |
| Transport policies | Cuts traffic emissions | Unpopular; needs alternatives |
| International agreements | Shared action | Targets may be missed; hard to enforce |
| Taxation | Price signal across economy | Hits poorer households; unpopular |
| Models and AI | Better planning | Predictions have uncertainty; need investment |
| Flood defences | Protect homes and land | Expensive; can fail; move the problem downstream |
| Resistant crops | Secure yields | Research cost; seed cost for farmers |
| Building laws and design | Prevent future losses | Do not protect existing buildings; raise costs |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Enhanced greenhouse effect | Extra warming from increased greenhouse gases due to human activity |
| Global warming | Rise in average temperature of the Earth's surface |
| Climate change | Long-term change in climate, which can result from global warming |
| Carbon footprint | Total greenhouse gas emissions caused by a person, product, organisation or country |
| Ocean acidification | Fall in seawater pH as more CO₂ dissolves |
| Permafrost | Ground that stays frozen all year |
| Afforestation | Planting trees where there was no forest |
| Reforestation | Replanting trees where forest was lost |
| Carbon sequestration | Capturing, removing and storing carbon dioxide |
| Adaptation | Altering behaviour, practices and infrastructure to cope with impacts |

## Data skills

- Percentage change = (new − old) / old × 100. Invented example: CO₂ rising from 360 ppm to 414 ppm is 54 / 360 × 100 = 15%.
- When describing a graph: give the overall trend, quote start and end values with units, and note any fluctuations, such as yearly ups and downs in CO₂ caused by plant growth seasons.
- Carbon footprint maths: distance × emission factor; compare totals before and after a change.

## Exam traps

- Global warming and climate change are not the same: warming is one cause of the change.
- Ozone depletion is a separate problem (4.4). Do not use it to explain global warming.
- Deforestation reduces CO₂ **removal**; wetland drainage releases **methane**.
- Mitigation (cutting footprints) ≠ adaptation (coping with effects). Read which one the question wants.
- Sea-level rise comes from melting **land ice** (ice sheets and glaciers) and warming water expanding.
- "Discuss" needs both sides and a judgement linked to the context.

## Self-test

1. Define a greenhouse gas.
2. Name the three greenhouse gases in the syllabus.
3. Give **three** agricultural sources of methane.
4. Explain how cement manufacture releases carbon dioxide.
5. How does deforestation increase carbon dioxide in the atmosphere?
6. What is the link between global warming and climate change?
7. Explain why sea levels rise as the climate warms.
8. What is ocean acidification and why does it harm corals?
9. Give **two** sustainable food choices that reduce carbon footprints.
10. Define climate change adaptation.
11. Give **two** adaptation strategies named in the syllabus.
12. Give **one** limitation of international agreements.

**Answers**

1. A gas that absorbs radiation and emits the energy as thermal (heat) energy.
2. Carbon dioxide, water vapour, methane.
3. Digestion by cattle, manure decomposition, rice fields.
4. Calcium carbonate is heated to produce calcium oxide and carbon dioxide; fuel burned to heat the kiln adds more.
5. Fewer trees remove carbon dioxide by photosynthesis; burning cleared trees also releases it.
6. Global warming, caused by greenhouse gases, can result in climate change.
7. Ice sheets and glaciers melt, adding water to the oceans, and warmer water expands.
8. More carbon dioxide dissolves in seawater, lowering its pH; this makes it harder for corals to build their skeletons.
9. Any two: plant-based diet, seasonal food, locally produced food.
10. Altering behaviour, practices and infrastructure to adapt to the impacts of climate change.
11. Any two: resistant crops, improved flood defences, building legislation for coasts and floodplains, building design and materials.
12. Countries may not meet targets, or may not sign, and enforcement is difficult.
