These questions cover section 3.13.4, Hydroelectric power and pumped storage, of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), which is International A-level only content. Learn the content first with the [hydroelectric power study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-hydroelectric-power-and-pumped-storage/) and the [hydroelectric power revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-hydroelectric-power-and-pumped-storage-revision-notes/). Take g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹, the density of water as 1000 kg m⁻³ and the density of air as 1.2 kg m⁻³.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State the function of the turbine and of the generator in a hydroelectric power station. **[2]**

**2.** Describe the energy changes from water in a high reservoir to electricity leaving the station. **[2]**

**3.** Calculate the speed of water after falling through a height of 45 m, ignoring energy losses. **[2]**

**4.** A turbine with blades of length 1.2 m sits in a river flowing at 2.5 m s⁻¹. Calculate the maximum power available. **[2]**

**5.** Explain the difference between a base-power station and a back-up power station. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why a pumped storage system is not a source of energy. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A hydroelectric station has a head of 64 m and a water flow rate of 75 m³ s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the power available from the falling water. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the speed of the water reaching the turbine, ignoring losses. **[1]**

**(c)** The station is 85% efficient. Calculate its electrical output. **[1]**

**(d)** Give two causes of energy loss in the station. **[2]**

**8.** A tidal-stream turbine has blades of length 3.0 m in a current of 2.2 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum power available. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the wind speed at which a wind turbine with the same blade length would have the same maximum power available. **[2]**

**(c)** Use your answer to (b) to explain an advantage of water turbines. **[1]**

**9.** A pumped storage scheme has an upper reservoir holding 1.5 × 10⁶ m³ of water at a mean head of 280 m. Pumping is 80% efficient and generating is 90% efficient.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational potential energy stored when the upper reservoir is full. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the electrical energy used to fill it. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the electrical energy returned when it is emptied. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the round-trip efficiency. **[1]**

**(e)** Explain why the scheme is worth operating even though energy is lost. **[2]**

**10.** Demand on a grid rises suddenly by 1.8 GW for 20 minutes. A pumped storage station with a head of 400 m and a generating efficiency of 90% meets this rise.

**(a)** Calculate the electrical energy supplied. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of water that must flow through the turbines. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why a hydroelectric station is better suited to meeting this rise than a nuclear station. **[1]**

**11.** **(a)** Explain how the generator in a hydroelectric station produces an emf. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the output of a hydroelectric station can be changed quickly. **[1]**

**(c)** State two environmental effects of building a large dam. **[2]**

**12.** A mountainous island currently burns imported oil for all its electricity. Its government is considering either a hydroelectric scheme with pumped storage or a wind farm. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of each option for the island, and reach a conclusion. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The turbine is turned by the moving water, transferring its kinetic energy to rotation of a shaft [1]; the generator, turned by the shaft, converts this rotational kinetic energy into electrical energy [1] **[2]**

**2.** Gravitational potential energy of the water becomes kinetic energy as it falls [1]; the water turns the turbine (kinetic energy of the turbine), which drives the generator to produce electrical energy, with some energy dissipated as heat and sound [1] **[2]**

**3.** v = √(2gh) [1] = √(2 × 9.81 × 45) = 30 m s⁻¹ [1] **[2]**

**4.** P = ½πr²ρv³ = ½ × π × 1.2² × 1000 × 2.5³ [1] = 3.5 × 10⁴ W [1] **[2]**

**5.** A base-power station runs continuously at a steady output to meet the minimum demand that is always present [1]; a back-up station is brought into use quickly when demand rises above the base supply [1] **[2]**

**6.** It only stores energy that was generated elsewhere, by using electricity to pump water uphill [1]; losses in pumping and generating mean it returns less energy than it takes in [1] **[2]**

**7. (a)** P = ρQgh = 1000 × 75 × 9.81 × 64 [1] = 4.7 × 10⁷ W [1]

**(b)** v = √(2 × 9.81 × 64) = 35 m s⁻¹ [1]

**(c)** 0.85 × 47.1 = 40 MW [1]

**(d)** Any two: friction between water and the pipe walls; turbulence in the water; friction in the turbine and generator bearings; resistive heating in the generator coils [1] [1] **[6]**

**8. (a)** P = ½ × π × 3.0² × 1000 × 2.2³ [1] = 1.5 × 10⁵ W [1]

**(b)** v³ = 1.51 × 10⁵ / (½ × π × 3.0² × 1.2) = 8.87 × 10³ [1]; v = 21 m s⁻¹ [1]

**(c)** A wind speed of 21 m s⁻¹ is a gale, while 2.2 m s⁻¹ is a modest current: because water is much denser, a small, slow water turbine gives as much power as a wind turbine in very strong wind [1] **[5]**

**9. (a)** E = ρVgh = 1000 × 1.5 × 10⁶ × 9.81 × 280 [1] = 4.1 × 10¹² J [1]

**(b)** 4.12 × 10¹²/0.80 = 5.2 × 10¹² J [1]

**(c)** 4.12 × 10¹² × 0.90 = 3.7 × 10¹² J [1]

**(d)** 3.71/5.15 = 0.72, or 72% [1]

**(e)** It uses spare electricity at times of low demand, when it is cheap or would otherwise be wasted [1], and supplies electricity within minutes at times of high demand, so base stations can run steadily and fewer back-up stations are needed [1] **[7]**

*Examiner insight:* Dividing by the pumping efficiency gives the energy used; multiplying by it is a common slip.

**10. (a)** E = 1.8 × 10⁹ × 1200 = 2.2 × 10¹² J [1]

**(b)** Potential energy needed = 2.16 × 10¹²/0.90 = 2.40 × 10¹² J [1]; V = E/(ρgh) = 2.40 × 10¹²/(1000 × 9.81 × 400) = 6.1 × 10⁵ m³ [1]

**(c)** A hydroelectric station reaches full output within minutes by opening valves, whereas a nuclear station cannot change its output quickly and is used for base power [1] **[4]**

**11. (a)** The turbine rotates coils relative to a magnetic field (or rotates magnets inside coils) [1]; the flux linkage of the coils changes, inducing an emf [1]

**(b)** The water flow, and so the output, is controlled simply by opening or closing valves, with no fuel to heat [1]

**(c)** Any two: flooding of land and habitats behind the dam; people or farmland displaced; changed river flow and sediment downstream; barriers to fish migration [1] [1] **[5]**

**12.** Marking points, one mark each, up to 6 **[6]**:

- Hydroelectric: mountains give the high heads needed, and the scheme produces no carbon dioxide during operation, replacing imported oil [1]
- Hydroelectric with pumped storage can respond within minutes and store energy, so it can meet changing demand [1]
- Hydroelectric disadvantages: high building cost, flooding of land and effects on rivers and wildlife [1]
- Wind: islands are often windy, turbines need no fuel and are quicker to build [1]
- Wind disadvantages: output is intermittent, so on its own it cannot guarantee supply; visual impact and noise [1]
- A justified conclusion, for example: hydroelectric power with pumped storage is preferred because it can supply reliably and store energy, possibly combined with wind whose surplus can be stored by pumping (another reasoned conclusion is accepted) [1]

*Examiner insight:* A conclusion that combines sources is acceptable if it is justified by the points made.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using air density for water in ½πr²ρv³.
- Confusing ρQgh with ½πr²ρv³.
- Multiplying by the pumping efficiency instead of dividing.
- Calling pumped storage an energy source.
- Ending an evaluation without a conclusion.
