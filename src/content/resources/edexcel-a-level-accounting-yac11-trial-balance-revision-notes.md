Condensed notes for outcomes 1.2.1 (the trial balance) and 1.2.2 (the limitations of a trial balance) in Unit 1 of Pearson Edexcel International A Level Accounting (YAC11), International AS content. For full explanations and a worked redraft, use the [trial balance study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-trial-balance/); to test yourself under exam conditions, use the [trial balance practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-trial-balance-practice/).

## The core idea in four lines

- A trial balance is a **list of ledger balances at one date**, split into a debit column and a credit column.
- Double entry gives every transaction an equal debit and credit, so the column totals **should agree**.
- Agreement checks **arithmetic only**; it does not prove the books are correct.
- It is the **starting point** for the statement of profit or loss and the statement of financial position.

## Getting each balance

```
1. Add both sides of the account.
2. Write the difference on the SMALLER side: "Balance c/d".
3. Total both sides (now equal).
4. Bring the balance down on the OPPOSITE side: "Balance b/d".
5. Balance b/d on the debit side  -> debit column.
   Balance b/d on the credit side -> credit column.
```

An account whose two sides are equal has no balance and is left out of the trial balance.

## Column rules

**DEAD:** **D**ebit = **E**xpenses, **A**ssets, **D**rawings.
**CLIC:** **C**redit = **L**iabilities, **I**ncome, **C**apital. Add the contra-asset accounts (allowances and provisions) to the credit side.

| Debit | Credit |
|---|---|
| Non-current assets at cost | Provision for depreciation |
| Opening inventory | Allowance for irrecoverable debts |
| Trade receivables, bank in hand, cash | Trade payables, bank overdraft, loans |
| Purchases, sales returns | Revenue, purchases returns |
| Carriage inwards, carriage outwards | Discount received |
| Wages, rent paid, insurance, irrecoverable debts written off | Rent received, commission received, interest received |
| Discount allowed, drawings | Capital |

**Not in the trial balance:** closing inventory (given as a note), and anything that is an adjustment still to be made (accruals, prepayments, the year's depreciation charge).

## Missing figures

- Missing capital = debit total − all other credits.
- A missing figure on the debit side = credit total − all other debits.
- Always show the subtraction as working.

**Effect of one misplaced balance:** moving a balance of x from the correct column to the wrong column changes the difference between the totals by **2x**. A gap of 1,500 can therefore be a 750 balance on the wrong side.

## Purposes and limitations (1.2.2)

| Purposes | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Checks that total debits equal total credits | Six types of error leave it agreeing: omission, commission, principle, original entry, complete reversal, compensating |
| Signals that an error exists when it disagrees | Does not say where an error is or what type it is |
| Collects balances in one list for preparing financial statements | Is not a financial statement: shows no profit, net assets or liquidity |
| Helps find errors before the financial statements are prepared | Is drawn up before year-end adjustments, so balances are not final |
| | Shows one date only, not activity during the period |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Trial balance | List of all ledger balances at a date, in debit and credit columns |
| Balance c/d | The balancing figure placed on the smaller side to close an account for the period |
| Balance b/d | The same figure on the opposite side, opening the next period; its side decides the column |
| Debit balance | Asset, expense or drawings |
| Credit balance | Liability, income or capital |
| Contra-asset account | An account with a credit balance that reduces an asset: provision for depreciation, allowance for irrecoverable debts |
| Arithmetical accuracy | Debits equal credits; the one thing an agreeing trial balance confirms |
| Compensating error | Two or more errors that cancel, so the totals still agree |

## Exam traps

- **Bank:** read the side of the balance b/d. A credit balance is an overdraft and goes in the credit column.
- **Returns:** sales returns are a debit (they reduce revenue); purchases returns are a credit.
- **Carriage:** both kinds are expenses and debits, even though only carriage inwards goes into cost of sales.
- **Allowance and provision:** credit balances, never debits.
- **Inventory:** only the opening figure is a ledger balance when the trial balance is drawn up.
- **"Prove":** never write that an agreeing trial balance proves accuracy. Say it **checks arithmetical accuracy**.
- **Explain a limitation:** give the point and its consequence; naming an error type alone only identifies it.
- **Heading:** "Trial balance **at** 31 March 2026", not "for the year ended", because it is a list at one date.

## Quick self-test

1. In which column does a balance of discount received appear?
2. A bank account has receipts of 9,400 and payments of 9,950. State the balance and its column.
3. Debits total 84,600; credits other than capital total 51,250. Find capital.
4. Where does carriage outwards appear?
5. Why is closing inventory not listed in the trial balance?
6. A trial balance has an allowance for irrecoverable debts of 320 in the debit column. By how much does this alone make the totals differ?
7. State two purposes of a trial balance.
8. Name three types of error that a trial balance will not reveal.
9. Explain why a trial balance is not a financial statement.
10. Give an example of a compensating error.
11. A trial balance is prepared before rent of 600 owing at the year end is recorded. Which limitation does this illustrate?
12. Correct this heading: "Trial balance for the year ended 30 June 2026".

### Answers

1. Credit column (income).
2. Payments exceed receipts by 550, so the balance b/d is on the credit side: an overdraft of **550** in the **credit** column.
3. 84,600 − 51,250 = **33,350**.
4. Debit column: it is a selling and distribution expense.
5. It is not a ledger balance at that point; it is recorded when the financial statements are prepared, so it is given as a note.
6. Credit total too low by 320 and debit total too high by 320, so they differ by **640**.
7. Any two: to check the arithmetical accuracy of the double entry; to signal that errors exist; to provide a list of balances for preparing the financial statements.
8. Any three of: omission, commission, principle, original entry, complete reversal, compensating.
9. It lists balances but does not calculate profit for the year or show the financial position (net assets, capital employed), and it is drawn up before year-end adjustments.
10. For example, revenue under-added by 200 and wages under-added by 200: both totals fall by 200, so they still agree.
11. It is drawn up before year-end adjustments, so the rent expense balance is not the final figure.
12. "Trial balance **at** 30 June 2026".

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a bank overdraft a debit or a credit in the trial balance?**
A credit. The business owes the bank, so it is a liability; the balance b/d of the bank account is on the credit side.

**What does an agreeing trial balance actually prove?**
Only that total debits equal total credits, so the double entry is arithmetically complete. It does not prove that entries are in the right accounts or for the right amounts.
