## Section A

**1.** Which is the most reasonable estimate of the mass of air in a school laboratory of floor area 80 m² and height 3 m?

A: 0.3 kg

B: 3 kg

C: 300 kg

D: 3000 kg **[1]**

**2.** Which is the most reasonable estimate of the kinetic energy of a cyclist and bicycle travelling at a steady speed on a flat road?

A: 3 J

B: 30 J

C: 3 × 10³ J

D: 3 × 10⁶ J **[1]**

**3.** State what is meant by saying that a physical quantity consists of a numerical magnitude and a unit, using the example m = 0.25 kg. **[2]**

**4.** Convert a speed of 90 km h⁻¹ into m s⁻¹, showing the unit substitution. **[2]**

**5.** A student records the following column headings in a table of results: `length mm`, `T (s)`, `T² / s²`. Identify the heading that is not acceptable and rewrite it correctly. **[2]**

**6.** The efficiency of a motor is quoted as "0.62". Explain why no unit is given. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A column headed `A / mm²` contains the value 0.80.

**(a)** State the area A in mm². **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate A in m², giving your answer in standard form. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why converting by "moving the decimal point three places" would give the wrong answer. **[1]**

**8.** Estimate the pressure exerted on the floor by an elephant standing on all four feet. State each value you assume. **[4]**

**9.** Estimate the useful power output of a person climbing a ladder 5 m high in 10 s. State your assumptions. **[3]**

**10.** A student calculates the density of a wooden block from measurements and obtains "650".

**(a)** State what is missing from this answer. **[1]**

**(b)** The student's data were mass = 0.39 kg and dimensions 0.10 m × 0.10 m × 0.060 m. Calculate the density, with its unit. **[2]**

**(c)** Use the density of water to comment on whether the answer is reasonable for wood. **[1]**

**11.** A light-emitting diode emits light of wavelength 590 nm.

**(a)** State whether this wavelength is a reasonable value for visible light. Give a reason. **[1]**

**(b)** Express the wavelength in metres in standard form. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the number of wavelengths that fit into a length of 1.0 mm. **[2]**

**12.** A student estimates that the mass of water in a full domestic bath is 0.3 kg.

**(a)** Make your own estimate of the volume of a full bath, stating the dimensions you assume. **[2]**

**(b)** Use your estimate to find the mass of water. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest what error the student made. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Volume = 80 × 3 = 240 m³; mass ≈ 1.2 × 240 ≈ 290 kg, so **C** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Mass of cyclist and bicycle ≈ 80 kg, speed ≈ 8 m s⁻¹; KE ≈ ½ × 80 × 8² ≈ 2.6 × 10³ J, so **C** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The value is the number 0.25 multiplied by the unit, one kilogram [1]; neither part alone describes the quantity, and changing the unit (for example to 250 g) changes the number but not the quantity [1]. **[2]**

**4.** 90 km h⁻¹ = 90 × 1000 m ÷ 3600 s [1] = **25 m s⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** `length mm` is not acceptable [1]; rewrite as `length / mm` (or `l / mm`, or `length (mm)`) [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Efficiency is a ratio of two energies (or two powers), so the units cancel and it is a pure number [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** A = **0.80 mm²** [1].

**(b)** 1 mm² = (10⁻³ m)² = 10⁻⁶ m² [1], so A = 0.80 × 10⁻⁶ = **8.0 × 10⁻⁷ m²** [1].

**(c)** The prefix is squared along with the unit, so the factor is 10⁻⁶, not 10⁻³ [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Mass of elephant ≈ 5000 kg, so weight ≈ 5 × 10⁴ N [1]. Area of one foot ≈ 0.1 m² (a foot of diameter about 0.35 m) [1], so total area ≈ 0.4 m² [1]. p = F/A ≈ 5 × 10⁴ ÷ 0.4 ≈ **1 × 10⁵ Pa** [1]. Accept answers from 5 × 10⁴ Pa to 3 × 10⁵ Pa with reasonable stated values. **[4]**

**9.** Mass ≈ 70 kg [1]. Energy gained = mgh ≈ 70 × 9.81 × 5 ≈ 3400 J [1]. Power ≈ 3400 ÷ 10 ≈ **300 W** (accept 3 × 10² W to 4 × 10² W) [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** The unit [1].

**(b)** Volume = 0.10 × 0.10 × 0.060 = 6.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³ [1]; density = 0.39 ÷ 6.0 × 10⁻⁴ = **650 kg m⁻³** [1].

**(c)** It is less than the density of water, 1000 kg m⁻³, which is reasonable because most wood floats [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** Yes, because it lies between about 400 nm and 700 nm [1].

**(b)** 590 nm = **5.9 × 10⁻⁷ m** [1].

**(c)** Number = 1.0 × 10⁻³ m ÷ 5.9 × 10⁻⁷ m [1] ≈ **1700** (no unit) [1]. **[4]**

**12. (a)** For example 1.5 m × 0.6 m × 0.3 m [1], giving about 0.3 m³ (accept 0.1 m³ to 0.4 m³) [1].

**(b)** Mass = 1000 × 0.3 ≈ **300 kg** [1].

**(c)** The student multiplied a volume in m³ by a density of 1 (the value in g cm⁻³) instead of 1000 kg m⁻³, mixing units [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a final answer with no unit, or a unit for a pure number.
- Converting squared or cubed units with the factor for a single power.
- Writing an estimate to three or four significant figures.
- Making an estimate without stating the values assumed.
- Mixing grams and kilograms, or litres and cubic metres, in one calculation.
