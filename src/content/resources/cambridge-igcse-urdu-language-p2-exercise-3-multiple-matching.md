This guide covers **Exercise 3 of Paper 2 Listening**, the multiple matching task, in Cambridge IGCSE
Urdu as a Second Language (0539). The site's
[Paper 2 Listening guide](/resources/igcse-cambridge-urdu-paper-2-listening/) introduces all four
listening exercises. This page looks closely at the third: why it tests different skills from Exercises 1
and 2, how Urdu speakers signal attitude, how the printed options paraphrase what is said, and how to
avoid the keyword trap.

## What the syllabus says

- Candidates **hear six short extracts** and **match each speaker to appropriate content**.
- The exercise is worth **6 marks**, one for each speaker.
- It assesses **L3**, show understanding of the connections between ideas, opinions and attitudes, and
  **L4**, understand what is implied but not directly stated, for example gist, the speaker's purpose,
  intention and feelings.
- As in all of Paper 2, each exercise is heard **twice**, with pauses to read and write. Everything is in
  Urdu.

The syllabus does not say how many options are printed. Some papers in this style give exactly one
option per speaker; others add one or two that are not needed, which makes elimination harder. Practise
both, and look at the specimen paper and recent papers for your series to see which design is used.

## Why this exercise is different

Exercises 1 and 2 reward catching a stated fact. Exercise 3 does not, because L3 and L4 are about
*relationships* and *implications*:

- **L3, connections:** a speaker may like one thing but worry about another, or agree with a common view
  only up to a point. The right option captures that connection, not just one half of it.
- **L4, implied meaning:** a speaker may never say "I am against this". They show it through a rhetorical
  question, a complaint or a tone of regret, and the option states it plainly.

Usually all six speakers talk about the **same topic**. So the topic word cannot tell them apart. Their
**attitude** does.

## How Urdu speakers signal attitude

Train your ear for the small words that carry the opinion. They are often more important than the nouns.

| Signal | Urdu | What usually follows |
|---|---|---|
| contrast | لیکن، مگر، پر، البتہ | the speaker's real point or reservation |
| concession | یہ ٹھیک ہے کہ ... لیکن | a partial agreement, then a disagreement |
| change of mind | پہلے میں سمجھتا تھا ... اب | the current view, which is the one to match |
| reported view | لوگ کہتے ہیں ... / سنا ہے ... | often a view the speaker goes on to reject |
| doubt | شاید، پتا نہیں، مجھے نہیں لگتا | uncertainty, not a firm opinion |
| regret or wish | کاش ... | something the speaker misses or wants changed |
| rhetorical question | کیا فائدہ اگر ...؟ | an implied negative judgement |
| emphasis | بالکل، ہرگز، واقعی | a strong, settled view |

**The word after لیکن often decides the match.** "آن لائن خریداری آسان تو ہے، لیکن ..." is not a speaker
who simply thinks online shopping is easy. The تو ... لیکن pattern is a signal that a reservation is
coming.

## Options paraphrase the speakers

The printed options almost never repeat the speaker's words. They summarise the speaker's position in
different, often more general, language. Practise recognising the same idea in two forms:

| Speaker says | Option might say |
|---|---|
| روز دو گھنٹے ٹریفک میں پھنسا رہتا ہوں | سفر میں بہت وقت ضائع ہوتا ہے |
| پیسے تو بہت لگتے ہیں | یہ مہنگا ہے |
| پہلے مجھے اچھا نہیں لگتا تھا، اب عادت ہو گئی ہے | رائے وقت کے ساتھ بدل گئی |
| لوگ کہتے ہیں یہ خطرناک ہے، مجھے تو کبھی کچھ نہیں ہوا | ایک عام خیال سے متفق نہیں |
| کاش بچپن میں کسی نے سکھایا ہوتا | افسوس ہے کہ یہ پہلے نہیں سیکھا |

A useful habit is to **summarise each speaker in three or four of your own words** while listening, for
example "likes, but too expensive" or "changed mind, now for". Then match your summary to the options,
rather than hunting for the speaker's words in them.

## The keyword trap

Because the options are written about the same topic, an option will often contain a word that a speaker
used, while saying something that speaker did not mean. If speaker 3 mentions ٹریفک and an option
mentions آلودگی (pollution), the shared city setting may tempt you; but if speaker 3's point is about
lost family time, the pollution option is wrong for them.

Ask of each match: **does the option state this speaker's overall point?** Not "does it share a word?"

## A routine for the two hearings

```
In the pause:   read every option; underline the word that makes each one
                different (متفق نہیں، بدل گئی، شک، افسوس، سب سے اہم);
                notice pairs of options that are close in meaning
First hearing:  for each speaker, note a 3-4 word summary + a sign:
                (+) for, (-) against, (+/-) mixed, (→) changed mind
Second hearing: confirm the summaries; listen again to any speaker
                whose match is uncertain
After:          match the confident ones first and cross those options off;
                then decide the rest by elimination; fill every answer
```

## Worked example

The extracts below are original, written for this guide. Have them read aloud twice.

> **Speaker 1:** "آن لائن خریداری آسان تو ہے، لیکن کپڑے کبھی ویسے نہیں نکلتے جیسے تصویر میں دکھائی
> دیتے ہیں۔"
>
> **Speaker 2:** "لوگ کہتے ہیں آن لائن چیزیں سستی ملتی ہیں۔ ڈیلیوری کا خرچ ملا کر دیکھیں تو بازار ہی
> سستا پڑتا ہے۔"

Options:

- **A.** سہولت کو مانتا ہے، مگر معیار پر بھروسا نہیں کرتا۔
- **B.** آن لائن خریداری کو سستا سمجھنے والوں سے متفق نہیں۔
- **C.** آن لائن خریداری سے وقت کی بچت ہوتی ہے۔

**Speaker 1 → A.** The concession (آسان تو ہے) matches "سہولت کو مانتا ہے", and the point after لیکن (the
clothes never look as they do in the picture) is a doubt about quality, "معیار پر بھروسا نہیں". This is
L3: the option has to capture both halves of the connection.

**Speaker 2 → B.** The speaker reports a common view (لوگ کہتے ہیں) and then shows it is wrong once
delivery costs are added. They never say "I disagree", so this is L4: the disagreement is implied.

**Why C is wrong for speaker 1.** آسان might suggest saving time, but the speaker's overall point is the
reservation about quality. C picks up one word's association and ignores the main idea.

## Common mistakes

- **Matching on a shared word** rather than the speaker's overall point.
- **Stopping at the first half** of a "yes, but" answer and choosing an option that captures only the
  positive part.
- **Taking a reported view as the speaker's own.** After لوگ کہتے ہیں, wait for what the speaker thinks.
- **Matching the old view** when a speaker has changed their mind. The view after اب is the one that
  counts.
- **Forgetting to cross options off**, so the same option is used twice and another speaker is left
  without a match.
- **Leaving a speaker blank.** Elimination usually narrows the last one or two down to a sensible choice.

## Exam technique summary

Read every option in the pause and underline what makes each one distinct. Summarise each speaker in a
few words of your own, with a sign for their attitude. Listen hardest to what comes after لیکن، مگر or
اب. Match the clear cases first, then eliminate. For more on the paper as a whole, see the
[Paper 2 Listening revision notes](/resources/igcse-cambridge-urdu-paper-2-listening-revision-notes/) and
the full [Paper 2 listening practice set](/resources/urdu-paper-2-listening-practice/).
