These questions cover section 6 of Unit M1: Mechanics 1 in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics (YMA01): the moment of a force, and simple problems with coplanar parallel forces acting on a body and the conditions for equilibrium. All rods and planks are horizontal. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² and give answers that use g to 3 significant figures.

Related: [M1 moments study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m1-moments-edexcel-alevel-maths/) and [M1 moments revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m1-moments-edexcel-alevel-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** A force of 12 N acts at right angles to a rod at a point 0.75 m from a hinge O. Find the moment of the force about O. **[1]**

**2.** A light rod AB of length 2.5 m lies horizontally, with A to the left of B. Vertical forces act on it: 10 N downwards at A, 6 N upwards at C, where AC = 1 m, and 4 N downwards at B. Find the resultant moment of the forces about C, stating its sense. **[3]**

**3.** A uniform rod of length 2 m is pivoted at its midpoint. A particle of mass 3 kg is attached 0.4 m from one end. Find where a particle of mass 2 kg must be attached so that the rod balances horizontally. **[3]**

**4.** A light rod AB of length 1.5 m rests on supports at A and B. A particle of mass 9 kg is placed on the rod 0.5 m from A. Find the reaction at each support. **[3]**

**5.** A uniform beam rests on two supports and is on the point of tilting about one of them. State what this tells you about the forces on the beam. **[2]**

**6.** A uniform rod AB has length 2 m and mass 6 kg. A particle of mass 4 kg is attached at A. The rod rests horizontally on a single support at C. Find the distance AC and the reaction at C. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** A non-uniform rod AB has length 5 m and mass 8 kg. It rests horizontally on supports at A and B. A particle of mass 4 kg is placed at C, where AC = 3 m. The reaction at B is 1.5 times the reaction at A.

**(a)** Find the reactions at A and B. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the distance of the centre of mass of the rod from A. **[3]**

**8.** A uniform plank AB has length 7 m and mass 40 kg. It rests horizontally on supports at C and D, where AC = 1.5 m and DB = 2 m. A man of mass 80 kg stands on the plank.

**(a)** Find the reactions at C and D when the man stands 4 m from A. **[4]**

**(b)** The man walks towards B. Find his greatest distance from A for which the plank does not tilt. **[3]**

**(c)** A child sits at A so that the man can stand at B without the plank tilting. Find the least mass of the child. **[3]**

**9.** A uniform rod AB has length 6 m and mass 10 kg. It hangs horizontally from two vertical strings, one at A and one at C, where CB = 1 m. A particle of mass 4 kg is attached at D. The tension at C is three times the tension at A.

**(a)** Find the tension in each string. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the distance AD. **[3]**

**10.** A uniform beam AB has length 4 m and mass 25 kg. It rests horizontally on supports at A and C, where AC = 3 m.

**(a)** Find the reactions at A and C. **[3]**

**(b)** A load of mass M kg is placed at B. Find the greatest value of M for which the beam stays horizontal, and the reaction at C in that case. **[3]**

**(c)** State one modelling assumption about the load that you have used. **[1]**

**11.** A light rod AB of length 2 m lies horizontally, with A to the left of B. Vertical forces act on it: 6 N upwards at A, 10 N downwards at C, where AC = 0.5 m, and 4 N upwards at B.

**(a)** Show that the resultant force on the rod is zero. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the resultant moment about A, stating its sense. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain whether the rod is in equilibrium. **[1]**

**12.** A non-uniform rod AB has length 3 m and mass m kg, and its centre of mass is x m from A. When the rod rests on a single support 1 m from A, it balances horizontally with a particle of mass 1.2 kg attached at A. When it rests on a single support 1 m from B, it balances horizontally with a particle of mass 4.8 kg attached at B. Find m and x. **[6]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Moment = 12 × 0.75 = **9 N m** [1].

**2.** About C: 10 N at A has moment 10 × 1 = 10 N m anticlockwise [1].

4 N at B has moment 4 × 1.5 = 6 N m clockwise [1].

Resultant = **4 N m anticlockwise** [1].

**3.** The 3 kg particle is 0.6 m from the pivot. Moments: 3g(0.6) = 2g(d) [1], so d = 0.9 [1].

The 2 kg particle goes **0.9 m from the pivot, on the other side** [1].

**4.** Moments about A: 1.5R_B = 9g(0.5) [1], so **R_B = 29.4 N** [1].

R_A = 9g − 29.4 = **58.8 N** [1].

**5.** The reaction at the other support is zero [1], and the moments about the support it is tilting about still balance [1].

**6.** The rod's weight acts 1 m from A. Moments about C: 4g(AC) = 6g(1 − AC) [1].

10 AC = 6, so **AC = 0.6 m** [1].

Reaction at C = 10g = **98 N** [1].

**7. (a)** R_A + R_B = 12g and R_B = 1.5R_A [1], so 2.5R_A = 12g [1].

**R_A = 4.8g = 47.0 N** and **R_B = 7.2g = 70.6 N** [1].

**(b)** Moments about A: R_B(5) = 8g(x) + 4g(3) [1].

36g = 8gx + 12g [1], so **x = 3 m** [1].

**8. (a)** The plank's weight acts at 3.5 m from A, 2 m from C. The man is 2.5 m from C, and D is 3.5 m from C.

Moments about C: 3.5R_D = 40g(2) + 80g(2.5) [1], with correct distances [1].

3.5R_D = 280g, so **R_D = 80g = 784 N** [1]. R_C = 120g − 80g = **392 N** [1].

**(b)** About to tilt about D, so R_C = 0 [1].

The plank's weight is 1.5 m from D. Moments about D: 40g(1.5) = 80g(y) [1], so y = 0.75 m beyond D: **5.75 m from A** [1].

**(c)** With the man at B (2 m beyond D) and the child at A (5 m from D), on the point of tilting R_C = 0 [1].

Moments about D: 40g(1.5) + mg(5) = 80g(2) [1], so 60 + 5m = 160 and **m = 20 kg** [1].

**9. (a)** T_A + T_C = 14g and T_C = 3T_A, so T_A = 3.5g = **34.3 N** [1] and T_C = 10.5g = **103 N** [1].

**(b)** C is 5 m from A. Moments about A: T_C(5) = 10g(3) + 4g(AD) [1].

52.5g = 30g + 4g(AD) [1], so **AD = 5.625 m** (5.63 m) [1].

**10. (a)** The weight 25g = 245 N acts 2 m from A. Moments about A: 3R_C = 245 × 2 [1], so **R_C = 163 N** [1] and **R_A = 245 − 163.3 = 81.7 N** [1].

**(b)** On the point of tilting about C, R_A = 0. The weight is 1 m from C and B is 1 m beyond C: 25g(1) = Mg(1) [1], so **M = 25** [1]. Then R_C = 50g = **490 N** [1].

**(c)** The load is a particle, so its weight acts at the single point B [1].

**11. (a)** Upward 6 + 4 = 10 N; downward 10 N; resultant zero [1].

**(b)** About A: 10 × 0.5 = 5 N m clockwise, and 4 × 2 = 8 N m anticlockwise [1].

Resultant = **3 N m anticlockwise** [1].

**(c)** No: the resultant moment is not zero, so the rod turns even though the resultant force is zero [1].

**12.** First balance, moments about the support (1 m from A): 1.2g(1) = mg(x − 1) [2] (one mark for each side).

Second balance, moments about the support (2 m from A): 4.8g(1) = mg(2 − x) [1].

Adding: 6 = m(1) [1], so **m = 6** [1].

Then x − 1 = 1.2/6 = 0.2, so **x = 1.2 m** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Measuring a distance from the end of the rod instead of from the point you take moments about.
- Leaving out the weight of the rod or plank.
- Setting the reaction at the wrong support to zero when tilting.
- Treating a non-uniform rod's weight as acting at its midpoint.
- Giving a moment without its sense.
- Not checking that a position found lies on the rod.
