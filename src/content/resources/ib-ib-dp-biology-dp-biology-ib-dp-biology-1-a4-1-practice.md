## Questions

**1.** A student writes: "Over many years, individual moths in a polluted area became darker to hide from birds." Explain what is wrong with this statement and rewrite it so that it describes evolution correctly. **[3]**

**2.** The table shows a 20-base section of one gene in four species.

| Species | Sequence |
|---|---|
| P | GATTACCGTAGCTTAGGCAT |
| R | GATTACCGTTGCTTAGGCAT |
| T | GACTACCGATGCTCAGGCTT |
| W | CTCTTCAGATCATCTGCCTA |

**(a)** Determine the number of differences between P and each of the other species. **[1]**

**(b)** Deduce the order in which the lineages are most likely to have split, giving a reason. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why similarities in base sequence are stronger evidence of a close relationship than similarities in body shape. **[2]**

**4.** Maize was developed by farmers from a wild grass, teosinte, over several thousand years. Modern maize cobs are many times larger than teosinte ears. Explain how this provides evidence for evolution. **[3]**

**5.** The forelimbs of a human, a bat and a whale contain the same set of bones in different proportions.

**(a)** Name the type of structures these are. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why they are evidence for evolution. **[2]**

**6.** Classify each pair as homologous or analogous, giving a reason.

**(a)** The forelimb of a human and the flipper of a whale. **[1]**

**(b)** The spines of a cactus (modified leaves) and the spines of a hedgehog (modified hairs). **[1]**

**(c)** The wing of a bird and the wing of a bat, considered as wings. **[1]**

**7.** Explain how convergent evolution can produce similar structures in unrelated species. **[2]**

**8.** A population of lizards lives on a mainland. A storm carries a few lizards to a distant island with different vegetation and predators. Explain how a new species of lizard might form on the island. **[4]**

**9.** **(a)** Explain why gradual change in one lineage over time, without splitting, does not increase the number of species. **[1]**

**(b)** State why reproductive isolation is needed for speciation. **[1]**

**10.** (HL only) Two forms of a cricket live in the same meadow. Females respond only to males whose song matches their own form, and the two forms rarely interbreed.

**(a)** State the type of isolation shown. **[1]**

**(b)** State whether speciation here would be sympatric or allopatric, and explain why. **[1]**

**11.** (HL only) Species J (2n = 40) and species K (2n = 36) occasionally produce hybrids that grow normally but produce no offspring.

**(a)** State the number of chromosomes in the hybrid. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the hybrid is sterile. **[2]**

**(c)** Give two barriers that could act before fertilisation to keep J and K apart. **[2]**

**12.** (HL only) A plant species with 2n = 12 hybridizes with a species with 2n = 18. A fertile plant later arises from the hybrid by doubling of its chromosome number.

**(a)** State the chromosome number of the fertile plant. **[1]**

**(b)** The fertile plant is crossed with the 2n = 12 parent. State the chromosome number of the offspring and explain why they would be sterile. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Individuals do not change their genes during their lifetime; evolution happens in populations [1]. Darker moths were more likely to survive predation and reproduce, so the allele for dark colour became more frequent [1]. Rewritten: "Over many generations, the proportion of dark moths in the population increased because dark moths were less likely to be eaten and passed on their alleles" [1] **[3]**

**2. (a)** P–R: 1; P–T: 5; P–W: 14 [1]

**(b)** W split off first, then T, and P and R split most recently [1]; species with fewer sequence differences share a more recent common ancestor [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* You can check the order with the other pairs: R–T has 4 and T–W has 9, consistent with W being the most distant lineage.

**3.** Similar body shapes can evolve independently in unrelated species by convergent evolution [1]; a long, matching base sequence is very unlikely to arise twice by chance, so it indicates shared ancestry [1] **[2]**

**4.** Farmers chose plants with larger ears to breed from in each generation [1]. The large cob size is heritable, so the population changed [1]. A large change in a short time shows that selection can change species substantially, as natural selection could over longer periods [1] **[3]**

**5. (a)** Homologous structures [1]

**(b)** The same basic bone arrangement is used for very different functions [1]; this is best explained by inheritance from a common ancestor, followed by adaptation in each lineage [1] **[3]**

**6. (a)** Homologous: same pentadactyl bone structure inherited from a common ancestor [1]

**(b)** Analogous: similar function (defence) but different origins, leaves versus hairs [1]

**(c)** Analogous as wings: flight evolved separately in birds and bats, with the wing surface made of feathers in one and skin in the other (though the bones are homologous as forelimbs) [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Part (c) rewards precision: name the level at which you are comparing.

**7.** Unrelated lineages face similar selection pressures, for example the need to swim fast in water [1]; natural selection favours similar features in each lineage independently, giving structures with similar functions but different underlying structure [1] **[2]**

**8.** The island population is reproductively isolated, as there is no gene flow with the mainland [1]. It starts with a small, possibly different set of alleles, and new mutations arise separately [1]. Different vegetation and predators cause differential selection, favouring different alleles [1]. Over many generations the gene pools diverge until the two populations could not produce fertile offspring together [1] **[4]**

**9. (a)** There is still only one species at any time; a new species is added only when a population splits [1]

**(b)** Without isolation, gene flow keeps mixing alleles, so populations cannot diverge [1] **[2]**

**10. (a)** Behavioural isolation [1]

**(b)** Sympatric: the forms live in the same area and gene flow is reduced by behaviour, not geography [1] **[2]**

**11. (a)** 20 + 18 = **38** [1]

**(b)** The chromosomes from J and K are not homologous [1], so they cannot pair up in meiosis and viable gametes are not produced [1]

**(c)** Any two: different courtship behaviour; breeding at different times; living in different habitats [1] [1] **[5]**

**12. (a)** Gametes 6 + 9 = 15; doubled: **30** [1]

**(b)** Gametes of 15 and 6 give **21** chromosomes [1]; these do not all have homologous partners, so meiosis fails and the offspring are sterile [1] **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing individuals evolving, or evolution with a purpose.
- Missing "heritable" or "population" in the definition.
- Classifying from function alone.
- Leaving out differential selection when explaining speciation.
- Forgetting to halve the chromosome number to find gametes.
- Calling behavioural isolation geographical.
