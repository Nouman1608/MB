## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term "trial balance". **[2]**

**2.** State whether each balance appears in the debit or the credit column of a trial balance.

**(a)** Carriage inwards **[1]**

**(b)** Provision for depreciation of machinery **[1]**

**(c)** Purchases returns **[1]**

**(d)** Drawings **[1]**

**3.** Explain **two** purposes of preparing a trial balance. **[4]**

**4.** Explain why a trial balance whose totals agree may still contain errors. **[2]**

**5.** Name the type of error in each case. Each error does not affect the agreement of the trial balance.

**(a)** A credit sale of $640 to R Bhatti was debited to R Bhatia's account. **[1]**

**(b)** Repairs to the shop roof, $1,800, were debited to the premises account. **[1]**

**(c)** A purchase invoice for $390 from Lakeside Supplies was lost and never recorded. **[1]**

**(d)** Discount received of $75 was debited to the supplier's account and credited to discount received, when the correct amount was $57. **[1]**

**6.** A trial balance has a debit total of $84,350 and a credit total of $83,190. The only error is one balance placed in the wrong column. Calculate the amount of that balance and state on which side it should appear. **[2]**

**7.** Give an example, with an amount, of an error of complete reversal involving the bank account and the rent received account. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A list of the balances on the ledger accounts at a stated date [1], set out in debit and credit columns to check that the totals agree [1]. **[2]**

**2.** **(a)** Debit [1]

**(b)** Credit [1]

**(c)** Credit [1]

**(d)** Debit [1] **[4]**

**3.** It checks the arithmetical accuracy of the double entry [1]: every debit should have an equal credit, so equal totals suggest the postings are arithmetically correct [1]. It provides a summary of balances [1] from which the statement of profit or loss and statement of financial position are prepared [1]. Also accept, as either purpose: it shows that errors exist, so they can be found and corrected before the statements are prepared. **[4]**

**4.** Some errors involve an equal debit and an equal credit [1], for example an error of omission or of principle, so the totals still agree even though accounts are wrong [1]. **[2]**

**5.** **(a)** Commission [1]

**(b)** Principle [1]

**(c)** Omission [1]

**(d)** Original entry: the wrong amount was used for both entries [1] **[4]**

**6.** Difference $84,350 − $83,190 = $1,160, so the balance is $1,160 ÷ 2 = $580 [1]. The debit total is too high, so a $580 credit balance has been placed in the debit column; it belongs in the credit column [1]. **[2]**

**7.** For example, rent received of $500 by bank transfer [1] was debited to rent received and credited to bank, instead of debit bank, credit rent received [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Items wrongly debited: $3,900 + $340 + $1,250 = $5,490; items wrongly credited: $280 + $6,500 = $6,780 [1]. Moving them changes the debit total by +$6,780 − $5,490 = +$1,290 and the credit total by −$1,290, which closes the original difference of $83,080 − $80,500 = $2,580 (twice $1,290) [1]. **[11]**

**10.** **(a)** It affects the trial balance [1]: the debit total is higher by $1,250 [1].

**(b)** It does not affect the trial balance (error of commission) [1].

**(c)** It affects the trial balance [1]: the wages debit balance is $200 too low, so the credit total is higher by $200 [1].

**(d)** It affects the trial balance [1]: two debits and no credit make the debit total higher by $830 × 2 = $1,660 [1]. **[7]**

**11.** **(a)** Two named errors, each with a correct example. Error of omission [1]: a credit purchase of $700 is never recorded, so neither account includes it [1]. Error of principle [1]: $2,000 spent on a vehicle repair is debited to the motor vehicles account, so profit and assets are both overstated [1]. Other acceptable answers: commission, complete reversal, original entry, compensating, each with a correct example.

**(b)** Commission is an entry in the wrong account of the **same** class, such as the wrong customer's account [1]. Principle is an entry in the wrong **class** of account, such as an expense recorded as a non-current asset [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **(a)** Item 1: error of commission (both purchases and wages are expense accounts) [1]. Item 2: error of principle (a non-current asset recorded as an expense) [1].

**(b)** Item 1: gross profit is understated by $3,600, because purchases are in cost of sales [1]; profit for the year is unaffected, because the $3,600 is still deducted, in cost of sales instead of in expenses [1]. Item 2: gross profit is unaffected [1]; profit for the year is understated by $1,100 [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "for the year ended" in a trial balance heading.
- Putting the allowance for irrecoverable debts, the provision for depreciation or a bank overdraft in the debit column.
- Naming an error without saying why it does not affect the totals.
- Forgetting that two debits and no credit doubles the difference.
