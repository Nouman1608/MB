This guide covers **Exercise 3** of Cambridge O Level Second Language Urdu 3248 Paper 2 Grammar, Writing and Translation. The syllabus describes it as the **Extended writing exercise**: "candidates write a piece of continuous prose of approximately 200 words. A stimulus of short prompts will be printed in the question paper. A purpose, format and audience for the writing will be specified." It is worth **20 marks**, the largest of the four exercises in the paper, and tests the writing objectives **W1, W2, W3, W4 and W5**. For the whole paper, see the [Paper 2 Grammar, Writing and Translation study guide](/resources/o-level-cambridge-urdu-second-language-paper-2-grammar-writing-translation/).

## Where Exercise 3 sits

Exercise 3 is the third of four exercises in a paper of 1 hour 30 minutes and 50 marks. For this exercise the question and the response are in Urdu, and dictionaries may not be used. Twenty marks out of fifty make it the exercise that most affects your Paper 2 result, so give it the time it deserves.

| Objective | In the extended writing exercise |
|---|---|
| W1 communicate information, ideas and opinions clearly, accurately and effectively | cover the prompts with relevant, developed ideas |
| W2 organise ideas into coherent paragraphs using a range of linking devices | plan the order of paragraphs and link them |
| W3 manipulate the target language accurately | use a range of structures, tenses and vocabulary correctly |
| W4 show control of punctuation and spelling | use *۔*، *،*، *؟* correctly and spell accurately |
| W5 use appropriate register and style or format for the purpose and audience | match tone and layout to the task |

The syllabus does not print how the 20 marks are divided among these objectives. Use the mark scheme of a past paper to see how content, organisation and language are rewarded.

## Reading the task: purpose, format, audience

Before you write, answer three questions from the task.

| Question | What to look for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose**: why am I writing? | inform, persuade, describe, advise, thank, complain, invite | to persuade classmates |
| **Format**: what kind of text? | article, blog, report, review, informal letter or email, speech | an article for the school magazine |
| **Audience**: who will read it? | friend, teacher, principal, readers of a magazine | other students |

The syllabus lists forms of writing such as "a summary, an informal letter/email, an article, a blog, a report or review". Each has its own layout and tone, so learn the features below.

| Format | Features |
|---|---|
| Informal letter or email | greeting (*پیارے دوست*)، friendly tone, *تم* or *آپ*, closing (*تمھارا دوست*) |
| Article | title, short opening, body paragraphs, a closing thought |
| Blog | personal voice, direct address to the reader, a lighter tone |
| Report | title, short sections, facts, a recommendation |
| Review | what it is, what is good, what is weak, a verdict |
| Speech | greeting to the audience, direct appeals, a strong ending |

## The prompts

A stimulus of **short prompts** is printed. Treat each prompt as a bullet you must cover. Add your own ideas, but never skip a prompt. Aim to give each prompt a paragraph or a clear part of a paragraph.

## Planning in five minutes

1. **Underline** purpose, format and audience.
2. **List the prompts** as a skeleton.
3. **Add one idea or example** to each prompt.
4. **Choose the order** of paragraphs.
5. **Decide the opening and closing.**

A simple structure for a 200-word piece:

| Part | Length | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | 20 to 30 words | greeting or title, and the main idea |
| Paragraph 1 | 50 to 60 words | first prompt, with an example |
| Paragraph 2 | 50 to 60 words | second prompt, with a reason or result |
| Paragraph 3 | 40 to 50 words | third prompt |
| Closing | 20 to 30 words | summary, appeal, or good wishes |

## Worked example

**Task.** *آپ اسکول میگزین کے لیے ایک مضمون لکھ رہے ہیں جس کا مقصد طلبہ کو ماحول دوست عادتیں اپنانے پر آمادہ کرنا ہے۔ تقریباً 200 الفاظ میں لکھیے۔ ان نکات کو شامل کیجیے:* ہمارے اسکول میں کون سی عادتیں اپنائی جا سکتی ہیں؟ ان کے کیا فائدے ہوں گے؟ طلبہ اس مہم میں کیسے حصہ لے سکتے ہیں؟

- **Purpose:** to persuade.
- **Format:** magazine article.
- **Audience:** students.

**Model answer** (about 190 words):

> **ماحول دوست عادتیں: ہماری ذمہ داری**
>
> پیارے ساتھیو!
>
> آج کل ہر طرف ماحول کے مسائل کی باتیں ہو رہی ہیں۔ میرا خیال ہے کہ ہم اپنے اسکول سے اس کام کی ابتدا کر سکتے ہیں۔
>
> سب سے پہلے ہمیں کاغذ کا استعمال کم کرنا چاہیے۔ کاپیوں کے دونوں طرف لکھنا اور پرانے کاغذ کو ردی میں دینے کے بجائے دوبارہ استعمال کرنا اچھی عادتیں ہیں۔ اس کے علاوہ ہر جماعت میں کچرے کے دو ڈبے رکھے جا سکتے ہیں، ایک گیلے اور دوسرا سوکھے کچرے کے لیے۔ پانی کے نلکے استعمال کے بعد بند کرنا بھی بہت ضروری ہے۔
>
> ان عادتوں کے کئی فائدے ہیں۔ اس سے درخت کٹنے سے بچیں گے، اسکول صاف ستھرا رہے گا اور پانی اور بجلی کے بل میں بھی کمی آئے گی۔ بچی ہوئی رقم لائبریری یا کھیل کے سامان پر خرچ ہو سکتی ہے۔
>
> ہر طالب علم اس مہم میں حصہ لے سکتا ہے۔ ہم جماعت کے نگران بن سکتے ہیں، پوسٹر بنا سکتے ہیں اور اپنے گھر والوں کو بھی ان عادتوں کے بارے میں بتا سکتے ہیں۔ آئیے آج ہی عہد کریں کہ ہم اپنے اسکول کو ایک مثال بنائیں گے۔

### Why this answer works

| Feature | Where it shows |
|---|---|
| Purpose | persuasive phrases: *میرا خیال ہے*، *ضروری ہے*، *آئیے عہد کریں* |
| Format | title, greeting to readers, clear paragraphs, closing appeal |
| Audience | *پیارے ساتھیو* and *ہم* make the tone close and friendly |
| Prompts covered | habits (paragraph 2), benefits (paragraph 3), taking part (paragraph 4) |
| Organisation | one prompt per paragraph; linking phrases *سب سے پہلے*، *اس کے علاوہ*، *ان عادتوں کے* |
| Language range | modal verbs (*کر سکتے ہیں*، *چاہیے*)، future (*بچیں گے*)، conditionals of purpose, a variety of sentence lengths |
| Accuracy | verb agreement (*بل میں کمی آئے گی*)، punctuation with *۔* and *،* |

## Language that raises the quality

### Linking words and phrases

| Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|
| Sequence | سب سے پہلے، پھر، اس کے بعد، آخر میں |
| Addition | اس کے علاوہ، نیز، مزید برآں |
| Contrast | لیکن، البتہ، تاہم |
| Reason and result | اس لیے، چنانچہ، جس کی وجہ سے |
| Opinion | میرے خیال میں، میری رائے ہے کہ |
| Conclusion | خلاصہ یہ ہے کہ، آخر میں |

### Register

| Task | Register |
|---|---|
| Friend | informal, *تم*, warm closing |
| Teacher or principal | formal, *آپ*, respectful opening (*محترم*) |
| Magazine readers | friendly but clear, *ہم*, direct address |
| Report | neutral, third person, facts |

Do not mix registers. Choosing *تم* in one line and *آپ* in the next lowers the mark for W5.

### Variety

Use a mix of short and longer sentences, a range of tenses and a few connectives. Do not use difficult words you cannot spell; a simple correct word is better than a complicated wrong one.

## Common mistakes

- **Missing a prompt**, so part of the task is unanswered.
- **Ignoring the format**, for example no greeting in a letter or no title in an article.
- **Wrong register**, such as slang in a message to a principal.
- **One long paragraph**, or a list of short sentences with no links.
- **Wrong length**: far below 200 words has too little to credit; far above risks errors and lost time.
- **Translated English structures**, such as word-for-word idioms.
- **Verb agreement and gender errors**, especially in the past tense with *نے*.
- **Weak punctuation**, for example commas where a *۔* is needed.

## Exam technique

- Spend about five minutes planning and the rest writing and checking, leaving three minutes at the end.
- Write neatly in the space given.
- Check each paragraph against the prompts.
- Check verbs, genders, numbers and spelling in the final minutes.
- Practise counting your own handwriting: find how many lines make about 200 words, so that you can judge length quickly in the exam.
