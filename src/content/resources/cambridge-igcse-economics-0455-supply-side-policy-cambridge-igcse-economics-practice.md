All countries, firms and figures are invented. Learn the content first from the [supply-side policy study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-supply-side-policy-cambridge-igcse-economics/) and the [supply-side policy revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-supply-side-policy-cambridge-igcse-economics-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define supply-side policy. **[2]**

**2.** Which of the following is a supply-side policy measure?

A: a cut in the rate of interest

B: an increase in unemployment benefits during a recession

C: government funding for apprenticeships

D: a rise in the money supply

**[1]**

**3.** Identify **two** supply-side policy measures named in the syllabus, other than privatisation. **[2]**

**4.** Define privatisation. **[1]**

**5.** Which macroeconomic aim is supply-side policy most likely to harm in the short run?

A: economic growth

B: redistribution of income, if it includes cuts in income tax for high earners

C: balance of payments stability, if exports become more competitive

D: stable prices, if costs per unit fall

**[1]**

**6.** A firm buys machinery for 120,000. Corporation tax is 20%, and the full cost may be deducted from taxable profit. Calculate the tax saved. **[1]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Ana receives benefits of 160 a week. A job offers 280 a week before income tax of 15%. Her benefits stop if she takes the job.

**(a)** Calculate her weekly gain from taking the job. **[2]**

**(b)** The government lets new workers keep 50 a week of benefits for their first year in work. Calculate her new weekly gain. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how this change may help reduce unemployment. **[3]**

**8.** Explain how education and training may help a government achieve economic growth. **[4]**

**9.** A country removes rules that allowed only three firms to run bus services between its cities.

**(a)** Identify the supply-side measure being used. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain **two** possible benefits for consumers. **[4]**

**10.** Explain why supply-side policy may not reduce unemployment during a deep recession. **[3]**

**11.** Analyse how supply-side policy may help a country achieve balance of payments stability. **[5]**

**12.** Discuss whether privatisation will improve the performance of a state-owned electricity company. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Government measures to increase the productive capacity of the economy [1]; by improving the quantity, quality or efficiency of factors of production and markets [1]. **[2]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A and D are monetary policy; B is fiscal policy that raises spending.

**3.** Any two: education and training [1]; labour market reforms [1]; lower direct taxes; deregulation; improving incentives to work and invest. **[2]**

**4.** The sale of state-owned firms or assets to the private sector [1]. **[1]**

**5.** B [1]. **[1]**

**6.** 120,000 x 0.20 = **24,000** [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** Take-home pay = 280 x 0.85 = 238 [1]; gain = 238 - 160 = **78** a week [1].

**(b)** 78 + 50 = **128** a week [1].

**(c)** The extra 50 a week makes working clearly more rewarding than staying on benefits [1], so more unemployed people are willing to accept jobs [1]. As they take vacancies, unemployment falls and output rises [1]. **[6]**

**8.** Education and training raise workers' skills and knowledge [1], so each worker produces more output per hour [1]. Higher productivity increases the economy's productive capacity, shifting the PPC outwards [1]. Skilled workers can also fill vacancies in new industries, so more of the labour force is used productively, raising real GDP [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** Deregulation [1].

**(b)** New firms can enter, so competition rises and fares are likely to fall [1], leaving travellers with more income to spend on other goods [1]. Firms compete for passengers by offering more routes, more frequent buses or better service [1], so consumers have more choice and better quality [1]. **[5]**

**10.** In a deep recession, unemployment is mainly caused by weak spending [1]. Supply-side policy raises the economy's ability to produce, but firms will not hire more workers if they cannot sell extra output [1]. Measures such as training also take years, so they do not help quickly [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Training and investment raise productivity, so firms' costs per unit fall [1]. Lower costs let exporters charge lower prices abroad, so foreign demand for exports rises [1]. Better quality and new products also make domestic goods more attractive than imports [1], so consumers and firms buy fewer imports [1]. Higher exports and lower imports improve the current account, moving it towards stability [1]. **[5]**

**12.** Improve: private owners seeking profit have an incentive to cut waste and costs [1]; they may invest in new technology and better customer service to attract customers [1]; the government receives sale revenue and no longer has to cover any losses [1].
Not improve: electricity networks may be a natural monopoly, so a private owner facing no competition may raise prices [1]; it may cut investment in unprofitable rural supply or maintenance to raise profit [1].
Judgement: privatisation is more likely to improve performance if competition is introduced or prices are regulated; without these, the gains may go to owners rather than consumers [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling interest rate cuts or benefit increases supply-side policy.
- Forgetting to deduct income tax before comparing pay with benefits.
- Ignoring time lags when evaluating training or investment.
- Treating privatisation as automatically beneficial, without discussing competition.
- Explaining a measure without linking it to the aim named in the question.
