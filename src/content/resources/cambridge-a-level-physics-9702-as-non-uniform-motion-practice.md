## Section A

**1.** An object falls from rest through air. Which statement describes its acceleration before it reaches terminal velocity?

A: It is constant and equal to g.

B: It increases from zero to g.

C: It decreases from g towards zero.

D: It is zero throughout. **[1]**

**2.** A skydiver is falling at terminal velocity. Which statement is correct?

A: No forces act on the skydiver.

B: The drag is greater than the weight.

C: The resultant force is zero and the velocity is constant.

D: The drag is still increasing. **[1]**

**3.** State the simple model of drag force that the syllabus uses. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** ways in which friction and air resistance are similar. **[2]**

**5.** A cyclist and bicycle of total mass 85 kg have a driving force of 220 N. The total resistive force is 90 N. Calculate the acceleration. **[2]**

**6.** Two balls of identical size and shape, one heavier than the other, are dropped from a high building. State which ball reaches the higher terminal velocity and explain why. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A skydiver of mass 90 kg jumps from a stationary balloon.

**(a)** Calculate the skydiver's weight. **[1]**

**(b)** At one instant the drag is 520 N. Calculate the acceleration at that instant. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain, in terms of forces, why the skydiver eventually falls at constant velocity. **[3]**

**8.** The skydiver in Question 7 later opens the parachute while falling at terminal velocity. Immediately after opening, the drag is 2400 N.

**(a)** Calculate the magnitude and state the direction of the acceleration immediately after the parachute opens. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how the velocity changes after the parachute opens until the skydiver lands. **[3]**

**9.** Sketch a velocity-time graph for the whole descent in Questions 7 and 8, from leaving the balloon until just before landing. Label **three** key features. **[4]**

**10.** A ball of mass 0.20 kg is thrown vertically upwards. At a certain speed the air resistance on it is 0.50 N.

**(a)** Calculate the acceleration of the ball when it is rising at this speed. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the acceleration when it is falling at the same speed. **[1]**

**(c)** State the acceleration at the highest point, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why the ball returns to the thrower's hand at a lower speed than it was thrown. **[1]**

**11.** A small steel ball is released in a tall cylinder of oil. Its weight is 0.020 N and the upthrust on it is 0.0030 N.

**(a)** Name the three forces acting on the ball while it falls, and give their directions. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the viscous drag on the ball when it reaches terminal velocity. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest why the ball reaches terminal velocity in a much shorter distance than it would in air. **[1]**

**12.** A car moving along a level road has a constant driving force. Explain why the car reaches a maximum speed, even though the driving force does not change. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The drag force increases as the speed increases (and acts opposite to the velocity) [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two: both are resistive forces that oppose motion [1]; both transfer kinetic energy to thermal energy (and other forms) [1]; both act opposite to the direction of relative motion. **[2]**

**5.** Resultant = 220 − 90 = 130 N [1]; a = 130 ÷ 85 = **1.5 m s⁻²** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The heavier ball [1]. It needs a larger drag to balance its larger weight; since drag increases with speed, it must reach a higher speed before drag equals weight [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** W = 90 × 9.81 = **883 N** [1].

**(b)** Resultant = 883 − 520 = 363 N [1]; a = 363 ÷ 90 = **4.0 m s⁻²** downwards [1].

**(c)** As the speed increases, the drag increases [1]; the resultant force (weight − drag), and so the acceleration, decreases [1]; when drag equals weight the resultant is zero and the velocity is constant (terminal velocity) [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** Resultant = 883 − 2400 = −1517 N, i.e. 1517 N upwards [1]; a = 1517 ÷ 90 = **16.9 m s⁻² upwards** [1].

**(b)** The skydiver keeps moving downwards but slows down [1]; as the speed falls, the drag falls, so the deceleration decreases [1]; a new, lower terminal velocity is reached when drag again equals 883 N, and the skydiver falls at that constant speed until landing [1]. **[5]**

**9.** Any three labelled features, with a curve of correct shape:
Initial gradient equal to g (straight-ish start through the origin) [1].
Curve with decreasing gradient levelling off at the first terminal velocity [1].
Sharp but continuous fall in velocity when the parachute opens [1].
Second, lower horizontal section labelled as the new terminal velocity [1].
*(Award the fourth mark only for a correct overall shape with the three features labelled.)* **[4]**

**10. (a)** W = 0.20 × 9.81 = 1.96 N; rising, drag acts downwards: resultant = 1.96 + 0.50 = 2.46 N [1]; a = 2.46 ÷ 0.20 = **12.3 m s⁻² downwards** [1].

**(b)** Falling, drag acts upwards: a = (1.96 − 0.50) ÷ 0.20 = **7.3 m s⁻² downwards** [1].

**(c)** **9.81 m s⁻²** (g) downwards [1], because the ball is momentarily at rest, so the drag is zero and only weight acts [1].

**(d)** Work is done against air resistance throughout the flight, so some kinetic energy is transferred to the air and the ball has less kinetic energy when it returns [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** Weight, downwards; upthrust, upwards [1]; viscous drag, upwards (opposite to the velocity) [1].

**(b)** At terminal velocity: drag = weight − upthrust = 0.020 − 0.0030 = **0.017 N** [1].

**(c)** The viscous drag in oil is much larger than air resistance at the same speed, so the drag balances the weight at a much lower speed, which is reached quickly [1]. **[4]**

**12.** As the car speeds up, the resistive forces (air resistance) increase [1]. The resultant force (driving force − resistive force) therefore decreases, so the acceleration decreases [1]. When the resistive force equals the driving force, the resultant force is zero and the car moves at constant (maximum) speed [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing the acceleration as constant before terminal velocity.
- Saying no forces act at terminal velocity.
- Stating that the skydiver moves upwards when the parachute opens.
- Forgetting that drag acts downwards while a thrown ball rises.
- Omitting upthrust when an object falls through a liquid.
- Giving an explanation that jumps straight to "terminal velocity" without the chain from speed to drag to resultant force to acceleration.
