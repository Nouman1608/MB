## Tests for the presence of water

| Anhydrous reagent | Before | After adding water |
|---|---|---|
| copper(II) sulfate | **white** | **blue** |
| cobalt(II) chloride | **blue** | **pink** |

- Positive result = water is **present**.
- Says nothing about whether the liquid is **pure** water.
- Both changes reverse on heating (water driven off).

## Testing purity

| | Pure water | Impure water |
|---|---|---|
| Melting point | exactly 0 °C | below 0 °C, over a range |
| Boiling point | exactly 100 °C | above 100 °C, over a range |

- Measure the boiling point with the thermometer **in the boiling liquid**.
- Pure substances have **sharp** melting and boiling points.

## Distilled water in practical chemistry

- Tap water contains **dissolved chemical impurities** (for example metal ions and chloride ions).
- Distilled water contains **fewer** chemical impurities.
- Impurities could give **false results in tests**, **react** with chemicals, or change **concentrations**.

## Substances in natural water

| Substance | Effect |
|---|---|
| Dissolved oxygen | beneficial: aquatic life needs it |
| Metal compounds | some beneficial (essential minerals); some harmful (toxic) |
| Plastics | harmful: harm aquatic life |
| Sewage | harmful: contains microbes that cause disease |
| Harmful microbes | harmful: cause disease |
| Nitrates (from fertilisers) | harmful: deoxygenation, damage to aquatic life |
| Phosphates (from fertilisers and detergents) | harmful: deoxygenation, damage to aquatic life |

**Phosphate sources:** fertilisers **and detergents**. Nitrate source: fertilisers.

## Domestic water treatment

1. **Sedimentation** – large insoluble particles settle.
2. **Filtration** – smaller insoluble particles removed (for example through sand and gravel).
3. **Carbon** – removes **tastes and odours**.
4. **Chlorination** – **kills microbes**.

**Not removed:** dissolved salts. Treated water is safe to drink but not pure.

## Why the stages come in this order

- **Sedimentation first:** the largest particles settle, so the filter does not clog.
- **Filtration next:** removes the smaller particles that did not settle.
- **Carbon:** removes tastes and odours from the clear water.
- **Chlorination last:** kills the microbes that solids, filters and carbon cannot remove.

## Answer frames

**"Describe a test for water" (2 marks):** reagent and its starting colour, then the colour with water. "Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate turns from white to blue."

**"How would you show the water is pure?" (2–3 marks):** what you measure, then the values. "Measure the boiling point; pure water boils at exactly 100 °C."

**"Explain why distilled water is used" (2 marks):** tap water contains dissolved impurities, and these could react or give false test results.

**"Is this substance beneficial or harmful?":** name the effect, not just the label. "Dissolved oxygen is beneficial because aquatic life needs it."

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Anhydrous | without water (water of the crystals removed) |
| Hydrated | containing chemically combined water |
| Pure substance | a single substance with nothing mixed in; sharp melting and boiling points |
| Distilled water | water purified by distillation, with fewer chemical impurities |
| Sedimentation | letting insoluble particles settle out of still water |
| Filtration | separating insoluble solids from a liquid using a filter |
| Chlorination | adding chlorine to water to kill microbes |
| Deoxygenation | loss of dissolved oxygen from water |
| Microbes | microorganisms, some of which cause disease |

## Exam traps

- **Colour tests ≠ purity tests.** Use melting and boiling points for purity.
- **Cobalt(II) chloride: blue to pink.** Copper(II) sulfate: white to blue.
- **Impurities raise boiling point** and lower melting point.
- **Each treatment stage has one job.** Do not say chlorine removes solids or smells.
- **Filtration does not remove dissolved substances.**
- **No eutrophication essay.** "Deoxygenation of water and damage to aquatic life" is the expected answer.
- **Metal compounds can be beneficial or harmful.** Give both if asked to classify them.
- **Phosphates come from detergents as well as fertilisers.**

## Self-test

1. Describe the test for water using anhydrous copper(II) sulfate.
2. State the colour change when water is added to anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride.
3. A colourless liquid turns anhydrous copper(II) sulfate blue. Does this prove it is pure water? Explain.
4. State the melting point and boiling point of pure water.
5. A water sample boils at 102 °C. What does this show?
6. Give **one** reason why distilled water is used rather than tap water in a test for chloride ions.
7. Name **two** substances in natural water that are beneficial.
8. Name the **two** sources of phosphates in natural water.
9. State why sewage in water is harmful.
10. State the purpose of treating water with carbon.
11. State the purpose of chlorination.
12. Explain why treated tap water is not pure.

**Answers:**

1. Add the water sample to white anhydrous copper(II) sulfate; it turns blue if water is present.
2. Blue to pink.
3. No. It shows water is present, but there could be dissolved substances; only melting and boiling points show purity.
4. 0 °C and 100 °C.
5. It is impure: dissolved impurities raise the boiling point.
6. Tap water may already contain chloride ions, which would give a false positive result.
7. Dissolved oxygen; some metal compounds (essential minerals).
8. Fertilisers and detergents.
9. It contains harmful microbes that cause disease.
10. To remove tastes and odours.
11. To kill microbes.
12. Treatment removes insoluble solids, tastes, odours and microbes, but not dissolved salts; chlorine is also added.
