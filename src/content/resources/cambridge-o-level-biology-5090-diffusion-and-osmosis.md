## Water as a solvent

Water dissolves many substances. In organisms it is the **solvent** in which chemical reactions happen, and it carries substances from place to place. The syllabus names three roles.

- **Digestion**: food is broken down in a watery mixture, and the enzymes and the substances they act on are dissolved in it.
- **Excretion**: waste such as urea dissolves in water, so it can be removed in urine.
- **Transport**: dissolved glucose, amino acids, ions and hormones travel in the blood plasma, and dissolved minerals and sucrose move in plants.

## Where the energy comes from

Molecules and ions in liquids and gases are always moving in random directions. This is **kinetic energy**. Diffusion and osmosis need **no energy from respiration**. They are called **passive** processes because the energy comes from the random movement of the particles themselves.

## Diffusion

**Diffusion is the net movement of molecules or ions from a region of their higher concentration to a region of their lower concentration, down a concentration gradient, as a result of their random movement.**

Three points make this definition score.

1. It is **net** movement. Particles move in every direction, but more move from the high concentration to the low than the other way, so overall there is movement down the gradient.
2. It happens **down** a concentration gradient. It stops being a "net" movement when the concentrations are equal, although the particles keep moving.
3. It is a result of **random movement**, not of a force that pushes the particles.

Examples: oxygen diffuses from the air in the alveoli into the blood; carbon dioxide diffuses from respiring cells into the blood; the smell of cooking spreads through a room.

### Factors that affect the rate of diffusion

| Factor | Effect on rate | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Surface area | Larger area, faster diffusion | More particles can cross at the same time |
| Temperature | Higher temperature, faster diffusion | Particles have more kinetic energy and move faster |
| Concentration gradient | Steeper gradient, faster diffusion | More particles move down the gradient than back |
| Distance | Shorter distance, faster diffusion | Particles have less far to travel |

These four are the only factors the syllabus lists, so learn them with their reasons.

### Worked example: surface area to volume ratio

*Agar cubes of side 1 cm, 2 cm and 3 cm are placed in dilute hydrochloric acid. The acid diffuses in and removes the pink colour. Which cube loses colour fastest, and why?*

For a cube of side L, surface area = 6 x L x L and volume = L x L x L.

| Side | Surface area (cm squared) | Volume (cm cubed) | Surface area : volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 cm | 6 | 1 | 6 : 1 |
| 2 cm | 24 | 8 | 3 : 1 |
| 3 cm | 54 | 27 | 2 : 1 |

The 1 cm cube has the largest ratio, so acid needs to travel the shortest distance to reach its centre and there is more surface per unit of volume. It loses colour first. This is why small organisms can rely on diffusion across their surface, while large organisms need special exchange surfaces and transport systems.

## Osmosis

**Osmosis is the net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential to a region of lower water potential, through a partially permeable membrane.**

Water potential is a measure of how freely water can move. **Pure water has the highest water potential.** A dilute solution has a high water potential, and a concentrated solution has a low water potential. Osmosis is therefore a special case of diffusion: it is the diffusion of water across a membrane that lets water through but stops some dissolved substances.

A model answer about osmosis should include three things: **water molecules**, **net movement from higher to lower water potential**, and **partially permeable membrane**. Leaving out the membrane is the most common lost mark.

### The cell membrane and cell wall

The cell membrane is **partially permeable**: it lets water and some small molecules through but controls the passage of larger ones. The cellulose cell wall of a plant cell is **fully permeable**, so it does not control what passes through.

## Osmosis in plant cells

Consider a plant cell placed in different solutions.

| Surroundings | Direction of water movement | Result for the cell |
|---|---|---|
| Pure water or a dilute solution (higher water potential than the cell) | Into the cell | Vacuole swells and presses the cytoplasm against the wall. The cell becomes **turgid**. |
| A solution with the same water potential | No net movement | The cell is neither turgid nor plasmolysed. |
| A concentrated solution (lower water potential than the cell) | Out of the cell | The cell loses water and becomes **flaccid**. With more water loss the membrane pulls away from the wall: **plasmolysis**. |

**Turgor pressure** is the pressure of the cell contents pushing outwards on the cell wall. A turgid cell is firm. The strong wall stops the cell bursting. Plants are **supported by turgor pressure**: a plant with turgid cells stays upright, and a plant that loses water wilts because its cells go flaccid.

## Osmosis in animal cells

Animal cells have no cell wall. A red blood cell placed in pure water takes in water by osmosis. It swells and bursts, because there is no wall to resist the expansion. In a concentrated solution it loses water and shrinks. This is why intravenous fluids and body fluids must have a water potential close to that of blood cells.

## Practical: potato cylinders

*Identical cylinders of potato are weighed and placed in sucrose solutions of different concentration for a set time. They are removed, blotted dry and weighed again.*

| Sucrose (mol per dm cubed) | Start mass (g) | End mass (g) | Change (g) | Percentage change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 3.10 | 3.52 | +0.42 | +13.5 |
| 0.2 | 3.05 | 3.20 | +0.15 | +4.9 |
| 0.4 | 3.08 | 3.03 | -0.05 | -1.6 |
| 0.6 | 3.12 | 2.82 | -0.30 | -9.6 |
| 0.8 | 3.10 | 2.64 | -0.46 | -14.8 |

**Method for the percentage change**: change in mass divided by start mass, multiplied by 100. For the 0.2 solution: 0.15 divided by 3.05 is 0.049, so +4.9 per cent.

Percentage change is used because the start masses differ slightly, and a percentage allows a fair comparison.

**Interpreting the data**: potato gained mass in weak solutions because water entered by osmosis, and lost mass in strong solutions because water left. The concentration at which there is **no change in mass** shows where the solution and the potato cells have the same water potential. This lies between 0.2 and 0.4. Using a straight line between those two points, the zero crossing is about 0.35 mol per dm cubed.

**Controls and reliability**: same size cylinders, same volume of solution, same time and temperature, blotting in the same way, and repeating each concentration to find a mean.

## Practical: dialysis tubing

Dialysis tubing is a partially permeable membrane. Small molecules such as water and glucose pass through, but large molecules such as starch do not.

*A student fills a tube with a mixture of starch and glucose solutions and stands it in a beaker of water.*

After 20 minutes the water outside is tested.

- With iodine solution: no colour change, so starch did **not** pass through.
- With Benedict's solution and heating: the colour changes from blue to orange-red, so glucose **did** pass through.

This shows that the tubing is partially permeable. It models the way a cell membrane lets small molecules through but holds larger ones back.

## Common mistakes

- Writing that osmosis is "diffusion of water" with no mention of a **partially permeable membrane** and **water potential**.
- Using "concentration of water" when the syllabus term is **water potential**.
- Saying a plasmolysed cell has burst; plant cells do not burst, because of the wall.
- Saying diffusion needs energy from respiration. It does not.
- Forgetting percentage change when starting masses differ.
- Stating that particles stop moving at equilibrium; only the net movement stops.

## Command words

- **Define**: give the exact meaning, for example of osmosis.
- **Describe**: state what happens, for example the effect of a solution on a plant cell.
- **Explain**: give the reason using water potential and the membrane.
- **Calculate**: show working, as in a percentage change in mass.
- **Suggest**: apply the idea to a new situation, for example a wilted plant.
