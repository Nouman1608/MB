These questions cover syllabus section 3.15 of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (first assessment 2021), which is HL only: classifying lines as coincident, parallel, intersecting or skew, and finding points of intersection. Questions are labelled "(calculator-free)" or "(calculator allowed)"; give calculator answers to 3 significant figures. Vectors are written in rows. The methods are in the [3.15 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-15/) and the [3.15 revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-15-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (calculator-free) Two lines have direction vectors (1, −2, 3) and (−2, 4, −6).

**(a)** State the relationship between the directions. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the lines cannot be skew. **[1]**

**2.** (calculator-free) L₁: r = (1, 0, 2) + λ(2, 1, −1) and L₂: r = (5, 2, 1) + μ(4, 2, −2). Determine whether the lines are coincident or parallel. **[3]**

**3.** (calculator-free) Show that L₁: r = (1, 1, 0) + λ(1, 2, 1) and L₂: r = (2, 0, 4) + μ(0, 1, −1) intersect, and find the point of intersection. **[4]**

**4.** (calculator-free) Find the point of intersection of r = (1, 2) + λ(3, 1) and r = (4, 0) + μ(1, −1). **[3]**

**5.** (calculator-free)

**(a)** Define skew lines. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why two lines in a plane cannot be skew. **[1]**

**6.** (calculator allowed) Find the acute angle between the lines in question 3. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** (calculator-free) Show that L₁: r = (3, 1, 0) + λ(1, 1, 2) and L₂: r = (0, 2, 1) + μ(2, −1, 1) are skew. **[5]**

**8.** (calculator-free) L₁: r = (1, −1, 2) + λ(2, 1, 0) and L₂: r = (3, k, 5) + μ(1, 0, 3). Given that the lines intersect, find k and the point of intersection. **[5]**

**9.** (calculator allowed) Two particles move so that their positions after t seconds are A: r = (2, −1, 3) + t(1, 2, −1) and B: r = (5, 8, −3) + t(0, −1, 1), in metres.

**(a)** Show that the particles collide, and find the time and position of the collision. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the speed of each particle. **[2]**

**10.** (calculator-free) Two drones have positions A: r = t(1, 1, 1) and B: r = (1, 3, 2) + t(2, 0, 1) after t minutes.

**(a)** Show that their paths cross, and find the point where they cross. **[3]**

**(b)** Show that the drones do not collide. **[2]**

**11.** (calculator-free) L₁: r = (1, 2, 3) + λ(2, a, −1) and L₂: r = (5, b, 1) + μ(4, 6, −2) are coincident. Find a and b. **[4]**

**12.** (calculator allowed) L₁: r = λ(1, 2, 3) and L₂: r = (1, 1, 1) + μ(1, 0, −1).

**(a)** Show that the lines intersect and find the point of intersection. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the acute angle between the lines. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** (−2, 4, −6) = −2(1, −2, 3), so the directions are **parallel** [1]

**(b)** Skew lines must have non-parallel directions, so these lines are parallel or coincident [1]

**2.** (4, 2, −2) = 2(2, 1, −1), so the directions are parallel [1]. Test (5, 2, 1) in L₁: x gives λ = 2, then y = 2 ✓ but z = 2 − 2 = 0 ≠ 1 [1]. The point is not on L₁, so the lines are **parallel** (and distinct) [1]

**3.** x: 1 + λ = 2, so λ = 1 [1]; y: 1 + 2 = μ, so μ = 3 [1]. z: L₁ gives 1, L₂ gives 4 − 3 = 1, so the third equation holds and the lines intersect [1]. Point **(2, 3, 1)** [1]

**4.** 1 + 3λ = 4 + μ and 2 + λ = −μ [1]. Substituting μ = −2 − λ: 1 + 3λ = 2 − λ, so λ = 1/4 [1]. Point **(7/4, 9/4)** [1]

**5. (a)** Lines that are **not parallel and do not intersect** [1]

**(b)** In a plane, two non-parallel lines always meet, so non-parallel lines in two dimensions always intersect [1]

**6.** |(1, 2, 1) · (0, 1, −1)| = |0 + 2 − 1| = 1 [1]; magnitudes √6 and √2, so cos θ = 1/√12 [1]; **θ = 73.2°** [1]

**7.** (2, −1, 1) is not a multiple of (1, 1, 2), so the lines are not parallel [1]. x: 3 + λ = 2μ and y: 1 + λ = 2 − μ [1]. Subtracting: 2 = 3μ − 2, so μ = 4/3 [1] and λ = −1/3 [1]. z: L₁ gives 2λ = −2/3 but L₂ gives 1 + μ = 7/3, which are not equal, so there is no common point. The lines are not parallel and do not intersect, so they are **skew** [1]

*Examiner insight:* The final mark needs both reasons in the conclusion. "The z-components don't match, so skew" without mentioning the directions is incomplete.

**8.** Equate: x: 1 + 2λ = 3 + μ; y: −1 + λ = k; z: 2 = 5 + 3μ [1]. From z, **μ = −1** [1]. From x, 1 + 2λ = 2, so **λ = 1/2** [1]. From y, **k = −1/2** [1]. The point is **(2, −1/2, 2)** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Start with the equations that do not contain k; the remaining equation then gives k directly.

**9. (a)** Use the same t: x: 2 + t = 5, so t = 3 [1]. y: −1 + 6 = 5 and 8 − 3 = 5 ✓ [1]; z: 3 − 3 = 0 and −3 + 3 = 0 ✓ [1]. The particles collide at **t = 3 s at (5, 5, 0)** [1]

**(b)** A: √(1 + 4 + 1) = **√6 = 2.45 m s⁻¹** [1]; B: √(0 + 1 + 1) = **√2 = 1.41 m s⁻¹** [1]

**10. (a)** Use parameters s and u: s = 1 + 2u, s = 3, s = 2 + u [1]. From the second, s = 3; then the third gives u = 1, and the first gives 1 + 2 = 3 ✓ [1]. The paths cross at **(3, 3, 3)** [1]

**(b)** A is at (3, 3, 3) when t = 3, but B is there when t = 1 [1]. They are at the crossing point at different times, so they **do not collide** [1]

**11.** For coincident lines the directions are parallel: (4, 6, −2) = 2(2, a, −1), so 2a = 6 and **a = 3** [1]. (5, b, 1) lies on L₁: x gives 1 + 2λ = 5, so λ = 2 [1]; z: 3 − 2 = 1 ✓ [1]; y: b = 2 + 3(2), so **b = 8** [1]

**12. (a)** λ = 1 + μ, 2λ = 1 and 3λ = 1 − μ [1]. From the second, λ = 1/2, so μ = −1/2; the third gives 3/2 = 1 + 1/2 ✓ [1]. Point **(1/2, 1, 3/2)** [1]

**(b)** |(1, 2, 3) · (1, 0, −1)| = |1 − 3| = 2, so cos θ = 2/(√14 × √2) [1]; **θ = 67.8°** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using one parameter for both lines when testing for intersection.
- Claiming skew without first showing that the directions are not parallel.
- Calling parallel-direction lines "parallel" without testing for a common point.
- Treating crossing paths as a collision, as in question 10.
- Not giving the point of intersection after showing that the lines meet.
