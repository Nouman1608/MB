This guide goes deeper into one sub-topic of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028: **19.1 Energy flow**, the first part of topic 19, Organisms and their environment. It has two learning outcomes, and both are **Core**, so every candidate needs them on every paper. There is no Supplement content in 19.1, but the ideas here underpin the Extended work on energy pyramids and efficiency in 19.2.

## What sub-topic 19.1 asks you to do

| Outcome | Syllabus wording (summarised) | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| 19.1.1 | State that the Sun is the principal source of energy input to biological systems | Core |
| 19.1.2 | Describe the flow of energy through living organisms, including light energy from the Sun and chemical energy in organisms, and its eventual transfer to the environment | Core |

Outcome 1 says **state**: one sentence is enough. Outcome 2 says **describe**, so you must give the energy forms and the transfers in the right order.

## 19.1.1 The Sun as the principal energy source

Almost every food chain on Earth begins with a producer that photosynthesises. Producers absorb light energy from the Sun, so the energy in a grass blade, a rabbit that eats it and a fox that eats the rabbit all came from sunlight. That is why the Sun is described as the **principal** (main) source of energy input to biological systems.

Two consequences follow:

- **Energy must keep entering.** Because energy leaves living systems all the time, an ecosystem only keeps running while light keeps arriving. If light is cut off, producers stop photosynthesising and, in time, every organism that depends on them runs short of energy.
- **Stored energy came from the Sun too.** Coal, oil and gas formed from organisms that lived millions of years ago. The chemical energy in fossil fuels was originally light energy captured by producers in photosynthesis.

## 19.1.2 Following the energy step by step

Learn the flow as a sequence of **forms** and **transfers**.

### Step 1: light energy to chemical energy (producers)

Chlorophyll in the chloroplasts of producers absorbs **light energy**. In photosynthesis this energy is transferred into **chemical energy** in glucose. The plant then uses glucose to make other organic compounds: starch for storage, cellulose for cell walls, and, with mineral ions, proteins and fats. All of these contain chemical energy. Revise the details in the [photosynthesis study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-photosynthesis-cambridge-igcse-biology/).

Only part of the light reaching a plant is captured. Some light is reflected, some passes straight through the leaf and some falls on parts that do not photosynthesise.

### Step 2: chemical energy passed along by feeding

When a herbivore eats a plant, it digests and absorbs the organic compounds, so some of the chemical energy passes to the herbivore. When a carnivore eats the herbivore, some chemical energy passes on again. Energy moves along a food chain **only as chemical energy in food**.

Not all the chemical energy in one organism reaches the next:

- some parts are **not eaten**, such as roots, bones or the woody stem;
- some food is **not digested** and leaves in faeces;
- much of the absorbed food is **respired**, not built into the body.

### Step 3: respiration and the uses of energy

Every organism, producers included, respires. Respiration releases energy from glucose. The organism uses this energy for processes such as:

- muscle contraction and movement;
- protein synthesis and growth;
- cell division;
- active transport;
- transmission of nerve impulses;
- maintaining a constant body temperature in mammals and birds.

Whichever process the energy is used for, it ends up being transferred to the surroundings as **heat** (thermal energy). A running gazelle warms the air around it; a growing seedling releases heat as it respires.

### Step 4: dead material, waste and decomposers

Energy in dead leaves, dead bodies, faeces and urine is not lost to living things straight away. **Decomposers**, such as bacteria and fungi, feed on this material and respire, so they too transfer the energy to the environment as heat. A compost heap is warm because so many decomposers are respiring inside it.

### The complete flow

```
light energy (Sun)
   → chemical energy in producers   (photosynthesis)
   → chemical energy in consumers   (feeding)
   → chemical energy in decomposers (dead material and waste)
At every stage: respiration → energy transferred to the environment as heat
```

## Energy flows; nutrients cycle

Energy follows a **one-way path**: it enters as light and leaves as heat, and plants cannot use heat to photosynthesise. Chemical elements such as carbon and nitrogen are different: decomposers return them to the soil and air, and producers use them again. This contrast is tested often.

| | Energy | Carbon and other nutrients |
|---|---|---|
| Enters as | light | carbon dioxide, mineral ions |
| Leaves living systems as | heat | carbon dioxide, ions released by decomposers |
| Reused by producers? | no | yes |
| Described as | flowing through | being recycled |

## Worked example 1: reading an energy-flow diagram

A diagram shows energy entering and leaving a cow in one day (illustrative figures, in kJ):

- chemical energy in grass eaten: 100 000
- energy in faeces: 60 000
- energy in urine and methane: 4 000
- energy transferred to the environment as heat from respiration: 32 000
- energy in new body tissue: the remainder

**(a) Calculate the energy in new body tissue.**
100 000 − 60 000 − 4 000 − 32 000 = **4 000 kJ**.

**(b) What happens to the energy in the faeces?**
Decomposers feed on the faeces. They respire and transfer the energy to the environment as heat.

**(c) Why is heat from respiration not passed to the next consumer?**
Heat is transferred to the surroundings and is not stored in the cow's body, so a consumer that eats the cow cannot obtain it.

## Worked example 2: annotated model answer

**Question:** Describe the flow of energy from the Sun through a food chain of grass, rabbit and fox. [4]

**Answer:** Grass absorbs light energy from the Sun using chlorophyll (1). Photosynthesis transfers it to chemical energy in glucose and other organic compounds (1). When the rabbit eats grass, and the fox eats the rabbit, chemical energy passes along the chain in food (1). At each stage energy is transferred to the environment as heat, mainly from respiration (1).

**Why it scores:** it names both energy forms, the process that changes one into the other, how energy moves along the chain and how it leaves.

## Common mistakes

- Saying plants "get energy from the soil" or "from water". The energy source is light.
- Writing that energy is "made", "produced" or "used up". Energy is **transferred**.
- Saying respiration "makes energy". It **releases** energy from glucose.
- Describing energy passing as "light energy" from plant to herbivore. It passes as chemical energy in food.
- Claiming energy is recycled by decomposers. Decomposers recycle nutrients; they release energy as heat.
- Forgetting that producers and decomposers respire and lose heat too.

## Exam technique for 19.1 command words

- **State**: "The Sun is the principal source of energy input to biological systems." Nothing more is needed.
- **Describe** energy flow: give forms in order (light, then chemical), name the processes (photosynthesis, feeding, respiration) and end with transfer to the environment as heat.
- **Explain** a value in an energy diagram: say where the energy went (heat, faeces, uneaten parts) and why it does not reach the next organism.
- **Calculate**: subtract every listed loss from the input and give the unit (kJ).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is energy flow Core or Extended in Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610?**
Sub-topic 19.1 is entirely Core. The Extended ideas about pyramids of energy and the efficiency of energy transfer are in sub-topic 19.2.

**Why is energy not recycled in an ecosystem?**
Energy leaves living organisms as heat, and producers cannot use heat to make food. Only light can be captured by photosynthesis, so new light energy must keep entering.

**In what form does energy pass from one organism to the next?**
As chemical energy in the organic compounds of food.
