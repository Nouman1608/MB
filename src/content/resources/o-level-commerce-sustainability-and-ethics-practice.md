## Section A - multiple choice

**1.** Which action is an example of *sustainable logistics*? **[1]**
A selling eco-friendly products   B planning delivery routes to reduce fuel use
C offering free repairs   D using persuasive advertising

**2.** Which best describes *ethics* in commerce? **[1]**
A rules made by government   B moral principles guiding business behaviour
C methods of payment   D ways of raising finance

**3.** A business that offers a platform for customers to sell their used items to other customers is
offering: **[1]**
A a cash and carry   B a digital resale marketplace   C a loss leader   D debt factoring

## Section B - structured questions

**4.** *Bloom Cosmetics sells skin cream in plastic jars and sends orders by courier.*
(a) Explain **three** actions Bloom Cosmetics could take to improve sustainability. **[6]**
(b) Explain **one** reason Bloom Cosmetics might be reluctant to take these actions. **[2]**

**5.** *An online shoe seller finds that one in four pairs is returned.*
(a) Explain why a high level of returns is unsustainable. **[2]**
(b) Explain **two** ways the seller could reduce returns. **[4]**

**6.** *A soft-drinks factory is located in a region with water shortages.* Discuss the importance of water
stewardship for the factory. **[6]**

**7.** *A café chain is deciding whether to sell only fair trade coffee.*
(a) Explain the role of fair trade in commerce. **[3]**
(b) Discuss whether the café chain should make this change. **[6]**

**8.** *A gym advertises "Lose 10 kg in a week - guaranteed!" A battery maker is found to be dumping waste
in a river.*
(a) Identify the ethical issue in each case. **[2]**
(b) Explain how each issue could affect the business. **[4]**

**9.** Explain **three** reasons why consumer protection is needed. **[6]**

**10.** *Leila's new kettle stops working after a week. The shop says it is "not our problem".*
(a) Describe the actions Leila could take to resolve her complaint. **[4]**
(b) Explain why it is in the shop's interest to resolve complaints fairly. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** B. **[1]**

**2.** B. **[1]**

**3.** B. **[1]**

**4.** (a) Any three explained: reduce or replace plastic packaging with refillable or recyclable jars **[2]**;
use sustainable materials (responsibly sourced ingredients) **[2]**; implement sustainable logistics, such
as couriers using electric vehicles or grouped deliveries **[2]**; recycle and reduce waste in production
**[2]**; sell eco-friendly products **[2]**. **[max 6]**
(b) Higher short-term costs could reduce profit or force higher prices, and customers may buy cheaper
brands **[2]**.

**5.** (a) Each return means extra transport (fuel and emissions) and packaging **[1]**, and some returned goods
cannot be resold and are wasted **[1]**.
(b) Any two explained: accurate size guides and fitting advice **[2]**; clear photos and descriptions **[2]**;
customer reviews about fit **[2]**; virtual try-on tools **[2]**. **[max 4]**

**6.** **[6]** Importance: water is a key input, so careful use protects the factory's own supply; reducing
use lowers costs; treating waste water prevents pollution and fines; sharing water fairly maintains good
relations with the community and protects reputation. Limits: water-saving and treatment equipment is
costly; benefits take time. Conclusion: very important in a water-short region, because without water
the factory cannot operate.

**7.** (a) Fair trade ensures producers receive a fair price and better working conditions **[1]**; it gives
businesses ethically sourced products to sell **[1]** and consumers a way to support producers in
low-income countries **[1]**.
(b) **[6]** For: attracts ethically minded customers; improves reputation; secure supply from loyal
producers; may allow a slightly higher price. Against: fair trade coffee may cost more, reducing profit
or raising prices; some customers care more about price; limited choice of coffee types. Conclusion: likely
to be worthwhile if the chain's customers value ethics and the extra cost is small per cup.

**8.** (a) Gym: misleading advertising **[1]**; battery maker: harmful disposal of products/waste **[1]**.
(b) Gym: customers who do not get results complain or leave, and the gym may face legal action, damaging
trust **[2]**. Battery maker: pollution harms people and wildlife, leading to fines, boycotts and a damaged
reputation **[2]**.

**9.** Any three explained: sellers have more knowledge than buyers, so consumers can be misled **[2]**;
faulty or unsafe goods can cause harm **[2]**; individuals have little power against large businesses
**[2]**; consumers should receive goods as described and good value for money **[2]**. **[max 6]**

**10.** (a) Keep the receipt **[1]**; return to the shop and explain the fault calmly, asking for a refund,
repair or replacement **[1]**; write to the manager or head office **[1]**; contact a consumer protection
organisation, or take legal action as a last resort **[1]**.
(b) Fair handling keeps the customer loyal and encourages repeat purchases **[2]**; it avoids bad reviews and
damage to reputation, and the cost of legal action **[2]**.

## A note on exam technique for this topic

For each action you suggest, say how it reduces environmental harm and how it affects the business in the
question. In 6-mark "discuss" items, give developed points for and against and a final judgement.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which ethical issues does 7100 name?** Misleading advertising and harmful disposal of products, alongside
the role of fair trade in commerce.

**Do I need to name consumer protection laws?** No. The syllabus asks for the reasons for consumer protection
and the actions buyers and sellers take to resolve complaints.

**How many sustainability actions should I know?** Nine are listed in 6.1.2, from sustainable materials and
logistics to free repair services and water stewardship.
