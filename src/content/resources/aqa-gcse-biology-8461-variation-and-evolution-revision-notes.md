## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Variation | Differences in the characteristics of individuals in a population |
| Mutation | A change in DNA; the source of all genetic variants |
| Evolution | A change in the inherited characteristics of a population over time through natural selection |
| Natural selection | Individuals with phenotypes best suited to the environment survive and breed more |
| Species | A group that can interbreed to produce fertile offspring |
| Selective breeding | Humans breeding plants and animals for particular genetic characteristics (artificial selection) |
| Inbreeding | Breeding closely related individuals; breeds become prone to disease or inherited defects |
| Genetic engineering | Modifying a genome by introducing a gene from another organism |
| GM crop | A crop whose genes have been modified by genetic engineering |
| Vector | A plasmid or virus used to carry a gene into cells (Higher tier only) |
| Clone | A genetically identical copy |

## Variation

- Causes: **genetic**, **environmental**, or **both**.
- Usually **extensive genetic variation** in a population.
- All variants arise from **mutations**: most have **no effect**; some **influence** phenotype; very few
  **determine** it.
- Mutations occur **continuously**; a rare new phenotype that suits an environmental change can cause a
  **relatively rapid change in the species**.

## Evolution by natural selection

1. Variation within the population (from mutations).
2. Some phenotypes better suited to the environment.
3. Those individuals more likely to survive.
4. They breed and pass on their alleles.
5. Over many generations the alleles become more common.

All species evolved from simple life forms that first developed **more than three billion years ago**.
**New species:** populations so different they **can no longer interbreed to produce fertile offspring**.

## Selective breeding

Choose parents with the desired characteristic → breed → choose offspring with it → breed → repeat over many
generations until all offspring show it.

| Examples | Risk |
|---|---|
| Disease-resistant crops; more meat or milk; gentle dogs; large or unusual flowers | Inbreeding → prone to disease or inherited defects; less variation |

## Genetic engineering

- Crops: disease resistance; bigger, better fruits; resistance to insects or herbicides; generally increased yields.
- Bacteria: human insulin.
- Medicine: research into overcoming some inherited disorders.
- Concerns: effects on wild flowers and insects; effects on human health not fully explored.

**Higher tier only:** enzymes isolate the gene → gene inserted into a vector (plasmid or virus) → vector inserts the
gene into the required cells → transfer at an early stage of development.

## Cloning (Biology only)

| Method | Key point |
|---|---|
| Tissue culture | Small groups of plant cells → identical plants; rare species, nurseries |
| Cuttings | Older, simple method used by gardeners |
| Embryo transplants | Split embryo cells before they specialise → identical embryos into host mothers |
| Adult cell cloning | Remove egg nucleus → insert adult body-cell nucleus → electric shock → embryo → ball of cells into a womb |

## Writing a natural selection answer

Use these five linked ideas, in order, with the context in the question:

- **variation** exists (from mutation);
- one variant is **better suited** to the environment (name the advantage from the question);
- it is **more likely to survive**;
- it **breeds and passes on its alleles**;
- over **many generations** the characteristic becomes **more common in the population**.

## Comparing the ways humans change organisms

| | Selective breeding | Genetic engineering | Cloning (Biology only) |
|---|---|---|---|
| What changes | Allele frequencies over generations | A gene added from another organism | Nothing; copies are identical |
| Speed | Many generations | One step, then the organism develops | One generation |
| Variation | Reduced over time | A new characteristic added | None between clones |

## Exam traps

- Organisms do not mutate "in order to" survive; mutations are random.
- Individuals do not evolve; populations do.
- Natural selection: environment selects. Selective breeding: humans select.
- The adult-cell clone matches the **body-cell donor**, not the egg donor or the surrogate.
- "GM crops are dangerous" is an opinion; give the specification's concerns.
- Inbreeding problems come from **reduced variation**.
- Vector = plasmid or virus, not "a bacterium" (Higher tier only).

## Self-test

1. Give the three causes of variation.
2. What proportion of mutations have no effect on the phenotype?
3. When can a mutation lead to a rapid change in a species?
4. Define evolution.
5. What defines two populations as separate species?
6. Give the steps of selective breeding.
7. Give one problem caused by inbreeding.
8. Name a substance made by genetically engineered bacteria.
9. Give one concern about GM crops.
10. (Higher tier only) Name the two types of vector.
11. Why are embryo cells split before they become specialised?
12. In adult cell cloning, what stimulates the egg cell to divide?

### Answers

1. Genes inherited; conditions of development (environment); a combination of both.
2. Most of them.
3. When it produces a new phenotype suited to an environmental change.
4. A change in the inherited characteristics of a population over time through natural selection.
5. They can no longer interbreed to produce fertile offspring.
6. Choose parents with the characteristic, breed them, choose offspring with it, breed them, repeat over many
   generations.
7. Breeds become prone to disease or inherited defects.
8. Human insulin.
9. Effect on populations of wild flowers and insects, or effects on human health not fully explored.
10. Bacterial plasmid; virus.
11. So each separated group of cells can still develop into a whole embryo.
12. An electric shock.
