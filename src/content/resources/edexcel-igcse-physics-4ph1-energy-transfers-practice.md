## Section A

**1.** Name the energy store in each of these.

**(a)** A compressed spring **[1]**
**(b)** A cup of hot water **[1]**
**(c)** A sandwich **[1]**

**2.** A student lists four energy stores: chemical, thermal, light and nuclear. Identify the one that is not an energy store and explain why. **[2]**

**3.** Solar water-heating panels are painted matt black. Give the reason. **[1]**

**4.** A television has a total energy output of 1200 J, of which 900 J is useful. Calculate its efficiency. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the heating element of an electric kettle is placed at the bottom of the kettle. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** A ball is dropped from a height, hits the ground and bounces up to a lower height.

**(a)** Describe the energy transfers from the moment the ball is released until it reaches the top of its bounce. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain, using the principle of conservation of energy, why the ball does not return to its starting height. **[2]**

**7.** An electric drill is supplied with 800 J. It transfers 480 J to the kinetic store of the drill bit and 240 J by heating. The rest is transferred as sound.

**(a)** Calculate the energy transferred as sound. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the efficiency of the drill. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how you would draw a Sankey diagram for the drill using a scale of 1 small square = 20 J. **[3]**

**8.** A gas-fired power station has an efficiency of 38%. Each second the total energy output is 1500 MJ.

**(a)** Calculate the useful electrical energy output each second. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy wasted each second and state where most of it goes. **[2]**

**9.** A student fills two identical metal cans with 200 cm³ of water at 80 °C. One can is painted matt black and the other is shiny silver. After 10 minutes, the black can's water is at 62 °C and the silver can's water is at 70 °C.

**(a)** State two variables the student kept the same. **[2]**
**(b)** State what the results show. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the result in terms of the surfaces. **[1]**

**10.** A teacher places a small crystal of coloured dye at the bottom of a beaker of cold water, against one side, and heats the water gently directly below the crystal.

**(a)** Describe what the class sees. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the movement of the water in terms of density. **[3]**

**11.** A vacuum flask keeps hot drinks hot. Explain how three different features of the flask reduce energy transfer from the drink. **[6]**

**12.** A student fixes a pin with wax to the end of each of three rods of the same length and thickness, made of copper, aluminium and steel. The other ends are heated equally. The pins fall after 40 s (copper), 65 s (aluminium) and 180 s (steel).

**(a)** State which metal is the best conductor. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why metals are good thermal conductors. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why the rods must be the same length and thickness. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Elastic store [1].
**(b)** Thermal store [1].
**(c)** Chemical store [1].

**2.** Light is not a store [1]. Light is a way energy is transferred (by radiation) between stores, not a place where energy is held [1].

**3.** Matt black surfaces are the best absorbers of infrared radiation, so the water is heated most [1].

**4.** efficiency = 900 ÷ 1200 × 100% [1] = 75% [1].

**5.** Water heated by the element expands and becomes less dense, so it rises [1]. Cooler, denser water sinks down to the element to be heated [1]. This sets up a convection current, so all the water is heated (an element at the top would heat only the top layer) [1].

**6. (a)** As the ball falls, energy is transferred from its gravitational store to its kinetic store, mechanically, by the work done by gravity [1]. When it hits the ground, the ball and ground deform, so energy moves from the kinetic store to the elastic store [1]. As the ball rebounds, energy returns from the elastic store to the kinetic store [1]. As it rises, energy moves from the kinetic store to the gravitational store [1].
**(b)** Energy is not destroyed, but some is transferred to the thermal store of the ball and surroundings (by heating during the bounce and through air resistance) and as sound [1]. Less energy is left in the gravitational store at the top of the bounce, so the height is lower [1].

**7. (a)** 800 − 480 − 240 = 80 J [1].
**(b)** efficiency = 480 ÷ 800 × 100% [1] = 60% [1].
**(c)** Input arrow 40 squares wide (800 J) [1]. Useful arrow 24 squares wide going straight on [1]. Two wasted arrows bending away: heating 12 squares and sound 4 squares, so the outputs add up to 40 squares [1].

**8. (a)** useful = 0.38 × 1500 [1] = 570 MJ [1].
**(b)** wasted = 1500 − 570 = 930 MJ [1]. Most goes by heating to the thermal store of the surroundings (air and cooling water) [1].

**9. (a)** Any two: volume of water; starting temperature; size, shape and material of the can; time of cooling; room conditions [1] [1].
**(b)** The water in the matt black can cooled more (18 °C compared with 10 °C) [1], so the matt black surface transferred energy away faster than the shiny silver one [1].
**(c)** A matt black surface is a better emitter of infrared radiation than a shiny silver surface [1].

**10. (a)** A coloured streak rises from the crystal above the heated area [1], spreads across the top of the water and sinks down the other side of the beaker [1].
**(b)** Water heated above the flame expands and becomes less dense [1], so it rises and carries the dye upwards [1]. At the top it cools, becomes denser and sinks, and cooler water flows in to replace it, forming a convection current [1].

**11.** For each of three features, one mark for the feature and one for how it reduces transfer:
Vacuum between the double walls [1]: there are no particles, so no conduction or convection can happen across it [1].
Silvered (shiny) surfaces on the walls [1]: they reflect infrared radiation back towards the drink, reducing transfer by radiation [1].
Stopper (plastic or cork) [1]: it stops warm air rising out by convection, and is a poor conductor [1].
(An insulating outer case or supports, reducing conduction, can replace any one feature.)

**12. (a)** Copper [1].
**(b)** Metals contain free electrons [1], which move through the metal and carry energy quickly from the hot end to the cold end, as well as energy passed by vibrating particles [1].
**(c)** Length and thickness also affect how quickly energy is conducted, so they must be controlled for the test to compare only the metals (a fair test) [1].

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I always need to mention the wasted energy?**
When a question asks you to describe all the energy transfers, yes: name where the wasted energy goes as well as the
useful transfer.
