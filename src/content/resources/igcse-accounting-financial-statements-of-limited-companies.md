## Types of company

A limited company is a separate legal entity owned by shareholders. Its activity decides how the statement of profit or loss starts.

- A **trading** company buys and sells goods, so its statement of profit or loss has a cost of sales section.
- A **service** company sells skills, such as surveying, so it has no cost of sales: fees are revenue and expenses follow.
- A **manufacturing** company makes what it sells; its factory cost of production replaces purchases in cost of sales (5.4).
- A **combination** does more than one of these, such as making furniture and charging fitting fees.

## Limited liability

Limited liability caps a shareholder's loss. Suppose a company fails owing $70,000 and its assets raise &#36;30,000, a shortfall of &#36;40,000. A shareholder with 5,000 fully paid &#36;1 shares loses the &#36;5,000 invested and nothing more; creditors cannot claim that person's home or savings. A partly paid shareholder can be asked only for the unpaid part. A sole trader would have to find the &#36;40,000 from private resources.

## Advantages and disadvantages

| Advantages of a limited company | Disadvantages of a limited company |
|---|---|
| Shareholders risk only what they paid or agreed to pay | More rules to follow, with set-up and running costs |
| Many investors can supply large sums | Profit is divided among shareholders as dividends |
| The company carries on if a shareholder sells up or dies | Accounts may have to be made public |
| Lenders may lend more readily | Owners may not run it |

In evaluation, tie each point to the situation: "raises large sums" matters for a planned factory.

## Equity and capital structure

**Equity** is the shareholders' stake: total assets minus total liabilities. It is made up of four parts.

| Part | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Preference share capital | Shares with a fixed dividend rate, paid before any ordinary dividend |
| Ordinary share capital | Shares sharing the remaining profit; the dividend can rise, fall or be nil |
| General reserve | Profit set aside from retained earnings and kept in the business |
| Retained earnings | Profit kept after dividends and transfers, built up over the years |

Check: assets $150,000 less liabilities &#36;40,000 gives equity of &#36;110,000, which could be ordinary shares &#36;70,000, general reserve &#36;15,000 and retained earnings &#36;25,000. A transfer to the general reserve moves money within equity, so the total is unchanged.

## Shares or debentures?

**Loan capital** is money lent to the company, usually as **debentures**. Tarnbridge Textiles Ltd needs $80,000 and expects profit from operations of &#36;50,000. Compare two ways.

- Issue 80,000 ordinary shares of $1: profit for the year stays &#36;50,000. A dividend of &#36;0.10 a share on them (&#36;8,000) appears in the statement of changes in equity; none is owed if the directors pay none.
- Issue $80,000 of 8% debentures: interest of &#36;6,400 is a finance cost, so profit for the year falls to &#36;43,600. The interest must be paid whatever the profit.

| Ordinary share capital | Loan capital (debentures) |
|---|---|
| Holders are owners and usually vote | Holders are lenders and do not vote |
| Dividend varies and is not compulsory | Fixed interest, compulsory |
| Part of equity | A liability: non-current while repayment is over a year away |
| Dividend leaves the statement of changes in equity | Interest is a finance cost in the statement of profit or loss |
| Not normally repaid while trading | Repaid on the agreed date |

## Issued, called-up and paid-up

Marchwood Foods Ltd issues 120,000 ordinary shares of $0.50 each.

- **Issued** share capital: 120,000 x $0.50 = &#36;60,000.
- **Called-up**: it asks for $0.40 a share: 120,000 x &#36;0.40 = &#36;48,000.
- **Paid-up**: holders of 3,000 shares have not paid the $0.40, so 3,000 x &#36;0.40 = &#36;1,200 is unpaid and paid-up capital is &#36;48,000 - &#36;1,200 = &#36;46,800.

Check which word the question uses; each gives a different figure.

## The three statements

1. **Statement of profit or loss**: as for a sole trader down to profit from operations, then deduct finance costs (debenture interest) to reach profit for the year. Dividends never appear here.
2. **Statement of changes in equity**: a column for each part of equity, running from opening balance through shares issued, profit for the year, transfers and dividends paid to the closing balance.
3. **Statement of financial position**: the equity section copies the closing line of statement 2; debentures go under non-current liabilities.

## Second example: a service company with a loss

Sedgley Surveying Ltd earned fees of $96,000 and had expenses of &#36;98,500, a loss from operations of &#36;2,500. Interest on &#36;25,000 of 8% debentures is &#36;2,000, so the loss for the year is &#36;4,500. An ordinary dividend of &#36;0.06 a share is paid.

```
                          Ordinary  General  Retained   Total
                             share  reserve  earnings
                           capital      USD       USD     USD
At start                   100,000    6,000    20,000 126,000
Loss for the year                              (4,500) (4,500)
Ordinary dividend paid                         (6,000) (6,000)
At end                     100,000    6,000     9,500 115,500
```

A dividend can still be paid in a loss year, from earlier profits in retained earnings.

## Adjustments from 5.1

Section 5.3 points back to the 5.1 list. Treatment is the same in a company.

| Adjustment | Statement of profit or loss | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Accrued expense / prepaid expense | Add to / deduct from the expense | Other payables / other receivables |
| Accrued income / prepaid income | Add to / deduct from the income | Other receivables / other payables |
| Irrecoverable debt | Expense | Lower trade receivables |
| Allowance for irrecoverable debts | Charge the increase, or add back a decrease | Deducted from trade receivables |
| Depreciation (straight line, reducing balance) | Expense | Raise accumulated depreciation |
| Revaluation method | Opening value + additions - closing valuation | Asset at closing valuation |
| Drawings | Method as in the sole trader guide | Method as in the sole trader guide |

## Common errors

- Putting dividends in the statement of profit or loss.
- Showing debentures within equity.
- Leaving unpaid debenture interest out of other payables.
- Using opening retained earnings in the statement of financial position.
