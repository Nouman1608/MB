## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Asexual reproduction | One parent. Offspring made by mitosis. Genetically identical to the parent (clones). No gametes. |
| Sexual reproduction | Two gametes fuse at fertilisation. Offspring genetically different from each parent and from each other. |
| Zygote | The cell formed when two gametes fuse. Diploid. |
| Diploid (2n) | Cell with two sets of chromosomes, in pairs. |
| Haploid (n) | Cell with one set of chromosomes, one of each pair. |
| Meiosis | Cell division in the reproductive organs that makes haploid gametes from a diploid cell. |

## B5.1f Asexual versus sexual reproduction

| | Asexual | Sexual |
|---|---|---|
| Parents | One | Two (two gametes) |
| Offspring | Identical to the parent | Varied |
| Speed | Usually quick, no mate to find | Usually slower, needs a mate |
| Numbers | Can be very many in a short time | Often fewer at a time |
| Strength | Good in a stable environment that suits the parent | Good in a changing environment |
| Weakness | One new disease or change can affect all | Costs time and energy, fewer offspring |

### Examples to learn

- Bacteria: binary fission. Some species can divide about every 20 minutes in good conditions.
- Strawberry: runners (asexual) and flowers (sexual).
- Potato: tubers (asexual) and flowers (sexual).
- Humans and mice: sexual only. Humans usually have one baby per birth, mice have a litter.
- Fungi: spores (asexual), and some also reproduce sexually.

### Method: "evaluate the advantages of ..." in steps

1. Name the type of reproduction and the organism.
2. State a feature: one parent or two, number of offspring, speed.
3. Link it to a consequence: numbers rise quickly, or the population varies.
4. Link variation to environmental pressure: disease, predators, competition, climate change.
5. Give the balancing point: what the other method does better.

### Linking points to environmental pressures

- Identical individuals have identical weaknesses.
- Variation means some individuals are likely to survive a new pressure.
- Variation is what natural selection acts on.
- A well-adapted organism in a stable habitat can keep its successful features by cloning.

## B5.1g Haploid and diploid

| Organism | 2n | n |
|---|---|---|
| Human | 46 | 23 |
| Pea | 14 | 7 |
| Fruit fly | 8 | 4 |
| Dog | 78 | 39 |

- Body cells: diploid. Gametes: haploid. Zygote: diploid.
- 2n is always even, because chromosomes are in pairs.
- A gamete has one chromosome from each pair, not half of every chromosome.
- One chromosome of each pair came from the mother and one from the father.

### Method: chromosome-number questions

1. Decide whether the cell is a body cell, a gamete or a zygote.
2. Body cell or zygote: diploid, 2n. Gamete: haploid, n.
3. To go from 2n to n, halve it. To go from n to 2n, double it.
4. Check the answer. Is a diploid number even? Is the gamete exactly half?

## B5.1h Meiosis

### Why it matters

- Gametes must be haploid so that fertilisation restores the diploid number.
- Without it the number would double every generation (human: 46, then 92, then 184).
- It is a source of genetic variation.

<figure class="topic-figure">
  <img src="https://img.marlbridge.com/igcse-biology-0610-drugs--07-bacteria-doubling.webp" width="900" height="890" alt="Extended worked example. A resistant bacterium dividing every 30 minutes makes 8 divisions in 4 hours, so 2 to the power 8 = 256 bacteria. The line graph curves steeply upwards." loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>A resistant strain can become common very quickly because numbers double with every division.</figcaption>
</figure>


### Steps

1. DNA is copied. Each chromosome now has two identical copies.
2. First division: pairs separate. Two haploid cells. This halves the number.
3. Second division: copies separate. Four cells.
4. Four haploid cells, all genetically different.

### Mitosis versus meiosis

| | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Body cells | Reproductive organs |
| Divisions | 1 | 2 |
| Cells made | 2 | 4 |
| Chromosomes | Stay at 2n | Halved to n |
| Cells | Identical | Different |
| Purpose | Growth, repair, asexual reproduction | Gametes |

### Where the variation comes from

- Each pair separates independently of the others.
- Which chromosome of a pair ends up in a gamete is random.
- Any sperm can fuse with any egg, adding more variation.

## Must-know distinctions

- Clone versus sibling. Asexual offspring are clones of one parent. Sexual offspring are siblings with different mixtures of genetic information.
- Gamete versus zygote. A gamete is haploid and a zygote is diploid.
- Haploid versus diploid. One set of chromosomes versus two sets. The diploid number is twice the haploid number.
- Mitosis versus meiosis. Mitosis keeps the number, makes identical cells and needs one division. Meiosis halves the number, makes different cells and needs two divisions.
- Fertilisation versus meiosis. Meiosis halves the number to make gametes. Fertilisation doubles it again by fusing two gametes.
- Variation among offspring versus variation caused by the environment. Meiosis and fertilisation produce genetic variation. Clones can still differ in size if one gets more light or water.

## Model explanations

**Why is the potato crop at risk when grown from tubers?** Tubers grow into plants with the same genetic information as the parent, so all the plants are genetically identical. They share the same weaknesses. If a pathogen can infect one plant it can infect them all, and there are no resistant plants to survive and reproduce.

**Why is sexual reproduction an advantage when the environment changes?** Sexual reproduction produces offspring that vary. If the environment changes, for example a new disease appears or the climate becomes warmer, some individuals are likely to have features that help them survive. They reproduce and pass those features on.

**Why must gametes be haploid?** Fertilisation joins two gametes. If each had 46 chromosomes, the zygote would have 92. Haploid gametes with 23 chromosomes each give a zygote with 46, so the diploid number is maintained in every generation.

**Why are the four cells from meiosis different?** Each pair of chromosomes separates independently, and which chromosome of each pair goes to which cell is random, so every cell ends up with its own combination.

## Maths reminders

| Skill | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Doubling | Number after d divisions = 2 to the power d | 6 divisions: 2⁶ = 64 |
| Combinations of chromosomes | 2 to the power (number of pairs) | 23 pairs: 2²³ = 8 388 608 |
| Probability | Multiply independent chances | One specific parent's chromosome in all of 3 pairs: (1/2)³ = 0.125 |
| Ratios | Write as simple whole numbers | 8 : 4 = 2 : 1 for diploid : haploid |

## Worked reminders

**Chromosome number.** A cat has 38 chromosomes in a skin cell. A skin cell is diploid, so 2n = 38, and a sperm cell has n = 38 ÷ 2 = 19. A kitten's cells have 19 + 19 = 38.

**Doubling.** A bacterium divides every 20 minutes. In 2 hours there are 120 ÷ 20 = 6 divisions, so 2⁶ = 64 cells, assuming none die.

**Probability.** A mouse gamete receives one chromosome from each of 20 pairs. For each pair the chance of the maternal copy is 1/2. The chance of getting the maternal copy of two named pairs is 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4 = 0.25.

## Quick self-test

1. State the number of chromosomes in a human sperm cell.
2. A horse has 64 chromosomes in a body cell. How many are in an egg cell?
3. Give two advantages of asexual reproduction.
4. Give two disadvantages of sexual reproduction.
5. How many cells does one cell make by meiosis, and how many by mitosis?
6. Why are gametes haploid?
7. A bacterium divides every 30 minutes. How many bacteria after 3 hours from one cell?
8. Explain why a crop grown from clones is at risk from a new disease.
9. Where does meiosis happen in a flowering plant?
10. State how meiosis causes variation.

### Answers

1. 23.
2. 32.
3. Any two: one parent only, no need for a mate, fast, many offspring quickly, offspring keep the successful features of the parent.
4. Any two: needs two parents or two gametes, takes time and energy to find a mate, usually slower, fewer offspring at a time.
5. Meiosis four, mitosis two.
6. So that fertilisation restores the diploid number and the number does not double each generation.
7. 3 hours = 180 minutes, 180 ÷ 30 = 6 divisions, 2⁶ = 64.
8. The plants are genetically identical, so any weakness is shared. A disease that can infect one can infect them all.
9. In the anthers and the ovules.
10. The pairs of chromosomes separate independently, so every gamete ends up with its own combination of chromosomes from the two parents.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "identical twins" or "clones" for sexual offspring.
- Describing asexual offspring as "the same" without saying genetic information.
- Saying meiosis makes "two" cells.
- Stating that gametes have 46 chromosomes, or 23 pairs.
- Explaining the advantage of sexual reproduction without linking variation to an environmental change or disease.
- Giving "it is quicker" as the only point on sexual reproduction, when asexual is usually the quicker one.
- Saying haploid cells are "half a cell".
- Not stating that fertilisation restores the diploid number.
- Showing no working in a doubling question, so a wrong power of 2 cannot be traced to a slip in counting the divisions.

## Frequently asked questions

**How does meiosis cause genetic variation?**
Each pair of chromosomes separates independently, so which chromosome of a pair ends up in a gamete is random and each gamete gets a different mixture. Fertilisation adds more variation, because any sperm can fuse with any egg.

**Why is variation important when the environment changes?**
Identical individuals share identical weaknesses, so a new disease or environmental pressure can affect them all. A population with variation is more likely to contain some individuals that survive, and variation is what natural selection acts on.

**Can clones look different from each other?**
Yes. Clones have the same genetic information, but their phenotype can still differ if the environment differs, for example if one plant gets more light or water.

**What is the difference between a gamete and a zygote?**
A gamete, such as a sperm or egg cell, is haploid. A zygote is the fertilised egg formed when two gametes fuse, and it is diploid.
