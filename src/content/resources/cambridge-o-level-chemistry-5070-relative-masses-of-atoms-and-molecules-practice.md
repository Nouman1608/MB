These questions cover section 3.2, Relative masses of atoms and molecules, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). Use these relative atomic masses unless a question gives others: H = 1, C = 12, N = 14, O = 16, Al = 27, S = 32, Cl = 35.5, Ca = 40, Fe = 56, Cu = 64, Zn = 65, Br = 80. Revise first with the [relative masses of atoms and molecules study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-relative-masses-of-atoms-and-molecules/) and the [relative masses of atoms and molecules revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-relative-masses-of-atoms-and-molecules-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Describe what is meant by the *relative atomic mass* of an element. **[2]**

**2.** State why relative atomic mass has no units. **[1]**

**3.** Calculate the relative molecular mass of each molecule.

**(a)** Butane, C₄H₁₀ **[1]**

**(b)** Ethene, C₂H₄ **[1]**

**4.** Calculate the relative formula mass of zinc nitrate, Zn(NO₃)₂. **[1]**

**5.** Calculate the relative formula mass of iron(III) sulfate, Fe₂(SO₄)₃. **[1]**

**6.** State whether the term *relative molecular mass* or *relative formula mass* should be used for each substance, and give a reason.

**(a)** Sulfur dioxide, SO₂ **[1]**

**(b)** Magnesium oxide, MgO **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Blue copper(II) sulfate crystals have the formula CuSO₄·5H₂O.

**(a)** Calculate the relative formula mass of the crystals. **[2]**

**(b)** A student calculates the answer as 640 by multiplying the whole of CuSO₄ by 5. Explain the error. **[1]**

**8.** A student calculates the relative formula mass of ammonium carbonate, (NH₄)₂CO₃, and gets 78. Identify the error and calculate the correct value. **[3]**

**9.** A metal hydroxide has the formula X(OH)₃ and relative formula mass 78. Calculate the relative atomic mass of X and identify X. **[2]**

**10.** Hydrated calcium chloride has the formula CaCl₂·*n*H₂O and relative formula mass 219. Calculate the value of *n*. **[3]**

**11.** Use relative atomic masses to answer the following.

**(a)** How many times heavier, on average, is a bromine atom than an oxygen atom? **[1]**

**(b)** The relative atomic mass of copper on many Periodic Tables is given as 63.5. Explain why it is not a whole number. **[2]**

**12.** An element exists as two isotopes: 75.0% has a mass of 35 and 25.0% has a mass of 37.

**(a)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of the element. **[2]**

**(b)** Use your answer to calculate the relative molecular mass of tetrachloromethane, CCl₄. **[2]**

**13.** Two oxides have the formulae NOₓ and SOᵧ.

**(a)** The oxide of nitrogen has Mr = 46. Deduce the value of *x*. **[1]**

**(b)** The oxide of sulfur has Mr = 80. Deduce the value of *y*. **[1]**

**14.** Explain why the relative atomic mass of an element is defined using carbon-12 rather than by stating the actual mass of its atoms in grams. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The **average mass of the isotopes** of an element [1] compared to **1/12th of the mass of an atom of carbon-12** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** It is a **ratio** of two masses (compared with carbon-12), so the units cancel [1]. **[1]**

**3. (a)** (4 × 12) + (10 × 1) = **58** [1]

**(b)** (2 × 12) + (4 × 1) = **28** [1]

**[2]**

**4.** 65 + (2 × 14) + (6 × 16) = 65 + 28 + 96 = **189** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** (2 × 56) + (3 × 32) + (12 × 16) = 112 + 96 + 192 = **400** [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** **Relative molecular mass**: sulfur dioxide is simple molecular, made of SO₂ molecules [1]

**(b)** **Relative formula mass**: magnesium oxide is ionic, with no molecules [1]

**[2]**

**7. (a)** CuSO₄ = 64 + 32 + 64 = 160; 5H₂O = 5 × 18 = 90 [1]; Mr = **250** [1]

**(b)** The 5 applies **only to the water molecules**, not to CuSO₄ [1]

**[3]**

*Examiner insight:* in a hydrated formula, the dot separates the salt from its water of crystallisation; add the two parts.

**8.** The student has **ignored the subscript 2** after the bracket, counting only one NH₄ group (14 + 4 + 12 + 48 = 78) [1]. Correct atom count: N 2, H 8, C 1, O 3 [1]. Mr = 28 + 8 + 12 + 48 = **96** [1]. **[3]**

**9.** 3 × (16 + 1) = 51; Ar of X = 78 − 51 = **27** [1]; X is **aluminium** [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Mr of CaCl₂ = 40 + 71 = 111 [1]; 18*n* = 219 − 111 = 108 [1]; *n* = **6** [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** 80 ÷ 16 = **5** times [1]

**(b)** Copper is a mixture of **isotopes** with different masses [1]; Ar is the **average** mass, weighted by abundance, so it is not a whole number [1]

**[3]**

**12. (a)** (35 × 75.0 + 37 × 25.0) ÷ 100 [1] = 3550 ÷ 100 = **35.5** [1]

**(b)** 12 + (4 × 35.5) [1] = 12 + 142 = **154** [1]

**[4]**

**13. (a)** 46 − 14 = 32 = 2 × 16, so *x* = **2** (NO₂) [1]

**(b)** 80 − 32 = 48 = 3 × 16, so *y* = **3** (SO₃) [1]

**[2]**

**14.** Atoms have **extremely small masses** in grams, which are inconvenient to use [1]; comparing with a **standard** (carbon-12, given exactly 12) gives simple numbers that can be used to compare atoms directly [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out "average" or "carbon-12" from the description of Ar.
- Adding units such as g to Ar or Mr values.
- Not multiplying every atom inside a bracket.
- Multiplying the whole salt, rather than only the water, in a hydrated formula.
- Using one chlorine (35.5) instead of two (71) in a formula such as CaCl₂.
- Calling the Mr of an ionic compound a relative molecular mass.
