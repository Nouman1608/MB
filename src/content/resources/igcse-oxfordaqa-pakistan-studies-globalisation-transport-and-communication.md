**Globalisation, Transport and Communication in Pakistan is topic 3.2.3 of OxfordAQA International
GCSE Pakistan Studies 9236, one of two optional topics examined in Paper 2, Section C (21 marks).**
It asks how global connections affect Pakistan's economy, people and environment, how
transnational corporations operate there, how transport networks are distributed and developing,
including the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor, and how communication and financial systems support
development.

## What is globalisation and how does it affect Pakistan?

**Globalisation** is the growing interconnection of countries through trade, investment, migration,
technology and culture. For Pakistan:

| | Positive impacts | Negative impacts |
|---|---|---|
| Economy | Export markets for textiles; foreign investment; IT outsourcing jobs | Competition harms local firms; exposure to global price shocks and crises |
| People | Remittances from workers abroad; access to information, education and global products | Widening gap between connected cities and rural areas; cultural change seen by some as a threat to local traditions |
| Environment | Access to cleaner technologies and renewable energy equipment | Pollution from factories producing for export; more imported packaging and waste |

## What are TNCs and what is their role in Pakistan?

A **transnational corporation** has its headquarters in one country and operations in several. TNCs
are usually large, invest heavily, bring brands and technology, and organise production and sales
across borders. Examples active in Pakistan include consumer-goods firms such as Unilever and
Nestlé, car makers producing in joint ventures with Pakistani partners (such as Pak Suzuki and
Indus Motor, which assembles Toyota vehicles), soft-drink and fast-food companies, and technology
and telecommunications firms.

**Positive impacts:** jobs and training; tax revenue; new technology and management skills; exports;
better products for consumers; investment in local suppliers.
**Negative impacts:** profits sent back to the home country; competition that pushes small local
firms out of business; pressure on labour standards in supply chains; water use, waste and
pollution; dependence on decisions made abroad.

## How are consumer cultures changing?

Rising incomes in cities, smartphones and cheap mobile data have created **digital consumers** who
shop online, pay with mobile wallets and follow global trends on social media. E-commerce
platforms, such as Daraz, deliver nationwide. **Global brands** in clothing, food and electronics are
popular with young urban consumers, while local brands, such as Pakistani fashion labels, compete by
blending tradition and modern design. Large **shopping malls** in Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad
combine retail, food courts and entertainment.

## Why are transport networks unevenly distributed?

- **Road and rail** are densest on the flat, populous Indus Plain, linking Karachi, Hyderabad, Multan, Lahore, Islamabad and Peshawar. They are sparse in Balochistan and the northern mountains, where steep relief, landslides, snow, earthquakes, deserts and low population raise costs and reduce demand.
- **Air** links the major cities and serves remote northern towns such as Gilgit and Skardu, where roads are slow.
- **River** transport is very limited, because flow varies greatly between seasons and barrages block navigation.
- **Sea** trade passes through Karachi Port, Port Qasim and Gwadar.

## What are the opportunities and challenges for transport development?

**Road:** motorways such as the M-2 Lahore–Islamabad and newer routes built under CPEC have cut
journey times and costs; the Karakoram Highway links Pakistan to China. *Challenges:* cost, foreign
debt, land acquisition, maintenance, overloaded trucks and road safety.

**Rail:** the main Karachi–Peshawar line (ML-1) is planned for upgrading under CPEC to allow faster
passenger and freight trains, and Lahore opened the Orange Line metro in 2020. *Challenges:* old
track and rolling stock, financial losses, competition from road haulage.

**Ports and airports:** **dry ports** in inland cities such as Lahore, Faisalabad and Sialkot let
exporters clear customs near their factories; **Gwadar**, a deep-water port, is being developed with
Chinese investment; new airports, such as Islamabad International (2018), have expanded capacity.
*Challenges:* security concerns and limited local water and power at Gwadar; congestion at Karachi;
high costs and debt.

**CPEC**, launched in 2015, is a programme of Chinese investment in roads, railways, power stations,
Gwadar port and special economic zones. Supporters say it fills infrastructure and energy gaps and
creates jobs; critics point to debt, the share of contracts and jobs going to Chinese firms, and the
need for benefits to reach local communities, especially in Balochistan.

## How are communication networks expanding?

Radio and television reach most of the population; private TV channels expanded rapidly from the
2000s. Mobile phones are now far more common than landlines, and the auction of 3G and 4G licences
in 2014 brought mobile internet to millions. **Opportunities:** e-commerce, online education,
telemedicine, freelancing and IT exports, and access to market prices for farmers. **Challenges:**
mountain and desert terrain raises the cost of masts and fibre; power cuts; low digital literacy
and a gender gap in phone ownership; cyber-security and internet shutdowns.

## Why do financial systems matter for development?

Banks, microfinance institutions, Islamic banking, the stock exchange and mobile money channel
savings into investment. The **State Bank of Pakistan** regulates banks and runs the instant payment
system **Raast** (launched 2021). Mobile wallets such as JazzCash and Easypaisa let people without
bank accounts send money, pay bills and receive government cash transfers, which supports
**financial inclusion**, especially for women and rural households.

## Frequently asked questions

**What makes a company a TNC?** It operates in more than one country, usually with headquarters in a
richer country and factories, offices or sales networks in others.

**What is a dry port?** An inland terminal linked to a seaport by road or rail, where goods are cleared
through customs and containerised.

**Why is Gwadar important?** It is a deep-water port close to the Strait of Hormuz and the southern
end of CPEC, offering a route for trade with China, Central Asia and the Gulf.
