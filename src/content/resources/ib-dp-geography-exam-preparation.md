The [assessment revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-geography-assessment-revision-notes/) set out each
paper's weighting and timing. These notes turn that into an exam-day plan -- how to pace each paper
given its specific time allowance, and how to handle Paper 2's resource-based questions -- with a
worked scenario and a checklist.

## First: confirm your school's elective themes

Paper 1's elective themes are chosen by your school from the syllabus options, so revision material
that assumes a particular set of themes may not match what you were actually taught. Before doing
any Paper 1 exam preparation, confirm with your geography department exactly which themes (two at
SL, three at HL) you are being examined on -- this single check prevents wasted revision time on
content you will never be tested on.

## Paper 1: elective themes (1h30 SL / 2h10 HL, 35% at both levels)

Because Paper 1 keeps the same 35% weighting at both levels but HL students cover one extra theme
in a proportionally similar time allowance, the time-per-theme pressure is broadly similar at SL and
HL -- pace practice papers accordingly, allocating roughly equal time to each elective theme's
questions rather than assuming HL's longer paper means more time per theme.

## Paper 2: the compulsory core (1h15 at both levels, 40% SL / 25% HL)

Paper 2 is the one paper every DP Geography candidate sits on identical content, which makes it the
paper where structured, close revision pays off most reliably -- there is no ambiguity about what
might be examined. Because SL's 1h15 covers a larger share of the SL grade (40%) than the same
1h15 does at HL (25%), SL students in particular should prioritise Paper 2 preparation highly
relative to Paper 1.

**Resource-based questions specifically test reading unfamiliar material** -- a map, graph, photograph
or data table you have not seen before -- so practising with genuinely unfamiliar resources, not just
ones already discussed in class, builds the specific skill these questions reward.

## HL Paper 3: the synoptic paper (1h + 5 min reading, 20%)

Paper 3 is HL-only and demands more extended, **synoptic** answers -- drawing connections across
different parts of the syllabus on global interactions, rather than answering from one isolated
topic. The 5 minutes of reading time before writing begins is meant to be used for genuine planning:
identify which syllabus areas the question draws on and sketch a brief structure before writing,
rather than starting to write immediately.

## Worked practice scenario: a resource-based question

A Paper 2-style question gives a choropleth map showing a country's regional unemployment rates,
ranging from under 5% in the capital region to over 18% in a peripheral rural region, and asks:
"Using the map, describe the pattern of unemployment shown, and suggest one reason for it."

```
Weak answer:    "Unemployment is higher in the countryside." -- vague, no use of
                specific values or regions named on the map.

Strong answer:  Describe: unemployment is lowest in the capital region (under
                5%) and rises with distance from it, reaching over 18% in the
                most peripheral rural region -- reading and stating approximate
                values directly from the map's key. Suggest: this pattern is
                consistent with a core-periphery structure, where economic
                activity, infrastructure and investment concentrate in and
                around the capital, leaving peripheral regions with fewer
                formal employment opportunities.
```

The strong answer does two things a resource-based question specifically rewards: it reads and
states approximate values directly from the resource (satisfying "describe"), and it links the
pattern to a named geographic concept rather than a generic guess (satisfying "suggest") -- this
describe-then-explain-the-pattern structure applies to any resource-based question, whatever the
specific map, graph or data table shows.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exam**: confirm your school's specific elective themes for Paper 1; prioritise Paper
  2 preparation given its uniform content and, at SL, its larger weighting; practise Paper 3's
  synoptic style specifically if sitting HL, using the reading time to plan before writing.
- **During Paper 1**: pace questions evenly across your elective themes rather than favouring one.
- **During Paper 2**: read unfamiliar resources carefully and state approximate values directly from
  them before explaining the pattern shown.
- **During HL Paper 3**: use the 5 minutes' reading time to identify which syllabus areas the
  question draws on and sketch a structure before writing.

## Why timing awareness matters more here than in most subjects

Because Paper 1's duration itself differs between SL (1h30) and HL (2h10), and Paper 2 and Paper 3
carry different weightings at each level, a generic "spend X minutes per mark" rule copied from
another subject will not transfer cleanly to Geography. Work out your own per-paper timing plan from
the actual weightings and durations above -- roughly, time spent per question should track its mark
allocation within that paper's own time limit, rather than assuming every DP subject's papers are
timed the same way.
