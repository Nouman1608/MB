This guide covers subtopic **11.4 Blood** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series. The syllabus asks you to identify red and white blood cells (lymphocytes and phagocytes) as seen under the light microscope on prepared slides and in diagrams and photomicrographs, to list the components of blood, to state the functions of red blood cells, white blood cells, platelets and plasma, and to describe the transfer of substances between blood in capillaries, tissue fluid and body cells.

## Components of blood

Blood is a tissue with four components: **red blood cells**, **white blood cells**, **platelets** and **plasma**. Plasma is the liquid part, and the others are cells or cell fragments suspended in it.

## Red blood cells

**Function: transport of oxygen.**

| Feature | How it helps |
|---|---|
| Contain **haemoglobin**, a red pigment | Haemoglobin combines with oxygen in the lungs and releases it in the tissues |
| **Biconcave disc** shape (flattened with a dip on both sides) | Large surface area to volume ratio, so oxygen diffuses in and out quickly |
| **No nucleus** | More room for haemoglobin |
| Small and flexible | Can squeeze through narrow capillaries |

**Recognition**: on a stained slide, red blood cells are the most numerous cells, small, round and pink-red, and they look pale in the centre because of the biconcave shape. They have no nucleus.

## White blood cells

White blood cells defend the body against pathogens. The syllabus names two types.

### Phagocytes

- **Function**: engulf pathogens and digest them. This is called **phagocytosis**.
- **Recognition**: large cells with a lobed or irregularly shaped nucleus, and granular cytoplasm.

### Lymphocytes

- **Function**: produce **antibodies**, which are proteins that help to destroy pathogens (see subtopic 12.3).
- **Recognition**: slightly larger than a red cell, with a large, round nucleus that fills most of the cell and very little cytoplasm.

White blood cells are fewer than red blood cells, have a nucleus, and appear colourless or pale unless stained.

## Platelets

Platelets are **small fragments of cells** with no nucleus.

**Function: clotting.** When a blood vessel is cut, platelets help to start the process in which the soluble protein **fibrinogen** is converted into insoluble **fibrin**. The fibrin forms a mesh of fibres across the wound that traps red blood cells and forms a clot. The clot dries to a scab.

Clotting **prevents blood loss** and **stops the entry of pathogens** through the wound.

## Plasma

Plasma is a pale yellow liquid, mostly water. It **transports** many substances in solution or suspension. The syllabus lists:

| Carried in plasma | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| Blood cells | Bone marrow and spleen | Around the body |
| Ions | Gut, tissues | Wherever needed |
| Glucose | Gut and liver | Cells for respiration |
| Amino acids | Gut | Cells for making proteins |
| Hormones | Glands | Target organs |
| Carbon dioxide (mostly dissolved) | Respiring cells | Lungs |
| Urea | Liver | Kidneys |
| Vitamins | Gut | Cells |
| Plasma proteins | Liver | Throughout the blood |

## Summary table

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| Red blood cells | Oxygen transport |
| White blood cells | Antibody production by lymphocytes; engulfing pathogens by phagocytes |
| Platelets | Clotting by converting soluble fibrinogen into insoluble fibrin |
| Plasma | Transport |

## Worked example: magnification of a red blood cell

*On a photomicrograph, a red blood cell has a diameter of 28 mm. The actual diameter is 7 micrometres. Calculate the magnification.*

1. Convert to the same unit. 7 micrometres = 7 divided by 1000 = 0.007 mm.
2. Magnification = image size divided by actual size = 28 divided by 0.007 = **x4000**.

If you were given a magnification and the image size, you would use the formula in the other direction: actual size = image size divided by magnification.

### Worked example: cell counts

*A sample of blood contains 5 000 000 red blood cells and 7500 white blood cells in each cubic millimetre. How many red cells are there for every white cell?*

5 000 000 divided by 7500 = 666.7, so about **670 red cells** for each white cell. This is why red blood cells are the most numerous cells in a photomicrograph.

## Tissue fluid

**Tissue fluid** is the fluid that surrounds the body cells. It is formed from blood plasma.

**How it forms**: blood in capillaries is under pressure. The capillary wall is one cell thick and has tiny gaps. Plasma, with its dissolved substances, is forced out through the gaps. Red blood cells and large plasma proteins stay in the blood because they are too large to pass through.

**Transfer of substances**

| Direction | Substances | Process |
|---|---|---|
| Blood → tissue fluid → cell | Oxygen, glucose, amino acids, ions, hormones | Diffusion (and active transport into cells for some) down concentration gradients |
| Cell → tissue fluid → blood | Carbon dioxide, urea, other waste | Diffusion down concentration gradients |

Oxygen and glucose are used by the cells, so their concentration in the tissue fluid is lower than in the blood, and they diffuse in. Carbon dioxide and urea are made by the cells, so their concentration in the tissue fluid is higher than in the blood, and they diffuse out into the blood.

**Return of fluid**: most of the tissue fluid returns to the blood in the capillaries, and some drains into the lymph vessels, which carry it back to the blood. This is why the body does not swell with fluid.

Tissue fluid links the capillary and the cell, so the capillary wall's thinness and large surface area (subtopic 11.3) are what makes the exchange possible.

## Common mistakes

- Saying that white blood cells carry oxygen, or that red blood cells fight infection.
- Saying that red blood cells have a nucleus.
- Confusing lymphocytes (antibodies) with phagocytes (engulfing).
- Saying that platelets are cells with a nucleus. They are fragments.
- Saying that fibrin is converted into fibrinogen. It is fibrinogen to fibrin.
- Describing plasma as "mostly red blood cells".
- Saying that red blood cells leave the capillaries into tissue fluid.
- Forgetting that blood clotting stops pathogens entering as well as stopping blood loss.

## Command words

- **Identify**: name the cell on a diagram or photomicrograph, giving a visible reason.
- **List**: give the four components.
- **State**: give the function of each component.
- **Describe**: give the transfer of substances between capillary, tissue fluid and cell.
