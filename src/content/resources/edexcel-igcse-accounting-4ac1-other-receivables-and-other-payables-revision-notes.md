## What you must be able to do

- (a) Explain why it is necessary to account for other receivables and payables.
- (b) Distinguish between them.
- (c) Calculate and record them in the books of account.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| Accrued expense | Expense incurred but not yet paid | Other payable |
| Prepaid expense | Expense paid for a later period | Other receivable |
| Accrued income | Income earned but not yet received | Other receivable |
| Income received in advance | Income received for a later period | Other payable |
| Accruals concept | Match income and expenses to the period they relate to | |
| Time apportionment | Splitting an amount by months either side of the year end | |

Older names (glossary): prepayments for other receivables; accruals for other payables.

## Why they are necessary

1. Correct profit: income earned and expenses incurred, not cash.
2. Complete statement of financial position: every asset and liability shown.
3. Fair comparison between years.

## The four cases at a glance

| Case | Effect on expense or income | Effect on profit | Balance c/d written on | Balance b/d |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Expense owed | Expense up | Down | Debit side | Credit (liability) |
| Expense prepaid | Expense down | Up | Credit side | Debit (asset) |
| Income owed to us | Income up | Up | Credit side | Debit (asset) |
| Income received in advance | Income down | Down | Debit side | Credit (liability) |

Memory hook: if the business **owes** (accrued expense or income in advance), profit falls and there is a liability; if the business **is owed** (prepaid expense or accrued income), profit rises and there is an asset.

## Formulas

Expense for the year = paid − opening accrual + closing accrual + opening prepayment − closing prepayment.

Income for the year = received − opening accrued income + closing accrued income + opening in advance − closing in advance.

Safer route: income statement figure = months in the year × monthly amount; the closing balance is then the balancing figure in the account.

## Mini-examples

- Electricity paid 2,960; 310 owed at year end; no opening balance. Expense 3,270; other payable 310.
- Insurance 1,800 paid 1 April for 12 months; year end 31 December. Expense 9 × 150 = 1,350; other receivable 450.
- Rent receivable 500 a month; received 6,500 in the year; no opening balance. Income 6,000; received in advance 500, an other payable.
- Interest receivable 360 for the year, only 270 received. Income 360; other receivable 90.

## Ledger reminders

- Opening balances are brought down on the first day: accrual as a credit, prepayment as a debit (for expenses).
- The transfer to the income statement is the period's correct figure.
- The balancing figure is the closing other receivable or payable.

## Exam traps

- Charging the cash paid as the expense.
- Bringing down an accrued expense as a debit.
- Counting rent received in advance as this year's income.
- Ignoring the opening balance.
- Calling a prepaid expense a liability.
- Counting the months wrongly: 1 October to 31 December is three months, not two.
- Mixing up trade receivables with other receivables.

## Quick self-test

1. Explain why other payables must be accounted for.
2. Classify: wages owed at the year end.
3. Classify: rent received from a tenant for next month.
4. Classify: commission earned but not received.
5. Rates of 3,000 for 12 months were paid on 1 July. Year end 31 December. Calculate the expense and the other receivable.
6. Wages paid 41,200; opening accrual 600; closing accrual 750. Calculate the expense.
7. Insurance paid 2,500; opening prepayment 400; closing prepayment 550. Calculate the expense.
8. Rent receivable: received 5,000; opening in advance 250; closing owed to us 500. Calculate the income.
9. Draft profit 18,000. Omitted: expense owed 450; expense prepaid 200. Adjusted profit?
10. On which side is an opening accrued expense brought down?
11. Where is income received in advance shown in the statement of financial position?
12. Which concept underlies section 2.7?

### Answers

1. Expenses incurred but unpaid belong to this period (accruals), so leaving them out overstates profit and understates liabilities.
2. Other payable (accrued expense).
3. Other payable (income received in advance).
4. Other receivable (accrued income).
5. 3,000 ÷ 12 = 250 a month; July to December 6 months: expense 1,500; other receivable 1,500.
6. 41,200 − 600 + 750 = 41,350.
7. 2,500 + 400 − 550 = 2,350.
8. 5,000 + 250 + 500 = 5,750.
9. 18,000 − 450 + 200 = 17,750.
10. The credit side, because it is a liability.
11. Current liabilities, under other payables.
12. The accruals concept.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I remember which balances are assets?**
If the business has paid ahead or is still owed money, it has an asset (other receivable). If it still owes money or has been paid ahead, it has a liability (other payable).

**What is the quickest reliable way to find the year's expense?**
Multiply the monthly amount by the months in the year, then let the ledger account give the closing balance as the balancing figure.
