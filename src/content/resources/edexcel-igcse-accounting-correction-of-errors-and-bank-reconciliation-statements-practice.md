## Questions

**1.** Rushgrove Traders found four errors. For each one, state whether the trial balance still agreed, and give a reason.

**(a)** A purchase of goods on credit for 1,750 was debited to purchases but not credited to the supplier. **[2]**
**(b)** A credit sale of 610 was entered as 160 in both the sales account and the customer's account. **[2]**
**(c)** An insurance cheque for 480 was debited to the rent account. **[2]**
**(d)** The credit side of the sales account was added up 100 too low. **[2]**

**2.** Write journal entries, with narratives, to correct each error.

**(a)** A credit purchase of 530 from T. Hale was never recorded. **[2]**
**(b)** Office furniture bought for 1,400 was debited to purchases. **[2]**
**(c)** A cheque of 360 from a customer, D. Marsh, was debited to Marsh's account and credited to bank. **[2]**
**(d)** A rent payment of 650 was recorded as 560 in both the rent and bank accounts. **[2]**

**3.** Explain two reasons why an agreeing trial balance does not prove that the books are free from error. **[4]**

**4.** A business's trial balance has debit totals of 39,110 and credit totals of 38,720. State which side of a suspense account receives the difference and the amount, and give one error that could cause this. **[3]**

**5.** The trial balance of Mirebrook Stores at 31 December 2026 has a debit total of 61,845 and a credit total of 62,510. The difference was placed in a suspense account. These errors are then found.

- Wages of 275 were paid by cheque. The bank entry was made but the wages account was not debited.
- A sales return of 150 was credited to the sales returns account instead of being debited.
- The purchases account was added up 90 too low.

**(a)** State the entry made in the suspense account for the difference. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the journal entry to correct each error. **[6]**
**(c)** Prepare the suspense account. **[3]**

**6.** Explain why a business might open a suspense account, and why it should not stay open. **[4]**

**7.** **(a)** Explain the purpose of a bank reconciliation statement. **[3]**
**(b)** State two reasons why the bank statement and cash book balances differ. **[2]**

**8.** For each item, state whether it needs a cash book entry or appears on the bank reconciliation statement. For a cash book entry, say whether it increases or decreases the cash book balance.

- (i) A cheque to a supplier not yet cleared by the bank **[1]**
- (ii) Bank charges **[1]**
- (iii) A lodgement made on the last day of the month, not yet on the statement **[1]**
- (iv) A standing order **[1]**
- (v) A customer's cheque returned unpaid **[1]**
- (vi) Bank interest received **[1]**

**9.** The cash book (bank column) of Dovecross Carpets at 30 November 2026 shows a debit balance of 5,120. The bank statement shows bank charges of 62, a credit transfer from a customer of 840, a direct debit of 175 and bank interest received of 9. A cheque issued for 1,920 was entered in the cash book as 1,290. Update the cash book and state the new balance. **[6]**

**10.** The updated cash book of Dovecross Carpets at 30 November 2026 shows a balance of 5,102. Cheques of 410, 275 and 135 have not been presented, and a lodgement of 1,650 is not yet on the bank statement. Prepare the bank reconciliation statement. **[6]**

**11.** The cash book (bank column) of Farnleigh Joinery at 31 December 2026 shows an overdraft of 2,340. The bank statement shows these items that are not in the cash book: bank charges 85, interest charged on the overdraft 48, a direct debit 210, a credit transfer from a customer 900 and a customer's cheque for 320 dishonoured. Cheques of 760 have not been presented, and a lodgement of 1,150 is not on the statement.

**(a)** Show how the cash book is updated and state the new balance. **[7]**
**(b)** Prepare the bank reconciliation statement. **[5]**

**12.** A bank statement shows a balance of 3,020. The cash book, with no items missing from it, shows 2,680. Cheques of 640 have not been presented and a lodgement of 300 is not yet on the statement. Sana says 340 has been lost and should be written off. Show whether she is right. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** No: the debit has no matching credit [1]. This is a one-sided entry [1].
**(b)** Yes: both entries use the same wrong figure [1]. This is an error of original entry [1].
**(c)** Yes: the debit and credit are equal, only the wrong expense account was used [1]. This is an error of commission [1].
**(d)** No: a casting error makes the credit total 100 lower than the debit total [1]. It disturbs one side only [1].
*Examiner insight:* a bare "yes" or "no" is rarely enough on a question like this; the reason about equal or unequal debits and credits is what shows understanding.

**2. (a)** Dr Purchases 530 [1], Cr T. Hale 530, narrative: purchase omitted [1].
**(b)** Dr Office furniture 1,400 [1], Cr Purchases 1,400, narrative: capital item [1].
**(c)** Dr Bank 720 [1], Cr D. Marsh 720, narrative: receipt posted to the wrong sides [1].
**(d)** Dr Rent 90 [1], Cr Bank 90, narrative: payment under-recorded [1].
*Examiner insight:* the amount in a complete reversal is twice the original, and the amount in an original entry error is only the difference; check both before moving on.

**3.** A compensating error leaves the totals equal [1] because one overstatement offsets an equal understatement on the opposite side [1]. An error of omission leaves the totals equal [1] because the debit and credit were both missed [1]. Other types of error are acceptable if explained.
*Examiner insight:* naming an error type without explaining why the totals still agree usually leaves half the marks unclaimed.

**4.** Debit total is higher by 390 (39,110 − 38,720) [1], so the difference is credited [1] to suspense. One cause: a purchase debited to purchases with no credit to the supplier [1].
*Examiner insight:* work out which total is higher before choosing a side; the total that is lower needs the extra entry.

**5. (a)** Credit total − debit total = 62,510 − 61,845 = 665 [1]. Debit suspense with 665 [1].
**(b)** Wages: Dr Wages 275, Cr Suspense 275 [1][1]. Sales returns: Dr Sales returns 300, Cr Suspense 300 [1][1]. Purchases: Dr Purchases 90, Cr Suspense 90 [1][1].
**(c)**

| Suspense account | | | |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Difference per trial balance | 665 | Wages | 275 |
| | | Sales returns | 300 |
| | | Purchases | 90 |
| | **665** | | **665** |

Debit side shows 665 [1]. Credit side shows 275, 300 and 90 [1]. Both sides total 665, so the account clears [1].
*Examiner insight:* the return credited instead of debited is wrong by twice the amount, so the journal is 300, not 150.

**6.** It is opened when a trial balance does not agree [1] so the totals can be made equal while the errors are traced [1]. It should be cleared because the balance is not a real asset or liability [1]; it is only a placeholder for unfound mistakes, and a balance left in it shows errors remain [1].
*Examiner insight:* "to make the trial balance balance" is the starting point; the second mark generally needs the idea that it is temporary.

**7. (a)** To check the cash book against the bank's record [1], find errors or omissions by either side [1] and show why balances differ, helping to detect fraud [1].
**(b)** Any two: unpresented cheques; uncredited lodgements; bank items not yet in the cash book (charges, direct debits, credit transfers); errors [1][1].
*Examiner insight:* give two different reasons, not one reason in two wordings; examples such as "bank charges" help to make a point specific.

**8.** (i) Reconciliation statement [1]. (ii) Cash book, decreases [1]. (iii) Reconciliation statement [1]. (iv) Cash book, decreases [1]. (v) Cash book, decreases [1]. (vi) Cash book, increases [1].
*Examiner insight:* the dishonoured cheque is the one often placed on the reconciliation by mistake; it is a bank entry the business has not yet recorded.

**9.** Opening balance 5,120 [1]. Less bank charges 62 [1]. Add credit transfer 840 and bank interest 9 [1]. Less direct debit 175 [1]. Less error 630 (1,920 − 1,290), because the payment was understated [1]. 5,120 − 62 + 840 + 9 − 175 − 630 = **5,102** [1].
*Examiner insight:* show the working line by line; a wrong sign on one item is easier to spot and partly credit when each step is visible.

**10.**

| Bank reconciliation statement at 30 November 2026 | |
|---|---:|
| Balance per updated cash book | 5,102 |
| Add: unpresented cheques (410 + 275 + 135) | 820 |
| | 5,922 |
| Less: uncredited lodgement | 1,650 |
| **Balance per bank statement** | **4,272** |

Heading with date [1]. Starts with 5,102 [1]. Adds 820 [1]. Deducts 1,650 [1]. Correct result 4,272 [1]. Final line labelled [1].
*Examiner insight:* label the first and last lines clearly; an unlabelled figure at the end can be unclear about which balance it is.

**11. (a)** Overdraft 2,340 [1]. Add bank charges 85 [1]. Add interest 48 [1]. Add direct debit 210 [1]. Add dishonoured cheque 320 [1]. Less credit transfer 900 [1]. Updated overdraft: 2,340 + 85 + 48 + 210 + 320 − 900 = **2,103** [1].
**(b)** Reconciliation statement at 31 December 2026 [1]. Overdraft per updated cash book (2,103) [1]. Add unpresented cheques 760 giving (1,343) [1]. Less uncredited lodgement 1,150 [1]. Overdraft per bank statement **(2,493)** [1].
*Examiner insight:* write the overdraft as a negative or with the word "overdrawn"; leaving off the label changes the meaning of the figure.

**12.** Cash book 2,680 plus unpresented cheques 640 [1] gives 3,320, less uncredited lodgement 300 [1] gives 3,020 [1]. This equals the bank statement, so the 340 difference is explained by timing and nothing has been lost [1]. Sana is wrong.
*Examiner insight:* a conclusion that links back to the question ("so no write-off is needed") is usually needed in addition to the arithmetic.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying whether the trial balance agrees without giving a reason.
- Journalising a complete reversal for the single amount.
- Journalising an original entry error for the full figure rather than the difference.
- Putting the suspense balance on the wrong side.
- Writing a journal with no narrative or with an unclear account name.
- Placing a dishonoured cheque on the reconciliation statement.
- Adjusting the cash book for unpresented cheques or uncredited lodgements.
- Mixing up the signs when overdrawn.
- Failing to label the first and last lines of the reconciliation statement.
