## Questions

**1.** Complete the balanced symbol equation for aerobic respiration.

C₆H₁₂O₆ + ........ → ........ + 6H₂O **[2]**

**2.** A student writes this equation for aerobic respiration:

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 12H₂O

Explain why the equation is not balanced and write the correct version. **[3]**

**3.** Muscle cells contain many mitochondria.

**(a)** State **two** processes, other than muscle contraction, that use energy from ATP in cells. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why muscle cells contain many mitochondria. **[2]**

**4.** After heavy rain, a field becomes waterlogged and the soil spaces fill with water.

**(a)** Explain why the root cells of the crop start to respire anaerobically. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the word equation for anaerobic respiration in the root cells. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why the roots take up fewer mineral ions while the soil is waterlogged. **[2]**

**5.** A student sets up three vacuum flasks of germinating peas and three of boiled peas. All six flasks start at 20.0 °C. The table shows the temperatures after 2 days.

| Flask | Germinating peas / °C | Boiled peas / °C |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 24.3 | 20.1 |
| 2 | 24.9 | 20.3 |
| 3 | 24.6 | 20.2 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean final temperature of the germinating peas. **[2]** (calculator allowed)
**(b)** Calculate the mean rate of temperature rise of the germinating peas, in °C per day. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why both batches of peas were rinsed in disinfectant before being put in the flasks. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the temperature of the germinating peas rose. **[2]**

**6.** A student places 5 woodlice in tube P and 10 woodlice in tube Q. Each tube contains 10 cm³ of hydrogen-carbonate indicator below a gauze platform. Both tubes are sealed and kept at 20 °C.

**(a)** State the colour change the student would see. **[1]**
**(b)** Predict which tube changes colour first. Explain your prediction. **[2]**
**(c)** Give **two** variables, other than temperature and volume of indicator, that should be kept the same. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest why the woodlice are kept on a gauze platform. **[1]**

**7.** During a 200 m sprint, an athlete's leg muscles respire anaerobically.

**(a)** Explain why the leg muscles respire anaerobically during the sprint. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why anaerobic respiration releases less energy from each glucose molecule than aerobic respiration. **[2]**

**8.** Yeast is grown in two flasks of sugar solution at 30 °C. Air is bubbled through flask X. Flask Y is sealed so no air can enter.

**(a)** Name the gas produced by the yeast in both flasks. **[1]**
**(b)** Name **one** substance produced in flask Y but not in flask X. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the yeast population grows faster in flask X. **[2]**

**9.** A carbon dioxide sensor is used to measure the volume of carbon dioxide released by 20 g of germinating seeds. At 25 °C, the seeds release 6.0 cm³ of carbon dioxide in 15 minutes. (calculator allowed)

**(a)** Calculate the rate of carbon dioxide release in cm³ per minute. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the rate of carbon dioxide release per gram of seeds, in cm³ per gram per minute. **[1]**
**(c)** The experiment is repeated at 15 °C. The seeds release 3.6 cm³ of carbon dioxide in 15 minutes. Calculate the percentage decrease in the rate compared with 25 °C. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain why the rate is lower at 15 °C. **[2]**

**10.** Compare aerobic respiration in a leaf cell with anaerobic respiration in a yeast cell. **[4]**

**11.** Describe how you could investigate the effect of temperature on the rate of respiration of germinating seeds, using hydrogen-carbonate indicator. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** 6O₂ (on the left) [1]; 6CO₂ (on the right) [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Each formula needs the correct number in front; "O₂" without the 6 or "CO₂" without the 6 does not score, and "6O" is not accepted for oxygen.

**2.** There are 24 hydrogen atoms on the right but only 12 on the left [1]; there are 24 oxygen atoms on the right but only 18 on the left [1]; correct equation: **C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "It doesn't add up" is too vague for credit; the marks need a named element with the atom counts on each side.

**3. (a)** Any two: active transport [1]; building large molecules such as proteins [1]; cell division / growth; nerve impulses
**(b)** Muscle cells need a lot of energy for contraction [1]; more mitochondria means more aerobic respiration, so more ATP / energy is released [1]
*Examiner insight:* An answer saying mitochondria "make energy" loses the second mark in (b); write "release energy" or "produce ATP".

**4. (a)** Water fills the air spaces, so little oxygen reaches the roots [1]; without enough oxygen, aerobic respiration cannot happen [1]
**(b)** glucose → [1] ethanol + carbon dioxide [1]
**(c)** Anaerobic respiration releases less energy / makes less ATP [1]; mineral ions are taken up by active transport, which needs energy [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), lactic acid as the product scores zero for the products mark because it is the animal equation; both ethanol and carbon dioxide are needed.

**5. (a)** (24.3 + 24.9 + 24.6) ÷ 3 [1] = **24.6 °C** [1]
**(b)** Rise = 24.6 − 20.0 = 4.6 °C [1]; rate = 4.6 ÷ 2 = **2.3 °C per day** [1]
**(c)** To kill microorganisms on the peas [1]; so that the microorganisms do not respire and release heat, and any temperature rise is due to the peas only [1]
**(d)** The germinating peas respire and release energy as heat [1]; the vacuum flask insulates them, so the heat is not lost [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), an error carried forward from (a) is allowed: a wrong mean used correctly still earns the rate mark.

**6. (a)** From red (orange) to yellow [1]
**(b)** Tube Q, because more woodlice respire [1]; so carbon dioxide is released faster [1]
**(c)** Any two: size / mass of woodlice [1]; type / species of woodlice [1]; size of tube; time the tubes are left
**(d)** So the woodlice do not touch / contaminate the indicator (and are not harmed by it) [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (a) both colours are needed for the mark; "turns yellow" alone does not show a change.

**7. (a)** Muscles need energy faster than oxygen can be supplied by the blood [1]; so some glucose is broken down without oxygen [1]
**(b)** Glucose is only partly broken down [1]; much of the energy remains in the lactic acid [1]
*Examiner insight:* "No oxygen" on its own in (a) is not enough; the mark is for oxygen supply being too slow for the demand.

**8. (a)** Carbon dioxide [1]
**(b)** Ethanol [1]
**(c)** Yeast in X respires aerobically [1]; this releases more energy / ATP for growth and reproduction [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c), "more respiration" earns nothing unless you say it is aerobic and that it releases more energy.

**9. (a)** 6.0 ÷ 15 [1] = **0.40 cm³ per minute** [1]
**(b)** 0.40 ÷ 20 = **0.02 cm³ per gram per minute** [1]
**(c)** Rate at 15 °C = 3.6 ÷ 15 = 0.24 cm³ per minute [1]; (0.40 − 0.24) ÷ 0.40 × 100 [1] = **40%** [1]
**(d)** Respiration is controlled by enzymes [1]; at a lower temperature the enzymes and substrate molecules move more slowly, so fewer successful collisions happen per minute [1]
*Examiner insight:* Saying the enzymes are "denatured" at 15 °C is wrong and cancels a mark in (d); low temperature slows enzymes but does not denature them.

**10.** Any four: aerobic needs oxygen, anaerobic does not [1]; aerobic produces carbon dioxide and water, anaerobic produces ethanol and carbon dioxide [1]; aerobic releases more energy / ATP per glucose [1]; aerobic breaks glucose down completely, anaerobic only partly [1]; aerobic happens mainly in mitochondria, anaerobic in the cytoplasm; both produce carbon dioxide / both release energy / both use glucose **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A "compare" answer must link both processes in each point; four facts about aerobic respiration alone do not show a comparison and score no marks.

**11.** Any six:
- Put the same volume of hydrogen-carbonate indicator in a set of boiling tubes [1]
- Place the same mass of germinating seeds of the same type on a gauze platform above the indicator in each tube [1]
- Seal each tube with a bung [1]
- Put the tubes in water baths at a range of temperatures, e.g. 10, 20, 30 and 40 °C [1]
- Time how long the indicator takes to change from red to yellow [1]
- Set up a control at each temperature with boiled (dead) seeds or glass beads [1]
- Keep the tubes in the dark so photosynthesis does not affect the result; repeat and calculate a mean time
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each mark needs a separate, practical step; "keep everything fair" earns nothing unless the controlled variables are named.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "respiration produces energy" instead of "releases energy" or "produces ATP".
- Giving lactic acid for plants or yeast, or ethanol for animals.
- Leaving carbon dioxide out of the plant/yeast anaerobic equation.
- Missing the 6 in front of O₂ or CO₂ in the symbol equation.
- Colour changes stated without the starting colour.
- Not explaining why boiled seeds act as a control (dead cells do not respire).
- Rates without units, or the wrong time unit.
- Saying low temperature "denatures" enzymes.
- Comparison answers that only describe one type of respiration.
