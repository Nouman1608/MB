## Answers

**1. (a)** 9 + 30 = **39** [1]

**(b)** 15 × 5 = **75** [1]

**(c)** 50 − 16 × 3 = 50 − 48 = **2** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* In part (c) only the 4 is squared, and the multiplication comes before the subtraction.

**2. (a)** 12 × 3 = **36** [1]

**(b)** 20 − 8 = **12** [1]

**(c)** 6 + 6 − 8 [1]; **4** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Part (a) is left to right: dividing first gives 36. Doing 7 × 3 first would give 4, which is wrong.

**3. (a)** **−36** [1]

**(b)** **36** [1]

**(c)** −1 × 4 = **−4** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* In part (a) the power acts on 6 only, and the minus sign is applied afterwards.

**4.** Numerator 32 + 4 = 36 and denominator 9 − 5 = 4 [1]; **9** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The fraction bar groups the top and bottom. Finish both before dividing.

**5.** √(100 − 36) = √64 = 8 [1]; 8 + 8 = **16** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* √(10² − 6²) is not 10 − 6. The root acts on the whole of 100 − 36.

**6. (a)** **1/8** [1]

**(b)** **4** [1]

**(c)** **5/3** (or 1 2/3) [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A number times its reciprocal is 1. Check: 0.25 × 4 = 1.

**7.** (1 + 2)² = 9 and 9 × 3 = 27 [1]; 30 − 27 = 3 [1]; 4 × 3 = **12** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Inside the square bracket the power comes first, then the multiplication, then the subtraction.

**8.** (−3)³ = −27 and (−4)² = 16 [1]; 2 × 16 = 32 [1]; −27 − 32 = **−59** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* An odd power keeps the negative sign; an even power removes it.

**9. (a)** **(4 + 6) × 5 − 3** [1]

**(b)** **4 + 6 × (5 − 3)** [1]

**(c)** **(4 + 6) × (5 − 3)** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Check each version from left to right: 10 × 5 − 3 = 47, 4 + 12 = 16 and 10 × 2 = 20.

**10. (a)** She multiplied 4 × 3 before dividing; division and multiplication have equal priority and are done left to right [1]

**(b)** 36 ÷ 4 = 9, 9 × 3 = 27, 27 − 1 = **26** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* An "explain" mark needs the rule, not just "she did it in the wrong order".

**11. (a)** Numerator 14.7 and denominator 2.94 [1]; **5** [1]

**(b)** **15.9** [1]; his keying divides only the 3.7 by 2.1 (and then multiplies by 1.4) instead of dividing the whole numerator by the whole denominator [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* An estimate shows the problem: the top is about 15 and the bottom about 3, so the answer should be about 5.

**12. (a)** **3 × (5 + 7) − 4²** [1]

**(b)** 36 − 16 = **20** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* "The sum of 5 and 7" must be found before multiplying by 3, so it needs a bracket.

**13. (a)** 4 + 6 = **10** [1]

**(b)** 1/4 + 1/6 = 3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12 [1]; reciprocal **12/5** (= 2.4) [1]

**(c)** **No**: 10 ≠ 12/5, so the two are not equal in general [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* One counter-example is enough to show that a general statement is false.

**14. (a)** For example **(2 + 5) × 7** [1]

**(b)** For example **5 × 2 − 7** [1]

**(c)** For example **(7 − 5) ÷ 2** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Any correct calculation using each number exactly once scores. Write it with brackets where needed so the order is unambiguous.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Squaring the minus sign in −6², or not squaring it in (−6)².
- Doing every division before any multiplication because of the order of letters in BIDMAS.
- Working a chain of subtractions from right to left.
- Taking a root of each term separately instead of the whole expression under the sign.
- Dividing only the last term of a numerator, on paper or on a calculator.
- Giving an explanation that names no rule.
- Adding reciprocals instead of finding the reciprocal of the sum.
