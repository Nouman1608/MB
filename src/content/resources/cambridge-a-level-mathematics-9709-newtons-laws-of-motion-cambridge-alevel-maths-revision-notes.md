Condensed recall for subtopic 4.4 of Cambridge 9709 Paper 4. The [Newton's laws study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-newtons-laws-of-motion-cambridge-alevel-maths/) explains each set-up with worked examples; the [Newton's laws practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-newtons-laws-of-motion-cambridge-alevel-maths-practice/) test it.

## Scope

- Particle of **constant mass**, **constant forces**, motion in a **straight line**.
- Forces may include friction, tension in an inextensible string and thrust in a connecting rod.
- W = mg with **g = 10 m s⁻²**.
- Vertical motion, inclined planes (rough or smooth), connected particles (string over a smooth pulley; car and trailer by rope or rigid tow-bar).
- Air resistance or other resistances appear **only if the question states them**.

## The laws in one line each

| Law | Use in a question |
|---|---|
| First | Zero resultant ⇔ constant velocity; resolve with "= 0" in any direction without acceleration |
| Second | Resultant force in the direction of acceleration = ma |
| Third | Equal and opposite forces between connected bodies; same T acts on both ends of a light string |

## Normal reaction: the step that decides everything

| Situation | Normal reaction R |
|---|---|
| Horizontal surface, horizontal force | mg |
| Horizontal surface, pull P at θ above horizontal | mg − P sin θ |
| Horizontal surface, push P at θ below horizontal | mg + P sin θ |
| Plane inclined at α, force parallel to plane | mg cos α |
| Plane inclined at α, horizontal force P pushing into plane | mg cos α + P sin α |

Friction while sliding: **F = μR**, opposing the motion.

## Standard equations

```
Lift, accelerating up at a:          T − mg = ma
Lift, accelerating down at a:        mg − T = ma
Rough slope, moving up:              −mg sin α − μmg cos α = ma
Rough slope, moving down:             mg sin α − μmg cos α = ma
Pulley, hanging A (heavier) and B:   m_A g − T = m_A a,   T − m_B g = m_B a
Table and pulley (A on table):       T − μm_A g = m_A a,  m_B g − T = m_B a
Car and trailer (system):            D − R_car − R_trailer = (M + m)a
Car and trailer (trailer only):      T − R_trailer = ma
```

## Two-phase problems

1. While the string is taut, find a and T from the connected-particle equations.
2. Use v² = u² + 2as to find the speed when the string goes slack (a particle hits the floor) or breaks.
3. After that, each particle moves on its own:
   - a hanging particle moving upwards decelerates at g;
   - a particle on a rough horizontal table decelerates at μg;
   - a particle on a slope has the single-particle slope acceleration.
4. Add the distances from each phase if the question asks for a total.

## Slides back or stays?

After stopping on a rough slope, the particle slides back **only if tan α > μ** (weight component mg sin α beats maximum friction μmg cos α). State the comparison in the answer.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Light string | negligible mass; tension the same throughout |
| Inextensible | does not stretch; connected particles share speed and acceleration magnitude |
| Smooth pulley | no friction at the pulley; tension equal on both sides |
| Thrust | a pushing force in a rod or tow-bar |
| Driving force | forward force from an engine, given or found from power |
| Resistance | force opposing motion, other than friction from a surface, stated in the question |
| Taut / slack | string under tension / string with zero tension |

## Exam traps

- **R = mg on autopilot.** Wrong on slopes and with angled forces.
- **"Moving up" taken to mean "accelerating up".** A lift moving up and slowing has downward acceleration.
- **Internal forces in the system equation.** Tension or thrust cancels; it can only be found from one body's equation.
- **Rope that pushes.** A rope cannot be in thrust; a negative tension means the rope is slack.
- **Accelerations after the string goes slack.** They are no longer equal; work out each particle separately.
- **Friction direction after reversal.** Up the slope it acts down the slope; on the way down it acts up the slope.
- **Mass and weight.** Weight in newtons is ten times the mass in kilograms when g = 10.
- **Premature rounding.** Keep sin and cos values to at least four significant figures through every line.

## Self-test

1. A 4 kg particle is pulled along a smooth horizontal surface by a horizontal 18 N force against a 6 N resistance. Find its acceleration.
2. A lift of mass 600 kg moves upwards and accelerates at 0.5 m s⁻². Find the tension in the cable.
3. The lift in question 2 now moves upwards while slowing down at 0.5 m s⁻². Find the tension.
4. A 2 kg particle slides down a smooth plane inclined at 30°. Find its acceleration.
5. A 5 kg box on rough horizontal ground (μ = 0.4) is pulled by a rope at 30° above the horizontal with tension 20 N. Find R.
6. Particles of masses 5 kg and 3 kg hang over a smooth pulley. Find the acceleration.
7. Find the tension in question 6.
8. A 2 kg block on a rough table (μ = 0.5) is joined over a pulley at the edge to a hanging 3 kg particle. Find the acceleration.
9. A particle on a rough slope inclined at 25° has μ = 0.5. After coming to rest on its way up, does it slide back?
10. A car (1000 kg) tows a trailer (500 kg) with a tow-bar. The system decelerates at 2 m s⁻² and the trailer has resistance 400 N. Find the force in the tow-bar and say whether it is a tension or a thrust.
11. A particle is projected up a smooth plane inclined at α where sin α = 0.6. Find its deceleration.
12. Why are the accelerations of two particles joined by a string over a pulley equal in magnitude?

### Answers

1. 12 = 4a, **a = 3 m s⁻²**.
2. T − 6000 = 600 × 0.5, **T = 6300 N**.
3. 6000 − T = 300, **T = 5700 N**.
4. a = 10 sin 30° = **5 m s⁻²**.
5. R = 50 − 20 sin 30° = **40 N**.
6. (50 − 30) ÷ 8 = **2.5 m s⁻²**.
7. 50 − T = 5 × 2.5, **T = 37.5 N**.
8. (30 − 0.5 × 20) ÷ 5 = **4 m s⁻²**.
9. tan 25° = 0.466 < 0.5, so **it stays at rest**.
10. Trailer, forward positive, tension T: T − 400 = 500 × (−2), so T = −600. The negative tension means the bar pushes back on the trailer: **a thrust of 600 N**.
11. a = 10 × 0.6 = **6 m s⁻²**.
12. The string is **inextensible**, so the particles move the same distance in the same time while it is taut.
