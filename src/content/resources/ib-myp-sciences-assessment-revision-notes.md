Condensed for quick recall of how the subject is assessed. For the full subject overview, use the
[IB MYP Sciences subject overview](/resources/ib-myp-sciences-subject-guide/).

## No SL/HL split — four criteria instead

| Criterion | Focus |
|---|---|
| A — Knowing and understanding | Recall and scientific understanding |
| B — Inquiring and designing | Formulating investigations |
| C — Processing and evaluating | Handling data, drawing conclusions |
| D — Reflecting on the impacts of science | Ethical/social/environmental implications |

Applies **regardless of whether your school teaches discrete Biology/Chemistry/Physics or an
integrated science course** — same four criteria either way. Each scored on an **8-point scale (1-8)**.

## If your school offers MYP eAssessment

Available in biology, chemistry, physics **or** integrated sciences — three tasks:

| Task | Criteria | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Knowing and understanding | A | 25 |
| Investigation skills (inquiry, design, data, evaluation) | B + C | 50 |
| Applying science (real-life implications) | D | 25 |

The investigation-skills task is worth **half the total marks** — the single biggest component.

## Exam traps

- Focusing revision only on recall (Criterion A, 25 marks) and neglecting investigation-skills practice (Criteria B+C, 50 marks).
- Treating "reflecting on impacts" (Criterion D) as an optional add-on rather than a formally assessed criterion.
- Assuming discrete-science students are assessed differently from integrated-science students — the criteria are identical either way.

## Self-test

1. Name the four MYP Sciences criteria.
2. Which eAssessment task is worth the most marks, and what does it cover?
3. Does the assessment model differ between discrete and integrated science courses?
4. What does Criterion D specifically assess?

**Answers:** 1. Knowing and understanding; Inquiring and designing; Processing and evaluating; Reflecting on the impacts of science. 2. The investigation-skills task (50 marks), covering inquiry, design, data collection and evaluation. 3. No — the same four criteria apply regardless of whether a school teaches discrete sciences or an integrated course. 4. Reflecting on the real-life implications of science — social, ethical and environmental.
