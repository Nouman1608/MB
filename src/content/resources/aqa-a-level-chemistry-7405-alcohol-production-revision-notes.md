These notes condense section **3.3.5.1 Alcohol production** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2 at AS and A-level. For worked examples and the full comparison of routes, read the [Alcohol production study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-alcohol-production/); for questions, use the [Alcohol production practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-alcohol-production-practice/). The whole topic is summarised in the [Alcohols revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-alcohols-revision-notes/).

## What you must be able to do

- **Outline the mechanism** for an alkene reacting with steam and an acid catalyst.
- **Justify the conditions** for fermentation of glucose.
- **Explain the meaning** of biofuel.
- **Write equations** that support the carbon-neutral claim, and **give reasons** it is not valid.
- **Discuss** environmental and ethical issues in biofuel decisions.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hydration | Addition of water across a C=C bond to give an alcohol |
| Electrophilic addition | Addition started by an electron-pair acceptor (here H⁺) attacking the C=C bond |
| Carbocation | Ion with a positively charged carbon |
| Fermentation | Conversion of glucose to ethanol and CO₂ by enzymes in yeast, without air |
| Biofuel | Fuel produced from biological material that has been grown recently and can be regrown |
| Carbon neutral | Producing no net CO₂ emission to the atmosphere |
| Batch process | Made one batch at a time, then the vessel is emptied and restarted |
| Continuous process | Reactants fed in and products removed without stopping |

## Hydration: mechanism checklist

| Step | Arrow from | Arrow to | Species formed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | C=C bond | H⁺ | Carbocation |
| 2 | Lone pair on O of H₂O | C⁺ | R–OH₂⁺ |
| 3 | O–H bond of R–OH₂⁺ | O | Alcohol + H⁺ (regenerated) |

## Hydration: which alcohol?

1. Draw the alkene. If both C=C carbons carry the same groups, there is one product.
2. Otherwise, place H⁺ on each carbon in turn and classify each carbocation.
3. Tertiary > secondary > primary: the more stable carbocation gives the **major** alcohol.

| Alkene | Major product | Minor product |
|---|---|---|
| Pent-1-ene | Pentan-2-ol | Pentan-1-ol |
| 2-methylbut-2-ene | 2-methylbutan-2-ol | 3-methylbutan-2-ol |
| 2-methylpropene | 2-methylpropan-2-ol | 2-methylpropan-1-ol |
| Cyclohexene | Cyclohexanol only | None |
| But-2-ene | Butan-2-ol only | None |

## Fermentation: condition + reason

| Condition | Reason |
|---|---|
| Yeast | Enzymes catalyse the reaction |
| About 35 °C | Colder: too slow. Hotter: enzymes denature |
| Anaerobic | Oxygen → aerobic respiration (CO₂ + H₂O) and oxidation of ethanol to ethanoic acid |
| Aqueous | Glucose and enzymes in solution |
| Then fractional distillation | Ethanol (b.p. about 78 °C) separated from water |

Equation: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂.

## Two routes in one table

| | Hydration of ethene | Fermentation |
|---|---|---|
| Feedstock | Crude oil, finite | Crops, renewable |
| Rate | Fast | Slow |
| Process | Continuous | Batch |
| Purity | High | Dilute; must be distilled |
| Atom economy | 100% | 51.1% |
| Conditions | About 300 °C, high pressure, H₃PO₄ on silica | About 35 °C, atmospheric pressure, yeast |

Atom economy of fermentation = (2 × 46.0) ÷ 180.0 × 100 = 51.1%.

## Carbon neutrality in four lines

```
6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂        6 CO₂ absorbed
C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂           2 CO₂ released
2C₂H₅OH + 6O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O        4 CO₂ released
net: 6 − 6 = 0
```

**Not valid because** fossil fuels supply energy for farm machinery, fertiliser manufacture, distillation and transport, all releasing CO₂ not absorbed by the crop.

## Discuss-question checklist

- Advantage: renewable; conserves crude oil; can lower net CO₂.
- Disadvantage: fossil-fuel inputs; deforestation releases stored carbon and destroys habitats; fertiliser run-off; water use.
- Ethical: land for fuel, not food; higher food prices.
- Conclusion: a reasoned judgement.

## Exam traps

- First arrow drawn from H⁺, not from the C=C bond.
- No + charge on the carbocation or the oxonium ion.
- H⁺ not shown as regenerated.
- Major and minor products swapped.
- "High temperature" for fermentation.
- "No air" without the reason.
- "Bioethanol releases no CO₂."
- Listing conditions without justification.

## Self-test

1. Name the catalyst used for the industrial hydration of ethene.
2. Name the major product of hydrating pent-1-ene.
3. Why is the H⁺ ion described as a catalyst in hydration?
4. Name the only product of hydrating cyclohexene.
5. Give one reason why fermentation is not carried out at 60 °C.
6. Why is fermentation carried out without air?
7. Calculate the atom economy of fermentation for making ethanol.
8. Give one advantage of hydration over fermentation.
9. Give one advantage of fermentation over hydration.
10. Define biofuel.
11. Explain how the three key equations support the claim that bioethanol is carbon neutral.
12. Give one ethical issue linked to biofuel crops.

### Answers

1. Phosphoric acid (on a silica support).
2. Pentan-2-ol.
3. It is used in the first step and released in the last, so it is not used up.
4. Cyclohexanol.
5. The enzymes in yeast would be denatured.
6. Oxygen would let yeast respire aerobically and would oxidise the ethanol to ethanoic acid.
7. 92.0 ÷ 180.0 × 100 = **51.1%**.
8. Faster, continuous, purer product or 100% atom economy (any one).
9. Uses a renewable raw material, or lower temperature and pressure (any one).
10. A fuel made from biological material that has been grown recently and can be regrown.
11. Photosynthesis absorbs 6 CO₂ per glucose; fermentation releases 2 and combustion of the 2 ethanol releases 4, so the totals balance.
12. Land used for fuel crops is not available for food, which can raise food prices.
