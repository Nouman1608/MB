## Section A

**1.** Define a *greenhouse gas*. **[1]**

**2.** State the chemical formula of methane. **[1]**

**3.** Name the gas released when calcium carbonate is heated during cement manufacture. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** impacts of climate change on the oceans. **[2]**

**5.** Distinguish between *reforestation* and *afforestation*. **[2]**

**6.** State what is meant by *carbon sequestration*. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** The table shows the concentration of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere at a monitoring station. The figures are invented for practice.

| Year | 1960 | 1980 | 2000 | 2020 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Carbon dioxide (parts per million) | 320 | 340 | 370 | 415 |

(a) Calculate the increase in concentration between 1960 and 2020. **[1]**
(b) Calculate the mean rate of increase per year between 1960 and 2020, to one decimal place. **[1]**
(c) Describe how the rate of increase changed over the period. Use data in your answer. **[2]**
(d) Explain **two** human activities that could account for the increase. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how the melting of ice sheets and glaciers can lead to the forced migration of people living in low-lying coastal areas. **[3]**

**9.** Explain how climate change can lead to increased pest outbreaks and invasive species. **[2]**

**10.** A household's annual activities are shown. The emission factors are invented for practice.

| Activity | Amount | Emission factor |
|---|---|---|
| Electricity | 2,400 kWh | 0.6 kg CO2 per kWh |
| Bottled gas for cooking | | 300 kg CO2 in total |
| Car travel | 12,000 km | 0.18 kg CO2 per km |

(a) Calculate the household's emissions from car travel and its total emissions, in kg of CO2. **[2]**
(b) Calculate the percentage of the total that comes from car travel, to one decimal place. **[1]**
(c) Suggest **two** strategies from the syllabus that would reduce this household's carbon footprint the most. Give a reason for each. **[2]**

**11.** Explain how a reduction in the number of children per woman can reduce carbon footprints. **[2]**

**12.** A farming region is becoming hotter and drier, and its river floods more often in the short wet season.
(a) Describe **two** adaptation strategies suitable for this region. **[2]**
(b) Explain why adaptation alone cannot solve the problem of climate change. **[2]**

**13.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of international agreements and taxation as strategies to manage the impacts of climate change. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** A gas that absorbs radiation and emits the energy as thermal (heat) energy **[1]**.

**2.** CH4 **[1]**.

**3.** Carbon dioxide **[1]**.

**4.** Any two of: increased ocean surface temperature; ocean acidification; sea-level rise from melting land ice. First **[1]**; second **[1]**.

**5.** Reforestation is replanting trees where forest has been cut down or lost **[1]**; afforestation is planting trees on land that was not previously forest **[1]**.

**6.** Capturing, removing and storing carbon dioxide so it does not stay in the atmosphere **[1]**.

**7.** (a) 415 − 320 = 95 parts per million **[1]**.
(b) 95 ÷ 60 = 1.6 parts per million per year **[1]**.
(c) The rate increased over time **[1]**: 20 ppm in 1960 to 1980 (1.0 per year), 30 ppm in 1980 to 2000 (1.5 per year) and 45 ppm in 2000 to 2020 (2.25 per year) **[1]**.
(d) Combustion of fossil fuels in power stations, vehicles and industry releases carbon dioxide **[1]**; deforestation means fewer trees remove carbon dioxide by photosynthesis **[1]**. Also accept cement manufacture, or rising population and energy use, with explanation.

**8.** Melting ice sheets and glaciers add water to the oceans, so sea level rises **[1]**. Low-lying coasts suffer more frequent flooding and permanent loss of land, and salt water may contaminate farmland and fresh water **[1]**. Homes and livelihoods are lost, so people are forced to move to higher ground or other regions **[1]**.

**9.** Warmer conditions allow pests to survive winters and breed more times a year, so outbreaks are larger **[1]**; species can spread into regions that were previously too cold, where they become invasive and compete with native species **[1]**.

**10.** (a) Car: 12,000 × 0.18 = 2,160 kg **[1]**. Total: (2,400 × 0.6 = 1,440) + 300 + 2,160 = 3,900 kg **[1]**.
(b) 2,160 ÷ 3,900 × 100 = 55.4% **[1]**.
(c) Reduce use of fossil fuels for travel by using public transport, cycling or walking, because the car is the largest source **[1]**; increase energy efficiency, or switch to renewable electricity, because electricity is the second largest source **[1]**.

**11.** Fewer children per woman slows population growth **[1]**, so in future fewer people will need energy, food and transport, and total greenhouse gas emissions grow more slowly **[1]**.

**12.** (a) Plant crops that resist warmer and drier conditions **[1]**; improve flood defences along the river or pass laws to stop building on the floodplain **[1]**.
(b) Adaptation deals with the impacts but does not reduce greenhouse gas emissions **[1]**, so warming continues and impacts may eventually become too severe or too expensive to adapt to **[1]**.

**13.** One mark per point; at least one benefit and one limitation of each strategy and a judgement for full marks.
- International agreements benefit: commit many countries to emission targets, so cuts happen on a global scale, which matters because greenhouse gases mix through the whole atmosphere **[1]**.
- International agreements limitation: countries may not sign, may set weak targets or may fail to meet them, and enforcement is difficult **[1]**.
- Taxation benefit: taxes on fuel or carbon emissions make high-emission choices more expensive, so people and businesses change behaviour **[1]**.
- Taxation benefit: tax income can be invested in renewable energy, public transport or adaptation **[1]**.
- Taxation limitation: taxes can be unpopular and can fall hardest on low-income households who spend a larger share of income on fuel **[1]**.
- Judgement: for example, agreements set the targets and taxation is one way each country can meet them, so the two work best together alongside other strategies **[1]**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 1**: writing "a gas that traps heat" instead of the syllabus definition.
- **Question 7(b)**: dividing by the wrong number of years.
- **Question 7(c)**: saying the concentration increased (true) when the question asks about the **rate**.
- **Question 10(c)**: suggesting strategies without using the data to justify them.
- **Question 12(a)**: giving strategies that reduce emissions instead of adaptation.
