Cambridge International AS & A Level Economics (9708) studies how individuals, firms and governments make choices when resources are scarce, and what those choices mean for markets, whole economies and trade between countries. It is built in two stages. The AS Level stage introduces the core ideas of microeconomics and macroeconomics; the A Level stage returns to the same areas with more advanced theory and wider policy debate. The syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028 is assessed by four written papers. Measured against the full A Level, Paper 1 AS Level Multiple Choice (60 minutes, 30 marks) carries 17%, Paper 2 AS Level Data Response and Essays (120 minutes, 60 marks) 33%, Paper 3 A Level Multiple Choice (75 minutes, 30 marks) 17%, and Paper 4 A Level Data Response and Essays (120 minutes, 60 marks) 33%.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus lists eleven topics. Six belong to the AS stage and five to the A Level stage, and the A Level topics revisit five of the AS themes in greater depth.

**AS Level topics (assessed in Papers 1 and 2)**

1. **Basic economic ideas and resource allocation**: scarcity, choice and opportunity cost; economic methodology; factors of production; resource allocation in different economic systems; production possibility curves; classification of goods and services.
2. **The price system and the microeconomy**: demand and supply curves; price, income and cross elasticity of demand; price elasticity of supply; the interaction of demand and supply; consumer and producer surplus.
3. **Government microeconomic intervention**: why and how governments intervene in markets, and addressing income and wealth inequality.
4. **The macroeconomy**: national income statistics, an introduction to the circular flow of income, aggregate demand and aggregate supply analysis, economic growth, unemployment and price stability.
5. **Government macroeconomic intervention**: policy objectives, fiscal policy, monetary policy and supply-side policy.
6. **International economic issues**: the reasons for international trade, protectionism, the current account of the balance of payments, exchange rates and policies to correct current account imbalances.

**A Level topics (assessed in Papers 3 and 4)**

7. **The price system and the microeconomy**: utility; indifference curves and budget lines; efficiency and market failure; private and social costs and benefits and externalities; costs, revenue, profit and production in the short and long run; market structures; the growth and survival of firms; firms' objectives and policies.
8. **Government microeconomic intervention**: correcting market failure, equity and redistribution, and labour market forces and government intervention.
9. **The macroeconomy**: the circular flow of income, economic growth and sustainability, employment and unemployment, and money and banking.
10. **Government macroeconomic intervention**: policy objectives, how macroeconomic problems are linked, and how effective policy options are in meeting all objectives together.
11. **International economic issues**: policies to correct balance of payments disequilibrium, exchange rates, economic development, countries at different levels of development and the relationships between them, and globalisation.

Topic 1 has no A Level counterpart; it supplies the vocabulary and methods the rest of the course depends on.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting (full A Level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | AS Level Multiple Choice | 60 minutes | 30 | 17% |
| Paper 2 | AS Level Data Response and Essays | 120 minutes | 60 | 33% |
| Paper 3 | A Level Multiple Choice | 75 minutes | 30 | 17% |
| Paper 4 | A Level Data Response and Essays | 120 minutes | 60 | 33% |

9708 is a staged qualification, which gives candidates a choice of route:

- **AS Level only.** Papers 1 and 2 can be certificated on their own as a standalone AS Level. On this route the weightings change: Paper 1 is 33% and Paper 2 is 67% of the AS Level.
- **Full A Level.** Candidates continue to Papers 3 and 4, and all four papers together make up the A Level, using the weightings in the table.

Every paper is a written examination. Each stage pairs a multiple-choice paper, which tests breadth and accuracy, with a data response and essays paper, which tests interpretation, analysis and extended argument and carries the larger share of the marks.

## How the parts fit together

The structure of 9708 is a spiral. You meet the price system, government intervention in markets, the macroeconomy, macroeconomic policy and international issues at AS, then meet each again at A Level with sharper tools. Elasticity and market equilibrium from Topic 2 are prerequisites for market structures and externalities in Topic 7. The introduction to the circular flow in Topic 4 becomes a full treatment in Topic 9. Fiscal, monetary and supply-side policy in Topic 5 are judged against one another in Topic 10, where the emphasis is on how objectives conflict.

Microeconomics and macroeconomics also connect. Government microeconomic intervention addresses inequality at AS and redistribution at A Level, which feeds into macroeconomic debates about growth and sustainability. International topics draw on both, since exchange rates and trade affect prices, output and employment.

Two skills run through every topic: drawing and explaining diagrams accurately, and evaluating, which means weighing arguments and reaching a supported judgement rather than listing points.

## Planning your study

For most students the natural order is to work through the AS topics first, numbered as in the syllabus, then the A Level topics.

- **AS stage.** Begin with Topic 1 to establish key terms and the production possibility curve, then build microeconomics through Topics 2 and 3 before turning to macroeconomics and policy in Topics 4 and 5. Finish with international issues in Topic 6, which uses ideas from all the earlier topics.
- **A Level stage.** Before starting each A Level topic, briefly review its AS counterpart. Topic 7 has more sections than any other topic and benefits from early attention; Topics 10 and 11 work well near the end because they ask you to connect the whole course.

Within each stage, practise multiple-choice questions throughout, since they reveal gaps quickly, and begin data response and essay practice as soon as a topic has been taught. Move to full past papers once all the topics for a stage are complete, and use the course's exam-preparation page for paper-by-paper guidance.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers does Cambridge A Level Economics 9708 have?**
Four. Papers 1 and 2 are the AS Level papers; Papers 3 and 4 complete the A Level. Each stage has a multiple-choice paper and a data response and essays paper.

**Can I take AS Level Economics on its own?**
Yes. Papers 1 and 2 can be certificated as a standalone AS Level, where Paper 1 is worth 33% and Paper 2 67% of the AS grade.

**How are the papers weighted in the full A Level?**
Paper 1 is 17%, Paper 2 33%, Paper 3 17% and Paper 4 33%.

**Why do the A Level topics have the same names as the AS topics?**
The A Level topics revisit the price system, government intervention, the macroeconomy, macroeconomic policy and international issues in greater depth, so AS knowledge is the base for them.
