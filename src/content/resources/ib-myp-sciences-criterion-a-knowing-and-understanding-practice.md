> **These are original questions written for Marlbridge** for revision and practice on Criterion A. They are **not** reproduced IB material and they do not copy the structure or mark tariffs of any on-screen examination. Real MYP work is judged against criterion level descriptors (achievement levels 1-8), so the `[n]` marks below are a revision aid, not IB marks.

Study first: the [Criterion A study guide](/resources/ib-myp-sciences-criterion-a-knowing-and-understanding/) and [revision notes](/resources/ib-myp-sciences-criterion-a-knowing-and-understanding-revision-notes/). Also: [IB MYP Sciences hub](/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-sciences/) and [printable checklist](/checklists/ib/ib-myp/myp-sciences/). The questions use the MYP command terms. Use a calculator, show working and give units. Give final answers to three significant figures unless a question says otherwise.

## Questions

**1.** (Criterion A) Define the term *density*. **[1]**

**2.** (Criterion A) A rectangular block has a mass of 135 g. It measures 5.0 cm by 3.0 cm by 6.0 cm. Calculate its density. **[3]**

**3.** (Criterion A) State the name of the process by which particles of a gas spread from a region of high concentration to a region of low concentration. **[1]**

**4.** (Criterion A) Outline, using the particle model, why a gas always fills the whole of its container. **[2]**

**5.** (Criterion A) A trolley moves at 5.0 m/s and accelerates uniformly at 2.5 m/s² for 6.0 s.

**(a)** Calculate its final speed. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the distance it travels in the 6.0 s. **[2]**

**6.** (Criterion A) Describe the process of photosynthesis. Include the reactants, the products and the conditions needed. **[3]**

**7.** (Criterion A) A student half-fills a plastic bottle with hot water, screws on the lid tightly and leaves it to cool. After an hour the sides of the bottle are pushed inwards. Explain this observation. **[3]**

**8.** (Criterion A) A student writes: "Ice cools a drink because cold flows from the ice into the drink." Evaluate this statement. **[4]**

**9.** (Criterion A) An enzyme was tested at five temperatures. The reaction rate is in arbitrary units.

| Temperature (°C) | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate | 3 | 7 | 12 | 15 | 4 |

**(a)** Interpret the data. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the rate at 50 °C is much lower than at 40 °C. **[3]**
**(c)** A student concludes that the optimum temperature is exactly 40 °C. Judge this conclusion. **[2]**

**10.** (Criterion A) An advert for a metal bracelet says: "Our bracelet balances your body's energy. In our test, 9 out of 10 wearers said they felt more energetic." Analyse and evaluate the scientific quality of this claim and make a judgment. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Density is mass per unit volume (mass divided by volume) [1]. **[1]**
*Marking note:* "How heavy something is" does not earn the point, because it leaves out volume.

**2.** Volume = 5.0 × 3.0 × 6.0 = 90 cm³ [1]. Density = mass ÷ volume = 135 ÷ 90 [1]. Density = **1.50 g/cm³** [1]. **[3]**
*Marking note:* The final point needs the unit. An answer of 1.5 with no unit loses it.

**3.** Diffusion [1]. **[1]**

**4.** The particles of a gas move randomly in all directions at high speed [1]. The forces between them are very weak, so nothing holds them together and they spread out until they occupy the whole volume [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** v = u + at [1]. v = 5.0 + (2.5 × 6.0) = **20 m/s** [1].
**(b)** s = ut + ½at² = (5.0 × 6.0) + (0.5 × 2.5 × 6.0²) [1]. s = 30 + 45 = **75 m** [1].
*Marking note:* Part (b) can also be found from average speed: (5.0 + 20) ÷ 2 × 6.0 = 75 m. Either method earns both points.

**6.** Reactants are carbon dioxide and water [1]. Products are glucose and oxygen [1]. Light energy is absorbed by chlorophyll in the chloroplasts, which is needed for the reaction to happen [1]. **[3]**
*Marking note:* A word equation (carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen) with light and chlorophyll named also earns all three.

**7.** As the bottle cools, the air and water vapour inside lose energy and the particles move more slowly [1]. They hit the inside walls less often and with less force, and some vapour condenses, so the pressure inside falls [1]. The atmospheric pressure outside is now greater than the pressure inside, so the sides are pushed inwards [1]. **[3]**
*Marking note:* The explanation must compare the pressure inside with the pressure outside. "The air shrinks" alone is only an outline.

**8.** Any four of the following points.
- The idea of "cold flowing" is wrong: energy is transferred from warmer objects to cooler ones, not cold from ice to the drink [1].
- The drink is warmer than the ice, so thermal energy moves from the drink to the ice [1].
- Some of this energy is used to melt the ice, which keeps the temperature of the ice near 0 °C until it has melted [1].
- The drink therefore loses energy and its temperature falls [1].
- A judgment that the observation (the drink cools) is correct but the explanation is not [1].
**[4]**

**9. (a)** The rate rises as the temperature rises from 10 °C to 40 °C [1]. It then falls sharply at 50 °C, so the highest rate among the values tested is at 40 °C [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** At a higher temperature the shape of the enzyme, including its active site, changes [1]. The substrate no longer fits, so the enzyme is denatured [1]. This change is permanent, so the reaction rate falls [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** The conclusion is not fully supported [1]. The optimum could lie anywhere between 30 °C and 50 °C, because no values were tested between 40 °C and 50 °C. More readings (for example at 35, 40, 45 °C) are needed [1]. **[2]**
*Marking note:* A judgment needs a verdict and a reason that refers to the data.

**10.** Any six marks from the following.
- The claim "balances body energy" gives no scientific mechanism or measurable quantity, so it cannot be tested [1].
- "Felt more energetic" is a subjective, qualitative opinion and not a measurement [1].
- There was no control group, for example people wearing a plain bracelet [1].
- People who expect an effect may report one (a placebo effect) [1].
- The size of the sample is small and the selection of wearers is unknown [1].
- No data, repeats or independent check are shown, and the test was run by the seller [1].
- A supported judgment: the claim is not scientifically supported, and a fair test would use a control group, objective measures and a larger sample [1].
**[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating a fact when the command term asks for an explanation.
- Leaving the unit off a final answer.
- Skipping the line that shows the equation and substituted numbers.
- Describing what happens in the bottle without comparing the pressure inside and outside.
- Judging a claim as "good" or "bad" with no reason.
- Treating a claim as scientific because it uses technical words.

## Frequently asked questions

**How should I use these questions?**
Attempt each one without notes, then mark yourself against the marking points. Redo any question where you lost the explanation points. The marks are a revision aid, not IB marks.

**What is the difference between question 8 and question 10?**
Question 8 evaluates an explanation using a scientific idea. Question 10 evaluates the quality of evidence in a claim. Both need a verdict at the end.

**Do I have to show working in calculations?**
Yes. The command term "calculate" asks for the relevant stages of the working, and working earns credit even if the arithmetic slips.
