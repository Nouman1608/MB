These questions cover section **4.7.2 Reactions of alkenes and alcohols (chemistry only)** of the AQA GCSE
Chemistry (8462) specification, for teaching from September 2016. The content is assessed on Paper 2 in the
separate chemistry qualification. Question 9 tests why carboxylic acids are weak acids, which the specification
marks (HT only), and is labelled **Higher tier only**.

Learn the content first in the
[reactions of alkenes and alcohols study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-reactions-of-alkenes-and-alcohols/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-reactions-of-alkenes-and-alcohols-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Alkenes are a homologous series of hydrocarbons.

**(a)** State the general formula of the alkenes. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the molecular formula of the alkene with five carbon atoms. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why alkenes are described as unsaturated. **[2]**

**2.** Ethene reacts with bromine.

**(a)** Describe what you would see when ethene is shaken with bromine water. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the balanced equation for the reaction of ethene with bromine. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the type of reaction and explain what happens to the double bond. **[2]**

**3.** Ethene reacts with hydrogen and with steam.

**(a)** Name the product of each reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** State the catalyst used in each reaction. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why each reaction produces only one product. **[1]**

**4.** Draw the fully displayed structural formula of the product of the reaction of propene with chlorine. **[2]**

**5.** Alkenes tend to burn in air with a smoky flame.

**(a)** Name the black solid that makes the flame smoky. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why alkenes burn with a smokier flame than alkanes. **[2]**

**6.** Ethanol can be made by fermentation of sugar solution.

**(a)** Name the substance added to the sugar solution to make ethanol. **[1]**
**(b)** State **two** conditions for fermentation. **[2]**
**(c)** Write the balanced equation for the fermentation of glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why oxygen must be excluded. **[1]**

**7.** Alcohols are a homologous series.

**(a)** Name the functional group in an alcohol and give the formula of propanol. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe what you would see when a small piece of sodium is added to ethanol, and name the gas made. **[2]**
**(c)** Write the balanced equation for the combustion of ethanol in excess oxygen. **[2]**
**(d)** Acidified potassium dichromate is added to ethanol and warmed. State the colour change and name the organic product. **[2]**

**8.** Ethanoic acid reacts with sodium carbonate and with ethanol.

**(a)** State **two** observations when ethanoic acid is added to sodium carbonate solution. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the salt formed. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the organic product of the reaction of ethanoic acid with ethanol and state its smell. **[2]**
**(d)** Name the other product of the reaction in part (c). **[1]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) A 0.1 mol/dm³ solution of ethanoic acid has a pH of about 3. A 0.1 mol/dm³ solution of hydrochloric acid has a pH of about 1.

**(a)** Explain why ethanoic acid is a weak acid. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the hydrogen ion concentration is in the hydrochloric acid solution. **[1]**

**10.** A student tests three colourless liquids, X, Y and Z. They are ethanol, ethanoic acid and hexene, but not in that order.

| Test | X | Y | Z |
|---|---|---|---|
| Add bromine water | stays orange | stays orange | turns colourless |
| Add sodium carbonate | no fizzing | fizzing | no fizzing |

**(a)** Identify X, Y and Z. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how you used the results to decide. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** CₙH₂ₙ [1]
**(b)** C₅H₁₀ [1]
**(c)** They contain a **C=C double bond** [1], so they have **fewer hydrogen atoms** than the alkane with the same number of carbon atoms (they can add more atoms) [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** The bromine water changes from **orange to colourless** [1].
**(b)** C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂ [1]
**(c)** An **addition** reaction [1]; the double bond becomes a **single bond** as a bromine atom adds to each carbon [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** Hydrogen: **ethane**; steam: **ethanol** [1] [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Hydrogen: **nickel** [1]; steam: **phosphoric acid** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** The atoms **add across the double bond** and nothing else is formed (no other molecule is lost) [1]. **[1]**

**4.** A three-carbon chain with a chlorine atom on each of the first two carbons, with all bonds drawn: H–C(H)(H)–C(H)(Cl)–C(H)(H)Cl, that is CH₃–CHCl–CH₂Cl, fully displayed [formula correct 1; all atoms and single bonds shown 1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Draw every C–H bond. A drawing with CH₃ written as a group does not meet "fully displayed".

**5. (a)** **Carbon** (soot) [1]
**(b)** Alkenes have a **higher proportion of carbon** (less hydrogen) than alkanes [1], so **incomplete combustion** is more likely and carbon forms [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** **Yeast** [1]
**(b)** Any two [1] each: about **30 to 40 °C** (warm); **no oxygen** (anaerobic). **[2]**
**(c)** C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ [formulae 1; balanced 1] **[2]**
**(d)** Oxygen would oxidise the ethanol to **ethanoic acid** (so little or no ethanol would be made) [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** **–OH** [1]; **C₃H₇OH** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** The sodium **fizzes** (and dissolves) [1] giving off **hydrogen** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O [formulae 1; balanced 1] **[2]**
**(d)** **Orange to green** [1]; **ethanoic acid** (a carboxylic acid) [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** **Fizzing / bubbles** [1] and the solid dissolves / the gas turns limewater milky [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** **Sodium ethanoate** [1]
**(c)** **Ethyl ethanoate** [1]; a **sweet / fruity** smell [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** **Water** [1]

**9. (a)** Ethanoic acid is **only partially ionised** in solution [1], so a **lower proportion** of its molecules release hydrogen ions, giving a lower hydrogen ion concentration / higher pH than a strong acid of the same concentration [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** pH 1 compared with pH 3 is 2 units, so 10 × 10 = **100 times** [1]. **[1]**

**10. (a)** X is **ethanol** [1], Y is **ethanoic acid** [1], Z is **hexene** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Z decolourised bromine water, which shows it has a **C=C** bond, so it is the alkene [1]. Y gave **fizzing with carbonate**, which shows it is a **carboxylic acid** [1]. X is left, so it is the alcohol (ethanol). **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that the double bond "breaks" with no mention of it becoming a single bond.
- Missing the catalyst for hydrogen (nickel) or steam (phosphoric acid).
- Not drawing every bond in a "fully displayed" formula.
- Forgetting that oxygen must be excluded in fermentation.
- Confusing the orange to colourless change (alkenes) with orange to green (oxidation of alcohols).
