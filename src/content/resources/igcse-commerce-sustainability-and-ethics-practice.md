These questions cover all three sections of Topic 6, Sustainability and ethics, in Cambridge IGCSE
Commerce (0715, 2028): sustainability in the supply chain, ethics and fair trade, and consumer
protection. Most are applied to business scenarios, because Paper 2 bases all its questions on commercial
contexts.

## Section A - multiple choice

**1.** Which action most directly reduces the number of delivery journeys in an online retailer's supply
chain? **[1]**
A free gifts   B reducing returns and exchanges   C persuasive advertising   D loss leaders

**2.** A business pays cocoa farmers a guaranteed minimum price and a premium for community projects.
This is an example of: **[1]**
A debt factoring   B fair trade   C collective advertising   D water stewardship

**3.** An advert claims a drink "cures colds", but there is no evidence for this. This is: **[1]**
A misleading advertising   B informative advertising   C sales promotion   D generic advertising

## Section B - structured questions

**4.** State the purpose of sustainability in the supply chain. **[2]**

**5.** *Verde Foods delivers groceries across a city using diesel vans. It also wraps fruit in plastic.*
(a) Explain **two** actions Verde Foods could take to make its logistics more sustainable. **[4]**
(b) Discuss whether Verde Foods should stop using plastic packaging. **[6]**

**6.** *A phone retailer is considering offering a free repair service and a digital resale marketplace.*
(a) Explain how each action improves sustainability. **[4]**
(b) Explain **one** possible disadvantage to the retailer. **[2]**

**7.** *A textile factory uses large amounts of water from a local river.* Explain what water stewardship would
involve for the factory. **[3]**

**8.** Define *ethics* in commerce. **[2]**

**9.** *A supermarket is deciding whether to stock only fair trade bananas.*
(a) Explain **two** benefits to the supermarket. **[4]**
(b) Explain **one** risk to the supermarket. **[2]**

**10.** *An electronics company sends old products to a country without safe recycling facilities, where
they are burned in open dumps.*
(a) Identify the ethical issue. **[1]**
(b) Explain **two** ways this could harm the company. **[4]**

**11.** Explain **two** reasons why consumers need protection. **[4]**

**12.** *Ravi buys a jacket online. The zip breaks after two days.*
(a) Describe **three** actions Ravi should take to resolve the problem. **[3]**
(b) Evaluate the actions the seller could take to keep Ravi as a customer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** B. **[1]**

**2.** B. **[1]**

**3.** A. **[1]**

**4.** To make commercial processes eco-friendly and environmentally responsible **[1]** for future
generations **[1]**.

**5.** (a) Any two explained: switch to electric vans, lowering emissions **[2]**; plan routes to reduce
distance and fuel **[2]**; fill vans fully so fewer journeys are needed **[2]**; group deliveries by area
or time slot **[2]**. **[max 4]**
(b) **[6]** For stopping: reduces plastic waste and pollution; meets customer demand for eco-friendly
shopping; may reduce packaging costs if loose fruit is sold. Against: plastic protects fruit and extends
shelf life, so removing it may increase food waste; alternatives such as paper or compostable packaging
can cost more; some customers prefer pre-packed goods. Conclusion: reduce plastic where fruit is robust and
switch to alternative packaging for delicate items.

**6.** (a) Free repairs extend the life of phones, so fewer are thrown away and fewer resources are needed
for new ones **[2]**; a resale marketplace lets used phones be bought by new owners instead of being
discarded **[2]**.
(b) Any one explained: fewer new phone sales in the short term **[2]**; cost of repair staff and running
the platform **[2]**.

**7.** Measuring and reducing the amount of water used **[1]**; treating waste water before returning it to the
river **[1]**; working with the local community so that the water supply is shared fairly and protected
**[1]**.

**8.** Moral principles about right and wrong **[1]** that guide how a business behaves, beyond what the law
requires **[1]**.

**9.** (a) Any two explained: improved reputation with ethical shoppers **[2]**; customers willing to pay a
higher price **[2]**; secure, long-term supply from producers **[2]**. **[max 4]**
(b) Higher buying costs may mean higher prices, so price-sensitive customers buy elsewhere **[2]**.

**10.** (a) Harmful disposal of products. **[1]**
(b) Any two explained: damage to reputation if the practice is exposed **[2]**; consumers boycott the
brand, reducing sales **[2]**; legal action or fines **[2]**; difficulty attracting investors or staff
**[2]**. **[max 4]**

**11.** Any two explained: sellers know more about products than buyers **[2]**; goods may be unsafe or
faulty **[2]**; consumers may be misled by adverts or prices **[2]**; individuals have less power than large
firms in a dispute **[2]**. **[max 4]**

**12.** (a) Any three: keep the receipt or order confirmation **[1]**; contact the seller promptly with
photos of the fault **[1]**; ask for a repair, replacement or refund **[1]**; escalate to a manager or
consumer protection organisation if not resolved **[1]**. **[max 3]**
(b) **[6]** Refund: quick and satisfies Ravi, but the sale is lost. Replacement: keeps the sale and shows
quality, but costs delivery and stock. Repair: cheaper and sustainable, but slower and Ravi may be
unhappy waiting. Goodwill, such as a voucher or apology: encourages repeat purchase at low cost.
Investigating the cause prevents future complaints. Conclusion: a fast replacement plus an apology is
likely to keep Ravi as a customer.

## A note on exam technique for this topic

Use the business in the question. A generic answer about "being green" earns little; an answer that
names Verde Foods' vans and fruit packaging, and weighs cost against benefit, reaches the higher marks.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many sustainability actions does 0715 list?** Nine, including sustainable materials and logistics,
reducing packaging, eco-friendly products, recycling, reducing returns, digital resale, free repairs and
water stewardship.

**Which ethical issues are named in the syllabus?** Misleading advertising and harmful disposal of products,
together with the role of fair trade.

**Do I need to know specific consumer protection laws?** No. The syllabus asks for the reasons for consumer
protection and the actions buyers and sellers take to resolve complaints.
