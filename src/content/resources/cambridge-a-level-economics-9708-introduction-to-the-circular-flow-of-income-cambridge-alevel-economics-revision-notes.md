These notes condense section **4.2 Introduction to the circular flow of income** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Economics 9708 syllabus (examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028), AS content in Topic 4. For explanations and worked examples, use the [circular flow study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-economics-9708-introduction-to-the-circular-flow-of-income-cambridge-alevel-economics/); for exam-style questions, use the [circular flow practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-economics-9708-introduction-to-the-circular-flow-of-income-cambridge-alevel-economics-practice/).

**Not required at AS:** the multiplier and marginal or average propensities. Explain the direction of change in national income, not its size.

## Sectors and flows (4.2.1)

| Between | Real flow | Money flow |
|---|---|---|
| Households -> firms | Factor services (labour, land, capital, enterprise) | Consumer spending |
| Firms -> households | Goods and services | Factor incomes (wages, rent, interest, profit) |
| Households and firms -> government | | Taxes |
| Government -> firms and households | Public services | Government spending on goods, services and employees |
| Domestic economy -> international economy | Exports of goods and services | Spending on imports |
| International economy -> domestic economy | Imports of goods and services | Spending on exports |

- **Closed economy:** no international trade; sectors are households, firms and government.
- **Open economy:** adds the international economy, through exports and imports.

## Injections and leakages (4.2.2)

| Leakage | Injection |
|---|---|
| Saving (S): income not spent | Investment (I): firms' spending on capital goods |
| Taxation (T): income paid to government | Government spending (G) on goods and services |
| Imports (M): spending on foreign output | Exports (X): foreign spending on domestic output |

- **Leakage:** income not passed on as spending on domestic output.
- **Injection:** spending on domestic output that does not come from households' current income.
- **Neither:** consumer spending on domestic goods, wages paid by firms, transfer payments (until spent).

## Equilibrium and disequilibrium (4.2.3)

```
Equilibrium:   S + T + M = I + G + X   (open economy)
               S + T     = I + G       (closed, with government)
               S         = I           (two sectors)
```

- **Equilibrium:** no tendency for national income to change; only the **totals** must be equal.
- **Injections > leakages:** spending > income paid out -> stocks fall -> firms raise output and employment -> national income **rises** -> leakages rise until equal.
- **Leakages > injections:** spending < income paid out -> stocks rise -> firms cut output and employment -> national income **falls** -> leakages fall until equal.
- Equilibrium can occur with high unemployment: it is not a judgement of welfare.

**Mini example (invented, billion cu).** S 45, T 80, M 65 (leakages 190); I 50, G 85, X 70 (injections 205). Injections exceed leakages by 15, so national income rises.

## Tracing events

- **Export boom:** X rises -> injections > leakages -> firms raise output -> income rises -> S, T and M rise -> new, higher equilibrium.
- **Tax rise:** T rises -> leakages > injections -> spending falls short -> output and income fall -> leakages fall back -> lower equilibrium.
- **Higher imports replacing domestic goods:** M rises -> income leaves the domestic flow -> national income falls.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Circular flow of income | Model of the flows of income and spending between sectors of the economy |
| Closed economy | An economy with no international trade |
| Open economy | An economy that trades with the rest of the world |
| Leakage (withdrawal) | Income removed from the flow of spending on domestic output |
| Injection | Spending added to the flow that does not come from households' current income |
| Transfer payment | Payment with no good or service in return, such as a pension |
| Equilibrium national income | Level of income at which injections equal leakages |
| Disequilibrium | Injections and leakages differ, so national income is changing |

## Exam traps

- Calling a pension or unemployment benefit an injection.
- Treating consumer spending on domestic output as an injection.
- Requiring S = I, T = G and X = M separately.
- Calculating a multiplier when only the direction is asked.
- Saying equilibrium means full employment.
- Getting the direction of the money flow for imports wrong: money leaves the country when imports are bought.

## Self-test

1. Name the leakages in an open economy.
2. Name the injections in an open economy.
3. What is the difference between a closed and an open economy?
4. A household pays for a holiday abroad. Injection or leakage?
5. A foreign student pays tuition fees to a domestic university. Injection or leakage?
6. Why is a state pension not an injection in itself?
7. State the equilibrium condition in a closed economy with government.
8. S = 40, T = 70, M = 50, I = 55, G = 60, X = 40. What happens to national income?
9. In Question 8, what single change in G would bring equilibrium?
10. Explain how firms' stocks signal disequilibrium.
11. Can an economy be in equilibrium with a budget deficit?
12. Why does national income stop rising after an injection increases?

**Answers**

1. Saving, taxation and imports.
2. Investment, government spending and exports.
3. A closed economy does not trade with other countries; an open economy has exports and imports.
4. Leakage: spending on imports (services bought from abroad).
5. Injection: spending by non-residents on domestic output, an export of a service.
6. It is a transfer payment: no output is bought; it only adds to spending if the pensioner spends it, as consumption.
7. S + T = I + G.
8. Leakages = 160; injections = 155; leakages exceed injections, so national income falls.
9. A rise in G of 5 (to 65).
10. If injections exceed leakages, stocks fall as spending outruns output; if leakages exceed injections, unsold stocks build up. Firms respond by changing output.
11. Yes: G can exceed T if the other pairs make total injections equal total leakages.
12. As income rises, saving, taxes and imports rise, until total leakages equal the higher injections.
