Quick recall for section 3.5 Accounts analysis of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1), Issue 2, February 2026, points 3.5.1 to 3.5.3. A full worked set of ratios across two years and against a rival is in the [accounts analysis study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-accounts-analysis/); questions are in the [accounts analysis practice set](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-accounts-analysis-practice/). Figures are invented, in dollars.

## 1. The specification list

- **3.5.1 Ratios:** gross profit margin, operating profit margin, markup, return on capital employed (ROCE), current ratio, acid test ratio. Formulae are in Appendix 4 and are provided in the examination.
- **3.5.2 Liquidity:** the concept and importance of liquidity; comparisons with previous years and/or with other business organisations.
- **3.5.3 Use of financial documents:** to assess the performance of the business and to inform decision making.

## 2. Formulae and answer forms

| Ratio | Formula | Write as |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin | gross profit ÷ revenue × 100 | % |
| Operating profit margin | operating profit ÷ revenue × 100 | % |
| Markup | profit per item ÷ cost per item × 100 | % |
| ROCE | operating profit ÷ capital employed × 100 | % |
| Current ratio | current assets ÷ current liabilities | x : 1 |
| Acid test ratio | (current assets − inventory) ÷ current liabilities | x : 1 |

Supporting lines: gross profit = revenue − cost of sales; operating profit = gross profit − other operating expenses.

**Memory hook:** the three profit-on-revenue or profit-on-capital ratios are percentages; the two "can we pay?" ratios are written x : 1; markup is the only one based on cost.

## 3. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Profitability | How well a business turns revenue or capital into profit |
| Liquidity | Ability to pay short-term debts on time from cash or assets quickly turned into cash |
| Capital employed | Long-term finance in the business: equity + non-current liabilities |
| Inventory | Stock of materials and goods; excluded from the acid test because it must be sold first |
| Percentage point | The unit for a change between two percentages (40% to 42% is a rise of 2 percentage points) |

## 4. Worked reminder: one business, all six

Revenue 155,000; gross profit 62,000; operating profit 15,500; capital employed 124,000; current assets 46,000 (inventory 29,000); current liabilities 23,000.

- Gross profit margin = 62,000 ÷ 155,000 × 100 = **40.0%**
- Operating profit margin = 15,500 ÷ 155,000 × 100 = **10.0%**
- ROCE = 15,500 ÷ 124,000 × 100 = **12.5%**
- Current ratio = 46,000 ÷ 23,000 = **2.0 : 1**
- Acid test = (46,000 − 29,000) ÷ 23,000 = 17,000 ÷ 23,000 = **0.74 : 1**
- Markup on an item costing 19.50 and selling for 26.00: profit per item 6.50; markup = 6.50 ÷ 19.50 × 100 = **33.3%** (margin on the price would be 6.50 ÷ 26.00 × 100 = 25.0%)

Reading it: the current ratio looks comfortable, but more than half of current assets are inventory, so the acid test is below 1 : 1.

## 5. Rearrangements

- Gross profit = gross profit margin × revenue ÷ 100.
- Cost of sales = revenue − gross profit.
- Selling price = cost × (1 + markup ÷ 100).
- Operating profit needed for a target ROCE = target % × capital employed ÷ 100.

## 6. What changes usually mean

| Change | Likely causes | Possible response |
|---|---|---|
| Gross margin falls | Supplier prices up; selling prices cut; more waste | Renegotiate; review prices; reduce waste |
| Operating margin falls but gross margin steady | Running costs up (wages, rent, marketing) | Control expenses |
| ROCE falls | Profit down, or new capital not yet earning | Delay further investment; improve use of assets |
| Current ratio steady, acid test falls | Inventory rising | Order less; sell slow stock |
| Both liquidity ratios fall | Cash down or short-term debts up | Overdraft; chase receivables; delay spending |

## 7. Fair comparisons

- **With previous years:** shows the trend; check for one-off events.
- **With other businesses:** choose a rival of similar type and size, for the same period.
- A very high liquidity ratio can mean idle cash or excess stock.
- Ratios show *what* changed, not always *why*; use the statements to find the cause.

## 8. Exam traps

- Margin as a ratio, or a liquidity ratio as a percentage.
- Dividing by revenue for ROCE.
- Taking away all current assets in the acid test, rather than inventory alone.
- Using the selling price as the base for markup.
- Calling a fall from 12% to 10% a "2% fall" when you mean 2 percentage points.
- Judging a ratio good or bad with nothing to compare it to.
- Forgetting the formulae are given: marks come from correct substitution and interpretation.

## 9. Answer frames

- **Calculate:** write the formula, substitute, answer with the right unit.
- **Analyse** a ratio change: the change → its cause from the statements → its consequence for the business.
- **Evaluate** performance: profitability evidence, liquidity evidence, a comparison, judgement with "it depends on".

## Quick self-test

1. Give the formula for ROCE.
2. Which ratio excludes inventory?
3. Revenue is 80,000 and gross profit is 30,000. Calculate the gross profit margin.
4. Operating profit is 9,000 and revenue is 120,000. Calculate the operating profit margin.
5. An item costs 40 and sells for 52. Calculate the markup.
6. Current assets are 36,000 and current liabilities 24,000. Calculate the current ratio.
7. Using question 6, inventory is 18,000. Calculate the acid test ratio.
8. Define liquidity.
9. Gross margin is steady but operating margin has fallen. Where should managers look?
10. Why might a very high current ratio be a problem?
11. A business has revenue of 300,000 and a gross margin of 35%. Calculate its cost of sales.
12. Give one limitation of using ratios to judge performance.

### Answers

1. Operating profit ÷ capital employed × 100.
2. The acid test ratio.
3. 30,000 ÷ 80,000 × 100 = 37.5%.
4. 9,000 ÷ 120,000 × 100 = 7.5%.
5. Profit per item 12; 12 ÷ 40 × 100 = 30%.
6. 36,000 ÷ 24,000 = 1.5 : 1.
7. (36,000 − 18,000) ÷ 24,000 = 0.75 : 1.
8. The ability of a business to pay its short-term debts as they fall due.
9. At other operating expenses, such as wages, rent or marketing.
10. Cash or inventory may be sitting idle instead of being used to grow the business.
11. Gross profit = 0.35 × 300,000 = 105,000; cost of sales = 300,000 − 105,000 = 195,000.
12. Any one of: based on past data; does not explain causes; ignores non-financial factors; one year may be distorted; rivals may not be comparable.
