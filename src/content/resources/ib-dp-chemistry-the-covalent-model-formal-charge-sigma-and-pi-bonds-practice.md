## Questions

**1.** In the nitrite ion, NO₂⁻, nitrogen is bonded to two oxygen atoms.

**(a)** Draw the two resonance structures of NO₂⁻, showing all lone pairs. **[2]**
**(b)** State and explain how the lengths of the two N–O bonds compare. **[2]**

**2.** Use these values. Cyclohexene + H₂ → cyclohexane, ΔH = −121 kJ mol⁻¹. Benzene + 3H₂ → cyclohexane, ΔH = −210 kJ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the resonance energy of benzene from these data. **[2]**
**(b)** Give one piece of physical evidence, other than enthalpy data, and one piece of chemical evidence for a delocalized structure in benzene. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why benzene tends to undergo substitution rather than addition reactions. **[2]**

**3.** For each species, deduce the number of electron domains round the central atom, the electron domain geometry and the molecular geometry.

**(a)** BrF₃ **[3]**
**(b)** I₃⁻ (the central iodine is bonded to the other two) **[3]**

**4.** Dinitrogen monoxide, N₂O, has the atom order N–N–O.

**(a)** Calculate the formal charge on each atom in the Lewis formulas N=N=O and N≡N–O. **[2]**
**(b)** Deduce which of these two Lewis formulas is preferred, giving a reason. **[1]**
**(c)** Use formal charge to explain why the atom order N=O=N is not favoured. **[2]**

**5.** Two Lewis formulas for the phosphate ion, PO₄³⁻, have either four P–O single bonds or one P=O and three P–O bonds.

**(a)** Calculate the formal charge on the phosphorus atom in each Lewis formula. **[2]**
**(b)** Deduce which Lewis formula is preferred. **[1]**
**(c)** State the oxidation state of phosphorus in PO₄³⁻ and explain why it differs from its formal charge in the preferred formula. **[2]**

**6.**

**(a)** State the number of σ bonds and π bonds in propenoic acid, CH₂=CHCOOH. **[2]**
**(b)** State the number of σ bonds and π bonds in cyanogen, N≡C–C≡N. **[2]**
**(c)** State the hybridization of each carbon atom in cyanogen and deduce the shape of the molecule. **[2]**

**7.** Aminoethanenitrile has the structure H₂N–CH₂–C≡N.

**(a)** State the hybridization of the amine nitrogen atom, the CH₂ carbon atom and the nitrile carbon atom. **[3]**
**(b)** State the C–C≡N bond angle. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain, in terms of orbital overlap, how the C≡N bond forms. **[3]**

**8.**

**(a)** The NO₂⁺ ion is linear. Draw its Lewis formula, showing the formal charge on each atom, and deduce the hybridization of nitrogen. **[3]**
**(b)** An atom in a molecule is sp² hybridized and carries one lone pair. State its electron domain geometry and the molecular geometry around it. **[2]**

**9.** Ozone, O₃, and oxygen, O₂, are both split by ultraviolet light. Use these values: bond enthalpy of O=O in O₂ = 498 kJ mol⁻¹; speed of light = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹; Planck constant = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s; Avogadro constant = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Draw the two resonance structures of O₃ and calculate the formal charge on each atom in one of them. **[3]**
**(b)** State the hybridization of the central oxygen atom and predict the O–O–O bond angle. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the longest wavelength of light, in nm, that can break the bond in one O₂ molecule. **[3]**
**(d)** Without further calculation, explain why ozone can be broken apart by light of longer wavelength than oxygen. **[2]**

**10.** In XeO₂F₂, xenon is bonded to two O atoms and two F atoms.

**(a)** Calculate the total number of valence electrons in XeO₂F₂. **[1]**
**(b)** Lewis formula A has two Xe–O single bonds; B has two Xe=O double bonds. Both have two Xe–F bonds and one lone pair on Xe. Calculate the formal charges on Xe and each O in A and in B. **[3]**
**(c)** Deduce which Lewis formula is preferred. **[1]**
**(d)** Deduce the number of electron domains round xenon, the electron domain geometry and the molecular geometry. **[3]**
**(e)** State the number of σ bonds and π bonds in Lewis formula B. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 18 electrons: N with one lone pair, one N=O (two lone pairs on O) and one N–O (three lone pairs on O), in brackets with the charge [1]. Second structure with the double bond to the other O, joined by ↔ [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** **Equal in length** [1]: the π electrons are delocalized over both positions, so each bond is between single and double [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Resonance structures with missing lone pairs or no charge lose credit; count to 18 before you finish.

**2. (a)** Expected for three isolated C=C bonds = 3 × (−121) = −363 kJ mol⁻¹ [1]. Resonance energy = −210 − (−363) = +153, so benzene is **153 kJ mol⁻¹** more stable than the Kekulé structure [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Physical: all six C–C bonds are equal in length, between single and double [1]. Chemical: only one 1,2-disubstituted isomer exists (or no ready decolourization of bromine water) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Addition would break up the delocalized π system and lose the resonance energy [1]; substitution keeps the ring intact, so it is favoured [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), state the answer as a stabilization and keep the sign consistent with your wording; a bare "−153" alongside "more stable" contradicts itself.

**3. (a)** Br: 7 valence electrons; 3 in bonds, leaving 2 lone pairs, so **5 domains** [1]. Electron domain geometry **trigonal bipyramidal** [1]. Both lone pairs are equatorial, so the molecule is **T-shaped** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Central I: 7 + 1 = 8 electrons; 2 in bonds, leaving 3 lone pairs, so **5 domains** [1]. Electron domain geometry **trigonal bipyramidal** [1]. All three lone pairs are equatorial, so the ion is **linear** (180°) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Trigonal bipyramidal" alone gives only the electron domain geometry; the molecular geometry must describe the atoms only.

**4. (a)** N=N=O: terminal N −1, central N +1, O 0 [1]. N≡N–O: terminal N 0, central N +1, O −1 [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** **N≡N–O**: the charges are equally small, but the negative one sits on oxygen, the more electronegative atom [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** In N=O=N each N is −1 and O is **+2** [1]. The charges are larger, and the most electronegative atom is positive, so it is not favoured [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the formal charge on every atom; a conclusion with no charges to support it is hard to credit.

**5. (a)** Four single bonds: P = 5 − (0 + 4) = **+1** [1]. One P=O: P = 5 − (0 + 5) = **0** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The formula with **one P=O double bond**, as its charges are closer to zero [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** **+5** [1]. Oxidation state gives all bonding electrons to the more electronegative O; formal charge assumes equal sharing [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Check that the formal charges in your chosen structure add to −3; a total that does not match the ion's charge shows a counting error.

**6. (a)** Bonds: three C–H, C=C, C–C, C=O, C–O and O–H. **8 σ** [1] and **2 π** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Two C≡N and one C–C: **3 σ** [1] and **4 π** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Each carbon has two electron domains: **sp** [1]. Both carbons are linear with no lone pairs, so the molecule is **linear** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), the O–H bond is easily missed; list every bond before totalling.

**7. (a)** Amine N (three bonds and one lone pair): **sp³** [1]. CH₂ carbon (four bonds): **sp³** [1]. Nitrile carbon (two domains): **sp** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** **180°** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Head-on overlap of an sp orbital on C with an sp orbital on N gives one **σ bond**, with density along the axis [1]. Each atom keeps two unhybridized p orbitals at right angles to the axis [1]. These overlap sideways in pairs to give **two π bonds**, with density on opposite sides of the axis [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Three overlapping orbitals" earns little; name which orbitals form the σ bond and which form the π bonds.

**8. (a)** [O=N=O]⁺ with two lone pairs on each O and none on N [1]; formal charges: N +1, each O 0 [1]. N has two electron domains, so it is **sp** hybridized [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** **Trigonal planar** electron domain geometry [1]; **bent (V-shaped)**, slightly below 120° [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), the formal charges must add up to +1; checking this catches a missing or extra lone pair.

**9. (a)** O=O–O ↔ O–O=O, with one lone pair on the central O, two on the double-bonded O and three on the single-bonded O [1]. Central O: 6 − (2 + 3) = **+1**; double-bonded O: 6 − (4 + 2) = **0** [1]; single-bonded O: 6 − (6 + 1) = **−1** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Central O has three domains (two bonding, one lone pair): **sp²** [1]; bond angle slightly **less than 120°** because the lone pair repels more strongly [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Energy per bond = 498 000 ÷ (6.02 × 10²³) = 8.27 × 10⁻¹⁹ J [1]. λ = hc ÷ E = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸) ÷ (8.27 × 10⁻¹⁹) [1] = 2.40 × 10⁻⁷ m = **240 nm** [1]. **[3]**
**(d)** Delocalization makes each O–O bond in ozone between single and double, so weaker than O=O [1]. Less energy is needed, so lower-frequency, longer-wavelength photons can break it [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c), converting kJ to J and per mole to per bond are separate steps; show each on its own line so the working can be followed.

**10. (a)** 8 + (2 × 6) + (2 × 7) = **34** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** A: Xe = 8 − (2 + 4) = **+2** [1]; each O = 6 − (6 + 1) = **−1** [1]. B: Xe = 8 − (2 + 6) = **0** and each O = 6 − (4 + 2) = **0** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** **B**, because every atom has a formal charge of zero [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Four bonding domains plus one lone pair = **5 domains** [1]; electron domain geometry **trigonal bipyramidal** [1]; with the lone pair equatorial the molecule is **seesaw** [1]. **[3]**
**(e)** **4 σ** bonds [1] and **2 π** bonds [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In (d), each Xe=O double bond is one domain; counting it as two gives seven domains and a wrong geometry.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving lone pairs, brackets or the charge off resonance structures of ions.
- Confusing electron domain geometry with molecular geometry.
- Placing lone pairs axially in a trigonal bipyramid.
- Using all the bonding electrons, instead of half, in the formal charge formula.
- Choosing a structure with a negative formal charge on the less electronegative atom.
- Counting two or three σ bonds in a multiple bond.
- Deducing hybridization from attached atoms instead of electron domains.
