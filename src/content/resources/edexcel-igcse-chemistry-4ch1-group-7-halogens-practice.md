Questions marked **(Paper 2 only)** test point 2.8C. ## Section A: short questions

**1.** Give the colour and physical state of chlorine at room temperature. **[2]**

**2.** Give the formula of a bromine molecule. **[1]**

**3.** State the colour of the vapour seen when iodine crystals are warmed. **[1]**

**4.** State the trend in reactivity of the halogens down the group. **[1]**

**5.** Name the halogen formed when chlorine water is added to sodium bromide solution and give the colour of the solution. **[2]**

**6.** State what is meant by a halide. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** The table shows the boiling points of three halogens.

| Halogen | Mr | Boiling point / °C |
|---|---|---|
| Chlorine | 71 | −34 |
| Bromine | 160 | 59 |
| Iodine | 254 | 184 |

**(a)** Explain the trend in boiling point. **[3]**
**(b)** Fluorine (Mr 38) is above chlorine. Predict whether fluorine has a boiling point above or below −34 °C and state its physical state at room temperature. **[2]**

**8.** A student adds solutions of halogens to solutions of potassium halides.

| | Potassium chloride | Potassium bromide | Potassium iodide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chlorine water | A | Turns orange | Turns brown |
| Bromine water | No change | B | C |
| Iodine solution | No change | No change | D |

**(a)** State the result expected at C. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why A, B and D were not tested. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how the results show that bromine is less reactive than chlorine but more reactive than iodine. **[2]**
**(d)** Write a balanced equation, with state symbols, for the reaction that turns the potassium bromide solution orange. **[2]**

**9.** Bromine water is added to potassium iodide solution.

**(a)** Write the ionic equation for the reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the spectator ion. **[1]**
**(c)** State which particle loses electrons. **[1]**

**10.** A colourless solution contains one potassium halide. Two tests are done.

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Add chlorine water | Solution turns brown |
| Add bromine water | Solution turns brown |

**(a)** Identify the halide. Give a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** Predict the result if iodine solution were added to the same halide solution. **[1]**

**11.** Astatine is below iodine in Group 7.

**(a)** Predict the colour and state of astatine at room temperature. **[2]**
**(b)** Predict whether astatine reacts with sodium bromide solution. Explain your answer. **[2]**
**(c)** Iodine reacts with sodium astatide, NaAt. Write the balanced equation. **[1]**

**12.** **(Paper 2 only)** Chlorine has the electronic configuration 2,8,7. Fluorine has the configuration 2,7.

**(a)** State how a halogen atom reacts to form a halide ion. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why fluorine is more reactive than chlorine. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Pale yellow-green (green) [1]; gas [1].

**2.** Br₂ [1].

**3.** Purple [1].

**4.** Reactivity decreases down the group [1].

**5.** Bromine [1]; orange [1].

**6.** A compound that contains a halide ion (Cl⁻, Br⁻ or I⁻), e.g. potassium chloride [1].

**7. (a)** Boiling point increases as Mr increases [1]. Larger molecules have stronger intermolecular forces [1], so more energy is needed to overcome them [1].
**(b)** Below −34 °C [1]; gas [1].

**8. (a)** Turns brown (iodine formed) [1].
**(b)** Each halogen would be added to its own halide, so no reaction is possible / nothing would change [1].
**(c)** Chlorine displaces bromine from potassium bromide, so bromine is less reactive than chlorine [1]. Bromine displaces iodine from potassium iodide, so bromine is more reactive than iodine [1].
**(d)** Cl₂(aq) + 2KBr(aq) → 2KCl(aq) + Br₂(aq): formulae and balancing [1]; state symbols [1].

**9. (a)** Br₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Br⁻ + I₂: correct species [1]; balanced [1].
**(b)** K⁺ [1].
**(c)** The iodide ion (I⁻) [1].

**10. (a)** Iodide (potassium iodide) [1]: both chlorine and bromine displace it, so it must be the least reactive halide of the three; brown shows iodine formed [1].
**(b)** No change / no reaction [1].

**11. (a)** Black [1]; solid [1].
**(b)** No [1]: astatine is less reactive than bromine, so it cannot displace bromine from bromide [1].
**(c)** I₂ + 2NaAt → 2NaI + At₂ [1].

**12. (a)** It gains one electron [1].
**(b)** Fluorine has fewer occupied shells (two compared with three), so its outer shell is closer to the nucleus [1]. There is less shielding by inner electrons [1]. So the attraction between the nucleus and an incoming electron is stronger [1], and fluorine gains an electron more easily [1].
*Marking note:* An answer about losing electrons scores zero in (b): halogens react by gaining an electron.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "iodine is purple" for the solid.
- Writing Br and I in equations instead of Br₂ and I₂.
- Explaining a displacement result without naming which halogen is more reactive.
- Describing a "no change" result as a reaction because the added reagent is coloured.
- Using "loses electrons" in the Group 7 explanation.
