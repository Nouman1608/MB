The course is untiered and a calculator may be used on both papers. Question 12 tests the bold **B** statement 2.95B and is labelled "Paper 2 only".

## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by homeostasis and give **two** examples of it in the human body. **[3]**

**2.** A cat hears a dog bark and runs up a tree. Identify:

**(a)** the stimulus **[1]**
**(b)** the receptor **[1]**
**(c)** an effector **[1]**

**3.** A bean seedling is laid on its side in a dark cupboard for two days.

**(a)** Describe the direction of growth of the root and of the shoot. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the stimulus and the type of response shown by the root. **[2]**

**4.** A student removes the tips from oat shoots that had been lit from one side. She places each tip on two agar blocks separated by a thin barrier, so that auxin from the lit side and the shaded side collects in different blocks. She measures the auxin in each block in arbitrary units.

| Block | Auxin (arbitrary units) |
|---|---|
| Lit side | 27 |
| Shaded side | 48 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of the total auxin that collected on the shaded side. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the results to explain why an intact shoot bends towards the light. **[4]**

**5.** Give **four** differences between nervous and hormonal communication in humans. **[4]**

**6.** Describe how an impulse passes from one neurone to the next across a synapse. **[4]**

**7.** A person steps barefoot on a sharp stone and lifts the foot before feeling any pain.

**(a)** Describe the pathway of this reflex, from receptor to effector. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why this response is an advantage. **[2]**

**8.** A student reading a phone looks up at a bus approaching in the distance.

**(a)** Name the part of the eye that contains the light receptors and the part that carries impulses to the brain. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how the eye adjusts so that the distant bus is focused on the retina. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how the eye responds when a person walks from a dark room into bright sunlight. **[3]**

**10.** A scientist measures blood flow through the skin of a volunteer's hand at two air temperatures.

| Air temperature | Skin blood flow (arbitrary units) |
|---|---|
| 30 °C | 18.0 |
| 10 °C | 4.5 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in skin blood flow from 30 °C to 10 °C. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the change in blood flow helps keep body temperature constant at 10 °C. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain how sweating cools the body at 30 °C. **[2]**

**11.** A volunteer drinks a glucose solution. Her blood glucose concentration peaks at 8.4 mmol/dm³ at 40 minutes and falls to 5.4 mmol/dm³ at 100 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of fall in blood glucose between 40 and 100 minutes. Give the unit. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the hormone responsible for the fall, state where it is made, and explain how it lowers blood glucose. **[3]**
**(c)** Later she is startled by a loud noise. Name the hormone released and give **two** of its effects. **[3]**

**12.** (Paper 2 only)

**(a)** A man's urine production falls from 150 cm³ per hour to 60 cm³ per hour after a long walk on a hot day. Calculate the percentage change. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the role of ADH in causing this change. **[4]**
**(c)** Name the gland that secretes FSH and LH, and state **one** role of each in the menstrual cycle. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Maintenance of a constant internal environment [1]; body water content [1]; body temperature [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Keeping the body the same" is too vague for the definition mark; the word "internal" (or "inside the body") is expected.

**2. (a)** The sound of the bark [1]
**(b)** Sound receptors in the ear [1]
**(c)** Leg muscles [1]
*Examiner insight:* "The ear" alone may lose the receptor mark if the scheme asks for receptors; and "the legs" is weaker than "leg muscles", because an effector is a muscle or gland.

**3. (a)** Root grows (curves) downwards [1]; shoot grows (curves) upwards [1]
**(b)** Gravity [1]; positive geotropism [1]
*Examiner insight:* The cupboard is dark so that light cannot cause the response; naming light as the stimulus scores nothing, and "geotropism" without "positive" loses the second mark.

**4. (a)** 48 ÷ (48 + 27) × 100 [1] = **64%** [1]
**(b)** More auxin is on the shaded side (48 compared with 27 units) [1]; auxin causes cell elongation [1]; cells on the shaded side elongate more, so that side grows faster [1]; the shoot curves towards the light [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Use the results" means a mark is reserved for quoting the data; an answer with no numbers from the table usually cannot reach full marks.

**5.** Nervous uses electrical impulses, hormonal uses chemicals [1]; nervous travels along neurones, hormones travel in the blood [1]; nervous is faster [1]; nervous effects are short-lived, hormonal effects last longer [1]. Also creditworthy in place of any one of these: nervous acts on a precise area, hormones can affect several organs. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each difference needs both sides stated or clearly implied; "hormones are slow" on its own is often accepted, but listing only features of one system rarely scores four.

**6.** Impulse arrives at the end of the first neurone [1]; neurotransmitter is released into the gap [1]; it diffuses across the gap [1]; it binds to receptors on the next neurone, starting a new impulse [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "The impulse jumps across the gap" scores nothing; the marks are for the chemical neurotransmitter and the process of diffusion.

**7. (a)** Pain (pressure) receptors in the skin of the foot [1]; sensory neurone carries impulses to the spinal cord [1]; relay neurone in the spinal cord [1]; motor neurone to the leg muscle, which contracts [1]
**(b)** The response is fast and automatic [1]; so damage to the foot is reduced [1]
*Examiner insight:* This tests the reflex arc in a new context; keep the same three-neurone sequence and name the spinal cord, because "the brain" as the co-ordinator loses a mark.

**8. (a)** Retina [1]; optic nerve [1]
**(b)** Ciliary muscle relaxes [1]; suspensory ligaments become tight [1]; lens is pulled thinner [1]; light is refracted less, so it focuses on the retina [1]
*Examiner insight:* Accommodation is marked as a sequence; reversing one step (for example "ciliary muscle contracts") usually costs that mark and makes the lens-shape mark inconsistent.

**9.** Circular muscles of the iris contract [1]; radial muscles relax [1]; pupil becomes smaller, so less light enters and the retina is protected [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming "the iris muscles" without saying which set contracts gains little; the mark scheme rewards circular versus radial correctly matched.

**10. (a)** (4.5 − 18.0) ÷ 18.0 × 100 [1] = **−75%** (a 75% decrease) [1]
**(b)** Vasoconstriction: arterioles supplying the skin capillaries narrow [1]; less blood flows near the skin surface [1]; so less heat is lost by radiation [1]
**(c)** Water in sweat evaporates from the skin [1]; evaporation uses heat energy from the body [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b) "capillaries move away from the surface" is a common wrong idea and scores nothing; in (a) dividing by 4.5 instead of 18.0 loses both marks.

**11. (a)** (8.4 − 5.4) ÷ (100 − 40) = 3.0 ÷ 60 [1] = **0.05 mmol/dm³ per minute** [1]
**(b)** Insulin [1]; made in the pancreas [1]; causes the liver and muscles to convert glucose to glycogen (and cells to take up glucose) [1]
**(c)** Adrenaline [1]; increases heart rate [1]; raises blood glucose by causing glycogen breakdown in the liver [1]. Also accept: increases breathing rate, or widens the pupils (any two effects).
*Examiner insight:* In (a) the unit is part of the answer mark; in (b) "glucagon" for glycogen loses the mark even if the rest is right.

**12. (a)** (60 − 150) ÷ 150 × 100 [1] = **−60%** (a 60% decrease) [1]
**(b)** Water lost in sweat makes the blood more concentrated [1]; the pituitary gland releases more ADH [1]; ADH makes the collecting ducts more permeable to water [1]; more water is reabsorbed into the blood, so less, more concentrated urine is made [1]
**(c)** Pituitary gland [1]; FSH causes an egg to mature (and stimulates oestrogen production) [1]; LH triggers ovulation [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (b), "ADH reabsorbs water" is wrong: the kidney reabsorbs water and ADH only changes the permeability of the collecting duct, so name the collecting duct to score.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out "internal" from the homeostasis definition.
- Naming an organ ("the ear", "the legs") when the question asks for the receptor or effector.
- Not quoting numbers when a question says "use the results".
- Saying auxin causes cell division instead of cell elongation.
- Describing the impulse "jumping" across a synapse.
- Putting the reflex through the brain instead of the spinal cord.
- Reversing the ciliary muscle action for near and distant objects.
- Dividing by the final value in a percentage change.
- Writing "glucagon" instead of glycogen.
- Saying ADH itself reabsorbs water.
