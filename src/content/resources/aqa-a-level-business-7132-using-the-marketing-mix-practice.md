These questions cover section 3.3.4 Making marketing decisions: using the marketing mix of the AQA A-level Business (7132) specification. All businesses and figures are invented. Revise first with the [marketing mix study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-using-the-marketing-mix/) and the [marketing mix revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-using-the-marketing-mix-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** A product has a 12% share of its market. The largest rival has a 24% share. What is the product's relative market share? **[1]**

A: 0.5

B: 2.0

C: 12

D: 24

**2.** Which **one** of the following describes price skimming? **[1]**

A: A low launch price to win market share quickly

B: Matching rivals' prices exactly

C: A high launch price that is lowered over time

D: Cutting price when sales fall

**3.** Define the term "extension strategy". **[2]**

**4.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of building a strong brand. **[3]**

**5.** Ashby Foods classes a market as high growth if growth is above 10% a year and relative share as high if it is above 1.0.

| Product | Market growth | Ashby's share | Largest rival's share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Granola | 16% | 20% | 10% |
| Porridge | 3% | 30% | 15% |
| Rice cakes | 2% | 4% | 16% |

**(a)** Calculate the relative market share of each product. **[3]**

**(b)** Classify each product on the Boston Matrix. **[3]**

**6.** Tarn Tech is launching a tablet. Under skimming it would sell 3,000 units at £400 in the first year. Under penetration it would sell 9,000 units at £180.

**(a)** Calculate the first-year revenue under each strategy. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain **one** reason why Tarn Tech might still choose skimming. **[2]**

**7.** Explain **one** way in which the process of ordering and paying affects a customer's experience in a restaurant. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**8.** Brightcrumb is a chocolate biscuit brand in the maturity stage of its life cycle. Analyse **two** extension strategies it could use. **[6]**

**9.** Lorne Threads is a small online fashion retailer with a limited budget. Assess whether it should rely mainly on social media and viral marketing to promote its products. **[9]**

**10.** Analyse how the marketing mix of a dental practice (a service) differs from the mix of a business selling bicycle helmets. **[6]**

**11.** Explain **two** risks to a business of developing new products. **[4]**

**12.** Evaluate the importance of an integrated marketing mix to a luxury hotel. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** **A** [1]. 12 ÷ 24 = 0.5. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. Skimming sets a high price at launch for early adopters and lowers it later. **[1]**

**3.** An action taken to prolong the life of a product [1] before it enters decline, for example by redesigning it, finding new uses or entering new markets [1]. **[2]**

**4.** A strong brand makes the product easily recognised and trusted [1], so customers are more likely to buy it again, which builds loyalty [1] and may allow the business to charge a higher price or launch new products under the same name more cheaply [1]. **[3]**

**5. (a)** Granola: 20 ÷ 10 = **2.0** [1]. Porridge: 30 ÷ 15 = **2.0** [1]. Rice cakes: 4 ÷ 16 = **0.25** [1].

**(b)** Granola: star (high growth, high relative share) [1]. Porridge: cash cow (low growth, high relative share) [1]. Rice cakes: dog (low growth, low relative share) [1]. **[6]**

**6. (a)** Skimming: 3,000 × £400 = **£1,200,000** [1]. Penetration: 9,000 × £180 = **£1,620,000** [1].

**(b)** Skimming earns a high margin from early adopters who value being first [1], and a high price signals quality, with the chance to lower the price later once costs fall or rivals appear; penetration's extra revenue may come with low profit per unit [1]. **[4]**

**7.** A slow or confusing process, such as waiting a long time to order or to pay the bill, annoys customers and may stop them returning [1]. A quick, easy process, such as ordering at the table by app, improves the experience and can allow more customers to be served [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Strategy 1: new variants or packaging [1]. Adding a new flavour or a redesigned pack gives customers a reason to buy again [1], and may attract new buyers, raising sales in a mature market [1]. Strategy 2: new markets or channels [1]. Selling to export markets or through online supermarkets reaches people not yet buying [1], so sales can rise without the product itself changing, though extra distribution costs must be covered [1]. Credit also: new uses (as an ingredient), promotion, price changes. **[6]**

**9.** For: social media is low cost and can be targeted by age and interest [1]. It gives direct contact with customers and instant feedback on styles [1]. A successful viral post can reach very large audiences for almost no spend [1]. Fashion customers often follow trends online [1]. Against: viral success cannot be planned or controlled [1]; negative comments can spread as fast as positive ones [1]. Content has to be produced constantly, which takes staff time [1]. Social media alone may not build the trust or wide recognition that other promotion could [1]. Judgement: social media should be the core of the promotion for a small online business with little money, but it should be supported by other low-cost methods such as email to existing customers, and results measured [1]. **[9]**

**10.** The dentist's mix places greater weight on people, process and physical environment [1]. Patients judge the dentist by the skill and manner of staff [1], by how easily appointments are booked [1] and by the clean, calm surroundings [1]. The helmet seller's mix puts more weight on the physical product (safety features, design, packaging) and on distribution through retailers [1]. Price and promotion differ too: helmets are advertised and compared on price; dental services rely on reputation and recommendation [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Risk 1: high cost and failure [1]. Research, design, testing and launch cost a lot, and many new products fail to sell enough to cover them [1]. Risk 2: slow or poorly timed [1]. Development may take so long that tastes change or a rival launches first, and the business may also distract management from existing products [1]. **[4]**

**12.** For: a luxury hotel sells an overall image, so all elements must send the same message [1]. A high price, exclusive location and elegant design suggest quality [1]. Well-trained, attentive staff (people) are part of what guests pay for [1]. A smooth check-in (process) and an excellent room (physical environment) deliver the promise [1]. Promotion that shows luxury in magazines and social media attracts the target market [1]. Any weak element, such as rude staff or a slow check-in, damages guests' view of the whole experience [1]. Against: the product and the staff may matter more than the way all elements fit together [1]. Some elements, such as price, are strongly influenced by competitors and demand rather than the hotel's choice [1]. A rigid mix can fail to adapt, for example to a downturn when guests want offers [1]. Factors: how far the hotel's guests judge by experience, and the strength of competition [1]. Judgement: integration is critical because a luxury position depends on consistency [1], but it must be reviewed as markets change, with the mix flexed without damaging the position [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* Tie each element of the mix to the business in the case and judge which matters most for its target market.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing the 7Ps without applying them to the business.
- Using absolute market share instead of relative market share.
- Using "penetration" or "skimming" without referring to a launch price.
- Treating extension strategies as new products.
- Ignoring people, process and physical environment in service businesses.
- Failing to say how the elements of the mix fit together.
