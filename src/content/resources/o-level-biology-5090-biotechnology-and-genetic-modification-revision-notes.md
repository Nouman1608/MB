## Key definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Biotechnology | Using living organisms, or their enzymes, to make useful products |
| Genetic modification | Changing the genetic material of an organism by removing, changing or inserting individual genes |
| Plasmid | A small, separate circle of DNA in a bacterial cell |
| Fermenter | A large, closed vessel in which microorganisms are grown in controlled conditions to make a product on a large scale |
| Lactose | The sugar in milk |
| Pectin | A substance in plant cell walls that helps hold cells together |

## 18.1.1 Yeast: bread and ethanol

Equation to know: `glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide` (anaerobic respiration in yeast)

| | Bread | Ethanol |
|---|---|---|
| Useful product | Carbon dioxide | Ethanol |
| What it does | Bubbles trapped in the dough make it rise | Collected for drinks or fuel |
| What happens to the other product | Most of the ethanol evaporates during baking | Carbon dioxide is let out |
| Key condition | Warm, so yeast enzymes work fast | No oxygen, so respiration is anaerobic |

**Method in steps: explaining why dough rises (or does not)**

1. Yeast respires anaerobically, using sugar in the dough.
2. Carbon dioxide is released.
3. The gas is trapped as bubbles, so the dough rises.
4. If the dough rises slowly, check the temperature: too cold and the yeast enzymes work slowly; too hot and they are denatured, so little or no carbon dioxide is made.

## 18.1.2 and 18.1.3 Why bacteria are used

Four syllabus reasons. Give the feature **and** the benefit.

1. **Rapid reproduction rate** → large population, lots of product, quickly.
2. **Make complex molecules** → can produce proteins such as human insulin.
3. **No ethical concerns** over their manipulation and growth → no animal-welfare objections.
4. **Plasmids** → a gene can be inserted into a plasmid and taken into the bacterium.

**Method in steps: counting bacteria after repeated division**

1. Convert the total time to minutes.
2. Divide by the time for one division to get the number of divisions, n.
3. Number of cells = starting number × 2ⁿ.

Reminder: a cell that divides every 25 minutes gives 100 ÷ 25 = 4 divisions in 100 minutes, so 1 cell becomes 2⁴ = **16** cells.

## 18.1.4 Fermenter conditions

The syllabus limits you to five. Learn the control and the reason as a pair.

| Condition | Controlled by | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | Water jacket (cooling) + temperature probe | Respiration releases heat; too hot denatures enzymes |
| pH | pH probe; acid or alkali added | Keeps enzymes at their optimum pH |
| Oxygen | Sterile air bubbled in; stirring paddles | Aerobic respiration for growth; stirring mixes oxygen and nutrients |
| Nutrient supply | Added through an inlet | Energy source and raw materials for growth and product |
| Waste products | Gas outlet; drained off | Build-up slows growth and alters pH |

**Method in steps: explaining a change in fermenter conditions**

1. Name the condition that has changed and which way it moved (for example, pH has fallen).
2. Give the cause (waste products such as acids are building up, or the control has failed).
3. Link it to enzymes: away from the optimum, enzymes in the microorganisms work more slowly; too hot and they are denatured.
4. State the effect on growth and on the yield of product.

Reminder: if the oxygen supply fails, the microorganisms cannot respire aerobically, so growth slows and less product is made.

Examples of fermenter products: insulin (from GM bacteria) and penicillin (from the fungus *Penicillium*).

## 18.1.5 Enzymes in industry

| Use | Enzyme(s) | What it does | Benefit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biological washing powder | Proteases, lipases (amylase may be added) | Digest protein and fat stains into small soluble molecules | Stains wash out at lower temperatures, saving energy |
| Fruit juice | Pectinase | Breaks down pectin in plant cell walls | More juice from the same fruit; clearer juice |
| Lactose-free milk | Lactase | lactose → glucose + galactose | People who are lactose intolerant can drink the milk |

**Method in steps: reading washing powder data**

1. Compare biological and non-biological powder at the **same** temperature.
2. Find the temperature where the biological powder works best (its enzymes' optimum).
3. Above that temperature, explain the fall: the enzymes are denatured.
4. Quote values from the table or graph, with units.

Reminder: percentage increase = (change ÷ starting value) × 100. If juice volume rises from 40 cm³ to 50 cm³ with pectinase, the increase is (10 ÷ 40) × 100 = **25%**.

## 18.2 Genetic modification

**Insulin (18.2.2):** the gene that controls the production of human insulin has been inserted into bacterial DNA. The bacteria multiply in fermenters and make human insulin, which is extracted for people with diabetes.

**GM crops (18.2.3):**

| Gene inserted to | Effect |
|---|---|
| (a) Resist herbicides | Field sprayed with herbicide kills weeds but not the crop → less competition → higher yield |
| (b) Resist insect pests | Plant makes a toxin (Bt gene from a bacterium) that kills certain insect larvae → less damage, less insecticide |
| (c) Provide additional vitamins | e.g. Golden Rice makes beta-carotene, converted to vitamin A in the body |

**Advantages and risks (18.2.4), crops and bacteria only:**

| Advantages | Risks |
|---|---|
| Higher yields | Genes spread to wild relatives by pollen (e.g. herbicide-resistant weeds) |
| Less insecticide used | Pests become resistant to the toxin |
| More nutritious crops | Non-pest insects harmed |
| Plentiful, cheaper human insulin | Possible health effects such as allergies; seed costs for farmers |

**Method in steps: a "discuss" question on GM**

1. Name the GM organism and the inserted gene's effect.
2. Give at least two advantages, each with its reason.
3. Give at least two risks, each with its reason.
4. Keep every point to crop plants or bacteria, the only examples the syllabus asks for.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Bread vs ethanol:** both use anaerobic respiration in yeast; bread needs the carbon dioxide, ethanol production needs the ethanol.
- **Herbicide vs insecticide:** herbicides kill weeds (plants); insecticides kill insects. Herbicide resistance protects the crop from the spray, not from insects.
- **Pectin vs cellulose:** pectinase breaks down pectin, not cellulose.
- **Lactose vs lactase:** lactose is the sugar; lactase is the enzyme (enzyme names end in -ase).
- **Denatured vs killed:** enzymes are denatured; microorganisms are killed.
- **Plasmid vs chromosome:** bacteria have a large circular DNA loop plus small plasmids; there is no nucleus.

## Quick self-test

1. State the useful product of yeast in bread making.
2. What happens to most of the ethanol made in dough?
3. Apart from plasmids and ethics, give two reasons why bacteria are useful in biotechnology.
4. A bacterium divides every 20 minutes. How many cells come from one cell in 80 minutes?
5. Why does a fermenter need a water jacket?
6. Name two conditions in a fermenter that are monitored by a probe.
7. Name the two groups of enzymes in biological washing powders that digest protein and fat stains.
8. State the two benefits of adding pectinase to crushed fruit.
9. State the products when lactase acts on lactose.
10. Define genetic modification.
11. Name the three reasons the syllabus gives for inserting genes into crop plants.
12. Juice volume rises from 15 cm³ to 24 cm³ with pectinase. Calculate the percentage increase.

### Answers

1. Carbon dioxide (bubbles make the dough rise).
2. It evaporates during baking.
3. Rapid reproduction rate; ability to make complex molecules.
4. 80 ÷ 20 = 4 divisions; 2⁴ = **16** cells.
5. Respiration of the microorganisms releases heat; cooling water stops the temperature rising above the optimum and denaturing enzymes.
6. Temperature and pH.
7. Proteases and lipases.
8. More juice is released; the juice is clearer.
9. Glucose and galactose.
10. Changing the genetic material of an organism by removing, changing or inserting individual genes.
11. Resistance to herbicides; resistance to insect pests; additional vitamins.
12. Change = 9 cm³; (9 ÷ 15) × 100 = **60%**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "yeast produces oxygen" or "alcohol makes bread rise".
- Giving "bacteria are small" or "bacteria are cheap" instead of the syllabus reasons.
- Stating a feature of bacteria without the benefit, for example "they have plasmids" with no mention of inserting a gene.
- Saying a fermenter is heated, rather than cooled.
- Listing fermenter conditions outside the five in the syllabus, and missing one of the five.
- Writing that high temperature "kills" enzymes.
- Saying lactase makes "glucose" only.
- Confusing herbicide resistance with insect resistance.
- Giving only advantages in a "discuss" question about GM.
- Dividing by the final value when calculating a percentage increase.
