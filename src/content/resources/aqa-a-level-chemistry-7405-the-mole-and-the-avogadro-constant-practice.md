These questions cover section **3.1.2.2 The mole and the Avogadro constant** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 1. Learn the methods in the [Mole and Avogadro constant study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-the-mole-and-the-avogadro-constant/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-the-mole-and-the-avogadro-constant-revision-notes/). More questions on this sub-topic and the one before it are in the [Relative Mass, the Mole and the Avogadro Constant practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-mole-practice/). Use L = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. Give answers to an appropriate number of significant figures.

## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by the Avogadro constant. **[1]**

**2.** Calculate the amount, in moles, in each sample.

**(a)** 6.60 g of carbon dioxide, CO₂ (Mᵣ = 44.0) **[1]**

**(b)** 3.20 g of sulfur dioxide, SO₂ (Mᵣ = 64.1) **[1]**

**3.** A flask contains 0.0250 mol of nitrogen gas, N₂. Calculate the number of molecules and the number of atoms in the flask. **[2]**

**4.** A diamond contains 1.204 × 10²⁴ carbon atoms. Calculate its mass (Aᵣ C = 12.0). **[2]**

**5.** Calculate the number of electrons in 3.40 g of ammonia, NH₃ (Mᵣ = 17.0). **[3]**

**6.** Sucrose has the formula C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ and Mᵣ = 342.0. Calculate the mass of one sucrose molecule:

**(a)** in grams **[1]**

**(b)** in kilograms **[1]**

**7.** 2.65 g of sodium carbonate, Na₂CO₃ (Mᵣ = 106.0), is dissolved in water and made up to 250 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the concentration of the sodium carbonate solution in mol dm⁻³. **[2]**

**(b)** State the concentration of sodium ions in this solution. **[1]**

**8.** Calculate the volume, in cm³, of 0.0800 mol dm⁻³ potassium hydroxide solution that contains 1.50 × 10⁻³ mol of KOH. **[2]**

**9.** 40.0 cm³ of 0.150 mol dm⁻³ sodium sulfate, Na₂SO₄, is mixed with 60.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ sodium chloride, NaCl. Calculate the concentration of sodium ions in the mixture. **[3]**

**10.** Ammonia is oxidised: 4NH₃ + 5O₂ → 4NO + 6H₂O.

**(a)** Calculate the amount of oxygen, in moles, that reacts with 0.60 mol of ammonia. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of water molecules formed from 0.60 mol of ammonia. **[2]**

**11.** A student calculates the amount of magnesium in 0.48 g of magnesium (Aᵣ = 24.3) and writes the answer as 0.019753 mol. Give the answer to an appropriate precision and justify your choice. **[2]**

**12.** 150 cm³ of a calcium nitrate solution, Ca(NO₃)₂, contains 1.806 × 10²² nitrate ions. Calculate the concentration of the calcium nitrate solution in mol dm⁻³. **[3]**

**13.** A technician makes 250 cm³ of 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ copper(II) sulfate solution from hydrated copper(II) sulfate, CuSO₄·5H₂O (Mᵣ = 249.6).

**(a)** Calculate the mass of hydrated copper(II) sulfate needed. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of Cu²⁺ ions in a 25.0 cm³ portion of the solution. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the number of oxygen atoms in the mass of solid weighed out in part (a). **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The number of particles (atoms, molecules, ions or electrons) in one mole of a substance [1]. **[1]**

**2. (a)** 6.60 ÷ 44.0 = **0.150 mol** [1]

**(b)** 3.20 ÷ 64.1 = **0.0499 mol** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Molecules = 0.0250 × 6.022 × 10²³ = **1.51 × 10²²** [1]; each molecule has 2 atoms, so atoms = **3.01 × 10²²** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** n = 1.204 × 10²⁴ ÷ 6.022 × 10²³ = 2.00 mol [1]; mass = 2.00 × 12.0 = **24.0 g** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** n(NH₃) = 3.40 ÷ 17.0 = 0.200 mol [1]; each molecule has 7 + 3 = 10 electrons, so n(electrons) = 2.00 mol [1]; number = 2.00 × 6.022 × 10²³ = **1.20 × 10²⁴** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Stopping at 1.20 × 10²³ (the number of molecules) is the commonest error; the question asks about electrons.

**6. (a)** 342.0 ÷ 6.022 × 10²³ = **5.68 × 10⁻²² g** [1]

**(b)** 5.68 × 10⁻²² ÷ 1000 = **5.68 × 10⁻²⁵ kg** [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** n = 2.65 ÷ 106.0 = 0.0250 mol [1]; c = 0.0250 ÷ 0.250 = **0.100 mol dm⁻³** [1]

**(b)** **0.200 mol dm⁻³** (two Na⁺ per formula unit) [1]. **[3]**

**8.** V = n ÷ c = 1.50 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0800 = 0.01875 dm³ [1]; = **18.8 cm³** [1]. **[2]**

**9.** n(Na⁺) from Na₂SO₄ = 2 × 0.150 × 0.0400 = 0.0120 mol [1]; n(Na⁺) from NaCl = 0.100 × 0.0600 = 0.00600 mol [1]; c = 0.0180 ÷ 0.100 = **0.180 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The concentrations cannot be added or averaged; work in moles of Na⁺, then divide by the combined volume of 100 cm³.

**10. (a)** 0.60 × 5 ÷ 4 = **0.75 mol** [1]

**(b)** n(H₂O) = 0.60 × 6 ÷ 4 = 0.90 mol [1]; number = 0.90 × 6.022 × 10²³ = **5.4 × 10²³** [1]. **[3]**

**11.** **0.020 mol** [1]; the mass, 0.48 g, has only two significant figures, so the answer can be given only to two [1]. **[2]**

**12.** n(NO₃⁻) = 1.806 × 10²² ÷ 6.022 × 10²³ = 0.0300 mol [1]; n(Ca(NO₃)₂) = 0.0300 ÷ 2 = 0.0150 mol [1]; c = 0.0150 ÷ 0.150 = **0.100 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Each formula unit releases two nitrate ions, so the salt amount is half the ion amount; forgetting this gives 0.200 mol dm⁻³.

**13. (a)** n = 0.0500 × 0.250 = 0.0125 mol [1]; mass = 0.0125 × 249.6 = **3.12 g** [1]

**(b)** n(Cu²⁺) = 0.0500 × 0.0250 = 1.25 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; number = 1.25 × 10⁻³ × 6.022 × 10²³ = **7.53 × 10²⁰** [1]

**(c)** Each CuSO₄·5H₂O has 4 + 5 = 9 oxygen atoms, so n(O) = 9 × 0.0125 = 0.1125 mol [1]; number = 0.1125 × 6.022 × 10²³ = **6.77 × 10²²** [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* The water of crystallisation contains oxygen too; counting only the 4 in the sulfate gives 3.01 × 10²².

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the number of molecules when atoms, ions or electrons are asked for.
- Leaving volumes in cm³ in c × V.
- Adding concentrations when mixing.
- Forgetting the number of ions in each formula unit.
- Too many significant figures for the data.
