The [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-mexican-revolution/) explains each point in more detail, and the [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-mexican-revolution-practice/) let you test your essay planning.

## Key terms

- **Porfiriato**: the rule of Porfirio Díaz.
- **Científicos**: technocratic advisers to Díaz.
- **Rurales**: Díaz's rural police.
- **Hacienda**: large landed estate.
- **Ejido**: communal land held by a village.
- **Maximato**: period when Calles was "supreme chief" (1928 to 1934).
- **Cristeros**: Catholic rebels in the war of 1926 to 1929.
- **Soldaderas**: women who accompanied and served armies.
- **Indigenismo**: policy and culture valuing Indigenous heritage, usually directed by non-Indigenous people.
- **Corporatism**: organising groups such as peasants and workers under party and state control.

## Causes of the revolution

| Type | Points |
|---|---|
| Political | Díaz's long rule, managed elections, no successor; Madero's challenge in 1910 |
| Economic | Railways and foreign investment; land concentration; stagnant wages |
| Social | Peasants and workers without rights; Cananea and Río Blanco repressed; Flores Magón critics |
| Short-term trigger | Creelman interview (1908); 1910 election and Madero's arrest |

## Leaders

| Leader | Role | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Madero | Started revolt; president 1911 | No land reform; kept old army; murdered 1913 |
| Huerta | Military dictator 1913 to 1914 | United opponents against him |
| Villa | Division of the North; Chihuahua | No national programme; lost at Celaya |
| Zapata | Plan of Ayala; Morelos; village lands | Regional; killed 1919 |
| Carranza | Plan of Guadalupe; 1917 Constitution | Cautious on reform; killed 1920 |
| Obregón | Military victor; president 1920 to 1924 | Modest land reform |

## The 1917 Constitution

- Article 27: land and subsoil belong to the nation; basis for land reform and oil nationalisation.
- Article 123: eight-hour day, minimum wage, right to strike.
- Article 3: free, secular primary education.
- Church provisions: property limits, restrictions on schools and priests.
- Enforcement was slow and depended on later presidents.

## Post-revolutionary state (1920 to 1940)

| Period | Features |
|---|---|
| Obregón | Order; rural schools (Vasconcelos); murals; modest land reform |
| Calles | Anticlericalism; roads, irrigation, Bank of Mexico |
| Maximato | Calles as "supreme chief"; PNR (1929) stabilises succession |
| Cristero War | Catholic rebellion 1926 to 1929; negotiated end |
| Cárdenas | About 18 million hectares redistributed; unions and peasant confederations; railways (1937) and oil (1938) nationalised |

## Foreign contributions

- Motives: investments, oil, security, fear of radical reform.
- Military: US at Veracruz (1914); Pershing's Punitive Expedition (1916 to 1917).
- Economic: arms from the US; companies backing factions.
- Diplomatic: Ambassador Henry Lane Wilson and the Huerta coup; Woodrow Wilson refuses to recognise Huerta; Zimmermann Telegram (1917).

## Impact

- Economic: disruption, land reform, state control of resources.
- Culture: muralists (Rivera, Orozco, Siqueiros), corridos, mexicanidad, rural education.
- Women: soldaderas, activists such as Hermila Galindo; divorce law in 1917; no national vote until 1953.
- Marginalised groups: Indigenous peoples integrated into a mestizo national ideal; mixed gains.

## Debates

- Social revolution (Womack, Knight) or change of elites (revisionist view)?
- Peasant movements versus northern middle-class leaders.
- Cárdenas: fulfilment of the revolution or a corporatist structure of control?
- Foreign influence: decisive or secondary?

## Essay skeleton

1. Define the argument in one sentence.
2. Use at least two factors or leaders and rank them.
3. Add a perspective from historians.
4. Show change and continuity (what remained the same after 1920?).
5. Judge.

## Worked example: planning an answer

Question: "To what extent was the Constitution of 1917 the most important achievement of the Mexican Revolution?"

1. Line: it was a vital framework, but its importance depended on enforcement under Cárdenas.
2. Evidence for: Article 27 (land and subsoil), Article 123 (labour), Article 3 (education), and the church provisions.
3. Limits: Carranza's weak enforcement; the Cristero War; the Maximato.
4. Other achievements: Cárdenas's land reform (about 18 million hectares), oil nationalisation (1938), the party-state (1929), cultural change.
5. Perspective: Knight and Womack (real social change) against revisionists (change of elites).
6. Judgement: the Constitution was the legal base and promise, while the main achievements came when later governments acted on it.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing Zapata's and Villa's aims.
- Stopping at 1917.
- Treating the Constitution as fully enforced.
- Ignoring the Cristero War and Cárdenas.

## Self-test

1. What did the Plan of Ayala demand?
2. Which article of the 1917 Constitution dealt with land?
3. Why did Catholics rebel in the 1920s?
4. What happened in March 1938?
5. Name two forms of foreign involvement.
6. Why is the revolution called a "rebellion" by some historians?

**Answers:** 1. Return of village lands and land redistribution. 2. Article 27. 3. Anticlerical laws enforced by Calles restricted the Church. 4. Cárdenas nationalised foreign oil companies. 5. Any two: US occupation of Veracruz, the Punitive Expedition, arms supplies, diplomatic recognition decisions, the Zimmermann Telegram. 6. They argue that it changed political elites more than the social structure.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many leaders should I know?**
Know Madero, Huerta, Villa, Zapata and Carranza in detail, and the post-1920 presidents for the second half.

**What counts as an achievement of the revolution?**
Land reform, labour rights and education reform, set against corruption and political control.
