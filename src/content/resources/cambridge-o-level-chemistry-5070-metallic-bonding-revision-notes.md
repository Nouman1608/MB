These notes condense section 2.7, Metallic bonding, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). Both outcomes are required for every candidate. The full explanations and worked answers are in the [metallic bonding study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-metallic-bonding/); exam-style questions are in the [metallic bonding practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-metallic-bonding-practice/).

## The definition (learn word for word)

**Metallic bonding** is the electrostatic attraction between the positive ions in a giant metallic lattice and a "sea" of delocalised electrons.

Four key terms, all needed in a full description:

1. **positive ions**
2. **giant metallic lattice** (regular layers)
3. **delocalised electrons** ("sea")
4. **electrostatic attraction**

## Where the electrons come from

| Metal | Configuration | Electrons delocalised per atom | Ion in lattice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lithium | 2,1 | 1 | Li⁺ |
| Sodium | 2,8,1 | 1 | Na⁺ |
| Magnesium | 2,8,2 | 2 | Mg²⁺ |
| Aluminium | 2,8,3 | 3 | Al³⁺ |
| Calcium | 2,8,8,2 | 2 | Ca²⁺ |

The outer-shell electrons become delocalised. Total electron charge = total ion charge, so the metal is neutral.

## Property 1: electrical conductivity

```
delocalised electrons  →  free to move through lattice  →  carry charge  →  conducts
```

- Conducts when **solid and when molten** (the electron sea is present in both).
- Current in a metal = flow of **electrons**, not ions.

## Property 2: malleability and ductility

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Malleable | can be hammered or rolled into shape without breaking |
| Ductile | can be drawn out into wires |

Explanation (same for both):

1. Ions are arranged in **layers**.
2. Under a force, **layers slide** over each other.
3. Delocalised electrons **still attract** the ions in their new positions.
4. **Bonding is not broken**, so the metal changes shape instead of shattering.

## Metal vs ionic vs giant covalent

| | Metal | Ionic compound | Diamond |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mobile charged particles in the solid | delocalised electrons | none | none |
| Conducts as solid | yes | no | no |
| Conducts when molten | yes | yes (ions) | — |
| Response to hammering | bends (malleable) | shatters | resists (very hard) |

Ionic solids shatter because a shifted layer puts like charges next to each other, which repel. Metals have only positive ions, all held by the electron sea.

## Model answers to learn

**Describe the bonding in a metal (3 marks).** A giant lattice of positive metal ions in layers. The outer-shell electrons are delocalised, forming a sea of electrons free to move through the lattice. The metallic bond is the strong electrostatic attraction between the positive ions and the delocalised electrons.

**Explain why a metal conducts (2 marks).** The metal contains delocalised electrons. They are free to move through the structure and carry charge.

**Explain why a metal is malleable (3 marks).** The positive ions are arranged in layers. When a force is applied, the layers slide over each other. The delocalised electrons still attract the ions in their new positions, so the metallic bonding is not broken.

## Drawing checklist

- Positive ions in neat rows, each labelled with the correct charge.
- Electrons (e⁻) in the spaces between ions, not in shells around them.
- Number of electrons balances the total ion charge.
- A key or labels: "positive ion" and "delocalised electron".

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Metallic bond | electrostatic attraction between positive metal ions and delocalised electrons |
| Delocalised electrons | outer electrons free to move throughout the whole lattice |
| Giant metallic lattice | regular arrangement of positive ions in layers throughout the metal |
| Electrostatic attraction | force between opposite charges |
| Conductor | substance that allows electric current to pass |
| Malleable | can be hammered into shape |
| Ductile | can be drawn into wires |

## Exam traps

- "Sea of electrons", never "sea of ions" or "sea of protons".
- Metals conduct by moving **electrons**; ionic compounds by moving **ions**.
- "Layers slide" is only half the explanation; add "bonding is maintained".
- Electrons are not lost from the metal; the lattice is neutral overall.
- Metallic bonding is **strong**, even though the electrons move.
- Graphite also conducts via delocalised electrons, but it is not metallic.

## Self-test

1. Define metallic bonding.
2. Which electrons in a magnesium atom become delocalised?
3. What is the charge on the ions in a lattice of aluminium?
4. Why is a piece of sodium electrically neutral?
5. Explain why iron conducts electricity.
6. Does molten zinc conduct electricity? Explain.
7. What does *ductile* mean?
8. Explain why gold can be hammered into very thin sheets.
9. Why does a crystal of sodium chloride shatter when hammered, while sodium metal does not?
10. Name the particles that carry charge in (a) a copper wire and (b) molten lead(II) bromide.
11. A student draws a metal lattice with negative ions between positive ions. What is wrong?
12. Give **two** of the four key terms needed in a definition of metallic bonding.

## Answers

1. The electrostatic attraction between the positive ions in a giant metallic lattice and a sea of delocalised electrons.
2. The two outer-shell electrons.
3. 3+.
4. The total negative charge of the delocalised electrons equals the total positive charge of the Na⁺ ions.
5. It has delocalised electrons that are free to move through the lattice and carry charge.
6. Yes: the delocalised electrons are still present and free to move.
7. Able to be drawn out into wires.
8. Layers of gold ions slide over each other when hammered, and the delocalised electrons keep attracting the ions, so the bonding is not broken.
9. In NaCl, shifting a layer brings like-charged ions together, which repel; in sodium the layers slide and the electron sea keeps holding the ions.
10. (a) Delocalised electrons. (b) Ions (Pb²⁺ and Br⁻).
11. A metal lattice has no negative ions: the negative charge is a sea of delocalised electrons.
12. Any two of: positive ions; giant metallic lattice; delocalised electrons; electrostatic attraction.
