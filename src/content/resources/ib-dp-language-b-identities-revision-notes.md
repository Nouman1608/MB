The [full syllabus guide](/resources/ib-dp-language-b-syllabus-guide/) confirms all five prescribed
themes are compulsory and examined through unseen material in Paper 2. These notes cover
**Identities**, alongside the
[Sharing the planet revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-language-b-sharing-the-planet-revision-notes/)
already on the site.

## Why every theme needs genuine preparation

Because Paper 2's listening and reading sections use **unseen** material across all five themes, an
unfamiliar text on Identities is just as likely to appear as one on any other theme. Concentrating
revision only on the themes discussed most in classroom speaking practice leaves real gaps in
comprehension for the others.

## What Identities covers

Explores the nature of the self and what it means to be human. Recommended topics: lifestyles, health
and well-being, beliefs and values, subcultures, and language and identity. The theme's guiding
question -- how do language and culture contribute to forming an identity? -- is a useful lens to
apply to any Identities text, whether written, audio, visual or audio-visual.

## Vocabulary and comprehension practice

Because Paper 2's reading and listening sections are worth 50% combined -- as much as productive
writing and the internal oral put together -- comprehension vocabulary specific to this theme
(describing personality, values, cultural belonging, lifestyle choices) deserves dedicated practice
time, not just the vocabulary needed to talk about the theme yourself.

## Worked example: applying the guiding question to an unseen text

An unseen reading passage discusses how a community's traditional dress has changed across
generations.

```
Surface content:    describes changes in traditional dress over time
Guiding question
applied:             how does the described change in dress reflect
                     a shift in how the community's identity is
                     expressed or understood across generations
Comprehension
skill tested:        connecting specific detail in the passage
                     (what changed, when, why) to the broader theme
                     question, not just extracting isolated facts
```

Practising this connection -- specific detail to broader theme question -- under timed conditions is
more useful preparation than memorising vocabulary lists in isolation.

## Text types and register

For Paper 1's writing task, if a question falls under Identities, practise the specific text types the
syllabus expects (a blog post, a formal letter, an article) using vocabulary and register appropriate
to discussing lifestyle, values or personal identity specifically -- format and register are assessed
alongside content, so a well-argued response in the wrong text-type conventions loses marks.

## The individual oral connection

For SL students, the individual oral uses a visual stimulus relevant to one of the five themes — if
that stimulus relates to Identities, be ready to discuss lifestyle, values, subcultures or personal
identity using the theme's specific vocabulary rather than generic conversational language. For HL
students, whose oral stimulus comes from one of their two studied literary works, check early whether
either work engages with identity-related content, since a work that clearly connects to this theme
gives a ready source of textual reference to draw on if an Identities-related discussion arises.

## Cultural variation within the theme

Because Language B is taught across many different school and cultural contexts, "identity" itself can
be discussed very differently depending on the target-language culture being studied — family
structure, social expectations and the language used to discuss personal identity vary meaningfully
between cultures. Paying attention to how identity is discussed specifically within the culture(s)
associated with your target language, rather than assuming your own cultural assumptions transfer
directly, strengthens both comprehension of unseen material and the authenticity of your own spoken
and written responses.

## Common mistakes

Concentrating speaking and writing practice on Identities (often the first theme taught) while
neglecting comprehension practice for it once the class moves on to other themes. Discussing surface
content of an Identities text without connecting it to the theme's guiding question about language,
culture and identity formation. Assuming familiarity with a theme discussed heavily in class
guarantees comprehension of unfamiliar texts on it in the exam.

## Quick revision checklist

- Build vocabulary across all five recommended Identities topics (lifestyles, health/well-being,
  beliefs/values, subcultures, language and identity), not just the one or two discussed most in
  class.
- Practise unseen listening and reading comprehension on Identities specifically, not only
  speaking/writing output.
- For HL, check whether either of your two studied literary works connects to the Identities theme,
  since the individual oral stimulus is drawn from one of them.

## A note on assessment weighting

Like the other four themes, Identities is not tested as a separately named exam section — it is one of
the five themes Paper 2's unseen material can be drawn from, and one of several possible sources for
Paper 1's writing task and the individual oral's stimulus. Revision is most effective when it builds
transferable comprehension and production fluency around the theme's vocabulary and guiding question,
rather than treating Identities as a fixed block of content to recall verbatim.

## Self-test

1. Why does an unseen text on Identities deserve as much preparation as any other theme?
2. State the guiding question this theme returns to repeatedly.
3. Name two of the five recommended topics under Identities.
4. Why is comprehension vocabulary important, not just productive vocabulary?
5. What should a strong answer connect specific textual detail to?

**Answers:** 1. Because Paper 2's listening and reading sections use unseen material across all five
compulsory themes, so any theme, including Identities, can appear. 2. How do language and culture
contribute to forming an identity? 3. Any two of: lifestyles, health and well-being, beliefs and
values, subcultures, language and identity. 4. Because Paper 2's receptive skills (listening and
reading) are worth 50% combined -- as much as the productive writing task and internal oral combined --
so comprehension vocabulary carries equal weight to production. 5. The theme's broader guiding
question, not just isolated facts extracted from the passage.
