This guide covers outcomes **1.3.14 Receipts and payments accounts**, **1.3.15 Trading accounts of different activities**, **1.3.16 Subscription accounts**, **1.3.17 Long-term/life membership**, **1.3.18 Losses of inventory or cash** and **1.3.19 Income and expenditure accounts and statements of financial position**. They sit in Topic 1.3, Financial statements of organisations, in Unit 1 (The Accounting System and Costing) of the Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Subsidiary/Advanced Level in Accounting (YAC11), Specification Issue 2, September 2018. This is International AS content. All amounts are in dollars and every organisation named is invented.

Use it with the [financial statements of organisations revision notes](/resources/edexcel-ial-accounting-financial-statements-of-organisations-revision-notes/) and the [clubs practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-clubs-and-non-profit-making-organisations-practice/). Accruals and prepayments are explained in the [year-end adjustments guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-year-end-adjustments/).

## What the specification asks for

| Outcome | Wording | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1.3.14 | Receipts and payments accounts | Prepare and interpret the summary of cash and bank movements |
| 1.3.15 | Trading accounts of different activities | Calculate the profit or loss of activities such as a bar or a competition |
| 1.3.16 | Subscription accounts | Find the subscription income for the year, allowing for arrears and advance payments |
| 1.3.17 | Long-term/life membership | Spread life membership fees over a number of years |
| 1.3.18 | Losses of inventory or cash | Calculate the loss and show it in the accounts |
| 1.3.19 | Income and expenditure accounts and statements of financial position | Prepare both statements, with the accumulated fund |

A club or society exists to provide services to members, not to make a profit. It does not have an owner's capital. Instead it has an **accumulated fund**, which is the net assets built up from past surpluses. Profit is called a **surplus** and a loss is called a **deficit**.

## Receipts and payments account versus income and expenditure account

| | Receipts and payments account | Income and expenditure account |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | Cash and bank only | Accruals: income earned and costs incurred |
| Includes capital items | Yes, such as equipment bought | No, only revenue items and depreciation |
| Includes accruals and prepayments | No | Yes |
| Result | Closing cash and bank balance | Surplus or deficit |

The receipts and payments account is a summary of the cash book. It starts with the opening cash and bank balance on the receipts side and finishes with the closing balance on the payments side. Members get a clear view of cash received and spent, but it does not show whether the club has made a surplus.

## Worked example: Greenfield Cricket Club

The year ended 31 December 2026. At 1 January 2026 the club had clubhouse 80,000, equipment 12,000, bar inventory 2,100, subscriptions in arrears 300 and bank and cash 5,600. It owed bar suppliers 900 and members had paid subscriptions in advance of 150.

**Step 1: opening accumulated fund**

Assets 80,000 + 12,000 + 2,100 + 300 + 5,600 = 100,000. Less liabilities 900 + 150 = 1,050. Accumulated fund = **98,950**. Use the same logic as opening capital for a sole trader.

**Step 2: receipts and payments account for the year ended 31 December 2026**

| Receipts | $ | Payments | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Balance b/d | 5,600 | Bar purchases | 13,200 |
| Subscriptions | 14,600 | Bar wages | 3,600 |
| Bar sales | 21,400 | General wages | 8,500 |
| Donations | 1,500 | Repairs | 2,300 |
| Life membership fees | 1,800 | Insurance | 1,400 |
| Competition entry fees | 900 | Equipment | 3,000 |
| | | Competition prizes | 400 |
| | | Balance c/d | 13,400 |
| | **45,800** | | **45,800** |

Receipts total 5,600 + 14,600 + 21,400 + 1,500 + 1,800 + 900 = 45,800. Payments before the balance total 32,400, so the closing balance is 45,800 − 32,400 = 13,400.

**Other information:** subscriptions in arrears at the end of the year 500, subscriptions in advance 250. Bar suppliers owed 1,100 and bar inventory was 2,400 at the year end. Wages of 300 were owing. Insurance of 200 was paid in advance. Equipment is depreciated at 10% of the year-end cost (15,000). Life membership fees of 1,800 are to be spread over 10 years.

**Step 3: subscription account (1.3.16)**

| Dr | $ | Cr | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Balance b/d (arrears) | 300 | Balance b/d (in advance) | 150 |
| Income and expenditure (balancing figure) | 14,700 | Bank | 14,600 |
| Balance c/d (in advance) | 250 | Balance c/d (arrears) | 500 |
| | **15,250** | | **15,250** |

Subscription income = 15,250 − 300 − 250 = **14,700**. Check by formula: cash received 14,600 + closing arrears 500 + opening advance 150 − opening arrears 300 − closing advance 250 = 14,700. Arrears owed to the club are an asset; subscriptions paid in advance are a liability.

**Step 4: trading account of an activity (1.3.15)**

The bar and the competition each have their own account so that members can see whether each activity covers its costs.

*Bar.* Purchases = payments 13,200 + closing payables 1,100 − opening payables 900 = 13,400.

| | $ | $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bar sales | | 21,400 |
| Opening inventory | 2,100 | |
| Purchases | 13,400 | |
| Closing inventory | (2,400) | |
| Cost of sales | | (13,100) |
| Gross profit | | 8,300 |
| Bar wages | | (3,600) |
| **Profit on bar** | | **4,700** |

*Competition.* Entry fees 900 less prizes 400 = **surplus of 500**.

**Step 5: life membership (1.3.17)**

A life member pays once, but the club provides services for many years. Following the accruals concept, the fee is spread over the expected years. 1,800 ÷ 10 = 180 is income for this year, so the remaining **1,620** is a liability, shown as a life membership account. Each year the club transfers a further 180 to the income and expenditure account. When the fee covers several members, spread the total on the same basis.

| Subscriptions | | 14,700 |
| Donations | | 1,500 |

| Surplus on competition | | 500 |
| Profit on bar | | 4,700 |
| | | 21,580 |
| **Expenditure** | | |
| General wages (8,500 + 300) | 8,800 | |
| Repairs | 2,300 | |
| Insurance (1,400 − 200) | 1,200 | |

The equipment purchase of 3,000 is a capital item, so it is not charged here. Only the depreciation of 1,500 is.

## 1.3.18 Losses of inventory or cash

Inventory or cash can be lost through theft, fire or error. You find the loss by comparing what *should* be there with what *is* there.

**Inventory.** Opening inventory 2,000, purchases 9,000, sales 12,000 at a 25% margin. Gross profit = 3,000 and cost of sales = 9,000. Goods available = 11,000, so closing inventory should be 11,000 − 9,000 = 2,000. A count shows 1,400, so **600** has been lost.

**Cash.** Opening cash 500, receipts recorded 3,000, payments 2,900. Cash should be 500 + 3,000 − 2,900 = 600. A count finds 100, so **500** is missing.

Show the loss as an expense in the income and expenditure account (or as a deduction in the activity account), unless insurance will repay it. If a claim is accepted, record the amount as a receivable.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the receipts and payments account as if it showed the surplus.
- Putting equipment purchased in the income and expenditure account instead of the statement of financial position.
- Using the cash received for subscriptions as the income for the year, without adjusting for arrears and advance payments.
- Showing subscriptions in arrears as a liability. They are an asset, because members owe the club money.
- Forgetting that the opening and closing figures are on opposite sides of the subscription account.
- Crediting the whole life membership fee to income in the first year.
- Using the term "capital" or "profit" instead of accumulated fund and surplus.
- Omitting the opening accumulated fund calculation, so the statement of financial position cannot balance.

## Exam technique

- Calculate the accumulated fund first. It is the missing figure that makes the statement of financial position agree.
- Present each ledger account as a table, with the balancing figure labelled. It earns method marks if the arithmetic goes wrong.
- Use the club's own language: income and expenditure account, surplus or deficit, accumulated fund.
- For **comment**, state the figure and what it means. For example: "The bar earns 4,700, which pays for part of the club's running costs".
- Check that net assets equal the accumulated fund, as for any statement of financial position.
