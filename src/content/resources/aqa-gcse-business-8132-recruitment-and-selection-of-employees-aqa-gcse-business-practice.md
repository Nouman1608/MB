## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *person specification*. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** methods a business could use to advertise a vacancy externally. **[2]**

**3.** Which **one** of the following would appear on a job description rather than a person specification? A: five years' experience in retail. B: responsible for opening and locking the shop each day. C: a friendly, patient manner. D: a maths qualification. **[1]**

**4.** Explain **one** reason why a business might need to recruit new employees. **[2]**

**5.** Lindhurst Accountants pays a recruitment agency 14% of the first year's salary for each new trainee. The salary is £22,500. Calculate the agency fee. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of employing staff on full-time contracts. **[3]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Morrow Bakeries is replacing its head baker, who is retiring.

**(a)** Explain **one** advantage of filling the post internally. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain **one** disadvantage of filling the post internally. **[3]**

**8.** Kestle Leisure is recruiting a swimming instructor for its pool.

**(a)** Identify **two** items that would be included on the person specification. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse why Kestle Leisure should use more than an interview to select the instructor. **[4]**

**9.** Brayfield Hotel employs 20 housekeeping staff. Last year 12 of them left and each replacement cost £1,350 to recruit and train. After changing its selection process, the hotel expects only 5 to leave this year.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of housekeeping staff who left last year. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the expected saving in recruitment and training costs this year. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain **one** other benefit to the hotel of better staff retention. **[2]**

**10.** Pellow Events runs weddings and parties. The number of events each week varies from 0 to 8. Explain **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage to Pellow Events of employing waiting staff on zero-hour contracts. **[4]**

**11.** Tarnside Software needs a data security specialist, a role no current employee has done. An external advert and agency would cost £6,200. Promoting an existing programmer and paying for a course would cost £2,800, but the course takes four months to complete. Evaluate whether Tarnside Software should recruit internally or externally. **[6]**

**12.** Corley's Deli opens from 07:00 to 19:00, but most sales are made between 07:30 and 09:00 and between 12:00 and 14:00. It employs three full-time assistants. The owner is considering replacing one full-time assistant with two part-time assistants. Justify whether the owner should make this change. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** A document describing the qualities, skills, qualifications and experience [1] that the ideal applicant for a job should have [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* An answer that describes the duties of the job has defined a job description, so it earns nothing here.

**2.** Any two of: job websites, the business's own website or social media, printed press such as a local newspaper, a jobcentre, a recruitment agency [1] [1]. **[2]**

**3.** **B** [1]. It describes a duty of the job; the other options describe the person. **[1]**

**4.** The business is growing, for example opening a new branch [1], so it needs more staff to meet the higher level of work [1]. Also accept: replacing someone who has left or retired, or needing a new skill, with a development. **[2]**

**5.** 0.14 × £22,500 [1] = **£3,150** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Full-time staff are available throughout the working week [1], so they build experience and knowledge of the business quickly [1], which means customers receive consistent service and less time is spent on handovers [1]. Also accept loyalty or retention with a development. **[3]**

**7. (a)** The business already knows the baker's skill and reliability [1], so there is less risk of appointing someone who cannot do the job [1], and the person knows the recipes and equipment, so little induction is needed [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The promoted baker's old job becomes vacant [1], so Morrow Bakeries must recruit again [1], adding cost and time, and there may be a gap in production meanwhile [1]. Also accept fewer new ideas, or resentment from other bakers, if developed. **[3]**

**8. (a)** Any two of: a recognised swimming teaching or lifeguard qualification, first-aid skills, experience of teaching children or adults, patience, good communication [1] [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** An interview shows how well the applicant talks about teaching [1] but not whether they can actually teach and respond to an emergency in the water [1]. A practical test, such as running a short lesson, shows the skill directly [1], which reduces the risk of appointing an unsafe instructor and protects customer safety and the centre's reputation [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** 12 ÷ 20 × 100 [1] = **60%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (12 − 5) × £1,350 = 7 × £1,350 [1] = **£9,450** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Experienced staff work faster and know the hotel's standards [1], so rooms are cleaned to a higher standard, which improves guest satisfaction and reviews [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Advantage: Pellow Events only pays staff when there is an event [1], so wage costs fall in weeks with few bookings [1]. Disadvantage: staff have no guaranteed income, so they may take other work and be unavailable [1], meaning Pellow may struggle to staff a busy week, harming service at events [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Internal is £6,200 − £2,800 = £3,400 cheaper [1]. The programmer knows Tarnside's systems and promotion may motivate other staff [1]. However, nobody has done the role, so the business relies on a course that takes four months [1], leaving security risks unmanaged in the meantime [1]. External recruitment brings proven expertise immediately [1]. Judgement with a reason, for example: recruit externally, because data security is too important to leave uncovered for four months and the extra £3,400 is small compared with the cost of a security breach; internal would suit if the risk could be covered temporarily [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The decision earns credit only when it uses the four-month delay and the cost difference from the stem.

**12.** Knowledge: part-time staff work fewer, usually set, hours, while full-time staff work the standard week [1].
Application: sales peak in two short periods [1]; at quiet times, such as mid-afternoon, a third full-time assistant may have little to do [1].
Analysis: two part-timers could each cover one peak, so the deli has an extra pair of hands when queues form, cutting waiting times and lost sales [1]; wages are paid for busy hours instead of idle ones [1]; the deli has more people to call on if someone is ill [1].
Evaluation: there are two people to recruit, induct and train, which costs money and management time [1]; part-timers may be less experienced, so consistency of service could fall [1]. Judgement: make the change, because the deli's demand is concentrated in two peaks, so cover at those times matters more than all-day presence, provided the part-timers are well trained [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers reaching the top marks use the opening hours and peak times in the stem rather than writing about part-time work in general.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining a person specification as a list of the job's duties.
- Forgetting the second vacancy created by internal recruitment.
- Leaving out × 100 in a percentage, or not stating the unit with a money answer.
- Recommending an interview for every job without asking what skill must be tested.
- Describing zero-hour contracts only from the worker's point of view when the question asks about the business.
- Ending an evaluate or justify question without a decision, or with a decision that ignores the stem.

## Frequently asked questions

**How should I answer a question comparing internal and external recruitment?**
Use the case: does anyone inside have the skills, how urgent is the vacancy, and what do the figures show? Then decide which route suits this business.

**How many points does an "explain" question need?**
Usually one point developed with linked reasons. Each link in the chain, applied to the business, adds a mark.
