## Core facts

- **Nanoscience** refers to structures **1 to 100 nm** in size, of the order of **a few hundred atoms**.
- **Nanoparticles** are smaller than **fine particles (PM2.5)**, which have diameters between **100 and 2500 nm**
  (1 × 10⁻⁷ m and 2.5 × 10⁻⁶ m).
- **Coarse particles (PM10)** have diameters between 2.5 × 10⁻⁶ m and 1 × 10⁻⁵ m, and are often called **dust**.
- As the side of a cube **decreases by a factor of 10**, the **surface area to volume ratio increases by a factor
  of 10**.
- Nanoparticles may have **different properties** from the same material in bulk because of their **high surface
  area to volume ratio**.
- **Smaller quantities** may be needed to be effective.

## Size scale

| Object | Diameter |
|---|---|
| Atom | About 0.1 nm (1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m) |
| Nanoparticle | 1 to 100 nm (1 × 10⁻⁹ m to 1 × 10⁻⁷ m) |
| Fine particle (PM2.5) | 100 to 2500 nm |
| Coarse particle (PM10) | 2500 to 10 000 nm |

**1 nm = 1 × 10⁻⁹ m.** To convert nm to m, multiply by 10⁻⁹.

## Surface area to volume ratio of a cube

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Surface area | 6 × L² |
| Volume | L³ |
| Ratio | 6 ÷ L |

| Side length | Surface area | Volume | SA : V |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 cm | 24 cm² | 8 cm³ | 3 |
| 0.2 cm | 0.24 cm² | 0.008 cm³ | 30 |
| 20 nm | 2400 nm² | 8000 nm³ | 0.3 |
| 2 nm | 24 nm² | 8 nm³ | 3 |

Each time the side falls by a factor of 10, the ratio rises by a factor of 10.

## Why properties differ

1. Nanoparticles have a very high surface area to volume ratio.
2. A high proportion of their atoms is at the surface.
3. So they can react or interact more effectively.
4. So smaller quantities may be needed.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nanometre (nm) | One billionth of a metre, 1 × 10⁻⁹ m |
| Nanoscience | The study of structures 1 to 100 nm in size |
| Nanoparticle | A particle 1 to 100 nm across |
| PM2.5 | Fine particles, 100 to 2500 nm |
| PM10 | Coarse particles, often called dust |
| Surface area to volume ratio | Surface area divided by volume |
| Order of magnitude | The nearest power of ten |

## Worked calculations in brief

**Conversion.** 40 nm = 40 × 10⁻⁹ m = 4 × 10⁻⁸ m.

**Ratio.** A cube of side 5 nm has SA = 6 × 25 = 150 nm², V = 125 nm³, ratio = 150 ÷ 125 = 1.2 per nm.

**Order of magnitude.** A 1500 nm fine particle compared with a 15 nm nanoparticle: 1500 ÷ 15 = 100, about 10²
times larger.

**Number of atoms across.** A 10 nm particle compared with an atom of 0.1 nm: 10 ÷ 0.1 = 100 atoms across.

## Why dividing a material raises the surface area

A cube of side 1 cm has a surface area of 6 cm². Cut it into 1000 cubes of side 0.1 cm. The total volume is still
1 cm³, but the total surface area is 1000 × 0.06 = 60 cm². The same material now has 10 times as much surface
area. This is the idea behind the high surface area to volume ratio of nanoparticles.

## Particle classification checklist

1. Convert the diameter into nm if it is in metres.
2. 1 to 100 nm: nanoparticle.
3. 100 to 2500 nm: fine particle (PM2.5).
4. 2500 to 10 000 nm: coarse particle (PM10), dust.

## Exam traps

- **Writing the ratio goes down for smaller particles.** It goes up.
- **Mixing up the factor.** A factor of 10 in side length means a factor of 10 in the ratio.
- **Wrong standard form.** 1 nm is 1 × 10⁻⁹ m.
- **Forgetting the units of area and volume.** Area is in squared units, volume in cubed units.
- **"Nanoparticles are a different substance."** They are the same substance in a different size.
- **Using nm and m together in one calculation.** Convert first.

## Quick self-test

1. What size range does nanoscience cover?
2. What is 1 nm in metres?
3. Which is larger: a nanoparticle or a fine particle?
4. Give the size range of fine particles in nm.
5. What is another name for coarse particles?
6. A cube has side 3 cm. Calculate its surface area and volume.
7. What happens to the surface area to volume ratio when the side of a cube decreases by a factor of 10?
8. Why might a smaller quantity of a nanoparticle catalyst be needed?
9. Write 60 nm in metres in standard form.
10. How many 0.1 nm atoms fit across a 20 nm particle?

### Answers

1. 1 to 100 nm.
2. 1 × 10⁻⁹ m.
3. A fine particle.
4. 100 to 2500 nm.
5. Dust.
6. Surface area 6 × 3 × 3 = 54 cm²; volume 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 cm³.
7. It increases by a factor of 10.
8. The high surface area to volume ratio means more of the material is exposed to the reactants.
9. 60 × 10⁻⁹ = 6 × 10⁻⁸ m.
10. 20 ÷ 0.1 = 200.
