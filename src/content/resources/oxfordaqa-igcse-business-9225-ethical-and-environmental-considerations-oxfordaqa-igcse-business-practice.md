All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Hollin Bakery.

> **Case study: Hollin Bakery**
>
> Hollin Bakery makes bread for shops across its region and employs 85
> staff. Its lorries leave at 5 am and drive through residential streets,
> and neighbours have complained to the local newspaper. The bakery sends
> 30 tonnes of packaging waste to landfill each year at $120 a tonne. A
> recycling scheme would cut landfill waste by 60% but cost $1,500 a year
> for collections. Hollin spends $40,000 a year on flour brought from
> another country and is considering switching to flour from local farms,
> which costs 8% more. Its annual profit is $120,000. Several Muslim
> employees have asked for a quiet room for prayer during shifts.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an example of ethical behaviour by a business? **[1]**
- A. Raising prices when a rival closes
- B. Paying suppliers on the date agreed
- C. Cutting staff training to reduce costs
- D. Advertising a product as "new" when it is unchanged

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a sustainability issue named in the specification? **[1]**
- A. Inflation
- B. Interest rates
- C. Global warming
- D. Exchange rates

**3.** Define the term "trade-off". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** environmental considerations that a business may need to take into account. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of charitable giving. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** **(a)** Calculate Hollin's yearly cost of sending packaging waste to landfill. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the net yearly saving from the recycling scheme. **[2]**

**7.** Calculate the extra yearly cost of switching to local flour, and Hollin's profit after the switch if nothing else changes. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **two** ways in which the 5 am lorry departures may affect Hollin Bakery. **[4]**

**9.** Explain **one** benefit and **one** drawback to Hollin of providing a prayer room for its employees. **[4]**

**10.** Analyse the trade-off between ethics and profit in Hollin's decision about whether to buy local flour. **[6]**

**11.** Evaluate whether Hollin Bakery should spend money on becoming more environmentally responsible. Use the case study and your answers to Questions 6 and 7. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Giving up some of one thing [1] in order to gain more of another, for example accepting lower profit in return for behaving more ethically [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: traffic congestion [1]; recycling [1]; disposing of waste; noise pollution; air pollution. **[2]**

**5.** Giving to local charities builds goodwill in the community [1], so local people are more likely to buy from the business and speak well of it, which can increase sales [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 30 × $120 = **$3,600** a year [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Landfill saving = 60% × $3,600 = $2,160 [1]. Net saving = $2,160 − $1,500 = **$660** a year [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Extra cost = 8% × $40,000 = **$3,200** [1]. Profit = $120,000 − $3,200 = **$116,800** [1]. **[2]**

**8.** **Reputation:** neighbours have complained to the local newspaper [1], so bad publicity could put off local customers and shops that buy Hollin's bread [1].
**Pressure to change:** continued noise in residential streets could lead the local council to restrict delivery times [1], forcing Hollin to change its routes or schedules, which could raise costs or delay deliveries to shops [1]. **[4]**

**9.** **Benefit:** employees can pray during shifts without leaving the site [1], so they feel respected and are more likely to stay, reducing recruitment and training costs [1].
**Drawback:** Hollin must give up space and may need to furnish the room [1], which costs money, and it may need to plan shift cover for short prayer breaks [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Buying local flour supports farmers in Hollin's own area [1], which local customers and shops may see as fair and responsible [1]. It also means shorter transport distances, reducing fuel use and emissions [1]. However, it costs $3,200 more each year [1], cutting profit to $116,800 unless Hollin raises its prices [1]. The trade-off is worth making only if customers value local sourcing enough to stay loyal or pay a little more; since $3,200 is under 3% of profit, the risk is small [1]. **[6]**

**11.** **For:** the recycling scheme actually saves $660 a year, so it improves both the environment and profit [1]; local flour costs only $3,200 a year, less than 3% of profit [1]; acting on the lorry complaints and waste could repair the damage to Hollin's reputation in the local newspaper [1]; shops may prefer a supplier with a greener image [1].
**Against:** other changes, such as quieter or cleaner lorries or later delivery times, could cost much more than these two schemes [1]; customers buying everyday bread may be price-sensitive, so Hollin may not be able to pass on higher costs [1].
**Judgement:** Hollin should introduce the recycling scheme straight away because it saves money, and switch to local flour because the cost is small compared with profit [1]. The biggest issue is the lorries; whether Hollin should spend more on them depends on the cost of changing delivery times compared with the risk of losing local customers or facing restrictions [1]. A sensible approach is to start with the low-cost changes and publicise them [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating the landfill saving but forgetting to subtract the cost of the recycling collections.
- Calculating 8% of profit rather than 8% of the flour bill.
- Describing the lorry problem only from the neighbours' point of view, without the effect on the business.
- Treating the prayer room as costless.
- Writing "ethics is good for business" without stating the trade-off with profit.
