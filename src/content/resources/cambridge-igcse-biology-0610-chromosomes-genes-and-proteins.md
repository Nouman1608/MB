## From nucleus to gene (Core)

Work from the largest structure to the smallest:

```
cell → nucleus → chromosomes → DNA → genes
```

- **Chromosomes** are found in the nucleus and are **made of DNA**.
- DNA **contains genetic information** in the form of **genes**.
- A **gene** is **a length of DNA that codes for a protein**.
- An **allele** is **an alternative form of a gene**.

### Gene or allele?

A gene is the section of DNA that affects a feature. Alleles are the different versions of that gene. In pea plants, one gene affects seed shape; one allele of it gives round seeds and another gives wrinkled seeds. Both alleles are versions of the **same** gene, found at the same place on a chromosome.

A test: if you can say "the gene **for** seed shape", it is a gene. If you can say "the allele for **round** seeds", it is an allele.

## Inheritance of sex (Core)

Humans have one pair of **sex chromosomes**:

- females: **XX**;
- males: **XY**.

Every egg cell carries one **X** chromosome. Half of the sperm carry an **X** and half carry a **Y**. The sex of a child therefore depends on which type of sperm fertilises the egg.

### Worked example 1: a full genetic diagram

*Use a genetic diagram to show that there is an equal chance of a child being male or female.*

```
Parents:            mother (female)      father (male)
Sex chromosomes:          XX                  XY
Gametes:               X     X             X     Y

                 X (egg)    X (egg)
   X (sperm)       XX         XX
   Y (sperm)       XY         XY

Offspring: XX, XX, XY, XY
Ratio: 2 female : 2 male = 1 : 1
Probability of a boy = 2/4 = 1/2 (50%)
```

**Follow-up:** *a couple have three daughters. What is the probability that their next child is a son?* Still **1/2**. Each fertilisation is independent, so earlier children do not change the chance.

## How DNA decides what a protein is (Extended only)

The chain to learn has four links:

```
base sequence in a gene
   → sequence of amino acids in a protein
      → shape of the protein molecule
         → what the protein does
```

- The **sequence of bases** in a gene determines the **sequence of amino acids** used to make a specific protein. You do not need the details of nucleotide structure.
- **Different sequences of amino acids give different shapes** to protein molecules.
- Shape decides function. An enzyme works only if its active site has the right shape for its substrate (topic 5).

### DNA controls the cell through its proteins

The syllabus names three kinds of protein. Learn an example of what each does:

| Protein | Example of its role |
|---|---|
| Enzymes | catalyse the reactions of the cell, such as amylase breaking down starch |
| Membrane carriers | move substances across the cell membrane, as in active transport of ions into root hair cells |
| Receptors for neurotransmitters | bind neurotransmitter molecules at a synapse so an impulse starts in the next neurone (topic 14) |

By deciding which of these proteins a cell makes, DNA decides what the cell does.

## How a protein is made (Extended only)

The syllabus limits you to six points, and says details of transcription and translation are not required.

1. The **gene** coding for the protein **remains in the nucleus**.
2. **Messenger RNA (mRNA)** is made as a **copy of the gene**.
3. mRNA molecules are **made in the nucleus** and **move to the cytoplasm**.
4. The mRNA **passes through ribosomes**.
5. The **ribosome assembles amino acids** into protein molecules.
6. The **specific sequence of amino acids** is determined by the **sequence of bases in the mRNA**.

**Why mRNA is needed:** the gene stays in the nucleus, but ribosomes are in the cytoplasm. mRNA carries a copy of the gene's base sequence to where proteins are assembled.

## Gene expression (Extended only)

Most body cells in an organism contain **the same genes**, because they all came from one zygote by mitosis. But **many genes in a particular cell are not expressed**, because the cell **only makes the specific proteins it needs**.

### Worked example 2: explaining different cells

*Cells in the pancreas that secrete insulin and cells in the skin come from the same person. Explain why only the pancreas cells make insulin.* **[3]**

> Both cells contain the same genes, including the gene for insulin [1]. In the pancreas cells the insulin gene is expressed, so mRNA copies of it are made and insulin is produced at the ribosomes [1]. In the skin cells the insulin gene is not expressed, because a skin cell only makes the specific proteins it needs [1].

Saying "skin cells do not have the insulin gene" is the error examiners look for.

### Worked example 3: when the base sequence changes

*A change in the base sequence of the gene for an enzyme stops the enzyme working. Explain why.* **[3]**

> The changed base sequence gives a different sequence of amino acids [1]. This gives the enzyme molecule a different shape [1], so its active site no longer fits its substrate [1].

Changes in base sequence are called gene mutations, which you meet in topic 18. Here, the marks come from following the chain from bases to shape to function.

## Haploid and diploid nuclei (Extended only)

- A **haploid** nucleus contains a **single set** of chromosomes.
- A **diploid** nucleus contains **two sets** of chromosomes.
- In a diploid cell there is a **pair of each type of chromosome**. A human diploid cell has **23 pairs**, so 46 chromosomes.

| Human cell | Type of nucleus | Chromosomes |
|---|---|---|
| Skin cell | diploid | 46 (23 pairs) |
| Sperm cell | haploid | 23 |
| Egg cell | haploid | 23 |
| Zygote | diploid | 46 |

The sex chromosomes count as one of the 23 pairs: XX in females and XY in males.

## Common mistakes

- "Chromosomes are made of genes." They are made of **DNA**; genes are lengths of that DNA.
- Using "gene" and "allele" as if they mean the same thing.
- Saying the mother decides the sex of the child. The **sperm** carries either X or Y.
- Thinking the chance of a boy changes with earlier children.
- (Extended) "mRNA is made in the cytoplasm" or "the gene leaves the nucleus". mRNA is made in the **nucleus**; the gene **stays** there.
- (Extended) "Ribosomes make amino acids." Ribosomes **assemble** amino acids into proteins.
- (Extended) "Different cells have different genes." They have the **same** genes but **express** different ones.
- (Extended) Giving 23 chromosomes for a body cell. It has 23 **pairs**.

## Exam technique

- **Define** gene and allele: learn the syllabus sentences exactly.
- **Describe** sex inheritance: show parents, gametes, a Punnett square, offspring and the ratio or probability.
- **Explain** protein structure (Extended): follow the chain bases → amino acids → shape → function and use each link once.
- **Explain** protein synthesis (Extended): six points in order; "copy", "nucleus", "cytoplasm", "ribosome" and "sequence of bases" are the words that score.
