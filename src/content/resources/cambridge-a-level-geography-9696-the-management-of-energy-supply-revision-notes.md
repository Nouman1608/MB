These notes condense section 12.2, The management of energy supply, of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Geography (9696) syllabus for 2025 and 2026 (Paper 4, Topic 12 option). The case studies are explained in full in the [12.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-geography-9696-the-management-of-energy-supply/); test yourself afterwards with the [12.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-geography-9696-the-management-of-energy-supply-practice/).

## What the syllabus demands

Two separate case studies, each showing issues of **changes in demand and supply**, **power production and its location**, and an **evaluation**:

1. one country's **overall electrical energy strategy**;
2. one **named located scheme** to produce electricity, such as a power station.

## UK strategy at a glance

- **Demand:** fallen since the mid-2000s (efficiency, decline of heavy industry, prices); expected to rise with heat pumps and electric vehicles.
- **Policy tools:** Climate Change Act 2008 (80% cut by 2050, changed to net zero in 2019); carbon price floor from 2013; Contracts for Difference guaranteeing a strike price; interconnectors, such as North Sea Link to Norway (2021).
- **Supply change:** coal collapsed in the 2010s; last coal station, Ratcliffe-on-Soar, closed 2024; gas the largest single source for much of the period; rapid growth of offshore wind; nuclear fleet ageing (Hinkley Point B closed 2022); Drax converted to imported wood pellets.
- **Location change:** from inland river valleys near coalfields (such as the Trent) to the coast and the North Sea.
- **Issues:** grid constraints between northern wind and southern demand; payments to switch wind farms off; intermittency; 2021–22 gas price shock; biomass controversy; nuclear delays.
- **Judgement:** strong on cutting emissions; mixed on security; weak on affordability and on building grid and nuclear capacity on time.

## Hinkley Point C at a glance

- **What and where:** nuclear station under construction on the Bristol Channel coast, Somerset, beside Hinkley Point A and B. EDF with China General Nuclear as minority partner. Two reactors, about 3.2 GW, around 7% of UK electricity, design life about 60 years.
- **Site factors:** existing nuclear site, cooling water, grid connection point, firm foundations, local acceptance.
- **Contract:** strike price £92.50 per MWh (2012 prices) for 35 years.
- **Issues:** delays from a mid-2020s target into the next decade; rising costs; high strike price compared with offshore wind; disputed fish protection; new pylon line across Somerset; waste; local traffic and housing pressure but many construction jobs.
- **Judgement:** late and over budget so far; long-term low-carbon baseload may justify it; success depends on timescale and viewpoint.

## Evaluation criteria

| Criterion | Short test |
|---|---|
| Effectiveness | Did supply change as intended? |
| Emissions | Did carbon emissions fall? |
| Security | Reliable and diverse? |
| Affordability | What happened to prices and public cost? |
| Timeliness | On time? |
| Distribution | Who gained, who paid, locally and nationally? |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electrical energy strategy | A country's plan for how electricity will be produced, moved and used |
| Strike price | Fixed price guaranteed to a generator under a Contract for Difference |
| Contract for Difference | Payment to cover the gap between market price and strike price |
| Carbon price floor | Minimum price on carbon emissions from power generation |
| Interconnector | Cable linking the electricity grids of two countries |
| Baseload | Steady minimum demand met by constant generation |
| Grid constraint | Transmission limit that prevents power reaching demand |
| Decarbonisation | Reducing carbon emissions from an activity |
| Net zero | Emissions balanced by removals |
| Design life | Period a plant is built to operate |
| Intermittency | Variable output of wind and solar |
| Decommissioning | Safely closing and dismantling a power station |

## Exam traps

- **One case study for both.** The syllabus wants a strategy and a separate scheme.
- **No location.** Show where production was and where it is now.
- **Supply only.** Include demand change and its causes.
- **Unfinished schemes.** Use "so far" and "if completed" in judgements.
- **Single viewpoint.** Evaluate from government, consumers, local people and environmental groups.
- **Shaky figures.** Leave out numbers you cannot recall accurately.

## Self-test

1. What two case studies does 12.2 require?
2. Which four headings should each case study cover?
3. Why has UK electricity demand fallen since the mid-2000s?
4. What did the Climate Change Act 2008 set, and how was it changed in 2019?
5. How does a Contract for Difference work?
6. Where were the UK's large coal stations located, and why?
7. Where is new UK generation mainly located?
8. What is a grid constraint, and why is it an issue in the UK?
9. Give three site factors for Hinkley Point C.
10. What strike price was agreed for Hinkley Point C?
11. Give two issues the scheme has faced.
12. Why must judgement of Hinkley Point C refer to timescale?

### Answers

1. One country's overall electrical energy strategy and one named located scheme.
2. Changes in demand, changes in supply, power production and its location, evaluation.
3. More efficient appliances and lighting, decline of heavy industry, higher prices.
4. An 80% cut in emissions by 2050; changed to net zero by 2050.
5. The generator receives a fixed strike price; it is paid the difference when the market price is lower.
6. Inland along rivers such as the Trent, near coalfields and with cooling water.
7. At the coast and offshore in the North Sea (wind), and at existing coastal nuclear sites.
8. A transmission limit; northern wind power cannot all reach southern demand, so wind farms are paid to switch off.
9. Any three: existing nuclear site, cooling water, grid connection, firm foundations, local acceptance.
10. £92.50 per MWh in 2012 prices, for 35 years.
11. Any two: delays, rising cost, high strike price, fish protection dispute, pylon route, waste, local disruption.
12. It is late and over budget now, but its benefits come over about 60 years of operation.
