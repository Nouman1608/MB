These questions cover section **3.2.6 Respiration** of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Biology (9201)
specification, Version 5.2, for exams in May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered. All data are
invented for practice. A calculator is useful for questions 7 and 9.

## Section A

**1.**

**(a)** State what is meant by aerobic respiration. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the **two** products of aerobic respiration. **[1]**

**2.** Write the balanced symbol equation for anaerobic respiration in muscle cells. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why muscle cells contain many mitochondria. **[2]**

**4.** Explain how converting glycogen to glucose helps the muscles during exercise. **[2]**

**5.**

**(a)** Name the products of anaerobic respiration in plant root cells. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why anaerobic respiration transfers less energy than aerobic respiration. **[1]**

**6.** Explain how energy from respiration is used when a plant makes proteins. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Two vacuum flasks are set up with thermometers. Flask A contains germinating bean seeds. Flask B contains
the same mass of bean seeds that have been boiled. Both sets of seeds were washed in disinfectant.

| Day | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flask A (°C) | 18.0 | 19.1 | 20.3 | 21.6 | 22.8 |
| Flask B (°C) | 18.0 | 18.0 | 18.1 | 18.0 | 18.1 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of temperature rise in flask A. Give the unit. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the purpose of flask B. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the seeds were washed in disinfectant. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the temperature in flask A rises. **[2]**

**8.** A potted plant is sealed in a clear container with a carbon dioxide sensor.

| Condition | CO₂ at start (ppm) | CO₂ after 2 hours (ppm) |
|---|---|---|
| Dark | 400 | 520 |
| Bright light | 400 | 250 |

**(a)** Explain the change in carbon dioxide in the dark. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the change in carbon dioxide in bright light. **[2]**
**(c)** A student concludes that the plant respires only in the dark. Explain why this conclusion is wrong. **[1]**

**9.** Two students do the same step exercise for 3 minutes. Their pulse is measured at rest and then every
minute after exercise.

| | Resting pulse (bpm) | Pulse straight after exercise (bpm) | Recovery time (min) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student P | 66 | 132 | 4 |
| Student Q | 74 | 150 | 8 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in pulse for each student. Give each answer to the nearest whole number.
**[2]**
**(b)** Suggest which student is fitter, and give a reason from the data. **[1]**
**(c)** Give **two** variables that must be kept the same for this comparison to be fair. **[2]**

**10.** Compare aerobic respiration in a human cell with anaerobic respiration in yeast. **[4]**

**11.** After heavy rain, a field of maize is waterlogged for several days and the plants grow poorly. Explain
why. **[3]**

**12.** A runner takes part in a long, fast race. Explain how her body responds to the demand for energy during the
race, why her muscles may become fatigued, and what happens after the race. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Respiration that uses oxygen (to transfer energy from glucose) [1]
**(b)** Carbon dioxide and water (both needed) [1]

**2.** C₆H₁₂O₆ → C₃H₆O₃ (correct formulae) [1]; 2 in front of C₃H₆O₃ (balanced) [1]

**3.** Most of the reactions of aerobic respiration take place in mitochondria [1]; muscle cells need to transfer
a lot of energy for contraction, so many mitochondria allow more respiration [1]

**4.** Glycogen is a store of glucose in the muscles [1]; converting it to glucose supplies more glucose for
respiration, so more energy is transferred for contraction [1]

**5. (a)** Ethanol and carbon dioxide (both needed) [1]
**(b)** The breakdown of glucose is incomplete [1]

**6.** Energy from respiration is used to build sugars, nitrates and other nutrients into amino acids [1]; and to
join amino acids together to build proteins [1]

**7. (a)** (22.8 − 18.0) ÷ 4 = 4.8 ÷ 4 [1] = **1.2 °C per day** [1]
**(b)** It is a control: it shows the temperature rise is caused by living seeds, not by the flask or the
surroundings [1]
**(c)** To kill microorganisms, so any temperature rise is due to the seeds' respiration, not to microorganisms
respiring [1]
**(d)** The germinating seeds are alive and respire [1]; some of the energy transferred by respiration is released
as heat, warming the flask [1]

**8. (a)** In the dark there is no photosynthesis [1]; the plant respires and releases carbon dioxide, so the
concentration rises [1]
**(b)** In bright light the plant photosynthesises, taking in carbon dioxide [1]; photosynthesis is faster than
respiration, so carbon dioxide is used faster than it is released and the concentration falls [1]
**(c)** Plants respire continuously; in light the carbon dioxide from respiration is used in photosynthesis, so
respiration is hidden, not stopped [1]

**9. (a)** P: (132 − 66) ÷ 66 × 100 = **100%** [1]; Q: (150 − 74) ÷ 74 × 100 = **103%** [1]
**(b)** Student P, because the recovery time is shorter (4 minutes compared with 8) (or the resting pulse is
lower) [1]
**(c)** Any two of the following, 1 mark each [2]: the same exercise (step height and stepping rate); the same
duration of exercise; the same method and timing of pulse measurement; the same rest before starting

**10.** Any four of the following, 1 mark each [4]: aerobic respiration uses oxygen, whereas anaerobic respiration
in yeast does not; aerobic produces carbon dioxide and water, whereas yeast produces ethanol and carbon dioxide;
both produce carbon dioxide; aerobic breaks glucose down completely, whereas anaerobic breakdown is incomplete;
aerobic transfers much more energy than anaerobic; most aerobic reactions happen in mitochondria

**11.** Water fills the air spaces in the soil, so the roots receive little oxygen [1]; root cells respire
anaerobically, producing ethanol (and carbon dioxide) [1]; much less energy is transferred, so the roots cannot
take up enough water and mineral ions or grow well (and ethanol may damage the cells) [1]

**12.** Marking points, 1 mark each [6]:
- heart rate increases, increasing blood flow to the muscles
- the rate and depth of breathing increase, and glycogen in the muscles is converted to glucose
- these changes increase the supply of oxygen and glucose to the muscles and remove carbon dioxide faster
- if not enough oxygen reaches the muscles, they respire anaerobically, producing lactic acid
- lactic acid builds up and is one cause of fatigue, so the muscles stop contracting efficiently
- after the race an oxygen debt is repaid: extra oxygen oxidises the lactic acid to carbon dioxide and water,
  and blood flow removes the lactic acid from the muscles

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying respiration "produces energy" rather than transferring it.
- Giving lactic acid as a product of anaerobic respiration in plants or yeast.
- Concluding that plants do not respire in the light.
- Forgetting to explain what a control flask shows.
- Explaining exercise changes without linking them to oxygen, glucose and carbon dioxide.
- Missing "oxidise" and "lactic acid" when explaining an oxygen debt.
