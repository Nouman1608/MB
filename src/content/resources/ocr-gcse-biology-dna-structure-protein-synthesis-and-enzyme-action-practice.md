## Questions

**1.** Describe the structure of DNA. **[3]**

**2.** (a) Describe the parts of one DNA nucleotide. **[2]**
**(b)** One strand of DNA reads GTCCAGT. Write the sequence of the strand paired with it. **[2]**

**3.** A sample of double-stranded DNA contains 31% cytosine. Calculate the percentage of guanine, adenine and thymine. **[3]**

**4.** (Higher Tier only) Describe how a gene in the nucleus is used to make a protein. **[5]**

**5.** (Higher Tier only) The template strand of a gene reads TAC GGT CTT AAA. Data supplied: AUG = Met, CCA = Pro, GAA = Glu, UUU = Phe, GAU = Asp, UUC = Phe.
**(a)** Write the mRNA sequence. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the order of amino acids. **[2]**

**6.** (Higher Tier only) Use the gene and data in question 5.
**(a)** The third triplet changes from CTT to CTA. State the new amino acid order and explain how this could stop an enzyme working. **[3]**
**(b)** Instead the last triplet changes from AAA to AAG. Explain why the protein is unchanged. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why one enzyme will catalyse only one reaction. **[4]**

**8.** Catalase is heated in boiling water, then cooled. It no longer breaks down hydrogen peroxide. Explain why. **[4]**

**9.** An amylase and starch mixture is tested at six pH values. The table shows the time for the starch to disappear.

| pH | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time / s | 240 | 120 | 60 | 40 | 80 | 200 |

**(a)** Calculate 1000 ÷ time for pH 8 and pH 9 as a measure of rate. **[2]**
**(b)** The value for pH 7 is 25. Calculate the percentage decrease in rate from pH 7 to pH 9. **[2]**
**(c)** State the best pH tested and suggest how to find the optimum more precisely. **[2]**

**10.** An enzyme is tested with different substrate concentrations.

| Substrate / mmol per dm³ | 0 | 10 | 20 | 40 | 80 | 160 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate / cm³ per min | 0 | 4.5 | 8.8 | 15.6 | 18.9 | 19.2 |

**(a)** Describe the pattern. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in rate when substrate concentration doubles from 20 to 40. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the pattern between 80 and 160. **[2]**

**11.** Plan an experiment to find how temperature changes the speed of oxygen production when catalase acts on hydrogen peroxide. **[8]**

**12.** A mutation changes one amino acid in an enzyme made by a bacterium. The normal enzyme forms 24 units of product per minute. The mutant forms 6 units per minute.
**(a)** (Higher Tier only) Explain how a change in the gene can change the enzyme's activity. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage decrease in activity. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why a different base change in the gene might have no effect on the enzyme. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** DNA is a polymer [1] made of nucleotides, in two strands [1] forming a double helix [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that name the monomer and say how many strands there are tend to gain credit; just "a double helix" is a shape, not a description of what it is made from.

**2. (a)** Each nucleotide has a sugar and a phosphate group [1], with one of four bases attached to the sugar [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Pair G with C, T with A, then C, C, A, G, T with G, G, T, C, A [1]: **CAGGTCA** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the answer as a single clear string. A line of letters that is hard to read can be misread, and a pairing slip such as C with A is a common way to lose both marks.

**3.** Guanine pairs with cytosine, so G = **31%** [1]. A + T = 100 - 62 = 38% [1]. They are equal, so A and T are each **19%** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the subtraction. Writing 38% for A plus T makes your use of the pairing rule visible to the reader.

**4.** The DNA double helix unzips around the gene [1]. A copy of the gene is made as mRNA [1] in the nucleus (transcription), matching the base sequence of the DNA [1]. The mRNA leaves the nucleus for the cytoplasm [1]. The mRNA is translated: tRNA brings amino acids and they are joined in the order the mRNA specifies [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the stage and where it happens. Answers that blur transcription and translation, or leave the nucleus out, tend to lose marks on this kind of sequence question.

**5. (a)** T→A, A→U, C→G gives AUG [1]; the rest give CCA GAA UUU, so **AUG CCA GAA UUU** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Looking up each triplet [1]: **Met - Pro - Glu - Phe** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Work in triplets and write each step. A swap of A for T instead of A for U in the mRNA is a frequent slip.

**6. (a)** CTA gives mRNA GAU, so the order is **Met - Pro - Asp - Phe** [1]. A different amino acid changes how the chain folds [1], so the active site shape changes and the substrate no longer fits [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** AAG gives UUC [1], which codes for Phe, the same amino acid as UUU, so the protein is unchanged [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the change step by step: base, then amino acid, then shape, then function. Stating only "the enzyme changes" skips the reasoning.

**7.** The enzyme has an active site of a specific shape [1]. Only a substrate with a complementary shape fits [1], like a key in a lock [1], so only that reaction is catalysed [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the word "complementary" or "matching" about the shape of the active site and the substrate. Saying the enzyme "changes to fit" is not the model asked for here.

**8.** The high temperature changes the shape of the enzyme [1], including the active site [1]. The enzyme is denatured, so the substrate no longer fits [1]. Cooling does not restore the shape, because denaturation is permanent [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use "denatured" and explain what it means. "Killed" is wrong, because enzymes are not living.

**9. (a)** pH 8: 1000 ÷ 80 = **12.5** [1]. pH 9: 1000 ÷ 200 = **5.0** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (25 - 5) ÷ 25 × 100 [1] = **80%** decrease [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** **pH 7** gives the shortest time [1]. Test more values close to it, such as pH 6.5 and 7.5 [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Give "the best value tested" rather than "the optimum" unless the data pin it down. Saying which values to test next shows you understand the limits of the data.

**10. (a)** The rate rises as substrate concentration increases [1], at a slowing rate, then levels off at about 19 cm³ per min [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (15.6 - 8.8) ÷ 8.8 × 100 [1] = **77%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** All the active sites are occupied [1], so extra substrate cannot be converted faster; the enzyme concentration is limiting [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Pair each description with a figure from the table and each explanation with active sites. Answers that say the rate "stops" are weaker than ones that say it levels off at a stated value.

**11.** Any eight from:
- Set up water baths at different temperatures, for example 15, 25, 35, 45 and 55 °C [1].
- Measure the volume of oxygen collected in a fixed time, using a gas syringe or measuring cylinder [1].
- Put the enzyme and the hydrogen peroxide into the bath separately until each reaches the temperature, then mix [1].
- Keep the hydrogen peroxide volume and concentration identical in every run [1].
- Use the same amount of catalase source, such as liver, and the same pH [1].
- Repeat each temperature and calculate a mean [1].
- Calculate rate = volume ÷ time, and plot rate against temperature [1].
- Wear eye protection because hydrogen peroxide can irritate [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Plans gain credit for being specific: a named instrument, a named control and a stated measurement. "Keep everything else the same" with nothing named is too vague.

**12. (a)** A change in a base alters a triplet, so a different amino acid may be added [1]. This changes how the protein folds, including the active site [1]. The substrate no longer fits as well, so the activity falls [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** (24 - 6) ÷ 24 × 100 [1] = **75%** decrease [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The new triplet might code for the same amino acid [1], or the changed amino acid might be far from the active site, leaving its shape unchanged [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* When a question asks for a percentage change, divide by the original value. Dividing by the new value is a frequent slip and gives a different figure.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing DNA as made of protein or sugar.
- Pairing bases incorrectly, or using T in mRNA.
- Mixing up transcription and translation, or forgetting where each happens.
- Skipping a link in the chain from gene to base sequence to amino acids to shape to function.
- Saying enzymes are "killed" rather than denatured.
- Saying cold temperatures denature enzymes.
- Forgetting that the rate levels off because active sites are occupied.
- Dividing by the wrong value when working out a percentage change.
- Leaving variables uncontrolled, or naming none, in a practical plan.
