## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by water being a metabolite. Name one reaction in which water is released. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why sodium ions dissolve in water but triglycerides do not. **[3]**

**3.** A mass of 8.0 kg of body water absorbs 100 kJ of heat. Specific heat capacity of water = 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the temperature rise. **[2]**
**(b)** A liquid with a specific heat capacity of 1.5 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹ replaced the water. Calculate the new temperature rise. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the value for water benefits enzymes in cells. **[2]**

**4.** A hiker must lose 600 kJ of heat through the skin by evaporation. Latent heat of vaporisation = 2.4 kJ g⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of water that must evaporate. **[2]**
**(b)** Only 80% of the sweat produced evaporates, the rest dripping off. Calculate the total mass of sweat produced. **[2]**

**5.** A leaf takes in 14 kJ of energy per hour from sunlight. In that hour it loses 5.4 g of water by evaporation. Latent heat of vaporisation = 2.4 kJ g⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the energy removed by evaporation. **[1]**
**(b)** Express this as a percentage of the energy taken in. **[1]**
**(c)** The stomata close. Predict the effect on leaf temperature and explain your answer. **[3]**

**6.** Explain how surface tension arises and why a small insect can stand on the surface of a pond. **[3]**

**7.** A shoot is attached to a potometer. The capillary tube has a radius of 0.40 mm. The water column moves 48 mm in 15 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of water taken up per minute. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how cohesion allows water to be pulled up through the xylem. **[2]**

**8.** The table shows the initial rate of an enzyme-catalysed reaction at six pH values.

| pH | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate (units) | 1 | 8 | 24 | 40 | 22 | 5 |

**(a)** Describe the effect of pH on the rate. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage decrease in rate from the highest rate to pH 8. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the change in rate between pH 6 and pH 3. **[2]**

**9.** A red blood cell contains 2.8 × 10⁸ haemoglobin molecules.

**(a)** Each haemoglobin has four haem groups. Calculate how many O₂ molecules the cell holds when fully loaded. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a diet low in iron can reduce the oxygen carried by blood. **[2]**

**10.** Glucose uptake by pieces of ileum wall was measured (nmol per mg of tissue per minute).

| Condition | Uptake |
|---|---|
| Sodium ions present | 42 |
| Sodium ions absent | 9 |
| Sodium ions present, pump inhibitor added | 14 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage reduction in uptake when sodium ions are absent. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why sodium ions are needed for glucose uptake. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the result with the pump inhibitor. **[2]**

**11.** A double-stranded DNA fragment contains 15 base pairs.

**(a)** State the number of phosphate groups, the number of phosphodiester bonds, and the number of water molecules released as the fragment formed. **[3]**
**(b)** State what happens to ATP when it is hydrolysed and what the released phosphate can do. **[2]**

**12.** A tall tree has plenty of water in the soil on a hot, sunny afternoon. Explain how the properties of water help the tree. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** A substance that takes part in, or is produced by, metabolic reactions [1]. Condensation, for example forming a peptide or glycosidic bond, releases water [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming a reaction type is safer than writing "respiration" alone; say what is formed or joined.

**2.** Sodium ions are charged, so the polar water molecules (δ− oxygen) surround them [1]. Triglycerides are non-polar, with no significant charge for water to interact with [1]. So ions dissolve while triglycerides do not [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to gain credit when they link polarity of the solute to water's polarity, rather than saying "oil repels water".

**3. (a)** 8.0 kg = 8 000 g and 100 kJ = 100 000 J [1]. 100 000 ÷ (8 000 × 4.18) = **2.99 °C** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 100 000 ÷ (8 000 × 1.5) = **8.33 °C** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Water's high heat capacity means a lot of energy is needed to change temperature, so temperature changes slowly [1]. Enzymes stay nearer their optimum temperature and are less likely to denature [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Unit conversion is often where working goes wrong; write the converted values down so a slip is visible.

**4. (a)** Mass = energy ÷ latent heat = 600 ÷ 2.4 [1] = **250 g** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Total = 250 ÷ 0.80 [1] = **313 g** (312.5) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), dividing by 0.80 is the step to show; multiplying by 0.80 would give less sweat than is needed.

**5. (a)** 5.4 × 2.4 = **13.0 kJ** (12.96) [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 12.96 ÷ 14 × 100 = **92.6%** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Leaf temperature rises [1]. Less water evaporates, so less energy is carried away by escaping molecules [1]. The energy absorbed from sunlight is no longer removed [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Predict" needs the direction of change as well as the reason; quote the figure from (b) to support it.

**6.** Water molecules at the surface are attracted to neighbours by hydrogen bonds (cohesion) [1]. They are pulled inwards and sideways but not upwards, so the surface acts like a stretched skin [1]. The insect's weight is spread over the surface and the skin supports it [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming cohesion and hydrogen bonds earns the first step; "surface tension" repeated without a cause is not an explanation.

**7. (a)** Volume = πr²l = π × 0.40² × 48 [1] = 24.1 mm³ [1]. Rate = 24.1 ÷ 15 = **1.61 mm³ min⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Hydrogen bonds make water molecules attract each other (cohesion) [1]. So water is pulled up as a continuous column without breaking [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Using the radius (0.40) and not a diameter is the usual trap in a volume question; state the formula first.

**8. (a)** Rate increases from pH 3 to a maximum of 40 units at pH 6 [1], then decreases at higher pH, to 5 at pH 8 [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (40 − 5) ÷ 40 × 100 [1] = **87.5%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Low pH means a high H⁺ concentration, which changes the charges on R groups and disrupts ionic and hydrogen bonds [1]. The tertiary structure and active site change shape, so fewer substrate molecules fit and the enzyme denatures [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote figures when describing; for (c) name the bonds and the active site, not just "enzyme stops working".

**9. (a)** One O₂ per haem group, so four per haemoglobin [1]. 2.8 × 10⁸ × 4 = **1.12 × 10⁹** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Haem groups contain Fe²⁺ ions, which bind oxygen [1]. Less iron means less haemoglobin is made, so less oxygen is carried [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Linking iron to the haem group in haemoglobin is more precise than "iron carries oxygen".

**10. (a)** (42 − 9) ÷ 42 × 100 [1] = **78.6%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Sodium ions enter the cell down a gradient through a co-transporter, bringing glucose with them [1]. Glucose is therefore absorbed against its concentration gradient, which does not happen without sodium ions [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The sodium-potassium pump normally removes Na⁺ from the cell, keeping its concentration low [1]. Inhibiting it lets Na⁺ build up, the gradient is lost, and co-transport falls [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention the concentration gradient of sodium ions in (c); saying only "the pump stopped" is incomplete.

**11. (a)** 30 phosphate groups [1], 28 phosphodiester bonds (2 × 14) [1], 28 water molecules [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** ATP is hydrolysed to ADP and inorganic phosphate (Pi), catalysed by ATP hydrolase [1]. The phosphate can phosphorylate other compounds, often making them more reactive [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Count bonds per strand (14 each), not nucleotides; both strands then give 28.

**12.** Any six from: strong cohesion holds water as a continuous column in the xylem, so it is pulled up as one chain [1]; water is a solvent, so mineral ions are carried dissolved [1]; evaporation from leaves breaks hydrogen bonds and takes energy away [1]; high latent heat means a lot of cooling for little water lost [1]; high heat capacity means cell temperature changes slowly [1]; water is a metabolite used in photosynthesis [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* For six marks, each property should be paired with a role in the tree; a list of properties alone gains less.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Converting kJ to J or kg to g incorrectly in energy calculations.
- Using the diameter, not the radius, in a volume calculation.
- Explaining cooling without saying that energy is carried away by escaping molecules.
- Describing pH changes as "acid" with no reference to H⁺, R groups and active site shape.
- Writing that iron "carries oxygen" with no mention of the haem group.
- Saying sodium ions "pull" glucose in without a concentration gradient.
- Counting nucleotides when the question asks for bonds.
- Quoting a property with no biological role.
