## Section A

**1.** P(B) = 0.6, P(A | B) = 0.2 and P(A | B′) = 0.5.

**(a)** Find P(A). **[2]**

**(b)** Find P(B | A). **[2]**

**2.** A bag holds one fair coin and one coin with heads on both sides. A coin is chosen at random and
tossed twice. It shows heads both times. Find the probability that it is the double-headed coin. **[4]**

**3.** A box holds three dice: one numbered 1 to 4, one numbered 1 to 6 and one numbered 1 to 8, each
fair. A die is chosen at random and rolled, and it shows a 1. Find the probability that it was the
die numbered 1 to 4. **[4]**

**4.** 40% of the members of a running club train every day. A member who trains every day finishes
a particular race in under an hour with probability 0.8; a member who does not finishes in under an
hour with probability 0.25. A member finished in under an hour. Find the probability that this member
trains every day. **[4]**

**5.** P(B) = p, P(A | B) = 0.9 and P(A | B′) = 0.2. Given that P(B | A) = 0.5, find the value of p.
**[4]**

## Section B

**6.** A factory has three production lines. Line A makes 50% of the items, line B 30% and line C 20%.
The proportions of faulty items are 3% from A, 5% from B and 8% from C.

**(a)** Find the probability that a randomly chosen item is faulty. **[2]**

**(b)** An item is faulty. Find the probability that it was made on line C. **[2]**

**(c)** An item is not faulty. Find the probability that it was made on line A. **[3]**

**7.** A condition affects 1% of a population. A screening test gives a positive result for 98% of
people with the condition and for 3% of people without it.

**(a)** Find the probability that a randomly chosen person tests positive. **[2]**

**(b)** A person tests positive. Find the probability that they have the condition. **[2]**

**(c)** The test is used instead in a clinic where 10% of patients have the condition. Find the
probability that a patient who tests positive has the condition. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why the answers to (b) and (c) are so different. **[1]**

**8.** A commuter chooses route A with probability 1/2, route B with probability 1/3 and route C with
probability 1/6. The probabilities of arriving late are 1/10 on route A, 1/5 on route B and 2/5 on
route C.

**(a)** Find the probability that the commuter is late. **[2]**

**(b)** Given that the commuter is late, find the probability that route A was used. **[2]**

**(c)** Given that the commuter is on time, find the probability that route C was used. **[3]**

**9.** Box 1 holds 4 red and 6 blue balls. Box 2 holds 2 red and 8 blue balls. A fair coin is tossed:
heads, box 1 is used; tails, box 2. Two balls are taken from the chosen box without replacement, and
both are red. Find the probability that box 1 was used. **[5]**

**10.** In a town it rains on 20% of days. A forecast predicts rain on 90% of the days on which it
rains and on 15% of the days on which it does not.

**(a)** Find the probability that it rains, given that rain was forecast. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the probability that it rains, given that rain was not forecast. **[3]**

**11.** Events B₁, B₂ and B₃ are mutually exclusive and exhaustive, with P(B₁) = 0.2, P(B₂) = 0.5 and
P(B₃) = 0.3. For an event A, P(A | B₁) = 0.6, P(A | B₂) = 0.1 and P(A | B₃) = k. Given that
P(B₁ | A) = 0.4, find k. **[5]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** P(A) = 0.6 × 0.2 + 0.4 × 0.5 [1] = 0.12 + 0.2 = 0.32 [1]

**(b)** P(B | A) = 0.12/0.32 [1] = 3/8 [1]. **[4]**

**2.** Fair coin: (1/2)(1/4) = 1/8 [1]. Double-headed: (1/2)(1) = 1/2 [1].
P(two heads) = 1/8 + 1/2 = 5/8 [1]. P(double | two heads) = (1/2)/(5/8) = 4/5 [1]. **[4]**

**3.** Branches: (1/3)(1/4) = 1/12, (1/3)(1/6) = 1/18, (1/3)(1/8) = 1/24 [1].
P(1) = 6/72 + 4/72 + 3/72 = 13/72 [1]. P(four-sided | 1) = (6/72)/(13/72) [1] = 6/13 [1]. **[4]**

**4.** Daily and under an hour: 0.4 × 0.8 = 0.32 [1]. Not daily and under an hour:
0.6 × 0.25 = 0.15 [1]. Total 0.47 [1]. P(daily | under an hour) = 0.32/0.47 = 32/47 [1]. **[4]**

**5.** 0.5 = 0.9p/(0.9p + 0.2(1 − p)) [1]. So 0.9p = 0.45p + 0.1 − 0.1p [1], giving 0.55p = 0.1 [1]
and p = 2/11 [1]. **[4]**

**6. (a)** 0.5 × 0.03 + 0.3 × 0.05 + 0.2 × 0.08 [1] = 0.015 + 0.015 + 0.016 = 0.046 [1]

**(b)** 0.016/0.046 [1] = 0.348 (3 s.f.) [1]

**(c)** P(not faulty) = 0.954 [1]; P(A and not faulty) = 0.5 × 0.97 = 0.485 [1];
0.485/0.954 = 0.508 (3 s.f.) [1]. **[7]**

**7. (a)** 0.01 × 0.98 + 0.99 × 0.03 [1] = 0.0098 + 0.0297 = 0.0395 [1]

**(b)** 0.0098/0.0395 [1] = 0.248 (3 s.f.) [1]

**(c)** 0.1 × 0.98 = 0.098 and 0.9 × 0.03 = 0.027 [1]; 0.098/0.125 = 0.784 [1]

**(d)** The condition is far rarer in (b), so most positive results come from the large group without
the condition (false positives), which lowers the probability [1]. **[7]**

**8. (a)** (1/2)(1/10) + (1/3)(1/5) + (1/6)(2/5) [1] = 3/60 + 4/60 + 4/60 = 11/60 [1]

**(b)** (3/60)/(11/60) [1] = 3/11 [1]

**(c)** P(on time) = 49/60 [1]; P(C and on time) = (1/6)(3/5) = 1/10 [1];
(1/10)/(49/60) = 6/49 [1]. **[7]**

**9.** Box 1, both red: (1/2)(4/10)(3/9) = 1/15 [2]. Box 2, both red: (1/2)(2/10)(1/9) = 1/90 [1].
P(both red) = 6/90 + 1/90 = 7/90 [1]. P(box 1 | both red) = (6/90)/(7/90) = 6/7 [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** Rain and forecast: 0.2 × 0.9 = 0.18; dry and forecast: 0.8 × 0.15 = 0.12 [1].
P(forecast) = 0.30 [1]. P(rain | forecast) = 0.18/0.30 = 0.6 [1].

**(b)** Rain and no forecast: 0.2 × 0.1 = 0.02 [1]; P(no forecast) = 0.70 [1];
P(rain | no forecast) = 0.02/0.70 = 0.0286 (3 s.f.) [1]. **[6]**

**11.** P(A) = 0.2 × 0.6 + 0.5 × 0.1 + 0.3k = 0.17 + 0.3k [2]. P(B₁ | A) = 0.12/(0.17 + 0.3k) = 0.4
[1]. So 0.17 + 0.3k = 0.3 [1], and k = 0.13/0.3 = 13/30 (0.433) [1]. **[5]**

## How this set builds

Questions 1 to 5 are Paper 1 style: exact fractions, two and three events, and an unknown prior.
Questions 6 to 10 put the method into contexts where it is easy to pick the wrong branch: the observed
event is sometimes the complement (6(c), 8(c), 10(b)), and question 7 shows how strongly the answer
depends on how common the starting event is. Question 11 reverses the process to find an unknown
conditional.
