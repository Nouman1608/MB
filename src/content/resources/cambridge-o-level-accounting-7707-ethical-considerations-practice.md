These questions cover section 7.2 Ethical considerations of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. Revise first with the [ethical considerations study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-ethical-considerations/) and the [ethical considerations revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-ethical-considerations-revision-notes/). All people, businesses and figures are invented, and amounts are in dollars.

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** State **three** of the fundamental principles of the ethical framework in accounting. **[3]**

**2.** Explain **one** reason why accounting needs an ethical framework. **[2]**

**3.** An accountant must decide whether a debt owed by her close friend's business is irrecoverable. Which principle is most at risk?

A: integrity

B: objectivity

C: confidentiality

D: professional competence and due care

**[1]**

**4.** Explain what the principle of confidentiality requires of an accountant. **[2]**

**5.** Identify the principle breached in each case.

**(a)** A book-keeper accepts a job preparing statements for a type of business she has never studied and does not check the rules. **[1]**

**(b)** An accountant posts false comments online claiming that a rival firm cheats its clients. **[1]**

**6.** State **two** stakeholders who could be harmed if a business's accounts were falsified. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Lina keeps the books of Crestwood Builders. The owner asks her to record $8,400 of building materials, used to extend his own house, as business repairs. The draft profit, with the materials charged as repairs, is $61,200. The owner says this "will save tax".

**(a)** Calculate the profit for the year if the materials are recorded correctly. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the accounting concept that requires the correct treatment. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain **two** fundamental principles Lina would breach if she agreed. **[4]**

**(d)** Explain the significance to the tax authority and to society of Lina refusing. **[2]**

**8.** Jasper's trade receivables of $28,000 include $3,500 owed by a customer who has been declared bankrupt. Jasper is applying for a bank loan next week and asks his book-keeper not to write the debt off until afterwards. His draft profit is $22,000. Current assets are $42,000, including the trade receivables, and current liabilities are $24,000.

**(a)** Calculate the corrected profit for the year. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the current ratio before and after writing off the debt. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain the ethical issue for the book-keeper and how she should respond. **[4]**

**9.** A new assistant at an accounting firm is asked to prepare a partnership appropriation account without any training. He adds interest on drawings to the partners' share of profit instead of deducting it, and sends the account to the client without review.

**(a)** Identify the fundamental principle not applied. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain **two** ways the firm and the assistant could uphold this principle. **[4]**

**10.** Farid is the accountant of a manufacturing company. A supplier offers him a free family holiday a week before he recommends whether the supplier's contract should be renewed.

**(a)** Identify the fundamental principle at risk. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why this principle is at risk. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest what Farid should do. **[2]**

**11.** The tax authority, using its legal powers, asks an accountant for copies of a client's sales records. The client asks the accountant to refuse because the records are confidential. Explain whether providing the records would breach the principle of confidentiality. **[3]**

**12.** "Ethics only matter in large companies. The book-keeper of a small sole trader business does not need an ethical framework." Discuss this statement. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three: integrity [1]; objectivity [1]; professional competence and due care [1]; confidentiality; professional behaviour. **[3]**

**2.** Any one, explained: users such as banks rely on accounting figures they cannot check for themselves [1], so they need to be sure the figures are honest [1]; accountants may face pressure from owners to change figures, and a framework sets the standard in advance whoever is asking. **[2]**

**3.** B [1]. **[1]**

**4.** The accountant must not disclose information gained through work to others [1], or use it for personal advantage, unless there is a legal or professional duty to disclose it [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Professional competence and due care [1].

**(b)** Professional behaviour [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two: banks or lenders [1]; investors [1]; suppliers; employees; the tax authority; customers. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 61,200 + 8,400 [1] = **$69,600** [1].

**(b)** Business entity [1].

**(c)** Integrity [1]: Lina would knowingly record a private expense as a business cost, making the records false [1]. Professional behaviour [1]: helping the owner reduce tax using false records could break the law and damage trust in accountants [1]. (Also credit objectivity: giving in to the owner's pressure.)

**(d)** The tax authority collects tax on the true profit of $69,600, not $61,200 [1], so the tax that funds public services is not reduced by false records, and trust in accounting information is maintained [1]. **[9]**

**8. (a)** 22,000 - 3,500 = **$18,500** [1].

**(b)** Before: 42,000 / 24,000 = **1.75 : 1** [1]. After: 38,500 / 24,000 = **1.60 : 1** [1].

**(c)** Delaying the write-off would overstate profit by $3,500 and make liquidity look better, misleading the bank [1]; this would breach integrity because the figures would be knowingly false [1] and objectivity because the decision would be made under pressure [1]. She should explain that prudence requires the debt to be written off now and record it, producing only correct figures for the bank [1]. **[7]**

**9. (a)** Professional competence and due care [1].

**(b)** Any two, each explained: the firm should train the assistant before giving him the task [1], so that he has the knowledge needed [1]; his work should be reviewed by an experienced accountant before it is sent [1], so that errors are found [1]; the assistant should say he does not know how to do the task and ask for guidance, instead of guessing. **[5]**

**10. (a)** Objectivity [1].

**(b)** The gift creates a personal benefit linked to the supplier [1], so Farid's recommendation could be biased in its favour instead of being based on price and quality [1].

**(c)** Refuse the holiday [1] and tell his manager about the offer, or ask someone else to make the recommendation [1]. **[5]**

**11.** Confidentiality forbids disclosing work information without a legal or professional duty [1]. Here the tax authority is using its legal powers, so there is a legal duty to provide the records [1]. Providing them does not breach confidentiality, although the accountant should give only what is required [1]. **[3]**

**12.** For the statement: a small business has fewer outside users, such as shareholders [1]. Against: a sole trader still deals with banks, suppliers and the tax authority, who rely on its figures [1]. The book-keeper may face direct pressure from the owner, with no one else to turn to, so clear standards matter even more [1]. Private information, such as customers' details and the owner's income, must still be kept confidential [1]. Many small businesses failing because of false figures would harm suppliers, employees and trust in accounts generally [1]. Conclusion: the statement is wrong; the principles apply to anyone preparing accounting information, whatever the size of the business [1]. **[6]**
