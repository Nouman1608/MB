AQA A-Level English Literature B (7717) is linear and not tiered, with two written papers and a non-exam assessment: Paper 1 Literary genres (2 hours 30 minutes, closed book, 75 marks, 40%), Paper 2 Texts and genres (3 hours, open book, 75 marks, 40%) and the non-exam assessment Theory and independence (50 marks, 20%), 200 marks in total. This is the A-level (7717), not the AS (7716), with first A-level exams in 2027. Use the plan alongside our pages on [Paper 1 Literary genres](/resources/a-level-aqa-english-literature-b-literary-genres/), [Paper 2 Texts and genres](/resources/a-level-aqa-english-literature-b-texts-and-genres/) and [the Theory and independence NEA](/resources/aqa-a-level-english-literature-b-theory-and-independence/).

## Paper 1: Literary genres

Paper 1 is 2 hours 30 minutes (150 minutes), closed book, 75 marks and 40% of the A-level. You choose either Option 1A, Aspects of tragedy, or Option 1B, Aspects of comedy, and study one Shakespeare text, a second drama text and one further text, one of which must be pre-1900. The paper has three sections of 25 marks each: Section A is a Shakespeare passage-based question, Section B is a Shakespeare essay, and Section C is a texts-linking essay. **How to spend the time:** 150 minutes over 75 marks is 2 minutes per mark, so each 25-mark section deserves about 50 minutes. Because the paper is closed book, key quotations must be learned in advance.

## Paper 2: Texts and genres

Paper 2 is 3 hours (180 minutes), open book, 75 marks and 40% of the A-level. You choose Option 2A, Elements of crime writing, or Option 2B, Elements of political and social protest writing, and study one post-2000 prose text, one poetry text and one further text, one of which is pre-1900. There is also an unseen passage. The sections are again 25 marks each: Section A is an unseen-passage question, Section B is a set-text essay, and Section C is a texts-connecting essay. **How to spend the time:** 180 / 75 = 2.4 minutes per mark, so about 60 minutes per section. Open book does not mean unprepared: annotate your texts to find passages quickly, and keep your response focused on the question rather than copying material out.

## Non-exam assessment: Theory and independence

The non-exam assessment is worth 50 marks and 20% of the A-level. It consists of two essays of 1,250 to 1,500 words each on two texts, informed by the Critical Anthology, and one of the two may be re-creative with a commentary. It is teacher-assessed and moderated by AQA. **How to approach it:** treat it as a drafting project over weeks, not a revision task. Keep to the word range, make the critical perspectives you use explicit, and plan time to redraft with your teacher's general guidance within the rules your centre follows.

## Timing and marks-per-minute

- Paper 1: 150 / 75 = 2.0 minutes per mark; 25 marks x 2.0 = 50 minutes per section; 3 x 50 = 150.
- Paper 2: 180 / 75 = 2.4 minutes per mark; 25 marks x 2.4 = 60 minutes per section; 3 x 60 = 180.
- Weightings: 40 + 40 + 20 = 100 per cent; marks: 75 + 75 + 50 = 200.
- Allow roughly 5 minutes in each section for planning before you write.

## Question styles and command words

Literature questions generally ask you to explore, analyse or evaluate how writers use form, structure and language, and to connect texts through the genre in question. Work with the assessment objectives your teacher gives you: close reading, critical interpretation, context and comparison. For the passage-based and unseen questions, begin with the language in front of you before bringing in wider knowledge.

## Worked practice scenario: a plan and a model paragraph

**Question (original, Option 1A style):** "Explore how a dramatist presents the consequences of ambition in a tragedy you have studied."

**Plan:**

```
Argument : ambition drives the protagonist's rise and is also the cause of the fall
Point 1  : early confidence - language of aspiration and certainty
Point 2  : turning point - the structure shifts from action to consequence
Point 3  : late isolation - shorter, bleaker lines and fewer allies on stage
Link     : genre - tragedy as the working-out of a flaw
Close    : the consequences are internal as well as external
```

**Model paragraph (illustrative, using the well-known dagger soliloquy from Macbeth; replace with your own set text):** The dramatist presents ambition as a force that overrides the protagonist's moral judgement. When Macbeth asks "Is this a dagger which I see before me," the imagery of a hallucinated weapon suggests that his ambition has begun to distort perception itself. The question form enacts uncertainty, yet the soliloquy ends in resolve, showing how ambition converts doubt into action. This structural movement, from hesitation to commitment, sets up the later consequences and places the character on the path of tragic downfall.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Know whether you are answering Option 1A or 1B and 2A or 2B.
- For Paper 1, learn short quotations by theme, because it is closed book.
- For Paper 2, annotate your texts with tabs and short margin notes.
- Practise one timed section at a time, then a full paper.
- Write a short plan for each essay.
- Keep the genre question in view in the linking and connecting essays.
- Check the word range for the non-exam assessment before submission.
- Leave time to reread your conclusion.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many components are there in AQA A-Level English Literature B 7717?**
There are three: Paper 1 Literary genres (75 marks, 40%), Paper 2 Texts and genres (75 marks, 40%) and a non-exam assessment (50 marks, 20%), 200 marks in total.

**Is Paper 1 open book?**
No. Paper 1 is closed book and lasts 2 hours 30 minutes. Paper 2 is open book and lasts 3 hours.

**What are the options on each paper?**
Paper 1 offers Option 1A, Aspects of tragedy, or Option 1B, Aspects of comedy. Paper 2 offers Option 2A, Elements of crime writing, or Option 2B, Elements of political and social protest writing.

**How is the non-exam assessment marked?**
It is teacher-assessed and moderated by AQA, and consists of two essays of 1,250 to 1,500 words, one of which may be re-creative with a commentary.

**When are the first A-level exams?**
The first A-level exams for this updated specification are in 2027.
