**Major Teachings in the Hadiths of the Prophet is Paper 2 Question 1 of Cambridge IGCSE Islamiyat
0493.** The syllabus sets twenty Hadiths, all recorded by al-Bukhari or Muslim, about individual
conduct and life in the community. Four are printed in the exam in Arabic and English and you write
about two, explaining what each teaches Muslims to believe and how to act, and how Muslims can put
that teaching into practice today.

## Syllabus coverage

**0493 Paper 2, Topic 1: Major teachings in the Hadiths of the Prophet (2026–2027 syllabus)**
- Two groups of passages for close study: individual conduct and life in the community
- The teachings each passage contains about what Muslims should believe and how they should act
- Study with reference to the original Arabic as well as the English translation
- Exam: four passages set, candidates choose two (8 marks for the question)

## How should you answer a Hadith question?

For each Hadith chosen, write two paragraphs:

1. **Teaching.** Explain what the Hadith means in your own words, with reference to its key phrase.
2. **Application.** Show how a Muslim today can act on it, with a concrete example, and support it
   with a related verse of the Qur'an or another Hadith if you can.

Examiners reward answers that go beyond paraphrase. A good test: could your example of putting the
Hadith into practice only apply to *this* Hadith? If so, it is specific enough.

## What do the Hadiths teach about individual conduct?

**"Religion is sincerity" (to Allah, His Book, His Messenger, the leaders and the common people).**
Faith is not only ritual but sincere loyalty in every relationship. Sincerity to Allah means
worshipping Him alone with pure intention; to His Book, reading and following the Qur'an; to the
Messenger, following the Sunnah; to leaders, giving honest advice and support; to ordinary people,
wishing them well and treating them honestly.

**The man who asks whether obligatory prayer, fasting in Ramadan and observing the lawful and
forbidden are enough for paradise, and is told "Yes".** Islam is achievable: faithfully carrying
out the obligations and keeping within the limits of halal and haram is the foundation of salvation.
Muslims should not neglect the basics, and should not make religion harder than Allah has made it.

**"Every joint must perform a charity every day."** Charity (sadaqa) is not only money: judging
fairly between two people, helping someone onto a mount or with luggage, a kind word, each step to
prayer and removing something harmful from the road are all charity. Muslims can give thanks for
their health through small daily acts, such as helping a neighbour carry shopping.

**"No one eats better food than that which he eats from the work of his hand."** Honest work is
dignified and earning a lawful living is praised. Muslims should avoid begging when able to work,
and should earn by halal means.

**"He who studies the Qur'an is like the owner of tethered camels."** Knowledge of the Qur'an must be
maintained by regular recitation and revision or it will be lost, just as untied camels wander off.

**"Modesty produces nothing but good."** Modesty (haya) in speech, dress and behaviour protects a
person from wrongdoing and earns respect.

**"Faith as small as a mustard seed … pride as small as a mustard seed."** Even a little faith saves
from hell, while even a little arrogance blocks paradise. Muslims should be humble and never look
down on others.

**"The world is the believer's prison and the unbeliever's paradise."** A believer accepts limits on
desires in this life and looks to the hereafter, so worldly hardship is temporary.

**"God does not look at your forms and possessions but at your hearts and deeds."** Appearance,
wealth and status do not matter to Allah; intention and action do. Muslims should judge others by
character, not by looks or riches.

## What do the Hadiths teach about life in the community?

**"None of you believes until he wants for his brother what he wants for himself."** True faith
includes empathy and wishing others well. It rules out envy and selfishness and encourages fair
dealing in business, school and family life.

**"Speak good or keep silent … be generous to your neighbour … be generous to your guest."** Belief in
Allah and the Last Day shows itself in controlled speech and in hospitality. Muslims should avoid
gossip and hurtful words and look after neighbours and guests.

**"Whoever sees an evil, let him change it with his hand … tongue … heart."** Muslims are
responsible for the moral health of society: act if able, speak out if not, and at least hate the
wrong inwardly, though this is the weakest level of faith.

**"The most excellent of men is the believer who strives in the way of Allah with his person and his
property."** Striving (jihad in its wide sense) includes using one's time, effort and wealth for
good causes.

**The many kinds of martyr.** Martyrdom is wider than death in battle: dying naturally, or of plague
or cholera, while striving in the way of Allah also counts. This consoles the bereaved and shows
Allah's generosity.

**"One who manages the affairs of the widow and the poor is like one who strives in the way of
Allah."** Caring for vulnerable people is as valuable as worship such as night prayer and voluntary
fasting.

**"I and the one who brings up an orphan will be in paradise like this."** Caring for orphans earns
closeness to the Prophet (pbuh) in paradise; the Prophet was himself an orphan.

**"Be gentle and do not be hard; cause rejoicing and do not alienate."** Said when sending Abu Musa
and Mu'adh ibn Jabal to govern parts of Yemen: leaders and teachers should make religion easy and
attractive, not harsh.

**"May God show mercy to a man who is kindly when he sells, buys and demands repayment."** Islam
values fairness and gentleness in trade and debt.

**"God will not show mercy to him who does not show mercy to others."** Mercy to people and animals
is a condition of receiving Allah's mercy.

**"The believers are like a single man."** The Muslim community is one body: the suffering of one
part is felt by all, so Muslims should support those in need, at home and abroad.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which collections are these Hadiths from?**
The syllabus states that all of them are recorded by
al-Bukhari or by Muslim in their Sahih collections, many by both.

**Do I need to know which group a Hadith belongs to?**
The syllabus organises them under individual
conduct and life in the community; knowing the theme helps you explain the teaching, but the
marks come from explaining meaning and application.

**How long should each answer be?**
Two well-developed paragraphs per Hadith, one on the teaching
and one on its application today.
