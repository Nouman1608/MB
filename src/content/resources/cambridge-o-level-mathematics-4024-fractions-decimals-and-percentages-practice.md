These questions cover subtopic 1.4 Fractions, decimals and percentages of Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024, 2025 to 2027. All are non-calculator, as they could appear on Paper 1. Give fractions in their simplest form. Use dot notation for recurring decimals.

## Section A

**1.** Write 0.625 as a fraction in its simplest form. **[2]**

**2.** Write 9/25 as a decimal and as a percentage. **[2]**

**3.** Write 7/12 as a recurring decimal. **[2]**

**4.** **(a)** Write 41/6 as a mixed number. **[1]**

**(b)** Write 4 3/7 as an improper fraction. **[1]**

**5.** Write 175% as a decimal and as a fraction in its simplest form. **[2]**

**6.** Write 0.5̇4̇ as a fraction in its simplest form. **[2]**

**7.** Without a calculator, decide which of these fractions give terminating decimals. Give a reason for each choice.

11/80 7/15 9/24 5/18 **[4]**

## Section B

**8.** Alisha scores 35 marks out of 40 in a test. Write her score as a fraction in its simplest form, as a decimal and as a percentage. **[3]**

**9.** Work out 5/8 + 0.3 and give your answer as a percentage. **[2]**

**10.** Write 0.26̇ as a fraction in its simplest form. Show your working. **[3]**

**11.** Show that 0.2̇7̇ = 3/11. **[3]**

**12.** Write 0.14̇5̇ (which is 0.1454545...) as a fraction in its simplest form. **[3]**

**13.** **(a)** Write 3.7̇ as an improper fraction. **[2]**

**(b)** Write 1/7 as a recurring decimal, using dots to show the repeating digits. You are told that 1 ÷ 7 = 0.142857142857... **[2]**

**14.** Explain, using prime factors, why 13/40 gives a terminating decimal, and write it as a decimal. **[3]**

**15.** Write 0.0̇9̇ (which is 0.090909...) as a fraction in its simplest form. **[2]**

**16.** In a survey, 0.35 of the people chose tea and 2/5 chose coffee. Everyone else chose juice. What percentage chose juice? **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** 625/1000 **[1]**; **5/8** **[1]** **[2]**

**2.** 9/25 = 36/100, so **0.36** **[1]** and **36%** **[1]** **[2]**

**3.** 7 ÷ 12 = 0.58333... **[1]**; **0.583̇** **[1]** **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Only the 3 repeats. The dot goes over the 3, not over the 8.

**4. (a)** 41 ÷ 6 = 6 remainder 5, so **6 5/6** **[1]**

**(b)** 4 × 7 + 3 = 31, so **31/7** **[1]** **[2]**

**5.** **1.75** **[1]**; 175/100 = **7/4** **[1]** **[2]**

**6.** Let x = 0.5454...; 100x = 54.5454... **[1]**; 99x = 54, so x = 54/99 = **6/11** **[1]** **[2]**

**7.** 11/80: 80 = 2⁴ × 5, so **terminates** **[1]**; 7/15: 15 = 3 × 5 includes 3, so recurs **[1]**; 9/24 = 3/8, with 8 = 2³, so **terminates** **[1]**; 5/18: 18 = 2 × 3², includes 3, so recurs **[1]** **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Simplify 9/24 first. Its denominator before cancelling includes a 3, but the simplified fraction does not.

**8.** 35/40 = **7/8** **[1]**; **0.875** **[1]**; **87.5%** **[1]** **[3]**

**9.** 5/8 = 0.625 **[1]**; 0.625 + 0.3 = 0.925 = **92.5%** **[1]** **[2]**

**10.** Let x = 0.2666...; 10x = 2.666... and 100x = 26.666... **[1]**; 90x = 26.666... - 2.666... = 24 **[1]**; x = 24/90 = **4/15** **[1]** **[3]**

**11.** Let x = 0.2727...; 100x = 27.2727... **[1]**; 99x = 27 **[1]**; x = 27/99 = **3/11** **[1]** **[3]**

**12.** Let x = 0.1454545...; 10x = 1.454545... and 1000x = 145.4545... **[1]**; 990x = 145.4545... - 1.4545... = 144 **[1]**; x = 144/990 = **8/55** **[1]** **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Two digits repeat, so the second multiplier is 1000: one place for the digit that does not repeat, and two more for the block.

**13. (a)** 0.7̇ = 7/9 **[1]**; 3 + 7/9 = **34/9** **[1]**

**(b)** **0.1̇42857̇** **[2]**: one mark for dots on the first and last digits of the block, and one for the correct block 142857 **[4]**

**14.** 40 = 2³ × 5 **[1]**; the only prime factors are 2 and 5, so the decimal terminates **[1]**; 13/40 = **0.325** **[1]** **[3]**

**15.** Let x = 0.0909...; 100x = 9.0909... **[1]**; 99x = 9, so x = 9/99 = **1/11** **[1]** **[2]**

**16.** 0.35 = 35% and 2/5 = 40% **[1]**; 35 + 40 = 75 **[1]**; juice = 100 - 75 = **25%** **[1]** **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting the dot over the wrong digit in Question 3.
- Not cancelling 625/1000 to lowest terms in Question 1.
- Forgetting to simplify 9/24 before deciding in Question 7.
- Using 99 as the denominator in Questions 10 and 12, when digits come before the repeating block.
- Writing the answer to Question 8 as 0.875 only, without the fraction and percentage.
- Leaving 54/99 uncancelled in Question 6.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is 0.9̇ exactly equal to 1?**
Yes. If x = 0.999..., then 10x = 9.999..., so 9x = 9 and x = 1.

**Can a calculator tell me whether a decimal recurs?**
Not reliably. The display is rounded, so look at the denominator of the simplified fraction instead.
