## Questions

**1.** State three structural features found in all viruses. **[3]**

**2.** Compare and contrast the structure of bacteriophage lambda and HIV. **[3]**

**3.** Coronaviruses have an envelope with spike proteins. Explain the role of these structures in infecting a host cell, and where the envelope comes from. **[2]**

**4.** Outline the stages of the lytic cycle of bacteriophage lambda. **[4]**

**5.** Distinguish between the lytic and lysogenic cycles in terms of what happens to the viral DNA and to the host cell. **[2]**

**6.** Explain how lambda DNA can be passed to millions of bacterial cells without any new virions being made. **[2]**

**7.** Bacteria were infected with phages, and free phages were counted at intervals. At the start, 5.0 × 10⁵ cells per mL were each infected by one phage.

| Time / min | 0 | 15 | 25 | 35 | 45 | 55 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Free phages per mL | 5.0 × 10⁵ | 5.0 × 10⁵ | 5.0 × 10⁵ | 2.4 × 10⁷ | 6.0 × 10⁷ | 6.0 × 10⁷ |

**(a)** Explain why the count did not change for the first 25 minutes. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mean number of phages released per infected cell. **[2]**

**8.** A culture of bacteria carrying a prophage was exposed to ultraviolet light, and many cells lysed within an hour. Explain this observation. **[2]**

**9.** Evaluate the evidence that viruses have more than one origin. **[3]**

**10.** Explain how a new strain of influenza with very different surface proteins can arise in a single step. **[2]**

**11.** A patient with HIV is treated with a single antiretroviral drug. After some months the drug stops working. Explain why. **[3]**

**12.** In a population of HIV, 1 virion in 10⁴ is resistant to drug A and 1 in 10⁶ is resistant to drug B. Resistance to the two drugs arises independently.

**(a)** Calculate the proportion of virions resistant to both drugs. **[1]**

**(b)** A patient carries 10⁹ virions. Calculate the expected number resistant to both drugs. **[1]**

**(c)** Use your answers to explain why drugs are given in combination. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three: small, fixed size [1]; nucleic acid (DNA or RNA) as genetic material [1]; protein capsid [1]; no cytoplasm; few or no enzymes **[3]**

**2.** Similarity: both have a protein capsid around their genetic material [1]. Differences: lambda has double-stranded DNA whereas HIV has single-stranded RNA [1]; HIV has an envelope from host membrane (and carries reverse transcriptase) whereas lambda has no envelope and has a tail [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* "Compare and contrast" needs at least one similarity; an answer with differences only loses a mark.

**3.** Spike proteins bind to specific receptors on the host cell surface, allowing the virus to attach and enter [1]; the envelope is host cell membrane taken when the virus budded out of its previous host cell [1] **[2]**

**4.** The tail attaches to a specific receptor on the bacterium [1]. DNA is injected while the capsid stays outside [1]. Host ribosomes and enzymes make viral proteins and copy the viral DNA [1]. New phages assemble and the cell lyses, releasing them [1] **[4]**

**5.** In the lytic cycle viral DNA stays separate and is copied many times, whereas in the lysogenic cycle it is integrated into the host chromosome [1]; the lytic cycle destroys the host cell, whereas in the lysogenic cycle the host survives and divides [1] **[2]**

**6.** The DNA is integrated into the bacterial chromosome as a prophage, and a repressor keeps lytic genes switched off [1]; each time the bacterium divides the prophage is copied with the chromosome, so every descendant carries it [1] **[2]**

**7. (a)** New phages were being assembled inside the cells, but none had been released because the cells had not yet lysed [1]

**(b)** Released = 6.0 × 10⁷ − 5.0 × 10⁵ = 5.95 × 10⁷ per mL [1]; 5.95 × 10⁷ ÷ 5.0 × 10⁵ = **119 per cell** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Subtract the phages present at the start; they are not part of the burst.

**8.** Ultraviolet light damages the bacterial DNA, which triggers induction [1]; the prophage is cut out of the chromosome and the lytic cycle begins, so cells burst [1] **[2]**

**9.** Viruses are extremely diverse in genome type and structure, which is hard to explain from one origin [1]. Their shared features are those any obligate parasite would need, so they could be convergent rather than inherited [1]. However, all viruses use the same genetic code as cells, so the evidence supports origins from cellular life but cannot show how many times this happened [1] **[3]**

**10.** The influenza genome is in separate segments [1]; if two strains infect the same cell, segments can mix, giving a virus with a new combination of surface protein genes [1] **[2]**

**11.** HIV reverse transcriptase makes frequent copying errors, so many variants form [1]. By chance some carry mutations giving resistance to the drug [1]. The drug kills susceptible virions, so resistant ones survive, reproduce and come to dominate [1] **[3]**

**12. (a)** 1 in 10⁴ × 10⁶ = 1 in **10¹⁰** [1]

**(b)** 10⁹ ÷ 10¹⁰ = **0.1** [1]

**(c)** With two drugs, a virion resistant to both is unlikely to be present at all, whereas with one drug about 10⁵ resistant virions (for drug A) would be expected [1] **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving viruses cytoplasm, ribosomes or a membrane of their own.
- Writing that the whole lambda phage enters the cell.
- Leaving out integration when describing the lysogenic cycle.
- Forgetting to subtract the starting phages from a burst-size count.
- Writing that viruses mutate in response to drugs.
- One-sided evaluation of viral origins.
