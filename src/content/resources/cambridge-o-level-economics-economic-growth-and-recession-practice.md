## Questions

**1.** Define economic growth. **[2]**

**2.** Country G (invented) uses year 1 as its base year, with a price index of 100 and nominal GDP of 800 billion currency units. In year 2 nominal GDP is 918 billion and the price index is 108.

**(a)** Work out real GDP in year 2. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the growth rate of real GDP from year 1 to year 2. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the growth in nominal GDP is not a good measure of economic growth. **[2]**

**3.** Country H is invented. In year 1 real GDP is 1,260 billion with a population of 21.0 million. In year 2 real GDP is 1,323 billion with a population of 21.4 million.

**(a)** Find GDP per head for each year. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the percentage change in GDP per head and comment on what it shows that total real GDP does not. **[3]**

**4.** State what is meant by a recession and explain where an economy in recession is on its production possibility curve (PPC). **[3]**

**5.** Explain two possible causes of a recession. **[4]**

**6.** Country K is invented. Its real GDP, in billions, is 400 in quarter 1, 396 in quarter 2, 391 in quarter 3, 385 in quarter 4 and 388 in quarter 5. The government collects tax equal to 22% of real GDP.

**(a)** Identify, with a reason, whether the economy was in recession. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage fall in real GDP from quarter 1 to quarter 4. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the fall in tax revenue between quarter 1 and quarter 4. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain one other consequence of the recession for the government's finances. **[2]**

**7.** An economy makes capital goods and consumer goods and is at a point inside its PPC. For each event, state whether it causes a movement toward the PPC or a shift of the PPC, and explain.

**(a)** Many unemployed workers are hired after a rise in consumer spending. **[2]**
**(b)** A new invention lets each worker produce more of both goods. **[2]**

**8.** In Country L (invented), employment rises by 0.8% and output per worker rises by 1.6%. Population rises by 0.5%.

**(a)** Calculate the growth in real GDP. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the change in real GDP per head. **[2]**

**9.** Explain three ways in which the quantity or quality of the factors of production can lead to economic growth. **[6]**

**10.** Explain two benefits and two costs of economic growth for a low-income economy. **[6]**

**11.** A government in an economy with high unemployment and spare capacity is choosing between a cut in income tax and spending more on training for workers. Evaluate which policy is more likely to promote growth. **[6]**

**12.** Country M is invented. Its real GDP grows by 4.0% a year, but its population grows by 4.5% a year. Its government says that the economy is doing well because output is rising. Most of the extra output comes from a mining industry that is polluting rivers.

**(a)** Find the annual change in real GDP per head. **[2]**
**(b)** Discuss whether the government is right to say the economy is doing well. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** A rise in how much an economy produces [1] from one period to the next, measured by the percentage increase in real GDP [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "GDP going up" on its own is a weak definition; it reads better when it says output has risen over time and that real, not nominal, GDP is the measure.

**2. (a)** Real GDP = 918 / 108 x 100 [1] = **850 billion** [1].
**(b)** (850 - 800) / 800 x 100 [1] = **6.25%** [1].
**(c)** A higher price level lifts nominal GDP even if output is unchanged [1]. Nominal GDP grew 14.75%, but part of this was inflation, so output grew only 6.25% [1]. **[6 in total]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (c) a general statement is stronger when you use the numbers in the question to show the gap between 14.75% and 6.25%.

**3. (a)** Year 1: 1,260 / 21.0 = **60.0** [1]. Year 2: 1,323 / 21.4 = **61.82** [1].
**(b)** (61.82 - 60.0) / 60.0 x 100 = **3.04%** [1]. Total real GDP grew 5.0% [1], but population grew 1.9%, so the average person gained less than the total suggests, because output is shared among more people [1]. **[5 in total]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the division by the earlier year when you find a percentage change; answers that divide by the new figure usually end with the wrong number.

**4.** A recession is a period when real GDP falls [1], often judged by the common rule of thumb of two consecutive quarters of falling real GDP. The economy is inside its PPC [1] because resources such as labour and capital are unemployed or underused, and the recession takes it further inside [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Saying the PPC "shifts in" is a common slip; the curve stays where it is and the economy's position moves away from it.

**5.** Cause 1: a fall in consumer spending, for example because households fear losing their jobs [1]. Firms then sell less, cut output and jobs, and falling incomes cut spending further [1]. Cause 2: higher interest rates (or a banking crisis that limits lending) [1], which makes borrowing for investment and spending more costly or impossible, so total demand falls and output falls [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each cause gains most when it is followed by its link to lower output; naming causes alone tends to leave the explanation incomplete.

**6. (a)** Real GDP fell in quarters 2, 3 and 4 [1], so there were consecutive falls, which meets the common two-quarter rule of thumb [1].
**(b)** (385 - 400) / 400 x 100 [1] = **-3.75%** (a fall of 3.75%) [1].
**(c)** Tax in quarter 1 = 0.22 x 400 = 88 [1]. Tax in quarter 4 = 0.22 x 385 = 84.7, so tax revenue fell by **3.3 billion** [1].
**(d)** Unemployment rises in a recession [1], so the government pays more in unemployment benefits, and its budget deficit grows [1]. **[8 in total]**
*Examiner insight:* A consequence for the government's finances needs both sides, falling revenue and rising spending; part (d) asks for another consequence, so do not repeat the tax point.

**7. (a)** A **movement toward the PPC** [1], because workers who were unemployed are now used, so output rises without any increase in capacity [1].
**(b)** A **shift of the PPC to the right** [1], because better technology raises the maximum output the economy can produce, so capacity has grown [1]. **[4 in total]**
*Examiner insight:* Giving only the label gains little; the reason, idle resources used versus capacity raised, is what separates the two answers.

**8. (a)** 1.008 x 1.016 - 1 [1] = **2.41%** [1].
**(b)** 1.0241 / 1.005 - 1 [1] = **1.90%** [1]. **[4 in total]**
*Examiner insight:* Show that you combine growth rates by multiplying; adding 0.8 and 1.6 to get 2.4% is close here but is the wrong method.

**9.** Any three of: more workers (a larger labour force) increase the quantity of labour, so more can be produced [2]. Better education and training raise the quality of labour and so output per worker [2]. More capital (machines, factories) raises capacity and productivity [2]. Newly discovered natural resources raise the quantity of land, and better health makes workers more productive. Each way gains two marks, for the factor and its link to higher output. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A named factor with no link to output is thin; explain how the change raises what the economy can produce.

**10.** Benefit 1: higher incomes can reduce poverty [1] and let households afford food, health and schooling [1]. Benefit 2: more tax revenue lets the government spend on infrastructure, health and education [1]. Cost 1: environmental damage and resource depletion if growth relies on extraction [1], which harms people who depend on farming or fishing [1]. Cost 2: inequality can widen if the gains go to a small group [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Linking each point to a low-income economy, such as basic needs or dependence on farming, shows you are answering for that economy and not any economy.

**11.** Tax cut: leaves households with more income, so spending rises and idle resources are used [1]. It works fairly quickly [1]. It may be saved or spent on imports, and it reduces government revenue [1]. Training: raises labour quality and productivity, shifting the PPC outward [1], but results take years and it does not directly use the idle resources [1]. Judgement: with unemployment and spare capacity, the tax cut is the quicker route to growth, while training builds lasting capacity; a good answer reaches a supported conclusion [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation gains most when it ends with a judgement tied to the conditions in the question, here spare capacity, and not a bare list.

**12. (a)** 1.040 / 1.045 - 1 [1] = **-0.48%** [1].
**(b)** For: output is rising, which creates jobs and tax revenue [1]. Against: GDP per head is falling, so the average person has less output [1]. Against: pollution harms health, fishing and farming, and is not counted as a cost in GDP [1]. Against: growth from one polluting industry may not be lasting and may widen inequality [1]. Judgement: output growth alone does not show a better standard of living, so the government is only partly right [1]. Use the figures: 4.0% growth is outpaced by 4.5% population growth [1]. **[8 in total]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the numbers in the question when you discuss; a judgement supported by the fall in GDP per head tends to carry more weight than one with no evidence.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using nominal figures for growth without dividing by the price index.
- Dividing by the later year when finding a percentage change.
- Giving growth in total GDP when the question asks about GDP per head.
- Adding growth rates instead of multiplying them.
- Drawing a recession as an inward shift of the PPC.
- Stating a cause of recession and stopping before the fall in output.
- Writing one-sided answers on the costs and benefits of growth.
- Failing to link points to the type of economy named in the question.
- Describing policies with no comment on effectiveness or side effects.
