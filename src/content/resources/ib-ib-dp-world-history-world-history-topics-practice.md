IB DP History Paper 2 asks for two essays in 1 hour 30 minutes, each from a different world history topic, each marked out of 15. Below is one practice question for each of the twelve topics, followed by indicative content: the knowledge and lines of argument an examiner would expect, not a model answer. Answer the questions for the two topics your school studies, under timed conditions of about 45 minutes each.

## How the essays are marked

| Marks | Summary of the IB Paper 2 markband |
|---|---|
| 13-15 | Clear understanding of the question's demands; accurate, relevant knowledge; effective comparison where asked; clear argument; evaluation of different perspectives; consistent focus |
| 10-12 | Demands understood; mostly accurate knowledge; some critical analysis and evaluation; mostly focused |
| 7-9 | Generally understood; some knowledge, more description; limited analysis |
| 4-6 | Some understanding; limited knowledge; narrative |
| 1-3 | Little understanding or relevant knowledge |

## The questions

**1. Society and economy (750-1400).** Discuss the impact of trade on the economy of **one** society you have studied. **[15]**

*Indicative content (Song China as example):* growth of internal and maritime trade; paper money (*jiaozi*); commercialisation of agriculture through new rice strains; urban growth (Kaifeng, Hangzhou); state monopolies and taxation of trade. Balance: agriculture remained the economic base; limits on merchants' status in Confucian society. Judgement on the extent of impact.

**2. Causes and effects of wars (750-1500).** Compare and contrast the causes of **two** medieval wars, each chosen from a different region. **[15]**

*Indicative content (First Crusade and Mongol invasion of the Khwarazmian Empire):* religious motivation and papal appeal at Clermont (1095) against the Mongols' response to the murder of envoys and merchants at Otrar (1218); territorial and economic motives in both; leadership of Urban II versus Genghis Khan. Comparative structure by type of cause.

**3. Dynasties and rulers (750-1500).** Evaluate the methods used by **one** ruler to maintain power. **[15]**

*Indicative content (Charlemagne):* military campaigns (Saxony, Lombardy); *missi dominici* and counts; alliance with the papacy and imperial coronation (800); capitularies; patronage of learning. Limits: dependence on personal authority; division of the empire after Louis the Pious.

**4. Societies in transition (1400-1700).** To what extent did religious change transform **one** society between 1400 and 1700? **[15]**

*Indicative content (German lands):* Luther's 95 Theses (1517); spread through printing; Peasants' War (1524-25); Peace of Augsburg (1555) and *cuius regio, eius religio*; changes to church property and education. Continuities: princely power, social hierarchy, persistence of Catholicism in the south.

**5. Early Modern states (1450-1789).** Compare and contrast the ways in which **two** rulers, each chosen from a different region, strengthened central government. **[15]**

*Indicative content (Louis XIV and Tokugawa Ieyasu and successors):* control of nobles (Versailles; *sankin-kōtai* alternate attendance); bureaucracy (intendants; *bakufu* officials); control of religion (Revocation of the Edict of Nantes, 1685; suppression of Christianity in Japan); differences in military and foreign policy -- French wars versus Tokugawa restrictions on foreign contact.

**6. Causes and effects of Early Modern wars (1500-1750).** Examine the political effects of **one** Early Modern war. **[15]**

*Indicative content (Thirty Years' War):* Peace of Westphalia (1648); recognition of Calvinism alongside Lutheranism and Catholicism; increased sovereignty of German princes; decline of Habsburg imperial authority; rise of France and Sweden; independence of the Dutch Republic confirmed. Debate on Westphalia as the origin of state sovereignty.

**7. Industrialization (1750-2005).** "Government policy was the most important factor in the industrialization of a country." With reference to **two** countries, each chosen from a different region, to what extent do you agree? **[15]**

*Indicative content (Britain and Meiji Japan):* Britain: limited direct state role, but enclosure, patents, empire and navy; coal, capital, technology and labour. Japan: state-led modernisation after 1868 -- railways, model factories, education, sale of industries to *zaibatsu*. Judgement: decisive in Japan, enabling but secondary in Britain.

**8. Independence movements (1800-2000).** Compare and contrast the methods used by **two** independence movements, each chosen from a different region. **[15]**

*Indicative content (India and Algeria):* Indian National Congress and Gandhi's non-violence (Salt March, 1930; Quit India, 1942), alongside negotiation and the impact of the Second World War; the FLN's armed struggle from 1954, terrorism and guerrilla war, the Battle of Algiers, international diplomacy; independence in 1947 and 1962. Comparison of effectiveness of methods.

**9. Emergence and development of democratic states (1848-2000).** Evaluate the success of **one** democratic state in responding to the challenges it faced. **[15]**

*Indicative content (Weimar Germany):* responses to uprisings (1919-23), hyperinflation and Stresemann's recovery; constitutional weaknesses (proportional representation, Article 48); failure in the Depression; collapse by 1933. Alternative example: South Africa after 1994 -- constitution, Truth and Reconciliation Commission, economic inequality.

**10. Authoritarian states (20th century).** Compare and contrast the use of propaganda by **two** authoritarian leaders, each chosen from a different region. **[15]**

*Indicative content (Hitler and Mao):* Goebbels' ministry, radio and rallies versus mass campaigns, posters and the "Little Red Book"; personality cults; youth organisations (Hitler Youth, Red Guards); differences in targets (racial community versus class struggle) and in effectiveness.

**11. Causes and effects of 20th-century wars.** Examine the reasons for the outcome of **one** civil war. **[15]**

*Indicative content (Chinese Civil War, 1946-49):* communist land reform and peasant support; Guomindang corruption, inflation and military errors; Soviet assistance in Manchuria; communist strategy and leadership (Mao, Lin Biao); limited US support after the Marshall mission. Judgement on the most important factor.

**12. The Cold War.** "The Cuban Missile Crisis was a turning point in the Cold War." Discuss. **[15]**

*Indicative content:* the crisis (October 1962) and its resolution; the hotline and Partial Test Ban Treaty (1963); a move towards managed rivalry and later détente. Counter-arguments: the arms race accelerated; proxy wars continued (Vietnam); the Soviet resolve never again to be outgunned. Historians' perspectives on Khrushchev's motives may be evaluated.

## Planning drill

For each question: (1) choose your examples and check they meet any regional requirement; (2) write a one-sentence thesis; (3) set three or four analytical themes as paragraph headings; (4) note two pieces of precise evidence per theme for each example; (5) write a conclusion that answers the question directly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Must I use two examples in every Paper 2 question?**
Only when the question asks for them. Questions with "one" allow a single example; comparison questions need two.

**How much historiography should I include?**
Enough to evaluate different perspectives where it helps the argument. It is not compulsory, and a vague or inaccurate reference to a historian weakens an answer rather than strengthening it.

**What if my example is from the wrong region?**
Read the question again: "each chosen from a different region" is a requirement, and the IB History guide defines the four regions you must choose from.
