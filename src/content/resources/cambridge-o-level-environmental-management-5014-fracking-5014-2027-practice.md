## Section A

**1.** Define fracking. **[2]**

**2.** State whether shale is permeable or impermeable. **[1]**

**3.** Name the **two** fuels that can be extracted by fracking. **[2]**

**4.** State **two** substances, other than water, in fracking fluid. **[2]**

**5.** Identify **one** greenhouse gas that can leak from fracking wells. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Explain why gas held in shale cannot be extracted with an ordinary vertical well. **[3]**

**7.** Describe the steps used to extract gas from shale by fracking. **[4]**

**8.** *Invented data.* A fracking company drills 12 wells at one well pad. Each well uses 15 000 m³ of
water for fracturing. About 20% of this water returns to the surface as flowback.

(a) Calculate the total volume of water used at the well pad.

(b) Calculate the volume of flowback water that must be treated or disposed of.

(c) Explain why this water use could be a problem in a region with low rainfall. **[6]**

**9.** Explain how fracking could lead to contamination of drinking water. **[3]**

**10.** Explain why replacing coal-fired power stations with power stations burning fracked gas could
reduce carbon dioxide emissions, and suggest why the overall climate benefit may be smaller than expected.
**[4]**

**11.** A shale gas site is proposed near a village. Suggest why the village's unemployed young people and
its farmers might hold different views about the proposal. **[4]**

**12.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of fracking for a country that wants to reduce its energy
imports while also meeting targets to cut greenhouse gas emissions. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The extraction of natural gas or petroleum from shale rock [1] by hydraulic fracturing [1].

**2.** Impermeable [1].

**3.** Natural gas [1]; petroleum (oil) [1].

**4.** Sand [1]; chemicals [1].

**5.** Methane [1].

**6.** Any three:

- Shale is impermeable [1].
- Its pore spaces are too small and poorly connected for gas to flow through [1].
- So gas cannot move through the rock to reach the well [1].
- Cracks must be made in the shale to give the gas a path to the well [1].

**7.** Any four:

- A well is drilled vertically down to the shale layer [1].
- It is turned to run horizontally along the shale [1].
- The well is lined with steel casing and cement [1].
- Fluid of water, sand and chemicals is pumped down at very high pressure [1].
- The pressure cracks the shale, and the sand holds the cracks open [1].
- Gas flows through the cracks into the well and up to the surface [1].

**8.** (a) 12 x 15 000 = **180 000 m³** [1].
(b) 20 / 100 x 180 000 = **36 000 m³** [1 for the working, 1 for the answer].
(c) Any three:

- Water supplies in a low-rainfall region are already limited [1].
- The company would compete with farms for irrigation water [1], which could reduce crop yields [1].
- Less water would be available for homes and drinking [1].
- Taking water from rivers or aquifers could lower water levels and harm wildlife [1].

**9.** Any three:

- If the steel casing or cement around a well fails or cracks [1],
- chemicals in the fracking fluid, or methane, can escape into surrounding rock [1]
- and reach aquifers used for drinking water [1].
- Flowback water can be spilled at the surface and soak into the ground or run into rivers [1].

**10.** Reduction, any two:

- Natural gas releases less carbon dioxide than coal when burned to generate electricity [1].
- So switching the same amount of generation from coal to gas lowers emissions [1].

Smaller benefit, any two:

- Methane can leak from wells and pipes [1].
- Methane is a greenhouse gas, so leaks add to climate change and cancel part of the saving [1].
- Gas is still a fossil fuel, so carbon dioxide is still released [1].
- Cheap gas could slow investment in renewable resources [1].

**11.** Young unemployed people, any two:

- Likely to support it [1], because drilling, lorry driving and services create jobs [1].
- Income would be spent in the village, helping local businesses [1].

Farmers, any two:

- May oppose it [1], because fracking uses large volumes of water that compete with irrigation [1].
- Worry that spills or failed wells could pollute ground water and soil [1].
- May support it if they receive payments for land used for well pads [1].

**12.** Benefits, any three:

- Gives access to the country's own gas reserves in shale [1].
- Reduces dependence on imports, improving energy security [1].
- Replacing coal with gas reduces carbon dioxide emissions, helping to meet targets [1].
- Creates jobs and tax income [1].

Limitations, any three:

- Gas is a fossil fuel, so emissions continue and targets may still be missed [1].
- Methane leaks add to greenhouse gases [1].
- Investment in fracking may delay renewable resources, which cut emissions further [1].
- Water use, ground water risk, small earthquakes or local disruption [1].

Maximum 6 marks; a judgement is expected for full marks, for example: fracking can cut imports and
replace coal in the short term, but meeting emission targets in the long term needs renewables.

## Where marks are usually lost

- A definition missing "shale" or "hydraulic fracturing".
- In 8(b), finding 20% of one well's water instead of the total.
- Saying gas releases no carbon dioxide.
- Giving the same view for both groups in question 11.
- Writing a one-sided answer to question 12.
