## Questions

**1.** Country F has a savings rate of 12% of GDP and a capital-output ratio of 4. GDP is 80 billion.
**(a)** Calculate the Harrod-Domar growth rate. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the savings rate needed for 5% growth. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the annual savings gap in billions. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain one limitation of the model. **[2]**

**2.** Country G's population aged 0 to 14, 15 to 64 and 65 plus is 24m, 64m and 6m. Twenty years later it is 18m, 72m and 10m.
**(a)** Calculate the dependency ratio in each year. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the change could help development, and one condition for it. **[2]**

**3.** Country H's export price index is 95 and its import price index 125 (each index was 100 in the base year).
**(a)** Calculate the terms of trade. **[2]**
**(b)** State the percentage change from the base year. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage rise in export volume needed to buy the same volume of imports. **[1]**

**4.** Demand for a crop has price elasticity of −0.5. A drought cuts supply by 8%.
**(a)** Estimate the percentage change in price. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in total revenue for the whole sector. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a farmer who lost the whole crop does not gain. **[1]**

**5.** Country J owes external debt of 45 on GDP of 90. Debt service is 6 and export earnings are 24.
**(a)** Calculate debt to GDP. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the debt-service ratio. **[1]**
**(c)** Exports fall by 25% with debt service unchanged. Calculate the new ratio. **[2]**
**(d)** State one effect on development. **[1]**

**6.** Explain the inequality Kuznets curve. **[4]**

**7.** Country K has public debt of 60 and GDP of 150. It runs a deficit of 9. Nominal GDP grows 4%.
**(a)** Calculate debt to GDP at the start and after one year. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one argument for and one against the deficit. **[3]**

**8.** Explain two ways in which inequality of opportunity could slow development. **[6]**

**9.** Country L has exports of 45, imports of 39 and GDP of 140.
**(a)** Calculate the openness ratio. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the trade balance as a percentage of GDP. **[1]**
**(c)** State one risk of high openness. **[1]**

**10.** Evaluate the view that dependence on primary products holds back development. **[9]**

**11.** Evaluate whether strong institutions matter more for development than capital accumulation. **[12]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 12 / 4 = **3.0%** [1]
**(b)** s = g × k [1] = 5 × 4 = **20%** [1]
**(c)** Gap = 20 − 12 = 8 percentage points [1]; 0.08 × 80 = **6.4 billion** [1]
**(d)** For example, it assumes k stays constant [1], but technology and skills change how much output capital produces [1]. Equally valid: saving may not become productive investment.
*Examiner insight:* Show s = g × k before substituting so your method is visible, and give a limitation a reason, not just the word "unrealistic".

**2. (a)** Year 1: (24 + 6) / 64 × 100 = **46.9** [1]. Year 20: (18 + 10) / 72 × 100 = **38.9** [1]
**(b)** Fewer dependants per worker, so more saving and output per head [1], provided jobs are created for the larger working-age group [1].
*Examiner insight:* Strong answers state the condition (jobs, skills) rather than assuming a falling ratio helps by itself.

**3. (a)** 95 / 125 × 100 [1] = **76.0** [1]
**(b)** **Fall of 24.0%** [1]
**(c)** 125 / 95 = 1.316, so **31.6%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check the direction: export index over import index. Reversing it gives 131.6 and the wrong conclusion.

**4. (a)** Price change = 8 / 0.5 [1] = **16% rise** [1]
**(b)** 0.92 × 1.16 = 1.0672 [1], so **revenue up 6.7%** [1]
**(c)** Higher price is multiplied by a quantity of zero, so that farmer's income falls [1].
*Examiner insight:* State that the figure treats elasticity as constant and is an estimate; it is also worth adding that the sector gains while individual farmers can lose.

**5. (a)** 45 / 90 × 100 = **50%** [1]
**(b)** 6 / 24 × 100 = **25%** [1]
**(c)** Exports 24 × 0.75 = 18 [1]; 6 / 18 × 100 = **33.3%** [1]
**(d)** More export earnings go on debt, leaving less for imports of capital, health and education [1].
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) shows why exporters of one commodity are vulnerable; link the number to development, not just to the ratio.

**6.** Inequality first rises as income per head grows [1], because workers move from low-income farming to better-paid industry and gains are uneven [1]. At higher incomes it falls [1], as education, taxation and welfare spread gains, giving an inverted U shape [1].
*Examiner insight:* Describe the shape and give the economic reason for both halves; shape alone earns little.

**7. (a)** 60 / 150 × 100 = **40.0%** [1]. New debt 60 + 9 = 69 and GDP 150 × 1.04 = 156 [1]; 69 / 156 × 100 = **44.2%** [1]
**(b)** For: borrowing for roads or schools can raise productive capacity [1]. Against: the ratio rises and interest costs crowd out other spending, especially with foreign-currency debt [1]. Using the figures from part (a) to support the argument [1].
*Examiner insight:* Use your result from part (a); the argument against is stronger when it refers to the rise from 40.0% to 44.2%.

**8.** Point 1: children of poor households cannot afford school or health care [1], so talent is wasted and human capital stays low, reducing productivity and growth [1]. Point 2: credit-constrained households cannot borrow to start businesses [1], so investment is lower and the market for local firms stays small [1]. Developed point on each, such as lower tax revenue or unrest [1] and a stated condition or limit [1].
*Examiner insight:* Each point needs a chain from inequality to a development outcome, not just "the poor lose out".

**9. (a)** (45 + 39) / 140 × 100 [1] = **60.0%** [1]
**(b)** (45 − 39) / 140 × 100 = **4.3% surplus** [1]
**(c)** Exposure to a fall in world demand or a price shock [1].
*Examiner insight:* Label the balance as a surplus or deficit; the sign is part of the answer.

**10.** Case for: prices are unstable [1] because supply and demand are inelastic [1], so incomes, tax revenue and investment swing [1]. Prebisch-Singer: lower income elasticity means terms of trade fall [1]. Few manufacturing spillovers [1]. Against: resources can fund investment [1] if institutions are strong; critiques show prices do not always fall [1]. Judgement: depends on diversification and governance, with a reason [1]. Linked point, such as dependency interacting with debt [1].
*Examiner insight:* Aim for a final view that names what it hinges on, such as diversification, instead of a list of pros and cons.

**11.** Capital: raises productivity [1], saving funds investment [1], Harrod-Domar shows s and k matter [1]. Institutions: secure property rights allow borrowing and investment [1]; corruption raises costs [1]; conflict destroys capital [1]; the rule of law supports contracts and trade [1]. Interaction: capital is wasted where governance is weak [1]. Limits: institutions are slow to change [1]; some countries grew with weak institutions at first [1]. Judgement with conditions [1], and a supported final conclusion [1].
*Examiner insight:* Pick a side and justify it, while showing the factors interact; an even-handed answer without a judgement tends to stall.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the Harrod-Domar formula the wrong way up.
- Quoting a ratio without the working or with the wrong base.
- Describing the Kuznets curve without the cause of each half.
- Writing "unrealistic" as a limitation without saying why.
- Treating the terms of trade as a cause of falling GDP without explaining income elasticity.
- Calling debt always harmful, or deficits always helpful.
- Writing about growth when the question asks about development.
- Leaving the evaluation until a one-line final sentence.
