Condensed notes on section **3.9.1 Assessing a change in scale** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). Section 3.9 is A-level only. The unit-cost and overtrading examples are in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-change-in-scale/); questions are in the [practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-change-in-scale-practice/).

## Specification content

- The reasons why businesses grow or retrench.
- The difference between organic and external growth.
- How to manage issues caused by growth and retrenchment: economies of scale (technical, purchasing, managerial), economies of scope, diseconomies of scale, synergy, overtrading.
- The impact of growth or retrenchment on the functional areas.
- Assessing methods of growth (mergers, takeovers, ventures, franchising) and types (vertical backward and forward, horizontal, conglomerate).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organic growth | Growth from the firm's own resources |
| External growth | Growth by joining with or buying another firm |
| Retrenchment | Deliberate reduction in the size of the business |
| Economy of scale | Fall in unit cost as output rises |
| Diseconomy of scale | Rise in unit cost when a firm becomes too large |
| Economy of scope | Lower cost from producing related products together |
| Synergy | Combined firms worth more than the sum of the parts |
| Overtrading | Expanding sales faster than finances can support |
| Joint venture | A new business set up jointly by two firms for a purpose |
| Franchise | Right to use another firm's brand and system for fees |

## Why grow or retrench

| Growth | Retrenchment |
|---|---|
| Economies of scale | Falling demand or recession |
| Market power | Loss-making activities |
| Spread risk | Repaying debt |
| Survival against larger rivals | Refocus on core competences |
| Owner or shareholder pressure | Poor acquisitions to be reversed |

## Organic against external

| | Organic | External |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Slower | Faster |
| Risk | Lower | Higher |
| Control and culture | Kept | At risk |
| Finance | Profit and loans | Large loans or shares |

## Economies of scale (types)

Technical (better machinery, specialisation); purchasing (bulk discounts); managerial (specialist managers, cost spread); financial (cheaper borrowing); marketing (advertising spread over more units).

## Diseconomies of scale

Slow communication and decisions; lower motivation; co-ordination problems between sites; duplication and bureaucracy. They show as a **rising** average cost.

## Unit cost calculation

Average cost = total cost ÷ output.
Fixed £200,000, variable £5 a unit. At 40,000 units: total = £400,000; average = **£10.00**. At 80,000 units: total = £600,000; average = **£7.50**.

## Overtrading

- Cause: sales grow faster than working capital.
- Effect: cash shortage even when profitable.
- Extra receivables = increase in revenue × credit days ÷ 365.
- Solutions: slower growth; arranged finance; faster collection; less inventory; longer supplier credit; new equity.

## Impact by function

| Function | Growth | Retrenchment |
|---|---|---|
| Finance | Funding; cash flow | Cash from asset sales; redundancy costs |
| Marketing | Wider brand; managing range | Dropping products; reputation |
| Operations | More capacity; complexity | Closing capacity |
| HR | Recruiting, training, culture | Redundancy; morale |

## Methods and types

| Method | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Merger | Agreement to combine, usually of equals |
| Takeover | One firm buys control of another |
| Joint venture | Shared new business |
| Franchising | Brand and system licensed for fees |

| Integration | Direction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Horizontal | Same stage, same industry | Two supermarkets |
| Backward | Earlier in supply chain | Bakery buys flour mill |
| Forward | Later in supply chain | Brewer buys pubs |
| Conglomerate | Unrelated industry | Food firm buys a hotel |

## Assessing a change in scale: checklist

Objectives; finance and cash flow; risk and speed; economies and diseconomies; people and culture; market and rivals; time frame.

## Model analysis paragraph

"Growth by takeover may give the firm quick access to a new market, but it carries risks. The business must find the money, often by borrowing, which raises gearing. It must also combine two cultures. If the two firms' administration costs can be merged, synergy of, say, £150,000 a year may arise. Against this, culture clashes may cause staff to leave, so the expected gains may not appear. The decision depends on how closely the two businesses fit and whether the firm has the management skills to integrate them."

## Command words

- **Assess a change in scale:** consider objectives, finance, risk, speed and the effect on each function.
- **Calculate:** show unit cost workings; state units.

## Exam traps

- Growth is not always good. Check unit costs and cash.
- Overtrading is about working capital, not overspending.
- Merger = agreement; takeover = purchase.
- Backward = towards suppliers; forward = towards customers.
- Say what the change means for each function.

## Self-test

1. Define "organic growth".
2. Give two reasons a business might retrench.
3. Name two types of economy of scale.
4. Fixed costs are £120,000 and variable cost is £4 a unit. Calculate average cost at 30,000 units.
5. Give two causes of diseconomies of scale.
6. Define "synergy".
7. A firm increases revenue by £600,000 and allows customers 50 days' credit. Calculate the extra receivables.
8. Give one way to reduce the risk of overtrading.
9. Which type of integration is a cinema chain buying a film distributor? Explain.
10. Which type is a bakery merging with another bakery?
11. Give one advantage and one risk of franchising for the franchisor.
12. Explain one impact of retrenchment on human resources.

### Answers

1. Growth from the firm's own resources, such as more sales or new sites, rather than joining another business.
2. Any two: falling demand; loss-making activities; repaying debt; refocusing on core competences.
3. Any two: technical; purchasing; managerial; financial; marketing.
4. Total = 120,000 + 4 × 30,000 = £240,000; average = **£8.00**.
5. Any two: communication problems; low motivation; poor co-ordination; bureaucracy.
6. When two businesses combined are worth more than the sum of their separate values.
7. 600,000 × 50 ÷ 365 = **£82,192** (to the nearest £).
8. Any one: arrange finance in advance; slow the pace of growth; collect payments faster; hold less inventory.
9. Backward vertical integration, if the distributor supplies films to cinemas; the cinema buys a business earlier in its supply chain.
10. Horizontal integration.
11. Advantage: fast growth without investing in outlets. Risk: a franchisee could harm the brand and the franchisor has less day-to-day control.
12. Redundancies reduce morale and cause loss of knowledge, so remaining staff may be less motivated and productivity may fall.
