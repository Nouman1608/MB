These notes condense section 1.6, Momentum, of the Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. **All of section 1.6 is Supplement (Extended only)** and is examined on Papers 2 and 4. Explanations and five worked examples are in the [study guide on momentum](/resources/cambridge-igcse-physics-0625-momentum-cambridge-igcse-physics/); the [Motion, forces and energy revision notes](/resources/igcse-physics-motion-forces-and-energy-revision-notes/) cover the rest of Topic 1.

## Definitions

- **Momentum:** mass × velocity.
- **Impulse:** force × time for which the force acts.
- **Resultant force:** change in momentum per unit time.
- **Principle of conservation of momentum:** in a collision or explosion, the total momentum before equals the total momentum after (when no external resultant force acts).

## Equations

| Equation | Units |
|---|---|
| p = m v | kg m/s |
| impulse = F Δt = Δ(m v) | N s (= kg m/s) |
| F = Δp / Δt | N |

Links:

- F = Δp/Δt is F = ma for constant mass.
- Impulse and change in momentum are the same quantity, so a question may give either.
- Unit check: N s = (kg m/s²) × s = kg m/s.

## Signs and directions

- Momentum is a **vector**.
- Choose a positive direction; anything moving the other way is negative.
- Rebound: Δp = m(v − u) with v and u of **opposite sign**, so the change is the sum of the magnitudes.
  - 0.20 kg ball, 10 m/s in, 8.0 m/s out: Δp = 0.20 × (−8.0 − 10) = −3.6 kg m/s; size 3.6 N s.

## Conservation problems: three types

| Type | Before | After | Equation pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Objects join | m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ | (m₁ + m₂)v | m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v |
| Objects separate from rest (explosion, recoil) | 0 | m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ | m₁v₁ = −m₂v₂ |
| Objects bounce apart | m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ | m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ | solve for the one unknown |

Always finish with the **direction** of the velocity you found.

**Worked check (objects join).** A 0.50 kg trolley at 6.0 m/s hits a stationary 1.0 kg trolley and they stick: 0.50 × 6.0 = 1.5 v, so v = **2.0 m/s** in the original direction. Sense check: the joined trolleys must move more slowly than the moving one did, because the same momentum is shared by a larger mass.

**Worked check (recoil).** A 40 kg boy steps forwards off a stationary 80 kg boat at 2.0 m/s: 0 = 40 × 2.0 + 80 v, so the boat moves at **1.0 m/s backwards**.

## Force and time: safety

- For the same change in momentum, **longer time → smaller force**.
- Crumple zones, airbags, seat belts, helmets, crash mats, bending your knees: all **increase Δt**.
- A golfer or batter "following through" keeps the force acting for longer, so the ball gains more momentum (larger impulse).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Momentum | Mass × velocity; vector; kg m/s |
| Impulse | Force × time for which the force acts; equals change in momentum; N s |
| Conservation of momentum | Total momentum unchanged in a collision or explosion with no external resultant force |
| Recoil | Backward motion of one object when it pushes another forwards |
| Crumple zone | Part of a car designed to crush in a collision, increasing the stopping time |
| Rate of change of momentum | Δp/Δt; equal to the resultant force |

## Exam traps

- Adding momenta of objects moving in opposite directions without signs.
- Treating a rebound as a smaller change than stopping. It is larger.
- Writing "the airbag absorbs the momentum" or "reduces the impulse". The change in momentum is fixed; the time is longer, so the force is smaller.
- Forgetting the direction of a final velocity.
- Using the combined mass when objects bounce apart.
- Unit errors: momentum in kg/m s, impulse in N/s.
- Mixing grams and kilograms: 50 g is 0.050 kg.

## Quick self-test

1. Define momentum.
2. Calculate the momentum of a 1500 kg car moving at 20 m/s.
3. State the unit of impulse and show that it equals the unit of momentum.
4. A 0.050 kg ball moving at 30 m/s is stopped. Find its change in momentum.
5. A force of 120 N acts on a trolley for 0.25 s. Find the impulse.
6. A 2.0 kg trolley at 4.0 m/s hits and sticks to a stationary 2.0 kg trolley. Find their speed.
7. A 3.0 kg gun fires a 0.010 kg bullet at 300 m/s. Find the recoil speed of the gun.
8. A 0.20 kg ball hits a wall at 10 m/s and rebounds at 8.0 m/s. Find the size of its change in momentum.
9. If the ball in question 8 is in contact with the wall for 0.020 s, find the average force on it.
10. Explain why a crash mat reduces the force on a gymnast who lands on it.
11. State the condition for momentum to be conserved.
12. A 1.0 kg trolley at 6.0 m/s hits a 2.0 kg trolley moving at 3.0 m/s in the opposite direction. They stick. Find the final velocity.

### Answers

1. **Mass × velocity.**
2. 1500 × 20 = **30 000 kg m/s**.
3. **N s**; N s = kg m/s² × s = **kg m/s**.
4. 0.050 × 30 = **1.5 kg m/s** (opposite to the motion).
5. 120 × 0.25 = **30 N s**.
6. 8.0 = 4.0 v, so v = **2.0 m/s**.
7. 0.010 × 300 = 3.0 kg m/s; gun: 3.0 ÷ 3.0 = **1.0 m/s backwards**.
8. 0.20 × (10 + 8.0) = **3.6 kg m/s**.
9. 3.6 ÷ 0.020 = **180 N**.
10. The gymnast's change in momentum is the same, but the mat **increases the stopping time**, so the **rate of change of momentum**, and so the force, is smaller.
11. **No external resultant force** acts on the objects.
12. 6.0 − 6.0 = 0, so the trolleys **stop** (v = 0).
