## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cell cycle | The repeating sequence of growth, DNA replication and division that a dividing cell goes through |
| Chromatid | One of the two identical copies of a chromosome after replication, joined at the centromere |
| Centromere | The region where two sister chromatids are held together and where spindle fibres attach |
| Homologous chromosomes | A pair of chromosomes, one from each parent, of the same size and shape with the same genes at the same loci (possibly different alleles) |
| Diploid (2n) / haploid (n) | Two sets of chromosomes / one set of chromosomes |
| Differentiation | The process by which a cell becomes specialised for a particular function |
| Tissue | A group of similar cells, with any intercellular material, working together for a particular function |
| Organ | A group of different tissues working together to carry out a function |
| Organ system | A group of organs working together to carry out a major function |
| Stem cell | An undifferentiated cell that can divide repeatedly (self-renewal) and can differentiate into specialised cells |

## The cell cycle: 2.1.6(a)

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| **G1** (interphase) | Cell grows; proteins and organelles are made |
| **S** (interphase) | DNA is replicated, so each chromosome becomes two sister chromatids |
| **G2** (interphase) | Further growth; energy stores built up; replicated DNA is checked for errors |
| **Mitosis** | The nucleus divides into two genetically identical nuclei |
| **Cytokinesis** | The cytoplasm divides, giving two genetically identical daughter cells |

* Interphase is the longest part of the cycle; the cell is not "resting" but very active.
* A cell can leave the cycle into **G0**, where it stops dividing, for example when it differentiates or becomes senescent.
* Mitosis gives genetically identical cells because DNA is copied exactly in S phase and the sister chromatids are shared equally.

## Regulating the cell cycle: 2.1.6(b)

Checkpoints are control points where the cell is checked before it can move on.

| Checkpoint | When | Checks |
|---|---|---|
| G1 checkpoint | End of G1 | Cell size, nutrients, growth factors, DNA damage |
| G2 checkpoint | End of G2 | DNA fully replicated without damage; cell big enough to divide |
| Spindle assembly (metaphase) checkpoint | During mitosis | Every chromosome is attached to spindle fibres before anaphase starts |

* A cell that fails a checkpoint may pause for repair, enter G0 or undergo programmed cell death (apoptosis).
* If checkpoints fail, cells can divide uncontrollably, which can lead to a tumour.

## Mitosis: 2.1.6(c)

| Stage | Nuclear envelope | Chromosomes and chromatids | Centrioles and spindle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prophase** | Breaks down; nucleolus disappears | Chromatin condenses; chromosomes become visible as two sister chromatids joined at the centromere | Centrioles move to opposite poles (animal cells); spindle fibres form |
| **Metaphase** | Absent | Chromosomes line up at the equator, each attached by its centromere | Spindle fibres attached to centromeres |
| **Anaphase** | Absent | Centromeres divide; sister chromatids are pulled to opposite poles, centromere first (V shapes) | Spindle fibres shorten |
| **Telophase** | Reforms round each set of chromosomes; nucleoli reappear | Chromosomes uncoil back to chromatin | Spindle breaks down |

**Cytokinesis**

* **Animal cells:** the cell membrane is pulled inwards by the cytoskeleton to form a cleavage furrow, which pinches the cell in two.
* **Plant cells:** vesicles from the Golgi apparatus gather at the equator to form a cell plate, which becomes the new cell walls and membranes. Plant cells have no centrioles; their spindle still forms.

Memory order: **PMAT**, then cytokinesis.

## Observing mitosis in plant tissue (PAG1): 2.1.6(d)

**Root tip squash, in steps**

1. Cut the tips (a few millimetres) from growing roots of garlic or onion; the meristem there has many dividing cells.
2. Warm in dilute hydrochloric acid for a few minutes; this breaks down the middle lamella so cells separate and stops division.
3. Rinse in water, then place on a slide and add a stain that binds DNA (for example toluidine blue or acetic orcein) so chromosomes show up.
4. Break up the tip with a mounted needle, add a coverslip and press down gently (with filter paper on top) to spread the cells into a single layer. Do not twist the coverslip.
5. Find the cells at low power, then look at high power (×400).

* **Prepared stained sections** of root tips can be used in the same way.
* **Labelled diagrams:** clear continuous lines, no shading, label the stage and the chromosomes, spindle, cell wall and nuclear envelope where visible; include a title and magnification.
* **Mitotic index** = number of cells in mitosis ÷ total number of cells counted. A higher index means more active division.
* The proportion of cells in each stage reflects how long that stage lasts: most cells are in interphase.

## Significance of mitosis: 2.1.6(e)

* **Growth:** more cells, all genetically identical, in plants, animals and fungi.
* **Tissue repair and replacement:** for example skin cells and the lining of the gut.
* **Asexual reproduction:** offspring are clones of the parent.
  * Plants: runners, bulbs, tubers.
  * Animals: budding in *Hydra*.
  * Fungi: budding in yeast; spore production in many moulds.

## Significance of meiosis: 2.1.6(f)

* **Produces haploid cells** (gametes in animals) from a diploid cell, so the diploid number is restored at fertilisation and the chromosome number stays constant between generations.
* **Genetic variation** by:
  * **Crossing over** in prophase 1: non-sister chromatids of a homologous pair swap sections at chiasmata, making new combinations of alleles.
  * **Independent assortment** in metaphase 1 (and of chromatids in metaphase 2): each homologous pair lines up independently, so maternal and paternal chromosomes are shuffled.
* Variation, together with random fertilisation, gives the differences that natural selection acts on.

## Stages of meiosis: 2.1.6(g)

Meiosis has two divisions and gives **four genetically different haploid cells**. Interphase, with DNA replication, comes before it.

| Stage | Key events |
|---|---|
| **Prophase 1** | Chromosomes condense; homologous chromosomes pair up (bivalents); crossing over at chiasmata; nuclear envelope breaks down; spindle forms |
| **Metaphase 1** | Homologous pairs line up at the equator, randomly orientated (independent assortment) |
| **Anaphase 1** | Homologous chromosomes are pulled to opposite poles; sister chromatids stay joined |
| **Telophase 1** | Two haploid nuclei form; cytokinesis follows in most cells |
| **Prophase 2** | Nuclear envelopes break down again; new spindles form; no further DNA replication |
| **Metaphase 2** | Individual chromosomes line up at the equator; chromatids are assorted independently |
| **Anaphase 2** | Centromeres divide; sister chromatids are pulled to opposite poles |
| **Telophase 2** | Four haploid nuclei form; cytokinesis gives four cells |

No names of the sub-stages of prophase 1 are needed. Meiosis can also be observed in prepared slides of anthers (PAG1).

| | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Divisions | One | Two |
| Daughter cells | Two, diploid | Four, haploid |
| Genetically | Identical to parent and each other | Different from parent and each other |
| Homologous pairs | Do not pair | Pair and cross over |
| Role | Growth, repair, asexual reproduction | Gamete production, variation |

## Specialised cells: 2.1.6(h)

| Cell | Function | Adaptations |
|---|---|---|
| **Erythrocyte** (red blood cell) | Carries oxygen | Biconcave disc: large surface area to volume ratio; no nucleus, so more room for haemoglobin; flexible to squeeze through capillaries |
| **Neutrophil** | Phagocytosis of pathogens | Multilobed nucleus, helping it squeeze through gaps in capillary walls; many lysosomes with digestive enzymes; flexible membrane for engulfing |
| **Squamous epithelial cell** | Lines surfaces such as alveoli | Very flat and thin: short diffusion distance |
| **Ciliated epithelial cell** | Lines the trachea and bronchi | Cilia beat to move mucus; many mitochondria supply ATP for this |
| **Sperm cell** | Delivers the male haploid nucleus | Flagellum for swimming; many mitochondria; acrosome with digestive enzymes to penetrate the egg; haploid nucleus |
| **Palisade cell** | Photosynthesis | Many chloroplasts; tall, column shape packed near the upper leaf surface; large vacuole pushes chloroplasts to the edge |
| **Root hair cell** | Absorbs water and mineral ions | Long hair-like extension gives a large surface area; thin wall; many mitochondria for active transport of mineral ions |
| **Guard cell** | Opens and closes the stoma | Pair of cells; inner wall thicker than outer wall, so the cells bend apart when turgid; chloroplasts |

## Tissues, organs and organ systems: 2.1.6(i)

| Tissue | Structure | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Squamous epithelium** | One layer of flat cells on a basement membrane | Rapid diffusion, for example alveolar walls |
| **Ciliated epithelium** | Ciliated cells, with goblet cells secreting mucus | Moves mucus and trapped particles in the airways |
| **Cartilage** | Chondrocytes in a firm matrix containing collagen and elastin fibres | Support and flexibility, for example in the trachea, ear and ends of bones |
| **Muscle** | Cells or fibres that can contract (skeletal, smooth, cardiac) | Movement |
| **Xylem** | Dead, lignified vessels with no end walls, plus parenchyma and fibres | Transports water and mineral ions; support |
| **Phloem** | Sieve tube elements and companion cells | Transports assimilates such as sucrose |

* **Organ examples:** leaf, heart, lungs, stomach.
* **Organ system examples:** digestive, respiratory, circulatory (cardiovascular), and in plants the shoot and root systems.
* Cooperation between levels lets a multicellular organism carry out functions no single cell could.

## Stem cells: 2.1.6(j)

* Stem cells are a **renewing source of undifferentiated cells**: they divide by mitosis, keeping some cells as stem cells while others differentiate.
* Differentiation happens because different genes are switched on or off, so different proteins are made.

| Potency | Can become | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Totipotent | Any cell type, including placental cells | Zygote and early embryo cells |
| Pluripotent | Any body cell type (not placenta) | Embryonic stem cells |
| Multipotent | A range of cell types within one family | Bone marrow (haematopoietic) stem cells |
| Unipotent | One cell type | Stem cells in the testes that produce only sperm |

**Sources:** embryos, adult tissues (bone marrow), umbilical cord blood, and induced pluripotent stem (iPS) cells made by reprogramming adult cells. In plants, stem cells are in the **meristems**: at root and shoot tips (apical meristems) and in the vascular cambium.

## Erythrocytes and neutrophils from bone marrow: 2.1.6(k)

* Both come from the same multipotent stem cells in **bone marrow**, which produce all the blood cell types.
* **Erythrocytes:** the developing cells fill with haemoglobin and lose their nucleus, mitochondria, Golgi apparatus and ER, making room for more haemoglobin. They live about 120 days, so they are constantly replaced.
* **Neutrophils:** develop a multilobed nucleus and many lysosomes, and keep their organelles. They are short-lived and made in large numbers.
* Both differentiate along different routes because different genes are expressed.

## Xylem and phloem from meristems: 2.1.6(l)

* In the **vascular cambium**, dividing meristem cells produce xylem cells on the inside and phloem cells on the outside.
* **Xylem vessel elements:** cells elongate; lignin is laid down in their walls (spiral, ring or more continuous patterns); the cells die and the contents and end walls break down, forming continuous hollow tubes. Pits remain where lignin is absent.
* **Phloem sieve tube elements:** lose their nucleus and most organelles; the end walls become sieve plates with pores. Each is linked by plasmodesmata to a **companion cell**, which keeps its nucleus and many mitochondria and supports the sieve tube element.

## Uses of stem cells: 2.1.6(m)

* **Repair of damaged tissues:** for example bone marrow transplants to replace blood cells, and research into repairing damaged heart muscle, skin or cartilage.
* **Treating neurological conditions:** replacing lost or damaged nerve cells (no specific conditions need to be recalled).
* **Developmental biology research:** studying how cells differentiate and how organisms develop, and testing new drugs on tissues grown from stem cells.

| Issue | Points to weigh |
|---|---|
| Embryonic stem cells | Pluripotent and versatile, but collecting them destroys the embryo, which many people object to |
| Adult and iPS cells | Avoid destroying embryos; adult cells are only multipotent; iPS cells are still being refined |
| Safety | Risk of rejection, infection, or uncontrolled division leading to tumours |
| Regulation | Research is licensed and controlled by law in many countries |

## Worked reminders

**Mitotic index.** 36 of 240 cells counted are in mitosis: 36 ÷ 240 = 0.15 (15 %).

**Time in a stage.** The cycle lasts 20 hours and 5 % of cells are in metaphase: 0.05 × 20 = 1 hour, about 60 minutes.

**Chromosome numbers.** A human cell (2n = 46): after mitosis each daughter has 46; after meiosis each gamete has 23.

**Independent assortment.** With n pairs of homologous chromosomes, the number of combinations is 2ⁿ. For n = 3: 2³ = 8. For humans (n = 23): 2²³ = 8 388 608, before crossing over is counted.

**DNA content.** If a G1 nucleus holds x units of DNA, a G2 nucleus holds 2x; each daughter after mitosis holds x; each cell after meiosis holds 0.5x.

## Writing good answers

* **Name the structures** the specification lists for each stage: nuclear envelope, chromosomes, chromatids, centromere, centrioles, spindle fibres, cell membrane.
* **Separate homologous chromosomes from chromatids:** in anaphase 1 homologues separate; in anaphase 2 (and in mitosis) chromatids separate.
* **Link adaptation to function** in one sentence: "No nucleus, so more space for haemoglobin, so more oxygen carried".
* **Explain differentiation through genes:** different genes are expressed, so different proteins are made.
* **Balance a stem cell question:** give a benefit and an ethical or safety issue.

## Must-know distinctions

* Chromosome versus chromatid versus homologous chromosome.
* Mitosis (one division, identical diploid cells) versus meiosis (two divisions, varied haploid cells).
* Crossing over (prophase 1) versus independent assortment (metaphase 1 and 2).
* Animal cytokinesis (cleavage furrow) versus plant cytokinesis (cell plate).
* Tissue versus organ versus organ system.
* Totipotent versus pluripotent versus multipotent.
* Xylem (dead, lignified, water) versus phloem (living sieve tubes, assimilates).

## Where marks are usually lost

* Calling interphase a "resting stage", or saying DNA is replicated during prophase.
* Saying homologous chromosomes are identical; they carry the same genes but may carry different alleles.
* Writing that chromatids separate in anaphase 1.
* Giving "spindle fibres form" without saying they attach to the centromeres.
* Saying meiosis halves the chromosome number "to make gametes" without saying why (to keep the number constant at fertilisation).
* Twisting the coverslip in a squash, so chromosomes are smeared, or drawing cells with shading.
* Calling a tissue an organ (for example "xylem is an organ").
* Saying stem cells are found only in embryos, or forgetting that plant stem cells are in meristems.
