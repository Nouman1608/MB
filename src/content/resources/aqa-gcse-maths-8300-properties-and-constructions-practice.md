These questions cover section 3.4.1, **Properties and constructions** (G1 to G13), of the AQA GCSE Mathematics (8300) specification. Questions 1 to 9 suit both tiers. Questions 10 to 12 test Higher tier only content (negative scale factors, combined transformations and circle theorems) and are labelled. Learn the methods first in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-maths-8300-properties-and-constructions/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-maths-8300-properties-and-constructions-revision-notes/). No diagrams are needed: sketch each situation as you read.

## Questions

**1.**

**(a)** Work out the size of one interior angle of a regular decagon. **[2]**

**(b)** A regular polygon has an interior angle of 156°. How many sides does it have? **[3]**

**2.** Triangle ABC has AB = AC and angle BAC = 44°.

**(a)** Work out angle ABC. **[2]**

**(b)** Give a reason for your method. **[1]**

**3.** Lines AB and CD are parallel. A straight line crosses AB at E and CD at F. B and D are on the same side of EF, and angle BEF = 108°.

**(a)** Work out angle EFD. **[1]**

**(b)** Give a geometrical reason for your answer. **[1]**

**4.** Describe how to construct the perpendicular bisector of a line segment PQ, using only a ruler and compasses. **[3]**

**5.**

**(a)** Describe the locus of points that are the same distance from two fixed points A and B. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the region of points that are no more than 2 m from a straight fence 6 m long. **[2]**

**6.** Triangle ABC has AB = 7 cm, BC = 9 cm and angle ABC = 50°. Triangle DEF has DE = 7 cm, EF = 9 cm and angle DEF = 50°. Triangle PQR has a right angle at Q, PR = 13 cm and QR = 5 cm. Triangle XYZ has a right angle at Y, XZ = 13 cm and YZ = 5 cm.

**(a)** Show that triangles ABC and DEF are congruent. State the condition used. **[2]**

**(b)** Show that triangles PQR and XYZ are congruent. State the condition used. **[2]**

**7.** In triangle ABC, the side BC is extended to D. Prove that angle ACD equals the sum of angle BAC and angle ABC. **[3]**

**8.**

**(a)** The triangle with vertices (4, 4), (12, 4) and (12, 8) is enlarged by scale factor 3/4, centre the origin. Write down the coordinates of the image. **[2]**

**(b)** A, B and C are the points (2, 1), (8, 1) and (8, 5). ABCD is a rectangle. Write down the coordinates of D, and work out the length of the diagonal AC, to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**9.** A pentagonal prism has 7 faces and 10 vertices.

**(a)** How many edges does it have? **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the plan view of the prism when it stands on one of its pentagonal faces. **[1]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) The triangle with vertices (1, 1), (3, 1) and (3, 2) is enlarged by scale factor −2, centre the origin. Write down the coordinates of the image of the triangle. **[3]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) The point (3, 2) is reflected in the y-axis, and its image is then reflected in the x-axis.

**(a)** Write down the coordinates of the final image. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the single transformation that has the same effect on every point. **[2]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) A, B and C are points on a circle with centre O.

**(a)** Angle ACB = 37°. Work out angle AOB and give a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral and angle ABC = 108°. Work out angle ADC and give a reason. **[2]**

**(c)** The line TA is a tangent to the circle at A. OA = 9 cm and OT = 15 cm. Work out the length TA. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** Sum = (10 − 2) × 180 = 1440 [1]; each angle = 1440 ÷ 10 = **144°** [1].
**(b)** Exterior angle = 180 − 156 = 24° [1]; sides = 360 ÷ 24 [1] = **15** [1].

**2.** **(a)** Angles ABC + ACB = 180 − 44 = 136° [1]; ABC = 136 ÷ 2 = **68°** [1].
**(b)** The base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal, and angles in a triangle add to 180° [1].

**3.** **(a)** **72°** [1].
**(b)** Co-interior angles between parallel lines add to 180° (180 − 108 = 72) [1].

**4.** Open the compasses to more than half the length of PQ [1]. Draw arcs above and below the line centred on P and then on Q, with the same radius, so they cross at two points [1]. Join the two crossing points with a straight line; this is the perpendicular bisector [1].

**5.** **(a)** The perpendicular bisector of AB [1].
**(b)** A rectangle around the fence with semicircular ends (a "racetrack" shape), including the fence itself [1]; the sides are straight lines 2 m from the fence and each end is a semicircle of radius 2 m centred on the end of the fence [1].

**6.** **(a)** AB = DE, angle ABC = angle DEF, and BC = EF: two sides and the included angle are equal, so the triangles are congruent by **SAS** **[1] [1]**.
**(b)** Each triangle has a right angle, hypotenuse 13 cm (PR = XZ) and one other equal side (QR = YZ): congruent by **RHS** **[1] [1]**.

**7.** Let angle BAC = a and angle ABC = b. Angles in triangle ABC add to 180°, so angle ACB = 180 − a − b [1]. Angles on the straight line BCD add to 180°, so angle ACD = 180 − angle ACB [1] = 180 − (180 − a − b) = a + b, which is the sum of angle BAC and angle ABC [1].

**8.** **(a)** Multiply each coordinate by 3/4: **(3, 3), (9, 3), (9, 6)** **[2]** (one mark for two correct points).
**(b)** D = **(2, 5)** [1]. AC = √((8 − 2)² + (5 − 1)²) = √(36 + 16) [1] = √52 = **7.21 cm** (3 s.f.) [1].

**9.** **(a)** F + V − E = 2, so 7 + 10 − E = 2 [1], giving E = **15** [1].
**(b)** A pentagon [1].

**10.** (Higher tier only) Each point is multiplied by −2: (1, 1) goes to (−2, −2) [1]; (3, 1) goes to (−6, −2) [1]; (3, 2) goes to (−6, −4) [1].

**11.** (Higher tier only) **(a)** (3, 2) goes to (−3, 2) [1] and then to **(−3, −2)** [1].
**(b)** A **rotation** [1] of **180°** about the origin [1].

**12.** (Higher tier only) **(a)** Angle AOB = 2 × 37 = **74°** [1]; the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference [1].
**(b)** Angle ADC = 180 − 108 = **72°** [1]; opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180° [1].
**(c)** Angle OAT = 90° because a tangent is perpendicular to the radius [1]. TA² = 15² − 9² = 225 − 81 = 144 [1]; TA = **12 cm** [1].

## Which questions are Higher tier only?

Questions 10 to 12 test content that appears only in the Higher tier column of the specification: negative scale factors (G7), combinations of transformations (G8) and circle theorems (G10). Questions 1 to 9 use content that both tiers are assessed on, including fractional scale factors (question 8).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling angles "Z angles" or "F angles" instead of naming them.
- Forgetting to give a reason when the question asks for one.
- Using the wrong tool for the construction, or rubbing out arcs.
- Writing SSA as a congruence condition.
- Describing a transformation without the centre or angle.
