Full explanations are in the [mood disorders study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-psychology-9990-mood-affective-disorders-depressive-disorder-unipolar-and-bipolar-disorder/); test yourself with the [mood disorders practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-psychology-9990-mood-affective-disorders-depressive-disorder-unipolar-and-bipolar-disorder-practice/).

## 1.2.1 Diagnosis

| Episode or disorder | Key features |
|---|---|
| Depressive episode | Almost daily depressed mood or loss of interest for **at least two weeks**, plus symptoms such as poor concentration, worthlessness or guilt, hopelessness, thoughts of death, appetite or sleep change, psychomotor change, fatigue |
| Manic episode | **At least one week** of euphoria, irritability or expansiveness with increased activity or energy; rapid speech, racing thoughts, less need for sleep, impulsivity |
| Hypomanic episode | Milder elevation without marked impairment |
| Bipolar type I | At least one manic or mixed episode |
| Bipolar type II | At least one hypomanic and one depressive episode; no manic episode |

**Beck Depression Inventory**: 21 items, each 0–3, total 0–63. BDI-II bands: 0–13 minimal, 14–19 mild, 20–28 moderate, 29–63 severe.

- Strengths: quick, standardised, quantitative, tracks change.
- Weaknesses: self-report bias; depends on insight; may miss depression expressed through physical symptoms in some cultures.

## 1.2.2 Explanations

| Explanation | Core idea | Evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| Biochemical (monoamine) | Low serotonin and noradrenaline | Antidepressants support it; but mood lifts weeks after levels change; reductionist |
| Genetic | Inherited susceptibility | Family and twin studies; Oruč et al. show only small effects |
| Beck's cognitive theory | Negative schemas, automatic thoughts, negative triad, cognitive errors | Supported by cognitive therapy outcomes; cause or symptom? |
| Learned helplessness / attributional style | Uncontrollable events teach passivity; internal, stable, global attributions | Seligman et al. (1988): style became more optimistic with therapy; animal origins |

### Key study: Oruč et al. (1997)

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Aim | Association between bipolar disorder and 5-HT2C receptor and 5-HTT genes |
| Sample | 42 patients with bipolar I disorder; 40 healthy controls |
| Method | Genetic association analysis of DNA |
| Results | No significant association overall; trends in female patients for both genes |
| Conclusion | Minor increase in susceptibility at most, possibly in women |
| Evaluation | Objective, replicable; small sample; bipolar not unipolar; trend not significant |

## 1.2.3 Treatments

| Treatment | How it works | Drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Tricyclics | Block reuptake of serotonin and noradrenaline | Dry mouth, drowsiness; dangerous in overdose |
| MAOIs | Inhibit monoamine oxidase | Tyramine diet restriction |
| SSRIs | Block serotonin reuptake | Weeks to work; nausea |
| Beck's cognitive restructuring | Thought diary; test thoughts against evidence | Needs motivation and time |
| Ellis's REBT | ABC, then Disputing and Effective new beliefs | Can feel confrontational |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Unipolar | Mood disturbance in one direction (depression only) |
| Mixed episode | Manic and depressive symptoms together or rapidly alternating |
| Monoamines | Neurotransmitters including serotonin, noradrenaline and dopamine |
| Reuptake | Reabsorption of a neurotransmitter by the neuron that released it |
| Polymorphism | A common variation in a gene |
| Association study | Compares gene variants in patients and controls |
| Schema | Mental framework of beliefs built from experience |
| Automatic negative thoughts | Quick, unchallenged negative interpretations |
| Collaborative empiricism | Client and therapist test beliefs together like scientists |
| Irrational belief | Rigid, demanding belief, often a "must" |
| Psychometric | Standardised measurement of a psychological characteristic |

## Issues and debates hooks

- **Nature versus nurture**: small genetic effects plus life events; schemas from childhood.
- **Reductionism**: monoamine hypothesis versus cognitive and social factors.
- **Determinism versus free will**: biological accounts imply little control; cognitive therapies assume change is possible.
- **Individual and situational**: attributional style is individual; uncontrollable events are situational.
- **Cultural differences**: physical expression of distress; BDI norms.

## Exam traps

- Saying bipolar II includes mania: it includes hypomania.
- Giving the depressive-episode duration as one week (that is mania).
- Calling Oruč et al.'s result significant.
- Writing that SSRIs block noradrenaline reuptake.
- Describing learned helplessness without the attributional reformulation when the syllabus names attributional style.

## Self-test

1. How long must a depressive episode last in ICD-11?
2. What distinguishes bipolar type I from type II?
3. How many items does the BDI have, and how is each scored?
4. Give one cultural criticism of the BDI.
5. Which genes did Oruč et al. study?
6. What did Oruč et al. find in female patients?
7. Name the three parts of Beck's negative triad.
8. Define selective abstraction.
9. Describe a depressive attributional style.
10. What is the "cheese effect" risk with MAOIs?
11. What does the D stand for in REBT, and give one type.
12. Give one criticism of the monoamine hypothesis.

**Answers:** (1) at least two weeks. (2) type I has at least one manic or mixed episode; type II has hypomanic and depressive episodes but no mania. (3) 21; 0 to 3. (4) in some cultures depression is expressed more through physical complaints, which the items may underweight. (5) the 5-HT2C receptor gene and the serotonin transporter gene. (6) trends towards association with both genes. (7) self, world, future. (8) focusing on one negative detail and ignoring the wider picture. (9) internal, stable and global explanations for bad events. (10) tyramine-rich foods such as some cheeses can cause a dangerous rise in blood pressure. (11) Disputing; logical, empirical or pragmatic. (12) antidepressants raise monoamines quickly but mood takes weeks to improve.
