This set practises **3.3.4.2 Addition reactions of alkenes** from section 3.3.4 Alkenes of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 2 and in the synoptic Unit 5. Revise first with the [addition reactions study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-4-2/) or the [addition reactions revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-4-2-revision-notes/). Take Ar values as H = 1.0, C = 12.0 and Br = 79.9.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Which carbocation is the most stable?

A: CH₃CH₂CH₂C⁺H₂

B: CH₃CH₂C⁺HCH₃

C: (CH₃)₃C⁺

D: (CH₃)₂CHC⁺H₂ **[1]**

**2.** State the conditions used to add sulfuric acid to an alkene. **[1]**

**3.** Name the product of the reaction between pent-1-ene and bromine. **[1]**

**4.** Describe how bromine water is used to show that a liquid is unsaturated. Give the result for an unsaturated and for a saturated liquid. **[2]**

**5.** Classify each carbocation as primary, secondary or tertiary.

**(a)** (CH₃)₂C⁺CH₂CH₃ **[1]**

**(b)** CH₃CH₂C⁺H₂ **[1]**

**(c)** the cation formed when H⁺ adds to C1 of cyclohexene (the positive charge is on a ring carbon bonded to two other ring carbons) **[1]**

**6.** Explain why but-2-ene gives only one structural product with hydrogen bromide. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** 2-Methylpropene, (CH₃)₂C=CH₂, reacts with hydrogen bromide.

**(a)** Outline the mechanism for the formation of the major product. **[4]**

**(b)** Name the minor product and explain why it forms in smaller amounts. **[3]**

**8.** Propene reacts with cold, concentrated sulfuric acid.

**(a)** Outline the mechanism for the formation of the major product. Show the structure of the product. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why this product is the major one. **[2]**

**9.** 3-Methylbut-1-ene, CH₂=CHCH(CH₃)₂, reacts with HBr. Give the structures of the major and minor products and explain which is major. **[4]**

**10.** Two different alkenes with formula C₅H₁₀ both give 2-bromo-2-methylbutane, (CH₃)₂CBrCH₂CH₃, as the major product with HBr. Name both alkenes and give the structure of the minor product formed from each. **[4]**

**11.** A 1.40 g mixture of pent-1-ene, C₅H₁₀, and pentane reacts with exactly 12.5 cm³ of a 0.800 mol dm⁻³ solution of bromine in an inert solvent. Calculate the percentage by mass of pent-1-ene in the mixture. **[3]**

**12.** 1.64 g of cyclohexene, C₆H₁₀, reacts completely with bromine. Calculate the maximum mass of 1,2-dibromocyclohexane, C₆H₁₀Br₂, that can form. **[2]**

**13.** A student's mechanism for ethene with bromine shows: no dipole on Br₂; a curly arrow from one carbon atom of ethene to a bromine atom; and the product drawn as CH₃CHBr₂. Identify **three** errors. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]

**2.** Cold, concentrated sulfuric acid [1]

**3.** **1,2-Dibromopentane** [1]

**4.** Shake the liquid with bromine water [1]; unsaturated: orange to colourless; saturated: stays orange [1]

**5. (a)** Tertiary [1]

**(b)** Primary [1]

**(c)** Secondary [1]

**6.** But-2-ene is symmetrical: both C=C carbons carry one H and one CH₃ [1]; whichever carbon H adds to, the same secondary carbocation forms, giving only 2-bromobutane [1]

**7. (a)** Dipole Hδ+–Brδ− shown [1]; arrow from the C=C bond to H, and arrow from the H–Br bond to Br [1]; tertiary carbocation (CH₃)₂C⁺CH₃ drawn [1]; arrow from a lone pair on Br⁻ to C⁺, giving 2-bromo-2-methylpropane [1]

**(b)** **1-Bromo-2-methylpropane** [1]; it forms via a primary carbocation, (CH₃)₂CHC⁺H₂, which has only one electron-releasing alkyl group [1]; the primary carbocation is less stable than the tertiary one, so it forms less readily [1]

**8. (a)** Arrow from the C=C bond to an H of H₂SO₄ [1]; arrow from the O–H bond to O, giving ⁻OSO₂OH [1]; secondary carbocation CH₃C⁺HCH₃ [1]; arrow from a lone pair on the negatively charged O of hydrogensulfate to C⁺; product CH₃CH(OSO₂OH)CH₃ [1]

**(b)** The alternative carbocation, CH₃CH₂C⁺H₂, is primary [1]; the secondary carbocation is more stable because it has two electron-releasing alkyl groups, so it forms more readily [1]

**9.** Major: **CH₃CHBrCH(CH₃)₂** (2-bromo-3-methylbutane) [1]; minor: **BrCH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)₂** (1-bromo-3-methylbutane) [1]; the major product comes from the secondary carbocation CH₃C⁺HCH(CH₃)₂ [1]; which is more stable than the primary carbocation ⁺CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)₂ because more alkyl groups release electrons towards C⁺ [1] **[4]**

**10.** **2-Methylbut-1-ene**, CH₂=C(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ [1]; its minor product is BrCH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ [1]; **2-methylbut-2-ene**, (CH₃)₂C=CHCH₃ [1]; its minor product is (CH₃)₂CHCHBrCH₃ [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Both alkenes give the same tertiary carbocation, so the same major product; only the minor products differ.

**11.** n(Br₂) = 12.5 ÷ 1000 × 0.800 = 0.0100 mol [1]; n(pent-1-ene) = 0.0100 mol, so mass = 0.0100 × 70.0 = 0.700 g [1]; percentage = 0.700 ÷ 1.40 × 100 = **50.0 %** [1] **[3]**

**12.** n(C₆H₁₀) = 1.64 ÷ 82.0 = 0.0200 mol [1]; mass = 0.0200 × 241.8 = **4.84 g** [1]

**13.** No induced dipole (δ+/δ−) shown on Br₂ [1]; the first arrow must start from the C=C bond, not from a carbon atom [1]; the product should be CH₂BrCH₂Br (1,2-dibromoethane), with one Br on each carbon [1] **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Arrows starting at a carbon atom instead of the double bond.
- Hydrogensulfate attached through sulfur.
- Only one carbocation considered when explaining major and minor products.
- "More reactive" used where "more stable" is meant.
- Bromine water "goes clear".
