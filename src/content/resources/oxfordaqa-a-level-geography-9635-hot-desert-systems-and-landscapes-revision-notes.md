Condensed recall for **3.1.2 Section B: Hot desert systems and landscapes** (OxfordAQA International
AS and A-level Geography 9635, Unit 1, option 1a). For the full explanation, read the
[Physical Geography 1 study guide](/resources/a-level-oxfordaqa-geography-physical-geography-1/).

## Systems ideas

Use **inputs, outputs, energy, stores, flows, feedback and dynamic equilibrium**. Add **sediment
sources, cells and budgets**: a desert landscape balances sediment gained against sediment lost.

## Causes of aridity

| Cause | Why it dries the climate |
|---|---|
| Pressure | Sinking air under high pressure warms, so cloud and rain are suppressed |
| Winds | Prevailing winds from land bring little moisture |
| Continentality | Far from the sea, little moisture arrives |
| Relief | Rain shadow: air dried as it crosses mountains |
| Cold ocean currents | Cool the air above, reducing evaporation and rain on nearby coasts |

The **water balance** and the **aridity index** (rainfall compared with potential evaporation)
define dryness.

## Arid weathering

- **Thermal fracture:** heating and cooling cause expansion and contraction until rock cracks.
- **Exfoliation:** outer layers peel off in sheets.
- **Chemical weathering:** limited by lack of water but still occurs.
- **Block disintegration:** rock breaks along joints into blocks.
- **Granular disintegration:** individual grains loosen from the rock.

## Wind

- **Erosion:** **deflation** lifts loose particles; **abrasion** wears surfaces with wind-blown grains.
- **Transport:** suspension (fine dust), saltation (bouncing sand), surface creep (rolling).
- **Deposition** occurs when wind energy falls.

## Water

Water sources are **exogenous** (from outside, such as a river from wetter regions), **endorheic**
(drains inland, never reaching the sea) and **ephemeral** (short-lived). Water acts **episodically**:
**sheet flooding** and **channel flash flooding** after rare intense rain do much of the erosion
and transport.

## Landforms

| Wind (aeolian) | Water |
|---|---|
| Deflation hollows | Wadis (dry valleys) |
| Desert pavements | Bahadas (merged fans) |
| Ventifacts (wind-shaped stones) | Pediments (gentle rock slopes) |
| Yardangs (streamlined ridges) | Playas (dry lake beds) |
| Zeugen (table-like rock masses) | Inselbergs (isolated hills) |
| Barchan and seif dunes | |

## Desertification

Know the **changing extent of hot deserts over the last 10 000 years**, the causes (**climate
change and human impact**), areas at risk, impacts and **alternative futures**.

## Case studies

A hot desert environment engaging with field data, and a local-scale landscape where desertification
has occurred. Use named places and figures you are sure of.

## Exam traps

- Saying deserts are shaped only by wind: episodic water matters.
- Naming landforms with no formation process.
- Mixing up exogenous, endorheic and ephemeral.
- Treating desertification as natural only, or human only.
- Explaining aridity with one cause only.

## Self-test

1. Why does high pressure cause aridity?
2. Distinguish deflation from abrasion.
3. Name the three modes of wind transport.
4. What is a wadi?
5. Give two causes of desertification.

**Answers:** 1. Sinking air warms, which suppresses condensation and rain. 2. Deflation removes loose
particles; abrasion wears surfaces with wind-blown grains. 3. Suspension, saltation, surface creep.
4. A dry valley carved by water in rare floods. 5. Climate change and human impact.
