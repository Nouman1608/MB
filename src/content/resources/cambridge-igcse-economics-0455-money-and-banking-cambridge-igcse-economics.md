This guide covers section 3.1, Money and banking, of the Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455 syllabus for examination in 2026. The syllabus has two parts: 3.1.1 money ("the forms, functions and characteristics of money") and 3.1.2 banking ("the role and importance of central banks and commercial banks for government, producers and consumers"). All people, banks and figures below are invented.

The combined [money, banking and household spending guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-money-banking-and-household-spending-saving-and/) lists the functions, characteristics and bank roles. This page builds the skills that turn those lists into marks: testing whether an unfamiliar item works as money, explaining what happens when a function breaks down, and showing why a bank's role matters to a named group. ## 3.1.1 Money

### Linking functions to characteristics

Functions are what money **does**; characteristics are what money **is like**. Examiners reward answers that connect the two, because each function depends on particular qualities.

| Function | Characteristics it depends on | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Medium of exchange | Acceptability, portability, divisibility, security | Sellers must take it, buyers must carry it, small and large payments must be possible, and fakes must be rare |
| Unit of account (measure of value) | Uniformity, divisibility | Every unit must be identical so prices can be compared; small units allow precise prices |
| Store of value | Durability, limited supply | It must not rot or wear out, and its value must not collapse because more is created |
| Standard of deferred payment | Limited supply, acceptability over time | A lender must expect the money repaid later to still be accepted and worth something |

### Testing an unfamiliar item

A common question gives an item and asks whether it would make good money. Work through the characteristics, then link failures to functions.

**Worked example 1 (invented).** On Island R, people have started to pay for goods with bags of rice. Assess rice as money.

| Characteristic | Rice | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Acceptability | Everyone eats rice, so most sellers accept it | Passes |
| Durability | Spoils if damp; eaten by pests | Weak |
| Portability | Heavy for large payments | Fails for large values |
| Divisibility | Can be measured out in small amounts | Passes |
| Uniformity | Quality varies from bag to bag | Weak |
| Limited supply | A good harvest suddenly increases the supply | Weak |
| Security | Hard to fake, but easy to mix with poor grain | Mixed |

**Judgement:** rice works as a medium of exchange for small daily trades, but it is a poor store of value (it spoils and its value falls after a large harvest) and a poor standard of deferred payment (a lender cannot be sure what a bag will be worth next year). It would be replaced as soon as a more durable, uniform item became accepted.

### Forms of money and trust

Modern notes and coins are worth far more than the paper or metal they are made from. They work because people trust that others will accept them and that the central bank will limit how many are issued. Bank deposits are money for the same reason: a seller accepts a card payment because the deposit can be spent in turn. Mobile payment systems move deposit money; they are a way of transferring money, not a separate kind of value.

### When a function breaks down

**Worked example 2 (invented).** In Country V prices rise by 40% in one year. Kemal holds 1,000 in cash.

```
Real value after one year = 1,000 / 1.40 = 714.29 (in last year's prices)
Loss of purchasing power = 1,000 - 714.29 = 285.71, about 28.6%
```

Consequences, function by function:

- **Store of value:** households rush to spend cash or buy goods and foreign currency instead of holding money.
- **Unit of account:** shops change prices so often that comparing values becomes hard.
- **Standard of deferred payment:** lenders refuse long loans, or demand very high interest, because repayments will be worth much less.
- **Medium of exchange:** if trust collapses entirely, people return to barter or use a foreign currency.

This chain is useful when a question asks about the importance of money keeping its value.

## 3.1.2 Banking

### What a commercial bank balances

A commercial bank aims to make a profit, but it must also be able to repay depositors on demand. Lending more earns more interest but leaves less cash for withdrawals.

**Worked example 3 (invented).** Harbour Bank holds deposits of 50 million. It pays savers 1.5% and charges borrowers 8%. It can keep 10% or 25% of deposits as cash reserves and lend the rest.

| | Keeps 10% | Keeps 25% |
|---|---|---|
| Loans | 45 million | 37.5 million |
| Interest received (8%) | 3.6 million | 3.0 million |
| Interest paid to savers (1.5% of 50 million) | 0.75 million | 0.75 million |
| Margin before running costs | 2.85 million | 2.25 million |

Keeping 10% earns 0.6 million more, but if savers suddenly want to withdraw 8 million, the bank holding only 5 million in cash cannot pay. A loss of confidence could spread to other banks. This is why central banks supervise commercial banks and act as lender of last resort.

### The roles that matter to each group

Rather than one list, link each role to the group that gains from it. Questions often name one group.

**Government**

- *Central bank:* holds the government's accounts, manages its borrowing, carries out monetary policy and manages foreign currency reserves.
- *Commercial banks:* collect tax payments, lend to government by buying its debt, and process payments of wages and benefits.

**Producers**

- *Central bank:* keeps the financial system stable and sets the interest rate that influences what firms pay to borrow.
- *Commercial banks:* lend for machinery and stock, give overdrafts to cover gaps in cash flow, and provide foreign currency for importers and exporters.

**Consumers**

- *Central bank:* keeps confidence in the currency and protects deposits through supervision.
- *Commercial banks:* provide safe accounts, interest on savings, loans and mortgages for large purchases, and card and transfer services.

### Why banks are important: building the chain

Banks are **financial intermediaries**: they collect the savings of many households and lend them to people and firms who want to spend more than their income now.

**Annotated model answer.** *Explain why commercial banks are important to producers. [4]*

> Commercial banks lend to firms [role]. A small factory may not have enough savings to buy a new machine, but a bank loan lets it buy the machine now and repay from future revenue [developed with an example]. The machine raises output and may lower costs per unit, so the firm can grow and employ more workers [consequence for the producer]. Banks also provide overdrafts, which let a firm pay suppliers before its customers have paid it, so production does not stop [second role, developed].

## Common mistakes

- Listing characteristics when the question asks for functions, or the reverse.
- Saying money "is" valuable because of what it is made from. Modern money depends on trust and limited supply.
- Describing the central bank as a bank for households. Ordinary people do not usually hold accounts there.
- Explaining a role without saying why it matters to the group named in the question.
- Confusing interest paid to savers with interest charged to borrowers.

## Exam technique

- **Identify / State:** a single function, characteristic or role in a few words.
- **Explain:** link the characteristic to the function it supports, or the role to the group it helps.
- **Analyse:** follow a chain, for example from rising prices to the loss of each function.
- **Discuss:** weigh, for example, whether an item would make good money, then give a judgement on the balance of its strengths and weaknesses.
