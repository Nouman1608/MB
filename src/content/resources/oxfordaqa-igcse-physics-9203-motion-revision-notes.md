Condensed recall for **3.1.2 Motion** in OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203), for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [Motion study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-motion/); exam-style questions with answers are in the [Motion practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-motion-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Distance | How far an object travels along its path; a scalar, in m |
| Displacement | Straight-line distance from the start in a stated direction; a vector, in m |
| Speed | Distance travelled per unit time; a scalar, in m/s |
| Velocity | Speed in a given direction; a vector, in m/s |
| Distance-time graph | Graph of distance from a fixed point (y-axis) against time (x-axis) |
| Gradient | Change in y divided by change in x; on a distance-time graph it equals speed |
| Tangent | A straight line touching a curve at one point with the same slope there |
| Uniform motion | Motion at constant speed |
| Non-uniform motion | Motion in which the speed changes |
| Average speed | Total distance divided by total time, including any stops |

## The equation

```
v = s / t     velocity (m/s) = displacement (m) / time (s)
s = v x t     t = s / v
```

For non-uniform motion, the same form gives the average speed: total distance ÷ total time.

## Reading a distance-time graph

| Feature | Tells you |
|---|---|
| Horizontal line | Stationary |
| Straight rising line | Constant speed away from the reference point |
| Straight falling line | Constant speed back towards the reference point |
| Steeper line | Higher speed |
| Curve becoming steeper | Speed increasing |
| Curve becoming flatter | Speed decreasing |
| Gradient of a straight section | The speed in that section |
| Gradient of a tangent | The speed at that instant |

## Method: gradient of a straight section

1. Pick two points far apart on the line itself.
2. Read their coordinates and write them down.
3. Speed = (distance 2 − distance 1) ÷ (time 2 − time 1).
4. Add the unit, m/s.

## Method: speed at an instant on a curve

1. Rule a tangent touching the curve at the required time.
2. Extend it and form a large triangle on the tangent.
3. Gradient of the tangent = instantaneous speed.

## Method: average speed

1. Add every distance, in metres.
2. Add every time, in seconds, including stops.
3. Divide total distance by total time.

## Conversions

| Change | Rule |
|---|---|
| km → m | × 1000 |
| min → s | × 60 |
| h → s | × 3600 |
| km/h → m/s | ÷ 3.6 |
| m/s → km/h | × 3.6 |

## Graph-drawing checklist

- Time on the x-axis, distance on the y-axis.
- Axis labels with units, for example "distance / m".
- Linear scales using more than half the grid in each direction.
- Points plotted to within half a small square.
- One line of best fit: ruled if straight, smooth if curved.

## Two objects on one graph

- The steeper line belongs to the faster object.
- Where two lines cross, the objects are at the same distance from the reference point at the same time: one catches up with or passes the other.
- Parallel straight lines mean equal speeds; the gap between them stays the same.
- A line that starts higher on the y-axis began further from the reference point; a line that starts further along the x-axis set off later.

## Speed, distance, displacement: a quick check

A runner completes one lap of a 400 m track in 80 s and finishes at the start line.

- Distance = 400 m; average speed = 400 ÷ 80 = 5.0 m/s.
- Displacement = 0 m; average velocity = 0 m/s.

## Not in this section

Acceleration, velocity-time graphs and the area under a graph are in 3.1.3 Resultant forces. If a graph has velocity on the y-axis, a flat line means constant velocity, not stationary.

## Exam traps

- **Falling line.** On a distance-from-a-point graph it means returning, not slowing.
- **Averaging speeds.** Never take the mean of two speeds when the times differ.
- **Stops count.** Time spent stationary is part of the total time.
- **Velocity needs direction.** "8 m/s" is a speed; "8 m/s west" is a velocity.
- **Tangent coordinates.** Read them off the tangent, not the curve.
- **Small triangles.** They magnify reading errors; use a large one.
- **Units first.** Convert km and minutes before substituting.

## Self-test

**1.** State what the gradient of a distance-time graph represents.

**2.** Describe the motion shown by a horizontal line on a distance-time graph.

**3.** A distance-time line goes from (0 s, 0 m) to (25 s, 150 m). Find the speed.

**4.** A walker's distance from home falls steadily from 900 m to 300 m in 400 s. Find the speed and describe the direction.

**5.** Convert 90 km/h to m/s.

**6.** A plane flies 1200 m in 4.0 s. Find its speed.

**7.** How far does a runner go in 45 s at 6.0 m/s?

**8.** How long does a ship take to travel 2.4 km at 8.0 m/s?

**9.** A bus travels 3.0 km in 6.0 minutes and then waits 4.0 minutes at a stop. Find its average speed for the 10 minutes.

**10.** What is the difference between speed and velocity?

**11.** A tangent to a curve passes through (1.0 s, 2.0 m) and (4.0 s, 20 m). Find the speed at the touching point.

**12.** A curve on a distance-time graph becomes less steep. What is happening to the speed?

## Self-test answers

**1.** The speed of the object.

**2.** The object is stationary; its distance from the point is not changing.

**3.** 150 ÷ 25 = 6.0 m/s.

**4.** (900 − 300) ÷ 400 = 1.5 m/s, moving towards home.

**5.** 90 ÷ 3.6 = 25 m/s.

**6.** 1200 ÷ 4.0 = 300 m/s.

**7.** s = 6.0 × 45 = 270 m.

**8.** t = 2400 ÷ 8.0 = 300 s (5.0 minutes).

**9.** 3000 m ÷ 600 s = 5.0 m/s.

**10.** Velocity is speed in a given direction; velocity is a vector and speed is a scalar.

**11.** (20 − 2.0) ÷ (4.0 − 1.0) = 18 ÷ 3.0 = 6.0 m/s.

**12.** The speed is decreasing.
