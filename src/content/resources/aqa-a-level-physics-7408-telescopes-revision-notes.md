Condensed notes for sub-section 3.9.1 Telescopes of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408, AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards), part of the Astrophysics option (A-level only, Paper 3 Section B). Worked examples are in the [telescopes study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-telescopes/); test yourself with the [telescopes practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-telescopes-practice/). ## Equations

| Equation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| M = β/α | angular magnification: angle subtended by image at eye ÷ angle subtended by object at unaided eye |
| M = fₒ/fₑ | in normal adjustment |
| separation = fₒ + fₑ | lens spacing in normal adjustment |
| θ ≈ λ/D | Rayleigh criterion: minimum angular resolution (rad) |
| collecting power ∝ D² | energy collected per second depends on aperture area |
| feature size ≈ θ × distance | smallest resolvable detail on a distant object |

## 3.9.1.1 Refracting telescope: recall

- **Objective**: long focal length, collects light, forms a real inverted image at its focal plane.
- **Eyepiece**: short focal length, acts as a magnifier of that image.
- **Normal adjustment**: principal foci coincide; final image at infinity.
- Final image is **inverted**.
- Ray diagram checklist: three parallel incoming rays at α; one undeviated through objective centre; rays meet at one point in the shared focal plane; construction line through eyepiece centre; rays emerge parallel to it at β.

## 3.9.1.2 Reflecting telescopes: recall

- **Cassegrain**: parabolic concave primary → convex secondary → back through a hole in the primary → eyepiece behind it.
- **Spherical aberration**: edge and centre rays focus at different points; avoided with a parabolic mirror.
- **Chromatic aberration**: lenses refract colours differently (blue focuses closer); mirrors are free of it.

| Reflectors: advantages | Refractors: disadvantages |
|---|---|
| no chromatic aberration | chromatic aberration |
| supported from behind, can be very large | large lenses sag; supported only at edge |
| one surface to perfect | glass must be flawless throughout |
| little absorption | glass absorbs some wavelengths |

- Reflector drawback: the secondary mirror blocks some light and causes extra diffraction.

## 3.9.1.3 Non-optical telescopes: recall

| Type | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Radio | ground | atmosphere transparent to most radio wavelengths; works day, night and through cloud |
| Infrared | high dry sites or space | water vapour absorbs I-R; cooled detectors |
| Ultraviolet | space | atmosphere absorbs U-V |
| X-ray | space | atmosphere absorbs X-rays; grazing-incidence mirrors |

- Radio vs optical: both parabolic reflectors; dish may be mesh if gaps ≪ λ; much poorer resolving power for same D; much larger D possible, so greater collecting power; affected by man-made interference.

## 3.9.1.4 Large diameters and detectors: recall

- **Rayleigh criterion**: just resolved when central maximum of one pattern is on the first minimum of the other.
- Larger D → smaller θ → **better resolution**.
- Larger D → **more collecting power** (∝ D²) → fainter objects, shorter exposures.
- **Quantum efficiency**: fraction of incident photons that are detected.
- CCD vs eye: CCD much higher quantum efficiency; resolution set by pixel spacing (can be finer than the eye's receptor spacing); CCD integrates long exposures and gives a storable, processable digital image.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Normal adjustment | Telescope set so the final image is at infinity |
| Angular magnification | Ratio of angles subtended by the image and by the object |
| Objective | Lens or mirror that collects light from the object |
| Eyepiece | Lens that magnifies the intermediate image |
| Spherical aberration | Blurring because rays at different distances from the axis focus at different points |
| Chromatic aberration | Coloured fringes because different wavelengths focus at different points |
| Minimum angular resolution | Smallest angular separation at which two sources can be distinguished |
| Collecting power | Energy collected per second, ∝ D² |
| Quantum efficiency | Proportion of incident photons a detector records |

## Exam traps

- fₑ in mm must become metres before using M = fₒ/fₑ with fₒ in metres.
- M uses angles, not heights or sizes.
- λ and D in the same unit; θ in radians.
- Collecting power ratio uses D², resolution ratio uses D.
- "Reflectors are better because they are bigger" needs the reason: supported from behind.
- Radio dishes do not need to be in space.

## Self-test

**1.** State the lens separation of a telescope in normal adjustment.
**2.** fₒ = 0.80 m, fₑ = 20 mm. Find M.
**3.** Is the final image upright or inverted?
**4.** Name the two mirrors in a Cassegrain telescope and their shapes.
**5.** What causes chromatic aberration?
**6.** Why do parabolic mirrors avoid spherical aberration?
**7.** Why can radio telescopes work in cloudy weather?
**8.** Why must X-ray telescopes be in space?
**9.** Find θ for a 2.0 m mirror at 600 nm.
**10.** How many times greater is the collecting power of a 4.0 m mirror than a 1.0 m mirror?
**11.** Define quantum efficiency.
**12.** Give two reasons a CCD is more convenient than the eye.

## Self-test answers

**1.** fₒ + fₑ.
**2.** M = 0.80/0.020 = 40.
**3.** Inverted.
**4.** Primary: concave parabolic; secondary: convex.
**5.** The refractive index of glass varies with wavelength, so colours focus at different points.
**6.** A parabolic mirror brings all rays parallel to its axis to a single focus.
**7.** The atmosphere, including cloud, is transparent to most radio wavelengths.
**8.** The atmosphere absorbs X-rays.
**9.** θ ≈ 600 × 10⁻⁹/2.0 = 3.0 × 10⁻⁷ rad.
**10.** (4.0/1.0)² = 16.
**11.** The fraction of incident photons that a detector actually detects.
**12.** Long exposures accumulate faint light; the image is digital, so it can be stored and processed.
