| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Article 48 | President's power to rule by emergency decree |
| Proportional representation | Seats in proportion to votes — many parties, weak coalitions |
| *Diktat* | "Dictated peace": German view of Versailles |
| Stab in the back | Myth that the army was betrayed by politicians in 1918 |
| Hyperinflation | Prices rising out of control, 1923 |

| SA | Nazi stormtroopers, led by Röhm |
| SS / Gestapo | Elite guard under Himmler / secret state police |

## Nazi control at a glance

| Method | Examples | How successful? |
|---|---|---|
| Terror | SS, Gestapo, concentration camps, informers | Silenced most open opposition |
| Propaganda | Goebbels, rallies, cheap radios, film, Olympics | Strong among the young and those who gained work |
| Censorship | Book burning (May 1933), controlled press | Limited what Germans could know |
| Winning support | Falling unemployment, Strength Through Joy | Many accepted the regime; workers' wages remained low |
| Churches | Concordat (1933); Reich Church; arrests of critics | Partial — Churches remained the largest independent institutions |

## Worked example: two effects

*Explain two effects of the Depression on Germany, 1929–32.*

1. **Mass unemployment** — US loans were recalled and firms collapsed, so unemployment rose to about
   six million by 1932; this made millions of voters willing to listen to extremist promises of
   "work and bread".
2. **Political breakdown** — coalitions could not agree on cuts, so chancellors such as Brüning
   governed by Article 48 decrees; democracy was already being bypassed before Hitler took office.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Getting the order of 1933–34 wrong:** Fire → Enabling Act → banning of parties and unions →
  Long Knives → Hindenburg's death → Führer.
- **Saying Hitler was elected to power.** He was **appointed** chancellor by Hindenburg; the Nazis
  never won a majority in a free election.
- **Over-crediting Stresemann.** His recovery relied on short-term American loans — a weakness
  exposed in 1929.
- **Ignoring the extract's author.** In the impression question, work from the extract's words.
- **Treating Weimar's problems as one block.** Separate political, economic and foreign-policy
  problems in essays.
- **Running past 1945.** Post-war Germany is not part of this depth study.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which date marks the start of the dictatorship?**
Most answers use the Enabling Act (March 1933)
for legal dictatorship, and August 1934 for Hitler as Führer with total power.

**How did unemployment fall so fast?**
Public works such as the autobahns, the Labour Service,
rearmament and conscription — and statistics that left out women and Jews dismissed from work.

**Was there effective opposition?**
It existed (Edelweiss Pirates, White Rose, July Plot) but never
threatened the regime until military defeat.
