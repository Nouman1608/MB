Study first with the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-emergence-of-modern-china/) and the [revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-emergence-of-modern-china-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Explain two reasons why warlordism weakened China after 1916. **[4]**

**2.** Read the extract, which is *constructed for practice* and is not a real quotation.

> "The conference gave our province to the island empire, and we were told it was the will of the great powers. The students did not wait. They marched, they struck, and the ministers who signed away our rights lost their posts." (A student's diary of 1919, constructed for practice)

**(a)** Explain what the extract suggests about the causes of the May Fourth Movement. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one limitation of a student's diary for a historian studying the movement's impact. **[3]**

**3.** Analyse the importance of the Long March in the rise of communism in China. **[6]**

**4.** To what extent was the Second Sino-Japanese War the main reason for the Communist victory in the Chinese Civil War? **[15]**

**5.** Evaluate the successes and failures of Guomindang rule during the Nanjing decade (1927 to 1937). **[15]**

**6.** Explain the importance of the Xi'an Incident (1936). **[6]**

**7.** Explain two ways in which the Twenty-One Demands affected Chinese nationalism. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, each developed (1 mark for the reason, 1 for explanation). Provincial armies took taxes and ran regions independently, so the government in Beijing had no income or authority; warlord wars devastated areas and caused banditry, which hurt farmers and trade; foreign powers dealt with different warlords and used the division to gain concessions; the lack of unity meant China could not resist demands such as those of Japan. **[4]**

**2.** **(a)** The extract suggests anger at the Allies' decision to give Shandong to Japan (1), a sense that the great powers had betrayed China (1), and that students had acted directly and forced officials from office (1). **[3]**
**(b)** Award up to 3. A diary records one student's view and may exaggerate the success of the protest (1); it was written close to the event, so the writer did not know the movement's long-term effects, such as the founding of the CCP in 1921 (1); it says little about workers, merchants or the countryside who joined the strikes or were unaffected (1). **[3]**

**3.** Marking points (up to 6): it was a retreat from the Jiangxi Soviet after the fifth Encirclement Campaign, with very heavy losses (up to 2); the Zunyi Conference (1935) established Mao as leader (up to 1); the march moved the party to a base in Shaanxi and created the Yan'an Soviet (up to 1); it became a symbol of endurance and was used in propaganda to attract recruits (up to 2). **[6]**

**4.** Marking points (up to 15):
- A thesis and criteria for "main reason" (up to 2).
- The war: GMD loss of coastal bases, hyperinflation, corruption, and loss of legitimacy; CCP expansion through guerrilla warfare, membership of over a million, and Yan'an policies (up to 5).
- Civil War factors: Manchuria, the Liaoshen, Huaihai and Pingjin campaigns, land reform and peasant support, leadership by Mao and Lin Biao against Jiang's errors (up to 4).
- Foreign involvement: US aid and the failed Marshall mission, Soviet aid in Manchuria (up to 2).
- Perspectives: Chalmers Johnson against Westad (up to 1).
- A judgement that ranks the factors (up to 1). **[15]**

**5.** Marking points (up to 15):
- A thesis addressing both success and failure (up to 2).
- Successes: nominal unification in 1928, tariff control and the 1935 currency, roads and railways, some industrial growth, training of the army (up to 4).
- Failures: weak control over warlords, no land reform, corruption, one-party rule and suppression of critics, the Shanghai massacre of 1927 and its aftermath (up to 4).
- Japan: the response to Manchuria in 1931, the policy of internal pacification (up to 2).
- Perspectives: historians such as Lloyd Eastman against more positive recent views (up to 1).
- A reasoned judgement (up to 2). **[15]**

**6.** Marking points (up to 6): two generals kidnapped Jiang Jieshi in December 1936 to force him to end the civil war (up to 1); Zhou Enlai negotiated, and Jiang was released after agreeing in effect to resist Japan (up to 2); it led to the Second United Front in 1937, which saved the CCP from more encirclement (up to 2); it raised Jiang's standing as national leader but weakened his control of the army (up to 1). **[6]**

**7.** Any two, each developed (1 mark for the effect, 1 for explanation). The demands showed the danger of Japan's ambitions and made Japan the main enemy of nationalists; Yuan Shikai's acceptance undermined the government's claim to defend China; the date of the ultimatum was remembered as "National Humiliation Day", which became a symbol; the anger prepared the ground for the May Fourth Movement. **[4]**

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I write about the Long March without exaggeration?**
State the retreat, the losses and the symbolic effect, and separate what happened from the party's later telling.

**Should I mention the Soviet Union in every essay?**
Only where it affects the argument, for example the GMD–CCP alliance in the 1920s or Manchuria in 1945.
