**This guide covers content area 3.1.2.3 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), Salah: "the meaning and importance of Salah: how and why Muslims pray, including the conditions of Salah, times, directions, ablution (Wudu), Azaan, recitation, and concessions for those that are travelling or for the ill", "Salah at home and at the mosque, and the role of Salah in a Muslim's life" and "Friday prayers (Jummah) and their importance".** The prescribed passage is Qur'an 62:9-11. The Salah conditions, Wudu and Azaan [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-salah-conditions-wudu-azaan/) goes through the practical rules in depth. This guide gives the whole picture of Salah in one place and puts the weight on *why* Muslims pray, where they pray, and what prayer does in their lives.

## What Salah is

*Salah* (often translated as "prayer") is the formal worship of Allah, performed in a set form with bodily movements and recitation in Arabic. It is the second pillar of Islam and the most frequent act of worship, performed five times a day. It differs from *du'a*, personal supplication in any language, which a Muslim can make at any time.

The Qur'an gives its purpose: "Establish prayer for My remembrance" (20:14). Salah is a meeting with Allah, repeated through the day.

## Why Muslims pray

| Reason | Explanation | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Obedience** | Allah has commanded it as a duty at fixed times | "Prayer has been decreed upon the believers a decree of specified times" (4:103) |
| **Remembrance** | Five prayers stop the day being filled with only worldly things | "Establish prayer for My remembrance" (20:14) |
| **Moral protection** | Prayer helps keep a person from wrongdoing | "Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing" (29:45) |
| **Forgiveness** | The prayers wash away small sins like bathing in a river | Hadith in Bukhari and Muslim: a river at the door in which one bathes five times a day leaves no dirt |
| **Gratitude** | Prayer thanks Allah for His gifts | Qur'an and Sunnah |
| **Following the Prophet (pbuh)** | Muslims pray as he prayed | "Pray as you have seen me praying" (Sahih al-Bukhari) |
| **Accountability** | The first deed to be reckoned on the Day of Judgement is Salah | Hadith in Tirmidhi and Nasa'i |

Muslims also remember that the five daily prayers were given on the Night Journey and Ascension (Isra wal Mi'raj). They were first fifty, reduced to five, with the reward of fifty (Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim). This shows both the importance of prayer and Allah's mercy.

## How Muslims pray

### Preparation

Before prayer, a Muslim must be in a state of purity and meet the other conditions: clean body, clothes and place; covering the body modestly; facing the Qiblah (the direction of the Ka'aba in Makkah); praying within the prayer time; and having the intention (niyyah). Purity comes from **Wudu** (ablution: hands, mouth, nose, face, arms, wiping the head and washing the feet) or, after certain events, a full bath (*ghusl*). If no water is available or it would harm, **Tayammum** (using clean earth) replaces it. The call to prayer (**Azaan**) announces that the time has come.

### The five prayers

| Prayer | Time | Fard rak'ahs |
|---|---|---|
| Fajr | From dawn until sunrise | 2 |
| Zuhr | After midday until the afternoon | 4 |
| Asr | Afternoon until sunset | 4 |
| Maghrib | After sunset | 3 |
| Isha | Night | 4 |

Total fard rak'ahs: 2 + 4 + 4 + 3 + 4 = **17** each day. Many Muslims also pray extra *sunnah* and *nafl* prayers.

### One rak'ah

A rak'ah is one unit of prayer, made of repeated actions:

1. **Takbir:** raising the hands and saying "Allahu Akbar" (Allah is the Greatest).
2. **Standing (qiyam):** reciting Surah al-Fatiha and another passage of the Qur'an.
3. **Bowing (ruku'):** saying "Subhana Rabbiyal Azeem" (Glory to my Lord, the Mighty).
4. **Standing up** and then going down into prostration.
5. **Prostration (sujood):** forehead, nose, hands, knees and toes on the ground, saying "Subhana Rabbiyal A'la" (Glory to my Lord, the Most High). This is done twice, with a short sitting in between.

After the final rak'ah comes the sitting recitation (Tashahhud), which includes the Shahadah and blessings on the Prophet (pbuh), and the ending greeting "As-salamu alaykum wa rahmatullah" to right and left.

**What the movements express:** standing shows readiness to meet Allah; bowing shows respect; prostration, with the forehead (the noblest part) on the ground, shows complete humility. The Prophet (pbuh) said that a servant is nearest to his Lord in prostration (Sahih Muslim).

### Recitation

Recitation is in Arabic, the language of the Qur'an. Al-Fatiha is recited in every rak'ah. It praises Allah ("the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful"), declares worship and dependence ("You alone we worship and You alone we ask for help") and asks for guidance. Muslims who do not speak Arabic learn the meanings so that the words are understood.

### Concessions

Islam allows ease. A traveller may shorten four-rak'ah prayers to two (*qasr*) (4:101), and many may combine two prayers. Someone who is ill prays standing, or sitting, or lying down. A person who cannot find water uses Tayammum. These concessions follow the principle that "Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship" (2:185).

## Salah at home and at the mosque

Muslims pray both at home and in the mosque, and each has value.

| | At home | At the mosque |
|---|---|---|
| **Who** | Individuals or families | The community, led by an imam |
| **Benefits** | Privacy; easy to fit into daily life; children learn by watching parents; extra (nafl) prayers | Unity; equality in rows; reminders from the imam; friendship; support |
| **Evidence** | The Prophet (pbuh) said, "Do not make your houses graves" (Sahih al-Bukhari), urging Muslims to pray in their homes too | Praying in congregation is 27 times more rewarding than praying alone (Bukhari and Muslim) |
| **Special prayers** | Many *sunnah* prayers | Jummah, Eid prayers, Taraweeh, Janazah |

The mosque (*masjid*, "place of prostration") is also a centre for learning, charity and the whole community. In congregation, rich and poor, young and old, stand shoulder to shoulder in straight rows behind one imam, facing the same direction. This shows equality and discipline. Praying at the mosque is not obligatory for women in the same way, but they are welcome. The Prophet (pbuh) told his followers not to prevent women from going to the mosques (Bukhari and Muslim).

Parents are encouraged to teach their children to pray, starting at about seven years old and encouraging them firmly by about ten (Abu Dawud), so home is where the habit of prayer begins.

## Friday prayers (Jummah)

The **Jummah** prayer is held at midday on Friday and replaces Zuhr. It is obligatory for adult Muslim men who are resident and healthy, and women and travellers are not required to attend.

**How it works:**

1. People bathe, wear clean clothes and go early to the mosque.
2. The Azaan is called.
3. The imam gives a **khutbah** (sermon) in two parts, with a short sitting in between. It reminds the congregation of taqwa and often covers events or problems in the community.
4. The congregation prays two rak'ahs of fard behind the imam.

**Why it matters:**

- **It is commanded in the Qur'an:** "When [the Adhan] is called for the prayer on the day of Jumu'ah, then proceed to the remembrance of Allah and leave trade" (62:9).
- **It builds community:** Muslims of a town gather weekly and hear the same message.
- **It reminds them of priorities:** work stops for a short time to put worship first.
- **It is a special day:** the Prophet (pbuh) called Friday the best day on which the sun rises (Sahih Muslim).
- **It is educational:** the khutbah teaches Muslims about Islam and their duties.

## The prescribed passage: Qur'an 62:9-11

The passage has three parts:

1. **Verse 9: the command.** Leave trade and go to the remembrance of Allah. "That is better for you, if you only knew."
2. **Verse 10: the balance.** After prayer, "disperse within the land and seek from the bounty of Allah, and remember Allah often." Work is allowed and encouraged; Islam does not separate worship and daily life.
3. **Verse 11: the warning.** Some people rushed to a trading caravan and left the Prophet (pbuh) standing while he gave the khutbah. The verse says that "what is with Allah is better than diversion and than a transaction."

Use it to show that Salah is a priority, that worship and work are balanced, and that the community should not be distracted from it.

## The role of Salah in a Muslim's life

- **Structure:** prayer times divide the day and give it rhythm.
- **Discipline:** getting up for Fajr and stopping work for Zuhr and Asr trains self-control.
- **Peace of mind:** stepping away from worry to meet Allah brings calm.
- **Moral awareness:** facing Allah several times a day makes a person think about their conduct.
- **Equality and unity:** all Muslims face one direction and say the same words.
- **Connection:** prayer links a Muslim's actions with their beliefs, from Tawhid in the Azaan to humility in prostration.

## Differences between Muslims

Sunni and Shi'a Muslims both pray five times a day, facing the Qiblah, but with some differences. Many Sunni Muslims fold their arms in standing, while many Twelver Shi'a keep their arms at their sides. Many Shi'a prostrate on a small tablet of clay (*turbah*) from natural earth. Shi'a Muslims commonly combine Zuhr with Asr and Maghrib with Isha, making three times of prayer, whereas most Sunnis keep five separate times except in special cases such as travel. Both agree on the number of fard rak'ahs and on the central place of Salah.

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why Friday prayers (Jummah) are important to Muslims. **[4]**

> Jummah is important because it is a command from Allah in the Qur'an [1], which tells believers to leave trade and go to the remembrance of Allah when the call is made on Friday [1]. It is also important because it brings the community together [1], as Muslims of a town gather weekly in the mosque to hear the khutbah and to pray in rows beside each other, which strengthens unity [1].

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** ways in which Salah influences the daily life of a Muslim. Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority. **[6]**

> Salah gives a Muslim's day a structure, because there are five set times for prayer, so they must organise work, study and rest around them [1 and 1]. It also helps with good behaviour, because standing before Allah several times a day reminds a Muslim to be honest and kind [1 and 1]. The Qur'an says that "prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing" (29:45) [2], which supports the idea that regular prayer shapes character as well as worship.

## Common mistakes

- **Describing the movements without reasons.** Give why Muslims do them.
- **Confusing Salah and du'a.** Salah is the formal prayer; du'a is personal supplication.
- **Saying Muslims worship the Ka'aba.** They face it as a direction of unity and pray to Allah alone.
- **Saying the Friday prayer is an extra prayer.** It replaces Zuhr on Friday.
- **Forgetting concessions.** Include travellers and the ill when a question asks how Salah can be adapted.
