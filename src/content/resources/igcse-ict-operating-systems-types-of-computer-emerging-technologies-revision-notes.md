**These notes cover sections 1.3 Operating systems, 1.4 Types of computer and 1.5 Emerging
technologies of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 (syllabus for 2026–2028).** You need the characteristics,
differences, advantages and disadvantages of four interface types, the characteristics and uses of
desktop and mobile computers, including portability and expandability, and the impact of artificial
intelligence and extended reality on everyday life.

## Syllabus coverage

**Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417, 2026–2028, Topic 1**
- **1.3 Operating systems:** characteristics of operating systems including command line interface (CLI), graphical user interface (GUI), dialogue-based and gesture-based interfaces; their differences, advantages and disadvantages
- **1.4 Types of computer:** desktop computers, their characteristics and uses (office and business management, education, gaming, entertainment); mobile computers (laptops, smartphones, tablets and phablets), their characteristics and uses (as for desktops, plus remotely controlled devices); advantages and disadvantages, including portability and expandability
- **1.5 Emerging technologies:** the impact on everyday life of artificial intelligence (AI) and extended reality (virtual and augmented reality)

## 1.3 What does an operating system do?

The operating system (OS) manages the hardware and software and provides the **user interface**.
It handles memory, files, input and output, security (log-ins) and running applications.

## Interface types compared

| Interface | How the user interacts | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| CLI | Typing commands at a prompt | Fast for experts; precise control; uses little memory and processing power | Commands must be learned and typed exactly; hard for beginners; easy to make mistakes |
| GUI | Windows, icons, menus, pointer (WIMP) or touch | Easy to learn and use; no commands to remember; intuitive | Needs more memory, processing power and storage; can be slower for some tasks |
| Dialogue-based | Speaking (or typing) to the computer, which replies | Hands-free, e.g. while driving; helpful for users with limited mobility | Misrecognises accents or noise; limited commands; must be trained or learned |
| Gesture-based | Hand or body movements detected by touch screens, cameras or sensors | Natural and intuitive; no keyboard or mouse needed | Gestures may be misread; can be tiring; needs suitable sensors |

**Key difference:** a CLI gives the most direct control but is the hardest to learn; a GUI is the
easiest for most users but uses the most resources.

## 1.4 Desktop computers

**Characteristics:** separate monitor, keyboard and mouse; usually a tower case; mains powered; fixed
in one place.

**Uses:** office and business management (word processing, spreadsheets, databases), education
(computer rooms), gaming (high-performance graphics cards), entertainment (video and music).

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Easy to upgrade and expand (more RAM, storage, graphics card) | Not portable |
| Usually cheaper for the same performance | Needs desk space and mains power |
| Larger keyboard and screen; more comfortable for long use | Cables can be untidy or a hazard |
| Less likely to be stolen or damaged by dropping | Cannot be used when travelling |

## 1.4 Mobile computers

| Type | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| Laptop | Screen, keyboard and touchpad in one hinged unit; battery-powered; full operating system |
| Smartphone | Pocket-sized; touch screen; mobile network, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth; camera; apps; GPS |
| Tablet | Larger touch screen, no physical keyboard; apps; Wi-Fi and sometimes mobile network |
| Phablet | Between a smartphone and a tablet in size; phone and tablet features combined |

**Uses:** office and business management on the move, education (e-books, learning apps), gaming,
entertainment (streaming), and **remotely controlling devices**, such as smart heating, lights,
cameras or drones through an app.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Portable; can be used anywhere | Limited expandability; components hard to upgrade |
| Battery allows use without mains power | Battery life limits use; batteries degrade |
| Built-in wireless connectivity | Easier to lose, drop or have stolen |
| Smartphones and tablets have built-in cameras and sensors | Small screens and keyboards can be tiring |
| | Often more expensive for the same performance |

**Portability vs expandability** is the key trade-off: the more portable the device, the less it can
usually be upgraded.

## 1.5 Emerging technologies: impact on everyday life

**Artificial intelligence (AI)** is the ability of computer systems to carry out tasks that normally
need human intelligence, such as recognising speech or images, making predictions and learning from
data.

- *Positive impacts:* voice assistants and chatbots; recommendation systems for shopping and streaming; medical image analysis; fraud detection in banking; driver-assistance features; translation.
- *Negative impacts:* job losses in some roles; biased decisions if training data is biased; privacy concerns over data collection; over-reliance on technology; misinformation such as fake images.

**Extended reality (XR)** covers **virtual reality (VR)**, a fully computer-generated environment
viewed through a headset, and **augmented reality (AR)**, which overlays digital information on the
real world through a phone, tablet or glasses.

- *Positive impacts:* training simulations (pilots, surgeons) without risk; virtual tours in education and tourism; AR navigation and furniture-placement apps; immersive gaming.
- *Negative impacts:* motion sickness and eye strain; high cost of headsets; isolation from real-world contact; safety risks from not seeing surroundings.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Saying a GUI is "faster".** It is easier to learn, but a CLI can be faster for experts.
- **Generic advantages.** Tie each advantage to a use, e.g. "a tablet is portable, so a doctor can view records at the bedside".
- **Confusing VR and AR.** VR replaces the real world; AR adds to it.
- **Forgetting expandability.** It is named in the syllabus and often asked about.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a phablet?**
A mobile device with a screen size between a smartphone and a tablet,
combining phone calls with a larger display.

**Is a touch screen phone a GUI or gesture-based?**
It displays a GUI, and swipes and pinches are
gestures, so it uses both.
