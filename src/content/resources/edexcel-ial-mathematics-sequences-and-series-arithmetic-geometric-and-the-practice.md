## Questions

**1.** A sequence is defined by x₁ = 2 and xₙ₊₁ = 5xₙ − 7.

**(a)** Find x₂, x₃ and x₄. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the value of x₁ for which every term of the sequence is equal. **[1]**

**2.** An arithmetic sequence has fifth term 23 and twelfth term 51.

**(a)** Find the first term and the common difference. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the sum of the first 15 terms. **[2]**

**3.** Evaluate **(a)** Σ (r = 1 to 40) of (3r + 2) **[3]**, and **(b)** Σ (r = 11 to 40) of (3r + 2). **[2]**

**4.** Prove that the sum of the first n terms of an arithmetic series with first term a and common difference d is (n/2)(2a + (n − 1)d). **[4]** Hence write down 1 + 2 + … + n. **[1]**

**5.** Ria saves £20 in month 1, and each later month she saves £6 more than the month before.

**(a)** How much does she save in month 24? **[2]**
**(b)** How much has she saved in total after 24 months? **[2]**
**(c)** Find the first month at the end of which her total savings exceed £1200. **[3]**

**6.** **(a)** For uₙ = n² − 10n, show that uₙ₊₁ − uₙ = 2n − 9. **[2]**
**(b)** Hence describe where the sequence decreases and where it increases. **[2]**
**(c)** A sequence has x₁ = 2 and xₙ₊₁ = 6/xₙ. Show it is periodic, state its order, and find the sum of its first 51 terms. **[3]**

**7.** A geometric sequence has third term 20 and sixth term 160.

**(a)** Find the common ratio and the first term. **[3]**
**(b)** Write down an expression for the nth term. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the sum of the first 10 terms. **[2]**

**8.** A geometric series has first term 30 and sum to infinity 75.

**(a)** Find the common ratio. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the third term. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the sum of all terms from the fourth term onwards. **[2]**

**9.** Prove that the sum of the first n terms of a geometric series with first term a and common ratio r (r ≠ 1) is a(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r). **[4]** State the condition under which the series has a sum to infinity, and give its value. **[1]**

**10.** **(a)** £5000 is invested at 4% compound interest per year. Find the smallest whole number of years after which the value exceeds £8000. **[4]**
**(b)** A ball is dropped from 3 m. After each bounce it rises to 4/5 of its previous height. Find the total distance it travels before coming to rest, assuming the pattern continues indefinitely. **[3]**

**11.** **(a)** Expand (5 − 2x)⁴ in ascending powers of x. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the coefficient of x² in the expansion of (1 + 3x)(5 − 2x)⁴. **[3]**
**(c)** The coefficient of x² in the expansion of (1 + 2x)ⁿ is 180, where n is a positive integer. Find n. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** x₂ = 5(2) − 7 = 3 [1], x₃ = 8 and x₄ = 33 [1]
**(b)** All terms equal means x = 5x − 7, so 4x = 7 and **x₁ = 7/4** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* in a recurrence, show each substitution. A bare list of terms with one slip loses the later values, and the working lets a marker see which step went wrong.

**2. (a)** a + 4d = 23 and a + 11d = 51 [1]. Subtract: 7d = 28 [1], so **d = 4 and a = 7** [1]
**(b)** S₁₅ = (15/2)(2×7 + 14×4) [1] = (15/2)(70) = **525** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* write both simultaneous equations before solving. If you slip in the arithmetic, the correct equations still show the method.

**3. (a)** Σ(3r + 2) = 3Σr + 2×40 [1]; Σr = 40×41/2 = 820 [1]; 2460 + 80 = **2540** [1]
**(b)** Σ (r = 1 to 10) = 3×55 + 20 = 185 [1]; 2540 − 185 = **2355** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* for a sum starting at r = 11, subtract the sum to 10, not the sum to 11. That off-by-one choice is the usual way to lose the final mark.

**4.** Sₙ = a + (a + d) + … + (a + (n − 1)d) [1]. Reverse the order and add the two lines [1]: each pair totals 2a + (n − 1)d, and there are n pairs [1]. So 2Sₙ = n(2a + (n − 1)d) and **Sₙ = (n/2)(2a + (n − 1)d)** [1]. With a = 1, d = 1: **n(n + 1)/2** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* a proof is marked on its reasoning. Say that the reversed series is the same series, and show the pairing, rather than writing the result and stopping.

**5. (a)** Arithmetic, a = 20, d = 6 [1]: u₂₄ = 20 + 23×6 = **£158** [1]
**(b)** S₂₄ = 12(40 + 23×6) [1] = 12 × 178 = **£2136** [1]
**(c)** Sₙ = (n/2)(40 + 6(n − 1)) = n(3n + 17) [1]. Solve 3n² + 17n − 1200 > 0, positive root (−17 + √14689)/6 ≈ 17.4 [1]. S₁₇ = 1156 and S₁₈ = 1278, so **month 18** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* in a context question, state which model you are using and answer in the context's units. Checking the two neighbouring values of n protects against rounding the root the wrong way.

**6. (a)** uₙ₊₁ = (n + 1)² − 10(n + 1) = n² − 8n − 9 [1]; subtract uₙ = n² − 10n to get **2n − 9** [1]
**(b)** 2n − 9 < 0 for n ≤ 4, so u₁ > u₂ > … > u₅ (decreasing up to u₅ = −25) [1]; 2n − 9 > 0 for n ≥ 5, so the sequence increases from u₅ onwards [1]
**(c)** Terms 2, 3, 2, 3, … so x₃ = x₁, periodic with **order 2** [1]. 51 terms = 25 pairs plus x₅₁ = 2 [1]. Sum = 25 × 5 + 2 = **127** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* to claim a sequence is increasing or decreasing you need the sign of uₙ₊₁ − uₙ for all n, not a few terms. Say clearly where the behaviour changes.

**7. (a)** ar² = 20 and ar⁵ = 160 [1]. Divide: r³ = 8, so **r = 2** [1] and **a = 5** [1]
**(b)** **uₙ = 5 × 2ⁿ⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** S₁₀ = 5(2¹⁰ − 1)/(2 − 1) [1] = **5115** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* dividing the two equations is the efficient route. Write the powers as ar² and ar⁵, not ar³ and ar⁶, which is a common indexing slip.

**8. (a)** 30/(1 − r) = 75 [1], so 1 − r = 2/5 and **r = 3/5** [1]
**(b)** 30 × (3/5)² = **10.8** [1]
**(c)** The sum from term 4 is ar³/(1 − r) = 30 × (27/125)/(2/5) [1] = **16.2** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* either S∞ − S₃ or the tail formula can earn full credit if each step is shown. State |r| < 1 in (a) to justify using S∞.

**9.** Sₙ = a + ar + … + arⁿ⁻¹ [1]. Multiply by r: rSₙ = ar + … + arⁿ [1]. Subtract: Sₙ − rSₙ = a − arⁿ [1]. Factor: Sₙ(1 − r) = a(1 − rⁿ), so Sₙ = a(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r) [1]. The sum to infinity needs |r| < 1, since then rⁿ → 0 and **S∞ = a/(1 − r)** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* lay the two lines out one under the other so the cancelling is visible. Mention why r ≠ 1 before dividing.

**10. (a)** 5000 × 1.04ⁿ > 8000 [1], so 1.04ⁿ > 1.6 [1]. Take logs: n > ln 1.6/ln 1.04 = 11.98… [1], so **n = 12 years** [1]
**(b)** Total = 3 + 2(2.4 + 1.92 + …) [1], and the bracket is a geometric series with a = 2.4, r = 0.8 whose sum is 2.4/0.2 = 12 [1]. Total = 3 + 24 = **27 m** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* give a whole number of years, because 11.98 does not answer the question as asked. In (b), counting each bounce as an upward and a downward journey is the step to show.

**11. (a)** Coefficients 1, 4, 6, 4, 1 [1]. 625 − 4×125×2x + 6×25×4x² − 4×5×8x³ + 16x⁴ [1] = **625 − 1000x + 600x² − 160x³ + 16x⁴** [1]
**(b)** x² terms: 1 × 600x² and 3x × (−1000x) [1], so 600 + (−3000) [1] = **−2400** [1]
**(c)** ⁿC₂ × 2² = 180, so n(n − 1)/2 × 4 = 180 [1]. n² − n − 90 = 0, (n − 10)(n + 9) = 0 [1]. n is a positive integer, so **n = 10** [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* in (b) take every pair of terms that makes x²; using only 1 × 600x² is the usual error. In (c), say why you reject n = −9.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using n in place of n − 1 in the nth-term formulas.
- Not putting brackets round (−2x) before raising it to a power, which loses signs and the factor of 4.
- Missing a cross term when multiplying a binomial expansion by a bracket.
- Leaving n as a decimal when the question asks for a whole number.
- Not reversing the inequality when dividing by a negative logarithm.
- Quoting S∞ without checking |r| < 1.
- Proofs that start from the answer, or skip the line showing why terms cancel.
- Declaring a sequence increasing or decreasing from the first few terms only.
