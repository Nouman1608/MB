These questions cover section **3.12.2 Wave-particle duality** of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408), part of the A-level only Turning points in physics option. Learn the content first in the [wave-particle duality study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-wave-particle-duality-aqa-alevel/) and the [wave-particle duality revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-wave-particle-duality-aqa-alevel-revision-notes/).

Data: h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s; e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; mₑ = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg; c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹; μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ H m⁻¹; ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F m⁻¹. Apparatus values in questions 8 and 9 are illustrative.

## Section A

**1.** Explain how Newton's corpuscular theory accounted for refraction, and state what it predicted about the speed of light in water. **[2]**

**2.** Explain, in general terms, how bright and dark fringes form in Young's double slits experiment. **[2]**

**3.** Show that Maxwell's formula gives a speed of about 3.0 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ for electromagnetic waves in a vacuum. **[2]**

**4.** State the assumption Planck made to explain the shape of the black-body radiation curve. **[1]**

**5.** State de Broglie's hypothesis. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why an optical microscope cannot resolve individual atoms. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** In the eighteenth century most scientists accepted Newton's corpuscular theory rather than Huygens' wave theory.

**(a)** Give three reasons why Newton's theory was preferred. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how a measurement of the speed of light in water could decide between the theories, and state the result that supported Huygens. **[2]**

**8.** Hertz investigated radio waves.

**(a)** Describe how Hertz produced and detected radio waves. **[2]**
**(b)** Waves of frequency 1.2 GHz are reflected from a metal sheet, and a detector finds nodes 0.125 m apart. Calculate the speed of the waves. **[2]**
**(c)** State the significance of this result. **[1]**

**9.** In a Fizeau-type experiment, a wheel with 360 teeth rotates in front of a light source, and a mirror is 9.0 km away. The returning light is first blocked when the wheel turns at 23 revolutions per second.

**(a)** Calculate the time for the light's round trip. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed of light. **[1]**
**(c)** State the implication of this result for the nature of light. **[1]**

**10.** This question is about photoelectricity.

**(a)** State three predictions of classical wave theory about photoelectric emission that disagree with observations. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how Einstein's photon model accounts for the existence of a threshold frequency. **[2]**
**(c)** Ultraviolet light of wavelength 250 nm falls on a metal of work function 4.3 eV. Calculate the maximum kinetic energy of the photoelectrons in eV. **[2]**

**11.** In an electron diffraction tube, electrons accelerated through 80 V pass through a thin graphite film and form rings on a screen.

**(a)** Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of the electrons. **[2]**
**(b)** The accelerating p.d. is increased to 320 V. Calculate the new wavelength and describe the effect on the rings. **[2]**

**12.** This question is about the transmission electron microscope (TEM).

**(a)** Estimate the anode p.d. needed to give electrons a wavelength of 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ m. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the principle of operation of a TEM. **[3]**
**(c)** State one factor that limits the detail a TEM can resolve. **[1]**

**13.** Describe the principle of operation of a scanning tunnelling microscope in constant-current mode. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Corpuscles are attracted towards the surface of the denser medium, increasing their velocity component perpendicular to the surface, so they bend towards the normal [1]; it predicted that light travels faster in water than in air [1]

**2.** The two slits act as coherent sources and the waves from them superpose [1]; where they arrive in phase they reinforce (bright fringe), and where they arrive in antiphase they cancel (dark fringe) [1]

**3.** c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀) = 1/√(4π × 10⁻⁷ × 8.85 × 10⁻¹²) [1]; = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ [1]

**4.** Energy is emitted (and absorbed) only in discrete quanta of energy E = hf [1]

**5.** Particles have a wave nature, with wavelength λ = h/p (p is momentum) [1]

**6.** To resolve detail the wavelength must be similar to or smaller than the detail [1]; visible light wavelengths (about 500 nm) are thousands of times larger than an atom (about 10⁻¹⁰ m) [1]

**7. (a)** Newton's great reputation and authority [1]; his theory explained reflection, refraction and the straight-line travel of light (sharp shadows) simply [1]; there was no way at the time to measure the speed of light in water to test the theories [1]
**(b)** Newton's theory predicted light faster in water, Huygens' theory predicted it slower [1]; the measured speed in water was lower than in air, supporting Huygens' wave theory [1]

**8. (a)** A high-voltage spark gap oscillator produced the waves [1]; a wire loop with a small gap detected them, showing sparks across the gap when the waves arrived [1]
**(b)** λ = 2 × 0.125 = 0.25 m [1]; c = fλ = 1.2 × 10⁹ × 0.25 = **3.0 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** Radio waves travel at the speed of light, supporting Maxwell's prediction that light and radio waves are both electromagnetic waves [1]

**9. (a)** t = 1/(2Nf) = 1/(2 × 360 × 23) [1]; = **6.0 × 10⁻⁵ s** [1]
**(b)** c = 2d/t = 2 × 9000/6.04 × 10⁻⁵ = **3.0 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** The speed matches Maxwell's predicted speed of electromagnetic waves, so light is an electromagnetic wave [1]

**10. (a)** Light of any frequency would cause emission if intense enough (but there is a threshold frequency) [1]; with weak light there would be a time delay before emission (but emission is instant) [1]; greater intensity would give electrons more kinetic energy (but E_k(max) depends only on frequency) [1]
**(b)** Each electron absorbs the energy of a single photon, E = hf [1]; it can escape only if hf is at least the work function, so below the threshold frequency no photon has enough energy, whatever the intensity [1]
**(c)** E = hc/λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸/250 × 10⁻⁹ = 7.96 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = 4.97 eV [1]; E_k(max) = 4.97 − 4.3 = **0.67 eV** [1]

**11. (a)** λ = h/√(2meV) = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴/√(2 × 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 80) [1]; = **1.4 × 10⁻¹⁰ m** [1]
**(b)** λ ∝ 1/√V, so four times the p.d. halves λ to **6.9 × 10⁻¹¹ m** [1]; less diffraction, so the rings become smaller (closer to the centre) [1]

**12. (a)** V = h²/(2meλ²) = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴)²/(2 × 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × (1.0 × 10⁻¹⁰)²) [1]; = **150 V** [1]
**(b)** Electrons from a heated filament are accelerated by the anode [1]; a magnetic condenser lens forms a parallel beam that passes through a very thin sample [1]; magnetic objective and projector lenses form a magnified image on a fluorescent screen [1]
**(c)** Any one: sample thickness (electrons are slowed, so their wavelengths increase and spread); aberrations in the magnetic lenses [1]

**13.** A very sharp conducting tip is held about 1 nm above a conducting surface with a small p.d. between them [1]; electrons cross the gap by quantum tunnelling, and the current depends very strongly on the gap width [1]; as the tip scans, its height is adjusted to keep the current constant, so the tip's vertical movement maps the surface [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing Newton's and Huygens' predictions for the speed in water.
- Using the node spacing as the full wavelength.
- Forgetting the factor 2 in t = 1/(2Nf) or c = 2d/t.
- Saying brighter light gives faster photoelectrons.
- Saying faster electrons give larger rings.
