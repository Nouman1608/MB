This page practises three of the six Option A key questions in Cambridge O Level History 2147 (2024-2026): **1.1 Were the revolutions of 1848 important?**, **1.3 How was Germany unified?** and **1.6 What caused the First World War?** The site's existing Nineteenth Century practice page covers other key questions. On Paper 1 you answer two Section A questions; in each option, two questions come from key questions 1-3 and two from key questions 4-6. Each question is worth 20 marks: (a) 4, (b) 6, (c) 10.

## Question 1: The revolutions of 1848 (key question 1.1)

**(a)** Describe the events of the 1848 revolution in France. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why liberalism and nationalism had grown in influence by 1848. **[6]**

**(c)** "The revolutions of 1848 changed nothing." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

### Worked answer

**(a)** Award one mark per relevant point, up to four. In February 1848 protests in Paris against the ban on political banquets turned into street fighting; King Louis-Philippe abdicated; a provisional government declared the Second Republic and introduced universal male suffrage; National Workshops were set up for the unemployed; their closure led to the June Days rising, crushed by General Cavaignac; Louis Napoleon was elected president in December 1848.

**(b)** Explained reasons earn the higher level. *Liberalism* grew because the educated middle classes resented rule by monarchs and aristocrats and wanted constitutions, elected parliaments and a free press, ideas spread since the French Revolution. *Nationalism* grew because peoples such as Germans, Italians, Hungarians and Czechs were divided between states or ruled by foreign dynasties, and writers, students and historians encouraged a sense of common language and culture. Economic hardship in 1846-47 -- failed harvests, high bread prices, unemployment -- made more people willing to listen to both.

**(c)** A balanced answer:
- *Agree:* most revolutions were defeated by 1849. Austria restored its power in Vienna, Prague and Hungary (with Russian help); the Frankfurt Parliament broke up without uniting Germany; in Italy the old rulers returned and Austria defeated Piedmont at Custoza and Novara; in France the Second Republic gave way to Louis Napoleon's dictatorship and, by 1852, the Second Empire.
- *Disagree:* serfdom was abolished in the Austrian Empire in 1848 and was not restored; Piedmont kept its constitution (the *Statuto*), which made it the leader of later Italian unification; Prussia kept a (limited) constitution; universal male suffrage survived in France; and the failure showed nationalists that unification would need the power of a strong state rather than idealists.
- *Judgement:* "Changed nothing" is too strong. Few aims were met in 1848-49, but the revolutions changed the methods of later nationalists and left lasting reforms.

## Question 2: The unification of Germany (key question 1.3)

**(a)** What was the Zollverein? **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why the Frankfurt Parliament failed to unify Germany. **[6]**

**(c)** "Bismarck was responsible for the unification of Germany." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

### Worked answer

**(a)** A customs union led by Prussia, formed in 1834; it removed tariffs between member states; it set a common external tariff; Austria was excluded; most German states had joined by the 1850s; it made members economically linked to Prussia.

**(b)** It had no army of its own and depended on Prussian troops, for example in the war over Schleswig-Holstein; its members disagreed over whether to include Austria (*grossdeutsch* or *kleindeutsch*); it spent months debating rights while the rulers recovered; and when it offered the crown to Friedrich Wilhelm IV of Prussia in 1849 he refused a crown from a parliament. With Austria re-established by 1850, the parliament had no power to enforce its decisions.

**(c)** A balanced answer:
- *Bismarck's role:* appointed Minister-President in 1862, he strengthened the army; used the Schleswig-Holstein issue (1864) to fight alongside Austria and create a quarrel; isolated Austria before the Austro-Prussian War of 1866 (neutral France, an alliance with Italy); and used the Spanish succession and the Ems Telegram to provoke France into the Franco-Prussian War of 1870-71, which brought the southern states in.
- *Other factors:* the Zollverein and Prussia's industrial strength; the army reforms of Roon and the generalship of Moltke; German nationalism; the weakness of Austria after 1859 and the mistakes of Napoleon III.
- *Judgement:* Bismarck's diplomacy decided the timing and form of unification, but he used strengths that already existed.

## Question 3: The causes of the First World War (key question 1.6)

**(a)** What was agreed in the Anglo-Japanese Alliance of 1902? **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why problems in the Balkans were so difficult for the Great Powers to solve. **[6]**

**(c)** "The alliance system was the main cause of the First World War." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

### Worked answer

**(a)** Britain and Japan agreed that if either was at war with one other power, the ally would stay neutral; if either was attacked by two powers, the ally would join it; it was aimed at Russia's ambitions in the Far East; it ended Britain's isolation; it allowed Britain to concentrate its navy nearer home.

**(b)** The Ottoman Empire was declining and new states such as Serbia, Bulgaria and Greece wanted more land; Austria-Hungary feared Slav nationalism among its own subjects; Russia supported fellow Slavs and wanted access through the Straits; the Bosnian crisis (1908) left Serbia and Russia humiliated by Austria's annexation of Bosnia-Herzegovina; the Balkan Wars of 1912-13 enlarged Serbia. Each power's interests clashed, so a compromise satisfying all was impossible.

**(c)** A balanced answer:
- *Alliance system:* the Triple Alliance (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy) and Triple Entente (France, Russia, Britain) turned the July Crisis into a general war -- Germany's "blank cheque" to Austria, Russia's support for Serbia and France's alliance with Russia.
- *Other causes:* the naval and arms race; colonial rivalries such as the Moroccan crises (1905, 1911); Balkan nationalism; and the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 by Gavrilo Princip, followed by Austria's ultimatum to Serbia; German war plans that meant invading Belgium.
- *Judgement:* alliances were defensive and did not force everyone to fight (Italy stayed neutral in 1914). They explain why a Balkan war spread, while Balkan rivalry and the decisions of July 1914 explain why it started.

## Paper 2 style: a short source exercise (Germany, 1866)

**Source A** *(invented for practice)* -- A Prussian newspaper, July 1866: "At Königgrätz our army has shown Europe that Prussia, not Austria, leads Germany."

**Source B** *(invented for practice)* -- A Bavarian pamphlet, 1866: "Berlin calls it German unity. We call it Prussian conquest."

**Question:** How far do these two sources agree about the results of 1866? Explain your answer. **[7]**

**Guidance:** both agree that Prussia had become dominant; they disagree over whether this is welcome. A strong answer explains the difference from their origin -- a Prussian paper celebrating victory, a pamphlet from a southern state suspicious of Protestant Prussia -- and uses knowledge of the Treaty of Prague and the North German Confederation (1867).

## Where marks are usually lost

- In (a), writing explanation instead of describing several precise points.
- In (b), listing reasons without explaining how each led to the outcome.
- In (c), answering only one side, or giving a "judgement" that just repeats the question.
