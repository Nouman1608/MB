This guide covers subtopic **3.1 Marketing, competition and the customer** of Cambridge O Level Business Studies 7115 (2026 series). The combined [Marketing, Competition and the Customer and Market Research study guide](/resources/o-level-business-marketing-competition-and-the-customer-and-market-research/) introduces each idea with examples. This page builds the analysis behind them: tracing why a market has changed, matching a business response to the cause, deciding when a niche strategy beats a mass one, and choosing a segmentation method from data.

## What the syllabus asks for

- **3.1.1** The role of marketing: identifying customer needs; satisfying customer needs; maintaining customer loyalty; building customer relationships.
- **3.1.2** Market changes: why customer or consumer spending patterns may change; the importance of changing customer needs; why some markets have become more competitive; how businesses can respond to changing spending patterns and increased competition.
- **3.1.3** Concepts of niche marketing and mass marketing: benefits and limitations of both approaches.
- **3.1.4** How and why market segmentation is undertaken: how markets can be segmented, for example by age, socio-economic grouping, location and gender; potential benefits of segmentation to business; recommend and justify an appropriate method of segmentation in given circumstances.

## The role of marketing

Marketing is the set of activities that identify what customers want and make sure the business provides it profitably. The syllabus names four jobs.

| Job | What it involves | Why it helps the business |
|---|---|---|
| Identifying customer needs | Market research, studying sales data, listening to complaints | Products are designed for real demand, so fewer fail |
| Satisfying customer needs | Offering the right product at a price, place and time that suit customers | Customers buy, generating revenue |
| Maintaining customer loyalty | Rewards schemes, consistent quality, good after-sales service | Repeat sales; keeping a customer usually costs less than finding a new one |
| Building customer relationships | Personal contact, remembering preferences, responding to feedback | Customers recommend the business and are less likely to switch to rivals |

The four jobs form a cycle: a loyal customer gives feedback, which helps identify new needs.

## Why spending patterns change

Customers change what they buy for several reasons. Identifying the **cause** lets you suggest a response that fits.

| Cause | Example (invented) | Effect on spending |
|---|---|---|
| Change in income | Wages rise in a city | More spending on eating out, branded goods and travel |
| Change in tastes and fashion | A clothing style becomes popular | Demand moves to the new style |
| Changing population | More elderly people | More demand for health care and home services |
| New technology | Smartphones widely owned | Less spending on cameras and printed maps; more on apps |
| Greater health awareness | Campaigns on sugar | Fall in sugary drinks; rise in low-sugar options |
| Prices of other products | A competitor cuts prices | Customers switch to the cheaper rival |

**Why changing needs matter.** A business that ignores a change in needs keeps producing what customers no longer want: sales fall, unsold stock builds up and competitors who adapt take its customers. A business that spots the change early can launch new products first and gain market share.

## Why markets have become more competitive

- **Online selling**: customers compare prices and buy from businesses anywhere, not only local shops.
- **Imports**: businesses from other countries sell in the home market, often at lower prices.
- **Better transport and communication**: goods and information move faster and more cheaply.
- **New businesses**: entrepreneurs enter markets where start-up costs have fallen, such as online services.
- **Customers are better informed**: online reviews make it easier to switch to rivals.

## How businesses respond

| Response | When it suits |
|---|---|
| Improve quality or design | Customers are willing to pay more for better products |
| Cut costs to lower prices | Customers mainly choose on price |
| Develop new products | Old products no longer meet changed needs |
| Strengthen branding and loyalty schemes | Products are similar to rivals' |
| Better customer service | Personal contact matters to customers |
| Target a new segment or a niche | The mass market is crowded |
| Carry out market research | The cause of falling sales is unclear |

**Worked example 1 (invented business).** Kessler Stationers is a high-street shop whose sales have fallen 20% in two years as customers buy online, often more cheaply. Cutting prices to match online sellers is risky, because its shop costs are higher. A better response links to its strength: offering services online rivals cannot, such as printing, same-day pick-up and advice for school orders, plus a simple website so regular customers can order ahead. The response targets the cause (online competition) and the shop's advantage (being local).

## Niche and mass marketing

**Mass marketing** sells the same product to most of the market with one marketing approach. **Niche marketing** targets a small, specific part of the market with a product designed for it.

| | Mass marketing | Niche marketing |
|---|---|---|
| Benefits | High sales volume; lower cost per unit through large-scale production; one marketing campaign for everyone | Less competition; customers may pay higher prices; strong loyalty; suits small businesses |
| Limitations | Fierce competition, often on price; high marketing spending; product may not fully suit any group | Small market limits sales; risk if the niche shrinks; larger firms may enter if it becomes profitable |

Examiners reward matching the approach to the business. A small firm with a specialist product and limited capital usually suits a niche. A large firm able to produce cheaply at high volume suits mass marketing.

## Market segmentation

A **market segment** is an identifiable group of customers with similar characteristics. **Segmentation** means dividing a market into these groups so that products and marketing can be aimed at them.

| Method | Example of how it is used |
|---|---|
| Age | Toys for under-5s; holidays for over-60s |
| Socio-economic grouping (income and occupation) | Luxury cars for high-income professionals; budget ranges for low-income households |
| Location | Warm clothing in colder regions; products matched to local tastes |
| Gender | Grooming products designed and promoted differently |

**Benefits of segmentation**: products fit customers' needs more closely; promotion is aimed where it works, so less is wasted; prices can match what each segment will pay; untapped segments can be spotted and targeted.

### Recommending a method from data

**Worked example 2 (invented data).** Solace Spa surveys 400 customers.

| Age group | Customers | Average spend per visit ($) |
|---|---|---|
| 18–30 | 80 | 25 |
| 31–50 | 200 | 60 |
| Over 50 | 120 | 45 |

```
Share of customers aged 31–50: 200 ÷ 400 × 100 = 50%
Spending per group per visit: 18–30: 80 × 25 = $2,000
                              31–50: 200 × 60 = $12,000
                              Over 50: 120 × 45 = $5,400
Share of spending from 31–50: 12,000 ÷ 19,400 × 100 = 61.9%
```

> "Solace should segment by age and target 31–50-year-olds, because they are half its customers and provide 61.9% of spending per visit. It could design treatment packages and promotions for this group, for example offers advertised in places they visit. The risk is neglecting younger customers who may become regular, higher-spending clients later, so a smaller promotion for 18–30-year-olds could be kept."

## Common mistakes

- Defining marketing as advertising; promotion is only one part of it.
- Giving a reason for changing spending without saying how spending changes.
- Recommending price cuts for every competitive threat.
- Saying niche marketing is "better" without linking it to the business size and product.
- Naming a segment without a reason from the case or data.

## Exam technique for 7115 command words

- **Identify / State**: name a cause, a response or a method of segmentation.
- **Explain**: cause, then effect on customers, then effect on the business.
- **Recommend and justify**: choose a response or a segment, use data or facts from the case, and give a drawback.
