**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **A Level** subtopic
35.3: poly(alkenes) "chemically inert and can therefore be difficult to biodegrade"; some polymers
"degraded by the action of light"; polyesters and polyamides "biodegradable by acidic and alkaline
hydrolysis".

## Poly(alkenes): inert and persistent

- Backbone of **C–C and C–H** bonds only.
- Bonds are **strong** and **non-polar**: no δ+ site for water or OH⁻ to attack.
- **No functional group** left after polymerisation (the C=C has gone).
- Microorganisms cannot break them down, so they are **not biodegradable** and persist.
- AS disposal points: occupy landfill; burning can give **CO** (incomplete combustion) and, from PVC,
  **HCl**.

## Photodegradable polymers

- Contain bonds or groups (for example **C=O** in the chain) that **absorb light**, mainly UV.
- Absorbed energy breaks bonds; long chains become **shorter fragments**; the material turns
  brittle.
- Needs **exposure to light**; buried waste barely degrades.
- Fragmenting is not complete breakdown; small pieces may remain.

## Polyesters and polyamides: hydrolysis

- Links are **ester –COO–** or **amide –CONH–**, each with a **δ+ carbonyl carbon**.
- Water attacks with acid or alkali present; the C–O or C–N bond breaks.
- Products are the monomers, adjusted for conditions:
  - **acid:** carboxylic acids as –COOH; amines as **–NH₃⁺** salts;
  - **alkali:** carboxylic acids as **–COO⁻Na⁺**; amines as free –NH₂; alcohols unchanged.

| Polymer | Acid hydrolysis | Alkaline hydrolysis |
|---|---|---|
| PET | ethane-1,2-diol + benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid | ethane-1,2-diol + disodium benzene-1,4-dicarboxylate |
| poly(lactic acid) | 2-hydroxypropanoic acid | sodium 2-hydroxypropanoate |
| nylon-6,6 | hexanedioic acid + hexane-1,6-diammonium ion | hexane-1,6-diamine + disodium hexanedioate |
| polyamide from 6-aminohexanoic acid | ⁺H₃N(CH₂)₅COOH | H₂N(CH₂)₅COO⁻ |
| polypeptide | amino acid cations | amino acid anions |

## Repeat-unit equations

    –OCH₂CH₂OOCC₆H₄CO– + 2NaOH → HOCH₂CH₂OH + NaOOCC₆H₄COONa
    –NH(CH₂)₆NHCO(CH₂)₄CO– + 2H₂O + 2HCl → ⁺H₃N(CH₂)₆NH₃⁺ 2Cl⁻ + HOOC(CH₂)₄COOH
    –OCH(CH₃)CO– + H₂O → CH₃CH(OH)COOH                 (acid-catalysed)

- Count the **links per repeat unit**: two for a two-monomer polymer, one for a single-monomer
  polymer.
- One NaOH per ester link in alkali; in acid, one H₂O per link plus HCl for each amine end.

## Comparing the three types

| | Poly(alkene) | Photodegradable | Polyester / polyamide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Breakable site in chain | none | light-absorbing groups | ester or amide links |
| Agent | — | light (UV) | acid or alkali with water |
| Products | — | shorter fragments | monomers or their salts |

## Working backwards from hydrolysis products

Examiners also give the products and ask for the polymer.

1. Identify each product's functional groups: diol, dicarboxylic acid (or its salt), diamine (or its
   salt), hydroxy acid, amino acid.
2. Convert salts back to the neutral monomer: –COO⁻Na⁺ → –COOH; –NH₃⁺ → –NH₂.
3. Decide the link: an alcohol with an acid gives **ester** links; an amine with an acid gives
   **amide** links.
4. Join one of each monomer residue to draw the repeat unit.

Example: alkaline hydrolysis gives butane-1,4-diol and sodium butanedioate. The monomers are
HO(CH₂)₄OH and HOOC(CH₂)₂COOH, so the polymer is a **polyester** with repeat unit
–O(CH₂)₄OOC(CH₂)₂CO–.

If only **one** product forms and it carries both an –OH (or –NH₂) and a –COOH group, the polymer
was made from a single monomer.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| biodegradable | broken down by natural processes, for example by microorganisms |
| photodegradable | broken down by the action of light |
| chemically inert | does not react readily with common reagents |
| hydrolysis | breaking a bond by reaction with water, here with acid or alkali present |
| poly(alkene) | addition polymer of an alkene, such as poly(ethene) |
| polyester / polyamide | condensation polymers with ester / amide links |

## Exam traps

- Inertness needs **strong** and **non-polar** bonds and **no functional group**, not just
  "saturated".
- Photodegradation needs **light**; do not claim it works in landfill.
- In **alkaline** hydrolysis, acids appear as **carboxylate salts**.
- In **acid** hydrolysis, amines appear as **ammonium salts**.
- The diol from a polyester is the **same** in acid and in alkali.
- Two-monomer repeat units contain **two** links: double the reagent.
- "Degradable by light" and "biodegradable" are different claims: the first needs light, the second
  needs a breakable link such as an ester or amide.
- Hydrolysis of a polyamide gives **no** alcohol; hydrolysis of a polyester gives **no** amine. Check
  that your products match the link.

## Self-test

1. Give two reasons why poly(ethene) is chemically inert.
2. Define biodegradable.
3. Give one harmful product of burning PVC.
4. How does a photodegradable polymer break down?
5. Give one limitation of photodegradable polymers.
6. Why can polyesters be hydrolysed?
7. Give the products of acid hydrolysis of PET.
8. Give the products of alkaline hydrolysis of nylon-6,6.
9. Give the product of acid hydrolysis of poly(lactic acid).
10. How many moles of NaOH hydrolyse one mole of PET repeat units?
11. Why does the diamine appear as a salt in acid hydrolysis of nylon-6,6?
12. Name the products of hydrolysing a polypeptide in acid.

**Answers:**

1. Strong C–C and C–H bonds; non-polar bonds with no δ+ site; no functional group in the chain (any
   two).
2. Broken down by natural processes, for example by microorganisms, into smaller molecules.
3. Hydrogen chloride (accept carbon monoxide from incomplete combustion).
4. Groups in the chain absorb light (UV) energy and bonds break, giving shorter fragments.
5. It needs exposure to light (not effective when buried); fragments may persist.
6. Their ester links have a δ+ carbonyl carbon that water can attack with acid or alkali.
7. Ethane-1,2-diol and benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid.
8. Hexane-1,6-diamine and sodium hexanedioate.
9. 2-hydroxypropanoic acid (lactic acid).
10. Two.
11. The basic amine groups are protonated by the acid, forming –NH₃⁺.
12. Amino acids, present as their cations.
