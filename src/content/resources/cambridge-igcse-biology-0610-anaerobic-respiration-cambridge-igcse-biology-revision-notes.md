## Definition (Core)

> Anaerobic respiration is the **chemical reactions in cells** that **break down nutrient molecules** to **release energy without using oxygen**.

**Energy (Core):** releases **much less energy per glucose molecule** than aerobic respiration.

## Equations

| Organism | Word equation (Core) | Balanced equation |
|---|---|---|
| Yeast | glucose → alcohol + carbon dioxide | C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ (Extended only) |
| Muscles, vigorous exercise | glucose → lactic acid | not required |

**Check:** yeast makes a gas (carbon dioxide); muscles do not.

**Atom check (Extended):** C 6 = 4 + 2; H 12 = 12; O 6 = 2 + 4.

## Aerobic vs anaerobic

| | Aerobic | Anaerobic (yeast) | Anaerobic (muscle) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | used | not used | not used |
| Products | carbon dioxide + water | alcohol + carbon dioxide | lactic acid |
| Energy per glucose | much more | much less | much less |
| Carbon dioxide per glucose (Extended) | 6 molecules | 2 molecules | 0 |

## When anaerobic respiration happens

- **Yeast:** when oxygen is absent (for example sealed sugar solution; boiled-and-cooled solution under oil).
- **Muscles:** during vigorous exercise, when oxygen cannot be delivered fast enough. Aerobic respiration still continues.

## Oxygen debt (Extended only)

**Cause:** lactic acid builds up in muscles and blood during vigorous exercise → oxygen debt.

**Removal (three points only):**

1. Fast heart rate continues → transports lactic acid in the blood from muscles to the **liver**.
2. Deeper, faster breathing continues → supplies **oxygen** for aerobic respiration of lactic acid.
3. **Aerobic respiration of lactic acid in the liver.**

## Worked reminders

**Lactic acid curve.** Rises during the sprint (anaerobic respiration adds lactic acid) → peaks at the end → falls during recovery (carried to the liver and respired aerobically) → back to the resting value.

**Yeast set-up.** Boil the glucose solution to remove dissolved oxygen; cool before adding yeast so enzymes are not denatured; oil layer stops oxygen re-entering; limewater turns cloudy with the carbon dioxide released.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Anaerobic vs aerobic definition:** identical except "without using oxygen" replaces "use oxygen".
- **Yeast vs muscle products:** alcohol + carbon dioxide compared with lactic acid only.
- **Lactic acid vs oxygen debt (Extended):** lactic acid is the substance that builds up; the oxygen debt is the extra oxygen needed afterwards to remove it.
- **Heart vs lungs vs liver in recovery (Extended):** heart transports lactic acid, lungs supply oxygen, liver respires the lactic acid aerobically.
- **Much less energy vs no energy:** anaerobic respiration still releases energy, just much less per glucose molecule.

## Command words

- **Describe** anaerobic respiration: the full definition, ending "without using oxygen".
- **State** an equation: decide yeast or muscle first; write only that organism's equation.
- **Outline** (Extended oxygen debt): three short points, each with its purpose; no extra biochemistry.
- **Explain** data: link a rise in lactic acid to anaerobic respiration and a fall to transport and aerobic respiration in the liver.
- **Compare**: pair each point for yeast and muscle in one sentence using "whereas".

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Anaerobic | Without using oxygen |
| Alcohol (ethanol) | Product of anaerobic respiration in yeast; formula C₂H₅OH |
| Lactic acid | Product of anaerobic respiration in muscles |
| Vigorous exercise | Intense exercise when oxygen supply cannot meet demand |
| Oxygen debt | Extra oxygen needed after exercise because lactic acid has built up (Extended) |
| Liver | Organ where lactic acid is respired aerobically after exercise (Extended) |

## Exam traps

- Carbon dioxide written as a muscle product.
- Lactic acid written as a yeast product.
- "No energy" instead of "much less energy per glucose molecule".
- (Extended) Oxygen on the left of the yeast equation; 6CO₂ instead of 2CO₂.
- (Extended) Lactic acid "breathed out" or "broken down in the muscles".
- (Extended) Fewer than three oxygen-debt points, or points without their purpose.

## Self-test

1. Define anaerobic respiration.
2. How does the energy released by anaerobic respiration compare with aerobic respiration?
3. Write the word equation for anaerobic respiration in yeast.
4. Write the word equation for anaerobic respiration in muscles.
5. When do human muscles respire anaerobically?
6. Why is a glucose solution boiled and cooled before yeast is added in an anaerobic investigation?
7. Which test shows that yeast releases carbon dioxide?
8. (Extended) Write the balanced equation for anaerobic respiration in yeast.
9. (Extended) How many ethanol molecules are produced from 11 glucose molecules?
10. (Extended) What builds up in the blood during vigorous exercise, and what does it cause?
11. (Extended) Why does heart rate stay high after vigorous exercise?
12. (Extended) Where is lactic acid respired aerobically?

### Answers

1. The chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules to release energy without using oxygen.
2. It releases much less energy per glucose molecule.
3. Glucose → alcohol + carbon dioxide.
4. Glucose → lactic acid.
5. During vigorous exercise.
6. Boiling removes dissolved oxygen; cooling stops the yeast's enzymes being denatured.
7. Limewater turns cloudy (milky).
8. C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂.
9. 22.
10. Lactic acid; an oxygen debt.
11. To transport lactic acid in the blood from the muscles to the liver.
12. In the liver.
