Paper 1 draws exclusively on Topics 1 to 3. Businesses and people are invented; amounts are in dollars.

## Questions

**1.** State two benefits to a business of using accounting software instead of a manual ledger system. **[2]**

**2.** Tarn Hill Bakery records its weekly sales in a spreadsheet. Sales for four weeks, in cells B2 to B5, are 3,200, 4,150, 3,875 and 4,425.

**(a)** Write a formula that would show the total of the four weeks in cell B6. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the total sales and the mean weekly sales. **[2]**
**(c)** The figure in B3 was typed incorrectly and is corrected. Explain one benefit of using a spreadsheet in this situation. **[2]**

**3.** Coastline Pet Supplies uses a spreadsheet to track gross profit. Revenue is 48,600 and cost of sales is 29,160.

**(a)** Calculate gross profit and the gross profit margin as a percentage of revenue. **[3]**
**(b)** The supplier raises prices, and cost of sales is expected to be 31,000 with revenue unchanged. Calculate the new gross profit margin to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a spreadsheet is useful for the calculation in (b). **[1]**

**4.** Rookery Lane Tiles enters 6 transactions an hour. It works 09:00 to 17:00 each day, Monday to Friday. A full backup is made at 17:00 on Fridays. The computer fails at 11:00 on the following Wednesday.

**(a)** Calculate the number of transactions that must be entered again. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate how many fewer transactions would need re-entering if a backup were made at 17:00 every day. **[2]**

**5.** Explain the three data security issues of data loss, access and confidentiality. Use a different accounting example for each. **[6]**

**6.** A small firm keeps all its records on one computer in an open-plan office.

**(a)** Describe one hardware method of protecting the data and explain which security issue it addresses. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe one software method of protecting the data and explain which security issue it addresses. **[2]**
**(c)** The owner keeps a backup disk next to the computer. Explain why this is a weakness. **[2]**

**7.** Anvil Road Cycles has three people using its accounting software: a bookkeeper who enters invoices and receipts, a shop assistant who needs to check whether a customer has paid, and the owner. Recommend the access each person should have, and justify each recommendation. **[6]**

**8.** Describe the principles of integrity and objectivity as they apply to a professional accountant. **[4]**

**9.** Apply the principles of professional ethics to each situation.

**(a)** An accounts clerk accepts a 120 gift voucher from a supplier and then pays that supplier's invoice for 4,300 before older invoices from other suppliers. Identify the principle that is mainly at risk and explain why. **[3]**
**(b)** A payroll assistant tells her brother, who owns a rival shop, that her employer's monthly sales are 41,500. Identify the principle breached and explain why. **[3]**

**10.** Explain what is meant by the public interest, and explain how it is applied to the work of an auditor. **[4]**

**11.** Hollin Tools made a profit of 52,000 this year. The owner asks the accountant, Ewan Brask, to leave a repair bill of 7,500 out of the records so that profit shows as 59,500. The owner plans to show these figures to a bank to support a loan application.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage by which the owner's figure overstates the true profit. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify two professional ethics principles that Ewan would breach if he agreed, and explain each. **[4]**
**(c)** Explain how the public interest applies to Ewan's decision. **[3]**

**12.** A new business has to choose between a spreadsheet and accounting software for its records. Evaluate which it should choose, and reach a justified conclusion. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two benefits, each with a reason:
Speed, because one entry is posted to all linked accounts automatically [1]
Accuracy, because the program does the calculations and posts both sides of the entry [1]
(Other acceptable benefits: reports on demand; records easy to search.)
**[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A benefit such as "it is faster" needs its reason added.

**2. (a)** =SUM(B2:B5) (or =B2+B3+B4+B5) [1]
**(b)** Total: 3,200 + 4,150 + 3,875 + 4,425 = 15,650 [1]
Mean: 15,650 ÷ 4 = **3,912.50** per week [1]
**(c)** The formula in B6 (and any cell that depends on it) recalculates automatically when B3 is changed [1], so no re-adding by hand is needed and a new error is less likely [1]
**[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A formula should refer to cells; a typed-in total would not recalculate, which is the point of (c).

**3. (a)** Gross profit = 48,600 − 29,160 = **19,440** [1]
Margin = 19,440 ÷ 48,600 × 100 [1]
= **40.0%** [1]
**(b)** Gross profit = 48,600 − 31,000 = 17,600 [1]
Margin = 17,600 ÷ 48,600 × 100 = **36.2%** [1]
**(c)** Only the cost of sales cell needs changing; every dependent figure updates, so different "what if" cases can be tested quickly [1]
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the working line for a percentage, so that a small slip still shows a correct method.

**4. (a)** The last backup was Friday 17:00, so everything from Monday 09:00 is lost [1]
Hours lost: Monday 8 + Tuesday 8 + Wednesday 2 (09:00 to 11:00) = 18 hours [1]
18 × 6 = **108 transactions** [1]
**(b)** With a daily backup at Tuesday 17:00, only Wednesday's 2 hours are lost: 2 × 6 = 12 [1]
Difference: 108 − 12 = **96 fewer** [1]
**[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the hours lost on each day before multiplying, so a counting slip is easy to see.

**5.** Data loss: records are destroyed or become unreadable, for example a virus corrupts the sales ledger and the business cannot tell which customers owe money [2]
Access: control over who can open or change the records, for example a person without authority changes a supplier's bank details to divert a payment [2]
Confidentiality: information is kept private and only used for its purpose, for example a customer's balance or the business's profit is passed to a competitor [2]
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each issue needs a meaning and an accounting example; an example unrelated to records is harder to credit.

**6. (a)** Any one: uninterruptible power supply, backup on a drive kept off-site, locked room or cabinet, keycard or fingerprint entry [1]
Linked to the right issue, for example a locked room restricts access, or an off-site backup protects against data loss [1]
**(b)** Any one: passwords with access levels, encryption, antivirus and firewall, automatic backups [1]
Linked to the right issue, for example encryption protects confidentiality even if the file is taken [1]
**(c)** A fire, flood or theft that destroys the computer is likely to destroy the disk too [1], so the data would be lost completely [1]
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A method works best when tied to the issue it solves; "it is secure" is too vague.

**7.** Bookkeeper: read and write access to sales and purchases records [1] because the job is to enter transactions [1]
Shop assistant: read-only access to customer receivables [1] because they only need to see whether a balance is paid, and cannot then alter figures [1]
Owner: full access, including approving changes [1], because the owner is responsible and reviews others' work; each person has a separate password so changes can be traced [1]
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each recommendation to the person's job; full access for everyone, with no reason, is weak.

**8.** Integrity: being honest and straightforward in professional work [1]; not being associated with information that is false or misleading [1]
Objectivity: not allowing bias, a conflict of interest or pressure from other people to override professional judgement [1] [1]
**[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Describe" asks what the principle means; repeating the word itself is not a description.

**9. (a)** Objectivity [1] Accepting a gift creates pressure to favour the supplier, so judgement about the order of payment is no longer unbiased [1], and other suppliers are treated unfairly [1]
**(b)** Confidentiality [1] The monthly sales figure was learned through the job and was disclosed without permission [1], and a competitor could use it against the employer [1]
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the principle, then connect it to a fact in the scenario.

**10.** The public interest is the wellbeing of the wider community [1], including people who rely on accounts but are not the client, such as lenders, investors, suppliers and tax authorities [1]
An auditor is paid by the business but reports on whether the statements give a true picture to outsiders [1], so the auditor must report honestly even if the client dislikes the finding [1]
**[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show that the public interest goes beyond the client; "do what is best for the client" describes the opposite idea.

**11. (a)** Overstatement = 7,500 ÷ 52,000 × 100 [1] = **14.4%** [1]
**(b)** Integrity: leaving out a real expense produces figures that are false and misleading [1], and he would be linked with them [1]
Objectivity (or professional competence and due care): the owner's wish for a loan must not decide the figure [1]; the repair is an expense of the year and must be recorded [1]
**(c)** The bank will rely on the profit figure to decide whether to lend [1], so false figures could lead to a loss for people outside the business [1]; Ewan should refuse because his duty extends to those who rely on the accounts, not only the owner [1]
**[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In a scenario question, quote the 7,500 and the bank's reliance rather than writing generally.

**12.** Spreadsheet: low cost, flexible layout, good for budgets and what-if work [1]; relies on the user's formulas and has few built-in checks, so a mistyped cell may go unnoticed [1]
Accounting software: one entry posts to all accounts, with reports on demand [1]; costs more and takes time to learn [1]
A relevant factor such as the number of transactions, or the need for accurate trial balances and statements [1]
Justified conclusion, for example: software for a business with many daily transactions, because of automatic posting and accuracy [1]
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs a decision supported by a reason; listing both sides without choosing falls short.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a benefit with no reason.
- Mixing up an issue (such as data loss) with a method of protection (such as a backup).
- Giving a protection method that does not match the issue in the question.
- Counting backup hours from the wrong starting point.
- Naming a principle but not linking it to the facts in the scenario.
- Treating public interest as the interest of the client or employer alone.
- Evaluating without a conclusion.
