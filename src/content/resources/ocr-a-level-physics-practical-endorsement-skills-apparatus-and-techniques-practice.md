## Questions

**1.** A wire has a diameter of about 0.60 mm.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage uncertainty if the diameter is measured with a rule to ±0.5 mm. **[1]**
**(b)** Name a more suitable instrument, give its resolution, and calculate the percentage uncertainty for an uncertainty of ±0.01 mm. **[2]**

**2.** A pendulum completes 30 oscillations in 52.2 s. The uncertainty in the total time is ±0.2 s.
**(a)** Calculate the period T. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the absolute and percentage uncertainty in T. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how a fiducial marker helps the timing. **[1]**

**3.** **(a)** A micrometer shows 4.5 mm on the sleeve and 37 on the thimble. With the jaws closed it reads −0.02 mm. Find the corrected diameter. **[2]**
**(b)** A vernier caliper (resolution 0.02 mm) shows 23 mm on the main scale with the 13th vernier mark aligned. Find the reading. **[1]**
**(c)** Give two techniques that improve a wire diameter measurement. **[2]**

**4.** A student finds the resistivity of a wire. Six micrometer readings of the diameter (zero error zero) are 0.34, 0.35, 0.34, 0.36, 0.35 and 0.34 mm. The length is 0.800 ± 0.002 m. A p.d. of 1.50 V gives a current of 0.300 A.
**(a)** Calculate the mean diameter. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the cross-sectional area. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the resistance and the resistivity. **[3]**
**(d)** The diameter has an uncertainty of ±0.01 mm and the resistance ±2 %. Estimate the percentage and absolute uncertainty in the resistivity. **[2]**
**(e)** State where the voltmeter and ammeter are connected. **[1]**

**5.** An oscilloscope is set to 2.0 V/div and 0.5 ms/div. The trace is 3.2 divisions from peak to trough, and one cycle covers 4.0 divisions horizontally.
**(a)** Find the peak p.d. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the frequency. **[2]**
**(c)** Which setting change would show more cycles? **[1]**

**6.** A card of width 0.020 m passes light gate 1 in 0.016 s and light gate 2 in 0.008 s. The time between the gates is 0.40 s.
**(a)** Calculate both speeds. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the acceleration. **[2]**
**(c)** Give one advantage of light gates over a stopwatch. **[1]**

**7.** **(a)** An LED needs 2.4 V at 18 mA from a 9.0 V supply. Calculate the series resistance, then the current when the nearest larger standard value of 390 Ω is used. **[2]**
**(b)** A 5.0 V supply drives a divider with 3.0 kΩ above and 2.0 kΩ below the output. A 2.0 kΩ load is connected across the lower resistor. Calculate the new output p.d. **[2]**
**(c)** State two checks that show the LED circuit is correct. **[2]**

**8.** Stationary microwaves form between a transmitter and a metal plate. A probe finds seven successive minima over 8.4 cm.
**(a)** Calculate the wavelength. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the frequency (c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s). **[2]**
**(c)** Why is the minima spacing half a wavelength? **[1]**
**(d)** Why measure across seven minima, not two? **[1]**

**9.** A laser shines through a diffraction grating of 500 lines per mm. The first-order maximum is 0.498 m from the central maximum on a screen 1.50 m from the grating.
**(a)** Find the grating spacing d. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the angle of the first-order maximum. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the wavelength. **[2]**
**(d)** State two safety precautions. **[2]**

**10.** A Geiger-Muller tube records 150 counts in 300 s with no source. With a source it records 2610 counts in 60 s, and 1305 counts in 60 s with an absorber added.
**(a)** Explain why the first measurement is taken. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the corrected count rate with and without the absorber. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the fraction transmitted by the absorber. **[1]**
**(d)** Estimate the percentage uncertainty in the 2610 count. **[2]**
**(e)** Give two safe handling rules for the source. **[2]**

**11.** **(a)** A 50 Hz signal is recorded with a data logger. Suggest a sampling rate and justify it. **[2]**
**(b)** Write a citation for a web page, listing the details you would record. **[2]**
**(c)** Give two checks that a website is reliable. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 0.5 / 0.60 × 100 = **83 %** [1]
**(b)** A micrometer [1] with resolution 0.01 mm: 0.01 / 0.60 × 100 = **1.7 %** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Answers gain credit when the instrument is paired with its resolution and a calculation, not just "more accurate".

**2. (a)** T = 52.2 / 30 = **1.74 s** [1]
**(b)** Absolute: 0.2 / 30 = **0.0067 s** [1]; percentage: 0.0067 / 1.74 = **0.38 %** [1]
**(c)** It marks a fixed position, such as the equilibrium position, so every count starts and stops at the same point of the swing. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Dividing the total time uncertainty by the number of oscillations is the step to show clearly.

**3. (a)** 4.5 + 0.37 = 4.87 mm [1]; subtract the zero error: 4.87 − (−0.02) = **4.89 mm** [1]
**(b)** 23 + 13 × 0.02 = **23.26 mm** [1]
**(c)** Measure at several positions and orientations, then average [1]; use the ratchet so the wire is not compressed [1]
*Examiner insight:* A negative zero error is subtracted, so the correction increases the reading; sign errors lose credit.

**4. (a)** Sum = 2.08, mean = **0.3467 mm** (0.347 mm) [1]
**(b)** A = πd²/4 [1] = π × (0.3467 × 10⁻³)² / 4 = **9.44 × 10⁻⁸ m²** [1]
**(c)** R = 1.50 / 0.300 = 5.00 Ω [1]; ρ = RA/L = 5.00 × 9.44 × 10⁻⁸ / 0.800 [1] = **5.9 × 10⁻⁷ Ω m** [1]
**(d)** d is squared: 2 × (0.01 / 0.3467) = 5.8 %; length 0.25 %; R 2 %; total 8.0 % [1]; absolute = 0.08 × 5.9 × 10⁻⁷ = **±0.5 × 10⁻⁷ Ω m** [1]
**(e)** Ammeter in series with the wire; voltmeter in parallel across the wire. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep the unrounded mean until the end; squaring the diameter means its percentage uncertainty is counted twice.

**5. (a)** Peak-to-trough = 3.2 × 2.0 = 6.4 V [1]; peak = **3.2 V** [1]
**(b)** T = 4.0 × 0.5 = 2.0 ms [1]; f = 1 / 0.0020 = **500 Hz** [1]
**(c)** Increase the time-base setting (for example 1 ms/div). [1]
*Examiner insight:* Answers lose credit when the peak-to-trough reading is quoted as the amplitude or the time-base is not converted from ms.

**6. (a)** v₁ = 0.020 / 0.016 = **1.25 m/s** [1]; v₂ = 0.020 / 0.008 = **2.5 m/s** [1]
**(b)** a = (2.5 − 1.25) / 0.40 [1] = **3.1 m/s²** [1]
**(c)** There is no human reaction-time error. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Quote the gate interval and the card width from the question; the acceleration uses the time between gates, not either gate time.

**7. (a)** R = (9.0 − 2.4) / 0.018 = 367 Ω [1]; with 390 Ω, I = 6.6 / 390 = **16.9 mA** [1]
**(b)** Parallel combination: 2.0 kΩ and 2.0 kΩ is 1.0 kΩ [1]; V = 5.0 × 1.0 / (3.0 + 1.0) = **1.25 V** [1]
**(c)** Check the LED polarity before switching on [1]; measure the p.d. across the LED with a voltmeter, expecting about 2.4 V [1]
*Examiner insight:* Rounding to a larger standard value keeps the current safe; say so, and show the new current.

**8. (a)** Spacing = 8.4 / 6 = 1.4 cm [1]; λ = 2 × 1.4 = **2.8 cm** [1]
**(b)** f = c / λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ / 0.028 [1] = **1.07 × 10¹⁰ Hz** [1]
**(c)** Moving the probe by λ/2 changes the round-trip path of the reflected wave by λ, so the interference pattern repeats. [1]
**(d)** The fixed uncertainty in locating a minimum is shared over six spacings, so the percentage uncertainty is smaller. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Count spacings, not minima; seven minima give six gaps.

**9. (a)** d = 1 × 10⁻³ / 500 = **2.00 × 10⁻⁶ m** [1]
**(b)** θ = tan⁻¹(0.498 / 1.50) = **18.4°** [1]
**(c)** λ = d sin θ [1] = 2.00 × 10⁻⁶ × sin 18.4° = **630 nm** [1]
**(d)** Any two: never look into the beam or its reflection; remove reflective objects; stop the beam with a screen; use the lowest suitable laser power. [1] [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the angle from the tangent, not the screen distance, when the screen is flat; safety answers must be specific to lasers.

**10. (a)** To find the background count rate, which must be subtracted from the source readings. [1]
**(b)** Background = 150 / 300 = 0.50 s⁻¹; no absorber 2610 / 60 = 43.5, so **43.0 s⁻¹** [1]; with absorber 1305 / 60 = 21.75, so **21.3 s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** Fraction = 21.25 / 43.0 = **0.49** [1]
**(d)** Uncertainty is about √2610 = 51 [1]; 51 / 2610 = **2.0 %** [1]
**(e)** Any two: keep the source in its container when not in use; handle with tongs; point it away from people; keep exposure time short and distance large. [1] [1]
*Examiner insight:* Subtract the background before comparing the two readings.

**11. (a)** A rate of 1000 samples per second [1] gives 20 samples per 20 ms cycle, enough to show the waveform shape [1]
**(b)** Author or organisation, year, page title, web address and date accessed [1], placed beside the information it supports [1]
**(c)** Any two: it comes from a recognised body or textbook; it names its author or date; it agrees with a second source. [1] [1]
*Examiner insight:* A citation needs enough detail for someone else to find the page.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a percentage uncertainty without dividing by the measured value.
- Forgetting the zero error, or applying it with the wrong sign.
- Using the wrong number of intervals (minima, fringes, oscillations).
- Reading an oscilloscope without converting divisions into volts or seconds.
- Not stating that background was subtracted.
- Writing "be careful" instead of a specific hazard and control.
- Connecting a voltmeter in the current path, or an ammeter across a component.
- Quoting too many significant figures for the data.
