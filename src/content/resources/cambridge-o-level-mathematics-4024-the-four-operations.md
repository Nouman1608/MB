This guide covers subtopic **1.6 The four operations** in Topic 1, Number, of Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024, 2025 to 2027 series. The four operations are addition, subtraction, multiplication and division. The syllabus asks you to use them for calculations with integers, fractions and decimals, with the correct order of operations and brackets. The [Number revision notes](/resources/o-level-mathematics-number-revision-notes/) give a brief summary. This page gives the full methods, which you need on Paper 1 where no calculator is allowed.

Companion pages: the [The four operations revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-the-four-operations-revision-notes/) and the [The four operations practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-the-four-operations-practice/).

## What the syllabus asks

You must use the four operations with integers, fractions and decimals, including the correct ordering of operations and the use of brackets. The notes say the work includes:

- negative numbers
- improper fractions
- mixed numbers
- practical situations, for example temperature changes.

## Negative numbers

Think of a number line, or of temperatures. Adding moves right and subtracting moves left. Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding the matching positive number.

```
-8 + 15 = 7
-6 - (-9) = -6 + 9 = 3
4 - 11 = -7
```

For multiplication and division, use the sign rules:

| Signs | Result | Example |
|---|---|---|
| positive × positive | positive | 3 × 4 = 12 |
| negative × negative | positive | (-4) × (-7) = 28 |
| positive × negative | negative | 5 × (-6) = -30 |
| negative ÷ negative | positive | (-36) ÷ (-9) = 4 |
| negative ÷ positive | negative | (-20) ÷ 4 = -5 |

If the signs are the same, the answer is positive. If they differ, the answer is negative. For a longer product, count the negative signs. An even count gives a positive result.

**Worked example (temperature).** At midnight the temperature is -3 °C. It rises by 2.5 °C each hour for 4 hours, then falls by 6 °C. What is the final temperature?

```
rise: 2.5 x 4 = 10
-3 + 10 = 7
7 - 6 = 1
```

The final temperature is 1 °C.

## The order of operations

When a calculation has more than one operation, follow this order:

1. **Brackets** (and anything that works like a bracket, such as a fraction bar or the space under a root sign)
2. **Indices** (powers and roots)
3. **Division and multiplication**, working from left to right
4. **Addition and subtraction**, working from left to right

Division and multiplication have equal rank, so take them in the order they appear. The same applies to addition and subtraction.

**Worked example.** Work out 8 + 3 × (12 - 7)² ÷ 5.

```
brackets:    12 - 7 = 5
index:       5^2 = 25
x and ÷ in order:   3 x 25 = 75,   75 ÷ 5 = 15
add:         8 + 15 = 23
```

**Worked example.** Work out (-3)² - 2 × (-5).

```
(-3)^2 = 9
2 x (-5) = -10
9 - (-10) = 19
```

**Worked example.** Work out 2 - 3 × (-4).

```
multiply first:  3 x (-4) = -12
2 - (-12) = 14
```

### Brackets change the value

Brackets override the usual order. Compare 2 + 3 × 4 + 5, which is 19, with the other ways of bracketing it:

```
2 + 3 x 4 + 5       = 19
(2 + 3) x 4 + 5     = 25
2 + 3 x (4 + 5)     = 29
(2 + 3) x (4 + 5)   = 45
```

To make the statement 2 + 3 × 4 + 5 = 25 true, put brackets round the first two terms.

### Fraction bars

A fraction bar acts as a bracket on the top and the bottom. So (7 + 5)/(10 - 4) means 12 divided by 6, which is 2. Work out the top and bottom first, then divide.

## Adding and subtracting fractions

Fractions can only be added or subtracted when they have the same denominator. Find a common denominator (the lowest common multiple of the denominators is neatest), convert, add or subtract the numerators, and simplify.

**Worked example.** Work out 2 3/4 + 1 5/6.

```
Change to improper fractions:  11/4 + 11/6
Common denominator 12:         33/12 + 22/12 = 55/12
Mixed number:                  55 ÷ 12 = 4 remainder 7, so 4 7/12
```

**Worked example.** Work out 4 1/5 - 1 3/4.

```
21/5 - 7/4
Common denominator 20:  84/20 - 35/20 = 49/20
Mixed number:           2 9/20
```

You may also add the whole numbers and the fractions separately, but subtraction can need a borrowed whole number, so improper fractions are safer.

## Multiplying and dividing fractions

**Multiplying.** Change mixed numbers to improper fractions, multiply the numerators and multiply the denominators. Cancel common factors before multiplying to keep the numbers small.

```
3 1/3 x 2 1/4 = 10/3 x 9/4
              = (10 x 9)/(3 x 4)
              = 90/12
              = 15/2 = 7 1/2
```

Cancelling first: 10/3 × 9/4, where 9 and 3 share a factor of 3 and 10 and 4 share a factor of 2, gives 5/1 × 3/2 = 15/2. The result is the same.

**Dividing.** Multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction. The reciprocal is the fraction turned upside down.

```
5/6 ÷ 2 1/2 = 5/6 ÷ 5/2 = 5/6 x 2/5 = 10/30 = 1/3
```

**Fraction of a quantity.** "Of" means multiply.

```
3/8 of 64 = 3/8 x 64 = 24
```

**Worked example.** A tank holds 120 litres. Three eighths of the water is used, and then a quarter of what remains. How much is left?

```
used first: 3/8 x 120 = 45 litres
remaining:  120 - 45 = 75 litres
used next:  1/4 x 75 = 18.75 litres
left:       75 - 18.75 = 56.25 litres
```

Notice that the second fraction applies to the water that remains, not to the original 120 litres.

## Brackets with fractions

Work inside the brackets first, using a common denominator, then divide.

**Worked example.** Work out (5/6 - 1/4) ÷ 7/12.

```
5/6 - 1/4 = 10/12 - 3/12 = 7/12
7/12 ÷ 7/12 = 1
```

## Decimals

For addition and subtraction, line up the decimal points and add zeros if you need to.

```
  12.500
-  3.875
  ------
   8.625
```

For multiplication, ignore the decimal points, multiply the whole numbers, then place the decimal point so that the answer has as many decimal places as the two numbers together.

```
0.36 x 0.25:   36 x 25 = 900
decimal places: 2 + 2 = 4
0.36 x 0.25 = 0.0900 = 0.09
```

For division, make the divisor a whole number by multiplying both numbers by the same power of 10.

```
4.8 ÷ 0.06:  multiply both by 100  →  480 ÷ 6 = 80
```

Estimate first to check that the decimal point is in the right place: 0.36 × 0.25 is about 0.4 × 0.25 = 0.1, so 0.09 is sensible.

## A calculator check (Paper 2)

On Paper 2, key the expression in one go using the bracket keys and the fraction key. For example, (7.2 + 3.85) ÷ (9.1 - 4.6) = 11.05 ÷ 4.5 = 2.4555..., which is 2.46 to 3 significant figures. Without brackets, the calculator would divide only by 9.1 and then subtract 4.6, giving a wrong result. Make sure you know how your own calculator handles mixed numbers.

## Exam technique

- Write each step on a new line and keep the equals signs aligned. Marks are for method as well as the answer.
- With mixed numbers, convert to improper fractions before multiplying or dividing.
- Give fraction answers in simplest form, and as a mixed number if the question started with mixed numbers.
- Check the sign of each answer using the sign rules before writing it down.
- In a practical problem, include the unit (°C, litres) with the answer.

## Common mistakes

- Adding numerators and denominators: 1/4 + 1/6 is not 2/10.
- Multiplying whole numbers and fractions separately in mixed numbers: 3 1/3 × 2 1/4 is not 6 1/12.
- Turning the wrong fraction upside down in division, or turning both.
- Doing addition before multiplication, so that 8 + 3 × 5 becomes 55 instead of 23.
- Reading 12 ÷ 3 × 2 as 12 ÷ 6. It is left to right: 4 × 2 = 8.
- Losing the minus sign when subtracting a negative number.
- Misplacing the decimal point after multiplying decimals.

## Where this leads

Secure number operations are needed for everything that follows: percentages, ratio, standard form and algebra. In algebra the same order of operations governs substitution into formulas, and the sign rules apply to expanding brackets and solving equations.
