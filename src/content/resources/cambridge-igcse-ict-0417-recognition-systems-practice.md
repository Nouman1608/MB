## Section A: short answers

**1.** Which **one** of the following recognition systems does **not** need line of sight to read data? **[1]**

A: OMR

B: Barcode

C: RFID

D: OCR

**2.** State what is meant by OCR. **[1]**

**3.** Give **two** uses of RFID named in the syllabus. **[2]**

**4.** State **one** reason why NFC is suitable for paying with a smartphone. **[1]**

**5.** Which **one** of the following biometric methods measures the pattern of blood vessels at the back of the eye? **[1]**

A: Iris recognition

B: Face recognition

C: Retina recognition

D: Hand recognition

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** An examination centre uses OMR to mark multiple-choice answer sheets.

**(a)** Describe how the OMR reader detects the candidates' answers. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain **two** reasons why some answers might be marked incorrectly. **[4]**

**7.** A restaurant prints a QR code on each table. Customers scan it to see the menu.

**(a)** Give **two** reasons why a QR code is used rather than a barcode. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **one** security risk to customers. **[2]**

**8.** A shopping centre car park uses ANPR. Describe how the system records a car's arrival and opens the barrier. **[5]**

**9.** A clothing warehouse attaches RFID tags to every box.

**(a)** Explain **two** advantages of RFID over barcodes for checking deliveries. **[4]**

**(b)** Give **one** disadvantage of using RFID tags. **[1]**

**10.** A company wants staff to enter the building using a biometric system. Compare fingerprint recognition and face recognition for this purpose. **[4]**

## Section C: extended questions

**11.** An airport uses automatic passport gates. The passport contains an RFID chip and the gate uses face recognition. Describe how the gate checks a traveller's identity, and give **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of the system. **[6]**

**12.** Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of biometric recognition rather than passwords for logging in to a smartphone. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. RFID uses radio waves, so the tag can be read through packaging and from different angles. **[1]**

**2.** Optical Character Recognition: software that converts an image of text into editable text [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Tracking stock [1]; passports [1] (also credit: automobiles, contactless payment). **[2]**

**4.** Its very short range means it only works when the phone is held close to the reader, so the payment is hard to intercept [1] (also credit: two-way communication between phone and reader). **[1]**

**5.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** Light is shone onto the answer sheet and shaded marks reflect less light than blank paper [1]; the reader detects the position of each mark and works out which option was chosen [1].

**(b)** A candidate may shade a mark too faintly or outside the box [1], so the reader does not detect it and the answer is marked as blank [1]. A candidate may shade two options or not fully erase a changed answer [1], so the reader detects two marks and rejects or misreads the answer [1]. **[6]**

**7. (a)** A QR code can hold a website address for the menu, which a barcode cannot easily do [1]; it can be read with a smartphone camera from any angle, so customers need no special scanner [1].

**(b)** Someone could stick a fake QR code over the real one [1], sending customers to a malicious website that steals their personal or payment details [1]. **[4]**

**8.** A sensor detects the car approaching the barrier [1]; a camera captures an image of the number plate [1]; OCR software recognises the characters of the registration number [1]; the number and the arrival time are stored in the database [1]; the system signals the barrier to open [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** RFID tags do not need line of sight [1], so boxes can be read inside a stack or through packaging without being turned round [1]. Many tags can be read at the same time [1], so a whole delivery is checked in seconds rather than scanning each barcode separately [1].

**(b)** RFID tags are more expensive than printed barcodes [1] (also credit: metal or liquids can interfere with signals; tags can be read without permission). **[5]**

**10.** Fingerprint scanners are cheap and quick [1], whereas face recognition systems usually cost more [1]. Fingerprint recognition may fail if a finger is cut or dirty [1], whereas face recognition needs no contact but can be affected by lighting, glasses or masks [1]. **[4]**

**11.** The traveller places the passport on the reader, which reads the RFID chip containing the holder's details and photograph [1]; a camera captures an image of the traveller's face [1]; the system compares the captured face with the photograph stored on the chip [1]; if they match and the passport is valid, the gate opens [1]. Advantage: travellers pass through quickly, with fewer staff needed [1]. Disadvantage: the system may fail to recognise a genuine traveller, for example because of changes in appearance, so staff must still be available [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Advantages: a fingerprint or face cannot be forgotten, unlike a password [1]; unlocking is quicker than typing [1]; it is harder for someone else to copy a biometric feature than to watch a password being typed [1]. Disadvantages: the phone may reject the genuine user, for example with wet fingers or in poor light [1]; biometric data is sensitive and, if stolen, cannot be changed like a password [1]; a backup PIN is still needed, so the phone is only as secure as that PIN [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing OMR with OCR.
- Saying barcodes do not need line of sight.
- Describing ANPR without the OCR step.
- Making a point about only one method in a comparison question.
