## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Productive efficiency | Output produced at the lowest possible average cost |
| Allocative efficiency | Resources produce the goods consumers value most, in the right quantities |
| Pareto optimality | No one can be made better off without making someone else worse off |
| Pareto improvement | At least one person better off, nobody worse off |
| Dynamic efficiency | Improvements in methods and products over time that lower costs or raise quality |
| Static efficiency | Productive and allocative efficiency at a point in time |
| Market failure | The free market allocates resources inefficiently, so welfare could be raised |
| Complete market failure | The market provides none of a good |
| Partial market failure | The market provides the wrong quantity |

## Conditions at a glance

| Efficiency | Firm-level condition | Economy-level meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Productive | Output at minimum AC, where MC = AC | On the PPC |
| Allocative | P = MC | The point on the PPC society values most |
| Dynamic | Investment and innovation lower future costs | The PPC shifts outward over time |

**Why P = MC:** price shows the value of the last unit to consumers; MC shows the value of the resources used. P > MC: too little produced. P < MC: too much.

**Why MC = AC at minimum AC:** MC below AC pulls AC down; MC above AC pulls it up.

## Short run versus long run in perfect competition

```
Short run: price above minimum AC
  -> output where P = MC: allocatively efficient
  -> AC above its minimum: not productively efficient
  -> supernormal profit
Long run: entry pushes price down to minimum AC
  -> P = MC = minimum AC: both conditions hold
```

Quick table check: MC of units 5, 6, 7 = 22, 25, 34; AC at 5, 6, 7 = 26.00, 25.83, 27.00. Minimum AC at 6 units. At a price of 34, P = MC at 7 units.

## Pareto optimality: points to make

- A voluntary trade that helps both parties is a Pareto improvement.
- All markets productively and allocatively efficient → Pareto optimal economy.
- Many Pareto optimal allocations exist; some are very unequal.
- Pareto optimality is about efficiency, not fairness.

## Static versus dynamic

| Market power | Static view | Dynamic view |
|---|---|---|
| Monopoly | P > MC, often above minimum AC: inefficient | Supernormal profit may fund research and new methods |
| Perfect competition | P = MC = minimum AC in the long run: efficient | Only normal profit, so less money for innovation |

## Reasons for market failure

| Reason | What goes wrong |
|---|---|
| Negative externality | Output too high |
| Positive externality | Output too low |
| Public good | Free riders: no provision |
| Merit or demerit good | Information failure: consumption too low or too high |
| Asymmetric information | Trades distorted or prevented |
| Market power | P > MC, output restricted |
| Factor immobility | Resources stuck in declining uses; inside the PPC |

## Exam traps

- MC = AC is productive, not allocative.
- "No waste" describes productive efficiency.
- Pareto optimal does not mean fair.
- Dynamic efficiency is about change over time, not a point on a curve.
- Each reason for market failure needs a "what goes wrong" link.
- A point on the PPC is productively efficient but may not be allocatively efficient.

## Self-test

1. Define productive efficiency.
2. State the condition for allocative efficiency.
3. Why is the minimum of AC where MC = AC?
4. A firm's AC is 40 at 10 units, 38 at 11 units and 39 at 12 units. At which output is it productively efficient?
5. At its current output, a firm's price is 15 and MC is 11. Is output too high or too low for allocative efficiency?
6. Define Pareto optimality.
7. Give an example of a Pareto improvement.
8. Why can a Pareto optimal allocation be unfair?
9. Define dynamic efficiency.
10. Why may a monopoly be more dynamically efficient than a perfectly competitive firm?
11. Distinguish complete from partial market failure.
12. Explain how market power causes market failure.

**Answers:** 1. Producing output at the lowest possible average cost. 2. Price equals marginal cost. 3. When MC is below AC it pulls AC down and when above it pulls AC up, so AC is lowest where they are equal. 4. 11 units. 5. Too low: consumers value the next unit at more than it costs. 6. An allocation where no one can be made better off without making someone else worse off. 7. A voluntary trade that leaves both parties better off. 8. It only rules out waste; one person could hold almost everything and the allocation would still be Pareto optimal. 9. Improvements in production methods and products over time that lower costs or raise quality. 10. It can keep supernormal profit in the long run and invest it in research and new methods. 11. Complete: the market provides none of a good; partial: it provides the wrong quantity. 12. A firm with market power restricts output and sets price above MC, so units that consumers value above their cost are not produced.
