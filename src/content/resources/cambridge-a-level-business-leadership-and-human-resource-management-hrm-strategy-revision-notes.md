## 7.3.1 Leadership: key points

- **Purpose:** set direction, motivate people, make decisions, lead change.
- **Roles:** directors (strategy, accountable to owners); managers (plans, resources, departments); supervisors (daily work, front line); worker representatives (voice of employees, consultation).
- **Qualities:** vision, communication, decisiveness, integrity, empathy, delegation, adaptability.

Method for a "qualities" question:

1. Pick the qualities the case makes important.
2. Show how each helps (for example, empathy lifts morale).
3. Say whether the same qualities would matter in a different situation.

## 7.3.2 Theories: one-line recall

| Theory | Core idea | Main weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Trait | Leaders are born with personal traits | No set of traits works in every situation |
| Behavioural | What leaders do matters; behaviour can be learned | Assumes one best behaviour |
| Contingency | Best leadership depends on the situation | Leader must judge the situation correctly and keep changing |
| Power and influence | Leaders act through sources of power | Some sources win compliance, not commitment |
| Transformational | Leader inspires followers to a shared vision | Depends on one person; vision can be wrong |

Five bases of power (standard classification): legitimate, reward, coercive, expert, referent.

Transformational versus transactional: transformational inspires and develops; transactional trades rewards and penalties for results.

### Must-know distinctions

- **Trait versus behavioural:** who the leader is, against what the leader does.
- **Behavioural versus contingency:** one best behaviour, against the best behaviour depending on circumstances.
- **Legitimate versus referent power:** power of the position, against power of personal admiration.
- **Leader versus manager:** a leader inspires direction and change; a manager organises resources to meet plans. One person can be both.

## 7.3.3 Emotional intelligence (EQ)

Goleman's four competencies, as the syllabus lists them:

| Competency | One-line meaning |
|---|---|
| Self-awareness | Understands own emotions, strengths and limits |
| Self-management | Controls own reactions and stays steady |
| Social awareness | Understands others' feelings and needs |
| Social skills | Builds relationships, communicates, resolves conflict |

Application: a leader who loses temper under pressure lacks self-management. One who ignores signs of stress in the team lacks social awareness. Link each to a consequence such as absenteeism or turnover.

## 7.4.1 HRM approaches

**Hard HRM:** employees as a cost to be controlled; tight targets; flexible, short-term contracts.
**Soft HRM:** employees as an asset to be developed; training, involvement, long-term commitment.

Contract types to know: temporary, zero hours, part-time, full-time, annualised hours, flexi-time, home working, shift working, job sharing, compressed working hours, the gig economy.

| Contract | One benefit to the business | One drawback to the business |
|---|---|---|
| Temporary | Matches seasonal demand | Training wasted when staff leave |
| Zero hours | No pay when no work | Low commitment |
| Part-time | Covers peak periods | More people to manage |
| Full-time | Continuity | Cost fixed in slack periods |
| Annualised hours | Hours follow demand; less overtime | Complex rotas |
| Flexi-time | Attracts and keeps staff | Cover gaps |
| Home working | Lower premises costs | Weaker supervision |
| Shift working | Longer operating hours | Fatigue; higher pay |
| Job sharing | Two sets of skills for one post | Handover errors |
| Compressed hours | Fewer days of travel and start-up | Tiredness |
| Gig economy | Pay by task; easy to scale | Little control over service |

**Quick checks.**

- Compressed hours: 40 hours in 4 days is 40 / 4 = 10 hours a day.
- Job share: a 37.5-hour post shared equally is 37.5 / 2 = 18.75 hours each.
- Annualised hours: hours per year = hours per week x weeks; subtract the busy-season hours, then divide by the quiet weeks.

### Poor performance: measure, cause, consequence

| Measure | Standard form |
|---|---|
| Labour productivity | Output / number of employees |
| Labour turnover (%) | Leavers / average employees x 100 |
| Absenteeism rate (%) | Days lost / days available x 100 |
| Others | Defects, returns, complaints, appraisal results, missed targets |

**Worked reminder: Wetherlam Foods.** Average staff 80; 12 left in the year, so turnover is 12 / 80 x 100 = 15%. With 250 working days, days available are 80 x 250 = 20,000. If 800 days were lost, absenteeism is 800 / 20,000 x 100 = 4%. Output of 9,000 trays last year and 9,900 this year from 45 workers gives 200 and 220 trays a worker, up 10%.

**Causes:** poor selection; no training; low motivation; weak leadership; unclear objectives; poor equipment or conditions; personal problems.
**Consequences:** low output and quality; high unit cost; lost sales; falling morale; more absence and turnover.

### Improving performance: match the strategy to the cause

| Cause | Strategy |
|---|---|
| Skills gap | Training, coaching, mentoring |
| Low motivation | Financial or non-financial motivators, recognition |
| Unclear aims | Clear objectives, feedback, appraisal, MBO |
| Weak supervision | Leadership development |
| Poor conditions | Better equipment, hours or workspace |
| Persistent failure | Warnings, redeployment, dismissal |

### Management by Objectives (MBO)

Steps: set business objectives, cascade them, agree individual objectives with each employee, provide resources, monitor and review, reward and reset.

- **Useful when:** staff are skilled and trusted, and results can be measured.
- **Limits:** time-consuming; targets can be out of date; may encourage a focus on what is measured; poorly suited where tasks are routine and tightly controlled.

### IT and AI in HRM

| Area | Role of IT or AI | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Recruitment | Online applications; automated screening | Hidden bias; good candidates rejected |
| Training | Online courses and tracking | Less personal feedback |
| Records and payroll | Central database; fewer errors | Data security and privacy |
| Performance | Data tracking; spotting patterns | Staff feel watched |
| Staff queries | Chatbots | Impersonal; cannot handle complex cases |

Judgement line: these tools save time and cost, but people decisions still need human oversight.

## Quick self-test

1. State two roles of a supervisor.
2. Name the five types of leadership theory in the syllabus.
3. Which theory says leaders are born?
4. Which of Goleman's competencies is shown by a manager who keeps calm during a complaint?
5. Define labour turnover and give the standard form of the calculation.
6. A firm has an average of 60 employees and 9 leave. Calculate labour turnover.
7. A worker's contract is 1,500 hours a year. Busy season needs 36 hours a week for 20 weeks. How many weekly hours remain over 30 quiet weeks?
8. Give one advantage and one disadvantage to a business of a zero hours contract.
9. State the first two steps of implementing MBO.
10. Give one risk of using AI to screen job applications.
11. Output rises from 3,000 to 3,300 units with 30 employees both years. Calculate the percentage change in labour productivity.

### Answers

1. Directing day-to-day work; solving problems on the spot; passing information between staff and managers (any two).
2. Trait, behavioural, contingency, power and influence, transformational.
3. Trait theory.
4. Self-management.
5. The rate at which employees leave over a period; leavers / average employees x 100.
6. 9 / 60 x 100 = **15%**.
7. 36 x 20 = 720 hours. (1,500 - 720) / 30 = 780 / 30 = **26 hours a week**.
8. Advantage: pays only for hours needed. Disadvantage: staff insecurity, so low commitment and high turnover.
9. Set the business objectives, then cascade them to departments, with individual objectives agreed with each employee.
10. It may copy bias from past data and reject suitable candidates.
11. Productivity 100 to 110 units a person: **10% rise**.

## Command words and case-study skills

The syllabus defines the command words you will meet. Use them as follows with these topics.

| Command word | Meaning in the syllabus | What to do here |
|---|---|---|
| Define | Give precise meaning | State the idea exactly, for example labour turnover |
| Identify | Name, select or recognise | Pick the competency or contract type from the case |
| Calculate | Work out from given facts | Show formula, working and unit |
| Explain | Set out reasons and relationships, with evidence | Give the cause, then the effect on the business |
| Analyse | Examine in detail to show meaning and relationships | Build a chain from the case data |
| Evaluate | Judge or calculate the quality, importance, amount or value | Weigh both sides, then decide |
| Justify | Support a case with evidence or argument | Make a choice and back it with case facts |

Pattern for a leadership or HRM evaluation: state the choice, give one reason for it using case data, give one limit, then conclude by saying what the answer depends on, such as the skills of the workforce or the urgency of the problem.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a leadership theory without applying it to the leader in the case.
- Treating one style or theory as best in every situation.
- Listing all four competencies of EQ instead of choosing the relevant one and applying it.
- Dividing leavers by the opening headcount, not the average.
- Writing about the effect on employees when the question asks about the business.
- Giving a strategy for poor performance that does not match the cause in the case.
- Describing MBO without saying that objectives are agreed, reviewed and linked to business objectives.
- Concluding without a judgement in an evaluate or justify answer.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are Goleman's four competencies of emotional intelligence?**
Self-awareness, self-management, social awareness and social skills, as listed in the Cambridge 9609 syllabus. In an exam, choose the relevant competency and apply it to the leader in the case rather than listing all four.

**What is the difference between hard and soft HRM?**
Hard HRM treats employees as a cost to be controlled, with tight targets and flexible, short-term contracts. Soft HRM treats employees as an asset to be developed through training, involvement and long-term commitment.

**What are the five bases of power?**
Legitimate, reward, coercive, expert and referent power. Reward and coercive power can win compliance, while expert and referent power are more likely to win commitment.

**How do you calculate the absenteeism rate?**
Divide the days lost to absence by the days available and multiply by 100. Days available is the number of employees multiplied by the working days in the period.
