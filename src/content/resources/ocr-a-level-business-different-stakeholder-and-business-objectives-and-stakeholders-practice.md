## Questions

**1.** Define the term "stakeholder", and identify **one** internal and **one** external stakeholder of a bakery. **[3]**

**2.** Lowrigg Dental Group has the aim of being the most trusted dental practice in its region. Distinguish between a tactical objective and an operational objective, using an example of each for Lowrigg. **[4]**

**3.** The owner of Garstonby Garden Centre says: "We want more people to come back and buy from us." Rewrite this as a SMART objective, then identify **two** features of SMART that the original lacks. **[4]**

**4.** Daleswick Borough Leisure Trust is run for a local council. A rival gym is privately owned. Explain how the sector in which each operates is likely to affect its aims and objectives. **[6]**

**5.** Ironhaugh Farm Foods has 45 staff. Managers emailed everyone one line: "Cut waste this year." Some staff reduced packaging, others cut portion sizes.
**(a)** Explain **two** consequences for Ironhaugh of mis-communicating this objective. **[4]**
**(b)** Recommend **one** way the objective could be better communicated. **[2]**

**6.** Penderel Fabrics made a net profit of £150,000 on sales revenue of £2.5 million.
**(a)** Calculate its net profit margin. **[2]**
**(b)** The board sets an objective of a 9% net profit margin on sales revenue of £2.8 million. Calculate the net profit needed, and the percentage increase on the £150,000. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain **one** reason why a business such as Penderel may need to change its objectives. **[2]**

**7.** Ironhaugh Farm Foods earned a net profit of £240,000. Shareholders expect 60% of net profit to be paid as dividends. Employees ask for a 4% pay rise on a wage bill of £620,000. Assume the pay rise reduces net profit pound for pound.
**(a)** Calculate the current dividend. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the dividend if the pay rise is granted and the 60% policy is kept, and the fall in the dividend. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain **two** reasons why shareholders and employees have conflicting objectives. **[2]**
**(d)** Analyse **one** impact on Ironhaugh if this conflict is not resolved. **[2]**

**8.** Garstonby Garden Centre plans a larger site. A residents' group objects to the extra traffic. Evaluate the extent to which the local community can influence the decision. **[9]**

**9.** The council cuts its annual grant to Daleswick Borough Leisure Trust by 20% from £500,000. The trust's fee income is £600,000. Users want low prices, the council wants value for money, and staff want secure jobs. Recommend and justify how the trust should deal with these conflicting objectives. **[10]**

**10.** Lowrigg Dental Group's Saturday clinic brings in £90,000 a year and costs £78,000 to run. Some 300 patients rely on Saturday appointments and 14 staff work some Saturday shifts. The owners consider closing it.
**(a)** Calculate the annual surplus from the Saturday clinic. **[1]**
**(b)** Evaluate the impact of closing the clinic on Lowrigg's stakeholders. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** A stakeholder is any person or group that has a stake in what a business does or is touched by its actions [1]. Internal: employees (or owners, managers) [1]. External: customers (or suppliers, local residents) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition that mentions only "people who own the business" tends to lose credit, because the idea covers anyone affected, owner or not.

**2.** A tactical objective is a shorter-term target for a team or department that carries out the strategy [1]; for example, offering free hygiene checks to new patients for three months [1]. An operational objective is a day-to-day target for a person or process [1]; for example, reception staff confirm every appointment by phone the day before [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to earn credit when the two examples differ clearly in scale and time span; two similar examples blur the distinction.

**3.** SMART version: raise the share of sales to returning loyalty-card customers from 35% to 45% within 12 months [2]. The original is not measurable (no number to track) [1] and not time-bound (no deadline) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A rewrite earns credit when it contains a figure and a date; adding "specific" wording without either is unlikely to be enough.

**4.** Daleswick is public sector, funded through the council, so it is accountable for spending public money [1]. Its objectives will stress access, service quality, value for money and staying within budget [1], for example low-priced sessions for young people [1]. The gym is private sector, owned by people who risk their own money [1], so profit, return to owners and growth are likely to dominate [1]. Sector is a tendency: the trust may still need a surplus and the gym may pursue reputation [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to gain when each sector is tied to its source of funds or owners; listing objectives with no reason reads as description only.

**5. (a)** Staff worked towards different goals, so effort was wasted or duplicated [1] and the waste target may not be met [1]. Staff may feel confused or blamed, lowering morale [1], which could reduce trust in managers' later messages [1]. **(b)** Hold a short team briefing that gives a figure, such as waste cut by a stated percentage [1], and lets staff ask questions, which avoids different readings in a workforce of 45 [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Consequences are stronger when each is developed with a link to the case; a bare list such as "low morale" is a single point.

**6. (a)** Net profit margin = net profit ÷ sales revenue × 100 [1] = 150,000 ÷ 2,500,000 × 100 = **6%** [1]. **(b)** 9% × £2,800,000 = **£252,000** [1]. Increase = £102,000 [1], and 102,000 ÷ 150,000 × 100 = **68%** [1]. **(c)** Objectives may change when the market shifts, for example a new competitor [1], so old targets no longer fit the situation [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Showing the formula and units usually protects a calculation answer; credit is also lost by dividing by the target rather than the original profit.

**7. (a)** 60% × £240,000 = **£144,000** [1]. **(b)** Pay rise = 4% × £620,000 = £24,800 [1]. Net profit = £215,200 [1]. Dividend = £129,120, a fall of **£14,880** [1]. **(c)** Shareholders gain from higher profit while pay is a cost, so one group's gain is the other's loss [1]; they also hold different time views, as staff want security now and owners want long-run returns [1]. **(d)** Staff may become demotivated or leave [1], reducing output and raising recruitment costs [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to gain when the pay rise is shown to cut the profit available for dividends; conflict claims with no figure are less convincing.

**8.** For influence: residents can publicise the objection and affect reputation with customers [1], and a council may take their views into account [1]. Against: the community is outside the business and lacks formal control [1], and the owner can proceed if finance and support are in place [1]. Other stakeholders may matter more, such as lenders who fund the site and customers who want a larger range [1], and shareholders who want growth [1]. The influence depends on size and organisation of the group and the strength of the evidence [1]. Judgement: the community has moderate influence, mainly through delay and reputation, so Garstonby should consult early and offer traffic measures rather than treat the group as a veto [2]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation earns more when it weighs the community against other stakeholders and ends with a judgement; one-sided answers are limited.

**9.** The grant falls by 20% × £500,000 = £100,000 [1]. If covered by fees alone, income would need to rise by 100,000 ÷ 600,000 = 16.7% [1]. The conflict is mainly between users wanting low prices and the funding gap, with staff fearing cuts [1]. Raising all prices equally would hit low-income users and conflict with the access aim [1], and could cut visits [1]. Cutting opening hours saves money but risks jobs and access [1]. Recommendation: a smaller, targeted price rise, new sponsorship, and protected concession rates [1]. This keeps the trust's access objective while closing part of the gap [1]. Staff and the council should be consulted before decisions, so each group sees the trade-offs [1]. Some groups will lose, such as full-price users paying more, but this is justified by protecting the trust's main purpose [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Recommendations tend to gain when they name who loses and justify the choice against the organisation's own objectives, not general principles.

**10. (a)** £90,000 − £78,000 = **£12,000** [1]. **(b)** Owners lose a surplus of £12,000 [1], though they save management time and could redeploy staff [1]. Patients: 300 lose convenient access and may move to a rival [1], which could also cost weekday custom [1]. Staff: 14 workers lose Saturday hours or pay [1]. Judgement: the surplus is small at about 13% of Saturday revenue, yet the loss of patients and goodwill may be larger, so closure looks weak unless costs can be cut instead [2]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to gain when the figure from the data is used in the judgement; assertions about stakeholders with no evidence are limited.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining a stakeholder as someone who owns shares.
- Giving two similar examples for different levels of the hierarchy.
- Rewriting a SMART objective without a number or a date.
- Describing sectors without linking them to funding or owners.
- Listing consequences of mis-communication without developing them.
- Dividing by the wrong base in a percentage change.
- Stating that conflict exists without showing what each group wants.
- Evaluating with no judgement, or a recommendation without a cost to some group.
