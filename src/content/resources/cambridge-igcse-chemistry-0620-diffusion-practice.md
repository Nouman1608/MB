## Section A

**1.** Define diffusion. **[2]**

**2.** In which of the following does diffusion happen fastest at room temperature?

A: Copper atoms in a copper block

B: Sugar particles dissolving in still water

C: Perfume particles in air

D: Sodium chloride particles in a crystal

**[1]**

**3.** A bottle of perfume is opened at the front of a classroom. A few minutes later, students at the back can smell it, although the air in the room is still. Explain this using kinetic particle theory. **[2]**

**4.** A small purple crystal of potassium manganate(VII) is placed at the bottom of a beaker of still water and left for several hours.

**(a)** Describe what you would see. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain your answer in terms of particles. **[2]**

**5.** In a bromine vapour demonstration, the brown colour spreads upwards into a jar of air, although bromine is denser than air. State what this shows about the motion of the particles. **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended only)* Calculate the relative molecular mass of **(a)** ammonia, NH₃, **(b)** carbon dioxide, CO₂, and **(c)** chlorine, Cl₂. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** *(Extended only)* Rank the following gases in order of rate of diffusion, fastest first, at the same temperature. Show your working. Oxygen, O₂; helium, He; sulfur dioxide, SO₂; methane, CH₄. **[3]**

**8.** *(Extended only)* A long glass tube has cotton wool soaked in concentrated ammonia solution at one end and cotton wool soaked in concentrated hydrobromic acid, giving hydrogen bromide gas, at the other end. A white solid, ammonium bromide, forms inside the tube.

**(a)** Calculate the relative molecular masses of ammonia, NH₃, and hydrogen bromide, HBr. **[2]**
**(b)** Predict where in the tube the white solid forms. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain your prediction. **[2]**
**(d)** Write the equation for the reaction, with state symbols. **[1]**

**9.** Two identical sealed tubes each have a piece of damp red litmus paper at one end. At the same moment, ammonia gas is released at the other end of each tube. Tube 1 is at 20 °C and tube 2 is at 40 °C.

**(a)** Predict which litmus paper turns blue first. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain your prediction. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why diffusion is slower in liquids than in gases. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended only)* A student writes: "Carbon dioxide diffuses more slowly than oxygen because carbon dioxide particles are bigger." Explain why this reason is not the one the syllabus expects, and give the correct explanation. **[3]**

**12.** In the experiment in Question 4, the colour eventually becomes the same throughout the water. Explain why, and state what is happening to the particles at that point. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration [1], as a result of the random motion of the particles [1].

**2.** C [1].

**3.** The perfume particles are in constant random motion, colliding with air particles [1]. There is a net movement from where the perfume is concentrated, near the bottle, to the rest of the room, where its concentration is lower [1].

**4. (a)** The purple colour slowly spreads through the water until the whole beaker is evenly purple [1].

**(b)** The dissolved particles move randomly and collide with water particles [1]; there is a net movement from the high concentration around the crystal to the lower concentration in the rest of the water [1]. **[3]**

**5.** The particles move randomly in all directions, not just downwards [1].

**6. (a)** 14 + (3 × 1) = **17** [1].

**(b)** 12 + (2 × 16) = **44** [1].

**(c)** 2 × 35.5 = **71** [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Mᵣ values: He = 4; CH₄ = 16; O₂ = 32; SO₂ = 64 [1]. Lower Mᵣ means faster diffusion [1]. Order: **helium, methane, oxygen, sulfur dioxide** [1].

**8. (a)** NH₃ = **17** [1]; HBr = 1 + 80 = **81** [1].

**(b)** Nearer the hydrobromic acid (hydrogen bromide) end [1].

**(c)** Ammonia has a lower Mᵣ, so its particles move faster at the same temperature [1] and travel further along the tube in the same time before the gases meet [1].

**(d)** NH₃(g) + HBr(g) → NH₄Br(s) [1]. **[6]**

**9. (a)** The paper in tube 2, at 40 °C [1].

**(b)** At the higher temperature the ammonia particles have more kinetic energy [1], so they move faster and diffuse along the tube more quickly [1]. **[3]**

**10.** In a liquid the particles move more slowly than in a gas [1], and they are close together, so they collide very often and travel only a short distance between collisions [1].

**11.** The size of the particles is not what decides the rate here; the syllabus links rate to relative molecular mass [1]. CO₂ has Mᵣ 44 and O₂ has Mᵣ 32 [1]. At the same temperature the heavier CO₂ particles move more slowly on average, so carbon dioxide diffuses more slowly [1].

**12.** The particles have spread out until their concentration is the same everywhere, so there is no longer any net movement [1]. The particles are still moving randomly, but as many move in each direction as in any other [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Definitions that leave out "random motion" or reverse the direction of movement.
- Saying particles stop moving once they are evenly spread.
- Ranking gases without showing the Mᵣ calculations.
- Using reactivity, density or particle size to explain the tube experiment instead of Mᵣ and speed.
