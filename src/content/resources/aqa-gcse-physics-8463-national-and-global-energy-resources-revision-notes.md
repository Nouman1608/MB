## What 4.1.3 asks you to do

| The specification says you should be able to... | What that means in an answer |
|---|---|
| describe the main energy sources available | Name each resource and say what it is |
| distinguish renewable from non-renewable | Classify each one and justify with the definition |
| compare ways resources are used (transport, electricity generation, heating) | Say which resources suit which use, and why |
| understand why some resources are more reliable than others | Link reliability to weather, time of day, season or fuel supply |
| describe the environmental impact of using different resources | Name a specific impact and its cause |
| explain patterns and trends in the use of resources | Read data, quote numbers, then give reasons |
| consider environmental issues from the use of resources | Weigh impacts for a given situation, using data |
| show that science can identify issues but cannot always deal with them | Name the political, social, ethical or economic barrier |

The specification also says: **descriptions of how energy resources are used to generate electricity are
not required.**

## Definitions

- **Renewable energy resource**: one that is being (or can be) **replenished as it is used**.
- **Non-renewable energy resource**: one that is not replenished as it is used. Supplies are finite, so it
  will eventually run out.

Justify with the definition: "Bio-fuel is renewable because new crops can be grown as it is used."

## The main energy resources

The specification lists eleven: fossil fuels (coal, oil and gas), nuclear fuel, bio-fuel, wind,
hydro-electricity, geothermal, the tides, the Sun and water waves.

| Resource | Renewable? | What it is, in one line |
|---|---|---|
| Coal | No | Fossil fuel burned to release energy from its chemical store |
| Oil | No | Fossil fuel; refined into petrol, diesel and heating oil |
| Gas (natural gas) | No | Fossil fuel burned in power stations, boilers and cookers |
| Nuclear fuel | No | Uranium or plutonium; energy released from the nuclear store |
| Bio-fuel | Yes | Fuel from plants or animal waste (e.g. wood, bioethanol, biodiesel) |
| Wind | Yes | Moving air turns wind turbines |
| Hydro-electricity | Yes | Water falling from a reservoir behind a dam |
| Geothermal | Yes | Energy from hot rocks underground |
| Tides | Yes | Rise and fall of sea level, caused mainly by the Moon |
| Sun (solar) | Yes | Solar cells make electricity; solar panels heat water |
| Water waves | Yes | Up-and-down motion of waves at the sea surface |

Nuclear fuel is **not** a fossil fuel, but it **is** non-renewable. Keep the two ideas apart.

## Uses: transport, electricity generation, heating

| Use | Resources commonly used | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Transport | Petrol and diesel (from oil); bio-fuels; electricity for electric cars and trains | A fuel for vehicles must be portable and store a lot of energy |
| Electricity generation | Every resource on the list can be used | Electricity is then transferred to homes and industry |
| Heating | Gas, oil, coal and wood burned in homes; electric heaters; solar water heating; geothermal | Heating can use a fuel directly, or use electricity |

### Method in steps: "compare how two resources are used"

1. Name the use(s) each resource is suited to.
2. Give a reason for each, linked to a property (portable, available on demand, location).
3. Make an explicit comparison word: "whereas", "but", "more", "less".

*Worked reminder.* Oil gives petrol and diesel, liquid fuels a vehicle carries in its tank. Wind cannot be
carried, so it powers transport only by generating electricity for electric vehicles. Oil is used directly,
whereas wind is used only through electricity.

## Reliability

A resource is **reliable** if it can supply energy whenever it is needed, or if its output is predictable.

| Resource | Reliability | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Fossil fuels | Reliable | Available whenever needed, as long as fuel is supplied |
| Nuclear fuel | Reliable | Runs steadily all the time while fuel is supplied |
| Bio-fuel | Reliable | Can be stored and burned when needed |
| Geothermal | Reliable | Hot rocks are always there; output steady |
| Hydro-electricity | Reliable while the reservoir holds water | Output can be started quickly; a long dry spell reduces supply |
| Tides | Predictable, but output varies | Tides come in and out at known times; output varies with the tides through the day |
| Wind | Unreliable | No output on still days; output varies with wind speed |
| Sun | Unreliable | Nothing at night; less on cloudy days and in winter |
| Water waves | Unreliable | Wave height depends on the weather |

### Must-know distinctions

- **Renewable is not the same as reliable.** Wind is renewable but unreliable; coal is reliable but
  non-renewable.
- **Renewable is not the same as harmless.** Hydro-electric dams and tidal barrages are renewable but change
  habitats.
- **Tides vs waves.** Tides are predictable (driven mainly by the Moon). Waves depend on the weather.
- **Hydro vs tidal.** Hydro uses river water stored behind a dam on land. Tidal uses the rise and fall of
  the sea.
- **Source vs use.** "Electricity" is not an energy resource. It is produced from a resource.

## Environmental impact

Name the impact **and** its cause. "Bad for the environment" earns nothing.

| Resource | Main environmental impacts |
|---|---|
| Coal, oil, gas | Burning releases **carbon dioxide**, which contributes to global warming and climate change. Coal and oil also release **sulfur dioxide**, which causes acid rain. Oil spills harm sea life; mining and drilling damage landscapes |
| Nuclear fuel | No carbon dioxide released while running, but produces **radioactive waste** that must be stored safely for a very long time; risk of a serious accident |
| Bio-fuel | Releases carbon dioxide when burned, though growing the plants absorbs carbon dioxide; land used for fuel crops can destroy habitats or reduce land for food |
| Wind | Visual impact and noise near turbines; can harm birds |
| Hydro-electricity | Flooding a valley destroys habitats and can displace people; rotting plants in the flooded area can release methane and carbon dioxide |
| Tides | A barrage changes the estuary habitat for birds and fish and can affect shipping |
| Sun | Large solar farms take up land; visual impact |
| Water waves | Equipment can disturb marine habitats and be a hazard to boats; visual impact |
| Geothermal | Little impact, but only possible in a few places |

## Patterns and trends

### Method in steps: "explain the trend"

1. **Describe** the trend, using words such as increased, decreased, stayed roughly the same.
2. **Quote numbers** from the data (values or percentages), with units.
3. **Explain** with a reason for each change.

Reasons you can use:

- Use of fossil fuels for electricity in the UK has **fallen** because of concern about carbon dioxide and
  climate change, government targets to cut emissions, and because fossil fuels will run out. Britain's last
  coal-fired power station, Ratcliffe-on-Soar, closed on 30 September 2024.
- Use of renewables such as wind and solar has **risen** for the same reasons.
- World energy use has **increased** because the world population is growing and more countries are
  industrialising, with people using more energy per person.

### Worked reminder: reading a data table

The table shows invented data for electricity generated in a country in two years.

| Resource | 2005 (TWh) | 2025 (TWh) |
|---|---|---|
| Coal | 80 | 0 |
| Gas | 100 | 75 |
| Nuclear | 50 | 40 |
| Wind | 10 | 75 |
| Solar | 0 | 25 |
| Other renewables | 10 | 35 |
| **Total** | **250** | **250** |

```
Fossil fuels, 2005:  (80 + 100) / 250 × 100 = 72%
Fossil fuels, 2025:  (0 + 75) / 250 × 100   = 30%
Renewables, 2005:    (10 + 0 + 10) / 250 × 100  = 8%
Renewables, 2025:    (75 + 25 + 35) / 250 × 100 = 54%
Wind grew by a factor of 75 / 10 = 7.5
```

A full answer: "The fossil fuel share fell from 72% to 30%, and coal fell to zero. The renewable share rose
from 8% to 54%, with wind growing 7.5 times. Total generation stayed at 250 TWh, so renewables replaced
fossil fuels rather than meeting extra demand, probably to cut carbon dioxide emissions." Always calculate
percentages from the totals given.

## Science identifies issues but cannot always solve them

Scientists can link carbon dioxide to climate change and assess the risks of radioactive waste. Whether
anything is done depends on other factors.

| Consideration | Examples |
|---|---|
| Economic | Building nuclear or tidal power stations costs a lot; fossil fuel stations already exist; cost of energy to consumers |
| Political | Governments set targets and policy; international agreement is needed; energy security (relying on imported fuel) |
| Social | Local people may object to wind farms near their homes; jobs in coal mining or power stations may be lost |
| Ethical | Should we leave radioactive waste and climate change for future generations? Is it fair to ask poorer countries to use less energy? |

In an answer, name the type of consideration **and** give a specific example of it.

## Quick self-test

1. Use the definition to explain why wood is a renewable resource.
2. Name the renewable resource that gives a steady output but is only available in a few places.
3. Which is more predictable, tidal power or wave power? Give a reason.
4. Name two energy resources used directly to heat homes.
5. Name one renewable resource used as a fuel for transport.
6. Give one environmental impact of building a hydro-electric dam that floods a valley.
7. Burning coal releases sulfur dioxide. Name the environmental problem this causes.
8. A country generates 240 TWh of electricity in a year. 54 TWh comes from wind. What percentage is this?
9. Solar generation in a country rose from 18 TWh to 54 TWh. By what factor did it increase?
10. Solar provides 5% of a country's 300 TWh of electricity. How much electricity is this?
11. Give one reason why world energy use has increased.
12. Scientists agree that burning fossil fuels contributes to climate change. Give two reasons, other than
    scientific ones, why some countries still build fossil fuel power stations.

### Answers

1. Trees can be replanted and grown again, so wood can be replenished as it is used.
2. Geothermal.
3. Tidal. Tides happen at known times each day; waves depend on the weather.
4. Any two of: gas, oil, coal, wood (bio-fuel), the Sun (solar water heating), geothermal.
5. Bio-fuel (for example bioethanol or biodiesel).
6. Any one: habitats are destroyed; people may have to move; rotting plants release methane or carbon
   dioxide.
7. Acid rain.
8. 54 ÷ 240 × 100 = **22.5%**.
9. 54 ÷ 18 = **3** (it trebled).
10. 0.05 × 300 = **15 TWh**.
11. The world population is growing, or more countries are industrialising and using more energy per person.
12. Any two, each named and specific: economic (fossil fuel stations are cheaper to build, or the fuel is
    available locally); political (energy security, or no international agreement); social (jobs in the
    fuel industry); reliability (they supply electricity on demand).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying a renewable resource "never runs out" without the specification idea of being **replenished as it
  is used**.
- Calling nuclear fuel a fossil fuel, or calling it renewable because it releases no carbon dioxide.
- Treating "renewable" and "reliable" as the same thing in a comparison question.
- Writing "pollution" or "bad for the environment" instead of naming carbon dioxide, sulfur dioxide or
  radioactive waste and its effect.
- Mixing up tidal and wave power, or describing tidal power as unpredictable.
- Describing how a power station works when the question asks about the resource.
- Describing a trend without quoting numbers from the data, or quoting numbers without units.
- Working out a percentage from the wrong year's total.
- On "explain why the issue has not been solved", repeating the scientific problem instead of naming a
  political, social, ethical or economic barrier.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a renewable energy resource?**
A renewable energy resource is one that is being, or can be, replenished as it is used, such as wind, solar, tides or bio-fuel. Saying it "never runs out" without the idea of replenishment does not earn the mark.

**Is nuclear fuel a fossil fuel?**
No. Nuclear fuel, such as uranium or plutonium, is not a fossil fuel, but it is non-renewable because supplies are finite.

**What is the difference between renewable and reliable?**
Renewable means a resource is replenished as it is used; reliable means it can supply energy whenever needed. Wind is renewable but unreliable, while coal is reliable but non-renewable.

**Do you need to know how power stations generate electricity for AQA GCSE 4.1.3?**
No. The AQA 8463 specification states that descriptions of how energy resources are used to generate electricity are not required for section 4.1.3.
