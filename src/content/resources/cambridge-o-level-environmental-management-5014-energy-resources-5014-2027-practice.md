## Section A

**1.** Name the fossil fuel that formed from the remains of swamp plants. **[1]**

**2.** Classify each energy resource as renewable or non-renewable: biogas, uranium, tidal power, natural
gas. **[2]**

**3.** State the name of the device that converts light directly into electricity. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** conditions needed for the remains of marine organisms to become oil. **[2]**

**5.** Identify **three** factors from the syllabus that increase a country's demand for energy as it
develops. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** Describe how coal is formed. **[4]**

**7.** Explain why nuclear power is classed as non-renewable even though no fuel is burned. **[2]**

**8.** Describe how electricity is generated in a hydro-electric power station. **[3]**

**9.** Describe how a farm could use animal manure to generate electricity. **[3]**

**10.** *Invented data.* The table shows the electricity generated in a country in two years.

| Resource | 2015 (units) | 2025 (units) |
|---|---|---|
| Coal | 60 | 40 |
| Natural gas | 20 | 25 |
| Wind | 5 | 20 |
| Solar | 0 | 10 |
| Hydro-electric | 15 | 15 |
| Total | 100 | 110 |

(a) Calculate the percentage of electricity generated from renewable resources in 2025.

(b) Describe **three** changes in the country's electricity generation between 2015 and 2025.

(c) Suggest **one** reason why the country still generates electricity from fossil fuels. **[6]**

**11.** Explain how **two** of these factors affect a country's demand for energy: climate, unreliable
supply, scarcity of resources. **[4]**

**12.** A coastal country with steady strong winds is choosing between a new wind farm and a new coal-fired
power station. Discuss the benefits and limitations of each option. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Coal [1].

**2.** Biogas and tidal power renewable [1]; uranium and natural gas non-renewable [1].

**3.** Photovoltaic (solar) cell [1].

**4.** Any two: lack of oxygen [1]; burial under sediment [1]; heat [1]; pressure [1]; millions of years
[1].

**5.** Any three: transport [1]; personal and national wealth [1]; industry [1]; human population size
[1].

**6.** Any four:

- Plants grew in warm, swampy forests [1].
- Dead plants fell into waterlogged ground with little oxygen, so they did not fully decay [1].
- Layers of partly decayed material built up as peat [1].
- The peat was buried under layers of sediment [1].
- Heat and pressure over millions of years changed it into coal [1].

**7.** Nuclear power uses uranium as its fuel [1], which is mined from a finite supply in the Earth's crust
and is not replaced, so it will eventually run out [1].

**8.** Any three:

- A dam holds back water in a reservoir [1].
- Water is released and flows downhill through pipes [1].
- The moving water turns turbines [1].
- The turbines drive generators that produce electricity [1].

**9.** Any three:

- The manure is placed in a sealed tank (digester) without oxygen [1].
- Bacteria break it down, producing biogas, which is mainly methane [1].
- The biogas is burned [1].
- The heat or the burning gas drives a turbine or engine connected to a generator [1].

**10.** (a) (20 + 10 + 15) / 110 x 100 = 45 / 110 x 100 = **40.9%** [1 for the working, 1 for the answer].
(b) Any three, with figures: coal fell from 60 to 40 units [1]; wind rose from 5 to 20 units [1]; solar rose
from 0 to 10 units [1]; total generation rose from 100 to 110 units [1]; natural gas rose from 20 to 25
units [1]; hydro-electric stayed the same at 15 units [1].
(c) Any one: wind and solar output varies, so fossil fuels provide a reliable supply when it is calm or
dark [1]; existing power stations are still working [1]; coal or gas may be produced in the country, giving
jobs and secure supply [1].

**11.** Two factors, each with an effect [1] and a reason [1]:

- **Climate**: very hot or very cold places use more energy [1], for cooling with air conditioning or for
  heating homes [1].
- **Unreliable supply**: where power cuts are frequent, people and businesses change how they get energy
  [1], for example buying their own generators or batteries, or using other fuels [1].
- **Scarcity of resources**: scarce fuels become expensive [1], so people use less or switch to other
  resources [1].

**12.** Wind farm, benefits, any two: no fuel cost [1]; no carbon dioxide released while generating [1];
steady strong winds mean good output at this site [1]; renewable [1].
Wind farm, limitations, any two: output stops when the wind drops, so back-up is needed [1]; visual and
noise impacts [1]; high building cost [1].
Coal, benefits, any one: reliable output that can be adjusted to demand [1]; may use the country's own
coal [1].
Coal, limitations, any one: releases carbon dioxide, adding to climate change [1]; releases sulfur dioxide,
causing acid rain [1]; coal is finite [1].
Maximum 6 marks; a judgement linked to the strong winds is expected for full marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying oil formed from trees, or coal from marine organisms.
- In 10(a), dividing by 100 instead of the 2025 total of 110, or including natural gas in the
  renewable total.
- Describing change without quoting figures from the table.
- Naming a demand factor without explaining why it changes demand.
- Discussing only one of the two options in question 12.
