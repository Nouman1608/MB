Condensed notes on section **3.6.4 Making human resource decisions: improving motivation and engagement** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). The full explanations and worked examples are in the [motivation and engagement study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-motivation-and-engagement/); exam-style questions are in the [motivation and engagement practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-motivation-and-engagement-practice/).

## Specification content

- The benefits of motivated and engaged employees; theories of motivation including Taylor, Maslow and Herzberg.
- How to improve engagement and motivation: financial methods (piece rate, commission, salary schemes, performance-related pay); non-financial methods (empowerment, team working, flexible working, job enrichment, job rotation).
- Influences on the choice and assessment of the effectiveness of financial and non-financial methods.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Motivation | The drive to work towards a goal |
| Engagement | Deeper commitment, with discretionary effort |
| Piece rate | Pay per unit produced |
| Commission | Pay as a percentage of sales |
| Performance-related pay (PRP) | Bonus or pay rise linked to targets |
| Empowerment | Giving employees authority over decisions about their work |
| Job enrichment | More responsibility and challenge in a job (vertical) |
| Job rotation | Moving between jobs at the same level (horizontal) |
| Hygiene factor | Herzberg: prevents dissatisfaction but does not motivate |
| Motivator | Herzberg: creates satisfaction and motivation |

## Benefits of motivated and engaged staff

Higher productivity and quality; better customer service; less absence and turnover (lower recruitment and training costs); more ideas and acceptance of change; better employer reputation; less supervision needed.

## Theories

| Theorist | Idea | Application | Criticism |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Taylor** | People are motivated mainly by money; break jobs into simple tasks, find the best method, supervise closely, pay by results | Piece rate; targets; time and motion study | Ignores social needs; boring work; heavy supervision demotivates |
| **Maslow** | Five levels of need: physiological, safety, social, esteem, self-actualisation; lower needs first | Match rewards to the level staff have reached | People do not always move in order; differs by person and culture |
| **Herzberg** | Hygiene factors (pay, conditions, policy, supervision, security) prevent dissatisfaction; motivators (achievement, recognition, the work itself, responsibility, advancement) motivate | Get hygiene right, then add job enrichment and recognition | Based on professionals; pay motivates some people |

## Financial methods

| Method | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Piece rate | Pay linked to output; easy to understand | Quality falls; stress; unstable pay; needs measurable output |
| Commission | Strong sales incentive; cost rises only with sales | Hard selling; ignores service and teamwork; unstable income |
| Salary | Secure; supports teamwork and quality | Weak link to effort |
| PRP | Rewards achievement | Unfair targets; short-term focus; harms cooperation |

**Examples:** 520 units at £0.80 = **£416**. Basic £1,200 plus 5% of sales above £20,000, with sales of £36,000: £1,200 + £800 = **£2,000**. PRP of 6% on £30,000 = **£1,800**.

**Piece rate comparison:** hourly pay £400 for 500 units (£0.80 per unit). On piece rate, 600 units earn £480 (+£80). Extra 100 units add £180 after materials, so net gain **£100** per worker per week, unless quality falls or workers burn out.

## Non-financial methods

| Method | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Empowerment | Faster decisions; trust; development | Needs training; poor decisions possible |
| Team working | Social needs met; shared skills and ideas | Slower decisions; conflict; free riders |
| Flexible working | Work-life balance; retention; less absence | Harder to coordinate; not possible for all jobs |
| Job enrichment | Esteem and self-actualisation; uses skills | Training and pay needs; not all want more responsibility |
| Job rotation | Less boredom; broader skills; cover | No added challenge; training cost |

## Choosing a method: influences

Nature of work (routine and measurable suits piece rate; complex suits empowerment and enrichment); employees' needs and attitudes; management style and culture; cost; labour market; competitors; technology; business objectives; legal and ethical issues.

## Assessing effectiveness

Productivity; quality and complaints; turnover and absence; engagement surveys; customer satisfaction; cost against benefit. Difficult because many things change at once and effects take time.

## Exam traps

- Pay is mostly a **hygiene factor** for Herzberg.
- Job rotation is horizontal; job enrichment is vertical.
- Piece rate and commission can harm quality and teamwork.
- Apply the theory to the evidence: say which need or factor is involved.
- No single method suits every employee.

## Self-test

1. Give two benefits of motivated employees.
2. What did Taylor believe was the main motivator?
3. Name the five levels in Maslow's hierarchy.
4. Which level does job security belong to?
5. Give two Herzberg hygiene factors.
6. Give two Herzberg motivators.
7. A worker is paid £1.20 per unit and produces 350 units. Calculate pay.
8. A salesperson earns a basic £1,000 plus 4% commission on sales of £30,000. Calculate pay.
9. Give one disadvantage of piece rate.
10. Distinguish between job enrichment and job rotation.
11. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of flexible working.
12. Give two ways to assess whether a motivation method is working.

### Answers

1. Any two: higher productivity; better quality; better service; less absence and turnover; more ideas.
2. Money (economic reward linked to output).
3. Physiological, safety, social, esteem, self-actualisation.
4. Safety.
5. Any two: pay; working conditions; company policy; supervision; job security; relationships.
6. Any two: achievement; recognition; the work itself; responsibility; advancement.
7. 350 × £1.20 = **£420**.
8. £1,000 + 4% × £30,000 = £1,000 + £1,200 = **£2,200**.
9. Any one: quality may fall; stress; unstable pay; needs measurable output.
10. Job enrichment gives more responsibility and challenge in a job; job rotation moves workers between jobs at the same level.
11. Advantage (any one): work-life balance; retention; less absence. Disadvantage (any one): harder to coordinate; not suitable for all jobs.
12. Any two: productivity; quality; turnover or absence; engagement survey; customer satisfaction.
