## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following brain structures co-ordinates movement and balance? **[1]**

A. Brain stem
B. Thalamus
C. Cerebellum
D. Cortex

**2.** State the main function of **(a)** the thalamus and **(b)** the cortex. **[2]**

**3.** Identify Piaget's stage of development for each child. **[3]**

**(a)** Noah, aged 1, lifts a cushion to find a toy he saw hidden underneath it.
**(b)** Zara, aged 9, knows a ball of clay still has the same amount of clay when rolled into a sausage, but struggles with "What if people had no need to sleep?"
**(c)** Ethan, aged 14, argues about what would happen to society if money were abolished.

**4.** Explain what is meant by **conservation**, using an example. **[2]**

**5.** Distinguish between a **verbaliser** and a **visualiser**. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Describe Hughes' 'policeman doll' study. **[4]**

**7.** Explain how both nature and nurture contribute to early brain development. **[4]**

**8.** Mr Okafor teaches a class of six-year-olds. Using Piaget's theory, suggest **two** ways he could plan his lessons. **[4]**

**9.** A school decides to test every pupil's learning style and then teach verbalisers and visualisers in separate groups. Using Willingham's Learning Theory, explain why this may not improve their learning. **[3]**

**10.** Describe and evaluate Piaget's stage theory of cognitive development. Refer to research evidence in your answer. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** (cerebellum) [1].

**2. (a)** The thalamus **relays information from the senses** to the cortex (sensory processing) [1]. **(b)** The cortex is responsible for **cognition**, such as thinking, language and planning [1].

**3. (a)** **Sensorimotor** -- he shows object permanence [1]. **(b)** **Concrete operational** -- she conserves but cannot yet reason about abstract, hypothetical ideas [1]. **(c)** **Formal operational** -- abstract, hypothetical reasoning [1].

**4.** Conservation is understanding that a **quantity stays the same even when its appearance changes** [1], for example knowing that a row of counters still has the same number when spread out, or that juice poured into a taller glass is the same amount [1].

**5.** A **verbaliser** prefers to learn through **words**, such as reading or listening to explanations [1], **whereas** a **visualiser** prefers to learn through **images**, such as diagrams, pictures and charts [1].

**6.** Up to 4 marks: young children were shown a model of **two walls crossing** to form four sections [1]; after practising with one policeman doll, they had to **hide a boy doll from two policeman dolls** [1], so that only one section was out of sight of both [1]; **most children hid the doll correctly** [1], suggesting they could take others' points of view and were **less egocentric than Piaget claimed**, because the task made human sense to them [1]. Maximum 4.

**7.** Two marks for nature, two for nurture:
- **Nature:** genes provide a blueprint so that structures develop in a **fixed order** [1], with the brain stem (autonomic functions) first and the cortex (cognition) last, which is why the sequence is similar in all children [1].
- **Nurture:** the **environment in the womb** matters, such as the mother's diet and avoiding harmful substances like alcohol [1]; after birth, **stimulation, play and interaction** strengthen the connections between neurons that are used [1].

**8.** Any two, 2 marks each (suggestion plus link to Piaget):
- Use **concrete materials** such as counters or blocks [1], because pre-operational children struggle with abstract ideas [1].
- Plan **discovery learning** activities where children explore for themselves [1], because Piaget saw children as active learners building their own schemas [1].
- Set tasks **matched to readiness**, not beyond the stage [1], since children cannot be taught concepts such as conservation before they are ready, according to Piaget [1].
- Create activities that **challenge existing schemas** [1], so accommodation can take place [1].

**9.** Willingham argued there is **little evidence** that matching teaching to a learner's preferred style improves learning [1]. The method should match the **content** -- for example, a diagram of the heart is best learned visually by everyone, including verbalisers [1]. Learning depends more on pupils thinking about the **meaning** of the material, so separating groups by style may waste time without improving results [1].

**10.** Marked by levels.

*Indicative content (description):* children build **schemas** and develop through **assimilation** and **accommodation**; four stages in a fixed order -- **sensorimotor** (object permanence), **pre-operational** (egocentrism, no conservation), **concrete operational** (conservation, logical thinking about real objects), **formal operational** (abstract reasoning); egocentrism reduces and conservation develops with age.

*Indicative content (evaluation):* **strength** -- hugely influential in **education**, leading to discovery learning and readiness; **weakness** -- **McGarrigle and Donaldson** found more young children conserved number when a naughty teddy changed the row by accident, suggesting Piaget **underestimated** children; **weakness** -- **Hughes** found young children could take another's view in the policeman doll task, so egocentrism reduces earlier than Piaget claimed; **counterpoint** -- these studies changed the task, so they challenge Piaget's ages more than his sequence of stages; **judgement** -- the order of development is broadly supported but the ages and the effect of the testing method may be wrong.

- **Level 3 (7-9):** accurate, detailed description; evaluation well developed with evidence; clear judgement.
- **Level 2 (4-6):** mostly accurate description; some evaluation, partly developed.
- **Level 1 (1-3):** basic description; evaluation absent or asserted.
- **0:** no relevant content.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a stage name with no reason drawn from the scenario.
- Defining conservation without an example when one is asked for.
- Saying the policeman doll study tested conservation (it tested egocentrism).
- Describing learning styles in question 9 instead of explaining Willingham's criticism.
- In question 10, listing stages with no evaluation, or evaluating without naming studies.

## Frequently asked questions

**What development topics do these practice questions cover?**
They cover AQA GCSE Psychology 8182 section 3.1.3: early brain development, Piaget's stages, the Hughes and McGarrigle and Donaldson studies, Dweck's mindsets and Willingham's view of learning styles.

**How do I identify a Piagetian stage in a scenario?**
Use the age as a guide, then confirm it with the behaviour described, such as conservation or abstract reasoning, and quote that behaviour in your answer.
