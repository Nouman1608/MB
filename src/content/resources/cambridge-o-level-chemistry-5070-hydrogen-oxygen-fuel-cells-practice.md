## Section A

**1.** State the only chemical product of a hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell. **[1]**

**2.** Name the two substances supplied to a hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell, and state the form of energy it produces. **[3]**

**3.** Write the balanced equation, with state symbols, for the overall reaction in a hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why a hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell cannot produce carbon dioxide. **[1]**

**5.** State which reactant in a fuel cell is reduced, and explain your answer in terms of electrons. **[2]**

**6.** Name the toxic gas produced by incomplete combustion of petrol in an engine, and state why it is harmful. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A fuel-cell car uses 4.00 kg of hydrogen on a long journey.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of hydrogen used.

**(b)** Calculate the mass of water produced, in kg.

**(c)** Calculate the volume this hydrogen would occupy at r.t.p., in m³. (1 m³ = 1000 dm³)

**(d)** Use your answer to (c) to explain one disadvantage of hydrogen as a vehicle fuel. **[6]**

**8.** Describe the advantages and disadvantages of using hydrogen–oxygen fuel cells instead of petrol engines in vehicles. **[6]**

**9.** Petrol engines release oxides of nitrogen, but fuel cells do not, even though both take in air.

**(a)** Explain how oxides of nitrogen form in a petrol engine.

**(b)** Suggest why they do not form in a fuel cell. **[3]**

**10.** A student writes: "Fuel-cell cars cause no pollution at all." Evaluate this statement. **[3]**

**11.** Hydrogen for fuel cells can be made by the electrolysis of water, for example using dilute sulfuric acid.

**(a)** State the ratio of the volume of hydrogen to the volume of oxygen produced.

**(b)** Explain why making the hydrogen using electricity from a coal-fired power station reduces the environmental benefit of fuel-cell cars. **[3]**

**12.** Explain, in terms of electrons, how a hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell produces an electric current. **[3]**

**13.** Hydrogen is stored in fuel-cell cars in strong tanks at high pressure.

**(a)** Explain why high pressure is used.

**(b)** State **one** hazard of storing hydrogen in this way. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Water [1].

**2.** Hydrogen [1]; oxygen (from air) [1]; electrical energy / electricity [1].

**3.** 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, correct formulae and balancing [1]; state symbols (g), (g), (l) [1].

**4.** Neither hydrogen nor oxygen contains carbon [1].

**5.** Oxygen is reduced [1] because it gains electrons [1].

**6.** Carbon monoxide [1]; it is toxic (it reduces the blood's ability to carry oxygen) [1].

**7. (a)** 4000 g ÷ 2 g/mol = **2000 mol** [1].

**(b)** Ratio H₂ : H₂O = 1 : 1, so 2000 mol of water [1]; 2000 × 18 = 36 000 g = **36.0 kg** [1].

**(c)** 2000 × 24 = 48 000 dm³ [1] = **48.0 m³** [1].

**(d)** Hydrogen occupies a very large volume as a gas at room conditions, so it must be compressed into heavy, high-pressure tanks to fit in a car [1].

**8.** Advantages, any three, one mark each [3]: water is the only product; no carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas, is released by the vehicle; no carbon monoxide (toxic) or oxides of nitrogen (acid rain); hydrogen does not come from petroleum, which is non-renewable; more efficient conversion of chemical energy to electricity; quieter, with fewer moving parts.

Disadvantages, any three, one mark each [3]: hydrogen is a gas, so it is harder to store than liquid petrol and needs high-pressure tanks; hydrogen is highly flammable and forms explosive mixtures with air; hydrogen is mostly made from methane, releasing carbon dioxide, or by electrolysis using electricity from fossil fuels; there are few refuelling stations; fuel cells are expensive.

**9. (a)** Nitrogen and oxygen from the air [1] react at the high temperature inside the engine [1].

**(b)** There is no combustion in a fuel cell; the only reaction is between hydrogen and oxygen, which forms water [1].

**10.** The statement is partly correct: the car itself releases only water, with no CO₂, CO or oxides of nitrogen [1]. However, producing the hydrogen can cause pollution, such as CO₂ when it is made from methane or when the electricity for electrolysis comes from fossil fuels [1]. So the statement is incorrect overall; the pollution moves from the vehicle to where the hydrogen is made [1].

**11. (a)** 2 : 1 (hydrogen : oxygen) [1].

**(b)** Burning coal releases carbon dioxide (and sulfur dioxide) [1], so pollution is still produced, just at the power station instead of from the car [1].

**12.** Hydrogen loses electrons (is oxidised) at one electrode [1]. Oxygen gains electrons (is reduced) at the other electrode [1]. The electrons flow through the external circuit from the hydrogen side to the oxygen side, and this flow is the current [1].

**13. (a)** A gas occupies a very large volume at normal pressure; compressing it fits enough hydrogen into a tank of practical size [1].

**(b)** Hydrogen is highly flammable, so a leak could cause a fire or explosion / the tank could burst [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 2: writing "heat" as the main energy output. The cell is designed to produce electricity.
- Question 3: writing H₂O(g) is usually accepted for a hot cell, but missing state symbols altogether loses the mark.
- Question 7(b): multiplying 4.00 kg by 18 directly, without converting to moles.
- Question 7(c): stopping at dm³ when the question asks for m³.
- Question 8: listing only advantages, or writing vague points such as "better for the environment" without naming a substance.
- Question 9(b): saying "fuel cells do not use air". They usually do; the point is that no combustion takes place.
- Question 10: agreeing with the statement. Pollution from making hydrogen is the expected counter-argument.
- Question 12: saying electrons pass through the cell between the gases. They flow through the external circuit.
