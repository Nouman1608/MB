## Questions

**1.** Define productivity. **[2]**

**2.** Country W has GDP of 21,600 million and 9 million people. Country X has GDP of 270,000 million and 4.5 million people.

**(a)** Calculate income per head in W. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate income per head in X. **[1]**
**(c)** State how many times larger income per head is in X than in W. **[1]**

**3.** Country Y produces 30,000 million with 4 million workers. A year later it produces 32,400 million with the same workers. Calculate the percentage rise in output per worker. **[4]**

**4.** In Country S, GDP grows by 3.6% and the population by 2.4%.

**(a)** Calculate the approximate and the exact growth in income per head. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why growth in income per head is lower than growth in GDP. **[2]**

**5.** Country Z has a GDP of 40,000 million. The primary sector produces 38% of it, the secondary sector 17% and the tertiary sector 45%.

**(a)** Calculate the value of primary output. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why a large primary sector may keep income per head low. **[3]**

**6.** Country V has GDP of 50,000 million and 5 million workers, and invests 9% of GDP. Country U has GDP of 200,000 million and 2 million workers, and invests 24% of GDP.

**(a)** Calculate investment per worker in each country. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how this difference could affect future incomes. **[3]**

**7.** Explain two reasons why saving is low in a low-income country. **[4]**

**8.** Explain two ways in which low spending on education could lead to lower income per head. **[4]**

**9.** In Country T, workers lose 20 of 250 working days a year to illness. The workforce is 3 million and output per worker is 7,000.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of working time lost. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of full-time worker-years lost. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the output lost, in millions, assuming it falls in proportion. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain one other impact of poor healthcare on development. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why a country with a high birth rate may have a low level of saving. **[4]**

**11.** Evaluate whether investment is the most important cause of differences in economic development between countries. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Productivity is the output [1] obtained from each unit of a resource used, such as per worker or per hour [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Output" alone, or "how much a firm produces", does not show the per-input idea; name the input.

**2.** (a) 21,600 ÷ 9 = **2,400** [1].
(b) 270,000 ÷ 4.5 = **60,000** [1].
(c) 60,000 ÷ 2,400 = **25 times** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write out each division so that your method is visible even if one figure is wrong.

**3.** Output per worker at first: 30,000 ÷ 4 = 7,500 [1]. A year later: 32,400 ÷ 4 = 8,100 [1]. Change = (8,100 − 7,500) ÷ 7,500 [1] = **8%** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The base of a percentage change is the starting figure. Using the later figure is a common slip, and a bare percentage leaves nothing to show your method.

**4.** (a) Approximate 3.6 − 2.4 = 1.2% [1]. Exact 1.036 ÷ 1.024 − 1 = **1.17%** [1].
(b) Population growth must be subtracted [1] because the same output is shared among more people, so each person's share grows more slowly [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Tie the explanation to sharing output among people; "population is growing" without that link is only a restatement.

**5.** (a) 0.38 × 40,000 = **15,200 million** [1].
(b) Output per worker in primary activity is often low, so the sector adds little value per worker [1]. Raw material prices fluctuate with weather and world demand, so incomes are unstable [1]. If most workers are in this sector, average income per head stays low [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A three-mark explanation usually needs a cause, a link and an impact, not three separate statements.

**6.** (a) V: 0.09 × 50,000 = 4,500 million, so 4,500 ÷ 5 = **900** per worker [1]. U: 0.24 × 200,000 = 48,000 million [1], so 48,000 ÷ 2 = **24,000** per worker [1].
(b) Less capital per worker in V [1] means lower productivity [1] and lower output and incomes, and the gap widens as U's capital grows faster [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Work out total investment first, then divide by workers. Giving only the percentages misses the per-worker comparison the question asks for.

**7.** Reason one: incomes are low [1], so most is spent on necessities and little remains to save [1]. Reason two: weak or distant banks [1], so people hold cash or goods rather than deposits [1]. (Other valid reasons include high inflation or political risk.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each reason needs developing. "Low income" alone is a statement, so add what happens to spending and saving.

**8.** Way one: fewer skilled workers [1], so productivity is lower and output per worker is low [1]. Way two: less ability to use new technology [1], so firms cannot adopt methods that raise output, or foreign firms do not invest [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Finish each chain at income per head or output, which is what the question asks about.

**9.** (a) 20 ÷ 250 × 100 = **8%** [1].
(b) 0.08 × 3 million = **240,000** worker-years [1].
(c) 240,000 × 7,000 = 1,680,000,000, which is **1,680 million** [1].
(d) Poor health lowers school attendance [1], so fewer skills are built and productivity stays low [1]. (Other valid impacts include shorter working lives and higher family spending on medicine.) **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert units carefully, because the output is in millions: 240,000 workers multiplied by 7,000 gives 1,680,000,000, not 1,680. State the unit with the final answer, and show the multiplication so the method is visible.

**10.** A high birth rate means many children [1], so a large share of the population are dependants [1]. Households spend more on food and schooling [1], leaving little to save, so fewer funds reach banks for investment [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Move step by step from births to dependants to household spending to saving. A skipped link leaves the reasoning incomplete, so write each one out, even if it feels obvious. Saying only that children are costly does not reach saving.

**11.** Argument for [1]: investment adds capital per worker, which raises productivity and income [1]. Without it, skilled and healthy workers have too few machines [1]. Argument against [1]: investment needs saving, which depends on income and confidence, so it is often an effect, not a first cause [1]. Education and health raise the benefit of each machine, since skilled, healthy workers use capital more productively [1]. Judgement: investment is the most direct route to higher output, but it works best alongside skills and stability [1], and the answer depends on which factor is scarcest in the country [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong evaluation weighs factors and reaches a reasoned conclusion. A list of causes with no judgement stays at description.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving income per head when the question asks about GDP, or the reverse.
- Using the later year as the base of a percentage change.
- Using the approximate rule when the question asks for an exact figure.
- Explaining causes only, when impacts are also required.
- Stating "low income" or "poor health" with no chain to output or income.
- Leaving units out of calculations, especially "million".
- Treating a large population as always good or always bad.
- Writing an evaluation with no conclusion, or a conclusion that ignores the points above it.
