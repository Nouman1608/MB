These questions cover section **4.10.2 Life cycle assessment and recycling** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462)
specification, for teaching from September 2016. The content is assessed on Paper 2 at both Foundation and
Higher Tier. The specification marks nothing in this section (HT only), so no question is labelled Higher tier
only. The data in the questions are invented for practice.

[life cycle assessment and recycling study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-life-cycle-assessment-and-recycling/)

## Questions

**1.** A life cycle assessment (LCA) assesses the environmental impact of a product.

**(a)** Name the **four** stages of a product's life that an LCA considers. **[4]**
**(b)** State what else is included at each stage. **[1]**

**2.** An LCA gathers data on a glass bottle and a plastic bottle.

| Stage | Glass bottle / MJ | Plastic bottle / MJ |
|---|---|---|
| Extracting and processing raw materials | 0.8 | 0.6 |
| Manufacturing and packaging | 3.1 | 0.9 |
| Transport and distribution | 0.9 | 0.3 |
| Disposal | 0.2 | 0.2 |

**(a)** Calculate the total energy for each bottle. **[2]**
**(b)** The glass bottle is reused 10 times and the plastic bottle is used once. Calculate the energy per use for each bottle. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate which bottle has the lower impact on the environment, using your answers and one other factor. **[3]**

**3.** Explain why an LCA is not a purely objective process. **[2]**

**4.** A company advertises: "Our paper bag has the lowest carbon footprint," but it only reports the use stage.

**(a)** Name the type of LCA the company has used. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the claim might be misleading. **[2]**
**(c)** State **two** questions you would ask about the claim. **[2]**

**5.** A comparison of shopping bags includes the following data for one bag.

| | Plastic bag | Paper bag |
|---|---|---|
| Water used making / dm³ | 4 | 12 |
| Solid waste at disposal / g | 12 | 25 |
| Number of times used | 4 | 1 |

**(a)** Calculate the water used per use for each bag. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the solid waste per use for each bag. **[2]**
**(c)** Give one factor that cannot easily be quantified in an LCA. **[1]**

**6.** Glass bottles can be reused or recycled.

**(a)** Distinguish between reusing and recycling a glass bottle. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest **two** benefits of recycling glass. **[2]**

**7.** Scrap steel is added to iron from a blast furnace when making steel.

**(a)** Explain why this reduces the environmental impact of steelmaking. **[2]**
**(b)** A steelworks makes 1000 tonnes of steel. 25% of the steel is made from scrap. Calculate the mass of steel that must come from blast furnace iron (assume no loss of mass). **[2]**

**8.** Making aluminium from its ore needs 150 MJ per kilogram. Recycling aluminium needs 7.5 MJ per kilogram.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of energy saved by recycling. **[2]**
**(b)** Give **two** other benefits of recycling aluminium. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest **one** reason why not all aluminium is recycled. **[1]**

**9.** Quarrying limestone has environmental impacts.

**(a)** Describe **two** environmental impacts of quarrying. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the reuse of building materials reduces these impacts. **[2]**

**10.** A council is deciding between offering a bottle refill scheme and a glass recycling scheme.

Evaluate the two options. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Extracting and processing raw materials [1]; manufacturing and packaging [1]; use and operation during its lifetime [1]; disposal at the end of its useful life [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** **Transport and distribution** [1]. **[1]**

**2. (a)** Glass: 0.8 + 3.1 + 0.9 + 0.2 = **5.0 MJ** [1]; plastic: 0.6 + 0.9 + 0.3 + 0.2 = **2.0 MJ** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Glass: 5.0 ÷ 10 = **0.50 MJ** per use [1]; plastic: **2.0 MJ** per use [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Per use, the reused glass bottle has the **lower energy use** (0.50 MJ compared with 2.0 MJ) [1]; but this depends on the bottle really being reused 10 times, which needs **collection and washing** (energy and water) [1]; other factors: the plastic bottle is lighter so less transport energy, and plastic is made from crude oil, which is finite [1]; a conclusion with a reason [1]. (maximum **[3]**)

**3.** Allocating **numerical values** to pollutant effects is less straightforward [1] and needs **value judgements** about which effects matter most [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** A **selective** (abbreviated) LCA [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** It leaves out the **other stages** (raw materials, manufacture, disposal), where the paper bag may perform badly [1]; it may have been produced to support an **advertising** claim [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Any two [1] each: which stages were included? Who carried out the assessment? Are the data shown? What was the bag compared with? **[2]**

**5. (a)** Plastic: 4 ÷ 4 = **1 dm³** [1]; paper: 12 ÷ 1 = **12 dm³** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Plastic: 12 ÷ 4 = **3 g** [1]; paper: **25 g** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Any one: the effect of litter on wildlife; damage to habitats; effects of pollutants; effects on human health [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** **Reuse**: the bottle is washed and used again for the same purpose [1]; **recycle**: the glass is crushed and melted and remade into new glass products [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Any two [1] each: saves raw materials (sand, limestone); saves energy; reduces quarrying; reduces waste to landfill. **[2]**

**7. (a)** It **reduces the iron that must be extracted from ore** [1], so less mining, less energy and less carbon dioxide from the blast furnace [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 25% from scrap leaves 75% from blast furnace iron [1]; 0.75 × 1000 = **750 tonnes** [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** Saving = 150 − 7.5 = 142.5 MJ per kg [1]; 142.5 ÷ 150 × 100 = **95%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Any two [1] each: saves bauxite ore; reduces mining; reduces waste to landfill; reduces carbon dioxide emissions. **[2]**
**(c)** Any one: collection and sorting costs; items not returned; mixed materials are hard to separate. [1] **[1]**

**9. (a)** Any two [1] each: loss of habitat; noise; dust; spoil heaps / waste rock; visual impact; emissions from vehicles. **[2]**
**(b)** Reusing means less new material is needed [1], so less quarrying and fewer impacts, and less energy to process and transport new materials [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Credit up to six, with points on both options and a conclusion:
- Refill: the bottle is reused, so no remelting is needed and energy use is low [1].
- Refill: needs collection, washing (water, energy) and returns by customers [1].
- Recycling: crushing and melting use energy, but less than making new glass from sand, soda ash and limestone [1].
- Recycling: reduces quarrying and landfill waste [1].
- Recycling: colour sorting and collection add cost [1].
- Compare using data such as energy per bottle per use [1].
- A justified conclusion, such as refill if enough bottles return and the distances are short, otherwise recycling [1].
(maximum **[6]**)

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the transport and distribution that appear at every stage.
- Comparing totals without considering the number of uses.
- Treating an LCA as completely objective.
- Confusing reuse and recycling.
- Not using the data given in an evaluation.
