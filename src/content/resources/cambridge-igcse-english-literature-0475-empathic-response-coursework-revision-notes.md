**These notes cover the empathic option in Component 5 Coursework.** The syllabus states: "One of
the assignments may be an empathic response to a prose or drama text." They condense the
[empathic response study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-english-literature-0475-empathic-response-coursework/);
the portfolio rules in full are in the
[Component 5 coursework revision notes](/resources/igcse-english-literature-component-5-coursework-revision-notes/).

## The rules that apply

| Rule | Detail from the syllabus |
|---|---|
| How many | **One** of the two assignments may be empathic |
| Which texts | A **prose or drama** text |
| Length | 600–1200 words, including quotations; far over or under "may disadvantage" |
| Whole text | "Assignments must show that the candidate has studied the whole text" |
| Title | Must allow "scope for the assessment of all four assessment objectives" |
| Tools | Candidates "must not use online tools which act as writing assistants" |
| Drafting | Teachers give "only … brief summative comments on progress" during drafting |
| Marking | Out of 25 against the same eight levels as other assignments |

## How the levels word empathic work

| Level | AO1 (empathic wording) | AO3 (empathic wording) |
|---|---|---|
| 8 (23–25) | "seamlessly uses well-selected textual detail" | "sustains an entirely convincing voice for the character" |
| 7 (20–22) | "integrates much well-selected textual detail" | "sustains a convincing voice for the character" |
| 6 (17–19) | "supports with careful and relevant textual detail" | "develops a generally authentic voice for the character" |
| 5 (14–16) | "shows some thoroughness in the use of supporting textual detail" | "uses suitable features of expression suitable for the character" |
| 4 (11–13) | "uses some supporting textual detail" | "begins to assume a voice for the character" |

AO2 (critical understanding) and AO4 (personal and evaluative engagement) keep their usual wording.
The two levers are **voice** and **detail**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Empathic response | Writing in a character's voice at a chosen moment in the text |
| Voice | The character's distinctive vocabulary, rhythm, attitude and period |
| Voice sheet | A planning list of the character's words, sentence shapes and blind spots |
| Hindsight error | Letting the character know about events later in the text |
| Integrated detail | Events and phrases from the text recalled naturally in the character's thoughts |
| Gap in the text | A moment the narrative passes over, leaving room for reflection |
| In role | Writing entirely as the character, without stepping outside to explain |

## Planning routine

1. **Choose the moment:** after a discovery, quarrel, decision or loss; at a turning point; in a gap.
2. **Fix what the character knows** at that moment, and what they do not.
3. **Make a voice sheet:** ten typical words or phrases, three sentence shapes, two things they would
   never say.
4. **Select detail:** five or six events, images or phrases the character would recall.
5. **Shape the piece:** reaction → memory → struggle → where the character ends.
6. **Check consistency:** the ending must fit what happens next in the text.

## What a high-level piece shows

- A voice recognisable as **this** character and no other.
- Detail woven into thought, never listed.
- Understanding of the text's turning point shown from inside.
- Blind spots and self-deception that reveal critical insight.
- A clear movement of feeling across the piece.

## Exam traps (coursework traps)

- **Both assignments empathic.** Only one may be.
- **An empathic poem response.** The option is for prose or drama only.
- **First-person plot summary.** Retelling events in "I" form.
- **Hindsight.** Knowing what has not happened yet.
- **Wrong register.** Modern slang in an older or formal text.
- **Stepping out of role** to explain the piece.
- **Inventing major events** that contradict the text.

## Self-test

1. How many empathic assignments can a portfolio contain?
2. On which forms of text may an empathic response be based?
3. What is the word guidance for each assignment, and does it include quotations?
4. What does Level 8 require for AO1 in an empathic task?
5. What does Level 8 require for AO3 in an empathic task?
6. What is a hindsight error? Give an example.
7. What three things belong on a voice sheet?
8. Why is a gap in the text often a good moment to choose?
9. What is the difference between listing detail and integrating it?
10. Should an empathic piece include sentences explaining what it shows? Why?
11. What does the syllabus say about online writing-assistant tools?
12. A character in a text written long ago thinks "This is so not fair." What is wrong, and how
    could it be fixed?

**Answers:** 1. One. 2. Prose or drama. 3. 600–1200 words, including quotations but excluding
references and bibliography. 4. It "seamlessly uses well-selected textual detail". 5. It "sustains an
entirely convincing voice for the character". 6. Letting the character refer to events they could
not yet know, for example mentioning a death that happens two chapters later. 7. Typical words or
phrases; typical sentence shapes; things the character would never say. 8. The narrative passes over
it, so there is room for reflection without contradicting the text. 9. Listing names events one after
another; integrating has the character recall them naturally as part of their thinking. 10. No; the
piece should stay in role, showing understanding through the voice. 11. Candidates "must not use"
them in producing coursework. 12. The modern idiom breaks the voice for that period; replace it with
phrasing the character uses in the text, such as a formal complaint in their own manner of speaking.
