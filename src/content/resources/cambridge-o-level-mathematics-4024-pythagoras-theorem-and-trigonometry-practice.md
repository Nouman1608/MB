These questions cover section 6.4, Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry in 3D, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The methods are explained in the [Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry in 3D study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-pythagoras-theorem-and-trigonometry/), and the [Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry in 3D revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-pythagoras-theorem-and-trigonometry-revision-notes/) summarise them. Give lengths to 3 significant figures and angles to 1 decimal place unless told otherwise. Sketch each solid and each right-angled triangle you use.

## Section A

**1.** A cuboid measures 12 cm by 5 cm by 4 cm. Find the length of its space diagonal, and the angle the space diagonal makes with the base. **[5]**

**2.** A cone has slant height 17 cm and base radius 8 cm. Find its vertical height, and the angle between the slant side and the base. **[3]**

**3.** A cylinder has radius 3 cm and height 8 cm. Find the length of the longest straight line that can be drawn inside it. **[2]**

**4.** A vertical flagpole 12 m high is held by a straight wire from its top to a point on level ground 9 m from its base. Find the length of the wire and the angle it makes with the ground. **[3]**

## Section B

**5.** ABCDEFGH is a cuboid. The base ABCD is horizontal with AB = 9 cm and BC = 12 cm. The vertical edges, including CG, are 8 cm.

**(a)** Find AC and AG.

**(b)** Find the angle between AG and the base ABCD.

**(c)** Find the angle between AG and the face ABFE. **[7]**

**6.** A square-based pyramid has a base of side 8 cm and a vertical height of 10 cm. The apex is directly above the centre of the base.

**(a)** Find the length of a sloping edge.

**(b)** Find the angle between a sloping edge and the base.

**(c)** Find the angle between a sloping face and the base. **[7]**

**7.** A vertical tower TB is 20 m high. A is 15 m due west of B and C is 36 m due south of B. Find the distance AC and the angles of elevation of T from A and from C. **[4]**

**8.** A triangular prism has a base ABC with a right angle at B, AB = 5 cm and BC = 12 cm. The vertical edges are 6 cm long. F is the top corner above C. Find AF and the angle between AF and the base. **[4]**

**9.** A pyramid has a rectangular base 16 cm by 12 cm and a vertical height of 24 cm. The apex is directly above the centre of the base. Find the length of a sloping edge, and the angle that a sloping edge makes with the base. **[3]**

**10.** A room is 6 m long, 4 m wide and 3 m high. A bulb is fixed at the centre of the ceiling, and a switch is at floor level in one corner. Find the straight-line distance from the bulb to the switch. **[3]**

**11.** ABCDEFGH is a cube of side 6 cm, with G directly above C. Find the angle between the space diagonal AG and the edge AB. **[4]**

**12.** Explain why the angle between a line and a plane is found using the projection of the line on the plane. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Base diagonal² = 12² + 5² = 169, so it is 13 cm [1]. Space diagonal² = 169 + 16 = 185 [1], so it is **13.6 cm** [1]. tan θ = 4/13 [1], so θ = **17.1°** [1].

**2.** Height² = 17² − 8² = 225 [1], so height = **15 cm** [1]. cos θ = 8/17, so θ = **61.9°** [1].

**3.** The longest line is a diagonal across the cylinder: √(6² + 8²) [1] = **10 cm** [1].

**4.** Wire² = 12² + 9² = 225 [1], so the wire is **15 m** [1]. tan θ = 12/9, so θ = **53.1°** [1].

**5. (a)** AC² = 9² + 12² = 225, so **AC = 15 cm** [1]. AG² = 225 + 64 = 289, so **AG = 17 cm** [1].

**(b)** The projection of AG on the base is AC [1]. tan(GAC) = 8/15 [1], so the angle is **28.1°** [1].

**(c)** GF is perpendicular to the face ABFE, with GF = BC = 12 cm [1]. AF² = 81 + 64 = 145, so AF = 12.04 cm, and tan(GAF) = 12/12.04, so the angle is **44.9°** [1].

**6. (a)** Half the base diagonal = (8√2) ÷ 2 = 5.657 cm [1]. Edge² = 10² + 5.657² = 132 [1]. Edge = **11.5 cm** [1].

**(b)** tan θ = 10/5.657 [1], so θ = **60.5°** [1].

**(c)** The distance from the centre to the midpoint of a base edge is 4 cm. tan φ = 10/4 [1], so φ = **68.2°** [1].

**7.** AC² = 15² + 36² = 1521, so **AC = 39 m** [1]. Elevation from A: tan θ = 20/15, so **53.1°** [1][1]. Elevation from C: tan φ = 20/36, so **29.1°** [1].

**8.** AC² = 25 + 144 = 169, so AC = 13 cm [1]. AF² = 169 + 36 = 205 [1], so **AF = 14.3 cm** [1]. tan(FAC) = 6/13, so the angle is **24.8°** [1].

**9.** The base diagonal is √(256 + 144) = 20 cm, so half is 10 cm [1]. Edge² = 24² + 10² = 676, so the edge is **26 cm** [1]. The angle is tan⁻¹(24/10) = **67.4°** [1].

**10.** The horizontal distance from the point below the bulb to the corner = half the floor diagonal = 1/2 × √(36 + 16) = 3.606 m [1]. The bulb is 3 m above the floor, so the distance = √(3.606² + 3²) = √22 [1] = **4.69 m** [1].

**11.** BG is a diagonal of the face BCGF, so BG = 6√2 = 8.485 cm [1]. AB is perpendicular to the face BCGF, so angle ABG = 90° [1]. tan(GAB) = 8.485 ÷ 6 = 1.414 [1], so the angle is **54.7°** [1].

**12.** The projection is the shadow of the line on the plane, as seen from directly above [1]. The angle between the line and its shadow is the smallest angle the line makes with any line in the plane, so it is the unique measure of how steeply the line meets the plane [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a triangle that does not contain a right angle.
- Mixing up the base side with half the base diagonal in a pyramid.
- Reading lengths from the 3D sketch.
- Using the angle to an edge instead of the angle to the plane.
- Forgetting the half when finding the distance from the centre of the base to a corner.
- Rounding early.
