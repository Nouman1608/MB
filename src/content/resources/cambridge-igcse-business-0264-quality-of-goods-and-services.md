| Quality control | Explain how checking finds faults, and where checks happen |
| Quality assurance | Explain how a business prevents faults at every stage |
| Advantages and disadvantages of each | Weigh them for the business in the question |
| Recommend and justify | Choose one approach for a given situation and defend it |

## What quality means

Quality means meeting the expectations of customers. It does not mean "the most expensive" or "luxury". A product or service is good quality if it:

- does what it is meant to do (is fit for purpose)
- is free from faults
- is reliable and consistent each time it is bought
- matches what customers were led to expect for the price they paid

A cheap airline offering a basic seat can be good quality if flights leave on time and the seat is clean and safe. A luxury restaurant can be poor quality if the food arrives cold.

### Quality in services

Services are produced and consumed at the same time: a haircut is "made" while the customer sits there. That changes how quality is achieved.

| Feature of services | Effect on quality |
|---|---|
| Produced in front of the customer | A mistake cannot be removed before the customer sees it |
| Depends heavily on staff | Two employees may give very different service |
| Cannot be stored | A service done badly cannot be swapped from stock; it must be put right afterwards |

Because of this, services rely more on preventing problems (training, clear procedures) than on checking at the end. Checks still happen, such as a manager inspecting a hotel room after cleaning, but a check after the customer has been served comes too late for that customer.

### Measuring quality

Businesses use simple measures to see whether quality is improving:

- defect or reject rate: faulty items as a percentage of items checked
- complaints per 100 or per 1,000 customers
- returns and refunds as a percentage of sales
- repeat customers or online review scores

## Worked example: Tollerton Car Hire

Tollerton Car Hire rents out 4,000 cars a month. Last month it received 160 complaints.

```
Complaint rate = 160 / 4,000 x 100 = 4%
```

| Type of complaint | Number |
|---|---|
| Car not cleaned properly | 70 |
| Late handover of car | 50 |
| Mistake on the bill | 40 |

The manager has two options.

**Option 1: quality control.** Employ an inspector to check every car before it is handed over, costing $2,600 a month. This would catch dirty cars, but it would not stop late handovers (an extra check may even slow handovers) or billing mistakes.

**Option 2: quality assurance.** Spend $1,800 a month on training staff, a cleaning checklist that each cleaner signs, and a new booking system that prepares bills automatically. Every employee becomes responsible for quality at their own stage. This targets all three causes of complaints, but it takes time to train staff and depends on them following the procedures.

If Option 2 cut complaints to 1.5% of rentals:

```
New complaints = 1.5 / 100 x 4,000 = 60 a month (down from 160)
```

**Recommendation:** Tollerton should choose quality assurance. Its problems arise at three different stages (cleaning, handover and billing), and an end inspection can only reach one of them. Assurance also costs $800 a month less. The drawback is that results take time, so the manager could add short checks on cleaning while training is under way.

## Choosing between control and assurance

| Situation | Better approach | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Small workshop making a few simple products | Quality control | Cheap and quick to set up; faults are easy to see at the end |
| Food or medicine production | Quality assurance (with checks as well) | A fault could harm customers; prevention matters more than detection |
| Service business such as a hotel or salon | Quality assurance | The service is delivered in front of the customer; faults must be prevented |
| Business with a high reject rate and costly waste | Quality assurance | Preventing faults saves the materials and time wasted on rejects |
| Business with a tight budget and no time to retrain staff | Quality control | Assurance needs training and planning first |
| Business whose brand is built on reliability | Quality assurance | Consistency at every stage protects the reputation |

Two warnings for answers:

- Many businesses use **both**. Even with assurance, some final checks are sensible. If the question asks you to choose one, choose, then mention that the other may still have a role.
- The syllabus says "recommend and justify". The justification must come from the situation described, not from a general list.

## Using quality data to judge suppliers

Quality starts with inputs. Rossiter Bikes tests samples of brake cables from two suppliers.

| Supplier | Cables tested | Faulty | Faulty as a percentage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supplier A | 300 | 6 | 6 / 300 x 100 = 2.0% |
| Supplier B | 250 | 3 | 3 / 250 x 100 = 1.2% |

Supplier B has a lower fault rate. For a safety part like a brake cable, Rossiter should prefer Supplier B even if it charges a little more. Choosing and checking suppliers in this way is part of quality assurance, because it prevents faulty parts entering production.

## Annotated model answer

Question: *Recommend and justify whether a children's toy maker should use quality control or quality assurance.*

Model answer: The toy maker should use quality assurance. A fault in a children's toy, such as a small part that breaks off, could injure a child, which would damage the business's reputation and could lead to legal action. Quality assurance prevents faults at every stage, for example by checking that suppliers use safe materials and training workers to follow safety procedures, so fewer dangerous toys are made in the first place. Quality control only finds faults at the end and might miss some if only a sample is checked. Assurance costs more to set up, but for toys the risk of a fault reaching a child is too serious to rely on end checks alone.

Why it earns credit: a clear choice, a reason tied to the product (children's safety), a comparison with the other approach, a drawback, and a final justification.

## Exam technique

The command words in the 0264 syllabus include **calculate**, **define**, **identify**, **state**, **outline**, **explain**, **consider** and **justify**.

- **Define:** quality control is *checking* to find faults; quality assurance is *preventing* faults at every stage.
- **Calculate:** fault or complaint rates as percentages; show the division.
- **Justify:** make a choice and support it with evidence from the case, including at least one drawback of your choice.

## Common errors

- Defining quality as "expensive" or "luxury".
- Saying quality control prevents faults. It finds them after they happen.
- Saying quality assurance means no checks at all.
- Recommending an approach with reasons that would apply to any business.
- Comparing numbers of faults from samples of different sizes without turning them into percentages.
