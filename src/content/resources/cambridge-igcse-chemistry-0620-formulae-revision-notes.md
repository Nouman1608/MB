Condensed recall for **3.1 Formulae**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620. For explanations and
worked examples, use the [Formulae study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-formulae/).

> **Tier note:** items marked *(Supplement)* are for Extended candidates only.

## Definitions

- **Molecular formula:** the number and type of different atoms in one molecule.
- *(Supplement)* **Empirical formula:** the simplest whole number ratio of the different
  atoms or ions in a compound.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Formula | Symbols and numbers showing the atoms (or ratio of ions) in a substance |
| Subscript | Small number after a symbol or bracket; never changed when balancing |
| Coefficient | Large number in front of a formula; changed to balance |
| State symbol | (s), (l), (g) or (aq) |
| Aqueous | Dissolved in water |
| Compound ion | Ion made of more than one atom, e.g. SO₄²⁻ |
| Spectator ion *(Supplement)* | Ion present but unchanged; left out of an ionic equation |
| Ionic equation *(Supplement)* | Equation showing only the particles that react |

## Formulae to know by heart

**Diatomic elements:** H₂ N₂ O₂ F₂ Cl₂ Br₂ I₂

**Common compounds:** H₂O · CO₂ · CO · CH₄ · NH₃ · HCl · H₂SO₄ · HNO₃ · NaOH · NaCl ·
CaCO₃ · CaO · CuSO₄ · SO₂

**Ion charges (Supplement for building ionic formulae)**

```
1+  Na⁺ K⁺ Li⁺ Ag⁺ NH4⁺        1−  Cl⁻ Br⁻ I⁻ F⁻ OH⁻ NO3⁻
2+  Mg²⁺ Ca²⁺ Zn²⁺ Cu²⁺ Fe²⁺     2−  O²⁻ S²⁻ CO3²⁻ SO4²⁻
3+  Al³⁺ Fe³⁺                    3−  N³⁻
```

## Building an ionic formula (Supplement)

1. Write both ions with charges.
2. Find the lowest common multiple of the charges.
3. Take enough of each ion to reach it.
4. Brackets round a compound ion if you need more than one of it.

Results to check yourself against: MgO · Na₂O · AlCl₃ · Al₂O₃ · Ca(OH)₂ · Mg(NO₃)₂ ·
(NH₄)₂CO₃ · Fe₂(SO₄)₃ · K₂SO₄.

## Equation checklist

1. Words → correct formulae (diatomic gases!).
2. Balance with coefficients only.
3. Add state symbols: water (l); solution (aq); precipitate (s); gas (g).
4. Count every atom on both sides — a bracket multiplies everything inside.

## Ionic equations in four steps (Supplement)

1. Balanced equation with states.
2. Split **aqueous ionic** substances into ions.
3. Cancel spectator ions.
4. Check atoms **and** total charge balance.

**Patterns:**

```
precipitation:    Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s)
displacement:     Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s)
neutralisation:   H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H2O(l)
acid + carbonate: 2H⁺(aq) + CO3²⁻(aq) → H2O(l) + CO2(g)   (soluble carbonate)
```

## Molecular to empirical (Supplement)

Divide every subscript by the largest number that goes into all of them:

| Molecular | Divide by | Empirical |
|---|---|---|
| C₂H₆ | 2 | CH₃ |
| C₃H₆ | 3 | CH₂ |
| C₂H₄O₂ | 2 | CH₂O |
| N₂O₄ | 2 | NO₂ |
| C₃H₈ | 1 | C₃H₈ (cannot simplify) |

## Choosing state symbols

| Substance in the reaction | State symbol |
|---|---|
| A metal or a solid carbonate added to acid | (s) |
| Dilute acid, alkali or salt solution | (aq) |
| Water formed in neutralisation | (l) |
| Hydrogen, oxygen, carbon dioxide, chlorine | (g) |
| A precipitate | (s) |
| Molten lead(II) bromide in electrolysis | (l) |
| Steam above 100 °C | (g) |

## Words to equation: two quick runs

**Copper(II) carbonate is heated and forms copper(II) oxide and carbon dioxide.**
CuCO₃(s) → CuO(s) + CO₂(g) — already balanced: Cu 1, C 1, O 3 on each side.

**Sodium hydroxide solution neutralises hydrochloric acid.**
NaOH(aq) + HCl(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l) — the salt stays dissolved, so (aq).

## Balancing walk-through

Aluminium burns in oxygen to form aluminium oxide.

```
Al + O2 → Al2O3          O: 2 left, 3 right → LCM 6
Al + 3O2 → 2Al2O3        O: 6 = 6; Al: 1 left, 4 right
4Al + 3O2 → 2Al2O3       Al 4 = 4, O 6 = 6 ✓
```

Fixing oxygen first with the lowest common multiple, then the metal, avoids going round in circles.

## Exam traps

- Writing "Cl" or "O" for the elements in an equation instead of Cl₂ and O₂.
- Balancing by editing subscripts — changes the substance.
- Mg(OH)₂ means one Mg, two O, two H. Forgetting the bracket multiplies only the H.
- Brackets round a single compound ion are wrong: NaOH, not Na(OH).
- (aq) means dissolved in water — water itself is (l).
- In ionic equations, insoluble solids and covalent molecules stay whole.
- A molecular formula is *not* simplified: hydrogen peroxide is H₂O₂, even though its
  empirical formula is HO.
- Capitals matter: Co (cobalt) and CO (carbon monoxide) are different substances.

## Self-test

1. Define molecular formula.
2. *(Supplement)* Define empirical formula.
3. *(Supplement)* Give the empirical formula of C₆H₁₂.
4. A model shows one sulfur atom joined to two oxygen atoms. Write the formula.
5. Write the formula for bromine as it appears in an equation.
6. *(Supplement)* Deduce the formula of calcium nitrate.
7. *(Supplement)* Deduce the formula of ammonium chloride.
8. Balance: Mg + O₂ → MgO.
9. Write a balanced equation with state symbols for magnesium reacting with dilute
   sulfuric acid to form magnesium sulfate solution and hydrogen.
10. *(Supplement)* Write the ionic equation for silver nitrate solution reacting with sodium
    bromide solution to form a cream precipitate.
11. *(Supplement)* Name the spectator ions in: Zn(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + Cu(s).
12. *(Supplement)* How many oxygen atoms are shown in Fe₂(SO₄)₃?

**Answers**

1. The number and type of different atoms in one molecule.
2. The simplest whole number ratio of the different atoms or ions in a compound.
3. CH₂.
4. SO₂.
5. Br₂.
6. Ca²⁺ and NO₃⁻ → Ca(NO₃)₂.
7. NH₄⁺ and Cl⁻ → NH₄Cl.
8. 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO.
9. Mg(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → MgSO₄(aq) + H₂(g).
10. Ag⁺(aq) + Br⁻(aq) → AgBr(s).
11. Sulfate ions, SO₄²⁻.
12. Twelve (3 × 4).
