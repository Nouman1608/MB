These questions cover sub-topic **7.4 Chemical digestion** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Statements 1 to 5 are Core. Questions and parts on statements 6 to 8 (maltase, pepsin, trypsin and bile as a neutraliser) are labelled **(Extended)**. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. Experimental results are invented for practice.

## Answers

**1.** The breakdown of large insoluble molecules [1] into small soluble molecules [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**2.** **B** (lipase) [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** Amino acids [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**4.** Secreted by the pancreas [1]; acts in the small intestine (duodenum) [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** Kills harmful microorganisms in food [1]; provides an acidic pH for optimum enzyme activity [1]. Total: 2 marks.

*Examiner insight:* "Digests food" and "breaks down protein" are not functions of the acid and score zero.

**6.** *(Extended)* Maltase [1]; on the membranes of the epithelium lining the small intestine [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**7.** Amylase: acts in the mouth [1]; simple reducing sugars [1]. Protease: secreted by the stomach [1]; amino acids [1]. Lipase: acts in the small intestine [1]; fatty acids and glycerol [1]. Total: 6 marks.

*Examiner insight:* Lipase needs both products; "fatty acids" alone does not score.

**8. (a)** 120 s [1].

**(b)** Add Benedict's solution to a sample of the final mixture [1] and heat in a water bath [1]; a colour change from blue to green, yellow, orange or brick-red shows a reducing sugar [1].

**(c)** Repeat with boiled (denatured) amylase or with water in place of amylase; the iodine stays blue-black [1].

**(d)** Starch molecules are large and insoluble and cannot be absorbed [1]; digestion produces small soluble sugars that can be absorbed into the blood [1]. Total: 7 marks.

**9. (a)** Any three from: less starch digested, because pancreatic amylase is missing; less protein digested, because pancreatic protease is missing; less fat digested, because lipase is missing; fewer small soluble molecules available for absorption [3].

**(b)** Amylase from the salivary glands starts digesting starch in the mouth [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**10.** *(Extended)* Amylase from the salivary glands begins breaking starch down in the mouth [1]. Amylase from the pancreas continues in the small intestine [1]. Amylase breaks starch down to maltose [1]. Maltase on the membranes of the epithelium lining the small intestine breaks maltose down to glucose [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**11.** *(Extended)* **(a)** F is pepsin, because its optimum is pH 2, which is acidic like the stomach [1] [1]. G is trypsin, because its optimum is pH 8, which is alkaline like the small intestine [1] [1].

**(b)** At pH 7 the active site of F has changed shape (it is denatured), so the protein substrate no longer fits [1].

**(c)** Activity rises from 40 at pH 6 to a maximum of 100 at pH 8 [1], then falls to 45 at pH 10 [1]. Total: 7 marks.

**12.** *(Extended)* The stomach secretes hydrochloric acid in gastric juice [1], giving acidic conditions in which pepsin works best [1]. The acidic mixture then passes into the duodenum [1]. Bile, which is alkaline, neutralises it [1], giving alkaline conditions [1] in which trypsin works best [1]. Total: 6 marks.

*Examiner insight:* The bile mark needs "neutralises" or "alkaline"; "bile helps digestion" is too vague.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "glucose" for the product of amylase at Core; the Core answer is simple reducing sugars.
- Giving lipase one product instead of two.
- Saying pancreatic enzymes act in the pancreas.
- Giving hydrochloric acid a digesting role.
- Describing a control as "doing it again" rather than removing or denaturing the enzyme.
- (Extended) Placing maltase in the stomach, or pepsin in the small intestine.
- (Extended) Forgetting that bile neutralises the acidic mixture entering the duodenum.
