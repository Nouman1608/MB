This set practises section 3.8.3 Production of ammonia and sulfuric acid (statements a to d) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202) specification, Version 6.2, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Section A is recall of the two processes; Section B asks for explanations, evaluations and calculations. The course is untiered.

Use these relative atomic masses: H = 1, N = 14, O = 16, S = 32. One mole of any gas occupies 24 dm³ at room temperature and pressure.

## Section A

**1.** State the source of the nitrogen used in the Haber process. **[1]**

**2.** Which row gives the conditions used in the Haber process?

A: 450 °C, 1 atmosphere, vanadium(V) oxide

B: 450 °C, 200 atmospheres, iron

C: 200 °C, 450 atmospheres, iron

D: 200 °C, 1 atmosphere, vanadium(V) oxide

**[1]**

**3.** Write the balanced symbol equation for the Haber process, including the correct arrow. **[2]**

**4.** Name the substance burned in Stage 1 of the contact process and the product formed. **[2]**

**5.** State the catalyst, temperature and pressure used in Stage 2 of the contact process. **[3]**

**6.** Write the equation for Stage 3 of the contact process. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Describe how ammonia is manufactured in the Haber process, from the raw materials to the removal of the product. **[6]**

**8.** The gas leaving the Haber reactor contains ammonia, nitrogen and hydrogen.

**(a)** Explain how the ammonia is separated from the other gases. **[2]**

**(b)** In one pass through the reactor, 20% of the nitrogen reacts. Calculate the mass of ammonia formed in one pass when 1000 kg of nitrogen enters the reactor. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the overall conversion of nitrogen into ammonia is much higher than 20%. **[1]**

**9.** Explain the global need for fertilisers made from ammonia. **[4]**

**10.** Ammonium nitrate, NH₄NO₃, is made by reacting ammonia with nitric acid.

**(a)** Write a balanced equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of ammonia needed to make 400 tonnes of ammonium nitrate. **[3]**

**11.** A student suggests that Stage 2 of the contact process should be run at 200 °C and 200 atmospheres "to get the highest possible yield of sulfur trioxide". The forward reaction is exothermic.

**(a)** Explain why these conditions would increase the yield. **[2]**

**(b)** Evaluate the student's suggestion, explaining why the actual conditions are used instead. **[4]**

**12.** A contact process plant uses 3.2 tonnes of sulfur.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum mass of sulfur trioxide that can be made. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of sulfuric acid that can be made. **[1]**

**(c)** In a laboratory model of Stage 2, 240 dm³ of sulfur dioxide reacts completely with oxygen at room temperature and pressure. Calculate the volume of oxygen needed and the mass of sulfur trioxide formed. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The air [1].

**2.** B [1].

**3.** N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ balanced [1]; reversible arrow ⇌ [1].

**4.** Sulfur [1]; sulfur dioxide [1].

**5.** Vanadium(V) oxide, V₂O₅ [1]; about 450 °C [1]; atmospheric pressure [1].

**6.** H₂O + SO₃ → H₂SO₄ [1].

**7.** Any six points, one mark each:

- Nitrogen is obtained from the air.
- Hydrogen is obtained from natural gas (or other sources).
- The gases are purified.
- They are passed over an iron catalyst.
- At a temperature of about 450 °C.
- At a pressure of about 200 atmospheres.
- The reaction is reversible, so only some nitrogen and hydrogen react.
- The mixture is cooled so that ammonia liquefies and is removed.
- Unreacted nitrogen and hydrogen are recycled.

**8. (a)** The mixture is cooled [1]; ammonia liquefies (condenses) while nitrogen and hydrogen remain as gases, so the liquid is removed [1].

**(b)** Mass of N₂ reacting = 20% of 1000 = 200 kg [1]; mass ratio N₂ : NH₃ = 28 : 34 [1]; ammonia = 200 × 34 ÷ 28 = **243 kg** [1].

**(c)** Unreacted nitrogen and hydrogen are recycled through the reactor many times [1].

**9.**

- The world population is increasing, so more food is needed [1].
- Farmland is limited, so more food must be produced from each area of land [1].
- Plants need nitrogen compounds to grow, and crops remove them from the soil [1].
- Fertilisers replace the nitrogen compounds and so maximise crop yields [1].

**10. (a)** NH₃ + HNO₃ → NH₄NO₃ [1].

**(b)** Mᵣ NH₄NO₃ = 80; Mᵣ NH₃ = 17 [1]; ratio 1 : 1, so 17 parts of NH₃ make 80 parts of NH₄NO₃ [1]; 400 × 17 ÷ 80 = **85 tonnes** [1].

**11. (a)** The forward reaction is exothermic, so a lower temperature favours sulfur trioxide [1]; there are 3 gas molecules on the left and 2 on the right, so high pressure favours sulfur trioxide [1].

**(b)**

- At 200 °C the rate would be too slow, even with a catalyst [1].
- About 450 °C is a compromise that gives a good yield quickly [1].
- The yield of sulfur trioxide at atmospheric pressure is already high [1].
- So the extra cost of high-pressure equipment and compressing gases (and the safety risk) is not justified; the suggestion is not sensible [1].

**12. (a)** Ratio S : SO₃ = 1 : 1; Mᵣ SO₃ = 80 [1]; 3.2 × 80 ÷ 32 = **8.0 tonnes** [1].

**(b)** 3.2 × 98 ÷ 32 = **9.8 tonnes** [1].

**(c)** Oxygen = 240 ÷ 2 = **120 dm³** [1]; moles SO₃ = 240 ÷ 24 = 10 mol [1]; mass = 10 × 80 = **800 g** [1].

*Examiner insight:* In part (c) the volume of SO₃ equals the volume of SO₂ (2 : 2), so moles of SO₃ come straight from 240 dm³.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping the two catalysts or the two pressures.
- Missing cooling or recycling in a Haber description.
- Saying a catalyst improves the yield.
- Fertiliser answers that do not mention food yields or limited farmland.
- In calculations, using the 2 : 1 ratio of SO₂ : O₂ when the question asks about SO₂ and SO₃.
