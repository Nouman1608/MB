These notes cover section 3.2.3.2, Population and communication, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Geography (9230) specification, Version 4.3. Students study either Water and energy resources or Population and communication in Section C. For full explanations, read the [population and communication study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-population-and-communication/).

## Specification checklist

- **Population change** over time; **causes** of growth.
- **Impacts** of population change; **strategies** to reduce the birth rate (contrasting approaches).
- **International migration:** causes and impacts.
- **Ocean shipping, ports and airports:** development, patterns, advantages; **airport expansion case study**.
- **ICT:** developments, cause and pattern; role in NEE growth.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Birth rate | Births per 1 000 people per year |
| Fertility rate | Average children per woman |
| Natural increase | Birth rate minus death rate |
| Dependency | Young and old people relying on workers |
| Refugee | Person forced to flee war or persecution |
| Asylum seeker | Person who has applied for protection and awaits a decision |
| Economic migrant | Person moving to find work |
| Remittances | Money migrants send home |
| Containerisation | Shipping goods in standard steel boxes |
| Digital divide | Gap between those with and without ICT access |

## World population

- 1 billion about 1800; 2 billion late 1920s; 3 billion about 1960; 7 billion 2011; 8 billion 2022.
- Growth is slowing; peak projected around 10 billion later this century.
- Fastest growth in LICs, especially sub-Saharan Africa and parts of South Asia.

## Causes of rapid growth

| Cause | How it increases growth |
|---|---|
| Improved medical care | Fewer deaths, especially of children |
| Improved sanitation | Less disease, so death rates fall |
| Aid | Funds clinics, vaccines, water |
| Religion | Some beliefs encourage large families |
| Status of women | Less education means early marriage and more children |
| Role of children | Farm work and care in old age; high infant mortality |
| Age structure | Many women of childbearing age, so births continue |
| Education | Less education means more children |

Death rates fall first, birth rates fall later, so growth is rapid in between.

## Impacts of population change

- **Resource balance:** overpopulation (shortages) or underpopulation (under-use).
- **Economic:** labour and markets, but needs jobs and schools.
- **Age structure:** dependency of children or ageing population.
- **Food supply:** more demand; land over-used.
- **Environment:** deforestation, soil erosion, pollution, water stress.

## Birth-rate strategies

| | China (one-child policy, 1979 to 2015) | Thailand (voluntary) |
|---|---|---|
| Method | Legal limits, fines and rewards | Contraception, health workers, education, campaigns |
| Result | Fast fall in growth | Fertility from about six children per woman in the 1960s to about two by the 1990s |
| Problems | Ageing population, fewer women than men, rights concerns | Slower to take effect; needs funding and trust |

Other approaches: educate girls, improve child health, give women jobs.

## International migration

| Type | Cause | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Refugee | War, persecution | Syria from 2011 |
| Asylum seeker | Seeking protection | Syrians in Germany and Sweden, 2015 |
| Economic migrant | Jobs, higher income | South Asia to Gulf states; Mexico to USA; Poland to UK after 2004 |

**Push** factors: war, poverty, no jobs. **Pull** factors: safety, jobs, wages.

| Impact | Origin country | Destination |
|---|---|---|
| Demographic | Young adults leave | Younger population, more workers |
| Economic | Remittances; brain drain | Labour supply; costs for services |
| Social | Families split | Diversity; pressure on housing; tension |
| Political | Reliance on remittances | Immigration and integration debates |

## Ports and airports

- **Development:** containerisation, very large ships, deep-water ports, Panama Canal widened 2016, air hubs.
- **Pattern:** busiest container ports in East Asia (Shanghai, Singapore); Rotterdam in Europe; air hubs include Dubai, Heathrow and Atlanta.
- **Advantages:** cheaper goods movement (manufacturing and supply chains), more trade, more tourism, jobs, investment.

### Airport expansion: Heathrow (check the current position)

| Benefits | Challenges |
|---|---|
| More flights and links to growing markets | Noise |
| Jobs and growth | Air pollution and carbon emissions |
| Competes with other hubs | Homes and land lost; road traffic |
| Reduced delays | Cost; opposition; legal challenges |

## ICT

- **Developments:** internet, mobile phones, satellites, fibre-optic cables, cloud, social media.
- **Cause:** falling costs, technology, TNC investment, government policy.
- **Pattern:** highest access in HICs, lowest in LICs, particularly rural areas (digital divide); growing fastest in NEEs and LICs through mobile phones.
- **NEE growth (India):** call centres and outsourcing in Bangalore, Hyderabad, Gurgaon; TNC investment; software exports; online trade and tourism.
- **Problems:** shift work, competition from other countries, dependence on foreign firms, uneven access.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Assess the impacts of international migration on the country that migrants leave and the country they move to. [6 marks]

**Model answer:** "Migrants who leave a country such as Poland or Syria reduce the number of young working adults. This can lead to labour shortages and a brain drain, although remittances can be a valuable source of income for families. In the destination country, migrants fill jobs, pay taxes and bring cultural variety, so the population becomes younger and more diverse. However, they can also put pressure on housing, schools and health services, and may cause tension or debate about immigration. Overall, the impacts are mixed and depend on the number of migrants, their skills and how well they are integrated."

How it scores: impacts for both countries, at least two types of impact (demographic, economic, social, political), and a final judgement.

## Exam traps

- Define refugee, asylum seeker and economic migrant precisely.
- Population growth is not always a problem; link to resources and jobs.
- Evaluate China's policy: results **and** problems.
- Airport case study: name the airport and give facts for both sides.
- ICT: describe both the pattern (digital divide) and the cause.
- Give examples with names and dates.
- Use push and pull in migration answers.

## Self-test

1. Define natural increase.
2. Give **three** causes of rapid population growth in LICs.
3. Explain why a young age structure leads to continued growth.
4. Give **one** problem caused by China's one-child policy.
5. Why might voluntary programmes work well?
6. What is the difference between a refugee and an economic migrant?
7. Give **two** impacts of migration on the origin country.
8. What is containerisation?
9. Give **two** challenges of airport expansion.
10. Give **two** reasons why India has become a centre for call centres.

**Answers**

1. Birth rate minus death rate.
2. Any three: improved medical care; improved sanitation; aid; religion; status of women; role of children; education.
3. There are many young women of childbearing age, so total births keep rising even if each woman has fewer children.
4. Ageing population, or fewer women than men, or human rights concerns.
5. They change attitudes and give people choices, so lower birth rates last without resentment, as in Thailand.
6. A refugee is forced to flee conflict or persecution; an economic migrant chooses to move to find work.
7. Any two: loss of young workers; brain drain; remittances; separated families.
8. Shipping goods in standard-size steel containers that can be moved between ships, trains and lorries.
9. Any two: noise; air pollution and emissions; loss of homes and land; traffic; cost and opposition.
10. Large English-speaking workforce; lower wages than HICs; investment by TNCs; good telecommunications.
