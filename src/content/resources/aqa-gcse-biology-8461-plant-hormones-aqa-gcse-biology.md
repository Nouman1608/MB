## 4.5.4.1 Control and coordination

Plants produce hormones to **coordinate and control growth** and their responses to **light (phototropism)** and
**gravity (gravitropism, also called geotropism)**. Plants cannot move to food or light, so growing in the right
direction is how they respond: shoots grow towards light to photosynthesise, and roots grow down into the soil
for water, mineral ions and anchorage.

The key statement is that **unequal distributions of auxin cause unequal growth rates in plant roots and
shoots**. Learn the rule for each organ:

| Organ | Effect of auxin | Result when auxin collects on one side |
|---|---|---|
| Shoot | Stimulates cell elongation (growth) | That side grows faster, so the shoot bends away from it |
| Root | Inhibits growth | That side grows more slowly, so the root bends towards it |

### Phototropism in a shoot

1. Light shines on the shoot from one side.
2. Auxin moves to the **shaded side** of the shoot tip.
3. Cells on the shaded side **elongate faster** than cells on the lit side.
4. The shoot **bends towards the light**.

### Gravitropism in a shoot

1. A shoot is placed on its side.
2. Auxin collects on the **lower side**.
3. In a shoot, auxin stimulates growth, so the lower side grows faster.
4. The shoot **bends upwards**, against gravity.

### Gravitropism in a root

1. A root is placed on its side.
2. Auxin collects on the **lower side**.
3. In a root, auxin **inhibits** growth, so the lower side grows more slowly than the upper side.
4. The root **bends downwards**, with gravity.

Notice that auxin collects on the lower side in both shoot and root. The opposite responses come from the
**opposite effects** of auxin in the two organs.

**Annotated model answer**

*Question: A seedling is left on its side in the dark. Explain why its root grows downwards. [3]*

> Auxin collects on the lower side of the root because of gravity [1]. In roots, auxin inhibits growth, so the
> cells on the lower side grow more slowly [1]. The upper side grows faster, so the root bends downwards [1].

Darkness is there to rule out light as the cause. Answers that say "auxin makes the lower side grow faster" have
used the shoot rule for a root, and lose the second and third marks.

### Gibberellins and ethene (Higher tier only)

- **Gibberellins** are important in **initiating seed germination**.
- **Ethene** controls **cell division** and the **ripening of fruits**.

You do not need to know the mechanisms of how gibberellins and ethene work.

## Required practical 8

The specification requires you to **investigate the effect of light or gravity on the growth of newly germinated
seedlings**, and to record results both as **length measurements** and as **careful, labelled biological
drawings**.

**Light version.** Grow seedlings such as cress in three dishes: one in a box with a slit letting light in from
one side, one in all-round light, and one in darkness. After a few days, measure the shoots and draw them.

**Gravity version.** Germinate seeds such as beans on damp cotton wool in a dish, fix the dish so that it stands
on its edge, and keep it in the dark. Turn some dishes through 90 degrees and observe the roots and shoots after
a few days.

| Feature | Light version |
|---|---|
| Independent variable | Direction of light (one side, all round, none) |
| Dependent variable | Shoot length and direction of growth |
| Control variables | Type and age of seedling, temperature, volume of water, number of seeds |

**Worked example: analysing results** (invented data). Shoot lengths after four days:

| Condition | Mean shoot length (mm) | Direction of growth |
|---|---|---|
| Light from one side | 24 | Bent towards the light |
| All-round light | 22 | Straight up |
| Darkness | 38 | Straight up; pale and thin |

The one-sided light shoots bend because auxin moved to the shaded side. Shoots in darkness are longest because
they elongate rapidly when there is no light. A good **biological drawing** uses clear single lines, no shading,
labels with ruled lines, and shows the angle of bending accurately.

## 4.5.4.2 Use of plant hormones (Higher tier only)

Plant growth hormones are used in **agriculture and horticulture**.

| Hormone | Use | How it helps |
|---|---|---|
| Auxins | Weed killers | Cause targeted weeds to grow too fast and die, leaving the crop |
| Auxins | Rooting powders | Stimulate roots to grow on cuttings, so many identical plants are produced |
| Auxins | Promoting growth in tissue culture | Help small groups of cells grow into new plants |
| Ethene | Controlling ripening in the food industry | Fruit picked unripe is stored and transported without damage, then ripened with ethene when needed |
| Gibberellins | Ending seed dormancy | Seeds germinate when the grower wants |
| Gibberellins | Promoting flowering | Plants flower at a chosen time |
| Gibberellins | Increasing fruit size | Larger fruit for sale |

**Biodiversity.** The specification asks you to understand how the everyday use of hormones as weedkillers affects
biodiversity. Killing weeds removes wild plant species from fields and gardens, and with them the food and
shelter they provide for insects and other animals, so the variety of species falls.

## Common mistakes

- Saying light "destroys" auxin on the lit side. At GCSE, auxin **moves to the shaded side**.
- Writing that auxin makes roots grow faster. In roots it **inhibits** growth.
- Saying the shoot "grows towards the auxin". The side with more auxin grows faster, so the shoot bends away from
  that side.
- Writing that ethene makes fruit "grow". It controls **ripening** (and cell division).
- Using gibberellins as rooting powder; rooting powders contain auxins.
- Describing a practical with no control: the all-round light or no-light dish is needed for comparison.

## Exam technique for AQA command words

- **Explain** a tropism: say where auxin collects, what it does in that organ, and which way the organ bends.
- **Describe** a use (Higher tier only): name the hormone and the effect.
- **Evaluate** using weedkillers: weigh crop yield against effects on biodiversity.
- **Draw** (practical 8): single clear lines, no shading, ruled label lines.
