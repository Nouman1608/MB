This page is a guide to AQA GCSE Biology (8461) past papers for the June 2024, June 2025 and June 2026 series. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and, where AQA has published its reports on the examination, a short account of what the examiners saw, written by us. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

AQA publishes question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the assessment resources page for the subject. For this GCSE there is a separate report for each paper and each tier.

Use the three documents together. Sit the paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which tells you what each mark needs. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. Our notes for each series below are a quick way into that report. If a paper exposes a weak topic, go back to the matching study guide, for example [cell structure](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-cell-structure/), [cell division](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-cell-division/) or [bioenergetics](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-bioenergetics/).

Check which specification a paper was set on. GCSE Biology 8461 is a linear, tiered qualification first taught in September 2016 and first examined in June 2018, so every series on this page was set on the same specification. Each tier sits its own Paper 1 and Paper 2, each 1 hour 45 minutes and 100 marks, worth 50 per cent of the GCSE. Paper 1 covers topics 1 to 4 (cell biology; organisation; infection and response; bioenergetics) and Paper 2 covers topics 5 to 7 (homeostasis and response; inheritance, variation and evolution; ecology). The ten required practicals are examined within the written papers.

## June 2025

The June 2025 reports say candidates showed good knowledge on both tiers but lost marks through imprecise wording and unfamiliar contexts. On Paper 1, the new contexts of a liver transplant and monoclonal antibodies were hard, and the required practicals on osmosis, amylase and pondweed were known, but unusual set-ups (for example, a time taken for a set number of bubbles, which reverses the usual rate pattern) led some candidates to ignore what they knew about rates. On Paper 2, the mean mark rose slightly and questions were judged comparable with earlier years, but on Foundation Tier a large number of extended-response questions were not attempted. Both papers noted unclear use of "it", "low" used where "lower" or "lowest" was needed, and incorrect formulae for carbon dioxide.

**Where marks were lost**

- Papers 8461/1F and 1H: energy "produced" instead of "released" in respiration, the claim that water in osmosis moves down a concentration gradient, and breathing confused with respiration. The idea that the liver makes lipase also persisted.
- Papers 8461/1F and 1H: evaluation answers on a practical that improved the method but did not reach a judgement, and rate answers that said enzymes denature at low temperatures. Some candidates gave the dependent variable when another variable was asked for, and the method for a sterile agar plate was less well known.
- Papers 8461/1F and 1H: maths slips, including rate and its unit, converting units (cm or mm to micrometres), rounding values below 1, joining every point on a graph instead of a line of best fit, and not recognising a scatter graph.
- Papers 8461/1F and 1H: vaccination described for one person rather than a population, antitoxins confused with antibiotics, and heart and blood vessel questions that mixed up the pulmonary vein and aorta.
- Paper 8461/1H: monoclonal antibodies, where answers named white blood cells instead of lymphocytes or cancer cells instead of tumour cells, and injected the mouse with a pathogen as in vaccination.
- Papers 8461/2F and 2H: command words ignored in extended answers, illegible or very small handwriting, and conversion errors in calculations. Fossil formation answers said the whole body decayed, and sexual and asexual reproduction were compared without comparative language.
- Papers 8461/2F and 2H: tropism answers that described the uneven spread of auxin when the question asked about the response; gene and allele confused, an enzyme said to be denatured where the question needed a reason why it stopped working, and genetic engineering described with an unclear "it".

## June 2024

The June 2024 reports say mathematical skills were generally good on Paper 1, including drawing a bar graph and a hard magnification question, but converting units was a common problem. A recurring weakness was not following command words: an explanation was given where a description was asked for, or the other way round. Paper 2 showed an improvement on the year before on Higher Tier and a small drop on Foundation Tier, and examiners noted that candidates often missed marks by not using the information in the question stem.

**Where marks were lost**

- Papers 8461/1F and 1H: digestive enzymes, which were not answered well on either tier; food tests confused with one another; osmosis understanding weak in places; and bacterial resistance not explained clearly.
- Papers 8461/1F and 1H: writing a risk assessment for a required practical, naming apparatus correctly, and extra detail added in answers on photosynthesis in leaves and limiting factors that the question did not ask for.
- Papers 8461/1F and 1H: converting between units, mixing up numerator and denominator in a fraction even when the equation was given, and saying a value was "lower" when "lowest" was needed in a comparison.
- Papers 8461/2F and 2H: a pyramid of biomass many candidates could not complete, confusion between global warming, the greenhouse effect and climate change, and population graph answers that read the table by row instead of column.
- Papers 8461/2F and 2H: answers on a root growth apparatus that wrongly brought in gravity, auxin on the wrong side of a root, and hormone questions that named a gland instead of the hormone.
- Papers 8461/2F and 2H: gene and allele confused, "stronger" or "overpowering" alleles, a calculation that used the mass of one chicken instead of 1000, and unanswered questions on Foundation Tier.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find AQA GCSE Biology 8461 past papers?**
AQA publishes papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the assessment resources page for 8461, and offers a past paper finder on its website.

**Which 8461 papers should I sit when practising a series?**
Sit the two papers for your tier: Paper 1 and Paper 2, Foundation or Higher. Each is 1 hour 45 minutes and 100 marks. Mark them with the official mark scheme.

**Why are there no grade 9 to 6 boundaries for Foundation Tier?**
In the board's documents the Foundation Tier has boundaries for grades 5 to 1 and the Higher Tier for grades 9 to 3, so the tables show a dash where a tier has none.

**Why is there no examiner report summary for June 2026?**
When we checked, AQA's assessment resources page for 8461 listed reports only up to June 2025. We only summarise reports we have read, so June 2026 will be added once the board publishes them.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
