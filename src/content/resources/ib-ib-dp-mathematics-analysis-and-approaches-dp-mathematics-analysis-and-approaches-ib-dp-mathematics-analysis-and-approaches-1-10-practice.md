This set covers **syllabus section 1.10** of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (first assessment 2021): counting principles, permutations and combinations, and the binomial theorem for fractional and negative exponents. Every question is **HL only**. Questions are labelled "(calculator-free)" for Paper 1 style or "(calculator allowed)" for Paper 2 and 3 style.

Study the methods first in the [section 1.10 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-1-10/) and the [section 1.10 revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-1-10-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (calculator-free) Evaluate:

**(a)** 6P2 **[1]**
**(b)** 10C7 **[1]**

**2.** (calculator-free) A password consists of two different letters of the alphabet followed by three digits, where digits may repeat. Find the number of possible passwords. **[2]**

**3.** (calculator-free) A president, a secretary and a treasurer are to be chosen from the 12 members of a club. No member may hold more than one post. Find the number of ways this can be done. **[2]**

**4.** (calculator-free)

**(a)** Expand (1 − x)^(−1/2) in ascending powers of x up to and including the term in x². **[3]**
**(b)** State the values of x for which the expansion is valid. **[1]**

**5.** (calculator-free)

**(a)** Expand (4 + x)^(−1/2) in ascending powers of x up to and including the term in x². **[4]**
**(b)** State the values of x for which the expansion is valid. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** (calculator-free) Seven people, including Ana and Ben, sit in a row of seven seats.

**(a)** Find the number of possible seating arrangements. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the number of arrangements in which Ana and Ben sit next to each other. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the number of arrangements in which Ana sits at one end of the row. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the number of arrangements in which Ana and Ben occupy the two end seats. **[2]**

**7.** (calculator-free) A committee of 5 is chosen from 7 teachers and 5 students.

**(a)** Find the number of committees with exactly 3 teachers. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the number of committees with more teachers than students. **[3]**
**(c)** One particular teacher and one particular student refuse to serve together. Find the number of possible committees. **[3]**

**8.** (calculator-free) Four-digit numbers are formed from the digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5, with no digit repeated. A number cannot begin with 0.

**(a)** Find how many of these numbers are greater than 3000. **[3]**
**(b)** Find how many of these numbers are greater than 3000 and even. **[4]**

**9.** (calculator-free) Find the first three terms in the expansion of √(1 + x)/(1 − x) in ascending powers of x, and state the values of x for which the expansion is valid. **[5]**

**10.** (calculator-free)

**(a)** Expand (1 + x)^(1/3) in ascending powers of x up to and including the term in x². **[3]**
**(b)** Hence find an approximation to ∛1.03, giving your answer to 4 decimal places. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the expansion cannot be used with x = 3 to approximate ∛4. **[1]**

**11.** (calculator-free) The expansion of (1 + ax)ⁿ, where n ∈ ℚ and n ∉ ℕ, begins 1 + 6x + 24x² + ….

**(a)** Find the values of a and n. **[5]**
**(b)** Find the coefficient of x³. **[2]**
**(c)** State the values of x for which the expansion is valid. **[1]**

**12.** (calculator allowed) A shelf holds 10 different books: 4 on maths and 6 on history.

**(a)** Find the number of ways of choosing 3 of the books and arranging them in a row on a display stand. **[1]**
**(b)** Find how many of these arrangements include at least one maths book. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 6 × 5 = **30** [1]
**(b)** 10C7 = 10C3 = (10 × 9 × 8)/6 = **120** [1]

**2.** Letters: 26 × 25 = 650 ways; digits: 10³ = 1000 ways [1]. Total **650 000** [1]

**3.** Order matters because the posts differ: 12P3 [1] = 12 × 11 × 10 = **1320** [1]

**4. (a)** 1 + (−1/2)(−x) [1] + ((−1/2)(−3/2)/2)(−x)² [1] = **1 + x/2 + (3/8)x²** [1]
**(b)** **|x| < 1** [1]

**5. (a)** (4 + x)^(−1/2) = 4^(−1/2)(1 + x/4)^(−1/2) = (1/2)(1 + x/4)^(−1/2) [1]. (1 + y)^(−1/2) = 1 − y/2 + (3/8)y² [1]. With y = x/4: 1 − x/8 + (3/128)x² [1]. So (4 + x)^(−1/2) = **1/2 − x/16 + (3/256)x²** [1]
**(b)** |x/4| < 1, so **|x| < 4** [1]

**6. (a)** 7! = **5040** [1]
**(b)** Block AB with 5 others: 6! [1], times 2 for the order within the block: **1440** [1]
**(c)** Ana at either end: 2 ways [1]; the other six in 6! ways: **1440** [1]
**(d)** Ana and Ben at the ends: 2 ways [1]; the middle five in 5! ways: **240** [1]

**7. (a)** 7C3 × 5C2 [1] = 35 × 10 = **350** [1]
**(b)** Cases 3T 2S, 4T 1S, 5T 0S [1]: 350 + 7C4 × 5C1 + 7C5 = 350 + 175 + 21 [1] = **546** [1]
**(c)** All committees: 12C5 = 792 [1]. With both of them: 10C3 = 120 [1]. Required: 792 − 120 = **672** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), listing the cases explicitly earns the first mark even if an arithmetic slip follows. "More teachers than students" with 5 members means at least 3 teachers.

**8. (a)** First digit 3, 4 or 5: 3 ways [1]. Remaining three digits from the 5 left, in order: 5P3 = 60 [1]. Total **180** [1]
**(b)** First digit 4 (even): last digit 0 or 2, 2 ways; middle two from 4 remaining digits: 4P2 = 12; so 24 [1]. First digit 3 or 5: 2 ways; last digit 0, 2 or 4, 3 ways; middle 4P2 = 12; so 72 [2]. Total 24 + 72 = **96** [1]
*Examiner insight:* An even first digit uses up one of the even digits available for the last place, which is why the cases are needed.

**9.** (1 + x)^(1/2) = 1 + x/2 − x²/8 + … [1]; (1 − x)⁻¹ = 1 + x + x² + … [1]. Product: (1 + x/2 − x²/8)(1 + x + x²) [1] = 1 + (3/2)x + (1 + 1/2 − 1/8)x² = **1 + (3/2)x + (11/8)x²** [1]. Valid when both are valid: **|x| < 1** [1]

**10. (a)** 1 + (1/3)x [1] + ((1/3)(−2/3)/2)x² [1] = **1 + x/3 − x²/9** [1]
**(b)** x = 0.03 [1]: 1 + 0.01 − 0.0001 = **1.0099** (4 d.p.) [1]
**(c)** The expansion is valid only for |x| < 1, and x = 3 lies outside that interval, so the series does not converge [1]

**11. (a)** na = 6 [1] and (n(n − 1)/2)a² = 24 [1], so n(n − 1)a² = 48. Dividing by (na)² = 36: (n − 1)/n = 4/3 [1], so 3n − 3 = 4n and **n = −3** [1]; then **a = −2** [1]
**(b)** ((−3)(−4)(−5)/3!)(−2)³ [1] = (−10)(−8) = **80** [1]
**(c)** |−2x| < 1, so **|x| < 1/2** [1]

**12. (a)** 10P3 = **720** [1]
**(b)** Arrangements with no maths book: 6P3 [1] = 120 [1]. At least one maths book: 720 − 120 = **600** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Combinations used where posts or positions make order matter.
- Restricted positions filled last instead of first.
- Overlapping cases added together, or cases that should be added multiplied instead.
- aⁿ not factored out of (a + b)ⁿ before expanding.
- Validity given as |x| < 1 for every expansion, regardless of the bracket.
