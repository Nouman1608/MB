Condensed recall for **3.5.4 Genetic disorders** of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Biology (9201) specification,
Version 5.2, for exams in May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered. The specification asks for
sensitivity when teaching about inherited disorders.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Genetic disorders study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-biology-9201-genetic-disorders/).
Then try the [Genetic disorders practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-biology-9201-genetic-disorders-practice/).
For genetic variation and monohybrid inheritance, see the [Genetic variation, monohybrid inheritance, DNA and genetic disorders revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-biology-genetic-variation-monohybrid-inheritance-dna-and-revision-notes/).

## The essentials

- **Some disorders are inherited.**
- You must be able to **interpret data** about genetic disorders such as **polydactyly**, **cystic fibrosis** and
  **sickle cell anaemia**.
- **Some inherited conditions** are caused by inheritance of **abnormal numbers of chromosomes**; for example,
  **Down's syndrome** is caused by the presence of an **extra chromosome**.

## Alleles and disorders

| Disorder | Allele that causes it | Affected genotype | Carrier genotype |
|---|---|---|---|
| Polydactyly (extra fingers or toes) | Dominant | At least one copy of the dominant allele (DD or Dd) | None: one copy is enough to be affected |
| Cystic fibrosis | Recessive | Two copies (cc) | Cc |
| Sickle cell anaemia | Recessive | Two copies (hh) | Hh |

- **Dominant**: shows when one copy is present.
- **Recessive**: shows only when two copies are present.
- **Carrier**: has one copy of a recessive allele, is not affected and can pass it on.

## Genetic diagrams: results to know

| Cross | Offspring genotypes | Probability of affected child |
|---|---|---|
| Dd (affected, polydactyly) × dd | 1 Dd : 1 dd | 1/2 |
| Cc × Cc (two carriers) | 1 CC : 2 Cc : 1 cc | 1/4 |
| Hh (carrier) × hh (affected) | 1 Hh : 1 hh | 1/2 |
| Cc × CC | 1 CC : 1 Cc | 0 |

The probability applies to **each child separately**. It does not mean that exactly one in four children will be
affected.

## Working out inheritance from a family

- Two **unaffected** parents with an **affected** child: the allele is **recessive** and both parents are carriers.
- Two **affected** parents with an **unaffected** child: the allele is **dominant** and both parents are heterozygous.
- Affected people in **every generation**: suggests a dominant allele.

## Probability of several children

```
probability of independent events: multiply
two children both unaffected (Cc × Cc) = 3/4 × 3/4 = 9/16 = 0.56
at least one affected in a family of two = 1 − 9/16 = 7/16 = 0.44
```

Worked reminder: cystic fibrosis affects about 1 in 2 500 babies. In a year with 48 000 births:
48 000 ÷ 2 500 = 19.2, so about **19** affected babies are expected.

## Down's syndrome

- Caused by an **extra chromosome** (not an allele of a single gene).
- Body cells normally have **46** chromosomes (23 pairs). A person with Down's syndrome has **47**.
- Gametes have 23 chromosomes; a gamete with 24 joins with a normal gamete (23): 24 + 23 = 47.

## Drawing a genetic diagram: checklist

1. Write the key: which letter stands for which allele (capital for dominant).
2. Write the parents' genotypes.
3. Write the gametes each parent can produce.
4. Fill in a Punnett square.
5. Read off the offspring genotypes and say which are affected, carriers or unaffected.
6. Give the probability as a fraction, ratio or percentage, and say it is **for each child**.

Worked reminder: both parents are carriers of cystic fibrosis (Cc × Cc). The squares give CC, Cc, Cc and cc. One of four
is cc, so the probability of an affected child is **1/4**. Two of four are Cc, so the probability of a carrier child is
**1/2**.

## Reading a family tree

- A square is a male and a circle is a female (if the question uses those symbols); shaded means affected.
- Look for two unaffected parents with an affected child: this proves the allele is recessive.
- Remember that unaffected people in the tree can still be carriers.

## Interpreting data on disorders

- Say what the data show: quote values, compare groups and give a trend.
- Calculate a percentage or a ratio when asked, with working.
- Do not claim a cause from a table alone.
- Down's syndrome data are about the number of chromosomes (47, not 46), not about alleles.

## Model answers to learn

- **What is a carrier? (1 mark):** "A person who has one copy of a recessive allele and is not affected, but can pass the allele on."
- **Two unaffected parents have a child with cystic fibrosis. Explain how. (3 marks):** "The allele for cystic fibrosis is recessive. Both parents are carriers (Cc). The child inherited a recessive allele from each parent (cc)."
- **What causes Down's syndrome? (1 mark):** "An extra chromosome."

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Inherited disorder | A disorder passed from parents to children in the genetic material |
| Allele | A form of a gene |
| Dominant | An allele that has its effect when one copy is present |
| Recessive | An allele that has its effect only when two copies are present |
| Carrier | Has one recessive allele, but is not affected |
| Genotype | The combination of alleles |
| Polydactyly | Extra fingers or toes, caused by a dominant allele |
| Chromosome | A structure carrying many genes |

## Exam traps

- A probability is not a guarantee: say "1 in 4 chance for each child".
- Carriers are **not affected**.
- Down's syndrome is an **extra chromosome**, not a recessive allele.
- Use **capital letters** for dominant alleles.
- Multiply, do not add, for independent events.
- Show the key, gametes and offspring in a genetic diagram.
- Keep language factual and respectful.

## Self-test

1. Name one genetic disorder caused by a dominant allele.
2. Name two genetic disorders caused by recessive alleles.
3. What is a carrier?
4. Two carriers (Cc × Cc) have a child. What is the probability that the child has cystic fibrosis?
5. What is the genotype of a person with cystic fibrosis?
6. A parent with polydactyly (Dd) and an unaffected parent (dd) have a child. What is the probability that the child has polydactyly?
7. What does an extra chromosome cause?
8. How many chromosomes are in the body cells of a person with Down's syndrome?
9. Two carriers have two children. Calculate the probability that both are unaffected.
10. Cystic fibrosis affects about 1 in 2 500 babies. Calculate the expected number of affected babies in a year with 50 000 births.

### Answers

1. Polydactyly.
2. Any two of cystic fibrosis, sickle cell anaemia (as described in the specification).
3. A person with one copy of a recessive allele who is not affected, but can pass the allele on.
4. 1/4 (25 %).
5. cc (two copies of the recessive allele).
6. 1/2.
7. Down's syndrome (an inherited condition caused by an abnormal number of chromosomes).
8. 47.
9. 3/4 × 3/4 = 9/16 = **0.56**.
10. 50 000 ÷ 2 500 = **20**.
