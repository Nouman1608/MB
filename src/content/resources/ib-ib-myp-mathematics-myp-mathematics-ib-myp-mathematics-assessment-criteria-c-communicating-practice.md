These questions practise Criterion C (communicating) from the IB *Mathematics guide for the MYP* (first assessment 2022). Section A tests notation, terminology and the choice of representation. Section B asks you to move between forms, write reasoning and structure a solution. The marks are Marlbridge practice tariffs. Read the [Criterion C study guide](/resources/ib-ib-myp-mathematics-myp-mathematics-ib-myp-mathematics-assessment-criteria-c-communicating/) and the [Criterion C revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-myp-mathematics-myp-mathematics-ib-myp-mathematics-assessment-criteria-c-communicating-revision-notes/) first.

## Questions

### Section A: language and representation

**1.** Rewrite each of these in correct mathematical notation.
**(a)** 2.5E6 **[1]**
**(b)** x^3 + 2^n **[1]**
**(c)** 3 x 4 = 12 + 3 = 15 **[1]**

**2.** State whether each is an expression, an equation or an identity.
**(a)** 4x − 7 **[1]**
**(b)** 2x + 1 = 9 **[1]**
**(c)** (x + 1)² ≡ x² + 2x + 1 **[1]**

**3.** Choose the most appropriate representation for each set of information and give a reason.
**(a)** The heights of 200 students, measured to the nearest centimetre. **[1]**
**(b)** The number of students in a class who own each type of pet. **[1]**
**(c)** How the cost of electricity changes with the number of units used. **[1]**

**4.** A student's graph of temperature against time has no title. The axes are labelled only "T" and "t", and the time axis is marked 0, 2, 5, 10, 20 at equal spacing. Identify three faults and say how to correct each. **[3]**

**5.** A plumber charges a call-out fee of 35 and 28 for each hour worked.
**(a)** Write a formula for the total charge, defining each variable. **[2]**
**(b)** State what the number 28 represents on a graph of the formula. **[1]**

### Section B: linking forms, reasoning and structure

**6.** A spring hangs with masses attached. Its length L cm for mass m kg is shown.

| m (kg) | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L (cm) | 5 | 11 | 17 | 23 |

**(a)** Write an equation linking L and m. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the graph of L against m, using correct terminology. **[2]**
**(c)** Use your equation to find the mass that gives a length of 20 cm. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain what the numbers in your equation mean for the spring. **[2]**

**7.** In triangle ABC, AB = AC and ∠BAC = 40°. Find ∠ABC, giving a reason for each step. **[3]**

**8.** A student was asked for the total area of two circles, each of radius 5 cm, and wrote:

"Area = 3.14 x 5^2 = 78.5 x 2 = 157"

Rewrite the solution so that it is correct in notation, complete and clearly set out. **[4]**

**9.** Prove that the sum of any three consecutive even numbers is a multiple of 6. Your proof should be complete, coherent and concise. **[4]**

**10.** A student investigated how many matchsticks are needed for a row of n triangles. Their write-up has these parts, in this order:
- A: "Rule: M = 2n + 1"
- B: "Table of results for n = 1 to 5"
- C: "Aim: find how many matchsticks a row of n triangles needs"
- D: "Conclusion: a row of n triangles needs 2n + 1 matchsticks"
- E: "Check: for n = 8, counting the drawing gives 17"

**(a)** Put the parts into a logical order. **[2]**
**(b)** State two things missing that would improve the communication. **[2]**

**11.** A rectangle has perimeter 24 m and width x m.
**(a)** Show that its area A m² is given by A = x(12 − x), and complete a table of A for x = 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe the shape of the graph of A against x and state the maximum area. **[2]**
**(c)** Write a concluding sentence that answers "what dimensions give the largest area?", including the values of x for which the model makes sense. **[2]**

**12.** A solution to 3x + 7 = 22 reads:

```
Line 1: 3x + 7 = 22
Line 2: 3x + 7 − 7 = 22 − 7
Line 3: 3x = 15
Line 4: 3x/3 = 15/3
Line 5: x = 5
Line 6: so x is 5
Line 7: Check: 3(5) + 7 = 22
```

**(a)** Identify the lines that could be removed to make the solution more concise, and explain why. **[2]**
**(b)** State which line should be kept even though it is not needed to find x, and why. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** **2.5 × 10⁶** [1]
**(b)** **x³ + 2ⁿ** [1]
**(c)** **3 × 4 = 12, then 12 + 3 = 15** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the original claims 12 = 15. Each equals sign must join two equal values.

**2.** **(a)** Expression [1] **(b)** Equation [1] **(c)** Identity [1] **[3]**

**3.** **(a)** A histogram (or grouped frequency table), because the data are continuous and numerous [1]
**(b)** A bar chart or pie chart, because the data are categories [1]
**(c)** A graph (or a formula), because it shows how one variable changes with another [1] **[3]**

**4.** No title: add one such as "Temperature of water against time" [1]. No units: label the axes "Temperature (°C)" and "Time (minutes)" [1]. Uneven scale: space the time values in proportion, for example every 2 minutes [1] **[3]**

**5.** **(a)** C = 35 + 28h [1], where C is the total charge and h is the number of hours worked [1]
**(b)** The **gradient**: the extra charge for each hour [1] **[3]**

**6.** **(a)** L rises by 6 for every 2 kg, so 3 cm per kg [1]; **L = 3m + 5** [1]
**(b)** A straight line [1] with gradient 3 and vertical intercept 5 [1]
**(c)** 20 = 3m + 5, so **m = 5 kg** [1]
**(d)** 5 is the length of the spring with no mass, in cm [1]; 3 is the extension in cm for each extra kilogram [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (d) is where moving between forms is shown: the numbers in the equation are linked back to the spring.

**7.** Triangle ABC is isosceles with AB = AC, so ∠ABC = ∠ACB (base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal) [1]. Angles in a triangle add to 180°, so ∠ABC + ∠ACB = 180° − 40° = 140° [1]. Therefore **∠ABC = 70°** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The reasons carry the marks here. "70°" alone shows no reasoning.

**8.** Area of one circle = π × 5² = 25π cm² [1]. Total area = 2 × 25π = 50π cm² [1], which is 157.08… [1], so the total area is **157 cm² (3 s.f.)** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The rewrite removes the chained equals signs, uses × and a superscript, keeps π exact until the end, and gives units and a degree of accuracy.

**9.** Let the numbers be 2n, 2n + 2 and 2n + 4, where n is an integer [1]. Sum = 6n + 6 [1] = 6(n + 1) [1]. n + 1 is an integer, so the sum is a multiple of 6 [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Defining n as an integer and stating that n + 1 is an integer complete the argument; without them the last step is not justified.

**10.** **(a)** **C, B, A, E, D**: the aim first and the conclusion last [1]; the table, then the rule, then the check [1]
**(b)** Any two of these, one mark each: a definition of M and n; a diagram of the arrangement; a justification of where 2n and 1 come from; a described pattern (each triangle adds 2 matchsticks) before the rule [1] [1] **[4]**

**11.** **(a)** The length is (24 − 2x)/2 = 12 − x, so A = x(12 − x) [1]. Table: x = 0, 2, 4, 6 gives A = 0, 20, 32, 36 [1], and x = 8, 10, 12 gives A = 32, 20, 0 [1]
**(b)** A parabola (curve) symmetrical about x = 6 [1]; maximum area **36 m²** [1]
**(c)** The largest area is 36 m², when the rectangle is a 6 m by 6 m square [1]; the model makes sense for 0 < x < 12 [1] **[7]**

**12.** **(a)** Lines 2, 4 and 6 [1]: lines 2 and 4 write out operations that can be shown by going straight to lines 3 and 5, and line 6 repeats line 5 [1]
**(b)** Line 7, the check, because it shows the solution satisfies the original equation [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Concise does not mean removing reasoning; it means removing repetition. A check adds evidence, so it stays.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving chained equals signs in a rewrite.
- Naming a representation without a reason for choosing it.
- Describing a graph as "going up" instead of giving its gradient and intercept.
- Giving an angle with no geometric reasons.
- Defining a variable vaguely ("h is hours") instead of exactly ("h is the number of hours worked").
- Forgetting units and the degree of accuracy in a final answer.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I show that I have moved between forms, rather than just drawn two forms?**
Explain the link. For the spring, saying that the intercept 5 is the unstretched length and the gradient 3 is the extension per kilogram connects the equation and the graph to the situation.

**Is a shorter solution always more concise?**
No. Concise means nothing unnecessary. Removing a needed reason or a check makes the reasoning incomplete, which also costs credit.
