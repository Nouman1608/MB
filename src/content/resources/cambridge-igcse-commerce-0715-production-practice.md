## Section A - short questions

**1.** A company that builds bridges and roads is in which sector of production? **[1]**
A primary   B secondary   C tertiary   D quaternary

**2.** Which of these is a direct service? **[1]**
A insurance   B road haulage   C dentistry   D wholesaling

**3.** An overseas intermediary takes possession of a producer's goods and sells them for commission. This intermediary is a: **[1]**
A retailer   B wholesaler   C factor   D franchisee

**4.** A firm's computer system sends an order to its supplier's system automatically when stock falls to a set level. This is an example of: **[1]**
A automation of a production line   B electronic data interchange   C outsourcing   D specialisation

**5.** Define specialisation. **[2]**

**6.** Identify **four** types of cost involved in trade, commerce and production. **[2]**

## Section B - structured questions

**7.** *Island Coffee grows coffee beans, roasts them in its own factory, and runs a café where it serves coffee to customers.*
Identify the sector of production for each of the three activities and explain your choice for each. **[6]**

**8.** Explain **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of specialisation for:
(a) an individual (2 marks)
(b) a factory (2 marks)
(c) a country (2 marks) **[6]**

**9.** *Zuri Cosmetics makes skin creams using a secret recipe. It is thinking of outsourcing production to a large contract manufacturer.*
(a) Define outsourced production. (2 marks)
(b) Explain **two** benefits to Zuri Cosmetics of outsourcing production. (4 marks)
(c) Explain **two** benefits to Zuri Cosmetics of keeping production in-house. (4 marks) **[10]**

**10.** Explain the role of a wholesaler and of a retailer in a home trade supply chain. **[4]**

**11.** *A furniture factory is planning to replace part of its workforce with robotic assembly arms.*
Evaluate the impact of this automation on the factory. **[6]**

**12.** *Kadi Imports buys 600 phone cases at $3 each from abroad. Customs duty is 8% of the value of the goods. Transport costs $180, packaging is $0.20 per case, and bank charges for the international payment are $25.*
(a) Calculate the total cost of the order. (4 marks)
(b) Calculate the cost per phone case. (1 mark) **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** B. Construction is part of the secondary sector. **[1]**

**2.** C. Dentistry is provided personally to people; the others are commercial services. **[1]**

**3.** C. A factor is an agent with possession of the goods. **[1]**

**4.** B. Orders are exchanged automatically between computer systems. **[1]**

**5.** Concentrating on producing a limited range of goods or services [1], or on one task within a production process, instead of doing everything [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any four from: advertising, bank charges, commission, customs duties, inventory, labour, packaging, transport, utilities, warehousing. Two correct for 1 mark, four correct for 2 marks. **[2]**

**7.** Growing coffee beans is **primary** [1] because it takes a natural resource from the land by farming [1]. Roasting the beans is **secondary** [1] because it processes a raw material into a manufactured product [1]. Serving coffee in the café is **tertiary** [1] because it provides a service directly to customers [1]. **[6]**

**8.** (a) Individual: advantage, becomes highly skilled and quicker, so may earn more [1]; disadvantage, the work becomes repetitive, or the skill becomes outdated if demand changes [1]. (2 marks)
(b) Factory: advantage, higher output and lower cost per unit from specialist machinery [1]; disadvantage, reliance on one product, so falling demand hits hard, or a breakdown at one stage stops all output [1]. (2 marks)
(c) Country: advantage, makes the most of its resources or climate and earns export income [1]; disadvantage, depends on imports for other goods, or is exposed to a fall in world prices for its main product [1]. (2 marks) **[6]**

**9.** (a) When a business pays another business [1] to make its product or carry out an activity for it instead of doing it itself [1]. (2 marks)
(b) Any two, 2 marks each: access to expertise, because a large contract manufacturer has specialist equipment and skilled staff, so quality may improve [2]; cost savings, because the manufacturer produces in large volumes and can make the creams more cheaply per unit [2]; Zuri does not need to buy machinery, freeing capital for marketing [2]; managers can focus on developing and selling the brand [2]. (max 4 marks)
(c) Any two, 2 marks each: control of information, because the secret recipe is not shared with another firm that could copy it or leak it [2]; control on quality, because Zuri checks every stage, protecting a skin-care brand where safety matters [2]; flexibility to change the formula or quantity quickly [2]; no dependence on another firm meeting deadlines [2]. (max 4 marks) **[10]**

**10.** Wholesaler: buys large quantities from producers [1] and breaks bulk, storing the goods and selling smaller quantities to retailers, which saves producers dealing with many small buyers [1]. Retailer: buys from wholesalers or producers [1] and sells to the final consumer in convenient quantities, places and times [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Up to 2 for positive impacts, up to 2 for negative impacts, up to 2 for a justified judgement.
Positive: robots work continuously and faster, raising output [1]; consistent quality reduces faulty furniture and returns [1]; lower labour cost per unit in the long run [1]; robots take over heavy or dangerous lifting [1].
Negative: high cost to buy and install the robots [1]; workers lose jobs or need retraining, which may lower morale [1]; a breakdown can stop assembly [1]; harder to make customised, one-off pieces [1].
Judgement: for example, automation is worthwhile if the factory makes large volumes of standard furniture and can afford the investment, but less so if it relies on custom orders [2]. **[6]**

**12.** (a) Inventory: 600 x 3 = $1,800 [1]. Customs duty: 8 / 100 x 1,800 = $144 [1]. Packaging: 600 x 0.20 = $120 [1]. Total = 1,800 + 144 + 180 + 120 + 25 = **$2,269** [1]. (4 marks)
(b) 2,269 / 600 = **$3.78** (to the nearest cent; $3.7817) [1]. (1 mark) **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Placing construction in primary, or a café in secondary because it "makes" coffee drinks.
- Writing specialisation points that do not match the level asked.
- In outsourcing questions, giving general points without using the scenario, such as Zuri's secret recipe.
- Calculating customs duty on the total including transport, or forgetting the bank charges.
- In "evaluate" questions, stopping after listing points with no judgement.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is construction primary or secondary?** Secondary. The syllabus describes secondary industries as manufacturing and construction.

**What is quaternary production?** Activities based on digital technology, such as software development and data services.

**What is the difference between an agent and a factor?** Both act for a principal and earn commission, but a factor has possession of the goods and an agent does not.
