These questions cover AQA A-level Biology (7402) section 3.7.3, *Evolution may lead to speciation*. All data are invented. Teaching is in the [evolution and speciation study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-evolution-may-lead-to-speciation/) and the [evolution and speciation revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-evolution-may-lead-to-speciation-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** **(a)** Name the primary source of genetic variation. **[1]**

**(b)** Define *evolution*. **[1]**

**2.** Explain how meiosis contributes to genetic variation in a population. **[2]**

**3.** Plants of one variety grown in shade are shorter than those grown in full light. Explain why selection acting on this difference in height would not cause evolution. **[1]**

**4.** Name the type of selection described in each case.

**(a)** After several generations, the mean body length of a fish population is unchanged, but there are fewer very long and very short fish. **[1]**

**(b)** In a population of birds feeding on both very small and very large seeds, the distribution of beak size changes from one peak to two. **[1]**

**5.** A population of 8 diploid plants carries 8 copies of allele T among its 16 copies of the gene. Two plants that are both TT are eaten by a grazing animal by chance before they reproduce. Calculate the new frequency of allele T among the surviving plants. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why genetic drift has a large effect on allele frequencies in small populations but little effect in large populations. **[2]**

## Section B: longer questions

**7.** A species of snail has shells that are either light or dark, controlled by one gene. Birds eat the snails. On a beach where the rocks became darkened by a new type of algae, the proportion of dark-shelled snails increased over many generations. Explain how this change happened. **[5]**

**8.** The beak depth of a population of finches on an island was measured before and several generations after a period when only large, hard seeds were available.

| | Mean beak depth / mm | Range / mm |
|---|---|---|
| Before | 9.4 | 7.6–11.2 |
| After | 10.3 | 8.5–12.0 |

**(a)** Describe the change in beak depth. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the type of selection shown and explain the change. **[3]**

**9.** A river changes course and divides a population of small mammals into two. Explain how this could lead to the formation of two new species. **[6]**

**10.** Two groups of an insect species live on the same trees. One group breeds in spring and the other in late summer. Explain how this could lead to sympatric speciation. **[4]**

**11.** Describe how scientists could find out whether two populations have become separate species. **[2]**

**12.** Compare natural selection with genetic drift as causes of changes in allele frequency. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Mutation [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** A change in the allele frequencies in a population [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Crossing over between homologous chromosomes produces new combinations of alleles [1]; independent segregation of homologous chromosomes produces different combinations of chromosomes in gametes [1]. **[2]**

**3.** The difference is caused by the environment, not by different alleles, so it is not inherited and allele frequencies would not change [1]. **[1]**

**4. (a)** Stabilising [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Disruptive [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Four copies of T are lost, leaving 4 copies out of 12 [1]; frequency = 4 ÷ 12 = **0.33** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** In a small population, a chance event (such as the death of a few individuals, or which individuals happen to breed) affects a large proportion of the gene pool [1]. In a large population the same events affect only a tiny proportion, and chance effects tend to cancel out [1]. **[2]**

**7.** There was variation in shell colour, due to different alleles (originally from mutation) [1]. On darkened rocks, light-shelled snails were more visible to birds, so more were eaten (predation as the selection pressure) [1]. Dark-shelled snails were more likely to survive and reproduce [1], passing on the allele for dark shells to their offspring [1]. Over many generations, the frequency of the dark-shell allele increased in the gene pool [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Mutation is not caused by the darkened rocks; the allele was already present or arose by chance.

**8. (a)** Mean beak depth increased from 9.4 mm to 10.3 mm (an increase of 0.9 mm) [1]; the whole range shifted upwards, from 7.6–11.2 mm to 8.5–12.0 mm [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Directional selection [1]. Birds with deeper beaks could crack large, hard seeds, so they were more likely to survive and reproduce [1], passing on alleles for deeper beaks, whose frequency increased [1]. **[3]**

**9.** The river is a geographical barrier, so the two populations are reproductively separated (allopatric) [1]. There is no gene flow between them [1]. Different mutations arise in each population [1]. The two areas may have different conditions, so different selection pressures act and different alleles are favoured [1]. Allele frequencies change differently, so differences accumulate in the gene pools over many generations [1]. Eventually members of the two populations cannot interbreed to produce fertile offspring, so they are different species [1]. **[6]**

**10.** The two groups breed at different times, so they do not interbreed, even in the same area (reproductive isolation) [1]. There is no gene flow between them [1]. Different mutations and different selection pressures change allele frequencies differently in each gene pool [1]. Eventually they could no longer interbreed to produce fertile offspring, even if breeding times overlapped [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Bring members of the two populations together and see whether they mate [1]; if they produce offspring, test whether the offspring are fertile [1]. Accept: compare DNA base sequences. **[2]**

**12.** Natural selection depends on the phenotype giving an advantage, whereas drift is due to chance [1]. Selection changes allele frequencies in a consistent direction, whereas drift has no direction [1]. Drift is important mainly in small populations, whereas selection acts in populations of any size [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that organisms mutate or adapt "in order to" survive.
- Stopping at "they survive" without reproduction, inheritance and allele frequency.
- Mixing up directional and disruptive selection.
- Leaving out "fertile offspring" in speciation answers.
- Describing sympatric speciation without saying what prevents interbreeding.
