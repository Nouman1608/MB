## Questions

**1.** State two uses of ATP in a living cell. **[2]**

**2.** A student writes: "Respiration is breathing. Plants do not respire because they photosynthesise." Explain one error in each sentence. **[4]**

**3.** A student puts germinating bean seeds in flask A and boiled, cooled bean seeds in flask B. Both flasks start at 21.0 °C. After two days, A is at 26.4 °C and B is at 21.3 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the temperature rise due to respiration, allowing for the control. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why flask B was boiled. **[1]**
**(c)** State what the result shows about the energy transfer in respiration. **[1]**

**4.** Complete the comparison for respiration in a sprinter's muscle cell and in a yeast cell without oxygen. **[4]**

| | Sprinter's muscle | Yeast |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen needed? | no | (i) |
| Products | (ii) | (iii) |
| ATP per glucose | 2 | (iv) |

**5.** A cell needs 450 ATP. Calculate how many glucose molecules it uses (a) aerobically and (b) anaerobically, and state how many times more glucose the anaerobic route needs. **[3]**

**6.** A student measures gas from yeast in glucose solution over 8 minutes. At 20 °C the volume is 8 cm³, at 30 °C it is 28 cm³, at 40 °C it is 36 cm³ and at 60 °C it is 0 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the rate at 20 °C and at 30 °C, in cm³ per minute. **[2]**
**(b)** By what percentage does the rate rise between 20 °C and 30 °C? **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the result at 60 °C. **[2]**

**7.** A runner's blood lactate was measured at four speeds: 8 km/h, 1.0 mmol/dm³; 12 km/h, 1.4; 16 km/h, 3.0; 20 km/h, 8.4.

**(a)** Describe the pattern. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the pattern. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage increase in lactate from 16 to 20 km/h. **[2]**

**8.** **(a)** Explain the terms monomer and polymer. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the two types of smaller molecule that join to make a triglyceride. **[1]**

**9.** **(a)** Glucose (mass 180) joins fructose (mass 180) to make sucrose. Calculate the mass of sucrose. **[2]**
**(b)** A polypeptide has 85 amino acids. State the number of water molecules released in making it. **[1]**
**(c)** Glycerol (mass 92) joins three oleic acid molecules (mass 282 each) to make a triglyceride. Calculate its mass. **[2]**

**10.** A person eats cheese on bread. Explain why digestive enzymes are needed and what happens to the products. **[5]**

**11.** Plan an experiment to find how temperature changes the rate of anaerobic respiration in yeast. **[8]**

**12.** Compare anaerobic respiration in sprinters' muscles and in yeast, and explain why both release less ATP than aerobic respiration. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: muscle contraction, active transport, building (synthesising) large molecules, cell division, nerve impulses [1][1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name a specific use. "Energy for the cell" repeats the stem and gains nothing.

**2.** Respiration is a chemical process in cells that transfers energy to ATP [1]; breathing only moves air in and out of the lungs [1]. Plants respire in all living cells, all the time [1]; photosynthesis makes the glucose that respiration uses and does not replace it [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each error needs the correction as well as the identification. Say what is wrong and what is right.

**3. (a)** Rise in A = 26.4 − 21.0 = 5.4 [1]; minus rise in B (0.3) = **5.1 °C** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Boiling denatured the enzymes, so the seeds cannot respire [1] **[1]**
**(c)** Respiration transfers energy as heat to the surroundings (it is exothermic) [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A control is only explained when you say what it removes. "To compare" is too vague.

**4.** (i) no [1]; (ii) lactic acid [1]; (iii) ethanol and carbon dioxide [1]; (iv) 2 [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Both products are needed for yeast. Writing carbon dioxide for muscle is a common slip.

**5.** (a) 450 ÷ 30 = **15** glucose [1]; (b) 450 ÷ 2 = **225** glucose [1]; 225 ÷ 15 = **15 times** more [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each division and state the final ratio clearly, so that the reasoning behind your answer is visible.

**6. (a)** 8 ÷ 8 = **1.0 cm³ per min** [1]; 28 ÷ 8 = **3.5 cm³ per min** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** (3.5 − 1.0) ÷ 1.0 × 100 [1] = **250%** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Enzymes are denatured at high temperature [1]; active sites change shape, so glucose no longer fits and no respiration occurs [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the original value in percentage change; dividing by the new value is a frequent loss.

**7. (a)** Lactate stays low and rises slowly at 8 to 12 km/h [1]; it increases sharply above 16 km/h [1] **[2]**
**(b)** At high speed muscles need more ATP [1]; oxygen supply cannot meet demand, so more anaerobic respiration [1]; this makes lactic acid, which builds up [1] **[3]**
**(c)** (8.4 − 3.0) ÷ 3.0 × 100 [1] = **180%** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A description uses figures from the table; an explanation links the cause to oxygen and ATP.

**8. (a)** A monomer is a small unit [1]; a polymer is a long chain of many monomers joined together [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Glycerol and fatty acids [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Lipids are not chains of one repeating unit, so avoid calling the fatty acid a monomer of the lipid.

**9. (a)** 180 + 180 = 360 [1]; minus 18 for the water lost = **342** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** **84** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** 92 + 3 × 282 = 938 [1]; minus 3 × 18 = 54 gives **884** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Subtract the mass of the water from every join. The number of waters is one less than the monomers in a chain.

**10.** Cheese contains protein and fat; bread contains starch [1]. These are large molecules and cannot be absorbed [1]. Protease makes amino acids, lipase makes glycerol and fatty acids, carbohydrase makes sugars [1]. Cells rebuild amino acids into their own proteins [1]; glucose is used in respiration to make ATP [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Match each enzyme to its substrate and product. A list of enzyme names alone earns little.

**11.** In every flask keep the yeast mass, glucose volume and glucose concentration equal, using boiled, cooled glucose solution [1]; add a layer of oil to exclude oxygen [1]. Place flasks in water baths at several temperatures, for example 15, 25, 35 and 45 °C [1]; allow time to reach temperature [1]. Collect the gas in a gas syringe [1] over a fixed time [1]. Rate = volume ÷ time, repeated and a mean taken [1]. Plot rate against temperature to find the optimum [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the independent variable, the dependent variable and at least two controlled variables; vague "keep it fair" statements earn nothing.

**12.** Both use glucose without oxygen [1]. Muscle makes lactic acid [1]. Yeast makes ethanol and carbon dioxide [1]. Both make only 2 ATP per glucose [1]. Glucose is only partly broken down [1]; energy remains in lactic acid or ethanol [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* For a comparison, state a similarity and a difference in the same sentence structure.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating respiration as breathing.
- Saying plants respire only in the dark.
- Mixing up lactic acid and ethanol.
- Writing "makes energy" rather than ATP or "transfers energy".
- Dividing by the new value in percentage change.
- Forgetting units, or leaving out "per minute".
- Using the number of monomers instead of the number of joins.
- Giving the ATP figures the wrong way round: aerobic respiration gives the large yield.
- Naming only one product of fermentation in yeast.
- Describing a control as "to compare" without saying what it removes.
