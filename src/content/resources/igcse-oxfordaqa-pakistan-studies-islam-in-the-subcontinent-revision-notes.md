**These notes condense optional topic 3.1.3 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Pakistan Studies 9236,
Islam in the Subcontinent (c700–c1831), examined in Paper 1 Section C.** For each person you need
dates, beliefs or actions, and their importance, because Section C questions ask you both to
describe and to judge significance.

## Syllabus coverage

**9236 topic 3.1.3 (Version 1.9)**
- **3.1.3.1** Sufism; Muhammad bin Qasim and the Umayyad expansion; al-Ghazali and *The Revival of the Religious Sciences*; Ahmad Sirhindi; religious developments under Aurangzeb
- **3.1.3.2** Muslim weakness by the early 18th century; Shah Waliullah's background, reforms and political impact

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|

| *Silsila* | A Sufi order with a chain of masters |
| *Pir / shaikh* | Sufi master |
| *Murid* | Disciple of a Sufi master |

| *Mujaddid* | Renewer of the faith |

## People: what to remember

**Sufi saints**
- Spread Islam by example, service and open khanqahs, not by force.
- Orders: Chishti (Ajmer), Suhrawardi (Multan), Qadiri, Naqshbandi.

**Muhammad bin Qasim**
- Sent by Hajjaj bin Yusuf; took Debal and defeated Raja Dahir.
- Gave protected status to Hindus and Buddhists; built mosques; linked Sindh to the Muslim world.
- Sindh: *Bab-ul-Islam*.

**Al-Ghazali**
- Persian scholar who taught in Baghdad; *The Revival of the Religious Sciences*.
- Joined Sharia and Sufi spirituality; criticised philosophers who placed reason above revelation.
- Shaped madrasa and Sufi teaching in the subcontinent.

**Ahmad Sirhindi**
- Naqshbandi; *Mujaddid Alf Thani*.
- Opposed Akbar's *Din-i-Ilahi*; insisted Sufism obey the Sharia; *Maktubat* letters to nobles.
- Imprisoned by Jahangir at Gwalior, later released.
- Influence linked to orthodox policies under Aurangzeb.

**Shah Waliullah**
- Studied at Madrasa Rahimiya, Delhi; studied hadith in Madinah.
- Translated the Qur'an into Persian; wrote *Hujjat Allah al-Baligha*.
- Sought unity between sects and schools of thought.
- Letters to Ahmad Shah Abdali before Panipat (1761).
- Legacy through sons, especially Shah Abdul Aziz.

**Syed Ahmad Barelvi**
- Student of Shah Abdul Aziz; served in Amir Khan's cavalry.
- With Shah Ismail Shaheed led jihad against Sikh restrictions on Muslims from 1826.
- Weakened by Pathan rivalries and tax disputes; killed at Balakot (1831).
- Inspired Ahl-i-Hadith and Deobandi movements.

## Comparing the reformers

| Reformer | Problem they faced | Main method | Lasting importance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ahmad Sirhindi | Religious syncretism at Akbar's court | Letters to nobles; insistence on Sharia | Revived orthodox Naqshbandi Sufism |
| Shah Waliullah | Mughal decline and Muslim division | Persian Qur'an translation; writing; letters to rulers | Reform tradition carried on by his sons and students |
| Syed Ahmad Barelvi | Sikh restrictions on Muslims; un-Islamic customs | Preaching and armed jihad | Inspired Ahl-i-Hadith and Deobandi movements |

## Worked example

**Question:** Explain why Shah Waliullah translated the Qur'an into Persian. **[4]**

**Model answer:** Most Muslims in the subcontinent did not understand Arabic, so they relied on
others to explain the Qur'an [1]. Persian was the educated and official language of the time, so a
Persian translation could reach many more readers [1]. He believed Muslims had become weak because
they had drifted from the Qur'an and Sunnah [1], and direct understanding of the text would help
them return to correct practice and unite [1].

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Placing al-Ghazali in India.** He lived and taught in Persia and Iraq; his influence reached the subcontinent through his books.
- **Confusing Sirhindi with Shah Waliullah.** Sirhindi opposed Akbar (16th–17th century); Shah Waliullah worked during Mughal decline (18th century).
- **Saying Sufis converted people by force.** They spread Islam through preaching, example and service.
- **Missing the significance.** Every "how important" answer needs a judgement, not just a description.

## How is this topic examined?

This topic is examined in OxfordAQA 9236 Paper 1, Section C, worth 21 of the paper's 77 marks; each paper lasts 1 hour 30 minutes. The section mixes short and extended response questions on this optional topic. Short items reward precise named facts, so learn the dates, places and people in the tables above; the extended responses reward a clear line of argument, two or three developed points with evidence, and a judgement that answers the question.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who was Shah Ismail Shaheed?**
A grandson of Shah Waliullah who joined Syed Ahmad Barelvi in the
jihad movement and died with him at Balakot in 1831.

**What links these reformers?**
Each tried to strengthen Muslims by returning to the Qur'an, Sunnah
and Sharia, and each influenced the next generation.
