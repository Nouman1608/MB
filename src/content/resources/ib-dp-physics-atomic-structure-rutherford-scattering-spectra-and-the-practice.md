These questions cover sub-topic E.1 (Structure of the atom) of the IB *Physics guide*, first assessment 2025. Questions 1 to 5 are SL and HL. Questions 6 to 12 are HL only (additional higher level). Use these values: c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹, h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, electron charge magnitude e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻², u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg and electron mass m = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg; and take R₀ = 1.2 fm (1 fm = 10⁻¹⁵ m) and ke² = 1.44 MeV fm. Give numerical answers to three significant figures.

## Questions

**1.** A nucleus contains 47 protons and 61 neutrons. State its nucleon number and write it in nuclear notation, using X for the chemical symbol. **[2]**

**2.** Describe the Geiger-Marsden-Rutherford experiment and explain what two of its observations showed about the structure of the atom. **[5]**

**3.** White light is passed through cool hydrogen gas and then through a prism. Explain why the spectrum shows dark lines, and why they appear at the same wavelengths as the bright lines in the emission spectrum of hydrogen. **[3]**

**4.** An atom has energy levels at −9.0 eV (ground state), −4.0 eV, −2.0 eV and 0 (ionised).

**(a)** Atoms are excited to the −2.0 eV level. State the number of different photon energies that can be emitted as they return to the ground state. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the shortest wavelength emitted in part (a). **[3]**
**(c)** A beam of 6.0 eV photons is directed at atoms in the ground state. Explain whether they are absorbed. **[2]**

**5.** The light from a star shows a continuous spectrum crossed by dark lines. Several of the lines match wavelengths emitted by sodium in the laboratory. Explain what this suggests and how the dark lines arise. **[3]**

**6.** (HL only) A nucleus has nucleon number 125.

**(a)** Calculate its radius. **[2]**
**(b)** Another nucleus has radius 3.6 fm. Calculate its nucleon number. **[2]**
**(c)** State how the density of these two nuclei compare, and why. **[1]**

**7.** (HL only) A head-on alpha particle of kinetic energy 9.0 MeV is fired at an aluminium nucleus (Z = 13, A = 27), which you should treat as fixed.

**(a)** Calculate the distance of closest approach. **[3]**
**(b)** Compare it with the nuclear radius and state what this tells you about the scattering. **[2]**

**8.** (HL only) Alpha particles of increasing energy are fired at a thin aluminium foil (Z = 13, A = 27). Above a certain energy, the number scattered through large angles falls below the prediction of Rutherford scattering.

**(a)** Explain this deviation. **[3]**
**(b)** Estimate the alpha energy, in MeV, at which the deviation should begin, treating the alpha particle as a point charge. **[3]**

**9.** (HL only) A hydrogen electron drops from the fifth Bohr level to the third. Calculate the wavelength of the photon released and say which part of the spectrum it lies in. **[4]**

**10.** (HL only) An electron in a hydrogen atom moves at 1.09 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹ in a circular orbit of radius 2.12 × 10⁻¹⁰ m. Show that it is consistent with the quantisation of angular momentum for a whole number n, state n, and state its energy in eV. **[4]**

**11.** (HL only) Hydrogen atoms are in the ground state and are illuminated by photons of energy 10.20 eV, 11.00 eV, 12.09 eV and 12.75 eV.

**(a)** Determine which photons can be absorbed. **[3]**
**(b)** An atom is excited to n = 3. List the photon energies it can emit and calculate the longest wavelength emitted. **[5]**

**12.** (HL only) An alpha particle of kinetic energy 6.8 MeV approaches a lead nucleus head-on (Z = 82, A = 208).

**(a)** Calculate the nuclear radius. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the distance of closest approach. **[3]**
**(c)** Comment on how the two values relate. **[1]**
**(d)** The radius of a lead atom is about 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ m. Calculate the ratio of the volume of the atom to the volume of the nucleus, and state what this suggests about the density of the nucleus relative to the atom. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Nucleon number = 47 + 61 = **108** [1]. Notation: ¹⁰⁸₄₇X, with 108 at the top and 47 at the bottom [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* the top number counts protons and neutrons, the bottom number protons only; swapping them loses both marks.

**2.** A narrow beam of alpha particles is aimed at a very thin gold foil in a vacuum and detected at different angles [1]. Most alpha particles passed through undeflected or with small deflections, so most of the atom is empty space **[1][1]**. A very small number were scattered through large angles, almost straight back [1], so the positive charge and mass are concentrated in a tiny, dense nucleus [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* pair every observation with what it shows about the atom; observations alone gain little.

**3.** Atoms in the gas absorb only photons that match a gap between a pair of their energy levels [1], removing those wavelengths from the white light, which shows as dark lines [1]. The same gaps are the ones crossed in emission, so the absorbed and emitted wavelengths are the same [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* name the mechanism, photon energy matching a level gap. "The gas absorbs some colours" does not explain the sharp lines.

**4. (a)** Three photon energies: −2.0 to −4.0, −4.0 to −9.0 and −2.0 to −9.0 eV [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** The shortest wavelength comes from the largest gap, −2.0 to −9.0 eV = 7.0 eV [1]. 7.0 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 1.12 × 10⁻¹⁸ J [1]. λ = hc/ΔE = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸ / 1.12 × 10⁻¹⁸ = **1.78 × 10⁻⁷ m** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** The possible gaps from the ground state are 5.0 eV, 7.0 eV and 9.0 eV [1]. 6.0 eV matches none of them, so the photons are not absorbed [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* convert eV to joules before using h; mixing eV and J gives a wrong answer that is hard to credit.

**5.** The star's hot interior emits a continuous spectrum [1]. Cooler gas in the outer atmosphere absorbs photons at wavelengths that match its atoms' level gaps, leaving dark lines [1]. Lines matching sodium suggest sodium is present in the star's atmosphere [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* state where the absorbing gas is and why the pattern identifies an element.

**6. (a)** R = 1.2 × 125^(1/3) [1] = 1.2 × 5 = **6.0 fm** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** A = (R/R₀)³ = (3.6/1.2)³ [1] = 3³ = **27** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Both have the same density, since density is 3u/(4πR₀³), independent of A [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* explain why the densities match (mass and volume are both proportional to A); stating "the same" without a reason is incomplete.

**7. (a)** Kinetic energy = 9.0 × 10⁶ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 1.44 × 10⁻¹² J [1]. At closest approach, E_k = k(2e)(13e)/d, so d = 2 × 8.99 × 10⁹ × 13 × (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)² / 1.44 × 10⁻¹² [1] = **4.16 × 10⁻¹⁵ m** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** R = 1.2 × 27^(1/3) = 3.6 fm [1]. d (4.16 fm) is greater than R, so the alpha particle does not reach the nucleus and the electric force alone describes the scattering [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* use the alpha charge 2e and the nuclear charge 13e; using a single elementary charge for either particle gives the wrong factor.

**8. (a)** At high energy the alpha particle approaches closer to the nucleus [1]. When the distance is about the nuclear radius, the strong nuclear force (attractive, between nucleons) acts on the alpha particle [1]. Rutherford's prediction includes only electric repulsion, so measured counts differ from it [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Set the closest approach equal to R = 1.2 × 27^(1/3) = 3.6 fm [1]. E_k = 2kZe²/R = 2 × 13 × 1.44 MeV fm / 3.6 fm [1] = **10.4 MeV** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* name the force responsible for the deviation; "the model breaks down" is too vague for credit.

**9.** E₅ = −13.6/25 = −0.544 eV and E₃ = −13.6/9 = −1.51 eV [1]. ΔE = 0.967 eV = 1.55 × 10⁻¹⁹ J [1]. λ = hc/ΔE = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸ / 1.55 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **1.29 × 10⁻⁶ m** [1]. This is longer than visible wavelengths, so it is **infrared** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* subtract upper minus lower so the photon energy is positive.

**10.** mvr = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 1.09 × 10⁶ × 2.12 × 10⁻¹⁰ = 2.105 × 10⁻³⁴ J s [1]. n = mvr × 2π / h = 2.105 × 10⁻³⁴ × 2π / 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ = 1.995 [1], so **n = 2** [1]. E = −13.6/4 = **−3.40 eV** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* give the unrounded value before stating the integer n.

**11. (a)** Gaps from n = 1: to n = 2, 10.20 eV; to n = 3, 12.09 eV; to n = 4, 12.75 eV [1]. 10.20, 12.09 and 12.75 eV photons are absorbed [1]. 11.00 eV is not absorbed because it matches no gap [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The possible transitions are 3 to 1, 3 to 2 and 2 to 1 [1]. Photon energies 12.09 eV, 1.89 eV and 10.20 eV **[1][1]**. The longest wavelength comes from the smallest energy, 1.89 eV = 3.02 × 10⁻¹⁹ J [1]. λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸ / 3.02 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **6.58 × 10⁻⁷ m** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* show the gaps you checked against each photon energy.

**12. (a)** R = 1.2 × 208^(1/3) [1] = **7.11 fm** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** E_k = 6.8 × 10⁶ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 1.09 × 10⁻¹² J [1]. d = 2kZe²/E_k = 2 × 8.99 × 10⁹ × 82 × (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)² / 1.09 × 10⁻¹² [1] = **3.47 × 10⁻¹⁴ m** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** d (34.7 fm) is about five times R, so the alpha particle stops well outside the nucleus [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Radius ratio = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ / 7.11 × 10⁻¹⁵ = 1.41 × 10⁴, so volume ratio = (1.41 × 10⁴)³ = **2.8 × 10¹²** [1]. Almost all the mass is in the nucleus, so its density is far greater than the atom's average density [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* the volume ratio is the cube of the radius ratio, not the square.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Writing the photon energy as a level energy rather than the difference between two levels.
* Substituting eV straight into E = hf without converting to joules.
* Describing the scattering experiment without the conclusion each observation supports.
* Using charge e instead of 2e for the alpha particle in a closest-approach calculation.
* Stating that deviations occur at high energy without naming the strong nuclear force or the approach to the nuclear radius.
* Using a positive sign for Bohr energy levels.
