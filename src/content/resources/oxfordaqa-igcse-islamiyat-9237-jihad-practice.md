## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. The
4-mark questions give one mark for each accurate detail, or one for each point and one for
explaining it. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a
general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the
question. The 12-mark questions are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** To strive or struggle [1].

**2.** Bil-sayf [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: earning an honest living for the family; giving charity; caring for elderly parents; helping a neighbour; working to remove harm by lawful action; volunteering for a good cause.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: fight only in defence or for a just cause; do not transgress; do not harm women, children or the elderly; do not harm those who do not fight; do not destroy crops, trees or places of worship; stop fighting if the enemy accepts peace; treat prisoners humanely; war must be declared by a lawful leader.

**5.** Any four, [1] each: it is the inner struggle [1]; against temptation, such as anger, envy, pride or greed [1]; it means keeping the intention sincere (ikhlas) [1]; it involves controlling the self (nafs) [1]; Muslims train the self through prayer, fasting and remembering Allah [1]; it is the base of the other types because sincerity gives them value [1].

**6.** For each of two ways: way identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Explaining Islam** [1]: speaking politely about the faith to classmates who ask, which calls others "with wisdom and good instruction" [1].
- **Speaking up against unfairness** [1]: defending a pupil who is being bullied, which stops wrongdoing with words [1].
- **Advising a friend** [1]: telling a friend gently that backbiting or cheating is wrong [1].
- **Refusing to join in wrongdoing** [1]: declining to take part in gossip, and saying why [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **It is striving for Allah** [1]: all Jihad is effort made for the sake of Allah, so it is an act of worship [1].
- **It improves the individual** [1]: Jihad against the self builds patience, self-control and sincerity [1].
- **It improves society** [1]: speaking truth and working for justice reduce wrongdoing and help the community [1].
- **It defends the community** [1]: armed defence, within limits, protects life, faith and freedom [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "strive for Allah with the striving due to Him" (Qur'an 22:78) [2].
- "invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good instruction" (Qur'an 16:125): Jihad by words must be wise [2].

**8.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** armed defence can protect the lives and faith of a whole community, so the stakes are highest.
- **For the statement:** the Qur'an gives permission to fight those who attack, and Muslims have fought in defence in history.
- **Against the statement:** Kitaal is only one narrow part of Jihad; it is allowed only as a last resort, under strict rules.
- **Against the statement:** the most common and constant Jihad is inner and social: controlling the self, speaking the truth and working honestly.
- **Balanced view:** armed struggle is serious and limited; the other types shape every Muslim's daily life, so they are more important in practice.

*Use of the passage*

- "Fight in the way of Allah" shows that fighting must have a spiritual and just purpose.
- "those who fight against you" shows that it is defensive, not aggressive.
- "do not transgress" and "Allah does not like transgressors" show the firm limits, which is why armed struggle is not the main kind.

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Limits (AO1):

- **Defence only** [1]: Muslims may fight only those who fight them, never to force religion on others [1].
- **Protect non-combatants** [1]: women, children, the elderly, the sick and worshippers must not be harmed [1].
- **Do not transgress** [1]: force must not go beyond what is needed, and property such as crops and trees must not be destroyed [1].
- **Accept peace** [1]: if the enemy leans towards peace, fighting should end [1].
- **Lawful authority** [1]: only a proper leader may decide, so individuals cannot declare war [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "do not transgress. Indeed, Allah does not like transgressors" (Qur'an 2:190) [2].
- "If they incline to peace, then incline to it [also]" (Qur'an 8:61) [2].

**10.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** three of the four types (heart, tongue, hand) are ordinary parts of life: controlling anger, speaking truth, working honestly.
- **For the statement:** the Qur'an commands Muslims to strive for Allah in every area of life, not only in war.
- **For the statement:** because armed struggle is allowed only in defence and under limits, most Muslims never face it.
- **Against the statement:** armed defence can be a duty for the community when it is attacked, and the passage addresses it directly.
- **Balanced view:** Jihad is mainly daily struggle, with armed defence an exceptional and tightly limited part.

*Use of the passage*

- "Fight in the way of Allah" shows even fighting must be for Allah, which links it to the same purpose as daily Jihad.
- "those who fight against you" shows that fighting is a response, not a normal way of life.
- "do not transgress" shows that restraint, a daily virtue, is demanded even in war.
