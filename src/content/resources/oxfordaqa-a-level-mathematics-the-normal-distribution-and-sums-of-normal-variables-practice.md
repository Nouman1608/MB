## Questions

**1.** The lifetime X of a type of sensor is modelled by X ~ N(72, 8²) months.

**(a)** State P(X > 72). **[1]**
**(b)** Write down the approximate proportion of sensors with a lifetime between 64 and 80 months. **[1]**
**(c)** Write down the approximate proportion of sensors with a lifetime of more than 88 months. **[1]**

**2.** The journey time W of a commuter is modelled by W ~ N(36, 5²) minutes.

**(a)** Find P(W < 42). **[2]**
**(b)** Find P(30 < W < 42). **[3]**

**3.** The marks M in a test are modelled by N(62, 11²). The top 10% of marks receive a distinction. Given that P(Z < 1.2816) = 0.90, find the lowest whole-number mark that receives a distinction. **[4]**

**4.** The mass T of a tablet is modelled by N(μ, 4.5²) milligrams. A process is set so that 2.5% of tablets have a mass below 95 mg. Given that P(Z < 1.96) = 0.975, find μ to one decimal place. **[4]**

**5.** Y ~ N(85, σ²) and P(Y > 100) = 0.05. Given that P(Z < 1.6449) = 0.95, find σ to 3 significant figures. **[4]**

**6.** X ~ N(μ, σ²), P(X < 30) = 0.10 and P(X > 46) = 0.25. Given that P(Z < 1.2816) = 0.90 and P(Z < 0.6745) = 0.75, find μ and σ to 3 significant figures. **[6]**

**7.** The mass of a passenger's hold bag is X ~ N(23, 3²) kg, and the mass of the same passenger's cabin bag is Y ~ N(17, 2²) kg. The two are independent. Find the probability that the two bags have a total mass of more than 45 kg. **[5]**

**8.** A rod has length R ~ N(50.4, 0.3²) mm and a slot has width S ~ N(50.9, 0.4²) mm, independent. The rod fits if the slot is at least 0.2 mm wider than the rod. Find the probability that a randomly chosen rod fits a randomly chosen slot. **[5]**

**9.** The mass of a packet of rice is X ~ N(8, 1.2²) hundred grams.

**(a)** A box holds 5 independent packets. Find the probability that the total mass in a box is more than 43 hundred grams. **[3]**
**(b)** Find P(5X > 43). **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the answers to (a) and (b) are different. **[1]**

**10.** The length P of a pipe is modelled by N(μ, σ²) metres. 15% of pipes are shorter than 1.96 m and 20% are longer than 2.04 m. Given that P(Z < 1.0364) = 0.85 and P(Z < 0.8416) = 0.80:

**(a)** Find μ to 4 significant figures and σ to 3 significant figures. **[6]**
**(b)** Two pipes chosen independently are joined end to end. Using μ = 2.004 and σ = 0.0426, find the probability that the joined length is between 4.00 m and 4.10 m. **[4]**

**11.** X ~ N(20, 4²) and Y ~ N(15, 3²) are independent. A student writes: "X − Y ~ N(20 − 15, 4² − 3²) = N(5, 7)".

**(a)** Identify the mistake and state the correct distribution of X − Y. **[2]**
**(b)** Find P(X < Y). **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** The distribution is symmetric about the mean 72, so P(X > 72) = **0.5** [1]
**(b)** 64 to 80 is μ ± σ, so about **2/3** [1]
**(c)** 88 = μ + 2σ. About 0.95 lies within 2σ, so the upper tail is about **0.025** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Rewrite each end point as μ plus or minus a number of σ before quoting a proportion. A bare answer with no link to μ ± σ is harder to credit if it is wrong.

**2.** **(a)** z = (42 − 36)/5 = 1.2 [1], so P(W < 42) = Φ(1.20) = **0.8849** [1]
**(b)** z = (30 − 36)/5 = −1.2 [1]
P(W < 30) = 1 − 0.8849 = 0.1151 [1]
P(30 < W < 42) = 0.8849 − 0.1151 = **0.7698** [1]
*Examiner insight:* For an interval, show both standardised values. Using symmetry here (an answer of 2Φ(1.2) − 1) is a quicker route, but show the line.

**3.** Distinction means P(M > x) = 0.10, so P(M < x) = 0.90 [1]
(x − 62)/11 = 1.2816 [1]
x = 62 + 14.098 = 76.1 [1]
Marks are whole numbers and a mark of 76 is below 76.1, so the lowest whole mark is **77** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Read the last line of the question. The context asks for a whole-number mark, so stopping at 76.1 leaves the final step undone.

**4.** P(T < 95) = 0.025, so 95 is below the mean. By symmetry z = −1.96 [1]
(95 − μ)/4.5 = −1.96 [1]
95 − μ = −8.82 [1]
μ = **103.8** mg [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show how the negative sign arises. A positive 1.96 would give a mean below 95 mg, which cannot satisfy a lower tail of 2.5%.

**5.** P(Y < 100) = 0.95 [1]
(100 − 85)/σ = 1.6449 [1]
σ = 15/1.6449 [1]
σ = **9.12** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Convert the upper tail to a lower tail before choosing the percentage point. Using 0.05 instead of 0.95 gives the wrong sign.

**6.** P(X < 30) = 0.10, so (30 − μ)/σ = −1.2816 [1]
P(X < 46) = 0.75, so (46 − μ)/σ = 0.6745 [1]
μ − 1.2816σ = 30 and μ + 0.6745σ = 46 [1]
Subtract: 1.9561σ = 16 [1]
σ = 16/1.9561 = **8.18** [1]
μ = 30 + 1.2816 × 8.1795 = **40.5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep unrounded values in the working until the end. A rounded σ used in the substitution can shift the third significant figure of μ.

**7.** X + Y is normal [1] with mean 23 + 17 = 40 [1]
Variance = 3² + 2² = 13, so sd = 3.6056 [1]
z = (45 − 40)/3.6056 = 1.39 [1]
P(X + Y > 45) = 1 − Φ(1.39) = 1 − 0.9177 = **0.0823** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write the full distribution N(40, 13) and state the independence. Marks are best earned by showing the variance as a sum of two squares.

**8.** Let D = S − R. D is normal with mean 50.9 − 50.4 = 0.5 [1]
Variance = 0.4² + 0.3² = 0.25, so sd = 0.5 [1]
Required: P(D ≥ 0.2). z = (0.2 − 0.5)/0.5 = −0.6 [1]
P = P(Z > −0.6) = Φ(0.6) [1]
= **0.7257** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Translate the context into an inequality in one variable first. The variances add even though the means are subtracted.

**9.** **(a)** Total ~ N(5 × 8, 5 × 1.2²) = N(40, 7.2) [1]
z = (43 − 40)/√7.2 = 1.12 [1]
P = 1 − Φ(1.12) = 1 − 0.8686 = **0.1314** [1]
**(b)** 5X ~ N(40, 5² × 1.44) = N(40, 36) [1]
z = (43 − 40)/6 = 0.5 [1]
P = 1 − 0.6915 = **0.3085** [1]
**(c)** The five packets vary independently and partly cancel, so the total has variance 7.2. Scaling one packet by 5 gives variance 36, so the spread is larger [1]
*Examiner insight:* Both parts have mean 40, so the explanation must refer to the variances (7.2 and 36) and not to the means.

**10.** **(a)** P(P < 1.96) = 0.15, so (1.96 − μ)/σ = −1.0364 [1]
P(P > 2.04) = 0.20, so P(P < 2.04) = 0.80 and (2.04 − μ)/σ = 0.8416 [1]
μ − 1.0364σ = 1.96 and μ + 0.8416σ = 2.04 [1]
Subtract: 1.878σ = 0.08 [1]
σ = **0.0426** [1]
μ = 1.96 + 1.0364 × 0.042599 = **2.004** [1]
**(b)** Joined length ~ N(2 × 2.004, 2 × 0.0426²) = N(4.008, 0.00363) [1]
sd = 0.0602; z limits (4.00 − 4.008)/0.0602 = −0.13 and (4.10 − 4.008)/0.0602 = 1.53 [1]
P = Φ(1.53) − Φ(−0.13) = 0.9370 − (1 − 0.5517) [1]
= **0.4887** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In part (b), use the figures the question tells you to use. Two independent pipes mean variance doubles, so the sd is not 2 × 0.0426.

**11.** **(a)** Variances must be added, not subtracted, for a difference of independent variables [1]. The correct distribution is X − Y ~ **N(5, 25)** [1]
**(b)** P(X < Y) = P(X − Y < 0). z = (0 − 5)/5 = −1 [1]
P = 1 − Φ(1) = 1 − 0.8413 = **0.1587** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A response that only says "the formula is wrong" gains less than one that states variances add and gives the corrected distribution.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the standard deviation where the variance should be, as in N(35, 4) for a standard deviation of 4.
- Not subtracting from 1 for an upper-tail probability.
- Looking up a negative z directly, or forgetting the symmetry step.
- Choosing the percentage point for the tail probability (0.05) instead of the cumulative one (0.95).
- Wrong sign for z when the stated probability is below 0.5.
- Subtracting variances for a difference of two variables.
- Treating independent copies as a multiple, 5X, or the reverse.
- Giving μ or σ to too few significant figures, or rounding midway through simultaneous equations.
- Stopping at an unrounded value when the context asks for a whole number.
