## Equations and units

| Quantity | Equation | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Acceleration | a = Δv / t | m/s² |
| Change in velocity | Δv = final - initial | m/s |
| Resultant force | F = ma | N |
| Mass | m = F / a | kg |
| Acceleration from force | a = F / m | m/s² |

Rearrange before you substitute, and convert grams to kilograms and minutes to seconds first.

## Newton's Third Law

* The two forces of a pair sit on different objects, so neither is part of the other's resultant.
* Weight (the Earth pulling the object) and the push of a surface on it are not a pair: one object, two different types of force.
* Pair for weight: the object pulling the Earth upwards, same size.
* Pair for a push of a wall on your hand: your hand pushing the wall.

Method for naming a pair: write "A exerts a force on B" and swap to "B exerts the same type of force on A".

## Finding a resultant

Forces in a line:

1. Choose a positive direction.
2. Add forces that point that way, subtract the others.
3. State size and direction of the answer.

Scale drawing for two coplanar forces:

1. Pick a scale (for example 1 cm to 1 N).
2. Draw force 1 with correct length and direction.
3. From its end, draw force 2 at the correct angle, head to tail.
4. Join start to finish: this is the resultant.
5. Measure length (convert with the scale) and measure the angle with a protractor.
6. State the angle relative to a named force.

Sense check: the resultant lies between the difference and the sum of the two force sizes.

Worked reminder: forces of 10 N and 6 N with 60° between them give a resultant of about 14 N, roughly 22° from the 10 N force.

## Acceleration

* Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.
* Constant speed with changing direction is still acceleration, because velocity changes.
* Deceleration: negative acceleration, so Δv is negative.
* Circular path at steady speed: the resultant force points to the centre.

Worked reminder: velocity 5 m/s to 17 m/s in 4 s.

```
a = (17 - 5) / 4 = 3.0 m/s²
```

## Velocity-time graphs

| Feature of the line | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Straight line sloping up | Constant positive acceleration |
| Horizontal line | Constant velocity, zero resultant force |
| Straight line sloping down | Constant deceleration |
| Steeper line | Larger acceleration |
| Area under the line | Distance travelled |
| Curve getting steeper | Increasing acceleration |

Method for area: split the graph into rectangles and triangles, work out each, then add. Use half x base x height for a triangle.

Worked reminder: velocity rises from 0 to 9 m/s in 3 s, then stays at 9 m/s for 6 s.

```
Distance = 1/2 x 3 x 9 + 6 x 9 = 13.5 + 54 = 67.5 m
```

Do not confuse the graph types: gradient of a distance-time graph is speed, gradient of a velocity-time graph is acceleration.

## Newton's First Law

* Resultant zero, object moving: same speed, same direction.
* Resultant zero, object at rest: stays at rest.
* A steady speed in a straight line needs balanced forces, not a resultant force.
* When a vehicle brakes, loose passengers carry on forwards because nothing slows them until a seat belt or seat does.

## Newton's Second Law

Method in steps:

1. Draw and label all forces on the object.
2. Find the resultant (driving minus resistive, or net of all forces).
3. Rearrange F = ma for the unknown.
4. Substitute in base units and state the unit.

Worked reminder: a 2000 kg van has a driving force of 5000 N and resistive forces of 1400 N.

```
Resultant = 5000 - 1400 = 3600 N
a = 3600 / 2000 = 1.8 m/s²
```

Proportionality: more resultant force means more acceleration; more mass means less acceleration for the same force.

## Must-know distinctions

* Speed has size only; velocity has size and direction.
* Balanced forces (zero resultant) differ from no forces.
* Resultant force differs from any single force acting.
* Mass (kg) differs from weight (N).
* Deceleration is acceleration with a negative value, not a separate quantity.
* a Third Law pair involves two objects; balanced forces involve one.

## One extended reminder

A 900 kg car speeds up from 4 m/s to 24 m/s in 10 s, with resistive forces of 500 N. Find the acceleration, the resultant force, the driving force and the distance travelled, assuming constant acceleration.

```
a = (24 - 4) / 10 = 2.0 m/s²
Resultant force F = ma = 900 x 2.0 = 1800 N
Driving force = resultant + resistive = 1800 + 500 = 2300 N
Distance = area under the line = (4 + 24) / 2 x 10 = 140 m
```

The order is always the same: acceleration from the graph or from Δv / t, then resultant force from F = ma, then the individual force from the resultant.

## Command words and answer shape

* State: a short fact, such as the wording of a law.
* Calculate: show the equation, substitution and answer with units.
* Explain: give a reason that links cause and effect, such as "the resultant force is zero, so the velocity does not change".
* Describe: say what happens, using the graph features (gradient, horizontal section, area).
* Determine from a graph: show where the gradient or area comes from, with numbers read from the axes.

For a scale drawing, give the scale, the measured length, the converted force and the angle with its reference line.

## Quick self-test

1. State two features of a pair of forces in Newton's Third Law.
2. A box has 90 N pushing it right, 35 N and 20 N pushing it left. Find the resultant.
3. A boat's speed stays constant as it moves round a circular course. Is it accelerating? Explain.
4. A cyclist speeds up from 3 m/s to 15 m/s in 8 s. Calculate the acceleration.
5. A tram slows from 16 m/s to rest in 5.0 s. Give its acceleration.
6. Which quantity is found from the area under a velocity-time graph?
7. An object's velocity rises steadily from 0 to 16 m/s over 5.0 s. How far does it travel?
8. A car moves at constant velocity on a level road. What can you say about the forces on it?
9. A resultant force of 600 N acts on a 150 kg object. Find its acceleration.
10. A 0.25 kg trolley accelerates at 6.0 m/s². What resultant force acts on it?
11. A skater of mass 60 kg is given an acceleration of 0.50 m/s². Find the resultant force.
12. A force of 24 N and a force of 7 N act at right angles. Find the resultant size.

### Answers

1. Equal in size, opposite in direction (also: same type of force, acting on different objects).
2. 90 - 35 - 20 = 35 N to the right.
3. Yes. Its direction is changing, so its velocity is changing, even though its speed is constant.
4. a = (15 - 3) / 8 = 1.5 m/s².
5. a = (0 - 16) / 5.0 = -3.2 m/s² (deceleration of 3.2 m/s²).
6. The distance travelled.
7. Triangle: 1/2 x 5.0 x 16 = 40 m.
8. The forces are balanced: the resultant force is zero.
9. a = 600 / 150 = 4.0 m/s².
10. F = 0.25 x 6.0 = 1.5 N.
11. F = 60 x 0.50 = 30 N.
12. Pythagoras: √(24² + 7²) = √625 = 25 N.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Writing "the forces cancel" for a Third Law pair instead of saying they act on different objects.
* Subtracting forces in the wrong order and giving a negative resultant without a direction.
* Putting only the driving force into F = ma.
* Not subtracting the starting velocity when finding Δv.
* Missing the minus sign on a deceleration.
* Forgetting that motion in a circle at constant speed involves acceleration.
* Calculating the area of a triangle without the half.
* Giving a scale-drawing angle without saying which force it is measured from.
* Leaving out units, or leaving mass in grams.
