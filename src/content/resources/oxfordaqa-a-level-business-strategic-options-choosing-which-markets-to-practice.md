## Questions

**1.** State **two** features of a strategic decision. **[2]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by a strategy and why having one is significant for a business. **[3]**

**3.** Quarrendon Foods makes biscuits for its home market. Identify the Ansoff strategy shown by each move.

**(a)** Selling its existing biscuits in a neighbouring country. **[1]**
**(b)** Signing deals with more convenience stores in its home cities to stock its existing range. **[1]**
**(c)** Launching oat breakfast bars for its current shoppers. **[1]**
**(d)** Buying a chain of gyms. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why market penetration is usually the lowest-risk option in the Ansoff matrix. **[3]**

**5.** Skerrow Kayaks made 7,830 kayaks last year. Its capacity is 10,800 kayaks a year. Total kayak sales in its home market were 58,000.

**(a)** Calculate Skerrow's capacity utilisation. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate Skerrow's share of the home market by volume. **[2]**

**6.** Skerrow has chosen market development, selling its kayaks to tour operators in coastal holiday regions abroad. Explain how this strategy links to decisions in its marketing **and** finance functions. **[4]**

**7.** Tamsett Optics has non-current liabilities of £8.4 million and total equity of £5.6 million. It is considering diversification into hearing-test clinics.

**(a)** Calculate Tamsett's gearing. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one implication of your answer for funding the diversification. **[2]**

**8.** Mirefield Pet Foods plans product development: a grain-free dog food for its existing pet-shop customers. Explain **two** implications for its operations function. **[4]**

**9.** Ravensoak Hotels runs eight hotels in its home country and plans to open hotels in a country where it has never traded. Analyse how this market development strategy could affect its human resources function. **[6]**

**10.** Tillinghast Print's main product is printed catalogues for retailers. Orders have fallen for three years as retailers move to online catalogues, and a new managing director has been appointed.

**(a)** Identify **two** reasons from the case why Tillinghast may need to change its strategy. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse **one** challenge Tillinghast may face in changing its strategy. **[4]**

**11.** Brindmoor Dairy makes yoghurt. Data:

- Home yoghurt market: £400 million two years ago, £380 million last year
- Brindmoor's yoghurt sales: £48 million two years ago, £41.8 million last year
- Capacity utilisation: 68%; gearing: 18%
- Yoghurt sales in a neighbouring country are growing about 7% a year; Brindmoor has no presence there

The directors are choosing between **Option A**, market development (exporting its yoghurts to the neighbouring country), and **Option B**, diversification (opening a chain of frozen-yoghurt cafés at home).

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in the size of the home market and the change in Brindmoor's market share. **[4]**
**(b)** Evaluate which option Brindmoor should choose. **[12]**

**12.** Lunsford Glassworks makes glass bottles for drinks companies. Last year 45 employees produced 31,500 cases of bottles. This year 48 employees produced 30,240 cases. Six employees left this year; the industry labour turnover rate is 6%. The directors plan product development: lightweight bottles for their existing drinks-company customers.

**(a)** Calculate labour productivity for both years and the percentage change. **[3]**
**(b)** Assess how far this data should influence the decision to pursue product development. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: difficult to reverse [1]; high risk; involves significant resources; usually a one-off decision [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Each feature must be distinct; "expensive" and "uses a lot of money" make the same point and tend to be credited once.

**2.** A strategy is a long-term plan for how a business will achieve its objectives [1]. It gives every function a shared direction [1], so scarce resources are allocated to the same priorities rather than wasted on conflicting projects [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Credit beyond the definition needs a developed consequence; a list of three unlinked benefits earns less than one explained chain.

**3. (a)** Market development [1]. **(b)** Market penetration [1]. **(c)** Product development [1]. **(d)** Diversification [1].
*Examiner insight:* Read which element is new in each move; naming the box without that check is how (a) and (c) get swapped.

**4.** The business sells products it already makes to customers it already knows [1], so it needs little research, new investment or new skills [1]. Its knowledge of demand and rivals makes results easier to predict, so the chance of costly failure is lower [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Usually" matters; answers that call penetration risk-free can lose credit, because rivals may retaliate.

**5. (a)** 7,830 ÷ 10,800 × 100 [1] = **72.5%** [1].
**(b)** 7,830 ÷ 58,000 × 100 [1] = **13.5%** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the substituted formula before the answer, and give the % sign; a bare 0.725 does not answer the question as set.

**6.** Marketing must research tour operators' needs abroad [1] and set prices and distribution suited to those regions, which shapes its promotion budget [1]. Finance must fund set-up costs such as overseas sales staff and shipping before revenue arrives [1], and may need to manage currency risk if prices are set in another currency [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Both named functions are needed; an answer covering only marketing caps at half the marks however well developed.

**7. (a)** 8.4 ÷ (8.4 + 5.6) × 100 [1] = **60%** [1].
**(b)** Gearing of 60% is high, so lenders may refuse further loans or charge higher interest [1]; Tamsett may have to fund the clinics from share capital or retained profit, or delay the plan [1].
*Examiner insight:* Capital employed includes non-current liabilities; dividing by equity alone gives 150%, which is a different measure.

**8.** It may need new suppliers of grain-free ingredients [1], which could raise unit costs or create supply risk while contracts are set up [1]. Production lines may need cleaning or separating to avoid grain contamination [1], which could reduce output of existing products while changes are made [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each implication needs an operations consequence; "it will need new machines" alone is a point without development.

**9.** Ravensoak must recruit local staff who know the language and customs [1], which raises recruitment costs and takes time before hotels open [1]. Managers sent from home need training in local laws and culture [1]; without it, mistakes over contracts or guest expectations could damage service quality [1]. Pay and conditions must follow local employment law and wage levels [1], so HR may run two sets of policies, adding to workload and the risk of perceived unfairness between countries [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Reasoning that stays tied to Ravensoak's move abroad earns analysis credit; generic HR points that would apply to any firm gain less.

**10. (a)** Falling demand as retailers move online [1]; a new managing director who may set new objectives [1].
**(b)** Staff skilled in print production may resist change [1] because they fear job losses or new ways of working [1]. This can slow the change and lower productivity [1], so Tillinghast may need to retrain staff and explain the reasons early, adding cost and time [1].
*Examiner insight:* For "identify from the case" questions, points must come from the stem; outside reasons gain little.

**11. (a)** Market change = (380 − 400) ÷ 400 × 100 [1] = **−5%** [1]. Share before = 48 ÷ 400 × 100 = 12%; share after = 41.8 ÷ 380 × 100 = 11% [1]; change = **−1 percentage point** [1].
**(b)** The home market shrank by 5% and Brindmoor's share fell from 12% to 11% [1], so staying with penetration alone is unattractive: a threat in its main market [1].
Option A uses proven products and existing production [1]; capacity utilisation of 68% means about a third of capacity is idle, so exports could lower unit costs [1]. The 7% growth abroad is an opportunity matched to these strengths [1]. However, Brindmoor has no presence there, so it needs distributors and must meet local standards and tastes [1]; chilled export logistics may add cost [1].
Option B spreads risk away from supermarket yoghurt [1], but Brindmoor lacks retail and café-management skills and must lease sites and hire staff [1], the largest outlay for the least experience [1]. Its 18% gearing means borrowing for either option is possible [1].
Judgement: Option A is better, because it uses existing strengths against a growing market with lower risk; it depends on finding a reliable distributor and on exchange rates staying favourable [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong evaluations use the calculated figures and finish with a decision plus what it depends on; balanced lists with no judgement tend to score in the middle.

**12. (a)** Last year 31,500 ÷ 45 = 700 cases per employee; this year 30,240 ÷ 48 = 630 [1]. Change = (630 − 700) ÷ 700 × 100 [1] = **−10%** [1].
**(b)** Productivity fell by 10% [1], so unit labour costs are rising: a weakness that could make new, possibly more complex lightweight bottles costly to produce [1]. Labour turnover is 6 ÷ 48 × 100 = 12.5%, double the 6% average [1], so skills may be lost just when new production methods must be learned [1]. On the other hand, the product is for existing customers, so Lunsford keeps its market knowledge, and lighter bottles may cut customers' transport costs [1]. The data is internal only; it shows nothing about customer demand or rivals' plans [1], and one year's fall may reflect new staff still in training [1]. Judgement: the data should delay rather than stop the plan; Lunsford should tackle turnover and productivity first, then check demand before investing [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Assess how far" needs a limit on the data as well as its use; answers that only interpret the figures miss the evaluative credit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping market development and product development by not checking which element is new.
- Calling penetration risk-free.
- Dividing by total equity instead of capital employed in gearing.
- Writing "per cent" for a change in market share instead of percentage points.
- Giving functional implications that do not mention the case business or its strategy.
- Answering a two-function question with only one function.
- Using reasons from outside the case when told to identify them from the case.
- Ending an evaluation without a decision or without saying what it depends on.
