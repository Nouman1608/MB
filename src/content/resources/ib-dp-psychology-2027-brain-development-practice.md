> **These are original questions written for Marlbridge** on the brain development area of the human development context (first assessment 2027). They are not past-paper questions. The scenarios and the study in Question 6 are invented. Question 9 uses the 15-mark tariff of the concept-based extended response; other tariffs are for practice.

Related: [brain development study guide](/resources/ib-dp-psychology-2027-brain-development/) and [revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-psychology-2027-brain-development-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Explain what is meant by brain maturation. **[3]**

**2.** Distinguish between a critical period and a sensitive period. **[3]**

**3.** Explain the role of neuroplasticity in human development. **[4]**

**4.** Describe one study that investigated a critical period for development. **[4]**

## Section B

**5.** Read the scenario (invented for practice).

> Leah, 16, is a gifted musician and organises her own practice timetable well, but she sometimes takes risks with friends, such as crossing a busy road without checking. Her younger brother Omar, 8, struggles to follow a plan when he is distracted. Their father says "it's just their brains".

**(a)** Use brain maturation to explain Leah's behaviour. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one limitation of the father's claim. **[3]**

**6.** Read the summary of a constructed study.

> A research team scanned 40 children aged 7 before and after a 12-month programme. Twenty children, chosen at random, took weekly piano lessons and practised at home. Twenty children, also chosen at random, had weekly games lessons. The team measured grey matter in an area linked to hand control and found a greater increase in the piano group.

**(a)** State the experimental design used. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how the study supports the role of neuroplasticity in development. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest one limitation of the study. **[2]**

**7.** Explain one ethical consideration in research on critical periods. **[3]**

**8.** Evaluate the extent to which critical periods explain language development. **[6]**

## Section C

**9.** Evaluate the role of brain development in explaining human development. Refer to at least two concepts in your answer. **[15]**

## Answers

**1.** Brain maturation is the ordered biological growth and refinement of the brain with age **[1]**, including synaptic formation, pruning and myelination **[1]**. Different regions mature at different rates, with the prefrontal cortex maturing last **[1]**.

**2.** A critical period is a limited time window when an experience is needed for normal development **[1]**; if it is missed, the function is not fully acquired **[1]**. A sensitive period is a time of heightened responsiveness where development is still possible later, though harder **[1]**.

**3.** Neuroplasticity is the ability of the brain to change its structure and function with experience **[1]**, for example by strengthening used connections and pruning unused ones **[1]**. In development it explains why experiences such as practice, teaching and relationships shape abilities **[1]**, and why recovery or intervention remains possible later in life **[1]**.

**4.** Any one study. For example Hubel and Wiesel with kittens: one eye was covered for a period early in life **[1]**; the visual cortex then failed to respond normally to that eye even after the cover was removed **[1]**; this suggested a critical period for visual development **[1]**. Credit detail on method or finding, such as limited recovery when deprivation happened later or lasted longer **[1]**. Equally creditable: Genie, Johnson and Newport (1989), the English and Romanian Adoptees study.

**5.** (a) The prefrontal cortex, which supports planning and impulse control, continues to mature into the early twenties **[1]**. Leah can plan her practice because planning circuits are well developed **[1]**, but reward-related regions respond strongly with peers while control regions are not fully mature, so she takes risks **[1]**.

(b) Any one limitation, up to **[3]**. The claim is reductionist: experience, teaching and culture also shape behaviour **[1]**. Brain imaging evidence is correlational, so structure at an age does not prove that structure caused the behaviour **[1]**. Individual differences are large, so averages may not apply to Omar or Leah **[1]**.

**6.** (a) A true experiment with random allocation to two groups (independent measures), measured before and after (pretest-posttest) **[1]**.

(b) The piano group practised a skill and showed greater grey matter growth in the relevant region than the games group **[1]**. Random allocation and a control group mean the difference is more likely to be due to the lessons than to other factors **[1]**. This suggests that experience changes the brain, which is neuroplasticity **[1]**.

(c) Any one limitation, up to **[2]**: a small sample from one age group **[1]**; the games group did not control for motivation or the amount of practice at home **[1]**; no follow-up to show if the changes last **[1]**; the functional meaning of a small grey matter increase is unclear **[1]**.

**7.** Deliberate deprivation of children or animals to test a critical period would cause serious harm **[1]**, so researchers rely on natural experiments and case studies **[1]**, in which vulnerable participants need protection from harm, privacy and careful reporting **[1]**. Or: animal research raises concerns about welfare (reduce, refine, replace) and generalisation **[1]**.

**8.** Description (up to 2): the idea that language must be acquired in a window, for example before puberty **[1]**; evidence such as Genie or Johnson and Newport (1989) **[1]**. Evaluation (up to 4): supporting evidence of age effects on grammar **[1]**; confounds such as abuse in Genie's case and differences in motivation and exposure in immigrants **[1]**; most evidence supports a sensitive period with gradual decline, not a sharp cut-off **[1]**; recovery after early brain damage supports plasticity **[1]**; ethics prevent experiments **[1]**. Award up to **[6]**.

**9.** Markband guide: 13 to 15 for well-developed critical analysis, links between concepts throughout and a reasoned conclusion; 10 to 12 for good analysis lacking development; lower bands for description. Indicative content, one mark per developed point up to the markband:
- Maturation explains ordered change in cognition, adolescent behaviour and language, with imaging evidence, but is correlational and reductionist **[2]**.
- Critical or sensitive periods explain why early experience matters, with animal and human evidence, but ethics and confounds limit the claims **[2]**.
- Neuroplasticity shows that experience shapes the brain, and development continues, with experiments such as the juggling study giving causal support **[2]**.
- Causality: nature and nurture interact, so brain development is better seen as interaction than as an independent cause **[2]**.
- Measurement: MRI and longitudinal designs give objective data, but small structural changes are hard to interpret **[2]**.
- Perspective: biological accounts must be set against sociocultural ones such as Vygotsky's emphasis on cultural tools and social interaction **[2]**.
- Bias and responsibility: samples from Western populations, researcher interpretation, ethics of deprivation research and use of animals **[2]**.
- Change: if brain development is plastic, interventions can have effects, but the timing matters **[2]**.
- A conclusion that weighs the contributions, for example that brain development is necessary for explaining development but insufficient alone **[1]**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 2:** repeating that critical periods are "important times" without a contrast.
- **Question 5:** listing facts about the prefrontal cortex without applying them to Leah.
- **Question 6(b):** saying "the groups differed" without linking to random allocation and the control.
- **Question 9:** describing brain structures without evaluating how far they explain development.
