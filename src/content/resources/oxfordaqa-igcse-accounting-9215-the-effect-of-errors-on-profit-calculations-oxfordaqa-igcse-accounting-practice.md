These questions cover "The effect of errors on profit calculations" from section 3.2 of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Accounting (9215) specification, version 1.2: adjusting the profit or loss for a period after errors are corrected. Unless a question says otherwise, ignore depreciation. All amounts are in dollars. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured ones.

Revise first with the [effect of errors on profit study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-the-effect-of-errors-on-profit-calculations-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting/) and the [effect of errors on profit revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-the-effect-of-errors-on-profit-calculations-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Correcting which one of these errors would change gross profit?

A: wages overcast by 200

B: carriage outwards of 90 not recorded

C: purchases returns of 150 not recorded

D: rent received of 400 not recorded

**[1]**

**2.** The wages account was overcast by 250. State the effect on profit for the year of correcting the error. **[1]**

**3.** Closing inventory was overvalued by 800. State the effect of correcting this error on gross profit and on profit for the year. **[2]**

**4.** A payment of 600 for office furniture was debited to the motor vehicles account. Explain the effect of correcting this error on profit for the year. **[1]**

**5.** A business has a draft loss for the year of 2,300. The corrections of errors increase profit by a net 1,500. State the corrected result. **[2]**

**6.** Carriage inwards of 270 was debited to the carriage outwards account. State the effect of correcting this error on gross profit and on profit for the year. **[2]**

### Section B

**7.** Linden Hardware calculated a draft gross profit of 52,300 and a draft profit for the year of 19,840. These errors were then found:

1. A credit sale of 1,600 was entered as 1,060 in both the sales account and the customer's account.
2. Purchases returns of 480 were not recorded.
3. Closing inventory included goods of 900 that had been counted twice.
4. Loan interest of 350 paid by bank was debited to the loan account.
5. A new till costing 1,250 was debited to the shop expenses account.
6. Discount allowed of 130 was debited to the discount received account.

Prepare a statement showing the corrected gross profit and the corrected profit for the year. **[8]**

**8.** The trial balance of Ferris Catering did not agree, and the difference was entered in a suspense account. The draft profit for the year was 8,750. These errors were then found:

1. Rent received of 600 was debited to the bank account but not credited to the rent received account.
2. The wages account was overcast by 200.
3. A payment of 1,400 to a supplier was credited to the bank account but not debited to the supplier's account.
4. The sales returns journal was undercast by 150.
5. Electricity of 320 paid by the business was debited to the owner's drawings account.

**(a)** Prepare a statement of corrected profit for the year. **[5]**

**(b)** State which of the five errors would have been corrected through the suspense account. **[1]**

**9.** Explain the effect of correcting each error on profit for the year.

**(a)** Closing inventory was undervalued by 500. **[2]**

**(b)** A delivery van bought for 9,000 was debited to the motor expenses account. **[2]**

**(c)** Commission received of 300 was credited to the rent received account. **[2]**

**10.** Explain the difference between an error that changes both gross profit and profit for the year and an error that changes only profit for the year. Give one example of each. **[4]**

**11.** The draft profit for the year of Mira's Boutique is 15,400. The owner took goods costing 350 for her own use, and this was not recorded. She also paid her personal phone bill of 120 from the business bank account, and this was debited to the business telephone account. Calculate the corrected profit for the year. **[3]**

**12.** State the effect on profit for the year of each of these correcting journal entries.

**(a)** Dr Suspense 400, Cr Sales 400. **[1]**

**(b)** Dr Motor expenses 700, Cr Motor vehicles 700. **[1]**

**(c)** Dr T Lee 250, Cr T Leigh 250. **[1]**

**(d)** Dr Bank 180, Cr Commission received 180. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]

*Examiner insight:* purchases returns are part of cost of sales; the other three are other income or expenses, which affect only profit for the year.

**2.** Profit for the year **increases by 250** [1]

**3.** Gross profit **decreases by 800** and profit for the year **decreases by 800** [1] [1]

**4.** **No effect**: both accounts are non-current assets, so no income or expense account is involved [1]

**5.** −2,300 + 1,500 = −800 [1]; corrected result: **loss of 800** [1]

**6.** Gross profit **decreases by 270** [1]; profit for the year **no effect**, because carriage outwards falls by the same 270 [1]

**7.**

| | Gross profit | Profit for the year |
|---|---:|---:|
| Draft figures | 52,300 | 19,840 |
| 1 Sales understated | 540 | 540 |
| 2 Purchases returns not recorded | 480 | 480 |
| 3 Closing inventory overstated | (900) | (900) |
| 4 Loan interest not charged as an expense | 0 | (350) |
| 5 Till charged as an expense | 0 | 1,250 |
| 6 Discount allowed posted to discount received | 0 | 0 |
| **Corrected figures** | **52,420** | **20,860** |

Error 1: +540 in both columns [1]; error 2: +480 in both columns [1]; error 3: (900) in both columns [1]; error 4: (350) in profit for the year only [1]; error 5: +1,250 in profit for the year only [1]; error 6: no effect, because discount allowed was understated by 130 and discount received understated by 130, which cancel [1]; corrected gross profit **52,420** [1]; corrected profit for the year **20,860** [1]

**8. (a)**

```
Draft profit for the year                 8,750
1 Rent received not recorded               +600
2 Wages overcast                           +200
3 Payment to supplier (no effect)             0
4 Sales returns understated                −150
5 Electricity debited to drawings          −320
Corrected profit for the year             9,080
```

+600 [1]; +200 [1]; −150 [1]; −320 [1]; corrected profit **9,080**, with error 3 shown as no effect [1]

**(b)** Errors **1, 2, 3 and 4** [1]

*Examiner insight:* error 5 had equal debit and credit entries (the debit was just in the wrong account), so it did not cause the trial balance difference.

**9. (a)** Undervalued closing inventory made cost of sales too high, so profit was understated [1]; the correction **increases profit by 500** [1]

**(b)** A non-current asset was treated as an expense, so expenses were overstated and profit understated [1]; the correction **increases profit by 9,000** [1]

**(c)** Both are income accounts, so total income was correct [1]; **no effect** on profit [1]

**10.** An error in a trading item (sales, purchases, returns, carriage inwards or inventory) changes cost of sales or revenue, so it changes **gross profit and profit for the year** by the same amount [1]; example: purchases overcast by 200 [1]; an error in other income or an expense is shown below gross profit, so it changes **profit for the year only** [1]; example: rent paid not recorded [1]

**11.** Goods for own use: purchases were overstated by 350, so profit **+350** [1]; private phone bill is drawings, not a business expense, so profit **+120** [1]; corrected profit = 15,400 + 350 + 120 = **15,870** [1]

**12. (a)** **Increases** by 400 [1]

**(b)** **Decreases** by 700 [1]

**(c)** **No effect** [1]

**(d)** **Increases** by 180 [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adjusting in the wrong direction: overstated expenses mean understated profit.
- Putting expense errors into the gross profit column.
- Missing that inventory errors affect both profits.
- Ignoring errors with no effect instead of stating "no effect".
- Writing a corrected figure without saying whether it is a profit or a loss.
