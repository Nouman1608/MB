## Core recall

- **Economic efficiency:** no reallocation of resources can make someone better off without making someone else worse off. It needs both productive and allocative efficiency.
- **Productive efficiency:** maximum output from given inputs; the economy is on its PPC; a firm produces at the minimum of its average cost curve.
- **Allocative efficiency:** resources produce the goods and quantities society values most; for one good, price (marginal benefit) equals marginal cost; total surplus is at its maximum.
- Productive efficiency answers **how** to produce. Allocative efficiency answers **what and how much**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| PPC | Curve showing the maximum combinations of two goods with given resources and technology |
| Average cost (AC) | Total cost ÷ output: cost per unit |
| Marginal benefit | Extra benefit from consuming one more unit; shown by the demand curve |
| Marginal cost | Extra cost of producing one more unit; shown by the supply curve when there are no externalities |
| Consumer surplus | What buyers are willing to pay minus what they actually pay |
| Producer surplus | What sellers receive minus the lowest price they would accept |
| Total surplus | Consumer surplus + producer surplus |
| Deadweight (welfare) loss | Surplus lost because output is above or below the efficient level |
| Price mechanism | Prices acting as signals, incentives and rationing devices |

## Diagrams to know

1. **PPC with points:** on the curve = productively efficient; inside = inefficient (unemployed resources); outside = unattainable now. Mark one point on the curve as allocatively efficient and say it depends on preferences.
2. **AC curve:** U-shaped; productive efficiency at the lowest point.
3. **Demand and supply with surplus:** consumer surplus triangle above price, producer surplus below; restrict output and shade the deadweight triangle between the curves.

## Method boxes

**Is a point inside the PPC?** Fix one good at its actual level, read off how much of the other the PPC allows (interpolate along the straight section between two listed points), and compare.

*Reminder:* a PPC passes through (50 cars, 10 tractors) and (30 cars, 20 tractors). Output is 30 cars and 15 tractors. At 30 cars the curve allows 20 tractors, so 5 tractors are being wasted.

**Productively efficient output from a table:** work out AC = TC ÷ Q for each row and choose the lowest.

*Reminder:* TC of £800 at 100 units, £1,200 at 200 and £2,100 at 300 gives AC of £8, £6 and £7. The productively efficient output is 200 units.

**Welfare loss of a restriction:** ½ × (efficient Q − actual Q) × (price on demand − price on supply at the actual Q).

*Reminder:* Qd = 120 − 4P and Qs = 4P − 40 give P = £20, Q = 40. A cap of 32 units means buyers would pay £22 and marginal cost is £18, so the loss is ½ × 8 × 4 = **£16**.

## Why the two can differ

- On the PPC but at the wrong mix: productively efficient, allocatively inefficient.
- Minimum AC but too much or too little output for what consumers value: same result.
- Externalities: the market's private equilibrium is not where marginal social benefit equals marginal social cost.

## Economic systems: one-line verdicts

| System | Productive efficiency | Allocative efficiency | Main weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market | Strong (profit, competition) | Strong where markets work | Externalities, poor information, inequality |
| Planned | Weak (few incentives) | Weak (no price signals) | Shortages, surpluses, waste |
| Mixed | Depends on competition and public management | Government can fix market failure | Risk of government failure |

## Exam traps

- "Productively efficient" does not mean "produces the most". It means no waste of inputs.
- Not every point on a PPC is allocatively efficient.
- Market equilibrium is allocatively efficient only without externalities and with good information.
- Write the welfare loss as an area, with units.
- An efficient outcome can still be unfair: efficiency and equity are separate criteria.
- When evaluating systems, judge both kinds of efficiency, not just one.

## Self-test

1. Define productive efficiency.
2. Define allocative efficiency.
3. Where on a PPC is an economy productively inefficient, and why?
4. A firm's total cost is £900 at 150 units and £1,000 at 200 units. Which output has the lower average cost?
5. Give one cause of an economy producing inside its PPC.
6. Why is the demand curve a measure of marginal benefit?
7. Qd = 100 − 5P and Qs = 5P − 20. Find equilibrium price and quantity.
8. In question 7, calculate consumer surplus. (Demand meets the price axis at £20.)
9. Explain how an economy can be productively efficient but allocatively inefficient.
10. Why does a planned economy struggle to achieve allocative efficiency?
11. State one reason why market economies tend to be productively efficient.
12. Why might an externality mean the free market output is not allocatively efficient?

### Answers

1. Producing the maximum output from given inputs, or producing at the lowest point on the average cost curve.
2. Producing the combination and quantity of goods that society values most, where price (marginal benefit) equals marginal cost.
3. At any point inside the curve, because resources are unemployed or poorly used, so more of both goods could be produced.
4. 150 units: AC = £6.00; 200 units: AC = £5.00. **200 units** has the lower AC.
5. Any one of: unemployment in a recession, idle capital, poor organisation of production.
6. It shows the most buyers are willing to pay for each extra unit, which reflects the benefit they expect from it.
7. 100 − 5P = 5P − 20, so P = **£12** and Q = **40**.
8. ½ × 40 × (20 − 12) = **£160**.
9. It can be on its PPC, wasting no resources, but at a mix of goods that does not match what people want most.
10. Without prices, planners lack information about what consumers want and how much they value it, so they produce too much of some goods and too little of others.
11. Any one of: the profit motive rewards cutting costs; competition forces inefficient firms out; owners bear the losses from waste.
12. Firms and consumers ignore external costs or benefits, so marginal private cost or benefit differs from the social value and the market produces too much or too little.
