## Questions

**1.** A school wants to decide how many photocopiers to buy. Describe, in order, the four stages of the problem-solving cycle, giving a specific action at each stage for this problem. **[4]**

**2.** Plan P costs £8 per month plus 5p per minute of calls. Plan Q costs £14 per month plus 2p per minute.

**(a)** Write an expression for the monthly cost of each plan for m minutes. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the number of minutes for which the two plans cost the same. **[1]**
**(c)** A customer uses 150 minutes a month. Which plan is cheaper, and by how much? **[2]**

**3.** Let f(x) = x³ + 4x − 7. Show that f(x) = 0 has a root which is 1.3 to 1 decimal place. **[3]**

**4.** A plot measures 16.4 m by 7.2 m, each to 1 decimal place.

**(a)** Find the lower and upper bounds for its area. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the area to a justified degree of accuracy, with a reason. **[2]**

**5.** A hall is 20 m long, 12 m wide and 5 m high. One tin of paint covers 40 m² for one coat. Estimate the tins needed for two coats on the four walls. State your assumption and whether it makes the estimate too high or too low. **[4]**

**6.** A box of mass 4 kg is held at rest on a smooth slope inclined at 20° to the horizontal by a light string parallel to the slope.

**(a)** Draw and label a force diagram. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the tension in the string. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the normal reaction. **[1]**

**7.** A tank holds 800 litres. A model for the volume V litres left t minutes after the outlet opens is V = 800 − 24t.

**(a)** State two assumptions of this model. **[2]**
**(b)** Find when the model says the tank is empty. **[1]**
**(c)** At t = 20 the measured volume is 395 litres. Compare this with the model. **[1]**
**(d)** A second model is V = 800e^(−kt), where V = 560 at t = 10. Find k to 3 significant figures, and decide which model fits the measurement at t = 20 better. **[3]**

**8.** A parcel of mass 3 kg is released from rest at the top of a straight chute 4 m long, inclined at 30° to the horizontal.

**(a)** Model the parcel as a particle on a smooth chute. Find its speed at the bottom. **[3]**
**(b)** The measured speed is 5.4 m s⁻¹. Say what this suggests about the model, then find the coefficient of friction needed to match it. **[3]**

**9.** A van makes a 360 km journey at a constant speed of v km h⁻¹. The running cost is £(30 + 0.00003v³) per hour, which includes the driver's pay.

**(a)** Show that the total cost is C = 10 800/v + 0.0108v². **[2]**
**(b)** Find the speed that minimises C, and justify that it is a minimum. **[4]**
**(c)** The road has a speed limit of 70 km h⁻¹. State the cheapest allowed speed and its cost, and compare it with the answer to part (b). **[2]**

**10.** A model for the number of subscribers N thousand, t months after a service launches, is N = 12e^(0.15t). The market contains at most 400 thousand potential subscribers.

**(a)** Find when the model gives N = 100. **[2]**
**(b)** Use t = 36 to show the model is unsuitable for long-term prediction. **[2]**
**(c)** At t = 24 the true value is 250. A refined model is N = 400 − 388e^(−kt). Find k to 3 significant figures, then predict N when t = 36. **[3]**

**11.** Weekly sales t weeks after launch are modelled by S = 5e^(0.4t) (model A) or S = 40 + 12t (model B).

**(a)** Let g(t) = 5e^(0.4t) − 40 − 12t. Show that g(t) = 0 has a root between 8 and 8.5. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that g(t) = 0 can be written t = ln((40 + 12t)/5)/0.4. Starting from t₀ = 8, find t₁ and t₂ to 3 decimal places. **[3]**
**(c)** Show that the root is 8.33 to 2 decimal places. **[2]**
**(d)** Interpret the root in context, and comment on the models at 20 weeks. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Specify: decide exactly what is wanted, for example the smallest number of copiers so that no job waits more than 10 minutes [1]. Collect: gather copies needed per day, copier speed, opening hours and cost [1]. Process and represent: set demand against capacity and calculate the smallest number that meets it [1]. Interpret: state the number in context, check it against budget and queue times, and repeat the cycle with changed assumptions if it is unaffordable or implausible [1].
*Examiner insight:* Answers are more likely to gain credit when each stage names an action specific to the copiers; generic phrases such as "do the maths" say little.

**2. (a)** P = 8 + 0.05m (pounds) [1]; Q = 14 + 0.02m [1].
**(b)** 8 + 0.05m = 14 + 0.02m, so 0.03m = 6 and **m = 200 minutes** [1].
**(c)** At 150 minutes: P = £15.50 and Q = £17.00 [1]. **Plan P is cheaper, by £1.50** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Mixing pence and pounds is a common slip; convert once at the start.

**3.** f(1.25) = 1.953125 + 5 − 7 = −0.047 [1]. f(1.3) = 2.197 + 5.2 − 7 = 0.397 [1]. f is continuous and changes sign, so a root lies in 1.25 < x < 1.3; every number in this interval rounds to **1.3** to 1 d.p. [1]
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" answer is written for a reader who has not been told the result, so give both values, the sign change, and the conclusion.

**4. (a)** Lower: 16.35 × 7.15 = 116.9025 [1]. Upper: 16.45 × 7.25 = 119.2625 [1].
**(b)** Both bounds round to 120 at 2 significant figures [1], so the area is **120 m² to 2 significant figures**; the product 118.08 would suggest more precision than the data has [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the bounds you use for each length; a bare final answer leaves nothing to credit if it is wrong.

**5.** Wall area = 2(20 + 12) × 5 = 320 m² [1]. Two coats: 640 m² [1]. Tins = 640 ÷ 40 = **16** [1]. Assumption: doors and windows are ignored, so the wall area is too large and **the estimate is too high** [1].
*Examiner insight:* An estimate question rewards the stated assumption and its direction of error, not just the number.

**6. (a)** Three forces: weight 4g = 39.2 N vertically down, normal reaction R perpendicular to the slope, tension T up the slope [1]. The 20° marked between the weight and the perpendicular to the slope, or between the slope and the horizontal [1].
**(b)** Along the slope: T = 4g sin 20° [1] = **13.4 N** [1].
**(c)** R = 4g cos 20° = **36.8 N** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Label each force and mark the angle; a labelled diagram helps you catch sin and cos swapped.

**7. (a)** Water leaves at a constant rate [1]; nothing enters the tank, and the 800 litres is the starting volume [1].
**(b)** 800 − 24t = 0, so **t = 33.3 minutes** [1].
**(c)** The model gives 320 litres at t = 20, but 395 was measured: the model underestimates the volume by 75 litres, so a constant outflow rate is not realistic [1].
**(d)** 560 = 800e^(−10k), so k = ln(800/560)/10 [1]; **k = 0.0357** [1]. At t = 20, V = 800e^(−0.714) = 392, which is much closer to 395 than 320 is, so **the exponential model fits better** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Comparing two models needs a number from each, not just the word "better".

**8. (a)** Along the chute: a = g sin 30° = 4.9 m s⁻² [1]. v² = 2(4.9)(4) = 39.2 [1]. **v = 6.26 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** The model predicts a higher speed than measured, which is consistent with friction being ignored [1]. Required acceleration: a = 5.4²/(2 × 4) = 3.645 [1]. With friction, a = g(sin 30° − μ cos 30°), so 3.645 = 4.9 − 8.487μ and **μ = 0.148** [1].
*Examiner insight:* The mass of the parcel cancels.

**9. (a)** Time = 360/v hours [1]. C = (360/v)(30 + 0.00003v³) = 10 800/v + 0.0108v² [1].
**(b)** dC/dv = −10 800/v² + 0.0216v = 0 [1], so v³ = 500 000 [1] and **v = 79.4 km h⁻¹** [1]. d²C/dv² = 21 600/v³ + 0.0216 > 0 for v > 0, so this is a minimum [1].
**(c)** C decreases for 0 < v < 79.4, so the cheapest allowed speed is the limit, **70 km h⁻¹** [1], with C = 154.29 + 52.92 = **£207** compared with £204 at 79.4 km h⁻¹ [1].
*Examiner insight:* The unconstrained optimum lies outside the allowed range, so the answer in context is the boundary value, and the reason should be stated.

**10. (a)** 12e^(0.15t) = 100, so t = ln(100/12)/0.15 [1] = **14.1 months** [1].
**(b)** At t = 36, N = 12e^(5.4) ≈ 2660 thousand [1], which is far above the market limit of 400 thousand, so unlimited growth is not a valid assumption in the long term [1].
**(c)** 400 − 388e^(−24k) = 250, so e^(−24k) = 150/388 [1], giving **k = 0.0396** [1]. At t = 36, N = 400 − 388e^(−1.426) = **307 thousand** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Quote the figure that breaks the assumption, here 2660 against 400.

**11. (a)** g(8) = 5e^(3.2) − 40 − 96 = −13.3 and g(8.5) = 5e^(3.4) − 40 − 102 = 7.82 [1]. g is continuous and changes sign, so there is a root between 8 and 8.5 [1].
**(b)** 5e^(0.4t) = 40 + 12t, so e^(0.4t) = (40 + 12t)/5 and t = ln((40 + 12t)/5)/0.4 [1]. **t₁ = 8.258** [1], **t₂ = 8.314** [1].
**(c)** g(8.325) = −0.208 and g(8.335) = 0.232 [1]. The sign change means the root lies in 8.325 < t < 8.335, which rounds to **8.33** [1].
**(d)** The models predict equal weekly sales after about 8.3 weeks, and model A predicts more after that; at 20 weeks model A gives about 14 900 and model B gives 280, so data must decide which to trust [1].
*Examiner insight:* Use two values of g to either side of the root for the accuracy argument. The iteration values alone do not prove the second decimal place.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing generic stage names in a cycle question without tying them to the situation.
- Saying "the model is unrealistic" without naming an assumption and its effect.
- Quoting a raw product with more figures than the given data supports.
- Showing a sign change at the interval ends without saying the function is continuous.
- Ignoring a restriction on the allowed range.
- Omitting a force or an angle from a force diagram.
- Giving an optimisation value with no units or meaning.
- Claiming an iteration has converged after only one or two steps.
