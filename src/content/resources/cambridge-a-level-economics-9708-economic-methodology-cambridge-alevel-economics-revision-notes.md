## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Social science | The systematic study of human behaviour and society |
| Model | A simplified representation of reality built on assumptions |
| Assumption | A simplifying condition taken as given in a model |
| Hypothesis | A testable prediction derived from a model |
| Positive statement | A statement of fact or prediction that can be tested against evidence |
| Normative statement | A statement containing a value judgement about what ought to be |
| Value judgement | An opinion based on beliefs about what is good or fair |
| Ceteris paribus | All other influences held constant while one changes |
| Short run | At least one factor of production is fixed |
| Long run | All factors of production are variable; technology is given |
| Very long run | Technology and the quality of factors can also change |

## Economics as a social science

**Method:** observe, model, predict, test, revise.

| Similarities to natural science | Differences |
|---|---|
| Models and hypotheses | Few controlled experiments |
| Testing against data | People learn and react to predictions |
| Revising theories | Data imprecise and revised |
| | Values shape questions and uses |

A model is judged by the usefulness of its predictions; unrealistic assumptions are a reason to doubt it in a particular case.

## Positive and normative: the rules

1. Test: can evidence check it? If yes, positive.
2. Truth does not matter: a false but testable claim is positive.
3. Any value judgement makes it normative, even with facts included.
4. Controversy does not make it normative.
5. Signal words for normative: should, ought, fair, unfair, too much, too little, better, worse, more important.

**Quick classification drill**

| Statement | Type |
|---|---|
| The price of rice rose 7% this year | Positive |
| Rice farmers deserve a subsidy | Normative |
| A rice subsidy would raise output by 5% | Positive |
| Taxes on the rich are unfair | Normative |
| Raising taxes on the rich would reduce inequality | Positive |
| Food prices have risen 7%, so the government must act | Normative |

**Why it matters:** economists can agree on positive analysis and still disagree on policy, because policy also needs value judgements.

## Ceteris paribus

- Needed because many influences change at once in real data.
- Allows the effect of one variable to be isolated.
- Every curve shift on a diagram assumes it.
- In evaluation, relax it: "if other factors also change, the effect could be larger or smaller".

**Quick example.** In one year the price of a magazine rises 8% while a rival magazine closes. Sales of the magazine rise 2%. Ceteris paribus, the price rise alone might have cut sales by 6%; the rival's closure on its own might have raised them by 8%. The observed +2% hides both effects.

## Time periods

| Period | Defined by | Example: a bus company |
|---|---|---|
| Short run | One factor fixed | Run existing buses more often with extra drivers |
| Long run | All factors variable | Buy more buses and build a new depot |
| Very long run | Technology changes too | Switch to a new type of bus technology |

- Periods are defined by what can change, not by months.
- Length differs by industry.
- Responses are usually larger in the long run.
- Costs and benefits may fall in different periods, which affects judgements.

## Exam traps

- Calling a false statement normative.
- Calling a statement with data in it positive when it contains "should".
- Naming ceteris paribus without stating what is held constant.
- Defining time periods in months.
- Forgetting the very long run.
- Treating the assumptions of a model as claims about reality rather than simplifications.

## Self-test

1. Why is economics called a social science?
2. Give two ways economics differs from a natural science.
3. What is an economic model?
4. Define a positive statement.
5. Is "inflation will fall if interest rates rise" positive or normative? Why?
6. Is "inflation is the most damaging economic problem" positive or normative? Why?
7. Is "unemployment rose by 2%, so the government should spend more" positive or normative?
8. What does ceteris paribus mean?
9. Why is ceteris paribus needed when analysing real data?
10. Define the short run.
11. What distinguishes the very long run from the long run?
12. Why might the effect of a price rise be larger in the long run?

### Answers

1. It studies human behaviour and choices systematically, using models and evidence.
2. Any two of: few controlled experiments; people react to predictions; data are imprecise; values shape questions.
3. A simplified representation of reality based on assumptions, used to explain and predict.
4. A statement of fact or a prediction that can be tested against evidence.
5. Positive: it is a testable prediction.
6. Normative: "most damaging" is a value judgement.
7. Normative: the fact is followed by a value judgement ("should").
8. Other things being equal: all other influences are held constant.
9. Many influences change at once, so it is needed to isolate the effect of one.
10. A period in which at least one factor of production is fixed.
11. In the very long run, technology and the quality of factors can change as well.
12. Buyers have time to find substitutes and change habits, such as replacing equipment.
