## Questions

**1.** Define supernormal profit and explain what is meant by normal profit. **[3]**

**2.** A perfectly competitive firm sells at a market price of 25 cu. Explain why its average revenue equals its marginal revenue, and describe the shape of its total revenue curve. **[3]**

**3.** A monopolist faces the demand curve P = 84 − 3Q.

**(a)** Find the marginal revenue function from total revenue. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the output at which total revenue is greatest. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate price elasticity of demand at that output. **[2]**

**4.** A perfectly competitive firm has total fixed costs of 60 cu. It sells 10 units at 14 cu with an average variable cost of 11 cu.

**(a)** Calculate its total revenue, total variable cost and loss. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain whether it should continue to produce in the short run. **[2]**

**5.** A competitive firm has TVC = Q³ − 12Q² + 50Q, so MC = 3Q² − 24Q + 50 and AVC reaches its lowest value at Q = 6.

**(a)** Calculate the short-run shutdown price. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the quantity supplied at 50 cu. **[2]**
**(c)** State the supply of 25 identical firms at 50 cu. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the firm supplies nothing at 10 cu. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why supernormal profit cannot last in the long run in perfect competition. **[4]**

**7.** Thirty identical firms each have TFC = 50 cu and TVC = Q³ − 8Q² + 25Q. Market demand has fallen to Qd = 250 − 10P, and the price is 12 cu. Each firm produces 13/3 units.

**(a)** Show that the market is in short-run equilibrium. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate each firm's loss. **[2]**
**(c)** Minimum long-run average cost is 20 cu at Q = 5. Calculate the long-run price and number of firms. **[3]**

**8.** A monopolist faces P = 94 − 5Q and has TC = 200 + 10Q + Q², so MC = 10 + 2Q.

**(a)** Find the output and price at which the monopolist maximises profit. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate supernormal profit. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the deadweight loss compared with the allocatively efficient output. **[3]**
**(d)** Output of 7 gives ATC of about 45.57 cu, while minimum ATC is about 38.28 cu. Explain what this shows. **[2]**

**9.** A firm in monopolistic competition faces P = 34 − 2Q and has TC = 192 + 4Q + Q². In the long run, demand settles at P = 52 − 2Q.

**(a)** Calculate its short-run output, price and loss. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain with figures why it continues to produce in the short run. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that profit is zero when demand is P = 52 − 2Q. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain why the firm is neither allocatively nor productively efficient there. **[2]**

**10.** A firm sells 50 units at 30 cu with ATC of 26 cu. The lowest cost achievable is 21 cu.

**(a)** Calculate supernormal profit. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the cost of X-inefficiency. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why X-inefficiency is more likely in monopoly than in perfect competition. **[3]**

**11.** Evaluate whether perfect competition is more efficient than monopolistic competition in the long run. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Supernormal profit is profit above normal profit, where total revenue exceeds total cost [1]. Normal profit is the minimum reward needed to keep the owner in the industry [1], earned when total revenue equals total cost, so it is counted as a cost [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition earns credit when it links profit to revenue and cost; writing that normal profit is "zero profit" without the opportunity cost idea is incomplete.

**2.** The firm is a price taker, so it can sell any quantity at 25 cu [1]. Each extra unit adds 25 cu, so MR = AR = 25 [1]. Total revenue is a straight line through the origin, rising by 25 per unit [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* State the reason (price taker) as well as the result; the equality alone is a statement, not an explanation.

**3. (a)** TR = 84Q − 3Q² [1], so MR = 84 − 6Q [1].
**(b)** MR = 0 gives Q = 14 [1].
**(c)** At Q = 14, P = 42 [1]. PED = (1/3) × (42/14) = **1** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Differentiate TR, not P; an MR that starts at 84 but falls at the same rate as demand is a common slip.

**4. (a)** TR = 14 × 10 = 140 [1]. TVC = 11 × 10 = 110 [1]. Loss = 140 − (60 + 110) = **30** [1].
**(b)** Revenue exceeds variable cost by 30, which contributes to fixed costs [1]. Closing would lose all 60, so continue [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The decision needs a comparison of the two losses or of price and AVC; stating the loss alone does not justify the conclusion.

**5. (a)** Shutdown price is minimum AVC [1]. AVC at Q = 6 is 36 − 72 + 50 = **14 cu** [1].
**(b)** 3Q² − 24Q + 50 = 50 [1], so Q = 8 on the rising part of MC [1].
**(c)** 25 × 8 = **200** [1].
**(d)** At 10 cu, price is below minimum AVC of 14 [1], so each unit loses money and closing loses less [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that supply follows MC only above minimum AVC; many answers describe the whole MC curve.

**6.** Supernormal profit attracts new firms because entry is free [1]. Industry supply shifts right [1], so market price falls [1]. Entry continues until price equals minimum ATC, where profit is normal [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A chain needs each link; jumping from "profit" to "price falls" without entry earns less credit.

**7. (a)** Qd = 250 − 120 = 130, and 30 × 13/3 = 130 [1].
**(b)** TR = 12 × 13/3 = 52, TVC ≈ 39.48 [1]. Loss = 50 + 39.48 − 52 ≈ **37.48** [1].
**(c)** Exit continues until P = 20 [1]. Qd = 250 − 200 = 50 [1]. Firms = 50 ÷ 5 = **10** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert market quantity into firms using the long-run output per firm; the price alone does not give the number of firms.

**8. (a)** MR = 94 − 10Q [1]. 94 − 10Q = 10 + 2Q gives Q = **7** [1]. P = **59** [1].
**(b)** TR = 413 [1]. TC = 200 + 70 + 49 = 319, so profit = **94** [1].
**(c)** P = MC: 94 − 5Q = 10 + 2Q gives Q = 12 [1]. MC at Q = 7 is 24, so the gap is 35 [1]. DWL = 0.5 × 35 × 5 = **87.5** [1].
**(d)** The firm produces where ATC is above its minimum, so it is not productively efficient [1]. The gap shows a monopolist can restrict output and sit above the lowest cost without wasting inputs [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Read price from demand at the monopoly output; using MC as the price removes most of the marks.

**9. (a)** MR = 34 − 4Q = 4 + 2Q gives Q = **5** [1]. P = **24** [1]. TR = 120, TC = 192 + 20 + 25 = 237, loss = **117** [1].
**(b)** AVC = 4 + 5 = 9, below price 24 [1]. Closing loses 192 of fixed costs, more than 117 [1].
**(c)** MR = 52 − 4Q = 4 + 2Q gives Q = 8, P = 36 [1]. ATC = 192/8 + 4 + 8 = 36 [1]. TR = TC, so profit = **0** [1].
**(d)** P = 36 > MC = 20, so allocatively inefficient [1]. Output 8 is below the 13.86 at minimum ATC, so it has excess capacity [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* "Show that" needs MR, MC, output, price and ATC all written down.

**10. (a)** (30 − 26) × 50 = **200** [2].
**(b)** (26 − 21) × 50 = **250** [2].
**(c)** Monopoly profit is protected by barriers, so waste can continue [1]. In perfect competition, a firm with higher costs makes losses and exits [1]. Rivals also set a price that exposes poor cost control [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare both structures in the same sentence; describing only the monopolist leaves the question half answered.

**11.** In the long run, perfect competition has P = MC = minimum ATC at Q = 5, so it is both allocatively and productively efficient [1][1]. Monopolistic competition has normal profit but P above MC [1], and output below the minimum-ATC level, so excess capacity [1]. However, monopolistic competition offers variety and consumers may value it [1]. Product differentiation also encourages innovation [1]. Few markets meet every condition of perfect competition, such as identical products [1]. A judgement: perfect competition is more efficient in cost terms, but the gain is smaller if variety is valued [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation earns credit when it weighs both sides and reaches a conclusion; listing benefits of variety without a judgement stays descriptive.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading the monopoly price from MC.
- Using ATC instead of AVC for shutdown decisions.
- Giving a loss without comparing it with the loss from closing.
- Forgetting that supply stops below minimum AVC.
- Stating efficiency verdicts without separating short run and long run.
- Writing "show that" answers without every step.
- Skipping the entry or exit link in long-run explanations.
- Evaluation with no judgement.
