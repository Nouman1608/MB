These notes condense **3.3.1.3 Isomerism** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, examined in Unit 2 and able to reappear in Unit 5. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [isomerism study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-1-3/); then try the [isomerism practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-1-3-practice/).

## The subtopic in four lines

1. Structural isomers: same molecular formula, different structural formula (chain, position, functional group).
2. Stereoisomers: same structural formula, different arrangement in space.
3. E–Z isomerism comes from restricted rotation about the planar C=C bond, and needs two different groups on each C=C carbon.
4. CIP rules: higher atomic number wins at the first point of difference; same side Z, opposite sides E.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Structural isomers | Same molecular formula, different structural formulas |
| Chain isomers | Different carbon skeletons (branching) |
| Position isomers | Same skeleton, functional group in a different position |
| Functional group isomers | Different functional groups |
| Stereoisomers | Same structural formula, different arrangement of atoms in space |
| E–Z isomerism | Stereoisomerism caused by restricted rotation about C=C |
| CIP priority | Ranking of groups by atomic number of the attached atoms |
| Z isomer | Higher-priority groups on the same side of C=C |
| E isomer | Higher-priority groups on opposite sides of C=C |

## Structural isomers: quick examples

| Type | Pair |
|---|---|
| Chain | hexane and 2-methylpentane (C₆H₁₄) |
| Position | 1-bromopropane and 2-bromopropane (C₃H₇Br) |
| Functional group | butanal and butanone (C₄H₈O) |
| Functional group | pent-1-ene and cyclopentane (C₅H₁₀) |

Branching lowers boiling point: fewer surface contacts, weaker van der Waals forces.

## Method: all the isomers of a formula

1. Which series fit the general formula? (CₙH₂ₙ: alkene or cycloalkane.)
2. Skeletons: straight chain, then one branch, then two branches; rings if allowed.
3. Functional group in every distinct position.
4. Name each one; equal names mean duplicates.
5. Check every alkene for E–Z isomers.

| Formula | Structural isomers | Total including E–Z |
|---|---|---|
| C₄H₁₀ | 2 | 2 |
| C₅H₁₂ | 3 | 3 |
| C₄H₉Br | 4 | 4 |
| C₄H₈ (chains and rings) | 5 | 6 |
| C₅H₁₀ alkenes only | 5 | 6 |

The C₅H₁₀ alkenes are pent-1-ene, pent-2-ene (E and Z), 2-methylbut-1-ene, 3-methylbut-1-ene and 2-methylbut-2-ene.

## E–Z: the test

- Is there a C=C? If not, no E–Z isomers.
- Does **each** C=C carbon carry two different groups? If either carries two identical groups, no E–Z isomers.
- Restricted rotation: turning about C=C would break the π bond (sideways p-orbital overlap).

**No E–Z:** but-1-ene (=CH₂ end), 2-methylbut-2-ene (=C(CH₃)₂ end), 2-methylpropene.
**E–Z:** but-2-ene, pent-2-ene, 1,2-dichloroethene, 3-methylpent-2-ene.

## CIP rules in three steps

1. Compare the atoms attached directly to each C=C carbon: higher atomic number wins.
2. Tie? Compare the sets of atoms one bond further out, highest first; the first difference decides.
3. Same side Z; opposite sides E.

| Contest | Winner | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Br vs CH₃ | Br | 35 > 6 |
| CH₂CH₃ vs CH₃ | CH₂CH₃ | (C, H, H) beats (H, H, H) |
| CH₂OH vs CH(CH₃)₂ | CH₂OH | (O, H, H) beats (C, C, H): O > C |
| CH₂Cl vs CH₂OH | CH₂Cl | (Cl, H, H) beats (O, H, H) |
| OH vs CH₂Br | OH | first atom O (8) beats C (6) |

## Exam traps

- "Same formula, different structure" is not a definition. Say molecular and structural.
- Draw each isomer once: 1-chlorobutane and 4-chlorobutane are the same compound.
- A bent drawing of a straight chain is not a branched isomer.
- CIP is decided by atomic number, not by the size of the group.
- Cis does not always mean Z (2-bromobut-2-ene with both CH₃ on one side is E).
- Rings count as isomers of alkenes with the same formula.
- E–Z diagrams need clear 120° geometry so that "same side" is unambiguous.

## Self-test

Answer without looking, then check below.

1. Define structural isomers.
2. Define stereoisomers.
3. Name the type of isomerism shown by butan-1-ol and 2-methylpropan-1-ol.
4. Name the type of isomerism shown by propanal and propanone.
5. Explain why E–Z isomerism occurs.
6. Does pent-1-ene show E–Z isomerism? Explain.
7. How many structural isomers does C₃H₇Cl have? Name them.
8. Name a cyclic isomer of but-1-ene.
9. Which has the higher CIP priority: CH₂CH₂CH₃ or CH(CH₃)₂?
10. In ClCH=CHCH₃, Cl and CH₃ are on opposite sides. Is this E or Z?
11. Why does 2-methylbut-2-ene have no E–Z isomers?
12. Which of these would you expect to have the lowest boiling point: pentane, 2-methylbutane, 2,2-dimethylpropane? Why?

### Answers

1. Compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural formulas.
2. Compounds with the same structural formula but a different arrangement of atoms in space.
3. Chain isomerism.
4. Functional group isomerism.
5. Rotation about the C=C double bond is restricted, so groups cannot swap sides.
6. No: C1 carries two hydrogen atoms.
7. Two: 1-chloropropane and 2-chloropropane.
8. Cyclobutane (or methylcyclopropane).
9. CH(CH₃)₂: its attached atoms are (C, C, H) against (C, H, H).
10. E: Cl (17) beats H on C1, CH₃ beats H on C2, and they are on opposite sides.
11. C2 carries two identical CH₃ groups.
12. 2,2-dimethylpropane: most branched, so fewest surface contacts and weakest van der Waals forces.
