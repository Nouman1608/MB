These notes condense **3.2.1 Scalars and vectors** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [scalars and vectors study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-scalars-and-vectors-oxfordaqa/); for exam-style questions, use the [scalars and vectors practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-scalars-and-vectors-oxfordaqa-practice/).

## The specification in one box

- The nature of scalars and vectors: velocity/speed, mass, force/weight, acceleration, displacement/distance.
- Addition of vectors by calculation or scale drawing.
- Conditions for equilibrium for two or three coplanar forces acting at a point.
- Objects at rest or moving with constant velocity are in equilibrium.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Scalar | Quantity with magnitude only |
| Vector | Quantity with magnitude and direction |
| Resultant | The single vector equivalent to two or more vectors added together |
| Component | The part of a vector acting along a chosen direction |
| Resolving | Splitting a vector into two perpendicular components |
| Coplanar | Acting in the same plane |
| Equilibrium | Zero resultant force (for forces at a point) |

## Scalar–vector pairs

| Scalar | Vector |
|---|---|
| distance (m) | displacement (m) |
| speed (m s⁻¹) | velocity (m s⁻¹) |
| mass (kg) | weight (N) |
| — | force (N), acceleration (m s⁻²) |

Same unit does not mean same quantity: distance and displacement are both in metres.

## Methods

**Two perpendicular vectors**

- Magnitude: R = √(a² + b²).
- Direction: tan θ = opposite/adjacent; say what θ is measured from.

**Resolving a vector F at θ to a reference line**

- Along the line: F cos θ.
- Perpendicular to it: F sin θ.

**Any two vectors**

1. Resolve each into x and y components.
2. Add the x components; add the y components.
3. Recombine with Pythagoras and tan θ.

**Change in a vector**

- Δv = v − u: add the reverse of u to v.

**Scale drawing**

- State the scale; draw tip to tail with ruler and protractor; measure the resultant and its angle; convert with the scale.

## Equilibrium checklist

| Situation | Condition |
|---|---|
| Two forces at a point | Equal size, opposite direction, same line |
| Three forces at a point | Form a closed triangle tip to tail |
| Any number of forces | Sum of components = 0 in each of two perpendicular directions |
| Moving object | In equilibrium only if velocity is constant (size and direction) |

## Worked reminders

- **Range of resultants.** 8 N and 6 N can combine to anything from 2 N (opposite) to 14 N (same direction); at right angles, 10 N.
- **Rebound.** A ball hits a wall at 5.0 m s⁻¹ and rebounds at 4.0 m s⁻¹. Taking towards the wall as positive, Δv = −4.0 − (+5.0) = −9.0 m s⁻¹: 9.0 m s⁻¹ away from the wall.
- **Symmetric suspension.** A 40 N lamp on two strings each at 30° to the horizontal: 2T sin 30° = 40, so T = 40 N.
- **River crossing.** A boat at 4.0 m s⁻¹ north in a 3.0 m s⁻¹ eastward current: 5.0 m s⁻¹ at 36.9° east of north.

## Exam traps

- Weight is a vector; mass is a scalar.
- Adding non-parallel magnitudes directly.
- Swapping sin and cos when the angle is measured from the vertical.
- Δv = u − v instead of v − u.
- Resultant with no direction, or an angle with no reference.
- "Constant speed" is not "constant velocity": circular motion at steady speed is not equilibrium.
- A rope cannot be horizontal and support a weight: the tension would need to be infinite.

## Quick self-test

1. Classify as scalar or vector: mass, weight, speed, displacement.
2. Find the resultant of 5.0 N east and 12 N north.
3. Resolve a 50 N force acting at 30° above the horizontal.
4. Resolve an 80 N force acting at 20° to the vertical.
5. What is the largest and smallest possible resultant of 8 N and 6 N?
6. A car travels 3.0 km north then 4.0 km west. Give its distance travelled and its displacement.
7. A ball hits a floor at 7.0 m s⁻¹ and rebounds at 5.0 m s⁻¹. Find the change in velocity.
8. State the condition for two forces acting at a point to be in equilibrium.
9. What shape do three forces in equilibrium make when drawn tip to tail?
10. Is a lift moving upwards at a steady 2 m s⁻¹ in equilibrium? Explain.
11. A 60 N weight hangs from two strings, each at 45° to the horizontal. Find the tension in each.
12. A force of 25 N has a horizontal component of 20 N. Find its vertical component and its angle to the horizontal.

### Answers

1. Mass and speed are **scalars**; weight and displacement are **vectors**.
2. √(5.0² + 12²) = **13 N**, at tan⁻¹(12/5.0) = **67° north of east**.
3. Horizontal 50 cos 30° = **43 N**; vertical 50 sin 30° = **25 N**.
4. Vertical 80 cos 20° = **75 N**; horizontal 80 sin 20° = **27 N**.
5. Largest **14 N**; smallest **2 N**.
6. Distance **7.0 km**; displacement √(3.0² + 4.0²) = **5.0 km at 53° west of north**.
7. Taking down as positive: −5.0 − 7.0 = **−12 m s⁻¹**, i.e. 12 m s⁻¹ upwards.
8. Equal in size, opposite in direction, along the same line.
9. A **closed triangle**.
10. **Yes**: its velocity is constant, so the resultant force is zero.
11. 2T sin 45° = 60, so T = **42 N**.
12. √(25² − 20²) = **15 N**; cos θ = 20/25, so θ = **37°**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculations that give a magnitude but no direction.
- Scale drawings with no stated scale or a diagram too small to measure.
- Treating a moving object as necessarily unbalanced.
