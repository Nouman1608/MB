**HL only.** These notes cover Regional study 9, Europe and the Second World War (1918 to 1949), in the Europe option examined on HL Paper 3. The country case for the war's impact is France. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-europe-second-world-war/) for fuller explanation.

## What Paper 3 wants

Two 15-mark essays from different regional studies in two hours. Aim for in-depth understanding of the argument, a thorough, balanced evaluation of perspectives and a substantiated judgement. Handle the persecution and murder of marginalized groups with precision and care.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Collective security | Members acting together against an aggressor |
| Appeasement | Giving way to demands to avoid war |
| Anschluss | Union of Germany and Austria (1938) |
| Lebensraum | "Living space" in the east sought by Hitler |
| Lend-Lease | US scheme supplying Allies from 1941 |
| Einsatzgruppen | SS mobile killing units in the east |
| Wannsee Conference | 20 January 1942 meeting coordinating the "Final Solution" |
| Vichy | Collaborating French regime under Pétain |
| Righteous Among the Nations | Non-Jews honoured for rescuing Jews |
| Genocide | Acts intended to destroy a national, ethnic, racial or religious group |

## League of Nations

- **Structure:** Assembly, Council, Secretariat; unanimity; no army; USA absent.
- **1920s:** Åland success; Vilna and Corfu failures against stronger states.
- **Collective security:** Locarno and Kellogg-Briand outside the League; Disarmament Conference failed.
- **1930s:** Manchuria; Abyssinia (weak sanctions; Hoare-Laval); League lost credibility.

## Causes of the war

| Factor | Points |
|---|---|
| Italy | Abyssinia; Albania; Axis and Pact of Steel |
| Germany | Overturn Versailles; rearmament; Rhineland; Anschluss; Sudetenland; Czechoslovakia; Lebensraum |
| Appeasement | War fear, Depression, weak forces, belief Versailles harsh, anticommunism |
| Munich | Czechoslovakia excluded; time for rearmament or encouragement of Hitler |
| Soviet role | Collective security attempts; excluded from Munich; Pact buys time |
| Poland | Guarantee in March 1939; invasion 1 September 1939 |

## Allied victory: ranking factors

- **Leadership:** Grand Alliance and unconditional surrender; Hitler's interference; poor Axis coordination.
- **Economy:** US output and Lend-Lease; Soviet industry in the east; Germany's late total mobilisation and oil shortage.
- **Strategy:** Britain not defeated; two fronts; Stalingrad; Kursk; North Africa; Italy; Normandy.
- **Strengths and weaknesses:** Axis early speed against overstretch; Allied resources and unity.

## France as the case

| Theme | Points |
|---|---|
| Social and economic | Defeat in 1940; occupation costs; shortages; forced labour; damage in 1944 |
| Political | Vichy and collaboration; de Gaulle and the Resistance; Fourth Republic (1946) |
| Women | Hardship; Resistance roles; head-shaving; vote granted 1944 |
| Marginalized groups | Vichy anti-Jewish laws; about 75,000 Jews deported; Vel d'Hiv round-up |
| Reconstruction | Nationalisations; social security; Monnet Plan; Marshall aid |

## Persecution and murder: reasons

- **Long-term:** religious then racial antisemitism; Nazi racial ideology; stages of persecution (1933 to 1938).
- **Military:** Poland (ghettos); USSR (Einsatzgruppen, Babi Yar).
- **Political:** Göring's directive to Heydrich (July 1941); Wannsee (January 1942); extermination camps.
- **Collaboration:** German institutions; local police and officials; collaborating governments.
- **Debates:** intentionalists against functionalists; Browning against Goldhagen.

## Responses

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Individuals | Rescuers such as Sendler and Wallenberg; hiding families; Le Chambon |
| Collective | Warsaw Ghetto Uprising; Sobibor revolt; partisans; Danish rescue |
| International | Evian (1938); declaration of December 1942; War Refugee Board (1944); no bombing of the camps |
| Legal | Nuremberg Trials; later trials; Genocide Convention (1948) |

## Worked example: planning an essay

**Question:** "To what extent was the policy of appeasement responsible for the outbreak of war in 1939?"

1. **Thesis:** Appeasement allowed Hitler to grow stronger and encouraged him, but responsibility lay mainly with Nazi aims, with other factors making war likely.
2. **Paragraph 1, appeasement:** Rhineland, Anschluss, Munich showed Hitler that threats worked; allies lost Czech defences.
3. **Paragraph 2, Nazi aims:** Lebensraum, rearmament, the Pact of 1939.
4. **Paragraph 3, other powers:** weak League, Soviet exclusion, Italy's expansion, divided western forces.
5. **Perspective:** critics (Churchill, "Guilty Men") against revisionists who defend Chamberlain as realistic.
6. **Judgement:** appeasement contributed but did not cause the war.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the Holocaust as a single decision rather than a process.
- Using "Nazi" and "German" as the same word without care.
- Judging Chamberlain with hindsight alone.
- Writing about the whole war when the question asks for one country.
- Giving figures without "about" where they are estimates.

## Self-test

1. Name one League success and one failure in the 1920s.
2. Why was the Soviet Union not at Munich?
3. What did the Nazi-Soviet Pact include in secret?
4. Name two reasons for Allied victory.
5. What was decided at Wannsee?
6. What was the main legal response in 1945 to 1946?

**Answers:** 1. Åland (success); Vilna or Corfu (failure). 2. It was not invited, as western powers distrusted it. 3. A protocol dividing eastern Europe into spheres of influence. 4. For example greater economic resources and Hitler's overstretch. 5. The coordination of the administration of the "Final Solution". 6. The Nuremberg Trials.
