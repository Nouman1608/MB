## Questions

**1.** State three groups of organism that are usually called pathogens, and state whether archaea are known to cause human disease. **[3]**

**2.** Explain how skin and mucous membranes help to prevent infection. **[3]**

**3.** Outline how a cut in the skin is sealed by blood clotting. **[4]**

**4.** Distinguish between the innate and the adaptive immune system. **[3]**

**5.** Describe how a phagocyte removes bacteria from an infected wound. **[4]**

**6.** Explain why a B-cell needs contact with a helper T-cell as well as binding to its antigen before it can make antibody. **[4]**

**7.** One activated B-cell divides every 8 hours, and every cell keeps dividing.

**(a)** Calculate the number of cells after 72 hours. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the least time needed to reach 5000 cells. **[2]**

**8.** In a fictional experiment a volunteer receives the same harmless antigen twice. Peak antibody concentration was 25 arbitrary units after dose one and 800 after dose two.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in peak concentration. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the difference between the two responses. **[3]**

**9.** **(a)** State two ways in which HIV can be transmitted. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a person with advanced HIV infection suffers from opportunistic infections. **[3]**

**10.** A hospital culture contains 25 bacteria resistant to an antibiotic.

**(a)** Explain why the antibiotic blocks bacterial growth without harming human cells. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the same antibiotic does not treat a virus infection. **[2]**
**(c)** The antibiotic kills all non-resistant bacteria. The resistant bacteria divide every 30 minutes. Calculate their number after 5 hours. **[2]**
**(d)** State one action that slows the spread of multiresistant bacteria. **[1]**

**11.** **(a)** Describe how rabies is transferred to humans and why it is a zoonosis. **[2]**
**(b)** A vaccine contains messenger RNA coding for a surface protein of a virus. Explain how it can give immunity without causing the disease. **[3]**

**12.** Fictional data from the town of Brackenfield during an outbreak of a respiratory virus: weekly cases were 1250 in week 2 and 3500 in week 6. Hospital admissions per 100 000 people were 60 in the unvaccinated group and 15 in the vaccinated group. Of 6000 residents, 4200 were immune. A newspaper claims "vaccination caused the lower admissions".

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in weekly cases from week 2 to week 6. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage difference between the two admission rates. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage change in admissions from unvaccinated to vaccinated. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the percentage of residents who were immune. **[1]**
**(e)** Evaluate the newspaper's claim. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three of viruses, bacteria, fungi, protists [2]. Archaea are not known to cause any human disease [1].
*Examiner insight:* A list of three needs three different groups; naming two bacteria is still one group.

**2.** Skin is a physical barrier: tough keratinised outer layer that microorganisms cannot easily cross [1]. Skin secretions are chemical barriers that discourage microbial growth [1]. Mucus on membranes traps microorganisms, and cilia move it away [1].
*Examiner insight:* Say which point is physical and which is chemical, because the question asks you to explain how the barrier works.

**3.** Platelets release clotting factors [1]. A cascade of reactions follows [1]. Thrombin converts soluble fibrinogen to insoluble fibrin [1]. Fibrin traps erythrocytes, forming a clot that seals the cut [1].
*Examiner insight:* For an outline, the order matters, so write the steps in the order they happen.

**4.** Innate responds to broad categories of pathogen; adaptive responds to a specific pathogen [1]. Innate does not change during life; adaptive becomes more effective after exposure [1]. Innate has no memory; adaptive builds memory cells [1].
*Examiner insight:* A distinguish answer states both sides; writing only the adaptive features leaves the comparison unfinished.

**5.** Phagocyte moves from the blood to the wound by amoeboid movement [1]. It recognises the bacteria as foreign [1]. It engulfs them by endocytosis into a vesicle [1]. Lysosome enzymes digest the bacteria [1].
*Examiner insight:* Name the process (endocytosis) and the organelle (lysosome); a vague phrase such as "eats the bacteria" is weaker.

**6.** Activation needs two signals: the specific antigen [1] and a helper T-cell that has been activated by the same type of antigen [1]. Without both, the B-cell is not activated [1], so it neither forms plasma cells that secrete antibody nor memory cells [1].
*Examiner insight:* Mention that the helper T-cell must respond to the same antigen type, since the link to specificity is the point.

**7. (a)** 72 ÷ 8 = 9 divisions [1]. 2⁹ = **512 cells [1]**
**(b)** 2¹² = 4096 (too few), 2¹³ = 8192, so 13 divisions [1]. 13 × 8 = **104 hours [1]**
*Examiner insight:* In a doubling question, round the number of divisions up to a whole number, because a part-division does not exist, and show the working.

**8. (a)** (800 − 25) ÷ 25 × 100 [1] = **3100% [1]**
**(b)** First dose: few matching B-cells, so a slower clone build-up and lower peak [1]. First dose produced memory cells [1]. Second dose: memory cells respond faster, forming many plasma cells and a larger antibody peak [1].
*Examiner insight:* Quote the data and say "memory cells", because a bare "the body remembers" does not name the cells responsible.

**9. (a)** Any two of unprotected sex, shared needles, mother to child, unscreened blood transfusion, breast milk [2]
**(b)** HIV infects and kills helper T-cells [1]. Fewer B-cells are activated, so less antibody is made [1]. Microorganisms that rarely harm a healthy person then cause infections [1].
*Examiner insight:* Name helper T-cells, not "white blood cells", and link the loss to antibody production.

**10. (a)** It blocks a process that bacteria have, such as building a cell wall or bacterial ribosome function [1]. Human cells lack that process or structure [1].
**(b)** Viruses have no metabolism of their own or ribosomes and use the host cell's machinery [1], so there is no bacterial process for the antibiotic to block [1].
**(c)** 5 ÷ 0.5 = 10 divisions [1]. 25 × 2¹⁰ = **25 600 [1]**
**(d)** Any one of: finish the prescribed course, use only when needed, avoid routine use in farm animals [1].
*Examiner insight:* An explanation needs both parts: what the drug targets and why the host or virus lacks that target.

**11. (a)** Virus passes in saliva through a bite from an infected mammal [1]. It is a zoonosis because the infection transfers from other species to humans [1].
**(b)** The mRNA enters cells, which make the viral protein [1]. The immune system treats it as an antigen and activates lymphocytes [1]. Memory cells form, but no whole virus is present, so no disease [1].
*Examiner insight:* Do not say the vaccine contains antibodies; say it supplies an antigen or the code for one.

**12. (a)** (3500 − 1250) ÷ 1250 × 100 [1] = **+180% [1]**
**(b)** Difference 45, mean 37.5 [1]; 45 ÷ 37.5 × 100 = **120% [1]**
**(c)** (15 − 60) ÷ 60 × 100 = **−75% [1]**
**(d)** 4200 ÷ 6000 × 100 = **70% [1]**
**(e)** Vaccinated people had lower admission rates (60 against 15 per 100 000), which supports a link [1]. Correlation does not show cause: the groups may differ in age or health [1]. The difference in admissions may be partly due to other factors, and the report may precede evaluation by other scientists [1]. Judgement: the data are consistent with a protective effect but do not alone prove the claim [1].
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation scores best with a limitation, a supporting point and a reasoned judgement, not a simple yes or no.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing organisms of one group when asked for different groups of pathogen.
- Saying vaccines contain antibodies, or that antibiotics kill viruses.
- Omitting the helper T-cell from B-cell activation.
- Writing "white blood cells" when helper T-cells are needed.
- Not showing the substitution in a percentage change calculation.
- Using the wrong starting value, which changes the sign and size.
- Failing to round a number of divisions up to a whole number.
- Concluding that two trends moving together prove one caused the other.
