These questions cover section 6.1 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: the effect on the mean and standard deviation of adding a constant to each observation and of multiplying each observation by a constant. Revise with the [study guide on adding or multiplying by a constant](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-effect-of-adding-or-multiplying-by-a-constant/) or the [revision notes on adding or multiplying by a constant](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-effect-of-adding-or-multiplying-by-a-constant-revision-notes/). Standard deviations use the divide-by-n form. All data are invented.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** A set of data has mean 12 and standard deviation 3. Every value is increased by 5. State the new mean and the new standard deviation. **[2]**

**2.** A set of data has mean 2.5 and standard deviation 0.6. Every value is multiplied by 4. Find the new mean, standard deviation and variance. **[3]**

**3.** A variable x has mean 20 and standard deviation 4. Find the mean and standard deviation of y = 2x − 7. **[2]**

**4.** A variable x has mean 6 and standard deviation 2. Find the mean and standard deviation of y = −3x. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why subtracting the same number from every value in a data set does not change its standard deviation. **[2]**

**6.** The heights of a group of pupils have mean 165 cm and standard deviation 8 cm. Find the mean and standard deviation in metres. **[2]**

## Section B: longer questions

**7.** The masses, in grams, of five loaves are 2004, 2011, 1998, 2007 and 2010.

**(a)** Using u = x − 2000, find the mean and standard deviation of u. **[3]**

**(b)** Hence state the mean and standard deviation of the masses. **[1]**

**8.** The weekly wages of the staff of a shop have mean 380 and standard deviation 42 (units of currency).

**(a)** Every wage is increased by 5%. Find the new mean and standard deviation. **[2]**

**(b)** After the 5% increase, every worker also receives a fixed bonus of 20. Find the mean and standard deviation of the final wages. **[1]**

**(c)** If the bonus of 20 had been added before the 5% increase, find the mean and standard deviation of the final wages. **[2]**

**9.** For eight values of x, Σx = 96 and Σx² = 1240.

**(a)** Find the mean and variance of x. **[2]**

**(b)** Each value is transformed by y = 5x + 3. Find the mean and standard deviation of y. **[2]**

**10.** A survey records ratings on a scale of 1 to 5. The ratings have mean 3.8 and standard deviation 0.9. The researcher reverses the scale using y = 6 − x.

**(a)** Find the mean and standard deviation of the reversed ratings. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the standard deviation is unchanged. **[1]**

**11.** A test is marked out of 40. The marks have mean 26 and standard deviation 6.

**(a)** The marks are converted to percentages. Find the mean and standard deviation of the percentages. **[2]**

**(b)** Every pupil is then given 3 extra percentage points. Find the new mean and standard deviation. **[1]**

**12.** A data set has median 15, interquartile range 6 and variance 9. Each value is transformed by y = 2x + 10.

**(a)** Find the median and interquartile range of y. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the variance of y, and explain why it is not simply doubled. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Mean 12 + 5 = 17 [1]; standard deviation 3 [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Mean 4 × 2.5 = 10 [1]; standard deviation 4 × 0.6 = 2.4 [1]; variance 2.4² = 5.76 (or 16 × 0.36) [1]. **[3]**

**3.** Mean 2 × 20 − 7 = 33 [1]; standard deviation 2 × 4 = 8 [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Mean −3 × 6 = −18 [1]; standard deviation 3 × 2 = 6 [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Every value and the mean are reduced by the same amount [1], so each deviation from the mean (and therefore the spread) is unchanged [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Mean 1.65 m [1]; standard deviation 0.08 m [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** u = 4, 11, −2, 7, 10; Σu = 30, ū = 6 [1]. Σu² = 16 + 121 + 4 + 49 + 100 = 290 [1]; variance = 290/5 − 36 = 22, so σ = 4.69 g [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Mean 2006 g; standard deviation 4.69 g [1]. **[1]**

**8. (a)** Mean 1.05 × 380 = 399 [1]; standard deviation 1.05 × 42 = 44.1 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Mean 419; standard deviation 44.1 [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** y = 1.05(x + 20): mean 1.05 × 400 = 420 [1]; standard deviation 44.1 [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The order changes the mean only; the bonus never affects the spread.

**9. (a)** Mean 96/8 = 12 [1]; variance 1240/8 − 144 = 155 − 144 = 11 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Mean 5 × 12 + 3 = 63 [1]; variance 25 × 11 = 275, so standard deviation √275 = 16.6 [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** Mean 6 − 3.8 = 2.2 [1]; standard deviation 0.9 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Reversing the scale reflects the values but keeps every distance between them, so the deviations from the mean have the same sizes [1]. **[1]**

**11. (a)** Multiply by 100/40 = 2.5 [1]: mean 65, standard deviation 15 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Mean 68; standard deviation 15 [1]. **[1]**

**12. (a)** Median 2 × 15 + 10 = 40 [1]; IQR 2 × 6 = 12 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Variance 2² × 9 = 36 [1]. The variance is the mean of the squared deviations, and each deviation is doubled, so each squared deviation is multiplied by 4 [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding the constant to the standard deviation.
- Multiplying the variance by b rather than b².
- Not undoing coding at the end.
- Treating a percentage increase as adding a fixed amount.
- Quoting a negative standard deviation.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to show working for "state" questions?**
A brief reason, such as "adding 5 does not change the spread", protects the mark and costs little time.

**How do I handle "decrease by 10%"?**
Multiply by 0.9: the mean and the standard deviation are both multiplied by 0.9.
