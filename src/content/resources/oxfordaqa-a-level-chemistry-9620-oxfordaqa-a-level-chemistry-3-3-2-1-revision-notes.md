## The subtopic in four lines

1. Alkanes are **saturated hydrocarbons**: C and H only, C–C single bonds only.
2. Petroleum is a **mixture**, mainly of alkanes, separated by **fractional distillation**.
3. Separation works because boiling point rises with chain length (stronger van der Waals forces).
4. It is a **physical** process: no covalent bonds break.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Hydrocarbon | Compound of carbon and hydrogen only |
| Saturated | Only single carbon–carbon bonds |
| Alkane | Saturated hydrocarbon; chains fit CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ |
| Petroleum (crude oil) | Mixture consisting mainly of alkane hydrocarbons |
| Fraction | Mixture of hydrocarbons with boiling points in a narrow range |
| Fractional distillation | Separation of a liquid mixture by boiling point using a column with a temperature gradient |
| Temperature gradient | Hot at the base of the column, cooler towards the top |
| Van der Waals forces | Weak attractions between molecules caused by temporary and induced dipoles |
| Volatility | How readily a liquid evaporates |
| Viscosity | Resistance to flow |

## Alkane facts

- General formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (chains). Mr = 14n + 2.
- Tetrahedral carbon, about 109.5°; zig-zag chains.
- C–H bonds almost non-polar, so only van der Waals forces act between molecules.
- C1 to C4 are gases at room temperature; C5 upwards are liquids; long chains are waxy solids.

## Boiling point: the explanation chain

Longer chain → more electrons and more surface contact → stronger van der Waals forces between molecules → more energy to separate them → higher boiling point.

Branching (same formula): more compact → fewer contacts → weaker van der Waals forces → lower boiling point.

| Compound | bp / °C (approx.) |
|---|---|
| butane | −1 |
| pentane | 36 |
| 2-methylbutane | 28 |
| 2,2-dimethylpropane | 10 |
| hexane | 69 |
| 2-methylpentane | 60 |
| 2,2-dimethylbutane | 50 |

## The column in six steps

1. Crude oil heated in a furnace; mostly vaporised.
2. Vapour enters near the base.
3. Column is hottest at the base, coolest at the top.
4. Vapour rises and cools.
5. Each hydrocarbon condenses at the first level that is below its boiling point, and is drawn off there.
6. Short chains (low bp) leave near the top or as gases; long chains (high bp) condense low down; residue leaves the bottom.

## Trends going down the column

| Property | Trend | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| Chain length | increases | — |
| Boiling point | increases | stronger van der Waals forces |
| Volatility | decreases | more energy needed to escape the liquid |
| Viscosity | increases | molecules held together more strongly |
| Ease of ignition | decreases | less vapour forms |

## Answer frame for "explain the difference in boiling point"

1. Compare the molecules: longer chain (more electrons) or more branched (fewer contacts).
2. Name the force: van der Waals forces between molecules.
3. Compare its strength: stronger or weaker.
4. Link to energy: more or less energy is needed to separate the molecules.

Example: "Octane has a longer chain than pentane, so it has more electrons and more surface contact; the van der Waals forces between octane molecules are stronger, so more energy is needed to separate them."

## Exam traps

- "Saturated" means single C–C bonds only, not "contains lots of hydrogen".
- A fraction is a mixture, not a pure compound.
- No bonds are broken in fractional distillation; that is cracking.
- Boiling involves overcoming forces **between** molecules, not breaking bonds **within** them.
- Name **van der Waals** forces; "intermolecular forces" alone may not be enough.
- The column is hot at the bottom; small molecules come off at the top.
- Chloroalkanes and alcohols are not hydrocarbons.

## Self-test

Answer without looking, then check below.

1. Define a hydrocarbon.
2. What does "saturated" mean?
3. Give the molecular formula of the alkane with 15 carbon atoms.
4. An alkane has Mr = 128.0. Find n.
5. Why is petroleum described as a mixture?
6. What is a fraction?
7. Where in the column is it hottest?
8. Explain why decane has a higher boiling point than hexane.
9. Explain why 2-methylbutane boils at a lower temperature than pentane.
10. A tray is at 110 °C. Will octane (bp about 126 °C) condense there if it reaches it as vapour? Explain.
11. Which is more viscous, a C₁₈ fraction or a C₈ fraction? Why?
12. Why is fractional distillation a physical process?

### Answers

1. A compound containing carbon and hydrogen only.
2. Only single carbon–carbon bonds are present.
3. C₁₅H₃₂.
4. 14n + 2 = 128.0, so n = 9 (C₉H₂₀).
5. It contains many different compounds that are not chemically combined, and they can be separated by physical means.
6. A mixture of hydrocarbons with boiling points within a narrow range.
7. At the base (bottom).
8. Decane molecules are longer, with more electrons and more surface contact, so the van der Waals forces between them are stronger and need more energy to overcome.
9. 2-methylbutane is branched and more compact, so it has fewer surface contacts and weaker van der Waals forces.
10. Yes: the tray temperature is below octane's boiling point, so the vapour condenses.
11. The C₁₈ fraction: stronger van der Waals forces between longer molecules make it flow less easily.
12. No covalent bonds are broken; molecules are separated by boiling point and condense unchanged.
