## Formula sheet

```
Dividend = profit for the year x share paid out
Percentage change = (new - old) / old x 100
Pay ratio = highest pay / average employee pay
Lost profit from lost sales = lost revenue x margin
```

## Corporate culture

- **Culture:** shared values, beliefs and habits; "the way we do things here".
- **Strong:** widely shared and understood; consistent behaviour; often loyal staff; but can resist change.
- **Weak:** little shared identity; control by rules and supervision; inconsistent service.

| Type | Key feature | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Power | Central control by a person or small group | Quick decisions | Depends on the leader |
| Role | Rules, procedures, job descriptions | Stable and predictable | Bureaucratic and slow to change |
| Task | Teams solve problems; expertise valued | Flexible and innovative | Can be costly; competition for resources |
| Person | Individuals at the centre | Valued, satisfied staff | Hard to co-ordinate |

- **Formed by:** founder's values, history, leadership, industry, size and structure, recruitment, rewards and symbols.
- **Hard to change because:** deeply embedded; staff resist loss of status or habit; takes years; leaders may slip back; mergers bring clashes of culture.
- **Easing change:** explain the reasons, involve staff, change rewards, lead by example.

## Stakeholders and shareholders

| | Shareholder model | Stakeholder model |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Share price and dividends | All groups affected |
| View of other groups | Matter if they help profit | Have legitimate interests of their own |
| Time frame | Often shorter term | Often longer term |
| Risk | Neglect of staff, communities, environment | Lower returns to owners; slower decisions |

- **Internal stakeholders:** owners, managers, employees.
- **External stakeholders:** customers, suppliers, lenders, community, government, pressure groups.
- **Conflict:** shareholders want dividends, staff want pay, the community wants lower emissions; all draw on the same profit.
- **Resolving conflict:** negotiate, compromise, prioritise by power and interest, communicate openly.

## Business ethics

- **Ethics:** principles of right and wrong in business behaviour.
- **Trade-off:** more profit from lower-cost, less ethical choices against the risk of lost sales, fines and reputation.
- **Ethical advantages:** loyal customers who pay more, staff recruitment and retention, lower risk of scandal.
- **Ethical disadvantages:** higher costs; less profit in the short term; rivals who cut corners may undercut you.

### Pay and rewards

- Issues: living wage, fairness across roles and genders, executive pay compared with staff pay.
- Defence of high executive pay: responsibility, results, competition for talent.
- Criticism: rewards short-term share price gains; widens inequality; may demotivate staff.
- Linking rewards to long-term and ethical targets aligns managers with stakeholders.

### Corporate social responsibility

| Benefits | Costs and risks |
|---|---|
| Stronger brand and loyalty | Money spent reduces short-term profit |
| Easier recruitment | Greenwashing destroys trust |
| Fewer conflicts and legal problems | Hard to measure the return |
| Possible cost savings (for example, energy use) | May be copied by rivals at lower cost |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Corporate culture | Shared values and ways of working |
| Stakeholder | Any group with an interest in the business |
| Shareholder | Owner of shares in a company |
| Business ethics | Moral principles applied to business decisions |
| CSR | Responsible behaviour towards society and the environment beyond the law |
| Greenwashing | Claiming to be more responsible or green than is true |

## Exam traps

- A strong culture is not automatically good: it must fit the strategy.
- Stakeholders include shareholders, but shareholders are not the only stakeholders.
- The stakeholder model does not ignore profit.
- Ethical behaviour can raise profit in the long run, so do not write that ethics always lowers it.
- Always name which stakeholder is affected and how.
- For **assess**, weigh short-term against long-term effects and give a judgement that depends on the business.

## Answer frames

- **Culture question:** name the type, give evidence from the source (who decides, how rules are used), then link to a consequence (speed of decision, innovation or staff motivation).
- **Stakeholder conflict:** name the two groups, say what each wants, show why it cannot be met together, and suggest how the business could resolve it.
- **Ethics trade-off:** quantify the gain, name the risk, and compare the two. A small gain with a large risk is hard to defend.
- **CSR:** give one benefit and one cost for this business, then judge whether CSR is likely to pay for itself.

## Self-test

1. Profit for the year is $1.5m. A shareholder-model firm pays out 70% as dividends and a stakeholder-model firm pays out 40%. Calculate the dividends under each model.
2. Calculate the percentage fall in dividends under the stakeholder model.
3. A chief executive earns $640,000 and the average employee $32,000. Calculate the pay ratio.
4. Switching supplier saves $90,000 on a profit of $1.2m. Calculate the percentage rise in profit.
5. Which of Handy's cultures relies mainly on rules and job descriptions?
6. Give one reason why a culture is hard to change.
7. Distinguish an internal stakeholder from an external stakeholder.
8. State one benefit of CSR.
9. Give one cost of CSR.
10. What is greenwashing?

### Answers

1. Shareholder model: 0.70 x 1.5m = **$1.05m**; stakeholder model: 0.40 x 1.5m = **$0.6m**.
2. (1.05 - 0.6) / 1.05 x 100 = **42.9%** fall.
3. 640,000 / 32,000 = **20:1**.
4. 90,000 / 1,200,000 x 100 = **7.5%**.
5. Role culture.
6. Any one: deeply embedded; staff resist; takes a long time; leaders slip back; mergers clash.
7. An internal stakeholder (such as an employee, manager or owner) works within the business; an external stakeholder (such as a customer, supplier or community group) is outside it.
8. Any one: stronger brand; easier recruitment; fewer conflicts; cost savings.
9. Any one: money spent reduces short-term profit; hard to measure the return; risk of greenwashing.
10. Claiming to be more environmentally or socially responsible than is true.

## Linking the ideas

- Culture shapes which decisions are possible: a role culture will follow procedure, a task culture will experiment.
- The stakeholder and shareholder models explain how profit is used, and ethics explains how it is earned.
- Pay and CSR connect the two: they show whether a business treats staff and society as partners or as costs.
- In an extended answer, link at least two ideas from this section, for example "a power culture means decisions favour the owner's view of the shareholder model, so the community's interests may be ignored".
- Always finish with a judgement that names the factor on which your view depends.
