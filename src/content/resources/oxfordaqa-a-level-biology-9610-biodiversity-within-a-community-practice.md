## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the term *species richness*. **[1]**

**2.** Write the formula for the index of diversity and state what each symbol means. **[3]**

**3.** Name two ways in which genetic diversity within a species can be measured. **[2]**

**4.** Which community has the highest index of diversity? All have 20 individuals.

A: 4 species with 5 individuals each

B: 5 species with 4 individuals each

C: 2 species with 10 individuals each

D: 5 species, one with 16 individuals and four with 1 individual each **[1]**

### Section B: longer questions

**5.** Three samples were taken from a grassland in which three species of plant were counted: 15, 9 and 6 individuals.

**(a)** Calculate the index of diversity. Show your working. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the index of diversity gives more information than species richness. **[2]**

**6.** A field margin and an orchard were sampled. Each sample contained 30 individuals of insects.

| Site | Number of individuals of each species |
|---|---|
| Field margin | 9, 7, 6, 4, 3, 1 |
| Orchard | 20, 6, 3, 1 |

**(a)** Calculate the index of diversity for each site. **[4]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage by which the index of the orchard is lower than that of the field margin. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest two reasons for the difference in biodiversity between the sites. **[2]**

**7.** A woodland had an index of diversity of 4.03. After the trees were replaced by a conifer plantation, the index was 1.33.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in the index. **[2]**

**(b)** The number of species fell from five to four. Explain why the index fell by much more than the number of species. **[2]**

**8.** A population of a plant has a gene with three alleles at frequencies of 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2. After a disease killed most of the plants, the surviving population contained two alleles at frequencies of 0.8 and 0.2.

**(a)** Explain what has happened to the genetic diversity of the population. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest how this may affect the ability of the population to cope with future environmental change. **[2]**

**9.** A short region of a protein, 10 amino acids long, was compared in three species. Species X is the reference. Species Y differs at 1 position and species Z differs at 4 positions.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of amino acids in Y and in Z that are identical to those of X. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest what the data show about the relationship of the species, and one limitation of the conclusion. **[2]**

**10.** Describe how a student should collect data to compare the species diversity of two areas of grassland. **[4]**

**11.** Discuss the balance between the need for human food production and the need to conserve biodiversity. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The number of different species in a community [1]. **[1]**

**2.** d = N(N - 1) / sum of n(n - 1) [1]. N = total number of organisms of all species [1]. n = total number of organisms of each species [1]. **[3]**

**3.** Any two from: comparing the base sequences of DNA or mRNA [1]; comparing the frequency of specific base sequences or alleles in populations [1]; comparing the amino acid sequences of the encoded proteins [1]. **[2]**

**4.** **B** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* With N = 20, N(N - 1) = 380 for every option. The indices are A 380 / 80 = 4.75, B 380 / 60 = 6.33, C 380 / 180 = 2.11 and D 380 / 240 = 1.58. B is the highest because it has the most species and they are evenly spread.

**5. (a)** N = 15 + 9 + 6 = 30, so N(N - 1) = 30 x 29 = 870 [1]. Sum of n(n - 1) = (15 x 14) + (9 x 8) + (6 x 5) = 210 + 72 + 30 = 312 [1]. d = 870 / 312 = 2.79 [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Species richness counts only the number of species [1]. The index also takes account of the number of individuals of each species, so it shows whether the community is dominated by one species [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Field margin: N = 30, N(N - 1) = 870; sum = 72 + 42 + 30 + 12 + 6 + 0 = 162 [1]; d = 870 / 162 = 5.37 [1]. Orchard: sum = 380 + 30 + 6 + 0 = 416 [1]; d = 870 / 416 = 2.09 [1]. **[4]**

**(b)** (5.37 - 2.09) / 5.37 [1] x 100 = 61.1 % [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Any two from: the orchard is a managed habitat that may be sprayed with pesticides, killing many species [1]; the orchard has fewer plant species, so fewer food sources and habitats [1]; the field margin is less disturbed and has a variety of plants [1]; one species may dominate in the orchard, for example a pest that has no predators [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** (4.03 - 1.33) / 4.03 [1] x 100 = 67.0 % [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** The index also depends on how many individuals there are of each species [1]. In the plantation, one species made up most of the individuals, so the sum of n(n - 1) became much larger and the index fell [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** One allele has been lost, and the remaining alleles have very different frequencies [1], so genetic diversity has decreased [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** With fewer alleles, there are fewer variations on which natural selection can act [1], so it is less likely that any individuals have an allele that gives resistance to a new disease or a changed environment [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** Y: 9 / 10 = 90 % identical [1]. Z: 6 / 10 = 60 % identical [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Y is more closely related to X than Z is, because the sequences are more similar [1]. The limitation is that only a short region of one protein was compared, so a longer sequence or more genes would give a more reliable conclusion [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Choose quadrat positions using random numbers to avoid bias [1]. Use the same size of quadrat and the same number of samples in each area, large enough for the data to be representative [1]. Identify each species and record the number of individuals of each in every quadrat [1]. Calculate N and n for each area and use the index of diversity formula to compare the areas [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Points for food production: a growing population needs food [1]; high yields rely on large fields, monoculture and pesticides, which reduce biodiversity [1]. Points for conservation: biodiversity maintains food webs, ecosystem function and genetic variety [1]; lost species cannot be replaced, and could provide medicines or crops in future [1]. Compromises: leave field margins and hedgerows, reduce or target pesticide use, use protected areas [1]. Conclusion: a reasoned judgement that balances the two, noting that economic and social factors also matter [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the wrong formula, for example n squared.
- Not showing the sum of n(n - 1).
- Saying that species richness and the index measure the same thing.
- Describing human effects without naming a mechanism.
- Giving only one side in a "discuss" question.
