## Section A - short questions

**1.** A wholesaler buys 10 tonnes of rice and sells it in 25 kg sacks to shops. This function is: **[1]**
A bearing risk   B breaking bulk   C providing credit   D price stability

**2.** Which is a feature of a cash and carry wholesaler? **[1]**
A free delivery   B 60 days' credit   C self service by trade customers   D selling only to final consumers

**3.** A group of independently owned grocery shops trade under one name and buy much of their stock from one wholesaler. This is a: **[1]**
A multiple chain store   B voluntary chain   C cooperative retail society   D franchise

**4.** State **two** functions of a wholesaler other than breaking bulk. **[2]**

**5.** Define cash and carry. **[2]**

**6.** Identify **two** reasons why some retailers buy directly from manufacturers instead of from wholesalers. **[2]**

## Section B - structured questions

**7.** *Ocean Fish Ltd processes and freezes fish. It sells to four wholesalers rather than to the 600 shops that stock its products.*
Explain **three** ways in which the wholesalers are important to Ocean Fish Ltd. **[6]**

**8.** Explain **two** ways in which a wholesaler is important to a small independent retailer. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how a wholesaler bears risk, giving **two** examples of risks it faces. **[4]**

**10.** *Ahmed owns a corner shop. He usually buys from a wholesaler that delivers and gives 30 days' credit, at $9.60 per case. A cash and carry sells the same goods at $8.80 per case. Ahmed needs 75 cases. Collecting from the cash and carry would cost him $42.*
(a) Calculate the total cost of buying from each supplier. (3 marks)
(b) Advise Ahmed which supplier to use, giving reasons. (3 marks) **[6]**

**11.** *Six independent pharmacies in a city are losing customers to a large pharmacy chain. A local wholesaler suggests forming a voluntary chain.*
(a) Explain **two** benefits of the voluntary chain to the pharmacies. (4 marks)
(b) Explain **one** benefit of the voluntary chain to the wholesaler. (2 marks) **[6]**

**12.** Discuss whether wholesalers will continue to be important as ecommerce grows. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** B. Large quantities are divided into smaller ones. **[1]**

**2.** C. Trade customers serve themselves, pay immediately and take the goods. **[1]**

**3.** B. The shops stay independent but cooperate with one wholesaler. **[1]**

**4.** Any two: storage [1]; bearing risk [1]; assortment [1]; transport or delivery [1]; credit [1]; advice and information [1]; packing and labelling [1]; price stability [1]. **[max 2]**

**5.** A self-service wholesale warehouse [1] where trade customers pay immediately and take the goods away themselves, with no credit or delivery [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two: they buy in very large quantities [1]; they have their own central warehouses [1]; buying direct cuts out the wholesaler's profit, lowering cost [1]; fresher goods for perishables [1]. **[max 2]**

**7.** Any three, 2 marks each (function plus importance to Ocean Fish):
Breaking bulk: Ocean Fish deals with four buyers instead of 600 shops, cutting delivery, selling and invoicing costs [2].
Storage: the wholesalers hold frozen stock in their own cold stores, so Ocean Fish needs less freezer space [2].
Continuous production: wholesalers buy ahead of demand, so the factory can keep processing fish when catches are good [2].
Bearing risk: once sold, the risk of stock not selling or falling in price passes to the wholesalers [2].
Information: wholesalers report which products are selling, helping Ocean Fish plan output [2]. **[max 6]**

**8.** Any two, 2 marks each: the retailer can buy small quantities, so less money is tied up in stock [2]; a wide range from many manufacturers is available in one place, saving time [2]; credit lets the retailer sell goods before paying [2]; delivery saves collection trips [2]; the wholesaler stores the goods, so the shop needs little stock room [2]; advice on new products and what sells [2]. **[max 4]**

**9.** The wholesaler buys and owns the goods before it has sold them [1], so it suffers any loss if they cannot be sold at a profit [1]. Any two examples: prices fall while goods are in store [1]; goods are damaged, spoiled or stolen [1]; goods go out of fashion or demand falls [1]. **[max 4]**

**10.** (a) Delivered wholesaler: 75 x 9.60 = **$720** [1]. Cash and carry: 75 x 8.80 = $660 [1], plus $42 = **$702** [1]. (3 marks)
(b) Cash and carry is $18 cheaper (720 - 702) [1]. However, Ahmed must pay immediately and spend time collecting, while the delivered wholesaler gives 30 days' credit and saves him leaving the shop [1]. Recommendation with reason, for example: use the delivered wholesaler if his cash is tight, because a month of credit is worth more than an $18 saving; otherwise use the cash and carry [1]. (3 marks) **[6]**

**11.** (a) Any two, 2 marks each: lower prices, because the group's combined orders let the wholesaler buy in bulk, so the pharmacies can compete with the chain on price [2]; own-brand products under the group name at good margins [2]; joint advertising under a common name, which a single pharmacy could not afford [2]; advice on shop layout and a recognisable image, so customers trust the group [2]. (max 4 marks)
(b) Any one: guaranteed, regular orders from the six pharmacies [1], so the wholesaler can plan purchases and gain bulk discounts from manufacturers [1]; or the wholesaler keeps customers that might otherwise close [1], protecting its sales [1]. (2 marks) **[6]**

**12.** Up to 2 for reasons wholesalers may decline, up to 2 for reasons they remain important, up to 2 for a justified conclusion.
Decline: manufacturers sell directly to consumers online [1]; large online retailers and chains buy direct and run their own warehouses [1]; contract logistics firms store and deliver for sellers [1].
Remain important: thousands of small shops, cafés and market traders still need small quantities, credit and a wide range in one place [1]; many small online sellers buy stock from wholesalers [1]; wholesalers can sell online to retailers and offer cash and carry or voluntary chains [1].
Conclusion: for example, wholesalers stay important for small businesses but must adapt, for instance by taking online trade orders [2]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Explaining benefits to the retailer in a question about the manufacturer.
- Describing cash and carry without "pay immediately" and "no delivery".
- Treating a voluntary chain as one company owning many shops.
- In calculations, forgetting the collection cost.
- In "advise" questions, giving the cheaper answer without weighing credit and convenience.

## Frequently asked questions

**How does the independent wholesaler survive according to 0715?** Through cash and carry and voluntary chains.

**Is a voluntary chain the same as a chain store?** No. In a voluntary chain each shop is separately owned; a multiple chain store is one business owning many branches.
