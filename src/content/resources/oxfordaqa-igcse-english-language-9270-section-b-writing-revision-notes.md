**These notes condense Paper 2 Section B of OxfordAQA International GCSE English Language (9270):
writing for audience and purpose.** For full explanations and worked plans, use the
[Paper 2 Section B writing study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-english-language-9270-section-b-writing/).

## The facts to know

| Feature | What the specification says |
|---|---|
| Marks | Section B: 40 of Paper 2's 80 |
| Focus | Writing for audience and purpose |
| Audience and purpose | Could be **multiple and diverse** |
| Link | Writing is linked with the Section A reading sources |
| Choice | **Two** tasks; answer one |
| Assessed by | AO2: W1–W4 |
| Weighting | AO2 is 20% of the qualification through Paper 2 |

## Read every task with AFP + C

```
A  Audience   who? one group or several?
F  Form       letter / article / speech / leaflet / report / review / blog
P  Purpose    persuade / argue / inform / explain / advise / review? more than one?
C  Content    what must be covered? what can the sources give me?
```

**Multiple and diverse** = more than one audience or purpose in the same task. Name each in your plan
and address each in the writing.

## Sources: the four rules

1. **Ideas, not sentences.**
2. **Select** only what serves your purpose.
3. **Transform** source details into your form and voice.
4. **Add** your own examples and reasoning.

## Form conventions in brief

| Form | Must-have features |
|---|---|
| Formal letter | "Dear…", purpose stated early, paragraphs, "Yours sincerely" (named recipient) or "Yours faithfully" ("Dear Sir or Madam") |
| Article | Headline, strapline (optional), hook opening, paragraphs or sub-headings |
| Speech | Greeting to audience, direct address, signposting, repetition, memorable close ("Thank you") |
| Leaflet | Title, sub-headings, short sections, call to action or contact point |
| Report | Title, purpose, sections, findings, recommendations |
| Review | Overview, strengths and weaknesses, recommendation |
| Blog | Engaging title, personal voice, conversational links |

## Register ladder

| Level | Signs | Typical audience |
|---|---|---|
| Formal | No contractions or slang; precise vocabulary; complex sentences | Head teacher, council, employer |
| Semi-formal | Some contractions; friendly but respectful; rhetorical questions | Parents, local residents, school magazine |
| Informal | Conversational, humour, slang avoided in exams | Friend, peer blog |

**Mixed audiences:** pitch to the one with most authority; turn to address the others directly.

## Paragraph engine

```
Topic sentence  ->  development  ->  example (own or transformed from a source)
->  link back to purpose or audience
```

## Persuasive and explanatory tools

| Tool | Best for |
|---|---|
| Direct address, inclusive "we" | Building connection |
| Rhetorical question | Making readers think |
| Rule of three | Memorable emphasis |
| Anecdote | Making an issue personal |
| Counter-argument and rebuttal | Persuading sceptical readers |
| Signposting ("firstly", "above all") | Speeches and explanations |
| Imperatives | Calls to action, advice |

## Openings and endings

- **Open** with purpose plus engagement: a question, a scenario, a clear statement of what you
  propose.
- **End** to match purpose: call to action (persuade), recommendation (report), summary and advice
  (inform/advise), final verdict (review).

## Five-minute plan template

```
Task in one line:      ______________________________
Audience(s):           ______  and  ______
Purpose(s):            ______  then ______
Form features:         opening ____ / headings? ____ / sign-off ____
Paragraph order:       1 ____  2 ____  3 ____  4 ____  5 ____
Source ideas to transform:  ____ , ____
Ending type:           call to action / recommendation / verdict
```

Fill it in before writing a sentence of the response; tick each line again at the end.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Audience | The intended reader or listener |
| Purpose | What the writing aims to achieve |
| Form | The type of text and its conventions |
| Register | The level of formality |
| Strapline | A short line under a headline expanding it |
| Signposting | Phrases that guide a listener through a speech |
| Call to action | A direct request for the audience to do something |
| Rebuttal | Answering an opposing view |

## Exam traps

- An essay in place of the named form.
- One audience ignored when the task names two.
- Lifting sentences from the sources.
- Register drifting from formal to chatty.
- Drawing layout instead of writing headings in words.
- No ending: the piece simply stops.
- No proofreading time.

## Self-test

1. How many writing tasks are offered in Paper 2 Section B?
2. What is the focus of the writing task according to the specification?
3. What does "multiple and diverse" audience and purpose mean in practice?
4. How is Section B linked to Section A?
5. Give two rules for using the sources.
6. Name three conventions of a formal letter.
7. When do you sign off "Yours faithfully"?
8. Give three features of a speech.
9. Which register suits a letter to a local council?
10. How should you handle a task with two different audiences?
11. Name two ways to end a persuasive piece.
12. Which writing objective covers spelling, punctuation and grammar?

**Answers:** 1. Two; you answer one. 2. Writing for audience and purpose. 3. A task may address more
than one group of readers or combine purposes, such as informing and persuading, so the writing must
respond to each. 4. The writing is linked with the reading sources. 5. Any two: take ideas, not
sentences; select; transform; add your own. 6. Any three: greeting, purpose stated early, paragraphs,
formal sign-off, formal register. 7. When the letter begins "Dear Sir or Madam" and the recipient is not
named. 8. Any three: greeting to the audience, direct address, signposting, repetition, a memorable
close. 9. Formal. 10. Pitch the register to the reader with most authority and address each group
directly at points in the writing. 11. Any two: a call to action, a memorable final line, a rule of
three summary, a direct appeal. 12. W4.
