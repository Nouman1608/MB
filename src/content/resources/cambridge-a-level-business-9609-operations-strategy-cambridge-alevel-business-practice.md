## Section A

## Section B

**7.** Oakhurst Builders is planning a garden office project. Durations are in days.

| Activity | Follows | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| P | – | 2 |
| Q | P | 4 |
| R | P | 3 |
| S | Q | 5 |
| T | Q and R | 2 |
| U | S and T | 3 |

The network has nodes 1 to 6. P runs from 1 to 2, Q from 2 to 3, R from 2 to 4, a dummy from 3 to 4, S from 3 to 5, T from 4 to 5 and U from 5 to 6.

**(a)** Explain why the dummy activity is needed. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the earliest start time and latest finish time for each node. **[4]**

**(c)** State the critical path and the minimum project duration. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the total float and free float for activities R and T. **[4]**

**8.** Ilford Snacks is considering an ERP programme costing $300,000 to install. It is expected to reduce inventory holding costs by $55,000 a year and administration costs by $30,000 a year. Running costs are $25,000 a year.

**(a)** Calculate the payback period of the ERP programme. **[3]**

**(b)** Analyse how ERP could improve Ilford's efficiency in **two** of the areas named in the syllabus. **[6]**

**9.** Halden Motors assembles electric scooters. It holds four weeks of components in its warehouse. A consultant recommends JIT manufacturing with deliveries twice a day. Most components come from one overseas supplier, and shipping delays have occurred twice this year. Evaluate whether Halden should adopt JIT manufacturing. **[12]**

**10.** Wren Bridal makes made-to-measure wedding dresses. Demand peaks strongly in spring and summer. Assess whether Wren should give a higher priority to flexibility than to lean production. **[10]**

**11.** Calver Bakeries has been asked by a supermarket chain to supply bread overnight, seven days a week. Calver currently works one day shift. Analyse how the availability of human and finance resources will influence Calver's decision. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Changing current processes or adopting new ways of producing products or delivering services [1], for example to cut costs or improve speed or quality [1].

**2.** The ability to change the design or features of a product to meet a customer's needs [1] without large delays or costs [1], for example a furniture maker producing a sofa in the fabric and size each customer chooses [1].

**3.** Any two: one shared database [1]; linked modules for different functions [1]; real-time updating; automatic data transfer; reporting tools.

**4.** Lean production gives employees responsibility for quality and improvement [1], for example through kaizen suggestions and quality circles [1], so they need to be multi-skilled and involved rather than simply following instructions [1].

**5.** Total float is the time an activity can be delayed without delaying the whole project [1] [1]. Free float is the time it can be delayed without delaying the start of the next activity [1] [1].

**6.** Automation needs a large capital outlay [1]. If the business lacks retained profit or cannot borrow on acceptable terms, it may delay or choose a cheaper, partial automation [1], or lease the equipment, which affects how quickly productivity gains are achieved [1].

**7. (a)** T needs both Q and R to be finished, but S needs only Q [1]. The dummy shows that T depends on Q without making S depend on R [1].

**(b)** EST: node 1 = 0, node 2 = 2, node 3 = 6, node 4 = 6 (larger of 2 + 3 = 5 and 6 + 0 = 6), node 5 = 11 (larger of 6 + 5 = 11 and 6 + 2 = 8), node 6 = 14 [1] [1]. LFT: node 6 = 14, node 5 = 11, node 4 = 9, node 3 = 6 (smaller of 11 − 5 = 6 and 9 − 0 = 9), node 2 = 2 (smaller of 6 − 4 = 2 and 9 − 3 = 6), node 1 = 0 [1] [1].

**(c)** Critical path: P, Q, S, U [1]. Minimum duration = 2 + 4 + 5 + 3 = **14 days** [1].

**(d)** R: total float = 9 − 2 − 3 = **4** [1]; free float = 6 − 2 − 3 = **1** [1]. T: total float = 11 − 6 − 2 = **3** [1]; free float = 11 − 6 − 2 = **3** [1]. **[12]**

**8. (a)** Net annual saving = 55,000 + 30,000 − 25,000 = $60,000 [1]. Payback = 300,000 ÷ 60,000 [1] = **5 years** [1].

**(b)** Inventory control: live stock data and automatic re-ordering [1] mean Ilford holds less stock of ingredients and packaging [1], cutting holding costs and waste of out-of-date stock, as the $55,000 saving suggests [1]. Management information: all departments share one database [1], so managers see orders, costs and stock in real time [1] and can make faster pricing and production decisions with fewer errors [1]. **[9]**

**9.** For JIT: four weeks of components ties up working capital and warehouse space [1]; JIT would release cash and cut holding costs [1], and reduce the risk of components becoming obsolete as scooter designs change [1]. Against: twice-daily deliveries from one overseas supplier are risky [1]; shipping delays have already occurred twice this year [1], and under JIT any delay would stop assembly [1], causing lost sales and idle workers [1]. JIT also needs very reliable quality, as there is no buffer stock to replace faulty parts [1]. Alternatives: find a local supplier, or keep a smaller safety stock while moving towards JIT [1]. Judgement: Halden should not adopt full JIT now [1], because reliance on one distant supplier with a record of delays makes the risk of stopping production too high [1]; it should first reduce supplier risk, then move gradually [1]. **[12]**

**10.** Flexibility matters: each dress is made to measure, so flexibility of specification is essential to meet customer expectations [1] [1]. Demand peaks in spring and summer, so Wren needs volume flexibility, such as temporary skilled staff or overtime [1], or it will lose orders at its busiest time [1]. Delivery-time flexibility matters because wedding dates cannot move [1]. Lean production still has value: reducing waste of expensive fabric [1] and using kaizen to speed up fitting processes [1]. But lean's high capacity utilisation leaves little slack for peaks [1], and JIT is risky when a missing fabric would delay a fixed wedding date [1]. Judgement: Wren should prioritise flexibility, because its premium, customised product and fixed deadlines make responsiveness the source of value, while applying lean ideas to reduce fabric waste [1]. **[10]**

**11.** Human: overnight production needs a night shift, so Calver must recruit or move bakers [1]; night work may be hard to recruit for and may need higher pay [1], so if skilled bakers cannot be found, quality and reliability suffer and the supermarket contract could be lost [1]. Finance: extra staff, ingredients and possibly delivery vehicles must be paid for before the supermarket pays [1], straining working capital [1], so Calver may need an overdraft or may be unable to accept the order unless finance is arranged [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the zero-duration dummy when calculating the forward pass.
- Taking the smaller value on the forward pass.
- Forgetting running costs when calculating the payback period of new systems.
- Evaluating JIT without referring to supplier reliability in the case.
- Treating lean production and flexibility as the same thing.
