This guide covers **Unit 1 Marketing and people**, the compulsory
International AS unit for Pearson Edexcel International Advanced
Subsidiary/Advanced Level Business (YBS11/XBS11), Issue 1. It is
assessed by a single 2-hour paper worth 80 marks, built from
short-open response and extended-writing questions based on sources,
some of which require quantitative skills.

## Where this fits in YBS11

Unit 1 introduces students to how businesses identify market
opportunities and build competitive advantage, then turns to how they
recruit, train, organise and motivate the people who deliver on that
strategy. Alongside Unit 2 (Managing business activities), it forms
the International AS half of the qualification; Units 3 and 4 (Business
decisions and strategy; Global business) complete the full
International A Level.

## Syllabus coverage

**PEARSON EDEXCEL INTERNATIONAL A LEVEL BUSINESS (YBS11) — UNIT 1
MARKETING AND PEOPLE**

- 1.3.1 Meeting customer needs — mass and niche markets, dynamic

and uncertainty; primary and secondary market research methods and
sampling; market positioning, segmentation and product differentiation
- 1.3.2 The market — factors changing demand and supply, the
interaction of demand and supply, and the calculation and business
significance of price elasticity of demand (PED) and income elasticity
of demand (YED)
- 1.3.3 Marketing mix and strategy — marketing objectives and the
elements of the marketing mix
- 1.3.4 Managing people — recruitment, training, organisational
structure and motivation of employees
- 1.3.5 Entrepreneurs and leaders — the role of enterprising
individuals and leadership in business start-up and growth

## How to approach it

Meeting customer needs (1.3.1) and the market (1.3.2) are the most
quantitative parts of this unit — practise calculating and
interpreting PED and YED until the arithmetic is automatic, since the
paper explicitly tests quantitative skills alongside written analysis.
Because every question is source-based, get comfortable applying
concepts like market segmentation or motivation theory to an unfamiliar
business scenario rather than just defining them in the abstract. The
people-focused content in 1.3.4 and 1.3.5 pairs naturally with Unit 2's
management topics, so building a clear picture of how marketing
decisions and people decisions interact within one business will help
across both units.

## Meeting customer needs

Markets are classified as **mass** (large, standardised, low unit cost) or **niche** (small, specialised, higher margins but greater risk from a narrow customer base).

**Market size, share and growth** are the standard calculations:

```
market share = firm's sales / total market sales x 100
```

**Segmentation** divides customers demographically, geographically, psychographically or behaviourally, so the offer can be targeted. **Positioning** places the brand relative to competitors on a market map — commonly price against quality — and gaps on that map suggest opportunities, though a gap may exist simply because no demand is there.

**Dynamic markets** change through online retailing, changing consumer tastes and technology, so competitive advantage erodes and must be renewed.

## Demand, supply and elasticity

Demand shifts with income, competitor prices and actions, demographics, fashion and seasonality. Supply shifts with costs, technology, government intervention and external shocks.

```
PED = % change in quantity demanded / % change in price
YED = % change in quantity demanded / % change in income
```

The business application matters more than the formula. Where demand is **price inelastic**, a firm can raise price and increase total revenue — which is why differentiation and brand loyalty are pursued. Where demand is elastic, competing on price destroys revenue. **YED** guides product portfolio decisions: luxury goods suffer disproportionately in a recession.

## The marketing mix and strategy

The four Ps extend to seven for services with **people, process and physical environment**. The elements must be internally consistent — premium price requires premium product, place and promotion.

**Product life cycle** — development, introduction, growth, maturity, decline — with extension strategies at maturity. The **Boston Matrix** classifies a portfolio into stars, cash cows, question marks and dogs, and the insight is that cash cows fund the stars and question marks.

Branding builds loyalty, allows premium pricing and eases new product launches.

## Managing people

**Approaches to staffing** range from treating staff as an asset (investment, training, low turnover) to a cost (flexibility, minimum commitment). Flexible workforces use part-time, temporary, outsourced and multi-skilled workers.

**Recruitment, selection and training** — induction, on-the-job and off-the-job — carry costs justified by productivity and retention.

**Motivation theory**: Taylor (pay and control), Mayo (social needs and group belonging), Maslow (hierarchy of needs), Herzberg (hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction, motivators create satisfaction).

**Leadership styles** — autocratic, paternalistic, democratic, laissez-faire — suit different situations, and the examinable point is that no style is universally best.

## Worked example

A firm sells 40,000 units at $25. It cuts price to $22 and sales rise to 50,000. Calculate PED and advise.

```
% change in Qd = (50 000 - 40 000) / 40 000 x 100 = +25%
% change in P  = (22 - 25) / 25 x 100            = -12%

PED = 25 / -12 = -2.08  (elastic)

Revenue before = 40 000 x 25 = $1 000 000
Revenue after  = 50 000 x 22 = $1 100 000
```

Demand is elastic, so the price cut raised revenue by $100,000. Whether it raised *profit* depends on unit costs — a distinction worth making.

## Common mistakes

Confusing market size with market share. Ignoring the minus sign on PED and misclassifying elasticity. Assuming a gap on a market map is always an opportunity. Concluding on revenue when the question asks about profit. Listing motivation theories without applying them to the case.

## Quick revision checklist

- Distinguish mass from niche markets and calculate market share and growth.
- Explain segmentation, targeting and positioning using a market map.
- Calculate PED and YED and link them to pricing and portfolio decisions.
- Apply the marketing mix, product life cycle and Boston Matrix.
- Compare staffing approaches, training methods and leadership styles.
- Apply Taylor, Mayo, Maslow and Herzberg to a given workforce problem.
