## Section A

**1.** Distinguish between trade and aids to trade, giving two examples of each. **[4]**

**2.** State the three stages of production and give one example of an industry at each stage. **[3]**

## Section B

**3.** A furniture maker buys timber from a sawmill, builds tables, and sells them through a retailer in another city.

**(a)** Identify which stage of production each business is engaged in. **[3]**
**(b)** State which utility is created when the furniture is transported from the workshop to the retailer's city. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the furniture maker's activity is described as creating form utility. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **three** advantages and **two** disadvantages of specialisation. **[10]**

**5.** "Commerce is the same thing as production." Discuss whether this statement is correct. **[4]**

---

## Answers

**1. Trade** is the buying and selling of goods, split into home trade (wholesale and retail) and foreign trade (import, export, entrepot) [1] [1]. **Aids to trade** are the services that make trade possible, such as banking, insurance, transport, warehousing, communication and advertising [1] [1].

**2. Primary** — extraction of raw materials, e.g. mining or farming [1]. **Secondary** — turning raw materials into finished or semi-finished goods, e.g. manufacturing [1]. **Tertiary** — providing services that support the other two, e.g. retailing or banking [1].

**3. (a)** The sawmill is **primary** (extracting/processing raw timber) [1]; the furniture maker is **secondary** (manufacturing tables) [1]; the retailer is **tertiary** (selling to the final consumer) [1].
**(b)** **Place utility** [1].
**(c)** Form utility is created because the furniture maker **changes the timber into a more useful form** — a table — that better satisfies a consumer's want than the raw material did [1] [1].

**4. Advantages** (any three, e.g.): **higher output per worker**, since repeating one task builds skill and speed [1] [1]; **permits mechanisation and economies of scale**, lowering unit costs [1] [1]; **improved quality**, since a worker/firm becomes expert in a narrow task [1] [1]. **Disadvantages** (any two): work becomes **repetitive and demotivating** [1] [1]; specialisation creates **total interdependence**, so a breakdown anywhere in the chain of exchange disrupts everyone relying on it [1] [1].

**5.** The statement is **not correct** [1]. Production is the **creation of goods and services** through the primary, secondary and tertiary stages [1]. Commerce is narrower: it is the **distribution of what has already been produced**, covering trade and aids to trade only [1]; production and commerce are interdependent but are **distinct** activities, since a good can be produced without yet having been traded or moved to where it is wanted [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying commerce "includes production" instead of keeping the two distinct.
- Naming a utility without explaining *why* that utility applies to the scenario given.
- Listing advantages of specialisation without linking them to output, cost or quality.
- Forgetting that specialisation's biggest disadvantage — interdependence — is precisely what makes commerce necessary.
