## The definition to learn exactly

**Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of their higher concentration to a region of their lower concentration (i.e. down a concentration gradient), as a result of their random movement.**

Four words to check in your answer: **net**, **higher → lower**, **concentration gradient**, **random movement**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Concentration | The amount of a substance in a given volume |
| Concentration gradient | The difference in concentration between two regions |
| Net movement | The overall movement once movement in both directions is counted |
| Kinetic energy | Energy of movement; the energy source for diffusion |
| Solute | A substance dissolved in a solvent |
| Passive process | One that needs no energy from respiration |
| Equilibrium | Concentration equal throughout, so no net movement |

## Energy and membranes

- Energy: **kinetic energy of random movement of molecules and ions**.
- No energy from respiration is needed.
- Some substances move into and out of cells by diffusion **through the cell membrane**.

## Importance: substance, place, direction

| Substance | Place | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | Alveoli | Air → blood |
| Carbon dioxide | Alveoli | Blood → air |
| Oxygen | Body tissues | Blood → respiring cells |
| Carbon dioxide | Leaf | Air spaces → photosynthesising cells |
| Oxygen, water vapour | Leaf | Out through stomata |
| Glucose and other digested food | Small intestine | Gut → blood |

## The four factors

| Factor | Faster when | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Surface area | Larger | More particles cross at once |
| Temperature | Higher | Particles have more kinetic energy, move faster |
| Concentration gradient | Steeper | More net movement down the gradient |
| Distance | Shorter | Less far to travel |

**These four only:** the syllabus limits the investigation to surface area, temperature, concentration gradient and distance.

## Investigation checklist

- **Method:** pink agar cubes (alkali + phenolphthalein) in dilute acid; time until colourless.
- **Independent variable:** one factor only.
- **Dependent variable:** time to turn colourless (or distance coloured after a set time).
- **Controls:** keep the other three factors, acid volume and cube shape the same.
- **Repeats:** at least three per condition; calculate a mean; identify anomalies.
- **Safety:** eye protection; acid and alkali are irritants.
- **Rate:** 1 ÷ time, or 1000 ÷ time for easier numbers.

## Surface area : volume quick table

| Cube side | Surface area | Volume | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 cm | 6 cm² | 1 cm³ | 6 : 1 |
| 2 cm | 24 cm² | 8 cm³ | 3 : 1 |
| 4 cm | 96 cm² | 64 cm³ | 1.5 : 1 |

Bigger cube → smaller ratio and longer distance to the centre → longer time.

## Exam traps

- "Particles spread out" is not enough; give the direction and "net".
- Not energy from respiration: **kinetic energy**.
- Particles **do not stop** at equilibrium; net movement stops.
- Explain each factor using particles, not just "it is faster".
- Change one variable only in a plan.
- In a results description, quote numbers with units.

## Quick self-test

1. Give the full definition of diffusion.
2. State the source of energy for diffusion.
3. What does "down a concentration gradient" mean?
4. Name two gases that diffuse across the alveoli, with their directions.
5. Why does carbon dioxide diffuse into a photosynthesising leaf cell?
6. State the four factors named in the syllabus that influence diffusion.
7. Explain why increasing temperature increases the rate of diffusion.
8. Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of a cube with sides of 3 cm.
9. A 1 cm cube turns colourless in 100 s and a 2 cm cube in 260 s. Explain the difference.
10. In an investigation of temperature, state two variables to keep constant.
11. At equilibrium, have the particles stopped moving? Explain.
12. Calculate the rate as 1000 ÷ time for a cube that takes 250 s.

### Answers

1. The net movement of particles from a region of their higher concentration to a region of their lower concentration (down a concentration gradient), as a result of their random movement.
2. The kinetic energy of random movement of molecules and ions.
3. From a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration.
4. Oxygen from the air in the alveoli into the blood; carbon dioxide from the blood into the alveoli.
5. The cell uses carbon dioxide in photosynthesis, so its concentration inside is lower than in the air spaces.
6. Surface area, temperature, concentration gradient and distance.
7. Particles gain kinetic energy and move faster, so they spread down the gradient more quickly.
8. 54 cm² ÷ 27 cm³ = **2 : 1**.
9. The 2 cm cube has a smaller surface area to volume ratio and a greater distance to the centre, so acid takes longer to reach the centre.
10. Any two: cube size, acid concentration, volume of acid.
11. No. They keep moving randomly, but there is no net movement.
12. 1000 ÷ 250 = **4** (arbitrary units).
