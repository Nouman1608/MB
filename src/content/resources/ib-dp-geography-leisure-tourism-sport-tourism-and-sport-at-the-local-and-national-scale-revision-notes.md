Condensed recall for **5.2 Tourism and sport at the local and national scale** (IB DP Geography, Option E: Leisure, tourism and sport). See the [Leisure, tourism and sport study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-geography-leisure-tourism-sport-study-guide/).

## Types of tourism

Domestic and international; mass and niche; beach, cultural, heritage, adventure, ecotourism, urban.

## Models (evaluate, do not treat as fact)

| Model | Idea |
|---|---|
| Butler's tourism area life cycle | Exploration, involvement, development, consolidation, stagnation, then decline or rejuvenation |
| Doxey's irritation index | Host attitudes move from euphoria through apathy and annoyance to antagonism as visitor numbers rise |
| Plog's model | Links traveller personality types to destination popularity |

Limits: not every destination follows Butler's curve, and management can alter its path.

## Impacts

| | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| Economic | Employment, income, multiplier effects, foreign exchange | Seasonal work, low wages, leakage of profits, price inflation |
| Social and cultural | Cultural exchange, preservation of traditions | Commodification, crowding, displacement |
| Environmental | Funding for conservation | Pressure on habitats and water, waste, erosion, carbon emissions |

Always say **for whom** an impact is positive or negative.

## Sport at local and national scale

- Local clubs support health and community.
- National stadiums and events promote identity and regeneration.
- Large events can bring investment and infrastructure but may leave costly, underused facilities.
- Evaluate **legacy**: who benefits, and for how long?

## Worked check

Arrivals rise from 1.2 million to 1.5 million: 0.3 / 1.2 x 100 = 25 per cent increase.

## Exam traps

- Listing impacts without saying for whom.
- Using Butler's model as fact.
- Writing a general holiday description rather than geography.
- Ignoring leakage when judging economic benefit.

## Self-test

1. List the stages of Butler's model.
2. What does Doxey's index describe?
3. Give one economic and one environmental negative of tourism.
4. What is meant by the legacy of a sporting event?
5. Arrivals rise from 800,000 to 920,000: what is the percentage rise?

**Answers:** 1. Exploration, involvement, development, consolidation, stagnation, decline or rejuvenation. 2. Changing host attitudes as tourist numbers grow. 3. For example leakage and habitat pressure. 4. The lasting benefits and costs after the event, and who receives them. 5. 120,000 / 800,000 x 100 = 15 per cent.
