These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 9.01, Plane isometric transformations. Questions 1 to 10 are for both tiers. Questions 11 to 13 use combinations of transformations, which are **Higher tier only**, and are labelled. Shapes are given by the coordinates of their vertices, so you can work with coordinate rules or plot them on squared paper. Column vectors are written as (a over b). All questions can be answered without a calculator.

Learn the methods first in the [plane isometric transformations study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-plane-isometric-transformations-ocr-gcse-maths/) or the [plane isometric transformations revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-plane-isometric-transformations-ocr-gcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Write down the image of each point after the reflection given.

**(a)** (−2, 5) reflected in the x-axis **[1]**

**(b)** (4, −1) reflected in the line y = −x **[1]**

**(c)** (7, 3) reflected in the line y = 1 **[1]**

**2.** A triangle has vertices (1, 0), (3, 0) and (3, 2). Rotate it through 90° clockwise about the origin and write down the vertices of the image. **[2]**

**3.** **(a)** A triangle has vertices (−1, 4), (2, 4) and (2, 6). Translate it by (−3 over −5) and write down the vertices of the image. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the column vector that maps the image back onto the original triangle. **[1]**

**4.** A triangle with vertices (1, 2), (4, 2) and (4, 3) is reflected to give an image with vertices (1, −4), (4, −4) and (4, −5). Find the equation of the mirror line. **[2]**

**5.** A triangle with vertices (0, 1), (2, 1) and (2, 4) is mapped onto a triangle with vertices (6, 5), (4, 5) and (4, 2). Describe fully the single transformation. **[3]**

**6.** A triangle with vertices (2, 1), (5, 1) and (5, 3) is mapped onto a triangle with vertices (−1, 2), (−1, 5) and (−3, 5). Describe fully the single transformation. **[3]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Triangle P has vertices (3, 2), (5, 2) and (5, 3).

**(a)** Rotate P through 90° clockwise about the point (1, 2). Write down the vertices of the image. **[3]**

**(b)** Write down the coordinates of the only invariant point of this rotation. **[1]**

**8.** Shape Q has vertices (1, 1), (3, 1) and (3, 4).

**(a)** Reflect Q in the line x = −1. Write down the vertices of the image. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the invariant points of this reflection. **[1]**

**(c)** Taken in the order given, the vertices of Q go round anticlockwise. In which direction do the matching vertices of the image go? **[1]**

**9.** A triangle with vertices (1, 3), (1, 6) and (2, 6) is mapped onto a triangle with vertices (3, 1), (6, 1) and (6, 2).

**(a)** Describe fully the single transformation. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how you can tell that the transformation is not a rotation. **[1]**

**10.** Shape A is translated by (4 over −3) to give shape B. Shape B is translated by (−6 over 1) to give shape C.

**(a)** Write down the single vector that maps A onto C. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the vector that maps C onto A. **[1]**

**(c)** A vertex of C is at (5, −1). Find the coordinates of the matching vertex of A. **[1]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** Triangle T has vertices (1, 1), (3, 1) and (1, 2). T is reflected in the x-axis, and the image is then rotated through 90° anticlockwise about the origin.

**(a)** Write down the vertices of the final image. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe fully the single transformation that maps T onto the final image. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe the invariant points of the single transformation. **[1]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)** A shape is reflected in the line x = 1 and then the image is reflected in the line x = 4.

**(a)** Show that the combined effect is a translation, and give its vector. **[3]**

**(b)** Write down the vector if the reflections are done in the opposite order. **[1]**

**13.** **(Higher tier only)** A shape is rotated through 90° clockwise about the origin, and the image is then translated by (2 over 0).

**(a)** Show that the combined rule is (x, y) → (y + 2, −x). **[1]**

**(b)** Find the invariant point of the combined transformation. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe fully the single transformation. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **(−2, −5)** [1]

**(b)** (x, y) → (−y, −x) gives **(1, −4)** [1]

**(c)** (x, y) → (x, 2 − y) gives **(7, −1)** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* For y = 1, the point is 2 units above the line, so its image is 2 units below it.

**2.** (x, y) → (y, −x) [1]; **(0, −1), (0, −3), (2, −3)** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Check one vertex by turning the page: (3, 0) on the positive x-axis turns clockwise to the negative y-axis, at (0, −3).

**3. (a)** **(−4, −1), (−1, −1), (−1, 1)** [1]

**(b)** **(3 over 5)** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The reverse of a translation is the negative of its vector.

**4.** Midpoint of (1, 2) and (1, −4) is (1, −1) [1]; the x-coordinates are unchanged, so the mirror line is **y = −1** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Check with another pair: (4, 3) and (4, −5) have midpoint (4, −1), also on y = −1.

**5.** **Rotation** [1]; **180°** [1]; about **(3, 3)**, the midpoint of every object–image pair [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Pairs (0, 1) and (6, 5), (2, 1) and (4, 5), (2, 4) and (4, 2) all have midpoint (3, 3). A half turn needs no direction.

**6.** **Rotation** [1]; **90° anticlockwise** [1]; about the **origin** (every vertex fits (x, y) → (−y, x)) [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Giving the angle without the sense, or the centre without coordinates, loses marks. A full description has three details.

**7. (a)** Relative to (1, 2): (2, 0), (4, 0), (4, 1) [1]; rotated: (0, −2), (0, −4), (1, −4) [1]; **(1, 0), (1, −2), (2, −2)** [1]

**(b)** The centre, **(1, 2)** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* (3, 2) is 2 units right of the centre, so after a quarter turn clockwise it is 2 units below the centre, at (1, 0).

**8. (a)** (x, y) → (−2 − x, y) [1]; **(−3, 1), (−5, 1), (−5, 4)** [1]

**(b)** **Every point on the line x = −1** [1]

**(c)** **Clockwise**: reflection reverses orientation [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* (1, 1) is 2 units right of x = −1, so its image is 2 units left of it, at (−3, 1).

**9. (a)** **Reflection** [1]; in the line **y = x** (the coordinates of every vertex are swapped) [1]

**(b)** The orientation is **reversed**, which a rotation never does [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Rotations and translations keep the order of the labelled vertices. A change of order means a reflection.

**10. (a)** (4 − 6 over −3 + 1) = **(−2 over −2)** [1]

**(b)** **(2 over 2)** [1]

**(c)** (5 + 2, −1 + 2) = **(7, 1)** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Successive translations add as vectors, in either order.

**11. (a)** Reflection: (1, −1), (3, −1), (1, −2); rotation (x, y) → (−y, x) [1]; **(1, 1), (1, 3), (2, 1)** [1]

**(b)** **Reflection** [1]; in the line **y = x** [1]

**(c)** **All points on the line y = x** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* The overall rule is (x, y) → (x, −y) → (y, x). A reflection followed by a rotation reverses orientation, so a single reflection is the natural candidate to test.

**12. (a)** First reflection: (x, y) → (2 − x, y) [1]; second: (2 − x, y) → (8 − (2 − x), y) = (x + 6, y) [1]; a **translation by (6 over 0)** [1]

**(b)** **(−6 over 0)** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Two reflections in parallel lines give a translation of twice the distance between the lines, in the direction from the first line to the second.

**13. (a)** Rotation: (x, y) → (y, −x); translation adds 2 to the x-coordinate, giving (y + 2, −x) [1]

**(b)** x = y + 2 and y = −x [1]; so x = −x + 2, x = 1, y = −1: **(1, −1)** [1]

**(c)** **Rotation of 90° clockwise about (1, −1)** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* A rotation followed by a translation is a rotation through the same angle about a new centre. Find the new centre as the point that does not move.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reflecting in y = x or y = −x with the wrong rule.
- Rotating about the origin when another centre is given.
- Missing the sense or the centre in a description of a rotation.
- Giving a translation vector the wrong way round.
- Reflecting in x = a or y = b by changing a sign instead of using distances from the line.
- Describing a combined transformation as two separate steps.
