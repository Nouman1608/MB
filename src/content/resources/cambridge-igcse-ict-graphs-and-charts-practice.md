They test choosing data and chart types, every label named in the syllabus, a second data series and axis, number formats, axis scales and enhancements such as extracting a pie sector. Charts are produced in the practical papers, so Section C gives a practical task with an examiner-style checklist.

## Section B: structured questions

**5.** A spreadsheet holds the following data in cells A1:D5.

| | A | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shop | Sales ($) | Staff | Total |
| 2 | North | 12500 | 6 | |
| 3 | South | 9800 | 4 | |
| 4 | East | 15200 | 7 | |
| 5 | Total | 37500 | 17 | |

**(a)** A chart is needed comparing the sales of the three shops. State the cells that should be selected. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why row 5 should not be included. **[1]**
**(c)** A second chart must show sales and staff numbers for each shop. Explain why a secondary axis would be useful. **[2]**

**6.** A pie chart shows how a family spends its monthly budget.

**(a)** Name three labels that could be added to the pie chart. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe how to make the largest sector stand out. **[1]**
**(c)** The chart will be printed on a black-and-white printer. Describe one change to make the sectors easy to tell apart. **[1]**

**7.** A column chart shows the value of sales for each month. The value axis currently runs from 0 to 20000 with labels such as 15000.

**(a)** Describe how to make the axis run from 0 to 25000 in steps of 5000. **[2]**
**(b)** The axis labels must show the dollar sign with no decimal places. Describe the formatting needed. **[2]**
**(c)** Name two axis titles that should be added. **[2]**

## Section C: practical-style task

**8.** Enter this data in a spreadsheet: months January to June in A2:A7, *Visitors* in B2:B7 (420, 380, 510, 640, 720, 860) and *Income ($)* in C2:C7 (2100, 1900, 2550, 3200, 3600, 4300), with headings in row 1.

Create a chart that:
- shows *Visitors* as columns and *Income ($)* as a line on a secondary axis
- has the title *Visitors and Income, January to June*
- has a category axis title and both value axis titles
- has the visitors axis from 0 to 1000 in steps of 200
- shows income values with a $ symbol and 0 decimal places
- has a legend. **[8]**

## Worked answers

**1.** Pie chart. **[1]**

**2.** Line graph. **[1]**

**3.** Legend. **[1]**

**4.** Data in cells or ranges that are not next to each other (separate blocks). **[1]**

**5. (a)** A1:B4 (or A2:B4) — the shop names and their sales (1 for shop names, 1 for sales without the total). **[2]**
**(b)** It is a total, not a shop; including it would add a fourth, much larger bar and distort the comparison. **[1]**
**(c)** Sales are in thousands of dollars and staff numbers are below 10, so on one axis the staff values would be too small to see (1); a secondary axis gives staff numbers their own scale so both series are readable (1). **[2]**

**6. (a)** Any three of: chart title; legend; sector labels; sector values; percentages. **[3]**
**(b)** Extract (explode) that sector by pulling it out from the pie. **[1]**
**(c)** Apply different fill patterns (for example stripes, dots) to each sector. **[1]**

**7. (a)** Format the value axis: set the maximum to 25000 (the minimum stays 0) (1) and set the major unit/increment to 5000 (1). **[2]**
**(b)** Format the axis numbers as currency with a $ symbol (1) and set decimal places to 0 (1). **[2]**
**(c)** A category axis title such as *Month* (1) and a value axis title such as *Sales ($)* (1). **[2]**

**8.** 1 mark each:
1. Correct data selected (A1:C7), with no extra rows.
2. Visitors shown as columns.
3. Income shown as a line on the secondary axis.
4. Title exactly *Visitors and Income, January to June*.
5. Category axis title (for example *Month*).
6. Both value axis titles (for example *Visitors* and *Income ($)*).
7. Visitors axis minimum 0, maximum 1000, increment 200.
8. Income axis values with $ and 0 decimal places, and a legend identifying both series. **[8]**

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do examiners penalise including totals in a chart?**
A total is not one of the categories being compared, so it adds a misleading extra bar or slice. Select only the data the task specifies.

**Does every chart need a legend?**
No. A chart with one data series is usually clear without one, but a chart with two or more series, or a pie chart without sector labels, needs a legend.

**How do I know whether to use a bar chart or a line graph?**
Use a bar chart to compare separate categories and a line graph to show a trend over time or continuous data.
