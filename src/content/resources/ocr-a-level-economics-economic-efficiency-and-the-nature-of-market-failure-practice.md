## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between productive efficiency and allocative efficiency. **[4]**

**2.** Distinguish between static efficiency and dynamic efficiency, using an example. **[4]**

**3.** A small bakery in Country L (illustrative) has total cost TC = 72 + 8Q + 2Q², where Q is thousands of loaves and TC is in £ thousand. Marginal cost is MC = 8 + 4Q.

**(a)** Calculate average cost at 3, 6 and 9 thousand loaves. **[3]**
**(b)** State the productively efficient output and use MC to explain it. **[2]**
**(c)** The market price is £44. Find the output where P = MC, and the profit there. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain whether the bakery is productively and allocatively efficient at that output. **[2]**

**4.** A sheltered firm in Country M (illustrative) has unit cost £15. A comparable firm in a competitive market has unit cost £12. The sheltered firm makes 80,000 units a year.

**(a)** Calculate the extra annual cost compared with the competitive firm. **[2]**
**(b)** Express the excess as a percentage of the competitive firm's unit cost. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain two reasons why x-inefficiency may occur in the sheltered firm. **[2]**

**5.** A monopolist in Country N (illustrative) faces demand P = 66 − 2Q, so MR = 66 − 4Q. Marginal cost is constant at £30. Q is in thousands and P is in £.

**(a)** Find the allocatively efficient output and price. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the output and price that maximise the monopolist's profit. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the welfare loss. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain one reason why the monopolist may still be dynamically efficient. **[2]**

**6.** State whether each is a positive or negative externality, and of consumption or production.

**(a)** Late-night customers of a bar keep nearby residents awake. **(b)** A stone quarry spreads dust over neighbouring farms. **(c)** A person is vaccinated and so passes on less infection. **(d)** A firm trains staff, some of whom later join rival firms. **[4]**
**(e)** For (b), state whether market output is above or below the social optimum. **[1]**

**7.** A quarry in Country P (illustrative) faces MPB = 62 − 2Q and MPC = 2 + 2Q. Each unit imposes an external cost of £16 on farmers. Q is in thousands and prices in £.

**(a)** State MSC. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the market output and price. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the socially optimal output and price. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the welfare loss at the market output. **[2]**

**8.** A health screening service in Country R (illustrative) has MPB = 75 − 5Q and MPC = MSC = 15 + Q. Each screening gives an external benefit of £18. Q is in thousands and prices in £.

**(a)** Find the market output and price. **[2]**
**(b)** State MSB, then find the socially optimal output and price. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the welfare gain from reaching the optimum. **[2]**

**9.** Using a diagram that you describe in words, explain why a negative externality of consumption causes market failure. **[6]**

**10.** Evaluate whether a monopoly is always less efficient than a perfectly competitive market. **[10]**

**11.** Evaluate how externalities affect the markets for education and health. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Productive efficiency means output is made at the minimum average cost [1], the lowest point of the AC curve where MC = AC [1]. Allocative efficiency is using resources to produce what consumers value most [1], where price equals marginal cost [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a definition and its condition for each type. A statement that one is "about cost" and the other "about price" alone is thin.

**2.** Static efficiency is efficiency at a point in time [1], given resources and technology [1]. Dynamic efficiency is efficiency over time [1], for example a firm investing in new machinery that lowers its costs in later years [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A named, developed example earns the last point. An example with no link to time or cost change does not show dynamic efficiency.

**3. (a)** AC = TC ÷ Q [1]. At 3: (72 + 24 + 18) ÷ 3 = 114 ÷ 3 = **£38**; at 6: 192 ÷ 6 = **£32** [1]; at 9: 306 ÷ 9 = **£34** [1].
**(b)** **6 thousand loaves**, the lowest AC [1]; MC = 8 + 24 = £32, equal to AC [1].
**(c)** P = MC: 44 = 8 + 4Q [1], so **Q = 9** [1]. AC = £34, so profit = (44 − 34) × 9 = **£90 thousand** [1].
**(d)** Allocatively efficient, because P = MC [1]. Not productively efficient, because AC at 9 is £34, above the £32 minimum at 6 [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the working for each AC figure. In part (d), compare the actual AC with the lowest AC and quote both.

**4. (a)** Extra cost per unit = 15 − 12 = £3 [1]; 3 × 80,000 = **£240,000** [1].
**(b)** 3 ÷ 12 × 100 = **25%** [1].
**(c)** Little competition means no pressure to cut costs [1]. Managers may pursue goals other than lowest cost, or effort and monitoring may be slack [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Each reason needs a link to why cost is above the efficient level. Simply writing "it is a monopoly" does not explain the cause.

**5. (a)** P = MC: 66 − 2Q = 30 [1], so **Q = 18 thousand and P = £30** [1].
**(b)** MR = MC: 66 − 4Q = 30 [1], so **Q = 9 thousand** [1], and P = 66 − 18 = **£48** [1].
**(c)** Triangle with base 18 − 9 = 9 and height 48 − 30 = 18 [1]; area = ½ × 9 × 18 = **£81 thousand** [1].
**(d)** Supernormal profit can be used to fund research and new equipment [1], lowering costs or creating better products over time [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (d) needs the route from profit to investment to lower cost. Part (c) uses the gap between price and MC at the monopoly output, not at the efficient one.

**6. (a)** Negative externality of consumption [1]. **(b)** Negative externality of production [1]. **(c)** Positive externality of consumption [1]. **(d)** Positive externality of production [1].
**(e)** **Above** the social optimum, because MSC exceeds MPC [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name both parts of each answer, the sign and the activity. "Negative externality" alone does not say whether it arises from consumption or production.

**7. (a)** MSC = MPC + MEC = **18 + 2Q** [1].
**(b)** 62 − 2Q = 2 + 2Q [1], so **Q = 15 thousand and P = £32** [1].
**(c)** 62 − 2Q = 18 + 2Q [1], so **Q = 11 thousand and P = £40** [1].
**(d)** At Q = 15: MSC = 48 and MSB = 32, a gap of 16 [1]. Welfare loss = ½ × (15 − 11) × 16 = **£32 thousand** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the MSC curve, not MPC, to find the social optimum. A common slip is to take the gap at the wrong output.

**8. (a)** 75 − 5Q = 15 + Q [1], so **Q = 10 thousand and P = £25** [1].
**(b)** MSB = 75 − 5Q + 18 = **93 − 5Q** [1]. 93 − 5Q = 15 + Q gives **Q = 13 thousand** [1] and P = 15 + 13 = **£28** [1].
**(c)** At Q = 10: MSB = 43 and MSC = 25, a gap of 18 [1]. Gain = ½ × 3 × 18 = **£27 thousand** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Add the external benefit to MPB, not to MPC. State the direction of the shift so the working can be followed.

**9.** The market sets output where MPB equals MPC [1]. Consuming each unit imposes an external cost on third parties, such as noise or congestion [1]. So MSB lies below MPB [1]. Consumers ignore the external cost and consume at the market output, where MPB = MPC [1]. The social optimum is at the lower output where MSB = MSC [1]. Between the two outputs MSC exceeds MSB, so resources are over-allocated to the good and there is a welfare loss [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Name each curve and the two output levels. A chain from private decision to ignored cost to over-consumption gains more than a statement that "externalities cause market failure".

**10.** A monopoly sets price above MC, so it is allocatively inefficient [1], and it restricts output below the competitive level [1]. A perfectly competitive firm sets P = MC [1], and in the long run reaches the lowest AC [1]. However, perfect competition earns only normal profit in the long run, leaving little for research [1], while monopoly profit can fund innovation [1]. A monopoly protected from rivals may be x-inefficient [1]. A large firm may have economies of scale giving lower AC than many small ones [1]. Perfect competition is a benchmark rather than a description of most markets [1]. Overall a monopoly is not always less efficient: the outcome depends on the size of the price gap, the scale economies and how the profit is used [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* A one-sided answer that says "monopoly is inefficient" does not evaluate. Use arguments both ways and give a reasoned judgement.

**11.** Education has a positive externality of consumption, because skills benefit employers and society [1]. Individuals count only private benefit, so MSB exceeds MPB and education is under-consumed [1]. Vaccination has a positive externality because it lowers infection risk for others [1], whereas smoking has a negative one because it harms others, causing over-consumption [1]. The result is a welfare loss [1]. However, external benefits are hard to measure, so the right level of provision is uncertain [1]. Education benefits appear over many years [1]. Under-consumption may also arise from low income or poor information, not only externalities [1]. A small externality may cost more to correct than the gain [1]. Overall externalities are significant in both markets, but the extent is uncertain and judged by size and measurement [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each market to a specific externality. A final judgement that compares the markets or weighs size earns credit that a list does not.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using P = MC for allocative efficiency in a market with an externality, where MSB = MSC applies.
- Reading the welfare loss triangle from the wrong output or using MPC instead of MSC.
- Giving productive efficiency as "low cost" with no mention of the lowest point of AC.
- Not stating which of the four externality cases applies, or the direction of market output.
- Adding the external cost to the wrong curve.
- Evaluation that lists points but never weighs size, timing or measurement.
- Treating monopoly as always worse without considering scale economies or dynamic gains.
- Leaving out units, such as thousands of units and £ thousand.
