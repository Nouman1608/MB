**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **A Level** subtopic
34.4: acid/base properties and zwitterions "to include the isoelectric point", di- and tripeptides,
and electrophoresis "on mixtures of amino acids and dipeptides at varying pHs".

## Structure

- General formula **H₂NCH(R)COOH**.
- Glycine R = H (not chiral); alanine R = CH₃; all others chiral.
- Some R groups carry an extra acid (aspartic acid, –CH₂COOH) or base (lysine, –(CH₂)₄NH₂) group.

## Acid–base behaviour

| Condition | Species | Overall charge |
|---|---|---|
| low pH (acid added) | ⁺H₃NCH(R)COOH | +1 |
| isoelectric point | ⁺H₃NCH(R)COO⁻ (zwitterion) | 0 |
| high pH (alkali added) | H₂NCH(R)COO⁻ | −1 |

    H₂NCH(R)COOH + HCl  → ⁺H₃NCH(R)COOH Cl⁻
    H₂NCH(R)COOH + NaOH → H₂NCH(R)COO⁻Na⁺ + H₂O

- Amino acids are **amphoteric** (react with acids and with bases).
- **Zwitterion:** internal proton transfer from –COOH to –NH₂; charges + and −, no net charge.
- Zwitterions explain **high melting points** (ionic attractions in the solid) and **solubility in
  water**.

## The isoelectric point

- Definition: the **pH at which the amino acid has no overall charge** (exists as the zwitterion).
- Each amino acid has its own value.
- **pH < isoelectric point → positive; pH > isoelectric point → negative; pH = isoelectric point →
  neutral.**
- Extra –COOH in R → **low** isoelectric point. Extra –NH₂ in R → **high** isoelectric point.

| At about pH 7 | Side chain | Overall charge |
|---|---|---|
| glycine | –H | 0 (zwitterion) |
| aspartic acid | –CH₂COO⁻ | −1 |
| lysine | –(CH₂)₄NH₃⁺ | +1 |

## Peptides

- –COOH of one + –NH₂ of the next → **peptide (amide) bond –CONH–** + H₂O (condensation).
- Dipeptide has **one** peptide bond; tripeptide has **two**.
- Gly–Ala ≠ Ala–Gly (different structures).
- From a mixture of two amino acids: **4** dipeptides (Gly–Gly, Gly–Ala, Ala–Gly, Ala–Ala).
- Three different amino acids, each once: **6** tripeptides. Two amino acids, any order with
  repeats: **8** tripeptides.
- **Hydrolysis:** heat under reflux with aqueous acid → amino acids (as cations).

## Electrophoresis

| Charge at buffer pH | Moves towards | Distance |
|---|---|---|
| positive | cathode (−) | further for larger charge |
| zero | does not move | stays at origin |
| negative | anode (+) | further for larger charge |

- For equal charge, the **smaller** (lower mass) species moves **further**; a dipeptide usually moves
  less far than an amino acid with the same charge.
- Best separation: choose a pH that gives the components **different charges**.
- Apparatus set-up is **not** tested.

## Charge on a dipeptide at a given pH

Work through the three places where charge can sit:

1. **Free amine end:** –NH₃⁺ at low pH, –NH₂ at high pH.
2. **Free carboxyl end:** –COOH at low pH, –COO⁻ at high pH.
3. **Side chains:** add +1 for each protonated side-chain amine, −1 for each ionised side-chain acid.

Example: Gly–Ala, H₂NCH₂CONHCH(CH₃)COOH, is ⁺H₃NCH₂CONHCH(CH₃)COOH (+1) in strong acid,
⁺H₃NCH₂CONHCH(CH₃)COO⁻ (0) near its isoelectric point, and H₂NCH₂CONHCH(CH₃)COO⁻ (−1) in strong
alkali. The –CONH– link is never counted.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| amino acid | molecule with –NH₂ and –COOH on the same carbon, H₂NCH(R)COOH |
| zwitterion | species with both + and − charges and no overall charge |
| isoelectric point | pH at which the amino acid has no overall charge |
| amphoteric | reacts as both an acid and a base |
| peptide bond | amide link –CONH– between amino acids |
| dipeptide / tripeptide | two / three amino acids joined by peptide bonds |
| electrophoresis | separation of charged species by movement in an electric field |
| buffer | solution that keeps the pH nearly constant during electrophoresis |

## Exam traps

- At the isoelectric point, draw the **zwitterion**, not H₂NCH(R)COOH.
- **Positive** species move to the **negative** electrode (cathode).
- Do not assume the isoelectric point is 7.
- Count Gly–Ala and Ala–Gly separately.
- Distance depends on **charge and size**.
- A dipeptide's charge comes only from its free –NH₂ / –NH₃⁺ end, its free –COOH / –COO⁻ end, and any
  charged side chains; the peptide bond itself is not charged.

## Self-test

1. Give the formula of alanine.
2. Draw (write) the zwitterion of alanine.
3. Write the equation for glycine with HCl.
4. Write the equation for glycine with NaOH.
5. Define the isoelectric point.
6. Give the overall charge on lysine at pH 7 and explain it.
7. Write the structure of the dipeptide Ala–Gly.
8. How many dipeptides can form from a mixture of glycine and serine?
9. Give the conditions to hydrolyse a dipeptide into its amino acids.
10. An amino acid has isoelectric point 5.1. Predict its movement in electrophoresis at pH 8.0.
11. Two species both carry −1 at pH 9: an amino acid and a dipeptide. Which travels further, and why?
12. Explain why amino acids have high melting points.

**Answers:**

1. H₂NCH(CH₃)COOH.
2. ⁺H₃NCH(CH₃)COO⁻.
3. H₂NCH₂COOH + HCl → ⁺H₃NCH₂COOH Cl⁻.
4. H₂NCH₂COOH + NaOH → H₂NCH₂COO⁻Na⁺ + H₂O.
5. The pH at which the amino acid exists as the zwitterion with no overall charge.
6. +1: the side-chain –NH₂ is protonated to –NH₃⁺ while the main chain is a zwitterion.
7. H₂NCH(CH₃)CONHCH₂COOH.
8. Four: Gly–Gly, Gly–Ser, Ser–Gly, Ser–Ser.
9. Heat under reflux with aqueous acid (for example HCl(aq)).
10. pH 8.0 is above 5.1, so it is negatively charged and moves towards the anode (+).
11. The amino acid, because it is smaller (lower mass) for the same charge.
12. In the solid they exist as zwitterions, so there are strong ionic attractions between molecules.
