### Section A

**1.** Decree, measure or divine power [1].

**2.** Sahih al-Bukhari [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: Allah's knowledge (*ilm*); writing in the Preserved Tablet (*kitabah*); Allah's will (*mashee'ah*); creation (*khalq*).

**4.** Any two, [1] each: patience (*sabr*); saying "Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un"; trust in Allah (*tawakkul*); refusing to despair; seeking Allah's help in prayer; accepting what cannot be changed.

**5.** Predestination is the belief that Allah knows everything that will happen [1] and has written it [1]. Nothing happens unless Allah wills it [1], and He creates all things and the means by which they happen [1].

**6.** For each of up to two points: point [1] and development [1].

- **Knowing is not forcing** [1]: Allah's knowledge of what a person will choose does not remove the choice [1].
- **Commands and judgement** [1]: Allah gives commands and will judge people, which would be unjust unless they have real choice [1].
- **Capacity** [1]: Allah does not burden a soul beyond what it can bear (2:286), so people can act [1].
- **Using the means** [1]: Muslims plan and work, then rely on Allah, as shown by "tie it and trust" [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **It is part of faith** [1]: it is one of the six key beliefs, and rejecting it would leave belief in Allah's power and knowledge incomplete [1].
- **It gives comfort in hardship** [1]: Muslims believe suffering is within Allah's wisdom, so they stay patient [1].
- **It protects Tawhid** [1]: nothing is outside Allah's control, so Muslims do not trust luck or spirits [1].
- **It encourages gratitude and humility** [1]: success is seen as Allah's gift and not as proof of personal greatness [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Indeed, all things We created with predestination" (Qur'an 54:49) [2].
- "Allah does not require of any soul more than what it can afford" (2:286) [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Ways (AO1):

- **Patience** [1]: a Muslim who loses something accepts it as part of Allah's plan and does not become bitter [1].
- **Effort and trust** [1]: they work hard, then leave the result to Allah, which reduces anxiety [1].
- **Responsibility** [1]: they cannot excuse bad behaviour by saying "it was written", so they take care with their choices [1].
- **Gratitude** [1]: they thank Allah for ease, which keeps them humble [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- Saying "Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un" (Qur'an 2:156) when affected by a loss [2].
- Hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari 1362: "good deeds are made easy for the blessed", which encourages effort [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- The statement is a common misunderstanding that the Hadith itself addresses: the man asks if people should stop acting, and the Prophet (pbuh) tells them to act.
- Muslims believe Allah knows each person's end, but nobody else does, so each person must act as one hoping for Paradise.
- Belief in predestination encourages trust and patience, not passivity; it does not release anyone from duty.
- The reference to a funeral reminds the listeners that death is certain, which adds urgency to good deeds.
- **For the statement:** a person might misuse the belief to avoid effort, and some groups in history stressed the decree so strongly that human choice seemed to disappear.
- **Against the statement:** the Hadith, the verses recited ("gives in charity", "Allah-fearing", "believes") and the Qur'anic principle that people are judged by their deeds all show that responsibility remains.
- **Judgement:** the statement is a misreading; for most Muslims, belief in predestination increases responsibility, since it makes them take every action seriously.

*Use of the passage*

- "Should we not depend on what has been written for us" states the objection.
- "The good deeds are made easy for the blessed" gives the Prophet's reply.
- "As for him who gives (in charity) and is Allah-fearing And believes in the Best reward" links Al-Qadr to action.
- "place either in Paradise or in Hell assigned" shows Allah's knowledge.
