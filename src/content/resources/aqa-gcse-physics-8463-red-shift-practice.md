## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State what is meant by red-shift. **[1]**

**2.** Light from a distant galaxy is red-shifted. State what this shows about the galaxy's motion. **[1]**

**3.** Galaxy M shows a smaller red-shift than galaxy N. State which galaxy is further away. **[1]**

**4.** Complete the sentence: the Big Bang theory suggests that the universe began from a very small region that was
extremely ________ and ________. **[1]**

**5.** Name the type of observation, made since 1998, that suggests distant galaxies are receding ever faster. **[1]**

**6.** Give the name used for whatever is causing the expansion of the universe to speed up. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A dark line in the laboratory spectrum of an element has a wavelength of 590 nm. The same line is observed in
the light from three galaxies.

| Galaxy | Observed wavelength (nm) |
|---|---|
| J | 602 |
| K | 620 |
| L | 649 |

**(a)** Calculate the increase in wavelength for each galaxy. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in wavelength for galaxy L. **[2]**
**(c)** Put the galaxies in order of distance from Earth, nearest first, and explain your order. **[2]**

**8.** Explain, in terms of wave crests, why light from a galaxy that is moving away from us has a longer observed
wavelength. **[3]**

**9.** Explain how astronomers can tell that a line in the spectrum of a galaxy has been shifted, rather than being a
line from a different element. **[2]**

**10.** A student uses a balloon with dots drawn on it to model the universe.

**(a)** Describe what happens to the dots as the balloon is inflated. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the model shows why more distant galaxies recede faster. **[2]**
**(c)** Give one way the model shows that the universe is not expanding from a central point in space. **[1]**

**11.** Explain how the red-shift of light from distant galaxies provides evidence for the Big Bang theory. **[6]**

**12.** Scientists say there is still much about the universe that is not understood.

**(a)** Name two examples of things that are not yet understood. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the observations of supernovae since 1998 show that scientific theories can change. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The observed increase in the wavelength of light from (most) distant galaxies [1]

**2.** It is moving away from us (receding) [1]

**3.** **N** [1]

**4.** Hot; dense (both needed) [1]

**5.** Observations of supernovae [1]

**6.** Dark energy [1]

**7. (a)** J: 12 nm; K: 30 nm; L: 59 nm (all three for 2 marks; two correct for 1 mark) [2]
**(b)** 59 ÷ 590 × 100 [1]; = **10 %** [1]
**(c)** J, K, L [1]; a bigger red-shift means the galaxy is receding faster, and faster-receding galaxies are further away [1] **[6]**

**8.** As the galaxy moves away, each wave crest is emitted from a point further away than the one before [1]; so each crest has further to travel and the crests arrive more spread out [1]; a greater distance between crests is a longer wavelength [1]
*Examiner insight:* "The light is stretched" with no reference to crests or spacing earns at most one mark here.

**9.** The pattern of lines matches the pattern for a known element in the laboratory [1]; but every line in the pattern is at a longer wavelength by a similar fraction [1]
*Examiner insight:* One shifted line on its own could be mistaken for another element; the matching pattern of several lines is what makes the identification reliable.

**10. (a)** All the dots move further apart from each other [1]; dots that are further apart separate more quickly [1]
**(b)** There is more stretching balloon surface between distant dots [1]; so the distance between them increases faster, just as more distant galaxies recede faster [1]
**(c)** Every dot sees all the others moving away, and no dot on the surface is at the centre [1] **[5]**

**11.** Marked by levels; a top-level answer links these points in order:
- Light from most distant galaxies is red-shifted [1]
- So these galaxies are moving away from us [1]
- The further away the galaxy, the faster it recedes (bigger red-shift) [1]
- This shows the universe (space itself) is expanding [1]
- So in the past the universe was smaller [1]
- Tracing back, it began from a very small, extremely hot and dense region, as the Big Bang theory suggests, so red-shift supports the theory [1]

*Examiner insight:* Answers that jump from "galaxies are moving away" straight to "so there was a Big Bang" miss the expanding and smaller-in-the-past links and stay in the middle level.

**12. (a)** Dark mass [1]; dark energy [1]
**(b)** Earlier models did not predict that the expansion was speeding up [1]; the new observations suggested distant galaxies are receding ever faster, so ideas about the universe had to change to include this (for example the idea of dark energy) [1] **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining red-shift as "light turning red".
- Linking a bigger red-shift to a nearer galaxy.
- Comparing galaxy wavelengths with each other rather than with the laboratory value.
- Describing an explosion from a centre instead of space expanding.
- Big Bang chains with missing middle links.
- Saying red-shift proves the Big Bang.
