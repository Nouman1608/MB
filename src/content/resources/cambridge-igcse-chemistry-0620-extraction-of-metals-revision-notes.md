## Extraction and reactivity

| Metals | Method | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al (above carbon) | electrolysis | more reactive than carbon, so carbon cannot remove the oxygen |
| Zn, Fe, Cu (below carbon) | heat with carbon | carbon is more reactive, so it removes the oxygen |
| Au (very unreactive) | found uncombined | does not readily form compounds |

**Rule:** the higher the metal in the series, the harder it is to extract. Electrolysis is costly (lots of electricity), so it is used only when needed.

## Blast furnace: raw materials

- **Hematite** – iron(III) oxide, the iron ore.
- **Coke** – carbon; fuel and source of carbon monoxide.
- **Limestone** – calcium carbonate; removes sand impurity.
- **Hot air** – oxygen for burning the coke.

## Blast furnace: stages

| | Core (words) | *(Extended)* equation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | coke burns → heat + carbon dioxide | C + O₂ → CO₂ |
| 2 | CO₂ reduced by coke → carbon monoxide | C + CO₂ → 2CO |
| 3 | CO reduces iron(III) oxide → iron | Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ |
| 4 | limestone decomposes → calcium oxide | CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ |
| 5 | CaO + sand → slag | CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ |

- **Reducing agent:** carbon monoxide.
- **Slag:** calcium silicate; formed by basic CaO neutralising acidic SiO₂.
- **Products:** molten iron at the bottom; molten slag floats on it; waste gases out of the top.

## Aluminium

**Core:** main ore **bauxite**; extracted by **electrolysis**.

*(Extended)*:

- Electrolyte: **aluminium oxide dissolved in molten cryolite**.
- **Cryolite:** lowers the temperature needed to keep the electrolyte molten (pure Al₂O₃ melts above 2000 °C), saving energy.
- Electrodes: carbon (graphite).

| Electrode | Half-equation | Type |
|---|---|---|
| cathode (−) | Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al | reduction |
| anode (+) | 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻ | oxidation |

- **Anodes replaced** because oxygen reacts with the hot carbon: C + O₂ → CO₂.
- Overall: 2Al₂O₃ → 4Al + 3O₂.
- Purification of bauxite: **not required**.

## Simple proportion

- Mr Fe₂O₃ = 160; contains 112 of iron.
- So 160 t of iron(III) oxide → 112 t of iron (70 % by mass).
- Mr Al₂O₃ = 102; contains 54 of aluminium.
- So 102 t of aluminium oxide → 54 t of aluminium.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ore | rock containing a metal compound from which the metal can be extracted |
| Reduction | loss of oxygen, or gain of electrons |
| Oxidation | gain of oxygen, or loss of electrons |
| Reducing agent | a substance that reduces another and is itself oxidised (CO in the blast furnace) |
| Slag | calcium silicate, formed from calcium oxide and sand |
| Thermal decomposition | breaking down a compound by heating (limestone → calcium oxide) |
| Electrolysis | breaking down an ionic compound, molten or in solution, using electricity |
| Cryolite *(Extended)* | the molten solvent for aluminium oxide in the electrolysis cell |

## Exam traps

- **CO, not C, reduces iron(III) oxide** in the main reduction step.
- **Limestone removes sand (silicon(IV) oxide),** not iron.
- **Core answers do not need symbol equations** but must give the stages in the right order.
- *(Extended)* **Cryolite is not a catalyst.**
- *(Extended)* **Anode: 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻,** not O²⁻ → O.
- *(Extended)* **Aluminium forms at the cathode,** which is negative.
- **Aluminium cannot be extracted with carbon** because it is more reactive than carbon, not because it is "too expensive".

## Self-test

1. Name the main ore of iron and of aluminium.
2. Explain why zinc can be extracted by heating its oxide with carbon, but magnesium cannot.
3. State the **four** raw materials of the blast furnace.
4. Describe, in words, how carbon monoxide is formed in the blast furnace.
5. Name the substance that reduces iron(III) oxide in the blast furnace.
6. Explain why limestone is added to the blast furnace.
7. *(Extended)* Write the equation for the formation of slag.
8. *(Extended)* Explain the role of cryolite.
9. *(Extended)* Write the half-equation at the cathode in aluminium extraction.
10. *(Extended)* Explain why the anodes must be replaced.
11. Calculate the maximum mass of iron from 320 t of pure Fe₂O₃ (Mr = 160).
12. Why does molten slag form a separate layer above the iron?

**Answers:**

1. Hematite; bauxite.
2. Zinc is below carbon in the reactivity series, so carbon can remove oxygen from zinc oxide; magnesium is above carbon, so carbon cannot.
3. Hematite, coke, limestone, hot air.
4. Carbon dioxide from the burning coke is reduced by more hot coke to carbon monoxide.
5. Carbon monoxide.
6. It decomposes to calcium oxide, which reacts with the sand impurity to form slag that can be removed.
7. CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃.
8. Aluminium oxide dissolves in molten cryolite, so the electrolyte is molten at a much lower temperature, saving energy.
9. Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al.
10. Oxygen formed at the hot carbon anodes reacts with them to make carbon dioxide, so they burn away.
11. 112 × 320 ÷ 160 = 224 t.
12. Slag is less dense than molten iron, so it floats on top.
