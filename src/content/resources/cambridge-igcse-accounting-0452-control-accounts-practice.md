## Section A

**1.** State **two** purposes of a purchases ledger control account. **[2]**

**2.** Which item is entered on the credit side of a sales ledger control account?

A: a dishonoured cheque

B: interest charged on an overdue account

C: a contra with the purchases ledger

D: a refund paid to a customer **[1]**

**3.** From which book of prime entry is the discount received figure in the purchases ledger control account taken?

A: purchases journal

B: cash book

C: general journal

D: purchases returns journal **[1]**

**4.** Explain why cash sales are not entered in the sales ledger control account. **[2]**

**5.** Explain what is meant by a contra entry between the sales ledger and the purchases ledger. **[2]**

**6.** A sales ledger control account has an opening debit balance of 6,300. During the month: credit sales 14,800; receipts 13,950; discount allowed 280; sales returns 470. Calculate the closing balance. **[2]**

**12.** Redfern Garden Machinery is moving from manual ledgers to accounting software.

**(a)** In the manual system, one clerk keeps the sales ledger and a supervisor prepares the sales ledger control account. Explain how this helps to prevent fraud.

**(b)** Explain **two** ways the move to software will affect the preparation and use of the control accounts. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two [2]: an arithmetic check on the purchases ledger; helps locate errors; gives total trade payables quickly; discourages fraud through separation of duties; information for managing payments. Total: 2 marks.

**2.** **C** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** **B** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**4.** Cash sales are paid for immediately, so no customer account in the sales ledger is involved [1]; the control account only summarises entries in the sales ledger [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** A contra sets off an amount owed by a business that is both a customer and a supplier against the amount owed to it [1], reducing both the sales ledger balance and the purchases ledger balance by the same amount [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**6.** 6,300 + 14,800 - 13,950 - 280 - 470 [1] = **6,400** debit [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**11.** (i) Yes [1]: the control account is posted from the overcast journal total, so it is 200 higher, while customers' accounts are correct [1].

(ii) No [1]: the total of customer balances is unchanged and the control account is correct; only two individual accounts are wrong [1].

(iii) No [1]: nothing is recorded in either the control account or the customer's account, so both are overstated by the same 40 [1].

(iv) Yes [1]: the control account is posted from the cash book, so it omits the 150 while the customer's account includes it [1]. Total: 8 marks.

**12. (a)** The supervisor prepares the control account from the books of prime entry totals, independently of the clerk [1]; if the clerk altered or stole from customer accounts, the total of the ledger would no longer agree with the control account, so the fraud would be noticed [1].

**(b)** Two effects, each identified [1] and explained [1], for example: the control accounts are produced automatically as transactions are posted, so no separate preparation is needed and they are always up to date [2]; because they come from the same postings as the ledgers, they no longer act as an independent check, and input errors such as posting to the wrong customer are not revealed [2]; totals and overdue lists can be produced instantly, improving credit control. Total: 6 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Including cash sales, cash purchases, trade discount or the allowance for irrecoverable debts.
- Placing a dishonoured customer cheque or a refund to a customer on the credit side of the sales ledger control account.
- Placing a refund received from a supplier on the debit side of the purchases ledger control account.
- Bringing down only one balance when there are debit and credit balances.
- Not labelling the missing figure in a reconstruction.
