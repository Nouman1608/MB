These notes condense key question 2.3 of Cambridge O Level Sociology (2251), *What influences our
social identity?* Four traditional aspects (age, gender, ethnicity and social class) shape identity
differently in different societies. Newer influences are online life, globalisation, and the
postmodern claim that identity is now chosen through consumption and lifestyle. Most questions ask you
to weigh the old against the new.

Fuller explanations: [key question 2.3 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-sociology-2251-kq-2-3/).

## Syllabus coverage

2251 syllabus 2025–2027, **2.3**: 2.3.1 age, gender, ethnicity, social class and their impact in
different societies; 2.3.2 the digital self and online identities; 2.3.3 globalisation (cultural
defence, hybrid identities, homogenisation; cultural diversity, multiculturalism, global culture);
2.3.4 postmodernist views of identity as chosen (digital identities; consumption and lifestyle in a
"pick and mix" society).

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Social identity** | Sense of self based on group membership and how others see us |
| **Ethnicity** | Shared culture based on language, religion, customs and history |
| **Hegemonic masculinity** | The dominant ideal of manhood in a society (Connell) |
| **Cultural capital** | Tastes, knowledge and manners valued by schools (Bourdieu) |
| **Digital self** | The identity a person presents online |
| **Virtual community** | Group whose relationships are formed and kept online (Rheingold, 1993) |
| **Impression management** | Controlling how others see us (Goffman, 1959) |
| **Globalisation** | Growing interconnection of societies through trade, media, migration and technology |
| **Homogenisation** | Identities and cultures becoming more alike |
| **Hybrid identity** | Identity combining elements of more than one culture |
| **Cultural defence** | Strengthening a traditional identity in response to outside pressure |
| **Multiculturalism** | Policy or belief that different cultures deserve equal respect |
| **Global culture** | Media, brands, music and styles shared across countries |
| **"Pick and mix" identity** | Postmodern idea of building identity from many options |

## The four aspects at a glance

| Aspect | Socially constructed because… | Evidence or example | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age | Meaning of life stages varies | Ariès: childhood developed gradually as a separate stage | Rights, roles, respect |
| Gender | Masculinity and femininity are learned | Connell: hegemonic masculinity | Careers, domestic roles, freedom |
| Ethnicity | Shared culture is learned and maintained | Minority and migrant communities emphasising language and religion | Belonging; or stereotyping and exclusion |
| Class | Lifestyle and tastes differ by class | Bourdieu: cultural capital | Accent, aspirations, life chances |

**Debate on class:** some argue class identity has weakened with the decline of traditional
industries; others argue it still shapes identity even when people avoid class labels.

## The digital self

- **Positives:** explore identities; support networks; contact with diaspora; creativity and
  activism.
- **Negatives:** pressure to look perfect; cyberbullying; privacy risks; echo chambers.
- **Theory link:** Goffman's impression management becomes deliberate online.

## Globalisation: three effects

1. **Homogenisation** — global brands and media make identities alike (Ritzer's McDonaldization,
   1993).
2. **Hybridity** — new mixed identities among people of migrant heritage.
3. **Cultural defence** — traditional identities strengthened (Bruce: Catholicism and Polish national
   identity under communism).

| | Positive | Negative |
|---|---|---|
| Cultural diversity | More choice and understanding | Tension, feelings of threat |
| Multiculturalism | Minority identities respected (Canada's policy from 1971) | May keep communities separate |
| Global culture | Connects people worldwide | Weakens local languages and traditions |

## Postmodernism

- Traditional sources of identity have weakened.
- Identity is built through **consumption**, **lifestyle** and **digital identities**.
- **Criticisms:** choice depends on money; ascribed characteristics still matter; family and
  religion still constrain; cultural defence shows inherited identities persist.

## Exam traps

- Homogenisation (becoming alike) is not hybridity (mixing).
- Always give both positive and negative impacts when the syllabus says so.
- Age, gender and ethnicity are not just biology; explain the social meaning.
- "Different societies" in the syllabus invites comparison; use it.
- Postmodernism must be evaluated, not just described.

## Worked example: a 6-mark "explain three" answer (practice)

*Explain three ways social class may influence a person's identity.*

1. **Lifestyle and tastes:** class shapes leisure, food and dress; Bourdieu linked these tastes to
   cultural capital, which gives middle-class children confidence in school (2).
2. **Occupation:** work is a major source of identity; manual and professional jobs carry different
   status and values (2).
3. **Aspirations:** class affects expectations about education and careers, shaping how young people
   see their future selves (2).

## Self-test

1. Define social identity.
2. What did Ariès argue about childhood?
3. What is hegemonic masculinity?
4. Why may ethnic identity become stronger among migrants?
5. What is cultural capital?
6. Give one positive and one negative impact of social media on identity.
7. How does Goffman's idea of impression management apply online?
8. What is the difference between homogenisation and hybrid identities?
9. Give an example of cultural defence.
10. Give one criticism of multiculturalism.
11. What do postmodernists mean by a "pick and mix" society?
12. Give two criticisms of the postmodern view of identity.

### Answers

1. The sense of who we are based on the groups we belong to and how others see us.
2. That childhood as a separate, protected stage developed gradually; medieval children were treated
   much like small adults.
3. The dominant ideal of manhood in a society, against which other identities are judged.
4. As a minority, they may emphasise language, religion and customs to keep belonging, especially if
   they face discrimination.
5. Tastes, knowledge and manners valued by schools, which benefit middle-class children.
6. Positive: support networks or contact with family abroad. Negative: cyberbullying or pressure to
   look perfect.
7. People choose photos, edit posts and present a managed image to their audience.
8. Homogenisation means identities becoming alike; hybrid identities mix different cultures.
9. Catholicism as a symbol of Polish national identity under communism (Bruce).
10. It may keep communities separate rather than building shared identity.
11. People choose identities from many options, especially through consumption and lifestyle.
12. Choice depends on money; ascribed characteristics and family or religious expectations still
    shape identity.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is globalisation good or bad for identity?** Both. Strong answers show that it can homogenise,
create hybrids and provoke cultural defence at the same time.

**Which aspect of identity matters most?** It depends on the society and the person; argue this using
comparisons.
