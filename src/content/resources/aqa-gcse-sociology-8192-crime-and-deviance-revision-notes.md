These notes condense two sub-sections of AQA GCSE Sociology (8192) Crime and deviance: 3.5.1 *The
social construction of crime and deviance* and 3.5.3 *Criminal and deviant behaviour*. Crime and
deviance are defined by society and change over time and place; Merton explains crime through
anomie, Becker through labelling, Albert Cohen through delinquent subcultures and Carlen through the
breakdown of class and gender "deals"; and class, gender, ethnicity and age all shape who offends —
or who is recorded as offending.

Full explanation: [AQA GCSE crime and deviance study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-sociology-8192-crime-and-deviance/).

## Specification coverage

- **3.5.1** — social construction of crime and deviance; explanations including anomie, labelling,
  structural, subcultural and interactionist theories; interactionist, functionalist, feminist and
  Marxist perspectives; key ideas of **Merton** and **Becker**.
- **3.5.3** — factors affecting criminal and deviant behaviour (social class, gender, ethnicity,
  age); public debates (violent crime, sentencing, young offenders, the prison system, media
  coverage); perspectives; key ideas of **Albert Cohen** and **Carlen**.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Crime** | Behaviour that breaks the law |
| **Deviance** | Behaviour that breaks social norms |
| **Social construction** | Defined by society, varying with time, place and situation |
| **Anomie** | Normlessness; "anything goes" in pursuit of success (Merton) |
| **Innovation** | Accepting goals but using illegitimate means (Merton) |
| **Label** | A name or identity applied to someone by others (Becker) |
| **Master status** | An identity that overrides all others, e.g. "criminal" |
| **Self-fulfilling prophecy** | Acting in line with a label until it comes true |
| **Deviant career** | Progression into deviance after labelling (Becker) |
| **Status frustration** | Frustration at being unable to gain status legitimately (Cohen) |
| **Delinquent subculture** | Group with values rewarding delinquency (Cohen) |
| **Class deal / gender deal** | Rewards offered to women for conformity (Carlen) |
| **Control theory** | People conform when the rewards of conformity outweigh crime |

## Key sociologists at a glance

| Sociologist | Perspective | Main idea | Criticism |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Merton** ("Social Structure and Anomie", 1938) | Functionalist | Strain between success goals and means → five adaptations | Ignores power; assumes consensus; overstates working-class crime |
| **Becker** (*Outsiders*, 1963) | Interactionist | Deviance is labelled; label → master status → deviant career | Does not explain the first act; may ignore victims |
| **Albert Cohen** (*Delinquent Boys*, 1955) | Functionalist | Educational failure → status frustration → delinquent subculture | Boys may reject mainstream goals rather than share them |
| **Carlen** (*Women, Crime and Poverty*, 1988) | Feminist | When class and gender deals fail, crime becomes viable | Small sample of 39 women |

**Merton's five adaptations:** conformity, innovation, ritualism, retreatism, rebellion.

## Factors affecting crime (3.5.3)

- **Class:** blocked opportunity, subcultures, labelling; Marxists highlight crimes of the powerful.
- **Gender:** men offend more; women more controlled (Heidensohn); Carlen on women offenders.
- **Ethnicity:** over-representation in some statistics debated — discrimination or deprivation?
- **Age:** offending peaks in teenage years; young people more visible to police.

## Public debates

| Debate | Key question |
|---|---|
| Violent crime | Is it rising, and what causes it? |
| Sentencing | Are sentences fair, consistent and effective? |
| Young offenders | Should they be punished or rehabilitated? |
| Prison system | Does prison reduce reoffending? |
| Media coverage | Does reporting exaggerate crime and fear? |

## Perspectives summary

- **Functionalist:** crime from strain and subculture; some deviance reinforces norms.
- **Marxist:** laws reflect ruling-class interests; working class criminalised.
- **Feminist:** patriarchal control explains low female crime; women as victims.
- **Interactionist:** labelling by powerful agents creates deviance.

## Worked example: a 4-mark "identify and explain" answer

*Identify and explain one factor that may lead working-class boys to commit crime.* (practice)

- **Identify (1):** status frustration.
- **Explain (3):** Albert Cohen argued working-class boys share mainstream goals but often fail at
  school (1); unable to gain status legitimately, they feel frustrated (1); they form delinquent
  subcultures in which stealing and vandalism earn status from peers (1).

## Worked example: a 12-mark plan

*Discuss how far sociologists would agree that working-class people commit more crime than other
groups.* (practice)

1. **Agree:** official statistics show working-class people over-represented; Merton — blocked
   means lead to innovation; Albert Cohen — status frustration and delinquent subcultures; Carlen —
   working-class women turn to crime when the class deal fails.
2. **Disagree:** Becker — police label working-class youths more often, so statistics reflect
   reactions; Marxists — white-collar and corporate crimes by the powerful are hidden and rarely
   prosecuted; the dark figure means recorded crime is a poor guide.
3. **Conclusion:** working-class people are more likely to be *recorded* as offenders, but whether
   they *commit* more crime is unclear.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Confusing **Albert Cohen** (delinquent subcultures) with Stanley Cohen (moral panics, not named in
  8192).
- Listing Merton's adaptations without explaining goals and means.
- Saying Becker thinks deviants are "innocent" — he argues deviance is created by reactions.
- Forgetting Carlen's method (unstructured interviews) and sample size, often useful for evaluation.
- Not stating the **perspective** of each key sociologist.

## Quick self-test

1. Define deviance and give an example that is not a crime.
2. Which adaptation describes a drug addict who has dropped out?
3. What is a master status?
4. What are Carlen's two deals?
5. Name two public debates about crime in the specification.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know Stanley Cohen?** He is not named in 8192, but moral panic ideas can be credited
where relevant; the named sociologists are the priority.

**How is crime "socially constructed"?** Societies decide which acts are crimes, and these decisions
differ between places and change over time.

**Which perspective is Carlen?** Feminist, using control theory.
