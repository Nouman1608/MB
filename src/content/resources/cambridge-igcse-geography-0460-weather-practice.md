These questions cover topic 2.4 Weather in Theme 2 (The natural environment) of the Cambridge IGCSE Geography (0460) syllabus for examination in 2025 and 2026. All weather stations and readings are fictional and the data are invented. The humidity table in Question 8 is simplified for this question.

Revise first with the [Weather study guide](/resources/igcse-geography-weather/) and the [Weather revision notes](/resources/igcse-geography-0460-weather-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Name the instrument used to measure each of the following: wind direction; atmospheric pressure; hours of sunshine. **[3]**

**2.** State two features of the siting of a Stevenson screen. **[2]**

**3.** State what is measured in oktas. **[1]**

**4.** Name two types of cloud. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why a rain gauge is placed with its top a short distance above the ground and away from buildings and trees. **[2]**

**6.** State how a maximum-minimum thermometer is reset after a reading. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** The table shows readings from a fictional school weather station for one week.

| Day | Maximum (°C) | Minimum (°C) | Rainfall (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | 24 | 15 | 0 |
| Tue | 26 | 16 | 4.5 |
| Wed | 25 | 17 | 12.0 |
| Thu | 27 | 18 | 0 |
| Fri | 29 | 17 | 2.5 |
| Sat | 28 | 18 | 7.0 |
| Sun | 23 | 14 | 0 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean maximum temperature for the week. Show your working. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the day with the largest daily temperature range. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the total rainfall for the week. **[1]**

**8.** A wet-and-dry bulb thermometer (hygrometer) gives a dry-bulb reading of 20°C and a wet-bulb reading of 17°C. A simplified humidity table for a dry-bulb reading of 20°C is shown.

| Difference between dry and wet bulb (°C) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Relative humidity (%) | 91 | 81 | 72 | 64 |

**(a)** Calculate the difference between the two readings. **[1]**
**(b)** Use the table to find the relative humidity. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the wet-bulb thermometer gives a lower reading than the dry-bulb thermometer. **[2]**

**9.** Describe and explain the characteristics of a Stevenson screen. **[5]**

**10.** Describe how wind speed and wind direction are measured at a weather station. **[4]**

**11.** The table shows climate data for a fictional weather station.

| Month | J | F | M | A | M | J | J | A | S | O | N | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature (°C) | 26 | 26 | 27 | 27 | 27 | 26 | 26 | 26 | 27 | 27 | 27 | 26 |
| Rainfall (mm) | 250 | 230 | 280 | 290 | 260 | 180 | 160 | 170 | 200 | 260 | 280 | 270 |

**(a)** Calculate the annual temperature range and the total annual rainfall. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the climate shown by the data. **[3]**

**12.** A student wants to find out whether air temperatures are higher in the school car park than on the school playing field. Plan a method the student could use. **[6]**

**13.** Suggest advantages and disadvantages of using a digital weather station rather than traditional instruments at a school. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Wind direction: wind vane [1]. Atmospheric pressure: barometer [1]. Hours of sunshine: sunshine recorder [1]. **[3]**

**2.** On an open, grassy site, away from buildings and trees [1]. With the door facing away from the sun (north in the northern hemisphere), so the thermometers are not in direct sunlight when it is opened [1]. Also credit a standard height above the ground, about 1.2 m. **[2]**

**3.** The amount of the sky covered by cloud, in eighths [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Cumulus [1]. Cirrus [1]. Also credit stratus or cumulonimbus. **[2]**

**5.** A short distance above the ground prevents rain splashing in from the surface, and surface water running in [1]. Away from buildings and trees so that rain is not blocked, and drips from leaves or roofs do not add to the reading [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The metal markers (indexes) are moved back to touch the liquid using a magnet, or by pressing a reset button [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** (24 + 26 + 25 + 27 + 29 + 28 + 23) ÷ 7 = 182 ÷ 7 [1] = 26.0°C [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Friday (range 12°C) [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** 26.0 mm [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Always give units. A mean temperature of "26" or rainfall of "26" without °C or mm may not be credited.

**8. (a)** 20 − 17 = 3°C [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 72% [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Water evaporates from the wet muslin around the wet bulb, and evaporation takes heat from the thermometer, cooling it [1]. The drier the air, the faster the evaporation, so the bigger the difference between the readings and the lower the humidity [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Painted white, to reflect the sun's rays so the temperature recorded is the shade air temperature [1]. Louvred (slatted) sides, so air can circulate freely while direct sunlight is kept out [1]. Made of wood, a poor conductor of heat, so the box itself does not heat the air inside [1]. Raised on legs above the ground, so thermometers are not affected by heat from the ground [1]. Sited on grass in an open area away from buildings, so readings are not affected by heat from concrete or walls [1]. **[5]**

**10.** Wind speed is measured with an anemometer: cups catch the wind and spin [1], and the number of rotations is converted into a speed, such as km/h, shown on a dial or digital display [1]. Wind direction is measured with a wind vane, which turns so that its pointer faces the direction the wind is coming from [1]. Both should be mounted high, away from buildings that would block or channel the wind [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** Annual temperature range = 27 − 26 = 1°C [1]. Total annual rainfall = 2,830 mm [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Temperatures are high all year, between 26 and 27°C, with almost no seasonal change [1]. Rainfall is very high, with every month receiving at least 160 mm [1]. There is a slightly drier period from June to September (160 to 200 mm), with peaks of 290 mm in April and 280 mm in March and November [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A description should quote figures with months. Naming the climate (equatorial) is not needed when a question only says "describe".

**12.** Choose several sampling points in the car park and the same number on the playing field [1]. Use the same type of thermometer or digital thermometer at each point [1], held at the same height above the ground and shaded from direct sunlight [1]. Take readings at the same time of day, or as close together in time as possible, ideally on several days [1]. Record the results in a table and calculate a mean for each area [1]. Compare the means, for example in a bar chart, to decide whether the car park is warmer [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Plan" questions reward fair-test details: same equipment, same height, same time.

**13.** Advantages: readings are recorded automatically and continuously, including at night and at weekends [1]; data can be stored and downloaded straight to a computer, reducing errors when copying readings [1]. Disadvantages: it is more expensive to buy and may need a power supply and maintenance [1]; sensors can fail or drift out of calibration without anyone noticing, so data may be lost or inaccurate [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing the anemometer and the wind vane.
- Saying a wind vane points to where the wind is blowing to, rather than where it comes from.
- Leaving units off calculations.
- Giving Stevenson screen features with no explanation of why each matters.
- "Plan" answers with no fair-test controls.
