These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 8.03, Angles. Questions 1 to 13 are for both tiers. Question 14 is a more formal proof and is **Higher tier only**. No diagrams are given: draw a sketch from each description and mark the facts on it. Where reasons are asked for, write each fact in full. All questions can be answered without a calculator.

Learn the facts first in the [angles study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-angles/) or the [angles revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-angles-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Three angles lie together on a straight line. They are 35°, 2a° and (a + 25)°. Find a, giving a reason. **[2]**

**2.** Five angles meet at a point. They are 72°, 3b°, 3b°, 2b° and 80°. Find b. **[2]**

**3.** Two straight lines cross. Two vertically opposite angles are (4c − 10)° and (2c + 30)°. Find c and the sizes of all four angles. **[3]**

**4.** For a regular nonagon (9 sides), find

**(a)** the size of each exterior angle **[1]**

**(b)** the size of each interior angle. **[1]**

**5.** **(a)** Find the sum of the interior angles of an octagon. **[1]**

**(b)** Seven of the interior angles of an octagon are each 140°. Find the eighth angle. **[2]**

**6.** Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 162°. Find the number of sides. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Lines AB and CD are parallel, with A to the left of B and C to the left of D, and AB above CD. A straight line crosses AB at P and CD at Q, running down from left to right. Angle BPQ = 64°. Give a reason for each answer.

**(a)** Find angle PQC. **[2]**

**(b)** Find angle PQD. **[2]**

**(c)** Find angle APQ. **[1]**

**8.** A transversal crosses two parallel lines. Two corresponding angles are (5x − 12)° and (3x + 20)°. An angle y° is co-interior with the angle (5x − 12)°.

**(a)** Find x. **[2]**

**(b)** Find y. **[2]**

**9.** Two parallel lines run from left to right: AB on top, with A to the right of B, and CD below, with C to the right of D. A point E lies between the lines, to the right of B and D. Angle ABE = 41° and angle CDE = 33°. Find angle BED, giving reasons. **[3]**

**10.** Prove that the interior angles of any quadrilateral add up to 360°. **[3]**

**11.** In triangle ABC, AB = AC. The side BC is extended beyond C to the point D. Angle ACD = 115°. Find angle BAC, giving a reason for each step. **[3]**

**12.** Each interior angle of a regular polygon is four times its exterior angle. Find the number of sides of the polygon. **[3]**

**13.** ABCDEF is a regular hexagon. ABGH is a square drawn on the outside of the hexagon, sharing the side AB.

**(a)** Find angle FAH. **[2]**

**(b)** Find angle AFH. **[2]**

**14.** **(Higher tier only)** Use the result that the interior angles of an n-sided polygon add up to (n − 2) × 180° to prove that the exterior angles of any polygon add up to 360°. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** 35 + 2a + a + 25 = 180 because angles on a straight line add up to 180° [1]; 3a = 120, **a = 40** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Collect the like terms carefully: 2a + a = 3a and 35 + 25 = 60.

**2.** 72 + 80 + 8b = 360 (angles at a point) [1]; 8b = 208, **b = 26** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Check: 72 + 78 + 78 + 52 + 80 = 360.

**3.** 4c − 10 = 2c + 30 (vertically opposite angles are equal), so **c = 20** [1]; those two angles are **70°** each [1]; the other two are 180 − 70 = **110°** each (angles on a straight line) [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Four angles at a crossing come in two equal pairs, and neighbouring angles add to 180°.

**4. (a)** 360 ÷ 9 = **40°** [1]

**(b)** 180 − 40 = **140°** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Finding the exterior angle first is quicker than (9 − 2) × 180 ÷ 9, and both give 140°.

**5. (a)** 6 × 180 = **1080°** [1]

**(b)** 7 × 140 = 980 [1]; 1080 − 980 = **100°** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* An octagon splits into 6 triangles from one vertex, which is where (n − 2) comes from.

**6.** Exterior angle = 180 − 162 = 18° [1]; 360 ÷ 18 = **20 sides** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Dividing 360 by 162 is a common error. Always divide by the exterior angle.

**7. (a)** **64°** [1]; alternate angles are equal [1]

**(b)** 180 − 64 = **116°** [1]; co-interior angles add up to 180° (or angles on a straight line with angle PQC) [1]

**(c)** **116°**, because angles on a straight line add up to 180° [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* "Z angles" is not accepted as a reason. Name the fact: alternate angles are equal.

**8. (a)** 5x − 12 = 3x + 20 (corresponding angles are equal) [1]; **x = 16** [1]

**(b)** 5(16) − 12 = 68° [1]; y = 180 − 68 = **112** (co-interior angles add up to 180°) [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Substitute x back into both expressions as a check: 3(16) + 20 = 68 as well.

**9.** Draw a line through E parallel to AB and CD [1]; the two parts of angle BED are 41° and 33°, because alternate angles are equal [1]; angle BED = **74°** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The construction line is the key step. State that it is parallel to the given lines, or the alternate-angle reasons do not hold.

**10.** Draw one diagonal, splitting the quadrilateral into two triangles [1]; the angles of each triangle add up to 180°, so together they total 360° [1]; the angles of the two triangles make up exactly the four interior angles of the quadrilateral, so these add up to 360° [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A proof must work for every quadrilateral, so do not use particular angle sizes. The last sentence links the triangles back to the quadrilateral.

**11.** Angle ACB = 180 − 115 = 65°, angles on a straight line add up to 180° [1]; angle ABC = 65°, base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal [1]; angle BAC = 180 − 65 − 65 = **50°**, angles in a triangle add up to 180° [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Alternatively, the exterior angle 115° equals angle BAC + angle ABC, so angle BAC = 115 − 65 = 50°.

**12.** Exterior angle e and interior 4e: e + 4e = 180 [1]; e = 36° [1]; 360 ÷ 36 = **10 sides** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Interior and exterior angles at a vertex lie on a straight line, which gives the equation.

**13. (a)** Interior angle of a regular hexagon = 120°, and the square's angle is 90° [1]; angle FAH = 360 − 120 − 90 = **150°** [1]

**(b)** AF = AB = AH, so triangle AFH is isosceles [1]; angle AFH = (180 − 150) ÷ 2 = **15°** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* The three angles round A, from the hexagon, the square and the gap between them, make 360°. Mark the equal sides on your sketch to see the isosceles triangle.

**14.** At each vertex interior + exterior = 180°, so over n vertices the total is 180n° [1]; the interior angles total (n − 2) × 180° = 180n − 360 [1]; so the exterior angles total 180n − (180n − 360) = **360°**, whatever the value of n [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A formal proof works with n throughout. Checking a hexagon or a pentagon only illustrates the result; it does not prove it.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reasons written as "Z angles" or "F angles", or missing altogether.
- Assuming lines are parallel without a mark or a statement.
- Treating co-interior angles as equal.
- Using n × 180° for the interior sum, or dividing 360 by the interior angle.
- Finding x but not the angle asked for.
- Proving a general result with one example.
