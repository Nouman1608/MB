**These notes condense key question 2.5 of Cambridge O Level History 2147: how effectively the United States contained
the spread of communism.** Learn each case study as aims, actions and outcome. The question is the Paper 2 prescribed
topic for November 2026. The full explanation is in the
[containment study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-containment-communism/); exam practice is in
the [containment practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-containment-communism-practice/).

## Korea, 1950–53

**Aims:** defend South Korea; prove containment would be enforced after the communist victory in China (1949).

**Actions and course:**
- North Korea invaded on 25 June 1950.
- UN Security Council backed action while the USSR boycotted it over China's seat.
- UN force led by the USA under MacArthur; 16 nations sent combat troops.
- Pusan perimeter (summer 1950); Inchon landing (September 1950); Seoul retaken.
- Advance across the 38th parallel to the Yalu River (October–November 1950).
- Chinese intervention drove UN forces south; Seoul lost again (January 1951).
- MacArthur sacked (April 1951) for demanding war on China.
- Stalemate; armistice at Panmunjom (July 1953); border near the 38th parallel.

**Outcome:** South Korea saved (success for containment); rollback of the North failed; heavy casualties.

## Cuba, 1959–62

**Aims:** remove or isolate a communist state close to the USA; stop Soviet missiles being based there.

**Actions and course:**
- Castro took power (January 1959); nationalised US-owned property.
- US cut sugar purchases (1960) and imposed a trade embargo; Castro turned to the USSR.
- Bay of Pigs invasion by CIA-trained exiles failed (April 1961).
- U-2 photographs of missile sites (14 October 1962).
- Quarantine announced (22 October); Soviet ships turned back (24 October).
- Khrushchev's two letters; U-2 shot down (27 October).
- Deal (28 October): missiles removed; US pledge not to invade; secret removal of US missiles from Turkey.
- Hotline (1963); Partial Test Ban Treaty (August 1963).

**Outcome:** missiles removed without war (success); Cuba stayed communist (failure); embarrassment in 1961.

## Vietnam, 1955–75

**Aims:** stop the domino effect in Southeast Asia; keep South Vietnam non-communist.

**Reasons for involvement:** Geneva Accords (1954) and the 17th parallel; elections of 1956 not held; support for
Diem; domino theory; advisers under Kennedy; Gulf of Tonkin Resolution (August 1964).

**Tactics and strategy:** Rolling Thunder bombing (1965–68); ground troops from March 1965; search and destroy;
napalm and Agent Orange; strategic hamlets.

**Communist tactics:** guerrilla warfare; tunnels; Ho Chi Minh Trail; support from China and the USSR.

**Reasons for withdrawal:** Tet Offensive (1968) turned opinion; My Lai; anti-war protests and Kent State (1970); cost
and casualties; Vietnamisation under Nixon; Paris Peace Accords (January 1973).

**Outcome:** Saigon fell (April 1975); Vietnam united under communism; Laos and Cambodia communist (failure).

## Comparison grid

| Case | Aim achieved? | Main reason |
|---|---|---|
| Korea | Partly | Military force held the South, but Chinese intervention prevented rollback |
| Cuba | Partly | Firm diplomacy removed the missiles, but Castro survived |
| Vietnam | No | Guerrilla war, a weak Southern government and loss of support at home |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Containment | Preventing the spread of communism to new countries |
| Rollback | Pushing communism back from countries it already controlled |
| NSC-68 | 1950 US policy paper calling for a major military build-up |
| 38th parallel | Line dividing North and South Korea |
| Domino theory | Belief that if one country fell to communism, its neighbours would follow |
| ExComm | Kennedy's group of advisers in the Missile Crisis |
| Quarantine | Kennedy's term for the naval blockade of Cuba |
| Brinkmanship | Pushing a crisis to the edge of war to force the other side to back down |
| Viet Cong | Communist guerrillas in South Vietnam |
| Search and destroy | US missions to find and kill Viet Cong units |
| Vietnamisation | Nixon's policy of handing the fighting to South Vietnam's army |
| Credibility gap | The difference between official claims and what Americans saw of the war |

## Exam traps

- **Saying the UN force was purely American.** It was mostly American, but many nations took part.
- **Mixing up Bay of Pigs (1961) and the Missile Crisis (1962).**
- **Calling Tet a communist military victory.** It was a military defeat but a political success for the North.
- **Forgetting that "effectively" needs a judgement** in each case and overall.
- **Writing about Vietnam only from 1965.** Involvement runs from 1955.

## Self-test

1. Why could the USSR not veto UN action in Korea in June 1950?
2. What was the significance of the Inchon landing?
3. Why did China enter the Korean War?
4. Why did Truman dismiss MacArthur?
5. Why did Castro turn to the USSR?
6. Why did the Bay of Pigs invasion fail?
7. Why did Kennedy choose a quarantine rather than air strikes?
8. What did each side gain from the settlement of 28 October 1962?
9. What did the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution allow?
10. Give **two** reasons why US tactics in Vietnam failed.
11. Why was the Tet Offensive a turning point?
12. What was Vietnamisation?

**Answers:** 1. It was boycotting the Security Council over the UN's recognition of Nationalist China. 2. It cut North
Korean supply lines and turned the war, allowing Seoul to be retaken. 3. UN forces approached the Yalu River on its
border, and China feared invasion. 4. He publicly demanded an attack on China, risking a wider war against Truman's
policy. 5. US hostility after he nationalised American property, including the embargo, left him needing a trading
partner and protector. 6. The expected popular rising did not happen, and the exiles lacked air support. 7. It gave
Khrushchev time to back down and avoided killing Soviet personnel, which could have led to nuclear war. 8. USA: missiles
removed; USSR: pledge not to invade Cuba and secret removal of US missiles from Turkey. 9. It allowed President Johnson
to use military force in Vietnam without a declaration of war. 10. Any two: guerrillas were hard to find; search and
destroy and chemical weapons killed civilians and lost support; the Ho Chi Minh Trail kept supplies flowing; the South
Vietnamese government was unpopular. 11. It convinced many Americans the war could not be won, and Johnson decided not
to seek re-election. 12. Nixon's policy of building up South Vietnamese forces so US troops could withdraw.
