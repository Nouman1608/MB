These questions cover section **3.5.3 Progressive waves** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification: particle oscillation, the wave quantities, c = fλ, f = 1/T and phase difference "as angles (radians or degrees) or as fractions of a cycle". Marks are shown for each part. All data are invented for practice.

Revise first with the [progressive waves study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-progressive-waves/) and the [progressive waves revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-progressive-waves-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by the phase difference between two points on a progressive wave. **[2]**

**2.** A sound wave has frequency 250 Hz and wavelength 1.36 m. What is its speed?

A: 0.0054 m s⁻¹

B: 184 m s⁻¹

C: 340 m s⁻¹

D: 251 m s⁻¹ **[1]**

**3.** Two points on a progressive wave are 0.40 m apart. The wavelength is 1.20 m. Calculate the phase difference between them in degrees and in radians. **[2]**

**4.** A particle on a water wave takes 0.040 s to move from its highest position to its lowest position. Calculate the frequency of the wave. **[2]**

**5.** Two points on a wave oscillate in antiphase. Which could be their separation?

A: λ/4

B: λ/2

C: λ

D: 2λ **[1]**

**6.** Explain why the frequency of a wave does not change when the wave passes from one medium into another. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A transverse wave travels along a rope in the +x direction. The wavelength is 2.4 m and the period is 0.60 s. At one instant, point A (x = 0.60 m) is at a crest, point B (x = 1.20 m) has zero displacement and point C (x = 1.80 m) is at a trough.

**(a)** Calculate the wave speed. **[1]**

**(b)** State and explain the direction in which B is moving at this instant. **[2]**

**(c)** State the phase difference between A and C in radians. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the time that passes before C next reaches its maximum positive displacement. **[1]**

**(e)** State which of A and B leads, and by what fraction of a cycle. **[2]**

**8.** Two microphones are connected to the two inputs of an oscilloscope and placed in front of a loudspeaker. The time-base is set to 0.20 ms per division. Each trace shows one complete cycle in 4.0 divisions, and the second trace is shifted 1.5 divisions to the right of the first.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency of the sound. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the phase difference between the two signals in radians. **[2]**

**(c)** The speed of sound is 330 m s⁻¹. Calculate the smallest possible distance between the microphones along the direction of travel. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why the separation cannot be found for certain from the traces alone. **[1]**

**9.** An ocean wave of period 15 minutes travels at 200 m s⁻¹ across deep water. Near the coast its speed falls to 20 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength in deep water. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the wavelength near the coast and explain why it changes. **[2]**

**10.** A vibration generator drives one end of a long stretched string at 50 Hz. Waves travel away along the string. The distance from the first to the fifth of five consecutive crests is 1.6 m.

**(a)** Calculate the wave speed on the string. **[3]**

**(b)** The frequency is doubled. The wave speed on the string does not change. State the new wavelength. **[1]**

**(c)** At the new frequency, calculate the phase difference between two points on the string 0.15 m apart, in degrees. **[2]**

**11.** A particle in a medium oscillates with amplitude 4.0 mm and period 20 ms as a wave passes. The wave speed is 15 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength. **[2]**

**(b)** A second particle is 0.050 m further from the source. Calculate the phase difference between the two particles in radians, and state which one lags. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the time taken for the wave to travel from the first particle to the second, and show that it is consistent with your answer to (b). **[2]**

**12.** Explain, in terms of the particles of the medium, how a progressive wave transfers energy without transferring matter. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** How far one point is ahead of or behind the other in its cycle of oscillation [1], expressed as a fraction of a cycle or as an angle in degrees or radians [1].

**2.** **C** [1]. c = fλ = 250 × 1.36 = 340 m s⁻¹.

**3.** 0.40/1.20 = 1/3 of a cycle [1]; phase difference = **120°** = **2π/3 rad (2.09 rad)** [1].

**4.** Highest to lowest is half a cycle, so T = 2 × 0.040 = 0.080 s [1]; f = 1/T = **12.5 Hz** [1].

**5.** **B** [1]. Antiphase needs an odd number of half-wavelengths.

**6.** The frequency is set by the source [1]; the number of waves arriving at the boundary each second must equal the number leaving it, so only the speed and wavelength change [1].

**7.**

**(a)** c = λ/T = 2.4/0.60 = **4.0 m s⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** B is moving **upwards** (in the positive displacement direction) [1]; the particle just behind B, on the source side, is displaced upwards, and B is about to copy that displacement [1].

**(c)** A and C are 1.20 m = λ/2 apart, so the phase difference is **π rad** [1]

**(d)** From a trough to a crest is half a period: 0.60/2 = **0.30 s** [1]

**(e)** **A leads** [1], because B is further along the direction of travel; the separation is 0.60 m = λ/4, so the lead is **a quarter of a cycle** [1]

**8.**

**(a)** T = 4.0 × 0.20 ms = 0.80 ms [1]; f = 1/(0.80 × 10⁻³) = **1250 Hz** [1]

**(b)** Fraction of a cycle = 1.5/4.0 = 0.375 [1]; phase difference = 0.375 × 2π = **2.36 rad** (3π/4) [1]

**(c)** λ = c/f = 330/1250 = 0.264 m [1]; smallest separation = 0.375 × 0.264 = **0.099 m** [1]

**(d)** A shift of 0.375 cycle looks the same as 1.375 or 2.375 cycles, so the separation could be 0.099 m plus any whole number of wavelengths [1].

**9.**

**(a)** T = 15 × 60 = 900 s [1]; λ = cT = 200 × 900 = **1.8 × 10⁵ m (180 km)** [1]

**(b)** λ = 20 × 900 = **1.8 × 10⁴ m (18 km)** [1]; the period is fixed by the source, so when the speed falls by a factor of 10 the wavelength falls by the same factor [1]

**10.**

**(a)** Five crests span four wavelengths [1], so λ = 1.6/4 = 0.40 m [1]; c = fλ = 50 × 0.40 = **20 m s⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** λ = 20/100 = **0.20 m** [1]

**(c)** 0.15/0.20 = 0.75 cycle [1]; phase difference = **270°** [1] (equivalently, 90° the other way)

**11.**

**(a)** λ = cT = 15 × 0.020 [1] = **0.30 m** [1]

**(b)** 0.050/0.30 = 1/6 cycle, so Δφ = **π/3 rad (1.05 rad)** [1]; the second particle, further from the source, **lags** [1]

**(c)** t = 0.050/15 = **3.3 × 10⁻³ s** [1]; this is 3.3 ms/20 ms = 1/6 of a period, the same fraction of a cycle as in (b) [1]

**12.** Each particle oscillates about a fixed equilibrium position and ends where it started [1]; it passes energy to its neighbour through the forces between them [1], so the disturbance moves on through successive particles while no particle travels with the wave [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the crest-to-trough distance as a wavelength (Question 7).
- Counting five crests as five wavelengths instead of four (Question 10).
- Leaving a phase difference without a unit, or not saying which point lags (Questions 3, 8 and 11).
- Forgetting that a trace shift cannot tell whole cycles apart (Question 8).
- Converting minutes or milliseconds incorrectly before using c = fλ (Questions 9 and 11).
- Describing particles as moving along with the wave (Question 12).
