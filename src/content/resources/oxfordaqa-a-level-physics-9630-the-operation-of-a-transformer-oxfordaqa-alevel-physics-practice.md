These questions cover section 3.10.6, The operation of a transformer, of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), including Required practical 7. It is International A-level content. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [transformer study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-the-operation-of-a-transformer-oxfordaqa-alevel-physics/) or the [transformer revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-the-operation-of-a-transformer-oxfordaqa-alevel-physics-revision-notes/) first.

All voltages and currents are rms values. All data are invented for practice.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Explain why a transformer gives no output when its primary is connected to a steady dc supply. **[2]**

**2.** An ideal step-up transformer has a turns ratio Np : Ns of 1 : 20. The primary is connected to 12 V and the secondary delivers 0.50 A. What is the primary current?

A: 0.025 A

B: 0.50 A

C: 10 A

D: 20 A

**[1]**

**3.** A transformer has 2000 turns on its primary, which is connected to 230 V. Calculate the number of secondary turns needed for an output of 11.5 V. **[2]**

**4.** A transformer takes 0.30 A from a 230 V supply and delivers 2.6 A at 24 V.

**(a)** Calculate its efficiency. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the power wasted. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why transformer cores are laminated. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why transformer cores are made of soft iron. **[2]**

### Section B

**7.** Explain how a transformer produces an alternating output voltage from an alternating input voltage. **[4]**

**8.** An ideal transformer at a substation steps 11 kV down to 230 V. The secondary current is 150 A.

**(a)** Calculate the turns ratio Np : Ns. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the power delivered. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the primary current. **[2]**

**9.** A power of 120 kW is transmitted through cables with a total resistance of 0.80 Ω.

**(a)** Calculate the current, the power lost and the p.d. lost in the cables when the power is sent at 6.0 kV. **[3]**

**(b)** Repeat the calculation for 600 V. **[1]**

**(c)** Use your answers to explain why power is transmitted at high voltage. **[2]**

**10.** A transformer is 88% efficient. It delivers 150 W at 12 V from a 230 V supply.

**(a)** Calculate the input power. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the primary current. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the secondary current. **[1]**

**11.** In Required practical 7, a student keeps the primary p.d. at 12.0 V and changes the load on the secondary.

| Ip / A | Vs / V | Is / A |
|---|---|---|
| 0.15 | 5.8 | 0.29 |
| 0.52 | 5.6 | 1.02 |
| 0.95 | 5.3 | 1.80 |

**(a)** Calculate the efficiency for each row. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe and explain the trend. **[2]**

**(c)** State one safety precaution and one control variable for this investigation. **[2]**

**12.** Electrical power passes through a step-up transformer of efficiency 96%, a transmission line that wastes 3% of the power entering it, and a step-down transformer of efficiency 98%. Calculate the percentage of the original power that reaches the consumers. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A steady current produces a steady flux in the core [1]; the flux linkage of the secondary does not change, so no emf is induced (Faraday's law) [1]. **[2]**

**2.** **C** [1]. Vs = 240 V, so power = 240 × 0.50 = 120 W and Ip = 120/12 = 10 A. **[1]**

**3.** Ns = Np × Vs/Vp = 2000 × 11.5/230 [1] = **100 turns** [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** Input = 230 × 0.30 = 69.0 W; output = 24 × 2.6 = 62.4 W [1]; efficiency = 62.4/69.0 = **0.90 (90%)** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** 69.0 − 62.4 = **6.6 W** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** The changing flux induces eddy currents in the core, which heat it and waste energy [1]; thin sheets insulated from each other break up the current paths, so the eddy currents and the heating are reduced [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The core is magnetised and demagnetised every cycle, which wastes energy (hysteresis) [1]; soft iron is easily magnetised and demagnetised, so this loss is small [1]. **[2]**

**7.** The alternating current in the primary [1] produces an alternating magnetic flux in the iron core [1]. The core carries this flux through the secondary, so the flux linkage of the secondary changes continuously [1]. By Faraday's law an alternating emf is induced in the secondary, at the same frequency [1]. **[4]**

**8. (a)** Np : Ns = 11 000 : 230 = **48 : 1** (47.8 : 1) [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** P = 230 × 150 = **3.45 × 10⁴ W** (34.5 kW) [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** IpVp = IsVs [1]; Ip = 34 500 / 11 000 = **3.1 A** [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** I = 120 000/6000 = **20 A** [1]; loss = 20² × 0.80 = **320 W** [1]; p.d. lost = 20 × 0.80 = **16 V** [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** I = 200 A; loss = **32 kW**; p.d. lost = **160 V** [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** At the higher voltage the current is ten times smaller [1], so the power lost (∝ I²) is a hundred times smaller: 0.27% of the power instead of 27%, and the voltage drop is also much smaller [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** 150/0.88 = **170 W** [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** 170.5/230 = **0.74 A** [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** 150/12 = **12.5 A** [1]. **[1]**

**11. (a)** Row 1: 5.8 × 0.29 / (12.0 × 0.15) = 1.68/1.80 = **93%** [1]. Row 2: 5.71/6.24 = **92%** [1]. Row 3: 9.54/11.4 = **84%** [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Efficiency falls as the load current rises [1]; the heating of the coils is proportional to I², so a larger fraction of the input power is wasted at high current [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Safety: use a low-voltage ac supply and never connect the transformer to the mains (or switch off between readings to avoid overheated coils) [1]. Control: keep the primary voltage, the supply frequency or the transformer (turns and core) the same [1]. **[2]**

**12.** Fraction delivered = 0.96 × 0.97 × 0.98 [1] = 0.913, so **91%** [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Inverting the turns ratio.
- Calculating efficiency from voltages without the currents.
- Writing "heat loss" without naming the cause.
- Saying laminations stop eddy currents completely.
- Using the transmission voltage instead of the line current to find the power lost.
- Adding percentage losses through a chain instead of multiplying efficiencies.
