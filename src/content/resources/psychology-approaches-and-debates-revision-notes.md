## The approaches in one table

| Approach | Core assumption | Methods | Key names |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biological | Behaviour has physical causes — genes, neurochemistry, brain structure | Scans, twin and family studies | Selye, Sperry |
| Behaviourist | All behaviour is learned from the environment | Lab experiments, animals | Pavlov, Skinner, Watson |
| Social learning | Learning through observation and imitation of models | Lab experiments | Bandura |
| Cognitive | Internal mental processes can be studied scientifically | Lab experiments, models | Atkinson & Shiffrin, Baddeley |
| Psychodynamic | Unconscious conflicts and childhood shape behaviour | Case studies | Freud |
| Humanistic | People have free will and strive towards self-actualisation | Qualitative, subjective | Rogers, Maslow |

## Learning theory essentials

- **Classical conditioning** — learning by association. NS + UCS → UCS response; NS becomes CS producing a CR. (Pavlov's dogs.)
- **Operant conditioning** — learning by consequence. Positive reinforcement, negative reinforcement (removing something unpleasant) and punishment. (Skinner's box.)
- **Social learning** — attention, retention, reproduction, motivation; **vicarious reinforcement** and identification with a model. (Bandura's Bobo doll.)

Negative reinforcement **increases** behaviour — it is not punishment. This is the most-confused pair in the topic.

## The debates

| Debate | The two poles |
|---|---|
| Nature vs nurture | Genetic inheritance vs environmental experience → **interactionist** position now dominant; diathesis-stress |
| Free will vs determinism | Choice vs causes; hard, soft, biological, environmental, psychic determinism |
| Reductionism vs holism | Explaining by breaking down to parts vs the whole system; **levels of explanation** |
| Idiographic vs nomothetic | The unique individual vs general laws |

## Bias and ethics

- **Alpha bias** — exaggerates differences between groups. **Beta bias** — minimises or ignores them. **Androcentrism** — male behaviour as the norm.
- **Ethnocentrism** — judging other cultures by one's own standards; **cultural relativism** is the corrective.
- Ethics: informed consent, deception, protection from harm, right to withdraw, confidentiality; weighed by **cost–benefit analysis**.

## Exam traps

- Negative reinforcement ≠ punishment.
- Social learning theory is **not** the same as behaviourism — it includes mediational cognitive processes.
- "Determinism" needs its type specified: biological, environmental or psychic.
- Reductionism is not automatically bad — it enables scientific testing.
- Alpha and beta bias are opposites; check which way round before writing.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish negative reinforcement from punishment.
2. Name the four mediational processes in social learning theory.
3. What is the diathesis-stress model?
4. Give one strength and one weakness of the psychodynamic approach.
5. Define alpha bias and give an example of its effect.

**Answers:** 1. Negative reinforcement removes an unpleasant stimulus and *increases* behaviour; punishment adds an unpleasant consequence and *decreases* it. 2. Attention, retention, reproduction, motivation. 3. Behaviour results from a genetic vulnerability (diathesis) triggered by an environmental stressor — an interactionist position on nature/nurture. 4. Strength: first to recognise the influence of childhood and the unconscious, with real therapeutic application. Weakness: concepts are unfalsifiable and evidence rests on unrepresentative case studies. 5. Alpha bias exaggerates differences between groups — e.g. theories presenting men and women as fundamentally different can reinforce stereotypes.
