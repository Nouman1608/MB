## Core recall

### Why businesses need finance

To start up, to cover short-term cash gaps, and to grow.

### The nine named sources

| Source | Internal or external | Time scale | Key advantage | Key disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Retained profit | Internal | Any | No interest, no loss of control | New firms have none; owners give up that income |
| Selling unwanted assets | Internal | One-off | Cash without borrowing | Only once; may sell cheaply |
| Family and friends | External | Short to medium | Flexible, often little or no interest | Small sums; relationships at risk |
| New share issue | External | Long | Nothing to repay, no interest | Ownership and profits shared; companies only |
| Loan | External | Medium to long | Large sum; planned repayments | Interest; must be repaid; may need security |
| Mortgage | External | Long | Very large sums for property over many years | Property can be taken if repayments are missed |
| Overdraft | External | Short | Flexible; interest only on amount used | High interest; bank can withdraw it |
| Trade credit | External | Short | Pay suppliers later at no cost | Late payment harms supplier trust; early-payment discounts lost |
| Hire purchase | External | Medium | Use the asset now, spread the cost | Costs more than cash price; not owned until paid off |
| Government grants | External | One-off | Often not repaid | Conditions; slow and competitive |

Memory hook for internal: **R**etained and **S**old, the two **R**eal **S**avings.

### Matching rules

- Short-term need → short-term source (overdraft, trade credit).
- Long-term asset → long-term source (loan, mortgage, share issue, retained profit).
- Big sum → loan, mortgage or share issue; small sum → family and friends, overdraft.
- Keep control → borrow; avoid repayments → shares or retained profit.

### New against established

| New business | Established business |
|---|---|
| No retained profit | Retained profit available |
| No track record, so loans are harder and dearer | Accounts prove it can repay |
| Little security | Property and equipment as security |
| Relies on owners' savings, family and friends, grants, trade credit, hire purchase | Can use retained profit, loans, mortgages, share issues |

## Calculations

- **Total repaid on a loan** = number of instalments × instalment. 24 × £450 = £10,800.
- **Cost of the loan** = total repaid − amount borrowed. £10,800 − £9,500 = £1,300.
- **Hire purchase extra cost** = deposit + (instalments × payment) − cash price. £1,000 + 12 × £300 − £4,200 = £1,000 + £3,600 − £4,200 = £400.
- **Ownership after a share issue** = shares held ÷ total shares × 100. Founder with 6,000 of 8,000 shares owns 75%.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Internal finance | Money raised from within the business |
| External finance | Money raised from outside the business |
| Retained profit | Profit kept in the business rather than paid to owners |
| Share issue | Selling new shares to raise finance |
| Loan | A fixed sum borrowed and repaid with interest over an agreed period |
| Mortgage | A long-term loan secured on property |
| Overdraft | An arrangement to spend more than the balance in a bank account, up to a limit |
| Trade credit | Buying from suppliers and paying at a later date |
| Hire purchase | Paying for an asset in instalments, owning it after the final payment |
| Government grant | Money from government that usually does not have to be repaid, given if conditions are met |
| Security | An asset a lender can take if a loan is not repaid |

## Exam traps

- Retained profit is not the owner's own money: it is profit kept in the business.
- Sole traders and partnerships cannot issue shares.
- An overdraft for a long-term asset is a poor match.
- Hire purchase and loans cost more than the cash price; say so in an evaluation.
- For a new business, mention the lack of track record when discussing loans.
- Use the figures in the case, such as interest cost or how much retained profit exists.

## Self-test

1. State the two internal sources of finance named in the specification.
2. Define trade credit.
3. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of an overdraft.
4. Why might a new business struggle to obtain a bank loan?
5. What is the main difference between a loan and a share issue?
6. Which source is a long-term loan secured on property?
7. When does a business own an asset bought by hire purchase?
8. A loan of £15,000 is repaid in 48 instalments of £360. Calculate the cost of borrowing.
9. A founder owns 9,000 shares. The company issues 3,000 more to an investor. What percentage does the founder now own?
10. Suggest a suitable source for a shop that needs stock before the Christmas rush, with a reason.
11. Give one condition-related drawback of a government grant.
12. Why might an established business choose retained profit over a loan?

### Answers

1. Retained profit and selling unwanted assets.
2. Buying goods or services from a supplier now and paying for them later.
3. Advantage: flexible, with interest only on the amount used. Disadvantage: high interest, or the bank can withdraw it.
4. It has no trading record to show it can repay and little security to offer.
5. A loan must be repaid with interest; a share issue is not repaid but gives away part-ownership.
6. A mortgage.
7. After the final instalment is paid.
8. 48 × £360 = £17,280; £17,280 − £15,000 = **£2,280**.
9. 9,000 ÷ 12,000 × 100 = **75%**.
10. Trade credit or an overdraft, because the need is short-term and sales will soon bring in cash to pay.
11. The business must meet conditions, such as creating jobs in a set area, and applications are slow and competitive.
12. It pays no interest and keeps full control, and an established business may have enough retained profit available.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which sources of finance are external?**
Family and friends, a new share issue, a loan or mortgage, an overdraft, trade credit, hire purchase and government grants.

**Which source is best for a short-term cash shortage?**
Usually an overdraft or trade credit, because they are flexible and only used for as long as needed.
