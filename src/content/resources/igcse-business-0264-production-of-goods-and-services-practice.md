## Questions

**1.** State two reasons why businesses hold inventory. **[2]**

**2.** Redmarle Lighting made 7,560 lamps with 27 employees in March. In April it made 7,200 lamps with 24 employees.

**(a)** Calculate labour productivity in March. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in labour productivity from March to April. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[3]**

**3.** Explain two reasons why efficiency is important for a business. **[4]**

**4.** Hollinside Tiles pays each worker £600 a week. At present 30 workers make 9,000 tiles a week. Demand has risen to 11,700 tiles a week. The owner is considering two options.

Option A: buy an automated pressing machine for £240,000. Only 26 workers would then be needed to make 11,700 tiles a week.
Option B: spend £9,000 on training, which would raise output to 340 tiles per worker per week. All 30 workers would stay.

**(a)** Calculate labour productivity at present and under option A. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the labour cost per tile at present and under option A. Give each answer to the nearest penny. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate which option Hollinside Tiles should choose. **[6]**

**5.** Thurlstone Biscuits holds a short meeting every week where factory staff suggest small changes to the way biscuits are made and packed.

**(a)** Define the term Kaizen. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one way Kaizen could reduce waste at Thurlstone Biscuits. **[2]**

**6.** Elmwick Candles uses just-in-time (JIT) inventory control for its wax and glass jars.

**(a)** Define the term lean production. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one advantage to Elmwick Candles of using JIT. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one disadvantage to Elmwick Candles of using JIT. **[2]**

**7.** Gilmerton Garden Tools sells lawnmowers. Most of its sales happen in spring and summer. Its supplier is overseas, delivery takes six weeks and is sometimes late. The firm has a small warehouse.

Explain two factors that will affect how much inventory Gilmerton Garden Tools holds. **[4]**

**8.**
**(a)** Identify the most suitable production method for each situation. Choose from job, batch or flow production.
(i) A hand-built church organ made for one parish.
(ii) A dairy making 600 cartons of strawberry ice cream and then switching to vanilla.
(iii) A plant making identical plastic bottles non-stop, day and night. **[3]**
**(b)** Define the term flow production. **[2]**

**9.** Pickworth Toys makes wooden toys in batches. Each time it changes from one toy to the next, the set-up costs £360 whatever the size of the batch.

**(a)** Calculate the set-up cost per toy for a batch of 500 toys and for a batch of 800 toys. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one advantage to Pickworth Toys of batch production. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one disadvantage to Pickworth Toys of batch production. **[2]**

**10.** Ainsty Boats builds five sailing boats a year. Each is designed with the customer and sells for about £60,000. A director suggests building a standard 20-boat range using batch production, to sell at about £28,000 each. This would need £300,000 of new equipment, and the sales team is not sure that enough customers would buy a standard boat.

Recommend which production method Ainsty Boats should use. Justify your answer. **[8]**

**11.** Colbeck Laundry has 12 staff who process 2,400 items a day. After it buys a conveyor system, the same staff process 2,880 items a day.

**(a)** Calculate labour productivity before the new system. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in labour productivity. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one disadvantage for employees of the new system. **[2]**

**12.** Yarnscombe Preserves makes jam. Its batch method uses 8 workers and makes 24,000 jars a month. A flow line would use 10 workers and make 85,000 jars a month, but costs £450,000. A supermarket may order 80,000 jars a month, though it has not yet signed a contract. Current sales are 24,000 jars a month.

Recommend whether Yarnscombe Preserves should switch to flow production. Justify your answer. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two of the following, one mark each:
- to keep production going if a supplier delivers late [1]
- to meet a sudden rise in demand without losing sales [1]
*Examiner insight:* Short, clear points suit "state". Repeating one reason in new words does not give a second reason.

**2. (a)** Labour productivity = output / employees [1]. 7,560 / 27 = **280 lamps per employee** [1].
**(b)** April: 7,200 / 24 = 300 lamps per employee [1]. Change = (300 - 280) / 280 x 100 [1]. = **7.1%** increase [1].
*Examiner insight:* Work out productivity in both months before comparing. Total output fell, but output per worker rose.

**3.** Any two reasons, each with a developed explanation:
- Efficiency lowers the cost of each unit [1], so the business can make more profit or cut its price [1].
- Efficiency makes the business more competitive [1], because it can charge less than rivals or spend more on quality [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Explain" answers gain credit when a reason is followed by its effect. "It saves money" alone is only a statement.

**4. (a)** At present: 9,000 / 30 = 300 tiles per worker [1]. Option A: 11,700 / 26 = **450 tiles per worker** [1].
**(b)** Now: 30 x £600 = £18,000; 18,000 / 9,000 = **£2.00 per tile** [1]. Option A: 26 x £600 = £15,600; 15,600 / 11,700 = **£1.33 per tile** [1].
**(c)** Option A raises productivity from 300 to 450, a 50% rise, and meets the full demand of 11,700 tiles [1]. Option B lifts output to 340 x 30 = 10,200 tiles, which is 1,500 tiles short of demand [1]. Option A also lowers the labour cost per tile from £2.00 to £1.33 [1]. The old method would pay £23,400 in wages for 11,700 tiles, so option A saves £7,800 a week and pays back in about 31 weeks [1]. Option B costs only £9,000 and avoids redundancies, though trained staff may leave [1]. Judgement: option A is better, because only it meets demand and it pays back within a year, provided demand stays near 11,700 tiles [1].
*Examiner insight:* An "evaluate" answer needs a judgement that rests on the data, such as the 1,500-tile shortfall and the payback period.

**5. (a)** Kaizen is continuous improvement [1] achieved through many small changes suggested by all employees [1].
**(b)** Workers may suggest changing the packing layout so fewer biscuits are broken [1], which reduces wasted biscuits and lowers the cost of each pack [1].
*Examiner insight:* Application means using the biscuits or the weekly meeting. "Kaizen reduces waste" with no how does not use the case.

**6. (a)** Lean production means making goods or services with as little waste as possible [1] while keeping quality high [1].
**(b)** With JIT Elmwick holds little wax and few jars [1], so it spends less money on storing stock and has more cash to use elsewhere [1].
**(c)** If a supplier delivers late, Elmwick has no spare wax or jars [1], so candle production stops and orders may be lost [1].
*Examiner insight:* "It is cheaper" or "it is risky" gains little. State the cause (low stock, late delivery), then the effect on this business.

**7.** Any two factors, each developed and applied:
- Demand is seasonal [1], so Gilmerton needs more stock before spring to avoid running out when sales peak [1].
- The supplier is overseas and sometimes late [1], so the firm needs a bigger buffer than a local supply would require [1].
*Examiner insight:* Pick factors the stem plainly supports, and link each to a direction: hold more or hold less.

**8. (a)** (i) **Job production** [1]. (ii) **Batch production** [1]. (iii) **Flow production** [1].
**(b)** Flow production is the continuous production of identical products [1] on a production line, with each stage following the last [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Identify" needs just the method name. Give one clear answer per situation.

**9. (a)** Batch of 500: 360 / 500 = **£0.72 per toy** [1]. Batch of 800: 360 / 800 = **£0.45 per toy** [1].
**(b)** Pickworth can switch toys as demand changes [1], offering variety with lower unit costs than one-off production [1].
**(c)** Machines stand idle while they are reset [1], so output is lost and each changeover adds a cost of £360 [1].
*Examiner insight:* Use your part (a) figures: a larger batch spreads the same set-up cost over more toys.

**10.** Recommendation: continue with job production [1]. Reasons: each boat is designed with its customer, so a standard batch removes what customers pay for [1]; the £60,000 premium would fall to £28,000 [1]; with only five boats a year, £300,000 of equipment is very costly [1]. Batch production needs firm evidence of demand for 20 standard boats, and the sales team is unsure [1]; if demand fell short, the equipment would sit idle [1]. Counter-argument: job production is slow, needs costly skilled labour and has a high cost per boat [1]. Conclusion: job production suits Ainsty Boats better, because customers value a bespoke boat and the batch plan carries cost and demand risk [1].
*Examiner insight:* State a method first, use several facts from the stem (five boats, £60,000, £300,000, unsure demand) and say why the rejected option fits less well.

**11. (a)** Labour productivity = output / employees [1]. 2,400 / 12 = **200 items per employee per day** [1].
**(b)** After: 2,880 / 12 = 240 [1]. (240 - 200) / 200 x 100 = **20.0%** increase [1].
**(c)** The conveyor may replace tasks staff did, so jobs could be lost [1]. Remaining staff may find work repetitive or feel insecure, lowering motivation [1].
*Examiner insight:* A service business can show productivity as items per employee. Write the unit next to your answer.

**12.** Recommendation: do not switch yet, or switch only once the contract is signed [1]. For switching: flow makes 8,500 jars per worker against 3,000, nearly 2.8 times as much, so labour cost per jar falls [1]; it makes 85,000 jars, enough to meet the possible supermarket order of 80,000 [1]. Against switching: current sales are only 24,000 jars, which the batch method already makes [1]; the £450,000 cost would be heavy, and with no contract signed the flow line could stand mostly idle [1]; flow production is inflexible, so a firm making many flavours would find changes harder [1]. Alternative: wait for a signed contract, or add batch capacity [1]. Judgement: keep batch production until demand is secure, because £450,000 is justified only if sales fill the line [1].
*Examiner insight:* A recommendation with a condition (a signed contract) works if the reasoning is clear. Weigh the productivity gain against the risk that demand does not arrive.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving productivity without a unit or period.
- Using total output instead of output per worker.
- Rounding a percentage change too early.
- Defining Kaizen as one big project, not continuous small changes.
- Saying JIT means no stock, rather than very little.
- Giving advantages with no link to the named business.
- Recommending a method without quoting figures from the question.
- Giving one side only when asked to justify or evaluate.
