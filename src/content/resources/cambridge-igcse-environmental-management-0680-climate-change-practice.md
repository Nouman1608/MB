## Section A

**1.** State **two** changes in agriculture or land use that release methane. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between afforestation and reforestation. **[2]**

**3.** Write the word equation for the reaction that releases carbon dioxide during cement manufacture. **[2]**

**4.** State **two** impacts of climate change on food supply. **[2]**

**5.** State what is meant by *carbon sequestration*. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Explain how burning fossil fuels can lead to climate change. **[3]**

**7.** Explain how ocean acidification can affect a coral reef ecosystem. **[3]**

**8.** A school uses 120,000 kWh of electricity a year. Each kWh from the national grid is responsible for 0.4 kg of carbon dioxide. The school fits solar panels that supply 30% of its electricity with no emissions in use.
(a) Calculate the school's yearly emissions from electricity before the panels were fitted.
(b) Calculate the yearly saving in carbon dioxide after the panels are fitted.
(c) Suggest **one** other strategy the school could use to reduce its carbon footprint. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how warmer winters can lead to more pest outbreaks, and how this affects farmers. **[3]**

**10.** Explain how eating a more plant-based diet and buying seasonal, locally produced food can reduce a person's carbon footprint. **[3]**

**11.** Describe how research, climate models and artificial intelligence (AI) can help governments respond to climate change. **[3]**

**12.** A farming region is experiencing longer droughts and occasional heavy floods. Describe **two** climate change adaptation strategies from the syllabus that would help, and explain how each helps. **[4]**

**13.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of carbon sequestration and of reducing the number of children per woman as strategies to limit the impacts of climate change. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, 1 mark each: cattle farming (digestion by cattle or manure decomposition) [1]; draining wetlands [1]. Also accept rice fields.

**2.** Afforestation is planting trees in an area that was not forest before [1]; reforestation is replanting trees where forest has been lost [1].

**3.** Calcium carbonate → calcium oxide + carbon dioxide: correct reactant [1]; both products correct [1].

**4.** Any two, 1 mark each: changes in crop yields, with lower yields in hotter, drier regions [1]; food shortages due to drought, floods or crop damage by extreme weather [1]. Also accept: more pest outbreaks damaging crops; disrupted marine food chains reducing fish catches.

**5.** Capturing, removing and storing carbon dioxide so it does not stay in the atmosphere [1].

**6.** Burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas [1]; higher concentrations mean more radiation is absorbed and re-emitted as heat, causing global warming [1]; global warming can result in long-term climate change, such as altered rainfall and more extreme weather [1].

**7.** More carbon dioxide dissolves in seawater, lowering its pH [1]; corals and shell-forming organisms find it harder to build skeletons and shells, so reefs grow more slowly or break down [1]; the many species that depend on the reef for food and shelter decline, reducing biodiversity [1].

**8. (a)** 120,000 × 0.4 = 48,000 kg [1].
**(b)** 48,000 × 30 / 100 [1] = 14,400 kg [1].
**(c)** Any one, for example: improve energy efficiency with LED lighting or insulation; encourage walking, cycling or buses to school; plant trees on school land [1].

**9.** Fewer pests are killed by cold weather, so more survive the winter [1]; pests can also spread to areas that were previously too cold for them [1]; crops are damaged, so yields and farmers' income fall, or more pesticide must be bought [1].

**10.** Livestock, especially cattle, release methane through digestion and manure, so eating less meat reduces methane emissions [1]; seasonal food does not need heated greenhouses or long-term cold storage, which use energy [1]; locally produced food travels shorter distances, so less fuel is burned in transport [1].

**11.** Research and climate models predict how temperature, rainfall and sea level may change in the future [1]; this lets governments plan where flood defences or new crops are needed [1]; AI can analyse large amounts of data to predict impacts and help develop strategies to reduce them [1].

**12.** Two strategies, 2 marks each. Crops that resist drier or wetter conditions [1] keep yields up during droughts or floods, protecting food supply and income [1]. Improved flood defences, such as embankments or wetlands that store floodwater [1], protect fields and homes during heavy floods [1]. Also accept legislation preventing building on floodplains, or building design and materials, each with an explanation.

**13.** Carbon sequestration: benefit, captures carbon dioxide at large sources such as power stations, so emissions are cut without closing them immediately [1]; limitation, it is expensive and the stored carbon dioxide must not leak back out [1]; limitation, it may encourage continued use of fossil fuels [1]. Fewer children per woman: benefit, slower population growth reduces future demand for energy and food [1]; limitation, it takes decades to have an effect and is a sensitive personal and cultural choice [1]. Judgement: sequestration can act sooner on large emitters, while population change works over the long term, so neither is enough alone [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Jumping from "carbon dioxide" straight to "climate change" without the global warming step.
- Confusing afforestation with reforestation.
- Giving an adaptation strategy when the question asks for a footprint strategy, or the reverse.
- Forgetting to work out the percentage of the total before calculating a saving.
- Giving only benefits in a "discuss" question, or no final judgement.
