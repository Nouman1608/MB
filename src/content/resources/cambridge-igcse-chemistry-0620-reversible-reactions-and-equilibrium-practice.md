## Section A

**1.** State the meaning of the symbol ⇌ in a chemical equation. **[1]**

**2.** Blue crystals of hydrated copper(II) sulfate are heated in a test tube.

**(a)** State the colour of the solid left at the end. **[1]**

**(b)** State what is seen when a few drops of water are added to the cooled solid. **[2]**

**3.** Anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride paper is used to test a colourless liquid. State the colour change if the liquid contains water. **[2]**

**4.** *(Extended)* Which statement about a reaction at equilibrium in a closed system is correct?

A: The forward and reverse reactions have both stopped.

B: The concentrations of reactants and products are equal.

C: The rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction.

D: Only the forward reaction continues. **[1]**

**5.** *(Extended)* State the typical temperature, pressure and catalyst used in the Contact process to convert sulfur dioxide to sulfur trioxide. **[3]**

**6.** *(Extended)* State the source of the hydrogen used in the Haber process. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** *(Extended)* Hydrogen and iodine react in a closed container.

H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g)

The forward reaction is exothermic. Iodine vapour is purple; hydrogen and hydrogen iodide are colourless.

**(a)** Predict and explain the effect of raising the temperature on the colour of the mixture. **[3]**

**(b)** Predict the effect of increasing the pressure on the position of equilibrium. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**8.** *(Extended)* Ethanol can be made from ethene and steam.

C₂H₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ C₂H₅OH(g)

The forward reaction is exothermic.

**(a)** Predict and explain the effect on the yield of ethanol of increasing the pressure. **[2]**

**(b)** Predict and explain the effect on the yield of ethanol of increasing the temperature. **[2]**

**(c)** State the effect on the yield of using a catalyst. **[1]**

**9.** *(Extended)* The Haber process makes ammonia.

**(a)** Write the symbol equation, with state symbols. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why a pressure of 200 atm is used, rather than a lower or a much higher pressure. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why an iron catalyst is used. **[2]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Explain why the Contact process uses a pressure of only 2 atm, even though higher pressure would increase the yield of sulfur trioxide. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* State the **two** sources of sulfur dioxide for the Contact process. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A student says: "At equilibrium nothing is happening because the amounts do not change." Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**13.** Describe an experiment to show that the dehydration of hydrated cobalt(II) chloride is reversible. Include what you would see at each stage. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The reaction is reversible / can go in both the forward and reverse directions [1].

**2. (a)** White [1].

**(b)** The solid turns blue [1]; the mixture gets warm / heat is released [1].

**3.** Blue [1] to pink [1].

**4.** **C** [1].

**5.** 450 °C [1]; 200 kPa / 2 atm [1]; vanadium(V) oxide [1].

**6.** Methane [1].

**7. (a)** The mixture becomes darker purple [1]; raising the temperature favours the endothermic direction, which is the reverse reaction [1]; so the equilibrium shifts left and the amount of iodine increases [1].

**(b)** No change in position [1]; there are equal numbers of moles of gas (2) on each side [1].

**8. (a)** Yield increases [1]; there are fewer moles of gas on the right (1 compared with 2), so higher pressure shifts the equilibrium to the right [1].

**(b)** Yield decreases [1]; the forward reaction is exothermic, so a higher temperature favours the reverse (endothermic) reaction / shifts the equilibrium to the left [1].

**(c)** No effect on the yield [1].

**9. (a)** N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g): correct formulae and balancing [1]; reversible arrow and state symbols [1].

**(b)** Higher pressure shifts the equilibrium to the right (4 mol gas → 2 mol gas), increasing the yield, and also increases the rate [1]; a much higher pressure would be too dangerous / need very expensive, strong equipment [1]; 200 atm is a compromise between yield, rate, safety and cost [1].

**(c)** It increases the rate of reaction / equilibrium is reached faster [1]; so a reasonable rate is obtained at a moderate temperature, which keeps the yield acceptable / reduces energy costs [1].

**10.** The position of equilibrium already lies far to the right at low pressure / the yield is already high [1]; a higher pressure would increase cost (equipment, energy) for very little extra yield [1].

**11.** Burning sulfur [1]; roasting sulfide ores [1].

**12.** Equilibrium is dynamic: the forward and reverse reactions are both still happening [1]; they happen at equal rates, so the concentrations stay constant [1].

**13.** Heat pink hydrated cobalt(II) chloride crystals in a test tube [1]; the solid turns blue (and water vapour is given off) [1]; allow to cool, then add a few drops of water [1]; the solid turns back to pink (and warms up), showing the reaction goes in the reverse direction [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating that concentrations are *equal* at equilibrium instead of *constant*.
- Counting liquid or solid substances, or all particles, when the pressure argument needs moles of gas only.
- Saying a catalyst increases the yield; it only changes how quickly equilibrium is reached.
- Answering a condition question with only a yield point or only a rate point; a compromise answer needs both.
- Swapping the Haber pressure (20 000 kPa) and the Contact pressure (200 kPa).
- Reversing the cobalt(II) chloride colours: anhydrous is blue, hydrated is pink.
