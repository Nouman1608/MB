8. **6.1.8** Eight management strategies.

## Earth structure

- **Crust**: thin, rigid; oceanic (thin, dense) and continental (thick, less dense).
- **Mantle**: hot, slowly moving rock; convection currents.
- **Core**: hottest and densest; iron and nickel; heat source.

## Convection currents in four steps

1. Core heats mantle rock at the bottom.
2. Hot rock becomes less dense and rises.
3. It spreads beneath the crust, cools and sinks.
4. The circular motion drags plates apart, together or past each other.

## Plate boundaries

| Boundary | Movement | Earthquakes | Volcanoes | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Divergent (constructive) | Apart | Yes, usually smaller | Yes | Mid-Atlantic Ridge, Iceland |
| Convergent (destructive) | Together, subduction | Yes, can be very powerful | Yes, a line of volcanoes | Andes, Japan |
| Conservative | Slide past | Yes | No | San Andreas Fault |

**Hotspot**: plume of hot mantle rock under the middle of a plate; chain of volcanoes as the plate moves over it (Hawaii).

## Earthquake features

- **Release of energy**: locked plates suddenly slip; seismic waves.
- **Focus**: underground starting point.
- **Epicentre**: surface point directly above the focus.
- **Magnitude**: Moment Magnitude Scale; +1 ≈ 32 times the energy, +2 ≈ 1,000 times.

## Eruption features

Magma (underground) → lava (at surface) · ash · gases: **water vapour, carbon dioxide, sulfur dioxide** · acid rain (from sulfur dioxide) · volcanic bombs (large lumps of lava that harden in the air) · pyroclastic flows (fast, hot clouds of gas, ash and rock).

## Impacts: memory list of ten

Buildings and infrastructure · crops, livestock, habitats · evacuation · fire · tsunamis · landslides · contaminated drinking water · water-related disease · financial losses · human health.

Chain to learn: **earthquake → burst water and sewage pipes → contaminated drinking water → water-related disease such as cholera.**

## Management by stage

| Before | During | After |
|---|---|---|
| Monitoring and warning | Evacuation | Shelters (continue) |
| Land use zoning | Shelters | Rebuilding of damaged areas |
| Structure of buildings | | International aid |
| Disaster preparation: plans, drills, emergency supplies, rescue teams | | |

One benefit and one limitation each:
- **Monitoring and warning**: warns of eruptions and tsunamis / earthquakes not precisely predictable.
- **Land use zoning**: keeps key buildings off danger zones / land scarce, enforcement weak.
- **Structure of buildings**: cross-bracing, flexible foundations prevent collapse / costly, hard to retrofit.
- **Disaster preparation**: quick, practised response / needs funding and repeated drills.
- **Evacuation**: saves lives / blocked roads, people unwilling to leave.
- **Shelters**: safety, food, water / overcrowding, disease.
- **Rebuilding**: restores services, chance to build better / slow, expensive.
- **International aid**: resources beyond local capacity / late, uneven, dependency.

## Opportunities from volcanoes

Fertile soils · extraction of minerals · building materials · geothermal power · tourism.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tectonic plate | Large slab of the Earth's crust that moves |
| Subduction | One plate sinking beneath another into the mantle |
| Convection current | Circular movement of heated mantle rock |
| Hotspot | Plume of hot mantle rock beneath a plate, away from boundaries |
| Focus | Point underground where an earthquake starts |
| Epicentre | Point on the surface directly above the focus |
| Magnitude | Amount of energy released by an earthquake |
| Pyroclastic flow | Fast cloud of hot gas, ash and rock |
| Tsunami | Waves caused by the seabed moving and displacing water |
| Land use zoning | Controlling which activities are allowed in which areas |

## Exam traps

- Focus and epicentre reversed.
- Volcanoes placed at conservative boundaries.
- Hotspots left out of "describe the distribution".
- Writing "Richter" when the syllabus names the Moment Magnitude Scale.
- Strategies listed without saying whether they act before, during or after.
- Volcanic opportunities given as general "jobs"; name the specific opportunity, such as tourism or geothermal power.

## Self-test

1. Name the three layers of the Earth.
2. Name the seven continents.
3. What is a hotspot?
4. Describe what happens at a convergent boundary.
5. Why are there no volcanoes at conservative boundaries?
6. Distinguish focus from epicentre.
7. Roughly how much more energy does a magnitude 7.0 earthquake release than a magnitude 5.0?
8. Name the three volcanic gases in the syllabus.
9. What is a pyroclastic flow?
10. How can an undersea earthquake cause a tsunami?
11. Give one benefit and one limitation of international aid.
12. Name two opportunities volcanoes provide.

**Answers:** 1. Crust, mantle, core. 2. Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Europe, North America, Oceania, South America. 3. A plume of hot mantle rock beneath the middle of a plate that forms volcanoes. 4. Plates move together and one is subducted beneath the other, causing earthquakes and a line of volcanoes. 5. Plates slide past each other, so no crust is subducted or pulled apart and no magma rises. 6. The focus is the underground starting point; the epicentre is on the surface directly above. 7. About 1,000 times (32 × 32). 8. Water vapour, carbon dioxide, sulfur dioxide. 9. A fast-moving cloud of hot gas, ash and rock flowing down a volcano. 10. The seabed moves suddenly and displaces the water above it, sending out waves. 11. Brings money, rescue teams and supplies beyond local capacity; may arrive late, miss remote areas or create dependency. 12. Any two: fertile soils, minerals, building materials, geothermal power, tourism.
