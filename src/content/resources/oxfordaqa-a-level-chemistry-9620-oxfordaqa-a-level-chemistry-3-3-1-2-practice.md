These questions cover **3.3.1.2 Reaction mechanisms** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 2 and able to reappear in Unit 5. Section A has short recall and application questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. Where a question asks you to describe curly arrows, say where each one starts and where it ends. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [reaction mechanisms study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-1-2/) or the [reaction mechanisms revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-1-2-revision-notes/) first.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Which equation is a propagation step?

A: Cl₂ → 2Cl•

B: CH₄ + Cl• → •CH₃ + HCl

C: •CH₃ + Cl• → CH₃Cl

D: 2•CH₃ → C₂H₆ **[1]**

**2.** This question is about bond fission.

**(a)** Define heterolytic fission. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the formulas of the two species formed when the C–Br bond in CH₃Br breaks heterolytically. **[1]**

**3.** State whether each step is initiation, propagation or termination.

**(a)** Br• + CH₃CH₃ → CH₃CH₂• + HBr **[1]**

**(b)** 2Br• → Br₂ **[1]**

**(c)** Br₂ → 2Br• **[1]**

**4.** A student writes this propagation step: CH₄ + Cl• → CH₃Cl + H•. Explain what is wrong with it. **[1]**

**5.** Ammonia acts as a nucleophile towards a halogenoalkane.

**(a)** State where the curly arrow from the ammonia molecule starts. **[1]**

**(b)** State the charge on the nitrogen atom immediately after it forms the new bond to carbon. **[1]**

**6.** In the first step of the reaction of but-2-ene with hydrogen bromide, an H atom bonds to one carbon of the C=C. Give the structural formula of the carbocation formed. **[1]**

### Section B

**7.** Cyclohexane, C₆H₁₂, reacts with chlorine in ultraviolet light to form chlorocyclohexane, C₆H₁₁Cl.

**(a)** Write an equation for the initiation step. **[1]**

**(b)** Write equations for the two propagation steps. **[2]**

**(c)** Write an equation for a termination step that forms a compound with the molecular formula C₁₂H₂₂. **[1]**

**(d)** Show that the two propagation steps add up to the overall equation. **[1]**

**8.** 1-Iodopropane, CH₃CH₂CH₂I, reacts with cyanide ions.

**(a)** Describe the two curly arrows in the mechanism. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the organic product. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the cyanide ion is described as a nucleophile. **[1]**

**9.** Ethene reacts with bromine by electrophilic addition.

**(a)** Bromine is a non-polar molecule. Explain how it becomes able to act as an electrophile when it approaches ethene. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the two curly arrows in the first step. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe the curly arrow in the second step. **[1]**

**(d)** Name the product. **[1]**

**10.** A student outlines the mechanism for the reaction of 2-bromopropane with hydroxide ions. The answer contains these features:

- an arrow from the O atom of HO⁻, with no lone pair drawn, to the carbon bonded to bromine
- an arrow from the Br atom to the C–Br bond
- the product shown as propan-2-ol and Br (no charge).

Identify three errors and correct each one. **[3]**

**11.** 2-Bromopropane also reacts with hydroxide ions by elimination, with the hydroxide ion acting as a base.

**(a)** Describe the three curly arrows in this mechanism. **[3]**

**(b)** Give the organic product. **[1]**

**12.** Ammonia reacts with bromoethane in two steps.

Step 1: CH₃CH₂Br + NH₃ → CH₃CH₂NH₃⁺ + Br⁻

Step 2: CH₃CH₂NH₃⁺ + NH₃ → CH₃CH₂NH₂ + NH₄⁺

**(a)** Describe the two curly arrows in step 1. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the nitrogen in CH₃CH₂NH₃⁺ carries a positive charge. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe the two curly arrows in step 2. **[2]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** B [1]

**2.**

**(a)** A covalent bond breaks with both electrons of the shared pair going to one of the atoms [1]

**(b)** CH₃⁺ and Br⁻ (both needed) [1]

**Total: 2**

**3.**

**(a)** Propagation [1]

**(b)** Termination [1]

**(c)** Initiation [1]

**Total: 3**

**4.** The chlorine radical removes a hydrogen atom to form HCl and a methyl radical (CH₄ + Cl• → •CH₃ + HCl); free H• is not formed [1]

**5.**

**(a)** At the lone pair on the nitrogen atom [1]

**(b)** +1 (positive) [1]

**Total: 2**

**6.** CH₃C⁺HCH₂CH₃ [1]

### Section B

**7.**

**(a)** Cl₂ → 2Cl• [1]

**(b)** C₆H₁₂ + Cl• → C₆H₁₁• + HCl [1]; C₆H₁₁• + Cl₂ → C₆H₁₁Cl + Cl• [1]

**(c)** 2C₆H₁₁• → C₁₂H₂₂ [1]

**(d)** Adding gives C₆H₁₂ + Cl• + C₆H₁₁• + Cl₂ → C₆H₁₁• + HCl + C₆H₁₁Cl + Cl•; cancelling the radicals that appear on both sides leaves C₆H₁₂ + Cl₂ → C₆H₁₁Cl + HCl [1]

**Total: 5**

*Examiner insight:* Dots must be on the carbon of the cyclohexyl radical. Curly arrows drawn on these equations are ignored at best and can contradict the answer.

**8.**

**(a)** From the lone pair on the carbon atom of CN⁻ to the δ+ carbon bonded to iodine [1]; from the C–I bond to the iodine atom, which leaves as I⁻ [1]

**(b)** Butanenitrile [1]

**(c)** It donates an electron pair to form a new covalent bond (to the δ+ carbon) [1]

**Total: 4**

*Examiner insight:* The nitrile has four carbons, because the CN carbon joins the three-carbon chain. "Propanenitrile" is a common slip.

**9.**

**(a)** The high electron density of the C=C repels the electrons in the Br–Br bond, inducing a dipole (Brδ+–Brδ−) [1]

**(b)** From the C=C double bond to the δ+ bromine atom [1]; from the Br–Br bond to the δ− bromine atom, which leaves as Br⁻ [1]

**(c)** From a lone pair on Br⁻ to the positive carbon of the carbocation [1]

**(d)** 1,2-dibromoethane [1]

**Total: 5**

**10.** One mark for each error identified with its correction [3]:

- No lone pair drawn on O: the arrow must start from a lone pair on the oxygen of HO⁻.
- The C–Br arrow points the wrong way: it must start at the C–Br bond and end at Br.
- Bromine product has no charge: it leaves as Br⁻, carrying the bonding pair.

**Total: 3**

**11.**

**(a)** From a lone pair on the oxygen of OH⁻ to a hydrogen atom on a carbon next to the C–Br carbon [1]; from that C–H bond to the C–C bond between the two carbons, forming C=C [1]; from the C–Br bond to Br, which leaves as Br⁻ [1]

**(b)** Propene [1]

**Total: 4**

*Examiner insight:* In elimination the hydroxide ion attacks a hydrogen, not the carbon. That is why it is acting as a base here and as a nucleophile in substitution.

**12.**

**(a)** From the lone pair on the nitrogen of NH₃ to the δ+ carbon bonded to Br [1]; from the C–Br bond to Br, which leaves as Br⁻ [1]

**(b)** Nitrogen has donated its lone pair to form a fourth covalent bond, so it now has one electron fewer under its control than in neutral NH₃, giving a +1 charge [1]

**(c)** From the lone pair on the nitrogen of the second NH₃ to an H atom on the N⁺ of CH₃CH₂NH₃⁺ [1]; from that N–H bond to the nitrogen of CH₃CH₂NH₃⁺, leaving its lone pair restored [1]

**Total: 5**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Arrows that do not start at a drawn lone pair or at a bond.
- Arrows pointing from the atom that leaves towards the bond.
- Missing charges on leaving groups and intermediates.
- H• in radical propagation steps.
- Miscounting the carbons in a nitrile product.
