## Enquiry 1 — the fall of tsarism, 1905–17

**1(a)** Describe **two** features of Stolypin's policies, 1906–11. **[6]**

**Source A** (invented). From the diary of a Petrograd factory worker, February 1917.

> "Three days of queues and no bread. Today the soldiers would not shoot. Some put their caps on their
> rifles and walked over to us. After that nobody was afraid."

**Source B** (invented). From a telegram sent by a senior Duma politician to the Tsar, February 1917.

> "The capital is in chaos. The government is paralysed. Troops are firing on one another. Any delay
> is fatal — a new government trusted by the country must be formed at once."

**1(b)** How far does Source A support the evidence of Source B about the situation in Petrograd in
February 1917? **[8]**

**Extract C** (invented interpretation).

> "The First World War, not revolutionary ideas, destroyed the Romanov monarchy."

**1(c)** How far do you agree with the interpretation in Extract C about the fall of tsarism? Use
Extract C, Sources A and B and your own knowledge. **[16]**

## Enquiry 2 — the Bolsheviks in power, 1917–24

**2(a)** Describe **two** features of War Communism. **[6]**

**Source D** (invented). From a Bolshevik newspaper, November 1917.

> "The workers and soldiers of Petrograd have overthrown the government of landlords and capitalists.
> Under the leadership of our party, the Soviets now hold power, and peace and land will follow."

**Source E** (invented). From the memoirs of a Menshevik who was in Petrograd in October 1917,
published abroad in 1925.

> "Most of the city went to work as usual. A few thousand Red Guards and sailors took the bridges and
> the telegraph while the ministers waited for troops who never came."

**2(b)** How far does Source D support the evidence of Source E about the Bolshevik seizure of power?
**[8]**

**Extract F** (invented interpretation).

> "The Bolsheviks won the Civil War because of Trotsky's leadership of the Red Army."

**2(c)** How far do you agree with the interpretation in Extract F? Use Extract F, Sources D and E and
your own knowledge. **[16]**

## Answers and mark guidance

### 1(a) [6]

Any two developed features: **repression** — field courts martial and many executions ("Stolypin's
necktie"); **land reform** — peasants allowed to leave the commune and consolidate holdings, helped by
the Peasant Land Bank and resettlement to Siberia; **changes to the electoral law** (1907) to produce a
more conservative Duma.

### 1(b) [8]

**Support:** both describe a breakdown of order and the loss of military control — A's soldiers who
"would not shoot", B's "troops… firing on one another"; both imply the government had lost authority
("nobody was afraid"; "paralysed").
**Difference:** A stresses hunger and the crowd's growing confidence; B stresses chaos and the need for
a new government.
**Provenance:** A is an eyewitness participant; B is written to persuade the Tsar to act, so may
exaggerate the danger. **Judgement:** strong support on the collapse of authority.

### 1(c) [16]

**Supporting:** military defeats and casualties; food and fuel shortages from transport breakdown;
inflation; Nicholas's decision to take command and the Rasputin scandal; Source A's bread queues;
the mutiny of war-weary soldiers.
**Challenging:** long-term problems — autocracy, peasant land hunger, urban working conditions, 1905
and the failure of the Dumas; the spread of socialist ideas; Nicholas's own character.
**Judgement:** war turned long-standing discontent into collapse; revolutionary parties played little
direct part in February, which supports the extract.

### 2(a) [6]

Two features: nationalisation of industry; forced requisitioning of grain; ban on private trade and
rationing; strict labour discipline; use of the Cheka to enforce it.

### 2(b) [8]

**Support:** both agree the Bolsheviks took power and the government fell.
**Challenge:** D presents a mass rising by "the workers and soldiers"; E describes a small operation by
a few thousand Red Guards and sailors while most of the city carried on as normal.
**Provenance:** D is Bolshevik propaganda written days later to legitimise the takeover; E is a hostile
Menshevik account written in exile. **Own knowledge:** the Winter Palace fell with little fighting;
the Military Revolutionary Committee planned the seizure. **Judgement:** limited support.

### 2(c) [16]

**Supporting:** Trotsky built the Red Army from about 1918, used ex-tsarist officers watched by
commissars, imposed discipline and toured the fronts in his armoured train.
**Other factors:** control of the central industrial area and railways; Lenin's political leadership;
War Communism and the Cheka; propaganda; White disunity, scattered fronts and brutality; limited
foreign help to the Whites; peasant fear of the return of landlords.
**Judgement:** Trotsky was important, but the Whites' weaknesses and Red geography were decisive.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing one feature twice in (a).
- In (b), comparing sources without reaching a judgement on "how far".
- In (c), ignoring the sources provided.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is the Paper 2 historical investigation question structured?**
Part (a) asks you to describe two features (6 marks), part (b) asks how far one source supports another (8 marks) and part (c) is a 16-mark judgement that uses the sources and your own knowledge.

**Which topics do these enquiries cover?**
The fall of tsarism, 1905–17, and the Bolsheviks in power, 1917–24, including Stolypin, War Communism and the NEP.

**What loses marks in part (c)?**
Ignoring the sources provided: the judgement must use them together with your own knowledge.
