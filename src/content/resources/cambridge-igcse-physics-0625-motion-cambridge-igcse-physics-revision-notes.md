These notes condense section 1.2, Motion, of the Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Supplement content, marked **Extended only**, is examined on Papers 2 and 4. For explanations and worked examples use the [study guide on motion](/resources/cambridge-igcse-physics-0625-motion-cambridge-igcse-physics/); the [Motion, forces and energy revision notes](/resources/igcse-physics-motion-forces-and-energy-revision-notes/) cover the rest of Topic 1.

## Equations to recall and use

| Equation | Symbols and units | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| v = s / t | v speed (m/s), s distance (m), t time (s) | Core |
| average speed = total distance travelled ÷ total time taken | m/s | Core |
| a = Δv / Δt | a acceleration (m/s²), Δv change in velocity (m/s), Δt time taken (s) | Extended only |

Rearranged: s = vt; t = s/v; Δv = aΔt.

Unit conversions: km/h ÷ 3.6 = m/s; minutes × 60 = s; hours × 3600 = s; km × 1000 = m.

## Condensed recall

- **Speed:** distance travelled per unit time. Scalar.
- **Velocity:** speed in a given direction. Changes if speed **or** direction changes.
- **g:** near the Earth's surface, approximately constant, about **9.8 m/s²**.
- **Average speed** is never found by averaging speeds that last different times.

### Reading graphs

| | Distance–time graph | Speed–time graph |
|---|---|---|
| horizontal line | at rest | constant speed (at rest if on the time axis) |
| straight line sloping up | constant speed | accelerating at a constant rate |
| line getting steeper | accelerating | acceleration increasing *(Extended)* |
| line getting less steep | decelerating | acceleration decreasing *(Extended)* |
| sloping down | cannot happen (distance travelled never decreases) | decelerating |
| gradient gives | speed | acceleration *(Extended)* |
| area under gives | nothing useful | distance travelled |

### Areas under speed–time graphs (Core)

- Rectangle: speed × time.
- Triangle: ½ × time × change in speed.
- Trapezium (speeding up from u to v): split into a rectangle and a triangle, or use ½ × (u + v) × t.
- Add the areas for each stage; read values from the axes, with units.

### Acceleration (Extended only)

- Change in velocity per unit time.
- Constant acceleration: straight sloping speed–time line.
- Changing acceleration: curved speed–time line.
- **Deceleration = negative acceleration.** Braking from 15 m/s to 0 in 5.0 s: a = −3.0 m/s².

### Falling (Extended only)

- **No air resistance:** constant acceleration g; straight speed–time line, gradient 9.8 m/s².
- **With air or liquid resistance:**
  - speed rises → drag rises → resultant force falls → acceleration falls;
  - drag = weight → resultant force zero → **terminal velocity** (constant speed).
- Parachute opens: drag > weight → decelerates → new, lower terminal velocity.
- Terminal velocity is reached much sooner in a liquid than in air.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Speed | Distance travelled per unit time |
| Velocity | Speed in a given direction |
| Average speed | Total distance travelled ÷ total time taken |
| Gradient | Change in the vertical-axis quantity ÷ change in the horizontal-axis quantity |
| Acceleration (Extended) | Change in velocity per unit time |
| Deceleration (Extended) | Negative acceleration; slowing down |
| Acceleration of free fall, g | Acceleration of an object falling freely near the Earth, about 9.8 m/s² |
| Terminal velocity (Extended) | Constant speed reached when drag balances weight |

## Exam traps

- Mixing up graph types: always read the vertical axis label first.
- Counting squares when the two axes have different scales: use the axis values.
- Using a short gradient triangle: draw one across most of the straight section.
- Forgetting to convert minutes to seconds before using v = s/t.
- Giving the area of a triangle as base × height (forgetting the ½).
- *(Extended)* Writing "terminal velocity is when the object stops accelerating because there are no forces"; the forces are **balanced**, not absent.
- *(Extended)* Saying a heavier object falls with a larger acceleration in a vacuum: all objects fall with acceleration g.

## Quick self-test

1. Convert 54 km/h into m/s.
2. A runner covers 400 m in 64 s. Find her average speed.
3. A car travels 3.0 km in 2.0 minutes then 6.0 km in 4.0 minutes. Find its average speed in m/s.
4. What does a horizontal line on a distance–time graph show?
5. What does a horizontal line above the axis on a speed–time graph show?
6. A train accelerates steadily from rest to 20 m/s in 40 s. Find the distance it travels in this time.
7. State the approximate value of the acceleration of free fall near the Earth.
8. *(Extended)* Define acceleration.
9. *(Extended)* A ball's velocity changes from 2.0 m/s to 14 m/s in 4.0 s. Find its acceleration.
10. *(Extended)* A lorry slows from 18 m/s to 6.0 m/s in 8.0 s. Find its acceleration.
11. *(Extended)* What does a curved, flattening speed–time line show about the acceleration?
12. *(Extended)* Explain why a falling object reaches a terminal velocity.

### Answers

1. 54 ÷ 3.6 = **15 m/s**.
2. 400 ÷ 64 = **6.25 m/s** (6.3 m/s).
3. 9000 m ÷ 360 s = **25 m/s**.
4. The object is **at rest**.
5. **Constant speed**.
6. Area = ½ × 40 × 20 = **400 m**.
7. About **9.8 m/s²**.
8. **Change in velocity per unit time**.
9. (14 − 2.0) ÷ 4.0 = **3.0 m/s²**.
10. (6.0 − 18) ÷ 8.0 = **−1.5 m/s²** (a deceleration of 1.5 m/s²).
11. The acceleration is **decreasing**.
12. As speed increases, **drag increases** until it **equals the weight**; the resultant force is then zero, so the acceleration is zero and the speed stays constant.
