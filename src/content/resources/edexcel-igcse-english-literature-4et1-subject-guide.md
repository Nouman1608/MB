Pearson Edexcel International GCSE English Literature (4ET1) asks students to
read poetry, prose and drama closely and to write about how writers create
meaning and effect. The course combines unseen poetry with anthology poems,
a modern prose text, a modern play and a text from the literary
heritage. Every student takes Component 1, a closed-book exam of 120 minutes
worth 90 marks and 60%. The other 40% (60 marks) comes from either
Component 2, an open-book exam, or Component 3, two coursework assignments.
The specification was first taught in September 2016 and first examined in
June 2018.

## How the course is organised

The course has three components, two of which are alternatives covering the
same kinds of text.

- **Component 1: Poetry and Modern Prose** is compulsory and has three
  sections. Section A is unseen poetry, a poem you have not studied before.
  Section B is anthology poetry. Section C is modern prose.
- **Component 2: Modern Drama and Literary Heritage Texts** is the examined
  option. Section A is modern drama; Section B is a literary heritage text.
- **Component 3: Modern Drama and Literary Heritage Texts** is the
  coursework alternative to Component 2. Assignment A is on modern drama and
  Assignment B is on a literary heritage text.

In practice this means two halves. Component 1 is about poetry and prose,
including the skill of responding to an unfamiliar poem. Components 2 and 3
are about drama and older literature, and your centre decides whether that
half is examined or assessed through coursework.

## How it's assessed

4ET1 is untiered. Each student completes Component 1 plus either Component 2
or Component 3.

| Component | Paper code | Format | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Component 1: Poetry and Modern Prose | 4ET1/01 | Written exam, closed book, externally assessed | 120 minutes (2h) | 90 | 60% |
| Component 2: Modern Drama and Literary Heritage Texts (examined) | 4ET1/02 | Written exam, open book, externally assessed | 90 minutes (1h30) | 60 | 40% |
| Component 3: Modern Drama and Literary Heritage Texts (coursework) | 4ET1/03 | Two assignments, internally set and assessed, externally moderated by Pearson | Not timed | 60 | 40% |

Component 2's two sections are worth 30 marks each. Component 3's two
assignments are also worth 30 marks each. Since both alternatives carry the
same marks and weighting, the choice affects how the drama and heritage work
is assessed, not how much it counts.

Pearson also publishes a separate modular version of the course (4XET1).
It is not covered on this page.

## How the parts fit together

The difference between closed book and open book shapes revision. Component
1 is closed book, so for the anthology poems and the modern prose text you
need to know the texts well enough to recall key moments and short
quotations without the book in front of you. Component 2, if your centre
chooses it, is open book, so the emphasis there moves from recall towards
finding and analysing the right evidence quickly.

Unseen poetry is the one part of the course that cannot be revised by
learning a text. It depends entirely on transferable skills: noticing how
imagery, form, structure and sound create meaning. Those same skills are
what you use when writing about the anthology poems, the prose text, the
play and the heritage text, so practising unseen analysis improves your
work in every component.

Across the whole qualification, the core habits are the same: answer the
question asked, support each point with precise reference to the text,
explain the effect of the writer's choices, and show awareness of context
where it helps the argument.

## Planning your study

Ask early which route your centre follows. If you are taking Component 3,
the two assignments are completed during the course according to your
centre's schedule, so find out the deadlines and rules at the start. If you
are taking Component 2, the drama and heritage texts are revised for a timed
open-book paper.

A sensible two-year plan starts with the anthology poems and regular unseen
poetry practice, since both build analytical confidence, and moves on to the
modern prose text. The modern drama and literary heritage texts usually
follow, though centres differ. In the second year, keep returning to the
Component 1 texts, as a closed-book paper rewards repeated rereading, and
keep doing short unseen poetry exercises right up to the exam.

Begin timed practice once all the texts have been read at least once. For
advice on dividing time across the sections of each paper, use the
exam-preparation page rather than this overview.

## Frequently asked questions

**What components are in Pearson Edexcel IGCSE English Literature 4ET1?**
Component 1 (Poetry and Modern Prose) is compulsory. Students then take either Component 2, an exam on modern drama and a literary heritage text, or Component 3, coursework on the same kinds of text.

**Is Component 1 open book or closed book?**
Component 1 is closed book. Component 2 is open book.

**How much is each component worth?**
Component 1 is a 120-minute exam worth 90 marks and 60%. Component 2 is a 90-minute exam worth 60 marks and 40%. Component 3 is worth 60 marks and 40%, with 30 marks for each of its two assignments.

**What is the unseen poetry section?**
Section A of Component 1 asks you to respond to a poem you have not studied, so it tests analytical skills rather than knowledge of a set text.

**Who marks the Component 3 coursework?**
Component 3 is internally set and assessed by your centre and then externally moderated by Pearson.
