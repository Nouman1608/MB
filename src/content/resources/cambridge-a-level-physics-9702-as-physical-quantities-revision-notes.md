Condensed for the final weeks. For the full explanation and worked estimates, use the [Physical quantities study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-physical-quantities/); for exam-style questions, the [Physical quantities practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-physical-quantities-practice/). These notes cover subtopic **1.1 Physical quantities** of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702, 2025–2027 series.

## The two learning outcomes

1. Every physical quantity consists of a **numerical magnitude and a unit**.
2. Make **reasonable estimates** of physical quantities included within the syllabus.

## Magnitude and unit: what to recall

```
quantity = number x unit
l = 0.45 m   means   l = 0.45 x (1 m)
l / m = 0.45      l / cm = 45      l / mm = 450
```

- The unit is a **multiplying factor**. Conversions are substitutions: replace "km" by "1000 m", "h" by "3600 s", "g" by "10⁻³ kg".
- Units cancel and combine **algebraically**: N ÷ m² = N m⁻² = Pa; J ÷ s = W; kg × m s⁻² = N.
- If the units of an answer do not match the quantity asked for, the method has an error.
- A **ratio of like quantities** (efficiency, strain, refractive index, a ratio of two lengths) is a pure number: no unit.

## Table headings and graph axes

The syllabus practical section asks for column headings that include **both quantity and unit**, following accepted conventions.

| Heading | Acceptable? |
|---|---|
| `I / mA` | Yes, the usual form |
| `I (mA)` or `I in mA` | Allowed |
| `current / mA` | Allowed |
| `I mA` | No |
| `mA` alone | No |

Graph axes follow the same convention as table headings. Entries in a `t / s` column are pure numbers: the value of t divided by one second.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Physical quantity | A measurable property, expressed as a number multiplied by a unit |
| Numerical magnitude | The number part of the value |
| Unit | The standard amount the number multiplies, such as the metre |
| Pure number | A value with no unit, from a ratio of like quantities |
| Estimate | A reasonable value to 1 or 2 significant figures, based on stated assumptions |
| Order of magnitude | The power of ten nearest to a value |

## How to estimate in four steps

1. Write the physics equation linking the quantity to things you can picture.
2. Choose a value for each input, with its unit, to 1 significant figure.
3. Calculate, rounding freely (g ≈ 10 m s⁻²).
4. Give the result to 1 or 2 significant figures and check it is believable.

**Recall examples (rounded):**

- Kinetic energy of a sprinter: ½ × 70 × 10² = 3500 J, about 4 × 10³ J.
- Weight of a textbook: 1 kg × 10 N kg⁻¹ = 10 N.
- Volume of a person: mass ÷ density ≈ 70 ÷ 1000 = 0.07 m³ (a human body has a density close to that of water).
- Momentum of a car on a motorway: 1000 × 30 = 3 × 10⁴ kg m s⁻¹.

## Exam traps

- Leaving the unit off a final answer.
- "Moving the decimal point" for squared units: 1 cm² is 10⁻⁴ m², not 10⁻² m².
- Quoting an estimate to 3 or more significant figures.
- Writing a heading as `mA` or `I mA`.
- Giving a unit to efficiency or strain.
- Choosing an estimate option that is right only if the mass is in grams: check every input is in SI units before calculating.
- Assuming a large-looking number is wrong: 200 kg of air in a classroom is correct.

## Self-test

1. What two parts make up every physical quantity?
2. Convert 54 km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹.
3. A column is headed `d / mm` and an entry reads 3.5. What is d in metres?
4. Why does efficiency have no unit?
5. Which of these headings is not acceptable: `m / g`, `mass (g)`, `g`?
6. Estimate the weight of an adult.
7. Estimate the kinetic energy of a car of mass 1000 kg moving at 30 m s⁻¹.
8. Estimate the mass of water in a full bath of volume 0.2 m³.
9. To how many significant figures should an estimate be given?
10. Give the unit obtained when a force in newtons is divided by an area in square metres.
11. Estimate the pressure at the bottom of a swimming pool 2 m deep due to the water alone.
12. A student estimates walking speed as 15 m s⁻¹. Explain why this is unreasonable.

### Answers

1. A numerical magnitude and a unit.
2. 54 × 1000 ÷ 3600 = 15 m s⁻¹.
3. 3.5 × 10⁻³ m.
4. It is a ratio of two energies (or powers), so the units cancel.
5. `g` alone, because it does not state the quantity.
6. About 700 N.
7. ½ × 1000 × 30² = 4.5 × 10⁵ J, about 5 × 10⁵ J.
8. 1000 × 0.2 = 200 kg.
9. One, or at most two.
10. N m⁻², which is the pascal.
11. ρgh ≈ 1000 × 10 × 2 = 2 × 10⁴ Pa.
12. 15 m s⁻¹ is faster than a sprinter (about 10 m s⁻¹); walking is about 1.5 m s⁻¹, so a factor of ten has been lost, probably in a unit conversion.
