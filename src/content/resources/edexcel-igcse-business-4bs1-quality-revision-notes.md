Quick recall for section 5.4 Quality of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1) specification, Issue 2, February 2026, Topic 5 Business operations. The specification statement 5.4.1 reads: "The concept of quality and its importance in: the production of goods and the provision of services: quality control, total quality management (TQM); allowing a business to gain a competitive advantage." Full explanations are in the [quality study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-quality/); exam-style questions are in the [quality practice set](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-quality-practice/). Both papers can set quality questions, on a small business (Paper 1) or a large one (Paper 2). All figures below are invented.

## 1. Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Quality | Meeting or exceeding what customers reasonably expect for the price, consistently |
| Quality standard | The level a business sets that each good or service must reach |
| Defect | A product or service that fails to meet the standard |
| Quality control | Checking output against the standard and removing or correcting items that fail, usually by inspectors |
| Total quality management (TQM) | Every employee is responsible for quality; faults are prevented at source, aiming for zero defects |
| Internal customer | The next person or department in a process, treated as a customer under TQM |
| Product recall | Asking customers to return a product already sold because of a fault, often a safety fault |
| Competitive advantage | A reason customers choose a business rather than its rivals |

## 2. Measuring quality

| Indicator | Formula |
|---|---|
| Defect rate | faulty items ÷ items produced × 100 |
| Return rate | items returned ÷ items sold × 100 |
| Complaint rate | complaints ÷ customers served × 100 |
| Repeat-customer rate | returning customers ÷ total customers × 100 |
| Cost of defects | number of defects × cost per defect |

Always compare **rates**, not raw numbers, when output or customer numbers change.

Mini example: a print shop completes 2,500 orders and 75 must be reprinted at 8 each. Defect rate = 75 ÷ 2,500 × 100 = 3%. Cost = 75 × 8 = 600.

## 3. Why quality matters

**Goods**

- Inside the business: scrap, rework, wasted labour and machine time, so higher unit costs.
- Outside the business: refunds, replacements, repairs under guarantee, recalls, possible legal action.
- Reputation: retailers and customers avoid unreliable brands, so future sales fall.

**Services**

- Produced and consumed at the same time, so they cannot be inspected before the customer receives them.
- Staff behaviour is part of the product, so quality varies from employee to employee.
- Intangible, so customers rely on reviews and recommendations.
- Handling complaints well (apology, refund, quick fix) can keep a customer.

## 4. Quality control in practice

- **Where to check**: incoming materials, during production, finished goods.
- **How often**: every item for expensive or safety-critical products; a sample for large volumes of cheap items.
- **Services**: mystery customers, recorded calls, staff checklists, feedback cards.
- **Strengths**: simple, quick to set up, stops many faults reaching customers, produces data.
- **Weaknesses**: finds faults after they happen, inspectors cost money, samples miss faults, workers may leave quality to inspectors.

## 5. TQM in practice

- Everyone checks their own work and stops problems at their cause.
- Each stage passes only good work to the next stage (its internal customer).
- Needs training for all staff, managers who let staff solve problems, and time.
- **Strengths**: less waste, lower long-run costs, fewer faults reach customers, staff more involved.
- **Weaknesses**: training cost, takes time, possible staff resistance, relies on commitment from everyone.

## 6. Quality control or TQM?

| Situation in the case | Likely better choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Sole trader or very small team | Quality control | Owner can check personally; training budget is small |
| Many production stages | TQM | Faults caught early cost less than faults found at the end |
| Safety-critical product | TQM plus final checks | A fault reaching a customer is very costly |
| High staff turnover | Quality control | Training is lost when staff leave |
| Business needs a quick improvement | Quality control | TQM benefits take time |

A business can combine them: prevention through TQM and a final check for what slips through.

## 7. Quality and competitive advantage

Chain to learn: higher quality → fewer problems for customers → better reviews and repeat purchases → higher sales or a higher price → more revenue and possibly more profit.

Conditions: customers must **notice** the quality, **value** it enough to pay for it, and **not find it easily** at a rival.

Mini example: a café charges 4.20 for coffee against a rival's 3.50. Premium = 0.70, which is 0.70 ÷ 3.50 × 100 = 20%. If better beans and training add 0.30 per cup, extra profit per cup = 0.40.

## 8. Exam traps

- Quality is not luxury: a cheap product can be high quality if it meets expectations.
- Better quality usually costs more: weigh the extra cost per unit against the price premium or savings.
- TQM does not remove all checking; it moves the focus to prevention.
- A falling number of complaints means little if customer numbers fell faster: calculate the rate.
- In a Justify question, choose one option and say why the other is weaker.
- In an Evaluate question, end with a judgement and what it depends on.
- Outline needs two linked points in context; Explain needs a point plus two expansions.

## 9. Self-test

1. Define quality.
2. State two reasons why poor quality raises a manufacturer's costs.
3. Give one reason why quality is harder to control in a service than in a good.
4. A factory makes 4,000 units and 120 are faulty. Calculate the defect rate.
5. What is an internal customer?
6. Give one advantage of inspecting a sample rather than every item.
7. State two features of TQM.
8. Give one reason why a sole trader might prefer quality control to TQM.
9. A hotel had 48 complaints from 2,400 guests. Calculate the complaint rate.
10. State one condition needed for quality to give a competitive advantage.
11. A product sells for 30 while a rival's sells for 25. Calculate the price premium as a percentage of the rival's price.
12. What is a product recall?

### Answers

1. Meeting or exceeding what customers reasonably expect for the price, consistently.
2. Any two: scrapped materials, rework labour, wasted machine time, refunds and replacements, repairs under guarantee.
3. It is produced and consumed at the same time, so it cannot be checked before the customer receives it (or: staff vary in how they deliver it).
4. 120 ÷ 4,000 × 100 = 3%.
5. The next person or department in a process, whose needs the previous stage must meet.
6. It is cheaper and quicker, especially for large volumes of low-value items.
7. Any two: everyone responsible for quality, prevention at source, zero-defect aim, staff training, internal customers.
8. Training costs and time are high for a small business, and the owner can check work personally.
9. 48 ÷ 2,400 × 100 = 2%.
10. Customers must notice it, value it, or be unable to get it easily from rivals (any one).
11. (30 − 25) ÷ 25 × 100 = 20%.
12. Asking customers to return a product already sold because it is faulty or unsafe.
