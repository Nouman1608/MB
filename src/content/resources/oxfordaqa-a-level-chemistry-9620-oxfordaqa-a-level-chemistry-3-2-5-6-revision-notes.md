These notes condense **3.2.5.6 Catalysts** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content, examined in Unit 3 and able to reappear in Unit 5. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [catalysts study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-5-6/); then try the [catalysts practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-5-6-practice/).

## The subtopic in five lines

1. **Heterogeneous**: different phase from reactants; reaction at **active sites on the surface**.
2. A **support medium** maximises surface area and minimises cost; **poisons** block active sites and cost money.
3. **Homogeneous**: same phase as reactants; the reaction goes through an **intermediate species**.
4. **Variable oxidation states** let a transition metal accept electrons in one step and return them in the next.
5. Named examples: V₂O₅ (Contact), Fe (Haber), Fe²⁺ (S₂O₈²⁻ + I⁻), Mn²⁺ (autocatalysis of MnO₄⁻ + C₂O₄²⁻).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Catalyst | Increases rate by giving a route of lower activation energy; not used up overall |
| Heterogeneous catalyst | In a different phase from the reactants |
| Homogeneous catalyst | In the same phase as the reactants |
| Active site | Place on the catalyst surface where reactants are adsorbed and react |
| Adsorption | Reactants forming weak bonds to the catalyst surface |
| Desorption | Products leaving the surface |
| Support medium | Inert material with a large surface area carrying a thin layer of catalyst |
| Poisoning | Impurity blocking active sites, so efficiency falls |
| Intermediate | Species formed in one step and used in a later one |
| Autocatalysis | A product of the reaction acts as its catalyst |

## Heterogeneous catalysts

**Sequence at the surface:** adsorption → bonds weakened, reactants held close → reaction → desorption.

| Example | Equation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Contact process | 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ | V₂O₅ catalyst; vanadium cycles between +5 and +4 |
| Haber process | N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ | Fe catalyst; poisoned by sulfur compounds |
| Catalytic converter | 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂ | Pt/Rh on a ceramic honeycomb support; poisoned by lead |

**V₂O₅ steps (learn exactly):**

```
V₂O₅ + SO₂ → V₂O₄ + SO₃      (+5 → +4)
V₂O₄ + ½O₂ → V₂O₅            (+4 → +5)
```

**Cost implications of poisoning:** lower rate and output; catalyst cleaned or replaced more often; feedstock must be purified.

## Homogeneous catalysts

**Fe²⁺ and the S₂O₈²⁻ / I⁻ reaction**

```
S₂O₈²⁻ + 2Fe²⁺ → 2SO₄²⁻ + 2Fe³⁺
2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂
```

- Uncatalysed: two negative ions repel, high activation energy.
- Catalysed: each step is between oppositely charged ions.
- Fe³⁺ works too; the steps happen in the other order.
- E⦵ check: Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ (+0.77 V) lies between S₂O₈²⁻/SO₄²⁻ (+2.01 V) and I₂/I⁻ (+0.54 V), so both steps are feasible.

**Mn²⁺ autocatalysis**

```
MnO₄⁻ + 4Mn²⁺ + 8H⁺ → 5Mn³⁺ + 4H₂O
2Mn³⁺ + C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 2CO₂
```

- Slow at first (little Mn²⁺; two negative ions react).
- Speeds up as Mn²⁺ forms.
- Slows again as reactants run out.
- [MnO₄⁻]–time graph: S-shaped fall. Rate–time graph: rises, then falls.
- Adding Mn²⁺ at the start removes the slow stage.

## Recognising the type

| Catalyst | Reactant phase | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Fe(s), Haber | gases | heterogeneous |
| V₂O₅(s), Contact | gases | heterogeneous |
| Pt/Rh(s), exhaust | gases | heterogeneous |
| Fe²⁺(aq) | aqueous ions | homogeneous |
| Mn²⁺(aq) | aqueous ions | homogeneous (autocatalyst) |

## Exam traps

- Heterogeneous means **different phase**, not simply "solid".
- A support medium is there for **surface area and cost**; quote both.
- Poisons **block** active sites; they do not "use up" the catalyst.
- Show V₂O₅ being regenerated; give oxidation states if asked to explain.
- For Fe²⁺ catalysis, give the charge argument; E⦵ values show feasibility only.
- Intermediates: Fe³⁺ in the iodide reaction; Mn³⁺ in autocatalysis.
- An autocatalysis curve is not an exponential decay; describe all three stages.
- Iron metal is heterogeneous; aqueous Fe²⁺ is homogeneous.

## Self-test

Answer without looking, then check below.

1. Define a heterogeneous catalyst.
2. Give the three stages of heterogeneous catalysis in order.
3. State two reasons for using a support medium.
4. Name the catalyst in the Haber process and one substance that poisons it.
5. Write the two equations showing how V₂O₅ catalyses the oxidation of SO₂.
6. Give two cost implications of catalyst poisoning.
7. Define a homogeneous catalyst and state what species it always forms.
8. Why is the reaction between S₂O₈²⁻ and I⁻ slow without a catalyst?
9. Write the two equations for Fe²⁺ catalysis of this reaction.
10. Why does Fe³⁺ catalyse the same reaction?
11. What is autocatalysis? Give the autocatalyst in the MnO₄⁻ / C₂O₄²⁻ reaction.
12. Describe how the rate of the MnO₄⁻ / C₂O₄²⁻ reaction changes with time.

### Answers

1. A catalyst in a different phase from the reactants; reaction occurs at active sites on its surface.
2. Adsorption, reaction, desorption.
3. Maximises the surface area of the catalyst; minimises the cost (less catalyst needed).
4. Iron; sulfur compounds.
5. V₂O₅ + SO₂ → V₂O₄ + SO₃; V₂O₄ + ½O₂ → V₂O₅.
6. Any two: lower efficiency so less product per hour; cost of replacing or regenerating the catalyst; cost of purifying the feedstock.
7. A catalyst in the same phase as the reactants; it forms an intermediate species.
8. Both reactants are negative ions, which repel, so the activation energy is high.
9. S₂O₈²⁻ + 2Fe²⁺ → 2SO₄²⁻ + 2Fe³⁺; 2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂.
10. It can react with I⁻ first, forming Fe²⁺, which then reacts with S₂O₈²⁻; the same two steps occur in the other order.
11. A reaction catalysed by one of its own products; Mn²⁺.
12. Slow at first, then faster as Mn²⁺ builds up, then slower as the reactants are used up.
