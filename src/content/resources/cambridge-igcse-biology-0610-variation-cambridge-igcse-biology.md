This guide goes deeper into one sub-topic of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028: **18.1 Variation**, the first part of topic 18, Variation and selection. It has ten learning outcomes. Outcomes 1 to 8 are Core and are tested on every paper. Outcomes 9 and 10, on gene mutation and the sources of genetic variation, are Supplement content, so they are **Extended only** (Papers 2 and 4). Outcome 5 is an investigation, so it can also appear on the practical papers.

The [variation and selection study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-variation-and-selection/) covers the whole topic. This page goes further on 18.1: deciding which type of variation a set of data shows, separating genetic from environmental causes, planning an investigation and writing precise answers on mutation. ## 18.1.1 What counts as variation

Variation is the differences between individuals **of the same species**. A lion and a tiger differ, but that is a difference between species, not variation in the syllabus sense. Two lions with different mane colours do show variation.

Every difference you can observe is part of the **phenotype**, the observable features of an organism.

## 18.1.2 and 18.1.3 Telling the two types apart

Ask two questions about any feature.

1. **Can the feature take any value within a range?** If yes, it is continuous. Body mass can be 61.2 kg, 61.3 kg or anything between.
2. **Are there a few distinct categories with nothing in between?** If yes, it is discontinuous. A person is blood group A, B, AB or O; nobody is "halfway between A and B".

| Feature | Type | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Body length of a fish | Continuous | any value between the shortest and longest |
| Mass of a potato tuber | Continuous | any value in a range |
| ABO blood group | Discontinuous | four groups, no intermediates |
| Seed shape in peas (round or wrinkled) | Discontinuous | two categories |
| Seed colour in peas (yellow or green) | Discontinuous | two categories |

**Trap:** the way data are recorded does not change the type. Heights rounded to the nearest centimetre still show continuous variation, because the feature itself could take any value.

## 18.1.4 Genes, environment or both?

- **Discontinuous variation is usually caused by genes only.** Your ABO blood group is set by the alleles you inherit. Diet, climate and exercise cannot change it.
- **Continuous variation is caused by both genes and the environment.** Alleles set a potential range; conditions decide where an individual falls within it. A plant's height depends on its alleles and also on light, water, temperature and mineral ions in the soil.

### How scientists separate the two causes

The neat way to show that the environment matters is to keep the genes the same. Organisms with identical genes, such as plants grown as cuttings from one parent or identical twins, have the same genotype. Any differences between them must come from the environment.

**Worked example 1.** Twenty cuttings were taken from one geranium plant. Ten grew on a sunny windowsill and ten in a shaded corner. After six weeks the sunny plants had a mean height of 24 cm and the shaded plants 15 cm (illustrative figures).

- **Why use cuttings?** They are genetically identical, so genes cannot explain the difference.
- **Conclusion:** the height difference was caused by the environment (light intensity).

## 18.1.5 Investigating variation

A good investigation of **continuous** variation needs:

- one clearly defined feature, measured the same way each time (for example, leaf length from the base of the blade to the tip, excluding the stalk);
- a large sample, chosen at random, so the results represent the population;
- organisms of a similar age and from the same area, if the aim is to see variation within one group;
- measurements grouped into **equal class intervals**, then drawn as a **histogram** with touching bars.

For **discontinuous** variation, count how many individuals fall into each category and draw a **bar chart** with separate bars.

**Worked example 2 (continuous).** A student measured 50 leaves from one bay tree (illustrative data).

| Leaf length / mm | 40–49 | 50–59 | 60–69 | 70–79 | 80–89 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number of leaves | 4 | 11 | 19 | 12 | 4 |

- Modal class: **60–69 mm**.
- Percentage in the modal class: 19 ÷ 50 × 100 = **38%**.
- Graph: histogram, leaf length on the x-axis, number of leaves on the y-axis, bars touching.
- All leaves came from one tree, so they share the same genes: the variation here is caused by the **environment**, for example how much light each leaf received.

**Worked example 3 (discontinuous).** A student sorted 240 pea seeds harvested from one plot by shape: 182 round and 58 wrinkled (illustrative data).

- Percentage wrinkled: 58 ÷ 240 × 100 = 24.2% (to 3 significant figures).
- Graph: bar chart with two separate bars, because there are only two categories.
- No seed is "slightly wrinkled" in this classification, which is what makes the variation discontinuous.

## 18.1.6 to 18.1.8 Mutation

Learn the three Core statements word for word:

- **Mutation is genetic change.**
- **Mutation is the way in which new alleles are formed.** Without mutation there would be no new alleles; meiosis and fertilisation only reshuffle alleles that already exist.
- **Ionising radiation and some chemicals increase the rate of mutation.** Examples of ionising radiation are X-rays, gamma rays and the radiation given out by radioactive substances. Some chemicals in tobacco smoke are an example of chemicals that increase the mutation rate.

Mutations happen at a low rate anyway. Radiation and chemicals do not create a new kind of process; they make an existing one happen more often. This is why "mutation is caused by radiation" loses the mark, while "radiation increases the rate of mutation" earns it.

## 18.1.9 Gene mutation (Extended only)

A **gene mutation** is a **random change in the base sequence of DNA**. Two words carry the marks:

- **random**: it is not directed towards anything useful; an organism cannot "mutate to survive";
- **base sequence**: the order of the bases A, T, C and G in the DNA of a gene.

Linking to topic 17: the base sequence of a gene codes for the sequence of amino acids in a protein. A changed base sequence can change the amino acid sequence, and so the shape and function of the protein, such as an enzyme's active site. See the [inheritance study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-inheritance/) for the protein-synthesis background.

## 18.1.10 Sources of genetic variation (Extended only)

| Source | How it produces variation |
|---|---|
| Mutation | forms new alleles |
| Meiosis | produces gametes that are genetically different from each other |
| Random mating | any individual may mate with any other, so different combinations of parents occur |
| Random fertilisation | any male gamete can fuse with any female gamete, so each zygote gets a new combination of alleles |

Only mutation creates new alleles. The other three produce **new combinations** of existing alleles. In organisms that reproduce asexually, such as bacteria, offspring are genetically identical to the parent, so mutation is the main source of genetic variation.

## Common mistakes

- Defining variation as "differences between organisms" without "of the same species".
- Calling height discontinuous because it was recorded in whole centimetres.
- Writing that discontinuous variation is caused by "the environment". It is usually genes only.
- Saying continuous variation is caused by "the environment" alone; it is genes **and** environment.
- Drawing a bar chart with gaps for continuous data, or a histogram for blood groups.
- Writing "radiation causes mutation" instead of "increases the rate of mutation".
- (Extended) Leaving "random" out of the definition of gene mutation, or listing "meiosis" as a way of forming new alleles.

## Exam technique for 18.1 command words

- **State** (type of variation): give the type and, if asked, one reason: "continuous, because there is a range of values between two extremes".
- **Describe** (variation, mutation): use the syllabus wording; one precise sentence often earns the mark.
- **Investigate / describe a method**: name the feature, how it is measured, sample size, how data are grouped and which graph is drawn.
- **Suggest** (causes in data): apply the genes-plus-environment idea to the organism in the question, for example sunlight for a plant or diet for an animal.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is mutation Core or Extended in Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610?**
Both. The Core outcomes state that mutation is genetic change, forms new alleles and has its rate increased by ionising radiation and some chemicals. The definition of gene mutation as a random change in the base sequence of DNA is Extended only.

**Why is continuous variation caused by genes and the environment?**
Alleles set the range an organism can grow within, but conditions such as diet, light or water decide where in that range each individual ends up.
