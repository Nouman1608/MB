## Section B

**7.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Expand and simplify (3 + 2√5)(4 − √5). Give your answer in the form a + b√5, where a and b are integers. **[3]**

**8.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Rationalise the denominator of 15/(4 − √6). Give your answer in its simplest form. **[3]**

**10.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse √50 cm and one of the shorter sides √18 cm.

**(a)** Find the length of the third side. Give your answer in the form k√2. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the area of the triangle. **[2]**

**11.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A rectangle has area (7 + 4√3) cm² and length (2 + √3) cm.

**(a)** Find its width. Give your answer in the form a + b√3. **[3]**

**(b)** Find its perimeter. **[1]**

**12.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Write these numbers in order of size, starting with the smallest. Show how you decide.

2√7     3√3     √29 **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **3√5** [1]

**(b)** **6√3** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* for √108, 108 = 36 × 3. Using 4 × 27 gives 2√27, which is not yet in simplest form.

**2.** √80 = 4√5 and √20 = 2√5 [1]; **2√5** [1] **[2]**

**3.** 2√27 = 6√3 and √75 = 5√3 [1]; **11√3** [1] **[2]**

**4. (a)** 25 − 2 = **23** [1]

**(b)** 3 + 2√3 + 2√3 + 4 [1] = **7 + 4√3** [1] **[3]**

**5.** 14√7/7 [1] = **2√7** [1] **[2]**

**6.** √8 × √18 = √144 [1] = 12 [1]; or 2√2 × 3√2 = 6 × 2 = 12 **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* this is a "show that" question, so the line √144 or 2√2 × 3√2 must be written down. Writing only "= 12" earns nothing.

**7.** 12 − 3√5 + 8√5 − 2 × 5 [1]; 12 − 10 = 2 and −3√5 + 8√5 = 5√5 [1]; **2 + 5√5** [1] **[3]**

**8.** Multiply by (4 + √6)/(4 + √6) [1]; denominator 16 − 6 = 10, giving 15(4 + √6)/10 [1]; = **3(4 + √6)/2**, or **(12 + 3√6)/2** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* 15 and 10 share a factor of 5, so the answer must be simplified to a denominator of 2.

**9.** Multiply by (3 + √2)/(3 + √2) [1]; numerator (3 + √2)² = 9 + 6√2 + 2 = 11 + 6√2 [1]; denominator 9 − 2 = 7, so **(11 + 6√2)/7** [1] **[3]**

**10. (a)** x² = 50 − 18 [1] = 32 [1]; x = √32 = **4√2 cm** [1]

**(b)** ½ × √18 × √32 = ½ × √576 [1] = ½ × 24 = **12 cm²** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* in (a), subtract the squares because √50 is the hypotenuse. Adding gives √68, which loses all three marks.

**11. (a)** width = (7 + 4√3)/(2 + √3); multiply top and bottom by (2 − √3) [1]; numerator 14 − 7√3 + 8√3 − 12 = 2 + √3, denominator 4 − 3 = 1 [1]; width = **2 + √3 cm** [1]

**(b)** 2(2 + √3) + 2(2 + √3) = **8 + 4√3 cm** [1] **[4]**

*Check:* (2 + √3)² = 4 + 4√3 + 3 = 7 + 4√3, so the rectangle is a square.

**12.** (2√7)² = 28, (3√3)² = 27 and (√29)² = 29 [1]; order **3√3, 2√7, √29** [1] **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not using the largest square factor, as in Question 1(b).
- Missing the middle term when squaring a bracket, as in Questions 4(b) and 9.
- Multiplying √5 × √5 as 25 instead of 5 in Question 7.
- Rationalising without simplifying the final fraction, as in Question 8.
- Adding the squares instead of subtracting when the hypotenuse is given.
- Ordering surds by the number outside the root instead of by their squares.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are surds on the Core paper?**
No. Subtopic 1.18 Surds is Extended only in Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580.

**When must I rationalise the denominator?**
When the question asks you to, or asks for an answer in a form with a whole-number denominator, as in Questions 8 and 9.

**Can a rationalised answer have a negative denominator?**
Not in its final form. If the denominator works out negative, such as −1, divide every term in the numerator by it so that the answer has a positive denominator or none at all.

**How do I check a surd answer without a calculator in the exam?**
Square it or multiply it back. For example, multiplying the width in Question 11 by the length should give the area.
