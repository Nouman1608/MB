All businesses are invented. Money is in dollars.

## 10.3.1 Why appraise?

- Large sums at stake.
- Long time scale, so uncertain forecasts.
- Hard to reverse.
- Limited funds: choosing one project has an opportunity cost.
- Gives a consistent basis to compare projects and justify decisions to managers and lenders.

## 10.3.2 Payback

**Question answered:** how quickly is the cost recovered?

**Method:**

1. Cumulative net cash flow, year by year.
2. Find the year in which it passes the initial cost.
3. Fraction of that year = amount still needed ÷ that year's inflow.
4. Months = fraction × 12.

Example: cost 90,000; inflows 25,000, 35,000, 40,000. Cumulative 25,000, 60,000; still needed 30,000; 30,000 ÷ 40,000 = 0.75 → **2 years 9 months**.

## 10.3.2 ARR

**Question answered:** what average annual return is earned on the money tied up?

**Syllabus formula:** ARR = (average profit / average investment) × 100.

**Standard steps:**

- Total profit = total inflows (including residual value) − initial cost.
- Average profit = total profit ÷ years.
- Average investment = (initial cost + residual value) ÷ 2.

Example: cost 60,000; residual 10,000; inflows total 80,000 over 4 years (plus the 10,000). Total profit = 90,000 − 60,000 = 30,000; average 7,500; average investment 35,000; ARR = **21.4%**.

## 10.3.3 NPV

**Question answered:** how much value is added, in today's money?

- Present value = cash flow × discount factor.
- Residual value: discount with the final year's factor.
- Initial cost: not discounted (paid now).
- NPV = total present value − initial cost.
- Positive NPV: the project earns more than the discount rate.

**Discount rate:** often the cost of borrowing, raised for risk. Higher rate → later cash worth less → NPV falls, most for projects with late cash.

## Interpreting results

| Result | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Payback within the firm's maximum | Acceptable on liquidity and risk |
| ARR above criterion rate | Acceptable on average return |
| Positive NPV | Adds value at that discount rate |
| Methods disagree | They measure different things: speed, average return, value after timing |
| NPV close to zero | Small forecast errors could reverse the decision |

**Ranking conflicts.** A project with early cash flows wins on payback and gains at higher discount rates; a project with steady or late flows and a residual value may win on ARR and at low discount rates.

## 10.3.4 Qualitative factors

- Fit with objectives and strategy.
- Effect on employees: training, redundancies, morale.
- Customers and image, including environmental impact.
- Risk: competitors' reactions, technology changes, economic conditions.
- Reliability of forecasts.
- How the project will be financed, and its effect on gearing and liquidity.
- Disruption while the project is introduced.

## Comparison of methods

| | Payback | ARR | NPV |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uses all cash flows | No | Yes | Yes |
| Allows for timing | Partly (only to payback) | No | Yes |
| Easy to calculate and explain | Yes | Yes | Less so |
| Output | Time | % | $ |
| Main limitation | Ignores cash after payback | Ignores timing | Depends on chosen discount rate |

All three rely on forecasts that become less reliable further ahead.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Investment appraisal | Assessing whether a capital project is worth its cost |
| Payback period | Time for net cash inflows to repay the initial cost |
| ARR | Average profit as a percentage of average investment |
| Criterion rate | Minimum ARR a business will accept |
| Time value of money | Money now is worth more than the same sum later |
| Discount factor | Multiplier converting a future cash flow to present value |
| NPV | Total present value of inflows minus the initial cost |
| Residual value | Expected value of the asset at the end of the project |

## Exam traps

- Discounting the initial cost.
- Leaving out residual value.
- Using initial cost instead of average investment for ARR.
- Deducting depreciation again in ARR after subtracting the cost.
- Writing payback as "3.25 years" when months are asked for.
- Treating a positive NPV as automatically "yes".

## Self-test

1. Give two reasons why investment appraisal is needed.
2. Cost 50,000; inflows 20,000, 20,000, 25,000. Calculate payback in years and months.
3. Write the ARR formula as given in the syllabus.
4. Cost 100,000; residual value 20,000. Calculate average investment.
5. Total inflows over 5 years are 160,000 (including residual value); cost 100,000. Calculate average profit.
6. Using questions 4 and 5, calculate ARR.
7. A Year 2 inflow of 30,000 is discounted at a factor of 0.857. Calculate its present value.
8. Why is the initial cost not discounted?
9. What does a negative NPV show?
10. Why can a higher discount rate change which project ranks first?
11. Give one limitation of payback.
12. Give two qualitative factors in an investment decision.

### Answers

1. Large sums and hard to reverse; long time scale with uncertain forecasts; limited funds mean an opportunity cost (any two).
2. Cumulative 20,000, 40,000; need 10,000 of 25,000 = 0.4 → **2 years 5 months** (0.4 × 12 = 4.8 months, rounded up).
3. ARR = (average profit / average investment) × 100.
4. (100,000 + 20,000) ÷ 2 = **60,000**.
5. (160,000 − 100,000) ÷ 5 = **12,000**.
6. 12,000 ÷ 60,000 × 100 = **20%**.
7. 30,000 × 0.857 = **25,710**.
8. It is paid now, in Year 0, so it is already in present-value terms.
9. The project earns less than the discount rate, so on this measure it reduces value.
10. It reduces later cash flows more than early ones, so projects with late cash lose more value.
11. It ignores cash flows after the payback point (or ignores the time value of money).
12. Any two: fit with objectives, staff morale, image, environmental impact, risk, reliability of forecasts.
