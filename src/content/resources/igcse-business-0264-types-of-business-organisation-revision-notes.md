## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sole trader | A business owned (and usually run) by one person |
| Partnership | A firm with two or more owners who agree to share profit, work and risk |
| Private limited company (Ltd) | A company owned by shareholders; the public cannot buy its shares directly |
| Public limited company (plc) | A company owned by shareholders; it may offer its shares to the general public |
| Shareholder | An owner of a company, holding shares |
| Dividend | Part of a company's profit paid to shareholders |
| Unlimited liability | Owners can be made to pay business debts from personal assets |
| Limited liability | Owners can lose only the money they invested |
| Franchise | A right to use another business's brand, products and methods in return for payment |
| Franchisor | The business that grants the franchise |
| Franchisee | The business or person that buys the franchise |
| Royalty | A regular payment by the franchisee, often a percentage of sales |
| Joint venture | Two or more businesses cooperating on a project or in a market, sharing costs, risks and rewards |
| Social enterprise | A business that trades mainly to achieve social or environmental aims |

## The four main types at a glance

| | Sole trader | Partnership | Private limited | Public limited |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Owners | 1 | 2 or more | Shareholders | Shareholders |
| Liability | Unlimited | Unlimited (usual position) | Limited | Limited |
| Share sale to the public | No | No | No | Yes |
| Capital | Smallest | Small to medium | Medium | Largest |
| Control | Owner alone | Shared | Founders or shareholders | Spread widely |
| Set-up | Easiest | Easy | More formal | Most formal |

## Advantages and disadvantages: quick recall

**Sole trader**
- For: simple, cheap, full control, keeps all profit.
- Against: unlimited liability, little capital, owner carries all work and risk.

**Partnership**
- For: shared capital, skills and workload; more than one decision-maker.
- Against: unlimited liability, shared profit, possible disputes.

**Private limited company**
- For: limited liability, more capital through share sales, founders choose who joins.
- Against: more paperwork and cost, cannot sell shares to the public, control can dilute.

**Public limited company**
- For: large capital, limited liability, easy to trade shares, easier borrowing.
- Against: high costs, strict regulation, accounts widely available, risk of takeover, pressure for short-term results.

## Franchise: both sides

| | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Franchisor | Expands with the franchisee's money; motivated local owner; fee and royalty income | Shares profit; brand harmed by weak franchisees; less control than owned outlets |
| Franchisee | Known brand; support and training; lower risk than starting alone | Pays fee and royalty; must follow rules; limited freedom; shares brand reputation |

## Joint venture

- For: shared costs and risk; combined skills and knowledge; faster entry to a new market.
- Against: shared profit; possible disagreement; slower decisions; unclear responsibility.

## Social enterprise

- Sells goods or services like any business, but social or environmental aims come first.
- Profit is mainly reinvested in the aims.
- For: goodwill, committed staff, a clear purpose.
- Against: less money for growth, harder to attract investors who want returns.

## Method boxes

**Method: recommend and justify a type (case study)**
1. Underline the facts: capital needed, risk, control wanted, growth plans, number of owners.
2. Match facts to features in the table above.
3. State your recommendation in one sentence.
4. Give two or three reasons, each linked to a fact from the case.
5. Explain why one other type is weaker for this business.
6. End with a short judgement that mentions any drawback of your choice.

**Method: franchise payments**
1. Royalty = percentage x sales.
2. Franchisee profit after royalty = profit before royalty - royalty.
3. If asked about year one, also take off the initial fee.

**Method: profit shares**
1. Add the ratio parts to find the total.
2. Share = own part divided by total, multiplied by profit.
3. Check the shares add back to total profit.

## Small worked reminders

Partnership profit of 45,000 shared 2:1. Total parts = 3. One partner gets 2/3 x 45,000 = 30,000 and the other gets 15,000.

A plc pays a dividend of 0.25 per share. An investor holding 8,000 shares receives 8,000 x 0.25 = 2,000. If the company has 500,000 shares, the investor owns 8,000/500,000 = 1.6%.

A franchisee has sales of 150,000 and pays an 8% royalty: 0.08 x 150,000 = 12,000 a year, or 60,000 over five years.

A joint venture costing 500,000 is shared 3:2. The first partner pays 3/5 x 500,000 = 300,000 and the second pays 200,000.

A sole trader owes 20,000 and the business assets raise 14,000. The shortfall is 6,000. With unlimited liability, the owner pays it. With limited liability, the creditors lose it.

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Key difference |
|---|---|
| Private v public limited company | Public can offer shares to the general public; private cannot |
| Sole trader v private limited company | Sole trader has unlimited liability and one owner; Ltd has limited liability and shareholders |
| Franchise v joint venture | Franchise is brand-use for a fee; joint venture is two businesses sharing a project and its results |
| Franchisor v franchisee | Franchisor owns the brand; franchisee runs the outlet |
| Social enterprise v ordinary business | Social aims come first, with profit mainly reinvested |
| Limited liability v no risk | Owners can still lose all they invested |

## Applying the content

Cambridge assesses application, analysis and evaluation as well as knowledge, so a list of textbook points is rarely enough. Name the business, use its figures, and say what a feature means for that business. A sentence such as "limited liability protects the owner's house" is knowledge. Adding "so Ms Okoro can borrow to open a second shop without risking her home" is application.

## Quick self-test

1. Define the term limited liability.
2. State two types of business with unlimited liability.
3. What is the main difference between a private and a public limited company?
4. A partnership makes a profit of 60,000 shared 3:1. How much does the larger partner get?
5. A company with 200,000 shares pays out 30,000 as dividends. What is the dividend per share?
6. A franchisee pays a royalty of 5% on sales of 180,000. Calculate the royalty.
7. Give one advantage of a franchise to the franchisor.
8. Give one disadvantage of a franchise to the franchisee.
9. State one reason two businesses might form a joint venture.
10. What is a social enterprise?

### Answers

1. Owners can lose only the money they invested in the business; their personal assets cannot be claimed for business debts.
2. Sole trader and partnership.
3. A plc can sell shares to the general public; a private limited company cannot.
4. 3/4 x 60,000 = 45,000. The other partner gets 15,000.
5. 30,000/200,000 = 0.15 per share.
6. 0.05 x 180,000 = 9,000.
7. It can expand using the franchisee's capital, or it receives fees and royalties.
8. It must pay an initial fee and royalties, or it must follow the franchisor's rules.
9. To share costs and risks, or to gain skills or access to a market.
10. A business that trades mainly to achieve social or environmental aims, reinvesting most profit in them.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using general points that could apply to any business, with no link to the case.
- Stating an advantage or disadvantage without explaining the reason or effect.
- Mixing up limited liability and limited risk.
- Saying private companies "sell shares privately to anyone" rather than that they cannot offer them to the public.
- Treating the franchisee as part of the franchisor's business.
- Forgetting to calculate the royalty before the franchisee's profit.
- Giving a recommendation with no reasons or no comparison with another type.
- Making a one-sided judgement that ignores the drawbacks of the type chosen.
- Writing "profit" when the question asks for revenue or dividends.
