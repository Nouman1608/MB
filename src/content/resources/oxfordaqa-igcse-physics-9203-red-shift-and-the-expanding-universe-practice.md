The section is marked P, so it is for the full Physics award only. Galaxy data are illustrative supplied values. Use wave speed = frequency × wavelength where needed.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** A wave source moves away from an observer. State what happens to the observed wavelength and the observed frequency. **[2]**

**2.** Give two different kinds of wave source that can show the Doppler effect. **[1]**

**3.** Which of these is red shift?

A: Light from a nearby star appears red because the star is cool.

B: The observed wavelength of light from a distant galaxy is longer than the wavelength measured in a laboratory.

C: The speed of light from a distant galaxy is lower than the speed of light on Earth.

D: The observed frequency of light from a distant galaxy is higher than expected. **[1]**

**4.** State what cosmic microwave background radiation (CMBR) is, and where it came from. **[2]**

**5.** Write the age of the universe that scientists believe in standard form, in years. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why a source moving towards an observer produces a shorter observed wavelength. **[3]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** A train horn emits sound of frequency 512 Hz. The speed of sound in air is 340 m/s. A listener on a platform hears a frequency of 540 Hz.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength of the sound emitted by the horn. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the wavelength the listener receives. **[1]**

**(c)** State whether the train is moving towards or away from the listener, and give a reason. **[1]**

**(d)** Predict how the frequency the listener hears changes once the train has passed. **[1]**

**8.** A line in the spectrum of calcium has a laboratory wavelength of 393.4 nm. The table shows the wavelength of the same line in light from four galaxies.

| Galaxy | Distance (million light-years) | Observed wavelength (nm) |
|---|---|---|
| P | 80 | 395.7 |
| Q | 160 | 397.9 |
| R | 240 | 400.2 |
| S | 400 | 404.7 |

**(a)** Calculate the increase in wavelength for galaxies P and S. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the pattern shown by the data. **[1]**

**(c)** State two conclusions about the motion of the galaxies. **[2]**

**9.** Light from one nearby galaxy shows the calcium line at 392.7 nm, shorter than the laboratory value of 393.4 nm.

**(a)** State what this tells you about the motion of that galaxy relative to us. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why this does not contradict the idea that the universe is expanding. **[1]**

**10.** A student says: "All the distant galaxies are moving away from us, so the Earth must be at the centre of the universe." Explain why this conclusion is not justified. **[3]**

**11.** Light from galaxy X shows a red shift of 4.0 nm and light from galaxy Y shows a red shift of 12.0 nm for the same spectral line.

**(a)** State which galaxy is further away. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain your answer. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why scientists accept the Big Bang theory. Use two pieces of evidence in your answer. **[6]**

**13.** Explain why red shift on its own supports the Big Bang model but does not prove it, and why CMBR strengthens the case. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Observed wavelength increases [1]; observed frequency decreases [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Both changes are needed, each with the correct direction.

**2.** Any two of light, sound and microwaves (both needed for the mark) [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* These are the three examples the specification names.

**3.** **B** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Red shift is about an increase in wavelength. The speed of light does not change (C), and a higher frequency (D) would be the opposite effect.

**4.** Electromagnetic radiation, in the microwave region, that fills the universe [1]. It comes from radiation that was present shortly after the beginning of the universe [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Left over from the Big Bang" is acceptable for the second mark; "from stars" is not.

**5.** **1.4 × 10¹⁰ years** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* 14 thousand million is 14 × 10⁹, which is 1.4 × 10¹⁰.

**6.** Between emitting one crest and the next, the source moves closer to the observer [1]. Each new crest therefore starts nearer to the observer than the last [1], so the crests arrive closer together, which is a shorter wavelength [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain using the spacing of crests. Restating "it moves towards you so the wavelength is shorter" earns nothing.

**7. (a)** 340 ÷ 512 = **0.664 m** [1].

**(b)** 340 ÷ 540 = **0.630 m** [1].

**(c)** Towards, because the frequency heard is higher than emitted (the wavelength is shorter) [1].

**(d)** It drops to a frequency lower than 512 Hz [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* In (d), the change is from higher than 512 Hz to lower than 512 Hz, not just "back to 512 Hz".

**8. (a)** P: 395.7 − 393.4 = **2.3 nm** [1]. S: 404.7 − 393.4 = **11.3 nm** [1].

**(b)** The further away the galaxy, the greater the increase in wavelength (red shift) [1].

**(c)** All four galaxies are moving away from us [1]; the more distant galaxies are moving away faster [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) describes the data; part (c) interprets it. Keep them separate.

**9. (a)** It is moving towards us [1].

**(b)** The specification refers to most distant galaxies; a nearby galaxy can move towards us while the general pattern for distant galaxies is red shift increasing with distance [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The word "most" in the specification matters. One nearby galaxy does not overturn the pattern seen for distant ones.

**10.** The red shift pattern suggests that space itself is expanding [1]. If space is expanding, every galaxy moves away from every other galaxy [1], so an observer in any galaxy would see the same pattern, and it does not show that Earth is at the centre [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The key idea is that space expands everywhere, not that galaxies fly away from one point.

**11. (a)** Galaxy **Y** [1].

**(b)** Y has the larger red shift, so it is moving away faster [1]; more distant galaxies move away faster, so Y is further away [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use both links: shift to speed, and speed to distance.

**12.** Light from most distant galaxies is red-shifted [1], showing that they are moving away [1]. The further away the galaxy, the larger the red shift and the faster it moves [1], which suggests space is expanding, so the universe began from a very small initial point [1]. CMBR fills the universe and comes from radiation present shortly after the beginning [1]. The Big Bang theory is currently the only theory that can explain CMBR [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Two pieces of evidence are required. An answer using only red shift is capped at four marks here.

**13.** Red shift shows that galaxies are moving apart and is consistent with an expanding universe that started small, so it supports the model [1]. It is evidence for the expansion, but on its own it does not rule out other explanations of how the universe began [1]. CMBR strengthens the case because the Big Bang theory is currently the only theory that can explain its existence [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Supports" and "proves" are different claims. Scientists use evidence to support a model while it explains the observations better than the alternatives.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Getting the direction of the Doppler change wrong for a receding source.
- Saying the speed of light changes.
- Describing red-shifted galaxies as looking red.
- Confusing describing a pattern with drawing a conclusion from it.
- Using only one piece of evidence when two are asked for.
- Writing that red shift "proves" the Big Bang.
- Giving the age as 14 million years.
