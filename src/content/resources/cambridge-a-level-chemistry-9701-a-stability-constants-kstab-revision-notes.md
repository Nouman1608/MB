subtopic 28.5 Stability constants, Kstab.

All numerical values below are **illustrative**. Use the values given in your question.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Kstab** | the equilibrium constant for the formation of the complex ion in a solvent from its constituent ions or molecules |
| **lg Kstab** | log₁₀ Kstab; used because Kstab values are very large |
| **Stable complex** | one with a large Kstab: little of the metal remains as the aqua ion at equilibrium |
| **Ligand exchange** | replacement of one ligand by another; favours the complex with the larger Kstab |
| **Polydentate ligand** | forms several dative bonds per ligand, e.g. EDTA⁴⁻ (six) |

## Writing a Kstab expression: four rules

1. Write the **formation** equation (complex on the right).
2. Products over reactants, each concentration raised to its coefficient.
3. **Leave out [H₂O]** (the solvent).
4. **Keep the aqua ion** (it is a dissolved species), if it is written in the equation.

## Expressions and units at a glance

| Equation (aq unless stated) | Kstab | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Ag⁺ + 2NH₃ ⇌ [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ | [[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺] / ([Ag⁺][NH₃]²) | mol⁻² dm⁶ |
| [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CuCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O(l) | [[CuCl₄]²⁻] / ([[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺][Cl⁻]⁴) | mol⁻⁴ dm¹² |
| [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 6NH₃ ⇌ [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺ + 6H₂O(l) | [[Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺] / ([[Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺][NH₃]⁶) | mol⁻⁶ dm¹⁸ |
| [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 3en ⇌ [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ + 6H₂O(l) | [[Ni(en)₃]²⁺] / ([[Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺][en]³) | mol⁻³ dm⁹ |
| [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + EDTA⁴⁻ ⇌ [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ + 6H₂O(l) | [[Cu(EDTA)]²⁻] / ([[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺][EDTA⁴⁻]) | mol⁻¹ dm³ |

**Units shortcut:** n ligand particles → mol⁻ⁿ dm³ⁿ. Count ligand **particles**, not donor atoms.

## lg Kstab

    Kstab = 10^(lg Kstab)          lg Kstab = 13.1  →  Kstab ≈ 1.3 × 10¹³
    larger lg Kstab  →  larger Kstab  →  more stable complex

## Three calculation patterns

| Asked for | Rearranged form |
|---|---|
| Kstab | Kstab = [complex] / ([aqua ion][L]ⁿ) |
| [aqua ion] | [aqua ion] = [complex] / (Kstab × [L]ⁿ) |
| [complex] | [complex] = Kstab × [aqua ion] × [L]ⁿ |
| ratio [complex] : [aqua ion] | ratio = Kstab × [L]ⁿ |

**Large-excess-ligand method:**

1. Assume nearly all the metal is complexed (Kstab is very large).
2. Subtract the ligand used: n × [metal] from the initial ligand concentration.
3. Rearrange and substitute to find the tiny [aqua ion].
4. Check that it is negligible compared with the complex concentration.

Example: 0.020 mol dm⁻³ Ni²⁺ with 2.00 mol dm⁻³ NH₃ initially. NH₃ used = 6 × 0.020 = 0.12, so
[NH₃] = 1.88 mol dm⁻³ at equilibrium.

## Ligand exchange in terms of Kstab

- Ligands compete for the metal ion; the **larger Kstab** complex is **more stable** and is favoured.
- Illustrative order for copper(II): lg Kstab [CuCl₄]²⁻ 5.6 < [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ 13.1 < [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ 18.8.
- So EDTA⁴⁻ displaces NH₃ from the ammine complex, and NH₃ displaces H₂O from the aqua ion.
- A complex with a smaller Kstab can still form if its ligand is present at **high concentration**
  (e.g. concentrated HCl gives [CuCl₄]²⁻); dilution reverses this.
- For an exchange between two complexes of the same metal ion, K(exchange) = Kstab(new) ÷ Kstab(old).

**Why polydentate ligands give large Kstab:** one EDTA⁴⁻ replaces six H₂O, so the number of particles
increases (2 → 7) and entropy increases, favouring formation.

## Exam traps

- **[H₂O] is never in the expression**; the aqua ion is.
- **Power = number of ligand particles** in the balanced equation (3 for en in [Ni(en)₃]²⁺, 1 for EDTA⁴⁻).
- **Units change with n**: always derive them.
- **Equilibrium, not initial, ligand concentration** in excess-ligand questions.
- **Explain with stability**: "larger Kstab, so a more stable complex" earns the mark; "it is stronger" does not.
- **lg values**: subtract lg values to divide Kstab values; do not divide the lg values.

## Self-test

1. Define Kstab.
2. Why is [H₂O] not included in a Kstab expression?
3. Write the Kstab expression for Fe³⁺(aq) + SCN⁻(aq) ⇌ [Fe(SCN)]²⁺(aq), with units.
4. Give the units of Kstab for [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺ formed from [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺.
5. lg Kstab for a complex is 8.6. Calculate Kstab.
6. Kstab = 5.0 × 10⁷ mol⁻² dm⁶ for [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺. At equilibrium [NH₃] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³ and
   [[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺] = 0.050 mol dm⁻³. Calculate [Ag⁺].
7. Using the copper data above, predict whether Cl⁻ displaces NH₃ from [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ at moderate
   concentration.
8. Calculate the ratio [[Cu(EDTA)]²⁻] : [[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺] when [EDTA⁴⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ and
   Kstab = 6.3 × 10¹⁸ mol⁻¹ dm³.
9. What does a large Kstab tell you about a complex?
10. Calculate K for the exchange of NH₃ by EDTA⁴⁻ around Cu²⁺ using lg Kstab values 13.1 and 18.8.
11. Explain why [CuCl₄]²⁻ forms in concentrated HCl even though its Kstab is relatively small.

**Answers:**

1. The equilibrium constant for the formation of a complex ion in a solvent from its constituent ions or
   molecules.
2. Water is the solvent; its concentration is effectively constant, so it is not included.
3. Kstab = [[Fe(SCN)]²⁺] / ([Fe³⁺][SCN⁻]); mol⁻¹ dm³.
4. mol⁻⁶ dm¹⁸.
5. 10⁸·⁶ = 4.0 × 10⁸.
6. [Ag⁺] = 0.050 / (5.0 × 10⁷ × 0.10²) = 0.050 / (5.0 × 10⁵) = 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ mol dm⁻³.
7. No: the ammine complex has the larger Kstab, so it is the more stable and is not replaced by Cl⁻ at
   moderate concentration.
8. Ratio = Kstab × [EDTA⁴⁻] = 6.3 × 10¹⁸ × 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ = 6.3 × 10¹⁴.
9. The equilibrium lies far to the right: the complex is stable, and very little metal remains as the aqua ion.
10. K = 10¹⁸·⁸ ÷ 10¹³·¹ = 10⁵·⁷ ≈ 5.0 × 10⁵.
11. The very high concentration of Cl⁻ shifts the position of equilibrium to the right, so the complex forms
    even though Kstab is small; diluting with water shifts it back.
