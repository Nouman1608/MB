## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by leadership. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between Theory X and Theory Y. **[3]**

**3.** Brindle Print has 32 employees. During the year 8 of them leave. Calculate its staff turnover as a percentage. **[2]**

**4.** Which **one** of the following describes a manager with a score of 9, 1 on the managerial grid?

A: High concern for people and low concern for production.

B: High concern for production and low concern for people.

C: High concern for both.

D: Low concern for both. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a paternalistic style might be suitable for a family-owned business. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**6.** Tolland Bakery has 16 staff. Last year, under an autocratic manager, 6 staff left. After a new manager adopted a democratic style, 2 staff left this year. Each leaver costs the bakery £2,500 to replace.

**(a)** Calculate the change in staff turnover. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the saving in replacement costs. **[2]**

**(c)** Evaluate whether the democratic style is the cause of the improvement. **[6]**

**7.** Explain Adair's Three Circles and how a leader could use them. **[5]**

**8.** Analyse **two** reasons why a contingency approach may be more useful to a business than trait theory. **[6]**

**9.** Marrick Engineering makes aircraft parts and employs 60 highly skilled engineers. A safety audit is due in three weeks and the plant is behind on its inspection records. The managing director normally uses a laissez-faire style. Recommend and justify the leadership method she should use over the next three weeks. **[8]**

**10.** Evaluate the importance of leadership to a supermarket chain and its stakeholders. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Leadership is influencing other people so that they work willingly towards a goal [1], for example by setting direction, inspiring effort and shaping the culture of a team [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Theory X is the belief that most people dislike work, avoid responsibility and need control [1]. Theory Y is the belief that people enjoy work, accept responsibility and are self-motivated [1]. The difference is what the manager assumes, which shapes the style: close control under X, delegation and trust under Y [1]. **[3]**

**3.** 8 ÷ 32 [1] × 100 = **25%** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** A family-owned business often values loyalty and long service [1], and a paternalistic leader decides but explains and acts in what they see as the staff's interests, which builds loyalty and a sense of belonging [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Last year: 6 ÷ 16 × 100 = 37.5% [1]. This year: 2 ÷ 16 × 100 = 12.5% [1]. Change: a fall of **25 percentage points** [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Four fewer leavers (6 − 2) [1] × £2,500 = **£10,000** [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** For: turnover fell sharply after the style changed, and the only change described is the manager's style [1]. Staff may feel more valued when consulted, so they are less likely to leave [1].
Against: it is only one year of data and a small team, so one or two leavers change the percentage a lot [1]. Other causes could include a local pay rise, a change in the job market or the original manager being unpopular for personal reasons [1].
Judgement: the democratic style is probably a major cause, because the timing matches and the effect is large [1], but the bakery should check exit interviews and compare with other bakeries before it treats this as proof [1]. **[6]**

**7.** Adair's model says that effective leaders balance three overlapping needs: the task, the team and the individual [1] [1]. The task is what must be achieved; the team is keeping the group working together; the individual is each person's needs and development [1]. A leader could use the circles as a checklist: set a clear objective (task), hold regular team meetings (team) and agree a development plan with each person (individual) [1]. If one circle is neglected, the others suffer; for example, focusing only on the task may lead to poor morale and staff leaving [1]. **[5]**

**8.** Trait theory says leaders are born with fixed qualities, so a business using it would select leaders and then expect them to succeed in every situation [1]. This is a weakness because the same person may succeed in a crisis and fail with a team of experts [1].
Contingency theory says the right style depends on the situation, the task and the people [1], so the business can train leaders to adapt, for example by being autocratic in an emergency and democratic when planning [1].
It is more useful because it can be applied to existing managers through training, so the business does not need to replace them [1], and because it explains why one leader's results vary between teams [1]. **[6]**

**9.** Recommendation: she should move towards an autocratic or boss-centred style for the three weeks [1], because there is a fixed deadline and the records are behind, so quick, clear decisions are needed [1].
Justification from the case: the audit relates to safety, where errors are costly [1]. A laissez-faire style leaves decisions to staff and risks inconsistent records [1].
Counter-argument: the engineers are highly skilled and may resent close control, so it could damage motivation and some might leave [1].
Reducing the risk: she should explain why the style is changing and for how long, and consult the engineers on how to complete the records, keeping control of the deadline (a point towards the middle of the continuum) [1].
Judgement: a short, explained period of firmer leadership is justified, then a return to a democratic style once the audit is passed [1]. This would change if the engineers already manage the records well [1]. **[8]**

**10.** Importance for the business: leadership affects staff morale and retention, which matter in an industry with high turnover [1]; it influences customer service and so sales [1]; it sets the culture and standards in each store [1].
Importance for stakeholders: employees benefit from fair, clear management [1]; customers see the effect in service and availability [1]; shareholders gain if good leadership raises profit [1]; suppliers gain from reliable orders [1].
Evaluation: leadership matters less where procedures are tightly fixed, as in till operation, so its importance is greatest for store managers handling change and staff problems [1]. Judgement: it is highly important, because the same system can produce very different results under different store managers, and a poor leader can cost a chain many leavers and lost customers [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a theory without applying it to the case.
- Calling a style "best" without saying when.
- Using percentages without stating the base figure.
- Forgetting that evaluation needs a judgement.
- Mixing up the managerial grid axes.
