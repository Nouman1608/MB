## Equations to know

| Equation | Use it when |
|---|---|
| pV = nRT | you know or want the number of moles |
| pV = NkT | you know or want the number of molecules |
| p₁V₁/T₁ = p₂V₂/T₂ | the mass of gas is fixed |
| n = mass/M; N = nN_A; m = M/N_A | converting between mass, moles and molecules |
| k = R/N_A | linking the two forms of the equation |
| ρ = pM/RT | finding the density of a gas |
| Work done = pΔV | the pressure is constant |
| Work done = area under p–V graph | the pressure changes |

Constants: R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹; k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹; N_A = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

## Condensed recall

- **Boyle's law:** for a fixed mass at constant temperature, p ∝ 1/V.
- **Charles's law:** for a fixed mass at constant pressure, V ∝ T (kelvin).
- **Pressure law:** for a fixed mass at constant volume, p ∝ T (kelvin).
- **Mass:** at fixed V and T, p ∝ n. Pumping gas into a rigid container raises its pressure.
- The gas laws are **experimental** (empirical) relationships.
- **Absolute zero** (0 K, about −273 °C) is the temperature at which an ideal gas would exert zero pressure. Gas-law lines extrapolate to it for every gas.
- T / K = θ / °C + 273. A temperature *difference* is the same in K and °C.
- Isotherms at higher temperature lie further from the origin on a p–V graph.
- An ideal gas obeys pV = nRT exactly; real gases come close at low pressure and high temperature.
- N/V = p/kT is the same for all ideal gases at the same p and T.
- Required practical 9: change volume slowly and wait so temperature stays constant (Boyle); heat in steps and wait for equilibrium (Charles); plot p against 1/V and V against θ.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ideal gas | A gas that obeys pV = nRT at all pressures and temperatures |
| Absolute zero | The lowest possible temperature, 0 K, where an ideal gas would have zero pressure |
| Mole | Amount of substance containing N_A particles |
| Avogadro constant, N_A | Number of particles in one mole, 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ |
| Molar gas constant, R | Constant in pV = nRT, 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ |
| Boltzmann constant, k | R/N_A, the gas constant per molecule, 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹ |
| Molar mass, M | Mass of one mole of a substance, in kg mol⁻¹ |
| Molecular mass, m | Mass of one molecule, M/N_A |
| Isotherm | Curve on a p–V graph for a fixed mass of gas at one temperature |

## Exam traps

- Celsius temperature in pV = nRT or in a ratio.
- p₁V₁/T₁ = p₂V₂/T₂ used for a leaking or refilled container. Work in moles instead.
- Molar mass used where the mass of one molecule is needed, or the reverse.
- pΔV used along a sloping p–V line; take the area under the line.
- Stating a gas law without "fixed mass" and the constant variable.
- Saying the gas laws were derived from kinetic theory. They came from experiment.
- Forgetting the volume of connecting tubing in Boyle's law data.

## Quick self-test

1. State Charles's law.
2. Convert 27 °C to kelvin and 350 K to degrees Celsius.
3. Find the number of moles in 0.10 m³ of gas at 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 300 K.
4. How many molecules are in 1.0 m³ of gas at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 290 K?
5. Find the mass of one helium atom (M = 0.0040 kg mol⁻¹).
6. A fixed mass of gas at constant volume is heated from 300 K to 450 K. By what factor does its pressure change?
7. A gas expands by 0.50 dm³ at a constant 1.2 × 10⁵ Pa. Find the work done by the gas.
8. A graph of p against 1/V is a straight line through the origin. Which law does this show, and under what conditions?
9. Explain why pumping air into a rigid tyre at constant temperature raises the pressure.
10. Find the density of air (M = 0.029 kg mol⁻¹) at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 290 K.
11. A fixed mass of gas at constant pressure is heated from 20 °C to 40 °C. By what factor does its volume change?
12. Two isotherms are drawn for the same sample of gas. How can you tell which is at the higher temperature?

### Answers

1. For a fixed mass of gas at constant pressure, volume is proportional to absolute temperature.
2. 300 K; 77 °C.
3. n = pV/RT = (2.0 × 10⁵ × 0.10) / (8.31 × 300) = 8.0 mol.
4. N = pV/kT = 1.0 × 10⁵ / (1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 290) = 2.5 × 10²⁵.
5. m = 0.0040 / 6.02 × 10²³ = 6.6 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.
6. p ∝ T, so it rises by a factor of 450/300 = 1.5.
7. W = pΔV = 1.2 × 10⁵ × 0.50 × 10⁻³ = 60 J.
8. Boyle's law, for a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature.
9. The volume and temperature are fixed, so p ∝ n; adding air increases n and therefore p.
10. ρ = pM/RT = (1.0 × 10⁵ × 0.029) / (8.31 × 290) = 1.2 kg m⁻³.
11. V ∝ T in kelvin, so the factor is 313/293 = 1.07, not 2.
12. The isotherm further from the origin has the larger pV and so the higher temperature.
