## Section A

**1.** Events A and B are mutually exclusive, with P(A) = 0.42 and P(B) = 0.33. Find P(A ∪ B) and P(A′ ∩ B′). **[2]**

**2.** Events A and B are independent, with P(A) = 0.25 and P(B) = 0.8. Find P(A ∩ B), P(A ∪ B) and P(A′ ∩ B′). **[3]**

**3.** Two fair six-sided dice are rolled. Find the probability that the total is at least 10, and the probability that the total is at least 10 given that at least one die shows a 5. **[3]**

**4.** Explain the difference between mutually exclusive events and independent events. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** A sports club has 80 members. 40 play tennis (T), 38 swim (W) and 35 run (R). 20 play tennis and swim, 18 swim and run, 16 play tennis and run, and 12 do all three.

**(a)** Find the number of members in each region of a Venn diagram, including the number who do none of the three. **[3]**

**(b)** A member is chosen at random. Find the probability that they do exactly two of the activities. **[2]**

**(c)** Find P(R | T). **[2]**

**(d)** Determine whether the events T and R are independent. **[2]**

**6.** P(A) = 0.6, P(B) = 0.45 and P(B | A) = 0.5.

**(a)** Find P(A ∩ B) and P(A ∪ B). **[3]**

**(b)** Find P(A | B). **[1]**

**(c)** Find P(B | A′). **[2]**

**7.** A learner driver passes the test at the first attempt with probability 0.6. If she fails, she passes at the second attempt with probability 0.7. If she fails twice, she passes at the third attempt with probability 0.8. She stops after passing or after three attempts.

**(a)** Find the probability that she passes at the second attempt. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the probability that she passes within three attempts. **[2]**

**(c)** Given that she passes within three attempts, find the probability that she needed exactly two attempts. **[3]**

**8.** A bag contains 5 blue, 3 yellow and 2 white counters. Three counters are taken at random, without replacement.

**(a)** Find the probability that all three are the same colour. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the probability that exactly two are blue. **[3]**

**(c)** Find the probability that at least one is white. **[2]**

**9.** A commuter uses route 1, route 2 or route 3 with probabilities 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2. The probabilities that she is late using these routes are 0.1, 0.2 and 0.4.

**(a)** Find the probability that she is late on a randomly chosen day. **[2]**

**(b)** Given that she is late, find the probability that she used route 3. **[2]**

**10.** A bag contains 6 red and 4 black balls. Two balls are taken at random. Find the probability that they are of different colours when the first ball is

**(a)** replaced before the second is taken; **[2]**

**(b)** not replaced. **[2]**

**11.** A and B are independent events. P(A) = 0.4 and P(A ∪ B) = 0.76. Find P(B). **[3]**

**12.** A and B are independent events. Prove that A′ and B′ are also independent. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** P(A ∪ B) = 0.42 + 0.33 = **0.75** [1]. P(A′ ∩ B′) = 1 − 0.75 = **0.25** [1].

**2.** P(A ∩ B) = 0.25 × 0.8 = **0.2** [1]. P(A ∪ B) = 0.25 + 0.8 − 0.2 = **0.85** [1]. P(A′ ∩ B′) = 0.75 × 0.2 = **0.15** [1].

**3.** Totals of at least 10: (4, 6), (5, 5), (5, 6), (6, 4), (6, 5), (6, 6), so P = 6/36 = **1/6** [1]. At least one 5: 11 outcomes [1]; of these, (5, 5), (5, 6) and (6, 5) total at least 10, so the conditional probability is **3/11** [1].

**4.** Mutually exclusive events cannot both happen, so P(A ∩ B) = 0 [1]. Independent events can both happen, but one happening does not change the probability of the other, so P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B) [1].

**5. (a)** All three: 12. Double overlaps only: T and W 8, W and R 6, T and R 4 [1]. Single only: T 16, W 12, R 13 [1]. Inside the circles: 71, so **none = 9** [1].

**(b)** Exactly two: 8 + 6 + 4 = 18 [1], so P = 18/80 = **0.225** [1].

**(c)** Inside T and R: 4 + 12 = 16 [1], so P(R | T) = 16/40 = **0.4** [1].

**(d)** P(T)P(R) = 0.5 × 0.4375 = 0.21875, while P(T ∩ R) = 16/80 = 0.2 [1]. These differ, so **T and R are not independent** [1].

**6. (a)** P(A ∩ B) = 0.6 × 0.5 = **0.3** [1]. P(A ∪ B) = 0.6 + 0.45 − 0.3 [1] = **0.75** [1].

**(b)** P(A | B) = 0.3/0.45 = **2/3** [1].

**(c)** P(A′ ∩ B) = 0.45 − 0.3 = 0.15 [1], so P(B | A′) = 0.15/0.4 = **0.375** [1].

**7. (a)** 0.4 × 0.7 [1] = **0.28** [1].

**(b)** 0.6 + 0.28 + 0.4 × 0.3 × 0.8 [1] = 0.6 + 0.28 + 0.096 = **0.976** [1].

**(c)** P(two attempts | pass within three) = P(two attempts) / P(pass within three) [1] = 0.28/0.976 [1] = **0.287** (3 s.f.) [1].

**8. (a)** All blue: (5 × 4 × 3)/(10 × 9 × 8) = 60/720; all yellow: 6/720; all white is impossible [1]. Total = 66/720 = **11/120** [1].

**(b)** One order, B B not-B: (5/10)(4/9)(5/8) = 100/720 [1]; there are 3 orders [1], so P = 300/720 = **5/12** [1].

**(c)** P(no white) = (8/10)(7/9)(6/8) = 336/720 [1], so P(at least one white) = 384/720 = **8/15** [1].

**9. (a)** 0.5 × 0.1 + 0.3 × 0.2 + 0.2 × 0.4 [1] = 0.05 + 0.06 + 0.08 = **0.19** [1].

**(b)** 0.08/0.19 [1] = **0.421** (3 s.f.) [1].

**10. (a)** P(RB) + P(BR) = 0.6 × 0.4 + 0.4 × 0.6 [1] = **0.48** [1].

**(b)** (6/10)(4/9) + (4/10)(6/9) [1] = 48/90 = **8/15** [1].

**11.** P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A)P(B), using independence [1]. 0.76 = 0.4 + P(B) − 0.4P(B), so 0.36 = 0.6P(B) [1]. **P(B) = 0.6** [1].

**12.** P(A′ ∩ B′) = 1 − P(A ∪ B) = 1 − P(A) − P(B) + P(A ∩ B) [1]. By independence, this is 1 − P(A) − P(B) + P(A)P(B) [1], which factorises as [1 − P(A)][1 − P(B)] = P(A′)P(B′), so A′ and B′ are independent [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting the triple overlap inside every double overlap in Question 5.
- Using P(B | A) = P(B) in Question 6 without evidence of independence.
- Dividing by the wrong total when reversing a conditional in Questions 7 and 9.
- Forgetting the factor of 3 for the orders in Question 8(b).
- Treating draws without replacement as if they were replaced.
