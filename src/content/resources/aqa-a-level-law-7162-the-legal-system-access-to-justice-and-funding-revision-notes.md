Condensed notes for the section 3.1 row **The legal system: access to justice and funding**: alternative sources of legal advice, private funding and public funding. For explanation and a worked scenario, use the [access to justice and funding study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-law-7162-the-legal-system-access-to-justice-and-funding/).

## The three questions every scenario raises

1. Where can this person get **advice**?
2. Can they **pay privately**?
3. Do they qualify for **state funding**?

Answer them in that order, applying each to the person's problem and means.

## Alternative sources of advice

| Source | Key features | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Help lines | Free telephone and online advice from government, charities, insurers and Acas | Quick first advice and signposting |
| Citizens Advice | National charity, local offices, trained volunteers, free and confidential | Debt, benefits, housing, consumer, employment |
| Law centres | Not-for-profit; solicitors and caseworkers; first in North Kensington in 1970 | Social welfare law, often with representation |
| Trade unions | Advice and representation for members, often union solicitors | Employment disputes, workplace injuries |

**Weaknesses:** uneven provision and funding; volunteers are not lawyers; union help is for members only.

## Private funding

**Own resources:** hourly or fixed fees; full choice of lawyer; high cost; in civil cases the loser usually pays much of the winner's costs.

**Insurance:**
- **BTE** (before the event): bought before any dispute, often an add-on to home or motor insurance.
- **ATE** (after the event): bought after a dispute arises, mainly to cover the other side's costs if the claim fails.

**Conditional fee agreements:**

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Basic idea | No fee or reduced fee if lost; normal fee plus success fee if won |
| Success fee | Up to 100% of the normal fee |
| Introduced | Courts and Legal Services Act 1990 |
| Not allowed | Criminal and family proceedings |
| 1999 position | Success fee and ATE premium recoverable from the loser (Access to Justice Act 1999) |
| Since April 2013 | Not recoverable from the loser; paid from damages; capped at 25% of damages (excluding future loss) in personal injury (LASPO) |
| Related | Damages-based agreements: lawyer takes a percentage of damages |

## Public funding

**Administered by:** the Legal Aid Agency (replaced the Legal Services Commission under LASPO 2012).

**Civil legal aid tests:**
1. **Scope:** many areas removed by LASPO (most private family law without abuse evidence, most clinical negligence, most employment, welfare benefits and debt). Still in scope: care proceedings, domestic abuse protection, home at risk, asylum, mental health tribunals.
2. **Means:** disposable income and capital below limits; contributions possible.
3. **Merits:** prospects of success and cost-benefit.
4. **Exceptional case funding:** s10 LASPO where refusal would breach human rights.

**Criminal legal aid:**
- Police station advice: free to all detainees (s58 PACE 1984; duty solicitor).
- Court representation: **interests of justice** test (loss of liberty, livelihood or reputation; substantial point of law; inability to understand or present the case) plus a **means test**.
- Crown Court: income contributions, and capital contributions if convicted.
- Duty solicitor at the magistrates' court on the day.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Access to justice | Being able to find out, assert and enforce legal rights whatever one's means |
| Litigant in person | A party who represents themselves without a lawyer |
| BTE insurance | Legal expenses cover bought before a dispute arises |
| ATE insurance | Cover bought after a dispute arises, mainly for the opponent's costs |
| Conditional fee agreement | "No win, no fee" agreement with a success fee if won |
| Success fee | Uplift on the normal fee, up to 100% |
| Means test | Assessment of disposable income and capital |
| Merits test | Assessment of prospects of success and benefit |
| Interests of justice test | Test for criminal legal aid representation in court |
| Exceptional case funding | Civil legal aid outside scope where human rights require it |

## Evaluation snapshots

- **Advice sources:** cheap and early, but patchy and dependent on funding.
- **CFAs:** opened personal injury claims to people of ordinary means, but lawyers choose strong cases, and winners lose part of their damages.
- **Civil legal aid:** protects the most vulnerable in scope, but LASPO cuts increased litigants in person, especially in family courts.
- **Criminal legal aid:** police station advice is a key safeguard, but low fixed fees discourage lawyers from criminal work.

## Exam traps

- Skipping the scope question for civil legal aid.
- Reversing BTE and ATE.
- Suggesting a CFA for a criminal or family case.
- Saying a successful CFA client keeps all the damages.
- Calling police station advice means-tested.
- Listing every source of advice without applying any to the facts.
