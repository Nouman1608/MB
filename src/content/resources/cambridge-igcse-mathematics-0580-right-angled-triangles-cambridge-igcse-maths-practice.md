These questions cover subtopic 6.2, Right-angled triangles, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions labelled **(Extended)** use angles of elevation and depression or the shortest distance to a line, which are in the Extended statements only; every other question suits both tiers. A calculator is needed throughout. Give lengths to 3 significant figures and angles to 1 decimal place.

For explanations, read the [right-angled triangles study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-right-angled-triangles-cambridge-igcse-maths/); for a summary, use the [right-angled triangles revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-right-angled-triangles-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** In triangle ABC, angle B = 90°, AC = 16 cm and angle A = 27°. Calculate BC. **[2]**

**2.** In triangle PQR, angle Q = 90°, PQ = 11.3 cm and angle P = 49°. Calculate PR. **[2]**

**3.** A right-angled triangle has a side of 7.5 cm opposite angle x and a side of 4.2 cm adjacent to it. Calculate x. **[2]**

**4.** In triangle XYZ, angle Y = 90°, YZ = 4 cm and angle X = 62°. Calculate XY. **[2]**

**5.** The bearing of Y from X is 312°. Write down the bearing of X from Y. **[1]**

**6.** A ladder 4.5 m long leans against a vertical wall. It makes an angle of 72° with the horizontal ground. Calculate how far up the wall the ladder reaches. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** An isosceles triangle has two sides of 10 cm and a base of 12 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the size of each base angle. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the size of the third angle. **[1]**

**8.** In triangle PQR, angle PQR = 90°, PQ = 9 cm and angle QPR = 38°. S is the point on PQ with QS = 4 cm.

**(a)** Calculate QR. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate angle QSR. **[2]**

**9.** A ship sails 25 km from a port on a bearing of 060°. It then sails 18 km on a bearing of 150°.

**(a)** Explain why the two parts of the journey meet at a right angle, and calculate the direct distance of the ship from the port. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the bearing of the ship from the port. **[3]**

**10.** A rectangle measures 14 cm by 9 cm. Calculate the angle between a diagonal and a longer side. **[2]**

**11.** (Extended) From a point A on level ground, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is 32°. From a point B, 20 m closer to the tower in a straight line, the angle of elevation is 47°. Calculate the height of the tower. **[5]**

**12.** (Extended) From a window 18 m above level ground, the angle of depression of a car is 25°.

**(a)** Calculate the horizontal distance from the building to the car. **[2]**

**(b)** A second car is further away, in the same direction, at an angle of depression of 15°. Calculate the distance between the two cars. **[2]**

**13.** (Extended) A lighthouse L is 8 km from a port P, on a bearing of 040° from P. A boat leaves P and sails due east.

**(a)** Calculate the shortest distance between the lighthouse and the boat's course. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why this is the shortest distance. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** sin 27° = BC / 16 [1]; BC = 16 sin 27° = **7.26 cm** [1]

**2.** cos 49° = 11.3 / PR [1]; PR = 11.3 / cos 49° = **17.2 cm** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the hypotenuse must be longer than 11.3 cm; 11.3 × cos 49° = 7.41 is a sign that the rearrangement went the wrong way.

**3.** tan x = 7.5 / 4.2 [1]; x = **60.8°** [1]

**4.** tan 62° = 4 / XY [1]; XY = 4 / tan 62° = **2.13 cm** [1]

**5.** 312° − 180° = **132°** [1]

**6.** sin 72° = h / 4.5 [1]; h = 4.5 sin 72° = **4.28 m** [1]

**7. (a)** The line of symmetry halves the base, so cos x = 6 / 10 [1]; x = **53.1°** [1]

**(b)** 180° − 2 × 53.13…° = **73.7°** [1]

**8. (a)** tan 38° = QR / 9 [1]; QR = **7.03 cm** [1]

**(b)** tan(angle QSR) = 7.031… / 4 [1]; angle QSR = **60.4°** [1]

*Examiner insight:* use the unrounded 7.0315…; with 7.03 the angle is still 60.4°, but carrying full values is the safe habit.

**9. (a)** 150° − 060° = 90°, so the ship turns through a right angle [1]; distance = √(25² + 18²) = √949 = **30.8 km** [1]

**(b)** Angle at the port between the first leg and the line to the ship: tan θ = 18 / 25, θ = 35.75…° [1]; the ship turned clockwise, so add to the first bearing [1]; bearing = 060° + 35.75…° = **095.8°** [1]

**10.** tan θ = 9 / 14 [1]; θ = **32.7°** [1]

**11.** Let the distance from B to the foot of the tower be d m and the height h m. Then h = d tan 47° [1] and h = (d + 20) tan 32° [1]. So d tan 47° = d tan 32° + 20 tan 32°, giving d = 20 tan 32° ÷ (tan 47° − tan 32°) [1] = 27.93… m [1]; h = 27.93… × tan 47° = **29.9 m** [1]

*Examiner insight:* forming two expressions for the same height is the key step; each triangle alone has only one known length.

**12. (a)** The angle of elevation of the window from the car is 25° [1]; distance = 18 / tan 25° = **38.6 m** [1]

**(b)** 18 / tan 15° = 67.17… m [1]; 67.17… − 38.60… = **28.6 m** [1]

**13. (a)** The course runs due east, so the angle between PL and the course is 90° − 40° = 50° [1]; shortest distance = 8 × sin 50° [1] = **6.13 km** [1]

**(b)** The shortest distance from a point to a line is the perpendicular distance; any other line from L to the course is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle and so is longer [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Labelling opposite and adjacent from the wrong angle.
- Multiplying when the unknown side is on the bottom of the ratio.
- Angles to 3 significant figures instead of 1 decimal place.
- Bearings without three figures or measured anticlockwise.
- (Extended) Putting the angle of depression inside the triangle at the top.
- (Extended) Rounding an intermediate distance before the final step.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I find a bearing after two legs of a journey?**
Draw a north line at the starting point, find the angle at the start inside the triangle with trigonometry, then add or subtract it from the first bearing depending on which way the journey turned.

**What if my calculator gives an error for sin⁻¹?**
The value you entered is greater than 1, which is impossible for a sine. Check that you divided the shorter side by the hypotenuse, not the other way round.
