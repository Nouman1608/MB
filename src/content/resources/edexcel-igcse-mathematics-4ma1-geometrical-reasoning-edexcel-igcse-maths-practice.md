## Section A

**1.** Three angles lie together on a straight line. They are x°, 2x° and 63°. Work out x, giving a reason. **[2]**

**2.** Two straight lines cross. One of the four angles formed is 74°. Work out the other three angles, giving reasons. **[2]**

**3.** In an isosceles triangle, the angle between the two equal sides is 38°. Work out the size of each of the other angles, giving reasons. **[2]**

**4.** In triangle PQR, side QR is extended to S. Angle QPR = 54° and angle PQR = 67°. Work out angle PRS, giving a reason. **[2]**

**5.** Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 24°. Work out the number of sides, giving a reason. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** AB and CD are parallel lines. A straight line crosses AB at P and CD at Q, and B and D are on the same side of PQ. Angle BPQ = (4x − 10)° and angle PQD = (x + 15)°.

Work out the size of angle BPQ, giving reasons. **[3]**

**7.** AB and CD are parallel lines, with B and D at their right-hand ends. A point E lies between the lines, to the left of B and D. Angle ABE = 32° and angle CDE = 41°, where both angles open towards E.

Work out angle BED, giving reasons. **[3]**

**8.** The angles of quadrilateral ABCD, in order, are A = (x + 15)°, B = 2x°, C = (x + 40)° and D = (x + 30)°.

**(a)** Work out the value of x, giving a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** Show that AD is parallel to BC. **[2]**

**9.** ABCDEF is a regular hexagon.

**(a)** Work out the size of each interior angle, giving a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out angle ACD, giving reasons. **[2]**

**10.** A circle has centre O. A tangent touches the circle at A, and P is a point on the tangent. Angle AOP = 58°. Work out angle APO, giving reasons. **[2]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** A tangent touches a circle at A, and T is a point on the tangent. B and C lie on the circle, with C in the alternate segment. Angle TAB = 52° and CA = CB.

Work out angle CAB, giving reasons. **[4]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)** A, B, C and D lie on a circle with centre O. A is on the major arc BD and C is on the minor arc BD. Angle BOD = 140°.

**(a)** Work out angle BAD, giving a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out angle BCD, giving a reason. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** x + 2x + 63 = 180, so 3x = 117 and x = **39** [1]; angles on a straight line add up to 180° [1].

**2.** **74°** opposite it (vertically opposite angles are equal) and **106°** for each of the other two (angles on a straight line add up to 180°): both values [1], both reasons [1].

**3.** (180° − 38°) ÷ 2 = **71°** each [1]; base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal and angles in a triangle add up to 180° [1].

**4.** 54° + 67° = **121°** [1]; the exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles [1].

**5.** 360 ÷ 24 = **15** sides [1]; the exterior angles of a polygon add up to 360° [1].

**6.** 4x − 10 + x + 15 = 180 [1], with the reason that co-interior (allied) angles add up to 180° [1]. 5x = 175, x = 35, so angle BPQ = 4 × 35 − 10 = **130°** [1].

**7.** Draw a line through E parallel to AB and CD [1]. The part of angle BED above this line equals angle ABE = 32° and the part below equals angle CDE = 41°, because alternate angles are equal [1]. Angle BED = 32° + 41° = **73°** [1].

**8. (a)** 5x + 85 = 360, so x = **55** [1]; angles in a quadrilateral add up to 360° [1].

**(b)** Angle A = 70° and angle B = 110°, which add to 180° [1]. Angles A and B lie between AD and BC on the same side of AB, so they are co-interior angles adding to 180°, and therefore AD is parallel to BC [1].

**9. (a)** Exterior angle = 360° ÷ 6 = 60°, so interior angle = **120°** [1]; exterior angles add up to 360° and interior + exterior = 180° [1].

**(b)** AB = BC, so angle BCA = (180° − 120°) ÷ 2 = 30° (base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal) [1]. Angle ACD = 120° − 30° = **90°** [1].

**10.** Angle OAP = 90° because a tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact [1]. Angle APO = 180° − 90° − 58° = **32°**, because angles in a triangle add up to 180° [1].

**11.** Angle ACB = 52° [1], by the alternate segment theorem [1]. Triangle ABC is isosceles with CA = CB, so its base angles are equal [1]. Angle CAB = (180° − 52°) ÷ 2 = **64°** [1].

**12. (a)** Angle BAD = 140° ÷ 2 = **70°** [1]; the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference [1].

**(b)** Angle BCD = 180° − 70° = **110°** [1]; opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180° [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 2: giving the angles with no reasons, or writing "X angles" for vertically opposite.
- Question 6: setting the two angles equal. They are co-interior, so they add to 180°.
- Question 7: adding the angles without the extra parallel line and the word "alternate".
- Question 8(b): showing that A + B = 180° but not saying what that proves.
- Question 11: quoting "angles in a triangle" but leaving out "base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal".

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I write "Z angles" as a reason?**
No. Write the standard statement "alternate angles are equal". Similarly, use "corresponding angles are equal" and "co-interior (allied) angles add up to 180°".

**Do Foundation candidates need circle theorem reasons?**
No. The Foundation statement only requires reasons for lines (including chords and tangents), triangles and polygons. The circle theorems are Higher tier only.
