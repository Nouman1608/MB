## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Antigen | A molecule, usually on a pathogen or cell, that the immune system recognises |
| Antibody | Protein made by a B lymphocyte; its binding site fits one antigen |
| Monoclonal antibody | Identical antibodies from one clone of cells, all binding the same antigen |
| Hybridoma | A cell made by fusing a B lymphocyte with a tumour cell |
| hCG | Hormone in the urine of a pregnant woman, detected in pregnancy tests |
| PSA | Protein in blood that can be raised in prostate cancer, measured with monoclonal antibodies |
| Aseptic technique | Methods that keep unwanted microorganisms out of a culture and your culture out of the surroundings |
| Autoclave | Steam sterilisation under pressure |
| Preclinical testing | Laboratory cell, computer and animal tests before any human use |
| Clinical trial | Testing of a drug on people, in phases |
| Placebo | A dummy treatment that looks like the real drug |
| Double-blind | Neither patients nor doctors know who gets the drug |

## B6.3m Making monoclonal antibodies (Higher Tier only)

Method in steps:

1. Inject the antigen into a mouse.
2. Take B lymphocytes making the antibody (often from the spleen).
3. Fuse them with tumour cells to make hybridomas.
4. Screen to select hybridomas making the required antibody.
5. Clone the selected cells and grow them in large amounts.
6. Collect and purify the antibody.

Why both parent cells: the lymphocyte gives specificity (one antibody), the tumour cell gives unlimited division. The hybridoma gives both.

## B6.3n Uses of monoclonal antibodies (Higher Tier only)

| Use | How it works | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| Pregnancy test | Antibodies on a strip bind hCG in urine; beads gather at the test line | Control line shows the test worked |
| Detecting disease | Antibodies bind a marker such as PSA in blood, or carry a label to show where an antigen is | A raised PSA is a reason for more tests, not proof |
| Cancer treatment | Antibody carries a drug to antigens on cancer cells | Less harm to healthy cells, still potential and costly |

Pregnancy test read-out:

| Test line | Control line | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Coloured | Coloured | hCG present |
| Absent | Coloured | hCG not detected |
| Either | Absent | Test invalid, repeat |

## B6.3p Aseptic technique

| Technique | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Alcohol on bench and hands | Kills microorganisms on surfaces |
| Autoclave glassware and media | Steam under pressure, about 121 °C, kills all microorganisms and spores |
| Flame the loop and bottle neck | Sterilises the loop, and the air current at the neck carries contaminants away |
| Work near a Bunsen burner | Upward air current carries microorganisms away from dishes |
| Lid lifted slightly, briefly | Fewer microorganisms fall in |
| Tape the lid in a few places | Keeps the lid on but lets oxygen in |
| Incubate around 25 °C (schools) | Avoids conditions that suit human pathogens |
| Uninoculated control plate | Shows whether sterile technique worked |

Contamination: wrong organisms grow, so results cannot be trusted and a pathogen could be spread.

## B6.3q New medicines

1. Discovery: from plants, microorganisms or made by chemists, then screened.
2. Preclinical: cells and tissues, computer models, live animals. Checks toxicity, effect and dose.
3. Phase 1: small number of healthy volunteers, safety and dose.
4. Phase 2: small number of patients, effectiveness and dose.
5. Phase 3: large number of patients, compared with a placebo or the current treatment.
6. Approval, then continued monitoring.

Trial design points: random allocation, placebo, double-blind, large sample, informed consent. Thalidomide shows why testing, including use in pregnancy, matters.

## Maths reminders

| Skill | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Area of circular colony | πr², r is half the diameter | Diameter 4.0 cm gives r = 2.0 cm, area = 12.6 cm² |
| Area of clear zone around a disc | π(R² − r²) | R = 12 mm, r = 3 mm gives 424 mm² |
| Percentage | part ÷ whole × 100 | 6 of 40 plates contaminated is 15% |
| Percentage change | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 | 8.0 to 5.2 is a 35% decrease |
| Scatter diagram | Describe the trend; correlation is not proof of cause | Higher dose with more side effects is a positive correlation |
| Sampling | A larger, random, representative sample gives more reliable results | |

## Worked reminders

**Cross-sectional area of a culture.** A colony has a diameter of 15 mm.
Step 1: r = 15 ÷ 2 = 7.5 mm. Step 2: area = π × 7.5² = 177 mm² (3 significant figures).

**Clear zone around a disc.** An antibiotic disc has a diameter of 6 mm and the clear zone has a diameter of 14 mm. Clear area around the disc = π × (7² − 3²) = π × 40 = 126 mm².

**Comparing groups in a trial.** In a trial with 150 patients on the new drug, 9 had side effects (6.0%). With 150 on the existing drug, 4 had side effects (2.7%). The new drug has the higher rate, but a rate from 9 people is a small number, so a larger trial is needed before concluding it is less safe.

## Must-know distinctions

| Compare | Point |
|---|---|
| Antibody and antigen | The antibody is the protein from a B lymphocyte; the antigen is the molecule it binds |
| One clone, one target | Monoclonal means one clone and one binding site, so only one antigen is targeted |
| Lymphocyte and tumour cell in a hybridoma | Lymphocyte gives the specific antibody; tumour cell gives repeated division |
| Test line and control line | Test line shows hCG; control line shows the strip worked |
| Diagnosis and treatment uses | Detection labels or measures an antigen; treatment carries a drug to a target |
| Sterilising and disinfecting | Autoclave and flame sterilise equipment; alcohol cleans a surface or skin |
| Preclinical and clinical | Preclinical uses cells, models and animals; clinical uses people |
| Phase 1 and phase 3 | Phase 1 is safety in few healthy people; phase 3 is effectiveness in many patients |
| Placebo and double-blind | Placebo is the dummy treatment; double-blind hides who has what from patients and doctors |

## Evaluation points for longer answers

For monoclonal antibodies, benefits include specific targeting, quick results from pregnancy tests and the possibility of fewer side effects in cancer treatment. Limitations include cost, that not every cancer has a suitable antigen, and that treatment is still being developed.

For animal testing in drug development, arguments for are that it shows effects on a whole body before people are exposed. Arguments against are animal suffering and the fact that an animal does not always respond as a human does.

For clinical trials, arguments for include protecting the public from harmful drugs. Arguments against include the time and cost, which delay patient access, and the risk to volunteers. A balanced answer gives a point from each side and ends with a judgement.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the two types of cell fused to make a hybridoma. (Higher Tier only)
2. Why can a hybridoma make large amounts of antibody? (Higher Tier only)
3. Which hormone does a pregnancy test detect? (Higher Tier only)
4. What does the control line show? (Higher Tier only)
5. Give one reason a raised PSA does not prove prostate cancer. (Higher Tier only)
6. Why is a drug attached to a monoclonal antibody less harmful to healthy cells? (Higher Tier only)
7. Why is the neck of a bottle passed through a flame?
8. Give one reason the lid of a Petri dish is not completely sealed.
9. A colony has a diameter of 15 mm. Calculate its area to 3 significant figures.
10. Why is a placebo used in a phase 3 trial?
11. A cell count falls from 8.0 × 10⁵ to 5.2 × 10⁵. Calculate the percentage change.
12. Who takes part in a phase 1 trial and why?

### Answers

1. A B lymphocyte (antibody-producing white blood cell) and a tumour cell.
2. It divides without limit like the tumour cell and makes the one antibody like the lymphocyte.
3. hCG.
4. That the test worked, because the antibodies there bind the mobile antibodies whether or not hCG is present.
5. Other prostate conditions can also raise PSA, so it needs further tests.
6. The antibody binds antigens on cancer cells, so the drug is delivered to them and fewer healthy cells are exposed.
7. It sterilises the neck and moves air upward, which stops contaminants entering.
8. So oxygen can enter and dangerous anaerobic bacteria are less likely to grow.
9. r = 7.5 mm. Area = π × 7.5² = 177 mm².
10. So that any improvement caused by expectation or natural recovery can be separated from the effect of the drug.
11. (5.2 − 8.0) ÷ 8.0 × 100 = −35%, a decrease of 35%.
12. A small group of healthy volunteers, to test safety and how the body handles the drug.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the hybridoma "makes monoclonal cells" or that the tumour cell makes the antibody.
- Describing a pregnancy test without naming hCG, or without saying why the control line is needed.
- Writing that monoclonal antibodies "cure" cancer. They are described as a potential treatment.
- Giving "kills bacteria" for every aseptic step without saying which object or surface is made sterile.
- Mixing up sterilising equipment (autoclave) and cleaning a surface (alcohol).
- Using the diameter instead of the radius, or forgetting to subtract the disc from a clear zone.
- Calling phase 1 a test of effectiveness when its main aim is safety.
- Omitting the comparison group: results from a drug group with no placebo or control cannot show the drug works.
- Leaving out units or the sign of a percentage change.
