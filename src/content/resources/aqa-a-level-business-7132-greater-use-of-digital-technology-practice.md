These questions cover section 3.9.4 greater use of digital technology of the AQA A-level Business (7132) specification. All businesses and figures are invented. Revise first with the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-greater-use-of-digital-technology/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-greater-use-of-digital-technology-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an example of data mining? **[1]**

A: Selling goods through a website

B: Using software to find which customers are likely to stop buying

C: Installing a robot to pack boxes

D: Storing customer names in a spreadsheet

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a likely limit of automation? **[1]**

A: Higher unit costs at all levels of output

B: High set-up costs and possible redundancies

C: Lower consistency of quality

D: Less output per hour

**3.** Define "e-commerce". **[2]**

**4.** A website has 120,000 visitors a month and 4% of them buy, spending an average of £35. Calculate the monthly revenue. **[3]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why customer expectations may force a business to adopt digital technology. **[3]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**6.** The conversion rate on the website in Question 4 rises to 5%. Calculate the new monthly revenue and the increase. **[3]**

**7.** Kestrel Cheese has 30,000 subscription customers, each paying £90 a year. Data mining helps reduce the annual loss rate of customers from 15% to 11%. Calculate the number of customers kept and the extra annual revenue. **[4]**

**8.** A packaging firm can buy a machine for £400,000. It would replace 8 workers each costing £28,000 a year, and would cost £30,000 a year to run. Calculate the net annual saving and the payback period to two decimal places. **[4]**

**9.** Analyse **two** non-financial factors the firm should consider before buying the machine in Question 8. **[6]**

**10.** Explain **two** risks to a business of relying on big data. **[4]**

**11.** A family-run independent bookshop is considering starting an online shop. Analyse **two** benefits and **one** drawback. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate whether a business should always adopt the latest digital technology. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Data mining searches data for patterns, such as those that predict customers leaving. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. Automation needs heavy investment and can cut jobs. **[1]**

**3.** Buying and selling [1] over the internet [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Orders = 120,000 × 4% = 4,800 [1]; revenue = 4,800 × £35 [1] = **£168,000** [1]. **[3]**

**5.** Customers now expect to buy online, track deliveries and get quick replies [1]. If a business does not offer this, they will switch to competitors that do [1], so sales and market share fall unless it adopts the technology [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Orders = 120,000 × 5% = 6,000; revenue = 6,000 × £35 = **£210,000** [2]; increase = £210,000 − £168,000 = **£42,000** [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Customers lost before: 15% × 30,000 = 4,500; after: 11% × 30,000 = 3,300 [1]. Customers kept = 4,500 − 3,300 = **1,200** [1]. Extra revenue = 1,200 × £90 [1] = **£108,000** a year [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Labour saved = 8 × £28,000 = £224,000 [1]; net saving = £224,000 − £30,000 = **£194,000** [1]; payback = £400,000 ÷ £194,000 [1] = **2.06 years** [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Factor 1: employees [1]. Eight redundancies may harm morale and reputation among remaining staff and the local community [1], and redundancy costs must be paid; retraining some workers could reduce the impact [1]. Factor 2: reliability and flexibility [1]. A machine breakdown could stop production, and it may be less able to handle custom orders [1], so the firm needs maintenance staff and a backup plan [1]. (Credit also: effect on quality; strategic fit; customer reaction.) **[6]**

**10.** Risk 1: poor data quality [1]. If the data is wrong or out of date, decisions will be poor [1]. Risk 2: legal and trust issues [1]. Data protection law restricts how personal data can be used, and a breach could bring fines and a loss of customer trust [1]. (Credit also: cost; patterns that are not causes.) **[4]**

**11.** Benefit 1: a wider market [1]. The shop can sell to customers across the country, not only locally, increasing sales [1]. Benefit 2: customer data [1]. It can see what customers search for and buy and target emails, helping it stock the right books [1]. Drawback: competition and costs [1]. Large online sellers can charge lower prices and the shop must pay for a website, packing and delivery [1]. **[6]**

**12.** For: technology can lower costs, improve quality and open new markets [1]. Rivals that adopt it may gain an advantage [1], and customers increasingly expect digital service [1]. Early adoption can give a first-mover advantage [1]. Against: new technology is expensive and may not pay back [1]. Staff may resist or lack skills [1]. Technology can fail or be attacked [1]. The latest technology may be untested, and a follower may learn from the mistakes of others [1]. Factors: the size and finances of the business [1], whether it fits the strategy (for example a craft business may rely on personal service) [1], and the pace of change in the market [1]. Judgement: a business should adopt technology that supports its objectives and gives a clear return [1], not simply the newest. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* Good answers name the specific technology, link it to a function and weigh the cost and risk against the benefit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing about technology in general rather than naming the form.
- Confusing big data with data mining.
- Ignoring the effect on employees and customers.
- Leaving out data protection and security.
- Concluding without saying what the decision depends on.
