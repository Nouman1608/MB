The International option of Cambridge 9489 Paper 4 covers three topics: the Soviet Empire in Eastern Europe (1953-91), the end of minority rule in South Africa and Zimbabwe (1948-94), and the route to independence in Malaysia and Indonesia (c.1909-67). You answer two 30-mark essays from this option, and each must **assess** or **evaluate** a claim with a balanced argument, explicit criteria and a sustained judgement.

**Stagnation factors (7.2):** Kosygin's limited reforms; the 10th Five Year Plan's missed targets; the burden of defence spending; Afghanistan; Western influence through Helsinki and radio; the Church in Poland.
**Gorbachev in 1985 (7.3):** a stagnant economy, food shortages and rationing, a budget deficit, absenteeism and poor living conditions, with pressure for change inside and outside the USSR.

## Worked example: building a judgement

**Question:** "Gorbachev's reforms were the main reason for the collapse of Soviet control in Eastern Europe." Assess this view.

- **Criterion:** what removed the *means* of control (military threat) versus what created the *will* to leave (popular opposition).
- **For:** the Sinatra Doctrine removed the threat of intervention; glasnost encouraged open criticism; withdrawal from Afghanistan signalled restraint.
- **Against:** Solidarity's decade of organisation; economic failure across the bloc; nationalism in Hungary, Czechoslovakia and the Baltic states; Western pressure.
- **Judgement:** Gorbachev's reforms were the necessary trigger, but long-term opposition made collapse rapid once the threat was lifted.

## Common mistakes / exam traps

- **Running the three topics together.** Answer on the topic in the question; comparison is optional.
- **Over-narrating 1989.** Analyse why regimes fell, not each country in turn.
- **Ignoring the date limits.** 8.1 ends in 1959; Sharpeville belongs to 8.2.
- **Treating Malaya and Indonesia alike.** British decolonisation was negotiated; Dutch decolonisation involved war.
- **Unsupported claims.** Every paragraph needs named events, laws or people -- the AO1 grid rewards precision.

## Frequently asked questions

**How are the essays marked?**
Each is out of 30: 15 for AO1 knowledge and 15 for AO2 analysis and judgement, on five levels in the published 9489 Paper 4 mark schemes.

**What does "stagnation" mean in key question 7.2?**
It describes the slowing of Soviet economic growth and the lack of political renewal under Brezhnev and his ageing successors. The syllabus links it to corruption, the nomenklatura system, the gerontocracy and defence spending.

**Do I need historians' views?**
No. Paper 4 assesses AO1 and AO2 only.

## Self-test

1. Why was the Warsaw Pact formed in 1955? *In response to West Germany joining NATO, formalising Soviet military control.*
2. What were the 21 Demands? *Solidarity's demands at Gdańsk in 1980, including free trade unions.*
3. What did the Freedom Charter set out? *A non-racial, democratic South Africa (1955).*
4. What was UDI? *Smith's Unilateral Declaration of Independence for Rhodesia (1965).*
5. What was Supersemar? *The Order of 11 March 1966 transferring authority to Suharto.*
