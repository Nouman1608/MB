These notes cover the section 3.4 statement "The advantages and disadvantages of different organisations" (sole traders, partnerships and limited companies) and the related point "Distinguish between share capital and loan capital". The full explanation and an annotated answer are in the [business organisations study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-business-organisations-compared-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting/); practise with the [business organisations practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-business-organisations-compared-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sole trader | A business owned by one person, who takes all the profit and bears all the risk |
| Partnership | Two or more people carrying on a business together with a view to profit |
| Limited company | A business owned by shareholders and legally separate from them |
| Unlimited liability | Owners' personal assets can be used to pay the business's debts |
| Limited liability | Shareholders can lose only what they paid, or agreed to pay, for their shares |
| Share capital | Finance from shareholders, who become owners and may receive dividends |
| Loan capital | Long-term borrowing, such as debentures, which earns interest and is repaid |
| Partnership agreement | Written terms on profit sharing, interest on capital, salaries and drawings |
| Retained earnings | Profits kept in a company rather than paid out as dividends |

## Side by side

| | Sole trader | Partnership | Limited company |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liability | Unlimited | Unlimited (ordinary partners) | Limited |
| Finance | Savings, bank | Partners' capital, bank | Shares, debentures, bank |
| Decisions | Fast, one person | Shared, may disagree | Directors; shareholders vote |
| Profit | All to owner | Shared by agreement | Retained or paid as dividends |
| Continuity | Ends with owner | Disrupted when a partner leaves | Continues |
| Formalities | Few | Few; agreement advised | Many legal rules, more cost |
| Ownership in accounts | Capital account | Capital and current accounts | Share capital and retained earnings |
| Owners' withdrawals | Drawings | Drawings | Dividends |

## Main advantages and disadvantages

**Sole trader**
- For: keeps all profit; quick decisions; easy, cheap to set up; privacy.
- Against: unlimited liability; limited finance; heavy workload; depends on one person.

**Partnership**
- For: more capital and skills; work and losses shared; easy to form.
- Against: unlimited liability; profits shared; disagreements; partners bound by one another's decisions.

**Limited company**
- For: limited liability; can raise large finance; continuity; attracts investors.
- Against: costly rules and paperwork; risk of losing control when shares are sold; profits shared among many.

## Share capital or loan capital?

| | Share capital | Loan capital |
|---|---|---|
| Providers are | Owners | Lenders |
| Reward | Dividend, not compulsory | Interest, compulsory |
| Repaid? | Not normally | Yes, on a set date |
| Voting rights | Yes (ordinary shares) | No |
| Statement of financial position | Equity | Non-current liability |
| Income statement | Dividends not an expense | Interest is an expense |

## Quick example

A company has 8% debentures of 50,000. Interest = 8% × 50,000 = 4,000 a year, charged as an expense whether profit is high or low. If it raised the 50,000 by issuing shares instead, it would pay no compulsory return, but the new shareholders would share control and future dividends.

## Building an assess answer

1. Pick the two organisations the question compares.
2. For each, give one advantage and one disadvantage, each tied to a fact in the case: the amount of finance needed, the level of risk, whether the owners want to keep control, how many people will run it.
3. Weigh them: which point matters most for this business, and why?
4. End with a clear judgement and the condition that would change it, for example "a company, unless the owners cannot afford the extra legal costs".

Facts that usually tip the balance:

- **Large borrowing or a risky venture** points towards limited liability.
- **A small, low-risk business run by people who trust each other** points towards a sole trader or partnership.
- **Owners who want to keep control** favour loan capital over issuing shares to outsiders.
- **A need for skills, not just money,** favours taking on a partner.

## Exam traps

- Calling dividends or drawings an expense.
- Putting debentures in equity.
- Writing "limited liability means the business has limited debts". It limits what the owners can lose.
- Forgetting that partners' profit shares go to their current accounts, not their capital accounts, when fixed capital accounts are kept.
- Answering "assess" with a one-sided list.
- Giving advantages of a company to a sole trader in the question who has no plans to raise finance; always apply.

## Self-test

1. Define unlimited liability.
2. Give two advantages of being a sole trader.
3. Give two disadvantages of a partnership.
4. Why does limited liability help a company raise finance?
5. State one disadvantage of a limited company for its original owners.
6. Where does a share of profit go in a partnership with fixed capital accounts?
7. State two differences between share capital and loan capital.
8. Calculate the annual interest on 6% debentures of 30,000.
9. Is debenture interest an expense or an appropriation of profit?
10. Which organisation continues unaffected when an owner dies?
11. What does a partnership agreement usually cover?
12. Where are retained earnings shown?

**Answers**

1. The owner's personal assets can be taken to pay the business's debts.
2. Any two: keeps all profit; quick decisions; easy to set up; privacy.
3. Any two: unlimited liability; profit shared; disagreements; bound by other partners' decisions.
4. Investors know they can lose only what they pay for shares, so more people are willing to invest.
5. Any one: loss of control as shares are issued; more rules and cost; profit shared with more shareholders.
6. The partner's current account (credit).
7. Any two: owners versus lenders; dividends optional versus interest compulsory; not normally repaid versus repaid; voting rights versus none; equity versus liability.
8. 6% × 30,000 = 1,800.
9. An expense.
10. A limited company.
11. Profit sharing ratio, interest on capital, partnership salaries, interest on drawings and how much each partner contributes.
12. In equity in the statement of financial position, with share capital.
