**Case A: Orvelle Sleep.** Orvelle makes mid-priced mattresses sold through furniture stores. Two large rivals run automated factories and sell at lower prices. Orvelle's managers are considering repositioning as a premium brand: hand-finished springs, natural fillings, a ten-year guarantee, and sales through its own showrooms and website. Incomes in its home market are rising slowly, and more buyers now research mattresses online before buying.

**Case B: Kestrun Express.** Kestrun runs intercity coaches on one busy route. Its newest coaches have reclining seats and Wi-Fi, and it has the best on-time record on the route. A passenger survey scored each operator's comfort out of 10.

| Operator | Fare per trip | Comfort score |
|---|---|---|
| Kestrun Express | £31 | 8.2 |
| Marrowind Travel | £29 | 6.4 |
| Stoatley Coaches | £24 | 6.0 |
| Hopwell Coaches | £18 | 4.5 |

## Questions

**1.** State the purpose of market mapping. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** benefits to a business of having a competitive advantage. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why a business following a low cost strategy does not have to charge the lowest price in its market. **[3]**

**4.** Use the Case B table.

**(a)** Calculate the fare per comfort point for each operator, to 2 decimal places. **[3]**
**(b)** Using your answers, identify **one** operator that looks vulnerable and **one** gap in the market. **[2]**

**5.** Orvelle currently sells 9,000 mattresses a year at £520, with a variable cost of £310 per mattress and fixed costs of £1,400,000. If it repositions, it expects to sell 6,500 a year at £760, with a variable cost of £430 and fixed costs of £1,650,000.

**(a)** Calculate Orvelle's current annual profit. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate its expected annual profit after repositioning. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage change in profit. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **two** influences on whether Orvelle should choose a differentiation strategy. **[4]**

**7.** Analyse the implications of the premium repositioning for Orvelle's operations and human resources. **[6]**

**8.** Explain why a business offering the same or fewer benefits than rivals at a higher price is unlikely to keep its customers. **[3]**

**9.** Kestrun's comfort features allow it to charge £4.50 more per trip than it could otherwise charge, and it carries 120,000 passengers a year. Marrowind then buys new coaches with reclining seats and Wi-Fi. Kestrun's premium falls to £2.00, although passengers rise to 126,000 as the route grows.

**(a)** Calculate the annual revenue Kestrun earned from its premium before Marrowind's change. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the revenue from the premium afterwards and the percentage fall. **[3]**

**10.** Using Case A and your answers to questions 5 and 6, evaluate whether Orvelle should reposition as a premium mattress brand. **[12]**

**11.** Using Case B and your answers to questions 4 and 9, evaluate how far Kestrun Express can maintain its competitive advantage. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** A market map shows where competing brands or businesses are positioned on two features that matter to customers [1], so that a business can identify its closest rivals and any gaps to target when planning its own position [1].
*Examiner insight:* A definition of what a map looks like, with no purpose, answers only half the question; add what a manager would use it for.

**2.** Any two, one mark each: higher margins and profit; customer loyalty and steadier sales; greater bargaining power with suppliers or retailers; retained profit to reinvest; a stronger base for growth [1] [1].
*Examiner insight:* On a "state" question, two distinct benefits are needed; "more profit" and "more revenue" are close enough that they may be treated as one point.

**3.** A low cost strategy means having lower costs than rivals for a similar product [1]. The business can choose to match rivals' prices instead of undercutting them [1], which gives it a wider margin per unit and avoids starting a price war that would reduce profits for everyone [1].
*Examiner insight:* The key is separating cost from price; answers that treat them as the same thing miss the point the question is testing.

**4. (a)** Fare ÷ comfort score for each operator [1]. Kestrun 31 ÷ 8.2 = **£3.78**; Marrowind 29 ÷ 6.4 = **£4.53** [1]; Stoatley 24 ÷ 6.0 = **£4.00**; Hopwell 18 ÷ 4.5 = **£4.00** [1].
**(b)** Marrowind looks vulnerable: it charges almost as much as Kestrun but scores much lower, so it gives the worst value per point [1]. Gap: no operator offers a comfort score of 7 or more for a fare in the low to mid twenties [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the division for each operator; a list of final figures with no method leaves nothing to credit if one value is wrong.

**5. (a)** (520 − 310) × 9,000 − 1,400,000 [1] = 1,890,000 − 1,400,000 = **£490,000** [1].
**(b)** (760 − 430) × 6,500 − 1,650,000 [1] = 2,145,000 − 1,650,000 = **£495,000** [1].
**(c)** (495,000 − 490,000) ÷ 490,000 × 100 [1] = **1.0% rise** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Remember to deduct the higher fixed costs after repositioning; comparing contribution alone overstates the gain.

**6.** Any two influences, each identified and then explained in Orvelle's context: **customers**: incomes are rising only slowly, so many buyers may stay price-sensitive and not pay £760 [1] [1]; **competitors**: the two automated rivals have lower costs, so Orvelle cannot beat them on cost and differentiation avoids a contest it would lose [1] [1]; **resources and finance**: showrooms and hand-finishing need £250,000 more fixed costs a year, which Orvelle must be able to fund. Credit any two, up to four marks.
*Examiner insight:* Generic influences such as "competition" gain little until they are tied to evidence in the case, such as the rivals' automated factories.

**7.** **Operations:** hand-finished springs and natural fillings need slower, more skilled production and tighter quality assurance [1], so capacity per worker falls and unit variable cost rises from £310 to £430 [1]; the ten-year guarantee means faults are costly, so quality must be built in, not inspected at the end [1]. **Human resources:** Orvelle needs skilled craft workers, which may mean retraining current staff or recruiting [1]; skilled staff may need higher pay and stronger retention policies, as losing them would weaken the product [1]; showroom staff must be trained to explain the product's benefits to customers [1].
*Examiner insight:* For each function, link the change to its effect on cost, quality or staff; an unconnected list of changes rarely reaches the higher levels on an "analyse" question.

**8.** Customers compare what they get for the price [1]. If rivals offer at least the same benefits for less, customers gain nothing by paying more and switch [1]. Such a position only survives where customers lack choice or information, for example a sole supplier in a remote area, and that rarely lasts [1].
*Examiner insight:* Strong answers mention the exception (little competition or poor information), which shows understanding beyond the basic idea.

**9. (a)** 4.50 × 120,000 [1] = **£540,000** [1].
**(b)** 2.00 × 126,000 = **£252,000** [1]; fall = 540,000 − 252,000 = £288,000 [1]; 288,000 ÷ 540,000 × 100 = **53.3%** [1].
*Examiner insight:* State which figure is the base for the percentage; dividing by the new value instead of the original gives a different, wrong answer.

**10.** *For repositioning:* profit rises slightly, from £490,000 to £495,000 [1], because contribution per mattress rises from £210 to £330 [1], so fewer sales are needed: break-even falls from about 6,667 to 5,000 mattresses [1]. Orvelle cannot match the automated rivals on cost, so a differentiated position avoids a price war it would lose [1].
*Against:* the gain is only 1.0%, so the extra risk earns very little [1]; the margin of safety falls from 25.9% to 23.1%, so profit is more exposed if sales disappoint [1]; if sales reach only 6,000, profit would be £330,000, well below today's [1]; slowly rising incomes may limit how many buyers pay £760, and showrooms and a brand take time and money to build [1].
*Judgement:* On these forecasts the financial case is weak, because the change in profit is small and the downside is large [1]. The decision turns on whether customers will value hand-finished, natural mattresses enough to hit 6,500 sales [1]. Orvelle could test demand first, for example selling a premium line online alongside its current range [1], before committing to showrooms; if early sales confirm demand, full repositioning becomes a stronger choice [1].
*Examiner insight:* Use the calculated figures in the evaluation; a judgement that rests on numbers you have already worked out is more convincing than one that ignores them.

**11.** *Reasons it can maintain the advantage:* Kestrun still has the best value on the route at £3.78 per comfort point [1] and the best on-time record, which depends on operating skill and is harder to copy than equipment [1]; the route is growing, so passengers rose to 126,000 even after Marrowind's upgrade [1]; it can reinvest profit in new features to stay ahead [1].
*Reasons it may not:* reclining seats and Wi-Fi can be bought by any rival, so the advantage was always easy to imitate [1]; revenue from the premium fell 53.3%, from £540,000 to £252,000, showing how fast it eroded [1]; Stoatley and Hopwell may upgrade too, and an entrant could target the gap for high comfort at mid-range fares [1].
*Judgement:* The equipment-based part of the advantage is fading and cannot be protected [1]. Kestrun's lasting edge is reliability and brand, so it should invest there, for example in punctuality guarantees or loyalty rewards [1]. It can keep an advantage, but a smaller one, unless it keeps innovating faster than rivals copy [1].
*Examiner insight:* Separate advantages that can be bought from those built on skills or reputation; that distinction is what lets a judgement go beyond "it depends".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a market map without saying what decision it supports.
- Treating low cost and low price as identical.
- Forgetting fixed costs when comparing the profit from two positions.
- Listing functional changes without linking cause and effect to the case.
- Claiming an advantage will last without asking how easily rivals could copy it.
- Ending an evaluation without a clear, case-specific decision and the factor it depends on.
