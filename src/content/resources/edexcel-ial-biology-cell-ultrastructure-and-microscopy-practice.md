## Questions

**1.** (a) State two features that are common to all living cells. **[2]**
**(b)** A student writes "the heart is a tissue." Correct this statement by naming the level of organisation and two tissues found in the heart. **[2]**

**2.** Name the eukaryotic organelle described in each case.
**(a)** A sac with a single membrane containing hydrolytic enzymes. **[1]**
**(b)** The dense region inside the nucleus where ribosomal RNA is made. **[1]**
**(c)** Two short hollow cylinders at right angles to each other, involved in forming the spindle. **[1]**
**(d)** Tubular membranes with no ribosomes, involved in lipid synthesis. **[1]**

**3.** Cells from a gland were given radioactive amino acids for 2 minutes and then transferred to a medium with non-radioactive amino acids. The table shows where the radioactive protein was found, as a percentage of the total radioactive protein.

| Time after the start (min) | rER | Golgi apparatus | Secretory vesicles | Outside the cell |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 90 | 8 | 2 | 0 |
| 10 | 52 | 34 | 13 | 1 |
| 20 | 18 | 28 | 44 | 10 |
| 40 | 6 | 8 | 30 | 56 |

**(a)** State the time at which the Golgi apparatus held the greatest percentage of radioactive protein. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the change in the percentage of radioactive protein in the rER. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why radioactive protein appeared in the Golgi apparatus before it appeared in secretory vesicles in large amounts. **[2]**
**(d)** State how the protein leaves the cell. **[1]**

**4.** (a) State the function of each of these structures in a bacterial cell: the capsule, the plasmid and the pili. **[3]**
**(b)** State two differences between the DNA of a bacterium and the DNA of a human liver cell. **[2]**

**5.** A transmission electron micrograph of a liver cell shows structure X, an oval about 1.5 micrometres long with a double boundary whose inner layer is folded inwards, and structure Y, a stack of flattened sacs with small round vesicles at one edge.
**(a)** Identify X and Y, and state one function of each. **[4]**
**(b)** The same type of organelle looks circular in one micrograph and long and thin in another. Explain why. **[2]**

**6.** A photomicrograph of a cell is 63 mm long. The real cell is 15 micrometres long. Calculate the magnification. **[3]**

**7.** An electron micrograph has a scale bar that is 30 mm long and represents 5 micrometres.
**(a)** Work out the magnification of this micrograph. **[2]**
**(b)** A vesicle measures 48 mm across on the micrograph. Calculate its real diameter in micrometres. **[2]**

**8.** (a) Distinguish between magnification and resolution. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why an electron microscope can show more detail than a light microscope. **[2]**

**9.** (a) Explain why a specimen of animal cells is stained before being viewed with a light microscope. **[2]**
**(b)** State two reasons why living cells cannot be viewed in an electron microscope. **[2]**

**10.** A student calibrates an eyepiece graticule using a stage micrometer with divisions of 10 micrometres. At low power, 100 graticule divisions line up with 28 stage divisions.
**(a)** Calculate the length in micrometres of one graticule division. **[2]**
**(b)** A cell is 14 graticule divisions long. Calculate its length. **[2]**
**(c)** The student changes to an objective lens that is four times as strong. State and explain the length of one graticule division now. **[2]**
**(d)** The student draws the cell from (b) with a length of 98 mm. Calculate the magnification of the drawing. **[1]**

**11.** A student makes a labelled drawing of cheek cells seen with a light microscope. Describe four features of a good drawing. **[4]**

**12.** A transmission electron micrograph shows a pancreatic cell that secretes digestive enzymes. The scale bar is 20 mm long and represents 1 micrometre. A mitochondrion in the cell measures 24 mm long on the micrograph. The cell has extensive rER and many mitochondria.
**(a)** Calculate the magnification of this image. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the real length of the mitochondrion in micrometres. **[2]**
**(c)** List, in order, the organelles and structures a digestive enzyme passes through from its synthesis to its release. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain why this cell contains extensive rER and many mitochondria. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Any two from: cell surface membrane; DNA as genetic material; cytoplasm where metabolic reactions occur; ribosomes. One mark each: [1] [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The heart is an organ [1], made of tissues such as cardiac muscle and connective tissue (or epithelium) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the level and then two named tissues gives a complete correction; writing only "it is made of different cells" does not use the hierarchy the question asks for.

**2. (a)** Lysosome. [1]
**(b)** Nucleolus. [1]
**(c)** Centrioles. [1]
**(d)** Smooth endoplasmic reticulum (sER). [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the full name of the organelle; "ER" alone does not show whether you mean the rough or the smooth form.

**3. (a)** **10 minutes** [1]
**(b)** It falls [1] from 90% at 2 minutes to 6% at 40 minutes, with the fall being steepest early on (for example 90 to 52 between 2 and 10 minutes) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The protein is made on the rER and then moves in vesicles to the Golgi apparatus [1], where it is modified and packaged into secretory vesicles, which takes more time [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** By exocytosis, when secretory vesicles fuse with the cell surface membrane [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* In a data question, quote figures from the table with their times; a description that only says "decreases" tends to gain less than one that includes values.

**4. (a)** Capsule: protects the cell or helps it stick to surfaces [1]. Plasmid: carries extra genes, for example antibiotic resistance [1]. Pili: attach to surfaces or other cells (some transfer DNA) [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Bacterial DNA is circular, human DNA is linear [1]. Bacterial DNA lies free in the cytoplasm, not in a nucleus enclosed by a nuclear envelope [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both sides stated. "Circular" on its own leaves the human cell unmentioned, so write each difference as a pair.

**5. (a)** X is a mitochondrion [1]; it makes ATP in aerobic respiration [1]. Y is a Golgi apparatus [1]; it modifies and packages proteins [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** A micrograph shows a thin section [1], so the organelle is cut at different angles, giving a circle when cut across and a long shape when cut along its length [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* For recognition questions, quote the identifying feature (double boundary with cristae folds, stack of sacs with vesicles) and then give the function; naming alone is rarely enough when the question asks for both.

**6.** Convert 63 mm to micrometres: 63 × 1000 = 63 000 [1]. Magnification = image ÷ real = 63 000 ÷ 15 [1]. **×4200** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* If you divide 63 by 15 without converting units you get 4.2, a value that is clearly wrong for an electron or light micrograph; checking for sense is a quick safeguard.

**7. (a)** Bar length 30 mm = 30 000 micrometres; magnification = 30 000 ÷ 5 [1] = **×6000** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Real size = 48 ÷ 6000 mm [1] = 0.008 mm = **8.0 micrometres** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the conversion step. An answer left in mm or in the wrong unit shows the method but is easy to lose the final result on.

**8. (a)** Magnification is the enlargement factor of the image compared with the object [1]; resolution is the least separation at which two points are still seen as two [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Electrons have a much shorter wavelength than light [1], so the electron microscope has a higher resolution and shows finer detail (ultrastructure) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Link wavelength to resolution explicitly. A statement that an electron microscope "magnifies more" does not answer a question about detail.

**9. (a)** Cell structures are almost transparent, with little contrast [1]; a stain binds to particular structures and makes them visible [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Any two from: the specimen is in a vacuum; the specimen is cut as a thin section and treated with fixatives and heavy metal stains; the beam of electrons would damage the cells. One mark each: [1] [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Give two separate reasons. Repeating "they would die" twice in different words is one point.

**10. (a)** 28 stage divisions × 10 = 280 micrometres [1]; 280 ÷ 100 = **2.8 micrometres** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** 14 × 2.8 [1] = **39.2 micrometres** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** **0.7 micrometres** [1], because the objective is four times as strong, so each division covers a quarter of the distance: 2.8 ÷ 4 [1] **[2]**
**(d)** 98 mm = 98 000 micrometres; 98 000 ÷ 39.2 = **×2500** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Hold the unrounded value until the last line; using 39 in place of 39.2 would shift the final magnification to about 2513.

**11.** Any four from: drawn in pencil with clear, continuous lines; no shading or colouring; large drawing, with only a few cells drawn, showing the outline and structures seen; label lines drawn with a ruler and not crossing; label lines touching the structure; a title; the magnification, or a scale bar, included. One mark each: [1] [1] [1] [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Be specific about what you do. "Draw neatly" is too vague to gain credit, whereas "no shading" and "label lines do not cross" are checkable.

**12. (a)** 20 mm = 20 000 micrometres; magnification = 20 000 ÷ 1 [1] = **×20 000** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** 24 ÷ 20 000 mm [1] = 0.0012 mm = **1.2 micrometres** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Ribosomes on the rER (translation) [1]; transport vesicle to the Golgi apparatus, which modifies and packages the protein [1]; secretory vesicle that fuses with the cell surface membrane (exocytosis) [1]. **[3]**
**(d)** The cell makes large quantities of protein, so it needs extensive rER to fold and process it [1]. Protein synthesis, vesicle movement and exocytosis all need ATP [1], so many mitochondria supply this by aerobic respiration [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each structure to a process: "extensive rER, so more protein made" gains credit where "it is a secretory cell" alone does not.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming "ER" without saying rough or smooth.
- Stating that mitochondria "make energy" rather than ATP.
- Forgetting to convert millimetres to micrometres before dividing.
- Giving a magnification with a unit; it is written as a multiple such as ×4200.
- Writing that the Golgi apparatus makes proteins; ribosomes make them.
- Describing data without quoting figures from the table.
- Saying prokaryotes have no DNA, rather than circular DNA outside a nucleus.
- Drawings with shading, crossing label lines or no magnification.
- Using one graticule calibration for every objective lens.
