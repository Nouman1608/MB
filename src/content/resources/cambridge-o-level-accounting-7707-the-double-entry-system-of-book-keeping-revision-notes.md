Condensed recall for **2.1 The double entry system of book-keeping**, Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707, 2027-2029 syllabus. Covers: the double entry system; preparing ledger accounts; posting transactions; balances and transfers to financial statements; interpreting accounts; three-column running balance format; division of the ledger; digital ledgers. Folio columns are not required. Figures below are invented; amounts in dollars.

## The rule

Every transaction: **one debit, one credit, same amount**. In each account the details column names the **other** account.

| Account type | Increase | Decrease |
|---|---|---|
| Assets, expenses, drawings | Debit | Credit |
| Liabilities, capital, income | Credit | Debit |

## Common entries

| Transaction | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Owner pays capital into bank | Bank | Capital |
| Goods for resale bought on credit | Purchases | Supplier |
| Goods sold on credit | Customer | Sales |
| Cash sales | Cash (or bank) | Sales |
| Goods returned by a customer | Sales returns | Customer |
| Goods returned to a supplier | Supplier | Purchases returns |
| Customer pays by bank transfer | Bank | Customer |
| Supplier paid by bank transfer | Supplier | Bank |
| Expense paid by bank | Expense (e.g. rent) | Bank |
| Equipment bought by bank | Equipment | Bank |
| Owner takes cash | Drawings | Cash |
| Loan received | Bank | Loan |

## Balancing in four steps

1. Total each side.
2. Difference on the **smaller** side as **Balance c/d**.
3. Totals level with each other.
4. **Balance b/d** on the **opposite** side, dated the next day.

**Quick figures.** Debits 4,500 + 1,200 = 5,700; credits 2,900. Balance c/d 2,800 on the credit side; balance b/d **2,800 Dr**.

## Reading balances

| Balance | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Debit on a customer account | Trade receivable: the customer owes the business |
| Credit on a supplier account | Trade payable: the business owes the supplier |
| Debit on bank | Money in the bank |
| Credit on bank | Overdraft |
| Debit on an expense account | Total expense so far |
| Credit on sales | Total sales so far |

## Year-end transfers

- **Income and expenses** (sales, purchases, returns, wages, rent): closed by transfer to the **statement of profit or loss**.
- **Assets, liabilities, capital**: balanced and carried down; shown in the **statement of financial position**.
- An expense account's transfer is on its **credit** side; the sales account's transfer is on its **debit** side.

## Three-column running balance format

| Date | Details | Debit | Credit | Balance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Balance | | | 800 Dr |
| 4 | Wages | | 1,100 | 300 Cr |
| 9 | Customer | 1,500 | | 1,200 Dr |

Update the balance after every entry and mark it **Dr** or **Cr**. Same final balance as the two-sided account.

## Division of the ledger

| Ledger | Accounts |
|---|---|
| Sales ledger | Credit customers (trade receivables) |
| Purchases ledger | Credit suppliers (trade payables) |
| Nominal (general) ledger | Everything else: sales, purchases, returns, expenses, assets, liabilities, capital, drawings |

Benefits: work can be shared between staff; accounts are easier to find; errors are easier to trace.

## Digital ledgers

- One input posts both sides; balances update automatically; reports are instant.
- Same rules apply; wrong account or missing entries are **not** detected automatically.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ledger | The book or file containing the accounts |
| Debit / credit | Left / right side of an account |
| Balance c/d / b/d | Carried down / brought down |
| Personal account | Account of a customer or supplier |
| Nominal account | Account for income, expenses, assets, liabilities, capital |
| Running balance | Balance updated after every entry |

## Exam traps

- **Capital is credit.** Bank debit, capital credit.
- **Purchases, not inventory**, for goods bought for resale.
- **Balance b/d on the opposite side** from c/d.
- **Returns have their own accounts.** Do not reduce sales or purchases directly.
- **Dr/Cr in running balances.** A bank balance in Cr is an overdraft.
- **Transfer only income and expenses** to the statement of profit or loss.

## Self-test

1. Which account is debited when a customer pays by bank transfer?
2. Give the double entry for goods returned to a supplier.
3. Which side records an increase in a liability?
4. Debits total $6,300 and credits $4,100. State the balance b/d and its side.
5. What does a credit balance on the bank account mean?
6. What does a debit balance on R Khan's account in the sales ledger mean?
7. Which accounts are transferred to the statement of profit or loss at the year end?
8. Where does the balance on the loan account appear?
9. In a running balance bank account the balance is $400 Dr and a payment of $900 is made. What is the new balance?
10. Name the ledger for: a supplier's account; the wages account; a customer's account.
11. Give one advantage of keeping ledgers digitally.
12. Give one error a digital ledger will not detect.

**Answers:** 1. Bank (credit the customer). 2. Debit the supplier, credit purchases returns. 3. Credit. 4. $2,200 debit balance b/d (balance c/d on the credit side). 5. A bank overdraft. 6. R Khan owes the business that amount. 7. Income and expense accounts, such as sales, purchases, returns, wages and rent. 8. In the statement of financial position, as a liability. 9. $500 Cr (overdraft). 10. Purchases ledger; nominal (general) ledger; sales ledger. 11. Any one: both sides posted from one input; automatic balances; instant reports; easy back-up. 12. Any one: posting to the wrong account; a transaction left out; a wrong amount keyed in.
