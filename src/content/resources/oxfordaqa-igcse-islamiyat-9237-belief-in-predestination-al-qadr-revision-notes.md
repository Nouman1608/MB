**These notes condense content area 3.1.1.5 of 9237: "the meaning, importance and influence of beliefs about predestination for Muslims".** The full explanation is in the [Belief in predestination (Al-Qadr) study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-belief-in-predestination-al-qadr/).

## Definition

**Al-Qadr** is the belief that Allah knows all things before they happen, has written them, wills them and creates them. Nothing happens outside His knowledge and permission. Humans still choose and are responsible.

- Qur'an 54:49: "Indeed, all things We created with predestination."
- Qur'an 57:22: events are "in a register before We bring it into being".
- Hadith of Jibril (Muslim): belief in "the divine decree, its good and its bad".

## Four levels

| Level | Word | One line |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Ilm* (knowledge) | Allah knows everything |
| 2 | *Kitabah* (writing) | It is recorded in the Preserved Tablet |
| 3 | *Mashee'ah* (will) | Nothing happens unless Allah wills |
| 4 | *Khalq* (creation) | Allah creates all things and means |

**Memory aid:** **K**nows, **W**rites, **W**ills, **C**reates.

## Free will and justice: the key answers

1. Knowing is not forcing.
2. The Qur'an tells people to choose (18:29).
3. Allah does not burden a soul beyond ability (2:286).
4. Muslims use the means: "Tie it and trust in Allah" (Tirmidhi).
5. People are judged by choices, so they must be free to choose.

**Historical positions (very brief):** Jabriyya stressed decree over choice; Qadariyya stressed choice over prior decree; mainstream Sunni teaching holds both, saying Allah creates all things and humans acquire their actions by choice; Shi'a teaching stresses divine justice ('Adl) and human freedom.

## The passage: Sahih al-Bukhari 1362

| Detail | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Funeral at Baqi' al-Gharqad | Death makes the lesson urgent |
| The Prophet (pbuh) scratches the ground | He stops the group and teaches |
| "A place in Paradise or Hell assigned" | Allah's knowledge includes each person's end |
| The man: "Should we not depend on what has been written?" | The question every learner asks |
| "Good deeds are made easy for the blessed..." | Act; effort is part of the decree |
| The verses (92:5-6): "gives in charity, is Allah-fearing, believes in the Best reward" | What to do now: give, be conscious of Allah, believe |

**Key message:** do not use the decree as an excuse; act as someone who hopes for Paradise.

## Why it is important

- Completes faith (the sixth key belief).
- Protects Tawhid: Allah alone controls events.
- Explains hardship as a test (2:155).
- Gives security and calm: what was meant to reach you will reach you.

## Influence on Muslims

| Event | Response |
|---|---|
| Hardship | Patience (*sabr*); "Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un" (2:156) |
| Success | Gratitude (*shukr*); no pride |
| Failure | No despair; try again |
| Planning | Effort first, then *tawakkul* |
| Wrongdoing | No excuse; repent |

**Umar (RA) and the plague:** Umar (RA) turned back from a land struck by plague. "We flee from the decree of Allah to the decree of Allah." Taking precautions is part of the belief.

## Exam traps

- Do not write "Muslims believe in fate so they do nothing". They believe in effort and trust.
- Do not say Allah makes people sin.
- Show both parts: the decree and human responsibility.
- Do not mix up Qadr (decree) with Qada (decision); use "Al-Qadr" as the specification does.
- Use a quotation from the Hadith, not just a summary.

## Model answer shapes

- **2 marks "Give two ways belief in Al-Qadr affects a Muslim":** patience in hardship; gratitude in success.
- **4 marks "Explain why Al-Qadr is important":** It completes belief in Allah's power and knowledge, since nothing is outside His knowledge [1 and 1]. It brings comfort in difficulty, because Muslims believe the hardship is a test within Allah's wisdom [1 and 1].
- **6 marks:** two developed points plus a source (Bukhari 1362, 2:286 or 54:49).
- **12 marks:** structure as support (importance), challenge (does it remove responsibility?), reply (the Hadith: deeds made easy), judgement.

## A model 6-mark paragraph

**Question:** Explain **two** ways in which belief in Al-Qadr influences a Muslim. Apply **one** teaching. **[6]**

Belief in Al-Qadr makes a Muslim patient when facing hardship [1]. If a family member is ill or a business fails, they say "Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un" and trust that the event is within Allah's knowledge and wisdom, so they do not give way to despair [1]. It also makes a Muslim take responsibility for effort [1], because they are told to use the means available, such as seeing a doctor or studying, and then to rely on Allah for the result [1]. In the Hadith from Sahih al-Bukhari, the Prophet (pbuh) tells his companions that deeds are made easy for what a person was created for [1], which means that belief in the decree should push people to act, not to wait [1].

**Why it scores:** two separate influences, each followed by a reason or example, and a source that supports the second.

## Quick comparison: fatalism and Al-Qadr

| Fatalism (not the Muslim position) | Al-Qadr (the Muslim position) |
|---|---|
| Everything is fixed, so choice is an illusion | Allah's knowledge is complete, and choice is real |
| No point in effort | Effort is commanded and rewarded |
| Blame fate for wrongdoing | Take responsibility and repent |
| Passive acceptance | Patient acceptance after effort |

## Self-test

1. What does Al-Qadr mean?
2. Name the four levels.
3. Which verse says that "all things We created with predestination"?
4. What did the man ask in the Hadith of Bukhari 1362?
5. How did the Prophet (pbuh) answer?
6. Why does belief in Al-Qadr not remove responsibility?
7. Give two ways the belief affects behaviour.
8. What does *tawakkul* mean?
9. What did Umar (RA) say about fleeing from the decree?
10. What is the Preserved Tablet?

**Answers:** 1. The decree: Allah's knowledge, writing, will and creation of all things. 2. Knowledge, writing, will, creation. 3. Qur'an 54:49. 4. Whether people should rely on what is written and leave their deeds. 5. Good deeds are made easy for the blessed and bad deeds for the wretched; he recited the verses about giving and being Allah-fearing. 6. Because Allah's knowledge is not compulsion, humans are told to choose, and Allah does not burden a soul beyond its ability. 7. Any two: patience; gratitude; no despair; effort and trust; calm about the future. 8. Trust in Allah after making effort. 9. "We flee from the decree of Allah to the decree of Allah." 10. The record in which Allah has written all things (Al-Lawh al-Mahfuz).
