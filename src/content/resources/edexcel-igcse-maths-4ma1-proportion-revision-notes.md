## What the specification asks (2.5A)

- Set up problems involving **direct** or **inverse** proportion.
- **Relate algebraic solutions to graphical representation** of the equations.
- Only these forms are included: y ∝ x, y ∝ 1/x, y ∝ x², y ∝ 1/x², y ∝ x³, y ∝ 1/x³, y ∝ √x, y ∝ 1/√x.

Foundation statement 1.7D (unknown quantities in direct proportion, missing values in a table) is assumed knowledge.

## Key words

- **∝** means "is proportional to".
- **Directly proportional** / **varies directly as** / **varies as**: y = k × (power of x).
- **Inversely proportional** / **varies inversely as**: y = k ÷ (power of x).
- **Constant of proportionality**: the number k. It is the same for every pair of values.

## Formulae

| In words | Symbols | Formula | Constant check |
|---|---|---|---|
| y is directly proportional to x | y ∝ x | y = kx | y/x = k |
| y is proportional to the square of x | y ∝ x² | y = kx² | y/x² = k |
| y is proportional to the cube of x | y ∝ x³ | y = kx³ | y/x³ = k |
| y is proportional to the square root of x | y ∝ √x | y = k√x | y/√x = k |
| y is inversely proportional to x | y ∝ 1/x | y = k/x | xy = k |
| y is inversely proportional to the square of x | y ∝ 1/x² | y = k/x² | x²y = k |
| y is inversely proportional to the cube of x | y ∝ 1/x³ | y = k/x³ | x³y = k |
| y is inversely proportional to the square root of x | y ∝ 1/√x | y = k/√x | y√x = k |

None of these is on the Higher tier formulae sheet.

## Method in steps

> **Finding and using a proportion formula**
> 1. Write the formula with k (pick the power or root from the wording).
> 2. Substitute the given pair of values.
> 3. Solve for k, then rewrite the formula with the number in place of k.
> 4. Substitute the new value, or rearrange to find the unknown.

> **Undoing the power when finding x**
> - y = kx²: divide by k, then square-root.
> - y = kx³: divide by k, then cube-root.
> - y = k√x: divide by k, then square.
> - y = k/x²: multiply by x², divide by y, then square-root.

### Worked reminder

P is inversely proportional to √Q. P = 6 when Q = 25. Find P when Q = 36.

```
P = k/√Q
6 = k/5   so   k = 30
P = 30/√Q = 30/6 = 5
```

### Worked reminder: from a graph

A graph of y against x has the shape of y ∝ x² and passes through the point (2, 12). Find y when x = 4.

```
y = kx²
12 = k × 2² = 4k   so   k = 3
y = 3x² = 3 × 16 = 48
```

Pick a point where both coordinates can be read exactly, ideally one marked on the graph.

### Worked reminder: no k needed

y is inversely proportional to x. When x = 3, y = 20. Find y when x = 12, without finding k.

```
x is multiplied by 12 ÷ 3 = 4
y ∝ 1/x, so y is divided by 4
y = 20 ÷ 4 = 5
```

Finding k (k = 60, y = 60/12 = 5) gives the same answer. Use whichever you are less likely to get wrong.

## Scale factors

If y ∝ xⁿ and x is multiplied by c, y is multiplied by cⁿ. If y ∝ 1/xⁿ, y is divided by cⁿ.

| Form | Power n | x × 2 | x × 10 | x × 1.1 (10% rise) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| y ∝ x | 1 | y × 2 | y × 10 | y × 1.1 |
| y ∝ x² | 2 | y × 4 | y × 100 | y × 1.21 |
| y ∝ x³ | 3 | y × 8 | y × 1000 | y × 1.331 |
| y ∝ √x | ½ | y × √2 | y × √10 | y × √1.1 |
| y ∝ 1/x² | −2 | y ÷ 4 | y ÷ 100 | y ÷ 1.21 |

Percentage change: convert to a multiplier, raise it to the power, then convert back. A 10% rise in x with y ∝ x³ gives y × 1.331, a **33.1%** rise.

## Graphs: must-know shapes

With k > 0 and x > 0:

- **y ∝ x**: straight line through the origin, gradient k.
- **y ∝ x²** and **y ∝ x³**: curves through the origin that get steeper.
- **y ∝ √x**: curve through the origin that gets less steep.
- **All four inverse forms**: curves that decrease, approaching both axes without touching them.

Reading a graph: any point on it satisfies the formula, so substitute one clear point to find k.

Straight-line test: y ∝ x² gives a straight line through the origin if you plot y against x² (gradient k). The same holds for each form with the matching power on the x-axis.

## Must-know distinctions

| | Direct proportion | Inverse proportion |
|---|---|---|
| Formula | y = k × xⁿ | y = k ÷ xⁿ |
| As x increases (k > 0) | y increases | y decreases |
| What stays constant | y ÷ xⁿ | y × xⁿ |
| Graph through origin? | Yes | No, the graph never meets either axis |

- **"Proportional to the square of x"** (y = kx²) is not the same as **"the square of y is proportional to x"** (y² = kx). The specification only lists forms with y on its own, so read which quantity is squared.
- **A straight line not through the origin** (such as y = 3x + 2) is a linear relationship but **not** direct proportion.
- **k is a constant**: if your value of k changes between two pairs of data, the relationship is not the one you assumed.

## Quick self-test

1. y is directly proportional to x. y = 14 when x = 4. Find y when x = 10.
2. y ∝ x². y = 48 when x = 4. Find y when x = 5.
3. y is inversely proportional to x. y = 6 when x = 5. Find y when x = 12.
4. y ∝ 1/x². y = 10 when x = 2. Find x when y = 0.4, given x > 0.
5. y is proportional to the cube of x. y = 54 when x = 3. Find y when x = 5.
6. y ∝ √x. y = 10 when x = 4. Find x when y = 35.
7. y ∝ 1/x³. y = 2 when x = 2. Find y when x = 4.
8. y ∝ x². x is doubled. What happens to y?
9. y ∝ 1/√x. x is multiplied by 9. What happens to y?
10. Which of the eight forms has a straight-line graph through the origin when y is plotted against x?
11. y ∝ x³. x is increased by 10%. Find the percentage increase in y.
12. The table shows x = 1, 2, 3 with y = 4, 16, 36. Write a formula for y in terms of x.

### Answers

1. k = 14/4 = 3.5, y = 3.5x, so **y = 35**.
2. k = 48/16 = 3, y = 3x², so **y = 75**.
3. k = 6 × 5 = 30, y = 30/x, so **y = 2.5**.
4. k = 10 × 4 = 40, so 0.4 = 40/x², x² = 100, **x = 10**.
5. k = 54/27 = 2, y = 2x³, so **y = 250**.
6. k = 10/2 = 5, so 35 = 5√x, √x = 7, **x = 49**.
7. k = 2 × 8 = 16, y = 16/x³, so **y = 16/64 = 0.25**.
8. **y is multiplied by 4** (2² = 4).
9. **y is divided by 3** (√9 = 3).
10. **y ∝ x** only.
11. 1.1³ = 1.331, so a **33.1%** increase.
12. y/x² = 4 every time, so **y = 4x²**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing y = x² + k, or y = kx + c, instead of y = kx². In proportion k multiplies and there is no added constant.
- Choosing y = k/x when the question says "inversely proportional to the **square** of x".
- Stopping at "k = 3" when the question asks for a formula for y in terms of x; write y = 3x².
- Leaving x² = 100 as the answer, or forgetting to square √x = 7 to get 49.
- Giving both ±10 when the context (length, time, speed) or the question says the value is positive.
- Adding percentages for a power: a 10% rise in x with y ∝ x² is a 21% rise in y, not 20%.
- Rounding k early, so later answers are slightly out. Keep exact values or use the calculator memory.
- Calling any curve through the origin "y ∝ x²" without checking whether it gets steeper (x², x³) or less steep (√x).
- Saying a straight line with a y-intercept shows direct proportion.
