All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Quillon Call Centre.

> **Case study: Quillon Call Centre**
>
> Quillon Call Centre answers customer calls for energy companies.
> It has just recruited 15 new advisers. At present 9% of the 20,000
> calls it handles each month contain errors, and each error costs
> Quillon $3 to put right. The manager believes that training could cut
> the error rate to 5%. She is comparing two options. Option 1 is an
> off-the-job course costing $500 per adviser, in which each adviser would
> be away from the phones for 2 days, with each day of lost work worth $90.
> Option 2 is on-the-job training, in which an experienced adviser would
> train each new adviser for 4 days at a cost of $100 a day, while the
> trainee's output would be $45 a day lower. Many advisers say that
> they were given no introduction to the company when they started.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an example of off-the-job training? **[1]**
- A. Shadowing an experienced colleague at work
- B. Attending a course at a college
- C. Being shown the fire exits on the first day
- D. Learning by doing the work alone

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a benefit of training to a business? **[1]**
- A. Higher productivity
- B. Lower motivation
- C. More mistakes
- D. A fall in quality

**3.** Define the term "induction". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** drawbacks of on-the-job training. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** way in which training could help a business to deal with changes in technology. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate the total cost of Option 1, including the lost output. **[3]**

**7.** Calculate the total cost of Option 2, including the trainee's lost output. **[3]**

**8.** Calculate how many fewer calls with errors there would be each month if the error rate fell from 9% to 5%, and the monthly saving. **[3]**

**9.** Calculate the annual saving from fewer errors, and state whether it is higher than the cost of the cheaper option. **[2]**

**10.** Explain **two** reasons why the advisers need induction training. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse **two** benefits to Quillon of training the new advisers. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate which method of training Quillon Call Centre should use for the new advisers. Use your answers to Questions 6 and 7. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **A** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The introduction of new employees [1] to the business, its rules, safety procedures and the job [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: the trainer's output falls [1]; the trainee works slowly at first [1]; the trainer may be a poor teacher or pass on bad habits [1]; mistakes may annoy customers [1]; training is limited to what the business already knows [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Training teaches employees to use new machines or software [1], so the business can adopt new technology quickly and without costly errors [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Fees = 15 x $500 = $7,500 [1]; lost output = 15 x 2 x $90 = $2,700 [1]; total = **$10,200** [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Trainer cost per adviser = 4 x $100 = $400 [1]; lost output per adviser = 4 x $45 = $180, so $580 each [1]; total = 15 x $580 = **$8,700** [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Errors now = 9% x 20,000 = 1,800 [1]; errors after training = 5% x 20,000 = 1,000, so 800 fewer [1]; saving = 800 x $3 = **$2,400** a month [1]. **[3]**

**9.** $2,400 x 12 = **$28,800** a year [1]; this is higher than the cost of Option 2, which is $8,700 [1]. **[2]**

**10.** **Understanding the business:** advisers said they had no introduction to the company [1], so they may not know its rules, systems or who to ask for help, which leads to mistakes [1].
**Settling in and retention:** new staff who do not feel welcome may leave early [1], which would waste the cost of recruiting them [1]. **[4]**

**11.** **Fewer errors:** training is expected to cut the error rate from 9% to 5% [1], saving 800 errors a month, or $2,400 a month ($28,800 a year) [1].
**Better customer service:** advisers who know how to deal with callers answer questions correctly [1], so energy companies who use Quillon are more satisfied and may renew their contracts [1].
**Motivation and retention:** trained advisers feel more confident and valued [1], so fewer leave and Quillon saves recruitment costs [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **Option 1 (off-the-job):** costs $10,200 [1]; a specialist course may teach skills and knowledge that Quillon's own staff do not have [1]; advisers may gain a qualification, which motivates them [1].
**Option 2 (on-the-job):** costs $8,700, which is $1,500 less [1]; the training is specific to Quillon's systems and starts straight away [1]; trainees earn while learning, since they are still handling some calls [1]; but the trainers' output falls and they may teach bad habits [1].
**Judgement:** Option 2 is probably better, because it is cheaper and the savings from fewer errors ($28,800 a year) exceed the cost of either option [1]. However, Quillon should start with a short induction and monitor the trainers, and could add an off-the-job course later for specialist skills [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting the lost output when working out the cost of Option 1.
- Calculating Option 2 as 4 x $100 only, and leaving out the trainee's lower output.
- Using 9% or 5% as the number of errors rather than percentages of 20,000 calls.
- Writing that on-the-job training is free.
- Choosing a method without using the costs and savings in the case study.
