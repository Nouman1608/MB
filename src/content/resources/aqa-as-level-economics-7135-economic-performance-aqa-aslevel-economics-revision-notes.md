These notes condense **3.2.3 Economic performance** of AQA AS Economics (7135), specification version 1.3, parts 3.2.3.1 to 3.2.3.5. The [3.2.3 study guide](/resources/aqa-as-level-economics-7135-economic-performance-aqa-aslevel-economics/) explains how the output gap ties the objectives together; the [3.2.3 practice questions](/resources/aqa-as-level-economics-7135-economic-performance-aqa-aslevel-economics-practice/) test each part. All figures are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Short-run growth | Rise in real national income, often using spare capacity |
| Long-run growth | Rise in productive capacity; LRAS and the PPC shift outwards |
| Trend rate of growth | Long-run average rate of growth of real output |
| Economic cycle | Fluctuations of real GDP around its trend |
| Positive output gap | Real GDP above productive potential |
| Negative output gap | Real GDP below productive potential |
| Claimant count | Number claiming unemployment-related benefits |
| Labour Force Survey measure | Survey count of people without work, available and actively seeking work |
| Frictional unemployment | Between jobs |
| Structural unemployment | Mismatch of skills or location after industries decline |
| Cyclical unemployment | Too little aggregate demand |
| Seasonal unemployment | Jobs available only at some times of year |
| Demand-pull inflation | AD growing faster than capacity |
| Cost-push inflation | Rising costs shifting SRAS left |
| Deflation | Falling general price level |
| Disinflation | Falling rate of inflation, prices still rising |
| Primary income | Income from investments and employment across borders |
| Secondary income | Transfers such as aid and remittances |

## 3.2.3.1 Growth and the cycle

- Determinants of short-run growth: demand side (C, I, G, X - M). Long-run trend: supply side (productivity, capital, labour force, technology).
- PPC: short-run growth is a move from inside towards the curve; long-run growth shifts the curve out. AD/AS: AD right with spare capacity, or LRAS right.
- Phases: boom, downturn, recession, recovery. Use real GDP, inflation, unemployment and investment together.

```
Output gap % = (actual - potential) / potential x 100
Actual 3,120, potential 3,000 -> +4%
Actual 2,910, potential 3,000 -> -3%
```

## 3.2.3.2 Employment and unemployment

```
Unemployment rate = unemployed / labour force x 100
2.1 m / 35 m -> 6.0%
```

- Two measures: claimant count and LFS; they can differ because not every unemployed person claims.
- Causes: demand side (cyclical) and supply side (frictional, structural, seasonal patterns). Policy depends on the cause.
- Rest of world: export demand, import competition, firms moving production.

## 3.2.3.3 Inflation and deflation

- Demand-pull versus cost-push; world commodity prices feed into costs; inflation abroad raises import prices.
- Cost pass-through: input share x input price change. Energy 15% of costs rising 20% raises costs by 3%.
- Deflationary policies reduce AD; they need not cause deflation.

## 3.2.3.4 Current account

- Four parts: goods, services, primary income, secondary income.
- Influences: productivity, inflation, exchange rate, activity abroad.
- Express as % of GDP: deficit 30 bn with GDP 1,500 bn -> -2%.

## 3.2.3.5 Conflicts

| Output gap | Unemployment | Inflation pressure | Current account |
|---|---|---|---|
| Negative | Above normal | Weak | Imports subdued |
| Positive | Low | Strong | Imports rising |

Reconciling: time demand policy, use supply-side policy to raise potential output; long-run productivity growth eases conflicts.

## Chains to learn

- **World recession**: exports fall, AD shifts left, negative output gap widens, cyclical unemployment rises, demand-pull pressure eases, the current account may improve as imports fall with incomes.
- **Commodity price jump**: firms' costs rise, SRAS shifts left, cost-push inflation, real output and employment fall.
- **Domestic boom**: AD grows faster than potential, positive gap, labour shortages push up wages, inflation rises, imports rise and the current account worsens.
- **Productivity rise**: unit costs fall, SRAS and LRAS shift right, faster growth with less inflation, exports more competitive.

## Matching unemployment to policy

| Data clue | Likely type | Policy direction |
|---|---|---|
| Rising unemployment as GDP falls | Cyclical | Raise AD |
| Vacancies unfilled while unemployment is high | Structural | Training, mobility support |
| Peaks each winter in tourist towns | Seasonal | Diversify local jobs |
| Short spells between jobs | Frictional | Better job matching |

## Exam traps

- "Inflation fell from 5% to 3%, so there is deflation." It is disinflation.
- Policy for structural unemployment that only raises AD.
- Calling the current account "the balance of payments".
- Growth from closing a negative gap labelled as long-run growth.
- Identifying a cycle phase from one indicator.
- Output gap percentages divided by actual rather than potential output.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between short-run and long-run growth.
2. Actual output is 4,700 and potential is 5,000. Calculate the output gap.
3. Which phase of the cycle shows falling real GDP and rising unemployment?
4. Name the two UK measures of unemployment.
5. A labour force of 30 million has 1.2 million unemployed. Calculate the unemployment rate.
6. Coal mines close and miners lack skills for local jobs. Which type of unemployment?
7. Distinguish between deflation and disinflation.
8. Imported materials are 25% of costs and their price rises 12%. By how much do total costs rise?
9. List the four parts of the current account.
10. Goods -45, services +30, primary income +5, secondary income -10. Find the balance.
11. What does a positive output gap suggest about inflationary pressure?
12. Give one way to reconcile growth and price stability in the long run.

### Answers

1. Short-run: rise in real output, often using spare capacity. Long-run: rise in productive capacity.
2. -300 / 5,000 = -6%.
3. Recession.
4. Claimant count; Labour Force Survey measure.
5. 4%.
6. Structural.
7. Deflation: prices falling. Disinflation: inflation rate falling but prices still rising.
8. 0.25 x 12 = 3%.
9. Trade in goods, trade in services, primary income, secondary income.
10. -20, a deficit.
11. Resources are stretched, so wages and prices are likely to rise faster.
12. Supply-side policies that raise productivity and potential output.
