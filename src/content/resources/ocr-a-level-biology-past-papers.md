This page is a guide to OCR A Level Biology A (H420) past papers from June 2023 to June 2026. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from OCR's grade-boundary documents, and, for June 2023 to June 2025, a short account of what examiners reported, written by us from OCR's own examiners' reports. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on OCR's site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [OCR A Level Biology A (H420) qualification page](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/as-and-a-level/biology-a-h020-h420-from-2015/)
- [OCR A Level Biology A (H420) assessment page, with question papers, mark schemes and examiners' reports by series](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/as-and-a-level/biology-a-h020-h420-from-2015/assessment/)
- [OCR grade boundaries](https://www.ocr.org.uk/administration/grade-boundaries/) and the [grade boundaries archive](https://www.ocr.org.uk/administration/grade-boundaries/grade-boundaries-archive/grade-boundaries-archive.aspx)

OCR now says full assessment teaching materials, including secure assessment materials, are on its Teach Cambridge platform, with examples shown on the public assessment page. Check the page for the series you want, as what is listed can change.

Use paper, mark scheme and examiners' report together. Sit the paper under timed conditions, mark it strictly against the mark scheme, then read the report for that component to see why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. Our notes for each series are a quick way into the report, not a replacement for it.

Check the specification version before you rely on an older paper. H420 has three written components, all linear: Paper 1 Biological processes (H420/01, 100 marks, 2 hours 15 minutes), Paper 2 Biological diversity (H420/02, 100 marks, 2 hours 15 minutes) and Paper 3 Unified biology (H420/03, 70 marks, 1 hour 30 minutes), 270 marks in total. The Practical Endorsement is reported separately and carries no marks towards the grade. The series below all use this structure. For the latest specification text, use OCR's qualification page; our other [H420 revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-biology-enzymes-revision-notes/) are organised by topic rather than by paper.

## June 2025

Examiners described Paper 1 as well handled where answers followed a clear structure, with good results on recall, graph reading and practical calculations. The recurring weakness across the three papers was transfer: applying known biology to an unfamiliar set-up, linking structure to function precisely, and reading a question closely enough to use its command word.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (H420/01): choosing the right statistical test and the degrees of freedom, even when a null hypothesis was written correctly; adapting practical knowledge to an unfamiliar indicator-based photosynthesis context; describing a structure (chloroplast, gills) without saying what it does; matching an ECG trace to the heart's electrical activity; writing about red blood cells when asked about haemoglobin; keeping a mean and relative intensity consistent with the data given.
- Paper 2 (H420/02): misreading command words; general rather than precise terms; weak links between Module 4 and Module 6 content; rounding errors in a Simpson's index calculation and uncertainty about what the value means; unit conversions when finding a cell's actual size; remembering how to apply the Hardy-Weinberg equations even though they are supplied; long Level of Response answers on variation that drifted away from continuous, discontinuous, intraspecific and interspecific variation.
- Paper 3 (H420/03): extrapolation and choice of units; recalling mechanisms such as active loading and the action of adrenaline; thin answers to practical-method questions; answers left unfinished or blank. Stronger scripts interpreted graphs fluently and wrote about resolution and standardised variables.

## June 2024

The June 2024 reports describe papers of unchanged style and demand. Paper 1 candidates improved at 'analyse' and 'evaluate' questions, Paper 2 exposed gaps in later modules, and Paper 3 produced a good spread of marks but proved harder on the data-handling and synoptic questions.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (H420/01): applying knowledge to new contexts, evaluating, and using information from diagrams, graphs and tables; weaker candidates were more secure on naming terms and completing tables and gap-fills. Multi-step calculations such as finding a rate from a tangent were done well by stronger candidates.
- Paper 2 (H420/02): gaps in synthetic biology, T-lymphocyte activity and ecosystems; not using a graticule correctly; mixing up gene, allele, base and nucleotide; loose wording such as 'stimulate' and 'become' where 'activate' and 'differentiate' were needed; treating fungi or algae as plants and bacteria as animals; Level of Response answers that ran on without staying coherent.
- Paper 3 (H420/03): graph and data application, chemiosmosis, cloning and a population calculation from a serial dilution; AO2 and AO3 marks lagging behind AO1; confusing electroporation with electrofusion; a data-based Level of Response question on genetic and environmental influences that many could not turn into a valid conclusion.

## June 2023

In June 2023 examiners found the papers accessible and the Level of Response technique improving, but practical skills still looked patchy, which they linked in part to disrupted teaching during the pandemic.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (H420/01): applying knowledge and using the information in diagrams, graphs and figures; weaker candidates were mostly secure on short answers, tables and gap-fills. Candidates who did well analysed data and gave balanced evaluations.
- Paper 2 (H420/02): confusing independent and dependent variables; inappropriate rounding in a calculation; the belief that selective breeding, cloning or antibiotics cause mutations; misconceptions about plants and photosynthesis; confusion about feeding relationships and migration.
- Paper 3 (H420/03): mixing up sympatric and allopatric speciation; calculations; answers that repeated the question stem or lacked depth on practical questions; difficulty using images and unfamiliar information.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find OCR H420 past papers and mark schemes?**
On the assessment page for OCR A Level Biology A, linked above, which lists question papers, mark schemes and examiners' reports by series. OCR also points teachers to Teach Cambridge for the full set of secure materials.

**How is the overall H420 grade decided?**
The three written papers are added to give a mark out of 270, and the overall boundaries in each table apply to that total. The Practical Endorsement is a separate pass or not-classified result.

**Are the Paper 1 and Paper 2 boundaries the same every year?**
No. The tables show them moving from series to series, and the two papers can move in different directions in the same year.

**Which mistakes do examiners report most often?**
Answers that stay general when a precise biological term is needed, unfamiliar practical contexts, statistics and calculations (rounding, units, choosing a test), and Level of Response answers that are too long or off the point.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and are only a guide for the series they belong to.
