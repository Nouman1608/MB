These questions cover section **3.1.6.2 Equilibrium constant Kc for homogeneous systems** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Papers 1 and 2 (and Paper 3). Learn the methods in the [Equilibrium constant Kc study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-equilibrium-constant-kc-for-homogeneous-systems/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-equilibrium-constant-kc-for-homogeneous-systems-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Write an expression for Kc for each equilibrium.

**(a)** N₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2NO(g) **[1]**

**(b)** 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g) **[1]**

**(c)** N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g) **[1]**

**2.** Deduce the units of Kc for each equilibrium in question 1. **[3]**

**3.** At equilibrium in the reaction 2NO(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g), the concentrations are [NO] = 0.020, [O₂] = 0.010 and [NO₂] = 0.040 mol dm⁻³. Calculate the value of Kc and give its units. **[3]**

**4.** 0.100 mol of N₂O₄ is placed in a 1.00 dm³ vessel. At equilibrium, 0.060 mol of N₂O₄ remains, according to N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g).

**(a)** Calculate the equilibrium amount of NO₂. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate Kc, with units. **[3]**

**5.** 2.00 mol of hydrogen and 2.00 mol of iodine are heated in a closed vessel, and 3.00 mol of hydrogen iodide is present at equilibrium: H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g).

**(a)** Calculate the equilibrium amounts of hydrogen and iodine. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate Kc. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the volume of the vessel is not needed. **[1]**

**6.** 0.800 mol of SO₃ is heated in a 2.00 dm³ vessel. At equilibrium 0.100 mol of O₂ is present: 2SO₃(g) ⇌ 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g). Calculate Kc, with units. **[5]**

**7.** For H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g), Kc = 50 at a certain temperature. At equilibrium [H₂] = [I₂] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³. Calculate the concentration of HI. **[2]**

**8.** Kc for A(g) ⇌ B(g) is 0.25. Give Kc for B(g) ⇌ A(g). **[1]**

**9.** The forward reaction 2NO(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g) is exothermic. Explain what happens to Kc when the temperature is raised. **[3]**

**10.** Explain why the addition of extra reactant to an equilibrium mixture does not alter the value of Kc. **[2]**

**11.** State the effect of a catalyst on the value of Kc, and explain. **[2]**

**12.** In the reaction CH₃COOH(l) + C₂H₅OH(l) ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅(l) + H₂O(l), Kc = 4.0. 3.00 mol of ethanoic acid and 3.00 mol of ethanol are mixed. Calculate the amount of ester at equilibrium. **[4]**

**13.** A student writes the Kc expression for N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ as [N₂][H₂] ÷ [NH₃]. Identify two errors. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Kc = [NO]² ÷ ([N₂][O₂]) [1]

**(b)** Kc = [SO₃]² ÷ ([SO₂]²[O₂]) [1]

**(c)** Kc = [NO₂]² ÷ [N₂O₄] [1]. **[3]**

**2.** (a) no units [1]; (b) mol⁻¹ dm³ [1]; (c) mol dm⁻³ [1]. **[3]**

**3.** Kc = [NO₂]² ÷ ([NO]²[O₂]) [1] = 0.040² ÷ (0.020² × 0.010) = 0.0016 ÷ (4.0 × 10⁻⁶) [1] = **400 mol⁻¹ dm³** [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** N₂O₄ reacted = 0.100 − 0.060 = 0.040 mol [1]; NO₂ formed = 2 × 0.040 = **0.080 mol** [1]

**(b)** Concentrations in the 1.00 dm³ vessel: [NO₂] = 0.080, [N₂O₄] = 0.060 mol dm⁻³ [1]; Kc = 0.080² ÷ 0.060 [1] = **0.11 mol dm⁻³** (0.107) [1]. **[5]**

**5. (a)** HI formed = 3.00 mol, so H₂ and I₂ each reacted = 1.50 mol [1]; remaining = 2.00 − 1.50 = **0.50 mol** each [1]

**(b)** Kc = 3.00² ÷ (0.50 × 0.50) [1] = **36** [1]

**(c)** There are 2 moles of gas on each side of the expression, so the volume cancels (Kc has no units) [1]. **[5]**

**6.** n(SO₂) = 0.200 mol; n(SO₃) = 0.800 − 0.200 = 0.600 mol [1]; concentrations (÷ 2.00 dm³): [SO₃] = 0.300, [SO₂] = 0.100, [O₂] = 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ [1]; Kc = [SO₂]²[O₂] ÷ [SO₃]² [1]; = (0.100² × 0.0500) ÷ 0.300² [1] = **5.6 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[5]**

**7.** [HI]² = Kc × [H₂][I₂] = 50 × 0.10 × 0.10 = 0.50 [1]; [HI] = **0.71 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[2]**

**8.** 1 ÷ 0.25 = **4.0** [1]. **[1]**

**9.** Kc decreases [1]; a higher temperature shifts the position in the endothermic direction, which is the reverse reaction [1]; so there is less NO₂ and more NO and O₂ at equilibrium, giving a lower ratio of products to reactants [1]. **[3]**

**10.** The position of equilibrium shifts to use up the extra reactant [1]; until the ratio of concentrations in the expression returns to the original value of Kc, which is fixed at a given temperature [1]. **[2]**

**11.** There is no effect on Kc [1]; a catalyst speeds up the forward and reverse reactions equally, so the equilibrium composition is unchanged [1]. **[2]**

**12.** Let x = amount of ester at equilibrium. Then 3.00 − x of each reactant remains [1]; the number of moles on each side is equal, so the volume cancels: x² ÷ (3.00 − x)² = 4.0 [1]; x ÷ (3.00 − x) = 2.0 [1]; x = **2.00 mol** [1]. **[4]**

**13.** The products must be on the top and the reactants on the bottom [1]; the concentrations must be raised to the powers of the coefficients: [NH₃]² on the top and [H₂]³ on the bottom [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Inverting the expression or missing the powers.
- Using initial rather than equilibrium amounts.
- Leaving out units, or writing the wrong units.
- Using moles when the volume does not cancel.
- Saying that Kc changes with concentration, pressure or a catalyst.
