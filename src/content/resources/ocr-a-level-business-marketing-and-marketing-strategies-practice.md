## Questions

**1.** Define market segmentation and state two ways in which a business could segment its market. **[3]**

**2.** Veyrane Oat Drinks sold £7.2m in a market worth £48m in 2025. In 2026 it sold £7.56m in a market worth £54m. Calculate its market share in each year and the market growth, and explain what the figures show. **[5]**

**3.** Skerrimoor Bikes recorded these weekly repair jobs: 12, 15, 18, 21, 24 and 30. Calculate the mean and the standard deviation (use division by n), and explain how the owner could use the result if the data is normally distributed. **[4]**

**4.** Cadmoor Kitchenware raised the price of a pan from £20 to £22. Monthly sales fell from 500 to 440. Calculate the price elasticity of demand and its effect on revenue, and advise on the price rise. **[5]**

**5.** Imbersley Stationery finds that a 5% rise in average household income raises demand for its leather notebooks by 8%. When a rival raises its price by 10%, Imbersley's notebook demand rises by 3%. Calculate income elasticity and cross elasticity and interpret each. **[4]**

**6.** Imbersley Stationery raised advertising spend from £20,000 to £25,000. Sales of its planners rose from 8,000 to 8,600 units. Calculate advertising elasticity of demand and evaluate its usefulness to the business. **[6]**

**7.** Cadmoor Kitchenware makes 8,000 pans a year. Variable cost is £9 per pan and fixed costs are £40,000. (a) Using a 30% mark-up on full cost, calculate the cost-plus price per pan. **[2]** (b) A hotel offers to buy an extra 1,500 pans at £11 each using spare capacity. Calculate the contribution and advise whether to accept. **[4]**

**8.** Veyrane Oat Drinks sells four products. A business analyst treats market growth above 10% as high and market share above 15% as high. Product A: growth 3%, share 30%. Product B: growth 14%, share 28%. Product C: growth 15%, share 4%. Product D: growth 2%, share 3%. Classify each using the Boston matrix and recommend how the firm should use the cash from the first. **[5]**

**9.** Explain how a dental clinic could use the AIDA model when promoting a new teeth-whitening service. **[4]**

**10.** Mordaunt Dental is opening a second clinic. Explain how process, people and physical evidence could each contribute to its marketing. **[6]**

**11.** A sports club has 600 members: 40% aged under 30, 30% aged 30 to 49, 30% aged 50 or over. It wants a stratified sample of 50 members. (a) Calculate the number to be sampled from each group. **[3]** (b) Explain one advantage of this method over convenience sampling. **[3]**

**12.** Skerrimoor Bikes sells only in the UK, where its sales are £3m and its market is growing at 2% a year. A neighbouring country has a market growing at 8% a year, with several established brands, and the owner is considering selling there online. Recommend and justify a marketing strategy, weighing the risks. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Splitting the market into customer groups that share traits or needs [1]. Any two of age, income, location, lifestyle or usage rate [1] [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Two named ways earn separate credit, but a list with no definition leaves the first point unmet.

**2.** 2025 share 7.2/48 x 100 = 15% [1]; 2026 share 7.56/54 x 100 = 14% [1]; market growth (54 - 48)/48 x 100 = 12.5% [1]. Share fell by one percentage point [1] because Veyrane's sales grew by only 5%, slower than the market, so rivals are gaining ground despite higher sales [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Showing the formula and working keeps credit available if one figure slips, and a comment on what the numbers mean is what turns a calculation into analysis.

**3.** Mean = 120/6 = 20 [1]. Squared deviations 64, 25, 4, 1, 16, 100 total 210, divided by 6 gives 35 [1], square root gives **5.9** jobs [1]. If normal, about 68% of weeks fall within 14.1 to 25.9 jobs, so the owner can plan staffing for that range and treat weeks outside it as unusual [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Stating which divisor you used makes your method clear; the interpretation must be linked to a decision such as staffing, not left as a bare statement.

**4.** %ΔP = 2/20 = +10% [1]; %ΔQ = -60/500 = -12% [1]; PED = -12/10 = **-1.2** [1]. It is elastic, so the rise lowers revenue from £10,000 to £9,680 [1]. The rise is unwise unless costs fell enough to protect profit; a cut or hold would raise revenue [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Giving the sign and saying "elastic" with its revenue consequence shows you can interpret the figure rather than only compute it.

**5.** YED = 8/5 = **1.6** [1], above 1, so notebooks are income elastic, a normal good whose demand rises faster than income and which is vulnerable in a downturn [1]. XED = 3/10 = **0.3** [1]; positive but small, so the rival's product is a weak substitute and Imbersley gains little from rival price rises [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The sign and size of each figure should each be interpreted separately; one generic sentence for both loses credit.

**6.** %Δ spend = 5,000/20,000 = 25% [1]; %Δ sales = 600/8,000 = 7.5% [1]; AED = 7.5/25 = **0.3** [1]. It is useful for comparing campaigns and judging whether spend gives good returns [1]. But sales may have been raised by other factors, such as seasonality or price, and effects may be delayed [1]. Overall it is a helpful guide but not proof, so Imbersley should combine it with other measures such as profit per campaign before deciding on further spend [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation question needs a judgement at the end; listing strengths and weaknesses without a conclusion usually stays at the lower levels.

**7. (a)** Fixed cost per pan = 40,000/8,000 = £5, so full cost = £14 [1]; price = 14 x 1.3 = **£18.20** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Contribution per pan = 11 - 9 = £2 [1]; total 1,500 x 2 = **£3,000** [1]. Accept, as fixed costs are already covered by regular sales so the order adds profit [1], provided spare capacity is real and the lower price does not leak to regular customers or damage the brand [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A mark-up is a percentage of cost, not of price; stating the base shows which you used, and the advice should rest on the contribution figure.

**8.** A: low growth, high share, so a cash cow [1]. B: high growth, high share, a star [1]. C: high growth, low share, a question mark [1]. D: low growth, low share, a dog [1]. Use A's surplus cash to fund B's growth and test whether C can gain share, while reviewing whether to withdraw D [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the quadrant gets the classification, but the recommendation is what shows you can use the model to make a decision.

**9.** Attention: a striking local advert or social media post about whitening results [1]. Interest: facts about how it works and its safety [1]. Desire: before-and-after photos or an introductory offer, so patients want it [1]. Action: an easy online booking link or a call to action with a deadline [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the four stages is not enough; each needs a clinic-specific action to count as applied.

**10.** Process: a simple booking system and short waits make the service easy and reliable [1], which supports repeat visits [1]. People: friendly, qualified dentists and receptionists build trust, which is central to health services [1], and staff recommendations spread by word of mouth [1]. Physical evidence: a clean, modern waiting room and clear signage make an intangible service visible [1], reassuring new patients about quality [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each element needs both an explanation and a link to this clinic; defining the terms alone does not answer a question set in a context.

**11. (a)** Under 30: 600 x 0.4 = 240 members, so 50 x 0.4 = **20** [1]; 30 to 49: 50 x 0.3 = **15** [1]; 50 or over: **15** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Stratified sampling reflects the age mix of the membership [1], so results represent each group in proportion [1], whereas convenience sampling might over-represent whoever is easiest to reach, such as those who attend often, producing bias [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The comparison must name the biased outcome of the alternative method, not only praise the chosen one.

**12.** Sound reasons to expand: the neighbouring market grows at 8%, against 2% at home [1], so the owner could raise sales and spread risk [1]. Recommend a cautious Ansoff-style market development: sell the existing range online, adapt the website language and delivery to local customers [1], and first run trial marketing or secondary research on local tastes [1]. Use social media promotion to build awareness cheaply and use the UK reputation as a USP [1]. Risks: established brands mean strong competition, so a penetration price may be needed, cutting margins [1]; international delivery adds logistics cost, and cultural, legal and exchange rate differences may apply [1]. A small test lets the firm limit losses, funded by the UK business [1]. On balance expansion is justified if it is staged and checked against results, because the faster growth outweighs the risks only if the firm keeps costs controlled [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* A recommendation that cites figures from the case and weighs a risk against the benefit, then reaches a clear judgement, reads as evaluation rather than description.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a formula but not substituting the numbers, so there is no working to credit.
- Giving a percentage change in the wrong direction or without the sign in elasticity questions.
- Stating "elastic" or "inelastic" without saying what it means for revenue or pricing.
- Confusing market share with market growth, or sales growth with either.
- Naming a Boston matrix quadrant without a recommendation.
- Defining AIDA or process, people and physical evidence without applying them to the business.
- Giving advantages of a sampling or pricing method with no contrast against another.
- Finishing "recommend" and "justify" questions without a judgement.
