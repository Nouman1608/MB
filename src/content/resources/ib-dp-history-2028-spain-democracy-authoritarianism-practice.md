> **Original questions written for Marlbridge.** They are not past-paper questions and do not copy the real exam's structure or mark tariffs.

**HL only.** These questions practise Regional study 10 in the Europe option (HL Paper 3). Revise first with the [revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-spain-democracy-authoritarianism-revision-notes/) and the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-spain-democracy-authoritarianism/).

## Questions

**1.** State two reforms of Azaña's government (1931 to 1933). **[2]**

**2.** Explain why the Primo de Rivera dictatorship ended in 1930. **[4]**

**3.** Read this short extract, constructed for practice.

> "The government in Madrid lost us the fleet, the barracks and half the cities, but it kept the gold, the factories and the sea. We have the discipline, and our friends abroad send what we need." (Constructed for practice, in the voice of a Nationalist officer in autumn 1936.)

**(a)** Explain what the extract suggests about the strengths of each side at the start of the war. **[3]**

**(b)** Analyse one limitation of the extract as evidence of why the Nationalists won. **[3]**

**4.** Explain two ways in which the International Brigades or the Condor Legion affected the war. **[4]**

**5.** Analyse the methods Franco used to maintain power between 1939 and 1959. **[6]**

**6.** Examine the part played by Juan Carlos in the transition to democracy. **[6]**

**7.** "The Republic failed because of its own divisions rather than the strength of its opponents." To what extent do you agree? Plan an answer: state your argument, three themes and a counter-argument. **[15]**

**8.** Compare the economic policies of Franco before and after 1959. **[4]**

**9.** Discuss how historians disagree about the causes of the Spanish Civil War. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: army reform that retired officers; church reforms such as ending state funding of the clergy, divorce and limits on religious schools; the Catalan Statute (1932); the Agrarian Reform Law (1932). One mark each. **[2]**

**2.** Up to 4. The Depression and a falling peseta damaged his economic record (1); officers and intellectuals had been alienated and Catalans resented his suppression of Catalan identity (1); he lost the support of the army and then of the king, who had relied on him (1); he resigned in January 1930 and the monarchy was weakened by its link to the dictatorship (1). **[4]**

**3. (a)** Up to 3: the Republic kept the gold reserves, industry and key territory, which suggests economic strength (1); the Nationalists had army discipline and a unified command (1); foreign help for the Nationalists is mentioned as a factor in their favour (1). **[3]**
**(b)** Up to 3: it is one officer's opinion at the start of the war, so it cannot explain the final result (1); it is constructed and optimistic and does not mention Republican divisions or Soviet aid (1); it ignores later events such as the loss of the north and the Ebro offensive (1). **[3]**

**4.** Up to 4. The Condor Legion bombed civilian and military targets, such as Guernica in April 1937, and helped Nationalist advances (2). The International Brigades brought volunteers and some skill to the Republic, notably at Madrid in 1936, but were too few to offset larger Nationalist supplies (2). **[4]**

**5.** Up to 6 using a markband approach. The single party and the title of Caudillo concentrated power (1); balance among army, Falange, Church and monarchists (1); executions, imprisonment and exile of opponents (1); bans on unions and parties (1); censorship and National Catholicism shaping education (1); a judgement on which method mattered most, such as repression (1). **[6]**

**6.** Up to 6: Franco chose him in 1969, expecting continuity (1); after 1975 he supported reform and appointed Suárez in 1976 (1); he supported the Law for Political Reform and the legalisation of parties (1); he accepted a constitutional monarchy under the 1978 constitution (1); his television address helped defeat the coup of 23 February 1981 (1); a judgement weighing his role against that of Suárez, the opposition and social change (1). **[6]**

**7.** Credit a plan with these features, 3 marks each, 15 in all. Understanding of the claim: Republican infighting between anarchists, communists and socialists, and weak government (3). Evidence of division: Barcelona, May 1937; the dispute over collectivization and militias; changes of government (3). Evidence of opponents' strength: German and Italian aid, the Army of Africa, Franco's command and Non-Intervention (3). Perspectives: historians who stress internal divisions against those who stress foreign intervention (3). Counter-argument and judgement: for instance that the divisions mattered but would not have been fatal without foreign aid to the Nationalists (3). **[15]**

**8.** Up to 4. Before 1959 autarky, state control and regulation led to hunger and slow growth (1); after 1959 the Stabilization Plan opened the economy to foreign investment, tourism and trade (1); growth in the 1960s changed society, with urbanisation and emigration (1); a judgement that the regime changed its economics while keeping political control (1). **[4]**

**9.** Up to 6: one view stresses class conflict between landowners and peasants, and workers and employers (2); another stresses the Church-state clash, military politics or the failure of democratic compromise (2); explains why they differ, for example by emphasis on long-term or short-term factors (1); a reasoned judgement (1). **[6]**
