Pearson Edexcel A-Level Urdu (9UR0) is a linear qualification assessed by three externally assessed written papers, all sat together in one May/June series. Paper 1 (9UR0/01) is Translation into English, Reading Comprehension and Writing (Research Question) in Urdu: 2 hours 30 minutes, 80 marks, 40%. Paper 2 (9UR0/02) is Translation into Urdu and Written Response to Works: 2 hours 40 minutes, 110 marks, 30%. Paper 3 (9UR0/03) is Listening, Reading and Writing in Urdu: 2 hours 15 minutes, 60 marks, 30%. This is the UK-regulated Level 3 Advanced GCE in Urdu, not an International Advanced Level, and it has no separate AS certification route. The site's pages on [Translation and Reading](/resources/a-level-edexcel-urdu-translation-and-reading/), [Translation and Works](/resources/a-level-edexcel-urdu-translation-and-works/) and [Listening, Reading and Writing](/resources/a-level-edexcel-urdu-listening-reading-writing/) cover each paper in more detail.

## Whole-qualification planning

The specification states that students must complete all assessments in May/June in any single year, so there is no opportunity to bank a paper early. First assessment was May/June 2020. The total across the three papers is 80 + 110 + 60 = 250 marks over 150 + 160 + 135 = 445 minutes, and the weightings 40% + 30% + 30% add up to 100%. Speaking is explicitly unassessed; the qualification's only assessed listening is in Paper 3. The four content themes are Evolving Pakistani Society, Art and Culture in the Urdu-Speaking World, Immigration and the Multicultural Society, and Pakistani politics, alongside prescribed literary texts and films. The first three themes have study guides on the site, for example [Theme 1](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-theme-1-study-guide/).

## Paper 1: translation, reading and research writing

Paper 1 is 150 minutes for 80 marks, so 150 / 80 = 1.875 minutes per mark. Its marks divide into translation into English (20), reading (20) and an independent-research writing question (40). At 1.875 minutes per mark, that suggests roughly 37.5 minutes each for the 20-mark translation and reading sections and about 75 minutes for the 40-mark writing question, before any checking time. **Preparation priority**: the research question is half of the paper's marks, so begin research early, build a bank of facts, vocabulary and arguments from your chosen area, and rehearse writing a structured Urdu response. Translation into English rewards accuracy of meaning and natural English, not word-for-word substitution.

## Paper 2: translation into Urdu and response to works

Paper 2 is 160 minutes for 110 marks, so 160 / 110 = about 1.45 minutes per mark. It combines translation into Urdu (20 marks) with an internal-choice written response to two prescribed literary texts, or one text and one film (90 marks in total). The choice of works does not change the paper's marks or weighting. **Preparation priority**: because the 90-mark response is the bulk of the paper, learn your chosen works thoroughly: events, characters, themes, language and the writer's methods, with short quotations you can use accurately. For translation into Urdu, practise producing grammatical, idiomatic sentences with correct agreement and spelling.

## Paper 3: listening, reading and writing

Paper 3 is 135 minutes for 60 marks, or 135 / 60 = 2.25 minutes per mark. It has a listening comprehension (30 marks) and a combined listening, reading and writing question (30 marks). **Preparation priority**: practise regular listening to spoken Urdu from varied sources, noting key facts, opinions and attitudes, and practise writing answers in Urdu from what you have heard and read. Check that your answers use the information from the sources, not only general knowledge.

## Question styles and command words

Without relying on any particular paper, expect translation tasks, comprehension questions, extended writing and essay-style response. In writing tasks, a clear structure (introduction, developed points, conclusion), a consistent register and accurate language all matter. In responses to literary works, back points with evidence from the text or film and explain its effect.

## Worked practice scenario: translating into English

Task (original): translate into natural English the sentence below.

ہجرت کرنے والے لوگ نئے معاشرے میں اپنی زبان اور ثقافت کو زندہ رکھنے کی کوشش کرتے ہیں۔

```
Step 1  Identify the structure:
        Subject  - ہجرت کرنے والے لوگ (people who migrate)
        Place    - نئے معاشرے میں (in the new society)
        Object   - اپنی زبان اور ثقافت (their own language and culture)
        Verb     - زندہ رکھنے کی کوشش کرتے ہیں (try to keep alive)
Step 2  Reorder for English syntax (subject, verb, object, place).
Step 3  Model translation:
        "People who migrate try to keep their own language and
        culture alive in their new society."
```

The word اپنی refers back to the subject, which is why "their own" is more faithful than a plain "their". Checking that every element has been translated, and that none has been added, is the habit that protects marks.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Remember that all three papers are sat in one May/June series.
- Start the independent research for Paper 1 early.
- Know your chosen literary texts or film thoroughly for Paper 2.
- Practise listening to Urdu regularly before Paper 3.
- Time translation sections so that the larger writing tasks keep their share.
- Proofread Urdu writing for spelling and agreement.
- Read each question to see which source or work it asks you to use.
- Keep a few minutes at the end for checking.

## Self-test

1. How many marks is the Paper 1 research writing question worth?
2. How many minutes per mark does Paper 3 allow?
3. Is speaking assessed in 9UR0?
4. What is the total number of marks over all three papers?
5. Why is "their own" a better rendering of اپنی in the worked sentence?

**Answers:** 1. 40 marks. 2. 135 / 60 = 2.25 minutes per mark. 3. No, speaking is unassessed. 4. 250 marks. 5. Because اپنی refers back to the subject and signals ownership by the same people.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Edexcel A-Level Urdu 9UR0?**
Three externally assessed papers: Paper 1 (40%), Paper 2 (30%) and Paper 3 (30%).

**Is the qualification staged into AS and A2?**
No. It is linear, and all assessments must be completed in May/June in a single year.

**What do I study for Paper 2?**
You write a response to two prescribed literary texts, or one text and one film, as well as translating into Urdu.

**Is there a speaking test?**
No. Speaking is stated as unassessed.

**Is this an International Advanced Level qualification?**
No. It is the UK-regulated Level 3 Advanced GCE in Urdu.
