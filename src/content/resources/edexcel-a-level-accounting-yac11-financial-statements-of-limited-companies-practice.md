## Section A

**1.** Name the **three** financial statements that make up a set of company accounts under IAS 1. **[3]**

**2.** Define other comprehensive income and give **one** example. **[2]**

**3.** A company made a profit for the year of 85,000 and a revaluation gain on property of 30,000. Calculate total comprehensive income. **[1]**

**4.** Explain the role of the auditor of a limited company. **[3]**

**5.** State **two** items that appear in a Directors' Report. **[2]**

**6.** Explain what is meant by a qualified audit opinion. **[2]**

## Section B

Orrin Plastics Ltd has the following information for the year ended 30 June 2026.

| | $ |
|---|---:|
| Revenue (continuing operations) | 820,000 |
| Cost of sales | 497,000 |
| Distribution costs | 64,000 |
| Administrative expenses (including a legal settlement of 18,000) | 98,000 |
| Debenture interest (5% on 160,000 of debentures) | 8,000 |
| Income tax for the year | 31,000 |
| Loss from discontinued operations, after tax | 6,000 |
| Revaluation gain on land | 45,000 |
| Ordinary dividends paid | 24,000 |

Equity at 1 July 2025: ordinary share capital 300,000; share premium 40,000; revaluation reserve 15,000; retained earnings 120,000.

At 30 June 2026: property, plant and equipment (after the revaluation) 678,000; inventory 70,000; trade receivables 85,000; bank 22,000; trade payables 52,000; tax payable 31,000; 5% debentures 160,000.

**7. (a)** Prepare the statement of comprehensive income for the year ended 30 June 2026. **[11]**

**(b)** Prepare the statement of changes in equity for the year. **[7]**

**(c)** Prepare the statement of financial position at 30 June 2026. **[8]**

**8.** Explain why Orrin Plastics Ltd should show the loss from discontinued operations separately. **[3]**

**9.** The legal settlement of 18,000 is an exceptional item. Calculate the profit from operations excluding it, and explain why disclosing it is useful. **[4]**

**10.** Evaluate the importance of the external audit to the shareholders of a limited company. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Statement of comprehensive income [1]; statement of changes in equity [1]; statement of financial position [1].

**2.** Gains and losses that are recognised in equity but not included in profit for the year [1]. Example: a revaluation gain on property [1].

**3.** 85,000 + 30,000 = **115,000** [1].

**4.** The auditor is independent of the directors [1]; the auditor examines the financial statements and gives an opinion on whether they give a true and fair view [1]; this gives shareholders and others more confidence in the figures [1].

**5.** Any two: a review of the business and its performance [1]; principal risks and uncertainties [1]; dividends proposed [1]; names of directors [1]; significant events after the year end [1].

**6.** The auditor believes the accounts give a true and fair view except for a specified matter [1], for example where the directors will not make a necessary provision, and it warns users to treat the affected figures with caution [1].

**8.** Discontinued operations will not continue, so their loss is not a guide to future profit [1]; showing it separately lets users see that the continuing business made 122,000 [1]; combining it would hide the performance of the continuing business and could mislead users when they forecast [1].

**9.** Profit from operations excluding the settlement: 161,000 + 18,000 = **179,000** [2]. Disclosure is useful because it shows users the underlying performance [1] and that the settlement is not expected to occur each year, so it should not be used to forecast future profit [1].

**10.** For: the auditor is independent of the directors, so shareholders who do not run the company can rely on the accounts [1]; the audit may deter errors and fraud [1]; a clean opinion supports confidence when buying and selling shares and raising loans [1]. Against: an audit gives reasonable but not absolute assurance, because it is based on sampling [1]; it costs money, which reduces profit [1]; the auditor depends on information supplied by directors and is paid by the company [1]. Judgement: the audit is important because shareholders cannot check the accounts themselves, although they should also read the Directors' Report and consider the limits of the audit [1]. (Maximum 6 marks.)

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting the revaluation gain in profit for the year (Question 7).
- Deducting dividends in the statement of comprehensive income (Question 7).
- Omitting the discontinued operations line, or deducting tax twice.
- Describing an audit as a guarantee that the accounts are correct (Question 10).
- Failing to give a consequence when asked to explain why a disclosure matters.
- Not checking that equity in the statement of changes in equity equals equity in the statement of financial position.

## Approaching these questions

Use the IAS 1 headings and add a subtotal at every stage. Build the statement of changes in equity column by column and carry the closing figures to the statement of financial position. For written questions, state who benefits (shareholders, lenders), give a reason and link it to the case.
