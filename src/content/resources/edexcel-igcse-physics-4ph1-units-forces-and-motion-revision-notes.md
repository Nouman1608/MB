Condensed notes on sub-topic **1(a) Units** of Pearson Edexcel
International GCSE Physics (4PH1), Issue 4. For full explanations and
worked examples, use the
[Units for forces and motion study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-4ph1-units-forces-and-motion/).

## What the specification lists

- **1.1:** kg, m, m/s, m/s², N, s, N/kg
- **1.2P (Physics only, Paper 2P):** Nm, kg m/s

## Key units table

| Unit | Quantity | Built from | In base units |
|---|---|---|---|
| kg | mass | base | kg |
| m | distance, extension, perpendicular distance | base | m |
| s | time | base | s |
| m/s | speed, velocity | distance ÷ time | m/s |
| m/s² | acceleration | change in velocity ÷ time | m/s² |
| N | force (weight, friction, drag, tension) | mass × acceleration | kg m/s² |
| N/kg | gravitational field strength g | weight ÷ mass | m/s² |
| Nm (P) | moment | force × perpendicular distance | kg m²/s² |
| kg m/s (P) | momentum | mass × velocity | kg m/s |

## Links to remember

- 1 N gives 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m/s².
- N/kg and m/s² are the same unit: g = 10 N/kg means free fall without
  air resistance has acceleration 10 m/s².
- A change in momentum can also be written in N s (force × time). It is
  equal to kg m/s; quote kg m/s unless told otherwise.
- The joule is also N × m, but moments are given in **Nm** and energies in
  **J**. Never write J for a moment.

## Conversion factors

| From | To | Operation | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| g | kg | ÷ 1000 | 250 g = 0.25 kg |
| tonne | kg | × 1000 | 3.5 t = 3500 kg |
| mm | m | ÷ 1000 | 8.0 mm = 0.0080 m |
| cm | m | ÷ 100 | 60 cm = 0.60 m |
| km | m | × 1000 | 2.4 km = 2400 m |
| min | s | × 60 | 4.0 min = 240 s |
| h | s | × 3600 | 0.25 h = 900 s |
| km/h | m/s | ÷ 3.6 | 54 km/h = 15 m/s |
| m/s | km/h | × 3.6 | 30 m/s = 108 km/h |

## The unit check

Write the units of each term. Both sides of a correct equation have the
same unit.

```
v² = u² + 2as     m²/s² = m²/s² + (m/s²)(m)     consistent
F = (mv − mu)/t   N = (kg m/s)/s = kg m/s²        consistent
```

If an answer comes out in the wrong unit, the method is wrong:
acceleration in m/s means no division by time; a moment in N means no
distance.

## Recall points

- Mass (kg) is the same everywhere; weight (N) depends on g.
- A speed in m/s is a smaller number than the same speed in km/h.
- Distances in moments must be in metres before multiplying.
- Momentum and velocity are vectors, so a negative value means the
  opposite direction; the unit stays the same.
- Round only at the end of a multi-step calculation, to the number of
  significant figures in the data (usually 2 or 3).
- Use standard form for very large or small values and keep the unit:
  2.0 × 10⁻³ kg, 1.5 × 10⁴ N.

## Exam traps

- "Weight = 50 kg". Weight is a force: 50 kg has weight 500 N where
  g = 10 N/kg.
- Substituting 600 for a mass of 600 g. The force answer comes out 1000
  times too large.
- Dividing km/h by 60 instead of 3.6 (that gives km per minute, not m/s).
- Using 25 for a distance of 25 cm in a moment, giving N cm.
- Writing m/s/s or m/s2 sloppily. Write m/s² (or m s⁻²); both are the same
  unit.
- Giving the unit as "kgm/s" run together, which can be read as kilo-gram-
  metre. Leave a space: kg m/s.
- Reading a graph axis in minutes or cm and forgetting to convert the
  gradient or area to SI units.

## Self-test

1. State the unit of gravitational field strength.
2. Express the newton in terms of kg, m and s.
3. State two quantities in Topic 1 measured in metres.
4. Convert 72 km/h to m/s.
5. Convert 450 g to kg and 1.5 minutes to seconds.
6. State the unit of momentum and the unit of the moment of a force.
7. Show that N/kg is the same unit as m/s².
8. A student writes "acceleration = 4.0 m/s". What mistake might this
   reveal?
9. Calculate the weight of a 2.5 kg bag where g = 10 N/kg, with the unit.
10. Calculate the moment of a 15 N force acting 40 cm from a pivot.
11. A 0.20 kg ball moves at 90 km/h. Calculate its momentum in kg m/s.
12. Why should you not give the unit of a moment as J?

## Answers

1. N/kg (newton per kilogram).
2. 1 N = 1 kg m/s².
3. Any two: distance moved, displacement, extension, perpendicular
   distance from a pivot, braking distance.
4. 72 ÷ 3.6 = 20 m/s.
5. 0.45 kg; 90 s.
6. Momentum: kg m/s. Moment: Nm.
7. N/kg = (kg m/s²)/kg = m/s².
8. The velocity change was not divided by time, or a speed has been
   labelled as an acceleration.
9. W = m × g = 2.5 × 10 = 25 N.
10. 40 cm = 0.40 m; moment = 15 × 0.40 = 6.0 Nm.
11. 90 km/h = 25 m/s; p = 0.20 × 25 = 5.0 kg m/s.
12. Although N × m equals a joule, the joule is used for energy
    transferred; the specification gives the unit of moment as Nm, which
    shows the force and distance are perpendicular rather than along the
    same line.
