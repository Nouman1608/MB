## Section B

**6.** The table shows regions of the digestive system and four functions.

| Letter | Function |
|---|---|
| W | Storage of faeces |
| X | Production of bile |
| Y | Production of acid and protease |
| Z | Absorption of most nutrients |

**(a)** Name the region responsible for each of W, X, Y and Z. **[4]**

**(b)** State what happens to bile before it reaches the small intestine. **[1]**

**7.** Pancreatic juice contains amylase, protease and lipase.

**(a)** State the substrate and the end-product(s) of each of the three enzymes. **[3]**

**(b)** State why pancreatic juice is alkaline. **[2]**

**8.** A student mixed a protease from the stomach with protein at different pH values. The table shows the time taken to digest the protein.

| pH | Time to digest the protein (minutes) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 12 |
| 2 | 6 |
| 4 | 25 |
| 7 | no digestion after 60 |

**(a)** State the pH at which the enzyme worked fastest. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate how many times faster the digestion was at pH 2 than at pH 4. Show your working. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain the result at pH 7. **[2]**

**(d)** Suggest why the stomach protease would not work well in the small intestine. **[2]**

**9.** Bile is added to a drop of oil in water.

**(a)** Describe what happens to the oil. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why the stomach contains hydrochloric acid. **[2]**

**11.** Describe how peristalsis moves food along the oesophagus. **[3]**

**12.** Compare the functions of molars and canines. **[2]**

**13.** A person's gall bladder is removed. Suggest the effect on the digestion of a meal that is high in fat, and explain your answer. **[3]**

**14.** Explain why the bread in a sandwich tastes slightly sweet after it has been chewed for a long time. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A [1]. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3.** To bite and cut food [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Pulp [1]. **[1]**

**5.** The breakdown of large molecules [1] into small molecules [1]. **[2]**

**6.**

**(a)** W: rectum [1]. X: liver [1]. Y: stomach [1]. Z: ileum (small intestine) [1].

**(b)** It is stored in the gall bladder [1]. **[5]**

**7.**

**(a)** Amylase: starch to maltose [1]. Protease: protein to amino acids [1]. Lipase: lipids (fats) to fatty acids and glycerol [1].

**(b)** The juice neutralises the acid from the stomach [1], giving the alkaline conditions in which the enzymes in the small intestine (such as trypsin) work best [1]. **[5]**

**8.**

**(a)** pH 2 [1].

**(b)** 25 divided by 6 [1] = 4.2 times faster (accept 4) [1].

**(c)** At pH 7 the enzyme shape is changed (it is denatured) [1], so the substrate no longer fits the active site and the protein is not digested [1].

**(d)** The small intestine is alkaline (about pH 8) [1], which is not the optimum pH for the stomach protease, so its shape would change and it would stop working [1]. **[7]**

**9.**

**(a)** The oil is broken into many tiny droplets [1], which is called emulsification [1].

**(b)** The total surface area of the fat is bigger [1], so lipase can act on more of it and digest it faster [1].

**(c)** Before: 6 x 2 x 2 = 24 cm squared [1]. After: 8 x (6 x 1 x 1) = 48 cm squared [1]. Increase = 24 cm squared, which is double [1]. **[7]**

**10.** It kills bacteria that are swallowed with the food [1], and provides the acid conditions in which pepsin works best [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Circular muscles in the wall contract behind the food [1], which squeezes the food forwards [1]. The longitudinal muscles also contract, and waves of contraction follow one another along the tube [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Molars crush and grind food [1], but canines are pointed and tear food [1]. **[2]**

**13.** Less bile would be stored and released [1], so less fat would be emulsified, which gives a smaller surface area [1]. Lipase would digest the fat more slowly [1]. **[3]**

**14.** Saliva contains amylase [1], which digests starch in the bread to maltose [1], a sugar, which tastes sweet [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that bile digests fat, rather than emulsifies it.
- Saying that amylase produces glucose.
- Not naming both a substrate and its end-product.
- Mixing up the liver (makes bile) and the gall bladder (stores bile).
- Forgetting the surface area reason in answers about chewing and bile.
