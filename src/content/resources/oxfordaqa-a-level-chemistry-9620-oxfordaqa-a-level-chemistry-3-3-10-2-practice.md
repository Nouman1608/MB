This set practises **3.3.10.2 Electrophilic substitution** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3, including free-radical attack by chlorine. It is International A2 content, assessed in Unit 4 and in the synoptic Unit 5. If you get stuck, go back to the [electrophilic substitution study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-10-2/) or the [electrophilic substitution revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-10-2-revision-notes/). Use Ar values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0 and Cl = 35.5.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Which species is the electrophile when benzene reacts with fuming sulfuric acid?

A: H₂SO₄

B: HSO₄⁻

C: SO₃

D: SO₄²⁻

**[1]**

**2.** Write an equation to show how the electrophile forms when propanoyl chloride reacts with aluminium chloride. **[1]**

**3.** Name the organic product when benzene is warmed with chloroethane and anhydrous aluminium chloride. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why methylbenzene can be nitrated at a lower temperature than benzene. **[2]**

**5.** State the reagent and condition needed to convert methylbenzene into (dichloromethyl)benzene, C₆H₅CHCl₂. **[1]**

**6.** Give one reason why nitration is an important step in industrial synthesis and one reason why sulfonation is. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** Benzene reacts with butanoyl chloride, CH₃CH₂CH₂COCl, in the presence of aluminium chloride.

**(a)** Write an equation for the formation of the electrophile. **[1]**

**(b)** Outline the mechanism for the reaction of the electrophile with benzene. **[3]**

**(c)** Name the organic product. **[1]**

**(d)** Write an equation to show that aluminium chloride acts as a catalyst. **[1]**

**8.** Phenylamine is made from benzene in two steps.

**(a)** Give the reagents for step 1 and for step 2, and name the intermediate. **[2]**

**(b)** 9.36 g of benzene is used. Step 1 has a yield of 82.0% and step 2 a yield of 70.0%. Calculate the mass of phenylamine (Mr = 93.0) obtained. **[3]**

**(c)** State one industrial use of aromatic amines made by this route. **[1]**

**9.** Methylbenzene is warmed with fuming sulfuric acid.

**(a)** Name the main organic product. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why sulfur trioxide acts as an electrophile even though it has no overall charge. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe the structure of the intermediate formed when sulfur trioxide attacks the ring. **[2]**

**10.** Methylbenzene reacts with chlorine in bright sunlight. A product, X, has Mr = 126.5.

**(a)** Identify X. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the type of mechanism. **[1]**

**(c)** Write the two propagation steps that form X. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why chlorine attacks the side chain and not the ring under these conditions. **[1]**

**11.** Two isomers of C₇H₇Cl are (chloromethyl)benzene, C₆H₅CH₂Cl, and 1-chloro-4-methylbenzene, ClC₆H₄CH₃, in which the chlorine is bonded to the ring.

**(a)** Describe a test, using aqueous sodium hydroxide and silver nitrate, that would distinguish them. Give the results. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain the difference in reactivity of the chlorine atoms in the two isomers. **[3]**

**12.** 1-Phenylethanol, C₆H₅CH(OH)CH₃, is to be made from benzene in two steps.

**(a)** Give the reagents for each step and name the intermediate. **[3]**

**(b)** 7.80 g of benzene gives the intermediate with a 60.0% yield. Calculate the mass of intermediate (Mr = 120.0) formed. **[2]**

**13.** In a Friedel–Crafts alkylation, a large excess of benzene is used, and the aluminium chloride must be anhydrous.

**(a)** Explain why a large excess of benzene is used. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest why the aluminium chloride and the apparatus must be dry. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. The sulfur atom is strongly δ+ because three oxygen atoms withdraw electron density.

**2.** CH₃CH₂COCl + AlCl₃ → CH₃CH₂CO⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ [1]

**3.** Ethylbenzene [1]

**4.** The methyl group releases electron density into the ring [1]; so the ring is more electron-rich and attracts the electrophile NO₂⁺ more strongly, reacting faster [1]

**5.** Chlorine (excess) with ultraviolet light or sunlight [1]

**6.** Nitration: making explosives, or making aromatic amines by reduction [1]; sulfonation: making surfactants or sulfonamides [1]

**7. (a)** CH₃CH₂CH₂COCl + AlCl₃ → CH₃CH₂CH₂CO⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ [1]

**(b)** Curly arrow from the delocalised ring to the C⁺ of the acylium ion [1]; intermediate with H and COCH₂CH₂CH₃ on the same carbon and a horseshoe with + over the other five carbons [1]; curly arrow from the C–H bond into the ring, H⁺ lost and the delocalised ring restored [1]

**(c)** 1-Phenylbutan-1-one, C₆H₅COCH₂CH₂CH₃ [1]

**(d)** H⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ → AlCl₃ + HCl [1]

*Examiner insight:* the arrow to the electrophile must point at the positive carbon, not at the oxygen of the acylium ion.

**8. (a)** Step 1: concentrated nitric acid and concentrated sulfuric acid; intermediate nitrobenzene [1]. Step 2: tin and concentrated hydrochloric acid, then sodium hydroxide [1]

**(b)** n(benzene) = 9.36 ÷ 78.0 = 0.120 mol [1]; overall yield = 0.820 × 0.700 = 0.574, so n(phenylamine) = 0.120 × 0.574 = 0.0689 mol [1]; mass = 0.0689 × 93.0 = **6.41 g** [1]

**(c)** Making dyes [1]

*Examiner insight:* averaging the yields (76.0%) or adding them gives wrong answers; the fraction surviving step 1 is the starting point for step 2.

**9. (a)** 4-Methylbenzenesulfonic acid (accept 2-methylbenzenesulfonic acid as a minor product) [1]

**(b)** The sulfur atom carries a large δ+ charge because it is bonded to three electronegative oxygen atoms [1]

**(c)** The attacked carbon carries both H and an –SO₃⁻ group [1]; the other five carbons share a positive charge, drawn as a horseshoe with + inside [1]

**10. (a)** (Chloromethyl)benzene, C₆H₅CH₂Cl [1]

**(b)** Free-radical substitution [1]

**(c)** C₆H₅CH₃ + Cl• → C₆H₅CH₂• + HCl [1]; C₆H₅CH₂• + Cl₂ → C₆H₅CH₂Cl + Cl• [1]

**(d)** Removing an H from the methyl group leaves the delocalised ring intact, so the ring keeps its extra stability [1]

**11. (a)** Warm each with aqueous NaOH, then acidify with dilute nitric acid and add silver nitrate solution [1]; (chloromethyl)benzene: white precipitate [1]; 1-chloro-4-methylbenzene: no precipitate (stays colourless) [1]

**(b)** In (chloromethyl)benzene the C–Cl bond is a normal polar bond to a side-chain carbon, which OH⁻ can attack [1]; in 1-chloro-4-methylbenzene a lone pair on Cl overlaps with the delocalised ring, making the C–Cl bond stronger [1]; and the electron-rich ring repels the nucleophile, so no substitution occurs [1]

**12. (a)** Step 1: ethanoyl chloride with anhydrous aluminium chloride [1]; intermediate phenylethanone, C₆H₅COCH₃ [1]; step 2: NaBH₄ (reduction) [1]

**(b)** n(benzene) = 7.80 ÷ 78.0 = 0.100 mol; n(intermediate) = 0.100 × 0.600 = 0.0600 mol [1]; mass = 0.0600 × 120.0 = **7.20 g** [1]

**13. (a)** The alkylbenzene formed is more reactive than benzene, so with excess benzene the electrophile is more likely to meet benzene, giving the monosubstituted product [1]

**(b)** Water reacts with (hydrolyses) aluminium chloride, so it could no longer act as a catalyst [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Arrows starting from a ring carbon or from the H, rather than the delocalised ring.
- No + sign, or a full circle, in the intermediate.
- Forgetting HCl when the AlCl₃ catalyst is regenerated.
- Mixing up UV light (radical, side chain) with AlCl₃ (electrophile, ring).
- Adding or averaging step yields.
