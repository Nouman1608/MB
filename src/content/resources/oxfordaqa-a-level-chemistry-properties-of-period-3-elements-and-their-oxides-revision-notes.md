## Definitions

- **Basic oxide**: neutralises an acid, giving a salt plus water (Na₂O, MgO).
- **Acidic oxide**: neutralises a base, giving a salt plus water (SiO₂, P₄O₁₀, SO₂, SO₃).
- **Amphoteric**: reacts with acids and with bases. Al₂O₃.
- **Hydrolysis**: a reaction in which water breaks bonds in a compound. SiCl₄, PCl₅, aluminium chloride.
- **Co-ordinate bond**: a shared pair of electrons that both come from one atom. Holds Al₂Cl₆ together.

## Sodium and magnesium with water

| Reaction | Key point | Equation |
|---|---|---|
| sodium in cold water | fast; floats, melts, fizzes; pH about 14 | `2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂` |
| magnesium in cold water | slow, few bubbles; Mg(OH)₂ barely soluble; pH about 10 | `Mg + 2H₂O → Mg(OH)₂ + H₂` |
| magnesium heated in steam | burns with white light; oxide, not hydroxide | `Mg + H₂O → MgO + H₂` |

Na needs to lose one electron per atom; Mg needs to lose two, which costs far more energy.

## Elements with oxygen and with chlorine

| Element | With oxygen | With chlorine |
|---|---|---|
| Na | `4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O` | `2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl` (orange flame) |
| Mg | `2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO` | `Mg + Cl₂ → MgCl₂` (white flame) |
| Al | `4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃` | `2Al + 3Cl₂ → Al₂Cl₆` (pale yellow solid, sublimes) |
| Si | `Si + O₂ → SiO₂` | `Si + 2Cl₂ → SiCl₄` (colourless liquid) |
| P | `P₄ + 5O₂ → P₄O₁₀` | `P₄ + 10Cl₂ → 4PCl₅` (excess Cl₂; off-white solid) |
| S | `S + O₂ → SO₂`; then `2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃` with V₂O₅ | not required |

Oxidation state in the highest oxide: +1, +2, +3, +4, +5, +6 from Na to S. In the chlorides it runs
+1 to +5 from Na to P.

**Al₂Cl₆ in one line:** Al in AlCl₃ has only six outer electrons, so a Cl lone pair from a second unit
forms a co-ordinate bond; two such bonds make the bridged dimer.

## Structure and melting point

| Compound | Structure | What melting overcomes | Melting point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Na₂O, MgO | giant ionic | ionic attraction | high (MgO highest of all) |
| Al₂O₃ | giant ionic, partly covalent | ionic attraction | high, below MgO |
| SiO₂ | macromolecular | covalent bonds | high |
| P₄O₁₀, SO₃, SO₂ | simple molecular | van der Waals forces | low |
| NaCl (801 °C), MgCl₂ (714 °C) | giant ionic | ionic attraction | high |
| aluminium chloride | molecular Al₂Cl₆ when vaporised | weak intermolecular forces | sublimes about 180 °C |
| SiCl₄ (−69 °C) | simple molecular | van der Waals forces | low |
| PCl₅ | treated as simple molecular | van der Waals forces | vaporises about 160 to 170 °C |

Oxides, lowest to highest melting point: SO₂ < SO₃ < P₄O₁₀ < Na₂O < SiO₂ < Al₂O₃ < MgO.

**Method in steps: comparing two melting points**

1. Name each structure.
2. Name the force or bond overcome in each.
3. Say which is stronger and why (ion charge and size; number of electrons; covalent bonds versus
   intermolecular forces).
4. Link stronger forces to more energy needed, so higher melting point.

## Oxides with water

| Oxide | pH | Why | Equation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Na₂O | 14 | ionic; `O²⁻ + H₂O → 2OH⁻` | `Na₂O + H₂O → 2NaOH` |
| MgO | 9 | ionic, but hydroxide sparingly soluble | `MgO + H₂O → Mg(OH)₂` |
| Al₂O₃ | 7 | giant lattice, does not dissolve | no reaction |
| SiO₂ | 7 | macromolecular, does not dissolve | no reaction |
| P₄O₁₀ | 0 | simple molecular; forms acid | `P₄O₁₀ + 6H₂O → 4H₃PO₄` |
| SO₂ | 3 | weak acid, partly ionised | `SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃` |
| SO₃ | 0 | strong acid | `SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄` |

## Chlorides with water

| Chloride | Observation | Equation | pH |
|---|---|---|---|
| NaCl | dissolves | `NaCl → Na⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq)` | 7 |
| MgCl₂ | dissolves | `MgCl₂ → Mg²⁺(aq) + 2Cl⁻(aq)` | 6 |
| AlCl₃ | hisses, steamy fumes | `AlCl₃ + 6H₂O → [Al(H₂O)₆]³⁺ + 3Cl⁻` | 3 |
| SiCl₄ | violent; white solid and fumes | `SiCl₄ + 2H₂O → SiO₂ + 4HCl` | 1 |
| PCl₅ | violent; fumes | `PCl₅ + 4H₂O → H₃PO₄ + 5HCl` | 1 |

- Acidity of hydrated aluminium: `[Al(H₂O)₆]³⁺ ⇌ [Al(H₂O)₅(OH)]²⁺ + H⁺`, because the small, 3+ ion
  polarises the O–H bonds of its water ligands.
- With little water: `AlCl₃ + 3H₂O → Al(OH)₃ + 3HCl` and `PCl₅ + H₂O → POCl₃ + 2HCl`.
- The pattern: ionic chlorides dissolve; covalent chlorides are hydrolysed and release HCl.

## Structures of acids and anions

| Formed from | Acid | Anions | Shape at P or S |
|---|---|---|---|
| P₄O₁₀, PCl₅ | H₃PO₄ (P=O, three P–OH) | H₂PO₄⁻, HPO₄²⁻, PO₄³⁻ | tetrahedral |
| SO₂ | H₂SO₃ (S=O, two S–OH, lone pair) | HSO₃⁻, SO₃²⁻ | pyramidal |
| SO₃ | H₂SO₄ (two S=O, two S–OH) | HSO₄⁻, SO₄²⁻ | tetrahedral |
| SiCl₄, PCl₅ | HCl | Cl⁻ | not applicable |

Negative charges sit on single-bonded oxygen atoms. Each proton lost turns one O–H into O⁻.

## Acid and base equations

| Substance | Reagent | Equation |
|---|---|---|
| Na₂O | HNO₃ | `Na₂O + 2HNO₃ → 2NaNO₃ + H₂O` |
| MgO | HCl | `MgO + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂O` |
| Al₂O₃ | HNO₃ | `Al₂O₃ + 6HNO₃ → 2Al(NO₃)₃ + 3H₂O` |
| Al₂O₃ | KOH | `Al₂O₃ + 2KOH + 3H₂O → 2KAl(OH)₄` |
| SiO₂ | Na₂O, heat | `SiO₂ + Na₂O → Na₂SiO₃` |
| P₄O₁₀ | KOH | `P₄O₁₀ + 12KOH → 4K₃PO₄ + 6H₂O` |
| SO₂ | Ca(OH)₂ | `SO₂ + Ca(OH)₂ → CaSO₃ + H₂O` |
| MgCl₂ | NaOH | `MgCl₂ + 2NaOH → Mg(OH)₂ + 2NaCl` |
| AlCl₃ | excess NaOH | `AlCl₃ + 4NaOH → NaAl(OH)₄ + 3NaCl` |
| SiCl₄ | NaOH | `SiCl₄ + 6NaOH → Na₂SiO₃ + 4NaCl + 3H₂O` |
| PCl₅ | NaOH | `PCl₅ + 8NaOH → Na₃PO₄ + 5NaCl + 4H₂O` |

**Method in steps: chloride plus alkali**

1. Write the hydrolysis products (for PCl₅: H₃PO₄ + 5HCl).
2. Count the acidic hydrogens (3 + 5 = 8).
3. Use that many OH⁻; the products are the sodium salts plus water.

## Must-know distinctions

- **AlCl₃ vs Al₂Cl₆**: the element reaction gives Al₂Cl₆; AlCl₃ is the formula used for its reaction
  with water.
- **Dissolves vs hydrolysed**: NaCl and MgCl₂ dissolve; SiCl₄ and PCl₅ react with water.
- **Insoluble vs neutral**: Al₂O₃ and SiO₂ leave water at pH 7, yet one is amphoteric and one is acidic.
- **H₂SO₃ vs H₂SO₄**: S stays +4 from SO₂ and +6 from SO₃.
- **Mg + cold water vs steam**: hydroxide vs oxide.

## Quick self-test

1. Write the equation for aluminium reacting with chlorine, as the specification requires.
2. Give the pH of magnesium chloride solution, to the nearest whole number.
3. Which Period 3 chloride listed in the specification is a liquid at room temperature?
4. Explain in one sentence why SiCl₄ has a low melting point.
5. Write the full hydrolysis equation for PCl₅.
6. Name the bond type that joins the two AlCl₃ units in Al₂Cl₆.
7. How many moles of HCl form when 0.040 mol of PCl₅ is fully hydrolysed?
8. What mass of MgCl₂ forms when 0.486 g of Mg burns in chlorine? (Ar: Mg = 24.3, Cl = 35.5)
9. How many moles of Cl₂ react with 0.050 mol of P₄ to form PCl₅?
10. How many moles of NaOH neutralise 0.015 mol of SiCl₄ to give Na₂SiO₃?
11. State the shape around sulfur in HSO₃⁻.
12. Why does AlCl₃ solution have a lower pH than MgCl₂ solution?

### Answers

1. `2Al + 3Cl₂ → Al₂Cl₆`
2. About 6.
3. SiCl₄.
4. Its molecules are held together only by weak van der Waals forces, which need little energy to overcome.
5. `PCl₅ + 4H₂O → H₃PO₄ + 5HCl`
6. Co-ordinate (dative covalent) bonds.
7. 5 × 0.040 = **0.20 mol**.
8. 0.486 / 24.3 = 0.0200 mol; Mr MgCl₂ = 95.3; mass = **1.91 g**.
9. 10 × 0.050 = **0.50 mol**.
10. 6 × 0.015 = **0.090 mol**.
11. Pyramidal (three bonding regions and one lone pair).
12. The Al³⁺ ion has a higher charge density than Mg²⁺, so its water ligands are polarised more and more H⁺ is released.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing `2Al + 3Cl₂ → 2AlCl₃`; the specification lists Al₂Cl₆ as the product.
- Describing SiCl₄ or PCl₅ as "dissolving to give a neutral solution".
- Leaving out the steamy fumes of HCl when describing covalent chloride hydrolysis.
- Giving MgCl₂ the same pH as NaCl without comment.
- Saying Si–Cl or P–Cl bonds break when the chlorides melt.
- Using 3 mol NaOH per mole of PCl₅ instead of 8.
- Writing MgO as the product of magnesium with cold water.
- Calling Al₂O₃ a "neutral oxide" because water stays at pH 7.
- Missing sulfur's lone pair in H₂SO₃, HSO₃⁻ or SO₃²⁻.
