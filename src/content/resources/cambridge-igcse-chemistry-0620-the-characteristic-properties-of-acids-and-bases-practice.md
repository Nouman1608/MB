## Section A

**1.** Complete the word equations.

**(a)** magnesium + sulfuric acid → ______ + ______ **[1]**

**(b)** sodium carbonate + hydrochloric acid → ______ + ______ + ______ **[1]**

**2.** A solution turns thymolphthalein blue. State the colour of litmus and of methyl orange in the same solution. **[2]**

**3.** Which substance is a base but **not** an alkali?

A: potassium hydroxide

B: sodium hydroxide

C: copper(II) oxide

D: lithium hydroxide **[1]**

**4.** State the ion present in all aqueous solutions of acids and the ion present in all aqueous solutions of alkalis. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* Define a *weak acid*. **[1]**

**6.** Universal indicator paper turns orange in solution X and purple in solution Y. State what each colour shows about the solution. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A student adds powdered zinc carbonate to dilute nitric acid until no more reacts.

**(a)** Describe **two** observations. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the balanced symbol equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe a test to identify the gas produced, giving the result. **[2]**

**8.** Solid ammonium chloride is warmed with aqueous calcium hydroxide.

**(a)** Name the gas given off. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the test for this gas and its result. **[2]**

**(c)** State what this reaction shows about calcium hydroxide. **[1]**

**9.** Four solutions were tested with universal indicator paper.

| Solution | Colour |
|---|---|
| A | Green |
| B | Red |
| C | Blue |
| D | Yellow |

**(a)** Give the letter of the solution with the highest hydrogen ion concentration. **[1]**

**(b)** Arrange the solutions in order of increasing pH. **[1]**

**(c)** Solution C is added slowly to solution B until in excess. Describe how the colour of universal indicator in the mixture changes. **[2]**

**10.** Aqueous potassium hydroxide neutralises dilute hydrochloric acid.

**(a)** Write the ionic equation for the reaction, including state symbols. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the spectator ions. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Hydrogen chloride gas reacts with ammonia gas to form a white solid.

HCl(g) + NH₃(g) → NH₄Cl(s)

Use the proton definitions to identify the acid and the base. Explain your answer. **[3]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A student has two bottles of acid, both 0.1 mol/dm³: hydrochloric acid and ethanoic acid.

**(a)** Write equations to show how each acid behaves in water. **[2]**

**(b)** Predict which acid has the lower pH. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**(c)** 25 cm³ of each acid is added to excess calcium carbonate. Compare the rate of effervescence and the total volume of carbon dioxide produced. **[2]**

**13.** A piece of iron is added to dilute hydrochloric acid.

**(a)** Write the word equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe a test for the gas produced and give the result. **[2]**

**(c)** A piece of copper is added to a second sample of the same acid. Predict what is seen. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Magnesium sulfate + hydrogen [1].

**(b)** Sodium chloride + water + carbon dioxide [1].

**2.** Litmus: blue [1]; methyl orange: yellow [1].

**3.** **C** [1]. Copper(II) oxide is a metal oxide (a base) but is insoluble in water.

**4.** Acids: H⁺ (hydrogen ions) [1]; alkalis: OH⁻ (hydroxide ions) [1].

**5.** An acid that is partially dissociated (ionised) in aqueous solution [1].

**6.** X: weakly acidic (pH about 3–4) [1]; Y: strongly alkaline (pH about 11–14) [1].

**7. (a)** Any two from: effervescence / bubbles [1]; the white solid dissolves / disappears (until excess remains) [1]; a colourless solution forms.

**(b)** ZnCO₃ + 2HNO₃ → Zn(NO₃)₂ + H₂O + CO₂: correct products [1]; balanced [1].

**(c)** Bubble the gas through limewater [1]; limewater turns milky / cloudy (carbon dioxide) [1].

**8. (a)** Ammonia [1].

**(b)** Hold damp red litmus paper in the gas [1]; it turns blue [1].

**(c)** It is a base / it reacts as a base with ammonium salts [1].

**9. (a)** B [1].

**(b)** B, D, A, C [1].

**(c)** Starts red [1]; changes through orange/yellow to green at neutral, then blue when alkali is in excess [1].

**10. (a)** H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O [1]; state symbols (aq), (aq), (l) [1].

**(b)** K⁺ and Cl⁻ [1].

**11.** HCl is the acid [1] because it donates a proton (H⁺) [1]; NH₃ is the base because it accepts the proton, forming NH₄⁺ [1].

**12. (a)** HCl(aq) → H⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) [1]; CH₃COOH(aq) ⇌ H⁺(aq) + CH₃COO⁻(aq) [1].

**(b)** Hydrochloric acid [1]; it is completely dissociated, so its H⁺ concentration is higher than that of the partially dissociated ethanoic acid [1].

**(c)** Faster effervescence with hydrochloric acid [1]; the same total volume of carbon dioxide, because both contain the same amount (moles) of acid [1].

**13. (a)** Iron + hydrochloric acid → iron(II) chloride + hydrogen [1].

**(b)** Hold a lighted splint at the mouth of the tube [1]; the gas burns with a squeaky pop (hydrogen) [1].

**(c)** No reaction / no bubbles, because copper is too unreactive to react with dilute acid [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing only two products for an acid + carbonate reaction.
- Muddling indicator colours, especially methyl orange (yellow in alkali) and thymolphthalein (colourless in acid).
- Describing a pH value without linking it to H⁺ concentration when the question asks for a comparison.
- Missing state symbols in the neutralisation ionic equation.
- Saying ethanoic acid produces less gas: it produces gas more slowly, not less of it.
- Defining a strong acid as "concentrated".
