This guide expands on **Subtopic 1.1: Major Themes of the Qur'an**, part of Paper 1 in Cambridge O Level
Islamiyat (2058). The site's existing guide to [Paper 1](/resources/o-level-islamiyat-paper-1/) covers all
four Paper 1 subtopics, but develops 1.1 in only a single summary bullet before moving on to focus on 1.2's
compilation history and the connected timeline of 1.3 and 1.4 -- this guide develops 1.1 specifically.

## Where this fits in 2058

Subtopic 1.1 is examined through Question 1, which is compulsory and sets three short passages on the
major themes of the Qur'an, from which candidates choose any two to write on. Because this question is
compulsory, breadth across the theme categories below matters more here than in subtopics only examined
through optional essay questions.

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE O LEVEL ISLAMIYAT (2058) -- SUBTOPIC 1.1: MAJOR THEMES OF THE QUR'AN**

The Qur'an's major themes are organised around three broad categories:

- God in Himself: the nature and attributes of God (Allah), including His oneness (tawhid), power,
  knowledge, and mercy
- God's relationship with the created world: how God relates to creation, humanity's purpose and
  responsibility, guidance for individual conduct and communal life, and accountability in the afterlife
  (akhirah)
- God's Messengers: the role and significance of the prophets sent by God, the guidance and revelation
  they brought, and their relationship with the communities they were sent to

Candidates study set passages illustrating these themes (drawn from an approved list), with reference to
both the original Arabic text and its English translation, since examination passages are set in both
languages.

## How to approach it

Because Question 1 gives a choice of any two from three set passages, revision should aim for confident
familiarity with a spread of passages across all three theme categories, rather than deep knowledge of
only one or two -- a candidate who has only prepared passages illustrating one category risks finding none
of the three set passages accessible on exam day.

## Tawhid: the foundational theme

Tawhid, the oneness of God, is often treated as the foundational theme underlying all others in the
Qur'an, since it frames how every other theme (God's relationship with creation, the role of the
prophets, guidance for conduct) is ultimately understood. Passages illustrating tawhid typically emphasise
God's uniqueness, that nothing shares in His nature or is worthy of worship alongside Him, and His power
and knowledge over all creation. Recognising when a set passage is centrally about tawhid, even if its
surface subject matter is something else (such as a description of creation), is a skill candidates are
expected to develop through close reading.

## Guidance and accountability, together

Themes concerning God's relationship with creation frequently pair practical guidance for how to live with
the theme of accountability -- the idea that human conduct will be judged, and that the afterlife (akhirah)
gives ultimate meaning to moral choices made in this life. A passage instructing believers toward a
particular conduct (honesty, charity, patience) is rarely presented as guidance alone in the Qur'an, but is
typically also linked to its consequence in the hereafter -- understanding this pairing helps candidates
explain not just what a passage teaches, but why it matters.

## The role of God's Messengers as a theme

Passages illustrating the third category focus on why God sends messengers at all -- to deliver guidance,
warn communities of the consequences of rejecting that guidance, and model righteous conduct for others to
follow. The Qur'an frequently draws parallels between different prophets' missions, presenting them as a
connected chain conveying a consistent core message across different times and communities, rather than
as unrelated individual figures. A passage in this category often combines an account of a specific
prophet's mission with a broader lesson about faith, patience, or the response of the people they were
sent to guide, which is why answers that only narrate the prophet's story without drawing out this wider
lesson tend to miss the fuller thematic point.

## Worked example: identifying a passage's theme category

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official past
paper or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - read the passage fully before categorising it:
avoid assuming a theme from the first line alone.

Step 2 - ask what the passage is fundamentally establishing about
God:
His nature (God in Himself), His relationship with creation and
humanity's response to it, or the role of a prophet or messenger.

Step 3 - note any secondary themes present:
many passages touch more than one category, so identify the
primary theme while acknowledging others.

Step 4 - support the categorisation with a specific textual
reference:
naming a specific phrase or idea from the passage, rather than
categorising it in general terms alone.
```

## Common mistakes

Treating the three theme categories as rigidly separate, when many passages genuinely combine more than
one. Preparing passages from only one or two theme categories, leaving a candidate with no accessible
option if the set passages that year favour a different category. Describing a passage's content without
identifying which of the three theme categories it primarily illustrates. Answering Question 1 with only
general knowledge of Qur'anic themes rather than specific reference to the set passage provided.

## Quick revision checklist

- Be able to categorise a Qur'anic passage under God in Himself, God's relationship with creation, or
  God's Messengers.
- Prepare set passages spanning all three categories, not just one or two.
- Practise identifying secondary themes within a passage, alongside its primary theme.
- Support every thematic identification with a specific reference to the passage's content or wording.
- Revise how tawhid underlies the other theme categories, since recognising this connection strengthens
  answers across all three.
