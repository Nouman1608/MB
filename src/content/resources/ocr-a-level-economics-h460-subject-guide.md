OCR A Level Economics (H460) covers how markets allocate resources, why they sometimes
fail, and how national economies and governments behave within a global context. The
current specification was first assessed in 2021. Students take three compulsory
written components at the end of the course: Microeconomics, Macroeconomics and Themes
in Economics. Each lasts 2 hours, carries 80 marks and is worth a third of the A Level.
The third component is synoptic, applying both halves of the course to an unseen theme.
This page sets out how the content is arranged and what that means for planning.

## How the course is organised

The content has two main areas, followed by a component that combines them.

**Microeconomics** has five areas:

- *Introduction to microeconomics*: the basic economic problem and how economists think
  about choice.
- *The role of markets*: demand, supply, price and how markets allocate resources.
- *Business objectives*: what firms aim for and why their aims can differ.
- *Market structures*: competition, market power and how firms behave in different
  types of market.
- *The labour market*: how wages and employment are determined.

**Macroeconomics** also has five areas:

- *Aggregate demand and aggregate supply*: the model at the centre of macroeconomic
  analysis.
- *Economic policy objectives*: the goals governments pursue, such as growth and stable
  prices.
- *Implementing policy*: the tools available and their effects.
- *The global context*: trade, exchange rates and development.
- *The financial sector*: the role of banks, financial markets and central banks.

**Themes in economics** has no separate content of its own. It is the component in which
micro and macro ideas are brought together and applied.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H460/01 | Microeconomics | 2 hours | 80 | 33.33% |
| H460/02 | Macroeconomics | 2 hours | 80 | 33.33% |
| H460/03 | Themes in Economics | 2 hours | 80 | 33.34% |

OCR prints each weighting as 33⅓%; the table shows 33.33%, 33.33% and 33.34% so that
the figures add up to 100%. All three components are compulsory, untiered and taken at
the end of the course.

Component 03 is the synoptic paper. It applies content from both Component 01 and
Component 02 to an unseen theme, so you will not know the specific context in advance.
That makes it a test of how flexibly you can use the whole course, not of a particular
block of content.

## How the parts fit together

Microeconomics and macroeconomics are taught separately, but they rest on shared
foundations. Supply and demand analysis from the role of markets reappears in the labour
market and, at the level of the whole economy, in aggregate demand and aggregate supply.
Business objectives and market structures help explain how firms respond to changes in
interest rates, taxes or trade conditions discussed in macroeconomics. The financial
sector links directly to the implementation of monetary policy.

The skills are common to every component:

- **Knowledge and understanding** of economic terms, models and diagrams.
- **Application** of that knowledge to data and real-world contexts.
- **Analysis** that sets out a clear chain of reasoning from cause to effect.
- **Evaluation** that weighs evidence, considers alternatives and reaches a judgement.

Themes in Economics makes these links explicit, but questions on Components 01 and 02
also reward answers that recognise the connections between micro and macro.

## Planning your study

Schools vary in the order they teach H460. Some teach microeconomics first and then
macroeconomics; others run the two in parallel with different teachers. Either way, a
workable plan is:

- **First year:** most or all of microeconomics, and the opening macroeconomics areas
  (aggregate demand and aggregate supply and policy objectives).
- **Second year:** the remaining macroeconomics, including the global context and the
  financial sector, followed by synoptic practice for Themes in Economics and revision
  of the whole course.

From the start, build a diagram file in which each diagram is labelled, explained and
linked to at least one real example. Read economic news regularly, since the unseen
theme in Component 03 favours students who are used to applying theory to unfamiliar
situations. Start exam-style questions as each area is completed, move to full papers in
the second year, and use the exam preparation page for detailed advice on Component 03.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in OCR A Level Economics?**
Three compulsory components: Microeconomics, Macroeconomics and Themes in Economics. Each is a 2-hour written paper worth 80 marks.

**Are the three H460 papers equally weighted?**
Yes. OCR prints each as 33⅓% of the A Level.

**What is Themes in Economics?**
It is Component 03, a synoptic paper that applies content from both the microeconomics and macroeconomics components to an unseen theme.

**Is OCR A Level Economics linear?**
Yes. All three components are taken at the end of the course, and there are no tiers.

**When was H460 first assessed?**
The current H460 specification was first assessed in 2021.
