## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Which base is a pyrimidine found only in DNA? **[1]**

A: Adenine

B: Guanine

C: Thymine

D: Uracil

**2.** Which bond does DNA polymerase catalyse the formation of? **[1]**

A: Hydrogen bonds between complementary bases

B: Phosphodiester bonds between adjacent nucleotides

C: Peptide bonds between amino acids

D: Glycosidic bonds between sugars

**3.** Describe the structure of a molecule of ATP. **[2]**

**4.** One strand of DNA has the sequence 5′-ATCAGGTC-3′. Write the complementary strand in the 5′ to 3′ direction. **[2]**

**5.** A sample of double-stranded DNA contains 35% adenine. Calculate the percentage of guanine. **[2]**

**6.** State two structural differences between mRNA and DNA. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** A 40-base-pair section of DNA contains 14 C–G base pairs.

**(a)** Calculate the total number of hydrogen bonds holding the two strands together in this section. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why sections rich in C–G pairs need more energy to separate. **[2]**

**8.** In the experiment of Meselson and Stahl (1958), bacteria grown for many generations in ¹⁵N were transferred to ¹⁴N, and their DNA was separated by density after each generation.

**(a)** Describe the DNA bands expected after one generation in ¹⁴N if replication were (i) semi-conservative and (ii) conservative. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of DNA molecules that would be hybrid after four generations of semi-conservative replication. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how the result after one generation showed that replication is not conservative. **[1]**

**9.** Describe the semi-conservative replication of DNA. **[5]**

**10.** Explain why the lagging strand is synthesised in fragments. **[3]**

**11.** Explain why a purine always pairs with a pyrimidine in DNA. **[2]**

**12.** A virus contains a nucleic acid with this base composition: A 24%, T 31%, G 20%, C 25%. Suggest, with a reason, the structure of this nucleic acid. **[2]**

**13.** Describe the structure of a DNA molecule. **[4]**

**14.** Compare the structure of an RNA nucleotide with the structure of ATP. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. Uracil is the pyrimidine found only in RNA; A and G are purines. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A nucleotide containing the base adenine and the sugar ribose [1], with three phosphate groups attached [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Complement read 3′ to 5′: TAGTCCAG [1]; written 5′ to 3′: **5′-GACCTGAT-3′** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** T = 35%, so A + T = 70% and C + G = 30% [1]; G = **15%** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two: mRNA is single-stranded, DNA is a double helix [1]; mRNA contains ribose, DNA deoxyribose [1]; mRNA contains uracil, DNA thymine; mRNA is much shorter. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 26 A–T pairs [1]; (14 × 3) + (26 × 2) = 42 + 52 = **94** [1].

**(b)** Each C–G pair is held by three hydrogen bonds rather than two [1], so more hydrogen bonds must be broken to separate the strands [1]. **[4]**

**8. (a)** (i) A single intermediate (hybrid) band [1]. (ii) Two bands, one heavy and one light [1].

**(b)** 2⁴ = 16 molecules, of which 2 are hybrid [1]; 2 ÷ 16 × 100 = **12.5%** [1].

**(c)** Only one intermediate band appeared, so every molecule contained one heavy and one light strand, which conservative replication cannot produce [1]. **[5]**

**9.** Hydrogen bonds between complementary bases break and the two strands separate [1]. Each strand acts as a template [1]. Free DNA nucleotides pair with exposed bases by complementary base pairing, A with T and C with G [1]. DNA polymerase joins the new nucleotides by forming phosphodiester bonds [1]. Each new molecule contains one original strand and one new strand [1]. **[5]**

**10.** The two strands of DNA are antiparallel [1]. DNA polymerase can only add nucleotides to the 3′ end, so it builds new strands only 5′ to 3′ [1]. On one template it must work away from the replication fork, so it makes short fragments as more template is exposed, which DNA ligase joins [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Purines have a double ring and pyrimidines a single ring [1], so a purine–pyrimidine pair is always the same width, keeping the diameter of the double helix constant [1]. **[2]**

**12.** It is single-stranded DNA [1]: it contains thymine, so it is DNA, but A does not equal T and G does not equal C, so the bases are not paired [1]. **[2]**

**13.** Two polynucleotide strands wound into a double helix [1]. Each strand has a sugar–phosphate backbone with nucleotides joined by phosphodiester bonds [1]. The strands are antiparallel [1]. They are held together by hydrogen bonds between complementary bases: A–T with two and C–G with three [1]. **[4]**

**14.** Similarity: both contain the sugar ribose and a nitrogenous base [1]. Differences: an RNA nucleotide has one phosphate group, ATP has three [1]; an RNA nucleotide may contain adenine, uracil, cytosine or guanine, whereas the base in ATP is always adenine [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* "Compare" needs at least one similarity as well as differences. Giving ATP deoxyribose is a common error.

*Examiner insight:* In question 10, both the antiparallel strands and the 5′ to 3′ rule are needed. Either alone does not explain why one strand is discontinuous.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not reversing the complementary strand when asked for 5′ to 3′.
- Forgetting to subtract C–G pairs from the total to find A–T pairs.
- Assigning three hydrogen bonds to A–T.
- Saying DNA polymerase forms hydrogen bonds.
- Using base-pairing rules for single-stranded nucleic acids.
