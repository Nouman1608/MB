These questions cover **2.1.1 Atomic structure and isotopes** in OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432), Version 3.1 (May 2026): statements (a) to (e). Mass spectra use singly charged ions only, as the specification requires, and whole-number isotopic masses unless stated. Isotope abundances in the questions are given data for the calculation.

Revise with the [2.1.1 revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-h432-ocr-a-level-chemistry-2-1-1-revision-notes/) and the [atomic structure and isotopes study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-atomic-structure-and-isotopes/). Questions on formulae and equations are in the [2.1.2 practice set](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-h432-ocr-a-level-chemistry-2-1-2-practice/).

Use Ar values: H = 1.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0, Mg = 24.3, S = 32.1.

## Questions

**1.** Give the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in each species.

**(a)** ⁵⁹Co³⁺ (atomic number 27) **[1]**

**(b)** ³¹P³⁻ (atomic number 15) **[1]**

**(c)** ¹⁸O (atomic number 8) **[1]**

**2.** A species contains 20 protons, 20 neutrons and 18 electrons. Write its symbol, showing its mass number and its charge. **[2]**

**3.** **(a)** Define the term relative atomic mass. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the relative atomic mass of chlorine is not a whole number. **[1]**

**4.** Bromine-79 and bromine-81 are isotopes.

**(a)** Explain why the two isotopes have the same chemical reactions. **[2]**

**(b)** State one way in which the isotopes differ, other than in the number of neutrons. **[1]**

**5.** A sample of lithium is 7.6% lithium-6 and 92.4% lithium-7. Calculate the relative atomic mass of lithium to one decimal place. **[2]**

**6.** The mass spectrum of a sample of chromium has four peaks. The heights are relative values.

| m/z | 50 | 52 | 53 | 54 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Relative height | 0.52 | 10.0 | 1.13 | 0.28 |

Calculate the relative atomic mass of chromium in this sample to one decimal place. **[3]**

**7.** Silver has two isotopes, silver-107 and silver-109, and a relative atomic mass of 107.9. Calculate the percentage abundance of each isotope. **[3]**

**8.** A sample of silicon contains three isotopes. Silicon-28 is 92.2% and silicon-29 is 4.7% of the sample; the rest is silicon-30.

**(a)** State the percentage of silicon-30. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of silicon to one decimal place. **[2]**

**9.** Lead in a rock sample has the isotopic composition below.

| Isotope | ²⁰⁴Pb | ²⁰⁶Pb | ²⁰⁷Pb | ²⁰⁸Pb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Abundance / % | 1.4 | 24.1 | 22.1 | 52.4 |

Calculate the relative atomic mass of lead in this sample to one decimal place. **[2]**

**10.** **(a)** State whether "relative molecular mass" or "relative formula mass" should be used for carbon dioxide and for silicon dioxide, and explain the difference. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the relative formula mass of ammonium sulfate, (NH₄)₂SO₄. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the relative formula mass of hydrated magnesium sulfate, MgSO₄·7H₂O. **[1]**

**11.** Later models of the atom describe electrons using orbitals, yet chemists still use the Bohr model of electrons in shells. Suggest why. **[2]**

**12.** The mass spectrum of chlorine atoms shows peaks at m/z 35 and 37 with heights in the ratio 3 : 1.

**(a)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of chlorine. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest why chlorine from a different source could give a slightly different value. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 27 protons, 32 neutrons, 24 electrons [1]

**(b)** 15 protons, 16 neutrons, 18 electrons [1]

**(c)** 8 protons, 10 neutrons, 8 electrons [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A 3− ion has gained three electrons; subtracting the charge, as for a positive ion, is the most common slip.

**2.** ⁴⁰Ca: 20 protons identify calcium and 20 + 20 = 40 is the mass number [1]; two fewer electrons than protons, so the charge is 2+: **⁴⁰Ca²⁺** [1] **[2]**

**3. (a)** The weighted mean mass of an atom of an element [1] compared with 1/12th of the mass of an atom of carbon-12 [1]

**(b)** Chlorine is a mixture of isotopes with different masses, so the weighted mean lies between their masses [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* "Average mass of an atom compared with carbon-12" misses two required ideas: weighting by abundance and the 1/12th.

**4. (a)** Both have 35 protons and 35 electrons [1], so the same electron arrangement, which determines chemical reactions [1]

**(b)** Mass (or a mass-dependent physical property such as density) [1] **[3]**

**5.** (6 × 7.6 + 7 × 92.4) ÷ 100 [1] = 6.924 = **6.9** [1] **[2]**

**6.** Sum of heights = 0.52 + 10.0 + 1.13 + 0.28 = 11.93 [1]. Σ(m/z × height) = 26.0 + 520.0 + 59.89 + 15.12 = 621.01 [1]. Ar = 621.01 ÷ 11.93 = **52.1** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The heights do not total 100, so dividing by 100 gives 6.2, which is impossible: Ar must lie between 50 and 54.

**7.** Let silver-109 be x%: 107(100 − x) + 109x = 10 790 [1]. 10 700 + 2x = 10 790, so x = 45 [1]. **55% silver-107 and 45% silver-109** [1] **[3]**

**8. (a)** 100 − 92.2 − 4.7 = **3.1%** [1]

**(b)** (28 × 92.2 + 29 × 4.7 + 30 × 3.1) ÷ 100 [1] = 28.109 = **28.1** [1] **[3]**

**9.** (204 × 1.4 + 206 × 24.1 + 207 × 22.1 + 208 × 52.4) ÷ 100 [1] = 20 724.1 ÷ 100 = **207.2** [1] **[2]**

**10. (a)** Carbon dioxide: relative molecular mass; silicon dioxide: relative formula mass [1]. CO₂ is made of simple molecules, while SiO₂ has a giant covalent structure with no discrete molecules [1]

**(b)** 2 × (14.0 + 4 × 1.0) + 32.1 + 4 × 16.0 = **132.1** [1]

**(c)** 24.3 + 32.1 + 64.0 + 7 × 18.0 = **246.4** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* The water of crystallisation counts in full: 7 × 18.0, not 18.0.

**11.** The shell model still explains the observations needed, such as elements in the same group having the same number of outer-shell electrons and so similar reactions [1]. Different models are useful for explaining different phenomena; a simpler model is kept where it works [1] **[2]**

**12. (a)** (35 × 3 + 37 × 1) ÷ 4 = **35.5** [1]

**(b)** The relative abundances of the isotopes may differ slightly between sources [1] **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving "weighted mean" or "1/12th of carbon-12" out of the definition of Ar.
- Defining isotopes by mass number only, without neutrons.
- Dividing by 100 when peak heights are relative values.
- Forgetting to subtract the given abundances from 100 when one is missing.
- Using "relative molecular mass" for a giant structure.
- Counting the water of crystallisation only once.
