## Section A

**1.** Which statement describes metallic bonding?

A: Electrons are transferred from metal atoms to non-metal atoms.

B: Pairs of electrons are shared between neighbouring metal atoms.

C: Positive ions are attracted to a sea of delocalised electrons.

D: Positive and negative ions attract each other in a lattice.

**[1]**

**2.** Which particles carry the electric current through a copper wire?

A: copper atoms

B: copper ions

C: delocalised electrons

D: protons

**[1]**

**3.** State what is meant by the term *delocalised electrons*. **[1]**

**4.** State what is meant by the terms *malleable* and *ductile*. **[2]**

**5.** Potassium has the electronic configuration 2,8,8,1. State the charge on the ions in a potassium lattice and the number of electrons each atom contributes to the sea of electrons. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Describe metallic bonding. **[3]**

**7.** Describe the features that a labelled diagram of the structure of a solid metal should show. **[3]**

**8.** Explain, in terms of structure and bonding, why metals are good conductors of electricity. **[2]**

**9.** Gold can be hammered into extremely thin sheets.

**(a)** State the name of this property. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain this property in terms of structure and bonding. **[3]**

**10.** Copper is used to make electrical wires.

**(a)** Explain why copper can be drawn into wires. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why a copper wire conducts electricity. **[2]**

**11.** A student writes: "When a metal is bent, the metallic bonds break and the layers of atoms slide over each other." Identify **two** errors in this statement. **[2]**

**12.** Compare how electricity is conducted by solid magnesium and by molten magnesium chloride. **[4]**

**13.** Graphite and metals both conduct electricity as solids. State the feature their structures have in common that explains this, and give one way in which their structures differ. **[2]**

**14.** Mercury is a metal that is liquid at room temperature. Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc.

**(a)** Predict whether liquid mercury conducts electricity. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**(b)** Brass is harder and less malleable than pure copper. Suggest why, using the idea of layers of ions in the lattice. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. A describes ionic bond formation, B covalent bonding and D an ionic lattice.

**2.** **C** [1].

**3.** Electrons that are **not attached to any one atom** and are **free to move through the structure** [1].

**4.** Malleable: can be **hammered (or pressed) into shape** without breaking [1]. Ductile: can be **drawn out into wires** [1].

**5.** **1+** (K⁺) [1]; **one** electron per atom [1].

**6.** A **giant lattice of positive ions** [1]; surrounded by a **sea of delocalised electrons** [1]; held together by **electrostatic attraction between the positive ions and the delocalised electrons** [1].

**7.** **Positive ions** arranged in **regular rows / layers**, labelled [1]; **electrons drawn between the ions**, not attached to any one ion, labelled **delocalised electrons** [1]; **no negative ions** and no electron pairs drawn as bonds between two ions [1].

**8.** Metals have **delocalised electrons** [1] that are **free to move through the lattice and carry charge** [1].

**9.**

**(a)** **Malleability** (gold is malleable) [1].

**(b)** The positive ions are in **layers** [1] that can **slide over one another** when a force is applied [1]; the delocalised electrons move with them, so the **attraction is maintained / bonding is not broken** [1].

**10.**

**(a)** **Layers of positive ions can slide** over one another [1] while the **delocalised electrons keep the bonding intact**, so the metal stretches without breaking (it is ductile) [1].

**(b)** It has **delocalised electrons** [1] which **move through the wire and carry charge** [1].

**11.** The metallic bonds **do not break** — the attraction is maintained because the electrons move with the ions [1]. It is layers of **positive ions**, not atoms, that slide [1].

**12.** Solid magnesium: **delocalised electrons** [1] move through the lattice [1]. Molten magnesium chloride: **ions** (Mg²⁺ and Cl⁻) [1] are free to move and carry charge [1].

**13.** Both have **delocalised electrons** that can move [1]. Difference: any one — a metal has **positive ions** in a lattice whereas graphite has **covalently bonded carbon atoms** in layers; or graphite is a non-metal with weak forces between its layers [1].

**14.**

**(a)** Yes, it conducts [1]: liquid mercury still has a **sea of delocalised electrons** that are free to move and carry charge [1].

**(b)** Zinc atoms (ions) are a **different size** from copper ions [1]; they **disrupt the regular layers** of the lattice [1]; so the layers **cannot slide over one another as easily**, making brass harder [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "atoms" instead of "positive ions" in the description of the lattice.
- Leaving out "electrostatic attraction" so the answer describes the particles but not the bond.
- Explaining conductivity with ions moving, as for an ionic compound.
- Stating that layers slide without saying why the metal does not break — the electrons move with the ions and the attraction remains.
- Giving a different explanation for ductility from malleability; both rest on sliding layers.
- In comparison questions, naming the charge carrier for only one of the two substances.
- Forgetting that molten and liquid metals still conduct: the electron sea does not depend on the metal being solid.
- In alloy questions, saying the alloy has "stronger bonds" instead of explaining that different-sized atoms stop the layers sliding.
