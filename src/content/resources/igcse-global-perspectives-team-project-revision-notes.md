## Mark breakdown — know where the marks actually sit

| Element | Component | Marks | Marked how |
|---|---|---|---|
| Team | Explanation of Research and Planning | 10 | Same mark, every team member |
| Team | Evidence of Action | 5 | Same mark, every team member |
| Personal | Reflective Paper | 45 | Individual, per candidate |
| Both | Collaboration | 10 (5 team + 5 individual) | Teacher observation |

**The Reflective Paper is nearly two-thirds of the 70-mark component**
— it is where an individual candidate's grade is most directly
decided, even though the project itself is a team effort.

## Hard limits — marks are lost, not deducted, beyond the cap

| Item | Limit | What happens if exceeded |
|---|---|---|
| Explanation of Research and Planning | 300–400 words | No marks beyond word 400 |
| Evidence of Action (video) | 10 minutes max | Not marked beyond 10 min |
| Reflective Paper | 750–1000 words | No marks beyond word 1000 |

These are **hard caps, not guidance** — a 1400-word Reflective Paper
is only ever marked on its first 1000 words. Draft to the limit, not
past it.

## What actually happens in a Team Project

1. Team (2–5 candidates) selects a topic from the syllabus list and
identifies a **local issue** — a specific local instance of it.
2. Each member researches a **different aspect/perspective**.
3. Team decides, through discussion, on a course of **action**.
4. Team plans and carries out that action.
5. Team measures how successful the action was.

The **Explanation of Research and Planning** is built in three stages
across this process (topic/issue identification → plan of action and
roles → final review of changes) — it is not written once at the end.

## Evidence of Action: the trap that costs marks

Evidence of Action must show what the team **actually did** — posters,
leaflets, web pages, videos, presentations, or event photographs.
**Research or planning-meeting evidence does not count**, even if
extensive. This is the most common way teams lose marks on this
element despite genuine effort.

## The Reflective Paper — three things it must do

1. Summarise the candidate's own research findings and how the team
did/didn't use them.
2. Reflect on personal learning — about the issue, teamwork, and
themselves.
3. Evaluate how effective both the action taken and the candidate's
own collaboration were.

**Description of the project alone caps the achievable mark well
below the top band** — reflection and evaluation are what the mark
scheme actually rewards, not a narrative account of events.

## Collaboration (10 marks) — individually variable

Assessed by teacher observation across the whole project, not from
one document. Behaviours examiners look for: clear communication,
sharing/developing ideas, agreeing targets and deadlines, solving
problems, flexibility, dividing tasks and recognising every member's
contribution, asking for and giving support.

**All team members get the same team mark, but individual
collaboration marks can and do vary** — a candidate contributing
little to the collaborative process can lose marks here even when
the team's project itself is strong.

## How Component 3 differs from Components 1 and 2

Unlike the Written Exam (Component 1, externally examined) and the
Individual Report (Component 2, an independent piece of work), the
Team Project is **internally assessed and externally moderated**, and
is the only component with a genuine team element. It also produces
the most separate pieces of evidence of any component: a shared
Explanation of Research and Planning, shared Evidence of Action, and
an individual Reflective Paper per candidate. Marks from Component 2
and Component 3 can be carried forward between series, which is worth
factoring into planning when each component is actually sat — a
strong Individual Report result banked in one series changes how much
pressure sits on the Team Project in a later one.

## Exam traps

- Submitting only research/planning-meeting evidence as Evidence of
Action, when action evidence specifically is required.
- Writing a Reflective Paper that describes the project rather than
reflecting on learning and evaluating effectiveness.
- Exceeding word or time limits, wasting effort that can't be
credited past the cap.
- Assuming Collaboration is automatically shared equally — it is
observed and individually variable.
- Leaving the Reflective Paper's structure to the final week instead
of drafting it as reflection happens throughout the project.

## Self-test

1. Which element carries the most marks, and why does that matter
for an individual candidate's grade?
2. What happens to marks beyond a component's word/time limit?
3. Name one thing that does NOT count as Evidence of Action.
4. What three things must a Reflective Paper do, beyond describing
the project?
5. Why can two candidates on the same team receive different
Collaboration marks?

**Answers:** 1. The Reflective Paper (45 of 70 marks); because it's
marked individually, it's where an individual candidate's grade is
most directly determined, unlike the team-marked elements. 2. No
marks can be awarded beyond the limit — a paper exceeding 1000 words
is only marked on its first 1000. 3. Evidence of research or planning
meetings alone, without evidence of the action itself. 4. Summarise
research findings and their use, reflect on personal learning, and
evaluate the effectiveness of the action and the candidate's own
collaboration. 5. Because Collaboration is assessed via teacher
observation of each individual's behaviour throughout the project,
not awarded uniformly to the whole team.
