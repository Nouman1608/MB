These questions cover **3.2.1.1 Classification** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification: classifying elements as s, p, d or f block from their position, which is set by proton number. They use electron configurations up to Z = 36 from 3.1.1.3. This is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1. The letters A to E in Question 8 label elements for this page only.

## Section A

**1.** State what determines the position of an element in the Periodic Table. **[1]**

**2.** State the block to which each element belongs.

**(a)** Z = 4 **[1]**

**(b)** Z = 17 **[1]**

**(c)** Z = 25 **[1]**

**(d)** Z = 36 **[1]**

**3.** Helium is placed in Group 0. Explain why it is nevertheless classified as an s-block element, and why it is placed in Group 0. **[2]**

**4.** Write the full electron configuration of the element with Z = 31 and state its block. **[2]**

**5.** Aluminium is a metal. Explain why it is classified as a p-block element. **[1]**

**6.** An atom has the configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁴. State the period and the group of the element. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Element X has proton number 29.

**(a)** Write the electron configuration of an atom of X. **[1]**

**(b)** State the block of X. **[1]**

**(c)** Write the electron configuration of the X²⁺ ion. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why forming X²⁺ does not change the block in which X is classified. **[1]**

**8.** The table gives the electron configurations of five elements.

| Element | Configuration |
|---|---|
| A | 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹ |
| B | [Ar] 4s² |
| C | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p¹ |
| D | [Ar] 3d³ 4s² |
| E | 1s² 2s² 2p³ |

**(a)** Identify the two s-block elements. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify the d-block element. **[1]**

**(c)** Identify the two p-block elements. **[1]**

**(d)** Identify the three elements in Period 4. **[1]**

**9.** Period 4 is the first period to contain d-block elements.

**(a)** Explain why. **[2]**

**(b)** Period 4 contains 18 elements from potassium to krypton. Show how this number follows from the sub-shells filled across the period. **[2]**

**10.** A student writes: "Zinc must be a transition metal because it is in the d block." Evaluate this statement for this specification. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why the s block of the Periodic Table is two columns wide and the p block is six columns wide. **[2]**

**12.** Element Y is in Period 4. It forms Y³⁺ ions with the configuration [Ar] 3d⁵.

**(a)** Write the configuration of an atom of Y. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify Y. **[1]**

**(c)** State the block of Y. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Its proton number (atomic number) [1]

**2. (a)** Beryllium, 1s² 2s²: **s block** [1]

**(b)** Chlorine, [Ne] 3s² 3p⁵: **p block** [1]

**(c)** Manganese, [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s²: **d block** [1]

**(d)** Krypton, [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p⁶: **p block** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Krypton is a noble gas in Group 0, but its last electrons enter 4p, so it is p block. Helium is the only noble gas in the s block.

**3.** Its only electrons, 1s², are in an s sub-shell [1]; it is placed in Group 0 because its outer shell is full and its properties match those of the other noble gases [1]

**4.** **1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p¹** [1]; **p block** (gallium) [1]

**5.** Its outer (highest-energy) electron is in a p sub-shell: 3s² 3p¹ [1]

**6.** **Period 3** (outer shell n = 3) [1]; **Group 6** (six outer electrons) [1]

**7. (a)** **1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d¹⁰ 4s¹** (copper) [1]

**(b)** **d block** [1]

**(c)** **[Ar] 3d⁹** [1]

**(d)** The block depends on the element's position in the Periodic Table, which is fixed by its proton number; ionisation does not change the proton number [1]

*Examiner insight:* Writing 3d⁹ 4s² for the atom is the usual error; copper is one of the two Period 4 exceptions.

**8. (a)** **A and B** [1]

**(b)** **D** [1]

**(c)** **C and E** [1]

**(d)** **B, C and D** [1]

**9. (a)** The 3d sub-shell is higher in energy than 4s, so it does not begin to fill until after 4s [1]; 4s is the first sub-shell of Period 4, so the first d-block elements (Sc to Zn) appear in Period 4 [1]

**(b)** Across Period 4 the sub-shells 4s, 3d and 4p fill [1]; they hold 2 + 10 + 6 = 18 electrons, so there are 18 elements [1]

**10.** Zinc is a d-block element, because it lies in the d block and its 3d sub-shell fills in that row [1]; but the specification describes transition metal properties for Ti to Cu only, so zinc is not treated as a transition metal and the statement is incorrect [1]

**11.** An s sub-shell has one orbital and holds 2 electrons, so two elements fill it in each period [1]; a p sub-shell has three orbitals and holds 6 electrons, so six elements fill it [1]

**12. (a)** Y has three more electrons: 4s electrons are lost first, so the atom is **[Ar] 3d⁶ 4s²** [1]

**(b)** **Iron** (Z = 26) [1]

**(c)** **d block** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Placing krypton or helium in the wrong block because both are in Group 0.
- Forgetting the chromium and copper exceptions.
- Removing 3d electrons before 4s when writing configurations of d-block ions.
- Classifying an ion rather than the element.
