> Original questions written for Marlbridge, not past-paper questions.

**How to use this set.** Topic 4 essays are worth 14 marks: part (a) 10 for knowledge, part (b) 4 for understanding (syllabus levels).

## Section A: Short questions

**1.** Name the six Articles of Faith. **[3]**

**2.** What are the three kinds of meaning of jihad listed in the syllabus? **[3]**

**3.** Give three duties or features of angels. **[3]**

**4.** State what zakat is and the usual rate on savings above the nisab. **[2]**

**5.** Name two groups of people exempt from fasting in Ramadan. **[2]**

**6.** Name two of the observances of hajj. **[2]**

## Section B: Essay-style

**7.** (a) Describe what Muslims believe about the prophets. **[10]** (b) Explain why belief in Muhammad (pbuh) as the Seal of the Prophets is important to Muslims. **[4]**

**8.** (a) Describe how Muslims perform salat, including preparations. **[10]** (b) Explain the importance of congregational prayer. **[4]**

**9.** (a) Describe the main events of the Last Day according to Muslim belief. **[10]** (b) Explain how belief in the Last Day affects the way Muslims live. **[4]**

## Section C: Evaluate

**10.** "Hajj matters less than the other Pillars because it is performed only once." Give two reasons why this is not a full account. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** God; angels; books; prophets; predestination and decree; Resurrection and the last day [3 for all six, 2 for four or five, 1 for two or three].

**2.** Physical [1]; mental [1]; spiritual [1].

**3.** Created from light [1]; do not eat or sleep [1]; obey Allah without free will [1]; Jibril delivers revelation; the Kiraman Katibin record deeds [accept any correct duty, 1 each].

**4.** An obligatory yearly payment [1]; 2.5 per cent [1].

**5.** Any two: the sick, travellers, the elderly, children, pregnant and nursing women, menstruating women [1 each].

**6.** Any two: ihram, tawaf, sa'y, standing at Arafat, night at Muzdalifa, stoning the jamarat at Mina, sacrifice, cutting the hair [1 each].

**7.** (a) Prophets are human beings chosen by Allah [1]; truthful, trustworthy and of excellent character [1-2]; conveyed His message [1]; lived it as an example [1]; messengers (rusul) brought a book or law [1]; the Qur'an names twenty-five prophets [1]; examples: Ibrahim, Musa, Dawud, Isa and Muhammad (pbuh) with their books [up to 3]. (b) His message is universal and complete [1-2]; Seal of the Prophets (33.40) so no new prophet is needed [1-2].

**8.** (a) Five times daily at set times [1]; intention [1]; wudu [1]; clean body, clothing and place [1]; facing the qibla [1]; rak'at with standing, recitation, bowing and prostration [up to 3]; Friday and Eid prayers [1]. (b) Unity and equality in congregation [up to 2]; discipline and remembrance of Allah [up to 2].

**9.** (a) Trumpet is blown and creation ends [1]; resurrection [1]; gathering for judgement [1]; each receives a book of deeds [1]; deeds are weighed [1]; the sirat is crossed [1]; the righteous enter paradise and the wicked hell [1-2]; detail [up to 2]. (b) Accountability gives life purpose [up to 2]; encourages good deeds and repentance [up to 2].

**10.** It is obligatory for those able, so it is still a Pillar [1]; it brings equality of all believers and remembrance of Ibrahim, Hajar and Isma'il [1]; credit one further reasoned point [1].
