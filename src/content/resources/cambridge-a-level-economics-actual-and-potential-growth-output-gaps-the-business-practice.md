## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between actual growth and potential growth. **[2]**

**2.** Country A has actual output of 1,368 and potential output of 1,440. Calculate the output gap as a percentage of potential output and state what type of gap it is. **[3]**

**3.** In Country B, actual output rises from 470 to 487.9 and potential output from 500 to 512.5.

**(a)** Calculate actual growth and potential growth. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the output gap before and after, and explain what has happened to it. **[2]**

**4.** Explain two consequences of a positive output gap. **[4]**

**5.** Explain, with reference to an aggregate demand and aggregate supply diagram, how actual growth can occur without potential growth. **[5]**

**6.** Country C has annual real GDP growth of 3.8%, 1.4%, −1.1%, −2.3%, 0.9% and 3.2% in years 1 to 6. Real GDP at the start of year 1 is an index of 100.

**(a)** Calculate the index at the end of year 2 and at the end of year 4. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the phase of the cycle in year 3, at the end of year 4 and in year 5, and show whether output in year 5 has regained its earlier peak. **[3]**

**7.** In Country D, mps = 0.15, the marginal rate of tax = 0.25 and mpm = 0.20. Exports fall by 36.

**(a)** Calculate the multiplier and the fall in national income. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the fall in income if there were no income-linked taxes, and comment. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the fall in tax revenue in the original case. **[1]**

**8.** Distinguish between an automatic stabiliser and discretionary fiscal policy, giving an example of each. **[4]**

**9.** In Country E, potential output is 600. The labour force grows by 1.8% and output per worker by 2.2%.

**(a)** Calculate potential growth and the new level of potential output. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain two supply-side policies that could raise output per worker. **[4]**
**(c)** State one reason actual growth could still be below potential growth. **[1]**

**10.** Country F has potential output of 1,800 and actual output of 1,740. The marginal rate of tax is 0.25, mps is 0.10 and mpm is 0.15.

**(a)** Calculate the output gap as a percentage. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the rise in government spending needed to close the gap. **[2]**
**(c)** Government spending rises by 24. Calculate the new gap. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the same rise would be harmful if actual output were 1,890. **[3]**

**11.** Evaluate whether supply-side policies are more effective than demand-side policies in promoting economic growth. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** Actual growth is a rise in real GDP [1]. Potential growth is a rise in the economy's productive capacity [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers gain credit by contrasting output with capacity. Two definitions that never compare them tend to be thin.

**2.** Gap = 1,368 − 1,440 = −72 [1]; −72 / 1,440 × 100 = **−5.0%** [1]. Actual output is below potential, so it is a negative gap [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by potential output. Giving the sign and the type in words as well as the number protects the final mark.

**3. (a)** Actual: (487.9 − 470) / 470 = **3.8%** [1]. Potential: (512.5 − 500) / 500 = **2.5%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Before: (470 − 500) / 500 = −6.0%. After: (487.9 − 512.5) / 512.5 = −4.8% [1]. The gap narrows because actual growth (3.8%) exceeds potential growth (2.5%), but spare capacity remains [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A comment on the gap needs the cause, which is the comparison of the two growth rates.

**4.** Inflation: demand exceeds sustainable supply [1], so firms raise prices and labour shortages push up wages and costs [1]. Current account: strong demand pulls in imports [1], so net exports fall and the deficit may widen [1]. (Other valid: unemployment below its natural rate; unsustainable growth; pressure for tighter policy.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each consequence needs a development, not just a label. A list of four labels earns less than two chains.

**5.** Start from a negative gap, with spare capacity [1]. A rise in AD, from consumption, investment, government spending or net exports, shifts AD right [1]. Real GDP rises along the short-run aggregate supply curve [1]. The long-run aggregate supply line does not move, so capacity is unchanged [1]. Once output reaches capacity, extra demand mainly raises the price level and the growth cannot be sustained [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the curves and the equilibrium. A shift of AD with no mention of spare capacity does not show why potential output is unchanged.

**6. (a)** End of year 2: 100 × 1.038 × 1.014 = **105.25** [1]. End of year 4: 105.25 × 0.989 × 0.977 = **101.70** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Year 3: output falls, so a downturn into recession [1]. End of year 4: output at its lowest, the trough [1]. Year 5: recovery, but 101.70 × 1.009 = 102.62, still below the earlier peak of 105.25 [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Phase labels should rest on the evidence. Quote the growth sign or the index.

**7. (a)** Multiplier = 1 / (0.15 + 0.25 + 0.20) = 1 / 0.60 = **1.67** [1]. Fall = 36 × 1.667 [1] = **60** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Without taxes: 1 / (0.15 + 0.20) = 2.86; fall = 36 × 2.857 = **102.9** [1]. Income-linked taxes cut the multiplier and reduce the fall, which is the stabilising effect [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 60 × 0.25 = **15** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the multiplier before using it, and state the comparison in words.

**8.** An automatic stabiliser changes automatically with the level of income, with no new decision [1], for example progressive income tax or unemployment benefit [1]. Discretionary policy is a deliberate government decision to change spending or taxes [1], for example a new infrastructure programme or a tax cut announced in a budget [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Pair each definition with an example that fits it.

**9. (a)** 1.018 × 1.022 = 1.0404 [1]. Growth = **4.04%** [1]. New potential = 600 × 1.0404 = **624.2** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Education and training raises skills [1], so each worker produces more [1]. Tax incentives for investment encourage firms to buy capital [1], giving workers more equipment [1]. **[4]**
**(c)** Weak aggregate demand leaves resources unused [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A policy needs a route to output per worker. Naming a policy alone gains little.

**10. (a)** (1,740 − 1,800) / 1,800 × 100 = **−3.33%** [1]. The gap is −60 [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Multiplier = 1 / (0.25 + 0.10 + 0.15) = 2 [1]. Spending = 60 / 2 = **30** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 24 × 2 = 48, so output = 1,788 [1]. Gap = −12 / 1,800 = **−0.67%** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Gap would be 90 / 1,800 = +5.0% [1]. Output would rise to 1,938, a gap of +7.67% [1]. Demand would exceed capacity, causing inflation and import growth instead of lasting growth [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A calculation that feeds a later part is easier to follow when each result is stated clearly.

**11.** Supply-side policies raise potential growth: education, training, infrastructure and R&D raise productivity and shift LRAS right [1]. This allows output to rise without inflation pressure and is lasting [1]. Demand-side policies raise actual growth: fiscal or monetary stimulus shifts AD right, and the multiplier magnifies it [1]. Demand-side policy is effective when there is a negative gap, because spare resources are used [1]. Supply-side policy has long lags: education pays back only as people join the workforce [1]. It is costly and its results are uncertain [1]. Demand-side policy acts faster, which matters in a recession [1]. Demand-side policy near capacity raises prices, not output [1]. Evaluation: the answer depends on the gap, so supply-side policy is more effective when the gap is positive or zero [1]. Crowding out and borrowing can limit fiscal stimulus [1]. Supply-side policies can have unequal effects or cost jobs, such as through deregulation or trade opening [1]. A reasoned judgement: supply-side policy is more effective for sustained growth, while demand-side policy is better for a deep negative gap, so a mix is best [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong essays judge the policies against a stated context, such as the gap or the time horizon, rather than listing them.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing the gap by actual output.
- Defining potential growth as growth in AD.
- Describing a stabiliser as a decision by the government.
- Giving the multiplier with no working.
- Stating a phase of the cycle without evidence.
- Ignoring the sign of the gap when discussing stimulus.
- Listing policies without effects, lags or costs.
- Evaluating with only "it depends" and no stated condition.
