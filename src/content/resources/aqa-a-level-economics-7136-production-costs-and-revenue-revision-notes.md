All figures are invented.

## Production and productivity (3.1.4.1–3.1.4.2)

- **Production:** converting inputs (factor services) into output.
- **Labour productivity** = output ÷ number of workers (or hours worked).
- **Specialisation and division of labour** raise productivity: skill from repetition, less time lost switching
  tasks, use of specialist machinery, matching workers to tasks they are good at.
- Specialisation needs **exchange**; money as a **medium of exchange** avoids the double coincidence of wants
  that barter needs.
- Drawbacks of the division of labour: monotony, dependence on others in the chain, narrow skills.

## Returns (3.1.4.3)

| Concept | Definition |
|---|---|
| Short run | At least one factor of production is fixed |
| Long run | All factors are variable |
| Total product | Total output from the inputs used |
| Average product | Total product ÷ units of the variable factor |
| Marginal product | Change in total product from one more unit of the variable factor |
| Law of diminishing returns | As more of a variable factor is added to a fixed factor, marginal product eventually falls |
| Increasing returns to scale | All inputs rise by x%; output rises by more than x% |
| Constant returns to scale | Output rises by exactly x% |
| Decreasing returns to scale | Output rises by less than x% |

## Cost formulae (3.1.4.4)

- TC = TFC + TVC
- ATC = TC ÷ Q; AFC = TFC ÷ Q; AVC = TVC ÷ Q
- MC = ΔTC ÷ ΔQ (equally ΔTVC ÷ ΔQ, since fixed cost does not change)
- With one variable input, MC = price of the input ÷ marginal product.

## Why the curves have their shapes

- **SRMC** falls then rises: marginal product rises then diminishing returns set in.
- **AFC** falls continuously as fixed cost is spread over more output.
- **AVC and ATC** are U-shaped; the gap between them (AFC) narrows as output rises.
- **MC cuts AVC and ATC at their minimum points.**
- **LRAC** shape comes from economies and diseconomies of scale, not diminishing returns.
- **Factor prices and productivity:** higher wages shift cost curves up unless productivity rises; firms substitute
  towards the relatively cheaper factor.

## Economies and diseconomies of scale (3.1.4.5)

| Internal economies | External economies | Diseconomies |
|---|---|---|
| Technical (bigger, more efficient capital) | Skilled local labour pool | Coordination problems |
| Purchasing (bulk buying) | Specialist suppliers nearby | Communication breakdowns |
| Managerial (specialist managers) | Shared infrastructure and research | Low motivation and alienation |
| Financial (cheaper credit) | Industry training schemes | Slower decision making |
| Risk-bearing (diversification) | | |

- Increasing returns to scale, with constant factor prices, give falling LRAC.
- **L-shaped LRAC:** costs fall to the minimum efficient scale and then stay constant.
- **Minimum efficient scale (MES):** the lowest output at which LRAC is minimised. A large MES relative to
  market size means few firms and a barrier to entry.

## Revenue formulae (3.1.4.6)

- TR = P × Q; AR = TR ÷ Q = P; MR = ΔTR ÷ ΔQ.
- **AR curve = firm's demand curve.**
- Price taker: AR = MR = price (horizontal line).
- Downward-sloping demand: MR lies below AR; for a straight-line demand curve MR falls twice as steeply.
- TR rises while MR > 0, peaks where **MR = 0**, falls when MR < 0.

## Profit (3.1.4.7)

- Profit = TR − TC.
- **Normal profit:** the minimum return needed to keep the firm in the industry; included in costs.
- **Abnormal (supernormal) profit:** profit above normal profit.
- Role of profit: signal of where resources should go, reward for risk, source of investment funds, incentive
  for entry.

## Technological change (3.1.4.8)

- **Invention:** a new idea; **innovation:** putting it into commercial use.
- Effects: methods of production, productivity, efficiency, costs; new products and markets; destruction of
  existing markets; changes in market structure.
- **Creative destruction:** new technologies and firms displace old ones.

## Worked reminder (invented)

Fixed cost 120. Output 0, 10, 25, 35 at total cost 120, 180, 240, 330.

- ATC at 25 = 240 ÷ 25 = **9.60**.
- MC from 10 to 25 = 60 ÷ 15 = **4.00**; MC from 25 to 35 = 90 ÷ 10 = **9.00**.
- AVC at 35 = (330 − 120) ÷ 35 = **6.00**.

## Must-know distinctions

- Diminishing returns (short run) versus diseconomies of scale (long run).
- Returns to scale (inputs to output) versus economies of scale (output to cost).
- Internal versus external economies of scale.
- Normal versus abnormal profit.
- Invention versus innovation.

## Quick self-test

1. Define labour productivity.
2. Why does specialisation need a means of exchange?
3. State the law of diminishing returns.
4. A firm doubles all inputs and output rises by 150%. What returns to scale does it have?
5. Fixed cost is 500 and total cost at 100 units is 1,300. Calculate AVC.
6. Why does the MC curve cut the ATC curve at its minimum?
7. Give two internal economies of scale and one external economy.
8. What is the minimum efficient scale, and why does it matter for industry structure?
9. Price falls from 12 to 11 and sales rise from 20 to 21. Calculate MR of the 21st unit.
10. Why is the AR curve the firm's demand curve?
11. Distinguish normal from abnormal profit.
12. Give one way technological change can destroy an existing market.

### Answers

1. Output per worker, or per hour worked.
2. Specialists produce only part of what they need, so they must trade; money makes trade efficient by
   avoiding barter's double coincidence of wants.
3. When more of a variable factor is added to a fixed factor, the marginal product of the variable factor will
   eventually fall.
4. **Increasing returns to scale** (output rises by a larger proportion than inputs).
5. TVC = 1,300 − 500 = 800, so AVC = 800 ÷ 100 = **8**.
6. When MC is below ATC it pulls the average down; when above, it pulls it up; so ATC is at its minimum where
   MC equals it.
7. Internal: any two of technical, purchasing, managerial, financial, risk-bearing. External: for example a
   local pool of skilled labour.
8. The lowest output at which LRAC is minimised; if it is large relative to market demand, only a few firms can
   produce at lowest cost, creating a barrier to entry and a concentrated industry.
9. TR falls from 240 to 231, so MR = **−9**.
10. AR equals TR ÷ Q, which is the price, and the price at each quantity is what the demand curve shows.
11. Normal profit is the minimum needed to keep the firm in the industry and is counted as a cost; abnormal
    profit is any profit above that.
12. For example, streaming services sharply reducing demand for physical DVDs and the shops that sold them.
