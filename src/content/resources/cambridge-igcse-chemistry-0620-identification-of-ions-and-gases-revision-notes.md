Condensed recall for **12.5 Identification of ions and gases**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 (2026–2028). All of 12.5 is **Core**. Reasons and worked deductions are in the [ions and gases study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-identification-of-ions-and-gases/); exam-style questions are in the [ions and gases practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-identification-of-ions-and-gases-practice/).

## Anions

| Anion | Add | See |
|---|---|---|
| CO₃²⁻ | dilute acid; test gas with limewater | fizzing; limewater milky |
| Cl⁻ | dilute HNO₃, then AgNO₃(aq) | **white** ppt. |
| Br⁻ | dilute HNO₃, then AgNO₃(aq) | **cream** ppt. |
| I⁻ | dilute HNO₃, then AgNO₃(aq) | **yellow** ppt. |
| SO₄²⁻ | dilute HNO₃, then Ba(NO₃)₂(aq) | **white** ppt. |
| SO₃²⁻ | acidified KMnO₄(aq) | **purple → colourless** |
| NO₃⁻ | NaOH(aq) + aluminium foil; warm | ammonia: damp red litmus → blue |

Why nitric acid: removes carbonate (which would also precipitate); HCl would add Cl⁻; H₂SO₄ would add SO₄²⁻.

## Cations: NaOH(aq) and NH₃(aq)

| Cation | NaOH: ppt. | NaOH: excess | NH₃: ppt. | NH₃: excess |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Al³⁺ | white | **dissolves** (colourless) | white | insoluble |
| NH₄⁺ | ammonia on warming | — | — | — |
| Ca²⁺ | white | insoluble | none / very slight white | — |
| Cr³⁺ | green | **dissolves** | green | insoluble |
| Cu²⁺ | light blue | insoluble | light blue | **dissolves → dark blue** |
| Fe²⁺ | green | insoluble; browns on standing | green | insoluble; browns on standing |
| Fe³⁺ | red-brown | insoluble | red-brown | insoluble |
| Zn²⁺ | white | **dissolves** (colourless) | white | **dissolves** (colourless) |

### Separating look-alikes

| Look-alikes | Deciding test |
|---|---|
| Al³⁺ vs Zn²⁺ | excess NH₃: Zn dissolves, Al does not |
| Ca²⁺ vs Al³⁺/Zn²⁺ | excess NaOH: Ca does not dissolve |
| Ca²⁺ vs others with NH₃ | Ca gives no or very slight ppt. |
| Cr³⁺ vs Fe²⁺ | excess NaOH: Cr dissolves, Fe²⁺ does not |
| Fe²⁺ vs Fe³⁺ | green vs red-brown ppt. |
| Cu²⁺ | dark blue solution in excess NH₃ |

## Gases

| Gas | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| NH₃ | **damp red** litmus | blue |
| CO₂ | limewater | milky |
| Cl₂ | **damp** litmus | bleached |
| H₂ | **lighted** splint | pops |
| O₂ | **glowing** splint | relights |
| SO₂ | acidified KMnO₄(aq) | purple → colourless |

## Flame tests

| Ion | Colour |
|---|---|
| Li⁺ | red |
| Na⁺ | yellow |
| K⁺ | lilac |
| Ca²⁺ | orange-red |
| Ba²⁺ | light green |
| Cu²⁺ | blue-green |

Memory aids: the two **reds** are Li (red) and Ca (orange-red); the two **greens** are Ba (light green) and Cu (blue-green).

## Deduction routine for an unknown salt

1. **Flame test** → identifies Li⁺, Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Ba²⁺ or Cu²⁺.
2. **NaOH(aq), then excess** → narrows the cation; warm to check for NH₄⁺.
3. **NH₃(aq), then excess** → separates Al³⁺ from Zn²⁺; confirms Cu²⁺.
4. **Dilute acid** → fizzing means CO₃²⁻.
5. **HNO₃ then AgNO₃** → halides; **HNO₃ then Ba(NO₃)₂** → sulfate.
6. Combine the cation and anion into a name, e.g. zinc sulfate.

Each observation should rule something in **or** out; say which.

## Ionic equations *(Extended)*

```
Ag⁺(aq) + Br⁻(aq) → AgBr(s)
Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s)
Fe²⁺(aq) + 2OH⁻(aq) → Fe(OH)₂(s)
Al³⁺(aq) + 3OH⁻(aq) → Al(OH)₃(s)
```

The number of OH⁻ ions equals the charge on the metal ion.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Precipitate (ppt.) | insoluble solid formed when two solutions are mixed |
| Soluble in excess | the precipitate dissolves when more reagent is added |
| Acidify | add dilute acid first, here nitric acid |
| Effervescence | fizzing, as a gas is produced |
| Damp litmus | litmus paper wetted so the gas can dissolve and act on it |

## Exam traps

- **Nitric** acid in halide and sulfate tests, never hydrochloric or sulfuric.
- **Barium nitrate** for sulfate (the syllabus reagent), **silver nitrate** for halides.
- **Warm** in the ammonium and nitrate tests.
- Always report what happens **in excess** for cations.
- **Lilac**, not purple, for potassium.
- **Lighted** splint for hydrogen; **glowing** splint for oxygen.
- Litmus for ammonia and chlorine must be **damp**.
- Limewater turns **milky** with carbon dioxide; describing a gas as "fizzing" is not a test.
- Sulfite (anion) and sulfur dioxide (gas) both turn acidified potassium manganate(VII) colourless: read which is being tested.

## Self-test

1. Describe the test for chloride ions.
2. What colour precipitate does bromide give with acidified silver nitrate?
3. Why is hydrochloric acid not used to acidify in the halide test?
4. Describe the test for sulfate ions.
5. Describe the test for nitrate ions.
6. A solution gives a light blue precipitate with NaOH. Name the cation and give the result with excess ammonia.
7. How do you distinguish Al³⁺ from Zn²⁺?
8. Give the result of adding NaOH to a solution of iron(III) ions.
9. Describe the test for oxygen.
10. Which gas bleaches damp litmus paper?
11. State the flame colour of potassium ions.
12. A solid gives a yellow flame and a white precipitate with acidified barium nitrate. Name it.

**Answers:**

1. Acidify with dilute nitric acid, add aqueous silver nitrate: white precipitate.
2. Cream.
3. It would add chloride ions, giving a white precipitate whatever the sample.
4. Acidify with dilute nitric acid, add aqueous barium nitrate: white precipitate.
5. Add aqueous sodium hydroxide and aluminium foil and warm carefully; ammonia is produced, which turns damp red litmus blue.
6. Copper(II); the precipitate dissolves in excess ammonia to give a dark blue solution.
7. Add excess aqueous ammonia: zinc's white precipitate dissolves, aluminium's does not.
8. Red-brown precipitate, insoluble in excess.
9. A glowing splint relights.
10. Chlorine.
11. Lilac.
12. Sodium sulfate.
