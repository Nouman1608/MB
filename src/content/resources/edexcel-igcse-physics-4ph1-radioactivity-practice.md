## Section A

**1.** State the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in a neutral atom of ³⁷₁₇Cl. **[3]**

**2.** Which two of these nuclei are isotopes of the same element: ¹⁴₆C, ¹⁴₇N, ¹²₆C? Give a reason. **[2]**

**3.** Bismuth-212 (²¹²₈₃Bi) decays by alpha emission to thallium (Tl). Write the balanced nuclear equation. **[3]**

**4.** Iodine-131 (¹³¹₅₃I) decays by beta-minus emission to xenon (Xe). Give the mass number and atomic number of the xenon nucleus formed. **[2]**

**5.** Name two ways of detecting ionising radiation. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why the count rate measured by a detector on board an aircraft flying at high altitude is higher than at ground level. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A nucleus emits an alpha particle, and the nucleus formed then emits two beta-minus particles, one after the other. Explain how the final nucleus is related to the original one. **[3]**

**8.** A student uses a source and a Geiger-Müller tube to investigate penetration. The background count rate is 30 counts per minute.

| Absorber | None | Paper | 3 mm aluminium | 10 mm lead |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Count rate / counts per min | 830 | 828 | 210 | 40 |

**(a)** Calculate the corrected count rate for each absorber. **[1]**

**(b)** Deduce which types of radiation the source emits. Use the data to justify each conclusion. **[3]**

**(c)** State two precautions the student should take when handling the source. **[2]**

**9.** The count rate from a sample is measured every 5 minutes. The background count rate is 40 counts per minute.

| Time / min | 0 | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Measured count rate / counts per min | 520 | 379 | 280 | 210 | 160 |

**(a)** Calculate the corrected count rate at 0 min and at 10 min. **[1]**

**(b)** Determine the half-life of the sample. **[2]**

**(c)** Predict the measured count rate the student would record at 30 min. **[2]**

**(d)** The readings fluctuate when repeated. State why, and how the student could make the results more reliable. **[2]**

**10.** An engineer needs to find a leak in a water pipe buried 1 m underground. Four sources are available.

| Source | Radiation | Half-life |
|---|---|---|
| A | alpha | 2 hours |
| B | gamma | 6 hours |
| C | gamma | 30 years |
| D | beta | 8 days |

Choose the most suitable source and explain your choice, giving reasons for rejecting two of the others. **[4]**

**11.** Packs of surgical gloves are sealed in plastic and then exposed to gamma radiation.

**(a)** Explain why gamma radiation is used rather than alpha. **[2]**

**(b)** A customer worries that the gloves are now radioactive. Explain why they are not. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why a source that emits alpha radiation is far more dangerous inside the body, for example after radioactive dust is breathed in, than outside it. **[3]**

**13.** Discuss the problems caused by radioactive waste and how the risks can be reduced. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Protons: 17 [1]; neutrons: 37 − 17 = 20 [1]; electrons: 17 [1].

**2.** ¹⁴₆C and ¹²₆C [1]; they have the same atomic (proton) number, 6, but different mass numbers, so different numbers of neutrons [1].

**3.** ²¹²₈₃Bi → ²⁰⁸₈₁Tl + ⁴₂He: correct alpha symbol ⁴₂He [1]; mass number 208 [1]; atomic number 81 [1].

**4.** Mass number 131 [1]; atomic number 54 [1].

**5.** Photographic film [1]; Geiger-Müller tube (and counter) [1].

**6.** Cosmic rays from space are a source of background radiation [1]; at high altitude there is less atmosphere above to absorb them, so more reach the detector [1].

**7.** Alpha emission: A decreases by 4 and Z decreases by 2 [1]. Each beta-minus emission: A unchanged, Z increases by 1, so two of them raise Z by 2 [1]. The final nucleus has the same atomic number but a mass number 4 less, so it is an isotope of the original element [1].

**8. (a)** 800, 798, 180 and 10 counts per minute [1].
**(b)** No alpha: paper hardly changes the count rate (800 to 798) [1]. Beta present: 3 mm of aluminium causes a large drop (798 to 180) [1]. Gamma present: some radiation passes through the aluminium and is only reduced (not stopped completely) by 10 mm of lead [1].
**(c)** Any two: use tongs [1]; keep the source pointed away from people [1]; keep the time the source is out of its box short [1]; keep it at a distance from the body [1]; return it to its lead-lined box after use [1]; maximum 2.

**9. (a)** 0 min: 520 − 40 = 480 counts per minute; 10 min: 280 − 40 = 240 counts per minute [1].
**(b)** The corrected count rate halves from 480 to 240 [1] in 10 min, so the half-life is **10 min** [1].
**(c)** 30 min is 3 half-lives: corrected rate = 480 ÷ 8 = 60 counts per minute [1]; measured = 60 + 40 = **100 counts per minute** [1].
**(d)** Radioactive decay is random [1]; take readings over a longer time (or repeat and average) [1].

**10.** Source B [1]. Gamma can pass through 1 m of soil to the detector at the surface [1]. Its half-life is long enough to complete the search but short enough that the water does not stay radioactive for long [1]. Rejections, any two for the final mark: A is alpha, which cannot penetrate the soil; C's half-life is so long that the water would stay radioactive for years; D is beta, which would be absorbed by the soil [1].

**11. (a)** Gamma is highly penetrating, so it passes through the sealed plastic and reaches the gloves [1]; alpha would be absorbed by the packaging [1].
**(b)** The gloves have been irradiated, not contaminated [1]; no radioactive material is transferred to them, so they emit no radiation once removed from the source [1].

**12.** Outside the body, alpha is stopped by the dead outer layer of skin (or by air) [1]. Inside the body, the source is next to living cells [1], and alpha is the most strongly ionising radiation, so all its energy is absorbed by nearby cells, damaging them or causing mutations [1].

**13.** Problems, any two: some waste stays radioactive for thousands of years because of long half-lives [1]; it can damage cells and cause mutations if it escapes [1]; it could contaminate water or land [1]. Reducing risks, any two: seal it in containers [1]; shield it with concrete, lead or water [1]; store it securely, for example deep underground in stable rock [1]; monitor the storage site [1]. Maximum 4: at least one problem and one method.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the measured, not corrected, count rate to find a half-life (Question 9).
- Concluding "gamma only" when a big drop with aluminium shows beta as well (Question 8).
- Choosing a source by radiation type only, ignoring half-life (Question 10).
- Writing "the gloves are contaminated".
- Forgetting that two beta decays raise the atomic number by 2.
