## Section A

**1.** Triangle ABC is similar to triangle PQR. AB = 6 cm, BC = 9 cm and AC = 12 cm. PQ = 8 cm, and PQ corresponds to AB.

**(a)** Work out the scale factor of the enlargement from ABC to PQR. **[1]**

**(b)** Work out the length of QR. **[1]**

**(c)** Work out the length of PR. **[1]**

**2.** Rectangle A measures 12 cm by 8 cm. Rectangle B measures 21 cm by 14 cm. Rectangle C measures 18 cm by 10 cm.

**(a)** Show that rectangles A and B are similar. **[2]**

**(b)** Show that rectangles A and C are not similar. **[2]**

**3.** At noon a vertical pole 2.5 m tall has a shadow 1.8 m long. At the same time the shadow of a building is 21.6 m long. Work out the height of the building. **[3]**

**4.** A map has a scale of 1 : 25 000.

**(a)** Two farms are 6.4 cm apart on the map. Work out the real distance between them, in kilometres. **[2]**

**(b)** The real distance between two bridges is 3.5 km. Work out the distance between them on the map. **[2]**

**5.** A room is 4.8 m long and 3.6 m wide. Marcus draws a plan of the room using a scale of 1 cm to 60 cm. Work out the length and width of the room on his plan. **[2]**

**6.** In triangle ABC, D lies on AB and E lies on AC. DE is parallel to BC. AD = 4 cm, DB = 2 cm and DE = 5 cm.

**(a)** Explain why triangles ADE and ABC are similar. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out the length of BC. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The scale on a plan is 1 : 200. A hall has a length of 12 cm on the plan. Work out the real length of the hall, in metres. **[2]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) Shape P and shape Q are similar. The area of P is 36 cm² and the area of Q is 81 cm². The perimeter of P is 30 cm. Work out the perimeter of Q. **[3]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) Two jugs are mathematically similar. The smaller jug is 12 cm tall and holds 240 ml. The larger jug is 18 cm tall. Work out how much the larger jug holds. **[3]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) Two solids are similar. Their lengths are in the ratio 2 : 3.

**(a)** The surface area of the smaller solid is 40 cm². Work out the surface area of the larger solid. **[2]**

**(b)** The volume of the larger solid is 270 cm³. Work out the volume of the smaller solid. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) A model of a statue is made to a scale of 1 : 20.

**(a)** The surface area of the model is 150 cm². Work out the surface area of the statue, in m². **[3]**

**(b)** The volume of the statue is 2.4 m³. Work out the volume of the model, in cm³. **[3]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) Two cones are similar. Their volumes are 54 cm³ and 128 cm³. The height of the larger cone is 16 cm. Work out the height of the smaller cone. **[4]**

**13.** (Higher tier only) Tin A and tin B are similar. The label on tin A has an area of 50 cm² and the label on tin B has an area of 128 cm². Tin A holds 250 ml. Work out how much tin B holds. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 8 ÷ 6 = **4/3** (or 1.33...) [1].

**(b)** QR = 9 × 4/3 = **12 cm** [1].

**(c)** PR = 12 × 4/3 = **16 cm** [1].

**2. (a)** 21 ÷ 12 = 1.75 and 14 ÷ 8 = 1.75 [1]. The ratios of corresponding sides are equal and all angles are 90°, so the rectangles are similar [1].

**(b)** 18 ÷ 12 = 1.5 but 10 ÷ 8 = 1.25 [1]. The ratios are not equal, so they are not similar [1].

**3.** The triangles are similar because the Sun's rays are parallel, so the angles are the same [1]. Scale factor = 21.6 ÷ 1.8 = 12 [1]. Height = 2.5 × 12 = **30 m** [1].

**4. (a)** 6.4 × 25 000 = 160 000 cm [1] = **1.6 km** [1].

**(b)** 3.5 km = 350 000 cm [1]. 350 000 ÷ 25 000 = **14 cm** [1].

**5.** Convert to centimetres: 480 cm by 360 cm [1]. Divide by 60: **8 cm by 6 cm** [1].

**6. (a)** Angle A is common to both triangles [1]. Angle ADE equals angle ABC because they are corresponding angles on parallel lines (so the triangles have equal angles) [1].

**(b)** AB = 4 + 2 = 6 cm [1]. AD ÷ AB = DE ÷ BC, so 4/6 = 5/BC and BC = 5 × 6 ÷ 4 = **7.5 cm** [1].

**7.** 12 × 200 = 2400 cm [1] = **24 m** [1].

**8.** Area ratio 36 : 81 = 4 : 9 [1]. Length ratio = 2 : 3, scale factor 1.5 [1]. Perimeter = 30 × 1.5 = **45 cm** [1].

**9.** Length scale factor = 18 ÷ 12 = 1.5 [1]. Volume scale factor = 1.5³ = 3.375 [1]. Volume = 240 × 3.375 = **810 ml** [1].

**10. (a)** Area scale factor = (3/2)² = 2.25 [1]. 40 × 2.25 = **90 cm²** [1].

**(b)** Volume ratio = 27 : 8, so the smaller is 8/27 of the larger [1]. 270 × 8 ÷ 27 = **80 cm³** [1].

**11. (a)** Area scale factor from model to statue = 20² = 400 [1]. 150 × 400 = 60 000 cm² [1] = **6 m²** [1].

**(b)** Volume scale factor = 20³ = 8000 [1]. The model volume = 2.4 ÷ 8000 = 0.0003 m³ [1] = **300 cm³** [1].

**12.** Volume ratio 54 : 128 = 27 : 64 [1]. Length ratio = ∛27 : ∛64 = 3 : 4 [2]. Height of smaller = 16 × 3/4 = **12 cm** [1].

**13.** Area scale factor = 128 ÷ 50 = 2.56 [1]. Length scale factor = √2.56 = 1.6 [1]. Volume scale factor = 1.6³ = 4.096 [1]. Volume = 250 × 4.096 = **1024 ml** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: matching QR with AC instead of with BC.
- Question 2: comparing sides in the wrong order, for example 21 ÷ 8, which compares unlike sides.
- Question 3: dividing 2.5 by 1.8 and using that as the answer without applying it to the shadow of the building.
- Question 4: forgetting to change 160 000 cm to km (100 000 cm = 1 km).
- Question 6(b): using 4/2 instead of 4/6, so using DB in place of AB.
- Question 9: using the length scale factor 1.5 on the volume.
- Question 11: dividing by 20 instead of 8000 for the volume of the model.
- Question 12: using the volume ratio 54 : 128 directly as the ratio of the heights.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which of these questions test Higher Tier content only?**
Questions 8 to 13, which use the squares and cubes of the scale factor for areas and volumes. Questions 1 to 7 test content that both tiers cover.

**How do I decide whether to square or cube the scale factor?**
Ask what is being compared. Lengths use k, areas (including surface area) use k², and volumes (including capacity) use k³.
