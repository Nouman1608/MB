## Section A

**1.** Which of these is a pure substance in the chemical sense?

A: air

B: distilled water

C: orange juice

D: sea water

**[1]**

**2.** State what is meant by a **mixture**. **[2]**

**3.** In paper chromatography, name:

**(a)** the stationary phase **[1]**

**(b)** the mobile phase **[1]**

**4.** State the equation for calculating an Rf value. **[1]**

**5.** Give **one** reason why it is important to measure the purity of a food product. **[1]**

**6.** A label says "100% pure mineral water". Explain why a chemist would not describe mineral water as pure. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Pure compound T melts at 154 °C (value invented). Three samples are tested.

| Sample | Melting behaviour |
|---|---|
| 1 | melts between 145 °C and 150 °C |
| 2 | melts sharply at 154 °C |
| 3 | melts sharply at 98 °C |

**(a)** Identify the pure sample of T. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain what the result shows about sample 1. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest what sample 3 is. **[1]**

**8.** A student analyses a green ink using paper chromatography in water. The solvent front moves 8.0 cm. The ink gives spots 2.4 cm and 5.6 cm from the start line.

**(a)** Calculate the Rf value of each spot. **[2]**

**(b)** Reference dyes have Rf values in water of: blue 0.30, red 0.45, yellow 0.70. Identify the dyes in the green ink. **[2]**

**(c)** State how many substances the ink contains, and what this shows about its purity. **[1]**

**9.** Describe how to carry out a paper chromatography experiment to find out whether a brown food colouring contains a dye called sunset yellow. **[5]**

**10.** A black permanent marker ink does not move from the start line when the chromatogram is run in water.

**(a)** Suggest why the ink does not move. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest how the student could separate the dyes in the ink. **[1]**

**11.** Explain, in terms of the stationary and mobile phases, why two dyes in a mixture travel different distances on the paper. **[3]**

**12.** A chemist makes a sample of a drug. It gives one spot on a chromatogram run in water.

**(a)** Explain why this does not prove the sample is pure. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest **two** further chromatographic tests the chemist could do to check its purity. **[2]**

**13.** A mixture of sand, salt and water is separated. Name the physical method used to:

**(a)** remove the sand **[1]**

**(b)** obtain salt crystals from the salt solution **[1]**

**(c)** obtain pure water from the salt solution **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Two or more elements or compounds [1] that are not chemically combined together [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** The paper [1]

**(b)** The solvent [1]

**[2]**

**4.** Rf = distance moved by substance ÷ distance moved by solvent [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Any one [1]: impurities could be harmful; impurities could change the taste, colour or quality; to check it meets its description. **[1]**

**6.** In chemistry, pure means containing only one substance [1]; mineral water contains dissolved minerals as well as water, so it is a mixture [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Sample 2 [1]

**(b)** Sample 1 is impure (T containing an impurity) [1], because it melts over a range of temperatures, below 154 °C [1].

**(c)** A different (pure) substance, because it melts sharply but at a temperature that does not match T [1].

**[4]**

**8. (a)** 2.4 ÷ 8.0 = 0.30 [1]; 5.6 ÷ 8.0 = 0.70 [1]

**(b)** Blue [1] and yellow [1]

**(c)** Two substances, so the ink is a mixture / not pure [1].

**[5]**

**9.** Five marks from:

- draw a start line in pencil near the bottom of chromatography paper [1]
- put a spot of the brown colouring and a spot of pure sunset yellow on the line [1]
- place the paper in a solvent (such as water) with the solvent below the start line [1]
- leave until the solvent has nearly reached the top, then remove and mark the solvent front [1]
- if a spot from the colouring is at the same height / has the same Rf as the sunset yellow, the colouring contains sunset yellow [1]
- cover the container to reduce evaporation (max 5)

**[5]**

**10. (a)** The dyes in the ink are insoluble in water [1].

**(b)** Run the chromatogram again using a different solvent in which the dyes dissolve, such as ethanol [1].

**[2]**

**11.** The paper is the stationary phase and the solvent is the mobile phase [1]. Each dye is distributed between the two phases according to its relative solubility [1]; the dye that is more soluble in the solvent spends more time in the mobile phase, so it travels further [1]. **[3]**

**12. (a)** Two or more substances could have the same Rf value in water, so they would appear as one spot [1].

**(b)** Any two [2]: run it in a different solvent; run it alongside a known pure sample of the drug; check that its spot has the same Rf as the pure drug.

**[3]**

**13. (a)** Filtration [1]

**(b)** Crystallisation [1]

**(c)** Distillation [1]

**[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the everyday meaning of "pure".
- Stating that impurities raise the melting point.
- Calling the paper the mobile phase.
- Writing the Rf equation upside down or measuring from the bottom of the paper.
- Forgetting to run a reference substance alongside the sample.
- Concluding that one spot always proves purity.
