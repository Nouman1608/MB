## Questions

**1.** This question is about general principles.

**(a)** State the meaning of the term nucleophile. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe what happens to the bonding electrons in heterolytic fission and state what type of species is formed. **[2]**

**2.** Classify each reaction as addition, elimination, substitution, oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis or polymerisation. Give one label for each.

**(a)** C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O **[1]**
**(b)** n CH₂=CHCH₃ → poly(propene) **[1]**
**(c)** CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂I + H₂O → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH + HI **[1]**
**(d)** CH₃CH₂CHBrCH₃ + KOH (ethanol) → CH₃CH=CHCH₃ + KBr + H₂O **[1]**

**3.** Three isomers of C₅H₁₁Cl are shown.

A: CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂Cl
B: (CH₃)₂CHCHClCH₃
C: (CH₃)₂CClCH₂CH₃

Name each isomer and state whether it is a primary, secondary or tertiary halogenoalkane. **[6]**

**4.** 1-chloro-3-methylbutane, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂Cl, is reacted separately with four reagents.

**(a)** For KOH(aq), name the organic product and give a balanced equation. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the organic product with KOH dissolved in ethanol and state the role of the hydroxide ion. **[2]**
**(c)** Name the organic product with KCN in ethanol and state why this reaction is useful in synthesis. **[2]**
**(d)** State the conditions for the reaction with ammonia and name the organic product. **[2]**

**5.** Describe the mechanism for the reaction of 1-bromo-2-methylbutane, CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂Br, with excess ammonia. Include dipoles, curly arrows, lone pairs and the final products. **[5]**

**6.** 2-chloropentane, CH₃CHClCH₂CH₂CH₃, is heated under reflux with ethanolic potassium hydroxide.

**(a)** Name all three alkenes that can form. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the alkenes include two that differ in the position of the C=C. **[1]**

**7.** A student added equal amounts of three primary halogenoalkanes to silver nitrate in ethanol at 40 °C and timed the first appearance of a precipitate.

| Halogenoalkane | Time / s |
|---|---|
| 1-chloro-4-methylpentane | 1800 |
| 1-bromo-4-methylpentane | 240 |
| 1-iodo-4-methylpentane | 30 |

**(a)** Calculate 1/time for each compound. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times faster the iodo compound reacts than the bromo compound. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain the trend in rate using the bond enthalpy data. **[2]**
**(d)** State why the polarity of the C–X bond does not explain this trend. **[1]**

**8.** You are asked to compare the rates of hydrolysis of the primary, secondary and tertiary isomers of C₄H₉I using aqueous silver nitrate in ethanol.

**(a)** State two variables you would control. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why ethanol is used. **[1]**
**(c)** Predict the order of rate, fastest first. **[1]**
**(d)** State one safety precaution and its reason. **[1]**

**9.** In Core Practical 6, 11.10 g of 2-methylpropan-2-ol was shaken with excess concentrated hydrochloric acid. After purification, 9.85 g of 2-chloro-2-methylpropane was collected.

(CH₃)₃COH + HCl → (CH₃)₃CCl + H₂O

**(a)** Calculate the percentage yield. **[4]**
**(b)** The density of 2-chloro-2-methylpropane is 0.85 g cm⁻³. State which layer in the separating funnel contains the product. **[1]**
**(c)** State the purpose of washing with sodium hydrogencarbonate solution and of adding anhydrous sodium sulfate. **[2]**
**(d)** The boiling point of the product is 51 °C and that of the alcohol is 82 °C. State which fraction should be collected in the final distillation. **[1]**

**10.** Liquid Y is a halogenoalkane, CₙH₂ₙ₊₁X. A sample of Y was warmed with NaOH(aq), acidified with dilute nitric acid, and silver nitrate solution was added. A cream precipitate formed. It did not dissolve in dilute ammonia but did dissolve in concentrated ammonia.

**(a)** Explain why the mixture was acidified before adding silver nitrate. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify X. **[1]**
**(c)** 2.055 g of Y gave 2.817 g of the dried precipitate. Deduce the molecular formula of Y. **[3]**
**(d)** Y reacts faster with silver nitrate in ethanol than any of its structural isomers. Give the structural formula and name of Y. **[2]**

**11.** This question is about bond properties and synthesis.

**(a)** The C–F bond is the most polar carbon–halogen bond, yet fluoroalkanes are almost unreactive towards nucleophiles. Explain this. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest a two-step route from propene to 2-methylpropanenitrile, (CH₃)₂CHCN. Give the reagent and conditions for each step, the intermediate, and the type of each reaction. **[4]**
**(c)** Starting from 6.30 g of propene, each step has a yield of 80%. Calculate the mass of 2-methylpropanenitrile formed. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** An electron-pair donor: it uses a lone pair to make a new covalent bond [1]
**(b)** Both bonding electrons go to one atom [1]; ions form, a positive and a negative ion (giving electrophiles or nucleophiles) [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Donates electrons" alone is vague; say a lone pair or electron pair, and that a covalent bond forms.

**2. (a)** Oxidation (combustion) [1]
**(b)** Polymerisation [1]
**(c)** Hydrolysis (substitution is also correct) [1]
**(d)** Elimination [1]
*Examiner insight:* When a question asks for one label, give one; a list of two that includes a wrong term may lose the mark.

**3.** A: **1-chloro-2-methylbutane** [1], primary [1]
B: **2-chloro-3-methylbutane** [1], secondary [1]
C: **2-chloro-2-methylbutane** [1], tertiary [1]
*Examiner insight:* For B both directions give locants 2 and 3, so chloro, first alphabetically, takes the lower number; 3-chloro-2-methylbutane is wrong.

**4. (a)** **3-methylbutan-1-ol** [1]; (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂Cl + KOH → (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂OH + KCl [1]
**(b)** **3-methylbut-1-ene** [1]; OH⁻ acts as a base (removes H⁺) [1]
**(c)** **4-methylpentanenitrile**, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂CN [1]; it increases the length of the carbon chain by one carbon [1]
**(d)** Ammonia dissolved in ethanol, heated in a sealed tube (under pressure) [1]; **3-methylbutylamine** (3-methylbutan-1-amine) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name the nitrile by counting the CN carbon; "3-methylbutanenitrile" here describes the wrong chain length.

**5.** Dipole shown, Cδ+–Brδ− [1]
Curly arrow starting at the nitrogen lone pair of NH₃ and ending at the δ+ carbon [1]
Curly arrow from the C–Br bond to Br, giving Br⁻ [1]
Intermediate CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂NH₃⁺ shown [1]
Second NH₃ lone pair removes H⁺ (arrow from N–H bond to N), giving **CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂NH₂ and NH₄⁺** (NH₄Br overall) [1]
*Examiner insight:* An arrow that starts from the nitrogen atom with no lone pair drawn is ambiguous; draw the lone pair and start the arrow on it.

**6. (a)** **Pent-1-ene** [1]; **(E)-pent-2-ene** [1]; **(Z)-pent-2-ene** [1]
**(b)** H⁺ can be removed from either carbon next to the C–Cl carbon (carbon 1 or carbon 3) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Treat the E and Z isomers as separate products; giving "pent-2-ene" once only accounts for one of them.

**7. (a)** Chloro 5.56 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹; bromo 4.17 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ [1]; iodo 3.33 × 10⁻² s⁻¹ [1]
**(b)** 240 ÷ 30 = **8.0 times faster** [1]
**(c)** Bond enthalpy falls from C–Cl (346) to C–Br (290) to C–I (228 kJ mol⁻¹) [1]; the weaker bond breaks more easily, so hydrolysis is faster [1]
**(d)** C–Cl is the most polar, so polarity would predict the chloro compound to react fastest, which is the opposite of what is seen [1]
*Examiner insight:* Quote the bond enthalpy values in the comparison; a statement that "C–I is weak" without comparing all three bonds gives a thinner answer.

**8. (a)** Any two from: temperature (water bath); volume and concentration of silver nitrate; volume of ethanol; amount of each halogenoalkane [2]
**(b)** Halogenoalkanes do not mix with water; ethanol is a common solvent for both [1]
**(c)** Tertiary > secondary > primary [1]
**(d)** Heat with a water bath, not a flame, because ethanol and halogenoalkanes are flammable [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Same amount" is too loose; name the quantity, for example the same volume of silver nitrate solution of the same concentration.

**9. (a)** M(C₄H₁₀O) = 74.0 and M(C₄H₉Cl) = 92.5 g mol⁻¹ [1]
n(alcohol) = 11.10 ÷ 74.0 = 0.1500 mol [1]
Theoretical mass = 0.1500 × 92.5 = 13.88 g [1]
Yield = 9.85 ÷ 13.88 × 100 = **71.0%** [1]
**(b)** The **upper layer**, because the product is less dense than water [1]
**(c)** NaHCO₃ neutralises (removes) remaining acid [1]; anhydrous sodium sulfate removes water (dries the product) [1]
**(d)** The fraction boiling at about **51 °C** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep at least three significant figures in intermediate values; early rounding can shift the final yield.

**10. (a)** To remove excess OH⁻ ions, which would react with Ag⁺ and form a precipitate that hides the result [1]
**(b)** Bromine (bromide ions, AgBr precipitate) [1]
**(c)** n(AgBr) = 2.817 ÷ 187.8 = 0.01500 mol, which equals n(Y) [1]
M(Y) = 2.055 ÷ 0.01500 = 137.0 g mol⁻¹ [1]
14n + 1.0 + 79.9 = 137.0 gives n = 4, so **C₄H₉Br** [1]
**(d)** (CH₃)₃CBr [1]; **2-bromo-2-methylpropane** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the link from moles of precipitate to moles of Y; a molecular formula stated without the mole ratio gives the examiner no method to credit.

**11. (a)** The C–F bond has the highest bond enthalpy (467 kJ mol⁻¹) [1]; so it is very hard to break, and bond strength outweighs polarity [1]
**(b)** Step 1: HBr (at room temperature) gives 2-bromopropane as the major product [1]; electrophilic addition [1]
Step 2: KCN in ethanol, heated under reflux [1]; nucleophilic substitution [1]
**(c)** n(propene) = 6.30 ÷ 42.0 = 0.150 mol [1]
Overall yield = 0.80 × 0.80 = 0.64 [1]
Mass = 0.150 × 0.64 × 69.0 = **6.62 g** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Combine step yields by multiplying; adding or averaging the two percentages gives an overall yield that is too high.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "KOH" without "aqueous" or "in ethanol", so the conditions do not fix the product.
- Calling ethanolic hydroxide a nucleophile; in elimination it acts as a base.
- Missing lone pairs, dipoles or the second ammonia molecule in mechanisms.
- Using polarity to explain why iodoalkanes hydrolyse fastest.
- Counting nitrile chains without the CN carbon.
- Forgetting to acidify with dilute nitric acid before the silver nitrate test.
- Placing the Core Practical 6 product in the lower layer.
- Rounding moles too early in yield calculations.
