## Core recall

- A business uses resources to provide goods or services that customers want.
- The specification's five reasons for starting a business: producing goods, supplying services, distributing products, fulfilling a business opportunity, providing a good or service to benefit others.
- Goods are physical and can be stored; services are activities and usually cannot be stored.
- Needs are essential for living; wants are not, so they are cut first when incomes fall.
- Every business combines land, labour, capital and enterprise; a shortage of one limits output.
- Opportunity cost is the single next best alternative given up.
- Sectors are linked in a chain of production from primary to secondary to tertiary.
- Characteristics describe what an entrepreneur is like; objectives describe what they want.
- The environment keeps changing through technology, the economic situation, legislation and environmental expectations.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Business | An organisation that uses resources to provide goods or services |
| Goods | Physical products that can be touched and stored |
| Services | Activities carried out for customers |
| Needs | Things people must have to live: food, water, shelter, clothing |
| Wants | Things people would like but can live without |
| Land | Natural resources and the site a business uses |
| Labour | The people who work in the business |
| Capital | Man-made resources used in production, such as machinery and vehicles |
| Enterprise | Spotting an opportunity, organising resources and taking the risk |
| Entrepreneur | A person who shows enterprise by setting up and running a business |
| Opportunity cost | The next best alternative given up when a choice is made |
| Primary sector | Extracting or growing natural resources |
| Secondary sector | Making or building products from raw materials |
| Tertiary sector | Providing services |

## Spotting the reason in a case

| If the case says... | The reason is... |
|---|---|
| makes, builds, bakes | producing goods |
| repairs, teaches, cleans, advises | supplying services |
| buys from producers and delivers to shops | distributing products |
| nobody else offers it locally | fulfilling a business opportunity |
| profits fund a cause, free service to the community | benefiting others |

## Characteristics and objectives side by side

| Characteristics (what they are like) | Objectives (what they want) |
|---|---|
| Hard working | To be their own boss |
| Innovative | Flexible working hours |
| Organised | To pursue an interest |
| Willing to take a risk | To earn more money |
| | To identify a gap in the market |
| | Dissatisfaction with current job (a reason to leave) |

## The changing environment: chain to use

```
Change → effect on the business → effect on costs, sales or profit → functional area
Example: tougher packaging expectations → switch to card packaging
         → higher unit costs → operations and finance
```

## Opportunity cost method

1. List the options and their benefits.
2. Identify the option chosen.
3. Name the single next best option and its benefit: that is the opportunity cost.
4. Never add the other options together.

## Exam traps

- Capital means equipment, not money, in the factors of production.
- Opportunity cost is one option, not all options.
- Classify each activity of a business, not its name.
- A characteristic is not an objective.
- Always give an effect when describing a change in the environment.
- Apply every point to the business in the case.

## Quick self-test

1. List the five reasons for starting a business named in the specification.
2. State one difference between goods and services that matters to a business.
3. Explain why a business selling wants may suffer more in a downturn.
4. Identify the factor of production: (a) a delivery van; (b) the owner who takes the risk; (c) the shop site; (d) a part-time assistant.
5. Define opportunity cost.
6. A business can spend £8,000 on a website (extra profit £2,500 a year), a new oven (£3,100) or staff training (£1,900). It buys the oven. What is the opportunity cost?
7. Classify each activity: (a) a fishing boat catching fish; (b) a factory canning the fish; (c) a supermarket selling the cans.
8. A farm grows strawberries, makes jam and sells it in its own shop. Which sectors does it work in?
9. Is "willing to take a risk" a characteristic or an objective?
10. Give two objectives of an entrepreneur.
11. Name the four sources of change in the business environment listed in the specification.
12. A new law raises the minimum wage. State one effect on a small café.

### Answers

1. Producing goods, supplying services, distributing products, fulfilling a business opportunity, providing a good or service to benefit others.
2. Goods can be stored as stock; services usually cannot, so unsold time is lost.
3. Customers cut spending on wants before needs when incomes fall, so its sales drop faster.
4. (a) Capital; (b) enterprise; (c) land; (d) labour.
5. The next best alternative given up when a choice is made.
6. The website and the **£2,500** a year of extra profit it would have earned (the next best option).
7. (a) Primary; (b) secondary; (c) tertiary.
8. Primary (growing), secondary (making jam) and tertiary (selling).
9. A characteristic.
10. Any two: be their own boss; flexible working hours; pursue an interest; earn more money; identify a gap in the market; dissatisfaction with current job.
11. Technology, the economic situation, legislation and environmental expectations.
12. Higher wage costs, which reduce profit unless prices rise or staff hours are cut.
