Fractional indices are Extended only, so those questions are labelled **(Extended)**. Questions marked (non-calculator) match Paper 1 or Paper 2 conditions; questions marked (calculator) match Paper 3 or Paper 4.

## Questions

**1.** (non-calculator) Write down the value of

**(a)** 15² **[1]**
**(b)** ∛27 **[1]**
**(c)** 4³ **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) Work out

**(a)** √(10² − 6²) **[2]**
**(b)** 3 × ∛125 − 2³ **[2]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Work out

**(a)** (−2)⁵ **[1]**
**(b)** 0.4² **[1]**
**(c)** √0.49 **[1]**
**(d)** (2/3)³ **[1]**

**4.** (calculator)

**(a)** Work out 1.35⁴. Write down all the figures on your calculator display. **[1]**
**(b)** Work out the fifth root of 0.0761. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Work out ∛(6.2² + 4.8). Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**5.** (non-calculator) Find the value of

**(a)** 6⁰ **[1]**
**(b)** 2⁻⁵ **[1]**
**(c)** (1/3)⁻² **[1]**
**(d)** 10⁻², written as a decimal **[1]**

**6.** (non-calculator) Simplify each expression, leaving your answer as a power of 5.

**(a)** 5⁷ × 5⁻³ **[1]**
**(b)** 5⁸ ÷ 5¹¹ **[1]**
**(c)** (5⁻²)⁴ **[1]**
**(d)** Find the value of (5⁶ × 5⁻⁴) ÷ 5⁵. Give your answer as a fraction. **[2]**

**7.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Write 1/16 as a power of 2. **[1]**
**(b)** Write 8² × 4⁻³ ÷ 16 as a single power of 2. **[3]**

**8.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Find the value of

**(a)** 49^(1/2) **[1]**
**(b)** 1000^(2/3) **[2]**
**(c)** 16^(−3/4) **[2]**
**(d)** (9/25)^(−1/2) **[2]**

**9.** (Extended) (non-calculator)

**(a)** Find the value of 9^(3/2) × 27^(−2/3). **[3]**
**(b)** Write √2 × 8 as a single power of 2. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that 4^(−1/2) × 32^(2/5) = 2. **[2]**

**10.** (calculator)

**(a)** A square has an area of 70.56 cm². Calculate its perimeter. **[2]**
**(b)** A cube has a volume of 42.875 cm³. Calculate the area of one face of the cube. **[2]**
**(c)** Work out √(4.6² + 3.9²) ÷ 1.7³. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(d)** Write these numbers in order of size, starting with the smallest.

2⁻³      0.4²      √0.0225      3⁻² **[2]**

**11.** (Extended) (non-calculator)

**(a)** Find the value of 125^(−1/3) + 8⁰. **[2]**
**(b)** Write (√8 × 4⁻¹) ÷ 2^(1/2) as a single power of 2. **[3]**
**(c)** Ravi says that 36^(−1/2) = −6. Explain his error and give the correct value. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the value of 0.25^(−3/2). **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **225** [1]
**(b)** **3** [1]
**(c)** **64** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Write down" means no working is expected, so the mark depends entirely on accurate recall; 4³ = 12 or ∛27 = 9 scores zero.

**2. (a)** 10² − 6² = 100 − 36 = 64 [1]. √64 = **8** [1].
**(b)** ∛125 = 5 and 2³ = 8 [1]. 3 × 5 − 8 = **7** [1].
*Examiner insight:* The method mark in (a) is for working out the inside of the root first; writing √10² − √6² = 10 − 6 = 4 earns nothing because the method is wrong.

**3. (a)** **−32** [1]
**(b)** **0.16** [1]
**(c)** **0.7** [1]
**(d)** **8/27** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each part is a single accuracy mark, so 32 (sign lost), 1.6 (decimal places miscounted) or 0.07 all score zero with no partial credit.

**4. (a)** **3.32150625** [1]
**(b)** 0.0761^(1/5) = 0.597416… [1] = **0.597** (3 s.f.) [1].
**(c)** 6.2² + 4.8 = 43.24 [1]. ∛43.24 = 3.50990… = **3.51** (3 s.f.) [1].
*Examiner insight:* Writing the unrounded value before rounding protects you: a correct long display usually earns the method mark even if the final rounding is wrong, while a bare wrong 3 s.f. answer earns nothing.

**5. (a)** **1** [1]
**(b)** 1/2⁵ = **1/32** [1]
**(c)** (3/1)² = **9** [1]
**(d)** 1/100 = **0.01** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A negative index means reciprocal, not negative: answers of −32 in (b) or −9 in (c) score zero, and 0 or 6 in (a) also score zero.

**6. (a)** 5^(7 + (−3)) = **5⁴** [1]
**(b)** 5^(8 − 11) = **5⁻³** [1]
**(c)** 5^(−2 × 4) = **5⁻⁸** [1]
**(d)** 5⁶ × 5⁻⁴ ÷ 5⁵ = 5^(6 − 4 − 5) = 5⁻³ [1] = **1/125** [1].
*Examiner insight:* When the question says "as a power of 5", evaluating instead (for example 625 in (a)) does not answer the question as set; in (d) the instruction asks for a fraction, so 5⁻³ alone gets only the method mark.

**7. (a)** **2⁻⁴** [1]
**(b)** 8² = (2³)² = 2⁶ [1]. 4⁻³ = (2²)⁻³ = 2⁻⁶ and 16 = 2⁴ [1]. 2⁶ × 2⁻⁶ ÷ 2⁴ = 2^(6 − 6 − 4) = **2⁻⁴** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Method marks come from rewriting each number with base 2; combining 8² × 4⁻³ as 32⁻¹ or similar by mixing bases gains no credit.

**8. (a)** √49 = **7** [1]
**(b)** ∛1000 = 10 [1]. 10² = **100** [1].
**(c)** Fourth root of 16 = 2, so 16^(3/4) = 2³ = 8 [1]. Negative index gives **1/8** [1].
**(d)** (9/25)^(1/2) = 3/5 [1]. Reciprocal: **5/3** [1].
*Examiner insight:* The first mark in (b) to (d) is for a correct root; an answer of 8 in (c) shows the root and power but misses the reciprocal, so it scores one of the two marks.

**9. (a)** 9^(3/2) = (√9)³ = 27 [1]. 27^(−2/3) = 1/(∛27)² = 1/9 [1]. 27 × 1/9 = **3** [1].
**(b)** √2 = 2^(1/2) and 8 = 2³ [1]. 2^(1/2 + 3) = **2^(7/2)** [1].
**(c)** 4^(−1/2) = 1/√4 = 1/2 [1]. 32^(2/5) = (fifth root of 32)² = 2² = 4, and 1/2 × 4 = **2** [1].
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" the answer is given, so every value must be written down; writing 1/2 × 4 = 2 without showing where 4 came from loses the second mark.

**10. (a)** √70.56 = 8.4 cm [1]. Perimeter = 4 × 8.4 = **33.6 cm** [1].
**(b)** ∛42.875 = 3.5 cm [1]. Area of one face = 3.5² = **12.25 cm²** [1].
**(c)** √(21.16 + 15.21) = √36.37 = 6.03075… and 1.7³ = 4.913 [1]. 6.03075… ÷ 4.913 = 1.22750… = **1.23** (3 s.f.) [1].
**(d)** Values: 2⁻³ = 0.125, 0.4² = 0.16, √0.0225 = 0.15, 3⁻² = 0.111… [1]. Order: **3⁻², 2⁻³, √0.0225, 0.4²** [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (d) the final mark needs the original forms in the correct order; converting each value to a decimal first earns the method mark even if one value is wrong.

**11. (a)** 125^(−1/3) = 1/∛125 = 1/5 and 8⁰ = 1 [1]. 1/5 + 1 = **6/5** (or 1 1/5 or 1.2) [1].
**(b)** √8 = 2^(3/2) [1]. 4⁻¹ = 2⁻² [1]. 2^(3/2 − 2 − 1/2) = **2⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** A negative index means the reciprocal, not a negative number [1]. 36^(−1/2) = 1/√36 = **1/6** [1].
**(d)** 0.25 = 1/4, so 0.25^(−3/2) = 4^(3/2) [1] = (√4)³ = **8** [1].
*Examiner insight:* An explanation mark in (c) needs a reason, not just the right value; "he is wrong, it is 1/6" scores only one of the two marks because it does not say what the negative index means.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a negative index as a negative answer: 2⁻⁵ is 1/32, not −32.
- Giving 0 or the base itself for a zero index: 6⁰ = 1.
- Multiplying indices when they should be added: 5⁷ × 5⁻³ = 5⁴, not 5⁻²¹.
- Using index rules with different bases without converting first, such as 8² × 4⁻³ treated as 32 to some power.
- Losing a sign when subtracting a negative index: 6⁻² ÷ 6⁻⁴ = 6², not 6⁻⁶.
- Square-rooting each term separately inside √(a² + b²).
- Miscounting decimal places in powers of decimals: 0.4² = 0.16 and √0.49 = 0.7.
- Rounding to 3 s.f. too early in a calculator question, or dropping a final zero (3.20 written as 3.2).
- (Extended) Doing the power before the root with large bases, or forgetting the reciprocal for a negative fractional index.
