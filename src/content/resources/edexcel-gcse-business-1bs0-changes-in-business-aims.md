This guide covers content item 2.1.2, Changes in business aims and objectives, in Topic 2.1 (Growing the business) of the Pearson Edexcel Level 1/Level 2 GCSE (9-1) in Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. It belongs to Theme 2, examined in Paper 2, and builds on 1.3.1 (business aims and objectives) from Theme 1. The broader [guide to business growth and changing aims](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-business-growth-and-changing-aims/) lists the reasons and the changes. This page links each reason to the change it is most likely to cause, so that you can build a chain of reasoning for any business in a source. All businesses and figures are invented.

Companion pages: the [changing aims revision notes](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-changes-in-business-aims-revision-notes/) and the [changing aims practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-changes-in-business-aims-practice/).

## What 2.1.2 contains

| Heading in the specification | Points listed |
|---|---|
| Why business aims and objectives change as businesses evolve | In response to: market conditions, technology, performance, legislation, internal reasons |
| How business aims and objectives change as businesses evolve | Focus on survival or growth; entering or exiting markets; growing or reducing the workforce; increasing or decreasing product range |

Remember the difference from Theme 1: an **aim** is a long-term goal; an **objective** is a specific, measurable target that helps achieve it. In 2.1.2 you are explaining why these targets move, and what the new targets look like.

## Why aims and objectives change: the five reasons

### Market conditions

Market conditions are the state of the market the business sells in: how many rivals there are, whether demand is rising or falling, and what customers want.

- A new, aggressive competitor can force a switch from growth to protecting market share or survival.
- A growing market can encourage a business to expand while demand is strong.
- A change in tastes can lead a business to exit a declining market and enter a growing one.

### Technology

New technology can create opportunities or make products and methods out of date.

- Online selling can make new markets reachable, so growth becomes realistic.
- Automation may lead to an objective of cutting costs, sometimes with fewer staff.
- A product made obsolete by new technology may be withdrawn from the range.

### Performance

Performance is how well the business is doing, measured by figures such as sales, profit and market share.

- Strong performance (rising profit, objectives met early) supports more ambitious objectives, such as growth.
- Weak performance (falling sales, losses, cash shortages) often pushes the business back towards survival.

### Legislation

New laws can raise costs or ban certain products or practices.

- A higher legal minimum wage may lead to an objective of improving productivity or limiting staff numbers.
- A ban or restriction on a product may force a business to exit that market or change its range.

### Internal reasons

These come from inside the business.

- A new owner or manager with different priorities.
- The founder deciding to retire, or to reduce their workload.
- The business having grown enough that the owner now wants stability rather than further expansion.
- A shortage of finance or skills that limits what the business can do.

## How aims and objectives change: the four directions

| Change | In one direction | In the other |
|---|---|---|
| Focus on survival or growth | Survival when performance is weak or conditions are hard | Growth when performance is strong and finance is available |
| Entering or exiting markets | Entering new markets (new customer groups, new areas, overseas) | Exiting markets where sales are falling or losses are made |
| Growing or reducing the workforce | Recruiting to support expansion | Reducing staff to cut costs or after automation |
| Increasing or decreasing product range | Adding products to serve new customers | Dropping products that sell poorly or cost too much |

## Linking a reason to a change

The strongest answers connect the "why" to the "how". This table gives typical chains, but always check the facts in the source.

| Reason (why) | Likely change (how) | Example chain |
|---|---|---|
| Market conditions: new rival | Survival; reduce product range | Rival cuts prices → sales fall → focus on survival → drop low-margin lines |
| Technology: online selling | Growth; enter new markets | Website reaches distant customers → new market entered → workforce grows to pack orders |
| Performance: profit rising | Growth; increase product range | Profit retained → funds new products → objective to raise sales by 20% |
| Legislation: higher compliance costs | Reduce workforce or range | Costs rise → objective to cut costs → fewer staff hours or fewer products |
| Internal: owner nearing retirement | Survival or stability; exit markets | Owner wants less risk → stops expanding → exits a demanding market |

## Worked example: Copperleaf Bakery over five years

| Year | Event | Reason for change | How objectives changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opens one shop | Start-up | Survival: break even by month 9 |
| 3 | Profit £45,000, objectives met early | Performance | Growth: open a second shop; recruit 4 staff |
| 4 | Starts selling cakes online | Technology | Enter a new market: customers beyond the town |
| 5 | A supermarket bakery opens nearby; sales of bread fall | Market conditions | Exit bread rolls for cafés; reduce range to its best sellers; protect profit |

Some figures from year 5:

1. Bread sales fall from £120,000 to £96,000. Percentage fall = £24,000 ÷ £120,000 × 100 = 20%.
2. The range falls from 36 products to 27. Percentage fall = 9 ÷ 36 × 100 = 25%.
3. Staff fall from 14 to 12 by not replacing two who leave. Percentage fall = 2 ÷ 14 × 100 = 14.3%.

Notice that the business can grow in one way while shrinking in another: in year 5 Copperleaf keeps growing online while cutting its bread range. Objectives do not all move in the same direction at once.

## Annotated model answer

**Question (Explain style):** Explain **one** reason why a business might change its objective from growth to survival. [3]

> A new competitor may enter the market *(reason: market conditions)*. The competitor may take some customers, so the business's sales and profit fall *(expansion 1)*. With less cash available, the business may need to stop expanding and focus on covering its costs to stay open *(expansion 2, the change)*.

## Common mistakes

- Giving a reason without the change it causes, or a change without the reason.
- Assuming aims only ever move towards growth.
- Writing "legislation" without saying which kind of law and what it costs.
- Treating "internal reasons" as vague; name the reason, such as a new owner.
- Ignoring the figures in the source when they show performance.

## Exam technique

- **Give / State:** a reason or a change from the specification's list.
- **Explain:** one reason with two linked expansions ending in the change.
- **Analyse:** use the business's figures and events to show why its objectives changed.
- **Evaluate:** judge whether a change of objective is right for the business, considering stakeholders such as staff affected by a smaller workforce.
