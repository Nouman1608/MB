## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. The
4-mark "describe" question gives one mark for each relevant, accurate detail; the 4-mark "explain"
question gives one mark for each point and one for explaining it, up to two marks for each of the
two. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general,
accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The
12-mark questions are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** Jibril (AS) [1].

**2.** Ramadhan [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: told him to "Iqra" (read or recite); held him tightly; repeated the command three times; recited the first verses of Surah al-Alaq.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: confirmed that the angel was the same one sent to Musa (AS); reassured him that the revelation was genuine; warned him that his people would oppose him; promised to support him if he lived.

**5.** Any four, [1] each: he was frightened and shaking [1]; he left the cave and went home [1]; he asked Khadija (RA) to cover him [1]; he told her what had happened [1]; he said he feared for himself [1]; he needed comfort and advice [1]; he had not expected to be a prophet [1].

**6.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Proof that it was not his own words** [1]: he could not read or write, so the Qur'an could not have been written by him [1].
- **It shows the source of the message** [1]: the revelation came from Allah through Jibril (AS), not from human learning [1].
- **It shows humility** [1]: he did not claim ability, but only received what he was given [1].
- **It supports the miracle of the Qur'an** [1]: an unlettered man delivered a book of great beauty and depth [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **She comforted him** [1]: his fear was calmed when she reminded him of his goodness [1].
- **She believed him** [1]: as the first to accept Islam, she gave him confidence that the experience was real [1].
- **She knew his character** [1]: she said Allah would never disgrace a man who helps the poor and keeps family ties [1].
- **She took him to Waraqa** [1]: this gave him knowledge from scripture [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Did He not find you in need and make [you] self-sufficient?" (Qur'an 93:8): Allah reminds him of his help, including the care of Khadija (RA) [2].
- "Recite in the name of your Lord who created" (Qur'an 96:1): Khadija (RA) accepted the message that began with these words [2].

**8.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the first command is "Recite", before any law or ritual.
- **For the statement:** the verses mention "the pen" and "taught man that which he knew not", which shows that knowledge is a gift from Allah.
- **For the statement:** Muslims have a long tradition of learning, which they link to this command.
- **Against the statement:** the verses also teach faith in the Creator ("in the name of your Lord who created"), so belief is the foundation, with learning growing from it.
- **Against the statement:** Islam also stresses worship, justice and conduct.
- **Balanced view:** learning is central, but it is linked to faith in Allah and to action.

*Use of the passage*

- "Recite" opens with an act of learning.
- "who created" ties learning to the Creator.
- "Who taught by the pen" shows the value of writing and education.
- "Taught man that which he knew not" shows that revelation is knowledge from Allah.

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Reasons (AO1):

- **Confirmation** [1]: Waraqa knew the earlier scriptures and said the angel was the one who came to Musa (AS), which reassured the Prophet (pbuh) [1].
- **Link to earlier prophets** [1]: it showed that Muhammad's (pbuh) message was part of the same line of prophets [1].
- **Warning** [1]: Waraqa said that the people would drive him out, preparing him for opposition [1].
- **Promise of support** [1]: he said he would help if he lived, showing that there were people who believed [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Indeed, We have revealed to you, [O Muhammad], as We revealed to Noah and the prophets after him" (Qur'an 4:163) [2].
- "Say, 'I am not something original among the messengers'" (Qur'an 46:9) [2].

**10.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** it is the beginning of the Qur'an and of the message of Islam; everything else followed from it.
- **For the statement:** it made him a Prophet and Messenger and gave him a mission to all people.
- **Against the statement:** other events were also vital: the Hijrah, which created the first Muslim community; the conquest of Makkah; and the farewell sermon.
- **Against the statement:** the call needed the support of Khadija (RA) and others to take effect.
- **Balanced view:** the call was the foundation of all that followed, so it is hard to place another event above it.

*Use of the passage*

- "Recite in the name of your Lord who created" shows the start of the message and its source.
- "Taught man that which he knew not" shows that revelation brought new guidance.
- The opening command shows that the mission began with a duty to read, learn and teach.
