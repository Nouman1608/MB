## Questions

**1.** Three fatty acids are shown as condensed formulae.
Acid P: CH3(CH2)12COOH. Acid Q: CH3(CH2)5CH=CH(CH2)7COOH. Acid R: CH3(CH2)4(CH=CHCH2)2(CH2)6COOH.
Identify which acid is saturated and which has the most double bonds. Give a reason for each. **[2]**

**2.** Glycerol has Mr 92. Lauric acid, C12H24O2, has Mr 200. One molecule of glycerol reacts with three molecules of lauric acid.
**(a)** Name the bond formed and state how many water molecules are released. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the Mr of the triglyceride. **[2]**

**3.** A food label gives the energy content of fat as 37 kJ per gram and of carbohydrate as 17 kJ per gram.
**(a)** Calculate the mass of carbohydrate that releases the same energy as 15 g of fat. **[2]**
**(b)** Using the structure of a triglyceride, explain why fat releases more energy per gram. **[2]**

**4.** Compare the structures of a triglyceride and a phospholipid, then explain why phospholipids but not triglycerides form a bilayer in water. **[4]**

**5.** The melting points of three fatty acids with 18 carbon atoms are: stearic acid 69 °C, oleic acid 13 °C and linoleic acid minus 5 °C. Stearic acid has no double bonds, oleic acid has one and linoleic acid has two. Explain the trend. **[3]**

**6.** A student tests four samples. The results are in the table.

| Sample | Emulsion test | Biuret test |
|---|---|---|
| W | Cloudy white | Blue |
| X | Clear | Purple |
| Y | Clear | Blue |
| Z | Cloudy white | Purple |

**(a)** State what each sample contains, using only these two tests. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe how the emulsion test is carried out. **[2]**

**7.** **(a)** Draw the general structure of an amino acid, labelling every group attached to the central carbon except hydrogen. **[2]**
**(b)** State what makes one of the twenty common amino acids different from another. **[1]**

**8.** Serine has the formula C3H7NO3 and valine has the formula C5H11NO2.
**(a)** Name the reaction that joins them and the bond that forms. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the Mr of the dipeptide. **[2]**
**(c)** A polypeptide has 133 amino acids. State how many peptide bonds it contains and how many water molecules are needed to hydrolyse it completely. **[2]**

**9.** Describe the primary, secondary and tertiary structure of a protein, naming the bonds that hold each level. **[5]**

**10.** In a study of a protein, researchers replaced one cysteine with alanine. Alanine cannot form a disulfide bridge. The temperature at which half the protein had lost its shape was 72 °C for the normal protein and 58 °C for the altered protein.
Explain these results and suggest why the replacement changes only some bonds. **[5]**

**11.** A biuret test was used to estimate protein concentration. The absorbance of standard solutions was 0.00, 0.11, 0.22, 0.33 and 0.44 at 0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0 mg per cm³. The absorbance is proportional to concentration.
**(a)** A sample gave an absorbance of 0.30. Calculate its protein concentration. **[2]**
**(b)** A second sample was diluted so that one part sample was mixed with three parts water. The diluted sample gave 0.176. Calculate the concentration of the original sample. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the biuret reagent changes colour only when protein is present. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Acid P is saturated because its chain has only single bonds [1]. Acid R has the most double bonds because its formula shows two C=C groups, against one in acid Q [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote evidence from the formula; "it is long" or "it is bent" does not identify saturation.

**2. (a)** Ester bond [1]; three water molecules [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Mass of reactants = 92 + (3 × 200) = 692 [1]. Subtract water: 692 - (3 × 18) = **638** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the subtraction of three waters; forgetting to remove them is a common loss of a mark.

**3. (a)** Energy from fat = 15 × 37 = 555 kJ [1]. Carbohydrate = 555 / 17 = **32.6 g** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Triglycerides have long hydrocarbon chains with many C-H bonds [1], so more energy is released on oxidation per gram than from carbohydrate [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the structural feature (C-H bonds in chains) and link it to energy. "Fat has more energy" alone repeats the question.

**4.** A triglyceride has glycerol with three fatty acids [1]; a phospholipid has one fatty acid replaced by a phosphate-containing group [1]. The phosphate group is hydrophilic and the fatty acid tails are hydrophobic [1]. In water the heads face the water and the tails face inward, forming a bilayer; a triglyceride has no hydrophilic part so cannot do this [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both molecules mentioned in each point; describing only the phospholipid scores less.

**5.** More double bonds lower the melting point [1]. Double bonds put bends in the chain, so molecules pack less closely [1]. Weaker forces between molecules need less energy to separate them, so melting happens at a lower temperature [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Describe the trend first, then explain it; the mechanism (packing and forces) earns the marks.

**6. (a)** W contains lipid only [1]. X contains protein only [1]. Y contains neither lipid nor protein; Z contains both [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Shake the sample with ethanol so lipid dissolves [1]. Pour into water; a cloudy white emulsion shows lipid [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Say "contains" and also state the negative result; the result alone is not a conclusion.

**7. (a)** Central carbon with NH2, COOH, H and R attached [1]; NH2 labelled amine, COOH labelled carboxyl and R labelled as the side chain [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Only the R group [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Attaching the groups to the wrong atom in your drawing can lose the structure mark, even when the labels are correct.

**8. (a)** Condensation [1]; peptide bond [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Mr of serine = 36 + 7 + 14 + 48 = 105; valine = 60 + 11 + 14 + 32 = 117 [1]. Dipeptide = 105 + 117 - 18 = **204** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 132 peptide bonds [1] and 132 water molecules [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Remember the single water lost; and for a chain, bonds are one fewer than amino acids.

**9.** Primary: the sequence of amino acids joined by peptide bonds [1]. Secondary: the chain coils into an alpha helix or folds into a beta pleated sheet [1], held by hydrogen bonds [1]. Tertiary: further folding into a specific three-dimensional shape [1], held by hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds and disulfide bridges between R groups [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Match each bond to its level; "hydrogen bonds" alone is too vague.

**10.** The disulfide bridge is a covalent bond that holds the tertiary structure [1]. Without it, the altered protein is held only by weaker hydrogen and ionic bonds [1]. Less heat is needed to break these, so the shape is lost at a lower temperature [1]. Only the cysteine R group is changed, so other R groups still form hydrogen and ionic bonds [1], and the sequence otherwise is unchanged [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each change in bonding to the observed temperature difference; a description of the numbers is not enough.

**11. (a)** Standards give 0.22 per mg cm-3 [1]. 0.30 / 0.22 = **1.36 mg per cm³** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Diluted concentration = 0.176 / 0.22 = 0.80 mg per cm³ [1]. Dilution factor is 4 [1]. Original = 0.80 × 4 = **3.2 mg per cm³** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** The reagent contains copper(II) ions in alkali [1]; these react with peptide bonds, giving a purple colour [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Multiply back by the dilution factor and give units; the unscaled value is a common error.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a bond as "hydrogen" when the question asks for the one linking amino acids.
- Giving the formula of a triglyceride without subtracting three waters.
- Saying a phospholipid has "a hydrophilic tail".
- Describing a trend in data without an explanation.
- Giving only the colour of a food test, without saying what it shows.
- Putting disulfide bridges in secondary structure.
- Writing one peptide bond per amino acid instead of one fewer.
