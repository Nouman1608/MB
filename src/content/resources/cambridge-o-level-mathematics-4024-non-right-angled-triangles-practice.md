These questions cover section 6.3, Non-right-angled triangles, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The methods are explained in the [non-right-angled triangles study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-non-right-angled-triangles/), and the [non-right-angled triangles revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-non-right-angled-triangles-revision-notes/) summarise them. Give lengths to 3 significant figures and angles to 1 decimal place unless told otherwise. In each triangle ABC, side a is opposite angle A, and so on. Diagrams are not drawn to scale.

## Section A

**1.** In triangle ABC, A = 50°, B = 70° and a = 12 cm. Find b. **[3]**

**2.** In triangle ABC, b = 8 cm, c = 11 cm and A = 48°. Find a. **[3]**

**3.** A triangle has sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 9 cm. Find the largest angle. **[3]**

**4.** Two sides of a triangle are 9 cm and 12 cm, with an angle of 55° between them. Find the area of the triangle. **[2]**

**5.** A triangle has sides 4 cm, 5 cm and 8 cm. Find the largest angle. **[3]**

**6.** In triangle ABC, a = 10 cm, b = 7 cm and A = 65°. Find angle B, and explain why there is only one possible triangle. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** In triangle ABC, AB = 12 cm, AC = 9 cm and angle ABC = 40°.

**(a)** Find the two possible values of angle ACB.

**(b)** Find the two possible lengths of BC. **[6]**

**8.** A triangle has sides 7 cm, 8 cm and 9 cm.

**(a)** Find the angle between the sides of 7 cm and 8 cm.

**(b)** Find the area of the triangle. **[4]**

**9.** A ship sails 15 km from A to B on a bearing of 070°, then 20 km from B to C on a bearing of 130°.

**(a)** Show that angle ABC = 120°.

**(b)** Find AC.

**(c)** Find the bearing of C from A, to the nearest degree. **[7]**

**10.** A triangle has sides of 10 cm and 8 cm and an area of 30 cm². Find the acute angle between the two sides. **[3]**

**11.** The corners of a triangular field are A, B and C. AB = 120 m, AC = 150 m and angle BAC = 70°. Find BC and the area of the field. **[5]**

**12.** A pupil says that the sine rule cannot be used to find an angle when all three sides are known. Explain why, and say which rule to use. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** b / sin 70° = 12 / sin 50° [1]. b = 12 × sin 70° ÷ sin 50° [1] = **14.7 cm** [1].

**2.** a² = 8² + 11² − 2 × 8 × 11 × cos 48° [1] = 185 − 176 cos 48° = 67.23 [1]. **a = 8.20 cm** [1].

**3.** The largest angle is opposite the 9 cm side [1]. cos θ = (36 + 64 − 81) / (2 × 6 × 8) = 19/96 [1]. θ = **78.6°** [1].

**4.** Area = 1/2 × 9 × 12 × sin 55° [1] = **44.2 cm²** [1].

**5.** cos θ = (16 + 25 − 64) / (2 × 4 × 5) [1] = −23/40 = −0.575 [1]. θ = **125.1°** [1].

**6.** sin B / 7 = sin 65° / 10 [1], so sin B = 0.6344 and B = **39.4°** [1]. The other value, 180° − 39.4° = 140.6°, would give A + B = 205.6°, which is more than 180°, so only one triangle exists. The given angle is opposite the longer side [1].

**7. (a)** sin C / 12 = sin 40° / 9, so sin C = 0.8571 [1]. C = **59.0°** or **121.0°** [1][1].

**(b)** When C = 59.0°, A = 81.0° and BC = 9 × sin 81.0° ÷ sin 40° = **13.8 cm** [1][1]. When C = 121.0°, A = 19.0° and BC = **4.56 cm** [1].

**8. (a)** The angle between the sides of 7 and 8 is opposite the side of 9 [1]. cos C = (49 + 64 − 81)/(2 × 7 × 8) = 32/112, so C = **73.4°** [1].

**(b)** Area = 1/2 × 7 × 8 × sin 73.4° [1] = **26.8 cm²** [1].

**9. (a)** The bearing of A from B is 070° + 180° = 250° [1]. Angle ABC = 250° − 130° = **120°** [1].

**(b)** AC² = 15² + 20² − 2 × 15 × 20 × cos 120° = 225 + 400 + 300 = 925 [1][1]. **AC = 30.4 km** [1].

**(c)** sin A / 20 = sin 120° / 30.41, so angle BAC = 34.7° [1]. Bearing of C from A = 070° + 34.7° = 104.7°, so **105°** [1].

**10.** 1/2 × 10 × 8 × sin θ = 30 [1]. 40 sin θ = 30, so sin θ = 0.75 [1]. θ = **48.6°** [1].

**11.** BC² = 120² + 150² − 2 × 120 × 150 × cos 70° [1] = 36 900 − 12 312.7 = 24 587 [1]. **BC = 157 m** [1]. Area = 1/2 × 120 × 150 × sin 70° [1] = **8460 m²** [1].

**12.** The sine rule needs a side and its opposite angle as a known pair, and with three sides and no angle there is no such pair [1]. Use the **cosine rule** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the sine rule when the cosine rule is needed.
- Calculating (b² + c² − 2bc) × cos A instead of subtracting 2bc cos A.
- Forgetting that the cosine of an obtuse angle is negative.
- Missing the second solution in the ambiguous case, or not testing it.
- Using an angle that is not between the sides in the area formula.
- Rounding too early.
