This set covers **section 2.12 (AHL only)** of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches, first assessment 2021: polynomial functions, zeros, roots and factors, the factor and remainder theorems, and sums and products of roots. All questions are calculator-free, as on HL Paper 1. Review the methods in the [polynomials study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-2-12/) and the [polynomials revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-2-12-revision-notes/) first.

## Section A

**1.** Let p(x) = 3x³ − 5x² + 2x − 4. Find the remainder when p(x) is divided by:

**(a)** (x − 2) **[1]**
**(b)** (3x − 2) **[2]**

**2.** Write down the sum and the product of the roots of 2x⁵ − 3x⁴ + x² − 8 = 0. **[2]**

**3.** Let p(x) = 2x³ + 3x² − 8x + 3.

**(a)** Show that (2x − 1) is a factor of p(x). **[1]**
**(b)** Hence factorise p(x) fully. **[3]**

**4.** Find the quotient and the remainder when x³ + 2x² − 5 is divided by (x − 1). **[3]**

**5.** The polynomial p(x) = x³ + ax² + bx + 6 has factors (x − 2) and (x + 3).

**(a)** Find the values of a and b. **[4]**
**(b)** Write down the third linear factor of p(x). **[1]**

**6.** Let y = −(x + 2)(x − 1)²(x − 4).

**(a)** Write down the x-intercepts and state whether the graph crosses or touches the axis at each. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the y-intercept. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the behaviour of the graph as x → ∞ and as x → −∞. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Let p(x) = 2x⁴ + x³ − 8x² − x + 6.

**(a)** Show that (x − 1) and (x + 1) are both factors of p(x). **[2]**
**(b)** Hence factorise p(x) fully. **[3]**
**(c)** Verify that your roots agree with the formulae for the sum and the product of the roots. **[2]**

**8.** The graph of a cubic function y = p(x) touches the x-axis at (3, 0), crosses it at (−1, 0) and passes through (1, −8).

**(a)** Find p(x) in factorised form. **[3]**
**(b)** Write down the y-intercept of the graph. **[1]**
**(c)** Express p(x) in the form ax³ + bx² + cx + d. **[2]**
**(d)** Use your answer to (c) to confirm the sum of the roots. **[1]**

**9.** The quartic equation x⁴ + bx³ + cx² + dx + e = 0, where b, c, d and e are real, has roots 2i and 1 − i.

**(a)** Write down the other two roots, giving a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the sum and product of the roots to find b and e. **[3]**
**(c)** Find c and d. **[2]**

**10.** The roots of 8x³ − 28x² + kx − 8 = 0 form a geometric sequence.

**(a)** By writing the roots as m/r, m and mr, show that m = 1. **[2]**
**(b)** Find k. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the other two roots. **[2]**

**11.** When p(x) = x³ + ax² + bx − 4 is divided by (x − 1) the remainder is −6, and when it is divided by (x + 3) the remainder is −10.

**(a)** Find the values of a and b. **[4]**
**(b)** Hence factorise p(x) fully. **[3]**

**12.** The equation x³ − 3x² − 4x + 12 = 0 has two roots that are the negatives of each other. Use the sum and product of the roots to find all three roots. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** p(2) = 24 − 20 + 4 − 4 = 4 [1]
**(b)** Remainder is p(2/3) [1] = 8/9 − 20/9 + 12/9 − 36/9 = −4 [1]

**2.** Sum = −(−3)/2 = 3/2 [1]; product = (−1)⁵(−8)/2 = 4 [1]
*Examiner insight:* The degree is 5, so the sign factor is (−1)⁵ = −1. Missing x³ and x terms do not change the formulae.

**3. (a)** p(1/2) = 1/4 + 3/4 − 4 + 3 = 0, so (2x − 1) is a factor [1]
**(b)** p(x) = (2x − 1)(x² + bx − 3) by the leading term and constant [1]; x² terms: 2b − 1 = 3, so b = 2 [1]; p(x) = (2x − 1)(x + 3)(x − 1) [1]

**4.** Division: x³ + 2x² + 0x − 5 = (x − 1)(x² + 3x + 3) − 2 [1]. Quotient x² + 3x + 3 [1]; remainder −2 [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check the remainder independently: p(1) = 1 + 2 − 5 = −2.

**5. (a)** p(2) = 0: 8 + 4a + 2b + 6 = 0, so 2a + b = −7 [1]. p(−3) = 0: −27 + 9a − 3b + 6 = 0, so 3a − b = 7 [1]. Adding, 5a = 0 [1], so a = 0 and b = −7 [1]
**(b)** p(x) = x³ − 7x + 6 = (x − 2)(x + 3)(x − 1), so the factor is (x − 1) [1]

**6. (a)** x = −2, crosses [1]; x = 1, touches [1]; x = 4, crosses [1]
**(b)** y = −(2)(1)(−4) = 8 [1]
**(c)** The leading term is −x⁴ (even degree, negative coefficient) [1], so y → −∞ both as x → ∞ and as x → −∞ [1]

**7. (a)** p(1) = 2 + 1 − 8 − 1 + 6 = 0 [1]; p(−1) = 2 − 1 − 8 + 1 + 6 = 0 [1]
**(b)** (x − 1)(x + 1) = x² − 1, so p(x) = (x² − 1)(2x² + x − 6) [1]; 2x² + x − 6 = (2x − 3)(x + 2) [1]; p(x) = (x − 1)(x + 1)(2x − 3)(x + 2) [1]
**(c)** Sum: 1 − 1 + 3/2 − 2 = −1/2 = −1/2 from −a₃/a₄ [1]. Product: (1)(−1)(3/2)(−2) = 3 = (+1)(6)/2 [1]

**8. (a)** p(x) = a(x − 3)²(x + 1) [1]. At (1, −8): a(4)(2) = −8 [1], so a = −1 and p(x) = −(x − 3)²(x + 1) [1]
**(b)** p(0) = −(9)(1) = −9, so (0, −9) [1]
**(c)** −(x² − 6x + 9)(x + 1) = −(x³ − 5x² + 3x + 9) [1] = −x³ + 5x² − 3x − 9 [1]
**(d)** −b/a = −5/(−1) = 5, and 3 + 3 + (−1) = 5 [1]
*Examiner insight:* The double root counts twice in the sum.

**9. (a)** Real coefficients mean non-real roots come in conjugate pairs [1], so −2i and 1 + i [1]
**(b)** Sum = 2i − 2i + (1 − i) + (1 + i) = 2 = −b, so b = −2 [1]. Product = (2i)(−2i)(1 − i)(1 + i) = 4 × 2 = 8 [1]; for degree 4 the product is +e, so e = 8 [1]
**(c)** (x² + 4)(x² − 2x + 2) = x⁴ − 2x³ + 6x² − 8x + 8 [1], so c = 6 and d = −8 [1]

**10. (a)** Product of roots = (m/r)(m)(mr) = m³ [1], and m³ = (−1)³(−8)/8 = 1, so m = 1 [1]
**(b)** x = 1 is a root: 8 − 28 + k − 8 = 0 [1], so k = 28 [1]
**(c)** 8x³ − 28x² + 28x − 8 = 4(x − 1)(2x² − 5x + 2) = 4(x − 1)(2x − 1)(x − 2) [1], so the other roots are 1/2 and 2 [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check the sequence: 1/2, 1, 2 has common ratio 2.

**11. (a)** p(1) = 1 + a + b − 4 = −6, so a + b = −3 [1]. p(−3) = −27 + 9a − 3b − 4 = −10, so 3a − b = 7 [1]. Adding, 4a = 4 [1], so a = 1 and b = −4 [1]
**(b)** p(x) = x³ + x² − 4x − 4 = x²(x + 1) − 4(x + 1) [1] = (x + 1)(x² − 4) [1] = (x + 1)(x − 2)(x + 2) [1]

**12.** Let the roots be α, −α and β. Sum: α − α + β = 3, so β = 3 [1]. Product: (α)(−α)(3) = −(12)/1 [1], so −3α² = −12 and α² = 4 [1]. The roots are −2, 2 and 3 [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check with the factor theorem: p(3) = 27 − 27 − 12 + 12 = 0.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Sign errors in the remainder theorem for divisors such as (x + 3) or (3x − 2).
- Missing the concluding statement after showing p(r) = 0.
- The wrong sign in the product of roots for odd degree.
- Forgetting that a double root is counted twice in the sum and product.
- Leaving out the scaling constant when building a polynomial from its zeros.
- Not stating "conjugate" as the reason for the extra complex roots.
