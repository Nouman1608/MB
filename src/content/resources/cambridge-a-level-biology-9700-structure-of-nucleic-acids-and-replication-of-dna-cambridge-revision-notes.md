## 6.1.1 Nucleotides

| | DNA nucleotide | RNA nucleotide | ATP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sugar | Deoxyribose | Ribose | Ribose |
| Bases | A, T, C, G | A, U, C, G | Adenine |
| Phosphates | 1 | 1 | 3 |

- Nucleotides join by **condensation** to form **phosphodiester bonds** (phosphate of one to sugar of the next).
- Each strand has a **5′ end** (phosphate) and a **3′ end** (–OH on the sugar).

## 6.1.2 Bases

| Purines (double ring) | Pyrimidines (single ring) |
|---|---|
| Adenine, guanine | Cytosine, thymine, uracil |

**Memory aid:** "Pure As Gold" for purines. Thymine only in DNA; uracil only in RNA.

## 6.1.3 The double helix

- Two strands, **antiparallel** (5′→3′ beside 3′→5′).
- **Complementary base pairing:** A–T (2 hydrogen bonds), C–G (3 hydrogen bonds).
- Each pair is one purine + one pyrimidine → constant width.
- Backbone: sugar–phosphate, held by phosphodiester bonds (strong).
- Strands held together by many hydrogen bonds (weak individually; stable together; separable).

### Methods

**Complementary strand:**
```
5' - C A G T T A C - 3'
3' - G T C A A T G - 5'   (written 5'->3': G T A A C T G)
```

**Base proportions (double-stranded only):** A = T, C = G, A + T + C + G = 100%.
```
T = 31% -> A = 31% -> C + G = 38% -> C = G = 19%
```

**Hydrogen bonds:** (A–T pairs × 2) + (C–G pairs × 3).
```
25 bp with 10 C-G pairs: 10 x 3 + 15 x 2 = 30 + 30 = 60
```

## 6.1.4 Replication

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 | Hydrogen bonds break; strands separate; both are templates |
| 2 | Free DNA nucleotides pair with exposed complementary bases |
| 3 | DNA polymerase forms phosphodiester bonds between adjacent new nucleotides |
| 4 | DNA ligase joins fragments of new DNA |
| 5 | Two molecules, each with one old and one new strand |

- Happens in **S phase** of interphase.
- DNA polymerase adds only to the **3′ end** → new strand built **5′ to 3′**.
- **Leading strand:** synthesis follows the fork → continuous.
- **Lagging strand:** synthesis moves away from the fork → short fragments joined by ligase.

### Leading and lagging strands compared

| | Leading strand | Lagging strand |
|---|---|---|
| Direction of synthesis | 5′ to 3′ | 5′ to 3′ |
| Relative to the replication fork | Towards the opening fork | Away from the opening fork |
| How it is made | Continuously | In short fragments as template is exposed |
| Enzymes | DNA polymerase | DNA polymerase, then DNA ligase to join fragments |

**Why:** the two templates are antiparallel and DNA polymerase adds only to the 3′ end, so only one template can be copied in the same direction as the fork moves.

### Density evidence (Meselson and Stahl, 1958)

| Generations after moving from ¹⁵N to ¹⁴N | Bands |
|---|---|
| 0 | Heavy |
| 1 | Intermediate only |
| 2 | Half intermediate, half light |
| n | 2 of 2ⁿ molecules hybrid; rest light |

Conservative replication would give heavy + light after one generation, not intermediate.

## 6.1.5 mRNA

Single strand; ribose; uracil not thymine; complementary to the template strand of one gene; shorter than DNA; read in codons.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nucleotide | Pentose sugar + phosphate + nitrogenous base |
| Phosphodiester bond | Bond linking sugar of one nucleotide to phosphate of the next |
| Antiparallel | Strands running in opposite directions |
| Complementary base pairing | A with T (or U), C with G |
| Semi-conservative replication | Each new molecule has one original and one new strand |
| DNA polymerase | Enzyme joining new nucleotides with phosphodiester bonds |
| DNA ligase | Enzyme joining fragments of DNA |
| Leading strand | New strand made continuously |
| Lagging strand | New strand made in fragments |

## Exam traps

- C–G has **three** hydrogen bonds; A–T has two.
- Label 5′ and 3′ on both strands.
- DNA polymerase does not break hydrogen bonds.
- Ligase joins backbones, not bases.
- Lagging strand explanation needs antiparallel strands **and** the 5′→3′ rule.
- A = T only in double-stranded DNA.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the three components of a nucleotide.
2. State two differences between a DNA nucleotide and ATP.
3. Which bases are purines?
4. Why does purine–pyrimidine pairing matter?
5. How many hydrogen bonds join C and G?
6. Write the complementary strand of 5′-TTGACG-3′, 5′ to 3′.
7. A DNA sample has 17% cytosine. Give the percentage of adenine.
8. Which enzyme forms phosphodiester bonds between new nucleotides?
9. What does DNA ligase do?
10. Explain why "semi-conservative" describes DNA replication.
11. After 3 generations in ¹⁴N, what fraction of DNA molecules is hybrid?
12. Give three ways mRNA differs from DNA.

### Answers

1. Pentose sugar, phosphate group, nitrogenous base.
2. Any two: ATP has ribose, not deoxyribose; three phosphates, not one; base is always adenine.
3. Adenine and guanine.
4. Each pair has the same width, so the helix has a constant diameter.
5. Three.
6. Complement 3′-AACTGC-5′, written 5′-CGTCAA-3′.
7. G = 17%, so A + T = 66%; A = 33%.
8. DNA polymerase.
9. Joins fragments of new DNA by forming phosphodiester bonds.
10. Each new molecule keeps one original strand and has one newly made strand.
11. 2 of 8 = one quarter.
12. Any three: single strand; ribose; uracil instead of thymine; much shorter.
