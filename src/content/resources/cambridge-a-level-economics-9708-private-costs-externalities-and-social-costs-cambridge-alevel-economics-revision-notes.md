## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Private cost | Cost borne by the producer or consumer making the decision |
| External cost | Cost borne by a third party, with no compensation |
| Social cost | Private cost + external cost |
| Private benefit | Benefit to the party making the decision |
| External benefit | Benefit to a third party, who does not pay for it |
| Social benefit | Private benefit + external benefit |
| Negative externality | An external cost |
| Positive externality | An external benefit |
| Deadweight welfare loss | Net loss to society from producing a quantity other than the social optimum |
| Asymmetric information | One party to a transaction knows more than the other |
| Moral hazard | Taking more risk or less care because someone else bears the cost |

## Where each curve goes

| Type | Shift |
|---|---|
| Negative production | MSC above MPC |
| Positive production | MSC below MPC |
| Negative consumption | MSB below MPB |
| Positive consumption | MSB above MPB |

Market: MPB = MPC. Optimum: MSB = MSC.

## Table method

1. Add MEC to MPC to get MSC, or MEB to MPB to get MSB.
2. Find the market quantity where MPB = MPC.
3. Find the optimum where MSB = MSC.
4. At the market quantity, find the gap between MSB and MSC.
5. Welfare loss = ½ × gap × difference in quantity (watch the units).

Quick example: market output 50, optimum 40 (thousands); at 50, MSC = 90 and MSB = 60. Loss = ½ × 30 × 10,000 = 150,000.

## Over- and under-production

| Externality | Market vs optimum | Welfare loss is |
|---|---|---|
| Negative | Market output too high | Excess of social cost over social benefit on extra units |
| Positive | Market output too low | Social benefit lost on units not produced |

## Asymmetric information versus moral hazard

| | Asymmetric information | Moral hazard |
|---|---|---|
| When | Before or at the time of a trade | After an agreement is made |
| What happens | Less-informed party misjudges quality or risk | Behaviour changes because the cost falls on someone else |
| Example | Buyer cannot judge a used phone's battery | Insured driver takes less care |
| Result | Good trades lost or wrong prices | More risk-taking, higher costs for insurers or lenders |

## Cost-benefit decisions

```
Net private benefit = PB - PC   -> what a private firm considers
Net social benefit  = SB - SC   -> what society should consider
Go ahead if net social benefit > 0; choose the option with the largest
```

**Evaluation:** intangibles are hard to value; forecasts may be wrong; the decision may flip if one value changes; distribution of gains and losses may be ignored.

## Exam traps

- External costs add to costs; external benefits add to benefits.
- A cost to the firm itself is private, not external.
- Shade only the triangle between MSB and MSC, bounded by the two quantities.
- Multiply by the quantity units (thousands) in welfare loss.
- Moral hazard is a change in behaviour after the agreement.
- Net present value is not required.

## Self-test

1. Write the formula for social cost.
2. MPB is 40, MEB is 8. Calculate MSB.
3. MPC is 25, MSC is 32. Calculate MEC.
4. Give one example of a positive production externality.
5. Which curve lies above which for a negative consumption externality?
6. Where does the free market settle, and where is the social optimum?
7. Market output is 70 thousand and the optimum is 60 thousand. At 70 thousand, MSC is 54 and MSB is 38. Calculate the welfare loss.
8. Why does a positive externality lead to a welfare loss even though no harm is done?
9. Define moral hazard.
10. Give one example of asymmetric information.
11. A project has PB 12, EB 6, PC 10, EC 5. Calculate net private and net social benefit.
12. Give one problem in valuing costs and benefits.

**Answers:** 1. SC = PC + EC. 2. 48. 3. 7. 4. A beekeeper whose bees pollinate a neighbour's crops (or a firm whose training benefits other employers). 5. MPB lies above MSB. 6. Market: MPB = MPC; optimum: MSB = MSC. 7. ½ × 16 × 10,000 = 80,000. 8. Units that society values above their cost are not produced, so potential welfare is lost. 9. Taking more risk or less care because someone else bears the cost of the outcome. 10. A used-phone seller knows more about the battery than the buyer (other valid examples accepted). 11. Net private = 12 − 10 = +2; net social = 18 − 15 = +3. 12. Intangible items such as noise or health effects have no market price.
