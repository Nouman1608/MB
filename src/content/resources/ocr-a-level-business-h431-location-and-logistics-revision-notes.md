Condensed notes on the Location and logistics content (4.16 on this site) in the area of study Productive quality (fit for purpose), OCR A Level Business H431, Version 2.1 (January 2026), final assessment summer 2027. Full explanations and three costed examples are in the [location and logistics study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-location-and-logistics/). Test yourself with the [location and logistics practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-location-and-logistics-practice/). All figures are invented.

## What the specification lists

- Factors affecting the choice of location; advantages and disadvantages of locations; impact and importance of location; recommend and justify locations.
- Logistics: supply chain management, distribution management, warehousing, distribution centres, digital.
- Factors affecting logistical decisions; internal and external provision; impact and importance; recommend a logistics strategy.
- Outsourcing, subcontracting, offshoring and re-shoring, and their impact.

## Core recall

### Location factors

| Factor | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Customers | Do they visit? How far must goods travel? |
| Suppliers and materials | Are inputs heavy, bulky or perishable? |
| Labour | Skills and wage levels available locally |
| Costs | Rent or price, rates, utilities |
| Transport and communications | Road, rail, port, air, broadband |
| Government help | Grants, tax incentives |
| Competitors | Do they attract customers or take them? |
| Room to expand | Space for extra capacity |
| Legal and environmental limits | Planning permission, pollution rules |
| Personal and ethical | Owner's home area; community links |

### Typical sites

| Site | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| City centre | Footfall; labour pool | High rent; congestion |
| Out of town | Space; parking; lower cost | Further from customers; car dependent |
| Business park | Shared services; transport links | Cost; fewer passing customers |
| Rural or regional | Low cost; grants | Distance from market; fewer skilled staff |
| Overseas | Low labour cost; new markets | Risk, distance, quality control |

- **Weighted scoring:** score each site 1 to 5 on each factor, multiply by the weight, add up. Useful structure, but scores and weights are judgements.

### Logistics terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Logistics | Managing the movement and storage of materials and products |
| Supply chain management | Coordinating all the businesses and activities from raw material to customer |
| Distribution management | Planning routes, vehicles and transport modes to customers |
| Warehousing | Storing stock until needed |
| Distribution centre | Receives bulk goods, sorts and dispatches to shops or customers |
| Digital logistics | Software to track stock and vehicles, plan routes and give delivery updates |

- Factors: cost, speed, reliability, nature of product, number and location of customers, environment, technology.

### Internal against external logistics

| | In-house | External provider |
|---|---|---|
| Control | High | Lower |
| Cost pattern | Mostly fixed | Mostly variable |
| Flexibility | Low | High |
| Best when | Steady, high volume | Variable or low volume |

### Outsourcing and related terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Outsourcing | Paying another business to carry out a function or process |
| Subcontracting | Passing part of a job or order to another business, often for peaks or specialist skills |
| Offshoring | Moving production or functions to another country |
| Re-shoring | Bringing them back to the home country |

- Offshoring costs people forget: shipping, duties, extra stock, quality control, exchange-rate risk.
- Reasons for re-shoring: rising overseas wages, supply disruption, speed, automation, customers wanting local goods.

## Worked reminder

Tarn Interiors (invented).

- Site A total cost £486,000 ÷ 60,000 units = **£8.10**; Site B £430,000 ÷ 60,000 = **£7.17**. B is £56,000 cheaper.
- Weighted scores: A 3.30, B 3.50. With cost and access both weighted 30, A 3.60 and B 3.30.
- In-house fleet: fixed £132,000, fuel £0.75 a delivery. External £7.20 a delivery. Break-even = 132,000 ÷ (7.20 − 0.75) = **20,465 deliveries**.
- Offshore unit cost £9.50 + £1.80 + £0.57 + £0.35 = **£12.22** against £14.00 in the UK; saving £71,200 on 40,000 units; payback on £150,000 set-up = **2.1 years**. With 4% extra defects, payback is **2.7 years**.

## Recommending: a four-step method

1. Identify what matters most to this business (customers, cost, skills, speed).
2. Compare at least two options with figures where given.
3. State the risks and hidden costs.
4. Conclude, and say what the choice depends on.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Footfall | Number of people passing or entering a location |
| Enterprise zone | Area with tax or planning incentives |
| Lead time | Time from order to delivery |
| Buffer stock | Minimum stock held as protection |
| Tariff | A tax on imported goods |
| Weighted score | Total of scores multiplied by the importance of each factor |

## Exam traps

- Say which factors matter most for this business, not all of them.
- A weighted score is a tool, not the answer.
- Offshoring means a different country; outsourcing means a different business.
- Count shipping, duties, stock and quality as costs of offshoring.
- Logistics includes storage as well as transport.
- Compare in-house and external costs at different volumes.

## Self-test

1. Give three factors that a manufacturer might consider when choosing a location.
2. Why is footfall important to a retailer?
3. Site P costs £210,000 a year to make 30,000 units. Calculate the cost per unit.
4. Define a distribution centre.
5. A fleet costs £15,000 a year fixed plus £2 a delivery. An external firm charges £8 a delivery. Calculate the break-even number of deliveries.
6. Distinguish between outsourcing and offshoring.
7. Give two reasons why a business might re-shore.
8. Give two extra costs of offshoring beyond the overseas production cost.
9. What is subcontracting?
10. Why might a weighted score be misleading?

### Answers

1. Any three: closeness to suppliers; labour skills; costs; transport links; government grants; room to expand.
2. More passing customers means more potential sales, without needing advertising.
3. £210,000 ÷ 30,000 = **£7.00**.
4. A large facility that receives goods in bulk, sorts them and dispatches them to shops or customers.
5. £15,000 ÷ (£8 − £2) = **2,500 deliveries**. Above this, in-house is cheaper.
6. Outsourcing is paying another business to do a function; offshoring is moving the work to another country (and may be done inside the same business).
7. Any two: rising overseas wages; supply disruption; wish for faster response; automation reducing labour cost advantage; customers wanting locally made goods.
8. Any two: shipping; import duties; extra stock holding; quality control; exchange-rate risk.
9. Passing part of a job or order to another business, often to cope with peaks or use specialist skills.
10. The scores and weights are judgements, so a small change can alter the result, and the total gives a false sense of precision.
