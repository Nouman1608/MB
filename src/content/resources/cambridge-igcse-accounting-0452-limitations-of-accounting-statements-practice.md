## Short-answer questions

Cambridge IGCSE Accounting 0452 question papers are not divided into lettered sections, so the questions below are grouped only by length.

**1.** State the **three** factors that the syllabus gives as causes of the limitations of accounting statements. **[3]**

**2.** Which of the following is a non-financial aspect that limits the usefulness of accounting statements?

A: The allowance for irrecoverable debts

B: The skill of the workforce

C: The net book value of equipment

D: The depreciation method

**[1]**

**3.** Equipment was bought five years ago for $60,000. It is depreciated at 15% of cost each year.

**(a)** Calculate its net book value now. **[2]**

**(b)** State **one** reason why this may differ from what the equipment is worth today. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why the location of a business is not shown in its financial statements, even though it may affect its profit. **[2]**

**5.** A shop's revenue rose from $150,000 to $162,000. Its selling prices rose by 8% during the year. Calculate the revenue at last year's prices and comment on the result. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why the allowance for irrecoverable debts is an example of the application of accounting policies. **[2]**

## Longer questions

**7.** Ruthven Limited and Selby Limited are identical businesses. Each has revenue of $250,000 and profit of $75,000 before depreciation and the allowance for irrecoverable debts. Each bought new equipment for $40,000 at the start of the year and is creating an allowance on trade receivables of $30,000 for the first time.

- Ruthven: straight line depreciation at 12.5% of cost; allowance of 3%.
- Selby: reducing balance depreciation at 40%; allowance of 6%.

**(a)** Calculate the profit for the year and the profit margin for each company. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain what your answer to (a) shows about comparing the two companies' financial statements. **[2]**

**8.** Tarn Limited has profit before interest of $45,000 and capital employed of $250,000. Its premises are included at their cost of $70,000, paid twenty years ago. They are now worth about $230,000.

**(a)** Calculate Tarn's ROCE from its statements. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate what the ROCE would be if the premises were included at their current value, with the increase added to reserves. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why historic cost can make a business's ROCE misleading. **[2]**

**9.** Two hotels in the same city, the Linden and the Marlow, report the same revenue and profit. The Linden's staff have years of training in hospitality and it is next to the main railway station. The Marlow employs mainly new, untrained staff and is on a road that will be closed for building works for two years. Explain **three** ways in which non-financial aspects mean the statements give an incomplete picture of the two hotels. **[6]**

**10.** Closing inventory of Usk Garden Supplies cost $18,000. This includes damaged goods that cost $7,000. The owner estimates that the damaged goods could be sold for between $2,000 and $5,000. Profit before valuing the damaged goods below cost is $40,000.

**(a)** Calculate the highest and lowest profit for the year that could be reported. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain how this illustrates a limitation of accounting statements. **[1]**

**11.** Explain how the economic climate can limit the usefulness of last year's financial statements to a bank considering a loan. **[4]**

**12.** Vale Engineering Limited reports a ROCE of 24.00%, well above other companies in its trade. Most of its machinery is over fifteen years old and nearly fully depreciated. The economy is entering a recession. A shareholder is deciding whether to buy more shares. Evaluate how far the shareholder can rely on Vale's financial statements in making this decision. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Historic cost [1]; application of accounting policies [1]; non-financial aspects, for example skill of the workforce, location of the business, economic climate [1]. **[3]**

**2.** B [1]. It affects performance but cannot be measured in money. **[1]**

**3. (a)** Annual depreciation 60,000 × 15% = 9,000 [1]; net book value 60,000 − (5 × 9,000) = **$15,000** [1].

**(b)** Any one: the statements use historic cost, so current market prices for similar equipment are not shown [1]; the estimated rate may not match how the equipment has actually worn; replacement cost may have risen. **[3]**

**4.** Only items that can be reliably measured in money are recorded (money measurement) [1]. A good location can raise sales, but the advantage it gives cannot be given a reliable money value, so only the cost of the premises or the rent paid appears [1]. **[2]**

**5.** 162,000 ÷ 1.08 = **$150,000** at last year's prices [1]. The quantity sold has not increased; the whole rise is due to higher prices, which the statements do not reveal [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The business chooses the percentage or method used to estimate irrecoverable debts [1], so another business with the same receivables could charge a different amount and report a different profit and net trade receivables [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Ruthven: depreciation 40,000 × 12.5% = 5,000; allowance 30,000 × 3% = 900; profit 75,000 − 5,900 = **$69,100** [1]; profit margin 69,100 ÷ 250,000 × 100 = **27.64%** [1].

Selby: depreciation 40,000 × 40% = 16,000; allowance 30,000 × 6% = 1,800; profit 75,000 − 17,800 = **$57,200** [1]; profit margin 57,200 ÷ 250,000 × 100 = **22.88%** [1].

**(b)** Identical trading gives profits $11,900 apart [1], so differences between businesses' statements may come from accounting policies rather than performance, and a user should check the policies before comparing [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** 45,000 ÷ 250,000 × 100 = **18.00%** [1].

**(b)** Capital employed 250,000 + (230,000 − 70,000) = 410,000 [1]; ROCE 45,000 ÷ 410,000 × 100 = **10.98%** [1].

**(c)** Assets bought long ago are recorded at their old, lower cost, so capital employed is understated compared with the value of the resources used [1]. ROCE then looks higher than the return on what the assets are worth today, and comparison with businesses that bought assets recently is unfair [1]. **[5]**

**9.** One mark for each aspect identified, one for explaining its effect, up to three aspects.

- Skill of the workforce [1]: the Linden's trained staff are likely to give better service and win repeat bookings, but staff skill is not an asset in the statements [1].
- Location of the business [1]: the Linden's site by the station attracts guests, while the Marlow's road closure may cut bookings for two years, yet neither appears in the figures [1].
- Future prospects or the economic climate [1]: the equal profits are past results; the road works and staff differences suggest the Marlow's future profit will fall, which the statements cannot show [1].
- Also creditable: reputation or customer loyalty. **[6]**

**10. (a)** Write-down at the highest estimate: 7,000 − 5,000 = 2,000, so profit **$38,000** [1]. Write-down at the lowest estimate: 7,000 − 2,000 = 5,000, so profit **$35,000** [1]. Profit could therefore be reported anywhere from $35,000 to $38,000, a range of $3,000 [1].

**(b)** Inventory valuation depends on an estimate of net realisable value, so reported profit depends on how accounting policies are applied [1]. **[4]**

**11.** The statements record what happened last year, in last year's conditions [1]. In a recession customers may spend less, so revenue and profit may fall [1]; higher interest rates or rising prices may raise the business's costs [1]. The bank therefore cannot assume that last year's profit and liquidity will continue, so its judgement of whether the loan will be repaid needs information beyond the statements [1]. **[4]**

**12.** For relying on the statements: ROCE of 24.00% shows high profit compared with the capital employed [1], and the statements are prepared on recognised concepts, so the figures are a useful starting point [1].

Against: the machinery is old and nearly fully depreciated, so capital employed is small at historic cost and ROCE looks higher than a business with new machinery would show [1]; the machinery will need replacing soon, which will require large spending [1]; the recession is a non-financial aspect that may reduce future sales, and the statements show only past results [1].

Judgement: the shareholder should not rely on the ROCE alone; it overstates Vale's strength relative to rivals, and the shareholder needs information about replacement plans and expected demand before buying more shares [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling historic cost an error rather than explaining its effect.
- Referring to "policies" without naming one that fits the case.
- Naming non-financial aspects without saying how each affects the business.
- Ignoring that statements are past results when the question asks about future decisions.
- Giving no judgement in Evaluate questions.
