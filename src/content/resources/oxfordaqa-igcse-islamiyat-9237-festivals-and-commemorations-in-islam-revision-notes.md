**These notes condense content area 3.1.2.8 of 9237: "The origins and importance of Eid-Al-Adha, Eid-Ul-Fitr and Ashura and how they are observed."** The full explanation is in the [festivals and commemorations study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-festivals-and-commemorations-in-islam/).

## At a glance

| | Eid-ul-Fitr | Eid-ul-Adha | Ashura |
|---|---|---|---|
| Date | 1 Shawwal | 10 Dhul Hijjah | 10 Muharram |
| Type | Festival | Festival | Commemoration |
| Origin | End of Ramadhan; began in Madinah | Ibrahim (AS) and Ismail (AS); ram replaces the sacrifice | Sunni: Allah saved Musa (AS). Shia: Karbala, 61 AH |
| Main acts | Zakah al-Fitr; Eid prayer; visits; gifts | Eid prayer; Qurbani; sharing meat | Sunni: fasting. Shia: mourning gatherings |
| Core idea | Gratitude and joy | Obedience and sacrifice | Remembrance, gratitude, justice |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Eid | Festival |
| Fitr | Breaking the fast |
| Adha | Sacrifice |
| Takbir | Saying "Allahu Akbar" |
| Zakah al-Fitr | Food or its value given to the poor before the Eid prayer |
| Qurbani | The sacrifice of an animal at Eid-ul-Adha |
| Ashura | The 10th day of Muharram |
| Muharram | First month of the Islamic year |
| Majalis | Gatherings in which the story of Karbala is told |
| Tashriq | The days after Eid-ul-Adha |

## Eid-ul-Fitr

**Origin:** the end of Ramadhan (2:183-185); a thanksgiving for a month of fasting.

**How observed:**

1. Moon sighted, or thirty days complete.
2. Zakah al-Fitr paid before the prayer.
3. Wash, wear best clothes, eat dates, say the takbir.
4. Eid prayer (two rak'ahs) in congregation; sermon **after** the prayer.
5. Visits, greetings ("Eid Mubarak"), gifts, meals, forgiveness.

**Importance:** thanks to Allah; reward for fasting; care for the poor; unity.

## Eid-ul-Adha

**Origin:** the test of Ibrahim (AS): Allah asked him to sacrifice his son, both obeyed, Allah replaced the sacrifice with a ram (37:102-107). It falls on the day after Arafat, so it joins the whole Ummah to the pilgrims.

**How observed:**

1. Eid prayer and sermon.
2. Qurbani by those who can afford it: sheep, goat, cow or camel (large animals shared by up to seven).
3. Meat shared in three: family, relatives and friends, the poor.
4. Visits and meals over the days of Tashriq.

**Importance:** submission and trust in Allah; generosity; global unity; taqwa (22:37: it is piety that reaches Allah).

## Ashura

| | Sunni | Shia |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Allah saved Musa (AS) and the Israelites from Pharaoh; the Prophet (pbuh) fasted | The death of Hazrat Husayn (RA) at Karbala in 61 AH (680 CE) |
| Observance | Fasting on the 10th, often with the 9th or the 11th | Mourning gatherings, recitation of Karbala, processions, charity |
| Mood | Thankful and quiet | Sorrowful and reflective |
| Lesson | Gratitude and Allah's help to prophets | Standing for justice and truth at great cost |

**Remember:** Ashura is a day of remembrance, not a feast. Describe both traditions respectfully.

## The prescribed passage: Sunan Ibn Majah 1722

| Words | Use for |
|---|---|
| "The Messenger of Allah forbade fasting on these two days" | Fasting is forbidden on both Eids; they are days of celebration |
| "the Day of Fitr ... the day when you break your fast" | Meaning of Eid-ul-Fitr: end of Ramadhan |
| "on the Day of Adha you eat the meat of your sacrifices" | Link to Qurbani and sharing meat |
| "He started with the prayer before the sermon" | The Eid prayer comes first; this differs from Friday prayer |

## Comparing the two Eids

| Point | Fitr | Adha |
|---|---|---|
| Linked to | Ramadhan | Hajj and Ibrahim (AS) |
| Special giving | Zakah al-Fitr before the prayer | Qurbani meat shared |
| Lesson | Gratitude | Obedience and sacrifice |
| Fasting that day | Forbidden | Forbidden |

## The Islamic calendar and the dates

- The calendar is **lunar** and begins with the Hijrah.
- A month starts when the new moon is sighted.
- The lunar year is about ten or eleven days shorter than the solar year, so all three occasions move earlier through the seasons each year.
- Order of the months you need: Muharram (1st), Ramadhan (9th), Shawwal (10th), Dhul Hijjah (12th).

## Why the festivals matter to a Muslim

| Theme | Fitr | Adha | Ashura |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gratitude | For completing Ramadhan | For Allah's mercy to Ibrahim and Ismail | For Allah's help to Musa (Sunni) |
| Charity | Zakah al-Fitr | Qurbani meat shared | Food and charity given at gatherings (many communities) |
| Community | Prayer in congregation, visits | Prayer, visits, link to the pilgrims | Shared fasting or shared mourning |
| Obedience | Completing the fast | Ibrahim's submission | Following the Sunnah (Sunni); standing for truth (Shia) |

## How to build a 6-mark answer

1. State the first reason or way in one sentence (AO1).
2. Explain it with a detail, such as the three parts of the meat (AO1).
3. State and explain the second reason or way (AO1).
4. Add one source, such as 22:37 or the Ibn Majah hadith, and say how it supports the point (AO2).

**Mini example (Eid-ul-Fitr):** "Muslims give Zakah al-Fitr before the prayer so that the poor can enjoy the festival. This shows that the end of Ramadhan is not only about the person who fasted but about the whole community."

## Exam traps

- Fitr = **fast ending**; Adha = **sacrifice**.
- Zakah al-Fitr is not the same as the yearly Zakah.
- Ashura is **not** an Eid; Shia mourn, Sunni fast.
- Do not write that Muslims fast on Eid. It is forbidden.
- The Eid sermon comes **after** the prayer.
- Say **why** each act is done, not only what is done.

## Self-test

1. On which date is Eid-ul-Fitr?
2. What does "Fitr" mean?
3. What must be given before the Eid-ul-Fitr prayer?
4. Which prophet is remembered at Eid-ul-Adha?
5. What replaced the sacrifice of Ismail (AS)?
6. Into how many parts is the Qurbani meat commonly divided?
7. On which date is Ashura?
8. Give the Sunni reason for fasting on Ashura.
9. Whose death at Karbala do Shia Muslims remember on Ashura?
10. According to the hadith in Ibn Majah, what is forbidden on the two Eids?

**Answers:** 1. 1 Shawwal. 2. Breaking the fast. 3. Zakah al-Fitr. 4. Ibrahim (AS). 5. A ram. 6. Three: family, relatives and friends, the poor. 7. 10 Muharram. 8. Allah saved Musa (AS) and the Israelites from Pharaoh, and the Prophet (pbuh) fasted. 9. Hazrat Husayn (RA). 10. Fasting.
