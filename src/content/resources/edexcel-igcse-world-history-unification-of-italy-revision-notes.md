"Development of a nation: unification of Italy, 1848–70". For full explanations use the

## Key people

| Person | One-line role |
|---|---|
| Mazzini | Republican nationalist; leader of the Roman Republic, 1849 |
| Charles Albert | King of Piedmont; granted the Statuto; defeated 1848–49 |
| Victor Emmanuel II | King of Piedmont from 1849, King of Italy from 1861 |
| Cavour | Piedmont's Prime Minister from 1852; diplomat of the 1859 war |
| Garibaldi | Soldier; defended Rome 1849; conquered Sicily and Naples 1860 |
| Napoleon III | French emperor; ally in 1859; protector of the Pope in Rome |
| Pius IX | Pope; turned against nationalism in 1848; lost Rome in 1870 |
| Radetzky | Austrian commander who defeated Piedmont in 1848–49 |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Risorgimento | The movement for Italian national revival and unification |
| Statuto | Piedmont's 1848 constitution, kept after 1849 |
| *Connubio* | Cavour's 1852 alliance with Rattazzi's centre-left |
| Plombières | Secret 1858 agreement between Cavour and Napoleon III |
| Villafranca | Armistice of July 1859 that left Venetia Austrian |
| Plebiscite | Popular vote used to approve annexations (1860, 1866, 1870) |
| Piedmontisation | Imposing Piedmont's laws, taxes and officials on all Italy |
| Brigandage | Armed resistance in the south after 1861 |

## Factor comparison for "who mattered most?" essays

| Factor | Strongest evidence | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Cavour | Crimea, Plombières, French alliance; central states; moved army south in 1860 | Did not plan the southern conquest; died 1861 |
| Garibaldi | Conquered Sicily and Naples with about a thousand volunteers | Failed at Aspromonte and Mentana; needed Piedmont's army |
| Napoleon III | French armies won Magenta and Solferino | Stopped at Villafranca; kept Rome from Italy until 1870 |
| Foreign events | 1866 and 1870 wars delivered Venetia and Rome | Italy itself was beaten in 1866 |
| Victor Emmanuel II | Kept the Statuto; accepted Garibaldi's conquests at Teano | Often followed rather than led |

## Worked example: impression question

*Invented extract (for practice only):* "Garibaldi gave Italy its south almost by accident, and
Cavour spent the rest of 1860 trying to catch up with him."

A strong answer: the author gives the impression that the conquest of the south was
**unplanned and driven by Garibaldi**, not Cavour. "Almost by accident" suggests a lack of design,
and "trying to catch up" presents Cavour as reacting rather than leading. Own knowledge, such as
the Piedmontese march through the Papal States to reach Naples before Garibaldi could move on
Rome, supports the reading.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Mixing up the two battles of Custoza** (1848 and 1866) — both Italian defeats, eighteen years
  apart.
- **Saying Italy was complete in 1861.** Venetia (1866) and Rome (1870) were still outside.
- **Treating Garibaldi and Cavour as partners.** They distrusted each other; Garibaldi never forgave
  the loss of Nice.
- **Forgetting the price of French help:** Nice and Savoy.
- **Narrating instead of judging.** A 16-mark answer must weigh the named factor against at least
  one other and reach a conclusion.
- **Over-crediting Italian military power.** Austria was beaten in 1859 mainly by French armies and
  in 1866 by Prussia.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best one-line summary of 1848?**
Revolutions broke out across Italy but failed
because of Austrian strength, Italian divisions and the Pope's withdrawal; Piedmont alone kept its
constitution.

**Why did Napoleon III help Piedmont?**
He wanted to weaken Austria, gain Nice and Savoy and
extend French influence, and the Orsini Affair pushed him to act.

**How did Rome become part of Italy?**
France withdrew its garrison during the Franco-Prussian War,
and Italian troops entered the city in September 1870.
