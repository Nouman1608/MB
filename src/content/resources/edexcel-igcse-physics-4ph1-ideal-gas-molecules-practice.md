## Section A

**1.** State the value of absolute zero in degrees Celsius. **[1]**

**2.** Convert each temperature.

**(a)** 15 °C into kelvin **[1]**
**(b)** 450 K into degrees Celsius **[1]**

**3.** State the relationship between the Kelvin temperature of a gas and the average kinetic energy of its molecules. **[1]**

**4.** State what happens to the average speed of the molecules of a gas when the gas is heated. **[1]**

**5.** State the two conditions under which p₁/T₁ = p₂/T₂ applies to a gas. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A gas is sealed in a box.

**(a)** Explain how the molecules of the gas exert a pressure on the walls of the box. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the pressure is the same on every wall. **[1]**

**7.** A syringe contains 40 cm³ of air at a pressure of 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. The end is sealed and the plunger is pushed in slowly until the volume is 16 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the new pressure of the air. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain, in terms of molecules, why the pressure increases. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why the plunger is pushed in slowly. **[1]**

**8.** A gas cylinder contains gas at a pressure of 8.0 × 10⁵ Pa at 20 °C. A safety valve opens if the pressure reaches 1.0 × 10⁶ Pa. The volume of the cylinder does not change.

**(a)** Calculate the temperature, in °C, at which the valve opens. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain, in terms of molecules, why heating the gas increases its pressure. **[2]**

**9.** A student measures the pressure of a fixed mass of gas at constant volume. At 10 °C the pressure is 99.0 kPa; at 90 °C it is 127.0 kPa. The pressure rises by the same amount for each degree.

**(a)** Use these results to estimate absolute zero in °C. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why the gas cannot be cooled below this temperature. **[1]**

**10.** The molecules of a gas at 300 K have a certain average kinetic energy.

**(a)** Calculate the temperature, in °C, at which their average kinetic energy would be twice as large. **[2]**
**(b)** State the temperature, in kelvin, at which their average kinetic energy would be half as large. **[1]**

**11.** A bubble of gas of volume 1.5 cm³ is released at the bottom of a lake 15 m deep. The density of the water is 1000 kg/m³ and atmospheric pressure is 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. Assume the temperature of the gas stays constant. Calculate the volume of the bubble when it reaches the surface. **[4]**

**12.** A student says: "If I heat a sealed can of gas from 50 °C to 100 °C, I double the temperature, so the pressure doubles." Evaluate this statement, using a calculation. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** −273 °C [1].

**2. (a)** 15 + 273 = 288 K [1].
**(b)** 450 − 273 = 177 °C [1].

**3.** The Kelvin temperature is (directly) proportional to the average kinetic energy of the molecules [1].

**4.** It increases [1].

**5.** A fixed mass of gas [1] at constant volume [1].

**6. (a)** The molecules move randomly at high speed [1] and collide with the walls of the box [1]. Each collision exerts a force on the wall, and the total force per unit area is the pressure [1].
**(b)** Because the motion is random, the molecules hit every wall at the same rate on average [1].

**7. (a)** p₁V₁ = p₂V₂ [1], so p₂ = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 40 ÷ 16 [1] = 2.5 × 10⁵ Pa [1].
**(b)** The molecules have the same average speed but less space to move in [1], so they hit the walls more often, giving a greater force per unit area [1].
**(c)** So that the temperature of the air stays constant (pushing quickly would do work on the gas and warm it) [1].

**8. (a)** T₁ = 20 + 273 = 293 K [1]. p₁/T₁ = p₂/T₂, so T₂ = T₁ × p₂ ÷ p₁ = 293 × 1.0 × 10⁶ ÷ 8.0 × 10⁵ [1] = 366 K [1]. In °C: 366 − 273 = 93 °C [1].
**(b)** The molecules move faster on average [1], so they collide with the walls more often and each collision exerts a greater force [1].

**9. (a)** Rise in pressure per °C = (127.0 − 99.0) ÷ (90 − 10) = 28.0 ÷ 80 [1] = 0.35 kPa per °C [1]. Cooling needed from 10 °C to reach zero pressure = 99.0 ÷ 0.35 = 283 °C [1]. Absolute zero ≈ 10 − 283 = −273 °C [1].
**(b)** At absolute zero the molecules have the minimum possible kinetic energy, so no more energy can be removed (the pressure would be zero) [1].

**10. (a)** Average kinetic energy is proportional to Kelvin temperature, so it doubles at 600 K [1]; 600 − 273 = 327 °C [1].
**(b)** 150 K [1].

**11.** Pressure at the bottom = 1.0 × 10⁵ + 15 × 1000 × 10 [1] = 2.5 × 10⁵ Pa [1]. p₁V₁ = p₂V₂, so V₂ = 2.5 × 10⁵ × 1.5 ÷ 1.0 × 10⁵ [1] = 3.75 cm³ (3.8 cm³) [1].

**12.** The statement is wrong, because the gas law needs Kelvin temperatures: 50 °C = 323 K and 100 °C = 373 K [1]. The pressure increases by a factor of 373 ÷ 323 = 1.15 [1], so it rises by only about 15%, not 100%; to double the pressure the Kelvin temperature would have to double, to 646 K (373 °C) [1].

## Frequently asked questions

**When do I add atmospheric pressure in a gas-law question?**
When the gas is under a liquid, as in Question 11. The gas law needs the total pressure on the gas, which is the
atmospheric pressure plus h × ρ × g.
