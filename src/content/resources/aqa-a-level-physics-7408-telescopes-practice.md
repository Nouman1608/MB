These questions cover sub-section 3.9.1 Telescopes of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408, AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards), part of the Astrophysics option (A-level only, Paper 3 Section B).

Revise from the [telescopes study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-telescopes/) and [telescopes revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-telescopes-revision-notes/) first. ## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State what is meant by normal adjustment for an astronomical refracting telescope, and define angular magnification. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why a telescope with a large objective lens produces coloured fringes around images of stars. **[2]**

**3.** Give two reasons why the largest optical telescopes are reflectors rather than refractors. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why infrared telescopes are built on high mountains or in space, and why their detectors are cooled. **[2]**

**5.** State the Rayleigh criterion for two point sources to be just resolved. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** A refracting telescope has an objective of focal length 1.5 m and an eyepiece of focal length 50 mm, used in normal adjustment.

**(a)** Calculate the angular magnification. **[1]**
**(b)** The Moon subtends 9.1 × 10⁻³ rad at the unaided eye. Calculate the angle subtended by its final image. **[2]**
**(c)** State where the intermediate image forms and describe how the rays leave the eyepiece. **[2]**

**7.** An amateur wants a telescope with angular magnification 60 using a 25 mm eyepiece.

**(a)** Calculate the focal length of the objective needed and the length of the telescope in normal adjustment. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the angular magnification if a 10 mm eyepiece is used with the same objective. **[1]**

**8.** Two stars of a binary system have an angular separation of 1.2 × 10⁻⁶ rad.

**(a)** Calculate the minimum diameter of telescope that could resolve them at 600 nm. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times more collecting power this telescope has than a 0.10 m telescope. **[1]**

**9.** A radio telescope of diameter 100 m observes at 21 cm. An optical telescope of diameter 4.0 m observes at 500 nm.

**(a)** Calculate the minimum angular resolution of each. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the ratio of the collecting power of the radio dish to that of the optical telescope. **[1]**
**(c)** Give two differences between how the two telescopes are positioned or used. **[2]**

**10.** Two car headlights are 1.5 m apart. Take the diameter of the pupil of the eye as 5.0 mm and the wavelength as 500 nm. Calculate the maximum distance at which the eye could resolve the headlights as two sources. **[3]**

**11.** Telescope A has a mirror of diameter 2.0 m and telescope B has a diameter of 0.10 m. Telescope A records an image of a faint galaxy in 30 s. Calculate the exposure time telescope B would need to collect the same energy, and state one assumption. **[2]**

**12.** Compare the eye and a CCD as detectors for astronomy in terms of quantum efficiency, resolution and convenience of use. **[3]**

**13.** An X-ray telescope has an effective aperture of 1.2 m and detects X-rays of wavelength 1.0 nm.

**(a)** Calculate its minimum angular resolution. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the mirrors of X-ray telescopes are arranged so that X-rays strike them at grazing incidence. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Normal adjustment: the principal foci of the objective and eyepiece coincide, so the final image is at infinity [1]. Angular magnification = angle subtended by the image at the eye ÷ angle subtended by the object at the unaided eye [1].

**2.** The refractive index of glass depends on wavelength, so blue light is refracted more than red [1]; different colours focus at different points, giving coloured fringes (chromatic aberration) [1].

**3.** Any two, one mark each: mirrors have no chromatic aberration; a mirror can be supported across its back so it can be made very large without sagging; only one surface needs to be perfect, whereas a lens must be flawless throughout; mirrors do not absorb light passing through glass [2].

**4.** Water vapour in the atmosphere absorbs infrared, so high dry sites or space are needed [1]; the telescope and its surroundings emit infrared themselves, so detectors are cooled to stop this swamping the signal [1].

**5.** Two sources are just resolved when the central maximum of the diffraction pattern of one [1] falls on the first minimum of the other [1].

**6. (a)** M = 1.5/0.050 = **30** [1]
**(b)** β = Mα = 30 × 9.1 × 10⁻³ [1] = **0.27 rad** [1]
**(c)** In the focal plane of the objective, which is also the focal plane of the eyepiece (real and inverted) [1]; the rays from each point leave the eyepiece parallel to each other, so the final image is at infinity [1].

**7. (a)** fₒ = Mfₑ = 60 × 0.025 = **1.5 m** [1]; length = 1.5 + 0.025 = **1.525 m** [1]
**(b)** M = 1.5/0.010 = **150** [1]

**8. (a)** D = λ/θ = 600 × 10⁻⁹/1.2 × 10⁻⁶ [1] = **0.50 m** [1]
**(b)** (0.50/0.10)² = **25** [1]

**9. (a)** Radio: θ ≈ 0.21/100 = **2.1 × 10⁻³ rad** [1]; optical: θ ≈ 500 × 10⁻⁹/4.0 = **1.25 × 10⁻⁷ rad** [1]
**(b)** (100/4.0)² = **625** [1]
**(c)** Any two, one mark each: the radio telescope works in daylight and through cloud; the optical telescope needs clear, dark skies; the radio telescope is affected by man-made radio interference so is sited away from it; the optical telescope is placed at high altitude away from light pollution [2].
*Examiner insight:* The radio dish is 25 times wider but its resolution is about 17 000 times worse, because the wavelength is 420 000 times longer.

**10.** θ ≈ λ/D = 500 × 10⁻⁹/5.0 × 10⁻³ = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ rad [1]; separation ≈ θ × distance, so distance = 1.5/1.0 × 10⁻⁴ [1] = **1.5 × 10⁴ m** [1]

**11.** Collecting power ratio = (2.0/0.10)² = 400, so t = 400 × 30 = **1.2 × 10⁴ s** [1]; assumption: both detectors have the same quantum efficiency (or there are no other losses) [1].

**12.** Quantum efficiency: the CCD detects a much larger fraction of incident photons than the eye [1]; resolution: set by pixel spacing in a CCD, which can be finer than the spacing of the eye's receptor cells [1]; convenience: a CCD can integrate light over long exposures and stores a digital image for processing, whereas the eye only views in real time [1].

**13. (a)** θ ≈ 1.0 × 10⁻⁹/1.2 = **8.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ rad** [1]
**(b)** X-rays striking a mirror at large angles pass into it or are absorbed; they reflect only when they strike at very shallow (grazing) angles [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving the eyepiece focal length in mm.
- Using D instead of D² for collecting power.
- Comparing only one detector in "compare" answers.
- Giving only "the atmosphere absorbs it" without naming what absorbs infrared.
- Forgetting to multiply the angle by the distance to get a size.
