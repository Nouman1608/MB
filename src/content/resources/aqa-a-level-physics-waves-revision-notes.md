## Equations for this topic

| Equation | Symbols | Use |
|---|---|---|
| c = fλ | c speed, f frequency, λ wavelength | Any progressive wave |
| f = 1/T | T period | Period from frequency |
| phase difference = 2πx/λ rad | x separation along the wave | Two points on one wave |
| f = (1/2l) √(T/μ) | l length, T tension, μ mass per unit length | First harmonic on a string |
| w = λD/s | w fringe spacing, D slit–screen distance, s slit separation | Young's double slits |
| d sin θ = nλ | d slit spacing, n order | Grating at normal incidence |
| n = c/c_s | c_s speed in the substance | Refractive index |
| n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂ | angles from the normal | Snell's law |
| sin θc = n₂/n₁ | n₁ > n₂ | Critical angle |

Recall: n for air ≈ 1. All EM waves travel at the same speed in a vacuum, 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹.

## 3.3.1.1 Progressive waves -- definitions

- **Amplitude**: maximum displacement from equilibrium.
- **Wavelength**: distance between adjacent points in phase.
- **Frequency**: oscillations per second. **Period**: time for one oscillation.
- **Phase difference**: how far one point is ahead of another in its cycle. Give it as a fraction of a cycle, in degrees, or in radians: ¼ cycle = 90° = π/2 rad; ½ cycle = 180° = π rad.

The particles oscillate about fixed positions; energy, not matter, moves along.

## 3.3.1.2 Longitudinal vs transverse

| | Transverse | Longitudinal |
|---|---|---|
| Oscillation vs energy direction | Perpendicular | Parallel |
| Examples | Waves on a string, all EM waves | Sound |
| Can be polarised? | Yes | No |

**Polarisation** confines oscillations to one plane. It is **evidence** that a wave is transverse.

- Polaroid filters: crossed at 90° they block light; sunglasses reduce glare from reflected light.
- Aerials: a receiving aerial must line up with the plane of polarisation of the transmitted signal.
- Malus's law is not required.

## 3.3.1.3 Stationary waves

**Formation (method in steps)**

1. Two progressive waves of the same frequency travel in opposite directions (often a wave and its reflection).
2. They superpose: resultant displacement = sum of the two displacements.
3. Where they are always in antiphase: **node**, zero amplitude.
4. Where they are always in phase: **antinode**, maximum amplitude.
5. Draw the two waves a quarter period apart and add them to show this graphically.

**Must-know distinctions: progressive vs stationary**

| Feature | Progressive | Stationary |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | Transferred | Stored, not transferred |
| Amplitude | Same everywhere (no losses) | Zero at nodes, maximum at antinodes |
| Phase | Changes continuously along the wave | Same within a loop; antiphase in adjacent loops |
| Spacing | Crest to crest = λ | Node to node = λ/2 |

**Harmonics on a string fixed at both ends**

| Harmonic | Loops | Wavelength | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | 1 | 2l | f₁ |
| 2nd | 2 | l | 2f₁ |
| nth | n | 2l/n | nf₁ |

AQA uses "first harmonic", not "fundamental", and does not use "overtone".

**Proportionalities from f = (1/2l) √(T/μ)**: f ∝ 1/l, f ∝ √T, f ∝ 1/√μ. Quadruple T and f doubles; quadruple μ and f halves.

**Microwaves and sound**: a reflector (metal plate, hard wall) sets up a stationary wave; a probe or microphone finds nodes λ/2 apart.

**Worked reminder**: a probe moves 6.0 cm from the 1st node to the 4th node. That is 3 spacings, so 3 × λ/2 = 6.0 cm and λ = 4.0 cm. Then f = c/λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ / 0.040 = 7.5 × 10⁹ Hz. Count the gaps, not the nodes.

**Required practical 1**: frequency of stationary waves on a string versus length, tension (hanging masses, T = mg) and mass per unit length. Change one variable at a time; plot f against 1/l or √T.

## 3.3.2.1 Interference -- method in steps

1. Check the sources are **coherent**: same frequency, constant phase difference.
2. Find the **path difference** to the point.
3. Path difference = nλ → constructive (maximum). Path difference = (n + ½)λ → destructive (minimum), for sources in phase.

**Young's double slits**: w = λD/s. Measure across several fringes and divide.

**White-light fringes**: central white fringe; others are coloured, blue nearer the centre.

**Laser safety**: do not look along the beam or at reflections; remove shiny surfaces; keep the beam below eye level.

**Sound and microwaves**: two loudspeakers on one signal generator, or microwaves through two gaps, give maxima and minima explained by path difference.

**History of ideas**: Newton's corpuscular theory was long preferred to Huygens' wave theory; Young's interference fringes supported the wave theory; the photoelectric effect later showed a particle nature (wave–particle duality).

**Required practical 2**: interference with Young's slits and with a diffraction grating to find λ.

## 3.3.2.2 Diffraction

**Single slit**: wide, bright central maximum (twice the width of the others), dimmer side maxima. With white light: white centre, side maxima are spectra.

Central maximum **wider** if λ increases or slit width decreases.

**Grating**: path difference between adjacent slits = d sin θ, so d sin θ = nλ for maxima. To derive it, draw the right-angled triangle between two adjacent slits and the direction θ, and state that a maximum needs the path difference to be a whole number of wavelengths. d = 1/(lines per metre). Highest order = largest whole number ≤ d/λ (round **down**). Uses: spectra of stars and gases, measuring wavelengths.

## 3.3.2.3 Refraction and fibres

- Light bends **towards** the normal entering a medium of higher n, and slows down.
- TIR needs **n₁ > n₂** and angle of incidence **> θc**.
- **Cladding**: lower n than the core (allows TIR), protects the core from scratches, stops crossover between fibres.
- **Absorption**: amplitude falls along the fibre.
- **Pulse broadening**: pulses lengthen and may overlap.
  - **Material dispersion**: different wavelengths, different speeds. Fix: monochromatic source.
  - **Modal dispersion**: different paths, different distances. Fix: very narrow (single-mode) core.
- Step-index fibres only.

## Quick self-test

1. A wave has period 0.020 s. What is its frequency?
2. Find the wavelength of a 98 MHz radio wave in a vacuum.
3. Two points on a wave are λ/3 apart. Give the phase difference in degrees and radians.
4. Which of these can be polarised: sound, light, a wave on a string?
5. A stationary wave has wavelength 0.60 m. How far apart are adjacent nodes?
6. A string's first harmonic is 120 Hz. The tension is multiplied by 2.25. Find the new first harmonic.
7. Find the wavelength of the fifth harmonic on a string of length 1.20 m.
8. Light of wavelength 480 nm passes through slits 0.40 mm apart onto a screen 1.50 m away. Find w.
9. A grating has d = 1.6 µm. What is the highest order seen with 650 nm light?
10. Light enters water (n = 1.33) from air at 50°. Find the angle of refraction.
11. Find the critical angle for a glass–air boundary if the glass has n = 1.60.
12. Why must the cladding have a lower refractive index than the core?

### Answers

1. f = 1/0.020 = **50 Hz**.
2. λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ / 98 × 10⁶ = **3.06 m**.
3. ⅓ cycle = **120°** = **2π/3 rad** (2.09 rad).
4. **Light** and **a wave on a string** (transverse). Sound is longitudinal.
5. λ/2 = **0.30 m**.
6. f ∝ √T, √2.25 = 1.5, so f = **180 Hz**.
7. λ = 2l/5 = 2.40/5 = **0.48 m**.
8. w = (480 × 10⁻⁹ × 1.50)/(0.40 × 10⁻³) = **1.8 × 10⁻³ m** (1.8 mm).
9. d/λ = 1.6 × 10⁻⁶ / 650 × 10⁻⁹ = 2.46, so **n = 2**.
10. sin θ₂ = sin 50°/1.33 = 0.576, θ₂ = **35.2°**.
11. sin θc = 1/1.60 = 0.625, θc = **38.7°**.
12. Total internal reflection only happens when light meets a boundary with a medium of **lower** refractive index.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking node-to-node distance as λ rather than λ/2, or counting nodes instead of the gaps between them.
- Giving phase difference without saying whether it is in degrees or radians.
- Describing a stationary wave without stating that the two waves have the same frequency and travel in opposite directions.
- Saying polarisation "proves light is a wave": it is evidence that light is **transverse**.
- Leaving slit separation in mm or wavelength in nm in w = λD/s.
- Using lines per mm as d, or rounding the highest grating order up.
- Defining coherence as "same frequency" only, missing the constant phase difference.
- Mixing up material dispersion (wavelengths) and modal dispersion (paths), or giving the wrong remedy for each.
- Writing sin θc = n₁/n₂, which gives a sine bigger than 1.
