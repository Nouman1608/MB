## Questions

**1.** Differentiate with respect to x.
**(a)** y = 6e^(x/2) + 2 sin 3x **[2]**
**(b)** y = 5 ln x − tan 2x **[2]**

**2.** Differentiate with respect to x.
**(a)** y = (5x² − 2)⁴ **[2]**
**(b)** y = e^(sin x) **[2]**

**3.** A curve has equation y = x⁴ cos 2x.
**(a)** Find dy/dx. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the exact gradient of the curve where x = π/4. **[2]**

**4.** Show that the gradient of y = (3x + 1)/(2x − 5) is negative at every point where x ≠ 5/2. **[4]**

**5.** Given y = (1 − cos x)/sin x, show that dy/dx = 1/(1 + cos x). **[5]**

**6.** A curve has equation x = y³ + 4y. Find dy/dx at the point (5, 1). **[3]**

**7.** A curve has equation x = 3y² − 12y + 5.
**(a)** Find dy/dx in terms of y. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of the point on the curve where dy/dx = 1/6. **[2]**

**8.** A curve has equation y = x³ sin 2x.
**(a)** Find dy/dx. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the exact value of dy/dx when x = π/4. **[2]**

**9.** The function f is defined by f(x) = e^(2x)/x for x > 0.
**(a)** Show that f′(x) = e^(2x)(2x − 1)/x². **[4]**
**(b)** Find the exact coordinates of the point where the gradient of the curve y = f(x) is zero. **[2]**
**(c)** Write down the exact value of f′(1). **[1]**

**10.** A flywheel has speed v metres per second t seconds after its power is cut, where v = 12e^(−0.25t).
**(a)** Find dv/dt. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the value of dv/dt when t = 3. Give your answer to 3 significant figures and include its units. **[2]**
**(c)** The speed is falling at a rate of 0.5 metres per second per second at time T. Find T to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**11.** A curve has equation y = e^x cos 2x.
**(a)** Show that dy/dx = e^x(cos 2x − 2 sin 2x). **[3]**
**(b)** Find the smallest positive value of x at which the gradient is zero. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The derivative of 6e^(x/2) is 6 × (1/2)e^(x/2) = 3e^(x/2) [1]. The derivative of 2 sin 3x is 6 cos 3x [1]. dy/dx = **3e^(x/2) + 6 cos 3x**.
**(b)** The derivative of 5 ln x is 5/x [1]. The derivative of tan 2x is 2 sec²2x [1]. dy/dx = **5/x − 2 sec²(2x)**. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the factor from the inside of each function, 1/2 and 2 here, is the usual place to lose credit, so write it in as you differentiate.

**2. (a)** Let u = 5x² − 2, so y = u⁴ and du/dx = 10x [1]. dy/dx = 4u³ × 10x = **40x(5x² − 2)³** [1].
**(b)** Let u = sin x, so y = e^u and du/dx = cos x [1]. dy/dx = **cos x · e^(sin x)** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* naming the inner function u makes the chain rule visible, and it keeps your working easy to follow if a later slip occurs.

**3. (a)** Let u = x⁴ and v = cos 2x, so u′ = 4x³ [1] and v′ = −2 sin 2x [1]. dy/dx = x⁴(−2 sin 2x) + cos 2x (4x³) = **4x³ cos 2x − 2x⁴ sin 2x** [1].
**(b)** At x = π/4, 2x = π/2, so cos 2x = 0 and sin 2x = 1 [1]. The gradient is −2(π/4)⁴ = −2π⁴/256 = **−π⁴/128** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* when the question says exact, evaluate cos(π/2) and sin(π/2) as 0 and 1 and leave π in the answer. A decimal approximation would not answer it.

**4.** Let u = 3x + 1 and v = 2x − 5, so u′ = 3 and v′ = 2 [1]. dy/dx = (3(2x − 5) − 2(3x + 1))/(2x − 5)² [1]. The numerator is 6x − 15 − 6x − 2 = −17, so dy/dx = **−17/(2x − 5)²** [1]. A square is positive for x ≠ 5/2, so dividing −17 by it always gives a negative value, which shows the gradient is negative everywhere [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* in a "show that" question, give the reason as well as the algebra. A sentence about the sign of a square is part of the argument.

**5.** Let u = 1 − cos x and v = sin x, so u′ = sin x and v′ = cos x [1]. dy/dx = (sin x · sin x − (1 − cos x) cos x)/sin²x [1]. The numerator is sin²x − cos x + cos²x = 1 − cos x [1]. Using sin²x = 1 − cos²x = (1 − cos x)(1 + cos x) [1], dy/dx = (1 − cos x)/((1 − cos x)(1 + cos x)) = **1/(1 + cos x)** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* the given answer means every line must lead to it. State which identity you use, and do not skip the cancelling step.

**6.** dx/dy = 3y² + 4 [1]. At y = 1, dx/dy = 7 [1]. dy/dx = 1/(dx/dy) = **1/7** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* it is easy to stop at dx/dy = 7. The question asks for dy/dx, so show the reciprocal as a separate final line.

**7. (a)** dx/dy = 6y − 12 [1]. dy/dx = **1/(6y − 12)** [1].
**(b)** Setting 1/(6y − 12) = 1/6 gives 6y − 12 = 6, so y = 3 [1]. Then x = 3(9) − 12(3) + 5 = −4, giving **(−4, 3)** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* give coordinates when coordinates are asked for. The y-value found from the derivative is only half of the answer.

**8. (a)** Let u = x³ and v = sin 2x, so u′ = 3x² [1] and v′ = 2 cos 2x [1]. dy/dx = x³(2 cos 2x) + sin 2x (3x²) = **2x³ cos 2x + 3x² sin 2x** [1].
**(b)** At x = π/4, cos 2x = 0 and sin 2x = 1 [1]. The gradient is 3(π/4)² = **3π²/16** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* this question combines the product rule with a chain-rule step in v′. Differentiate sin 2x on its own line first, so the factor 2 is not lost.

**9. (a)** Let u = e^(2x) and v = x, so u′ = 2e^(2x) and v′ = 1 [1]. f′(x) = (x · 2e^(2x) − e^(2x) · 1)/x² [1]. The numerator is 2x e^(2x) − e^(2x) [1] = e^(2x)(2x − 1), so f′(x) = **e^(2x)(2x − 1)/x²** [1].
**(b)** e^(2x) is never zero, so 2x − 1 = 0 and x = 1/2 [1]. f(1/2) = e/(1/2) = 2e, so the point is **(1/2, 2e)** [1].
**(c)** f′(1) = e²(1)/1 = **e²** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* once the derivative is factorised, part (b) takes one line. If you leave it as two separate terms, finding where it equals zero is much harder.

**10. (a)** The chain rule gives a factor −0.25 for the exponent [1]. dv/dt = 12(−0.25)e^(−0.25t) = **−3e^(−0.25t)** [1].
**(b)** At t = 3, dv/dt = −3e^(−0.75) [1] = **−1.42 m s⁻²** (3 s.f.) [1].
**(c)** A fall of 0.5 means dv/dt = −0.5, so −3e^(−0.25T) = −0.5 [1]. Then e^(−0.25T) = 1/6 and −0.25T = −ln 6 [1]. T = 4 ln 6 = **7.17 seconds** (3 s.f.) [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* the negative sign carries the meaning that the speed falls. State the unit in part (b), and in part (c) turn "falling at 0.5" into dv/dt = −0.5 before solving.

**11. (a)** Let u = e^x and v = cos 2x, so u′ = e^x and v′ = −2 sin 2x [1]. dy/dx = e^x(−2 sin 2x) + cos 2x · e^x [1] = **e^x(cos 2x − 2 sin 2x)** [1].
**(b)** The gradient is zero when cos 2x − 2 sin 2x = 0, since e^x is never zero [1]. So tan 2x = 1/2 [1]. The smallest positive solution has 2x = arctan(0.5) = 0.4636, so x = **0.232** (3 s.f.) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* divide by e^x by reasoning that it is never zero, not by cancelling silently. Radians matter here, since the answer depends on the arctangent in radians.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Omitting the factor from the inner function in e^(kx), sin(kx), cos(kx) or a bracket raised to a power.
- Writing sin x instead of −sin x as the derivative of cos x.
- Reversing the quotient-rule numerator, so that it reads u v′ − v u′.
- Stopping at dx/dy and forgetting to take the reciprocal.
- Approximating when the question asks for an exact value such as π⁴/128 or e².
- Expanding a product-rule answer instead of factorising, which makes later steps harder.
- Failing to explain the sign of a squared denominator in a "show that" question.
- Working in degrees when the derivative results require radians.
