These questions cover sub-topic 6.1, Earthquakes and volcanoes, of the 2027-2029 Cambridge O Level
Environmental Management (5014) syllabus. The contexts are a subduction coast, a city on a fault line and a
farming community on the slopes of an active volcano.

## Section A

**1.** Name the layer of the Earth that lies between the crust and the core. **[1]**

**2.** State the name of the scale used to measure earthquake magnitude in the syllabus. **[1]**

**3.** State what is meant by a *volcanic bomb*. **[1]**

**4.** Name **two** continents that lie entirely in the Southern Hemisphere. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** An oceanic plate is moving towards a continental plate along the west coast of a continent.
(a) Name this type of plate boundary. **[1]**
(b) Explain why the oceanic plate is subducted. **[2]**
(c) Explain why a line of volcanoes forms inland from the coast. **[2]**

**6.** Describe how convection currents in the mantle cause plates to move. **[3]**

**7.** Two earthquakes occur in different countries. Earthquake X has a magnitude of 6.0 and Earthquake Y has
a magnitude of 8.0.
(a) Calculate roughly how many times more energy Earthquake Y released than Earthquake X. **[1]**
(b) Earthquake X killed more people than Earthquake Y. Suggest **three** reasons for this. **[3]**

**8.** Describe **three** features of a volcanic eruption that are dangerous to people living on the slopes of
the volcano. **[3]**

**9.** Explain how a volcanic eruption can damage forests and lakes many kilometres away. **[2]**

**10.** A city of four million people lies on a conservative plate boundary.
(a) Describe **two** ways buildings in the city could be designed to reduce damage in an earthquake. **[2]**
(b) Explain how disaster preparation could reduce deaths. **[3]**
(c) Explain why fire is a risk after an earthquake in the city. **[2]**

**11.** Describe how land use zoning could reduce the impacts of a volcanic eruption. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why many farmers continue to live on the slopes of an active volcano. **[3]**

## Section C

**13.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of evacuation and international aid for managing the impacts of
a volcanic eruption. **[6]**

**14.** "Low-income countries suffer more from tectonic events than high-income countries." Discuss this
view. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The mantle. **[1]**

**2.** Moment Magnitude Scale. **[1]**

**3.** A lump of lava thrown out during an eruption that cools and hardens in the air before landing. **[1]**

**4.** Any two of: Antarctica; Oceania (Australia); South America is mostly but not entirely southern, so
not accepted. Accept Antarctica **[1]** and Oceania **[1]**.

**5.** (a) Convergent (destructive) boundary. **[1]**
(b) Oceanic crust is denser than continental crust **[1]**, so it sinks beneath the continental plate as the
plates are pushed together **[1]**.
(c) The subducted plate melts as it sinks into the hot mantle **[1]**; the magma rises through the
continental plate above, erupting at a line of volcanoes parallel to the boundary **[1]**.

**6.** Heat from the core warms rock at the base of the mantle, which becomes less dense and rises **[1]**;
it spreads out beneath the crust, cools and sinks again **[1]**; this circulation drags the plates above it,
moving them apart, together or past each other **[1]**.

**7.** (a) Two steps: 32 x 32 ≈ **1 000 times** (accept 900 to 1 100). **[1]**
(b) Any three: X had a shallower focus **[1]**; X's epicentre was closer to a densely populated area **[1]**;
buildings near X were poorly built **[1]**; the country hit by X had less disaster preparation, fewer rescue
teams or less aid **[1]**; X struck at a time when more people were indoors **[1]**. **[max 3]**

**8.** Any three: pyroclastic flows of hot gas, ash and rock move fast and burn or bury people **[1]**; volcanic
bombs can strike people and buildings **[1]**; lava flows destroy homes and farmland **[1]**; heavy ash
collapses roofs and causes breathing problems **[1]**; gases can be harmful to breathe **[1]**. **[max 3]**

**9.** Sulfur dioxide released in the eruption is carried by wind **[1]** and dissolves in water in the atmosphere
to form acid rain, which damages trees and acidifies lakes **[1]**. (Accept ash fall smothering vegetation as
an alternative second point.)

**10.** (a) Any two: flexible or steel frames that bend without breaking **[1]**; cross-bracing **[1]**; deep
or isolated foundations **[1]**; lightweight roofs or materials **[1]**. **[max 2]**
(b) Plans and drills teach people to take cover and where to go **[1]**; emergency supplies of food, water
and medical kits are ready **[1]**; trained emergency rescue teams can reach trapped people quickly **[1]**.
(c) Ground shaking breaks gas pipes and electricity cables **[1]**; leaking gas ignites, and damaged roads and
water pipes make fires hard to put out **[1]**.

**11.** Mapping the areas most at risk from lava, pyroclastic flows or ash **[1]** and banning or limiting
housing and key buildings there, using the land for low-risk uses such as grazing or parks **[1]**.

**12.** Weathered lava and ash make soils fertile, giving high crop yields **[1]**; eruptions are infrequent, so
farmers judge the long-term benefit greater than the risk **[1]**; other income comes from tourism or
minerals, or families have lived there for generations and have no other land **[1]**.

**13.** **[6]** One mark per point, up to six. Evacuation: moves people and livestock out of the danger zone
before pyroclastic flows or ash fall, saving lives **[1]**; volcanoes often give warning signs, so there is time
to evacuate **[1]**; but people may refuse to leave homes and animals **[1]**, and evacuation needs transport
and roads that may be blocked **[1]**. International aid: provides money, food, shelter and medical teams the
country lacks **[1]**; but it may arrive late, be poorly distributed or create dependence **[1]**. Conclusion:
evacuation saves more lives; aid mainly supports recovery **[1]**. **[max 6]**

**14.** **[6]** One mark per point, up to six. Agree: buildings are less likely to be earthquake-resistant
**[1]**; less money for monitoring and warning **[1]**; fewer rescue teams and hospitals, so more deaths after
the event **[1]**; recovery depends more on international aid **[1]**. Disagree: financial losses are often
greater in high-income countries because property and infrastructure are worth more **[1]**; the size of the
event, depth of focus and distance from cities also matter **[1]**. Conclusion: low-income countries usually
suffer more deaths and slower recovery, though not always greater financial losses **[1]**. **[max 6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a convergent boundary without the word subduction.
- Treating magnitude as linear (saying magnitude 8.0 is "a third bigger" than 6.0).
- Naming a strategy without saying how it reduces a specific impact.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know the Richter scale?**
The 2027-2029 syllabus names only the Moment Magnitude Scale for 6.1.5.

**Are tsunamis part of 6.1?**
Yes. Tsunamis are listed as one of the impacts of tectonic events in 6.1.7.
