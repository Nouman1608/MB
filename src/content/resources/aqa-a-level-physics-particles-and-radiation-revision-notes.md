## Key equations

| Equation | Use | Units |
|---|---|---|
| specific charge = Q/m | nuclei, ions, particles | C kg⁻¹ |
| E = hf = hc/λ | photon energy | J |
| hf = ϕ + Ek(max) | photoelectric effect | J (or eV on both sides) |
| eVs = Ek(max) | stopping potential | Vs in V |
| hf = E1 − E2 | photon emitted between levels | J |
| λ = h/mv | de Broglie wavelength | m |
| 1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J | energy conversion | -- |

Constants used here: h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹, e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, mₑ = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg.

## 3.2.1.1 The atom in numbers

| | Charge / C | Rel. charge | Mass / kg | Rel. mass |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| p | +1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ | +1 | 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ | 1 |
| n | 0 | 0 | 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ | 1 |
| e⁻ | −1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ | −1 | 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ | ≈ 0.0005 |

- Z = protons; A = nucleons; neutrons = A − Z. Nuclide notation: ᴬ_Z X.
- Isotopes: same Z, different number of neutrons.
- **Specific charge = charge/mass** (C kg⁻¹). For an ion use the net charge (number of electrons gained or lost × e) and mass ≈ A × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.

## 3.2.1.2 Nuclear stability

- Strong nuclear force: attractive to about **3 fm**, repulsive below about **0.5 fm**, acts between nucleons.
- Alpha: ᴬ_Z X → ᴬ⁻⁴_(Z−2) Y + ⁴₂α.
- β⁻: ᴬ_Z X → ᴬ_(Z+1) Y + ⁰₋₁β + ν̄ₑ.
- Neutrino proposed because beta particles have a **range** of energies: energy conservation needed another particle.

## 3.2.1.3 Antiparticles and photons

| Particle | Antiparticle | Rest energy / MeV |
|---|---|---|
| electron | positron | 0.511 |
| proton | antiproton | 938.3 |
| neutron | antineutron | 939.6 |
| neutrino | antineutrino | -- |

Same mass and rest energy; opposite charge.

- **E = hf = hc/λ**.
- **Annihilation**: particle + antiparticle → **two** photons (momentum). E_photon(min) = rest energy of one particle.
- **Pair production**: one photon → particle + antiparticle. E_photon(min) = 2 × rest energy.

*Worked reminder.* Minimum photon energy to make a neutron-antineutron pair = 2 × 939.6 = **1879.2 MeV**. Any extra energy becomes kinetic energy of the pair.

## 3.2.1.4 Exchange particles

| Force | Exchange particle | Acts on |
|---|---|---|
| Electromagnetic | virtual photon | charged particles |
| Weak | W⁺, W⁻ | all particles |
| Strong (between nucleons) | pion | hadrons |

**Method: drawing a weak-interaction diagram**
1. Write the equation. Put the incoming particles at the bottom.
2. Find which particle changes identity (for example p → n).
3. Choose W⁺ or W⁻ so charge balances at that vertex.
4. At the other vertex, the W turns into (or meets) the leptons; check charge again.

Covered: β⁻, β⁺, electron capture (p + e⁻ → n + νₑ), electron-proton collision (e⁻ + p → n + νₑ). Gluon, Z⁰ and graviton are not tested.

## 3.2.1.5–3.2.1.6 Classification and quarks

| Quark | Q | B | S |
|---|---|---|---|
| u | +⅔ | +⅓ | 0 |
| d | −⅓ | +⅓ | 0 |
| s | −⅓ | +⅓ | −1 |

Antiquarks: reverse every sign.

| Family | Members | B | Quarks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baryons | p (uud), n (udd) | +1 | qqq |
| Antibaryons | p̄ (ūūd̄), n̄ (ūd̄d̄) | −1 | q̄q̄q̄ |
| Mesons | π⁺ ud̄, π⁻ dū, π⁰ uū/dd̄, K⁺ us̄, K⁻ sū, K⁰ ds̄, K̄⁰ sd̄ | 0 | qq̄ |
| Leptons | e⁻, μ⁻, νₑ, ν_μ (+ antiparticles) | 0 | none |

- Proton: the only stable baryon. Kaon: decays into pions. Muon: decays into an electron.
- Lepton numbers: Lₑ = +1 for e⁻ and νₑ, −1 for e⁺ and ν̄ₑ; L_μ = +1 for μ⁻ and ν_μ, −1 for μ⁺ and ν̄_μ.
- Strange particles: made in pairs by the strong interaction, decay by the weak interaction.
- Large international teams validate new particle results.

## 3.2.1.7 Conservation checklist

| Quantity | Strong | Weak |
|---|---|---|
| Charge Q | conserved | conserved |
| Baryon number B | conserved | conserved |
| Lₑ and L_μ (separately) | conserved | conserved |
| Strangeness S | conserved | can change by 0, ±1 |
| Energy, momentum | conserved | conserved |

- β⁻: **d → u**. β⁺: **u → d**. Neutron decay: n → p + e⁻ + ν̄ₑ.
- Data will be given for particles outside the specified list.

**Method: is this interaction possible?**
1. Write Q, B, Lₑ, L_μ and S under every particle, on both sides.
2. Add each row. Any mismatch in Q, B, Lₑ or L_μ: impossible.
3. If only S changes, by 1: possible by the weak interaction only.
4. If S changes by 2 or more: impossible.

*Worked reminder.* K⁺ → π⁺ + π⁰. Q: +1 → +1 + 0. B: 0 → 0. S: +1 → 0 + 0, a change of 1. Allowed, **weak** decay -- as expected, since strange particles decay by the weak interaction.

## 3.2.2.1 Photoelectric effect

- **hf = ϕ + Ek(max)**; threshold **f₀ = ϕ/h**; stopping potential **eVs = Ek(max)**.
- One photon frees at most one electron. Below f₀: no emission at any intensity.
- More intensity → more photoelectrons per second; same Ek(max).
- Higher frequency → larger Ek(max).

*Worked reminder.* Radiation of 1.5 × 10¹⁵ Hz on a metal with ϕ = 3.6 eV: hf = 9.95 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = 6.22 eV, so Ek(max) = 6.22 − 3.6 = **2.62 eV** and Vs = **2.62 V**. Working in eV throughout avoids converting back and forth.

## 3.2.2.2 Collisions of electrons with atoms

- **1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.** eV → J: multiply. J → eV: divide.
- Excitation: electron moves up a level. Ionisation: electron removed.
- An incoming electron can give part of its energy; a photon is absorbed only if its energy exactly matches a gap (or exceeds the ionisation energy).
- Fluorescent tube: electrons excite mercury atoms → UV photons → phosphor absorbs UV → emits visible light.

## 3.2.2.3 Energy levels

- Line spectra → discrete energy levels.
- **hf = E1 − E2** (levels in J or eV; convert to J before using h).
- n levels give at most n(n − 1)/2 different emission lines.

## 3.2.2.4 Wave-particle duality

- Photoelectric effect → light behaves as particles. Electron diffraction → particles behave as waves.
- **λ = h/mv**. Greater momentum → shorter λ → less diffraction (smaller rings).

## Must-know distinctions

- **Hadron vs lepton**: hadrons feel the strong interaction; leptons do not.
- **Baryon vs meson**: three quarks (B = ±1) vs quark-antiquark (B = 0).
- **Annihilation vs pair production**: two photons out, minimum energy each = one rest energy; versus one photon in, minimum energy = two rest energies.
- **Excitation vs ionisation**: moving up a level vs leaving the atom.
- **Intensity vs frequency**: intensity sets the number of photoelectrons; frequency sets Ek(max).

## Quick self-test

1. Find the energy of a photon of frequency 5.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz, in J and in eV.
2. Convert 2.4 eV to joules.
3. A metal has work function 2.3 eV. Find its threshold frequency.
4. State the minimum photon energy, in MeV, to produce an electron-positron pair.
5. Find the de Broglie wavelength of a 0.20 kg ball moving at 30 m s⁻¹.
6. Calculate the specific charge of an alpha particle.
7. Find the wavelength emitted when hydrogen falls from −1.51 eV to −13.6 eV.
8. Find the de Broglie wavelength of an electron moving at 2.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹.
9. Give the quark make-up and strangeness of a K⁻.
10. Is p → n + e⁺ + ν̄ₑ allowed? Give a reason.
11. Which exchange particle is involved in electron capture?

### Answers

1. E = hf = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 5.0 × 10¹⁴ = **3.3 × 10⁻¹⁹ J**, which is **2.1 eV**.
2. 2.4 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **3.84 × 10⁻¹⁹ J**.
3. f₀ = (2.3 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)/6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ = **5.6 × 10¹⁴ Hz**.
4. 2 × 0.511 = **1.022 MeV**.
5. λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴/(0.20 × 30) = **1.1 × 10⁻³⁴ m** -- far too small to diffract.
6. (2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)/(4 × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷) = **4.79 × 10⁷ C kg⁻¹**.
7. ΔE = 12.09 eV = 1.934 × 10⁻¹⁸ J; λ = hc/ΔE = **1.03 × 10⁻⁷ m** (ultraviolet).
8. λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴/(9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 2.0 × 10⁶) = **3.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ m**.
9. **sū**, S = **−1**.
10. **No**: Lₑ goes from 0 to −2 (positron −1, antineutrino −1). It should be a neutrino.
11. **W⁺** (from proton to electron), or equivalently W⁻ from electron to proton.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a neutrino in β⁻ decay or an antineutrino in β⁺ decay.
- Saying annihilation makes one photon; momentum needs two.
- Using the pion as the exchange particle for the weak interaction.
- Checking strangeness for a weak decay and calling it forbidden.
- Saying higher intensity gives faster photoelectrons.
- Leaving energy in eV when substituting into λ = hc/E.
- Calling a meson a baryon because it contains quarks; count the quarks.
- Giving the electron capture products as a neutron and an antineutrino.
- Forgetting the strong force is repulsive below about 0.5 fm when explaining why nuclei do not collapse.
