## Section A

**1.** A train sounds its horn as it approaches a person on a platform and then moves away. Describe how the pitch of the horn heard by the person changes, and explain why in terms of wavelength. **[3]**

**2.** State what is meant by the reference wavelength in the red-shift equation. **[1]**

**3.** State two pieces of evidence that support the Big Bang theory. **[2]**

**4.** A line in the spectrum of a galaxy has a reference wavelength of 500 nm and is observed at 512 nm. Calculate the velocity of the galaxy. **[3]**

**5.** Describe the past evolution of the universe according to the Big Bang theory. **[4]**

## Section B

**6.** A galaxy is moving away from Earth at 4.5 × 10⁶ m/s.

**(a)** Calculate the ratio v / c. **[1]**

**(b)** A spectral line has a reference wavelength of 600 nm. Calculate the wavelength at which it is observed in light from this galaxy. **[2]**

**(c)** State how the observed frequency of this line compares with its frequency from a laboratory source. **[1]**

**7.** Astronomers measure the red-shift of the same spectral line (reference wavelength 400 nm) in three galaxies at different distances from Earth.

| Galaxy | Relative distance from Earth | Change in wavelength / nm |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1 | 2.0 |
| B | 2 | 4.0 |
| C | 3 | 6.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the velocity of galaxy C. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the relationship between the red-shift of a galaxy and its distance from Earth shown by the data. **[2]**

**8.** Explain why the red-shift of galaxies provides evidence that the universe is expanding. **[4]**

**9.** A student says: "Because all galaxies are moving away from us, Earth must be at the centre of the universe." Evaluate this statement. **[3]**

**10.** A line with a reference wavelength of 500 nm is observed at 499.5 nm in light from a nearby galaxy.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the galaxy. **[2]**

**(b)** State the direction in which the galaxy is moving relative to Earth, and give a reason. **[2]**

**11.** Describe what cosmic microwave background radiation is, and explain why its existence supports the Big Bang theory. **[4]**

**12.** Galaxy P shows a red-shift of 1.5% of the reference wavelength. Galaxy Q shows a red-shift of 4.5%.

**(a)** Deduce which galaxy is further from Earth. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate how many times faster Q is receding than P. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** As the train approaches, the pitch is higher than normal; as it moves away, the pitch is lower [1]. When it approaches, the sound waves are squashed together, so the wavelength is shorter and the frequency higher [1]; when it moves away, the waves are stretched, so the wavelength is longer and the frequency lower [1].

**2.** The wavelength of the spectral line measured from a source that is not moving relative to the observer (in a laboratory) [1].

**3.** Red-shift of light from distant galaxies [1]; cosmic microwave background (CMB) radiation [1].

**4.** Δλ = 512 − 500 = 12 nm [1]; v = c × Δλ / λ₀ = 3.0 × 10⁸ × 12 / 500 [1] = **7.2 × 10⁶ m/s** (away from Earth) [1].

**5.** Any four: the universe began in an extremely small, hot, dense state [1]; about 13.8 billion years ago [1]; it expanded [1] and cooled as it expanded [1]; atoms of light elements such as hydrogen and helium formed [1]; gravity pulled matter together to form stars and galaxies [1]; it is still expanding today [1]; maximum 4.

**6. (a)** v / c = 4.5 × 10⁶ / 3.0 × 10⁸ = **0.015** [1].
**(b)** Δλ = 0.015 × 600 = 9.0 nm [1]; observed wavelength = 600 + 9.0 = **609 nm** [1].
**(c)** The observed frequency is lower [1].

**7. (a)** v = c × Δλ / λ₀ = 3.0 × 10⁸ × 6.0 / 400 [1] = **4.5 × 10⁶ m/s** [1].
**(b)** The further away the galaxy, the greater the red-shift [1]; the red-shift is proportional to distance (doubling the distance doubles the change in wavelength) [1].

**8.** Light from (almost) all distant galaxies is red-shifted [1], so they are all moving away from us [1]. More distant galaxies show greater red-shift, so they are moving away faster [1]. This pattern is what is expected if space itself is expanding, carrying galaxies apart [1].

**9.** The statement is wrong [1]. Space itself is expanding, so every galaxy is moving away from every other galaxy [1]. An observer in any galaxy would see the same pattern of red-shifts, so the observation does not show that Earth is at the centre [1].

**10. (a)** Δλ = 0.5 nm; v = 3.0 × 10⁸ × 0.5 / 500 [1] = **3.0 × 10⁵ m/s** [1].
**(b)** Towards Earth [1], because the observed wavelength is shorter than the reference wavelength (blue-shift) [1].

**11.** CMB is microwave radiation detected coming from every direction in space [1], with almost the same intensity in all directions [1]. The Big Bang theory says the early universe was extremely hot and filled with high-energy radiation [1]; as the universe expanded and cooled, this radiation was stretched to longer wavelengths and is now detected as microwaves [1].

**12. (a)** Galaxy Q, because it has the greater red-shift [1].
**(b)** 4.5 / 1.5 = **3 times** faster [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the observed wavelength as the denominator in the red-shift equation.
- Giving Δλ when the question asks for the observed wavelength (Question 6).
- Describing only the wavelength change, or only the frequency change, in Doppler answers.
- Answering Question 8 with "galaxies are moving away" but not linking distance to speed.
- Describing the CMB without explaining why it supports the Big Bang (Question 11).
