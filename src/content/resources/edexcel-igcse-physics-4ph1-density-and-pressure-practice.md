## Section A

**1.** State the relationship between density, mass and volume. **[1]**

**2.** A force of 50 N acts on an area of 0.25 m². Calculate the pressure. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why a sharp knife cuts more easily than a blunt one when the same force is used. **[2]**

**4.** State how the pressure at a point in a gas at rest acts. **[1]**

**5.** Gold has a density of 19 300 kg/m³. Calculate the mass of a gold bar with a volume of 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ m³. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A student wants to find the density of a cooking liquid.

**(a)** Describe how she could measure the mass and the volume of a sample of the liquid. **[4]**
**(b)** An empty measuring cylinder has a mass of 95.0 g. With 80.0 cm³ of the liquid in it, the total mass is 191.0 g. Calculate the density of the liquid in kg/m³. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest one way to make her value more accurate. **[1]**

**7.** A rectangular block of wood measures 10.0 cm × 5.0 cm × 4.0 cm and has a mass of 140 g.

**(a)** Calculate the density of the wood in kg/m³. **[3]**
**(b)** A second block of the same wood has twice the volume. State its density. **[1]**

**8.** An elephant weighs 50 000 N and stands with its weight spread evenly over four feet, each with an area of 0.10 m². A person weighing 500 N stands for a moment with all her weight on one shoe heel of area 0.50 cm².

**(a)** Calculate the pressure under the elephant's feet. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the pressure under the heel. **[3]**
**(c)** Use your answers to explain why some floors can be damaged by narrow heels but not by an elephant. **[1]**

**9.** A water tank is filled to a depth of 2.5 m. The density of water is 1000 kg/m³.

**(a)** Calculate the pressure difference between the surface and the bottom of the tank. **[2]**
**(b)** A drain plug at the bottom has an area of 0.0020 m². Calculate the force the water exerts on the plug, due to the pressure difference. **[2]**
**(c)** The water is moved into a narrower tank and filled to the same depth. State what happens to the pressure difference at the bottom. **[1]**

**10.** Atmospheric pressure at the surface of a freshwater lake is 101 kPa. Calculate the depth at which the total pressure in the lake is three times atmospheric pressure. **[3]**

**11.** A student punches three small holes in a tall plastic bottle of water. Two holes are at the same height near the bottom, on opposite sides. The third is near the top.

**(a)** Describe what the student would see. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain these observations. **[2]**

**12.** A submarine hatch has an area of 0.60 m². The submarine is 80 m below the surface of the sea, where the density of sea water is 1030 kg/m³. Calculate the force on the hatch due to the pressure difference caused by the water. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** density = mass ÷ volume (ρ = m ÷ V) [1].

**2.** p = F ÷ A = 50 ÷ 0.25 [1] = 200 Pa [1].

**3.** A sharp knife has a much smaller area of contact with the food [1], so the same force produces a much larger pressure (p = F ÷ A) [1].

**4.** Equally in all directions [1].

**5.** m = ρ × V = 19 300 × 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ [1] = 0.386 kg (0.39 kg) [1].

**6. (a)** Place the empty measuring cylinder on a top-pan balance and record its mass (or zero the balance with it in place) [1]. Pour in some of the liquid and read the volume at the bottom of the meniscus [1], with the eye level with the liquid surface [1]. Record the total mass; mass of liquid = total mass − mass of empty cylinder [1].
**(b)** mass of liquid = 191.0 − 95.0 = 96.0 g [1]. ρ = 96.0 ÷ 80.0 = 1.20 g/cm³ [1] = 1200 kg/m³ [1].
**(c)** Any one: use a larger volume of liquid; repeat with different volumes and take a mean; use a measuring cylinder with finer scale divisions [1].

**7. (a)** V = 10.0 × 5.0 × 4.0 = 200 cm³ [1]. ρ = 140 ÷ 200 = 0.70 g/cm³ [1] = 700 kg/m³ [1].
**(b)** 700 kg/m³ (density does not depend on the size of the block) [1].

**8. (a)** Total area = 4 × 0.10 = 0.40 m² [1]. p = 50 000 ÷ 0.40 [1] = 125 000 Pa (1.25 × 10⁵ Pa) [1].
**(b)** A = 0.50 cm² = 0.50 × 10⁻⁴ = 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ m² [1]. p = 500 ÷ 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ [1] = 1.0 × 10⁷ Pa [1].
**(c)** The pressure under the heel is about 80 times greater than under the elephant's feet, even though the force is much smaller, so the heel can dent the floor [1].

**9. (a)** p = h × ρ × g = 2.5 × 1000 × 10 [1] = 25 000 Pa [1].
**(b)** F = p × A = 25 000 × 0.0020 [1] = 50 N [1].
**(c)** It stays the same, because pressure depends only on depth, density and g, not on the width of the tank [1].

**10.** Pressure difference needed = 3 × 101 − 101 = 202 kPa = 202 000 Pa [1]. h = p ÷ (ρ × g) = 202 000 ÷ (1000 × 10) [1] = 20.2 m (20 m) [1].

**11. (a)** Water spurts equally far from the two holes at the same height [1], and further than from the hole near the top [1].
**(b)** Pressure at a point acts equally in all directions, so holes at the same depth have the same pressure pushing water out sideways [1]. Pressure increases with depth, because there is more water above, so water leaves the lower holes faster than the upper one [1].

**12.** p = h × ρ × g = 80 × 1030 × 10 = 824 000 Pa [1]. F = p × A = 824 000 × 0.60 [1] = 494 400 N (4.9 × 10⁵ N) [1].

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do some questions say "due to the pressure difference"?**
Because h × ρ × g gives only the extra pressure from the water. The total pressure also includes the atmospheric
pressure on the surface; read each question to see which one it wants.
