Condensed notes for subtopic **1.11 Ratio and proportion** of Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024, 2025 to 2027. The [Ratio and proportion study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-ratio-and-proportion/) explains each method with worked examples; the [Ratio and proportion practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-ratio-and-proportion-practice/) give exam-style questions with mark schemes.

## Syllabus outcome

Understand and use ratio and proportion to give ratios in their simplest form, divide a quantity in a given ratio, and use proportional reasoning and ratios in context (adapting recipes, map scales, best value).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Ratio | comparison of quantities in the same units | 3 : 5 |
| Part | one unit of the ratio | 5 parts |
| Simplest form | whole numbers with no common factor | 12 : 18 = 2 : 3 |
| Equivalent ratios | same ratio, different numbers | 2 : 3 = 8 : 12 |
| Direct proportion | one quantity increases at the same rate as the other | cost and quantity |
| Scale | ratio of map length to real length | 1 : 25 000 |
| Unit price | cost per single unit | $0.36 per 100 g |

## Simplifying

| Type | Method | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Whole numbers | divide by HCF | 36 : 48 : 60 = 3 : 4 : 5 |
| Different units | same unit first | 1.2 m : 40 cm = 120 : 40 = 3 : 1 |
| Time | convert to minutes | 45 min : 1 h 30 min = 1 : 2 |
| Decimals | multiply by 10, 100 | 2.5 : 4 = 25 : 40 = 5 : 8 |
| Fractions | multiply by LCM of denominators | 3/4 : 5/6 = 9 : 10 |
| To 1 : n | divide by first term | 4 : 10 = 1 : 2.5 |

## Dividing a quantity in a ratio

1. Add the parts.
2. Divide the quantity by the total number of parts.
3. Multiply by each ratio number.
4. Check the total.

| Task | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| $360 in 5 : 3 : 4 | 360 ÷ 12 = 30 | $150, $90, $120 |
| 7.2 m in 5 : 4 | 7.2 ÷ 9 = 0.8 | 4 m, 3.2 m |
| 180° in 2 : 3 : 7 | 180 ÷ 12 = 15 | 30°, 45°, 105° |
| 45 kg in 2 : 7 | 45 ÷ 9 = 5 | 10 kg, 35 kg |

## Finding the whole from one part or a difference

| Information | Method | Example |
|---|---|---|
| One share known | find one part | boys : girls = 7 : 5, girls = 35, one part = 7, boys = 49 |
| Difference known | use difference in parts | 5 : 3 and difference 24, 2 parts = 24, so 12 each, shares 60 and 36 |
| Total known | add the parts first | see dividing above |

## Combining ratios

Make the shared term equal in both ratios.

```
a : b = 2 : 3 and b : c = 4 : 5
b to 12:   a : b = 8 : 12,   b : c = 12 : 15
a : b : c = 8 : 12 : 15
```

## Changing a quantity

```
red : blue = 3 : 5, total 40  →  red 15, blue 25
add 8 red  →  23 : 25
```

Work out the actual numbers first, make the change, then write the new ratio.

## Direct proportion: unitary method

| Step | Recipe example |
|---|---|
| Given | 4 people need 250 g flour |
| Find one unit | 250 ÷ 4 = 62.5 g per person |
| Scale | 10 people: 62.5 × 10 = 625 g |
| Scale factor check | 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5; 250 × 2.5 = 625 |

Round in a sensible direction if items cannot be split.

## Map scales

| Scale | Map | Real |
|---|---|---|
| 1 : 25 000 | 8.4 cm | 8.4 × 25 000 = 210 000 cm = 2.1 km |
| 1 : 25 000 | 14 cm | 350 000 cm = 3.5 km |
| 1 : 50 000 | 6 cm | 300 000 cm = 3 km |
| 1 : 200 | 4.5 cm | 900 cm = 9 m |

Unit conversions: 100 cm = 1 m; 100 000 cm = 1 km.

## Best value

Compare the cost per unit or the amount per unit of money.

| Pack | Price | Cost per 100 g |
|---|---|---|
| 750 g | $2.85 | $0.38 |
| 1.2 kg | $4.32 | $0.36 |
| 500 g | $1.95 | $0.39 |

The lowest cost per 100 g gives the best value. If comparing per dollar instead, the highest amount per dollar wins.

## Exam traps

- Order matters: 3 : 5 is not 5 : 3.
- Same units before simplifying.
- Do not divide the quantity by one ratio number; divide by the sum of the parts.
- A difference of 24 means the difference in parts equals 24.
- Check the shares add up to the whole.
- Express the final ratio with whole numbers.
- Do not use "part" numbers as final answers; multiply by the value of one part.
- Match units when finding best value.

## Self-test

1. Simplify 24 : 40.
2. Simplify 0.6 kg : 450 g.
3. Share $84 in the ratio 3 : 4.
4. Orange and lemon juice are mixed 2 : 5. How much orange is in 3.5 litres?
5. Girls : boys = 4 : 3, with 12 more girls than boys. How many students?
6. Write 1 : 1.5 as a ratio of whole numbers.
7. On a 1 : 50 000 map, two towns are 7 cm apart. Find the real distance in km.
8. Which is cheaper per litre: 3 L for $5.40 or 5 L for $8.75?

**Answers**

1. **3 : 5**.
2. 600 g : 450 g = **4 : 3**.
3. 84 ÷ 7 = 12, so **$36 and $48**.
4. 7 parts = 3.5 L, 1 part = 0.5 L, orange = 2 parts = **1 L**.
5. 1 part = 12, total parts 7, so **84 students**.
6. **2 : 3**.
7. 7 × 50 000 = 350 000 cm = **3.5 km**.
8. $1.80 per litre and $1.75 per litre, so the **5 L** pack is cheaper.
