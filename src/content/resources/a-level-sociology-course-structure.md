An overview of how AQA A Level Sociology (7192) is put together, for
students starting the course and for anyone deciding whether it is the
right specification for them. Understanding the structure early is
genuinely useful, since 7192's cross-cutting theory and methods content
means the way it is assessed shapes how it is best studied from the
very first weeks of the course.

## Three components

AQA structures A Level Sociology subject content into three components:

- **Education, with Theory and Methods** — how education systems work,
alongside core sociological theory and research methods, taught in this
context.
- **Topics in Sociology** — a set of options schools choose from, letting
teachers play to their strengths and giving students a varied, coherent
course rather than one fixed topic list. Confirm with your teacher which
specific options your centre has chosen, since this component's content
varies between schools far more than Education or Crime and Deviance,
which are fixed for every 7192 candidate.
- **Crime and Deviance, with Theory and Methods** — how societies define
and respond to crime and deviance, again taught alongside core theory and
methods.

Running theory and methods through more than one component, rather than
teaching them once in isolation, means research methods and sociological
theory come up repeatedly across the course rather than as a single
self-contained unit to revise separately. This spiral structure is a
deliberate curriculum design choice, intended to deepen a student's
grasp of theory and methods through repeated, varied application rather
than a single pass through the material early in the course.

## How it's assessed

Assessment uses a mix of question types rather than one single format:
short-answer questions, and extended writing and essay questions, testing
knowledge and understanding, application, and analysis and evaluation.
Because every component draws on this same mix of question types, exam
technique built practising one component's papers transfers directly to
the others, even where the substantive content is entirely different.
One distinctive AQA Sociology question type is the **"methods in
context"** question, which specifically tests your understanding of
research methods by applying them to a set topic — rewarding genuine
understanding of how sociological research is actually done, not just
memorised definitions. It appears within the Education component
specifically, so a candidate needs to be equally comfortable applying
research method knowledge to education-related scenarios as to the
purely theoretical strengths-and-limitations questions found elsewhere.

## AS and A Level together

AQA designs the AS (7191) and A Level (7192) specifications to be
**co-teachable within the first year of study** — schools running both
qualifications can teach a shared first year, and in principle a student
could move between the AS and A Level route during that first year. This
co-teachability is what gives some centres flexibility to make a final
AS-versus-A-Level decision later than at the very start of the course,
though the exact practical arrangements vary by school.
Confirm with your teacher which specific qualification you are being
entered for, since Marlbridge publishes study material against the full
A Level (7192).

## Study approach

Because theory and methods are taught inside both the Education and the
Crime and Deviance components rather than as one standalone block, the
highest-value habit to build early is linking every substantive topic
back to the sociological theories and research methods that explain it —
rather than revising "theory" and "topics" as separate piles of
information. Practising the methods-in-context question style specifically
is worth prioritising, since it rewards applying method knowledge to a
concrete scenario rather than reciting a definition — students often
under-prepare for this specific question style precisely because it
does not map neatly onto a single revision topic the way most other
questions do.

## Why theory and methods run through two components rather than one

AQA's design choice to embed theory and methods inside both Education and Crime and Deviance,
rather than examining them as a single standalone unit, has a direct practical consequence for
revision: a student cannot "finish" theory and methods once and move on, since it resurfaces in a
different substantive context each time. This is deliberate — sociological theory and research
methods are tools for understanding real social phenomena, not abstract content to be learned in
isolation, and AQA's assessment structure reflects that view directly. Building a single reference
sheet of the core theories (functionalism, Marxism, feminism, interactionism, postmodernism) and
core methods (their strengths and limitations) that gets applied afresh to each substantive topic,
rather than separate notes per component, mirrors how the exam itself actually tests the material.

## The methods-in-context question in more depth

The "methods in context" question is unusual among A Level Sociology question types because it
specifically requires combining two skills that are often taught somewhat separately: genuine
knowledge of a research method's practical strengths and limitations, and the ability to apply that
knowledge to the concrete practicalities of researching a *specific* topic (such as researching a
sensitive or hard-to-access group). A strong answer doesn't just restate generic method strengths —
it explains why a particular strength or weakness becomes especially significant, or is reduced, in
the context of the specific topic given in the question. Building a small bank of topic-and-method
combinations practised in advance (for example, using covert participant observation to research a
criminal subculture) is a more effective preparation strategy than only revising methods and topics
as separate lists.
