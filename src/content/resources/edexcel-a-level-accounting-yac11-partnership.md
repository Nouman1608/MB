This guide covers outcomes **1.3.9** (statements of profit or loss and appropriation accounts), **1.3.10** (partners' current accounts and capital accounts on a fixed or floating basis), **1.3.11** (use of the partnership agreement in appropriation of profit or loss), **1.3.12** (the provisions of Section 24 of the Partnership Act 1890) and **1.3.13** (introduction or retirement of a partner, including the introduction of assets and the treatment of goodwill). They sit in Topic 1.3, Financial statements of organisations, in Unit 1 (The Accounting System and Costing) of the Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Subsidiary/Advanced Level in Accounting (YAC11), Specification Issue 2, September 2018. This is International AS content. All amounts are in dollars and every business named is invented.

## The statement of profit or loss and the appropriation account

A partnership statement of profit or loss is prepared exactly like a sole trader's, down to **profit for the year**. Partners are owners, not employees, so their salaries and the interest on their capital are not expenses. They are **appropriations** of profit, shown in a separate **appropriation account** below the profit for the year.

Interest on a partner's **loan** is different. It is a genuine expense of the business and is deducted in the statement of profit or loss, before profit for the year.

The appropriation account has three parts:

1. Profit for the year (brought from the statement of profit or loss).
2. Add interest charged on drawings (this is income for the firm, taken from the partners).
3. Less partners' salaries and interest on capital, then share the **residual profit** in the profit-sharing ratio.

## The partnership agreement and Section 24

The partners can agree their own terms, usually in writing. A typical agreement covers capital contributed, salaries, interest on capital, interest on drawings, the profit-sharing ratio and what happens when a partner joins or leaves. Where the agreement is silent, the rules in **Section 24 of the Partnership Act 1890** apply.

| Item | Section 24 default |
|---|---|
| Profit and loss | Shared equally, whatever the capital or work of each partner |
| Salaries | None |
| Interest on capital | None |
| Interest on drawings | None |
| Interest on loans from partners | 5% a year |
| New partners | Need the consent of all existing partners |

If a dispute arises over a point on which the agreement is silent, the default applies. If the agreement says a partner is to receive a salary, it overrides the default for that item only.

## Worked example: Amara and Bilal

Amara and Bilal trade in partnership. Their agreement says:

- Capital is fixed: Amara 60,000 and Bilal 40,000.
- Interest on capital is 5% a year.
- Bilal receives a salary of 12,000.
- Interest on drawings is 3% of the drawings for the year.
- Remaining profit or loss is shared 3 : 2 (Amara : Bilal).

For the year ended 31 December 2026, profit before partnership loan interest was 49,000. Bilal made a loan of 10,000 to the firm (interest at 5%). Drawings were Amara 14,000 and Bilal 10,000. At 1 January 2026 the current accounts were Amara 2,100 credit and Bilal 1,300 debit.

**Step 1: profit for the year.** Loan interest 10,000 × 5% = 500, an expense. Profit for the year = 49,000 − 500 = **48,500**.

**Step 2: interest on drawings.** Amara 14,000 × 3% = 420 and Bilal 10,000 × 3% = 300.

**Step 3: appropriation account for the year ended 31 December 2026**

| | Amara | Bilal | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Profit for the year | | | 48,500 |
| Add interest on drawings | 420 | 300 | 720 |
| | | | 49,220 |
| Less salary: Bilal | | 12,000 | (12,000) |
| Less interest on capital (5%) | 3,000 | 2,000 | (5,000) |
| **Residual profit** | | | **32,220** |
| Share 3 : 2 | 19,332 | 12,888 | 32,220 |

Residual profit = 49,220 − 12,000 − 5,000 = 32,220. Amara's share is 32,220 × 3/5 = 19,332 and Bilal's share is 32,220 × 2/5 = 12,888.

**Step 4: current accounts**

| | Amara | Bilal |
|---|---:|---:|
| Balance b/d (credit)/debit | 2,100 | (1,300) |
| Salary | | 12,000 |
| Interest on capital | 3,000 | 2,000 |
| Share of residual profit | 19,332 | 12,888 |
| Drawings | (14,000) | (10,000) |
| Interest on drawings | (420) | (300) |
| **Balance c/d** | **10,012** | **15,288** |

Amara: 2,100 + 3,000 + 19,332 − 14,000 − 420 = 10,012. Bilal: −1,300 + 12,000 + 2,000 + 12,888 − 10,000 − 300 = 15,288. In the statement of financial position the capital accounts of 60,000 and 40,000 and the current accounts of 10,012 and 15,288 are shown, with Bilal's loan of 10,000 as a non-current liability.

**What if there were no agreement?** Section 24 applies: profit of 48,500 is shared equally, 24,250 each, with no salary or interest on capital or drawings. Bilal still receives 5% on his loan. The comparison shows why agreements matter: Bilal works more and has a smaller capital, so the equal split would favour Amara.

## Fixed and floating (fluctuating) capital

| | Fixed capital | Floating capital |
|---|---|---|
| Capital account | Stays at the agreed amount until partners change it | Changes every year |
| Current account | Used for salary, interest, share of profit and drawings | Not used |
| Where profit goes | Current account | Capital account |
| Where drawings go | Current account | Capital account |

Fixed capital with current accounts shows clearly what each partner has put in and what they have earned and withdrawn. A debit balance on a current account means the partner has drawn more than was earned. In the statement of financial position of a floating-capital partnership there is only a capital account for each partner.

## Introducing a partner and goodwill

When a new partner joins, the old partnership ends and a new one starts. Assets and liabilities are **revalued** to fair values, **goodwill** is recognised, and the capital accounts are adjusted.

- **Revaluation account:** the gain or loss on revaluing assets is shared by the old partners in the *old* ratio.
- **Goodwill** is the amount by which the value of the business exceeds the value of its net assets. One simple method is a multiple of average profits. If annual profits are 14,000, 16,000 and 15,000, the average is 15,000, and two years' purchase gives 30,000.
- **Old ratio:** goodwill is created at the value agreed and credited to the old partners in the old ratio.
- **New ratio:** it is then written off, debiting the capital accounts in the new profit-sharing ratio. Alternatively, it is kept in the statement of financial position as an intangible asset.

**Example: Chen joins.** Amara and Bilal share profits 3 : 2. Chen joins and the new ratio is Amara 2/5, Bilal 2/5, Chen 1/5. Premises are revalued from 60,000 to 70,000 and inventory from 10,000 to 8,000. Goodwill is valued at 30,000. Chen introduces cash of 20,000 and a vehicle worth 8,000.

- Revaluation gain: 10,000 − 2,000 = 8,000. Amara 8,000 × 3/5 = 4,800 and Bilal 8,000 × 2/5 = 3,200.
- Goodwill created: Amara 30,000 × 3/5 = 18,000 and Bilal 12,000.
- Goodwill written off in the new ratio: Amara 12,000, Bilal 12,000 and Chen 6,000.

| | Amara | Bilal | Chen |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Balance before | 60,000 | 40,000 | |
| Revaluation gain | 4,800 | 3,200 | |
| Goodwill created | 18,000 | 12,000 | |
| Cash and vehicle introduced | | | 28,000 |
| Goodwill written off | (12,000) | (12,000) | (6,000) |
| **Capital after admission** | **70,800** | **43,200** | **22,000** |

The total of the capital accounts is 136,000, which is 60,000 + 40,000 + 8,000 revaluation gain + 28,000 introduced. Goodwill increases and then decreases the total by the same amount, so it has no net effect on total capital, though it moves value from Chen to Amara. That transfer compensates the old partners for the business they built.

**Retirement.** When a partner retires, the same steps apply: revalue assets, create goodwill in the old ratio, and then write it off in the new ratio of the remaining partners. If Bilal retired in the same example, his capital of 40,000 plus 12,000 goodwill would be 52,000. This is paid in cash or, more often, transferred to a loan account until the business can pay.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a partner's salary or interest on capital as an expense in the statement of profit or loss.
- Deducting interest on a partner's loan in the appropriation account instead of the statement of profit or loss.
- Adding interest on drawings to the partner's account instead of deducting it.
- Sharing the residual profit when the salary and interest alone exceed the profit. Losses after appropriations are shared in the ratio.
- Using the new ratio for the revaluation gain or for creating goodwill.
- Applying the default ratio when the agreement states one.
- Mixing fixed and floating capital methods in the same answer.

## Exam technique

- Prepare the statement of profit or loss first, then the appropriation account, then the current accounts.
- Use three columns (Amara, Bilal, Total) in the appropriation account and check that the columns add to the total.
- Quote Section 24 when no agreement is given: say "equal shares, no salary, no interest on capital or drawings, 5% on loans".
- For goodwill, show the three stages as three separate entries so each earns a mark.
- For **explain** questions on fixed capital, link the reason to a consequence, such as seeing the partner's drawings separately from their investment.
