Condensed recall for **3.1 The characteristics of extreme environments** (IB DP Geography, Option C: Extreme environments). Fuller coverage is in the [Extreme environments study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-geography-extreme-environments-study-guide/).

## What counts as extreme?

Conditions at or beyond the usual limits for human settlement or ecosystem productivity. Extremeness may come from temperature, aridity, altitude, ice cover or a combination. It is **relative**: what is extreme for one species or society may be home for another.

## Typical characteristics

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Climate | Very low or very high temperatures; large diurnal ranges in deserts; low, unreliable precipitation; strong winds; long snow cover in cold areas |
| Vegetation and soils | Sparse cover, slow decomposition, thin, immature or saline soils; adaptations such as deep roots, waxy leaves, cushion growth forms |
| Ecology | Low net primary productivity and biodiversity, though specialised species can be abundant |
| Hazards | Drought, flash floods, avalanches, frost, glacial outburst floods, altitude sickness |

## Two calculations

- **Aridity index = precipitation / potential evapotranspiration.** Lower values mean drier. Example: 180 / 1,500 = 0.12. Quote the classification you use, as thresholds differ between sources.
- **Environmental lapse rate:** about 6.5 degrees Celsius of cooling per 1,000 m gained (an average; local conditions vary). Example: 4,000 m higher gives 6.5 x 4 = 26 degrees colder.

## Global distribution and why

- Hot deserts cluster in the subtropical belts of descending air.
- Cold environments dominate high latitudes.
- Mountain ranges rise at many latitudes.

Explain the cause of each pattern; naming locations alone earns little.

## Exam traps

- Using "extreme" as if it meant the same for everyone.
- Treating all deserts as hot and sandy: many are rocky, and some are cold.
- Quoting the lapse rate as exact rather than an average.
- Citing aridity values without saying which classification applies.

## Self-test

1. Why is "extreme" a relative term?
2. Give three characteristics of vegetation or soils in extreme environments.
3. Define the aridity index.
4. Calculate the index for P = 90 mm and PET = 1,800 mm.
5. By how much does temperature fall over 2,000 m using the average lapse rate?

**Answers:** 1. What is extreme for one species or society may be normal for another. 2. For example sparse cover, thin or saline soils, deep roots. 3. Precipitation divided by potential evapotranspiration. 4. 90 / 1,800 = 0.05. 5. 6.5 x 2 = 13 degrees Celsius.
