This guide covers section 5.1, Units of measure, in the Mensuration topic of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The syllabus asks you to **use metric units of mass, length, area, volume and capacity in practical situations and convert quantities into larger or smaller units**. The units named are mm, cm, m and km for length, mm², cm², m² and km² for area, mm³, cm³ and m³ for volume, ml and l for capacity, and g and kg for mass. Conversions include area (for example cm² to m²) and volume to capacity (for example m³ to litres). After this guide, use the [units of measure revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-units-of-measure-revision-notes/) and the [units of measure practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-units-of-measure-practice/).

## What section 5.1 asks for

1. Know the metric units and the links between them.
2. Convert between units of length, mass and capacity.
3. Convert between units of area and between units of volume, where the factors are squared and cubed.
4. Convert between volume and capacity.
5. Use these in practical problems, such as filling a tank or covering a floor.

## Length

| Conversion | Factor |
|---|---|
| 1 cm | 10 mm |
| 1 m | 100 cm = 1000 mm |
| 1 km | 1000 m |

To go to a smaller unit, multiply. To go to a larger unit, divide. A quick check: the number should get bigger when the unit gets smaller. 2.5 m is 250 cm, and 4500 m is 4.5 km.

## Mass

| Conversion | Factor |
|---|---|
| 1 kg | 1000 g |

So 2.4 kg = 2400 g and 750 g = 0.75 kg.

## Capacity

| Conversion | Factor |
|---|---|
| 1 litre (l) | 1000 millilitres (ml) |

So 0.45 l = 450 ml and 3500 ml = 3.5 l.

## Area

An area unit is a length unit squared, so its conversion factor is the length factor squared.

| Conversion | Factor |
|---|---|
| 1 cm² | 10 × 10 = 100 mm² |
| 1 m² | 100 × 100 = 10 000 cm² |
| 1 km² | 1000 × 1000 = 1 000 000 m² |

### Why the factor is squared

A square of side 1 m has area 1 m². It is also a square of side 100 cm, so its area is 100 × 100 = 10 000 cm². Never use 100 to convert between m² and cm².

### Worked example 1

Convert (a) 3.6 m² to cm², (b) 450 000 cm² to m², (c) 2.5 km² to m², (d) 2400 mm² to cm².

```
(a) 3.6 × 10 000 = 36 000 cm²
(b) 450 000 ÷ 10 000 = 45 m²
(c) 2.5 × 1 000 000 = 2 500 000 m²
(d) 2400 ÷ 100 = 24 cm²
```

## Volume

A volume unit is a length unit cubed, so the factor is the length factor cubed.

| Conversion | Factor |
|---|---|
| 1 cm³ | 10 × 10 × 10 = 1000 mm³ |
| 1 m³ | 100 × 100 × 100 = 1 000 000 cm³ |

### Worked example 2

Convert (a) 0.8 m³ to cm³, (b) 7 200 000 cm³ to m³, (c) 3.2 cm³ to mm³.

```
(a) 0.8 × 1 000 000 = 800 000 cm³
(b) 7 200 000 ÷ 1 000 000 = 7.2 m³
(c) 3.2 × 1000 = 3200 mm³
```

## Volume and capacity

Capacity is the amount of liquid a container holds. The link between volume and capacity is:

```
1 cm³ = 1 ml
1000 cm³ = 1 litre
1 m³ = 1000 litres
```

Check the last one: 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³ = 1 000 000 ml = 1000 l.

### Worked example 3

A rectangular tank measures 150 cm by 80 cm by 60 cm. How many litres does it hold when full?

```
volume = 150 × 80 × 60 = 720 000 cm³
capacity = 720 000 ÷ 1000 = 720 litres
```

### Worked example 4

A water tank measures 2 m by 1.5 m by 0.8 m. It is filled at a rate of 12 litres per minute. How long does it take to fill?

```
volume = 2 × 1.5 × 0.8 = 2.4 m³
capacity = 2.4 × 1000 = 2400 litres
time = 2400 ÷ 12 = 200 minutes = 3 hours 20 minutes
```

### Worked example 5

A box measures 12 cm by 8 cm by 5 cm. Find its volume in cm³ and its capacity in litres.

```
volume = 12 × 8 × 5 = 480 cm³
capacity = 480 ÷ 1000 = 0.48 litres (480 ml)
```

## Practical problems

Practical questions mix units. A good method is to convert everything into one unit first, then calculate, then convert the answer if the question asks for another unit.

### Worked example 6

A floor measures 5 m by 4 m. It is covered with square tiles of side 25 cm. How many tiles are needed?

```
convert: 5 m = 500 cm, 4 m = 400 cm
floor area = 500 × 400 = 200 000 cm²
tile area = 25 × 25 = 625 cm²
number of tiles = 200 000 ÷ 625 = 320
```

Check by lengths: 500 ÷ 25 = 20 tiles along one side and 400 ÷ 25 = 16 along the other, and 20 × 16 = 320. 

### Worked example 7

A stack of 500 sheets of paper is each 0.3 mm thick. Find the height of the stack in cm.

```
500 × 0.3 = 150 mm
150 ÷ 10 = 15 cm
```

### Worked example 8

A recipe uses 15 g of sugar per portion. A bag holds 2.4 kg. How many portions can be made?

```
2.4 kg = 2400 g
2400 ÷ 15 = 160 portions
```

### Rainfall and volume

Rain falling on a flat area forms a layer like a cuboid. If 15 mm of rain falls on a rectangular yard of area 20 000 m², the volume is 0.015 m × 20 000 m² = 300 m³, which is 300 000 litres.

## Large and small answers in standard form

Conversions between m³, cm³ and mm³ produce very large or very small numbers. Standard form keeps them readable.

```
2.5 km² = 2.5 × 1 000 000 m² = 2.5 × 10⁶ m²
450 cm³ = 450 ÷ 1 000 000 m³ = 0.00045 m³ = 4.5 × 10⁻⁴ m³
```

The size of the exponent tells you how many places the decimal point moves. Converting to a larger unit gives a smaller number, so the exponent goes down.

## Rates with different units

A rate has two units. To change the unit of volume or time in a rate, convert each part separately.

### Worked example 9

A tap fills a tank at 12 litres per minute. Express this rate in m³ per hour.

```
12 litres per minute = 12 × 60 = 720 litres per hour
720 litres = 0.72 m³
rate = 0.72 m³ per hour
```

### Worked example 10

A swimming pool measures 25 m by 10 m with a constant depth of 2 m. It is filled by a pump delivering 250 litres per minute. How long does it take to fill?

```
volume = 25 × 10 × 2 = 500 m³
capacity = 500 × 1000 = 500 000 litres
time = 500 000 ÷ 250 = 2000 minutes = 33 hours 20 minutes
```

## Choosing sensible units

Sensible answers use the unit that suits the size of the quantity. The thickness of a coin is measured in mm, a room in m, a journey in km, a bottle in ml and a swimming pool in m³ or litres. When a question gives an answer in a very large or small number of units, check whether a different unit is better.

## Common mistakes

- Using 100 for m² to cm², or 1000 for m³ to cm³, instead of 10 000 and 1 000 000.
- Multiplying when you should divide, so a bigger unit gives a bigger number.
- Mixing units in a single calculation, such as multiplying m by cm.
- Forgetting that 1 litre is 1000 cm³, not 100 cm³.
- Writing the answer without a unit, or with the wrong unit.
- Dropping a zero when working with large numbers like 1 000 000.

## Exam technique

- **Convert** or **work out**: show the multiplication or division by the factor.
- Write the unit after every number.
- Check the direction: smaller unit means bigger number.
- If a problem uses mixed units, convert them at the start.
