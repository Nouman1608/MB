These notes cover subtopic **4.3 Density and pressure** of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702, 2025–2027 series.

## Definitions and equations

| Quantity | Definition | Equation | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Density | Mass per unit volume | ρ = m/V | kg m⁻³ |
| Pressure | Normal force per unit area | p = F/A | Pa (N m⁻²) |
| Hydrostatic pressure difference | Derived from the two definitions | Δp = ρgΔh | Pa |
| Upthrust (Archimedes) | Weight of fluid displaced | F = ρgV | N |

**Pressure under solids:** same force, smaller area, larger pressure. A 600 N person on one heel of area 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ m² exerts 6.0 × 10⁶ Pa; standing flat on two shoes of total area 0.050 m², only 1.2 × 10⁴ Pa.

**Unit conversion:** 1 g cm⁻³ = 1000 kg m⁻³; 1 cm² = 10⁻⁴ m².

## The derivation in five lines

1. Column of liquid: area A, height Δh, so volume V = AΔh.
2. Mass m = ρV = ρAΔh (density definition).
3. Weight W = mg = ρAΔhg.
4. Pressure difference Δp = W/A (pressure definition).
5. Δp = ρgΔh: the area cancels.

**Assumptions:** constant density (incompressible liquid), uniform g.

## What Δp = ρgΔh tells you

- Pressure in a fluid increases linearly with **vertical** depth.
- Points at the same depth in the same connected liquid are at the same pressure.
- Container shape and width do not matter.
- Total pressure at depth h below an open surface = atmospheric pressure + ρgh.
- Layers: add ρgΔh for each layer separately.

**Recall example:** in seawater (1030 kg m⁻³) at 30 m, pressure due to water = 1030 × 9.81 × 30 = 3.03 × 10⁵ Pa; total = 1.01 × 10⁵ + 3.03 × 10⁵ = 4.04 × 10⁵ Pa.

## Upthrust: why it exists

- Pressure on the lower surface is greater than on the upper surface (greater depth).
- Upward force on the bottom > downward force on the top.
- Net upward force = upthrust.
- Forces on vertical sides cancel in pairs.
- For a cuboid: ρg(h₂ − h₁)A = ρgV, which is Archimedes' principle.

## Using F = ρgV correctly

| Symbol | Means | Not |
|---|---|---|
| ρ | Density of the **fluid** | Density of the object |
| V | Volume of fluid **displaced** (submerged volume) | Total volume, unless fully submerged |
| F | Upthrust, upwards | Weight of the object |

## Float, sink or hang

| Situation | Force balance |
|---|---|
| Floating at rest | Upthrust = weight; only part submerged |
| Fully submerged and sinking | Weight > upthrust |
| Held under water on a string from above | Tension + upthrust = weight |
| Held down by a string from below | Upthrust = weight + tension |

**Floating fraction:** fraction submerged = ρ(object) ÷ ρ(fluid). Ice (920 kg m⁻³) in seawater (1030 kg m⁻³): 0.89, so about 89% is below the surface.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fluid | A liquid or a gas |
| Hydrostatic pressure | Pressure in a fluid at rest, due to the weight of fluid above |
| Upthrust | Upward resultant force on an object in a fluid |
| Displaced volume | Volume of fluid pushed aside by the submerged part of an object |
| Atmospheric pressure | About 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa at sea level |

## Exam traps

- Writing p = F × A, or leaving out "normal" in the definition of pressure.
- Using the object's density in F = ρgV.
- Using the length of a sloping pipe instead of the vertical height difference.
- Forgetting atmospheric pressure when the total pressure is asked for.
- Including atmospheric pressure when only the pressure due to the liquid is asked for.
- Saying upthrust grows as an object sinks deeper.
- Skipping the density step in the derivation.

## Self-test

1. Define density.
2. Define pressure.
3. A metal block has mass 0.40 kg and volume 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ m³. Find its density.
4. Write the five steps of the derivation of Δp = ρgΔh.
5. Why does the area cancel in the derivation, and what does this imply?
6. Find the pressure difference between the top and bottom of a 25 m column of water.
7. Find the total pressure 30 m below the surface of seawater (1030 kg m⁻³), with atmospheric pressure 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa.
8. Explain why upthrust acts upwards.
9. A stone of volume 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ m³ is fully submerged in water. Find the upthrust.
10. What fraction of a block of density 800 kg m⁻³ floats below the surface of water?
11. A helium balloon has volume 0.020 m³. Find the upthrust from air of density 1.2 kg m⁻³.
12. Does the upthrust on a fully submerged stone change as it sinks deeper? Explain.

### Answers

1. Mass per unit volume.
2. Normal force per unit area.
3. 0.40 ÷ 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ = 8000 kg m⁻³.
4. V = AΔh; m = ρAΔh; W = ρAΔhg; Δp = W/A; Δp = ρgΔh.
5. Both the weight of the column and the area it acts on are proportional to A; the pressure depends only on depth, not on container shape.
6. 1000 × 9.81 × 25 = 2.45 × 10⁵ Pa.
7. 1.01 × 10⁵ + 3.03 × 10⁵ = 4.04 × 10⁵ Pa.
8. Pressure increases with depth, so the upward force on the lower surface is greater than the downward force on the upper surface.
9. 1000 × 9.81 × 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ = 2.4 N.
10. 800 ÷ 1000 = 0.80.
11. 1.2 × 9.81 × 0.020 = 0.24 N.
12. No: the displaced volume and fluid density do not change, so F = ρgV is constant (the difference between top and bottom pressures stays the same).
