## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between a taxis and a kinesis, and give one example of either. **[3]**

**2.** In 30 seconds a woodlouse travelled 66 mm in damp air and 150 mm in dry air.
**(a)** Calculate its speed in each condition, in mm s⁻¹. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in speed in dry air compared with damp air, to the nearest whole number. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how this response helps woodlice to stay in damp areas. **[2]**

**3.** Forty animals were placed in the centre of a choice chamber with a dry half and a damp half. After 10 minutes, 29 were in the damp half and 11 in the dry half.
**(a)** State a null hypothesis for the investigation. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate χ². The expected number in each half is 20. **[2]**
**(c)** There is one degree of freedom and the critical value at p = 0.05 is 3.84. State and explain your conclusion. **[2]**

**4.** A student investigates the effect of light intensity on the movement of fly larvae using a choice chamber.
**(a)** Describe how the student should carry out the investigation so that the results are reliable. **[4]**
**(b)** Give one ethical precaution. **[1]**

**5.** A cyclist's eye is struck by a flying insect and the eyelid closes before the cyclist has registered what happened.
**(a)** Name the three neurones in this reflex arc, in order, and state the role of each. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why this type of reflex has a protective effect. **[2]**

**6.** A reflex arc is 1.2 m long and impulses travel along it at 40 m s⁻¹. There are two synapses, each adding a delay of 0.8 ms. Calculate the total response time in milliseconds. **[3]**

**7.** Sections of shoot and root were kept for 24 hours in water or in a high concentration of IAA. Shoot sections lengthened by 3.0 mm in water and 7.5 mm in IAA. Root sections lengthened by 8.0 mm in water and 2.0 mm in IAA.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in lengthening caused by IAA for the shoot and for the root. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what the results show about the effect of IAA on cell elongation. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest one way to improve the investigation. **[1]**

**8.** A seedling is laid on its side. Explain how its root bends downward. **[4]**

**9.** Describe how pressure on a Pacinian corpuscle can lead to an action potential in a sensory neurone. **[5]**

**10.** A Pacinian corpuscle has a resting potential of −70 mV and a threshold of −55 mV. Pressure of 1.0 kPa gives a membrane potential of −64 mV, 1.5 kPa gives −58 mV and 2.0 kPa gives −51 mV.
**(a)** Calculate the depolarisation at 1.0 kPa. **[1]**
**(b)** State whether 1.5 kPa triggers an action potential and explain your answer. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why shining a bright light on the corpuscle gives no response. **[1]**

**11.** **(a)** Explain why you see fine detail in daylight. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why you cannot see colour in very dim light. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why you can see dim objects at night. **[2]**

**12.** The table gives data supplied for two regions of a human retina.

| Region | Cones per mm² | Rods per mm² |
|---|---|---|
| A | 160 000 | 0 |
| B | 8 000 | 90 000 |

**(a)** Calculate the ratio of rods to cones in region B, to the nearest whole number. **[1]**
**(b)** A person has damage to region A. Predict and explain the effect on reading small print in bright light. **[3]**
**(c)** Astronomers sometimes see a faint star better by looking slightly to one side of it. Explain this. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A taxis is a directional movement in response to the direction of a stimulus [1]; a kinesis is a change in speed of movement or rate of turning in response to the intensity of a stimulus, not its direction [1]; example, such as woodlice moving faster in dry air (kinesis) or *Euglena* swimming towards light (taxis) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Define the pair by the word "direction" versus "intensity"; answers that only say "movement in response to a stimulus" for both do not separate them.

**2. (a)** Damp: 66 / 30 = **2.2 mm s⁻¹** [1]. Dry: 150 / 30 = **5.0 mm s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** (5.0 − 2.2) / 2.2 × 100 = 127.3, so **127%** [1].
**(c)** In dry air it moves faster, so it is more likely to leave the dry area [1]; in damp air it moves slowly, so it tends to remain there [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the division before the answer and include units; a percentage change should be divided by the starting (damp) value.

**3. (a)** The animals are distributed equally between the damp and dry halves, so any difference is due to chance [1].
**(b)** (29 − 20)² / 20 = 4.05 and (11 − 20)² / 20 = 4.05 [1]; χ² = **8.1** [1].
**(c)** 8.1 is greater than 3.84, so the null hypothesis is rejected [1]; the difference is significant at p = 0.05, so the animals prefer the damp half [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Conclusions that compare the calculated value with the critical value and then refer back to the biology tend to gain credit; stating only "significant" leaves the link to the animals unsaid.

**4. (a)** Cover one half with black card or foil and leave the other half lit [1]. Put the same number of larvae in the centre [1]. Count the number in each half at fixed times and keep other variables such as temperature the same [1]. Repeat with new larvae and calculate a mean [1].
**(b)** Handle larvae gently, limit exposure time, and return them to where they were collected [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the control variables that matter and the repeat with new animals usually separates a strong method from a list of apparatus.

**5. (a)** Sensory neurone carries impulses from the receptor to the central nervous system [1]; relay neurone connects the sensory and motor neurones [1]; motor neurone carries impulses to the effector (muscle) [1].
**(b)** The pathway is short with few synapses, so the response is very rapid [1]; it is automatic, so the eyelid closes before any decision, reducing damage to the eye [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Pair each neurone with its role in order; "fast" alone is weaker than "fast because few synapses".

**6.** Conduction time = 1.2 / 40 = 0.030 s = 30 ms [1]. Synaptic delay = 2 × 0.8 = 1.6 ms [1]. Total = 30 + 1.6 = **31.6 ms** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert seconds to milliseconds before adding the synaptic delays; mixing units is a common loss.

**7. (a)** Shoot: (7.5 − 3.0) / 3.0 × 100 = **+150%** [1]. Root: (2.0 − 8.0) / 8.0 × 100 = **−75%** [1].
**(b)** IAA stimulates cell elongation in the shoot [1]; it inhibits cell elongation in the root [1].
**(c)** Test a range of IAA concentrations, or repeat and use a mean [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* State the sign or direction of each percentage change and interpret each organ separately; one comment covering both organs hides the difference.

**8.** IAA moves to the lower side of the root [1]. A high concentration of IAA inhibits cell elongation in roots [1]. The upper side elongates faster than the lower side [1]. The root therefore curves downward, which is positive gravitropism [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The same sequence applied to a shoot gives the opposite bend; name the organ in every sentence.

**9.** Pressure deforms the lamellae and stretches the neurone membrane [1]. Stretch-mediated sodium ion channels widen [1]. Sodium ions diffuse into the neurone [1]. The membrane depolarises, producing a generator potential [1]. If the generator potential reaches threshold, an action potential is set up [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the events in order and name the channels as stretch-mediated; "channels open because of the voltage" describes a different mechanism.

**10. (a)** −64 − (−70) = **6 mV** [1].
**(b)** No [1]. The potential reaches −58 mV, which is a depolarisation of 12 mV, and a threshold of −55 mV needs 15 mV [1].
**(c)** A Pacinian corpuscle responds only to its specific stimulus, which is pressure [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the numbers from the question in the explanation; a statement that only repeats "below threshold" is thinner than one that shows the gap.

**11. (a)** Cones each connect to their own bipolar neurone [1], so two nearby points of light send separate impulses to the brain [1].
**(b)** Cones need higher light intensity to be stimulated [1]; rods contain one pigment, so rods give no colour discrimination [1].
**(c)** Rhodopsin in rods is broken down by low-intensity light [1]; many rods connect to one bipolar neurone, so summation of generator potentials reaches threshold [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each feature (connection, pigment) to its effect on the impulse sent to the brain, not just to the name of the cell.

**12. (a)** 90 000 / 8 000 = 11.25, so about **11 : 1** [1].
**(b)** Reading would be impaired [1]. Region A has the highest density of cones, each with its own bipolar neurone [1], so loss of region A reduces the acuity needed to see fine detail [1].
**(c)** Region B has many rods and few cones [1]. Many rods connect to one bipolar neurone, so summation of generator potentials reaches threshold in faint light [1]. Region A has no rods, so looking directly at the star does not allow this [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the data (cone density, rod presence) in your explanation and name the property being explained, sensitivity or acuity.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a kinesis as directional movement.
- Working out percentage change relative to the final figure rather than the starting one.
- Omitting units or the conversion from seconds to milliseconds.
- Stating a chi-squared result without the null hypothesis, degrees of freedom and comparison with the critical value.
- Writing a vague method for a choice chamber with no controls and no repeats.
- Saying IAA has the same effect in roots and shoots.
- Writing that a stimulus "makes an action potential" without naming the generator potential and threshold.
- Explaining sensitivity or acuity without linking it to how rods and cones connect to bipolar neurones.
