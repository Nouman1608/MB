## Section A — short-answer questions

**1.** In which year was the 18th Amendment passed? **[1]**

A: 2008 &nbsp; B: 2010 &nbsp; C: 2013 &nbsp; D: 2015

**2.** Which province was renamed by the 18th Amendment? **[1]**

A: Sindh &nbsp; B: Balochistan &nbsp; C: North-West Frontier Province &nbsp; D: Punjab

**3.** Who succeeded Yousaf Raza Gilani as Prime Minister in 2012? **[1]**

A: Raja Pervaiz Ashraf &nbsp; B: Shahid Khaqan Abbasi &nbsp; C: Nawaz Sharif &nbsp; D: Imran Khan

**4.** Which military operation began in North Waziristan in 2014? **[1]**

A: Rah-e-Rast &nbsp; B: Zarb-e-Azb &nbsp; C: Radd-ul-Fasaad &nbsp; D: Gibraltar

**5.** State **two** ways in which the 18th Amendment increased the powers of the provinces. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** challenges faced by the PPP-led government between 2008 and 2013. **[2]**

**7.** State **two** energy policies of the PML-N government (2013-18). **[2]**

## Section B — extended questions

**8.** Identify and explain **two** reasons why the 19th Amendment was passed. **[4]**

**9.** Identify and explain **two** ways in which the PPP-led government tried to address the

**10.** Analyse **two** ways in which the 18th Amendment changed the balance of power between the

**13.** Which was the more important achievement of the PML-N government (2013-18)?

## Answers

Multiple-choice questions score 1 mark. "State" questions score 1 mark per valid point. "Identify
and explain" questions score 1 mark for each point identified and 1 for its explanation. The 6- and
9-mark questions are marked by levels; the level descriptions follow the answers. Credit other
accurate points.

**1.** **B: 2010** [1], in April. **[1]**

**2.** **C: North-West Frontier Province** [1], renamed Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. **[1]**

**3.** **A: Raja Pervaiz Ashraf** [1]. Abbasi replaced Nawaz Sharif in 2017. **[1]**

**4.** **B: Zarb-e-Azb** [1]. Rah-e-Rast was the 2009 operation in Swat. **[1]**

**5.** Two ways, 1 mark each: the Concurrent List was abolished, so subjects such as education and
health passed to the provinces [1]; the Council of Common Interests was strengthened, giving provinces
more say in shared decisions [1]. Also credit: a share in ownership of oil and gas in their territory.
**[2]**

**6.** Two challenges, 1 mark each: militant violence and terrorism [1]; serious electricity
shortages and load-shedding [1]. Also credit: the 2010 floods; economic difficulty and the IMF loan;
tension with the USA after the Abbottabad raid; conflict with the judiciary. **[2]**

**7.** Two policies, 1 mark each: building new coal-fired and hydel power projects [1]; importing
liquefied natural gas (LNG) [1]. Also credit: power projects financed under CPEC. **[2]**

**8.** Two reasons, each identified (1) and explained (1):
- the Supreme Court raised concerns about the 18th Amendment's method of appointing judges [1], and
  parliament wanted to avoid a direct conflict with the judiciary [1];
- senior judges wanted a stronger role in choosing judges [1], to protect the independence of the
  courts from political influence [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Two ways, each identified (1) and explained (1):
- the 7th NFC Award (2009) [1] gave the provinces a larger share of federal revenue and used several
  criteria, not population alone, which helped Balochistan [1];
- the Aghaz-e-Huqooq-e-Balochistan package (2009) [1] offered jobs, funds and political measures to
  respond to Baloch grievances [1].

Also credit: the Gilgit-Baltistan Order (2009) giving an elected assembly; renaming NWFP Khyber
Pakhtunkhwa; devolution under the 18th Amendment. **[4]**

**10.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- *Dissolution power removed:* the President could no longer dissolve the National Assembly at his own
  discretion, a power that had been used to dismiss governments in the 1990s; the Prime Minister's
  government became secure while it kept a majority.
- *Prime Minister as chief executive:* executive authority passed to the Prime Minister and cabinet,
  and the President became largely a ceremonial head of state acting on advice.
- *Appointments:* key appointments, such as those of judges and the Chief Election Commissioner, moved
  to commissions and consultation rather than presidential discretion.
- *Effect:* Pakistan returned to the parliamentary system intended by the 1973 Constitution. **[6]**

**11.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- *Unpopularity of the PPP government:* load-shedding, rising prices and militant violence made voters
  look for change.
- *PML-N strength in Punjab:* the party had governed Punjab since 2008 and could point to projects
  such as the Lahore metro bus; Punjab elects the largest number of National Assembly seats.
- *Leadership:* Nawaz Sharif was an experienced former Prime Minister who promised to end
  load-shedding and revive the economy.
- *Opposition strongest elsewhere:* PTI attracted many younger and urban voters, but most of the
  seats it won were in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, so it did not overturn the PML-N's lead in Punjab. **[6]**

**12.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Indicative content:
- *Agree:* the 18th Amendment restored parliamentary democracy, removed the President's dissolution
  power, devolved powers to the provinces, guaranteed the right to education and made suspending the
  constitution high treason; its effects lasted beyond 2013.
- *Disagree:* other changes mattered: the 7th NFC Award reshaped provincial finances; BISP created a
  lasting social-protection system; the restoration of the Chief Justice strengthened the judiciary;
  completing a full term in 2013 set a democratic precedent.
- *Judgement:* for example, the 18th Amendment was the most important because it changed the
  structure of the state, while the other changes were policies that later governments could alter.
  **[9]**

**13.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Answers must address both bullet points. Indicative
content:
- *Security:* Zarb-e-Azb from 2014 and the National Action Plan after the Peshawar school attack led to
  a fall in terrorist attacks; the Karachi operation reduced violence in the city; but military courts
  raised concerns about rights.
- *Energy and infrastructure:* new power projects and LNG imports reduced load-shedding; CPEC brought
  Chinese investment in roads, power and Gwadar; but costs and debt raised concerns.
- *Judgement:* for example, security was more important because economic projects such as CPEC
  depended on a safer country, or energy was more important because load-shedding affected every
  household and business. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling Zardari Prime Minister, or mixing up the Prime Ministers of 2008-18.
- Writing about the 18th Amendment as a list without explaining the effect of each change.
- Attributing PML-N measures such as the FATA merger to a later government.
- In 9-mark answers, failing to compare the two factors directly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know amendment numbers beyond the 18th and 19th?**
The specification names the 18th and 19th. Knowing the 20th, 21st and 25th helps you add precise
evidence to extended answers.

**Is it fair to say Pakistan became a "parliamentary system" in 2010?**
The 1973 Constitution was already parliamentary; the 18th Amendment restored that balance after
earlier changes had strengthened the President.
