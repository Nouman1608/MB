These questions cover section 3.3.2, The electromagnetic spectrum (statements a to m), of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) specification, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered. Question 7 draws on content marked for the full Physics award. Use c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s for the speed of EM waves in a vacuum.

## Questions

**1.** **(a)** State whether electromagnetic waves are transverse or longitudinal. **[1]**
**(b)** State what electromagnetic waves transfer, and where it is transferred from and to. **[2]**

**2.** The following are all parts of the electromagnetic spectrum: gamma rays, microwaves, ultraviolet, radio waves, visible light, X-rays, infrared. Write them in order of increasing wavelength. **[3]**

**3.** A TV remote control sends infrared of wavelength 940 nm. Calculate the frequency of the infrared. **[3]**

**4.** **(a)** Calculate the frequency of a gamma ray of wavelength 1.0 × 10⁻¹² m. **[2]**
**(b)** A radio wave has wavelength 3.0 m. Calculate how many times greater the frequency of the gamma ray is than the frequency of the radio wave. **[2]**

**5.** A space probe is 7.5 × 10⁹ m from Earth. A command is sent by radio waves.
**(a)** Calculate the time taken for the command to reach the probe. **[2]**
**(b)** The probe replies at once. Calculate the time between sending the command and receiving the reply. **[1]**

**6.** A student has two identical metal cans, one painted matt black and one shiny silver. Each holds the same mass of water at 20 °C. The cans stand the same distance from a radiant heater. After 10 minutes the water in the black can is at 44 °C and the water in the silver can is at 30 °C. In a second test, both cans are filled with water at 78 °C and left to cool. After 10 minutes the black can is at 49 °C and the silver can is at 63 °C.
**(a)** Calculate the temperature rise of each can in the first test and how many times larger the black can's rise is. **[2]**
**(b)** State what the first test shows about the two surfaces. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the temperature fall of each can in the second test and state what the second test shows. **[2]**
**(d)** State one variable the student must keep the same. **[1]**

**7.** **(a)** State what is meant by black-body radiation. **[1]**
**(b)** The element of an electric heater is switched on. At first it feels warm but looks the same. After a while it glows dull red and later bright orange. Explain these observations. **[4]**

**8.** Choose the electromagnetic wave that fits each job. **[5]**
**(a)** night vision devices
**(b)** security marking
**(c)** satellite television
**(d)** killing harmful bacteria in food
**(e)** medical imaging of bones

**9.** **(a)** State the hazard of overexposure to microwaves and the hazard of overexposure to infrared. **[2]**
**(b)** State two hazards of overexposure to ultraviolet. **[2]**
**(c)** A hiker spends a day on a snow-covered mountain. Suggest two ways the hiker can reduce the risk from ultraviolet. **[2]**

**10.** **(a)** Explain what is meant by ionisation. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why gamma rays are more hazardous to the human body than microwaves. **[2]**
**(c)** State the harm to a cell that gamma rays can cause. **[1]**

**11.** A dental X-ray uses X-rays of wavelength 0.015 nm. The X-rays are detected on photographic film placed inside the mouth.
**(a)** Calculate the frequency of these X-rays. **[3]**
**(b)** The developed image shows a pale outline for a tooth with a metal filling and the jawbone, but a darker region for the gum. Explain why. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest three precautions that reduce the exposure of the dental staff and the patient. **[3]**

**12.** **(a)** Describe how X-rays can be used to treat some cancers, and explain why the dose is carefully controlled. **[3]**
**(b)** State what a CT scan does and give one medical condition that X-rays can help to diagnose. **[2]**
**(c)** A hospital monitors the radiation exposure of its radiographers. State how this can be done and explain why it is needed. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** Transverse. [1]
**(b)** Energy [1], transferred from a source to an absorber [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* On a question like this, answers that name energy score; answers that say the waves carry "matter", "heat" only, or "power" with no source and absorber are weaker.

**2.** Gamma rays are shortest and radio waves longest, so those two go at the ends [1]. Then X-rays, ultraviolet, visible light in that order [1], and infrared then microwaves before the radio waves [1].
Order: **gamma rays, X-rays, ultraviolet, visible light, infrared, microwaves, radio waves**. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Check the direction asked for. A correct list written from longest to shortest is the wrong way round for "increasing wavelength" and is unlikely to earn full credit.

**3.** λ = 940 nm = 9.40 × 10⁻⁷ m [1]. f = c / λ = (3.00 × 10⁸) / (9.40 × 10⁻⁷) [1]. f = 3.19 × 10¹⁴, so **3.2 × 10¹⁴ Hz** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the conversion from nanometres. A final answer that is out by a power of ten usually signals a missed conversion, and clear working helps the marker see where the slip happened.

**4.** **(a)** f = c / λ = (3.00 × 10⁸) / (1.0 × 10⁻¹²) [1] = **3.0 × 10²⁰ Hz** [1].
**(b)** Radio frequency = (3.00 × 10⁸) / 3.0 = 1.0 × 10⁸ Hz [1]. Ratio = (3.0 × 10²⁰) / (1.0 × 10⁸) = **3.0 × 10¹²** times [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A ratio has no unit. Give the number of times, and use standard form so the size is clear.

**5.** **(a)** Time = distance / speed = (7.5 × 10⁹) / (3.00 × 10⁸) [1] = **25 s** [1].
**(b)** The signal makes the journey twice: 2 × 25 = **50 s** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) rewards noticing the journey is made twice. Writing the equation time = distance / speed before substituting makes your method easy to follow.

**6.** **(a)** Black: 44 − 20 = 24 °C and silver: 30 − 20 = 10 °C [1]. Ratio = 24 / 10 = **2.4 times** [1].
**(b)** The matt black surface absorbs more infrared than the shiny silver surface, so it is the better absorber [1].
**(c)** Black fell 78 − 49 = 29 °C and silver fell 78 − 63 = 15 °C [1]. The black can lost energy faster, so a dark, matt surface is a better emitter of infrared [1].
**(d)** Any one from: mass of water, starting temperature, size or material of the can, distance from the heater [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each result to a property (absorber, emitter). "The black can is better" is incomplete; "the black can is a better absorber, because its rise was larger" is a stronger answer.

**7.** **(a)** The range of electromagnetic radiation emitted by an object at a particular temperature [1].
**(b)** Every object emits infrared, and the warm element gives out infrared that you feel but cannot see [1]. As it gets hotter it radiates more infrared in a given time [1]. It also radiates at higher frequencies [1], which reach into the visible range, first red and then orange as higher frequencies are added [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong answers make both points about heating: more radiation, and higher frequencies. A reply that says only "it gets hotter so it glows" does not explain the colour change.

**8.** **(a)** Infrared [1]. **(b)** Ultraviolet [1]. **(c)** Microwaves [1]. **(d)** Gamma rays [1]. **(e)** X-rays [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give one wave for each use. Listing two waves for the same use risks losing the mark if one of them is wrong.

**9.** **(a)** Microwaves: heating of body tissue [1]; infrared: skin burns [1].
**(b)** Skin cancer [1] and blindness [1].
**(c)** Wear sunscreen or cover exposed skin [1]; wear sunglasses or goggles that block ultraviolet [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A protection measure should match the hazard. "Wear clothes" is vague; "cover the skin to block ultraviolet" shows why it works.

**10.** **(a)** Ionisation is the removal of an electron from an atom or molecule [1].
**(b)** Gamma rays have much higher energy than microwaves [1], enough to cause ionisation, whereas microwaves only have a heating effect [1].
**(c)** Genetic mutations (damage to the cell's genetic material) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that gamma rays can cause ionisation. "Gamma rays are radioactive" does not answer a question about the energy of the wave.

**11.** **(a)** λ = 0.015 nm = 1.5 × 10⁻¹¹ m [1]. f = (3.00 × 10⁸) / (1.5 × 10⁻¹¹) [1]. f = **2.0 × 10¹⁹ Hz** [1].
**(b)** Metal and bone absorb X-rays strongly [1], whereas gum, as healthy soft tissue, transmits them [1]. Less radiation reaches the film behind the tooth and bone, so those areas stay paler [1].
**(c)** Any three from, one mark each: the dental staff stand behind a screen or leave the room during the exposure; the patient wears a lead apron; short exposure times; staff wear a monitoring badge; only take an X-ray when it is needed [3]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In explanations about images, name both the tissue and what happens to the X-rays. Stating only "bone is dense" misses the property the question is looking for: absorption.

**12.** **(a)** A beam of X-rays is aimed at the tumour [1] and the high dose kills the cancer cells [1]. The dose is controlled because X-rays also damage healthy cells [1].
**(b)** CT scanning uses X-rays to build up detailed images of the inside of the body [1]. Any one of: bone fractures, dental problems [1].
**(c)** Staff wear a monitoring badge (for example a film badge) [1] that records their exposure [1], so the exposure can be checked and kept low, because ionising radiation can be dangerous [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* For "explain why" parts, finish with the reason, not just the method. Writing "to keep exposure low because ionising radiation is harmful" completes the answer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the spectrum in the wrong direction, or putting microwaves and infrared the wrong way round.
- Leaving wavelengths in nanometres or frequencies in megahertz when substituting into the wave equation.
- Giving a final answer with no unit, or with a unit from a different quantity.
- Saying "the black can is better" without saying that it is a better absorber or emitter of infrared.
- Forgetting that all objects emit infrared, and that hotter objects emit more.
- Answering a black-body question with temperature alone, without mentioning higher frequencies.
- Naming a hazard without naming the wave that causes it, or giving a protection measure that does not match the hazard.
- Saying that X-rays "go through bone" rather than being absorbed by it.
