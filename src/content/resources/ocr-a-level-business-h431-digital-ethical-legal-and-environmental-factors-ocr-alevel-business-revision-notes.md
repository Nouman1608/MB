Condensed notes on **the digital revolution and ethical, legal and environmental factors** in External influences, OCR A Level Business (H431), Version 2.1 (January 2026), final assessment summer 2027. Full explanations and worked cases are in the [digital, ethical, legal and environmental factors study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-digital-ethical-legal-and-environmental-factors-ocr-alevel-business/); test yourself with the [practice questions on these factors](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-digital-ethical-legal-and-environmental-factors-ocr-alevel-business-practice/).

## The digital revolution: recall

- **Digital revolution**: the shift from mechanical and analogue technology to digital technology.
- **Information age**: information is collected, stored, analysed and shared almost instantly and cheaply.
- **Opportunities**: wider markets, lower transaction costs, customer data for targeting, new digital products, automation.
- **Threats**: online rivals and price comparison, cyber-crime, data misuse, constant reinvestment, disintermediation (customers bypassing middlemen).
- **Most affected**: products that can be digitised; intermediaries; firms whose customers shop online.
- **Least affected**: services needing physical presence and local trust.
- **Rapid change**: first-mover advantage and productivity, against short product lives, retraining, redundancy and excluded customers.

## Ethical factors: recall

- **Law**: compulsory rules enforced with penalties. **Ethics**: moral principles; a business chooses how far beyond the law to go.
- **Ethical issues**: wages and conditions, supplier treatment, honest marketing, product safety, animal testing, tax behaviour, use of customer data.
- **More affected**: long overseas supply chains, high public profile, vulnerable customers, values-based brands.
- **Ethical trading methods**: certified fair-trade sourcing, supplier audits, no child or forced labour, fair pay, safe conditions, transparency.
- **Improving the ethical profile**: code of conduct, independent certification, audits, published reports, staff training.
- **Managing conflicts**: phase changes in, share cost with customers, find savings elsewhere, set ethical and financial targets together.

## Legal factors: the eleven areas

Employment; equality; company and partnership; health and safety; data protection; intellectual property; planning; environmental; consumer protection; contract; financial conduct.

| Area | Example UK law |
|---|---|
| Employment | Employment Rights Act 1996 |
| Equality | Equality Act 2010 |
| Company | Companies Act 2006 |
| Health and safety | Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 |
| Data protection | Data Protection Act 2018 |
| Consumer protection | Consumer Rights Act 2015 |

- **Ways to comply**: policies, training, named manager, audits, expert advice.
- **Consequences of failure**: fines, compensation, court orders, lost licences or contracts, reputational damage, possible closure.

## Environmental factors: recall

- **Issues**: carbon emissions, pollution, waste and packaging, energy use, resource depletion.
- **Why consider them**: law, customer and investor pressure, staff attitudes, rising energy and waste costs, supply risks.
- **Environmental policy**: written aims, targets and actions to reduce impact.
- **Sustainability**: meeting present needs without reducing future generations' ability to meet theirs.
- **Acting sustainably**: renewable energy, less and recycled waste, responsible sourcing, durable and repairable products.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Information age | Era of fast, cheap creation and sharing of information |
| Disintermediation | Customers dealing directly with producers, bypassing intermediaries |
| Ethics | Moral principles guiding what is right beyond legal duty |
| Ethical profile | How ethical a business is seen to be by stakeholders |
| Fair trade | Trading that guarantees producers fair prices and conditions |
| Compliance | Meeting the requirements of the law |
| Intellectual property | Legal ownership of ideas, designs, brands and creative work |
| Environmental policy | Written commitment, targets and actions to reduce impact |
| Sustainability | Meeting today's needs without harming future generations' ability to meet theirs |

## Quick calculations

- **Ethical cost on margin.** Price £12, unit cost £7.20, margin 40%. An ethical supplier adds £0.60: margin = 4.20 ÷ 12 = 35%.
- **Green payback.** LED lighting costs £8,400 and saves £2,100 a year: payback = 4 years.

## Exam traps

- Calling something unethical "illegal" without evidence that a law is broken.
- Writing about the digital revolution as if it only means having a website.
- Listing ethical or environmental benefits with no cost, conflict or trade-off.
- Naming a law without saying what it requires of the business.
- Forgetting "being seen as" ethical or green: perception drives stakeholder behaviour.
- Recommending without justifying why the option beats the alternative.

## Self-test

1. Define the information age.
2. Why are travel agents more affected by the digital revolution than plumbers?
3. Give one opportunity and one threat of the digital revolution for a bookshop.
4. Give an example of something legal but unethical.
5. Name two ethical trading methods.
6. Give two ways a business could improve its ethical profile.
7. Which law protects people from discrimination at work in the UK, and which characteristic-based idea does it use?
8. State two consequences of failing to comply with data protection law.
9. Define sustainability.
10. What is greenwashing and why is it risky?
11. A product sells at £20 with unit cost £14. Recycled packaging adds £0.50. What is the new margin?
12. Give one way to manage a conflict between environmental and profit objectives.

### Answers

1. A period in which information can be collected, stored, analysed and shared almost instantly and at low cost.
2. Customers can book travel directly online, bypassing the agent; a plumber's service needs physical presence.
3. Opportunity: sell online to a national market. Threat: low-price online rivals and e-books.
4. Paying the legal minimum wage to staff who cannot cover basic living costs (or legal tax avoidance).
5. Certified fair-trade sourcing; independent supplier audits (or refusing child labour, fair pay).
6. Publish a code of conduct; gain independent certification (or audits, transparent reporting).
7. The Equality Act 2010, which protects people with protected characteristics.
8. A regulator's fine; compensation claims and lost customer trust.
9. Meeting present needs without reducing future generations' ability to meet theirs.
10. Unsupported environmental claims; it can bring complaints and damage trust more than saying nothing.
11. Profit per unit = 20 − 14.50 = £5.50; margin = 5.50 ÷ 20 = 27.5%.
12. Phase the change in over time so costs are spread (or target energy and waste savings to offset costs).
