## What is assessed

| Assessment objective | What it asks you to do with this topic | Where |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 Research, analysis and evaluation | Find identity and inclusion issues, analyse perspectives, judge evidence and sources, build a line of reasoning, assess actions | Components 1, 2, 3 |
| AO2 Reflection | Consider other people's views on identity objectively and with empathy; justify your own view | Components 2, 3 |
| AO3 Communication and collaboration | Present arguments clearly with citations and references; contribute to the Team Project | Components 2, 3 |

Component 1 carries AO1 only. That means the Written Exam rewards analysis and evaluation of the sources, not what you already know about the topic.

## Source-analysis terms at a glance

The syllabus says you will benefit from understanding these eight terms. Learn one identity-themed example of each.

| Term | Test | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Claim | Is it asserted as true? | "Mixed classes reduce prejudice." |
| Fact | Can it be checked? | "The library has two lifts." |
| Opinion | Is it a personal view? | "The festival was boring." |
| Generalisation | Does it judge a whole group from a few cases? | "Teenagers ignore tradition." |
| Prediction | Is it about the future? | "The new dress rule will empty the youth club by summer." |
| Value | What does the writer think matters most? | Fairness, tradition, unity, freedom |
| Vested interest | Does the writer gain if you agree? | A translation firm urging more translated signs |
| Bias | Is relevant evidence left out? | An article that quotes only one community |

## Real reference points at a glance

| Document or body | What it says or does | One evaluation point |
|---|---|---|
| Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948), Article 2 | Rights apply without distinction of any kind, such as race, colour, sex, language or religion | Sets a standard but does not by itself force action |
| UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities | Adopted 2006, in force 2008 | Signing it does not show how well a country carries it out |
| Sustainable Development Goal 10, target 10.2 | Promote social, economic and political inclusion of all by 2030 | A target is an aim, not proof of progress |
| World Health Organization estimate | About 1.3 billion people, around 1 in 6, experience significant disability | Global figure; may not match a local situation |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Topic vs issue.** "Social identity and inclusion" is the topic. "Should school dress rules allow cultural exceptions?" is an issue.
- **Fact vs claim.** A fact can be checked. A claim may be true or false until supported.
- **Opinion vs perspective.** An opinion is a view. A perspective is a view based on evidence and reasoning.
- **Global vs national vs local.** The Individual Report needs local and/or national and global perspectives. The Team Project needs a local issue.
- **Cause vs consequence.** Cause: why exclusion happens. Consequence: what it leads to, and for whom.
- **Proposing vs assessing an action.** Proposing says what to do. Assessing judges how well it would work, for whom, at what cost.
- **Reliable vs credible.** A reliable source gives consistent, accurate information. A credible source has expertise and reason to be trusted. Check both.
- **Integration vs multiculturalism.** Two contested approaches. Explain each fairly before judging.

## Cause and consequence chains

- No ramp at the youth club → wheelchair users cannot enter → they miss activities → fewer friendships → feeling of not belonging.
- A dress rule bans a traditional hairstyle → students feel their identity is not accepted → conflict with staff or missed lessons → weaker sense of belonging.
- Stereotypes in media → employers make assumptions → fewer job offers for a group → income gaps.

Each chain can also be reversed into a course of action: fix the first link and you may break the chain.

## Mini essay plan

**Question:** "Quotas are the most effective way to include under-represented groups in public life." Evaluate this view.

1. **Introduction.** Define quotas (reserved places). Explain why the issue divides opinion. Say you will weigh evidence and arguments.
2. **Perspective for.** Supporters argue quotas produce change quickly and give visible role models. Analyse: the reasoning depends on the claim that visibility changes attitudes. Evaluate: is there evidence, or is it a prediction?
3. **Perspective against.** Critics argue selection should be on individual merit alone and that quotas can create resentment or suggest people were chosen only for their group. Analyse the value behind this: fairness to the individual.
4. **Alternative actions.** Mentoring, removing barriers in recruitment, awareness campaigns. Compare cost, speed and who benefits.
5. **Judgement.** Reach a reasoned judgement on whether quotas are the most effective option, linked to the strength of the evidence. A balanced judgement can be conditional: "effective where..., less so where...".

The plan shows the method. Your own conclusion should follow from the evidence you have, not from the order of the paragraphs.

## Individual Report and Team Project in one line each

- **Individual Report:** a global research question that divides opinion, local and/or national and global perspectives, causes and consequences, one proposed course of action, evaluation of sources, your own view and an answer to your question. See [Individual Report practice](/resources/igcse-global-perspectives-individual-report-practice/).
- **Team Project:** a local issue such as newcomer belonging or step-free access, a team action you can carry out, evidence of action, a way to measure success and an individual Reflective Paper. See [Team Project practice](/resources/igcse-global-perspectives-team-project-practice/).

## Quick self-test

1. Is "Social identity and inclusion" a topic or an issue?
2. Give the syllabus definition of a perspective.
3. "Older residents never use the internet." Which source term fits this statement?
4. Name the assessment objective that is the only one assessed in Component 1.
5. A company that builds lifts argues for a law requiring lifts in all schools. What does the company have?
6. What level of issue must the Team Project address?
7. Give one cause of exclusion that is a physical barrier.
8. Why is "Discrimination is wrong" a weak Individual Report question?
9. "If the rule stays, no newcomer will join the school's clubs next year." Which source term fits?
10. What is the difference between proposing and assessing a course of action?
11. Give one limitation of using the WHO global disability estimate to support a local decision.

### Answers

1. A topic. Issues are narrower questions within it.
2. A viewpoint on an issue based on evidence and reasoning.
3. A generalisation.
4. AO1 Research, analysis and evaluation.
5. A vested interest.
6. A local issue.
7. Steps with no ramp, or no lift to an upper floor.
8. It does not divide opinion, so there are no clearly different perspectives to research.
9. A prediction.
10. Proposing says what should be done. Assessing judges how effective, costly or fair it would be and for whom.
11. It is a global figure, so it does not show how many people in that place are affected.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing identity or inclusion from memory instead of using the source material.
- Labelling a statement "biased" without saying what has been left out or why.
- Listing perspectives without the reasoning or values behind them.
- Giving consequences without saying who is affected.
- Treating a prediction or a target, such as an SDG, as evidence that something has already happened.
- Assessing only one course of action, or praising an action without weighing its limitations.
- Choosing an Individual Report question that does not divide opinion.
- Choosing a national rather than a local issue for the Team Project.
- Stating a verdict on a contested question without reasoned support.
