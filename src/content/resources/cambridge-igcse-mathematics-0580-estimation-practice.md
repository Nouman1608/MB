These questions cover subtopic 1.9 Estimation of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The content is the same for Core and Extended, so every question suits both tiers. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Where an estimate is asked for, write down each rounded value before you calculate.

**(a)** Write 0.050 49 correct to 3 significant figures. **[1]**

**(b)** Write 384.7 correct to 2 significant figures. **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) Write 2.9973 correct to

**(a)** 2 decimal places, **[1]**

**(b)** 1 significant figure. **[1]**

**3.** (non-calculator) By writing each number correct to 1 significant figure, estimate the value of (21.7 × 0.396) ÷ 3.84. **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** By writing each number correct to 1 significant figure, estimate the value of (3.9 × 68.7) ÷ (0.24 × 20.4). **[2]**

**(b)** Without working out the exact value, explain whether your estimate is larger or smaller than the exact value. **[2]**

**9.** (non-calculator) A theatre sells 287 tickets at $19.20 each.

**(a)** By writing each number correct to 1 significant figure, estimate the total money taken. **[2]**

**(b)** State whether your estimate is an overestimate or an underestimate. Give a reason. **[1]**

**10.** A rectangular floor measures 4.8 m by 3.1 m. Each floor tile covers exactly 0.25 m².

**(a)** (non-calculator) By writing each length correct to 1 significant figure, estimate the area of the floor. **[1]**

**(b)** (non-calculator) Use your answer to part (a) to estimate the number of tiles needed. **[2]**

**(c)** (calculator) Work out the exact area and the number of tiles that must be bought. **[2]**

**11.** A cylindrical water tank has radius 0.38 m and height 1.9 m. The volume of a cylinder is V = πr²h.

**(a)** (non-calculator) Taking π as 3 and writing 0.38 and 1.9 correct to 1 significant figure, estimate the volume of the tank in m³. **[2]**

**(b)** (calculator) Calculate the volume of the tank in m³, giving your answer correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**(c)** (calculator) 1 m³ = 1000 litres. The tank is filled from empty using a bucket that holds 9 litres. Find the number of full buckets needed to fill the tank completely. **[2]**

**12.** Ravi uses his calculator to work out 7.92 ÷ 0.0386 and writes down 20.5.

**(a)** (non-calculator) By writing each number correct to 1 significant figure, estimate the value of 7.92 ÷ 0.0386. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain what your estimate shows about Ravi's answer. **[1]**

**(c)** (calculator) Work out 7.92 ÷ 0.0386 correct to 3 significant figures. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **0.0505** [1]

**(b)** **380** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* in (a) the zeros before the 5 are not significant, so the three figures are 5, 0 and 4, and the next digit 9 rounds the 4 up to 5.

**2. (a)** **3.00** [1]

**(b)** **3** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* 3 is not accepted in (a): 2 decimal places must show two digits after the point, even when they are zeros.

**3.** 20, 0.4 and 4 seen [1]; (20 × 0.4) ÷ 4 = 8 ÷ 4 = **2** [1] **[2]**

**4.** √100 and 2 seen [1]; 10 × 2 = **20** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* 97.3 to 1 s.f. is 100, which happens to be a square number. Rounding the result of the square root instead, or using 97 instead of 100, does not follow the instruction.

**5.** 4, 10 and 0.5 seen [1]; (16 + 10) ÷ 0.5 = 26 ÷ 0.5 = **52** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* square the rounded value 4, not 4.12. Dividing by 0.5 doubles the numerator; an answer of 13 comes from multiplying by 0.5 instead.

**6.** 0.5 × 800 [1] = 400, so the answer is about **400, not 40**; Kai's answer is ten times too small [1] **[2]**

**7.** 60, 0.02 and 3 seen [1]; 60 × 0.02 = 1.2 and 1.2 ÷ 3 = 0.4 [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* in a "show that" question the answer is given, so both the rounded values and the intermediate result 1.2 must be written down for full credit.

**8. (a)** 4, 70, 0.2 and 20 seen [1]; (4 × 70) ÷ (0.2 × 20) = 280 ÷ 4 = **70** [1]

**(b)** The numbers on top (3.9 and 68.7) were both rounded up, which makes the estimate larger [1]; the numbers on the bottom (0.24 and 20.4) were both rounded down, which also makes the estimate larger, so it is **larger than the exact value** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* both halves of the reasoning are needed. The exact value is 54.7 to 3 s.f., so the estimate is well above it.

**9. (a)** 300 × 20 [1] = **$6000** [1]

**(b)** **Overestimate**, because both 287 and 19.20 were rounded up [1] **[3]**

**10. (a)** 5 × 3 = **15 m²** [1]

**(b)** 15 ÷ 0.25 [1] = **60 tiles** [1]

**(c)** Area = 4.8 × 3.1 = 14.88 m² [1]; 14.88 ÷ 0.25 = 59.52, so **60 tiles** must be bought [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* 59.52 tiles must round up to 60 because 59 tiles would leave part of the floor uncovered. Dividing by 0.25 is the same as multiplying by 4, which makes part (b) quick without a calculator.

**11. (a)** 3 × 0.4² × 2 [1] = 3 × 0.16 × 2 = **0.96 m³** [1]

**(b)** π × 0.38² × 1.9 = 0.861 927... [1] = **0.862 m³** [1]

**(c)** 861.927... litres ÷ 9 = 95.77... [1]; **96 buckets** [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* in (c), use the unrounded volume from (b). Here 862 ÷ 9 also gives 95.8, so the answer is the same, but carrying the full value is the safe habit. A bucket count must be rounded up so that the tank is completely full.

**12. (a)** 8 ÷ 0.04 [1] = 800 ÷ 4 = **200** [1]

**(b)** The estimate is about ten times larger than 20.5, so Ravi has **made an error**, probably entering the decimal point in the wrong place [1]

**(c)** **205** (to 3 s.f.) [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* using an estimate to check a calculator answer is exactly what 1.9.2 is for. An answer that differs from the estimate by a factor of 10 nearly always means a mis-keyed decimal.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not writing down the 1 s.f. values, so the method mark is lost even when the estimate is right.
- Rounding 0.396 to 0.39 or 0.3 instead of 0.4.
- Dropping the zeros needed to show the accuracy, such as 3.00 to 2 d.p.
- Explaining over- or underestimates without saying which way each number was rounded.
- Rounding a count of tiles, cans or buckets down when the question needs enough to finish the job.
- Using a rounded earlier answer in a later part.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I round 9.79 to 9.8 to make an estimate more accurate?**
Not when the question says "by writing each number correct to 1 significant figure". The instruction fixes the rounding, so 9.79 must become 10.

**What if my 1 s.f. estimate does not come out as a whole number?**
That is fine. Give the result of the rounded calculation, such as 16.7 or 0.09. You do not need to round the estimate again.

**How do I know whether an estimate is too big?**
Look at the direction of each rounding and its place in the calculation. Rounding a multiplied number up, or a divisor down, makes the estimate larger.
