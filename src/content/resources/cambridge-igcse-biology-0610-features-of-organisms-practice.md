These questions cover sub-topic 1.3, Features of organisms, of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 3 are Core. Questions on outcomes 4 to 7 (the five kingdoms, ferns and flowering plants, and viruses) are labelled **(Extended)**, and a Core candidate can skip them. Organisms labelled with letters are invented descriptions.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which feature is found in mammals but in no other group of vertebrates? **[1]**

A: Scales

B: Mammary glands

C: Lungs

D: Eggs

**2.** State **two** features used to place an organism in the animal kingdom. **[2]**

**3.** Name the vertebrate group of each animal.

**(a)** An animal with feathers and a beak that cannot fly. **[1]**

**(b)** An animal with fins, gills and scales. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** differences between a crustacean and an insect. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* State the features of a virus. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** The table describes five arthropods. Identify the group of each.

| Arthropod | Description |
|---|---|
| A | 5 pairs of legs; 2 pairs of antennae; hard exoskeleton |
| B | 4 pairs of legs; 2 body parts; no antennae |
| C | 3 pairs of legs; 3 body parts; 2 pairs of wings |
| D | Long body of about 40 segments; 2 pairs of legs on each segment; 1 pair of antennae |
| E | 3 pairs of legs; 3 body parts; no wings; 1 pair of antennae |

**[5]**

**7.** A student says that a dolphin is a fish because it lives in water and has fins. Explain why the student is wrong, using **two** features. **[3]**

**8.** A salamander has moist skin without scales. It lays its eggs in ponds, and its young have gills.

**(a)** Name the vertebrate group of the salamander. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the salamander is not a reptile. **[2]**

**9.** *(Extended)* Classify each organism into one of the five kingdoms.

| Organism | Description |
|---|---|
| P | Single-celled; has a nucleus and chloroplasts |
| Q | Single-celled; no nucleus; circular DNA |
| R | Made of thread-like hyphae; cell walls not made of cellulose; no chloroplasts |
| S | Multicellular; cellulose cell walls; chloroplasts |
| T | Multicellular; no cell walls; feeds on other organisms |

**[5]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A plant has no flowers and reproduces by spores produced on the underside of its leaves.

**(a)** Name the group of plants it belongs to. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the name for the leaves of this group. **[1]**

**(c)** State **one** feature of a flowering plant that this plant lacks. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A seed contains two cotyledons.

**(a)** State the group of flowering plants it belongs to. **[1]**

**(b)** Predict **two** features of the adult plant's leaves and flowers. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)* Yeast is single-celled. Explain why it is classified as a fungus and not as a prokaryote. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Reptiles and fish have scales, birds and reptiles also have lungs, and several groups lay eggs. **[1]**

**2.** Any two: multicellular [1]; no cell walls [1]; no chloroplasts; feeds on organic substances made by other organisms. **[2]**

**3. (a)** Birds [1].

**(b)** Fish [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: crustaceans have more than 4 pairs of legs, insects have 3 pairs [1]; crustaceans have 2 pairs of antennae, insects have 1 pair [1]; insects usually have wings, crustaceans do not; insects have 3 body parts. **[2]**

**5.** A protein coat [1] surrounding genetic material [1]. **[2]**

**6.** A: crustacean [1]. B: arachnid [1]. C: insect [1]. D: myriapod [1]. E: insect [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* E has no wings, but 3 pairs of legs, 3 body parts and 1 pair of antennae still make it an insect. Wings are "usually" present, not a deciding feature.

**7.** Fins and living in water do not decide the group [1]. The dolphin breathes air with lungs, not gills [1], and feeds its young on milk from mammary glands, a feature only of mammals, so it is a mammal [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** Amphibians [1].

**(b)** Reptiles have dry, scaly skin, but the salamander's skin is moist and has no scales [1]. Reptiles lay eggs with rubbery shells on land, whereas the salamander lays eggs in water and its young have gills [1]. **[3]**

**9.** P: protoctist [1]. Q: prokaryote [1]. R: fungus [1]. S: plant [1]. T: animal [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* P has chloroplasts but is single-celled, so it is a protoctist, not a plant; the plant kingdom is multicellular.

**10. (a)** Ferns [1].

**(b)** Fronds [1].

**(c)** Flowers, or seeds [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** Dicotyledons [1].

**(b)** Leaves with a branching network of veins [1]; flower parts in fours or fives, or multiples of them [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Yeast has a nucleus [1], whereas prokaryotes have no nucleus [1]. Yeast has a cell wall not made of cellulose and no chloroplasts, and feeds by absorbing substances, which are features of fungi [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Classifying by habitat ("lives in water, so it is a fish") rather than by features.
- Treating wings as essential for insects.
- Confusing legs with pairs of legs when counting.
- (Extended) Calling a single-celled organism with chloroplasts a plant.
- (Extended) Forgetting that ferns have no flowers or seeds.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many features should I give when I classify an organism?**
Give the group and at least one deciding feature from the evidence; two features make the answer secure.

**Are viruses in a kingdom?**
No. The syllabus requires only that a virus has a protein coat and genetic material.
