Data: ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F m⁻¹, so 1/(4πε₀) = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻²; e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; electron mass 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg. Treat air as a vacuum and spheres as point charges.

## Questions

**1.** Define absolute electric potential. State whether the potential near an isolated negative charge is positive or negative, and explain why. **[3]**

**2.** A small sphere carries a charge of +4.0 nC.
**(a)** Calculate the potential at a point 0.025 m from its centre. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the work done by an external agent in moving a +2.0 nC charge from infinity to that point. **[2]**

**3.** A charge of +6.0 nC and a charge of −3.0 nC are 0.20 m apart.
**(a)** Calculate the potential at the midpoint of the line joining them. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the point between the charges, measured from the +6.0 nC charge, where the potential is zero. **[3]**

**4.** An alpha particle (charge +2e, mass 6.64 × 10⁻²⁷ kg) starts from rest and is accelerated through a potential difference of 1.8 kV in a vacuum. Calculate its kinetic energy and its final speed. **[4]**

**5.** The potential V is measured at distances r from the centre of a small charged sphere.

| r / cm | 4.0 | 6.0 | 8.0 | 10.0 | 12.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V / V | 540 | 360 | 270 | 216 | 180 |

**(a)** Show that the data fit V ∝ 1/r. **[2]**
**(b)** Determine the charge on the sphere. **[2]**
**(c)** Use the slope of the line between r = 6.0 cm and r = 10.0 cm to estimate the field strength at 8.0 cm, and explain why this is larger than the value from E = Q/(4πε₀r²). **[2]**

**6.** At 0.20 m from a point charge the field strength is 9.0 × 10³ V m⁻¹.
**(a)** Calculate the charge. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the field strength at 0.50 m. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the potential difference between 0.20 m and 0.50 m. **[2]**
**(d)** A student estimates the same potential difference from the area of a trapezium under the E against r graph. Calculate the estimate and say whether it is too large or too small. **[2]**

**7.** A parallel plate capacitor has square plates of side 0.12 m, separated by 0.80 mm of air.
**(a)** Calculate its capacitance. **[2]**
**(b)** A material of relative permittivity 2.3 fills the gap. Calculate the new capacitance and the charge on one plate at 15 V. **[3]**
**(c)** State what is meant by relative permittivity. **[1]**

**8.** Explain how a dielectric made of polar molecules increases the capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor. **[4]**

**9.**
**(a)** Calculate the energy stored in a 2200 μF capacitor charged to 12 V. **[2]**
**(b)** A 680 μF capacitor holds a charge of 1.5 mC. Calculate the pd across it and the energy stored. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how the energy stored is found from a graph of charge against pd. **[1]**

**10.** A 150 pF air-filled parallel plate capacitor is charged to 50 V and disconnected from the supply. A slab of relative permittivity 4.0, filling the gap, is then slid in.
**(a)** Calculate the charge and the energy stored before the slab is inserted. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the capacitance, pd and energy stored after insertion. **[3]**
**(c)** Account for the change in stored energy. **[1]**
**(d)** The experiment is repeated with the supply left connected. Calculate the charge and energy stored after insertion, and say where the extra energy comes from. **[3]**

**11.** Two parallel plates, each of area 0.015 m², are 6.0 mm apart in air with 900 V across them.
**(a)** Calculate the capacitance. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the charge on one plate and the energy stored. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the field strength between the plates. **[1]**
**(d)** An electron is released from rest at the negative plate. Calculate its kinetic energy and speed after it has moved 2.0 mm. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Work done per unit positive charge [1] when a small test charge is moved in from infinity to the point [1]. Negative: the positive test charge is attracted, so the work done by the external agent bringing it from infinity (where V = 0) is negative [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Include "per unit charge", "from infinity" and "small test charge".

**2. (a)** V = Q/(4πε₀r) = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 4.0 × 10⁻⁹ / 0.025 [1] = **1.4 × 10³ V** (1439 V) [1]
**(b)** ΔW = QΔV = 2.0 × 10⁻⁹ × 1439 [1] = **2.9 × 10⁻⁶ J** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Carry the unrounded value from (a) into (b) and show the substitution.

**3. (a)** Each charge is 0.10 m away. V = 8.99 × 10⁹ × (6.0 − 3.0) × 10⁻⁹ / 0.10 [1] = **2.7 × 10² V** [1]
**(b)** Let x be the distance from the +6.0 nC charge: 6.0/x = 3.0/(0.20 − x) [1]. 6.0(0.20 − x) = 3.0x, so 1.2 = 9.0x [1], giving x = **0.13 m** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the two terms set equal, with the right distance for each charge.

**4.** ΔW = QΔV = 2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 1.8 × 10³ [1] = **5.8 × 10⁻¹⁶ J** [1]. ½mv² = 5.76 × 10⁻¹⁶ so v = √(2 × 5.76 × 10⁻¹⁶ / 6.64 × 10⁻²⁷) [1] = **4.2 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A charge of e instead of 2e halves the energy. State the charge you use.

**5. (a)** Product Vr: 540 × 0.040 = 21.6, 360 × 0.060 = 21.6, 270 × 0.080 = 21.6 (and the same for the others) [1]. Vr is constant, so V ∝ 1/r [1]
**(b)** Vr = Q/(4πε₀), so Q = 21.6 / 8.99 × 10⁹ [1] = **2.4 × 10⁻⁹ C** [1]
**(c)** E ≈ ΔV/Δr = (360 − 216) / 0.040 = **3.6 × 10³ V m⁻¹** [1]. Formula: 8.99 × 10⁹ × 2.4 × 10⁻⁹ / 0.080² = 3.4 × 10³ V m⁻¹. The V–r curve is convex, so the chord slope is steeper than the slope at the midpoint [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* For "show that", work the product for several pairs of readings. In (c), say why the values differ.

**6. (a)** Q = E × 4πε₀r² = 9.0 × 10³ × 0.20² / 8.99 × 10⁹ [1] = **4.0 × 10⁻⁸ C** [1]
**(b)** E ∝ 1/r², so E = 9.0 × 10³ × (0.20/0.50)² = **1.44 × 10³ V m⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** ΔV = (Q/4πε₀)(1/0.20 − 1/0.50) = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 4.0 × 10⁻⁸ × 3.0 [1] = **1.1 × 10³ V** [1]
**(d)** Area = ½(9.0 × 10³ + 1.44 × 10³) × 0.30 = **1.6 × 10³ V** [1]. Too large, because the curve lies below the straight line joining its end points [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the curve's shape to the direction of the error; "the graph is curved" alone is weaker.

**7. (a)** A = 0.12² = 0.0144 m² [1]. C = ε₀A/d = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 0.0144 / 0.80 × 10⁻³ = **1.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ F** [1]
**(b)** C = 2.3 × 1.593 × 10⁻¹⁰ = **3.7 × 10⁻¹⁰ F** [1]. Q = CV = 3.66 × 10⁻¹⁰ × 15 [1] = **5.5 × 10⁻⁹ C** [1]
**(c)** The ratio of the material's permittivity to ε₀, equal to the factor by which the capacitance rises when the material fills the gap [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert 0.80 mm to metres before substituting.

**8.** With no field the polar molecules point in random directions [1]. When the plates are charged, the field acts with opposite forces on the two charged ends, forming a couple that rotates each molecule towards alignment [1]. Layers of opposite charge appear on the dielectric surfaces, creating a field opposing the applied field [1]. The net field and so the pd fall for the same plate charge, and C = Q/V rises [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the order: rotation, opposing field, smaller pd, larger C.

**9. (a)** E = ½CV² = ½ × 2200 × 10⁻⁶ × 12² [1] = **0.16 J** (0.158 J) [1]
**(b)** V = Q/C = 1.5 × 10⁻³ / 680 × 10⁻⁶ = **2.2 V** [1]. E = ½QV = ½ × 1.5 × 10⁻³ × 2.21 = **1.7 × 10⁻³ J** [1]
**(c)** The energy is the area under the Q against V line (a triangle, ½QV) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Choose the energy form that uses the data given.

**10. (a)** Q = CV = 150 × 10⁻¹² × 50 = **7.5 × 10⁻⁹ C** [1]. E = ½CV² = ½ × 150 × 10⁻¹² × 50² = **1.9 × 10⁻⁷ J** [1]
**(b)** C = 4.0 × 150 = **600 pF** [1]. V = Q/C = 7.5 × 10⁻⁹ / 600 × 10⁻¹² = **12.5 V** [1]. E = Q²/2C = (7.5 × 10⁻⁹)² / (2 × 600 × 10⁻¹²) = **4.7 × 10⁻⁸ J** [1]
**(c)** The stored energy falls by a factor of 4.0 (1.4 × 10⁻⁷ J lost); the capacitor does work pulling the slab into the gap [1]
**(d)** V stays 50 V, so Q = 600 × 10⁻¹² × 50 = **3.0 × 10⁻⁸ C** [1]. E = ½ × 600 × 10⁻¹² × 50² = **7.5 × 10⁻⁷ J** [1]. The supply moves the extra charge (2.25 × 10⁻⁸ C) onto the plates [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Say which quantity is held fixed: Q when isolated, V when connected.

**11. (a)** C = ε₀A/d = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 0.015 / 6.0 × 10⁻³ [1] = **2.2 × 10⁻¹¹ F** [1]
**(b)** Q = CV = 2.21 × 10⁻¹¹ × 900 = **2.0 × 10⁻⁸ C** [1]. E = ½CV² = ½ × 2.21 × 10⁻¹¹ × 900² [1] = **9.0 × 10⁻⁶ J** [1]
**(c)** E = V/d = 900 / 6.0 × 10⁻³ = **1.5 × 10⁵ V m⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** ΔV = Ex = 1.5 × 10⁵ × 2.0 × 10⁻³ = 300 V, so KE = eΔV = **4.8 × 10⁻¹⁷ J** [1]. v = √(2 × 4.8 × 10⁻¹⁷ / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) [1] = **1.0 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹** [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Linking (c) and (d) through ΔV = Ex avoids finding force and acceleration.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using e where the charge is 2e.
- Not converting mm, cm, pF, nC or μF to SI units.
- Adding potentials as vectors, or using the midpoint distance for both charges.
- Leaving "from infinity" or "per unit charge" out of the definition.
- Using r² in the potential formula.
- Writing capacitor energy as QV rather than ½QV.
- Not saying whether Q or V is held constant when a dielectric goes in.
- Describing dielectric action without the opposing field.
