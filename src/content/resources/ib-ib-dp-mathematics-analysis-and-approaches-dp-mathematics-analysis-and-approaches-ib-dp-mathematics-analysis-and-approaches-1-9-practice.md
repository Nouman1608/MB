## Section A

**1.** (calculator-free)

**(a)** Use the formula nCr = n!/(r!(n − r)!) to show that 9C4 = 126. **[2]**
**(b)** Hence write down the value of 9C5. **[1]**

**2.** (calculator-free) Expand (x − 3)⁴ fully. **[3]**

**3.** (calculator-free) Find the fourth term in the expansion of (2 + x)⁷ in ascending powers of x. **[2]**

**4.** (calculator allowed) Use technology to find the two values of r for which 14Cr = 1001. **[2]**

**5.** (calculator-free) The coefficient of x³ in the expansion of (1 + kx)⁶ is −160. Find k. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** (calculator-free)

**(a)** Find the first four terms in the expansion of (1 + x/2)⁵ in ascending powers of x. **[3]**
**(b)** Hence find an approximation to 1.005⁵, giving your answer to 6 decimal places. **[2]**

**7.** (calculator-free) Find the constant term in the expansion of (2x² − 1/x)⁶. **[4]**

**8.** (calculator-free) Find the coefficient of x⁵ in the expansion of (1 − 2x)(1 + x)⁷. **[3]**

**9.** (calculator-free) The first three terms in the expansion of (1 + ax)ⁿ, in ascending powers of x, are 1 + 12x + 60x². Find the values of a and n. **[5]**

**10.** (calculator-free)

**(a)** Show that (1 + √5)⁴ = 56 + 24√5. **[3]**
**(b)** Hence write down (1 − √5)⁴ in the form p + q√5. **[1]**
**(c)** Hence find the exact value of (1 + √5)⁴ + (1 − √5)⁴. **[1]**

**11.** (calculator-free) In the expansion of (2 + x)ⁿ, where n ≥ 2, the coefficient of x² is equal to the coefficient of x. Find n. **[4]**

**12.** (calculator-free) Consider the expansion of (x + 2/x²)¹⁰.

**(a)** Show that the general term can be written as 10Cr 2ʳ x^(10 − 3r). **[1]**
**(b)** Find the term in x⁴. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the expansion has no term in x². **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 9!/(4! 5!) = (9 × 8 × 7 × 6)/(4 × 3 × 2 × 1) [1] = 3024/24 = 126 [1]
**(b)** 9C5 = 9C4 = **126**, by symmetry [1]

**2.** Coefficients 1, 4, 6, 4, 1 with a = x, b = −3 [1]. x⁴ + 4x³(−3) + 6x²(9) + 4x(−27) + 81 [1] = **x⁴ − 12x³ + 54x² − 108x + 81** [1]

**3.** Fourth term: r = 3, so 7C3 × 2⁴ × x³ [1] = 35 × 16x³ = **560x³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The fourth term has r = 3. Using r = 4 gives the fifth term, 35 × 8x⁴, which scores nothing.

**4.** Tabulate 14Cr for r = 0 to 14 [1]: **r = 4 or r = 10** [1]

**5.** 6C3 k³ = 20k³ [1]. 20k³ = −160, so k³ = −8 [1] and **k = −2** [1]

**6. (a)** 1 + 5(x/2) + 10(x/2)² + 10(x/2)³ [1] = 1 + (5/2)x + (5/2)x² + (5/4)x³ [2]
**(b)** 1 + x/2 = 1.005 gives x = 0.01 [1]. 1 + 0.025 + 0.00025 + 0.00000125 = **1.025251** (6 d.p.) [1]
*Examiner insight:* The substitution is x/2 = 0.005, so x = 0.01, not 0.005. Choosing x is a method mark in its own right.

**7.** General term: 6Cr (2x²)⁶⁻ʳ(−1/x)ʳ = 6Cr 2⁶⁻ʳ(−1)ʳ x^(12 − 3r) [1]. Constant term: 12 − 3r = 0, so r = 4 [1]. Term = 6C4 × 2² × (−1)⁴ [1] = 15 × 4 = **60** [1]

**8.** From (1 + x)⁷: x⁵ coefficient 7C5 = 21 and x⁴ coefficient 7C4 = 35 [1]. x⁵ in the product: 1 × 21x⁵ + (−2x) × 35x⁴ [1], so the coefficient is 21 − 70 = **−49** [1]

**9.** na = 12 [1] and nC2 a² = n(n − 1)a²/2 = 60 [1]. So n(n − 1)a² = 120, and (na)² = 144. Dividing: (n − 1)/n = 120/144 = 5/6 [1], so **n = 6** [1] and **a = 2** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check: (1 + 2x)⁶ = 1 + 12x + 60x² + …. A quick check like this catches an algebra slip before you move on.

**10. (a)** 1 + 4√5 + 6(√5)² + 4(√5)³ + (√5)⁴ [1] = 1 + 4√5 + 30 + 20√5 + 25 [1] = 56 + 24√5 [1]
**(b)** Replacing √5 by −√5 changes the sign of the odd powers only: **56 − 24√5** [1]
**(c)** **112** [1]

**11.** Coefficient of x: nC1 × 2ⁿ⁻¹ = n2ⁿ⁻¹ [1]. Coefficient of x²: nC2 × 2ⁿ⁻² = (n(n − 1)/2)2ⁿ⁻² [1]. Equating and dividing by n2ⁿ⁻²: (n − 1)/2 = 2 [1], so **n = 5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check: (2 + x)⁵ has coefficients 80 for x and 80 for x². Dividing by n is allowed because n ≥ 2.

**12. (a)** 10Cr x¹⁰⁻ʳ(2x⁻²)ʳ = 10Cr 2ʳ x^(10 − r − 2r) = 10Cr 2ʳ x^(10 − 3r) [1]
**(b)** 10 − 3r = 4, so r = 2 [1]. Term = 10C2 × 2² x⁴ [1] = **180x⁴** [1]
**(c)** 10 − 3r = 2 would need r = 8/3 [1]. r must be a whole number from 0 to 10, so no term in x² exists [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving the sign or coefficient of b outside the power: (−3)³ = −27, (x/2)² = x²/4.
- Counting terms from r = 1.
- Finding only one of the two contributions in a product of brackets.
- Solving for n or a and not checking the result.
- Stating "there is no x² term" without showing that r would not be a whole number.
