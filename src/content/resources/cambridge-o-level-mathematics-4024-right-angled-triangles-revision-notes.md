These notes summarise section 6.2, Right-angled triangles, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Full explanations are in the [right-angled triangles study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-right-angled-triangles/), and exam-style questions are in the [right-angled triangles practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-right-angled-triangles-practice/).

## What is examined

- Sine, cosine and tangent for acute angles in right-angled triangles.
- Two-dimensional problems using Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry.
- The perpendicular distance from a point to a line is the shortest distance.
- Angles of elevation and depression.
- Angles in degrees, answers in degrees to 1 decimal place. Knowledge of bearings may be required.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hypotenuse | the side opposite the right angle |
| Opposite | the side across from the angle being used |
| Adjacent | the side next to the angle, other than the hypotenuse |
| Angle of elevation | angle above the horizontal, looking up |
| Angle of depression | angle below the horizontal, looking down |
| Bearing | angle clockwise from north |
| Inverse function | sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹ on the calculator |

## The ratios

```
sin θ = O / H
cos θ = A / H
tan θ = O / A
```

SOH CAH TOA.

## Choosing a ratio

| Known sides | Ratio |
|---|---|
| opposite and hypotenuse | sin |
| adjacent and hypotenuse | cos |
| opposite and adjacent | tan |

## Method box: finding a side

1. Mark the angle given and label O, A and H.
2. Choose the ratio that links the side you know with the side you want.
3. Write the equation. Rearrange.
4. If the unknown is the denominator: unknown = known ÷ ratio.
5. Calculate with the calculator in degree mode.

```
opposite  = H × sin θ
adjacent  = H × cos θ
opposite  = A × tan θ
H = O ÷ sin θ        H = A ÷ cos θ        A = O ÷ tan θ
```

## Method box: finding an angle

1. Write the ratio as a fraction of two known sides.
2. Use the inverse function.
3. Round to 1 decimal place.
4. The other acute angle is 90° minus the first.

```
tan θ = 5/12  →  θ = tan⁻¹(5/12) = 22.6°
sin θ = 7/10  →  θ = 44.4°
cos θ = 4/7   →  θ = 55.2°
```

## Applications

| Situation | Triangle |
|---|---|
| tower or building | horizontal distance, height, line of sight |
| cliff and boat | angle of depression equals the angle at the boat |
| ramp or slope | length, height, angle |
| ladder | wall, ground, ladder |
| bearings | north–south and east–west legs |
| isosceles triangle | split into two right-angled triangles |
| shortest distance | perpendicular from the point to the line |

## Bearings

For a distance d on bearing θ:

```
north component = d cos θ
east component  = d sin θ
```

For bearings between 90° and 270°, work with the acute angle to the north–south line. For 125°, the south component is d cos 55°.

## Elevation and depression

- Both angles are measured from the horizontal.
- The angle of depression from A to B equals the angle of elevation from B to A.
- If an observer's eye is above the ground, add the eye height at the end.

## Two-triangle problems

Use algebra when two observation points are given. Call the unknown height h and write each distance in terms of h. For elevations of 30° and 45° from points 20 m apart, h = 27.3 m.

## Worked patterns

```
Tower 50 m away, elevation 32°:            h = 50 × tan 32° = 31.2 m
Cliff 80 m high, depression 25°:           d = 80 ÷ tan 25° = 172 m
Ship 20 km on 070°:                         east 18.8 km, north 6.84 km
Isosceles, sides 10, apex 50°:              base 8.45, height 9.06
Ramp 5 m long rising 0.8 m:                 sin θ = 0.16, θ = 9.2°
Point 12 cm from A, angle 35° to the line:  shortest distance 12 × sin 35° = 6.88
```

## Checks before you finish

1. Is the answer a sensible size? The hypotenuse is the longest side.
2. Does an angle of 30° give a side about half the hypotenuse for sin?
3. Have you used degrees, to one decimal place for angles?
4. Does the answer to a bearings problem match a quick sketch?

## Must-know distinctions

| Idea | Be clear that |
|---|---|
| sin and cos | each uses the hypotenuse |
| tan | no hypotenuse |
| Elevation vs depression | up vs down from the horizontal |
| Bearing vs angle from east | bearings start from north |
| sin θ and sin⁻¹ | use the inverse for angles |

## Exam traps

- Calculator in radian or gradian mode.
- Labelling sides from the wrong angle.
- Using sin when tan is needed.
- Multiplying when you should divide for a hypotenuse.
- Rounding before the end.
- Confusing elevation and depression.

| Command word | What to do |

## Self-test

1. A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 10 cm and an angle of 30°. Find the opposite side.
2. A ship sails 20 km on a bearing of 048°. Find how far north it travels, to 3 significant figures.
3. Find the angle with tan θ = 3/5.
4. Find the angle with cos θ = 4/7.
5. From a point 15 m from a wall base, the angle of elevation of the top is 25°. Find the height of the wall.
6. A pole 30 m tall casts a line of sight at 55° elevation. How far is the observer from the base?
7. A right-angled triangle has opposite side 3 cm and hypotenuse 8 cm. Find the angle.
8. A ladder 8 m long makes an angle of 20° with the wall. How far is its foot from the wall?
9. A ramp rises 12 m. Its angle is 36°. Find its length.
10. The opposite side is 7 cm and the adjacent side is 24 cm. Find the angle.

### Answers

1. 10 × sin 30° = **5 cm**.
2. 20 × cos 48° = **13.4 km**.
3. tan⁻¹(0.6) = **31.0°**.
4. cos⁻¹(4/7) = **55.2°**.
5. 15 × tan 25° = **6.99 m**.
6. 30 ÷ tan 55° = 21.0, so the observer is **21.0 m** from the base.
7. sin⁻¹(3/8) = **22.0°**.
8. The angle with the wall is 20°, so the foot is 8 × sin 20° = **2.74 m** from the wall.
9. 12 ÷ sin 36° = **20.4 m**.
10. tan⁻¹(7/24) = **16.3°**.
