These questions cover section 3.5 Accounts analysis (points 3.5.1 to 3.5.3) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1) specification, Issue 2, February 2026. The ratio formulae are given in the specification's Appendix 4 and in the examination; they are also listed in the [accounts analysis revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-accounts-analysis-revision-notes/). Worked examples are in the [accounts analysis study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-accounts-analysis/). Every business is invented and amounts are in dollars. Give percentages to 1 decimal place and ratios to 2 decimal places where needed.

## Questions

### Section A: shorter questions

**1.** Define liquidity. **[2]**

**2.** Give two profitability ratios. **[2]**

**3.** Tamar Tiles had revenue of 240,000, cost of sales of 156,000 and other operating expenses of 60,000. **(Calculation)**

**(a)** Calculate the gross profit margin. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the operating profit margin. **[2]**

**4.** Oriel Books buys a book for 12.50 and sells it for 20.00. **(Calculation)**

**(a)** Calculate the markup. **[2]**
**(b)** Oriel wants a markup of 75% on a book that costs 16.00. Calculate the selling price. **[1]**

**5.** Sandby Motors has current assets of 64,000, including inventory of 40,000. Its current liabilities are 48,000. **(Calculation)**

**(a)** Calculate the current ratio. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the acid test ratio. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why the acid test ratio is a stricter test of liquidity than the current ratio. **[3]**

**7.** Outline one reason why a business should compare its ratios with those of a competitor. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**8.** Winslade Hotels had operating profit of 180,000 and capital employed of 1,500,000 in Year 1. In Year 2, after opening a new hotel, operating profit was 210,000 and capital employed was 2,100,000.

**(a)** Calculate ROCE for each year. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse why ROCE fell even though operating profit rose. **[4]**

**9.** Halston Electrical is a wholesaler. Its ratios and those of a similar rival are:

| | Halston | Rival |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin | 30% | 34% |
| Operating profit margin | 6% | 9% |
| Current ratio | 0.9 : 1 | 1.6 : 1 |
| Acid test ratio | 0.4 : 1 | 1.0 : 1 |

Analyse one concern these figures would raise for a supplier deciding whether to give Halston credit. **[4]**

**10.** Brightlane Cosmetics has current assets of 90,000, made up of inventory 30,000, trade receivables 15,000 and cash 45,000. Its current liabilities are 40,000, including a bank overdraft of 24,000. The owner has two choices for 24,000 of the cash.

- **Option A:** repay the overdraft in full.
- **Option B:** buy 24,000 of extra inventory for a summer promotion.

**(a)** Calculate the current ratio and acid test ratio now. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the acid test ratio after each option. **[2]**
**(c)** Justify which option Brightlane Cosmetics should choose. **[6]**

**11.** Rowan Dairy's figures for two years are:

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 800,000 | 900,000 |
| Gross profit | 240,000 | 252,000 |
| Operating profit | 64,000 | 81,000 |
| Capital employed | 640,000 | 675,000 |
| Gross profit margin | 30.0% | ? |
| Operating profit margin | 8.0% | 9.0% |
| ROCE | 10.0% | ? |
| Current ratio | 1.4 : 1 | 1.1 : 1 |
| Acid test ratio | 0.9 : 1 | 0.6 : 1 |

**(a)** Calculate the gross profit margin and ROCE for Year 2. **[4]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether Rowan Dairy's financial performance improved in Year 2. **[9]**

**12.** **(a)** Give one limitation of using ratios to judge a business's performance. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain one way a business could improve its gross profit margin. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The ability of a business to pay its short-term debts as they fall due [1], using cash or assets that can quickly be turned into cash [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two of: gross profit margin, operating profit margin, markup, ROCE [1 each]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** Gross profit = 240,000 − 156,000 = 84,000 [1]; 84,000 ÷ 240,000 × 100 = **35.0%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Operating profit = 84,000 − 60,000 = 24,000 [1]; 24,000 ÷ 240,000 × 100 = **10.0%** [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** Profit per item = 20.00 − 12.50 = 7.50 [1]; 7.50 ÷ 12.50 × 100 = **60.0%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 16.00 × 1.75 = **28.00** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Markup divides by the cost, not the selling price. Dividing 7.50 by 20.00 gives 37.5%, which is the margin on the price, not the markup.

**5. (a)** 64,000 ÷ 48,000 = **1.33 : 1** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** (64,000 − 40,000) ÷ 48,000 [1] = **0.50 : 1** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The acid test leaves out inventory [1], because inventory has to be sold, and often paid for by customers, before it becomes cash [1]. So the acid test only counts assets that are cash or close to cash, which shows more strictly whether short-term debts can be paid on time [1]. **[3]**

**7.** A ratio on its own does not show whether the figure is strong or weak [1], so comparing with a similar competitor shows whether the business is performing better or worse than others in the same market [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** Year 1: 180,000 ÷ 1,500,000 × 100 = **12.0%** [1]. Year 2: 210,000 ÷ 2,100,000 × 100 = **10.0%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Capital employed rose by 600,000, which is 40%, while operating profit rose by only 30,000, about 16.7% [1]. The new hotel needed a large investment [1], but a new hotel may take time to build up bookings, so it has not yet earned its full profit [1]. As a result each dollar invested earned less, although ROCE may recover as the new hotel becomes established [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Halston's current ratio is below 1 : 1 and its acid test is only 0.4 : 1, against the rival's 1.0 : 1 [1]. This means Halston does not have enough current assets to cover its short-term debts, and without inventory it has only 0.40 for every 1 owed [1]. A supplier offering credit is adding to those short-term debts [1], so there is a risk Halston pays late or not at all, especially as its low operating margin of 6% leaves little profit to fall back on [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** Current ratio = 90,000 ÷ 40,000 = **2.25 : 1** [1]. Acid test = (90,000 − 30,000) ÷ 40,000 = **1.50 : 1** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Option A: current assets 66,000, inventory 30,000, current liabilities 16,000; acid test = 36,000 ÷ 16,000 = **2.25 : 1** [1]. Option B: current assets 90,000, inventory 54,000, current liabilities 40,000; acid test = 36,000 ÷ 40,000 = **0.90 : 1** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** For Option A: repaying the overdraft removes interest costs [1] and greatly strengthens liquidity, with the acid test rising to 2.25 : 1 [1]. Against Option A: there is no extra stock for the promotion, so possible summer sales are lost [1]. For Option B: more inventory could raise revenue and profit in the summer [1]. Against Option B: the acid test falls below 1 : 1 to 0.90 : 1, so Brightlane relies on selling the stock to pay its bills, and the overdraft interest continues [1]. Judgement: for example, Option A, because the business keeps 21,000 in cash and a strong liquidity position, and it could fund a smaller promotion later from that cash; Option B only if the promotion's sales are very likely [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The current ratio is 2.25 : 1 under Option B as well, so it hides the change. Using the acid test, as the question directs, shows why the choice matters.

**11. (a)** Gross profit margin = 252,000 ÷ 900,000 × 100 [1] = **28.0%** [1]. ROCE = 81,000 ÷ 675,000 × 100 [1] = **12.0%** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Improved: operating profit rose from 64,000 to 81,000 [1], so the operating margin rose from 8.0% to 9.0%, showing better control of running costs [1]. ROCE rose from 10.0% to 12.0%, so the capital invested is earning more [1]. Worse: gross margin fell from 30.0% to 28.0% [1], perhaps because milk prices paid to farmers rose or selling prices were cut to win sales [1]. Liquidity weakened, with the current ratio falling to 1.1 : 1 and the acid test to 0.6 : 1 [1], so Rowan may find it harder to pay short-term debts on time [1]. Judgement: profitability improved, because operating profit and ROCE rose, but this has come with weaker liquidity [1], so performance improved overall only if the business acts to rebuild its cash position, for example by reducing inventory or collecting receivables faster [1]. **[9]**

**12. (a)** Any one of: based on past figures; does not explain causes; ignores non-financial factors; one year can be distorted by one-off events; rivals may not be directly comparable [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Negotiate lower prices with suppliers [1], which reduces cost of sales [1], so each sale leaves more gross profit and the margin rises if selling prices stay the same [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Raising selling prices is also valid, but a complete answer notes that higher prices may reduce the number of sales.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing current and acid test ratios as percentages.
- Using revenue as the base for ROCE.
- Taking all current assets, rather than inventory only, out of the acid test.
- Saying a ratio is weak without comparing it with a previous year or rival.
- Giving a judgement on performance that ignores liquidity, or one that ignores profitability.
