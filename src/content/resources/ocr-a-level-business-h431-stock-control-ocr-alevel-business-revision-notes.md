## What the specification lists

- Meaning of stock control.
- Methods: stock control charts, just in time (JIT), kanban, fixed reorder stock level, fixed time reordering, economic order quantity (EOQ) and electronic methods.
- Construct, calculate and interpret stock control charts: buffer stock, reorder level and quantities, lead time.
- Evaluate the impact and importance of holding and controlling stock.
- Evaluate the relationship between stock control and methods of production.

## Core recall

### Why stock matters

| Holding stock | Benefit | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Too much | Meets demand peaks, bulk discounts | Storage, insurance, cash tied up, waste, damage, out-of-date goods |
| Too little | Low holding cost | Stock-outs, idle workers, lost sales, rush orders |

### Chart terms and formulae

- **Buffer stock:** minimum level, protection against delay or demand surges.
- **Lead time:** order to delivery.
- **Reorder level** = buffer stock + (daily usage × lead time).
- **Reorder quantity:** amount ordered each time.
- **Maximum stock** = reorder level + reorder quantity − (daily usage × lead time) = buffer + reorder quantity.
- **Time between orders** = reorder quantity ÷ daily usage.
- The specification gives no formulae; these are the standard forms.

### Drawing the chart

1. Time on the horizontal axis, stock on the vertical axis.
2. Sloping line down at the usage rate.
3. Horizontal lines at buffer and reorder level.
4. Order placed at the reorder level; delivery after the lead time, when stock is at the buffer.
5. Vertical line up to the maximum on delivery.

### Methods compared

| Method | How it works | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed reorder stock level | Order a set quantity at the reorder level | Responds to actual usage | Needs constant monitoring |
| Fixed time reordering | Order at set intervals; quantity varies | Simple routine | Needs bigger buffer; may over- or under-order |
| EOQ | Order size with lowest ordering plus holding cost | Cuts total cost | Assumes steady demand and costs |
| JIT | Stock arrives when needed | Frees cash and space; less waste | One late delivery halts production |
| Kanban | Card or container signals a need for more | Pulls stock by demand | Needs discipline and reliable suppliers |
| Electronic | Real-time records, automatic orders | Accuracy and speed | Cost; system failure |

### EOQ

EOQ = √(2 × annual demand × cost per order ÷ annual holding cost per unit).

- At the EOQ, annual ordering cost equals annual holding cost.
- Average stock is taken as half the order size.
- Limits: ignores discounts, shelf life and changes in demand.

### Stock control and production methods

| Method of production | Stock control point |
|---|---|
| Job | Order for each job; long lead times for special items |
| Batch | Stock builds between stages; reorder levels and EOQ suit shared materials |
| Flow | Continuous supply essential; JIT or large buffer; kanban common |

### Evaluation points

- Cost of holding against cost of shortages.
- Cash flow and customer service.
- Reliability of suppliers and lead time.
- Perishable or fashion goods lose value quickly.
- Stakeholders: employees (idle time), suppliers (tight timetable), customers (availability), owners (cash freed or sales lost).

## Worked reminder

Hendry Paints (invented): usage 40 tins a day, lead time 5 days, buffer 150, reorder quantity 500.

- Reorder level = 150 + 40 × 5 = **350**.
- Maximum stock = 350 + 500 − 200 = **650**.
- Time between orders = 500 ÷ 40 = **12.5 days**.
- Annual demand 10,000, order cost £80, holding cost £2.50: EOQ = √(2 × 10,000 × 80 ÷ 2.5) = √640,000 = **800**.
- Total cost at 500: £1,600 + £625 = £2,225. At 800: £1,000 + £1,000 = £2,000.
- If lead time rises to 8 days, usage in the wait is 320 and the reorder level should be 470.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stock (inventory) | Materials, work in progress and finished goods held |
| Buffer stock | Minimum stock level held as protection |
| Lead time | Time from placing an order to receiving it |
| Reorder level | Stock level at which a new order is placed |
| Stock-out | Running out of stock |
| JIT | Receiving stock only when needed |
| Kanban | Card-based signal to replace used stock |
| EOQ | Order size that minimises ordering plus holding cost |

## Exam traps

- Multiply daily usage by lead time before adding the buffer.
- Subtract usage in the lead time when finding the maximum.
- Do not call JIT "always cheaper"; weigh supplier reliability.
- Buffer stock is a minimum; reorder quantity is the amount ordered.
- Show working for EOQ, and state its assumptions when evaluating.
- Link the method to the production type and product in the case.

## Self-test

1. Define lead time.
2. Usage is 30 units a day, lead time 4 days, buffer 80. Calculate the reorder level.
3. The reorder quantity is 300. Calculate the maximum stock level.
4. How many days pass between orders?
5. Give two costs of holding too much stock.
6. Give two effects of a stock-out.
7. Annual demand is 3,600, order cost is £50, holding cost is £4 a unit. Calculate EOQ.
8. What does kanban mean in stock control?
9. Why is JIT risky for flow production?
10. Give one benefit of electronic stock control.

### Answers

1. The time between placing an order and receiving it.
2. 80 + (30 × 4) = 80 + 120 = **200 units**.
3. 200 + 300 − 120 = **380 units** (also 80 + 300).
4. 300 ÷ 30 = **10 days**.
5. Any two: storage cost; insurance; money tied up; waste or damage; products going out of date.
6. Any two: lost sales; idle workers and machines; rush orders at high cost; customers lost to rivals.
7. √(2 × 3,600 × 50 ÷ 4) = √90,000 = **300 units**.
8. A card or container signals that material has been used and more should be supplied, so production is pulled by demand.
9. Continuous production stops at once if a delivery is late, and a stoppage is very costly.
10. Any one: real-time stock records; automatic reordering; fewer errors.
