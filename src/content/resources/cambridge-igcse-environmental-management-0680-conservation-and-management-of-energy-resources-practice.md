## Section A

**1.** Name **two** strategies from the syllabus that change or secure energy supply rather than reduce demand. **[2]**

**2.** State what is meant by an electrically propelled vehicle and give **one** example. **[2]**

**3.** Explain how battery storage helps a country that generates much of its electricity from solar power. **[2]**

**4.** State **one** benefit and **one** limitation of an air source heat pump. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why blue hydrogen is not automatically a low-carbon fuel. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A family is considering three improvements to its home. Costs and savings are in invented currency units.

| Improvement | Cost | Saving per year |
|---|---|---|
| Solar panels | 6,000 | 500 |
| Draught-proofing | 200 | 80 |
| Efficient new boiler | 2,400 | 300 |

**(a)** Calculate the payback time for each improvement. **[3]**

**(b)** Recommend which improvement the family should make first, and give a reason. **[2]**

**7.** An office replaces 40 lamps of 36 W with 40 LED lamps of 18 W. The lamps are on for 10 hours a day on 250 days a year.

**(a)** Calculate the total power saved, in watts. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy saved in one year, in kWh. Show your working. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest **one** reason why the actual saving might be smaller than your answer to (b). **[1]**

**8.** A city plans to replace its diesel buses with buses powered by green hydrogen.

**(a)** Describe how green hydrogen is produced. **[2]**

**(b)** Discuss the benefits and limitations of this plan. **[4]**

**9.** A city suffers from traffic congestion and poor air quality. Suggest and explain **three** transport policies that would reduce its use of petroleum. **[6]**

**10.** Explain why education on energy conservation is often described as low-cost but slow to take effect. **[3]**

**11.** Explain why heat pumps are often installed at the same time as insulation. **[3]**

**12.** Energy demand in a country is rising. Discuss whether the government should spend its money on grants for home insulation or on building new power stations. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: exploiting existing energy resources [1]; battery storage [1]; development of new energy resources [1].

**2.** A vehicle driven by electric motors instead of a petrol or diesel engine [1]. Example, any one: electric car, electric bus, electric train, tram, electric bicycle [1].

**3.** Solar panels produce surplus electricity in the middle of the day [1]; batteries store it and release it in the evening or at night when there is no sunlight, so less fossil-fuel back-up is needed [1].

**4.** Benefit, any one: cheaper and easier to install than a ground source heat pump; needs no land for trenches; no emissions on site; delivers more heat than the electricity it uses [1]. Limitation, any one: less efficient in very cold weather; needs electricity to run; high installation cost compared with a boiler; fan unit can be noisy [1].

**5.** Blue hydrogen is produced from natural gas, and the process releases carbon dioxide [1]; it is only low-carbon if that carbon dioxide is captured and stored, which is costly and not fully effective [1].

**6. (a)** Solar panels: 6,000 ÷ 500 = 12 years [1]. Draught-proofing: 200 ÷ 80 = 2.5 years [1]. Boiler: 2,400 ÷ 300 = 8 years [1].

**(b)** Draught-proofing [1], because it has the lowest cost and the shortest payback time, so it saves money soonest [1].

**7. (a)** 40 × (36 − 18) = 720 W [1].

**(b)** 720 W × 10 h = 7,200 Wh = 7.2 kWh per day [1]; 7.2 × 250 = 1,800 kWh per year [1].

**(c)** Any one: lamps may not be on for the full 10 hours every day [1]; some lamps may be left on longer, or new lamps added [1]; the office may be open on fewer days than assumed [1].

**8. (a)** Renewable electricity, for example from wind or solar power [1], is used to split water into hydrogen and oxygen [1].

**(b)** Any four, with at least one benefit and one limitation:
- Benefit: the only product from the buses is water, so air quality in the city improves [1].
- Benefit: very low carbon dioxide emissions across the whole process [1].
- Benefit: reduces the city's demand for imported diesel [1].
- Limitation: green hydrogen is currently expensive to produce [1].
- Limitation: new fuelling stations and storage are needed; hydrogen is a light, flammable gas that is hard to store [1].
- Limitation: large amounts of renewable electricity are needed [1].

**9.** Three policies, each identified and explained, 2 marks each:
- **Bus lanes or better public transport**: make buses faster and more reliable [1], so people leave cars at home and less petrol is burned [1].
- **Congestion charge**: drivers pay to enter the city centre [1], which discourages car journeys and raises money for public transport [1].
- **Cycle lanes**: safer cycling [1] replaces short car journeys [1].
- **Park-and-ride**: drivers park on the edge of the city and take a bus in [1], reducing traffic in the centre [1].
- **Car sharing or fuel taxes**: fewer cars carry more people, or higher fuel prices [1] reduce the number of journeys [1].
- **Low-emission zones or electric buses**: restrict polluting vehicles [1], encouraging efficient and electrically propelled vehicles [1].

**10.** Campaigns, energy labels and school lessons cost little compared with building new infrastructure [1]. They rely on people choosing to change their habits [1], which happens gradually and may need repeating, so savings build up slowly and are hard to measure [1].

**11.** Heat pumps deliver a steady, moderate amount of heat [1]; in a poorly insulated building, heat escapes faster than the pump can replace it [1]; insulation reduces heat loss, so the pump can keep the building warm using less electricity [1].

**12.** Levels-based. Credit up to 6 from:
- **Insulation grants, benefits**: reduce demand, so less fuel is needed in total [1]; cheaper per unit of energy saved and quick to install [1]; lower household bills [1].
- **Insulation grants, limitations**: depend on households taking up the grants [1]; some buildings are hard to insulate; savings may not keep pace with rising demand [1].
- **New power stations, benefits**: meet rising demand directly with reliable supply [1]; create jobs [1].
- **New power stations, limitations**: very high cost and long building time [1]; if fossil-fuelled, increase emissions and use finite resources [1].
- **Judgement**: for example, fund insulation first because it is cheaper and reduces the size of any new power stations needed, or a combination [1].

Maximum 6; an answer that considers only one option scores no more than 3.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 3**: writing "batteries store energy" without explaining the mismatch between when solar power is produced and when it is needed.
- **Question 6(a)**: dividing the saving by the cost, which gives a fraction of a year rather than the payback time.
- **Question 7(b)**: forgetting to convert watt-hours to kilowatt-hours, giving an answer 1,000 times too large.
- **Question 8(b)**: describing only benefits. A discuss answer needs limitations too.
- **Question 12**: giving a judgement without reasons, or discussing only one option.
