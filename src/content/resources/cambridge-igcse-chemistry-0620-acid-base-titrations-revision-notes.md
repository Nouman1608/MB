Condensed recall for **12.2 Acid–base titrations**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 (2026–2028). The full method and worked examples are in the [acid–base titrations study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-acid-base-titrations/); exam-style questions are in the [acid–base titrations practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-acid-base-titrations-practice/).

> **Tier note:** both 12.2 outcomes (describing a titration; identifying the end-point) are Core. Titration calculations belong to Topic 3 and are Supplement, marked *(Extended)*.

## The two outcomes

1. Describe an acid–base titration using a **burette**, a **volumetric pipette** and a **suitable indicator**.
2. Describe how to identify the **end-point** using an indicator.

## Apparatus checklist

| Item | Job |
|---|---|
| volumetric pipette + pipette filler | exact fixed volume (e.g. 25.0 cm³) into the flask |
| burette (50 cm³) | adds the other solution; measures the titre |
| conical flask | holds the solution; easy to swirl |
| indicator | colour change at the end-point |
| white tile | makes the colour change easy to see |
| small funnel | fills the burette; **removed** before reading |

## Method in nine words

**Rinse → fill → read → pipette → indicator → titrate → dropwise → read → repeat.**

- Rinse burette with **acid**; rinse pipette with **alkali**; flask may be rinsed with **distilled water**.
- Jet of the burette must be **full** (no air bubble).
- Swirl throughout; add **drop by drop** near the end-point.
- **Titre = final reading − initial reading.**
- Repeat until **concordant** (within about 0.10 cm³); mean of concordant titres only.

## Indicators

| Indicator | In acid | In alkali | Suitable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| methyl orange | red | yellow | yes – sharp change |
| thymolphthalein | colourless | blue | yes – sharp change |
| universal indicator | red, orange or yellow | blue or purple | **no** – gradual change through many colours, no clear end-point |

## End-point colour changes

| Titration | Thymolphthalein | Methyl orange |
|---|---|---|
| acid added to alkali in flask | blue → colourless | yellow → orange |
| alkali added to acid in flask | colourless → blue | red → orange |

Always give **both** colours.

## Recording results

- Burette readings to **2 decimal places**, ending in **0 or 5** (nearest 0.05 cm³).
- Table rows: final reading, initial reading, titre; units in the row or column labels.
- **Rough** titre: first, quick, overshoots; **not** used in the mean.

## Calculation route *(Extended)*

1. Moles of known solution = concentration × volume (in dm³).
2. Use the **equation ratio**.
3. Concentration of unknown = moles ÷ volume (in dm³).

Convert cm³ to dm³ by dividing by **1000**.

| Equation | Ratio acid : alkali |
|---|---|
| HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O | 1 : 1 |
| HNO₃ + KOH → KNO₃ + H₂O | 1 : 1 |
| H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O | 1 : 2 |

## Making a soluble salt by titration

Titrate with indicator → note volumes → **repeat without indicator** → evaporate to crystallising point → cool → filter → dry crystals.

## Errors and their effect on the titre

| Error | Effect on titre | Why |
|---|---|---|
| burette rinsed with water, not acid | larger | acid diluted, so more volume needed |
| air bubble in the burette jet | reading too large | bubble fills with acid, counted as volume added |
| funnel left in burette | unreliable reading | drips from the funnel change the level |
| end-point overshot | larger | extra acid added after neutralisation |
| flask rinsed with distilled water | no change | same amount of alkali in the flask |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Titration | adding one solution to another from a burette to find the exact volume that reacts |
| End-point | the point at which the indicator just changes colour permanently |
| Titre | volume of solution added from the burette to reach the end-point |
| Concordant titres | titres that agree closely (typically within 0.10 cm³) |
| Rough titration | quick first titration to find the approximate end-point |
| Neutralisation | acid + alkali → salt + water |

## Exam traps

- **Volumetric pipette**, not "pipette" or "measuring cylinder".
- **Universal indicator** is not suitable for titrations.
- Do not rinse the burette with water last; rinse with the solution it will hold.
- Remove the funnel before reading the burette.
- Do not average the rough titre or an outlier.
- *(Extended)* Use the equation ratio – not every titration is 1 : 1.

## Self-test

1. Name the apparatus used to measure 25.0 cm³ of alkali into the flask.
2. Why is a white tile placed under the flask?
3. Why is the burette rinsed with the acid it will contain?
4. Why is universal indicator not used?
5. State the colour change of thymolphthalein when acid is added to alkali.
6. State the colour of methyl orange in an acid.
7. A burette reads 0.40 cm³ at the start and 22.85 cm³ at the end. Calculate the titre.
8. Titres: 22.90 (rough), 22.85, 22.40, 22.50 cm³. Which are concordant, and what is the mean?
9. Why is the acid added drop by drop near the end-point?
10. Why is a titration repeated without indicator when making a salt?
11. *(Extended)* 20.00 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ HCl neutralises 25.0 cm³ of NaOH. Calculate the concentration of the NaOH.
12. *(Extended)* How many moles of NaOH react with 0.0010 mol of H₂SO₄?

**Answers:**

1. Volumetric pipette.
2. To see the colour change clearly.
3. Water would dilute the acid, changing its concentration.
4. It changes colour gradually, so there is no sharp end-point.
5. Blue to colourless.
6. Red.
7. 22.45 cm³.
8. 22.40 and 22.50 cm³ (within 0.10 cm³ of each other); 22.85 is left out. Mean = (22.40 + 22.50) ÷ 2 = 22.45 cm³.
9. So the end-point is not overshot.
10. So the salt is not contaminated by indicator.
11. Moles HCl = 0.200 × 0.0200 = 0.00400 mol = moles NaOH; 0.00400 ÷ 0.0250 = 0.160 mol/dm³.
12. 0.0020 mol.
