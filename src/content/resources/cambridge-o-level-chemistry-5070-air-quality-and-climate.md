Section 10.3 of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028) has eight learning outcomes. You must be able to state the composition of clean, dry air; state the source and the adverse effects of six pollutants; describe how carbon dioxide and methane cause global warming; "state and explain strategies to reduce the effects" of climate change and acid rain; explain how oxides of nitrogen form in car engines and how catalytic converters remove them; and describe photosynthesis with its word and symbol equations. 5070 has no Core/Extended split, so every outcome is examined for every candidate.

The broader [Chemistry of the Environment guide](/resources/chemistry-of-the-environment/) lists each pollutant's source and effect. This guide adds the equations behind each source, a step-by-step explanation of the greenhouse effect, and the reasoning examiners want in "explain" questions about strategies.

## Composition of clean, dry air

| Gas | Approximate percentage by volume |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen, N₂ | 78% |
| Oxygen, O₂ | 21% |
| Noble gases and carbon dioxide | the remaining 1% |

"Dry" matters: water vapour varies from place to place, so it is left out of the standard figures.

**Worked example 1.** A sample of 500 cm³ of clean, dry air is passed over heated copper until all the oxygen has reacted. Calculate the volume of gas remaining.

*Answer.* Oxygen = 21% of 500 cm³ = 105 cm³ [1]. Volume remaining = 500 − 105 = **395 cm³** [1]. The remaining gas is mostly nitrogen, with the noble gases and carbon dioxide.

## Pollutants: where each one comes from

Most of the six pollutants come from burning fuels. Equations make the sources easier to remember and explain.

**Carbon dioxide, from complete combustion.** With plenty of oxygen, carbon in the fuel is fully oxidised:

```
CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O
```

**Carbon monoxide and particulates, from incomplete combustion.** With too little oxygen, some carbon forms carbon monoxide, and some is left as tiny solid particles of carbon (soot):

```
2CH4 + 3O2 → 2CO + 4H2O
CH4 + O2 → C + 2H2O
```

The same fuel can therefore produce different pollutants depending on the oxygen supply. That is why a gas heater in a poorly ventilated room is dangerous.

**Methane**, from the decomposition of vegetation and from waste gases produced by digestion in animals such as cattle. It is not mainly a combustion product.

**Oxides of nitrogen**, from car engines (see the catalytic converter section below).

**Sulfur dioxide**, from burning fossil fuels that contain sulfur compounds. The sulfur in the fuel burns:

```
S + O2 → SO2
```

## The adverse effects

| Pollutant | Effect named in the syllabus |
|---|---|
| Carbon dioxide | higher levels lead to increased global warming, which leads to climate change |
| Carbon monoxide | toxic gas |
| Particulates | increased risk of respiratory problems and cancer |
| Methane | higher levels lead to increased global warming, which leads to climate change |
| Oxides of nitrogen | acid rain, photochemical smog and respiratory problems |
| Sulfur dioxide | acid rain |

Learn the exact chain for the greenhouse gases: **higher levels → increased global warming → climate change**. Writing "carbon dioxide causes acid rain" is a frequent and costly error; at this level, acid rain is linked to sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen.

## How greenhouse gases cause global warming

The syllabus limits this to two ideas: the absorption, reflection and emission of thermal energy, and reducing thermal energy loss to space. A full answer runs in a sequence.

1. Energy from the Sun reaches the Earth. Some is **reflected** back into space; the rest is **absorbed** by the Earth's surface, which warms up.
2. The warm surface **emits** thermal energy back towards space.
3. Carbon dioxide and methane in the atmosphere **absorb** some of this thermal energy.
4. These gases then **emit** thermal energy in all directions, including back towards the Earth's surface.
5. So **less thermal energy is lost to space**, and the average temperature of the Earth rises.

**Worked example 2.** Describe how increasing the amount of methane in the atmosphere leads to global warming. [3]

*Annotated answer.* "Methane absorbs thermal energy emitted from the Earth's surface [1]. It re-emits thermal energy, some of it back towards the Earth [1]. This reduces the thermal energy lost to space, so the Earth's average temperature rises [1]."

Each mark uses one of the syllabus verbs: absorb, emit, reduce loss to space. Answers that say greenhouse gases "trap the Sun's rays" or "make a hole in the ozone layer" score nothing.

## Strategies and why they work

"State and explain" means name the strategy **and** link it to the pollutant it reduces.

| Problem | Strategy | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Climate change | planting trees | trees remove carbon dioxide from the air by photosynthesis |
| Climate change | reduction in livestock farming | fewer animals produce less methane from digestion |
| Climate change | decreasing use of fossil fuels | less carbon dioxide released by combustion |
| Climate change | increasing use of hydrogen | hydrogen burns to form only water, so no carbon dioxide |
| Climate change | renewable energy, e.g. wind, solar | electricity generated without burning fuels, so no carbon dioxide |
| Acid rain | catalytic converters in vehicles | convert oxides of nitrogen into nitrogen |
| Acid rain | low-sulfur fuels | less sulfur to burn, so less sulfur dioxide |
| Acid rain | flue gas desulfurisation with calcium oxide | calcium oxide removes sulfur dioxide from waste gases |

Flue gas desulfurisation works because calcium oxide is a **basic** oxide and sulfur dioxide is an **acidic** oxide (section 7.2), so they react:

```
CaO + SO2 → CaSO3
```

## Oxides of nitrogen and catalytic converters

Air drawn into a car engine is mostly nitrogen and oxygen. Normally nitrogen is very unreactive, but the **very high temperature** inside the engine provides enough energy for nitrogen and oxygen to react:

```
N2 + O2 → 2NO
2NO + O2 → 2NO2
```

The nitrogen comes from the **air**, not from the fuel. This is the point the syllabus asks you to explain.

A **catalytic converter** in the exhaust contains a catalyst on which harmful gases react to form less harmful ones. Carbon monoxide and nitrogen monoxide react together:

```
2CO + 2NO → 2CO2 + N2
```

This is a redox reaction: carbon monoxide gains oxygen and is oxidised; nitrogen monoxide loses oxygen and is reduced. Note that the converter produces carbon dioxide, so it reduces acid rain and toxic gases but does not help with global warming.

## Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the reaction between carbon dioxide and water to produce glucose and oxygen, in the presence of **chlorophyll** and using energy from **light**.

```
carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen
6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2
```

Chlorophyll and light are written above the arrow; they are not reactants. Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide from the air, which is why planting trees is a strategy against climate change.

## Common mistakes

- Giving oxygen as 20% or nitrogen as 80%. Use 78% and 21%.
- Saying carbon monoxide comes from complete combustion.
- Linking carbon dioxide to acid rain or the ozone layer.
- Writing that oxides of nitrogen come from nitrogen in petrol. The nitrogen comes from the air.
- Saying catalytic converters remove carbon dioxide.
- Listing a strategy without explaining which pollutant it reduces.
- Writing chlorophyll or light as a reactant in the photosynthesis equation.

## Exam technique: the command words

- **State**: the percentage, the source or the effect, in a few words.
- **Describe** (global warming): absorb, emit, less thermal energy lost to space.
- **Explain** (nitrogen oxides): high temperature in the engine makes nitrogen and oxygen from the air react.
- **State and explain** (strategies): strategy, then "because" and the pollutant it reduces.
