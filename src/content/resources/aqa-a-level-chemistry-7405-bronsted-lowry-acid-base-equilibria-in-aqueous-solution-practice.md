These questions cover section **3.1.12.1 Brønsted–Lowry acid–base equilibria in aqueous solution** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [Brønsted–Lowry acid–base equilibria study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-bronsted-lowry-acid-base-equilibria-in-aqueous-solution/) and the [Acids and bases revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-acids-and-bases-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Define each term in terms of protons.

**(a)** a Brønsted–Lowry acid **[1]**

**(b)** a Brønsted–Lowry base **[1]**

**2.** Hydrogen bromide dissolves in water: HBr + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Br⁻.

**(a)** Identify the acid and the base in the forward reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the ion H₃O⁺ and state how it forms. **[2]**

**3.** For the equilibrium CH₃COOH + H₂O ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H₃O⁺, write down the two conjugate acid–base pairs. **[2]**

**4.** In each equation, label the acid on the left-hand side and its conjugate base on the right-hand side.

**(a)** HNO₃ + H₂O → NO₃⁻ + H₃O⁺ **[1]**

**(b)** NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ ⇌ NH₃ + H₂O **[1]**

**(c)** HCO₃⁻ + H₃O⁺ ⇌ H₂CO₃ + H₂O **[1]**

**5.** Ammonia is a weak base.

**(a)** Write an equation to show ammonia acting as a base in water. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain, in terms of its structure, how ammonia is able to accept a proton. **[1]**

**(c)** State the role of water in this equilibrium. **[1]**

**6.** The hydrogencarbonate ion, HCO₃⁻, is amphoteric.

**(a)** State what is meant by amphoteric. **[1]**

**(b)** Write one equation to show HCO₃⁻ acting as an acid and one to show it acting as a base, each with water as the other species. **[2]**

**7.** Sulfuric acid is a diprotic acid. Write equations for the two stages in which it reacts with water, and state which stage goes almost to completion. **[3]**

**8.** Methylamine, CH₃NH₂, is a weak base. Write an equation for its reaction with water and give the conjugate pairs. **[3]**

**9.** A student states that "a strong acid is a concentrated acid". Explain why this is incorrect and give a pair of solutions that shows the difference. **[3]**

**10.** Water is described as amphoteric.

**(a)** Write an equation for the self-ionisation of water. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how the equation shows that water behaves as both an acid and a base. **[2]**

**11.** The reaction between hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide is often described as proton transfer.

**(a)** Write the ionic equation for the reaction, showing the proton transfer with H₃O⁺. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify the spectator ions. **[1]**

**12.** The equilibrium HF + H₂O ⇌ F⁻ + H₃O⁺ lies to the left. Deduce, with a reason, whether the fluoride ion is a stronger or weaker base than the chloride ion in the equilibrium HCl + H₂O → Cl⁻ + H₃O⁺. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** An acid is a proton donor [1]

**(b)** A base is a proton acceptor [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** HBr is the acid [1]; water is the base [1]

**(b)** The oxonium ion [1]; it forms when a water molecule uses a lone pair on oxygen to accept a proton (H⁺) from HBr [1]. **[4]**

**3.** CH₃COOH / CH₃COO⁻ [1] and H₃O⁺ / H₂O [1] (acid first). **[2]**

**4. (a)** HNO₃ is the acid and NO₃⁻ is its conjugate base [1]

**(b)** NH₄⁺ is the acid and NH₃ is its conjugate base [1]

**(c)** H₃O⁺ is the acid on the left and H₂O is its conjugate base. HCO₃⁻ gains a proton so it is the base, and H₂CO₃ is its conjugate acid [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* In (b) and (c) the acid is not always the first species written. Look for the species that loses H⁺.

**5. (a)** NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ [1]

**(b)** Nitrogen has a lone pair of electrons, which forms a bond to the proton [1]

**(c)** Water acts as an acid (proton donor) [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** A species that can act as an acid (proton donor) and as a base (proton acceptor) [1]

**(b)** As an acid: HCO₃⁻ + H₂O ⇌ CO₃²⁻ + H₃O⁺ [1]. As a base: HCO₃⁻ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ + OH⁻ [1]. **[3]**

**7.** H₂SO₄ + H₂O → HSO₄⁻ + H₃O⁺ [1]; HSO₄⁻ + H₂O ⇌ SO₄²⁻ + H₃O⁺ [1]; the first stage goes almost to completion [1]. **[3]**

**8.** CH₃NH₂ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃NH₃⁺ + OH⁻ [1]; conjugate pair CH₃NH₃⁺ / CH₃NH₂ [1]; conjugate pair H₂O / OH⁻ [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Strength describes how far the acid dissociates, whereas concentration is the amount of acid per dm³ of solution [1]. For example, 0.001 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid is dilute but strong, because HCl is almost fully dissociated [1]; 5 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid is concentrated but weak, because only a small fraction of molecules dissociate [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** H₂O + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + OH⁻ [1]

**(b)** One water molecule donates a proton, so it acts as an acid [1]; the other accepts the proton, so it acts as a base [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** H₃O⁺ + OH⁻ → 2H₂O [1]

**(b)** Na⁺ and Cl⁻ [1]. **[2]**

**12.** Fluoride is the stronger base [1]; HF is a weak acid, so its conjugate base accepts protons easily (the equilibrium lies to the left), while chloride is the conjugate base of a strong acid and barely accepts protons [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining acids and bases by H⁺ and OH⁻ only, rather than proton donor and proton acceptor.
- Writing conjugate pairs with the acid and base in the wrong order or differing by more than one proton.
- Using a single arrow for a weak acid or weak base.
- Mixing up strength and concentration.
- Not saying which species has the lone pair that accepts the proton.
