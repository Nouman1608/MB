Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Level Physics (YPH11) is a modular qualification of six externally assessed units. The first three are the IAS units: Unit 1 Mechanics and Materials (WPH11/01, 1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks, 20%), Unit 2 Waves and Electricity (WPH12/01, 1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks, 20%) and Unit 3 Practical Skills in Physics I (WPH13/01, 1 hour 20 minutes, 50 marks, 10%). The second three are the IA2 units: Unit 4 Further Mechanics, Fields and Particles (WPH14/01, 1 hour 45 minutes, 90 marks, 20%), Unit 5 Thermodynamics, Radiation, Oscillations and Cosmology (WPH15/01, 1 hour 45 minutes, 90 marks, 20%) and Unit 6 Practical Skills in Physics II (WPH16/01, 1 hour 20 minutes, 50 marks, 10%). Topic support on the site includes [Materials](/resources/edexcel-ial-physics-materials/), [Electric Circuits](/resources/edexcel-ial-physics-electric-circuits/) and [Further Mechanics](/resources/edexcel-ial-physics-further-mechanics/).

## How the six units fit together

The four main content units (1, 2, 4 and 5) are each 20% of the full qualification, and the two practical skills units (3 and 6) are each 10%, giving 20 + 20 + 10 + 20 + 20 + 10 = 100%. Units 3 and 6 are written examinations, not laboratory tests: the qualification assesses practical competence through a dedicated written paper. The specification also states that a minimum of 40% of marks across the papers overall target mathematics at Level 2 or above, so mathematical fluency is part of preparing for every unit, not an extra.

## Units 1 and 2: Mechanics, Materials, Waves and Electricity

Each of these is 90 minutes for 80 marks, so 90 / 80 = 1.125 minutes per mark. The pace is brisk, and many marks come from short calculations and explanations. **Preparation priority**: build a routine for every calculation (write the equation, substitute with units, calculate, check the order of magnitude), and practise the descriptive explanations that connect a principle to a situation. Units 1 and 2 together are 40% of the qualification.

## Units 4 and 5: the heavier content units

Units 4 and 5 are each 105 minutes for 90 marks, so 105 / 90 = about 1.17 minutes per mark. They cover Further Mechanics, Fields and Particles, and Thermodynamics, Radiation, Oscillations and Cosmology. **Preparation priority**: these units build on earlier content, so revisit forces, energy and waves while studying them. Practise multi-step problems that combine several ideas, and make sure you can derive or recall the relationships the specification expects.

## Units 3 and 6: Practical Skills in Physics I and II

Each is 80 minutes for 50 marks, or 80 / 50 = 1.6 minutes per mark, and each is 10%. Because these are written papers, you prepare for them by doing practical work and then practising the written skills: planning a method, identifying variables, recording and processing data, plotting and interpreting graphs, estimating uncertainties and evaluating a procedure. **Preparation priority**: when you do a practical in class, write down afterwards what the dependent variable was, what the main uncertainty was and one realistic improvement. That habit is exactly what the written papers test.

## Total time and marks

Sitting all six units means 90 + 90 + 80 + 105 + 105 + 80 = 550 minutes of examination for 80 + 80 + 50 + 90 + 90 + 50 = 440 marks. The IAS units (1 to 3) total 260 minutes and 210 marks, and the IA2 units (4 to 6) total 290 minutes and 230 marks.

## Command words and question styles

Common command words are state, define, describe, explain, calculate, determine, show that, sketch and justify. "Show that" requires the working to lead to the given value, so show every line. "Determine" often means to use data from a graph or table. **Preparation priority**: practise reading graphs for gradients and intercepts, and always include units and an appropriate number of significant figures.

## Worked practice scenario: Young modulus from a wire

Question (original): "A steel wire of diameter 0.50 mm and original length 2.000 m carries a load of 60 N and extends by 3.0 mm. Calculate the Young modulus of the steel."

```
Step 1  Area A = pi x (d/2)^2 = pi x (0.25 x 10^-3)^2
        = 1.96 x 10^-7 m^2
Step 2  Stress = F / A = 60 / 1.96 x 10^-7 = 3.06 x 10^8 Pa
Step 3  Strain = extension / original length
        = 0.0030 / 2.000 = 1.5 x 10^-3
Step 4  Young modulus = stress / strain
        = 3.06 x 10^8 / 1.5 x 10^-3 = 2.0 x 10^11 Pa
```

The answer is given to two significant figures, matching the least precise data. Converting millimetres to metres before substituting is the step where candidates most often slip.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Know the unit codes and which units are in your entry.
- Check your calculator mode and bring spare batteries or a spare calculator.
- Practise unit conversions, powers of ten and significant figures.
- Revisit earlier units while studying Units 4 and 5.
- Rehearse written practical skills for Units 3 and 6.
- Underline the command word and the data given in each question.
- Show every step in "show that" questions.
- Check that final answers have units and sensible magnitudes.

## Self-test

2. How many minutes per mark does Unit 3 allow?
3. Are Units 3 and 6 laboratory practical tests?
4. How many marks do all six units total?
5. In the worked scenario, what is the strain?

**Answers:** 1. 20%. 2. 80 / 50 = 1.6 minutes per mark. 3. No, they are written examinations. 4. 440 marks. 5. 1.5 x 10^-3.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many units are there in Edexcel IAL Physics YPH11?**
Six: three IAS units (1 to 3) and three IA2 units (4 to 6), all externally assessed.

**How are the units weighted?**
Units 1, 2, 4 and 5 are 20% each, and Units 3 and 6 are 10% each.

**Is there a practical exam in the laboratory?**
Units 3 and 6 are written examinations that assess practical skills.

**How long is Unit 5?**
Unit 5 is 1 hour 45 minutes and 90 marks.

**When was the qualification first assessed?**
First teaching was September 2018 and first examination was January 2019.
