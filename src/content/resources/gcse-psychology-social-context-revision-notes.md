## Paper 2 at a glance

Four topics, each pairing a "nature" (biological) explanation with a "nurture" (psychological) one —
spot this pattern and comparison questions become far easier.

| Topic | Core content |
|---|---|
| 2.1 Social influence | Conformity (Asch), obedience (Milgram), bystander behaviour (Piliavin), crowd behaviour |
| 2.2 Language, thought, communication | Piaget vs Sapir-Whorf, human/animal communication, non-verbal behaviour |
| 2.3 Brain and neuropsychology | Nervous system, neurons, brain structure, scanning techniques |
| 2.4 Psychological problems | Depression and addiction: biological vs psychological explanations and treatments |

## Piaget vs Sapir-Whorf — opposite claims

**Piaget:** language depends on thought (thought develops first). **Sapir-Whorf:** thinking depends on
language (language shapes thought). These are opposite positions, not variations on the same idea —
confusing them is one of the most common Paper 2 errors.

## Conformity vs obedience — keep separate

Distinct processes with distinct named studies: **conformity** (Asch — majority influence, social and
dispositional factors) and **obedience** (Milgram's Agency Theory — agency, authority, culture,
proximity, plus Adorno's dispositional Authoritarian Personality). Treating them as the same process
loses marks.

## Worked example: comparing explanations of depression

```
BIOLOGICAL:      serotonin imbalance in the brain -- treated with
                 antidepressant medication
PSYCHOLOGICAL:   negative schemas/attributions learned through
                 experience -- treated with CBT
COMPARISON:      biological treats depression as happening TO the
                 brain (nature); psychological treats it as learned
                 (nurture) -- note treatments often combine both in
                 practice, and neither explanation alone accounts for
                 why not everyone exposed to the same risk factor
                 develops depression
```

Listing both explanations without explicitly comparing them will not reach the higher mark bands when
a question asks for comparison specifically.

## The four brain lobes, fast recall

Learn with a labelled diagram, not a bullet list — this consistently makes brain and neuropsychology
content stick better, since it is the topic students find least intuitive.

## Non-verbal communication and its explanations

Non-verbal behaviour (eye contact, body language, postural echo, touch, personal space) is explained
two ways: **Darwin's evolutionary account** (non-verbal signals are innate, evolved for survival value)
versus evidence that some non-verbal behaviour is **learned** (for example, Yuki's cross-cultural study
of emoticon use showing culturally specific patterns). As with the other three topics, prepare both
explanations side by side rather than just one.

## The nervous system and fight-or-flight

Learn the autonomic nervous system's role in the fight-or-flight response alongside the James-Lange
theory of emotion (that physiological arousal precedes and causes the subjective experience of
emotion, rather than the reverse) — a frequently tested application point when a scenario describes a
stressful situation and asks candidates to explain the resulting physical and emotional response using
named psychological theory rather than everyday description.

## Exam traps

- Confusing Piaget's and Sapir-Whorf's positions on language and thought.
- Treating conformity and obedience as the same process instead of distinct, separately studied
  phenomena.
- Confusing addiction/dependence with substance misuse when defining addiction.
- Treating antidepressants and CBT as competing rather than complementary — both are valid on real
  mark schemes.
- Naming a study (Asch, Milgram, Piliavin, Penfield, Tulving, Wiles, Kaij) without being able to say
  what it actually did.

## Human vs animal communication

Von Frisch's bee study (the "waggle dance" communicating the direction and distance of a food source)
is the specification's named example contrasting animal communication with human language. The key
distinguishing point examiners look for: human language has the property of displacement (talking
about things not present, past or future events) and productivity (generating infinite new
sentences), whereas most animal communication systems studied are limited to a fixed, immediate
repertoire of signals. Learn Von Frisch's study alongside this distinction, not as an isolated fact.

## Addiction: characteristics and interventions

Mirror the depression structure for addiction. **Characteristics**: the ICD dependence syndrome
(tolerance, withdrawal, compulsion to use, difficulty controlling use). **Explanations**: biological
(genetic vulnerability, evidenced by Kaij's twin study) versus psychological (peer influence).
**Interventions**: aversion therapy (biological/behavioural route) versus self-management programmes
(psychological route). As with depression, a strong answer pairs each explanation with its matching
intervention rather than listing explanations and treatments as separate, disconnected lists.

## Command words on Paper 2

Paper 2 uses the same command-word conventions as Paper 1: "describe" asks for accurate detail without
evaluation, "explain" requires a reasoned chain of "because" statements, and "evaluate" or "discuss"
requires balanced strengths-and-weaknesses judgement leading to a supported conclusion. Since Paper 2
covers memory, perception, development, social influence and addiction across a wide range of studies
and explanations, practising the same command words across different content areas builds the
transferable exam skill faster than restricting practice to a single topic.

## Self-test

1. State Piaget's and Sapir-Whorf's positions on language and thought.
2. Name the named study associated with obedience, and the theory it supports.
3. What two explanations does the specification pair for depression, and their matching treatments?
4. Why does listing both explanations of depression without comparing them lose marks?
5. What is the most effective way to revise the four brain lobes?

**Answers:** 1. Piaget: language depends on thought; Sapir-Whorf: thinking depends on language — these
are opposite claims. 2. Milgram's study, supporting the Agency Theory of obedience. 3. Biological
(serotonin imbalance, treated with antidepressants) and psychological (negative schemas, treated with
CBT). 4. Because the question specifically asks for comparison, and simply describing both
explanations side by side does not demonstrate the comparative skill being assessed. 5. Using a
labelled diagram rather than a prose or bullet-list description.
