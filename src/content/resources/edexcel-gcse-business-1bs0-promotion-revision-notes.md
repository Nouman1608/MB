These notes condense content item 2.2.3 Promotion from Topic 2.2 Making marketing decisions in Theme 2 of the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. The [promotion study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-promotion/) has full explanations and worked examples; the [promotion practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-promotion-practice/) give exam-style practice. Businesses and figures are invented.

## The specification in one view

- **Promotion strategies for different market segments:** advertising, sponsorship, product trials, special offers, branding.
- **Technology in promotion:** targeted advertising online, viral advertising via social media, e-newsletters.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Promotion | Communicating with customers to inform and persuade them to buy |
| Advertising | Paid messages in media such as TV, radio, print, posters or websites |
| Sponsorship | Paying to support an event, team or organisation in return for the brand being shown |
| Product trial | Letting customers try a product free or cheaply before buying |
| Special offer | A temporary incentive: discount, multi-buy, free gift, loyalty points |
| Brand | The name, logo, design and image that make products recognisable and distinct |
| Targeted online advertising | Adverts shown online to people selected by age, location, interests or searches |
| Viral advertising | Content that spreads quickly as people share it on social media |
| E-newsletter | A regular email sent to people who have signed up to receive it |

## Each strategy in one line

| Strategy | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Advertising | Wide reach; medium can match segment | Paid for whether or not people buy |
| Sponsorship | Links brand with something the segment likes | Sales effect hard to measure |
| Product trials | Removes the risk of trying something new | Cost of free product; some never buy |
| Special offers | Urgency; attracts price-sensitive buyers | Cuts profit per unit; customers wait for offers |
| Branding | Recognition, trust, premium price, loyalty | Slow and costly to build; easily damaged |

## Technology methods

| Method | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Targeted online adverts | Small budgets; precise targeting; measurable | Ad blockers; can feel intrusive |
| Viral social media | Huge reach at little cost | Cannot be controlled or guaranteed |
| E-newsletters | Cheap; reaches interested customers | Needs a list; many unopened |

## Matching to segments: the method

1. Identify the segment in the case (age, income, location, lifestyle).
2. Ask where those customers spend their time and what persuades them.
3. Check the cost against the size of the business.
4. Explain the choice using a detail from the case.

| Case detail | Appropriate choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A new vegan café near a university | Free tasting trials and student offers on social media | Students are price-sensitive and active online; a trial removes the risk of a new menu |
| A plumber covering one town | Targeted online adverts by postcode and a local sponsorship | Customers are local and search when they need help |
| A premium skincare range | Branding and an e-newsletter to past buyers | Image and trust matter; repeat buyers already showed interest |

## Useful calculations

- Cost per new customer = promotion cost ÷ number of new customers (standard form).
- Effect of a discount: new contribution = (discounted price − unit variable cost) × new sales.
- Percentages from a newsletter: subscribers × open rate × click rate × buying rate.

**Quick calculation.** Hollins Hardware gives away 500 free tester pots of paint costing £0.80 each. Cost = £400. If 50 testers lead to a full tin sale with £9 contribution, the trial brings £450, a gain of **£50**.

## Exam traps

- Describing a strategy with no link to the segment.
- Treating a rise in sales from an offer as a rise in profit.
- Claiming viral content is guaranteed.
- Confusing branding with advertising.
- Choosing an expensive method for a tiny local business without comment.

## Self-test

1. Name the five promotion strategies in the specification.
2. Name the three uses of technology in promotion in the specification.
3. Define sponsorship.
4. Give one reason why product trials suit a new product.
5. Give one disadvantage of special offers.
6. Why might branding allow a business to charge a higher price?
7. An advert costs £2,400 and brings 80 new customers. Calculate the cost per new customer.
8. Give one advantage of targeted online advertising for a small business.
9. Why can a business not rely on viral advertising?
10. A newsletter goes to 2,000 people; 25% open it and 8% of openers click a link. How many click?
11. Suggest one appropriate promotion method for a luxury watch brand, with a reason.
12. A product sells 500 units at £10 with a £4 variable cost. A 10% discount raises sales to 560. Calculate the change in contribution.

### Answers

1. Advertising, sponsorship, product trials, special offers, branding.
2. Targeted advertising online, viral advertising via social media, e-newsletters.
3. Paying to support an event, team or organisation in return for the business's name or brand being displayed.
4. Customers can try it without risk, so they are more willing to switch from a product they know.
5. Lower profit per unit, or customers learn to wait for offers.
6. Customers recognise and trust the brand and see it as better or more desirable, so they will pay more.
7. £2,400 ÷ 80 = **£30**.
8. It can be done on a small budget, aimed at chosen customers, and its results can be measured.
9. Whether people share content cannot be controlled, so it may not spread, or it may spread for the wrong reasons.
10. 2,000 × 25% = 500 open; 500 × 8% = **40** click.
11. Branding or adverts in selective upmarket magazines, because image matters more than price; special offers could cheapen the brand.
12. Before: 500 × £6 = £3,000. After: 560 × (£9 − £4) = £2,800. Contribution falls by **£200**.
