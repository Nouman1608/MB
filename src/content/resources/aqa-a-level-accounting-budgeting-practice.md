## Questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following appears in a cash budget but **not** in a budgeted income statement? **[1]**

A. Depreciation of equipment
B. A loan received from the bank
C. An irrecoverable debt written off
D. Wages accrued at the end of the period

**2.** Explain **one** purpose of budgeting other than control. **[2]**

**3.** Ashby Kettles plans sales of 3 000 units in quarter 1, 3 600 in quarter 2 and 4 000 in quarter 3. Closing inventory of finished goods is 15% of the next quarter's sales. Opening inventory for quarter 1 is 420 units. Calculate the production budget for quarters 1 and 2. **[4]**

**4.** Brockwell Candles plans production of 2 000 candles in January, 2 400 in February and 2 200 in March. Each candle uses 0.4 kg of wax costing £6 per kg. Closing wax inventory is 30% of the next month's usage. Opening inventory on 1 January is 240 kg. Prepare the materials purchases budget, in kg and £, for January and February. **[5]**

**5.** Corran Tools plans to make 1 500 units in May. Each unit needs 2.5 direct labour hours. The 20 employees each work 160 normal hours a month at £15 per hour. Extra hours are paid at time-and-a-half. Calculate the labour budget for May. **[4]**

**6.** Galloway Paints makes all sales on credit: March £20 000, April £24 000, May £28 000. 40% of customers pay in the month after sale and take 2.5% discount allowed; 55% pay two months after sale; 5% are expected to become irrecoverable. Calculate the budgeted receipts from customers for May and June. **[5]**

**7.** Ivo Marsh, a sole trader, provides budget data for October to December 2026.

- Bank overdraft on 1 October: £1 200.
- Sales, all on credit, received the month after sale: September £19 000, October £20 000, November £26 000, December £30 000.
- Purchases, paid the month after purchase: September £12 000, October £15 000, November £18 000, December £14 000.
- Wages £3 400 a month, paid in the same month.
- General expenses £1 100 a month, paid the month after they are incurred.
- Depreciation £600 a month.
- A bank loan of £10 000 is received in November; loan interest of £150 is paid in December.
- New equipment costing £16 000 is paid for in November.
- Drawings £1 500 a month.

**(a)** Prepare a cash budget for each of the three months. **[8]**
**(b)** The bank has agreed an overdraft limit of £5 000. Suggest **two** actions Ivo could take, using your answer to (a). **[2]**

**8.** Nia Brook, a sole trader, had this statement of financial position on 1 January 2027: fixtures at cost £24 000, accumulated depreciation £6 000; inventory £5 000; trade receivables £7 000; bank £2 000; trade payables £4 000; capital £28 000.

Budget for the quarter ending 31 March 2027:

- Credit sales £45 000; receipts from customers £43 000.
- Credit purchases £28 000; payments to suppliers £27 000.
- Closing inventory £6 000.
- Expenses paid £9 000, including £600 rent prepaid for April. Expenses of £400 will be owing at 31 March.
- Fixtures are depreciated at 10% a year on cost.
- Drawings £4 500.

**(a)** Prepare the budgeted income statement for the quarter. **[5]**
**(b)** Prepare the budgeted statement of financial position at 31 March 2027. **[7]**

**9.** Orchard Pressings Ltd budgeted to sell 50 000 cartons of juice at £1.20 each. Budgeted variable costs per carton: materials £0.35, labour £0.25, variable overheads £0.10. Budgeted fixed overheads: £15 000. Actual results for 46 000 cartons: revenue £56 580, materials £16 560, labour £11 960, variable overheads £4 370, fixed overheads £14 600.

**(a)** Prepare a flexed budget for 46 000 cartons, showing profit. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the variance for each item and for profit against the flexed budget, stating whether each is favourable or adverse. **[4]**
**(c)** Reconcile the original budgeted profit with the actual profit. **[1]**

**10.** Using your answers to question 9, interpret the variances for the directors of Orchard Pressings Ltd. **[4]**

**11.** Fellgate Buses Ltd runs a regional bus network. For ten years its budgets have been set by adding 4% to the previous year's figures for every department, including maintenance, marketing, driver training and administration. A new finance director proposes zero-based budgeting. Evaluate the proposal. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** -- a loan received is a cash inflow but is not income [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A and C are non-cash and D is an accrual, so only B fits; one mark, no credit for two letters.

**2.** Co-ordination: the sales, production, purchasing and personnel budgets must agree [1], so that, for example, enough materials and staff are planned for the output the sales team expects [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The second mark needs the consequence explained; naming "co-ordination" alone earns one mark.

**3.** Quarter 1 closing inventory 15% × 3 600 = 540 [1]; production 3 000 + 540 − 420 = **3 120 units** [1].
Quarter 2 closing inventory 15% × 4 000 = 600 [1]; production 3 600 + 600 − 540 = **3 660 units** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Using quarter 1's closing figure as quarter 2's opening inventory is the step most often missed; own-figure credit is usually allowed after an earlier slip.

**4.** Usage: January 800 kg, February 960 kg, March 880 kg [1]. Closing inventory: January 30% × 960 = 288 kg; February 30% × 880 = 264 kg [1].
January purchases 800 + 288 − 240 = **848 kg** [1]; February 960 + 264 − 288 = **936 kg** [1]; at £6: **£5 088** and **£5 616** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Pricing usage instead of purchases loses the final mark.

**5.** Hours needed 1 500 × 2.5 = 3 750 [1]; normal hours 20 × 160 = 3 200, so overtime 550 hours [1].
Basic 3 200 × £15 = £48 000; overtime 550 × £22.50 = £12 375 [1]; total **£60 375** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Paying all 3 750 hours at £15 ignores the capacity limit and loses the last two marks.

**6.** May: from April sales 24 000 × 40% × 97.5% [1] = £9 360 [1]; from March sales 20 000 × 55% = £11 000 [1]; total **£20 360** [1].
June: 28 000 × 40% × 97.5% = 10 920 + 24 000 × 55% = 13 200, total **£24 120** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The 5% irrecoverable debts must be left out completely; including them as a receipt loses the accuracy mark in each month.

**(b)** The limit is breached in November and December. Delay the equipment purchase beyond December, or lease it, so November's outflow falls by £16 000 [1]; negotiate a larger loan or a higher limit before November [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Including the £600 depreciation loses a mark and also every later closing balance, unless own-figure credit applies.

**9. (a)** Revenue 46 000 × £1.20 = £55 200 [1]; materials £16 100, labour £11 500, variable overheads £4 600, fixed £15 000 [1]; flexed profit **£8 000** [1].
**(b)** Revenue £1 380 F [1]; materials £460 A and labour £460 A [1]; variable overheads £230 F and fixed overheads £400 F [1]; profit **£1 090 F** (actual £9 090) [1].
**(c)** Budgeted profit £10 000 − volume variance £2 000 A (8 000 − 10 000) + flexed variances £1 090 F = **£9 090** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Flexing the fixed overheads is a common slip and loses the profit mark in (a) and the fixed overhead mark in (b).

**10.** Revenue is favourable because the price averaged £1.23, not £1.20 [1]; the higher price may explain why 4 000 fewer cartons were sold, costing £2 000 of profit [1]. Materials cost £0.36 a carton, perhaps a supplier price rise or more wastage, and labour £0.26, perhaps overtime or slower work; both need investigating [1]. Overall profit fell by £910, mainly through lower volume, so the directors should judge whether the price rise was worth it [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Interpretation needs causes linked to the figures and a judgement; restating the variances from question 9 earns nothing.

**11.** ZBB would make each department justify all its spending, so ten years of automatic 4% rises would be challenged [1]. Waste carried forward, perhaps in administration, could be removed [1]. Spending could be redirected to current priorities, such as maintenance for safety [1]. However, ZBB is slow and costly, and managers need training to prepare it [1]. Some costs, such as fuel, are essential and gain little from review [1]. Managers may favour easily measured activities and cut driver training, whose benefits are long-term [1]. Morale may fall if departments must defend their existence [1]. Judgement: Fellgate should use ZBB for discretionary budgets such as marketing and administration, perhaps every few years, and keep incremental budgeting for routine costs [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Without a reasoned conclusion full marks are out of reach, and points not tied to the bus company score less.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting depreciation, irrecoverable debts or provisions into a cash budget.
- Receiving credit sales in the wrong month, or ignoring discount allowed.
- Pricing materials usage instead of purchases.
- Recording expenses on the cash basis in a budgeted income statement.
- Flexing fixed overheads.
- Ending an evaluation without a judgement.
