Condensed recall for the **fieldwork internal assessment** (IA). For the full explanation and the
worked Spearman example, read the
[Fieldwork study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-geography-fieldwork-study-guide/). Always check the current subject guide and your teacher's instructions for exact requirements,
word limit and criteria; they take priority.

## What the IA is

An individual investigation: identify a geographical question, collect **primary data**, process and
present it, analyse it and evaluate the whole investigation in a written report. Small, well-designed
local studies usually beat ambitious ones that cannot be done carefully.

## Planning

1. A focused, answerable **question** linked to course ideas.
2. State the geographical **context** and theory.
3. **Hypothesis** (predicts a relationship or difference) and **null hypothesis** (no relationship or
   difference), which is what a statistical test examines.
4. List variables, measures and units.
5. **Sampling:** random avoids bias; systematic uses regular intervals; stratified represents each
   sub-group in proportion. Justify the method and give sample size and limits.
6. **Pilot** to expose problems; **risk assessment** and ethics (consent, respect for property and
   wildlife).

## Collecting and presenting data

Record on prepared sheets with units, times, dates and exact locations; note conditions; repeat
readings; keep raw data.

- Graphs: scatter (relationships), bar (categories), line (change).
- Maps, annotated photographs and sketches.
- Summary statistics: mean, median, range, interquartile range.
- Every figure: **title, labelled axes with units, source**, and referred to in the text.

## Statistical tests

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Spearman's rank | Strength of association between two ranked variables |
| Chi-squared | Observed against expected frequencies in categories |
| Mann-Whitney U | Compare two independent samples |

Spearman: rs = 1 - 6(sum of d squared) / n(n squared - 1). Worked example from the study guide
(invented data): sum of d squared = 166, n = 8, rs = 1 - 996 / 504 = -0.976, a very strong negative
correlation, past the critical value 0.738 at the 5 per cent level (two-tailed). Check the table
in your own materials.

## Analysis and evaluation

Describe patterns, then explain; interpret significance as the chance of such data if the null
hypothesis were true, **not proof**. Evaluate reliability and accuracy, sample size, sampling bias,
validity, limits of time, access and equipment, and realistic improvements.

## Exam traps

- A question too broad to answer with the data you can collect.
- Methods described but not justified.
- Treating correlation as proof of cause.
- A long method with a thin evaluation.

## Self-test

1. Why write a null hypothesis?
2. Distinguish systematic from stratified sampling.
3. Why run a pilot study?
4. Which test examines association between two ranked variables?
5. What must every graph include?

**Answers:** 1. Statistical tests examine whether data are consistent with no relationship or
difference. 2. Systematic uses regular intervals; stratified represents each sub-group in
proportion. 3. To find problems in methods, timing and equipment before main collection. 4.
Spearman's rank. 5. A title, labelled axes with units, and a source, referred to in the text.
