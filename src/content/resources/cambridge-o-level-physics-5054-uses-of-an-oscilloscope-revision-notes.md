Condensed notes for subtopic 4.6 Uses of an oscilloscope in Cambridge O
Level Physics 5054. For full worked examples, use the
[Oscilloscope study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-physics-5054-uses-of-an-oscilloscope/);
for exam-style questions, use the
[Oscilloscope practice questions](/resources/o-level-physics-uses-of-an-oscilloscope-practice/).

## What the syllabus asks

- Describe the use of an oscilloscope to display waveforms.
- Describe how to measure p.d. and short intervals of time using the
  Y-gain and timebase.
- The structure of an oscilloscope is **not required**.

## The screen

- A graph of **p.d. (vertical)** against **time (horizontal)**.
- Grid of **divisions** (squares); each usually split into 5 small marks,
  so read to 0.2 division.
- Centre line = 0 V when no signal is connected.

## The two controls

| Control | Unit | Use | Smaller setting makes the trace… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Y-gain | V/div | Measure p.d. | Taller |
| Timebase | s/div, ms/div, µs/div | Measure time | More spread out (fewer cycles visible) |

The settings change the **picture**, never the signal.

## Equations

```
p.d.       = vertical divisions × Y-gain
time       = horizontal divisions × timebase
period T   = divisions for one cycle × timebase
frequency  f = 1 / T
peak p.d.  = (peak-to-peak divisions ÷ 2) × Y-gain
```

## Traces to recognise

| Input | Trace with timebase on | Timebase off |
|---|---|---|
| None | Flat line on the centre | Spot at centre |
| d.c. (cell, battery) | Flat line above (or below) centre | Spot displaced up or down |
| a.c. (mains transformer, generator) | Regular wave either side of centre | Vertical line, length = peak-to-peak |
| Microphone with a steady note | Wave; height ↔ loudness, spacing ↔ pitch, shape ↔ timbre | Vertical line |
| Pulse and echo | Two separate spikes along the trace | Not useful |

## Reading method (four steps)

1. Note the settings and their units.
2. Count divisions carefully (spaces, not lines), to 0.2 division.
3. Multiply by the setting.
4. Convert units (ms → s, µs → s) before finding a frequency or speed.

## Mini examples

- Y-gain 0.5 V/div, peak 3.0 div above centre → peak p.d. = **1.5 V**.
- Peak-to-peak 6.0 div at 10 V/div → peak = 3.0 × 10 = **30 V**.
- One cycle 4.0 div at 0.5 ms/div → T = 2.0 ms → f = **500 Hz**.
- Two pulses 2.5 div apart at 20 µs/div → interval = **50 µs**.
- d.c. line jumps 2.0 div at 5 V/div → **10 V**.

## Choosing settings for precision

- Y-gain: trace as tall as possible while staying on the screen.
- Timebase: one to a few cycles, or both pulses, visible.
- Measure the biggest distance available (peak-to-peak; several cycles)
  and divide.

## Typical uses in the 5054 course

| Use | What is measured |
|---|---|
| Comparing sounds (3.4) | Amplitude, frequency and waveform shape of microphone signals |
| Echo timing | Time between a pulse and its echo, for speed or depth |
| a.c. supplies and generators | Peak p.d., period and frequency |
| Checking a d.c. supply | Steady p.d. from the line's displacement |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Waveform | The shape of a varying p.d. shown against time |
| Division | One square of the screen grid |
| Y-gain | Vertical scale setting, in volts per division |
| Timebase | Horizontal scale setting, in time per division |
| Peak p.d. | Maximum p.d. measured from the centre (zero) line |
| Peak-to-peak p.d. | P.d. from the bottom of a trough to the top of a crest |
| Period | Time for one complete cycle |
| Frequency | Number of cycles per second; f = 1 / T |

## Exam traps

- Count **divisions** between grid lines, not the lines themselves.
- Peak ≠ peak-to-peak. Halve the peak-to-peak height for the peak.
- A **larger** Y-gain value (more V/div) makes the trace **shorter**.
- One period is crest to crest (or trough to trough), not crest to trough.
- 1 ms = 10⁻³ s; 1 µs = 10⁻⁶ s. Convert before f = 1 / T.
- For echoes, the time read off the screen is for the journey there **and
  back**.
- Do not describe the inside of an oscilloscope; it is outside the
  syllabus.

## Self-test

1. What quantity is plotted on each axis of an oscilloscope screen?
2. State the unit of the Y-gain setting.
3. A d.c. trace sits 3.5 divisions above the centre at 2.0 V/div. Find the
   p.d.
4. An a.c. trace is 5.0 divisions peak-to-peak at 1.0 V/div. Find the peak
   p.d.
5. One cycle covers 2.5 divisions at 4.0 ms/div. Find the period.
6. Find the frequency in question 5.
7. The Y-gain is changed from 5 V/div to 2 V/div. What happens to the
   trace?
8. The timebase is changed from 1 ms/div to 2 ms/div. What happens to the
   number of cycles shown?
9. What does an a.c. signal look like with the timebase switched off?
10. A pulse and its echo are 3.0 divisions apart at 50 µs/div. Find the
    time between them.
11. Why are several cycles measured, rather than one, to find a period?
12. A microphone picks up a louder note of the same pitch. How does the
    trace change?

**Answers**

1. P.d. on the vertical axis; time on the horizontal axis.
2. Volts per division (V/div).
3. 3.5 × 2.0 = **7.0 V**.
4. (5.0 ÷ 2) × 1.0 = **2.5 V**.
5. 2.5 × 4.0 = **10 ms**.
6. f = 1 ÷ 0.010 = **100 Hz**.
7. It becomes taller (2.5 times the height).
8. Twice as many cycles fit on the screen.
9. A vertical line whose length shows the peak-to-peak p.d.
10. 3.0 × 50 = **150 µs** (1.5 × 10⁻⁴ s).
11. Reading a larger distance reduces the effect of the reading error on
    each period.
12. It becomes taller; the spacing of the waves stays the same.
