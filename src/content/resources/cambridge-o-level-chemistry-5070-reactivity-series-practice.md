Revise first with the [Reactivity series study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-reactivity-series/) and the [Reactivity series revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-reactivity-series-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Place these in order of reactivity, most reactive first: iron, potassium, copper, aluminium, zinc. **[2]**

**2.** Which metal reacts with steam but only very slowly with cold water?

A: sodium

B: magnesium

C: copper

D: silver

**[1]**

**3.** State, in terms of ions, what is meant by saying that magnesium is more reactive than zinc. **[1]**

**4.** Name the products when calcium reacts with cold water. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why silver does not react with dilute hydrochloric acid. **[1]**

**6.** Write the ionic equation for the reaction of magnesium with iron(II) ions. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A piece of iron is placed in blue copper(II) sulfate solution.

**(a)** Give **two** observations. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the ionic equation, including state symbols. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain the reaction in terms of electron transfer. **[2]**

**8.** Copper wire is placed in colourless silver nitrate solution.

**(a)** Describe what is seen. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the ionic equation. **[2]**

**(c)** Predict whether silver wire would react with copper(II) nitrate solution. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**9.** A student adds four metals, W, X, Y and Z, to solutions of each other's sulfates. The table shows whether a reaction occurred (✓) or not (✗).

| Metal | W²⁺(aq) | X²⁺(aq) | Y²⁺(aq) | Z²⁺(aq) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W | — | ✗ | ✓ | ✗ |
| X | ✓ | — | ✓ | ✓ |
| Y | ✗ | ✗ | — | ✗ |
| Z | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ | — |

**(a)** Deduce the order of reactivity, most reactive first. **[2]**

**(b)** The four metals are magnesium, zinc, iron and copper. Identify each letter. **[2]**

**10.** Small, equal-sized pieces of magnesium, zinc, iron and copper are added to separate tubes of the same dilute hydrochloric acid.

**(a)** State which metal produces bubbles fastest and which produces none. **[2]**

**(b)** Write a balanced equation for the reaction of zinc with hydrochloric acid. **[1]**

**(c)** State **two** variables that must be kept the same to make the comparison fair. **[2]**

**11.** A student places a piece of aluminium foil in dilute hydrochloric acid. For several minutes nothing happens; then bubbles appear and become rapid.

**(a)** Explain why there is no reaction at first. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the reaction then becomes rapid. **[1]**

**(c)** State the position of aluminium in the reactivity series relative to zinc. **[1]**

**12.** Potassium, sodium and calcium all react with cold water.

**(a)** Describe how the vigour of the reaction differs between potassium and calcium. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain this difference using the reactivity series. **[2]**

**(c)** Write a balanced equation for sodium with water. **[1]**

**13.** Explain why zinc can be used to obtain copper from copper(II) sulfate solution, but copper cannot be used to obtain zinc from zinc sulfate solution. Refer to the tendency of each metal to form positive ions. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Potassium, aluminium, zinc, iron, copper: fully correct [2]; one adjacent pair swapped [1].

**2.** B [1].

**3.** Magnesium has a greater tendency than zinc to lose electrons / form positive ions [1].

**4.** Calcium hydroxide [1]; hydrogen [1].

**5.** Silver is below hydrogen in the reactivity series [1].

**6.** Mg + Fe²⁺ → Mg²⁺ + Fe [1].

**7. (a)** Pink-brown / reddish solid forms on the iron [1]; the blue colour fades / solution becomes paler (pale green) [1].

**(b)** Fe(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Fe²⁺(aq) + Cu(s): correct equation [1]; state symbols [1].

**(c)** Iron atoms lose electrons to form Fe²⁺ ions [1]; copper(II) ions gain these electrons to form copper atoms [1].

**8. (a)** Silvery-grey crystals / solid form on the copper [1]; the solution turns blue [1].

**(b)** Cu + 2Ag⁺ → Cu²⁺ + 2Ag: correct species [1]; balanced (2Ag⁺ and 2Ag) [1].

**(c)** No reaction [1]; silver is less reactive than copper, so it cannot displace copper from its ions [1].

**9. (a)** X > Z > W > Y: fully correct [2]; one adjacent pair swapped [1].

**(b)** X = magnesium, Z = zinc, W = iron, Y = copper: all four [2]; two correct [1].

**10. (a)** Fastest: magnesium [1]; none: copper [1].

**(b)** Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂ [1].

**(c)** Any two: concentration of acid; volume of acid; temperature; mass / size / surface area of metal [2].

**11. (a)** The aluminium is covered by a layer of aluminium oxide [1], which stops the acid reaching the metal [1].

**(b)** The acid removes / dissolves the oxide layer, exposing the reactive aluminium beneath [1].

**(c)** Above zinc (more reactive) [1].

**12. (a)** Potassium reacts violently (with a lilac flame), calcium reacts steadily / less vigorously [1].

**(b)** Potassium is higher in the reactivity series than calcium [1], so it has a greater tendency to lose electrons / form positive ions [1].

**(c)** 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂ [1].

**13.** Zinc is above copper in the reactivity series / more reactive [1]; zinc has a greater tendency to form positive ions, so zinc atoms give electrons to copper(II) ions, forming copper [1]; copper has a smaller tendency to form ions than zinc, so it cannot reduce zinc ions / no reaction [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Placing aluminium below zinc or iron.
- Leaving carbon and hydrogen out when asked for the full series (or putting metals in their places).
- Unbalanced ionic equations with silver ions.
- Saying copper reacts slowly with acid instead of not at all.
- Explaining displacement with "it is stronger" rather than comparing tendencies to form positive ions.
