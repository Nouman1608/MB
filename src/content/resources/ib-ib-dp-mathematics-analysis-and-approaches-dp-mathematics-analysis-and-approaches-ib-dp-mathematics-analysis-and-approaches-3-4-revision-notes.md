Explanations and worked examples are in the [radian measure study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-4/); exam-style questions are in the [radian measure practice set](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-4-practice/).

## Condensed recall

**Radians**

- One radian: the angle subtended at the centre by an arc equal in length to the radius.
- π rad = 180°; 2π rad = 360°; 1 rad ≈ 57.3°.
- Degrees to radians: × π/180. Radians to degrees: × 180/π.
- Radian measure may be given as exact multiples of π or as decimals.
- On exam papers, assume radians unless the question indicates degrees.

**Key conversions**

- 30° = π/6, 45° = π/4, 60° = π/3, 90° = π/2.
- 120° = 2π/3, 135° = 3π/4, 150° = 5π/6, 180° = π.
- 210° = 7π/6, 240° = 4π/3, 270° = 3π/2, 300° = 5π/3, 330° = 11π/6.

**Formulae (θ in radians)**

- Arc length: l = rθ.
- Sector area: A = ½r²θ.
- Sector perimeter: 2r + rθ.
- Both formulae come from the fraction θ/2π of the full circle.

**Reverse use**

- θ = l/r; θ = 2A/r²; r = l/θ; r = √(2A/θ).
- Given perimeter and area: substitute rθ = P − 2r into A = ½r(rθ) and solve the quadratic in r.
- Reject any solution with θ > 2π or r ≤ 0.

**Major and minor**

- Minor sector angle θ; major sector angle 2π − θ.
- Major arc = r(2π − θ); major sector area = ½r²(2π − θ).
- Major segment = whole circle − minor segment.

**Combinations with triangles**

- Area of triangle OAB = ½r² sin θ.
- Chord AB = 2r sin(θ/2) (split the isosceles triangle) or by the cosine rule.
- Minor segment area = ½r²(θ − sin θ).
- Segment perimeter = arc + chord.
- Annular sector (between radii r and R): area ½(R² − r²)θ; perimeter Rθ + rθ + 2(R − r).

**Calculator**

- Radian mode whenever sin θ is evaluated with θ in radians.
- Exact answers keep π: 6π cm, 55π/4 m².

**Method: a shaded-region question**

1. Name every centre and radius on the diagram.
2. Write the region in words as a sum or difference of sectors, triangles and other simple shapes.
3. Find each centre angle in radians (convert if the diagram uses degrees).
4. Calculate each piece separately and store it.
5. Combine, then round once at the end.

**Two-line reminder: sector from perimeter and area**

- Perimeter 20, area 16: rθ = 20 − 2r, so ½r(20 − 2r) = 16, giving r² − 10r + 16 = 0.
- r = 2 gives θ = 8 > 2π (reject); r = 8 gives θ = 0.5.

**Where the formulae come from (useful if you forget one)**

- Arc: θ/2π of the circumference 2πr gives rθ.
- Sector: θ/2π of the area πr² gives ½r²θ.
- Segment: sector ½r²θ minus triangle ½r² sin θ.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Radian | Angle subtending an arc equal to the radius |
| Arc | Part of the circumference between two points |
| Sector | Region bounded by two radii and an arc |
| Segment | Region bounded by a chord and an arc |
| Chord | Straight line joining two points on the circle |
| Major / minor | The larger / smaller of the two parts |
| Subtended angle | The angle an arc or chord makes at the centre |

## Exam traps

- l = rθ and ½r²θ fail in degrees.
- Sector perimeter needs both radii as well as the arc.
- Segment = sector − triangle, not plus.
- Using the minor angle when the major sector is shaded.
- A quadratic for r can give an impossible angle; check θ ≤ 2π.
- Degree mode when finding sin 1.2 (gives about 0.0209 instead of about 0.932).
- 180/π and π/180 swapped.
- Using the diameter instead of the radius in l = rθ when a question gives a diameter.
- Calling an angle of 4 radians acute: it is more than π, so it is reflex.

## Self-test

1. Write 210° in radians.
2. Write 5π/12 radians in degrees.
3. Write 3.2 radians in degrees.
4. Find the exact arc length for radius 6 cm and angle 7π/9.
5. Find the area of a sector with radius 5 cm and angle 1.2 rad.
6. A sector of radius 9 cm has area 27π cm². Find its angle.
7. An arc of length 10π cm subtends 5π/4 at the centre. Find the radius.
8. Find the perimeter of a sector with radius 7 cm and angle 2 rad.
9. A circle has radius 6 cm. The minor sector angle is π/3. Find the exact area of the major sector.
10. Find the length of the chord subtending π/3 at the centre of a circle of radius 8 cm.
11. Find the exact area of the minor segment when r = 4 cm and θ = π/2.
12. Find the area between two concentric arcs of radii 3 cm and 5 cm with angle 1.5 rad.

### Answers

1. 210 × π/180 = 7π/6.
2. 5π/12 × 180/π = 75°.
3. 3.2 × 180/π = 183° (183.3°).
4. 6 × 7π/9 = 14π/3 cm.
5. ½ × 25 × 1.2 = 15 cm².
6. ½ × 81 × θ = 27π, so θ = 2π/3.
7. r = 10π ÷ 5π/4 = 8 cm.
8. 14 + 14 = 28 cm.
9. ½ × 36 × 5π/3 = 30π cm².
10. 2 × 8 × sin(π/6) = 8 cm (the triangle is equilateral).
11. ½ × 16 × (π/2 − 1) = 4π − 8 cm².
12. ½(25 − 9)(1.5) = 12 cm².
