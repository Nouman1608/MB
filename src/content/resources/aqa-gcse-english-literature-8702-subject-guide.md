AQA GCSE English Literature (8702) is a course in reading
plays, novels and poems closely and writing about them with confidence.
It suits students who enjoy thinking about how writers create meaning
and who want to practise building an argument about a text. The whole qualification is assessed by two written
papers: Paper 1 lasts 1 hour 45 minutes and is worth 64 marks (40% of
the GCSE), and Paper 2 lasts 2 hours 15 minutes and is worth 96 marks
(60%).

## How the course is organised

AQA groups the content into two text-based areas plus a set of skills
that run through both.

- **Shakespeare and the 19th-century novel.** You study one Shakespeare
  play and one novel from the 19th century. These are the two oldest
  texts on the course, and both are tested on Paper 1.
- **Modern texts and poetry.** You study one modern prose or drama text,
  a group of poems from the AQA poetry anthology, and you practise responding
  to unseen poetry, meaning poems you have never seen before the exam.
  All three are tested on Paper 2.
- **Skills.** Reading comprehension and reading critically, together
  with writing, are not a separate unit with their own paper. They are
  the abilities every answer on both papers depends on: understanding
  what a text says, analysing how it says it, and organising a clear,
  well-supported argument.

Your school chooses which play, novel, modern text and anthology poems
you study, so check the exact list with your teacher early on.

## How it's assessed

The course is linear and not tiered: everyone sits the same two papers
at the end of the course, and all assessments are compulsory. The exams
are closed book, so you will not have your copy of the text in the
room; any extract or stimulus material is printed in the paper itself.

| Paper | Content | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8702/1 | Paper 1: Shakespeare and the 19th-century novel | 1 hour 45 minutes | 64 | 40% |
| 8702/2 | Paper 2: Modern texts and poetry | 2 hours 15 minutes | 96 | 60% |

Paper 1 has two sections. Section A is one question on your
Shakespeare play and Section B is one question on your 19th-century
novel. Both ask you to analyse an extract in detail and then discuss
the text as a whole.

Paper 2 has three sections. Section A is one essay on your modern prose
or drama text, chosen from two questions. Section B is one comparative
question on the poetry anthology. Section C, Unseen poetry, has one
question on a single unseen poem and then a second question comparing
it with another unseen poem.

The two papers are deliberately unequal. Paper 2 carries more marks,
more time and more of the final grade, partly because it asks you to
handle three kinds of reading in one sitting. The specification was
first taught in September 2015 and first examined in June 2017.

## How the parts fit together

Although the texts are divided between two papers, the same reading
habits are tested throughout. Extract work on Paper 1 trains you to
zoom in on a short passage and then link it to the rest of the text;
that same movement between detail and the bigger picture is what a
strong modern-text essay on Paper 2 needs. Comparing anthology poems
in Section B prepares you directly for comparing two unseen poems in
Section C, because the questions ask you to notice similarities and
differences in how poets present ideas, not only what the poems are
about.

Because the papers are closed book, memory matters more than in some
literature courses. You need short, flexible quotations from every set
text and a secure sense of each text's plot, characters, themes and
structure. The unseen poetry section is the one place where no prior
knowledge of the text can help you, so it tests the reading skills in
their purest form. Practising it regularly is one of the best ways to
sharpen your analysis of the set texts too.

## Planning your study

Most schools spread the three set texts and the anthology poems across
two years. A workable pattern looks like this:

1. **Early in the course:** begin with one text that suits your class,
   often the modern text or the Shakespeare play, and build the habit
   of annotating language, form and structure from the first lesson.
2. **Through the first year:** add the 19th-century novel and start the
   anthology poems a few at a time, comparing each new poem with ones
   you already know.
3. **Across both years:** read an unseen poem every week or two and write
   a short response. This keeps the skill alive rather than leaving it
   to the final term.
4. **Second year:** finish the remaining texts and poems, then shift to
   consolidation: quotation banks, theme maps that link characters to
   ideas, and timed paragraphs.
5. **Final months:** move on to full papers and the paper-by-paper exam
   preparation page, so that timing and section choices are practised
   under realistic conditions.

Rereading the set texts in full at least once during the second year
is worth the time. Students who rely only on notes often lose the sense
of how a text develops from beginning to end, which is exactly what
whole-text discussion on Paper 1 asks for.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in AQA GCSE English Literature?**
There are two written papers. Paper 1 (Shakespeare and the 19th-century novel) is 1 hour 45 minutes, 64 marks and 40% of the GCSE; Paper 2 (Modern texts and poetry) is 2 hours 15 minutes, 96 marks and 60%.

**Is AQA GCSE English Literature an open-book exam?**
No. Both papers are closed book, so you cannot take your set texts into the exam. Any extracts or stimulus material you need are printed in the paper.

**Is GCSE English Literature 8702 tiered?**
No. The qualification is not tiered, so all students sit the same two papers, and every assessment is compulsory.

**What is the unseen poetry section?**
It is Section C of Paper 2. You answer one question on a poem you have not studied, then a second question comparing it with another unseen poem.

**When was this specification first examined?**
AQA GCSE English Literature 8702 was first taught from September 2015 and first examined in June 2017.
