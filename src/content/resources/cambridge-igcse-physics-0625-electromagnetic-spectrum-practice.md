## Section A

**1.** Name the region of the electromagnetic spectrum that has:

**(a)** the highest frequency **[1]**
**(b)** the longest wavelength. **[1]**

**2.** State one property that all electromagnetic waves have in common. **[1]**

**3.** Name the region of the electromagnetic spectrum used in:

**(a)** a television remote controller **[1]**
**(b)** detecting fake bank notes **[1]**
**(c)** an airport security scanner. **[1]**

**4.** State the harmful effect on people of excessive exposure to:

**(a)** microwaves **[1]**
**(b)** infrared radiation. **[1]**

**5.** State the region of the electromagnetic spectrum mainly used to communicate with artificial satellites. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** This question is about the order of the electromagnetic spectrum.

**(a)** Write these four regions in order of increasing frequency: ultraviolet, radio waves, X-rays, infrared. **[2]**
**(b)** Region P lies between ultraviolet and gamma rays. Name region P and give one use of it. **[2]**

**7.** This question is about hazards and uses in a hospital.

**(a)** Surgical instruments are sealed in plastic packets and then sterilised with gamma rays. Explain why gamma rays are suitable. **[2]**
**(b)** State the harmful effect of excessive exposure to X-rays. **[1]**
**(c)** Excessive exposure to sunlight can damage the skin and eyes. Explain why the damage from ultraviolet is to the skin and eyes rather than to organs deep inside the body. **[2]**

**8.** This question is about artificial satellites.

**(a)** State the type of satellite used for direct broadcast satellite television. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why a fixed dish on a house can receive signals from this type of satellite all the time. **[2]**
**(c)** Some satellite phones use low orbit satellites instead. Suggest one advantage and one disadvantage of using low orbit satellites. **[2]**

**9.** *(Extended)* This question is about the speed of electromagnetic waves.

**(a)** State the speed of electromagnetic waves in a vacuum. **[1]**
**(b)** A long-wave radio station broadcasts at a frequency of 200 kHz. Calculate the wavelength of the radio waves. **[2]**
**(c)** Infrared radiation from a warm object has a wavelength of 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ m. Calculate its frequency. **[2]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A geostationary satellite is 3.6 × 10⁷ m above a ground station.

**(a)** Calculate the time taken for a microwave signal to travel from the ground station to the satellite. **[2]**
**(b)** Mobile phones also use microwaves. Give two reasons why microwaves are suitable for mobile phones. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* This question is about wireless and cable communication.

**(a)** Explain why Bluetooth, used to link a phone to nearby headphones, uses radio waves. **[2]**
**(b)** High-speed broadband is carried into homes by optical fibres. Name the regions of the spectrum used in optical fibres and give two reasons for using them. **[3]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A singer's voice is picked up by a microphone and sent to a distant listener.

**(a)** Describe the difference between an analogue signal and a digital signal. **[2]**
**(b)** State whether the signal produced directly by the microphone is analogue or digital. **[1]**
**(c)** The signal is converted into digital form for transmission over a long distance. Explain why a digital signal can be sent further than an analogue signal without losing quality. **[3]**
**(d)** State one other benefit of digital signalling. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Gamma rays** [1].
**(b)** **Radio waves** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any one: they all travel at the same speed in a vacuum; they are all transverse waves [1]. **[1]**

*Common mistake:* "they all have the same frequency". Frequency is what distinguishes the regions.

**3. (a)** **Infrared** [1].
**(b)** **Ultraviolet** [1].
**(c)** **X-rays** [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** Internal heating of body cells [1].
**(b)** Skin burns [1]. **[2]**

*Common mistake:* answering "cancer" for microwaves. The named hazard is internal heating.

**5.** **Microwaves** [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** Radio waves, infrared, ultraviolet, X-rays: all correct [2] (one adjacent pair swapped scores 1 mark).
**(b)** **X-rays** [1]; medical scanning or security scanners [1]. **[4]**

**7. (a)** Gamma rays pass through the plastic packaging [1] and kill bacteria (microorganisms) on the instruments [1].
**(b)** Mutation or damage to cells in the body [1].
**(c)** Ultraviolet is absorbed at the surface (it does not penetrate far into the body) [1], so it damages the surface cells of the skin and the eyes [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** **Geostationary** satellite [1].
**(b)** It orbits once every 24 hours, in the same direction as the Earth rotates [1], so it stays above the same point on the Earth's surface and the dish always points at it [1].
**(c)** Advantage: closer to the Earth, so signals travel a shorter distance (shorter delay) [1]. Disadvantage: each satellite moves across the sky, so many satellites are needed to keep contact [1]. **[5]**

*Common mistake:* saying a geostationary satellite does not move. It moves fast, but keeps pace with the Earth's rotation.

**9. (a)** **3.0 × 10⁸ m/s** [1].
**(b)** λ = v / f = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ (200 × 10³) [1] = **1500 m** [1].
**(c)** f = v / λ = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ [1] = **3.0 × 10¹³ Hz** [1]. **[5]**

*Common mistake:* using 200 instead of 200 × 10³ Hz, giving 1.5 × 10⁶ m.

**10. (a)** t = distance / speed = 3.6 × 10⁷ ÷ 3.0 × 10⁸ [1] = **0.12 s** [1].
**(b)** Microwaves can penetrate some walls [1]; they need only a short aerial for transmission and reception [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** Radio waves pass through walls [1], but the signal is weakened in doing so, so it suits a short-range link [1].
**(b)** **Visible light or infrared** [1]; glass is transparent to them [1]; their short wavelengths can carry high rates of data [1]. **[5]**

**12. (a)** An analogue signal varies continuously and can take any value in a range [1]; a digital signal has only a few set values, usually two (0 and 1) [1].
**(b)** **Analogue** [1].
**(c)** All signals weaken and pick up noise as they travel [1]. A digital signal can be regenerated at intervals because each pulse only has to be identified as 0 or 1 [1]; the regenerated signal is an exact copy with the noise removed, so range increases (an amplified analogue signal keeps its noise) [1].
**(d)** Increased rate of transmission of data [1]. **[7]**

*Common mistake:* writing "digital signals do not get noise". They do; the advantage is that the noise can be removed.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping infrared and ultraviolet in the order.
- Claiming one region travels faster than another in a vacuum.
- Giving cancer as the effect of microwaves or infrared.
- Saying satellites use radio waves.
- Missing the prefix (k, M, G) in a wave-equation calculation.
- Explaining digital signalling with "clearer" instead of regeneration.
