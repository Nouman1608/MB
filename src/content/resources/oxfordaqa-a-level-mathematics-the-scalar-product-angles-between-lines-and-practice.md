## Questions

**1.** The vectors a = (3, −2, 5) and b = (−1, 4, 2) are given. Find a.b and state, with a reason, whether the angle between a and b is acute or obtuse. **[2]**

**2.** The vectors u = (k, −5, −2) and v = (k, k, 3) are perpendicular. Find the possible values of k. **[4]**

**3.** Two lines have equations

r = (−2, 5, 1) + λ(3, −4, 12) and r = (4, 0, −3) + μ(2, 3, 6).

Find the acute angle between the lines, correct to 1 decimal place. **[4]**

**4.** The points A, B and C have coordinates A(0, 1, 3), B(1, −1, 5) and C(2, 4, 5).

**(a)** Show that angle BAC is a right angle. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the exact area of triangle ABC. **[3]**

**5.** The point P has coordinates (2, 0, 7). The line l has equation r = (1, 0, 2) + t(1, 2, −2).

**(a)** Find the coordinates of the foot of the perpendicular from P to l. **[4]**
**(b)** Hence find the perpendicular distance from P to l. **[2]**

**6.** A line in two dimensions has equation r = (−1, 5) + t(3, −2). Find the perpendicular distance from the point (4, 6) to this line. **[4]**

**7.** The line l₁ has direction (3, 0, 4) and the line l₂ has direction (2, p, 1), where p is a constant. The acute angle θ between the lines satisfies cos θ = 2/3. Find the possible values of p. **[5]**

**8.** A drone flies along the line r = (−20, 10, 2) + t(12, −4, 3). Distances are in hundreds of metres, t is the time in minutes and the axes point east, north and upwards. A sensor stands at the point P(5, 5, 8).

**(a)** Find the value of t when the drone is closest to the sensor. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the least distance between the drone and the sensor, in metres to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the speed of the drone in metres per minute. **[1]**

**9.** Show that the acute angle between the lines y = 2x − 1 and y = −3x + 4 is 45°. **[4]**

**10.** A straight cable has direction vector (2, −3, 6). Find the angle between the cable and the upward vertical direction k = (0, 0, 1), to 1 decimal place. **[3]**

**11.** The points A, B and C have coordinates A(7, 1, 3), B(1, −2, 3) and C(7, −5, 9).

**(a)** Find angle ABC, correct to 1 decimal place. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of the point D, the foot of the perpendicular from A to the line BC. **[4]**
**(c)** Hence find the area of triangle ABC. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** a.b = (3)(−1) + (−2)(4) + (5)(2) = −3 − 8 + 10 = **−1** [1]
The scalar product is negative, so cos θ is negative and the angle is **obtuse** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A reason is usually needed with the word "obtuse". Link the sign of a.b to the sign of cos θ.

**2.** u.v = k² − 5k − 6 [1]
Set u.v = 0 for perpendicular vectors: k² − 5k − 6 = 0 [1]
(k − 6)(k + 1) = 0 [1]
**k = 6 or k = −1** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Perpendicular means u.v = 0 and no magnitudes are needed. Give both roots, because a quadratic condition has two solutions.

**3.** Direction vectors (3, −4, 12) and (2, 3, 6); the position vectors are not used. d₁.d₂ = 6 − 12 + 72 = 66 [1]
|d₁| = √(9 + 16 + 144) = 13 and |d₂| = √(4 + 9 + 36) = 7 [1]
cos θ = 66 / (13 × 7) = 66/91 [1]
**θ = 43.5°** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the fraction 66/91 until the last step. The scalar product is positive here, so no supplement is needed.

**4. (a)** AB = (1, −2, 2) and AC = (2, 3, 2) [1]
AB.AC = 2 − 6 + 4 = 0, so AB is perpendicular to AC and angle BAC = **90°** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** |AB| = √(1 + 4 + 4) = 3 [1]
|AC| = √(4 + 9 + 4) = √17 [1]
Area = ½ × 3 × √17 = **3√17/2** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" question, write the vectors, the calculation and the conclusion. Part (b) uses the right angle from part (a), so state that it is the base and height.

**5. (a)** X = (1 + t, 2t, 2 − 2t) [1]
PX = X − P = (t − 1, 2t, −5 − 2t); PX.d = (t − 1) + 4t − 2(−5 − 2t) = 9t + 9 [1]
9t + 9 = 0, so t = −1 [1]
Foot: **(0, −2, 4)** [1] **[4]**
**(b)** PX = (−2, −2, −3) (or its negative) [1]
Distance = √(4 + 4 + 9) = **√17** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Substitute t back to get a point. Checking PX.d = 0 with your numbers is a quick way to find sign slips.

**6.** X = (−1 + 3t, 5 − 2t), PX = X − P = (3t − 5, −1 − 2t) [1]
PX.d = 3(3t − 5) − 2(−1 − 2t) = 13t − 13 = 0 [1]
t = 1, so X = (2, 3) and PX = (−2, −3) [1]
Distance = **√13** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The same method works in two dimensions. Give the distance as a surd unless a decimal is requested.

**7.** d₁.d₂ = 6 + 0 + 4 = 10 for every p [1]
|d₁| = 5 and |d₂| = √(5 + p²) [1]
10 / (5√(5 + p²)) = 2/3 [1]
√(5 + p²) = 3, so 5 + p² = 9 [1]
**p = 2 or p = −2** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Notice that the scalar product does not depend on p. Both roots remain valid, so state both.

**8. (a)** X = (−20 + 12t, 10 − 4t, 2 + 3t) [1]
X − P = (12t − 25, 5 − 4t, 3t − 6) [1]
(X − P).d = 12(12t − 25) − 4(5 − 4t) + 3(3t − 6) = 169t − 338 = 0 [1]
**t = 2 minutes** [1] **[4]**
**(b)** At t = 2, X = (4, 2, 8) and X − P = (−1, −3, 0) [1]
Distance = √10 hundred metres = **316 m** (3 s.f.) [1] **[2]**
**(c)** |d| = √(144 + 16 + 9) = 13 hundred metres per minute = **1300 m per minute** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Read the units in the stem. The distance in the units of the question is √10, and the final answer has to be converted to metres.

**9.** Direction vectors (1, 2) and (1, −3) [1]
d₁.d₂ = 1 − 6 = −5, |d₁| = √5, |d₂| = √10 [1]
cos θ = −5 / √50 = −1/√2, so the vectors are at 135° [1]
The acute angle between the lines is 180° − 135° = **45°** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A negative cosine gives an obtuse angle. A "show that" question wants the step from 135° to 45° written out.

**10.** d.k = 6 and |d| = √(4 + 9 + 36) = 7 [1]
cos θ = 6/7 [1]
**θ = 31.0°** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* k is already a unit vector, so |k| = 1. Showing cos θ = 6/7 first gives a clear line of working.

**11. (a)** BA = (6, 3, 0) and BC = (6, −3, 6) [1]
BA.BC = 36 − 9 + 0 = 27, |BA| = 3√5, |BC| = 9 [1]
cos ABC = 27 / (27√5) = 1/√5 [1]
**angle ABC = 63.4°** [1] **[4]**
**(b)** Line BC: r = (1, −2, 3) + t(6, −3, 6) [1]
X − A = (6t − 6, −3t − 3, 6t), and (X − A).(6, −3, 6) = 81t − 27 = 0 [1]
t = 1/3 [1]
**D = (3, −3, 5)** [1] **[4]**
**(c)** AD = |(−4, −4, 2)| = 6 [1]
Area = ½ × BC × AD = ½ × 9 × 6 = **27** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Both vectors in part (a) must start at B. Part (c) is a "hence" question, so use AD from part (b) instead of starting again.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using position vectors, not direction vectors, when finding the angle between two lines.
- Dividing by |u| only, or forgetting the denominator in cos θ.
- Sign slips with negative components. Bracket every product.
- Giving an obtuse angle when asked for the angle between lines, or the reverse.
- Solving PX.d = 0 and stopping at t. The foot is a point.
- Using the distance from P to a point on the line, not the foot.
- Early rounding that carries into the final decimal.
- Skipping lines in a "show that" argument.
- Losing track of units, such as hundreds of metres.
