These questions cover **Section E: Trigonometry (E1 to E9)** of the AQA A-level Mathematics (7357) specification, version 1.3 (31 January 2018), for A-level exams from June 2018 onwards. Section E is Paper 1 content and can also be assessed on Papers 2 and 3. A calculator is allowed throughout 7357, but where a question asks for an exact answer or says "show that", give full working.

## Questions

**1.** A sector of a circle has angle 1.5 radians and area 27 cm².

**(a)** Find the radius of the circle. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the perimeter of the sector. **[1]**

**2.** Find the exact value of:

**(a)** sin(7π/4) **[1]**
**(b)** cosec(2π/3) **[1]**
**(c)** Use a compound angle formula to show that tan(π/12) = 2 − √3. **[3]**

**3.** **(a)** State the range of y = arccos x. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the exact value of arccos(cos(5π/3)). **[2]**
**(c)** For 0 ≤ x ≤ 2π, state the values of x for which sec x is undefined, and state the range of sec x. **[2]**

**4.** In triangle PQR, PQ = 9 cm, PR = 6 cm and angle QPR = 2π/3 radians.

**(a)** Show that QR = 3√19 cm. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the exact area of the triangle. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate angle PQR, in degrees to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**5.** **(a)** Show that, when θ is small, (4 cos θ − 4 + 3θ sin 2θ) / (θ tan θ) ≈ 4. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the exact value of the expression when θ = 0.1, and find the percentage error in the approximation. **[2]**

**6.** Given that sec θ = −3 and π/2 < θ < π, find the exact value of:

**(a)** tan θ **[2]**
**(b)** sin 2θ and cos 2θ **[3]**

**7.** Solve 3 tan²x + 5 sec x + 1 = 0 for 0° ≤ x < 360°. **[5]**

**8.** Solve 2 cos(3x − 20°) = 1 for 0° ≤ x ≤ 180°, giving your answers to 1 decimal place. **[5]**

**9.** **(a)** Prove that sin 3θ ≡ 3 sin θ − 4 sin³θ. **[4]**
**(b)** Hence solve sin 3θ = 2 sin θ for 0 ≤ θ < 2π, giving exact answers. **[4]**

**10.** **(a)** Express √5 cos x + 2 sin x in the form R cos(x − α), where R > 0 and 0 < α < π/2. Give α to 3 decimal places. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the maximum value of 1 / (4 + √5 cos x + 2 sin x) and the value of x in 0 ≤ x < 2π where it occurs. **[2]**
**(c)** Solve √5 cos x + 2 sin x = 2 for 0 ≤ x < 2π. **[4]**

**11.** The height above the ground of a seat on an observation wheel is modelled by h = 22 − 20 cos(πt/12) metres, t minutes after the seat passes the lowest point.

**(a)** State the maximum height and the first time it is reached. **[2]**
**(b)** State the time for one revolution. **[1]**
**(c)** For how long in each revolution is the seat more than 32 m above the ground? **[4]**

**12.** A particle is in equilibrium under three forces: its weight of 20 N acting vertically downwards, a horizontal force of 12 N acting to the left, and a force of P N acting to the right at an angle α above the horizontal.

**(a)** Resolve horizontally and vertically to form two equations in P and α. **[2]**
**(b)** Find P, in exact form and to 3 significant figures, and find α to 1 decimal place. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** ½r²(1.5) = 27 [1], so r² = 36 and **r = 6 cm** [1].
**(b)** Arc = 6 × 1.5 = 9 cm, so perimeter = 9 + 6 + 6 = **21 cm** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the perimeter of a sector includes both radii; giving the arc length alone loses the mark.

**2. (a)** **−√2/2** [1]
**(b)** 1 / sin(2π/3) = 2/√3 = **2√3/3** [1]
**(c)** tan(π/3 − π/4) = (tan π/3 − tan π/4) / (1 + tan π/3 tan π/4) [1]
= (√3 − 1) / (1 + √3) [1]
= (√3 − 1)² / (3 − 1) = (4 − 2√3)/2 = **2 − √3** [1]
*Examiner insight:* a calculator decimal for tan(π/12) earns nothing on a "show that"; the compound angle step and the rationalising step must both be written.

**3. (a)** **0 ≤ y ≤ π** [1]
**(b)** cos(5π/3) = ½ [1], and arccos(½) = **π/3** [1].
**(c)** Undefined at **x = π/2 and x = 3π/2** [1]; range **sec x ≤ −1 or sec x ≥ 1** [1].
*Examiner insight:* answering 5π/3 in (b) scores at most the first mark, because arccos only returns values in [0, π].

**4. (a)** QR² = 9² + 6² − 2(9)(6) cos(2π/3) [1]
= 117 + 54 = 171, so QR = √171 = **3√19 cm** [1].
**(b)** Area = ½(9)(6) sin(2π/3) [1] = **27√3/2 cm²** [1].
**(c)** sin Q = 6 sin(2π/3) / (3√19) [1] = 0.3974…, so **Q = 23.4°** [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (a) the substitution with cos(2π/3) = −½ must be visible; writing "QR² = 171" with no line before it does not show the given result.

**5. (a)** 4 cos θ − 4 ≈ 4(1 − θ²/2) − 4 = −2θ² [1]
3θ sin 2θ ≈ 3θ(2θ) = 6θ², and θ tan θ ≈ θ² [1]
(−2θ² + 6θ²) / θ² = **4** [1]
**(b)** Exact value at θ = 0.1: 3.9485 (5 s.f.) [1]; percentage error = (4 − 3.9485)/3.9485 × 100 = **1.30%** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the small angle results only hold in radians, and sin 2θ must become 2θ, not θ; either slip loses the method mark.

**6. (a)** tan²θ = sec²θ − 1 = 8 [1]; θ is in the second quadrant where tan is negative, so **tan θ = −2√2** [1].
**(b)** cos θ = −1/3 and sin θ = 2√2/3 [1]
sin 2θ = 2(2√2/3)(−1/3) = **−4√2/9** [1]
cos 2θ = 2(−1/3)² − 1 = **−7/9** [1]
*Examiner insight:* the sign mark depends on a reason; state the quadrant rather than just choosing the negative root.

**7.** 3(sec²x − 1) + 5 sec x + 1 = 0 [1]
3 sec²x + 5 sec x − 2 = 0 [1]
(3 sec x − 1)(sec x + 2) = 0; sec x = 1/3 has no solutions because |sec x| ≥ 1 [1]
sec x = −2, so cos x = −½ [1]
**x = 120°, 240°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* an answer that tries to solve cos x = 3 without saying it is impossible usually loses the rejection mark.

**8.** cos(3x − 20°) = ½ [1]
Interval: −20° ≤ 3x − 20° ≤ 520° [1]
3x − 20° = 60°, 300°, 420° [1]
**x = 26.7°, 106.7°, 146.7°** [1] for any two correct, [1] for all three with no extras.
*Examiner insight:* the final accuracy mark is lost for an extra answer outside 0° to 180° as well as for a missing one, so check every value against the interval.

**9. (a)** sin 3θ = sin(2θ + θ) = sin 2θ cos θ + cos 2θ sin θ [1]
= 2 sin θ cos²θ + (1 − 2sin²θ) sin θ [1]
= 2 sin θ(1 − sin²θ) + sin θ − 2sin³θ [1]
= **3 sin θ − 4 sin³θ** [1]
**(b)** 3 sin θ − 4 sin³θ = 2 sin θ, so sin θ(1 − 4 sin²θ) = 0 [1]
sin θ = 0: θ = **0, π** [1]
sin θ = ½: θ = **π/6, 5π/6** [1]
sin θ = −½: θ = **7π/6, 11π/6** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Hence" means the result from (a) must be used; dividing by sin θ throws away 0 and π and costs a mark.

**10. (a)** R cos α = √5 and R sin α = 2 [1]
R = √(5 + 4) = **3** [1]
tan α = 2/√5, so **α = 0.730** [1]
**(b)** Least value of the denominator is 4 − 3 = 1, so the maximum is **1** [1], when x − α = π, so **x = 3.87** [1].
**(c)** cos(x − α) = 2/3 [1]
x − α lies in [−0.730, 5.553), so x − α = 0.8411 or 2π − 0.8411 = 5.4421 [1]
**x = 1.57** (exactly π/2) [1]
**x = 6.17** [1]
*Examiner insight:* in (b), the maximum of a reciprocal comes from the minimum of the denominator; giving 1/7 is a common route to zero marks.

**11. (a)** Maximum when cos(πt/12) = −1: **h = 42 m** [1], first at **t = 12 minutes** [1].
**(b)** Period = 2π ÷ (π/12) = **24 minutes** [1].
**(c)** 22 − 20 cos(πt/12) > 32 gives cos(πt/12) < −½ [1]
For 0 ≤ t ≤ 24: 2π/3 < πt/12 < 4π/3 [1]
8 < t < 16 [1]
So the seat is above 32 m for **8 minutes** [1].
*Examiner insight:* reversing the inequality when dividing by −20 is the key step; finding only the boundary times without the interval loses the final two marks.

**12. (a)** Horizontally: **P cos α = 12** [1]; vertically: **P sin α = 20** [1].
**(b)** P² = 12² + 20² = 544 [1], so **P = 4√34 = 23.3 N** [1]
tan α = 20/12 = 5/3 [1], so **α = 59.0°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* squaring and adding uses sin²α + cos²α ≡ 1; say so, and give the force with units.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Answering in degrees when the interval is in radians, or the reverse.
- Missing solutions in a multiple angle question because the interval was not changed first.
- Dividing by sin θ or cos θ instead of factorising.
- Not rejecting impossible values such as sec x = 1/3, or rejecting them without a reason.
- Choosing a sign for tan θ or sin θ without naming the quadrant.
- Using the principal value of arccos or arcsin when it lies outside the question's interval.
- Skipping the identity step in a "show that" or "prove" question.
- For a cos x + b sin x in R cos(x − α) form, using tan α = a/b instead of b/a.
- In context questions, giving a boundary time instead of the length of the interval asked for.
