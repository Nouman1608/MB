Condensed recall for **3.1.6 Forces and terminal velocity** (P content, full Physics award) in OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203), for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Forces and terminal velocity study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-forces-and-terminal-velocity/); exam-style questions are in the [Forces and terminal velocity practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-forces-and-terminal-velocity-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fluid | A liquid or a gas |
| Drag | The frictional force on an object moving through a fluid; it increases with speed |
| Air resistance | Drag in air |
| Resultant force | The single force with the same effect as all the forces acting |
| Terminal velocity | The constant velocity reached when drag equals the driving force (weight, for a falling object), so the resultant force is zero |
| Maximum velocity | The top speed of a vehicle, reached when drag equals the maximum driving force |
| Streamlining | Shaping an object so a fluid flows past it smoothly, reducing drag |
| Deceleration | Negative acceleration; velocity decreasing |

## Condensed recall

**Drag (a).** Acts opposite to the motion. Faster → more drag.

**Falling (b).**
1. Start: drag = 0, so resultant = weight and acceleration is greatest.
2. Speed rises → drag rises → resultant falls → acceleration falls.
3. Drag = weight → resultant = 0 → acceleration = 0 → terminal velocity.

**Parachutes (c).** Large area → much more drag at a given speed → drag balances weight at a lower speed → lower terminal velocity.

**Streamlining (d).** Less drag at a given speed → drag reaches the maximum driving force at a higher speed → higher maximum velocity.
- Shark: smooth, tapered body (adaptation).
- Car: smooth, curved body and sloping front (design feature).

## Equations used

```
W = m x g
resultant force = weight - drag      (falling object)
resultant force = driving force - drag   (vehicle)
a = F / m
```

## Velocity-time graph for a skydive

| Stage | Gradient of graph | Drag compared with weight | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Just after jumping | Steepest, positive | Drag < weight | Accelerating fastest |
| Falling faster | Positive, decreasing | Drag < weight, rising | Still accelerating, less each second |
| First terminal velocity | Zero | Drag = weight | Constant high velocity |
| Parachute opens | Steep, negative | Drag > weight | Decelerating, still moving down |
| Slowing | Negative, getting smaller | Drag > weight, falling | Deceleration decreasing |
| Second terminal velocity | Zero | Drag = weight | Constant low velocity |
| Landing | Drops to zero | Ground exerts a large upward force | Stops |

## How to sketch the graph

- Axes: velocity on y, time on x, labelled.
- Start at the origin with the steepest slope.
- Curve smoothly into the first horizontal line.
- Show a sharp drop when the parachute opens, curving into a lower horizontal line.
- The second horizontal line must be clearly lower than the first and above zero.

## Comparing terminal velocities

| Change to the object | Drag at a given speed | Weight | Terminal velocity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Larger area (open parachute) | Larger | Same | Lower |
| More streamlined shape | Smaller | Same | Higher |
| Heavier, same size and shape | Same | Larger | Higher |
| Lighter, same size and shape | Same | Smaller | Lower |

Reasoning pattern for any row: terminal velocity is the speed at which drag has grown to equal the weight. More drag per unit speed, or less weight to balance, means that speed is lower.

## Calculation chain at any instant

1. Weight = m × g.
2. Resultant force = weight − drag (downwards if positive).
3. Acceleration = resultant force ÷ mass.
4. If you are given the acceleration instead, work backwards: drag = weight − (m × a).

## Exam traps

- **Equal, not greater.** At terminal velocity drag equals weight.
- **Zero acceleration is not zero velocity.**
- **Weight is constant** during the fall; only drag changes.
- **Parachutes increase drag**; they do not change weight or gravity.
- **Still moving down.** When the parachute opens the skydiver decelerates but keeps falling.
- **Curved, not kinked.** The approach to terminal velocity is a smooth curve.
- **Streamlining explanation.** Describe the shape and its effect on drag; "it is streamlined" alone is not enough.

## Self-test

**1.** State what happens to drag as an object's speed through a fluid increases.

**2.** Give the acceleration of a falling object at the instant it is released (ignore any initial drag).

**3.** A 70 kg skydiver has a weight of 686 N. At one instant drag is 350 N. Find the resultant force.

**4.** Using question 3, find the acceleration.

**5.** State the drag on this skydiver at terminal velocity.

**6.** Why does a parachute reduce terminal velocity?

**7.** Describe the direction of the resultant force just after a parachute opens, and the effect on the velocity.

**8.** What does a horizontal section of a velocity-time graph mean?

**9.** A car's maximum driving force is 3800 N. What is the drag at its top speed?

**10.** Explain how streamlining a car increases its top speed.

**11.** Give the streamlining example the specification names as an adaptation.

**12.** Two balls are the same size and shape, but one is heavier. Which has the higher terminal velocity, and why?

## Self-test answers

**1.** It increases.

**2.** Equal to g (for example 9.8 m/s² when g = 9.8 N/kg), because drag is zero at that instant.

**3.** 686 − 350 = 336 N downwards.

**4.** a = 336 ÷ 70 = 4.8 m/s².

**5.** 686 N.

**6.** Its large area gives much more drag at a given speed, so drag equals weight at a lower speed.

**7.** Upwards; the skydiver decelerates (velocity decreases) while still moving down.

**8.** Constant velocity (zero acceleration, zero resultant force).

**9.** 3800 N.

**10.** Streamlining reduces drag at each speed, so the car can go faster before drag equals the maximum driving force.

**11.** The shape of a shark.

**12.** The heavier ball: drag must rise to a larger value to equal its weight, which happens only at a higher speed.
