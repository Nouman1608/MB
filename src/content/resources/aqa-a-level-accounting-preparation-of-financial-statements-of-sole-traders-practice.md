These questions cover section 3.6, Preparation of financial statements of sole traders, of the AQA A-level Accounting (7127) specification (version 1.1, October 2026, A-level exams June 2019 onwards). The qualification is not tiered and section 3.6 is assessed on Paper 1. Use a calculator throughout. All businesses and figures are invented.

## Questions

**1.** Fenwick Tutoring's year ends 31 December. Insurance of £420 was prepaid at 1 January 2025. On 1 April 2025 it paid £2,040 for the 12 months to 31 March 2026. Prepare the insurance account for 2025. **[4]**

**2.** Brook Storage sublets part of its unit. At 1 January 2025 rent of £300 had been received in advance. During 2025 it received £5,100. At 31 December 2025 the tenant owes £450. Prepare the rent receivable account for 2025. **[4]**

**3.** Dale Couriers (year end 31 March) bought a van on 1 April 2023 for £28,000. Policy: 20% reducing balance, a full year's charge in the year of purchase and none in the year of disposal. The van was sold on 15 January 2026 for £16,500.

**(a)** Calculate the accumulated depreciation at disposal. **[3]**
**(b)** Prepare the disposal account. **[4]**

**4.** Kestrel Supplies had a provision for doubtful debts of £1,500 on 1 January 2024. The provision is kept at 4% of trade receivables.

- 31 December 2024: trade receivables £46,000, before writing off irrecoverable debts of £2,000.
- 31 December 2025: trade receivables £38,500, after all irrecoverable debts.

**(a)** Prepare the provision for doubtful debts account for 2024 and 2025. **[4]**
**(b)** Show the 2025 entries in the income statement and statement of financial position. **[3]**

**5.** Lowfield Café's debt of £720 was written off in the year ended 30 June 2024. On 12 March 2026 it paid the £720 in full. Show the entries and state the effect on profit for the year ended 30 June 2026. **[4]**

**6.** Calculate the value of closing inventory for these three product lines. **[3]**

| Line | Cost £ | Information |
|---|---:|---|
| A | 5,400 | Selling price £7,100 |
| B | 3,250 | Selling price £3,400; selling costs £350 |
| C | 1,900 | Damaged; will sell for £1,200 after repairs costing £200 |

**7.** Moss Interiors' draft profit is £41,300. Not yet dealt with:

- Goods sent on sale or return at selling price £2,400 (mark-up 60% on cost) were recorded as a credit sale. They are not yet accepted and not in closing inventory.
- The owner took goods costing £680 for her own use.
- Electricity of £290 is accrued.

Calculate the corrected profit for the year. **[5]**

**8.** Ashby Consulting is a service business. Extract from its trial balance at 30 June 2026:

| | Dr £ | Cr £ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fees receivable | | 98,600 |
| Commission received | | 2,400 |
| Salaries | 41,200 | |
| Rent and rates | 12,600 | |
| Office expenses | 4,380 | |
| Motor expenses | 3,150 | |
| Irrecoverable debts | 510 | |
| Provision for doubtful debts 1 July 2025 | | 700 |
| Trade receivables | 12,400 | |
| Office equipment at cost / provision for depreciation | 18,000 | 7,200 |
| Car at cost / provision for depreciation | 22,000 | 8,800 |

Notes: work completed and earned but not invoiced, £1,200; commission includes £400 for next year; salaries accrued £800; rent and rates prepaid £1,050; write off a further irrecoverable debt of £400; provision for doubtful debts 5% of trade receivables; depreciate office equipment at 15% on cost and the car at 30% reducing balance.

Prepare the income statement for the year ended 30 June 2026. **[12]**

**9.** Hartley Cycles' profit for the year ended 31 December 2025, after all adjustments, is £27,340. Balances before the notes below:

- Equipment at cost £36,000; provision for depreciation at 1 January 2025 £12,600
- Inventory £23,450 (already at the lower of cost and NRV); trade receivables £18,600; insurance prepaid £600
- Trade payables £14,900; wages accrued £720; bank overdraft £3,180
- Bank loan repayable 2030 £15,000
- Capital at 1 January 2025 £21,985; drawings £21,600

Notes: depreciation for the year is 20% reducing balance; goods at selling price £1,350 (cost £900) recorded as a credit sale are still on sale or return; the owner took goods costing £950, not yet recorded; create a provision for doubtful debts of 2% of trade receivables.

Prepare the statement of financial position at 31 December 2025. **[10]**

**10.** A sole trader says: "A provision for doubtful debts is pointless. It is only a guess, and it just makes my profit look smaller." Evaluate this view. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.**

```
Insurance account
2025                      £      2025                       £
1 Jan Balance b/d       420      31 Dec Income statement 1,950
1 Apr Bank            2,040      31 Dec Balance c/d        510
                      2,460                              2,460
```

Opening prepayment £420 debited [1]; payment £2,040 [1]; closing prepayment 2,040 x 3/12 = **£510** carried down [1]; charge 420 + 2,040 - 510 = **£1,950** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A closing prepayment carried down on the wrong side usually costs the balance and transfer marks.

**2.**

```
Rent receivable account
2025                        £      2025                       £
31 Dec Income statement 5,850      1 Jan Balance b/d         300
                                   Bank                    5,100
                                   31 Dec Balance c/d        450
                        5,850                              5,850
2026 1 Jan Balance b/d    450
```

Opening £300 received in advance credited [1]; receipts £5,100 [1]; income statement **£5,850** [1]; income due £450 carried down and brought down as a debit (current asset) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* In an income account the transfer to the income statement is on the debit side.

**3. (a)** Year to 31 March 2024: 28,000 x 20% = 5,600 [1]. Year to 31 March 2025: (28,000 - 5,600) x 20% = 4,480 [1]. No charge in the year of disposal, so accumulated depreciation = **£10,080** [1].
**(b)**

```
Disposal account
                         £                                     £
Van (cost)          28,000    Provision for depreciation   10,080
                              Bank                         16,500
                              Income statement (loss)       1,420
                    28,000                                 28,000
```

Cost debited [1]; accumulated depreciation credited [1]; proceeds credited [1]; **loss of £1,420** to the income statement [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* An error in (a) can be carried into (b) as own figure if the account layout is correct.

**4. (a)**

```
Provision for doubtful debts account
2024                       £     2024                       £
31 Dec Balance c/d     1,760     1 Jan Balance b/d      1,500
                                 31 Dec Income statement  260
                       1,760                            1,760
2025                             2025
31 Dec Income statement  220     1 Jan Balance b/d      1,760
31 Dec Balance c/d     1,540
                       1,760                            1,760
```

2024 provision 4% x (46,000 - 2,000) = £1,760 [1]; increase £260 credited from the income statement [1]; 2025 provision 4% x 38,500 = £1,540 [1]; decrease **£220** debited to the income statement [1].
**(b)** Income statement: decrease in provision for doubtful debts **£220** shown as income [1]. Trade receivables £38,500 [1] less provision £1,540 = **£36,960** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Charging the whole £1,540 to the income statement loses the mark; only the change goes there.

**5.** Reinstate: debit Lowfield Café, credit irrecoverable debts recovered, £720 [1]. Cash: debit bank, credit Lowfield Café, £720 [1]. Irrecoverable debts recovered £720 is income in the 2026 income statement [1]; profit rises by **£720** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Reopening the 2024 accounts earns nothing; the recovery belongs to the year the cash arrives.

**6.** A at cost £5,400. B: NRV 3,400 - 350 = £3,050, below cost [1]. C: NRV 1,200 - 200 = £1,000, below cost [1]. Total 5,400 + 3,050 + 1,000 = **£9,450** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare each line separately; comparing total cost with total NRV gives the wrong value and loses the final mark.

**7.** Remove the sale: -£2,400 [1]. Add the goods to inventory at cost, 2,400 / 1.6 = 1,500: +£1,500 [1]. Goods for own use reduce purchases: +£680 [1]. Accrued electricity: -£290 [1]. Corrected profit 41,300 - 2,400 + 1,500 + 680 - 290 = **£40,790** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each adjustment with its sign; a wrong bare final figure earns no marks, while shown adjustments can still earn method marks.

**8.**

```
Ashby Consulting: Income statement for the year ended 30 June 2026
                                               £          £
Fees receivable (98,600 + 1,200)                     99,800
Commission received (2,400 - 400)                     2,000
Decrease in provision for doubtful debts                100
                                                    101,900
Less expenses
  Salaries (41,200 + 800)                  42,000
  Rent and rates (12,600 - 1,050)          11,550
  Office expenses                           4,380
  Motor expenses                            3,150
  Irrecoverable debts (510 + 400)             910
  Depreciation: office equipment            2,700
  Depreciation: car                         3,960
                                                     68,650
Profit for the year                                  33,250
```

Fees £99,800 [1]; commission £2,000 [1]; provision 5% x (12,400 - 400) = £600 [1]; decrease of £100 shown as income [1]; salaries £42,000 [1]; rent and rates £11,550 [1]; office and motor expenses [1]; irrecoverable debts £910 [1]; office equipment 15% x 18,000 = £2,700 [1]; car 30% x (22,000 - 8,800) = £3,960 [1]; total expenses £68,650 [1]; profit **£33,250** [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* A service business has no cost of sales or gross profit; adding a trading section wastes time.

**9.**

```
Hartley Cycles: Statement of financial position at 31 December 2025
                                 Cost   Acc. dep.   Carrying
Non-current assets                  £          £           £
  Equipment                    36,000     17,280      18,720
Current assets
  Inventory (23,450 + 900)               24,350
  Trade receivables          17,250
  Less provision                345      16,905
  Other receivables                         600
                                         41,855
Current liabilities
  Trade payables             14,900
  Accrued expenses              720
  Bank overdraft              3,180      18,800
Net current assets                                    23,055
                                                      41,775
Non-current liabilities: bank loan                    15,000
Net assets                                            26,775

Capital (equity)
  Opening capital                                     21,985
  Add profit for the year                             27,340
                                                      49,325
  Less drawings (21,600 + 950)                        22,550
                                                      26,775
```

Depreciation 20% x 23,400 = 4,680, carrying amount £18,720 [1]; inventory £24,350 [1]; receivables 18,600 - 1,350 = 17,250, less 2% provision, £16,905 [1]; prepayment as a current asset [1]; current liabilities £18,800 [1]; net current assets £23,055 [1]; loan as non-current [1]; net assets **£26,775** [1]; drawings £22,550 [1]; closing capital agrees [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Subheadings carry marks; a loan listed under current liabilities loses the non-current liability mark and distorts net current assets.

**10.** Against: prudence -- receivables are not shown above what the business expects to collect [1]. Accruals -- the likely cost of credit sales is matched to the year of the revenue [1]. A realistic profit stops drawings being taken from profit that may never become cash [1]. For: it is an estimate, so a poor percentage misstates profit [1]; it does not affect cash [1]. Judgement: the view is weak, since the estimate rests on past experience and only the change hits profit each year [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" needs both sides and a supported judgement; one-sided answers cannot reach full marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Charging the full provision for doubtful debts instead of the change.
- Calculating the provision before writing off further irrecoverable debts.
- Applying reducing balance to cost instead of to the carrying amount.
- Valuing sale or return goods at selling price.
- Placing an income received in advance or a loan in the wrong section.
- Leaving out workings, so no own-figure credit is possible.
