These questions cover section 3.1.3, Setting business aims and objectives, of the AQA GCSE Business (8132) specification, for first teaching from September 2017. All businesses and figures are invented. Short questions come first, then case-based questions. Learn the content in the [aims and objectives study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-business-8132-setting-business-aims-and-objectives/) and the [aims and objectives revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-business-8132-setting-business-aims-and-objectives-revision-notes/) first.

## Questions

**1.** Explain the difference between an aim and an objective. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** business objectives other than profit maximisation. **[2]**

**3.** Identify the objective shown in each statement.

**(a)** "We want 20% of all pet food sales in the region." **[1]**
**(b)** "We aim to open our first store in France next year." **[1]**
**(c)** "We will only buy coffee from farms paid a fair price." **[1]**

**4.** Explain **one** reason why a new start-up may have different objectives from a large public limited company. **[3]**

**5.** Corran Cycles set two objectives for 2026: revenue growth of 8% and a market share of 9%. Its revenue was £640,000 in 2025 and £704,000 in 2026. The total market was worth £8.8 million in 2026.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in revenue. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate Corran Cycles' market share in 2026. **[2]**
**(c)** State which of the two objectives was met. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **two** ways in which objectives help a business to run successfully. **[4]**

**7.** Explain how the arrival of a large new competitor might change a small business's objectives. **[3]**

**8.** Explain **one** way in which two business objectives might conflict. **[3]**

**9.** A business surveyed customers in two years.

| Year | Customers surveyed | Customers satisfied |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | 720 | 540 |
| 2026 | 780 | 702 |

**(a)** Calculate the satisfaction score for each year. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why customer satisfaction may be a useful measure of success alongside profit. **[2]**

**10.** Analyse how a business's objectives may change after it becomes a public limited company. **[6]**

**11.** Wyndham Ales is a brewery. Its objectives for 2026 were to grow the number of pubs stocking its beer, to increase market share and to cut carbon emissions by 25%.

| Measure | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Profit | £150,000 | £132,000 |
| Market share | 4.0% | 5.2% |
| Pubs stocking its beer | 80 | 104 |
| Carbon emissions (change from 2025) | – | −30% |

Evaluate whether 2026 was a successful year for Wyndham Ales. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** An aim is a long-term goal that gives the general direction of the business [1]; an objective is a specific, measurable target, often with a deadline, that helps it achieve the aim [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* An example of each strengthens the answer, but the contrast between general and specific earns the marks.

**2.** Any two: survival; growth (domestic or international); market share; customer satisfaction; social and ethical objectives; shareholder value [1] [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Making money" is profit and does not count here.

**3. (a)** Market share [1]
**(b)** International growth [1]
**(c)** Social and ethical objective [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Growth" alone is acceptable for (b), but "international growth" matches the specification wording.

**4.** A start-up has few customers and limited cash [1], so its main objective is usually survival, covering its costs while it builds a customer base [1]. A large plc is already established and has shareholders expecting returns, so it can aim for shareholder value, international expansion or market dominance [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention both businesses; the question asks why they differ.

**5. (a)** (704,000 − 640,000) ÷ 640,000 × 100 [1] = **10%** [1]
**(b)** 704,000 ÷ 8,800,000 × 100 [1] = **8.0%** [1]
**(c)** The revenue objective was met, because 10% is above the 8% target [1]; the market share objective was missed, because 8.0% is below 9% [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A business can grow its revenue strongly and still miss a market share target if the whole market grows.

**6.** Any two developed points, 2 marks each. They give **direction** [1], so all staff and departments work towards the same targets rather than in different directions [1]. They help **decision making** [1], because managers can choose the option that best fits the objective, such as a price cut to win market share [1]. They allow **success to be measured** [1], because results can be compared with targets and action taken if they are missed [1]. They can **motivate** staff [1] who know what they are trying to achieve [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each role needs a consequence for the business.

**7.** A large rival may take customers with lower prices or wider ranges [1], so the small business may switch from growth or profit maximisation to survival [1], or focus on customer satisfaction to keep loyal customers by offering better service than the rival [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the old objective and the new one, and explain the link.

**8.** For example, a business wanting market share may cut its prices to attract customers [1], but lower prices reduce the profit made on each sale [1], so pursuing market share can conflict with profit maximisation, at least in the short term [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Ethics against profit and growth against survival are equally valid if explained.

**9. (a)** 2025: 540 ÷ 720 × 100 = **75%** [1]; 2026: 702 ÷ 780 × 100 = **90%** [1]
**(b)** Satisfied customers are more likely to return and recommend the business [1], so a rising score suggests future sales and profit even if this year's profit is low [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare percentages, not the raw numbers, because the sample size changed.

**10.** Indicative chains, up to 6.
- Many shareholders now own the company and expect returns [1], so increasing shareholder value through dividends and a rising share price becomes a central objective [1].
- The flotation raises large sums of finance [1], so the company can set more ambitious growth objectives, such as international expansion or becoming the dominant business in its market [1].
- It faces more public scrutiny [1], so ethical and environmental objectives may become more important to protect its reputation [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each chain should start from a feature of being a plc and end with a named objective.

**11.** Indicative points, one mark each up to 9.
- Profit fell from £150,000 to £132,000, a fall of 12%, so on profit alone it was not successful [1].
- Market share rose from 4.0% to 5.2%, meeting its objective [1].
- Pubs stocking its beer rose from 80 to 104, an increase of 30%, meeting its growth objective [1].
- Carbon emissions fell by 30%, beating the 25% target [1].
- The fall in profit may be the cost of achieving these objectives, for example spending on marketing to win pubs or on greener equipment [1].
- Greater market share and more pubs could raise sales and profit in future years [1].
- The emissions cut may improve its reputation with environmentally aware customers and pubs [1].
- However, if profit kept falling, the business might struggle to fund growth or reward its owners [1].
- Judgement: 2026 was largely successful, because Wyndham met all three of its stated objectives, which were not about profit; the fall in profit is a concern only if it continues [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Judge success against the business's own objectives, using the figures; an answer that says "profit fell, so it failed" ignores the specification's point that success is not only profit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing aims and objectives.
- Percentage change calculated from the wrong base.
- Comparing raw survey numbers instead of percentages.
- Saying objectives change without a trigger.
- Judging success by profit alone.
- Evaluations without a final, justified judgement.
