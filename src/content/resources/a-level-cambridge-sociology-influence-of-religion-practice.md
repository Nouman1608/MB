influence of religion* (12.1, 12.2 and 12.3). Each Paper 4 essay is worth 35 marks and needs
contrasting views or evidence.

## Warm-up questions

**W1.** Define *secularisation*. **[2]**

**W2.** Explain what Grace Davie meant by *believing without belonging*. **[2]**

**W3.** Identify Wallis's **three** types of new religious movement. **[3]**

**W4.** Give **two** explanations for the growth of fundamentalism. **[4]**

### Warm-up answers

- **W1.** The process by which religious thinking, practices and institutions lose social
  significance (Wilson, 1966).
- **W2.** People continue to hold religious beliefs privately even though they no longer attend
  services or belong to religious organisations (*Religion in Britain since 1945*, 1994).
- **W3.** World-rejecting, world-accommodating and world-affirming (Wallis, 1984).
- **W4.** Any two, developed: cultural defence — protecting identity against external threat;
  cultural transition — helping migrants cope; marginality — deprived groups seeking certainty;
  disengagement — withdrawal from a corrupt secular world; religious revival — renewal against
  moral decline; reaction to globalisation (Giddens, 1999). 2 marks each.

## Essay 1 (12.1)

**"Rationalisation has led to a decline in religious belief." Evaluate this view.** **[35]**

### Indicative content

**For:**
- Weber: rational, scientific thought produces disenchantment.
- Berger (1967): pluralism removes the single sacred canopy, making belief a matter of choice and
  doubt.
- Bruce (2002): technology gives practical control; generational decline in belief.
- Survey evidence of falling belief among the young in many Western societies.

**Against:**
- Berger's later desecularisation argument (1999).
- Norris and Inglehart (2004): insecurity, not rationality, explains religiosity; global growth.
- Stark and Bainbridge (1985): demand for religion constant; compensators for unmet desires.
- Growth of spirituality, NRMs and Pentecostalism in rationalised societies.
- Scientists and highly educated people may be religious; science and religion can coexist.

**Evaluation:** rationalisation may explain institutional decline more than belief; consider
regional differences and measurement problems.

## Essay 2 (12.1)

**"Religion has lost its social significance." Evaluate this view.** **[35]**

### Indicative content

- **For:** Wilson (1966); disengagement of religion from law, education, welfare; decline of
  religious rites of passage; Bruce on generational decline.
- **Against:** Casanova (1994) — deprivatisation; religion in politics and conflict (link to 11.3:
  Iran, US Evangelicals); religion as identity for minorities and migrants (cultural defence and
  transition); faith schools and religious charities; global growth (Norris and Inglehart).
- **Evaluation:** distinguish personal belief from institutional influence; judge between
  societies.

## Essay 3 (12.2)

**"Religion is a source of patriarchal oppression." Evaluate this view.** **[35]**

### Indicative content

**For:**
- de Beauvoir (1949): religion compensates women for subordination.
- Male leadership and exclusion of women from clergy in many faiths.
- Patriarchal interpretations of texts; restrictions on dress, marriage and divorce; ideas of
  female impurity.
- Armstrong (1993): the rise of male-dominated monotheism displaced earlier goddesses.

**Against:**
- El Saadawi (1980): patriarchal *interpretation*, not religion itself, oppresses women.
- Moves to equality: Church of England women priests (1994) and bishop (2015); women rabbis.
- Watson (1994): veiling as chosen identity and protection from the male gaze.
- Woodhead: women use religion for their own ends; women's prominence in New Age spirituality.

**Evaluation:** liberal versus radical feminist positions; differences between and within
religions; agency versus constraint.

## Essay 4 (12.3)

**"Post-modern religion is a matter of personal choice." Evaluate this view.** **[35]**

### Indicative content

**For:**
- Privatised religion; spiritual shopping (Lyon, *Jesus in Disneyland*, 2000).
- Hervieu-Léger's pilgrims; decline of inherited religious identity.
- Heelas and Woodhead (2005): holistic spirituality growing in Kendal.
- New Age ideas and world-affirming NRMs centred on the self.

**Against:**
- Fundamentalism and cultural defence show religion as collective, inherited and binding rather
  than chosen.
- Ethnic minority religiosity is often tied to family and community identity.
- Kendal's spiritual revolution was too small to replace congregational religion.
- Choice may be constrained by class, gender and socialisation.

**Evaluation:** choice may describe religion among affluent Western groups more than globally;
judge whether resacralisation is individual or collective.

## Essay 5 (12.3)

**"The growth of fundamentalism is best explained as a form of cultural defence." Evaluate this
view.** **[35]**

### Indicative content

**For:** Bruce (1996) — religion becomes a focus for identity when a group feels threatened by an
outside power, modernity or globalisation; Giddens (1999) — fundamentalism defends tradition in a
traditional way against globalising change; examples of religion fused with national or ethnic
identity.

**Alternative explanations from the syllabus:**
- **Cultural transition** — migrants and those moving from rural to urban life use religion to cope
  with rapid change and keep a sense of community.
- **Marginality** — deprived and excluded groups are drawn to faiths offering certainty and a
  promise of reward (Weber's theodicy of disprivilege).
- **Disengagement** — believers withdraw from a secular society they see as morally corrupt.
- **Religious revival** — renewal movements arise in cycles, as religious market theory (Stark and
  Bainbridge, 1985) predicts.

**Evaluation:** explanations overlap; cultural defence fits national conflicts, marginality and
transition fit migrant and urban poor communities; "fundamentalism" is itself a contested label
that can be applied unfairly to devout but peaceful believers. Conclude on which explanation fits
most cases.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I avoid repeating the same evidence in secularisation essays?** Group evidence by type —
practice, belief, institutional significance — and tailor it to each question's focus.

**Is fundamentalism a sign of secularisation or resacralisation?** Both are argued: it may be a
reaction to secularisation, but its growth also shows religion remains powerful.

**Do feminist essays need non-Western evidence?** Using more than one faith and society strengthens
evaluation and application.
