**Fiscal policy** is the use of government spending and taxation to influence the economy.

## The budget, deficits and surpluses

The **government budget** is the government's plan of revenue (mostly taxes) and spending for a period, usually a year.

- **Budget deficit:** spending exceeds revenue; the gap is financed by borrowing.
- **Budget surplus:** revenue exceeds spending; the surplus can repay debt.
- **Balanced budget:** the two are equal.

### Worked example 1: Country F (invented, billion cu)

| Revenue | | Spending | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Income tax | 180 | Current spending | 380 |
| Corporation tax | 60 | Capital spending | 85 |
| Sales tax | 140 | | |
| Excise duties | 40 | | |
| **Total** | **420** | **Total** | **465** |

```
Budget balance = 420 - 465 = -45 (a deficit of 45)
With GDP of 1,800: deficit = 45 / 1,800 x 100 = 2.5% of GDP
```

Expressing the deficit as a share of GDP allows comparison between years and countries of different sizes.

The budget balance also changes **without any decision** by the government. In a downturn, incomes and spending fall, so tax receipts fall, while more people claim unemployment benefits. The deficit widens. In a boom the opposite happens. Analysis of a change in the deficit should therefore ask whether it reflects policy or the state of the economy.

## The national debt

The **national debt** is the total stock of government borrowing that has not yet been repaid. The deficit is a **flow** over one year; the debt is a **stock** at a point in time. Each deficit adds to the debt; each surplus used for repayment reduces it.

### Worked example 2: the debt ratio

Country F starts the year with debt of 600 and GDP of 1,500 (both billion cu), so debt is 40.0% of GDP. It runs a deficit of 30, and nominal GDP grows by 8%.

```
New debt   = 600 + 30 = 630
New GDP    = 1,500 x 1.08 = 1,620
Debt ratio = 630 / 1,620 x 100 = 38.9%
```

The debt rose, yet the **debt-to-GDP ratio fell**, because GDP grew faster than the debt. The ratio is a better guide to the burden than the cash figure, since GDP indicates the country's capacity to pay interest through tax revenue.

### Significance of the national debt

- **Interest payments** have an opportunity cost: money spent on interest cannot be spent on services.
- **Future taxes or spending cuts** may be needed to service or reduce it.
- **Who holds it matters:** interest paid to domestic lenders stays in the economy; interest paid to foreign lenders leaves it.
- **What it financed matters:** borrowing for capital spending such as roads or power networks can raise future output and tax revenue; borrowing for current spending does not add to capacity.
- **Confidence:** a rapidly rising ratio may lead lenders to demand higher interest rates.

## Taxation

### Types

- **Direct taxes** are levied on income or wealth and paid by the person or firm that bears them, such as income tax and corporation tax.
- **Indirect taxes** are levied on spending and collected by sellers, such as sales tax and excise duties.

Classified by the **average rate of tax** as income rises:

- **Progressive:** the average rate rises with income.
- **Proportional:** the average rate is the same at all incomes.
- **Regressive:** the average rate falls as income rises.

### Rates: mrt and art

```
Average rate of tax (art)  = total tax / total income x 100
Marginal rate of tax (mrt) = change in tax / change in income x 100
```

### Worked example 3: a three-band income tax

An invented income tax: 0% on the first 8,000 cu; 15% on income from 8,000 to 30,000 cu; 35% on income above 30,000 cu.

| Income (cu) | Tax (cu) | art | mrt on the next cu |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8,000 | 0 | 0% | 15% |
| 20,000 | 0.15 x 12,000 = 1,800 | 9.0% | 15% |
| 30,000 | 0.15 x 22,000 = 3,300 | 11.0% | 35% |
| 50,000 | 3,300 + 0.35 x 20,000 = 10,300 | 20.6% | 35% |

The art rises with income, so the tax is **progressive**. The mrt is above the art at every income above the allowance, which pulls the average up. A taxpayer moving from 30,000 to 50,000 cu pays 7,000 cu more tax on 20,000 cu more income: an mrt of 35%.

### Indirect taxes and regressivity

A fixed duty on fuel takes 300 cu a year from a household earning 15,000 cu (2.0% of income) and 600 cu from one earning 75,000 cu (0.8%). The richer household pays more in total, but a **smaller proportion** of income, so the duty is regressive in effect.

### Reasons for taxation

- Raise **revenue** for government spending.
- **Redistribute** income through progressive taxes.
- **Discourage** consumption of demerit goods by raising their price.
- **Influence aggregate demand** as part of fiscal policy.

## Government spending

- **Current spending** is on items used up within the year: public sector wages, medicines, benefits and interest on the debt.
- **Capital (investment) spending** adds to the stock of long-lasting assets: new hospitals, schools, roads and ports.

Classify by **what is bought**, not by the service. Building a new school is capital spending; paying its teachers is current spending.

### Reasons for government spending

- Provide **public goods**, which markets do not provide, and **merit goods**, which are under-consumed.
- **Redistribute** income through transfer payments and free services.
- **Raise productive capacity** through infrastructure, education and health.
- **Influence aggregate demand** to support output and employment.

## Expansionary and contractionary fiscal policy

| | Expansionary | Contractionary |
|---|---|---|
| Measures | Raise government spending, cut taxes, or both | Cut government spending, raise taxes, or both |
| Effect on AD | Shifts right | Shifts left |
| Effect on budget | Deficit widens (or surplus narrows) | Deficit narrows (or surplus widens) |
| Suited to | Low output and cyclical unemployment | Demand-pull inflation |

## AD/AS analysis on the three sections of LRAS

The syllabus allows LRAS to be drawn in three sections: **highly elastic**, **upward sloping** and **vertical**. Where the economy starts determines how an AD shift divides between output and the price level.

**Expansionary fiscal policy (AD shifts right):**

1. **Highly elastic section (much spare capacity).** Firms can raise output using idle resources without bidding up costs. Real output and employment rise; the price level changes little. Equilibrium national income rises.
2. **Upward-sloping section.** Some resources become scarce, so costs rise as output expands. Real output, employment and the price level all rise.
3. **Vertical section (full capacity).** Output cannot rise further. The whole effect is a higher price level; real output and employment are unchanged.

**Contractionary fiscal policy (AD shifts left):** on the vertical section, mainly the price level falls (or rises more slowly); on the upward-sloping section, both the price level and real output fall; on the highly elastic section, the main effect is lower output and employment.

The analysis shows why **diagnosis comes first**: expansionary policy suits an economy with spare capacity, while contractionary policy is most appropriate when the economy is near full capacity and inflation is rising.

## Annotated model answer

**Question:** Explain, using AD/AS analysis, how a cut in income tax may affect real output, the price level and employment in an economy with some spare capacity. **[4]**

> A cut in income tax raises households' **disposable income**, so **consumer spending rises** *(1: mechanism)*. Consumption is a component of AD, so **AD shifts to the right** *(1: shift)*. With some spare capacity the economy is on the **upward-sloping part of AS**, so the new equilibrium has **higher real output and a higher price level** *(1: output and prices)*. Firms need more workers to produce the extra output, so **employment rises** *(1: employment)*.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing the deficit (a yearly flow) with the debt (a stock).
- Calling a tax progressive because richer people pay more in total. The average rate must rise.
- Using the mrt when asked for the art.
- Classifying teachers' salaries as capital spending because they work in a new school.
- Showing only output rising after expansionary policy when the economy is at full capacity.
- Forgetting employment when the question asks for output, the price level **and** employment.

## Exam technique

- **Calculate** art and mrt with each step shown; give percentages to one decimal place.
- **Distinguish** questions want both terms defined and the difference made clear.
- **Explain** a fiscal change with the chain: instrument, component of AD, shift, new equilibrium, output, price level, employment.
- **Analyse** with a labelled AD/AS diagram, showing where on AS the economy starts.
- In Section C essays, judge fiscal policy by the amount of spare capacity, the effect on the budget and the debt, and what the spending is on.
