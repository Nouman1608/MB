> **Original questions written for Marlbridge.** They are not past-paper questions and do not copy the real exam's structure or mark tariffs.

**HL only.** These questions practise Regional study 5 in the Europe option (HL Paper 3). Revise first with the [revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-german-italian-unification-revision-notes/) and the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-german-italian-unification/).

## Questions

**1.** State two features of the settlement for Germany made at the Congress of Vienna. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why the Zollverein mattered for the rise of Prussia. **[4]**

**3.** Read this short extract, constructed for practice.

> "We gather not as Prussians or Bavarians but as Germans. Yet we cannot agree whether Vienna belongs to our Germany. Without an army of our own, we are a parliament of speeches." (Constructed for practice, in the voice of a delegate at Frankfurt in 1848.)

**(a)** Explain what the extract suggests about the weaknesses of the Frankfurt Parliament. **[3]**

**(b)** Analyse one limitation of the extract as evidence of liberal opinion in Germany. **[3]**

**4.** Explain two reasons why the Italian revolts of 1820 to 1821 and 1831 failed. **[4]**

**5.** Analyse the role of Garibaldi in the unification of Italy. **[6]**

**6.** Examine the reasons why the revolutions of 1848 failed in Germany and Italy. **[6]**

**7.** "Bismarck's diplomacy, rather than Prussian military strength, was the main reason for German unification." To what extent do you agree? Plan an answer: state your argument, three themes and a counter-argument. **[15]**

**8.** Compare the role of Austria in the unification of Italy and of Germany. **[4]**

**9.** Discuss how historians disagree about whether Bismarck planned unification. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: a German Confederation of 39 states; Austria presided over the Diet at Frankfurt; Prussia was enlarged in the west, including the Rhineland; the old Holy Roman Empire was not restored. One mark each. **[2]**

**2.** Up to 4. The Zollverein removed internal tariffs and encouraged trade and railways, which helped Prussia's economy grow (2). It tied German states economically to Prussia and not to Austria, which was excluded, so Prussia gained leadership without a political union (2). **[4]**

**3. (a)** Up to 3: the delegates felt a shared German identity but disagreed over the borders, especially whether Austria should be included (1); the Parliament had no army or power to enforce decisions (1); the phrase "a parliament of speeches" suggests it talked rather than acted, and relied on rulers (1). **[3]**
**(b)** Up to 3: it is one delegate's view and may be exaggerated for effect (1); it is a constructed extract expressing frustration, and it does not show the opinion of ordinary Germans (1); liberals were divided between moderates and radicals, so one voice cannot represent them (1). **[3]**

**4.** Up to 4, two reasons explained. They lacked mass support, because the Carbonari were officers, nobles and professionals, not peasants or workers (2). Austria intervened with troops at the request of rulers, and the great powers accepted this under the Congress System (2). Accept: poor organisation and secrecy. **[4]**

**5.** Up to 6 using a markband approach. Landed in Sicily in May 1860 with about a thousand volunteers (1); took advantage of local unrest and Bourbon weakness to take Sicily and Naples (1); his popular appeal made unification seem a national cause and not just Piedmontese expansion (1); his advance on Rome threatened to bring in the French, which led Cavour to send Piedmontese troops (1); he handed his conquests to Victor Emmanuel II, allowing the creation of the kingdom in 1861 (1); analysis of his importance compared with Cavour and foreign help, with a judgement (1). **[6]**

**6.** Up to 6: in Germany the Frankfurt Parliament split over Greater and Lesser Germany (1); it had no army and relied on rulers, and Frederick William IV refused the crown (1); in Italy Charles Albert was defeated at Custoza and Novara (1); Austria and France used force to restore order, as in Rome in 1849 (1); the liberal middle classes were divided and peasants and workers had different aims (1); a judgement on the main reason, such as the strength of conservative armies (1). **[6]**

**7.** Credit a plan with these features, 3 marks each, 15 in all. Understanding of the claim: diplomacy meant isolating Denmark, Austria and France before each war (3). The case for diplomacy: the Gastein agreement, support or neutrality from Russia and Italy, and the Ems telegram of 1870 (3). The case for military strength: Roon's and Moltke's army reforms, railways and rifles, Königgrätz and Sedan (3). Perspectives: a historian's view, such as Taylor on Bismarck's opportunism, against views that stress structural factors like the Zollverein and the economy (3). Counter-argument and judgement: for instance that diplomacy made war possible but victory depended on the army, so neither alone was the main reason (3). **[15]**

**8.** Up to 4. In both, Austria was the main opponent of change and the dominant power in 1815 (1); in Italy it fought Piedmont in 1848 to 1849 and 1859 and was driven from Lombardy (1); in Germany it was excluded in 1866 after defeat at Königgrätz (1); a judgement on difference, such as Italy gaining Austrian territory while Germany was reorganised without Austria (1). **[4]**

**9.** Up to 6: one view sees Bismarck as a planner who aimed at a Prussian-led Germany (2); another sees an opportunist who improvised and kept options open, as Taylor argued (2); explains why they differ, for example reliance on Bismarck's own memoirs against his actions at the time (1); a reasoned judgement (1). **[6]**
