These notes summarise section 5.5, Compound shapes and parts of shapes, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Full explanations are in the [compound shapes and parts of shapes study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-compound-shapes-and-parts-of-shapes/), and exam-style questions are in the [compound shapes and parts of shapes practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-compound-shapes-and-parts-of-shapes-practice/).

## What is examined

- Perimeters and areas of compound shapes and parts of shapes.
- Surface areas and volumes of compound solids and parts of solids.
- Answers may be asked for in terms of π.
- Example in the syllabus: surface area and volume of a frustum.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Compound shape | a shape made by joining simpler shapes |
| Composite | another word for compound |
| Segment | region between a chord and an arc |
| Sector | region between two radii and an arc |
| Hemisphere | half a sphere |
| Frustum | a cone or pyramid with the top cut off parallel to the base |
| Annulus | the region between two circles with the same centre |
| Cross-section | the shape you see when a solid is cut through |

## Method box: compound plane shapes

1. Sketch and label the shape.
2. Choose: split into simple parts and add, or subtract a part from a larger shape.
3. Calculate each area using the formulas for rectangles, triangles, circles and so on.
4. Add or subtract.
5. For the perimeter, add the outer edges only, including curved parts, and leave out any joins.

| Pattern | Area | Perimeter |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangle with a semicircle on one side | rectangle + 1/2 πr² | three sides + πr |
| Rectangle with a triangle on one side | rectangle + triangle | four sides + two sloping sides |
| Square with a circle removed | square − circle | outer edges (and the circle if it is a hole) |
| Segment | sector − triangle | arc + chord |
| Ring | π(R² − r²) | 2πR + 2πr |

Segment area at 90°: πr²/4 − r²/2. For other angles, the triangle is 1/2 r² sin θ.

## Method box: compound solids

1. List the simple solids.
2. Volume: add or subtract the separate volumes.
3. Surface area: add only the outside surfaces. Joined faces are not counted.
4. Use Pythagoras for any slant height.

| Solid | Volume | Surfaces to count |
|---|---|---|
| Cylinder + hemisphere on top | πr²h + 2/3 πr³ | cylinder base, cylinder curved, hemisphere curved |
| Cylinder + cone on top | πr²h + 1/3 πr²h₁ | cylinder base, cylinder curved, cone curved |
| Cone on hemisphere | 1/3 πr²h + 2/3 πr³ | cone curved, hemisphere curved |
| Half cylinder | 1/2 πr²l | curved half, flat rectangle, two half-circles |
| Hollow pipe | π(R² − r²)l | all four surfaces if needed |

## Frustum method

1. Find the volume of the whole cone and the volume of the removed cone.
2. Subtract: frustum = big − small.
3. For the curved surface, subtract the small cone's curved area from the big cone's: πRL − πrl.
4. For the total surface area, add the circular top and bottom faces.

If the small cone has scale factor k compared with the big one, its volume is k³ of the big one. For k = 1/2, the frustum is 7/8 of the big cone.

```
cone R = 6, H = 12 minus cone r = 3, h = 6:
V = 144π − 18π = 126π = 396
curved = π(6 × 13.42 − 3 × 6.708) = 189.7
total = 189.7 + 28.3 + 113.1 = 331
```

## Must-know distinctions

| Idea | Be clear that |
|---|---|
| Perimeter of a compound shape | only outer edges |
| Surface area of a compound solid | only exposed faces |
| Frustum volume | big cone − small cone |
| Slant height vs vertical height | volume uses vertical, curved area uses slant |
| Hemisphere flat face | counted when exposed, not when joined |

## Exam traps

- Counting joins in a perimeter or hidden faces in a surface area.
- Forgetting the flat face of a hemisphere when it is exposed.
- Forgetting to subtract the small cone.
- Treating a segment as a sector.
- Using the diameter as the radius.
- Leaving a unit out.

| Command word | What to do |
|---|---|

## Self-test

1. An L-shape is a 10 cm by 6 cm rectangle with a 4 cm by 2 cm rectangle removed from a corner. Find its area and perimeter.
2. A rectangle 12 cm by 8 cm has a semicircle on one 8 cm side. Find the area and the perimeter, to 3 significant figures.
3. A cylinder of radius 5 cm and height 10 cm has a hemisphere of radius 5 cm on top. Find the volume in terms of π.
4. A cone has volume 512 cm³. A cone with a scale factor of 1/2 is cut off the top. Find the volume of the frustum.
5. Find the area of the minor segment cut off by a chord that subtends 90° at the centre of a circle of radius 4 cm.
6. A circle of radius 3 cm is inside a square of side 6 cm, touching all sides. Find the shaded area outside the circle.
7. A cone of radius 4 cm and height 6 cm has a cone of radius 2 cm and height 3 cm cut off the top. Find the volume of the frustum in terms of π.
8. A pipe has outer radius 3 cm, inner radius 2 cm and length 10 cm. Find the volume of material in terms of π.
9. Find the volume of half a cylinder with radius 5 cm and length 8 cm, in terms of π.
10. Find the perimeter of a quarter circle of radius 6 cm.

### Answers

1. Area = 60 − 8 = **52 cm²**. Perimeter = 2(10 + 6) = **32 cm**.
2. Area = 96 + 8π = **121 cm²**. Perimeter = 12 + 12 + 8 + 4π = **44.6 cm**.
3. 250π + 250π/3 = **1000π/3 cm³** (1050 cm³).
4. 7/8 × 512 = **448 cm³**.
5. Sector = 4π, triangle = 8, segment = 4π − 8 = **4.57 cm²**.
6. 36 − 9π = **7.73 cm²**.
7. 32π − 4π = **28π cm³**.
8. π(9 − 4) × 10 = **50π cm³**.
9. 1/2 × π × 25 × 8 = **100π cm³**.
10. Arc = 1/4 × 2π × 6 = 3π. Perimeter = 3π + 12 = **21.4 cm**.
