These questions cover **3.3.1.1 Nomenclature** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 2 and able to reappear in Unit 5. Section A has short recall and application questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [nomenclature study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-1-1/) or the [nomenclature revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-1-1-revision-notes/) first. Take Ar values as C = 12.0, H = 1.0 and O = 16.0.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Which compound has the same empirical formula and molecular formula?

A: C₂H₄

B: C₄H₁₀

C: C₃H₈

D: C₆H₁₂ **[1]**

**2.** Give the IUPAC name of each compound.

**(a)** CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₃ **[1]**

**(b)** CH₃CH=CHCH₂CH₃ **[1]**

**3.** Propanal, CH₃CH₂CHO, belongs to a homologous series.

**(a)** Give the general formula of this series. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the next member of the series after propanal. **[1]**

**4.** Give the structural formula of each compound.

**(a)** 2,3-dichlorobutane **[1]**

**(b)** hexan-3-one **[1]**

**5.** Give the IUPAC name of ClCH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂OH. **[1]**

**6.** A skeletal formula shows a five-membered ring of lines with a double line from one corner to an O atom.

**(a)** Give the molecular formula of the compound. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the compound. **[1]**

### Section B

**7.** Compound P has the structural formula CH₂=C(CH₃)CH₂CH₂OH.

**(a)** Give the IUPAC name of P. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the molecular formula of P. **[1]**

**(c)** Give the empirical formula of P. **[1]**

**(d)** Draw the displayed formula of P. **[1]**

**(e)** Name a compound from a different homologous series that has the same molecular formula as P. **[1]**

**8.** Each name below is incorrect. For each, give the correct IUPAC name and state the rule that was broken.

**(a)** 2-propylbutane **[2]**

**(b)** 1,1-dimethylpropane **[2]**

**(c)** 3-methylpropan-1-ol **[2]**

**9.** An unbranched carboxylic acid has Mr = 102.0.

**(a)** Use the general formula of carboxylic acids to deduce its molecular formula. Show your working. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the acid. **[1]**

**(c)** Give the name of a carboxylic acid with the same molecular formula and a branched chain. **[1]**

**10.** Give the formula requested for each compound.

**(a)** The structural formula of 2-methylbut-2-ene **[1]**

**(b)** The molecular formula of cyclohexa-1,3-diene **[1]**

**(c)** The structural formula of 2-hydroxy-2-methylpropanoic acid **[1]**

**(d)** The molecular formula of 1,2-dibromocyclopentane **[1]**

**11.** The boiling points of the first four unbranched primary alcohols are given.

| Alcohol | methanol | ethanol | propan-1-ol | butan-1-ol |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boiling point / °C | 65 | 78 | 97 | 118 |

**(a)** Explain the trend in boiling point. **[2]**

**(b)** Predict the boiling point of pentan-1-ol. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why ethanol and butan-1-ol react in similar ways. **[1]**

**12.** Give the IUPAC name of each compound, explaining how you chose the suffix and the numbering.

**(a)** CH₃CH(OH)CH(CH₃)COOH **[2]**

**(b)** CH₂=CHCH(Br)CH=CH₂ **[2]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** C [1]

**2.**

**(a)** 2-methylpentane [1]

**(b)** pent-2-ene [1]

**Total: 2**

**3.**

**(a)** CₙH₂ₙO [1]

**(b)** Butanal [1]

**Total: 2**

**4.**

**(a)** CH₃CHClCHClCH₃ [1]

**(b)** CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₂CH₃ [1]

**Total: 2**

**5.** 4-chloro-2-methylbutan-1-ol [1]

*Examiner insight:* The –OH carbon is C1, so the numbering runs from the CH₂OH end; chloro comes before methyl alphabetically.

**6.**

**(a)** C₅H₈O [1]

**(b)** Cyclopentanone [1]

**Total: 2**

### Section B

**7.**

**(a)** 3-methylbut-3-en-1-ol [1]

**(b)** C₅H₁₀O [1]

**(c)** C₅H₁₀O [1]

**(d)** All atoms and bonds shown: H₂C=C(CH₃)–CH₂–CH₂–O–H with every C–H and the O–H bond drawn [1]

**(e)** Any one: pentanal, pentan-2-one, pentan-3-one, cyclopentanol (or another correct isomer from a different series) [1]

**Total: 5**

*Examiner insight:* In (d), an –OH written as a group rather than with the O–H bond drawn is not a displayed formula.

**8.**

**(a)** 3-methylhexane [1]; the longest carbon chain was not chosen (the propyl group continues the chain) [1]

**(b)** 2-methylbutane [1]; the longest chain was not chosen, because a methyl on C1 extends the chain [1]

**(c)** Butan-1-ol [1]; a methyl group on the end carbon lengthens the main chain, so the longest chain is four carbons [1]

**Total: 6**

**9.**

**(a)** Mr of CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH = 12.0n + (2n + 1)(1.0) + 12.0 + 32.0 + 1.0 = 14n + 46 [1]; 14n + 46 = 102, so n = 4 and the formula is C₄H₉COOH (C₅H₁₀O₂) [1]

**(b)** Pentanoic acid [1]

**(c)** 2-methylbutanoic acid, 3-methylbutanoic acid or 2,2-dimethylpropanoic acid [1]

**Total: 4**

**10.**

**(a)** (CH₃)₂C=CHCH₃ [1]

**(b)** C₆H₈ [1]

**(c)** (CH₃)₂C(OH)COOH [1]

**(d)** C₅H₈Br₂ [1]

**Total: 4**

**11.**

**(a)** Boiling point increases as the chain gets longer (each member has an extra CH₂) [1]; larger molecules have more electrons and greater surface contact, so the van der Waals forces between molecules are stronger and more energy is needed to separate them [1]

**(b)** Any value from 135 °C to 142 °C [1]

**(c)** They have the same functional group (–OH), which determines the chemical reactions [1]

**Total: 4**

*Examiner insight:* All these alcohols form hydrogen bonds, so the hydrogen bonding does not explain the trend; the increase in van der Waals forces does.

**12.**

**(a)** 3-hydroxy-2-methylbutanoic acid [1]; the carboxylic acid takes priority and is the suffix, so its carbon is C1, and –OH becomes the hydroxy prefix on C3 with methyl on C2 [1]

**(b)** 3-bromopenta-1,4-diene [1]; the two C=C bonds make it a diene with an "a" added to the stem; the chain is numbered so the double bonds have the lowest locants (1 and 4 from either end), and Br is on C3 [1]

**Total: 4**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not finding the longest chain when a branch extends it.
- Putting a locant on an aldehyde or carboxylic acid.
- Ordering prefixes by number or by di-/tri- instead of alphabetically.
- Missing the O–H bond in a displayed formula.
- Leaving the "a" out of diene names.
