## Section A

**1.** State the difference between a current account and a deposit (savings) account. **[2]**

**2.** Name **two** customer services provided by banks. **[2]**

**3.** Identify the payment method described: "A fixed amount is paid from the payer's account to the same person on the same date each month." **[1]**

**4.** State what is meant by a mortgage. **[1]**

**5.** State **one** disadvantage of digital banking. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A saver deposits $4,500 in an account paying 2.4% interest a year. Calculate the interest after one year and the new balance. **[3]**

**7.** A business borrows $9,000 for 3 years at 6% simple interest a year.

**(a)** Calculate the total interest. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the total to repay and the monthly repayment over 36 months. **[2]**

**8.** A buyer can pay $800 cash for a cooker or buy on hire purchase with a deposit of $150 and 10 monthly payments of $70.

**(a)** Calculate the total cost under hire purchase. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate how much more it costs than paying cash. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain one reason the buyer may still choose hire purchase. **[2]**

**9.** Explain the difference between a standing order and a direct debit. Give an example of when each is suitable. **[4]**

**10.** An exporter is worried that a foreign buyer might not pay. Explain how a documentary credit (letter of credit) helps. **[3]**

**11.** A shopkeeper finishes work after the bank has closed and holds a lot of cash. Recommend a banking service and justify it. **[3]**

**12.** Describe **two** impacts of digital banking on society. **[4]**

**13.** Evaluate whether a bank should close most of its branches and only offer internet and mobile banking. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** A current account is used for everyday money and has little or no interest [1]. A deposit (savings) account is for saving and earns interest, though withdrawals may be restricted [1].

**2.** Any two from: ATM; night safe; foreign currency; financial advice; telephone banking [1 each].

**3.** Standing order [1].

**4.** A long-term loan for buying property, with the property as security [1].

**5.** Any one from: needs internet access; risk of fraud or hacking; excludes customers who cannot use technology; technical failure; less face-to-face advice [1].

**6.** Interest = 2.4% × 4,500 [1] = $108 [1]. New balance = $4,608 [1].

**7.** **(a)** One year's interest = 6% × 9,000 = $540 [1]. Three years = $1,620 [1].

**(b)** Total = 9,000 + 1,620 = $10,620 [1]. Monthly = 10,620 ÷ 36 = $295 [1].

**8.** **(a)** 10 × 70 = 700 [1]; 700 + 150 = $850 [1].

**(b)** 850 − 800 = $50 [1].

**(c)** The buyer does not have $800 now and can spread the cost [1], so they can use the cooker immediately and pay from monthly income [1].

**9.** A standing order is a fixed amount that the payer orders the bank to pay on set dates [1], suitable for rent, which is the same each month [1]. A direct debit lets the receiver collect amounts that can change [1], suitable for an electricity bill that varies [1].

**10.** The exporter's bank (or the buyer's bank) promises to pay the exporter [1] once the exporter shows the correct shipping documents [1]. The exporter is therefore sure of payment even if the buyer fails to pay [1].

**11.** A night safe [1]. The shopkeeper can deposit the takings at the bank when it is closed [1], so there is less risk of theft than keeping cash in the shop or carrying it home [1].

**12.** Any two developed: bank branches close [1] because fewer customers visit, so some people, such as older customers or those in rural areas, find it harder to bank [1]; more digital-only banks start [1], which have lower costs and may charge lower fees [1]; less use of cash [1], so a cashless society develops, which can be convenient but excludes people without bank accounts or technology [1]. Maximum 4.

**13.** Any five from: digital banking saves the bank money on rent and staff [1]; customers can bank at any time [1]; but some customers cannot use technology [1]; or have poor internet access [1]; customers may value face-to-face advice, for example for a mortgage [1]; the risk of fraud and technical failure rises [1]; the bank could lose customers who want branches [1]; a justified conclusion, for example to close some branches but keep a few for advice [1]. Maximum 5.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 6: leaving out the new balance.
- Question 7: finding only one year's interest, or dividing by 3 instead of 36 for monthly payments.
- Question 8: forgetting the deposit.
- Question 9: swapping the examples or leaving out who controls the payment.
- Question 10: not saying the documents must be shown.
- Question 13: only listing and not making a judgement.
