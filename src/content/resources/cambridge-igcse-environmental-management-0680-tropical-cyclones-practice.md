These questions cover sub-topic 6.2 of Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680, 2027-2029): formation conditions, impacts, and strategies before, during and after a tropical cyclone. Places and figures in the sources are invented.

---

## Section A

**1.** State the range of latitudes in which tropical cyclones form. **[1]**

**2.** State the minimum ocean temperature and the minimum ocean depth needed for a tropical cyclone to form. **[2]**

**3.** Name the regional term used for a tropical cyclone in the north-west Pacific Ocean. **[1]**

**4.** Define *storm surge*. **[2]**

**5.** Name **two** strategies for managing the impacts of a tropical cyclone that take place before it arrives. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Explain why a deep layer of warm ocean water is needed for a tropical cyclone to keep strengthening. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why tropical cyclones weaken when they move over land. **[2]**

**8.** Source: *A cyclone made landfall on the low-lying delta coast of the invented country of Marisa. Winds reached 180 km/h. The storm surge raised sea level by 4 m and flooded rice fields up to 15 km inland. Many villagers kept their cattle with them and did not reach the shelters.*
(a) Using the source, describe **two** impacts on farming. **[2]**
(b) Explain why the effect of the flooding on rice fields may last for more than one season. **[2]**
(c) Suggest **two** reasons why villagers did not reach the shelters. **[2]**

**9.** Explain how a tropical cyclone can increase water-related disease. **[3]**

**10.** Describe **three** features of a building designed to withstand a tropical cyclone. **[3]**

**11.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of monitoring and warning as a strategy for managing the impacts of tropical cyclones. **[4]**

**12.** "A low-income country should spend its limited money on shelters rather than on rebuilding after a cyclone." Discuss this statement. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Between 5° and 30° north or south of the Equator **[1]**.

**2.** Ocean temperature of at least 27 °C **[1]**; ocean depth of at least 60 m **[1]**.

**3.** Typhoon **[1]**.

**4.** A rise in sea level **[1]** caused by strong winds pushing water onshore and low air pressure, which floods coastal land **[1]**.

**5.** Any two: monitoring and warning **[1]**; structure of buildings **[1]**; disaster preparation such as plans, drills, emergency supplies and rescue teams **[1]**. **[max 2]**

**6.** The storm's strong winds stir the ocean and bring water up from below **[1]**; if the warm layer is shallow, cold water is mixed to the surface, cutting off the heat and moisture that power the storm, so a depth of at least 60 m keeps the supply going **[1]**.

**7.** Over land there is no warm sea to supply heat and moisture through evaporation **[1]**, and friction with the land surface slows the winds **[1]**.

**8.** (a) Any two from the source: rice fields flooded up to 15 km inland, so crops lost **[1]**; cattle exposed to the storm or drowned **[1]**; farm buildings and stores damaged by 180 km/h winds **[1]**. **[max 2]**
(b) The storm surge is sea water **[1]**, so salt is left in the soil after the water drains, and rice cannot grow well until rain has washed the salt out **[1]**.
(c) Any two: they would not leave their cattle, and shelters may not accept animals **[1]**; shelters were too far away to reach on foot in time **[1]**; warnings arrived late or were not believed **[1]**; flooded or blocked roads **[1]**. **[max 2]**

**9.** Flood water and the storm surge enter wells and reservoirs **[1]**; burst sewers mix sewage with the water supply **[1]**, so people drink contaminated water carrying pathogens such as those that cause cholera, while standing water allows mosquitoes to breed **[1]**.

**10.** Any three: roof strapped or tied down to the walls **[1]**; shutters or strong glass over windows **[1]**; floor raised on stilts or a platform above the expected surge level **[1]**; reinforced concrete frame or strong foundations **[1]**; low-pitched or hipped roof that resists wind lift. **[max 3]**

**11.** Benefits: satellites, radar and aircraft track storms, giving days of warning **[1]**, so people can protect property and evacuate before the surge arrives **[1]**. Limitations: the path and strength can change, so warnings may be inaccurate and people may ignore later warnings after false alarms **[1]**; warnings may not reach remote areas without phones or radio, and are only useful if people have somewhere safe to go **[1]**. **[4]**

**12.** Award up to 6 for a balanced discussion with a judgement. For the statement: shelters save lives during the storm, when most deaths from storm surge occur **[1]**; they can be used again in every future cyclone and as schools or community centres between storms **[1]**; fewer people are injured, which reduces the demand on hospitals and emergency services after the storm **[1]**. Against: people still need homes, water supplies and roads after the storm, so rebuilding cannot be avoided **[1]**; rebuilding to stronger standards also reduces future damage **[1]**; international aid may fund rebuilding, so both can happen **[1]**. Judgement: shelters are the better use of limited money for saving lives, but rebuilding is still needed for recovery **[1]**. **[max 6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Approximate values, such as "about 26 °C", instead of the syllabus figure of at least 27 °C.
- Not using the source details, such as the 15 km of flooded rice fields or the cattle.
- Missing the link between sea water and salt left in the soil.
- Listing building features without saying what each protects against.
- Discussing only one side of a statement.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these Tropical cyclones questions from Cambridge past papers?**
No. They are original questions written for Marlbridge and do not copy past papers or their mark tariffs. Use them alongside the past papers for Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680).

**Is land use zoning a cyclone strategy in the syllabus?**
It is not on the 6.2.3 list, which names monitoring and warning, structure of buildings, disaster preparation, evacuation, shelters, rebuilding and international aid.

**Do hurricanes and typhoons form under different conditions?**
No. They are regional names for tropical cyclones, and the same conditions apply: 5° to 30° from the Equator, at least 27 °C and at least 60 m deep.
