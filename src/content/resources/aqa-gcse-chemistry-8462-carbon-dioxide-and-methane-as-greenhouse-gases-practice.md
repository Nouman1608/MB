These questions cover section **4.9.2 Carbon dioxide and methane as greenhouse gases** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry
(8462) specification, for teaching from September 2016. The content is assessed on Paper 2 at both Foundation
and Higher Tier. The specification marks nothing in this section (HT only), so no question is labelled Higher
tier only.

## Questions

**1.** Greenhouse gases keep the Earth warm enough to support life.

**(a)** Name **three** greenhouse gases. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe the greenhouse effect in terms of the radiation that reaches and leaves the Earth. **[4]**

**2.** Give **two** human activities that increase the amount of each gas in the atmosphere.

**(a)** Carbon dioxide **[2]**
**(b)** Methane **[2]**

**3.** Explain why deforestation increases the amount of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. **[2]**

**4.** A student's data show the average temperature of one city rising by 0.4 °C over 5 years. The student says, "This proves that human activities are causing global climate change."

**(a)** Explain why the data do not prove this. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe what other evidence scientists need. **[2]**

**5.** Climate scientists use computer models to predict future temperatures.

**(a)** Suggest **two** reasons why predictions from the models have uncertainty. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the importance of peer review of research on climate change. **[2]**

**6.** Describe briefly **four** potential effects of global climate change. **[4]**

**7.** A magazine article claims that cows have no effect on climate change. The article does not name its sources.

Evaluate the quality of the evidence in the article. **[3]**

**8.** Define the term carbon footprint. **[2]**

**9.** A family wants to reduce its carbon footprint.

**(a)** Describe **three** actions the family could take. **[3]**
**(b)** Give **two** reasons why these actions may be limited. **[2]**

**10.** Discuss the scale, risk and environmental implications of rising sea levels caused by global climate change. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Water vapour [1], carbon dioxide [1], methane [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** **Short wavelength** radiation from the Sun passes through the atmosphere and is **absorbed by the Earth's surface** [1]; the surface **emits long wavelength (infrared)** radiation [1]; greenhouse gases **absorb** some of it [1] and **re-radiate** it in all directions, including back towards the surface, keeping the Earth warmer [1]. **[4]**

**2. (a)** Any two [1] each: burning fossil fuels (in power stations, vehicles, homes); deforestation. **[2]**
**(b)** Any two [1] each: cattle farming; rice paddies; landfill sites; gas leaks. **[2]**

**3.** Trees take in carbon dioxide by **photosynthesis**, so fewer trees means less carbon dioxide is removed [1]; burning or rotting of the cleared trees **releases carbon dioxide** [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** Five years is a **short time** [1] and one city is not global, so natural variation could explain the change; it does not show the cause [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Data on **average global temperature** over **many decades** [1], measured at many places, and evidence linking temperature rise to **greenhouse gas concentrations**, checked by **peer review** [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Any two [1] each: the climate is a **complex system** with many variables; models are **simplified**; natural variation; limited past data. **[2]**
**(b)** Other scientists **check** the methods and conclusions before publication [1], which helps to remove errors and bias and makes the evidence more reliable [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any four [1] each: rising sea levels / coastal flooding; more extreme weather; changes in rainfall and water supplies; loss of habitats or species; changes in crop yields; melting ice caps. **[4]**

**7.** Any three [1] each: **no named sources**, so it cannot be checked; not **peer reviewed**; may be **biased** or show only part of the evidence; no data presented; scientific evidence says cattle produce **methane**, a greenhouse gas. **[3]**

**8.** The **total amount of carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases** emitted [1] over the **full life cycle** of a product, service or event [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** Any three [1] each: use less energy at home (insulation, efficient appliances); use renewable electricity; walk, cycle or use public transport; plant trees; reduce food waste; eat less beef. **[3]**
**(b)** Any two [1] each: **cost**; unwilling to change lifestyle; public transport may not be available; needs **international agreement**; technology limits. **[2]**

**10.** One mark per point up to six, with a balance of scale, risk and implications:
- Scale: many people live in coastal areas, so very large numbers could be affected [1]; low-lying countries and cities could lose land [1].
- Risk: flooding of homes, farmland and drinking water supplies by salt water [1]; storm surges become more damaging [1].
- Environmental implications: loss of coastal habitats such as wetlands and mangroves [1]; species lose breeding areas, reducing biodiversity [1].
- Cause: ice melting and the thermal expansion of warming oceans [1] (credit a link to global temperature rise).
(maximum **[6]**)

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing the greenhouse effect with the ozone layer.
- Saying greenhouse gases reflect the Sun's light.
- Giving only one activity when two are asked for.
- Not referring to sample size, time period or peer review in evidence questions.
- Missing the limits to actions (cost, lifestyle, agreement).
