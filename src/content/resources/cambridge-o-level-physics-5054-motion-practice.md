## Section A

**1.** Define velocity and explain why it is a vector quantity. **[2]**

**2.** Define acceleration and state its SI unit. **[2]**

**3.** Convert 54 km/h into m/s, showing your working. **[2]**

**4.** State what each of the following shows on a distance–time graph: **(a)** a horizontal line, **(b)** a straight sloping line, **(c)** a curve that becomes steeper, **(d)** a curve that becomes less steep. **[4]**

**5.** State what each of the following shows on a speed–time graph: **(a)** a line lying along the time axis, **(b)** a horizontal line above the time axis, **(c)** a straight line sloping upwards, **(d)** a curved line. **[4]**

## Section B

**6.** A bus travels 12 km in 20 minutes, waits at a stop for 5 minutes, then travels 8 km in 10 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the average speed for the whole journey in km/h. **[2]**
**(b)** Express this average speed in m/s. **[1]**

**7.** A car travelling at 30 m/s brakes uniformly and stops in 5.0 s.

**(a)** Calculate the acceleration of the car. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the distance travelled while braking. **[2]**

**8.** A sprinter accelerates uniformly from rest to 8.0 m/s in 2.5 s and then runs at a constant 8.0 m/s until 12.5 s after the start.

**(a)** Calculate the sprinter's acceleration. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the total distance run in 12.5 s. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the average speed over the 12.5 s. **[2]**

**9.** A cyclist's distance–time graph shows three straight sections: 300 m in the first 60 s; no change in distance from 60 s to 100 s; then a further 450 m from 100 s to 150 s.

**(a)** Calculate the speed in each section. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the average speed for the whole 150 s. **[2]**

## Section C

**10.** Explain the difference between uniform and non-uniform acceleration, giving one example of each. **[4]**

**11.** A stone is dropped from rest from a cliff and falls for 2.0 s. Air resistance is negligible.

**(a)** State the acceleration of the stone and say whether it changes during the fall. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed of the stone after 2.0 s. **[2]**
**(c)** Use the area under a speed–time graph to calculate the distance fallen in 2.0 s. **[2]**

**12.** Describe and explain the shape of the speed–time graph for a skydiver from the moment of leaving the aircraft until a steady speed is reached, before the parachute opens. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Velocity is the **change in displacement per unit time** [1]. It is a vector because displacement has a **direction**, so velocity has both magnitude and direction [1].

**2.** Acceleration is the **change in velocity per unit time** [1]; unit **m/s²** [1].

**3.** 54 km/h = 54 000 m ÷ 3600 s [1] = **15 m/s** [1].

**4. (a)** At rest [1]. **(b)** Constant speed [1]. **(c)** Accelerating [1]. **(d)** Decelerating [1].

**5. (a)** At rest [1]. **(b)** Constant speed [1]. **(c)** Constant acceleration [1]. **(d)** Changing acceleration [1].

**6. (a)** Total distance = 20 km; total time = 35 min = 0.583 h, including the stop [1]; average speed = 20 ÷ 0.583 = **34 km/h** [1].
**(b)** 20 000 m ÷ 2100 s = **9.5 m/s** [1].

**7. (a)** a = (0 − 30) ÷ 5.0 [1] = **−6.0 m/s²** (a deceleration of 6.0 m/s²) [1].
**(b)** Area of triangle = ½ × 5.0 × 30 [1] = **75 m** [1].

**8. (a)** a = 8.0 ÷ 2.5 [1] = **3.2 m/s²** [1].
**(b)** Accelerating stage: ½ × 2.5 × 8.0 = 10 m [1]; constant stage: 8.0 × 10.0 = 80 m [1]; total = **90 m** [1].
**(c)** Average speed = 90 ÷ 12.5 [1] = **7.2 m/s** [1].

**9. (a)** First section: 300 ÷ 60 = **5.0 m/s** [1]; second section: **0 m/s** (at rest) [1]; third section: 450 ÷ 50 = **9.0 m/s** [1].
**(b)** Average speed = 750 ÷ 150 [1] = **5.0 m/s** [1].

**10.** **Uniform** acceleration: velocity changes by **equal amounts in equal times** [1], for example an object in free fall with negligible air resistance [1]. **Non-uniform** acceleration: the **rate of change of velocity changes** [1], for example a car pulling away and changing gear, or a falling object as air resistance increases [1].

**11. (a)** 9.8 m/s², approximately constant throughout the fall [1].
**(b)** v = g × t = 9.8 × 2.0 [1] = **19.6 m/s** [1].
**(c)** Distance = ½ × 2.0 × 19.6 [1] = **19.6 m** [1].

**12.** At first the line is **steep and almost straight**, with gradient close to 9.8 m/s², because air resistance is small at low speed [1]. The line then **curves and becomes less steep**, showing decreasing acceleration as air resistance increases with speed [1]. Finally it becomes **horizontal**: the skydiver moves at a constant (terminal) speed once air resistance equals weight [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the waiting time when calculating average speed.
- Averaging two speeds instead of dividing total distance by total time.
- Reading a horizontal speed–time line as "stationary".
- Using the area under a distance–time graph.
- Forgetting the unit m/s², or writing m/s for an acceleration.
- Losing the minus sign for a deceleration.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these motion questions from past papers?**
No. They are original practice questions for subtopic 1.2 and do not copy the exam's structure, question count or mark tariffs.

**Why does Question 12 mention air resistance?**
The syllabus asks you to recognise changing acceleration on a speed–time graph. A skydiver is the standard example; the force explanation is developed further in subtopic 1.5.

**Do I need equations of motion beyond those listed?**
No. Subtopic 1.2 uses speed = distance ÷ time, average speed, a = Δv ÷ Δt, gradients and areas.
