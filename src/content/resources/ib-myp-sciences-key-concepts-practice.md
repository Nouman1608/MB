This practice set tests the key concepts of IB MYP Sciences -- change, relationships and systems -- and how they build statements of inquiry and inquiry questions. It follows the IB *MYP Sciences guide* (for use from September 2014/January 2015; published May 2014, updated May 2019), and suits MYP years 4 and 5 (there is no SL/HL split). MYP has no prescribed content list; schools design their own units, but the key concepts are prescribed for all of them.

Each question is labelled with the criterion it trains: A Knowing and understanding, B Inquiring and designing, C Processing and evaluating, D Reflecting on the impacts of science. Real MYP work is judged against criterion level descriptors (achievement levels 1–8), so the `[1]` points in the answers are a revision aid, not IB marks. All data in the questions are fictional.

Study first with the [key concepts study guide](/resources/ib-myp-sciences-key-concepts/) and [revision notes](/resources/ib-myp-sciences-key-concepts-revision-notes/). Also useful: the [course hub](/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-sciences/), the [checklist](/checklists/ib/ib-myp/myp-sciences/) and the [criteria in practice questions](/resources/ib-myp-sciences-criteria-in-practice-practice/).

## Questions

**1.** (Criterion A)

**(a)** Name the three key concepts the IB guide gives for MYP sciences. **[1]**
**(b)** Name two other key concepts the guide says can also be important in sciences. **[2]**

**2.** (Criterion A) Classify each change as reversible, irreversible or self-perpetuating, giving a reason for each.

**(a)** Frying an egg. **[1]**
**(b)** Water vapour condensing on a cold window. **[1]**
**(c)** A nuclear fission chain reaction. **[1]**

**3.** (Criterion A)

**(a)** Using the guide's meaning, distinguish between an open system and a closed system. Give one example of each. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why scientists building a model often treat the system as closed. **[2]**

**4.** (Criterion C) A student times a pendulum's swing (period, T) for four lengths (L).

| L (m) | 0.20 | 0.40 | 0.80 | 1.60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T (s) | 0.90 | 1.27 | 1.79 | 2.54 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship between L and T. State whether T is directly proportional to L, using the data. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate T²/L for the first and last rows and comment on the result. **[2]**
**(c)** Predict the period for a length of 3.20 m. **[1]**

**5.** (Criterion B) A student wants to investigate the relationship between the thickness of insulation around a beaker and how fast hot water in it cools.

**(a)** State the independent and dependent variables. **[2]**
**(b)** Write a testable hypothesis with a scientific reason. **[2]**
**(c)** State one variable to control and how to control it. **[1]**

**6.** (Criterion C) Marble chips (calcium carbonate) react with dilute hydrochloric acid, releasing carbon dioxide. The reaction is run twice, starting at a total mass of 152.40 g each time.

- Flask A, open to the air: final mass 151.08 g.
- Flask B, sealed with a bung: final mass 152.40 g.

**(a)** Calculate the mass lost from flask A as a percentage of its starting mass. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the difference between the two results using the key concept of systems. **[2]**

**7.** (Criterion A) Explain how form relates to function in each case.

**(a)** The alveoli in human lungs. **[2]**
**(b)** The long, pointed nose of a high-speed train. **[2]**

**8.** (Criterion A) Classify each inquiry question as factual, conceptual or debatable.

**(a)** What is the boiling point of pure water at sea level?
**(b)** How does the structure of a substance affect how it behaves?
**(c)** Should genetically modified crops be grown where food is scarce? **[3]**

**9.** (Criteria A and D) A teacher plans a chemistry unit on fuels and combustion. The key concept is change, the related concepts are energy and consequences, and the global context is globalization and sustainability.

**(a)** Write a statement of inquiry for this unit. **[2]**
**(b)** Write one conceptual and one debatable inquiry question that come from your statement. **[2]**

**10.** (Criterion C) A fictional bean seedling is measured as it grows.

| Day | 0 | 8 |
|---|---|---|
| Height (mm) | 15 | 63 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean growth rate in mm per day. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in height. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe one qualitative change you would expect to observe over the same eight days. **[1]**

**11.** (Criterion D) A fictional island, Kelmora, gets all its electricity from a diesel power station. Its council proposes replacing it with solar panels and a large battery store. Using the key concepts of change, relationships and systems, evaluate the proposal. Include at least one strength, one limitation and a conclusion. **[8]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Change, relationships and systems (all three needed). [1]
**(b)** Any two from: development [1]; time, place and space [1]; creativity. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Energy" and "models" are related concepts, not key concepts, so naming them in (b) earns nothing however well you know them.

**2. (a)** Irreversible: the egg proteins change permanently and cannot return to their raw state. [1]
**(b)** Reversible: the water can evaporate again if the window warms. [1]
**(c)** Self-perpetuating: neutrons released by each fission split further nuclei, which release more neutrons. [1]
*Examiner insight:* A classification with no reason is a bare guess; each point here needs the "because" to show understanding.

**3. (a)** A closed system has resources that are not removed or replaced, for example a sealed jar with a burning candle; [1] an open system has its resources renewed, for example a river or pond. [1]
**(b)** Treating the system as closed fixes what can enter or leave, [1] so there are fewer variables and the model is simpler to build and test. [1]
*Examiner insight:* An example that clearly breaks the definition, such as calling an aquarium with a filter pump "closed", cancels the credit for the definition.

**4. (a)** As L increases, T increases. [1] T is not directly proportional to L: doubling L from 0.40 m to 0.80 m multiplies T by only about 1.4, not 2. [1]
**(b)** 0.90² ÷ 0.20 = **4.05 s²/m**; 2.54² ÷ 1.60 = **4.03 s²/m**. [1] T²/L is roughly constant (about 4.0), so T² is proportional to L. [1]
**(c)** T = √(4.03 × 3.20) = **3.59 s** (accept 3.6 s). [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Proportional" needs evidence from the numbers; saying "T goes up with L so they are proportional" loses the second point in (a).

**5. (a)** Independent: thickness of insulation; [1] dependent: rate of cooling (temperature fall in a fixed time). [1]
**(b)** The thicker the insulation, the slower the water cools, [1] because thicker insulation reduces the rate of energy transfer to the surroundings. [1]
**(c)** Starting temperature of the water, for example heat all water to 80 °C before timing. [1]
*Examiner insight:* A controlled variable earns credit only with a way of controlling it; "keep it fair" names nothing.

**6. (a)** Mass lost = 152.40 − 151.08 = 1.32 g. [1] Percentage = 1.32 ÷ 152.40 × 100 = **0.87%**. [1]
**(b)** Flask A is an open system: the carbon dioxide gas escapes into the air, so mass is lost. [1] Flask B is closed: no matter leaves, so total mass stays the same. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Stopping at 0.0087 (the fraction) without multiplying by 100 gives a different quantity and loses the accuracy point, even with the subtraction right.

**7. (a)** There are very many alveoli, giving a large surface area, [1] and their walls are one cell thick, giving a short diffusion distance, so gas exchange is fast. [1]
**(b)** The streamlined nose lets air flow smoothly around the train, [1] which reduces air resistance, so less energy is needed at high speed. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each answer needs both halves: a feature alone ("thin walls") or a function alone ("for gas exchange") earns half at most.

**8. (a)** Factual. [1]
**(b)** Conceptual. [1]
**(c)** Debatable. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Question (b) has no single look-up answer, yet it is not debatable either; it asks you to explain a pattern that transfers, which is the mark of a conceptual question.

**9. (a)** For example: "Changes that release energy can have consequences for the environment that are felt far beyond where the change happens." Credit: uses change, energy and consequences; [1] links to sustainability and does not just name the topic. [1]
**(b)** Conceptual, for example: "How does the energy released by a chemical change depend on the substances involved?" [1] Debatable, for example: "Should wealthy countries cut fossil fuel use faster than poorer ones?" [1]
*Examiner insight:* A statement such as "Burning fuels releases carbon dioxide" is a topic fact; it does not transfer to other units and earns no credit.

**10. (a)** (63 − 15) ÷ 8 = **6.0 mm per day**. [1]
**(b)** Increase = 48 mm; [1] 48 ÷ 15 × 100 = **320%**. [1]
**(c)** For example: the first leaves unfold and turn green. [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), percentage change uses the starting value as the base; dividing by 63 gives 76% and loses the accuracy point.

**11.** Indicative points:
- Change: switching sources changes the island's energy system from burning fuel to transferring light energy into electrical energy. [1]
- Strength (change and consequences): solar panels release no carbon dioxide when generating, so emissions from electricity fall. [1]
- Strength (systems): the island no longer depends on imported diesel, an input from outside its system that can be delayed or rise in price. [1]
- Relationships: solar output depends on light intensity, so it varies with time of day, season and cloud cover. [1]
- Limitation (systems): at night, output is zero, so the battery store becomes a vital component; if it is too small, supply fails. [1]
- Limitation: making panels and batteries uses energy and raw materials, and batteries must be recycled or disposed of at the end of their life. [1]
- Weighing up: whether the plan works depends on matching battery capacity to night-time demand, and perhaps keeping the diesel station as a backup at first. [1]
- Conclusion: a reasoned judgement, for example that the proposal is sound if storage is sized for demand, with a phased change-over. [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* An answer that lists strengths and limitations but never weighs them into a judgement stays in the lower criterion D bands, however accurate the science.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming related concepts (energy, models, evidence) when a key concept is asked for.
- Classifying a change as reversible or irreversible with no reason.
- Claiming proportionality without checking the numbers: doubling one variable must double the other.
- Using the final value as the base in a percentage change.
- Giving a form with no function, or a function with no form.
- Calling a system closed when matter clearly enters or leaves it.
- Writing a statement of inquiry that only restates the topic.
- Listing pros and cons in a criterion D answer without reaching a judgement.
- Controlled variables listed without a method of controlling them.
