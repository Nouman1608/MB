These questions cover **section 3.2.2.3 Employment and unemployment** in the AQA GCSE Economics (8136) specification for first teaching from September 2017: how employment and unemployment are measured, the types, causes and consequences of unemployment, and policies to reduce it. There are no tiers. All countries, towns and figures are invented. For explanations, see the [employment and unemployment study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-economics-8136-employment-and-unemployment/) and the [employment and unemployment revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-economics-8136-employment-and-unemployment-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following people is economically inactive? **[1]**

A: A part-time waiter who would like more hours

B: A retired teacher who is not looking for work

C: A graduate applying for jobs who could start next week

D: A farm worker laid off for the winter who is looking for work

**2.** Which **one** of the following policies is most likely to reduce structural unemployment? **[1]**

A: A cut in the Bank Rate

B: A government-funded retraining scheme

C: A rise in income tax

D: A rise in VAT

**3.** State what is meant by the labour force. **[1]**

**4.** Country K has a working-age population of 18.0 million: 13.5 million are employed, 0.9 million are unemployed and 3.6 million are economically inactive.

**(a)** Calculate the unemployment rate to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the employment rate. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the inactivity rate. **[1]**

**5.** Country L has a labour force of 6.0 million and an unemployment rate of 5.5%.

**(a)** Calculate the number of people unemployed. **[1]**

**(b)** A year later the labour force is 6.2 million and 0.31 million people are unemployed. Calculate the new unemployment rate. **[1]**

**(c)** State the change in the unemployment rate in percentage points. **[1]**

**6.** Identify the type of unemployment in each case. **[4]**

- A fruit picker out of work between harvests.
- A bank clerk whose job is replaced by online banking and who has no other skills.
- A nurse who has resigned and is taking a month to choose between job offers.
- A furniture-shop worker laid off when spending falls across the whole economy.

**7.** Explain **one** reason why the claimant count may rise even though ILO unemployment has not changed. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**8.** Explain **two** causes of structural unemployment. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how a recession can lead to cyclical unemployment. **[3]**

**10.** Explain **two** consequences of rising unemployment for firms. **[4]**

**11.** Harbourton is an invented coastal town. Its fishing industry has declined for twenty years, and most remaining jobs are in hotels that close from October to March.

**(a)** Identify **two** types of unemployment likely to be found in Harbourton. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why a cut in interest rates may do little to reduce unemployment in Harbourton. **[3]**

**12.** A steelworks in the invented region of Northvale has closed, and 4,000 workers have lost their jobs. The government is choosing between two policies:

- Policy A: extra spending on road building across the whole country.
- Policy B: funded retraining courses and relocation grants for Northvale workers.

Evaluate which policy is more likely to reduce unemployment in Northvale. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. A part-time worker is employed; C and D are unemployed because they are available and looking for work. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. Retraining tackles the mismatch between workers' skills and the jobs available. **[1]**

**3.** The number of people who are employed plus those who are unemployed (available for and seeking work) [1]. **[1]**

**4. (a)** Labour force = 13.5 + 0.9 = 14.4 million [1]. Rate = 0.9 ÷ 14.4 × 100 = **6.3%** (6.25%) [1].

**(b)** 13.5 ÷ 18.0 × 100 = **75%** [1].

**(c)** 3.6 ÷ 18.0 × 100 = **20%** [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* The unemployment rate uses the labour force; the employment and inactivity rates use the working-age population.

**5. (a)** 0.055 × 6.0 = **0.33 million** (330,000) [1].

**(b)** 0.31 ÷ 6.2 × 100 = **5.0%** [1].

**(c)** A fall of **0.5 percentage points** [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Fruit picker: seasonal [1]. Bank clerk: structural [1]. Nurse: frictional [1]. Furniture-shop worker: cyclical [1]. **[4]**

**7.** A change in benefit rules may make more people eligible to claim [1], so more are counted as claimants even though the number without work, available and looking for work is the same [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Any two developed causes: new technology replaces workers in an industry [1], and their skills are not needed by other employers [1]; cheaper imports cause a domestic industry to decline [1], so workers lose jobs and lack skills for growing industries [1]; geographical immobility [1] means workers cannot move to areas with vacancies, for example because housing costs more there [1]. **[4]**

**9.** In a recession total spending in the economy falls [1]. Firms sell less, so they cut output [1] and need fewer workers, so they make staff redundant in many industries at once [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Any two developed consequences: unemployed people have less income to spend [1], so firms selling non-essential goods see sales and profits fall [1]; firms receive more applicants for each vacancy [1], so recruitment is easier and there is less pressure to raise wages, which keeps costs down [1]; discount retailers may gain [1] as households switch to cheaper goods [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** Structural (decline of fishing) [1] and seasonal (hotels closing in winter) [1].

**(b)** Lower interest rates raise spending by making borrowing cheaper [1], which helps when unemployment is caused by too little demand [1]. Harbourton's unemployment comes from the permanent decline of fishing and from the season, so extra spending does not give fishers new skills or keep hotels open in winter [1]. **[5]**

**12.** *Policy A:*

- Road building creates construction jobs and raises demand across the economy [1].
- But the jobs may not be in Northvale, and steelworkers may lack construction skills, so the effect on Northvale is uncertain [1] [1].

*Policy B:*

- Retraining tackles structural unemployment by giving workers skills in growing industries [1].
- Relocation grants reduce geographical immobility, so workers can take jobs elsewhere [1].
- But courses take time and only work if they match employers' needs; relocation may move unemployment out of Northvale rather than create local jobs [1] [1].

*Judgement:* Policy B is more likely to help because the closure caused structural unemployment, which extra demand does not cure [1]; it would work best if combined with support for new firms in Northvale so retrained workers can stay [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* Start the evaluation by naming the type of unemployment in the case; the right policy follows from the cause.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by the working-age population when the question asks for the unemployment rate.
- Writing "a 0.5% fall" when the rate fell by 0.5 percentage points.
- Classifying a recession job loss as structural.
- Giving consequences for firms that are all negative, with no mention of easier recruitment or lower wage pressure.
- Reaching a judgement that ignores the cause of unemployment in the case.
