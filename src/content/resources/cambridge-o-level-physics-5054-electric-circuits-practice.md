## Section A

**1.** State the rule for the p.d.s across components **(a)** in a series circuit, and **(b)** across branches in parallel. **[2]**

**2.** A current of 3.0 A enters a junction and divides into two branches. One branch carries 1.2 A. State the current in the other branch. **[1]**

**3.** Calculate the combined resistance of resistors of 6.0 Ω, 8.0 Ω and 10 Ω connected in series. **[1]**

**4.** Calculate the combined resistance of a 20 Ω resistor and a 30 Ω resistor connected in parallel. **[2]**

**5.** State how the resistance changes for **(a)** a light-dependent resistor when the light intensity increases, and **(b)** an NTC thermistor when its temperature increases. **[2]**

**6.** State the function in a circuit of **(a)** a diode and **(b)** a relay. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Resistors of 8.0 Ω and 4.0 Ω are connected in series with a 24 V supply.

**(a)** Calculate the combined resistance. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the current in the circuit. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the p.d. across each resistor. **[2]**
**(d)** Show that your answers to (c) agree with the potential divider equation R₁ / R₂ = V₁ / V₂. **[1]**

**8.** A 20 Ω resistor and a 5.0 Ω resistor are connected in parallel. This pair is connected in series with a 6.0 Ω resistor and a 12 V supply.

**(a)** Calculate the combined resistance of the parallel pair. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the total resistance and the current from the supply. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the p.d. across the parallel pair. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the current in the 20 Ω resistor. **[1]**

**9.** Two identical lamps, each of resistance 6.0 Ω, are connected in parallel across a 3.0 V supply. Assume the resistance of each lamp stays constant.

**(a)** Calculate the current in each lamp and the current from the supply. **[2]**
**(b)** A third identical lamp is connected in parallel with the others. State the new supply current and the effect on the brightness of the first two lamps. **[2]**

**10.** Resistors R₁ = 2.0 kΩ and R₂ = 6.0 kΩ are connected in series across a 10 V supply. Calculate the p.d. across each resistor. **[3]**

**11.** A night light uses an LDR in series with a fixed 10 kΩ resistor across a 9.0 V supply. The output p.d. is taken across the LDR. In daylight the LDR has a resistance of 1.0 kΩ; in the dark it is 80 kΩ.

**(a)** Calculate the output p.d. in daylight and in the dark. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why this arrangement is suitable for switching on a lamp when it gets dark. **[2]**

**12.** An overheat alarm uses an NTC thermistor in series with a fixed resistor. The alarm sounds when the p.d. across the fixed resistor rises above a set value.

**(a)** Explain why the p.d. across the fixed resistor rises as the temperature rises. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest how the circuit could be changed so that the alarm sounds at a lower temperature. **[1]**

**13.** Describe how a resistor with a sliding contact can be used as a variable potential divider, and state the range of output p.d. available from a 6.0 V supply. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The p.d.s across the components **add up to the total (supply) p.d.** [1].
**(b)** The **p.d. across each branch is the same** as across the whole parallel arrangement [1].

**2.** 3.0 − 1.2 = **1.8 A** [1].

**3.** 6.0 + 8.0 + 10 = **24 Ω** [1].

**4.** R = (20 × 30) ÷ (20 + 30) = 600 ÷ 50 [1] = **12 Ω** [1].

**5. (a)** It **decreases** [1].
**(b)** It **decreases** [1].

**6. (a)** Allows current to flow **in one direction only** [1].
**(b)** Uses a **small current in its coil to switch on or off a separate circuit** (which may carry a larger current) [1].

**7. (a)** 8.0 + 4.0 = **12 Ω** [1].
**(b)** I = V ÷ R = 24 ÷ 12 [1] = **2.0 A** [1].
**(c)** 8.0 Ω: 2.0 × 8.0 = **16 V** [1]; 4.0 Ω: 2.0 × 4.0 = **8.0 V** [1].
**(d)** R₁ / R₂ = 8.0 / 4.0 = 2 and V₁ / V₂ = 16 / 8.0 = 2, so the ratios are equal [1].

**8. (a)** R = (20 × 5.0) ÷ (20 + 5.0) = 100 ÷ 25 [1] = **4.0 Ω** [1].
**(b)** Total = 4.0 + 6.0 = **10 Ω** [1]; I = 12 ÷ 10 = **1.2 A** [1].
**(c)** P.d. across 6.0 Ω = 1.2 × 6.0 = 7.2 V [1]; p.d. across the pair = 12 − 7.2 = **4.8 V** [1].
**(d)** I = 4.8 ÷ 20 = **0.24 A** [1].

**9. (a)** Each lamp: I = 3.0 ÷ 6.0 = **0.50 A** [1]; supply current = 0.50 + 0.50 = **1.0 A** [1].
**(b)** Supply current = 3 × 0.50 = **1.5 A** [1]; the first two lamps are **equally bright as before**, because each still has the full 3.0 V across it [1].

**10.** Total R = 8.0 kΩ, so V₁ = 10 × 2.0 ÷ 8.0 [1] = **2.5 V** [1]; V₂ = 10 − 2.5 = **7.5 V** [1].

**11. (a)** Daylight: V = 9.0 × 1.0 ÷ (1.0 + 10) [1] = **0.82 V** [1]. Dark: V = 9.0 × 80 ÷ (80 + 10) [1] = **8.0 V** [1].
**(b)** In the dark the **LDR's resistance rises greatly, so it takes most of the supply p.d.** [1]; the **output rises from under 1 V to 8.0 V**, a large change that can be used to switch on the lamp (for example through a relay) [1].

**12. (a)** As temperature rises, the **thermistor's resistance decreases** [1]. It therefore takes a **smaller share of the supply p.d.** [1], so the **fixed resistor takes a larger share** and its p.d. rises [1].
**(b)** **Replace the fixed resistor with a variable resistor and increase its resistance** (or use a larger fixed resistor), so the set p.d. is reached at a smaller fall in thermistor resistance [1].

**13.** The **resistor is connected across the supply and the output is taken between the sliding contact and one end** [1]; moving the slider gives an output that varies **continuously from 0 V to 6.0 V** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding parallel resistances as if in series, or forgetting to take the reciprocal at the end.
- Using the full supply p.d. across one resistor in a series circuit.
- Not checking that branch currents add to the supply current.
- Saying an NTC thermistor's resistance rises with temperature.
- Explaining sensor circuits with only one step; examiners expect the change in resistance, then the change in share of p.d., then the output.
- Claiming lamps get dimmer when more are added in parallel; each keeps the full supply p.d.
