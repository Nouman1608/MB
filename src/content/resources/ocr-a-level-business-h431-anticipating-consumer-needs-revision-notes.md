Condensed notes on the Anticipating consumer needs content (3.4 on this site) in Customer needs, OCR A Level Business H431, Version 2.1 (January 2026), final assessment summer 2027. Full explanations and the Marlow Chocolate and Fernlea Footwear worked examples are in the [anticipating consumer needs study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-anticipating-consumer-needs/); test yourself with the [anticipating consumer needs practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-anticipating-consumer-needs-practice/). The overview of the whole topic is in the [marketing and marketing strategies revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-marketing-and-marketing-strategies-revision-notes/).

## The specification points

1. Explain buying behaviour including impulse and planned purchases.
2. Distinguish niche marketing and mass marketing, and evaluate their usefulness.
3. Explain consumer buyer behaviour through AIDA, hierarchy of effects/DAGMAR and ATR, and evaluate the models.
4. Evaluate the impact and importance of a business' decisions on, and of understanding, buyer behaviour.
5. Distinguish customer and product orientation and evaluate their importance.
6. Evaluate the importance of customer service in buyer behaviour.
7. Explain market segmentation and ways to segment, evaluate its importance, and recommend and justify a strategy.

## Core recall

### Buying behaviour

| | Planned purchase | Impulse purchase |
|---|---|---|
| Decision made | Before reaching the point of sale, often after research | On the spot |
| Typical goods | Laptops, holidays, appliances | Sweets at the till, sale items |
| What matters | Information, reviews, price, comparison | Display, layout, promotion, mood |

Business decisions (price, layout, website speed, delivery) shape behaviour. Understanding it cuts wasted spending and raises repeat purchases. Ethical limit: avoid pressure on vulnerable buyers.

### Niche against mass

| | Niche | Mass |
|---|---|---|
| Market | Small, specific segment | Large, broad market |
| Margin | Usually higher | Usually lower |
| Competition | Often less | Large rivals, price competition |
| Risk | Depends on a small group | Spreads risk; needs big marketing budget |
| Suits | Small firms; strong brands | Firms with scale and wide distribution |

### Models

| Model | Stages |
|---|---|
| AIDA | Attention, Interest, Desire, Action |
| Hierarchy of effects | Awareness, knowledge, liking, preference, conviction, purchase |
| DAGMAR | Unaware, aware, comprehension, conviction, action, with measurable goals and dates |
| ATR | Awareness, Trial, Reinforcement |

Usefulness: simple framework; shows where the campaign loses people; DAGMAR gives measurable goals. Limits: buyers skip stages (impulse); ignores price, social influence, rivals; hard to measure each stage.

### Orientation and service

- Customer-oriented: starts from customer needs and research. Higher satisfaction, fewer failed launches, but research cost and customers may not foresee needs.
- Product-oriented: starts from what the firm can make. Can produce breakthroughs, but may not match demand.
- Customer service: affects return visits, reviews and loyalty; costs staff and training; most important where the purchase is planned, costly or repeated.

### Segmentation

| Basis | Example |
|---|---|
| Demographic | Age, gender, income, family size |
| Geographic | Region, urban or rural |
| Behavioural | Usage rate, reason for buying |
| Psychographic | Lifestyle, values |
| Socio-economic | Occupation, income group |

Benefits: tailored mix; less waste; higher prices possible. Drawbacks: research cost; more versions; segments may be too small. To recommend: choose segments by size, spend, achievable share and competition, then state the risk.

## Quick worked reminder

Marlow Chocolate (invented): niche revenue 3,000 × £220 = £660,000; margin 35% = £231,000; less marketing £40,000 gives £191,000. Mass revenue 20,000 × £35 = £700,000; margin 20% = £140,000; less £90,000 gives £50,000.

AIDA: 100,000 see; interest 12% = 12,000; desire 25% = 3,000; action 30% = 900. Conversion 0.9%. Cost £15,000 gives £16.67 per purchase. With interest at 15%, 1,125 purchases and £13.33 each.

Segments: expected sales = people × spend × achievable share (for example 55,000 × £110 × 6% = £363,000).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Impulse purchase | An unplanned purchase made on the spot |
| Niche marketing | Targeting a small, specific segment with a tailored mix |
| Mass marketing | Selling one product to a broad market |
| AIDA | Attention, Interest, Desire, Action |
| DAGMAR | A model setting measurable communication goals for advertising |
| ATR | Awareness, Trial, Reinforcement |
| Customer orientation | Designing products from customer needs |
| Market segmentation | Dividing a market into groups with similar needs |

## Exam traps

- Apply the model to the campaign in the case, not just list the letters.
- Customer orientation is not always better.
- Choose a segment using spend, share and competition, not size alone.
- Give both a benefit and a limit when evaluating.

## Evaluation prompts

- Niche or mass: which gives the better contribution after marketing costs, and how much risk comes from depending on a small group?
- Models: does the campaign lose most people at one stage? That stage is the one to improve.
- Orientation: how far can customers tell the business what they want? A new technology may need a product-led start.
- Segments: do the estimates of share rest on evidence? A trial can test them before the full launch.
- Always finish with a judgement tied to the size, resources and aims of the business in the case.

## Command words

- **Explain:** define the model or term and apply it to the case.
- **Distinguish:** state the difference between niche and mass, or customer and product orientation.
- **Evaluate:** give a benefit and a limit of the model or method for the business, then judge.
- **Recommend and justify:** choose segments using spend, share and competition, and state a risk.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish planned and impulse purchases.
2. Give one way a retailer encourages impulse buying.
3. What does the A in ATR stand for, and what is reinforcement?
4. State the four stages of AIDA.
5. Give one limit of marketing models.
6. 60,000 people see an advert; 10% become interested and 20% of those desire the product. How many desire it?
7. Define customer orientation.
8. Give one risk of product orientation.
9. Name two bases for segmenting a market.
10. A segment has 20,000 people spending £50 a year each, and the firm expects a 4% share. Find the expected sales.
11. Give one drawback of segmentation.
12. Why might a niche strategy suit a small firm?

### Answers

1. Planned: decided before the point of sale, often after research. Impulse: unplanned, on the spot.
2. Any one: checkout displays, store layout, limited-time offers.
3. Awareness. Reinforcement encourages regular buyers to keep buying and feel the choice was right.
4. Attention, Interest, Desire, Action.
5. Any one: buyers skip stages; ignores price and rivals; hard to measure.
6. 60,000 × 0.10 = 6,000; 6,000 × 0.20 = **1,200** people.
7. Starting from customer needs and research and designing the product to fit.
8. The product may not match what customers want.
9. Any two: demographic, geographic, behavioural, psychographic, socio-economic.
10. 20,000 × 50 = £1,000,000; × 4% = **£40,000**.
11. Any one: research cost; more product versions; segments may be too small.
12. It avoids competing directly with large rivals and can earn higher margins from loyal customers.
