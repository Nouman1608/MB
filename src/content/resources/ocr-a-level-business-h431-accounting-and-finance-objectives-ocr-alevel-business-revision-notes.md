## What the specification lists

- Explain accounting and finance objectives; how and why a business uses them; the need for clear objectives.
- Evaluate the usefulness of the objectives to a business and its stakeholders.
- Evaluate the importance of the objectives in achieving business objectives.
- Recommend and justify objectives for a business.

## Core recall

### Meaning

- **Accounting and finance objective:** a measurable target for the finance function over a period.
- It turns a business objective (such as growth) into numbers.

### Main types and measures

| Type | Example | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| Profit | Net margin 8% | Net profit ÷ revenue × 100 |
| Return | ROCE at least 12% | Operating profit ÷ capital employed × 100 |
| Cash and liquidity | Cash above £50,000; current ratio 1.5 | Cash; current assets ÷ current liabilities |
| Capital structure | Gearing at most 40% | Non-current liabilities ÷ capital employed × 100 |
| Growth | Revenue up 20% in 3 years | Growth rate |
| Cost control | Overheads down 5% | Actual against budget |
| Shareholder return | Dividend 15p a share | Dividend per share |
| Efficiency | Debtor days 45 | Receivables ÷ revenue × 365 |

### SMART test

Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. "Raise the net margin from 7% to 8% by next year" passes; "make more profit" fails.

### Why used

- Set budgets and plans.
- Guide decisions such as loan or shares.
- Judge performance by comparing with targets.
- Motivate and reward managers.
- Communicate with stakeholders.
- Control risk through liquidity and gearing limits.

### Why they must be clear

- Managers pull in different directions without them.
- No standard for judging performance.
- Stakeholders cannot assess the business.
- Funding and investment have no rule.

### Usefulness: stakeholder view

| Stakeholder | Use |
|---|---|
| Owners | Is the return worth the investment? |
| Managers | Clear targets; bonuses |
| Lenders | Gearing and liquidity limits give comfort |
| Employees | Stability, but cost-cutting pressure |
| Suppliers | Signs that bills will be paid |

### Limits

- Based on forecasts and history.
- Targets can conflict (profit against training).
- Short-term action can meet the target and damage the future.
- Non-financial aims are missed.

### Link to business objectives

Finance objectives make business objectives testable and affordable, and may constrain them. For example, a gearing limit can rule out a debt-funded expansion.

### Recommend and justify

Choose two or three SMART objectives, link them to the business aim, justify with figures, name a risk and give a judgement.

## Quick worked reminder

Brackenfield Dairy (invented): revenue £4,000,000, net profit £280,000, operating profit £330,000, capital employed £2,100,000, non-current liabilities £700,000, current assets £600,000, current liabilities £380,000. Net margin 7.0% (target 8%: missed); ROCE 15.7% (target 12%: met); gearing 33.3% (limit 40%: met); current ratio 1.58 (target 1.5: met). A £600,000 loan would take gearing to 48.1%, above the limit.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Accounting and finance objective | A measurable target for the finance function |
| Net profit margin | Net profit as a percentage of revenue |
| ROCE | Operating profit as a percentage of capital employed |
| Capital employed | Equity plus non-current liabilities |
| Gearing | Non-current liabilities as a percentage of capital employed |
| Liquidity | Ability to pay short-term debts |
| Current ratio | Current assets divided by current liabilities |
| SMART | Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound |

## Exam traps

- A finance objective is a figure; "grow the business" is a business objective.
- Say what the objective is used for, not just what it is.
- Show the calculation when you test an objective.
- Mention a conflict between objectives where there is one.
- In recommend questions, include numbers and a time limit.

## Self-test

1. Define an accounting and finance objective.
2. Give two examples of finance objectives.
3. What does SMART stand for?
4. Revenue is £1,500,000 and net profit £150,000. Calculate the net profit margin.
5. Operating profit is £170,000 and capital employed £1,000,000. Calculate ROCE.
6. Non-current liabilities are £350,000 and capital employed £1,000,000. Calculate gearing.
7. Give one reason why a lender cares about gearing.
8. Give one conflict between two finance objectives.
9. Explain one use of finance objectives to a manager.
10. Why might a profit target encourage short-termism?
11. State one limit of finance objectives.
12. Gearing limit is 40%. Gearing is 35%. Is the objective met?

### Answers

1. A measurable target for the finance function, set for a period, such as a profit margin or cash balance.
2. Any two: net profit margin, ROCE, cash balance, gearing limit, dividend per share, cost reduction.
3. Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.
4. 150,000 ÷ 1,500,000 × 100 = **10%**.
5. 170,000 ÷ 1,000,000 × 100 = **17%**.
6. 350,000 ÷ 1,000,000 × 100 = **35%**.
7. High gearing means high fixed interest, so the risk that the business cannot repay is greater.
8. A high margin target may require cutting training or marketing spend, which conflicts with a revenue growth target.
9. Targets guide budgets and decisions, and results can be judged against them.
10. Managers may cut maintenance or training to raise this year's profit, harming future profit.
11. They are based on forecasts and history, and ignore non-financial aims.
12. Yes: 35% is below the 40% limit.
