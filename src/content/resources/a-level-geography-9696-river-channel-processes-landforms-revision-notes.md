Condensed recall for **1.3 River channel processes and landforms** (erosion, transport, deposition). For full explanations, read the [Core Physical Geography study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-geography-core-physical-geography/).

## Processes

| Type | Processes |
|---|---|
| Erosion | **Hydraulic action** (force of water in cracks), **abrasion** (load scraping bed and banks), **attrition** (load particles wearing each other smaller and rounder), **solution** (dissolving of soluble rock) |
| Transport | **Traction** (rolled along the bed), **saltation** (bouncing), **suspension** (carried in the flow), **solution** (dissolved) |
| Deposition | Occurs when velocity falls and the river lacks energy to carry its load |

## More key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Competence | The largest particle a river can carry at a given velocity |
| Capacity | The total load a river can carry |
| Bedload | Coarse load moved by traction and saltation |
| Thalweg | Line of fastest flow along a river |
| Helicoidal flow | Corkscrew flow around a bend, moving water across the channel |
| Vertical / lateral erosion | Downcutting of the bed / sideways erosion of the banks |
| Flocculation | Fine clay particles clumping together in salt water and settling |
| Sinuosity | Channel length divided by straight-line valley length |

**Controls on erosion:** velocity and discharge (more energy), load (more tools for abrasion), rock resistance, and channel shape. Most erosion and transport happen during high flows, so a few floods can do more work than months of normal flow.

**Why deposition happens:** velocity falls where the river enters a lake or the sea, on the inside of bends, when discharge drops after a flood, where overbank water spreads onto the floodplain, and where the load is suddenly increased, for example by a landslide.

## Hjulström curve

Plots particle size against flow velocity and shows fields of erosion, transport and deposition. Key feature: **fine clay and silt need a high velocity to be eroded because the particles are cohesive**, while sand is eroded at the lowest velocity. Once moving, fine particles stay in suspension at low velocity; deposition starts when velocity drops below the lower curve.

**Reading the curve:** find the particle size on the horizontal axis, read up to the velocity. Above the upper (critical erosion velocity) curve the particle is picked up; between the curves it stays in transport; below the lower (settling velocity) curve it is deposited. Note both axes are usually logarithmic, so each division is ten times the last.

## Channel efficiency

**Hydraulic radius** = cross-sectional area / wetted perimeter. A high value means less friction and a more efficient channel; a semi-circular channel is the most efficient shape.

**Worked example:** a channel 10 m wide and 2 m deep (rectangular) has area 10 x 2 = 20 m² and wetted perimeter 2 + 10 + 2 = 14 m, so hydraulic radius = 20 / 14 = **1.43 m**. A channel 20 m wide and 1 m deep has the same area, but a wetted perimeter of 1 + 20 + 1 = 22 m, so hydraulic radius = 20 / 22 = **0.91 m**. The deeper channel loses less energy to friction.

**Roughness** also matters: boulders, vegetation and an irregular bed slow the flow, even at the same hydraulic radius.

## Channel types

| Type | Features | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Straight | Rare over long distances; the thalweg still swings from side to side | Often controlled by geology or engineering |
| Meandering | Sinuous bends; pools at bends, riffles between | Moderate slope; cohesive banks |
| Braided | Several channels divided by shifting bars and islands | High, coarse load; variable discharge; weak banks, as in glacial meltwater rivers |

## Landform sequences

- **Waterfall and gorge:** hard rock over softer rock; softer rock eroded faster; plunge pool and overhang form; overhang collapses; the fall retreats upstream, leaving a gorge.
- **Rapids and potholes:** rapids form where bands of hard and soft rock alternate, giving a stepped, turbulent bed; potholes are hollows drilled into the bed by pebbles swirled in eddies (abrasion).
- **Meander:** helicoidal flow; fastest flow and erosion on the outside (river cliff); slow flow and deposition on the inside (slip-off slope); pool and riffle sequence.
- **Ox-bow lake:** meander neck narrows by erosion; breached in flood; deposition seals the old loop.
- **Levée:** overbank flow drops the coarsest material next to the channel, building a raised bank.
- **Floodplain:** flat valley floor built by deposition and lateral erosion; finer sediment spreads further from the channel.
- **Delta:** needs a large sediment load and low wave and tidal energy; flocculation of clay in salt water speeds deposition; the river splits into distributaries.

**Delta shapes:** **arcuate** (fan-shaped, as on the Nile), **bird's-foot** (long distributaries extending into the sea, as on the Mississippi) and **cuspate** (pointed, shaped by waves from both sides, as on the Tiber). Shape reflects the balance between river supply and wave and tidal energy.

**Pools and riffles to meanders:** pools (deep, slow) and riffles (shallow, fast, coarse bed) alternate at a spacing of roughly five to seven channel widths. The thalweg swings between them, starting the bank erosion and deposition that develop bends.

## Annotated model paragraph

*"Explain the formation of a meander."*

> Flow is fastest on the outside of a bend, so hydraulic action and abrasion undercut a river cliff (**erosion**). Helicoidal flow carries sediment to the inside of the bend (**transport**), where slow flow deposits a slip-off slope (**deposition**). The bend therefore migrates sideways and downstream over time (**change**).

## Exam traps

- Say that **the meander moves** (erodes outside, deposits inside); it does not just "get bigger".
- Do not describe the Hjulström curve as showing erosion only.
- Name a delta type only if you can describe its shape and conditions.
- Attrition changes the load, not the channel.
- Competence and capacity are different: one is particle size, the other total amount.
- Explain landforms as a sequence of processes; a labelled diagram helps but must be explained.

8. What is the difference between competence and capacity?
9. Give two conditions that favour a braided channel.
10. How does flocculation help delta formation?
11. Name the three main delta shapes.
12. How do potholes form?
