## Questions

**1.** Find the exact volume generated when the region under y = 3/√x, from x = 1 to x = 4, makes a complete revolution about the x-axis. **[3]**

**2.** The curve y = x(4 − x) meets the x-axis at the origin and at (4, 0). The finite region between the curve and the x-axis makes a complete revolution about the x-axis. State the exact volume of the solid generated. **[4]**

**3.** The curve y = x² + 1 meets the line y = 5 at the point P in the first quadrant. The finite region R is bounded by the curve, the line y = 5 and the y-axis. Region R makes a complete revolution about the x-axis. State the exact volume of the solid generated. **[5]**

**4.** A curve has parametric equations x = ln t, y = t², t ≥ 1. The region under the curve between x = 0 and x = ln 2 makes a complete revolution about the x-axis. State the exact volume generated. **[5]**

**5.** A curve has parametric equations x = 3 cos t, y = 2 sin t, 0 ≤ t ≤ π. The curve is rotated through 360° about the x-axis. Use sin³t ≡ ¼(3 sin t − sin 3t) to find the exact volume enclosed. **[5]**

**6.** Solve dy/dx = 2xy², given that y = 1 when x = 0. Give y in terms of x. **[4]**

**7.** Given that y > 0 and y = 1 when x = 0, solve dy/dx = y cos²x, giving y in terms of x. **[5]**

**8.** A drink cools so that its temperature θ °C at time t minutes satisfies dθ/dt = −k(θ − 20), where k is a positive constant. At t = 0, θ = 100. At t = 10, θ = 60. Find the value of t when θ = 30. **[7]**

**9.** A curve has parametric equations x = 2t², y = t(4 − t²), t ≥ 0. The curve meets the x-axis at the origin and at the point A. Find the area of the finite region between the curve and the x-axis. **[5]**

**10.** A curve has parametric equations x = tan t, y = cos²t, 0 ≤ t ≤ π/4. Find the area under the curve between x = 0 and x = 1. **[4]**

**11.** The curve C passes through (0, 3) and satisfies dy/dx = −2y/(x + 1) for x > −1.

**(a)** Find y in terms of x. **[5]**
**(b)** The region bounded by C, the axes and the line x = 2 makes a complete revolution about the x-axis. State the exact volume generated. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** y² = 9/x [1]. V = π ∫ (1 to 4) 9/x dx = 9π [ln x] (1 to 4) [1]. = 9π ln 4 = **18π ln 2** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the squared integrand before integrating. Rewriting 9π ln 4 as 18π ln 2 is optional, but state one clear exact form.

**2.** y² = x²(4 − x)² = x⁴ − 8x³ + 16x² [1]. V = π ∫ (0 to 4) (x⁴ − 8x³ + 16x²) dx = π [x⁵/5 − 2x⁴ + 16x³/3] (0 to 4) [1]. At x = 4: 1024/5 − 512 + 1024/3 [1]. V = **512π/15** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Expand y² fully and integrate term by term. Integrating y and squaring the result is the commonest slip.

**3.** x² + 1 = 5 gives x = 2 at P [1]. Cylinder of radius 5, length 2: π × 25 × 2 = 50π [1]. (x² + 1)² = x⁴ + 2x² + 1 [1]. π ∫ (0 to 2) (x⁴ + 2x² + 1) dx = π [x⁵/5 + 2x³/3 + x] (0 to 2) = 206π/15 [1]. V = 50π − 206π/15 = **544π/15** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Subtract the two volumes. Squaring the difference (5 − y) would give a different, incorrect result, so keep the two boundaries separate.

**4.** dx/dt = 1/t [1]. x = 0 gives t = 1 and x = ln 2 gives t = 2 [1]. V = π ∫ (1 to 2) t⁴ × (1/t) dt [1] = π ∫ (1 to 2) t³ dt = π [t⁴/4] (1 to 2) [1] = π (4 − 1/4) = **15π/4** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* State the change of limits explicitly. Leaving 0 and ln 2 in a t integral is an error that is easy to spot.

**5.** dx/dt = −3 sin t [1]. x runs from −3 (t = π) to 3 (t = 0), so V = π ∫ (π to 0) 4 sin²t × (−3 sin t) dt [1]. Reversing the limits: V = 12π ∫ (0 to π) sin³t dt [1]. ∫ sin³t dt = ¼ [−3 cos t + ⅓ cos 3t] (0 to π) = ¼ (8/3 + 8/3) = 4/3 [1]. V = 12π × 4/3 = **16π** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A negative volume tells you the limits are the wrong way round. Check that x is increasing through the range you integrate over.

**6.** ∫ y⁻² dy = ∫ 2x dx [1]. −1/y = x² + c [1]. x = 0, y = 1: c = −1 [1]. **y = 1/(1 − x²)** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Integrating y⁻² gives −1/y. Include the constant at the integration step, before substituting the values.

**7.** ∫ 1/y dy = ∫ cos²x dx [1]. cos²x = ½(1 + cos 2x) [1]. ln y = x/2 + ¼ sin 2x + c [1]. x = 0, y = 1: c = 0 [1]. **y = e^(x/2 + ¼ sin 2x)** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The double-angle identity is needed to integrate cos²x. The answer is requested for y, so take the exponential at the end.

**8.** ∫ 1/(θ − 20) dθ = ∫ −k dt [1]. ln(θ − 20) = −kt + c [1]. t = 0, θ = 100: ln 80 = c, so θ − 20 = 80e^(−kt) [1]. t = 10: 40 = 80e^(−10k) [1], so k = (ln 2)/10 [1]. θ = 30: 10 = 80e^(−kt), so kt = ln 8 [1]. t = ln 8 × 10/ln 2 = **30 minutes** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Subtract 20 before taking the logarithm, as the equation is in terms of θ − 20. Keep k exact until the end to avoid rounding errors.

**9.** y = 0 for t > 0 gives t = 2, so the limits are t = 0 to t = 2 [1]. dx/dt = 4t [1]. Area = ∫ (0 to 2) (4t − t³) × 4t dt = ∫ (16t² − 4t⁴) dt [1]. = [16t³/3 − 4t⁵/5] (0 to 2) [1]. = 128/3 − 128/5 = **256/15** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Finding where the curve meets the axis gives the upper limit in t. Substituting a point's x-value into a t integral is the usual error.

**10.** dx/dt = sec²t [1]. y dx/dt = cos²t × sec²t = 1 [1]. x = 0 gives t = 0 and x = 1 gives t = π/4 [1]. Area = ∫ (0 to π/4) 1 dt = **π/4** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Simplify the integrand before integrating. Seeing that cos²t × sec²t = 1 makes the integral trivial.

**11. (a)** ∫ 1/y dy = ∫ −2/(x + 1) dx [1]. ln y = −2 ln(x + 1) + c [1]. x = 0, y = 3: c = ln 3 [1]. ln y = ln 3 − ln (x + 1)² [1]. **y = 3/(x + 1)²** [1]
**(b)** y² = 9/(x + 1)⁴. V = π ∫ (0 to 2) 9(x + 1)⁻⁴ dx [1] = 9π [−1/(3(x + 1)³)] (0 to 2) [1] = 3π (1 − 1/27) = **26π/9** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) uses the equation from part (a). If part (a) is wrong, a correct method in (b) still shows what you know, so attempt it.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Squaring y wrongly, or integrating y before squaring.
- Forgetting dx/dt in a parametric integral.
- Leaving x-limits in an integral with respect to t.
- Reversing limits when x decreases, giving a negative volume.
- Squaring a difference when rotating a region between a curve and a line, instead of subtracting two volumes.
- Missing the constant of integration, or adding it after substituting values.
- Mishandling ln: y = e^x + c in place of y = A e^x.
- Not rearranging to y = ... when the question asks for y.
- Giving a decimal when the question asks for an exact value.
