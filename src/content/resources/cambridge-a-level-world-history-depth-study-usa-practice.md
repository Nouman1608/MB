Cambridge 9489 Paper 4's American option asks for two 30-mark essays on the USA, 1945-93, in 1 hour 45 minutes. The twelve questions below match the option's twelve key questions one for one, so you can practise every area; each is followed by indicative content of the kind found in Cambridge mark schemes.

## How Paper 4 essays are marked

Each essay is marked out of 15 for AO1 (knowledge) and 15 for AO2 (analysis and judgement), on five levels (published 9489 Paper 4 mark schemes).

| Level | AO2 (15) | AO1 (15) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 (13-15) | Valid, wide-ranging criteria; consistently analytical; balanced, sustained argument; clear sustained judgement | Carefully selected, fully focused, wide-ranging, precise |
| 4 (10-12) | Valid criteria; analytical but uneven; attempts balance; supported judgement | Appropriate, mostly focused, range uneven in depth |
| 3 (7-9) | Attempted criteria; some analysis, some description; lacks balance | Mostly relevant, descriptive in places, narrow |
| 2 (4-6) | Implicit criteria; descriptive; assertion rather than judgement | Narrative, not linked to argument |
| 1 (1-3) | Addresses the topic, not the question | Limited, vague or inaccurate |

## Topic 4: Truman, Eisenhower and post-war USA, 1945-61

**1.** "McCarthyism was the most serious internal problem Truman faced." Assess this view. **[30]** *(4.1)*

*Indicative content.* For: the loyalty programme and HUAC created a climate of fear; McCarthy's Wheeling speech (1950) attacked the administration; the "who lost China" charge and Korea damaged Truman. Against: reconversion problems -- inflation after price controls ended, the 1946 strike wave, Taft-Hartley (1947); the defeat of national health insurance and much of the Fair Deal by a conservative coalition in Congress; Southern Democrat opposition over civil rights, shown by the Dixiecrat revolt of 1948. Criterion: "serious" in terms of threat to Truman's programme or to American liberties. Many answers judge that congressional conservatism blocked more of Truman's agenda than McCarthy did.

**2.** Evaluate the effectiveness of Eisenhower's response to the civil rights movement. **[30]** *(4.2)*

*Indicative content.* Effective: appointed Earl Warren, whose court delivered *Brown* (1954); sent the 101st Airborne to Little Rock (1957); signed the first Civil Rights Acts since Reconstruction (1957, 1960); completed desegregation of the armed forces. Limited: avoided endorsing *Brown* publicly; acted at Little Rock to uphold federal authority rather than civil rights as such; the 1957 Act was weakened by Southern senators; did little during the Montgomery Bus Boycott. Judgement: effective in upholding the law, reluctant as a moral leader; the movement itself (NAACP, King, SNCC) drove change.

**3.** "The 1950s were an age of prosperity for all Americans." Assess this view. **[30]** *(4.3)*

*Indicative content.* For: GDP growth, the GI Bill, defence spending and the highway programme; mass ownership of cars, televisions and suburban homes; expanding credit; youth spending power. Against: rural poverty in the South and Southwest; inner-city decline as the middle class moved to the suburbs; lower pay for women and minorities; non-unionised and unskilled workers left behind. Criterion: averages versus distribution.

**4.** Assess the extent to which Eisenhower's foreign policy differed from Truman's. **[30]** *(4.4)*

*Indicative content.* Continuity: containment remained the aim; commitment to NATO and Berlin; anti-communism in Asia (support for South Vietnam after 1954). Change: the New Look and reliance on nuclear deterrence to cut costs; brinkmanship; covert action through the CIA (Iran 1953, Guatemala 1954); peaceful co-existence after Stalin's death -- Geneva (1955), Khrushchev's visit (1959) -- ended by the U2 affair (1960); restraint over Hungary (1956) and opposition to Britain and France over Suez (1956). Judgement: a change in *methods* more than in *aims*.

## Topic 5: A time of challenges, 1961-74

**5.** "Johnson's domestic achievements were greater than Kennedy's." Assess this view. **[30]** *(5.1)*

*Indicative content.* Kennedy: New Frontier proposals often stalled in Congress (Medicare, federal aid to education); some gains (minimum wage, Area Redevelopment Act, Equal Pay Act 1963); late commitment to a civil rights bill. Johnson: used Kennedy's death and his own congressional skill to pass the Civil Rights Act (1964), Voting Rights Act (1965), Medicare and Medicaid, ESEA and the Economic Opportunity Act. Counter-arguments: Johnson built on Kennedy's agenda; Vietnam undermined the Great Society; urban riots and backlash. Judgement usually favours Johnson on legislation, with a criterion about lasting impact.

**6.** Evaluate the reasons for the escalation of US involvement in Vietnam, 1961-68. **[30]** *(5.2)*

*Indicative content.* Fear of communism and the domino theory; credibility after the Bay of Pigs and Berlin; weakness and then collapse of Diem (1963); the Gulf of Tonkin incident and Resolution (1964); military advice favouring escalation; Johnson's fear of appearing weak and losing domestic support. Judgement: link Cold War ideology (long-term) with specific triggers (short-term).

**7.** "Federal action was more important than civil rights campaigners in winning progress for African Americans in the 1960s." Assess this view. **[30]** *(5.3)*

*Indicative content.* Federal: Civil Rights Act (1964), Voting Rights Act (1965), Fair Housing Act (1968), *Loving v. Virginia* (1967), federal marshals for the Freedom Riders. Campaigners: Birmingham (1963) and the March on Washington created the pressure for the 1964 Act; Freedom Summer and Selma created it for the 1965 Act. Opposition from Wallace, Connor, Southern Congressmen and the Klan shaped what was achievable. Criterion: which side *initiated* change; many answers argue that campaigners forced federal action.

**8.** Assess the reasons why there were challenges to authority in the USA, 1961-74. **[30]** *(5.4)*

*Indicative content.* Frustration with the pace of civil rights (Black Power, Black Panthers); women's dissatisfaction (Friedan, NOW, the women's liberation movement); identity politics (Stonewall, the American Indian Movement); the Vietnam War (anti-war protest, the 1968 Chicago convention, Kent State); urban poverty (Watts, 1968 riots). Judgement: Vietnam acted as a catalyst, but each movement had its own long-term grievances.

## Topic 6: The USA, 1974-93

**9.** "Economic problems were the greatest challenge facing the USA in the late 1970s." Assess this view. **[30]** *(6.1)*

*Indicative content.* Economy: stagflation, energy crises (1973 and 1979), unemployment; Ford's "Whip Inflation Now"; Carter's energy policy and the Energy Security Act. Other challenges: post-Watergate loss of trust and the Nixon pardon; social division over busing, abortion and gay rights; the rise of the New Right; failures abroad, especially Iran. Judgement: economic problems fed the loss of trust that Reagan exploited in 1980.

**10.** Evaluate the impact of Reaganomics on the American people. **[30]** *(6.2)*

*Indicative content.* Economic Recovery Tax Act (1981), deregulation, the PATCO dismissals (1981) and weakened unions; inflation fell and growth resumed after the 1981-82 recession; deficits and federal debt rose sharply with defence spending; cuts to some welfare programmes; widening inequality; criticism over AIDS and civil rights enforcement. Criterion: different groups -- wealthier taxpayers, industrial workers, the poor.

**11.** "Carter's foreign policy was a failure." Assess this view. **[30]** *(6.3)*

*Indicative content.* Successes: Camp David Accords (1978) and the Egypt-Israel treaty (1979); human rights placed on the agenda in Latin America (pressure on Chile, Argentina and Brazil). Failures: the Iran hostage crisis and the failed rescue (1980); inability to shape events in Nicaragua; collapse of détente after Afghanistan. Judgement: successes in diplomacy, failure in crisis management -- and the hostage crisis decisively shaped perceptions.

**12.** Assess the reasons why relations between the USA and the USSR changed in the years 1972-89. **[30]** *(6.4)*

*Indicative content.* Détente's motives (Vietnam, cost, nuclear parity) and achievements (SALT I, Helsinki); its failure (Afghanistan, SALT II unratified, ideology, new leaders); Reagan's rearmament, SDI and the Reagan Doctrine in Nicaragua, Afghanistan and Angola; Gorbachev's new thinking and the summits from Geneva (1985) to Washington (1987). Judgement: weigh Reagan's pressure against Gorbachev's choices.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should each essay be?**
With 1 hour 45 minutes for two essays, plan for about 5 minutes and write for about 45 minutes per essay.

**Should I use historians' views?**
Paper 4 does not test AO4, so historians are optional; an argued judgement backed by precise evidence matters more.

**What is a "criterion" in practice?**
A stated basis for judgement -- for example, the president's own aims, the number of people affected, or the lasting impact -- that you apply in every paragraph.
