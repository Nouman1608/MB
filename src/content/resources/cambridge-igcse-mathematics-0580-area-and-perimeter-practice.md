These questions cover subtopic 5.2, Area and perimeter, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The Core and Extended statements are identical. Question 11 is labelled **(Extended)** only because it needs a quadratic equation; every other question suits both tiers. Remember that only the triangle area formula is given in the exam.

For explanations, read the [area and perimeter study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-area-and-perimeter/); for a summary, use the [area and perimeter revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-area-and-perimeter-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) A rectangle measures 12 cm by 3.5 cm.

**(a)** Find its area. **[1]**

**(b)** Find its perimeter. **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) A parallelogram has base 14 cm, perpendicular height 6 cm and slant sides of 7.5 cm.

**(a)** Find its area. **[1]**

**(b)** Find its perimeter. **[1]**

**3.** (non-calculator) A trapezium has parallel sides of 9 cm and 15 cm. The perpendicular distance between them is 8 cm. Find its area. **[2]**

**4.** (non-calculator) A triangle has area 45 cm² and base 12 cm. Find its perpendicular height. **[2]**

**5.** (non-calculator) A square has perimeter 36 cm. Find its area. **[2]**

**6.** (non-calculator) Lena says the area of a parallelogram with sides 10 cm and 6 cm is 60 cm². Explain why she is not necessarily right. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** (non-calculator) A trapezium has parallel sides x cm and 2x cm, and perpendicular height 10 cm. Its area is 120 cm². Find x. **[3]**

**8.** (calculator) A rectangular lawn measures 18.6 m by 11.4 m. Edging is fixed along all four sides at $3.75 per metre. Turf for the lawn costs $8.20 per square metre. Find the total cost of the edging and the turf. **[5]**

**9.** (non-calculator) Triangle PQR has vertices P(−2, 1), Q(4, 1) and R(6, 7).

**(a)** Find the area of the triangle. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the perpendicular height from R to the line PQ lies outside the triangle. **[1]**

**10.** (calculator) A parallelogram has vertices A(0, 0), B(5, 0), C(7, 4) and D(2, 4).

**(a)** Find its area. **[1]**

**(b)** Find its perimeter, correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**11.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A rectangle has length (2x + 1) cm and width (x − 1) cm. Its area is 35 cm². Find the perimeter of the rectangle. **[4]**

**12.** (calculator) A triangle with base 8.4 cm has the same area as a trapezium with parallel sides 5.6 cm and 7.2 cm and perpendicular height 4.5 cm. Find the perpendicular height of the triangle. **[3]**

**13.** (non-calculator) A rectangle has perimeter 46 cm. Its length is 5 cm more than its width. Find its area. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 12 × 3.5 = **42 cm²** [1]

**(b)** 2 × 12 + 2 × 3.5 = **31 cm** [1]

**2. (a)** 14 × 6 = **84 cm²** [1]

**(b)** 2 × 14 + 2 × 7.5 = **43 cm** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the slant side is used for the perimeter only. 14 × 7.5 = 105 is not the area.

**3.** ½ × (9 + 15) × 8 [1] = **96 cm²** [1]

**4.** ½ × 12 × h = 45 [1]; h = **7.5 cm** [1]

**5.** Side = 36 ÷ 4 = 9 cm [1]; area = **81 cm²** [1]

**6.** The area is base × **perpendicular** height; 6 cm is a slant side unless the angles are right angles, so the height is less than 6 cm and the area is less than 60 cm² (it equals 60 cm² only if the shape is a rectangle) [1]

**7.** ½ × (x + 2x) × 10 = 120 [1]; 15x = 120 [1]; **x = 8** [1]

**8.** Perimeter = 2 × (18.6 + 11.4) = 60 m [1]; edging = 60 × 3.75 = $225 [1]; area = 18.6 × 11.4 = 212.04 m² [1]; turf = 212.04 × 8.20 = $1738.73 [1]; total = **$1963.73** [1]

*Examiner insight:* write money to the nearest cent; $1963.7 or $1964 loses the final mark.

**9. (a)** PQ is horizontal with length 4 − (−2) = 6; height = 7 − 1 = 6 [1]; area = ½ × 6 × 6 = **18 square units** [1]

**(b)** R has x-coordinate 6, which is beyond Q (x = 4), so the vertical line from R meets the line PQ extended at (6, 1), outside the side PQ; the angle at Q is obtuse [1]

**10. (a)** AB = 5 is horizontal and the height is 4, so area = **20 square units** [1]

**(b)** AD = √(2² + 4²) = √20 = 4.472… [1]; perimeter = 2 × 5 + 2 × 4.472… = **18.9 units** [1]

**11.** (2x + 1)(x − 1) = 35, so 2x² − x − 36 = 0 [1]; (2x − 9)(x + 4) = 0 [1]; x = 4.5 (x = −4 is rejected because the width would be negative) [1]; length 10 cm, width 3.5 cm, perimeter = **27 cm** [1]

*Examiner insight:* check: 10 × 3.5 = 35 cm².

**12.** Trapezium area = ½ × (5.6 + 7.2) × 4.5 = 28.8 cm² [1]; ½ × 8.4 × h = 28.8 [1]; h = **6.86 cm** (3 s.f.) [1]

**13.** 2(w + w + 5) = 46 [1]; 4w = 36, w = 9, so the length is 14 [1]; area = 14 × 9 = **126 cm²** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a slant side instead of the perpendicular height.
- Leaving out the ½ in the triangle or trapezium formula.
- Forgetting that the trapezium, parallelogram and rectangle formulas are not given on the paper.
- Giving areas in linear units or perimeters in square units.
- Keeping a negative root of a quadratic as a length.
- Rounding money to fewer than two decimal places.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I find the area of a triangle on a coordinate grid?**
Look for a horizontal or vertical side to use as the base; the height is then the difference in the other coordinate between the base line and the third vertex.

**What should I do if a question gives the area and asks for a length?**
Write the formula with the unknown in it, substitute the known values and solve the equation. Check by putting your answer back into the formula.
