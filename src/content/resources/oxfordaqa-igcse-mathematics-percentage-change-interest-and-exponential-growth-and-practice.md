These questions are aligned to the OxfordAQA International GCSE Mathematics (9260) specification, Version 5.1, for exams from May/June 2018 onwards. They cover N15 (section 3.1.2) and the repeated proportional change and exponential growth and decay parts of N20 (section 3.1.3). Questions 1 to 6 are Core content, which can be assessed at both tiers. Questions 7 to 12 are marked **(Extension only)**.

## Questions

**1.** A jacket costs £96. In a sale the price is reduced by 35%. Find the sale price. **[2]**

**2.** A monthly phone bill rises from £38.00 to £45.60. Work out the percentage increase. **[3]**

**3.** Simple interest is paid on a savings account.

**(a)** Find the simple interest on £4,500 invested for 5 years at 2.8% a year. **[2]**
**(b)** Maya invests £2,000. After 2.5 years she has received £150 in simple interest. Find the yearly rate of interest. **[2]**

**4.** Compound interest is paid on £6,000. The rate is 3% for the first year, 3% for the second year and 4% for the third year. Find the total interest earned over the three years. **[4]**

**5.** A shop raises a price by 20%. A month later it reduces the new price by 15%. Find the overall percentage change in the original price. **[3]**

**6.** Ayesha has £3,000 to invest for 3 years. Account A pays 5% a year simple interest. Account B pays 4% a year compound interest.

**(a)** Find the total in account A at the end of 3 years. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the total in account B at the end of 3 years. **[2]**
**(c)** Which account is better, and by how much? **[1]**

**7.** (Extension only)

**(a)** After an 8% pay rise, a salary is £29,160. Find the salary before the rise. **[2]**
**(b)** A sofa is reduced by 30% in a sale. The sale price is £52.50. Find the original price. **[2]**

**8.** (Extension only) £12,500 is invested at 3.6% a year compound interest.

**(a)** Find the value of the investment after 7 years. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the smallest whole number of years for the value to exceed £20,000. You must show the values you test. **[4]**

**9.** (Extension only) A deposit of £15,000 becomes £17,117.49 after 3 years, with the same percentage of interest added each year. Find that percentage. **[3]**

**10.** (Extension only) A machine is bought for £34,000. Its value falls by 15% each year.

**(a)** Write down a formula for its value, V pounds, after n years. **[1]**
**(b)** Find its value after 4 years. **[2]**
**(c)** After how many whole years is the value first below £10,000? **[3]**

**11.** (Extension only) A culture starts with 480 bacteria. The number increases by 30% every hour.

**(a)** Find the number of bacteria after 4 hours, to the nearest whole number. **[2]**
**(b)** In which hour does the number first exceed 5,000? **[3]**

**12.** (Extension only) A rare book gains 6% of its value each year. It is now worth £3,150, exactly 4 years after it was bought.

**(a)** Find the price paid for the book, to the nearest pound. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the overall percentage increase in value over the 4 years, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** How many further years are needed for the value to exceed £4,000? **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Multiplier for a 35% decrease = 0.65 [1]
96 × 0.65 = **£62.40** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing the multiplier as its own line shows your method even if you slip in the multiplication. A bare answer leaves nothing to follow.

**2.** Change = 45.60 − 38.00 = 7.60 [1]
7.60 ÷ 38 × 100 [1]
= **20% increase** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Dividing by the original bill is the step that is often lost. Stating "increase" in the answer line also answers the question fully.

**3. (a)** Interest = 4500 × 0.028 × 5 [1]
= **£630** [1]
**(b)** 150 = 2000 × (r/100) × 2.5, so r = 150 ÷ 50 [1]
**r = 3%** a year [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the substitution into the simple interest formula before rearranging. Keeping the units (years, pounds) straight avoids using 2.5 as a percentage by mistake.

**4.** Multipliers 1.03, 1.03 and 1.04 [1]
Total = 6000 × 1.03 × 1.03 × 1.04 [1]
= £6,620.02 [1]
Interest = 6620.02 − 6000 = **£620.02** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The question asks for the interest, not the final amount, so the last subtraction matters. Show a multiplier for each year because the rate changes.

**5.** Multipliers 1.2 and 0.85 [1]
1.2 × 0.85 = 1.02 [1]
Overall change = **2% increase** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Adding +20% and −15% gives 5%, which is wrong. Multiplying the multipliers works for any starting price, so you do not need to invent one.

**6. (a)** Interest = 3000 × 0.05 × 3 = £450 [1]
Total = **£3,450** [1]
**(b)** Total = 3000 × 1.04 × 1.04 × 1.04 [1]
= **£3,374.59** [1]
**(c)** **Account A** is better, by 3450 − 3374.59 = **£75.41** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison question needs a conclusion in words as well as the two totals. Check the totals use the same time period before you compare.

**7. (a)** Multiplier = 1.08, so original = 29160 ÷ 1.08 [1]
= **£27,000** [1]
**(b)** Sale price is 70% of the original, so original = 52.50 ÷ 0.7 [1]
= **£75** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Finding 8% of £29,160 and subtracting is the common error. A quick forward check (27,000 × 1.08) confirms the answer.

**8. (a)** Value = 12500 × 1.036⁷ [1]
= **£16,011.36** [1]
**(b)** Value after n years = 12500 × 1.036ⁿ [1]
n = 13: £19,796.43, which is below £20,000 [1]
n = 14: £20,509.10, which is above £20,000 [1]
So the smallest whole number is **14 years** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A trial-and-improvement answer should show a value just below and a value just above the target. Stating only the final year gives no evidence of the search.

**9.** (1 + r/100)³ = 17117.49 ÷ 15000 [1]
1 + r/100 = ∛1.14117... = 1.045 [1]
**r = 4.5%** a year [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* After the cube root, remember to subtract 1 before converting to a percentage. Giving 1.045 or 104.5% as the rate would not answer the question.

**10. (a)** **V = 34000 × 0.85ⁿ** [1]
**(b)** V = 34000 × 0.85⁴ [1]
= **£17,748.21** [1]
**(c)** n = 7: £10,899.62, which is above £10,000 [1]
n = 8: £9,264.68, which is below £10,000 [1]
So the value first falls below £10,000 after **8 years** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* For decay, the multiplier is 0.85, not 0.15. Check that your model gives a smaller value each year before you use it.

**11. (a)** Number = 480 × 1.3⁴ [1]
= **1,371 bacteria** (1370.9 rounded) [1]
**(b)** After 8 hours: 480 × 1.3⁸ = 3,916, below 5,000 [1]
After 9 hours: 480 × 1.3⁹ = 5,090, above 5,000 [1]
The number first exceeds 5,000 in the **9th hour** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Read what the question asks: "in which hour" wants a whole number of hours. Rounding the answer to the nearest whole number of bacteria is a separate instruction, given in part (a).

**12. (a)** Multiplier for 4 years = 1.06⁴ [1]
Price = 3150 ÷ 1.06⁴ [1]
= **£2,495** [1]
**(b)** 1.06⁴ = 1.2625 (4 d.p.) [1]
Overall increase = **26.2%** [1]
**(c)** 3150 × 1.06⁴ = £3,976.80, which is below £4,000 [1]
3150 × 1.06⁵ = £4,215.41, which is above £4,000 [1]
So **5 further years** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* The book's age is 4 years, but part (c) starts counting from the value of £3,150 now. Mixing up the starting point is an easy way to give an answer one year out.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by the new amount, not the original, when finding a percentage change.
- Using a multiplier of 1.35 for a 35% decrease, or 0.2 for a 20% increase.
- Adding percentages in a repeated change question instead of multiplying the multipliers.
- Taking a percentage of the final amount when the question asks for the starting amount. Divide by the multiplier.
- Giving the final total when the question asks for the interest earned.
- Using simple interest for a compound question, or the reverse.
- Rounding the multiplier or an intermediate value, which can change the final penny.
- Stating a trial-and-improvement answer without showing the values either side of the target.
- Forgetting to subtract 1 after taking a root when finding a rate.
- Leaving out the words "increase" or "decrease", or the pound sign on a money answer.
