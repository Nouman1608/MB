Revise first with the [national income statistics study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-economics-9708-national-income-statistics-cambridge-alevel-economics/) and the [national income statistics revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-economics-9708-national-income-statistics-cambridge-alevel-economics-revision-notes/). ## Section A

**1.** Which item is included when national income is measured?

A: A state pension paid to a retired worker

B: The purchase of existing shares on a stock exchange

C: A new bus bought by a transport firm

D: The sale of a second-hand car between two households

**[1]**

**2.** GNI is 450 billion cu and depreciation is 50 billion cu. What is NNI?

A: 400 billion cu

B: 450 billion cu

C: 500 billion cu

D: It cannot be found without GDP

**[1]**

**3.** Define Gross Domestic Product. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why only final goods and services are counted in national income. **[2]**

**5.** State the formula for converting a value at market prices to a value at basic prices. **[2]**

**6.** Explain the difference between a gross value and a net value. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Furniture passes through four stages of production in an invented economy.

| Stage | Sales value (cu) |
|---|---|
| Forester sells logs to sawmill | 12 |
| Sawmill sells timber to furniture maker | 30 |
| Furniture maker sells tables to retailer | 85 |
| Retailer sells tables to households | 120 |

**(a)** Calculate the value added at each stage. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why adding all four sales values would overstate national income. **[2]**

**8.** Country Y (invented figures, billion cu):

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| GDP at market prices | 1,200 |
| Primary income received from abroad | 60 |
| Primary income paid abroad | 95 |
| Depreciation | 150 |
| Taxes on products | 130 |
| Subsidies on products | 25 |

**(a)** Calculate GNI at market prices. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate NNI at market prices. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate GDP at basic prices and NNI at basic prices. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain what the difference between GDP and GNI suggests about Country Y. **[2]**

**9.** Country Z reports NNI at basic prices of 480 billion cu. Taxes on products are 60, subsidies on products are 12, depreciation is 70 and net primary income from abroad is -18 (all billion cu).

**(a)** Calculate NNI at market prices. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate GNI at market prices. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate GDP at market prices. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why GDP at market prices could rise in a year in which the quantity of goods and services produced did not change. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why NNI may give a better picture than GNI of the income a country can use without reducing its future productive capacity. **[3]**

**12.** Country A's GDP is larger than Country B's, but Country B's GNI is larger than Country A's. Explain how this could happen. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. A new bus is final output (investment). Pensions are transfer payments, shares are financial transactions and second-hand sales were counted when the goods were first produced.

**2.** **A** [1]. NNI = GNI - depreciation = 450 - 50.

**3.** The total value of **final goods and services produced within a country's borders** [1] **over a period, usually a year**, regardless of who owns the factors of production [1].

**4.** Intermediate goods are **already included in the price of final goods** [1], so counting them as well would **double count** output [1].

**5.** Basic prices = market prices **minus taxes on products** [1] **plus subsidies on products** [1].

**6.** A gross value makes **no allowance for depreciation** [1]; a net value **subtracts depreciation**, the fall in value of the capital stock through wear and obsolescence [1].

**7. (a)** Forester 12; sawmill 30 - 12 = 18; furniture maker 85 - 30 = 55; retailer 120 - 85 = 35 [1]. Total value added = **120 cu**, equal to the final sale [1].

**(b)** Each sales value **includes the value of the inputs bought from the previous stage** [1], so the logs would be counted four times and the timber three times: summing sales gives 247 cu, more than double the 120 cu of final output [1].

**8. (a)** Net primary income = 60 - 95 = -35 [1]. GNI = 1,200 - 35 = **1,165** [1].

**(b)** NNI = 1,165 - 150 = **1,015** [1].

**(c)** Net adjustment = -130 + 25 = -105. GDP at basic prices = 1,200 - 105 = **1,095** [1]. NNI at basic prices = 1,015 - 105 = **910** [1].

**(d)** GNI is below GDP, so **more primary income is paid abroad than received** [1]. This suggests that a significant share of production in Country Y uses factors **owned by non-residents**, such as foreign-owned firms sending profits home [1].

**9. (a)** NNI at market prices = 480 + 60 - 12 = **528** [1].

**(b)** GNI at market prices = 528 + 70 = **598** [1].

**(c)** GDP = GNI - net primary income [1] = 598 - (-18) = **616** [1].

**10.** GDP at market prices includes **taxes on products** [1]. If product tax rates rise (or subsidies are cut), market prices and so market-price GDP rise even though output is unchanged [1]. (Also creditworthy: a general rise in prices raises the money value of unchanged output.)

**11.** NNI **subtracts depreciation** from GNI [1]. Part of gross income must be used to **replace capital that has worn out** [1]; if it were all consumed, the capital stock would shrink and future output would fall, so NNI shows the income available without running down productive capacity [1].

**12.** GDP measures **output produced within the borders**, while GNI measures **residents' income**, including net primary income from abroad [1]. Country A may have a lot of **foreign-owned production**, so much of its output generates profit paid abroad, giving negative net primary income [1]. Country B's residents may own **large overseas assets**, earning profits and interest from abroad [1]. B's positive net primary income can lift its GNI above A's even though its GDP is smaller [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Getting the sign of net primary income wrong in Question 8 and Question 9.
- Reversing the adjustments incorrectly when working backwards in Question 9.
- Adding all sales values in Question 7.
- Removing all taxes, rather than taxes on products, in the market-to-basic step.
