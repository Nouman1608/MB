## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.**

**(a)** Define the term tissue. **[2]**
**(b)** Define the term organ. **[2]**

**2.** State the level of organisation of each of these: red blood cell, xylem, heart, digestive system. **[4]**

**3.** Put these in order, starting with the smallest: organ, organism, tissue, organ system, cell. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why the stomach is described as an organ. **[3]**

**5.** Explain why muscle tissue on its own is not an organ. **[2]**

**6.** A student says that blood is an organ because it is found all over the body. Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**7.** A student looks at onion epidermis under a microscope. Each cell is 0.25 mm long.

**(a)** Convert 0.25 mm into micrometres. **[1]**
**(b)** The strip of epidermis is 5 cm long. Calculate how many cells would fit end to end along it. **[2]**

**8.** A liver cell is about 20 µm across. A human liver is about 20 cm across.

**(a)** Calculate how many times wider the liver is than one liver cell. **[2]**
**(b)** State this difference as a number of orders of magnitude. **[1]**

**9.** A leaf is 0.25 mm thick. Its spongy mesophyll layer is 0.1 mm thick. Calculate the percentage of the leaf's thickness made up of spongy mesophyll. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**10.** An amoeba is a single-celled organism that lives in pond water. Explain why an amoeba does not need organ systems for gas exchange but a human does. **[4]**

**11.** Describe how the digestive system, the respiratory system and the circulatory system work together to supply a muscle cell in the leg with what it needs for aerobic respiration. **[6]**

**12.**

**(a)** Name the plant organ system that transports substances around a plant, and the organs it is made from. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why a leaf is described as an organ. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A group of cells [1] with a similar structure and function [1]
**(b)** An aggregation (collection) of different tissues [1] performing a specific function [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (a), "a group of similar cells" alone misses the function; for (b), "a group of cells" scores nothing because an organ is made of tissues.

**2.** Red blood cell: cell [1]; xylem: tissue [1]; heart: organ [1]; digestive system: organ system [1]
*Examiner insight:* Xylem is often called an organ; it is many similar cells with one function, so it is a tissue.

**3.** Cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism [1]
*Examiner insight:* All five must be in the correct order for the mark.

**4.** It contains several different tissues, such as muscular and glandular tissue [1]; for example, muscular tissue churns the food while glandular tissue produces digestive juices [1]; the tissues work together to perform a specific function, digesting food [1]
*Examiner insight:* Naming the tissues earns more than repeating the definition of an organ.

**5.** Muscle tissue is only one type of tissue [1]; an organ is made of several different tissues working together to carry out a specific function [1]
*Examiner insight:* The key idea is "different tissues", plural.

**6.** Blood is a tissue, not an organ [1]: it is a group of cells (in plasma) with a shared function, transport, and it is not made of several different tissues [1]
*Examiner insight:* Where a structure is found in the body does not decide its level of organisation.

**7. (a)** 0.25 × 1000 = **250 µm** [1]
**(b)** 5 cm = 50 mm [1]; 50 ÷ 0.25 = **200 cells** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Either unit works if both lengths are converted to it first: 50 000 µm ÷ 250 µm also gives 200.

**8. (a)** 20 cm = 200 000 µm [1]; 200 000 ÷ 20 = **10 000 times** [1]
**(b)** 10 000 = 10⁴, so **four** orders of magnitude [1]
*Examiner insight:* Dividing 20 by 20 to get 1 is the classic unit error; always convert first.

**9.** 0.1 ÷ 0.25 × 100 [1] = **40%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The working mark is for dividing the part by the whole.

**10.** An amoeba is a single cell, so it has a large surface area compared with its volume [1]; oxygen can diffuse across its surface fast enough to reach the whole cell [1]. A human is large and has a small surface area compared with its volume, so most cells are too far from the surface for diffusion alone [1]; it needs organ systems such as the lungs and circulatory system to exchange gases and transport them to every cell [1]
*Examiner insight:* Comparing surface area to volume for both organisms is what turns a description into an explanation.

**11.** Marking points (AQA marks 6-mark questions by levels; a top answer gives a clear, linked sequence):
- the digestive system digests food into small soluble molecules, such as glucose [1]
- glucose is absorbed from the small intestine into the blood [1]
- in the lungs (respiratory system), oxygen diffuses from the alveoli into the blood in the capillaries [1]
- the heart pumps the blood through arteries to the leg muscle [1]
- glucose and oxygen diffuse from capillaries into the muscle cell for respiration [1]
- carbon dioxide diffuses from the muscle cell into the blood and is carried back to the lungs to be breathed out [1]
*Examiner insight:* The question is about the systems working together, so each step should hand on to the next system rather than describing each one in isolation.

**12. (a)** The transport system, made of the roots, stem and leaves [1]
**(b)** A leaf contains several different tissues, such as the epidermis, palisade mesophyll, spongy mesophyll, xylem and phloem [1]; which work together to carry out a specific function, photosynthesis [1]
*Examiner insight:* Two named tissues and a named function make the strongest answer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining an organ as a group of cells rather than a group of tissues.
- Calling blood or xylem an organ.
- Leaving out "function" from the definition of a tissue.
- Dividing quantities given in different units.
- Describing organ systems separately when the question asks how they work together.
