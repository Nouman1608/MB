## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Which of the following is a feature of a market economy? **[1]**

A: Output targets are set by a central planning authority.

B: Most capital is owned by the state.

C: Consumer spending signals to firms what to produce.

D: Prices are fixed by law for most goods.

**2.** In a planned economy, decisions about what to produce are mainly made by: **[1]**

A: Consumers through their spending.

B: A central authority.

C: Firms competing for profit.

D: Workers' trade unions.

**3.** A firm finds that producing one more unit adds £30 to revenue and £34 to cost. A rational, profit-maximising firm should: **[1]**

A: Produce the unit, because revenue is positive.

B: Not produce the unit, because profit would fall by £4.

C: Produce the unit, because total profit is high.

D: Shut down completely.

**4.** Which of the following is a weakness of a market economy? **[1]**

A: Prices carry information about wants.

B: Firms have an incentive to cut costs.

C: Output goes to those able to pay rather than those in greatest need.

D: Consumers can choose between competing sellers.

**5.** Explain how the price mechanism decides what is produced in a market economy. **[2]**

**6.** Explain what is meant by consumer sovereignty and give one reason why it may not hold. **[3]**

**7.** A bike-hire firm is deciding how many extra staff hours to add on Saturdays. Each hour costs £40 in wages. The extra revenue from each hour is £70 for the first, £55 for the second, £38 for the third and £20 for the fourth.

**(a)** State how many extra hours the firm should add, and why. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the extra profit from the hours chosen. **[1]**

### Section B

**8.** *Extract (invented).* For forty years the state in Country T owned all factories and set the price of bread, fuel and clothing. Shops often ran out, and queues were common. After reforms, most firms were sold to private owners and prices were freed. Within five years queues had largely disappeared and the range of goods in shops doubled. Over the same period the share of national income received by the richest tenth of households rose from 25% to 34%.

**(a)** Identify two features of Country T's economy before the reforms. **[2]**

**(b)** Using the extract, explain one benefit of the move towards a market economy. **[3]**

**(c)** Using the extract, explain one cost of the move. **[3]**

**9.** Explain two roles of government in a market economy. **[4]**

**10.** Explain why economists assume consumers behave rationally, and explain one limitation of this assumption. **[4]**

**11.** Explain why a market economy might provide too few flood defences. **[3]**

**12.** Evaluate the view that a market economy is the best way to solve the basic economic problem. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. Consumer spending steers production through prices and profit. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** **B** [1]. The marginal cost exceeds the marginal revenue, so the unit reduces profit. **[1]**

**4.** **C** [1]. The other options are strengths. **[1]**

**5.** When consumers want more of a good, demand rises and its price rises [1]. Higher prices make the good more profitable, so firms move resources into producing it, while goods with falling demand see lower prices and resources move out [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Consumer sovereignty is the idea that consumers' spending decides what firms produce [1]. It may not hold because large firms use advertising to shape what consumers want [1], or because a firm with market power can restrict choice and set prices with little competition [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Two hours [1]. The first two hours bring more revenue than their £40 cost (£70 and £55), while the third brings only £38, less than its cost [1].

**(b)** (70 - 40) + (55 - 40) = **£45** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare each hour's extra revenue with its extra cost; the third hour would cut profit by £2.

**8. (a)** The state owned the factories [1]; prices of key goods were set by the state [1].

**(b)** Freeing prices let them rise to the level that matched demand and supply [1], so queues largely disappeared as prices rationed goods [1], and the profit motive encouraged firms to supply more variety, with the range of goods doubling [1].

**(c)** The share of income going to the richest tenth rose from 25% to 34% [1], so inequality increased [1]. Because a market allocates by ability to pay, low-income households may now afford less of essential goods such as fuel [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote the figures from the extract; "inequality rose" with no evidence earns less.

**9.** Government enforces property rights and contracts [1], without which people would not invest or trade because they could not keep the returns [1]. It also corrects external costs, for example by taxing pollution [1], because the market ignores harm to third parties and so over-produces such goods [1]. Other roles: providing public goods, redistribution, promoting competition, stabilisation. **[4]**

**10.** Assuming rationality means agents act consistently to achieve their aims [1], which allows economists to predict behaviour, for example that a consumer buys less when price rises [1]. A limitation is that consumers often lack full information [1], or act out of habit, so they may not choose the option that gives them the greatest satisfaction and predictions can be wrong [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Once flood defences are built they protect everyone in the area, including those who do not pay [1]. People therefore have an incentive to wait for others to pay (free riding) [1], so a private firm cannot raise enough revenue to cover the cost and too few defences are provided [1]. **[3]**

**12.** For: prices bring together information about wants and scarcity that no planner could collect [1]; profit gives firms an incentive to cut costs and innovate, so resources go to valued uses [1]; competition gives consumers choice [1]. Evidence from economies that moved from planning to markets, as in question 8, shows shortages can fall [1]. Against: markets fail to supply public goods such as flood defences [1] and ignore external costs such as pollution [1]; they answer "for whom" by ability to pay, which can leave basic needs unmet [1]; booms and slumps waste resources [1]. Judgement: no real economy relies on markets alone, and the best answer is a mixed economy in which markets allocate most goods while government corrects failures [1]; how large a role government should play depends on whether intervention itself works well [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* "Best way" invites comparison; an answer that only describes the market economy, without weighing it against planning or a mix, misses the evaluation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Choosing a strength when the question asks for a weakness in multiple-choice items.
- Explaining the price mechanism without the profit link.
- Using total profit instead of marginal values for the bike-hire decision.
- Not quoting the extract's figures in question 8.
- Ending the essay without a judgement.
