## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. The
4-mark "describe" question gives one mark for each relevant, accurate detail; the 4-mark "explain"
question gives one mark for each point and one for explaining it, up to two marks for each of the
two. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general,
accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The
12-mark questions are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** Mount Safa [1].

**2.** Tawhid [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: Khadija (RA); Ali (RA); Zayd ibn Harithah (RA); Abu Bakr (RA).

**4.** Any two, [1] each: ban on burying girls alive; a share of inheritance; the right to own property; the right to receive the mahr; the right to agree to a marriage; equal reward for good deeds.

**5.** Any four, [1] each: he invited his family and the clan of Banu Hashim to a meal [1]; he climbed Mount Safa and called the Quraysh [1]; he asked whether they would believe him, and they said they would because he never lied [1]; he told them he was a warner [1]; he called them to worship Allah alone [1]; he spoke at the Ka'aba and to pilgrims [1]; his uncle Abu Lahab insulted him [1].

**6.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Respect for ancestors** [1]: the idols were part of the religion of their fathers, and rejecting them seemed to insult tradition [1].
- **Loss of income** [1]: pilgrims came to Makkah to visit the idols, which brought trade [1].
- **Loss of status** [1]: the Quraysh were guardians of the Ka'aba, and the new message reduced their honour [1].
- **Equality before Allah** [1]: the message that all are equal threatened the position of the powerful over slaves and the poor [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **They cause enmity** [1]: drinking and betting led to quarrels and hatred between people [1].
- **They distract from Allah** [1]: they stop a person from prayer and remembering Allah [1].
- **They waste wealth** [1]: gamblers and drinkers lose money that their families need [1].
- **They harm health and judgement** [1]: an intoxicated person cannot think or act responsibly [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Satan only wants to cause between you animosity and hatred through intoxicants and gambling and to avert you from the remembrance of Allah and from prayer" (Qur'an 5:91) [2].
- "In them is great sin and [yet, some] benefit for people. But their sin is greater than their benefit" (Qur'an 2:219) [2].

**8.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the surah teaches Tawhid ("Lord of the worlds"), worship of Allah alone, accountability and the need for guidance, which were the foundations of the early message.
- **For the statement:** it is recited in every prayer, so the central message is repeated each day.
- **Against the statement:** the early preaching also included detailed social teachings, such as the rights of women and the end of infanticide, that are not in this surah.
- **Against the statement:** the surah does not describe the practices of the community, such as the stages of private and public preaching.
- **Balanced view:** the surah is a short summary of belief and worship, and other parts of the Qur'an develop the teachings about conduct.

*Use of the passage*

- "Lord of the worlds" shows Tawhid.
- "Sovereign of the Day of Recompense" shows accountability.
- "It is You we worship" rejects idols.
- "Guide us to the straight path" shows the need for revelation.

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Ways (AO1):

- **Right to life** [1]: the ban on burying girls alive gave every daughter the right to live [1].
- **Right to inherit** [1]: women were given a fixed share instead of being treated as property [1].
- **Right to own property** [1]: a woman keeps her own wealth and the mahr given to her [1].
- **Equality before Allah** [1]: men and women are rewarded equally for good deeds, which gave women spiritual equality [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "And do not kill your children for fear of poverty" (Qur'an 17:31) [2].
- "For men is a share of what the parents and close relatives leave, and for women is a share" (Qur'an 4:7) [2].

**10.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the community was small and weak, and open preaching might have brought an early attack.
- **For the statement:** private preaching gave time to teach the Qur'an and prayer to the first believers and to build a firm group.
- **For the statement:** he started with people who knew his honesty, who believed him.
- **Against the statement:** secrecy limited the number who heard the message; the public call was needed to reach the whole of Makkah.
- **Against the statement:** Muslims believe that Allah decided when to make the message public, so the move was guided.
- **Balanced view:** each phase had a purpose: preparation in private and wider invitation in public.

*Use of the passage*

- "Guide us to the straight path" shows the need for guidance, which the private teaching of the first believers provided.
- "Lord of the worlds" shows that the message was always meant for all people, so the public phase was part of its nature.
- "It is You we worship and You we ask for help" shows the reliance on Allah that supported the early believers.
