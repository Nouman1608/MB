## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between tax avoidance and tax evasion. **[2]**

**2.** Country G has GDP of 400 billion currency units (cu) and a fiscal deficit of 28 billion cu. The government cuts spending by 10 billion cu. The multiplier is 2 and tax revenue is 25% of any change in GDP. Calculate the new deficit as a percentage of the new GDP, to 1 decimal place. **[4]**

**3.** Country H's currency falls from 0.80 to 0.64 units of foreign currency. An imported good costs 16 foreign units. Imports are 30% of the consumer price basket and all the price change is passed on.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in the home-currency price of the good, and the effect on the price level. **[3]**
**(b)** State one policy implication for a government that wants to control inflation. **[1]**

**4.** Country M exports a primary product. Its export price index falls to 72 and its import price index rises to 96 (both from 100).

**(a)** Calculate the terms of trade index. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one macroeconomic effect of this external shock. **[3]**

**5.** Firm Y has a subsidiary in Country S (corporate tax 25%) that makes 50,000 units at a cost of 40 each. It sells them to a subsidiary in Country T (tax 12.5%), which sells them to customers at 90 each. The market price between unrelated firms is 70.

**(a)** Calculate the total tax paid if the transfer price is 70. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the total tax paid if the transfer price is 45. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the tax saved by the firm. **[1]**
**(d)** State one measure that governments use to regulate transfer pricing. **[1]**

**6.** In Country K, GDP is 600 billion cu. The marginal propensities to save, tax and import are 0.05, 0.15 and 0.30. Investment falls by 30 billion cu after a financial shock.

**(a)** Calculate the multiplier and the fall in GDP. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the government spending that would restore GDP. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain one reason why the restoration might not work as calculated. **[1]**

**7.** Country P taxes income at 0% up to 8,000 cu, 25% from 8,000 to 40,000 cu and 45% above 40,000 cu. Calculate the ratio of the incomes of a household earning 100,000 cu to one earning 10,000 cu before and after tax, and comment on the change. **[6]**

**8.** Officials in Country J find that actual GDP is 470 billion cu and estimate potential GDP at 500 billion cu. The multiplier is 2.

**(a)** Calculate the output gap as a percentage of potential GDP. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the spending increase needed to close the gap. **[2]**
**(c)** Revised data show that potential GDP was 480 billion cu. Explain what happens and why this matters for policy. **[4]**

**9.** Assess the effects of a rise in the central bank's interest rate in Country L on its local, national and global economies. **[9]**

**10.** Assess whether governments can control tax avoidance by TNCs. **[10]**

**11.** Evaluate the use of demand-side policies in response to the global financial crisis of 2008. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** Avoidance is the legal reduction of tax paid, for example by using allowances or moving profit to a low-tax country [1]. Evasion is the illegal failure to pay tax that is owed [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* a clear legal versus illegal contrast is what separates the two terms; saying only that both reduce tax paid does not.

**2.** GDP falls by 10 x 2 = 20, to 380 [1]. Revenue falls by 0.25 x 20 = 5 [1]. The net saving is 10 - 5 = 5, so the deficit is 28 - 5 = 23 [1]. 23/380 x 100 = **6.1%** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* answers that ignore the revenue lost through lower GDP overstate the saving; showing each stage makes your method visible.

**3. (a)** Home price before = 16/0.80 = 20, after = 16/0.64 = 25 [1]. Change = (25 - 20)/20 = **25%** [1]. Price level effect = 0.30 x 25 = **7.5%** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** A fall in the currency raises import prices, so it works against an inflation target; the government would need another tool such as higher interest rates [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* take care over the direction: a fall in the exchange rate makes imports dearer, and answers that reverse this make every later figure wrong.

**4. (a)** Terms of trade = 72/96 x 100 [1] = **75.0** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Export prices fall relative to import prices, so the same exports buy fewer imports [1]. Export revenue and net exports fall, so AD and output fall [1]. The government cannot control world prices, so it can only respond, for example with fiscal stimulus or by diversifying exports [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* link the index to a macroeconomic variable such as AD, growth or the current account; defining terms of trade again earns little extra.

**5. (a)** Profit in S = (70 - 40) x 50,000 = 1.5m, tax = 0.375m [1]. Profit in T = (90 - 70) x 50,000 = 1.0m, tax = 0.125m, total **0.50m** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Profit in S = 5 x 50,000 = 0.25m, tax = 0.0625m. Profit in T = 45 x 50,000 = 2.25m, tax = 0.28125m [1]. Total = **0.34375m** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 0.50 - 0.34375 = **0.15625m** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** The arm's length principle (or documentation rules or country-by-country reporting) [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* set out each subsidiary's profit and tax in a small table; unlabelled figures are hard to follow and easy to mix up.

**6. (a)** mpw = 0.05 + 0.15 + 0.30 = 0.50 [1]. Multiplier = 1/0.50 = **2** [1]. Fall in GDP = 30 x 2 = **60 billion cu** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 60/2 = **30 billion cu** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** A high marginal propensity to import means some spending leaks abroad, or confidence may stay low so the real multiplier is smaller [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* write the three propensities down before adding them; omitting one is a common slip, and the later parts depend on it.

**7.** Tax on 10,000: 0.25 x 2,000 = 500, net 9,500 [1]. Tax on 100,000: 0.25 x 32,000 = 8,000 [1] plus 0.45 x 60,000 = 27,000, total 35,000, net 65,000 [1]. Before tax the ratio is 100,000/10,000 = 10.0 [1]. After tax it is 65,000/9,500 = **6.8** [1]. A progressive tax narrows the gap in disposable income, though very high rates may weaken incentives [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* apply each rate only to its own slice of income; applying the top rate to the whole income is the usual error, and the comment should refer to your own numbers.

**8. (a)** (470 - 500)/500 [1] = **-6.0%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Gap = 30 billion cu [1]. Spending = 30/2 = **15 billion cu** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The true gap is 480 - 470 = 10 billion cu, but the stimulus adds 15 x 2 = 30 billion cu, so output overshoots by 20 billion cu [1]. AD exceeds potential output, so inflationary pressure builds [1]. Data are estimates and are revised [1], so policy sized on inaccurate information can be the wrong size [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the best answers calculate the overshoot and then name the problem (inaccurate information) rather than saying "data can be wrong" in general.

**9.** Local: firms and households in areas reliant on credit-sensitive sectors face higher borrowing costs [1]; jobs may be lost locally while savers gain [1]; the effect depends on the area's industry mix, so some places barely notice [1]. National: borrowing and spending fall, shifting AD left [1]; output and inflation fall and unemployment may rise [1]; time lags and spare capacity affect the outcome, and cost-push inflation responds less [1]. Global: higher rates attract capital and appreciate the currency [1]; partner countries sell less to Country L but face lower import prices from it [1]; the effect is small if Country L is a small economy, and other central banks may respond [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* answers that give three developed scales, each with a chain and a qualification, tend to do better than a national answer with the other scales added as one line.

**10.** Measures: the arm's length principle forces intra-firm prices towards market prices, reducing profit shifting [2]. Country-by-country reporting shows where profits and tax arise, so authorities can challenge mismatches [2]. Limits: a TNC can threaten to move FDI to a low-tax country, so governments compete on rates [2]. Authorities lack information on private intra-firm pricing, and rules differ between countries, so cooperation is needed and enforcement capacity may be weak [2]. Judgement: control is partial; it improves with agreement among many countries, and avoidance within the law persists until loopholes close, so effectiveness depends on cooperation and the country's strength [2]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* a judgement that names what the outcome depends on is stronger than "it depends"; keep it tied to the points already made.

**11.** Monetary: policy rates were cut to very low levels and QE lowered long-term yields [2]. Fiscal: banks were rescued and governments raised spending or cut taxes, with automatic stabilisers adding to deficits [2]. Chain: these actions supported spending, and the multiplier spread the effect, preventing a deeper fall in output [2]. Evaluation: banks and firms wanted to repair balance sheets, so lower rates did not fully revive lending [1]; leakages abroad cut the multiplier [1]; debts and deficits rose, which later constrained policy [1]; QE raised asset prices, benefiting wealthier households [1]. Judgement: policy probably limited the fall, but the recovery was slow because the underlying problem was in finance; the effect is hard to prove because we cannot observe what would have happened otherwise [2]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* each tool needs a link to its cause of weakness or side effect; a list of measures with no evaluation earns little credit for the evaluation skill.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the spending cut as the full deficit saving, with no multiplier effect on revenue.
- Reversing the effect of a currency fall on import prices.
- Leaving out a unit or the base of a percentage (percentage of what?).
- Applying the top tax rate to all income instead of the top slice.
- Treating transfer pricing as evasion; it is usually legal avoidance.
- Describing the 2008 policies as a list with no evaluation.
- Covering only the national level when the question names local and global.
- Concluding without weighing short run against long run.
