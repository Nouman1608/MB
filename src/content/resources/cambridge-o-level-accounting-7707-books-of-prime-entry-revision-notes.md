Condensed recall for **2.3 Books of prime entry**, Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707, 2027-2029 syllabus. Figures below are invented; amounts in dollars.

## Key terms: the seven books

| Book | Records | Posting |
|---|---|---|
| Sales journal | Credit sales (net of trade discount) | Dr each customer; Cr sales (total) |
| Purchases journal | Credit purchases of goods for resale | Cr each supplier; Dr purchases (total) |
| Sales returns journal | Returns from credit customers | Cr each customer; Dr sales returns (total) |
| Purchases returns journal | Returns to credit suppliers | Dr each supplier; Cr purchases returns (total) |
| Cash book | All cash and bank receipts and payments | Other side of each entry to the ledger |
| Petty cash book | Small cash payments | Dr each expense with its analysis column total |
| General journal | Everything else, with a narrative | Each debit and credit as stated |

**General journal uses:** non-current assets bought on credit; goods taken by the owner; opening entries; correction of errors; writing off irrecoverable debts; year-end transfers.

## Discounts

| | Trade discount | Cash discount |
|---|---|---|
| Reason | Trade customer, bulk buying | Prompt payment |
| On the invoice | Deducted | Terms only |
| Recorded? | **Never** | Discount allowed (expense) / discount received (income) |

**Quick figures.** List 1,500, trade discount 20%: invoice **1,200**. Cash discount 2.5% if paid in 10 days: discount **30**, payment **1,170**.

## Cash book

- **Dual function**: a book of prime entry *and* the ledger's cash and bank accounts.
- **Three columns** each side: discount, cash, bank. Discount columns are memorandum: total discount allowed (receipts side) is debited to discount allowed; total discount received (payments side) is credited to discount received.
- **Contra**: cash paid into bank (Dr bank, Cr cash) or cash withdrawn (Dr cash, Cr bank); no ledger posting.
- **Balances**: cash can never be in credit; bank in credit = overdraft.

| Method of receipt or payment | Column |
|---|---|
| Notes and coins | Cash |
| Cheques | Bank |
| Debit and credit cards | Bank |
| Online and bank transfers, standing orders, direct debits | Bank |

## Petty cash and the imprest system

- **Imprest** = fixed float.
- Spend with vouchers → record in analysis columns → at period end **restore the amount spent**.
- Cash in hand + vouchers = imprest at all times.

**Purpose:** frees senior staff from small payments; limits losses; easy to check; tidy postings to expense accounts.

**Quick figures.** Imprest 250; spent 168; cash left 82; restore **168**.

## Cash at the business property

| Benefits | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Small payments and change available | Theft, loss, fraud |
| Fewer bank trips | Safes, security, insurance |
| Access when banks are closed | Counting errors; no interest |

## Manual vs digital original entry

| | Benefits | Limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Manual | Cheap; no equipment; simple | Slow; arithmetic and copying errors; posting by hand; paper at risk |
| Digital | Fast; automatic totals; direct posting; easy back-up and search | Cost, training; security; keying errors spread; equipment dependence |

## Posting the cash book

| Cash book entry | Ledger posting |
|---|---|
| Receipt from a customer (receipts side) | Credit the customer's account |
| Discount allowed column total | Debit discount allowed; each customer credited with its discount |
| Payment to a supplier (payments side) | Debit the supplier's account |
| Discount received column total | Credit discount received; each supplier debited with its discount |
| Expense paid | Debit the expense account |
| Cash sales | Credit sales account |
| Contra | No posting |

## Exam traps

- Recording trade discount anywhere in the books.
- Deducting cash discount on the invoice.
- Opening a bank account in the ledger as well as keeping a cash book.
- Posting contras to the ledger.
- Card sales in the cash column.
- Non-current assets bought on credit in the purchases journal.
- Restoring the imprest with the float instead of the amount spent.
- Discount allowed on the payments side, or received on the receipts side.

## Self-test

1. Name the seven books of prime entry.
2. In which book is a credit purchase of office furniture recorded?
3. What is posted from the sales journal total?
4. Explain the dual function of the cash book.
5. A customer owes $700 and pays within terms allowing 3% cash discount. Calculate the discount and the amount received.
6. Where is the total of the discount allowed column posted?
7. Which column records a sale paid by credit card?
8. What is a contra entry?
9. An imprest of $300 has $112 left. How much is restored?
10. Give two purposes of the imprest system.
11. Give one benefit and one limitation of keeping cash at the business.
12. Give one benefit of digital original entry and one limitation.

**Answers:** 1. Cash book; petty cash book; sales journal; purchases journal; sales returns journal; purchases returns journal; general journal. 2. The general journal. 3. It is credited to the sales account. 4. It records receipts and payments for the first time (book of prime entry) and its cash and bank columns are the cash and bank accounts of the ledger. 5. Discount 700 × 3% = **$21**; received **$679**. 6. Debit side of the discount allowed account. 7. Bank. 8. An entry where both sides are in the cash book, such as paying cash into the bank. 9. 300 − 112 = **$188**. 10. Any two: frees senior staff from small payments; limits losses; easy to check at any time; convenient postings to expense accounts. 11. Benefit, any one: cash for small payments or change; fewer bank trips. Limitation, any one: theft, loss, fraud; cost of security; no interest. 12. Benefit, any one: speed; automatic totals; direct posting; easy back-up. Limitation, any one: cost; training; security; keying errors spread.
