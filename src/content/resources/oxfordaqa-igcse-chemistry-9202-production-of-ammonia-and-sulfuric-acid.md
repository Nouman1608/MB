| Statement | Content |
|---|---|
| 3.8.3 a | Raw materials for the Haber process: nitrogen from the air; hydrogen from natural gas or other sources |
| 3.8.3 b | Ammonia is a raw material for fertilisers; explain the global need for fertilisers to maximise food yields |
| 3.8.3 c | Purified gases pass over an iron catalyst at about 450 °C and about 200 atmospheres; the reaction is reversible; ammonia is liquefied by cooling and removed; unreacted gases are recycled |
| 3.8.3 d | The three stages of the contact process; explain why a catalyst, a high temperature and atmospheric pressure are used in Stage 2 |

## The Haber process as a loop

Follow the gases around the plant. Each step answers a likely exam question.

1. **Raw materials.** Nitrogen is separated from the **air**. Hydrogen is obtained from **natural gas** or other sources.
2. **Purify.** Impurities are removed before the gases reach the catalyst.
3. **Compress** the mixture to about **200 atmospheres**.
4. **React** over an **iron catalyst** at about **450 °C**. Only some of the nitrogen and hydrogen react, because the reaction is reversible:

   nitrogen + hydrogen ⇌ ammonia

   N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)

5. **Cool** the gas mixture. Ammonia **liquefies** (it condenses at a much higher temperature than nitrogen or hydrogen) and is **removed** as a liquid.
6. **Recycle** the unreacted nitrogen and hydrogen back to the reactor.

Recycling is what makes a low conversion per pass acceptable: the same gases go round again and again until almost all of them have become ammonia.

### Why these conditions, in one line each

- **Iron catalyst:** reaches equilibrium faster; no change to yield.
- **About 450 °C:** the forward reaction is exothermic, so lower temperatures give more ammonia, but too slowly; 450 °C is a compromise.
- **About 200 atmospheres:** 4 molecules of gas become 2, so high pressure raises the yield and the rate; higher still would cost too much and be less safe.

## Fertilisers and food (3.8.3 b)

The explanation examiners want is a chain of linked points:

1. The world's population is growing, so more food is needed.
2. The area of farmland is limited, so **more food must be grown on each field**.
3. Plants need nitrogen compounds to grow (to make proteins); they cannot use nitrogen gas from the air directly.
4. Harvesting crops removes nitrogen compounds from the soil.
5. Fertilisers made from ammonia **replace** these nitrogen compounds, so crop **yields are maximised**.

### From ammonia to fertiliser: linking both processes

Ammonia is alkaline, so it neutralises acids to form ammonium salts (3.5.1 d). Two common fertilisers use products of this section:

- Ammonium sulfate uses **both** ammonia and sulfuric acid: 2NH₃ + H₂SO₄ → (NH₄)₂SO₄
- Ammonium nitrate uses ammonia and nitric acid: NH₃ + HNO₃ → NH₄NO₃

### Worked example 1: raw materials for a fertiliser

*Calculate the masses of ammonia and sulfuric acid needed to make 66 tonnes of ammonium sulfate.* (Aᵣ: H = 1, N = 14, O = 16, S = 32)

- Mᵣ: NH₃ = 17; H₂SO₄ = 98; (NH₄)₂SO₄ = 132.
- From the equation, 2 × 17 = 34 parts of ammonia and 98 parts of sulfuric acid give 132 parts of ammonium sulfate.
- Scale factor = 66 ÷ 132 = 0.5.
- Ammonia = 34 × 0.5 = **17 tonnes**; sulfuric acid = 98 × 0.5 = **49 tonnes**.
- Check: 17 + 49 = 66 tonnes.

## The contact process (3.8.3 d)

Sulfuric acid is made in three stages. The specification prints these equations:

| Stage | Reaction | Equation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sulfur is burned in air to give sulfur dioxide | S(s) + O₂(g) ⇌ SO₂(g) |
| 2 | Sulfur dioxide reacts with more oxygen to give sulfur trioxide | 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g) |
| 3 | Sulfur trioxide reacts with water to give sulfuric acid | H₂O(l) + SO₃(g) → H₂SO₄(aq) |

Stage 2 is the **reversible, exothermic, catalysed** step, run with **vanadium(V) oxide, V₂O₅**, at around **450 °C** and **atmospheric pressure**.

### Worked example 2: a model answer comparing the two processes

*Compare the conditions used in the Haber process and in Stage 2 of the contact process, and explain the differences.* **[6]**

**Model answer**

- Both reactions are reversible and exothermic in the forward direction [1].
- Both use a temperature of about 450 °C, a compromise: lower temperatures would give a higher yield but the reactions would be too slow [1].
- Both use a catalyst (iron for Haber, vanadium(V) oxide for contact) to increase the rate without changing the yield [1].
- The Haber process uses about 200 atmospheres because 4 gas molecules form 2, so high pressure significantly increases the yield [1].
- Stage 2 also has fewer molecules on the right (3 form 2), so high pressure would raise its yield [1].
- But the yield at atmospheric pressure is already high, so the extra cost of high-pressure equipment is not worthwhile [1].

**Why it scores:** it states each similarity and difference, and gives a reason for each one.

## Calculations in industrial contexts

### Worked example 3: sulfuric acid from sulfur dioxide

*What is the maximum mass of sulfuric acid that can be made from 128 tonnes of sulfur dioxide, and what mass of oxygen does Stage 2 need?* (Aᵣ: H = 1, O = 16, S = 32)

- Each SO₂ becomes one SO₃ and then one H₂SO₄, so the ratio SO₂ : H₂SO₄ is 1 : 1.
- Mᵣ SO₂ = 64; Mᵣ H₂SO₄ = 98. Scale factor = 128 ÷ 64 = 2.
- Maximum mass of acid = 98 × 2 = **196 tonnes**.
- Stage 2: 2SO₂ + O₂, so 2 × 64 = 128 parts of SO₂ need 32 parts of O₂: **32 tonnes** of oxygen.

### Worked example 4: gas volumes in the Haber process

*600 dm³ of nitrogen reacts completely with hydrogen at room temperature and pressure. Calculate the volume of hydrogen needed and the mass of ammonia formed.* (Aᵣ: N = 14, H = 1; molar gas volume 24 dm³)

- Gas volumes react in the same ratio as moles: N₂ : H₂ : NH₃ = 1 : 3 : 2.
- Hydrogen = 3 × 600 = **1800 dm³**. Ammonia = 2 × 600 = 1200 dm³.
- Moles of ammonia = 1200 ÷ 24 = 50 mol; mass = 50 × 17 = **850 g**.

In a real plant the gases are not at room conditions and do not react completely; the calculation gives the theoretical maximum.

## Common mistakes

- Saying hydrogen comes from the air, or nitrogen from natural gas.
- Saying the ammonia is removed "by filtering" or "as a gas"; it is cooled, liquefied and removed.
- Forgetting to mention recycling of unreacted gases.
- Stating that the catalyst increases the yield.
- Giving high pressure as a Stage 2 condition.
- Naming the contact process catalyst as iron, or the Haber catalyst as vanadium(V) oxide.
- Explaining fertilisers only as "they help plants grow" without the food-supply chain.

## Exam technique: command words

- **Describe the Haber process:** raw materials, conditions with numbers, the reversible equation, cooling and removal, recycling.
- **Explain why a condition is used:** state its effect on yield **and** on rate or cost.
- **Explain the global need for fertilisers:** give the linked chain, from population to yield.
- **Calculate:** use the mass-ratio method and keep units consistent.
