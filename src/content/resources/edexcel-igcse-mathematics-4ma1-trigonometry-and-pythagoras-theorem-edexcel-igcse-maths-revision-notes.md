Condensed recall for section 4.8 (Trigonometry and Pythagoras' theorem) of the 4MA1 specification. Pythagoras in 2D, right-angled trigonometry and bearings are needed on both tiers; obtuse angles, elevation and depression, the sine and cosine rules, ½ab sin C and 3D work are **Higher tier only**. The worked examples are in the [Trigonometry and Pythagoras study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-trigonometry-and-pythagoras-theorem-edexcel-igcse-maths/), and exam-style questions in the [Trigonometry and Pythagoras practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-trigonometry-and-pythagoras-theorem-edexcel-igcse-maths-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hypotenuse | Longest side of a right-angled triangle, opposite the right angle |
| Opposite | Side facing the angle being used |
| Adjacent | Side next to the angle that is not the hypotenuse |
| Included angle | Angle between two given sides |
| Angle of elevation | Angle upwards from the horizontal (Higher) |
| Angle of depression | Angle downwards from the horizontal (Higher) |
| Projection | The "shadow" of a line on a plane (Higher, 3D) |
| Bearing | Clockwise angle from north, three figures |

## Formulae

**Not on the formulae sheet (learn them):**

```
a² + b² = c²            (c = hypotenuse)
sin θ = opp/hyp    cos θ = adj/hyp    tan θ = opp/adj
```

**On the Higher formulae sheet:**

```
a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C
a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A
area = ½ab sin C
```

Rearranged cosine rule for an angle: cos A = (b² + c² − a²)/2bc.

## Pythagoras checklist

- Finding the hypotenuse → **add** the squares.
- Finding a shorter side → **subtract** from the hypotenuse squared.
- Distance between (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂) → √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²).
- Isosceles triangle → split down the line of symmetry for a right angle.
- Common triples: 3, 4, 5; 5, 12, 13; 8, 15, 17; 7, 24, 25 (and multiples such as 6, 8, 10).

## Right-angled trigonometry checklist

1. Label hyp, opp, adj from the angle.
2. Pick the ratio that uses the two sides involved.
3. Unknown on top → multiply. Unknown on the bottom → divide.
4. Angle → use the inverse function (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹).
5. Check: the hypotenuse is the longest side; angles in the triangle add to 180°.

| Unknown | Formula |
|---|---|
| opp | hyp × sin θ, or adj × tan θ |
| adj | hyp × cos θ, or opp ÷ tan θ |
| hyp | opp ÷ sin θ, or adj ÷ cos θ |

## Bearings with trigonometry

Distance d on bearing θ (θ between 000° and 090°): east = d sin θ, north = d cos θ. For other bearings, find the acute angle with the nearest north–south line first. Draw a north line at every point.

## Higher tier only

**Obtuse angles:** sin(180° − θ) = sin θ; cos(180° − θ) = −cos θ; tan(180° − θ) = −tan θ. A negative cosine means an obtuse angle.

**Sine rule for an angle:** two possible answers, θ and 180° − θ. Keep the obtuse one only if it fits with the other angle(s) in the triangle.

**Elevation and depression:** both from the horizontal. Depression from A to B = elevation from B to A.

**Which rule?**

| Given | Find | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Side + opposite angle + one more | Side or angle | Sine rule |
| Two sides + included angle | Third side | Cosine rule |
| Three sides | Angle | Cosine rule (rearranged) |
| Two sides + included angle | Area | ½ab sin C |

**3D:** find a right-angled triangle inside the solid. Space diagonal of a cuboid = √(l² + w² + h²). Angle between a line and a plane = angle between the line and its projection on the plane. The angle between two planes is not required.

## Exam traps

- Calculator in radians.
- Using the rounded value of a side in the next step.
- Adding squares to find a shorter side.
- Multiplying when dividing is needed.
- Taking only the acute sin⁻¹ answer when an obtuse angle is possible.
- Cosine rule with the angle not between the two sides.
- Measuring an angle of elevation from the vertical.
- 3D: using a vertical face diagonal instead of the projection on the base.

## Self-test

1. Legs 8 cm and 15 cm. Find the hypotenuse.
2. Hypotenuse 17 cm, one side 8 cm. Find the other side.
3. Opposite 5 cm, adjacent 12 cm. Find the angle.
4. Hypotenuse 20 cm, angle 30°. Find the opposite side.
5. Adjacent 6 cm, angle 60°. Find the hypotenuse.
6. A point is 3 km east and 3 km north of A. Give its bearing from A.
7. (Higher) Write sin 145° in terms of an acute angle.
8. (Higher) cos C = −0.3. Is C acute or obtuse?
9. (Higher) Sides 5 cm and 8 cm, included angle 30°. Find the area.
10. (Higher) Which rule finds an angle from three sides?
11. (Higher) A cuboid is 2 cm by 3 cm by 6 cm. Find the space diagonal.
12. (Higher) From the top of a 20 m building, the angle of depression of a car is 45°. How far is the car from the building?

### Answers

1. √(64 + 225) = 17 cm.
2. √(289 − 64) = 15 cm.
3. tan⁻¹(5/12) = 22.6°.
4. 20 sin 30° = 10 cm.
5. 6 ÷ cos 60° = 12 cm.
6. 045°.
7. sin 35°.
8. Obtuse.
9. ½ × 5 × 8 × sin 30° = 10 cm².
10. The cosine rule.
11. √(4 + 9 + 36) = 7 cm.
12. 20 m (tan 45° = 1).

## Frequently asked questions

**Are the sine and cosine rules on the 4MA1 formulae sheet?**
Yes, on the Higher Tier formulae sheet, together with ½ab sin C. Pythagoras' theorem and the right-angled ratios are not printed, so learn them.

**What is the angle between a line and a plane?**
It is the angle between the line and its projection on the plane. Find a right-angled triangle containing the line, its projection and a perpendicular to the plane.
