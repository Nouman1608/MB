## Structures and names

All three are carboxylic acid derivatives containing the acyl group, R–C=O.

| Compound | General formula | Example | Name ending |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acyl chloride | R–CO–Cl | CH₃COCl | -oyl chloride |
| Acid anhydride | R–CO–O–CO–R | (CH₃CO)₂O | -oic anhydride |
| Amide | R–CO–NH₂ | CH₃CONH₂ | -amide |

Ethanoyl chloride is CH₃COCl. Ethanoic anhydride is (CH₃CO)₂O, two acyl groups joined through one oxygen, formally two carboxylic acid molecules less one water. Ethanamide is CH₃CONH₂. When an amide comes from a primary amine, the group on nitrogen is shown with N-: CH₃CONHCH₃ is N-methylethanamide.

## Why acyl chlorides are so reactive

In an acyl chloride the carbonyl carbon is bonded to two electronegative atoms, oxygen and chlorine. Both draw electron density away, so the carbon is strongly δ+. Chlorine is also a good leaving group. As a result, acyl chlorides react with even weak nucleophiles such as water at room temperature. Acid anhydrides are less reactive, because the leaving group is a carboxylate ion rather than a chloride ion, but they react in the same way.

## The mechanism: nucleophilic addition–elimination

The name tells you the two stages.

1. **Addition**: the nucleophile attacks the δ+ carbonyl carbon. The pi electrons of C=O move onto oxygen, giving a tetrahedral intermediate with an O⁻ and a positive charge on the attacking atom.
2. **Elimination**: the lone pair on O⁻ re-forms the C=O bond and the chloride ion leaves. A proton is then lost from the attacking atom.

Written for ethanoyl chloride with water:

```
Step 1 (addition)
  Arrow 1: lone pair on O of H₂O → the δ+ carbon of C=O
  Arrow 2: the C=O bond pair → onto the oxygen (O⁻)
  Intermediate: CH₃–C(O⁻)(Cl)–O⁺H₂

Step 2 (elimination)
  Arrow 3: lone pair on O⁻ → reforms C=O
  Arrow 4: the C–Cl bond pair → Cl (leaving as Cl⁻)
  then H⁺ is lost from the O⁺H₂ group

Products: CH₃COOH + HCl
```

Exactly the same pattern applies to every nucleophile. Only the atom that attacks changes.

| Nucleophile | Attacking atom | Organic product | Inorganic product |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water, H₂O | O | Carboxylic acid | HCl |
| Alcohol, R′OH | O | Ester | HCl |
| Ammonia, NH₃ | N | Primary amide | NH₄Cl |
| Primary amine, R′NH₂ | N | N-substituted amide | R′NH₃Cl |

For ammonia and amines, two molecules are needed per acyl chloride. The first attacks. The second removes H⁺ from the nitrogen, forming NH₄⁺ (or R′NH₃⁺), which pairs with Cl⁻.

## The reactions with acyl chlorides

```
CH₃COCl + H₂O → CH₃COOH + HCl
CH₃COCl + CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + HCl
CH₃COCl + 2NH₃ → CH₃CONH₂ + NH₄Cl
CH₃COCl + 2CH₃NH₂ → CH₃CONHCH₃ + CH₃NH₃Cl
```

Observations include **steamy white fumes of hydrogen chloride** and a vigorous reaction. With ammonia or an amine, a white solid forms (the ammonium or alkylammonium chloride). Unlike esterification from the carboxylic acid, these reactions are not reversible and go to completion.

## The reactions with acid anhydrides

Ethanoic anhydride reacts in the same way, but the second product is ethanoic acid (or its salt) rather than HCl.

```
(CH₃CO)₂O + H₂O → 2CH₃COOH
(CH₃CO)₂O + CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + CH₃COOH
(CH₃CO)₂O + NH₃ → CH₃CONH₂ + CH₃COOH
(CH₃CO)₂O + CH₃NH₂ → CH₃CONHCH₃ + CH₃COOH
```

(With excess ammonia or amine, the ethanoic acid is neutralised to give the ammonium or alkylammonium ethanoate.) The reactions are slower than for acyl chlorides and usually need warming.

## Aspirin

Aspirin is made by acylating the –OH group of 2-hydroxybenzoic acid (salicylic acid) using ethanoic anhydride:

```
C₆H₄(OH)COOH + (CH₃CO)₂O → C₆H₄(OCOCH₃)COOH + CH₃COOH
```

The product is aspirin, 2-ethanoyloxybenzoic acid, together with ethanoic acid.

### Why ethanoic anhydride is preferred industrially

| Factor | Ethanoyl chloride | Ethanoic anhydride |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | More expensive | Cheaper |
| Reactivity with water | Reacts violently with moisture | Reacts slowly; safer and easier to handle |
| By-product | Corrosive hydrogen chloride gas | Ethanoic acid, which is less hazardous and can be reused |
| Corrosion of equipment | Severe | Less severe |

An examiner expects at least two of these points: cheaper, less corrosive, less vigorous reaction with water, and a by-product that is not toxic and corrosive.

## Required practical 10: preparing and purifying aspirin

1. Warm 2-hydroxybenzoic acid with excess ethanoic anhydride and a few drops of an acid catalyst (concentrated sulfuric or phosphoric acid) in a water bath.
2. Add cold water to hydrolyse the unreacted anhydride and precipitate the aspirin.
3. Filter the crude solid by suction (Buchner) filtration.
4. **Recrystallise**: dissolve the solid in the minimum volume of hot solvent, filter hot to remove insoluble impurities, cool slowly so pure crystals form, and filter again. Wash with a little ice-cold solvent and dry.
5. **Test purity**: measure the melting point. A pure solid melts sharply at the data-book temperature. An impure solid melts over a wider range and at a lower temperature.

## Worked example 1: products and equation

**Question.** Write an equation for the reaction of ethanoyl chloride with propan-1-ol and name the ester formed.

```
CH₃COCl + CH₃CH₂CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ + HCl
```

The ester is propyl ethanoate. The alcohol supplies the propyl group; the acyl chloride supplies the ethanoate.

## Worked example 2: percentage yield of aspirin

2.76 g of 2-hydroxybenzoic acid (Mᵣ = 138.0) reacts with excess ethanoic anhydride, and 2.52 g of aspirin (Mᵣ = 180.0) is obtained.

```
n(2-hydroxybenzoic acid) = 2.76 ÷ 138.0 = 0.0200 mol
theoretical n(aspirin)   = 0.0200 mol
theoretical mass         = 0.0200 × 180.0 = 3.60 g
percentage yield         = 2.52 ÷ 3.60 × 100 = 70.0 %
```

## Worked example 3: an amide from an acyl chloride

3.925 g of ethanoyl chloride (Mᵣ = 78.5) reacts with excess ammonia. Calculate the mass of ethanamide (Mᵣ = 59.0) formed if the yield is 100 %.

```
n(CH₃COCl)   = 3.925 ÷ 78.5 = 0.0500 mol
n(CH₃CONH₂)  = 0.0500 mol          (1 : 1)
mass         = 0.0500 × 59.0 = 2.95 g
```

Two moles of ammonia are used per mole of ethanoyl chloride, but the amide is formed 1 : 1.

## Worked example 4: atom economy is not the whole story

Atom economy of the aspirin synthesis using ethanoic anhydride: 180.0 ÷ (138.0 + 102.0) × 100 = 75.0 %. Using ethanoyl chloride (Mᵣ 78.5) it would be 180.0 ÷ (138.0 + 78.5) × 100 = 83.1 %, which is higher. Even so, industry uses the anhydride, because cost, safety and the nature of the by-product matter as well as atom economy.

## Common mistakes

- Drawing the first arrow from the carbon or chlorine instead of from the lone pair of the nucleophile.
- Showing C=O pi electrons staying in place, so that the carbon has five bonds in the intermediate.
- Giving water as a product of the reaction with an alcohol (the products are an ester and HCl).
- Using one mole of ammonia per mole of acyl chloride.
- Naming the product of an amine and an acyl chloride as an ordinary amide, with no N-substituent.
- Writing ethanoic acid as an alternative to HCl in acyl chloride reactions.
- Saying the anhydride is better "because it is more reactive". It is less reactive, which is an advantage.
- Not showing the O⁻ and charges in the intermediate.

## Exam technique

- **Outline the mechanism**: draw the intermediate with its charges, four arrows, and the products with Cl⁻.
- **Write the equation**: for ammonia and amines, use two moles of the nucleophile.
- **Industrial advantage**: give at least two reasons and link each to cost or safety.
- **Purity**: describe the melting point and compare it with the data value.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is it addition–elimination and not substitution in one step?**
The product is a substitution overall, but the mechanism passes through an intermediate where the nucleophile has added to the carbon before the chloride is removed.

**Why does the second molecule of ammonia matter?**
The proton lost from the nitrogen after attack would otherwise make hydrogen chloride and use up the amine. The second ammonia takes the proton.

**Why not make aspirin by esterification with ethanoic acid?**
The reaction is reversible and slow. The anhydride gives a fast, complete reaction.
