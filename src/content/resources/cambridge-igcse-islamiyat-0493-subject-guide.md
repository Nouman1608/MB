Cambridge IGCSE Islamiyat (0493) is a course in the sources, history and practice of Islam, studied through the Qur'an, the Hadiths, the life of the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh), the early Muslim community and the core beliefs and duties of the faith. It is assessed by two compulsory written papers of equal size: Paper 1 and Paper 2, each lasting 1 hour 30 minutes and carrying 50 marks, so each is worth half of the final result. Answers are written in English, and grades run from A* to G. This page explains how the content is divided between the papers, how they relate to one another and how to plan a year of study.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus does not give the papers separate titles; it simply calls them Paper 1 and Paper 2. Each paper has its own block of content, made up of four areas.

**Paper 1** focuses on the Qur'an and the beginnings of Islam:

- Major Themes of the Qur'an
- The History and Importance of the Qur'an
- The Life and Importance of the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh)
- The First Islamic Community

**Paper 2** focuses on the Hadiths, early leadership and the foundations of belief and practice:

- Major Teachings in the Hadiths of the Prophet
- The History and Importance of the Hadiths
- The Period of Rule of the Rightly Guided Caliphs and Their Importance as Leaders
- The Articles of Faith and the Pillars of Islam

A useful way to see the shape of the course is that each paper pairs a body of sacred text with the history around it. Paper 1 pairs the Qur'an with the life of the Prophet (pbuh) and the community he led; Paper 2 pairs the Hadiths with the leadership that followed and the beliefs and practices that define Muslim life.

## How it's assessed

The assessment is linear and not tiered: both papers are taken in the same series at the end of the course.

| Paper | Content | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Qur'an, the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh) and the first Islamic community | 1 hour 30 minutes | 50 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Hadiths, the Rightly Guided Caliphs, the Articles of Faith and the Pillars of Islam | 1 hour 30 minutes | 50 | 50% |

The syllabus does not print a percentage for each paper. The 50/50 split follows directly from the fact that both papers are compulsory and each carries 50 of the 100 available marks. The record used here covers the 2026-2027 examination window, first examined in 2026.

## How the parts fit together

Although the two papers are examined separately, the content is closely connected, and knowledge from one regularly helps with the other.

The life of the Prophet (pbuh) in Paper 1 is the background to almost everything in Paper 2. The Hadiths record his sayings and actions, so understanding the events of his life makes their teachings easier to place and explain. Likewise, the Rightly Guided Caliphs led the community that is introduced in Paper 1 under The First Islamic Community, so the two topics read as one continuous story.

The relationship between the two sacred sources is another link. Paper 1 asks about the history and importance of the Qur'an and Paper 2 about the history and importance of the Hadiths. Studying them side by side, noting how each was preserved and why each matters, gives you clearer answers on both papers.

Finally, the Articles of Faith and the Pillars of Islam in Paper 2 connect to the Major Themes of the Qur'an in Paper 1. Beliefs about God, prophethood and the afterlife, and duties such as prayer and fasting, can be supported with reference to the Qur'an, which strengthens explanation and evaluation.

Across both papers, the same skills recur: knowing the content accurately, explaining its meaning and significance, and relating it to the lives of Muslims.

## Planning your study

Since the two papers carry equal marks, both deserve equal time. A sensible order follows the history: start with the Qur'an and the life of the Prophet (pbuh), move to the first Islamic community, and then continue with the Hadiths, the Rightly Guided Caliphs and finally the Articles of Faith and Pillars of Islam.

In general terms, a course plan might look like this:

- **First stage:** Major Themes of the Qur'an and The History and Importance of the Qur'an, learning set passages and their themes steadily rather than in one block.
- **Second stage:** The Life and Importance of the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh) and The First Islamic Community, building a clear timeline of events.
- **Third stage:** the Hadiths, both their teachings and their history, followed by the Rightly Guided Caliphs.
- **Final stage:** the Articles of Faith and the Pillars of Islam, then mixed revision across both papers.

Regular short recall practice works well for the text-based topics, while timelines and summary tables help with the historical ones. Begin answering exam-style questions on each area as soon as it is taught, and move to full past papers in the final months. ## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge IGCSE Islamiyat 0493?**
There are two compulsory papers, Paper 1 and Paper 2. Each lasts 1 hour 30 minutes and is marked out of 50.

**What does Paper 1 cover?**
Paper 1 covers the Major Themes of the Qur'an, the History and Importance of the Qur'an, the Life and Importance of the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh) and the First Islamic Community.

**What does Paper 2 cover?**
Paper 2 covers the Major Teachings in the Hadiths, the History and Importance of the Hadiths, the period of the Rightly Guided Caliphs, and the Articles of Faith and the Pillars of Islam.

**Are the two papers worth the same?**
Yes. The syllabus does not state a percentage per paper, but both are compulsory and each carries 50 of the 100 total marks, so each is worth 50%.

**What language are answers written in?**
Answers are given in English. The syllabus is not tiered and candidates are graded A* to G.
