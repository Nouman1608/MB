## Questions

**1.** Describe two ways in which a root hair cell is adapted to take in water and mineral ions. For each, say how it helps. **[4]**

**2.** The concentration of nitrate ions is 0.3 mmol per dm³ in soil water and 4.5 mmol per dm³ inside a root hair cell. The cell still takes up nitrate.

**(a)** Name the process by which nitrate enters the cell. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why diffusion could not move the nitrate in. **[2]**
**(c)** After heavy rain the soil is waterlogged and contains very little oxygen. Explain what happens to nitrate uptake. **[2]**

**3.** A gardener puts far too much fertiliser on a potted plant. Within a day the plant wilts. Use osmosis to explain why. **[4]**

**4.** The xylem and phloem are both transport tissues.

**(a)** Copy and complete the table.

| | Xylem | Phloem |
|---|---|---|
| Substances carried | | |
| Direction of movement | | |
| Cells living or dead | | |

**[3]**
**(b)** Explain how lignin in the walls of xylem vessels helps the plant. **[2]**
**(c)** State the job of a companion cell. **[1]**

**5.** Describe how water moves from the xylem in a leaf to the air outside the plant. **[5]**

**6.** A leaf impression was made from each surface of a leaf. Stomata were counted in five fields of view, each 0.40 mm in diameter.

| Surface | Counts in five fields |
|---|---|
| Upper | 2, 3, 1, 2, 2 |
| Lower | 11, 9, 13, 12, 10 |

**(a)** Calculate the area of one field of view, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the stomatal density of the lower surface in stomata per mm², to the nearest whole number. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why the upper surface has fewer stomata. **[2]**

**7.** A ring of bark containing the phloem was cut from one branch of a tree, leaving the xylem. After some weeks the sugar concentration in bark tissue was measured just above and just below the ring, and at the same positions on an unringed branch.

| Position | Sugar in bark (mg per g) |
|---|---|
| Ringed branch, above ring | 42 |
| Ringed branch, below ring | 6 |
| Unringed branch, above | 24 |
| Unringed branch, below | 25 |

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater the sugar concentration is above the ring than below it, and state what the unringed branch shows. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the results for the ringed branch. **[2]**
**(c)** The leaves above the ring did not wilt. Explain why. **[2]**

**8.** **(a)** Explain why a rise in temperature increases the rate of transpiration. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a fan blowing on a plant increases the rate of transpiration. **[2]**
**(c)** Name one other environmental factor, not given in (a) or (b), that changes the rate of transpiration. **[1]**

**9.** A potometer has a capillary tube of internal radius 0.50 mm. The distance moved by the bubble in 5.0 minutes was measured three times at each light level.

| Light level | Distance moved (mm) |
|---|---|
| Bright | 40, 42, 41 |
| Dim | 20, 22, 21 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of bubble movement in bright light, in mm per minute. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the radius to find the volume of water absorbed each minute in bright light, in mm³ per minute, to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** The mean rate in dim light is 4.2 mm per minute. Calculate the percentage decrease in rate from bright to dim light, to 2 significant figures. **[2]**

**10.** Describe how you would use a potometer to investigate the effect of air movement on the rate of water uptake by a leafy shoot. **[6]**

**11.** Two similar leafy shoots, A and B, were each placed in a tube of water with a layer of oil on top. The lower surfaces of the leaves of shoot B were covered in petroleum jelly. Each tube was weighed at the start and after 4.0 hours in the same conditions.

| Shoot | Start mass (g) | Final mass (g) |
|---|---|---|
| A | 150.0 | 146.4 |
| B | 148.0 | 147.2 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage loss in mass of A. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage loss in mass of B, to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** What do the results suggest about where most water is lost from a leaf? Explain. **[2]**

**12.** A potometer was used outdoors on one day, giving these rates of bubble movement.

| Time | 06:00 | 09:00 | 12:00 | 15:00 | 18:00 | 21:00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate (mm per minute) | 0.8 | 3.2 | 5.6 | 6.4 | 2.4 | 0.4 |

**(a)** At what time was the rate highest? Calculate the percentage increase in rate from 09:00 to that time. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean rate of change of the rate between 09:00 and 12:00, in mm per minute per hour. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the rise in rate from 06:00 to 15:00. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain the fall in rate from 15:00 to 21:00. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Long thin extension, giving a large surface area for absorbing water and ions [1] [1]. Many mitochondria, releasing energy for active transport of mineral ions [1] [1]. (Other valid pairs: a thin wall giving a short diffusion distance, or a large vacuole keeping the water concentration low.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A feature earns little without its function, so add "which means".

**2. (a)** **Active transport** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Diffusion moves particles from high to low concentration [1]. The nitrate concentration is lower in the soil than in the cell, so diffusion would move it out, not in [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Uptake falls [1], because with little oxygen there is less respiration, so less energy for active transport [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote both concentrations in a gradient argument. Saying only "against the gradient" is a weaker answer than showing which way diffusion would go.

**3.** The soil water becomes a concentrated solution, so its water concentration falls below that inside the root hair cells [1]. Water moves by osmosis [1] out of the root cells, through the partially permeable membrane, down the concentration gradient [1]. The cells lose water and become flaccid, so the plant wilts [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say which has the higher water concentration, soil or cell. "Water moves to where there is more salt" gives no gradient.

**4. (a)** Xylem: water and mineral ions; up only; dead. Phloem: dissolved sugars; up and down; living. One mark for each correct row [1] [1] [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Lignin makes the walls strong and waterproof [1], so the vessels do not collapse inward when water is pulled up, and they give support [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** It supplies energy (it has many mitochondria) to load sugars into the sieve tube [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the exact substance, such as dissolved sugars. "Food" is vague.

**5.** Water evaporates from the surface of mesophyll cells [1] into the air spaces in the leaf [1]. The water vapour diffuses out through open stomata [1] down a concentration gradient into the drier air [1]. More water is pulled up the xylem to replace it [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use "evaporates" for the liquid-to-vapour step and "diffuses" for the movement out.

**6. (a)** Radius = 0.20 mm [1]. Area = π × 0.20² = **0.126 mm²** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Mean = (11 + 9 + 13 + 12 + 10) ÷ 5 = 11 [1]. Density = 11 ÷ 0.1257 = **88 per mm²** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The upper surface is more exposed to sun and warm air [1], so fewer stomata means less water lost [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the unrounded area until the end. Rounding it to 0.126 first gives 87, so you can lose accuracy from early rounding.

**7. (a)** 42 ÷ 6 = **7 times** [1]. The unringed branch shows about the same concentration above and below, so the ring causes the difference [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Sugar made in the leaves travels down in the phloem [1]. The ring removed the phloem, so the sugar builds up above the cut and little reaches the tissue below [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The xylem was left intact [1], so water and mineral ions still moved up to the leaves [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the unringed branch as the comparison.

**8. (a)** Water evaporates faster from the mesophyll cells [1] and vapour diffuses out faster, so the rate of transpiration increases [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Draughts remove vapour from beside the stomata [1], so the concentration gradient stays steep and diffusion out is quicker [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Light intensity (or humidity) [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A reason must say what happens at the leaf. "Because it is hotter" restates the question.

**9. (a)** Mean distance = (40 + 42 + 41) ÷ 3 = 41 mm [1]; 41 ÷ 5.0 = **8.2 mm per minute** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Area = π × 0.50² = 0.785 mm² [1]; 8.2 × 0.785 = **6.4 mm³ per minute** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** (8.2 − 4.2) ÷ 8.2 × 100 [1] = **49%** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Percentage change uses the starting value, here the bright light rate.

**10.** Cut the shoot while submerged and connect it to the potometer underwater, with airtight joints [1]. Introduce an air bubble and record its start position [1]. Time the bubble over a fixed interval, such as 5 minutes, and record its distance [1]. Change one variable, such as the distance of a fan from the shoot [1], and keep temperature, light and the shoot the same [1]. Repeat each setting, calculate the mean rate (distance ÷ time) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A plan needs the independent variable (fan distance), the measurement and the controls. "Keep it fair" names no control.

**11. (a)** Loss = 150.0 − 146.4 = 3.6 g [1]; 3.6 ÷ 150.0 × 100 = **2.4%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Loss = 148.0 − 147.2 = 0.8 g [1]; 0.8 ÷ 148.0 × 100 = **0.54%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Most water is lost from the lower surface [1], because blocking it cut the loss from 2.4% to 0.54%, and the lower surface has most of the stomata [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Back the conclusion with numbers taken from the table.

**12. (a)** 15:00 [1]; (6.4 − 3.2) ÷ 3.2 × 100 = **100%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (5.6 − 3.2) ÷ 3 [1] = **0.8 mm per minute per hour** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Light intensity and temperature increase through the morning [1], so the stomata open wider and evaporation and diffusion speed up [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Light fades and temperature drops [1], so stomata close and the gradient becomes smaller, slowing water loss [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A rate of change needs the time interval and units with "per hour".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing osmosis for the uptake of mineral ions.
- Describing a feature without its function.
- Saying plants lose water through the xylem instead of through the stomata.
- Mixing up xylem and phloem direction and cell type.
- Calculating percentage change from the final value.
- Forgetting the cross-sectional area in a volume rate.
- Leaving out units, or omitting "per hour" in a rate of change.
- Naming no control variables in a potometer method.
