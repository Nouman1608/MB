These questions cover **3.3.2.2 Modification of alkanes by cracking** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 2 and able to reappear in Unit 5. Section A has short recall and application questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [cracking study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-2-2/) or the [cracking revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-2-2-revision-notes/) first. Take Ar values as C = 12.0 and H = 1.0.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Which set of conditions is used for catalytic cracking?

A: high pressure, high temperature, no catalyst

B: slight pressure, high temperature, zeolite catalyst

C: high pressure, low temperature, zeolite catalyst

D: slight pressure, low temperature, iron catalyst **[1]**

**2.** State the type of bond that is broken when an alkane is cracked. **[1]**

**3.** Pentadecane, C₁₅H₃₂, cracks to give nonane, C₉H₂₀, and propene only. Write the equation. **[1]**

**4.** Tridecane, C₁₃H₂₈, cracks to give one molecule of hexane, one molecule of propene and two molecules of compound X only. Identify X. **[2]**

**5.** State the main use of the products of catalytic cracking. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why the products of cracking an alkane always include at least one unsaturated compound. **[2]**

### Section B

**7.** A company wants to make ethene and propene to produce polymers.

**(a)** State which type of cracking it should use, and give the conditions. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why alkenes are more useful than alkanes as starting materials for making polymers. **[1]**

**(c)** Undecane, C₁₁H₂₄, cracks to give one molecule of an alkane, one molecule of ethene and two molecules of propene. Write the equation and name the alkane. **[2]**

**8.** Hexadecane is cracked by the reaction C₁₆H₃₄ → C₈H₁₈ + 4C₂H₄.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum mass of ethene, in tonnes, made from 2.50 tonnes of hexadecane. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the total product mass that is ethene. **[1]**

**9.** The figures below are invented to show the situation at one refinery.

| Fraction | Supply from distillation / % | Demand / % |
|---|---|---|
| Refinery gases | 3 | 8 |
| Petrol | 18 | 31 |
| Kerosene | 13 | 11 |
| Diesel | 24 | 25 |
| Fuel oil and residue | 42 | 25 |

**(a)** Identify the fraction with the largest surplus, and the fraction with the largest shortage. Use the data. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the refinery cracks some of its fuel oil. **[2]**

**10.** Catalytic cracking uses a zeolite.

**(a)** Describe the structure of a zeolite and explain why it makes a good catalyst. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why catalytic cracking does not need a high pressure, unlike thermal cracking. **[1]**

**(c)** Name the type of hydrocarbon, other than branched alkanes, that catalytic cracking is used to produce for motor fuels. **[1]**

**11.** Dodecane, C₁₂H₂₆, can crack in more than one way.

**(a)** Write an equation for dodecane cracking into octane and one other product only, one molecule of each. **[1]**

**(b)** Write an equation for dodecane cracking into hexane and propene only. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the product of cracking dodecane in a refinery is a mixture, even when the conditions are fixed. **[1]**

**(d)** Bromine water is shaken with the gaseous products. State what is seen and what this shows. **[2]**

**12.** Explain the economic reasons for cracking alkanes. **[4]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** B [1]

**2.** C–C (carbon–carbon single) bonds [1]

**3.** C₁₅H₃₂ → C₉H₂₀ + 2C₃H₆ [1]

**4.** Carbon in 2X: 13 − 6 − 3 = 4; hydrogen in 2X: 28 − 14 − 6 = 8 [1]; X = C₂H₄, ethene [1]

**Total: 2**

**5.** Motor fuels (for example petrol), and aromatic hydrocarbons [1]

**6.** An alkane CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ has too few hydrogen atoms for all the fragments to be alkanes (two alkanes would need two more H atoms) [1]; so at least one product has a C=C bond, an alkene [1]

**Total: 2**

### Section B

**7.**

**(a)** Thermal cracking [1]; high temperature and high pressure [1]

**(b)** Alkenes have a reactive C=C double bond, which can undergo addition polymerisation; alkanes are unreactive [1]

**(c)** C₁₁H₂₄ → C₃H₈ + C₂H₄ + 2C₃H₆ [1]; propane [1]

**Total: 5**

*Examiner insight:* Check (c) by subtraction: carbon 11 − 2 − 6 = 3 and hydrogen 24 − 4 − 12 = 8, giving C₃H₈.

**8.**

**(a)** Mr(C₁₆H₃₄) = 226.0; n = 2.50 × 10⁶ ÷ 226.0 = 1.106 × 10⁴ mol [1]; n(C₂H₄) = 4 × 1.106 × 10⁴ = 4.425 × 10⁴ mol [1]; mass = 4.425 × 10⁴ × 28.0 = 1.24 × 10⁶ g = 1.24 tonnes [1]

**(b)** (4 × 28.0) ÷ 226.0 × 100 = 49.6 % [1]

**Total: 4**

*Examiner insight:* You can work in tonnes throughout: 2.50 × (4 × 28.0 ÷ 226.0) = 1.24 tonnes. Mixing grams and tonnes in one line is where marks go.

**9.**

**(a)** Largest surplus: fuel oil and residue, 42 % supplied against 25 % demand [1]; largest shortage: petrol, 18 % supplied against 31 % demand [1]

**(b)** Cracking breaks the long-chain molecules of the surplus fuel oil into shorter molecules that can be used in petrol [1]; petrol is in higher demand than supply, so it is worth more than fuel oil, and cracking also gives alkenes for making polymers [1]

**Total: 4**

**10.**

**(a)** A porous aluminosilicate solid with a honeycomb-like structure [1]; this gives a very large surface area for the reaction [1]

**(b)** The catalyst provides a route with a lower activation energy, so the reaction is fast enough at a slight pressure [1]

**(c)** Aromatic hydrocarbons [1]

**Total: 4**

**11.**

**(a)** C₁₂H₂₆ → C₈H₁₈ + C₄H₈ [1]

**(b)** C₁₂H₂₆ → C₆H₁₄ + 2C₃H₆ [1]

**(c)** C–C bonds can break at many different points along the chain, so many different products form [1]

**(d)** Bromine water is decolourised (orange to colourless) [1]; this shows that an alkene (an unsaturated compound) is present [1]

**Total: 5**

**12.** Fractional distillation gives fixed proportions of each fraction, set by the crude oil [1]; there is more of the long-chain fractions than is needed, and not enough of the short-chain fractions such as petrol [1]; the short-chain fractions are in high demand, so they are more valuable, and cracking converts low-value surplus into these products [1]; cracking also produces alkenes, which are valuable feedstock for polymers and other chemicals [1]

**Total: 4**

*Examiner insight:* "To make more petrol" earns at most one mark. The full answer links supply, demand, value and the use of alkenes.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping the conditions or products of thermal and catalytic cracking.
- Cracking equations with no alkene, or with hydrogen not balanced.
- Mixing units in mass calculations.
- Economic answers with no reference to supply, demand or value.
- Saying bromine water "goes clear": the expected wording is "decolourised" or "orange to colourless".
