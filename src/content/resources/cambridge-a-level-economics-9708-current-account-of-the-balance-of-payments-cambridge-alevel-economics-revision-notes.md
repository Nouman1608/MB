Condensed notes for **section 6.3 Current account of the balance of payments** (AS Level, Cambridge 9708, 2026–2028). For worked examples and full explanations, use the [current account study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-economics-9708-current-account-of-the-balance-of-payments-cambridge-alevel-economics/). Figures are invented.

## The four components

| Component | Records | Credit example | Debit example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trade in goods | Physical exports and imports | Selling copper abroad | Buying foreign cars |
| Trade in services | Intangible exports and imports | Foreign tourists' spending at home | Paying a foreign shipping firm |
| Primary income | Pay for work and income from owning assets across borders | Dividends from shares held abroad | Profit sent home by a foreign-owned firm |
| Secondary income | Transfers with nothing given in return | Money sent home by migrants living abroad | Cash aid given to another country |

**Sign rule:** follow the money. Money in = credit (+). Money out = debit (−).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Current account | Record of a country's trade in goods and services, primary income and secondary income with the rest of the world over a period |
| Balance | Credits minus debits on a line or on the whole account |
| Surplus | Credits greater than debits (positive balance) |
| Deficit | Debits greater than credits (negative balance) |
| Imbalance | A deficit or a surplus |
| Visible trade | Trade in goods |
| Invisible trade | Trade in services (often extended to include income flows) |
| CAB | Current account balance |
| Price competitiveness | Ability to sell at lower prices than rivals |
| Non-price competitiveness | Ability to win sales through quality, design, reliability or service |

## Calculation chain

```
Goods balance            = X goods - M goods
Services balance         = X services - M services
Goods and services       = goods balance + services balance
CAB                      = goods and services + primary + secondary
CAB as % of GDP          = CAB / GDP x 100
```

**Missing value method:** write the full identity, substitute what you know, rearrange, then check by recalculating the CAB.

Quick example: goods −20, services +12, primary −5, secondary +4. Goods and services = −8; CAB = −8 − 5 + 4 = −9.

## Causes of a deficit

| Temporary | Long-lasting |
|---|---|
| Domestic boom raising imports | Low productivity, high unit costs |
| Recession in export markets | Weak quality or design |
| One-off fall in an export price | Narrow export base with falling demand |
| Temporary currency appreciation | Inflation persistently above partners' |
| Poor harvest | Large income payments to foreign owners of assets |

Causes of a surplus are broadly the reverse.

## Consequences

**Domestic (deficit):**

- Lower net exports → lower AD → lower output and jobs in exporting and import-competing firms.
- More currency supplied than demanded → downward pressure on the exchange rate → dearer imports → possible inflation.
- Consumption and investment above current production → higher living standards now, financed by borrowing or asset sales → future income outflows.

**External:** partners' exports and jobs depend on the deficit country's imports; sharp cuts in its imports slow their growth; persistent imbalances raise the risk of protectionism.

**Surplus:** higher AD and jobs; possible inflation near full capacity; upward pressure on the currency; consumption below production.

## When a deficit matters more

| Matters less | Matters more |
|---|---|
| Small relative to GDP | Large relative to GDP |
| Short-lived | Persistent |
| Caused by imports of capital goods | Caused by imports of consumer goods |
| Caused by a temporary boom | Caused by weak competitiveness |

## Exam traps

- Tourism is a service; foreign tourists spending at home is a credit.
- Profit, interest and dividends belong in primary income, not services.
- The goods and services balance is not the CAB.
- A deficit is a flow, not a debt.
- Keep the minus sign on a deficit.
- Buying a foreign factory or foreign bonds is not a current account item; the income they later earn is.

## Self-test

1. Name the four components of the current account.
2. A foreign student pays tuition fees to a university in Country A. Which component, and which sign, for Country A?
3. A Country A firm pays dividends to foreign shareholders. Which component and sign?
4. Country A's government receives a cash grant from another government. Which component and sign?
5. Define a current account deficit.
6. Goods balance −15, services +9, primary +2, secondary −4. Calculate the goods and services balance and the CAB.
7. CAB is −14 and GDP is 350. Express the CAB as a percentage of GDP.
8. Exports of goods are 60, the goods balance is −8. Calculate imports of goods.
9. Give one temporary and one long-lasting cause of a deficit.
10. Explain how a deficit can reduce employment.
11. Why may a deficit raise inflation?
12. Explain one way a deficit in Country A affects its trading partners.

**Answers:** 1. Trade in goods, trade in services, primary income, secondary income. 2. Trade in services, credit (an export of education services). 3. Primary income, debit. 4. Secondary income, credit. 5. Total debits on the current account exceed total credits over the period. 6. Goods and services = −6; CAB = −6 + 2 − 4 = −8. 7. −14 ÷ 350 × 100 = −4% of GDP. 8. 60 − M = −8, so M = 68. 9. Temporary: a domestic boom; long-lasting: low productivity (other valid answers accepted). 10. Lower net exports reduce AD, so firms producing exports and goods that compete with imports cut output and need fewer workers. 11. The deficit can push the exchange rate down, raising import prices and costs. 12. Partners earn export revenue from Country A's imports, so their output and jobs gain; a cut in Country A's imports would hurt them.
