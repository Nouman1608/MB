## Section A

**1.** Name the instrument used to measure each of the following:
(a) air pressure, (b) wind speed, (c) hours of sunshine. **[3]**

**2.** State the unit in which rainfall is measured. **[1]**

**3.** A maximum-minimum thermometer shows a maximum of 27 °C and a
minimum of 15 °C. Calculate (a) the temperature range and (b) the
mean temperature for the day. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why a rain gauge should not be placed close to a
building. **[2]**

**5.** An observer records the sky as 2 oktas of cumulus cloud.
Describe what the sky looks like. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A school wants to set up a weather station on its site.

**(a)** Describe three features of a Stevenson Screen and explain how
each helps to give accurate readings. **[6]**
**(b)** The school places its screen on a tarmac playground beside the
main building. Suggest two reasons why its temperature readings may
not be representative. **[2]**

**7.** The table shows readings from a wet-and-dry bulb thermometer on
three days.

| Day | Dry bulb (°C) | Wet bulb (°C) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 26 | 26 |
| 2 | 26 | 22 |
| 3 | 26 | 18 |

**(a)** Calculate the difference between the readings on each day. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify the day with the lowest relative humidity and explain
your answer. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the wet bulb reading is usually lower than the dry
bulb reading. **[2]**

**8.** The table shows the number of days on which the wind blew from
each direction during one month at a weather station.

| Direction | N | NE | E | SE | S | SW | W | NW |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Days | 3 | 2 | 1 | 4 | 6 | 9 | 4 | 1 |

**(a)** Name the instrument used to record wind direction. **[1]**
**(b)** State the prevailing wind direction. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage of days on which the wind came from
the prevailing direction. **[2]**
**(d)** Name a diagram that could be used to show these data. **[1]**

**9.** The table shows monthly climate data for a weather station.

| | J | F | M | A | M | J | J | A | S | O | N | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temp (°C) | 12 | 14 | 18 | 23 | 28 | 32 | 34 | 33 | 29 | 23 | 17 | 13 |
| Rain (mm) | 5 | 4 | 3 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 4 |

**(a)** Calculate the annual temperature range. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the total annual rainfall. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the pattern of temperature and rainfall shown. Use
figures in your answer. **[4]**

**10.** Weather data can be collected with traditional instruments or
with an automatic digital weather station. Evaluate the use of a
digital weather station for a school. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Barometer [1]. (b) Anemometer [1]. (c) Sunshine recorder [1].

**2.** Millimetres (mm) [1].

**3.** (a) 27 − 15 = 12 °C [1]. (b) (27 + 15) ÷ 2 = 21 °C [1].

**4.** The building would shelter the gauge from rain blowing in from
one direction, so too little would be collected [1]; or rain running
off or splashing from the roof and walls could add extra water, so too
much would be collected [1]. Either way the reading would not be
representative.

**5.** Most of the sky is clear, with only about a quarter
(two-eighths) covered [1] by white, heaped clouds with flat bases [1].

**6. (a)** Any three, each with its reason, 2 marks each [6]:
painted white, to reflect the Sun's radiation so the box does not
heat up; louvred sides, so air circulates freely while direct sunlight
and rain are kept off the thermometers; raised on legs about 1.2 m
above the ground, so heat radiated from the ground does not affect the
readings; insulated or double roof, to reduce heating from above; door
facing away from the Sun, so sunlight does not fall on the
thermometers while they are read.
**(b)** Any two [2]: tarmac absorbs heat and releases it, raising
temperatures; the building radiates heat or gives shade, so readings
are too high or too low; the building shelters the screen from wind,
so air does not circulate normally.

**7. (a)** Day 1: 0 °C; Day 2: 4 °C; Day 3: 8 °C [1].
**(b)** Day 3 [1]. It has the largest difference between dry and wet
bulb [1]. A large difference means a lot of evaporation is taking place
from the wet bulb, which only happens when the air is dry [1].
**(c)** Water evaporates from the muslin around the wet bulb [1];
evaporation uses heat, which cools the bulb [1].

**8. (a)** Wind vane [1].
**(b)** South-west (south-westerly) [1].
**(c)** Total days = 30 [1]; 9 ÷ 30 × 100 = 30 per cent [1].
**(d)** Wind rose [1].

**9. (a)** 34 − 12 = 22 °C [1].
**(b)** 20 mm [1].
**(c)** Up to 4 marks: temperatures are high in summer, with a
maximum of 34 °C in July [1]; lowest in January at 12 °C, so there is a
large annual range of 22 °C [1]; rainfall is very low all year, only
20 mm in total [1]; there is no rain from May to September, and the
wettest month, January, has only 5 mm [1]. (This pattern fits a hot
desert climate in the northern hemisphere, which links to subtopic
2.5.)

**10.** Up to 4 marks for a balanced answer. Advantages: records
automatically and continuously, including at night and in holidays
[1]; data can be downloaded straight into graphs and are less affected
by reading errors [1]. Disadvantages: expensive to buy and repair, and
needs a power supply [1]; faults may go unnoticed, and it must still be
sited correctly to be accurate [1]. A conclusion that weighs both sides,
for example that a digital station is worthwhile if the school can
check and maintain it, is needed for full marks.

## A note on exam technique for this subtopic

Most marks in 2.4 are lost through imprecision rather than lack of
knowledge. Pair every Stevenson Screen feature with its reason in the
same sentence, state the unit after every calculation, and remember
that winds are named by the direction they come from.
