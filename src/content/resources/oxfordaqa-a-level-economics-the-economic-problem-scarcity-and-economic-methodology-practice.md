## Questions

**1.** Define scarcity and explain why it forces choices. **[3]**

**2.** Classify each statement about Country Red as positive or normative.

**(a)** Unemployment rose from 6% to 8% last year. **[1]**
**(b)** The government ought to spend more on railways. **[1]**
**(c)** Raising the minimum wage will reduce employment. **[1]**
**(d)** The gap between rich and poor is too wide. **[1]**

**3.** A café raises the price of a coffee. Explain the role of ceteris paribus in predicting that quantity demanded will fall, and show how the prediction could appear to fail. **[4]**

**4.** A potter has 36 hours a week. A bowl takes 1.5 hours and sells for 12. A vase takes 4 hours and sells for 34.

**(a)** State the maximum number of bowls or vases she could make in a week. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out what one vase costs her in bowls. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate which product she should make. **[3]**

**5.** Five items are used by a small firm: a delivery van, a coal seam it mines, the owner who risks personal savings to run it, a call-centre agent's hours and a managed forest it owns.

**(a)** Classify each as land, labour, capital or enterprise. **[5]**
**(b)** Which of the natural resources is renewable? **[1]**

**6.** A fishery has a stock of 8,000 tonnes. The stock regrows by 10% of the opening stock each year. 1,200 tonnes are caught each year.

**(a)** Calculate the stock after three years. **[3]**
**(b)** State the catch that would keep the stock constant in year 1. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why this shows that the environment is a scarce resource. **[2]**

**7.** Country Beta produces tractors (T) and bolts of textile (B). Boundary points (T, B): (0, 90), (20, 84), (40, 66), (60, 30).

**(a)** Calculate the opportunity cost per tractor of raising output from 20 to 40 tractors. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the opportunity cost rises as more tractors are made. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe what each point means: (T = 40, B = 60) and (T = 60, B = 50). **[3]**
**(d)** Explain the effect on the diagram of a technology that improves both industries. **[2]**

**8.** In question 7, society values one extra tractor at 1.2 bolts. Identify the allocatively efficient point and justify it. **[5]**

**9.** A firm makes 200 units a day. Method X uses 10 workers and 2 machines. Method Y uses 4 workers and 5 machines. A machine costs 50 a day.

**(a)** Compare daily costs at wages of 18 and 32. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the wage at which the methods cost the same. **[2]**

**10.** Assess the advantages and disadvantages of allocating resources through a centrally planned system rather than a free market. **[8]**

**11.** Country Red has five people who each earn 150. Country Blue has five people earning 500, 100, 50, 50 and 50.

**(a)** Calculate Blue's mean and median income and the richest person's share of total income. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why average income gives an incomplete view of economic welfare. **[3]**
**(c)** "Blue should redistribute income." Explain why economists might disagree. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Scarcity is limited resources set against unlimited wants [1]. Not all wants can be met [1], so choices must be made between uses, and each choice has an opportunity cost [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention both sides, limited resources and unlimited wants. Saying only "there is not enough" is incomplete.

**2.** **(a)** Positive: a testable fact [1]. **(b)** Normative: "ought" is a value judgement [1]. **(c)** Positive: a prediction that evidence can test, even if it proves wrong [1]. **(d)** Normative: "too wide" is a judgement [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A prediction is positive even if it might be false. Explaining your classification is safer than a bare label.

**3.** Ceteris paribus means other factors are held constant [1]. This isolates the effect of price on quantity demanded [1]. If incomes rise, or a nearby rival closes, at the same time, demand could increase and offset the price effect [1]. Sales might then not fall, although the price relationship still holds. The prediction is conditional [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Make the example specific: name the other factor that moved and the direction of its effect.

**4.** **(a)** 36 ÷ 1.5 = **24 bowls** [1]; 36 ÷ 4 = **9 vases** [1].
**(b)** Vase hours ÷ bowl hours: 4 ÷ 1.5 [1] = 2.67 bowls (8/3) [1].
**(c)** All bowls earn 24 × 12 = 288; all vases earn 9 × 34 = 306, so vases earn 18 more [1]. Per hour, bowls earn 8 and vases earn 8.50 [1]. The gap is small, so the decision depends on whether she can sell 24 bowls or 9 vases, on risk and on her preferences [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation requires a judgement. State the choice, then say what could change it.

**5.** **(a)** Delivery van: capital [1]. Coal seam: land [1]. Owner who risks savings: enterprise [1]. Agent's hours: labour [1]. Managed forest: land [1]. **(b)** The managed forest is renewable (the coal seam is not) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the four standard terms. A manager's role can be labour or enterprise depending on risk-taking, so justify it.

**6.** **(a)** Year 1: 8,000 + 800 - 1,200 = 7,600 [1]. Year 2: 7,600 + 760 - 1,200 = 7,160 [1]. Year 3: 7,160 + 716 - 1,200 = **6,676 tonnes** [1].
**(b)** 10% of 8,000 = **800 tonnes** [1].
**(c)** The fish stock is a limited natural resource [1]. Catching more than regrowth reduces future output, so using it now has an opportunity cost in future catch [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the opening stock for each year, because growth is based on a changing stock.

**7.** **(a)** Bolts lost: 84 - 66 = 18 [1]. Per tractor: 18 ÷ 20 = **0.9 bolts** [1].
**(b)** Some resources suit one good better than the other [1]. Making more tractors draws in resources better suited to textiles, so more bolts are lost per extra tractor [1].
**(c)** (40, 60): boundary at T = 40 is 66, so 6 bolts (9.1%) short, meaning unemployed or inefficiently used resources [1][1]. (60, 50): boundary at T = 60 is 30, so it is unattainable now [1].
**(d)** The whole boundary shifts outwards [1], showing economic growth, a rise in productive capacity [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Distinguish between inside (possible but inefficient) and outside (unattainable).

**8.** Moving from T = 20 to 40 costs 0.9 bolts per tractor, below the value of 1.2 [1], so it is worthwhile [1]. Moving from 40 to 60 costs 1.8, above 1.2 [1], so it is not [1]. The allocatively efficient point is **(T = 40, B = 66)**. The other boundary points are also productively efficient, so productive efficiency alone cannot identify the best mix [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare opportunity cost with society's valuation at each step. Naming the point with no comparison earns little.

**9.** **(a)** At 18: X = 180 + 100 = 280 and Y = 72 + 250 = 322, so X is cheaper [1]. At 32: X = 320 + 100 = 420 and Y = 128 + 250 = 378, so Y is cheaper [1]. Each gap is 42, or 0.21 a unit [1].
**(b)** 10w + 100 = 4w + 250 [1], so 6w = 150 and **w = 25** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the cost equations out. Quote totals and the per-unit difference to support the conclusion.

**10.** A planned system can direct resources to social goals [1] and avoid some market failures [1]. But planners lack the full information that prices supply, causing shortages or surpluses [1], and weak incentives can reduce effort and innovation [1]. A free market responds to wants through prices and profit [1] and rewards efficient firms [1]. But it ignores those without income [1] and may under-provide public goods [1]. Evaluation: most economies are mixed, so what matters is the extent and form of intervention. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Weigh both systems against a criterion such as efficiency or fairness. A list of pros and cons without a judgement is weaker.

**11.** **(a)** Mean = 750 ÷ 5 = **150** [1]; median = **50** [1]; richest share = 500 ÷ 750 = **66.7%** [1].
**(b)** Red and Blue have the same mean [1], but in Blue four of five people receive less than the mean and three receive only one-third of it [1], so welfare depends on how output is distributed as well as its total [1].
**(c)** The statement is normative, a value judgement [1]. People weigh equality and efficiency differently [1]. They may also disagree on positive questions, such as whether redistribution reduces incentives [1]. Evidence can inform the debate but cannot settle it [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Separate disagreement over facts from disagreement over values. That distinction is the point of the question.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing opportunity cost as "money spent" rather than the best option rejected.
- Calculating an opportunity cost the wrong way up (bowls per vase instead of vases per bowl).
- Describing a point inside the boundary as unattainable.
- Calling any boundary point allocatively efficient without comparing cost with value.
- Forgetting that growth in a renewable stock depends on the opening stock.
- Classifying a prediction as normative because it might be wrong.
- Giving one-sided answers to questions asking for assessment or evaluation.
- Leaving out units, or the good in which an opportunity cost is measured.
