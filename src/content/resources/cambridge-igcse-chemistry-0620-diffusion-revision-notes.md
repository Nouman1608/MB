These notes cover Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 sub-topic **1.2 Diffusion** (2026–2028). Points marked *(Extended only)* come from the Supplement. The full explanation and worked examples are in the [diffusion study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-diffusion/); questions are in the [diffusion practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-diffusion-practice/).

## Definition to learn word for word

**Diffusion** is the net movement of particles from a region of **higher concentration** to a region of **lower concentration**, as a result of their **random motion**.

Check any definition you write for those three highlighted ideas.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Kinetic particle theory | All matter is made of particles in constant, random motion |
| Random motion | Movement in unpredictable directions, changed by collisions |
| Concentration | How many particles there are in a given volume |
| Net movement | The overall movement once movements in all directions are added up |
| Relative molecular mass, Mᵣ | Sum of the relative atomic masses of the atoms in a molecule |
| Rate of diffusion | How quickly particles spread out |

## The particle explanation in five steps

1. Particles are in constant random motion.
2. They collide with each other and change direction.
3. Where there are more particles, more of them move away into the less crowded region than come back.
4. So there is a net movement from higher to lower concentration.
5. When the particles are evenly spread, movements balance and net movement stops; the particles keep moving.

## Gases, liquids and solids

| | Diffusion? | Speed | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gas | Yes | Fast | Particles fast and far apart |
| Liquid | Yes | Slow | Particles slower and close together, colliding very often |
| Solid | No (at this level) | — | Particles only vibrate about fixed positions |

## Demonstrations

- **Coloured crystal in still water** (for example potassium manganate(VII)): purple colour spreads through the water over hours with no stirring.
- **Bromine vapour and air**: brown colour spreads upwards into the air jar, against gravity; done in a fume cupboard.
- **Ammonia and hydrogen chloride tube**: white ring of ammonium chloride forms nearer the hydrochloric acid end.

NH₃(g) + HCl(g) → NH₄Cl(s)

## Relative molecular mass and rate *(Extended only)*

- **Effect**: the lower the Mᵣ, the faster the gas diffuses.
- **Reason**: at the same temperature, all gas particles have the same average kinetic energy, so lighter particles move faster.

Useful Mᵣ values (using H = 1, He = 4, C = 12, N = 14, O = 16, S = 32, Cl = 35.5):

| Gas | Mᵣ |
|---|---|
| H₂ | 2 |
| He | 4 |
| CH₄ | 16 |
| NH₃ | 17 |
| N₂ | 28 |
| O₂ | 32 |
| HCl | 36.5 |
| CO₂ | 44 |
| SO₂ | 64 |
| Cl₂ | 71 |

Fastest at the top, slowest at the bottom.

**Tube method**: whichever gas has the lower Mᵣ travels further, so the product forms **nearer the end of the heavier gas**.

## Temperature

Diffusion is an example of kinetic particle theory in action, so the same particle reasoning that explains gas pressure and changes of state also explains why smells and colours spread.

Higher temperature → particles have more kinetic energy → move faster → faster diffusion.

## Model answer: the tube experiment *(Extended only)*

Question: explain why the white ring forms nearer the hydrochloric acid end.

- Ammonia has Mᵣ 17 and hydrogen chloride has Mᵣ 36.5. **(states data)**
- Ammonia particles are lighter, so at the same temperature they move faster. **(explains)**
- Ammonia travels further along the tube in the same time, so the gases meet nearer the hydrochloric acid end. **(links to observation)**

## Exam traps

- Forgetting "random motion" or "net" in the definition.
- Writing "from low to high concentration".
- Saying particles stop moving when diffusion is complete.
- Explaining the ring position by reactivity or density rather than Mᵣ and speed.
- Describing the Mᵣ effect without explaining it (Extended answers need both).
- Calculating Mᵣ with wrong atomic masses, such as O = 8 (that is oxygen's proton number).
- Claiming diffusion happens through a solid at IGCSE.

## Self-test

1. Define diffusion.
2. Why does a smell spread through a room without any air currents?
3. Why is diffusion slower in a liquid than in a gas?
4. Why does diffusion not happen in a solid at this level?
5. When a purple crystal has fully dissolved and the colour is even, have the particles stopped moving?
6. Why is the bromine demonstration done in a fume cupboard?
7. *(Extended only)* Calculate the Mᵣ of chlorine, Cl₂.
8. *(Extended only)* Which diffuses faster at the same temperature, neon (Mᵣ 20) or argon (Mᵣ 40)? Explain.
9. *(Extended only)* Rank N₂, CO₂ and He from fastest to slowest diffusion.
10. *(Extended only)* In the ammonia and hydrogen chloride tube, which end is the white ring nearer, and why?
11. State the effect of raising the temperature on the rate of diffusion.
12. Name the white solid formed in the tube experiment.

**Answers**

1. The net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration, as a result of their random motion.
2. The gas particles move randomly and spread from where they are concentrated to the rest of the room.
3. Liquid particles move more slowly and are close together, so they collide very often and travel only short distances.
4. The particles only vibrate about fixed positions and cannot move through the solid.
5. No; they are still moving randomly, but there is no net movement.
6. Bromine is toxic.
7. 2 × 35.5 = 71.
8. Neon, because its lighter particles move faster at the same temperature.
9. He (4), N₂ (28), CO₂ (44).
10. Nearer the hydrochloric acid end, because ammonia (Mᵣ 17) is lighter than hydrogen chloride (Mᵣ 36.5), so it moves faster and travels further.
11. The rate increases.
12. Ammonium chloride.
