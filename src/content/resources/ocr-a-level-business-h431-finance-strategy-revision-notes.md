## What the specification lists

- Evaluate the impact and importance of a financial strategy for a business and its stakeholders.
- Recommend and justify a financial strategy for a business.

## Core recall

### Meaning

- **Strategy:** a long-term plan to achieve objectives.
- **Finance strategy:** a long-term plan for how the business will raise, use and control its money to achieve its objectives.
- It looks years ahead; routine finance tasks are not strategy.

### Components

| Component | Key question |
|---|---|
| Finance objectives | What returns, margins, cash and gearing levels? |
| Funding | Which mix of sources? |
| Investment | Which projects, appraised how? |
| Budgeting and control | How are plans made and monitored? |
| Cash and working capital | How is liquidity kept? |
| Dividend policy | How much is paid out or retained? |
| Risk management | How are interest, exchange and bad debt risks handled? |
| Reporting and compliance | How are duties met and stakeholders told? |

### Fit

- Supports business objectives (growth needs funds; stability suggests low gearing).
- Parts are consistent (growth with high dividends contradicts itself).
- Suits size, legal form and the external environment.

### Debt against shares: the trade-off

| | More debt | More shares |
|---|---|---|
| Earnings per share | Usually higher if the project succeeds | Diluted by more shares |
| Gearing and interest cover | Gearing up, cover down | Gearing down, cover up |
| Control | Kept | Shared |
| Downside | Fixed interest in bad years; may break lender covenants | Lower risk; permanent claim on dividends |

### Impact

- Secures funds at acceptable cost.
- Controls risk.
- Improves returns through investment choice.
- Gives control and early warning.
- Builds lender and investor confidence.

### Importance

- Greatest when growing, highly geared, in volatile markets or short of cash.
- Lower for small stable businesses with no borrowing.
- Only as good as its assumptions; review regularly.

### Recommend and justify: five steps

1. Diagnose objectives and position.
2. Choose funding, investment, controls, dividend policy.
3. Justify with figures.
4. Weigh risk and an alternative.
5. Judge and say what would change the view.

## Quick worked reminder

Stanton Leisure (invented): 400,000 shares, equity £2,000,000, 6% loan £800,000, operating profit £450,000, tax 20%. Three new gyms cost £1,200,000 and add £210,000 profit; £300,000 comes from retained profit. Strategy A borrows £900,000 at 7%: EPS 109.8p, gearing 45.9%, interest cover 5.9. Strategy B borrows £500,000 and issues 80,000 shares at £5: EPS 96.2p, gearing 35.1%, interest cover 8.0. With a covenant limiting gearing to 40%, B is the safer choice.

## Calculation reminders for funding comparisons

- Total interest = existing interest + rate × new loan.
- Profit before tax = operating profit − total interest.
- Profit after tax = profit before tax × (1 − tax rate).
- Earnings per share = profit after tax ÷ shares in issue (including any new shares).
- Gearing = non-current liabilities ÷ (equity + non-current liabilities) × 100; a share issue raises equity, a loan raises non-current liabilities.
- Interest cover = operating profit ÷ total interest.
- Existing owners' share after a share issue = old shares ÷ new total shares.
- Test the downside: recalculate with a lower profit from the project to see which option is riskier.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Finance strategy | A long-term plan for raising, using and controlling money |
| Dividend policy | The decision on how much profit to pay out |
| Covenant | A condition in a loan, such as a limit on gearing |
| Dilution | A fall in each owner's share when new shares are issued |
| Gearing | Non-current liabilities as a share of capital employed |
| Interest cover | Operating profit divided by interest |
| Earnings per share | Profit for the year divided by the number of shares |
| Retained profit | Profit kept in the business |
| Financial risk | The chance that debts cannot be met |

## Exam traps

- A strategy is long term and linked to objectives.
- Support a funding recommendation with gearing, interest cover or EPS.
- Always consider risk and control.
- Higher EPS is not a good reason alone to borrow more.
- A strategy needs review; say what would trigger it.

## Self-test

1. Define a finance strategy.
2. Give three components.
3. Why should a strategy fit the business objectives?
4. Profit after tax is £461,600 and there are 480,000 shares. Calculate EPS.
5. Non-current liabilities £1,300,000; capital employed £3,700,000. Calculate gearing.
6. Operating profit £660,000; interest £83,000. Calculate interest cover.
7. Give one benefit of funding by a share issue.
8. Give one benefit of funding by a loan.
9. What is a covenant?
10. Give one reason why a small, stable business may not need a formal strategy.
11. Give one stakeholder and how a poor strategy harms them.
12. Give one reason a strategy needs regular review.

### Answers

1. A long-term plan for how a business raises, uses and controls its money to achieve its objectives.
2. Any three: objectives, funding, investment, budgeting and control, cash and working capital, dividend policy, risk management, reporting.
3. So that funding and spending support what the business is trying to achieve, and the parts do not contradict each other.
4. £461,600 ÷ 480,000 × 100 = **96.2p**.
5. 1,300,000 ÷ 3,700,000 × 100 = **35.1%**.
6. 660,000 ÷ 83,000 = **8.0 times** (7.95).
7. No fixed interest or repayment, and gearing stays lower.
8. Existing owners keep control, and interest is a cost that reduces tax.
9. A condition in a loan agreement, for example a limit on gearing.
10. With little borrowing and simple, stable operations, an informal approach may be enough.
11. Lenders (missed repayments), employees (job losses), suppliers (unpaid bills), or owners (lost investment).
12. Forecasts and external factors such as interest rates change, so plans may become out of date.
