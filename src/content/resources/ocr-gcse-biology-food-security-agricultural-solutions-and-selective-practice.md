## Questions

**1.** State two biological factors that can reduce food security. **[2]**

**2.** A field without pesticide loses 18% of its potential yield to insect pests. The actual yield is 8.5 tonnes per hectare. Calculate the potential yield. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**3.** A field produces crops containing 80 000 kJ of energy. One person needs 10 000 kJ per day (supplied value). Assume 10% of the crop energy is passed to chickens when they eat the crop.

**(a)** For how many person-days could the crop provide energy if people ate it directly? **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy available from the chickens, and the number of person-days it supplies. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why rising meat consumption in wealthier populations reduces food security. **[1]**

**4.** Explain how fertiliser washed from a field into a lake can cause fish to die. **[4]**

**5.** A farmer trials phosphate-rich fertiliser on potatoes.

| Fertiliser (kg per hectare) | 0 | 40 | 80 | 120 | 160 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yield (tonnes per hectare) | 21 | 30 | 36 | 38 | 38.5 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in yield from 0 to 80 kg per hectare. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest which rate the farmer should use. Give two reasons. **[2]**

**6.** Five hydroponic plants have masses of 212, 198, 225, 205 and 210 g. The mean mass of soil-grown plants is 171 g.

**(a)** Calculate the mean mass of the hydroponic plants. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how much greater this mean is than the soil mean, as a percentage of the soil mean. **[2]**
**(c)** State one disadvantage of hydroponics. **[1]**
**(d)** Give one reason why the conclusion from this data should be treated with caution. **[1]**

**7.** Ladybirds were released onto some bean plants to control aphids. The table shows mean aphids per plant.

| Week | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No ladybirds | 20 | 95 | 310 | 640 | 820 |
| Ladybirds released | 20 | 60 | 120 | 45 | 30 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage fewer aphids at week 8 on plants with ladybirds. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the fall in aphids between weeks 4 and 8 on plants with ladybirds. **[2]**
**(c)** Give one advantage of this method over spraying insecticide. **[1]**
**(d)** Give one disadvantage of this method. **[1]**

**8.** Describe how a plant breeder could produce tomato plants with large fruits that also resist a fungal disease. **[4]**

**9.** Hens were selectively bred for six generations. Mean egg production rose from 190 to 310 eggs per hen per year.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean increase per generation. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest how the breeder could show that the increase was caused by the hens' genes, not their feed. **[1]**

**10.** A farmer grows a single high-yield wheat variety bred from only a few parent plants. A new fungal disease reaches the area.

**(a)** Explain why the whole crop might be lost. **[4]**
**(b)** Suggest two ways to reduce this risk in the future. **[2]**

**11.** A growing country wants more food from limited land. Evaluate the use of fertilisers, pesticides, hydroponics and biological control. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two from: increasing population; new pests or pathogens; changing diets (more meat); environmental change such as drought; cost or sustainability of inputs. First factor [1]; second factor [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Two distinct factors are needed, so "more people" and "population growth" count as one idea, not two.

**2.** Actual yield is 100 - 18 = 82% of potential [1]. Potential = 8.5 / 0.82 [1] = **10.4 tonnes per hectare** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by 0.82 to find the whole; multiplying 8.5 by 1.18 uses the wrong base, a common slip.

**3.** **(a)** 80 000 / 10 000 = **8 person-days** [1].
**(b)** 80 000 × 0.10 = 8 000 kJ [1]; 8 000 / 10 000 = **0.8 person-days** [1].
**(c)** Energy is lost between trophic levels, so animals need much more crop than people would, and more land is needed per person fed [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers on diet gain more credit when they link to energy loss between feeding levels, not only to "more land".

**4.** Fertiliser (nitrates and phosphates) washes into the lake in run-off [1]. Algae grow rapidly, forming a bloom [1]. The bloom blocks light, so plants below die and decomposers respire aerobically, using up oxygen [1]. With too little oxygen, fish die [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming oxygen use by decomposers is what separates a full explanation from "the algae poison the fish".

**5.** **(a)** (36 - 21) / 21 [1] × 100 = **71.4%** [1].
**(b)** 80 kg per hectare [1]. Above 80 the extra yield is small (2 then 0.5 tonnes), so more fertiliser costs more than it earns and raises run-off [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote figures from the table when justifying a choice; "it gives the best yield" alone ignores cost.

**6.** **(a)** (212 + 198 + 225 + 205 + 210) / 5 = **210 g** [1].
**(b)** (210 - 171) / 171 [1] × 100 = **22.8%** [1].
**(c)** Any one: high set-up or energy cost; needs skilled management; disease spreads in the solution; power failure [1].
**(d)** Only five plants were measured, so the mean may not represent all plants [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the soil mean, as the question asks; dividing by 210 gives a different answer.

**7.** **(a)** (820 - 30) / 820 [1] × 100 = **96.3%** [1].
**(b)** Ladybirds feed on aphids [1], and ladybird numbers had risen, so aphids were eaten faster than they reproduced [1].
**(c)** No chemical residues, or specific to the pest, or no resistance [1].
**(d)** Slow to act, or does not remove the pest completely, or needs suitable conditions [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In an explanation, give the cause (predation) and the effect on numbers, and do not just repeat that aphids fell.

**8.** Choose parent plants with large fruit and parents with resistance [1]. Breed them together [1]. Select offspring with large fruit and resistance, and breed those [1]. Repeat over many generations [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Describe repetition over generations; a single cross rarely produces the finished variety.

**9.** **(a)** (310 - 190) / 190 [1] × 100 = **63.2%** [1].
**(b)** 120 / 6 = **20 eggs per generation** [1].
**(c)** Keep all generations on the same diet and housing [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A control question needs one named variable held constant, not "do a fair test".

**10.** **(a)** Breeding from few parents reduces genetic variation [1]. The plants are genetically very similar [1]. If none carries an allele for resistance, all are susceptible [1]. The disease spreads through the whole crop [1].
**(b)** Any two from: keep seed banks or wild relatives to retain alleles; grow several varieties; breed in resistance from other varieties. First way [1]; second way [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the loss directly to missing alleles, not just "weak plants".

**11.** Fertilisers replace removed ions and raise yield [1]; run-off causes eutrophication [1]. Pesticides protect yield from pests, weeds and fungi [1]; they harm non-target species and resistance evolves [1]. Hydroponics gives exact control, saves land and water [1]; it needs high energy cost and skill [1]. Biological control leaves no residues and is specific [1], but is slow and incomplete. A judgement with a reason, such as using integrated methods suited to the farm's money and land [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation answers tend to gain more when each method has both a benefit and a limit, followed by a reasoned conclusion.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying fertiliser is "plant food" instead of a source of mineral ions.
- Missing the oxygen step in eutrophication.
- Using the wrong base in percentage change questions.
- Forgetting units such as kJ, grams or tonnes per hectare.
- Writing "expensive" without saying what costs.
- Saying pests become "immune" rather than describing survival of resistant individuals.
- Describing selective breeding as one cross instead of repeated selection.
- Reading only one line of a table or graph.
