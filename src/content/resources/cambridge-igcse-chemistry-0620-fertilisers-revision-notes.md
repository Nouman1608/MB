**Syllabus:** Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620, 2026–2028, subtopic 10.2 Fertilisers.

## The two syllabus statements

1. **Ammonium salts** and **nitrates** are used as fertilisers.
2. **NPK fertilisers** provide the elements **nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium** for **improved plant growth**.

## Why fertilisers are used

- Plants absorb N, P and K from the soil (dissolved in soil water).
- Harvesting removes them from the field.
- Fertilisers replace them, so crops grow better.

## Reading a fertiliser formula

| Ion in the formula | Element provided |
|---|---|
| NH₄⁺ (ammonium) | N |
| NO₃⁻ (nitrate) | N |
| PO₄³⁻ (phosphate) | P |
| K⁺ (potassium) | K |

| Salt | Formula | Provides |
|---|---|---|
| ammonium nitrate | NH₄NO₃ | N |
| ammonium sulfate | (NH₄)₂SO₄ | N |
| ammonium phosphate | (NH₄)₃PO₄ | N, P |
| potassium nitrate | KNO₃ | K, N |
| potassium chloride | KCl | K |

**NPK fertiliser:** a mixture of salts that provides all three, for example ammonium phosphate + potassium chloride.

**Choosing a mixture:** match the salts to the elements the soil lacks. A soil already rich in nitrogen needs a phosphorus salt and a potassium salt, not more ammonium nitrate. Any extra element supplied is not harmful to the crop, but unused fertiliser can be washed away.

## Solubility link

- Fertilisers must be **soluble** so plants can absorb them.
- All ammonium salts, all potassium salts and all nitrates are soluble.

## Making a fertiliser salt

- Neutralise an alkali with an acid: 2NH₃ + H₂SO₄ → (NH₄)₂SO₄.
- Both reactants soluble → **titration** method, then crystallise.
- Ammonium nitrate: ammonia + nitric acid. Potassium nitrate: potassium hydroxide + nitric acid.

## Ammonium fertiliser + base

- Bases react with ammonium salts to release **ammonia gas**.
- (NH₄)₂SO₄ + Ca(OH)₂ → CaSO₄ + 2NH₃ + 2H₂O
- So do not add lime and an ammonium fertiliser together: nitrogen is lost.

## Testing a fertiliser

| Ion | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| NH₄⁺ | warm with NaOH(aq) | ammonia: damp red litmus turns blue |
| NO₃⁻ | warm with NaOH(aq) and aluminium foil | ammonia: damp red litmus turns blue |
| SO₄²⁻ | dilute nitric acid, then barium nitrate(aq) | white precipitate |

## Percentage of nitrogen *(Extended)*

% N = (number of N atoms × 14) ÷ Mr × 100

| Salt | Mr | % N |
|---|---|---|
| NH₄NO₃ | 80 | 35.0 |
| (NH₄)₂SO₄ | 132 | 21.2 |
| KNO₃ | 101 | 13.9 |

## Answer frames

**"Which elements does this fertiliser provide?":** read each ion. "KNO₃ contains K⁺, so potassium, and NO₃⁻, so nitrogen."

**"Suggest a mixture for a soil short of P and K" (2–3 marks):** choose one salt for each missing element and name which element each one supplies.

**"Explain why the farmer should not add lime and an ammonium fertiliser together" (2 marks):** lime is a base that reacts with ammonium salts; ammonia gas is given off, so nitrogen is lost.

**"Describe the use of NPK fertilisers" (2 marks):** they provide nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium; for improved plant growth.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fertiliser | a substance added to soil to provide elements for plant growth |
| NPK fertiliser | a fertiliser providing nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium |
| Ammonium salt | a salt containing the NH₄⁺ ion |
| Nitrate | a salt containing the NO₃⁻ ion |
| Neutralisation | the reaction of an acid with a base (or alkali) to form a salt and water |
| Titration | adding one solution to another in measured volumes to find the exact neutralisation point |

## Exam traps

- **Elements, not pure elements.** N, P and K are supplied as ions in compounds.
- **Two nitrogen families:** ammonium salts **and** nitrates.
- **Spot the second element:** KNO₃ gives K and N; (NH₄)₃PO₄ gives N and P.
- **Brackets matter:** (NH₄)₂SO₄ has two ammonium ions.
- **Lime + ammonium salt = ammonia lost.**
- *(Extended)* **Count every N:** NH₄NO₃ has two.
- **Excess fertiliser** washes into rivers (link to 10.1: deoxygenation and damage to aquatic life).

## Self-test

1. Name the **two** types of salt used as nitrogen fertilisers.
2. What do the letters N, P and K stand for?
3. State why farmers add fertilisers to fields used for crops.
4. Name the elements provided by potassium nitrate.
5. Name a salt that provides both nitrogen and phosphorus.
6. Explain why fertiliser salts need to be soluble.
7. Name the acid and alkali used to make ammonium sulfate.
8. Why is the titration method used to make ammonium sulfate?
9. Explain why lime should not be spread at the same time as ammonium nitrate.
10. Describe the test for the ammonium ion.
11. *(Extended)* Calculate the percentage of nitrogen in ammonium nitrate.
12. Give one harmful effect of fertiliser washed into rivers.

**Answers:**

1. Ammonium salts and nitrates.
2. Nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium.
3. To replace the elements removed by harvested crops, so plants grow better.
4. Potassium and nitrogen.
5. Ammonium phosphate.
6. Plants absorb elements dissolved in soil water through their roots.
7. Sulfuric acid and ammonia (aqueous).
8. Both reactants are soluble, so excess cannot be filtered off; exact volumes must be found.
9. Lime is a base; it reacts with the ammonium salt to release ammonia gas, so nitrogen is lost.
10. Warm with aqueous sodium hydroxide; ammonia is given off, turning damp red litmus paper blue.
11. Mr = 80; % N = 28 ÷ 80 × 100 = 35.0 %.
12. Deoxygenation of the water, damaging aquatic life.
