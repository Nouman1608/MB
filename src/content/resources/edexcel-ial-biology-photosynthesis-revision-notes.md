## 5.1 and 5.2 Overall reaction and ATP

| Item | Recall |
|---|---|
| Equation | 6CO₂ + 12H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ + 6H₂O |
| Role of light | Energy to split the strong bonds in water |
| Hydrogen | Stored in glucose by combining it with CO₂ |
| Oxygen | From water, released to the atmosphere |
| Photophosphorylation | ADP + Pi → ATP, energy from light |
| ATP hydrolysis | ATP + H₂O → ADP + Pi, immediate energy for processes |

Remember: ATP is the immediate energy currency. Glucose is the stored fuel.

## 5.3 Light-dependent reactions

Site: thylakoid membranes. Needs: light, water, ADP + Pi, NADP.

Method in steps (non-cyclic):

1. Photon absorbed, electron in chlorophyll excited and leaves PSII.
2. Electrons pass down the electron transport chain, losing energy.
3. Energy pumps H⁺ into the thylakoid space; H⁺ returns through ATP synthase, making ATP (chemiosmosis).
4. PSI excited; electrons reduce NADP.
5. PSII refilled from photolysis: 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂.

| | Non-cyclic | Cyclic |
|---|---|---|
| Photosystem | PSII + PSI | PSI |
| Photolysis | Yes | No |
| ATP | Yes | Yes |
| Reduced NADP | Yes | No |
| O₂ | Yes | No |

Why cyclic exists: extra ATP without extra reduced NADP, so the supply can be balanced with demand.

## 5.4 Light-independent reactions

Site: stroma. Needs: CO₂, ATP, reduced NADP, enzymes.

| Compound | Carbons | Role |
|---|---|---|
| RuBP | 5 | CO₂ acceptor |
| RUBISCO | enzyme | Fixes CO₂ to RuBP |
| GP | 3 | First stable product; reduced to GALP |
| GALP | 3 | Used to regenerate RuBP or to make carbohydrate and other molecules |

Method in steps:

1. CO₂ + RuBP → unstable 6C → 2 GP (RUBISCO).
2. GP → GALP (uses ATP and reduced NADP).
3. Most GALP → RuBP (uses ATP).
4. A fraction of GALP leaves the cycle.

Totals for one glucose: 6 turns, 6 CO₂, 18 ATP, 12 reduced NADP. Per CO₂: 3 ATP and 2 reduced NADP.

Worked reminder: 7.5 µmol CO₂ fixed needs 7.5 × 3 = 22.5 µmol ATP and 7.5 × 2 = 15 µmol reduced NADP.

Light off: GP up, RuBP down. CO₂ off: RuBP up, GP down.

### 5.4 (ii) Uses of the products

| Product of photosynthesis | Made into |
|---|---|
| Simple sugars | Respiration (all organisms that obtain them) |
| Glucose units | Starch, cellulose and other polysaccharides |
| Carbon skeletons plus nitrate | Amino acids, then proteins |
| GALP-derived compounds | Lipids |
| Sugars and phosphate with nitrogenous bases | Nucleic acids |

## 5.5 Chloroplast structure

| Structure | Function |
|---|---|
| Envelope (two membranes) | Selective barrier |
| Grana and thylakoids | Large surface area for photosystems, carriers, ATP synthase |
| Intergranal lamellae | Connect grana |
| Thylakoid space | Small, so H⁺ gradient forms quickly |
| Stroma | Calvin cycle enzymes, starch grains |
| Own DNA and ribosomes | Make some chloroplast proteins |

Tip: link every structure to a function with a "so that" statement.

## 5.6 Spectra

| Term | Axes | Shows |
|---|---|---|
| Absorption spectrum | Absorbance against wavelength | Which wavelengths a pigment absorbs |
| Action spectrum | Rate of photosynthesis against wavelength | Which wavelengths drive photosynthesis |

Chlorophylls absorb mainly blue and red and reflect green. Accessory pigments widen the range of wavelengths used. The two spectra have similar shapes. A difference means that not all absorbed light is used effectively.

## 5.7 Chromatography

Rf = origin-to-spot distance ÷ origin-to-front distance.

Method in steps:

1. Pencil line (origin) on the strip.
2. Add concentrated pigment extract as a small spot and let it dry.
3. Stand the strip in solvent below the origin and let it rise.
4. Mark the solvent front at once.
5. Measure to the middle of each spot, calculate Rf, then compare with reference values from the same solvent.

Worked reminder: front 7.5 cm, spot at 6.0 cm gives Rf = 6.0 ÷ 7.5 = 0.80.

A pigment with a higher Rf is more soluble in the solvent and is held less firmly by the paper.

## 5.8 Core Practical 10

| Variable | Independent | Method | Controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light intensity | Lamp distance | Ruler | Temperature, CO₂ |
| Wavelength | Filter colour | Filters over lamp | Intensity, temperature |
| Temperature | Water bath | Thermometer | Light, CO₂ |
| CO₂ | Hydrogencarbonate concentration | Dilution series | Light, temperature |

Dependent variable: bubbles per minute, or gas volume per minute.

Reminders:

- Intensity ∝ 1 ÷ d². Lamp from 40 cm to 20 cm: (40 ÷ 20)² = 4 times.
- Gas in a capillary: V = πr²l.
- Use a heat shield or LED lamp and allow time for the plant to adjust.
- Repeat and calculate a mean.
- Rate becomes steady when another factor is limiting. Identify it from conditions.

## Worked reminders for calculations

Rate from counts. Bubbles in 3 minutes: 33, 30, 36. Mean = 33. Rate = 33 ÷ 3 = 11 bubbles per minute.

Percentage change. A rate rises from 8.0 to 10.4 bubbles per minute. Change = 2.4. Percentage increase = 2.4 ÷ 8.0 × 100 = 30%.

Gas volume. A bubble 8.0 mm long in a capillary of radius 0.5 mm: V = π × 0.5² × 8.0 = 6.3 mm³. If it forms in 4 minutes, the rate is 6.3 ÷ 4 = 1.6 mm³ per minute.

Dilution. To make 40 cm³ of 0.02 mol dm⁻³ hydrogencarbonate from 0.10 mol dm⁻³ stock: volume of stock = 0.02 × 40 ÷ 0.10 = 8 cm³, plus 32 cm³ of water.

Cost of fixing carbon. A turn of the cycle that fixes 1 CO₂ uses 3 ATP. For 9 CO₂: 27 ATP and 18 reduced NADP.

## Answer frameworks

Explain how ATP is made in the light-dependent reactions: photon absorbed, electron excited, electron passes along the chain, energy released, H⁺ pumped into the thylakoid space, gradient forms, H⁺ flows through ATP synthase, ADP + Pi joined.

Explain a change in GP or RuBP: say what has changed in the supply of ATP, reduced NADP or CO₂, say which step is affected, and then say which compound builds up and which runs out.

Explain a limiting factor graph: where the curve climbs, the factor being varied is the one in short supply, so more of it raises the rate. In the flat part, something else is limiting; name the likeliest factor from the conditions given in the question. A fall at high temperature is usually enzyme denaturation, including RUBISCO.

Link structure to function: name the structure, state the feature, then say what that makes possible. For example, thylakoid membranes have a large surface area, so many photosystems and ATP synthase molecules fit in.

Compare cyclic and non-cyclic: always give a similarity and differences, with both columns of the table complete.

## Must-know distinctions

- Absorption spectrum against action spectrum.
- Photophosphorylation (making ATP using light) against ATP hydrolysis (releasing energy).
- Light-dependent (thylakoids, makes ATP and reduced NADP) against light-independent (stroma, uses them).
- GP (first product) against GALP (reduced product).

## Quick self-test

1. State the source of the oxygen released in photosynthesis.
2. Name the process by which water is split in the light-dependent reactions.
3. Which photosystem is used in cyclic photophosphorylation?
4. Name the enzyme that combines CO₂ with RuBP.
5. A leaf disc fixes 12 µmol CO₂. How much ATP is needed?
6. Define Rf and work it out for a spot 3.6 cm from the origin with a solvent front at 9.0 cm.
7. A lamp is moved from 50 cm to 25 cm. How many times greater is the light intensity?
8. What happens to the amount of GP when the light is switched off, and why?
9. Why is the thylakoid space small?
10. Define action spectrum.

### Answers

1. Water (photolysis).
2. Photolysis.
3. Photosystem I (PSI).
4. RUBISCO.
5. 12 × 3 = 36 µmol ATP.
6. Rf is the origin-to-spot distance divided by the origin-to-front distance; 3.6 ÷ 9.0 = 0.40.
7. (50 ÷ 25)² = 4 times.
8. GP rises. ATP and reduced NADP fall, so GP cannot be reduced to GALP, but CO₂ is still being fixed.
9. So that the H⁺ concentration inside rises quickly, making a steeper gradient for ATP synthase.
10. A graph of rate of photosynthesis against wavelength of light.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that CO₂ is split to give oxygen.
- Saying "light energy is used to make ATP" without mentioning excited electrons and the electron transport chain.
- Leaving out reduced NADP as a product of the non-cyclic route, or including it for the cyclic route.
- Writing "light-independent reactions need no light" with no mention that they need the products of the light-dependent stage.
- Giving only a name for a chloroplast structure and no function.
- Describing an action spectrum as the amount of light absorbed.
- Rf given with units, or as a value above 1, or measured to the edge of the spot.
- In Core Practical 10, changing more than one variable, or naming the control variables without saying how they are kept constant.
- Describing a plateau in a graph without naming the limiting factor.
