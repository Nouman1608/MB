## Section A — Multiple choice (topic sample)

**1.** What is the relative charge of a neutron?
A) +1
B) −1
C) 0
D) 2 **[1]**

**2.** Chlorine has two isotopes, ³⁵Cl and ³⁷Cl. Which statement correctly explains why the relative atomic mass of chlorine (35.5) is not a whole number?
A) The mass number of an atom is always non-integer
B) It is the weighted mean of the isotope masses, weighted by their relative abundance
C) Neutrons have a slightly different mass to protons in this element
D) The value includes the mass of the electrons, which reduces the whole-number total **[1]**

**3.** Which statement correctly explains why reactivity increases down Group 1?
A) The atoms become smaller, so gain electrons more readily
B) The number of protons decreases down the group, reducing nuclear attraction
C) The outer electron is more strongly attracted to the nucleus, so is lost more easily
D) The outer electron is further from the nucleus with more shielding, so is lost more easily **[1]**

**4.** Which statement correctly explains the trend in reactivity down Group 7?
A) Reactivity decreases, because an incoming electron is attracted less strongly as shielding increases and the outer shell is further from the nucleus
B) Reactivity increases, because the outer shell is further from the nucleus and gains an electron more easily
C) Reactivity increases, because the atoms become smaller
D) Reactivity decreases, because the outer electron is lost more easily further down the group **[1]**

**5.** An atom has the electron configuration 2,8,3. Which group and period is it in?
A) Group 8, Period 3
B) Group 2, Period 8
C) Group 3, Period 3
D) Group 3, Period 2 **[1]**

## Answers

**1. C)** A neutron carries no charge; it is protons that carry +1 and electrons that carry −1 [1].

**2. B)** Relative atomic mass is the weighted mean of the masses of an element's isotopes, weighted by their relative abundance — it is not a simple average of the mass numbers, which is why it need not be a whole number [1].

**3. D)** Down Group 1 the outer electron is in a shell further from the nucleus with more shielding from inner shells, so it is attracted less strongly and lost more easily, making the atom more reactive [1].

**4. A)** Down Group 7 a halogen must gain an electron to react; as the outer shell gets further from the nucleus and shielding increases, an incoming electron is attracted less strongly, so reactivity decreases [1].

**5. C)** The number of outer-shell electrons (3) gives the group number, and the number of occupied shells (2,8,3 = three shells) gives the period number [1].

**6.** Proton: mass 1, charge **+1** [1]. Neutron: mass 1, charge **0** [1]. Electron: mass **very small (1/1836)**, charge **−1** [1].

**7.** Chlorine exists as **two isotopes** (Cl-35 and Cl-37) [1], and the relative atomic mass is the **weighted average** of their masses allowing for abundance [1].

**8.** They have the **same number of electrons in their outer shell** [1], and it is the outer electrons that determine chemical behaviour [1].

**9. (a)** **Most passed straight through** [1] — the atom is mostly **empty space** [1].
**Some were deflected** [1] — the nucleus is **positively charged** (repelling the positive alpha particles) [1].
**A very few bounced back** [1] — the nucleus is **very small but contains most of the mass** [1].

**(b)** The plum pudding model predicted **all particles would pass through with little deflection** [1], because charge and mass were spread evenly — the observations could not be explained by it [1].

**10. (a)** sodium + water → sodium hydroxide + hydrogen [1].

**(b)** Reactivity **increases** down the group [1].

**(c)** The outer electron is in a shell **further from the nucleus** [1]; there is **more shielding** by inner shells [1]; so the **attraction between the nucleus and the outer electron is weaker** and the electron is lost more easily [1].
*All three parts are required — most answers give only one.*

**11. (a)** Reactivity **decreases** down Group 7 [1].

**(b)** A halogen must **gain** an electron rather than lose one [1]. Further down the group the outer shell is **further from the nucleus with more shielding** [1], so an incoming electron is **attracted less strongly** and is gained less readily [1].

**(c)** The solution turns **orange/brown** [1].
chlorine + potassium bromide → potassium chloride + bromine [1].

**12.** They have **full outer electron shells** [1], so they have **no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons** [1].
*Not "because they are gases".*

**13. Group 2** — the number of outer-shell electrons (2) [1]; **Period 3** — the number of occupied shells (2,8,2 = three shells) [1].

**14.** Melting and boiling points **increase** down Group 7, because the molecules get larger and **intermolecular forces strengthen** [1]. Reactivity instead **decreases**, because a halogen must **gain** an electron, and further down the group the outer shell is further from the nucleus with more shielding, so an incoming electron is attracted less strongly [1] — melting/boiling point and reactivity are governed by two entirely different factors [1].

**15.** He left **gaps for undiscovered elements** and correctly **predicted their properties** [1]; the later discovery of gallium and germanium, matching his predictions, confirmed his table was correct [1].

**16.** A **more reactive** halogen displaces a **less reactive** one from its salt solution [1]. Iodine will **not** displace chlorine, since iodine is **less reactive** than chlorine (Group 7 reactivity decreases down the group, so iodine is the least reactive of the three) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the alpha-scattering observations without the conclusions.
- Saying reactivity increases down both Group 1 and Group 7.
- Giving only one reason for the Group 1 trend.
- Explaining noble gas unreactivity by their physical state.
- Forgetting that relative atomic mass is a **weighted** average.
- Confusing group number (outer-shell electrons) with period number (occupied shells).
- Assuming melting/boiling point and reactivity change in the same direction down Group 7 — they don't.
- Predicting a less reactive halogen can displace a more reactive one — displacement only ever goes from more reactive to less reactive.
