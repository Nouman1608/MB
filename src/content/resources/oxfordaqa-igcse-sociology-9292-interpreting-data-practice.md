## Section A: short questions

**Item A** — Percentage of children of primary-school age not enrolled in school, Region R, 2000–2020

*Illustrative data, invented for this practice question. In the exam, data like this might be shown
as a line graph.*

| Year | 2000 | 2005 | 2010 | 2015 | 2020 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Not enrolled (%) | 30 | 24 | 18 | 16 | 17 |

**1.** Using Item A, describe the overall trend between 2000 and 2020. **[2]**

**2.** Using Item A, calculate the change between 2000 and 2015 in percentage points, and as a
percentage of the 2000 figure. Give the percentage to one decimal place. **[3]**

**3.** Using Item A, identify the period in which the pattern changed, and describe the change. **[2]**

**Item B** — Reported online fraud cases in two cities in one year

*Illustrative data, invented for this practice question.*

| City | Reported cases | Population |
|---|---|---|
| M | 2,400 | 120,000 |
| N | 1,800 | 60,000 |

**4.** Using Item B, calculate the rate of reported cases per 1,000 people in each city, and state which
city has the higher rate. **[3]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why the figures in Item B may not show the true amount of online fraud.
**[2]**

**Item C** — Main source of news for 200 adults surveyed in Town T

*Illustrative data, invented for this practice question. In the exam, data like this might be shown
as a pie chart.*

| Source | Television | Social media | News websites | Newspapers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Share (%) | 40 | 35 | 15 | 10 |

**6.** Using Item C, calculate how many of the adults surveyed mainly used social media. **[1]**

**7.** Identify **two** limitations of Item C for drawing conclusions about news use in the whole
society. **[2]**

## Section B: longer questions

**Item D** — Extract from an unstructured interview with a mother in a rural area

*Original extract, invented for this practice question.*

> "Our daughter was doing well, but when the school went online during the closures we had one phone
> between four children, and the signal only works on the hill behind the house. She would walk up
> there in the evening to download her lessons. After a few weeks she stopped. Now she helps me in the
> shop. Her teacher says she could go back, but it is hard to start again."

**8.** Using Item D, identify **two** themes and support each with a short quotation. **[4]**

**9.** Using Item D, explain how the extract illustrates inequalities in access to digital education.
**[4]**

**10.** Explain **two** limitations of using Item D on its own to study the effects of school closures.
**[6]**

**11.** A student writes: "Item A proves that building more schools caused enrolment to rise." Explain
why this claim cannot be supported from the data. **[3]**

**12.** Using Items A and D and your own knowledge, explain how a researcher could use triangulation to
study why some children are not enrolled in school. **[6]**

## Answers: Section A

**A1** [2 marks]

The percentage not enrolled **fell** overall [1], from 30% in 2000 to 17% in 2020 [1].

**A2** [3 marks]

30 − 16 = **14 percentage points** [1]. 14 ÷ 30 × 100 [1] = **46.7%** decrease [1].

**A3** [2 marks]

Between **2015 and 2020** [1] the figure rose slightly, from 16% to 17%, after falling in every earlier
period [1].

**A4** [3 marks]

City M: 2,400 ÷ 120,000 × 1,000 = **20 per 1,000** [1]. City N: 1,800 ÷ 60,000 × 1,000 = **30 per 1,000**
[1]. **City N** has the higher rate, although City M has more cases [1].

**A5** [2 marks]

Many victims **do not report** online fraud [1], for example because they are embarrassed, do not
realise they have been defrauded or report only to their bank, so the figures undercount it [1].

**A6** [1 mark]

35 ÷ 100 × 200 = **70** adults [1].

**A7** [2 marks — 1 each]

Any two: the sample is only 200 people; it covers one town, which may not represent the whole society;
it gives only the **main** source, not all sources used; it does not show differences by age or other
groups; we do not know how the sample was chosen.

## Answers: Section B

**A8** [4 marks — 2 per theme]

Two from, [1] for the theme and [1] for a supporting quotation: **digital inequality** — "one phone
between four children"; **geographical barriers** — "the signal only works on the hill"; **leaving
education** — "After a few weeks she stopped"; **family economic need** — "Now she helps me in the
shop"; **difficulty returning** — "it is hard to start again".

**A9** [4 marks]

When teaching moved online, access depended on devices and connection [1]. The family shared one phone
between four children [1] and had poor signal at home, so the daughter had to walk to the hill [1].
These barriers led her to stop learning, showing that rural and poorer families can be excluded from
digital education [1].

**A10** [6 marks — up to 3 per limitation]

Two from: **representativeness** [1] — it is one family in one area [1], so it cannot be generalised
to all children affected by closures [1]; **no quantitative evidence** [1] — it does not show how many
children stopped learning [1], so it cannot measure the scale of the effect [1]; **one perspective**
[1] — only the mother's account is given [1], not the daughter's or the teacher's [1]; **interviewer
or memory effects** [1] — the mother may remember selectively or say what she thinks the interviewer
wants [1], affecting validity [1].

**A11** [3 marks]

Item A shows only that enrolment changed over time [1]; it does not include any data on school
building [1]. Even if school building increased, this would be a **correlation**, and other factors,
such as reduced fees or changing attitudes, could explain the change, so causation is not proven [1].

**A12** [6 marks]

Triangulation means using **more than one method or source** to study the same issue [1], improving
validity [1]. Quantitative data such as Item A shows **how many** children are not enrolled and how
this changes [1]. Qualitative data such as Item D shows **why**, through families' experiences [1]. A
researcher could combine official enrolment statistics with unstructured interviews with parents and
children [1], and perhaps observation in schools or a survey of households, to check whether
patterns and explanations support each other [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a percentage-point change when a percentage change is asked for, or the reverse.
- Comparing raw numbers instead of rates.
- Describing a trend without quoting figures.
- Quoting long passages from a qualitative extract instead of short phrases.
- Treating one interview as representative.
