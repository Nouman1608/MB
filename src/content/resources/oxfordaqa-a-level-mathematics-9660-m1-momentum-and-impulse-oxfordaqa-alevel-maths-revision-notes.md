These notes condense section M1.4 (Momentum and impulse) of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Mathematics (9660) specification, Version 5.2, part of the Mechanics content of International AS Unit PSM1. Everything here is restricted to motion in a straight line. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [momentum and impulse study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-9660-m1-momentum-and-impulse-oxfordaqa-alevel-maths/); to test yourself, use the [momentum and impulse practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-9660-m1-momentum-and-impulse-oxfordaqa-alevel-maths-practice/).

## The four things M1.4 covers

1. The concept of momentum, momentum = mv.
2. Conservation of momentum applied to two particles. Newton's law of restitution is not required.
3. Impulse = change in momentum.
4. Direct impact with a fixed surface, for particles moving perpendicular to a fixed smooth surface.

## Equations to know without the booklet

```
Momentum                    p = mv                    (N s or kg m s⁻¹)
Impulse                     I = mv − mu               (N s)
Impulse of constant force   I = Ft
Newton's second law         F = rate of change of momentum
Conservation (two bodies)   m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂
Coalescing bodies           m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v
```

u is the velocity before and v the velocity after. Every velocity carries a sign.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning in M1.4 |
|---|---|
| Momentum | Mass × velocity; a vector, signed in one dimension |
| Impulse | Change in momentum of one body; equals Ft for a constant force |
| Conservation of momentum | Total momentum of two colliding particles is unchanged when no external impulse acts along the line |
| Coalesce | The particles join and move on with one common velocity |
| Fixed surface | A wall or floor that does not move, so it can supply an external impulse |
| Smooth surface | No friction, so the contact force is perpendicular to the surface |
| Direct impact | The particle moves perpendicular to the surface before and after impact |
| Particle | A body treated as a point mass, so its size and rotation are ignored |

## Method for any collision question

1. Draw "before" and "after" diagrams with every velocity as an arrow.
2. State the positive direction in words.
3. Write total momentum before and after, putting minus signs on velocities in the negative direction.
4. Solve, then interpret the sign of the answer as a direction.
5. Check: the impulses on the two particles should be equal and opposite.

## Method for a fixed surface

1. Do not use conservation: the surface is external.
2. Take the direction of the rebound (or of the approach) as positive.
3. Impulse on the particle = m × (velocity after) − m × (velocity before).
4. If the particle falls or rises, use v² = u² + 2as for the speeds before and after the impact.
5. Average force during contact = impulse ÷ contact time.

## Quick worked checks

**Explosion from rest.** A 5 kg trolley and a 2 kg trolley are pushed apart by a released spring. The 2 kg trolley moves at 4 m s⁻¹. Total momentum is zero, so 5v = 2 × 4 and the 5 kg trolley moves at 1.6 m s⁻¹ in the opposite direction.

**Force from contact time.** A 0.45 kg ball hits a wall at 8 m s⁻¹ and rebounds at 6 m s⁻¹ after 0.05 s of contact. Impulse = 0.45 × (6 − (−8)) = 6.3 N s. The average force is 6.3 ÷ 0.05 = 126 N, directed away from the wall.

**Force against the motion.** A 4 kg particle moving at 5 m s⁻¹ meets a constant opposing force of 8 N for 3 s. Then 4v − 20 = −24, so v = −1: it ends up moving at 1 m s⁻¹ in the reverse direction.

## Exam traps

- **Rebound sign.** A ball arriving at 7 m s⁻¹ and leaving at 5.6 m s⁻¹ has a change in velocity of 12.6 m s⁻¹, not 1.4 m s⁻¹.
- **Wrong body.** "The impulse exerted by A on B" is B's change in momentum, not A's.
- **Wall conservation.** Writing m₁u₁ = m₁v₁ for a ball and a wall forces the rebound speed to equal the approach speed, which is generally false.
- **Mass units.** Masses given in grams must be converted to kilograms first.
- **Direction missing.** An impulse or velocity without a direction is incomplete.
- **Unwarranted assumption.** You cannot find two unknown final velocities from one momentum equation. The question will give a second fact, such as coalescence or one final speed.
- **Premature rounding.** Keep surds or full calculator values from v² = 2gh until the final line.

## Self-test

1. State the SI unit of impulse in two equivalent forms.
2. A 900 kg car moves at 20 m s⁻¹. Find its momentum.
3. A 3 kg particle at 4 m s⁻¹ collides with a 1 kg particle at rest and they coalesce. Find the common speed.
4. Why is momentum not conserved when a ball bounces on a fixed floor?
5. A 0.5 kg ball hits a wall at 10 m s⁻¹ and rebounds at 4 m s⁻¹. Find the magnitude of the impulse.
6. In question 5 the contact lasts 0.02 s. Find the average force.
7. A constant 6 N force acts for 4 s on a 2 kg particle initially at rest. Find its final speed.
8. Particle A (2 kg) at 3 m s⁻¹ meets particle B (1 kg) at 3 m s⁻¹ head-on. They coalesce. Find the common velocity.
9. Which law does the specification say you do not need for M1.4?
10. What does "smooth" tell you about the force from the surface?
11. After a collision your answer for v is −2.5. What does this mean?
12. A ball falls 0.9 m from rest onto a floor. Find its speed on impact, using g = 9.8 m s⁻².

### Answers

1. N s and kg m s⁻¹.
2. 900 × 20 = 18 000 N s.
3. 12 = 4v, so v = 3 m s⁻¹.
4. The floor is fixed and exerts an external impulse on the ball.
5. 0.5 × (4 − (−10)) = 7 N s.
6. 7 ÷ 0.02 = 350 N.
7. 6 × 4 = 2v, so v = 12 m s⁻¹.
8. 2(3) + 1(−3) = 3v, so v = 1 m s⁻¹ in A's original direction.
9. Newton's law of restitution.
10. It acts perpendicular to the surface only; there is no friction.
11. The particle moves at 2.5 m s⁻¹ in the negative direction.
12. v² = 2(9.8)(0.9) = 17.64, so v = 4.2 m s⁻¹.
