**This guide covers content area 3.1.1.3 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), Concept of belief in Allah: "the meaning and importance of the 99 names of Allah, including omnipotence (Qudrah), Allah as creator (Al-Khaliq), Allah as most gracious (Ar-Rehman), Allah as merciful (Ar-Raheem) and Allah as all-knowing (Al-Aleem)".** The prescribed passage is Qur'an 59:22-24. Tawhid says that Allah is one. This topic goes on to ask what Allah is like, because Muslims learn about Him through the names He has given Himself.

## Why Allah's names matter

A Muslim does not guess what God is like. Islam teaches that Allah has told humanity about Himself in the Qur'an and through the Prophet (pbuh), and the names are the clearest summary. The Qur'an says: "And to Allah belong the best names, so invoke Him by them" (7:180). Two ideas follow.

- **Knowing Allah:** each name describes a quality, so learning the names helps a believer understand and love Allah.
- **Calling on Allah:** a Muslim prays using the names, for example "O Most Merciful, have mercy on me".

A famous Hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim says that Allah has ninety-nine names, one hundred less one, and that whoever "memorises them" (some scholars say "knows and acts on them") will enter Paradise. Scholars note that this does not mean Allah has only ninety-nine names. It means that these are the names a believer should learn, understand and live by. Several lists of the 99 exist, and the names are gathered from the Qur'an and Hadith.

Muslims begin acts and speech with "Bismillah ir-Rahman ir-Raheem", and many boys and girls carry names such as Abdur-Rahman ("servant of the Most Gracious") or Abdul-Khaliq. These practices keep the names present in daily life.

## Names, attributes and the limit of understanding

Be clear on two words.

- A **name** is how Allah is called: *Al-Qadir* (the All-Powerful).
- An **attribute** (*sifah*) is the quality it describes: *Qudrah* (power, omnipotence).

The specification uses the attribute for the first example and the name for the others, so learn both forms. Islam adds a limit: human words are small, and nothing created is like Allah. "There is nothing like unto Him" (42:11). When we say "merciful" or "knowing" we use words from human life to point to qualities that are perfect in Allah.

## The five names in the specification

### 1. Qudrah: omnipotence (Al-Qadir, Al-Qadeer)

**Meaning:** Allah has complete power over everything. Nothing is too hard for Him, nothing limits Him and nothing can defeat Him. "Indeed, Allah is over all things competent" (2:20). Creation is His command: "His command is only when He intends a thing that He says to it, 'Be,' and it is" (36:82).

**Importance for a Muslim:**

- It gives **hope**, because no problem is beyond Allah.
- It gives **humility**, because the strongest ruler is still weak before Him.
- It supports **trust** (*tawakkul*): after effort, the Muslim leaves the outcome to the One who has power over it.

**Example:** a student who has revised thoroughly prays for success before an exam. They do their part, then accept the result as Allah's decision.

### 2. Al-Khaliq: the Creator

**Meaning:** Allah brought everything into being from nothing, and keeps it in existence. The passage includes "He is Allah, the Creator, the Producer, the Fashioner" (59:24), where three names show three steps: planning (*Al-Khaliq*), bringing into existence (*Al-Bari'*) and giving form (*Al-Musawwir*). "Allah is the Creator of all things" (13:16).

**Importance:**

- It supports **gratitude**: life, health and the world are gifts.
- It encourages **care for creation**. Humans are trusted as stewards (*khalifah*, 2:30), not owners.
- It teaches that **every person has worth**, because each human is Allah's creation.

**Example:** a Muslim who avoids waste, plants trees or looks after animals is acting on belief in the Creator.

### 3. Ar-Rehman: the Most Gracious

**Meaning:** the name comes from the root *r-h-m* (mercy) and describes mercy that is wide and covers all creation, believers and non-believers alike, in this world. The passage describes Allah as "the Entirely Merciful" (59:22). The Qur'an says, "My mercy encompasses all things" (7:156).

**Importance:** it shows that Allah gives food, air and health to all, and it encourages Muslims to be generous to everyone. It is part of the opening words of all but one surah of the Qur'an.

### 4. Ar-Raheem: the Merciful

**Meaning:** from the same root, but understood by many scholars as mercy that is specific and lasting, especially towards believers and especially in the Hereafter. The Qur'an says, "He is ever, to the believers, Merciful" (33:43). The passage calls Him "the Especially Merciful".

**Importance:** it gives believers hope of forgiveness and Paradise, and it comforts those who have sinned. "Do not despair of the mercy of Allah" (39:53).

**Pair them for the exam:** Ar-Rehman is mercy for all creation now; Ar-Raheem is mercy that is especially for believers and continues to the Day of Judgement. A Hadith reports that Allah created a hundred parts of mercy and sent down only one to the earth, which is why a mother lifts her foot from her child, and that He keeps ninety-nine for the Last Day (Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim).

### 5. Al-Aleem: the All-Knowing

**Meaning:** Allah's knowledge is complete, covering past, present and future, the visible and the hidden, the open act and the secret thought. The passage says He is "Knower of the unseen and the witnessed" (59:22). "No leaf falls but that He knows it" (6:59).

**Importance:**

- It encourages **honesty**, because nothing is hidden from Allah.
- It brings **comfort**, because He knows a person's struggles, even when no one else does.
- It supports belief in **the decree** and in the recording of deeds, because He knows what will happen.

**Example:** a shopkeeper gives the right change to a customer who did not notice the mistake, because Allah is Al-Aleem.

## The prescribed passage: Qur'an 59:22-24

The passage has more than twenty names in three verses. You do not need to list them all. Use it like this:

- **Opening, "other than whom there is no deity":** repeats Tawhid and ties the names to belief in one God.
- **"Knower of the unseen and the witnessed":** Al-Aleem.
- **"The Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful":** Ar-Rehman and Ar-Raheem.
- **"The Sovereign, the Pure, the Perfection, the Grantor of Security":** majesty and peace.
- **"The Exalted in Might, the Compeller, the Superior":** power, linked to Qudrah.
- **"The Creator, the Producer, the Fashioner":** Al-Khaliq.
- **"Exalted is Allah above whatever they associate with Him":** a rejection of Shirk.
- **"Whatever is in the heavens and earth is exalting Him":** all creation praises Allah.

Note that the names come in balance: power with mercy, majesty with peace. A good answer shows that Muslims fear Allah's justice and hope for His mercy at the same time.

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** ways in which belief in Allah as Al-Aleem affects a Muslim. **[4]**

> A Muslim who believes that Allah is all-knowing tries to be honest even when no one can see them [1], because they believe Allah knows every secret action and thought and will hold them to account [1]. It also gives comfort in difficulty [1], because Muslims believe that Allah knows their needs and sufferings even when other people do not understand [1].

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why the names Ar-Rehman and Ar-Raheem are important to Muslims. Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority. **[6]**

> They are important because they show that Allah is merciful to all creatures [1]. Muslims believe this mercy covers believers and non-believers in this life, so they are encouraged to show kindness to everyone [1]. They are also important because they give believers hope of forgiveness [1], since Ar-Raheem is understood as Allah's special mercy towards believers, which continues to the Day of Judgement [1]. The passage describes Allah as "the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful", and the Qur'an says "do not despair of the mercy of Allah" [2], which encourages a Muslim who has sinned to repent instead of giving up.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing up mercy names.** Ar-Rehman covers all creation; Ar-Raheem is especially for believers.
- **Saying there are only 99 names.** The Hadith teaches ninety-nine to learn, but Allah's names are not limited to that number.
- **Writing "omnipotence" with no explanation.** Say what it means: all-powerful, nothing beyond His ability.
- **Forgetting to show effect.** "Importance" questions want what the name does to belief or behaviour.
- **Describing Allah like a human.** Do not say Allah "gets tired" or "has a body". Muslims affirm His qualities without comparison.
