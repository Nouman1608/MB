OxfordAQA International GCSE English Language (9270) develops reading and
writing in English across literary non-fiction, source-based texts and
students' own composition. It is designed for international students
studying in English and is a core qualification for many of them. The
specification was first taught in September 2016 and first examined in
May/June 2018. It is linear and offers two routes: every candidate sits
Paper 1, worth 60%, and the remaining 40% comes from either Paper 2, an
exam (Route A), or a non-exam assessment (Route B).

## How the course is organised

The specification has four elements.

**Literary non-fiction and composition.** This is the compulsory core,
with a Section A and a Section B. In broad terms it pairs close reading of
literary non-fiction with writing of your own, so you study how skilled
writers craft prose and then put those techniques to use.

**Source-based reading and directed writing.** Also in two sections, this
element works with source material: reading for information and
viewpoint, and then writing in response for a set purpose. It is studied
by students on Route A.

**Non-exam assessment.** For Route B students, this replaces the
source-based paper with a written task and commentary completed during
the course.

**Speaking and listening (optional endorsement).** This is separately
certificated. It carries no marks towards the 9270 grade, but it is a
useful record of spoken English skills.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 (all candidates) | Literary Non-Fiction and Composition | 120 minutes | 80 | 60% |
| Route A: Paper 2 | Source-Based Reading and Directed Writing | 120 minutes | 80 | 40% |
| Route B: NEA | Written task and commentary | Not timed | 60 | 40% |

Paper 1 and Paper 2 are externally assessed exams. The non-exam
assessment is marked by your teacher and moderated by the board. Each
student follows one route only, usually decided by the school, so the
qualification is made up of Paper 1 plus either Paper 2 or the NEA. There
are no tiers. The speaking and listening endorsement, if your school
offers it, is reported separately from the grade.

## How the parts fit together

Paper 1 is the anchor: as the largest component and the one every
student sits, it rewards sustained practice in both reading and writing.
The skills it develops are the same ones needed for the second
component, whichever route you take.

On Route A, Paper 2 shifts the emphasis from literary craft to the
handling of sources: selecting information, comparing viewpoints and
writing for a directed purpose and audience. On Route B, the NEA asks you
to produce a polished written task and then reflect on your own choices
in a commentary, which draws on the analytical vocabulary you use in
Paper 1 when discussing other writers.

The common thread is reading as a writer and writing as a reader. When
you analyse how a text achieves its effect, you are also learning
techniques for your own writing; when you write, you should be able to
explain the choices you made. Accurate spelling, punctuation and grammar
matter across every component.

## Building writing skills over the course

Writing is assessed in every part of 9270, so it is worth treating it as
a skill that grows steadily rather than one you revise at the end. Good
writing at this level comes from three things: a clear sense of purpose
and audience, control of structure, and accurate, varied sentences.

Practise short pieces often. A paragraph of description, an opening for
an argument or a letter to a specific reader can each be written in a
few minutes and then improved. Redrafting is where most progress
happens, so keep earlier drafts and compare them with later ones. Your
reading supports this directly: when a writer's technique impresses you,
try it in your own work the same week. Students on Route B will find the
habit especially useful, because the NEA rewards a carefully revised
final piece and a commentary that explains the choices behind it.

## Planning your study

Start with the reading and writing skills of Paper 1, since these
underpin everything else. Read widely beyond the classroom, including
travel writing, memoir, journalism and essays, and keep short notes on
techniques you could borrow.

Find out early which route your school follows. On Route A, build regular
practice with unseen source texts and directed writing tasks into each
term. On Route B, plan the NEA timeline with your teacher, allowing time
to draft, redraft and write the commentary, and keep exam practice for
Paper 1 going alongside it.

Once the core skills are secure, move to timed questions and then full
papers in the final months. The course's exam-preparation page explains
the Route A and Route B choice in detail, so look there for advice on
your second component.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is OxfordAQA International GCSE English Language 9270 assessed?**
All candidates sit Paper 1, Literary Non-Fiction and Composition, which is 120 minutes long, worth 80 marks and 60% of the grade. The other 40% comes from either Paper 2 or the non-exam assessment.

**What is the difference between Route A and Route B?**
Route A adds Paper 2, Source-Based Reading and Directed Writing, an exam of 120 minutes and 80 marks. Route B adds a non-exam assessment made up of a written task and commentary, worth 60 marks, marked by the teacher and moderated by the board.

**Does speaking and listening count towards the grade?**
No. The speaking and listening endorsement is optional and separately certificated, and it carries no marks towards the 9270 grade.

**Is 9270 tiered?**
No. There is a single entry for all candidates.

**When was 9270 first examined?**
First teaching was in September 2016 and the first exams were in May/June 2018.
