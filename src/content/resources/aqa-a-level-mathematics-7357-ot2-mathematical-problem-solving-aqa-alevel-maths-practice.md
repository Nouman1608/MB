These questions practise overarching theme **OT2 Mathematical problem solving** (OT2.1 to OT2.7) of the AQA A-level Mathematics (7357) specification, for first teaching 2017. Each problem sits in a different content area, because OT2 is assessed inside questions on any topic. All contexts and figures are invented. A calculator is required, but show your method: most marks are for the working.

Learn the methods first in the [OT2 study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-mathematics-7357-ot2-mathematical-problem-solving-aqa-alevel-maths/) and the [OT2 revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-mathematics-7357-ot2-mathematical-problem-solving-aqa-alevel-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (OT2.1, OT2.3) Sam saves £50 in the first month, and each month saves £5 more than the month before. Find the first month in which Sam's total savings exceed £3000. **[4]**

**2.** (OT2.2, OT2.3) A colony starts with one bacterium and the number doubles every hour. Find the least whole number of hours after which the colony has more than one million bacteria. **[3]**

**3.** (OT2.5) Without calculating, explain whether the trapezium rule gives an overestimate or an underestimate of ∫₁³ ln x dx. **[2]**

**4.** (OT2.5) A runner covers 120 m, measured to the nearest 10 m, in 15.2 s, measured to the nearest 0.1 s. Find the upper and lower bounds for her mean speed, and hence state the speed to an appropriate degree of accuracy. **[3]**

**5.** (OT2.6) Name the four stages of the problem-solving cycle described in the specification, and state what should happen if the interpretation stage shows that the answer is unsuitable. **[3]**

**6.** (OT2.5) Estimate the number of times a person's heart beats in 80 years. State your assumptions and give your answer to 1 significant figure. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** (OT2.1, OT2.6) An open-topped box has a square base of side x cm and height h cm. Its volume is 32 000 cm³.

**(a)** Show that the external surface area is S = x² + 128 000/x. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the value of x that minimises S, and the minimum surface area. **[4]**

**(c)** The box must be at least 25 cm tall. Find the least surface area now possible. **[3]**

**8.** (OT2.4, OT2.5) Let f(x) = x³ − 2x − 5.

**(a)** Use the Newton–Raphson method with x₀ = 2 to find x₁ and x₂. **[3]**

**(b)** Show that the root is 2.095 correct to 3 decimal places. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why x₀ = 0.8 would be a poor starting value. **[1]**

**9.** (OT2.7) A train starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 0.5 m s⁻² for 40 s. It then travels at constant speed for T seconds before decelerating uniformly at 1 m s⁻² to rest. The total distance is 3 km.

**(a)** Sketch a velocity–time graph for the journey. **[2]**

**(b)** Find T and the total time for the journey. **[4]**

**10.** (OT2.4, OT2.5) The exact value of ∫₁³ ln x dx is 3 ln 3 − 2.

**(a)** Use the trapezium rule with 4 strips to estimate the integral, to 4 decimal places. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage error in your estimate. **[2]**

**11.** (OT2.1, OT2.3) A ball is dropped from a height of 2 m. After each bounce it rises to 70% of its previous height.

**(a)** Explain why the total distance travelled can be found using a geometric series. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the total distance travelled by the ball before it comes to rest. **[3]**

**(c)** Comment on whether your answer is realistic. **[1]**

**12.** (OT2.2) A sector of a circle has radius r cm and angle θ radians. Its perimeter is 20 cm. Find, with justification, the greatest possible area of the sector. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Savings form an arithmetic series with a = 50, d = 5 [1]. Sₙ = (n/2)(100 + 5(n − 1)) > 3000, so n² + 19n − 1200 > 0 [1]. Positive root n = 26.4 [1]. S₂₆ = 2925 < 3000 and S₂₇ = 3105, so the **27th month** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the final mark is for the integer answer with a check; "26.4 months" does not answer the question.

**2.** 2ⁿ > 10⁶ [1]; n > 6/log₁₀ 2 = 19.93 [1]; **20 hours** (2¹⁹ = 524 288, 2²⁰ = 1 048 576) [1].

**3.** y = ln x is concave on [1, 3] (it bends downwards; d²y/dx² = −1/x² < 0) [1]. Each trapezium's top lies below the curve, so the rule gives an **underestimate** [1].

**4.** Upper bound = 125/15.15 = 8.25 m s⁻¹ [1]; lower bound = 115/15.25 = 7.54 m s⁻¹ [1]. Both round to **8 m s⁻¹ (1 s.f.)**, so this is the appropriate accuracy [1].
*Examiner insight:* the upper bound for a quotient uses the largest numerator and the smallest denominator.

**5.** Specify the problem [1]; collect information, then process and represent it [1]; interpret the results; if unsuitable, repeat the cycle with the specification or information changed [1].

**6.** Assume about 70 beats per minute throughout life (any value from 60 to 80 accepted) [1]. 70 × 60 × 24 × 365 × 80 ≈ 2.9 × 10⁹ [1], so about **3 × 10⁹** beats [1].

**7. (a)** x²h = 32 000, so h = 32 000/x² [1]. S = x² + 4xh = x² + 4x(32 000/x²) = x² + 128 000/x [1].

**(b)** dS/dx = 2x − 128 000/x² = 0 [1], so x³ = 64 000 and **x = 40** [1]. d²S/dx² = 2 + 256 000/x³ > 0, so a minimum [1]. S = 1600 + 3200 = **4800 cm²** [1].

**(c)** h ≥ 25 means 32 000/x² ≥ 25, so x ≤ √1280 = 35.78 [1]. S is decreasing for x < 40, so the minimum on x ≤ 35.78 is at x = 35.78 [1]: S = 1280 + 128 000/35.78 = **4860 cm²** (3 s.f.) [1].

**8. (a)** f′(x) = 3x² − 2 [1]. x₁ = 2 − (−1)/10 = **2.1** [1]. x₂ = 2.1 − 0.061/11.23 = **2.0946** (4 d.p.) [1].

**(b)** f(2.0945) = −0.00057 [1]; f(2.0955) = 0.0106 [1]. Sign change and f is continuous, so 2.0945 < root < 2.0955, giving **2.095 (3 d.p.)** [1].

**(c)** f′(0.8) = 3(0.64) − 2 = −0.08, close to zero, so the tangent is nearly horizontal and x₁ would be far from the root [1].

**9. (a)** Straight line from (0, 0) to (40, 20); horizontal line at v = 20 [1]; straight line down to v = 0, taking 20 s [1].

**(b)** Top speed = 0.5 × 40 = 20 m s⁻¹; deceleration time = 20/1 = 20 s [1]. Distances: ½ × 40 × 20 = 400 m and ½ × 20 × 20 = 200 m [1]. 400 + 20T + 200 = 3000 [1], so **T = 120 s**; total time = 40 + 120 + 20 = **180 s** [1].

**10. (a)** h = 0.5; y-values: 0, 0.4055, 0.6931, 0.9163, 1.0986 [1]. Area ≈ 0.25 × [0 + 1.0986 + 2(0.4055 + 0.6931 + 0.9163)] [1] = **1.2821** [1].

**(b)** Exact = 1.2958 [1]; error = (1.2958 − 1.2821)/1.2958 × 100 = **1.06%** [1].

**11. (a)** Each bounce height is 0.7 times the previous one, so heights form a geometric sequence [1].

**(b)** Distance = 2 + 2 × (1.4 + 0.98 + …) [1] = 2 + 2 × 1.4/(1 − 0.7) [1] = **11.3 m** (34/3 m) [1].

**(c)** The sum to infinity assumes infinitely many bounces and an exact 70% each time; in reality the ball stops after a finite number of bounces, so the answer is a slight overestimate [1].

**12.** Perimeter: 2r + rθ = 20, so θ = (20 − 2r)/r [1]. Area A = ½r²θ = ½r(20 − 2r) = 10r − r² [1]. dA/dr = 10 − 2r = 0 [1], so r = 5 [1]. d²A/dr² = −2 < 0, so a maximum [1]. A = 50 − 25 = **25 cm²** (with θ = 2 radians) [1].
*Examiner insight:* the structure is a quadratic in r once θ is eliminated; spotting that is the OT2.1 skill.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving non-integer answers in whole-number contexts.
- Rounding bounds before comparing them.
- Claiming a root to 3 d.p. without the sign-change interval.
- Forgetting the up-and-down distance in bounce problems.
- No check that a stationary point is a maximum or minimum.

## Frequently asked questions

**How are these questions organised?**
Section A has shorter problems; Section B has longer, multi-step problems. The OT2 statements practised are in brackets at the start of each question.

**Is OT2 tested on its own paper?**
No. It is an overarching theme, so it is assessed inside questions on content from any section, on all three papers.
