## Section A

**1.** Define the term cloud computing. **[2]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by shared computing resources in cloud computing. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** ways in which an individual might use cloud computing. **[2]**

**4.** Describe what is meant by scalability in cloud computing. **[2]**

**5.** Give **two** disadvantages of cloud computing for an individual. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A user stores 200 GB of photographs in the cloud at £0.015 per GB per month.

**(a)** Calculate the monthly cost. **[1]**

**(b)** The user backs up 30 GB of new photographs over a connection of 100 megabits per second. Calculate the time taken in minutes. Show your working. **[3]**

**7.** Treetop Games hires cloud servers at £0.35 per hour. For a game launch it needs 6 servers for 10 hours.

**(a)** Calculate the cost of the launch. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the cloud suits this need better than buying 6 servers. **[3]**

**8.** A web shop needs 3 servers all year (24 hours a day, 365 days). Cloud servers cost £0.35 per hour.

**(a)** Calculate the yearly cloud cost. **[2]**

**(b)** The shop could buy 3 servers for £8,000 and spend £1,200 a year on power and staff time. Compare the first year's cost with the cloud cost. **[2]**

**(c)** State **two** other factors the shop should consider. **[2]**

**9.** A small accountancy firm with no IT staff is moving its accounts software to a cloud service. Evaluate this decision. **[6]**

**10.** Explain how cloud computing helps an organisation to recover from a disaster such as a fire at its office. **[3]**

**11.** Describe **three** security risks of storing an organisation's customer data in the cloud and one way to reduce each. **[6]**

**12.** Distinguish between a public cloud and a private cloud, and explain why a bank might use a hybrid cloud. **[4]**

**13.** A school moves its email and shared documents to a cloud service. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages for the school. **[6]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Using storage, processing power or software [1] held on remote servers and reached over the internet, instead of on local equipment [1]. **[2]**

**2.** The provider's servers form a pool [1] that is shared between many customers, each using a part of it and paying only for what they need [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two from: storing photographs, music or documents online [1]; web-based email [1]; streaming video or music [1]; automatic backup of a phone [1]; editing shared documents [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The ability to increase or decrease the resources used [1] quickly, as demand changes, without buying or removing hardware [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two from: needs an internet connection to reach files [1]; privacy concerns, as the provider holds the files [1]; limited free storage and costs may rise [1]; little control over the service [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 200 × 0.015 = £3 [1].

**(b)** 30 GB = 30 × 8,000 = 240,000 megabits [1]; 240,000 ÷ 100 = 2,400 seconds [1]; 2,400 ÷ 60 = 40 minutes [1]. **[4]**

**7. (a)** 6 × 10 × 0.35 [1] = £21 [1].

**(b)** The servers are needed only for a short time [1]; with the cloud the company pays only for the hours used [1], whereas bought servers would cost much more and be idle most of the time [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** 3 × 24 × 365 = 26,280 server-hours [1]; × 0.35 = £9,198 [1].

**(b)** Buying costs £8,000 + £1,200 = £9,200 in the first year [1], almost the same as the cloud (£9,198), but the purchased servers would last for further years [1].

**(c)** Any two from: staff to maintain the servers [1]; the need to replace them after a few years [1]; the ability to grow when demand rises [1]; security and backup [1]; dependence on the internet connection for the cloud [1]. **[6]**

**9.** Advantages: no servers or maintenance to buy, so lower start-up costs [1]; the provider updates and backs up the software [1]; staff can reach the accounts from anywhere [1]; a fixed monthly cost is easier to budget [1]. Disadvantages: needs a reliable internet connection, so work stops if it fails [1]; client financial data is held by a third party, so there are security and legal concerns [1]; costs continue every month [1]; changing provider later may be hard [1]. Conclusion, for example that it suits a firm with no IT staff provided it chooses a reputable provider with encryption and backups [1]. Maximum 6 marks. **[6]**

**10.** The data and systems are stored on the provider's servers in another location, not in the office [1], so they are not destroyed by the fire [1]. Staff can reach them from other premises or from home, and the company can start up its systems on new cloud servers quickly [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Any three risks with a method, [1] for the risk and [1] for the reduction: unauthorised access through stolen passwords [1], reduced by strong passwords and two-factor authentication [1]; a data breach at the provider [1], reduced by encrypting the data before it is stored [1]; loss of data if the provider fails [1], reduced by keeping a second copy elsewhere [1]; data held in a country with different laws [1], reduced by choosing a provider that stores data in an approved country [1]; staff misusing access [1], reduced by access rights and logging [1]. **[6]**

**12.** A public cloud is shared by many customers and run by a provider [1]; a private cloud is used by one organisation only [1]. A bank might keep confidential customer data in a private cloud for security [1] and use a public cloud for extra capacity, such as its website at busy times [1]. **[4]**

**13.** Advantages: staff and pupils can reach email and documents from school or home [1]; the provider handles storage, updates and backups, saving the school's IT time [1]; easy sharing and group work on documents [1]; storage can grow as needed [1]. Disadvantages: needs a reliable connection, and pupils without internet at home may be disadvantaged [1]; pupils' personal data is held by a third party, so data protection duties apply [1]; subscription costs continue [1]; the school may find it hard to move to another provider [1]. Conclusion [1]. Maximum 6 marks. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining the cloud as "the internet" or "online storage" only.
- Calculations that leave out hours per day, or forget to convert gigabytes to megabits.
- Giving "cheaper" with no explanation of shared or pay-per-use costs.
- Forgetting that cloud access depends on the internet connection.
- Writing about security in general terms with no risk and no way of reducing it.
