## Questions

**1.** Pallisade Cycles Ltd ceases trading owing creditors $55,000. Its assets raise &#36;20,000. Rina holds 3,000 ordinary shares of &#36;1, all fully paid.

**(a)** State what limited liability means. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the amount the creditors are short of and state the most Rina can lose. **[2]**

**2.** A sole trader is thinking of forming a limited company. State two advantages and two disadvantages of doing so. **[4]**

**3.** A company has total assets of $212,000 and total liabilities of &#36;73,500. Its ordinary share capital is &#36;100,000 and its general reserve &#36;18,000. It has no preference shares.

**(a)** Calculate its equity. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate its retained earnings. **[1]**

**4.** State where each item appears in a statement of financial position: (i) 9% debentures repayable in seven years; (ii) general reserve; (iii) 4% preference shares; (iv) debenture interest owing; (v) retained earnings. **[5]**

**5.**
**(a)** State two differences between ordinary share capital and debentures. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a company with uncertain profits might prefer issuing ordinary shares to debentures. **[2]**

**6.** Thornfield Toys Ltd has issued 200,000 ordinary shares of $0.25 each. It has called up &#36;0.20 per share. The holders of 5,000 shares have not paid.

**(a)** Calculate the issued, called-up and paid-up share capital. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain the difference between called-up and paid-up share capital. **[1]**

**7.** Pallisade Cycles Ltd, a trading company, at 31 December 2027: revenue $318,000; inventory at 1 January &#36;24,600; purchases &#36;190,000; wages &#36;52,000; rent &#36;14,400; electricity &#36;6,100; trade receivables &#36;25,000; allowance for irrecoverable debts &#36;800; equipment at cost &#36;60,000 with accumulated depreciation &#36;18,000; 8% debentures &#36;45,000; debenture interest paid &#36;1,800.

Adjustments: inventory at 31 December $27,900; rent prepaid &#36;1,200; electricity owing &#36;450; write off irrecoverable debts of &#36;750, then set the allowance at 4% of remaining receivables; depreciate equipment at 20% reducing balance; the rest of the year's debenture interest is unpaid.

Prepare the statement of profit or loss for the year ended 31 December 2027. **[10]**

**8.** Merrin Interiors Ltd had at 1 July 2027: ordinary shares $60,000; 6% preference shares &#36;30,000; general reserve &#36;8,000; retained earnings &#36;14,200. It issued 15,000 ordinary shares of &#36;1 at &#36;1, all paid. Profit for the year was &#36;19,800. It transferred &#36;2,500 to the general reserve and paid the preference dividend and &#36;0.05 on every ordinary share. Prepare the statement of changes in equity for the year ended 30 June 2028. **[6]**

**9.** Ainsley Design Ltd is a service company. At 30 June 2028: premises $90,000; office equipment cost &#36;24,000, accumulated depreciation &#36;9,600; trade receivables &#36;7,800; allowance for irrecoverable debts &#36;300; other receivables &#36;450; bank &#36;12,150; trade payables &#36;4,200; other payables &#36;650; 7% debentures repayable in eight years &#36;20,000; ordinary shares &#36;70,000; general reserve &#36;5,000; retained earnings &#36;24,650. Prepare the statement of financial position. **[8]**

**10.** Brackenhill Joinery Ltd makes furniture. For the year: raw materials used $61,000; direct wages &#36;44,000; factory overheads &#36;29,500; work in progress &#36;6,200 at the start and &#36;5,400 at the end; finished goods inventory &#36;9,800 at the start and &#36;11,300 at the end; revenue &#36;210,000.

**(a)** Calculate prime cost and the factory cost of production. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate cost of sales and gross profit. **[2]**

**11.** Zara Whitlow runs Whitlow Fabrics as a sole trader. She needs $120,000 for larger premises and is considering forming a limited company and issuing ordinary shares to family members. Evaluate this plan and recommend. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A shareholder can lose no more than the amount paid, or agreed to be paid, for their shares [1].
**(b)** Shortfall $55,000 - &#36;20,000 = &#36;35,000 [1]; Rina can lose at most the &#36;3,000 she paid for her shares [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the cap on the loss and its figure.

**2.** Advantages: loss is limited [1]; large sums can be raised, or the company continues if an owner leaves, or borrowing is easier [1]. Disadvantages: more legal requirements and cost [1]; profit shared as dividends, or accounts may be public, or owners may not manage [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Four separate points are needed.

**3. (a)** $212,000 - &#36;73,500 [1] = **&#36;138,500** [1].
**(b)** $138,500 - &#36;100,000 - &#36;18,000 = **&#36;20,500** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the subtraction line.

**4.** (i) Non-current liabilities [1]; (ii) equity [1]; (iii) equity [1]; (iv) current liabilities, as other payables [1]; (v) equity [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the heading exactly.

**5. (a)** Two differences, for example: shareholders are owners, debenture holders are lenders [1]; ordinary dividend varies and need not be paid, debenture interest is fixed and compulsory [1].
**(b)** Directors can cut the dividend if profit is poor [1], but debenture interest must be paid whatever the profit [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison states both sides.

**6. (a)** Issued 200,000 x $0.25 = &#36;50,000 [1]; called-up 200,000 x &#36;0.20 = &#36;40,000 [1]; paid-up &#36;40,000 - (5,000 x &#36;0.20) = &#36;39,000 [1].
**(b)** Called-up is the amount asked for; paid-up is the part of it actually received [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Label each of the three figures.

**10. (a)** Prime cost $61,000 + &#36;44,000 = &#36;105,000 [1]; factory cost of production &#36;105,000 + &#36;29,500 + &#36;6,200 [1] - &#36;5,400 = **&#36;135,300** [1].
**(b)** Cost of sales $9,800 + &#36;135,300 - &#36;11,300 = **&#36;133,800** [1]; gross profit &#36;210,000 - &#36;133,800 = **&#36;76,200** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Factory cost of production replaces purchases; do not add both.

**11.** Advantages applied: limited liability protects Zara's private assets if the larger premises fail [1]; family members can supply the $120,000, which she may not raise alone [1]. Disadvantages applied: more legal requirements and costs than a sole trader faces [1]; profit will be shared as dividends and she loses sole control [1]. Judgement, for example: forming the company is sensible as &#36;120,000 is a large personal risk [1], if the costs and shared profit are acceptable [1]. A reasoned opposite view is valid. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Refer to Zara's own figures and plans.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting dividends in the statement of profit or loss, or debentures in equity.
- Adding a general reserve transfer into the total column.
- Confusing issued, called-up and paid-up share capital.
