## Questions

**1.** A student soaks 20 pea seeds in each of three dishes kept at 10 °C, 20 °C and 30 °C, and counts how many have germinated after four days. Identify the independent variable and the dependent variable, and name one control variable with a way of keeping it constant. **[3]**

**2.** Protease enzymes are proteins that digest egg white. Using the idea of the active site, write a hypothesis and a prediction for an investigation comparing the digestion of egg white at 40 °C and at 80 °C. **[3]**

**3.** A student counts the leaves with holes on each of ten plants: 6, 3, 8, 5, 3, 7, 3, 9, 4, 2. Calculate the mean, median, mode and range. **[4]**

**4.** The number of stomata in five fields of view of a leaf impression was 31, 29, 33, 58 and 30.
**(a)** Identify the anomalous result and suggest one possible cause. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean without the anomaly, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest one way to check whether the anomaly is genuine. **[1]**

**5.** Two students each measure the temperature of a beaker of ice and water, which is really 0.0 °C. Student X records 2.1, 2.0 and 2.2 °C. Student Y records -0.6, 0.5 and 0.2 °C.
**(a)** State which student's readings are more precise and give evidence from the range. **[2]**
**(b)** Compare the accuracy of the two means and name the type of error that most likely affected student X. **[2]**

**6.** **(a)** Name the best form of graph or chart for each data set. **[3]**
- (i) The number of seeds in each of five mass classes (0.10 to 0.19 g, 0.20 to 0.29 g and so on).
- (ii) The rate of an enzyme reaction at 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 °C.
- (iii) The number of woodlice found in four named habitats.

**(b)** A graph's x axis is 12 cm long and must cover 0 to 30 minutes. Suggest a suitable scale and explain why 7 minutes per cm would be a poor choice. **[2]**

**7.** A line of best fit for gas volume against time passes through (2 min, 3.6 cm³) and (12 min, 21.6 cm³).
**(a)** Calculate the gradient. **[3]**
**(b)** State what the gradient represents, with its unit. **[1]**

**8.** A drawing of a root hair cell is 45 mm long. The real cell is 150 µm long.
**(a)** Calculate the magnification of the drawing. **[3]**
**(b)** Another cell is 0.12 mm long. How long is it in a drawing at the same magnification? **[2]**

**9.** **(a)** A seedling grows from 7.2 cm to 10.0 cm. Calculate the percentage increase in height to 2 significant figures. **[3]**
**(b)** A bacterium is 0.0000025 m long. Write this in standard form, then in µm. **[2]**

**10.** A field measures 30 m by 20 m. A student places ten 0.25 m² quadrats and counts daisy plants: 4, 2, 0, 3, 1, 5, 2, 3, 2, 3.
**(a)** Explain why the quadrats should be placed at random. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean number of daisies per quadrat. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the density in daisies per m². **[1]**
**(d)** Estimate the number of daisies in the field. **[1]**
**(e)** Suggest one change that would make the estimate more reliable. **[1]**

**11.** Equal-looking cubes of beetroot, cut by eye, are placed in tubes of water for 5 minutes at different temperatures, one cube per temperature. A colorimeter measures how much red pigment has leaked into the water. Absorbance: 20 °C 0.05; 30 °C 0.08; 40 °C 0.12; 50 °C 0.45; 60 °C 1.10; 70 °C 1.32.
**(a)** Describe the pattern, quoting data. **[3]**
**(b)** Identify three weaknesses in the method and suggest an improvement for each. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain the results in terms of the cell membrane. **[2]**

**12.** Plan an investigation into the effect of pH on the activity of amylase, which digests starch. Include a hypothesis, how you will vary and measure, the control variables, repeats, one safety precaution, and how you will set out your results. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Independent variable: temperature [1]. Dependent variable: the number (or percentage) of seeds germinated after four days [1]. Control, for example: the same volume of water in each dish, kept constant by measuring it with a measuring cylinder (or: the same pea variety, taken from one packet) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A control variable named without saying how it is held constant tends to earn less than one with a method attached.

**2.** Hypothesis: a very high temperature changes the shape of the protease active site, so egg white is digested more slowly [1]. Prediction: egg white at 80 °C will be digested slower than (or not at all compared with) egg white at 40 °C [1]. Explanation: the enzyme is denatured, so the substrate no longer fits the active site [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A prediction states what you expect to see; a hypothesis gives the scientific reason, so keep the two apart.

**3.** Total = 50, so mean = 50 / 10 = **5.0** [1]. Ordered list is 2, 3, 3, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, so median = (4 + 5) / 2 = **4.5** [1]. Mode = **3** [1]. Range = 9 - 2 = **7** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Order the values before choosing the median.

**4.** **(a)** 58 is the anomaly [1], possibly because a different area was counted or two stomata were counted twice, or a counting slip [1].
**(b)** 31 + 29 + 33 + 30 = 123 [1]; 123 / 4 = **30.8** [1].
**(c)** Repeat the count on that field of view, or count more fields [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say why you believe a value is anomalous and what you did with it; deleting it without comment loses the explanation.

**5.** **(a)** Student X is more precise [1] because X's range is 0.2 °C but Y's range is 1.1 °C [1].
**(b)** X's mean is 2.1 °C, a long way from 0.0 °C, while Y's mean is 0.03 °C, so Y is more accurate [1]. X's readings are all shifted the same way, so the error is systematic, such as a thermometer that reads too high [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Precision is about the spread of repeats and accuracy is about the distance of the mean from the true value, so quote numbers for each.

**6.** **(a)** (i) histogram, bars touching, because the mass classes are continuous [1]. (ii) line graph, because temperature is a continuous variable [1]. (iii) bar chart, with gaps, because the habitats are categories [1].
**(b)** 2.5 minutes per cm uses the full 12 cm (12 x 2.5 = 30) [1]. 7 minutes per cm makes plotting awkward and wastes or overruns the axis (30 / 7 is not a whole number of centimetres) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the reason for each chart type, since a bare name leaves nothing to support it.

**7.** **(a)** Change in volume = 21.6 - 3.6 = 18.0 cm³ [1]. Change in time = 12 - 2 = 10 min [1]. Gradient = 18.0 / 10 = **1.8** [1].
**(b)** The rate of gas production, **1.8 cm³ per min** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Read points from the line itself, and include the unit.

**8.** **(a)** 45 mm = 45 000 µm [1]. Magnification = 45 000 / 150 [1] = **x300** [1].
**(b)** 0.12 mm = 120 µm [1]. Drawn length = 120 x 300 = 36 000 µm = **36 mm** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert both lengths to the same unit before dividing; an unconverted ratio is the most common source of a wrong power of ten.

**9.** **(a)** Increase = 10.0 - 7.2 = 2.8 cm [1]. 2.8 / 7.2 x 100 = 38.9% [1]. To 2 significant figures = **39%** [1].
**(b)** **2.5 x 10⁻⁶ m** [1] = **2.5 µm** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Round only at the end; rounding 2.8 / 7.2 early can change the second significant figure.

**10.** **(a)** Random placement avoids bias towards areas that look good, so the sample represents the whole field [1].
**(b)** 25 / 10 = **2.5** per quadrat [1].
**(c)** 2.5 / 0.25 = **10 per m²** [1].
**(d)** Area = 30 x 20 = 600 m², so 10 x 600 = **6000 daisies** [1].
**(e)** Use more quadrats (for example 30), which reduces the effect of chance [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Estimate" is the right word for part (d); the field was only sampled, not counted.

**11.** **(a)** Absorbance changes little from 20 °C to 40 °C (0.05 to 0.12) [1]. It rises sharply between 40 °C and 60 °C (0.12 to 1.10) [1]. It rises less between 60 °C and 70 °C (1.10 to 1.32) [1].
**(b)** Cubes cut by eye vary in surface area, so cut with a cork borer to one length [1]. Only one cube per temperature, so repeat each and take a mean [1]. Water bath temperature may drift, so keep a thermometer in the tube and check it [1].
**(c)** High temperature damages the membrane (protein parts are denatured) [1], so it becomes more permeable and pigment leaks out [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In evaluations, a weakness plus its effect plus a specific fix gives a clear answer; "do it more carefully" gives nothing to credit.

**12.** Hypothesis: amylase works fastest at a particular pH and slower either side of it, because pH changes the shape of the active site [1]. Independent variable: pH, using buffer solutions at five values, for example pH 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 [1]. Dependent variable: the time taken for starch to disappear, tested with iodine solution on a spotting tile every 30 seconds [1]. Controls, two of: temperature kept at 35 °C in a water bath; same volume and concentration of starch; same volume and concentration of amylase [2]. Repeat each pH at least three times and calculate a mean [1]. Safety: wear eye protection and wipe up iodine spills (or take care with hot water) [1]. Results table: pH in the first column, times for each repeat, then the mean time, with units in the headings [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the apparatus and the values you will use; "change the pH and see" gives little to credit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a control variable without saying how it is kept constant.
- Writing a prediction and calling it a hypothesis.
- Finding a median without ordering the values first.
- Leaving an anomaly in a mean, or deleting it without explanation.
- Calling a result accurate because the repeats agree.
- Choosing a bar chart for two continuous variables.
- Mixing mm and µm in a magnification calculation.
- Leaving units off a rate or a gradient.
- Describing a graph without quoting values from it.
- Giving a vague improvement such as "be more careful" instead of a specific change.
