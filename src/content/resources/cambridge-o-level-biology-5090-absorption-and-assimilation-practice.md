## Section B

**6.** The diagram description shows a villus. It has a layer of cells covering the surface, a network of capillaries just beneath the cells, and a single blind-ended tube in the centre.

**(a)** Name the tube in the centre. **[1]**

**(b)** State the nutrients that enter this tube. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe **three** features of the villus that make it well adapted for absorption. **[3]**

**(d)** Explain why the blood flowing through the capillaries helps absorption. **[2]**

**7.** A student made a model of the lining of the small intestine. A flat piece of card measures 10 cm by 10 cm. The student then stuck 20 strips of card, each 10 cm long and 1 cm high, upright on the flat piece, with both sides of every strip exposed.

**(a)** Calculate the area of the flat piece of paper. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the total area of the sides of the 20 strips, if each strip has two sides each measuring 10 cm by 1 cm. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how the model shows the importance of villi. **[2]**

**8.** The table shows the volume of water in the gut over one day.

| Source or site | Volume of water (dm cubed) |
|---|---|
| Entering the gut in food and drink | 2.0 |
| Entering the gut in secretions | 7.0 |
| Absorbed in the small intestine | 8.4 |
| Absorbed in the colon | 0.5 |
| Lost in faeces | 0.1 |

**(a)** Calculate the total volume of water that enters the gut. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of this water that is absorbed in the small intestine. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why a person with severe diarrhoea may become dehydrated. **[2]**

**9.** Explain why the cells lining the ileum contain many mitochondria. **[3]**

**10.** After a meal, a sample of blood is taken from the hepatic portal vein and another from the hepatic vein.

**(a)** Predict which sample has the higher concentration of glucose. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain your prediction. **[3]**

**11.** Distinguish between absorption and assimilation, using glucose as an example. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The small intestine (the ileum) [1]. **[1]**

**4.** The uptake [1] and use of nutrients from the blood by the cells of the body [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two of: diffusion; osmosis; active transport [1 each]. **[2]**

**6.**

**(a)** Lacteal [1].

**(b)** Fatty acids [1] and glycerol [1].

**(c)** Any three of: the wall is one cell thick, so the diffusion distance is short [1]; microvilli increase the surface area [1]; a network of capillaries carries away the absorbed nutrients [1]; villi are numerous and increase the internal surface area of the ileum [1]; a lacteal absorbs fat products [1].

**(d)** The blood carries the absorbed glucose and amino acids away [1], so the concentration in the blood stays low, which keeps a steep concentration gradient so that more diffuses in [1]. **[8]**

**7.**

**(a)** 10 x 10 = 100 cm squared [1].

**(b)** Area of each strip's two sides = 2 x (10 x 1) = 20 cm squared [1]. For 20 strips = 400 cm squared [1].

**(c)** The strips add area on top of the flat piece, giving a much bigger total area (500 cm squared against 100 cm squared) [1]. Villi do the same for the lining of the ileum, so more nutrients can be absorbed at the same time [1]. **[5]**

**8.**

**(a)** 2.0 + 7.0 = 9.0 dm cubed [1].

**(b)** 8.4 divided by 9.0 [1] x 100 = 93.3 per cent (accept 93) [1].

**(c)** In diarrhoea, water is not absorbed from the intestines [1], so it leaves the body in the faeces and the body loses too much water [1]. **[5]**

**9.** The mitochondria carry out aerobic respiration, which releases energy [1]. The energy is used for active transport [1] of nutrients such as glucose against a concentration gradient into the cells [1]. **[3]**

**10.**

**(a)** The hepatic portal vein [1].

**(b)** The hepatic portal vein carries blood from the ileum, where glucose has been absorbed [1]. The liver removes excess glucose and stores it as glycogen [1], so the blood in the hepatic vein, leaving the liver, has a lower concentration [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Absorption is the movement of glucose from the intestine into the cells lining it, and then into the blood [1]. Assimilation is the uptake of glucose from the blood by the body cells [1], where it is used, for example in respiration [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using absorption and assimilation as if they were the same.
- Writing that glucose enters the lacteal.
- Not explaining that a thin wall gives a short diffusion distance.
- Writing "large surface area" without saying that this allows more absorption in the same time.
- Saying that the hepatic portal vein carries blood away from the liver.
