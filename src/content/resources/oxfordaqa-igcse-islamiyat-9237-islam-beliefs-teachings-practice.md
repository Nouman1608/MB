**What these questions cover.** Topic 3.1.1 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat 9237 is
examined in Paper 1 Section A (25 marks), whose 12-mark question uses a prescribed passage from
Section 6 of the specification. This page gives more practice than one exam section, across every
part of 3.1.1: Tawhid and shirk, the key beliefs, the names of Allah, angels, Al-Qadr, life after
death, prophethood and holy books, and the Rightly Guided Caliphs.

## Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Name the angel who will blow the trumpet to announce the Day of Judgement. **[1]**

**2.** Name **one** prophet, other than Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh), to whom a holy book was revealed,
and name that book. **[1]**

**3.** Give **two** of the names of Allah named in the specification and the meaning of each.
**[2]**

**4.** Give **two** forms of shirk. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **two** Muslim beliefs about the Kiraman Katibeen. **[4]**

**6.** Explain **two** reasons why Muslims believe Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh) is the Seal of the
Prophets. **[4]**

**7.** Explain **two** Muslim death rituals and why they are important. **[4]**

## Section B: longer-answer questions

**8.** Explain **two** reasons why belief in Risalah (prophethood) is important for Muslims.
Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority in your answer. **[6]**

**9.** Explain **two** ways in which belief in the Day of Judgement influences Muslims today.
Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority in your answer. **[6]**

**10.** Explain **two** reasons why the four Rightly Guided Caliphs are important to Muslims.
Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority in your answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** The 1- and 2-mark questions give one mark for each correct answer.
The 4-mark questions give one mark for each point identified and one more for explaining it, up to
two marks for each of the two. The 6-mark questions award those four AO1 marks plus up to two AO2
marks for applying a source of authority: **one** mark for a general, relevant and accurate use,
**two** marks for a specific, accurate use applied directly to the question. The 12-mark questions
are AO2 and are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points of significance; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent use of the passage, applied directly |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point of significance is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not referred to** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

Other correct answers of the same quality receive the same credit.

**1.** Isra'fil (AS) [1].

**2.** Any one pairing [1]: Musa (AS) and the Tawrat; Dawud (AS) and the Zabur; Isa (AS) and the
Injil; Ibraheem (AS) and the Suhuf.

**3.** Any two, [1] each for a name with its meaning: Al-Khaliq, the Creator; Ar-Rehman, the Most
Gracious (Entirely Merciful); Ar-Raheem, the Most Merciful (Especially Merciful); Al-Aleem, the
All-Knowing; Qudrah, Allah's power over all things (omnipotence).

**4.** Any two, [1] each: worshipping anything or anyone besides Allah, such as idols; believing
that a created being shares Allah's power to create, control or provide; giving Allah's unique
names or attributes to created beings; showing off in worship (riya'), described as a minor form
of shirk.

**5.** For each of two beliefs: belief identified [1] and explained [1].

- They are the **two honourable recorders** assigned to each person [1], one recording good deeds
  and one recording bad deeds [1].
- Their record is **complete and accurate** [1], so nothing a person does, even in private, is left
  out (Qur'an 82:10-12) [1].
- The record will be **presented on the Day of Judgement** [1], when each person receives their
  book of deeds, so it affects their eternal reward [1].
- Belief in them makes Muslims **conscious of their actions** [1], encouraging good conduct and
  repentance [1].

**6.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- The **Qur'an states it** [1]: "Muhammad is not the father of [any] one of your men, but [he is]
  the Messenger of Allah and seal of the prophets" (Qur'an 33:40) [1].
- The **message is complete** [1]: the Qur'an is the final revelation, preserved, so no new prophet
  is needed [1].
- His message is **for all humanity** [1], unlike earlier prophets sent to particular peoples, so
  it does not need replacing [1].
- His **Sunnah gives a complete example** [1] of how to live by the Qur'an in every part of life
  [1].

**7.** For each of two rituals: ritual identified [1] and its importance explained [1].

- Encouraging the dying person to say the **Shahadah** [1], so that their last words affirm belief
  in Allah [1].
- **Ghusl**, washing the body [1], to show respect and ensure the body is clean before burial [1].
- Wrapping in a simple **white shroud (kafan)** [1], showing that all are equal in death, rich or
  poor [1].
- The **funeral prayer (Salat al-Janazah)** [1], in which the community asks Allah to forgive the
  deceased [1].
- **Prompt burial** with the body facing the Qiblah [1], reflecting the belief that the soul moves
  on to Barzakh and the body returns to the earth [1].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**:
one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- Prophets **bring Allah's guidance** [1]; without them people would not know how to worship or
  live rightly [1].
- Prophets are **examples to follow** [1], showing obedience and patience in hardship, as Ibraheem
  (AS) and Musa (AS) did [1].
- Belief in **all the prophets** is part of faith [1], so Muslims respect Adam (AS), Ibraheem (AS),
  Musa (AS) and Isa (AS) as messengers of one message of Tawhid [1].
- Belief in Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh) as **the final prophet** [1] means his Sunnah is followed
  alongside the Qur'an [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "We make no distinction between any of His messengers" (Qur'an 2:285), so Muslims must accept
  every prophet, which shows that Risalah unites all revelation as one message [2].
- "There has certainly come to you a Messenger from among yourselves ... to the believers is kind
  and merciful" (Qur'an 9:128), showing that the Prophet's (pbuh) care for believers makes him a
  model to follow [2].

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two influences, the influence identified [1] and explained [1].
AO2 **[2]**: as in question 8.

Influences (AO1):

- Muslims **try to do good deeds** [1], knowing every action will be judged [1].
- Muslims **avoid sin and seek forgiveness** [1], repenting before death, since there is no chance
  to return [1].
- Muslims **treat others justly** [1], because wrongs done to others will be accounted for [1].
- Muslims face **hardship with patience** [1], because reward in the Hereafter outweighs worldly
  loss [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "So whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil
  will see it" (Qur'an 99:7-8), so Muslims value even small good deeds and avoid small sins [2].
- Qur'an 80:34-37 teaches that on that Day a person will flee from their own family, so Muslims
  understand that they are responsible for their own deeds and cannot rely on others [2].

**10.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2
**[2]**: as in question 8.

Reasons (AO1):

- They **kept the community united** after the Prophet's (pbuh) death [1]; Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA)
  led the community through the challenge of those who refused Zakah and the false prophets [1].
- They **preserved the Qur'an** [1]: it was collected into one volume under Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA),
  and Hazrat Uthman (RA) sent standard copies to the main cities [1].
- They **set examples of just leadership** [1]: Hazrat Umar (RA) organised government and was known
  for his justice and care for the poor [1].
- They are **models of knowledge and piety** [1]: Hazrat Ali (RA) was renowned for his learning and
  closeness to the Prophet (pbuh) [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- In the hadith of the farewell counsel (40 Hadith an-Nawawi 28), the Prophet (pbuh) told Muslims
  to keep to his Sunnah "and to the Sunnah of the Khulafa ar-Rashideen (the Rightly Guided
  Caliphs)", so Muslims follow their example as part of following the Prophet (pbuh) [2].

**11.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- Tawhid is the **foundation** of all the key beliefs: angels, books, prophets and the Hereafter
  all depend on belief in one Allah.
- It is the first part of the **Shahadah**, so it is how a person enters Islam.
- **Shirk** is its opposite and the gravest sin, so Tawhid defines what Muslims must avoid.
- It shapes **worship**: all worship is for Allah alone, which is why Muslims pray directly to
  Him without intermediaries.
- It gives **unity and equality**: one Creator for all people supports the idea of one Ummah.
- Other beliefs, such as Al-Qadr or Akhirah, could also be argued to be central, but each rests on
  belief in Allah's oneness and power.

*Use of the passage*

- "He is Allah, [who is] One" states Tawhid directly.
- "The Eternal Refuge" shows that all creation depends on Allah, while He depends on nothing, so
  Muslims turn to Him alone in need.
- "He neither begets nor is born" rejects any idea that Allah has a son or parent, so Muslims
  reject beliefs that make a created being divine.
- "Nor is there to Him any equivalent" means nothing shares His names or attributes, which
  supports Tawhid of names and attributes.

**12.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- Belief in Akhirah is one of the **key beliefs**, so it is part of faith itself.
- It gives life **purpose**: this life is a test, and deeds have eternal consequences.
- It encourages **personal responsibility**, since each person is judged for their own deeds.
- It influences **daily choices**: honesty in business, kindness to family, prayer and charity.
- It offers **hope and justice**: wrongs not punished in this life will be judged by Allah.
- Some Muslims might stress that intention and Allah's mercy also matter, not only fear of
  judgement.

*Use of the passage*

- "A man will flee from his own siblings ... his mother and his father ... his wife and his
  children" shows that no family tie will help on that Day, so Muslims must prepare for themselves.
- "Every man, that Day, will be a matter adequate for him" shows how completely each person will be
  concerned with their own account.
- "Faces ... bright. Laughing, rejoicing at good news" describes the reward of believers, which
  encourages good deeds.
- "Faces ... will have upon them dust. Blackness will cover them" describes the fate of the wicked,
  a warning that shapes behaviour.

An answer that explains a range of points, reasons about their implications for Muslim life, and
uses the passage directly reaches Level 4.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving one answer where two are asked for.
- Naming a belief without explaining it in the 4-mark questions.
- Vague references ("the Qur'an says") that limit AO2 credit to one mark.
- Making only one point, or ignoring the passage, in the 12-mark questions.
