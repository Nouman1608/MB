## 1. Economic growth and the policy objective

**Economic growth** is an increase in the real output of an economy over time, usually measured as the percentage change in real GDP. **GDP (Gross Domestic Product)** is the total value of goods and services produced within a country in a period.

Governments aim for **sustained** growth: steady growth close to the economy's long-run trend, avoiding booms that end in recession. Steady growth supports rising living standards, employment and tax revenue, while avoiding the inflation and instability of rapid, unsustainable expansion.

## 2. Real and nominal GDP, and growth rates

- **Nominal GDP** is measured at current prices. It rises if output rises **or** if prices rise.
- **Real GDP** is measured at constant prices, removing the effect of inflation. It changes only when output changes.

**Real GDP = nominal GDP ÷ price index × 100**

**Growth rate (%) = (real GDP this year − real GDP last year) ÷ real GDP last year × 100**

**Worked example 1 (invented Country M).**

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Nominal GDP | 800 bn | 864 bn |
| Price index (year 1 = 100) | 100 | 104 |
| Real GDP (year 1 prices) | 800 bn | 864 ÷ 104 × 100 = **830.8 bn** |

- Nominal growth = (864 − 800) ÷ 800 = **8.0%**.
- Real growth = (830.8 − 800) ÷ 800 = **3.85%**.

More than half of the nominal rise is inflation. A quick check: (1 + real growth) × (1 + inflation) = 1.0385 × 1.04 = 1.08, the nominal growth factor.

**Changes in GDP over time.** A fall in the **growth rate** is not a fall in GDP. If growth slows from 3% to 1%, output is still rising, just more slowly. GDP falls only when the growth rate is negative.

## 3. GDP per capita

**GDP per capita = GDP ÷ population**

It is a better guide to average living standards than total GDP, because a country whose population grows quickly needs more output just to stand still.

**Worked example 2 (Country M continued).** Population rises from 40.0 million to 40.4 million (+1%).

- Real GDP per capita, year 1 = 800 bn ÷ 40.0 m = **20,000**.
- Year 2 = 830.8 bn ÷ 40.4 m = **20,564**.
- Growth of real GDP per capita = **2.82%**, close to the shortcut of real growth minus population growth (3.85 − 1.0 = 2.85).

Average figures hide distribution: real GDP per capita can rise while many households see no gain.

## 4. The economic cycle

Actual output fluctuates around its long-run trend. The **economic cycle** (business cycle) has four stages:

| Stage | Output | Typical signs |
|---|---|---|
| Boom | Growth above trend; output may exceed potential | Falling unemployment, rising inflation, skill shortages, rising imports |
| Downturn (slowdown) | Growth slows | Confidence falls; firms delay investment |
| Recession | Real GDP falls; commonly defined as two consecutive quarters of negative growth | Rising unemployment, falling inflation, business failures |
| Recovery | Real GDP grows again from the trough | Confidence returns; unemployment falls slowly |

**Worked example 3 (invented Country N, real GDP index by quarter).**

| Quarter | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Real GDP | 100.0 | 100.8 | 101.5 | 101.9 | 101.6 | 101.0 | 100.9 | 101.4 | 102.2 |
| Growth on previous quarter (%) | | 0.80 | 0.69 | 0.39 | −0.29 | −0.59 | −0.10 | 0.50 | 0.79 |

- Growth slows over quarters 2 to 4: a downturn while output still rises. The **peak** is quarter 4.
- Growth is negative in quarters 5, 6 and 7, three consecutive falls, so Country N is in **recession**. The **trough** is quarter 7.
- **Recovery** begins in quarter 8. Output passes its previous peak (101.9) only in quarter 9.

## 5. Short run and long run growth

**Short run growth** is an increase in actual output using existing spare capacity, usually driven by a rise in aggregate demand (AD).

**Long run growth** is an increase in the economy's productive capacity (potential output), driven by more or better factors of production or improved technology.

**Diagrams.**

- **PPC:** short run growth is a movement from a point **inside** the PPC towards the curve. Long run growth is an **outward shift** of the PPC.
- **AD/AS:** short run growth is a rightward shift of AD along an upward-sloping SRAS, raising real output (and usually the price level). Long run growth is a rightward shift of **LRAS**, raising potential output and easing pressure on prices.

The distinction matters for policy. Raising AD can lift output only while there is spare capacity. Once output reaches potential, further AD growth mainly raises inflation. Sustained growth needs capacity to grow too.

## 6. Causes of growth

| Short run (demand side) | Long run (supply side) |
|---|---|
| Higher consumer spending, from confidence, rising real incomes or lower interest rates | Larger labour force: population growth, migration, higher participation |
| Higher investment, from better expected profits or cheaper borrowing | Better education and training raising labour productivity |
| Higher government spending or lower taxes | Investment in capital and infrastructure |
| Higher net exports, for example after a fall in the exchange rate | New technology and innovation |
| | Institutions, competition and stable economic policy |

Investment is a cause in both columns: spending on new machinery raises AD now and productive capacity later.

## 7. Consequences of growth

| | Benefits | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| Short run | More jobs and lower unemployment; higher incomes; higher tax revenue and lower benefit spending | Inflation if AD outruns capacity; rising imports worsen the current account |
| Long run | Higher average living standards; resources for public services; easier to reduce poverty and public debt relative to GDP | Pollution and resource depletion; uneven distribution of gains; structural change leaves some workers and regions behind |

**Evaluation.** Whether growth is beneficial depends on its **source** (supply-led growth is more sustainable than credit-fuelled demand), its **pace** (rapid growth beyond capacity creates inflation), its **distribution** and its **environmental cost**. GDP itself also leaves out unpaid work, leisure and the quality of goods.

## Common mistakes

- Using nominal GDP growth as a measure of economic growth.
- Saying GDP fell when only the growth rate fell.
- Calling a slowdown a recession.
- Shifting AD to show long run growth.
- Forgetting population when commenting on living standards.

## Exam technique

- **Calculate:** show the formula, the substitution and the answer to two decimal places, with units (%, bn, per head).
- **Explain:** define the term, apply it to the data and link the chain to output, employment or prices.
- **Explain, with the aid of a diagram:** label axes and curves, mark both equilibria and refer to the diagram in your writing.
- **Evaluate:** distinguish the short run from the long run and make the judgement depend on the source and pace of growth.
