## Section B

**5.** A farmer tests three soils.

| Soil | Water retained after 1 hour (%) | Drainage | Ease of digging |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 15 | Very fast | Easy |
| B | 70 | Very slow, surface puddles | Hard |
| C | 45 | Moderate | Easy |

(a) Identify which soil is most likely to be loam. Give a reason from the table. **[2]**
(b) Explain why soil B could reduce crop growth. **[3]**
(c) Explain **two** reasons, other than those in the table, why loam is a good medium for crop growth.
**[2]**

**6.** Explain why the length of the growing season and daylight hours affect crop yield. **[3]**

**7.** Distinguish between:
(a) subsistence and commercial agriculture; **[2]**
(b) arable and pastoral agriculture. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Sand, silt and clay. **[1]**

**2.** (a) nitrate (NO3-) **[1]**; (b) phosphate (PO4 3-) **[1]**; (c) potassium ion (K+) **[1]**.

**3.** The day-to-day conditions of the atmosphere in a location. **[1]**

**4.** Producing enough food for the present generation **[1]** using methods that ensure future generations
can grow food from the same land **[1]**.

**5.** (a) Soil C **[1]** - it retains a moderate amount of water and drains, and is easy to dig **[1]**.
(b) Slow drainage leads to waterlogging **[1]**, so pore spaces fill with water and lack air **[1]**; roots
cannot get oxygen for respiration, and the soil is hard to cultivate **[1]**.
(c) Any two: contains organic content **[1]**; contains inorganic ions such as nitrate **[1]**; ideal
combination of mineral particles and pore spaces **[1]**. **[max 2]**

**6.** Crops need enough time with suitable temperature and rainfall to grow and ripen **[1]**; more daylight
hours give more time for photosynthesis **[1]**, so more glucose is made for growth and yield is higher
**[1]**.

**7.** (a) Subsistence farming produces food for the farmer's family **[1]**; commercial farming produces
food or crops for sale **[1]**.
(b) Arable farming grows crops **[1]**; pastoral farming rears livestock **[1]**.

**8.** (a) Water drips directly to roots, reducing waste by evaporation, so crops get water even in a dry
season **[1]**. (b) Plants grow with roots in nutrient solution in a controlled environment, so nutrients
and water are optimal all year **[1]**. (c) Trees grown with crops provide shelter and add organic matter
to soil, improving growth **[1]**.

**9.** An organism whose genetic material has been changed by removing, changing or inserting individual
genes **[1]**; e.g. pest resistance, drought tolerance or higher yield **[1]**.

**10.** (a) Some insects have natural resistance **[1]**; they survive spraying and reproduce **[1]**; over
generations most of the population becomes resistant, so the insecticide no longer works **[1]**.
(b) Any one: pest resurgence; harm to pollinators such as bees; water pollution. **[1]**
(c) Biological control (using a natural predator) or crop rotation. **[1]**

**11.** Too much irrigation water evaporates from the soil surface in a hot climate **[1]**, leaving dissolved
salts behind that build up and damage crops **[1]**.

**12.** Any four: silting of rivers **[1]**; desertification **[1]**; landslides or mudslides **[1]**; loss of
habitats and biodiversity **[1]**; reduced crop yields **[1]**; malnutrition and famine **[1]**; displacement
of people **[1]**. **[max 4]**

**13.** Terracing creates flat steps on slopes **[1]**, which slow surface run-off and stop soil being washed
downhill **[1]**. Wind breaks (lines of trees or hedges) reduce wind speed **[1]**, so less soil is blown away
and crops are protected **[1]**.

**14.** **[6]** NPK benefits: exact amounts of nitrate, phosphate and potassium; fast-acting; large yield
increases. NPK limitations: cost; overuse causes leaching into water, nutrient enrichment and
eutrophication; adds no organic matter, so soil structure can decline. Organic benefits: cheap or free
(manure, crop residue, mulch), improve soil structure and water retention, reduce erosion. Organic
limitations: bulky, slow to release nutrients and variable in content. Conclusion: a mix is often most
sustainable.

**15.** **[6]** Harm: cash crops (coffee, cotton, soya beans, palm oil) replace food crops, causing food
shortages; monoculture and intensification reduce biodiversity and pollute soil and water; land for
palm oil and soya may come from deforestation. Good: earn income and foreign exchange, create jobs and can
fund food imports or schools. Conclusion: harm is greatest where food crops are displaced and profits leave
the area; benefits are greater with fair prices and mixed farming.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing data without quoting the figures in the table.
- Giving "fertiliser pollutes water" without the chain leaching → nutrient enrichment → eutrophication.
- In "discuss" questions, giving only benefits or only limitations.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many strategies to increase food production must I know?**
Twelve are listed in syllabus 2.2.3, from
mixed cropping and irrigation to GMOs, controlled environments, fertilisers and pest control.

**Do I need soil percentages?**
No. The 5014 syllabus says knowledge of the percentage composition of soils
is not required.

**What is the difference between pest resistance and pest resurgence?**
Resistance means the pesticide no
longer kills the pest. Resurgence means pest numbers rebound, often because the pesticide also killed the
pest's natural predators.
