These notes condense sub-topic **4(c) Work and power** (statements 4.11 to 4.17) of Pearson Edexcel International
GCSE Physics 4PH1, Issue 4. For explanations and six worked examples, use the
[Work and power study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-4ph1-work-and-power/); for exam-style questions, the
[Work and power practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-4ph1-work-and-power-practice/).

## Equations

```
W = F × d                (work done; d is in the direction of the force)
GPE = m × g × h          (h is the vertical change in height)
KE = ½ × m × v²
P = W / t                (power: work done or energy transferred per second)
```

Rearranged forms you will need:

```
F = W / d       d = W / F
h = GPE / (m × g)       m = GPE / (g × h)
v = √(2 × KE / m)       m = 2 × KE / v²
W = P × t       t = W / P
```

## Facts to recall

- Work done = energy transferred (4.12). Both in joules.
- No work is done if the object does not move, or moves at right angles to the force.
- KE ∝ v²: double the speed, four times the KE; triple the speed, nine times the KE.
- GPE ∝ h and ∝ m.
- Power is the **rate** of energy transfer or of doing work (4.16). 1 W = 1 J/s.
- Same work in less time = more power.

## The energy link (4.15)

For a free fall (no air resistance): **GPE lost = KE gained**, so `m g h = ½ m v²`.

- Mass cancels: `v = √(2 g h)` and `h = v² / (2 g)`.
- With friction or air resistance: **GPE lost = KE gained + work done against friction**.
- For braking on a level road: **work done by brakes = KE lost**, so `F × d = ½ m v²`.

| Situation | Energy statement |
|---|---|
| Object falls, no air resistance | m g h = ½ m v² |
| Object falls, with drag or friction | m g h = ½ m v² + work against friction |
| Thrown up, reaches top | ½ m v² = m g h |
| Brakes stop a car | F × d = ½ m v² |
| Lifting at steady speed | work done by the lifting force = m g h |

## Measuring personal power (stairs)

Measure mass (scales), **vertical** height of the stairs (tape measure, step height × number of steps) and time
(stopwatch). Work done = m g h; power = m g h / t. The result underestimates the body's total power, because some
energy heats the body.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Work done | Energy transferred when a force moves an object in the direction of the force |
| Joule (J) | Work done when a 1 N force moves through 1 m |
| Gravitational potential energy | Energy in the gravitational store, changed by m g h when height changes by h |
| Kinetic energy | Energy in the kinetic store of a moving object, ½ m v² |
| Gravitational field strength, g | Force per kilogram from gravity (about 10 N/kg on Earth) |
| Power | Rate of doing work or transferring energy |
| Watt (W) | One joule per second |
| Work done against friction | Energy transferred to the thermal store of the surroundings by friction |

## Exam traps

- Using the slope length instead of the vertical height in m g h.
- Squaring ½ × v, or forgetting to square v at all.
- Stopping at v² and forgetting the square root.
- Saying holding a weight still "does work on it".
- Time in minutes in P = W / t.
- In a friction question, setting GPE lost equal to KE gained: friction takes some of the energy.
- Writing "power is the energy" rather than "the energy transferred per second".

## Self-test

Take g = 10 N/kg.

1. A 250 N force pushes a box 3.0 m along the floor. Calculate the work done.
2. Calculate the GPE gained by a 2.0 kg bag lifted through 1.2 m.
3. Calculate the KE of a 0.50 kg ball moving at 12 m/s.
4. An apple falls 1.8 m from a tree. Calculate its speed just before landing (ignore air resistance).
5. A ball is thrown straight up at 6.0 m/s. Calculate the maximum height it reaches.
6. A motor does 4800 J of work in 1.0 minute. Calculate its power.
7. A car's speed triples. By what factor does its kinetic energy change?
8. Explain why no work is done on a box that is held still.
9. State what is meant by power.
10. A 1200 W motor runs for 30 s. Calculate the energy it transfers.
11. A car of mass 800 kg at 20 m/s brakes with a constant 4000 N force. Calculate its braking distance.
12. A child's speed at the bottom of a slide is lower than the value calculated from m g h = ½ m v². Explain why.

**Answers:** 1. W = 250 × 3.0 = 750 J. 2. GPE = 2.0 × 10 × 1.2 = 24 J. 3. KE = 0.5 × 0.50 × 12² = 36 J.
4. v = √(2 × 10 × 1.8) = √36 = 6.0 m/s. 5. h = 6.0² ÷ (2 × 10) = 36 ÷ 20 = 1.8 m. 6. P = 4800 ÷ 60 = 80 W.
7. It increases nine times (3²). 8. The box does not move, so there is no distance moved in the direction of the force
and no energy is transferred to it. 9. The rate of doing work, or the energy transferred per second. 10. W = P × t =
1200 × 30 = 36 000 J. 11. KE = 0.5 × 800 × 20² = 160 000 J; d = 160 000 ÷ 4000 = 40 m. 12. Some of the GPE is
transferred by work done against friction (and air resistance) to the thermal store of the slide, child and air, so
less than the full GPE becomes kinetic energy.
