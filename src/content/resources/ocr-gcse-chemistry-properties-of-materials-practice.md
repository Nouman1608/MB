## Questions

**1.** State the number of covalent bonds that one carbon atom forms. **[1]**

**2.** Explain why carbon forms far more compounds than any other element. **[2]**

**3.** Use these values to answer the question.

| Substance | Melting point / °C | Boiling point / °C |
|---|---|---|
| K | −101 | −34 |
| L | 44 | 280 |
| M | 17 | 118 |
| N | 801 | 1413 |

**(a)** State the physical state of K and of L at 20 °C. **[2]**
**(b)** Which substance is a liquid at 200 °C? **[1]**
**(c)** Identify the substance with the strongest forces between its particles. Explain your choice. **[2]**

**4.** Diamond and graphite both contain only carbon atoms.

**(a)** Only one of these two materials could serve as an electrode. Explain, in terms of electrons, which one and why the other could not. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how this shows that atoms themselves do not have the bulk properties of a material. **[2]**

**5.** A flat drawing of a model shows carbon atoms, each joined to three others, in a single hexagonal sheet one atom thick.

**(a)** Name this form of carbon. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why this material conducts electricity. **[2]**
**(c)** Carbon nanotubes are made of this sheet rolled into a cylinder. Suggest why nanotubes are used to reinforce the frames of racing bicycles. **[2]**

**6.** Use these values: water boils at 100 °C; sodium chloride boils above 1400 °C. A student says: "When water boils, the bonds between the hydrogen and oxygen atoms break." Explain why the student is wrong, and why water boils at a much lower temperature than sodium chloride. **[4]**

**7.** Five materials, V to Z, are tested.

| Material | Melting point / °C | Electrical conduction | Other observation |
|---|---|---|---|
| V | 1700 | Not as solid or liquid | Very hard |
| W | 2852 | Only when molten | Brittle |
| X | −95 | Not as solid or liquid | Liquid at room temperature |
| Y | 660 | As solid and liquid | Can be hammered into sheets |
| Z | Softens from 105 to 120 | Not as solid | Long-chain solid, flexible |

Identify the type of structure in each material: ionic, simple molecular, giant covalent, metallic or polymer. **[5]**

**8.** A nanoparticle of a catalyst is 25 nm across. Take the diameter of one atom as 0.25 nm.

**(a)** Write 25 nm in metres, in standard form. **[1]**
**(b)** Estimate the number of atoms that would fit side by side across the nanoparticle. **[2]**
**(c)** A grain of sand is 0.5 mm across. Calculate how many times wider it is than the nanoparticle. **[2]**

**9.** A catalyst is made either as cubes of side 60 nm or as cubes of side 6 nm.

**(a)** Calculate the surface area, the volume and the surface area to volume ratio of one 60 nm cube. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of one 6 nm cube, and state how many times larger it is than in part (a). **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the same mass of catalyst works faster as 6 nm cubes. **[2]**

**10.** A student investigates how particle size affects dissolving. She drops an effervescent tablet into 100 cm³ of water at 20 °C and times how long it takes to dissolve. Her results:

| Tablet form | Trial 1 / s | Trial 2 / s | Trial 3 / s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole | 62 | 65 | 59 |
| Four pieces | 41 | 44 | 38 |
| Powder | 18 | 31 | 20 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean time for the whole tablet and for the four pieces. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the anomalous result. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the mean time for the powder, leaving out the anomalous result. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain the trend in the results. **[2]**

**11.** A company plans to add zinc oxide nanoparticles to a clear varnish for outdoor wooden furniture. The nanoparticles absorb ultraviolet light, which otherwise damages wood. Evaluate this plan. Refer to the properties of nanoparticles, the benefits and the possible risks. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** **Four** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A number on its own answers this; extra detail that is wrong risks contradicting a correct answer.

**2.** Carbon forms four covalent bonds, including strong bonds to other carbon atoms [1]. So it forms chains and rings of many lengths, giving families of similar compounds [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Two separate points are needed for two marks: the bonding of carbon, and the chains, rings or families that result.

**3. (a)** K: **gas**, as 20 °C is above its boiling point of −34 °C [1]. L: **solid**, as 20 °C is below its melting point [1].
**(b)** **L** (200 °C lies between 44 °C and 280 °C) [1].
**(c)** **N** [1]. It has the highest melting and boiling points, so the most energy is needed to overcome the forces between its particles [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* With negative values, write the comparison out (for example "20 is above −34") so the reasoning is visible and the direction cannot be misread.

**4. (a)** In graphite each carbon bonds to three others, leaving one delocalised electron per atom that can move through the layers [1]. Diamond's carbons each bond to four others, so no outer electron is left free to move [1].
**(b)** Both contain identical carbon atoms, yet one conducts and the other does not [1], so conduction depends on how the atoms are bonded and arranged, not on the atoms themselves [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say "delocalised electrons"; answers that call the moving particles in graphite "ions" or "atoms" describe the wrong particle.

**5. (a)** **Graphene** [1].
**(b)** Each carbon uses three electrons in bonds [1], so one electron per carbon is delocalised and moves across the sheet carrying charge [1].
**(c)** Nanotubes are very strong because of strong covalent bonds along their length [1], and they are light, so the frame gains strength without much added mass [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In "suggest" questions, link each property to the use; "strong and light" alone earns less than "strong, so the frame resists bending".

**6.** Water is a simple molecular substance; boiling overcomes the weak intermolecular forces between molecules [1], and the covalent bonds inside the molecules do not break [1]. Sodium chloride is a giant ionic lattice with strong electrostatic attraction between ions [1]. Far more energy is needed to overcome these, so its boiling point is much higher [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the force in both substances; an answer that only says "water has weaker bonds" is too vague to gain much.

**7.** V: **giant covalent** [1]. W: **ionic** [1]. X: **simple molecular** [1]. Y: **metallic** [1]. Z: **polymer** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the conduction column first: conducting as a solid points to a metal, conducting only when molten points to ionic.

**8. (a)** **2.5 × 10⁻⁸ m** [1].
**(b)** 25 ÷ 0.25 [1] = **100 atoms** [1].
**(c)** 0.5 mm = 5 × 10⁻⁴ m; 5 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 2.5 × 10⁻⁸ [1] = **20 000 times** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert both sizes to the same unit before dividing, and write the conversion down so your method is clear.

**9. (a)** Surface area = 6 × 60² = **21 600 nm²** [1]. Volume = 60³ = **216 000 nm³** [1]. Ratio = 21 600 ÷ 216 000 = **0.1 nm⁻¹** (0.1 : 1) [1].
**(b)** 6 × 6² ÷ 6³ = 216 ÷ 216 = **1.0 nm⁻¹** [1], which is **10 times larger** [1].
**(c)** The same mass as 6 nm cubes has a much larger total surface area (ten times as much) [1], so more catalyst atoms are at the surface where reacting particles collide with it [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Units on area (nm²) and volume (nm³) are worth writing; a ratio with the wrong power of ten usually comes from cubing or squaring the wrong number.

**10. (a)** Whole: (62 + 65 + 59) ÷ 3 = **62 s** [1]. Four pieces: (41 + 44 + 38) ÷ 3 = **41 s** [1].
**(b)** **31 s** (powder, trial 2) [1].
**(c)** (18 + 20) ÷ 2 [1] = **19 s** [1].
**(d)** The smaller the pieces, the shorter the dissolving time [1], because smaller pieces have a larger surface area to volume ratio, so more of the tablet is in contact with water [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* If you leave out an anomaly, divide by the number of values you actually used; dividing 38 by 3 is a common slip.

**11.** Any eight creditworthy points, including benefits, risks and a judgement:

- Zinc oxide nanoparticles absorb ultraviolet light, protecting the wood from damage [1].
- They are too small to scatter much visible light, so the varnish stays clear rather than white [1].
- Their large surface area to volume ratio means a small mass gives good coverage [1].
- Furniture lasts longer, so less wood is replaced [1].
- Risk: as varnish wears or is sanded, nanoparticles may be released and breathed in [1].
- Risk: their large surface area may make them more reactive or toxic than bulk zinc oxide [1].
- Risk: rain may wash particles into soil and water, where they could harm organisms [1].
- Judgement: long-term effects are not fully known, so the plan is reasonable only with safety testing, labelling and guidance on sanding [1]. **[8]**

*Examiner insight:* An "evaluate" answer needs both sides and a reasoned conclusion; a list of benefits with no risks, or no final judgement, limits the credit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that boiling breaks the covalent bonds in a simple molecular liquid.
- Calling the mobile particles in graphite "ions" instead of delocalised electrons.
- Reading negative melting and boiling points in the wrong direction.
- Giving a structure type without using the evidence in the table (conduction, hardness, melting point).
- Mixing nm, mm and m in one division.
- Forgetting to square for area or cube for volume.
- Dividing by the wrong number after leaving out an anomalous result.
- Listing nanoparticle risks without linking them to size or surface area.
