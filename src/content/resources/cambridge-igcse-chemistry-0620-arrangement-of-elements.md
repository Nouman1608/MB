## What the syllabus asks for

**Core** (all candidates):

- Describe the Periodic Table as an arrangement of elements in periods and groups and in order of increasing proton number / atomic number.
- Describe the change from metallic to non-metallic character across a period.
- Describe the relationship between group number and the charge of the ions formed from elements in that group.
- Explain similarities in the chemical properties of elements in the same group in terms of their electronic configuration.
- Explain how the position of an element in the Periodic Table can be used to predict its properties.

**Supplement** (Extended candidates only):

- Identify trends in groups, given information about the elements.

## Periods, groups and proton number

The elements are arranged in order of **increasing proton number** (atomic number), not relative atomic mass.

- A **period** is a horizontal row. The period number equals the number of occupied electron shells.
- A **group** is a vertical column. For Groups I to VII the group number equals the number of outer-shell electrons. Group VIII, the noble gases, have a full outer shell.

So position and electronic configuration carry the same information:

| Element | Proton number | Electronic configuration | Period | Group |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lithium | 3 | 2,1 | 2 | I |
| Magnesium | 12 | 2,8,2 | 3 | II |
| Aluminium | 13 | 2,8,3 | 3 | III |
| Sulfur | 16 | 2,8,6 | 3 | VI |
| Chlorine | 17 | 2,8,7 | 3 | VII |
| Potassium | 19 | 2,8,8,1 | 4 | I |
| Calcium | 20 | 2,8,8,2 | 4 | II |

*Reading it:* chlorine has three occupied shells, so it is in Period 3, and seven outer electrons, so it is in Group VII.

## Metallic to non-metallic across a period

Moving left to right across a period, the elements change from metals to non-metals. Period 3 shows the full sequence:

| Na | Mg | Al | Si | P | S | Cl | Ar |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Metal | Metal | Metal | Non-metal (giant covalent) | Non-metal | Non-metal | Non-metal | Noble gas |

Evidence for "metallic character" that you can quote:

- Metals conduct electricity and form **positive** ions; their oxides are **basic**.
- Non-metals are generally poor conductors and form **negative** ions or covalent bonds; their oxides are **acidic**.

The change is gradual rather than sudden. Silicon, in the middle of the period, is a non-metal with a giant covalent structure.

## Group number and ion charge

Atoms lose or gain electrons to reach a full outer shell, like a noble gas. Elements on the left lose their few outer electrons; elements on the right gain the few they need.

| Group | Outer electrons | Electrons lost or gained | Ion charge | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | 1 | Lose 1 | 1+ | Na⁺ |
| II | 2 | Lose 2 | 2+ | Mg²⁺ |
| III | 3 | Lose 3 | 3+ | Al³⁺ |
| V | 5 | Gain 3 | 3− | N³⁻ |
| VI | 6 | Gain 2 | 2− | O²⁻ |
| VII | 7 | Gain 1 | 1− | Cl⁻ |

Group IV elements do not usually form simple ions (carbon and silicon form covalent bonds), and Group VIII elements do not form ions at all. Transition elements are not in a numbered group and can form ions of more than one charge, as covered in 8.4.

Ion charges let you write formulae: calcium (Ca²⁺) and chlorine (Cl⁻) give CaCl₂; aluminium (Al³⁺) and oxygen (O²⁻) give Al₂O₃.

## Why a group shares chemical properties

Chemical reactions involve the **outer-shell electrons**. Elements in the same group have the **same number of outer-shell electrons**, so they react in similar ways and form compounds with similar formulae:

- Lithium, sodium and potassium each have one outer electron; each forms a 1+ ion and a chloride with the formula MCl (LiCl, NaCl, KCl).
- Chlorine, bromine and iodine each have seven outer electrons; each forms a 1− ion and a sodium salt NaX.

A complete explanation names the number: "same number of outer-shell electrons (one in Group I)" scores better than "similar electrons".

## Using position to predict properties

Position gives you three things: metal or non-metal (from the period trend), the ion charge (from the group), and the general chemistry (from the elements above and below).

### Worked example 1

*Strontium is in Group II, Period 5. Predict three properties.*

- It is a **metal** (far left of its period).
- It forms **Sr²⁺** ions (Group II, two outer electrons).
- Its chloride is **SrCl₂** and its oxide **SrO**, which is **basic** (metal oxide).

### Worked example 2

*Selenium is in Group VI, Period 4. Predict the formula of its compound with sodium.*

Group VI → gains 2 electrons → Se²⁻. Sodium forms Na⁺. Two Na⁺ balance one Se²⁻: **Na₂Se**.

## Identifying trends from given information (Supplement)

You may be given data for elements you have not studied and asked to describe a trend or predict a missing value. Real data for Group VII:

| Element | Melting point / °C |
|---|---|
| Fluorine | −220 |
| Chlorine | −101 |
| Bromine | −7 |
| Iodine | 114 |

**Trend:** melting point increases down the group. **Prediction for astatine** (below iodine): higher than 114 °C, so a solid at room temperature.

Method for any data question:

1. State the direction of the trend ("increases down the group").
2. Quote values to support it.
3. Make the prediction in the same direction, with a sensible value or range, and the state at room temperature if asked.
4. If one value breaks the pattern, say so rather than ignoring it.

## Common mistakes

- **Saying the table is arranged by mass.** It is by proton number.
- **Mixing up groups and periods.** Groups are columns (same outer electrons); periods are rows (same number of shells).
- **Giving Group VI ions a 6− charge.** Non-metals gain (8 − group number) electrons: Group VI forms 2− ions.
- **Explaining similar properties with "same number of shells".** That describes a period, not a group.
- **Predicting without reasons** when the command word is "explain".

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Describe** the arrangement: rows are periods, columns are groups, order of increasing proton number.
- **Explain** group similarities: same number of outer-shell electrons.
- **Predict**: give the property and, if asked, base it on the group or period position.
- **Identify** a trend (Extended): direction plus data, then the prediction.
