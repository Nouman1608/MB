**These notes compress Cambridge 9489 AS topic 1, France, 1774-1814, into what you need to recall under time pressure.** The topic has four key questions: the causes and immediate consequences of 1789 (1.1), how France became a republic by 1792 (1.2), how well France was governed in 1793-99 (1.3) and why Napoleon rose and fell (1.4). For full explanations, use the [France, 1774-1814 study guide](/resources/a-level-world-history-france-1774-1814-study-guide/); for exam-style work, use the [France, 1774-1814 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-france-1774-1814-practice/).

**Rotation (2027-2029 syllabus):** Paper 1 (Historical Sources) in 2027; Paper 2 (Outline Study) in 2028 and 2029.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ancien Régime | The pre-1789 system of absolute monarchy, privilege and corporate bodies |
| *Taille* | Main direct tax, largely paid by the Third Estate |
| *Parlements* | Law courts that registered royal edicts and could protest against them |
| *Cahiers de doléances* | Lists of grievances drawn up for the 1789 elections |
| Assignats | Paper notes backed by confiscated Church land; lost value through over-issue |
| Refractory priests | Clergy who refused the oath to the Civil Constitution |
| Sans-culottes | Politically active Parisian artisans and shopkeepers |
| Mountain / Plain | Radical Jacobin deputies / uncommitted centre in the Convention |
| Federalist revolts | 1793 provincial risings (Lyon, Marseille, Toulon) against Paris |
| *Levée en masse* | Mass conscription decree, August 1793 |
| Maximum | Price (and wage) controls of 1793 |
| Thermidorians | Deputies who overthrew Robespierre and dismantled the Terror |
| Two-Thirds Decree | 1795 rule that two-thirds of the new councils must be former Convention members |
| Prefect | Centrally appointed head of a department under Napoleon |

## The three assemblies -- do not mix them up

| Assembly | Dates | Achievement | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| National (Constituent) Assembly | 1789-91 | August Decrees, Declaration, departments, Civil Constitution, 1791 constitution | Left a King who did not accept the settlement |
| Legislative Assembly | Oct 1791-Sept 1792 | Declared war; faced royal vetoes | Swept away on 10 August |
| National Convention | Sept 1792-Oct 1795 | Republic, regicide, Terror, Thermidor, Year III constitution | Purges and coups replaced consent |

## Key question summaries

**1.1 Causes.** Long-term: privilege, an inefficient tax system, Enlightenment criticism, American war debt. Short-term: the failure of Calonne and Brienne, the Parlement's resistance, the 1788 harvest and bread prices. The "revolt of the nobility" (1787-88) opened the crisis; the Third Estate then turned it against privilege.

**1.2 Republic.** Reforms created winners (peasants freed from dues, purchasers of Church land) and losers (non-juring clergy, nobles). Louis's flight, the war and fear of invasion made the King appear a traitor; the Girondins' war gamble turned into the monarchy's fall.

**1.3 Government.** Judge by criteria: did it keep France secure, solvent and stable? The Terror saved the Republic militarily but at a cost of liberty and lives; the Directory ended terror but depended on coups (Fructidor 1797, Floréal 1798) and the army.

**1.4 Napoleon.** Rise: reputation, the weakness of the Directory, Sieyès's plot. Control: prefects, police, censorship, plebiscites. Reforms: Code, lycées, Bank of France, Concordat. Fall: conscription, economic strain from the Continental System, the Spanish "ulcer", nationalism, Russia, Leipzig.

## Exam traps

- **"Bourgeois revolution" as the whole story.** Sources from nobles and the Parlement in 1787-88 show privileged resistance began the crisis.
- **Dates of the republic.** The monarchy fell on 10 August 1792 but was abolished on 21 September 1792.
- **Robespierre as dictator.** He was one of twelve members of the Committee of Public Safety; the Convention could and did remove him.
- **The Directory as pure failure.** Credit financial stabilisation (the writing-off of two-thirds of the debt in 1797) and military success in Italy.
- **Napoleon's battles.** Write about their effect inside France (taxes, conscription, trade, opinion), not the campaigns.
- **Paper 1 (a).** Compare two sources and explain why they differ through their authors and dates; content-matching alone stays in the lower levels.

## Self-test

1. Which body refused Calonne's land tax in 1787? *The Assembly of Notables.*
2. What did the Parlement of Paris argue in 1787? *That only the Estates-General could approve new taxes.*
3. Why was the voting method at the Estates-General so important? *Voting by order let the two privileged estates outvote the Third; voting by head, with the Third doubled, would give it a majority.*
4. What did the Tennis Court Oath commit deputies to? *Not to separate until France had a constitution.*
5. Name two reforms of the Constituent Assembly. *Any two: departments with elected officials; assignats; uniform taxation; elected judges and juries; the Civil Constitution of the Clergy.*
6. Why did the Civil Constitution of the Clergy weaken the Revolution? *The oath split the clergy and gave Catholic opponents, especially in the west, a cause.*
7. What happened at the Champ de Mars? *The National Guard fired on petitioners demanding the King's removal, July 1791.*
8. Which group pressed hardest for war in 1792, and why? *The Girondins, believing war would expose traitors and spread the Revolution.*
9. What two measures of September 1793 were central to the Terror? *The Law of Suspects and the General Maximum.*
10. Why did Robespierre fall in July 1794? *Victory (Fleurus) made the Terror seem unnecessary and fellow deputies feared they would be next.*
11. What was the Constitution of the Year III designed to prevent? *Both dictatorship and popular rule: five Directors, two councils and property qualifications.*
12. Give two reasons Napoleon lost support inside France after 1810. *Any two: conscription; economic hardship from the Continental System; losses in Spain and Russia; heavy taxation.*
