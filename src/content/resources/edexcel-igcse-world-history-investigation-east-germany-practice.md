## Enquiry 1 — the Wall and the stable years, 1958–87

**1(a)** Describe **two** features of the refugee problem in the GDR before 1961. **[6]**

**Source A** (invented). From an article in an SED newspaper, August 1961.

> "Our state has secured its border against Western agents, speculators and warmongers. Peace in Europe is
> safer tonight, and the citizens of our republic can build socialism without interference."

**Source B** (invented). From the diary of an East Berlin nurse, August 1961.

> "My sister lives four streets away, on the other side. This morning soldiers were laying wire across our
> road. Nobody in my ward speaks of peace — only of who got out in time."

**1(b)** How far does Source A support the evidence of Source B about the closing of the border in August
1961? **[8]**

**Extract C** (invented interpretation).

> "The GDR survived for so long because most of its people accepted a bargain: security and welfare in
> return for political silence."

**1(c)** How far do you agree with the interpretation in Extract C about why the GDR remained stable in the
years 1962–87? Use Extract C, Sources A and B and your own knowledge. **[16]**

## Enquiry 2 — Ostpolitik and the Peaceful Revolution

**2(a)** Describe **two** features of the Basic Treaty (1972). **[6]**

**Source D** (invented). From a report by a Stasi officer in Leipzig, October 1989.

> "The Monday gathering at the church grew again. Participants chanted 'We are the people' and carried
> candles. They avoided violence, which makes intervention difficult to justify."

**Source E** (invented). From the memoirs of a West German journalist, published in 1995.

> "What brought the regime down was not one speech in Moscow or one decision in Budapest but tens of
> thousands of ordinary East Germans who stopped being afraid."

**2(b)** How far does Source D support the evidence of Source E about the protests of 1989? **[8]**

**Extract F** (invented interpretation).

> "The GDR fell because Gorbachev refused to defend it."

**2(c)** How far do you agree with the interpretation in Extract F? Use Extract F, Sources D and E and
your own knowledge. **[16]**

## Answers and mark guidance

### 1(a) [6]

Two features: about 2.7 million left between 1949 and 1961, most via the open border in Berlin; many were
young and skilled professionals, which damaged the economy; the flow accelerated in 1960–61 as fears grew
that the border would close.

### 1(b) [8]

**Agreement:** both confirm that the border was sealed in August 1961.
**Disagreement:** A presents it as protection and peace; B shows it dividing families and causing fear,
with people focused on escape.
**Provenance:** A is SED propaganda presenting the official justification; B is a private diary with no
reason to deceive. **Judgement:** they agree on the event but not its meaning.

### 1(c) [16]

**Supporting:** full employment, cheap rents, subsidised food and childcare; Honecker's consumer and
housing policies from 1971; many valued security and accepted limits; sporting success built pride.
**Challenging:** stability also rested on fear and force — the Stasi's surveillance, the Wall itself
(Source B), Soviet troops; censorship and propaganda (Source A); opposition existed (Biermann, church
groups); the "bargain" was failing by the 1980s as debts rose and goods were scarce.
**Judgement:** acceptance and coercion worked together; the extract underplays repression.

### 2(a) [6]

Two features: signed in December 1972 by the two German states; each recognised the other's borders and
independence; they agreed to exchange permanent representatives rather than ambassadors; it opened the way
for both to join the UN in 1973.

### 2(b) [8]

**Support:** both suggest that ordinary people's mass, peaceful protest was powerful — D's growing crowds
and "We are the people", E's "tens of thousands… who stopped being afraid".
**Difference:** D focuses on one city and the problem of intervening; E makes a general claim about why the
regime fell.
**Provenance:** D is an internal Stasi report, written at the time for superiors, so likely accurate on
numbers; E is written with hindsight by an outside observer. **Judgement:** strong support on the nature of
the protests.

### 2(c) [16]

**Supporting:** Gorbachev's reforms encouraged protest; he made clear Soviet troops would not intervene,
unlike in 1953 or 1968; his October 1989 visit undermined Honecker.
**Other factors:** the opening of the Hungarian–Austrian border and the mass exodus; peaceful mass protest
in Leipzig and Berlin (Sources D and E); the economic crisis and debt; the SED leadership's confusion and the
accidental opening of the Wall; West German support and the attraction of the deutschmark in 1990.
**Judgement:** Soviet non-intervention was a necessary condition, but East Germans themselves brought
the regime down.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating SED propaganda as factual evidence.
- In (b), ignoring when and why each source was produced.
- In (c), writing only about 9 November 1989.
