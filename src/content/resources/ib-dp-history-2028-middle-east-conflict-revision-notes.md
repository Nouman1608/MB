**HL only.** These notes condense Regional study 9 of the Africa and the Middle East option (IB DP History, first assessment 2028): conflict and instability in the Middle East, 1896 to 2020. Paper 3 asks for two 15-mark essays from different regional studies. The subject is contested, so aim for balance and precision. The fuller account is in the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-middle-east-conflict/).

## Key dates

- 1897: First Zionist Congress. 1916: Sykes-Picot. 1917: Balfour Declaration.
- 1922: Mandate confirmed. 1936 to 1939: Arab Revolt. 1939: White Paper.
- 29 November 1947: UN partition vote. 14 May 1948: Israel declared. 15 May: Arab states enter.
- 1949: armistices. 1956: Suez. 1964: PLO. 5 to 10 June 1967: Six-Day War.
- 6 October 1973: October War. 1979: Egypt-Israel treaty. 1987: First Intifada; Hamas.
- 1993: Oslo. 1995: Rabin assassinated. 2000: Camp David; Second Intifada. 2002: Arab Peace Initiative.

## Origins: promises and plans

| Document | Date | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Hussein-McMahon correspondence | 1915 to 1916 | British support for Arab independence in return for revolt (interpretation of boundaries disputed) |
| Sykes-Picot | 1916 | Secret British-French division of Ottoman Arab lands |
| Balfour Declaration | 1917 | Support for a "national home for the Jewish people" without prejudice to non-Jewish communities |
| Mandate for Palestine | 1922 | British rule, with Balfour wording included |

## Tensions to 1945

- **British policy:** 1922 White Paper (immigration by economic capacity); 1939 White Paper (limits on immigration and land sales).
- **Causes of tension:** land, labour, holy places (1929), fear of losing control, rising immigration.
- **Arab Revolt:** strike from April 1936; Arab Higher Committee; Mufti; harsh British repression; Peel partition plan rejected.
- **Jewish responses:** Haganah, Jewish Agency, Irgun; building of institutions.

## 1948

- **Causes:** Holocaust and displaced persons; illegal immigration; Britain's withdrawal; UN partition plan (Jews accepted, Arab states rejected); civil war then regular war.
- **Who:** David Ben-Gurion; Arab League (Egypt, Transjordan, Syria, Iraq, Lebanon).
- **Results:** Israel survives and holds more land than the plan; Jordan and Egypt hold the West Bank and Gaza; about 700,000 Palestinians leave or are expelled.
- **Perspectives:** traditional Israeli view (defensive war); New Historians (Morris, Shlaim, Pappé: archives show a complex picture, with disagreements); Palestinian view (the *Nakba*).

## Escalation

| Event | Detail |
|---|---|
| Suez 1956 | Nasser nationalises the Canal; Israel, Britain, France attack; US and USSR force withdrawal |
| 1967 | Israel takes Sinai, Gaza, West Bank, East Jerusalem, Golan; Resolution 242 |
| 1973 | Egyptian-Syrian attack; Israel recovers; opens way to diplomacy; oil embargo |
| PLO | Formed 1964; Arafat leads from 1969; fighting from Jordan then Lebanon |
| Israel | Settlements; invasion of Lebanon 1982; anti-PLO policy |
| Palestinians | First Intifada 1987 to 1993; Hamas 1987; Second Intifada from 2000 |

## Impact

- **Political and economic:** military spending, Cold War rivalry, oil embargo, pressure on neighbours.
- **Social and migration:** refugees (UNRWA), Jewish immigration from Europe and Arab states.
- **Women:** roles in movements, military service, loss and displacement.
- **Marginalised groups:** Palestinian citizens of Israel (military government until 1966), refugees in Lebanon, Syria and Jordan, Mizrahi Jews.

## Peace attempts compared

| Attempt | Outcome | Why it succeeded or failed |
|---|---|---|
| Egypt-Israel 1979 | Treaty; Sinai returned | Carter's mediation; Sadat and Begin; a bilateral deal that left the Palestinian issue open |
| Oslo 1993 | Mutual recognition; interim self-rule | Final status issues left for later; settlements, violence, Rabin's murder |
| Camp David 2000 | No agreement | Jerusalem, refugees, borders; blame disputed |
| Arab Peace Initiative 2002 | Offer of normal relations for withdrawal | Not accepted by Israel; limited follow-up |

## Arguments to practise

1. "The Balfour Declaration caused the conflict." For: promised a home without consulting the Arab majority. Against: Zionism, immigration and Arab nationalism were already growing.
2. "The UN plan could have worked." For: Jewish acceptance. Against: Arab rejection, civil war, no enforcement.
3. "1967 was the key turning point." See the study guide.
4. "Peace failed because of weak mediation." For: US role, timing. Against: positions on core issues.

## Worked example

*Question:* "To what extent was British policy responsible for the failure to find a peaceful settlement in Palestine before 1948?"

- For: contradictory promises, changes in immigration policy, harsh repression of the Arab Revolt, handing the problem to the UN.
- Against: the nature of the competing national claims, the leaders' refusal to compromise, the Holocaust's effect on Jewish immigration, and Arab rejection of partition.
- Judgement: British policy increased distrust, but a solution would have been very hard in any case.

## Common mistakes

- Treating one side's account as neutral fact.
- Mixing Suez 1956 with the 1967 and 1973 wars.
- Ignoring the role of Arab states other than Egypt.
- Giving the Balfour Declaration in full from memory but with errors; paraphrase it carefully.

## Self-test

1. Who proclaimed the state of Israel on 14 May 1948?
2. What did the 1939 White Paper do?
3. Which war in 1967 led to the occupation of the West Bank?
4. Which treaty brought peace between Egypt and Israel?
5. What did Oslo provide?
6. What was the Arab Peace Initiative?

**Answers:** 1. David Ben-Gurion. 2. Limited Jewish immigration and land purchases. 3. The Six-Day War. 4. The Egypt-Israel Peace Treaty of 1979. 5. Mutual recognition and phased Palestinian self-rule. 6. A 2002 Arab League plan offering normal relations in exchange for withdrawal and a settlement of the refugee issue.

## Key terms

- **Zionism:** the movement for a Jewish national home.
- **Mandate:** League of Nations authority for a power to govern a territory.
- **Nakba:** the Palestinian term for the 1948 displacement.
- **Intifada:** an Arabic word for "uprising".
- **Settlements:** Israeli civilian communities in territory occupied since 1967.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know events after 2005?** The guide runs to 2020, but the specified content is mostly earlier. Know the later context, including Hamas in Gaza.

**Is it acceptable to give my own view?** Your judgement must be supported by evidence and by a fair evaluation of competing arguments.
