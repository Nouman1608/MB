These questions cover section 3.4.1 Force, energy and momentum (3.4.1.1 to 3.4.1.8) of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408), including Required practical 3. Section A has short questions; Section B has structured questions. Learn the content first with the [force, energy and momentum study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-force-energy-and-momentum/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-force-energy-and-momentum-revision-notes/). Take g = 9.81 m s⁻² and ignore air resistance unless told otherwise.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Explain the difference between a scalar and a vector, giving one example of each. **[2]**

**2.** **(a)** Define the moment of a force about a point. **[1]**
**(b)** State the principle of moments. **[2]**

**3.** A cyclist accelerates uniformly from rest at 1.5 m s⁻² for 6.0 s. Calculate the final speed and the distance travelled. **[3]**

**4.** Explain why a skydiver falling from rest reaches a terminal speed. **[3]**

**5.** State Newton's third law and give an example of a pair of forces it describes. **[2]**

**6.** State what is meant by impulse and give its unit. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A uniform plank 4.0 m long and of weight 180 N rests on two supports, A and B, each 0.50 m from an end. A painter of weight 700 N stands 1.0 m from the end nearer A. Calculate the force on the plank from each support. **[4]**

**8.** A stone is thrown from the top of a 30 m cliff at 15 m s⁻¹ at 40° above the horizontal. It lands in the sea below.

**(a)** Calculate the horizontal and vertical components of the initial velocity. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the time taken to reach the sea. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate how far from the foot of the cliff it lands. **[1]**

**9.** A lift of total mass 800 kg is supported by a cable. Calculate the tension in the cable when the lift is:

**(a)** accelerating upwards at 1.2 m s⁻² **[2]**
**(b)** moving upwards at a constant speed **[1]**
**(c)** moving upwards and slowing down at 0.80 m s⁻². **[1]**

**10.** Ball A, of mass 0.40 kg, moves at 5.0 m s⁻¹ and collides head-on with stationary ball B, of mass 0.60 kg. After the collision A moves backwards at 1.0 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the velocity of B after the collision. **[2]**
**(b)** Determine whether the collision is elastic. **[2]**

**11.** A 0.45 kg football at rest is kicked. The force on it rises steadily from zero to 600 N and falls steadily back to zero over a contact time of 0.050 s.

**(a)** Calculate the impulse on the ball. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed of the ball as it leaves the boot. **[1]**

**12.** A cyclist and bicycle of total mass 85 kg ride up a slope inclined at 5.0° to the horizontal at a steady 4.0 m s⁻¹. Resistive forces total 25 N.

**(a)** Calculate the driving force needed. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the cyclist's useful power output. **[2]**

**13.** A roller-coaster car of mass 500 kg passes the top of a slope at 2.0 m s⁻¹ and descends 32 m vertically along 120 m of track, reaching 22 m s⁻¹ at the bottom.

**(a)** Calculate the work done against resistive forces. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the average resistive force along the track. **[1]**

**14.** In Required practical 3, a student drops a ball through different heights h and measures the time t. A graph of h against t² has a gradient of 4.88 m s⁻².

**(a)** Calculate the value of g obtained. **[1]**
**(b)** The release mechanism adds a short, constant delay to every time. State the type of error and its effect on the graph. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A scalar has magnitude only; a vector has magnitude and direction [1]. Examples: speed or mass (scalar), velocity or force (vector) [1].

**2. (a)** Force × perpendicular distance from the point to the line of action of the force [1].
**(b)** For a body in equilibrium [1], the sum of the clockwise moments about any point equals the sum of the anticlockwise moments about that point [1].

**3.** v = u + at = 0 + 1.5 × 6.0 = **9.0 m s⁻¹** [1]. s = ut + ½at² [1] = ½ × 1.5 × 6.0² = **27 m** [1].

**4.** At first the weight is greater than the air resistance, so the skydiver accelerates [1]. Air resistance increases as speed increases [1]. When air resistance equals weight, the resultant force and acceleration are zero, so the speed stays constant [1].

**5.** If body A exerts a force on body B, then B exerts an equal and opposite force of the same type on A [1]. Example: the Earth pulls a person down with their weight and the person pulls the Earth up with an equal gravitational force [1].

**6.** Impulse is the change in momentum, equal to force × time for which it acts (the area under a force–time graph) [1]. Unit: **N s** (equivalent to kg m s⁻¹) [1].

**7.** Supports A and B are 3.0 m apart; the plank's weight acts at its centre, 1.5 m from A, and the painter stands 0.50 m from A [1]. Moments about A: R_B × 3.0 = 180 × 1.5 + 700 × 0.50 = 620 N m [1], so **R_B = 207 N** [1]. Vertically, R_A + R_B = 880 N, so **R_A = 673 N** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Taking moments about A removes R_A from the equation. Check: the painter is near A, so A should carry most of the load.

**8. (a)** uₓ = 15 cos 40° = 11.5 m s⁻¹ and u_y = 15 sin 40° = 9.64 m s⁻¹ [1].
**(b)** Taking up as positive: −30 = 9.64t − 4.905t² [1]; 4.905t² − 9.64t − 30 = 0 [1]; t = **3.64 s** (taking the positive root) [1].
**(c)** Distance = 11.5 × 3.64 = **41.9 m** [1].
*Examiner insight:* The displacement is −30 m because the stone ends below its starting point. Using +30 m gives no real solution or a wrong time.

**9. (a)** T − mg = ma [1], so T = 800 × (9.81 + 1.2) = **8810 N** (8808 N) [1].
**(b)** T = mg = **7850 N** (7848 N) [1].
**(c)** T = 800 × (9.81 − 0.80) = **7210 N** (7208 N) [1].

**10. (a)** 0.40 × 5.0 = 0.40 × (−1.0) + 0.60v [1], so v = 2.4/0.60 = **4.0 m s⁻¹** in A's original direction [1].
**(b)** Ek before = ½ × 0.40 × 5.0² = 5.0 J; Ek after = ½ × 0.40 × 1.0² + ½ × 0.60 × 4.0² = 0.2 + 4.8 = 5.0 J [1]. Total kinetic energy is unchanged, so the collision is **elastic** [1].

**11. (a)** Impulse = area under the force–time graph = ½ × 600 × 0.050 [1] = **15 N s** [1].
**(b)** v = Δp/m = 15/0.45 = **33 m s⁻¹** [1].

**12. (a)** Component of weight down the slope = 85 × 9.81 × sin 5.0° = 72.7 N [1]; driving force = 72.7 + 25 = **97.7 N** [1].
**(b)** P = Fv [1] = 97.7 × 4.0 = **390 W** [1].

**13. (a)** Ep lost = 500 × 9.81 × 32 = 1.57 × 10⁵ J [1]. Ek gained = ½ × 500 × (22² − 2.0²) = 1.20 × 10⁵ J [1]. Work against resistance = **3.7 × 10⁴ J** (36 960 J) [1].
**(b)** F = W/s = 36 960 / 120 = **310 N** (308 N) [1].

**14. (a)** h = ½gt², so gradient = g/2 and g = 2 × 4.88 = **9.76 m s⁻²** [1].
**(b)** A systematic error [1]; every t is too large, so the line is shifted and does not pass through the origin (the plotted t² values are all too high) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking moments without a perpendicular distance, or about a point that leaves two unknowns.
- Using +30 m instead of −30 m for a projectile that lands below its start.
- Forgetting mg when finding a cable tension.
- Ignoring the sign of a rebound velocity in momentum conservation.
- Using the peak force × time instead of the area of a triangle for impulse.
- Leaving out the weight component when a vehicle climbs a slope.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do you find support forces on a beam?**
Take moments about one support to remove its unknown force, solve for the other support, then use the fact that the upward forces equal the total downward force.

**How can you tell whether a collision is elastic?**
Calculate the total kinetic energy before and after. If it is the same, the collision is elastic; if some is lost, it is inelastic. Momentum is conserved either way.
