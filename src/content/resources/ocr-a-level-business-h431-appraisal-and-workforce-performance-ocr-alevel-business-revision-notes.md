All figures are invented.

## What the specification lists

- What appraisal is and its purpose.
- Appraisal methods: managerial, peer, self-assessment, 360 degree assessment, target setting.
- Measures of workforce performance: lateness, absenteeism, labour turnover, productivity, holiday entitlement, performance related pay; calculate and interpret.
- Usefulness and importance of measures and of appraisal; recommend methods of appraisal; employee contribution.

## Core recall

### Purposes of appraisal

Review achievement; agree objectives; identify training needs; decide pay and promotion; motivate through feedback; support communication; provide evidence for discipline.

### Methods

| Method | Who assesses | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Managerial | Line manager | Knows the job; simple | Subjective; one view |
| Peer | Colleagues | See daily work | Friendship or rivalry bias |
| Self-assessment | The employee | Involves them | Inflated or too modest |
| 360 degree | Manager, peers, subordinates, customers | Full picture | Time-consuming; costly |
| Target setting | Agreed goals reviewed against results | Clear and objective | Unfair or narrow targets |

### Formulae (standard forms)

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Lateness (%) | Late arrivals ÷ total possible attendances × 100 |
| Absenteeism (%) | Days lost ÷ total working days available × 100 |
| Labour turnover (%) | Leavers ÷ average number employed × 100 |
| Productivity | Output ÷ number of employees (or hours) |
| Statutory holiday | 5.6 weeks a year; part-timers pro rata: 5.6 × days worked a week |
| PRP bonus | Rate × output above target |

- Total working days = employees × days each year.
- Cost of absence = days lost × cost per day.

### Interpreting measures

- Rates can be compared with targets, past years and rivals.
- A figure tells what, not why; investigate the cause.
- A single measure can mislead (productivity up through unsafe hours).
- Measures can be gamed if they decide rewards.
- Use several together.

### Performance related pay

| Benefits | Drawbacks |
|---|---|
| Motivates higher output | Quality may suffer |
| Fair reward for effort | Teamwork may suffer |
| Links pay to business results | Hard to measure some jobs |
| Costs rise only when output rises | Income uncertain for staff; target hard to set |

### Employee contribution

- Value added through output, quality, ideas, service and teamwork, less the cost of employing them.
- Estimates: output, revenue or profit per employee.
- Numbers miss helping colleagues; low output may be caused by management or equipment.

## Worked reminder

Penhallow Distribution (invented), 50 staff, 250 days each.

- Working days 50 × 250 = **12,500**. Absence 750 days: 750 ÷ 12,500 × 100 = **6.0%**; cost 750 × £95 = **£71,250**.
- Lateness 1,200 ÷ 12,500 × 100 = **9.6%**.
- Turnover 9 ÷ 50 × 100 = **18%**.
- Productivity 150,000 ÷ 50 = **3,000** parcels each; last year 135,000 ÷ 48 = 2,812.5; change **+6.7%**.
- Holiday: 5.6 × 5 = **28 days**; part-time 3 days a week: 5.6 × 3 = **16.8 days**.
- PRP £2 a parcel above 2,800: bonus £400 each, **£20,000** in total; extra contribution 10,000 × £3.50 = **£35,000**; net **£15,000**.

## Choosing an appraisal method

| Job | Suitable method |
|---|---|
| Measurable output (sales, production) | Target setting with manager review |
| Team-based work | Peer assessment and manager review |
| Managers and supervisors | 360 degree |
| Professional staff | Self-assessment plus manager review |

State the cost, the risk and what it depends on.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Appraisal | Formal review of an employee's performance |
| 360 degree assessment | Feedback from many people around the employee |
| Absenteeism | Staff absence from work |
| Productivity | Output per employee or hour |
| Performance related pay | Pay linked to performance or output |
| Holiday entitlement | Paid leave an employee is entitled to |
| Contribution | The value an employee adds |

## Exam traps

- Absence is divided by days available, not by the number of staff.
- Use the average number employed for turnover.
- Peer assessment is by colleagues; 360 degree includes several groups.
- PRP does not always raise motivation.
- A measure shows what, not why.
- Part-time holiday entitlement is pro rata.

## Self-test

1. State two purposes of appraisal.
2. Distinguish between peer assessment and 360 degree assessment.
3. 40 staff each work 240 days. Calculate total working days.
4. 480 days are lost to absence. Calculate the absence rate.
5. 6 staff leave and the average number employed is 48. Calculate labour turnover.
6. 90,000 units are made by 36 staff. Calculate productivity.
7. Calculate the statutory holiday for a part-timer working 4 days a week.
8. A bonus of £1.50 is paid on each unit above 2,400 per employee. An employee makes 2,600. Calculate the bonus.
9. Give one problem with PRP.
10. Why is a productivity figure alone not enough?

### Answers

1. Any two: review performance; agree objectives; identify training needs; decide pay or promotion; motivate.
2. Peer assessment is by colleagues; 360 degree assessment gathers views from several groups, such as manager, peers, subordinates and customers.
3. 40 × 240 = **9,600 days**.
4. 480 ÷ 9,600 × 100 = **5%**.
5. 6 ÷ 48 × 100 = **12.5%**.
6. 90,000 ÷ 36 = **2,500 units per employee**.
7. 5.6 × 4 = **22.4 days**.
8. (2,600 − 2,400) × £1.50 = 200 × £1.50 = **£300**.
9. Any one: quality may fall; teamwork may suffer; targets may be unfair; hard to measure some jobs.
10. It shows what has happened but not why; it may rise because of new machines or long hours, and does not show quality.
