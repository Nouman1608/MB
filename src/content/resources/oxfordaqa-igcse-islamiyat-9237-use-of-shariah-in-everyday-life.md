**This guide covers content area 3.2.2.7 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), The use of Shariah in everyday life: "Following the Qur'an and Sunnah in everyday life, including Halal and Haram, dietary laws and dress laws for men and women. The role of parents including religious upbringing and developing good morals and character."** The prescribed passage is Sahih al-Bukhari 5027.

Earlier content areas explained where Shariah comes from. This one shows how it appears in an ordinary day: what a Muslim eats and wears, how he or she earns a living, and how children are raised.

## Shariah as a complete way of life

Shariah means "the path". It covers worship, but also food, dress, work, family and conduct. Scholars sort actions into five grades.

| Grade | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Wajib / fard** | Required; reward for doing, sin for neglecting | Five daily prayers |
| **Mustahabb** | Recommended; reward for doing, no sin for leaving | Voluntary fasting |
| **Mubah** | Permitted; neither rewarded nor blamed | Choosing what colour to wear |
| **Makruh** | Disliked; reward for avoiding | Wasting water |
| **Haram** | Forbidden; sin for doing | Eating pork, drinking alcohol |

## Halal and Haram

**Halal** means lawful and permitted. **Haram** means unlawful and forbidden. The basic rule is that everything is halal unless a text forbids it. The Qur'an says that Allah has made lawful the good things (5:4) and forbids what is impure (7:157).

**Between them.** A Hadith reported by al-Bukhari and Muslim says that the halal is clear and the haram is clear, and between them are doubtful matters. Whoever avoids the doubtful protects his religion and honour. Muslims therefore try to avoid what is uncertain.

**Halal earnings.** Halal and Haram apply to income as well as food. A Muslim should earn by honest work and avoid interest, theft, fraud and gambling. The Prophet (pbuh) said that Allah is pure and accepts only what is pure (Sahih Muslim).

**Reasons for following them.** Obedience to Allah; protection from harm; discipline; a sign of faith.

## Dietary laws

**Forbidden foods.** The Qur'an forbids dead animals, flowing blood, the flesh of swine and what has been dedicated to other than Allah (2:173; 5:3). Alcohol and other intoxicants are forbidden because they are "defilement from the work of Satan" (5:90). Qur'an 5:3 also lists animals killed by strangling or a blow or a fall or by the horns of another.

**Halal slaughter (*dhabihah*).** Meat is lawful when:

- the animal is alive and healthy at the time of slaughter;
- the name of Allah is said ("Bismillah, Allahu Akbar");
- the throat is cut quickly with a very sharp knife;
- the blood is allowed to drain.

**Kindness.** The Prophet (pbuh) taught that Allah has prescribed excellence in everything, so when you slaughter, do it well, sharpen the knife and spare the animal suffering (Sahih Muslim).

**Necessity.** The Qur'an allows a person to eat forbidden food if he is forced by hunger, without desiring it and without exceeding need (2:173). The same exception appears in 5:3.

**Food of the People of the Book.** The Qur'an permits the food of those given the Scripture (5:5).

**Everyday effects.** Muslims check ingredients for gelatine, alcohol and animal fat; they look for halal certification; they say Bismillah before eating; they eat with the right hand; and they avoid waste.

## Dress laws

The Qur'an and Sunnah ask both men and women to dress with modesty (*haya'*). The Prophet (pbuh) said that modesty is part of faith (Sahih al-Bukhari). Dress is also connected to lowering the gaze: "Tell the believing men to reduce [some] of their vision and guard their private parts" (24:30), and the same instruction is given to women (24:31).

| | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| **Covering** | At least from the navel to the knees in the view of most scholars; in public, clothes should be modest in practice | The body except the face and hands, in the view of most scholars (24:31; 33:59) |
| **Other rules** | Silk and gold jewellery are forbidden for men in Hadith; clothes should not show pride | Loose clothing that does not show the shape; a head covering (*khimar* or *hijab*) |
| **Both** | Clean, simple clothes; no arrogance in dress or imitation of immodesty | |

The Qur'an tells women to draw their head coverings over their chests (24:31) and, in another verse, to bring down over themselves part of their outer garments so that they are known and not harassed (33:59).

**Reasons given.** Modesty; dignity; respect for oneself and others; identity as a Muslim; protection from harassment; obedience.

**Variety.** The Qur'an and Sunnah set principles. The style varies by country: shalwar kameez, thobe, jilbab, abaya and many others. Some scholars differ on whether the face must be covered. Ja'fari scholars require the same basic modesty.

## The role of parents

Parents are the first teachers. The Prophet (pbuh) said that every one of you is a shepherd and responsible for his flock, and a man is the shepherd of his family (Sahih al-Bukhari). The Qur'an says: "O you who have believed, protect yourselves and your families from a Fire" (66:6).

**Religious upbringing.**

- Teaching the Shahadah and the basics of belief.
- Giving a newborn a good name; many families also say the call to prayer to the baby.
- Teaching children to pray from the age of about seven, and encouraging them to fast gradually.
- Teaching them to read the Qur'an and to learn its meaning.
- Taking them to the mosque and into the community.
- Setting a good example. Children learn more from what parents do than from what they say.

**Developing good morals and character.** The Prophet (pbuh) said that the best of you are those best in character (Sahih al-Bukhari). Parents teach honesty, kindness, respect for elders, sharing and gratitude. The advice of Luqman to his son in Surah Luqman (31:13-19) is a model: worship Allah alone, keep up prayer, enjoin good, be patient, avoid arrogance and keep your voice low.

**Fairness.** Parents should treat children justly and without favouritism.

**Parents and the Qur'an.** The prescribed Hadith says: "The best among you (Muslims) are those who learn the Qur'an and teach it." Parents who teach their children the Qur'an fulfil this directly. The Hadith also applies to the family as a whole: learning together makes the home a place of faith.

## A day in the life: Shariah in practice

Consider an ordinary school day for a Muslim student. She wakes for Fajr and prays. She says Bismillah before breakfast and checks that the food is halal. She dresses modestly in clothes that suit her school's rules. In lessons she is honest in her work, and at lunch she eats only halal food or a vegetarian option. She is polite to teachers and kind to classmates. Back home she prays, helps her parents and reads some Qur'an. Almost every step touches a teaching of the Qur'an or Sunnah, and none of it needs a special occasion. This is what the specification means by following the Qur'an and Sunnah in everyday life: the rules are not a separate part of life but a way of living all of it.

## The prescribed passage: Sahih al-Bukhari 5027

| Part | Use |
|---|---|
| "The best among you" | Excellence is measured by knowledge and teaching the Qur'an, not wealth or status |
| "those who learn the Qur'an" | Following Shariah starts with learning its source |
| "and teach it" | The duty is passed on, especially by parents to children |

Use it in answers about parents (religious upbringing), about everyday life (living by the Qur'an) and about character (the best people serve others with knowledge).

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** ways in which parents help their children to follow Shariah. **[4]**

> Parents teach their children the basics of Islam, such as how to pray and read the Qur'an [1], so that the children grow up knowing their duties [1]. They also set an example of good character, such as honesty and kindness [1], because children copy what they see, and the Prophet (pbuh) said the best of people are those best in character [1].

## Common mistakes

- **Treating Halal and Haram as only about food.** They also cover income, speech and conduct.
- **Saying the dress laws apply only to women.** Men also have rules of modesty.
- **Stating one view as the only view.** Scholars differ on details, so say "most scholars".
- **Forgetting the exception for necessity.** The Qur'an allows it in cases of real need.
- **Listing duties of parents without a reason or source.** Add why it matters and a verse or Hadith.
