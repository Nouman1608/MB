# Planning Paper 1 as a whole

**Paper 1 (Drama and Poetry) is a 2-hour written paper worth 50 marks.** It has two sections, Section A: Drama and Section B: Poetry, each worth 25 marks. You answer two questions: one on a drama set text in Section A and one on a poetry set text in Section B. On each set text you choose between question (a) and question (b); the (b) question is based on a passage or a poem from the set text printed on the paper. Both need an essay-length response. Set texts are not allowed in the exam room and dictionaries may not be used. Paper 1 is half of the AS Level and a quarter of the A Level.

Related pages: [Section A: Drama](/resources/cambridge-a-level-english-literature-9695-section-a-drama/) and [Section B: Poetry](/resources/cambridge-a-level-english-literature-9695-section-b-poetry/).

## What every answer is assessed on

All questions on Paper 1 assess AO1 to AO4, each weighted at 25 per cent of the paper. There is no AO5 on this paper.

| AO | Syllabus wording (short) | What it means in an essay |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 | Knowledge and understanding, with an appreciation of relevant contexts | Know the text; use context only when it sheds light |
| AO2 | Analyse writers' choices of language, form and structure | Name a choice and say what effect it has |
| AO3 | Informed, independent opinions and interpretations | Argue a view of your own |
| AO4 | Communicate a relevant, structured and supported response | Plan, order points and support with evidence |

## Choosing between (a) and (b)

| | (a) Essay | (b) Passage or poem |
|---|---|---|
| Printed on paper | A question only | A question plus a passage or poem |
| Main risk | Writing about the text in general terms without evidence | Retelling the extract instead of analysing it |
| Good fit when | You know the whole text well | You prefer close reading and can still link to the rest of the text |

Choose by asking: can I name three pieces of evidence for my argument without the book? If yes, (a) is workable. For (b), the question is based on a passage or a poem, so your analysis starts from the printed words.

## A planning routine for each essay

1. Underline the command word and the key terms in the question (the syllabus lists words such as analyse, assess, compare, comment, consider, discuss, examine and explain).
2. Write a one-sentence argument.
3. List three or four points, each with a piece of evidence and a named choice (language, form or structure).
4. Decide the order so that the argument develops.
5. Write the conclusion first in note form so you know where the essay ends.

## Balancing the two sections

The sections are worth the same, 25 marks each, so give them similar time and effort. A common mistake is writing a long first essay and a thin second. Timings are your own judgement: the syllabus does not give a time per section. Practise full-length essays so you know how long your planning and writing take.

## Revision priorities

- One drama text and one poetry text, learned equally well.
- A bank of short pieces of evidence you can recall for each text.
- Practice at writing on an unfamiliar printed passage and poem from your set texts (the (b) task).
- Vocabulary for staging and verse: only terms you can explain, not terms you can only name.
