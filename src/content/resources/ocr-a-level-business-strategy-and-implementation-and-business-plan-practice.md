## Questions

**1.** Pendlecote Dairy has annual sales revenue of £1,800,000. Its managers make three statements.

Statement 1: "Raise sales revenue by 20% within four years."
Statement 2: "Supply two new regions through the supermarket chains."
Statement 3: "Hand out free sample packs to shoppers during the first month in each region."

**(a)** Identify which statement is an objective, which is a strategy and which is a tactic. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the sales revenue Pendlecote Dairy needs to reach its objective. **[2]**

**2.** Define the term "business plan". **[2]**

**3.** State **two** contents of a business plan and explain why each is included. **[4]**

**4.** Prindlemere Tutors is about to launch an online tuition service as part of a new strategy. Explain **two** reasons why Prindlemere Tutors needs to plan the implementation of this strategy. **[4]**

**5.** Explain what is meant by the "Plan-Do-Review" cycle. **[3]**

**6.** Vantergill Print planned to complete 2,400 printing jobs in a quarter at an average sales revenue of £45 a job. It completed 2,070 jobs at an average of £45.

**(a)** Calculate the sales revenue variance and state whether it is favourable or adverse. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain **one** action Vantergill Print could take at the "Review" stage. **[2]**

**7.** Aldersyde Garden Centre plans a café. Forecast spend per customer is £6.50 and variable cost per customer is £2.60. Fixed costs are £2,730 a month. The plan forecasts 900 customers a month.

**(a)** Calculate the number of customers needed each month to break even. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the margin of safety in customers and the forecast monthly profit. **[3]**

**8.** Prindlemere Tutors planned 120 online lessons a week but delivered an average of 84 in month one. A review found that most cancelled lessons were in evening slots, when tutors were also teaching in person. Analyse how the Plan-Do-Review cycle can improve the performance of Prindlemere Tutors. **[6]**

**9.** Aldersyde Garden Centre asks its bank for a £90,000 loan, repayable over five years, to build the café in question 7. The plan forecasts a profit of £780 a month. Evaluate the impact and importance of the business plan to the stakeholders of Aldersyde Garden Centre. **[8]**

**10.** Dev Sawbridge plans to start Sawbridge Restoration, restoring antique furniture from his garage with his own savings and no loan. A friend says writing a business plan is a waste of time. Evaluate the advantages and disadvantages to Dev of having a business plan. **[8]**

**11.** Pendlecote Dairy considers a dairy-free range costing £240,000. The owners expect extra cash inflows of £80,000 in year 1 and £100,000 in each of years 2, 3 and 4. The owners want payback within three years.

**(a)** Calculate the payback period for the investment. **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate the proposed strategy and recommend whether Pendlecote Dairy should go ahead. **[9]**

**12.** After the weak quarter in question 6, Vantergill Print carries out a strategic review. Its strategy is low-priced, high-volume work for local businesses, but online printers now charge less. Two options emerge. Option A is to cut prices by 10% to keep volume. Option B is to spend £30,000 on equipment for large-format and wedding stationery, which is forecast to add £12,000 a year in extra profit. Recommend and justify how Vantergill Print should alter its strategy. **[8]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Statement 1 is the objective, a target to reach [1]. Statement 2 is the strategy, the long-term route [1]. Statement 3 is a tactic, a short-term action within the strategy [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 1,800,000 x 1.20 [1] = **£2,160,000** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Labels tend to gain more when each is tied to a reason such as time horizon or measurability.

**2.** A written document [1] setting out what a business intends to do, how, and the results it expects [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Saying only "a plan for a business" gives little to credit; say what the document contains.

**3.** Any two from the following, each with a reason. Financial forecasts [1] show whether the business can survive and repay finance [1]. Market research [1] gives evidence that customers exist and will pay [1]. Other pairs are acceptable, such as a marketing plan or a risks section. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "State and explain" asks for both parts. Four contents with no reasons gain less than two contents each given a reason.

**4.** Resources must be ready at the right time [1]: Prindlemere needs cash for the platform and time to train tutors, or the launch may stall [1]. Staff need to know their roles and why the change is happening [1]: if tutors are not told who schedules online sessions, lessons may be missed or tutors may resist [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A reason is developed best when it uses the stem, such as the platform or tutors.

**5.** A repeating process [1] in which a business sets a plan with targets, carries it out [1], then compares actual results with the plan and uses the lessons in the next plan [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The word "cycle" matters: showing the Review feeding the next Plan shows that the process repeats.

**6. (a)** Planned sales revenue = 2,400 x 45 = £108,000 [1]. Actual sales revenue = 2,070 x 45 = £93,150 [1]. Variance = 108,000 - 93,150 = **£14,850 adverse** [1] (actual is below plan). **[3]**
**(b)** Investigate why 330 jobs (13.75%) were missed, by checking competitor prices and customer feedback [1], then change the next plan, such as the forecast or marketing approach [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A variance answer needs the direction as well as the number. A review action gains more when it links a cause to a change in the next plan.

**7. (a)** Contribution per customer = 6.50 - 2.60 = £3.90 [1]. Break-even = 2,730 / 3.90 [1] = **700 customers** a month [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Margin of safety = 900 - 700 = **200 customers** [1]. Forecast profit = 900 x 3.90 [1] - 2,730 = **£780 a month** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the contribution per customer before dividing, so the method is visible.

**8.** The Plan stage gave a clear target of 120 lessons a week [1], which made the shortfall measurable: 84 is 70% of plan, 36 lessons short [1]. The Do stage produced data on which slots failed [1], and the Review identified the cause, evening clashes with in-person teaching [1]. The next plan can move in-person teaching or recruit evening tutors so that capacity matches demand [1]. Over repeated cycles forecasts improve, though this needs accurate data and action on the review [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each stage should lead to a consequence in the next; three stage names alone do not analyse.

**9.** Bank: the plan lets the bank judge risk [1]. Repaying capital alone over five years needs £18,000 a year, but forecast profit is 780 x 12 = £9,360 [1], so the gap could lead to a smaller loan, a longer term or a refusal [1]. Owners: the plan shows, before building, whether the café is viable [1], so weak figures are a useful warning rather than a failure [1]. Employees: a sound plan suggests lasting jobs [1]. Suppliers: may offer credit if it looks sound [1]. Judgement: the plan matters most to the bank, since it decides funding, but helps the owners most if it prompts them to improve the proposal first [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Using a case figure and weighing stakeholders against each other tends to beat describing each in turn.

**10.** Advantage: writing the plan forces Dev to test the idea, such as demand for restoration work and the cost of tools and materials [1]. It sets targets and a benchmark for his first year [1], and shows weaknesses before he spends savings that are at risk [1]. Disadvantage: it takes time he could spend finding customers [1]. Forecasts for a new business are uncertain, so the plan may be wrong or soon out of date [1]. With no lender to persuade, the plan has no outside audience [1]. Weighing: because Dev risks his own savings, a short plan covering demand, costs and cash flow is worth its cost [1]; a long plan is less justified [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* The stem detail (own savings, no loan) separates a reasoned judgement from a generic list.

**11. (a)** Cumulative inflows: year 1 £80,000, year 2 £180,000, year 3 £280,000 [1]. The £240,000 is recovered during year 3, with 240,000 - 180,000 = £60,000 still needed at the start of year 3 [1]. Payback = 2 + 60,000 / 100,000 = **2.6 years** (about 2 years 7 months) [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Payback of 2.6 years meets the owners' three-year limit [1], and total inflows of £380,000 exceed the cost by £140,000 [1]. A new range could attract customers the business does not reach now [1]. However, payback ignores cash after the payback point and the time value of money [1], and the inflows are forecasts for a new product [1]. If every inflow were 15% lower (£68,000 in year 1, then £85,000 a year), cumulative inflow after three years would be £238,000, so payback would slip just beyond three years [1]. A failed launch could weaken cash flow for the whole business [1]. Recommendation: go ahead in stages, committing part of the spend and checking early sales against the forecast before releasing the rest [1], because this limits the downside while the 2.6-year payback leaves some room against the owners' limit [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* A recommendation is stronger when conditional on evidence and when it names a limitation of the calculation.

**12.** Recommend Option B [1]. Option A cuts price on every job, so on 2,070 jobs it lowers revenue by 10% of £93,150, which is £9,315, and online printers could undercut again [1]. It keeps the business competing on price, where it is weakest [1]. Option B targets work where service and quality matter more than price [1]. Payback is 30,000 / 12,000 = 2.5 years [1]. Risks: £30,000 of spending and unproven demand [1]; reduce them by testing demand with a small run of orders first [1]. Owners accept short-term cost for long-term survival, and employees gain skills but may fear change [1]. Judgement: B is better if cash can cover it. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A "recommend and justify" answer is stronger when it rejects an alternative with a reason and names a risk of its own choice.

## Where marks are usually lost
- Labelling a short-term action as a strategy, or an objective as a tactic.
- Giving a variance figure without saying whether it is favourable or adverse.
- Listing plan contents with no reasons.
- Treating Plan-Do-Review as three words, not a loop.
- Ending with a repeat of earlier points instead of a judgement.
