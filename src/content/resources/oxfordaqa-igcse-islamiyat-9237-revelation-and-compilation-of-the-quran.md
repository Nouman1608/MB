**This guide covers content area 3.2.2.1 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), The revelation and compilation of the Qur'an: "The significance of the Qur'an as the final revelation, including the way in which it was revealed, preserved, and compiled."** The prescribed passage is a Hadith from Sahih al-Bukhari (4987) about the copying of the Qur'an in the time of Hazrat Uthman (RA).

The question to hold in mind is simple: how do we know that the Qur'an in our hands is the Qur'an that was revealed? The answer comes in three stages. It was **revealed** to the Prophet (pbuh) over about twenty-three years. It was **preserved** in memory and in writing during his lifetime. It was **compiled** into a single book after his death, and then copied to a standard text.

## The significance of the Qur'an as the final revelation

- **The word of Allah.** Muslims believe the Qur'an is the speech of Allah, revealed in Arabic through the angel Jibril (AS) to the Prophet (pbuh), not his own composition.
- **The last in a line of scriptures.** Earlier books, such as the Tawrat given to Musa (AS) and the Injil given to Isa (AS), came before it. The Qur'an confirms the truth in them and stands as the final guidance (Qur'an 5:48).
- **For all people and all times.** It describes itself as "guidance for the people" (2:185). The Prophet (pbuh) is the "seal of the prophets" (33:40), so no new scripture is expected.
- **Completed.** Qur'an 5:3 states that the religion has been perfected.
- **Protected.** "Indeed, it is We who sent down the Qur'an and indeed, We will be its guardian" (15:9). Muslims take this as Allah's promise that the text would not be lost or changed.

## How the Qur'an was revealed

**The first revelation.** In the Cave of Hira, in the month of Ramadhan, Jibril (AS) came to the Prophet (pbuh) and said "Read". The first five verses of Surah al-Alaq (96:1-5) came, beginning "Recite in the name of your Lord who created". Muslims remember this night as Laylat al-Qadr.

**Gradual revelation.** The rest came in parts for about twenty-three years, in Makkah and then in Madinah. Reasons given by scholars include:

- **To strengthen the Prophet's (pbuh) heart** as he faced hardship (25:32).
- **To answer questions and events** as they happened, for example about the Hijrah, battles and the Isra wal Mi'raj.
- **To make it easy to learn and remember** for a mostly oral society.
- **To introduce laws step by step**, as happened with the prohibition of alcohol, which was introduced over several stages.

**Ways of revelation.** Tradition describes revelation as sometimes coming as a sound like ringing, and sometimes through Jibril (AS) appearing as a man. The Prophet (pbuh) would at times feel heavy, even in cold weather.

**Order.** Each verse was placed in its position under the Prophet's (pbuh) direction. The order of the surahs in the book is therefore not the order in which they were revealed.

## How the Qur'an was preserved in his lifetime

1. **Memorisation.** The Prophet (pbuh) recited what he received, and the companions memorised it. People who know the whole Qur'an by heart are called *huffaz* (singular *hafiz*). Memorisation was a normal skill in Arabia, and in the Qur'an it was a form of worship.
2. **Writing.** He appointed scribes of the revelation, among them Zayd ibn Thabit (RA), Ali (RA), Uthman (RA) and Ubayy ibn Ka'b (RA). The text was written on leather, bones, palm stalks and flat stones.
3. **Review.** Every Ramadhan Jibril (AS) reviewed the Qur'an with him, and in the last year they reviewed it twice. Zayd (RA) was present at this final review, which is one reason for his later role.
4. **Prayer and teaching.** The Qur'an was recited in the five daily prayers and in teaching circles, so a mistake would be noticed and corrected at once.

By his death, then, the Qur'an existed in two forms, memory and writing, held by many people, but not in one bound volume.

## The compilation under Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA)

**Cause.** In the first caliphate, many huffaz were martyred in the Battle of Yamama against false claimants to prophethood. Hazrat Umar (RA) feared that parts of the Qur'an might be lost if more huffaz died, and urged Abu Bakr (RA) to have it collected in one place.

**Process.** Abu Bakr (RA) hesitated because the Prophet (pbuh) had not done this, but became convinced that it was a good work. He asked Zayd ibn Thabit (RA), who had written for the Prophet (pbuh) and had a very good memory, to do it. Zayd (RA) was careful: he accepted a verse only when it was both written down in the Prophet's (pbuh) presence and confirmed by witnesses who had heard it. The sheets (*suhuf*) were kept by Abu Bakr (RA), then by Umar (RA), and then by Umar's daughter Hafsa (RA), a wife of the Prophet (pbuh).

**Importance.** This produced a single, checked, written reference copy.

## The standard copies under Hazrat Uthman (RA)

**Cause.** By the time of the third caliph, Islam had spread to Syria, Iraq, Armenia and Azerbaijan. Recitation differed between regions as people learnt from different teachers. In the prescribed Hadith, Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman (RA), who had been with the armies, came to Uthman (RA) in alarm and said: "O chief of the Believers! Save this nation before they differ about the Book (Qur'an) as the Jews and the Christians did before."

**Process, as the Hadith reports it.**

1. Uthman (RA) asked Hafsa (RA) to send him the sheets.
2. He ordered Zayd ibn Thabit (RA) and three members of the Quraysh to copy them into volumes.
3. Where they disagreed on a point of spelling, they were to write in the dialect of the Quraysh, since the Qur'an was revealed in it.
4. A copy was sent to each main region, with a reciter where needed, and other personal copies or fragments were ordered to be burned to prevent confusion.
5. The original sheets were returned to Hafsa (RA).

**Importance.** The Muslims agreed on a single written text. Because the early community, including the companions who had learnt it from the Prophet (pbuh), accepted it, its authority has been unquestioned. Later, the marks that show vowels and dots were added to help non-Arabs read it correctly, but the text itself was not changed.

| Stage | Who | Why | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revelation | Jibril (AS) to the Prophet (pbuh) | Guidance for humankind | 23 years of gradual revelation |
| Preservation | The Prophet (pbuh), huffaz, scribes | Protection of the text | Memory and writing; yearly review |
| First compilation | Abu Bakr (RA) and Zayd (RA) | Deaths at Yamama | One checked set of sheets |
| Standard copies | Uthman (RA) | Differences in recitation | Copies sent to regions |

## Why this matters for Muslims

- **Authority.** A text so carefully checked can be the first source of Shariah.
- **Trust.** The method used, with memory and writing checking each other, is why Muslims are confident in it.
- **Unity.** Muslims worldwide read the same text.
- **Respect.** The care taken explains why Muslims treat the Qur'an with reverence.

## Evidence that the text was not changed

Muslims point to several features of the process. First, the Qur'an was never held by one person: many people memorised it, so any change would have been noticed. Second, the written and the memorised versions were used to check each other, both under Abu Bakr (RA) and under Uthman (RA). Third, the work was done openly by the companions, in the lifetime of those who had heard the Prophet (pbuh) recite. Fourth, none of the regions rejected the standard copies. These points explain why Muslims describe the Qur'an as preserved, and they are good evidence to use when a question asks about the significance of compilation.

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why the Qur'an was compiled in the time of the early caliphs. Use a source of authority. **[6]**

> The first reason was the deaths of many huffaz at Yamama [1]. Umar (RA) feared that parts of the Qur'an might be lost if more memorisers died, so Abu Bakr (RA) had Zayd (RA) collect it in one place [1]. The second reason was disagreement over recitation in the new provinces [1]: Hudhayfa (RA) warned Uthman (RA) to "save this nation before they differ about the Book" as earlier communities had, so Uthman (RA) had standard copies made [1]. The Hadith shows that the early community acted to protect the text [2].

## Common mistakes

- **Saying Uthman (RA) wrote a new Qur'an.** He standardised copies of the text already compiled.
- **Confusing the two stages.** Abu Bakr (RA) collected the text after Yamama; Uthman (RA) distributed standard copies after the dispute over recitation.
- **Forgetting the Prophet's (pbuh) lifetime.** Preservation began with him.
- **Giving the order of revelation as the order of the book.**
- **Not using the Hadith.** It is the prescribed passage and gives the cause, the people and the method.
