These notes condense **4.5.1 Exothermic and endothermic reactions** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462)
specification (for teaching from September 2016). Paper 1 content. Bond energy calculations (4.5.1.3) are
Higher tier only. For explanations use the
[exothermic and endothermic reactions study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-exothermic-and-endothermic-reactions/);
to test yourself use the
[practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-exothermic-and-endothermic-reactions-practice/).

## Core facts

- Energy is **conserved**: it is transferred, never created or destroyed.
- **Exothermic**: transfers energy **to** the surroundings. Surroundings **get hotter**.
- **Endothermic**: takes in energy **from** the surroundings. Surroundings **get colder**.
- Decide from the **temperature change of the surroundings** (calculating ΔH is not needed in 4.5.1.1).
- **Activation energy**: the minimum energy particles need when they collide for a reaction to occur.

## Comparison table

| | Exothermic | Endothermic |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature of surroundings | rises | falls |
| Products vs reactants | lower energy | higher energy |
| Examples | combustion, oxidation, neutralisation | thermal decomposition, citric acid + sodium hydrogencarbonate |
| Everyday use | self-heating cans, hand warmers | sports injury (cold) packs |

## Required practical 4 summary

- Fixed volume of solution in a **polystyrene cup with a lid** (reduces heat loss).
- Record **start temperature**, add reactant, **stir**, record the **highest or lowest** temperature.
- Temperature change = final − start.
- Independent variable: for example concentration, or which metal. Control the rest.
- Improve accuracy: lid, insulation, digital thermometer, repeat and take a mean.

## Reaction profiles

| Feature | Exothermic | Endothermic |
|---|---|---|
| Reactants | high | low |
| Products | lower than reactants | higher than reactants |
| Overall energy change arrow | downwards | upwards |
| Activation energy | up from reactants to the peak | up from reactants to the peak |

Drawing checklist:

1. Label the **vertical axis** energy and the **horizontal axis** progress of reaction.
2. **Curved** line from the reactant level, over a peak, to the product level.
3. Arrow for **activation energy**: reactant level to peak.
4. Arrow for **overall energy change**: reactant level to product level.
5. Label reactants and products.

## Evaluating uses

| Use | Reaction | Points for | Points against |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hand warmer | exothermic | portable, no power needed | single use (some types), waste |
| Self-heating can | exothermic | no stove needed | extra packaging, cost |
| Cold pack | endothermic | instant, no fridge | single use, short cooling time |

For evaluation: compare **cost, safety, convenience, how long it lasts, reusability and waste**, then give a
judgement.

## Higher tier only: bond energy calculations

- **Breaking bonds** needs energy (**endothermic**).
- **Making bonds** releases energy (**exothermic**).
- Energy change = **energy to break bonds − energy released making bonds** (kJ/mol).
- **Negative** answer: exothermic. **Positive** answer: endothermic.

Worked: H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl with H–H 436, Cl–Cl 243, H–Cl 432 (kJ/mol).

- Broken: 436 + 243 = 679
- Formed: 2 × 432 = 864
- Energy change = 679 − 864 = **−185 kJ/mol** (exothermic)

Worked: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O with C–H 413, O=O 498, C=O 805, O–H 464.

- Broken: 4 × 413 + 2 × 498 = 2648
- Formed: 2 × 805 + 4 × 464 = 3466
- Energy change = 2648 − 3466 = **−818 kJ/mol**

Method checklist: write the balanced equation, draw out the bonds, count each type, add broken, add formed,
subtract, state the sign and what it means.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Exothermic | Transfers energy to the surroundings |
| Endothermic | Takes in energy from the surroundings |
| Activation energy | Minimum energy needed for a successful collision |
| Reaction profile | Diagram of energy against reaction progress |
| Bond energy | Energy needed to break one mole of a particular bond |
| Surroundings | Everything in contact with the reacting mixture |

## Exam traps

- **"Energy is made".** It is transferred to the surroundings, not created.
- **Wrong arrow.** Activation energy goes up to the peak, never down.
- **Straight lines.** The specification says to use a curved line.
- **Needs heating to start.** This does not make it endothermic; look at the products' energy level.
- **Sign errors.** Broken minus formed. If formed is bigger, the answer is negative.
- **Miscounting bonds.** Count every bond in every molecule shown in the equation.
- **Units.** kJ/mol for bond energy answers.

## Quick self-test

1. What happens to the temperature of the surroundings in an exothermic reaction?
2. Give two examples of exothermic reactions.
3. Give one example of an endothermic reaction.
4. State what is meant by activation energy.
5. On a profile for an exothermic reaction, are the products higher or lower than the reactants?
6. Why is a lid used on a polystyrene cup in a temperature change experiment?
7. A reaction raises the temperature from 20.0 °C to 27.5 °C. Calculate the temperature change and say what type of reaction it is.
8. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of a disposable hand warmer.
9. (Higher tier only) Which process, breaking bonds or making bonds, releases energy?
10. (Higher tier only) A reaction needs 1500 kJ/mol to break bonds and releases 1800 kJ/mol on forming bonds. Calculate the overall energy change.
11. (Higher tier only) Calculate the energy change for H₂ + F₂ → 2HF with H–H 436, F–F 158, H–F 568 kJ/mol.
12. (Higher tier only) Is the reaction in Question 11 exothermic or endothermic? Explain.

### Answers

1. It rises.
2. Any two: combustion, neutralisation, oxidation, displacement of copper by zinc.
3. Thermal decomposition, or citric acid with sodium hydrogencarbonate.
4. The minimum energy particles must have when they collide for a reaction to occur.
5. Lower.
6. To reduce heat loss to the surroundings, so the measured temperature change is more accurate.
7. +7.5 °C; exothermic (temperature rises).
8. Advantage: portable, needs no external heat source. Disadvantage: single use, so it creates waste.
9. Making bonds.
10. 1500 − 1800 = −300 kJ/mol.
11. Broken: 436 + 158 = 594. Formed: 2 × 568 = 1136. Energy change = 594 − 1136 = −542 kJ/mol.
12. Exothermic, because the energy released making bonds (1136) is greater than the energy needed to break bonds (594), so the change is negative.
