These questions cover section 3.10.1, Magnetic flux density, of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), which is International A-level content. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [magnetic flux density study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-magnetic-flux-density/) or the [magnetic flux density revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-magnetic-flux-density-revision-notes/) first.

Use g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹. In every question the wire is perpendicular to the field unless stated.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Define the tesla. **[2]**

**2.** Show that the tesla is equivalent to kg s⁻² A⁻¹. **[2]**

**3.** A straight wire carrying a current lies along the lines of a uniform magnetic field of flux density B. What is the force on the wire?

A: BIL

B: zero

C: BIL/2

D: it depends on the direction of the current

**[1]**

**4.** A straight wire 0.25 m long carries 3.2 A at right angles to a field of 0.15 T. Calculate the force on it. **[2]**

**5.** A vertical wire carries a current upwards. A horizontal magnetic field points east. State the direction of the force on the wire. **[1]**

**6.** A wire 1.2 m long passes between the poles of a magnet, with 0.050 m of it in a uniform field of 0.40 T. The force on the wire is 0.030 N. Calculate the current. **[2]**

### Section B

**7.** A horizontal aluminium rod of mass 5.0 g and length 0.10 m lies across a horizontal field of 0.060 T.

**(a)** Calculate the current needed for the magnetic force to support the rod's weight. **[2]**

**(b)** The current is doubled. Calculate the initial acceleration of the rod and give its direction. **[2]**

**8.** A metal rod of mass 20 g rests across two horizontal rails 0.080 m apart. A vertical field of 0.25 T acts over the rails. A current of 4.0 A flows through the rod. Ignore friction and assume the current stays constant.

**(a)** Calculate the force on the rod. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate its acceleration. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate its speed 0.50 s after starting from rest. **[1]**

**9.** A rectangular coil of 40 turns carries 0.80 A in a uniform field of 0.15 T. Its plane is parallel to the field, and the two sides perpendicular to the field are each 0.050 m long.

**(a)** Calculate the force on one of these sides. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the coil starts to rotate but there is no resultant force on it. **[2]**

**10.** A student measures the force on a wire carrying a fixed current of 2.5 A for different lengths of wire in a uniform field.

| L / m | 0.020 | 0.040 | 0.060 | 0.080 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F / mN | 4.1 | 7.9 | 12.1 | 16.0 |

**(a)** State the quantity represented by the gradient of a graph of F against L. **[1]**

**(b)** Use the data to determine B. **[3]**

**11.** At one location the Earth's magnetic field may be treated as horizontal, of flux density 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ T, pointing north. A horizontal cable 40 m long carries 250 A from east to west.

**(a)** Calculate the force on the cable. **[2]**

**(b)** State the direction of the force. **[1]**

**12.** A rectangular wire frame hangs from a newtonmeter, with its lower horizontal side, 0.060 m long, in a uniform horizontal field perpendicular to it. The upper part of the frame is outside the field. With no current the reading is 0.250 N. With a current of 2.0 A the reading is 0.274 N.

**(a)** Calculate the flux density. **[2]**

**(b)** State the direction of the magnetic force on the lower side. **[1]**

**(c)** Predict the reading when the current is reversed. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The flux density of a field in which a force of 1 N acts [1] on a 1 m length of wire carrying a current of 1 A at right angles to the field [1]. **[2]**

**2.** B = F/(IL), so T = N A⁻¹ m⁻¹ [1]; N = kg m s⁻², so T = kg m s⁻² A⁻¹ m⁻¹ = **kg s⁻² A⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** F = BIL = 0.15 × 3.2 × 0.25 [1] = **0.12 N** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** **North** [1]. **[1]**

**6.** I = F/(BL) = 0.030/(0.40 × 0.050) [1] = **1.5 A** [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** BIL = mg, so I = 5.0 × 10⁻³ × 9.81 / (0.060 × 0.10) [1] = **8.2 A** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** The magnetic force doubles to 2mg, so the resultant force is mg upwards [1]; a = **9.81 m s⁻² upwards** [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** F = 0.25 × 4.0 × 0.080 = **0.080 N** [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** a = 0.080/0.020 = **4.0 m s⁻²** [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** v = at = 4.0 × 0.50 = **2.0 m s⁻¹** [1]. **[1]**

**9. (a)** F = NBIL = 40 × 0.15 × 0.80 × 0.050 [1] = **0.24 N** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** The current flows in opposite directions in the two sides, so the forces are equal and opposite and their sum is zero [1]; they act along different lines, forming a couple that turns the coil [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** BI (flux density × current) [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Gradient from the line ≈ (16.0 − 4.1) × 10⁻³ / (0.080 − 0.020) [1] = 0.198 N m⁻¹ [1]; B = 0.198 / 2.5 = **0.079 T** [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** F = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ × 250 × 40 [1] = **0.18 N** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Vertically **downwards** [1]. **[1]**

**12. (a)** F = 0.274 − 0.250 = 0.024 N [1]; B = 0.024 / (2.0 × 0.060) = **0.20 T** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Vertically **downwards** (it increases the reading) [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** 0.250 − 0.024 = **0.226 N** [1]. **[1]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the full length of a wire instead of the length in the field.
- Forgetting the number of turns on a coil.
- Using electron flow, or the right hand, to find a direction.
- Taking the gradient of a graph from two table values when a best-fit line is expected.
- Defining the tesla without saying the wire is at right angles to the field.
