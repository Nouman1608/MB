Recall notes for section 3.4 of OCR GCSE (9-1) Economics (J205, Version 2.0, June 2026), examined in Component 02. The [price stability study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-economics-j205-price-stability-ocr-gcse-economics/) explains every method in full; the [price stability practice questions](/resources/ocr-gcse-economics-j205-price-stability-ocr-gcse-economics-practice/) test them. Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- Explain **price stability** and **inflation**, including **real** and **nominal** values.
- Explain how inflation is measured using the **CPI**.
- **Calculate** the effect of inflation on prices.
- **Analyse** recent and historical inflation figures.
- **Evaluate** causes and consequences for **consumers, producers, savers and the government**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Inflation | A sustained rise in the general price level |
| Inflation rate | Percentage change in the price index over a year |
| Price stability | Low and predictable inflation (UK target: 2% CPI) |
| Deflation | A sustained fall in the general price level |
| Price index | Prices compared with a base year set at 100 |
| CPI | Consumer Price Index: weighted index of a typical household basket |
| Weight | An item's share of household spending |
| Nominal value | In money of the day |
| Real value | Adjusted for inflation; shows purchasing power |
| Purchasing power | The quantity of goods and services money can buy |
| Menu costs | Costs to firms of changing prices often |
| Wage-price spiral | Prices up → pay claims → costs up → prices up again |

## Formulae

| Task | Formula |
|---|---|
| Single-item index | Current price ÷ base-year price × 100 |
| Inflation rate | (new index − old index) ÷ old index × 100 |
| Contribution of one category | Weight × its price change (percentage points) |
| New price | Old price × (1 + rate) |
| Several years | Multiply the growth factors |
| Real value | Nominal value ÷ price index × 100 |
| Real change | (1 + nominal change) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1 |
| Purchasing power of cash | Amount ÷ cumulative growth factor |

## Worked reminders

**Contributions.** Food (weight 0.30) +6%, energy (0.10) +20%, other (0.60) +1.5%: 1.8 + 2.0 + 0.9 = **4.7%**. Energy is a tenth of spending but supplies over 40% of the inflation.

**Purchasing power.** With 3% inflation for two years, £500 of cash buys 500 ÷ 1.0609 = **£471.30** of goods at starting prices.

**Real interest.** Savings at 4.5% with inflation at 3.0%: real return ≈ 1.045 ÷ 1.03 − 1 = **+1.5%**. Savers gain only when the interest rate beats inflation.

## Why not zero inflation?

- The CPI may overstate inflation when quality improves.
- Zero leaves no margin before **deflation**.
- Deflation: people delay purchases, firms' sales and jobs fall, and debts become harder to repay.

## Causes

| Cause | Trigger |
|---|---|
| Demand-pull | Spending grows faster than output: confidence, cheap credit, tax cuts, government spending, exports |
| Cost-push | Higher energy, raw material, wage or indirect tax costs |
| Imported | A weaker currency raises import prices |
| Expectations | Pay claims based on expected price rises keep inflation going |

## Consequences: losers and winners

| Group | Lose | May gain |
|---|---|---|
| Consumers | Pay lagging prices; fixed incomes | Fixed-rate borrowers |
| Producers | Rising costs, menu costs, lost export competitiveness, uncertainty | Firms able to raise prices quickly |
| Savers | Interest below inflation | Interest above inflation |
| Government | Higher costs of wages, supplies, benefits | More tax revenue; real value of debt falls |

**Evaluate with:** size, expected or unexpected, how long, whether incomes and interest rates keep pace.

## Exam traps

- Falling inflation ≠ falling prices.
- Price stability ≠ zero inflation.
- Multiply, do not add, across years.
- A large weight with a small rise can matter less than a small weight with a big rise.
- Name the group the question asks about.

## Self-test

1. Define inflation.
2. What is meant by price stability, and what is the UK's inflation target?
3. Define deflation and give one reason it can harm an economy.
4. A cinema ticket cost £4.00 in the base year and costs £4.80 now. Calculate its price index.
5. The CPI rises from 112.0 to 115.4. Calculate the inflation rate to one decimal place.
6. Food has a weight of 0.15 and its prices rise 8%. What does food contribute to inflation?
7. An £80 item faces inflation of 4% and then 6%. What does it cost after two years?
8. Pay rises 3% while inflation is 5%. Calculate the change in real pay to one decimal place.
9. Nominal pay is £1,800 and the price index is 120. Calculate real pay in base-year prices.
10. Explain why savers may lose from inflation.
11. Who may gain from unexpected inflation?
12. Explain how a wage-price spiral keeps inflation going.

### Answers

1. A **sustained rise in the general level of prices**.
2. **Low and predictable** inflation; the UK target is **2%** CPI inflation.
3. A sustained **fall** in the general price level; people **delay spending**, so firms cut output and jobs (or: debts become harder to repay).
4. 4.80 ÷ 4.00 × 100 = **120**.
5. 3.4 ÷ 112.0 × 100 = **3.0%**.
6. 0.15 × 8 = **1.2 percentage points**.
7. 80 × 1.04 × 1.06 = **£88.19**.
8. 1.03 ÷ 1.05 − 1 = **−1.9%**.
9. 1,800 ÷ 120 × 100 = **£1,500**.
10. If the **interest rate is below inflation**, the balance grows more slowly than prices, so it buys less.
11. **Borrowers** with fixed-rate debts (and the government, whose debt falls in real terms).
12. Rising prices lead workers to **demand higher pay**; higher wages raise firms' **costs**, so firms **raise prices** again, and the cycle repeats.
