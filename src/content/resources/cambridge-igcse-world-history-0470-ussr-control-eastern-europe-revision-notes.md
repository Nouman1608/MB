These notes condense **key question 6 of Core Content Option B** in Cambridge IGCSE History 0470: "How secure was the
USSR's control over Eastern Europe, 1948-c.1989?" The specified content has four parts: resistance in Hungary (1956)
and Czechoslovakia (1968), the Berlin Wall, Solidarity in Poland, and Gorbachev and the collapse of Soviet control. For
explanation and worked answers, read the
[Soviet control study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-ussr-control-eastern-europe/); to apply the
facts, try the [Soviet control practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-ussr-control-eastern-europe-practice/).

## How control was kept

- **Military:** Red Army garrisons; the Warsaw Pact (1955) bound the satellites into a Soviet-led alliance.
- **Political:** loyal communist parties; leaders approved by Moscow; secret police in every state.
- **Economic:** Comecon (1949) organised trade around Soviet needs.
- **Ideological:** censorship and propaganda; the Brezhnev Doctrine (1968) justified intervention.

## Hungary, 1956: sequence

1. **February 1956** -- Khrushchev's Secret Speech attacks Stalin.
2. **July 1956** -- Rákosi removed but replaced by another hard-liner, Gerő.
3. **23 October** -- students and workers demonstrate in Budapest; Stalin's statue toppled; fighting begins.
4. **24 October onwards** -- Imre Nagy becomes prime minister; Soviet troops pull back from Budapest.
5. **1 November** -- Nagy announces neutrality and withdrawal from the Warsaw Pact.
6. **4 November** -- Soviet tanks crush the rising; Kádár installed.
7. **Aftermath** -- thousands killed, about 200,000 refugees; Nagy executed in 1958.

**Shows about security:** control could be restored only by force; the West would not intervene.

## Czechoslovakia, 1968: sequence

1. **January 1968** -- Dubček replaces Novotný.
2. **April 1968** -- Action Programme: "socialism with a human face".
3. **Spring-summer** -- censorship ends; open debate; Warsaw Pact leaders alarmed.
4. **20-21 August** -- Warsaw Pact invasion led by the USSR; Romania takes no part.
5. **Resistance** -- non-violent; Jan Palach's self-burning (January 1969).
6. **April 1969** -- Dubček replaced by Husák; "normalisation" restores hard-line rule.

**Shows about security:** even reform within communism was not allowed; the Brezhnev Doctrine made intervention policy.

## The Berlin Wall

- **Cause:** mass emigration from East to West through Berlin; loss of skilled workers; propaganda damage.
- **Background:** Khrushchev's Berlin ultimatum (1958); failed Vienna summit with Kennedy (June 1961).
- **Built:** night of 12-13 August 1961; wire first, then concrete.
- **Results:** emigration stopped; East Germany stabilised; tension at Checkpoint Charlie (October 1961); Kennedy's
  Berlin visit (June 1963); symbol of division until 9 November 1989.

**Shows about security:** control of East Germany needed a physical barrier.

## Solidarity, 1980-89

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1978-79 | Polish pope John Paul II elected and visits Poland |
| August 1980 | Gdańsk shipyard strikes led by Lech Wałęsa; Gdańsk Agreement |
| 1980-81 | Solidarity grows to about 10 million members |
| December 1981 | Martial law under General Jaruzelski; Solidarity banned |
| 1983 | Wałęsa awarded the Nobel Peace Prize |
| February-April 1989 | Round-table talks between government and Solidarity |
| June 1989 | Partly free elections; Solidarity wins almost every seat it can contest |
| August-September 1989 | First non-communist prime minister in the bloc |

**Shows about security:** workers themselves rejected communism; martial law delayed but did not end opposition.

## 1989: the collapse

- **Hungary** opens its border with Austria; East Germans escape west.
- **East Germany:** mass demonstrations in Leipzig; Honecker replaced; Berlin Wall opened on **9 November 1989**.
- **Czechoslovakia:** the "Velvet Revolution"; Václav Havel becomes president in December.
- **Bulgaria:** Zhivkov removed in November.
- **Romania:** violent revolution; Ceaușescu executed in December.
- **Afterwards:** Germany reunited (October 1990); Warsaw Pact dissolved (1991).

## Gorbachev: responsible or not?

| Gorbachev responsible | Other causes |
|---|---|
| Glasnost and perestroika encouraged demands for change | Soviet economy stagnating; arms race costs |
| Abandoned the Brezhnev Doctrine: no tanks in 1989 | Afghanistan war drained resources |
| Troop cuts announced in 1988 | Solidarity and popular movements forced the pace |
| Told satellite leaders they must reform | Communism had never won consent; force had held it |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| De-Stalinisation | Khrushchev's criticism of Stalin and relaxation of terror after 1956 |
| ÁVH | Hungary's hated secret police |
| Prague Spring | Period of reform in Czechoslovakia in 1968 |
| Socialism with a human face | Dubček's slogan for a freer, reformed communism |
| Brezhnev Doctrine | Claim that the bloc could intervene to protect socialism in any member state |
| Normalisation | Restoration of hard-line control in Czechoslovakia after 1968 |
| Solidarity | Independent Polish trade union founded in 1980 |
| Martial law | Military rule with civil rights suspended |
| Glasnost | Openness: more freedom of speech and information |
| Perestroika | Restructuring of the Soviet economy |

## Exam traps

- **Nagy versus Dubček.** Nagy wanted to leave the Warsaw Pact; Dubček did not.
- **Who invaded in 1968.** Warsaw Pact forces led by the USSR, not the USSR alone.
- **Berlin dates.** Blockade 1948-49; Wall built 1961; opened 1989.
- **Solidarity was not crushed for good.** Banned in 1981, legal again and winning elections in 1989.
- **Gorbachev's aim.** Reform, not the end of communism; a judgement must weigh other factors.

## Self-test

1. What did Khrushchev's Secret Speech of February 1956 do? *It denounced Stalin's crimes and encouraged hopes of reform.*
2. Who became Hungarian prime minister during the rising? *Imre Nagy.*
3. What announcement on 1 November 1956 triggered the Soviet invasion? *Hungary's withdrawal from the Warsaw Pact.*
4. Who replaced Nagy? *János Kádár.*
5. What was the Action Programme of 1968? *Dubček's reform plan, including an end to censorship.*
6. Which Warsaw Pact state refused to take part in the 1968 invasion? *Romania.*
7. Who replaced Dubček in 1969? *Gustáv Husák.*
8. Why was the Berlin Wall built? *To stop East Germans emigrating to the West through Berlin.*
9. In which city did Solidarity begin? *Gdańsk.*
10. Who declared martial law in Poland in December 1981? *General Jaruzelski.*
11. What did Hungary do in 1989 that helped East Germans escape? *Opened its border with Austria.*
12. On what date was the Berlin Wall opened? *9 November 1989.*
