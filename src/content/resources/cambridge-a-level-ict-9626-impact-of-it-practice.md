## Section A

**1.** Give **two** ways in which IT is used in sport. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** benefits to a business of using robots in manufacturing. **[2]**

**3.** Identify **two** drawbacks of online banking for customers. **[2]**

**4.** Describe how IT has changed the way news is reported. **[2]**

**5.** State **one** effect of IT on family life and explain why it happens. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Redwood Logistics compares robots and staff in its warehouse. A robot picks 90 items an hour and a member of staff picks 30 items an hour. Both work for an 8-hour shift.

**(a)** Calculate the number of items picked by one robot and by one member of staff in a shift. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate how many times more items the robot picks. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain **two** reasons why the company may still keep some staff. **[4]**

**7.** A government department processes 40,000 tax returns each year. A paper return costs £12 to process and an online return costs £1.50.

**(a)** Calculate the yearly saving if all returns are made online. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **two** drawbacks of making online returns the only option. **[4]**

**8.** A hospital introduces electronic patient records that can be seen in any ward.

**(a)** Explain **two** benefits to patients. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain **two** risks and how each might be reduced. **[4]**

**9.** A shopping centre installs 64 CCTV cameras. Each camera records 2 gigabytes of video a day, and the recordings are kept for 30 days.

**(a)** Calculate the storage needed in gigabytes. **[2]**

**(b)** Discuss the impact of the cameras on shoppers and on the police. **[6]**

**10.** A company plans to record the websites its employees visit and the number of keystrokes they type. Evaluate this monitoring from the point of view of the company and of the employees. **[6]**

**11.** Discuss the impact of IT on politics. **[6]**

**12.** Explain why a small independent shop may be harmed by the growth of e-business, and give **one** way in which it may use IT to respond. **[4]**

**13.** A football club introduces sensors in training shirts that record each player's heart rate and distance run. Assess the impact of this on the club and on the players. **[5]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Any two from: performance analysis with sensors or video [1]; wearable devices recording heart rate and speed [1]; video review of referees' decisions [1]; ball and goal-line tracking [1]; online ticket sales or streaming of events [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two from: faster and more consistent production [1]; can work 24 hours a day [1]; remove people from dangerous work [1]; lower cost per item over time [1]; fewer errors [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two from: no face-to-face service, and branches may close [1]; risk of phishing and fraud [1]; needs internet access and a device [1]; systems can fail or be unavailable [1]. **[2]**

**4.** News is now published online and shared on social media almost as it happens [1]; ordinary people can record and report events with phones, not just journalists [1]. **[2]**

**5.** One effect [1] and a reason [1]. For example: families can keep in touch with distant relatives [1], because video calls are cheap and show the person [1]. Or: there is less face-to-face time at home [1], because members spend time on separate devices [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Robot: 90 × 8 = 720 items [1]; staff: 30 × 8 = 240 items [1].

**(b)** 720 ÷ 240 = 3 times [1].

**(c)** Any two reasons, each with an explanation: robots cannot easily handle unusual or fragile items [1], so staff are needed for them [1]; robots need people to program, maintain and repair them [1], so skilled staff are still needed [1]; staff can fill in when a robot fails, so work does not stop [1]. **[7]**

**7. (a)** Saving per return = 12 − 1.50 = £10.50 [1]. 10.50 × 40,000 = £420,000 [1].

**(b)** Any two, each worth 2 marks (the drawback [1] and the reason or effect [1]): people without internet access or a device cannot file a return, so they are excluded [2]; some people lack the skills or confidence and may make errors [2]; online systems can fail or be hacked, exposing personal and financial data [2]; the website may be unavailable at the filing deadline [2]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** Any two, each worth 2 marks (the benefit [1] and the explanation [1]): doctors in any ward can see the whole history, so treatment is more accurate and quicker [2]; records are not lost or unreadable, so wrong or repeated treatment is less likely [2]; a patient's allergies and drugs are shown immediately in emergencies [2]. **[4]**

**(b)** Hackers or staff may view data without authority [1]; reduced by passwords, access levels and audit logs [1]. A system failure could make records unavailable [1]; reduced by backups and a stand-by system [1]. Any two risks with a method. **[4]**

**9. (a)** 64 × 2 × 30 [1] = 3,840 GB [1].

**(b)** Shoppers: feel safer and thefts are deterred [1]; help can be sent quickly in an emergency [1]; but they are watched all the time, which reduces privacy [1]; images could be misused or shared [1]. Police: recordings provide evidence to identify and prosecute offenders [1]; help to track suspects [1]; but a lot of time is needed to review footage, and facial recognition may identify people wrongly [1]. Maximum 6 marks. **[8]**

**10.** Company: can check that staff are working and not misusing time [1]; can detect security risks such as visits to unsafe sites [1]; can show evidence in a disciplinary case [1]. Employees: feel mistrusted, causing stress and lower morale [1]; keystroke counts do not measure quality of work [1]; the company holds personal data that must be protected [1]. Conclusion, for example that monitoring is acceptable if staff are told, it is limited to work use and it is proportionate [1]. Maximum 6 marks. **[6]**

**11.** Benefits: parties reach voters directly and cheaply through social media and targeted adverts [1]; people can find policies and register to vote online [1]; more young people take part [1]. Drawbacks: false information spreads quickly [1]; targeted messages can be used to manipulate opinion [1]; electronic voting systems could be hacked, reducing trust [1]; people without access to IT are left out [1]. Conclusion with a judgement [1]. Maximum 6 marks. **[6]**

**12.** Online shops can sell to a wider market at lower costs, since they do not need expensive premises or many staff [1], and they are open all the time [1], so a small shop may lose customers who then buy online at lower prices [1]. The shop could set up its own website or sell through an online marketplace [1]. **[4]**

**13.** Club: coaches can plan training and avoid injuries by detecting over-work [1]; helps pick players and measure improvement [1]. Players: personal health data is held by the club and could be misused or shared [1]; players may feel under constant pressure [1]. Judgement: benefits outweigh drawbacks if data is protected and players give consent [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a technology and stopping, without saying what changes.
- Making only positive points in discuss or evaluate questions.
- Forgetting to give the unit in calculations, or the number of days or cameras.
- Giving general privacy points without saying what data is held and who might misuse it.
- Not applying the answer to the group named (shoppers, police, staff and employers).
