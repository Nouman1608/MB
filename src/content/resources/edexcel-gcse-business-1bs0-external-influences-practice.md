## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an external influence on a business? **[1]**

A: The owner's choice of price

B: A change in interest rates

C: The layout of the shop

D: The number of staff the owner decides to employ

**2.** Define the term external influence. **[2]**

**3.** Which **two** of the following are possible responses by a café to falling consumer incomes? **[2]**

A: Introduce a cheaper lunch menu

B: Stop paying the legal minimum wage

C: Promote value-for-money offers

D: Open a second, more expensive café

E: Raise all prices by 20%

**4.** State **one** possible response by a shop to customers expecting to pay by contactless card. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why external influences are important to a start-up business. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** possible response by a business to a rise in interest rates. **[3]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Fairhaven Fudge sells boxes of fudge for £8. Its variable cost is £3.50 a box and it sells 12,000 boxes a year. A new law on food labelling adds £0.30 to the variable cost of each box.

**(a)** Calculate the fall in total annual contribution if price and sales do not change. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the price Fairhaven Fudge would need to charge to keep its contribution per box the same. **[1]**

**8.** Outline **one** response Fairhaven Fudge could make to the new labelling law, other than raising its price. **[2]**

**9.** Outline **one** way a weaker pound could be an opportunity for a UK business. **[2]**

**10.** Brookfield Books is an independent bookshop. A large online retailer now offers next-day delivery of books at lower prices. Analyse how Brookfield Books could respond to this change in technology. **[6]**

**11.** Ashgrove Plastics makes plastic food containers. It imports raw materials priced in US dollars. Last year it paid $200,000 for materials when £1 = $1.25. This year the same materials cost $200,000 but £1 = $1.16.

**(a)** Calculate the cost of materials in pounds last year and this year. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in the cost in pounds. **[2]**

**12.** Rowan Physio is a physiotherapy clinic. Its rent and wages have risen with inflation, and a rise in income tax means some clients are spending less. The owner is considering two responses.

Option 1: raise the price of a session from £50 to £55. Forecast sessions fall from 4,000 to 3,700 a year.

Option 2: keep the price at £50 and offer online video sessions, which cost £8 less per session to provide because no treatment room is used. Forecast sessions stay at 4,000, of which 1,000 would be online.

Justify which option the owner should choose. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A factor outside the business that it cannot control [1] but that affects its revenue, costs or decisions [1]. **[2]**

**3.** A [1]. C [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any one from: install a contactless card reader; accept mobile phone payments [1]. **[1]**

**5.** A start-up usually has little spare cash [1]. A sudden external change, such as a rise in costs or a fall in customer spending, can quickly create a cash shortage [1], so the owner must respond quickly, for example by cutting costs, or the business may not survive [1]. **[3]**

**6.** The business could repay part of its variable-rate loan or overdraft using savings [1]. This reduces the amount on which the higher rate is charged [1], so its interest costs rise by less and more profit is kept, although it leaves less cash for emergencies [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Extra cost = 12,000 × £0.30 [1] = **£3,600** fall in contribution [1]

**(b)** New price = £8.00 + £0.30 = **£8.30** [1] **[3]**

**8.** Fairhaven Fudge could look for a cheaper supplier of packaging or ingredients [1] to offset the extra £0.30 a box, so it keeps its profit without risking customers' reaction to a higher price [1]. **[2]**

**9.** A weaker pound makes UK products cheaper for overseas customers [1], so a UK business could start or increase exporting and sell more abroad without cutting its pound price [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Brookfield Books cannot match the online retailer's prices on every book, so it could compete on service instead [1], such as staff recommendations and author events, which an online retailer cannot offer in the same way [1]. This gives customers a reason to visit, which could protect sales [1]. It could also start its own website or a click-and-collect service [1], so local customers can order books for the next day too, although building and running a website adds costs [1]. Either response takes time to work, and if customers mainly choose on price, Brookfield's sales may still fall [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** Last year: $200,000 ÷ 1.25 = **£160,000** [1]. This year: $200,000 ÷ 1.16 = **£172,414** (to the nearest pound) [1]

**(b)** Increase = £172,414 − £160,000 = £12,414 [1]. £12,414 ÷ £160,000 × 100 = **7.8%** [1] **[4]**

**12.** Option 1 revenue = 3,700 × £55 = £203,500 [1]. Present revenue = 4,000 × £50 = £200,000, so Option 1 adds £3,500 of revenue, and with 300 fewer sessions some session costs are also saved [1].

Option 2 revenue stays at £200,000, but 1,000 online sessions save £8 each, so costs fall by £8,000 [1]. Compared with now, Option 2 improves profit by about £8,000 [1].

For Option 1: it covers rising rent and wages directly [1], but raising prices when clients' incomes are falling risks losing more than 300 sessions [1].

For Option 2: it keeps the price at £50 when clients are spending less, and online sessions may suit clients who want to save travel time [1]; however, some treatments need hands-on work, so not every client may accept an online session [1].

Judgement: the owner should choose Option 2, because it is forecast to improve profit by £8,000 without raising prices while clients have less to spend; Option 1 would only match this if each of the 300 lost sessions saved more than £15 of costs ((£8,000 − £3,500) ÷ 300), which is unlikely when rent and wages are mainly fixed [1]. This depends on 1,000 clients being willing to have online sessions. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* Strong answers notice that Option 1 changes revenue while Option 2 changes costs, and compare the effect on profit, not just revenue.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing the external change without suggesting a response.
- Suggesting a response that breaks the law, as in Question 3 option B.
- Multiplying by the exchange rate instead of dividing in Question 11.
- Comparing revenue only, not profit, in Question 12.
