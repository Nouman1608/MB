## Questions

**1.** (Higher Tier only) Describe how monoclonal antibodies against a chosen antigen are produced. **[4]**

**2.** (Higher Tier only) Explain why a B lymphocyte is fused with a tumour cell rather than being used on its own. **[3]**

**3.** (Higher Tier only) A pregnancy test strip has mobile antibodies with coloured beads, a test line and a control line.

**(a)** Name the substance the test detects. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how a coloured test line forms in a positive result. **[3]**
**(c)** A test shows no coloured line at the control line. State what this means. **[1]**

**4.** (Higher Tier only) A laboratory measures PSA in the blood of four men using monoclonal antibodies. In this question, levels above 4.0 ng/mL lead to further tests.

| Man | A | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PSA (ng/mL) | 1.4 | 3.6 | 5.2 | 9.8 |

**(a)** State which men are offered further tests. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times higher man D's level is than 4.0 ng/mL. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the result alone cannot confirm prostate cancer. **[2]**

**5.** (Higher Tier only) Explain how a monoclonal antibody could be used to treat a cancer with fewer side effects than the drug alone. **[4]**

**6.** A student prepares to inoculate a nutrient agar plate. State three precautions that prevent contamination of the culture. **[3]**

**7.** Explain each of the following in aseptic technique.

**(a)** Nutrient agar and glassware are autoclaved before use. **[2]**
**(b)** The lid of the Petri dish is taped in only two or three places. **[2]**

**8.** (Calculation) A bacterial colony grows from a diameter of 12 mm to a diameter of 18 mm. Calculate the percentage increase in its area. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. **[5]**

**9.** (Calculation) Two antibiotic discs, each of diameter 6 mm, are placed on a plate of bacteria. Antibiotic X gives a clear zone of diameter 18 mm. Antibiotic Y gives a clear zone of diameter 26 mm. Calculate the area of clear agar around each disc (not counting the disc) and state what the results suggest. **[5]**

**10.** Put these stages of developing a new medicine in the correct order, and give the main aim of each of the clinical stages: phase 3 trial, preclinical testing, phase 1 trial, phase 2 trial, discovery of a candidate chemical. **[4]**

**11.** A company tests an inhaled drug for asthma. 100 patients were given the drug and 100 were given a placebo inhaler. Over 12 weeks, 64 patients on the drug and 38 on the placebo had no asthma attacks. Side effects were reported by 12 patients on the drug and 5 on the placebo.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of each group with no attacks, and the gap between them. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why a placebo group and double-blind design were used. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate whether the company should apply for approval, using the data. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The antigen is injected into a mouse [1] and B lymphocytes making the antibody are taken from it [1]. These are fused with tumour cells to make hybridoma cells, which are screened to select those making the right antibody [1]. The selected cells are cloned and grown in large numbers, and the antibodies are collected and purified [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A description needs the stages in a logical order; "fuse" without saying what is fused earns less than naming lymphocytes and tumour cells.

**2.** A lymphocyte makes the specific antibody [1] but does not divide for long, so would give little antibody [1]. The tumour cell divides without limit, so the hybridoma forms a large clone making one antibody [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Explain" needs the role of each cell stated separately; answers saying the tumour cell makes the antibody lose the first point.

**3. (a)** hCG [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Urine carries mobile antibodies bound to hCG along the strip [1]. Fixed antibodies at the test line bind the hCG [1]. Beads attached to the antibodies collect there and give a coloured line [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** The test has not worked and should be repeated [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the hormone, not just "pregnancy hormone", and say that the antibodies are held in place at the line.

**4. (a)** Men C and D [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 9.8 ÷ 4.0 = 2.45, so about 2.5 times [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Other conditions of the prostate can also raise PSA [1], so a raised level is a reason for further tests, not proof of cancer [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In data questions, quote the value and use the cut-off given in the question rather than a value you remember.

**5.** Cancer cells have antigens on their surface that few healthy cells have [1]. A monoclonal antibody that binds this antigen is made [1] and attached to the drug [1]. The drug is delivered to the cancer cells, so fewer healthy cells are exposed and there are fewer side effects [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Both the targeting (antigen and antibody) and the benefit (fewer healthy cells harmed) are needed. Do not claim the treatment cures cancer.

**6.** Any three from the following, one mark each: wipe the bench with alcohol disinfectant; sterilise the loop in a flame until red hot; work close to a Bunsen burner; lift the lid only slightly and briefly; use sterilised (autoclaved) agar and dishes. [1][1][1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Three different precautions are needed. Repeating "keep it sterile" in different words scores once at most.

**7. (a)** Steam under pressure heats the items above 100 °C [1], which kills all microorganisms and their spores so none grow in the culture other than the one added [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Oxygen can still enter [1], so anaerobic pathogens are less likely to grow [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Each "explain" needs a reason as well as the technique; for taping, "to keep the lid on" alone gives only the obvious half.

**8.** First r = 12 ÷ 2 = 6 mm [1] and area = π × 6² = 113.1 mm² [1]. Then r = 18 ÷ 2 = 9 mm [1] and area = π × 9² = 254.5 mm² [1]. Increase = (254.5 − 113.1) ÷ 113.1 × 100 = **125%** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the radius, not the diameter. Percentage change is divided by the original value, not the new one.

**9.** Radius of disc = 3 mm [1]. X: π × (9² − 3²) = π × 72 = **226 mm²** [1]. Y: π × (13² − 3²) = π × 160 = **503 mm²** [1]. Y gives about 2.2 times the area [1]. Y appears more effective against this bacterium, provided both discs held the same amount of antibiotic and the plates were treated the same [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A fair comparison needs the conditions kept equal. Say what must be controlled before drawing the conclusion.

**10.** Order: discovery of a candidate chemical, preclinical testing [1], phase 1, phase 2, phase 3 [1]. Phase 1 aims to test safety (healthy volunteers) [1]. Phase 3 aims to confirm that it works and is safe in many patients compared with a placebo or existing treatment [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Two aims are asked for. Writing "check if it works" for phase 1 is a common slip because phase 1 is mainly about safety.

**11. (a)** Drug: 64 ÷ 100 × 100 = 64% [1]. Placebo: 38% [1]. Difference = 26 percentage points [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The placebo group shows how many improve without the drug, through expectation or natural change [1]. Double-blind means neither patients nor doctors know who has the drug, so they cannot bias the results [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** More were attack-free on the drug (64% against 38%), a difference of 26 points [1]. Side effects were more common on the drug (12% against 5%), but this was still a small proportion [1]. Reasonable to apply for approval, as the benefit appears greater than the risk, though further monitoring is needed and a larger sample would reduce the effect of chance [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs figures from both benefit and harm, followed by a judgement. A conclusion with no data from the table is not enough.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that the tumour cell makes the antibody, or that the hybridoma is "a monoclonal antibody".
- Naming "a pregnancy hormone" instead of hCG, and forgetting what the control line shows.
- Describing monoclonal antibody cancer treatment as a proven cure.
- Giving three aseptic precautions that are really one idea, such as "keep it clean".
- Leaving out why a technique works, for example not saying that autoclaving also kills spores.
- Using the diameter in πr², or forgetting to subtract the disc when asked for clear agar.
- Dividing by the new value rather than the original when finding percentage change.
- Treating phase 1 as a test of effectiveness.
- Judging a trial from the drug group alone, without comparing with the placebo group.
