Condensed recall for **7.1 Accounting concepts**: business entity, consistency, duality, going concern, historic cost, matching / accruals, materiality, money measurement, prudence, realisation. Figures below are invented; amounts in dollars.

## Key terms: the ten concepts

| Concept | One-line meaning | Typical clue in a question |
|---|---|---|
| Business entity | The business is separate from its owner | Owner's private bill paid by the business |
| Consistency | Same methods every period | Owner wants to change depreciation method to raise profit |
| Duality | Every transaction has two equal effects | "Name the accounts debited and credited" |
| Going concern | The business will continue for the foreseeable future | Business about to close; assets at break-up value |
| Historic cost | Assets recorded at original cost | Asset's market value has risen |
| Matching / accruals | Revenue and expenses in the period they belong to | Unpaid bills at year end; payments in advance |
| Materiality | Small items may be treated simply | Stapler or waste bin bought; small amount |
| Money measurement | Only items reliably measured in money | Skilled staff, reputation, morale |
| Prudence | Do not overstate profit or assets; recognise expected losses | Inventory below cost; doubtful debts |
| Realisation | Revenue recognised when goods or services are provided | Deposit for goods delivered next year |

## Effects to remember

| Situation | Concept | Profit | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Private expense charged to the business | Business entity | Increase when corrected | Drawings up, capital down |
| Expense owing at year end | Matching | Decrease | Other payables (accrual) |
| Expense paid in advance | Matching | Increase | Other receivables (prepayment) |
| Income received in advance | Matching / realisation | Decrease | Other payables |
| Inventory net realisable value below cost | Prudence | Decrease | Inventory down |
| Goods ordered but not delivered, recorded as sales | Realisation | Decrease | Trade receivables down |
| Small item capitalised against policy | Materiality | Decrease | Non-current assets down |
| Market value of premises rises | Historic cost | No change | No change |

**Quick figures.** Insurance of $1,800 paid on 1 October for 12 months, year end 31 December: expense $450 (3 months), prepaid $1,350. Electricity paid $2,100 includes $300 for last year; $500 for December is owing: expense 2,100 − 300 + 500 = **$2,300**.

## Look-alike pairs

- **Matching vs realisation**: matching places revenue and expenses in the right period; realisation decides whether revenue has been earned at all.
- **Prudence vs realisation**: both stop gains being anticipated; prudence also makes you recognise expected losses now.
- **Historic cost vs going concern**: historic cost gives the value recorded; going concern justifies keeping it (less depreciation) instead of a sale value.
- **Business entity vs money measurement**: both explain omissions; entity excludes the owner's affairs, money measurement excludes what cannot be valued.
- **Materiality vs consistency**: materiality allows simple treatment of small items; consistency requires the same limit every year.

## Exam traps

- **Definition only.** Apply the concept to the case and state the effect on figures.
- **"Prudence means understate."** It means do not overstate.
- **Wrong months.** For prepayments and income in advance, count the months belonging to next year.
- **Drawings, not expenses.** Private spending never reduces profit.
- **One-sided corrections.** Every adjustment changes profit and an asset or liability.
- **Going concern at closure.** Name the assumption that fails and the value that replaces book value.
- **Market value.** Historic cost means a rise in value is not recorded.

## Self-test

1. Name the concept: the owner takes goods for her own use and they are recorded as drawings.
2. Name the concept: a business keeps using reducing balance depreciation each year.
3. A shop pays rent of $4,800 on 1 September for 12 months. Its year ends 31 December. What is the rent expense and the prepayment?
4. Which concept explains why a highly skilled manager is not shown as an asset?
5. Inventory cost $900 and can be sold for $600. At what value is it shown, and which concept applies?
6. A customer pays a $500 deposit in December for goods delivered in February. How is it treated at the December year end?
7. Distinguish between matching and realisation.
8. Why does going concern support showing assets at cost less depreciation?
9. Land bought for $70,000 is now worth $150,000. What value appears and why?
10. A waste bin costing $25 that will last five years is charged as an expense. Which concept allows this?
11. Use duality to explain the effect of repaying $2,000 of a loan by bank transfer.
12. The draft profit is $30,000. A private holiday of $1,200 was charged to travel expenses and wages of $800 are owing. Calculate the corrected profit.

**Answers:** 1. Business entity. 2. Consistency. 3. Expense 4,800 × 4/12 = **$1,600**; prepayment **$3,200**. 4. Money measurement. 5. $600, prudence (lower of cost and net realisable value). 6. Not revenue yet (realisation); shown as a current liability, income received in advance (or a payable to the customer). 7. Matching places revenue and expenses in the period they belong to, whatever the cash timing; realisation decides when revenue is earned, when goods or services are provided. 8. The assets will be used in the business rather than sold, so spreading their cost over their useful life is sensible. 9. $70,000, historic cost. 10. Materiality. 11. Assets (bank) fall by $2,000 and liabilities (loan) fall by $2,000. 12. 30,000 + 1,200 − 800 = **$30,400**.
