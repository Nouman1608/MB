## What coronary heart disease is

Coronary heart disease (CHD) is a **non-communicable** disease: it is not caused by a pathogen and cannot be
passed from person to person.

### Method in steps: the cause-and-effect chain

```
1. Layers of fatty material (including cholesterol) build up inside the coronary arteries.
2. The arteries narrow.
3. Less blood flows through them to the heart muscle.
4. The heart muscle gets less oxygen (and glucose).
5. Less aerobic respiration in the muscle cells, so they release less energy and can be damaged or die.
```

A complete blockage can cause a heart attack. Name the **coronary** arteries: CHD is not a blockage in the aorta
or in the heart chambers.

## Treating narrowed coronary arteries

| Treatment | How it works | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stent | A small mesh tube placed inside the narrowed artery to hold it open | Blood flow improves straight away; the procedure is far less major than open-heart surgery; recovery is quick | Any procedure carries a risk of infection or bleeding; a blood clot can form at the stent; the artery can narrow again |
| Statins | Drugs that lower blood cholesterol, which slows the rate at which fatty material is deposited | No surgery; reduce the risk of heart attack and stroke; easy to take | Must be taken regularly, long term; can cause side effects (such as muscle aches); act slowly, so do not clear an existing blockage |

Stents treat the narrowing that is already there. Statins reduce further build-up. They are often used together.

## Faulty heart valves

Valves stop blood flowing backwards. A valve can go wrong in two ways.

| Fault | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Valve does not open fully (stiff) | Less blood gets through, so the heart has to work harder |
| Valve leaks | Blood flows backwards, so less blood is pumped forwards with each beat |

Either way, less oxygen reaches the body cells, so the person may feel tired and breathless.

| Replacement valve | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Biological (from animal or human tissue) | Works like a natural valve; usually no need for lifelong anti-clotting drugs | Wears out sooner, so may need replacing |
| Mechanical (man-made) | Very long-lasting | Patient usually needs anti-clotting drugs for life, because clots can form on it |

Both need heart surgery, which carries risks.

## Heart failure

When the heart can no longer pump well enough:

| Treatment | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Transplant of a donor heart, or heart and lungs | Can give a long-term solution | Few donors, so long waits; major surgery; risk of rejection, so immunosuppressant drugs for life |
| Artificial heart | Keeps a patient alive while they wait for a transplant, or lets the heart rest to aid recovery; less likely to be rejected than donor tissue | Usually a short-term measure; risk of blood clots and infection; patient depends on a power supply |

## Method in steps: evaluating a treatment

```
1. Name the treatment and say what it does.
2. Give at least one advantage (benefit).
3. Give at least one disadvantage (risk, side effect, cost, waiting time).
4. Compare with an alternative if the question gives one.
5. Finish with a justified judgement for THIS patient.
```

**Worked reminder.** A patient has one narrowed coronary artery and high cholesterol. A stent restores blood flow
now; statins lower cholesterol so new fatty deposits build up more slowly. Using both treats the blockage and
reduces the chance of another. The judgement depends on the patient's fitness for a procedure and on side effects.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Stent vs statin:** mechanical device that holds the artery open vs drug that lowers cholesterol.
- **Biological vs mechanical valve:** wears out sooner vs anti-clotting drugs for life.
- **Donor heart vs artificial heart:** long-term but rejection risk vs usually temporary.
- **Coronary arteries vs aorta:** the coronary arteries supply the heart muscle itself.
- **Non-communicable vs communicable:** CHD is not caused by a pathogen.

## Quick self-test

1. Why is coronary heart disease called non-communicable?
2. Describe how fatty deposits in the coronary arteries damage the heart.
3. What is a stent, and how does it help?
4. How do statins reduce the risk of coronary heart disease?
5. Give one disadvantage of a stent and one of statins.
6. Describe the two ways a heart valve can be faulty, and one consequence of each.
7. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of a mechanical valve compared with a biological valve.
8. Give two situations in which an artificial heart might be used.
9. Give two disadvantages of a heart transplant.

### Answers

1. It is not caused by a pathogen, so it cannot be passed from one person to another.
2. Fatty material builds up and narrows the arteries, so less blood flows to the heart muscle. The muscle gets
   less oxygen, so it respires less and can be damaged.
3. A mesh tube placed in the narrowed coronary artery. It holds the artery open so blood flow to the heart
   muscle improves.
4. They lower blood cholesterol, which slows the rate at which fatty material is deposited in the arteries.
5. Stent: risk of infection, a blood clot or the artery narrowing again. Statins: must be taken long term and
   can cause side effects.
6. It may not open fully, so less blood gets through and the heart works harder. It may leak, so blood flows
   backwards and less is pumped forwards.
7. Advantage: it lasts much longer. Disadvantage: the patient usually needs anti-clotting drugs for life.
8. To keep a patient alive while they wait for a transplant; to let the heart rest as an aid to recovery.
9. Any two: shortage of donors (long waits); major surgery; risk of rejection; immunosuppressant drugs for life.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "the arteries get blocked" without the chain: **narrowed** -> less blood flow -> less **oxygen** to the **heart muscle**.
- Saying statins "remove" fatty deposits. They slow the build-up.
- Saying a stent lowers cholesterol.
- Saying CHD is a blockage in the aorta or the heart chambers.
- Listing advantages only in an "evaluate" question, or ending without a judgement.
- Confusing heart valve faults (a leak or a stiff valve) with blocked arteries.
