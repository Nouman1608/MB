## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Resonance structures | Two or more valid Lewis formulas for one species that differ only in where the double bond (π electrons) sits |
| Resonance hybrid | The real species: one structure, an average of the resonance structures |
| Delocalization | π electrons spread over more than two atoms |
| Resonance energy | Extra stability of the real species compared with one resonance structure |
| Expanded octet | More than eight electrons round a central atom (period 3 and below) |
| Formal charge | V − (L + ½B), assuming bonding electrons are shared equally |
| σ bond | Head-on overlap of orbitals; electron density along the bond axis |
| π bond | Sideways overlap of parallel p orbitals; electron density on opposite sides of the axis |
| Hybridization | Mixing atomic orbitals on one atom to make equivalent hybrid orbitals for bonding |

## 2.2.11 Resonance in four lines

- Draw the Lewis formula, then ask: could a double bond go to another equivalent atom?
- Draw each option, joined by ↔. Move electrons, never atoms.
- Delocalized bonds are **equal in length**, between single and double.
- Bonds per position = (total shared pairs over those positions) ÷ (number of positions). Two positions sharing three pairs gives 1.5.

## 2.2.12 Benzene: evidence checklist

A good "discuss" answer gives both kinds of evidence and links each to the delocalized model.

| Evidence | Type | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| All six C–C bonds equal, between single and double in length | Physical | No alternating single and double bonds |
| Planar hexagon, 120° angles | Physical | Each C is sp²; its leftover p orbital stands perpendicular to the ring plane |
| Enthalpy of hydrogenation much less negative than 3 × that of cyclohexene | Physical (thermochemical) | Extra stability: resonance energy |
| Only one 1,2-disubstituted isomer | Chemical | Every ring bond is the same |
| Does not readily decolourize bromine water; reacts by substitution | Chemical | Addition would destroy the delocalized ring |

**Method: resonance energy from data**

1. Expected ΔH = 3 × ΔH(hydrogenation of cyclohexene).
2. Resonance energy = measured ΔH(benzene) − expected ΔH. Give it as a positive stabilization.
3. State that both routes form cyclohexane, so benzene starts lower in energy.

## 2.2.13 Five and six electron domains

**Method in steps**

1. Valence electrons of the central atom, adding an electron for each negative charge and removing one for each positive charge.
2. Subtract one electron per bond to a monovalent atom such as F or Cl. Halve the rest for lone pairs.
3. Domains = bonding domains + lone pairs.
4. Electron domain geometry from the domain count; molecular geometry from the atoms only.

| Domains | Lone pairs | Molecular geometry | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 0 | trigonal bipyramidal | SbCl₅ |
| 5 | 1 | seesaw | TeCl₄ |
| 5 | 2 | T-shaped | ClF₃ |
| 5 | 3 | linear | XeF₂ |
| 6 | 0 | octahedral | TeF₆ |
| 6 | 1 | square pyramidal | IF₅ |
| 6 | 2 | square planar | XeF₄ |

Rules: lone pairs go **equatorial** in a trigonal bipyramid; two lone pairs go **opposite** each other in an octahedron. Lone pairs shrink neighbouring bond angles slightly.

## 2.2.14 Formal charge

**FC = V − (L + ½B)**

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| V | valence electrons in the free atom |
| L | electrons in lone pairs on the atom |
| B | electrons in all bonds to the atom |

Shortcut: FC = V − (number of lone-pair electrons) − (number of bonds).

**Choosing the preferred Lewis formula**

1. Check every candidate has the right electron total.
2. Work out FC on every atom. Check they add up to the overall charge.
3. Prefer formal charges closest to zero.
4. If two are tied, prefer the negative FC on the more electronegative atom.

**Formal charge vs oxidation state**

| | Formal charge | Oxidation state |
|---|---|---|
| Bonding electrons | shared equally | all given to the more electronegative atom |
| S in preferred SO₄²⁻ | 0 | +6 |

## 2.2.15 σ and π bonds

| Bond | σ | π |
|---|---|---|
| single | 1 | 0 |
| double | 1 | 1 |
| triple | 1 | 2 |

- σ: s–s, s–p, p–p end-on, or hybrid orbitals. Density on the axis.
- π: p–p side-on only. Density above and below (or either side of) the axis.
- Count σ = number of bonds of any kind; count π = extra bonds in double and triple bonds.

## 2.2.16 Hybridization

| Domains on atom | Hybridization | Unhybridized p left | Electron domain geometry | Ideal angle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | sp³ | 0 | tetrahedral | 109.5° |
| 3 | sp² | 1 | trigonal planar | 120° |
| 2 | sp | 2 | linear | 180° |

- Hybrid orbitals make σ bonds and hold lone pairs. Leftover p orbitals make π bonds.
- Count domains, not atoms: N in NH₃ has three atoms but four domains, so it is sp³.
- Work backwards too: "sp" means two domains and a linear arrangement around that atom.
- Only sp, sp² and sp³ are in the guide. Do not assign hybridization to five- or six-domain atoms.
- Organic and inorganic examples are both expected: CH₃ carbon sp³, C=O carbon sp², nitrile C and N sp; O in H₂O sp³, B in BF₃ sp², Be in BeCl₂ sp.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Resonance structure vs resonance hybrid**: drawings on paper vs the one real species.
- **Electron domain geometry vs molecular geometry**: arrangement of all domains vs arrangement of atoms only.
- **σ vs π**: head-on vs sideways; on the axis vs either side of it.
- **Hybrid vs unhybridized orbitals**: σ bonds and lone pairs vs π bonds.
- **Formal charge vs oxidation state**: equal sharing vs electrons to the more electronegative atom.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Drawing resonance structures with a moved H atom, which makes a different compound.
- Writing that the double bond "switches back and forth" instead of describing one delocalized hybrid.
- Giving only physical evidence for benzene when the question asks for physical and chemical evidence.
- Forgetting the charge when counting electrons on ions such as ICl₂⁻ or PF₆⁻.
- Putting a lone pair in an axial position of a trigonal bipyramid, which gives the wrong shape.
- Naming the electron domain geometry when the molecular geometry was asked for.
- Using all the bonding electrons instead of half of them in the formal charge formula.
- Picking a structure with a negative formal charge on the less electronegative atom.
- Counting a double bond as two σ bonds.
- Giving sp² to an atom with three bonded atoms plus a lone pair (that is four domains: sp³).

## Quick self-test

1. Draw the resonance structures for the nitric acid molecule, HNO₃, and state how many there are.
2. Calculate the formal charge on N in NH₄⁺.
3. Predict the molecular geometry of XeF₂.
4. Predict the electron domain geometry and molecular geometry of IF₅.
5. State the number of σ and π bonds in CS₂.
6. State the number of σ and π bonds in propyne, CH₃C≡CH.
7. Calculate the formal charges on each N atom in the azide ion drawn as [N=N=N]⁻.
8. State the hybridization of the carbon atom in CO₂ and of the nitrogen atom in CH₃CN.
9. Rank the C–O bond length in CH₃OH, CO₂ and the ethanoate ion, CH₃COO⁻, from shortest to longest.
10. State the formal charge and the oxidation state of S in the preferred Lewis formula of SO₄²⁻.
11. Explain why the C=C bond in an alkene contains one π bond, not two σ bonds.
12. State the electron domain geometry of SbCl₅ and the Cl–Sb–Cl angles present.

### Answers

1. **Two.** H–O–N(=O)–O ↔ H–O–N(–O)=O, with the double bond moving between the two O atoms not bonded to H. The N carries +1 and the single-bonded O carries −1 in each.
2. FC = 5 − (0 + 4) = **+1**.
3. Five domains (two bonds, three lone pairs, all lone pairs equatorial): **linear**, 180°.
4. Six domains: **octahedral** electron domain geometry; one lone pair gives a **square pyramidal** molecule.
5. **2 σ, 2 π** (two C=S double bonds).
6. **6 σ, 2 π** (four C–H, one C–C, one σ in C≡C; two π in C≡C).
7. Each terminal N: 5 − (4 + 2) = **−1**. Central N: 5 − (0 + 4) = **+1**. Total −1, matching the charge.
8. C in CO₂: two domains, **sp**. N in CH₃CN: one triple bond and one lone pair, two domains, **sp**.
9. **CO₂ < CH₃COO⁻ < CH₃OH.** Double bonds are shortest, the delocalized 1.5 bonds in ethanoate are intermediate, the single bond is longest.
10. Formal charge **0**; oxidation state **+6**.
11. Only one head-on overlap fits between two nuclei along the axis. The second bond comes from sideways overlap of parallel unhybridized p orbitals, which is a π bond.
12. **Trigonal bipyramidal**; **90°** (axial to equatorial), **120°** (between equatorial) and **180°** (axial to axial).
