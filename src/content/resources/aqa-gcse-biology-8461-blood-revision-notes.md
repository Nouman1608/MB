## Blood is a tissue

Blood is a **tissue**: a group of cells (plus cell fragments) with a shared function, carried in a liquid.

```
blood = plasma (liquid) + red blood cells + white blood cells + platelets (suspended in the plasma)
```

It is **not** an organ: it is one tissue, not several tissues working together.

## The four components

| Component | What it is | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Plasma | Pale yellow liquid, mostly water | Carries the cells and dissolved substances around the body |
| Red blood cells | Small cells, no nucleus, packed with haemoglobin | Carry oxygen from the lungs to the body cells |
| White blood cells | Larger cells, each with a nucleus | Defend the body against pathogens (part of the immune system) |
| Platelets | Tiny fragments of cells, no nucleus | Help the blood clot at a wound |

### What plasma carries

| Substance | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| Glucose and amino acids (soluble products of digestion) | Small intestine | Body cells |
| Carbon dioxide | Respiring body cells | Lungs |
| Urea | Liver | Kidneys |
| Hormones | Glands | Target organs |
| Antibodies and other proteins | Made in the body | Around the body |

Plasma also carries the blood cells and platelets themselves, and transfers heat around the body.

## Adaptations to function

### Red blood cells

| Feature | Effect |
|---|---|
| Biconcave disc shape (dented on both sides) | Large surface area for oxygen to diffuse in and out |
| No nucleus | More room inside for haemoglobin |
| Haemoglobin | Red pigment that binds oxygen in the lungs and releases it in the tissues |
| Small and flexible | Can squeeze through narrow capillaries |

### White blood cells

- They have a **nucleus** (often large or lobed), which makes them easy to tell apart from red cells.
- Some **engulf and digest** pathogens (phagocytosis).
- Some make **antibodies**, which target particular pathogens, and **antitoxins**, which neutralise toxins.
- Some can change shape to squeeze out of capillaries into infected tissue.

### Platelets

- Small fragments of cells with **no nucleus**.
- At a wound they help form a **clot**, which dries to a scab.
- The clot **stops blood loss** and stops **pathogens getting in** through the wound.

## Recognising blood cells in a photograph or diagram

| You see | It is |
|---|---|
| Many round cells, pale in the centre, no nucleus | Red blood cells |
| Fewer, larger cells with a darkly stained nucleus | White blood cells |
| Tiny specks or fragments between the cells | Platelets |
| The background liquid (not visible as cells) | Plasma |

Red blood cells far outnumber white blood cells in any normal blood smear, so a field of view is mostly red cells.

### Method in steps: observing and drawing blood cells

```
1. Use a prepared, stained blood smear slide (the stain shows up nuclei).
2. Start with the lowest-power objective and focus with the coarse focus.
3. Switch to a higher power and sharpen with the fine focus.
4. Draw in pencil with clear, unshaded outlines, the right relative sizes, and ruled label lines.
5. Write the magnification (eyepiece x objective) beside the drawing.
```

### Worked reminder: actual size

```
actual size = image size / magnification
```

A red blood cell measures 3.5 mm across in a photo at x500. Actual size = 3.5 / 500 = 0.007 mm.
Convert: 0.007 x 1000 = **7 µm**. Always convert to µm for cells.

## Evaluating blood products

The specification asks you to weigh the risks of using blood products, such as **transfusions** of donated blood.

| Benefit | Risk |
|---|---|
| Replaces blood lost in an accident, surgery or childbirth | A pathogen could be passed on in donated blood (so donations are screened) |
| Platelets or plasma can be given to people who need just that part | The donated blood must be matched to the recipient, or their immune system may react against it |
| Can save a life quickly | Supply depends on donors and blood must be stored correctly |

In an evaluation, give a benefit and a risk, then a judgement (for example: the benefit of saving a life
usually outweighs a small, controlled risk of infection).

## Must-know distinctions

- **Red vs white blood cells:** carry oxygen, no nucleus, very many vs defend against pathogens, nucleus, fewer.
- **Platelets vs cells:** platelets are fragments of cells, not whole cells.
- **Plasma vs blood:** plasma is the liquid part only. Blood is plasma plus everything suspended in it.
- **Haemoglobin vs red blood cell:** haemoglobin is the protein pigment; the red blood cell is the cell that contains it.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the four components of blood.
2. Is blood a tissue or an organ? Explain.
3. Give two ways a red blood cell is adapted to carry oxygen, and the effect of each.
4. Name the substance in red blood cells that binds oxygen.
5. How can you tell a white blood cell from a red blood cell in a micrograph?
6. Give two ways white blood cells defend the body.
7. What are platelets, and what do they do?
8. Name three substances carried in plasma, and where each is carried to.
9. A white blood cell is 6 mm wide in a photo at x500. Calculate its actual width in µm.
10. Give one benefit and one risk of a blood transfusion.

### Answers

1. Plasma, red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets.
2. A tissue. It is one group of cells with a shared function, suspended in plasma, not several tissues working together.
3. Any two: biconcave shape gives a large surface area for diffusion; no nucleus leaves more room for
   haemoglobin; small and flexible to pass through capillaries.
4. Haemoglobin.
5. The white blood cell is larger and has a nucleus; the red blood cell has no nucleus.
6. Any two: engulfing and digesting pathogens; making antibodies; making antitoxins.
7. Fragments of cells with no nucleus. They help the blood clot, which stops blood loss and keeps pathogens out of wounds.
8. Any three: glucose and amino acids to body cells; carbon dioxide to the lungs; urea to the kidneys;
   hormones to target organs.
9. 6 / 500 = 0.012 mm = **12 µm**.
10. Benefit: replaces lost blood and can save a life. Risk: a pathogen could be passed on, or the blood could
    be wrongly matched, causing an immune reaction.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying red blood cells have "no nucleus so they can carry more oxygen" without mentioning **haemoglobin**.
- Calling platelets cells, or giving them a nucleus.
- Writing that white blood cells "fight disease" without saying **how** (engulf, antibodies, antitoxins).
- Forgetting that plasma carries **carbon dioxide** and **urea**, not just food.
- Calling blood an organ.
- Leaving an actual-size answer in mm when the question asks for µm.
