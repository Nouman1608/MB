These questions cover sub-topic **4.2 Accounting for depreciation and disposal of non-current assets**. Cambridge 0452 papers are not divided into lettered sections, so Sections A and B below simply group shorter and longer questions. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. Businesses and figures are invented; amounts are in dollars.

## Section A

**1.** Define net book value. **[1]**

**2.** A machine cost 24,000 and is depreciated at 25% per year on the reducing balance. What is the depreciation charge in its third year?

A: 6,000

B: 4,500

C: 3,375

D: 3,000 **[1]**

**3.** Which entry records the annual depreciation charge?

A: debit non-current asset, credit provision for depreciation

B: debit statement of profit or loss, credit non-current asset

C: debit statement of profit or loss, credit provision for depreciation

D: debit provision for depreciation, credit statement of profit or loss **[1]**

**4.** State **two** causes of depreciation. **[2]**

**5.** Loose tools were valued at 3,100 on 1 January. Tools costing 1,240 were bought during the year, and the tools were valued at 2,960 on 31 December. Calculate the depreciation for the year. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why the reducing balance method is suitable for motor vehicles. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Larchmont Laundry, whose year ends on 31 December, bought washing machines on 1 March 2027 for 21,000. They are expected to last eight years and then be sold for 1,800. Depreciation is by the straight-line method, calculated monthly.

**(a)** Calculate the annual depreciation.

**(b)** Calculate the depreciation for the year ended 31 December 2027.

**(c)** Calculate the net book value at 31 December 2028.

**(d)** Explain **one** reason why the straight-line method is suitable for the washing machines. **[6]**

**8.** A van costs 32,000. The owner is choosing between straight-line depreciation at 20% of cost per year and reducing balance depreciation at 30% per year.

**(a)** Calculate the depreciation for each of the first three years under each method.

**(b)** Calculate the net book value at the end of year 3 under each method.

**(c)** State the effect on profit for year 1 of choosing the reducing balance method instead of the straight-line method. **[7]**

**9.** Larchmont Laundry depreciates vehicles at 25% of cost per year, with a full year's charge in the year of purchase and none in the year of disposal. A van bought in 2024 for 28,000 was sold on 15 May 2027 for 5,600, received by bank transfer.

**(a)** Calculate the accumulated depreciation on the van and the profit or loss on disposal.

**(b)** Prepare the journal entries to record the disposal, including the transfer of the profit or loss. Narratives are not required. **[7]**

**10.** On 1 January 2027 Larchmont Laundry's vehicles account had a balance of 64,000, including the van in Question 9, and the provision for depreciation of vehicles had a balance of 31,500. On 1 June 2027 a new van was bought for 30,000 by bank transfer. Depreciation is 25% of the cost of vehicles held at 31 December.

Prepare for the year ended 31 December 2027:

**(a)** the vehicles account;

**(b)** the provision for depreciation of vehicles account;

**(c)** the disposal of vehicles account. **[9]**

**11.** Equipment was bought on 1 October 2025 for 9,600. It is depreciated at 20% of cost per year, calculated monthly. The year end is 31 December. It was sold on 31 March 2028 for 4,900.

**(a)** Calculate the profit or loss on disposal.

**(b)** Show how the equipment would appear in the statement of financial position at 31 December 2027. **[5]**

**12.** Larchmont Laundry's profit is low this year. The owner proposes not to charge depreciation, because "it does not involve paying any money".

**(a)** Explain why depreciation is charged even though no cash is paid.

**(b)** Evaluate the owner's proposal. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The cost of a non-current asset less its accumulated depreciation [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**2.** **C** [1]. Year 1: 6,000, net book value 18,000; year 2: 4,500, net book value 13,500; year 3: 13,500 x 25% = 3,375. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** **C** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**4.** Any two [2]: wear and tear; passage of time; becoming out of date (obsolescence). Total: 2 marks.

**5.** 3,100 + 1,240 - 2,960 [1] = **1,380** [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**6.** Vehicles lose most of their value in their early years [1], and repair costs rise as they age, so a falling depreciation charge keeps the total yearly cost more even [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**7. (a)** (21,000 - 1,800) / 8 = **2,400** [1].

**(b)** 2,400 x 10/12 = **2,000** [1].

**(c)** Accumulated depreciation 2,000 + 2,400 = 4,400 [1]; net book value 21,000 - 4,400 = **16,600** [1].

**(d)** The machines are likely to be used to a similar extent every year [1], so charging the same amount each year matches their cost to the revenue they help to earn [1]. Total: 6 marks.

**8. (a)** Straight-line: 32,000 x 20% = 6,400 in each year [1]. Reducing balance: year 1 9,600 [1]; year 2 (32,000 - 9,600) x 30% = 6,720 [1]; year 3 (22,400 - 6,720) x 30% = 4,704 [1].

**(b)** Straight-line: 32,000 - 19,200 = **12,800** [1]. Reducing balance: 32,000 - 21,024 = **10,976** [1].

**(c)** Profit is lower by 9,600 - 6,400 = 3,200 [1]. Total: 7 marks.

**9. (a)** Depreciation charged for 2024, 2025 and 2026: 3 x 28,000 x 25% = **21,000** [1]. Net book value 28,000 - 21,000 = 7,000 [1]. Loss on disposal 7,000 - 5,600 = **1,400** [1].

**(b)**

One mark for each correct entry [4]. Total: 7 marks.

*Examiner insight:* Under this policy there is no depreciation for 2027 on the van sold, so the accumulated figure covers only three years.

**11. (a)** Months owned 1 October 2025 to 31 March 2028 = 30 [1]; accumulated depreciation 9,600 x 20% x 30/12 = 4,800 [1]; net book value 4,800, so profit on disposal = 4,900 - 4,800 = **100** [1].

**(b)** Cost 9,600, less accumulated depreciation 9,600 x 20% x 27/12 = 4,320 [1], net book value **5,280** [1]. Total: 5 marks.

**12. (a)** The cash was paid when the asset was bought [1]; depreciation spreads that cost over the years the asset is used, matching it against the revenue it helps to earn [1].

**(b)** Leaving out depreciation would raise this year's profit [1]. However, profit would be overstated and the vans and machines would be shown at more than a realistic value, breaking the matching and prudence concepts [1]. Changing treatment because profit is low also breaks consistency, so profits could not be compared with other years [1]. The proposal should be rejected: depreciation is a real cost of using the assets, even though no cash is paid this year [1]. Total: 6 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Applying the reducing balance rate to cost in later years.
- Ignoring residual value or the months-owned policy in straight-line calculations.
- Charging depreciation in the year of disposal when the policy says none.
- Comparing proceeds with cost instead of net book value.
- Leaving a balance on the disposal account or putting the loss on the wrong side.
