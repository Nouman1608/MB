> **These are original questions written for Marlbridge**, for practice on 1123 Paper 1 Question 1,
> the Comprehension task, which asks for "short answers testing understanding of both explicit and
> implicit meanings" in a narrative Text A. The extract is original and shorter than the real Text A
> (about 900 words). The twelve questions total 16 marks, the tariff of Question 1; individual
> tariffs are practice values.

Read the extract (original, written for this practice set).

> (1) The storm arrived an hour before it was due, as storms on that coast usually did. By ten o'clock
> the rain was coming sideways off the sea, and the little café at the end of the harbour wall had only
> one customer. Mei, who had been working there on Saturdays since the spring, wiped the same table for
> the third time and watched him through the steamed-up window.
>
> (2) He was an old man in a yellow oilskin that had faded almost to cream. He had ordered a pot of tea
> and had not touched it. Instead he sat with both hands flat on the table, as if he were holding it
> down, and stared at the grey water beyond the harbour mouth.
>
> (3) "That's Mr Pellow," said Mrs Rudd, the owner, coming out of the kitchen with a tray of scones
> nobody would buy. "His son's boat went out at six. Should have been back by now." She said it
> lightly, the way people mention the weather, but she put two scones on a plate and took them over to
> him without being asked, and Mei noticed that she did not charge for them.
>
> (4) For a while nothing happened. The radio behind the counter crackled through the shipping
> forecast. A gull landed on the railing outside, thought better of it, and was gone. Mr Pellow did not
> move, except once, when a door banged somewhere along the quay; then his head turned so quickly that
> Mei felt her own heart jump.
>
> (5) At half past eleven Mei took him a fresh pot of tea, because the first one had gone cold. She did
> not know what to say, so she said nothing, but she stood beside him for a moment longer than she
> needed to. He looked up at her as though he had only just noticed she was there.
>
> (6) "Forty years I fished out of this harbour," he said. "Never once did I think about the people
> waiting." He gave a short laugh with no amusement in it. "Serves me right, I suppose."
>
> (7) It was nearly one o'clock when the small blue boat came round the harbour wall, low in the water
> and rolling badly. Mr Pellow stood up so fast that his chair fell over behind him. He did not pick it
> up. He went out into the rain without his hat, and Mei watched him walk, then half-run, down the
> slippery steps to the water.
>
> (8) Mrs Rudd picked up the chair and set it straight. "Well," she said, to nobody in particular,
> "I'll put the kettle on."

## Section A

**1.** At what time did Mr Pellow's son's boat go out? **[1]**

**2.** On which day of the week did Mei work at the café? **[1]**

**3.** What did Mrs Rudd take to Mr Pellow without being asked? **[1]**

**4.** Why did Mei take Mr Pellow a fresh pot of tea? **[1]**

**5.** Give two details from paragraph 7 which show that the boat had a difficult journey. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** What does the phrase "as storms on that coast usually did" (paragraph 1) suggest about storms
there? **[1]**

**7.** Mr Pellow sits "with both hands flat on the table, as if he were holding it down". What does
this suggest about how he is feeling? **[2]**

**8.** Mrs Rudd speaks "lightly" about the son's boat. Explain how her actions show that she is more
concerned than she sounds. **[2]**

**9.** Why does Mr Pellow's head turn "so quickly" when a door bangs (paragraph 4)? **[1]**

**10.** Explain in your own words what Mr Pellow means when he says, "Never once did I think about
the people waiting." **[2]**

**11.** What does Mr Pellow's leaving his chair on the floor and going out "without his hat" suggest
about him at that moment? **[1]**

**12.** What impression of Mrs Rudd do her final words give? **[1]**

---

## Answers

**1.** At six (o'clock) [1].

**2.** Saturday [1]. "Since the spring" is when she started, not the day.

**3.** Two scones (on a plate) [1].

**4.** Because the first pot had gone cold (he had not drunk it) [1].

**5.** Any two, one mark each [2]: it was low in the water; it was rolling badly; it arrived very late
(nearly one o'clock, when it should have been back much earlier).

**6.** Storms there often come earlier than forecast / cannot be relied on to arrive when expected
[1].

**7.** Two points [2]: he is tense or anxious [1]; he is trying to keep control of himself, bracing
himself as if to stop his fear from showing / holding himself steady [1]. "He is tired" is not
credited: nothing in the text supports it.

**8.** Two points [2]: she brings him food without being asked [1]; she does not charge him, which
shows sympathy and kindness towards a worried man [1]. Credit also: she mentions the boat at all,
showing it is on her mind.

**9.** He is on edge / hoping it is news or a sign of his son's return [1].

**10.** Two points, own words [2]: while he was at sea for all those years, he never considered how
anxious his family must have felt [1]; now that he is the one waiting, he understands their worry
for the first time [1]. Lifting "never thought about the people waiting" earns no mark for the first
point.

**11.** He is overwhelmed with relief / thinks only of reaching his son, forgetting everything else
[1].

**12.** She is calm and practical, quietly kind: she turns to an ordinary task to welcome them back
and to steady herself after the tension [1].

**Total: 16.**

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 2**: "since the spring" answers a different question.
- **Question 5**: giving "it was a small blue boat", which is description, not difficulty.
- **Question 7**: naming a feeling with no reference to the gesture, or interpreting the image
  literally (he is stopping the table from blowing away).
- **Question 10**: copying the quotation instead of explaining it, or giving only the first half of the
  meaning.
- **Question 12**: "She likes tea": the question asks what her words show about her character.
