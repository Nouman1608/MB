These questions cover subtopic E6.6, Pythagoras’ theorem and trigonometry in 3D, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The subtopic is Extended only. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Every cuboid ABCDEFGH has a horizontal base ABCD, with E above A, F above B, G above C and H above D. Give lengths to 3 significant figures and angles to 1 decimal place unless told otherwise. Learn the methods first with the [3D trigonometry study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-pythagoras-theorem-and-trigonometry-cambridge-igcse-maths/) and the [3D trigonometry revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-pythagoras-theorem-and-trigonometry-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** (calculator) A cuboid ABCDEFGH has AB = 10 cm, BC = 7 cm and AE = 5 cm. Find the length of AG. **[3]**

**2.** (calculator) For the cuboid in question 1, find the angle between AG and the base ABCD. **[2]**

**3.** (non-calculator) A cube has edges of length 4 cm. Find the exact length of a space diagonal, in its simplest form. **[2]**

**4.** (calculator) A cone has base radius 5 cm and slant height 13 cm. Find the angle between a slant line and the base. **[2]**

**5.** (calculator) A storeroom is a cuboid 5 m long, 4 m wide and 3 m high. Find the length of the longest straight rod that fits inside it. **[2]**

**6.** In cuboid ABCDEFGH, name

**(a)** the angle between BH and the base ABCD, **[1]**

**(b)** the angle between CE and the base ABCD. **[1]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** (calculator) A pyramid VABCD has a horizontal square base ABCD of side 10 cm. The vertex V is vertically above M, the centre of the base. Each sloping edge is 15 cm long.

**(a)** Show that AM = 5√2 cm. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the height VM. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the angle between VA and the base. **[2]**

**(d)** N is the midpoint of BC. Find the angle between VN and the base. **[2]**

**8.** (calculator) A tent is a triangular prism. Its rectangular floor ABCD has AB = 6 m and AD = 10 m. The ridge PQ is horizontal and 4 m above the floor, with P vertically above the midpoint of AB and Q vertically above the midpoint N of DC.

**(a)** Find the length of AQ. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the angle between AQ and the floor. **[2]**

**9.** (calculator) A vertical tower PT stands with its foot P on level ground. A and B are points on the ground with AB = 80 m, angle PAB = 50° and angle PBA = 60°.

**(a)** Find the distance AP. **[2]**

**(b)** The angle of elevation of T from A is 28°. Find the height of the tower. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the angle of elevation of T from B. **[3]**

**10.** (calculator) A cuboid has a square base of side x cm and a height of 6 cm. Its space diagonal is 11 cm.

**(a)** Find x. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the angle between the space diagonal and the base. **[2]**

**11.** (non-calculator) A cuboid measures 3 cm by 4 cm by 12 cm, with the 3 cm by 4 cm face as its base.

**(a)** Find the length of a space diagonal. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the exact value of the sine of the angle between a space diagonal and the base. **[2]**

**12.** (calculator) A cuboid ABCDEFGH has AB = 12 cm, AD = 4 cm and AE = 3 cm. M is the midpoint of the top edge GH.

**(a)** Find the length of AM. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the angle between AM and the base ABCD. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** AC² = 10² + 7² = 149 [1]; AG² = 149 + 5² = 174 [1]; AG = **13.2 cm** [1]

**2.** tan θ = 5 ÷ √149 [1]; θ = **22.3°** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the projection of AG on the base is AC, not AB. Using AB gives tan θ = 5 ÷ 10 and 26.6°, which scores nothing.

**3.** Face diagonal = √32 = 4√2 [1]; space diagonal = √(32 + 16) = √48 = **4√3 cm** [1]

**4.** cos θ = 5 ÷ 13 [1]; θ = **67.4°** [1]

**5.** √(5² + 4² + 3²) = √50 [1] = **7.07 m** [1]

**6. (a)** **Angle HBD** [1]

**(b)** **Angle ECA** [1]

*Examiner insight:* find the point on the base directly below the top end of the line (D below H, A below E), then join it to the other end of the line.

**7. (a)** AC = √(10² + 10²) = √200 = 10√2 [1]; AM = half of AC = **5√2 cm** [1]

**(b)** VM = √(15² − (5√2)²) = √(225 − 50) = √175 [1] = **13.2 cm** [1]

**(c)** cos θ = 5√2 ÷ 15 (or tan θ = √175 ÷ 5√2) [1]; θ = **61.9°** [1]

**(d)** MN = 5 cm (half a side) [1]; tan φ = √175 ÷ 5, φ = **69.3°** [1]

*Examiner insight:* in (d) the projection is MN, which is shorter than MA, so the angle is steeper than in (c). A sketch of the base from above makes this clear.

**8. (a)** N is the midpoint of DC, so AN² = 3² + 10² = 109 [1]; Q is 4 m above N, so AQ² = 109 + 16 = 125 [1]; AQ = **11.2 m** [1]

**(b)** tan θ = 4 ÷ √109 [1]; θ = **21.0°** [1]

**9. (a)** Angle APB = 180° − 50° − 60° = 70°; AP ÷ sin 60° = 80 ÷ sin 70° [1]; AP = **73.7 m** [1]

**(b)** PT = 73.728… × tan 28° [1] = **39.2 m** [1]

**(c)** BP = 80 × sin 50° ÷ sin 70° = 65.216… m [1]; tan θ = 39.202… ÷ 65.216… [1]; θ = **31.0°** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the ground triangle needs the sine rule (E6.5) and the vertical triangles need SOH CAH TOA. Keep AP and PT in the calculator between parts.

**10. (a)** Base diagonal² = x² + x² = 2x² [1]; 2x² + 36 = 121 [1]; x² = 42.5, x = **6.52** [1]

**(b)** sin θ = 6 ÷ 11 [1]; θ = **33.1°** [1]

**11. (a)** Base diagonal = √(9 + 16) = 5 cm [1]; diagonal = √(25 + 144) = **13 cm** [1]

**(b)** The opposite side is the 12 cm height and the hypotenuse is 13 cm [1]; sin θ = **12/13** [1]

**12. (a)** M is directly above N, the midpoint of DC, so DN = 6 cm [1]; AN² = 4² + 6² = 52 [1]; AM = √(52 + 3²) = √61 = **7.81 cm** [1]

**(b)** tan θ = 3 ÷ √52 [1]; θ = **22.6°** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the right angle at D (between AD and DC) lets you find AN; then the vertical triangle ANM has its right angle at N.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a base edge instead of the projection when finding an angle with a plane.
- Using the full base diagonal of a pyramid instead of half.
- Taking a sloping edge as the vertical height.
- Rounding intermediate lengths such as √149 or √109.
- Not naming the angle being calculated, which makes partial credit harder to award.
