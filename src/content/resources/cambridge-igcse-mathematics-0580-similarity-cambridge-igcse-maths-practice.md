These questions cover subtopic 4.4 Similarity of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions without a label use Core content (lengths of similar shapes) and suit both tiers. Questions marked **(Extended)** use area and volume relationships or ask you to show that triangles are similar, which is Extended content. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Diagrams are described in words; sketch them first.

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) A rectangle 12 cm long and 8 cm wide is similar to a rectangle 18 cm long and w cm wide. Find w. **[2]**

**2.** (non-calculator) Triangles ABC and PQR are similar, with A corresponding to P, B to Q and C to R. AB = 4 cm, PQ = 10 cm, AC = 6 cm and QR = 15 cm.

**(a)** Find the scale factor from triangle ABC to triangle PQR. **[1]**

**(b)** Find PR. **[1]**

**(c)** Find BC. **[1]**

**3.** (calculator) A model car is similar to the real car. The model is 18 cm long and the real car is 4.32 m long. The real car is 1.44 m high. Find the height of the model in centimetres. **[2]**

**4.** (non-calculator) In triangle ABC, D is on AB and E is on AC, with DE parallel to BC. Triangles ADE and ABC are similar. AD = 5 cm, DB = 3 cm and DE = 7.5 cm. Find BC. **[2]**

**5.** (non-calculator) Explain why a rectangle 3 cm by 5 cm is not similar to a rectangle 6 cm by 8 cm. **[1]**

**6.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Two similar solids have length scale factor 4.

**(a)** The smaller has surface area 5 cm². Find the surface area of the larger. **[1]**

**(b)** The smaller has volume 2 cm³. Find the volume of the larger. **[1]**

**7.** (Extended) (non-calculator) The areas of two similar triangles are in the ratio 16 : 49. Find the ratio of their corresponding sides. **[1]**

## Section B

**8.** (non-calculator) The straight lines AE and BD cross at C. AB is parallel to DE. AC = 4 cm, CE = 6 cm, AB = 5 cm and CD = 9 cm.

**(a)** (Extended) Show that triangles ABC and EDC are similar. **[2]**

**(b)** Find DE. **[1]**

**(c)** Find BC. **[1]**

**9.** (calculator) At the same time of day, a vertical post 1.8 m tall casts a shadow 2.4 m long, and a vertical tree casts a shadow 16 m long. The triangles formed by each object, its shadow and the sun's rays are similar. Find the height of the tree. **[2]**

**10.** (Extended) (calculator) Two similar jugs have heights 15 cm and 25 cm.

**(a)** The smaller jug holds 540 ml. Find the capacity of the larger jug. **[3]**

**(b)** The surface area of the larger jug is 450 cm². Find the surface area of the smaller jug. **[2]**

**11.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Two similar containers have volumes 128 cm³ and 250 cm³.

**(a)** The smaller container is 8 cm tall. Find the height of the larger container. **[3]**

**(b)** The larger container has surface area 300 cm². Find the surface area of the smaller container. **[1]**

**12.** (Extended) (calculator) A solid cone has height 15 cm and base radius 6 cm. A small cone of height 5 cm is cut from the top, parallel to the base, leaving a frustum.

Find the volume of the frustum. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. [The volume of a cone is ⅓πr²h.] **[4]**

**13.** (Extended) (non-calculator) In triangle ABC, D is a point on AC and E is a point on AB. Angle AED = angle ACB. AE = 4 cm, AD = 3 cm and AC = 8 cm.

**(a)** Explain why triangles AED and ACB are similar. **[2]**

**(b)** Find AB. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** k = 18 ÷ 12 = 1.5 [1]; w = 8 × 1.5 = **12** [1]

**2. (a)** 10 ÷ 4 = **2.5** [1]

**(b)** 6 × 2.5 = **15 cm** [1]

**(c)** 15 ÷ 2.5 = **6 cm** [1]

*Examiner insight:* in (c) the shape is getting smaller, so divide by the scale factor.

**3.** k = 432 ÷ 18 = 24 [1]; model height = 144 ÷ 24 = **6 cm** [1]

**4.** AB = 5 + 3 = 8 cm, so k = 8 ÷ 5 = 1.6 [1]; BC = 7.5 × 1.6 = **12 cm** [1]

*Examiner insight:* using DB, 3 cm, gives k = 0.6 and BC = 4.5 cm, which is shorter than DE and clearly wrong.

**5.** 6/3 = 2 but 8/5 = 1.6, so the sides are not multiplied by the same scale factor [1]

**6. (a)** 5 × 4² = **80 cm²** [1]

**(b)** 2 × 4³ = **128 cm³** [1]

**7.** √16 : √49 = **4 : 7** [1]

**8. (a)** Angle ACB = angle ECD (vertically opposite angles) [1]; angle BAC = angle DEC (alternate angles, AB parallel to DE), so the triangles are similar [1]

**(b)** k = CE ÷ AC = 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5; DE = 5 × 1.5 = **7.5 cm** [1]

**(c)** CD corresponds to CB, so BC = 9 ÷ 1.5 = **6 cm** [1]

*Examiner insight:* A corresponds to E and B to D, because of the alternate angles. Matching AC with CD gives a wrong scale factor.

**9.** k = 16 ÷ 2.4 = 6.666… [1]; tree height = 1.8 × 6.666… = **12 m** [1]

**10. (a)** k = 25 ÷ 15 = 5/3 [1]; volume factor = (5/3)³ = 125/27 [1]; 540 × 125/27 = **2500 ml** [1]

**(b)** Area factor = (3/5)² = 9/25 [1]; 450 × 9/25 = **162 cm²** [1]

**11. (a)** Volume factor = 250/128 = 125/64 [1]; k = ∛(125/64) = 5/4 [1]; height = 8 × 5/4 = **10 cm** [1]

**(b)** Area factor = (4/5)² = 16/25; 300 × 16/25 = **192 cm²** [1]

*Examiner insight:* square-rooting 125/64 instead of cube-rooting it is the most common error; always ask whether the ratio given is of lengths, areas or volumes.

**12.** Volume of whole cone = ⅓ × π × 6² × 15 = 180π = 565.48… cm³ [1]; k = 5/15 = 1/3 [1]; small cone = 180π × (1/3)³ = 20.94… cm³ [1]; frustum = 565.48… – 20.94… = 544.5… = **545 cm³** [1]

**13. (a)** Angle A is common to both triangles [1]; angle AED = angle ACB (given), so the third angles are also equal and the triangles are similar [1]

**(b)** E corresponds to C and D corresponds to B, so AE corresponds to AC and AD corresponds to AB; k = AC ÷ AE = 8 ÷ 4 = 2 [1]; AB = AD × 2 = **6 cm** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the triangles are not in the same orientation, so matching AD with AC because "D is on AC" gives the wrong answer. Match the sides opposite equal angles.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using part of a side instead of the whole side in nested triangles.
- Matching sides by position rather than by equal angles.
- Multiplying when the shape is getting smaller.
- (Extended) Using k for area or volume.
- (Extended) Square-rooting a volume ratio.
- (Extended) Giving "parallel lines" as a reason instead of "alternate angles" or "corresponding angles".
