These revision notes are for **higher level (HL) only**. The emergence of independent states in Southeast Asia (1900 to 1990) is a regional study examined on Paper 3, which standard level students do not sit. They condense the IB *History guide*, first assessment 2028. Every question on this study requires you to refer to **one** state from Southeast Asia, so choose one and learn it in depth. These notes use Indonesia as the main example with comparisons. Read the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-southeast-asia-independent-states/) first, then try the [practice set](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-southeast-asia-independent-states-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Divide and rule | Colonial policy of favouring some groups against others to prevent unity |
| Cultivation System | Dutch system (1830 to 1870) of forced export-crop production in Java |
| Adat | Customary law and practice |
| Pancasila | Five principles proposed by Sukarno as the basis of the state |
| Romusha | Forced labourers under Japanese rule in the Indies |
| PETA | Indonesian auxiliary army trained by Japan from 1943 |
| Pemuda | The youth who pressed for independence in 1945 |
| Police action | The Dutch name for their military offensives in 1947 and 1948 |
| Guided Democracy | Sukarno's system (1957 to 1965) of presidential leadership |
| Viet Minh | Vietnamese independence league led by Ho Chi Minh |

## Comparison grid: four paths to independence

| State | Colonial ruler | Main method | Date of independence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indonesia | Netherlands | Armed and diplomatic struggle | Proclaimed 1945; recognised 1949 |
| Vietnam | France | War led by the Viet Minh | Declared 1945; Geneva 1954 |
| Burma | Britain | Negotiation after mass mobilisation | 4 January 1948 |
| Philippines | United States | Negotiated transfer | 4 July 1946 |
| Malaya | Britain | Negotiation by moderate parties | 31 August 1957 |

## Impact of colonialism: a checklist

1. **Political:** divide and rule, indirect rule, favoured groups in the army.
2. **Economic:** export crops and minerals; profits abroad; Depression hardship.
3. **Social:** racial ranking, plantation labour, new educated elite, displacement.
4. **Cultural:** missionary schools, challenge to traditional beliefs, new press.

## Origins of nationalism: four causes

- Ideas (liberalism, self-determination, Islamic reform, communism).
- Economic grievance (exploitation, the Depression).
- Political repression (arrests, exile, no real representation).
- Shared identity (language, religion, colonial borders).

## Growth of nationalist movements

- **Indonesia:** Budi Utomo, Sarekat Islam, PKI, PNI and the Youth Pledge.
- **Leaders:** Sukarno (orator, Pancasila), Hatta (organiser); Aung San in Burma; Ho Chi Minh in Vietnam.
- **External factors:** the First World War, Versailles and the League did not extend self-determination to the colonies; the Russian Revolution offered communism.

## Japanese occupation: a balance sheet

| Helped nationalists | Harmed the people |
|---|---|
| Destroyed colonial prestige | Forced labour (*romusha*) and rice requisitions |
| Trained youth in PETA and other groups | Brutal military police |
| Gave Indonesians senior posts | Collapse of trade and rising prices |
| Allowed Sukarno and Hatta public platforms | Pressure on Chinese and women |

## Methods after 1945

- **Mass mobilisation:** pemuda, villages and the Republic's institutions.
- **Conflict:** guerrilla warfare; Yogyakarta captured in December 1948.
- **External support:** United Nations; US pressure after Madiun; India and Australia.
- **Colonial weakness:** the Netherlands was exhausted and short of money.

## First ten years: Indonesia scorecard

| Area | Achievement | Challenge |
|---|---|---|
| Social | Schools; national language | Few trained teachers and doctors |
| Economic | Export revenue | Foreign ownership; weak state |
| Political | First free election in 1955 | Unstable cabinets; no majority |
| Ethnic and religious | A shared national idea in Pancasila | Darul Islam, South Moluccas, Sumatra and Sulawesi revolts |

## Perspectives

- Anderson: imagined community; youth in the revolution.
- Ricklefs: the role of Islam, class and local conflict.
- Japan as liberator against Japan as brutal occupier.
- Leaders against movements.

## Worked example: a short plan

**Question:** "Evaluate the importance of leaders in the growth of nationalist movements in one Southeast Asian state."

1. Thesis: leaders were important but depended on the conditions that made followers.
2. Sukarno: the PNI, speeches, Pancasila, exile.
3. Hatta and others: organisation and education.
4. Movements without a single leader: Sarekat Islam, pemuda, villages.
5. Conditions: repression, the Depression, Japan.
6. Judgement.

## Common mistakes

- Treating Southeast Asia as one place.
- Forgetting that the guide extends to 1990 but the focus is on emergence and the first ten years.
- Stating that Japan "gave" independence.
- Mixing up the two Dutch "police actions".

## Self-test

1. What was divide and rule?
2. Who founded the PNI?
3. What was PETA?
4. When did Indonesia proclaim independence?
5. Why did the United States begin to support the Republic?
6. What happened in the 1955 election?
7. Name one regional rebellion in the 1950s.
8. When did Burma become independent?

**Answers:** 1. Favouring some groups against others to keep colonial control. 2. Sukarno (1927). 3. The Indonesian auxiliary army trained by Japan from 1943. 4. 17 August 1945. 5. The Republic put down the Madiun communist revolt in 1948, and the Cold War made it useful to Washington. 6. No party won a majority: the PNI, Masyumi, NU and PKI led. 7. Darul Islam, the South Moluccas, or the Sumatra and Sulawesi revolts. 8. 4 January 1948.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use a different state from the one in these notes?**
Yes. The same headings apply. Choose one state and fill each heading with its own evidence.

**How much of the period to 1990 do I need?**
The guide's inquiry topics focus on emergence and the first ten years of independence. Know the later period only as context for your state.
