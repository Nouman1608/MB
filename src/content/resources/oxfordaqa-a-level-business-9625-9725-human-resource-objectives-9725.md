This guide covers **3.2.2.1 Human resource objectives** from OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Business (9725), Version 1.1, in Unit 2 Managing operations, human resources and finance (International AS). The [Human resources study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-human-resources/) sets out the advantages and disadvantages of each method; this page shows how the parts fit into one planning process, with a workforce plan worked through in numbers and guidance on matching recruitment, selection, contracts and training to a situation. Afterwards, use the [human resource objectives revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-human-resource-objectives-9725-revision-notes/) and the [human resource objectives practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-human-resource-objectives-9725-practice/).

All businesses and figures are invented.

## What the specification asks for

- **Human resource objectives**: number and skill level of employees, employee engagement, employee retention.
- **Human resource planning**: recruitment, training, redeployment, retention, redundancies; the purpose and value of HR planning.
- **Recruitment**: the importance of job analysis, job description and person specification; internal versus external recruitment.
- **Selection methods**: CV, application forms, interviews, references, testing, assessment centres.
- **Employment contracts**: full time, part time, temporary, permanent, zero hours.
- **Value** of effective recruitment and selection, and of training: induction, off the job, on the job.

## The three HR objectives

HR objectives are targets for the people side of the business, set to support the corporate objectives.

| Objective | What it means | Example target |
|---|---|---|
| Number and skill level of employees | Enough people, with the right skills, in the right places | Have 12 qualified electricians in post by March |
| Employee engagement | How committed and involved employees are | Raise the "proud to work here" survey score from 58% to 70% |
| Employee retention | Keeping the employees the business wants | Keep 85% of new recruits for at least a year |

The objectives depend on each other. Low engagement usually leads to more people leaving, which lowers retention and forces repeat recruitment; high retention keeps skills in the business, which helps meet the skill-level objective.

**Linking to corporate objectives.** A business aiming to grow needs more staff and new skills; one cutting costs may aim to reduce numbers through redeployment and natural wastage; one competing on service quality will put engagement first.

## Human resource planning

HR planning compares the workforce the business will need with the workforce it will have, then closes the gap. The specification names five actions: **recruitment**, **training**, **redeployment** (moving staff to different roles or sites), **retention** and **redundancies**.

### Worked example: a workforce plan

Copperleaf Hotels employs 140 staff. It is opening a new hotel next year, which will need 35 more staff, so it will need 175 in total. Based on past years, it expects labour turnover of 15%.

```
Expected leavers    = 140 × 15% = 21
Staff remaining     = 140 − 21 = 119
Staff to recruit    = 175 − 119 = 56
```

Skills: the hotels will need 14 qualified chefs. Copperleaf has 10, and 2 are expected to leave.

```
Chefs available     = 10 − 2 = 8
Chef gap            = 14 − 8 = 6
```

Three kitchen assistants have started cooking qualifications, so Copperleaf can **train** them to fill three chef posts and **recruit** three chefs externally. If the new hotel replaces an older site, staff there could be **redeployed** instead of being made **redundant**. And if a **retention** plan cut turnover from 15% to 10%, only 14 would leave, reducing the recruitment need from 56 to 49.

**Purpose and value of HR planning.** It ensures the right staff are ready when needed, avoids costly last-minute hiring or overstaffing, gives time to train internal candidates, and links HR to the corporate plan. **Limits**: it rests on sales forecasts and turnover estimates, which can be wrong, and people leave unexpectedly.

## The recruitment process

1. **Job analysis**: study what the job involves: tasks, responsibilities, the skills it uses.
2. **Job description**: a document describing the **job**: title, duties, who the job-holder reports to, working conditions.
3. **Person specification**: a document describing the **person**: qualifications, experience, skills and qualities needed, often divided into essential and desirable.

**Example: night receptionist.** The job description lists checking guests in and out, handling payments, dealing with emergencies, and reporting to the duty manager. The person specification lists essential calm under pressure and good communication, and desirable experience with hotel booking systems and a second language. Shortlisting compares each applicant against the person specification, so a vague specification makes fair selection impossible.

**Internal or external?** Internal recruitment is quicker and cheaper and rewards existing staff, but limits fresh ideas and leaves a vacancy behind. External recruitment brings new skills and a wider choice, but costs more and the candidate is less well known. Use internal recruitment when the skills exist inside the business and continuity matters; external when new skills or a change of culture are needed.

## Choosing selection methods

Each method tests something different, so most businesses combine several.

| Role | Sensible combination | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery driver | Application form, driving test, references | Driving skill can be tested directly; references check reliability |
| Graduate trainee | Application form, assessment centre | Large applicant numbers; group tasks show teamwork and potential |
| Software developer | CV, coding test, interview | Technical skill is measurable; interview checks fit with the team |
| Shop assistant (seasonal) | Short application form, brief interview | Speed and low cost matter for short contracts |

**Value of effective recruitment and selection.** The right person becomes productive sooner, stays longer and needs less supervision. A poor choice brings repeat recruitment costs, lost output and possibly damage to customer relationships.

## Employment contracts

| Contract | Fits when | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Full time, permanent | Skills are scarce and needed all year | Higher fixed labour costs; hard to reduce numbers quickly |
| Part time | Demand peaks at certain hours | Coordination and communication across shifts |
| Temporary | Seasonal peaks or one-off projects | Less training and commitment; knowledge leaves with them |
| Zero hours | Demand is unpredictable | No guaranteed income for staff; may hurt engagement and retention |

The choice links to HR objectives: heavy use of temporary and zero hours contracts gives flexibility but makes engagement and retention targets harder to hit.

## Training

- **Induction**: introduces new staff to the business, its procedures, safety rules and colleagues. Done well, it reduces early leaving.
- **On the job**: learning while working, often from an experienced colleague. Cheap and directly relevant, but may pass on bad habits and slows the trainer.
- **Off the job**: courses away from the workplace. Brings wider expertise and formal qualifications, but costs more and output is lost while staff are away.

**Value of training**: higher productivity and quality, staff ready for promotion (supporting internal recruitment), and better engagement. **Risk**: trained staff may leave for rivals, which is why training is often paired with retention measures.

## Annotated model answer

**Question:** Explain **one** reason why Copperleaf Hotels might train kitchen assistants rather than recruit chefs externally.

> The kitchen assistants already know Copperleaf's menus, kitchens and standards **[context]**, so after qualifying they will be productive faster than an outside chef who needs a full induction **[development]**. Promotion also shows other staff that they can progress, which may raise engagement and help Copperleaf reduce its 15% labour turnover **[consequence linked to an HR objective]**.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up the job description (the job) and the person specification (the person).
- Treating HR planning as recruitment only, and forgetting training, redeployment, retention and redundancies.
- Calling zero hours contracts simply "cheap" without considering engagement and retention.
- Recommending an assessment centre for every role, regardless of cost.

## Exam technique

- **Explain**: link the method to an HR objective in the case (numbers, skills, engagement, retention).
- **Analyse**: follow the chain, for example poor selection → early leaving → repeat recruitment cost → missed staffing target.
- **Evaluate**: weigh cost and speed against quality of hire, and say what the choice depends on (scarcity of skills, length of need, budget).
