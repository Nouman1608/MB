| 14.5.4 | Explain phototropism and gravitropism of a shoot as examples of the chemical control of plant growth | Extended only |

## What makes a response "tropic"

A tropic response is a **growth** response. The plant does not move as an animal does: one side of a shoot or root grows more than the other, so the organ curves. That is why tropisms take hours or days and are measured as a change in angle or direction.

Two features define each tropism in this syllabus:

- **The stimulus** comes from one direction: light from one side (phototropism) or gravity (gravitropism).
- **The direction of growth** is either **towards** or **away from** that stimulus.

The syllabus definitions use the words "towards or away from", so always say which. "The shoot shows phototropism" earns less than "the shoot grows towards the light source".

### The four responses to learn

| Organ | Response to light from one side | Response to gravity |
|---|---|---|
| Shoot | grows **towards** the light | grows **away from** gravity (upwards) |
| Root | grows **away from** the light | grows **towards** gravity (downwards) |

Each response helps the plant. A shoot that grows towards light places its leaves where they absorb more light for photosynthesis. A root that grows downwards anchors the plant and reaches soil water and mineral ions.

## Investigating tropisms (Core)

Outcome 14.5.3 means you may be asked to plan an investigation, describe results or identify flaws. Every good plan has the same skeleton.

### Phototropism in shoots

1. Grow several seedlings of the same species and age (cress, mustard or oat seedlings are common).
2. Put one group in a box with a single slit, so light comes from **one side** only.
3. Put a second group in the same conditions but with light from **all sides**, or on a slowly rotating turntable (a clinostat) so every side receives the same light.
4. Leave both groups for the same time, for example two to three days.
5. Measure the **angle** each shoot bends from vertical with a protractor, and calculate a mean for each group.

The group with light from all sides is the control: it shows that bending is caused by the one-sided light, not by something else about the box.

### Gravitropism in roots and shoots

1. Pin germinating seeds, such as broad beans, to damp cotton wool with their young roots pointing in different directions: down, up and horizontal.
2. Keep them in the **dark**, so light cannot be the stimulus.
3. Fix a second set on a clinostat turning slowly in a vertical plane. Rotation means gravity acts on every side equally, so there is no one-sided stimulus.
4. After a few days, record the direction of root and shoot growth.

Expected results: on the fixed set, every root curves to grow downwards and every shoot curves to grow upwards, whatever direction they started in. On the clinostat, roots and shoots grow straight out in whatever direction they started.

### Variables to state

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Independent | direction of light, or direction of gravity relative to the seedling |
| Dependent | angle of curvature, or direction of growth |
| Controlled | species, age and number of seedlings; temperature; water; time; light intensity |

A plan with only one seedling per group is weak. Use several and calculate a mean, so one unusual seedling does not distort the result.

## Auxin and the chemical control of shoot growth (Extended only)

Outcome 14.5.4 asks you to explain the shoot responses as **chemical control**: a chemical, auxin, made in one part of the plant changes growth in another part. Outcome 14.5.5 limits the explanation to four points. Learn them in order, because a full explanation uses all four.

1. **Auxin is made in the shoot tip.**
2. **Auxin diffuses** through the plant from the shoot tip, down into the region just behind it.
3. Auxin is **unequally distributed** in response to light and gravity.
4. Auxin **stimulates cell elongation**: the cells get longer, not more numerous.

**Shoot lit from one side.** More auxin ends up on the **shaded side**. Cells on the shaded side elongate more than cells on the lit side. The shaded side becomes longer, so the shoot curves **towards** the light.

**Shoot lying horizontally.** More auxin ends up on the **lower side**. Lower-side cells elongate more, so the shoot curves **upwards**, away from gravity.

The syllabus limits the auxin explanation to shoots. You must describe root responses, but you are not asked to explain them with auxin.

### Worked example 1: interpreting shoot-tip experiments

Young oat shoots were given different treatments. The results are those biologists obtained in classic experiments of this kind.

| Shoot | Treatment | Result |
|---|---|---|
| A | untreated, light from the left | bends towards the left |
| B | tip cut off, light from the left | no bending |
| C | opaque cap over the tip, light from the left | grows straight up |
| D | opaque sleeve over the region below the tip, light from the left | bends towards the left |
| E | tip cut off; agar block that had a tip resting on it for some hours placed on the **left** half of the cut surface; kept in the dark | bends towards the **right** |
| F | as E but with a plain agar block | no bending |

**What each comparison shows**

- A and B: the tip is needed for the response.
- C and D: the **tip** detects the light. Covering the region below the tip does not stop bending.
- E and F: a chemical passed from the tip into the agar. Placed on the left, it made the left side elongate more, so the shoot curved to the right. This supports "auxin diffuses from the tip" and "auxin stimulates cell elongation".
- F is the control for E. It shows agar alone has no effect.

Notice that shoot E bends in the dark. The unequal distribution came from where the block was placed, not from light.

### Worked example 2: a calculation from cell measurements

A shoot lit from one side was marked with ink at 10.0 mm intervals on both sides. After a day, one interval on the shaded side measured 13.0 mm and the matching interval on the lit side measured 11.2 mm.

```
Shaded side growth = 13.0 − 10.0 = 3.0 mm
Lit side growth    = 11.2 − 10.0 = 1.2 mm
Percentage increase, shaded = 3.0 ÷ 10.0 × 100 = 30%
Percentage increase, lit    = 1.2 ÷ 10.0 × 100 = 12%
Shaded side grew 3.0 ÷ 1.2 = 2.5 times as much
```

The shaded side grew more, which is consistent with more auxin on that side stimulating more cell elongation.

### Annotated model answer

*Explain why a seedling shoot laid on its side on a bench grows upwards.* **[4]**

> Auxin is made in the shoot tip [1] and diffuses back through the shoot [1]. Because of gravity, more auxin collects on the lower side of the shoot [1]. Auxin stimulates cell elongation, so cells on the lower side elongate more and the shoot curves upwards [1].

Each sentence carries one of the syllabus points. An answer that stops at "auxin collects on the lower side" leaves out the effect on cells and loses the final mark.

## Common mistakes

- Saying the plant "moves" or "turns" towards light. It **grows** unequally.
- Writing "auxin makes cells divide". The syllabus effect is **cell elongation**.
- Putting more auxin on the lit side. It is the **shaded** side, and the **lower** side of a horizontal shoot.
- Describing the shoot response correctly but mixing up roots: roots grow **towards** gravity and **away from** light.
- Running a gravitropism investigation in the light, so two stimuli act at once.
- Using one seedling per treatment or giving no control.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Describe** (a result or a response): state the direction of growth and the stimulus, using "towards" or "away from". Use numbers from the data if they are given.
- **Explain** (Extended): give the four auxin points in sequence and finish with the effect on the direction of growth.
- **Suggest** (an advantage or a reason): link to light for photosynthesis, or to water, mineral ions and anchorage for roots.
- **Predict**: say what the shoot or root will do, then give a one-line reason from the auxin sequence or the stimulus.
- **Identify** (a control or variable): name it precisely, such as "light from all sides", not "normal conditions".
