These notes condense **section 5.1 Personal data** (Topic 5 eSecurity) of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Information Technology 9626 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. AS Level content, examined in Paper 1 Theory. Full explanations and a worked scenario are in the [personal data study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-personal-data/); test yourself with the [personal data practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-personal-data-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Personal data | Information about a living person who can be identified from it |
| Confidentiality | Only authorised people can see the data |
| Security | Protecting data from unauthorised access, loss or change |
| Geotag | Location information stored in a photo or video file |
| Anonymising | Removing or changing details so a person cannot be identified |
| Aggregating | Combining records into totals or averages |
| Duty of confidence | A legal or contractual obligation to keep information secret |
| Social engineering | Tricking people into giving information |
| Firewall | Hardware or software that filters network traffic using rules |

## Personal data

Name, address, email, phone number, date of birth, photos, bank and card details, medical records, biometrics, location data. **Sensitive** data (health, religion, political views, biometrics) needs extra care. A person can be identified by **combining** data, such as postcode, date of birth and job.

## Why keep it confidential

- Identity theft and fraud.
- Personal safety (stalking, burglary).
- Discrimination and embarrassment.
- Legal duty under data protection laws, with fines.
- Customer trust.

## Keeping it confidential: the syllabus methods

| Method | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Remove geotags | Strips location metadata | Turn off location tagging before posting photos |
| Anonymise | Removes identifying details | Replace patient names with codes in research data |
| Aggregate | Combines into totals | Publish the percentage who passed, not each mark |
| Duty of confidence | Obligation not to share | Staff contract, non-disclosure agreement, training, access rights |

## Keeping it secure

Strong passwords and two-factor authentication · access rights · firewalls · encryption · anti-malware · updates · back-ups · physical security · monitoring and logs.

## Unauthorised gathering

| Attack | How | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Phishing | Fake email with a link to a false website | Do not click links; check the sender and address; spam filter |
| Smishing | Fake SMS with a link or request | Ignore links in unexpected texts; contact the organisation directly |
| Vishing | Phone call asking for details or codes | Never give PINs or codes by phone; hang up and call the official number |
| Pharming | Code or DNS tampering redirects a correct address to a fake site | Anti-malware, updates, check the padlock and certificate |

Warning signs: generic greeting, urgency, spelling mistakes, an address that does not match, a request for codes.

## Firewalls

| | Hardware | Software |
|---|---|---|
| Form | Separate device or router feature | Program on one computer |
| Protects | Whole network | One device |
| Strengths | Does not slow the PC; central control | Cheap; per-program rules; works on any network |
| Weaknesses | Costly; specialist set-up | Uses memory and processor; can be disabled |

A firewall filters traffic using **rules**. It cannot stop a user who is **tricked** into giving data away.

## Advantages and disadvantages of methods

- **Anonymising:** allows data sharing; but can sometimes be reversed by combining data.
- **Aggregating:** hides individuals; but loses detail, and small groups can reveal people.
- **Geotag removal:** simple; but must be done every time.
- **Duty of confidence:** clear consequences; but relies on people following rules.
- **Firewall:** blocks unauthorised traffic; but does not stop social engineering.
- **Training:** reduces mistakes; but needs repeating.

## Building a six-mark answer

Method → how it works → what it stops → one limitation → judgement for the scenario. For example: "A hardware firewall protects every device on the school network because it filters traffic at the entry point. It stops unauthorised connections. However, it cannot stop a pupil being tricked by a phishing email, so staff training is also needed."

## Quick scenario

A school receives a call from someone claiming to be from the exam board, asking the office to read out a list of pupils' dates of birth and home addresses "to check the records".

1. **Attack:** vishing, because the request comes by voice and tries to collect personal data.
2. **Risk:** the data could be used for identity fraud or to find pupils at home.
3. **Response:** do not give the data; end the call; ring the official number to check; report the call to the data protection lead; remind staff of the duty of confidence.
4. **Longer term:** training for office staff and clear rules on who may release data.

## Exam traps

- Defining personal data as name and address only.
- Swapping anonymise and aggregate.
- Saying pharming needs a click.
- Saying a firewall stops phishing.
- Mixing up smishing and vishing.
- Giving general security advice that is not linked to the scenario.

## Self-test

1. Define personal data.
2. Give three examples of personal data.
3. Why should a photo's geotag be removed before posting?
4. What is the difference between anonymising and aggregating?
5. Give two duty of confidence measures.
6. What is smishing?
7. What is vishing?
8. How does pharming differ from phishing?
9. Give two warning signs of a phishing email.
10. State one difference between hardware and software firewalls.
11. Why can a firewall not stop all phishing?
12. Give two network security measures that protect personal data.

### Answers

1. Information about a living person that can identify them.
2. Any three: name, address, phone number, email, date of birth, photo, bank details, medical record.
3. It contains location data that could reveal where the person lives or is.
4. Anonymising removes identifying details; aggregating combines records into totals.
5. Any two: staff contracts, non-disclosure agreements, training, access rights, disciplinary rules.
6. Phishing by text message.
7. Phishing by phone call or voicemail.
8. Pharming redirects the user to a fake site even when the correct address is typed; phishing needs the user to follow a link.
9. Any two: generic greeting, urgent threat, spelling mistakes, mismatched sender or link.
10. Hardware protects the whole network from a separate device; software protects one computer only.
11. It filters traffic by rules but cannot prevent a person from choosing to click a link or give details.
12. Any two: firewall, encryption, access rights, two-factor authentication, anti-malware, updates.
