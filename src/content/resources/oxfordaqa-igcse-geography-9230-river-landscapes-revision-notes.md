These notes cover section 3.1.4.2, River landscapes, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Geography (9230) specification, Version 4.3. Students study either river landscapes or hot desert landscapes in Section D. Key ideas: "The shape of river valleys changes as rivers flow downstream"; "Distinctive fluvial landforms result from different physical processes"; "Different management strategies can be used to protect river landscapes from the effects of flooding." For full explanations, read the [river landscapes study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-river-landscapes/). For the whole unit, see the [Living with the Physical Environment study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-geography-living-with-the-physical-environment/).

## Specification checklist

- **Long profile**; changing **cross profile**.
- **Fluvial processes:** erosion, transport, deposition.
- **Landforms:** interlocking spurs, waterfalls, gorges, meanders, ox-bow lakes, levées, floodplains, estuaries.
- **Flood risk:** precipitation, geology, relief, land use; **hydrographs**.
- **Management:** hard and soft engineering, costs and benefits.
- **Example scheme** in the region.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Long profile | Section from source to mouth |
| Cross profile | Section across channel and valley |
| Discharge | Volume of water per second (cumecs) |
| Hydraulic action | Force of water on banks and bed |
| Abrasion | Wearing by the river's load |
| Attrition | Load breaking down into smaller, rounder pieces |
| Solution | Dissolving of rock |
| Traction / saltation / suspension | Rolling / bouncing / carried in flow |
| Floodplain | Flat land that floods |
| Lag time | Time from peak rainfall to peak discharge |

## River profiles

| | Upper | Middle | Lower |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gradient | Steep | Gentler | Very gentle |
| Valley | Narrow V-shape | Wider | Wide flat floodplain |
| Channel | Narrow, shallow, rocky | Wider, deeper | Widest, deepest, smooth |
| Main process | Vertical erosion | Lateral erosion and transport | Deposition |
| Load | Large, angular | Smaller | Fine, rounded |

Long profile: a smooth concave curve. Discharge increases downstream.

## Processes

| Erosion | Transport | Deposition |
|---|---|---|
| Hydraulic action | Traction (large stones rolled) | When energy falls: lower discharge, shallow water, inside of bends, entering a lake or sea, load too heavy |
| Abrasion | Saltation (small stones bounce) | |
| Attrition | Suspension (silt, clay) | |
| Solution | Solution (dissolved minerals) | |
| Vertical (down) and lateral (sideways) erosion | | |

## Landforms (formation in steps)

**Interlocking spurs (upper course):** river cuts down by vertical erosion, winding around hillsides; spurs of higher land overlap like teeth of a zip.

**Waterfall and gorge:**
1. Hard rock lies over softer rock.
2. Soft rock is eroded faster (abrasion, hydraulic action).
3. A step forms; water plunges and cuts a plunge pool.
4. The hard rock above is undercut and collapses.
5. The waterfall retreats upstream, leaving a steep-sided gorge.

**Meander and ox-bow lake:**
1. Fastest flow on the outside bend, so erosion forms a river cliff.
2. Slow flow on the inside bend, so deposition forms a slip-off slope.
3. Bend grows; the neck narrows.
4. A flood cuts through the neck.
5. Deposition seals the ends; the old loop becomes an ox-bow lake.

**Floodplain and levée:**
- Floodplain: flat land made by lateral erosion and deposition of silt.
- Levée: when a river floods, the water slows at the bank and drops its coarsest load first, building natural embankments.

**Estuary:** tidal river mouth, with mudflats and salt marsh from deposition.

**Region examples:** Tiger Leaping Gorge (upper Yangtze), Hukou Waterfall (Yellow River), meanders in the Jingjiang reach of the Yangtze, lower Yellow River levées, Yangtze estuary at Shanghai.

## Flood risk factors

| Factor | Higher risk when |
|---|---|
| Precipitation | Heavy, prolonged, or snowmelt |
| Geology | Impermeable rock |
| Relief | Steep slopes |
| Land use | Urban surfaces, deforestation, drainage |

## Hydrograph terms

- **Base flow**, **rising limb**, **peak discharge**, **falling limb**, **lag time**.
- **Flashy** (short lag, steep rising limb, high peak): steep, impermeable, urban or deforested catchments.
- **Flatter** (long lag, low peak): gentle, permeable, vegetated catchments.
- Lag time = time of peak discharge minus time of peak rainfall.

## Flood management

| Strategy | Hard or soft | Benefit | Cost or drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dams and reservoirs | Hard | Store water, make power | Expensive; flood land; displace people; trap silt |
| Straightening | Hard | Speeds water away | Pushes flood downstream; damages habitats |
| Embankments | Hard | Increase channel capacity | Costly; can fail; raise flood levels downstream |
| Flood relief channels | Hard | Divert water around towns | Costly; use land |
| Flood warnings | Soft | Cheap; time to evacuate | Do not stop the flood |
| Flood plain zoning | Soft | Keeps building away from risk | Limits development |
| Planting trees | Soft | Slows runoff | Slow; needs land |
| River restoration | Soft | Natural, creates wetlands | Needs land; may flood farmland |

## Example: Three Gorges Dam, Yangtze (check against your notes)

- **Why:** historic devastating floods; millions on the floodplain; need for electricity.
- **Scheme:** concrete gravity dam about 2.3 km long, about 185 m high; main wall finished 2006, fully operating 2012; very large hydroelectric capacity (over 20 000 MW).
- **Social:** over a million people relocated; towns and cultural sites flooded.
- **Economic:** electricity; flood protection; bigger ships go further upstream; very high cost; some lost livelihoods.
- **Environmental:** traps sediment (less silt downstream); changes habitats; landslide risk; less coal burned.

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain how a levée is formed. [3 marks]

**Model answer:** "When a river floods, water overflows the channel and spreads over the floodplain. As it leaves the channel the flow slows down and loses energy. The river drops its heaviest material first, right beside the channel. After many floods this material builds up into raised banks called levées."

How it scores: one mark each for flooding and loss of energy, deposition of the coarsest load next to the channel, and repeated floods building the bank.

## Quick recall: five ideas to remember

- Upper course: steep, V-shaped valley, vertical erosion. Lower course: flat floodplain, deposition.
- Outside of a meander bend is eroded; the inside is deposited.
- A waterfall retreats because the hard rock is undercut and collapses.
- Lag time is the gap between peak rainfall and peak discharge.
- Hard engineering costs more but gives strong protection; soft engineering is cheaper and works with nature.

## Exam traps

- Explain **how** a landform forms, in steps.
- Attrition is erosion of the **load**; do not describe it as transport.
- Inside of a meander bend: **deposition**; outside: **erosion**.
- Levées are made by **deposition**, not erosion.
- Hydrograph answers need figures with units.
- A scheme answer needs the **name, place and details**, and social, economic and environmental points.

## Self-test

1. Describe the cross profile of the upper course.
2. State the dominant process in the lower course.
3. Name the four types of fluvial erosion.
4. Describe how a gorge forms.
5. Explain why the outside of a meander is eroded.
6. How is an ox-bow lake formed?
7. Why does a river deposit its load?
8. Define lag time and say how it is measured.
9. Give **one** hard and **one** soft engineering strategy, each with one disadvantage.
10. Give **one** social, **one** economic and **one** environmental impact of a flood scheme you have studied.

**Answers**

1. Narrow, V-shaped valley with steep sides.
2. Deposition.
3. Hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition, solution.
4. A waterfall retreats upstream as the hard cap rock is undercut and collapses, leaving a steep-sided gorge.
5. The water flows faster and with more energy on the outside, so hydraulic action and abrasion undercut the bank to form a river cliff.
6. A tight meander's neck is eroded through during a flood; the river takes the shortcut and deposition seals off the old loop.
7. It loses energy: low discharge, shallow water, slower flow on bends, or reaching the sea or a lake.
8. The time between peak rainfall and peak discharge; read from the hydrograph.
9. Example: dam (expensive, floods land); flood plain zoning (restricts development).
10. Example (Three Gorges): over a million people relocated; electricity generated; less silt for the downstream floodplain.
