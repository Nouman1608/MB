These questions cover section 5 of Unit P4: Pure Mathematics 4 in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics (YMA01): differentiation of functions defined implicitly or parametrically, tangents and normals to such curves, and forming simple differential equations, including connected rates of change.

Related: [P4 differentiation study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-p4-differentiation-edexcel-alevel-maths/) and [P4 differentiation revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-p4-differentiation-edexcel-alevel-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** The curve C has equation x²y + y² = 10. Find dy/dx in terms of x and y, and hence find the gradient of C at the point (3, 1). **[4]**

**2.** Given that x = ln y + y², y > 0, find dy/dx in terms of y. **[3]**

**3.** A curve has parametric equations x = t³ + 1, y = t² − 2t. Find the coordinates of the point where t = 2 and the gradient of the curve at that point. **[3]**

**4.** A metal block is placed in a room kept at 18 °C. The temperature of the block is θ °C at time t minutes. The block cools at a rate proportional to the difference between its temperature and the temperature of the room. Write down a differential equation for θ. **[2]**

**5.** A circular ripple spreads on a pond so that its area increases at a constant 12 cm² s⁻¹. Find the rate at which the radius is increasing when the radius is 3 cm. **[3]**

**6.** A curve has parametric equations x = 2 cos t, y = 5 sin t. Find the exact gradient of the normal to the curve at the point where t = π/6. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** The curve C has equation x³ + y³ = 9xy. The point P(2, 4) lies on C. Find an equation of the tangent to C at P, giving your answer in the form ax + by + c = 0, where a, b and c are integers. **[5]**

**8.** The curve C has equation x² − 2xy + 2y² = 8.

**(a)** Show that dy/dx = (y − x)/(2y − x). **[3]**

**(b)** Find the exact coordinates of the points on C where the tangent is parallel to the x-axis. **[3]**

**9.** The curve C has parametric equations x = 2t², y = 4t. The point P on C has parameter t = 1.

**(a)** Find an equation of the normal to C at P. **[4]**

**(b)** The normal meets C again at the point Q. Find the coordinates of Q. **[3]**

**10.** The ellipse E has parametric equations x = 3 cos t, y = 2 sin t, 0 ≤ t < 2π.

**(a)** Show that the tangent to E at the point where t = π/4 has equation 2x + 3y = 6√2. **[5]**

**(b)** The tangent meets the x-axis at A and the y-axis at B. Find the area of triangle OAB, where O is the origin. **[3]**

**11.** A ladder of length 5 m leans against a vertical wall with its foot on horizontal ground. The foot is x m from the wall and the top is y m above the ground. The foot slides away from the wall at 0.3 m s⁻¹. Find the rate at which the top slides down the wall at the instant when x = 3. **[4]**

**12.** A sphere of ice melts so that its volume decreases at a rate proportional to its surface area. Show that its radius decreases at a constant rate. **[4]**

**13.** A curve has parametric equations x = t² + 1, y = t³ − 3t.

**(a)** Find the coordinates of the points where the tangent to the curve is parallel to the x-axis. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the coordinates of the point where the tangent to the curve is parallel to the y-axis. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 2xy + x² dy/dx + 2y dy/dx = 0 [2] (one mark for the product rule on x²y, one for 2y dy/dx).

dy/dx = −2xy/(x² + 2y) [1].

At (3, 1): −6/(9 + 2) = **−6/11** [1].

**2.** Differentiate with respect to x: 1 = (1/y) dy/dx + 2y dy/dx [1].

1 = dy/dx (1 + 2y²)/y [1], so **dy/dx = y/(1 + 2y²)** [1].

**3.** At t = 2: x = 9, y = 0, so the point is **(9, 0)** [1].

dy/dx = (2t − 2)/(3t²) [1]. At t = 2: 2/12 = **1/6** [1].

**4.** **dθ/dt = −k(θ − 18)** [1], where k is a positive constant [1].

**5.** A = πr², so dA/dr = 2πr = 6π at r = 3 [1].

dr/dt = (dA/dt)/(dA/dr) = 12/(6π) [1] = **2/π ≈ 0.637 cm s⁻¹** [1].

**6.** dy/dx = (5 cos t)/(−2 sin t) = −(5/2) cot t [1].

At t = π/6, cot t = √3, so dy/dx = −5√3/2 [1].

Normal gradient = 2/(5√3) = **2√3/15** [1].

**7.** 3x² + 3y² dy/dx = 9y + 9x dy/dx [2] (one mark for the left side, one for the product rule on the right).

dy/dx = (3y − x²)/(y² − 3x) [1].

At (2, 4): (12 − 4)/(16 − 6) = 4/5 [1].

y − 4 = (4/5)(x − 2), so **4x − 5y + 12 = 0** [1].

**8. (a)** 2x − (2y + 2x dy/dx) + 4y dy/dx = 0 [2].

dy/dx (4y − 2x) = 2y − 2x, so dy/dx = (y − x)/(2y − x) [1].

**(b)** dy/dx = 0 when y = x [1].

Substitute: x² − 2x² + 2x² = 8, so x² = 8 and x = ±2√2 [1].

Points **(2√2, 2√2)** and **(−2√2, −2√2)** [1]. At both, 2y − x ≠ 0.

**9. (a)** dx/dt = 4t and dy/dt = 4, so dy/dx = 1/t [1].

At t = 1: P = (2, 4) and the tangent gradient is 1 [1]. Normal gradient = −1 [1].

Normal: y − 4 = −(x − 2), so **y = 6 − x** [1].

**(b)** Substitute: 4t = 6 − 2t² [1], so t² + 2t − 3 = 0 and (t + 3)(t − 1) = 0 [1].

t = −3 gives **Q(18, −12)** [1].

**10. (a)** dx/dt = −3 sin t and dy/dt = 2 cos t [1], so dy/dx = −(2 cos t)/(3 sin t) [1].

At t = π/4: dy/dx = −2/3, and the point is (3√2/2, √2) [1].

y − √2 = −(2/3)(x − 3√2/2) [1].

3y − 3√2 = −2x + 3√2, so **2x + 3y = 6√2** [1].

**(b)** A = (3√2, 0) and B = (0, 2√2) [2].

Area = ½ × 3√2 × 2√2 = **6** [1].

**11.** x² + y² = 25, so when x = 3, y = 4 [1].

Differentiate with respect to t: 2x dx/dt + 2y dy/dt = 0 [1].

3(0.3) + 4 dy/dt = 0 [1], so dy/dt = −0.225. The top slides down at **0.225 m s⁻¹** [1].

**12.** dV/dt = −kS = −4πkr², with k > 0 [1].

V = (4/3)πr³, so dV/dt = 4πr² dr/dt [1].

4πr² dr/dt = −4πkr² [1], so dr/dt = −k, which is constant: **the radius decreases at the constant rate k** [1].

**13. (a)** dy/dt = 3t² − 3 = 0 gives t = ±1 [1].

t = 1 gives **(2, −2)** [1]; t = −1 gives **(2, 2)** [1]. At both, dx/dt = 2t ≠ 0.

**(b)** dx/dt = 2t = 0 gives t = 0 [1], and dy/dt = −3 ≠ 0, so the point is **(1, 0)** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting dy/dx on a y term, or the product rule on xy and x²y.
- Substituting x and y into a parametric gradient instead of the value of t.
- Giving a tangent equation when the normal was asked for.
- Leaving out "k > 0" or the minus sign for a quantity that decreases.
- Dividing by the wrong derivative in a rates question.
- In "parallel to the x-axis" questions, finding the line y = x and not substituting back into the curve.
