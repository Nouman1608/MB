Condensed notes for the section 3.2 row **Non-fatal offences against the person**: assault and battery, and ss47, 20 and 18 of the Offences Against the Person Act 1861. For explanation and a worked problem, use the [non-fatal offences study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-law-7162-non-fatal-offences-against-the-person/).

## Step 1: classify the injury

| Injury | Level | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| No injury, victim expected force | Assault | *Ireland* (1997) |
| Touching, no injury | Battery | *Collins v Wilcock* (1984) |
| Bruising, grazes, minor cuts not through both skin layers, brief unconsciousness, recognised psychiatric injury, cut hair | ABH | *Miller* (1954); *Chan-Fook* (1994); *T v DPP* (2003); *DPP v Smith* (2006) |
| Break in both layers of skin | Wound | *JCC v Eisenhower* (1984) |
| Really serious harm, judged against this victim; serious psychiatric illness; HIV infection | GBH | *DPP v Smith* (1961); *Bollom* (2003); *Burstow* (1997); *Dica* (2004) |

## Step 2: assault

- **Actus reus:** causing apprehension of immediate unlawful force.
- **No apprehension, no assault:** *Lamb* (1967).
- **Immediate = imminent:** *Smith v Woking* (1983); *Constanza* (1997) letters.
- **Words and silence:** *Ireland* (silent calls); *Tuberville v Savage* (1669) words negating; *Read v Coker* (1853) conditional threat.
- **Imitation weapon:** *Logdon v DPP* (1976).
- **Mens rea:** intention or subjective recklessness as to causing apprehension (*Savage*; *Parmenter*, 1991).

## Step 3: battery

- **Actus reus:** application of unlawful force.
- **Any touching:** *Collins v Wilcock*; through clothes: *Thomas* (1985).
- **Indirect:** *Haystead* (2000); *DPP v K* (1990).
- **Continuing act:** *Fagan* (1969). **Omission:** *Santana-Bermudez* (2003).
- **Mens rea:** intention or subjective recklessness as to applying force.

## Step 4: the 1861 Act offences

| | s47 | s20 | s18 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Actus reus | Assault or battery occasioning ABH | Wound or inflict GBH | Wound or cause GBH |
| Mens rea | Mens rea of assault/battery only (*Roberts*, 1971; *Savage*) | Intention/recklessness as to some harm (*Mowatt*, 1968; *Parmenter*) | Intention to cause GBH (*Belfon*, 1976), or to resist arrest (*Morrison*, 1989) |
| Intent type | Basic | Basic | Specific |
| Maximum | 5 years | 5 years | Life |

**"Inflict":** no force needed (*Burstow*). **"Maliciously":** *Cunningham* (1957) subjective recklessness. **Several injuries:** can together be GBH (*Brown and Stratton*, 1998). **Causation for s47:** *Roberts*: victim's foreseeable reaction.

## Step 5: work down the ladder

```
Wound or GBH caused?
   yes -> intention to cause GBH (or to resist arrest)?  yes -> s18
                                                        no  -> foresaw some harm? yes -> s20
                                                                                 no  -> s47 if assault/battery mens rea
   no  -> ABH caused by an assault or battery?  yes -> s47
                                                no  -> force applied? battery : apprehension? assault
```

## Mini applications

**Scenario:** A slaps B, leaving a red mark that fades in minutes.
- A red mark that fades quickly is probably transient or trifling, so not ABH (*Miller*).
- Force was applied intentionally: battery.

**Scenario:** C punches D, intending only to hurt; D's eye socket is fractured.
- Fracture is GBH (*DPP v Smith*, 1961).
- No intention to cause GBH, so s18 fails.
- C intended some harm, so s20 (*Mowatt*).

**Scenario:** E shouts "I'm going to smash your face in" while walking towards F.
- F apprehends immediate force: assault.
- Words alone can be enough (*Ireland*).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Apprehension | Expectation of force, not necessarily fear |
| Immediate | Imminent, not necessarily instantaneous |
| Unlawful force | Force without consent or lawful justification |
| Occasioning | Causing |
| Actual bodily harm | Hurt or injury interfering with health or comfort, more than trifling |
| Wound | Break in both layers of the skin |
| Grievous bodily harm | Really serious harm |
| Maliciously | Intentionally or subjectively recklessly |
| Inflict | Cause; no application of force needed |

## Evaluation points

- **Correspondence breached:** s47 (no foresight of harm), s20 (foresight of some harm for serious harm).
- **Fair labelling:** s47 and s20 share a maximum; "assault occasioning" covers batteries.
- **Language:** "maliciously", "grievous", "inflict" and "cause" are outdated or inconsistent.
- **Reform:** Law Commission report (2015) recommended a modern statute; not enacted.
- **Defence of the law:** judges have adapted the Act to psychiatric injury and disease (*Burstow*; *Dica*).

## Exam traps

- Assault is about apprehension; battery is about force. A punch the victim sees coming is both.
- No injury is needed for battery.
- s47 needs no foresight of any harm.
- s20 needs foresight of some harm, not serious harm.
- Internal bleeding is not a wound.
- Recklessness is never enough for the GBH intent in s18.
