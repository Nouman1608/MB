## The six specification points

1. Explain marketing objectives.
2. Explain how and why marketing objectives are used by a business.
3. Explain the need for a business to have clear marketing objectives.
4. Evaluate the usefulness of marketing objectives for a business and its stakeholders.
5. Evaluate the importance of marketing objectives in the achievement of a business' objectives.
6. Recommend and justify marketing objectives for a business.

## Core recall

### What a marketing objective is

- A target for marketing to achieve in a stated time. It says what, not how.
- The how is the marketing strategy and mix.
- Not the same as a corporate objective (profit, survival), though it supports it.

### Types

| Type | Example (invented) |
|---|---|
| Sales volume or value | Sell 90,000 units; reach £2.4m revenue |
| Market share | Hold 7.5% of the market by value |
| Market growth or penetration | Persuade more of the target group to buy |
| Brand awareness | Recognition up from 25% to 35% |
| Loyalty and retention | Keep 80% of customers year to year |
| New market or segment | Launch in a new region |
| New product launch | Introduce by a set date |
| Profit contribution | Raise margin on a line |

### SMART

Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic, Time-bound. "Raise online sales from £420,000 to £500,000 by the end of next year" passes; "increase sales" does not.

### The chain

Corporate objective, then marketing objective, then marketing strategy and mix, then team targets and budgets.

### How and why used

- Direction and coordination of the mix.
- Control: compare results with targets and act on variances.
- Resource allocation: a loyalty objective needs a different budget from an awareness objective.
- Motivation: targets, bonuses.
- Information for stakeholders (lenders, shareholders).

### Need for clarity

Without clear objectives: teams pull in different directions, money is wasted, success cannot be shown, and the mix becomes inconsistent (for example premium image with heavy discounting).

### Usefulness and limits

| Useful | Limits |
|---|---|
| Direction and focus | Based on forecasts that can be wrong |
| Measurement and control | Can go out of date quickly |
| Resource decisions | Measuring one target can harm another |
| Motivation | Pressure may cause short-term or pushy behaviour |
| Finance and operations can plan | Over-optimistic targets cause stock-outs |

Conclusion usually: very useful if realistic, reviewed regularly and tied to a budget.

### Conflicts

- Share against margin: a price cut raises volume but lowers contribution per unit.
- Awareness against short-term profit: advertising costs come before sales.
- Growth against quality or service: rapid growth may overstretch staff.
- Resolve by referring to the corporate objective and the stage of the business.

### Recommending objectives

| Situation | Suitable objective |
|---|---|
| Launch | Awareness, trial, first-year sales |
| Growing market | Share and growth |
| Mature market | Retention, margin, defend share |
| Falling sales | Reposition, new segment |
| Cash shortage | Margin, short-term sales |
| Strong rivals | Differentiation, loyalty |

A good recommendation: two or three linked objectives, each with a number and a date, a reason tied to the case, and a risk.

## Quick worked reminder

Harbrook Bakery (invented): market £8,000,000 growing 4% a year; share 5% (sales £400,000). Market in two years = 8,000,000 × 1.04² = £8,652,800. Target 7.5% share gives sales of £648,960, growth of 62.24%. The market itself grows only about 8.2%, so most of the gain must come from winning share.

Conflict: price £5.00, variable cost £3.50, 40,000 units. Contribution £60,000. At £4.50 the unit contribution is £1.00, so 60,000 units are needed to match £60,000, an increase of 50%.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Marketing objective | A target for marketing to achieve in a stated period |
| SMART | Specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, time-bound |
| Market share | A firm's sales as a percentage of total market sales |
| Brand awareness | The share of target customers who recognise a brand |
| Customer retention | The share of customers who buy again |
| Contribution | Selling price minus variable cost per unit |
| Variance | The difference between target and actual |

## Exam traps

- A corporate objective is not a marketing objective.
- Give a number and a date.
- Link every objective to this business and its stage.
- Do not assume objectives are compatible.
- For evaluate questions, add limits and a judgement, not a list of benefits only.

## Self-test

1. Define a marketing objective.
2. Give two types of marketing objective.
3. State what SMART stands for.
4. Why is "increase sales" a weak objective?
5. Market sales £12m; a firm sells £900,000. Find its market share.
6. Give two reasons a business needs clear marketing objectives.
7. Give one conflict between two marketing objectives.
8. Suggest a suitable objective for a business launching a new product and explain why.
9. Give one limit of marketing objectives.
10. How can marketing objectives help a business achieve its corporate objective?
11. A product sells at £8 with variable cost £5. Find the margin.
12. Why might a mature market lead a firm to favour retention?

### Answers

1. A target for the marketing function to achieve in a stated period.
2. Any two: sales volume or value; market share; brand awareness; customer retention; new market entry; new product launch.
3. Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic, Time-bound.
4. It has no number, no time scale and no measure of success.
5. 900,000 ÷ 12,000,000 × 100 = **7.5%**.
6. Any two: direction; measurement and control; resource allocation; consistent mix; motivation.
7. For example market share against profit margin, because discounts raise volume but cut margin.
8. Brand awareness or trial, because few customers know the product.
9. Any one: based on forecasts; can go out of date; may encourage a short-term focus; may conflict with other objectives.
10. They set the customer and market results needed, for example a share target that produces the sales behind the profit target.
11. (8 − 5) ÷ 8 × 100 = **37.5%**.
12. New customers are expensive to win and the market is not growing, so keeping existing customers protects profit.
