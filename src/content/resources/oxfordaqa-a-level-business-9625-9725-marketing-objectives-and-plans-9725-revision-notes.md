These notes condense **3.1.2.1 Marketing objectives and plans** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Business (9725) specification, Version 1.1 (International AS, Unit 1 Business and markets). The full teaching, with worked examples, is in the [marketing objectives and plans study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-marketing-objectives-and-plans-9725/). Test yourself afterwards with the [marketing objectives and plans practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-marketing-objectives-and-plans-9725-practice/). All figures below are invented.

## Recall in one minute

- **Marketing objectives (9725 list):** sales volume, sales value, market share, customer retention and repeat sales, brand loyalty, sales per product/employee/region/store/customer. You must calculate and interpret them.
- **Marketing plan elements (9725):** marketing objectives, marketing mix, marketing budget. (Strategy and tactics belong to the older 9625 wording.)
- **One calculation on plans:** return on marketing spending.
- **Influences:** market conditions and degrees of competition; resources (budget, skills of employees, capacity); influences on demand (price, income, competition, marketing activities, number of customers, customer preferences).

## Formulas (9725 Appendix: Formulae and Key data)

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Sales volume | number of units sold in a time period |
| Sales value | number of units sold × average selling price per unit |
| Market share (%) | sales of a business ÷ total market sales × 100 |
| Market growth (%) | change in market size ÷ original market size × 100 |
| Customer retention rate (%) | (end period customers − new customers) ÷ start period customers × 100 |
| Sales per product | value of sales by volume or value |
| Sales per employee | value of total sales ÷ average number of employees in the period |
| Sales per region / store / customer | value of total sales ÷ number of regions / stores / customers |
| Return on marketing spending (%) | profit from marketing activities ÷ marketing spend × 100 |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Marketing objective | A measurable target for the marketing function that supports corporate objectives |
| Sales volume | The quantity of units sold in a period |
| Sales value | The revenue earned from sales in a period |
| Market share | A firm's sales as a percentage of total sales in its market |
| Customer retention | Keeping existing customers over a period |
| Repeat sales | Purchases made again by existing customers |
| Brand loyalty | Customers' tendency to keep choosing the same brand, often over cheaper rivals |
| Marketing plan | A document setting out marketing objectives, the marketing mix to reach them and the budget |
| Marketing mix | Decisions on product, price, promotion and place |
| Marketing budget | The money allocated to marketing activities for a period |
| Return on marketing spending | Profit from marketing activities as a percentage of what was spent on them |
| Capacity | The maximum output a business can produce; it caps how much demand marketing should create |

## How to interpret each measure

- **Volume up, value down** → the average price fell (discounting or a shift to cheaper lines).
- **Value up faster than volume** → the average price rose.
- **Sales up, share down** → the market grew faster than the firm; rivals are gaining.
- **Share up in a shrinking market** → the firm is winning customers from rivals, but total sales may still fall.
- **Retention down while total customers rise** → new customers are hiding the loss of existing ones.
- **Sales per store down after opening a store** → the new store is weaker than the others or is taking sales from them.
- **Return on marketing spending** → compares campaigns of different sizes; a higher % is not always the bigger total profit.

## Plan elements: purpose and value

| Element | Purpose | Value | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Objectives | Set the target and timescale | Direction; yardstick for review | Unrealistic targets demotivate |
| Marketing mix | Decide how to reach the target | Coordinates the 4Ps and informs other functions | Rivals and customers may not respond as planned |
| Budget | Allocate money | Controls spending; forces priorities; enables return calculations | May be set by what is affordable, not what is needed |

## Influences in one line each

- **Growing market**: volume and share targets realistic.
- **Mature or shrinking market**: retention, loyalty and value targets matter more.
- **Many close competitors**: less freedom on price; rival reactions limit outcomes.
- **Budget**: decides which promotion and research the firm can afford.
- **Skills of employees**: decide which activities can be run well (for example digital campaigns).
- **Capacity**: demand created beyond capacity means lost sales and disappointed customers.
- **Demand influences**: only price and marketing activities are controlled by the firm; income, competition, customer numbers and preferences change outside it.

## Exam traps

- Retention: subtract the **new** customers from the end figure before dividing by the **start** figure.
- Market share is a **%**; leaving it as a decimal (0.085) loses the accuracy mark.
- Market growth uses the **original** market size as the base.
- Return on marketing spending uses **profit**, not revenue.
- Sales per employee uses the **average** number of employees in the period.
- Do not call a falling market share "falling sales": they are different measures.
- Name the 9725 plan elements exactly: objectives, marketing mix, budget.
- In an "explain" answer, link the influence to a specific objective or decision in the case.

## Self-test

1. A firm sells 12,000 units at an average price of $8. Calculate sales value.
2. A firm's sales are $360,000 in a market worth $2.4m. Calculate its market share.
3. A gym starts the year with 600 members, gains 100 and ends with 640. Calculate the customer retention rate.
4. A campaign cost $6,000 and generated $9,000 of profit from marketing activities. Calculate the return on marketing spending.
5. A retailer has sales of $2.7m across 9 stores. Calculate sales per store.
6. Sales volume rose 5% and sales value fell 2%. What happened to the average selling price?
7. A market grew from $40m to $46m. Calculate market growth.
8. Name the three elements of a 9725 marketing plan.
9. Give one reason why a firm in a shrinking market might prefer a retention objective to a volume objective.
10. Explain why capacity is an influence on marketing objectives.
11. Which two influences on demand are directly under the firm's control?
12. A firm's sales rose 4% while its market grew 9%. What happened to its market share?

### Answers

1. 12,000 × 8 = **$96,000**.
2. 360,000 ÷ 2,400,000 × 100 = **15%**.
3. (640 − 100) ÷ 600 × 100 = 540 ÷ 600 × 100 = **90%**.
4. 9,000 ÷ 6,000 × 100 = **150%**.
5. 2,700,000 ÷ 9 = **$300,000** per store.
6. It **fell**: more units brought in less revenue.
7. 6 ÷ 40 × 100 = **15%**.
8. Marketing objectives, marketing mix, marketing budget.
9. Few new customers are entering the market, so keeping existing customers protects sales more cheaply than chasing new buyers.
10. If marketing creates more demand than operations can supply, orders go unfilled and the brand suffers, so targets must fit capacity.
11. **Price** and **marketing activities**.
12. It **fell**, because the firm grew more slowly than the market.
