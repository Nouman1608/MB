**HL only.** These notes cover Regional study 12, Europe during and after the Cold War (1945 to 2020), in the Europe option examined on HL Paper 3. The country case after the Cold War is Germany. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-europe-cold-war/) for fuller explanation.

## What Paper 3 wants

Two 15-mark essays from different regional studies in two hours. Aim for in-depth understanding of the argument, a thorough and balanced evaluation of perspectives and a substantiated judgement.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Iron curtain | Churchill's 1946 image of the divide across Europe |
| Truman Doctrine | 1947 US pledge to support states resisting communism |
| Marshall Plan | US economic aid to rebuild Europe from 1948 |
| Cominform / COMECON | Soviet bodies for party coordination (1947) and economic coordination (1949) |
| NATO / Warsaw Pact | Rival military alliances (1949 and 1955) |
| EEC / EU | European Economic Community (1957); European Union (1993) |
| Ostpolitik | West German policy of better relations with the East |
| Stasi | East German secret police |
| Prague Spring | Czechoslovak reform movement of 1968 |
| Solidarity | Independent Polish trade union (1980) |
| Brexit | Britain's departure from the EU |

## Origins of the Cold War

- **Alliance breakdown:** disagreement at Yalta and Potsdam over Poland, Germany and reparations.
- **Ideology:** capitalist democracy against Soviet communism; mutual fear.
- **Leaders:** Stalin (security, buffer); Truman (firm, containment).
- **Economics:** Marshall Plan; Soviet rejection; Cominform and COMECON.
- **Historiography:** traditional (Soviet expansion), revisionist (US economic aims), post-revisionist (mutual fear).

## Germany, 1945 to 1991

| Stage | Key points |
|---|---|
| Blockade (1948 to 1949) | Currency reform; airlift; two states formed |
| Wall (1961) | Exodus of about 2.7 million; Ulbricht; Khrushchev's pressure; at least 140 deaths |
| West | Economic miracle; guest workers; 1968 protests; Ostpolitik |
| East | SED control; Stasi; work and childcare; shortages |
| Reunification | East's economic weakness; Gorbachev; Hungary's border; Leipzig protests; Kohl; US support; Two Plus Four |

## Western and Northern Europe

- **NATO:** US guarantee; West Germany in 1955.
- **EEC:** ECSC (1951); Rome (1957); Britain joins in 1973; growth and welfare.
- **Social:** peace movements; American culture; consumer society.
- **USA:** dependence and tension; Suez (1956); France leaves NATO command (1966); Finland neutral; Sweden non-aligned; Norway and Denmark limit bases.

## Central and Eastern Europe

| Theme | Points |
|---|---|
| Motives | Buffer against attack; security against Germany; ideology |
| Methods | Red Army; communists in key posts; rigged elections; Czech coup (1948); show trials; Warsaw Pact |
| Economics | COMECON; Soviet planning; heavy industry; lagging living standards |
| Social | Censorship; secret police; repression of churches; travel limits; welfare without freedom |

## Opposition to Soviet control

- **Yugoslavia:** Tito's break (1948); self-management; non-alignment; Western aid.
- **Hungary 1956:** Nagy, Warsaw Pact exit promise, Soviet invasion.
- **Czechoslovakia 1968:** Dubček, "socialism with a human face"; invasion; Brezhnev Doctrine.
- **Poland:** Solidarity; martial law; Round Table (1989).

## Integration to 2020

| Theme | Points |
|---|---|
| Expansion | 2004 (ten states); 2007; 2013 |
| Economic | Single market; euro (1999 and 2002); CAP; 2009 euro crisis; austerity |
| Social | Schengen; Erasmus; migration tension (2015) |
| Resistance | Danish no (1992); French and Dutch no (2005); populist parties; Brexit (2016) |

## Germany after the Cold War

- **Social:** east-west divide; unemployment and migration west; arrivals in 2015; rise of the AfD.
- **Economic:** Treuhand; transfers; Hartz reforms; strong exports.
- **Cultural:** capital moved to Berlin (1999); memorials; World Cup 2006.
- **Europe:** euro and enlargement supporter; austerity criticism.

## Worked example: planning an essay

**Question:** "To what extent was the Marshall Plan the main reason for the division of Europe by 1949?"

1. **Thesis:** The Marshall Plan deepened the division, but it followed earlier disagreement and Soviet moves.
2. **Paragraph 1, Marshall Plan:** US aid and Stalin's rejection; Cominform and COMECON in response.
3. **Paragraph 2, earlier causes:** Yalta and Potsdam disputes; Soviet control in eastern Europe; Truman Doctrine.
4. **Paragraph 3, Germany:** currency reform, blockade and two states.
5. **Perspective:** traditional, revisionist and post-revisionist views.
6. **Judgement:** Marshall aid was a turning point, not the sole cause.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the Cold War as only a US-Soviet story without Europe's own actors.
- Using the Wall as the only German event.
- Confusing the EEC with the EU.
- Using exact figures for refugees or aid without "about".
- Ignoring Northern Europe when the question asks.

## Self-test

1. What did the Truman Doctrine promise?
2. What was COMECON?
3. Why was the Berlin Wall built?
4. How did Tito's Yugoslavia differ from other communist states?
5. What did the Maastricht Treaty create?
6. What was Brexit?

**Answers:** 1. US support for countries resisting communism. 2. The Soviet-led economic organisation of 1949. 3. To stop mass emigration from East Germany. 4. It was independent of Moscow, with self-management and non-alignment. 5. The European Union. 6. Britain's departure from the EU (vote 2016, exit 2020).
