## Ideas and examples at a glance

| Who or what | Idea | Key point | One evaluation point |
|---|---|---|---|
| David Ricardo (1817) | Comparative advantage | Countries gain by specialising and trading | Assumes gains are shared; workers may lose out |
| UN 0.7% target (1970) | Aid as a share of GNI | Sets a benchmark for donors | Few donors meet it; national budgets compete |
| Jeffrey Sachs, *The End of Poverty* (2005) | More, well-planned aid | Aid can end extreme poverty | Depends on aid being managed well |
| Dambisa Moyo, *Dead Aid* (2009) | Trade and investment over aid | Long-term aid can cause dependency | Ignores areas where aid clearly works, such as vaccination |
| Fair trade certification | Fairer prices | Can give producers a better price | Certification costs; not all benefits reach workers |
| EU GSP+ (Pakistan since 2014) | Trade linked to conditions | Lower tariffs if 27 conventions are ratified and implemented | Hard to check implementation |
| Remittances (World Bank, 2023) | Private money flows | Larger than FDI and official aid to low- and middle-income countries | Depends on jobs abroad; not targeted at the poorest |
| UK aid cuts (2021 and 2025) | National priorities | 0.7% cut to 0.5%, then a plan for 0.3% by 2027 | Shows national and global perspectives in conflict |

Remember: these are **context**, not content to learn for marks. Use them to practise identifying perspectives and evaluating claims.

## Source terms (Component 1)

The syllabus says candidates will benefit from understanding **bias, claim, fact, generalisation, opinion, prediction, value and vested interest**.

- **Claim**: a statement presented as true. Ask: what supports it?
- **Fact**: can be checked. "Exports were 2 million tonnes."
- **Opinion**: a judgement. "Aid is wasted."
- **Generalisation**: one case stretched to all. "All aid is stolen."
- **Prediction**: about the future. "Exports will double."
- **Value**: a belief about right and wrong. "Rich countries have a duty to help."
- **Vested interest**: the author gains from the outcome.
- **Bias**: one-sided selection or language.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Topic vs issue**: "Development, trade and aid" is a topic; "Should aid be tied to conditions?" is an issue.
- **Perspective vs opinion**: a perspective rests on evidence and reasoning.
- **Fact vs claim**: a claim may not be true; a fact can be checked.
- **Correlation vs cause**: certified farmers earning more does not prove certification caused it.
- **Income vs profit**: higher sales may not mean higher earnings once costs are paid.
- **Cause vs consequence**: a cause comes before and helps produce the issue; a consequence follows from it.
- **Short-term vs long-term consequences**: an aid cut saves money now but may harm health for years.
- **Local/national vs global perspectives**: the Individual Report needs both kinds.

## Perspective map

| Who | Likely perspective | Why they may hold it |
|---|---|---|
| Garment or farm worker | Wants secure jobs and fair pay | Their income depends on it |
| Exporters' association | Supports lower tariffs | Members gain more orders |
| Donor-country politician | May want to cut or redirect aid | Answers to taxpayers at home |
| Aid charity | Wants aid to continue | Sees urgent needs; may rely on aid funding |
| Receiving government | Wants aid without strict conditions | Wants control over its own spending |
| International organisation | Supports aid and trade targets | Works towards global goals such as the SDGs |

Always ask: what is their role, what do they gain or lose, and what values shape their view?

## Cause and consequence chains

- Tariff removed -> cheaper exports -> more orders -> more jobs -> pressure on conditions unless standards are enforced.
- Aid cut -> clinic funding falls -> fewer vaccinations -> health effects that last for years.
- Low prices for raw crops -> low farm incomes -> young people leave farming -> local food supply affected.

## Courses of action checklist

For each action, ask: does it tackle a cause or a symptom; who must agree; what does it cost; who gains and who loses; will it last when funding stops; what could go wrong?

## Mini essay plan

*Question type: "Evaluate which course of action would be most effective in reducing poverty in a country that exports raw materials. Use all the sources."*

1. **Judgement first**: name the action you think is most effective and the criterion you are using (for example, long-term effect on incomes).
2. **Action 1, e.g. more aid**: what the sources say; who benefits; strength (fast, targeted); weakness (dependency, depends on donor budgets).
3. **Action 2, e.g. trade deal with lower tariffs**: evidence of jobs; vested interest of exporters; prediction not yet proven.
4. **Action 3, e.g. investing in local processing**: adds value; slower; needs investment.
5. **Compare** against the same criteria: effectiveness, feasibility, sustainability, who gains and loses.
6. **Reasoned conclusion**: answer the question, and say what evidence would change your view.

## Quick self-test

1. Is "Development, trade and aid" a topic or an issue?
2. Does Component 1 credit your prior knowledge of trade?
3. Give the syllabus definition of a perspective.
4. Define tied aid.
5. "Removing tariffs will double our exports." Which source term is this?
6. Why might an exporters' association have a vested interest in a trade deal?
7. Name two questions to ask about a study comparing certified and non-certified farmers.
8. Which perspectives must the Individual Report include?
9. Why is "What is fair trade?" a weak Individual Report question?
10. What must a Team Project team say about its action in the Explanation of Research and Planning?
11. Which assessment objectives carry no marks in Component 1?

### Answers

1. A topic. Issues sit within it.
2. No. The syllabus says candidates are not assessed on prior knowledge of the topic or issue in the sources.
3. "A viewpoint on an issue based on evidence and reasoning."
4. Aid that must be spent on goods or services from the donor country.
5. A prediction (and a claim).
6. Its members gain more orders and profit if the deal goes ahead.
7. Any two: were the groups alike in other ways; how large was the sample; who funded it; does it measure income or profit; does it show cause or only correlation.
8. Local and/or national perspectives, and global perspectives.
9. It is descriptive and does not divide opinion.
10. Any of: the action, the plan, roles and responsibilities, how the action will be evidenced and how its success will be measured.
11. AO2 Reflection and AO3 Communication and collaboration (Component 1 is 100% AO1).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing what you know about aid and trade instead of using the sources.
- Labelling a source "biased" without naming the interest and quoting it.
- Mixing up a prediction or a generalisation with a fact.
- Accepting a "rise in income" without asking about costs, sample or comparison group.
- Treating "aid or trade" as the only two options.
- Giving perspectives without explaining why each person holds them.
- Evaluating an action without saying who wins, who loses and whether it lasts.
- Ending an extended answer with a summary instead of a judgement.
