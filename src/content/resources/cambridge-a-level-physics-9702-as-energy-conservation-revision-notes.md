Condensed notes on subtopic 5.1 Energy conservation (Cambridge 9702, AS Topic 5).
For full explanations and worked examples, use the
[energy conservation study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-energy-conservation/);
for the whole topic, see the [Work, Energy and Power revision notes](/resources/as-physics-work-energy-revision-notes/).

## What 5.1 asks you to do

- Understand work and use work done = force × displacement in the direction of the force
- Recall and apply the principle of conservation of energy
- Recall that efficiency = useful energy output ÷ total energy input, and use it in problems
- Define power as work done per unit time; use P = W/t; derive and use P = Fv

## Core equations

```
W = F s cos(theta)          joules (J); theta between F and s
efficiency = useful output / total input      (energy or power, no unit)
P = W / t                   watts (W) = J s^-1
P = F v                     F along the direction of v
```

## Work done in one line each

- Force along the motion: W = Fs, positive, energy given **to** the object.
- Force against the motion (θ = 180°): W = −Fs, energy taken **from** the object.
- Force perpendicular (θ = 90°): W = 0. Normal reaction on a level floor, pendulum string tension, the centripetal force on a body in circular motion.
- Work done **against** friction = friction force × distance = energy dissipated.
- A force that does not move its point of application does no work, however large it is: holding a load stationary transfers no energy to the load.

## Conservation of energy

> Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only be transferred from one form to another, so the total energy of a closed system stays constant.

Use it as a balance between two moments:

```
initial energy + energy supplied = final energy + energy dissipated
```

Typical energy supplied: work done by an engine, motor or applied force.
Typical energy dissipated: work done against friction, air resistance or electrical resistance, ending as internal energy of the surroundings.

## Efficiency

- Ratio, so **no unit**; as a decimal it lies between 0 and 1.
- Can use energies or powers, because both are measured over the same time.
- Chain of devices: overall efficiency = product of individual efficiencies.
- Rearrangements: useful output = efficiency × input; input = useful output ÷ efficiency.

**Example.** A crane motor draws 14 kW and lifts a 2.0 × 10³ kg load at a steady 0.50 m s⁻¹.

```
useful power = F v = mg v = 2000 x 9.81 x 0.50 = 9810 W
efficiency   = 9810 / 14 000 = 0.70  (70%)
```

Note how P = Fv gives the useful power directly when the speed is constant: the lifting force equals the weight.

## Power

**Derivation of P = Fv** (learn the order):

1. P = W/t (definition)
2. W = Fs for a force parallel to the displacement
3. so P = Fs/t
4. s/t = v, so P = Fv

**Constant speed:** driving force = total resistive force, so P = F_resistive × v.
**Maximum speed** with a fixed power: v_max = P ÷ F_resistive.
**Accelerating:** driving force F = P/v; resultant force = F − F_resistive = ma.

**Example.** A cyclist produces 240 W of useful power and travels at a steady 8.0 m s⁻¹ on a level road. Total resistive force = P/v = 240 ÷ 8.0 = **30 N**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to recall |
|---|---|
| Work done | Force × displacement in the direction of the force |
| Joule | Work done when a force of 1 N moves its point of application 1 m in the direction of the force |
| Principle of conservation of energy | Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred; total energy of a closed system is constant |
| Dissipated energy | Energy transferred to internal energy of the surroundings, no longer available for useful work |
| Efficiency | Useful energy output ÷ total energy input |
| Power | Work done per unit time |
| Watt | One joule per second |

## Exam traps

- Writing W = Fs when the force is at an angle: the cos θ term is missing.
- Using the distance along a slope where the vertical height belongs (that is the GPE step of an energy account).
- Putting the resistive force into P = Fv while the vehicle is accelerating.
- Forgetting one store in the energy balance, most often the work done against friction.
- An efficiency above 1 or 100%: the ratio has been inverted.
- Defining power as "energy per second" without "work done" or "energy transferred".
- Mixing kW and W, or minutes and seconds, in P = W/t.

## Self-test

1. State the principle of conservation of energy.
2. Define power.
3. A force of 80 N pulls a block 5.0 m. The force is at 60° to the displacement. Find the work done by the force.
4. Why does the normal contact force do no work on a block sliding along a level floor?
5. A 2.0 kg trolley moving at 5.0 m s⁻¹ is brought to rest by friction over 10 m. Find the average friction force.
6. A 2.0 kW heater runs for 60 s. How much energy does it transfer?
7. A motor (efficiency 0.90) drives a pump (efficiency 0.75). Find the overall efficiency.
8. A kettle has an input power of 2.2 kW and an efficiency of 80%. Find the useful energy output in 3.0 minutes.
9. Write out the derivation of P = Fv.
10. A car moves at a steady 25 m s⁻¹ against a resistive force of 800 N. Find the output power.
11. The same car's engine power stays constant. At 20 m s⁻¹, with the same resistive force, is the resultant force forwards, backwards or zero?
12. Why is it wrong to say that energy is "lost" in a collision?

**Answers:**
1. Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it is only transferred from one form to another, so the total energy of a closed system is constant.
2. Work done per unit time.
3. W = 80 × 5.0 × cos 60° = **200 J**.
4. It acts at 90° to the displacement, so cos 90° = 0 and no work is done.
5. Kinetic energy = ½ × 2.0 × 5.0² = 25 J; force = 25 ÷ 10 = **2.5 N**.
6. W = Pt = 2000 × 60 = **1.2 × 10⁵ J**.
7. 0.90 × 0.75 = **0.675** (67.5%).
8. Input = 2200 × 180 = 3.96 × 10⁵ J; useful = 0.80 × 3.96 × 10⁵ = **3.2 × 10⁵ J**.
9. P = W/t; W = Fs; so P = Fs/t; s/t = v; so P = Fv.
10. P = Fv = 800 × 25 = **2.0 × 10⁴ W** (20 kW).
11. Forwards: driving force = 20 000 ÷ 20 = 1000 N, which exceeds 800 N, so the car accelerates.
12. The energy is transferred to internal energy and sound; it still exists but is dissipated, so it is not lost.
