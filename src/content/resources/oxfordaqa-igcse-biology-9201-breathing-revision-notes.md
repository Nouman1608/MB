## Purpose of the breathing system

- Takes air **into and out of** the body.
- So **oxygen** from the air can diffuse **into** the bloodstream.
- And **carbon dioxide** can diffuse **out of** the bloodstream into the air.

## Where things are

- **Lungs** in the **thorax** (upper body).
- Protected by the **ribcage**.
- Separated from the **abdomen** by the **diaphragm**.

**Air path**

```
nose or mouth → trachea → bronchi (2) → bronchioles (many) → alveoli
```

**Parts to recognise on a diagram**: ribs, intercostal muscles, diaphragm, lungs, trachea, bronchi,
bronchioles, alveoli.

## Inhaling and exhaling

| | Inhale | Exhale |
|---|---|---|
| Intercostal muscles | Contract, ribcage pulled up | Relax, ribcage moves down |
| Diaphragm muscles | Contract, diaphragm flattens | Relax, diaphragm returns to dome |
| Thorax volume | Increases | Decreases |
| Pressure in thorax | Decreases, below atmospheric | Increases |
| Air | Enters the lungs | Leaves the lungs |

**Answer order**: muscles → volume → pressure → air movement.

## The bell-jar model

| Model part | Represents |
|---|---|
| Bell jar | Thorax (ribcage) |
| Tube | Trachea |
| Y-branches | Bronchi |
| Balloons | Lungs |
| Rubber sheet | Diaphragm |

- Pull sheet down → volume up → pressure down → balloons inflate (inhale).
- **Limits**: rigid jar cannot model rib movement; sheet is pulled from outside, not contracted; no alveoli or
  gas exchange.

## Alveoli

- **Very large surface area** (very many tiny sacs).
- **Richly supplied with blood capillaries**.
- So gases readily diffuse into and out of the blood.
- Blood flow keeps oxygen concentration in the blood low and carbon dioxide high next to the alveoli, so the
  gradients are kept.

Worked reminder: many small spheres have far more surface area than one large sphere of the same total volume;
1000 spheres of radius 0.1 cm have 10 times the area of one sphere of radius 1 cm.

## Inhaled and exhaled air (typical approximate values)

| Gas | Inhaled | Exhaled |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | 21% | 16% |
| Carbon dioxide | 0.04% | 4% |

Worked reminder: 15 breaths × 0.4 dm³ = 6.0 dm³ per minute; oxygen absorbed ≈ 6.0 × 0.05 = **0.30 dm³ per
minute**.

## Mechanical ventilators (B)

- Used when **spontaneous breathing stops** because of disease or injury.

| Type | Action |
|---|---|
| Negative pressure | Lowers the pressure around the chest, so air is **drawn** into the lungs |
| Positive pressure | **Forces** air into the lungs at above atmospheric pressure |

## Model answers to learn

- **Describe how you breathe in (4 marks):** "The intercostal muscles contract, pulling the ribcage up. The
  diaphragm muscles contract and the diaphragm flattens. The volume of the thorax increases, so the pressure
  falls below atmospheric pressure. Air enters the lungs."
- **Why does gas exchange in the alveoli happen quickly? (2 marks):** "The alveoli give a very large surface area,
  and the rich capillary supply keeps the concentration gradients steep."

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Thorax | Upper part of the body containing the lungs and heart |
| Abdomen | Lower part of the body, below the diaphragm |
| Intercostal muscles | Muscles between the ribs |
| Diaphragm | Sheet of muscle separating thorax from abdomen |
| Trachea | Windpipe carrying air to the bronchi |
| Bronchus (plural bronchi) | One of the two tubes from the trachea to the lungs |
| Bronchiole | Small branching tube inside the lung |
| Alveolus (plural alveoli) | Tiny air sac where gas exchange happens |
| Ventilation (breathing) | Movement of air into and out of the lungs |
| Mechanical ventilator | Machine that helps a patient breathe |

## Exam traps

- On inhaling the diaphragm **flattens** (moves down); it does not move up.
- Pressure in the thorax **falls** when you breathe in.
- Lungs have no muscles; they do not "suck".
- Breathing is not respiration.
- Exhaled air still contains about **16% oxygen**.
- **Negative** = drawn in; **positive** = forced in.

## Self-test

1. What is the function of the breathing system?
2. What separates the thorax from the abdomen?
3. List the air passages in order from the trachea to the alveoli.
4. What happens to the intercostal muscles when you inhale?
5. What happens to the diaphragm when you inhale?
6. Explain why air enters the lungs during inhalation.
7. What happens to thorax volume and pressure during exhalation?
8. Give the two adaptations of alveoli named in the specification.
9. In the bell-jar model, what does the rubber sheet represent?
10. Give one limitation of the bell-jar model.
11. When might a patient need a mechanical ventilator?
12. How does a negative pressure ventilator help a patient inhale?

### Answers

1. To take air into and out of the body so oxygen can diffuse into the blood and carbon dioxide can diffuse out.
2. The diaphragm.
3. Trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli.
4. They contract, pulling the ribcage upwards.
5. Its muscles contract and it flattens.
6. Thorax volume increases, so pressure falls below that of the surrounding air, and air moves in.
7. Volume decreases and pressure increases, so air leaves.
8. A very large surface area; a rich supply of blood capillaries.
9. The diaphragm.
10. Any one: rigid jar cannot show rib movement; sheet pulled from outside rather than contracting; no gas
    exchange.
11. When spontaneous breathing stops because of disease or injury.
12. It lowers the pressure around the chest, so the chest expands and air is drawn into the lungs.
