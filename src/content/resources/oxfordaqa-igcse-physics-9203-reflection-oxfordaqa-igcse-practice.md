These questions cover section 3.3.4 Reflection, statements a to c, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) specification, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered. If a topic is new, start with the [reflection study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-reflection-oxfordaqa-igcse/) or the [reflection revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-reflection-oxfordaqa-igcse-revision-notes/). Where a question asks for a diagram, describe it in words if you are working without paper.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State the law of reflection. **[1]**

**2.** Define the normal to a reflecting surface. **[2]**

**3.** A ray of light strikes a plane mirror. The angle between the ray and the surface of the mirror is 34°.

**(a)** State the angle of incidence. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the angle between the incident ray and the reflected ray. **[1]**

**4.** Which word describes the image formed by a plane mirror?

A: real

B: inverted

C: virtual

D: magnified

**[1]**

**5.** Explain what is meant by saying that the image in a plane mirror is laterally inverted. **[1]**

**6.** Straight water waves in a ripple tank strike a straight barrier. The wavefronts arrive so that their direction of travel makes 50° with the normal to the barrier.

**(a)** State the angle the reflected waves' direction makes with the normal. **[1]**

**(b)** State whether the wavelength of the reflected waves is larger, smaller or the same, and give a reason. **[2]**

**7.** A boy stands 1.8 m in front of a plane mirror on a wall. He then walks 0.6 m towards the mirror. Calculate the new distance between the boy and his image. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**8.** A ray of light from a small lamp, L, strikes a plane mirror at point P with an angle of incidence of 40°.

**(a)** Describe how you would construct the normal at P and the reflected ray accurately. **[3]**

**(b)** A second ray from L strikes the mirror at a different point. Explain how the two reflected rays can be used to locate the image of L. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the image of L is described as virtual. **[2]**

**9.** A periscope is made from a vertical tube with two plane mirrors fixed parallel to each other, each at 45° to the vertical. Light from a distant tree enters the top horizontally.

**(a)** Calculate the angle of incidence at the top mirror. **[2]**

**(b)** State the direction of the light after reflection at the top mirror. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe the image of the tree seen at the bottom of the periscope, compared with the tree. **[2]**

**10.** Two plane mirrors, M1 and M2, are fixed with their surfaces at 90° to each other. A ray strikes M1 with an angle of incidence of 30° and then strikes M2.

**(a)** Calculate the angle between the ray reflected from M1 and the surface of M1. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the angle of incidence at M2. **[2]**

**(c)** Compare the direction of the ray leaving M2 with the direction of the original ray. **[1]**

**11.** A laser beam strikes a plane mirror with an angle of incidence of 20°. Keeping the beam fixed, a student rotates the mirror until the angle of incidence is 32°.

**(a)** Calculate the angle between the incident and reflected rays before and after the rotation. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the angle through which the reflected beam has turned, and compare it with the rotation of the mirror. **[2]**

**12.** A parallel beam of light falls on a polished metal plate and on a sheet of white paper.

**(a)** Explain, using the idea of the normal, why the metal plate produces a clear image but the paper does not. **[3]**

**(b)** A student says, "The paper does not obey the law of reflection." Explain whether the student is correct. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A construction line perpendicular (at 90°) to the reflecting surface [1] drawn at the point of incidence [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** 90° − 34° = **56°** [1].

**(b)** 56° + 56° = **112°** [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The angle given is to the surface. Using 34° as the angle of incidence loses both marks.

**4.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Left and right are reversed in the image (for example, writing appears backwards) [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** **50°** [1].

**(b)** The same [1]: the waves are still in the same medium, so their speed and frequency do not change, and nor does the wavelength [1]. **[3]**

**7.** New distance from mirror = 1.8 − 0.6 = 1.2 m [1]. Image is 1.2 m behind the mirror, so separation = 1.2 + 1.2 = **2.4 m** [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The image is as far behind the mirror as the boy is in front, so the separation is double his distance from the mirror.

**8. (a)** Use a protractor to draw a dashed line at 90° to the mirror at P (the normal) [1]. Measure 40° from the normal on the other side from the incident ray [1]. Draw the reflected ray with a ruler and add an arrow pointing away from P [1].

**(b)** Extend both reflected rays backwards behind the mirror as dashed lines [1]. The image is where the dashed lines cross [1].

**(c)** The reflected rays do not actually come from the image position; they only appear to [1]. No light reaches the point behind the mirror, so the image cannot be formed on a screen [1]. **[7]**

**9. (a)** The horizontal ray makes 45° with the mirror surface [1], so the angle of incidence = 90° − 45° = **45°** [1].

**(b)** Vertically downwards (down the tube) [1].

**(c)** Upright [1] and the right way round (not laterally inverted), because the second reflection reverses the first [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* "Virtual" also earns credit in part (c), but the two features that distinguish a periscope image are that it is upright and not reversed.

**10. (a)** 90° − 30° = **60°** [1].

**(b)** The mirrors and the ray form a triangle: angle between the ray and M2's surface = 180° − 90° − 60° = 30° [1]. Angle of incidence at M2 = 90° − 30° = **60°** [1].

**(c)** It travels back parallel to the original ray, in the opposite direction [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* A check is that the two angles of incidence (30° and 60°) add up to 90° whenever the mirrors are at right angles.

**11. (a)** Before: 20° + 20° = 40° [1]. After: 32° + 32° = 64° [1].

**(b)** Turn = 64° − 40° = **24°** [1]. The mirror rotated by 32° − 20° = 12°, so the beam turns through twice the mirror's rotation [1]. **[4]**

**12. (a)** The metal surface is smooth, so the normals at every point are parallel [1]. Each ray of the parallel beam has the same angle of incidence, so the reflected rays stay parallel and an image can form [1]. The paper surface is rough, so the normals point in many directions and the reflected rays scatter in many directions [1].

**(b)** The student is wrong [1]. Every ray reflected from the paper still has angle of incidence equal to angle of reflection; only the direction of the normal changes from point to point [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the angle to the surface instead of the angle to the normal.
- Giving "real" or "upside down" for a plane mirror image.
- Leaving out "at the point of incidence" when defining the normal.
- Forgetting to double the distance when finding the separation between an object and its image.
- Drawing ray diagrams freehand, without arrows, or with solid lines behind the mirror.
- Claiming that reflection changes wavelength or speed.
