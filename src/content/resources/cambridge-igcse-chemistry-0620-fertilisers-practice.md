## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which compound provides **two** of the three elements in an NPK fertiliser?

A: ammonium sulfate

B: potassium chloride

C: potassium nitrate

D: ammonium nitrate **[1]**

**2.** State the **two** types of salt named in the syllabus as fertilisers. **[2]**

**3.** Describe the purpose of an NPK fertiliser. **[2]**

**4.** Give the formula of ammonium sulfate. **[1]**

**5.** Name the element provided by potassium chloride in a fertiliser. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why a farmer would not use an insoluble compound of nitrogen as a fertiliser. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A farmer's soil is short of potassium and phosphorus but has enough nitrogen. The table shows four fertiliser salts.

| Salt | Formula |
|---|---|
| ammonium nitrate | NH₄NO₃ |
| ammonium phosphate | (NH₄)₃PO₄ |
| potassium chloride | KCl |
| potassium nitrate | KNO₃ |

**(a)** Identify the elements, from N, P and K, that ammonium phosphate provides. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest the **two** salts the farmer should mix to supply both missing elements. Explain your choice. **[3]**

**8.** Ammonium sulfate is made in the laboratory from aqueous ammonia and dilute sulfuric acid.

**(a)** Write a balanced equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why this salt is prepared by titration rather than by adding an excess of a solid and filtering. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe how crystals of ammonium sulfate are obtained once the correct volumes are known. **[2]**

**9.** A farmer spreads calcium hydroxide (slaked lime) and ammonium sulfate on a field on the same day. A strong smell is noticed.

**(a)** Name the gas responsible for the smell. **[1]**

**(b)** Complete and balance the equation: (NH₄)₂SO₄ + Ca(OH)₂ → CaSO₄ + ........ + ........ **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why this is wasteful for the farmer. **[1]**

**10.** A student is given a white fertiliser and is told it is either ammonium sulfate or potassium nitrate.

**(a)** Describe a test that would give a positive result with ammonium sulfate but not with potassium nitrate. Give the result. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe a second test that would confirm the presence of sulfate ions. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Compare the percentage by mass of nitrogen in ammonium nitrate, NH₄NO₃, and potassium nitrate, KNO₃. Show your working. **[4]**

**12.** Fertilisers improve crop growth, but heavy rain can wash them off fields into rivers.

**(a)** Name **two** ions from fertilisers that are found in river water. **[2]**

**(b)** State the effect these ions have on the river. **[2]**

**13.** A bag of fertiliser is labelled "contains ammonium nitrate and potassium chloride".

**(a)** State which of the three NPK elements this fertiliser does **not** provide. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest a salt that could be added to the mixture to make it a complete NPK fertiliser, and explain your choice. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. Potassium nitrate provides K and N.

**2.** Ammonium salts [1]; nitrates [1].

**3.** To provide the elements nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium [1] for improved plant growth [1].

**4.** (NH₄)₂SO₄ [1].

**5.** Potassium [1].

**6.** Plants can only absorb substances dissolved in soil water [1]; an insoluble compound would not dissolve, so the nitrogen could not be taken up [1].

**7. (a)** Nitrogen and phosphorus [1].

**(b)** Ammonium phosphate and potassium chloride (or potassium nitrate) [1]. Ammonium phosphate supplies phosphorus [1]; the potassium salt supplies potassium [1]. (Nitrogen is also supplied, which does no harm.)

**8. (a)** 2NH₃ + H₂SO₄ → (NH₄)₂SO₄: correct formulae [1]; balanced [1].

**(b)** Both reactants are solutions / soluble [1], so there is no excess solid that could be filtered off; exact volumes must be found with an indicator [1].

**(c)** Mix the same volumes again without the indicator [1]; evaporate to the point of crystallisation, leave to crystallise, then filter and dry the crystals [1].

**9. (a)** Ammonia [1].

**(b)** 2NH₃ [1] + 2H₂O [1].

**(c)** Nitrogen is lost to the air as ammonia instead of staying in the soil for the crop [1].

**10. (a)** Warm the fertiliser with aqueous sodium hydroxide [1] (no aluminium foil). Ammonia is given off [1], which turns damp red litmus paper blue [1]. Potassium nitrate gives no ammonia under these conditions.

**(b)** Dissolve it, acidify with dilute nitric acid and add aqueous barium nitrate [1]; a white precipitate forms [1].

**11.** NH₄NO₃: Mr = 14 + 4 + 14 + 48 = 80 [1]; % N = 28 ÷ 80 × 100 = 35.0 % [1].
KNO₃: Mr = 39 + 14 + 48 = 101 [1]; % N = 14 ÷ 101 × 100 = 13.9 % [1].
Ammonium nitrate has the greater percentage of nitrogen.

**12. (a)** Nitrate [1]; phosphate [1].

**(b)** Deoxygenation of the water [1], damaging aquatic life [1].

**13. (a)** Phosphorus [1].

**(b)** Ammonium phosphate (or another soluble phosphate) [1], because it contains phosphate ions, which provide phosphorus [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming only nitrates as nitrogen fertilisers and forgetting ammonium salts.
- Missing the second element in potassium nitrate or ammonium phosphate.
- Writing NH₄SO₄ instead of (NH₄)₂SO₄.
- Counting one nitrogen atom instead of two in ammonium nitrate.
- Choosing excess-solid preparation for a salt made from two solutions.
- Adding aluminium foil in the ammonium test: that is the nitrate test.
- Writing a full eutrophication sequence when only deoxygenation and damage to aquatic life are needed.
