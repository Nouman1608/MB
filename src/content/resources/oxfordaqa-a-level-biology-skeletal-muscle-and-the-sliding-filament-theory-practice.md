## Questions

**1.** A person bends the elbow to lift a book. Explain why the arm needs two muscles, the biceps and the triceps, and why the skeleton must be incompressible. **[3]**

**2.** **(a)** Describe the gross structure of a skeletal muscle. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the microscopic structure of a skeletal muscle fibre. **[3]**

**3.** An electron micrograph of one myofibril is at ×24 000. A relaxed sarcomere measures 60.0 mm on the image and a contracted sarcomere measures 46.8 mm. The A band measures 37.2 mm.
**(a)** Calculate the actual lengths of the relaxed and contracted sarcomeres, in µm. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate by what percentage the sarcomere shortens. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the length of the I band in the relaxed and contracted sarcomeres. **[2]**

**4.** Four images (W to Z) show the same myofibril at different stages. Each thin filament is 0.95 µm and the A band is 1.55 µm. The sarcomere lengths are: W 1.85 µm, X 2.50 µm, Y 2.15 µm, Z 3.05 µm.
**(a)** Put the images in order from most contracted to most relaxed. **[1]**
**(b)** For image Y, calculate the I band and the H zone. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what the data show about the filaments. **[3]**

**5.** Describe how the filaments in a sarcomere change position and how the interaction of myosin, actin, calcium ions and ATP brings this about. **[6]**

**6.** A chemical stops ATP binding to myosin heads. It does not affect calcium ions. A muscle fibre in calcium-rich fluid is treated with it. Predict the state of the fibre and explain your answer. **[4]**

**7.** The table shows the tension developed by a muscle preparation at different calcium ion concentrations (data supplied).

| Calcium ions / µmol dm⁻³ | 0.05 | 0.2 | 0.5 | 1.0 | 2.0 | 5.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tension / % of maximum | 0 | 8 | 40 | 72 | 96 | 100 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship shown. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean increase in tension per µmol dm⁻³ between 0.5 and 1.0. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain the tension at 5.0 µmol dm⁻³ compared with 0.05 µmol dm⁻³. **[3]**

**8.** A muscle holds 7.2 mmol of ATP and 21.6 mmol of phosphocreatine per kg. In an all-out effort it uses 2.4 mmol of ATP per kg each second. Take it that each phosphocreatine molecule regenerates one ATP.
**(a)** Calculate the time for which the ATP store alone would supply the muscle. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how long both stores last together. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage of the stored ATP supply that comes from phosphocreatine. **[1]**

**9.** The table shows the phosphocreatine and ATP content of a muscle during 10 s of maximal effort, as a percentage of the resting value (data supplied).

| Time / s | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phosphocreatine | 100 | 70 | 45 | 25 | 12 | 8 |
| ATP | 100 | 99 | 97 | 95 | 92 | 90 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of fall of phosphocreatine between 0 and 4 s. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the ATP content falls so little. **[2]**
**(c)** Between 6 s and 8 s the phosphocreatine falls at a lower rate than between 0 s and 2 s. Suggest why. **[2]**
**(d)** Describe how the phosphocreatine store is restored after the effort. **[2]**

**10.** Data supplied for two fibre types from one muscle.

| | Fibre J | Fibre K |
|---|---|---|
| Diameter / µm | 48 | 76 |
| Mitochondria / % of fibre volume | 7.5 | 2.1 |
| Time to peak force / ms | 110 | 38 |
| Time for force to fall to 50% / s | 540 | 24 |

**(a)** Identify the slow fibre, using two pieces of evidence. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the mitochondrial volume of J is than K, to 1 decimal place. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how the properties of J suit a postural muscle. **[3]**

**11.** Bending the elbow lifts a forearm. Describe and explain the changes in the sarcomeres of the biceps and of the triceps as the elbow bends, referring to antagonistic action. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Muscles can only pull when they contract, not push [1]; the biceps contracts to bend the elbow while the triceps relaxes, and the triceps contracts to straighten it, so two muscles are needed to move the bone in both directions [1]; an incompressible skeleton does not squash, so the pull is transmitted through the tendon to the bone, which acts as a lever [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Say "pull" and "relaxes" explicitly. An answer that says the muscles "work together" does not show the opposing action.

**2. (a)** Many bundles of muscle fibres wrapped in connective tissue [1]; supplied with blood vessels and nerves, and attached to bone by tendons [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Each fibre is one long cell with many nuclei [1]; the sarcoplasm contains mitochondria and sarcoplasmic reticulum, and is full of parallel myofibrils [1]; a surface membrane (sarcolemma) surrounds the fibre and the myofibrils give cross-striations in a longitudinal section [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Gross" describes the whole organ and "microscopic" describes the cells. Give features at the right scale or the points are lost.

**3. (a)** Relaxed: 60.0 ÷ 24 000 = 0.0025 mm = **2.5 µm** [1]; contracted: 46.8 ÷ 24 000 = 0.00195 mm = **1.95 µm** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** (2.5 − 1.95) ÷ 2.5 × 100 = **22.0%** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** A band = 37.2 ÷ 24 000 = 1.55 µm; relaxed I band = 2.5 − 1.55 = **0.95 µm** [1]; contracted I band = 1.95 − 1.55 = **0.40 µm** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the unit conversion and give the answer in the unit asked for.

**4. (a)** **W, Y, X, Z** [1] **[1]**
**(b)** I band = 2.15 − 1.55 = **0.60 µm** [1]; H zone = 2.15 − 2 × 0.95 = **0.25 µm** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** The A band is the same in every image [1], so the thick filaments do not change length [1]; the I band and H zone shrink as the sarcomere shortens (H zone is 0.60 µm in X but 0 in W), so the thin filaments are moving between the thick ones rather than getting shorter [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote values from the data when you explain, and say which band stays constant.

**5.** Thin filaments are drawn inwards between the thick filaments, so the Z lines approach each other and the sarcomere shortens [1]; calcium ions released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum move tropomyosin off the binding sites on actin [1]; myosin heads attach to those sites, forming actomyosin bridges [1]; the heads change shape and drag the actin filaments towards the middle of the sarcomere [1]; ATP binds to the heads so they detach [1]; ATP is hydrolysed and the energy returns the heads to their original position, so the cycle repeats [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Follow a sequence and say what each molecule does. A list of the four names, with no roles, shows little.

**6.** The fibre stays contracted, rigid and unable to relax [1]; calcium ions keep binding sites exposed so myosin heads bind and tilt [1]; without ATP the heads cannot detach [1]; so the bridges stay attached and the fibre cannot lengthen or cycle further [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Predict first, then explain. "No contraction" is a poor prediction, since binding does not need ATP.

**7. (a)** Tension rises as calcium ion concentration rises [1]; the rise is steepest between about 0.2 and 1.0 µmol dm⁻³ and levels off near 100% above 2.0 [1] **[2]**
**(b)** (72 − 40) ÷ (1.0 − 0.5) = **64% per µmol dm⁻³** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** At 0.05 µmol dm⁻³ tropomyosin still covers the binding sites, so no bridges form and tension is zero [1]; at 5.0 µmol dm⁻³ calcium ions have moved tropomyosin and exposed binding sites [1]; so many myosin heads bind and tension is at its maximum [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Describe" needs the pattern with values, including where it levels off. Explanations should name tropomyosin and the exposed binding sites.

**8. (a)** 7.2 ÷ 2.4 = **3.0 s** [1] **[1]**
**(b)** (7.2 + 21.6) = 28.8 mmol per kg [1]; 28.8 ÷ 2.4 = **12.0 s** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** 21.6 ÷ 28.8 × 100 = **75.0%** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the total before dividing. The assumption of one ATP per phosphocreatine is what allows the two stores to be added.

**9. (a)** (100 − 45) ÷ 4 = **13.75%, about 13.8% per s** [1] **[1]**
**(b)** Phosphocreatine passes its phosphate to ADP, regenerating ATP as fast as it is used [1]; so the ATP concentration stays nearly constant while phosphocreatine falls [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Less phosphocreatine is left to use [1]; some ATP is now being supplied by glycolysis and respiration (so less is needed from phosphocreatine) [1] **[2]**
**(d)** During rest, ATP from aerobic respiration [1] is used to add phosphate to creatine, rebuilding phosphocreatine [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain the small fall in ATP by regeneration, not by saying the muscle is not using it. In recovery, state the source of the ATP.

**10. (a)** J is the slow fibre: it has a higher proportion of mitochondria (7.5% against 2.1%) [1]; it has a longer time to peak force and force falls much more slowly, so it resists fatigue [1] **[2]**
**(b)** 7.5 ÷ 2.1 = **3.6** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** Postural muscles must contract for long periods [1]; many mitochondria allow sustained aerobic respiration [1]; slower fatigue, with a rich oxygen supply from myoglobin and capillaries, means the muscle keeps holding posture without tiring [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each property to function. Naming "many mitochondria" earns little unless you say what it allows.

**11.** The biceps contracts and the triceps relaxes [1]; in the biceps, myosin heads form bridges and pull thin filaments inwards [1]; the sarcomere, I band and H zone get shorter while the A band stays the same [1]; the triceps is stretched by the contracting biceps, with no bridges holding the filaments, so its thin filaments are pulled back out [1]; its sarcomeres get longer, with wider I bands and H zones [1]; the tendons pass the pull to the forearm, a lever on the incompressible skeleton [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Cover both muscles. Answers about the biceps alone leave half of the question unanswered.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating that the filaments, A band or myosin get shorter in contraction.
- Leaving out calcium ions or tropomyosin when explaining how binding sites become available.
- Describing ATP as "energy for the muscle" with no mention of detachment or resetting of the head.
- Forgetting that removal of calcium ions, and ATP for the pumps, is needed for relaxation.
- Dividing by magnification without converting mm to µm.
- Mixing up the I band and H zone when calculating from sarcomere length.
- Treating phosphocreatine as a long-term store.
- Explaining only one muscle of an antagonistic pair.
- Giving fibre properties without naming the fibre type.
