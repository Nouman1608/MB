These questions cover section 1.2, Types of organisations, of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1) specification, Issue 2, February 2026. Use the [types of organisations study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-types-of-organisations/) and the [types of organisations revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-types-of-organisations-revision-notes/) first if you need them. Every business is invented and amounts are in dollars. Show your workings and give percentages to 1 decimal place.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.**
**(a)** Define public corporation. **[1]**
**(b)** Define franchisee. **[1]**

**2.** Give two sources of finance available to a sole trader. **[2]**

**3.** Priya and Leon each put 15,000 into a new events business and agreed to share profits equally. Neither sold shares. State the type of business ownership. **[1]**

**4.** A partnership made a profit of 54,000. The two partners share profit in the ratio 2 : 1. Calculate each partner's share. **[2]**

**5.** A family owns 70,000 of the 120,000 shares in a private limited company. Calculate the family's percentage holding. **[2]**

**6.** Jonah runs a sandwich shop as a franchisee of a national brand. Outline one drawback to Jonah of operating as a franchisee. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Explain one benefit of limited liability to the owners of a business. **[3]**

**8.** Brennan Boats Ltd has 200,000 shares. Its founder, Siobhan, holds 110,000. The company plans to issue 50,000 new shares to outside investors to pay for a larger boatyard.
**(a)** Calculate Siobhan's current percentage holding. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate her percentage holding after the issue. **[2]**
**(c)** Analyse one problem the share issue may cause for Siobhan. **[4]**

**9.** Zara wants to open a coffee shop. She has two options.
- Option 1, a franchise: start-up fee 20,000, then 7% of revenue each year. Expected revenue 240,000; other costs 170,000.
- Option 2, her own independent shop: no fees. Expected revenue 180,000; other costs 150,000.

**(a)** Calculate the franchise outlet's profit in the first year, including the start-up fee. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the independent shop's expected profit. **[1]**
**(c)** Justify which option Zara should choose. **[6]**

**10.** Westvale Water is a public corporation supplying water to two million people. Many of its pipes are old and leak. Its prices are among the lowest in the region. The government is considering selling it to a private company that has promised to invest in new pipes. Evaluate whether Westvale Water should remain in public ownership. **[8]**

**11.** Second Thread is a social enterprise that repairs and sells used clothing. It employs young people leaving care and trains them in sewing and retail. Last year it made a profit of 18,000, all put back into training. Analyse the benefits and limitations to Second Thread of operating as a social enterprise. **[6]**

**12.** Tom is a sole trader plumber. He is turning away work because he cannot do every job himself. Ella, a qualified heating engineer, has offered to join him as a partner and invest 20,000 in a van and tools. Tom values making his own decisions. Evaluate whether Tom should form a partnership with Ella. **[8]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A business owned by the state (government) on behalf of the public [1].
**(b)** A person or business that pays a franchisor to trade using its brand and methods [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two of: the owner's savings [1], a bank loan [1], retained profit, an overdraft, money from family and friends. **[2]**

**3.** Partnership [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Two owners sharing profits with no shares points to a partnership, not a company.

**4.** One share = 54,000 ÷ 3 = 18,000; first partner **36,000** [1]; second partner **18,000** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** 70,000 ÷ 120,000 × 100 [1] = **58.3%** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Jonah must pay continuing fees to the franchisor [1], so part of the shop's revenue goes to the brand owner and his profit is lower than if the shop were his own (or: he must follow the franchisor's rules, so he cannot choose his own menu or suppliers) [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Limited liability means owners can lose only the money they invested [1]. Their personal assets, such as their homes, cannot be used to pay the business's debts [1], so they are more willing to invest and take risks such as expanding [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Explain" is a statement plus two expansion points; no context is needed.

**8. (a)** 110,000 ÷ 200,000 × 100 = **55%** [1].
**(b)** New total 250,000 shares [1]; 110,000 ÷ 250,000 × 100 = **44%** [1].
**(c)** Siobhan falls below half of the shares [1], so outside investors together could outvote her on decisions such as who the directors are [1]. As the founder she may lose control of the boatyard's direction, for example if investors want faster growth or higher dividends than she does [1], which could lead to conflict or even her removal as a director [1]. **[7]**

**9. (a)** Fee 7% × 240,000 = 16,800 [1]; profit 240,000 − 170,000 − 16,800 − 20,000 [1] = **33,200** [1].
**(b)** 180,000 − 150,000 = **30,000** [1].
**(c)** The franchise is the better choice [1]. Even after the start-up fee, first-year profit is 3,200 higher [1], and from the second year, with no start-up fee, it would be 240,000 − 170,000 − 16,800 = 53,200, far above 30,000 [1]. The known brand explains the higher expected revenue and lowers the risk of a new business [1]. The independent shop gives Zara full control and no fees [1], but its revenue forecast is lower and less certain because customers do not know it [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Either option can be justified if the case figures support it; Zara's wish for independence, if stated, could tip the decision the other way.

**10.** For remaining public: water is essential, so a public owner can keep prices low for two million people [1], and decisions can put public health before profit [1]. A private owner would need to earn a return, so prices may rise [1]. Against: the leaking pipes suggest too little investment under public ownership [1]; a private company has promised to invest, which could reduce waste of water [1], and it would face pressure from shareholders to cut costs and run efficiently [1]. Taxpayers would no longer fund the investment [1]. Judgement: for example, it should remain public only if the government can fund the new pipes, because affordable water is the priority; otherwise selling with rules on prices may serve customers better [1]. **[8]**

**11.** Benefits: its social purpose, training young people leaving care, can attract customers who want to support it [1], so sales may be higher than for an ordinary second-hand shop [1]. It may find it easier to gain goodwill and volunteers [1]. Limitations: all 18,000 profit goes into training, so little is kept to replace equipment or grow [1]. Trainees are still learning, so output may be slower and costs per item higher [1], which can make it hard to compete on price with larger clothing retailers [1]. **[6]**

**12.** For: Ella's 20,000 pays for a van and tools without a loan [1], and her heating skills let the business take jobs Tom now turns away [1], so revenue should rise [1]. Two people can cover holidays and illness [1]. Against: Tom would share decisions, which matters because he values making his own [1], and profit is shared, so his income rises only if revenue grows enough [1]. Both partners have unlimited liability, so Tom could be responsible for debts from Ella's decisions [1]. Judgement: for example, form the partnership with a written agreement on roles and profit share, because lost work is costing him income; but if control matters most to him, hiring an employee would keep decisions in his hands [1]. **[8]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing a public corporation with a public limited company.
- Forgetting the new total of shares when calculating a holding after an issue.
- Leaving the start-up fee out of a franchise's first-year profit.
- Recommending a form without using the owner's stated priority.
- Writing a one-sided "evaluate" answer.
