## Questions

**1.** State two functions of money and give an example of one of them. **[3]**

**2.** Explain two characteristics that make bank notes more suitable as money than cattle. **[4]**

**3.** A market in Country A has 9 goods and no money. Calculate the number of exchange ratios needed under barter and the number of prices needed if money is used. **[3]**

**4.** A household has gross income of 2,600 a month. Tax and other deductions are 450 and it spends 1,870. Calculate its disposable income, its saving and its saving ratio to 1 decimal place. **[4]**

**5.** The central bank of Country B acts as lender of last resort.

**(a)** State two other roles of a central bank. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the lender of last resort role is important to consumers. **[3]**

**6.** Explain two ways in which a commercial bank can help a small producer. **[4]**

**7.** A bank in Country C holds deposits of 800,000 and has lent 520,000 at 10% a year. It pays depositors 3% a year on all deposits.

**(a)** Calculate the interest the bank receives. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the interest the bank pays out. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the difference between the two. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why a commercial bank charges borrowers a higher rate than it pays savers. **[2]**

**8.** A household has an interest-only loan of 35,000. The rate of interest rises from 4% to 6% a year.

**(a)** Calculate the increase in the household's annual interest payment. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain two ways the rise may change the household's behaviour. **[4]**

**9.** The table shows monthly figures for two households in Country D.

| | Disposable income | Spending |
|---|---|---|
| Household H | 1,600 | 1,520 |
| Household J | 4,800 | 3,600 |

**(a)** Calculate the saving of each household. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate each household's saving ratio. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain two reasons why Household J saves a larger share of its income. **[4]**

**10.** A household in Country E has disposable income of 3,000 a month in year 1 and 3,150 in year 2. Its monthly spending is 2,670 in year 1 and 2,646 in year 2.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in disposable income and in spending, to 1 decimal place. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the saving ratio in each year. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain two reasons why the household may have spent less although its income rose. **[4]**

**11.** "A rise in the rate of interest will always reduce household spending." Evaluate this statement. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two functions [1] [1]: medium of exchange, measure of value, store of value or standard for deferred payment. One example of use, such as using notes to buy bread or stating a loan in money terms [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A list of two functions earns the function marks; the example is a separate point and has to describe money being used, not repeat the function's name.

**2.** First characteristic named, for example durability [1], with explanation: notes last through repeated use while cattle can fall ill and die [1]. Second characteristic named, for example divisibility or portability [1], with explanation: notes come in many values and are easy to carry, while a cow cannot be divided to give change [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming a characteristic is not enough for the explanation mark. Contrast notes with cattle in the same sentence.

**3.** Barter ratios = 9 x 8 / 2 [1] = **36** [1]. With money, one price per good = **9** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Working shown matters: a bare 36 is weaker evidence than the formula. Write the number of ratios and the number of prices clearly labelled.

**4.** Disposable income = 2,600 - 450 = **2,150** [1]. Saving = 2,150 - 1,870 = **280** [1]. Saving ratio = 280 / 2,150 x 100 [1] = **13.0%** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Using gross income of 2,600 as the base gives a different ratio; the disposable figure is the one to divide by.

**5. (a)** Any two [1] [1]: issues notes and coins; banker to the government; banker to commercial banks; sets monetary policy or interest rate; holds foreign currency reserves; supervises banks. **[2]**
**(b)** If a bank cannot meet withdrawals, the central bank lends to it [1]. Savers keep their money safe and are less likely to panic and withdraw all their deposits [1]. Consumers keep access to savings, payments and credit, so confidence in the banking system is maintained [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong answer links the role to the consumer's own position, savings and payments, rather than describing the banking system in general.

**6.** First way, for example a loan or overdraft [1], explained: it pays for equipment or stock before sales income arrives, so output can rise [1]. Second way, for example a business account and payment services [1], explained: it lets the firm receive payments and pay wages and suppliers safely [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each way needs a development. Naming "loans" and "overdrafts" counts as only one way if the explanation is the same.

**7. (a)** 520,000 x 0.10 = **52,000** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 800,000 x 0.03 = **24,000** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** 52,000 - 24,000 = **28,000** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** The bank needs a gap between the rates to cover its costs and make a profit [1]. It also has to charge more for lending because some borrowers may fail to repay [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (c) the gap is before running costs. Calling it profit is a common overstatement.

**8. (a)** Before: 35,000 x 0.04 = 1,400 [1]. After: 35,000 x 0.06 = 2,100, so increase = **700 a year** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Effect one: the household has less income left after repayments, so it cuts spending on other goods [1]. Reason: its repayments are now higher, 2,100 against 1,400 a year [1]. Effect two: saving now earns more, so it may save more [1]. Reason: the reward for not spending has risen, so saving is more attractive [1]. Other valid effects, such as borrowing less, can replace either. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say which way the behaviour moves. "Affects spending" is too vague to show understanding.

**9. (a)** H: 1,600 - 1,520 = **80** [1]. J: 4,800 - 3,600 = **1,200** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** H: 80 / 1,600 x 100 = **5%** [1]. J: 1,200 / 4,800 x 100 = **25%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Higher income leaves more after necessities, so a larger share can be saved [1] [1]. Possible second reason: J may have fewer dependants or commitments, or be saving for retirement, so it chooses to save more [1] [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Reasons that only restate the figures, such as "J has more money", gain less than reasons that explain why a larger share is saved.

**10. (a)** Income: (3,150 - 3,000) / 3,000 x 100 = **5.0%** [1]. Spending: (2,646 - 2,670) / 2,670 x 100 [1] = **-0.9%** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Year 1: (3,000 - 2,670) / 3,000 x 100 = **11.0%** [1]. Year 2: (3,150 - 2,646) / 3,150 x 100 = **16.0%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Lower confidence about jobs or future income [1], so the household chose to build up savings [1]. Higher interest rates on savings or loans [1], which made saving more attractive or borrowing dearer [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the sign of the change. A fall in spending written as a positive percentage loses the point.

**11.** Points for the statement: a higher rate raises the cost of borrowing and of existing variable loans [1]; households with loans have less left to spend [1]; saving becomes more rewarding, so some spending is replaced by saving [1]. Points against: the effect depends on the size of the rise and whether it is expected [1]; households with no debts may not change their spending [1]; high income households may be little affected [1]; confidence or income growth could outweigh the rate, so spending still rises [1]. Judgement: it is likely to reduce spending for borrowers, so "always" is too strong, with a reason supported by the points [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Words such as "always" invite a counter-argument. Credit goes to a conclusion that makes a judgement and explains what it depends on.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a function or characteristic without explaining what it means.
- Mixing up the functions of the central bank and of commercial banks.
- Explaining the importance of banks in general terms, without referring to the group in the question.
- Using gross income instead of disposable income when finding saving.
- Not showing working or the sign of a percentage change.
- Claiming that higher interest rates always cut spending, or that more income always means more saving.
- Giving two reasons that are really the same point reworded.
- Ending an evaluation without a judgement.
