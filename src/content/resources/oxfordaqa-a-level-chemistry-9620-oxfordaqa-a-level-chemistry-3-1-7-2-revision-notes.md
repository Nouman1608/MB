## Core recall

- Kc is deduced from the **balanced equation** for a reversible reaction.
- The square-bracket term [X] means the equilibrium concentration of X in **mol dm⁻³**.
- For aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD: **Kc = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ / ([A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ)**.
- **Homogeneous**: every species in the same phase (all gases, or all in one liquid mixture or solution).
- Kc is **not affected** by changes in concentration or by a catalyst.
- Only **temperature** changes Kc.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Equilibrium constant, Kc | The ratio of equilibrium concentrations, each raised to its coefficient, products over reactants |
| [X] | Concentration of X at equilibrium, mol dm⁻³ |
| Homogeneous system | All reactants and products in the same phase |
| Equilibrium table | Initial, change and equilibrium amounts, with the change set by the mole ratio |
| Position of equilibrium | The equilibrium composition; can move while Kc stays the same |

## Units in four steps

1. Write the expression.
2. Replace every concentration with mol dm⁻³.
3. Cancel.
4. Write the result; n = (powers on top) − (powers underneath); units = (mol dm⁻³)ⁿ.

| n | Units |
|---|---|
| +2 | mol² dm⁻⁶ |
| +1 | mol dm⁻³ |
| 0 | none |
| −1 | mol⁻¹ dm³ |
| −2 | mol⁻² dm⁶ |

## Calculation method

1. **Initial** moles of each species.
2. **Change**, from one known equilibrium amount and the **mole ratio**.
3. **Equilibrium** moles.
4. Divide by the **volume** to get concentrations (skip only when n = 0, because V cancels).
5. Substitute into Kc, calculate, give units.

**To find an unknown concentration from Kc:** rearrange the expression. If two species form 1 : 1 from one reactant, they have equal concentrations, which often leads to a square root.

**Reversed or scaled equations:** reversing an equation gives 1/Kc; halving all coefficients gives √Kc.

### Worked reminder

0.500 mol of A and 0.300 mol of B react in a 2.00 dm³ flask, A(g) + B(g) ⇌ 2C(g); 0.400 mol of C is present at equilibrium.

| | A | B | 2C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial / mol | 0.500 | 0.300 | 0 |
| Change / mol | −0.200 | −0.200 | +0.400 |
| Equilibrium / mol | 0.300 | 0.100 | 0.400 |

Kc = 0.400² / (0.300 × 0.100) = **5.33**, no units. The volume cancels here because n = 0; dividing each amount by 2.00 first gives the same answer.

## Temperature and Kc

| Forward ΔH | Raise temperature | Lower temperature |
|---|---|---|
| Negative (exothermic) | Kc decreases | Kc increases |
| Positive (endothermic) | Kc increases | Kc decreases |

**Deducing ΔH from data:** if Kc falls as T rises, the position moves left on heating, so the forward reaction is exothermic.

## Concentration, catalyst and Kc

- Adding a reactant makes the ratio of concentrations momentarily smaller than Kc; more product forms until the ratio equals Kc again.
- Removing a product does the same.
- A catalyst changes neither the position nor Kc; equilibrium is reached sooner.

## Exam traps

- Moles in place of concentrations when n ≠ 0.
- Initial amounts in place of equilibrium amounts.
- Forgetting the 2 in "2HI" in both the change row and the power.
- Reactants on top of the fraction.
- Missing or guessed units.
- Saying that Kc changes with concentration, pressure or a catalyst.
- Giving the change in Kc on heating without linking it to the sign of ΔH.
- Rounding intermediate concentrations too early; keep at least four significant figures until the end.

## Quick self-test

1. Write the expression for Kc for N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g).
2. Give the units of Kc for that reaction.
3. Give the units of Kc for 2NO(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g).
4. For 2NO₂(g) ⇌ N₂O₄(g), [NO₂] = 0.0400 mol dm⁻³ and [N₂O₄] = 0.0120 mol dm⁻³ at equilibrium. Calculate Kc.
5. For A ⇌ B, Kc = 20. What is Kc for B ⇌ A?
6. For N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂, Kc = 0.0050 mol dm⁻³. If [N₂O₄] = 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ at equilibrium, find [NO₂].
7. 0.50 mol of A and 0.50 mol of B react, A + B ⇌ 2C, and 0.60 mol of C is present at equilibrium. Calculate Kc.
8. Why can moles be used directly in question 7?
9. The forward reaction of an equilibrium is endothermic. What happens to Kc when the temperature is lowered?
10. Kc for a reaction rises from 0.12 to 0.85 when the temperature increases. Deduce the sign of ΔH.
11. More reactant is added to an equilibrium at constant temperature. What happens to Kc and to the position?
12. What is meant by "homogeneous" in the title of this specification point?

### Answers

1. Kc = [NH₃]² / ([N₂][H₂]³)
2. (mol dm⁻³)² / (mol dm⁻³)⁴ = **mol⁻² dm⁶**
3. (mol dm⁻³)² / (mol dm⁻³)³ = **mol⁻¹ dm³**
4. Kc = 0.0120 / 0.0400² = 0.0120 / 0.00160 = **7.50 mol⁻¹ dm³**
5. 1/20 = **0.050**
6. [NO₂]² = 0.0050 × 0.0200 = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴, so [NO₂] = **0.010 mol dm⁻³**
7. A = B = 0.20 mol; Kc = 0.60² / (0.20 × 0.20) = **9.0** (no units)
8. There are equal powers on top and underneath, so the volume cancels.
9. Kc **decreases** (the position moves in the exothermic direction, to the left).
10. Kc increases with temperature, so the position moves right on heating: ΔH is **positive** (endothermic).
11. Kc is **unchanged**; the position shifts **right** until the ratio returns to Kc.
12. All reactants and products are in the **same phase**.
