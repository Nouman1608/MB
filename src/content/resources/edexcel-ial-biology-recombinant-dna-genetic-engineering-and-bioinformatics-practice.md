## Questions

**1.** Describe the roles of a restriction endonuclease and of DNA ligase in making recombinant DNA. **[4]**

**2.** A linear DNA molecule of 7,400 base pairs (bp) is cut by one restriction endonuclease at two sites, 1,500 bp and 5,200 bp from one end.

**(a)** Calculate the sizes of the fragments. **[2]**
**(b)** State which fragment would travel furthest in gel electrophoresis, and why. **[2]**

**3.** A plasmid of 3,900 bp is cut once and a gene of 1,800 bp is ligated into it.

**(a)** Calculate the size of the recombinant plasmid. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how cutting the recombinant plasmid with the same enzyme could show that the gene is present. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why the human gene for a protein hormone is often obtained as cDNA before it is put into bacteria, and describe how the cDNA is made. **[4]**

**5.** Describe how recombinant DNA is inserted into:

**(a)** bacterial cells **[3]**
**(b)** plant cells **[2]**

**6.** Bacteria were mixed with 0.005 µg of plasmid DNA and allowed to recover in 500 µl of broth. A 50 µl sample was spread on agar containing an antibiotic, and 60 colonies grew. Calculate the transformation efficiency in colonies per µg of DNA. **[3]**

**7.** A plasmid carries genes for resistance to ampicillin and to kanamycin. A gene was inserted into the kanamycin-resistance gene. After transformation, cells were spread on agar with ampicillin and 212 colonies grew. These were copied by replica plating onto agar containing both antibiotics, and 47 colonies grew.

**(a)** Calculate the number of colonies with the recombinant plasmid. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate this as a percentage of the colonies on the ampicillin plate. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain the reasoning behind your answer to (a). **[2]**

**8.** A herd of genetically modified goats secretes a human protein in its milk at 1.5 g per dm³. The herd produces 400 dm³ of milk per day.

**(a)** Explain why the gene was joined to a promoter that is active only in mammary gland cells. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of protein available from 30 days of milk. **[2]**
**(c)** Give one advantage and one disadvantage of using goats rather than bacteria. **[2]**

**9.** A microarray compares gene activity in tumour tissue and healthy tissue. Tumour cDNA is labelled red and healthy cDNA is labelled green.

| Gene | Red intensity | Green intensity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5,600 | 700 |
| 2 | 900 | 880 |
| 3 | 150 | 1,200 |
| 4 | 2,400 | 1,200 |

**(a)** Describe how the labelled cDNA is prepared and how it identifies active genes on the array. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the red to green ratio for each gene and state what each shows. **[4]**

**10.** **(a)** State what is meant by bioinformatics. **[1]**
**(b)** Two aligned 20-base sequences of the same gene from different species are:

species P: ATGCCGTTAGGCATCCGATA
species Q: ATGCCCTTAGGAATCCGTTA

Calculate the percentage of bases that are identical. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest what a higher percentage identity suggests about two species, and give one other use of bioinformatics. **[2]**

**11.** A trial of a GM crop gave the following results.

| | Non-GM | GM |
|---|---|---|
| Yield in tonnes per hectare | 5.2 | 6.5 |
| Seed cost per hectare | 30 | 45 |
| Herbicide applications per season | 4 | 1 |

Evaluate the use of this crop, using the data and your own knowledge of risks and benefits. **[8]**

**12.** Compare antibiotic-resistance marker genes with a gene for green fluorescent protein as ways of identifying transformed bacteria. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Restriction endonuclease: cuts DNA at a specific recognition sequence [1], hydrolysing the sugar-phosphate backbone, often leaving sticky ends [1]. DNA ligase: forms phosphodiester bonds between the backbones [1], permanently joining the gene to the vector once sticky ends have paired [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the bond that ligase makes; "joins the pieces" is a description of the result, not of the enzyme's action.

**2. (a)** First fragment 1,500 bp [1]. Middle fragment 5,200 − 1,500 = 3,700 bp, and end fragment 7,400 − 5,200 = 2,200 bp [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The 1,500 bp fragment [1] because it is the smallest and moves most easily through the gel pores [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A linear molecule cut twice gives three fragments; stating only two sizes is a common slip, so check that your fragments add to the total.

**3. (a)** 3,900 + 1,800 = **5,700 bp** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** The enzyme should cut at the two ends of the insert, releasing it [1]; on a gel there should be a band at 1,800 bp and one at 3,900 bp [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the expected band sizes in a "show it is present" answer; "there will be two bands" alone gives no way to check.

**4.** The gene in genomic DNA contains introns [1]; bacteria cannot splice out introns, so the protein would not be made correctly [1]. mRNA for the protein is extracted from cells that make it [1], and reverse transcriptase copies it into DNA, then DNA polymerase makes the second strand [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Both halves of a "why and how" question need marks; a full method with no reason for using cDNA leaves two marks uncollected.

**5. (a)** Mix the bacteria with the plasmids in calcium chloride solution, chilled [1]. Apply a brief heat shock, about 42 °C [1], making the membrane permeable so the plasmids enter [1]. Electroporation is an alternative. **[3]**
**(b)** Put the gene into the Ti plasmid of *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* [1], which infects plant cells and transfers the DNA into the plant chromosomes [1]. A gene gun is an alternative. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Heat shock" alone does not explain anything; link it to membrane permeability.

**6.** Total colonies in 500 µl = 60 × (500 ÷ 50) = 600 [1]. Efficiency = 600 ÷ 0.005 [1] = **120,000 colonies per µg** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Scaling up from the sample plated to the whole volume is the step most often missed; state it clearly and include units.

**7. (a)** Colonies with a plasmid = 212 [1]. Recombinant = 212 − 47 = **165** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 165 ÷ 212 × 100 = **77.8%** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Growth on ampicillin shows the cell has the plasmid [1]. Failure to grow on kanamycin shows the kanamycin-resistance gene was disrupted by the insert, so these cells carry the gene [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain insertional inactivation in terms of the gene being broken; stating that colonies are "recombinant" repeats the question.

**8. (a)** The promoter switches on transcription only in mammary gland cells [1], so the protein is made in the milk, where it is easily collected, and not in other tissues [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Per day: 1.5 × 400 = 600 g [1]. Over 30 days: 600 × 30 = 18,000 g = **18.0 kg** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Advantage: large volumes of protein with complex folding made by an animal cell, or a continuous supply [1]. Disadvantage: slow to produce a new herd, expensive, or animal welfare concerns [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a comparative point against bacteria; comments on goats alone do not answer "rather than".

**9. (a)** Extract mRNA from each tissue and use reverse transcriptase to make cDNA, labelled with a fluorescent dye [1]. Apply to the array so that cDNA binds to complementary DNA probes [1]. Wash off unbound cDNA and scan; brighter fluorescence shows more mRNA, so a more active gene [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Gene 1: 5,600 ÷ 700 = 8.0, much more active in tumour [1]. Gene 2: 900 ÷ 880 = 1.02, about equal [1]. Gene 3: 150 ÷ 1,200 = 0.125, more active in healthy tissue [1]. Gene 4: 2,400 ÷ 1,200 = 2.0, twice as active in tumour [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Interpret each ratio in words as well as stating the number, and say which tissue is more active.

**10. (a)** The use of computers and software to store, analyse and compare biological data, such as DNA and protein sequences [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Count identical positions: 17 of 20 [1]; 17 ÷ 20 × 100 = **85%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Species are more closely related, with a more recent common ancestor [1]. Other use: finding genes in a genome, or linking variants with disease [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Align and count positions carefully; miscounting by one changes the percentage and the answer to the following part.

**11.** Benefits, any three with data where possible: yield rises (6.5 − 5.2) ÷ 5.2 × 100 = 25.0% [1]; herbicide applications fall by 75%, saving labour and cost [1]; less herbicide may mean less pollution [1]. Also relevant: weeds controlled without damaging the crop. Risks, any three: seed costs 50% more [1]; gene flow to wild relatives could create herbicide-resistant weeds [1]; reliance on one herbicide may select for resistant weeds [1]. Also relevant: effects on biodiversity, or concerns about patents. Judgement that weighs the data, such as "worthwhile if the extra yield pays for the dearer seed" [1], with a condition or limit such as monitoring for resistance or the trial being limited [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs a conclusion that weighs the data; lists of benefits and risks with no weighing do not evaluate.

**12.** Antibiotic resistance: only transformed cells grow on agar with the antibiotic, so they are selected directly [1]. Fluorescent protein: transformed cells glow under UV light, so they can be identified without being killed or treated [1]. Comparison: the antibiotic method adds resistance genes, which may spread, while fluorescent markers need UV equipment [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Compare" needs both methods in the same answer with a link between them; describing each method on its own, with no link between them, does not compare.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming the enzymes but not the bond or the specific sequence involved.
- Forgetting that a linear molecule cut twice gives three pieces.
- Stating a gene sequence is "copied" without naming reverse transcriptase.
- Calling the heat shock a way of killing or weakening bacteria.
- Not scaling colony counts from the volume plated to the whole volume.
- Leaving out units such as bp and µg.
- Quoting a microarray ratio without saying which sample is more active.
- Giving risks or benefits without a final judgement in an evaluation.
