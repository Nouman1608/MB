This guide covers subtopic **16.2 Asexual and sexual reproduction** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series. The syllabus asks you to describe asexual reproduction as a process resulting in the production of genetically identical offspring from one parent; to identify examples of asexual reproduction; to describe sexual reproduction as the fusion of haploid nuclei (fertilisation) to form a diploid zygote, producing genetically different offspring; and to discuss the advantages and disadvantages of asexual and sexual reproduction.

The [Development of organisms and continuity of life topic guide](/resources/o-level-biology-5090-development-of-organisms-and-continuity-of-life/) covers the whole topic. This page compares the two types of reproduction in depth, with worked examples of the "discuss" questions. Use the [16.2 revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-asexual-and-sexual-reproduction-revision-notes/) for recall and the [16.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-asexual-and-sexual-reproduction-practice/) to test yourself.

## Asexual reproduction

**Asexual reproduction is a process resulting in the production of genetically identical offspring from one parent.**

Three features define it.

1. **One parent** is needed. There are no gametes and no fertilisation.
2. The offspring are produced by **mitosis**, so they are genetically identical to the parent and to each other. Genetically identical organisms produced in this way are called **clones**.
3. Variation among the offspring is absent, unless a mutation happens by chance.

### Examples of asexual reproduction

| Organism | Method | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Bacteria | Binary fission | A bacterial cell copies its DNA and divides into two identical cells |
| Yeast | Budding | A small bud grows on the parent cell, receives a copy of the nucleus and breaks off |
| Strawberry plant | Runners | A stem grows along the ground, forms roots at intervals, and each new plant separates from the parent |
| Potato | Tubers | An underground storage organ grows buds that grow into new plants |
| Many plants | Cuttings and bulbs | New plants grow from a piece of the parent plant, for example a bulb in onion or daffodil |

The examples show that asexual reproduction is common in bacteria, fungi and plants, and it is also how many gardeners make new plants identical to a parent that has useful features.

## Sexual reproduction

**Sexual reproduction is the process involving the fusion of haploid nuclei (fertilisation) to form a diploid zygote and the production of genetically different offspring.**

Four features define it.

1. Two **gametes** are produced, usually by two parents, by meiosis.
2. Each gamete has a **haploid nucleus**.
3. At **fertilisation**, a male and a female gamete fuse. The nuclei join to form a **diploid zygote**.
4. The offspring are **genetically different** from each other and from both parents.

Examples include flowering plants, mammals, birds and fish. The details for plants and humans are in the next two subtopics.

### Why offspring are genetically different

There are several reasons, which are dealt with in the inheritance topic: meiosis shuffles the genes into gametes in a new combination, which gametes meet at fertilisation is random, and parents may have different alleles. For this subtopic it is enough to state that the offspring are genetically different, because they receive genes from two parents and these are combined in new ways.

## Comparing the two types

| Feature | Asexual reproduction | Sexual reproduction |
|---|---|---|
| Parents | One | Usually two |
| Gametes | None | Male and female gametes |
| Fertilisation | Not needed | Needed |
| Type of nuclear division | Mitosis | Meiosis (to make gametes) |
| Offspring | Genetically identical (clones) | Genetically different |
| Variation | None | Present |
| Speed | Usually fast | Usually slower |

## Advantages and disadvantages

The syllabus asks you to **discuss** both. A discussion gives points on both sides, and you should say something about the idea, not just list a word.

### Asexual reproduction

**Advantages**

- Only **one parent** is needed, so there is no need to find a mate.
- It is **fast**, and many offspring can be produced in a short time. A bacterium can divide about every 20 minutes in good conditions.
- Little energy is used in finding a mate or making gametes.
- The offspring have the **same useful features** as the parent, so a population well suited to the environment stays well suited.
- Organisms that cannot move, such as plants, can spread without needing pollinators.

**Disadvantages**

- There is **no variation** (the offspring are identical), so the whole population can be affected by the same disease or change in the environment.
- If conditions change, there are no individuals with different features that might survive, so adaptation by natural selection is slow.
- The offspring tend to grow close to the parent and compete with it for resources.

### Sexual reproduction

**Advantages**

- The offspring are **genetically different**, so a population shows variation.
- If the environment changes or a new disease appears, some individuals may be better adapted and survive. This increases the chance that the species will survive, and it provides the variation that natural selection acts on.
- Variation makes it possible to produce new varieties in selective breeding.

**Disadvantages**

- It needs **two parents** (or at least two gametes), so a mate must be found.
- It is **slower** and uses more energy, because gametes must be made and fertilisation must take place.
- Fewer offspring may be produced.
- Favourable features of the parents may not be passed on, because the combination of genes is different.

## Worked example 1: a discussion answer

*Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of a farmer growing potatoes from tubers rather than from seeds.*

- Tubers are produced by asexual reproduction, so the new plants are genetically identical to the parent. If the parent plant has a good yield and disease resistance, the offspring will have it as well. [Advantage]
- The plants grow quickly because the tuber already contains stored food. [Advantage]
- All the plants are genetically identical, so a disease that affects one will affect them all. [Disadvantage]
- There is no variation, so new varieties cannot arise this way. [Disadvantage]

A good answer takes both sides and relates each point to the situation.

## Worked example 2: how fast can bacteria multiply?

*A bacterium reproduces by binary fission every 20 minutes. Starting from one bacterium, how many are there after 3 hours?*

1. 3 hours = 180 minutes. Number of divisions = 180 divided by 20 = 9.
2. Number of bacteria = 2 to the power 9 = 512.

All 512 are genetically identical to the original unless a mutation occurs. This shows both the speed and the lack of variation of asexual reproduction.

## Worked example 3: identify the type

*For each description, decide whether the reproduction is asexual or sexual.*

- A new strawberry plant grows from a runner: **asexual** (one parent, identical offspring).
- A zygote forms when a pollen nucleus fuses with an egg nucleus: **sexual** (fusion of haploid nuclei).
- A yeast cell forms a bud: **asexual**.
- Two parent rabbits produce young with varied coat colours: **sexual** (genetically different offspring).

## Asexual reproduction in plants: how gardeners use it

Gardeners and farmers use asexual reproduction (vegetative propagation) to make exact copies of a plant with desirable features, such as large fruit or attractive flowers. A cutting is a piece of stem that is placed in soil or water, where it grows roots. Potato tubers, onion bulbs and strawberry runners all do the same job naturally. The new plants are clones of the parent. This is reliable, because the grower knows what the plants will be like, but it gives no new varieties.

By contrast, a plant breeder who wants a new variety uses sexual reproduction, crossing two plants and growing seeds. The seedlings vary, and the breeder chooses the ones that show the best features. Then the breeder can use asexual methods to produce many copies of the chosen plant. This shows how the two types of reproduction work together in agriculture.

## Reproduction and survival

The balance between the two types is linked to the environment. A species living in a stable environment where conditions rarely change may do well by reproducing asexually, because the parent is already well adapted. A species living in a changing environment, or one facing diseases that evolve, gains from the variation produced by sexual reproduction. Many organisms use both: a strawberry plant spreads by runners in a good season, and flowers to make seeds that give variation.

## Common mistakes

- Writing that asexual offspring are "similar". They are genetically identical.
- Saying that asexual reproduction involves gametes.
- Writing that sexual reproduction involves one parent only.
- Forgetting that the zygote is diploid and the gametes are haploid.
- Giving only advantages when asked to discuss. Include disadvantages too.
- Writing that variation is a disadvantage of sexual reproduction. It is an advantage in a changing environment.
- Writing that asexual reproduction uses meiosis. It uses mitosis.
- Using "cloning" without explaining that the offspring are genetically identical.

## Command words

- **Describe**: give the definition and features.
- **Identify**: pick out examples of asexual reproduction.
- **Discuss**: give advantages and disadvantages, with explanation.
- **Compare**: give similarities and differences.
