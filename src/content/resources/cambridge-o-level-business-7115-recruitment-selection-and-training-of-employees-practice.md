These questions cover subtopic **2.3 Recruitment, selection and training of employees** of Cambridge O Level Business Studies 7115 (2026 series). All businesses, people and figures are invented. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question.

Related: the [Recruitment, selection and training study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-recruitment-selection-and-training-of-employees/) and the [Recruitment, selection and training revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-recruitment-selection-and-training-of-employees-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Explain the difference between recruitment and selection. **[2]**

**2.** Identify **two** methods of external recruitment. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** differences between a job description and a person specification. **[2]**

**4.** Identify **two** benefits of training to employees. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** difference between dismissal and redundancy, using an example. **[2]**

**6.** A business sends 6 employees on a two-day off-the-job course costing $450 per person. Each employee's lost output is worth $120 a day. Calculate the total cost of the training. **[3]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** Saltmarsh Garden Centre is busy from March to June and quiet for the rest of the year. It is deciding whether to employ extra part-time staff for the spring.

**(a)** Explain **two** benefits to Saltmarsh of employing part-time staff.

**(b)** Explain **one** limitation of employing part-time staff. **[6]**

**8.** Harbour Kitchen, a busy restaurant, needs a new head chef. The person specification says: at least 5 years' experience in a professional kitchen (essential); experience of managing kitchen staff (essential); ability to create new dishes (desirable).

| | Candidate X | Candidate Y |
|---|---|---|
| Kitchen experience | 9 years | 6 years |
| Managing staff | None; has always worked alone in a small café | Deputy head chef, managing 5 cooks |
| New dishes | Won a regional cooking award for new recipes | Has added dishes to the current menu |
| Internal or external | External | Internal (current deputy at Harbour Kitchen) |

Recommend which candidate Harbour Kitchen should appoint. Justify your answer. **[6]**

**9.** Clearline is opening a call centre and has recruited 40 new employees.

**(a)** Explain why induction training is important for Clearline.

**(b)** Clearline must teach the new employees to use its customer database. Consider whether on-the-job or off-the-job training would be more suitable. Justify your answer. **[8]**

**10.** Ferncliff Packaging is reducing its number of employees.

**(a)** Identify **two** situations that might make it necessary for Ferncliff to reduce its workforce.

**(b)** Explain **two** reasons why Ferncliff should use clear criteria when choosing which employees to make redundant. **[6]**

**11.** Ridgeway Builders is a construction company.

**(a)** Explain how laws against discrimination affect the way Ridgeway recruits employees.

**(b)** Explain **two** impacts of health and safety laws on Ridgeway. **[6]**

**12.** A cleaning company employs 25 workers, each working 35 hours a week at $6.50 an hour. The legal minimum wage rises to $7.00 an hour.

**(a)** Calculate the increase in the company's weekly wage bill.

**(b)** Calculate the increase over a 52-week year.

**(c)** Explain **one** way the company might respond to the increase. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Recruitment is attracting suitable people to apply for a job [1]; selection is choosing the most suitable person from those who applied [1].

**2.** Any two [1] [1]: newspaper advert; job website; recruitment agency; job centre; specialist magazine; social media advert.

**3.** Any two [1] [1]: a job description describes the job, a person specification describes the person needed; a job description lists duties, hours and pay, a person specification lists qualifications, experience and qualities; a person specification is used to shortlist applicants.

**4.** Any two [1] [1]: new skills; greater confidence; better chance of promotion; possibly higher pay; more job security.

**5.** Dismissal is because of the employee's conduct or ability, such as stealing stock [1]; redundancy is because the job is no longer needed, such as when a branch closes [1].

**6.** Cost per person = 450 + (2 × 120) = $690 [1] [1]. Total = 6 × 690 = **$4,140** [1].

**7.** **(a)** Two benefits, each identified [1] and explained [1]: wages are paid only for the hours needed in the busy season, keeping costs down in quiet months; part-time staff can be scheduled at the busiest times, such as weekends, improving customer service; part-time work attracts students and parents, giving a wider pool of applicants.

**(b)** One limitation identified [1] and explained [1]: more people must be recruited and trained, which costs time and money; or part-time staff may have less product knowledge and give poorer advice to customers; or communication is harder across many short shifts.

**8.** Candidate Y meets both essential criteria: 6 years' experience and managing 5 cooks [1]. Candidate X has more experience and a strong record of new dishes, which meets the desirable criterion [1], but has no experience of managing staff, which is essential for a head chef in a busy kitchen [1]. Y is internal, so already knows the kitchen, menu and staff and needs little induction [1], and promoting Y may motivate other staff [1]. Recommendation: appoint Y, because Y meets every essential criterion; the risk of fewer new dishes is smaller than the risk of a head chef who cannot manage a team, and Y's deputy post must be filled [1].

**9.** **(a)** New employees learn the rules, layout, safety procedures and their duties [1], so they settle quickly, make fewer early mistakes and become productive sooner [1].

**(b)** On-the-job: learning on the real system beside an experienced colleague is cheap and directly relevant [1], but Clearline is new, so there may be few experienced staff to train 40 people, and mistakes with real customer records could upset customers [1]. Off-the-job: a trainer can teach all 40 together on a practice version of the database, with no risk to real customer data [1], but it costs more and delays opening [1]. Judgement: off-the-job, because there are many trainees and few experienced staff, and errors with customer data are costly; on-the-job support can follow once the centre opens [1] [1].

**10.** **(a)** Any two [1] [1]: automation replaces workers; demand for products falls; a factory or branch closes; costs must be cut to survive.

**(b)** Two reasons, each identified [1] and explained [1]: clear, job-related criteria reduce the risk of claims of unfair treatment or discrimination; Ferncliff keeps the employees with the skills it needs in future; remaining staff are more likely to accept the decision as fair, protecting morale.

**11.** **(a)** Ridgeway must select applicants only on job-related criteria such as skills and experience [1], so adverts, person specifications and interviews must not favour or exclude people because of characteristics such as gender or religion [1].

**(b)** Two impacts, each identified [1] and explained [1]: Ridgeway must buy safety equipment such as helmets and harnesses, which raises costs; workers must be trained in safe working, taking time away from building; site inspections and safety records take management time; fewer accidents reduce absence, delays and compensation claims.

**12.** **(a)** Increase per hour = 7.00 − 6.50 = $0.50 [1]. Weekly increase = 25 × 35 × 0.50 = **$437.50** [1].

**(b)** 52 × 437.50 = **$22,750** [1].

**(c)** One response identified [1] and explained [1]: raise prices to customers, which may lose some customers if rivals charge less; or reduce workers' hours, which may lower service quality; or accept lower profit; or invest in equipment so fewer hours are needed.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using recruitment and selection as if they mean the same thing.
- Choosing the most experienced candidate in question 8 without checking the essential criteria.
- Forgetting lost output, or the second day, in question 6.
- One-sided answers in question 9(b) with no judgement.
- Describing a named country's law instead of the general impact in question 11.
