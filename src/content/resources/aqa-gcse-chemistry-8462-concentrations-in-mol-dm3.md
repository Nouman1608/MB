This guide covers **4.3.4 Using concentrations of solutions in mol/dm³ (chemistry only) (HT only)** of the AQA
GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification, for teaching from September 2016 onwards (version 1.2). The whole
subsection is marked (HT only), so it is **Higher Tier** content on Paper 1. The "chemistry only" label
separates it from combined science; in this qualification it is examined in full. The specification links it to
titrations, but there is no separate required practical named in this subsection.

## The key relationship

The concentration in **mol/dm³** is the number of moles of solute in each cubic decimetre (1 dm³ = 1000 cm³) of
solution.

```
concentration (mol/dm3) = amount of solute (mol) / volume (dm3)
amount (mol) = concentration (mol/dm3) x volume (dm3)
volume (dm3) = volume (cm3) / 1000
```

Rearranged: **moles = concentration × volume** and **volume = moles ÷ concentration**. Always convert cm³ to
dm³ first.

### Worked example 1: concentration from moles

*0.050 mol of solute is dissolved to make 250 cm³ of solution. Calculate the concentration in mol/dm³.*

```
volume = 250 / 1000 = 0.250 dm3
concentration = 0.050 / 0.250 = 0.20 mol/dm3
```

### Worked example 2: moles and mass from concentration

*Calculate the amount and the mass of sodium hydroxide (NaOH) in 25.0 cm³ of a solution of concentration
0.10 mol/dm³. (Mr of NaOH = 40.)*

```
volume = 25.0 / 1000 = 0.0250 dm3
moles = 0.10 x 0.0250 = 0.0025 mol
mass = moles x Mr = 0.0025 x 40 = 0.10 g
```

## Converting between g/dm³ and mol/dm³

Mass concentration (g/dm³) and amount concentration (mol/dm³) are linked by the relative formula mass, since the
mass of one mole in grams equals Mr.

```
concentration (g/dm3) = concentration (mol/dm3) x Mr
concentration (mol/dm3) = concentration (g/dm3) / Mr
```

### Worked example 3: from mass to mol/dm³

*4.0 g of sodium hydroxide is dissolved in water and made up to 500 cm³. Calculate the concentration in g/dm³
and in mol/dm³.*

```
volume = 0.500 dm3
g/dm3: 4.0 / 0.500 = 8.0 g/dm3
mol/dm3: 8.0 / 40 = 0.20 mol/dm3
```

### Worked example 4: from mol/dm³ to g/dm³

*A solution of hydrochloric acid has a concentration of 0.50 mol/dm³. Calculate the concentration in g/dm³.
(Mr of HCl = 36.5.)*

```
0.50 x 36.5 = 18.25 g/dm3 = 18 g/dm3 (2 s.f.)
```

### Worked example 5: making a solution

*Calculate the mass of anhydrous sodium carbonate (Mr = 106) needed to make 250 cm³ of a 0.100 mol/dm³
solution.*

```
moles = 0.100 x 0.250 = 0.0250 mol
mass = 0.0250 x 106 = 2.65 g
```

## Titration calculations

In a **titration**, a measured volume of one solution is reacted with just enough of a second solution to
neutralise it completely. If you know the two volumes and the concentration of one solution, you can find the
concentration of the other.

### Method

1. Write the balanced equation and note the mole ratio.
2. Work out the moles of the solution whose concentration you know (moles = concentration × volume in dm³).
3. Use the ratio to find the moles of the other reactant.
4. Divide by its volume (in dm³) to find its concentration. If asked, convert to g/dm³ using Mr.

### Worked example 6: 1 : 1 ratio

*25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution is neutralised by 20.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid.
NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O. Calculate the concentration of the sodium hydroxide.*

```
moles HCl = 0.100 x 0.0200 = 0.00200 mol
Ratio NaOH : HCl = 1 : 1, so moles NaOH = 0.00200 mol
concentration NaOH = 0.00200 / 0.0250 = 0.0800 mol/dm3
```

### Worked example 7: 2 : 1 ratio

*25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide is neutralised by 12.5 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ sulfuric acid.
H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O. Calculate the concentration of the sodium hydroxide in mol/dm³ and g/dm³.*

```
moles H2SO4 = 0.200 x 0.0125 = 0.00250 mol
Ratio H2SO4 : NaOH = 1 : 2, so moles NaOH = 2 x 0.00250 = 0.00500 mol
concentration NaOH = 0.00500 / 0.0250 = 0.200 mol/dm3
mass concentration = 0.200 x 40 = 8.0 g/dm3
```

### Worked example 8: finding a volume

*What volume of 0.200 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid reacts completely with 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ sodium
carbonate? Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂*

```
moles Na2CO3 = 0.100 x 0.0250 = 0.00250 mol
Ratio Na2CO3 : HCl = 1 : 2, so moles HCl = 0.00500 mol
volume = 0.00500 / 0.200 = 0.0250 dm3 = 25.0 cm3
```

## Explaining how concentration, mass and volume are related

For a given substance, the concentration in mol/dm³ depends on both the **mass of solute** and the **volume of
solution**. Increasing the mass in the same volume raises the concentration in proportion. Increasing the volume
with the same mass lowers it in inverse proportion. A good explanation uses the chain: mass → moles (divide by Mr)
→ concentration (divide by volume in dm³).

*Example:* 5.85 g of sodium chloride (Mr 58.5) in 500 cm³ gives 0.100 mol in 0.500 dm³ = 0.200 mol/dm³. The same
mass in 250 cm³ gives 0.400 mol/dm³.

## Preparing a standard solution

To make a solution of known concentration in mol/dm³, you calculate the mass of solute needed and then dissolve
it carefully.

1. Calculate the moles required: concentration × volume (dm³).
2. Calculate the mass: moles × Mr.
3. Weigh the solid accurately on a balance.
4. Dissolve it in a little water in a beaker, stirring.
5. Transfer the solution to a volumetric flask, rinsing the beaker so no solute is lost.
6. Add water up to the mark on the neck of the flask, then mix by inverting.

### Worked example 9: a dilution (HT only)

*25.0 cm³ of a 0.400 mol/dm³ solution is diluted to 100 cm³ with water. Calculate the new concentration.*

```
moles in 25.0 cm3 = 0.400 x 0.0250 = 0.0100 mol
The moles do not change on dilution.
new concentration = 0.0100 / 0.100 = 0.100 mol/dm3
```

The concentration has fallen by a factor of 4, because the volume has increased by a factor of 4.

## Mean titres and accuracy

In a titration, the volume of the second solution is read from a burette. You repeat the titration until you have
at least two concordant results (results that agree closely). The mean is calculated using those results only.
For example, concordant titres of 18.60, 18.55 and 18.65 cm³ give a mean of 18.60 cm³. Use the mean in the
calculation. Using a pipette to measure the volume of the first solution, and a burette for the second, gives
accurate volumes.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting to divide the cm³ by 1000.
- Using the mole ratio the wrong way round in a titration calculation.
- Using the volume of the wrong solution at the final step.
- Mixing g/dm³ with mol/dm³ without using Mr.
- Writing moles in the answer when the question asks for concentration.
- Rounding too early. Carry extra digits and round at the end.

## Exam technique

- **Show each step:** moles of known, ratio, moles of unknown, concentration.
- **Use units at every line:** it exposes the cm³ and dm³ mistakes.
- **Check by estimating:** a 1 : 1 reaction with equal volumes means equal concentrations.
- **Significant figures:** give the answer to the same number of significant figures as the data.
