## 1. Direct and indirect costs

A **direct cost** can be traced to the goods being made. An **indirect cost** is needed to run the factory but cannot be traced to one unit of output. Costs of the office and of selling are not factory costs at all.

Tarnley Canoes makes plastic canoes. Here is how its costs are classified.

| Cost | Class | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Polyethylene sheet | Direct (material) | Becomes part of every canoe |
| Wages of the moulding team | Direct (labour) | Paid to the people who make the canoes |
| Wages of the factory supervisor | Indirect (factory overhead) | Helps all production, traced to no single canoe |
| Factory power | Indirect (factory overhead) | Runs the machines for all output |
| Depreciation of moulding machine | Indirect (factory overhead) | A cost of owning the factory equipment |
| Salary of the sales manager | Not a factory cost | Goes in the statement of profit or loss |

## 2. Direct material, direct labour, prime cost and factory overheads

**Direct material** is shown as the cost of raw materials consumed, not as purchases, because some materials are in inventory at each end of the year.

**Cost of raw materials consumed = opening inventory of raw materials + purchases + carriage inwards - returns outwards - closing inventory of raw materials**

**Direct labour** is the wages of the production workers.

**Prime cost = direct material + direct labour**

**Factory overheads** are all the indirect factory costs: indirect wages, factory rent, power, insurance and depreciation of factory machinery. Add them to prime cost.

## 3. Work in progress and factory cost of production

**Work in progress** (WIP) is part-finished goods still in the factory. The opening WIP was paid for last year but is finished this year, so you add it. The closing WIP has been paid for this year but is not finished, so you deduct it.

**Factory cost of production = prime cost + factory overheads + opening WIP - closing WIP**

Effect of a change: if closing WIP is 1,000 higher, factory cost of production is 1,000 lower, cost of sales is 1,000 lower and gross profit is 1,000 higher. An understated closing WIP does the reverse.

## 4. How the statements link

- The manufacturing account ends with factory cost of production.
- In the statement of profit or loss, a manufacturer's cost of sales is opening inventory of finished goods + factory cost of production - closing inventory of finished goods. A trader would use purchases here.
- A business that both makes goods and buys finished goods to resell adds purchases of finished goods to that cost of sales.
- Office, selling and distribution expenses stay in the statement of profit or loss, below gross profit.
- The statement of financial position shows three inventories under current assets: raw materials, work in progress and finished goods.

## 6. Adjustments from section 5.1 in a manufacturing business

| Adjustment | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| Depreciation, straight line (cost - residual value) / years, reducing balance (a % of net book value), revaluation (opening value + additions - closing value) | Factory assets: manufacturing account, as a factory overhead. Office assets: statement of profit or loss |
| Accrued and prepaid factory expenses | Adjust the expense in the manufacturing account; accrual is a current liability, prepayment a current asset |
| Accrued and prepaid office expenses | Adjust the expense in the statement of profit or loss |
| Accrued and prepaid income | Adjust the income added after gross profit; accrued income is a current asset, income received in advance a current liability |
| Irrecoverable debts and allowance for irrecoverable debts | Statement of profit or loss; receivables shown net of the allowance |
| Goods taken by the owner | Deducted in cost of sales at the cost given; added to drawings |
| Owner's private expenses paid by the business | Removed from the expense; added to drawings |

If a question tells you to split one expense, such as rent, between the factory and the office, apply the given fraction and put each part in its own statement.

## Common errors

- Putting purchases of raw materials, not the cost of raw materials consumed, into prime cost.
- Adding closing WIP and deducting opening WIP.
- Treating the factory supervisor's wages as direct labour.
- Putting office salaries or carriage outwards into factory overheads.
- Forgetting that the factory machinery depreciation goes in the manufacturing account, not the statement of profit or loss.
- Using factory cost of production where prime cost is asked for, or the reverse.
- Showing one lump of inventory in the statement of financial position instead of raw materials, WIP and finished goods.
- Leaving the owner's goods or private expenses out of drawings.
