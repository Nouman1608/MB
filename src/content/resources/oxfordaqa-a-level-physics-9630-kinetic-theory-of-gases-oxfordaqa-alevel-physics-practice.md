These questions cover section 3.11.4, Kinetic theory of gases, of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), which is International A-level only content. Learn the content first with the [kinetic theory study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-kinetic-theory-of-gases-oxfordaqa-alevel-physics/) and the [kinetic theory revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-kinetic-theory-of-gases-oxfordaqa-alevel-physics-revision-notes/). Use k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹, R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ and N_A = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. Treat all gases as ideal.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Smoke particles in air are viewed through a microscope. Describe what is seen. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why the observation in Question 1 is evidence for the existence of molecules. **[2]**

**3.** State two assumptions of the kinetic theory model of an ideal gas. **[2]**

**4.** Four gas molecules have speeds of 300, 400, 500 and 600 m s⁻¹. Calculate their mean speed and their r.m.s. speed. **[2]**

**5.** Calculate the mean kinetic energy of a gas molecule at 27 °C. **[2]**

**6.** The gas laws are described as empirical, while pV = ⅓Nm(c_rms)² comes from a theoretical model. Explain the difference. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A fixed mass of gas is in a container.

**(a)** Explain how the molecules exert a pressure on the walls of the container. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain, in terms of molecules, why the pressure rises when the gas is heated at constant volume. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain, in terms of molecules, why the pressure rises when the gas is compressed at constant temperature. **[2]**

**8.** A container of volume 0.030 m³ holds 1.5 mol of helium at 320 K. The molar mass of helium is 4.0 × 10⁻³ kg mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the pressure of the gas. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mean kinetic energy of a helium atom. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the r.m.s. speed of the atoms. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the internal energy of the gas. **[2]**

**9.** **(a)** Starting from pV = ⅓Nm(c_rms)², show that p = ⅓ρ(c_rms)², where ρ is the density of the gas. **[2]**

**(b)** A sample of helium has a density of 0.18 kg m⁻³ at a pressure of 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. Calculate the r.m.s. speed of its atoms. **[1]**

**(c)** Use your answer to (b) to calculate the temperature of the helium. The molar mass of helium is 4.0 × 10⁻³ kg mol⁻¹. **[2]**

**10.** A container holds a mixture of hydrogen (M = 2.0 × 10⁻³ kg mol⁻¹) and oxygen (M = 3.2 × 10⁻² kg mol⁻¹) at 300 K.

**(a)** State the ratio of the mean kinetic energy of a hydrogen molecule to that of an oxygen molecule. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the ratio of the r.m.s. speed of hydrogen molecules to that of oxygen molecules. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the temperature at which oxygen molecules would have the same r.m.s. speed as hydrogen molecules at 300 K. **[2]**

**11.** An argon atom of mass 6.64 × 10⁻²⁶ kg moves at 400 m s⁻¹ back and forth between two opposite walls of a cubic box of side 0.20 m. It strikes the walls at right angles and collides with nothing else.

**(a)** Calculate the change in momentum of the atom each time it hits a wall. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the time between successive collisions with the same wall. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the mean force the atom exerts on that wall. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why a real gas in the box exerts a steady pressure even though each impact is a separate event. **[2]**

**12.** The derivation of pV = ⅓Nm(c_rms)² uses several assumptions.

**(a)** Explain how the assumption that collisions are elastic is used in the derivation. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the derivation needs the duration of each collision to be negligible. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how random motion of a large number of molecules leads to the factor ⅓. **[2]**

**(d)** Real gases at high pressure do not obey the equation. Suggest two reasons, linking each to an assumption. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Small bright specks (the smoke particles) [1] moving in a random, jerky, zig-zag way that never stops [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* This is a description; save the explanation for the next question.

**2.** The particles are hit by air molecules that are too small to see and are in fast, random motion [1]; at any instant more impacts occur on one side than the other, giving a changing net force, so the particle's motion is irregular [1] **[2]**

**3.** First correct assumption [1]; second correct assumption [1]. Accept any two of: very large number of identical molecules in random motion; perfectly elastic collisions; molecular volume negligible compared with the container; no forces between molecules except in collisions; collision time negligible compared with time between collisions; Newton's laws apply **[2]**

**4.** Mean speed = 1800/4 = 450 m s⁻¹ [1]; mean square = 2.15 × 10⁵ m² s⁻², so c_rms = 464 m s⁻¹ [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Square first, then average, then square-root. The r.m.s. speed is always at least the mean speed.

**5.** T = 300 K [1]; E = 3kT/2 = 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 300 = 6.2 × 10⁻²¹ J [1] **[2]**

**6.** Empirical laws come from experimental measurements and describe behaviour without explaining it [1]; the theoretical model starts from assumptions about molecules and Newton's laws and predicts the behaviour [1] **[2]**

**7. (a)** Molecules collide with the walls and rebound, so their momentum changes [1]; by Newton's second law each wall exerts a force on the molecule, and by Newton's third law the molecule exerts an equal and opposite force on the wall [1]; the very large number of collisions per second gives a steady force, and pressure is this force per unit area [1]

**(b)** The molecules gain kinetic energy and move faster [1], so each collision gives a larger momentum change and collisions happen more often, giving a larger force per unit area [1]

**(c)** The speeds of the molecules are unchanged because the temperature is constant [1]; in a smaller volume they strike the walls more frequently, so the force per unit area rises [1] **[7]**

*Examiner insight:* In (c), any statement that the molecules move faster contradicts the constant temperature and loses the first mark.

**8. (a)** p = nRT/V = 1.5 × 8.31 × 320 / 0.030 = 1.3 × 10⁵ Pa [1]

**(b)** E = 3kT/2 = 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 320 = 6.6 × 10⁻²¹ J [1]

**(c)** m = 4.0 × 10⁻³ / 6.02 × 10²³ = 6.64 × 10⁻²⁷ kg [1]; c_rms = √(2E/m) = √(2 × 6.62 × 10⁻²¹ / 6.64 × 10⁻²⁷) = 1.4 × 10³ m s⁻¹ [1]

**(d)** U = 3nRT/2 [1] = 1.5 × 1.5 × 8.31 × 320 = 6.0 × 10³ J [1] **[6]**

**9. (a)** Nm is the total mass of the gas [1]; dividing both sides by V gives p = ⅓(Nm/V)(c_rms)² = ⅓ρ(c_rms)² [1]

**(b)** c_rms = √(3p/ρ) = √(3 × 1.0 × 10⁵ / 0.18) = 1.3 × 10³ m s⁻¹ [1]

**(c)** ½m(c_rms)² = 3kT/2, so T = M(c_rms)²/(3R) [1] = 4.0 × 10⁻³ × (1291)² / (3 × 8.31) = 2.7 × 10² K [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* T = m(c_rms)²/(3k) with m = M/N_A gives the same result; either route earns the method mark.

**10. (a)** 1 : 1 [1]

**(b)** ½m(c_rms)² is the same for both, so c_rms ∝ 1/√M [1]; ratio = √(0.032/0.0020) = 4.0 [1]

**(c)** (c_rms)² ∝ T/M, so for oxygen T = 300 × 16 [1] = 4.8 × 10³ K [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Mean kinetic energy depends only on temperature; it does not matter that the molecules have different masses.

**11. (a)** Δp = 2mc = 2 × 6.64 × 10⁻²⁶ × 400 = 5.3 × 10⁻²³ kg m s⁻¹ [1]

**(b)** t = 2l/c = 0.40/400 = 1.0 × 10⁻³ s [1]

**(c)** F = Δp/t = 5.3 × 10⁻²³ / 1.0 × 10⁻³ = 5.3 × 10⁻²⁰ N [1]

**(d)** A real gas has an enormous number of molecules [1], so a vast number of impacts occur every second and their effects average to a steady force on each wall [1] **[5]**

**12. (a)** The molecule rebounds with the same speed, so its change in momentum at the wall is 2mc_x (and no kinetic energy is lost) [1]

**(b)** So that the time between collisions with a wall is simply the travel time 2l/c_x; the molecule is not held up at the wall [1]

**(c)** With random motion of many molecules, no direction is preferred, so the mean of c_x², c_y² and c_z² is equal [1]; since c² = c_x² + c_y² + c_z², the mean of c_x² is ⅓ of the mean of c² [1]

**(d)** The volume of the molecules is no longer negligible compared with the container [1]; the molecules are close enough for attractive forces between them to matter, so there are forces outside collisions [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Each reason must be tied to the assumption that fails, as the question asks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing Brownian motion when asked to explain it, or the reverse.
- Explaining Boyle's law with faster molecules.
- Using molar mass in place of molecular mass.
- Giving the mean speed as the r.m.s. speed.
- Saying heavier molecules have more kinetic energy at the same temperature.
- Stating assumptions loosely ("molecules have no volume").
