## Section A

**1.** Compare the motion of the particles in a transverse wave with the motion of the particles in a longitudinal wave. **[2]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by a compression and by a rarefaction in a longitudinal wave. **[2]**

**3.** State two properties that transverse and longitudinal waves have in common. **[2]**

**4.** State which type of graph you would use to determine **(a)** the wavelength and **(b)** the frequency of a wave. **[2]**

**5.** A microphone connected to an oscilloscope produces a sinusoidal trace when a note is played. A student concludes that sound must be a transverse wave. Explain why the conclusion is wrong. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A transverse wave travels to the right along a rope. The table gives the displacement y of points on the rope at distance x from one end, at one instant.

| x / m | 0 | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.30 | 0.40 | 0.50 | 0.60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y / mm | 0 | +5.0 | 0 | −5.0 | 0 | +5.0 | 0 |

**(a)** State the wavelength and the amplitude of the wave. **[2]**

**(b)** State the direction of motion, if any, of the particle at x = 0.10 m and of the particle at x = 0.20 m at this instant. **[2]**

**(c)** The frequency of the wave is 25 Hz. Calculate its speed. **[1]**

**(d)** Determine the phase difference between the oscillations of the particles at x = 0.10 m and x = 0.60 m. **[1]**

**7.** A sound wave travels to the right through air. The table gives the displacement s of air particles at distance x from the source at one instant. Positive displacement is to the right.

| x / cm | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 | 70 | 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| s / μm | 0 | +3.0 | 0 | −3.0 | 0 | +3.0 | 0 | −3.0 | 0 |

**(a)** State the wavelength. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify the positions of the centres of the compressions between x = 0 and x = 80 cm, and explain your answer. **[3]**

**(c)** State one position where the particle displacement is a maximum, and describe the spacing of the particles there compared with undisturbed air. **[1]**

**(d)** The speed of sound is 340 m s⁻¹. Calculate the frequency. **[1]**

**8.** A displacement–time graph for one particle in a sound wave shows a period of 5.0 ms and an amplitude of 0.20 μm. A displacement–distance graph for the same wave shows a wavelength of 1.7 m.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the speed of the wave. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the time for the wave to travel 50 m. **[1]**

**(d)** State the displacement of the particle 2.5 ms after it is at its maximum positive displacement. **[1]**

**9.** A transverse wave travels to the **left** along a string. At one instant, point P is at a crest, point Q is on the left-hand slope of the same crest, and point R is on the right-hand slope.

**(a)** State the direction of motion of P, Q and R at this instant. **[2]**

**(b)** State which of the three points is moving fastest, or explain if none of them can be identified as fastest from this information. **[1]**

**10.** At one instant, the pressure in a sound wave is a maximum at x = 0.40 m. The wavelength is 0.60 m.

**(a)** State the displacement of the air particle at x = 0.40 m. **[1]**

**(b)** State the positions of the two nearest rarefactions. **[2]**

**(c)** State one position, between the compression and a neighbouring rarefaction, where the particle displacement is a maximum. **[1]**

**11.** A transverse wave of frequency 2.0 Hz and wavelength 0.75 m travels along a rope. The amplitude is 4.0 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the total distance moved by one particle of the rope in 1.0 s. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the distance travelled by the wave in 1.0 s. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the answers to **(a)** and **(b)** are different. **[1]**

**12.** A longitudinal wave travels along a long spring at 1.8 m s⁻¹. The centres of adjacent compressions are 0.30 m apart.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency of the wave. **[1]**

**(b)** State the distance between the centre of a compression and the centre of the nearest rarefaction. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the time taken for a point on the spring to change from being at the centre of a compression to being at the centre of a rarefaction. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** In a transverse wave, particles oscillate **perpendicular** to the direction of energy transfer [1], whereas in a longitudinal wave they oscillate **parallel** to it [1].

**2.** A compression is a region where the particles are **closer together** than normal (higher pressure) [1]; a rarefaction is a region where they are **further apart** than normal (lower pressure) [1].

**3.** Any two [1] each: both transfer **energy without transferring matter**; both obey **v = fλ**; both can be reflected, refracted, diffracted or show interference.

**4. (a)** A **displacement–distance** graph [1]. **(b)** A **displacement–time** graph (giving the period, f = 1/T) [1].

**5.** The trace is a graph of the microphone's **output voltage against time** at one point [1]. It shows how the pressure there varies with time, not the direction of particle oscillation, which for sound is **parallel** to the direction of travel [1].

**6. (a)** λ = **0.40 m** [1]; amplitude = **5.0 mm** [1].

**(b)** At x = 0.10 m the particle is at a crest, so it is **momentarily at rest** [1]. At x = 0.20 m it is about to take the positive displacement of the particle behind it, so it is moving **upwards** (at maximum speed) [1].

**(c)** v = fλ = 25 × 0.40 = **10 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(d)** Separation 0.50 m = 1.25λ, so the phase difference is **90°** (π/2 rad) [1].

**7. (a)** λ = **40 cm** [1].

**(b)** Compressions at **x = 20 cm and x = 60 cm** [1]. At these points the displacement is zero [1], and particles on the left are displaced to the right while particles on the right are displaced to the left, so they crowd together [1].

**(c)** For example, x = 10 cm (or 30, 50 or 70 cm); the particle spacing there is **normal**, the same as in undisturbed air [1].

**(d)** f = v/λ = 340 ÷ 0.40 = **850 Hz** [1].

**8. (a)** f = 1 ÷ 5.0 × 10⁻³ = **200 Hz** [1].

**(b)** v = fλ = 200 × 1.7 = **340 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(c)** t = 50 ÷ 340 = **0.15 s** [1].

**(d)** 2.5 ms is half a period, so the displacement is the **maximum negative** value, −0.20 μm [1].

**9. (a)** P is at a crest, so **momentarily at rest**. For a wave moving left, Q is on the front slope, so it moves **upwards**; R is on the back slope, so it moves **downwards** [2] (one mark for P; one mark for both Q and R correct).

**(b)** None can be identified: the fastest particles are at **zero displacement**, and the exact displacements of Q and R are not given [1].

**10. (a)** **Zero**, because maximum pressure is at the centre of a compression [1].

**(b)** Rarefactions are λ/2 = 0.30 m from the compression [1], at **x = 0.10 m and x = 0.70 m** [1].

**(c)** A quarter-wavelength (0.15 m) from the compression: **x = 0.25 m or x = 0.55 m** [1].

**11. (a)** In one cycle a particle moves 4 × amplitude = 16 cm [1]; in 1.0 s there are 2.0 cycles, so it moves **32 cm** [1].

**(b)** v = fλ = 2.0 × 0.75 = 1.5 m s⁻¹, so the wave travels **1.5 m** [1].

**(c)** The particle only **oscillates about a fixed position**, while the wave energy moves along the rope [1].

**12. (a)** f = v/λ = 1.8 ÷ 0.30 = **6.0 Hz** [1].

**(b)** λ/2 = **0.15 m** [1].

**(c)** Half a period: T/2 = 1 ÷ (2 × 6.0) = **0.083 s** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Marking the compressions at the positive peaks of a longitudinal displacement graph (Question 7).
- Forgetting that a particle at a crest is momentarily stationary (Questions 6 and 9).
- Reversing particle directions when the wave travels to the left (Question 9).
- Reading a wavelength from a time axis (Question 8).
- Confusing the distance a particle moves with the distance the wave travels (Question 11).
- Describing wave types as "up and down" or "side to side" without reference to the direction of energy transfer.
