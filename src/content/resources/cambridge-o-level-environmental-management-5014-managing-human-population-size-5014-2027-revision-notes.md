> **Syllabus edition note.** These notes follow the Cambridge O Level Environmental Management 5014
> syllabus for exams in 2027, 2028 and 2029, sub-topic 7.3.

Sub-topic 7.3 has two outcomes: describe five strategies for managing human population size (7.3.1) and
discuss their benefits and limitations (7.3.2). Learn what each strategy does, which population factor it
changes, and at least one benefit and one limitation.

Full explanations: [managing human population size study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-5014-managing-human-population-size-5014-2027/).
Test yourself: [managing human population size practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-5014-managing-human-population-size-5014-2027-practice/).

## First, the aim

| Situation | Aim | Strategies most used |
|---|---|---|
| Rapid growth, young population (often an LIC) | Slow growth | Contraception, anti-natalist policy, education, healthcare |
| Low children per woman, ageing population (often an HIC) | Raise birth rate or add workers | Pro-natalist policy, encouraging immigration |

## The five strategies

| Strategy | What is done | Factor changed |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Access to contraception | Clinics, health workers, free or cheap supplies, information | Birth rate |
| (b) Population policies | Anti-natalist: incentives, campaigns, limits. Pro-natalist: payments, leave, childcare, housing help | Birth rate |
| (c) Access to education | Especially for girls and women | Birth rate (and child mortality) |
| (d) Access to healthcare | Clinics, vaccination, maternal care | Death rate first, birth rate later |
| (e) Control of migration | Visas, permits, quotas, border controls | Migration |

## Benefits and limitations

| Strategy | Benefit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Contraception | Choice; cheap; healthier spacing of births | Only works if smaller families are wanted; supply gaps; cultural objections |
| Anti-natalist (strict) | Fast fall in birth rate | Loss of freedom; gender imbalance; rapid ageing later |
| Pro-natalist | Slows ageing; supports families | Costly; small or temporary effect |
| Education | Lasting; many wider benefits | Slow; needs schools and teachers; girls may be kept out |
| Healthcare | Saves lives; lowers births over time | Growth rises first; costly in rural areas |
| Migration control | Fast way to add or limit workers | Hard to enforce; social tension; brain drain for sending countries |

**Example to quote**: China's one-child policy, a strict anti-natalist policy from about 1980 until 2015.

## The healthcare two-step

Better healthcare → death rates fall (especially child deaths) → growth **rises** at first → parents expect
children to survive → choose fewer children → birth rates fall → growth **slows**.

## Four tests for "discuss"

1. **Effectiveness**: does it change the factor intended?
2. **Speed**: months, years or a generation?
3. **Cost**: can the country afford it?
4. **Acceptability**: does it respect rights and choices?

Score each strategy against the tests, then judge which suits the country in the question.

**Judgement lines to reuse**
- "Voluntary strategies are slower but more acceptable and longer-lasting."
- "Strategies work best in combination: education increases demand for contraception, and healthcare
  makes smaller families a safe choice."
- "For an ageing HIC, immigration acts faster than pro-natalist policies."

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Population policy | A government plan to change population size or growth |
| Anti-natalist | Aiming to reduce the birth rate |
| Pro-natalist | Aiming to increase the birth rate |
| Contraception | Methods used to prevent pregnancy |
| Family planning | Choosing the number and timing of children |
| Child mortality | Deaths of children, usually under five |
| Replacement level | About 2.1 children per woman |
| Ageing population | Rising proportion of elderly people |
| Gender imbalance | Unequal numbers of males and females |
| Quota | A limit on numbers, for example of immigrants |
| Brain drain | Emigration of skilled and educated people |

## Exam traps

- Pro-natalist and anti-natalist mixed up ("pro" = for more births).
- Healthcare given as reducing population with no explanation of the two stages.
- Strict policies given only benefits.
- Migration control treated as one-directional.
- No judgement in a discuss answer.
- Growth calculations: 2% of 8 million is 160 000, not 1.6 million.

## Self-test

1. Name the five strategies in 7.3.1.
2. What is the difference between a pro-natalist and an anti-natalist policy?
3. Give two measures used in pro-natalist policies.
4. Explain how access to contraception reduces the birth rate.
5. Give two limitations of a strict limit on family size.
6. Why is education for girls linked to lower birth rates?
7. Explain why improved healthcare can at first increase population growth.
8. Give one benefit and one limitation of encouraging immigration to an ageing country.
9. Which strategy is usually fastest at changing a country's workforce?
10. What is meant by brain drain?
11. A population of 15 million grows at 3% a year. Calculate the increase in one year.
12. If the growth rate falls to 2%, how many fewer people are added in that year?

### Answers

1. Access to contraception; population policies (pro-natalist and anti-natalist); access to education;
   access to healthcare; control of migration.
2. Pro-natalist aims to increase the birth rate; anti-natalist aims to reduce it.
3. Any two: payments for each child; paid parental leave; subsidised childcare; tax relief; housing help.
4. Couples can choose the number and timing of children, so unplanned births fall.
5. Loss of personal freedom; gender imbalance; rapid ageing later.
6. Educated women tend to marry later, have more paid work and know more about family planning.
7. Death rates fall, especially among children, while birth rates stay high for a time.
8. Benefit: adds young workers quickly. Limitation: tension if services do not expand, or hard to manage.
9. Control of migration (encouraging immigration).
10. Emigration of skilled and educated people from a country.
11. 15 000 000 x 0.03 = 450 000.
12. At 2%: 300 000, so 150 000 fewer.
