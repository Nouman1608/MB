A **bank** is a business that keeps money safe, makes payments for its customers and lends money. Banks are an **aid to trade** because trade depends on safe, quick ways to pay and to borrow. Section 5.2 of the Cambridge IGCSE Commerce (0715) syllabus has two parts. Section 5.2.1 covers the **banking services** available to individuals and businesses, and **how to choose** between them. Section 5.2.2 covers **digital technology in banking**, including its advantages, disadvantages and impact. This guide covers both, with two worked examples and an annotated answer.

## What banking services does the syllabus list? (5.2.1)

The syllabus groups services into four types: accounts, customer services, payments and sources of finance.

### Accounts

| Account | Features | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Current account** | Money can be paid in and taken out at any time; usually has a debit card and cheque book; little or no interest | Everyday spending and receiving wages or sales income |
| **Deposit (savings) account** | Pays interest; may need notice or limit withdrawals | Money not needed soon |

### Customer services

- **ATM (automated teller machine):** cash withdrawal, balance checks and sometimes deposits, at any hour.
- **Night safe:** a secure chute at the bank where a business can deposit takings after the bank has closed.
- **Foreign currency:** exchange of one currency for another, important for importers, exporters and travellers.
- **Financial advice:** guidance on saving, loans, investment and planning.
- **Telephone banking:** managing an account by phone.

### Payment methods

| Method | How it works | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cash** | Notes and coins | Immediate and simple; risk of loss and theft; no record |
| **Cheque** | Written order to the bank to pay a sum from the account | Can bounce if the account has too little money; slow to clear |
| **Credit card** | Bank lends money to the buyer to pay later | Interest if the balance is not paid in full on time |
| **Debit card** | Payment is taken straight from the buyer's account | Spending limited to money in the account |
| **Store card** | Credit card issued by a shop for use in that shop | Often high interest; encourages loyalty |
| **Credit transfer** | Money moves from one account to another at the payer's instruction | Common for paying suppliers and wages |
| **Standing order** | Regular fixed payment set up by the account holder | Good for rent or fixed subscriptions |
| **Direct debit** | The receiver takes variable amounts from the account with permission | Good for bills that change, such as electricity |
| **Electronic transfer** | Funds sent between accounts by computer | Fast and cheap, widely used by businesses |
| **Documentary credit (letter of credit)** | Importer's bank promises to pay the exporter when documents show the goods were shipped | Gives security to both sides in international trade |

Standing orders and direct debits are often confused. In a **standing order** the **payer** instructs the bank to pay a **fixed** amount on set dates. In a **direct debit** the **receiver** collects an amount that **may change**, with the payer's permission.

### Sources of finance from banks

| Source | What it is | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Loan** | A fixed sum lent for a set period, repaid with interest | Buying equipment, starting a business |
| **Overdraft** | Permission to spend more than the balance, up to a limit | Short-term cash shortages |
| **Mortgage** | A long-term loan to buy property, secured on the property | Buying a home, shop or factory |
| **Hire purchase** | The buyer pays a deposit and then instalments, and owns the item after the last payment | Buying vehicles or machinery |

## How do you choose the right banking service in a given situation?

The syllabus asks for the **appropriateness** of services in given situations. Match the service to the need.

| Situation | Suitable service | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A shop owner wants to deposit takings late in the evening | Night safe | Safe, available after closing |
| A business pays the same rent every month | Standing order | Fixed amount, automatic |
| A business pays an electricity bill that changes each month | Direct debit | Amount varies; saves manual payment |
| A company buys goods from a supplier abroad it has not met | Documentary credit | The bank pays on proof of shipment, so both sides are protected |
| A bakery needs money for two weeks until a customer pays | Overdraft | Short-term, flexible |
| A firm wants to buy a lorry costing a large sum, repaid over time | Loan or hire purchase | Spread the cost |
| A family wants to buy a house | Mortgage | Long-term finance secured on property |
| A traveller needs money abroad | Foreign currency | Exchange rate service |

Think about **cost, speed, security, convenience and the amount involved**.

## What is digital banking? (5.2.2)

**Digital banking** means using electronic technology to carry out banking, usually without visiting a branch.

- **Internet banking:** managing accounts through a bank's website on a computer.
- **Mobile banking:** using an app on a phone or tablet, often with fingerprint or face recognition.

### Advantages and disadvantages

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week | Needs internet access and a device |
| Saves time: no travel or queues | Risk of fraud, phishing and hacking |
| Lower costs for the bank, so lower fees for customers | Customers who are not confident with technology may find it hard |
| Instant balance checks and transfers | Cannot deposit cash or get face-to-face advice easily |
| Easy to track spending and set alerts | System failures or power cuts stop access |

### Impact of digital banking

The syllabus names three effects.

1. **Closure of bank branches.** If fewer customers visit, banks close branches to save costs. This hurts people who rely on in-person service, especially in rural areas, and reduces jobs.
2. **More digital-only banks.** Some banks have no branches at all. They have lower costs and can offer better rates, but customers rely entirely on technology.
3. **Less use of cash (a cashless society).** Cards, phone payments and transfers replace notes and coins. This is faster and safer than carrying cash, but small traders may pay card fees, and people without bank accounts may be excluded.

## Worked example 1: the real cost of hire purchase (invented figures)

*A garage buys a car-lifting machine. The cash price is $2,400. On hire purchase, it pays a $400 deposit and 12 monthly instalments of $190.*

1. Instalments: 12 x 190 = **$2,280**.
2. Total paid: 400 + 2,280 = **$2,680**.
3. Extra cost compared with cash: 2,680 - 2,400 = **$280**.

**Judgement:** hire purchase costs $280 more, but the garage can start using the machine at once and spreads the payments. It is appropriate if the garage does not have $2,400 available, but cash is cheaper if the garage can afford it.

## Worked example 2: overdraft interest (invented figures)

*A florist has an overdraft of $1,500 for two months. The bank charges 12% a year.*

1. Interest for a year: 1,500 x 12 / 100 = $180.
2. Two months is 2/12 of a year: 180 x 2 / 12 = **$30**.

**Judgement:** a $30 charge for two months of flexibility is reasonable if the shop needs the money for stock. A loan would suit longer needs, because overdraft rates are usually higher.

## Annotated model answer

*Question: Explain two reasons why an importer might use documentary credit when buying from an overseas supplier.* **[4]**

> Documentary credit gives the **supplier security** [1], because the importer's bank promises to pay once documents prove the goods have been shipped, so the supplier does not fear non-payment [1]. It also protects the **importer** [1], because the bank pays only when the correct documents are shown, so the importer is not paying for goods that were never sent [1].

Why it scores: each reason names a party and explains the protection it gets.

## What are the common mistakes in 5.2?

- Mixing up **standing orders** (fixed, set by the payer) and **direct debits** (variable, collected by the receiver).
- Saying a **debit card** gives credit. It takes money straight from the account.
- Confusing an **overdraft** (short term, flexible) with a **loan** (fixed sum, fixed term).
- Naming a service without saying why it suits the situation.
- Writing that digital banking has only advantages. Mention fraud and exclusion risks.

## Exam technique

- **State** a service: give the name.
- **Describe** a payment method: say how it works.
- **Explain** a choice: link the feature of the service to the need in the case.
- **Assess** digital banking: give benefits and drawbacks, then a judgement for the customer in the question.
- In interest calculations, show the rate, the time and the arithmetic.
