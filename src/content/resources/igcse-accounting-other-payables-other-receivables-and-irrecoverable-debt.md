## Matching and why it matters

Under the matching / accruals concept, a period carries the expenses it incurred and the incomes it earned, whether or not cash moved in it. Use cash instead and a year in which two insurance premiums happened to be paid looks worse than it was, which misleads owners and lenders.

Tarnwood Cycles, a sole trader with a 30 June year end, paid $2,400 on 1 December 2027 for twelve months of insurance.
Only seven months (December to June) fall in the year to 30 June 2028, which is $1,400.
The other five months, $1,000, serve next year, so at the year end they are an asset: a prepaid expense.

In this guide, **other payables** are amounts the business owes that are not trade purchases, and **other receivables** are amounts due to the business, or paid ahead, that are not from trade customers. Both are current items in the statement of financial position.

| Adjustment | Meaning | Year-end journal | Balance b/d |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accrued expense | Used, not yet paid | Dr Expense, Cr Other payables | Credit |
| Prepaid expense | Paid, benefits next year | Dr Other receivables, Cr Expense | Debit |
| Accrued income | Earned, not yet received | Dr Other receivables, Cr Income | Debit |
| Income received in advance | Received, earned next year | Dr Income, Cr Other payables | Credit |

**Ledger method for any expense or income account:**

1. Enter the opening balance. An expense account opens with a debit if prepaid and a credit if accrued. An income account opens with a credit if received in advance and a debit if accrued.
2. Enter the cash paid or received.
3. Work out the closing balance by time-apportioning: months in the year divided by months paid for, times the amount.
4. Carry that balance down on the side that lets the account balance, and bring it down on the opposite side.
5. Whatever makes the account balance is moved to the statement of profit or loss.

## Worked example: Tarnwood Cycles, year ended 30 June 2028

**Insurance (prepaid expense).** The previous policy ran to 30 November 2027, so $900 was prepaid on 1 July 2027.
The new policy leaves $1,000 prepaid.

```
Insurance account ($)
Date      Details                Amount  Date      Details                Amount
1 Jul 27  Balance b/d               900  30 Jun 28 Profit or loss          2,300
1 Dec 27  Bank                    2,400  30 Jun 28 Balance c/d             1,000
                                 ------                                   ------
                                  3,300                                    3,300
                                 ======                                   ======
1 Jul 28  Balance b/d             1,000
```

**Wages (accrued expense).** The amounts in dollars: 380 owing on 1 July 2027, 14,260 paid in the year and 440 owing at 30 June 2028.

```
Wages account ($)
Date      Details                Amount  Date      Details                Amount
Various   Bank                   14,260  1 Jul 27  Balance b/d               380
30 Jun 28 Balance c/d               440  30 Jun 28 Profit or loss         14,320
                                 ------                                   ------
                                 14,700                                   14,700
                                 ======                                   ======
                                         1 Jul 28  Balance b/d               440
```

**Hire fees received (accrued income).** The business lets out a workshop bay. A customer had paid 150 in advance on 1 July 2027. During the year 3,900 was received. At 30 June 2028 a customer owes 260 for June.

```
Hire fees received account ($)
Date      Details                Amount  Date      Details                Amount
30 Jun 28 Profit or loss          4,310  1 Jul 27  Balance b/d               150
                                         Various   Bank                    3,900
                                         30 Jun 28 Balance c/d               260
                                 ------                                   ------
                                  4,310                                    4,310
                                 ======                                   ======
1 Jul 28  Balance b/d               260
```

The opening credit balance is earned this year, so it adds to income. The closing figure is entered on the credit side as balance c/d and appears as a debit when brought down, because the money is owed to the business.

**Sponsorship (income received in advance).** On 1 May 2028 a club paid $1,800 for twelve months.
Two months, $300, are earned by 30 June;
$1,500 is for next year.

```
Sponsorship income account ($)
Date      Details                Amount  Date      Details                Amount
30 Jun 28 Profit or loss            300  1 May 28  Bank                    1,800
30 Jun 28 Balance c/d             1,500
                                 ------                                   ------
                                  1,800                                    1,800
                                 ======                                   ======
                                         1 Jul 28  Balance b/d             1,500
```

```
Journal, 30 Jun 28 ($)                         Dr      Cr
Other receivables (prepaid insurance)       1,000
   Insurance                                        1,000
Wages                                         440
   Other payables (accrued wages)                     440
Other receivables (accrued hire fees)         260
   Hire fees received                                 260
Sponsorship income                          1,500
   Other payables (income in advance)               1,500
```

```
Statement of financial position extract, 30 Jun 28 ($)
Current assets
   Other receivables (1,000 + 260)          1,260
Current liabilities
   Other payables (440 + 1,500)             1,940
```

Taken together, these four year-end adjustments leave profit $680 lower than if all four were left out. The table shows the effect of leaving each adjustment out:

| If you leave out | Profit | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Accrued wages | Overstated by $440 | Liabilities understated |
| Prepaid insurance | Understated by $1,000 | Assets understated |
| Accrued hire fees | Understated by $260 | Assets understated |
| Sponsorship in advance | Overstated by $1,500 | Liabilities understated |

## Irrecoverable debts

An **irrecoverable debt** is money due from a credit customer that the business has given up expecting, for example because the customer is bankrupt or cannot be traced. The sale was already recorded as revenue, so the loss is an expense and sales are left unchanged. Journal: Dr Irrecoverable debts, Cr the customer.

Harrowgate Joinery has a 31 March year end. G Pellegrini owed $780 from a sale on 14 October 2027 and was declared bankrupt on 12 February 2028.
On 20 March 2028 the business learned that M Thackray's company, which owed $340, had closed.

```
G Pellegrini account ($)
Date      Details                Amount  Date      Details                Amount
14 Oct 27 Sales                     780  12 Feb 28 Irrecoverable debts       780
                                 ------                                   ------
                                    780                                      780
                                 ======                                   ======
```

```
Irrecoverable debts account ($)
Date      Details                Amount  Date      Details                Amount
12 Feb 28 G Pellegrini              780  31 Mar 28 Profit or loss          1,120
20 Mar 28 M Thackray                340
                                 ------                                   ------
                                  1,120                                    1,120
                                 ======                                   ======
```

Trade receivables fall by 1,120 dollars, and the whole amount is treated as a cost of the year in the statement of profit or loss.

## Irrecoverable debts recovered

A debt written off in an earlier year may later be paid. T Nakamura's $425 was written off in the year to 31 March 2027, and on 9 February 2028 he paid in full by cheque. Reinstate the debt first, so his account shows the whole history, then record the money.

```
Journal, 9 Feb 28 ($)                          Dr      Cr
T Nakamura                                    425
   Irrecoverable debts recovered                      425
Bank                                          425
   T Nakamura                                         425
```

```
T Nakamura account ($)
Date      Details                Amount  Date      Details                Amount
9 Feb 28  Debts recovered           425  9 Feb 28  Bank                      425
                                 ------                                   ------
                                    425                                      425
                                 ======                                   ======
```

```
Irrecoverable debts recovered account ($)
Date      Details                Amount  Date      Details                Amount
31 Mar 28 Profit or loss            425  9 Feb 28  T Nakamura                425
                                 ------                                   ------
                                    425                                      425
                                 ======                                   ======
```

(The details column says Debts recovered to save space.) The recovery is income of the year in which it is received.

## Why keep an allowance

The business cannot tell which customers will fail to pay, but some year-end trade receivables will probably not be collected. Without an allowance, trade receivables are shown at more than the business expects to receive, and the cost of the loss falls in a later year than the sale. The allowance is an estimate that applies matching / accruals and prudence. It is usually given as a percentage.

1. Write off the known irrecoverable debts first.
2. Allowance required = percentage × remaining trade receivables.
3. Compare it with the existing allowance balance.
4. An increase is an expense: Dr Statement of profit or loss, Cr Allowance. A decrease is income: Dr Allowance, Cr Statement of profit or loss.
5. In the statement of financial position, deduct the full allowance from trade receivables.

Harrowgate's policy is 5%. In the first year the allowance is created. In the second year receivables are 41,400 before the 1,120 of write-offs. In the third year collections have improved, so the rate falls to 4%. Figures are in dollars.

| Year end | Remaining trade receivables | Rate | Allowance required | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 31 Mar 2027 | 29,600 | 5% | 1,480 | Created: expense 1,480 |
| 31 Mar 2028 | 40,280 | 5% | 2,014 | Increase: expense 534 |
| 31 Mar 2029 | 34,000 | 4% | 1,360 | Decrease: income 654 |

Year one: Dr Statement of profit or loss 1,480, Cr Allowance 1,480. Year three: Dr Allowance 654, Cr Statement of profit or loss 654.

## Common mistakes

- Using the cash paid as the expense, instead of adjusting it for opening and closing balances.
- Showing a prepayment as a credit balance. Prepaid expenses and accrued incomes are debit balances.
- Recording a write-off as a reduction in sales rather than as an expense.
- Recording a recovered debt in the bank account only. Reinstate the customer first.
- Charging the entire allowance as an expense every year instead of just the change.
- Calculating the percentage before the irrecoverable debts are written off.
- Treating a decrease in the allowance as an expense. It is income.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between an accrued expense and a prepaid expense?**
An accrued expense has been used but not yet paid at the year end, so it is added to the expense and shown as an other payable (a current liability with a credit balance). A prepaid expense has been paid but benefits the next year, so it is deducted from the expense and shown as an other receivable (a current asset with a debit balance).

**How do you work out the expense for the year when amounts are owing?**
Take the cash paid, subtract the amount owing at the start of the year (last year's cost) and add the amount owing at the end of the year. The figure that makes the ledger account balance is the amount transferred to the statement of profit or loss.

**How do you record an irrecoverable debt in Cambridge IGCSE Accounting 0452?**
Debit the irrecoverable debts account and credit the customer's account, then transfer the irrecoverable debts to the statement of profit or loss as an expense. Sales are not reduced, because the sale was already recorded as revenue.

**How do you record a debt that was written off and is later paid?**
First reinstate the debt (debit the customer, credit irrecoverable debts recovered), then record the money received (debit bank, credit the customer). The recovery is income of the year in which it is received.

**How much of the allowance for irrecoverable debts goes to the statement of profit or loss?**
Only the change in the allowance: an increase is an expense and a decrease is income. The full allowance, calculated as a percentage of trade receivables after known irrecoverable debts are written off, is deducted from trade receivables in the statement of financial position.
