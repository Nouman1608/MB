## Core recall

### Four reasons businesses plan (the specification's list)

1. Importance in setting up a new business: test the idea on paper first.
2. Raising finance: give lenders and investors evidence.
3. Setting objectives: targets to aim for and measure against.
4. Detailing how functions will be organised: operations, human resources, marketing and finance.

The specification states that students will not be expected to write a business plan.

### Readers of a plan

| Reader | Main question they ask |
|---|---|
| Owner | Will this idea work, and what do I do first? |
| Bank | Can the loan and interest be repaid? |
| Investor | Will the business grow and give me a return? |
| Staff and managers | What are my responsibilities and targets? |

### Usual sections

Business idea and objectives; target market and research; marketing plan; operations; people; financial forecasts. The specification does not set a fixed list, so any sensible section with a clear purpose is acceptable.

### Benefits and drawbacks as chains

| Benefit chain | Drawback chain |
|---|---|
| Research → weak idea spotted early → money not wasted | Takes time → launch delayed → sales lost |
| Forecasts → lender can judge risk → finance more likely | Forecasts are estimates → wrong figures → poor decisions or over-borrowing |
| Objectives → progress measured → problems acted on early | Market changes → plan out of date → misleads if not reviewed |
| Roles set out → tasks not missed → smoother start | Rigid plan → owner slow to react to new chances |

### Formulas

- Revenue = price × quantity sold
- Total variable costs = variable cost per unit × quantity
- Total costs = fixed costs + total variable costs
- Profit (or loss) = revenue − total costs

### Working backwards

- Total costs = revenue − profit
- Fixed costs = total costs − total variable costs
- Variable cost per unit = total variable costs ÷ quantity
- Quantity = revenue ÷ price

### What happens when sales change

If sales fall, revenue and total variable costs fall, but fixed costs stay the same. So profit falls by more than you might expect. Each unit lost costs the business its price minus its variable cost.

Figures invented: a stall sells mugs at £12, with a variable cost of £4. Losing 50 sales cuts profit by 50 × (£12 − £4) = £400, whatever the fixed costs are.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Business plan | A written document setting out what a business aims to do and how it will do it |
| Entrepreneur | A person who sets up a business and takes the risk |
| Objective | A specific target the business aims to achieve |
| Forecast | An estimate of a future figure, such as sales or costs |
| Finance | Money used to start or run a business |
| Functional areas | Operations, human resources, marketing and finance |
| Fixed costs | Costs that do not change with output |
| Variable costs | Costs that change directly with output |
| Total costs | Fixed costs plus variable costs |
| Revenue | Income from selling goods or services |
| Profit | Revenue minus total costs, when positive |
| Loss | The result when total costs exceed revenue |

## Exam traps

- **"A plan guarantees success."** It reduces risk; it cannot remove it.
- **Only one purpose.** Raising finance is one of four reasons.
- **Fixed costs × quantity.** Fixed costs are already a total for the period.
- **Variable cost per unit used as total.** Multiply by quantity first.
- **Negative profit written as profit.** Write "a loss of £X".
- **Forecasts treated as facts.** In an evaluation, question where the numbers come from.
- **Revenue confused with profit.** High revenue can still mean a loss.
- **Generic benefits.** Apply them to the business and reader in the case.

## Self-test

1. State the four reasons for business planning in the specification.
2. Why is a plan especially important to a business with no trading history?
3. Name two sections of a business plan and give the purpose of each.
4. Give one drawback of business planning and explain its effect.
5. Classify as fixed or variable: rent, flour, packaging, insurance.
6. A business sells 800 units at £5. Calculate revenue.
7. Variable cost is £1.75 per unit for those 800 units and fixed costs are £1,900. Calculate total costs.
8. Calculate the profit or loss for the business in questions 6 and 7.
9. A plan shows revenue of £15,000 and a loss of £1,200. Calculate total costs.
10. Total costs are £9,400, fixed costs are £3,400 and 1,200 units are made. Find the variable cost per unit.
11. Why does a fall in sales hit profit hard when fixed costs are high?
12. Which reader of a plan is most interested in how functions are organised?

### Answers

1. Setting up a new business; raising finance; setting objectives; detailing how functions of the business will be organised.
2. Lenders and investors have no past results to judge, so the plan's forecasts and research are the main evidence.
3. Any two with purposes, for example: financial forecasts show whether the business can make a profit and repay loans; market research shows evidence that customers will buy.
4. For example: forecasts may be wrong, so the owner may borrow too little or too much, or spend on the wrong things.
5. Rent fixed; flour variable; packaging variable; insurance fixed.
6. 800 × £5 = £4,000.
7. 800 × £1.75 = £1,400; £1,400 + £1,900 = £3,300.
8. £4,000 − £3,300 = £700 profit.
9. Total costs = £15,000 + £1,200 = £16,200.
10. Total variable costs = £9,400 − £3,400 = £6,000; £6,000 ÷ 1,200 = £5 per unit.
11. Revenue falls, but fixed costs must still be paid in full, so less revenue is left over and profit falls or turns into a loss.
12. Managers and staff, because the plan shows who is responsible for each function. The owner also uses it.
