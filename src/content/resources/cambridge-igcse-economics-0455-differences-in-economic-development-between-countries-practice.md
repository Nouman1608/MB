All countries and figures are invented. Revise first with the [development differences study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-differences-in-economic-development-between-countries/) and the [development differences revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-differences-in-economic-development-between-countries-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Which activity is in the tertiary sector? **[1]**

A: Coal mining

B: Car assembly

C: Hotel services

D: Fishing

**2.** Which change is most likely to raise output per worker in a low-income country? **[1]**

A: A rise in the birth rate

B: Investment in irrigation equipment for farms

C: A fall in the saving ratio

D: A fall in school attendance

**3.** Explain the difference between economic growth and economic development. **[2]**

**4.** Explain one reason why saving is low in many low-income countries. **[2]**

**5.** Real GDP in Country N rises by 5% while its population rises by 3%. Calculate the percentage change in real GDP per head, to one decimal place. **[2]**

**6.** State two impacts of low levels of education on a country's development. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** The table shows data for two countries (invented).

| | Country P | Country Q |
|---|---|---|
| Workers in primary / secondary / tertiary (%) | 60 / 15 / 25 | 5 / 25 / 70 |
| Life expectancy (years) | 61 | 79 |
| Saving (% of GDP) | 8 | 21 |

**(a)** Identify which country is likely to be more developed, giving one piece of evidence from the table. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how the difference in saving could cause a difference in development. **[3]**

**8.** In Country R, output per worker is 1,500 in the primary sector, 6,000 in the secondary sector and 5,000 in the tertiary sector. Workers are split 60 / 15 / 25.

**(a)** Calculate average output per worker. **[2]**

**(b)** 15% of all workers move from the primary to the secondary sector. Calculate the new average output per worker. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage increase, to one decimal place. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why this change raises productivity even though no worker's output within a sector changes. **[2]**

**9.** Explain two ways in which poor healthcare can reduce a country's productivity. **[4]**

**10.** Analyse how rapid population growth can hold back economic development. **[4]**

**11.** Explain why a country that depends on exports from its primary sector may develop more slowly. **[4]**

**12.** Discuss whether investment in capital is more important than investment in education for a low-income country's development. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. A and D are primary; B is secondary. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. Irrigation equipment is capital, raising output per farm worker. **[1]**

**3.** Growth is an increase in a country's real GDP [1]. Development is a sustained improvement in living standards, including health, education and opportunities, which growth may or may not bring [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Most income is needed for food, housing and other necessities [1], so households have little left over to save [1]. Other creditable reason: few banks or low trust in them, so savings are not collected for lending. **[2]**

**5.** 1.05 ÷ 1.03 = 1.0194 [1]; real GDP per head rises by about **1.9%** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two: lower productivity [1]; workers cannot use new technology [1]; fewer people able to start businesses; higher birth rates. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Country Q [1]; any one: smaller primary sector or larger tertiary sector; higher life expectancy; higher saving [1].

**(b)** Higher saving provides more funds for investment [1], so Country Q can buy more machinery and infrastructure, raising capital per worker [1]. This raises productivity and income, which allows further saving, while Country P stays in a cycle of low saving and low income [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** (0.6 × 1,500) + (0.15 × 6,000) + (0.25 × 5,000) = 900 + 900 + 1,250 [1] = **3,050** [1].

**(b)** New shares 45 / 30 / 25: (0.45 × 1,500) + (0.30 × 6,000) + (0.25 × 5,000) = 675 + 1,800 + 1,250 [1] = **3,725** [1].

**(c)** (3,725 − 3,050) ÷ 3,050 × 100 = **22.1%** [1].

**(d)** Workers move from a sector where each produces 1,500 to one where each produces 6,000 [1], so the average across all workers rises because more of the workforce is in the higher-productivity sector [1]. **[7]**

**9.** Way 1: sick workers miss days of work [1], so output per worker over a year falls [1]. Way 2: children who are often ill miss school or learn less [1], so they grow into less-skilled workers with lower productivity [1]. Other creditable way: shorter life expectancy reduces the return on training. **[4]**

**10.** If population grows nearly as fast as output, real GDP per head rises slowly [1]. A young population needs more spending on schools and healthcare [1], which leaves less government revenue for infrastructure investment [1]. If jobs do not grow as fast as the labour force, unemployment rises and income per head stays low [1]. **[4]**

**11.** World prices of primary products can swing sharply [1], so export earnings, incomes and tax revenue are unstable, making it hard to plan investment [1]. Output per worker in primary production, especially small-scale farming, is usually low [1], so wages and income stay low compared with countries with large secondary and tertiary sectors [1]. **[4]**

**12.** For capital: machinery, power and roads raise output per worker directly [1]; investment can raise output more quickly than education [1]; infrastructure lets firms reach markets and attracts foreign firms [1]. For education: capital is of little use without workers skilled enough to operate and maintain it [1]; education raises productivity across all sectors and lets the country adopt new technology [1]; educated women tend to have fewer children, slowing population growth [1]. Low saving means the country may find it hard to fund either, so it may rely on borrowing or foreign investment [1]. Judgement: the two are complements; capital may give faster gains where skills already exist, but education is more important for long-lasting development, so a balanced approach is best [1]. **[8]**

*Examiner insight:* The strongest answers explain why each type of investment depends on the other before reaching a judgement.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding the growth rates of output and population instead of comparing them.
- Forgetting to change the sector shares before recalculating the average.
- Naming a factor without its impact on development.
- Treating growth as development.
- Concluding without a reason in "Discuss" questions.
