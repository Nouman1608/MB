These questions cover section 2.2, Thermal properties and temperature (2.2.1 to 2.2.3), of the Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Take the specific heat capacity of water as 4200 J/(kg °C).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** **(a)** List solids, liquids and gases in order of how much they expand for the same temperature rise, largest first. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe **one** consequence of thermal expansion that engineers must allow for. **[1]**

**2.** A bimetallic strip is made of brass and iron bonded together. Brass expands more than iron when heated.

**(a)** State which metal is on the outside of the curve when the strip is heated. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the strip bends. **[1]**

**3.** A metal lid is stuck on a glass jar. Explain why running hot water over the lid helps to loosen it. **[2]**

**4.** Ice at 0 °C is heated until it has all melted.

**(a)** State what happens to the temperature while the ice melts. **[1]**
**(b)** State what the energy supplied is used for during melting. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why a swimmer feels cold after getting out of a pool, even on a warm day. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Calculate the energy needed to heat 3.0 kg of oil from 25 °C to 65 °C. The specific heat capacity of the oil is 2000 J/(kg °C). **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** This question is about thermal expansion in structures.

**(a)** Explain why overhead power cables are hung loosely between pylons in summer. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a long road bridge has expansion joints. **[2]**
**(c)** *(Extended)* A balloon of air and a steel bar are heated by the same temperature rise. Explain, in terms of particles, why the air expands much more than the steel. **[2]**

**8.** Solid wax is heated steadily and its temperature is recorded every minute.

| Time / min | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature / °C | 30 | 45 | 54 | 54 | 54 | 54 | 65 | 76 |

**(a)** State the melting point of the wax. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the temperature stays constant from 2 to 5 minutes although energy is still supplied. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe what happens to the particles when liquid wax solidifies. **[2]**

**9.** This question is about evaporation.

**(a)** Describe evaporation in terms of particles. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why evaporation causes a liquid to cool. **[2]**
**(c)** State the boiling point of pure water at standard atmospheric pressure. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A student measures the specific heat capacity of aluminium using a 1.0 kg block, an electric heater and a joulemeter.

**(a)** Describe how the student should carry out the experiment. **[4]**
**(b)** The joulemeter reading is 13 500 J and the temperature rises from 21.0 °C to 35.5 °C. Calculate the specific heat capacity. **[2]**
**(c)** The accepted value is about 900 J/(kg °C). Suggest why the student's value is higher. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Wet washing is hung on a line to dry.

**(a)** State **three** differences between evaporation and boiling. **[3]**
**(b)** State and explain **three** conditions that make the washing dry faster. **[3]**

**12.** *(Extended)* This question is about temperature and internal energy.

**(a)** Describe an increase in temperature in terms of the particles of an object. **[1]**
**(b)** A mug contains 0.20 kg of tea, which cools from 80 °C to 50 °C. Taking the tea as water, calculate the energy it transfers to the surroundings. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a bottle of water wrapped in a wet cloth stays cooler on a hot day. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** gases, liquids, solids [1]
**(b)** Any one: gaps in railway tracks; expansion joints in bridges; slack in overhead cables; or a correct consequence such as roads or rails buckling [1].

**2. (a)** Brass [1]
**(b)** The two metals expand by different amounts, but they are joined, so the strip must curve, with the metal that expands more on the longer, outer side [1].

**3.** The metal lid expands more than the glass [1]; so the lid becomes slightly larger and less tight [1].

**4. (a)** It stays constant (at 0 °C) [1]
**(b)** Separating the particles / overcoming the forces between them, changing the solid into a liquid [1].

**5.** Water evaporates from the skin; the more-energetic particles escape [1]; the remaining water cools and so cools the skin [1].

**6.** ΔE = mcΔθ = 3.0 × 2000 × (65 − 25) [1] = **240 000 J** [1]

**7. (a)** In winter the cables contract (become shorter) [1]; if they were tight in summer they could snap or pull on the pylons in winter [1].
**(b)** The bridge sections expand on hot days [1]; the joints leave room for this so the bridge does not buckle or crack [1].
**(c)** In steel the particles are held close together by strong forces, so heating only makes them vibrate slightly further apart [1]; in air the particles are far apart with very weak forces, so as they move faster they push the balloon outwards much more [1].

**8. (a)** **54 °C** [1]
**(b)** The energy is being used to change the state, melting the wax [1]; it separates the particles (overcomes the forces between them) rather than increasing their kinetic energy [1].
**(c)** The particles lose energy and move more slowly [1]; the forces between them hold them in fixed positions, where they can only vibrate [1].

**9. (a)** Particles at the surface of the liquid escape [1]; only the more-energetic particles have enough energy to escape [1].
**(b)** The most energetic particles leave [1]; so the average kinetic energy of the particles left behind decreases, and the temperature falls [1].
**(c)** **100 °C** [1]

**10. (a)** Measure the mass of the block (1.0 kg) [1]; put the heater and a thermometer in the holes in the block (with oil in the thermometer hole) and insulate the block [1]; record the starting temperature and the joulemeter reading, then switch on for several minutes [1]; switch off, record the energy supplied and the highest temperature reached [1].
**(b)** Δθ = 35.5 − 21.0 = 14.5 °C [1]; c = 13 500 ÷ (1.0 × 14.5) = **930 J/(kg °C)** [1]
**(c)** Some energy was transferred to the surroundings, so the temperature rise was smaller than it would otherwise have been [1].

**11. (a)** Evaporation occurs at the surface, boiling throughout the liquid [1]; evaporation happens at any temperature, boiling only at the boiling point [1]; evaporation cools the liquid, while boiling needs energy supplied by heating [1].
**(b)** A higher temperature: more particles have enough energy to escape [1]; a larger surface area (washing spread out): more particles are at the surface [1]; wind (air movement): escaped particles are carried away before they can return [1].

**12. (a)** The average kinetic energy of the particles increases [1].
**(b)** ΔE = 0.20 × 4200 × (80 − 50) [1] = **25 200 J** [1]
**(c)** Water evaporates from the cloth, cooling the cloth [1]; energy is then transferred from the warmer bottle to the cooler cloth, so the bottle cools [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying particles expand when heated.
- Using final temperature instead of temperature change in ΔE = mcΔθ.
- Saying the temperature rises during melting.
- Explaining evaporative cooling without the most energetic particles escaping.
- *(Extended)* Giving the same factor twice when asked for three conditions.
