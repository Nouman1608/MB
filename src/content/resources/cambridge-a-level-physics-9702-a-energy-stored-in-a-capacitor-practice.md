## Section A

**1.** State what is represented by the area under a graph of potential difference against charge for a capacitor. **[1]**

**2.** Use a graph of potential difference V against charge Q to show that the energy stored in a capacitor is ½QV. **[3]**

**3.** A 680 μF capacitor is charged to a p.d. of 5.0 V. Calculate the energy stored. **[2]**

**4.** A capacitor stores 12 mJ when the p.d. across it is 4.0 V. Calculate the energy stored when the p.d. is 10 V. **[2]**

**5.** A capacitor holds a charge of 3.6 mC and stores 27 mJ. Calculate **(a)** the p.d. across it and **(b)** its capacitance. **[3]**

**6.** A student says: "If I halve the p.d. across a capacitor, half the energy leaves it." Correct the statement and give the fraction of the energy that leaves. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The graph of p.d. V against charge Q for a capacitor is a straight line from the origin to the point (9.0 mC, 18 V).

**(a)** Calculate the capacitance. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy stored when the p.d. is 18 V. **[1]**

**(c)** Determine, using the area under the graph, the energy released as the p.d. falls from 18 V to 6.0 V. **[3]**

**8.** A capacitor of capacitance 1500 μF is charged by a 12 V supply through a resistor.

**(a)** Calculate the charge on the capacitor when it is fully charged. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the work done by the supply in charging the capacitor. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the energy stored in the capacitor and explain the difference between your answers to (b) and (c). **[3]**

**(d)** The resistor is replaced by one of twice the resistance. State the effect on the energy dissipated during charging. **[1]**

**9.** A 220 μF capacitor is charged to 30 V and then disconnected. It is connected across an uncharged 440 μF capacitor.

**(a)** Calculate the initial charge and energy stored. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the common p.d. after connection. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the total energy stored after connection and the energy transferred elsewhere. **[2]**

**(d)** State where this energy goes. **[1]**

**10.** The flash of a camera is powered by a 150 μF capacitor charged to 300 V. The flash lasts 2.0 ms, and in that time the p.d. falls to 50 V.

**(a)** Calculate the energy released by the capacitor during the flash. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the mean power delivered during the flash. **[1]**

**11.** An isolated capacitor of capacitance 100 μF carries a charge of 2.0 mC.

**(a)** Calculate the energy stored. **[1]**

**(b)** Without the charge changing, the capacitance is reduced to 40 μF. Calculate the new energy stored and the new p.d. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest where the extra energy comes from. **[1]**

**12.** A student charges a capacitor to several different p.d.s and measures the energy stored each time with a joulemeter. She plots a graph of energy W against V².

**(a)** Explain why the graph is a straight line through the origin. **[1]**

**(b)** The gradient of the graph is 2.35 × 10⁻⁴ J V⁻². Calculate the capacitance. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The energy stored in the capacitor (the work done in charging it) [1].

**2.** V = Q/C, so V is proportional to Q and the graph is a straight line through the origin [1]. The work done adding a small charge ΔQ is VΔQ, the area of a strip under the line, so the energy stored is the total area under the graph [1]. The area is a triangle: ½ × Q × V = ½QV [1].

**3.** W = ½CV² = ½ × 680 × 10⁻⁶ × 5.0² [1] = 8.5 × 10⁻³ J [1].

**4.** W ∝ V², so W = 12 × (10/4.0)² [1] = 75 mJ [1].

**5. (a)** W = ½QV, so V = 2W/Q = 2 × 27 × 10⁻³ ÷ 3.6 × 10⁻³ [1] = 15 V [1].

**(b)** C = Q/V = 3.6 × 10⁻³ ÷ 15 = 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ F (240 μF) [1].

**6.** W ∝ V², so halving V leaves one quarter of the energy stored [1]; three quarters of the energy leaves [1].

**7. (a)** C = Q/V = 9.0 × 10⁻³ ÷ 18 = 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ F [1].

**(b)** W = ½QV = ½ × 9.0 × 10⁻³ × 18 = 0.081 J [1].

**(c)** At 6.0 V, Q = CV = 3.0 × 10⁻³ C [1]. Area of trapezium = ½ × (18 + 6.0) × (9.0 − 3.0) × 10⁻³ [1] = 0.072 J [1].

**8. (a)** Q = CV = 1500 × 10⁻⁶ × 12 = 0.018 C [1].

**(b)** W = QV = 0.018 × 12 = 0.216 J ≈ 0.22 J [1].

**(c)** Energy stored = ½QV = 0.108 J ≈ 0.11 J [1]. The supply moves all the charge through the full 12 V, but the capacitor's p.d. rises from 0 to 12 V, so only half the work is stored [1]; the other half (0.108 J) is dissipated as internal energy in the resistor [1].

**(d)** No change: the energy dissipated is still half the work done by the supply (0.108 J); charging just takes longer [1].

**9. (a)** Q = CV = 220 × 10⁻⁶ × 30 = 6.6 × 10⁻³ C [1]; W = ½CV² = ½ × 220 × 10⁻⁶ × 900 = 0.099 J [1].

**(b)** Charge is conserved, and the capacitors are in parallel with total capacitance 660 μF [1]; V = 6.6 × 10⁻³ ÷ 660 × 10⁻⁶ = 10 V [1].

**(c)** W = ½ × 660 × 10⁻⁶ × 10² = 0.033 J [1]; energy transferred = 0.099 − 0.033 = 0.066 J [1].

**(d)** To internal energy (heating) in the resistance of the connecting wires, with a little radiated [1].

**10. (a)** Energy at 300 V = ½ × 150 × 10⁻⁶ × 300² = 6.75 J [1]. Energy at 50 V = ½ × 150 × 10⁻⁶ × 50² = 0.1875 J [1]. Energy released = 6.75 − 0.19 = 6.6 J [1].

**(b)** P = 6.56 ÷ 2.0 × 10⁻³ = 3.3 × 10³ W [1].

**11. (a)** W = Q²/(2C) = (2.0 × 10⁻³)² ÷ (2 × 100 × 10⁻⁶) = 0.020 J [1].

**(b)** W = (2.0 × 10⁻³)² ÷ (2 × 40 × 10⁻⁶) = 0.050 J [1]; V = Q/C = 2.0 × 10⁻³ ÷ 40 × 10⁻⁶ = 50 V [1].

**(c)** From work done by whatever external agent changed the capacitor (against the attraction between the oppositely charged plates) [1].

**12. (a)** W = ½CV², so W is proportional to V² with C constant [1].

**(b)** Gradient = ½C, so C = 2 × 2.35 × 10⁻⁴ = 4.7 × 10⁻⁴ F (470 μF) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using W = QV for the energy stored instead of ½QV.
- Finding an energy change as ½C(V₁ − V₂)² rather than ½C(V₁² − V₂²).
- Leaving μF or mC unconverted.
- Assuming energy is conserved when charge is shared, instead of using conservation of charge to find the common p.d.
- Explaining the "missing" energy without naming the resistance in which it is dissipated.
- In graph questions, not showing which area was used or not stating the charges read from the axis.
