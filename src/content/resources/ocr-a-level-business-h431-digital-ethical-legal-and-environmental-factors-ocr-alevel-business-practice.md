Every business and figure is invented. Revise first with the [digital, ethical, legal and environmental factors study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-digital-ethical-legal-and-environmental-factors-ocr-alevel-business/) and the [revision notes on these factors](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-digital-ethical-legal-and-environmental-factors-ocr-alevel-business-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by the digital revolution. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between the law and ethics, using an example. **[3]**

**3.** Identify **two** areas of legislation that a restaurant must comply with, and state the purpose of **one** of them. **[3]**

**4.** Define sustainability. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why a clothing retailer is likely to be more affected by ethical issues than a local window-cleaning business. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** consequence for a business of failing to comply with health and safety legislation. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** Larchfield Coffee sells a cup of coffee for £3.20. The unit cost is £1.60. Switching to certified fair-trade beans would add 8p to the unit cost.

**(a)** Calculate the profit margin per cup after the switch, if the price is unchanged. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the price needed to keep a 50% margin after the switch. **[2]**

**(c)** Recommend whether Larchfield should switch to certified beans. Justify your answer. **[6]**

**8.** Ashgrove Brewery is considering solar panels costing £64,000, which would save £8,000 a year on electricity.

**(a)** Calculate the payback period. **[2]**

**(b)** Evaluate the importance to Ashgrove of being seen as an environmentally friendly business. **[6]**

**9.** Pennard Cameras has two high-street shops selling cameras, lenses and repairs. Evaluate the opportunities and threats the digital revolution presents to Pennard. **[9]**

**10.** Fenmoor Logistics holds the names, addresses and phone numbers of thousands of customers. Evaluate the ways Fenmoor could make sure it complies with data protection legislation. **[8]**

**11.** Cobbleton Furniture, a budget furniture maker, plans to use only certified sustainable timber, which would raise its material costs by 12%. Its main objective is to grow sales by 20% in three years. Recommend and justify how Cobbleton could manage the conflict between these objectives. **[8]**

**12.** Rillstone Toys ignored new safety rules on small parts and kept selling only through toy shops while its rivals moved online. Evaluate the consequences of ignoring these external factors. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The shift from mechanical and analogue technology to digital technology [1], creating an information age in which information is stored, processed and shared quickly and cheaply [1]. **[2]**

**2.** The law is a set of rules enforced by the state with penalties for breaking them [1]. Ethics are moral principles about what is right, which a business can choose to follow beyond the law [1]. For example, legal tax avoidance may be seen as unethical [1]. **[3]**

**3.** Any two from: health and safety [1]; consumer protection [1]; employment; equality; data protection. Purpose, for example: health and safety law protects staff and customers from harm, such as burns or food poisoning [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Meeting the needs of the present [1] without reducing the ability of future generations to meet their own needs [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The retailer may buy from long overseas supply chains where wages and conditions are hard to check [1]. It has a high public profile, so poor practice by a supplier attracts media attention [1]. A window cleaner has a short, local operation with few ethical risks in its supply chain [1]. **[3]**

**6.** It may be fined or prosecuted [1], which raises costs and damages its reputation, so customers and staff may go elsewhere [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** New unit cost = £1.68; profit = 3.20 − 1.68 = £1.52 [1]; margin = 1.52 ÷ 3.20 = **47.5%** [1].

**(b)** Price − 1.68 = 0.5 × price, so 0.5 × price = 1.68 [1]; price = **£3.36** [1].

**(c)** For switching: customers who value fair trade may stay loyal or pay more [1], and the 16p price rise to £3.36 is small compared with the cup price [1]. The margin falls only 2.5 points if price is held [1]. Against: nearby rivals may not raise prices, so price-sensitive customers could switch [1]. Recommendation: switch, holding price at first and promoting the change [1], because the cost is small and the ethical profile may attract customers, provided the shop's customers care about sourcing [1]. **[10]**

**8. (a)** 64,000 ÷ 8,000 [1] = **8 years** [1].

**(b)** Being seen as green may attract drinkers and pubs that choose suppliers on values [1], and can support a premium price [1]. It may help recruit staff who care about the environment [1]. However, many beer buyers choose on taste and price, so the effect on sales may be small [1]. Claims must be backed by evidence such as the solar panels, or Ashgrove risks accusations of greenwashing [1]. Judgement: moderately important; more so if Ashgrove sells to customers who check suppliers' environmental records [1]. **[8]**

**9.** Opportunities: Pennard can sell online to a national market [1], increasing sales without new shops [1]; it can use customer data to target offers, for example to people who bought a camera body [1]. Threats: online retailers may undercut its prices [1], and customers may browse in the shop then buy online [1]; smartphone cameras have reduced demand for cheaper cameras [1]. Repairs and expert advice are harder to digitise, so they protect part of the business [1]. Judgement: the threats are serious for product sales [1], but Pennard can survive by combining an online shop with expert in-store service and repairs, which online rivals find hard to copy [1]. **[9]**

**10.** Appoint a named manager responsible for data [1], so someone is accountable and keeps up with the law [1]. Train all staff [1], because many breaches come from human error such as sending data to the wrong person [1]. Secure systems with passwords, encryption and access controls [1], though this costs money and needs updating [1]. Collect and keep only the data needed, deleting it when no longer required [1]. Judgement: training is likely to be the most effective single step because it addresses the most common cause of breaches, but it works best alongside secure systems [1]. **[8]**

**11.** The conflict: higher timber costs reduce margin or force higher prices [1], which may slow sales growth in a price-sensitive budget market [1]. Options: phase the change in, starting with best-selling ranges [1]; absorb part of the cost by finding savings elsewhere, such as less waste in cutting [1]; promote the sustainable timber to attract customers who value it [1], though budget buyers may not pay more [1]. Recommendation: phase in certified timber over three years while targeting waste savings [1], because this spreads the cost and protects price competitiveness while sales grow [1]. **[8]**

**12.** Selling unsafe products breaks the law, so Rillstone faces fines, product recalls and possible legal action [1], and retailers may stop stocking it [1]. Ignoring the online shift means it loses customers to rivals [1], so sales fall [1]. The two combine: a recall makes shops even less willing to rely on Rillstone when sales are already weak [1]. Judgement: the legal failure is more serious in the short term because it can stop sales immediately, while the online gap erodes sales more slowly [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing ethics only as "doing the right thing", with no contrast to the law.
- Listing legal areas without stating what the law requires.
- Miscalculating the price needed for a target margin: divide the cost by (1 minus the margin).
- Giving only advantages of green or ethical choices, with no cost or conflict.
- Recommending without justifying why the option is better than the alternative.
