Condensed for the final weeks. For the worked extract, use the
[Unseen prose study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-english-literature-9275-unseen-prose/).

## At a glance

- Paper 2a, **Section C**, **Route A only**.
- One question on one unseen prose extract, **30 marks**.
- Last section of a 2 hour 15 minute, open-book, 90-mark paper.
- About **45 minutes**: protect this time.
- Route B takes the non-exam assessment instead.

## The 45-minute method

1. Read the **question** and underline its focus (1 minute).
2. Read the extract for **sense** (4 minutes).
3. Read again for **method**, annotating only what fits the question (5 minutes).
4. **Plan** an interpretation and three or four points in extract order (5 minutes).
5. **Write** (about 30 minutes), ending with the extract's final lines.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Narrative perspective | Who tells the story and from where |
| First-person narrator | The story is told by "I", a character in it |
| Third-person limited | The narration stays close to one character |
| Free indirect style | The narration takes on a character's own thoughts and phrasing |
| Withholding | Information the writer deliberately keeps back |
| Pace | The speed at which events unfold, shaped by sentence length and detail |
| Pathetic fallacy | Weather or setting reflecting feeling |
| Characterisation | How a writer builds a character through speech, action and description |
| Dialogue | Direct speech, revealing relationships and what is unsaid |
| Atmosphere | The overall mood created by setting and description |
| Turning point | Where the extract shifts in time, mood or understanding |

## Turning a detail into analysis

| Detail you notice | Weak comment | Stronger comment |
|---|---|---|
| A very short sentence after long ones | "This creates tension." | "The abrupt stop mirrors the character's shock, so the reader is halted at the same moment she is." |
| A repeated phrase | "The writer uses repetition." | "Each repetition sounds less certain, showing the character's confidence draining away." |
| An everyday simile | "This is a simile." | "Comparing a crisis to something ordinary shows a mind trying to keep control." |
| A missing explanation | "We don't know why." | "Withholding the cause makes the reader share the character's uncertainty." |

The stronger comments name the effect **at that moment** and connect it to a character or the reader.

## Every answer should include

- One point about **narrative perspective**.
- One point about **structure**: opening, a shift, or the ending.
- Several points about **language**, each with its effect on the reader.
- A clear **interpretation** that answers the question.

## Paragraph pattern

**Claim → short quotation → method → effect on the reader → link to the extract's development.**

## Exam traps

- Guessing what happens outside the extract.
- Paraphrasing paragraph by paragraph.
- "Short sentences create tension" with no specific explanation.
- Ignoring who tells the story.
- Running short of time because the poetry sections overran.
- Skipping the ending.

## Self-test

1. Which route and which section include unseen prose?
2. How many marks is it worth, and how long should you spend on it?
3. What is free indirect style?
4. Why should you not speculate about the rest of the novel?
5. Name three things every answer should include.
6. Why is timing especially important for this section?

**Answers:** 1. Route A, Paper 2a Section C. 2. 30 marks; about 45 minutes. 3. Third-person narration
that takes on a character's own thoughts and phrasing. 4. Marks come from analysing what is printed;
guesses are not supported by the text. 5. Any three of: a point on narrative perspective, a point on
structure, language analysis with effects, a clear interpretation. 6. It is the last section, so
overruns on the poetry sections reduce the time left for it.
