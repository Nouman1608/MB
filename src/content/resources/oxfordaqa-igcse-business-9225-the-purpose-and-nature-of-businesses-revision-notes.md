Condensed recall for section 3.1.1 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-the-purpose-and-nature-of-businesses/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- What a business is and **why people start one**: producing goods, supplying services, distributing products, fulfilling an opportunity, providing a good or service to others.
- **Goods and services**; **needs and wants**.
- The four **factors of production**: land, labour, capital, enterprise.
- Define **opportunity cost**.
- The **primary, secondary and tertiary sectors**, examples, and how they depend on each other.
- **Enterprise** and **entrepreneur**; their characteristics and objectives.
- The **dynamic** business environment: technology, economic situation, legislation, environmental expectations.

## Quick definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Business | An organisation that uses resources to produce goods or services for customers |
| Goods | Physical products that can be touched and stored |
| Services | Activities done for customers; produced and used at the same time |
| Need | Something people must have to survive |
| Want | Something people would like but can live without |
| Opportunity cost | The value of the next best alternative given up when a choice is made |
| Enterprise | Willingness to take a risk to set up and run a business |
| Entrepreneur | A person who takes the risk, organises the resources and aims to earn a profit |
| Gap in the market | A product or service customers want but nobody currently supplies |

## Reasons for starting a business

1. Produce **goods**.
2. Supply **services**.
3. **Distribute** products made by others.
4. Fulfil a **business opportunity**.
5. Provide a good or service **to others**.

## Factors of production

| Factor | Meaning | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| Land | Natural resources and sites | Rent |
| Labour | People doing the work | Wages |
| Capital | Machinery, vehicles, buildings and equipment | Interest |
| Enterprise | Organising the other factors and taking risk | Profit |

Memory aid: **L**and, **L**abour, **C**apital, **E**nterprise.

## Opportunity cost

- Resources are limited, so choosing one thing means giving up another.
- Opportunity cost is **not** the money spent. It is the **next best alternative** given up.
- Example: an oven-hour makes 5 loaves or 2 cakes. Using 4 hours for cakes gives 8 cakes and gives up 20 loaves.
- In an exam, finish the sentence: "The opportunity cost of buying the van is the benefit the business would have had from the new oven."

## The three sectors

| Sector | Activity | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Extracts or grows natural resources | Farming, mining, fishing, forestry |
| Secondary | Makes, processes or builds | Manufacturing, baking, construction |
| Tertiary | Provides services | Retail, banking, transport, education |

**Interdependence:** the secondary sector needs raw materials from the primary sector, the tertiary sector sells and moves what the other two produce, and all three use banks, insurers and transport. If one stage fails, the others suffer.

## Entrepreneurs

**Characteristics:** hard-working, innovative, organised, willing to take a risk.

**Possible objectives:**

- be their own boss
- flexible working hours
- pursue an interest
- earn more money
- identify a gap in the market
- dissatisfaction with current job.

Link motive to decisions: someone who wants flexible hours may stay small, whereas someone who wants more money may aim to grow.

## Why the business environment keeps changing

| Cause of change | Example effect |
|---|---|
| Technology | Online ordering opens new markets; machines replace some jobs |
| Economic situation | Falling incomes reduce demand for non-essential goods |
| Legislation | New safety or pay laws raise costs |
| Environmental expectations | Customers expect less packaging and lower pollution |

A business that fails to adapt can lose customers to rivals that do.

## Exam traps

- Retail is **tertiary**, not secondary.
- A business does not have to aim for profit; not-for-profit organisations are businesses too.
- Capital means machinery and equipment as well as money.
- Do not write "opportunity cost is what you spend". It is what you give up.
- Do not name a characteristic without saying why it helps the entrepreneur.
- For "dynamic", name the cause of change and its effect.

## Model answer outline: a 6-mark "analyse" question

Question style: "Analyse why a new bakery's owner might be called an entrepreneur."

1. **Point:** she is willing to take a risk. **Evidence:** she has spent her savings on an oven. **Effect:** if the bakery fails she could lose the money, which is what makes her an entrepreneur and not just an employee.
2. **Point:** she is innovative. **Evidence:** she sells a flavour no other bakery in town offers. **Effect:** this fills a gap in the market, so she may attract customers from rivals.
3. **Point:** she is organised. **Evidence:** she plans orders, staff rotas and deliveries. **Effect:** the business runs smoothly, so customers get fresh bread on time.

Use the same three steps (point, evidence, effect) for every explain or analyse answer in this section.

## Revision checklist

- I can state five reasons for starting a business.
- I can give an example of a good, a service, a need and a want.
- I can name the four factors of production and give an example of each.
- I can define opportunity cost and work out an example.
- I can place businesses in the primary, secondary and tertiary sectors and explain their links.
- I can outline four characteristics and six objectives of an entrepreneur.
- I can name four causes of a changing business environment.

## Self-test

1. State two reasons for starting a business.
2. Give one difference between a good and a service.
3. Give one example of a need and one of a want.
4. Name the four factors of production.
5. Define opportunity cost.
6. A garage can fit one car or two motorbikes in a bay for a day. It uses 5 bays for cars. How many motorbikes does it give up?
7. Name the sector a fishing fleet belongs to.
8. Name the sector a bank belongs to.
9. Explain one way the secondary sector depends on the primary sector.
10. State two characteristics of an entrepreneur.
11. Give two reasons why someone might leave a job to start a business.
12. Explain one way technology can change a business.

### Answers

1. Any two: produce goods; supply services; distribute products; fulfil a business opportunity; provide a good or service to others.
2. Goods are physical and can be stored; services are activities that are produced and used at the same time.
3. Need: food, water, shelter or clothing. Want: a games console, designer shoes or a holiday.
4. Land, labour, capital, enterprise.
5. The value of the next best alternative given up when a choice is made.
6. Each bay used for a car gives up 2 motorbikes, so 5 x 2 = **10 motorbikes**.
7. Primary.
8. Tertiary.
9. Manufacturers use raw materials from farms, mines or forests, so a poor harvest or a supply problem stops or slows production.
10. Any two: hard-working; innovative; organised; willing to take a risk.
11. Any two: to be their own boss; flexible working hours; to earn more money; dissatisfaction with their current job; to pursue an interest.
12. For example, online selling lets a business reach customers far away without opening new shops, which can raise sales.
