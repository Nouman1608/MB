These questions cover sub-topic 5.3, Managing biodiversity, of the 2027-2029 Cambridge O Level
Environmental Management (5014) syllabus: the ten strategies for conserving the biodiversity and genetic
resources of natural ecosystems, and their benefits and limitations. The contexts are a coastal fishery, a
divided forest reserve and an endangered bird.

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by *genetic resources*. **[1]**

**2.** Name the **three** threat levels used to classify organisms in the syllabus. **[1]**

**3.** State **one** way in which a seed bank keeps seeds alive for many years. **[1]**

**4.** Define *wildlife corridor*. **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** A coastal fishery introduces sustainable harvesting of a fish species. The table shows the estimated
stock. *The data are invented for practice.*

| Year | Stock / tonnes |
|---|---|
| 2016 | 5 000 |
| 2020 | 4 200 |
| 2024 | 4 830 |

(a) Calculate the percentage change in stock between 2020 and 2024. **[2]**
(b) Describe **two** measures the fishery could use to harvest sustainably. **[2]**
(c) Suggest **one** reason why the stock might not recover even with these measures. **[1]**

**6.** A forest reserve has been split into two parts by a new highway. A small population of forest deer
lives in each part.
(a) Explain why the two small populations are at risk of genetic depletion. **[2]**
(b) Describe how a wildlife corridor could reduce this risk. **[2]**
(c) State **one** limitation of using a corridor here. **[1]**

**7.** Describe how zoos and captive breeding can help conserve an endangered species. **[3]**

**8.** Explain why international cooperation is needed to stop the illegal animal trade. **[2]**

**9.** Describe how the regulation of trade in vulnerable and endangered species works. **[2]**

**10.** A bird species is classified as endangered. Its population is 900 and falling by about 10% a year.
(a) Calculate the expected population after **two** years if this rate continues. **[2]**
(b) Explain how classifying the bird as endangered can help it. **[2]**

**11.** Explain **two** ways sustainable forestry differs from clear-felling in its effect on biodiversity. **[4]**

**12.** Describe how ecotourism can give local people a reason to protect wildlife. **[2]**

## Section C

**13.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of national parks and of zoos and captive breeding for conserving
biodiversity. **[6]**

**14.** "Laws are less effective than giving local people an income from wildlife." Discuss this view with
reference to regulation of trade, sustainable harvesting and ecotourism. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The genes carried by wild species and varieties that may be useful in future, for example for crops or
medicines. **[1]**

**2.** Threatened, endangered and extinct (all three needed). **[1]**

**3.** Seeds are dried, or seeds are stored at low temperature. **[1]**

**4.** A strip of habitat that links two or more separated areas of habitat. **[1]**

**5.** (a) 4 830 - 4 200 = 630 tonnes **[1]**; 630 / 4 200 x 100 = **+15%** (an increase) **[1]**.
(b) Any two: catch quotas **[1]**; closed seasons during breeding **[1]**; minimum size limits or larger mesh
nets so young fish escape **[1]**; protected no-fishing areas **[1]**. **[max 2]**
(c) Any one: rules are broken or not enforced; illegal fishing by outside boats; pollution or habitat
damage; the quota is set too high because data are unreliable. **[1]**

**6.** (a) Each population is small and isolated, so animals can only breed within their own group **[1]**;
genetic variety is lost over generations, making them less able to adapt to disease or change **[1]**.
(b) A strip of forest habitat, for example over or under the highway, links the two parts **[1]**, so deer can
move between them, breed and mix their genes **[1]**.
(c) Any one: it is costly to build; deer may still be killed by traffic; poachers may use it; it may be too
narrow to be used. **[1]**

**7.** Endangered animals are kept safe from threats such as poaching **[1]**; they are bred to increase
numbers **[1]**; offspring can be released into the wild where the habitat is protected, or kept as a back-up
population (also research and education) **[1]**.

**8.** Animals and their parts are smuggled across borders, so one country cannot stop the trade alone **[1]**;
countries must share information and enforce laws at both the source and the destination **[1]**.

**9.** Trade in listed species is banned or allowed only with official permits **[1]**, checked at borders, so
harvesting for sale is reduced (for example, under CITES) **[1]**.

**10.** (a) After one year: 900 x 0.9 = 810 **[1]**; after two years: 810 x 0.9 = **729** **[1]**.
(b) The classification shows the species is at high risk of extinction **[1]**, so it can be given legal
protection, trade controls or conservation funding as a priority **[1]**.

**11.** Sustainable forestry fells only selected trees, so most of the canopy and habitat remain **[1]**, and
species can continue to live there **[1]**. It includes replanting and leaving mature seed trees **[1]**, so the
forest regenerates and genetic variety is kept, whereas clear-felling removes the whole habitat **[1]**.

**12.** Visitors pay to see living wildlife, bringing jobs and income as guides, in lodges or selling goods
**[1]**, so the wildlife is worth more alive than poached or its habitat cleared **[1]**.

**13.** **[6]** One mark per point, up to six, with both strategies addressed. National parks: protect whole
ecosystems and all their species **[1]**; species keep evolving in their habitat **[1]**; but they are costly to
patrol and boundaries are ignored by poachers **[1]**, and they may cause conflict with local people **[1]**.
Zoos and captive breeding: can save species close to extinction **[1]**; but they hold only small numbers with
limited genetic variety **[1]**, and released animals need a habitat to return to **[1]**. Conclusion: parks
conserve more biodiversity, with captive breeding as a back-up for the most endangered **[1]**. **[max 6]**

**14.** **[6]** One mark per point, up to six. Regulation of trade: bans and permits reduce demand-driven
harvesting **[1]**, but smuggling and forged permits continue where enforcement is weak **[1]**. Sustainable
harvesting: gives people food and income from a resource they want to keep **[1]**, but limits can be
exceeded if people are poor or data are poor **[1]**. Ecotourism: makes living wildlife valuable **[1]**, but
income depends on visitor numbers and can fall **[1]**. Conclusion: income schemes win local support, but
they work best alongside laws, so neither works fully alone **[1]**. **[max 6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying a strategy "protects the species" without saying how.
- Giving zoos or seed banks credit for protecting habitats.
- Calculating a 10% yearly fall as 20% over two years. Each year's fall is 10% of the new, smaller number.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many conservation strategies does 5.3 list?**
Ten, from sustainable harvesting of wild species to classifying organisms by threat level.

**Do I need to know named conservation projects?**
The 2027-2029 syllabus does not name case studies, so a well-explained general example is enough, but a
specific example you know well can make an answer more convincing.
