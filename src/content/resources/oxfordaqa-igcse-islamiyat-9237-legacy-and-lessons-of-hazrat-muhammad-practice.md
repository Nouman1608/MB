## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for explaining it. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

**1.** Arafat [1].

**2.** Al-Amin (the trustworthy) [1]. Accept al-Sadiq (the truthful).

**3.** Any two, [1] each: life, property and honour are sacred; trusts must be returned; interest is cancelled; blood feuds are ended; women must be treated well; no Arab is better than a non-Arab except by taqwa; Muslims are brothers; hold fast to the Qur'an; pass the message on.

**4. (a)** Any one [1]: do not say "uff" to them and lower to them the wing of humility (Qur'an 17:23-24); the Hadith in which the Prophet (pbuh) said "your mother", three times, then "your father" when asked who most deserved good company; Qur'an 31:14-15.

**(b)** One way identified [1] and explained [2].

- **Caring for their needs** [1]: providing food, a home, medical care and company [1], because they once cared for the child when it was weak and helpless [1].
- **Patience in speech** [1]: not showing irritation or raising the voice [1], which is the meaning of not saying "uff" [1].
- **Prayer for them** [1]: asking Allah to have mercy on them [1], since the Qur'an teaches Muslims to make this prayer [1].

**5.** For each of two ways: way identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Equal punishment** [1]: he refused to excuse a woman from a noble tribe who had stolen [1], and said he would apply the same rule to his own daughter [1].
- **Cancelling his own family's claims** [1]: in the Final Sermon he began the cancelling of interest and blood feuds with his own relatives [1].
- **Amnesty at Makkah** [1]: he did not take revenge on the people who had persecuted him [1], showing justice joined with mercy.
- **Equality** [1]: the Final Sermon said that no race is better than another except by taqwa [1].

**6.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Trust in society** [1]: people can trade, make promises and live together safely when they can rely on each other [1].
- **Following the Prophet's (pbuh) example** [1]: he was known as al-Amin even by his opponents [1].
- **A route to Paradise** [1]: the Hadith says that truthfulness leads to righteousness and righteousness to Paradise [1].
- **Return of trusts** [1]: the Final Sermon commands that trusts be returned to their owners [1].

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **Opposition in Makkah** [1]: he faced insults, boycott and persecution for years and needed endurance to keep calling people [1].
- **Bereavement** [1]: he lost Khadijah (RA), Abu Talib and his infant son Ibrahim, and kept his faith [1].
- **A model for believers** [1]: his patience shows Muslims how to meet hardship without anger [1].
- **Success at the right time** [1]: patience at Hudaibiyah and Makkah brought greater results than haste [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Indeed, Allah is with the patient" (Qur'an 2:153) [2].
- "The patient will be given their reward without account" (Qur'an 39:10) [2].
- The Hadith that all of the believer's affairs are good: patient in hardship, thankful in ease [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Reasons (AO1):

- **Rights and equality** [1]: it teaches that all people are equal except in taqwa, which guides how Muslims treat each other [1].
- **Protection of life and property** [1]: it declares them sacred, a basis for justice and peace [1].
- **Guidance to hold fast to** [1]: it directs Muslims to the Qur'an and Sunnah [1].
- **A reminder of duty** [1]: it asks every Muslim to pass the message on [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- The Final Sermon declared that life, property and honour are as sacred as the day, the month and the city of the pilgrimage [2].
- Qur'an 49:13: the most honoured in the sight of Allah is the most righteous [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the Qur'an and Sunnah are the sources for all Muslims; once the religion is complete, Muslims do not need new revelation; the verse came at the end of his life, after the community was secure.
- **Against the statement:** behaviour and values, such as justice, honesty, patience and kindness to parents, are also his legacy; the Ummah he built is a legacy; a complete religion is useful only if it is lived.
- **Balanced view:** the completed message and the lived example belong together; one tells Muslims what to do and the other shows how.

*Use of the passage*

- "I have perfected for you your religion" shows the message is complete.
- "Completed My favour upon you" shows it is a blessing that calls for gratitude.
- "Fear them not, but fear Me" shows that Muslims are accountable to Allah alone.
- "Despaired of [defeating] your religion" shows that the community had become secure.

Level 4 answers use the passage directly, develop points on both sides with named teachings, and reach a reasoned judgement.
