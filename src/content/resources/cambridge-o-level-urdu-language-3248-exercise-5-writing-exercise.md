This guide covers **Exercise 5** of Cambridge O Level Second Language Urdu 3248 Paper 1 Reading and
Writing, the **Writing exercise**. The syllabus says candidates "write a short piece of functional
prose such as an email, of approximately 150 words. A stimulus of short prompts and/or pictures will
be printed in the question paper. A purpose, format and audience for the writing will be specified."
It is worth **14 marks**, the most of any exercise on the paper. For the paper as a whole, see the
[O Level Urdu Paper 1 study guide](/resources/o-level-cambridge-urdu-first-and-second-language/).

## What is assessed

Exercise 5 tests all five writing objectives and nothing else:

| Objective | Syllabus wording | In a 150-word email |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | communicate information/ideas/opinions/key points clearly, accurately and effectively | every prompt answered, clearly |
| W2 | organise ideas into coherent paragraphs using a range of appropriate linking devices | short paragraphs, joined with varied connectives |
| W3 | manipulate the target language accurately to use appropriate examples of grammatical structures and vocabulary in context | tenses, agreement, a few richer structures |
| W4 | show control of punctuation and spelling | Urdu full stop (۔), comma (،), question mark (؟); correct spelling |
| W5 | use appropriate register and style/format for the given purpose and audience | the right greeting, address forms and closing |

The syllabus does not publish how the 14 marks divide between these objectives.

## Reading the brief: purpose, format, audience

Before writing, answer three questions in the margin.

1. **Purpose (*مقصد*)**: am I inviting, thanking, apologising, informing, requesting, describing or
   recommending? Many tasks combine two.
2. **Format (*طرزِ تحریر*)**: an email, a letter, a blog post, a short article? Each has a shape:
   an email has a subject line and a greeting; a blog has a title and a personal voice.
3. **Audience (*مخاطب*)**: a friend, a relative, a teacher, a principal, readers of a school website?
   The audience decides the register.

The skills content of the syllabus names the range: "a summary, an informal letter/email, an article,
a blog, a report or review". Exercise 5 is the short, functional end of that range.

## Covering the prompts

The stimulus gives short prompts and/or pictures. Treat each prompt as a paragraph or a clear section
of one. A missing prompt means a missing part of the message, which W1 cannot reward. If there are
pictures, use them for content: what they show is an invitation to describe, explain or give an
opinion, and naming two or three details from a picture gives your writing substance.

With about 150 words and usually three prompts, aim for roughly 40 words per prompt plus a short
opening and closing.

## Register: two sets of tools

| | Informal (friend, cousin) | Formal (teacher, principal, official) |
|---|---|---|
| Greeting | *پیاری سارہ،* / *عزیز دوست احمد،* | *محترمہ پرنسپل صاحبہ،* / *جناب ...* |
| Opening line | *کیسی ہو؟ امید ہے سب خیریت سے ہو گا۔* | *السلام علیکم۔ امید ہے آپ بخیریت ہوں گے۔* |
| Address | *تم* throughout | *آپ* throughout |
| Verb forms | *آؤ*، *بتاؤ*، *کرنا* | *تشریف لائیے*، *بتائیے*، *فرمائیے* |
| Closing | *تمھاری دوست، عائشہ* / *جلد جواب دینا!* | *والسلام، آپ کا شاگرد ...* / *شکریہ کے ساتھ* |

Choose once and hold it. Moving from *تم* to *آپ* within one email is the most common register fault.
Informal does not mean careless: spelling and grammar are assessed in the same way.

## Linking devices that fit 150 words

- To add: *اس کے علاوہ*، *ساتھ ہی*، *یہی نہیں بلکہ*
- To explain: *کیونکہ*، *اس لیے کہ*، *اسی وجہ سے*
- To contrast: *لیکن*، *البتہ*، *تاہم*
- To sequence: *سب سے پہلے*، *اس کے بعد*، *آخر میں*
- To conclude: *بس یہی کہنا تھا*، *امید ہے*

Two or three different connectives used naturally are enough to show range in a short piece.

## Worked example

Task (original): *آپ کے شہر میں اگلے ہفتے ایک ثقافتی میلہ ہو رہا ہے۔ اپنے دوست کو تقریباً 150 الفاظ
کی ای میل لکھیے۔ ای میل میں: میلے کے بارے میں بتائیے؛ اسے ساتھ چلنے کی دعوت دیجیے؛ بتائیے کہ آپ وہاں
کیا کرنا چاہتے ہیں۔* (Purpose: inform and invite. Format: email. Audience: a friend, informal.)

> **موضوع:** اگلے ہفتے میلہ — ساتھ چلو گی؟
>
> پیاری مریم،
>
> کیسی ہو؟ امتحان ختم ہونے کی بہت مبارک ہو! اب ذرا آرام اور مزے کا وقت ہے، اس لیے ایک خوش خبری سنو۔
> اگلے ہفتے ہمارے شہر کے بڑے پارک میں تین دن کا ثقافتی میلہ لگ رہا ہے۔ وہاں ملک بھر سے دستکار آئیں گے،
> لوک موسیقی ہو گی اور طرح طرح کے کھانوں کے اسٹال بھی ہوں گے۔
>
> کیا تم ہفتے کی شام میرے ساتھ چلو گی؟ ابو ہمیں گاڑی میں چھوڑ دیں گے اور واپسی پر لے بھی آئیں گے، اس
> لیے فکر کی کوئی بات نہیں۔
>
> میں تو سب سے پہلے مٹی کے برتن بنانے کی ورکشاپ میں حصہ لینا چاہتی ہوں۔ اس کے بعد ہم سندھی اجرک والے
> اسٹال پر جائیں گے، کیونکہ امی کے لیے ایک تحفہ خریدنا ہے۔ آخر میں گول گپے تو ضرور کھائیں گے!
>
> جلدی جواب دینا۔
>
> تمھاری دوست،
> ثنا

*Commentary:* each prompt has its own paragraph; the subject line and closing fit an informal email;
*تم* is held throughout; *اس لیے*، *سب سے پہلے*، *اس کے بعد*، *کیونکہ*، *آخر میں* link and sequence
the ideas; the length is close to 150 words.

**The same purpose, formal audience.** If the task asked you to invite the principal to open a
school stall at the festival, the paragraph of invitation would change to: *ہماری خواہش ہے کہ آپ
ہفتے کی شام تشریف لا کر ہمارے اسٹال کا افتتاح فرمائیں۔ آپ کی آمد ہمارے لیے باعثِ عزت ہوگی۔* Content
is similar; vocabulary, verb forms and tone are entirely different.

## Common mistakes

- **Missing a prompt**, often the last one, written in a hurry.
- **Wrong or mixed register**: *تم* to a teacher, or a formal tone to a close friend.
- **No format features**: an email with no greeting or closing, a blog with no title.
- **Far over the length.** "Approximately 150 words" gives a little freedom; 250 words means more
  errors and less checking time.
- **Learned paragraphs** that do not fit the task's details.
- **Agreement errors** in a short piece stand out: *میلہ لگ رہی ہے* for *لگ رہا ہے*.
- **English punctuation in Urdu**: a full stop dot instead of *۔*, or no question mark.

## Exam technique

- Spend two minutes on purpose, format, audience and a one-line plan per prompt.
- Write the greeting and closing first so the register is fixed from the start.
- Count paragraphs against prompts before you finish.
- Protect about 25 minutes for this exercise: it carries 14 of the paper's 50 marks, more than any
  other, so do not let the reading exercises run over.
- Leave three or four minutes to check gender and number agreement, *نے*, and punctuation.
