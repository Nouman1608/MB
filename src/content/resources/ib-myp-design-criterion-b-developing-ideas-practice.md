These questions practise Criterion B (Developing ideas) for IB Middle Years Programme Design, aligned to the International Baccalaureate Organization, Middle Years Programme Subject Brief -- *Design*, from 2014. The brief describes Criterion B as writing a detailed specification that drives the development of a solution, then presenting the solution. MYP has no SL/HL split; the set suits MYP years 4 and 5 and the design ePortfolio.

MYP has no prescribed content list -- schools design their own units -- and your teacher will share the task-specific clarifications. Real MYP work is judged against criterion level descriptors (eight achievement levels, 1-8, in four bands), so the `[1]` points below are a revision aid, not IB marks. All clients, products and prices are fictional.

Learn the skills first with the [study guide](/resources/ib-myp-design-criterion-b-developing-ideas/) and [revision notes](/resources/ib-myp-design-criterion-b-developing-ideas-revision-notes/). See also the [course hub](/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-design/) and [printable checklist](/checklists/ib/ib-myp/myp-design/).

## Questions

**1.** (Criterion B) State the two things the MYP Design subject brief says you do in Criterion B. **[2]**

**2.** (Criterion B) A student is designing a water-bottle holder for a bicycle frame. Rewrite each point so it can be tested: "It must be strong." "It must fit the bike." "It must be cheap." **[3]**

**3.** (Criterion B) Explain the difference between a design brief and a design specification. **[2]**

**4.** (Criterion B) A specification for a desk lamp contains: (i) height 350-400 mm; (ii) looks modern; (iii) runs on a 5 V USB supply; (iv) easy to adjust; (v) material cost £12 or less.

**(a)** Identify the two points that cannot be tested as written. **[2]**
**(b)** Rewrite one of them so it can be tested. **[1]**

**5.** (Criterion B) A student compares three phone-stand ideas. Cost is an essential point because the client's budget is fixed.

| Criterion (weight) | X folding plywood | Y bent acrylic | Z 3D-printed hinge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stability (3) | 4 | 3 | 5 |
| Adjustable viewing angle (2) | 3 | 2 | 5 |
| Cost (2) | 5 | 4 | 2 |
| Appearance (1) | 3 | 5 | 4 |

**(a)** Calculate the weighted total for each idea and state the maximum possible. **[3]**
**(b)** Z's cost score of 2 means it is over the client's budget. Explain whether Z should be chosen. **[2]**

**6.** (Criterion B) A storage box measures 360 mm long, 240 mm wide and 150 mm high. You will draw the front view, plan and right-hand end view in third-angle projection on A4 landscape with a usable area of 277 mm × 190 mm and 20 mm gaps between views.

**(a)** Show that a scale of 1:2 does not fit across the sheet. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that a scale of 1:5 fits in both directions. **[2]**
**(c)** State how long a 45 mm hole is drawn at 1:5, and what dimension you write on it. **[1]**

**7.** (Criterion B) For a lunchbox project, a student presents three ideas: a rectangular plastic box with a clip lid in blue, the same box in green, and the same box with rounded corners. Explain why this is not a range of ideas and suggest two ideas that would make it one. **[4]**

**8.** (Criterion B) A student has a £20 material budget and 6 hours of workshop time.

| Idea | Material cost | Estimated making time |
|---|---|---|
| I | £14.80 | 5.0 hours |
| II | £23.50 | 4.0 hours |
| III | £12.40 | 9.5 hours |

**(a)** Identify which ideas are feasible, giving a reason for each rejected idea. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest one change that could make Idea III feasible. **[1]**

**9.** (Criterion B, digital design) A student is designing a revision-quiz app for Year 7 pupils.

**(a)** Write two measurable specification points for the app. **[2]**
**(b)** Name two planning diagrams the student should produce, and state what each one shows. **[4]**

**10.** (Criterion B) A planning drawing of a wooden shelf is drawn in third-angle projection.

**(a)** State where the plan and the right-hand end view are placed relative to the front view. **[2]**
**(b)** State two items, other than the title, that should appear in the title block. **[2]**

**11.** (Criterion B, extended) A fictional primary school wants a display stand for pupils' clay models in a narrow corridor. The corridor is 1,500 mm wide and must keep at least 1,200 mm clear for safety, and the wall is 1,500 mm long between two doors.

**(a)** Write four measurable specification points, each with a reason. **[4]**
**(b)** Outline two genuinely different ideas. **[2]**
**(c)** Justify choosing one of them against your specification. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Write a detailed specification that drives the development of a solution [1]; present the solution [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Make a design" is Criterion C, so it does not earn the second point.

**2.** Example answers: holds a 750 ml full bottle without dropping it over 5 km of rough track [1]; fits frame tubes of 28-35 mm diameter [1]; material cost £6 or less [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Each rewrite needs a number with a unit or a yes/no test; "very strong" still earns nothing.

**3.** The brief is a short statement of the problem, client and goal [1]; the specification is a list of measurable requirements the solution must meet, used to judge ideas and test the product [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The second point needs the word "measurable" or "testable", not just "more detailed".

**4. (a)** (ii) "looks modern" [1] and (iv) "easy to adjust" [1].
**(b)** Example: the lamp head can be moved to a new angle with one hand in under 3 seconds [1].
*Examiner insight:* A rewrite of (ii) needs a user-rating test, for example at least 4 out of 5 from five users; "sleek" is still an opinion.

**5. (a)** X = 3×4 + 2×3 + 2×5 + 1×3 = **31**; Y = 3×3 + 2×2 + 2×4 + 1×5 = **26** [1]; Z = 3×5 + 2×5 + 2×2 + 1×4 = **33** [1]; maximum = 5 × (3 + 2 + 2 + 1) = **40** [1].
**(b)** No: Z has the highest total but fails an essential point, and a matrix total cannot outweigh a failed essential requirement [1]. X should be chosen unless Z can be redesigned within budget, for example by printing less of it [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b) the second point is only earned by naming a better choice or a fix, not by restating that Z is expensive.

**6. (a)** At 1:2 the front view is 180 mm and the end view 120 mm [1]; 180 + 20 + 120 = 320 mm, which is more than 277 mm [1].
**(b)** Across: 72 + 20 + 48 = 140 mm, within 277 mm [1]; down: 30 + 20 + 48 = 98 mm, within 190 mm [1].
**(c)** Drawn 9 mm; dimensioned as **45** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Writing the drawn size (9) as the dimension on a scaled drawing is wrong: dimensions always give real sizes.

**7.** All three share one form, material and closing method, so they are variants of one idea [1]; only colour or corner shape changes [1]. Example alternatives: a folding fabric wrap with a hook-and-loop fastening [1]; a stacking set of stainless tins held by a strap [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each suggested idea earns credit only if its material, form or working principle differs from the box.

**8. (a)** Idea I is feasible: £14.80 ≤ £20 and 5.0 h ≤ 6 h [1]. II is rejected because £23.50 is over budget [1]. III is rejected because 9.5 h is over the time available [1].
**(b)** Example: replace hand-cut joints with a simpler joint or laser-cut parts to reduce making time below 6 hours [1].
*Examiner insight:* A rejection needs the actual comparison (cost or time against the limit), not just "too hard".

**9. (a)** Examples: a new user starts a quiz in 3 taps or fewer [1]; each question screen loads in under 2 seconds on a school tablet [1].
**(b)** Wireframes [1] show the layout of each screen, buttons and text areas [1]. A flowchart or site map [1] shows how the user moves between screens [1].
*Examiner insight:* Naming "a mock-up" without saying what it shows earns only the naming point.

**10. (a)** The plan is above the front view [1]; the right-hand end view is to the right of the front view [1].
**(b)** Any two: scale; projection method or symbol; units ("All dimensions in mm"); name; date [1] [1].
*Examiner insight:* Placing the plan below the front view is first-angle layout, so it loses the first point if you said third angle.

**11. (a)** Indicative points, one for each measurable point with a reason: projects no more than 300 mm from the wall, so the corridor stays at least 1,200 mm wide [1]; overall width 1,500 mm or less to fit between the doors [1]; holds at least 20 models of up to 0.5 kg each [1]; no edge sharper than a 3 mm radius because young pupils pass it [1].
**(b)** Example: wall-mounted slatted shelves in birch plywood [1]; a shallow glass-fronted wall cabinet with adjustable shelves [1].
**(c)** Choice stated with scores or clear comparison, for example shelves chosen [1]; linked to specification points (depth 250 mm meets the 300 mm limit; width 1,400 mm fits between doors) [1]; comparison with the rejected idea (cabinet safer for models but over budget and heavier to fix) [1]; feedback or a weakness to develop (staff asked for a lip so models cannot be knocked off; will add a 20 mm front rail) [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) earns the comparison point only if the rejected idea is named with a specific reason tied to the specification.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Specification points built on adjectives ("strong", "modern", "easy") with no target or test.
- Points with no reason linking back to Criterion A research.
- Idea sets that are colour or size variants of one concept.
- Feasibility claims with no comparison against budget, time, tools or skills.
- Decision matrix totals quoted without checking essential points.
- Arithmetic slips in weighted totals: multiply each score by its weight before adding.
- Scaled drawings dimensioned with drawn sizes instead of real sizes.
- Third-angle and first-angle layouts mixed up, or no projection symbol shown.
- Digital projects that show a polished screen but no navigation flowchart.
- Justifications that praise the chosen idea without saying why the others were weaker.
