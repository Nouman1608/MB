**How to use this set.** In Paper 2, Question 1 is compulsory: four of the twenty Hadiths in the
syllabus appendix are printed in Arabic and English, and you write about any two. Part (a) asks for
their teachings about what Muslims believe **[4]** and part (b) how Muslims can put them into action
**[4]**. Section A checks your knowledge of individual Hadiths; Section B gives five exam-style pairs.
The English wording is the syllabus translation. In the exam each Hadith is also printed in Arabic, so
learn its key Arabic words.

## Section A — Short questions

**1.** The set Hadiths are divided into two groups. Name the groups and give **one** Hadith from each.
**[4]**

**2.** In the Hadith of the man who asked whether obligatory prayer, fasting in Ramadan and keeping to
the lawful and forbidden would take him to paradise, the Prophet (pbuh) answered "Yes". Explain what
this teaches about the religion. **[3]**

**3.** Explain why the Hadith about martyrs ("he who dies a natural death in the way of Allah is a
martyr…") widens the meaning of martyrdom. **[3]**

**4.** "I and the man who brings up an orphan will be in paradise like this", said while holding up
the index and middle fingers. What does the gesture show about caring for orphans? **[2]**

**5.** Explain the comparison in "He who studies the Qur'an is like the owner of tethered camels."
**[3]**

**6.** "Modesty produces nothing but good." Explain what is meant by modesty (*haya*) here. **[2]**

## Answers

**How Section B is marked.** Question 1 is marked by levels of response: part (a) Level 4 = 4,
Level 3 = 3, Level 2 = 2, Level 1 = 1; part (b) Level 2 = 3–4, Level 1 = 1–2. The two Hadiths are
read together. The credit guides below show one way to reach each tariff: in (a), for each Hadith the
central teaching [1] developed with a link to belief or a supporting reference [1]; in (b), for each
Hadith one practical action explained [2]. Other valid points of the same quality earn the same
credit.

**1.** **Individual conduct** [1], for example "Modesty produces nothing but good" [1]; **life in the
community** [1], for example "The believers are like a single man" [1]. Accept any correctly placed
Hadith.

**2.** Islam is built on obligatory duties that ordinary people can manage [1]; a person who performs
them sincerely and keeps to the limits of lawful and forbidden (*halal* and *haram*) is promised
paradise [1], so the religion is not a burden reserved for a few, matching "God does not burden a soul
beyond its capacity" (Sura 2.286) [1].

**3.** The Companions thought only those killed fighting were martyrs [1]. The Prophet (pbuh) included
those who die naturally, or of plague or cholera, while "in the way of Allah" [1], so the reward
depends on a life devoted to God and gives hope and comfort to families of believers who die of
illness [1].

**4.** The two fingers held side by side show that the one who raises an orphan will be as close to
the Prophet (pbuh) in paradise as the fingers are to each other [1]; the Prophet, himself an orphan,
places this care among the highest of good deeds [1].

**5.** A camel left untied wanders away, just as Qur'an knowledge fades if it is not revised [1]. The
owner who attends to his camels keeps them, so the one who recites regularly keeps what he has learned
[1]. Muslims should therefore recite and revise the Qur'an they have memorised [1].

**6.** *Haya* is a sense of shame and decency that holds a person back from wrongdoing [1]; because it
stops bad actions and encourages respect, it can only lead to good [1].

**7. (a)** **First Hadith:** faith is not complete without wanting good for others as for oneself
(*hatta yuhibba li-akhihi*) [1]; this reflects the Qur'an's teaching that "the believers are a single
brotherhood" (Sura 49.10) [1]. **Second Hadith:** God judges by intention and action, not appearance or
wealth (*qulub* and *a'mal*) [1]; Muslims believe that sincere intention gives deeds their value [1].
**(b)** A student who wants top marks helps a friend revise rather than hiding notes, and is glad
when others succeed [2]. Muslims avoid judging people by clothes, looks or money, and check their own
intentions so that prayer and charity are done for God rather than for praise [2].

**8. (a)** **First Hadith:** every part of the body owes thanks to God daily, and charity (*sadaqa*)
includes justice, help, a kind word, walking to prayer and removing harm [1]; Muslims believe every
good act, however small, is rewarded [1]. **Second Hadith:** honest work is honoured, and food earned
by one's own effort is the best [1]; the Prophets, such as Dawud, worked with their hands, so earning
a lawful living is part of faith [1].
**(b)** Muslims settle quarrels between friends fairly, carry shopping for elderly neighbours, speak
kindly and pick up litter or broken glass from the road [2]. Young Muslims take honest part-time jobs
rather than relying on others or cheating, and avoid income from forbidden sources [2].

**9. (a)** **First Hadith:** every Muslim is responsible for opposing wrong according to ability:
action, then speech, then at least hating it in the heart [1]; faith is shown by refusing to accept
injustice [1]. **Second Hadith:** leaders and teachers should be gentle and make religion attractive
[1], following the Qur'an's praise of the Prophet's gentleness, without which people "would have broken
away" (Sura 3.159) [1].
**(b)** A Muslim who sees bullying intervenes if safe, reports it or speaks up, and at least refuses to
join in or share abusive posts [2]. Parents, teachers and community leaders explain Islam patiently,
encourage rather than shame new learners, and make the mosque welcoming [2].

**10. (a)** **First Hadith:** even a tiny amount of faith saves from hell, while even a tiny amount of
pride (*kibr*) keeps a person from paradise [1]; pride, like Iblis's refusal to bow, is among the
gravest sins [1]. **Second Hadith:** this world restricts the believer, who restrains desires for
God's sake, while the true reward is in the Hereafter [1]; for the unbeliever this life is the only
pleasure he will have [1].
**(b)** Muslims stay humble about success, avoid boasting about wealth, grades or looks, and accept
correction [2]. They are patient with hardship, choose what is lawful even when it is harder, and do
not envy people who live without limits [2].

**11. (a)** **First Hadith:** belief in God and the Last Day must show in speech and in generosity to
neighbours and guests [1]; Muslims believe they are accountable for every word [1]. **Second Hadith:**
receiving God's mercy depends on showing mercy (*rahma*) to others [1]; God is the Most Merciful, and
the Prophet was "a mercy for all creatures" (Sura 21.107) [1].
**(b)** Muslims avoid gossip, lies and insults, online and offline, and stay silent rather than say
something hurtful; they share food with neighbours and welcome guests warmly [2]. They show mercy to
the weak, forgive people who wrong them, and treat animals kindly [2].

**12. (a)** **First Hadith:** the best people are believers who strive (*jihad*) in God's way with
both themselves and their wealth [1]; Muslims believe effort and sacrifice for God's cause raise a
person's rank (Sura 61.11) [1]. **Second Hadith:** caring for widows and the poor is rewarded like
striving in God's way, night prayer or fasting [1]; Muslims believe service to people is itself
worship, as the Qur'an lists giving to orphans and the needy as true righteousness (Sura 2.177) [1].
**(b)** Muslims give time to volunteering, teaching and community work, and give money to good causes
such as hospitals and schools [2]. They help widows and poor families with shopping, paperwork, school
costs or regular support, so that no household in the community is left alone [2].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving an action in part (a) or repeating the teaching in part (b).
- Writing about one Hadith only, or about three or four.
- General applications ("be good to people") instead of specific modern actions.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need the narrator's name for each Hadith?**
No. The syllabus asks for the teachings about belief and action; background is useful only where it
explains the teaching, as with Abu Musa and Mu'adh being sent to Yemen.

**Can I use the same application for two Hadiths?**
Avoid it: each Hadith carries half the marks of each part, so give each its own action.
