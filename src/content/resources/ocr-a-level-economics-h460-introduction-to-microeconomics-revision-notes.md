These notes condense **Introduction to microeconomics** (topics 1.1 The economic problem, 1.2 The allocation of resources and 1.3 Opportunity cost) from Component 01 of the OCR *A Level Economics (H460)* specification, Version 3.0 (June 2026). The evaluation arguments are developed in the [introduction to microeconomics study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-economics-h460-introduction-to-microeconomics/), and the [introduction to microeconomics practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-economics-h460-introduction-to-microeconomics-practice/) give exam-style work. All figures are invented.

## What you must be able to do

| Specification wording | In one line |
|---|---|
| Economic goods and free goods | Economic goods use scarce resources and have an opportunity cost; free goods do not |
| The economic problem: scarcity, choice, needs and wants | Unlimited wants, limited resources, so choices are forced |
| Normative and positive statements | Value judgement versus testable claim |
| The role of economic agents | Households, firms and government make and influence choices |
| Factors of production and their rewards | Land (rent), labour (wages), capital (interest), enterprise (profit) |
| Evaluate scarcity and choice; rationality; agents' objectives | Judge how binding scarcity is, where rationality predicts well, and how objectives conflict |
| Incentives; economic systems; productive and allocative efficiency | What motivates agents; how each system answers what, how and for whom |
| Evaluate incentives and allocation in different systems | Size, information, time, side effects; efficiency versus equity |
| Opportunity cost, trade-off and the PPC | Next best alternative forgone; movements and shifts of the PPC |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Scarcity | Wants exceed the resources available to satisfy them |
| Need | Something required for survival, such as food and shelter |
| Want | Something desired but not essential |
| Economic good | A good that uses scarce resources and so has an opportunity cost |
| Free good | A good available without using scarce resources, with zero opportunity cost |
| Positive statement | A statement that can be tested against evidence |
| Normative statement | A statement containing a value judgement |
| Rationality | Weighing costs and benefits to choose the option that best meets an objective |
| Incentive | Something that motivates an agent to act in a particular way |
| Productive efficiency | Producing at the lowest possible cost; on the PPC |
| Allocative efficiency | Producing the mix of goods that best matches society's wants |
| Opportunity cost | The value of the next best alternative forgone |
| Trade-off | Giving up some of one thing to get more of another |

## Evaluation toolkits

**Scarcity and choice**
- Universal, but felt differently by rich and poor agents.
- Eased by technology; tightened by depletion and pollution.
- Free goods can become economic goods as demand grows.
- Good choices need good information.

**Rationality**
- For: testable predictions, a benchmark, individual errors may cancel out in aggregate.
- Against: imperfect information, costly calculation, habit, emotion, concern for others.
- Verdict: strongest for frequent, simple purchases; weakest for rare, complex ones.

**Objectives of agents**
- Households: utility. Firms: profit. Government: social welfare.
- They conflict; competition, regulation and public opinion limit each.

**Incentives**
- Work best when large relative to the cost of changing, well understood, immediate and where alternatives exist.
- Watch for slow responses and unintended consequences.

**Economic systems**

| | Productive efficiency | Allocative efficiency | Equity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market | Strong: competition and profit | Reflects willingness and ability to pay | Weak: low incomes neglected |
| Planned | Weak: poor incentives | Weak: no price signals | Can be strong |
| Mixed | Depends on the balance | Depends on the balance | Adjusted by taxes and spending |

**Opportunity cost**
- Useful: reveals hidden costs, frames choice as comparison, explains specialisation and the PPC.
- Limits: next best option hard to identify and value; subjective; often ignored.

## PPC reminders

- On the curve: productively efficient. Inside: unemployed or misused resources. Outside: unattainable now.
- Movement along: reallocation, showing opportunity cost.
- Outward shift: more or better resources, or better technology. Inward shift: loss of resources, such as a natural disaster.

**Opportunity cost from a PPC (invented).** An economy moves from 40 units of food and 30 units of clothing to 34 food and 33 clothing. The 3 extra units of clothing cost 6 units of food: **2 food per unit of clothing**.

## Exam traps

- "All the alternatives" instead of "the next best alternative".
- Calling a statement normative because it might be wrong; testability is the criterion.
- Treating rationality as all or nothing.
- Asserting that an incentive works without its conditions.
- Mixing up productive and allocative efficiency.
- Showing a shift of the PPC for a reallocation, or a movement for growth.
- Forgetting the reward to enterprise is profit, not wages.

## Self-test

1. Define scarcity.
2. Distinguish between a free good and an economic good.
3. Is "a sugar tax would reduce sugar consumption" positive or normative? Why?
4. State the reward to each factor of production.
5. Give two reasons why rationality may be a weak guide to consumer behaviour.
6. Give one argument that rationality is still a useful assumption.
7. Explain how a firm's objective might conflict with households' objectives.
8. State three conditions that make an incentive more effective.
9. Distinguish between productive and allocative efficiency.
10. A firm uses a building it owns that could be rented for 30,000 cu a year. What is the opportunity cost of using it?
11. On a PPC, output moves from 50 food and 20 machines to 44 food and 22 machines. What is the opportunity cost of one machine?
12. State one limitation of the concept of opportunity cost.

### Answers

1. Wants are unlimited but the resources to satisfy them are limited.
2. An economic good uses scarce resources and has an opportunity cost; a free good does not, so it has no opportunity cost.
3. Positive: it is a claim that can be tested against evidence, whether or not it proves true.
4. Land: rent; labour: wages; capital: interest; enterprise: profit.
5. Any two of: imperfect information; calculation is costly so rules of thumb are used; habit; emotion; concern for others.
6. It produces testable predictions, such as buying less when price rises, and gives a benchmark for best choices.
7. For example, a profit-maximising firm may pollute to cut costs, lowering households' utility.
8. Any three of: large relative to the cost of changing; clear and understood; immediate; alternatives available; hard to abuse.
9. Productive efficiency is producing at lowest cost; allocative efficiency is producing the mix of goods society most wants.
10. The 30,000 cu of rent forgone each year.
11. 6 food for 2 machines, so **3 food per machine**.
12. Any one of: the next best alternative can be hard to identify or value; valuations are subjective; agents may ignore it.
