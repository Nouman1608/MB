## 3.2.5.1 Definitions to learn exactly

- **Transition metal characteristics** (Ti–Cu) arise from an **incomplete d sub-level in atoms or ions**.
- Four properties: **complex formation, coloured ions, variable oxidation state, catalytic activity**.
- **Ligand**: a molecule or ion that forms a co-ordinate bond with a transition metal by donating a pair of electrons.
- **Complex**: a central metal atom or ion surrounded by ligands.
- **Co-ordination number**: the number of co-ordinate bonds to the central metal atom or ion.

## 3.2.5.2 Ligands and substitution

| Ligand | Type | Bonds formed |
|---|---|---|
| H₂O, NH₃, Cl⁻ | monodentate | 1 |
| H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂ (en) | bidentate | 2 |
| C₂O₄²⁻ (ethanedioate) | bidentate | 2 |
| EDTA⁴⁻ | multidentate | 6 |

**Method in steps: predicting a ligand swap**

1. Is the new ligand NH₃ replacing H₂O? Similar size, both uncharged, so **co-ordination number unchanged** (stays 6, octahedral).
2. Is it Cl⁻ replacing H₂O? Cl⁻ is **larger**, so co-ordination number **can fall from 6 to 4** (tetrahedral).
3. Is a bidentate or multidentate ligand replacing monodentate ones? The **chelate effect** makes it favourable.
4. Balance: count ligands out, ligands in, and recompute the complex charge.

Key equations:

```
[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 4H₂O   (incomplete; CN stays 6)
[Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ → [CoCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O            (CN 6 → 4)
[Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ + 4Cl⁻ → [FeCl₄]⁻ + 6H₂O             (CN 6 → 4)
```

**Chelate effect in one line**: ΔH ≈ small (similar co-ordinate bonds broken and made), ΔS positive (more particles), so ΔG = ΔH − TΔS is negative.

**Haem**: iron(II) complex with a multidentate ligand. O₂ forms a co-ordinate bond to Fe(II) for transport in blood. **CO is toxic** because it replaces the O₂ co-ordinately bonded to Fe(II).

## 3.2.5.3 Shapes and isomers

| Shape | Typical ligands | Angle | Isomerism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Octahedral | small: H₂O, NH₃ | 90° | cis–trans (monodentate); optical (bidentate) |
| Tetrahedral | larger: Cl⁻ | 109.5° | — |
| Square planar | e.g. [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] | 90° | cis–trans; cisplatin = cis |
| Linear | [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ | 180° | — |

- Cis–trans isomerism here is a special case of E–Z isomerism.
- Optical isomers of [M(en)₃]ⁿ⁺ are non-superimposable mirror images. Draw both with wedges, as mirror images.

**Must-know distinction**: co-ordination number counts **bonds**, not ligands. [Fe(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻ has three ligands but co-ordination number 6.

## 3.2.5.4 Colour

- Some visible wavelengths are **absorbed**; the rest are **transmitted or reflected** and give the colour you see.
- Absorbed light moves d electrons from the **ground state to an excited state**.
- **ΔE = hν = hc/λ**. Changes in **oxidation state, co-ordination number or ligand** change ΔE and so change the colour.
- **Colorimetry**: filter for the colour absorbed → zero with a blank → calibration graph from standards (absorbance vs concentration) → read the unknown.

| Quantity | Unit to use |
|---|---|
| λ | m (convert nm × 10⁻⁹) |
| ν | s⁻¹ (Hz) |
| ΔE per ion | J |
| ΔE per mole | J mol⁻¹, then ÷ 1000 for kJ mol⁻¹ |

## 3.2.5.5 Variable oxidation states

Zinc in acid reduces vanadate(V): **VO₂⁺ (+5, yellow) → VO²⁺ (+4, blue) → V³⁺ (+3, green) → V²⁺ (+2, violet)**.

- The redox potential for a higher → lower oxidation state change depends on **pH** and on the **ligand**.
- **Tollens' reagent** [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ is reduced to silver by aldehydes, not ketones.

**Titration ratios**

| Reaction | Mole ratio MnO₄⁻ : other |
|---|---|
| MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O + 5Fe³⁺ | 1 : 5 |
| 2MnO₄⁻ + 16H⁺ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 8H₂O + 10CO₂ | 2 : 5 |
| 2MnO₄⁻ + 6H⁺ + 5H₂O₂ → 2Mn²⁺ + 8H₂O + 5O₂ | 2 : 5 |

**Method in steps: any MnO₄⁻ titration**

1. n(MnO₄⁻) = c × V/1000.
2. Multiply by the mole ratio to get the other species in the titrated portion.
3. Scale up to the whole solution (for example × 250/25.0).
4. Convert: mass = n × M, or Mr = mass ÷ n, or % = mass found ÷ sample mass × 100.

Self-indicating: end point is the **first permanent pale pink**. Use **dilute sulfuric acid**.

## 3.2.5.6 Catalysts

| | Heterogeneous | Homogeneous |
|---|---|---|
| Phase | different from reactants | same as reactants |
| How it works | active sites on the surface | through an intermediate species |
| Examples | V₂O₅ (Contact process), Fe (Haber process) | Fe²⁺ in S₂O₈²⁻ + I⁻; Mn²⁺ (autocatalyst) in C₂O₄²⁻ + MnO₄⁻ |

- **Support medium**: maximises surface area and minimises the cost of catalyst.
- **Poisoning**: impurities block active sites, so efficiency falls; this costs money.
- **Variable oxidation states** let the catalyst accept and donate electrons, then be regenerated.

```
Contact:   SO₂ + V₂O₅ → SO₃ + V₂O₄;   V₂O₄ + ½O₂ → V₂O₅
Fe²⁺:      S₂O₈²⁻ + 2Fe²⁺ → 2SO₄²⁻ + 2Fe³⁺;   2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂
Mn²⁺:      4Mn²⁺ + MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ → 5Mn³⁺ + 4H₂O;   2Mn³⁺ + C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 2CO₂
```

**Small worked reminder: why Fe²⁺ can catalyse S₂O₈²⁻ + I⁻.** Iron must be able to do both steps. Fe²⁺ is oxidised by S₂O₈²⁻ (step 1), and the Fe³⁺ formed oxidises I⁻ (step 2). Both steps are feasible because the Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ electrode potential (+0.77 V) lies between those of I₂/I⁻ (+0.54 V) and S₂O₈²⁻/SO₄²⁻ (+2.01 V). Use the values a question gives you.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Monodentate vs bidentate vs multidentate**: one, two or several co-ordinate bonds per ligand. Count bonds for co-ordination number.
- **Complete vs incomplete substitution**: [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺ has all six water ligands replaced; [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ has four of six replaced.
- **Cis vs trans**: cis means the two identical ligands are at 90°; trans means 180° (octahedral) or opposite corners (square planar).
- **Absorbed vs seen**: the colour seen is the light transmitted or reflected, not the light absorbed.
- **Heterogeneous vs homogeneous**: surface active sites vs an intermediate species in the same phase.
- **Catalyst vs autocatalyst**: an autocatalyst is a product of the reaction it speeds up (Mn²⁺).
- **Fe²⁺ vs C₂O₄²⁻ titrations**: 1 : 5 for iron(II); 2 : 5 for ethanedioate, and the ethanedioate mixture is warmed.

## Quick self-test

1. State the co-ordination number and oxidation state of iron in [Fe(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻.
2. Why can Cl⁻ change the co-ordination number of [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ but NH₃ cannot?
3. State the shape and bond angle of [CuCl₄]²⁻.
4. How many particles are on each side of [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + EDTA⁴⁻ → [Co(EDTA)]²⁻ + 6H₂O?
5. A ligand swap has ΔH = +2.0 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = +160 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Find ΔG at 298 K.
6. A complex absorbs at 450 nm. Find ΔE per ion (h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹).
7. Give the formula and colour of vanadium in the +4 state.
8. What amount of Fe²⁺ reacts with 20.0 cm³ of 0.0100 mol dm⁻³ MnO₄⁻? What mass of iron is that (Ar Fe = 55.8)?
9. What amount of H₂O₂ reacts with 15.0 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ MnO₄⁻?
10. Why is the uncatalysed reaction between S₂O₈²⁻ and I⁻ slow?
11. Name the isomer of [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] that is cisplatin, and give the Cl–Pt–Cl angle.

### Answers

1. **6** (three bidentate ligands); Fe + 3(−2) = −3, so **+3**.
2. Cl⁻ is larger, so only four fit; NH₃ and H₂O are similar in size and uncharged.
3. **Tetrahedral, 109.5°**.
4. **2 on the left, 7 on the right**, so ΔS is positive.
5. ΔG = 2.0 − 298 × 0.160 = **−45.7 kJ mol⁻¹**. Feasible even though ΔH is positive.
6. ΔE = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸)/(450 × 10⁻⁹) = **4.42 × 10⁻¹⁹ J**.
7. **VO²⁺, blue**.
8. 2.00 × 10⁻⁴ × 5 = **1.00 × 10⁻³ mol**; mass = **0.0558 g**.
9. 3.00 × 10⁻⁴ × 5/2 = **7.50 × 10⁻⁴ mol**.
10. Both reactants are **negative ions, which repel**, so the activation energy is high.
11. The **cis** isomer; **90°**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "a ligand donates electrons" without "a pair of electrons" and "co-ordinate bond".
- Stating the chelate effect as "stronger bonds" with no mention of the increase in the number of particles.
- Forgetting H⁺ in manganate(VII) equations, so charges do not balance.
- Not scaling up from the titrated portion to the whole volumetric flask.
- Using nm instead of m in hc/λ, giving an answer 10⁹ times too small.
- Describing colour as the light absorbed rather than the light transmitted or reflected.
- Writing one Contact process equation only; the oxidation of V₂O₄ back to V₂O₅ shows regeneration.
- Leaving out the oxidation states when asked how variable oxidation states help catalysis.
