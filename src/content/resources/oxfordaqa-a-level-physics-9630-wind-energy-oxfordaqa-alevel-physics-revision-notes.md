## The equation

**Maximum power available = ½πr²ρv³** (the specification labels it E; it is a power in W)

| Symbol | Quantity | Unit | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| r | blade length (radius of swept circle) | m | not the diameter |
| ρ | density of air | kg m⁻³ | about 1.2; use the value given |
| v | wind speed | m s⁻¹ | cubed |
| P | maximum power available | W | not the power delivered |

Rearranged: r = √(2P/(πρv³)); v = (2P/(πr²ρ))^(1/3).

Origin: mass per second ρπr²v × kinetic energy per kilogram ½v².

## Proportional reasoning

| Change (all else fixed) | Effect on maximum power |
|---|---|
| Blade length × 2 | × 4 |
| Wind speed × 2 | × 8 |
| Wind speed × 1.5 | × 3.4 |
| Wind speed × 0.75 (wind shadow) | × 0.42 |
| Air density × 0.92 (high site) | × 0.92 |

## Condensed recall

- Mean power over varying winds is found by averaging P for each speed, not by using the mean speed. Fast spells dominate because of v³.
- Air density ρ = pM/RT: lower at altitude, higher in cold air.
- **Why all the energy cannot be used:** air must leave the rotor still moving or the flow would stop (theoretical maximum about 59%, not required); friction, turbulence and generator losses; turbines stop in very low and very high winds.
- **Wind shadow:** slower, turbulent air downwind of a turbine. A turbine in it gets far less power.
- **Farm layout:** turbines spaced several rotor diameters apart, wider along the prevailing wind, often staggered.
- **Environmental factors:** for: no emissions in operation, no fuel, land usable between onshore turbines, steadier offshore winds. Against: intermittent output, visual impact, noise, harm to birds and bats, large area, higher offshore costs.
- Judgements must fit the site described.

## Averaging a variable wind

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Find ½πr²ρ once for the turbine |
| 2 | Cube each wind speed separately |
| 3 | Multiply each cube by its time, in seconds |
| 4 | Add, then multiply by ½πr²ρ to get the energy |
| 5 | Divide by the total time if a mean power is wanted |

Never cube the average speed: because of v³, that always underestimates the energy when the wind varies.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Swept area | Area of the circle traced by the blades, πr² |
| Maximum available power | Rate at which kinetic energy of the wind passes through the swept area |
| Efficiency | Useful electrical output divided by maximum available power |
| Wind shadow | Region of slower, turbulent air downwind of a turbine |
| Prevailing wind | The most common wind direction at a site |
| Intermittent source | Source whose output varies with conditions it cannot control |

## Exam traps

- Diameter substituted for r.
- v not cubed, or the mean speed cubed for a variable wind.
- ½πr²ρv³ called an energy.
- Maximum available power treated as the electrical output.
- "A perfect turbine could take all the energy": no, the air must keep flowing.
- Environmental points listed with no link to the given site.

## Quick self-test

1. Write the expression for the maximum power available from a wind turbine and give its unit.
2. The blade length of a turbine is doubled. By what factor does the maximum power change?
3. The wind speed rises from 6.0 m s⁻¹ to 9.0 m s⁻¹. By what factor does the maximum power change?
4. Find the maximum power for r = 20 m, v = 7.0 m s⁻¹ and ρ = 1.2 kg m⁻³.
5. Find the mass of air passing through that rotor each second.
6. Give the main physical reason a turbine cannot extract all the wind's kinetic energy.
7. Name two ways energy is lost inside a turbine.
8. What is a wind shadow?
9. A turbine receives 80% of the free wind speed. What fraction of the unshadowed power is available?
10. Give one environmental advantage and two disadvantages of wind turbines.
11. Wind blows at 10 m s⁻¹ for 2 hours and is still for 2 hours. How does the mean power compare with the power at the mean speed?
12. Why does a denser air mass give more power at the same speed?

### Answers

1. P = ½πr²ρv³, in watts.
2. × 4.
3. (9.0/6.0)³ = 3.4.
4. P = ½ × π × 20² × 1.2 × 7.0³ = 2.6 × 10⁵ W.
5. ρπr²v = 1.2 × π × 400 × 7.0 = 1.1 × 10⁴ kg s⁻¹.
6. If all the kinetic energy were removed, the air would stop at the rotor and block further air from flowing through.
7. Any two: friction in bearings or gearbox; turbulence at the blades; resistive heating in the generator and cables.
8. The region downwind of a turbine where the air is slower and more turbulent.
9. 0.80³ = 0.51.
10. Advantage: no emissions while operating (or no fuel needed). Disadvantages: any two of intermittent output, visual impact, noise, harm to birds and bats, large area needed.
11. Mean power = ½P(10), while P(5) = P(10)/8, so the mean power is four times the power at the mean speed.
12. Each cubic metre has more mass, so more kinetic energy passes through the rotor each second.
