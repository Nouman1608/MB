## Section A

**1.** Work out √(61.4 − 3.9²).

**(a)** Write down all the figures on your calculator display. **[1]**

**(b)** Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. **[1]**

**2.** **(a)** Work out (−2.3)⁴. **[1]**

**(b)** Hamid types −2.3⁴ into his calculator. Write down the value his calculator gives and explain why it differs from your answer to (a). **[2]**

**3.** Use your calculator to work out

**(a)** 3.2⁻², giving your answer as a decimal **[1]**

**(b)** **(Higher tier only)** 125^(2/3) **[1]**

**(c)** the fifth root of 0.00032. **[1]**

**4.** Priya needs to work out (15 + 21) ÷ 3. She types 15 + 21 ÷ 3 and gets 22.

**(a)** Explain why her calculator gives 22. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down a key sequence that gives the correct answer, and state the answer. **[1]**

**5.** Work out (3.6 × 10⁻⁴)² ÷ (4.5 × 10⁻¹¹). Give your answer in standard form. **[2]**

**6.** Leila works out cos 60 on her calculator and gets −0.952 (to 3 s.f.).

**(a)** Explain what is wrong. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the correct value of cos 60°. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Use your calculator to work out (1 ½ + 2 ¾) ÷ (3 ⅓ − 1 ⅚). Give your answer as a mixed number in its simplest form. **[3]**

**8.** Work out (5.82 + √17.3) / (2.4² − 1.07).

**(a)** Write down the value of the numerator and of the denominator, correct to 4 significant figures. **[2]**

**(b)** Write down all the figures on your calculator display for the whole calculation. **[1]**

**(c)** Use an estimate to show that your answer is sensible. **[2]**

**9.** The table shows the number of books read last month by 30 students.

| Books | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Students | 4 | 9 | 7 | 6 | 4 |

Work out the mean number of books read per student. **[3]**

**10.** A square has an area of 72 cm².

**(a)** Work out the perimeter of the square. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**(b)** Tom rounds the side length to 1 decimal place before working out the perimeter. Show that his answer is not correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**11.** The time, T seconds, for one swing of a pendulum of length l metres is given by T = 2π√(l / 9.8).

**(a)** Work out T when l = 1.2. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**(b)** Ali types 2 × π × √1.2 ÷ 9.8 and gets 0.702. Explain his error. **[1]**

**12.** **(a)** A ramp rises 0.45 m over a horizontal distance of 3.8 m. Work out the angle the ramp makes with the horizontal, correct to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**(b)** A ladder of length 5.2 m reaches 4.9 m up a vertical wall. Work out the angle between the ladder and the ground, correct to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 61.4 − 3.9² = 46.19, and √46.19 = **6.796322535** [1].

**(b)** **6.80** [1]. The zero must be written: 6.8 is only 2 significant figures.

**2. (a)** **27.9841** [1].

**(b)** The calculator gives **−27.9841** [1]. It raises 2.3 to the power 4 first and then applies the minus sign, so the negative is not part of the base; brackets are needed round −2.3 [1].

**3. (a)** **0.09765625** [1].

**(b)** **25** [1] (the cube root of 125 is 5, and 5² = 25).

**(c)** **0.2** [1].

**4. (a)** The calculator divides before it adds, so it works out 15 + 7 [1].

**(b)** ( 15 + 21 ) ÷ 3 =, giving **12** [1]. (The fraction template with 15 + 21 on top also scores.)

**5.** (3.6 × 10⁻⁴)² = 1.296 × 10⁻⁷, and 1.296 × 10⁻⁷ ÷ (4.5 × 10⁻¹¹) = 2880 [1]; **2.88 × 10³** [1].

**6. (a)** The calculator is in radian mode, not degree mode [1].

**(b)** **0.5** [1].

**7.** 1 ½ + 2 ¾ = 17/4 and 3 ⅓ − 1 ⅚ = 3/2 [1]; (17/4) ÷ (3/2) = 17/6 [1]; **2 ⅚** [1].

**8. (a)** Numerator = 5.82 + 4.159326... = **9.979** [1]; denominator = 5.76 − 1.07 = **4.690** (or 4.69) [1].

**(b)** **2.127788245** [1].

**(c)** 5.82 is about 6, √17.3 is about √16 = 4, 2.4² is about 6 and 1.07 is about 1, so the estimate is (6 + 4) / (6 − 1) = 10/5 = 2 [1]; the answer 2.13 is close to 2, so it is sensible [1].

**9.** Σfx = 0 + 9 + 14 + 18 + 16 = 57 [1]; Σf = 30 [1]; mean = 57 ÷ 30 = **1.9 books** [1].

**10. (a)** Side = √72 = 8.485281... cm [1]; perimeter = 4 × 8.485281... = 33.941... = **33.9 cm** [1].

**(b)** Tom's side is 8.5 cm, so his perimeter is 4 × 8.5 = 34.0 cm [1]; the correct value to 3 s.f. is 33.9 cm, so rounding before the last step changed the answer [1].

**11. (a)** 1.2 ÷ 9.8 = 0.122448... and √0.122448... = 0.349927... [1]; T = 2π × 0.349927... = 2.198656... = **2.20 s** [1].

**(b)** He took the square root of 1.2 only and then divided by 9.8; the division must be inside the square root, so 1.2 ÷ 9.8 needs brackets or the root template [1].

**12. (a)** tan θ = 0.45 / 3.8 [1]; θ = tan⁻¹(0.45 ÷ 3.8) = 6.7535...° = **6.8°** [1].

**(b)** sin θ = 4.9 / 5.2 [1]; θ = sin⁻¹(4.9 ÷ 5.2) = 70.442...° = **70.4°** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Rounding in a part that asks for all the figures on the display (questions 1(a) and 8(b)).
- Writing 6.8 instead of 6.80 when 3 significant figures are asked for.
- Typing a fraction line or a root over part of an expression only (questions 4, 8 and 11).
- Leaving the answer to question 5 as 2880, which is not in standard form.
- Dividing Σfx by 5 (the number of columns) instead of 30 in question 9.
- Finding tan⁻¹(0.45) and then dividing by 3.8 in question 12, which gives 6.4° instead of 6.8°.

## Checking habits that catch these errors

Before moving on from a calculator question, ask three things. Is the size right? A 1 significant figure estimate, as in question 8(c), catches a missing bracket because the wrong entry is usually far away from the estimate. Is the form right? Standard form, mixed numbers, significant figures and degrees each have their own way of being written. Is the context right? A swing time of 0.7 seconds for a pendulum over a metre long, or a ramp angle of 70°, should look wrong before you write it down.
