## States of matter

| | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shape | fixed | shape of container | fills container |
| Volume | fixed | fixed | fills container |
| Flows? | no | yes | yes |
| Compresses? | hardly | hardly | easily |
| Arrangement | regular | irregular | random |
| Separation | very close | close | far apart |
| Motion | vibrate about fixed positions | slide past each other | fast, random, straight lines between collisions |

**Changes of state:** melting (s → l); solidification or freezing (l → s); boiling and evaporation (l → g); condensation (g → l).

## Temperature

- Higher temperature → particles move faster on average.
- **Absolute zero = −273 °C = 0 K**: particles have the least kinetic energy.
- **T (K) = θ (°C) + 273.**
- A change of 1 °C = a change of 1 K.

## Forces between particles (Extended only)

- Particles (atoms, molecules, ions, electrons) attract strongly when close.
- Solid: strong forces, fixed positions.
- Liquid: forces keep particles close but cannot hold them in place.
- Gas: particles far apart, forces very weak; particles move freely.

## Gas pressure

- **Core:** gas particles collide with the container walls; the collisions cause the pressure.
- **Extended only:** each collision exerts a force on the wall; pressure = total force ÷ area of wall (force per unit area).

| Change (fixed mass of gas) | Particle explanation | Pressure |
|---|---|---|
| temperature up, volume constant | faster particles; more frequent, harder collisions | increases |
| temperature down, volume constant | slower particles; fewer, gentler collisions | decreases |
| volume down, temperature constant | same speeds, less space; more collisions per unit area per second | increases |
| volume up, temperature constant | fewer collisions per unit area per second | decreases |

## Brownian motion

- **Seen:** smoke particles in air (lit, viewed with a microscope) move randomly and jerkily, in a zigzag.
- **Cause:** random collisions with fast-moving air molecules, unequal from different sides.
- **Shows:** evidence for the kinetic particle model (the air is made of moving particles).
- *(Extended only)* Smoke specks are **microscopic particles**; air consists of **molecules**. Light, fast molecules can move a much more massive particle.

## pV = constant (Extended only)

- Fixed mass of gas, **constant temperature**.
- p₁V₁ = p₂V₂.
- Halve the volume → double the pressure.
- p against V: curve (falling, flattening). p against 1/V: straight line through the origin.
- Check data: p × V should be the same for every pair.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Kinetic particle model | Matter is made of tiny particles in constant motion |
| Absolute zero | −273 °C (0 K), lowest possible temperature, particles have least kinetic energy |
| Kelvin (K) | Unit of the absolute temperature scale |
| Brownian motion | Random motion of microscopic particles in a liquid or gas |
| Suspension | Microscopic particles spread through a liquid or gas |
| Gas pressure | Force per unit area from particles colliding with a surface |
| Condensation | Gas to liquid |
| Solidification | Liquid to solid |

## Exam traps

- Particles do **not** expand, melt or get hot themselves; they move faster.
- Gas pressure comes from collisions with the **walls**, not with each other.
- Kelvin to Celsius: **subtract** 273.
- Particle diagrams: same-size particles; liquid particles mostly touching.
- "Smoke particles move because they are hot" or "because of convection": wrong.
- *(Extended)* pV = constant only at constant temperature.
- *(Extended)* "Smoke molecules": they are microscopic particles.

## Quick self-test

1. Give **two** properties of a gas that a liquid does not have.
2. Describe the motion of particles in a solid.
3. State what happens to the motion of particles when a gas is heated.
4. State the value of absolute zero in °C.
5. Convert 27 °C into kelvin.
6. Convert 250 K into °C.
7. Explain, in terms of particles, how a gas exerts a pressure.
8. In a smoke cell, what is seen through the microscope?
9. Explain why a sealed can of gas bursts if it is heated strongly.
10. *(Extended)* A gas at 120 kPa has a volume of 300 cm³. It is compressed at constant temperature to 200 cm³. Find the new pressure.
11. *(Extended)* Explain why tiny air molecules can move a much larger smoke particle.
12. *(Extended)* Describe the graph of p against 1/V for a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature.

### Answers

1. Any two: it **fills its container**; it has **no fixed volume**; it is **easily compressed**.
2. They **vibrate about fixed positions**.
3. The particles **move faster** (more kinetic energy).
4. **−273 °C**.
5. 27 + 273 = **300 K**.
6. 250 − 273 = **−23 °C**.
7. Particles **collide with the walls**; each collision pushes on the wall; many collisions per second give the pressure.
8. Small **bright specks** moving **randomly and jerkily**.
9. Particles move faster, hit the walls **more often and harder**, so the pressure rises until the can bursts.
10. 120 × 300 = p₂ × 200, so p₂ = **180 kPa**.
11. The air molecules are **light but move very fast**, and many hit the particle; unequal numbers of collisions on different sides push it.
12. A **straight line through the origin**.
