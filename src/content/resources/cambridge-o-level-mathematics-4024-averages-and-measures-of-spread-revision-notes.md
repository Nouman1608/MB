These notes condense section 9.3, Averages and measures of spread, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The worked explanations are in the [averages and measures of spread study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-averages-and-measures-of-spread/), and exam-style questions are in the [averages and measures of spread practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-averages-and-measures-of-spread-practice/).

## The three outcomes

1. Mean, median, mode and range for individual data, and the purposes of each.
2. An estimate of the mean for grouped discrete or grouped continuous data.
3. The modal class of a grouped frequency distribution.

## Key terms and formulas

| Term | Meaning or formula |
|---|---|
| Mean | sum of values ÷ number of values; from a table Σfx ÷ Σf |
| Median | middle value of ordered data; position (n + 1) ÷ 2 |
| Mode | most frequent value (or category) |
| Range | largest value − smallest value |
| Σ | "the sum of" |
| Midpoint | (lower class limit + upper class limit) ÷ 2 |
| Estimated mean | Σ(f × midpoint) ÷ Σf |
| Modal class | the class with the highest frequency (equal-width classes) |
| Total from a mean | total = mean × number of values |

## Which average, when?

| Situation | Best choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Categorical data (favourite colour) | mode | the only average that works with words |
| Most popular size to stock | mode | the most common value is what matters |
| Data with an extreme value (incomes) | median | not pulled by the extreme value |
| Similar values, all important (test marks) | mean | uses every value |

The range is pulled by extreme values in the same way the mean is.

## Method boxes

**List of values**

1. Order the values.
2. Mean: add, divide by how many.
3. Median: position (n + 1) ÷ 2; if halfway, average the two middle values.
4. Mode: most common value. Range: largest − smallest.

Example: 8, 3, 5, 12, 7, 5. Ordered: 3, 5, 5, 7, 8, 12. Mean = 40 ÷ 6 = 6.67, median = (5 + 7) ÷ 2 = 6, mode = 5, range = 9.

**Frequency table (discrete)**

1. Add an fx column.
2. Mean = Σfx ÷ Σf.
3. Median: find position (n + 1) ÷ 2, then use running totals of f.
4. Mode: the x with the largest f.

Example: goals 0, 1, 2, 3 with frequencies 4, 7, 5, 4. Σf = 20, Σfx = 29, mean = 1.45. Positions 10 and 11 both fall in the value 1 (running totals 4, 11), so median = 1. Mode = 1.

**Grouped data**

1. Write the midpoint of each class.
2. Multiply each midpoint by its frequency.
3. Estimated mean = Σ(f × midpoint) ÷ Σf.
4. Modal class: the class with the highest frequency.

Example: 0 ≤ x < 10 (3), 10 ≤ x < 20 (7), 20 ≤ x < 30 (10). Midpoints 5, 15, 25. Σ = 15 + 105 + 250 = 370, so estimated mean = 370 ÷ 20 = 18.5. Modal class 20 ≤ x < 30.

**Missing value or combined groups**

- Missing value: total = mean × n; subtract the known values.
- Combined mean: add the two totals and divide by the combined number. Never just average the two means unless the groups are the same size.

Example: 10 boys with mean 45 kg and 15 girls with mean 40 kg: (450 + 600) ÷ 25 = **42 kg**.

## Effect of changes

| Change | Mean | Median | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Add 10 to every value | + 10 | + 10 | unchanged |
| Double every value | doubles | doubles | doubles |
| Add one very large value | rises a lot | changes a little | rises a lot |

## Exam traps

- Median found from unordered data.
- The position (n + 1) ÷ 2 given as the median.
- The highest frequency quoted as the mode.
- Σfx divided by the number of rows.
- Boundaries used instead of midpoints.
- Grouped mean called exact.
- Means of two groups averaged without weighting.

## Self-test

1. Find the mean, median, mode and range of 8, 3, 5, 12, 7, 5.
2. Goals 0, 1, 2, 3 have frequencies 4, 7, 5, 4. Find the mean.
3. For question 2, find the median and mode.
4. Estimate the mean for the classes 0 ≤ x < 10 (3), 10 ≤ x < 20 (7), 20 ≤ x < 30 (10).
5. The mean of five numbers is 12. Four are 10, 14, 9 and 15. Find the fifth.
6. 10 boys have mean mass 45 kg and 15 girls have mean mass 40 kg. Find the overall mean.
7. Which average should a shoe shop use to decide which size to order most of?
8. Why is the median better than the mean for house prices on a street with one mansion?
9. In which position is the median of 25 ordered values?
10. What is the midpoint of the discrete class 11–15?
11. Find the range of 2.5, 4.1, 3.7 and 1.9.
12. Every value in a data set is increased by 10. What happens to the mean and to the range?

### Answers

1. Mean **6.67**, median **6**, mode **5**, range **9**.
2. Σfx = 0 + 7 + 10 + 12 = 29, so mean = 29 ÷ 20 = **1.45**.
3. Median **1**, mode **1**.
4. 370 ÷ 20 = **18.5**.
5. 5 × 12 = 60, so fifth = 60 − 48 = **12**.
6. **42 kg**.
7. The **mode**.
8. The mansion's price **pulls the mean up**; the median is not affected by one extreme value.
9. (25 + 1) ÷ 2 = **13th**.
10. (11 + 15) ÷ 2 = **13**.
11. 4.1 − 1.9 = **2.2**.
12. The mean **increases by 10**; the range is **unchanged**.
