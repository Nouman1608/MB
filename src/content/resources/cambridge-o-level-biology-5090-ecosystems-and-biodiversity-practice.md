## Section A

**1.** What is a community?

A: all the organisms of one species in an area

B: all the populations of different species in an ecosystem

C: the living and non-living parts of an area

D: the number of species in an area

**2.** What does biodiversity measure?

A: the number of different species in an area

B: the number of individuals in a population

C: the mass of living material in an ecosystem

D: the rate of population growth

**[1]**

**3.** State what is meant by a population. **[2]**

**4.** State what an ecosystem contains, in addition to the community. **[1]**

**5.** Name **two** factors that can slow down the growth of a population. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A lake contains 300 perch, 1 200 roach, 40 pike, many species of algae and plants, insects, snails and bacteria.

**(a)** Identify which term describes all the perch. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify which term describes all the living organisms in the lake. **[1]**

**(c)** State what must be added to the answer to (b) to describe the lake as an ecosystem. **[1]**

**7.** A biologist compares two woodlands, A and B, using quadrats. Woodland A has 12 species of plant in the sample and woodland B has 30 species.

**(a)** State which woodland has the greater biodiversity. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the number of species is in the woodland with greater biodiversity. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest **one** reason why the biologist should place the quadrats at random. **[1]**

**(d)** Suggest why a plantation of one type of tree has lower biodiversity than a natural woodland. **[2]**

**8.** A few rabbits were introduced to an island with no predators. The table shows the number of rabbits over time.

| Year | Number of rabbits |
|---|---|
| 0 | 10 |
| 1 | 24 |
| 2 | 70 |
| 3 | 210 |
| 4 | 420 |
| 5 | 440 |
| 6 | 435 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in population between years 2 and 3. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the pattern of population growth shown by the data. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest **two** reasons for the pattern between years 4 and 6. **[2]**

**(d)** Foxes are later introduced to the island. Predict the effect on the rabbit population and explain. **[2]**

**9.** The numbers of a prey species and its predator were counted for several years. The prey peaked in years 2, 6 and 10. The predator peaked in years 3, 7 and 11.

**(a)** Explain why the peaks of the predator occur after the peaks of the prey. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the number of prey falls after the predator peaks. **[1]**

**10.** Explain how disease can limit the growth of a population. **[2]**

**11.** Describe **three** ways in which the growth of the human population is increasing the demand for global resources. **[3]**

**12.** In a country the population in 2000 was 25 million and in 2020 it was 32 million. Calculate the percentage increase. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A group of organisms of one species [1], living in the same area at the same time [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The environment (the non-living surroundings, such as water, soil, light and temperature) [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Any two of: shortage of food; competition; predation; disease [1 each]. **[2]**

**6.**

**(a)** A population [1].

**(b)** A community [1].

**(c)** The environment (the non-living parts of the lake and their interaction with the organisms) [1]. **[3]**

**7.**

**(a)** Woodland B [1].

**(b)** 30 divided by 12 = 2.5 times [1].

**(c)** To avoid bias, so that the sample represents the whole area [1].

**(d)** The plantation has only one species of tree, which supports fewer other species [1], and may have less variety of food and habitat for animals [1]. **[5]**

**8.**

**(a)** (210 - 70) divided by 70 x 100 [1] = 200 per cent [1].

**(b)** The population grows slowly at first [1], then rapidly in years 2 to 4 [1], and levels off between years 4 and 6 at about 435 to 440 [1].

**(c)** Any two of: food supply becomes limited; competition for food or space; disease spreads in a crowded population [1 each].

**(d)** The population of rabbits would fall [1], because foxes kill and eat the rabbits (predation) [1]. **[9]**

**9.**

**(a)** When prey numbers rise, there is more food for the predator [1], so the predator population increases a little later [1].

**(b)** More predators eat more prey, so the prey population falls [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Disease kills individuals or reduces breeding [1], and spreads more easily when the population is large and individuals are close together, so the population grows more slowly or falls [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Any three of: more food and land are needed for farming, so forests are cleared; more water is used; more energy is needed, so more fossil fuels are burned; more materials such as wood and minerals are used; more space is needed for housing [1 each]. **[3]**

**12.** Increase = 32 - 25 = 7 million [1]. Percentage = 7 divided by 25 x 100 = 28 per cent [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a population as all the organisms in an area.
- Leaving out the environment when describing an ecosystem.
- Counting individuals instead of species when talking about biodiversity.
- Stating a factor (for example "food") without saying how it changes growth.
- Dividing by the final value when calculating a percentage increase.
