Condensed recall for section 3.4.2 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-recruitment-and-selection-of-employees/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- **The need for recruitment.**
- **Methods** of recruitment and selection.
- The difference between **internal and external** recruitment, with the **benefits and drawbacks** of each.
- The **stages** in the recruitment and selection process.
- **Analyse** the benefits of an effective process: **high productivity, high-quality output or customer service, staff retention**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Recruitment | Attracting suitable people to apply for a job |
| Selection | Choosing the best candidate from the applicants |
| Internal recruitment | Filling a vacancy with someone already working for the business |
| External recruitment | Filling a vacancy with someone from outside |
| Job description | Document listing the duties, hours, pay and reporting line of a job |
| Person specification | Document listing the skills, qualifications and experience wanted |
| CV | Summary of a person's education, skills and work history |
| Shortlist | The best few applicants chosen to go forward to interview |
| Reference | A statement from a previous employer or teacher about the candidate |
| Induction | Introduction of a new employee to the business and the job |
| Staff turnover | Percentage of staff who leave in a year |

## Why recruit?

- Replace leavers.
- Business growth.
- New skills needed.
- Seasonal demand.
- Reorganisation or new products.

## Internal against external

| | Internal | External |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Cheaper | More expensive (adverts, time, agency fees) |
| Speed | Quicker | Slower |
| Choice | Small | Wide |
| Knowledge of candidate | Known | Less known; more risk |
| New ideas | Few | Many |
| Induction | Short | Longer |
| Motivation | Promotion chance motivates | Existing staff may feel overlooked |
| Side effect | Creates another vacancy | None |

## Stages of recruitment and selection

1. Identify the vacancy.
2. Prepare the **job description** and **person specification**.
3. **Advertise** (internally, externally or both).
4. Receive **applications** (CV, letter, application form).
5. **Shortlist** against the person specification.
6. **Select**: interview, tests, presentation or work trial.
7. Take up **references** and check qualifications.
8. **Offer** the job and contract.
9. **Induction.**

## Selection methods

| Method | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Interview | Face-to-face; tests communication | Interviewer bias; candidate may perform well only on the day |
| Test | Measures specific skills fairly | Does not show personality |
| Presentation or work trial | Shows real performance | Time-consuming |
| Group exercise | Shows teamwork | Quiet candidates may be overlooked |

## Benefits of effective recruitment

- **High productivity:** well-matched employees work faster and with fewer mistakes.
- **High-quality output or customer service:** right skills and attitude.
- **Staff retention:** fewer leavers, so lower recruitment and training costs.

Poor recruitment causes errors, unhappy customers, extra training and high turnover.

## Formulae

- Staff turnover (%) = number of leavers / average number of employees x 100. 18 / 120 x 100 = 15%.
- Cost of replacing leavers = leavers x cost of replacing one. 18 x $4,500 = $81,000.
- External recruitment cost = advertising + management time (hours x hourly cost). $1,800 + 40 x $25 = $2,800.

## Exam traps

- Recruitment is attracting applicants; selection is choosing between them.
- Job description is about the **job**; person specification is about the **person**.
- Internal recruitment is not always better; it leaves a vacancy and gives few new ideas.
- Link the answer to the business: a small shop may have no one to promote.
- Always state the benefit to the business, such as productivity, service or retention.
- Do not name specific laws unless the question does.

## Self-test

1. Define recruitment.
2. State three reasons why a business may need to recruit.
3. Distinguish between internal and external recruitment.
4. Give one benefit and one drawback of internal recruitment.
5. Give one benefit and one drawback of external recruitment.
6. What does a job description contain?
7. What does a person specification contain?
8. State two selection methods.
9. A firm has 200 staff and 30 leave in the year. Calculate staff turnover.
10. Each replacement costs $3,000. Calculate the cost of replacing the 30 leavers.
11. Explain how effective recruitment improves customer service.
12. Why might a business take up references before making a job offer?

### Answers

1. Attracting suitable people to apply for a job.
2. Any three: replace leavers; growth; new skills; seasonal demand; reorganisation.
3. Internal: filling the post with someone already working for the business. External: appointing someone from outside.
4. Benefit: cheaper and quicker (or the person is already known). Drawback: limited choice (or creates another vacancy).
5. Benefit: wider choice (or new ideas and skills). Drawback: more expensive and slower (or more risk).
6. Duties, hours, pay and who the employee reports to.
7. Skills, qualifications and experience needed by the successful candidate.
8. Any two: interview; test; presentation; work trial; group exercise.
9. 30 / 200 x 100 = **15%**.
10. 30 x $3,000 = **$90,000**.
11. Employees with the right skills and attitude deal with customers well, so customers are more satisfied and more likely to return.
12. To check that the candidate's claims about experience and character are true before committing to employ them.
