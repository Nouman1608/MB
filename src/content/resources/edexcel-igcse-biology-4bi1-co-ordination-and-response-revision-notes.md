## Key definitions (2.80–2.82)

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Stimulus | A change in the environment (internal or external) that can be detected |
| Receptor | Detects a stimulus (e.g. light receptors in the retina) |
| Effector | Carries out the response: a muscle (contracts) or a gland (secretes) |
| Homeostasis | Maintenance of a constant internal environment |
| Tropism | Growth response of a plant to a directional stimulus |
| Reflex | Rapid, automatic response that does not need conscious thought |
| Hormone | Chemical secreted by a gland into the blood, acting on target organs |

The two homeostasis examples the specification names: **body water content** and **body temperature**.

The co-ordinated response chain:

```
stimulus → receptor → co-ordinator (CNS / hormones) → effector → response
```

## Plants: tropisms and auxin (2.83–2.85)

| | Stem | Root |
|---|---|---|
| Phototropism (light) | Positive | Negative |
| Geotropism (gravity) | Negative | Positive |

**Method in steps -- explaining a stem bending to light:**

1. Auxin is made in the shoot tip.
2. Light from one side → auxin moves to the **shaded** side.
3. More auxin → more **cell elongation** on the shaded side.
4. Shaded side grows longer → stem bends **towards** the light.
5. Benefit: leaves absorb more light for photosynthesis.

Even light or darkness → auxin spread evenly → stem grows straight.

**Plant vs animal responses:** plants respond by **growth**, which is slow and usually permanent; animals respond with muscles and glands, which is fast and reversible. Both still follow stimulus → receptor → effector. In a stem, the tip detects the light and the elongating cells further down carry out the response.

## Nervous and hormonal systems (2.86–2.89)

| | Nervous | Hormonal |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | Electrical impulse | Chemical |
| Route | Neurones | Blood |
| Speed | Fast | Slow |
| Effect lasts | Short time | Longer |
| Target | Precise | Widespread |

- CNS = **brain + spinal cord**, linked to sense organs by nerves.
- Receptors stimulated → impulses along nerves **into** the CNS → impulses **out** to effectors → rapid response.
- Neurones: sensory (in), relay (within CNS), motor (out).

**Synapse in four steps:** impulse arrives → neurotransmitter released → diffuses across gap → binds to receptors on next neurone, starting a new impulse. One-way only.

## Reflex arc (2.90)

```
heat → receptor in skin → sensory neurone → relay neurone (spinal cord)
     → motor neurone → muscle (biceps) contracts → hand withdrawn
```

Fast because the route is short, has few synapses and bypasses conscious thought. Protects the body from harm.

## The eye (2.91–2.92)

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| Cornea | Refracts light |
| Iris | Controls pupil size |
| Lens | Fine focusing |
| Ciliary muscle + suspensory ligaments | Change lens shape |
| Retina | Receptors: rods (dim light), cones (colour) |
| Fovea | Most cones; sharpest vision |
| Optic nerve | Impulses to brain |
| Blind spot | No receptors |

**Accommodation:**

| Looking at | Ciliary muscle | Ligaments | Lens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Near object | Contracts | Slack | Fat, refracts more |
| Distant object | Relaxes | Tight | Thin, refracts less |

**Pupil size:**

| Light | Circular muscle | Radial muscle | Pupil |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bright | Contracts | Relaxes | Constricts |
| Dim | Relaxes | Contracts | Dilates |

## Skin and temperature (2.93)

| Too hot | Too cold |
|---|---|
| More sweat; evaporation removes heat | Less sweat |
| Vasodilation: arterioles to skin widen | Vasoconstriction: arterioles to skin narrow |
| More blood near surface, more heat radiated | Less blood near surface, less heat lost |

## Hormones (2.94, 2.95B)

| Hormone | Gland | Key effect |
|---|---|---|
| Adrenaline | Adrenal | Heart and breathing rate up; liver glycogen → glucose; pupils widen |
| Insulin | Pancreas | Blood glucose down; glucose → glycogen in liver and muscles |
| Testosterone | Testes | Male secondary sexual characteristics; sperm production |
| Oestrogen | Ovaries | Female secondary sexual characteristics; builds uterus lining |
| Progesterone | Ovaries | Maintains uterus lining |
| ADH (Paper 2 only) | Pituitary | Collecting duct more permeable → more water reabsorbed → less, more concentrated urine |
| FSH (Paper 2 only) | Pituitary | Egg matures; ovaries make oestrogen |
| LH (Paper 2 only) | Pituitary | Triggers ovulation |

The menstrual cycle itself is in Topic 3: see the [reproduction and inheritance revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-biology-reproduction-inheritance-revision-notes/).

## Worked reminders

**Percentage change.** Subtract the starting value from the final value, divide by the **starting** value, multiply by 100. A pupil that narrows from 5.0 mm to 2.0 mm changes by (2.0 − 5.0) ÷ 5.0 × 100 = −60%, a 60% decrease. Dividing by 2.0 instead gives a wrong answer of −150%.

**Rate from a graph or table.** Rate = change in the quantity ÷ time taken. Always give the unit, for example "degrees per hour" for a bending shoot or "mmol/dm³ per minute" for blood glucose.

**"Use the data" questions.** Quote at least one pair of numbers from the table or graph, then link them to the biology. "More auxin on the shaded side (48 vs 27 units), so more cell elongation there" is the shape that scores.

## Method in steps -- extended answers

**Body too hot (6-mark style):**

1. Temperature rise detected (receptors and brain).
2. Sweat glands release more sweat.
3. Water in sweat **evaporates**, using heat energy from the skin.
4. Arterioles supplying skin capillaries **dilate** (vasodilation).
5. More blood flows near the skin surface.
6. More heat lost to the surroundings by radiation.

**Body too cold:** reverse steps 2–6: less sweat; arterioles **constrict** (vasoconstriction); less blood near the surface; less heat lost.

**Water balance (Paper 2 only, links 2.81 to 2.95B):**

1. Sweating or little drinking → blood more concentrated.
2. Pituitary gland releases more ADH.
3. Collecting ducts become more permeable to water.
4. More water reabsorbed into the blood.
5. Small volume of concentrated urine.

Drinking a lot gives the opposite: less ADH, less water reabsorbed, large volume of dilute urine.

## Command words for this sub-topic

| Command word | What it needs here |
|---|---|
| State / Name | A single term: "relay neurone", "pancreas" |
| Describe | The sequence of events, in order, no reasons needed: the reflex pathway, accommodation steps |
| Explain | Each step **plus** why: "less blood near the surface, **so** less heat lost" |
| Suggest | Apply the ideas to an unfamiliar organism or set-up |
| Calculate | Show the working and give a unit |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Glycogen** (storage carbohydrate) vs **glucagon** (a different hormone, not on this specification list). Insulin turns glucose into glycogen.
- **Vasodilation** (widening, when hot) vs **vasoconstriction** (narrowing, when cold).
- **Positive** tropism (towards) vs **negative** tropism (away).
- **Receptor** (detects) vs **effector** (responds).
- **Contracting** ciliary muscle = near focus; **relaxing** = far focus.
- **Neurone** (one cell) vs **nerve** (a bundle of neurones).

## Quick self-test

1. Name the two examples of homeostasis given in the specification.
2. A root is placed horizontally. In which direction does it grow, and what is the response called?
3. To which side of a stem lit from the right does auxin move?
4. State what auxin does to cells in a stem.
5. Name the parts of the central nervous system.
6. What crosses the gap at a synapse, and by what process?
7. Name the neurone found in the spinal cord in a reflex arc.
8. A person looks up from a book to a distant hill. What happens to the ciliary muscle and the lens?
9. A pupil widens from 3.0 mm to 6.6 mm in a dark room. Calculate the percentage increase.
10. A runner loses sweat at 0.6 dm³ per hour for 2.5 hours. Calculate the volume of sweat lost.
11. Name the gland that secretes insulin and state its effect on blood glucose.
12. (Paper 2 only) Name the hormone that triggers ovulation and the gland that releases it.

### Answers

1. Body water content and body temperature.
2. Downwards (towards gravity): positive geotropism.
3. The left (shaded) side.
4. Stimulates cell elongation.
5. Brain and spinal cord.
6. Neurotransmitter; diffusion.
7. Relay neurone.
8. Ciliary muscle relaxes; suspensory ligaments tighten; lens becomes thinner and refracts light less.
9. (6.6 − 3.0) ÷ 3.0 × 100 = **120%**.
10. 0.6 × 2.5 = **1.5 dm³**.
11. Pancreas; lowers blood glucose.
12. LH; pituitary gland.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "auxin moves away from light" without saying which **side** of the stem it ends up on.
- Saying auxin makes cells divide rather than **elongate**.
- Mixing up which organ (root or stem) is positive for each tropism.
- Describing a reflex arc without the **relay neurone** or without naming the **spinal cord**.
- Forgetting that the neurotransmitter **diffuses** and **binds** to receptors on the next neurone.
- Reversing accommodation: the ciliary muscle **contracts** for near objects.
- Mixing up the circular and radial muscles of the iris.
- Saying capillaries "move closer to the skin" instead of arterioles widening.
- Writing "glucagon" when you mean glycogen.
- Giving a hormone's effect without its **source** gland when the question asks for both.
