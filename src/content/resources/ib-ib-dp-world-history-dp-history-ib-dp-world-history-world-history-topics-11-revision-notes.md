These notes condense world history topic 11 for Paper 2: the causes of twentieth-century wars, how they were fought and why they ended as they did, and their effects. The analysis behind them is in the [20th-century wars study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-dp-history-ib-dp-world-history-world-history-topics-11/); practise applying them with the [20th-century wars practice questions](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-dp-history-ib-dp-world-history-world-history-topics-11-practice/).

## The guide's three parts

| Part | Headings |
|---|---|
| Causes | Economic, ideological, political, territorial and other causes; short- and long-term causes |
| Practices and impact on outcome | Types of war (civil, guerrilla, limited, total); technology; theatres of war (air, land, sea); mobilization of human and economic resources; influence or involvement of foreign powers |
| Effects | Peacemaking; territorial changes; political repercussions; economic, social and demographic impact; role and status of women |

## Four types of war

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Total war | The whole society and economy mobilized; civilians become targets | First and Second World Wars |
| Limited war | Restricted aims, area or means | Falklands War (1982) |
| Civil war | War between groups within one state | Spanish Civil War (1936-39); Chinese Civil War (1946-49) |
| Guerrilla war | Irregular forces avoid open battle, rely on mobility and local support | Communist forces in China before 1946; the FLN in Algeria |

A single conflict can combine types: the Chinese Civil War began with guerrilla methods and ended with large conventional battles.

## Case notes

### First World War (1914-1918)

- **Causes:** alliances (Triple Alliance, Triple Entente); Anglo-German naval race; imperial rivalry; Balkan nationalism; war plans that made mobilization mean war.
- **Trigger:** assassination of Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo, 28 June 1914.
- **Practices:** trench warfare; gas (Ypres, 1915); tanks (the Somme, 1916); unrestricted submarine warfare (resumed February 1917); conscription in Britain from 1916; Defence of the Realm Act (1914).
- **Outcome:** US entry (April 1917); failure of the German spring offensive (1918); Allied advance; armistice 11 November 1918.
- **Effects:** Versailles (28 June 1919) and the other Paris treaties; the League of Nations; collapse of four empires (German, Austro-Hungarian, Russian, Ottoman); revolution in Russia; women's suffrage in several countries.

### Spanish Civil War (1936-1939)

- **Causes:** land inequality; Church and army against reformers; regional nationalism; polarization after the Popular Front victory (February 1936).
- **Trigger:** military rising, 17-18 July 1936.
- **Practices:** German and Italian aid to the Nationalists; Soviet aid and the International Brigades for the Republic; Non-Intervention Agreement; bombing of Guernica (26 April 1937); Battle of the Ebro (1938).
- **Outcome:** Nationalist victory, 1 April 1939: foreign aid, unified command under Franco, Republican divisions (May Days, Barcelona, 1937).
- **Effects:** Franco's dictatorship; repression and exile of Republicans; economic ruin.

### Falklands War (1982)

- **Causes:** Argentine sovereignty claim; the junta's economic and political crisis; British signals of reduced commitment.
- **Trigger:** Argentine invasion, 2 April 1982.
- **Practices:** British naval task force; sinking of the *General Belgrano* (2 May); Argentine air attacks on British ships; landings at San Carlos; Argentine surrender at Stanley (14 June).
- **Effects:** fall of the junta and return to civilian rule in Argentina (1983); boost to Thatcher's government.

### Iran-Iraq War (1980-1988)

- **Causes:** dispute over the Shatt al-Arab; Saddam Hussein's ambition for regional leadership; fear of revolutionary Iran.
- **Practices:** trench warfare and mass infantry offensives; Iraqi use of chemical weapons; attacks on cities and on oil tankers in the Gulf; foreign arms supplies to both sides.
- **Outcome:** stalemate; ceasefire under UN Security Council Resolution 598, accepted by Iran in 1988.
- **Effects:** huge casualties and economic damage with no territorial gain; Iraqi debts that formed part of the background to the invasion of Kuwait (1990).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mobilization | Organizing armed forces and the economy for war |
| Attrition | Wearing the enemy down through continuous losses |
| Home front | The civilian population and economy in wartime |
| Conscription | Compulsory military service |
| Non-intervention | Policy of not taking sides in another state's war (Britain and France in Spain) |
| Armistice | An agreement to stop fighting, before a peace treaty |
| Self-determination | The right of peoples to choose their own state |
| Demographic impact | Effects on population: deaths, births, migration and refugees |

## Exam traps

- **Causes of war versus causes of victory.** Different questions; different essays.
- **Narrating campaigns.** Use battles as evidence for an argument about outcome or effects.
- **Short-term effects only.** Show which effects lasted.
- **Women as a footnote.** The guide names the role and status of women; prepare a paragraph for each war you study.
- **Same-region examples** when two regions are required.

## Self-test

1. Name the five categories of cause in the guide. *Economic, ideological, political, territorial and other.*
2. What triggered the First World War? *The assassination of Franz Ferdinand on 28 June 1914.*
3. What was the Defence of the Realm Act? *A 1914 British law giving the government wide powers over the economy and civilian life in wartime.*
4. Why was the resumption of unrestricted submarine warfare significant? *It helped bring the USA into the war in April 1917.*
5. What began the Spanish Civil War? *The military rising of 17-18 July 1936.*
6. What was the Condor Legion? *The German air and military unit that fought for the Nationalists, which bombed Guernica.*
7. Give two reasons for Nationalist victory. *Any two: German and Italian aid, non-intervention, Franco's unified command, Republican divisions.*
8. When did Argentina invade the Falklands, and when did its forces surrender? *2 April and 14 June 1982.*
9. What political effect did defeat have in Argentina? *The junta fell and civilian rule returned in 1983.*
10. What was the main territorial cause of the Iran-Iraq War? *The dispute over the Shatt al-Arab waterway.*
11. How did the Iran-Iraq War end? *In a ceasefire under UN Resolution 598, accepted by Iran in 1988.*
12. Give one change in the status of women after the First World War. *The vote for women over 30 meeting a property qualification in Britain (1918), or women's suffrage in Germany (1918-19).*
