## Questions

**1.** Define a multinational company. **[2]**

**2.** Define foreign direct investment and explain how it differs from portfolio investment. **[3]**

**3.** A subsidiary of an MNC in Country D earns a profit of 8.0 million D-units before any internal charges. Country D taxes profit at 25%. The parent charges the subsidiary 3.0 million for services, and that income is taxed at 5% in the country where it is received. Calculate the tax Country D loses and the saving to the group. **[4]**

**4.** Explain two ways in which an MNC could benefit a low-income host country. **[4]**

**5.** Country D has GDP of 1,500 million D-units. A foreign firm makes an FDI inflow of 90 million. In the first year it sends 12 million of profit abroad. Calculate (a) the FDI inflow as a percentage of GDP, (b) the profit outflow as a percentage of the inflow, and (c) the inflow minus the profit outflow. **[3]**

**6.** Country C has external debt of 900 million foreign currency units (FCU), GNI of 2,500 million FCU, export earnings of 300 million FCU and annual debt service of 66 million FCU.
**(a)** Calculate the debt to GNI ratio and the debt service ratio. **[2]**
**(b)** Export earnings fall by 20% and debt service is unchanged. Calculate the new debt service ratio and comment on the change. **[2]**

**7.** Explain two possible causes of a rise in a country's external debt. **[4]**

**8.** Country C owes 400 million FCU. The exchange rate moves from 1 FCU = 3.0 C-units to 1 FCU = 3.6 C-units.
**(a)** Calculate the debt in C-units before and after, and the percentage rise. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one consequence of this change for the government's budget. **[1]**

**9.** Explain two roles of the International Monetary Fund. **[4]**

**10.** An IMF loan to Country C requires the government to cut spending by 60 million FCU. GDP is 1,000 million FCU and the multiplier is 2.
**(a)** Calculate the fall in GDP and express it as a percentage of GDP. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the government might still accept the loan. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate one possible long-run benefit and one cost of such conditions. **[2]**

**11.** Country C could borrow 120 million FCU from the World Bank at 2% interest or from commercial lenders at 6%.
**(a)** State two roles of the World Bank. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the annual interest on each loan and the difference. **[3]**

**12.** Evaluate whether FDI by an MNC in a low-income country is likely to raise living standards. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** A firm that owns or controls production or service operations [1] in more than one country [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition with only "large" or "global" in it describes size, not what makes a firm multinational; state the operations in more than one country.

**2.** FDI is investment in productive assets or a controlling stake in a business [1] in another country, such as building a factory [1]. Portfolio investment is buying shares or bonds without control and can be sold quickly, whereas FDI is a longer-term commitment [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The contrast is worth a separate point, so name both control and permanence rather than only saying that one is "bigger".

**3.** Tax before charge: 8.0 × 0.25 = 2.0 million [1]. Reported profit: 8.0 − 3.0 = 5.0; tax 5.0 × 0.25 = 1.25 million, so **Country D loses 0.75 million** [1]. Tax abroad: 3.0 × 0.05 = 0.15 million [1]. Group tax: 1.25 + 0.15 = 1.40 million against 2.0, so the **group saves 0.60 million** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Work out the tax both before and after the charge and subtract; stating only the new tax figure does not show the loss to the host.

**4.** Any two developed points. Example one: jobs and wages [1], because the MNC employs local workers, raising incomes and spending, and training raises productivity [1]. Example two: technology and capital [1], because new methods and machinery raise output and long-run supply [1]. Other creditworthy points: tax revenue funding public services, or exports earning foreign currency. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each benefit needs its link to a result such as higher income or output. A list of benefits with no explanation earns little.

**5.** (a) 90 ÷ 1,500 × 100 = **6.0%** [1]. (b) 12 ÷ 90 × 100 = **13.3%** (13.3 to one decimal place) [1]. (c) 90 − 12 = **78 million** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the division and keep the units (percentage or million D-units) for each part; three bare numbers are hard to follow.

**6.** (a) Debt to GNI = 900 ÷ 2,500 × 100 = **36%** [1]; debt service ratio = 66 ÷ 300 × 100 = **22%** [1]. (b) Exports: 300 × 0.80 = 240; ratio = 66 ÷ 240 × 100 = **27.5%** [1]. A larger share of export earnings goes on debt service, leaving less for imports and public spending [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Comment" needs a consequence of the figure, such as less money for other uses, not just a repeat of the higher percentage.

**7.** Any two developed causes. Example one: persistent current account deficits [1], funded by borrowing from abroad because imports exceed exports [1]. Example two: a fall in export earnings [1], from a lower price of the main export, so repayments take a larger share and new loans are needed [1]. Other creditworthy causes: rising interest rates on variable-rate loans, or depreciation raising the home cost of foreign-currency debt. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the cause and then show how it increases the amount owed or borrowed; "the country is poor" is a description, not a cause.

**8.** (a) Before: 400 × 3.0 = 1,200 million C-units [1]. After: 400 × 3.6 = 1,440 [1]. Rise: 240 ÷ 1,200 × 100 = **20%** [1]. (b) Debt service takes more of tax revenue in C-units, so less is left for spending on services, or taxes must rise [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert the same FCU amount at both rates and compare, rather than comparing the two exchange rates alone.

**9.** Any two roles with detail. Example one: surveillance [1], monitoring economies and exchange-rate policies and giving advice [1]. Example two: temporary lending [1] to countries with balance of payments problems, so they can finance imports [1]. Other creditworthy roles: conditionality (policy conditions meant to correct the problem) and technical assistance for officials. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the role and then say what it does or why; the IMF is about short-run payments problems, not general development funding.

**10.** (a) GDP falls by 60 × 2 = **120 million** [1], which is 120 ÷ 1,000 × 100 = **12%** [1]. (b) The loan fills a financing gap and avoids a disorderly fall in the exchange rate or a default [1], and it can restore confidence among other lenders [1]. (c) Benefit: reforms such as lower deficits or better tax collection could raise competitiveness and growth [1]. Cost: lower output, jobs and public services in the short run, and lasting damage if the cuts hit education or health [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) asks for a judgement, so say which effect matters more or what it depends on rather than only stating both.

**11.** (a) Any two: long-term loans for development projects such as roads or power [1]; very low-interest loans or grants to reduce poverty [1]; technical advice and support for reforms. (b) World Bank: 120 × 0.02 = 2.4 million [1]. Commercial: 120 × 0.06 = 7.2 million [1]. Difference: **4.8 million** a year [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the roles as functions, and do not use "gives aid" as a role; the IMF's short-run lending is a different role.

**12.** Gains (any two developed): jobs, wages and training raise incomes and productivity [1]; capital, technology and tax revenue raise output and long-run supply [1]. Costs (any two developed): profits are sent abroad, so national income rises by less than output [1]; transfer pricing reduces the tax paid [1]; local firms may close, and working conditions or the environment may suffer [1]. Evaluation: the outcome depends on how much is sourced locally and reinvested, the host's bargaining power and tax rules [1], the sector and whether workers gain transferable skills [1], and the short run versus the long run. Judgement: living standards are more likely to rise when the host captures a large share of the value added [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A balanced answer ends with a reasoned conclusion that names the conditions on which the outcome depends, not a restatement of the points.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining FDI or an MNC without control or operations in more than one country.
- Showing only the new tax figure in a transfer pricing calculation.
- Quoting a ratio without saying what it implies.
- Forgetting that a depreciation raises the home-currency cost of foreign-currency debt.
- Listing IMF or World Bank roles with no explanation.
- Treating the IMF and World Bank as the same.
- Stating austerity effects with no estimate or no long-run view.
- Concluding without saying what the outcome depends on.
