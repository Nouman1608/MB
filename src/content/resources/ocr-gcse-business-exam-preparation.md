OCR GCSE (9-1) Business (J204) is untiered and linear, assessed by two compulsory components of equal
size. Component 01 (Business activity, marketing and people) and Component 02 (Operations, finance and
influences on business) are each 1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks and 50% of the qualification. On OCR's
own specification-at-a-glance table, **Component 02 is marked as carrying synoptic assessment** --
meaning it can draw on the content of Component 01 as well as its own. These notes complement the
site's guides to
[Business Activity, Marketing and People](/resources/ocr-gcse-business-business-activity-marketing-and-people/)
and
[Operations, Finance and Influences on Business](/resources/ocr-gcse-business-operations-finance-and-influences-on-business/).

## Component 01's content stays live for Component 02

Because Component 02 is synoptic, the marketing and people content examined in Component 01 can
reappear in it. **Exam-preparation priority**: do not treat Component 01 as finished once it has been
sat. A question about a business's operations or cash flow may reasonably expect you to bring in what
you know about its market, its customers or its staffing -- and answers that make that connection are
exactly what synoptic assessment is designed to reward.

## Eighty marks in ninety minutes leaves no slack

At a little over a minute per mark, there is no room for an over-long answer to an early
low-tariff question. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise reading the mark allocation before the
question and writing to it. A one-mark identify question needs a phrase; a two-mark question needs a
point and a brief reason. Treating every question as an opportunity for a paragraph is the most common
way to run out of time on this paper.

## Case-study context is what separates a good answer from a generic one

Business questions on this specification are typically anchored to a described firm. **Exam-
preparation priority**: build the habit of noting three things about the business before answering --
what it sells, how big it is, and what pressure it faces -- and referring to at least one of them in
every developed answer. A textbook-correct point about cash flow that never mentions the business in
front of you is a generic answer, and it is marked as one.

## Calculation questions are followed by interpretation questions

Business finance content -- revenue, costs, profit, break-even, cash flow -- produces questions where
the arithmetic is only the first half. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise the second half.
Calculate the figure, then say what it means for this business's decision. The interpretation is
usually where the extra marks are, and it is the part most often left off.

## Evaluation questions want a decision, not a survey

Higher-tariff questions ask whether a business should do something. **Exam-preparation priority**:
practise finishing. State a recommendation, give the main reason, and name the factor it depends on.
An answer that presents advantages and disadvantages and then stops has done most of the thinking and
declined the mark for it.

## Worked routine: calculate, then interpret

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - write the relationship before the numbers:
"Break-even output = fixed costs / contribution per unit"

Step 2 - substitute with the figures given, showing working:
= 12,000 / (8 - 5) = 4,000 units

Step 3 - state what the figure means for THIS business:
"The business must sell 4,000 units before it makes any profit."

Step 4 - connect it to the decision the question asks about:
"Since its current monthly sales are 3,200 units, it is currently
operating below break-even, which supports the case for reducing
fixed costs before expanding."

Step 5 - name one limitation of the calculation:
Break-even assumes costs and price stay constant -- saying so is a
straightforward evaluation mark.
```

Steps 3 and 4 are where the marks beyond the arithmetic sit. A correct number with no interpretation
answers only the first part of what was asked.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: keep Component 01 content revised for Component 02's synoptic questions;
  practise writing to the mark tariff; practise finance calculations followed immediately by
  interpretation; rehearse finishing evaluation questions with a recommendation.
- **During either component**: read the mark allocation before the question and match the answer
  length to it.
- **On every case study**: note what the business sells, its scale, and the pressure it faces, and
  refer to one of them in each developed answer.
- **On every calculation**: state the relationship, show working, interpret the figure, connect it to
  the decision, and name one limitation.

## Self-test

1. Which component carries synoptic assessment, and what follows from that?
2. How much time is available per mark, and what is the implication?
3. What three things should be noted about a case-study business before answering?
4. What is usually missing from an incomplete calculation answer?

**Answers:** 1. Component 02 -- so Component 01's marketing and people content can be drawn on within
it, and revision of Component 01 should not stop once that paper is sat. 2. A little over a minute per
mark (90 minutes for 80 marks), so low-tariff questions must be answered briefly to protect time for
the higher-tariff ones. 3. What it sells, how big it is, and what pressure or constraint it faces --
at least one of which should appear in every developed answer. 4. The interpretation: what the
calculated figure means for this business and for the decision the question is about.
