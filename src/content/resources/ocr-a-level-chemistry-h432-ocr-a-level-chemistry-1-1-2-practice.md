The questions here practise **1.1.2 Implementing** in OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432), Version 3.1 (May 2026), for first assessment in 2017: using apparatus correctly (a), appropriate units (b) and presenting observations and data (c). Section A has short questions; Section B has calculations and tables. Use R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ and c = 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹ where needed.

Read the [1.1.2 study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-h432-ocr-a-level-chemistry-1-1-2/) and [1.1.2 revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-h432-ocr-a-level-chemistry-1-1-2-revision-notes/) first. See also the [OCR chemistry hub](/boards/ocr/a-level/chemistry/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Burette readings.

**(a)** The bottom of the meniscus is halfway between the 12.6 and 12.7 marks. Record the reading. **[1]**

**(b)** The initial reading is 0.40 cm³ and the final reading is 21.85 cm³. Calculate the titre. **[1]**

**2.** A balance displays 3.50 g. A student records the mass as 3.5 g. Explain why this record is not correct. **[1]**

**3.** Convert each value into the unit given.

**(a)** 18.25 cm³ into dm³. **[1]**

**(b)** 98 kPa into Pa. **[1]**

**(c)** 45 °C into K. **[1]**

**4.** Give the units of Kc for each equilibrium.

**(a)** PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g) **[1]**

**(b)** 2NO₂(g) ⇌ N₂O₄(g) **[1]**

**5.** Give the units of the rate constant k for each rate equation.

**(a)** rate = k[X][Y] **[1]**

**(b)** rate = k[X]²[Y] **[1]**

**6.** Rewrite each observation precisely.

**(a)** Silver nitrate was added to an acidified solution containing chloride ions: "it went cloudy". **[1]**

**(b)** A primary alcohol was warmed with acidified potassium dichromate(VI): "it went green". **[1]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** 0.200 g of a gas occupies 115 cm³ at 25 °C and 100 kPa. Calculate the Mr of the gas. **[4]**

**8.** In a reaction carried out in solution, 0.0250 mol of the limiting reactant reacts. The 50.0 g of solution rises in temperature by 8.5 K. Calculate the enthalpy change of reaction in kJ mol⁻¹, to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**9.** For N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), use the data ΔH = −92 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = −199 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate ΔG at 298 K. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the temperature above which the reaction is no longer feasible. **[2]**

**10.** A student records the temperature of a reaction mixture every half minute, using a thermometer marked every 1 °C.

| Time (min) | Temp |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | 21 °C |
| 1.0 | 21.5 °C |
| 1.5 | 24.25 °C |

State three ways in which the table should be improved. **[3]**

**11.** A student's titration results are shown.

| | Rough | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Final reading / cm³ | 25.9 | 25.40 | 26.55 | 25.90 |
| Initial reading / cm³ | 0 | 0.10 | 1.00 | 0.7 |

**(a)** Identify two errors in the way the readings have been recorded. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the titres for runs 1 to 3, choose the concordant titres and find the mean titre. **[3]**

**12.** Describe how a student should use a pipette to transfer exactly 25.0 cm³ of a solution into a conical flask. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **12.65 cm³** [1]

**(b)** 21.85 − 0.40 = **21.45 cm³** [1]

Total: 2

**2.** The balance shows the mass to two decimal places, so it should be recorded as 3.50 g; dropping the zero suggests a lower precision than was measured [1]

Total: 1

**3. (a)** 18.25 ÷ 1000 = **0.01825 dm³** [1]

**(b)** 98 × 1000 = **98 000 Pa** [1]

**(c)** 45 + 273 = **318 K** [1]

Total: 3

**4. (a)** Kc = [PCl₃][Cl₂] ÷ [PCl₅], so (mol dm⁻³)² ÷ mol dm⁻³ = **mol dm⁻³** [1]

**(b)** Kc = [N₂O₄] ÷ [NO₂]², so mol dm⁻³ ÷ (mol dm⁻³)² = **dm³ mol⁻¹** [1]

Total: 2

**5. (a)** mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ ÷ (mol dm⁻³)² = **dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ ÷ (mol dm⁻³)³ = **dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹** [1]

Total: 2

**6. (a)** **A white precipitate formed** [1]

**(b)** **The solution changed from orange to green** [1]

Total: 2

*Examiner insight:* "Cloudy" does not say a solid has formed or what colour it is, and "went green" gives no starting colour.

**7.** p = 1.00 × 10⁵ Pa, V = 1.15 × 10⁻⁴ m³, T = 298 K [1]; n = pV ÷ RT = (1.00 × 10⁵ × 1.15 × 10⁻⁴) ÷ (8.314 × 298) [1]; n = 4.64 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; Mr = 0.200 ÷ 4.64 × 10⁻³ = **43.1** [1]

Total: 4

*Examiner insight:* Leaving the volume in cm³ gives n a million times too large and an Mr of about 4 × 10⁻⁵, which should prompt a check of the units.

**8.** q = mcΔT = 50.0 × 4.18 × 8.5 = 1776.5 J [1]; = 1.7765 kJ [1]; ΔH = −1.7765 ÷ 0.0250 = **−71.1 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]

Total: 3

*Examiner insight:* The temperature rose, so the reaction is exothermic and ΔH is negative. The sign is part of the answer.

**9. (a)** ΔS = −0.199 kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹; ΔG = −92 − 298 × (−0.199) [1]; = −92 + 59.3 = **−32.7 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** Not feasible when ΔG > 0, which begins where ΔH − TΔS = 0, so T = ΔH ÷ ΔS = −92 ÷ −0.199 [1]; T = **462 K** [1]

Total: 4

*Examiner insight:* Without the conversion to kJ, part (a) gives ΔG = +59 210, a meaningless value. Both ΔH and ΔS are negative, so the reaction becomes unfeasible above 462 K, not below it.

**10.** Any three: give each heading with its unit using a solidus, "time / min" and "temperature / °C" [1]; remove the units from the body of the table [1]; record temperatures to the same precision, matching the thermometer's resolution, for example 21.0, 21.5 and 24.5 [1]; 24.25 °C is more precise than a thermometer marked every 1 °C can be read.

Total: 3

**11. (a)** The rough readings are given as 25.9 and 0 instead of 25.90 and 0.00 [1]. The initial reading for run 3 is given as 0.7 instead of 0.70 [1]

**(b)** Titres: 25.30, 25.55 and 25.20 cm³ [1]; concordant titres are 25.30 and 25.20 (within 0.10 cm³), so run 2 is left out [1]; mean = (25.30 + 25.20) ÷ 2 = **25.25 cm³** [1]

Total: 5

*Examiner insight:* The rough titre is never included in the mean. Show which titres you have chosen, for example by ticking them, so the mean can be followed.

**12.** Rinse the pipette with a little of the solution it will hold [1]. Using a pipette filler, fill it until the bottom of the meniscus is on the graduation mark, viewed at eye level [1]. Let the solution drain into the conical flask, touch the tip on the inside of the flask or the liquid surface, and do not blow out the drop left in the tip [1]

Total: 3

## Where marks are usually lost

- Burette readings or titres recorded without the second decimal place.
- Dropping trailing zeros from balance readings.
- Leaving volumes in cm³ in pV = nRT, or mixing kJ and J.
- Guessing the units of Kc or k instead of cancelling them.
- Missing the negative sign on an exothermic ΔH.
- Observations that give a conclusion, or only one colour.
- Including the rough titre in the mean.
