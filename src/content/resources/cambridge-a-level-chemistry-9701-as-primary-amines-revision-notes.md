**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **AS Level** subtopic 19.1:
recall the reaction of a halogenoalkane with NH₃ in ethanol heated under pressure to produce an amine.
Classification of amines is not tested at AS Level.

## The one reaction to know

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Reagent | ammonia, NH₃ (in excess) |
| Solvent | ethanol |
| Conditions | heat, under pressure, in a sealed vessel |
| Mechanism | nucleophilic substitution |
| Nucleophile | NH₃, via the lone pair on N |
| Products | primary amine + ammonium halide |
| General equation | R–X + 2NH₃ → R–NH₂ + NH₄X |

## Reasons for the conditions

- **Sealed vessel, under pressure:** NH₃ is a gas and would escape on heating.
- **Ethanol:** dissolves both reactants; no water, so the halogenoalkane is not hydrolysed to an alcohol.
- **Heat:** speeds up a slow substitution.
- **Excess NH₃:** provides the second NH₃ that removes H⁺, and makes it likely that the halogenoalkane
  meets ammonia.

## Two-step mechanism, in words

1. Lone pair on N of NH₃ → δ+ carbon; C–X bonding pair → X, which leaves as X⁻.
   Product: alkylammonium ion, R–NH₃⁺.
2. Second NH₃ removes H⁺ from R–NH₃⁺. Products: R–NH₂ + NH₄⁺.

Primary halogenoalkanes react by S<sub>N</sub>2 (one step, through a transition state); tertiary ones by
S<sub>N</sub>1 (via a carbocation).

## Equations to practise

    CH₃Br + 2NH₃ → CH₃NH₂ + NH₄Br                          (methylamine)
    CH₃CH₂Cl + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH₂NH₂ + NH₄Cl                    (ethylamine)
    CH₃CH₂CH₂I + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂ + NH₄I                (propylamine)
    CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂Br + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂NH₂ + NH₄Br        (butylamine)
    CH₃CHBrCH₃ + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH(NH₂)CH₃ + NH₄Br              (NH₂ on C2)

## Halogen and rate

| C–X | Bond energy / kJ mol⁻¹ | Rate of substitution |
|---|---|---|
| C–Cl | 340 | slowest |
| C–Br | 280 | intermediate |
| C–I | 240 | fastest |

The weaker the C–X bond, the faster the reaction.

## Nucleophilic substitutions of halogenoalkanes side by side

| Reagent and conditions | Nucleophile | Product from bromoethane |
|---|---|---|
| NaOH(aq), heat | OH⁻ | ethanol |
| KCN in ethanol, heat | CN⁻ | propanenitrile |
| NH₃ in ethanol, heat under pressure | NH₃ | ethylamine |

## Getting to an amine from other starting materials

| Start | Step 1 | Step 2 |
|---|---|---|
| alkene (ethene) | HBr(g), room temperature → bromoethane | NH₃, ethanol, heat under pressure → ethylamine |
| alcohol (ethanol) | PCl₅ or SOCl₂ → chloroethane | NH₃, ethanol, heat under pressure → ethylamine |
| alkane (ethane) | Br₂, UV light → bromoethane (in a mixture) | NH₃, ethanol, heat under pressure → ethylamine |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Primary amine | compound with an –NH₂ group bonded to a carbon chain, R–NH₂ |
| Nucleophile | electron-pair donor that attacks an electron-deficient carbon |
| Nucleophilic substitution | a nucleophile replaces a leaving group, here the halide |
| Alkylammonium ion | R–NH₃⁺, the intermediate formed when NH₃ bonds to the carbon |
| Heterolytic fission | bond breaking in which one atom takes both electrons (here the halogen) |
| Sealed tube | closed vessel that allows heating under pressure without losing gas |

## Exam traps

- Write **2NH₃** in the overall equation, with **NH₄X**, not HX, as the second product.
- Conditions must include **ethanol** and **pressure** (or sealed tube) as well as heat.
- The curly arrow starts at the **lone pair on N**.
- An amine from a halogenoalkane has the **same number of carbons**; a nitrile from KCN has one more.
- Primary/secondary/tertiary amine classification is **not tested at AS**: do not spend time on it.
- Propylamine is **C₃H₉N** (CₙH₂ₙ₊₃N).

## Self-test

1. Give the reagent and conditions to make ethylamine from bromoethane.
2. Write the overall equation for making propylamine from 1-chloropropane.
3. Explain why the reaction is carried out in a sealed tube.
4. Why is ethanol used as the solvent rather than water?
5. Name the type of mechanism.
6. Which feature of NH₃ makes it a nucleophile?
7. Give the formula of the intermediate ion formed from bromomethane and ammonia.
8. Why are two moles of ammonia needed per mole of halogenoalkane?
9. Which reacts fastest with ammonia: 1-chlorobutane, 1-bromobutane or 1-iodobutane? Explain.
10. Give the molecular formula of butylamine.
11. Suggest a two-step route from propene to an amine, naming the reagents.
12. 0.0500 mol of bromoethane reacts completely. What amount of ammonia is used?

**Answers:**

1. NH₃ in ethanol, heated under pressure (sealed tube).
2. CH₃CH₂CH₂Cl + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂ + NH₄Cl.
3. Ammonia is a gas; in a sealed tube it cannot escape on heating, so it stays in contact with the
   halogenoalkane.
4. Ethanol dissolves both reactants, and the absence of water prevents hydrolysis of the halogenoalkane to
   an alcohol.
5. Nucleophilic substitution.
6. The lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom.
7. CH₃NH₃⁺.
8. One NH₃ substitutes for the halogen; the second removes H⁺ from the alkylammonium ion to release the
   free amine.
9. 1-iodobutane: the C–I bond (240 kJ mol⁻¹) is the weakest, so it breaks most easily.
10. C₄H₁₁N.
11. HBr(g) at room temperature gives 2-bromopropane; then NH₃ in ethanol, heated under pressure, gives
    CH₃CH(NH₂)CH₃.
12. 0.100 mol (2 : 1).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the basicity of amines part of AS 19.1?**
No. At AS the only requirement is the reaction that makes amines. Basicity of amines is A Level content
in 34.1.

**Why do some books write the product as an ammonium salt of the amine?**
With only one mole of ammonia the product is the alkylammonium salt, such as CH₃CH₂NH₃⁺Br⁻. Excess ammonia
removes the H⁺ to give the free amine, which is what the 9701 equation shows.
