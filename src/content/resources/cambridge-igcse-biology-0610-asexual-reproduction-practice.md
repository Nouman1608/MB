Questions and parts marked **(Extended)** use Supplement outcome 16.1.3, or the link to mitosis; a Core candidate can skip them. Data in the questions are invented for practice.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which statement describes asexual reproduction? **[1]**

A: the fusion of two gametes from one parent

B: the production of genetically different offspring from one parent

C: the production of genetically identical offspring from one parent

D: the production of offspring from two parents without fertilisation

**2.** State **two** ways in which asexual reproduction differs from sexual reproduction. **[2]**

**3.** For each description, state whether it shows asexual or sexual reproduction.

**(a)** A spider plant grows long stems with small plantlets at their ends, which root in the soil. **[1]**

**(b)** A sunflower forms seeds after bees carry pollen to it from another sunflower. **[1]**

**(c)** A yeast cell grows a small outgrowth that separates as a new cell. **[1]**

**(d)** A female frog lays eggs, which a male frog fertilises in the water. **[1]**

**4.** In one example, a bacterium divides every 25 minutes. Calculate how many cells are produced from one cell after 150 minutes, if no cells die. **[2]**

**5.** A student writes: "Identical twins are produced by asexual reproduction, because they are genetically identical." Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Explain why offspring produced by asexual reproduction are genetically identical to their parent. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A floating pond plant grows short horizontal stems. A new plant forms at the end of each stem and then breaks away.

**(a)** Identify the type of reproduction shown and give a reason from the description. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why this plant can cover the surface of a pond quickly. **[2]**

**(c)** *(Extended)* Suggest one disadvantage to the population of reproducing only in this way if conditions in the pond change. **[2]**

**8.** A grower compared potato plants of one variety grown from tubers with potato plants grown from seeds. All plants were grown in the same field. The table shows the mass of potatoes from five plants of each kind.

| Plants grown from | Mass of potatoes per plant / kg |
|---|---|
| Tubers | 1.8, 1.9, 1.8, 1.9, 1.8 |
| Seeds | 1.1, 2.3, 0.7, 1.9, 1.5 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean mass per plant for each kind of plant. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the range of masses for each kind of plant. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the masses from tuber-grown plants vary less than the masses from seed-grown plants. **[2]**

**(d)** Suggest why the masses from tuber-grown plants are not all exactly the same. **[1]**

**9.** *(Extended)* Banana growers plant suckers, young shoots that grow from the base of a parent plant, to start new banana plants.

**(a)** State how the genes of the new plants compare with those of the parent plant. **[1]**

**(b)** Discuss the advantages and disadvantages to growers of producing banana plants in this way. **[4]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A species of grass grows in two areas. In area A, a stable meadow, it reproduces mainly asexually by underground stems. In area B, it reproduces mainly sexually by seeds.

**(a)** Suggest why asexual reproduction is successful for the population in area A. **[2]**

**(b)** A new fungal disease reaches both areas. Explain why the population in area B is more likely to survive. **[3]**

**11.** *Hydra* is a small freshwater animal. When food is plentiful, small *Hydra* grow from the side of an adult and break away. When conditions become unfavourable, *Hydra* produce gametes instead.

**(a)** Identify the type of reproduction used when food is plentiful. **[1]**

**(b)** State the number of parents involved in this type of reproduction. **[1]**

**(c)** *(Extended)* Suggest an advantage of switching to sexual reproduction when conditions become unfavourable. **[2]**

**12.** Copy and complete the table to compare asexual and sexual reproduction. **[4]**

| Feature | Asexual reproduction | Sexual reproduction |
|---|---|---|
| Number of parents | | usually two |
| Gametes involved | no | |
| Offspring genetically identical? | | no |
| Speed of producing offspring | | slower |

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]

*Examiner insight:* B reverses the genetic point, and A and D describe things that do not happen in asexual reproduction.

**2.** Any two of: only **one parent** is needed; **no gametes** are made; there is **no fertilisation**; offspring are **genetically identical** rather than genetically different [2].

**3. (a)** **Asexual** [1]

**(b)** **Sexual** [1]

**(c)** **Asexual** [1]

**(d)** **Sexual** [1]

**4.** 150 ÷ 25 = **6 divisions** [1]; 2⁶ = **64 cells** [1].

*Examiner insight:* A common slip is 2 × 6 = 12. Each division doubles the number of cells.

**5.** Identical twins develop from a **zygote** [1], which formed by **fertilisation of an egg by a sperm**, so they are the result of sexual reproduction [1].

**6.** The new cells are produced by **mitosis** [1], which gives cells with **the same chromosomes / genes as the parent cell** [1].

**7. (a)** **Asexual reproduction** [1]; the new plants grow from **part of one parent** with no gametes or fertilisation [1].

**(b)** Only one parent is needed, so **every plant can reproduce** without a mate or pollinator [1]; new plants form **quickly**, so the number of plants rises fast [1].

**(c)** All the plants are **genetically identical / there is no variation** [1], so if the change harms one plant it is likely to harm them **all**; the population cannot adapt [1].

**8. (a)** Tubers: 9.2 ÷ 5 = **1.84 kg** [1]. Seeds: 7.5 ÷ 5 = **1.50 kg** [1].

**(b)** Tubers: 1.9 − 1.8 = **0.1 kg**; seeds: 2.3 − 0.7 = **1.6 kg** [1].

**(c)** Tuber-grown plants come from **asexual reproduction**, so they are **genetically identical** [1]. Seed-grown plants come from **sexual reproduction**, so they are **genetically different** and their yields vary more [1].

**(d)** Small differences in the **environment**, such as water, soil nutrients or light, affect each plant [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (c), a mark needs the genetic reason, not just "they are clones" with no link to variation in yield.

**9. (a)** They are **genetically identical** to the parent [1].

**(b)** Advantages: every plant has the **same desirable features** as the parent, such as fruit quality [1]; plants are produced **quickly / without seeds**, which matters because the bananas grown commercially make few seeds [1]. Disadvantages: there is **no variation** [1], so a single **disease or pest** able to infect the variety could spread through and destroy the whole plantation [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Discuss" needs both sides; four advantages and no disadvantage would score at most two marks.

**10. (a)** The meadow is **stable**, so the features of the parent that suit it are kept in every offspring [1]; reproduction is **fast** and needs **no mate or pollination**, so the grass spreads easily [1].

**(b)** Sexual reproduction produces **genetic variation** [1], so some plants in area B may have alleles that make them **resistant** to the fungus [1]. These survive and reproduce, while in area A the plants are **genetically identical**, so if one is killed all may be killed [1].

**11. (a)** **Asexual** reproduction (budding) [1]

**(b)** **One** [1]

**(c)** Sexual reproduction produces **variation** among the offspring [1], so some offspring may have features that help them **survive the new conditions** [1].

**12.** Asexual, number of parents: **one** [1]. Sexual, gametes involved: **yes** [1]. Asexual, genetically identical: **yes** [1]. Asexual, speed: **faster** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- A definition with only "one parent" and no reference to genetically identical offspring.
- Classing seeds from self-pollination as asexual.
- Writing "no variation" without saying why it matters in the context given.
- (Extended) Giving only advantages in a "discuss" question.
- (Extended) Using wild-population points, such as finding a mate, in a crop question.
