Subtopic 22.2 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers the emission of photoelectrons from an illuminated metal surface, threshold frequency and threshold wavelength, the explanation in terms of photon energy and work function, the equation hf = Φ + ½mv²max, and why maximum kinetic energy is independent of intensity while photoelectric current is proportional to it. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Photoelectric effect study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-photoelectric-effect/); to apply the ideas, try the [Photoelectric effect practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-photoelectric-effect-practice/).

## The equations

```
hf = Φ + ½mv²max            (photon energy = work function + max KE)
f₀ = Φ / h                  (threshold frequency: minimum)
λ₀ = hc / Φ                 (threshold wavelength: maximum)
½mv²max = hf − Φ            (straight line: gradient h, f-intercept f₀, y-intercept −Φ)
photocurrent = (electrons per second) × e
```

## The explanation in five points

1. Radiation arrives as photons, each of energy hf.
2. One photon gives all its energy to one electron.
3. The electron escapes only if hf ≥ Φ (the minimum energy to leave the surface).
4. Surplus energy hf − Φ becomes kinetic energy; electrons from below the surface lose some on the way out, so this is a maximum.
5. An electron cannot accumulate energy from several photons, so below f₀ there is no emission at any intensity.

## Intensity versus frequency

| Change (others fixed) | Photons per second | Energy per photon | Max KE | Current |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intensity doubled (same f > f₀) | × 2 | same | same | × 2 |
| Frequency increased (same photon rate) | same | ↑ | ↑ | same |
| Frequency increased (same power) | ↓ | ↑ | ↑ | ↓ |
| Frequency below f₀ (any intensity) | — | < Φ | none | 0 |
| Metal with larger Φ (same light) | same | same | ↓ | may fall to 0 |

## Evidence against the wave model

| Wave model predicts | Observed |
|---|---|
| Emission at any frequency if intense enough | Threshold frequency |
| Higher intensity → higher electron energy | Max KE depends only on f |
| Delay at low intensity while energy builds up | Emission immediate |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Photoelectron | Electron emitted from a surface by electromagnetic radiation |
| Work function Φ | Minimum energy needed to remove an electron from the surface of a metal |
| Threshold frequency f₀ | Minimum frequency of radiation that causes photoelectric emission |
| Threshold wavelength λ₀ | Maximum wavelength of radiation that causes photoelectric emission |
| Maximum kinetic energy | hf − Φ; kinetic energy of the fastest emitted electrons |
| Intensity | Power per unit area; ∝ photons per second at fixed f |
| Photoelectric current | Rate of flow of photoelectrons × e |

## Graph of max KE against f

- Straight line; gradient = h (same for all metals).
- Crosses the f-axis at f₀.
- Extrapolates to −Φ on the KE-axis.
- Different metals: parallel lines.

## One calculation, start to finish

Light of wavelength 450 nm on a surface with Φ = 2.0 eV:

```
photon energy   hc/λ = 4.42 × 10⁻¹⁹ J  (2.76 eV)
work function   2.0 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
max KE          4.42 − 3.20 = 1.22 × 10⁻¹⁹ J  (0.76 eV)
max speed       √(2 × 1.22 × 10⁻¹⁹ / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) = 5.2 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹
threshold λ₀    hc/Φ = 6.2 × 10⁻⁷ m  (so 450 nm is short enough)
```

## Exam traps

- Work function defined without "minimum" or without "from the surface".
- Subtracting eV from J.
- Threshold wavelength called a minimum.
- "Brighter light, faster electrons."
- Dropping "maximum" from kinetic energy.
- Forgetting that the speed comes from ½mv²max with the electron mass.
- Saying the current is proportional to intensity even below f₀.

## Self-test

1. Define work function.
2. Define threshold frequency.
3. Calculate f₀ and λ₀ for a metal with Φ = 2.3 eV.
4. Radiation of wavelength 200 nm falls on a metal with Φ = 4.5 eV. Calculate the maximum kinetic energy in eV.
5. A metal has a threshold wavelength of 540 nm. Calculate its work function in eV.
6. What is the gradient of a graph of maximum kinetic energy against frequency?
7. Why do photoelectrons have a range of kinetic energies?
8. Light intensity is tripled at a fixed frequency above f₀. What happens to the current and the maximum kinetic energy?
9. Explain why very intense red light may cause no emission when weak ultraviolet does.
10. Why is there no time delay before emission?
11. The frequency is increased with the beam power kept constant. What happens to the number of photons per second?
12. On a graph of max KE against f for two metals, how are the lines related?

**Answers:** 1. The minimum energy needed to remove an electron from the surface of a metal. 2. The minimum frequency of electromagnetic radiation that causes emission of electrons from the surface. 3. f₀ = Φ/h = 3.68 × 10⁻¹⁹ / 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ = 5.6 × 10¹⁴ Hz; λ₀ = c/f₀ = 5.4 × 10⁻⁷ m. 4. Photon energy = 6.2 eV; max KE = 6.2 − 4.5 = 1.7 eV. 5. Φ = hc/λ₀ = 3.68 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = 2.3 eV. 6. The Planck constant h. 7. Electrons below the surface lose energy before escaping; only surface electrons leave with the maximum hf − Φ. 8. The current triples; the maximum kinetic energy is unchanged. 9. Each red photon has energy below Φ, and one photon interacts with one electron, so no number of red photons frees an electron; each ultraviolet photon has energy above Φ. 10. A single photon delivers all the energy needed in one interaction. 11. It decreases, because each photon carries more energy. 12. They are parallel (same gradient h), displaced along the frequency axis.
