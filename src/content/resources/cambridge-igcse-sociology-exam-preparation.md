Cambridge IGCSE Sociology (0495) is a linear, not-tiered qualification of two equally weighted written
papers, both externally assessed. Paper 1 (Research Methods, Identity and Inequality) is 2 hours, 80
marks, 50% -- structured as a compulsory Question 1 plus a choice of Question 2 or Question 3. Paper 2
(Family, Education and Crime) is 1 hour 45 minutes, 80 marks, 50% -- structured as a choice of two out of
three optional questions, with no compulsory question. These notes complement the site's guides to
[Research Methods, Identity and Inequality](/resources/igcse-sociology-research-methods-identity-and-inequality/)
and [Family, Education and Crime](/resources/igcse-cambridge-sociology-family-education-crime/).

## The two papers use genuinely different choice structures

Paper 1 forces every candidate through the same compulsory question before offering a choice; Paper 2
offers a free choice of two from three topics, with nothing compulsory. **Exam-preparation priority**:
revise Research Methods thoroughly regardless of topic preference, since Paper 1's compulsory question
cannot be avoided the way Paper 2's optional topics can be.

## Paper 2's genuine choice rewards deep preparation on fewer topics

Since Paper 2 only requires answering two of its three optional questions, candidates do not need equal
depth across all three of Family, Education and Crime. **Exam-preparation priority**: identify your two
strongest topics from Paper 2's three options ahead of the exam and prepare those two in real depth,
rather than spreading revision thinly across all three "just in case."

## Research Methods content underpins Paper 1's compulsory question specifically

Question 1's compulsory status means every candidate is examined on methods content regardless of which
optional question they later choose. **Exam-preparation priority**: treat sampling, questionnaires,
interviews, observation and their strengths/limitations as non-negotiable revision, distinct from the
identity and inequality content that only appears in the optional Questions 2/3.

## Sociological concepts need to be applied to named studies, not just defined

Across both papers, higher-mark questions reward applying a concept to specific sociological research
rather than defining the concept alone. **Exam-preparation priority**: pair every key concept learned
with at least one named study or example, so application material is ready regardless of which question
comes up.

## Evaluation is examined on both papers, not just description

Higher-mark questions on both Paper 1 and Paper 2 ask candidates to assess the strengths and limitations
of a sociological perspective, method or piece of research, not merely to describe it accurately.
**Exam-preparation priority**: for every major theory or method learned, prepare at least one stated
strength and one stated limitation in advance, so evaluative material is ready regardless of which
specific question is set.

## Worked routine: applying a concept to a named study

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - define the concept precisely, in one or two sentences:
Establishes understanding before application begins.

Step 2 - name a specific relevant study or example:
Not sociology "in general" -- a named piece of research or a
specific documented example.

Step 3 - explain exactly how the study illustrates the concept:
The connection must be stated, not left for the marker to infer.

Step 4 - note a limitation of the study or a competing
perspective:
Shows evaluative depth beyond simple description.

Step 5 - link back to the specific question asked:
Ties the whole answer to the actual terms of the question, not a
general essay on the topic.
```

Step 3 is the step most answers skip under time pressure -- naming a study without explaining how it
actually demonstrates the concept earns only partial credit for what could otherwise be a strong answer.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: revise Research Methods thoroughly regardless of topic preference, since Paper
  1's Question 1 is compulsory; pick your two strongest Paper 2 topics and prepare them in depth rather
  than all three thinly; pair every concept with a named study.
- **During Paper 1**: answer the compulsory question fully before choosing between the optional
  questions.
- **During Paper 2**: select your two strongest of the three optional questions early, rather than
  starting one and switching partway through.

## Self-test

1. How do Paper 1 and Paper 2's question structures differ?
2. Why should Research Methods be revised regardless of Paper 2 topic preference?
3. Why is it better to prepare two Paper 2 topics in depth rather than all three lightly?
4. What makes a concept-application answer stronger than a definition alone?

**Answers:** 1. Paper 1 has a compulsory Question 1 plus a choice of Question 2 or 3; Paper 2 has a free
choice of two from three optional questions, with nothing compulsory. 2. Because Paper 1's Question 1
tests Research Methods content and every candidate must answer it, regardless of which optional
questions they choose. 3. Because Paper 2 only requires answering two of its three optional questions,
so depth on two topics is more valuable than shallow coverage of all three. 4. Naming a specific study or
example and explicitly explaining how it demonstrates the concept, rather than defining the concept in
the abstract.
