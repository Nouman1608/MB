Condensed notes on section **6.3 Business and the international economy** of Cambridge O Level Business Studies 7115 (for examination in 2026). The reasoning chains and a model answer are in the [international economy study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-business-and-the-international-economy/); exam-style questions are in the [international economy practice set](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-business-and-the-international-economy-practice/).

**Remember:** exchange rate **calculations are not assessed**. You must explain the direction of the effects.

## The syllabus in three lines

- **6.3.1** Globalisation and its causes; opportunities and threats for businesses; why governments use import tariffs and quotas.
- **6.3.2** Why MNCs grow; benefits to the business and impact on its stakeholders; benefits and drawbacks for the host country.
- **6.3.3** Appreciation and depreciation; effects on importers and exporters (prices, competitiveness, profitability).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Globalisation | Increasing connection of the world's economies through trade, money, people and ideas |
| Import tariff | A tax on imported goods |
| Import quota | A limit on the quantity of a good that may be imported |
| Protectionism | Government measures that shield local businesses from foreign competition |
| Multinational company (MNC) | A business with operations in more than one country |
| Host country | The country where an MNC sets up operations |
| Repatriation of profits | Sending profits made abroad back to the MNC's home country |
| Exchange rate | The price of one currency in terms of another |
| Appreciation | Rise in the value of a currency: it buys more foreign currency |
| Depreciation | Fall in the value of a currency: it buys less foreign currency |
| Competitiveness | The ability to win customers from rivals, often on price |

## Why globalisation has grown

Cheaper transport; the internet and better communications; fewer trade barriers; growth of MNCs; rising incomes creating new markets.

## Opportunities and threats by business type

- **Exporter:** bigger markets and spread risk / foreign rivals and currency risk.
- **Buys inputs abroad:** cheaper or better inputs / disruption and cost rises.
- **Competes with imports at home:** partnerships and new ideas / loses customers to cheaper imports.
- **Sets up abroad:** lower costs, near customers / unfamiliar laws, culture, political risk.

## Tariffs and quotas

| | Tariff | Quota |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | Raises the price of imports | Limits the quantity of imports |
| Raises government revenue? | Yes | No |
| Effect on local producers | Less price competition | Less volume competition |
| Effect on consumers | Higher prices, less choice | Less choice; prices may rise as supply is limited |

**Why governments use them:** protect local firms and jobs; protect new industries; cut spending on imports; raise revenue (tariffs).
**Who loses:** consumers; local firms using imported inputs; exporters if other countries retaliate.

## Multinationals

**Benefits to the business:** new markets; lower costs; avoid trade barriers; spread risk; economies of scale.

**Stakeholder impact:** shareholders may gain higher dividends; home-country workers may lose jobs if production moves; customers get more choice; managers face more complex control.

**Host country:**

- Benefits: jobs, exports, increased choice, investment, new skills and technology.
- Drawbacks: local firms lose sales, repatriation of profits, senior jobs to foreign staff, the MNC may leave, possible environmental harm.

## Exchange rate effects (no calculations)

| Home currency | Exporter | Importer |
|---|---|---|
| Depreciates | Cheaper abroad → more competitive → sales and profit may rise | Imports dearer → costs rise → higher prices or lower margins |
| Appreciates | Dearer abroad → less competitive → sales may fall | Imports cheaper → costs fall → lower prices or higher margins |

A firm that both imports inputs and exports products feels both effects; the net result depends on which is bigger.

## Exam traps

- A quota does not raise revenue; a tariff does.
- Appreciation means **up**, depreciation means **down**.
- Always say whether the business is an exporter, an importer or both.
- An MNC is neither all good nor all bad for a host country.
- Finish every chain with an effect on price, competitiveness, sales or profit.

## Self-test

1. Define globalisation.
2. Give three reasons why globalisation has grown.
3. State one difference between an import tariff and an import quota.
4. Give two reasons why a government might introduce an import tariff.
5. A local bicycle maker uses imported tyres. How might a tariff on imported tyres affect it?
6. Give two benefits to a business of becoming a multinational.
7. How might workers in an MNC's home country be affected when it opens factories abroad?
8. Give two benefits and two drawbacks to a host country of an MNC.
9. What is meant by repatriation of profits?
10. The home currency depreciates. What is the likely effect on an exporter's competitiveness?
11. The home currency appreciates. What is the likely effect on an importer's costs?
12. Why is the effect of a depreciation on a business that imports materials and exports products uncertain?

### Answers

1. The increasing connection of the world's economies, as goods, services, money, people and ideas move more freely between countries.
2. Any three: cheaper transport; better communications and the internet; fewer trade barriers; growth of MNCs; rising incomes.
3. A tariff is a tax that raises the price of imports; a quota limits the quantity that can be imported.
4. Any two: protect local businesses and jobs; protect new industries; reduce spending on imports; raise revenue.
5. Its costs rise, so it must raise prices (and may lose sales) or accept lower profit margins.
6. Any two: new markets; lower costs; avoiding trade barriers; spreading risk; economies of scale.
7. They may lose jobs if production moves abroad, though some may gain new career opportunities.
8. Benefits: any two of jobs, exports, increased choice, investment, new skills. Drawbacks: any two of reduced sales for local firms, repatriation of profits, senior jobs to foreign staff, the MNC may leave, environmental harm.
9. An MNC sending the profits made in a host country back to its home country.
10. Its products become cheaper for foreign buyers, so it becomes more competitive and sales may rise.
11. Imported goods cost less in home currency, so its costs fall.
12. Its imported materials cost more, but its exports become more competitive; the overall effect depends on which effect is larger.
