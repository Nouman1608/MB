## Answers

**1.** In a rechargeable cell, an external current can reverse the electrode reactions and restore the reactants [1]; in a non-rechargeable cell the reactions cannot practically be reversed, so it is discarded when a reactant is used up [1]

**2.** Fuel (hydrogen) and oxygen are supplied continuously from outside, so the reactants never run out while the supply continues [1]

**3.** Oxidation at the negative electrode releases electrons [1]; the electrons flow through the external circuit to the positive electrode, where reduction uses them [1]; ions move through the electrolyte to balance the charge [1]

**4.** Li⁺ + CoO₂ + e⁻ → Li⁺[CoO₂]⁻ [1]; cobalt changes from +4 to +3 [1]

**5.** 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O [1]

**6. (a)** Zn + 2OH⁻ → ZnO + H₂O + 2e⁻ [1]

**(b)** 2MnO₂ + H₂O + 2e⁻ → Mn₂O₃ + 2OH⁻ [1]

**(c)** Zn + 2MnO₂ → ZnO + Mn₂O₃ [1]

**(d)** 0.15 − (−1.26) = **+1.41 V** [1]

**(e)** **+4 → +3** [1]

**7. (a)** Electrode that was negative: PbSO₄ + H⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb + HSO₄⁻ [1]; electrode that was positive: PbSO₄ + 2H₂O → PbO₂ + 3H⁺ + HSO₄⁻ + 2e⁻ [1]

**(b)** The product, PbSO₄, is an insoluble solid that stays on the electrodes, so it can be converted back into Pb and PbO₂ [1]

**(c)** Sulfuric acid (H⁺ and HSO₄⁻) is used up and water is formed in the overall reaction [1]

**8. (a)** Fe + 2NiO(OH) + 2H₂O [1] → Fe(OH)₂ + 2Ni(OH)₂ [1]

**(b)** 0.52 − (−0.89) = **+1.41 V** [1]

**(c)** Fe(OH)₂ + 2e⁻ → Fe + 2OH⁻ [1]

**9. (a)** CH₃OH + H₂O → CO₂ + 6H⁺ + 6e⁻ [1]

**(b)** Methanol equation ×2 and oxygen equation ×3 so that 12e⁻ cancel [1]; 2CH₃OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 4H₂O [1]

**(c)** 1.23 − 0.02 = **+1.21 V** [1]

**(d)** It releases carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas, whereas the hydrogen cell produces only water [1]

**10. (a)** 0.56 − (−3.04) = **+3.60 V** [1]

**(b)** Lithium has a very negative electrode potential, giving a large EMF [1]; lithium has a low mass (low density), so the cell is light for the energy it supplies [1]

**11. (a)** Negative: H₂ + 2OH⁻ → 2H₂O + 2e⁻ [1]; positive: O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻ [1]

**(b)** The OH⁻ ions used at the negative electrode are replaced by those formed at the positive electrode, so there is no net change [1]

**12.** Benefit: the only product at the point of use is water, so no carbon dioxide or other exhaust pollutants are released from the car [1]; risk: hydrogen is often produced using energy from fossil fuels, so carbon dioxide may still be released overall [1]; risk: hydrogen is flammable and explosive in air and must be stored compressed or liquefied, which is difficult and costly [1]; conclusion that weighs these, for example that the benefit depends on making hydrogen from renewable energy and on safe storage [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" needs points on both sides and a reasoned judgement; a list of advantages alone caps the mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the positive-electrode reaction backwards during discharge.
- Reversing only one half-equation for recharging.
- Leaving H₂O or OH⁻ uncancelled in overall equations.
- Forgetting to match electrons (12e⁻ in the methanol cell).
- Vague environmental points with no named substance.
