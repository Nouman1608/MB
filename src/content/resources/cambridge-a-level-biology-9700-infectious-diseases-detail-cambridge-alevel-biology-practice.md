These questions cover sub-topic **10.1 Infectious diseases** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027 (AS Level, Paper 1 and Paper 2 style). The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. All data are invented for practice. Calculators are allowed.

Revise first with the [infectious diseases study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-infectious-diseases-detail-cambridge-alevel-biology/) and the [infectious diseases revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-infectious-diseases-detail-cambridge-alevel-biology-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Which disease is caused by a protoctist?

A: cholera

B: HIV/AIDS

C: malaria

D: tuberculosis **[1]**

**2.** Which disease is transmitted by a vector?

A: cholera

B: HIV/AIDS

C: malaria

D: tuberculosis **[1]**

**3.** State what is meant by an infectious disease. **[2]**

**4.** Name the **two** species of bacteria that cause tuberculosis, and state how each one is most commonly transmitted to humans. **[4]**

**5.** Explain why an antibiotic would not be used to treat HIV/AIDS. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Explain how cholera is transmitted, and how sewage treatment and chlorination of drinking water reduce its spread. **[5]**

**7.** Explain how malaria is transmitted from one person to another. Do not describe the life cycle of the parasite. **[4]**

**8.** Discuss the biological, social and economic factors that need to be considered in controlling the spread of HIV. **[6]**

**9.** A town introduced a programme of supervised drug treatment for people with active TB. The table shows data for the town.

| Year | Population | New active TB cases |
|---|---|---|
| Before the programme | 80 000 | 184 |
| Three years after | 92 000 | 115 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate of new cases per 100 000 people in each year.

**(b)** Calculate the percentage decrease in the rate.

**(c)** Explain why supervised treatment reduces the number of new cases.

**(d)** Suggest **one** reason, other than the programme, for the fall. **[7]**

**10.** Discuss why malaria remains difficult to control in many countries, using biological, social and economic factors. **[6]**

**11.** A person has latent TB. Several years later they are infected with HIV.

**(a)** Explain why a person with latent TB is not infectious.

**(b)** Explain why infection with HIV may lead to the person developing active TB. **[3]**

**12.** After a flood, many families move into a temporary camp with shared water supplies and few toilets. Suggest and explain why cholera may spread rapidly in the camp, and **two** measures that would reduce the spread. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**2.** **C** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** A disease caused by a pathogen [1]; that can be transmitted from one host to another [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**4.** *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* [1]: in the air, in droplets from an infected person's coughs or sneezes [1]. *Mycobacterium bovis* [1]: in unpasteurised milk or undercooked meat from infected cattle [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**5.** HIV is a virus with no cell wall, ribosomes or metabolism of its own [1]; antibiotics act on bacterial structures or processes, so there is nothing for them to act on [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**6.** *Vibrio cholerae* leaves an infected person in faeces [1]; sewage contaminates drinking water or food [1]; the next person swallows the bacteria [1]; sewage treatment stops faeces reaching water supplies [1]; chlorination kills bacteria in the drinking water [1]. Total: 5 marks.

**7.** A female *Anopheles* mosquito takes a blood meal from an infected person [1]; and takes up *Plasmodium* with the blood [1]; when it feeds on another person it passes *Plasmodium* into their blood in its saliva [1]; the mosquito is the vector (or: also by transfusion, shared needles or across the placenta) [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**8.** Any six, with at least one of each type for full marks: biological: no vaccine; HIV mutates rapidly, changing its antigens; it hides inside T-helper cells; ART reduces viral load but does not cure, so infected people remain infected for life. Social: stigma discourages testing, so people do not know they are infected; condom use depends on education and willingness; needle-exchange schemes reduce sharing among drug users; screening of donated blood. Economic: ART must be taken for life and is expensive; testing, education and screening programmes need sustained funding [6]. Total: 6 marks.

*Examiner insight:* six control methods with no factors scores poorly; each point needs the reason it helps or limits control.

**9. (a)** Before: 184 ÷ 80 000 × 100 000 = **230 per 100 000** [1]; after: 115 ÷ 92 000 × 100 000 = **125 per 100 000** [1].

**(b)** (230 − 125) ÷ 230 × 100 [1] = **45.7 %** [1].

**(c)** Patients complete the long course of drugs, so they stop being infectious sooner and fewer people are infected by droplets [1]; fewer patients stop early, so fewer drug-resistant strains are selected [1].

**(d)** Any one: better housing or ventilation; less crowding; more BCG vaccination; changes in testing or reporting; fewer people with HIV [1]. Total: 7 marks.

**10.** Any six, with at least one of each type for full marks: biological: mosquitoes are resistant to insecticides; *Plasmodium* is resistant to antimalarial drugs; the parasite lives inside liver cells and red blood cells, hidden from the immune system, so a vaccine is difficult; mosquitoes breed in any small area of standing water. Social: nets must be used every night and kept in repair; people live far from clinics, so diagnosis and treatment are late; war or migration moves people into malarial areas. Economic: nets, insecticides, drugs and diagnosis cost money; health budgets in affected countries may be limited [6]. Total: 6 marks.

**11. (a)** The bacteria are held in check by the immune system and are not being released in droplets from the lungs [1].

**(b)** HIV infects and destroys T-helper cells [1]; so the immune response is weakened and can no longer contain the TB bacteria, which multiply and cause active disease [1]. Total: 3 marks.

**12.** Few toilets mean faeces may contaminate the shared water supply [1]; crowding means many people drink the same contaminated water, so many are infected quickly [1]; carriers without symptoms may spread bacteria unnoticed [1]. Any two measures: provide clean, chlorinated or boiled drinking water; build latrines away from the water supply; hand-washing facilities with soap; treat patients with oral rehydration and isolate them from the water supply [2]. Total: 5 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming the mosquito as the cause of malaria.
- Misspelling or mis-capitalising pathogen names.
- Lists without factors when the command word is "discuss".
- Comparing case numbers rather than rates.
- Describing the malarial life cycle, which is not expected.
