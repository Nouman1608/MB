## Section A

**1.** Identify three economic problems facing Ford, 1974-77. **[3]**

**2.** Explain two reasons why Carter won the 1976 election. **[4]**

**3.** Identify three groups or issues that mobilised the New Right in the late 1970s. **[3]**

**4.** State three key features of supply-side economics as applied by Reagan. **[3]**

**5.** Explain two reasons why Bush lost the 1992 election. **[4]**

**6.** Identify three ways in which Carter applied human rights to Latin America. **[3]**

**7.** Give three reasons why the Iran hostage crisis damaged Carter. **[3]**

**8.** Identify three agreements or contacts that showed détente in practice, 1972-79. **[3]**

---

## Section B

**9.** "Carter's domestic presidency failed because of his poor relations with Congress." Assess this view. **[30]**

**10.** Evaluate the impact of Reagan's domestic policies on the American people. **[30]**

**11.** Assess the reasons for the failure of the Democratic Party in presidential elections in the 1980s. **[30]**

**12.** "Gorbachev, not Reagan, was responsible for the improvement in US-Soviet relations after 1985." Assess this view. **[30]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: high inflation after the 1973 oil price rise (1); recession and unemployment reaching about 9 per cent in 1975 (1); energy dependence on imported oil (1); budget deficits and fiscal pressure, including New York City's near-bankruptcy (1975) (1); stagflation that made standard remedies contradictory (1). **[3]**

**2.** Two reasons, 2 marks each: post-Watergate disillusion made an honest outsider attractive, and Carter promised never to lie to the American people (2); the Nixon pardon and the economic difficulties of 1974-75 damaged Ford and the Republicans (2); Carter, a Southern governor and born-again Christian, won back much of the South and evangelical voters for the Democrats (2); Ford's debate error about Soviet domination of Eastern Europe hurt him (2). **[4]**

**3.** Any three, 1 mark each: evangelical Christians, through groups such as the Moral Majority (1); opposition to abortion after *Roe v. Wade* (1); opposition to the Equal Rights Amendment, led by Phyllis Schlafly (1); opposition to gay rights (1); tax revolts such as Proposition 13 (1); hostility to busing and to the counter-culture (1). **[3]**

**4.** Any three, 1 mark each: large cuts in income tax rates, especially for high earners, to encourage work and investment (1); cuts in domestic spending (1); deregulation of business (1); tight money to bring down inflation, supported by the Federal Reserve (1); a claim that growth would increase tax revenue (1). **[3]**

**5.** Two reasons, 2 marks each: the 1990-91 recession and rising unemployment, which Clinton's campaign made the central issue ("the economy") (2); breaking his "no new taxes" pledge in the 1990 budget deal lost conservative support (2); Ross Perot's independent candidacy took about 19 per cent of the vote and focused attention on the deficit (2); Clinton's appeal as a young centrist, and doubts about Quayle (2). **[4]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: cutting military aid to the Argentine junta (1); reducing support for Pinochet's regime in Chile (1); criticism that led Brazil to reject US military aid (1); withdrawing support from the Somoza dictatorship in Nicaragua (1); public criticism of abuses by allied military governments, linking aid to their human rights record (1). **[3]**

**7.** Any three, 1 mark each: it lasted 444 days, dominating the news (1); the failed rescue mission of April 1980 killed eight servicemen and looked incompetent (1); Secretary of State Vance resigned (1); it made the USA appear weak and Carter indecisive in the election year (1); the hostages were released only after his defeat, minutes after Reagan took office (1). **[3]**

**8.** Any three, 1 mark each: SALT I and the ABM Treaty (1972) (1); the Helsinki Accords (1975) (1); grain sales to the USSR (1); the Apollo-Soyuz space mission (1975) (1); the Vladivostok summit (1974) (1); the signing of SALT II (1979) (1). **[3]**

**9.** Indicative content. **For:** Carter's outsider style, distrust of Congress and refusal to trade favours alienated Democratic leaders; energy legislation was diluted and delayed; welfare and tax reforms stalled; Edward Kennedy's challenge reflected party discontent. **Other factors:** stagflation and the second oil shock were partly beyond his control; Volcker's high interest rates caused recession in the election year; post-Watergate Congress was assertive with any president; the "malaise" speech and cabinet purge were self-inflicted damage; the hostage crisis dominated 1980. **Achievements:** Department of Energy, Energy Security Act, deregulation of airlines, civil service reform. **Judgement:** relations with Congress explain the limited legislation, but the economy explains why he was seen to fail. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**10.** Indicative content. **Positive:** inflation fell sharply; growth was strong from 1983; tax cuts raised take-home pay, especially for higher earners; deregulation lowered some prices, such as airfares; confidence revived, shown by the 1984 landslide. **Negative:** the 1981-82 recession and unemployment above 10 per cent; cuts to housing, food stamps and welfare hit the poor; inequality grew; union power declined after PATCO; deficits and debt rose; deregulation contributed to the savings-and-loan crisis; the slow response to AIDS. **Criterion:** distinguish groups -- rich and poor, union members, minorities. **Judgement:** the impact was positive for many middle- and upper-income Americans but harmful for the poorest, so the overall verdict depends on whose experience is measured. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**11.** Indicative content. **Internal divisions:** the Carter-Kennedy contest of 1980; conflict between liberal and moderate wings over taxes, defence and social issues. **Candidates and campaigns:** Mondale's 1984 pledge to raise taxes and his link to Carter's record; Dukakis's weak response to Republican attacks in 1988. **Structural change:** the white South's shift to the Republicans after civil rights; the New Right's mobilisation of religious voters; the end of the Keynesian consensus. **Republican strengths:** Reagan's personal appeal, economic recovery after 1983, Cold War confidence. **Response:** the Democratic Leadership Council (1985) as evidence that Democrats recognised the problem. **Judgement:** long-term realignment explains the scale of defeats; poor candidates and divisions explain the margins. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**12.** Indicative content. **For Gorbachev:** economic crisis and the burden of defence; "new thinking", glasnost and perestroika; willingness to accept asymmetric cuts in the INF Treaty; withdrawal from Afghanistan (1988-89); acceptance of change in Eastern Europe. **For Reagan:** the military build-up and SDI raised the cost of competition; the Reagan Doctrine increased pressure in Afghanistan; his personal shift from confrontation to negotiation from 1984; the summits of Geneva, Reykjavik and Washington depended on his readiness to trust Gorbachev, despite opposition from some advisers. **Judgement:** Gorbachev's choices were the necessary condition for change, while Reagan's flexibility made agreement possible; strong answers explain the interaction rather than treating them separately. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**
