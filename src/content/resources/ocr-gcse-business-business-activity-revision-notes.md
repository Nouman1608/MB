## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Business activity | Providing goods or services to satisfy customers' needs |
| Enterprise | Spotting an opportunity and developing an idea for a business |
| Entrepreneur | Person who takes the risk of starting and running a business |
| Business plan | Written document setting out the idea, market, resources and finance |
| Liability | Responsibility for business debts |
| Aim and objective | Long-term goal and the target that helps reach it |
| Stakeholder | Someone with a stake in a business, affected by it or able to influence it |
| Organic growth | Growth from within, using the business's own resources |
| External growth | Growth by joining with another business |

## 1.1 Enterprise and entrepreneurship

- Purpose: **spot an opportunity**, **develop an idea**, **satisfy customer needs**.
- Characteristics to know: **creativity**, **risk taking**, **determination**, **confidence**.
- **Risk** is what may be lost: money, time, income given up. **Reward** is what may be gained: profit, independence.

Method in steps for a 1.1 case question:
1. Name the characteristic.
2. Quote the evidence from the case.
3. Say why it helps this business to start or survive.

Mini example: Arlo Pennick (invented) leaves a job to sell repaired bicycles at weekend markets. His risk is lost pay and the stock he buys; his reward is profit and being his own boss.

## 1.2 Business planning

- Purposes of planning: **reduce risk**, **help the business succeed**.
- Business plan helps with: **identifying markets**, **obtaining finance**, **identifying resources needed**, **achieving aims and objectives**.
- Limits: forecasts may be wrong; it takes time; it must be updated.

Must-know distinction: a plan **supports** success, it does not guarantee it. "Useful because it helps get a loan" is a start; add why a lender wants to see it.

## 1.3 Business ownership

| Type | Owners | Liability | Key advantage | Key drawback |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sole trader | 1 | Unlimited | Full control and profit | All risk; few sources of finance |
| Partnership | 2 or more | Unlimited | Shared skills and money | Profit shared; disagreements |
| Private limited (Ltd) | Shareholders | Limited | Owners protected | More rules to follow |
| Public limited (plc) | Shareholders | Limited | Shares sold to the public | Ownership spread; more reporting |

Note: the specification names the types and the concept of limited liability; the advantages and drawbacks in the table are general business knowledge, not specification text.

- **Unlimited liability**: personal assets can be used to pay business debts.
- **Limited liability**: a shareholder loses no more than they invested.
- Calculation pattern: shortfall = debts - assets. Debts £25 000, assets £18 000 gives a shortfall of £7 000, or £3 500 each for two equal partners.
- **Suitability**: start-up with small capital often sole trader or partnership; established business needing large funds may become a plc.

Common slip: limited liability does not remove the debt. It limits what the owner personally loses.

## 1.4 Aims and objectives

- The five to know: **profit**, **survival**, **growth**, **providing a service**, **market share**.
- Standard form: market share (%) = firm's sales ÷ total market sales × 100. For example £63 000 of £450 000 is 14%.
- **Objectives change** as a business evolves: survival, then profit, then growth.
- **Objectives differ** between businesses because of size, ownership, motives and the market.

| Business type | Likely objective |
|---|---|
| Brand-new start-up | Survival |
| Plc with shareholders | Profit, growth |
| Charity or community group | Providing a service |
| Challenger in a crowded market | Market share |

## 1.5 Stakeholders

| Group | Internal or external | Wants |
|---|---|---|
| Owners | Internal | Profit, growth |
| Employees | Internal | Pay, security, conditions |
| Customers | External | Quality, value, service |
| Suppliers | External | Orders, prompt payment |
| Government | External | Tax paid, rules followed |
| Local community | External | Jobs, little disturbance |

- **Business affects stakeholders**: a new site gives jobs but adds traffic.
- **Stakeholders affect business**: customers stop buying, employees leave, suppliers change terms.
- Conflicts are common: wages versus profit; growth versus community peace.

Method in steps for a stakeholder question:
1. Choose the stakeholder named.
2. State the interest.
3. Show how the decision helps or harms it.
4. Say which stakeholder matters most here and why.

## 1.6 Business growth

| Type | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Organic: increasing output | Produce more with own resources | Run a second shift |
| Organic: new customers | Sell to more people or places | Start online selling |
| Organic: new products | Add to the range | Launch a new flavour |
| Organic: market share | Win a bigger part of the market | Win custom from rivals |
| Merger | Two firms agree to combine | Two local builders join |
| Takeover | One firm buys another | A chain buys a competitor |
| Horizontal | Same stage of production | Brewer buys another brewer |
| Vertical backward | Buy a supplier | Brewer buys a hop farm |
| Vertical forward | Buy a customer or retailer | Brewer buys a pub |
| Diversification | New market or product | Brewer buys a hotel |

Percentage growth = (new - old) ÷ old × 100. Output rising from 400 to 500 units is 25% growth.

The specification lists merger and takeover; the plain-English meanings in the table are general knowledge, not specification wording.

**Compare:** organic is slower, cheaper and easier to control; external is faster but costs more, and the businesses may not fit together.

## Command words and case-study skills

The specification lists the command words used in the longer questions. Link each to what you must write.

| Command word | What the specification says it needs | How to set it out |
|---|---|---|
| Identify, State | Knowledge | One short answer, no development |
| Explain | Knowledge and understanding, applied | Point, reason, link to the business |
| Calculate | Quantitative skill | Formula, substitution, answer with units |
| Analyse | A logical chain of reasoning | Because... which leads to... so... |
| Discuss, Evaluate | Weigh both sides and reach a supported judgement | For, against, judgement with a reason |
| Recommend | A supported judgement | Choose one option and say why |

Case-study habits that help in section 1:

1. Underline the business type, its size and its stage (start-up or established).
2. Pull out any numbers in the stimulus and use them in the answer.
3. Use the right term: liability, objective, stakeholder, horizontal, takeover.
4. Finish evaluations with a clear decision, tied to the stage and size of the business.

## Evaluation frames

Ownership choice: say what the owners need (control, protection, finance), score each type against it, then recommend.

Growth choice: compare speed, cost, risk and control, then link the answer to the objective, such as market share or survival.

Business plan: give a benefit, a limit, and a judgement on how useful it is at this stage.

Stakeholder conflict: name the two groups, show what each gains or loses, and decide which should take priority in this case.

## Quick self-test

1. Name two characteristics of an entrepreneur.
2. State two purposes of business planning.
3. A partnership owes £40 000 and has assets of £31 000. What is the shortfall?
4. Explain why limited liability helps a business raise money from investors.
5. Which objective is most likely for a business in its first months?
6. A firm has sales of £9 000 in a market of £60 000. Calculate its market share.
7. Name two internal stakeholders and two external stakeholders.
8. A business buys its main supplier. Identify the type of growth.
9. Sales rise from 2 500 to 3 000 units. Calculate the percentage growth.
10. Evaluate in one sentence whether a plc is always better than a Ltd.

### Answers

1. Any two of creativity, risk taking, determination, confidence.
2. Reduce risk; help the business succeed (also acceptable: identify the resources needed).
3. 40 000 - 31 000 = **£9 000**.
4. Investors can lose only what they put in, so they are more willing to buy shares and the business can raise larger sums.
5. **Survival**.
6. 9 000 ÷ 60 000 × 100 = **15%**.
7. Internal: owners, employees. External: customers, suppliers, government, local community (any two).
8. External growth, **vertical (backward)**.
9. (3 000 - 2 500) ÷ 2 500 × 100 = **20%**.
10. No: a plc can raise more money, but it has more reporting and spreads ownership, so a small family business may prefer a Ltd.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a characteristic or stakeholder without linking it to the business in the case.
- Treating limited liability as freedom from debt.
- Calling a plc government-owned.
- Writing only "profit" when asked why objectives differ.
- Stating a type of growth without showing how it fits the example.
- Confusing merger with takeover, or horizontal with vertical.
- Giving a percentage without showing the working.
- Evaluating with one side only and no judgement.
