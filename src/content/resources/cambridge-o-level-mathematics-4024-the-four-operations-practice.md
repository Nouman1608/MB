## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading -6 - (-9) as -15 in Question 1(b).
- Adding before multiplying in Question 2.
- Multiplying whole numbers and fractions separately in Question 4.
- Turning the first fraction upside down in Question 5.
- Misplacing the decimal point in Question 6.
- Applying the quarter to 120 litres instead of 75 litres in Question 11.
- Not using brackets on the calculator in Question 12, so that only 3.85 is divided by 9.1.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is 12 ÷ 3 × 2 equal to 8 or 2?**
It is 8. Multiplication and division have equal rank, so work from left to right: 12 ÷ 3 = 4, then 4 × 2 = 8.

**Do I need a common denominator to multiply fractions?**
No. A common denominator is only for adding and subtracting. For multiplying, multiply the tops and the bottoms.

**What does the fraction bar do in (7 + 5)/(10 - 4)?**
It acts like brackets round the top and the bottom. Work out each part first, then divide.
