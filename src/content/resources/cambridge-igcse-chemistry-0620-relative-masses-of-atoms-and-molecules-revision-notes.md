Condensed recall for **3.2 Relative masses of atoms and molecules**, Cambridge IGCSE
Chemistry 0620. For explanations and worked examples, use the
[Relative Masses of Atoms and Molecules study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-relative-masses-of-atoms-and-molecules/).

> **Tier note:** all of 3.2 is **Core**. Reacting-mass calculations here do **not** use
> the mole.

## Definitions to learn

- **Relative atomic mass, Ar:** the average mass of the isotopes of an element compared to
  1/12th of the mass of an atom of carbon-12.
- **Relative molecular mass, Mr:** the sum of the relative atomic masses (of all atoms in
  the formula).
- **Relative formula mass, Mr:** the same sum, used for ionic compounds.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Isotopes | Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons |
| Carbon-12 | The standard: its atom is given a mass of exactly 12 |
| Average (in Ar) | Takes account of how common each isotope is |
| Coefficient | Number in front of a formula in an equation |
| Conservation of mass | Total mass of reactants = total mass of products |
| Simple proportion | Masses that scale up or down by the same factor |

## Ar values worth knowing (from the Periodic Table)

H 1 · C 12 · N 14 · O 16 · Na 23 · Mg 24 · Al 27 · S 32 · Cl 35.5 · K 39 · Ca 40 ·
Fe 56 · Cu 64 · Zn 65

Ar and Mr have **no units**.

## Reacting masses by proportion: four steps

1. Balanced equation.
2. **Coefficient × Mr** under the two substances that matter.
3. **Scale factor** = mass given ÷ mass in the equation.
4. Unknown mass = equation mass × scale factor. Same units throughout.

**Mini-example:** 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. 2 × 2 = 4 g hydrogen gives 2 × 18 = 36 g water. So
1 g hydrogen gives 36 ÷ 4 = **9 g** water.

**Reverse direction:** to *make* 14 g of CaO from CaCO₃ (100 → 56), scale factor =
14 ÷ 56 = 0.25, so 100 × 0.25 = **25 g** of CaCO₃ is needed.

**From data alone:** if 2.4 g of magnesium forms 4.0 g of oxide, then 0.6 g (a quarter)
forms 1.0 g, and the oxygen used is 4.0 − 2.4 = 1.6 g.

## "Show that" questions

When the answer is given, every step must appear. For "Show that the Mr of Na₂CO₃ is
106": 2 × 23 = 46; 12; 3 × 16 = 48; 46 + 12 + 48 = 106. Writing only "= 106" earns nothing,
because the value was already in the question.

## Exam traps

- **Atomic number is not Ar.** Oxygen's atomic number is 8; its Ar is 16.
- **Brackets multiply everything inside**: (OH)₂ means two O and two H.
- **Coefficients count in reacting masses** but not in an Mr calculation for one formula.
- **Chlorine is 35.5**, so many chlorides have half-number Mr values (NaCl = 58.5).
- **Diatomic gases**: Mr of Cl₂ is 71, of O₂ is 32, of H₂ is 2.
- **Hydrated salts** include the water in the formula: CuSO₄·5H₂O = 160 + 5 × 18 = 250.
- **Units**: if the question is in tonnes, the answer is in tonnes — no conversion needed.
- **"Average"** must appear when describing Ar; without it the description fits a single
  isotope.

## Self-test

1. Describe relative atomic mass.
2. Why is the Ar of chlorine not a whole number?
3. Calculate the Mr of nitric acid, HNO₃.
4. Calculate the Mr of calcium nitrate, Ca(NO₃)₂.
5. Calculate the Mr of aluminium nitrate, Al(NO₃)₃.
6. Why is "relative formula mass" used for sodium chloride rather than "relative molecular
   mass"?
7. What is the Mr of oxygen gas?
8. 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl. What mass of sodium chloride forms from 4.6 g of sodium?
9. CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂. What mass of calcium oxide forms from 50 tonnes of calcium carbonate?
10. CuO + H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + H₂O. What mass of copper(II) sulfate forms from 4.0 g of copper(II)
    oxide?
11. 3.0 g of carbon burns to form 11.0 g of carbon dioxide. What mass of carbon dioxide forms
    from 9.0 g of carbon?
12. In question 11, what mass of oxygen reacted with 3.0 g of carbon?

**Answers**

1. The average mass of the isotopes of an element compared to 1/12th of the mass of an atom
   of carbon-12.
2. It is an average of isotopes of different masses (chlorine-35 and chlorine-37).
3. 1 + 14 + 48 = 63.
4. 40 + 2 × 62 = 164.
5. 27 + 3 × 62 = 213.
6. Sodium chloride is ionic: it has no molecules.
7. 32.
8. 46 g Na → 117 g NaCl; 4.6 ÷ 46 = 0.1; 117 × 0.1 = 11.7 g.
9. 100 → 56; 50 ÷ 100 = 0.5; 56 × 0.5 = 28 tonnes.
10. 80 → 160; 4.0 ÷ 80 = 0.05; 160 × 0.05 = 8.0 g.
11. 9.0 ÷ 3.0 = 3; 3 × 11.0 = 33.0 g.
12. 11.0 − 3.0 = 8.0 g (conservation of mass).
