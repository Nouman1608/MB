This guide covers sub-topic **12.2 Aerobic respiration** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 and 2, the definition and the word equation, are Core. Outcome 3, the balanced chemical equation, is Supplement content, so it is **Extended only**.

The [respiration study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-respiration/) introduces both equations and checks that the balanced equation balances. This page goes further: unpacking each word of the definition, tracing where every reactant comes from and every product goes, using the balanced equation for ratio calculations, and avoiding the confusions examiners see most. Afterwards, use the [aerobic respiration revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-aerobic-respiration-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/) and the [aerobic respiration practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-aerobic-respiration-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/).

## What sub-topic 12.2 asks you to do

| Outcome | Syllabus wording | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| 12.2.1 | Describe aerobic respiration as the chemical reactions in cells that use oxygen to break down nutrient molecules to release energy | Core |
| 12.2.2 | State the word equation for aerobic respiration as: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water | Core |
| 12.2.3 | State the balanced chemical equation for aerobic respiration as: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O | Extended only |

## 12.2.1 The definition, phrase by phrase

The definition is short, but each phrase carries a mark-worthy idea.

| Phrase | What it tells you | Common error it rules out |
|---|---|---|
| "the chemical reactions" | respiration is a series of reactions, each controlled by enzymes | treating respiration as a single step or as a physical process |
| "in cells" | it happens inside every living cell | saying respiration happens "in the lungs" |
| "that use oxygen" | this is what makes it **aerobic** | confusing it with anaerobic respiration |
| "to break down nutrient molecules" | glucose is the usual nutrient molecule; others can be used | saying it builds molecules up |
| "to release energy" | energy already stored in the molecule is released | saying it "makes" or "produces" energy |

When a question says "describe aerobic respiration", write the whole definition. Leaving out "in cells" or "use oxygen" usually costs a mark.

### Respiration is not breathing

**Breathing** is the movement of air into and out of the lungs. **Gas exchange** is the diffusion of oxygen into the blood and carbon dioxide out of it. **Aerobic respiration** is the chemistry in cells that uses that oxygen. The three are linked but different. The [gas exchange in humans study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-gas-exchange-in-humans/) covers the first two.

## 12.2.2 The word equation

```
glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water
```

Learn it exactly. Energy is **released** by the reaction, but it is not a substance, so it is not written as a product in the syllabus equation. If you do add "(energy released)" after the arrow, never write "energy" as if it were a chemical.

### Where the reactants come from and the products go (in a human)

| Substance | Source or destination |
|---|---|
| Glucose | from digested carbohydrates, absorbed in the small intestine and carried in the blood plasma to cells |
| Oxygen | from the air, absorbed in the lungs and carried by haemoglobin in red blood cells to cells |
| Carbon dioxide | diffuses out of cells into the blood plasma, carried to the lungs and breathed out |
| Water | added to the body's water; any excess is removed, for example in urine |

### Worked example 1: linking respiration to other topics

**Question:** Explain why a person breathes faster and deeper during a long run. [3]

**Model answer:** Muscle cells carry out more aerobic respiration to release more energy for muscle contraction (1). They need more oxygen, so faster, deeper breathing takes in more oxygen for gas exchange (1). Respiration also produces more carbon dioxide, which must be removed from the body through the lungs (1).

The answer uses both sides of the equation: more oxygen in, more carbon dioxide out.

## 12.2.3 The balanced chemical equation (Extended only)

```
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O
```

| Formula | Name | Number in the equation |
|---|---|---|
| C₆H₁₂O₆ | glucose | 1 |
| O₂ | oxygen | 6 |
| CO₂ | carbon dioxide | 6 |
| H₂O | water | 6 |

Write the subscripts carefully. "6O₂" means six molecules of oxygen, each made of two atoms. Writing "O6" or "6O" is wrong.

### Using the ratio

The equation tells you the **ratio of molecules**: 1 glucose : 6 oxygen : 6 carbon dioxide : 6 water.

**Worked example 2: from glucose to products**

*How many molecules of carbon dioxide are released when 4 molecules of glucose are respired aerobically?*

```
1 glucose → 6 CO₂
4 glucose → 4 × 6 = 24 CO₂
```

**Answer: 24 molecules.**

**Worked example 3: working backwards**

*A cell uses 42 molecules of oxygen in aerobic respiration. How many glucose molecules are broken down, and how many water molecules are produced?*

```
6 O₂ are used per glucose
glucose = 42 ÷ 6 = 7 molecules
water = 7 × 6 = 42 molecules
```

**Answer: 7 glucose molecules; 42 water molecules.**

**Worked example 4: checking a student's equation**

A student writes: C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O. What is wrong?

- The formulae are correct, but the equation is **not balanced**.
- Left: 6 C, 12 H, 8 O. Right: 1 C, 2 H, 3 O.
- Correct form: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O, where both sides have 6 C, 12 H and 18 O.

## Comparing with photosynthesis

Photosynthesis (topic 6) uses carbon dioxide and water and produces glucose and oxygen. Its balanced equation has the same substances with the arrow running the other way:

| | Aerobic respiration | Photosynthesis |
|---|---|---|
| Reactants | glucose and oxygen | carbon dioxide and water |
| Products | carbon dioxide and water | glucose and oxygen |
| Where | all living cells, all the time | cells with chloroplasts, in light |
| Energy | released from glucose | light energy transferred to chemical energy |

Plants respire **all the time**, day and night, not only when photosynthesis stops. This is one of the most common errors in questions that combine the two topics.

## Aerobic compared with anaerobic

Aerobic respiration releases **much more energy per glucose molecule** than anaerobic respiration, because the glucose is broken down completely to carbon dioxide and water. This comparison is examined in [anaerobic respiration](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-anaerobic-respiration-cambridge-igcse-biology/).

## Common mistakes

- Saying respiration "produces" or "creates" energy.
- Writing that respiration happens in the lungs, or calling breathing "respiration".
- Swapping reactants and products, or writing the photosynthesis equation instead.
- Writing "energy" as a chemical product in the word equation.
- (Extended) Writing O instead of O₂, CO instead of CO₂, or forgetting the 6s.
- (Extended) Multiplying glucose by 6 when the question asks about glucose from oxygen; divide instead.
- Saying plants respire only at night.

## Exam technique for 12.2 command words

- **Describe** aerobic respiration: the full definition, including "in cells" and "use oxygen".
- **State** the word equation: exactly as the syllabus gives it.
- **State** the balanced equation (Extended): correct formulae, numbers and subscripts.
- **Calculate**: write the ratio first, then multiply or divide, and give a unit or "molecules".
- **Explain** (applied questions): link changes in oxygen use or carbon dioxide release to the equation.
