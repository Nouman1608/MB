## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for developing it, up to two marks for each of two points (question 5: two marks for the role and two for its importance). The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** They record the words and deeds of every person [1].

**2.** Light [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: they always obey Allah; they are made from light; they worship without tiring; some have wings; they do not eat or marry; they can appear in human form; they are unseen.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: What is faith (iman)? What is Islam? What is ihsan (excellence)? When will the Hour be?

**5.** Malak-al-Mowth is the angel of death, who takes each soul at the time fixed by Allah [1] (32:11) [1]. It is important because it teaches that death comes only by Allah's command and cannot be hastened or delayed [1], so Muslims prepare for the afterlife and accept death with patience [1].

**6.** For each of two teachings: teaching identified [1] and developed [1].

- **Three levels of religion** [1]: belief (iman), practice (Islam) and excellence (ihsan) are all part of religion [1].
- **Ihsan** [1]: worship Allah as if you see Him, and know that He sees you, which teaches sincerity [1].
- **The Hour is hidden** [1]: only Allah knows when it will come, so humans should prepare and not speculate [1].
- **Angels can appear as men** [1]: Jibril (AS) came to teach the people their religion, which shows the role of angels in guidance [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two roles, the role identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Roles (AO1):

- **Jibril brings revelation** [1]: he brought the Qur'an to the Prophet (pbuh), so the message is Allah's word [1].
- **Kiraman Katibeen record deeds** [1]: every action is written, which is the basis of the Day of Judgement [1].
- **Isra'fil blows the trumpet** [1]: his task marks the end of this world and the start of resurrection [1].
- **Mika'il is linked to rain and provision** [1]: Muslims see sustenance as coming from Allah [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "It is he who has brought it down upon your heart by permission of Allah" (Qur'an 2:97) [2].
- "[Appointed] over you are keepers, noble and recording; they know whatever you do" (82:10-12) [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Ways (AO1):

- **Accountability** [1]: a Muslim knows that every deed is recorded, so tries to avoid wrongdoing even in private [1].
- **Trust in the Qur'an** [1]: the Qur'an was delivered by Jibril, so the Muslim treats it as reliable guidance [1].
- **Comfort and hope** [1]: angels assist believers, so a Muslim in difficulty feels supported [1].
- **Obedience as a model** [1]: angels obey Allah perfectly, and Muslims try to follow Allah's commands willingly [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Who made the angels messengers having wings, two or three or four" (Qur'an 35:1) [2].
- "I will reinforce you with a thousand angels" (8:9) [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- Belief in angels is one of the six key beliefs; to deny angels is to deny part of the faith.
- Angels deliver revelation, so belief in angels supports belief in the Qur'an and the prophets.
- Angels record deeds, which links to accountability.
- **Against the statement:** belief in Allah is the foundation; angels are created beings, not worshipped, so they cannot be "as important" as the Creator. Tawhid is the first belief, and the other five depend on it.
- **Judgement:** both are essential, but they are not of equal rank; belief in angels is important because it flows from belief in Allah and His message.

*Use of the passage*

- "Creator of the heavens and the earth" shows that angels are created, so Allah is greater.
- "made the angels messengers" shows that their importance comes from the task Allah gives.
- "having wings, two or three or four" describes their nature.
- "He increases in creation what He wills" and "over all things competent" show Allah's power over all creatures.
