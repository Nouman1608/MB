Condensed recall for **6.5 Limitations of accounting statements**. The syllabus names three factors: historic cost; application of accounting policies; non-financial aspects, for example skill of the workforce, location of the business, economic climate. Figures below are invented; amounts in dollars.

## One-line summary

Statements can be accurate and still incomplete: they show **past** transactions at **original cost**, using **chosen methods**, and only what can be **measured in money**.

## 1. Historic cost

- Assets are recorded at what they cost, which can be checked, but may be far from today's value.
- Net book value is cost less depreciation; it is **not** a selling price or a replacement cost.
- Depreciation is a share of the original cost, so it may not reflect the cost of replacing the asset.
- Figures from different years are in money of different purchasing power, so rising revenue may partly reflect higher prices.
- Old, heavily depreciated assets make capital employed small, which can make ROCE look high.

**Quick figures.** A van cost $30,000 three years ago; straight line at 20% of cost gives net book value 30,000 − 3 × 6,000 = **$12,000**. A similar used van sells for $17,000 and a new one costs $38,000. The statement shows $12,000.

## 2. Application of accounting policies

Choices and estimates that change the figures:

- depreciation method, rate, useful life and residual value
- valuation of inventory at the lower of cost and net realisable value (an estimate)
- the allowance for irrecoverable debts (a percentage chosen by the business)
- the materiality limit for capitalising small items
- treating spending as capital or revenue

**Quick figures.** Equipment costs $20,000. Straight line at 10% gives $2,000 depreciation in year 1; reducing balance at 25% gives $5,000. Profit differs by **$3,000** with no difference in trading.

Consistency keeps one business comparable over time but does not make two businesses with different policies comparable, and a change of policy breaks the comparison with earlier years.

## 3. Non-financial aspects

| Aspect (syllabus examples first) | Effect not shown in the statements |
|---|---|
| Skill of the workforce | Quality, productivity and service; only wages appear |
| Location of the business | Passing trade, access, transport costs; only rent or cost of premises appears |
| Economic climate | Recession, interest rates, rising prices affect future sales and costs |
| Also accepted: reputation, customer loyalty, management quality, staff morale, competition, environmental impact | Affect future profit but cannot be reliably measured in money |

Link: the **money measurement** concept is the reason these are left out.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Historic cost | Recording assets at their original purchase price |
| Net book value | Cost less accumulated depreciation |
| Replacement cost | What it would cost now to buy an equivalent asset |
| Accounting policy | The method a business chooses to apply, such as a depreciation method |
| Estimate | A judgement of an uncertain amount, such as useful life or net realisable value |
| Non-financial aspect | A factor affecting performance that is not measured in money |
| Money measurement | Only items that can be reliably measured in money are recorded |

## Exam traps

- **"Historic cost is wrong."** It is an accepted concept; the limitation is that cost can differ from current value.
- **Unnamed policy.** Name one: depreciation method, inventory valuation, allowance for irrecoverable debts.
- **Listing without effect.** Say how each limitation could mislead the user in the question.
- **Limitation is not error.** A limitation exists even when the statements are correct.
- **Economic climate is external.** It is not something the business records, and it affects the future.
- **One-sided evaluation.** Say what the statements *do* show before their limits.

## Self-test

1. Name the three factors the syllabus gives as causes of the limitations of accounting statements.
2. Why is historic cost used even though it may not show current value?
3. A machine cost $25,000 two years ago and is depreciated at 20% reducing balance. What is its net book value, and why may it differ from its value today?
4. Revenue rose from $200,000 to $210,000 while selling prices rose by 5%. What does this suggest about the volume of sales?
5. Give **three** accounting policies or estimates that affect reported profit.
6. Two identical businesses use different depreciation methods. Why does this limit comparison of their profit margins?
7. How does the consistency concept help, and what does it not solve?
8. Give the three non-financial aspects the syllabus names.
9. Explain why the skill of the workforce is not shown as an asset.
10. Give one way the economic climate could make last year's statements a poor guide to next year.
11. Which accounting concept explains why non-financial aspects are left out?
12. Why can old assets make ROCE look high?
