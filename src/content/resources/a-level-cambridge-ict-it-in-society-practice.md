## Questions

**1.** A city's buses accept a transport card. Leila loads her card with 30.00 and makes eight journeys at 2.40 each.

**(a)** Describe what is meant by a stored value card. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the value left on Leila's card. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate how many further complete journeys she can make without topping up. **[1]**

**2.** Explain how a blockchain makes it difficult for someone to alter a cryptocurrency transaction after it has been recorded. **[4]**

**3.** A hospital is using data mining to predict which patients are likely to be readmitted. Identify the stage of the data mining process for each activity.

**(a)** Agreeing that the aim is to predict readmission within 30 days. **[1]**
**(b)** Removing records that have no discharge date. **[1]**
**(c)** Checking whether the model's predictions meet the accuracy the hospital agreed. **[1]**
**(d)** Adding the model to the patient records system so doctors see a risk score. **[1]**

**4.** A bank builds a data mining model to detect fraudulent card payments. A test sample contains 425 fraudulent payments. The model flags 400 payments, of which 340 are confirmed as fraudulent.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of flagged payments that were fraudulent. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of all fraudulent payments that the model detected. **[2]**
**(c)** The bank's objective was to detect at least 90% of fraudulent payments. State the stage of data mining this check belongs to, and suggest what the bank should do next. **[2]**

**5.** Data mining is used by many organisations.

**(a)** Explain how data mining can be used for national security. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how data mining can be used in scientific research. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe **two** ethical or privacy concerns about data mining. **[2]**

**6.** A small building company is choosing between instant messaging and email for communicating with its site workers. Compare the two services for this purpose. **[4]**

**7.** Discuss the impact of social networking platforms on governments. **[6]**

**8.** Explain **one** impact of IT on each of the following.

**(a)** Manufacturing **[2]**
**(b)** News **[2]**

**9.** A company plans to monitor its employees' emails and internet use. Evaluate this plan. **[6]**

**10.** A university plans to replace its live video-conferenced introductory course with a massive open online course (MOOC). Discuss the likely impact on students' motivation, achievement and autonomy. **[8]**

**11.** Discuss the impact and risks of cryptocurrencies for individuals and for businesses. **[8]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A card that holds a monetary value loaded in advance [1]; each payment reduces the stored value until it is topped up. [1]
**(b)** 30.00 − (8 × 2.40) = 30.00 − 19.20 = **10.80** [1]
**(c)** 10.80 ÷ 2.40 = 4.5, so **4** complete journeys [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c) a bare "4.5" does not answer "complete journeys"; round down and state the whole number.

**2.** The ledger is copied on many nodes, not held by one organisation. [1] Each block stores the hash of the previous block. [1] Altering a transaction changes that block's hash, so it no longer matches the hash stored in the next block. [1] The other nodes still hold the original version and must agree before changes are accepted, so the altered copy is rejected. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "It is encrypted so it cannot be hacked" earns nothing; each mark needs a separate feature of the mechanism (copies, hash link, broken link, consensus).

**3. (a)** Business understanding [1]
**(b)** Data preparation [1]
**(c)** Evaluation [1]
**(d)** Deployment [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the syllabus stage names exactly; "cleaning" for (b) describes the action but does not name the stage.

**4. (a)** 340 ÷ 400 × 100 [1] = **85%** [1]
**(b)** 340 ÷ 425 × 100 [1] = **80%** [1]
**(c)** Evaluation. [1] The 90% target is not met, so the bank should not deploy yet; it should go back to modelling or data preparation, for example by adding more examples of fraud, and retest. [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Check which total goes in the denominator: (a) divides by payments flagged, (b) by all fraudulent payments; swapping them loses the method mark.

**5. (a)** Large amounts of travel, financial or communication data are searched for patterns [1] that may reveal planned attacks or suspects, so action can be taken early. [1]
**(b)** Researchers search very large experimental data sets, such as genetic or climate data [1], to find relationships that would be too slow to find by hand, leading to new hypotheses. [1]
**(c)** Any two: data is used for purposes people did not consent to [1]; individuals can be profiled or treated unfairly based on patterns [1]; or large stores of personal data can be breached. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* For "Explain", say what the data mining finds and why that helps; naming the area alone gives little to credit.

**6.** Instant messaging is real time, so workers on site can get quick answers [1], whereas email may not be read until later. [1] Email gives a formal, searchable record with attachments such as plans or contracts [1], whereas instant messages are less suitable for formal records. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Compare" needs both services in each point; two separate descriptions with no "whereas" or "but" leave the comparison to the examiner and earn less.

**7.** Governments can distribute useful information and news directly to citizens quickly. [1] They can announce updated regulations and give advice on current issues. [1] Citizens can give feedback, so policy can respond to public opinion. [1] However, platforms can spread false or distorted information about government. [1] Some governments may use censorship of platforms to control debate. [1] Conclusion: platforms improve communication with citizens, but only if information is accurate and freedom of expression is protected. [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Discuss" asks for both sides; an answer with only benefits misses half the content, so include drawbacks and a reasoned conclusion.

**8. (a)** Robots and computer-controlled machines work continuously with consistent accuracy [1], increasing output and quality but reducing the number of unskilled jobs. [1]
**(b)** News can be published online and shared instantly [1], so people get updates as events happen, but unchecked stories can spread before being verified. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each part needs the technology and its effect; "IT makes manufacturing better" does not earn either mark.

**9.** Monitoring can detect data leaks or malware brought in through risky websites, protecting company data. [1] It can show whether staff are working, improving productivity. [1] It provides evidence if an employee misuses systems. [1] However, staff may feel distrusted, lowering morale. [1] It intrudes on privacy, especially for personal messages, and staff should be told about it. [1] Conclusion: justified for security if it is limited and transparent, but blanket monitoring may cost more in morale than it gains. [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" needs a judgement; finish with a justified conclusion rather than repeating points already made.

**10.** Motivation: students can study short videos and quizzes with instant feedback, which some find motivating [1], but with no live teacher or classmates some feel isolated and lose motivation. [1] Achievement: material can be replayed as often as needed, helping weaker students [1], but questions cannot be answered live, so misunderstandings may go uncorrected. [1] Autonomy: students choose when, where and how fast to study [1], but need self-discipline without fixed lesson times. [1] Teachers' motivation may fall if live teaching is replaced, or rise if content is reused. [1] Conclusion: a MOOC suits self-motivated learners; adding some live sessions would reduce the drawbacks. [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Cover all three named areas; an excellent answer on motivation alone cannot reach full marks.

**11.** Individuals: payments can be sent quickly and cheaply, including abroad [1], and a mobile wallet gives access without a bank account. [1] Risks: values can fall sharply, reducing savings [1]; losing a private key or password means losing the funds, and payments cannot be reversed. [1] Businesses: lower transaction fees and faster settlement than card payments [1], and access to new customers. [1] Risks: takings can lose value before conversion, and unclear regulation and tax rules add cost and uncertainty. [1] Conclusion: useful for fast, cheap transfers, but volatility and weak consumer protection make them risky as a main way to save or trade. [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep individuals and businesses separate; repeating the same point for both groups adds little, so give each group its own impacts and risks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a blockchain as encrypted or "unhackable" instead of explaining hashes, copies and consensus.
- Calling debit and credit cards "cryptocurrency" because they are digital.
- Mixing up data understanding (checking quality) and data preparation (fixing it).
- Swapping denominators in percentage questions about model accuracy.
- Writing one-sided answers to "Discuss" and "Evaluate" questions.
- Describing two services separately when the command word is "Compare".
- Ignoring one of the named groups or areas in an extended question.
- Generic points such as "IT is faster" with no technology or effect named.
