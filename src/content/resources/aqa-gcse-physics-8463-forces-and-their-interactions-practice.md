These questions cover section **4.5.1 Forces and their interactions** of the AQA GCSE Physics (8463)
specification, examined on Paper 2 at both tiers. Questions 11 to 14 test content marked (HT only) and are
labelled **Higher tier only**. A calculator, ruler and protractor are allowed throughout.

## Section A

**1.** State the difference between a scalar quantity and a vector quantity. **[1]**

**2.** Sort these forces into contact and non-contact forces: friction, magnetic force, tension,
electrostatic force. **[2]**

**3.** A 4.0 kg mass has a weight of 15 N on the surface of a planet. Calculate the gravitational field
strength on that planet. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why an astronaut's mass is the same on the Moon as on Earth, but her weight is smaller. **[2]**

**5.** State what is meant by the centre of mass of an object. **[1]**

**6.** In a tug-of-war, team A pulls with a force of 2400 N to the left and team B pulls with a force of
2150 N to the right. Calculate the resultant force on the rope. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A skydiver falls through the air. Her weight is 750 N and the air resistance on her is 520 N.

**(a)** Classify each of these two forces as contact or non-contact. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the resultant force on the skydiver. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe the force that forms an interaction pair with the skydiver's weight. **[2]**

**8.** A student hangs different masses from a calibrated newtonmeter.

| Mass (kg) | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.30 | 0.40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weight (N) | 0.98 | 1.96 | 2.94 | 3.92 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship between weight and mass shown by the data. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the data to determine the gravitational field strength. **[2]**
**(c)** Before taking readings, the student checks that the newtonmeter reads zero with nothing attached.
Explain why. **[1]**

**9.** Two charged balloons hang on threads near each other without touching. They repel. Balloon P pushes
balloon Q away with a force of 0.020 N.

**(a)** Name the type of force between the balloons and state whether it is contact or non-contact. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the force that balloon Q exerts on balloon P. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why these two forces do not cancel each other out. **[1]**

**10.** A car is driven along a straight, level road. The driving force is 3200 N. Air resistance is 1900 N
and other frictional forces total 600 N.

**(a)** Calculate the resultant force on the car and give its direction. **[2]**
**(b)** The car speeds up until air resistance is 2600 N. The other forces stay the same. Calculate the new
resultant force and state what this means about the forces. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) A box rests on a sloping ramp and does not slide.

**(a)** Name the three forces acting on the box, and give the direction of each. **[3]**
**(b)** State the resultant force on the box. **[1]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) A cable pulls on a post with a force of 250 N at 50° above the horizontal. A
student resolves the force using a scale drawing with a scale of 1 cm = 50 N.

**(a)** State the length of the line that represents the 250 N force. **[1]**
**(b)** Use a scale drawing to determine the horizontal and vertical components of the force. **[3]**

**13.** (Higher tier only) A boat's engine pushes it with a force of 900 N due north. A current pushes it
with a force of 400 N due east. Use a scale drawing to determine the size and direction of the resultant
force. **[4]**

**14.** (Higher tier only) A lamp of weight 30 N hangs at rest from two cables attached to a ceiling.
Explain how a vector diagram of the forces on the lamp shows that it is in equilibrium. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A scalar has magnitude only; a vector has magnitude and an associated direction [1]

**2.** Contact: friction and tension [1]; non-contact: magnetic force and electrostatic force [1]

**3.** g = W / m = 15 / 4.0 [1]; = 3.75, so **3.8 N/kg** [1]

**4.** Mass is the amount of matter in her body, which does not change with location [1]; weight depends
on the gravitational field strength, which is smaller on the Moon [1]

**5.** The single point at which the weight of the object may be considered to act [1]

**6.** 2400 − 2150 = 250 N [1]; **to the left** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A resultant without a direction earns only the first mark.

**7. (a)** Weight: non-contact [1]; air resistance: contact [1]
**(b)** 750 − 520 = 230 N [1]; downwards [1]
**(c)** The skydiver pulls the Earth upwards with a gravitational force [1]; of 750 N [1]
*Examiner insight:* Air resistance is not the pair of her weight: it is a different type of force acting
on the same object.

**8. (a)** Weight is directly proportional to mass [1]; doubling the mass (0.10 kg to 0.20 kg) doubles the
weight (0.98 N to 1.96 N) [1]
**(b)** g = W / m, for example 0.98 / 0.10 [1]; = **9.8 N/kg** [1]
**(c)** So that a zero error does not add to (or subtract from) every reading [1]

**9. (a)** Electrostatic force, which is non-contact [1]
**(b)** Q pushes P away (in the opposite direction) [1]; with a force of 0.020 N [1]
**(c)** They act on different objects, one on each balloon [1]

**10. (a)** 3200 − 1900 − 600 = 700 N [1]; forwards [1]
**(b)** 3200 − 2600 − 600 = **0 N** [1]; the forces on the car are balanced [1]

**11. (a)** Weight, vertically downwards [1]; normal contact force, at right angles to the ramp surface,
away from it [1]; friction, up the slope, along the surface [1]
**(b)** Zero [1]
*Examiner insight:* On a slope the normal contact force is perpendicular to the surface, not vertical, and
friction acts along the surface.

**12. (a)** 250 ÷ 50 = **5.0 cm** [1]
**(b)** A correct right-angled triangle drawn with the 5.0 cm line at 50° to the horizontal [1]; horizontal
side about 3.2 cm, so horizontal component about **160 N** [1]; vertical side about 3.8 cm, so vertical
component about **190 N** [1]

**13.** A suitable scale stated, for example 1 cm = 100 N, with 9.0 cm north and 4.0 cm east [1]; forces
drawn tip to tail and the resultant drawn from start to finish [1]; resultant line about 9.8 cm, so about
**980 N** [1]; direction about **24° east of north** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The direction must say which line the angle is measured from: "24° east of north" and
"66° north of east" describe the same direction.

**14.** The lamp is acted on by its weight and the tensions in the two cables [1]; when the three forces are
drawn to scale tip to tail, they form a closed triangle [1]; so the resultant force is zero and the lamp
is in equilibrium [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating a resultant force without its direction.
- Treating balanced forces on one object as an interaction pair.
- Giving weight in kilograms, or mass in newtons.
- Ignoring "2 significant figures" in a calculation.
- Drawing a scale diagram without stating the scale.
- Drawing the normal contact force vertically on a slope.
