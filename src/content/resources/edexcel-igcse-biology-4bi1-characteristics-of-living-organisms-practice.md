These questions cover sub-topic 1(a), **Characteristics of living organisms** (specification point 1.1), of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) specification, Issue 3. The qualification is untiered and point 1.1 can be examined on either paper. The set applies to the June and November series examined under Issue 3. A calculator may be used.

Learn the content first with the [characteristics of living organisms study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-biology-4bi1-characteristics-of-living-organisms/). The course hub is [Edexcel IGCSE Biology](/boards/edexcel/igcse/biology/).

## Questions

**1.** Name the characteristic of living organisms shown in each example.

**(a)** A fox cub becomes an adult fox. **[1]**
**(b)** A woodlouse moves away from a bright lamp. **[1]**
**(c)** A mouse passes urea in its urine. **[1]**
**(d)** A yeast cell buds to produce a new yeast cell. **[1]**

**2.** A student says: "Respiration and breathing are the same thing."

**(a)** Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**
**(b)** State **one** place in a plant where respiration takes place. **[1]**

**3.** Plants do not have a nervous system or legs.

**(a)** Describe **one** way in which a plant shows the characteristic of response. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a plant is described as showing movement even though it stays in one place. **[2]**

**4.** The table shows the height of a bean seedling.

| Day | 1 | 4 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Height in mm | 20 | 32 | 50 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in height between day 1 and day 7. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean growth rate in mm per day between day 1 and day 7. **[2]**

**5.** A student claims that a candle flame is alive. It uses wax as a fuel, releases energy and gives off carbon dioxide and water vapour.

**(a)** Suggest which **two** characteristics of living organisms the flame appears to show. **[2]**
**(b)** Give **two** characteristics that the flame does not show, and explain why the claim is wrong. **[3]**

**6.** Green plants make their own food.

**(a)** Name the process by which a green plant makes food. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the type of nutrition shown by a lion and give **one** difference between how a lion and a green plant obtain nutrients. **[2]**
**(c)** State the **two** uses of the nutrients that an organism takes in. **[2]**

**7.** The body temperature of a healthy person stays close to 37 °C on both a hot day and a cold day.

**(a)** Name the characteristic of living organisms that this shows. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why it is an advantage for an organism to keep its internal conditions steady. **[2]**

**8.** A mule is the offspring of a horse and a donkey. It cannot produce offspring.

Explain why a mule is still classified as a living organism, and why the characteristic of reproduction applies to the species and not to every individual. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** Growth (and development) [1]
**(b)** Response (to a stimulus) / movement [1]
**(c)** Excretion [1]
**(d)** Reproduction [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), the movement of the woodlouse is the response to light, and either answer is credited if the stimulus is clear.

**2.** **(a)** Respiration is a chemical process in cells that releases energy from nutrients such as glucose [1]; breathing is the movement of air into and out of the lungs (so is a different process) [1]
**(b)** Any one of: any living cell, root cells, leaf cells, mitochondria in a cell [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Respiration is breathing in oxygen" earns nothing. The mark is for stating that respiration releases energy in cells.

**3.** **(a)** A shoot grows towards light (accept a root grows downwards in response to gravity / water) [1]; the stimulus is light (or gravity or water) and the response is a change in direction of growth [1]
**(b)** Parts of the plant move, for example a shoot bends or a flower opens [1]; movement means a change in position of the organism or its parts, not only moving from place to place [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name both the stimulus and the response. "Plants respond to light" is too vague for two marks.

**4.** **(a)** (50 − 20) ÷ 20 × 100 [1] = **150%** [1]
**(b)** (50 − 20) ÷ (7 − 1) [1] = **5 mm per day** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), dividing by the final height (50) gives 60% and loses the answer mark. Divide by the original value.

**5.** **(a)** Any two of: nutrition (uses a fuel), respiration (releases energy from the fuel), excretion (gives off waste gases), movement (the flame moves/flickers) [2]
**(b)** Any two of: reproduction, growth and development (by itself), response, control of internal conditions [2]; a living organism must show all the characteristics, not only a few, and burning is combustion rather than respiration in cells [1]
*Examiner insight:* Reasoning marks go to a conclusion that refers to "all" the characteristics.

**6.** **(a)** Photosynthesis [1]
**(b)** Animal nutrition (the lion eats other organisms/takes in ready-made food) [1]; the plant makes its own food from carbon dioxide and water, using light energy [1]
**(c)** Source of energy [1]; materials for growth and repair [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), the contrast must be stated. Naming "carnivore" alone is not a difference in how the nutrients are obtained.

**7.** **(a)** Control of internal conditions (accept homeostasis) [1]
**(b)** Enzymes work best within a narrow range of conditions (accept temperature/pH) [1]; so keeping conditions steady means reactions in cells keep working at a suitable rate [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Enzymes work best" earns the first mark only. Link the steady condition to cell reactions for the second.

**8.** The mule shows the other seven characteristics, such as nutrition, respiration, excretion, response, movement, control of internal conditions and growth [1]; so it is alive [1]; the species (horses and donkeys) reproduce, so the species survives [1]; reproduction is needed for a species to continue, but an individual can still be living without reproducing [1]
*Examiner insight:* The key distinction is between an individual and a species. Answers that only say "mules are sterile" miss two marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing respiration with breathing.
- Dividing by the final value when calculating a percentage increase.
- Naming a characteristic without the evidence from the question.
- Saying that plants do not move or respond.
- Treating one or two characteristics as enough to call something alive.
