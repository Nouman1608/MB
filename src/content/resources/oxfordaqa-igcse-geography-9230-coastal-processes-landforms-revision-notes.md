These notes cover the first two key ideas of section 3.1.3.2, Coastal landscapes, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Geography (9230) specification, Version 4.3: "The coast is shaped by a number of physical processes" and "Distinctive coastal landforms are the result of rock type, structure and physical processes." For the whole unit, see the [Living with the Physical Environment study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-geography-living-with-the-physical-environment/); for full explanations, read the [coastal processes and landforms study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-coastal-processes-landforms/).

## Wave types

| | Constructive | Destructive |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | Low, gentle | High, steep |
| Frequency | Fewer per minute | More per minute |
| Swash vs backwash | Swash stronger | Backwash stronger |
| Effect | Build beaches | Erode beaches |

**Wave size** depends on wind strength, wind duration and **fetch** (distance of open water).

## The five process groups (specification wording)

| Group | Processes named | Key idea |
|---|---|---|
| Weathering | Mechanical, chemical | Breakdown in place |
| Mass movement | Sliding, slumping, rock falls | Downhill movement under gravity |
| Erosion | Hydraulic power, abrasion, attrition | Wearing away by the sea |
| Transportation | Longshore drift | Movement of sediment along the coast |
| Deposition | Why sediment is deposited | Sea loses energy |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fetch | Distance of open water over which wind blows |
| Swash / backwash | Water moving up / down the beach |
| Freeze-thaw | Water freezes in cracks, expands, breaks rock |
| Salt weathering | Salt crystals grow in cracks and force rock apart |
| Carbonation | Slightly acidic rain dissolves limestone and chalk |
| Slumping | Rotational slip of saturated material on a curved surface |
| Hydraulic power | Force of waves and compressed air in cracks |
| Abrasion | Sediment thrown at the cliff wears it away |
| Attrition | Sediment collides and becomes smaller and rounder |
| Longshore drift | Zigzag movement of sediment along the coast |
| Discordant coast | Rock bands at right angles to the coast |
| Concordant coast | Rock bands parallel to the coast |

## Longshore drift in four steps

Prevailing wind → waves approach at an angle → swash carries sediment up at that angle → backwash returns it straight down under gravity → zigzag along the coast.

## Where deposition happens

Sheltered bays; constructive waves; where the coast changes direction; large sediment supply (river mouths); behind groynes.

## Geology

- **Rock type:** resistant rock (e.g. granite, chalk) forms high cliffs and headlands; weak rock (e.g. clay, sands) forms bays and lower cliffs and slumps.
- **Structure:** discordant coast → headlands and bays; concordant coast → fewer bays, small coves where the resistant band is breached; joints and faults are weaknesses; the dip of layers affects cliff steepness.

## Landform sequences

**Wave-cut platform:** attack at cliff base → wave-cut notch → overhang → collapse → cliff retreats → platform left, exposed at low tide.

**Cave to stump:** weakness widened → cave → cave cuts through → arch → arch collapses → stack → eroded to stump.

**Spit:** longshore drift → coast changes direction → deposition in calmer water → ridge extends → recurved end (wind change) → salt marsh behind.

**Bar:** spit grows across a bay → joins other side → lagoon trapped.

**Sand dunes:** wide beach dries → wind blows sand inland → collects at obstacles (embryo dunes) → marram grass traps more sand → older, more vegetated dunes inland.

## Exam traps

- Weathering (in place) is not erosion (by the sea).
- Longshore drift is transport, not erosion.
- Attrition reduces sediment size; abrasion wears the cliff.
- The specification names "hydraulic power" for coasts; it does not list solution as a coastal erosion process.
- Sequences need every stage in order.
- Name regional examples where possible.

## Self-test

1. What three factors control wave size?
2. Give two differences between constructive and destructive waves.
3. Explain freeze-thaw weathering.
4. What is the difference between sliding and slumping?
5. Name and define the three coastal erosion processes in the specification.
6. Describe how longshore drift works.
7. Give three situations where deposition is likely.
8. How does a discordant coast form headlands and bays?
9. Describe the formation of a wave-cut platform.
10. What is the difference between a stack and a stump?
11. What is a recurved spit?
12. What landform is created when a spit joins the other side of a bay, and what lies behind it?

**Answers**

1. Wind strength, how long it blows and fetch.
2. Any two: constructive waves are low with a strong swash, destructive waves are high and steep with a strong backwash; constructive waves are less frequent; constructive waves build beaches, destructive waves erode them.
3. Water enters cracks, freezes and expands, widening the crack; repeated cycles break fragments off.
4. Sliding moves material along a flat surface; slumping is a rotational movement along a curved surface, often in saturated clay.
5. Hydraulic power: force of waves and compressed air in cracks. Abrasion: sediment thrown against the cliff wears it away. Attrition: sediment knocks together and becomes smaller and rounder.
6. Waves approach at an angle with the prevailing wind; swash carries sediment up the beach at that angle; backwash returns it straight down; the sediment zigzags along the coast.
7. Any three: sheltered bays; where constructive waves dominate; where the coast changes direction; near river mouths; behind groynes.
8. Alternating bands of hard and soft rock meet the sea at right angles; soft rock erodes back into bays, leaving hard rock as headlands.
9. Waves erode a notch at the cliff base; the overhang collapses; the cliff retreats, leaving a gently sloping rocky platform exposed at low tide.
10. A stack is a tall isolated pillar of rock; a stump is the low remains after the stack's base is eroded and it collapses.
11. A spit whose end curves inland because of changes in wind and wave direction.
12. A bar, with a lagoon behind it.
