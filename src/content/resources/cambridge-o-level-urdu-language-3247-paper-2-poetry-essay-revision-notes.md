These notes cover **Part 2 Poetry** of 3247 Paper 2 Texts: "Two essay questions based on the
prescribed set texts listed in the syllabus. Candidates answer one question." For the paper and full
set-text list see the [Paper 2 Texts study guide](/resources/o-level-cambridge-urdu-first-language-paper-2-texts/);
for the full method and a model paragraph see the
[Poetry essay study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-urdu-language-3247-paper-2-poetry-essay/).

## Core facts

- **15 marks**; **one** essay from a choice of **two**.
- Based on the **prescribed** poems only; set texts cannot be taken into the exam.
- 2027 list: **ten ghazals** (two each by Ahtish, Parveen Shakir, Momin, Iqbal, Faraz) and **three
  nazms** (Hali's *Musadas Hali*, seventeen selected stanzas; Zameer Jafree's *Admee*; Mahmood
  Sarhadi's *Qitaat*). Check the syllabus for your year: set texts can change.
- Marked on a **five-level** scheme shared with the Prose essay.

## The five levels in key phrases

| Level | Marks | Key phrase |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 13–15 | deeper implications; developed exploration of effects; careful, relevant reference; fully addresses the focus |
| 4 | 10–12 | some deeper implications; explores the way the writer uses language; some thoroughness of evidence; addresses the focus |
| 3 | 7–9 | some understanding; some reference to language; some detail; partly addresses the focus |
| 2 | 4–6 | basic surface meaning; a little reference; some attempt at the question |
| 1 | 1–3 | a few straightforward comments; little relevance |

Notice that **"focus of the question"** appears at every level.

## Form terms

| Term | Meaning | Use in an essay |
|---|---|---|
| شعر | couplet | the unit you analyse in a ghazal |
| مصرع | single line of a couplet | quote and compare the two lines |
| مطلع | opening couplet, both lines rhymed | how the ghazal announces its mood |
| مقطع | closing couplet with the pen name | the poet's final stance |
| تخلص | pen name | the poet speaking of himself or herself |
| ردیف | refrain repeated at each couplet's end | an idea the poem keeps returning to |
| قافیہ | rhyme before the refrain | sound that binds key words |
| نظم | poem developing one subject | follow its movement |
| مسدس | six-line stanza form | the closing couplet of each stanza turns or sharpens |
| قطعہ | short poem of connected couplets | one point, often a turn at the end |

## Technique terms (with effect)

*تشبیہ* simile, *استعارہ* metaphor, *تلمیح* allusion, *تضاد* contrast, *تکرار* repetition, *مبالغہ*
exaggeration, *طنز* satire, *علامت* symbol, *لہجہ* tone. Always add: what does it make the reader see,
feel or think?

## Question wording

- Direct: *آپ کی کیا رائے ہے؟* / *آپ کیا محسوس کرتے ہیں؟* ("What do you think?", "What are your
  feelings about ...?")
- By implication: *... کن طریقوں سے ...* / *کس طرح* ("Explore the ways in which ...")
- Mark: the **text**, the **focus**, the **method word**.

Personal response is asked for directly (آپ کی کیا رائے ہے، آپ کیا محسوس کرتے ہیں) or by implication (کن طریقوں سے، کس طرح)." loading="lazy">

## Paragraph method

**Point** (on the focus) – **short quotation** – **literal meaning** – **deeper meaning** – **method and
effect** – **link back**.

<figure class="topic-figure">
  <img src="https://img.marlbridge.com/cambridge-o-level-urdu-language-3247-paper-2-poetry-essay--07-worked-paragraph.webp" width="900" height="884" alt="Two illustrative lines, دیا جلا کے رکھا ہے ہوا کے رستے میں and یہ ضد ہے دل کی کہ شاید وہ لوٹ آئے کبھی, analysed in five moves: the image of a lamp in the wind's path, its deeper meaning, the word &quot;ضد&quot;, the uncertain words &quot;شاید&quot; and &quot;کبھی&quot;, and a link back to the focus, کشمکش." loading="lazy">

</figure>

## Ghazal vs nazm essay plan

| Ghazal | Nazm |
|---|---|
| select 3–4 couplets that fit the focus | follow opening, development and close |
| each couplet one paragraph | each stage one paragraph |
| comment on refrain if it carries the focus | comment on stanza endings and turns |

## Poem card (one per poem)

Central idea · tone · 4–6 memorised phrases with techniques · likely focuses · one judgement.

## Coverage

The Paper 2 study guide recommends knowing about six of the ten ghazals and two of the three nazms in
close detail, so that both questions stay open on the day.

## Exam traps

- Summary instead of argument.
- Couplets chosen for memory, not for the focus.
- Whole couplets copied, no analysis.
- Form named without effect.
- Biography in place of response.
- Misquotation.
- No personal view when the question asks for one.
- Overrunning: about 35 minutes is a fair share for 15 of 50 marks.

## Self-test

1. How many Poetry questions are set, and how many do you answer?
2. Name the poets of the ten prescribed ghazals.
3. What is a *مقطع*?
4. Why is a ghazal essay built by selecting couplets rather than by working through in order?
5. What is the stanza shape of a *مسدس*?
6. Which phrase appears in every level of the mark scheme?
7. What are the three things to mark in a question before planning?
8. Give an Urdu wording that asks for a personal response directly.
9. What is a *ردیف*, and when is it worth discussing?
10. What does a poem card contain?
11. Can you bring the text into the exam?
12. How many of the ghazals and nazms does the Paper 2 guide suggest knowing closely?

**Answers**

1. Two; one.
2. Khwaja Haider Ali Ahtish, Parveen Shakir, Momin Khan Momin, Allama Iqbal, Ahmed Faraz.
3. The closing couplet of a ghazal, usually containing the poet's pen name.
4. Each couplet is complete in itself and may treat a different mood, so only some couplets fit a
   given focus.
5. Six lines, usually four rhyming lines followed by a couplet on a new rhyme.
6. The focus of the question (fully, partly, little relevance).
7. The text, the focus and the method word.
8. *آپ کی کیا رائے ہے؟* or *آپ کیا محسوس کرتے ہیں؟*
9. The refrain repeated at the end of each couplet; worth discussing when the repeated words carry the
   idea the question asks about.
10. Central idea, tone, memorised phrases with techniques, likely focuses, a judgement.
11. No: set texts may not be taken into the examination.
12. About six of the ten ghazals and two of the three nazms.
