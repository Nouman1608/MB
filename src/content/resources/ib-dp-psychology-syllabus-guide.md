This guide covers the full syllabus structure of **IB Diploma Programme
Psychology**, first assessment 2019, for both Standard Level (SL) and
Higher Level (HL) students. It complements the
[subject overview](/resources/ib-dp-psychology-subject-guide/) and the
[assessment revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-psychology-assessment-revision-notes/)
already on the site by setting out what is actually taught -- the core
approaches, approaches to researching behaviour, and the four options.

Total teaching hours are 150 at SL and 240 at HL, made up of the core
(90 SL / 120 HL), the chosen option or options (20 SL / 40 HL), and the
internal assessment (20 hours at both levels).

## Core (90 hours SL / 120 hours HL, including research methodology)

- **Biological approach to understanding behaviour** -- how physiology,
  neurochemistry and genetics contribute to explaining behaviour.
- **Cognitive approach to understanding behaviour** -- how mental
  processes such as memory, perception and thinking shape behaviour.
- **Sociocultural approach to understanding behaviour** -- how social
  and cultural context shapes behaviour and mental processes.
- **Approaches to researching behaviour** -- 20 hours at both SL and HL,
  taught alongside the three content approaches above rather than as a
  separate block, covering the qualitative and quantitative methods
  psychologists use to test their ideas.

These three approaches are not alternatives to choose between -- every
student studies all three, and Paper 3's short-answer questions test
approaches to research specifically, separate from the content
approaches tested in Paper 1. Many topics within the core are
deliberately revisited from more than one approach -- memory, for
instance, can be examined biologically (through brain structures),
cognitively (through information-processing models) and socioculturally
(through the influence of schema and culture on recall) -- and the
course expects students to recognise this rather than filing each topic
under a single approach only.

## Options -- choose one (20 hours SL / 40 hours HL)

- **Abnormal psychology** -- the definition, explanation and treatment
  of psychological disorders.
- **Developmental psychology** -- how cognition, behaviour and identity
  change across the lifespan.
- **Health psychology** -- the psychological factors influencing
  health, illness and healthcare behaviour.
- **Psychology of human relationships** -- how relationships form,
  function and sometimes end.

Unlike the core, which is compulsory for everyone, your class studies
only **one** option at SL and **two** options at HL. At HL, Paper 2
requires one essay question from each of the two studied options -- so
HL students should confirm with their teacher exactly which two options
(not just one) their course has actually covered, since the SL/HL
option requirement genuinely differs and the exam allocation follows
that difference directly.

## Internal assessment -- experimental study (20 hours, both SL and HL)

A report on an experimental study designed, conducted (usually in a
small group) and individually written up by the student, then evaluated
with suggested modifications. It is externally moderated by IB
examiners. Although the study itself is often carried out collaboratively
with classmates, the write-up, analysis and evaluation must be each
student's own individual work, which is one of the more common points of
confusion for students new to the assessment.

## How to approach it

Because the three core approaches (biological, cognitive, sociocultural)
are examined together in Paper 1 through short-answer questions plus one
essay, practise moving between all three approaches on the same topic --
for example, explaining memory from a biological angle and then a
cognitive angle -- rather than revising each approach in an isolated
silo. Confirm early which single option your SL class (or which two
options your HL class) actually studied, since Paper 2 is
option-specific and revising an unstudied option wastes time. Approaches
to researching behaviour is easy to under-revise because it has no
options and feels like "just methodology", but it is tested directly in
Paper 3 through short-answer questions and is foundational to writing up
the internal assessment credibly.

For every study you learn across the three core approaches, practise
recalling not just its finding but its methodology and at least one
genuine evaluative point -- a strength or limitation of how it was
conducted -- since Paper 1's essay question specifically rewards
evaluation, not just accurate description of what a study found. This
matters just as much for the chosen option: because only one option is
studied at SL, the studies and theories within it need the same
depth of evaluative preparation as the compulsory core, not a lighter
treatment on the assumption that "it's only one topic".

The experimental study (internal assessment) is most often let down not
by weak content knowledge but by insufficient ethical planning and an
under-justified choice of design, so treat the research-methodology
content from "approaches to researching behaviour" as directly
applicable to your own IA rather than as separate exam content --
students who can explain why they chose their specific experimental
design, and what its ethical considerations were, in the same rigorous
way they would evaluate a published study tend to score more highly
than those who describe their procedure without justifying it.

Because the option content roughly doubles in hours between SL (20) and
HL (40), HL students should not assume their second option can be
covered at the same depth in less time simply because it was added
later in the course -- the syllabus allocates genuinely equal teaching
time to both of an HL student's two options, and Paper 2 examines them
with equal weight.
