These questions cover Reactivity 2.3, How far? The extent of chemical change (understandings 2.3.1 to 2.3.7), from the February 2023 IB Diploma Programme *Chemistry guide*, whose first exams ran in 2025. Questions 1 to 7 test content for **both SL and HL**. Questions 8 to 12 test understandings 2.3.5 to 2.3.7, which are **HL only**, and are labelled so.

## Questions

**1.** State two characteristics of a chemical system at dynamic equilibrium. **[2]**

**2.** A small volume of ethanol is placed in a stoppered flask kept at 25 °C. After a while, the pressure of ethanol vapour above the liquid stops changing. Explain, in terms of rates, how this equilibrium is established. **[3]**

**3.** **(a)** Deduce the equilibrium constant expression for 2NO(g) + 2H₂(g) ⇌ N₂(g) + 2H₂O(g). **[1]**
**(b)** Deduce the equilibrium constant expression for Ag⁺(aq) + 2NH₃(aq) ⇌ [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺(aq). **[1]**
**(c)** Write the expression for the reverse of the reaction in (b). **[1]**

**4.** Four reactions, W to Z, have these values of K at 298 K: W, 5.0 × 10⁻²⁰; X, 0.40; Y, 1.0; Z, 8.5 × 10¹⁵.

**(a)** Describe the composition of the equilibrium mixture for each reaction. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate K at 298 K for the reverse of reaction X. **[1]**

**5.** Ethyne and hydrogen reach equilibrium with ethane in a sealed vessel: C₂H₂(g) + 2H₂(g) ⇌ C₂H₆(g). The forward reaction is exothermic. For each change, state and explain the effect on the amount of C₂H₆ at equilibrium, and state the effect on the value of K.

**(a)** More hydrogen is added at constant temperature. **[2]**
**(b)** The temperature is raised. **[2]**
**(c)** The gas mixture is squeezed into half its original volume, with no change in temperature. **[2]**
**(d)** A catalyst is added. **[2]**

**6.** A closed flask contains aqueous ammonia with ammonia gas above it: NH₃(g) ⇌ NH₃(aq). Dissolving ammonia in water releases heat.

**(a)** Predict and explain the effect on the concentration of dissolved ammonia when more ammonia gas is pumped into the flask. **[2]**
**(b)** State the effect of warming the flask on the concentration of dissolved ammonia. **[1]**

**7.** For a gas-phase reaction, K = 4.6 × 10³ at 300 K and K = 2.1 at 500 K. Deduce, with a reason, whether the forward reaction is exothermic or endothermic. **[3]**

**8.** (HL only) For N₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2NO(g), K = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ at a set high temperature. At one moment a mixture at that temperature contains [N₂] = 0.50, [O₂] = 0.20 and [NO] = 0.0050 mol dm⁻³. Calculate Q, and deduce what happens to [NO] as the mixture moves to equilibrium. **[4]**

**9.** (HL only) 0.600 mol PCl₃ and 0.500 mol Cl₂ are sealed in a 5.00 dm³ vessel and heated: PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g) ⇌ PCl₅(g). At equilibrium the vessel holds 0.150 mol PCl₅. Calculate K at this temperature. **[5]**

**10.** (HL only) For 2BrCl(g) ⇌ Br₂(g) + Cl₂(g), K = 0.0625 at a set temperature. Pure BrCl at 0.300 mol dm⁻³ is allowed to reach equilibrium. Calculate the equilibrium concentrations of all three species. **[5]**

**11.** (HL only) For Cl₂(g) ⇌ 2Cl(g), use K = 2.5 × 10⁻⁷ at 1200 K. Chlorine at an initial concentration of 0.400 mol dm⁻³ is heated to 1200 K.

**(a)** Calculate [Cl] at equilibrium, stating the approximation you use. **[3]**
**(b)** Show that the approximation is reasonable. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate ΔG⦵ for the reaction at 1200 K. Use R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. **[2]**

**12.** (HL only) An isomerisation has ΔG⦵ = −12.4 kJ mol⁻¹ at 350 K.

**(a)** Calculate K at 350 K. Use R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the equilibrium mixture. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why any reaction with K < 1 has a positive ΔG⦵. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Choose from: forward and backward rates are equal; concentrations of reactants and products stay constant; both reactions continue; the system is closed. First correct characteristic [1]; second correct characteristic [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Concentrations are equal" is wrong and contradicts an otherwise correct answer; write "constant", not "equal".

**2.** At first only evaporation happens, at a constant rate set by the temperature [1]; as ethanol molecules build up in the vapour, the rate of condensation increases [1]; equilibrium is reached when the rate of condensation equals the rate of evaporation, so the vapour pressure no longer changes [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name both processes and compare their rates; "the liquid stops evaporating" contradicts the dynamic nature of equilibrium.

**3. (a)** K = [N₂][H₂O]² / ([NO]²[H₂]²) [1]
**(b)** K = [[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺] / ([Ag⁺][NH₃]²) [1]
**(c)** K = [Ag⁺][NH₃]² / [[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺] [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each coefficient becomes a power; writing 2[NO] or 2[NH₃] instead of [NO]² or [NH₃]² loses the mark.

**4. (a)** W: K ≪ 1, essentially only reactants; reaction hardly occurs [1]; X: K < 1, reactants predominate [1]; Y: K = 1, neither reactants nor products favoured [1]; Z: K ≫ 1, reaction goes almost to completion, essentially all products [1]
**(b)** 1 / 0.40 = **2.5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Describe the mixture, not the speed; "Z reacts quickly" earns nothing, because K gives no information about rate.

**5. (a)** Amount of C₂H₆ increases: the position shifts right to use up some of the added H₂ [1]; K unchanged [1]
**(b)** Amount of C₂H₆ decreases: raising the temperature favours the endothermic, backward reaction [1]; K decreases [1]
**(c)** Amount of C₂H₆ increases: halving the volume raises the pressure, and the position shifts right, to the side with fewer gas molecules (1 rather than 3) [1]; K unchanged [1]
**(d)** No change in the amount of C₂H₆: a catalyst speeds up forward and backward reactions equally, so equilibrium is only reached sooner [1]; K unchanged [1]
*Examiner insight:* For each change give direction, a reason and the K effect; a bare "shifts right" with no reason seldom gains full credit.

**6. (a)** Concentration of dissolved ammonia increases [1]; the higher concentration (partial pressure) of NH₃(g) shifts the position to the right, so more gas dissolves [1]
**(b)** It **decreases** (the endothermic direction, ammonia leaving solution, is favoured) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the information given; the heat released on dissolving tells you which direction is endothermic.

**7.** K is smaller at the higher temperature, so there is less product at equilibrium at 500 K [1]; raising the temperature favours the endothermic direction, which here must be the backward reaction [1]; so the forward reaction is **exothermic** [1]
*Examiner insight:* State the link explicitly: a K that falls as T rises means an exothermic forward reaction; a one-word answer does not show the reasoning.

**8.** Q = [NO]² / ([N₂][O₂]) [1]; Q = 0.0050² / (0.50 × 0.20) = **2.5 × 10⁻⁴** [1]; Q < K [1]; net forward reaction, so **[NO] increases** until Q = K [1]
*Examiner insight:* Square [NO]; without the power Q comes out as 0.050, which is greater than K and reverses the conclusion.

**9.** At equilibrium: PCl₃ = 0.600 − 0.150 = 0.450 mol and Cl₂ = 0.500 − 0.150 = 0.350 mol [1]; concentrations: [PCl₃] = 0.0900, [Cl₂] = 0.0700, [PCl₅] = 0.0300 mol dm⁻³ [1]; K = [PCl₅] / ([PCl₃][Cl₂]) [1]; K = 0.0300 / (0.0900 × 0.0700) [1] = **4.76** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Divide by 5.00 dm³ before substituting; using moles here gives 0.952, five times too small.

**10.** Let [Br₂] = [Cl₂] = x, so [BrCl] = 0.300 − 2x [1]; K = x² / (0.300 − 2x)² = 0.0625 [1]; square root: x / (0.300 − 2x) = 0.250 [1]; x = 0.0750 − 0.500x, so x = 0.0500 [1]; **[Br₂] = [Cl₂] = 0.0500 mol dm⁻³; [BrCl] = 0.200 mol dm⁻³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Two BrCl are used for each Br₂ formed; writing 0.300 − x in the change row is the commonest slip.

**11. (a)** K is very small, so assume [Cl₂]eqm ≈ [Cl₂]initial = 0.400 mol dm⁻³ [1]; [Cl]² = 2.5 × 10⁻⁷ × 0.400 = 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ [1]; [Cl] = **3.2 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³** [1]
**(b)** Cl₂ used = half of [Cl] = 1.6 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³, which is about 0.04% of 0.400, so [Cl₂] is effectively unchanged [1]
**(c)** ΔG⦵ = −8.31 × 1200 × ln(2.5 × 10⁻⁷) [1] = **+152 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* State the approximation in words before using it; also remember Cl₂ dissociates 1 : 2, so the Cl₂ used is half of [Cl].

**12. (a)** lnK = 12 400 / (8.31 × 350) = 4.263 [1]; K = e^4.263 = **71.0** [1]
**(b)** K > 1, so products predominate in the equilibrium mixture [1]
**(c)** The natural log of a number below 1 is negative, so −RT lnK is positive [1]
*Examiner insight:* Convert −12.4 kJ to −12 400 J; leaving it in kJ gives K ≈ 1.004 and the wrong description.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing equilibrium concentrations as "equal" when the correct word is "constant".
- Using coefficients as multipliers, not powers, in K and Q expressions.
- Stating that K changes with concentration or pressure; only temperature changes it.
- Giving Le Châtelier shifts with no reason, or no comment on K.
- Counting moles of all species, not gas molecules only, when judging a pressure change.
- Claiming a catalyst raises the equilibrium yield.
- (HL only) Substituting moles into K for a vessel larger than 1 dm³.
- (HL only) Ignoring the mole ratio in the change row of an ICE table.
- (HL only) Using the small-K approximation without stating or checking it.
- (HL only) Putting kJ or °C into ΔG⦵ = −RT lnK.
