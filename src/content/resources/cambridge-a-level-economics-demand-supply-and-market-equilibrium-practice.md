## Questions

**1.** Define effective demand. **[2]**

**2.** Three buyers' demand for a product is shown. Calculate market demand at each price.

| Price (cu) | Buyer A | Buyer B | Buyer C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 8 | 6 | 10 |
| 20 | 5 | 4 | 6 |
| 30 | 2 | 1 | 3 |

**[3]**

**3.** In a market for bicycles, state whether each event moves the market along a curve or moves the curve itself, and give the direction.

**(a)** Consumers' incomes rise (bicycles are a normal good). **[1]**
**(b)** The price of steel, an input, rises. **[1]**
**(c)** The price of bicycles rises. **[1]**
**(d)** The government introduces a subsidy for bicycle makers. **[1]**

**4.** In a market, Qd = 90 - 3P and Qs = 6P - 18, where P is in cu.

**(a)** Find the price and quantity at which the market clears. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the excess at a price of 16. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain how the market moves back towards equilibrium from a price of 16. **[3]**

**5.** Continue with the market in question 4.

**(a)** Incomes rise and demand becomes Qd = 108 - 3P. Calculate the new equilibrium. **[3]**
**(b)** Input costs then rise and supply becomes Qs = 6P - 36. Calculate the equilibrium with both changes. **[3]**
**(c)** Compare the result with the original equilibrium and explain why the change in quantity needs the numbers. **[2]**

**6.** The price of games consoles falls. Explain the likely effect on the market for console games. **[3]**

**7.** Demand for wooden furniture falls. Using derived demand, explain the effect on the market for timber. **[4]**

**8.** Lamb meat and wool come from the same sheep. Demand for lamb rises. Explain the effect on the market for wool. **[4]**

**9.** A theatre has 400 seats. Demand is Qd = 1200 - 4P.

**(a)** Calculate the price that sells every seat. **[2]**
**(b)** A star joins the cast and demand becomes Qd = 1360 - 4P. Calculate the excess demand at the old price. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the new price that clears the market. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain the rationing, signalling and incentivising functions of price in this case. **[3]**

**10.** A private clinic offers 60 appointments a week. Evaluate whether price is a fair way to ration them. **[8]**

**11.** Four markets show these changes. In each, assume only one curve shifted. State which curve shifted and in which direction.

| Market | Price | Quantity |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | rises | rises |
| (b) | falls | rises |
| (c) | rises | falls |
| (d) | falls | falls |

**[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Willingness to buy a good at a given price [1], backed by the ability to pay, meaning purchasing power [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition needs both parts. A wish without money behind it is a want, so name the ability to pay.

**2.** Add across the buyers at each price. At 10: 8 + 6 + 10 = **24** [1]. At 20: 5 + 4 + 6 = **15** [1]. At 30: 2 + 1 + 3 = **6** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the addition for each price. A table of three totals with no working gives the reader nothing to credit if one figure slips.

**3.** **(a)** Demand shifts right [1]. **(b)** Supply shifts left [1]. **(c)** Movement along the curves: quantity demanded contracts and quantity supplied extends [1]. **(d)** Supply shifts right [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For a price change write "movement along", not "shift". The direction word matters as much as the choice of curve.

**4.** **(a)** Set 90 - 3P = 6P - 18 [1]. So 108 = 9P, giving P = **12** [1]. Q = 90 - 36 = **54** [1].
**(b)** At 16, Qd = 90 - 48 = 42 [1] and Qs = 96 - 18 = 78 [1]. Excess supply = 78 - 42 = **36** [1].
**(c)** Sellers cannot sell all their stock at 16 and unsold goods build up [1]. Sellers cut price [1]. The falling price extends quantity demanded and contracts quantity supplied, until both equal 54 at a price of 12 [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (c) a chain is needed: the surplus leads to a price cut and the price cut changes both quantities. Naming the surplus alone is only the start.

**5.** **(a)** Set 108 - 3P = 6P - 18 [1]. So 126 = 9P, giving P = **14** [1]. Q = 108 - 42 = **66** [1].
**(b)** Set 108 - 3P = 6P - 36 [1]. So 144 = 9P, giving P = **16** [1]. Q = 108 - 48 = **60** [1].
**(c)** Price rises from 12 to 16 and quantity rises from 54 to 60 [1]. Demand raises quantity and supply lowers it, so the net effect on quantity depends on the size of each shift [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* When two curves shift, one effect is certain and the other is not. Say which, and tie it to the numbers.

**6.** Console and game are complements in joint demand. The fall in console price extends quantity demanded of consoles [1]. More consoles in use means demand for games shifts right [1]. Equilibrium price and quantity of games both rise [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the link in order: price of console, quantity of console, demand for games. A bare statement that demand rises misses the reasoning.

**7.** Timber is demanded because it is used to make furniture, so its demand is derived [1]. Less furniture is produced [1], so demand for timber shifts left [1]. Equilibrium price and quantity of timber fall [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the product whose demand has changed first. Derived demand answers go wrong when the shift is drawn in the furniture market instead of timber.

**8.** Lamb and wool are in joint supply [1]. Higher demand for lamb raises the number of sheep reared [1], so supply of wool shifts right [1]. Price of wool falls and quantity rises [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The cause is demand in one market, the effect is supply in the other. Do not draw a demand shift in the wool market.

**9.** **(a)** Set 1200 - 4P = 400 [1], so P = **200** [1].
**(b)** At 200, new Qd = 1360 - 800 = 560 [1]. Excess demand = 560 - 400 = **160** [1].
**(c)** 1360 - 4P = 400 gives P = **240** [1].
**(d)** Rationing: the higher price removes the 160 buyers with least willingness or ability to pay [1]. Signalling: the rise shows the show is popular, so the theatre knows demand has risen [1]. Incentivising: higher revenue encourages more performances in future [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Apply each function to the theatre. A definition of rationing on its own is not enough for a context question.

**10.** Price allocates appointments to those with effective demand, those willing and able to pay [1]. It is efficient in one sense because appointments go to those who value them most in money terms [1]. A higher price signals scarcity and gives the clinic an incentive to offer more appointments [2]. It may be unfair because willingness to pay depends on income, so patients with greater medical need but lower income may be excluded [2]. Rationing by queue or by need may be fairer, and fairness is a value judgement [1]. Judgement: price is fair only if you accept ability to pay as the test, which is weaker for health care than for goods like bicycles [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong answer weighs the argument and reaches a supported view. A list of advantages and drawbacks with no judgement usually leaves evaluation marks unearned.

**11.** **(a)** Demand shifts right [1]. **(b)** Supply shifts right [1]. **(c)** Supply shifts left [1]. **(d)** Demand shifts left [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Same direction for price and quantity points to demand; opposite directions point to supply. State the one-curve assumption in your answer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling a purely price-driven change a rise in demand.
- Giving only the ability to pay, or only the willingness, in a definition of effective demand.
- Adding prices rather than quantities when building a market curve.
- Not showing the algebra when finding an equilibrium.
- Naming a surplus or shortage without saying how price then changes.
- Putting a derived demand or joint supply shift in the wrong market.
- Listing the functions of price without applying them to the context.
- Ending an evaluation without a judgement.
