Condensed recall for **1.3 Water scarcity and water quality** (IB DP Geography, Option A: Freshwater). See the [Freshwater study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-geography-freshwater-study-guide/) for fuller treatment.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Physical scarcity | Not enough water to meet demand |
| Economic scarcity | Water exists but people lack infrastructure or money to use it |
| Water stress | Pressure on available supply, often compared with demand per person |
| Water footprint | Water used to produce goods and services |
| Virtual water | Water embedded in traded products, for example food exports from dry regions |
| Transboundary river | River shared by countries, so upstream use affects downstream users |

Keep these separate: students often blur scarcity, stress and quality.

## Causes and consequences of scarcity

- **Causes:** climate variability, population growth, rising per-capita demand, agricultural irrigation, industrial use, leaking infrastructure, transboundary sharing.
- **Consequences:** reduced food production, conflict risk, and health problems when people turn to unsafe sources.

## Water quality and pollutants

Sources: sewage, agricultural fertiliser and pesticides, industrial effluent, heavy metals, plastics, and saline intrusion in coastal aquifers.

Monitoring: dissolved oxygen, biological indicators and chemical tests.

## Eutrophication, in order

1. Nutrients (fertiliser, sewage) enter a lake or slow river.
2. Algal blooms form at the surface.
3. Blooms block light; algae die and sink.
4. Bacteria decomposing the dead algae use up **dissolved oxygen**.
5. Fish and other organisms die.

## Physical versus economic scarcity

| | Physical | Economic |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Supply too small | Access limited |
| Cause | Dry climate, over-abstraction | Lack of money, pipes, treatment |
| Response | Demand management, new sources | Investment, infrastructure, governance |

## Exam traps

- Using scarcity, stress and quality as if they were the same idea.
- Saying nutrients kill fish directly: the killer is the oxygen loss after decomposition.
- Ignoring transboundary issues when asked about causes.
- Inventing statistics for a named river or country.

## Self-test

1. Define virtual water.
2. Distinguish physical from economic scarcity.
3. Give three causes of water scarcity.
4. Outline how eutrophication reduces oxygen.
5. Name two sources of water pollution and one way to monitor quality.

**Answers:** 1. Water embedded in a traded product. 2. Physical: not enough water; economic: water exists but cannot be accessed for lack of money or infrastructure. 3. For example population growth, irrigation, leaking pipes. 4. Nutrients cause algal blooms; the algae die and decompose, and bacteria consume dissolved oxygen. 5. Sewage and fertiliser; dissolved oxygen tests.
