This guide covers section 4.4, Similarity, in the Geometry topic of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The syllabus lists three outcomes: calculate lengths of similar shapes, use the relationships between lengths and areas of similar shapes and between lengths, surface areas and volumes of similar solids, and solve problems and give simple explanations involving similarity, including showing that two triangles are similar using geometric reasons. The notes also give the volume relationship in the form Volume of A ÷ Volume of B = (length of A ÷ length of B)³. After this guide, use the [geometry revision notes](/resources/o-level-mathematics-geometry-revision-notes/) and the [similarity practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-similarity-practice/).

## What section 4.4 asks for

1. Find missing lengths in similar shapes using the scale factor.
2. Connect the scale factor for length with the factors for area and volume.
3. Explain, with reasons, why two triangles are similar, then use that to find lengths or ratios.

## What similar means

Two shapes are **similar** if one is an enlargement of the other, possibly turned or flipped. Then:

- all corresponding angles are equal, and
- all corresponding lengths are in the same ratio, called the **scale factor**.

Congruent shapes are similar with a scale factor of 1. Any two circles are similar, and so are any two squares or any two regular polygons with the same number of sides. Two rectangles are similar only if the ratio of length to width is the same.

## Finding lengths

If shape B is an enlargement of shape A with scale factor k, every length in B is k times the matching length in A. To find k, divide a length in B by the matching length in A.

### Worked example 1

Triangles ABC and DEF are similar with A matching D, B matching E and C matching F. AB = 5 cm, DE = 8 cm and BC = 7.5 cm. Find EF.

```
scale factor = DE ÷ AB = 8 ÷ 5 = 1.6
EF = 1.6 × 7.5 = 12 cm
```

Match the sides carefully. The side opposite angle A in ABC corresponds to the side opposite angle D in DEF. Write the vertices in matching order to avoid errors.

### Worked example 2

Two rectangles are similar. One is 6 cm by 4 cm. The other has a longer side of 15 cm. Find its shorter side.

```
scale factor = 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5
shorter side = 2.5 × 4 = 10 cm
```

### Shadow and pole problems

Objects in the same sunlight with their shadows on level ground form similar right-angled triangles, because the angle of the sun is the same.

### Worked example 3

A vertical stick of height 1.5 m casts a shadow of 2 m. At the same time a building casts a shadow of 28 m. Find the height of the building.

```
scale factor = 28 ÷ 2 = 14
height = 14 × 1.5 = 21 m
```

## Area and volume of similar shapes

Let the scale factor for length be k. Then:

| Measure | Multiplied by |
|---|---|
| Length (including perimeter) | k |
| Area (including surface area) | k² |
| Volume | k³ |

In terms of ratios, if the lengths are in the ratio a : b, the areas are in the ratio a² : b² and the volumes in the ratio a³ : b³.

### Worked example 4

Two similar pentagons have corresponding sides in the ratio 3 : 5. The area of the smaller is 36 cm². Find the area of the larger.

```
area ratio = 3² : 5² = 9 : 25
larger area = 36 × 25/9 = 100 cm²
```

### Worked example 5

Two similar cones have heights 6 cm and 9 cm. The smaller cone has volume 40 cm³. Find the volume of the larger.

```
length scale factor = 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5
volume scale factor = 1.5³ = 3.375
larger volume = 40 × 3.375 = 135 cm³
```

### Working backwards from areas or volumes

You are often given areas or volumes and asked for a length ratio. Take the square root of the area ratio, or the cube root of the volume ratio.

### Worked example 6

Two similar solids have surface areas 48 cm² and 75 cm². The larger has volume 500 cm³. Find the volume of the smaller.

```
area ratio = 48 : 75 = 16 : 25
length ratio = 4 : 5
volume ratio = 4³ : 5³ = 64 : 125
smaller volume = 500 × 64/125 = 256 cm³
```

Be careful to use the correct pair. The larger volume is 500 cm³, so the smaller volume is found by multiplying by 64/125, which is less than 1. The answer, 256 cm³, is smaller, as it should be.

### Worked example 7

Two similar bottles have heights 12 cm and 18 cm. The smaller holds 250 ml. How much does the larger hold?

```
length scale factor = 18 ÷ 12 = 1.5
volume scale factor = 1.5³ = 3.375
capacity = 250 × 3.375 = 843.75 ml
```

Since capacity depends on volume, the larger bottle holds nearly 3.4 times as much, even though it is only 1.5 times as tall. This idea is the reason why a small increase in size can change quantities like cost or weight a lot.

### Worked example 8

Two similar triangles have areas 50 cm² and 98 cm². The shortest side of the smaller is 5 cm. Find the shortest side of the larger.

```
area ratio = 98 ÷ 50 = 1.96
length ratio = √1.96 = 1.4
shortest side = 1.4 × 5 = 7 cm
```

### Worked example 9

A model ship is made to a scale of 1 : 200. The model is 45 cm long and has a volume of 250 cm³ for the hull. Find the real length in metres and the real hull volume in cubic metres.

```
real length = 45 × 200 = 9000 cm = 90 m
volume factor = 200³ = 8 000 000
real volume = 250 × 8 000 000 = 2 000 000 000 cm³ = 2000 m³
```

The conversion from cm³ to m³ divides by 1 000 000.

## Proving triangles similar

Two triangles are similar if either of these conditions holds.

- **Equal angles.** Two pairs of corresponding angles are equal (the third pair then must be equal as well).
- **Sides in proportion.** All three pairs of corresponding sides are in the same ratio.

When the diagram has parallel lines, use angle facts as reasons: corresponding angles are equal, alternate angles are equal, vertically opposite angles are equal, and a common angle is shared.

### Worked example 10

In triangle ABC, D lies on AB and E lies on AC, and DE is parallel to BC. AD = 3 cm, AB = 9 cm and DE = 4 cm. (a) Show that triangles ADE and ABC are similar. (b) Find BC. (c) The area of triangle ADE is 5 cm². Find the area of the quadrilateral DBCE.

```
(a) Angle DAE = angle BAC (common angle).
    Angle ADE = angle ABC (corresponding angles, DE parallel to BC).
    So the triangles are similar (two equal angles).
(b) scale factor = AB ÷ AD = 9 ÷ 3 = 3
    BC = 3 × 4 = 12 cm
(c) area scale factor = 3² = 9
    area of ABC = 5 × 9 = 45 cm²
    area of DBCE = 45 − 5 = 40 cm²
```

Give a reason for each statement. A mark scheme looks for the common angle, the corresponding angles with the parallel lines named, and a conclusion.

## Common mistakes

- Matching sides incorrectly, for example pairing the longest side in one triangle with a shorter side in the other.
- Using the length scale factor for an area or volume. A scale factor of 3 for length is 9 for area and 27 for volume.
- Dividing when you should multiply, so that a "larger" answer comes out smaller.
- Forgetting to square-root the area ratio when a length is needed.
- Giving no reason when proving triangles similar, or writing only "they look the same".
- Treating all rectangles as similar. Their sides must be in the same ratio.

## Exam technique

- **Show that** triangles are similar: state each equal pair of angles with its reason, then name the conclusion.
- **Calculate** a length: write the scale factor first, then multiply.
- For an area or volume, write the ratio of lengths first and then square or cube it.
- Check the size of your answer: it should be larger if you are going to the larger shape.
