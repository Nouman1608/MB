## Why the IA is weighted slightly higher at SL

The individual investigation carries a larger share of the final grade at SL (25%) than at HL (20%). HL students sit a longer Paper 1 (2 hours against 1 hour) that is worth more (30% against 25%), so the balance shifts toward the external papers at HL, even though the investigation itself and its criteria are the same at both levels.

## SL vs HL weightings

| Component | What it is | SL | HL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Data in a variety of forms on one previously unseen case study; all questions compulsory | 1 hour, 25% | 2 hours, 30% |
| Paper 2 | Section A: short-answer and data-based questions. Section B: structured essays, with a limited choice | 2 hours, 50% | 2 hours 30 minutes, 50% |
| Internal assessment (individual investigation) | A written report of up to 3,000 words | 10 hours, 25% | 10 hours, 20% |
| **Total external / internal** | | **75% / 25%** | **80% / 20%** |

These figures come from the IB's ESS subject brief for first assessment 2026. External assessment
totals 3 hours at SL and 4 hours 30 minutes at HL.

## The internal assessment

- An **open-ended individual investigation**: each student formulates their own research question,
  gathers and analyses data to answer it, and draws evidence-based conclusions. Fieldwork is a
  common route, but the brief does not limit the task to fieldwork.
- Capped at **3,000 words**, around **10 teaching hours** of work.
- Deliberately draws on methods from **both** the sciences and individuals-and-societies strands,
  reflecting ESS's dual nature.

## Why ESS is different from a single-group science or humanities subject

ESS can count as a **sciences** subject, an **individuals-and-societies** subject, or **both** —
freeing up an extra subject choice if counted as both. Paper 1 is built on one unseen case study, and Paper 2 mixes short-answer, data-based and essay
questions; both draw on the scientific **and** societal strands.

## The internal assessment in more depth

The individual investigation is graded against criteria covering the whole research process: formulating a focused research question suited to the issue; justifying and applying an appropriate methodology capable of generating reliable data; processing and presenting that data (including basic statistical treatment where relevant); and reaching evidence-based conclusions that are then evaluated for strengths and limitations, with specific, feasible suggestions for improving the investigation. Because ESS draws on both scientific and societal methods, a strong IA typically integrates both dimensions explicitly -- for example, connecting a measured environmental change to its social, economic or policy implications -- rather than treating the investigation as a purely scientific exercise that stops at the data. Your teacher holds the full criteria from the subject guide; ask for them before you plan.

## Command terms across the papers

Both written papers use the same command-term glossary as the other DP sciences and individuals-and-societies subjects: **state**, **identify** and **outline** at the lower end; **explain**, **compare** and **suggest** in the middle; **evaluate**, **discuss** and **to what extent** at the top, where a reasoned judgement weighing multiple perspectives or pieces of evidence is required. Because ESS explicitly combines scientific and societal content, a strong "evaluate" or "discuss" answer in this subject often needs to weigh evidence from both strands together -- for example, evaluating an environmental policy requires considering both its scientific effectiveness and its social/economic feasibility, not just one dimension.

## Why ESS's dual nature shapes its assessment structure

Because ESS can be counted toward either the sciences group or the individuals-and-societies group of the Diploma's six subject groups (or toward both, which frees up an additional subject choice elsewhere in the Diploma), its assessment is deliberately built to be defensible as either a science or a humanities subject. This is why both written papers draw on scientific methodology (measurement, data analysis, experimental design) and societal analysis (policy, ethics, economics, human systems) side by side, rather than separating the two strands into different papers -- a structural choice that reflects the subject's own stated aim of examining environmental issues through the combined lens of natural science and human society.

## How this compares to a single-group science

Like Biology, Chemistry and Physics, ESS does include a collaborative sciences project (10 of its 50 experimental-programme hours, shared by SL and HL) alongside its experimental work -- this is not unique to ESS. What is different is the weighting of the internal assessment. The individual investigation is the only internally assessed component in ESS, as in the other sciences. Its weighting (25% at SL, 20% at HL) is higher than the other sciences' flat 20% at SL, but equal to them at HL, not universally "more" -- so the extra weight on the investigation exists only at SL. ESS investigations also tend to look outward at environmental issues and their social context, where Biology, Chemistry and Physics investigations are more often laboratory-based.

## Exam traps

- Treating the IA as a pure science lab report, missing the required societal/sustainability dimension.
- Exceeding the 3,000-word cap, or choosing a research question too broad to answer with the data you can realistically gather.
- Forgetting that written papers are weighted differently at SL (75%) and HL (80%), and that Paper 1 is longer and worth more at HL.
- Revising only content for Paper 1: it is built on an unseen case study, so data-analysis practice matters as much as recall.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long is IB ESS Paper 1 at SL and HL?**
Paper 1 lasts 1 hour and is worth 25% at SL, and lasts 2 hours and is worth 30% at HL. It gives data in a variety of forms on one previously unseen case study, and all questions are compulsory.

**What is in IB ESS Paper 2?**
Section A has short-answer and data-based questions and Section B has structured essays with a limited choice. It lasts 2 hours at SL and 2 hours 30 minutes at HL, and is worth 50% at both levels.

**What is the word limit for the ESS internal assessment?**
The individual investigation is a written report of up to 3,000 words, taking around 10 teaching hours. Choosing a research question too broad for the data you can gather is a common trap.

**Does the ESS individual investigation have to be fieldwork?**
No. It is an open-ended investigation in which you formulate your own research question, gather and analyse data and draw evidence-based conclusions. Fieldwork is a common route, but the 2026 subject brief does not limit the task to it.
