## 4.7.1.1 Poles of a magnet

- Poles: where the magnetic forces are **strongest**.
- **Like poles repel; unlike poles attract.**
- Magnetic force is **non-contact**; it weakens with distance.
- **Permanent magnet**: produces its own magnetic field all the time.
- **Induced magnet**: becomes a magnet only when placed in a magnetic field.
- Induced magnetism **always attracts**: the near end becomes the opposite pole.
- Removed from the field, an induced magnet loses most or all of its magnetism **quickly**.

| | Permanent magnet | Induced magnet |
|---|---|---|
| Own field? | Yes, always | Only while in another field |
| Forces with a magnet | Attract **or** repel | Attract only |
| After leaving a field | Keeps magnetism | Loses most or all quickly |
| Example | Steel bar magnet | Iron nail or paper clip near a magnet |

**Method: identifying an unknown bar**

```
Step 1  bring each end of the bar near one pole of a known magnet
Step 2  attraction at both ends → could be unmagnetised (induced) OR a magnet; not proof
Step 3  repulsion at either end → it IS a permanent magnet
Reason: an induced magnet is never repelled
```

## 4.7.1.2 Magnetic fields

- Magnetic field: the **region around a magnet where a force acts** on another magnet or a magnetic material.
- Magnetic materials: **iron, steel, cobalt, nickel**. (Copper, aluminium, brass: not magnetic.)
- Magnet and magnetic material: force is **always attraction**.
- Field strength depends on **distance from the magnet**; **strongest at the poles**.
- Field direction at a point = direction of the force on a **north pole** there.
- Field lines run **N → S** outside the magnet.

**Checklist for a bar magnet field diagram**

- Arrows N to S on every line.
- Lines crowded at the poles, spread out further away.
- No lines crossing or touching.
- Smooth curves from pole to pole.

### Compass plotting (sequence to learn)

```
1  draw round the magnet
2  compass near the N pole; dot where the needle's N end points
3  move compass: needle tail on the dot; mark the new point
4  repeat to the S pole; join dots; arrow N → S
5  repeat from other starting points
```

Iron filings show the **shape** only; the compass method shows the **direction** too.

### The Earth

- A compass contains a small bar magnet.
- It points in a set direction (roughly north–south) wherever it is used, so the **Earth has a magnetic field**.
- This is evidence that the **Earth's core must be magnetic**.
- The compass N pole points to geographic north, so the Earth's magnetic pole there behaves as a **south** pole.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pole | Region of a magnet where the force is strongest |
| Permanent magnet | Produces its own magnetic field |
| Induced magnet | Becomes magnetic only when in a magnetic field |
| Magnetic material | Iron, steel, cobalt or nickel |
| Magnetic field | Region where a force acts on a magnet or magnetic material |
| Field line | Line showing the direction of the force on a north pole |
| Field strength | Shown by how close the field lines are |
| Plotting compass | Small, freely turning magnet that lines up with the field |
| Non-contact force | Force that acts without the objects touching |

## Exam traps

- "The magnet repelled the iron": impossible for unmagnetised iron.
- Using attraction to prove something is a magnet. Only **repulsion** proves it.
- Naming copper or aluminium as magnetic.
- Arrows from S to N, crossing lines, or evenly spaced lines.
- Saying filings show direction.
- Describing an induced magnet as "permanently" magnetised after it has been removed from the field.
- Forgetting that the field weakens with distance.

## Quick self-test

1. Where are the magnetic forces of a bar magnet strongest?
2. What happens when two S poles are brought together?
3. Define an induced magnet.
4. Why does induced magnetism always cause attraction?
5. Name the four magnetic materials.
6. An aluminium can and a steel can pass a strong magnet. Which is attracted?
7. In which direction do field lines point outside a bar magnet?
8. How does a field diagram show where the field is strongest?
9. Why must field lines never cross?
10. Iron filings are sprinkled round a magnet. What can they not show?
11. A bar is repelled by the N pole of a known magnet. What can you conclude?
12. Why is a compass evidence that the Earth's core is magnetic?

### Answers

1. At the poles.
2. They repel.
3. A material that becomes a magnet when it is placed in a magnetic field.
4. The end nearest the magnet becomes the opposite pole, and unlike poles attract.
5. Iron, steel, cobalt and nickel.
6. The steel can.
7. From the north pole to the south pole.
8. The lines are closest together there.
9. The field has only one direction at each point.
10. The direction of the field.
11. The bar is a permanent magnet (the end facing the N pole is also an N pole).
12. The compass is a small magnet that lines up in a set direction everywhere, so the Earth must have a magnetic
    field like that of a magnet in its core.
