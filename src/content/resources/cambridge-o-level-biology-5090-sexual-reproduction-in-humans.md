This guide covers subtopic **16.4 Sexual reproduction in humans** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series. The syllabus asks you to identify the parts of the male and female reproductive systems and state their functions; to relate the structure of sperm and egg cells to their functions; to describe fertilisation and compare the gametes; to describe the roles of testosterone and oestrogen at puberty; to describe the menstrual cycle and the roles of FSH, LH, oestrogen and progesterone; to describe the early development of the zygote and implantation; to state the functions of the amniotic sac and fluid; to describe the placenta and umbilical cord; and to state that some viruses can cross the placenta.

The [Development of organisms and continuity of life topic guide](/resources/o-level-biology-5090-development-of-organisms-and-continuity-of-life/) covers the whole topic. This page goes into more depth, with worked examples on the menstrual cycle. ## The male reproductive system

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| Testes | Produce sperm (by meiosis) and the hormone testosterone |
| Scrotum | A bag of skin that holds the testes outside the body, where the temperature is slightly lower, which suits sperm production |
| Sperm ducts | Tubes that carry sperm from the testes to the urethra |
| Prostate gland | Adds fluid to the sperm, which gives the sperm nutrients and helps them to swim; sperm and fluid form semen |
| Urethra | Carries semen (and urine, at a different time) through the penis to the outside |
| Penis | Places semen inside the vagina during sexual intercourse |

## The female reproductive system

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| Ovaries | Produce eggs (ova) and the hormones oestrogen and progesterone |
| Oviducts | Tubes that carry the egg to the uterus; the place where fertilisation usually occurs; lined with cilia |
| Uterus | Muscular organ where the embryo implants and the fetus develops |
| Cervix | A ring of muscle at the opening of the uterus; keeps the fetus in place during pregnancy |
| Vagina | Receives the penis and semen; the birth canal |

## The gametes

### Sperm cell

A sperm cell is adapted to swim to the egg and enter it.

- A **flagellum** (tail) allows the sperm to swim.
- Many **mitochondria** in the middle section release energy by respiration for the movement of the tail.
- The head contains the **haploid nucleus** and an **acrosome** with **enzymes**. These enzymes digest the jelly coat and membrane of the egg so that the sperm nucleus can enter.

### Egg cell

An egg cell is adapted to be fertilised and to nourish the early embryo.

- It is large and contains **energy stores** (food reserves, including lipid) that the early embryo can use.
- It is surrounded by a **jelly coat**. After one sperm has entered, the jelly coat changes so that no other sperm can get in. This prevents more than one sperm from fertilising the egg.
- It has a haploid nucleus.

### Comparing male and female gametes

| Feature | Sperm | Egg |
|---|---|---|
| Size | Very small | Much larger |
| Structure | Head, middle piece with mitochondria, tail | Round cell with cytoplasm and jelly coat |
| Numbers | Millions produced continuously | One usually released each month |
| Motility | Can swim | Cannot move by itself; carried by cilia and muscle action |
| Food stores | Little | Contains food reserves |

## Fertilisation

**Fertilisation is the fusion of the nuclei from a male gamete (sperm) and a female gamete (egg cell).** It usually occurs in the oviduct. The sperm head enters the egg, the jelly coat changes, and the haploid nuclei fuse to form a diploid zygote.

## Puberty and secondary sexual characteristics

Puberty is the stage at which the body becomes able to reproduce. The hormones that cause this are **testosterone** (produced by the testes) and **oestrogen** (produced by the ovaries). They control the development and regulation of **secondary sexual characteristics**.

| Hormone | Secondary sexual characteristics |
|---|---|
| Testosterone | Facial and body hair, deepening of the voice, muscle growth, development of the penis and testes |
| Oestrogen | Breast development, widening of the hips, pubic hair, the start of the menstrual cycle |

## The menstrual cycle

The menstrual cycle is a series of changes lasting about 28 days. It involves the development and release of an egg and changes in the lining of the uterus.

| Day (approximate) | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 to 5 | **Menstruation**: the lining of the uterus breaks down and passes out of the vagina |
| 5 to 13 | The lining of the uterus is repaired and thickens; an egg develops in a follicle in the ovary |
| 14 | **Ovulation**: the egg is released from the ovary |
| 15 to 28 | The lining stays thick and full of blood vessels, ready for an embryo; if no embryo arrives, the lining breaks down at the end |

### Roles of the hormones

| Hormone | Made by | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FSH | Pituitary gland | Stimulates the development of an egg in a follicle in the ovary; stimulates the ovary to produce oestrogen |
| Oestrogen | Ovaries | Causes the repair and thickening of the lining of the uterus; stimulates the pituitary to release LH |
| LH | Pituitary gland | Stimulates the release of the egg (ovulation) |
| Progesterone | Ovaries (the follicle after ovulation) | Maintains the lining of the uterus; helps to maintain pregnancy |

If the egg is not fertilised, progesterone levels fall, and the lining breaks down, so menstruation begins. If pregnancy occurs, progesterone remains high and the lining is kept.

### Worked example: reading the cycle

*In a 28-day cycle the thickness of the uterus lining was 1 mm on day 5, 4 mm on day 13 and 8 mm on day 24.*

1. Increase from day 5 to day 13 = 4 - 1 = 3 mm. Percentage = 3 divided by 1 x 100 = 300 per cent.
2. Increase from day 5 to day 24 = 8 - 1 = 7 mm, which is an increase of 700 per cent.
3. The thickening before day 14 is caused by oestrogen; the lining is kept thick after ovulation by progesterone.

The day of ovulation (about day 14) is the same time the egg could be fertilised; fertilisation is most likely in the few days around it.

## Early development and implantation

After fertilisation, the zygote divides by mitosis as it moves down the oviduct. It forms a **ball of cells** called an **embryo**. After a few days it reaches the uterus and becomes **implanted** in the thickened lining. Implantation means the embryo sinks into the lining, where it is supplied with nutrients.

## Protection and nutrition of the fetus

### Amniotic sac and fluid

The embryo develops inside a bag called the **amniotic sac**, which contains **amniotic fluid**. The functions are:

- the fluid **protects** the fetus from knocks and mechanical shock, acting as a shock absorber;
- it supports the fetus and allows movement;
- it helps to keep the temperature constant.

### Placenta and umbilical cord

The **placenta** is a disc-shaped organ that grows into the lining of the uterus. The **umbilical cord** joins the fetus to the placenta. The cord contains blood vessels that carry blood to and from the placenta. Structural details are not required.

The placenta is where substances are exchanged between the blood of the mother and the blood of the fetus, without the two bloods mixing.

| Passes from mother to fetus | Passes from fetus to mother |
|---|---|
| Dissolved nutrients such as glucose and amino acids | Excretory products such as urea |
| Oxygen | Carbon dioxide |

### Viruses and the placenta

The placenta acts as a barrier to many harmful substances and microorganisms, but **some viruses can pass across the placenta** and affect the fetus. Examples include the viruses that cause rubella (German measles) and the Zika virus. For this reason, women who are pregnant are advised to take care to avoid infections.

## Common mistakes

- Saying that the prostate gland makes sperm. It adds fluid to semen.
- Mixing up the oviduct (where eggs travel and fertilisation occurs) with the uterus.
- Saying that the placenta mixes the blood of mother and fetus. The bloods stay separate.
- Mixing up FSH and LH: FSH helps the egg to develop and LH triggers ovulation.
- Saying that oestrogen is made by the pituitary. It is made by the ovaries.
- Saying that the egg is released on day 1. It is about day 14.
- Confusing fertilisation (fusion of nuclei) with implantation (embedding in the uterus).
- Saying that a sperm cell has no nucleus.

## Command words

- **Identify**: name the part on a diagram of the male or female reproductive system.
- **Explain**: link a structure of a gamete to its function.
- **Compare**: give differences between sperm and eggs.
- **Describe**: give the stages of the menstrual cycle in order.
