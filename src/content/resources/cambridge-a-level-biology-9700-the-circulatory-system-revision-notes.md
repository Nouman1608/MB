It is AS content for Paper 1 and Paper 2, with recognition and drawing skills used on Paper 3. For explanations and worked examples, use the [circulatory system study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-the-circulatory-system/); for exam-style questions, use the [circulatory system practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-the-circulatory-system-practice/).

## The system in one sentence

The mammalian circulatory system is a **closed double circulation** made of a heart, blood, and blood vessels: **arteries, arterioles, capillaries, venules and veins**.

- Closed: blood stays in vessels, so pressure stays high and flow can be directed.
- Double: blood goes through the heart twice per circuit, so the lungs get low pressure and the body high pressure.

## Four named vessels

| Vessel | From | To | Blood |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vena cava | Body | Right atrium | Deoxygenated |
| Pulmonary artery | Right ventricle | Lungs | Deoxygenated |
| Pulmonary vein | Lungs | Left atrium | Oxygenated |
| Aorta | Left ventricle | Body | Oxygenated |

Arteries leave the heart; veins return to it.

## Vessel structure and function

| Vessel | Key features | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Elastic artery (aorta) | Thick tunica media, many elastic fibres | Stretches in systole, recoils in diastole: smooths pressure, keeps flow going |
| Muscular artery | More smooth muscle in the tunica media | Contracts or relaxes to control flow to organs |
| Arteriole | Smooth muscle | Directs blood to particular capillary beds; largest pressure drop |
| Capillary | Endothelium only; gaps between cells; lumen about one red cell wide | Short diffusion distance; plasma leaks out to form tissue fluid; red cells close to wall |
| Venule | Collects from capillaries | Joins to form veins |
| Vein | Wide lumen, thin wall, little muscle, valves | Low resistance at low pressure; squeezed by skeletal muscle; valves stop backflow |

### Pressure and speed

- Pressure: highest in arteries, falls most in arterioles, lowest in veins.
- Pulsing: strong in arteries, gone by the capillaries.
- Speed: slowest in capillaries, because their total cross-sectional area is largest.

## Recognising vessels

- **Artery**: round, thick wall, small lumen, folded endothelium.
- **Vein**: flattened or irregular, thin wall, wide lumen; valves in LS.
- **Capillary**: one-cell-thick wall; red cells in single file.
- Plan diagrams: layer outlines only, correct proportions, no cells, no shading.

### Worked reminder: wall-to-lumen ratio

Image wall 3.0 mm, lumen 6.0 mm → ratio 0.5. Same answer at any magnification.

## Blood cells

| Cell | Nucleus | Size vs red blood cell | Other |
|---|---|---|---|
| Red blood cell | None | — | Biconcave; pale centre |
| Lymphocyte | Round, fills most of cell | Slightly larger | Thin rim of cytoplasm |
| Neutrophil | Lobed | About twice | Granular cytoplasm |
| Monocyte | Kidney-shaped | Largest | Plenty of cytoplasm |

## Water in transport

- Main component of plasma and tissue fluid.
- **Solvent action** (polar water): carries glucose, amino acids, ions, urea, hormones, hydrogencarbonate.
- **High specific heat capacity** (hydrogen bonds): blood temperature stays stable; heat carried from active organs.

## Tissue fluid

**Functions**: supply oxygen and nutrients; remove carbon dioxide and wastes; stable environment for cells.

**Formation in steps**

1. Arterial end: high hydrostatic pressure forces plasma out through gaps in the capillary wall.
2. Water, ions, glucose, amino acids and oxygen leave; red blood cells and most plasma proteins stay.
3. Plasma proteins give blood a lower water potential than tissue fluid.
4. Venous end: hydrostatic pressure is lower, so water returns by osmosis.
5. Excess fluid drains into lymph vessels.

**If plasma proteins fall** → blood water potential higher → less water returns → fluid builds up → swelling.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Closed circulation | Blood remains inside vessels |
| Double circulation | Blood passes through the heart twice per circuit |
| Pulmonary circulation | Heart to lungs and back |
| Systemic circulation | Heart to body and back |
| Tunica media | Middle layer of artery or vein wall: smooth muscle and elastic fibres |
| Endothelium | Single layer of flat cells lining all vessels |
| Arteriole | Small artery with smooth muscle that controls flow into capillaries |
| Venule | Small vessel collecting blood from capillaries |
| Tissue fluid | Fluid around cells, formed from plasma |
| Hydrostatic pressure | Pressure exerted by a liquid, here blood pressure |
| Lymph | Excess tissue fluid in lymph vessels |

## Exam traps

- "Arteries carry oxygenated blood": the pulmonary artery does not.
- "Veins have thick walls" or "arteries have valves".
- Plasma proteins or red cells listed as leaving the capillary.
- Specific heat capacity explained without "stable temperature".
- Tissue fluid answers that describe only the arterial end.
- Neutrophil and monocyte nuclei swapped.

## Self-test

1. Explain what is meant by a double circulation.
2. Name the vessel carrying blood from the left ventricle.
3. Which vessel carries oxygenated blood to the heart?
4. Explain the role of elastic fibres in the aorta.
5. How do arterioles control which organs receive most blood?
6. Give two features of a capillary and the advantage of each.
7. Why do veins need valves?
8. Describe the nucleus of a monocyte.
9. Which white cell has a nucleus that fills most of the cell?
10. Explain how water's high specific heat capacity helps transport.
11. Which component of plasma does not normally enter tissue fluid?
12. Explain why water returns to the capillary at the venous end.

### Answers

1. Blood passes through the heart twice in one complete circuit: once through the pulmonary circulation and once through the systemic circulation.
2. Aorta.
3. Pulmonary vein.
4. They stretch as blood is pumped in and recoil between beats, smoothing pressure and keeping blood moving.
5. Smooth muscle contracts to narrow arterioles to some organs and relaxes to widen others.
6. Any two: one-cell-thick wall, short diffusion distance; gaps between cells, tissue fluid forms; narrow lumen, red cells close to wall; many capillaries, large surface area.
7. Blood pressure in veins is low, so valves stop blood flowing backwards.
8. Large and kidney-shaped.
9. Lymphocyte.
10. Blood temperature stays stable, and blood can carry heat from active organs around the body.
11. Plasma proteins (also red blood cells).
12. Hydrostatic pressure is lower there and the plasma proteins give the blood a lower water potential, so water moves in by osmosis.
