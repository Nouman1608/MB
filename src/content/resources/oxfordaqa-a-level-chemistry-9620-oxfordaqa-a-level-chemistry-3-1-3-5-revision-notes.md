These notes condense **3.1.3.5 Shapes of simple molecules and ions** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, examined in Unit 1. The [shapes study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-3-5/) explains each idea with worked examples; the [shapes practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-3-5-practice/) test it.

## Core ideas

- Bonding pairs and lone pairs are **charge clouds that repel**.
- Pairs arrange themselves **as far apart as possible** to minimise repulsion.
- Repulsion order: **lone–lone > lone–bond > bond–bond**.
- Lone pairs therefore squeeze bonding pairs together and **reduce bond angles**.
- The **shape** is named from the positions of the **atoms**, not the lone pairs.
- You need species with **up to six** electron pairs around the central atom.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bonding pair | A shared pair of electrons in a covalent bond to the central atom |
| Lone pair | A pair of outer electrons on the central atom not used in bonding |
| Electron-pair repulsion | The principle that electron pairs repel and take up positions as far apart as possible |
| Bond angle | The angle between two bonds meeting at the central atom |
| Equatorial position | One of the three positions in the triangle of a trigonal bipyramid (120° apart) |
| Axial position | One of the two positions above and below that triangle (90° to it) |

## Counting electron pairs (single bonds)

```
pairs = (outer electrons of central atom
         + 1 per bonded atom
         + 1 per negative charge  − 1 per positive charge) ÷ 2
bonding pairs = number of bonded atoms;  lone pairs = pairs − bonding pairs
```

Examples: H₃O⁺ (6 + 3 − 1) ÷ 2 = 4; NH₄⁺ (5 + 4 − 1) ÷ 2 = 4; ClF₄⁻ (7 + 4 + 1) ÷ 2 = 6; IF₄⁺ (7 + 4 − 1) ÷ 2 = 5.

## Lone-pair placement rules

- **Four pairs**: lone pairs sit at corners of the tetrahedron; each one closes the angle by about 2.5°.
- **Five pairs**: lone pairs go **equatorial** (more space, fewer 90° neighbours).
- **Six pairs**: the first lone pair can go anywhere; a second goes **opposite** the first (180° apart).

## Writing an explanation (four-step frame)

1. Number of bonding pairs and lone pairs (show the count for an ion).
2. Pairs repel and get as far apart as possible.
3. Lone pairs repel more than bonding pairs (where present).
4. Name the shape and give the angle.

## Exam traps

- Ion charge ignored, or added when it should be subtracted.
- Water called "tetrahedral" or "linear": it is **bent**, 104.5°.
- 109.5° quoted for ammonia: it is **107°**.
- Lone pairs placed axially in SF₄ or ClF₃.
- XeF₂ called "bent" because it has lone pairs: three equatorial lone pairs make it **linear**.
- XeF₄ called "tetrahedral": six pairs with two opposite lone pairs make it **square planar**.
- Drawings with no wedges or dashed bonds for a three-dimensional shape, or with no angle marked.

## Self-test

1. State the repulsion order for the three types of electron-pair repulsion.
2. How many electron pairs surround the central atom in AlCl₄⁻?
3. Name the shape of H₃O⁺ and give its bond angle.
4. Name the shape of BCl₃ and give its bond angle.
5. How many lone pairs does the central atom in ClF₃ have?
6. Name the shape of SF₄.
7. Why do lone pairs go into equatorial positions in a trigonal bipyramid?
8. Name the shape of I₃⁻ and give its bond angle.
9. Name the shape of SiF₆²⁻.
10. Name the shape of BrF₅ and say how its bond angles compare with 90°.
11. Explain why ClF₄⁻ is square planar rather than see-saw.
12. Why is the bond angle in NH₂⁻ smaller than in NH₃?

### Answers

1. Lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bond pair > bond pair–bond pair.
2. (3 + 4 + 1) ÷ 2 = **4** (all bonding).
3. **Pyramidal, 107°**.
4. **Trigonal planar, 120°**.
5. (7 + 3) ÷ 2 = 5 pairs, 3 bonding, so **2** lone pairs.
6. **See-saw** (4 bonding, 1 lone pair).
7. Equatorial positions have more space and fewer pairs at 90°, so repulsion is minimised.
8. **Linear, 180°** (2 bonding, 3 lone pairs, all lone pairs equatorial).
9. **Octahedral** (4 + 6 + 2 = 12 electrons, 6 bonding pairs).
10. **Square pyramidal**; the angles are slightly **less than 90°** because the lone pair repels the bonding pairs more strongly.
11. (7 + 4 + 1) ÷ 2 = 6 pairs, so the arrangement is octahedral, not trigonal bipyramidal; the two lone pairs sit opposite each other, leaving four atoms in a square.
12. NH₂⁻ has two lone pairs and NH₃ has one; more lone-pair repulsion pushes the bonding pairs closer (104.5° compared with 107°).
