**This guide covers content area 3.1.2.7 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), Jihad: "The different understandings of Jihad fee sabilillah" and "The meaning and importance of the 4 types of Jihad: with one's heart (Bil-qalb); with one's tongue (Bil-lisan), with one's hand (Bil-yad); and with a sword (Bil-sayf). The rules for conducting armed warfare (Kitaal)."** The word "Jihad" is often misunderstood. This page sets out what it means in Islam, how each of the four types works in daily life, and the strict rules that govern armed conflict.

## What Jihad means

**Jihad** comes from the Arabic root *j-h-d*, meaning to *strive*, *struggle* or *exert effort*. It does not mean "holy war". The phrase **fee sabilillah** means "in the path of Allah". So Jihad fee sabilillah is effort made for the sake of Allah: to be a better Muslim, to spread good and to prevent harm.

Armed fighting has its own Arabic word, **Kitaal**. This matters in an exam answer: Jihad is the wide idea of struggle, and Kitaal is only one narrow part of it.

## Different understandings of Jihad

Muslims have always understood Jihad in more than one way.

- **The struggle within.** Many Muslims place first the effort to control one's own desires, anger, pride and laziness, and to live by Islam. A saying often quoted, in which the Prophet (pbuh) returned from battle and said they were returning from the lesser Jihad to the greater Jihad, is described by some scholars as weak in its chain of narration. Even so, the idea that the struggle against the self is the hardest is widely taught, and the Qur'an commands Muslims to "strive for Allah with the striving due to Him" (22:78).
- **The struggle in society.** Working for justice, education, honesty and care for others, and speaking against wrongdoing.
- **The defence of the community.** Armed struggle, permitted only under strict conditions to protect life, faith and freedom.

Today some groups use the word to justify violence against civilians. Muslim scholars and the great majority of Muslims reject this as a distortion of Islam, because it breaks the rules explained below.

## The four types of Jihad

The specification names four types. They are often linked to a saying of the Prophet (pbuh): "Whoever among you sees an evil, let him change it with his hand; if he cannot, then with his tongue; and if he cannot, then with his heart, and that is the weakest of faith." This is the same idea as *Nahi annil munkar*, forbidding evil.

| Type | Arabic | What it involves | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heart | Bil-qalb | Inner struggle against temptation, bad intentions, envy and pride; keeping faith strong | Resisting the urge to lie even when it would help |
| Tongue | Bil-lisan | Speaking truth, teaching, advising, calling to Islam and speaking out against injustice | Explaining Islam politely to a friend; correcting a false rumour |
| Hand | Bil-yad | Good actions and effort: working to support a family, charity, helping others, stopping harm by action | Volunteering at a food bank; earning an honest living |
| Sword | Bil-sayf | Armed struggle in defence, within the rules of Kitaal | A just defence of a community that is under attack |

### Jihad with the heart (Bil-qalb)

This is the base for the others. Without sincerity (*ikhlas*) and a pure intention, other efforts lose their value. The Qur'an describes the self (*nafs*) that commands evil (12:53), and Muslims strive to train it through prayer, fasting, remembrance and repentance. An example is a student who feels angry at an unfair grade and chooses patience and forgiveness.

### Jihad with the tongue (Bil-lisan)

The Prophet (pbuh) said that "the best Jihad is a word of truth spoken before an unjust ruler." Speaking truth means being calm and polite, not rude. The Qur'an tells believers to "invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good instruction" (16:125). In daily life it includes teaching Islam, advising friends, refusing to join in backbiting, and speaking up for someone who is treated unfairly.

### Jihad with the hand (Bil-yad)

This means striving through action and effort. Earning an honest living to support one's family is regarded as striving in the path of Allah. So are caring for elderly parents, giving charity, helping neighbours, cleaning a community area or working for a good cause. It also covers the physical effort to stop wrongdoing, so long as it is done lawfully and wisely and does not cause greater harm.

### Jihad with the sword (Bil-sayf)

This is armed fighting, the last resort. It is the only type that involves force, and it is the one with the most rules.

## The rules for armed warfare (Kitaal)

The prescribed passage is Qur'an 2:190: "Fight in the way of Allah those who fight against you but do not transgress. Indeed, Allah does not like transgressors." It contains the main principles in one verse.

1. **Self-defence and just cause.** Fighting is allowed against "those who fight against you". Permission was first given to those who had been attacked and driven from their homes (22:39). It is never for conquest, greed or to force religion on others. The Qur'an says, "There shall be no compulsion in [acceptance of] the religion" (2:256).
2. **Proper authority.** A lawful leader or state decides to fight. An individual, a gang or a private group has no right to declare it.
3. **"Do not transgress".** Fighters must not go beyond what is necessary. They may use no more force than the need requires.
4. **Protection of non-combatants.** Women, children, the elderly, the sick, monks and others who do not fight must not be harmed. Places of worship are protected.
5. **Protection of the environment.** The first caliph, Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA), told his commanders not to kill women, children or the old, not to cut down fruit trees or destroy buildings, and not to kill livestock except for food.
6. **Peace when it is offered.** "If they incline to peace, then incline to it [also]" (8:61). Fighting must stop when the enemy stops.
7. **Fair treatment of prisoners.** Captives must be fed and treated humanely (76:8).
8. **Protection of all life.** The Qur'an teaches that whoever kills one innocent person "it is as if he had slain mankind entirely" (5:32).

Violent attacks on civilians, kidnapping, and attacks carried out by groups who claim no authority break these rules. They are not Jihad in Islam.

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** ways in which Jihad can be carried out other than by fighting. Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority in your answer. **[6]**

> First, Jihad with the tongue, which means speaking the truth and calling others to Islam in a wise and gentle way [1]. For example, a Muslim might advise a friend who is being unfair to others, or speak up for someone who is being bullied [1]. Second, Jihad with the heart, which is the inner struggle against temptation, envy and pride [1]. A Muslim who keeps calm and forgives when they are wronged is striving against their own anger [1]. The Qur'an tells believers to "invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good instruction" (16:125), which shows that Jihad by words must be gentle and wise [2].

The answer names each type, explains it with a concrete example, and finishes with a quotation that is explained, not just copied.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing that Jihad means holy war.** It means struggle. Armed struggle is called Kitaal and is one part.
- **Listing the four types without explaining them.** Add what each involves and an example.
- **Forgetting the rules.** The specification asks for the rules of armed warfare, so always mention defence, authority, protection of non-combatants and peace.
- **Saying anyone can declare Jihad.** The decision rests with lawful authority.
- **Giving the quotation but no application.** AO2 marks come from applying it.

## Exam technique

For **give** or **name** questions, state one term such as Bil-lisan. For **explain** questions, define the type, give an example and say why it matters. For the 12-mark question on Qur'an 2:190, use "Fight in the way of Allah" for the purpose, "those who fight against you" for defence, and "do not transgress" for limits. Examine both sides: the verse allows armed defence, but it surrounds it with firm limits, and the wider meaning of Jihad is the peaceful struggle of daily life.
