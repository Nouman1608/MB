## The specification points

1. Explain what is meant by 'place'.
2. Evaluate distribution strategies: channels of distribution.
3. Evaluate distribution strategies: online, digital and physical distribution (logistics).
4. Evaluate distribution strategies: patterns of distribution.
5. Evaluate the impact and importance of place to a business and its stakeholders.
6. Recommend and justify a 'place' mix as part of a marketing strategy.

## Core recall

### What place is

How a product reaches the customer: where it is sold, who handles it and how it is moved and stored. If customers cannot find or receive a product, it does not sell.

### Channels

| Channel | Route | Producer's position |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | Producer to consumer | Keeps the whole margin and the customer data; does selling and delivery itself |
| Retailer | Producer, retailer, consumer | Reach and display; shares margin |
| Wholesaler | Producer, wholesaler, retailer, consumer | Bulk sales to many small shops; smallest margin |
| Agent | Agent sells for commission | Common in export markets |

Longer channel: more reach, less margin and control. Shorter channel: more margin and control, more work and capital. Multi-channel selling combines reach and margin but risks channel conflict.

### Logistics

| Type | Features | Issues |
|---|---|---|
| Physical | Road, rail, sea, air to warehouses and shops | Cost rises with distance and speed; retailers need reliability |
| Online | Pick, pack and deliver small orders | High cost per item; customers expect speed and free returns |
| Digital | Download or streaming | Almost no delivery cost; piracy; platform fees |
| Click and collect, omnichannel | Order online, collect in store | Uses stores as collection points |

Evaluate by cost, speed, reliability and customer expectation. Good logistics: fewer stock-outs, lower costs, better service. Poor logistics: late or damaged goods, lost sales, bad reviews.

### Patterns of distribution

| Pattern | Outlets | Suits |
|---|---|---|
| Intensive | As many as possible | Convenience goods |
| Selective | Chosen, limited number | Goods customers compare |
| Exclusive | One or few per area | Luxury or specialist goods |

More outlets means more sales but less control of image and price. Fewer outlets means better control and service but less reach.

### Importance of place

- Availability and convenience drive sales.
- Distribution is a large share of the price of many goods.
- Place signals image.
- Stakeholders: customers (convenience), retailers (stock), employees in logistics (volumes), suppliers (deadlines), communities (traffic, pollution).

### Choosing a place mix

1. Start with the target customer and expectations.
2. Match the pattern to image and price.
3. Compare channels by contribution and control.
4. Check logistics capacity and cost.
5. State the risks: channel conflict, dependence on one retailer, supply failure.

## Quick worked reminder

Hollis Housewares (invented): pan costs £12; consumer price £30. Direct: £30 − £12 − £6 delivery = £12 per pan; 5,000 pans give £60,000, less £25,000 website costs = £35,000. Retailer: sells at £18, so £6 per pan; 20,000 pans give £120,000. Wholesaler: sells at £15, so £3 per pan; 50,000 pans give £150,000.

Patterns (invented chocolate brand): intensive 8,000 × £1,800 = £14.4m retail sales; producer receives 75% = £10.8m. Selective 900 × £9,000 = £8.1m; 70% = £5.67m. Exclusive 60 × £60,000 = £3.6m; 60% = £2.16m.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Channel of distribution | The route a product takes from producer to consumer |
| Wholesaler | A business that buys in bulk and sells smaller quantities to retailers |
| Logistics | Planning and control of the flow of goods to the customer |
| Omnichannel | Selling through linked online and physical channels |
| Intensive distribution | Selling through as many outlets as possible |
| Selective distribution | Selling through a limited number of chosen outlets |
| Exclusive distribution | Selling through one or very few outlets |
| Channel conflict | Disagreement when one channel competes with another |

## Exam traps

- Place includes online and digital distribution.
- Shorter channels earn more per unit, not necessarily more in total.
- Link the pattern of distribution to the product's image.
- Count delivery and returns costs for online sales.
- Compare options before recommending.

## Evaluation prompts

- Does the channel give enough reach, or only a high margin on a few sales?
- What does the producer keep per unit, and what is the total contribution?
- Does the pattern of distribution fit the brand image and the price?
- Can the logistics deliver on time and at a cost the price can bear?
- Would using more than one channel cause conflict, for example a website undercutting shops?
- Finish with a judgement for the product and business in the case.

## Self-test

1. Define place.
2. Name three channels of distribution.
3. Give one advantage of selling direct.
4. Give one advantage of using a wholesaler.
5. Cost £8; direct price £20; delivery £3. Find the contribution per unit.
6. Sells to a retailer at £13; cost £8. Find the contribution per unit.
7. Give one example of digital distribution.
8. Which pattern suits a luxury watch?
9. Give one disadvantage of intensive distribution for a premium brand.
10. What is click and collect?
11. Give one effect of poor logistics.
12. Why might a producer use more than one channel?

### Answers

1. How a product reaches the customer: where it is sold and how it is delivered.
2. Any three: direct; via retailer; via wholesaler; via agent.
3. Any one: keeps the whole margin; owns the customer data; controls the brand.
4. Any one: buys in bulk; reaches many small retailers; handles storage and delivery.
5. 20 − 8 − 3 = **£9**.
6. 13 − 8 = **£5**.
7. Any one: software download; streaming music; e-books.
8. Exclusive distribution.
9. It puts the product next to cheaper brands, which can weaken the premium image.
10. Customers order online and collect the goods from a store.
11. Any one: late or damaged goods; stock-outs; lost sales; poor reviews.
12. To combine the reach of retailers with the higher margin of direct sales.
