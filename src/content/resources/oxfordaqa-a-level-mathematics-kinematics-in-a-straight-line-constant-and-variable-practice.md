## Questions

**1.** A runner goes 120 m east along a straight track, then 50 m west, taking 40 s in total. Find the distance travelled, the displacement, the average speed and the average velocity. **[4]**

**2.** A car's speed increases uniformly from 6 m/s to 21 m/s in 5 s. Find its acceleration and the distance it travels in this time. **[4]**

**3.** A motorbike slows uniformly from 30 m/s to 12 m/s while travelling 126 m. Find its acceleration and the time taken. **[4]**

**4.** A stone is dropped from rest into a well and hits the water after 2.5 s. Find the depth of the water below the release point and the speed of the stone as it hits the water. **[4]**

**5.** A ball is thrown vertically upwards at 12 m/s from a point 1.5 m above the ground. Find **(a)** the greatest height above the ground, **[3]** **(b)** the time before the ball hits the ground. **[3]**

**6.** A boat starts from rest and accelerates uniformly to 9 m/s in 6 s. It then travels at 9 m/s for T seconds before slowing uniformly to rest in 3 s. The total distance is 180 m. **(a)** Sketch the velocity–time graph and find T. **[4]** **(b)** Find its average speed for the whole journey. **[2]**

**7.** Particle A moves in a straight line at a constant 6 m/s, passing a point O at time zero. Particle B starts from rest at O, 6 s later, and moves along the same line in the same direction with constant acceleration 1.5 m/s². Find how long after B starts it catches A, and the distance from O at which this happens. **[6]**

**8.** A particle moves on a straight line so that its displacement from O at time t seconds is s = t³ − 12t² + 36t metres, for t ≥ 0. **(a)** Find expressions for its velocity and acceleration. **[2]** **(b)** Find the times at which it is instantaneously at rest and its acceleration at each. **[3]** **(c)** Find the total distance it travels in the first 7 s. **[3]**

**9.** A particle moves on a straight line with acceleration a = 12 − 6t m/s². When t = 0 it is at O with velocity 4 m/s. **(a)** Find expressions for its velocity and its displacement from O. **[3]** **(b)** Find its velocity and displacement when t = 3. **[2]** **(c)** Find the greatest velocity and the time at which it occurs. **[2]**

**10.** A particle moves on a straight line with velocity v = 6√t − 2t m/s, for t ≥ 0, and is at O when t = 0. **(a)** Find the acceleration in terms of t and the time at which it is zero. **[3]** **(b)** Show that the particle is at rest again at t = 9 and find its displacement from O then. **[3]**

**11.** A train travels between two stations in 60 s. It accelerates uniformly from rest at 1.5 m/s² to a speed of 12 m/s, travels at 12 m/s, then decelerates uniformly at 2 m/s² to rest. **(a)** Find the time spent at constant speed. **[3]** **(b)** Find the distance between the stations. **[3]** **(c)** Find the average speed. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Distance = 120 + 50 = 170 m [1]. Displacement = 120 − 50 = **70 m east** [1]. Average speed = 170 ÷ 40 = **4.25 m/s** [1]. Average velocity = 70 ÷ 40 = **1.75 m/s east** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Stating the direction of the displacement and velocity is part of a complete answer, and quoting 170 m as the displacement shows the distance and displacement have been confused.

**2.** a = (21 − 6) ÷ 5 [1] = **3 m/s²** [1]. s = ½(6 + 21) × 5 [1] = **67.5 m** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Several equations give the distance; choose one that uses the given values rather than your calculated a, so the two parts are checked independently.

**3.** v² = u² + 2as: 144 = 900 + 252a [1], a = **−3 m/s²** (deceleration 3 m/s²) [1]. v = u + at: 12 = 30 − 3t [1], t = **6 s** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A bare "3" with no sign or the word deceleration leaves the meaning unclear, so say which you mean.

**4.** Down positive: u = 0, a = 9.8, t = 2.5. s = ½ × 9.8 × 2.5² [1] = **30.6 m** (30.625) [1]. v = 9.8 × 2.5 [1] = **24.5 m/s** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the substitution into the equation, because an answer with no working is difficult to credit if the number is wrong.

**5. (a)** Up positive: u = 12, a = −9.8, v = 0 at the top. 0 = 144 − 19.6s [1], s = 7.35 m above the launch point [1]. Height above ground = 7.35 + 1.5 = **8.85 m** [1].
**(b)** At the ground s = −1.5: −1.5 = 12t − 4.9t² [1], so 4.9t² − 12t − 1.5 = 0 and t = (12 + √(144 + 29.4)) ÷ 9.8 [1] = **2.57 s** (the negative root is rejected) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Remember to add the 1.5 m in part (a), and in part (b) state clearly that the negative root is rejected because time cannot be negative.

**6. (a)** Graph: a straight line from (0, 0) to (6, 9), horizontal to (6 + T, 9), then a straight line down to (9 + T, 0) [1]. Area: ½ × 6 × 9 + 9T + ½ × 3 × 9 [1] = 27 + 9T + 13.5 = 180 [1], so **T = 15.5** [1].
**(b)** Total time = 6 + 15.5 + 3 = 24.5 s [1]. Average speed = 180 ÷ 24.5 = **7.35 m/s** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Label the axes and the key values on the sketch, and remember the total time includes all three stages, not just the constant-speed stage.

**7.** Let B have moved for u seconds, so A has moved for u + 6 seconds. A: distance = 6(u + 6) [1]. B: distance = ½ × 1.5 × u² = 0.75u² [1]. Equate: 0.75u² = 6u + 36 [1], u² − 8u − 48 = 0 [1], (u − 12)(u + 4) = 0, so **u = 12 s** (reject −4) [1]. Distance = 0.75 × 144 = **108 m** (check: 6 × 18 = 108) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Define your time variable clearly for each particle; using the same t for both, with the 6 s delay left out, gives a different and incorrect equation.

**8. (a)** v = ds/dt = **3t² − 24t + 36** [1]; a = dv/dt = **6t − 24** [1].
**(b)** v = 0: 3(t² − 8t + 12) = 0 [1], 3(t − 2)(t − 6) = 0, so **t = 2 s and t = 6 s** [1]. a(2) = **−12 m/s²**, a(6) = **12 m/s²** [1].
**(c)** s(0) = 0, s(2) = 32, s(6) = 0, s(7) = 7 [1]. Legs: 32, then 32, then 7 [1]. Distance = 32 + 32 + 7 = **71 m** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Finding s(7) − s(0) = 7 gives the displacement only; the distance needs each leg between the times at rest, summed as positive values.

**9. (a)** v = ∫(12 − 6t) dt = 12t − 3t² + c, and v = 4 at t = 0 gives c = 4, so **v = 4 + 12t − 3t²** [1]. s = ∫v dt = 4t + 6t² − t³ + k [1], and s = 0 at t = 0 gives **s = 4t + 6t² − t³** [1].
**(b)** v(3) = 4 + 36 − 27 = **13 m/s** [1]; s(3) = 12 + 54 − 27 = **39 m** [1].
**(c)** v is greatest when a = 0 [1]: 12 − 6t = 0 gives t = 2, and v(2) = 4 + 24 − 12 = **16 m/s** [1]. (Since da/dt = −6 < 0, this is a maximum.) **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Each integration needs its own constant, found from its own condition, and the maximum should be justified rather than just assumed.

**10. (a)** v = 6t^(1/2) − 2t, so a = dv/dt = 3t^(−1/2) − 2 = **3/√t − 2** [1]. a = 0: 3/√t = 2 [1], √t = 1.5, so **t = 2.25 s** [1].
**(b)** v = 0: 6√t = 2t, so t = 0 or √t = 3 [1], giving t = 9 (at rest again). s = ∫v dt = 4t^(3/2) − t² [1] (the constant is zero as s = 0 at t = 0). s(9) = 4 × 27 − 81 = **27 m** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" question, state the equation you solve and the value it gives, rather than only substituting t = 9 without a conclusion; writing the powers as indices keeps the calculus accurate.

**11. (a)** Speed-up time = 12 ÷ 1.5 = 8 s [1]; slow-down time = 12 ÷ 2 = 6 s [1]; constant-speed time = 60 − 8 − 6 = **46 s** [1].
**(b)** Distance = ½ × 12 × 8 + 12 × 46 + ½ × 12 × 6 [1] = 48 + 552 + 36 [1] = **636 m** [1].
**(c)** Average speed = 636 ÷ 60 [1] = **10.6 m/s** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A velocity–time sketch with the three times marked makes the area calculation a quick sum and helps you spot an omitted stage.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not choosing and stating a positive direction, so signs of a, v and s clash.
- Using a = +9.8 for upward motion when up is positive.
- Quoting a distance when the displacement is required, or the reverse.
- Not splitting the motion at the times when v = 0 for a distance question.
- Leaving out the constant of integration, or not using the condition given.
- Using the constant acceleration equations when a is a function of t.
- Rounding intermediate values and then losing accuracy in the final answer.
- Giving a negative or zero root of a quadratic in time as a valid answer.
- Writing no units or the wrong units, such as m for a velocity.
