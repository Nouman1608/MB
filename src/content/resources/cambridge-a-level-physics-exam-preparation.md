Cambridge A Level Physics (9702, examination series 2025-2027) has five papers, shown here as percentages of the full A Level: Paper 1 Multiple Choice (1 hour 15 minutes, 40 marks, 15.5%), Paper 2 AS Level Structured Questions (1 hour 15 minutes, 60 marks, 23%), Paper 3 Advanced Practical Skills (2 hours, 40 marks, 11.5%), Paper 4 A Level Structured Questions (2 hours, 100 marks, 38.5%) and Paper 5 Planning, Analysis and Evaluation (1 hour 15 minutes, 30 marks, 11.5%). Together the weightings reach 100%. To build the content, pair this page with topic pages such as [Capacitance](/resources/a-physics-capacitance/), [Electric Fields](/resources/a-physics-electric-fields/) and [Astronomy and Cosmology](/resources/a-physics-astronomy-and-cosmology/).

## AS Level and A Level routes

Papers 1 to 3 form the AS Level. On the standalone AS route they are weighted 31%, 46% and 23% of the AS Level, instead of the A Level figures above. A candidate can certificate the AS Level on its own, or continue to Papers 4 and 5 for the full A Level. This page lists A Level weightings, so if you are sitting only the AS Level, work from the AS figures when deciding where your effort pays most.

## Paper 1: Multiple Choice (1 hour 15 minutes, 40 marks, 15.5%)

Multiple choice rewards accuracy under time pressure: 75 minutes for 40 marks gives 75 / 40 = 1.875 minutes per mark. The record does not state the question count, so check the syllabus. Practise eliminating wrong options, estimating orders of magnitude, and checking units before choosing an answer.

## Paper 2: AS Level Structured Questions (1 hour 15 minutes, 60 marks, 23%)

Paper 2 asks for written and calculated answers on the AS content. At 75 minutes for 60 marks, you have 75 / 60 = 1.25 minutes per mark, so the pace is brisk. Write concise definitions, state formulae before substituting, and carry units through calculations.

## Paper 3: Advanced Practical Skills (2 hours, 40 marks, 11.5%)

Paper 3 is the practical paper and is a laboratory assessment, not a written revision paper. At 120 minutes for 40 marks you have 120 / 40 = 3 minutes per mark, the most generous rate in the course, because you handle apparatus, take readings and record results. Practise tabulating measurements with consistent significant figures, plotting graphs and estimating uncertainties. Confirm the exact skills assessed in the syllabus.

## Paper 4: A Level Structured Questions (2 hours, 100 marks, 38.5%)

Paper 4 is the heaviest paper in the course. At 120 minutes for 100 marks you have 120 / 100 = 1.2 minutes per mark. It extends the AS content to topics such as fields, capacitance, alternating currents, quantum and nuclear physics, and cosmology. Build breadth by working through topic notes, for example [Gravitational Fields](/resources/a-physics-gravitational-fields-revision-notes/) and [Alternating Currents](/resources/a-physics-alternating-currents-revision-notes/), then practise long, multi-step questions.

## Paper 5: Planning, Analysis and Evaluation (1 hour 15 minutes, 30 marks, 11.5%)

Paper 5 is a written paper on experimental design and data handling. At 75 minutes for 30 marks you have 75 / 30 = 2.5 minutes per mark. Practise identifying the independent, dependent and control variables, describing a method clear enough for someone else to follow, rearranging an equation into the form y = mx + c, and judging how uncertainty limits a conclusion.

## Timing and marks per minute

- Paper 1: 75 / 40 = 1.875 minutes per mark.
- Paper 2: 75 / 60 = 1.25 minutes per mark.
- Paper 3: 120 / 40 = 3 minutes per mark.
- Paper 4: 120 / 100 = 1.2 minutes per mark.
- Paper 5: 75 / 30 = 2.5 minutes per mark.
- Whole course: 75 + 75 + 120 + 120 + 75 = 465 minutes for 40 + 60 + 40 + 100 + 30 = 270 marks.

Paper 4 is where pacing is tightest. A six-mark part deserves only about seven minutes including any calculation.

## Command words and question styles

In physics, "define" wants a precise statement of a quantity, usually with symbols explained. "State" wants a brief fact. "Describe" wants features or a sequence. "Explain" wants a cause and its effect in physical terms. "Calculate" and "determine" want working and a unit. "Show that" requires an unrounded value to more figures than the stated result. "Suggest" invites you to apply principles to an unfamiliar situation. Confirm the exact definitions in the current syllabus.

## Worked practice scenario

Original question: "A 470 microfarad capacitor discharges through a 22 kilohm resistor. Calculate the time constant, and the time for the potential difference to fall to 25% of its initial value."

```
Time constant: tau = R x C
  = 22 x 10^3 ohm x 470 x 10^-6 F
  = 10.34 s   (about 10 s)

Discharge: V = V0 x e^(-t / tau)
Fall to 25%: 0.25 = e^(-t / tau)
  ln(0.25) = -t / tau
  t = tau x ln(4) = 10.34 x 1.386 = 14.33 s
```

The time is about 14 s, which is about 1.4 time constants. Check with sense: after one half-life-style interval of tau x ln 2 = 7.17 s the potential difference is 50%, and after another 7.17 s it is 25%, giving 14.33 s in total. Giving the unit (seconds) at every stage and showing the natural logarithm step earns the method marks.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm whether you are sitting the AS Level (Papers 1 to 3) or the full A Level.
- Practise multiple choice under timed conditions with a strict per-question limit.
- Rehearse a practical session: tables, graphs, uncertainties and conclusions.
- Learn the planning structure for Paper 5 so that variables and method are never missed.
- Write formulae, substitutions and units on every calculation.
- Read each "show that" question and finish with the value to more figures than required.
- Use any spare time to check significant figures and signs.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge A Level Physics 9702?**
There are five: Paper 1 Multiple Choice, Paper 2 AS Level Structured Questions, Paper 3 Advanced Practical Skills, Paper 4 A Level Structured Questions and Paper 5 Planning, Analysis and Evaluation.

**Which paper has the highest weighting?**
Paper 4, A Level Structured Questions, with 100 marks in 2 hours and 38.5% of the A Level.

**Which papers are practical?**
Paper 3 Advanced Practical Skills is the practical paper, at 2 hours, 40 marks and 11.5%. Paper 5 is a written paper on planning, analysis and evaluation.

**Can I take the AS Level alone?**
Yes. Papers 1 to 3 can be certificated on their own, weighted 31%, 46% and 23% of the AS Level, or a candidate can continue to Papers 4 and 5 for the full A Level.

**Do the weightings add up to 100%?**
Yes: 15.5 + 23 + 11.5 + 38.5 + 11.5 = 100.
