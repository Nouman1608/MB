These questions cover section 5.4 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: "Measures for combined sets of data", including calculation of the mean and standard deviation. Revise with the [combined sets study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-measures-for-combined-sets-of-data/) or the [combined sets revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-measures-for-combined-sets-of-data-revision-notes/). Standard deviations use the divide-by-n form. Give answers to 3 significant figures where they are not exact. All data are invented.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** In a club, 18 men have a mean mass of 74 kg and 12 women have a mean mass of 61 kg. Find the mean mass of all 30 members. **[2]**

**2.** A set of 16 values has mean 12.5 and standard deviation 1.5. Find Σx and Σx². **[2]**

**3.** Group 1 has 20 values with mean 35. Group 2 has k values with mean 45. The combined mean is 41. Find k. **[3]**

**4.** Eleven values have mean 30 and standard deviation 6. A twelfth value, 18, is added.

**(a)** Find the new mean. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the new standard deviation. **[3]**

**5.** Set A has smallest value 12 and largest value 40. Set B has smallest value 18 and largest value 47.

**(a)** Find the range of the combined set. **[1]**

**(b)** Set A has median 25 and Set B has median 31. Explain why the median of the combined set cannot be found from this information. **[1]**

**6.** Two sets of data are summarised: Set 1 has n = 15, Σx = 240, Σx² = 4080; Set 2 has n = 10, Σx = 190, Σx² = 3730. Find the mean and standard deviation of the combined set. **[3]**

## Section B: longer questions

**7.** A bus company records journey times, in minutes, on one route.

| Journeys | Number | Mean | Standard deviation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning | 40 | 24.5 | 3.2 |
| Afternoon | 60 | 27.0 | 4.1 |

**(a)** Find the mean of all 100 journey times. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the standard deviation of all 100 journey times. **[4]**

**(c)** A clerk estimates the combined standard deviation as 0.4 × 3.2 + 0.6 × 4.1 = 3.74. Explain why the true value is larger. **[1]**

**8.** Group X has 15 values with mean 12 and standard deviation 2. Group Y has 25 values with mean 16. The standard deviation of all 40 values is 3.

**(a)** Find the combined mean. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the standard deviation of Group Y. **[4]**

**9.** Twenty measurements have mean 15 and standard deviation 3. It is found that one measurement was recorded as 21 but should have been 11.

**(a)** Find the correct mean. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the correct standard deviation. **[3]**

**10.** Three teams have 12, 18 and 30 members, with mean scores 40, 45 and 52.

**(a)** Find the mean score of all the members. **[2]**

**(b)** A pupil says the overall mean is (40 + 45 + 52) ÷ 3. Explain why this is wrong. **[1]**

**11.** A group of 50 values has mean 30 and standard deviation 6. A subgroup of 20 of these values has mean 25 and standard deviation 4. Find the mean and standard deviation of the other 30 values. **[5]**

**12.** Three groups each have 5 pupils. Group A's marks are 3, 4, 5, 9, 9 (median 5). Group B's marks are 5, 6, 7, 8, 8 (median 7). Group C's marks are 1, 2, 7, 8, 8 (median 7).

**(a)** Find the median of Groups A and B combined. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the median of Groups A and C combined. **[1]**

**(c)** Use your answers to explain why medians of groups cannot be combined like means. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Total mass = 18 × 74 + 12 × 61 = 1332 + 732 = 2064 [1]; mean = 2064/30 = 68.8 kg [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Σx = 16 × 12.5 = 200 [1]; Σx² = 16 × (1.5² + 12.5²) = 16 × 158.5 = 2536 [1]. **[2]**

**3.** 20 × 35 + 45k = 41(20 + k) [1]; 700 + 45k = 820 + 41k [1]; 4k = 120, so k = 30 [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** Σx = 330 + 18 = 348; mean = 348/12 = 29 [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Σx² = 11 × (36 + 900) = 10 296 [1]; new Σx² = 10 296 + 324 = 10 620 [1]; variance = 10 620/12 − 29² = 885 − 841 = 44, so σ = 6.63 [1]. **[3]**

**5. (a)** 47 − 12 = 35 [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** The medians do not show how the other values in each set are spread, so the middle value of the merged data cannot be located [1]. **[1]**

**6.** n = 25, Σx = 430, Σx² = 7810 [1]; mean = 17.2 [1]; variance = 312.4 − 295.84 = 16.56, σ = 4.07 [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Σx = 40 × 24.5 + 60 × 27.0 = 980 + 1620 = 2600 [1]; mean = 26.0 minutes [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Morning Σx² = 40 × (3.2² + 24.5²) = 40 × 610.49 = 24 419.6 [1]. Afternoon Σx² = 60 × (4.1² + 27²) = 60 × 745.81 = 44 748.6 [1]. Total Σx² = 69 168.2; variance = 691.682 − 676 = 15.682 [1]; σ = 3.96 minutes [1]. **[4]**

**(c)** Weighting the two standard deviations ignores the difference between the group means (24.5 and 27.0); merging the groups adds that variation, so the true value, 3.96, is larger [1]. **[1]**

**8. (a)** (15 × 12 + 25 × 16)/40 = (180 + 400)/40 = 14.5 [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Combined Σx² = 40 × (9 + 14.5²) = 40 × 219.25 = 8770 [1]. X: Σx² = 15 × (4 + 144) = 2220 [1]. Y: Σx² = 8770 − 2220 = 6550 [1]; σ² = 6550/25 − 16² = 262 − 256 = 6, σ = √6 = 2.45 [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** Σx = 20 × 15 = 300; corrected Σx = 300 − 21 + 11 = 290 [1]; mean = 14.5 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Σx² = 20 × (9 + 225) = 4680 [1]; corrected Σx² = 4680 − 441 + 121 = 4360 [1]; variance = 218 − 210.25 = 7.75, σ = 2.78 [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** Total = 12 × 40 + 18 × 45 + 30 × 52 = 480 + 810 + 1560 = 2850 [1]; mean = 2850/60 = 47.5 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** The teams are of different sizes, so each mean must be weighted by the number of members; the simple average, 45.7, gives the small teams too much weight [1]. **[1]**

**11.** Whole group: Σx = 1500, Σx² = 50 × (36 + 900) = 46 800 [1]. Subgroup: Σx = 500, Σx² = 20 × (16 + 625) = 12 820 [1]. Remaining: n = 30, Σx = 1000, Σx² = 33 980 [1]. Mean = 33.3 [1]. Variance = 33 980/30 − (1000/30)² = 1132.67 − 1111.11 = 21.56, σ = 4.64 [1]. **[5]**

**12. (a)** 3, 4, 5, 5, 6, 7, 8, 8, 9, 9: median = (6 + 7)/2 = 6.5 [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 8, 9, 9: median = (5 + 7)/2 = 6 [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** Groups B and C have the same median (7) and the same size, yet combining each with Group A gives different medians (6.5 and 6), so the combined median depends on the individual values, not just the group medians [1]. **[1]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a simple average of group means.
- Averaging standard deviations.
- Missing the square on the mean in Σx² = n(σ² + x̄²).
- Changing Σx but not Σx² after a correction.
- Rounding the mean before squaring it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should I show Σx and Σx² for each group?**
Yes. A clear table of n, Σx and Σx² earns method credit even if a later step slips.

**Can the variance ever come out negative?**
No. A negative result means an arithmetic or rounding slip, usually from squaring a rounded mean.
