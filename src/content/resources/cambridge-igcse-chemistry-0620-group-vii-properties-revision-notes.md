Condensed notes for subtopic **8.3 Group VII properties**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620, 2026–2028. This subtopic is **Core only**: every candidate needs all of it. Full explanations and a worked displacement answer are in the [Group VII study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-group-vii-properties/); exam-style questions are in the [Group VII practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-group-vii-properties-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Halogen | Group VII element (fluorine, chlorine, bromine, iodine, astatine) |
| Diatomic | Molecule made of two atoms, e.g. Cl₂ |
| Halide ion | 1− ion formed by a halogen, e.g. Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻ |
| Displacement | A more reactive halogen replaces a less reactive one in its compound |
| r.t.p. | Room temperature and pressure |

## Facts to learn word for word

| Halogen | Appearance at r.t.p. |
|---|---|
| Chlorine | Pale yellow-green **gas** |
| Bromine | Red-brown **liquid** |
| Iodine | Grey-black **solid** |

Trends down the group (the two the syllabus names):

- **Density increases.**
- **Reactivity decreases.**

Also visible: colour darkens; state changes gas → liquid → solid (melting and boiling points increase).

## Structure and ions

- Seven outer electrons.
- Diatomic molecules with a single covalent bond: Cl₂, Br₂, I₂.
- Gain one electron → halide ion with a 1− charge.
- Element: chlor**ine**; ion: chlor**ide**.

## Displacement grid

| Add ↓ to → | Cl⁻ (aq) | Br⁻ (aq) | I⁻ (aq) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cl₂ | — | ✓ orange | ✓ brown |
| Br₂ | ✗ | — | ✓ brown |
| I₂ | ✗ | ✗ | — |

Rule: a halogen displaces any halide **below** it in the group.

Colours of products in solution: bromine, **orange**; iodine, **brown**. The halide solutions start **colourless**.

## Equations

```
Cl2 + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br2
Cl2 + 2KI  → 2KCl + I2
Br2 + 2KI  → 2KBr + I2

Cl2 + 2Br- → 2Cl- + Br2        (ionic)
Cl2 + 2I-  → 2Cl- + I2         (ionic)
Br2 + 2I-  → 2Br- + I2         (ionic)
```

## Explaining displacement

1. Observation: colourless solution turns orange/brown (or no change).
2. Product: name the halogen formed.
3. Reason: the added halogen is **more reactive** than the halogen in the halide ion, so it displaces it (it gains electrons more readily, taking them from the halide ion).

## Reactivity order from a results table

- Count how many halides each halogen displaces.
- Most displacements = most reactive.
- If the halogens are real, match the order to the group: higher up = more reactive.
- Example: a halogen that displaces both others is chlorine; one that displaces none is iodine.

## Test for chlorine

Damp litmus paper is **bleached** (turns white). Damp blue litmus may turn red first, then white.

## Predicting fluorine and astatine

| | Fluorine | Astatine |
|---|---|---|
| Position | Above chlorine | Below iodine |
| Reactivity | Most reactive | Least reactive |
| State at r.t.p. | Gas | Solid |
| Colour | Pale yellow | Black (darker than iodine) |
| Displaces | Cl, Br, I from halides | None of Cl, Br, I |
| Formula / ion | F₂ / F⁻ | At₂ / At⁻ |

Data method: state the trend, quote values, continue it. Given melting points Cl −101 °C, Br −7 °C, I 114 °C → astatine above 114 °C.

## Model answer

*Bromine water is added to aqueous potassium iodide. Describe and explain what happens.* (3 marks)

> The colourless solution turns brown as iodine forms. Bromine is more reactive than iodine, so bromine displaces iodine from potassium iodide: Br₂ + 2KI → 2KBr + I₂.

Observation, product, reason: one mark each.

## Group VII compared with Group I

| | Group I | Group VII |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Metals | Non-metals |
| Outer electrons | 1 | 7 |
| Ion | 1+ (loses 1 e⁻) | 1− (gains 1 e⁻) |
| Reactivity down group | Increases | Decreases |
| Melting point down group | Decreases | Increases |
| Density down group | Increases | Increases |

Group I metals and Group VII halogens react together to form ionic salts such as NaCl and KBr.

## Exam traps

- Element names end in **-ine**; ion names end in **-ide**.
- Always write Cl₂, Br₂, I₂, never Cl, Br, I for the elements.
- Bromine is a **liquid**; iodine is a **solid**.
- Use the syllabus colours: chlorine pale yellow-green, bromine red-brown, iodine grey-black.
- A less reactive halogen **cannot** displace a more reactive one: no reaction for Br₂ + Cl⁻.
- Reactivity **decreases** down Group VII; the opposite of Group I.
- In an explanation, compare the **two** halogens involved: "chlorine is more reactive than bromine".
- Give both the starting and final colours in a colour change: "colourless to orange", not just "orange".
- When predicting from data, keep to the direction of the trend and give a value or range.

## Self-test

1. Why are the halogens described as diatomic?
2. State the appearance of bromine at r.t.p.
3. State the appearance of iodine at r.t.p.
4. State the trend in density down Group VII.
5. State the trend in reactivity down Group VII.
6. What is seen when chlorine water is added to aqueous potassium bromide?
7. Write the balanced equation for that reaction.
8. Why is there no reaction when iodine solution is added to aqueous potassium chloride?
9. Write the ionic equation for bromine reacting with iodide ions.
10. Predict the state of astatine at r.t.p.
11. Would fluorine displace chlorine from sodium chloride? Explain.
12. What is the charge on a halide ion, and why?

**Answers**

1. Their molecules contain two atoms (e.g. Cl₂).
2. Red-brown liquid.
3. Grey-black solid.
4. Increases.
5. Decreases.
6. The colourless solution turns orange.
7. Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂.
8. Iodine is less reactive than chlorine, so it cannot displace it.
9. Br₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Br⁻ + I₂.
10. Solid.
11. Yes; fluorine is more reactive than chlorine.
12. 1−; the atom gains one electron to complete its outer shell of eight.
