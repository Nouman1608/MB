Condensed recall for **2.3 Weather processes and phenomena** (atmospheric moisture, precipitation types). For full explanations, read the [Core Physical Geography study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-geography-core-physical-geography/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Humidity | Amount of water vapour in the air |
| Absolute humidity | Mass of water vapour in a given volume of air |
| Relative humidity | Water vapour present as a percentage of the maximum the air can hold at that temperature |
| Dew point | Temperature at which air is saturated and condensation begins |
| Condensation nuclei | Tiny particles on which droplets form |
| Adiabatic cooling | Cooling of rising air as it expands, with no heat exchanged with surroundings |
| Dry adiabatic lapse rate (DALR) | Rate of cooling in unsaturated rising air |
| Saturated adiabatic lapse rate (SALR) | Lower rate once condensation releases latent heat |
| Environmental lapse rate (ELR) | Actual temperature change with height in the surrounding air |
| Condensation level | Height at which rising air reaches dew point and cloud base forms |
| Conditional instability | Air is stable unless lifted to saturation, after which it becomes unstable |
| Advection | Horizontal movement of air |

## Moisture processes

The syllabus lists the processes by which water changes state in the atmosphere:

| Process | Change | Energy |
|---|---|---|
| Evaporation | Liquid to vapour | Latent heat absorbed (cools the surface) |
| Condensation | Vapour to liquid | Latent heat released (warms the air) |
| Freezing / melting | Liquid to ice / ice to liquid | Released / absorbed |
| Deposition | Vapour straight to ice, as in frost | Released |
| Sublimation | Ice straight to vapour | Absorbed |

Air can hold more water vapour when it is warm. So cooling air raises its relative humidity without changing the amount of vapour, until it reaches 100 per cent at the dew point.

## From vapour to rain

Air is lifted, expands and cools adiabatically to dew point; vapour condenses on nuclei into cloud droplets; droplets grow by collision and coalescence (or ice crystals grow in cold cloud) until they fall as precipitation.

Cloud droplets are far too small to fall: many millions are needed to make one raindrop. In **warm clouds** (above 0 degrees C), larger droplets fall faster, collide with smaller ones and merge (collision and coalescence). In **cold clouds**, ice crystals grow at the expense of supercooled water droplets, become heavy enough to fall, and melt into rain if the air below is warm (the Bergeron-Findeisen process).

## Lapse rates and stability: worked example

Approximate rates: DALR about **10 degrees C per km** (strictly 9.8); SALR lower and variable, often about **5 to 7 degrees C per km**; ELR averages about **6.5 degrees C per km** but changes from day to day.

A parcel of air at the surface is 24 degrees C, with a dew point of 14 degrees C.

- It rises unsaturated at the DALR: (24 - 14) / 10 = **1 km** to the condensation level, where it is at 14 degrees C. Cloud base forms here. (This simple method ignores the slight fall of dew point with height; use whatever figures a question gives.)
- Above 1 km it cools at the SALR (say 6 degrees C per km), so at 2 km it is 14 - 6 = **8 degrees C**.
- If the ELR that day is 8 degrees C per km, the surrounding air is 16 degrees C at 1 km and 8 degrees C at 2 km. At 1 km the parcel (14 degrees C) is colder than its surroundings, so it would sink back unless forced upwards: **stable while unsaturated**.
- Above 2 km the parcel cools at only 6 degrees C per km while its surroundings cool at 8, so it becomes warmer than them and rises freely: **conditional instability**, deep cumulus cloud and possibly thunderstorms.
- If the ELR were less than the SALR, say 4 degrees C per km, the parcel would stay colder than its surroundings at every height: **absolute stability**, with little or only flat layer cloud.

## Stability

- **Stable:** rising parcel is cooler than surroundings (ELR lower than lapse rate of parcel) and sinks back; little cloud.
- **Unstable:** rising parcel stays warmer than surroundings and keeps rising; deep cloud, showers, thunderstorms.
- **Conditional instability:** ELR lies between SALR and DALR. The parcel is stable while unsaturated, but once it is lifted to condensation, it cools at the slower SALR and becomes warmer than its surroundings, so it rises freely.

## Causes of precipitation

| Type | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Convectional | Surface heating gives rising warm air, common in summer and in the tropics |
| Relief (orographic) | Air forced up over high ground; rain on the windward side, rain shadow beyond |
| Frontal | Warm air rises over cold at a front |
| Radiation cooling | Surface cools at night, chilling air above and giving dew or fog |

Forms include cloud, rain, hail, snow, dew and fog.

**Rain shadow explained:** air rising over mountains cools, condenses and rains on the windward side. Descending on the leeward side, it warms at the faster DALR because it is now unsaturated, so it is warmer and drier than when it started.

## Forms of precipitation

- **Hail:** forms in tall cumulonimbus clouds with strong updrafts. Ice pellets are carried up and down through layers of supercooled water, adding layers of ice, until too heavy to be held up.
- **Snow:** ice crystals that clump into flakes and reach the ground without melting, because air is near or below freezing all the way down.
- **Dew:** condensation directly onto cold surfaces on clear, calm nights.
- **Fog:** cloud at ground level. **Radiation fog** forms on clear, calm nights over land; **advection fog** forms when moist air moves over a colder surface, such as a cold sea current.

## Annotated model paragraph

*"Explain the formation of relief rainfall."*

> Moist air is forced to rise over high ground (**uplift**). It expands and cools at the DALR until it reaches dew point (**adiabatic cooling**). Vapour condenses on nuclei to form cloud, and droplets grow until they fall on the windward slope (**condensation and growth**). Descending air warms and dries, giving a rain shadow (**leeward effect**).

## Exam traps

- Condensation needs cooling to dew point, and also nuclei.
- SALR is lower than DALR because latent heat is released.
- Do not mix up relief rain (on the windward side) and the rain shadow.
- Dew and fog form by cooling at the surface, not by uplift.
- Relative humidity can rise without any extra water vapour, simply because the air cools.
- In stability questions, compare the parcel with its surroundings at the same height.
