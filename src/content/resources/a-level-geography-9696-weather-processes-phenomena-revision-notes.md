Condensed recall for **2.3 Weather processes and phenomena** (atmospheric moisture, precipitation types). For full explanations, read the [Core Physical Geography study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-geography-core-physical-geography/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Humidity | Amount of water vapour in the air |
| Relative humidity | Water vapour present as a percentage of the maximum the air can hold at that temperature |
| Dew point | Temperature at which air is saturated and condensation begins |
| Condensation nuclei | Tiny particles on which droplets form |
| Adiabatic cooling | Cooling of rising air as it expands, with no heat exchanged with surroundings |
| Dry adiabatic lapse rate (DALR) | Rate of cooling in unsaturated rising air |
| Saturated adiabatic lapse rate (SALR) | Lower rate once condensation releases latent heat |
| Environmental lapse rate (ELR) | Actual temperature change with height in the surrounding air |

## From vapour to rain

Air is lifted, expands and cools adiabatically to dew point; vapour condenses on nuclei into cloud droplets; droplets grow by collision and coalescence (or ice crystals grow in cold cloud) until they fall as precipitation.

## Stability

- **Stable:** rising parcel is cooler than surroundings (ELR lower than lapse rate of parcel) and sinks back; little cloud.
- **Unstable:** rising parcel stays warmer than surroundings and keeps rising; deep cloud, showers, thunderstorms.

## Causes of precipitation

| Type | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Convectional | Surface heating gives rising warm air, common in summer and in the tropics |
| Relief (orographic) | Air forced up over high ground; rain on the windward side, rain shadow beyond |
| Frontal | Warm air rises over cold at a front |
| Radiation cooling | Surface cools at night, chilling air above and giving dew or fog |

Forms include cloud, rain, hail, snow, dew and fog.

## Exam traps

- Condensation needs cooling to dew point, and also nuclei.
- SALR is lower than DALR because latent heat is released.
- Do not mix up relief rain (on the windward side) and the rain shadow.
- Dew and fog form by cooling at the surface, not by uplift.

## Self-test

1. What is dew point?
2. Why is SALR lower than DALR?
3. Describe convectional rainfall.
4. Where is the rain shadow?
5. What makes air unstable?
6. How does dew form?

**Answers:** 1. Temperature at which air becomes saturated and condensation starts. 2. Condensation releases latent heat, offsetting some of the cooling. 3. Heated ground warms air, which rises, cools, condenses and gives showers. 4. On the leeward side of high ground. 5. A rising parcel remains warmer than the surrounding air. 6. Surface cooling at night chills air to dew point, condensing vapour on surfaces.
