These questions cover section **6.4 Sampling and estimation** of Probability & Statistics 2 (Paper 6) in the Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics 9709 syllabus for 2026 and 2027. A scientific calculator is allowed, but unsupported calculator answers earn no marks. Give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question.

Related: the [6.4 Sampling and estimation study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-sampling-and-estimation/) and the [6.4 Sampling and estimation revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-sampling-and-estimation-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** A manager wants to estimate the mean commuting distance of the 480 employees of a firm. She asks the drivers of the first 30 cars to arrive in the staff car park one morning.

**(a)** Give one reason why this sample may not be representative.

**(b)** Describe how random numbers could be used to choose a random sample of 30 employees. **[3]**

**2.** The random variable X has distribution N(65, 8²). A random sample of 10 observations of X is taken. Find the probability that the sample mean is greater than 68. **[3]**

**3.** Six measurements of a reaction time, in seconds, are 3.2, 4.1, 2.8, 3.6, 3.9, 3.4. Find unbiased estimates of the population mean and variance. **[3]**

**4.** In a random sample of 200 households, 64 have a solar panel. Calculate a 99% confidence interval for the proportion of all households that have a solar panel. **[3]**

**5.** A population is normally distributed with standard deviation 15. Find the smallest sample size for which a 95% confidence interval for the population mean has width at most 4. **[3]**

**6.** Two independent 99% confidence intervals for μ are calculated. Find the probability that exactly one of them contains μ. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** The times, t minutes, taken by 50 randomly chosen customers to complete an online form are summarised by Σ(t − 20) = −35 and Σ(t − 20)² = 1240.

**(a)** Find unbiased estimates of the population mean and variance of the times.

**(b)** Calculate a 95% confidence interval for the population mean time.

**(c)** Explain why it was not necessary to assume that the times are normally distributed. **[7]**

**8.** The lifetimes of a type of battery have mean 18.5 hours and standard deviation 4.2 hours. The distribution of lifetimes is not known. A random sample of 45 batteries is tested.

**(a)** State, with a reason, the approximate distribution of the sample mean lifetime.

**(b)** Find the probability that the sample mean lifetime is less than 17.5 hours.

**(c)** Find the probability that the total lifetime of the 45 batteries is more than 850 hours. **[7]**

**9.** The masses of bags of rice are normally distributed with standard deviation 0.8 kg. From a random sample of 25 bags, a confidence interval for the population mean mass was found to be (12.236, 12.764).

**(a)** State the sample mean mass.

**(b)** Find the confidence level of this interval.

**(c)** State, with a reason, whether the Central Limit Theorem was needed to calculate this interval. **[5]**

**10.** An online shop records that 135 of a random sample of 500 visitors to its website made a purchase.

**(a)** Calculate an approximate 95% confidence interval for the proportion of all visitors who make a purchase.

**(b)** The shop's manager claims that 30% of visitors make a purchase. Comment on this claim.

**(c)** Explain why the interval in part (a) is described as approximate. **[6]**

**11.** The volume of a liquid dispensed by a machine is normally distributed with standard deviation 3.6 ml.

**(a)** Find the smallest sample size for which a 98% confidence interval for the mean volume has width less than 2 ml.

**(b)** Twelve inspectors each independently calculate a 98% confidence interval for the mean volume. Find the probability that at least 11 of the intervals contain the true mean. **[6]**

**12.** The lengths, x cm, of a random sample of 10 rods are summarised by Σx = 482 and Σx² = 23 254. The lengths are normally distributed.

**(a)** Find unbiased estimates of the population mean and variance.

**(b)** Taking your estimates as the true values, find the probability that the mean length of a further random sample of 6 rods is greater than 49 cm. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** Employees who walk, cycle or use public transport, and those who arrive later, cannot be chosen [1]; these groups may have quite different commuting distances, so the sample may be biased.

**(b)** Number the employees 001 to 480 [1]. Generate three-digit random numbers, ignoring those above 480, 000 and repeats, until 30 employees are chosen [1].

**2.** X̄ ~ N(65, 64/10) [1]. z = (68 − 65)/(8/√10) = 1.186 [1]. P = 1 − Φ(1.186) = **0.118** [1].

**3.** Σx = 21.0, Σx² = 74.62 [1]. x̄ = **3.5** [1]. s² = (74.62 − 21.0²/6)/5 = 1.12/5 = **0.224** [1].

**4.** p̂ = 0.32 and z = 2.576 [1]. 0.32 ± 2.576 × √(0.32 × 0.68/200) = 0.32 ± 0.0850 [1]. Interval **(0.235, 0.405)** [1].

**5.** Half-width at most 2, so 1.96 × 15/√n ≤ 2 [1]. √n ≥ 14.7, so n ≥ 216.09 [1]. Smallest n = **217** [1].

**6.** 2 × 0.99 × 0.01 [1] = **0.0198** [1].

**7.** **(a)** t̄ = 20 + (−35/50) = **19.3** [1]. s² = (1240 − (−35)²/50)/49 [1] = 1215.5/49 = **24.8** (3 s.f.) [1].

**(b)** 19.3 ± 1.96 × √(24.81/50) [1] = 19.3 ± 1.38 [1]. Interval **(17.9, 20.7)** [1].

**(c)** The sample of 50 is large, so by the Central Limit Theorem the sample mean is approximately normally distributed [1].

**8.** **(a)** X̄ ≈ N(18.5, 4.2²/45) [1], by the Central Limit Theorem because n = 45 is large [1].

**(b)** Standard error = 4.2/√45 = 0.6261. z = (17.5 − 18.5)/0.6261 = −1.597 [1] [1]. P = Φ(−1.597) = 1 − 0.9449 = **0.0551** [1].

**(c)** Total > 850 is the same as X̄ > 850/45 = 18.889 [1]. z = 0.3889/0.6261 = 0.621, so P = 1 − Φ(0.621) = **0.267** [1].

**9.** **(a)** x̄ = (12.236 + 12.764)/2 = **12.5** kg [1].

**(b)** Half-width = 0.264 [1]. z × 0.8/√25 = 0.264, so z = 1.65 [1]. Φ(1.65) = 0.9505, so the level is 2 × 0.9505 − 1 = 0.901, which is **90%** [1].

**(c)** No: the masses are normally distributed and σ is known, so X̄ is exactly normal even for a sample of 25 [1].

**10.** **(a)** p̂ = 135/500 = 0.27 [1]. 0.27 ± 1.96 × √(0.27 × 0.73/500) = 0.27 ± 0.0389 [1]. Interval **(0.231, 0.309)** [1].

**(b)** 0.30 lies inside the interval [1], so the data are consistent with the claim; they do not prove it [1].

**(c)** The interval uses a normal approximation to the distribution of the sample proportion and uses p̂ in place of the unknown p [1].

**11.** **(a)** Width = 2 × 2.326 × 3.6/√n < 2 [1]. √n > 8.374, so n > 70.12 [1]. Smallest n = **71** [1].

**(b)** Number containing μ ~ B(12, 0.98) [1]. P(11) + P(12) = 12 × 0.98¹¹ × 0.02 + 0.98¹² [1] = 0.1922 + 0.7847 = **0.977** [1].

**12.** **(a)** x̄ = 482/10 = **48.2** [1]. s² = (23 254 − 482²/10)/9 [1] = 21.6/9 = **2.4** [1].

**(b)** X̄ ~ N(48.2, 2.4/6) = N(48.2, 0.4) [1]. z = (49 − 48.2)/√0.4 = 1.265 [1]. P = 1 − Φ(1.265) = **0.103** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving the "reason" off a sampling criticism: say who is excluded and why it matters for the variable.
- Dividing by n instead of n − 1, or mishandling the coded sum (−35 squared is +1225).
- Rounding a sample size down, as in questions 5 and 11.
- Converting a total into a mean incorrectly; question 8(c) needs 850 ÷ 45 first.
- Quoting the CLT for a normal population, as in question 9(c).
