These notes condense quantitative skill QS2, "calculate, use and understand percentages and percentage changes", from section 3.5 of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Economics (9640) specification, which says the quantitative skills could be assessed in any of the assessments. For full worked examples, read the [QS2 study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs2-quantitative-skills-oxfordaqa-alevel-economics/); to practise, use the [QS2 practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs2-quantitative-skills-oxfordaqa-alevel-economics-practice/). All figures are invented; "cu" means currency units.

## Formula sheet

```
Percentage of total        part / total × 100
Percentage change          (new − old) / old × 100
Multiplier                 1 + change/100      (+8% → 1.08, −8% → 0.92)
Reverse percentage         original = new / multiplier
Successive changes         multiply the multipliers
Per capita growth          (1 + g_total)/(1 + g_population) − 1
Average annual growth      (total multiplier)^(1/n) − 1
Revenue change             price multiplier × quantity multiplier − 1
Contribution of a part     change in part / original total × 100   (percentage points)
```

## Percentage points versus per cent

| Situation | Percentage points | Percentage change |
|---|---|---|
| Unemployment rate 7.5% → 6.0% | −1.5 points | −20% |
| Interest rate 2% → 3% | +1 point | +50% |
| Deficit 4.0% → 4.0% of GDP | 0 points | 0% (even if the deficit in cu rose) |

Use **percentage points** for the gap between two rates. Use **per cent** for a change relative to the starting value.

## Quick worked checks

**Rate versus level.** Unemployment rises from 1.8 to 1.9 million while the labour force rises from 30 to 32 million. The rate falls from 6.00% to 5.94%, a fall of 0.06 percentage points, even though 5.6% more people are unemployed.

**Per capita growth.** Real GDP grows 3% and population 1%: 1.03/1.01 = 1.0198, so GDP per capita grows 1.98%. Subtracting (2%) is close only because both rates are small.

**Average growth.** 20% total growth over 5 years: 1.20^(1/5) = 1.0371, so 3.71% a year, not 4%.

**Contribution.** GDP rises from 500 to 520. Consumption rises by 15, contributing 15/500 = 3.0 of the 4.0 percentage points of growth.

**Revenue.** Price +10% and quantity −15%: 1.10 × 0.85 = 0.935, so revenue falls 6.5%.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Base value | The starting value that a percentage change is measured against |
| Multiplier | The factor equivalent to a percentage change |
| Percentage point | The unit for a difference between two percentages |
| Compound growth | Growth where each year's change applies to the new, larger value |
| Per capita | Per person: a total divided by the population |
| Contribution to growth | A component's change as a share of the original total, in percentage points |

## Exam traps

- **Wrong base.** Divide by the old value, not the new one.
- **Asymmetry.** A 20% fall needs a 25% rise to recover; +10% then −10% leaves you 1% lower.
- **Points versus per cent.** A rate falling from 8% to 6% is a fall of 2 points, or 25%.
- **Adding, not multiplying.** Price +10% and quantity −15% give revenue −6.5%, not −5%.
- **Simple averages of growth.** 20% over 5 years is 3.71% a year, not 4%.
- **Level versus rate.** The number unemployed can rise while the unemployment rate falls.
- **Fastest versus largest contribution.** A small component can grow fastest but contribute least.
- **Reverse percentage.** After a 12% rise to 140, the original is 140/1.12, not 140 × 0.88.

## Self-test

1. Find the percentage change when a price rises from 250 to 290.
2. Find the percentage change when it falls back from 290 to 250.
3. The unemployment rate falls from 7.5% to 6%. State the change in percentage points and as a percentage change.
4. After a 25% rise, a price is 150. Find the original price.
5. Output rises by 5% and then falls by 5%. Find the overall percentage change.
6. GDP grows by 4% and population by 2%. Find the growth of GDP per capita to 2 decimal places.
7. A value grows by 30% in total over 4 years. Find the average annual growth rate.
8. Price falls by 20% and quantity sold rises by 30%. Find the percentage change in revenue.
9. GDP is 800 cu billion and consumption rises by 12 cu billion. Find consumption's contribution to GDP growth.
10. A deficit of 50 cu billion occurs when GDP is 1,250 cu billion. Express it as a percentage of GDP.
11. 2 million people are unemployed in a labour force of 40 million. Find the unemployment rate.
12. After a 50% fall, what percentage rise restores the original value?

### Answers

1. 40/250 × 100 = +16%.
2. −40/290 × 100 = −13.8%.
3. −1.5 percentage points; −1.5/7.5 × 100 = −20%.
4. 150/1.25 = 120.
5. 1.05 × 0.95 = 0.9975, a fall of 0.25%.
6. 1.04/1.02 = 1.0196, so 1.96%.
7. 1.30^(1/4) = 1.0678, so about 6.78% a year.
8. 0.80 × 1.30 = 1.04, a rise of 4%.
9. 12/800 × 100 = 1.5 percentage points.
10. 50/1,250 × 100 = 4%.
11. 2/40 × 100 = 5%.
12. 100% (the value must double).
