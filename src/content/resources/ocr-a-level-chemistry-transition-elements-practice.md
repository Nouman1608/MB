This set practises section 5.3.1, Transition elements, of the OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432) specification, Version 3.1 (May 2026), for first assessment in 2017. Each lettered outcome, 5.3.1(a) to (l), is tested at least once. H432 is the full A level. Questions 8, 9 and 11 are longer, combined problems.

Data: Ar values O = 16.0, Cu = 63.5, Zn = 65.4.

## Questions

**1.** This question is about electron configurations.

**(a)** Give the full sub-shell electron configuration of an Fe³⁺ ion (Z = 26). **[1]**
**(b)** Give the full sub-shell electron configuration of a copper atom (Z = 29). **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why copper is a transition element but zinc is not. **[2]**

**2.** 2,2'-Bipyridine, "bipy", is a neutral ligand that bonds through lone pairs on two nitrogen atoms. Iron(II) forms [Fe(bipy)₃]²⁺.

**(a)** Explain the term ligand. **[1]**
**(b)** State the oxidation number of iron in this complex. **[1]**
**(c)** State the coordination number and the shape of the complex. **[2]**
**(d)** Name the type of stereoisomerism shown by [Fe(bipy)₃]²⁺ and explain why it occurs. **[2]**

**3.** Using manganese and one other transition element, illustrate:

**(a)** more than one oxidation state, with the formula and colour of one species in each state **[3]**
**(b)** the catalytic behaviour of a transition element compound, with an equation for the catalysed reaction. **[2]**

**4.** Cis-platin is used to treat some cancers.

**(a)** Draw or describe the two stereoisomers of Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂ and explain why they exist. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how cis-platin prevents cancer cells dividing. **[3]**
**(c)** State one risk of using cis-platin as a drug. **[1]**

**5.** Aqueous copper(II) sulfate is split between two tubes.

**(a)** Excess concentrated hydrochloric acid is added to the first tube. Write an equation using complexed formulae, state the final colour, and describe the change in shape and coordination number. **[4]**
**(b)** Excess concentrated aqueous ammonia is added to the second tube. Write an equation for the formation of the final complex from the hexaaqua ion and state its colour. **[2]**

**6.** Explain how haemoglobin carries oxygen, and why carbon monoxide is toxic. **[3]**

**7.** Solutions P, Q and R each contain one of Cu²⁺, Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺, Mn²⁺ or Cr³⁺.

| Solution | NaOH(aq), a few drops then excess | NH₃(aq), a few drops then excess |
|---|---|---|
| P | green ppt, unchanged in excess; surface browns on standing | green ppt, unchanged in excess |
| Q | grey-green ppt; dark green solution in excess | grey-green ppt; purple solution in excess |
| R | light brown ppt, unchanged in excess; darkens on standing | light brown ppt, unchanged in excess |

**(a)** Identify the cation in each solution. **[3]**
**(b)** Give the ionic equation for P's precipitate forming, including state symbols. **[1]**
**(c)** Write equations for the two reactions of Q's precipitate in excess reagent. **[2]**

**8.** A student has 20.0 cm³ of 0.150 mol dm⁻³ Cr³⁺(aq).

**(a)** Aqueous sodium hydroxide is added dropwise until in excess. Describe what is seen. **[2]**
**(b)** Hydrogen peroxide is added and the mixture is warmed. The half-equations are:

[Cr(OH)₆]³⁻ + 2OH⁻ → CrO₄²⁻ + 4H₂O + 3e⁻
H₂O₂ + 2e⁻ → 2OH⁻

State the colour change and the change in oxidation number of chromium, then construct the overall equation. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the minimum volume, in cm³, of 0.250 mol dm⁻³ H₂O₂ needed to oxidise all the chromium. **[2]**
**(d)** The yellow solution is then acidified. State the colour change and give the equation. **[2]**
**(e)** The resulting dichromate(VI) is reduced back to Cr³⁺ with zinc and acid: Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 3Zn → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O + 3Zn²⁺. Calculate the minimum mass of zinc needed. **[2]**

**9.** 0.305 g of brass (copper and zinc) is dissolved in nitric acid. Excess acid is removed and excess KI(aq) added. The iodine formed reacts with 32.50 cm³ of 0.0950 mol dm⁻³ Na₂S₂O₃(aq).

2Cu²⁺ + 4I⁻ → 2CuI + I₂          I₂ + 2S₂O₃²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻

**(a)** Describe what is seen when KI(aq) is added. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage by mass of copper in the brass. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[4]**
**(c)** Suggest why excess nitric acid must be removed before adding iodide. **[1]**

**10.** Copper(I) oxide, Cu₂O, reacts with dilute sulfuric acid: Cu₂O + H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + Cu + H₂O.

**(a)** Explain, using oxidation numbers, how this reaction shows disproportionation. **[2]**
**(b)** State what is seen. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass of copper formed from 1.716 g of Cu₂O. **[2]**

**11.** Green aqueous [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺ gives a violet solution of [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ with excess ethane-1,2-diamine (en), and a green precipitate with aqueous sodium hydroxide. With less en, [Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ can form.

**(a)** Write equations, using complexed formulae, for the formation of [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ and of the green precipitate. **[2]**
**(b)** Classify each reaction in (a) and explain why neither is a redox reaction. **[2]**
**(c)** [Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ exists as three stereoisomers. Describe them and the types of isomerism involved. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶3d⁵** [1]
**(b)** **1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶3d¹⁰4s¹** [1]
**(c)** Cu forms Cu²⁺, 3d⁹, which has an incomplete d-sub-shell [1]. Zn forms only Zn²⁺, 3d¹⁰, a full d-sub-shell, so no ion has an incomplete d-sub-shell [1]
*Examiner insight:* The definition concerns an ion, so answers about the atom's configuration miss the point.

**2. (a)** A species with a lone pair that it gives to a central metal ion, forming a coordinate bond [1]
**(b)** **+2** [1]
**(c)** Coordination number **6** (three bidentate ligands) [1]; **octahedral** [1]
**(d)** **Optical isomerism** [1]. The complex has no plane of symmetry, so it has non-superimposable mirror images [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each bidentate ligand forms two coordinate bonds, so 3 is wrong.

**3. (a)** Mn: +2, Mn²⁺, very pale pink; +7, MnO₄⁻, purple [1]. Second element, e.g. Fe: +2, Fe²⁺, pale green; +3, Fe³⁺, yellow to orange-brown [1]. Each element shown in two or more oxidation states with correct formulae [1]
**(b)** MnO₂ catalyses the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide [1]: **2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The question asks for at least two elements, so an answer using manganese alone limits the credit available in (a).

**4. (a)** Square planar: cis has the two Cl ligands at 90° (adjacent), trans has them at 180° (opposite) [1]. The flat arrangement is fixed, and there are two pairs of different ligands [1]
**(b)** Chloride ligands are replaced inside the cell [1]. Pt bonds to nitrogen atoms on DNA bases [1]. The distorted DNA cannot be replicated, so the cell cannot divide [1]
**(c)** Any one: damages healthy dividing cells; nausea; hair loss; kidney damage [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a) draw clear square planar structures; a tetrahedral sketch does not show cis–trans isomers and is unlikely to gain credit.

**5. (a)** **[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CuCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O** [1]. Final colour yellow (green in between) [1]. Shape changes from octahedral to tetrahedral [1]. Coordination number falls from 6 to 4 because Cl⁻ ions are larger than H₂O molecules [1]
**(b)** **[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 4H₂O** [1]; deep blue solution [1]
*Examiner insight:* The guidance asks for complexed formulae in substitution equations; Cu²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ → CuCl₄²⁻ is weaker.

**6.** Fe²⁺ is bonded to four haem-ring N atoms and one protein N, leaving a sixth site [1]. O₂ binds there as a ligand in the lungs and is released in tissues [1]. CO replaces O₂, binds much more strongly and is not readily released, so less O₂ is carried [1]
*Examiner insight:* Say "replaces O₂" or "ligand substitution"; "blocks the blood" is too vague for credit.

**7. (a)** P = **Fe²⁺** [1]; Q = **Cr³⁺** [1]; R = **Mn²⁺** [1]
**(b)** **Fe²⁺(aq) + 2OH⁻(aq) → Fe(OH)₂(s)** [1]
**(c)** **Cr(OH)₃ + 3OH⁻ → [Cr(OH)₆]³⁻** [1]; **Cr(OH)₃ + 6NH₃ → [Cr(NH₃)₆]³⁺ + 3OH⁻** [1]
*Examiner insight:* P's brown surface comes from air oxidation; the green precipitate seen first identifies Fe²⁺.

**8. (a)** Grey-green precipitate [1], which dissolves in excess to give a dark green solution of [Cr(OH)₆]³⁻ [1]
**(b)** Green → yellow; Cr changes from +3 to +6 [1]. Multiply the chromium half-equation by 2 and the peroxide one by 3 so 6 electrons cancel [1]. **2[Cr(OH)₆]³⁻ + 3H₂O₂ → 2CrO₄²⁻ + 2OH⁻ + 8H₂O** [1]
**(c)** n(Cr) = 0.150 × 0.0200 = 3.00 × 10⁻³ mol; n(H₂O₂) = 3.00 × 10⁻³ × 3/2 = 4.50 × 10⁻³ mol [1]. Volume = 4.50 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.250 = 0.0180 dm³ = **18.0 cm³** [1]
**(d)** Yellow → orange [1]. **2CrO₄²⁻ + 2H⁺ ⇌ Cr₂O₇²⁻ + H₂O** [1]
**(e)** n(Cr₂O₇²⁻) = 3.00 × 10⁻³ ÷ 2 = 1.50 × 10⁻³ mol; n(Zn) = 3 × 1.50 × 10⁻³ = 4.50 × 10⁻³ mol [1]. Mass = 4.50 × 10⁻³ × 65.4 = **0.294 g** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), cancel OH⁻ and H₂O that appear on both sides before giving the final equation.

**9. (a)** A white (off-white) precipitate of CuI [1] in a brown solution, from iodine [1]
**(b)** n(S₂O₃²⁻) = 0.0950 × 0.03250 = 3.0875 × 10⁻³ mol [1]. Ratio Cu²⁺ : I₂ : S₂O₃²⁻ = 2 : 1 : 2, so n(Cu) = 3.0875 × 10⁻³ mol [1]. Mass = 3.0875 × 10⁻³ × 63.5 = 0.196 g [1]. Percentage = 0.196 ÷ 0.305 × 100 = **64.3%** [1]
**(c)** Nitric acid would also oxidise iodide to iodine, making the titre and percentage too high [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the 2 : 1 : 2 chain; a bare 1 : 1 ratio with no reasoning is easy to misapply.

**10. (a)** Copper in Cu₂O is +1; in CuSO₄ it is +2 (oxidised) and in Cu it is 0 (reduced) [1]. Copper is simultaneously oxidised and reduced [1]
**(b)** A blue solution and a red-brown solid (copper) [1]
**(c)** n(Cu₂O) = 1.716 ÷ 143.0 = 0.0120 mol = n(Cu) [1]. Mass = 0.0120 × 63.5 = **0.762 g** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Only half the copper in Cu₂O becomes metal, so do not double the moles.

**11. (a)** **[Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 3en → [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ + 6H₂O** [1]. **[Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Ni(OH)₂(H₂O)₄ + 2H₂O** [1]
**(b)** The first is ligand substitution; the second is precipitation [1]. Nickel stays at +2, so no oxidation number changes [1]
**(c)** A trans isomer, H₂O ligands opposite [1]. Two cis isomers, H₂O ligands adjacent [1]. The cis pair are non-superimposable mirror images (optical isomers); trans has a mirror plane, so the complex shows cis–trans and optical isomerism [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check the oxidation number first; it shows whether redox is involved.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Removing 3d electrons before 4s in ion configurations.
- Counting ligands instead of coordinate bonds.
- Writing "deep blue precipitate" for the copper–ammonia complex.
- Claiming Cu(OH)₂ or Fe(OH)₃ dissolves in excess NaOH.
- Leaving OH⁻ or H₂O uncancelled after combining half-equations.
- Using 2 : 1 for Cu²⁺ : S₂O₃²⁻ instead of tracing the ratio through iodine.
