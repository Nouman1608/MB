**What do these questions practise?** All four key developments of topic 3.2.2 in OxfordAQA
International GCSE Pakistan Studies 9236: population, industry and employment, trade and economic
development, and urbanisation. The mix of multiple-choice, short and extended items mirrors the
styles named for Paper 2 Section B.

## Multiple choice

**1.** Which measure combines life expectancy, education and income?
A GDP  B FDI  C HDI  D balance of trade **[1]**

**2.** Which city is the centre of Pakistan's sports goods and surgical instrument industries?
A Faisalabad  B Sialkot  C Quetta  D Hyderabad **[1]**

**3.** Natural increase is calculated as:
A birth rate + death rate  B birth rate − death rate  C immigration − emigration  D death rate − birth rate **[1]**

## Short answer

**4.** Define *infant mortality rate*. **[1]**

**5.** Give **two** reasons why death rates in Pakistan have fallen since 1947. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** physical factors that explain low population density in Balochistan. **[2]**

**7.** Name **two** of Pakistan's main exports and **one** main import. **[3]**

**8.** Explain how remittances affect Pakistan's economy. **[3]**

## Extended response

**9.** Explain why Faisalabad has become a major centre of the textile industry. **[4]**

**10.** Describe how the importance of the primary, secondary and tertiary sectors in Pakistan has
changed since independence. **[6]**

**11.** Explain the impacts of international migration on Pakistan's society and economy. **[6]**

**12.** Explain why the quaternary sector is concentrated in Pakistan's largest cities. **[4]**

**13.** "The challenges of urbanisation in Karachi and Lahore outweigh the opportunities." Evaluate
this statement. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1].

**2.** B [1].

**3.** B [1].

**4.** The number of babies who die before their first birthday per 1,000 live births in a year [1].

**5.** Any two: vaccination programmes; better medical care and hospitals; cleaner drinking water
and sanitation; improved food supply [2].

**6.** Any two: mountainous and plateau relief; very low rainfall and shortage of water; extreme
temperatures; poor soils in deserts [2].

**7.** Exports, any two: textiles/clothing, rice, leather goods, sports goods, surgical instruments
[2]. Import, any one: oil/petroleum, LNG, machinery, chemicals, palm oil [1].

**8.** Remittances bring in foreign exchange [1], which helps pay for imports and reduces the effect
of the trade deficit [1]. Families spend them on housing, education and small businesses, raising
living standards and demand in the economy [1].

**9.** Faisalabad lies in the cotton-growing area of central Punjab, giving a nearby supply of raw
cotton [1]. It has a large labour force, including skilled textile workers [1]. Road and rail links
connect it to Karachi's port for exports and imported machinery [1]. Clustering of mills, suppliers
and traders reduces costs and spreads skills [1].

**10.** Level-marked. At independence the **primary** sector, mainly farming, produced most output
and employed most workers [1–2]. Its share of GDP has fallen but it still employs more than a third
of workers [1]. The **secondary** sector grew from the 1950s, especially textiles, then cement,
fertiliser, cars and steel [1–2]. The **tertiary** sector now produces the largest share of GDP,
with growth in banking, telecoms, retail and IT, and a growing quaternary sector [1–2]. Maximum 6.

**11.** Economic impacts: remittances support families and the balance of payments [1–2]; loss of
skilled workers such as doctors and engineers reduces services at home [1–2]; returning migrants
bring savings and skills [1]. Social impacts: families separated for long periods [1]; diaspora
communities spread Pakistani culture and strengthen links with countries such as the UK [1];
changing consumption and aspirations among families receiving remittances [1]. Maximum 6, with both
economic and social impacts needed for the top level.

**12.** Quaternary firms need highly educated graduates, who are trained at universities in Lahore,
Karachi and Islamabad [1]. They depend on fast, reliable internet and power, which are better in
large cities [1]. Cities offer clients, investors and other technology firms, so companies benefit
from clustering [1]. Government technology parks and incentives are also located there [1].

**13.** Level-marked. Challenges: not enough formal jobs, so many work informally on low pay [1–2];
housing shortages and katchi abadis without proper water or sewerage [1–2]; congestion and long
commutes [1]; air pollution and winter smog in Lahore; sewage reaching the sea at Karachi [1–2].
Opportunities: jobs in industry and services and higher wages than in rural areas [1–2]; access to
universities, hospitals and innovation hubs [1]; investment in mass transit and infrastructure [1];
cities generate a large share of national output [1]. Top-level answers reach a supported judgement,
for example that challenges currently dominate for the urban poor but cities remain essential to
Pakistan's development if strategies such as settlement upgrading and mass transit are expanded.
Maximum 9.

## Frequently asked questions

**How should I answer a 9-mark evaluation?**
Give developed points on both sides with specific
examples from Karachi or Lahore, then a clear judgement that answers the statement.

**Do I need exact statistics?**
Use approximate, up-to-date figures from your course materials and
from any resource in the question; examiners reward accurate use of the data provided.
