## Questions

**1.** **(a)** Expand and simplify 5(x − 2) − 2(3x − 4). **[2]**
**(b)** Find the value of 4a² − 3ab when a = −3 and b = 2. **[2]**

**2.** Gym A charges £25 to join plus £18 each month. Gym B charges £40 to join plus £13 each month. Form an equation and find after how many months the total cost is the same, and what that cost is. **[4]**

**3.** The sequence 4, 11, 18, 25, ... continues in the same way.
**(a)** Find a rule for the nth term. **[2]**
**(b)** Decide whether 207 and 300 are terms of the sequence. **[2]**

**4.** The first five terms of a sequence are 3, 9, 19, 33, 51.
**(a)** Find a general rule, showing how you found it. **[3]**
**(b)** Prove that the difference between the (n + 1)th term and the nth term is 4n + 2. **[3]**

**5.** Solve the simultaneous equations 2x + 5y = 13 and 3x − y = 11. **[4]**

**6.** **(a)** Solve 3 − 4x > 19. **[2]**
**(b)** Solve −3 ≤ 3x + 4 < 16 and list the integers that satisfy it. **[3]**

**7.** **(a)** Factorize 14m²n − 21mn². **[2]**
**(b)** Factorize 5x² + 8x − 4. **[3]**

**8.** **(a)** Solve x² − 2x − 35 = 0 by factorization. **[3]**
**(b)** Use the quadratic formula to solve 2x² − 5x − 1 = 0. Give answers to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(c)** The graph of y = x² + 2x − 15 passes through (−5, 0) and (3, 0). Write down the solutions of x² + 2x − 15 = 0 and the equation of the line of symmetry. **[2]**

**9.** A rectangular banner is 6 m longer than it is wide. Its area is 112 m². Form and solve a quadratic equation to find the width, then find the perimeter. **[6]**

**10.** **(a)** Make h the subject of S = 2πr² + 2πrh. **[3]**
**(b)** Make x the subject of y = (4x + 3)/(x − 2). **[4]**

**11.** An algorithm starts with D = 1000 and k = 0. It repeats these steps: replace D with 0.7 × D, add 1 to k, and stop if D is less than 200. At the end it outputs k.
**(a)** Complete a trace table for D and k. **[3]**
**(b)** State the output. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the type of sequence D follows and give its common ratio. **[1]**

**12.** *Extended and enrichment.* An arithmetic sequence has first term 9 and common difference 4. A geometric sequence has first term 96 and common ratio 1/2.
**(a)** Find the 30th term of the arithmetic sequence. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the sum of its first 30 terms. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the 7th term of the geometric sequence. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the sum to infinity of the geometric series and explain why it exists. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** 5x − 10 − 6x + 8 [1] = **−x − 2** [1]
**(b)** 4(−3)² = 36 and −3(−3)(2) = +18 [1]; total **54** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep brackets around negative values when substituting. Writing −3² instead of (−3)² is a common way to lose the first step.

**2.** Let m be the number of months. 25 + 18m = 40 + 13m [1]. 5m = 15 [1]. **m = 3** months [1]. Cost: 25 + 18(3) = £79, and Gym B gives 40 + 39 = £79, so **£79** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* State the unknown first. Checking the answer in both expressions shows the cost really is the same and is worth including.

**3.** **(a)** The difference is 7, so the rule is 7n + c [1]. At n = 1, 7 + c = 4, so **7n − 3** [1]
**(b)** 7n − 3 = 207 gives n = 30, a whole number, so **207 is a term** [1]. 7n − 3 = 300 gives n = 303/7 = 43.29..., not a whole number, so **300 is not a term** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A reason such as "n must be a whole number" is needed; a yes/no on its own gives little to credit.

**4.** **(a)** First differences 6, 10, 14, 18; second differences are all 4 [1]. So the rule contains 2n², which gives 2, 8, 18, 32, 50 [1]. Each term is 1 more: **2n² + 1** [1]
**(b)** The nth term is 2n² + 1 and the (n + 1)th is 2(n + 1)² + 1 [1]. Difference: 2(n² + 2n + 1) + 1 − (2n² + 1) [1] = **4n + 2** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A proof must work for every n. Testing n = 1 and n = 2 only verifies; the algebra is what proves.

**5.** Multiply the second equation by 5: 15x − 5y = 55 [1]. Add to the first: 17x = 68 [1]. **x = 4** [1]. Substitute: 12 − y = 11, so **y = 1** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Once you have x and y, test them in the equation you did not use to find y; a slip in either value shows up straight away.

**6.** **(a)** −4x > 16 [1]; dividing by −4 reverses the sign: **x < −4** [1]
**(b)** Subtract 4: −7 ≤ 3x < 12 [1]. Divide by 3: **−7/3 ≤ x < 4** [1]. Integers: **−2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Listing integers tests the end points. 4 is excluded because of the strict sign, and −7/3 is about −2.33, so −2 is the smallest integer that fits.

**7.** **(a)** Common factor 7mn [1]: **7mn(2m − 3n)** [1]
**(b)** Two numbers with product 5 × (−4) = −20 and sum 8 are 10 and −2 [1]. 5x² + 10x − 2x − 4 = 5x(x + 2) − 2(x + 2) [1] = **(5x − 2)(x + 2)** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Take out the highest common factor, not just any factor. Expand your brackets to check.

**8.** **(a)** (x − 7)(x + 5) = 0 [1]; x − 7 = 0 or x + 5 = 0 [1]; **x = 7 or x = −5** [1]
**(b)** a = 2, b = −5, c = −1 so x = (5 ± √(25 + 8)) / 4 = (5 ± √33) / 4 [1]. x = 2.686... [1] and x = −0.186..., so **x = 2.69 or x = −0.186** [1]
**(c)** The solutions are the x-intercepts: **x = −5 and x = 3** [1]. The line of symmetry is halfway between: **x = −1** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Substitute b = −5 with its bracket so −b becomes +5 and 4ac becomes −4(2)(−1) = +8. Round only at the end.

**9.** Let w be the width in metres; the length is w + 6. w(w + 6) = 112 [1]. w² + 6w − 112 = 0 [1]. (w + 14)(w − 8) = 0 [1]. w = −14 or 8 [1]. A width cannot be negative, so **w = 8 m** and the length is 14 m [1]. Perimeter 2(8 + 14) = **44 m** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Rejecting the negative root with a reason connected to the context is part of a complete solution. Check that 8 × 14 gives 112.

**10.** **(a)** S − 2πr² = 2πrh [1]. Divide by 2πr [1]. **h = (S − 2πr²) / (2πr)** [1]
**(b)** y(x − 2) = 4x + 3 [1]. yx − 2y = 4x + 3 [1]. yx − 4x = 3 + 2y, so x(y − 4) = 3 + 2y [1]. **x = (2y + 3)/(y − 4)** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* When the subject appears twice, collect those terms on one side and factorize. Test with a value: x = 5 gives y = 23/3, and the formula returns 5.

**11.** **(a)** Pass 1: D = 700, k = 1; pass 2: D = 490, k = 2 [1]. Pass 3: D = 343, k = 3; pass 4: D = 240.1, k = 4 [1]. Pass 5: D = 168.07, k = 5, and 168.07 is below 200, so it stops [1]
**(b)** The output is **5** [1]
**(c)** A **geometric** sequence with common ratio **0.7** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Record each variable after each step and apply the stopping test to the new value. Testing the old value gives an output that is one pass too late.

**12.** **(a)** a₃₀ = 9 + 29 × 4 [1] = **125** [1]
**(b)** S₃₀ = (30/2)(9 + 125) [1] = **2010** [1]
**(c)** a₇ = 96 × (1/2)⁶ [1] = **1.5** [1]
**(d)** S∞ = 96 / (1 − 1/2) = **192** [1]. It exists because the common ratio, 1/2, lies between −1 and 1, so the terms shrink towards zero and the series converges [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the correct power: the nth term uses rⁿ⁻¹, so the 7th term uses the 6th power. Give a reason for convergence that refers to the ratio.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not defining the unknown or not writing the equation before solving it.
- Forgetting to check a context answer, such as a negative width or a non-integer term number.
- Reversing the inequality sign at the wrong time, or not reversing it after dividing by a negative.
- Giving only one root of a quadratic, or not rejecting an impossible one.
- Treating a pattern that fits a few terms as proven.
- Losing a sign when substituting a negative, especially in the quadratic formula.
- Using the nth power instead of the (n − 1)th in a geometric term.
- Stopping a trace table one pass early or late.
