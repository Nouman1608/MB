## Questions

### Section A: short answer

**1.** Identify two roles of marketing. **[2]**

**2.** Define the term **market share**. **[2]**

**3.** Halden Juice had sales revenue of $840,000 last year. Total sales revenue in the juice market was $5.6m. Calculate Halden Juice's market share. **[2]**

**4.** Define the term **niche market**. **[2]**

**5.** Identify the basis of segmentation used in each example.

**(a)** A supermarket sells a budget range and a luxury range of the same foods. **[1]**

**(b)** A clothing firm sends heavy coats only to its shops in mountain towns. **[1]**

**(c)** A travel company designs holidays for people who enjoy hiking and camping. **[1]**

**6.** Outline one reason why consumer spending patterns may change. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** The table shows sales revenue in the market for phone cases.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Ardley Cases sales revenue | $2.4m | $2.6m |
| Bexmoor Cases sales revenue | $4.0m | $6.0m |
| Total market sales revenue | $16.0m | $20.0m |

**(a)** Calculate Ardley Cases' market share in Year 1 and in Year 2. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate Bexmoor Cases' market share in Year 1 and in Year 2. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why Ardley Cases' managers should be concerned, even though its sales revenue increased. **[3]**

**8.** Explain how anticipating changes in customer needs could benefit a toy manufacturer. **[3]**

**9.** Tavish Coffee is a single coffee shop. A large coffee chain has opened a branch on the same street, selling coffee at lower prices.

**(a)** Explain two ways Tavish Coffee could respond to the increased competition. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain one reason why cutting prices may not be the best response for Tavish Coffee. **[2]**

**10.** Fennick Tailoring is a small business that makes made-to-measure suits for wealthy customers. Each suit takes a skilled tailor three weeks to make. The owner is thinking of producing ready-made suits in standard sizes to sell in the mass market.

**(a)** State one advantage to Fennick Tailoring of operating in a niche market. **[1]**

**(b)** Consider whether Fennick Tailoring should move into the mass market. Justify your answer. **[6]**

**11.** Galloway Cinemas is thinking of segmenting its market, with separate film times and prices for families, students and retired people.

**(a)** Explain one advantage to Galloway Cinemas of segmenting its market. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain one disadvantage to Galloway Cinemas of segmenting its market. **[3]**

**12.** Morland Bikes has an 8% share of a bicycle market worth $12.5m.

**(a)** Calculate Morland Bikes' sales revenue. **[1]**

**(b)** The market is expected to grow by 10% next year. Calculate Morland Bikes' sales revenue next year if its market share stays the same. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two from: identifying customer needs; satisfying customer needs; maintaining customer loyalty; building customer relationships; anticipating changes in customer needs. One mark for the first role named [1] and one for a second, different role [1]. **[2]**

**2.** The sales of one business [1] as a percentage of the total sales of the whole market [1]. **[2]**

**3.** 840,000 / 5,600,000 x 100 [1] = **15.0%** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** A small, specialised part of a larger market [1] in which customers have particular needs or wants [1]. **[2]**

**5.** **(a)** Income [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Location [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** Lifestyle [1]. **[1]**

**6.** A rise or fall in people's incomes [1] changes how much they can afford, so they may switch to dearer or cheaper products [1]. Also accept a change in tastes or fashion, the age structure of the population, technology, or the relative prices of products, with an outline of its effect. **[2]**

**7.** **(a)** Year 1: 2.4 / 16.0 x 100 = **15.0%** [1]. Year 2: 2.6 / 20.0 x 100 = **13.0%** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Year 1: 4.0 / 16.0 x 100 = **25.0%** [1]. Year 2: 6.0 / 20.0 x 100 = **30.0%** [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Ardley's market share fell from 15.0% to 13.0% [1], because the market grew by 25% while Ardley's sales grew by only about 8% [1]. Bexmoor's share rose to 30.0%, so Ardley is losing ground to a competitor and may fall further behind if the trend continues [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Comparing the two growth rates, or naming the rival that gained, turns a description into an explanation.

**8.** A toy maker that spots a change early, such as growing interest in educational toys [1], can develop new products before its competitors [1]. It can then win sales and customer loyalty while rivals are still catching up, instead of being left with toys that fewer children want [1]. **[3]**

**9.** **(a)** Improve the service or atmosphere, such as friendly staff who know regulars [1], so that customers value Tavish's personal service more than the chain's lower price [1]. Introduce a loyalty card [1], which rewards customers for returning and makes them less likely to try the chain [1]. Also accept other responses, such as new or specialist drinks, or targeting a segment the chain ignores, each with its explanation. **[4]**

**(b)** The large chain probably has lower costs per cup from buying in bulk, so it can keep cutting prices for longer [1]. Tavish could lose profit on every cup and still not win the price war [1]. **[2]**

**10.** **(a)** Any one: fewer competitors; customers will pay high prices; loyal customers; a specialist reputation [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** For the move: the mass market has many more customers, so Fennick could sell far more suits than it does now [1], which could raise revenue and reduce dependence on a few wealthy customers [1]. Against the move: a small business making suits by hand over three weeks would have high costs per suit and could not compete on price with large manufacturers [1], and it could damage its exclusive brand image with its existing wealthy customers [1]. Judgement: Fennick should stay in its niche [1], because its skills and high prices suit specialist customers, and its costs would make it unable to compete in a mass market against large firms [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* A justified decision can go either way. A recommendation to move would need a plan for lower costs, such as buying in machinery, to be credible.

**11.** **(a)** Galloway can set prices each group will pay, such as lower student tickets on quiet weekday afternoons [1]. This fills seats at times that would otherwise be empty [1], increasing total revenue without much extra cost, since the film is shown anyway [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Each segment needs its own promotion, such as separate adverts for students and for retired people [1]. This raises Galloway's marketing costs [1], and if a segment turns out to be small, the extra cost may be greater than the extra revenue [1]. **[3]**

**12.** **(a)** 8 / 100 x 12,500,000 = **$1,000,000** [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** New market size = 12.5m x 1.10 = $13.75m [1]. Sales revenue = 8 / 100 x 13,750,000 = **$1,100,000** [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying a business is "doing well" because sales rose, without checking market share.
- Using units for the business and dollars for the market in the same calculation.
- Defining a niche as just "a small market".
- Listing responses to competition without saying how each would help the named business.
- Weighing only one side in a "consider" question, or ending without a justified decision.
- Confusing lifestyle with age when identifying a basis of segmentation.
