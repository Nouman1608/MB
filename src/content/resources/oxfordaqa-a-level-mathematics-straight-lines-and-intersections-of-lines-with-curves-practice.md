## Questions

**1.** The points A and B have coordinates (−4, 3) and (2, −1).

**(a)** Find the gradient of AB. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the mid-point of AB. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the length of AB, giving your answer as a surd in its simplest form. **[2]**

**2.** **(a)** Find the gradient and the y-intercept of the line 6x − 4y + 9 = 0. **[2]**
**(b)** Find an equation of the line through (3, −2) with gradient −5/4, in the form ax + by + c = 0 where a, b and c are integers. **[3]**

**3.** The line l passes through P(−2, 9) and Q(6, −3).

**(a)** Find an equation of l in the form ax + by + c = 0, where a, b and c are integers. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of the points where l meets the axes. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the area of the triangle bounded by l and the two axes. **[2]**

**4.** Three lines have equations L₁: 4x − 6y + 5 = 0, L₂: 3x + 2y − 8 = 0 and L₃: 2x − 3y = 1.

**(a)** Identify, with reasons, a pair of parallel lines and a pair of perpendicular lines. **[4]**
**(b)** Find an equation of the line through (6, −2) perpendicular to L₁, in the form ax + by + c = 0. **[3]**

**5.** The points A and B have coordinates (1, −2) and (7, 6).

**(a)** Find an equation of the perpendicular bisector of AB, in the form ax + by + c = 0 with integer coefficients. **[4]**
**(b)** The point C is (8, −1). Show that C lies on the perpendicular bisector, and show that CA = CB. **[2]**

**6.** The line y = 2x + 2 meets the curve y = x² − 3x − 4 at the points M and N.

**(a)** Find the coordinates of M and N. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the mid-point of MN and the exact length of MN. **[3]**

**7.** Find the coordinates of the points where the curve y = 18/x meets the line 2x + y = 15. **[5]**

**8.** The line y = kx + 3 is a tangent to the curve y = x² + 4x + 7.

**(a)** Find the two possible values of k. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of the point of contact for each value of k. **[3]**

**9.** The line y = x + c and the curve y = 2x² − 3x + 1 are given. Find the values of c for which the line

**(a)** meets the curve at two distinct points, **(b)** is a tangent to the curve, **(c)** does not meet the curve. **[5]**

**10.** By solving a suitable equation, show that the line y = −2 touches the curve y = x³ − 3x at one point and crosses it at another, and give the coordinates of both points. **[5]**

**11.** The points P, Q and R have coordinates (1, 1), (5, 3) and (2, 9). The quadrilateral PQRS is a rectangle.

**(a)** Show that PQ is perpendicular to QR. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of S. **[2]**
**(c)** Find an equation of the diagonal PR in the form ax + by + c = 0. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the area of the rectangle. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Gradient = (−1 − 3)/(2 − (−4)) = −4/6 = **−2/3** [1]
**(b)** Mid-point = ((−4 + 2)/2, (3 − 1)/2) = **(−1, 1)** [1]
**(c)** AB² = 6² + (−4)² = 36 + 16 = 52 [1], so AB = √52 = **2√13** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep brackets round negative coordinates, and simplify the surd if the question asks for simplest form, because the question asks for a simplified surd, so leaving √52 would not answer it fully.

**2. (a)** Rearrange: 4y = 6x + 9, so y = (3/2)x + 9/4 [1]. Gradient **3/2**, y-intercept **9/4** [1]
**(b)** y + 2 = −(5/4)(x − 3) [1]
4y + 8 = −5x + 15 [1]
**5x + 4y − 7 = 0** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Showing the line in y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) form first makes your method visible and easier to check if the rearrangement into integers slips.

**3. (a)** Gradient = (−3 − 9)/(6 − (−2)) = −12/8 = −3/2 [1]
y − 9 = −(3/2)(x + 2), so 2y − 18 = −3x − 6 [1]
**3x + 2y − 12 = 0** [1]
**(b)** When y = 0, x = 4. When x = 0, y = 6. [1] The points are **(4, 0) and (0, 6)** [1]
**(c)** The triangle has base 4 and height 6 and a right angle at the origin [1], so area = ½ × 4 × 6 = **12** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check your equation by substituting the second point, and use the axis intercepts from your own equation so an early slip is easy to spot.

**4. (a)** L₁: y = (2/3)x + 5/6, gradient 2/3. L₂: y = −(3/2)x + 4, gradient −3/2. L₃: y = (2/3)x − 1/3, gradient 2/3. [2]
L₁ and L₃ have equal gradients, so they are **parallel** [1]. L₁ and L₂ have gradients with product (2/3)(−3/2) = −1, so they are **perpendicular** (L₂ and L₃ are also perpendicular) [1]
**(b)** Perpendicular gradient = −3/2 [1]
y + 2 = −(3/2)(x − 6), so 2y + 4 = −3x + 18 [1]
**3x + 2y − 14 = 0** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give the gradients as evidence for each conclusion. A statement that lines "look parallel" without gradients is unlikely to be enough.

**5. (a)** Mid-point = (4, 2) [1]. Gradient AB = 8/6 = 4/3 [1]. Perpendicular gradient = −3/4 [1]
y − 2 = −(3/4)(x − 4), so 4y − 8 = −3x + 12, giving **3x + 4y − 20 = 0** [1]
**(b)** 3(8) + 4(−1) − 20 = 24 − 4 − 20 = 0, so C lies on the line [1]. CA² = 7² + 1² = 50 and CB² = 1² + 7² = 50, so **CA = CB** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" part, write the substitution line and the conclusion explicitly rather than just stating that the answer is zero.

**6. (a)** x² − 3x − 4 = 2x + 2 [1]
x² − 5x − 6 = 0, so (x − 6)(x + 1) = 0 [1]
x = 6 or x = −1 [1]
Using y = 2x + 2: **M(−1, 0) and N(6, 14)** [1]
**(b)** Mid-point = **(5/2, 7)** [1]. MN² = 7² + 14² = 245 [1], so MN = **7√5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Substitute the x-values into the line, not the curve, and check them in the curve afterwards. A coordinate pair is needed for each point.

**7.** Substitute y = 15 − 2x: 18/x = 15 − 2x [1]
18 = 15x − 2x², so 2x² − 15x + 18 = 0 [1]
(2x − 3)(x − 6) = 0 [1]
x = 3/2 or x = 6 [1]
y = 15 − 2x gives **(3/2, 12) and (6, 3)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Multiply through by x only after you have written one equation in x. Give both coordinates for both points.

**8. (a)** x² + 4x + 7 = kx + 3, so x² + (4 − k)x + 4 = 0 [1]
Equal roots: (4 − k)² − 16 = 0 [1]
(4 − k)² = 16, so 4 − k = ±4 [1]
**k = 0 or k = 8** [1]
**(b)** The repeated root is x = −(4 − k)/2. For k = 0, x = −2 and y = 3, giving **(−2, 3)** [1]. For k = 8, x = 2 and y = 8(2) + 3 = 19, giving **(2, 19)** [1]. Check: both points satisfy y = x² + 4x + 7 [1]
*Examiner insight:* The tangent condition comes from the discriminant of the combined quadratic. Writing "equal roots" before setting it to zero shows your reasoning.

**9.** x + c = 2x² − 3x + 1, so 2x² − 4x + (1 − c) = 0 [1]
Discriminant = 16 − 8(1 − c) = 8 + 8c [1]
**(a)** Two distinct points: 8 + 8c > 0, so **c > −1** [1]
**(b)** Tangent: 8 + 8c = 0, so **c = −1** [1]
**(c)** No intersection: 8 + 8c < 0, so **c < −1** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Mark the strict or equals sign for each case carefully. Tangent is equal to zero, not greater than.

**10.** x³ − 3x = −2, so x³ − 3x + 2 = 0 [1]
x = 1 gives 1 − 3 + 2 = 0, so (x − 1) is a factor, and x³ − 3x + 2 = (x − 1)²(x + 2) [2]
The repeated root x = 1 means the line **touches** the curve at **(1, −2)** [1]. The single root x = −2 means it **crosses** at **(−2, −2)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Say what the repeated root means in words, not only the roots. A line through a point where the curve turns is the usual picture for a touch.

**11. (a)** Gradient PQ = 2/4 = 1/2 [1]. Gradient QR = 6/(−3) = −2 [1]. Product = −1, so **PQ is perpendicular to QR** [1]
**(b)** Mid-point of PR = (3/2, 5) [1]. Diagonals bisect each other, so the mid-point of QS is (3/2, 5), giving S = (3 − 5, 10 − 3) = **(−2, 7)** [1]
**(c)** Gradient PR = 8/1 = 8 [1]. y − 1 = 8(x − 1), so **8x − y − 7 = 0** [1]
**(d)** PQ = √(16 + 4) = 2√5 and QR = √(9 + 36) = 3√5 [1]. Area = 2√5 × 3√5 = **30** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the earlier parts. Finding S from the diagonal mid-point is quicker than solving gradient conditions, and it reuses the mid-point formula.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Mixing the order of subtraction in the gradient formula so the sign is wrong.
* Reading the gradient of ax + by + c = 0 as a/b instead of −a/b.
* Using the reciprocal of the gradient without changing its sign for a perpendicular line.
* Using a point other than the mid-point when building a perpendicular bisector.
* Giving only x-values for points of intersection.
* Substituting into the curve instead of the line, which makes the arithmetic harder.
* Applying the discriminant to the curve itself instead of the quadratic after substitution.
* Writing "greater than 0" for a tangent or "equal to 0" for two intersections.
* Expanding (4 − k)² incorrectly, or dividing only some terms when clearing fractions.
* Leaving answers as decimals when exact surds or fractions were expected.
