## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define market segmentation. **[2]**

**2.** Identify **two** ways a business can maintain customer loyalty. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** reasons why customer spending patterns may change. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **one** benefit of niche marketing. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why many markets have become more competitive. **[2]**

**6.** Outline **two** benefits of market segmentation to a business. **[4]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** Copperleaf Teas is a small business that sells specialist herbal teas online to customers who want caffeine-free drinks.

**(a)** Explain **two** benefits to Copperleaf of niche marketing.

**(b)** Explain **one** limitation to Copperleaf of niche marketing. **[6]**

**8.** Hallam Shoes sells three ranges of shoes.

| Range | Sales last year ($) | Sales this year ($) |
|---|---|---|
| Children's | 120,000 | 108,000 |
| Women's | 200,000 | 250,000 |
| Men's | 80,000 | 84,000 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in sales of each range.

**(b)** Recommend which range Hallam should focus its marketing on next year. Justify your answer. **[7]**

**9.** The Regent is a small town cinema. Over five years its ticket sales have fallen as more people watch films at home through online streaming services.

**(a)** Explain **two** reasons why customers' spending on cinema tickets may have changed.

**(b)** Consider **two** ways The Regent could respond to the increased competition. Recommend which it should choose. Justify your answer. **[10]**

**10.** **(a)** Explain the difference between maintaining customer loyalty and building customer relationships.

**(b)** Explain **two** ways a hairdresser could build relationships with its customers. **[6]**

**11.** Sunward Travel sells two types of holiday: budget beach holidays costing about $600 per person and luxury cruises costing about $5,000 per person. Its research shows that the cruise customers are mostly in high-income professional jobs, while beach holiday customers come from a wide range of jobs. Recommend a method of segmentation for Sunward. Justify your answer. **[6]**

**12.** Explain **two** limitations of mass marketing for a large soft-drinks producer. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Dividing a market into groups of customers with similar characteristics [1], so that products and marketing can be aimed at each group [1].

**2.** Any two [1] [1]: loyalty or reward schemes; consistent product quality; good after-sales service; personal offers to regular customers; dealing quickly with complaints.

**3.** Any two [1] [1]: changes in income; changes in tastes or fashion; population changes; new technology; health awareness; changes in the prices of rival products.

**4.** One benefit identified [1] and explained [1]: there is less competition in a small segment, so the business can charge a higher price; or customers' needs are met closely, so they become loyal.

**5.** One reason identified [1] and explained [1]: online selling lets customers compare and buy from businesses anywhere, so local firms face many more rivals; or imports from foreign producers increase choice; or new businesses can enter more easily.

**6.** Two benefits, each identified [1] and outlined [1]: products can be designed to fit each group's needs, increasing sales; promotion is aimed only at likely buyers, so less money is wasted; prices can be set to match what each segment will pay; segments not yet served can be identified as new opportunities.

**7.** **(a)** Two benefits, each identified [1] and explained [1]: few large producers target caffeine-free herbal teas, so Copperleaf faces less competition; customers who need caffeine-free drinks may be willing to pay higher prices for a specialist product; its customers are likely to be loyal because the product meets a specific need; it can reach this small group cheaply through targeted online promotion.

**(b)** One limitation identified [1] and explained [1]: the number of customers is small, which limits sales growth; or if a large tea company launches a similar range, Copperleaf may lose customers to a cheaper, well-known brand; or if the trend for caffeine-free drinks fades, sales could fall sharply.

**8.** **(a)** Children's: (108,000 − 120,000) ÷ 120,000 × 100 = **−10%** [1]. Women's: (250,000 − 200,000) ÷ 200,000 × 100 = **+25%** [1]. Men's: (84,000 − 80,000) ÷ 80,000 × 100 = **+5%** [1].

**(b)** Women's range: it is the largest range and grew fastest, by 25% [1], so marketing spending is likely to bring the biggest return [1]. Children's range: sales fell by 10%, so it may need marketing to reverse the decline, or the fall may show changing needs that marketing cannot fix [1]. Recommendation: focus on the women's range, since it is growing strongly, but research why children's sales fell before deciding whether to cut it [1].

**9.** **(a)** Two reasons, each identified [1] and explained [1]: new technology lets customers watch many films at home for one monthly payment, which may be cheaper than cinema tickets for a family; tastes have changed, with people preferring to choose when and where they watch; falling incomes may lead families to cut spending on outings.

**(b)** Response 1, for example screening special events or offering comfortable seating and food: this gives an experience streaming cannot copy [1], but costs money to set up [1]. Response 2, for example lower ticket prices on weekday afternoons: this could attract students and older people [1], but lower prices reduce revenue per ticket and may not cover costs [1]. Recommendation: improve the experience, because The Regent cannot compete with streaming on price but can offer something different that customers will pay more for [1]; this depends on whether it can afford the investment [1].

**10.** **(a)** Maintaining loyalty means keeping customers buying repeatedly from the business [1]; building relationships means creating an ongoing personal connection, such as regular contact and trust, which encourages that loyalty [1].

**(b)** Two ways, each identified [1] and explained [1]: keep a record of each customer's preferred style and stylist so visits feel personal; send reminders or birthday offers by text; ask for feedback after appointments and act on it; offer advice on hair care between visits.

**11.** Socio-economic grouping is suitable because the two holidays differ greatly in price [1] and the research shows cruise customers are high-income professionals [1]. Sunward could promote cruises in media read by professionals and stress luxury and service [1], and promote beach holidays to a wider group stressing value for money [1]. Age could also be used, since families and retired couples want different holidays [1], but income is the clearest difference between Sunward's two products, so socio-economic grouping should be used first [1].

**12.** Two limitations, each identified [1] and explained [1]: many large rivals sell similar drinks, so competition is fierce and often on price, reducing profit margins; national advertising campaigns are very expensive; one product for everyone may not fully satisfy any group, such as customers wanting low-sugar drinks, who may switch to specialist brands.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating percentage change from the new figure instead of the old one in question 8.
- Assuming the fastest-growing range is the only sensible focus, without considering why another range fell.
- Recommending price cuts in question 9 without considering costs.
- Treating loyalty and relationships as the same idea in question 10.
- Naming a segmentation method without using the research given in question 11.
