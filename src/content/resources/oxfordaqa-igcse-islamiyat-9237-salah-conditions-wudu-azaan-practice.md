## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In
the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for explaining it, up to two marks
for each of the two. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2
marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use
applied to the question. The 12-mark questions are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** Tayammum [1].

**2.** Two [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: making the intention; washing the hands; rinsing the mouth; rinsing the nose; washing the face; washing the arms to the elbows; wiping the head; wiping the ears; washing the feet to the ankles.

**4.** Any two, [1] each, in Arabic or English: *Allahu Akbar*; *Ashhadu an la ilaha illallah*; *Ashhadu anna Muhammadan rasulullah*; *Hayya 'alas-salah*; *Hayya 'alal-falah*; *La ilaha illallah*; *as-salatu khayrun min an-nawm* (Fajr).

**5.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Command of Allah** [1]: "turn your faces toward it" (2:144), so facing the Ka'aba is obedience [1].
- **Unity of the Ummah** [1]: Muslims everywhere turn to the same point, showing they are one community [1].
- **Link to Ibraheem (AS)** [1]: the Ka'aba was built by Ibraheem and Isma'il (AS) for the worship of one God, so facing it connects Muslims with their monotheism [1].
- **Focus** [1]: one fixed direction helps concentration and order in congregational rows [1].

**6.** For each of two differences: difference identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Shi'a addition to the Azaan** [1]: Shi'a Muslims add *Hayya 'ala khayr al-'amal*, which Sunnis do not [1].
- **The feet in Wudu** [1]: Sunni practice washes the feet, while Shi'a practice wipes over them, reading Qur'an 5:6 differently [1].
- **What breaks Wudu** [1]: Hanafis hold that flowing blood breaks Wudu; Shafi'is hold that touching a non-mahram of the opposite sex does [1].

**7.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **It is a condition of Salah** [1]: Qur'an 5:6 commands it, so a prayer without it is not valid [1].
- **Physical cleanliness** [1]: washing removes dirt before standing before Allah [1].
- **Spiritual preparation** [1]: the routine calms the mind and turns the worshipper's attention from daily life to Allah [1].
- **Following the Sunnah** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) showed the full method, so Muslims copy his example [1].

**8.** For each of two differences: difference identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Reward** [1]: congregational prayer in the mosque is twenty-seven times better than praying alone, according to the Hadith [1].
- **Leadership** [1]: in the mosque Muslims follow an imam in rows; at home a person may pray alone or lead the family [1].
- **Community** [1]: the mosque brings Muslims together and allows them to share news and support, while home prayer is private [1].
- **Voluntary prayers** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) encouraged offering some prayers at home so that houses are not like graves [1].

### Section B

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **Allah wants ease, not hardship** [1]: rules are adapted so that worship never becomes impossible [1].
- **Salah is never dropped** [1]: shortening or sitting keeps the duty alive in every situation, showing how central prayer is [1].
- **Islam is practical** [1]: it takes account of real lives, journeys and illness [1].
- **Mercy of Allah** [1]: the concessions reflect Allah as Ar-Rahman and Ar-Raheem [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "There is no blame upon you for shortening the prayer" when travelling (Qur'an 4:101), so a traveller who prays two rak'ahs is obeying, not cutting corners [2].
- The Prophet (pbuh) told a sick companion to pray standing, then sitting, then on his side if needed (Sahih al-Bukhari), so the ill keep praying in the way they can [2].

**10.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 9. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 9.

Reasons (AO1):

- **Remembrance through the day** [1]: prayers from dawn to night mean a Muslim is never long without thinking of Allah [1].
- **Discipline** [1]: stopping what one is doing at set times trains self-control [1].
- **Unity** [1]: Muslims in one place pray at the same times, which makes congregational prayer possible [1].
- **Obedience** [1]: the times are part of the command itself, so praying on time shows submission [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Indeed, prayer has been decreed upon the believers a decree of specified times" (Qur'an 4:103) [2].
- "Prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing" (Qur'an 29:45): regular prayer through the day keeps a Muslim's behaviour in check [2].

**11.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- Fixed prayer times mean Muslims **pause work** five times a day but return to it, so worship and work are woven together.
- Islam does **not ask people to leave the world**; earning an honest living is itself rewarded.
- Jummah shows the **priority**: when the call comes, worship comes first.
- Against the statement: some would say Salah is about worship, not balance; its main purpose is obedience and closeness to Allah, while balance is a side effect. Others argue that in modern workplaces the balance is hard to keep.

*Use of the passage*

- "Leave trade" at the Friday call shows that work must give way to prayer.
- "Disperse within the land and seek from the bounty of Allah" shows that after prayer, work is encouraged.
- "Remember Allah often that you may succeed" shows that remembrance continues during work.
- "They rushed to it and left you standing" warns against letting business crowd out worship.
- "Allah is the best of providers" reassures Muslims that time given to prayer does not reduce their provision.

**12.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- Congregational prayer is **twenty-seven times** better, according to the Hadith.
- The mosque **builds community**, equality and discipline in the rows.
- Jummah **must** be prayed in congregation by those obliged to attend.
- Against the statement: Muslims may pray anywhere clean; the Prophet (pbuh) encouraged some prayers at home; many women, the ill and those far from a mosque pray at home with full validity; sincerity matters more than place.
- A balanced conclusion: the mosque carries extra reward for obligatory prayers, especially for men, but home prayer is valid and has its own value.

*Use of the passage*

- "Proceed to the remembrance of Allah" shows that Jummah involves going out to gather with others.
- The call to "leave trade" shows that congregational worship takes priority over private business.
- "Disperse within the land" after prayer shows that the mosque is a place to come to and go out from, not a place to stay away from family and work.
- The passage concerns Friday; it does not say daily prayers must be in the mosque, which supports the counter-view.

An answer that explains a range of points, reasons about their implications and uses the passage directly reaches Level 4.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that concessions excuse the ill from praying.
- Listing Wudu actions when the question asks why Wudu matters.
- Confusing the Azaan with the Iqamah.
- Giving the Jummah passage as evidence for daily prayer times without saying how it applies.
- In the 12-mark questions, giving only one side or not quoting the passage.
