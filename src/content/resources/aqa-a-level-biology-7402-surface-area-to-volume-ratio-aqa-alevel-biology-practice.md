These questions cover AQA A-level Biology (7402) section 3.3.1, *Surface area to volume ratio*, including calculating surface areas and volumes of regular shapes. All data are invented. Formulae: sphere surface area 4π*r*², volume (4/3)π*r*³; cylinder surface area 2π*r*² + 2π*rh*, volume π*r*²*h*. Teaching is in the [surface area to volume ratio study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-surface-area-to-volume-ratio-aqa-alevel-biology/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-surface-area-to-volume-ratio-aqa-alevel-biology-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** **(a)** Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of a cube with sides of 3 mm. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the surface area to volume ratio of a cube decreases as its side length increases. **[2]**

**2.** A spherical single-celled organism has a radius of 10 µm. Calculate its surface area to volume ratio. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[3]**

**3.** A plant root hair is modelled as a cylinder of radius 0.5 mm and length 4 mm. Calculate its surface area to volume ratio. Show your working. **[3]**

**4.** A cell is shaped like a cuboid measuring 20 µm × 10 µm × 5 µm. Calculate its surface area to volume ratio. **[2]**

**5.** A spherical cell grows from a radius of 5 µm to a radius of 10 µm. Calculate the ratio of the new surface area to volume ratio to the old one, and state what this means for the cell. **[2]**

**6.** A cube and a cuboid each have a volume of 8 cm³. The cube is 2 cm × 2 cm × 2 cm and the cuboid is 1 cm × 2 cm × 4 cm. Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of the cuboid and compare it with the cube. **[3]**

## Section B: longer questions

**7.** Flatworms are thin, flat animals with no specialised gas exchange surface and no blood system. Explain how the shape of a flatworm allows it to survive without these. **[3]**

**8.** A student cut agar containing an indicator into cubes and placed them in dilute acid. The indicator changes colour when acid reaches it. The results are shown.

| Cube side / cm | Time for whole cube to change colour / min |
|---|---|
| 1 | 3 |
| 2 | 11 |
| 3 | 26 |

**(a)** Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of each cube. **[2]**

**(b)** Use the results to explain why large organisms cannot rely on diffusion across their body surface. **[3]**

**9.** Three mammals of different body mass were kept at the same air temperature, and their oxygen use was measured.

| Animal | Body mass / kg | Oxygen use / cm³ per g per hour |
|---|---|---|
| A | 0.02 | 7.0 |
| B | 2 | 1.6 |
| C | 200 | 0.4 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship between body mass and oxygen use per gram. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain the relationship. **[3]**

**(c)** A student concluded that animal C uses less oxygen in total than animal A. Use the data to show that this conclusion is wrong. **[2]**

**10.** Two closely related species of fox live in different habitats. One lives in a hot desert and has very large ears. The other lives in the Arctic and has small ears. Suggest how ear size helps each species to survive in its habitat. **[3]**

**11.** Using the idea of surface area to volume ratio, explain why a large multicellular animal needs both specialised exchange surfaces and a mass transport system. **[5]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Surface area = 6 × 3² = 54 mm² and volume = 3³ = 27 mm³ [1]; SA:V = **2 : 1** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Surface area increases with the square of the side length, but volume increases with the cube [1], so volume increases faster than surface area and the ratio falls [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* "Volume gets bigger" is not enough; say it increases faster than surface area.

**2.** Surface area = 4π(10)² = 1257 µm² [1]; volume = (4/3)π(10)³ = 4189 µm³ [1]; SA:V = 1257 ÷ 4189 = **0.30 : 1** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* For a sphere SA:V = 3 ÷ *r*, which gives the same 0.30 and is a good check.

**3.** Surface area = 2π(0.5)² + 2π(0.5)(4) = 0.5π + 4π = 4.5π = 14.1 mm² [1]; volume = π(0.5)²(4) = π = 3.14 mm³ [1]; SA:V = 14.1 ÷ 3.14 = **4.5 : 1** [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Surface area = 2(20 × 10 + 20 × 5 + 10 × 5) = 2(200 + 100 + 50) = 700 µm²; volume = 1000 µm³ [1]; SA:V = **0.7 : 1** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** SA:V = 3 ÷ *r*, so it falls from 0.6 to 0.3, a ratio of **0.5** (it halves) [1]. Each unit of volume now has half as much membrane for exchange, so exchange per unit volume is slower [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Cuboid surface area = 2(2 + 4 + 8) = 28 cm² [1]; SA:V = 28 ÷ 8 = **3.5 : 1** [1]. The cube has 24 ÷ 8 = 3 : 1, so the more elongated cuboid has the larger ratio for the same volume [1]. **[3]**

**7.** A flat shape gives a large surface area to volume ratio [1]. Every cell is close to the surface, so the diffusion distance is short [1]. So oxygen and carbon dioxide can diffuse across the body surface fast enough to meet the needs of all cells [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** 1 cm cube: 6 : 1; 2 cm cube: 3 : 1 [1]; 3 cm cube: 2 : 1 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** As SA:V decreases, the time taken for the acid to reach the whole cube increases, from 3 to 26 minutes [1]. The centre of a larger cube is further from the surface, so the diffusion distance is greater [1]. In a large organism, diffusion across the body surface would be too slow to supply cells far from the surface [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Quote figures from the table to support the trend.

**9. (a)** As body mass increases, oxygen use per gram decreases [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Smaller animals have a larger surface area to volume ratio [1], so they lose heat faster per unit mass [1]. They respire faster per unit mass to release heat and maintain body temperature, so they use more oxygen per gram [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** Total oxygen use of A = 7.0 × 20 g = 140 cm³ per hour; total for C = 0.4 × 200 000 g = 80 000 cm³ per hour [1]. C uses more oxygen in total; it uses less only per gram [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Convert kilograms to grams before multiplying, because the rate is per gram.

**10.** Large ears increase the surface area to volume ratio [1], so the desert fox loses more heat to the air and avoids overheating [1]. Small ears reduce surface area, so the Arctic fox loses less heat and keeps its body temperature [1]. **[3]**

**11.** As an animal gets larger, its surface area to volume ratio decreases [1]. Its body surface is too small to exchange enough substances for its volume of cells [1], so it needs specialised exchange surfaces with a large surface area and thin barrier, such as alveoli or villi [1]. Most cells are far from these surfaces, so diffusion alone would be too slow [1]. A mass transport system carries substances between the exchange surfaces and the cells, keeping diffusion distances short and maintaining concentration gradients [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Two separate needs, two separate solutions: surface for the ratio problem, transport for the distance problem.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the ratio upside down, or mixing mm and cm.
- Rounding π-based values too early, which changes the final ratio.
- Leaving "per unit mass" out of metabolic rate explanations.
- Describing a trend without quoting the data.
- Saying large animals have a smaller surface area rather than a smaller surface area to volume ratio.
