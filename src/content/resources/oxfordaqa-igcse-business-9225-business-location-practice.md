All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Rowan Furniture.

> **Case study: Rowan Furniture**
>
> Rowan Furniture makes wooden tables and chairs and sells them to
> homeowners. The owner, Imran, is choosing between two sites. Site X is
> in the town centre. Monthly costs there would be rent $5,200, wages
> $11,400 and delivery $900. Site Y is on an industrial estate 12 km
> outside town. Monthly costs there would be rent $2,800, wages $9,600
> and delivery $2,400. The government offers a grant of $1,000 a month
> for the first two years to firms that locate on the estate, because it
> wants to bring jobs to the area. Timber is delivered from a sawmill
> that is 5 km from the industrial estate, but 40 km from the town
> centre. Few customers visit the factory; most order from the website
> or visit a showroom at a separate address.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a factor affecting business location? **[1]**
- A. Availability of raw materials
- B. The colour of the logo
- C. The name of the owner
- D. The number of products sold last year

**2.** Which **one** of the following is an example of government influence on location? **[1]**
- A. A grant for firms in an area of high unemployment
- B. A rise in a firm's advertising budget
- C. A fall in the price of timber
- D. A new product launch

**3.** State **two** factors a business should consider when choosing a location. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **one** reason why a shop might locate close to its competitors. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a call centre might locate in an area with high unemployment. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate the total monthly cost of Site X. **[1]**

**7.** Calculate the total monthly cost of Site Y. **[1]**

**8.** Using your answers, calculate how much cheaper Site Y would be each year, before the grant. **[2]**

**9.** Calculate the annual saving at Site Y when the $1,000 monthly grant is included. **[2]**

**10.** Explain **two** advantages to Rowan Furniture of locating on the industrial estate. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage to Rowan Furniture of locating in the town centre. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate which site Imran should choose for Rowan Furniture. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **A** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **A** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Any two: proximity to the market [1]; availability of raw materials [1]; labour [1]; competition [1]; costs [1]; government influence [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Customers often compare shops before buying [1], so a group of similar shops attracts more customers to the area than one shop alone [1]. **[2]**

**5.** There are many people looking for work, so the call centre can recruit staff more easily [1], and wages may be lower, which cuts its main cost [1]. **[2]**

**6.** $5,200 + $11,400 + $900 = **$17,500** [1]. **[1]**

**7.** $2,800 + $9,600 + $2,400 = **$14,800** [1]. **[1]**

**8.** Monthly difference = $17,500 - $14,800 = $2,700 [1]; annual difference = $2,700 x 12 = **$32,400** [1]. **[2]**

**9.** With the grant the monthly cost at Y is $14,800 - $1,000 = $13,800, so the monthly saving is $17,500 - $13,800 = $3,700 [1]; annual saving = $3,700 x 12 = **$44,400** [1]. **[2]**

**10.** **Lower costs:** the estate is cheaper by $2,700 a month, and by $3,700 with the grant [1], which increases profit or allows Rowan to lower its prices [1].
**Raw materials:** the sawmill is 5 km away rather than 40 km [1], so timber delivery is cheaper and quicker [1]. Accept lower wages in a less expensive area [1] with a link to cost [1]. **[4]**

**11.** **Advantage:** the town centre is closer to customers and passing shoppers [1], which could lead to more showroom sales [1].
**Disadvantage:** the rent, wages and costs are higher, at $17,500 a month [1], which is $2,700 more than Site Y, so profit would be lower unless extra sales cover the cost [1]. Timber would also have to be transported from 40 km away [1], adding to delivery costs [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **For Site Y:** it costs $44,400 a year less with the grant [1]; most customers order online or use a separate showroom, so passing trade matters little for a factory [1]; timber is close, reducing delivery costs [1]; the grant helps new businesses to settle [1].
**For Site X:** a town centre has a larger pool of workers who live nearby [1] and may give the business a stronger image [1]; delivering furniture to customers in town could be quicker and cheaper than from 12 km away, as the delivery cost at Y is $2,400 compared with $900 [1].
**Judgement:** Site Y is the better choice, because the factory does not need customers to visit, the cost savings are large and timber is close [1]. The grant lasts for two years only, so Imran should check that Site Y is still cheaper than Site X without it, which it is by $32,400 a year before the grant [1]. A judgement is needed. **[9]** Award full marks for a well-supported alternative judgement.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding only one cost and forgetting the others when calculating the total cost of a site.
- Forgetting to multiply the monthly saving by 12 to get an annual figure.
- Choosing a site because of one factor without checking the others.
- Writing generally about "good transport links" without using details in the case.
- Failing to make a clear final recommendation.
