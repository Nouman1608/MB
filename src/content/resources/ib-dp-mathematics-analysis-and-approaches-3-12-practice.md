These questions cover syllabus section 3.12 of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (first assessment 2021), which is HL only: vectors, components and their algebraic and geometric operations, and vector proofs. All questions are calculator-free; give exact answers. Vectors are written in rows to save space. The methods are in the [3.12 study guide](/resources/ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-12/) and the [3.12 revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-12-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Let a = (3, −1, 2) and b = (−1, 4, 0).

**(a)** Find 2a − b. **[2]**

**(b)** Find |a|. **[1]**

**2.** The points A and B are A(2, −3, 1) and B(6, 1, −1).

**(a)** Find AB. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the distance AB. **[1]**

**(c)** Find a unit vector in the direction of AB. **[1]**

**3.** The vectors (p, 6, −3) and (2, q, 1) are parallel. Find p and q. **[3]**

**4.** Write 4i − 2k in component form and find its magnitude. **[2]**

**5.** Find the possible values of t for which |(t, 3, 4)| = 13. **[2]**

**6.** Find the vector of magnitude 6 in the direction opposite to (1, −2, 2). **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** PQRS is a parallelogram with P(1, 2, 3), Q(4, 0, 5) and R(6, 3, 4).

**(a)** Find PQ. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the coordinates of S. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the midpoint M of PR, and show that M is also the midpoint of QS. **[2]**

**8.** The points A(1, 2, −1), B(4, 1, 1) and C(10, k, 5) are collinear.

**(a)** Find k. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the ratio AB : BC. **[2]**

**9.** In triangle OAB, OA = a and OB = b. M is the midpoint of AB and N is the midpoint of OB. G is the point on OM with OG = (2/3)OM.

**(a)** Find OM in terms of a and b. **[2]**

**(b)** Find OG in terms of a and b. **[1]**

**(c)** Show that AG = (1/3)(b − 2a). **[1]**

**(d)** Hence show that A, G and N are collinear. **[3]**

**10.** The point P lies on AB, where A(−2, 5, 1) and B(7, −1, 4), with AP : PB = 2 : 1. Find the coordinates of P. **[3]**

**11.** Find the exact values of k for which |(k, k + 1, 4)| = 5. **[3]**

**12.** ABCD is any quadrilateral, and A, B, C, D have position vectors a, b, c, d. P, Q, R and S are the midpoints of AB, BC, CD and DA. Prove that PQRS is a parallelogram. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 2a = (6, −2, 4) [1], so 2a − b = **(7, −6, 4)** [1]

**(b)** √(9 + 1 + 4) = **√14** [1]

**2. (a)** **(4, 4, −2)** [1]

**(b)** √(16 + 16 + 4) = **6** [1]

**(c)** **(2/3, 2/3, −1/3)** [1]

**3.** Comparing the third components, (p, 6, −3) = −3(2, q, 1) [1]. So **p = −6** [1] and 6 = −3q, giving **q = −2** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Use the component where both values are known (here the third) to find the scalar first.

**4.** **(4, 0, −2)** [1]; magnitude √20 = **2√5** [1]

**5.** t² + 9 + 16 = 169 [1], so **t = ±12** [1]

**6.** |(1, −2, 2)| = 3 [1], so the vector is −2(1, −2, 2) = **(−2, 4, −4)** [1]

**7. (a)** **(3, −2, 2)** [1]

**(b)** PS = QR = (2, 3, −1) [1], so s = (1, 2, 3) + (2, 3, −1) and **S is (3, 5, 2)** [1]

**(c)** M = ((1 + 6)/2, (2 + 3)/2, (3 + 4)/2) = **(3.5, 2.5, 3.5)** [1]. Midpoint of QS = ((4 + 3)/2, (0 + 5)/2, (5 + 2)/2) = (3.5, 2.5, 3.5), the same point [1]

**8. (a)** AB = (3, −1, 2) and AC = (9, k − 2, 6) [1]. For collinearity AC = λAB; the first and third components give λ = 3 [1], so k − 2 = −3 and **k = −1** [1]

**(b)** BC = (6, −2, 4) = 2AB [1], so **AB : BC = 1 : 2** [1]

**9. (a)** OM = a + (1/2)(b − a) [1] = **(1/2)(a + b)** [1]

**(b)** OG = (2/3)(1/2)(a + b) = **(1/3)(a + b)** [1]

**(c)** AG = OG − OA = (1/3)(a + b) − a = **(1/3)(b − 2a)** [1]

**(d)** AN = ON − OA = (1/2)b − a = (1/2)(b − 2a) [1]. So AG = (2/3)AN [1]. AG and AN are parallel and share the point A, so **A, G and N are collinear** [1]

*Examiner insight:* G is the centroid of the triangle. The last mark needs the words "common point A"; the scalar multiple alone shows only that the lines are parallel.

**10.** p = a + (2/3)(b − a) [1] = (−2, 5, 1) + (2/3)(9, −6, 3) [1] = **(4, 1, 3)** [1]

**11.** k² + (k + 1)² + 16 = 25 [1], so 2k² + 2k − 8 = 0, that is k² + k − 4 = 0 [1]. **k = (−1 ± √17)/2** [1]

**12.** p = (a + b)/2, q = (b + c)/2, r = (c + d)/2, s = (d + a)/2 [1]. PQ = q − p = (c − a)/2 [1]. SR = r − s = (c − a)/2 [1]. So PQ = SR: one pair of opposite sides is equal and parallel, and **PQRS is a parallelogram** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Showing one pair of opposite sides equal as vectors is enough; it proves they are equal in length and parallel.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing AB as a − b, which reverses every later answer.
- Claiming collinearity without naming the shared point.
- Finding the scalar from a component that contains the unknown.
- Giving only one value when a magnitude equation has two solutions.
- Pairing the wrong sides of a parallelogram.
