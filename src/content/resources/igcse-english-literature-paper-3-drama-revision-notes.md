## Format at a glance

- **45 minutes, 25 marks** — the shortest externally assessed paper on the syllabus.
- **One question from a choice of two**, both on **the same single play**.
- **Open book** — a clean copy of the set text is allowed in the exam.
- Never taken alone — pairs with either Paper 4 (Unseen) or Component 5 (Coursework).

## What "open book" actually permits

- A **clean copy** of the set text — allowed.
- **Personal annotations, highlighting, underlining** — not allowed.
- Editions with extensive study notes or facing-page translations/explanations — not allowed.
- Brief intro/conclusion and foot/end notes — the edition is fine, but you must not use those notes during the exam.

Open book does not lower the standard: all four AOs are still tested in full. It rewards candidates who know the text well enough to locate passages **quickly**, not candidates who haven't learned it.

## One text, not two

Only one play is studied for this paper (unlike Paper 2's two). This allows deeper preparation of a single text, but 45 minutes is a tight window — efficient planning matters more here than on longer papers.

## The four AOs and what open book does (and doesn't) help with

| AO | What it rewards | Does open book help? |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 | Knowledge of content, shown via detail/quotation | Yes — locate/check quotations rather than relying purely on memory |
| AO2 | Understanding of characters, relationships, situations, themes | No — requires genuine understanding, not page-searching |
| AO3 | Understanding of writer's intentions/methods, response to language | Indirectly — can point precisely to specific lines |
| AO4 | Personal response (direct or by implication) | No — must be worked out in advance |

## How it fits the qualification

Paper 3 always pairs with either **Paper 4 Unseen** (1 hr 15 min, 25 marks, critical commentary on unseen poetry/prose) or **Component 5 Coursework** (25 marks, two portfolio assignments, 600–1200 words each, different texts). Both routes bring drama to 25% of the overall qualification. Which route you're on is set by your school, not chosen individually — confirm early.


## Worked example: preparing quotations for open-book use

Rather than trying to search the play cold during the exam, prepare a small bank of pre-identified key passages before the exam, organised by theme and character rather than by act or scene alone. For a play centred on a power struggle between two characters, for example, you might pre-select: one early passage establishing each character's initial position, one mid-play passage showing the turning point in their relationship, and one late passage showing the resolution or consequence. Knowing roughly where these passages sit in the text (even without page numbers memorised) lets you locate and use them quickly under the 45-minute time limit, rather than spending exam time flicking through the play looking for something relevant. This preparation strategy is what actually makes open book an advantage rather than a false sense of security -- the text being present only helps if you already know which parts of it are relevant to your argument.

## Why set texts must be confirmed for the current series

Both Paper 2 and Paper 3 draw on the same rotating set-text list, which changes by examination series. A candidate preparing from a previous year's reading list risks studying the wrong play entirely, so confirming the current series' set texts directly against the current published syllabus, rather than assuming continuity from a previous year, is an essential first step before any other Paper 3 preparation begins.

## Exam traps

- Treating open book as reducing the standard expected — it doesn't; all four AOs still apply in full.
- Bringing an edition with extensive study notes or facing-page translation — not permitted.
- Only preparing the scenes you expect to be asked about — both set questions could draw on any part of the play.
- Spending exam time searching the text cold instead of using pre-identified key passages.
- Confusing which paper (Paper 2 or Paper 3) requires a closed book — Paper 3 is open book; Paper 2 is closed.
