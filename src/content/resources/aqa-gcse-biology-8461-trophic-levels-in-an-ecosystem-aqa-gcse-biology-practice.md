specification, for first teaching from 2016. The whole section is **biology only**; every question suits both
tiers. All data are invented for practice. A calculator is useful for questions 4, 7, 8, 9 and 11.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** A food chain in a wood is: oak leaves → caterpillar → blue tit → sparrowhawk.

**(a)** Give the trophic level number of the blue tit. **[1]**
**(b)** Nothing eats sparrowhawks in this wood. Give the term for an animal like this. **[1]**

**2.** Describe how decomposers obtain food from dead leaves. **[2]**

**3.** Give **two** reasons why not all the biomass in rabbits becomes biomass in the foxes that eat them. **[2]**

**4.** A field receives 4 500 000 kJ of light energy per m² in a year. Producers transfer about 1% of the incident
light energy for photosynthesis. Calculate the energy transferred by the producers per m². **[2]**

**5.** Explain the difference between egestion and excretion, giving an example of each. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why a fox can be at trophic level 2 in one food chain and trophic level 3 in another. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** The table shows the biomass at each trophic level in a hedgerow.

| Trophic level | Biomass (g/m²) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1500 |
| 2 | 135 |
| 3 | 12 |

**(a)** A student uses a scale of 1 mm = 15 g/m² to draw a pyramid of biomass. Calculate the width of each bar.
**[3]**
**(b)** Describe the shape of the completed pyramid. **[1]**

**8.** Scientists measure the biomass of four trophic levels in a grassland.

| Organisms | Plants | Grasshoppers | Shrews | Owls |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Biomass (kg) | 24 000 | 2160 | 180 | 9 |

**(a)** Calculate the efficiency of biomass transfer from plants to grasshoppers. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the efficiency of biomass transfer from grasshoppers to shrews. Give your answer to 2
significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Give the efficiency of transfer from shrews to owls as a fraction in its simplest form. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the grassland can support only a few owls. **[2]**

**9.** In a feeding study, a young bullock eats 100 kg of grass (dry mass). Of this, 63 kg is egested, 33 kg is
lost as carbon dioxide and water from respiration, and the rest becomes new body tissue.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of the grass eaten that becomes new body tissue. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the grass eaten that is absorbed. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why keeping the bullock in a warm shed with little room to move could increase the mass of new
body tissue. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why food chains rarely have more than four or five trophic levels. **[3]**

**11.** A lake contains 50 000 kg of producers.

**(a)** Assume that 10% of biomass is transferred between each trophic level. Estimate the biomass of the tertiary
consumers. Show your working. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest why the real biomass of tertiary consumers might differ from your estimate. **[1]**

**12.** Describe and explain the shape of a pyramid of biomass for a food chain with four trophic levels. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 3 [1]
**(b)** Apex predator [1]

**2.** They secrete enzymes onto the leaves, which digest large molecules outside the decomposer [1]; the small
soluble food molecules then diffuse into the microorganism [1]

**3.** Any two of the following, 1 mark each [2]: some material is not absorbed and is egested as faeces; some
is lost as carbon dioxide and water in respiration; some is lost as water and urea in urine; large amounts of
glucose are used in respiration; parts of the rabbit (such as bones and fur) are not eaten

**4.** 1% of 4 500 000 = 0.01 × 4 500 000 [1] = **45 000 kJ per m²** [1]

**5.** Egestion is removal of food that was never absorbed, as faeces [1]; excretion is removal of waste made in
the body, such as urea in urine or carbon dioxide from respiration [1]

**6.** The trophic level depends on what the fox eats in that food chain [1]; if it eats fruit (a producer) it is
a primary consumer at level 2, but if it eats a herbivore such as a rabbit it is at level 3 [1]

**7. (a)** Level 1: 1500 ÷ 15 = **100 mm** [1]; level 2: 135 ÷ 15 = **9 mm** [1];
level 3: 12 ÷ 15 = **0.8 mm** [1]
**(b)** A pyramid: each bar is much narrower than the one below, with level 1 widest at the bottom [1]
*Examiner insight:* A 0.8 mm bar is correct; widening it to be "visible" makes the pyramid inaccurate.

**8. (a)** 2160 ÷ 24 000 × 100 [1] = **9%** [1]
**(b)** 180 ÷ 2160 × 100 [1] = **8.3%** [1]
**(c)** 9/180 = **1/20** [1]
**(d)** Biomass is lost at every transfer, so very little biomass reaches the owls' level (only 9 kg) [1]; this
can support only a small number of owls [1]

**9. (a)** 100 − 63 − 33 = 4 kg, so **4%** [1]
**(b)** 100 − 63 = 37 kg, so **37%** [1]
**(c)** Less glucose is used in respiration to keep warm and to move [1]; so more of the absorbed material is
used for growth (new biomass) [1]

**10.** Only about 10% of biomass is transferred from one trophic level to the next [1]; the rest is lost as
faeces, in respiration and in urine [1]; after a few transfers there is too little biomass left to support
another trophic level [1]

**11. (a)** Primary consumers 5000 kg; secondary consumers 500 kg [1]; tertiary consumers **50 kg** [1]
**(b)** Any one: the 10% figure is only approximate and real efficiency varies; animals at different levels lose
different amounts in respiration; some organisms feed at more than one level [1]

**12.** Marking points (AQA marks 6-mark questions by levels; a top answer describes and explains):
- level 1 (producers) is at the bottom with the largest bar [1]
- each higher level has a smaller bar, giving a pyramid shape [1]
- only about 10% of the biomass at one level is transferred to the next [1]
- some ingested material is not absorbed and is egested as faeces [1]
- some absorbed material is lost as carbon dioxide and water in respiration, and water and urea in urine [1]
- large amounts of glucose are used in respiration, so less biomass is available to the next level [1]
*Examiner insight:* An answer that describes the shape without any reason for the loss stays at the lowest level.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling herbivores trophic level 1.
- Dividing the wrong way round in an efficiency calculation.
- Leaving fractions unsimplified when the question asks for simplest form.
- Saying biomass or energy is "used up".
- Confusing egestion with excretion.
- Drawing pyramid bars that are not to scale.
