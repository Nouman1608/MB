These questions cover section 6.2 Stars and the Universe of the Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Where needed, use 1 light-year = 9.5 × 10¹⁵ m and H₀ = 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ per second.

> **Tier note:** questions 1 to 8 are Core and every candidate should do them, except part 6(b), which is marked *(Extended)*. Questions 9 to 13, marked *(Extended)*, use Supplement content (fusion in stars, the life cycle of stars, CMBR and the Hubble constant), examined on Papers 2 and 4 only. The marks shown are indicative only.

## Section A

**1.** State the size of the Sun compared with other stars. **[1]**

**2.** State approximately how many stars make up a galaxy. **[1]**

**3.** State whether a light-year is a unit of time or of distance, and define it. **[2]**

**4.** State approximately how many galaxies make up the Universe. **[1]**

**5.** The Sun radiates most of its energy in three regions of the electromagnetic spectrum. One is visible light. Name the other two. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** The Andromeda galaxy is about 2.5 million light-years from the Earth.

**(a)** State how long the light we see from Andromeda has been travelling. **[1]**
**(b)** *(Extended)* Convert this distance into metres. **[2]**

**7.** This question is about scale.

**(a)** Write these in order of size, starting with the smallest: the Milky Way, the Earth, the Universe, the Sun, the Solar System. **[2]**
**(b)** State the approximate diameter of the Milky Way in light-years. **[1]**

**8.** A line in the spectrum of hydrogen has a wavelength of 656 nm when measured on the Earth. In the light from a distant galaxy, the same line is observed at 690 nm.

**(a)** State whether the observed wavelength has increased or decreased. **[1]**
**(b)** Name this effect. **[1]**
**(c)** State what this tells you about the motion of the galaxy relative to the Earth. **[1]**
**(d)** Light from almost all distant galaxies shows this effect. State what this is evidence for. **[2]**

**9.** *(Extended)* This question is about how stars form and stay stable.

**(a)** Describe how a protostar forms from an interstellar cloud of gas and dust. **[2]**
**(b)** A stable star neither collapses nor expands. Name the two forces acting and state their directions. **[2]**
**(c)** State the nuclear reaction that keeps the centre of a stable star hot. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Star A has a similar mass to the Sun.

**(a)** State what star A becomes when most of the hydrogen in its centre has been converted to helium. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe what happens to star A after this stage. **[2]**
**(c)** The Earth contains many elements heavier than helium. Use the life cycle of massive stars to explain where these elements came from. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* The brightness of a supernova shows that a far galaxy is 120 million light-years from the Earth.

**(a)** Convert this distance into metres. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed at which the galaxy is moving away from the Earth. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate d/v for this galaxy, in seconds. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why d/v gives an estimate of the age of the Universe, and what this suggests about the matter in the Universe. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)* This question is about cosmic microwave background radiation (CMBR).

**(a)** Describe what is observed when astronomers detect CMBR. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain when the CMBR was produced and why it is now observed in the microwave region. **[2]**

**13.** *(Extended)* Astronomers measure the speed of a receding galaxy.

**(a)** State what measurement of the galaxy's light they use to find its speed. **[1]**
**(b)** A second galaxy is twice as far away. Use H₀ = v/d to state how its speed compares with the first. **[1]**
**(c)** State the current estimate of the Hubble constant, with its unit. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **Medium**-sized [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **Many billions** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A unit of **distance** [1]; the distance travelled by light in (the vacuum of) space in one year [1]. **[2]**

*Common mistake:* calling it a time because "year" is in the name.

**4.** **Many billions** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** **Infrared** and **ultraviolet** (both needed) [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** About **2.5 million years** [1].
**(b)** 2.5 × 10⁶ × 9.5 × 10¹⁵ [1] = **2.4 × 10²² m** [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Earth, Sun, Solar System, Milky Way, Universe: all correct [2] (one adjacent pair swapped scores 1 mark).
**(b)** About **100 000 light-years** [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** **Increased** [1].
**(b)** **Redshift** [1].
**(c)** The galaxy is moving **away** (receding) from the Earth [1].
**(d)** The Universe is **expanding** [1]; this supports the **Big Bang Theory** [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** The cloud collapses under its own internal gravitational attraction [1], and its temperature increases as it collapses [1].
**(b)** Gravitational attraction acting **inwards** [1]; an outward force due to the high temperature in the centre, acting **outwards** (the two are balanced) [1].
**(c)** **Fusion of hydrogen into helium** [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** A **red giant** [1].
**(b)** It forms a **planetary nebula** [1] with a **white dwarf** star at its centre [1].
**(c)** Heavier elements were made in earlier, more massive stars and scattered when they exploded as supernovae, forming a nebula containing new heavier elements [1]; the Solar System, including the Earth, formed from such a nebula [1]. **[5]**

*Common mistake:* in (b), describing a supernova. That is the path of a much more massive star.

**11. (a)** 120 × 10⁶ × 9.5 × 10¹⁵ = **1.14 × 10²⁴ m** [1].
**(b)** v = H₀d = 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ × 1.14 × 10²⁴ [1] = **2.5 × 10⁶ m/s** [1].
**(c)** d / v = 1.14 × 10²⁴ ÷ 2.5 × 10⁶ [1] = **4.5 × 10¹⁷ s** (equal to 1/H₀) [1].
**(d)** d/v is the time the galaxy would take to travel its present distance at its present speed, so it estimates how long ago the galaxies were together [1]; this suggests all the matter in the Universe was once at a single point [1]. **[7]**

**12. (a)** Microwave radiation of a specific frequency [1] observed at all points in space around us (from every direction) [1].
**(b)** It was produced shortly after the Universe was formed [1]; as the Universe expanded, the radiation was expanded (stretched) into the microwave region [1]. **[4]**

**13. (a)** The **change in wavelength** of its starlight due to redshift [1].
**(b)** It is moving away **twice as fast** (v is proportional to d) [1].
**(c)** **2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ per second** [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating a light-year as a time.
- Naming only one of infrared and ultraviolet for the Sun.
- Describing redshift as a change of colour rather than an increase in wavelength.
- Mixing up the paths of Sun-like and massive stars.
- Forgetting to convert light-years to metres before using H₀.
- Explaining the CMBR without saying it was stretched by the expansion.
