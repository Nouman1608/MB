These questions cover section **4.2.3.2 Plant organ system** of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, for teaching from September 2016 and exams from 2018 onwards. The content is assessed on Paper 1 at Foundation and Higher Tier, and nothing in 4.2.3.2 is marked (HT only). A calculator is useful for questions 5, 6 and 7. All readings are invented.

Learn the content first in the [plant organ system study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-plant-organ-system-aqa-gcse-biology/) and the [plant organ system revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-plant-organ-system-aqa-gcse-biology-revision-notes/). ## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Name the process by which:

**(a)** water enters a root hair cell **[1]**
**(b)** mineral ions enter a root hair cell **[1]**
**(c)** dissolved sugars move through the phloem. **[1]**

**2.** Explain why a root hair cell needs energy to absorb mineral ions from the soil. **[2]**

**3.** Describe what happens to a stoma when its guard cells lose water, and explain why this is useful to the plant. **[2]**

**4.** Predict and explain the effect on the rate of transpiration of each change.

**(a)** The air temperature rises from 15 °C to 25 °C. **[2]**
**(b)** Day turns to night. **[2]**

### Section B: data and longer questions

**5.** A student used a potometer to measure how far the bubble moved in 5 minutes, three times at each temperature.

| Temperature (°C) | Distances (mm) |
|---|---|
| 15 | 9, 11, 10 |
| 25 | 17, 19, 18 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of water uptake, in mm per minute, at each temperature. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in the rate from 15 °C to 25 °C. **[2]**
**(c)** Name one variable the student should keep the same. **[1]**

**6.** A student traces the leaves of a shoot on 1 cm² grid paper. The leaves cover 31 whole squares and 18 part squares.

**(a)** Estimate the leaf area, counting each part square as half a square. **[1]**
**(b)** The shoot takes up 0.9 cm³ of water in 30 minutes. Calculate the rate of uptake in cm³ per cm² of leaf per hour. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the student divided the rate by the leaf area. **[1]**

**7.** The capillary tube of a potometer has a diameter of 1.2 mm. The bubble moves 35 mm. Calculate the volume of water taken up, in mm³, to 3 significant figures. Use π = 3.14. **[3]**

**8.** Explain why sugars made in the leaves must be transported down to the roots. **[2]**

**9.** The table shows the rate of transpiration of a shoot at different light intensities.

| Light intensity (arbitrary units) | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate (mm/min) | 0.5 | 1.5 | 2.4 | 2.9 | 3.0 | 3.0 |

**(a)** Describe the pattern in the results. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the rate increases as light intensity increases. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why the rate stops increasing above a light intensity of 8. **[1]**

**10.** On a hot, dry, windy day a plant in a pot begins to wilt. Explain why this happens and how the plant's stomata respond. **[4]**

**11.** Explain how the structures of the root, stem and leaves are adapted for transporting water, mineral ions and sugars around a plant. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Osmosis [1]
**(b)** Active transport [1]
**(c)** Translocation [1]
*Examiner insight:* Diffusion is the most common wrong answer for (b).

**2.** Mineral ions are usually more concentrated inside the root hair cell than in the soil [1]; so they must be moved against the concentration gradient by active transport, which needs energy from respiration [1]
*Examiner insight:* The concentration gradient is the reason energy is needed; say which way it runs.

**3.** The guard cells become flaccid and the stoma closes [1]; this reduces water loss, which helps the plant when it is short of water [1]
*Examiner insight:* Closing stomata also stops carbon dioxide entering, which is why plants do not keep them closed all the time.

**4. (a)** The rate increases [1]; water molecules have more energy, so evaporation and diffusion of water vapour out of the leaf are faster [1]
**(b)** The rate decreases [1]; in the dark the stomata close, so less water vapour can diffuse out [1]
*Examiner insight:* A prediction without a reason earns only one of the two marks.

**5. (a)** 15 °C: mean 10 mm, 10 ÷ 5 = **2.0 mm/min** [1]; 25 °C: mean 18 mm, 18 ÷ 5 = **3.6 mm/min** [1]
**(b)** (3.6 − 2.0) ÷ 2.0 × 100 [1] = **80%** [1]
**(c)** Any one: light intensity, air movement, humidity, the same shoot (or leaf area) [1]
*Examiner insight:* "The amount of water" is not a control variable here; name a factor that affects transpiration.

**6. (a)** 31 + 18 ÷ 2 = **40 cm²** [1]
**(b)** 0.9 cm³ in 30 minutes = 1.8 cm³ per hour [1]; 1.8 ÷ 40 = **0.045 cm³ per cm² per hour** [1]
**(c)** So that shoots with different leaf areas can be compared fairly, because a larger leaf area loses more water [1]
*Examiner insight:* Convert to "per hour" before dividing by area, or the answer is 0.0225 and the unit is wrong.

**7.** Radius = 1.2 ÷ 2 = 0.6 mm; area = 3.14 × 0.6² = 1.1304 mm² [1]; volume = 1.1304 × 35 [1] = **39.6 mm³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using the diameter instead of the radius gives an answer four times too large.

**8.** Root cells cannot photosynthesise, because they receive no light, so they cannot make their own sugars [1]; they need sugars for respiration (for example to release energy for active transport) or to store [1]
*Examiner insight:* Link the answer to the specification's wording: sugars are moved "for immediate use or storage".

**9. (a)** The rate increases as light intensity increases from 0 to 8 [1]; it then levels off at 3.0 mm/min [1]
**(b)** Stomata open wider in brighter light (for photosynthesis) [1]; so more water vapour can diffuse out of the leaf [1]
**(c)** The stomata are fully open, so another factor, such as temperature or humidity, now limits the rate [1]
*Examiner insight:* Quote the data in (a): the plateau value earns the second mark.

**10.** Heat, low humidity and wind all increase the rate of transpiration [1]; water is lost from the leaves faster than the roots can take it up from the pot [1]; cells lose water and the plant wilts [1]; guard cells become flaccid and close the stomata, reducing further water loss [1]
*Examiner insight:* Link each condition to the rate before explaining the wilting.

**11.** Marking points (levels-marked; a top answer covers all three substances and links structures to function):
- root hair cells have a large surface area for absorbing water and mineral ions [1]
- water enters by osmosis; mineral ions by active transport, using energy from the many mitochondria [1]
- xylem in the root, stem and leaves is made of hollow tubes, so water and ions flow upwards in the transpiration stream [1]
- xylem walls are strengthened by lignin, so the tubes do not collapse and support the plant [1]
- phloem is made of elongated cells with pores in the end walls, so cell sap carrying sugars moves from cell to cell [1]
- sugars are translocated from the leaves to other parts of the plant for immediate use or storage [1]
*Examiner insight:* Answers that describe only water transport cannot reach the top level, because the question also asks about sugars.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying mineral ions enter by osmosis or diffusion.
- Predicting a change without explaining it.
- Using diameter instead of radius in the area of a tube.
- Forgetting to convert to "per hour" before dividing by leaf area.
- Leaving phloem out of a question about transport.
