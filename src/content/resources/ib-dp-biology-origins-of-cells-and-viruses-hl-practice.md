## Questions

**1.** State two ways in which conditions on early Earth differed from those today. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why viruses are classed as non-living. **[3]**

**3.** An electron micrograph of a virion has a scale bar of 25 mm that represents 100 nm. The virion measures 31 mm across.

**(a)** Calculate the magnification. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the actual diameter of the virion in nm. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why the formation of a membrane-bound compartment was needed before the first cells could evolve. **[3]**

**5.** A research group runs three versions of a Miller-Urey type experiment and measures the amino acids collected after 5 days.

| Set-up | Gases | Sparks | Amino acids (µmol) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | methane, ammonia, hydrogen, water vapour | on | 38 |
| B | carbon dioxide, nitrogen, water vapour | on | 3 |
| C | methane, ammonia, hydrogen, water vapour | off | 0.5 |

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater the yield in A is than in B, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what set-up C shows. **[2]**
**(c)** Using the data, evaluate the claim that the experiment shows how amino acids formed on early Earth. **[3]**

**6.** Explain why RNA, rather than DNA or protein, is presumed to have been the first genetic material. **[4]**

**7.** The last universal common ancestor (LUCA) is the most recent organism from which all living things descend.

**(a)** Outline two lines of evidence that LUCA existed. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why LUCA is not thought to have been the only form of life on early Earth. **[2]**

**8.** A rock sample contains an unstable isotope. The isotope has a half-life of 1.25 billion years, and 12.5% of the original amount remains.

**(a)** Calculate the age of the rock. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest two reasons why dating the first living cells is uncertain. **[2]**

**9.** Bacteriophage lambda infects a culture. After infection there are 4.0 × 10⁶ infected cells per mL. After lysis the culture holds 9.2 × 10⁸ phages per mL.

**(a)** Calculate the burst size. **[2]**
**(b)** Compare the lytic and lysogenic cycles of lambda. **[3]**

**10.** A culture of 2000 bacterial cells is infected with lambda. After the infection, 340 cells carry a prophage. The culture is then exposed to ultraviolet light and 85% of these cells lyse.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of cells that became lysogens. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of cells that lyse after ultraviolet exposure. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why ultraviolet light causes lysis. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why these cells do not lyse without ultraviolet light. **[2]**

**11.** A retrovirus has a genome of 8400 nucleotides. The error rate of its reverse transcriptase is 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ per nucleotide per copy. One infected person produces 5.0 × 10⁹ new virions per day.

**(a)** Calculate the mean number of mutations per genome copy. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of mutations per day in the new virions. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the consequences of this for drug treatment. **[2]**

**12.** Two influenza strains isolated three years apart differ at 9 of the 566 amino acids in a surface protein.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage difference, and the mean percentage change per year, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how point mutation and reassortment of genome segments differ in their effect on influenza. **[3]**
**(c)** Discuss the evidence that viruses have arisen more than once. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** No free oxygen, and so no ozone layer [1]; higher concentrations of carbon dioxide and methane, giving higher temperatures and more ultraviolet light reaching the surface [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Two distinct conditions are needed. "No oxygen" and "no ozone" are linked, so a second point should be separate, such as carbon dioxide, methane, temperature or ultraviolet light.

**2.** They have no cytoplasm, ribosomes or metabolism of their own, and few or no enzymes [1]; they cannot carry out functions such as respiration, growth or protein synthesis outside a host [1]; they reproduce only by using the host cell's machinery [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Saying only "they need a host" gives one idea. Link each missing feature to a characteristic of life that a cell does for itself.

**3. (a)** Convert: 25 mm = 25 000 000 nm [1]; 25 000 000 ÷ 100 = **×250 000** [1]
**(b)** Actual size = image ÷ magnification = 31 000 000 nm ÷ 250 000 [1] = **124 nm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write the unit conversion and the formula. A correct number with no working is less secure than a clear method, and a missing unit weakens the answer.

**4.** A boundary lets the inside chemistry differ from the outside [1]; molecules can be kept at high concentration so reactions are faster [1]; products are not lost by diffusion, and each compartment holds its own set of molecules, so selection can act on them [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "To protect the contents" is vague. State that the inside becomes different from the outside, then say what that allows.

**5. (a)** 38 ÷ 3 [1] = **12.7** times [1]
**(b)** Set-up C is a control with no energy input [1]; the tiny yield shows that the sparks, not the gases alone, drive formation of most of the amino acids [1].
**(c)** Support: amino acids formed from simple gases only when energy was supplied [1]. Limitation: set-up B, without methane or ammonia, gave little, so the result depends on a strongly reducing mixture that may not match early Earth [1]; only small molecules formed, so the experiment shows what is possible, not what happened, and does not explain polymers or cells [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation must end in a verdict, not just a set of points. Finish with a sentence such as "plausible for amino acids, unproven for early Earth".

**6.** RNA can be replicated by complementary base pairing, so it can carry heritable information [1]; RNA can also act as a catalyst, so it can function as both gene and enzyme [1]; this avoids the problem that DNA needs protein enzymes to replicate while protein needs DNA to be made [1]; ribozymes in the ribosome still catalyse peptide bond formation, which supports the idea [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming both roles, genetic material and catalyst, is the core of the answer. Saying only "RNA is simpler" gives no checkable reason.

**7. (a)** The genetic code is almost universal, so the same codons specify the same amino acids across life [1]; many genes, such as those for ribosome components, are shared by all organisms [1]; these similarities are better explained by common descent than by separate origins [1].
**(b)** Other forms of life may have evolved [1], but probably became extinct through competition with LUCA and its descendants [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* For "outline two lines of evidence", name the evidence and say what it suggests. Leaving the inference out leaves a bare fact.

**8. (a)** 12.5% is one eighth, which is three half-lives [1]; 3 × 1.25 = **3.75 billion years** [1]
**(b)** Very old rocks are rare or have been altered [1]; single-cell fossils are small and hard to identify as evidence of life, and the first protocells did not fossilise [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give two different reasons. Repeating "the evidence is old" in two ways adds little credit.

**9. (a)** Burst size = phages released ÷ infected cells [1] = 9.2 × 10⁸ ÷ 4.0 × 10⁶ = **230** [1]
**(b)** Both begin with attachment and injection of viral DNA [1]; in the lytic cycle the DNA is copied at once, new phages assemble and the cell bursts [1]; in the lysogenic cycle the DNA integrates as a prophage and passes to daughter cells, with the host surviving until induction [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs similarities or differences stated side by side. Describing each cycle in separate paragraphs, without a link, does not compare.

**10. (a)** 340 ÷ 2000 × 100 = **17%** [1]
**(b)** 340 × 0.85 [1] = **289** cells [1]
**(c)** Ultraviolet light damages DNA [1]; this triggers induction, so the prophage leaves the chromosome and the lytic cycle begins, ending in lysis [1].
**(d)** A repressor protein keeps lytic genes switched off [1]; the prophage is copied with the host chromosome at each division, so the host survives [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (b) show the multiplication. The answer is a whole number of cells, so give 289 and not a rounded 290.

**11. (a)** 8400 × 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ [1] = **0.34** (0.336) [1]
**(b)** 5.0 × 10⁹ × 0.336 [1] = **1.7 × 10⁹** mutations per day [1]
**(c)** Many different variants arise each day, so some are resistant to a drug [1]; resistant variants are selected and increase while a single drug is used, so combinations of drugs are needed [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A consequence should link cause to effect: more variants, then selection under the drug, then resistance. Stating only "HIV mutates" is a description.

**12. (a)** 9 ÷ 566 × 100 = **1.59%** [1]; 1.59 ÷ 3 = **0.530% per year** [1]
**(b)** Point mutations change the surface protein slightly and gradually, so immunity from earlier infection or vaccine fits less well over time [1]; reassortment mixes whole segments between strains, which can produce a sudden large change [1]; either way vaccines must be updated, because memory cells made against the old protein respond less well [1].
**(c)** Viruses have very different genomes, shapes and replication methods, which suggests several origins [1]; they share an extreme form of obligate parasitism, so common features such as a capsid and small size may be convergent evolution [1]; the genetic code is shared with living organisms, which fits origins from or alongside cells [1]; the evidence is indirect, so a single origin cannot be completely ruled out [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* A discuss answer should include both evidence for and a point of caution. Convergent evolution is a reason that shared features do not prove one origin.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing viruses as living because they carry genes or reproduce.
- Giving amino acid formation as proof of the origin of life instead of evidence that carbon compounds can form spontaneously.
- Writing "to protect the contents" for a membrane's role, with no mention of different internal chemistry.
- Missing the conversion between mm and nm in magnification.
- Confusing LUCA with the first living cell.
- Describing the lysogenic cycle without naming the prophage or the repressor.
- Using "mutation" for influenza reassortment, which is a mixing of segments.
- Giving a list for evaluate and discuss questions with no judgement.
