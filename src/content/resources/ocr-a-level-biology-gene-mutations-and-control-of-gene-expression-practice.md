## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by a gene mutation, and name two types of change that can occur in the nucleotide sequence of a gene. **[3]**

**2.** The mRNA for a short polypeptide is AUG AAG GCC CUA GAU UUC CAC UGA. Data supplied: AUG is Met; AAG is Lys; GCC is Ala; CUA and CUG are Leu; GAU is Asp; UUC is Phe; CAC is His; UGA and UAG are stop codons.

**(a)** The fourth codon changes from CUA to CUG. Explain the effect on the polypeptide. **[2]**
**(b)** The second codon changes from AAG to UAG. Explain the effect on the polypeptide. **[2]**

**3.** Use the mRNA and data in question 2.

**(a)** One C is deleted from the run of three consecutive C nucleotides (bases 8 to 10). Explain the effect on the polypeptide. **[3]**
**(b)** Instead, the whole codon GAU is deleted. Describe and explain the effect on the polypeptide. **[2]**

**4.** A bacterium acquires a substitution mutation in a gene for a protein that an antibiotic normally binds.

**(a)** Explain how a substitution could stop the antibiotic from affecting the protein. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the same mutation could be beneficial in one environment but neutral or harmful in another. **[3]**

**5.** Describe how the lac operon of *E. coli* stops the bacterium from making lactose-digesting enzymes when no lactose is present. **[4]**

**6.** Beta-galactosidase activity (arbitrary units) was measured in four strains of *E. coli* grown without and with lactose.

| Strain | No lactose | Lactose present |
|---|---|---|
| W | 3 | 900 |
| X | 850 | 870 |
| Y | 4 | 5 |
| Z | 0 | 0 |

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater the activity of strain W is with lactose than without. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain the results for strain X. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest two possible explanations for the results for strain Y. **[2]**
**(d)** State one disadvantage to strain X of growing without lactose. **[1]**

**7.** Explain why a liver cell and a muscle cell, both with identical DNA, make different proteins. **[4]**

**8.** A eukaryotic gene has four exons of 105, 192, 147 and 66 nucleotides. The introns between them are 640, 295 and 405 nucleotides long.

**(a)** Calculate the length of the primary mRNA. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the primary mRNA that is exons. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** Assume that all of the mature mRNA is translated and that the final codon is a stop codon. Work out how many amino acids the polypeptide contains. **[2]**
**(d)** State where in the cell the introns are removed. **[1]**

**9.** Explain how a hormone can activate an enzyme using cyclic AMP, without new protein being made. **[4]**

**10.** Homeobox sequences are found in plants, animals and fungi.

**(a)** Explain how a gene containing a homeobox can regulate gene expression. **[2]**
**(b)** A homeodomain from a fungus and the matching region in a plant protein have 44 identical amino acids out of 60. Calculate the percentage identity. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest why homeobox sequences have changed so little. **[2]**
**(d)** A mutation in a Hox gene of an insect makes one body segment form the structures normally found on the next segment. Explain this. **[2]**

**11.** In a developing mouse limb, the cells between the future digits die. The number of apoptotic cells per square millimetre of this tissue was 48 in a normal embryo and 6 when an inhibitor of caspase enzymes was added.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in apoptotic cells caused by the inhibitor. **[2]**
**(b)** Predict the appearance of the digits when the inhibitor is present. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how apoptosis separates the digits. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain the role of mitosis in the growth of the limb. **[2]**

**12.** Cells from a culture were examined. Of 300 cells without a growth factor, 27 were in mitosis. Of 300 cells with the growth factor, 51 were in mitosis.

**(a)** Calculate the mitotic index for each culture. **[2]**
**(b)** The growth factor is an external stimulus. Suggest how it causes the change in the mitotic index. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how DNA damage inside a cell could lead to apoptosis. **[2]**
**(d)** Predict what could happen to a damaged cell if the genes controlling apoptosis no longer respond to internal stimuli. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** A permanent alteration to the order of nucleotides in a gene [1]. Any two of: substitution, insertion, deletion [2]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name two different types; two sizes of the same type (insertion of one, insertion of two) are one type.

**2. (a)** CUA and CUG both code for Leu [1]. The polypeptide is unchanged (a silent change, because the code is degenerate) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** UAG is a stop codon [1]. Translation stops after Met, so only a very short polypeptide is made [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the data given: name the amino acid each codon codes for, not only "silent" or "stop".

**3. (a)** Deleting one nucleotide shifts the reading frame [1]. The codons after the deletion are read differently, so GCC UAG is read and UAG is a stop codon [1]. The polypeptide is Met-Lys-Ala only, much shorter [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Three nucleotides are lost, so the reading frame is kept [1]. One amino acid (Asp) is missing: Met-Lys-Ala-Leu-Phe-His [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the number of nucleotides (one or three) to the frame; "deletions cause frameshifts" alone is incomplete, because a deletion of three does not.

**4. (a)** The substitution changes a codon, so a different amino acid is in the primary structure [1]. This can change interactions between R groups, altering the tertiary structure [1]. The antibiotic can no longer bind to the changed protein [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** With the antibiotic present, the mutant survives and reproduces; others do not [1]. Without it there is no advantage [1]. If the changed protein works less well, the mutation may be harmful, so the effect depends on the environment [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Argue beneficial, neutral or harmful for a named environment; calling a mutation good or bad with no conditions is weak.

**5.** The regulatory gene lacI codes for the repressor protein [1]. With no lactose, the repressor binds to the operator [1]. This stops RNA polymerase transcribing lacZ and lacY [1]. No mRNA is made, so the enzymes are not made, saving energy and amino acids [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the operator (a DNA sequence) and the repressor (a protein) separate.

**6. (a)** 900 / 3 = **300** times [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Activity is high without lactose [1]. The repressor is not working, or cannot bind the operator, so the genes are always transcribed [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Any two of: a faulty promoter, so RNA polymerase cannot bind [1]; a repressor that cannot be released by lactose, so the operator stays covered [1]; (a third possibility is a faulty permease, so lactose cannot enter the cell). **[2]**
**(d)** It wastes energy and amino acids making enzymes that are not needed [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* In the "suggest" part, give two different faults; the same fault in different words is one idea.

**7.** Transcription factors bind to specific base sequences near genes [1]. Activators help RNA polymerase bind to the promoter and start transcription [1]. The two cell types have different transcription factors active [1]. So different genes are transcribed, giving different mRNA and proteins from the same DNA [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say the DNA is the same and the difference is which genes are transcribed; you need not name a transcription factor.

**8. (a)** 105 + 192 + 147 + 66 = 510 nucleotides of exons; 640 + 295 + 405 = 1340 of introns; primary mRNA = **1850** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 510 / 1850 × 100 [1] = **27.6%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Mature mRNA has 510 nucleotides, so 510 / 3 = 170 codons [1]. One is the stop codon, so **169** amino acids [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** In the nucleus [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the working for each stage so your reasoning is clear. Remember to subtract the stop codon.

**9.** The hormone binds to a surface receptor [1]. The cell then makes cyclic AMP [1]. Cyclic AMP binds to an inactive enzyme and changes its shape, so it becomes active [1]. The enzyme was already present, so no new transcription or translation is needed [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the protein already exists and is activated, not made.

**10. (a)** The gene codes for a protein with a DNA-binding domain [1]. The protein binds to specific base sequences of other genes and switches their transcription on or off (it is a transcription factor) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 44 / 60 × 100 = **73.3%** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** These genes control the body plan, so a mutation is likely to be harmful [1]. Such individuals are less likely to survive and reproduce, so selection keeps the sequence unchanged [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** The Hox gene codes for a transcription factor that switches on the genes for structures in one segment [1]. The mutation changes which genes are switched on in that segment, so the wrong structures form [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep homeobox genes (plants, animals and fungi) separate from Hox genes (a subset in animals).

**11. (a)** (48 − 6) / 48 × 100 [1] = **87.5%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The digits are joined by webbing (not separated) [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Genes trigger programmed cell death of the cells between the digits [1]. Caspase enzymes break the cells down and the fragments are removed, leaving separate digits [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Mitosis increases cell number, so the limb grows [1]. The new cells are genetically identical, so each has all the genes needed to specialise [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Percentage decrease is the change divided by the original value.

**12. (a)** Without: 27 / 300 × 100 = **9.0%** [1]. With: 51 / 300 × 100 = **17.0%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The growth factor binds to a receptor on the cell [1]. This activates the genes that drive the cell cycle, so more cells enter mitosis [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The damage is detected inside the cell (an internal stimulus) [1]. The genes for apoptosis are switched on, so the cell is broken down instead of dividing [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** The damaged cell survives and may keep dividing, forming a tumour [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote the two mitotic index values when comparing, and name each stimulus as internal or external.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "the gene is faulty" without naming the codon, amino acid or polypeptide affected.
- Saying all deletions cause frameshifts; a deletion of three keeps the frame.
- Mixing up the operator, promoter and regulatory gene, or saying lactose binds the operator.
- Saying introns are removed from DNA, or removed in the cytoplasm.
- Calling apoptosis damage-caused death rather than programmed death.
- Dividing by the wrong value in a percentage change, or forgetting the stop codon.
