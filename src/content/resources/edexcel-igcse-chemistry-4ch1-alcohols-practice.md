These questions cover sub-topic 4(e) Alcohols of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024): points 4.29C to 4.33C. Every point has a C reference, so **all of these questions are Paper 2 only**. Use Ar values: H = 1, C = 12, O = 16, and take the molar volume of a gas as 24 dm³ at room temperature and pressure. Revise with the [alcohols study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-alcohols/) and the [alcohols revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-alcohols-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** (Paper 2 only) Name the functional group in an alcohol. **[1]**

**2.** (Paper 2 only) Name the alcohol CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH. **[1]**

**3.** (Paper 2 only) Draw the displayed formula of ethanol. **[2]**

**4.** (Paper 2 only) Give the molecular formula of the alcohol with five carbon atoms in a chain, using the general formula. **[1]**

**5.** (Paper 2 only) State the colour change when ethanol is heated with acidified potassium dichromate(VI). **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** (Paper 2 only) Ethanol burns completely in air.

**(a)** Write the balanced equation. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide formed when 13.8 g of ethanol (Mr = 46) burns completely. **[3]**

**7.** (Paper 2 only) Ethanol can be made by fermentation of glucose.

**(a)** Write the balanced equation for fermentation. **[2]**
**(b)** State the temperature used and the reason for it. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why air must be excluded. **[2]**

**8.** (Paper 2 only) 90 g of glucose (Mr = 180) is fermented. The mass of ethanol formed is 36.8 g.

**(a)** Calculate the moles of glucose. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of ethanol that could form. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage yield. **[1]**

**9.** (Paper 2 only) Ethanol is also made by reacting ethene with steam.

**(a)** Write the balanced equation and state the catalyst. **[2]**
**(b)** State the temperature and pressure used. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass of ethanol that could be made from 14 g of ethene (Mr = 28). **[2]**

**10.** (Paper 2 only) Compare the two methods of making ethanol by giving two advantages of fermentation and two advantages of the hydration of ethene. **[4]**

**11.** (Paper 2 only) A bottle of wine is left open for a week and it turns sour. Explain what has happened. **[3]**

**12.** (Paper 2 only) Describe how you would show that ethanol can be oxidised by acidified potassium dichromate(VI), and name the product. **[3]**

**13.** (Paper 2 only) The atom economy of a reaction is the mass of the wanted product as a percentage of the total mass of all the products. Calculate the atom economy for ethanol in (a) the hydration of ethene (C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH) and (b) the fermentation of glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂, with Mr of CO₂ = 44 and of ethanol = 46). **[3]**

**14.** (Paper 2 only) Ethanol made by fermentation can be used as a fuel. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of using it. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** -OH (hydroxyl) [1].

**2.** Butan-1-ol (butanol) [1].

**3.** Two carbon atoms joined by a single bond [1]; each carbon has four bonds in total; the end carbon is bonded to an O-H group (O bonded to C and to H), with five other hydrogen atoms [1].

**4.** C₅H₁₁OH (C₅H₁₂O) [1].

**5.** Orange to green [1].

**6. (a)** C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O [1].
**(b)** Moles of ethanol = 13.8 / 46 = 0.30 mol [1]. Moles of CO₂ = 2 x 0.30 = 0.60 mol [1]. Volume = 0.60 x 24 = 14.4 dm³ [1].

**7. (a)** C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH [1] + 2CO₂ [1].
**(b)** About 30 °C [1]. It is warm enough for the enzymes in yeast to work quickly, but not so hot that the enzymes denature [1].
**(c)** Oxygen would oxidise the ethanol to ethanoic acid [1] (or the yeast would respire aerobically), so the yield of ethanol would be lower [1].

**8. (a)** 90 / 180 = 0.50 mol [1].
**(b)** Moles of ethanol = 2 x 0.50 = 1.0 mol [1]; mass = 1.0 x 46 = 46 g [1].
**(c)** 36.8 / 46 x 100 = 80% [1].

**9. (a)** C₂H₄ + H₂O ⇌ C₂H₅OH [1]; phosphoric acid catalyst [1].
**(b)** About 300 °C [1]; 60 to 70 atm [1].
**(c)** Moles of ethene = 14 / 28 = 0.50 mol [1]; mass of ethanol = 0.50 x 46 = 23 g [1].

**10.** Any two for fermentation: renewable raw material; low temperature and pressure so lower energy cost; cheap equipment [2]. Any two for hydration: fast reaction; continuous process; pure product (no distillation of a dilute solution) [2].

**11.** Air has got into the bottle [1]. Bacteria in the air oxidise the ethanol using oxygen [1]. The product is ethanoic acid (vinegar), which tastes sour [1].

**12.** Warm ethanol with acidified potassium dichromate(VI) solution (heating gently, under reflux) [1]. The orange solution turns green [1]. Ethanoic acid is formed [1].

**13. (a)** There is only one product, so the atom economy is 46 / 46 x 100 = 100% [1].
**(b)** Mass of ethanol = 2 x 46 = 92; total mass of products = 92 + 2 x 44 = 180 [1]. Atom economy = 92 / 180 x 100 = 51.1% [1].

**14.** Advantage (any one): made from a renewable raw material (plants); the carbon dioxide released when it burns was absorbed as the plants grew [1]. Disadvantage (any one): needs farmland that could be used for food; growing, fermenting and distilling use energy; fermentation is slow [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting that fermentation gives two moles of ethanol per mole of glucose.
- Writing the colour change as green to orange.
- Omitting carbon dioxide from the fermentation equation.
- Saying the yeast is killed at 30 °C or is "more active" at 80 °C.
- Giving hydration conditions without the catalyst, temperature or pressure.
- Drawing the alcohol group as C-H-O.
