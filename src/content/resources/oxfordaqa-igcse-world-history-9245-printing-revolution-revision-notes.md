**Condensed notes on "The Printing Revolution (c1440-c1800)" in Technology and change, c800-present day.** The specification names printing in Asia, Gutenberg's press, "the economies of book production; the development of printing industry, typefaces and layouts; new professions and opportunities, including for women; vernacular languages, copyright and intellectual property; the impact on the Renaissance and Reformation in Europe; increased literacy... libraries and universities; the printing revolution and global scientific progress." Full explanations are in the [Printing Revolution study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-world-history-9245-printing-revolution/); the other five periods are in the [Paper 2 Section B revision notes](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-world-history-thematic-studies-revision-notes/).

## Gutenberg's system

| Element | Why it mattered |
|---|---|
| Metal type cast in a hand mould | Thousands of identical, reusable letters |
| Screw press | Even pressure; fast printing |
| Oil-based ink | Stuck to metal type |
| Paper | Cheaper than parchment |

## Economics of printing

- High start-up costs; low cost per copy.
- Risk of unsold stock; many printers failed.
- Finance from merchants; sales at fairs such as Frankfurt.

## Industry, type and layout

- Centres: Mainz, Venice (Aldus Manutius), Westminster (Caxton).
- Type: Gothic (blackletter), roman, italic.
- Layout: title pages, page numbers, chapter headings, indexes, woodcut and engraved illustrations.

## Jobs and women

- New jobs: typefounder, compositor, pressman, proofreader, illustrator, binder, bookseller, publisher.
- Widows ran presses, e.g. Charlotte Guillard in Paris; women as compositors, binders, sellers and authors.

## Language and law

- From Latin to **vernacular** books; Luther's and Tyndale's New Testaments.
- Standardised spelling and national languages.
- Privileges from rulers; Stationers' Company (1557); Statute of Anne (1710): origins of **intellectual property**.

## Linking to the rest of the thematic study

- **Compared with the Islamic Golden Age:** both periods preserved and spread knowledge, but printing multiplied identical copies, while the House of Wisdom depended on translation and hand-copying.
- **Compared with the Communications Revolution:** printing spread ideas over months and years; the telegraph, telephone and radio spread them in seconds.
- **Factors:** individuals (Gutenberg, Manutius, Caxton), economics (profit, investors), religion (the Reformation) and government (privileges, censorship) all shaped the period.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Woodblock printing | Printing a page carved into one block of wood |
| Movable type | Individual letters arranged and reused |
| Compositor | Worker who sets type |
| Vernacular | The everyday language of a region |
| Privilege | A ruler's grant of sole right to print a work |
| Copyright | Legal right to copy and sell a work |
| Intellectual property | Ownership of ideas and creative work |
| Pamphlet | Short, cheap printed work |
| Censorship | Control of what may be printed |

## Exam traps

- Saying Gutenberg invented printing.
- Describing the press but not its consequences.
- Forgetting women, copyright and the global spread.
- Writing only about religion when the question asks about science or society.
- In a 9-mark answer, mixing two ways into one paragraph.

## Self-test

1. What is the earliest dated printed book, and where was it found?
2. Who made clay movable type in the 1040s?
3. Why did movable type suit European languages better than Chinese?
4. Name three elements Gutenberg combined.
5. Why did printing need investors?
6. Who introduced italic type, and where?
7. Who set up the first press in England?
8. Give two jobs created by printing.
9. Which 1710 law is often seen as the first copyright law?
10. Give two ways printing helped the Reformation.
11. Name two scientific works published in 1543.
12. Where did Ibrahim Müteferrika set up a press?

## Answers

1. The *Diamond Sutra* of 868, found at Dunhuang.
2. Bi Sheng.
3. European alphabets need a few dozen letters; Chinese uses thousands of characters.
4. Any three: metal type cast in a mould; a screw press; oil-based ink; paper.
5. Type, presses, paper and workers had to be paid for before any copies were sold.
6. Aldus Manutius, in Venice (1501).
7. William Caxton, at Westminster (1476).
8. Any two: typefounder, compositor, pressman, proofreader, illustrator, binder, bookseller, publisher.
9. The Statute of Anne.
10. Any two: rapid spread of Luther's ideas; illustrated pamphlets; vernacular Bibles; cheap copies beyond Church control.
11. Copernicus's work on the Earth moving around the Sun; Vesalius's *On the Fabric of the Human Body*.
12. Istanbul, in the 1720s.
