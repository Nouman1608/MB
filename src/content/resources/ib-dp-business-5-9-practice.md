## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *cybercrime*. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between data and information. **[2]**

**3.** Outline what is meant by big data. **[2]**

**4.** Lanyard Hotels is choosing security measures. Identify two cybersecurity measures it could use. **[2]**

**5.** Lanyard Hotels estimates a 0.12 chance of a serious breach this year, which would cost 350,000.

**(a)** Calculate the expected loss. **[1]**

**(b)** A security package costing 18,000 would cut the chance to 0.05. Calculate the expected cost with the package and state whether it is worth buying on expected values. **[3]**

**6.** Explain one way a farm could use the internet of things. **[2]**

**7.** Explain one benefit of virtual reality for training employees in a chemical plant. **[2]**

**8.** Explain why an artificial intelligence system used to approve loans could treat applicants unfairly. **[3]**

### Section B: structured questions

**9.** Brindle Books, an online bookseller, uses data mining on customer purchase histories. It finds that customers who buy travel guides are much more likely than average to buy luggage within a month. Brindle does not sell luggage.

**(a)** Explain how Brindle could use this finding. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain two limitations of acting on this finding. **[4]**

**10.** Kestrel Warehousing gives each picker a wrist device that sets the next task, records the time taken and ranks pickers daily. Output per picker has risen, but staff turnover has risen from 18% to 31% a year.

**(a)** Explain why Kestrel's system is an example of Digital Taylorism. **[2]**

**(b)** Kestrel employs 400 pickers. Calculate how many more pickers leave each year at the new turnover rate than at the old one. **[2]**

**(c)** Evaluate Kestrel's use of data to manage and monitor its employees. **[8]**

**11.** Ottery Pharmacy, a chain of 60 stores, is deciding whether to launch a loyalty programme with a mobile app. Its main rival already has one.

Discuss the benefits, risks and ethical implications of the programme for Ottery Pharmacy and its stakeholders. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Crime committed using computers and networks [1], such as hacking, ransomware, phishing or theft of personal data [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Data are raw, unprocessed facts such as individual sales [1], whereas information is data that has been processed and presented so that it is meaningful for a decision, such as weekly sales by store [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Data sets so large, arriving so fast and in such variety [1] that ordinary software cannot store or analyse them, so specialist systems are needed [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: staff training to spot phishing [1]; strong passwords and access controls [1]; encryption; regular backups; firewalls and monitoring software. **[2]**

**5. (a)** 0.12 × 350,000 = **42,000** [1].

**(b)** 0.05 × 350,000 = 17,500 [1]; 17,500 + 18,000 = **35,500** [1]; this is less than 42,000, so the package is worth buying on expected values, saving 6,500 a year [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Add the cost of the package to the reduced expected loss before comparing. Comparing 17,500 with 42,000 alone overstates the benefit.

**6.** Soil-moisture sensors send readings to a system that switches irrigation on only where needed [1], cutting water and energy costs and improving crop yields [1]. Also accept livestock tracking or machine monitoring. **[2]**

**7.** Employees can practise handling dangerous equipment or emergencies in a simulated environment [1], so they learn without risk of injury or costly damage, and scenarios can be repeated until they are confident [1]. **[2]**

**8.** The system learns patterns from past lending decisions [1]. If those decisions were biased against a group, the AI repeats the bias [1], and because its reasoning may not be transparent, applicants and managers may not be able to see or challenge why a loan was refused [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** Brindle could form a partnership with a luggage retailer, showing luggage offers to travel-guide buyers in return for a commission [1], or start selling a small range of luggage targeted at those customers [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** The finding shows association, not cause: both purchases may be driven by a planned trip, so offers may not change behaviour [1][1]. Customers may object to their purchase history being used to target offers from another business, damaging trust and raising legal or privacy concerns [1][1]. Also accept poor data quality or limited sample. **[6]**

**10. (a)** The work is broken into small, measured tasks set by the device [1], with standard times and continuous monitoring of output, which applies Taylor's scientific management through digital tools [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Old: 18% × 400 = 72. New: 31% × 400 = 124 [1]. Difference = **52 more pickers a year** [1].

**(c)** For: output per picker has risen, lowering labour cost per order [1]; managers get real-time information to spot problems and plan staffing [1]. Against: 52 extra leavers a year raise recruitment and training costs and may offset the productivity gain [1]; constant ranking may cause stress and lower morale, and pickers may cut corners on safety or accuracy to keep up [1]; privacy concerns and loss of autonomy may damage Kestrel's reputation as an employer [1]. Ethics: is the monitoring proportionate and are staff told how the data is used [1]? Conditions: depends on whether the cost of higher turnover is smaller than the productivity gain [1]. Judgement: Kestrel should keep task data for planning but stop daily public rankings and add measures of accuracy and safety, because the turnover figure suggests the current system is not sustainable [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* The turnover figure is evidence that the system has costs. Using it turns a general discussion of monitoring into an evaluation of this business.

**11.** Benefits to Ottery: repeat purchases and a switching cost that keeps customers from the rival [1]; purchase data allows targeted offers and better stock decisions across 60 stores [1]; matching the rival removes a competitive disadvantage [1]. Risks: rewards and app development reduce margins [1]; extra spending may come from customers who would have shopped at Ottery anyway [1]; a large customer database is a target for cybercrime [1]. Ethics and stakeholders: pharmacy purchases can reveal health conditions, so customers' privacy is especially sensitive [1]; customers must give informed consent and know how data is used, and offers should not exploit vulnerable customers [1]; employees may need training to explain and handle the data [1]. Judgement: Ottery should launch the programme because the rival already has one, but only with strong security and clear limits on the use of health-related data, since a breach would cost more than the programme gains [1]. **[10]**

*Examiner insight:* The pharmacy setting makes the ethics distinctive. Answers that notice health data is sensitive apply the concept to the case rather than repeating general points.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving only benefits of technology in an AO3 answer.
- Treating a data-mining pattern as proof of cause.
- Comparing reduced expected loss with the original loss without adding the security cost.
- Ignoring employees' views in monitoring questions.
- Not using case data, such as turnover figures, as evidence.
- Ending without a judgement and conditions.

## Frequently asked questions

**What do these questions cover?**
Section 5.9 of the IB DP Business Management guide (first assessment 2024): data analytics, databases, cybersecurity, critical infrastructures, VR, IoT, AI, big data, loyalty programmes, monitoring employees, data mining and ethics. The section is HL only.

**How do you judge whether security spending is worthwhile?**
Compare the expected loss without the measure with the reduced expected loss plus the cost of the measure, then question the estimates behind them.
