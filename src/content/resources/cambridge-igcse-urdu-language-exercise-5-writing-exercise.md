Exercise 5 of **Paper 1 Reading and Writing** in Cambridge IGCSE Urdu as a Second Language (0539) is a
short piece of **functional writing** worth **8 marks**. You write about **120 words**, such as an
email, in response to a stimulus of short prompts and/or pictures, for a purpose, format and audience
that the question specifies. It is the first of the two writing exercises; with Exercise 6 it carries
24 of the paper's 60 marks. This guide goes deeper on that one exercise than the
[Paper 1 Reading and Writing guide](/resources/igcse-cambridge-urdu-paper-1-reading-writing/).

## What the syllabus says

The 2025-2027 syllabus describes the task as "a short piece of functional prose such as an email, of
approximately 120 words", with a stimulus of short prompts and/or pictures printed in the question
paper, and a **purpose, format and audience** specified. All five writing objectives are tested:

- **W1** communicate information, ideas and opinions clearly, accurately and effectively
- **W2** organise ideas into coherent paragraphs using a range of linking devices
- **W3** use a range of grammatical structures and vocabulary accurately and effectively
- **W4** show control of punctuation and spelling
- **W5** use appropriate register and style/format for the purpose and audience

The syllabus's writing content lists formats such as an informal letter or email, an article, a blog,
a report and a review. For Exercise 5 the named example is an email, so prepare that format thoroughly,
and be ready for a closely related one such as an informal letter or a short message.

The syllabus does not publish how the eight marks divide. On this site's practice pages we use a
working convention of **up to 4 for task and communication** and **up to 4 for language**. That is our
practice convention, not Cambridge's scheme.

## Decoding the task: purpose, format, audience

Before writing a word, answer three questions from the task:

| Question | Examples | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose:** why am I writing? | دعوت دینا، شکریہ ادا کرنا، معذرت کرنا، معلومات دینا، مشورہ دینا، شکایت کرنا | the content of every paragraph |
| **Format:** what kind of text? | ای میل، خط، پیغام | the opening, closing and layout |
| **Audience:** who reads it? | دوست، بھائی، استاد، پرنسپل، مینیجر | the register (تم or آپ) |

Then count the **prompts**. Each bullet point in the stimulus is content you must cover. Missing one
costs content marks however good the rest of the piece is. If the stimulus includes **pictures**, they
suggest what to write about (for example, a picture of a park and a bicycle might stand for "where we
went" and "how we got there"); use them rather than ignoring them.

## Register: تم or آپ, and keeping to it

The audience decides the register, and the register decides grammar as well as vocabulary.

| | Informal (friend, sibling, cousin) | Formal (teacher, principal, manager) |
|---|---|---|
| Address | تم | آپ |
| Opening | پیارے احمد، / پیاری ثنا، | محترم پرنسپل صاحب، / محترمہ… |
| Greeting | السلام علیکم! کیسے ہو؟ | السلام علیکم۔ امید ہے آپ خیریت سے ہوں گے۔ |
| Request | مجھے بتاؤ، ضرور آنا | مجھے بتائیے، تشریف لائیے گا |
| Thanks | تمہارا بہت شکریہ | میں آپ کا بے حد شکر گزار ہوں |
| Closing line | جلدی جواب دینا۔ اپنا خیال رکھنا۔ | آپ کے جواب کا منتظر رہوں گا / رہوں گی۔ |
| Sign-off | تمہارا دوست، / تمہاری سہیلی، | والسلام، / آپ کا شاگرد، |

The most common register error is **mixing** the two: starting with آپ and slipping into تم, or using
آپ with a تم verb form (آپ آؤ instead of آپ آئیے). Once you choose, check every verb and pronoun
against it: تم takes forms such as **ہو، کرو، آؤ، تمہارا**; آپ takes **ہیں، کیجیے، آئیے، آپ کا**.

**Gender in your own verbs.** You are the writer, so your verbs agree with you: a boy writes **لکھ رہا
ہوں**, a girl **لکھ رہی ہوں**. Keep this consistent from opening to signature.

## Planning 120 words

120 words is short. A simple plan stops you spending 80 words on the first prompt.

```
Opening and greeting            10-15 words
Prompt 1 (one short paragraph)  about 30 words
Prompt 2                        about 30 words
Prompt 3                        about 30 words
Closing line and sign-off       10-15 words
```

For each prompt, aim for **statement + detail or reason + feeling or comment**. This turns a list
into communication and naturally adds connectives (کیونکہ، اس لیے، لیکن) and a range of tenses.

**Tense range** comes from the prompts themselves: "what happened" invites the past, "what you do now"
the present, and "invite" or "plan" the future or a conditional (اگر… تو…). Using all three where the
prompts allow is the easiest way to show W3 range.

## Worked example

**Task (written for this guide):** آپ کا دوست سلمان دوسرے شہر منتقل ہو گیا ہے۔ اسے تقریباً 120
الفاظ کا ای میل لکھیے:

- بتائیے کہ آپ نے اسکول کے کس نئے کلب میں داخلہ لیا ہے؛
- پچھلے ہفتے کلب کے ساتھ کیا کیا؛
- اسے کلب کے کسی پروگرام میں آنے کی دعوت دیجیے۔

**Purpose:** inform and invite. **Format:** email. **Audience:** a friend, so تم.

**Model email** (about 125 words):

> پیارے سلمان،
>
> السلام علیکم! امید ہے تم نئے شہر میں خوش ہو گے۔ یہ بتانے کے لیے لکھ رہا ہوں کہ میں نے اسکول کے فوٹوگرافی کلب میں داخلہ لے لیا ہے۔ ہم ہر جمعرات کو چھٹی کے بعد ملتے ہیں اور ہمارے استاد ہمیں روشنی اور زاویوں کے بارے میں سکھاتے ہیں۔
>
> پچھلے ہفتے ہم پرانا قلعہ دیکھنے گئے تھے۔ میں نے وہاں غروبِ آفتاب کی ایک تصویر کھینچی جو سب کو بہت پسند آئی۔ سچ پوچھو تو مجھے خود یقین نہیں آیا کہ یہ تصویر میں نے کھینچی ہے!
>
> اگلے مہینے کی پندرہ تاریخ کو کلب کی ایک نمائش ہو گی۔ اگر تم ان دنوں چھٹیوں پر یہاں آؤ تو ضرور آنا، میں تمہیں اپنی ساری تصویریں دکھاؤں گا۔
>
> جلدی جواب دینا۔
>
> تمہارا دوست،
> بلال

**Why it works:**

- **All three prompts** are covered, each in its own short paragraph.
- **Register** is consistently informal: تم، پوچھو، آنا، تمہیں، تمہارا.
- **Tense range:** present habit (ملتے ہیں، سکھاتے ہیں), past (گئے تھے، کھینچی), future (ہو گی،
  دکھاؤں گا) and a conditional (اگر… تو…).
- **The ergative نے** is used correctly with transitive perfective verbs: میں نے… داخلہ لے لیا، میں نے…
  تصویر کھینچی (the verb agrees with the feminine تصویر).
- **Development:** the photo is not only mentioned; the writer adds a reaction (مجھے خود یقین نہیں آیا),
  which gives the piece a personal voice.
- **Format:** a greeting, an opening line that states why he is writing, a closing request and a
  sign-off.

## Common mistakes

1. **Missing a prompt**, often the last one, written in a hurry.
2. **Mixing تم and آپ**, or using the wrong verb form for the pronoun.
3. **Ignoring the format:** no greeting or sign-off in a letter or email.
4. **Listing instead of developing:** "ہم قلعہ گئے۔ تصویر کھینچی۔ نمائش ہو گی۔" covers the prompts but
   communicates very little.
5. **Writing far too much.** "Approximately 120 words" is guidance on scope; a 250-word answer takes
   time from Exercise 6 and adds chances for error.
6. **Copying the prompt wording** as your sentences instead of writing your own.
7. **Agreement errors:** نئی کتاب, not نیا کتاب; میں نے کتاب خریدی, not میں نے کتاب خریدا.
8. **English word order:** the verb comes at the end in Urdu.

## Exam technique and timing

Exercise 5 carries 8 marks. Around **15 minutes** is a sensible budget: 2 minutes to note purpose,
format, audience and a word for each prompt; 10 minutes to write; 3 minutes to check. Keep more time
for Exercise 6, which is worth twice as much.

A final check that takes under two minutes:

- every prompt covered?
- one register throughout?
- greeting, opening line, closing line and sign-off present?
- your own verbs agree with your gender?
- نے with every transitive verb in a completed past tense?
- adjectives and verbs agree with their nouns?
