AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) is the separate science Chemistry GCSE, for students studying chemistry as a full
qualification rather than as part of combined science. It moves from atoms and bonding through quantitative work,
reactions and energy to organic chemistry, analysis, the atmosphere and the use of resources. The course is linear
and tiered, with Foundation and Higher entries. On either tier, students sit two written papers, each lasting
1 hour 45 minutes, worth 100 marks and carrying 50% of the GCSE.

## How the course is organised

The specification has ten content topics and a closing section of key ideas.

**Topics 1 to 5: the building blocks and core reactions.** Atomic structure and the periodic table covers a simple
model of the atom, symbols, relative atomic mass, electronic charge and isotopes, the periodic table and the
properties of transition metals (the last of these is chemistry only). Bonding, structure and the properties of
matter covers chemical bonds, ionic bonding and ionic compounds, covalent bonding and metallic bonding. These are
followed by quantitative chemistry, chemical changes and energy changes.

**Topics 6 to 10: rates, carbon chemistry and the wider world.** The rate and extent of chemical change, organic
chemistry, chemical analysis, chemistry of the atmosphere and using resources take the core ideas into new
contexts, from how fast reactions go to how chemists identify substances and how human activity affects the
planet.

**Topic 11: key ideas.** This section brings together the central ideas that recur across the course.

Content labelled "chemistry only" belongs to the separate Chemistry GCSE and is not part of combined science.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Tier | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Topics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8462/1F Paper 1 | Foundation | 1 hour 45 minutes | 100 | 50% | 1-5 |
| 8462/1H Paper 1 | Higher | 1 hour 45 minutes | 100 | 50% | 1-5 |
| 8462/2F Paper 2 | Foundation | 1 hour 45 minutes | 100 | 50% | 6-10 |
| 8462/2H Paper 2 | Higher | 1 hour 45 minutes | 100 | 50% | 6-10 |

Each tier has its own Paper 1 and Paper 2, so Foundation and Higher candidates sit different question papers.
Paper 1 covers topics 1 to 5. Paper 2 covers topics 6 to 10 and may also draw on the fundamental concepts in
sections 4.1 to 4.3 of the specification, which are atomic structure and the periodic table, bonding and
structure, and quantitative chemistry.

The assessment objectives are weighted AO1 40%, AO2 40% and AO3 20% overall. All papers are externally assessed.
The specification was first taught in September 2016 and first examined in May/June 2018.

## How the parts fit together

The rule that Paper 2 can reach back into sections 4.1 to 4.3 is the clearest statement of how the course hangs
together. Atomic structure, bonding and quantitative chemistry are not just Paper 1 content; they are the tools
used to explain everything on Paper 2. Rates of reaction need particle ideas; organic chemistry depends on
covalent bonding; chemical analysis and using resources often involve calculations from quantitative chemistry.

There are many other links worth noticing. Chemical changes and energy changes on Paper 1 set up the reversible
reactions and equilibrium ideas met in the rate and extent of chemical change. The atmosphere topic draws on
combustion and the products of reactions. Using resources connects to rates, equilibrium and quantitative work
through industrial processes and yields. Seeing these connections makes the content smaller, not larger, because
the same ideas keep doing the work.

With a fifth of the marks on AO3, students also need practice in interpreting data and evaluating methods, so
the experimental work done in lessons is worth reviewing as carefully as the theory.

## Planning your study

A sensible route follows the topic numbers:

1. **Begin with atomic structure and the periodic table, then bonding and structure.** These explain nearly
   everything that follows.
2. **Teach quantitative chemistry early and revisit it often.** Calculations improve with regular, short
   practice rather than one intensive block.
3. **Complete chemical changes and energy changes** to finish the Paper 1 content.
4. **Move through rates, organic chemistry, analysis, the atmosphere and resources** for Paper 2, linking each
   back to the first three topics.

Review the key ideas section after each topic. Topic questions can be used straight away; full past papers are
most useful once a paper's content is covered, and because Paper 2 can draw on the foundation topics, revisit
those before attempting Paper 2 practice. Your teacher will confirm your tier; preparing to the Higher standard
where possible keeps both options open. Paper-specific exam technique is on the exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers does AQA GCSE Chemistry have?**
Two. On either tier, each paper lasts 1 hour 45 minutes, is worth 100 marks and counts for 50% of the GCSE.

**Which topics are on Paper 1 and Paper 2?**
Paper 1 covers topics 1-5, from atomic structure to energy changes. Paper 2 covers topics 6-10, from the rate and extent of chemical change to using resources, and may also draw on sections 4.1-4.3.

**Is AQA GCSE Chemistry tiered?**
Yes. There are Foundation and Higher tiers, and each tier sits its own Paper 1 and Paper 2.

**How are the assessment objectives weighted?**
AO1 carries 40%, AO2 40% and AO3 20% of the qualification.

**When was this specification first examined?**
It was first taught in September 2016 and first examined in May/June 2018.
