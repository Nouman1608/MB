Condensed notes for section 5.3 of Cambridge IGCSE Commerce (0715). For fuller explanations, two worked examples and an annotated answer, use the [insurance study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-commerce-0715-insurance/).

## The syllabus in three parts

1. **5.3.1 Purposes of insurance:** risk reduction, compensation, financial protection, business confidence, investment.
2. **5.3.2 Types of insurance risk:** consequential loss, employer's liability, fire, motor, premises, public liability, theft.
3. **5.3.3 How enterprises use insurance:** pooling of risk, calculating premiums, insurance documents, the insurance broker.

## Five purposes

Mnemonic **"Rabbits Can Find Big Imitations"**:

| Letter | Purpose | One-line meaning |
|---|---|---|
| R | Risk reduction | Financial risk passes to the insurer; safety measures earn lower premiums |
| C | Compensation | Payment after an insured loss |
| F | Financial protection | One big loss does not ruin the business |
| B | Business confidence | Owners are willing to trade and expand |
| I | Investment | Insurers invest premiums; some policies build savings |

## Seven types of risk

| Risk | Covers | Do not confuse with |
|---|---|---|
| Consequential loss | Lost profit and extra costs after an insured event | Direct damage to property |
| Employer's liability | Injury or illness of employees | Public liability |
| Fire | Damage to buildings and stock by fire | Premises (wider damage) |
| Motor | Vehicle damage, theft, liability to others | Public liability |
| Premises | Damage to the building and fixtures (storm, flood, explosion) | Theft of stock |
| Public liability | Injury to customers or the public | Employer's liability |
| Theft | Stolen goods, cash, equipment | Fire |

Tip: ask **who** is hurt or **what** is lost. Employee = employer's liability. Customer or passer-by = public liability. Lost income = consequential loss.

## Pooling of risk

- Many people or firms pay premiums into one fund.
- Only a few suffer a loss each year.
- The fund pays the claims, so the cost is shared.
- Insurers use records of past claims to predict how many claims will occur in a large group.

## Premiums

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Meaning | The price paid for insurance |
| Must cover | Claims, running costs and profit |
| Formula | Premium = value insured x rate |
| Raises the premium | Higher value, high-risk place or activity, past claims |
| Lowers the premium | Alarms, sprinklers, good security, no claims |

Worked pattern: stock $40,000; rate 0.6%. Premium = 40,000 x 0.6 / 100 = $240.

## Three documents, in order

| Order | Document | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Proposal form | Person applying gives details of what is to be insured, its value and claims history |
| 2 | Cover note | Temporary proof of cover until the policy is ready |
| 3 | Policy | Formal contract: what is covered, exclusions, sum insured, premium, claims conditions |

False answers on a proposal form can lead the insurer to refuse a claim.

## Insurance broker

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| What | An independent agent who arranges insurance between customers and insurers |
| Does | Advises, compares insurers, completes paperwork, helps with claims |
| Paid by | Commission from the insurer |
| Benefit | Saves time and finds suitable cover and price |
| Limit | Commission may influence which insurer is recommended |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Insurance | Paying a premium so an insurer will pay for specified losses |
| Premium | The price of insurance cover |
| Insurer | The company that provides the cover |
| Policy | The insurance contract |
| Claim | A request to the insurer to pay for a loss |
| Sum insured | The maximum amount the insurer will pay |
| Pooling | Sharing risk among many contributors |
| Broker | Independent agent who arranges insurance |
| Proposal form | Application giving details of the risk |
| Cover note | Temporary evidence of insurance |
| Consequential loss | Lost profit following an insured event |

## Exam traps

- Insurance does not stop a loss; it pays for it.
- **Employer's liability** is about staff; **public liability** is about customers and the public.
- Do not call consequential loss "damage to the building".
- Keep the order of documents: proposal form, cover note, policy.
- When explaining pooling, say that **few** contributors claim, and the fund pays them.
- A premium is a price **paid by the customer**, not a payment received from the insurer.

## Self-test

1. State **two** purposes of insurance.
2. What is meant by compensation?
3. Name the seven types of insurance risk in the syllabus.
4. Which insurance covers an employee hurt at work?
5. Which insurance covers a customer who slips in a shop?
6. Define consequential loss.
7. Explain pooling of risk in one sentence.
8. Give **two** factors that could lower a premium.
9. What is a cover note?
10. In what order are the three insurance documents used?
11. State **two** things an insurance broker does.
12. A café insures equipment worth $25,000 at a rate of 0.4%. Calculate the premium.

**Answers**

1. Any two: risk reduction; compensation; financial protection; business confidence; investment.
2. Payment by the insurer to restore the insured person or business after a loss.
3. Consequential loss, employer's liability, fire, motor, premises, public liability, theft.
4. Employer's liability.
5. Public liability.
6. The loss of profit and extra costs a business suffers because of an insured event, such as closing after a fire.
7. Many people pay into one fund and the few who suffer losses are paid from it, so the risk is shared.
8. Any two: alarms or sprinklers; good security; few or no past claims; low-risk location.
9. A temporary document that proves cover until the full policy is issued.
10. Proposal form, cover note, policy.
11. Any two: gives advice; compares insurers; completes the paperwork; helps with claims.
12. 25,000 x 0.4 / 100 = $100.
