Cambridge A-Level Global Perspectives and Research (9239) is built from four components, all externally assessed. Component 1, Written Exam (1 hour 30 minutes, 45 marks, 18% of the A Level); Component 2, Essay (40 marks, 16%); Component 3, Team Project (40 marks, 16%); and Component 4, Cambridge Research Report (85 marks, 50%). Components 1 to 3 form the AS Level, and Component 4 completes the full A Level. First assessment was 2026. Support pages cover the [Written Exam](/resources/a-level-cambridge-global-perspectives-written-exam/), the [Essay](/resources/a-level-cambridge-global-perspectives-essay/), the [Team Project](/resources/a-level-cambridge-global-perspectives-team-project/) and the [Cambridge Research Report](/resources/a-level-global-perspectives-9239-component-4-cambridge-research-report/).

## AS Level or full A Level

The AS Level here has three components, 1, 2 and 3, not two. It can be a standalone qualification, or the first three components of the A Level, completed by adding Component 4 in a later series. Percentages on this page are shares of the full A Level (18 + 16 + 16 + 50 = 100). For the AS route the shares are different: 36%, 32% and 32%.

## Component 1: Written Exam (45 marks, 18%)

The only timed paper: 1 hour 30 minutes for 45 marks. It is the place to show analytical reading, the evaluation of arguments and the weighing of perspectives under time pressure. Practise with unfamiliar source material and aim for balanced, reasoned judgements. See the [Written Exam revision notes](/resources/a-level-cambridge-global-perspectives-written-exam-revision-notes/).

## Component 2: Essay (40 marks, 16%)

A 1,750 to 2,000 word essay, with no timed paper. The syllabus structure makes it a non-exam assessment, so the key skills are planning, sourcing and argument over several weeks. Build a clear line of reasoning that engages with more than one perspective. A [revision guide to the essay](/resources/a-level-global-perspectives-essay-revision-notes/) is available.

## Component 3: Team Project (40 marks, 16%)

An individual presentation plus a reflective paper, arising from a team project. All four components are described as externally assessed, unlike the IGCSE version. Keep a record of your own contribution and what you learned. Notes are in the [Team Project revision notes](/resources/a-level-cambridge-global-perspectives-team-project-revision-notes/).

## Component 4: Cambridge Research Report (85 marks, 50%)

This is half of the A Level and is for the full A Level only. It is an independent research report of up to 5,000 words, together with a research log. Choose a question you can genuinely investigate, keep the research log up to date from the start and build the report in stages. The [Component 4 revision notes](/resources/a-level-global-perspectives-9239-component-4-cambridge-research-report-revision-notes/) support this.

## Timing and marks-per-minute

- Component 1: 90 minutes / 45 marks = 2 minutes per mark.
- Total marks across all four components: 45 + 40 + 40 + 85 = 210.
- Marks for the AS route: 45 + 40 + 40 = 125.
- Value of a mark in the A Level: Component 1, 18 / 45 = 0.4%; Component 2, 16 / 40 = 0.4%; Component 3, 16 / 40 = 0.4%; Component 4, 50 / 85 = about 0.59%.

A mark in the Research Report is therefore worth more than a mark elsewhere. Components 2, 3 and 4 have no exam duration, so build a schedule of drafts and deadlines instead.

## Command words and question styles

- **Analyse**: break an argument down into its parts and examine each.
- **Evaluate**: judge the strength, reliability or impact of evidence or arguments.
- **Compare**: set perspectives side by side.
- **Justify**: give reasons for a choice or conclusion.
- **Reflect**: consider your own learning and decisions.

## Planning your preparation across the course

The four components ask for different working patterns. For Component 1, practise weekly with a short unfamiliar source and write a paragraph evaluating its argument and evidence. For the essay, draft a plan first and check that you consider at least two perspectives before you write the full 1,750 to 2,000 words. For the team project, keep dated notes of your own contribution to the group and of decisions you made. For the Research Report, which carries half of the A Level, begin early: settle the question, set up the research log on day one, and set interim deadlines for reading, drafting and revising so that the final piece of up to 5,000 words is edited rather than rushed. If you are taking the AS route first, you can leave the report until a later series.

## Worked practice scenario

An original planning exercise for Component 4: turning a broad interest into a researchable question.

Starting interest: "Plastic waste."

Refinement steps:

1. Narrow the focus: plastic packaging in coastal cities.
2. Make it a question: "To what extent do deposit-return schemes reduce plastic bottle waste in coastal cities?"
3. Identify perspectives: policymakers, retailers, consumers and environmental groups.
4. Plan evidence: reports and official data, comparison of schemes, and a short survey if suitable.
5. Plan the log: record each source, why you chose it, and what it changed in your thinking.

Model sentence for an introduction: "This report investigates to what extent deposit-return schemes reduce plastic bottle waste in coastal cities, weighing evidence on return rates and litter against costs and convenience for retailers and consumers."

The question is specific, arguable, answerable from evidence and open to more than one conclusion, which the broad topic was not.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Know whether you are taking the AS route or the full A Level.
- Plan the deadlines for Components 2, 3 and 4 early.
- Keep the research log up to date as you go.
- Practise source evaluation for Component 1.
- In Component 1, use about 2 minutes per mark.
- Respect word limits: up to 5,000 words for the report.
- Reference your sources consistently.
- Keep a record of your own contribution in the team project.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many components are in Cambridge A-Level Global Perspectives 9239?**
Four: Written Exam, Essay, Team Project and Cambridge Research Report. All four are externally assessed.

**How long is the written exam?**
It is 1 hour 30 minutes, 45 marks and 18% of the A Level (36% of the AS).

**Can I take only the AS Level?**
Yes. Components 1 to 3 can be a standalone AS Level, or you can add Component 4 in a later series for the full A Level.

**How long is the essay?**
It is 1,750 to 2,000 words and is worth 40 marks, 16% of the A Level (32% of the AS).

**What is the research report?**
It is an independent report of up to 5,000 words plus a research log, worth 85 marks and 50% of the A Level only.
