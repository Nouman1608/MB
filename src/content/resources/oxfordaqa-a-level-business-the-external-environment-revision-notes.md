## Definitions to know

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Market size (volume) | Number of units sold in the market |
| Market size (value) | Sales revenue in the market, which is volume × average price |
| Market growth or decline | Percentage change in market size between two periods |
| Market share | A business's sales as a percentage of total market sales |
| Four firm concentration ratio | Combined market share of the four largest firms |
| Barrier to entry | Anything that makes it hard or costly for a new firm to enter a market |
| Saturated market | Most potential buyers already buy; total sales barely grow |
| Dynamic market | A market that changes fast through new products, technology or tastes |
| Normal good | Demand rises when income rises (YED positive) |
| Inferior good | Demand falls when income rises (YED negative) |
| Income elasticity of demand (YED) | Responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in income |

## Formulae

| Measure | Form |
|---|---|
| Value of market | volume × average price |
| Growth (%) | (new - old) / old × 100 |
| Market share (%) | business sales / market sales × 100 |
| Four firm concentration ratio (%) | share 1 + share 2 + share 3 + share 4 |
| YED | % change in quantity demanded / % change in income |
| Change in quantity demanded (%) | YED × % change in income |

## Method in steps

Percentage change:
1. Subtract old from new.
2. Divide by the OLD figure.
3. Multiply by 100 and keep the sign. A minus sign means decline.

Concentration ratio:
1. Rank the firms and pick the four largest.
2. Turn each into a percentage share if given as sales.
3. Add the four shares.
4. Say what the answer means for competition and for barriers.

Applying YED:
1. Write the YED with its sign.
2. Multiply by the percentage change in income.
3. Apply the percentage to the current quantity.

Answering "analyse the effect of change X":
1. Name the change and who it affects.
2. Say which of costs, demand, decisions or profits moves and why.
3. Link the chain to profit or a decision.
4. For longer questions, weigh how big the effect is for THIS business and whether it is short or long term.

## Worked reminders

Market data. A market sells 250,000 units at 18 currency units (cu) in Year 1 and 270,000 units at 17.50 cu in Year 2.
- Year 1 value: 250,000 × 18 = 4.5m cu. Year 2 value: 270,000 × 17.5 = 4.725m cu.
- Volume growth: 20,000 / 250,000 = 8.0%. Value growth: 0.225m / 4.5m = 5.0%.
- A firm with Year 2 sales of 1.134m cu has share 1.134 / 4.725 = 24.0%.
- Lesson: volume grew faster than value because the average price fell.

Concentration. Shares of the four largest firms are 31%, 22%, 17% and 11%. Ratio = 31 + 22 + 17 + 11 = 81%. A few firms hold most of the market.

YED. Income rises 10% and quantity demanded falls 3%. YED = -3 / 10 = -0.3. Negative, so inferior. A different product with YED +2.0 and income up 3% sees quantity rise 2.0 × 3 = 6%.

## Barriers to entry

| Barrier | Why it blocks entry |
|---|---|
| Entry costs | Large sums needed for premises, equipment, stock or launch |
| Legal requirements | Licences or rules to be met before trading |
| Specialist knowledge | Skills take time and money to acquire |
| Loyalty to existing businesses | Customers will not switch without a strong reason |
| Access to resources or distribution | Scarce supplies or shelf space tied up by rivals |

| | High barriers | Low barriers |
|---|---|---|
| Existing businesses | Protected; steadier share and profit; less pressure to cut price | Threatened by newcomers; pressure on price; need to keep improving |
| Customers | Less choice; possibly higher prices | More choice; keener prices; better service |

## Market conditions

| Condition | Effect on demand | Likely decisions | Profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saturated | Total demand flat; gains come from rivals | Differentiate, promote, cut costs, find new segments | Squeezed if price cutting starts |
| Dynamic | Can change quickly in either direction | Invest in innovation and flexibility | High for adapters, low for laggards |

## External changes at a glance

| Change | Effect on costs | Effect on demand | Decisions and profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Income up | None directly | Normal goods up, inferior goods down | Shift range, stock and promotion |
| Interest rates up | Dearer variable rate loans | Less spending on credit funded goods | Delay investment; lower profit |
| Home currency stronger | Imports cheaper | Exports dearer abroad | Exporters lose competitiveness; importers gain |
| Home currency weaker | Imports dearer | Exports cheaper abroad | The reverse |
| Input costs up | Unit cost up | Falls if price is passed on | Margin squeezed or price raised |
| More competition | May need extra marketing spend | Share falls | Price pressure; profit falls |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Volume against value:** a market can grow in volume and shrink in value if prices fall.
- **Market size against market share:** size describes the whole market, share describes one business.
- **Concentration ratio against share:** the ratio is the total of four shares, not one firm's position.
- **Inferior against low quality:** "inferior" refers only to how demand responds to income.
- **Elastic against inelastic YED:** above 1 in absolute size is more responsive; below 1 is less.
- **Strong against weak currency:** strong makes imports cheap and exports dear; weak does the reverse.

## Quick self-test

1. A market is worth 48m cu and a firm sells 9.6m cu. What is its market share?
2. Market volume falls from 40,000 to 34,000 units. State the percentage change.
3. 120,000 units sell at an average price of 6.50 cu. What is the market value?
4. The four largest firms have sales of 50m, 36m, 30m and 24m cu in a market worth 200m cu. Calculate the four firm concentration ratio.
5. Income rises 8% and quantity demanded rises 12%. Calculate YED and state what type of good it is.
6. YED is -0.4. Income rises 5% and sales are 25,000. Find the new sales figure.
7. A firm borrows 120,000 cu at 4%, then the rate rises to 6%. By how much does annual interest rise?
8. A firm imports goods priced at 30,000 foreign currency units. At 1 cu = 1.5 units, then at 1 cu = 1.2 units, what is the cost in cu each time? Has the currency strengthened or weakened?
9. Give two barriers to entry and say how low barriers affect customers.
10. A product sells for 25 cu with a unit cost of 15 cu. All costs rise 10%. Find the new unit margin and margin as a percentage of price (price unchanged).

### Answers

1. 9.6 / 48 × 100 = **20.0%**.
2. (34,000 - 40,000) / 40,000 × 100 = **-15%**, a decline.
3. 120,000 × 6.50 = **780,000 cu**.
4. (50 + 36 + 30 + 24) / 200 × 100 = 140 / 200 = **70%**.
5. 12 / 8 = **+1.5**; positive, so a normal good, and above 1 so income elastic.
6. -0.4 × 5 = -2%; 25,000 × 0.98 = **24,500**.
7. Interest rises from 4,800 cu to 7,200 cu, an increase of **2,400 cu**.
8. 30,000 / 1.5 = **20,000 cu**; 30,000 / 1.2 = **25,000 cu**. The home currency has **weakened** and imports cost 25% more.
9. For example entry costs and legal requirements. Low barriers mean **more choice, keener prices** and pressure on firms to improve quality and service.
10. New unit cost 15 × 1.10 = 16.5 cu; margin 25 - 16.5 = **8.5 cu**; 8.5 / 25 × 100 = **34%** (down from 40%).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing a percentage change by the new figure instead of the old one.
- Giving a concentration ratio as a sum of sales instead of a percentage.
- Quoting a growth rate with no sign, or with the wrong sign when the market has shrunk.
- Calling a good inferior because it is cheap or poor quality, with no mention of income.
- Writing "profit falls" with no chain: which cost or demand figure changed first?
- Giving general lists of effects of an interest rate or exchange rate change without applying them to the business in the case.
- Mixing up strong and weak currency, especially which helps an exporter.
- Writing only one side of barriers to entry: say who gains and who loses.
- Making a judgement in an evaluation answer with no reason or evidence for why one effect outweighs another.
