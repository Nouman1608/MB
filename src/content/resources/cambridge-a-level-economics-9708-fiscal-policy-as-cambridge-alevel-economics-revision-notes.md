These notes condense section **5.2 Fiscal policy** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Economics 9708 syllabus (examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028), AS content in Topic 5. For explanations and worked examples, use the [fiscal policy study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-economics-9708-fiscal-policy-as-cambridge-alevel-economics/); for exam-style questions, use the [fiscal policy practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-economics-9708-fiscal-policy-as-cambridge-alevel-economics-practice/).

**Fiscal policy:** the use of government spending and taxation to influence the economy.

## Budget (5.2.1 and 5.2.2)

- **Government budget:** the plan of government revenue and spending for a period, usually a year.
- **Deficit:** spending > revenue -> borrowing. **Surplus:** revenue > spending -> debt repayment possible.
- Deficit as % of GDP = deficit / GDP x 100.
- The balance moves with the economy: in a downturn tax receipts fall and benefit spending rises, widening the deficit without any policy change.

## National debt (5.2.3)

- **Meaning:** the total outstanding stock of government borrowing.
- Deficit = flow (one year); debt = stock (accumulated).
- New debt = old debt + deficit (or - surplus used to repay).
- **Debt-to-GDP ratio** can fall even with a deficit if GDP grows faster than the debt.

**Significance:** interest costs (opportunity cost); future taxes or cuts; domestic versus foreign holders; whether borrowing financed capital or current spending; lenders' confidence and interest rates.

## Taxation (5.2.4)

| Classification | Definition |
|---|---|
| Direct | Tax on income or wealth, e.g. income tax, corporation tax |
| Indirect | Tax on spending, collected by sellers, e.g. sales tax, excise duty |
| Progressive | Average rate rises as income rises |
| Proportional | Average rate constant at all incomes |
| Regressive | Average rate falls as income rises |

```
art = total tax / total income x 100
mrt = change in tax / change in income x 100
mrt > art  -> art rising -> progressive
```

**Quick check (invented).** 0% on the first 10,000 cu, 20% above. Income 25,000 cu: tax = 3,000 cu, art = 12%, mrt = 20%.

Indirect taxes are often **regressive in effect**: a fixed duty is a larger share of a low income.

**Reasons for taxation:** revenue; redistribution; discouraging demerit goods; influencing AD.

## Government spending (5.2.5)

- **Current:** used up within the year (wages, medicines, benefits, debt interest).
- **Capital (investment):** long-lasting assets (roads, hospitals, schools).
- Classify by **what is bought**.

**Reasons:** provide public and merit goods; redistribute; raise productive capacity; influence AD.

## Expansionary versus contractionary (5.2.6)

| | Expansionary | Contractionary |
|---|---|---|
| Action | Spend more and/or tax less | Spend less and/or tax more |
| AD | Right | Left |
| Budget | Deficit wider | Deficit narrower |

## AD/AS analysis (5.2.7)

Effect of an AD shift right depends on where the economy is on LRAS:

| Section of LRAS | Real output | Price level | Employment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Highly elastic (spare capacity) | Rises | Little change | Rises |
| Upward sloping | Rises | Rises | Rises |
| Vertical (full capacity) | No change | Rises | No change |

Contractionary policy reverses the direction: mainly lower prices near capacity; mainly lower output and employment with much spare capacity.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fiscal policy | Use of government spending and taxation to influence the economy |
| Budget deficit | Government spending exceeds revenue in a period |
| Budget surplus | Government revenue exceeds spending in a period |
| National debt | Total outstanding government borrowing |
| Average rate of tax | Total tax as a percentage of total income |
| Marginal rate of tax | Tax on an extra unit of income, as a percentage |
| Current spending | Government spending on items used up within the year |
| Capital spending | Government spending on long-lasting assets |
| Expansionary fiscal policy | Higher spending or lower taxes to raise AD |
| Contractionary fiscal policy | Lower spending or higher taxes to reduce AD |

## Exam traps

- Mixing up deficit (flow) and debt (stock).
- Calling a tax progressive because the rich pay more in total.
- Quoting the mrt when the art is asked.
- Classifying public-sector wages as capital spending.
- Showing only output rising when the economy is at full capacity.
- Leaving out employment in a question that asks for it.

## Self-test

1. Define the government budget.
2. Revenue is 310 and spending 335. State the budget position.
3. Distinguish the budget deficit from the national debt.
4. Debt is 400 and GDP 1,000; the deficit is 20 and GDP grows to 1,100. Calculate the new debt ratio.
5. Give two reasons why the size of the national debt matters.
6. Define a progressive tax.
7. Income rises from 30,000 to 40,000 cu and tax rises from 4,500 to 7,000 cu. Calculate the mrt.
8. Calculate the art at an income of 40,000 cu in Question 7.
9. Why can a sales tax be regressive?
10. Classify: building a new bridge; paying nurses' salaries.
11. State two forms of expansionary fiscal policy.
12. What happens to real output and the price level after expansionary fiscal policy when the economy is on the vertical section of LRAS?

**Answers**

1. The government's plan of revenue and spending for a period, usually a year.
2. A deficit of 25.
3. The deficit is the excess of spending over revenue in one year (a flow); the national debt is the total accumulated borrowing outstanding (a stock).
4. (400 + 20) / 1,100 x 100 = 38.2%.
5. Any two: interest payments have an opportunity cost; future taxes or cuts may be needed; interest to foreign holders leaves the economy; lenders may demand higher interest rates.
6. A tax whose average rate rises as income rises.
7. (7,000 - 4,500) / (40,000 - 30,000) x 100 = 25%.
8. 7,000 / 40,000 x 100 = 17.5%.
9. Low-income households spend a larger share of their income on taxed goods, so the tax takes a larger proportion of their income.
10. Bridge: capital spending. Nurses' salaries: current spending.
11. Higher government spending; lower taxes.
12. Real output is unchanged; the price level rises.
