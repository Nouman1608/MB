These questions cover section 3.3 (Work, energy and power) of the OCR Advanced GCE in Physics A specification (H556), Version 3.0 (March 2026), for first assessment in 2017. Use g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹. Start with the [study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-physics-work-energy-power-and-efficiency/) or the [revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-physics-work-energy-power-and-efficiency-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Define the joule. **[2]**

**2.** A child pulls a toy cart 7.5 m along a level path using a rope. The tension is 22 N and the rope is at 40° to the path. Calculate the work done by the tension. **[2]**

**3.** A porter carries a box horizontally across a room at constant velocity.

**(a)** Explain why the upward force the porter exerts on the box does no work. **[2]**
**(b)** The box is then pushed along the floor and slows to rest. State the sign of the work done on it by friction and name the energy transfer. **[1]**

**4.** Derive Ep = mgh for a mass m raised through a height h in a uniform gravitational field. **[3]**

**5.** A dart of mass 0.020 kg travels at 14 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find its kinetic energy. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed at which its kinetic energy would be 5.0 J. **[2]**

**6.** A block of mass 0.80 kg is released from rest at a height of 1.2 m on a smooth curved track. At the bottom it slides onto a rough horizontal surface and stops after 3.0 m.

**(a)** Calculate its speed at the bottom of the track. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the average frictional force on the rough surface. **[3]**

**7.** A crane raises a load of mass 640 kg through 12 m at constant speed in 20 s. The electrical power supplied to its motor is 5.2 kW.

**(a)** Calculate the useful output power. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the efficiency of the crane. **[2]**

**8.**
**(a)** Derive P = Fv for a constant force F moving an object at constant speed v. **[2]**
**(b)** A cyclist rides at a steady 8.0 m s⁻¹ and the driving force is 45 N. Calculate the power developed at the wheels. **[2]**

**9.** A pump raises 900 kg of water every minute through a height of 8.0 m. Its electrical input power is 1.5 kW.

**(a)** Calculate the useful power output. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the efficiency. **[1]**
**(c)** State where most of the wasted energy goes. **[1]**

**10.** A car of mass 1250 kg travelling at 22 m s⁻¹ is braked to rest over 46 m by a constant braking force.

**(a)** Calculate the initial kinetic energy. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the braking force. **[2]**
**(c)** The car now travels at twice the speed and the same braking force is applied. Calculate the stopping distance. **[2]**

**11.** A cyclist and bicycle have a combined mass of 82 kg. Without pedalling, they descend a road 600 m long that falls 35 m vertically. Their speed increases from 3.0 m s⁻¹ to 11.0 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the loss of gravitational potential energy and the gain in kinetic energy. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy dissipated and the average resistive force. **[3]**
**(c)** On a level road the cyclist rides at a constant 11.0 m s⁻¹ against a resistive force of 48 N. Calculate the power needed at the wheels. **[1]**
**(d)** The cyclist's body is 24% efficient at supplying this power. Calculate the total power the body must produce. **[1]**

**12.** A crate of mass 9.0 kg is pulled 4.0 m up a slope inclined at 25° to the horizontal by a force of 85 N acting 20° above the slope surface. Friction is a constant 18 N and the crate starts from rest.

**(a)** Calculate the work done by the 85 N force. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the gain in gravitational potential energy. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the speed of the crate after 4.0 m. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** One joule is the work done (energy transferred) when a force of one newton [1] moves its point of application one metre in the direction of the force [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Force times distance" is an incomplete definition. Include "in the direction of the force", since that phrase carries the physics.

**2.** W = Fx cos θ = 22 × 7.5 × cos 40° [1] = **126 J** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the substitution and the angle used. Writing the equation and the numbers makes your method visible, even if a slip occurs later.

**3. (a)** The force acts upwards, at 90° to the horizontal displacement [1]. Work depends on the force component along the displacement, and cos 90° = 0, so no energy is transferred [1].
**(b)** Friction acts against the motion, so its work is **negative** [1]: kinetic energy is transferred to thermal energy. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Refer to the direction of the force relative to the displacement, not to "no movement". The box does move, so "nothing moves" is wrong.

**4.** Lift at constant speed, so the lifting force equals the weight, F = mg [1]. Work done = mg × h, the force multiplied by the distance moved along its own direction [1]. This energy is stored as gravitational potential energy, so **Ep = mgh** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A derivation needs the condition (constant speed, so no change in kinetic energy) and the link between work done and energy gained. Quoting the final formula alone shows no reasoning.

**5. (a)** Ek = ½mv² = ½ × 0.020 × 14² [1] = **1.96 J** [1].
**(b)** v = √(2Ek/m) = √(2 × 5.0 ÷ 0.020) [1] = **22 m s⁻¹** (22.4) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Rearrange before substituting. A common slip is to forget to square the speed in part (a).

**6. (a)** Loss of Ep = gain of Ek, so v = √(2gh) [1] = √(2 × 9.81 × 1.2) = **4.85 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** Energy dissipated on the rough surface = Ek at the bottom = mgh = 0.80 × 9.81 × 1.2 = 9.42 J [1]. Work done by friction = F × 3.0 [1], so F = 9.42 ÷ 3.0 = **3.1 N** (3.14 N) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The mass cancels in part (a), but you need it in part (b). Equating the work done by friction to the kinetic energy removed is the neat route.

**7. (a)** Useful power = mgh/t = 640 × 9.81 × 12 ÷ 20 [1] = **3.8 kW** (3767 W) [1].
**(b)** Efficiency = 3767 ÷ 5200 × 100% [1] = **72%** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Put both powers in the same unit before dividing. Efficiency has no unit; show a percentage sign.

**8. (a)** Work done W = Fx, so P = W/t = Fx/t [1]. At constant speed x/t = v, so **P = Fv** [1].
**(b)** P = Fv = 45 × 8.0 [1] = **360 W** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* State that x/t is the constant speed. This is the step that turns the derivation from an assertion into an argument.

**9. (a)** Useful power = mgh/t = 900 × 9.81 × 8.0 ÷ 60 [1] = **1.18 kW** (1177 W) [1].
**(b)** Efficiency = 1177 ÷ 1500 × 100% = **78%** [1].
**(c)** Thermal energy (in the motor and the pipework, with some sound) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert "per minute" to seconds. Name the final form of the wasted energy rather than saying it is "lost".

**10. (a)** Ek = ½ × 1250 × 22² [1] = **3.0 × 10⁵ J** (302 500 J) [1].
**(b)** Work done by the brakes = Ek, so F × 46 = 302 500 [1], giving F = **6.6 kN** (6576 N) [1].
**(c)** At twice the speed, Ek is four times as large [1]. With the same force, the distance is four times as large: 4 × 46 = **184 m** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (c), explain the effect of v² on Ek. Simply multiplying 46 by two is a common wrong answer.

**11. (a)** Loss of Ep = mgh = 82 × 9.81 × 35 = **2.82 × 10⁴ J** (28 155 J) [1]. Gain in Ek = ½ × 82 × (11.0² − 3.0²) [1] = **4.59 × 10³ J** [1].
**(b)** Energy dissipated = 28 155 − 4592 = **2.36 × 10⁴ J** [1]. This equals the work done against resistive forces, F × 600 [1], so F = 23 563 ÷ 600 = **39 N** (39.3 N) [1].
**(c)** P = Fv = 48 × 11.0 = **528 W** [1]
**(d)** Total power = 528 ÷ 0.24 = **2200 W** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the vertical height of 35 m in mgh and the road length of 600 m in the work done against resistance. Mixing the two distances is the usual error.

**12. (a)** W = Fx cos θ = 85 × 4.0 × cos 20° [1] = **319 J** [1].
**(b)** Height gained = 4.0 sin 25° = 1.69 m [1]. ΔEp = 9.0 × 9.81 × 1.69 = **149 J** [1].
**(c)** Work against friction = 18 × 4.0 = 72 J [1]. Gain in Ek = 319.5 − 149.3 − 72 = 98.2 J [1]. v = √(2 × 98.2 ÷ 9.0) = **4.7 m s⁻¹** (4.67 m s⁻¹) [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Set out the energy balance as work done by the pull = ΔEp + ΔEk + friction work. A clear balance lets a slip in one term be traced.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using Fx instead of Fx cos θ, or measuring θ from the wrong line.
- Using the length of the slope where the vertical height belongs in mgh.
- Forgetting to square the speed in ½mv², or squaring only part of it.
- Leaving out the energy dissipated by friction in an energy-balance question.
- Mixing units: kW with W, minutes with seconds, kW h with J.
- Writing a derivation without the starting condition (from rest, or constant speed) or the link between work and energy.
- Describing energy as "used up" instead of transferred to thermal energy.
- Giving an efficiency above 100%, or leaving it without a percentage sign.
- Rounding intermediate values and drifting from the correct answer. Carry full values and round at the end.
