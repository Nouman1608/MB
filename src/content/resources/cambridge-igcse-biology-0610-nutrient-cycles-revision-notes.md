These notes condense sub-topic **19.3 Nutrient cycles** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcome 1 (the carbon cycle) is Core. Outcomes 2 and 3 (the nitrogen cycle and the roles of microorganisms) are **Extended only**.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [nutrient cycles study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-nutrient-cycles/) first, then test yourself with the [nutrient cycles practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-nutrient-cycles-practice/). The [organisms and their environment revision notes](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-organisms-and-their-environment-revision-notes/) cover the rest of topic 19.

## Core recall: the carbon cycle in six processes

| Process | Carbon goes from → to |
|---|---|
| **Photosynthesis** | carbon dioxide → carbohydrates in producers |
| **Feeding** | carbon compounds in food → carbon compounds in consumers |
| **Respiration** | glucose in all organisms → carbon dioxide |
| **Decomposition** | dead organisms and waste → carbon dioxide (decomposers respire) |
| **Formation of fossil fuels** | incompletely decomposed remains → coal, oil, gas (millions of years) |
| **Combustion** | fossil fuels and wood → carbon dioxide |

- **Removes** carbon dioxide from the air: photosynthesis only.
- **Returns** carbon dioxide: respiration, decomposition, combustion.
- **Locks carbon away**: formation of fossil fuels.

**Fossil fuel conditions:** waterlogged, airless or acidic conditions slow decomposition, so dead material builds up and is compressed over millions of years.

**Human effect:** combustion of fossil fuels and burning of forests add carbon dioxide; removing forests reduces photosynthesis. Both raise carbon dioxide in the air.

## Extended recall: the nitrogen cycle in eight stages

1. **Nitrogen fixation:** nitrogen gas → nitrogen compounds, by **lightning** and **nitrogen-fixing bacteria** (free in soil and in root nodules of peas, beans, clover).
2. **Absorption:** plant roots absorb **nitrate ions**.
3. **Production of amino acids and proteins** in plants.
4. **Feeding and digestion:** animals digest plant protein to amino acids and make their own proteins.
5. **Deamination:** excess amino acids → urea (in the liver), excreted.
6. **Decomposition:** protein in dead material and waste → **ammonium ions**.
7. **Nitrification:** ammonium ions → **nitrate ions** (nitrifying bacteria).
8. **Denitrification:** nitrate ions → **nitrogen gas** (denitrifying bacteria).

**Conditions:** nitrifying bacteria need oxygen (well-aerated soil). Denitrification is greatest in waterlogged soil with little oxygen.

## Extended recall: roles of microorganisms

| Role | Conversion |
|---|---|
| Decomposition | protein → ammonium ions |
| Nitrification | ammonium ions → nitrate ions |
| Nitrogen fixation | nitrogen gas → nitrogen compounds |
| Denitrification | nitrate ions → nitrogen gas |

Names of individual bacteria are **not** required. Lightning fixes nitrogen but is not a microorganism.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Photosynthesis | Producers use carbon dioxide and water to make glucose, using light energy |
| Respiration | Release of energy from glucose, producing carbon dioxide |
| Decomposer | Organism that gets its energy from dead or waste organic material |
| Fossil fuel | Coal, oil or natural gas formed from remains that did not fully decompose |
| Combustion | Burning, which releases carbon dioxide |
| Nitrogen fixation (Extended) | Conversion of nitrogen gas into nitrogen compounds |
| Nitrification (Extended) | Conversion of ammonium ions into nitrate ions |
| Denitrification (Extended) | Conversion of nitrate ions into nitrogen gas |
| Deamination (Extended) | Removal of the nitrogen-containing part of amino acids to form urea |
| Root nodules (Extended) | Swellings on roots of plants such as beans, containing nitrogen-fixing bacteria |

## Exam traps

- **Carbon from soil.** Plants get carbon from carbon dioxide, not from soil.
- **Plants only photosynthesise.** Plants also respire, day and night.
- **Decomposition with no mechanism.** Say decomposers **respire**, releasing carbon dioxide.
- **Fossil fuels.** Include "do not fully decompose" and "millions of years".
- **(Extended) Three similar words.** Fixation (N₂ → compounds), nitrification (NH₄⁺ → NO₃⁻), denitrification (NO₃⁻ → N₂).
- **(Extended) Plants and nitrogen gas.** Plants absorb nitrate ions; they cannot use nitrogen gas directly.
- **(Extended) Lightning as a microorganism.** It is not.
- Leaving decomposers out of the carbon cycle.
- Writing "decomposition" with no mention of carbon dioxide being released.
- (Extended) Mixing up nitrification and nitrogen fixation.
- (Extended) Forgetting deamination and urea when describing how nitrogen leaves animals.
- (Extended) Naming bacteria incorrectly instead of describing their role.

## Command-word reminders

- **Describe** a cycle: process + from what + to what.
- **State** a role: type of bacteria + conversion.
- **Suggest** the effect of a condition: name the process affected and the direction of change.

## Self-test

1. Name the only process in the carbon cycle that removes carbon dioxide from the air.
2. Name three processes that return carbon dioxide to the air.
3. Which organisms respire in the carbon cycle?
4. How does carbon in a dead tree return to the air?
5. Describe how fossil fuels are formed.
6. How does burning fossil fuels affect the carbon cycle?
7. (Extended) In what form do plants absorb nitrogen?
8. (Extended) State two ways in which nitrogen gas is fixed.
9. (Extended) What do nitrifying bacteria do?
10. (Extended) What is produced when excess amino acids are deaminated?
11. (Extended) Why is denitrification greater in waterlogged soil?
12. (Extended) Why do farmers sometimes grow beans or clover before a cereal crop?

### Answers

1. Photosynthesis.
2. Respiration, decomposition, combustion.
3. All of them: producers, consumers and decomposers.
4. Decomposers feed on it and respire, releasing carbon dioxide.
5. Dead organisms do not fully decompose (for example in waterlogged or airless conditions) and are compressed over millions of years into coal, oil or gas.
6. Combustion releases carbon that was locked away for millions of years, adding carbon dioxide to the air quickly.
7. As nitrate ions.
8. Lightning and nitrogen-fixing bacteria.
9. Convert ammonium ions into nitrate ions.
10. Urea.
11. There is little oxygen, which favours denitrifying bacteria (and limits nitrifying bacteria).
12. Their root nodules contain nitrogen-fixing bacteria, which add nitrogen compounds to the soil.
