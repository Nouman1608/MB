These notes condense section **16.1 Passage of information from parents to offspring** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). It is A Level content, examined in Paper 4. For explanations and worked examples, read the [16.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-passage-of-information-from-parents-to-offspring-cambridge/); for exam-style questions, use the [16.1 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-passage-of-information-from-parents-to-offspring-cambridge-practice/).

## Core recall

### Definitions

- **Haploid (n)**: one set of chromosomes.
- **Diploid (2n)**: two sets of chromosomes, one set from each parent.
- **Homologous pair**: a maternal and a paternal chromosome with the same genes at the same loci, the same length and centromere position; alleles may differ.

### Why reduction division?

- Gametes must be haploid so that fertilisation restores the diploid number.
- Without halving, chromosome number would double every generation.
- Each gamete receives one chromosome of every homologous pair.

### The eight stages

| Stage | Key event |
|---|---|
| Prophase I | Homologues pair (bivalents); crossing over at chiasmata; nuclear envelope breaks down; spindle forms |
| Metaphase I | Bivalents on equator; random orientation |
| Anaphase I | Homologous chromosomes separate to opposite poles; centromeres intact |
| Telophase I | Nuclear envelopes may re-form; cytokinesis → two haploid cells |
| Prophase II | Chromosomes condense; new spindles form |
| Metaphase II | Single chromosomes on equator; random orientation of chromatids |
| Anaphase II | Centromeres divide; sister chromatids separate |
| Telophase II | Nuclear envelopes re-form; cytokinesis → four haploid cells |

### Plant versus animal cells

| Feature | Animal cell | Plant cell |
|---|---|---|
| Centrioles | Present; organise the spindle | Absent; spindle still forms |
| Cytokinesis | Cell surface membrane pinches in | New cell wall forms between daughter cells |

### Counting (2n = 6 example, per cell)

| Point | Chromosomes | Chromatids | DNA |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | 6 | 6 | x |
| After replication | 6 | 12 | 2x |
| After meiosis I | 3 | 6 | x |
| After meiosis II | 3 | 3 | 0.5x |

### Identifying stages

- Bivalents on the equator → metaphase I.
- Two-chromatid chromosomes moving apart → anaphase I.
- Two cells, single chromosomes on each equator → metaphase II.
- Single chromatids moving apart in two cells → anaphase II.
- Four nuclei → telophase II.
- Anaphase II sends n chromatids to each pole; mitotic anaphase sends 2n.

### Sources of variation

1. **Crossing over** in prophase I: non-sister chromatids exchange sections at chiasmata → new allele combinations on each chromatid.
2. **Random orientation** of homologous pairs at metaphase I (independent assortment) → random mix of maternal and paternal chromosomes.
3. **Random orientation** of sister chromatids at metaphase II (different after crossing over).
4. **Random fertilisation** → any gamete can fuse with any other.

Number of gamete combinations from independent assortment alone = 2ⁿ (n = number of homologous pairs).

### Meiosis compared with mitosis

| | Meiosis | Mitosis |
|---|---|---|
| Divisions | Two | One |
| Homologous chromosomes pair | Yes, in prophase I | No |
| Crossing over | Yes | No |
| Daughter cells | Four, haploid, genetically different | Two, diploid, genetically identical |
| Role | Producing gametes | Growth, repair, asexual reproduction |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bivalent | A pair of homologous chromosomes associated together in prophase I |
| Chiasma (plural chiasmata) | Point where non-sister chromatids have crossed over |
| Sister chromatids | Two identical copies of a chromosome joined at the centromere after replication |
| Non-sister chromatids | Chromatids belonging to different chromosomes of a homologous pair |
| Reduction division | A division that halves the chromosome number (meiosis I) |
| Independent assortment | Random orientation of homologous pairs at metaphase I |
| Gamete | Haploid sex cell |
| Zygote | Diploid cell formed when two gametes fuse |

## Exam traps

- Homologous chromosomes are **not** identical: alleles may differ.
- Crossing over is in **prophase I**, between **non-sister** chromatids.
- Centromeres divide in **anaphase II**, not anaphase I.
- Meiosis **I** is the reduction division.
- Plant cells have **no centrioles** and no cleavage furrow.
- Say "chromosomes" or "chromatids" precisely when counting.
- "Random orientation" applies to **pairs** at metaphase I and to **chromatids** at metaphase II.
- Sub-divisions of prophase I are not required; spending time on them earns nothing.

## Self-test

1. Define haploid.
2. Give two features shared by a pair of homologous chromosomes.
3. Explain why gametes must be haploid.
4. In which stage do bivalents form?
5. What happens to centromeres in anaphase I?
6. Name the stage in which sister chromatids separate.
7. Describe cytokinesis in a plant cell.
8. An organism has 2n = 10. How many chromosomes are in each cell at the end of telophase II?
9. The DNA content of a cell in G1 is 6 units. What is it in each cell after meiosis II?
10. Between which chromatids does crossing over occur?
11. A species has 2n = 12. How many gamete types are possible from independent assortment alone?
12. State how random fertilisation adds to variation.

### Answers

1. Having one set of chromosomes (n).
2. Any two: same genes at the same loci; same length; same centromere position.
3. So that fertilisation restores the diploid number and the chromosome number stays constant between generations.
4. Prophase I.
5. They do not divide; each chromosome moves with both its chromatids.
6. Anaphase II.
7. A new cell wall forms between the two daughter cells (no cleavage furrow).
8. 5.
9. 3 units.
10. Non-sister chromatids of a homologous pair.
11. 2⁶ = **64**.
12. Any male gamete can fuse with any female gamete, so the combination of alleles in the zygote is random.
