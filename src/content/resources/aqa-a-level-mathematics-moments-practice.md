These questions cover **Section S: Moments (S1)** of the AQA A-level Mathematics (7357) specification, version 1.3 (31 January 2018), for A-level exams from June 2018 onwards. The specification places Section S in **Paper 2**. A calculator is required in every 7357 paper, so every question here assumes you have one. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² and give answers to 3 significant figures where they are not exact, unless the question says otherwise.

## Questions

**1.** A force of 40 N acts at a perpendicular distance of 35 cm from a point O. Find the moment of the force about O. **[2]**

**2.** A light rod AB has length 2 m and is pivoted at A. A force of 25 N acts at B, at 60° to the rod. Find the moment of this force about A. **[3]**

**3.** A light rod PQ of length 3 m is horizontal, with P on the left, and is pivoted at its midpoint M. A downward force of 12 N acts at P, a downward force of 20 N acts at R where PR = 2.4 m, and an upward force of 6 N acts at Q. Find the resultant moment about M and state which way the rod starts to turn. **[3]**

**4.** A uniform rod AB has length 2 m and mass 3 kg. It rests horizontally on a single pivot at C, where AC = 0.8 m. A particle of mass m kg is attached at A and the rod is in equilibrium.

**(a)** Find m. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the reaction at the pivot. **[1]**

**5.** A uniform plank AB has length 4 m and mass 25 kg. It rests horizontally on a support at A and a support at C, where AC = 3.5 m. A box of mass 15 kg is placed on the plank 1 m from A. Find the reactions at A and at C. **[5]**

**6.** A non-uniform rod AB of length 3 m and mass 6 kg rests horizontally on supports at A and at B. The reaction at B is twice the reaction at A.

**(a)** Find the reactions at A and at B. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the distance of the centre of mass of the rod from A. **[3]**

**7.** A uniform rod AB of length 2 m and weight W N rests horizontally on supports at C and D, where AC = 0.4 m and AD = 1.5 m. When a particle of weight 30 N is attached at B, the rod is on the point of tilting.

**(a)** Find W. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the reaction at D. **[1]**

**8.** A uniform beam AB of length 8 m and mass 50 kg rests horizontally on supports at C and D, where AC = 2 m and AD = 6 m. A man of mass 80 kg walks along the beam from D towards B.

**(a)** Show that the beam tilts before the man reaches B. **[4]**
**(b)** A load of mass M kg is fixed at A so that the man can stand at B without the beam tilting. Find the least value of M. **[3]**
**(c)** State one modelling assumption you have made about the man. **[1]**

**9.** A uniform rod AB, of length 1.6 m and mass 5 kg, is hinged at A to a vertical wall. It is held horizontal by a light string attached at B and to a point on the wall vertically above A. The string makes 30° with the rod. A particle of mass 2 kg hangs from the rod at D, where AD = 1.2 m.

**(a)** Show that the tension in the string is 8g N. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the magnitude and direction of the force exerted by the hinge on the rod. **[5]**

**10.** A uniform ladder AB, of length 6 m and mass 20 kg, rests with A on rough horizontal ground and B against a smooth vertical wall. The ladder makes an angle θ with the ground, where tan θ = 12/5. A man of mass 75 kg stands on the ladder at C, where AC = 4.5 m. The ladder is about to slip.

**(a)** Find the normal reaction at A. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the reaction at B. **[4]**
**(c)** Find the coefficient of friction between the ladder and the ground. **[2]**
**(d)** The wall is in fact slightly rough. State, with a reason, how this would affect the friction needed at the ground. **[2]**

**11.** A diving board is modelled as a uniform rigid rod clamped at one end, and a diver is modelled as a particle.

**(a)** Explain what the word "uniform" allows you to assume in this model. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain what "rigid" allows you to assume. **[1]**
**(c)** Give one way in which the model of the diver as a particle may not match the real situation, and its effect on a calculated moment. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 35 cm = 0.35 m, moment = 40 × 0.35 [1]
= **14 N m** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* a bare 1400 (using centimetres) loses both marks here; the method mark depends on a distance in metres.

**2.** Perpendicular distance = 2 sin 60° (or component 25 sin 60° perpendicular to rod) [1]
moment = 25 × 2 sin 60° [1]
= **43.3 N m** (3 s.f.) [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* 25 × 2 = 50 N m earns nothing, because it ignores the angle; the first mark is for the perpendicular distance or the perpendicular component.

**3.** Anticlockwise: 12 × 1.5 + 6 × 1.5 = 27 N m; clockwise: 20 × 0.9 = 18 N m [1]
resultant = 27 − 18 [1]
= **9 N m anticlockwise**, so the P end goes down [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* the upward force at Q turns the rod the same way as the downward force at P; treating every force as clockwise on the right loses the method mark.

**4. (a)** Rod's weight acts 1 m from A, so 0.2 m from C [1]
moments about C: mg × 0.8 = 3g × 0.2 [1]
m = **0.75** [1]
**(b)** R = (3 + 0.75)g = **36.75 N** (36.8 N to 3 s.f.) [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* in (a) g cancels, so leaving it in or substituting 9.8 both work; in this scheme (b) is marked follow-through from your m.

**5.** Plank's weight 25g at 2 m from A [1]
moments about A: R_C × 3.5 = 25g × 2 + 15g × 1 [1]
R_C = 65g ÷ 3.5 = **182 N** [1]
resolve vertically: R_A + R_C = 40g [1]
R_A = 392 − 182 = **210 N** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* each moment term must pair a force with its own distance; one wrong distance usually costs the method mark for that equation and both accuracy marks.

**6. (a)** R_A + 2R_A = 6g [1]
**R_A = 19.6 N, R_B = 39.2 N** [1]
**(b)** moments about A: 6g × x = 39.2 × 3 [1]
58.8x = 117.6 [1]
x = **2 m** from A [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* in (b) the weight goes at an unknown x; writing 6g × 1.5 assumes the rod is uniform and scores nothing.

**7. (a)** about to tilt about D, so the reaction at C is 0 [1]
moments about D: W × (1.5 − 1) = 30 × 0.5 [1]
W = **30** [1]
**(b)** R_D = W + 30 = **60 N** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* in this scheme the first mark is for stating, not just using, that the reaction at C is zero; say it in words before the equation.

**8. (a)** about to tilt about D, so R_C = 0 [1]
man at y m beyond D; moments about D: 50g × 2 = 80g × y [1]
y = 1.25 m [1]
so tilting starts when he is 7.25 m from A, which is less than 8 m, so the beam tilts before he reaches B [1]
**(b)** with the man at B and R_C = 0, moments about D: 50g × 2 + Mg × 6 = 80g × 2 [1]
100 + 6M = 160 [1]
least M = **10** [1]
**(c)** The man is a **particle**, so his weight acts at a single point [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* in a "show that" the final mark needs the comparison written out (7.25 < 8 and a conclusion), not just y = 1.25.

**9. (a)** moments about A: T sin 30° × 1.6 = 5g × 0.8 + 2g × 1.2 [1]
0.8T = 6.4g [1]
T = **8g N** (78.4 N), as required [1]
**(b)** horizontal: H = T cos 30° = 8g cos 30° [1]
= 67.9 N, away from the wall [1]
vertical: V + T sin 30° = 7g, V = 68.6 − 39.2 = 29.4 N upwards [1]
magnitude = √(67.9...² + 29.4²) = **74.0 N** [1]
direction: tan⁻¹(29.4 ÷ 67.9...) = **23.4° above the horizontal** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* a "magnitude and direction" answer needs both; a correct 74.0 N with no angle leaves the last mark unearned.

**10. (a)** R = (20 + 75)g = **931 N** [1]
**(b)** sin θ = 12/13, cos θ = 5/13 [1]
moments about A: S × 6 sin θ = 20g × 3 cos θ + 75g × 4.5 cos θ [1]
S × 72/13 = 397.5g × 5/13 [1]
S = **271 N** (270.5...) [1]
**(c)** F = S and F = μR [1]
μ = 270.5... ÷ 931 = **0.291** (3 s.f.) [1]
**(d)** A rough wall gives an upward friction force at B [1]
which has a moment about A, so S, and hence the friction needed at A (F = S), would be smaller; the model **overestimates** the friction needed [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* using the 3 s.f. value 271 in (c) gives 0.291 as well, but rounding S to 270 gives 0.290; carry the unrounded value.

**11. (a)** The weight of the board acts at its **midpoint** [1]
**(b)** The board does not **bend**, so distances along it stay fixed [1]
**(c)** The diver's weight is spread over the feet, not at one point [1]
so the true moment about the clamp may be slightly different from the calculated one [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* in this scheme each mark needs a link to the diving board or diver; a textbook definition with no reference to the context is not credited.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the distance along a rod, not the perpendicular distance, for a force at an angle.
- Forgetting the weight of a rod that is not described as light.
- Placing a non-uniform rod's weight at the midpoint.
- Not stating that the far reaction is zero when a beam is about to tilt.
- Swapping sin θ and cos θ in ladder moments.
- Giving a hinge force without its direction.
- Rounding a tension or reaction before using it again.
- Leaving out units (N, N m) on final answers.
