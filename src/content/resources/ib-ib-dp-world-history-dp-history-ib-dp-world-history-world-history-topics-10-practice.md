## Section A

**1.** Identify **three** methods Stalin used to defeat his rivals between 1924 and 1929. **[3]**

**2.** State **two** measures that turned Germany into a single-party state in 1933. **[2]**

**3.** Outline **two** aims of the Great Leap Forward. **[2]**

**4.** Identify **three** factors that helped Castro come to power in 1959. **[3]**

**5.** Define "cult of personality" and give **one** example. **[2]**

**6.** Outline **two** ways in which Nazi policy aimed to change the role of women. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** **(a)** Explain **two** conditions that helped the emergence of the Nazi state. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why the Night of the Long Knives strengthened Hitler's position. **[3]**

**8.** **(a)** Explain the aims of Stalin's collectivization of agriculture. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain **two** consequences of collectivization. **[4]**

**9.** **(a)** Explain how Mao used mass campaigns to consolidate power between 1949 and 1957. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why Mao launched the Cultural Revolution in 1966. **[4]**

**10.** Evaluate the success of Castro's social policies in Cuba up to 1975. **[6]**

**11.** Compare and contrast the treatment of opposition in **two** authoritarian states, each chosen from a different region. **[6]**

**12.** "Authoritarian leaders maintained power more through popularity than through fear." With reference to **two** authoritarian states, each chosen from a different region, to what extent do you agree with this statement? **[15]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: using his power as General Secretary to appoint supporters to party posts; allying with Zinoviev and Kamenev against Trotsky; then allying with Bukharin against the Left; then turning on Bukharin and the Right over the grain crisis; presenting himself as Lenin's loyal heir, including at Lenin's funeral; exploiting the ban on factions (1921) to label rivals as divisive; suppressing Lenin's Testament criticizing him. **[3]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: the Enabling Act (March 1933); banning the Communist Party and then the SPD; the abolition of trade unions (May 1933); the law of July 1933 making the Nazi Party the only legal party. **[2]**

**3.** Any two, 1 mark each: to overtake Britain in industrial output within fifteen years; to raise steel production rapidly, including through backyard furnaces; to raise grain output by organizing peasants into large people's communes; to show that China could reach communism by its own path, faster than the Soviet model. **[2]**

**4.** Any three, 1 mark each: the unpopularity and corruption of Batista's regime; Castro's leadership and the propaganda value of the Moncada attack and his trial speech; the guerrilla campaign from the Sierra Maestra; support from peasants and the urban underground; the US arms embargo on Batista (1958); the collapse of the army's will to fight. **[3]**

**5.** 1 mark for the definition: the use of propaganda to present a leader as heroic, wise or infallible, and to make loyalty to him central to the state. 1 mark for an example: Stalin portrayed as Lenin's heir and the "father of nations"; the Hitler myth; Mao's Little Red Book and portraits; Castro as the hero of the Sierra Maestra. **[2]**

**6.** Any two, 1 mark each: encouraging women to leave paid work and focus on "children, kitchen, church"; marriage loans (from 1933) cancelled in part for each child; the Mother's Cross for women with many children; restrictions on women in some professions and in higher education; banning abortion and limiting contraception for "Aryan" women. **[2]**

**7.** **(a)** 2 marks for each developed condition: the economic crisis of the Depression, with around six million unemployed by 1932, drove voters towards extremes; the weakness of the Weimar system, with coalitions breaking down and government by presidential decree from 1930; resentment of defeat and the Treaty of Versailles; social division and fear of communism among the middle classes and elites. **[4]**
**(b)** 1 mark each: it removed Röhm and the SA leadership, who wanted a "second revolution" and threatened the army; it won the support of the army, which swore an oath of personal loyalty to Hitler after Hindenburg's death in August 1934; it eliminated other opponents such as Schleicher and warned others that Hitler would kill to keep power. **[3]**

**8.** **(a)** Up to 4 marks: to secure a reliable grain supply for the cities and the industrial workforce (1); to export grain to pay for machinery for the Five-Year Plans (1); to free peasant labour for industry through mechanized large farms (1); to destroy the *kulaks* as a class and bring the peasantry under party control (1). **[4]**
**(b)** 2 marks for each developed consequence: famine in 1932-33, especially in Ukraine and Kazakhstan, with millions of deaths; peasants slaughtered livestock rather than hand it over, so animal numbers fell sharply; deportation of millions of peasants labelled *kulaks*; the state gained control of grain procurement, which funded industrialization despite falling output. **[4]**

**9.** **(a)** 2 marks for each developed point: land reform (from 1950) mobilized peasants against landlords through "speak bitterness" meetings and mass trials, binding peasants to the party; the Three-Anti and Five-Anti campaigns (1951-52) targeted corrupt officials and businesses, extending control over cities; the Hundred Flowers campaign drew critics into the open, and the Anti-Rightist campaign (1957) punished them. **[4]**
**(b)** 2 marks for each developed reason: to regain the authority he lost after the failure of the Great Leap Forward, when Liu Shaoqi and Deng Xiaoping ran the economy; to purge party leaders he saw as "capitalist roaders"; to renew revolutionary spirit among the young, using the Red Guards; to prevent China following what he saw as Soviet "revisionism". **[4]**

**10.** Up to 6 marks: 2 for policies and aims, 2 for evaluating results, 2 for a supported judgement. Policies: the literacy campaign (1961) sent young volunteers to teach in the countryside; free education and healthcare expanded; racial discrimination was outlawed; the Federation of Cuban Women (1960) and the Family Code (1975) promoted equality. Results: literacy rose dramatically and health indicators improved; but dependence on Soviet aid sustained the programmes, free expression was restricted, and many educated Cubans emigrated. Judgement: highly successful by the regime's social aims, at the cost of political freedom. **[6]**

**11.** Up to 6 marks; both similarities and differences are needed. *Stalin and Mao, for example.* Similarities: both used mass arrests, labour camps (Gulag; *laogai*) and executions; both targeted party members as well as outsiders; both used show trials or public struggle sessions. Differences: Stalin's terror was carried out mainly by the secret police (NKVD), while Mao mobilized the masses themselves against "class enemies", as in land reform and the Cultural Revolution; Mao briefly invited criticism (Hundred Flowers) before punishing it. The top mark needs a judgement on the significance of the differences. **[6]**

**12.** Marked holistically using the 15-mark markbands; the indicative content is a guide, not a checklist. *Hitler (Europe) and Castro (the Americas), for example.* Popularity: Hitler's economic recovery, foreign policy successes and the Hitler myth; Castro's charisma, land reform, education and health gains, and nationalism strengthened by US hostility. Fear: the Gestapo, SS and concentration camps, the Night of the Long Knives; Castro's imprisonment of opponents, CDR surveillance and the emigration of dissenters. Complexity: popularity and fear worked together, as propaganda shaped "popularity" and fear discouraged dissent; consent varied by group (for example workers, churches, Jews in Germany; middle classes and Catholics in Cuba). A strong answer judges, for example, that both regimes relied more on consent than is often assumed, but that fear defined the limits of that consent. **[15]**

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use Hitler in both topic 9 and topic 10?**
Weimar's collapse belongs to topic 9; the Nazi state after 1933 belongs to topic 10. You can use knowledge from both, but answer the question asked.

**Which pairs meet the "different regions" requirement?**
Any pair across Europe, Asia and Oceania, the Americas, and Africa and the Middle East, such as Stalin and Mao, or Hitler and Castro.
