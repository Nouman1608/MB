These questions cover section 3.4.1 Kinetic theory, statements a to e, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) specification, for exams May/June 2018 onwards, including the stearic acid cooling curve practical. Use c(water) = 4200 J/kg °C, L_F(water) = 3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg and L_V(water) = 2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg unless a question gives other values. For help, see the [kinetic theory study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-kinetic-theory-oxfordaqa-igcse/) and the [kinetic theory revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-kinetic-theory-oxfordaqa-igcse-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Describe the arrangement and movement of the particles in a solid. **[2]**

**2.** In which state do the particles have the most energy?

A: solid

B: liquid

C: gas

D: all states have the same energy

**[1]**

**3.** Define the specific latent heat of vaporisation of a substance. **[2]**

**4.** An aluminium pan of mass 0.60 kg is heated from 20 °C to 180 °C. Calculate the energy transferred to the pan. (c = 900 J/kg °C) **[2]**

**5.** Calculate the mass of ice at 0 °C that 50 kJ of energy can melt. **[3]**

**6.** State the effect of dissolving salt in water on the boiling point of the water. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A 3.0 kW electric kettle heats 1.5 kg of water from 15 °C to 65 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the energy transferred to the water. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the time this takes, assuming no energy is lost. **[2]**

**(c)** State and explain whether the real time will be longer or shorter. **[2]**

**8.** A student heats a 1.0 kg metal block with a 30 W heater for 600 s. The temperature of the block rises by 20 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the energy supplied by the heater. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the specific heat capacity of the metal from these results. **[2]**

**(c)** The accepted value is lower than the student's value. Explain why, and suggest one improvement. **[2]**

**9.** A pan contains 0.30 kg of water at 20 °C. It is heated until the water boils, and 0.050 kg of the water boils away at 100 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the energy needed to heat the water to 100 °C. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy needed to boil away 0.050 kg. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the total energy. **[1]**

**10.** A sample of ice at −20 °C is heated at a constant rate until it has become steam at 110 °C.

**(a)** Describe the shape of the temperature–time graph. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the temperature is constant at 100 °C even though energy is still being supplied. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the section for heating ice is steeper than the section for heating liquid water. **[2]**

**11.** Liquid stearic acid is allowed to cool in a boiling tube, and its temperature is recorded every 30 s.

| Time (s) | 0 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 120 | 150 | 180 | 210 | 240 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature (°C) | 82 | 76 | 71 | 69 | 69 | 69 | 69 | 64 | 60 |

**(a)** State the melting point of this stearic acid. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain, in terms of particles and energy, why the temperature stays constant between 90 s and 180 s. **[3]**

**(c)** A second sample freezes over a range from 66 °C to 63 °C. Suggest a reason. **[1]**

**12.** A cook uses ice cubes to cool a drink.

**(a)** 0.040 kg of ice at 0 °C melts in the drink. Calculate the energy taken from the drink by the melting ice. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why ice at 0 °C cools a drink more than the same mass of water at 0 °C. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Particles are close together in a regular pattern [1] and vibrate about fixed positions [1]. **[2]**

**2.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The energy needed to change 1 kg of a substance from liquid to vapour [1] with no change in temperature [1]. **[2]**

**4.** E = m c Δθ = 0.60 × 900 × 160 [1] = **86 400 J** (8.6 × 10⁴ J) [1]. **[2]**

**5.** 50 kJ = 50 000 J [1]; m = E ÷ L_F = 50 000 ÷ 3.34 × 10⁵ [1] = **0.15 kg** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The unit conversion mark is often lost. Change kJ to J before dividing by a value in J/kg.

**6.** It raises the boiling point [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** E = 1.5 × 4200 × 50 [1] = **315 000 J** [1].

**(b)** t = E ÷ P = 315 000 ÷ 3000 [1] = **105 s** [1].

**(c)** Longer [1], because some energy is transferred to the kettle and the surroundings rather than to the water [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** E = P t = 30 × 600 [1] = **18 000 J** [1].

**(b)** c = E ÷ (m Δθ) = 18 000 ÷ (1.0 × 20) [1] = **900 J/kg °C** [1].

**(c)** Some of the energy is transferred to the surroundings, so less than 18 000 J actually heats the block, making the calculated value too high [1]. Improvement: wrap the block in insulation (lagging) [1]. **[6]**

**9. (a)** E = 0.30 × 4200 × 80 [1] = **100 800 J** [1].

**(b)** E = 0.050 × 2.26 × 10⁶ [1] = **113 000 J** [1].

**(c)** Total = 100 800 + 113 000 = **213 800 J** (about 2.1 × 10⁵ J) [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** Rises (ice warming) to 0 °C, then flat while melting [1]; rises (water warming) to 100 °C [1]; flat while boiling, a longer flat section, then rises again (steam warming) [1].

**(b)** The energy is used to overcome the forces between the particles and separate them [1], not to increase their kinetic energy, so the temperature does not rise [1].

**(c)** Ice has a lower specific heat capacity than water [1], so each kilogram needs less energy for each degree, and the temperature rises faster at the same power [1]. **[7]**

**11. (a)** **69 °C** [1].

**(b)** The liquid is solidifying (freezing) [1]. As particles are pulled into fixed positions by the forces between them, energy is released [1]. This balances the energy transferred to the surroundings, so the temperature stays constant until all the acid is solid [1].

**(c)** The second sample contains impurities [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* "The heat is lost" does not explain a plateau. Say that energy is released as the solid forms.

**12. (a)** E = 0.040 × 3.34 × 10⁵ [1] = **13 360 J** (about 1.3 × 10⁴ J) [1].

**(b)** The ice must first absorb energy to melt (its latent heat of fusion) [1] before it can warm up as water, so it takes more energy from the drink [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to convert kJ to J or g to kg.
- Missing out a stage in a multi-stage energy calculation.
- Describing a plateau as energy being "lost" instead of used to change state.
- Saying that impurities raise melting points.
- Giving the starting temperature of a cooling curve as the melting point.
- Stating "longer" or "shorter" without a reason linked to energy losses.
