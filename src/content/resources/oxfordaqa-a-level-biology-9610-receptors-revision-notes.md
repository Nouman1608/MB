These notes condense section 3.4.2 (Receptors) of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Biology (9610) specification, Version 5.1, in Unit 4. For explanations and worked data, use the [receptors study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-biology-9610-receptors/); then try the [practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-biology-9610-receptors-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Receptor | A cell or nerve ending that detects a stimulus and produces an electrical signal |
| Transducer | A structure that converts one form of energy into another |
| Generator potential | The change in membrane potential in a receptor when it is stimulated |
| Threshold | The potential that must be reached to produce an action potential |
| Stretch-mediated sodium ion channel | A channel that opens when the membrane is stretched |
| Rod | Photoreceptor with one pigment, highly sensitive to light |
| Cone | Photoreceptor with one of three pigments, giving colour and acuity |
| Bipolar neurone | The neurone between photoreceptors and the optic nerve |
| Visual acuity | The ability to tell two close points apart |
| Spatial summation | Adding the effects of several receptors acting on one neurone |

## The two ideas for every receptor

1. It responds only to a **specific** stimulus.
2. Stimulation produces a **generator potential**.

## The Pacinian corpuscle: the sequence

1. Pressure deforms the lamellae and stretches the neurone membrane.
2. Stretch-mediated sodium ion channels widen and open.
3. Sodium ions diffuse in.
4. The inside becomes less negative: a depolarisation called the generator potential.
5. If it reaches the threshold, an action potential is triggered in the sensory neurone.
6. A stronger stimulus gives a larger generator potential and more frequent action potentials, not larger action potentials.

**Structure to remember:** the end of a sensory neurone, wrapped in layers of connective tissue (lamellae) separated by gel.

### Quick data rule

Required depolarisation = threshold minus resting potential. Example: from -70 mV to -55 mV needs +15 mV. A generator potential of +9 mV is subthreshold: no action potential. +21 mV is above threshold: action potentials, more frequent than at +15 mV.

## The retina: rods against cones

| | Rods | Cones |
|---|---|---|
| Pigment | One type | Three types (different wavelengths) |
| Sensitivity | High (dim light) | Low (bright light) |
| Colour | No | Yes |
| Acuity | Low | High |
| Connection | Many rods to one bipolar neurone | One cone to one bipolar neurone |
| Position | Edge of retina | Mostly at the fovea |

### Why the differences arise

- **Sensitivity.** The rod pigment is broken down by low-intensity light, and many rods share one neurone, so small generator potentials add together (spatial summation) and reach the threshold.
- **Acuity.** Each cone has its own neurone, so two nearby points give two signals. Many rods give one signal for a group, so close points merge.
- **Colour.** Three cone types respond to different wavelengths. The brain compares the signals.

### Summation example

Each rod gives 2 mV in dim light and a bipolar neurone needs 10 mV. 10 / 2 = 5 rods must be stimulated together. A single cone on its own neurone gives only 2 mV and does not trigger an impulse in dim light.

## Model sentences to learn

- "Pressure deforms the lamellae and stretches the membrane, so the stretch-mediated sodium ion channels open and sodium ions enter, producing a generator potential."
- "If the generator potential reaches the threshold, an action potential is triggered in the sensory neurone."
- "A larger stimulus opens more sodium channels, so the generator potential is larger and the frequency of action potentials is higher."
- "Many rods share one bipolar neurone, so their small generator potentials add together and can reach the threshold in dim light."
- "Each cone has its own bipolar neurone, so two close points produce two separate impulses, which gives high visual acuity."

## Another data example

Resting potential -70 mV; threshold -55 mV. A generator potential of +10 mV gives a membrane potential of -60 mV, which is 5 mV short of the threshold, so no action potential. A generator potential of +18 mV gives -52 mV, which is above the threshold, so an action potential is triggered.

## Command words

| Word | What the answer needs |
|---|---|
| Describe | The sequence from stimulus to action potential, in order |
| Explain | The reason, naming the pigment, the channel or the connection |
| Compare | Both rods and cones in each point |
| Calculate | The working, including the threshold minus the resting potential |

## Exam traps

- A receptor produces a generator potential, not an action potential directly.
- A stronger stimulus does not make a bigger action potential. It makes more of them.
- Sensitivity belongs to rods and acuity to cones. Do not swap them.
- Explain a difference with a pigment or a connection. "Rods are better in the dark" is not an explanation.
- Say that the stretch-mediated channels open because the membrane is deformed.
- Do not describe the fovea as containing rods. It is mainly cones.

## Quick self-test

1. What does a transducer do?
2. Name the type of channel in the membrane of a Pacinian corpuscle that opens when pressure is applied.
3. Which ion enters the neurone to produce the generator potential?
4. What happens if the generator potential does not reach the threshold?
5. How does the nervous system detect that a stimulus is stronger?
6. Resting potential is -70 mV and the threshold is -50 mV. A generator potential of +14 mV is recorded. Will an action potential be produced?
7. Why are rods more sensitive to light than cones?
8. Why do cones give greater visual acuity?
9. How do we see colour?
10. Each rod gives a 2.5 mV generator potential. A bipolar neurone needs 10 mV. How many rods must be stimulated?
11. Where in the retina are cones concentrated?
12. Why can you not see colour well in dim light?

### Answers

1. It converts energy from a stimulus into electrical energy.
2. Stretch-mediated sodium ion channels.
3. Sodium ions.
4. No action potential is produced.
5. Through more frequent action potentials.
6. The depolarisation needed is +20 mV. +14 mV is less, so no action potential.
7. Their pigment is broken down by low-intensity light and many rods share one bipolar neurone, so their generator potentials add up.
8. Each cone has its own connection to a bipolar neurone, so close points produce separate signals.
9. Three types of cone respond to different wavelengths, and the brain interprets the combination of signals.
10. 10 / 2.5 = 4 rods.
11. At the fovea.
12. Only rods work in dim light, and they have a single pigment.
