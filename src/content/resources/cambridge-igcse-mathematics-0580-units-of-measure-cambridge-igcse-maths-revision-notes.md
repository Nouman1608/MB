These notes condense subtopic **5.1 Units of measure** from Topic 5 Mensuration of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The Core and Extended statements are identical. For explanations and longer examples, read the [units of measure study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-units-of-measure-cambridge-igcse-maths/); to practise, use the [units of measure practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-units-of-measure-cambridge-igcse-maths-practice/).

## The statement

Use metric units of mass, length, area, volume and capacity in practical situations and convert quantities into larger or smaller units. The syllabus notes name the units: mm, cm, m, km; mm², cm², m², km²; mm³, cm³, m³; ml, l; g, kg. Conversions include between units of **area** and between units of **volume and capacity**.

## Conversion facts

| Type | Facts to know |
|---|---|
| Length | 1 cm = 10 mm, 1 m = 100 cm, 1 km = 1000 m, 1 m = 1000 mm |
| Area | 1 cm² = 100 mm², 1 m² = 10 000 cm², 1 km² = 1 000 000 m² |
| Volume | 1 cm³ = 1000 mm³, 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³ |
| Capacity | 1 l = 1000 ml |
| Volume ↔ capacity | 1 cm³ = 1 ml, 1000 cm³ = 1 l, 1 m³ = 1000 l |
| Mass | 1 kg = 1000 g |

**Rule:** area factor = (length factor)², volume factor = (length factor)³.

## Direction

```
to a SMALLER unit  ->  more of them   ->  MULTIPLY
to a LARGER unit   ->  fewer of them  ->  DIVIDE
check: m² -> cm² must make the number bigger
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Capacity | How much a container holds, measured in ml or l |
| Volume | The space a solid occupies, measured in mm³, cm³ or m³ |
| Area | The space covered by a flat surface, measured in mm², cm², m² or km² |
| Mass | How heavy an object is, measured in g or kg |
| kilo- | × 1000 (kilometre, kilogram) |
| centi- | ÷ 100 (centimetre) |
| milli- | ÷ 1000 (millimetre, millilitre) |

## Combined factors worth knowing

| From | To | Operation |
|---|---|---|
| mm² | m² | ÷ 1 000 000 |
| km² | cm² | × 10 000 000 000 (10¹⁰) |
| mm³ | cm³ | ÷ 1000 |
| mm³ | m³ | ÷ 10⁹ |
| cm³ | l | ÷ 1000 |
| m³ | ml | × 1 000 000 |

When a factor is huge, write the answer in standard form.

## Method for practical problems

```
1. List every quantity with its unit.
2. Convert to ONE working unit.
3. Calculate.
4. Convert to the unit asked for; write the unit.
5. Round to suit the context (needed -> up; can be made -> down).
```

**Worked reminder.** A cuboid container is 1.5 m by 40 cm by 30 cm. How many litres does it hold?

```
work in cm: 150 × 40 × 30 = 180 000 cm³
÷ 1000: 180 litres
```

**Worked reminder.** A path is 2.4 km long and 1.5 m wide. Find its area in m².

```
2.4 km = 2400 m;  area = 2400 × 1.5 = 3600 m²
```

## Comparing quantities

Convert every value to **one** unit, compare, then answer using the units the question gave.

**Worked reminder.** Which holds more: a bottle of 1.75 l or a carton of 1800 cm³?

```
1800 cm³ = 1800 ml = 1.8 l,  and 1.8 l > 1.75 l, so the carton holds more
```

## Sensible sizes

Use rough sizes to check that an answer is believable.

| Unit | Roughly the size of |
|---|---|
| 1 mm | the thickness of a bank card |
| 1 m | a long stride |
| 1 ml | a few drops of water |
| 1 l | a large bottle of water |
| 1 kg | a bag of sugar |

A door with an area of 2 cm², or a mug holding 300 litres, signals a conversion error.

## Exam traps

- Dividing cm² by 100 to get m² (the length factor); it must be 10 000.
- Writing 1 m³ = 100 litres or 1 litre = 100 cm³.
- Multiplying the dimensions of a solid when they are in mixed units.
- Converting the right way numerically but leaving the old unit on the answer.
- Ordering quantities and then listing them in converted units instead of the units given.
- Rounding "how many tins are needed" down.
- Copying a long string of zeros wrongly; use standard form for very large or small values.

## Self-test

1. Convert 4.7 m to mm.
2. Convert 0.62 m² to cm².
3. Convert 8500 cm² to m².
4. Convert 3 km² to m².
5. Convert 2.4 cm³ to mm³.
6. Convert 0.35 m³ to litres.
7. Convert 2750 ml to litres and to cm³.
8. Convert 6.2 × 10⁶ mm² to m².
9. Write in order, smallest first: 1.2 l, 1150 cm³, 0.0013 m³.
10. A jug holds 1.25 litres. How many glasses of 200 ml can be completely filled from it?
11. Paint covers 12 m² per litre. A wall measures 450 cm by 280 cm. How many litres are needed for one coat? Give an exact answer.
12. A carton of 1.8 kg of flour is shared into bags of 250 g. How many full bags are made?

### Answers

1. 4.7 × 1000 = **4700 mm**
2. 0.62 × 10 000 = **6200 cm²**
3. 8500 ÷ 10 000 = **0.85 m²**
4. 3 × 1 000 000 = **3 000 000 m²**
5. 2.4 × 1000 = **2400 mm³**
6. 0.35 × 1000 = **350 litres**
7. **2.75 litres** and **2750 cm³**
8. 6.2 × 10⁶ ÷ 10⁶ = **6.2 m²**
9. 1150 cm³ = 1.15 l and 0.0013 m³ = 1.3 l, so the order is **1150 cm³, 1.2 l, 0.0013 m³**
10. 1250 ÷ 200 = 6.25, so **6 glasses**
11. 4.5 × 2.8 = 12.6 m²; 12.6 ÷ 12 = **1.05 litres**
12. 1800 ÷ 250 = 7.2, so **7 full bags**
