AQA AS Level Economics (7135) is a standalone AS qualification covering the core of the subject: how markets work
and fail, and how the national economy performs in a global context. It is a qualification in its own right, with its
own papers, rather than simply the first half of the A-level. It is linear and not tiered, and it is assessed by two compulsory written papers, each
lasting 1 hour 30 minutes, worth 70 marks and carrying 50% of the AS.

## How the course is organised

The AS content is set out in two sections, numbered 3.1 and 3.2.

**3.1 The operation of markets and market failure.** This section opens with economic methodology and the
economic problem, then covers price determination in a competitive market, and production, costs and revenue.
It goes on to competitive and concentrated markets, and closes with the market mechanism, market failure and
government intervention in markets.

**3.2 The national economy in a global context.** This section covers the measurement of macroeconomic
performance; how the macroeconomy works, including the circular flow of income, aggregate demand and aggregate
supply analysis, and related concepts; economic performance; and macroeconomic policy.

AQA publishes the AS (7135) and the A-level (7136) in a single specification document. The AS content is a
narrower selection: the A-level adds areas such as the labour market, the distribution of income and wealth,
and the international economy.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7135/1 Paper 1: The operation of markets and market failure | 1 hour 30 minutes | 70 | 50% | Section 3.1 |
| 7135/2 Paper 2: The national economy in a global context | 1 hour 30 minutes | 70 | 50% | Section 3.2 |

The two papers have the same format:

- **Section A**: 20 multiple-choice questions worth 20 marks.
- **Section B**: data-response questions requiring written answers, with a choice of one from two contexts,
  worth 50 marks.

The total for the AS is 140 marks, and each paper is scaled by 1, so raw marks count directly. There is no
coursework. The first AS exams for this specification were sat in June 2016, and the figures here are taken from
version 1.3 of the specification, dated 29 June 2022.

## How the parts fit together

Each paper covers one section, but the economics is connected. Market failure and government intervention in 3.1
lead directly into the policy questions in 3.2: a tax on a harmful good is a microeconomic intervention and also
part of the government's fiscal position. Production, costs and revenue help explain how firms respond when
aggregate demand changes. The supply-and-demand reasoning learned for individual markets reappears in the AD/AS
framework used for the whole economy.

Both papers test the same two skills. Section A rewards quick, accurate recall and the ability to apply a concept
to a short problem, often involving a diagram or simple calculation. Section B rewards reading data carefully,
applying theory to the context chosen and building an evaluative written answer. Because the formats match,
technique developed on one paper carries straight over to the other. The choice of context in Section B also
means it pays to read both sets of data briefly before committing, rather than choosing on the topic heading
alone.

## Planning your study

With only two content sections, the AS can be planned as one compact sequence:

1. **Start with methodology and the economic problem**, then price determination in competitive markets. Supply
   and demand analysis is the core tool for both papers.
2. **Move to production, costs and revenue, and competitive and concentrated markets.**
3. **Finish 3.1 with market failure and government intervention**, which links forward to the macro section.
4. **Teach 3.2 in order**: measurement first, then the circular flow and AD/AS, then economic performance and
   macroeconomic policy.

Sections 3.1 and 3.2 can also be taught alongside each other rather than one after the other; either works,
provided the links between them are made explicit. Practise drawing the core diagrams until they are quick and accurate, and
use short multiple-choice sets from the first weeks. Full papers are most useful once each section has been
completed.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many exams are there in AQA AS Level Economics?**
Two written papers. Each lasts 1 hour 30 minutes, is worth 70 marks and counts for 50% of the AS, giving 140 marks in total.

**What is on each AS Economics paper?**
Paper 1 covers section 3.1, The operation of markets and market failure. Paper 2 covers section 3.2, The national economy in a global context.

**What is the question format?**
Each paper has a Section A of 20 multiple-choice questions worth 20 marks and a Section B of data-response questions worth 50 marks, with a choice of one from two contexts.

**How does the AS relate to the A-level?**
AQA publishes AS (7135) and A-level (7136) Economics in one specification document. The AS covers a narrower selection of content and is assessed by its own two papers.

**When were the first AS exams?**
The first AS exams for this specification were sat in June 2016.
