These notes cover the syllabus writing skill **"demonstrate accuracy in spelling, punctuation and grammar"**, assessed as **W5**, "make accurate use of spelling, punctuation and grammar", in both sections of Paper 2. Where W5 fits among the writing objectives is in the [Paper 2 Writing revision notes](/resources/o-level-english-paper-2-writing-revision-notes/); full explanations and a corrected paragraph are in the [accuracy study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-english-language-1123-accuracy-spelling-punctuation-grammar/).

## Where W5 is assessed

| Task | W5 assessed? |
|---|---|
| Paper 2 Section A, Directed Writing | yes (with W1–W4) |
| Paper 2 Section B, Composition | yes (with W1, W2, W3) |
| Paper 1 Question 3a, Summary | no: the writing objectives there are W2 and W3 |

Accuracy still matters on Paper 1: errors that obscure meaning make answers harder to credit.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Main clause | a group of words with a subject and verb that can stand alone |
| Subordinate clause | a clause that cannot stand alone ("because the bus was late") |
| Comma splice | two main clauses joined only by a comma |
| Run-on sentence | two main clauses with no punctuation between them |
| Fragment | words punctuated as a sentence but missing a main clause |
| Subject-verb agreement | a singular subject takes a singular verb, a plural subject a plural verb |
| Pronoun reference | it must be clear which noun a pronoun stands for |
| Homophones | words that sound alike but differ in spelling and meaning |

## Sentence boundary fixes

Two main clauses can be joined by:

1. a full stop;
2. a comma **plus** a joining word (and, but, so, yet, or);
3. a semicolon;
4. making one clause subordinate ("When...", "Although...", "Because...").

Never by a comma alone.

## Comma rules

| Use a comma | Example |
|---|---|
| between list items | apples, pears and plums |
| after an opening clause or phrase | After the match, we went home. |
| around removable extra information | My aunt, a nurse, works nights. |
| before a joining word linking two sentences | It rained, but we played. |
| with direct speech | "Stop," he said. |

Do **not** put a comma between a subject and its verb.

## Apostrophes

| Case | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| contraction | apostrophe for missing letters | can't, it's |
| singular owner | 's | the girl's coat |
| plural owner ending in s | s' | the girls' coats |
| plural owner not ending in s | 's | the people's choice |
| plain plural | no apostrophe | tickets, the 1990s |
| its / it's | its = belonging to it; it's = it is / it has | |

## Agreement and tense checks

- Find the real subject: "The box of books **is** heavy."
- "Each", "every", "everyone", "nobody" take singular verbs.
- "Neither... nor": the verb agrees with the nearer subject.
- Keep the narrative tense consistent unless there is a reason to change it.
- "Could have", never "could of".
- "Fewer" for countable nouns, "less" for amounts.

## Confusables

| Words | Memory aid |
|---|---|
| there / their / they're | here is in there; their has "heir"; they're = they are |
| affect / effect | Affect is an Action (verb); Effect is the End result (noun) |
| practice / practise | ice is a noun; practise ends in "-ise" like the verb "advise" |
| principal / principle | the principal is your pal; a principle is a rule |
| lose / loose | lose has lost an o |
| quiet / quite | quiet ends like "diet" |

## Proofreading routine (about five minutes per section)

1. Commas between possible sentences.
2. Apostrophes and its/it's.
3. Main verbs: agreement and tense.
4. Your personal top three errors.
5. Spellings of key words and names from the question.

One neat line through an error; correction above.

## Mini example (original)

> *Wrong:* The new rules effects everyone, there not popular with the older students.
> *Right:* The new rules affect everyone, and they're not popular with the older students.

Three fixes: verb *affect* and its agreement with *rules*; the comma splice joined with *and*; *there* corrected to *they're*.

## Exam traps

- Comma splices, especially in fast writing.
- Apostrophes in plain plurals.
- its/it's and there/their/they're.
- "Could of".
- Tense drift in narratives.
- No time left to proofread.
- Ambitious punctuation (semicolons, colons) used incorrectly.

## Self-test

1. In which two tasks is W5 assessed?
2. What is a comma splice?
3. Give two ways to correct "The bell rang, everyone left."
4. When is a sentence fragment acceptable?
5. Punctuate correctly: "the teachers room" (one teacher).
6. Punctuate correctly: "the teachers room" (several teachers).
7. Choose the correct verb: "Each of the boys (has / have) a ticket."
8. Correct: "We should of left earlier."
9. Which is correct: "fewer cars" or "less cars"?
10. Fill the gap with "affect" or "effect": "The new timetable will ___ every class."
11. What does a colon do?
12. Why should proofreading be done one error type at a time?

**Answers**

1. Paper 2 Section A (directed writing) and Section B (composition).
2. Two main clauses joined only by a comma.
3. Any two: "The bell rang. Everyone left." / "The bell rang, and everyone left." / "The bell rang; everyone left." / "When the bell rang, everyone left."
4. Occasionally in a composition, for deliberate effect, when the rest of the writing shows control of full sentences.
5. the teacher's room
6. the teachers' room
7. has
8. "We should have left earlier."
9. "fewer cars".
10. affect
11. It introduces an explanation or a list that follows a complete sentence.
12. When reading for meaning, the eye corrects errors automatically; checking one type at a time makes them visible.
