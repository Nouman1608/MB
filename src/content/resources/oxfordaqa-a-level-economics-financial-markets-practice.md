Section 3.4 is the extra International A2 content, examined from May/June 2022. All figures and institutions are invented. ## Questions

**1.** Prices rise by 6% a year. A household keeps 5,000 units as cash for 2 years.
**(a)** Calculate the real value of the cash after 2 years, to 2 decimal places. **[2]**
**(b)** Which function of money has been weakened? **[1]**

**2.** Distinguish between the money market and the capital market, giving one asset traded in each. **[4]**

**3.** A bond has a face value of 100, a coupon of 7 a year and a market price of 95.
**(a)** Calculate its yield, to 2 decimal places. **[2]**
**(b)** Market interest rates rise to 8%. Calculate the price at which this bond would yield 8%, and explain why the price moves in that direction. **[2]**

**4.** An importer in Country D must pay 150,000 betas in 3 months. Today 1 alpha = 3.00 betas. The 3-month forward rate is 1 alpha = 2.90 betas.
**(a)** Explain the difference between a spot and a forward contract. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the alpha cost of the payment if the firm uses the forward contract. **[1]**
**(c)** In 3 months the spot rate is 1 alpha = 2.70 betas. Calculate the gain or loss from using the contract, compared with paying at the spot rate. **[2]**

**5.** A farm agrees to sell 80 tonnes of grain forward at 300 per tonne.
**(a)** Calculate the revenue the farm has fixed. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the gain or loss from the contract compared with selling at the spot price if the spot price at delivery is (i) 270 and (ii) 340. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the farm might still choose a forward contract. **[1]**

**6.** A firm with 240,000 shares needs 600,000. Option A is a 5% bond. Option B is 60,000 new shares at 10 each. Operating profit is 90,000.
**(a)** Calculate profit per share under each option. **[3]**
**(b)** Profit falls to 20,000. Calculate the result under each option and explain which is riskier. **[3]**

**7.**
**(a)** Distinguish between a commercial bank and an investment bank. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why a bank that does both may raise systemic risk. **[2]**

**8.** A bank has cash of 90 and loans of 810. Its deposits are 820 and its equity is 80.
**(a)** Calculate its capital ratio, to 2 decimal places. **[1]**
**(b)** 12% of deposits are withdrawn. Calculate the shortfall of cash. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the bank may be illiquid although it is solvent, and how a central bank could help. **[3]**

**9.** The required reserve ratio is 8%. A bank receives a new deposit of 2,000.
**(a)** Calculate the maximum credit multiplier and the maximum total deposits this supports. **[2]**
**(b)** The ratio rises to 10%. Calculate the fall in maximum total deposits. **[1]**
**(c)** Give one advantage and one drawback of the higher ratio. **[2]**

**10.** An index of asset prices is 100 in year 0, 140 in year 1 and 210 in year 2. In year 3 it falls by 40%.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage rise from year 0 to year 2. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the index in year 3 and the percentage rise needed to regain 210. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how herding behaviour and asymmetric information can help to create an asset price bubble. **[5]**

**11.** A shadow-banking lender holds assets of 800, financed by equity of 40 and short-term borrowing of 760. It has no deposit insurance.
**(a)** Calculate its leverage and the percentage fall in asset values that would remove all its equity. **[2]**
**(b)** Its failure cuts lending to firms by 90. The marginal propensity to withdraw is 0.4. Calculate the multiplier and the fall in GDP. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate whether stricter regulation of shadow banking is the best way to reduce systemic risk. **[6]**

**12.** An insurer sells 800 policies at a premium of 140. Each policy holder has a 0.5% chance of a claim of 24,000.
**(a)** Calculate expected claims, premium income and expected profit. **[3]**
**(b)** After the policies are sold, holders take less care and the claim chance rises to 0.8%. Calculate the result and name the problem. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Real value = 5,000 / 1.06² [1] = **4,449.98** [1]
**(b)** Store of value [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the price factor for each year of inflation; a single division by 1.12 gives a different figure and hides the method.

**2.** The money market trades short-term funds and assets, with a life of up to about a year [1], for example government bills [1]. The capital market trades long-term finance [1], for example shares or bonds [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say how the two markets differ, such as the time span of what is traded; naming an example without a difference leaves the comparison unfinished.

**3. (a)** Yield = 7 / 95 × 100 [1] = **7.37%** [1]
**(b)** Price = 7 / 0.08 = **87.50** [1]. The price falls because buyers can now earn 8% on new bonds, so they will pay less for the older bond until its yield matches [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say the direction of the price change and the reason together; the number alone does not explain the mechanism.

**4. (a)** A spot contract is settled now at today's price [1]. A forward contract fixes a price now for payment at a later date [1]
**(b)** 150,000 / 2.90 = **51,724.14 alphas** [1]
**(c)** Unhedged cost = 150,000 / 2.70 = 55,555.56 [1]; the contract **saved 3,831.42 alphas** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Currency questions reward a stated direction of division; check that dividing betas by betas per alpha gives alphas.

**5. (a)** 80 × 300 = **24,000** [1]
**(b)** (i) At 270, spot revenue is 21,600, so the contract **gained 2,400** [1]. (ii) At 340, spot revenue is 27,200, so the contract **lost 3,200** [1]
**(c)** It gives certainty of revenue for planning, which is hedging [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* State gain or loss against the spot alternative, not against the contract price.

**6. (a)** Interest = 30,000 [1]. Bond: (90,000 − 30,000) / 240,000 = **0.25 per share** [1]. Shares: 90,000 / 300,000 = **0.30 per share** [1]
**(b)** Bond: 20,000 − 30,000 = **−10,000** [1]. Shares: 20,000 / 300,000 = **0.07 per share** [1]. The bond is riskier because the interest must be paid whatever the profit [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the right number of shares for each option; many wrong answers come from keeping 240,000 shares in option B.

**7. (a)** A commercial bank accepts deposits and lends them on to households and firms [1]. An investment bank raises capital and advises clients, and trades financial assets [1]. An investment bank deals mainly with firms, governments and investors rather than ordinary savers [1]
**(b)** Trading losses could threaten the deposits of ordinary savers [1]. A large combined bank is connected to many others, so its failure spreads and is harder to resolve [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a contrast, not two separate descriptions, and link part (b) to risk spreading.

**8. (a)** 80 / 900 = **8.89%** [1]
**(b)** Withdrawals = 0.12 × 820 = 98.4 [1]; shortfall = 98.4 − 90 = **8.4** [1]
**(c)** Assets of 900 exceed deposits of 820, so the bank is solvent [1]. Loans are long term and cannot be called in quickly, so cash runs short (maturity mismatch) [1]. As lender of last resort, the central bank can lend against sound assets, which avoids a run or forced sales [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain the difference between having enough assets and having enough cash; this is the point the question tests.

**9. (a)** Multiplier = 1 / 0.08 = **12.5** [1]; maximum deposits = 2,000 × 12.5 = **25,000** [1]
**(b)** At 10%, 2,000 × 10 = 20,000, so the fall is **5,000** [1]
**(c)** Advantage: banks hold more reserves against withdrawals, so they are more stable [1]. Drawback: less credit is created, so investment and growth may be lower [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Pair each effect with its consequence; "banks lend less" needs a consequence for the economy.

**10. (a)** (210 − 100) / 100 = **110%** [1]
**(b)** 210 × 0.6 = **126** [1]; rise needed = (210 / 126) − 1 = **66.67%** [1]
**(c)** Buyers expect further price rises [1]. They copy others instead of judging value (herding) [1]. Prices rise above fundamental value [1]. Buyers cannot judge the true value or risk of the asset, so they rely on others' actions (asymmetric information) [1]. When confidence turns, selling is also herded, so prices fall sharply and borrowers who used the asset as security may default [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each concept to the price path; definitions of herding with no effect on prices tend to stay unrewarded.

**11. (a)** Leverage = 800 / 40 = **20** [1]; 40 / 800 = **5% fall** [1]
**(b)** Multiplier = 1 / 0.4 = **2.5** [1]; fall in GDP = 90 × 2.5 = **225** [1]
**(c)** Shadow banks can spread risk because they borrow short with high leverage and no deposit insurance [1]. Capital and liquidity rules would reduce leverage and the chance of failure [1]. Regulation could stop risk being hidden from supervisors [1]. But activity may move to institutions outside the rules, and compliance costs may cut lending and growth [1]. Other tools, such as central bank liquidity and better disclosure, may work alongside regulation [1]. A judgement is needed, such as: it depends on how large and connected the shadow sector is, and on how well rules can be enforced [1] **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* A conclusion that weighs size, connection and enforcement is better than listing advantages and disadvantages.

**12. (a)** Expected claims = 800 × 0.005 × 24,000 = 96,000 [1]; premium income = 800 × 140 = 112,000 [1]; expected profit = **16,000** [1]
**(b)** Claims = 800 × 0.008 × 24,000 = 153,600, so there is a **loss of 41,600** [1]. This is moral hazard: the insured take less care because the insurer bears the cost [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name who bears the cost when you describe moral hazard; otherwise the description is incomplete.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "liquid" when the answer needs "solvent", or the reverse.
- Forgetting that bond prices and yields move in opposite directions.
- Using the wrong number of shares when comparing debt with equity.
- Dividing a foreign currency amount by the wrong exchange rate.
- Calling the forward contract a loss without comparing it with the spot rate.
- Describing a bubble without the role of expectations.
- Explaining regulation with no cost or limit.
- Giving an extended answer with no judgement.
