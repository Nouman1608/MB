## Section A

**1.** For **two** assumptions of the kinetic theory of gases, one about the size of the molecules and one about the forces between them, state the assumption and explain how it is used in deriving pV = ⅓Nm⟨c²⟩. **[4]**

**2.** Four gas molecules have speeds of 300, 500, 500 and 700 m s⁻¹. Calculate their mean speed, mean-square speed and r.m.s. speed. **[3]**

**3.** State the unit of ⟨c²⟩, and show that pV and Nm⟨c²⟩ have the same unit. **[2]**

**4.** Calculate the average translational kinetic energy of a gas molecule at –23 °C. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the r.m.s. speed of the molecules of a gas doubles when its thermodynamic temperature is multiplied by four. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A nitrogen molecule of mass 4.65 × 10⁻²⁶ kg moves inside a cube of side 0.10 m. Its velocity component perpendicular to one wall is 500 m s⁻¹. Assume it collides elastically with the walls and with no other molecules.

**(a)** Calculate the change in momentum of the molecule each time it hits this wall. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the time between successive collisions with this wall. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the average force exerted by the molecule on this wall. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why the force of a real gas on the wall is steady, even though the force from one molecule is a series of separate impulses. **[1]**

**(e)** Explain where the factor ⅓ in pV = ⅓Nm⟨c²⟩ comes from. **[2]**

**7.** A gas at a pressure of 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa has a density of 2.4 kg m⁻³.

**(a)** Calculate the r.m.s. speed of its molecules. **[2]**

**(b)** The gas is heated at constant volume from 300 K to 450 K. Calculate the new pressure and the new r.m.s. speed. **[3]**

**8.** A container holds a mixture of neon (molar mass 20 g mol⁻¹) and argon (molar mass 40 g mol⁻¹) at 350 K.

**(a)** Calculate the average translational kinetic energy of a neon atom and of an argon atom. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the r.m.s. speed of each type of atom. **[3]**

**(c)** State the ratio of the r.m.s. speed of neon to that of argon. **[1]**

**9.** Starting from pV = ⅓Nm⟨c²⟩ and pV = NkT, deduce that the average translational kinetic energy of a molecule is ³⁄₂kT. **[3]**

**10.** Calculate the temperature at which the r.m.s. speed of nitrogen molecules (molar mass 28 g mol⁻¹) is 1000 m s⁻¹. **[2]**

**11.** A container of volume 0.020 m³ holds an ideal gas at a pressure of 1.5 × 10⁵ Pa.

**(a)** Show that the total translational kinetic energy of all the molecules is ³⁄₂pV. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate this energy. **[1]**

**12.** Use the kinetic theory of gases to explain why the pressure of a gas in a sealed rigid container increases when its temperature rises. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Size: the volume of the molecules is negligible compared with the volume of the gas [1]; so each molecule travels the full distance 2L between collisions with the same wall (time 2L/cₓ) [1]. Forces: there are no intermolecular forces except during collisions [1]; so molecules move at constant velocity in straight lines between collisions, keeping cₓ constant [1].

**2.** Mean speed = 2000 ÷ 4 = 500 m s⁻¹ [1]; ⟨c²⟩ = (9 + 25 + 25 + 49) × 10⁴ ÷ 4 = 2.7 × 10⁵ m² s⁻² [1]; c_r.m.s. = √(2.7 × 10⁵) = 520 m s⁻¹ [1].

**3.** m² s⁻² [1]. pV: Pa × m³ = N m = kg m² s⁻²; Nm⟨c²⟩: kg × m² s⁻² = kg m² s⁻² (N has no unit) [1].

**4.** T = 250 K [1]; E = ³⁄₂kT = 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 250 = 5.2 × 10⁻²¹ J [1].

**5.** ½m⟨c²⟩ = ³⁄₂kT, so ⟨c²⟩ ∝ T and c_r.m.s. ∝ √T [1]; multiplying T by 4 multiplies c_r.m.s. by √4 = 2 [1].

**6. (a)** Δp = 2mcₓ = 2 × 4.65 × 10⁻²⁶ × 500 = 4.65 × 10⁻²³ N s [1].

**(b)** t = 2L/cₓ = 0.20 ÷ 500 = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ s [1].

**(c)** F = Δp/t = 4.65 × 10⁻²³ ÷ 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ = 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ N [1].

**(d)** A real gas has a very large number of molecules hitting the wall every second, so their individual impulses average to a steady force [1].

**(e)** Motion is random, so the mean-square velocity components in the three perpendicular directions are equal [1]; since ⟨c²⟩ = ⟨cₓ²⟩ + ⟨c_y²⟩ + ⟨c_z²⟩ = 3⟨cₓ²⟩, the component used in the one-dimensional derivation is ⅓⟨c²⟩ [1].

**7. (a)** p = ⅓ρ⟨c²⟩, so ⟨c²⟩ = 3p/ρ = 3 × 2.0 × 10⁵ ÷ 2.4 = 2.5 × 10⁵ m² s⁻² [1]; c_r.m.s. = 500 m s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** At constant volume p ∝ T: p = 2.0 × 10⁵ × 450 ÷ 300 = 3.0 × 10⁵ Pa [1]. c_r.m.s. ∝ √T [1]: 500 × √1.5 = 612 m s⁻¹ (6.1 × 10² m s⁻¹) [1].

**8. (a)** Both: ³⁄₂kT = 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 350 = 7.2 × 10⁻²¹ J [1].

**(b)** c_r.m.s. = √(3RT/M) [1]; neon: √(3 × 8.31 × 350 ÷ 0.020) = 660 m s⁻¹ [1]; argon: √(3 × 8.31 × 350 ÷ 0.040) = 467 m s⁻¹ [1].

**(c)** √2 : 1 (about 1.41) [1].

**9.** Equate: ⅓Nm⟨c²⟩ = NkT [1]; cancel N and multiply by 3: m⟨c²⟩ = 3kT [1]; multiply by ½: ½m⟨c²⟩ = ³⁄₂kT [1].

**10.** c²_r.m.s. = 3RT/M, so T = c²M/3R [1] = (1000)² × 0.028 ÷ (3 × 8.31) = 1.1 × 10³ K [1].

**11. (a)** Total translational kinetic energy = N × ³⁄₂kT = ³⁄₂NkT [1]; and NkT = pV, so the total is ³⁄₂pV [1].

**(b)** 1.5 × 1.5 × 10⁵ × 0.020 = 4.5 × 10³ J [1].

**12.** A higher temperature means a greater mean kinetic energy, so the molecules move faster on average [1]. Each collision with a wall produces a larger change in momentum [1], and collisions with each wall are more frequent; with the volume fixed, the force on the walls, and so the pressure, increases [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating assumptions without linking them to the derivation when asked to.
- Using L/cₓ instead of 2L/cₓ, or mcₓ instead of 2mcₓ.
- Treating ⟨c²⟩ as the square of the mean speed.
- Leaving molar mass in g mol⁻¹ in √(3RT/M).
- Saying heavier atoms have more kinetic energy at the same temperature.
- Explaining increased pressure only by "more collisions" without the larger momentum change per collision.
