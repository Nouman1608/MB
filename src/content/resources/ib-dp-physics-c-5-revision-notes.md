These notes cover sub-topic C.5, Doppler effect, in theme C of the IB *Physics guide* (first assessment 2025). The light relationship, wavefront diagrams and spectral lines are SL and HL; the sound formulas are HL only. Test yourself with the [C.5 practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-physics-c-5-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Doppler effect | Change in observed frequency and wavelength due to relative motion of source and observer |
| Wavefront | Line joining points in phase, e.g. successive crests or compressions |
| Rest (emitted) value | f or λ measured with source and observer at relative rest |
| Redshift | Increase in observed wavelength: source and observer separating |
| Blueshift | Decrease in observed wavelength: source and observer approaching |
| Line-of-sight velocity | Component of velocity along the line joining source and observer |
| us | Speed of the source relative to the medium (HL) |
| uo | Speed of the observer relative to the medium (HL) |

## Wavefront diagrams (SL and HL)

| Case | Wavefronts | λ in medium | f observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Both at rest | Concentric, even | λ | f |
| Source moving towards observer | Bunched ahead of source | Shorter | Higher |
| Source moving away | Spread behind source | Longer | Lower |
| Observer moving towards source | Still concentric | Unchanged | Higher (meets fronts more often) |
| Observer moving away | Still concentric | Unchanged | Lower |

**Key idea:** wave speed in the medium never changes.

## Light (SL and HL)

```
Δf/f = Δλ/λ ≈ v/c      valid for v much less than c
```

*Worked reminder:* a line at rest wavelength 400.0 nm observed at 401.2 nm → Δλ/λ = 0.003 → v = 9.0 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹, **receding**.

**Spectral lines: method in steps**

1. Identify a known line and its rest wavelength.
2. Find Δλ = observed − rest.
3. Sign: + receding (red), − approaching (blue).
4. v = c × |Δλ|/λ.
5. All lines shift by the same fraction; periodic back-and-forth shift = orbital motion.

## HL only: sound and mechanical waves

```
Moving source:    f′ = f v/(v ± us)     (− approaching, + receding)
Moving observer:  f′ = f (v ± uo)/v     (+ approaching, − receding)
λ ahead of moving source = (v − us)/f;  behind = (v + us)/f
```

*Worked reminder (HL):* f = 900 Hz, observer approaches at 30 m s⁻¹, v = 340 m s⁻¹ → f′ = 900 × 370/340 = **979 Hz**.

*Worked reminder (HL):* f = 300 Hz, source approaches at 20 m s⁻¹ → f′ = 300 × 340/320 = **319 Hz**; λ ahead = 320/300 = **1.07 m**.

**Two-reading method (HL)**

1. Write the approach and recession equations.
2. Divide one by the other to cancel f.
3. Solve for the speed.
4. Substitute back to find f.

## Must-know distinctions

| Don't confuse | Difference |
|---|---|
| Moving source vs moving observer (sound) | Changes λ in medium vs changes rate of meeting fronts |
| Sound vs light | Medium sets a reference frame vs only relative velocity matters |
| Redshift vs blueshift | Separating (λ up) vs approaching (λ down) |
| Total velocity vs line-of-sight | Only the line-of-sight component produces a shift |

## Exam traps

- "The speed of sound increases when the source approaches." It does not.
- Wrong sign: approaching must give f′ > f.
- Applying the sound formulas to light.
- Using percentage shifts without converting nm consistently.
- Forgetting to state direction (towards or away) after finding a speed from a spectrum.
- Wavefront descriptions that do not mention wavelength or frequency.
- Sign errors in HL formulas.
- Not stating the direction of motion from a spectral shift.

## Quick self-test

1. Define the Doppler effect.
2. For a moving source, are wavefronts closer together ahead of or behind the source?
3. For a moving observer and stationary source, does the wavelength in the air change?
4. State the light Doppler relationship and its condition.
5. A galaxy's lines are shifted to longer wavelengths. What does this show?
6. A line of rest wavelength 589.0 nm is observed at 588.4 nm. Find the line-of-sight speed and direction.
7. Why does a star's line shift back and forth periodically?
8. (HL) A 500 Hz source approaches a listener at 34 m s⁻¹. Find the observed frequency (v = 340 m s⁻¹).
9. (HL) A listener moves away from a 1000 Hz source at 17 m s⁻¹. Find the observed frequency.
10. (HL) Find the wavelength behind a 400 Hz source moving at 40 m s⁻¹.
11. Which component of velocity produces a Doppler shift?
12. Why do sound formulas distinguish source and observer while the light relationship does not?

### Answers

1. The change in observed frequency (and wavelength) of a wave due to relative motion between source and observer.
2. **Ahead** of the source.
3. **No**; only the rate at which wavefronts are met changes.
4. Δf/f = Δλ/λ ≈ v/c, valid when v is much less than c.
5. The galaxy is **receding** (redshift).
6. Δλ = −0.6 nm; v = 3.00 × 10⁸ × 0.6/589.0 = **3.1 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹**, **approaching**.
7. It is orbiting, moving alternately towards and away from us.
8. f′ = 500 × 340/306 = **556 Hz**.
9. f′ = 1000 × 323/340 = **950 Hz**.
10. λ = (340 + 40)/400 = **0.95 m**.
11. The component along the line joining source and observer.
12. Sound travels through a medium, which gives a frame of reference; light needs no medium, so only relative velocity matters.
