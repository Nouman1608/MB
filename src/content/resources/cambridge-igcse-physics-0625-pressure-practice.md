These questions cover section 1.8, Pressure, of the Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Take g = 9.8 N/kg.

> **Tier note:** questions 1 to 5 and 7 to 10 use Core content only (1.8 Core 1–3), and every candidate should do them. Questions 6, 11 and 12, marked *(Extended)*, use Supplement content (1.8 Supplement 4), examined on Papers 2 and 4 only. The marks shown are indicative only.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** **(a)** Define pressure. **[1]**
**(b)** State the unit of pressure. **[1]**

**2.** A crate of weight 1500 N rests on the floor. The area in contact with the floor is 0.75 m². Calculate the pressure on the floor. **[2]**

**3.** A force of 2.0 N pushes a needle into fabric. The area of the needle's tip is 0.010 mm². Calculate the pressure under the tip in Pa. **[3]**

**4.** Explain why farm tractors are fitted with wide tyres. **[2]**

**5.** A swimmer dives from the surface to the bottom of a deep pool. State and explain how the pressure on her changes. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Calculate the pressure due to the water at a depth of 8.0 m in a reservoir. The density of water is 1000 kg/m³. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A solid metal block measures 0.40 m × 0.20 m × 0.10 m and has a mass of 64 kg.

**(a)** Calculate the weight of the block. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the largest and the smallest pressures the block can exert when it rests on a flat floor. **[4]**
**(c)** Explain why the pressure depends on which face the block rests on. **[1]**

**8.** A thumb pushes a drawing pin into a board with a force of 20 N. The area of the head is 1.0 cm² and the area of the point is 0.050 mm².

**(a)** Calculate the pressure on the thumb. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the pressure on the board. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the pin goes into the board but not into the thumb. **[1]**

**9.** A tall plastic bottle full of water has three small holes, one near the top, one in the middle and one near the bottom.

**(a)** Describe how the jets of water from the three holes compare. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain your answer to (a). **[1]**
**(c)** The bottle is refilled to the same depth with salt water, which is denser. State how the jet from the bottom hole changes. **[1]**
**(d)** A wider bottle is filled with water to the same depth. State how the pressure at its bottom hole compares with that in the first bottle. **[1]**

**10.** An elephant of weight 50 000 N stands on four feet, each of area 0.10 m². A woman of weight 500 N stands with all her weight on one shoe heel of area 0.80 cm².

**(a)** Calculate the pressure exerted by the elephant on the ground. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the pressure exerted by the woman's heel. **[2]**
**(c)** State which is more likely to damage a wooden floor, and why. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A submarine is at a depth of 150 m in sea water of density 1030 kg/m³.

**(a)** Calculate the pressure due to the sea water at this depth. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the force of the water on a window of area 0.20 m². **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why submarines that go very deep need very strong hulls. **[1]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A water tank stands on a tower. The water surface in the tank is 15 m above a tap.

**(a)** Calculate the pressure due to the water at the tap. **[2]**
**(b)** The tank is filled so that its water surface rises by 3.0 m. Calculate the new pressure due to the water at the tap. **[1]**
**(c)** In another tank, a liquid gives a pressure of 1.2 × 10⁴ Pa at a depth of 1.5 m. Calculate its density. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Force per unit area [1]
**(b)** Pa (pascal, or N/m²) [1]

**2.** p = F/A = 1500 ÷ 0.75 [1] = **2000 Pa** [1]

**3.** 0.010 mm² = 0.010 × 10⁻⁶ m² = 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ m² [1]; p = 2.0 ÷ 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ [1] = **2.0 × 10⁸ Pa** [1]

**4.** Wide tyres have a large area in contact with the ground [1]; for the same weight this gives a low pressure, so the tractor does not sink into soft ground [1].

**5.** The pressure increases [1]; there is a greater depth of water above her (pressure increases with depth) [1].

**6.** Δp = ρgΔh = 1000 × 9.8 × 8.0 [1] = **78 400 Pa** [1]

**7. (a)** 64 × 9.8 = **627 N** (627.2 N) [1]
**(b)** Smallest face: 0.20 × 0.10 = 0.020 m² [1]; largest pressure = 627.2 ÷ 0.020 = **31 400 Pa** [1]; largest face: 0.40 × 0.20 = 0.080 m² [1]; smallest pressure = 627.2 ÷ 0.080 = **7840 Pa** [1]
**(c)** The weight is the same but the area in contact changes, and pressure = force ÷ area [1].

**8. (a)** 1.0 cm² = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ m² [1]; p = 20 ÷ 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ = **2.0 × 10⁵ Pa** [1]
**(b)** 0.050 mm² = 5.0 × 10⁻⁸ m² [1]; p = 20 ÷ 5.0 × 10⁻⁸ = **4.0 × 10⁸ Pa** [1]
**(c)** The force is the same, but the point's tiny area gives a pressure 2000 times larger, large enough to push into the board [1].

**9. (a)** Water leaves the bottom hole fastest (the strongest jet) [1]; water leaves the top hole slowest (the weakest jet) [1].
*Examiner insight:* Compare the speed of the water as it leaves each hole. How far a jet lands also depends on how high the hole is above the ground, so "lands furthest" is not a safe answer.
**(b)** The pressure increases with depth below the water surface [1].
**(c)** It becomes faster (stronger), because the pressure is greater in a denser liquid [1].
**(d)** The same [1].

**10. (a)** area = 4 × 0.10 = 0.40 m² [1]; p = 50 000 ÷ 0.40 = **125 000 Pa** [1]
**(b)** 0.80 cm² = 8.0 × 10⁻⁵ m² [1]; p = 500 ÷ 8.0 × 10⁻⁵ = **6.25 × 10⁶ Pa** [1]
**(c)** The woman's heel: its pressure is about 50 times larger [1].
*Examiner insight:* Using one foot's area for the elephant, while it stands on all four, gives 500 000 Pa and loses the first mark.

**11. (a)** Δp = 1030 × 9.8 × 150 [1] = **1.5 × 10⁶ Pa** (1 514 100 Pa) [1]
**(b)** F = pA = 1.51 × 10⁶ × 0.20 [1] = **3.0 × 10⁵ N** [1]
**(c)** Pressure increases with depth, so at great depth the forces on the hull are very large [1].

**12. (a)** Δp = 1000 × 9.8 × 15 [1] = **147 000 Pa** [1]
**(b)** 1000 × 9.8 × 18 = **176 400 Pa** [1]
**(c)** ρ = Δp/(gΔh) = 1.2 × 10⁴ ÷ (9.8 × 1.5) [1] = **820 kg/m³** (816 kg/m³) [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Converting cm² or mm² to m² wrongly.
- Using mass instead of weight as the force.
- Saying a container's width changes the pressure at the same depth.
- Using the area of one foot when an animal stands on several.
- *(Extended)* Putting density in g/cm³ into Δp = ρgΔh.
