## Section A

**1.** Classify each overhead as fixed, variable, semi-fixed or semi-variable.

**(a)** Rent of a factory **[1]**

**(b)** One supervisor is needed for every 15 workers **[1]**

**(c)** A telephone bill made up of a line rental and a charge for each call **[1]**

**(d)** Packing materials used for each unit sold **[1]**

**2.** An electricity bill has a standing charge of 150 and a charge of 0.04 per unit used. Calculate the bill for a month in which 8,000 units are used. **[2]**

**3.** Distinguish between allocation, apportionment and absorption of overheads. **[3]**

**4.** A department has budgeted overheads of 96,000 and budgeted machine hours of 12,000. Calculate the machine hour rate and the overhead absorbed by a job that uses 35 machine hours. **[3]**

**5.** Overhead absorbed in a period was 68,400 and the actual overhead incurred was 70,150. Calculate the difference, state whether it is under- or over-absorbed and state how it affects profit. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** Cutler Engineering has two production departments, Cutting and Finishing, and one service department, Stores. The overheads for the year are:

| | Cutting | Finishing | Stores |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Allocated overheads | 18,000 | 12,000 | 6,000 |
| Floor area (square metres) | 500 | 300 | 200 |

Rent of 20,000 and lighting of 4,000 are shared by floor area. The cost of Stores is shared 60% to Cutting and 40% to Finishing. Budgeted machine hours in Cutting are 9,120 and budgeted direct labour hours in Finishing are 7,840.

**(a)** Prepare a table showing the overheads of each department after the rent and lighting have been apportioned. **[5]**

**(b)** Reapportion the cost of Stores to Cutting and Finishing. **[3]**

**(c)** Calculate the overhead absorption rate of each production department, using a machine hour rate for Cutting and a labour hour rate for Finishing. Give each rate to the nearest dollar. **[3]**

**(d)** Actual results were: Cutting 9,400 machine hours and overhead of 37,200; Finishing 7,500 labour hours and overhead of 23,900. Calculate the over or under absorption in each department. **[4]**

**7.** Hartley Works has two production departments, X and Y, and two service departments, Power and Repairs. The service departments serve each other. Overheads before sharing are X 30,000, Y 22,000, Power 10,000 and Repairs 8,000. Power is shared X 50%, Y 30% and Repairs 20%. Repairs is shared X 40%, Y 40% and Power 20%. Using simultaneous equations, calculate the total overhead of Power and Repairs after the sharing between them, and the final overhead of X and Y (to the nearest dollar). **[8]**

**8.** Evaluate the use of a labour hour rate rather than a machine hour rate in a department where most of the work is done by machines. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Fixed [1]

**(b)** Semi-fixed (stepped) [1]

**(c)** Semi-variable [1]

**(d)** Variable [1]

**2.** 150 + 8,000 × 0.04 [1] = 150 + 320 = **470** [1].

**3.** Allocation: charging a cost that belongs wholly to one department to that department [1]. Apportionment: sharing a cost used by several departments on a fair basis [1]. Absorption: charging the overheads of a department to units of output or jobs using a rate [1].

**4.** Rate 96,000 ÷ 12,000 = **8 per machine hour** [1]; absorbed 35 × 8 = **280** [1]. The method uses budgeted figures to calculate the rate [1].

**5.** 70,150 − 68,400 = **1,750** [1]; under-absorbed, because the absorbed overhead is less than actual [1]; it is an additional expense that reduces profit [1].

**6. (a)**

| | Cutting | Finishing | Stores | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Allocated | 18,000 | 12,000 | 6,000 | 36,000 |
| Rent (20,000 by 5 : 3 : 2) | 10,000 | 6,000 | 4,000 | 20,000 |
| Lighting (4,000 by 5 : 3 : 2) | 2,000 | 1,200 | 800 | 4,000 |
| **Total** | **30,000** | **19,200** | **10,800** | **60,000** |

Rent [2]; lighting [2]; totals 30,000, 19,200 and 10,800 [1].

**(b)** Stores 10,800: Cutting 60% = **6,480**; Finishing 40% = **4,320** [2]. New totals Cutting **36,480** and Finishing **23,520** [1].

**(c)** Cutting 36,480 ÷ 9,120 = **4 per machine hour** [1]; Finishing 23,520 ÷ 7,840 = **3 per labour hour** [1]. Rates are based on budgeted hours [1].

**(d)** Cutting: absorbed 9,400 × 4 = 37,600; actual 37,200; **400 over-absorbed** [2]. Finishing: absorbed 7,500 × 3 = 22,500; actual 23,900; **1,400 under-absorbed** [2].

**7.** Let P be the Power total and R the Repairs total, after the sharing between them [1].

P = 10,000 + 0.2R and R = 8,000 + 0.2P [2].

Substitute: P = 10,000 + 0.2(8,000 + 0.2P) = 11,600 + 0.04P, so 0.96P = 11,600 and P = **12,083.33** [2]. R = 8,000 + 0.2 × 12,083.33 = **10,416.67** [1].

X: 30,000 + 0.5 × 12,083.33 + 0.4 × 10,416.67 = 30,000 + 6,041.67 + 4,166.67 = 40,208.33, which is **40,208** [1]. Y: 22,000 + 0.3 × 12,083.33 + 0.4 × 10,416.67 = 22,000 + 3,625.00 + 4,166.67 = 29,791.67, which is **29,792** [1]. Check: 40,208 + 29,792 = 70,000, the total of all four departments.

(Continuous allotment by repeated sharing gives the same totals, within a dollar or two of rounding. Accept it with at least three rounds shown.)

**8.** Advantage of labour hour rate: simple, as labour hours are easy to record [1]. Disadvantage: where machines do most of the work, labour hours do not reflect what causes the overhead (depreciation, power, maintenance), so jobs using many machine hours but few labour hours are undercosted [1], which could lead to selling prices that are too low [1]. Judgement: a machine hour rate is more accurate for machine-intensive work; a labour hour rate is acceptable only where labour is the main resource [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating the rate from actual figures rather than budgeted figures (Questions 4 and 6).
- Sharing the cost of a service department to a department that does not use it.
- Calling an over-absorption a debit, or under-absorption a credit (Question 5).
- Losing money in the apportionment, so the totals do not match (Question 6).
- Forgetting that service departments may serve each other (Question 7).
- Using budgeted hours rather than actual hours to find the amount absorbed.

## Approaching these questions

Build one table with all departments as columns. Add the total as a check at every stage. Calculate the rate from budgeted figures and apply it to actual hours. State clearly whether the difference is over or under absorbed and how it affects profit.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these Edexcel YAC11 questions from past papers?**
No. They are original questions on outcomes 1.4.7 to 1.4.11, written for revision. Use them alongside the official past papers.

**Which method do I use for service departments that serve each other?**
Use continuous allotment, or simultaneous equations if the question asks for them. Both give the same result apart from rounding.
