## Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Demand | How much consumers are ready and able to buy at each price in a stated period |
| Supply | How much producers are ready and able to sell at each price in a stated period |
| Demand curve | Downward-sloping line, with price up the side and quantity along the bottom |
| Supply curve | Upward-sloping line on the same axes |
| Individual demand or supply | One buyer or one firm |
| Market demand or supply | All buyers or all firms, found by aggregation |
| Aggregation | Adding quantities at each price (a horizontal sum) |
| Condition of demand or supply | A non-price influence that shifts the curve |

The syllabus gives extension, contraction, increase and decrease as examples of terminology. It does not print a fixed list of conditions, so the lists below are the standard ones.

## Drawing a diagram: steps

1. Draw two axes. Label the vertical axis "Price" and the horizontal axis "Quantity".
2. Plot the points from the schedule, or sketch the line.
3. Label the curve D (slopes down) or S (slopes up).
4. For a shift, draw the new curve and label it D1 or S1 beside the original D0 or S0.
5. Add an arrow or word to show the direction of the shift.
6. For a movement, mark two points on the same curve and show the price and quantity change.

## Movement or shift

| | Movement | Shift |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Change in the good's own price | Change in any other condition |
| Curve | Stays put | Moves left or right |
| Demand terms | Extension (price falls), contraction (price rises) | Increase (right), decrease (left) |
| Supply terms | Extension (price rises), contraction (price falls) | Increase (right), decrease (left) |

Memory aid: for demand, extension goes with a price fall. For supply, extension goes with a price rise. Both are "more quantity".

## Conditions of demand

| Change | Effect on demand |
|---|---|
| Income rises, normal good | Increase |
| Income rises, inferior good | Decrease |
| Price of a substitute rises | Increase |
| Price of a substitute falls | Decrease |
| Price of a complement rises | Decrease |
| Tastes or advertising favour the good | Increase |
| Population or number of buyers rises | Increase |
| Expectation of higher future prices | Increase now |
| Season or weather suits the good | Increase |

## Conditions of supply

| Change | Effect on supply |
|---|---|
| Costs of production fall | Increase |
| Costs of production rise | Decrease |
| Better technology | Increase |
| More firms | Increase |
| Indirect tax imposed or raised | Decrease |
| Subsidy granted or raised | Increase |
| Good harvest | Increase |
| Poor harvest, flood or drought | Decrease |
| Other goods become more profitable to make | Decrease |

## Worked reminders

**Movement (demand).** Market demand for bus journeys in City B (illustrative):

| Price (cu) | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quantity demanded | 60 | 50 | 40 | 30 | 20 |

The price falls from 6 cu to 3 cu. Quantity demanded rises from 20 to 50, an extension of 30. Percentage change = (50 − 20)/20 × 100 = **+150%**. If the price rises from 3 cu to 6 cu: (20 − 50)/50 × 100 = **−60%**, a contraction.

**Shift (demand).** A fall in the price of rival taxis cuts demand by 30% at every price. New quantities: 42, 35, 28, 21, 14. The curve shifts left. This is a decrease in demand, not a contraction.

**Aggregation (demand).** 120 identical households each buy 4 journeys at 3 cu, so market quantity is 480. The population grows by 25% to 150 households. New market quantity = 150 × 4 = 600. That is 120 more, or (600 − 480)/480 × 100 = **25%**. Demand increases.

**Movement (supply).** A tea estate's supply (illustrative):

| Price (cu) | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quantity supplied | 0 | 20 | 40 | 60 | 80 |

The price rises from 4 cu to 6 cu. Supply extends from 40 to 80: (80 − 40)/40 × 100 = **+100%**.

**Shift (supply, cost fall).** Cheaper fertiliser raises supply by 20 at every price: 20, 40, 60, 80, 100. The curve shifts right.

**Shift (supply, tax).** A tax of 2 cu per unit means the estate keeps only the price minus 2 cu. Quantity at 5 cu is the old quantity at 3 cu: 20. Quantity at 6 cu is 40. Quantity at 4 cu or below is 0. Each point sits 2 cu higher, so the curve shifts up and left. A subsidy of 2 cu does the reverse: 40 at 2 cu, 60 at 3 cu and 80 at 4 cu.

**Aggregation (supply).** 8 identical farms each supply 50 kg at a given price, so market supply is 400 kg. Two more farms enter. Market supply is 10 × 50 = 500 kg, a rise of (500 − 400)/400 × 100 = **25%**. Supply increases.

## Must-know distinctions

- Quantity demanded (a point on the curve) versus demand (the whole curve).
- Extension and contraction (movement) versus increase and decrease (shift).
- A rise in price lowers quantity demanded but raises quantity supplied.
- Substitute (rival) versus complement (used together).
- Normal good versus inferior good: income rises, demand rises for one and falls for the other.
- A tax moves the supply curve left. A subsidy moves it right.
- Individual versus market: sum the quantities, never the prices.

## Reasoning and analysis

- **Why demand slopes down:** a higher price makes the good dearer than alternatives and cuts what buyers can afford, so they buy less.
- **Why supply slopes up:** a higher price brings more revenue per unit, so selling more is more profitable and new sellers are drawn in.
- **Chain for a shift:** condition changes, so buyers (or sellers) want more or less at every price, so the curve moves, so the quantity at each price is different.
- **Two things at once:** if one condition raises demand and another lowers it, the net effect depends on their size. Say so.
- **Time:** supply of crops cannot respond quickly, because planting and growing take time.
- **Product type:** higher income lifts demand for a normal good but lowers it for an inferior good.

## Quick self-test

1. Define supply.
2. The price of tea falls. State the term for the change in the demand for tea itself.
3. Name the term for a rise in income that moves the demand curve for a normal good to the right.
4. Three buyers demand 30, 45 and 25 units at one price. Calculate market demand.
5. One of those buyers demands 45 of the 100 units. Calculate that buyer's share of market demand.
6. After a shift, supply at a given price rises from 80 to 100. Calculate the percentage change and name the shift.
7. Quantity supplied falls from 100 to 80 as price falls. Calculate the percentage change and name the movement.
8. State the effect on the supply curve of a new indirect tax.
9. The price of cars rises. State the effect on the demand for petrol, giving the term.
10. Is a rise in wages for a bakery's workers a movement or a shift? Say in which direction.
11. 120 identical households each buy 4 units, and their number grows by 25%. State the new market quantity.

### Answers

1. How much of a good or service producers are ready and able to sell at each price in a stated period.
2. Extension of demand (quantity demanded rises). The curve does not move.
3. An increase in demand.
4. 30 + 45 + 25 = **100** units.
5. 45/100 × 100 = **45%**.
6. (100 − 80)/80 × 100 = **25%**. This is an increase in supply.
7. (80 − 100)/100 × 100 = **−20%**, a contraction of supply.
8. It shifts left (equivalently, upwards): a decrease in supply, because costs rise.
9. Cars and petrol are complements, so demand for petrol decreases (the curve shifts left).
10. A shift of the supply curve to the left: a decrease in supply, since costs of production rise.
11. 150 households × 4 = **600** units.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "demand increases" when the question describes a price fall. That is an extension.
- Answering a movement question by drawing a second curve.
- Leaving off axis labels or curve labels, or putting price on the horizontal axis.
- Forgetting that price on a diagram is the good's own price. Other prices are conditions.
- Treating a tax as an increase in supply. A tax raises costs, so it decreases supply.
- Adding prices when aggregating, or adding only some of the columns.
- Giving a cause with no chain: "income rises so demand shifts" is thinner than "income rises, buyers can afford more at each price, so demand increases".
- Forgetting to give the direction of the shift (left or right) or the term (increase or decrease).
- Percentage changes calculated from the wrong starting value. Divide by the original quantity.
