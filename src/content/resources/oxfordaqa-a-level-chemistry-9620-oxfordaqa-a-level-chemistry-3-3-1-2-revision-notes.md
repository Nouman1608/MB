These notes condense **3.3.1.2 Reaction mechanisms** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, examined in Unit 2 and able to reappear in Unit 5. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [reaction mechanisms study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-1-2/); then try the [reaction mechanisms practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-1-2-practice/).

## The subtopic in four lines

1. Radical mechanisms: dot for the unpaired electron, no curly arrows, a balanced equation for each step.
2. A curly arrow shows an **electron pair** moving.
3. A new bond: arrow from a **lone pair** or from a **bond**. A broken bond: arrow from **that bond**.
4. "Outline the mechanism" means structures of all species plus all curly arrows.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Mechanism | Step-by-step sequence of bond breaking and bond making |
| Radical | Species with an unpaired electron, shown by a dot |
| Homolytic fission | Bond breaks with one electron to each atom, giving two radicals |
| Heterolytic fission | Bond breaks with both electrons to one atom, giving ions |
| Curly arrow | Movement of an electron pair |
| Nucleophile | Electron pair donor; attacks a δ+ atom |
| Electrophile | Electron pair acceptor; attacked by an electron-rich centre |
| Carbocation | Ion with a positive charge on a carbon atom |
| Initiation | Step that forms radicals from molecules |
| Propagation | Step with one radical in and one radical out |
| Termination | Step in which two radicals combine |

## Radical steps at a glance

| Step | Radicals left → right | Example (bromine and methane) |
|---|---|---|
| Initiation | 0 → 2 | Br₂ → 2Br• |
| Propagation | 1 → 1 | CH₄ + Br• → •CH₃ + HBr |
| Propagation | 1 → 1 | •CH₃ + Br₂ → CH₃Br + Br• |
| Termination | 2 → 0 | •CH₃ + Br• → CH₃Br |
| Termination | 2 → 0 | 2•CH₃ → C₂H₆ |

**Line checks:** atoms balance; dot on the right atom; first propagation gives a hydrogen halide, never H•; the two propagation steps add up to the overall equation.

## Curly-arrow rules

**Start** at a lone pair (draw it) or the middle of a bond.
**End** at an atom (new bond to it, or lone pair left on it) or between two atoms (new bond).
**Never** start at a positive charge, at H⁺, or at an atom with no electrons drawn.

**Charge bookkeeping:**

- An atom that donates a lone pair to form a bond becomes one unit more positive.
- An atom that keeps both electrons of a broken bond becomes one unit more negative.
- Total charge on each side of every step must match.

## The mechanism shapes you will meet

| Mechanism | Arrow 1 | Arrow 2 | Then |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nucleophilic substitution | Nucleophile lone pair → δ+ C | C–X bond → X | X⁻ leaves |
| Electrophilic addition (HBr) | C=C → H of H–Br | H–Br bond → Br | Br⁻ lone pair → C⁺ |
| Elimination by OH⁻ | OH⁻ lone pair → H on the carbon next to C–X | That C–H bond → C–C bond | C–X bond → X |
| Acid-catalysed elimination | O lone pair → H⁺ (from the acid) | C–O bond → O, water leaves | C–H bond → C–C, H⁺ released |

These are developed in sections 3.3.3, 3.3.4 and 3.3.5; this subtopic only sets the rules for drawing them.

## Exam traps

- Arrows on radical steps: not required, and they confuse the answer.
- "H•" in the first propagation step: wrong. The product is HX.
- •CH₃ has its dot on carbon.
- A nucleophile's lone pair must be drawn for the arrow to start from it.
- Arrows point from electrons to where they go: from the C=C to H, not from H to the C=C.
- Every arrow needs a clear tail and head; an arrow ending vaguely between three atoms is not credited.
- The breaking-bond arrow starts on the bond, not on the atom that leaves.
- Check charge after each step: a missing "−" on Br⁻ or "+" on the carbocation costs marks.

## Self-test

Answer without looking, then check below.

1. What does a curly arrow represent?
2. State the two places a curly arrow can start.
3. Define homolytic fission and name the species formed.
4. Write the initiation step for the reaction of methane with bromine.
5. Write the two propagation steps for the formation of bromomethane.
6. Write a termination step that forms ethane.
7. How many radicals are on each side of a propagation step?
8. Where does the first curly arrow start in the reaction of CN⁻ with bromoethane?
9. Where does the second curly arrow in that reaction start and finish?
10. In the reaction of ethene with HBr, where does the first curly arrow start and end?
11. Ammonia's lone pair forms a bond to H⁺. What is the charge on the product?
12. Why are curly arrows not used for radical mechanisms in this specification?

### Answers

1. The movement of a pair of electrons.
2. A lone pair, or a covalent bond.
3. A bond breaks with one electron going to each atom; two radicals form.
4. Br₂ → 2Br•.
5. CH₄ + Br• → •CH₃ + HBr; •CH₃ + Br₂ → CH₃Br + Br•.
6. 2•CH₃ → C₂H₆.
7. One on each side.
8. At the lone pair on the carbon of CN⁻; it ends at the δ+ carbon bonded to Br.
9. It starts at the C–Br bond and ends at the Br atom, which leaves as Br⁻.
10. It starts at the C=C double bond and ends at the H atom of H–Br.
11. +1 (NH₄⁺); nitrogen has donated its lone pair.
12. The specification states they are not required; radical steps use single unpaired electrons shown with dots and balanced equations.
