AQA A-Level Psychology (7182) studies how people think, feel and
behave, and how psychologists investigate those questions
scientifically. This overview follows the updated specification for
first teaching from September 2025, which AQA lists with first AS
exams in 2026 and first A-level exams in 2027. The A-level is assessed
by three written papers, each lasting 2 hours and worth 96 marks, for
288 marks in total.

## How the course is organised

The content is arranged in three blocks, one for each paper.

**Introductory topics in Psychology.** Four compulsory topics that
introduce major areas of the subject: social influence, memory,
attachment, and clinical psychology and mental health.

**Psychology in context.** Three compulsory topics that give the
course its scientific and theoretical backbone: approaches in
psychology, biopsychology and research methods.

**Issues and options in Psychology.** One compulsory topic, issues and
debates in psychology, plus three options, one chosen from each of
three groups:

| Option group | Choose one of |
|---|---|
| Group 1 | Relationships, Gender, Cognition and development |
| Group 2 | Schizophrenia, Eating behaviour, Stress |
| Group 3 | Aggression, Forensic Psychology, Addiction |

Your centre normally decides which option from each group it teaches,
so confirm your three options early in the course.

## How it's assessed

The qualification is linear and not tiered, so all three papers are
taken at the end of the A-level course. Every section of every paper
mixes multiple-choice, short-answer and extended-writing questions.

| Paper | Content | Sections | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7182/1 | Paper 1: Introductory Topics in Psychology | Four sections of 24 marks | 2 hours | 96 | 33.33% |
| 7182/2 | Paper 2: Psychology in Context | Three sections of 24, 24 and 48 marks | 2 hours | 96 | 33.33% |
| 7182/3 | Paper 3: Issues and Options in Psychology | Four sections of 24 marks | 2 hours | 96 | 33.34% |

Each paper is worth a third of the A-level; the third is recorded as
33.34% only so the figures total exactly 100. Paper 3 assesses the
compulsory issues and debates content together with the three options
you have studied.

If you started the course before September 2025, check with your
teacher which version of the specification you are following, because
this overview is written against the updated version only.

## How the parts fit together

Paper 2 is the hub of the course. Research methods and approaches in
psychology are examined there as topics in their own right, but they
are used everywhere else. Every study you meet in social influence,
memory or attachment was carried out with a particular method, and
evaluating that study means judging its method. Every explanation you
learn, whether in social influence, schizophrenia or aggression,
comes from one or more of the approaches studied on Paper 2. Treating
research methods and approaches as tools rather than as a separate
block makes the rest of the course easier to evaluate.

Biopsychology works in a similar way. The biological explanations
studied there return in later topics such as stress, aggression,
schizophrenia and addiction, depending on your options.

Issues and debates in psychology sits on Paper 3, but it draws on
everything. The debates it covers can be illustrated from any topic
you have studied, compulsory or optional, so keeping a running list of
examples from each topic as you go is a simple way to prepare for it
without leaving it all to the second year.

## Planning your study

1. **Start with approaches and research methods.** A first look at the
   main approaches and the basic methods gives you the language to
   describe and evaluate studies from the outset.
2. **Teach the introductory topics.** Social influence, memory,
   attachment, and clinical psychology and mental health are usually
   covered in the first year, with research methods revisited through
   each one.
3. **Add biopsychology.** It links naturally to clinical psychology and
   to several of the later options.
4. **Move to issues and debates and the options.** These typically fill
   the second year. Issues and debates works well alongside the
   options, because each option provides fresh examples.
5. **Return to research methods regularly.** Short methods questions
   every week keep calculation, design and evaluation skills sharp.

Practise short and extended answers on each topic as soon as it is
taught. Full papers and the paper-by-paper exam preparation page
belong in the final months, when all compulsory content and your three
options are complete.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in AQA A-Level Psychology?**
There are three written papers. Each lasts 2 hours, is worth 96 marks and carries a third of the A-level, giving 288 marks in total.

**How many options do I study for Paper 3?**
Three: one from each of three groups. Group 1 is Relationships, Gender or Cognition and development; Group 2 is Schizophrenia, Eating behaviour or Stress; Group 3 is Aggression, Forensic Psychology or Addiction.

**Which topics are compulsory in AQA A-Level Psychology 7182?**
Social influence, memory, attachment, clinical psychology and mental health, approaches in psychology, biopsychology, research methods, and issues and debates in psychology are all compulsory.

**When is this version of 7182 first examined?**
For the specification taught from September 2025, AQA lists first AS exams in 2026 and first A-level exams in 2027.

**What question types appear in the 7182 papers?**
Every section mixes multiple-choice, short-answer and extended-writing questions. Papers 1 and 3 have four 24-mark sections, and Paper 2 has sections of 24, 24 and 48 marks.
