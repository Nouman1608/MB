## Section A: short answers

**1.** Explain why the Belgian state took over the Congo Free State in 1908. **[3]**

**2.** Describe the purpose and effect of identity cards in Belgian Rwanda. **[3]**

**3.** Identify two reasons why Katanga was important in the Congo Crisis. **[2]**

## Section C: essay questions

**7.** Evaluate the claim that Mobutu's rule was damaging to Zaire mainly because of his personal corruption. **[15]**

**8.** To what extent was control of minerals the main reason for the Second Congo War? **[15]**

## Answers

**1.** One mark for the cause: international exposure of abuses (rubber quotas, hostage taking, mutilation) by Morel, Casement and others. One for the political pressure: the Congo Reform Association, press and parliaments, and Leopold's loss of credit. One for the result: Belgium annexed the Congo in 1908 as a colony with a colonial charter, partly to protect its own reputation and interests. **[3]**

**2.** One mark for purpose: the Belgian authorities recorded each person as Hutu, Tutsi or Twa, in census work in the early 1930s, to organise administration and preference in jobs and chiefships. One for the effect: a flexible social difference became a fixed legal category. One for the consequence: the cards later made it possible to identify victims in 1994 and entrenched ethnic politics. **[3]**

**3.** One mark each for two of: its copper wealth through the Union Minière; its leader Moïse Tshombe seceded in July 1960 with Belgian support; the secession led to UN involvement and the Lumumba affair; the region was key for the Cold War backing of Mobutu. **[2]**

**4.** One mark for the claim that killing depended on fear rather than only prior hatred. One for the role of media and authority figures (radio and local officials) in shaping behaviour. One for the implication that the genocide was organised and encouraged from above. **[3]**

**5.** One mark for a challenge, up to two for development. For example: the extract plays down ideology and long-term discrimination, such as the 1959 to 1964 violence and the racial ideas spread under colonialism; or it overlooks the role of local gain, such as land and property; or it should be balanced against evidence of rescue and resistance by Hutu who refused to kill. **[3]**

**6.** Up to 6 marks. Credit reasons: the UN mandate was limited (UNAMIR) and force was reduced after Belgian peacekeepers were killed on 7 April; major powers had little strategic interest after Somalia in 1993; the Security Council debated language and avoided the word genocide; poor information and the civil war context; France's support for the Rwandan government. Credit counterpoints: warnings had been sent to the UN (Dallaire's cables); there was enough capacity for a small force to save lives. Level guide: 1 to 2 for description, 3 to 4 for partial examination, 5 to 6 for a balanced examination with a conclusion. **[6]**

**7.** Marking points, up to 15 in total:
- up to 3 for understanding the claim and choosing criteria for "damaging";
- up to 4 for evidence of personal corruption (plunder of the state, accounts abroad, reliance on patronage, the 1970s nationalisations given to allies);
- up to 4 for other factors (the colonial legacy of weak institutions, the Cold War support of the United States and Belgium, falling copper prices, debt and instability in the east);
- up to 4 for evaluation of perspectives and a judgement, for example that corruption was central to how the system worked but not the only cause. **[15]**

**8.** Marking points, up to 15 in total:
- up to 3 for understanding the claim and the competing explanations;
- up to 4 for evidence for minerals (coltan, diamonds, gold, copper; the UN panel of experts on illegal exploitation; Rwandan and Ugandan control of mining areas; companies and foreign governments);
- up to 4 for other causes (Kabila's break with his allies in 1998, the security threat from Hutu militias after 1994, regional rivalries, weakness of Zaire's state, ethnic politics in the east);
- up to 4 for evaluation and judgement, for example distinguishing the causes of the outbreak in 1998 from the reasons the war continued. **[15]**

## Model paragraph and feedback

Here is a model paragraph for Question 8, followed by points on how to improve a draft.

*Model paragraph:* "The Second Congo War has often been described as a war for minerals. The UN panel of experts documented how armies and companies profited from coltan, diamonds and gold, and this helps to explain why the fighting continued after the Lusaka ceasefire of 1999. However, the war began in 1998 when Laurent Kabila turned on his Rwandan allies, and Rwanda justified its intervention by the danger from armed groups left over from the genocide. This essay argues that security fears and political rivalry caused the war's outbreak, while minerals turned it into a long conflict with many actors."

*Feedback points:*

- The paragraph separates the cause of an outbreak from the reasons for continuation. This is a strong move in a cause question.
- Name sources of evidence, such as the UN panel, rather than saying "historians say".
- Make clear which groups are being discussed: governments, militias, companies.
- Avoid emotional or vague language about "African wars"; use dates, places and actors.
