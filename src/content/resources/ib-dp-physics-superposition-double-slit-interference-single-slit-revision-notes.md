## Key definitions

- **Superposition:** where waves or pulses meet, the resultant displacement is the sum of the individual displacements (signs included).
- **Coherent sources:** same frequency and a constant phase difference.
- **Path difference:** difference in distance from the two sources to a point.
- **Fringe separation s:** distance between adjacent bright (or adjacent dark) fringes.
- **Order n:** the whole number counting maxima outward from the central one (n = 0).

## Formulas

| Result | Equation | Level |
|---|---|---|
| Constructive (sources in phase) | path difference = nλ | SL and HL |
| Destructive (sources in phase) | path difference = (n + ½)λ | SL and HL |
| Young's double slit | s = λD / d | SL and HL |
| First single-slit minimum | θ = λ / b | HL only |
| Grating maxima | nλ = d sin θ | HL only |

Symbols: d = slit separation, D = slits to screen, b = slit width, θ in radians for θ = λ/b.

## Method boxes

**Interference at a point**
1. Find both distances to the point; subtract to get the path difference.
2. Divide by λ.
3. Whole number: constructive. Whole number plus a half: destructive. Anything else: in between.

**Young's fringes**
1. Convert units to metres (mm and nm).
2. Use s = λD/d, or rearrange for λ, D or d.
3. For several fringes, divide the measured total by the number of spacings first.

**Single slit**
1. θ = λ/b gives the angle from the axis to the first minimum.
2. Central maximum full width on a screen = 2Dθ.
3. Narrower slit: wider, dimmer pattern.

**Slit modulation**
1. Fringes from d sit inside an envelope from b.
2. Missing order = d/b, when this is a whole number.
3. Bright fringes inside the central envelope = 2(d/b) − 1.

**Grating**
1. d = 1/(lines per metre).
2. sin θ = nλ/d. If this exceeds 1, that order does not exist.
3. Maximum order is the largest whole n below d/λ.
4. Total maxima = 2 × (maximum order) + 1.

## Superposition reminders

- Add displacements with their signs; a pulse is not changed by passing through another.
- Constructive: displacements add to a larger resultant. Destructive: they cancel partly or fully.
- For equal-amplitude waves in phase the resultant amplitude is double, so the intensity is four times that of one wave (intensity depends on amplitude squared).
- Energy is not lost at a minimum: it is redistributed to the maxima.
- Use the words "in phase" and "in antiphase" when you explain a point, and say how the path difference produces them.
- Young's experiment supports the wave model of light, because particles in straight lines would not give alternating bright and dark bands.

## Must-know distinctions

| Idea | Two slits | Grating (many slits) |
|---|---|---|
| Maxima | Wide, fairly gentle | Narrow, sharp, bright |
| Main use | Show wave nature, measure λ roughly | Measure λ accurately, produce spectra |
| Equation | s = λD/d (small angles) | nλ = d sin θ |

| Idea | Double slit | Single slit |
|---|---|---|
| Key length | d (separation) | b (width) |
| Pattern | Equal-spaced fringes | Wide centre, weaker sides |
| Making it smaller | Smaller d: wider fringes | Smaller b: wider, dimmer pattern |

White light: a white central maximum, then spectra with violet nearest the centre and red furthest. Higher orders overlap.

## Worked reminders

**Fringes.** λ = 589 nm, d = 0.45 mm, D = 1.35 m: s = (589 × 10⁻⁹ × 1.35) / (0.45 × 10⁻³) = 1.77 × 10⁻³ m.

**Single slit.** λ = 500 nm, b = 0.12 mm, D = 2.0 m: first minimum at distance Dλ/b = 8.33 mm from the centre, so the central maximum is 16.7 mm wide.

**Grating.** 700 lines per mm gives d = 1.43 × 10⁻⁶ m. A second-order maximum at 40.0° gives λ = d sin θ / 2 = 459 nm.

## Sketches and descriptions to be able to give

**Double-slit pattern (ideal, narrow slits).** Equally spaced bright fringes of equal brightness, with the central one on the axis. Spacing s = λD/d.

**Single-slit pattern (HL).** Intensity against position: a tall central peak, with minima at ±λ/b, then much lower secondary peaks, each about half as wide as the central one. A wider slit squeezes the pattern inward and raises the peak.

**Double slit with finite slit width (HL).** The double-slit fringes under a single-slit envelope. The fringe heights follow the envelope curve; fringes vanish where the envelope reaches zero.

**Grating with monochromatic light (HL).** Thin, bright lines at θ = 0 and at each allowed order, with the lines further apart than for a coarser grating. A finer grating (more lines per mm) sends each order to a larger angle.

**Grating with white light (HL).** White centre; each order is a rainbow band. Violet is on the side nearer the centre.

## Model sentences for explain questions

- "The sources are coherent because they are two parts of one wavefront, so the phase difference stays constant."
- "At P the path difference is a whole number of wavelengths, so they arrive in phase, the displacements add and a maximum results."
- "At P the path difference is an odd number of half wavelengths, so they arrive in antiphase and the displacements cancel."
- "A narrower slit diffracts the light through a larger angle, so the central maximum is wider; less light passes through, so it is dimmer."
- "The grating has many slits, so the maxima are narrower and brighter and the angles can be measured more precisely."
- "Violet has the shortest wavelength, so sin θ is smallest and violet appears closest to the centre."

## Units and checks

- Convert first: 1 mm = 10⁻³ m, 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m, 1 μm = 10⁻⁶ m.
- Lines per mm to spacing: d = 10⁻³ / N metres.
- Small angles in radians: θ = λ/b only works when θ is small.
- Check that s, θ and the number of orders are sensible: a fringe separation of metres means a unit slip.

## Quick self-test

1. Two pulses of +6.0 cm and −2.5 cm coincide. What is the resultant displacement?
2. State two conditions for two sources to be coherent.
3. Sources in phase, λ = 0.40 m. A point is 3.2 m from one and 2.4 m from the other. What type of interference occurs?
4. Why do two separate torches not give fringes on a screen?
5. Find s for λ = 589 nm, d = 0.45 mm, D = 1.35 m, and state what happens to s if D is halved and d is doubled.
6. (HL) Green light is diffracted by a slit of width 0.12 mm. What happens to the central maximum if the slit is narrowed?
7. (HL) Find the width of the central maximum for λ = 500 nm, b = 0.12 mm, D = 2.0 m.
8. (HL) A double slit has d = 0.45 mm and b = 0.15 mm. Which order is missing, and how many bright fringes lie in the central envelope?
9. (HL) A grating has slit spacing 2.00 μm. For λ = 550 nm, find the angle of the first order and the highest order.
10. (HL) How many lines per millimetre give a first-order maximum at 20.0° for λ = 480 nm?
11. (HL) White light meets a grating. Describe the central maximum and the first-order maximum.

### Answers

1. +6.0 + (−2.5) = **+3.5 cm**.
2. Same frequency (wavelength) and a **constant phase difference**.
3. Path difference = 0.8 m = 2λ: **constructive**.
4. Each torch emits light with random, rapidly changing phase, so the phase difference between them is not constant. The pattern averages out.
5. s = **1.77 mm**. With D halved and d doubled, s falls to a quarter: **0.442 mm**.
6. It becomes **wider** and **less intense**.
7. 2Dλ/b = 2 × 2.0 × 500 × 10⁻⁹ / 0.12 × 10⁻³ = **16.7 mm** (1.67 cm).
8. d/b = 3: the **third order** is missing; 2(3) − 1 = **5** bright fringes.
9. sin θ = 550 × 10⁻⁹ / 2.00 × 10⁻⁶ = 0.275, θ = **16.0°**. d/λ = 3.64, so the highest order is **3**.
10. d = 480 × 10⁻⁹ / sin 20.0° = 1.40 × 10⁻⁶ m, giving **713 lines per mm** (to 3 s.f.).
11. The central maximum is **white**. The first order is a **spectrum**, violet nearest the centre and red furthest.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using nλ for dark fringes and (n + ½)λ for bright ones.
- Forgetting to change mm and nm to metres.
- Mixing up slit width b and separation d.
- Giving the half-width of the central maximum as its full width.
- Writing "narrower slit, narrower pattern".
- Saying the grating equation applies without checking that sin θ is at most 1.
- Writing "bright fringes get brighter" without linking it to the envelope.
- Calling the white central maximum coloured, or putting red nearest the centre.
- Explaining coherence without constant phase difference.
