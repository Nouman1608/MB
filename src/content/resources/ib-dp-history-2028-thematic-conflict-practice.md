These tasks follow the paper 2 structure described in the guide for the thematic study of conflict: a concept question (6 marks), a short response (4 marks) and an essay (15 marks). The study prescribes no examples, so answers below are suggestions. Any accurate and relevant examples from your own course earn credit.

## Section A: concepts

**1.** Using one example from your thematic study, analyse the concept of cause and consequence. **[6]**

**2.** Using one example from your thematic study, analyse the concept of perspectives. **[6]**

## Section B: short responses

**3.** Explain how technology affected the outcome of one conflict you have studied. **[4]**

**4.** Explain the experiences of women in one conflict you have studied. **[4]**

## Section B: essays

**5.** To what extent was leadership the main factor in determining the outcome of conflicts? Use examples from at least two regions. **[15]**

**6.** Evaluate the importance of economic factors in the emergence of conflicts. Use examples from at least two regions. **[15]**

## Answers

**1.** Marking points (6). Credit:
- a brief definition: the interplay of multiple reasons for, and results of, events, including actors and conditions **[1]**;
- an accurate, specific example, such as the American Civil War, with dates **[1]**;
- analysis of causes: for example, conditions (the slave economy, disputes over slavery in new territories) and actors (Lincoln's election, secession by Southern states) **[1 to 2]**;
- analysis of consequences: for example, the end of slavery, the destruction of Southern infrastructure, the amendments of Reconstruction, and the limits of Reconstruction **[1 to 2]**;
- a judgement that the war was not inevitable, or that long-term causes and short-term triggers interacted **[1]**.
Bands: 1 to 2 describes the concept with minimal analysis; 3 to 4 partial analysis with a relevant example; 5 to 6 clear and accurate analysis effectively supported by a specific example.

**2.** Marking points (6). Credit:
- a definition: diverse points of view from and about the past, not all equally valid **[1]**;
- a specific example, for example the Algerian War **[1]**;
- identification of different perspectives, such as the FLN, French settlers, the French army, Algerian women and the harkis, with the position of each **[1 to 2]**;
- analysis of validity, corroboration or contradiction, for example comparing accounts of the Battle of Algiers **[1 to 2]**;
- a short judgement about which perspectives are best supported or what each reveals **[1]**.
Use the same bands as Question 1.

**3.** Marking points (4). Award:
- identification of a relevant example **[1]**;
- accurate description, for example the telegraph and railways in the American Civil War, or radar in the Second World War **[1]**;
- explanation of the effect on the outcome, for example railways moved Union troops and supplies, which strengthened the blockade and invasion strategy **[1]**;
- a developed explanation, linking technology to who used it and how, for example that the North's industry produced rails and rifles in greater number **[1]**.

**4.** Marking points (4). Award:
- identification of a relevant conflict **[1]**;
- description of women's roles, for example in factories, as nurses, as couriers or fighters **[1]**;
- explanation of experiences, for example the wartime employment of women in Britain and the United States in the Second World War, with long hours and new independence, or the risk faced by FLN couriers **[1]**;
- explanation of change or limits, for example that many were pushed back into domestic roles after 1945, or that postwar recognition was uneven **[1]**.

**5.** Essay plan with marking points. A 13 to 15 answer will:
- state a clear thesis, for example that leadership mattered most when it shaped strategy and mobilisation, but was constrained by resources **[2]**;
- use two or more examples from at least two regions, such as Lincoln and Grant (Americas) and Mao (Asia), with accurate detail **[3]**;
- analyse leadership against other factors (resources, strategy, technology), with specific evidence such as Union industrial advantage or Mao's rural support **[4]**;
- make explicit connections: similarity (leaders who adapted strategy), difference (democratic against revolutionary leadership), and an exception such as Algeria, where the political cost to France mattered more than any single leader **[4]**;
- reach a reasoned judgement that is consistent with the analysis **[2]**.
Lower bands: 4 to 6 is descriptive, with minimal connections; 7 to 9 mostly descriptive with partial analysis; 10 to 12 mostly analytical with some connections.

**6.** Essay plan with marking points. A 13 to 15 answer will:
- state a clear thesis, for example that economic factors created conditions for conflict but political decisions turned them into war **[2]**;
- use two or more examples from at least two regions: for example the slave-based economy and the American Civil War (Americas), and Japanese resource needs before the Pacific War (Asia) **[3]**;
- analyse economic factors with specific evidence such as tariffs and slavery's expansion, or the effect of the Depression on Japanese policy **[4]**;
- consider other factors and connect examples: political choices (secession; military influence in Japan), ideology, and a case where economic motives were less important, such as religious motives in the Crusades **[4]**;
- reach a reasoned judgement **[2]**.

## Frequently asked questions

**Must I use the examples suggested in the answers?** No. Any accurate, relevant examples from at least two regions can earn full credit.

**How long should an essay be?** There is no set length. In the time available, a clear argument with developed paragraphs is better than a long narrative.
