## The three things 8.2 tests

- **Define** activation energy in the syllabus wording.
- **Sketch and use** the Boltzmann distribution to show why Eₐ matters.
- **Explain** the effect of temperature on rate using **both** the Boltzmann distribution **and** the frequency of effective collisions.

## Activation energy in one box

**Eₐ = the minimum energy required for a collision to be effective.**

- It is a fixed property of a given reaction pathway. Changing temperature does not change it.
- On a reaction pathway diagram, Eₐ is the vertical gap from the **reactants** up to the **peak**.
- Exothermic: reactants above products. Endothermic: products above reactants, so Eₐ ≥ ΔH.
- Reverse barrier: **Eₐ(reverse) = Eₐ(forward) − ΔH**. For ΔH = −40 kJ mol⁻¹ and Eₐ(forward) = +70 kJ mol⁻¹ (invented), Eₐ(reverse) = +110 kJ mol⁻¹.

## Boltzmann distribution checklist

When you sketch the curve, tick off each feature:

- Axes labelled **number of molecules** (vertical) and **energy** (horizontal).
- Starts at the **origin**.
- One peak, then a long tail. The curve is **not symmetrical**.
- The tail **never meets** the energy axis.
- Eₐ drawn **well to the right** of the peak.
- Area beyond Eₐ shaded: these are the molecules that **can react on collision**.
- Total area = total number of molecules.

## Two temperatures on one diagram

| Feature | Higher temperature, T₂ |
|---|---|
| Peak position | Moves **right** (to higher energy) |
| Peak height | **Lower** |
| Width | **Broader** |
| Total area | **Unchanged** (same number of molecules) |
| Crossing point | The curves cross once, before Eₐ |
| Tail beyond Eₐ | T₂ curve lies **above** the T₁ curve |
| Eₐ line | **Does not move** |
| Area beyond Eₐ | **Much larger** |

## The temperature explanation in order

1. Higher temperature → higher average kinetic energy.
2. Distribution shifts right → **greater proportion of molecules with E ≥ Eₐ**.
3. Molecules move faster → **more frequent collisions**.
4. Both increase the **frequency of effective collisions** → rate increases.
5. Point 2 is the **main** reason. Point 3 is a minor contribution.

A 10 K rise near room temperature increases collision frequency by under 2%, while the fraction of molecules with E ≥ Eₐ can easily double.

## Rates from timing practicals

- Time t to reach a fixed point (for example, a cross disappearing under a sulfur precipitate): **rate ∝ 1/t**.
- Sodium thiosulfate with hydrochloric acid: Na₂S₂O₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + S + SO₂ + H₂O.
- Control: volumes, concentrations, flask, cross, and the observer. Bring each solution to temperature **before** mixing. Record the temperature of the reaction mixture.
- Plot of 1/t against temperature: **curves upwards**, because each equal temperature step gives a larger increase in rate.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Activation energy, Eₐ | Minimum energy required for a collision to be effective |
| Effective collision | A collision with energy ≥ Eₐ (and suitable orientation) that leads to reaction |
| Boltzmann distribution | The spread of molecular kinetic energies in a sample at a given temperature |
| Most probable energy | The energy at the peak of the curve |
| Area beyond Eₐ | Number of molecules with enough energy to react on collision |
| Reaction pathway diagram | Enthalpy plotted against progress of reaction, showing ΔH and Eₐ |
| Frequency of effective collisions | Number of effective collisions per unit time; controls the rate |

## Exam traps

- Writing "energy needed to start a reaction" for Eₐ.
- Saying that heating "lowers the activation energy".
- Labelling the vertical axis "energy" and the horizontal axis "number of molecules" (the wrong way round).
- Letting the higher-temperature curve have a higher peak, or a bigger total area.
- Drawing the higher-temperature curve **below** the original in the tail.
- Answering a temperature question with collision frequency only.
- Measuring Eₐ downwards from the peak to the products.
- Using t, not 1/t, as the measure of rate.

## Self-test

1. Give the 9701 definition of activation energy.
2. What does the total area under a Boltzmann curve represent?
3. Why must the Boltzmann curve start at the origin?
4. Why must the tail never touch the energy axis?
5. State two ways the curve changes at a higher temperature.
6. Why is the total area unchanged at a higher temperature?
7. Does Eₐ change when the temperature rises? Explain.
8. Which is the more important effect of a temperature rise: more frequent collisions, or more molecules with E ≥ Eₐ?
9. A reaction has Eₐ(forward) = +90 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔH = +35 kJ mol⁻¹. Find Eₐ(reverse).
10. A cross disappears after 50 s at 25 °C and 26 s at 35 °C. Calculate 1/t for each and the ratio of the rates.
11. Why are the two solutions warmed separately before mixing in a temperature practical?

**Answers:**

1. The minimum energy required for a collision to be effective.
2. The total number of molecules in the sample.
3. No molecule has zero energy, so the number of molecules with zero energy is zero.
4. There is no maximum energy: a very small number of molecules always have very high energies.
5. Any two of: the peak moves to higher energy; the peak is lower; the curve is broader; the area beyond Eₐ is larger.
6. The number of molecules has not changed.
7. No. Eₐ is a property of the reaction pathway. Temperature changes how many molecules have energy ≥ Eₐ, not Eₐ itself.
8. More molecules with E ≥ Eₐ. The collision-frequency increase is small.
9. Eₐ(reverse) = 90 − 35 = **+55 kJ mol⁻¹**.
10. 1/50 = 0.020 s⁻¹ and 1/26 = 0.038 s⁻¹. Ratio = 0.038 ÷ 0.020 ≈ **1.9**: the rate nearly doubles.
11. So that the reaction runs at the stated temperature from the start. Mixing a warm solution with a cold one would give a lower, unknown temperature.
