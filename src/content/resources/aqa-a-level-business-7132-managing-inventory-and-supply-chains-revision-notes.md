Condensed notes on section **3.4.5 Making operational decisions to improve performance: managing inventory and supply chains** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). The full explanations and the bakery inventory chart example are in the [inventory and supply chains study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-managing-inventory-and-supply-chains/); exam-style questions are in the [inventory and supply chains practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-managing-inventory-and-supply-chains-practice/).

## Specification content

- How to manage supply to match demand and the value of doing so: outsourcing, temporary and part-time employees, producing to order.
- Influences on the amount of inventory held; inventory control charts, lead time, re-order levels, buffer inventory, re-order quantities.
- Influences on the choice of suppliers.
- How to manage the supply chain effectively and efficiently and the value of this.
- The value of outsourcing.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Inventory | Stock of materials, work in progress and finished goods |
| Lead time | Time between placing an order and its delivery |
| Buffer inventory | Minimum stock held as a safety margin |
| Re-order level | Stock level at which a new order is placed |
| Re-order quantity | Amount ordered each time |
| Stock-out | Running out of stock |
| Supply chain | All the organisations and activities from raw materials to final customer |
| Outsourcing | Paying another firm to carry out an activity |
| Producing to order | Making goods only after an order is received |

## Matching supply to demand

| Method | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Outsourcing | Extra capacity without investment; specialist skills; variable costs | Less control; supplier may fail |
| Temporary and part-time staff | Labour cost follows demand; quick to hire | Less trained and loyal; training cost |
| Producing to order | No unsold stock; customisation | Customers wait; peaks may overload capacity |
| Building inventory before peaks | Smooth production; goods ready | Storage and finance cost; obsolescence |
| Overtime and off-peak pricing | Flexible; moves demand | Overtime costly; discounts cut margins |

**Value of matching:** less waste, better customer service, lower costs, better reputation.

## Influences on inventory held

Demand and its predictability; lead time; supplier reliability; cost of holding stock; perishability; cost of a stock-out; bulk discounts; JIT or JIC approach; cash available.

## Inventory control chart: key formulas

- **Re-order level = (daily usage × lead time) + buffer**
- **Maximum inventory = buffer + re-order quantity**
- Average inventory is roughly **re-order quantity ÷ 2 + buffer**
- Chart shape: stock falls steadily, an order is placed at the re-order level, stock jumps up on delivery.

**Worked example.** Usage 100 kg a day; lead time 4 days; buffer 200 kg; re-order quantity 800 kg.

- Re-order level = 400 + 200 = **600 kg**.
- Maximum = 200 + 800 = **1,000 kg**.
- Cycle: 1,000 falls to 200 over 8 days (order placed on day 4, delivery on day 8).
- Demand rises to 130 a day: usage in lead time 520; stock at delivery 80 kg; new re-order level 720 kg.
- Lead time rises to 6 days: usage 600; stock at delivery 0 kg, so stock-out risk.

**Order size trade-off** (annual use 25,000 kg, £60 per order, £3 holding cost per kg a year):

- 800 kg orders: £1,875 ordering + £1,800 holding = **£3,675**.
- 1,600 kg orders: £937.50 + £3,000 = **£3,937.50**.
- Larger orders: fewer orders, higher holding cost.

**Limits of charts:** assume steady use and fixed lead time; say nothing of quality; software updating continuously is more accurate.

## Choosing suppliers

Price and credit terms; quality and consistency; reliability and lead time; flexibility; location; reputation and financial stability; ethical and environmental standards; relationship and data links; single supplier (discounts, dependence) against several (less risk, smaller discounts).

## Managing the supply chain

- Share information (forecasts, stock levels, IT links).
- Build long-term relationships; agree standards.
- Monitor performance; audit suppliers.
- Reduce lead times and waste.
- Manage risk with backup suppliers.
- Use technology for tracking and ordering.

**Value:** lower costs, fewer stock-outs, better quality, quicker response, protected reputation. **Difficulties:** long, complex chains; one failure can stop production; ethical checks beyond first supplier; lean chains have little slack.

## Outsourcing

**Benefits:** lower cost through supplier's scale; expertise and technology; flexibility; focus on core strengths; no capital investment. **Drawbacks:** less control of quality and timing; dependence; confidentiality; contract management costs; morale and jobs; reputation risk from supplier behaviour. Best for non-core activities or specialist skills.

## Exam traps

- Re-order level includes the **buffer**.
- Buffer is a **minimum**; maximum is buffer plus re-order quantity.
- Outsourcing is not automatically cheaper.
- Do not choose suppliers on price alone.
- Holding less stock is not always better: weigh the cost of a stock-out.

## Self-test

1. Define "lead time".
2. Define "buffer inventory".
3. A firm uses 50 units a day, the lead time is 6 days and the buffer is 100 units. Calculate the re-order level.
4. Its re-order quantity is 500 units. Calculate the maximum inventory.
5. Give two influences on the amount of inventory a business holds.
6. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of using temporary staff.
7. Give one advantage of producing to order.
8. Give two criteria for choosing a supplier.
9. Give two benefits of outsourcing.
10. Give two drawbacks of outsourcing.
11. Why might larger re-order quantities increase total costs?
12. If lead time increased from 6 to 8 days in question 3, what would the new re-order level be?

### Answers

1. The time between placing an order and receiving it.
2. The minimum stock held as a safety margin against late delivery or unexpected demand.
3. (50 × 6) + 100 = **400 units**.
4. 100 + 500 = **600 units**.
5. Any two: demand; lead time; supplier reliability; holding costs; perishability; cost of a stock-out; cash available.
6. Advantage (any one): labour cost follows demand; quick to hire. Disadvantage (any one): less trained or loyal; recruitment and training costs.
7. Any one: no unsold stock; low inventory costs; products can be customised.
8. Any two: price; quality; reliability; location; flexibility; ethical standards; financial stability.
9. Any two: lower costs; access to expertise; flexibility; focus on core activities; no capital investment.
10. Any two: less control; dependence on supplier; confidentiality risk; hidden contract costs; reputation risk.
11. Average inventory is higher, so storage, insurance and finance costs rise by more than the savings on ordering costs.
12. (50 × 8) + 100 = **500 units**.
