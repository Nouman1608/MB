**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **AS Level** subtopic 2.2: define and use the term mole in terms of the Avogadro constant.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mole (mol) | Amount of substance containing 6.022 × 10²³ specified particles |
| Avogadro constant, L | 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹, the number of particles per mole |
| Amount of substance, n | Quantity measured in moles |
| Molar mass, M | Mass of one mole of a substance, in g mol⁻¹ |
| Particle | The entity being counted: atom, molecule, ion, formula unit or electron |
| Formula unit | The simplest group of ions shown by an ionic formula, e.g. one NaCl |

## Core relationships

    n = m ÷ M          (mol = g ÷ g mol⁻¹)
    N = n × L          (particles = mol × mol⁻¹)
    mass of one particle = M ÷ L

- M in g mol⁻¹ equals the relative mass numerically: 1 g contains 1 ÷ (1.66 × 10⁻²⁴) = 6.022 × 10²³ u.
- Relative mass: no units. Molar mass: g mol⁻¹. L: mol⁻¹.

## Counting different particles

| Sample | Amount of formula/molecule | Particles counted | Multiply by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 mol H₂O | 1 mol molecules | atoms | 3 |
| 1 mol H₂O | 1 mol molecules | H atoms | 2 |
| 1 mol CaCl₂ | 1 mol formula units | ions | 3 |
| 1 mol Al₂O₃ | 1 mol formula units | Al³⁺ ions | 2 |
| 1 mol NH₃ | 1 mol molecules | electrons | 10 |
| 1 mol O₂ | 1 mol molecules | O atoms | 2 |

**Method: mass to number of particles**
1. n = m ÷ M (moles of formula units or molecules).
2. Multiply by the number of the required particle in one formula.
3. Multiply by L.

**Method: number of particles to mass**
1. n = N ÷ L.
2. Convert to moles of formula units if needed.
3. m = n × M.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Molecules in 9.00 g H₂O | 3.01 × 10²³ |
| Total ions in 11.1 g CaCl₂ | 1.80 × 10²³ |
| Mass of one Fe atom | 9.27 × 10⁻²³ g |
| Mass of 1.20 × 10²⁴ O₂ molecules | 63.8 g |
| Electrons in 3.40 g NH₃ | 1.20 × 10²⁴ |

## Equal masses are not equal numbers

- 1.00 g He = 0.250 mol atoms; 1.00 g H₂ = 0.500 mol molecules = 1.00 mol atoms.
- 10.0 g CH₄ (0.625 mol) has twice as many molecules as 10.0 g O₂ (0.313 mol).
- Lighter particles: more of them per gram.

## Where the number comes from

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| 1 u | 1.66 × 10⁻²⁴ g |
| u in 1 g | 1 ÷ 1.66 × 10⁻²⁴ = 6.022 × 10²³ |
| Mass of L carbon-12 atoms | 12 g |
| Mass of L particles of relative mass X | X g |

So L is the scaling factor between the atomic scale (u) and the laboratory scale (g), and molar mass in g mol⁻¹ equals relative mass.

## Significant figures

- Match the least precise data (or one more).
- Keep full calculator values between steps.
- Sizes: grams of substance usually contain 10²² to 10²⁴ particles; one particle has a mass of about 10⁻²⁴ to 10⁻²¹ g.

## Exam traps

- Mole defined as a mass: it is an amount.
- "One mole of chlorine": say Cl atoms or Cl₂ molecules.
- Forgetting the multiplier for atoms or ions per formula.
- L written without mol⁻¹.
- Using M = 1.0 for hydrogen gas or 16.0 for oxygen gas (they are H₂ and O₂).
- Dividing by L when converting moles to particles.

## Self-test

**1.** Define the mole.

**2.** State the value and units of the Avogadro constant.

**3.** Why does one mole of carbon-12 atoms have a mass of 12 g?

**4.** Calculate the number of molecules in 4.50 g of glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆ (Mr 180.0).

**5.** How many atoms are in the sample in Question 4?

**6.** Calculate the total number of ions in 5.10 g of Al₂O₃ (relative formula mass 102.0).

**7.** Calculate the mass of one gold atom (Ar 197.0).

**8.** Find the mass of 3.0 × 10²² molecules of CO₂ (Mr 44.0).

**9.** How many nitrogen atoms are in 6.61 g of (NH₄)₂SO₄ (relative formula mass 132.1)?

**10.** How many protons are in 1.00 g of water?

**11.** Which contains more molecules, 10.0 g of CH₄ or 10.0 g of O₂? By what factor?

**12.** 8.02 g of sulfur exists as S₈ molecules (Mr 256.8). How many molecules and how many atoms are present?

## Self-test answers

**1.** The amount of substance that contains 6.022 × 10²³ particles (the Avogadro constant).

**2.** 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

**3.** Each atom has a mass of 12 u, and there are 6.022 × 10²³ u in one gram, so 6.022 × 10²³ atoms have a mass of 12 g.

**4.** 4.50 ÷ 180.0 = 0.0250 mol; × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.51 × 10²² molecules.

**5.** 24 atoms per molecule: 3.61 × 10²³ atoms.

**6.** 0.0500 mol; 5 ions per formula unit; 0.250 mol ions = 1.51 × 10²³ ions.

**7.** 197.0 ÷ 6.022 × 10²³ = 3.27 × 10⁻²² g.

**8.** 3.0 × 10²² ÷ 6.022 × 10²³ = 0.0498 mol; × 44.0 = 2.2 g.

**9.** 0.0500 mol; 2 N per formula unit; 0.100 mol N = 6.03 × 10²² atoms.

**10.** 1.00 ÷ 18.0 = 0.0556 mol; 10 protons per molecule; 3.35 × 10²³ protons.

**11.** CH₄ (0.625 mol against 0.313 mol), twice as many.

**12.** 0.0312 mol S₈ = 1.88 × 10²² molecules; × 8 = 1.50 × 10²³ atoms.
