All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Zesta Snacks.

> **Case study: Zesta Snacks**
>
> Zesta Snacks makes spicy corn snacks. Its home town has 30,000
> people. About 20% are aged 12 to 25, who are its target market, and
> 15% of these buy a $2.50 pack of snacks once a month. A survey of
> 500 existing customers found that 335 would like a version with less
> sugar. Zesta now wants to sell in a country abroad, where the market for
> snacks is worth $600,000 a year and where Zesta currently has sales of
> $45,000. To sell there, Zesta would have to translate its labels
> ($5,000) and create a new flavour ($7,000). It expects the changes to
> bring in 3,200 extra packs sold, with a profit of $4 on each pack.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an example of an international market? **[1]**
- A. Customers in the same town
- B. Customers across the home country
- C. Customers in another country
- D. Customers who are members of the business

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a customer need? **[1]**
- A. The rent of the factory
- B. A product that is safe and works properly
- C. The owner's profit
- D. The number of competitors

**3.** Define the term "target market". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** reasons why a business needs to identify customer needs. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** way in which customers in another country may differ from customers in a business's home market. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate the number of people in Zesta's target market in its home town. **[2]**

**7.** Calculate the number of customers who buy a pack every month, and the monthly sales revenue from them. **[3]**

**8.** Calculate the percentage of surveyed customers who want a version with less sugar. **[2]**

**9.** Calculate Zesta's current market share in the market abroad. **[2]**

**10.** Calculate the net gain to Zesta from adapting its product for the country abroad. **[3]**

**11.** Explain **two** ways in which Zesta could use the survey result to satisfy its customers. **[4]**

**12.** Evaluate whether Zesta should adapt its product to sell more in the country abroad. Use your answers to Questions 9 and 10. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The group of customers [1] that a business aims to sell its product or service to [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: to sell products that customers will buy [1]; to increase sales and revenue [1]; to build customer loyalty [1]; to stay ahead of competitors [1]; to avoid wasting resources [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Customers abroad may speak a different language [1], so labels and advertising must be translated or customers will not understand the product [1]. Accept differences in culture, religion, income or laws, each with a consequence [1]. **[2]**

**6.** 20% x 30,000 [1] = **6,000** people [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Customers = 15% x 6,000 = 900 [1]; revenue = 900 x $2.50 [1] = **$2,250** a month [1]. **[3]**

**8.** 335 / 500 x 100 [1] = **67%** [1]. **[2]**

**9.** $45,000 / $600,000 x 100 [1] = **7.5%** [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Cost of adapting = $5,000 + $7,000 = $12,000 [1]; extra profit = 3,200 x $4 = $12,800 [1]; net gain = $12,800 - $12,000 = **$800** [1]. **[3]**

**11.** **New product:** 67% want less sugar, so Zesta could introduce a lower-sugar version [1], which would meet the needs of most existing customers and keep them loyal [1].
**Promotion and range:** Zesta could advertise the new version to the customers who asked for it [1], so these customers would know that Zesta has listened and might buy more often [1]. Accept: keeping the original for the 33% who did not ask for change [1], so no customers are lost [1]. **[4]**

**12.** **For adapting:** Zesta has a small market share of 7.5%, so there is a lot of room to grow [1]; the adaptation gives a net gain of $800 in the first year [1]; later years do not need the $5,000 translation again, so the gain should rise [1]; the new flavour should meet the needs of customers in the country [1].
**Against:** the net gain of $800 is small compared with the $12,000 cost [1]; the forecast of 3,200 extra packs may be wrong, and a loss would follow if fewer than about 3,000 extra packs are sold [1]; Zesta needs to understand local customers' tastes and income [1].
**Judgement:** Zesta should adapt the product, provided that it checks the forecast with research, because the market is large and Zesta's share is small, so the long-term gain is likely to exceed $800 [1]. However, if research shows that local customers would not buy a sweeter or different flavour, Zesta should not adapt, because it could lose its $12,000 [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the whole town population of 30,000 instead of the target market of 6,000.
- Forgetting that revenue is customers x price, not customers x 15%.
- Dividing by the wrong total in the market share calculation.
- Not including both the translation and the flavour development in the cost of adapting.
- Making general comments about "customers" without using the case details.
