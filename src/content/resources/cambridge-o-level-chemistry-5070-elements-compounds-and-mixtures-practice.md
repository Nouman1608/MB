## Section A

**1.** Define the term *element*. **[1]**

**2.** Classify each of the following as an element, a compound or a mixture.

**(a)** Nitrogen gas, N₂ **[1]**

**(b)** Limewater (aqueous calcium hydroxide) **[1]**

**(c)** Calcium carbonate **[1]**

**(d)** Bronze, an alloy of copper and tin **[1]**

**3.** State **two** differences between the properties of a mixture of iron powder and copper powder and the properties of the compound copper(II) oxide. **[2]**

**4.** Four boxes of particles are described.

- **W:** identical molecules, each made of two identical atoms.
- **X:** identical molecules, each made of one sulfur atom and two oxygen atoms.
- **Y:** single argon atoms and single neon atoms, not joined.
- **Z:** some molecules each made of two hydrogen atoms, and some molecules each made of two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom.

Identify the box that shows:

**(a)** a compound **[1]**

**(b)** a mixture of two elements **[1]**

**(c)** a mixture of an element and a compound **[1]**

**5.** A student says: "Carbon dioxide and air both contain carbon and oxygen atoms, so they are both compounds." Explain what is wrong with this statement. **[2]**

**6.** Name a physical method that would separate the components of each mixture.

**(a)** Chalk powder and water **[1]**

**(b)** Pure water from seawater **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Hydrogen gas and oxygen gas can be mixed in a balloon without any change. When a flame is applied, the mixture explodes and water is formed.

**(a)** Explain why the gases in the balloon before the flame is applied are a mixture. **[2]**

**(b)** Give **two** pieces of evidence that water is a compound and not a mixture of hydrogen and oxygen. **[2]**

**8.** Compare the composition of a compound with the composition of a mixture. Use magnesium oxide and a mixture of magnesium powder and sulfur powder as examples. **[3]**

**9.** Two colourless liquids, A and B, are heated and their temperatures recorded.

- Liquid A boils steadily at 78 °C until it has all boiled away.
- Liquid B starts to boil at 82 °C. Its temperature rises slowly to 95 °C as it boils.

**(a)** Which liquid is a mixture? Explain your answer. **[2]**

**(b)** Liquid A could be an element or a compound. State what further information you would need to decide which. **[1]**

**10.** Sodium is a soft metal that reacts vigorously with water. Chlorine is a pale green, poisonous gas. Sodium chloride is a white solid that dissolves in water and is safe to eat in small amounts. Use this information to explain **one** difference between a compound and a mixture. **[2]**

**11.** A box contains six identical molecules. Each molecule has two carbon atoms bonded to six hydrogen atoms.

**(a)** Classify the contents of the box. **[1]**

**(b)** Two of the molecules are replaced by molecules each made of two oxygen atoms. Classify the new contents and explain your answer. **[2]**

**12.** Describe the differences between elements, compounds and mixtures. Refer to their composition, the bonding between atoms, and how they can be separated. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** A substance made of only one type of atom (which cannot be broken down into simpler substances by a chemical reaction) [1]. **[1]**

**2. (a)** Element [1]

**(b)** Mixture [1]

**(c)** Compound [1]

**(d)** Mixture [1]

**[4]**

**3.** Any two [1] [1]: in the mixture each metal keeps its own properties (copper colour, iron is magnetic), whereas copper(II) oxide has new properties (a black solid, not magnetic, not metallic); the mixture can be separated physically (e.g. iron removed with a magnet), whereas the compound needs a chemical reaction; the mixture can have any proportions, whereas copper(II) oxide has a fixed ratio of copper to oxygen. **[2]**

**4. (a)** X [1]

**(b)** Y [1]

**(c)** Z [1]

**[3]**

**5.** Carbon dioxide is a compound: its carbon and oxygen atoms are **chemically bonded** in a fixed ratio [1]. Air is a **mixture**: it contains different substances (nitrogen, oxygen, carbon dioxide and others) that are **not bonded** to each other and whose proportions can vary [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Filtration [1]

**(b)** (Simple) distillation [1]

**[2]**

**7. (a)** Hydrogen and oxygen molecules are present but **not chemically bonded** to each other [1]; they can be in **any proportions** / no reaction has happened, so each gas keeps its own properties [1]

**(b)** Any two [1] [1]: forming water gives out a large amount of **energy**; water has **different properties** from hydrogen and oxygen (a liquid at room temperature that does not burn); water has a **fixed** ratio of 2 hydrogen atoms to 1 oxygen atom; water **cannot be separated** into hydrogen and oxygen by physical methods

**[4]**

**8.** Magnesium oxide has a **fixed composition**: magnesium and oxygen are chemically bonded in a 1 : 1 ratio (MgO) [1]. A mixture of magnesium and sulfur has a **variable composition**: any mass of each can be mixed [1]. In the mixture the atoms are **not bonded**, so the magnesium and sulfur keep their own properties, while in the compound the elements are bonded [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** Liquid **B** [1]. It boils over a **range** of temperatures; a pure substance boils at a single fixed temperature [1]

**(b)** Whether liquid A can be broken down into simpler substances by a chemical reaction / its formula / which elements it contains (any one) [1]

**[3]**

**10.** Sodium chloride has properties completely **different** from those of sodium and chlorine [1]; whereas in a mixture each component **keeps its own properties** [1]. **[2]**

**11. (a)** A compound (a pure compound: one kind of molecule containing two different elements) [1]

**(b)** A mixture of a compound and an element [1]: there are now **two kinds of molecule**, and the new molecules contain only **one kind of atom** (oxygen) [1]

**[3]**

**12.** One mark per point:

- an element contains only **one type of atom** and **cannot be broken down** into simpler substances by a chemical reaction [1]
- a compound contains two or more different elements **chemically bonded** together [1]
- a mixture contains two or more substances that are **not chemically bonded** [1]
- a compound has a **fixed** composition / ratio of elements; a mixture has a **variable** composition [1]
- a compound can only be separated into its elements by a **chemical reaction** [1]
- a mixture can be separated by **physical methods** such as filtration or distillation [1]

**[6]**

*Examiner insight:* a full answer covers all three substances against each heading. Answers that describe mixtures in detail but ignore elements cannot reach full marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling N₂, O₂ or Cl₂ a compound because the atoms are joined.
- Calling a solution or an alloy a compound.
- Writing "a compound has the properties of its elements".
- Giving "separated easily" instead of naming a physical method.
- Answering a "compare" question with two separate descriptions and no comparison.
