These notes summarise section 4.8, Circle theorems II, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Full explanations are in the [circle theorems II study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circle-theorems-ii/), and exam-style questions are in the [circle theorems II practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circle-theorems-ii-practice/).

## What is examined

Use these symmetry properties of circles, with reasons:

1. equal chords are equidistant from the centre,
2. the perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre,
3. tangents from an external point are equal in length.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Chord | a line joining two points on the circumference |
| Equidistant | the same distance away |
| Perpendicular bisector | a line that cuts a segment in half at 90° |
| Tangent | a line touching the circle at exactly one point |
| External point | a point outside the circle |
| Kite | a quadrilateral with two pairs of adjacent equal sides |
| Point of contact | the point where a tangent touches the circle |

## The three properties and the reasons to write

| Property | Reason |
|---|---|
| OM ⟂ AB, M the midpoint of chord AB | the perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord |
| the bisector of a chord passes through O | the perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre |
| equal chords have equal distances from O | equal chords are equidistant from the centre |
| PA = PB for tangents from P | tangents from an external point are equal in length |

## Method box: chord problems

1. Draw the radius to each end of the chord and the perpendicular from the centre.
2. Mark the midpoint M and the right angle at M.
3. Use the right-angled triangle OMA: hypotenuse r, sides d and half-chord.
4. Apply Pythagoras:

```
r² = d² + (AB/2)²
```

5. Remember to double the half-chord at the end if the chord length is asked for.

| Known | Find | How |
|---|---|---|
| r = 13, AB = 24 | d | d = √(169 − 144) = 5 |
| r = 10, d = 6 | AB | AB = 2 × √(100 − 36) = 16 |
| d = 8, AB = 30 | r | r = √(64 + 225) = 17 |

## Method box: tangent problems

1. Mark the radius to each point of contact. It meets the tangent at 90°.
2. Triangle OAP is right-angled at A, with hypotenuse OP.
3. Use Pythagoras:

```
PA² = OP² − r²
```

4. OP bisects angle APB and angle AOB. OAPB is a kite.
5. Angle at the centre + angle at P = 180°, because the other two angles are 90°.

| Known | Find | Result |
|---|---|---|
| OP = 17, r = 8 | PA | 15 |
| angle APB = 50° | angle PAB | 65° |
| angle APB = 50° | angle AOB | 130° |
| angle AOB = 118° | angle APB | 62° |

## Equal tangent lengths in a triangle

If a circle touches the three sides of triangle ABC, the two tangents from each vertex are equal. With s the semi-perimeter, the tangent length from a vertex is s minus the opposite side.

```
Triangle 13, 14, 15: s = 21
from A (opposite 14): 7
from B (opposite 15): 6
from C (opposite 13): 8
```

## Parallel chords

If two parallel chords have distances d₁ and d₂ from the centre, they are d₁ + d₂ apart when on opposite sides, and |d₁ − d₂| apart when on the same side.

## Must-know distinctions

| Idea | Be clear that |
|---|---|
| Distance to a chord | measured perpendicular, from the centre |
| Half the chord | the side of the right-angled triangle |
| Radius | the hypotenuse |
| Closer to the centre | longer chord |
| Diameter | the longest chord, at distance 0 |

## Exam traps

- Using the whole chord instead of half in Pythagoras.
- Stopping at half the chord.
- Putting the radius where a leg should go, or the other way round.
- Forgetting the 90° between tangent and radius.
- Quoting "tangents are equal" without "from an external point".
- Missing the second answer for parallel chords on either side of the centre.

## Command words you will meet

| Command word | What to do |
|---|---|

## Self-test

1. A circle has radius 10 cm and a chord 16 cm long. Find the distance from the centre to the chord.
2. A chord is 3 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 5 cm. Find the length of the chord.
3. P is 13 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 5 cm. Find the length of a tangent from P.
4. Tangents from P touch a circle at A and B and angle APB = 40°. Find angle OAB.
5. PA = (3x + 1) cm and PB = (2x + 6) cm are tangents from P. Find x and PA.
6. A chord of length 30 cm is 8 cm from the centre. Find the radius.
7. A chord is 5 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 13 cm. A second chord is also 5 cm from the centre. What is the length of the second chord?
8. A tangent from P has length 9 cm and the radius is 12 cm. Find OP.
9. Two chords are 3 cm and 5 cm from the centre of the same circle. Which is longer?
10. Chord AB subtends a right angle at the centre of a circle of radius 10 cm. Find AB to 3 significant figures.

### Answers

1. √(100 − 64) = **6 cm**.
2. 2 × √(25 − 9) = **8 cm**.
3. √(169 − 25) = **12 cm**.
4. Angle PAB = 70°, so angle OAB = 90 − 70 = **20°**.
5. 3x + 1 = 2x + 6, so **x = 5** and PA = **16 cm**.
6. √(15² + 8²) = √289 = **17 cm**.
7. Half of the first chord is √(169 − 25) = 12, so it is 24 cm long. The second chord is also **24 cm** (chords equidistant from the centre are equal).
8. √(81 + 144) = √225 = **15 cm**.
9. The chord **3 cm** from the centre is longer.
10. AB = √(10² + 10²) = √200 = **14.1 cm**.
