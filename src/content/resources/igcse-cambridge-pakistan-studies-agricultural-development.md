**Agricultural development is Section 4 of Paper 2 in Cambridge IGCSE Pakistan Studies 0448.** It
covers how Pakistan's farming systems work, where the main crops and livestock are found, the
natural and human factors that affect production, the types of irrigation, the problems of
waterlogging and salinity, and how government action has tried to raise output. Answers need named
crops, named areas and clear cause-and-effect links.

## Where does agriculture fit in 0448 Paper 2?

Agriculture sits between Power (Section 3) and Industrial development (Section 5). It draws on
Section 1 (topography and climate decide what grows where) and Section 2 (water supply from dams,
barrages and canals). It also feeds Section 5, because cotton, sugar cane and wheat are raw
materials for Pakistan's largest processing industries, and Section 6, because cotton goods and rice
are major exports.

## How does a farming system work?

Every farm can be shown as a system:

- **Inputs:** land, water, seeds, fertiliser, labour, machinery, capital, animals.
- **Processes:** ploughing, sowing, transplanting, irrigating, weeding, spraying, harvesting, threshing, milking.
- **Outputs:** crops, milk, meat, wool, eggs, income, and waste such as crop residue.

On a **subsistence** farm most outputs feed the family. On a **cash crop** farm outputs are sold,
so income becomes an input for next season. A **livestock** system adds fodder and veterinary care as
inputs.

## What are kharif and rabi crops?

| Season | Sown | Harvested | Main crops |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kharif | Summer, at or before the monsoon | Autumn | Cotton, rice, sugar cane, maize, millet |
| Rabi | Winter, after the monsoon | Spring | Wheat, pulses (such as gram), oilseeds |

Kharif crops need high temperatures and plenty of water. Rabi crops grow in the cooler, drier
winter and rely on irrigation, winter rain from western depressions, and moisture stored in the soil.

## Where are the main crops grown, and why?

- **Cotton:** southern Punjab and central Sindh, where summers are long, hot and sunny and canal water is available. It needs a frost-free growing season and dry weather at picking.
- **Rice:** the "rice belt" of northern Punjab around Gujranwala and Sialkot (known for basmati) and parts of northern Sindh. It needs high temperatures, standing water and clay soils that hold water.
- **Sugar cane:** central Punjab, central Sindh and the Peshawar valley. It needs heat, a long growing season and a great deal of water.
- **Wheat:** grown across the irrigated plains of Punjab and Sindh, and on barani land in the Potwar Plateau. It is the staple food crop.

Fruit examples to learn with location and use: mangoes (Multan, southern Punjab, Sindh), oranges
(kinnow around Sargodha), dates (Khairpur in Sindh and Balochistan oases), apples and apricots
(cooler northern valleys and Balochistan uplands), bananas (Sindh).

## How do natural and human factors affect production?

**Traditional rice on a small holding.** Family labour ploughs with oxen, raises seedlings in a
nursery, transplants them by hand into flooded fields, keeps the water level up, and harvests and
threshes by hand. Small, fragmented plots make machinery hard to use, and lack of capital limits
fertiliser and good seed, so yields stay low.

**Barani wheat.** On rain-fed land, output depends on winter rainfall. A dry winter means a poor
harvest, so farmers take few risks and invest little.

**Karez oasis farming.** In Balochistan, an underground channel brings groundwater from the foot of
the hills to the oasis by gravity. It loses little water to evaporation, but channels need constant
cleaning and are being replaced in places by tubewells, which lower the water table.

**Livestock.** Buffalo and cattle on small farms provide milk, draught power and dung. In the
mountains and Balochistan, sheep and goats are herded by nomads or by transhumance, moving to
high summer pastures and down to valleys in winter.

## Which types of irrigation are used?

| Type | How it works | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inundation canal | Takes water only when the river floods | Cheap to build | Water only in the flood season |
| Perennial canal | Fed from a barrage all year | Reliable year-round supply | Seepage causes waterlogging |
| Karez | Underground gravity channel | Little evaporation | Labour-intensive to maintain |
| Persian wheel | Animal-powered wheel lifting well water | Cheap and simple | Small area; slow |
| Tubewell | Electric or diesel pump from deep wells | Water when needed; lowers water table | Running costs; can over-draw groundwater |

Dams store water; barrages raise river level and divert it into canals; link canals move water
between rivers; distribution canals and field channels carry it to farms; bunds hold water on
fields.

## What causes waterlogging and salinity, and how can they be solved?

Seepage from unlined canals and over-watering raise the water table. When it nears the surface the
soil becomes waterlogged and roots cannot breathe. In the hot climate, water evaporates and leaves
salts behind on the surface, which is salinity.

**Restoring land:** tubewells pump groundwater out to lower the water table; drainage channels carry
salty water away; land is flushed with water and salt-tolerant crops are planted.
**Prevention:** lining canals, more careful water use, and better drainage.

## How has government action increased production?

Land reforms set limits on the size of holdings and aimed to transfer land to tenants. Government
has also promoted training through agricultural extension work, loans for machinery, subsidised
fertiliser and pesticides, and high-yielding seed varieties. Evaluate these: larger landowners have
often gained most, and chemicals raise costs and can pollute water.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between subsistence and cash crop farming?**
Subsistence farming grows food
mainly for the farmer's family. Cash crop farming grows crops such as cotton or sugar cane mainly for
sale.

**What is transhumance?**
The seasonal movement of herders and their animals between high summer
pastures and lower winter grazing.

**Why is waterlogging a major problem in the Indus Plain?**
Large perennial canal systems lose water
through seepage, raising the water table, and high evaporation then brings salts to the surface.
