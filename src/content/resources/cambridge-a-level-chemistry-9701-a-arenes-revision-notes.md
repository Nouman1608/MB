subtopic 30.1 Arenes.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Arene** | a hydrocarbon containing a benzene ring |
| **Electrophilic substitution** | an electrophile replaces a ring H; the delocalised ring is kept |
| **Halogen carrier** | AlCl₃ or AlBr₃: generates the halogen electrophile and is regenerated |
| **Friedel–Crafts reaction** | alkylation (adds –R) or acylation (adds –COR) using AlCl₃ |
| **Aromatic stabilisation** | extra stability of the delocalised ring compared with localised C=C bonds |
| **Directing effect** | the influence of a ring substituent on the position of the next substitution |

## Six reactions to learn exactly

| | Reagents and conditions | Benzene → | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | Cl₂ with AlCl₃, or Br₂ with AlBr₃ | chlorobenzene / bromobenzene | electrophilic substitution |
| (b) | **conc.** HNO₃ + **conc.** H₂SO₄, **25–60 °C** | nitrobenzene | electrophilic substitution |
| (c) | CH₃Cl, AlCl₃, **heat** | methylbenzene | electrophilic substitution |
| (d) | CH₃COCl, AlCl₃, **heat** | phenylethanone | electrophilic substitution |
| (e) | **hot alkaline KMnO₄, then dilute acid** (alkylbenzene only) | benzoic acid from methylbenzene | oxidation |
| (f) | H₂, **Pt or Ni**, heat | cyclohexane | addition (reduction) |

## Electrophile equations

    nitration:     HNO₃ + 2H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + H₃O⁺ + 2HSO₄⁻
    bromination:   Br₂ + AlBr₃ → Br⁺ + AlBr₄⁻
    alkylation:    CH₃Cl + AlCl₃ → CH₃⁺ + AlCl₄⁻
    acylation:     CH₃COCl + AlCl₃ → CH₃CO⁺ + AlCl₄⁻
    regeneration:  H⁺ + AlX₄⁻ → HX + AlX₃

## Mechanism checklist (nitrobenzene, bromobenzene)

1. Equation for the electrophile.
2. Curly arrow **from the delocalised ring** to the electrophile.
3. Intermediate: attacked C carries H and E; partial ring with **+**.
4. Curly arrow **from the C–H bond** into the ring.
5. **H⁺ lost**; ring restored; product.
6. Catalyst or H₂SO₄ regenerated.

**Substitution over addition:** addition would permanently destroy the delocalised π system; substitution
restores it.

## Methylbenzene essentials

- The ring is **more reactive** than benzene; –CH₃ directs to **2 and 4**.
- Ring halogenation: 2- and 4-chloromethylbenzene (Cl₂, AlCl₃).
- Nitration: 2- and 4-nitromethylbenzene.
- **Side-chain** halogenation (UV, no catalyst): (chloromethyl)benzene, C₆H₅CH₂Cl; with excess Cl₂ also C₆H₅CHCl₂
  and C₆H₅CCl₃.
- Oxidation: benzoic acid. Purple KMnO₄ → brown MnO₂ precipitate (alkaline).
- Hydrogenation: methylcyclohexane.

## Side-chain oxidation rule

Any alkyl side-chain → **one –COOH** on the ring. Ethylbenzene → benzoic acid. 1,4-dimethylbenzene →
benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid.

## Directing effects

| Group on ring | Directs to | Ring reactivity |
|---|---|---|
| –NH₂, –OH, –R | 2, 4 (6) | increased |
| –NO₂, –COOH, –COR | 3 (5) | decreased |

**Planning order:** the group already on the ring decides where the next one goes.

- 4-nitrobenzoic acid from methylbenzene: **nitrate, then oxidise**.
- 3-nitrobenzoic acid from methylbenzene: **oxidise, then nitrate**.

## Alkylation versus acylation

| | Alkylation | Acylation |
|---|---|---|
| Reagent | CH₃Cl | CH₃COCl |
| Electrophile | CH₃⁺ | CH₃CO⁺ |
| Product group | –CH₃, activating | –COCH₃, deactivating |
| Further substitution | likely (polysubstitution) | unlikely (monosubstitution) |

## Benzene compared with cyclohexene

| | Cyclohexene | Benzene |
|---|---|---|
| π electrons | localised in one C=C | delocalised round the ring |
| With Br₂ at room temperature, no catalyst | decolourised: electrophilic addition | no reaction |
| With Br₂ and AlBr₃ | — | bromobenzene: electrophilic substitution |
| Product keeps its π bonding? | no: 1,2-dibromocyclohexane | yes: the delocalised ring is kept |
| Hydrogenation | C₆H₁₀ + H₂ → C₆H₁₂ | C₆H₆ + 3H₂ → C₆H₁₂, needs Pt or Ni and heat |

The electron density of a localised π bond is concentrated between two carbons, so it can polarise Br₂ by
itself; the spread-out ring cannot without a halogen carrier.

## Exam traps

- **Concentrated** acids for nitration of benzene; above 60 °C more than one –NO₂ group is substituted.
- **AlBr₃ with Br₂**, **AlCl₃ with Cl₂**: match the halogen.
- **No UV** for ring halogenation; **UV and no catalyst** for side-chain.
- **"then dilute acid"** is part of the oxidation conditions.
- Methylbenzene gives **two** ring isomers (2 and 4), not one, and not the 3-isomer.
- Benzene does **not** decolourise bromine water at room temperature without a halogen carrier.
- Hydrogenation product is **cyclohexane**, not cyclohexene.

## Self-test

1. Give the reagents and conditions for the nitration of benzene.
2. Write the equation for the formation of the electrophile in Friedel–Crafts acylation with CH₃COCl.
3. Name the product of reacting benzene with CH₃Cl and AlCl₃.
4. Give the two organic products of nitrating methylbenzene.
5. What forms when Cl₂ reacts with methylbenzene in UV light?
6. Name the product of heating ethylbenzene with alkaline KMnO₄, then adding dilute acid.
7. Write the equation for the hydrogenation of benzene.
8. To which position does –COCH₃ direct?
9. Why does Friedel–Crafts alkylation often give more than one substitution?
10. Plan a two-step route from benzene to 3-nitrophenylethanone (–NO₂ at C3 relative to –COCH₃).
11. Explain why benzene reacts with bromine by substitution, not addition.
12. Write the equation showing regeneration of AlBr₃.

**Answers:**

1. Concentrated HNO₃ and concentrated H₂SO₄, 25–60 °C.
2. CH₃COCl + AlCl₃ → CH₃CO⁺ + AlCl₄⁻.
3. Methylbenzene.
4. 2-nitromethylbenzene and 4-nitromethylbenzene.
5. (Chloromethyl)benzene, C₆H₅CH₂Cl, by free-radical substitution (plus HCl).
6. Benzoic acid.
7. C₆H₆ + 3H₂ → C₆H₁₂ (Ni or Pt catalyst, heat).
8. The 3-position.
9. The alkyl group activates the ring, so the product is more reactive than benzene and reacts again.
10. Step 1: CH₃COCl, AlCl₃, heat, giving phenylethanone. Step 2: conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄, 25–60 °C; –COCH₃ directs to
    position 3.
11. Addition would destroy the delocalised π system and its stability; substitution restores it when H⁺ is lost.
12. H⁺ + AlBr₄⁻ → HBr + AlBr₃.
