Condensed recall for **4.8.1 Individual (moral), social (ethical), legal and cultural issues and opportunities**, AQA A-Level Computer Science (7517). The fuller explanation and a worked case study are in the [issues and opportunities study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-computer-science-7517-moral-ethical-legal-cultural-issues/); questions are in the [issues and opportunities practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-computer-science-7517-moral-ethical-legal-cultural-issues-practice/).

## What the specification asks for

- **Awareness of current opportunities and risks** of computing.
- Digital technologies have changed communication and information flows so that it is possible to: **monitor behaviour**; **amass and analyse personal information**; **distribute, publish, communicate and disseminate personal information**.
- Computer scientists and software engineers have **power and responsibility** for the algorithms they devise and the code they deploy.
- Software and algorithms **embed moral and cultural values**.
- The **issue of scale**: software used across the world gives individuals the potential for **great good** but also **great harm**.
- Be able to **discuss the challenges facing legislators** in the digital age.

## The four lenses

| Lens | Asks | Keywords |
|---|---|---|
| Moral (individual) | What is right or wrong for a person? | honesty, consent, conscience, whistleblowing |
| Ethical (social) | What is fair for groups and society? | fairness, bias, access, accountability, jobs |
| Legal | What do laws require or forbid? | data protection, misuse, copyright, interception |
| Cultural | How does it fit different communities' values? | privacy norms, language, censorship, customs |

## Opportunities and risks by capability

| Capability | Opportunities | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| Monitoring behaviour | Crime prevention; safety; finding missing people; independent living | Loss of privacy; chilling effect; misuse; misidentification; no consent |
| Amassing and analysing personal data | Medical advances; fraud detection; public services; useful recommendations | Hidden profiling; biased decisions; breaches; data used for new purposes |
| Distributing and publishing personal information | Free speech; community; whistleblowing; education | Harassment; private details exposed; misinformation; permanent records |

## Responsibility, values and scale

- **Responsibility:** consider who is affected, test for harm, be honest about limits, raise concerns, refuse unacceptable work.
- **Embedded values:** every design choice reflects a judgement (what to optimise, who the training data represents, how a system decides in a conflict).
- **Algorithmic bias:** a system trained on unrepresentative data may perform worse for some groups.
- **Scale:** one flaw reaches millions at once; one good idea benefits millions at once.

## Challenges for legislators

1. Technology changes **faster than law**.
2. The internet **crosses borders**, so jurisdiction and enforcement are unclear.
3. **Anonymity** and changeable digital evidence make it hard to identify offenders.
4. Rights must be **balanced**: privacy against security, free speech against harm, innovation against regulation.
5. Lawmakers need enough **technical understanding** for clear laws.
6. **Enforcing** at scale is difficult and raises its own privacy issues.
7. **Cultures differ** on what is acceptable, so global services face conflicting rules.

## UK law examples (illustrative, not a required list)

| Law | Main idea |
|---|---|
| Data Protection Act 2018 / UK GDPR | Personal data must be used lawfully, fairly, securely and only as long as needed; individuals have rights over their data |
| Computer Misuse Act 1990 | Offences for unauthorised access, access with intent to commit a further offence, and unauthorised modification of material |
| Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988 | Protects creators against unlicensed copying of software and other works |
| Regulation of Investigatory Powers Act 2000 | Controls interception of communications and surveillance by public bodies |

## Method for a discussion answer

1. **Stakeholders:** who gains and who bears the risk?
2. **Opportunities and risks** for each, taken from the scenario.
3. **Lens:** moral, ethical, legal or cultural.
4. **Safeguards:** consent, transparency, limits on use, testing, human review.
5. **Weigh up and conclude.**

**Sentence frame:** "This could [benefit] because [reason from scenario], but it also risks [harm] for [group], so [conclusion/safeguard]."

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Personal data | Information that identifies a living individual, directly or indirectly |
| Consent | Agreement freely given by a person who understands what they are agreeing to |
| Profiling | Using data to build a picture of a person's characteristics or behaviour |
| Algorithmic bias | Systematically unfair outcomes caused by an algorithm or its training data |
| Surveillance | Close monitoring of people's activities |
| Jurisdiction | The area or country within which a law applies and can be enforced |
| Whistleblowing | Reporting wrongdoing inside an organisation |
| Data breach | Unauthorised access to or loss of personal data |
| Function creep | Data collected for one purpose being used for another |
| Digital divide | The gap between people who have access to technology and those who do not |

## Exam traps

- **One-sided answers.** Give risks and opportunities.
- **No link to the scenario.** Quote or refer to specific details.
- **Equating legal with ethical.** An action can be legal and still unfair.
- **Unsure about an Act.** Describe the principle, not the clause.
- **No conclusion.** Finish with a reasoned judgement.
- **Vague terms.** Write "personal data" and "consent", not "stuff" or "permission".

## Self-test

1. Name the three capabilities of digital technology highlighted in the specification.
2. Give one opportunity and one risk of monitoring behaviour.
3. What is meant by algorithmic bias?
4. Why does scale matter when software is used worldwide?
5. Give two challenges facing legislators.
6. Which Act makes unauthorised access to a computer an offence?
7. What is meant by a cultural issue? Give one example.
8. Name two questions to ask about the safeguards for a new data-collecting service.
9. What is the difference between a moral issue and a legal issue?
10. Why can a lack of consent be a problem even when a service is legal?
11. What is function creep?
12. Why is a one-sided answer to a discussion question likely to lose marks?

**Answers**

1. Monitoring behaviour; amassing and analysing personal information; distributing, publishing and communicating personal information.
2. For example: opportunity, crime prevention; risk, loss of privacy.
3. Systematically unfair outcomes caused by an algorithm or its training data.
4. One fault or one good idea can reach millions at once.
5. Any two of: pace of change; jurisdiction; anonymity; balancing rights; technical understanding; enforcement; cultural differences.
6. The Computer Misuse Act 1990.
7. An issue that arises because communities hold different values or expectations, for example different attitudes to privacy or to what content is acceptable.
8. Any two of: is consent obtained; how long is data kept; who can see it; is it tested for bias; is there human review; is it secure.
9. A moral issue concerns right and wrong for a person; a legal issue concerns what the law requires or forbids.
10. Legal permission does not make it fair: people may not know their data is being used.
11. Using data collected for one purpose for another purpose.
12. The question asks for a discussion, which needs both sides and a judgement.
