**This guide covers content area 3.1.1.7 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), Prophethood (Nabuat) and Holy Books in Islam: "the life, role, and importance of Prophets Adam (AS), Ibraheem (AS), Musa (AS) and Isa (AS)", "the nature of holy books in Islam with reference to Tawrat, Zabur, Injil, and the Suhuf (scrolls) of Hazrat Ibraheem (AS)" and "the belief in Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh) as the Seal of Prophethood".** The prescribed passage is Qur'an 9:128-129. The [Prophets Adam, Ibraheem, Musa and Isa study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-prophets-adam-ibraheem-musa-isa/) gives a detailed life of each prophet. This page covers all three parts of the content area together and shows how they join into one argument: Allah guides humanity through prophets, who bring books, and the chain ends with Muhammad (pbuh).

## What prophethood means

A **prophet** (*nabi*) is a human being chosen by Allah to receive revelation and to guide people. A **messenger** (*rasul*) is a prophet who brings a message or scripture to a community. Many scholars say that every messenger is a prophet but not every prophet is a messenger. The Qur'an says that Allah sent a messenger to every community (16:36) and that the message was the same: worship Allah alone and avoid false gods.

Muslims believe the following about all prophets:

- **They were human.** They ate, married and died. They were not divine and not to be worshipped.
- **They were truthful and trustworthy.** They delivered the message completely.
- **They were supported by miracles** (*mu'jizat*) by Allah's permission, as signs of their truth.
- **They taught the same core message:** Tawhid, good conduct and accountability.

The Qur'an names twenty-five prophets, and Muslims believe there were many more. A Muslim must respect all of them: "We make no distinction between any of His messengers" (2:285).

## Four prophets in the specification

### Adam (AS)

**Life:** Allah created Adam from clay (15:26) and taught him the names of things (2:31). Allah told the angels to bow, and Iblis refused out of pride (7:11-12). Adam and his wife lived in the Garden. They were told to avoid one tree, were tempted by Satan, and ate from it. They said, "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy upon us, we will surely be among the losers" (7:23), and Allah accepted their repentance (2:37). They were sent to earth.

**Role:** the first human being and the first prophet, who taught his children to worship Allah. He was appointed as a *khalifah* (steward) on earth (2:30).

**Importance:** it shows human dignity (angels bowed) and human weakness, and it teaches that Satan is an enemy but that Allah forgives anyone who repents. Muslims do not believe in inherited sin; each person is responsible for their own deeds.

### Ibraheem (AS)

**Life:** Ibraheem grew up among idol worshippers and argued with his people. He broke their idols, leaving the largest, to show that they could not help themselves (21:57-63). He was thrown into a fire, but Allah commanded, "O fire, be coolness and safety upon Abraham" (21:69). He left his wife Hajar and son Isma'il in the valley of Makkah, where the spring of Zamzam appeared. Later he saw in a dream that he should sacrifice his son, and both submitted. Allah replaced the sacrifice with a ram (37:102-107). Ibraheem and Isma'il built the Ka'aba (2:127).

**Role:** a leader in Tawhid, called Allah's *Khalil* (close friend, 4:125). He received scrolls, the Suhuf (87:18-19). Many Muslims call him "the father of the prophets".

**Importance:** the example of total submission. Eid ul-Adha remembers the sacrifice, and Hajj rituals such as Sa'i between Safa and Marwah recall Hajar. The Ka'aba is the centre of prayer.

### Musa (AS)

**Life:** Musa was born in Egypt when Pharaoh was killing Israelite baby boys. His mother placed him in the river, and he was raised in Pharaoh's household (28:7-9). As an adult he struck a man who died, fled to Madyan and worked for a righteous man. On his return, Allah spoke to him at the burning bush and appointed him a prophet with signs, including a staff (20:9-24). He asked that his brother Harun support him. He confronted Pharaoh and led the Israelites out of Egypt, and Allah parted the sea (26:63). On Mount Tur he received the Tawrat.

**Role:** a messenger who confronted a tyrant, often called *Kalimullah* because Allah spoke to him (4:164). He is the prophet mentioned most often in the Qur'an.

**Importance:** it teaches courage, patience and justice, and that Allah supports the oppressed.

### Isa (AS)

**Life:** Isa was born to Maryam without a father, by Allah's command "Be" (3:47; 19:20-21). As an infant he spoke in the cradle: "Indeed, I am the servant of Allah. He has given me the Scripture and made me a prophet" (19:30). By Allah's permission he healed the blind and the leper and gave life to the dead (3:49). He received the Injil and had disciples (3:52).

**Role:** a messenger to the Children of Israel, calling them to worship Allah and confirming the Tawrat.

**What Muslims believe:** Isa (AS) was a mighty prophet but not divine and not the son of God (5:72-75). The Qur'an says that he was not killed or crucified but that Allah raised him (4:157-158). A Hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari says that he will return before the Last Day.

**Importance:** a model of humility and compassion, and a link between Muslims, Christians and Jews.

| Prophet | Known for | Book or sign | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adam (AS) | First human and prophet | Names taught by Allah | Repentance |
| Ibraheem (AS) | Tawhid and sacrifice | Suhuf | Submission |
| Musa (AS) | Confronting Pharaoh | Tawrat | Courage |
| Isa (AS) | Miraculous birth | Injil | Humility and mercy |

## Holy books

Muslims believe Allah revealed books through chosen prophets. The book is Allah's word, delivered through revelation (*wahy*). The specification names four earlier books and one final one.

| Book | Prophet | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Suhuf** (scrolls) | Ibraheem (AS) | Mentioned in 87:18-19; not preserved |
| **Tawrat** | Musa (AS) | Mentioned in 5:44 as guidance and light |
| **Zabur** | Dawud (AS) | Mentioned in 4:163; songs of praise |
| **Injil** | Isa (AS) | Mentioned in 5:46 as guidance and light |
| **Qur'an** | Muhammad (pbuh) | Final, preserved |

**The nature of the earlier books.** Muslims believe the earlier books came from Allah and contained guidance. They also believe that people changed or lost parts of the original text over time (2:79; 5:13), so Muslims do not treat the books now held by others as identical to the originals. Muslims respect Jews and Christians as "People of the Book" and honour their prophets.

**The nature of the Qur'an.** It is the final revelation, a "criterion" over earlier scriptures (5:48), and Allah has promised, "Indeed, it is We who sent down the Qur'an and indeed, We will be its guardian" (15:9). Muslims point to memorisation, recitation and one agreed text as signs of this preservation.

## Muhammad (pbuh) as the Seal of Prophethood

*Khatam an-Nabiyyin* means the last or seal of the prophets. Qur'an 33:40 says that Muhammad (pbuh) is "the Messenger of Allah and last of the prophets". The Prophet (pbuh) described himself as the last brick in a house that was completed (Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim), and said that there would be no prophet after him (Sahih al-Bukhari).

**What it means for Muslims:**

- The Qur'an is the final book, and its guidance is complete (5:3).
- His message is for all humanity, not just one people (21:107; 34:28).
- Muslims follow his Sunnah as the practical model.
- Islam does not wait for a new prophet; leadership continues through scholars and community.

## The prescribed passage: Qur'an 9:128-129

> "There has certainly come to you a Messenger from among yourselves. Grievous to him is what you suffer; [he is] concerned over you and to the believers is kind and merciful. But if they turn away, [O Muhammad], say, 'Sufficient for me is Allah; there is no deity except Him. On Him I have relied, and He is the Lord of the Great Throne.'"

| Words | Teaching |
|---|---|
| "from among yourselves" | He was human, which shows he was a model people could follow |
| "Grievous to him is what you suffer" | He felt the pain of others, a sign of compassion |
| "concerned over you" | He wanted guidance for people |
| "to the believers is kind and merciful" | His character reflected Allah's mercy |
| "Sufficient for me is Allah ... On Him I have relied" | Tawakkul, even when rejected |

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why Hazrat Ibraheem (AS) is important to Muslims. **[4]**

> He is important because he is an example of total submission to Allah [1], shown when he was willing to sacrifice his son and Allah replaced the sacrifice with a ram; Muslims remember this at Eid ul-Adha [1]. He is also important because he built the Ka'aba with Isma'il [1], which Muslims face in prayer and visit on Hajj, so his work is part of every Muslim's worship [1].

## Common mistakes

- **Treating prophets as divine.** They were humans chosen by Allah.
- **Saying Muslims reject earlier books.** They believe in them as originally revealed, while holding that the texts changed.
- **Writing a story with no importance.** Finish each prophet with a lesson or role.
- **Confusing Suhuf with the Zabur.** Suhuf are Ibraheem's scrolls; Zabur was given to Dawud (AS).
- **Forgetting the Seal.** Link it to the Qur'an being final.
