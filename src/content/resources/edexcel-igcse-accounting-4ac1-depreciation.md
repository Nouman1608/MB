## What depreciation is, and is not

Depreciation is the part of a non-current asset's cost that is used up in an accounting period, charged as an expense in that period. It applies the accruals concept: the asset helps to earn revenue over several years, so its cost is spread over those years.

Depreciation is not a payment and does not set cash aside. The cash left the business when the asset was bought. Nor is the carrying value an estimate of what the asset would sell for; it is simply cost less the depreciation charged so far.

## Causes of depreciation

| Cause | Explanation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Wear and tear | Physical use makes the asset deteriorate | A delivery van's engine and bodywork wear with mileage |
| Obsolescence | A newer, better or cheaper version makes the asset outdated | Office computers replaced because new software will not run on them |
| Passage of time | The asset's useful life is fixed by time | A ten-year lease on premises loses a tenth of its life each year |
| Inadequacy | The asset can no longer meet the business's needs | A small oven that cannot keep up with a growing bakery |

"Explain" needs both the cause and why it reduces the asset's useful life. Match the cause to the asset in the question: obsolescence fits technology, passage of time fits leases, wear and tear fits machines and vehicles.

## Distinguishing the two methods

| Feature | Straight line | Reducing balance |
|---|---|---|
| Calculation | (Cost − residual value) ÷ useful life, or a percentage of cost | A fixed percentage of the carrying value at the start of each year |
| Charge each year | The same | Largest in Year 1, then smaller every year |
| Residual value | Deducted before dividing | Not deducted; the percentage is chosen so that the asset reaches roughly its residual value |
| Carrying value | Falls by equal amounts | Falls quickly at first, then slowly; never reaches nil |
| Suits | Assets used evenly over their life, such as furniture, fittings, leases | Assets that lose most value early or need more repairs as they age, such as vehicles and machinery |

### Worked example 1: the same van, both methods

Pelham Removals buys a van for 32,000. It expects to keep it for 4 years and sell it for 8,000.

Straight line: (32,000 − 8,000) ÷ 4 = **6,000** a year.

Reducing balance at 30%:

| Year | Opening carrying value | Depreciation (30%) | Closing carrying value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 32,000.00 | 9,600.00 | 22,400.00 |
| 2 | 22,400.00 | 6,720.00 | 15,680.00 |
| 3 | 15,680.00 | 4,704.00 | 10,976.00 |
| 4 | 10,976.00 | 3,292.80 | 7,683.20 |

The reducing balance carrying value after four years, 7,683.20, is close to the expected 8,000. Profit is lower in early years under reducing balance and higher in later years.

Why reducing balance often suits a van: repair costs are low when it is new and rise as it ages. High early depreciation plus low repairs, then low depreciation plus high repairs, gives a fairly even total cost of using the van each year.

## Depreciation policies for part years

Questions state the policy. Read it before calculating.

- **Full year in the year of purchase, none in the year of disposal.** The commonest policy in questions.
- **Month by month (time apportionment).** A machine costing 9,000, depreciated at 10% a year on cost, bought on 1 April with a 31 December year end: 9,000 × 10% × 9/12 = **675** for the first year.

Whatever the policy, apply it consistently, as the consistency concept requires.

## Recording depreciation

Entries each year:

1. Debit depreciation (expense), credit provision for depreciation, with the year's charge.
2. At the year end, transfer the depreciation expense to the income statement.

The asset account always stays at cost. The provision for depreciation account holds the accumulated depreciation and carries a credit balance. In the statement of financial position each class of asset is shown in three columns: cost, accumulated depreciation, and carrying value (cost less accumulated depreciation).

Some questions use the journal for these entries; the narrative would read "depreciation for the year on machinery".

## Disposals

When an asset is sold, open a disposal account and make three entries:

| Entry | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Remove cost | Disposal | Asset at cost |
| Remove accumulated depreciation on that asset | Provision for depreciation | Disposal |
| Record proceeds | Bank (or cash) | Disposal |

The balancing figure is the profit or loss. Proceeds above carrying value give a profit, credited to the income statement; proceeds below carrying value give a loss, debited. Check: profit or loss = proceeds − (cost − accumulated depreciation).

### Worked example 2: three years with a disposal

Quarrybank Builders has a 31 December year end. Machinery is depreciated at 20% reducing balance, with a full year's charge in the year of purchase and none in the year of disposal.

- 2025: buys mixer A for 15,000.
- 2026: buys mixer B for 10,000.
- 2027: sells mixer A for 8,900.

**Depreciation schedule**

| Year | Mixer A | Mixer B | Total charge |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2025 | 15,000 × 20% = 3,000 | | 3,000 |
| 2026 | 12,000 × 20% = 2,400 | 10,000 × 20% = 2,000 | 4,400 |
| 2027 | sold, no charge | 8,000 × 20% = 1,600 | 1,600 |

At the start of 2027, accumulated depreciation is 3,000 + 4,400 = 7,400, of which 3,000 + 2,400 = 5,400 belongs to mixer A. Mixer A's carrying value is 15,000 − 5,400 = 9,600, so selling for 8,900 gives a loss of **700**.

Machinery at cost

|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1 Jan Balance b/d | 25,000 | Disposal | 15,000 |
| | | 31 Dec Balance c/d | 10,000 |
| | **25,000** | | **25,000** |

|---|---:|---|---:|
| Disposal | 5,400 | 1 Jan Balance b/d | 7,400 |

## Exam technique

- **Explain the causes**: name the cause, say how it shortens useful life, and link it to an asset.
- **Distinguish**: compare the methods feature by feature (calculation, pattern of charge, residual value), not by describing one only.
- **Calculate**: show the formula, the figures substituted and the answer. For reducing balance, set out a year-by-year table.
- **Record**: date the entries, name the other account in each line, balance the accounts and bring balances down.

## Common errors

- Deducting residual value before applying a reducing balance percentage.
- Applying the reducing balance rate to cost in Year 2 instead of to carrying value.
- Crediting depreciation to the asset account when a provision account is used.
- Moving the whole provision balance to disposal instead of only the sold asset's share.
- Charging depreciation in the year of disposal when the policy says none.
- Saying depreciation is "money saved to replace the asset".

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is residual value not deducted under reducing balance?**
The percentage is applied to the carrying value each year, and the rate is chosen so that the carrying value falls towards the expected residual value by the end of the asset's life.

**Which depreciation method suits a delivery van?**
Reducing balance often does, because vans lose more value early and need more repairs later, so total yearly cost is more even.

**How is the profit or loss on disposal calculated?**
Proceeds minus carrying value, where carrying value is cost less the accumulated depreciation on that asset. The disposal account shows it as the balancing figure.
