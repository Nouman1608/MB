All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Orchard Foods.

> **Case study: Orchard Foods**
>
> Orchard Foods makes fruit juice in a factory on the edge of a small
> town. It has 150,000 shares and last year made a profit of $240,000.
> The owners want to pay out 60% of the profit as dividends. The
> employees, whose total wage bill is $1,200,000, have asked for a 5%
> pay rise. The local council has told Orchard that residents are
> complaining about lorries, and that it may need to install a $45,000
> pollution filter. The lorries made 30 trips a day last year and the
> factory plans to increase this to 45 trips a day. Orchard's main
> supplier, a local orchard, has asked to be paid within 14 days
> rather than 60 days.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a stakeholder of Orchard Foods? **[1]**
- A. The employees
- B. A business in another country that has no link with Orchard
- C. A person who has never heard of the company
- D. A newspaper from another country

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a typical objective of the local community? **[1]**
- A. Higher dividends
- B. Lower pollution
- C. Higher prices
- D. Lower wages

**3.** Define the term "dividend". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** objectives of employees. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** way in which the government is affected by the activities of a business. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate the cost to Orchard Foods of the 5% pay rise. **[2]**

**7.** Calculate the dividend per share if the owners get their wish. **[2]**

**8.** Calculate the percentage increase in lorry trips planned by Orchard. **[2]**

**9.** Explain **two** ways in which the local community may be affected by Orchard Foods. **[4]**

**10.** Explain **one** way in which the supplier has influence over Orchard Foods. **[2]**

**11.** Analyse the conflict between the owners and the employees. Use your answers to Questions 6 and 7. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate whether Orchard Foods should put the interests of the local community or the interests of the owners first. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **A** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A payment of part of a company's profit [1] to its shareholders [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: higher pay [1]; job security [1]; good working conditions [1]; training and promotion [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The government receives taxes from the business's profit and its employees' pay [1], so a business that grows pays more tax and creates more jobs, which reduces the cost of support for the unemployed [1]. Accept: the government makes laws that businesses must obey [1] with a consequence for the business [1]. **[2]**

**6.** 5% x $1,200,000 [1] = **$60,000** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Dividends = 60% x $240,000 = $144,000 [1]; $144,000 / 150,000 = **$0.96** per share [1]. **[2]**

**8.** (45 - 30) / 30 x 100 [1] = **50%** [1]. **[2]**

**9.** **Jobs and spending:** Orchard creates jobs in the town [1], and workers spend wages in local shops [1].
**Noise, traffic and pollution:** the lorries and the factory cause noise, congestion and pollution [1], which residents dislike, and the number of lorry trips is planned to rise by 50% [1]. **[4]**

**10.** The orchard is the supplier of the fruit used for juice [1], so by asking for faster payment it can pressure Orchard Foods, which may have to find extra cash earlier or risk losing supplies [1]. **[2]**

**11.** **The conflict:** the owners want a dividend of $144,000 [1], while the employees want a 5% pay rise that costs $60,000 [1].
**The effect:** if Orchard pays the pay rise from profit, profit falls to $180,000 and the 60% dividend would be $108,000, or $0.72 a share [1], a loss of $36,000 for owners [1]. Employees gain a pay rise that may improve motivation and keep staff [1], but owners lose [1]. Accept alternative suggestions for the profit available, with the working shown. **[6]**

**12.** **For the community:** the community can complain to the council, which might limit lorry trips or refuse permission to expand [1]; a pollution filter costing $45,000 would reduce harm and keep Orchard's good name in the town [1]; good relations help Orchard recruit local workers [1].
**For the owners:** the owners provide the capital and expect dividends of $144,000 [1]; if they are unhappy they could sell their shares or replace managers [1]; the filter and pay rise reduce profit available to them [1].
**Judgement:** in the long term Orchard needs both, because ignoring the community could lead to restrictions that cut output and profit [1]. In the short term, it should put the community first on the pollution filter, because a legal order would cost more than $45,000 of lost production, and compromise with the owners by phasing the dividend [1]. A clear judgement with a reason for the priority is needed. **[9]** Award full marks for the opposite conclusion if it is justified with evidence from the case.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that stakeholders "want money" without naming what each wants.
- Forgetting that a dividend is paid per share and dividing by the number of shares.
- Not showing the formula for percentage change.
- Saying that employees and owners both want the same thing in a conflict question.
- Ignoring the case details in an evaluation answer, such as the 50% rise in lorry trips.
