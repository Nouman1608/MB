## Section A — Knowledge and terms

**1.** State the two types of question available on each Paper 2 Section A set text. **[2]**

**2.** Define **free indirect discourse** and explain one advantage it gives a writer. **[2]**

**3.** Explain the difference between an **omniscient** and a **limited** third-person narrator. **[2]**

**4.** Define **analepsis** and **prolepsis**. **[2]**

**5.** Explain what it usually signals when a narrative slows from **summary** into **scene**. **[2]**

## Section B — Analysing original passages

**6.** Read this **original** passage.

> I want to be clear that I did not take the money. I moved it, which is different, from the tin on the
> kitchen shelf to the inside of my school shoe, where it would be safer. My brother says this is the
> same thing. My brother is nine and says a great many things.

Explain how the writer uses **first-person narration** to create humour and to make the reader doubt the
narrator. **[6]**

**7.** Read this **original** passage.

> The guests arrived in the order of their importance, which was also, Celia noticed, the order of their
> cars. The Bishop came last, in a car so old it was almost a statement. He shook her hand as though
> he were blessing it. How wonderful, she thought, to be so sure of everything.

**(a)** Identify where the passage moves into **free indirect discourse** and explain its effect.
**(b)** Explain how the narrator uses irony in the first sentence. **[5]**

**8.** Explain how the simile "as though he were blessing it" presents the Bishop in the passage in
Question 7. **[3]**

**9.** Read this **original** opening of a short story.

> Every summer the river took something. One year it was a goat, one year a bridge, one year the
> Mensah boy, who was a strong swimmer. This summer the water was low and brown and lazy, and the
> village children played on the stones, and the old women watched them and said nothing.

Explain how this opening uses **structure and pacing** to create foreboding. **[6]**

**10.** A candidate writing an (a) essay on a set novel writes four paragraphs, each retelling one
chapter in order. Explain why this approach limits the answer and suggest **two** improvements. **[4]**

**11.** An (a) question on a short-story selection asks you to "discuss ways in which **two** stories
present family conflict." Explain **three** decisions that would make a strong answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** An **(a) essay question** on the text as a whole [1]; a **(b) question** on a passage from the
text printed on the paper, related to the wider work [1].

**2.** Free indirect discourse is **third-person narration that takes on a character's own words, rhythms
or attitudes** [1]. It lets a writer present a character's thoughts closely while keeping **ironic
distance**, so the reader can share and judge at once [1].

**3.** An **omniscient** narrator can enter many characters' minds and comment freely [1]; a **limited**
narrator stays close to **one character's perceptions** and knowledge [1].

**4.** **Analepsis** is a flashback to earlier events [1]; **prolepsis** is a flash-forward to later
events [1].

**5.** It signals that the moment is **important** [1], inviting the reader to attend closely, often at a
turning point or moment of tension [1].

**6.** The narrator's insistent opening ("I want to be clear") sounds like a **defence before any
accusation**, which makes the reader suspicious [1] [1]. The distinction between "take" and "moved" is a
**self-serving rationalisation** that the reader sees through [1]. The comic understatement of hiding
money "in my school shoe, where it would be safer" reveals a **child's logic** [1]. The dismissal of the
brother's view uses the narrator's **sense of superiority** for humour [1], while the brother's plain
judgement is in fact correct, creating a **gap between narrator and reader** [1].

**7. (a)** The final sentence, "How wonderful, she thought, to be so sure of everything," moves into
**Celia's own voice and attitude** [1]. Its effect is to let the reader share her **private, sardonic
judgement** of the Bishop [1], while the narration keeps a slight distance, so the irony may also
include her [1]. **(b)** The narrator links "importance" to "cars", so the social order is **exposed as
a matter of wealth and display** [1]; the matter-of-fact phrasing makes the satire dry rather than
openly mocking [1].

**8.** The simile suggests the Bishop treats an ordinary greeting as a **religious act**, presenting him
as self-important [1]. "As though" signals that this is an **appearance or performance** [1], so the
reader is invited to judge his piety as part of his social display [1].

**9.** The opening sentence states a **pattern** ("Every summer the river took something"), so the reader
expects the pattern to continue [1] [1]. The list moves from a goat to a bridge to a boy, a **climax that
escalates** from animal to object to human loss [1]. The detail that he "was a strong swimmer" **removes
any sense of safety** [1]. The final sentence slows with repeated "and", creating a **deceptively calm**
pace [1], while the old women's silence implies their knowledge of what may follow, creating
foreboding [1].

**10.** Retelling chapters in order is **narrative summary**, so the essay has **no argument** and limits
AO2 and AO3 [1] [1]. Improvements: begin with a **clear thesis** that answers the question [1]; organise
paragraphs **by point**, selecting moments from across the text and analysing narrative method in each
[1].

**11.** Choose **two stories that present family conflict differently**, so there is a comparative
argument [1] [1]. Organise **by point**, moving between the stories rather than writing on one then the
other [1] [1]. Analyse **narrative method** in each, such as voice, structure and the ending, with short
precise references [1] [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Discussing characters as real people and never mentioning the narrator.
- Missing irony in a narrator's tone.
- Retelling instead of arguing in (a) essays.
- Analysing a passage's content without discussing how it is narrated or structured.
