These notes condense sub-topic **7.2 Transport mechanisms** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). It is AS content for Paper 1 and Paper 2, assumed on Paper 4. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [transport mechanisms study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-transport-mechanisms/); for exam-style questions, use the [transport mechanisms practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-transport-mechanisms-practice/).

## The big picture

| | Xylem | Phloem |
|---|---|---|
| Carries | Water and mineral ions (dissolved) | Assimilates (sucrose, amino acids) dissolved in water |
| Direction | Upwards, roots to leaves | Source to sink, up or down |
| Driving force | Tension from transpiration | Hydrostatic pressure gradient |
| ATP needed? | No (Sun's energy drives evaporation) | Yes, for proton pumps at the source |
| Cells | Dead, lignified vessels | Living sieve tubes with companion cells |

## Soil to xylem

**Apoplast**
- Through cellulose cell walls and intercellular spaces.
- No membranes crossed; little resistance.
- Water held to cellulose by hydrogen bonds and pulled along by cohesion.
- Lignified xylem walls keep vessels open; water crosses at pits.

**Symplast**
- Through cytoplasm, cell to cell via plasmodesmata.
- Enters by osmosis across the root hair cell membrane.

**Endodermis**
- Casparian strip = band of **suberin** in radial and end walls.
- Suberin is waterproof, so the apoplast is blocked.
- Water and ions must cross the endodermal cell membrane into the symplast.
- Result: the plant controls which ions reach the xylem.

## Transpiration in two steps

1. **Evaporation** of water from mesophyll cell walls into leaf air spaces.
2. **Diffusion** of water vapour through stomata to the atmosphere.

Faster when: air is dry (steeper vapour gradient), air is moving (humid layer removed), temperature is higher (faster evaporation and diffusion), stomata are open.

## Cohesion-tension in a chain

1. Water evaporates from mesophyll cell walls.
2. Water is drawn from leaf xylem to replace it.
3. Pressure at the top of the xylem falls: the column is under **tension**.
4. **Cohesion** (hydrogen bonds between water molecules) lets the whole column be pulled up.
5. **Adhesion** (hydrogen bonds between water and cellulose) supports the column.
6. **Lignin** stops vessels collapsing.

Evidence: stems narrow slightly at midday; cut xylem draws air in.

## Xerophyte annotations

| Feature (label) | Annotation (explanation) |
|---|---|
| Rolled leaf, stomata inside | Humid air trapped; smaller water vapour gradient |
| Sunken stomata in pits | Still, moist air in pit; less diffusion |
| Hairs | Trap still, moist air; longer diffusion path |
| Thick waxy cuticle | Less evaporation through epidermis |
| Few stomata / small leaf area | Smaller surface for water vapour loss |

## Phloem loading at the source

1. Proton pumps use ATP to pump **H⁺ out** of the companion cell.
2. High H⁺ outside: an H⁺ gradient.
3. H⁺ diffuse back in through **cotransporter proteins**, bringing **sucrose** with them.
4. Sucrose moves into the sieve tube element through **plasmodesmata**.

Remember: the protons are pumped; sucrose is **cotransported**.

## Mass flow from source to sink

1. Sucrose loaded at source → water potential of sap falls.
2. Water enters by osmosis → **high hydrostatic pressure** at source.
3. Sucrose removed at sink (respired or stored as starch) → water leaves → **lower pressure** at sink.
4. Sap flows down the **hydrostatic pressure gradient**.

Evidence: sap exudes under pressure from a cut aphid stylet; the sap is rich in sucrose.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Apoplast | Route through cell walls and spaces between cells |
| Symplast | Route through cytoplasm and plasmodesmata |
| Casparian strip | Band of suberin in endodermal cell walls blocking the apoplast |
| Suberin | Waterproof substance in the Casparian strip |
| Transpiration | Evaporation from internal leaf surfaces then diffusion of vapour out |
| Cohesion | Attraction between water molecules by hydrogen bonds |
| Adhesion | Attraction between water and cellulose by hydrogen bonds |
| Tension | Negative pressure in the xylem column |
| Xerophyte | Plant adapted to reduce water loss in dry conditions |
| Assimilates | Substances made by the plant, such as sucrose and amino acids |
| Source | Part of the plant releasing assimilates into the phloem |
| Sink | Part of the plant removing assimilates from the phloem |
| Cotransporter | Carrier protein moving two substances at once |
| Mass flow | Bulk movement of a liquid down a pressure gradient |

## Exam traps

- "Water evaporates from the stomata": it evaporates inside the leaf.
- "Transpiration needs ATP": it does not.
- "Casparian strip made of lignin": it is suberin.
- Cohesion and adhesion explained without hydrogen bonds.
- "Sucrose is actively pumped": the protons are.
- Mass flow explained with "concentration gradient" only.
- Saying phloem flows only downwards.

## Self-test

1. State what the xylem and phloem have in common as transport media.
2. Name the substance forming the Casparian strip.
3. Explain why the Casparian strip lets the plant control ion uptake.
4. Name the two processes that make up transpiration.
5. Explain how humid air reduces the rate of transpiration.
6. Define cohesion.
7. Name the polymer in cell walls to which water adheres.
8. Why do xylem vessels not collapse under tension?
9. Give two xerophyte features and explain one.
10. Which ion is pumped out of companion cells?
11. Name the type of protein that carries sucrose into a companion cell.
12. Explain why hydrostatic pressure is lower at a sink.

### Answers

1. Both carry substances dissolved in water.
2. Suberin.
3. Water and ions must cross the endodermal cell surface membrane, where transport proteins select which ions pass.
4. Evaporation (from mesophyll cell walls) and diffusion (of water vapour through stomata).
5. It reduces the water vapour gradient between leaf air spaces and the air, so diffusion out is slower.
6. Attraction between water molecules due to hydrogen bonds.
7. Cellulose.
8. Their walls are thickened with lignin.
9. For example: sunken stomata, which trap humid air and reduce the vapour gradient; thick waxy cuticle; rolled leaf; hairs.
10. Hydrogen ions (protons, H⁺).
11. Cotransporter protein.
12. Sucrose is removed and used or stored, so the sap's water potential rises and water leaves by osmosis.
