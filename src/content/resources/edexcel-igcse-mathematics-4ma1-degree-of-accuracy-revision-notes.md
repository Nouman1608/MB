These notes condense **section 1.8, Degree of accuracy**, of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2. Statements 1.8 A–D are for both tiers; solving problems with upper and lower bounds is **Higher tier only**. For explanations and worked examples, use the [section 1.8 study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-degree-of-accuracy/).

## Condensed recall: rounding (1.8 A, B)

- Find the last digit you keep; look at the next digit: 5–9 round up, 0–4 round down.
- **Power of 10:** fill with zeros to keep the size. 61 749 to the nearest 1000 = 62 000.
- **Decimal places:** count after the point. 2.6849 to 2 d.p. = 2.68.
- **Significant figures:** start at the first non-zero digit. 0.030 47 to 3 s.f. = 0.0305.
- Keep trailing zeros that are significant: 2.0 (2 s.f.), 0.0050 (2 s.f.).
- Rounding can carry: 9.97 to 1 d.p. = 10.0.

## Condensed recall: bounds (1.8 C)

- Bounds = rounded value ± half the rounding unit.
- Error interval: LB ≤ x < UB.

| Given as | Unit | Bounds |
|---|---|---|
| 4.6 kg to 1 d.p. | 0.1 | 4.55 ≤ m < 4.65 |
| 70 cm to nearest 10 cm | 10 | 65 ≤ l < 75 |
| 0.83 to 2 s.f. | 0.01 | 0.825 ≤ x < 0.835 |
| 31 000 to 2 s.f. | 1000 | 30 500 ≤ x < 31 500 |
| 600 people to nearest 100 (discrete) | 100 | 550 to 649 people |

## Condensed recall: estimation (1.8 D)

- Round each number to 1 s.f., write the rounded calculation, then work it out.
- ÷ by a small decimal: multiply both numbers by 10, 100, … first. 18 ÷ 0.03 = 1800 ÷ 3 = 600.
- Over or under? Rounding a numerator up, or a denominator down, makes the estimate too big.

## Condensed recall: bounds in calculations (Higher tier only)

| To get the largest… | Use |
|---|---|
| sum a + b | UB + UB |
| difference a − b | UB − LB |
| product a × b | UB × UB |
| quotient a ÷ b | UB ÷ LB |

For the smallest result, swap every choice: LB + LB, LB − UB, LB × LB, LB ÷ UB.

- **Suitable accuracy:** round both bounds; the answer is the most accurate rounding at which they agree, with that reason stated.
- **"Will definitely fit / be enough":** use the worst case for every value.

## Worked recall

**Bounds from significant figures.** A distance of 0.0730 km, correct to 3 s.f., has unit 0.0001 and half-unit 0.00005, so 0.07295 ≤ d < 0.07305.

**Estimating a square root.** √(0.0083) ≈ ? Round to 1 s.f.: 0.008, which has no simple root; use the nearby square 0.0081 = 0.09², so the estimate is 0.09.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Significant figure | A digit that carries meaning about the size of a number, counted from the first non-zero digit |
| Decimal place | A digit position after the decimal point |
| Upper bound | The smallest value that would round up to the next value; the top of the error interval |
| Lower bound | The smallest value that rounds to the given value |
| Error interval | The inequality LB ≤ x < UB |
| Estimate | An approximate answer found by rounding to 1 s.f. |
| Discrete data | Data that can take only separate values, such as numbers of people |
| Continuous data | Data that can take any value in a range, such as length or time |

## Exam traps

- **Place-holder zeros:** 285 000 to 1 s.f. is 300 000, not 3.
- **Leading zeros are not significant:** 0.000 72 has 2 s.f.
- **Upper bound written correctly:** 4.65, not 4.649.
- **Discrete bounds:** 550 to 649 people, not 650.
- **Crossed bounds:** division and subtraction need opposite bounds.
- **Estimation method:** 1 s.f., not 1 d.p.; 0.0397 becomes 0.04.
- **Show the full display** before rounding a final calculator answer.

## Self-test

1. Round 4 739 260 to the nearest 10 000.
2. Round 0.070 49 to 3 significant figures.
3. Round 19.996 to 2 decimal places.
4. How many significant figures does 30.08 have?
5. A time is 12.7 s, correct to 1 d.p. Write its error interval.
6. A field's area is 3800 m², correct to 2 s.f. Write down its bounds.
7. A crowd is 2400 people, to the nearest 100. What is the largest possible number of people?
8. Estimate (39.6 × 0.48) ÷ 0.021.
9. Is your estimate in question 8 too big or too small? Give a reason.
10. (Higher) a = 7.2 and b = 3.5, both to 1 d.p. Find the upper bound of a − b.
11. (Higher) Using the same values, find the lower bound of a ÷ b to 3 d.p.
12. (Higher) The bounds of a quantity are 25.47 and 25.53. Write the quantity to a suitable degree of accuracy, with a reason.

**Answers**

1. 4 740 000.
2. 0.0705.
3. 20.00.
4. Four (zeros between non-zero digits count).
5. 12.65 ≤ t < 12.75.
6. 3750 ≤ A < 3850.
7. 2449.
8. (40 × 0.5) ÷ 0.02 = 20 ÷ 0.02 = 1000.
9. Too big: 39.6 and 0.48 were both rounded up, which makes the numerator bigger, and 0.021 was rounded down, which makes the quotient bigger still. (The calculator value is 905 to 3 s.f.)
10. 7.25 − 3.45 = 3.8.
11. 7.15 ÷ 3.55 = 2.014.
12. 25.5: both bounds round to 25.5 to 1 d.p. (and to 3 s.f.), but they differ at 2 d.p.
