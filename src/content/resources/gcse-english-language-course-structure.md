An overview of how AQA GCSE English Language (8700) is put together, for
students starting the course and for anyone deciding whether it is the
right specification for them.

## The two papers

AQA GCSE English Language is assessed across two equally-weighted
examined papers, each built around a distinct pair of reading and writing
skills:

- **Paper 1 — Explorations in Creative Reading and Writing.** Looks at
how writers use narrative and descriptive techniques to engage a reader,
paired with creative writing tasks of your own.
- **Paper 2 — Writers' Viewpoints and Perspectives.** Looks at how
different writers present a similar topic across different time periods,
paired with writing tasks that argue or present a viewpoint.

Reading sources across both papers are drawn from the 19th, 20th and 21st
centuries, giving a genuine spread of prose styles to read, understand
and analyse. Each paper's reading material is deliberately linked to the
theme of that paper's writing task, so the reading sources also act as a
springboard for the writing you are asked to do.

## Spoken language

Alongside the two written papers, AQA assesses **spoken language**
separately, through a presentation and discussion. This is reported
individually rather than counted into the overall GCSE grade for English
Language — but it is still a required part of the course, not an
optional extra.

## A skills-based approach

Across both papers, the specification takes a skills-based approach:
questions are sequenced from shorter, more contained tasks through to
longer, more extended responses, giving you a clear route through each
paper rather than a single all-or-nothing essay.

The two skills the whole course keeps returning to are:

- **Reading** — understanding, summarising, analysing language and
structure, and comparing how different writers present ideas.
- **Writing** — writing clearly, coherently and accurately, using a
deliberate range of vocabulary and sentence structures for a specific
purpose and audience.

## Studying alongside English Literature

AQA designs GCSE English Language to be **fully co-teachable** with GCSE
English Literature (8702) — the two specifications share underlying
skills (particularly close reading and structured written analysis), so
many schools teach them together rather than as fully separate courses.

## Study approach

Because both papers are built on unseen reading extracts, the single
highest-value skill to build across the course is **fast, accurate
close reading** — practising with a wide range of unfamiliar 19th, 20th
and 21st century texts matters more than memorising content, since (unlike
many other GCSEs) there is no fixed list of texts to revise. Pair that with
regular timed writing practice, since both papers combine reading analysis
with extended writing under exam conditions.
