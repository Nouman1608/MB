## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Anomaly | A measurement that does not fit the pattern of the repeats or the trend |
| Limitation | A feature of the method that reduces confidence in the results |
| Precision | How close repeated measurements are to each other |
| Accuracy | How close a measurement is to the true value |
| Random error | Unpredictable scatter in readings; reduced by repeating |
| Systematic error | A consistent shift in readings; not reduced by repeating |
| Uncertainty | The range within which the true value is expected to lie, such as ±0.05 cm³ |
| Percentage error | Uncertainty expressed as a percentage of the quantity measured |
| Margin of error | The ± range around a result |

## Evaluating results: 1.1.4(a)

1. Do the data support the conclusion? Quote values.
2. How strong is the evidence? Number of repeats, spread, range, statistical test.
3. What else could explain the result? Name an uncontrolled variable.
4. Limit the conclusion to the conditions tested.

## Anomalies: 1.1.4(b)

- Identify by comparing with repeats and the trend: 8.0, 8.5, 12.5 cm³ has an anomaly at 12.5.
- Suggest a cause: air bubble, timing error, different tissue, contamination.
- Repeat the measurement if possible; otherwise leave it out of the mean and say so.
- Effect on the mean: 9.7 cm³ with the anomaly, 8.25 cm³ without.

## Limitations: 1.1.4(c)

| Limitation | Effect |
|---|---|
| Temperature drift | Rate measured at wrong temperature |
| Few repeats | Unreliable mean; anomalies not found |
| Few values of the independent variable | Optimum or pattern not located |
| End point judged by eye | Subjective and inconsistent |
| Tissue of different size | Different surface area or mass |
| Delay before reading | Conditions change |

Write each limitation with its effect: "...so the time varies between repeats".

## Precision, accuracy and uncertainty: 1.1.4(d)

- Precise: small spread, small standard deviation.
- Accurate: close to the true value.
- A thermometer that reads 2 °C too high gives precise but inaccurate readings.
- Random error: reduced by repeating. Systematic error: needs calibration or a better method.

**Uncertainty of apparatus** (common estimate): half the smallest division.

| Instrument | Reads to | Uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Balance | 0.01 g | ±0.005 g |
| Syringe | 0.1 cm³ | ±0.05 cm³ |
| Measuring cylinder | 1 cm³ | ±0.5 cm³ |
| Thermometer | 1 °C | ±0.5 °C |

**Percentage error (specification formula):**

percentage error = 2 × absolute uncertainty ÷ quantity measured × 100 %

- Syringe, 4.0 cm³: 2 × 0.05 ÷ 4.0 × 100 = 2.5 %.
- Balance, 2.40 g: 2 × 0.005 ÷ 2.40 × 100 = 0.42 %.
- Measuring cylinder, 20.0 cm³: 2 × 0.5 ÷ 20.0 × 100 = 5.0 %.

**Difference of two readings:** the uncertainties add. 3.10 g − 2.79 g = 0.31 g with an uncertainty of ±0.01 g, which is a percentage error of 0.01 ÷ 0.31 × 100 = 3.2 %.

**Lowering the percentage error:** use a smaller-uncertainty instrument, use a larger quantity, or measure a larger change.

## Improvements: 1.1.4(e)

Pattern: **weakness, effect, improvement.**

| Weakness | Improvement |
|---|---|
| Water bath drifts | Thermostatically controlled bath |
| Large instrument uncertainty | Syringe or gas syringe instead of a measuring cylinder |
| Few values | More values, closer together near the optimum |
| End point by eye | Colorimeter or pH meter |
| One result per value | Three or more repeats and a mean |
| Uneven tissue | Same mass or cut with a cork borer |

## Writing good answers

- Say what is wrong, what it did to the results, and how to fix it.
- Make improvements specific: name the apparatus, volume or value.
- Do not write "human error", "be more careful" or "more accurate equipment".
- Use data where they are given.

## Exam traps

- Mixing up precision and accuracy.
- Saying repeating removes systematic error.
- Dropping an anomaly without a reason.
- Stating a limitation with no effect on the results.
- Giving an improvement that does not fix the stated weakness.
- Forgetting the factor of 2 in the specification's percentage-error formula.
- Treating a percentage error in a difference as if it were the same as the error in each reading.

## Self-test

1. Three repeats give 12.0, 12.4 and 15.9 cm³. Which result is anomalous?
2. Calculate the mean without the anomaly.
3. What is the uncertainty of a balance that reads to 0.01 g?
4. Calculate the percentage error for a volume of 5.0 cm³ measured with a syringe reading to 0.1 cm³, using 2 × absolute uncertainty ÷ quantity × 100.
5. A thermometer always reads 3 °C above the true temperature. Are the readings precise, accurate, or both?
6. Name the type of error in question 5.
7. Give two ways of reducing the percentage error in a measurement.
8. A student heats a water bath on a hotplate and the temperature drifts. State the effect and an improvement.
9. Why can repeating an experiment not remove a systematic error?
10. A balance reading changes from 5.20 g to 4.70 g. Each reading is ±0.005 g. Calculate the percentage error in the change.

### Answers

1. 15.9 cm³.
2. (12.0 + 12.4) ÷ 2 = 12.2 cm³.
3. ±0.005 g.
4. 2 × 0.05 ÷ 5.0 × 100 = 2.0 %.
5. Precise if the readings are close together, but not accurate.
6. A systematic error.
7. Use an instrument with a smaller uncertainty; measure a larger quantity; (for a difference) use a larger change.
8. The temperature is not controlled, so the rate is measured at the wrong temperature; use a thermostatically controlled water bath.
9. Every repeat shifts the result by the same amount in the same direction.
10. Change = 0.50 g; uncertainty ±0.01 g; 0.01 ÷ 0.50 × 100 = 2.0 %.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between a limitation and an improvement?**
A limitation is a weakness in the method and its effect. An improvement is the change that overcomes it. Give both when the question asks for an evaluation.

**Do I have to write ± when I give an uncertainty?**
Yes, if you are quoting an uncertainty. It shows that the value lies in a range around the reading.

**Can I use "reliable"?**
Use it carefully. It is better to say what you mean: repeats are close together (precise), the true value is close (accurate), or the experiment is repeatable.
