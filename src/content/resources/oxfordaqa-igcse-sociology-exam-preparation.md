OxfordAQA International GCSE Sociology (9292) is a **brand-new specification**: OxfordAQA states first
teaching from September 2026, with first examinations from May/June 2028. It is linear, untiered, and
assessed with no coursework by two equally weighted written papers. Paper 1 (Families; Education;
Research Methods) and Paper 2 (Differences and Inequalities; Socialisation and Social Control; Research
Methods) each run 1 hour 30 minutes, carry 60 marks and are worth 50%. **Research methods content
appears on both papers**, rather than being confined to one. These notes complement the site's guides
to [Introducing Sociology](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-sociology-introducing-sociology/) and
[Socialisation](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-sociology-socialisation/), and should be read alongside your
centre's own scheme of work, since this specification is new and teaching materials are still
developing.

## Research methods is not confined to one paper -- it is examined everywhere

Because methods content sits on both papers, it functions as a toolkit applied throughout rather than
a single topic to revise once. **Exam-preparation priority**: revise methods early, and then
deliberately practise using methods vocabulary -- sampling, validity, reliability, ethics -- when
answering questions on families, education, inequality and socialisation. A methodologically aware
answer to a substantive question reads very differently from one that simply asserts a claim.

## Paper 1 and Paper 2 are substantively different territories

Paper 1 covers families and education; Paper 2 covers differences and inequalities, and socialisation
and social control. **Exam-preparation priority**: revise them as genuinely separate content areas, and
resist the temptation to let concepts from one paper substitute vaguely for concepts on the other --
each has its own key studies, concepts and debates, even where methods vocabulary is shared.

## Ninety minutes for 60 marks is 1.5 minutes per mark

**Exam-preparation priority**: practise complete papers to that budget, with particular attention to
the higher-tariff evaluation questions, which typically sit towards the end of each paper and reward
planning as much as content knowledge.

## Concepts need to be defined and then applied to a specific example

Sociological concepts -- socialisation, social control, stratification, subculture -- score well when
defined precisely and then applied to a named, specific example, and score poorly when used as
unexplained vocabulary. **Exam-preparation priority**: for each key concept, rehearse a one-sentence
definition paired with a one-sentence application to a concrete case from the syllabus.

## Evaluation questions want both sides taken seriously, then a judgement

The higher-tariff questions on both papers reward weighing perspectives against each other rather than
presenting one view at length. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise the structure -- position,
supporting evidence, the strongest counter-position, and a reasoned judgement -- since content
knowledge alone rarely distinguishes strong answers from adequate ones at this level.

## Worked routine: applying a concept to a specific example

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper, specimen material or mark scheme (none of which existed for this new specification at the
time of writing).

```
Step 1 - define the concept precisely, in one sentence:
The definition itself is creditable and disciplines what follows.

Step 2 - name a specific, concrete example:
A particular institution, practice or pattern -- not "society in
general".

Step 3 - explain how the example demonstrates the concept:
The connecting sentence -- this is where understanding, rather than
recall, is shown.

Step 4 - where relevant, note a methodological point:
How would a sociologist study this? What are the challenges of
researching it reliably?

Step 5 - link back to the question's specific demand:
A closing sentence confirming the point addresses what was asked.
```

Step 3 is the step that most reliably separates a strong answer from a list of correct but
disconnected facts: it is where the example is shown to demonstrate the concept, rather than merely
sitting beside it.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: revise research methods early and reuse its vocabulary across both papers;
  keep Paper 1 and Paper 2 content as genuinely separate revision areas; pair every concept definition
  with a specific applied example; practise the position-evidence-counter-judgement structure for
  evaluation questions.
- **During either paper**: budget roughly 1.5 minutes per mark and protect time for the higher-tariff
  evaluation questions.
- **In every concept-based answer**: define, exemplify, explain the connection, and close by answering
  the question's specific demand.
- **Given the new specification**: check with your teacher for any updated guidance or specimen
  materials as they become available, since this qualification is still in its early cohorts.
