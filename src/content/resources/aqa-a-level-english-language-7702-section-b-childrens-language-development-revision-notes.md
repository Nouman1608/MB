Condensed recall for **3.1.3 Children's language development** (ages 0 to 11), assessed by one discursive essay from
a choice of two in Paper 1 Section B (30 marks) of AQA A-level English Language (7702). Full explanations and a worked
transcript are in the
[children's language development study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-english-language-7702-section-b-childrens-language-development/).

## What you must cover

Functions of children's language; phonological, pragmatic, lexical, semantic and grammatical development; genres of
speech and writing; spoken, written and multimodal modes; theories and research.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Holophrase | A single word carrying the meaning of a whole utterance |
| Telegraphic speech | Content words with function words and inflections omitted |
| Virtuous error | A logical mistake from over-applying a rule ("runned", "sheeps") |
| Overextension / underextension | Word used too widely / too narrowly |
| Child-directed speech | Adapted adult speech: higher pitch, repetition, simple syntax, questions |
| Recast / expansion | Adult repeats the child's utterance in correct form / with added detail |
| Scaffolding | Support from a more knowledgeable person, withdrawn as the child gains skill |
| Zone of proximal development | Vygotsky: what a child can do with help but not yet alone |
| LAD | Chomsky's proposed innate Language Acquisition Device |
| LASS | Bruner's Language Acquisition Support System: the caregiver's supportive interaction |
| Phonological process | Regular simplification of adult sounds (deletion, substitution, assimilation) |
| Orthography | The spelling system of a language |

## Development checklist by level

**Phonological:** babbling (reduplicated, variegated); deletion ("ca" for "cat"); substitution such as fronting
("tat" for "cat"); assimilation ("gog" for "dog"); cluster reduction ("pider"); reduplication ("wawa"). Perception
ahead of production: Berko and Brown's "fis" phenomenon.

**Lexical and semantic:** early words mostly naming, then action, social and modifying words; vocabulary spurt;
over- and underextension (Rescorla: categorical, analogical, mismatch); Aitchison's labelling, packaging and
network building.

**Grammatical:** holophrastic, two-word, telegraphic, post-telegraphic; inflections "-ing" and plural "-s" early,
"-ed" and third-person "-s" later; Berko's "wug" test (1958); negatives from "no bed" to "I no want" to "I don't
want"; questions from intonation, to wh-word without inversion, to inversion.

**Pragmatic:** turn-taking; politeness routines; repair; awareness of the listener; Halliday's functions widening
from instrumental and regulatory towards representational and heuristic uses.

**Writing:** letter formation and orientation; phonetic and over-generalised spellings; punctuation acquired
gradually; Kroll's preparation, consolidation, differentiation, integration; genre awareness (story, recount,
instructions, letter); influence of teaching on structure and connectives.

## Halliday's functions (quick recall)

| Function | Child's purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Instrumental | Getting needs met | "want biscuit" |
| Regulatory | Controlling others | "stop it" |
| Interactional | Relating to others | "love you mummy" |
| Personal | Expressing self | "me sad" |
| Heuristic | Finding out | "what that?" |
| Imaginative | Pretending | "I'm a dragon" |
| Representational | Giving information | "doggy in garden" |

## Theories in one line each

- **Skinner (behaviourist):** imitation and reinforcement. Weakness: virtuous errors are not imitated.
- **Chomsky (nativist):** innate LAD; poverty of the stimulus. Weakness: underplays input and interaction.
- **Piaget (cognitive):** language follows concepts such as object permanence. Weakness: some language appears to
  outpace concepts.
- **Bruner and Vygotsky (interactionist):** scaffolding, LASS, zone of proximal development. Weakness: children
  acquire language even where child-directed speech is less common.

## Child-directed speech: what to look for in the adult's turns

| Feature | Example | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Questions handing over the turn | "And what's this?" | Invites the child to practise; scaffolding |
| Recasts | Child: "me goed"; adult: "you went, didn't you" | Models correct forms without correction |
| Expansions | Child: "big car"; adult: "yes, a big red car" | Extends grammar and vocabulary |
| Repetition and simple syntax | "Look, a ball. A big ball." | Makes structure easy to pick out |
| Exaggerated intonation | Rising pitch on key words | Holds attention and marks important words |

## Method for the essay

```
1. Read the question: what view must you evaluate?
2. Note the data's mode, genre and the child's age.
3. Annotate features by level, including the adult's language.
4. Match features to the view: which support it, which challenge it?
5. Bring in theory and research beyond the data.
6. Conclude with a qualified judgement.
```

## Exam traps

- Treating ages as fixed milestones.
- Listing theories without using the data.
- Mixing levels: calling "pider" a virtuous error (it is phonological) or "goed" a phonological process (it is
  grammatical).
- Ignoring the adult's turns, which often provide the best evidence for interactionist arguments.
- Writing about a child's written text only in terms of spelling.
- Forgetting to reach a judgement on the essay question.

## Self-test

1. List the five areas of study the specification names for 3.1.3, besides genres and modes.
2. What is a holophrase?
3. Which phonological process is "gog" for "dog"?
4. What did Berko's "wug" test show?
5. Give the typical three-step sequence for negatives.
6. What is the difference between a recast and an expansion?
7. Which theory does a virtuous error most directly challenge, and why?
8. What does LASS stand for, and whose term is it?
9. What is the zone of proximal development?
10. Name Kroll's four stages of writing development.
11. Classify "what that?" using Halliday's functions.
12. What is the "fis" phenomenon evidence of?

### Answers

1. Functions of children's language; phonological development; pragmatic development; lexical and semantic
   development; grammatical development; plus theories and research.
2. A single word used to express a whole utterance's meaning, such as "up" for "pick me up".
3. Assimilation.
4. That children apply grammatical rules, such as plural "-s", to invented words they have never heard.
5. "No" at the edge ("no bed"), negative inside the utterance ("I no want"), correct auxiliary ("I don't want").
6. A recast repeats the child's meaning in correct form; an expansion adds detail or grammar to extend it.
7. Behaviourism, because the child produces a form ("goed") that no adult models, so it cannot be pure imitation.
8. Language Acquisition Support System; Jerome Bruner.
9. Vygotsky's term for the gap between what a child can do alone and what they can do with help.
10. Preparation, consolidation, differentiation, integration.
11. Heuristic.
12. That children perceive adult sound distinctions before they can produce them.
