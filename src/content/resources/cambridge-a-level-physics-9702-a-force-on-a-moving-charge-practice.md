## Section A

**1.** An electron moves from left to right across the page through a uniform magnetic field directed out of the page. State the direction of the magnetic force on the electron. **[1]**

**2.** Explain why a charged particle moving at right angles to a uniform magnetic field follows a circular path at constant speed. **[3]**

**3.** An alpha particle (charge +3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) moves at 1.5 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹ in a uniform field of 1.2 T.

**(a)** Calculate the force on it when it moves perpendicular to the field. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the force when its velocity makes 30° with the field. **[1]**

**4.** A wire contains n charge carriers per unit volume, each of charge q and drift speed v. Starting from F = BIL and I = nAvq, show that the force on each carrier is Bqv when the wire is perpendicular to a field B. **[3]**

**5.** A Hall probe gives a Hall voltage V_H. State the effect on V_H of each change, with the others kept constant:

**(a)** the current through the slice is doubled

**(b)** a slice of half the thickness is used

**(c)** the semiconductor slice is replaced by a metal slice of the same size. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** An electron is accelerated from rest through a p.d. of 500 V. It then enters a uniform magnetic field of flux density 1.5 mT, moving at right angles to the field.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the electron. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the radius of its path in the field. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the time for one complete orbit. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why the kinetic energy of the electron does not change while it is in the magnetic field. **[1]**

**7.** A rectangular slice of semiconductor has width 4.0 mm and thickness 0.15 mm. It carries a current of 25 mA along its length. A magnetic field of 0.30 T acts perpendicular to its largest face. The number density of charge carriers is 1.2 × 10²² m⁻³, and each carrier has charge +1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.

**(a)** Explain how the Hall voltage arises. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the Hall voltage. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the drift speed of the carriers. **[2]**

**(d)** Show that, for your answers, the electric force on a carrier equals the magnetic force on it. **[2]**

**8.** Describe how a calibrated Hall probe is used to measure the flux density at a point between the poles of a magnet. **[4]**

**9.** A velocity selector has parallel plates 5.0 mm apart with a p.d. of 250 V between them, and a uniform magnetic field of 0.020 T perpendicular to the electric field.

**(a)** Explain why only particles of one speed pass through undeflected. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate this speed. **[2]**

**(c)** A particle with a higher speed enters the selector. State which force is larger and how this affects its path. **[1]**

**10.** Two singly charged positive ions, of masses 3.32 × 10⁻²⁶ kg and 3.65 × 10⁻²⁶ kg, leave a velocity selector with speed 2.5 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹. They enter a uniform field of 0.30 T at right angles to their motion and travel through a semicircle before striking a detector.

**(a)** Calculate the radius of the path of each ion. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the distance between the points where the two ions strike the detector. **[2]**

**11.** In a particle detector, the track of a particle with charge 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C is part of a circle of radius 0.12 m in a uniform field of 0.80 T perpendicular to the track.

**(a)** Calculate the momentum of the particle. **[2]**

**(b)** Further along, the track curves more tightly. Suggest why. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Conventional current is from right to left (opposite to the electron's motion), so the force is up the page [1].

**2.** The magnetic force is always perpendicular to the velocity, so it does no work and the speed is constant [1]. The force has constant magnitude BQv because B, Q and v are constant [1]. A force of constant size always perpendicular to the velocity provides a centripetal force, giving uniform circular motion [1].

**3. (a)** F = BQv = 1.2 × 3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 1.5 × 10⁷ [1] = 5.8 × 10⁻¹² N [1].

**(b)** F = 5.76 × 10⁻¹² × sin 30° = 2.9 × 10⁻¹² N [1].

**4.** Force on the wire F = BIL = B(nAvq)L [1]. Number of carriers in length L = nAL [1]. Force per carrier = B(nAvq)L ÷ (nAL) = Bqv [1].

**5. (a)** V_H doubles [1].

**(b)** V_H doubles [1].

**(c)** V_H becomes much smaller, because n for a metal is far larger [1].

**6. (a)** ½mv² = eV, so v = √(2eV/m) = √(2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 500 ÷ 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) [1] = 1.3 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** r = mv/(Be) = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 1.33 × 10⁷ ÷ (1.5 × 10⁻³ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) [1] = 0.050 m [1].

**(c)** T = 2πm/(Be) = 2.4 × 10⁻⁸ s (or 2πr/v) [1].

**(d)** The magnetic force is always perpendicular to the velocity, so it does no work on the electron [1].

**7. (a)** The moving carriers experience a magnetic force BQv at right angles to their motion and are pushed towards one edge of the slice [1]. Charge builds up on that edge, with opposite charge on the other edge, producing an electric field across the slice [1]. The build-up stops when the electric force on each carrier balances the magnetic force, leaving a steady p.d., the Hall voltage [1].

**(b)** V_H = BI/(ntq) = 0.30 × 0.025 ÷ (1.2 × 10²² × 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) [1] = 0.026 V [1].

**(c)** v = I/(nAq) with A = 4.0 × 10⁻³ × 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ = 6.0 × 10⁻⁷ m² [1]; v = 0.025 ÷ (1.2 × 10²² × 6.0 × 10⁻⁷ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) = 22 m s⁻¹ [1].

**(d)** E = V_H/d = 0.026 ÷ 4.0 × 10⁻³ = 6.5 V m⁻¹, so electric force qE = 1.04 × 10⁻¹⁸ N [1]. Magnetic force Bqv = 0.30 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 21.7 = 1.04 × 10⁻¹⁸ N, which is equal [1].

**8.** Place the probe at the point with the face of the slice in the field [1]. Rotate the probe until the Hall voltage reading is a maximum, so the face is perpendicular to the field [1]. Keep the current through the probe constant (the same as during calibration) [1]. Read V_H and use the calibration (V_H ∝ B) to find B [1].

**9. (a)** The electric force QE and the magnetic force BQv act in opposite directions [1]. The electric force is the same for all speeds, but the magnetic force is proportional to speed [1], so the forces balance, giving zero resultant force and no deflection, only when v = E/B [1].

**(b)** E = V/d = 250 ÷ 5.0 × 10⁻³ = 5.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹ [1]; v = E/B = 5.0 × 10⁴ ÷ 0.020 = 2.5 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹ [1].

**(c)** The magnetic force is larger, so the particle is deflected in the direction of the magnetic force (and misses the exit slit) [1].

**10. (a)** r = mv/(BQ): r₁ = 3.32 × 10⁻²⁶ × 2.5 × 10⁵ ÷ (0.30 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) = 0.173 m [1]; r₂ = 0.190 m [1].

**(b)** Each ion travels a semicircle, so lands 2r from the entry point [1]; separation = 2(0.190 − 0.173) = 0.034 m [1].

**11. (a)** p = BQr = 0.80 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 0.12 [1] = 1.5 × 10⁻²⁰ kg m s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** The particle loses kinetic energy (and momentum) as it moves through the detector, so r = p/(BQ) decreases [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Applying the left-hand rule to an electron without reversing the current.
- Saying the magnetic field speeds the particle up or does work on it.
- Using the slice width instead of its thickness in V_H = BI/(ntq).
- Forgetting to convert mT to T or mm to m.
- In selector questions, failing to say that the magnetic force depends on speed while the electric force does not.
- Forgetting that a particle crossing a semicircle lands a distance 2r, not r, from where it entered.
