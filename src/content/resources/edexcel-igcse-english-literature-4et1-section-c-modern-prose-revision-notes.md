## The task in one box

| Feature | Section C |
|---|---|
| Text | One novel from the modern prose list, **not** available in the exam |
| Choice | One question from two on your novel |
| Marks | 40 (the largest tariff on Paper 1) |
| Assessment objectives | AO1 (knowledge, argument, personal engagement) and AO4 (context) |
| Recommended time | 45 minutes |

**Set novels:** *To Kill a Mockingbird*, *Of Mice and Men*, *The Whale Rider*, *The Joy Luck Club*,
*Things Fall Apart*, *Klara and the Sun*, *Western Lane*. Set texts change with the specification
issue, so confirm yours.

## The four-moment plan

| Moment | What to show | Context question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | How the focus is first established | What was normal in this society? |
| Turning point | The event that tests or changes it | What pressures make this happen? |
| Development | Consequences and reactions | How do other characters reflect their society? |
| Ending | Where the writer leaves it | What does the writer want readers of the time, or now, to think? |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning in Section C |
|---|---|
| AO1 | A clear, sustained argument answering the question, with precise reference across the novel |
| AO4 | How the contexts of writing and setting shape the text's meaning |
| Overview | Your answer to the question in one or two opening sentences |
| Textual reference | A short quotation or a precise paraphrase of a specific moment |
| Embedded quotation | A quotation woven into your own sentence |
| Narrative voice | Who tells the story (first person, retrospective, an unusual narrator) and how that shapes what we see |
| Structure | The order of events: openings, turning points, endings, circular patterns |

## The quotation bank

- Fifteen to twenty short quotations for your novel.
- For each: where it appears, who says it, which themes and characters it can serve.
- Practise writing them from memory; an accurate paraphrase beats a misquotation.

## Context that works

- Name the specific condition (segregation, economic depression, colonial rule, migration, a debate
  about technology), not "the time".
- Link it to one moment in the text in the same sentence.
- Use it to explain **why** the writer shows something.

## Traps

- Plot summary instead of argument.
- A history paragraph at the end.
- Discussing one scene instead of the whole novel.
- Long, inaccurate quotations.
- Arriving at Section C with too little time.

## Self-test

1. Why does Section C demand a quotation bank when Sections A and B do not?
2. Which two assessment objectives are tested, and what does each require?
3. Rewrite as a stronger AO4 sentence: "People back then were unfair to workers."
4. What four moments make a reliable essay plan?
5. Which is better in the exam: a misremembered long quotation or a precise paraphrase?

**Answers:** 1. It is closed book: the novel is not available, whereas the unseen and anthology poems
are on or with the paper. 2. AO1, a sustained, informed argument with close reference to the text;
AO4, understanding of how the text relates to its contexts. 3. For example: "Because migrant
labourers in the Depression had no job security, Steinbeck shows Candy's fear of being sent away once
he can no longer work as a realistic, not exaggerated, anxiety." 4. Opening, turning point,
development, ending. 5. A precise paraphrase: it is accurate textual reference, while a misquotation
undermines your knowledge.
