## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term "irrecoverable debt recovered". **[2]**

**2.** Prepare the journal entry to write off a debt of $720 owed by B Akhtar. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why a customer's account is reinstated before the receipt of a debt previously written off is recorded. **[2]**

**4.** Trade receivables at the year end were $63,000 before writing off an irrecoverable debt of $1,000. The allowance for irrecoverable debts is to be 2% of trade receivables. The allowance at the start of the year was $1,100. Calculate the change in the allowance and state whether it is an expense or an income. **[3]**

**5.** Explain why only the change in the allowance for irrecoverable debts, not the full allowance, is charged to the statement of profit or loss each year. **[2]**

**6.** State how the allowance for irrecoverable debts is shown in the statement of financial position, and explain why. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Noor keeps an allowance for irrecoverable debts of 5% of trade receivables. On 1 January 2027 the allowance was $1,450.

- On 31 December 2027 trade receivables were $31,600, before writing off P Gill's debt of $1,600 as irrecoverable.
- On 31 December 2028 trade receivables, after writing off irrecoverable debts, were $26,000.

**(a)** Prepare the journal entries at 31 December 2027 to record the irrecoverable debt and the change in the allowance. **[4]**

**(b)** Prepare the allowance for irrecoverable debts account for the years ended 31 December 2027 and 31 December 2028. **[6]**

**(c)** Prepare an extract from the statement of financial position at 31 December 2028 showing trade receivables. **[2]**

**8.** In 2026 Yusuf wrote off a debt of $900 owed by C Ortiz. On 5 May 2028 C Ortiz paid the $900 in full by bank transfer.

**(a)** Prepare C Ortiz's account and the irrecoverable debts recovered account for the year ended 31 December 2028. **[5]**

**(b)** Explain **one** benefit to Yusuf of reinstating the debt in C Ortiz's account. **[2]**

**9.** Grace's draft profit for the year ended 31 December 2027 was $35,200. These items have not been recorded:

1. Irrecoverable debts of $760 are to be written off.

2. The allowance for irrecoverable debts, $1,950 at 1 January 2027, is to be 4% of the remaining trade receivables of $42,500.

3. $300 was received in cash for a debt written off two years ago.

**(a)** Calculate Grace's corrected profit for the year. **[4]**

**(b)** Prepare an extract from her statement of financial position at 31 December 2027 showing trade receivables. **[2]**

**10.** Experience shows that about 3% of Raj's trade receivables are not collected. Raj wants to make the allowance 15% this year so that his profit, and the tax he pays, are lower.

**(a)** State the effect on profit for the year of setting the allowance too high. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why Raj's plan is not appropriate, naming the accounting concept involved. **[3]**

**11.** A business has trade receivables of $20,000 and an allowance for irrecoverable debts of $600. A customer owing $500 is declared bankrupt and the debt is written off. The allowance is kept at 3% of the remaining trade receivables.

Calculate the new allowance and the total effect of the write-off and the allowance adjustment on profit for the year. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** A debt that was written off as irrecoverable in an earlier period [1] and is later paid by the customer, in full or in part [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Dr Irrecoverable debts $720 [1], Cr B Akhtar $720 [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Reinstating the debt gives the customer's account a complete record of what was owed and paid [1], which helps the business decide whether to give the customer credit again [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Receivables after write-off $62,000; allowance 2% × $62,000 = $1,240 [1]. Change $1,240 − $1,100 = $140 increase [1], which is an expense [1]. **[3]**

**5.** The allowance brought forward has already been charged in earlier years [1], so charging the full amount again would count the same expected loss twice and understate profit [1]. **[2]**

**6.** It is deducted from trade receivables under current assets [1], so that receivables are shown at the amount the business expects to collect (prudence) [1]. **[2]**

**7.** **(a)**

```
                                                    Debit $   Credit $
31 Dec 2027  Irrecoverable debts                     1,600
               P Gill                                           1,600
31 Dec 2027  Statement of profit or loss                50
               Allowance for irrecoverable debts                   50
```

Write-off entry [2]. Allowance required 5% × ($31,600 − $1,600) = $1,500; increase $50 [1]; entry Dr Statement of profit or loss, Cr Allowance [1].

**(b)**

```
Allowance for irrecoverable debts account
Date     Details                        $  Date     Details                        $
2027                                       2027
31 Dec   Balance c/d                1,500  1 Jan    Balance b/d                1,450
                                           31 Dec   Profit or loss                50
                                    -----                                      -----
                                    1,500                                      1,500
                                    =====                                      =====
2028                                       2028
31 Dec   Profit or loss               200  1 Jan    Balance b/d                1,500
31 Dec   Balance c/d                1,300
                                    -----                                      -----
                                    1,500                                      1,500
                                    =====                                      =====
                                           2029
                                           1 Jan    Balance b/d                1,300
```

2027: opening $1,450 [1]; increase $50 [1]; balance $1,500 carried and brought down [1]. 2028: decrease $200 on the debit side [1]; balance c/d $1,300 (5% × $26,000) [1]; balance b/d on 1 January 2029 [1].

**(c)** Trade receivables $26,000, less allowance for irrecoverable debts $1,300 [1], = $24,700 [1]. **[12]**

**8.** **(a)**

```
C Ortiz account
Date     Details                        $  Date     Details                        $
2028                                       2028
5 May    Irrecoverable debts          900  5 May    Bank                         900
           recovered
```

```
Irrecoverable debts recovered account
Date     Details                        $  Date     Details                        $
2028                                       2028
31 Dec   Profit or loss               900  5 May    C Ortiz                      900
```

C Ortiz debited with irrecoverable debts recovered [1] and credited with bank [1]; irrecoverable debts recovered credited with C Ortiz [1]; transfer of $900 to profit or loss on the debit side [1]; dates and amounts correct throughout [1].

**(b)** The account shows that C Ortiz did eventually pay [1], so if he asks for credit again Yusuf can see his full payment history and decide on sensible credit terms [1]. **[7]**

**9.** **(a)** $35,200 − $760 [1]. Allowance required 4% × $42,500 = $1,700; decrease $1,950 − $1,700 = $250, added [1]. Recovered debt $300 added [1]. Corrected profit **$34,990** [1].

**(b)** Trade receivables $42,500, less allowance $1,700 [1], = $40,800 [1]. **[6]**

**10.** **(a)** Profit for the year is understated [1].

**(b)** Prudence requires that assets and profit are not overstated [1], but it does not permit deliberately understating them [1]. The allowance should be a realistic estimate based on experience, about 3%; a 15% allowance would make the statements misleading to users and would understate tax due [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Remaining receivables $20,000 − $500 = $19,500; new allowance 3% × $19,500 = **$585** [1]. Decrease in allowance $600 − $585 = $15, an income [1]. Irrecoverable debt $500, an expense [1]. Total effect: profit reduced by $500 − $15 = **$485** [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Charging the whole allowance each year instead of the change.
- Calculating the percentage before writing off irrecoverable debts.
- Recording a recovered debt without reinstating the customer's account.
- Showing the allowance as a liability.
- Recording a decrease in the allowance as an expense.
