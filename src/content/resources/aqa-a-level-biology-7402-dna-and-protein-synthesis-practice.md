These questions cover AQA A-level Biology (7402) section 3.4.2, *DNA and protein synthesis*. Experimental data are invented. Use only the codons given: AUG = Met (start), UUU = Phe, GGC = Gly, AAA = Lys, UGG = Trp, CAU = His, GCU = Ala, CCA = Pro, UAG = stop. Teaching is in the [DNA and protein synthesis study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-dna-and-protein-synthesis/) and the [DNA and protein synthesis revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-dna-and-protein-synthesis-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** **(a)** Define the term *genome*. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the enzyme that joins RNA nucleotides during transcription. **[1]**

**2.** Give two differences between the structure of a DNA molecule and an mRNA molecule. **[2]**

**3.** The template strand of a section of DNA reads:

TAC AAA CCG TTT ACC ATC

**(a)** Give the mRNA sequence transcribed from this template. **[1]**

**(b)** Use the codons given to write the amino acid sequence. **[1]**

**(c)** Give the anticodon of the tRNA that carries the fourth amino acid. **[1]**

**4.** The tRNA molecules used to translate a short mRNA had the anticodons GUA and then CGA. Give the DNA template strand sequence and the two amino acids coded. **[2]**

**5.** A eukaryotic pre-mRNA is 2,950 nucleotides long. Splicing removes introns totalling 1,780 nucleotides. The mature mRNA contains 75 nucleotides that are not translated, outside the region from the start codon to the stop codon inclusive. Calculate the number of amino acids in the polypeptide. **[3]**

**6.** Explain why the mRNA used in translation in a eukaryotic cell is shorter than the gene it was transcribed from. **[2]**

## Section B: longer questions

**7.** Describe how a molecule of mRNA is produced from a gene in a eukaryotic cell. **[5]**

**8.** Describe the roles of tRNA and ribosomes in translation. **[4]**

**9.** Explain the role of ATP in translation. **[2]**

**10.** Bacterial cells were treated with one of two substances. Substance X binds to ribosomes and stops them working. Substance Y inhibits RNA polymerase. The rate of protein synthesis was measured.

| Time after treatment / min | Substance X (% of control) | Substance Y (% of control) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 100 | 100 |
| 5 | 8 | 81 |
| 20 | 3 | 36 |

**(a)** Explain why substance X reduced protein synthesis faster than substance Y. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage decrease in protein synthesis between 5 and 20 minutes with substance Y. **[1]**

**11.** A muscle cell and a nerve cell from the same person contain the same genome but have different proteomes. Explain why. **[2]**

**12.** A scientist knows the amino acid sequence of a polypeptide. Explain why she cannot work out the exact base sequence of the gene from it. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The complete set of genes in a cell [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** RNA polymerase [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Any two [1 each]: DNA is double-stranded, mRNA single-stranded; DNA contains deoxyribose, mRNA ribose; DNA contains thymine, mRNA uracil; DNA is much longer; DNA has hydrogen bonds between base pairs along its length, mRNA has none. **[2]**

**3. (a)** AUG UUU GGC AAA UGG UAG [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Met – Phe – Gly – Lys – Trp (then stop) [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** The fourth codon is AAA, so the anticodon is **UUU** [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* Check that there is no T in your mRNA, and that the stop codon is not counted as an amino acid.

**4.** Codons are CAU and GCU, so the DNA template is **GTA CGA** [1]; amino acids **His and Ala** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** mRNA = 2,950 − 1,780 = 1,170 nucleotides [1]. Translated region = 1,170 − 75 = 1,095 nucleotides = 365 codons [1]. Subtract the stop codon: **364 amino acids** [1]. **[3]**

**6.** The gene contains introns, which are transcribed into pre-mRNA [1] and then removed by splicing, so only the exons remain in the mRNA [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Hydrogen bonds between the DNA strands break, separating them at the gene [1]. One strand acts as a template [1]. Free RNA nucleotides pair with complementary bases on the template (with uracil pairing with adenine) [1]. RNA polymerase joins adjacent RNA nucleotides, forming pre-mRNA [1]. Pre-mRNA is spliced: introns are removed and exons joined to form mRNA [1]. **[5]**

**8.** tRNA: each tRNA carries a specific amino acid [1] and has an anticodon that pairs with the complementary codon on the mRNA, so amino acids are placed in the correct order [1]. Ribosomes: hold the mRNA and two tRNAs in position [1], and move along the mRNA one codon at a time while peptide bonds form between adjacent amino acids [1]. **[4]**

**9.** ATP is hydrolysed to release energy [1] for attaching amino acids to tRNA and for forming peptide bonds [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** Substance X stops translation directly, so protein synthesis falls almost at once (to 8% in 5 minutes) [1]. Substance Y stops new mRNA being made [1], but mRNA already present can still be translated until it is broken down, so synthesis falls more slowly (81% at 5 minutes, 36% at 20 minutes) [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** (81 − 36) ÷ 81 × 100 = **55.6%** [1]. **[1]**

**11.** Different genes are transcribed and translated in each type of cell [1], so the range of proteins produced differs, even though the genes present are the same [1]. **[2]**

**12.** The genetic code is degenerate: most amino acids are coded for by more than one triplet [1], so several different base sequences could code for the same amino acid sequence; also introns would not be shown [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing T in mRNA, or copying the template strand as if it were the mRNA.
- Naming DNA polymerase instead of RNA polymerase.
- Saying splicing removes exons.
- Counting the stop codon as an amino acid.
- Describing inhibitor data without quoting values.
