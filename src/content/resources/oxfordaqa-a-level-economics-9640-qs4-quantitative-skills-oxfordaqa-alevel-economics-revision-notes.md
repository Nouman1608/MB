These notes condense quantitative skill QS4, "construct and interpret a range of standard graphical forms", from section 3.5 of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Economics (9640) specification, which says the quantitative skills could be assessed in any of the assessments. For full worked examples, read the [QS4 study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs4-quantitative-skills-oxfordaqa-alevel-economics/); to practise, use the [QS4 practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs4-quantitative-skills-oxfordaqa-alevel-economics-practice/). All figures are invented.

## Construction rules

1. Pick the form that answers the question (trend, comparison, share, relationship).
2. Choose scales that use most of the space, with equal steps; mark 0 or show a break if the axis does not start at 0.
3. Label each axis with the variable and its unit.
4. Plot accurately; join points only when the data form a continuous relationship.
5. Label every curve or line, and every equilibrium or key point.
6. Refer to the labels in your written answer.

## Forms the specification content relies on

| Form | Axes | Key reading |
|---|---|---|
| Production possibility diagram (3.1.1.4) | Good A (horizontal), good B (vertical) | Gradient = opportunity cost; inside = unused resources; outward shift = growth |
| Lorenz curve (3.3.5.1) | Cumulative % of households, cumulative % of income | Further from the 45° line = more unequal; higher Gini |
| Consumption function (3.2.2) | National income, consumption | Gradient = MPC |
| AD/AS diagram (3.2.2.2) | Real national output, price level | Shifts change P and Y; vertical LRAS shows capacity |
| Supply and demand (3.1.2) | Quantity, price | Equilibrium where curves cross |
| Cost curves (3.3.2) | Output, cost | Gradient of total cost = marginal cost |
| Bar chart with negatives | Year, balance | Bars below 0 are deficits |

## Gradient method

```
gradient = change in vertical variable / change in horizontal variable
```

Choose two points far apart on a straight line. Keep the units: a consumption function rising 80 cu billion for each 100 cu billion of income has gradient 0.8, the MPC.

## Shapes and what they mean

| Shape | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Straight production possibility boundary | Constant opportunity cost |
| Concave (bowed out) boundary | Rising opportunity cost: resources not equally suited to both goods |
| Lorenz curve on the diagonal | Perfect equality, Gini = 0 |
| Lorenz curve sagging far below the diagonal | High inequality, Gini closer to 1 |
| Two Lorenz curves crossing | No simple ranking of inequality |
| Steepening line on a level graph | Growth rate increasing |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gradient | Rate of change of the vertical variable with the horizontal one |
| Intercept | Where a line meets an axis |
| Line of equality | The 45° line on a Lorenz diagram |
| Concave | Bowed outwards from the origin |
| Shift | The whole curve moves because a non-axis variable changes |
| Movement along | A change in a variable on the axis |

## Exam traps

- AD/AS axes labelled "price" and "quantity" instead of "price level" and "real national output".
- A Lorenz curve that does not start at (0, 0) and end at (100, 100).
- No line of equality drawn on a Lorenz diagram.
- Joining production possibility points with straight segments when a smooth curve is intended.
- Reading a gradient from two close points.
- Calling a narrower deficit a "surplus".
- An unlabelled second curve, so the shift cannot be credited.
- Describing a diagram in the text without referring to its labels.

## Self-test

1. What labels go on the axes of an AD/AS diagram?
2. On a production possibility diagram, food rises from 10 to 20 thousand units and machines fall from 48 to 42 thousand. Find the opportunity cost of one unit of food.
3. What does a point inside a production possibility boundary show?
4. What does a concave production possibility boundary show about opportunity cost?
5. Income shares of the five quintiles are 8%, 12%, 16%, 24% and 40%. Give the cumulative shares for a Lorenz curve.
6. What line must be drawn on every Lorenz diagram?
7. Consumption rises from 50 to 110 cu billion as income rises from 100 to 200 cu billion. Find the MPC from the graph.
8. What can you conclude if two Lorenz curves cross?
9. A current account balance moves from −3% to −1% of GDP. Describe the change.
10. Data run from 0 to 450 and the axis is 15 cm long. Suggest a scale.
11. How is economic growth shown on a production possibility diagram?
12. What does the gradient of a total cost curve represent?

### Answers

1. Vertical: price level. Horizontal: real national output (or real GDP).
2. 6/10 = 0.6 machines per unit of food.
3. Resources are unemployed or used inefficiently.
4. Opportunity cost rises as more of a good is produced.
5. 8%, 20%, 36%, 60%, 100%.
6. The line of equality from (0, 0) to (100, 100).
7. 60/100 = 0.6.
8. Neither distribution is unambiguously more equal.
9. The deficit narrowed by 2 percentage points but it is still a deficit.
10. 30 per cm (or 50 per cm using 9 cm), with equal steps.
11. An outward shift of the whole boundary.
12. Marginal cost.
