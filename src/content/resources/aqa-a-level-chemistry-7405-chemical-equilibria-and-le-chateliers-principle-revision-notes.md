These notes condense section **3.1.6.1 Chemical equilibria and Le Chatelier's principle** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Papers 1 and 2 (and Paper 3). The [Chemical equilibria study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-chemical-equilibria-and-le-chateliers-principle/) explains each idea with worked examples, and the [practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-chemical-equilibria-and-le-chateliers-principle-practice/) give exam-style practice.

## What the specification asks

- Many reactions are **reversible**.
- At equilibrium the **forward and reverse rates are equal** and **concentrations remain constant**.
- **Le Chatelier's principle** predicts the effect of changes in temperature, pressure and concentration on the position of equilibrium in homogeneous reactions.
- **A catalyst does not affect the position** of equilibrium.
- **Explain** why a compromise temperature and pressure may be used in industry.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reversible reaction | One in which products can react to re-form the reactants (⇌) |
| Dynamic equilibrium | Forward and reverse reactions continue at equal rates in a closed system |
| Closed system | No matter enters or leaves |
| Position of equilibrium | The relative amounts of reactants and products at equilibrium |
| Le Chatelier's principle | A system at equilibrium shifts to oppose a change |
| Homogeneous | All species in the same phase |
| Compromise conditions | Conditions balancing yield, rate and cost |

## Dynamic equilibrium: required points

1. Closed system.
2. Forward rate = reverse rate.
3. Concentrations of reactants and products constant.
4. Both reactions continue ("dynamic").
5. Reached from either direction.

## Le Chatelier's principle: the shifts

| Change | Shift |
|---|---|
| Increase [reactant] | Right (towards products) |
| Decrease [reactant] | Left |
| Increase [product] or remove product | Left, or right if product removed |
| Increase pressure | Towards the side with fewer moles of gas |
| Decrease pressure | Towards the side with more moles of gas |
| Increase temperature | In the endothermic direction |
| Decrease temperature | In the exothermic direction |
| Add catalyst | No shift |

Equal moles of gas on both sides: pressure does not change the position.

## Haber process summary

N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)  ΔH = −92 kJ mol⁻¹

| Factor | Favours yield | Problem | Compromise |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature | Low (exothermic) | Rate too slow | About 450 °C |
| Pressure | High (4 moles to 2) | Cost, safety, equipment | About 200 atm |
| Catalyst | No effect on yield | Cost, poisoning | Iron |
| Recycling | Unreacted gases returned | Extra plant | Used |

## Other industrial example

C₂H₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ C₂H₅OH(g)  ΔH = −45 kJ mol⁻¹

- Exothermic and fewer moles of gas on the right: low temperature and high pressure favour the yield.
- Compromise: about 300 °C and 60–70 atm with a phosphoric acid catalyst.
- Very high pressure would cost more and cause polymerisation of ethene.

## Worked reminders

**Colour change.** [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ (pink) + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻ (blue) + 6H₂O. Adding concentrated HCl raises [Cl⁻], so the position shifts right and the solution turns blue. Adding water shifts it left (pink).

**Equal moles.** H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g) has 2 moles of gas on each side, so pressure does not shift the position.

**Rate and position.** For an exothermic reaction a higher temperature increases both rates, but the position shifts to the left.

**Catalyst.** Both forward and reverse activation energies fall by the same amount. Equilibrium is reached sooner. The composition is the same.

**Effect on yield versus rate.** A high pressure raises the Haber yield and also increases the rate, but costs more.

## Answer pattern for an industrial compromise

1. State the condition that favours the yield and why (Le Chatelier).
2. State the disadvantage (rate, cost, safety).
3. State the compromise condition used.
4. Mention the catalyst and its effect on rate only.

## Common mistakes

- Saying reactions stop at equilibrium.
- Omitting the closed system.
- Saying the position shifts to "increase" a change.
- Applying pressure to a reaction with equal moles of gas.
- Saying a catalyst increases the yield.
- Counting solids and liquids when counting moles of gas.
- Forgetting that temperature changes the rate as well as the position.
- Saying that Le Chatelier's principle explains why equilibrium is reached faster.

## Self-test

1. State two features of a dynamic equilibrium.
2. State Le Chatelier's principle.
3. For N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g), ΔH = +58 kJ mol⁻¹, state the effect of increasing the pressure on the position.
4. For the same reaction, state the effect of increasing the temperature.
5. For 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), ΔH = −98 kJ mol⁻¹, state the effect of removing SO₃.
6. State the effect of a catalyst on the position of equilibrium.
7. Explain why a catalyst does not change the position.
8. Why is 450 °C used for the Haber process rather than 100 °C?
9. Why is a pressure of about 200 atm used and not 1000 atm?
10. How is the overall yield of ammonia increased in the industrial process?
11. For H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g), state the effect of increasing the pressure on the position.
12. Describe what you would see when water is added to a blue solution containing [CoCl₄]²⁻, and explain.

### Answers

1. Forward and reverse reactions occur at the same rate; concentrations are constant (in a closed system).
2. When a system at equilibrium is subjected to a change, the position shifts to oppose the change.
3. The position shifts to the left (towards N₂O₄), because there are fewer moles of gas on the left.
4. The forward reaction is endothermic, so the position shifts to the right (towards NO₂).
5. The position shifts to the right, replacing the SO₃ removed.
6. No effect.
7. It lowers the activation energy of the forward and reverse reactions equally, so both rates rise by the same proportion.
8. At 100 °C the rate would be too slow, even though the yield would be higher.
9. Very high pressures need stronger, more expensive equipment, use more energy and are more hazardous.
10. Unreacted nitrogen and hydrogen are recycled, and the ammonia is removed by liquefying it.
11. No effect on the position, because there are equal moles of gas on both sides.
12. The solution turns from blue to pink; adding water dilutes the chloride, so the position shifts to the left, to the pink hexaaqua complex.
