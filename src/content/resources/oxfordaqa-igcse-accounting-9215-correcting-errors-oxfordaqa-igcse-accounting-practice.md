These questions cover "How to correct errors in double entry records" from section 3.2 of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Accounting (9215) specification, version 1.2, which includes the use of the general journal and the suspense account. Narratives are required only where a question asks for them. All amounts are in dollars. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured ones.

Revise first with the [correcting errors study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-correcting-errors-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting/) and the [correcting errors revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-correcting-errors-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Which one of these errors would be corrected using a suspense account?

A: a credit sale entered in neither account

B: the electricity account undercast by 100

C: a payment to S Rana debited to S Rani's account

D: a cash purchase of stationery debited to the furniture account

**[1]**

**2.** A receipt of 530 from the customer D Mensah was credited to the account of D Mensa. Prepare the correcting journal entry. Narratives are not required. **[2]**

**3.** A trial balance has debits of 63,480 and credits of 64,050. State the side on which the suspense account opens and the amount. **[2]**

**4.** A credit purchase of 1,270 from Vale Supplies was not recorded in the books. Prepare the correcting journal entry. Narratives are not required. **[2]**

**5.** A cash sale of 380 was debited to the sales account and credited to the cash account. Prepare the correcting journal entry. Narratives are not required. **[2]**

**6.** Write a suitable narrative for the correcting journal in question 5. **[1]**

### Section B

**7.** Prepare journal entries to correct the following errors. Narratives are not required.

**(a)** Sales returns of 340 from the customer P Quist were debited to P Quist's account and credited to the sales returns account. **[2]**

**(b)** Rent of 750 paid by bank was debited to the rates account. **[2]**

**(c)** The sales journal was overcast by 1,000. **[2]**

**(d)** Goods costing 200 taken by the owner for her own use were not recorded. **[2]**

**(e)** Bank interest received of 65 was debited to the bank account and also debited to the interest received account. **[2]**

**8.** The trial balance of Penrose Electrical at 31 March has debits of 86,410 and credits of 87,130. The difference was entered in a suspense account. These errors were then found:

1. The purchases returns journal was undercast by 250.
2. A payment of 980 to a supplier was credited to the bank account but not debited to the supplier's account.
3. The insurance account was overcast by 40.
4. Commission received of 430 by bank was credited to the commission received account as 340.
5. Discount allowed of 120 was credited to the customer's account but not entered in the discount allowed account.
6. A credit sale of 600 to L Hart was debited to the account of L Hartley.

**(a)** Prepare the journal entries to correct errors 1 to 5. Narratives are not required. **[5]**

**(b)** Prepare the suspense account, showing the opening entry and the corrections. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why error 6 does not appear in the suspense account. **[1]**

**9.** A credit sale of 1,250 to M Okafor was debited to M Okafor's account as 1,520 and credited to the sales account as 1,205. Prepare the single journal entry to correct the error. Narratives are not required. **[3]**

**10.** After a business has corrected all the errors it found, its suspense account still has a credit balance of 140. Explain what this shows and what the business should do. **[2]**

**11.** **(a)** Shelving costing 2,400, paid by bank, was debited to the purchases account. Name the error and prepare the correcting journal entry. **[2]**

**(b)** A sales invoice for 3,860 to G Bello was entered in the sales journal as 3,680 and posted from there. Prepare the correcting journal entry. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why neither correction in (a) or (b) involves the suspense account. **[1]**

**12.** The sales journal for May was undercast by 600. A student corrects the error by debiting each customer's account and crediting the sales account. Explain why the student is wrong, and give the correct journal entry. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]

**2.** Debit **D Mensa 530** [1]; credit **D Mensah 530** [1]

**3.** Credits are higher by 64,050 − 63,480 = 570 [1]; suspense opens as a **debit of 570** [1]

**4.** Debit **Purchases 1,270** [1]; credit **Vale Supplies 1,270** [1]

**5.** Debit **Cash 760** [1]; credit **Sales 760** [1]

*Examiner insight:* this is a complete reversal; 380 would only cancel the wrong entries, so the correct entries need another 380.

**6.** "Being correction of a cash sale of 380 entered on the wrong sides of the cash and sales accounts" [1]

**7. (a)** Debit **Sales returns 680** [1]; credit **P Quist 680** [1]

**(b)** Debit **Rent 750** [1]; credit **Rates 750** [1]

**(c)** Debit **Sales 1,000** [1]; credit **Suspense 1,000** [1]

**(d)** Debit **Drawings 200** [1]; credit **Purchases 200** [1]

**(e)** Debit **Suspense 130** [1]; credit **Interest received 130** [1] **[10]**

**8. (a)** 1: Dr Suspense 250, Cr Purchases returns 250 [1]

2: Dr Supplier 980, Cr Suspense 980 [1]

3: Dr Suspense 40, Cr Insurance 40 [1]

4: Dr Suspense 90, Cr Commission received 90 [1]

5: Dr Discount allowed 120, Cr Suspense 120 [1]

**(b)**

```
Dr                       Suspense                             Cr
31 Mar  Difference in TB     720  | 31 Mar  Supplier              980
31 Mar  Purchases returns    250  | 31 Mar  Discount allowed      120
31 Mar  Insurance             40  |
31 Mar  Commission received   90  |
                           1,100  |                             1,100
```

Opening debit of 720 [1]; corrections on the correct sides [1]; totals 1,100 with no balance remaining [1]

**(c)** It is an error of commission: the debit and credit were equal, so it did not cause the trial balance difference [1] **[9]**

**9.** Credit **M Okafor 270** (1,520 − 1,250) [1]; credit **Sales 45** (1,250 − 1,205) [1]; debit **Suspense 315** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the customer's account was overstated and the sales account understated, so both corrections are credits; suspense takes the total of 315 on the debit side.

**10.** At least one error causing the trial balance difference **has not yet been found** [1]; the business should keep checking the ledger (for example additions, postings and balances) until the suspense account clears to nil [1]

**11. (a)** **Error of principle** [1]; debit **Shelving (fixtures and fittings) 2,400**, credit **Purchases 2,400** [1]

**(b)** Debit **G Bello 180**, credit **Sales 180** [1]

**(c)** In both cases the debit and credit entries were for the same amount, so the trial balance totals agreed and there was no difference to place in suspense [1] **[4]**

**12.** The customer accounts were posted from the individual invoices, so they are already correct [1]; only the total posted to the sales account was wrong, so debiting the customers would make their accounts wrong [1]; correct journal: debit **Suspense 600**, credit **Sales 600** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using suspense for errors that left the trial balance totals equal.
- Correcting complete reversals or wrong-side entries with the single amount.
- Opening the suspense account on the wrong side.
- Changing personal accounts for a day book casting error.
- Leaving the suspense account unbalanced or without totals.
