These questions cover section 6.3 Inter-business comparison of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. Give percentages to two decimal places. Revise first with the [inter-business comparison study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-inter-business-comparison/) and the [inter-business comparison revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-inter-business-comparison-revision-notes/). All businesses and figures are invented, and amounts are in dollars.

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** State **two** factors that may cause two businesses in the same trade to have different gross profit margins. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why different year-end dates are a problem when comparing two businesses. **[2]**

**3.** Business P made a profit of $21,000 on revenue of $140,000. Business Q made a profit of $33,000 on revenue of $275,000. Calculate each profit margin. **[2]**

**4.** Two businesses have identical trading results. Which would make Business A's ROCE lower than Business B's?

A: A rents its premises and B owns its premises

B: A owns its premises and B rents its premises

C: A pays loan interest and B does not

D: A has a later year-end date

**[1]**

**5.** Explain how different credit policies can affect the trade receivables turnover of two businesses. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** problems of inter-business comparison. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Two cafés provide the following for the same year.

| | Café Rosa | Café Sol |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 160,000 | 240,000 |
| Cost of sales | 56,000 | 108,000 |
| Profit for the year | 24,000 | 28,800 |
| Average inventory | 2,400 | 5,400 |

**(a)** Calculate for each café the gross profit margin, the profit margin and the inventory turnover in days. **[6]**

**(b)** Café Rosa is in a city centre and charges high prices. Café Sol is beside a main road and sells cheaper meals to many travellers. Explain how location and pricing policy may explain the differences in the ratios. **[4]**

**8.** Hartley Tools and Dunmore Tools are similar shops with no loans.

| | Hartley Tools | Dunmore Tools |
|---|---|---|
| Profit for the year | 36,000 | 21,000 |
| Capital employed | 240,000 | 90,000 |

Hartley owns its premises, which have a book value of $150,000; its expenses include $3,000 depreciation on them. Dunmore rents similar premises for $18,000 a year.

**(a)** Calculate the ROCE of each shop. **[2]**

**(b)** Recalculate Hartley's ROCE as if it rented its premises at $18,000 a year. **[3]**

**(c)** Comment on your results. **[2]**

**9.** Firm M and Firm N each buy equipment for $50,000 at the start of year 1. Firm M depreciates at 10% a year on the straight line basis. Firm N depreciates at 30% a year on the reducing balance. Each firm makes a profit of $40,000 a year before depreciation.

**(a)** Calculate the depreciation charged by each firm in year 1 and year 2. **[4]**

**(b)** Calculate the profit for year 1 of each firm. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why this makes it difficult to compare the two firms. **[2]**

**10.** Kemp's Furnishings is a sole trader business; the owner works full time and takes drawings instead of a salary. Lark Furnishings Ltd pays its managing director a salary of $18,000, included in expenses. Both had revenue of $230,000. Kemp's profit for the year was $46,000 and Lark's was $28,000.

**(a)** Calculate each business's profit margin. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate Lark's profit margin before the director's salary. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the unadjusted profit margins give a misleading comparison. **[2]**

**11.** Two garden centres have different year-end dates. Garden Centre A's year ends on 31 March, just before its busy spring season. Garden Centre B's year ends on 30 September, after the season.

| | A | B |
|---|---|---|
| Current ratio | 3.20 : 1 | 1.80 : 1 |
| Acid test ratio | 0.70 : 1 | 1.40 : 1 |

**(a)** Explain how the year-end dates may explain the differences in these ratios. **[4]**

**(b)** Suggest **two** ways a fairer comparison of the two businesses could be made. **[2]**

**12.** An investor must choose between X Ltd and Y Ltd. X Ltd's ROCE is 18% and Y Ltd's is 12%. X Ltd uses reducing balance depreciation and its machinery is ten years old; Y Ltd uses straight line depreciation and bought new machinery last year. Their year-end dates are different. Evaluate whether the investor should choose X Ltd on the basis of ROCE alone. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: different pricing policies [1]; different buying power or bulk discounts [1]; different product mix; different inventory valuation; location affecting the prices customers accept. **[2]**

**2.** Inventory, receivables and cash vary during a year, especially in seasonal trades [1], so the two statements of financial position may show the businesses at different points in their cycle, making liquidity and efficiency ratios hard to compare [1]. **[2]**

**3.** P: 21,000 / 140,000 x 100 = **15.00%** [1]. Q: 33,000 / 275,000 x 100 = **12.00%** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** B [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* Owned premises add to capital employed. Interest is added back for ROCE, so C does not change it.

**5.** A business offering long credit periods to attract customers [1] will have higher trade receivables days than one that sells mainly for cash or insists on prompt payment, even if both collect their debts on time [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two: different accounting policies [1]; differences in size, trade or location [1]; different year-end dates; limited information; historic cost; one year only; non-financial factors excluded. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Rosa: gross profit = 104,000; margin = 104,000 / 160,000 x 100 = **65.00%** [1]. Sol: gross profit = 132,000; margin = 132,000 / 240,000 x 100 = **55.00%** [1]. Profit margin: Rosa 24,000 / 160,000 x 100 = **15.00%** [1]; Sol 28,800 / 240,000 x 100 = **12.00%** [1]. Inventory turnover: Rosa 2,400 / 56,000 x 365 = **15.64 days** [1]; Sol 5,400 / 108,000 x 365 = **18.25 days** [1].

**(b)** Rosa's city-centre customers accept higher prices, so her gross profit margin is higher [1], although city-centre rent and wages are likely to be higher, which narrows the gap in profit margin [1]. Sol sells cheaper meals in large numbers, so it earns a lower margin on each sale but more revenue and more profit in dollars [1]; Sol may hold more inventory to serve its many travellers, which may explain its slower inventory turnover [1]. **[10]**

**8. (a)** Hartley: 36,000 / 240,000 x 100 = **15.00%** [1]. Dunmore: 21,000 / 90,000 x 100 = **23.33%** [1].

**(b)** Adjusted profit = 36,000 + 3,000 - 18,000 = $21,000 [1]. Adjusted capital employed = 240,000 - 150,000 = $90,000 [1]. ROCE = 21,000 / 90,000 x 100 = **23.33%** [1].

**(c)** Dunmore appears to earn a much higher return [1], but on a like-for-like basis both shops earn 23.33%, so the difference comes from Hartley owning its premises, not from better trading [1]. **[7]**

**9. (a)** M: year 1, 50,000 x 10% = **$5,000** [1]; year 2, **$5,000** [1]. N: year 1, 50,000 x 30% = **$15,000** [1]; year 2, (50,000 - 15,000) x 30% = **$10,500** [1].

**(b)** M: 40,000 - 5,000 = **$35,000** [1]. N: 40,000 - 15,000 = **$25,000** [1].

**(c)** The firms traded identically, but N's profit is $10,000 lower in year 1 [1] only because of its depreciation policy, so comparing profit or profit margins would wrongly suggest M performed better [1]. **[8]**

**10. (a)** Kemp: 46,000 / 230,000 x 100 = **20.00%** [1]. Lark: 28,000 / 230,000 x 100 = **12.17%** [1].

**(b)** (28,000 + 18,000) / 230,000 x 100 = **20.00%** [1].

**(c)** Kemp's owner works without a salary, and drawings are not an expense, so Kemp's expenses are lower [1]; once the salary is removed, both earn 20%, so neither is more profitable [1]. **[5]**

**11. (a)** At 31 March, A has just bought large amounts of plants and goods for the spring [1], so inventory is high, which raises its current ratio to 3.20 : 1 [1]. Its acid test, which excludes inventory, is low at 0.70 : 1 because cash has been spent on that inventory [1]. B's year end comes after the season, when inventory has been sold and turned into cash, so its acid test is higher [1].

**(b)** Any two: compare each business with its own figures at the same point in its season [1]; use statements prepared to the same date, or average figures over the year [1]; compare several years' trends. **[6]**

**12.** For choosing X: an ROCE of 18% means X earns $18 for each $100 of capital employed, against $12 for Y [1]. Against: X's machinery is ten years old, so its low book value keeps capital employed small and raises ROCE [1]; Y's new machinery adds to its capital employed and lowers its ROCE now, but may make it more efficient in future [1]. The different depreciation methods change both profit and asset values [1], and different year ends mean the statements are not at the same date [1]. Judgement: the investor should not choose on ROCE alone; she should adjust for these differences, look at several years and consider other ratios and non-financial factors [1]. **[6]**
