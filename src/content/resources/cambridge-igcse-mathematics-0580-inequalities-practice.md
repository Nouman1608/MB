These questions cover subtopic **2.6 Inequalities** in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions 1 to 4 use Core content (C2.6), representing and interpreting inequalities, and suit every candidate. Questions marked **(Extended)** use E2.6 content: solving linear inequalities and working with regions in two variables. Number lines and regions are described in words so that you can sketch them; use the syllabus conventions (open or closed circles, broken or solid lines, shade the unwanted region).

For explanations, read the [inequalities study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-inequalities/); for a summary, use the [inequalities revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-inequalities-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** A number line shows an open circle at 1 with an arrow pointing to the left. Write down the inequality it represents. **[1]**

**2.** A number line shows a closed circle at −4 and an open circle at 2, joined by a line.

**(a)** Write down the inequality shown. **[1]**

**(b)** List the integers that satisfy it. **[1]**

**3.** Write each statement as an inequality.

**(a)** The maximum load of a lift, w kg, is 750 kg. **[1]**

**(b)** There are more than 30 students, s, in the hall. **[1]**

**4.** Describe how you would show −1 < x ≤ 3 on a number line. **[2]**

**5.** (Extended) Solve 9x − 4 ≤ 5x + 10. **[2]**

**6.** (Extended) Solve 3(x − 2) > 5x + 4. **[3]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** (Extended)

**(a)** Solve −7 ≤ 4x + 1 < 13. **[2]**

**(b)** List the integer values of x that satisfy the inequality. **[1]**

**8.** (Extended)

**(a)** Solve (2x − 1)/3 > (x + 2)/5. **[3]**

**(b)** Write down the smallest integer that satisfies the inequality. **[1]**

**9.** (Extended) A phone plan costs $12 per month plus 5 cents for each minute of calls. Leon wants to spend no more than $20 in a month.

**(a)** Write an inequality for the number of minutes, m, that Leon can use. **[1]**

**(b)** Solve your inequality and interpret the answer. **[2]**

**10.** (Extended) n is an integer that satisfies both 2n + 3 > 6 and 3n − 1 ≤ 14. Find all the possible values of n. **[3]**

**11.** (Extended) Region R is defined by x ≥ 0, y > 1 and x + y ≤ 6.

**(a)** Which of the three boundary lines should be drawn broken? Give a reason. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the coordinates of the three vertices of R. **[2]**

**(c)** Find how many points with integer coordinates lie in R. **[2]**

**12.** (Extended) A triangular region has vertices (1, 0), (5, 0) and (1, 4). The sides along x = 1 and y = 0 are solid lines. The third side is a broken line. Write down the three inequalities that define the region. **[4]**

**13.** (Extended) Pam solves 4 − x > 1 like this:

4 − x > 1, so −x > −3, so x > 3.

**(a)** Explain the mistake in Pam's working. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the correct solution. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **x < 1** [1].

**2. (a)** **−4 ≤ x < 2** [1].

**(b)** **−4, −3, −2, −1, 0, 1** [1].
*Examiner insight:* −4 is included because its circle is closed; 2 is not, because its circle is open.

**3. (a)** **w ≤ 750** [1].

**(b)** **s > 30** [1].

**4.** An open circle at −1 and a closed circle at 3 [1], joined by a line between them [1].

**5.** 4x ≤ 14 [1], **x ≤ 3.5** [1].

**6.** 3x − 6 > 5x + 4 [1]. −10 > 2x [1]. **x < −5** [1].
*Examiner insight:* collecting the x terms on the right avoids dividing by −2. If you do divide by −2, the sign must reverse.

**7. (a)** −8 ≤ 4x < 12 [1], **−2 ≤ x < 3** [1].

**(b)** **−2, −1, 0, 1, 2** [1].

**8. (a)** Multiply by 15: 5(2x − 1) > 3(x + 2) [1]. 10x − 5 > 3x + 6, so 7x > 11 [1]. **x > 11/7** [1] (about 1.57).

**(b)** **2** [1].

**9. (a)** **12 + 0.05m ≤ 20** [1] (or 1200 + 5m ≤ 2000 in cents).

**(b)** 0.05m ≤ 8, so m ≤ 160 [1]. Leon can use **at most 160 minutes** of calls in a month [1].

**10.** 2n > 3 gives n > 1.5 [1]. 3n ≤ 15 gives n ≤ 5 [1]. **n = 2, 3, 4, 5** [1].

**11. (a)** **y = 1**, because y > 1 is a strict inequality [1].

**(b)** x = 0 meets y = 1 at (0, 1), y = 1 meets x + y = 6 at (5, 1), and x = 0 meets x + y = 6 at (0, 6). Two correct [1], all three **(0, 1), (5, 1), (0, 6)** [1].

**(c)** y = 1 is excluded, so y runs from 2 to 6. Counts: y = 2: 5 points; y = 3: 4; y = 4: 3; y = 5: 2; y = 6: 1 [1]. Total **15** [1].
*Examiner insight:* points on the broken line y = 1 are not in R, so (0, 1) to (5, 1) are left out, while points on the solid lines x = 0 and x + y = 6 are counted.

**12.** **x ≥ 1** [1]. **y ≥ 0** [1]. Third side through (5, 0) and (1, 4): gradient −1, so y = −x + 5, i.e. x + y = 5 [1]. Testing (2, 1) inside the triangle: 3 < 5, so **x + y < 5** [1].

**13. (a)** Dividing (or multiplying) both sides by −1 must reverse the inequality sign, and Pam did not reverse it [1].

**(b)** **x < 3** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing x ≤ 1 for an open circle, or listing an excluded end point as an integer.
- Forgetting to reverse the sign after dividing by a negative.
- Applying an operation to only two parts of a double inequality.
- Drawing every boundary solid, whatever the inequality.
- Counting points on a broken boundary as inside a region.
- Giving a numerical answer to "interpret" without a sentence in context.
