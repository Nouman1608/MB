The IB Middle Years Programme Individuals and Societies subject group
incorporates disciplines traditionally studied under humanities and
social sciences. It encourages learners to respect and understand the
world around them, and equips them with the skills to inquire into
historical, geographical, political, social, economic and cultural
factors that affect individuals, societies and environments.

## What schools can offer within this subject group

For MYP Individuals and Societies, schools develop courses drawing on
integrated humanities, history, economics, geography, philosophy,
sociology/anthropology, business management, psychology, and world
religions. The IB's approach includes a strong focus on inquiry and
investigation: students collect, describe and analyse data, test
hypotheses, and learn to interpret increasingly complex information,
including original source material.

## Aims

The course encourages and enables students to:

- develop inquiry skills leading towards conceptual understandings of
  the relationships between individuals, societies and the
  environments in which they live
- appreciate critically the diversity of human culture, attitudes and
  beliefs
- recognize that both content and methodology in this subject group
  can be debatable and controversial, practising tolerance of
  uncertainty.

## How it's assessed

MYP Individuals and Societies has no SL/HL distinction and no single
weighted final exam; instead, teachers assess coursework throughout the
year against four equally weighted criteria, each scored on an eight-point
scale (1-8): Criterion A, Knowing and understanding; Criterion B,
Investigating; Criterion C, Communicating; and Criterion D, Thinking
critically.

Schools that opt into MYP eAssessment for an IB-validated result (available
in history, geography and integrated humanities) sit a 2-hour on-screen
exam built from three tasks: an investigating task combining criteria A and
B; a communicating task combining criteria A and C; and a thinking
critically task -- an extended piece of writing -- combining criteria A, C
and D. As with other MYP subjects, most day-to-day assessment happens
through classroom-based, criterion-referenced work rather than this
optional external exam.

Where schools do offer MYP eAssessment, it is only available in three of
the many disciplines individuals and societies can draw on -- history,
geography and integrated humanities -- and the on-screen exam questions
span topics such as demographics and human movement, historical empires
and alliances, rights and social protest, and economic agents including
consumers, producers, governments and banks, always requiring students to
work with source material rather than recall facts in isolation.

The three eAssessment tasks are weighted by marks rather than equally:
an investigating task worth 26 marks, a communicating task worth 18 marks,
and a thinking-critically task -- built around an extended piece of
writing -- worth 36 marks, the largest single component of the exam.
