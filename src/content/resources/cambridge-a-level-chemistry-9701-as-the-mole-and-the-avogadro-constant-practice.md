**1.** Which sample contains the greatest number of molecules? (H 1.0, C 12.0, N 14.0, O 16.0)

A: 2.0 g of H₂

B: 16.0 g of O₂

C: 14.0 g of N₂

D: 22.0 g of CO₂

**[1]**

**2.** Define the term *mole* in terms of the Avogadro constant. **[1]**

**3.** State the units of the Avogadro constant and of molar mass. **[1]**

**4.** Calculate the number of molecules in 2.30 g of ethanol, C₂H₅OH (Mr 46.0). **[2]**

**5.** Calculate the mass of one molecule of sulfur dioxide, SO₂ (Mr 64.1). **[1]**

**6.** Calculate the mass of 2.50 × 10²³ atoms of copper (Ar 63.5). **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Magnesium chloride, MgCl₂, has a relative formula mass of 95.3. A sample of 4.76 g is dissolved in water.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of MgCl₂. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of chloride ions in the sample. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the total number of ions in the sample. **[1]**

**8.** Methane, CH₄, has Mr 16.0.

**(a)** Calculate the number of electrons in 0.500 g of methane. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of hydrogen atoms in the same sample. **[1]**

**9.** White phosphorus exists as P₄ molecules (Mr 124.0). Red phosphorus is often written simply as P (Ar 31.0).

**(a)** Calculate the number of molecules in 1.00 g of white phosphorus. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of atoms in 1.00 g of white phosphorus and in 1.00 g of red phosphorus. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain your answers to (b). **[1]**

**10.** Iron(III) sulfate, Fe₂(SO₄)₃, has a relative formula mass of 399.9.

**(a)** Show how the relative formula mass is calculated (Fe 55.8, S 32.1, O 16.0). **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of oxygen atoms in 2.00 g of iron(III) sulfate. **[3]**

**11.** The mass of one copper atom is measured as 1.055 × 10⁻²² g.

**(a)** Use this and Ar(Cu) = 63.5 to calculate a value for the Avogadro constant. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why molar mass in g mol⁻¹ is numerically equal to relative atomic mass. **[2]**

**12.** A drop of water has a volume of 0.050 cm³. The density of water is 1.00 g cm⁻³ (H₂O, Mr 18.0).

**(a)** Calculate the number of water molecules in the drop, to 2 significant figures. **[2]**

**(b)** Hydrated sodium sulfate, Na₂SO₄·10H₂O, has a relative formula mass of 322.1. Calculate the number of water molecules in 3.22 g of the crystals. **[2]**

**(c)** Equal masses of sodium (Ar 23.0) and potassium (Ar 39.1) are compared. Calculate how many times more atoms the sodium sample contains. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** A [1]. 2.0 g of H₂ is 1.00 mol; each of the others is 0.500 mol. *(Total: 1 mark)*

**2.** The amount of substance containing 6.022 × 10²³ particles (the Avogadro constant number of particles) [1]. *(Total: 1 mark)*

**3.** Avogadro constant: mol⁻¹; molar mass: g mol⁻¹ [1]. *(Total: 1 mark)*

**4.** n = 2.30 ÷ 46.0 = 0.0500 mol [1]; 0.0500 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 3.01 × 10²² molecules [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**5.** 64.1 ÷ 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.06 × 10⁻²² g [1]. *(Total: 1 mark)*

**6.** n = 2.50 × 10²³ ÷ 6.022 × 10²³ = 0.415 mol [1]; mass = 0.415 × 63.5 = 26.4 g [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**7. (a)** 4.76 ÷ 95.3 = 0.0499 mol [1].

**(b)** Moles of Cl⁻ = 2 × 0.0499 = 0.0999 mol [1]; × 6.022 × 10²³ = 6.02 × 10²² ions [1].

**(c)** 3 × 0.0499 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 9.02 × 10²² ions [1]. *(Total: 4 marks)*

**8. (a)** n(CH₄) = 0.500 ÷ 16.0 = 0.03125 mol [1]. Electrons per molecule = 6 + 4 = 10 [1]. 0.03125 × 10 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.88 × 10²³ electrons [1].

**(b)** 0.03125 × 4 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 7.53 × 10²² atoms [1]. *(Total: 4 marks)*

**9. (a)** 1.00 ÷ 124.0 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 4.86 × 10²¹ molecules [1].

**(b)** White: 4 × 4.86 × 10²¹ = 1.94 × 10²² atoms [1]. Red: 1.00 ÷ 31.0 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.94 × 10²² atoms [1].

**(c)** Both samples are 1.00 g of the same element, so they contain the same number of atoms; only the way the atoms are grouped differs [1]. *(Total: 4 marks)*

**10. (a)** 2(55.8) + 3[32.1 + 4(16.0)] = 111.6 + 288.3 = 399.9 [1].

**(b)** n = 2.00 ÷ 399.9 = 0.00500 mol [1]. Oxygen atoms per formula unit = 3 × 4 = 12 [1]. 0.00500 × 12 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 3.61 × 10²² atoms [1]. *(Total: 4 marks)*

**11. (a)** L = molar mass ÷ mass of one atom = 63.5 ÷ 1.055 × 10⁻²² [1] = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** Relative atomic mass is the mass of an atom in unified atomic mass units (1 u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁴ g) [1]. One gram contains 6.022 × 10²³ u, so L atoms of relative mass X have a mass of X grams [1]. *(Total: 4 marks)*

**12. (a)** Mass = 0.050 g; n = 0.050 ÷ 18.0 = 2.78 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.7 × 10²¹ molecules [1].

**(b)** n = 3.22 ÷ 322.1 = 0.0100 mol [1]; 10 water molecules per formula unit: 0.0100 × 10 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 6.02 × 10²² molecules [1].

**(c)** 39.1 ÷ 23.0 = 1.70 times as many [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not multiplying by the number of atoms, ions or electrons per formula.
- Using 1.0 or 16.0 for the molar masses of H₂ and O₂.
- Missing or wrong units on L and on molar mass.
- Rounding the amount too early, which changes the third significant figure.
- Answers to the wrong number of significant figures when the question specifies them.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the mole a mass or a number?**
It is an amount of substance: the amount that contains 6.022 × 10²³ specified particles. Its mass depends on the particles, which is why molar mass is needed.

**How do I count ions in a sample of an ionic compound?**
Find the moles of formula units with n = m ÷ M, multiply by the number of ions in one formula unit, then multiply by the Avogadro constant.
