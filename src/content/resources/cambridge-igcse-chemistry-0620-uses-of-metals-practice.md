## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which property explains why copper is used for electrical wiring?

A: low density and malleability

B: good electrical conductivity and ductility

C: resistance to corrosion and low density

D: high melting point and malleability **[1]**

**2.** State the property of aluminium that makes it suitable for the manufacture of aircraft. **[1]**

**3.** Explain why aluminium is used to make trays for cooked food. **[2]**

**4.** Name the metal used for overhead electrical cables and give **two** properties that make it suitable. **[3]**

**5.** A student writes: "Copper is used for wires because it is malleable." Identify the error and correct it. **[2]**

**6.** Calculate the mass of a 250 cm³ block of aluminium. The density of aluminium is 2.70 g/cm³. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** An engineer compares two metals for a 1.0 km overhead cable. Each cable would have a volume of 0.50 m³ of metal.

| Metal | Density / kg/m³ | Approximate relative electrical conductivity (copper = 100) |
|---|---|---|
| copper | 8960 | 100 |
| aluminium | 2700 | 60 |

**(a)** Calculate the mass of each cable. **[2]**

**(b)** Use your answers and the table to explain why aluminium is chosen even though copper is the better conductor. **[3]**

**8.** The table shows data for four metals, labelled P to S.

| Metal | Density / g/cm³ | Relative electrical conductivity | Ductile? | Effect of leaving in damp air for a month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P | 1.7 | 38 | Yes | Surface badly corroded |
| Q | 2.8 | 58 | Yes | No visible change |
| R | 8.9 | 97 | Yes | Thin dull layer only |
| S | 11.3 | 8 | No | No visible change |

**(a)** Suggest which metal is best for the body of a small aircraft. Explain why you rejected metal P. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest which metal is best for wiring inside a house. Give **two** reasons from the table. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest why metal S would not be used for electrical wiring, even though it does not corrode. **[2]**

**9.** Aluminium foil is used to wrap food.

**(a)** State the property of aluminium, named in the syllabus, that makes it suitable for food containers. **[1]**

**(b)** Aluminium foil is made by rolling the metal into very thin sheets. Name the physical property that allows this. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why foil made from a metal that corroded easily would be unsuitable for wrapping food. **[2]**

**10.** Describe the uses of aluminium in terms of its physical properties. Refer to **three** different uses. **[6]**

**11.** Copper costs more than aluminium per kilogram. Explain why copper is still used for wiring inside buildings rather than aluminium. **[2]**

**12.** A teacher shows two wires of the same length and thickness: one copper and one aluminium. Predict which wire is heavier and explain your prediction using density. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

**2.** Low density [1].

**3.** It resists corrosion [1], so it is not attacked by moist or acidic food and does not weaken or contaminate the food [1].

**4.** Aluminium [1]; low density [1]; good electrical conductivity [1].

**5.** The property is wrong: malleable means it can be hammered into shape [1]. Copper is used for wires because it is **ductile** (can be drawn into wires) and a good electrical conductor [1].

**6.** mass = density × volume = 2.70 × 250 [1] = **675 g** [1].

**7. (a)** Copper: 8960 × 0.50 = **4480 kg** [1]. Aluminium: 2700 × 0.50 = **1350 kg** [1].

**(b)** The aluminium cable is about a third of the mass of the copper one [1], so the pylons need to support much less mass / can be further apart [1]; aluminium still has good electrical conductivity (about 60 % of copper's), which is enough to carry the current [1].

**8. (a)** Q [1]: it has low density and does not corrode [1]. P has a lower density but it corrodes badly in damp air, so the aircraft body would weaken [1].

**(b)** R [1]: it has the highest electrical conductivity [1] and it is ductile, so it can be drawn into wires [1].

**(c)** It has very low electrical conductivity [1] and it is not ductile, so it cannot easily be drawn into wires [1].

**9. (a)** Resistance to corrosion [1].

**(b)** Malleability [1].

**(c)** The foil would react with air, water or substances in the food [1], so it would wear away / form holes / contaminate the food [1].

**10.** One mark for each use and one for the matching property, maximum [6]:

- aircraft [1] because of its low density [1];
- overhead electrical cables [1] because of its low density **and** good electrical conductivity [1] (both properties needed for this mark);
- food containers [1] because of its resistance to corrosion [1].

**11.** Copper is a better electrical conductor than aluminium [1]. Inside buildings the wires are supported, so copper's higher density is not a problem, and copper is ductile so it is easily drawn into thin wires [1].

**12.** The copper wire is heavier [1]. The wires have the same volume, and copper has a higher density than aluminium, so the copper wire has a greater mass [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a use without the property that explains it.
- Giving only one of the two properties for overhead cables or for copper wiring.
- Writing "malleable" for wires instead of "ductile".
- Describing aluminium as "unreactive" instead of "resistant to corrosion".
- In data questions, choosing the metal with the best value in one column while ignoring a column that rules it out, such as corrosion.
- Leaving out units, or not showing mass = density × volume, in a calculation.
