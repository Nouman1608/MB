## What the syllabus asks

1. Distinguish capital and revenue expenditure, and account for each.
2. Distinguish capital and revenue receipts, and account for each.
3. Identify and calculate the effect of incorrect treatment on **profit**.
4. Identify and calculate the effect of incorrect treatment on **asset
valuations**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Capital expenditure | Spending on buying, adding to or improving non-current assets, including costs of getting them ready for use |
| Revenue expenditure | Spending on running the business and maintaining non-current assets in their existing condition |
| Capital receipt | Money received not from normal trading: capital introduced, loans received, proceeds of selling non-current assets |
| Revenue receipt | Money received from normal trading and regular income: sales, rent, commission, interest, discount received |
| Net book value | Cost of a non-current asset less accumulated depreciation |

## One-question test

**Does it create or improve an asset that benefits more than one period?**
Yes → capital. No, it keeps things running → revenue.

## Capital expenditure checklist

- Purchase price of a non-current asset
- Delivery (carriage) on the asset
- Installation and testing
- Legal fees on buying property
- Extensions and improvements that add capacity or extend life
- Own staff's wages and materials used to build or install an asset
- Initial painting of a business name on a new vehicle

## Revenue expenditure checklist

- Repairs, servicing, cleaning and repainting existing assets
- Fuel, insurance, licences
- Wages for normal work
- Rent, electricity, advertising
- Goods bought for resale (purchases)
- Depreciation (the revenue charge for using capital items)

## Recording

| Item | Debit | Credit | Final destination |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asset bought by bank | Non-current asset | Bank | Statement of financial position |
| Asset bought on credit | Non-current asset | Supplier (other payable) | Statement of financial position |
| Running cost paid | Expense | Bank | Statement of profit or loss |
| Capital introduced | Bank (or asset) | Capital | Capital section |
| Loan received | Bank | Loan | Liabilities |
| Asset sold | Bank | Disposal | Profit or loss on disposal only |
| Rent or commission received | Bank | Income account | Statement of profit or loss |

## Effects of wrong treatment

| Error | Profit | Non-current assets |
|---|---|---|
| Capital expenditure as revenue | Understated | Understated |
| Revenue expenditure as capital | Overstated | Overstated |
| Loan or capital introduced as revenue | Overstated | No effect (liability or capital understated instead) |
| Sale proceeds of an asset as revenue | Overstated by proceeds − profit on disposal (or + loss) | Overstated by net book value |
| Revenue receipt as capital | Understated | No effect |

**With depreciation:** an asset item wrongly expensed understates profit by
the amount **minus** the depreciation that should have been charged.
Example: $4,000 machine expensed, depreciation 25% on cost → profit
understated by $4,000 − $1,000 = $3,000; machinery understated by $3,000.

**Wrongly capitalised and depreciated:** the profit overstatement is the
amount minus the depreciation wrongly charged. Example: repairs $900
capitalised, 20% depreciation charged ($180) → profit overstated by $720;
assets overstated by $720.

## Exam traps

- Delivery and installation of a new asset are capital, not carriage
inwards or repairs.
- Repairs remain revenue even when they are large.
- Proceeds of selling an asset are not revenue; only the profit or loss on
disposal reaches the statement of profit or loss.
- Purpose decides: a van bought by a car dealer to sell is purchases.
- Always give direction **and** amount.
- A loan error affects liabilities, not assets.

## Quick self-test

1. Define "capital expenditure".
2. Classify: (a) cost of a new shop counter; (b) monthly cleaning of the
shop.
3. Classify: (a) a $10,000 loan from a relative; (b) commission received.
4. Legal fees of $1,200 on buying a warehouse were charged to general
expenses. Effect on profit? (Ignore depreciation.)
5. State the effect of the error in question 4 on non-current assets.
6. Engine servicing of $650 was debited to motor vehicles. Effect on profit
and on assets? (Ignore depreciation.)
7. A machine costing $6,000 was debited to purchases. Depreciation is 10% on
cost. Effect on profit?
8. Proceeds of $2,000 from selling a van with net book value $2,600 were
credited to revenue. Effect on profit?
9. Give the double entry for a machine bought on credit from Hale Ltd for
$3,500.
10. Explain why distinguishing capital from revenue expenditure matters.
11. Which receipt is a revenue receipt: capital introduced, sale of
equipment, or interest received?
12. Own staff's wages of $800 for installing new shelving were debited to
wages. Classify the mistake.

### Answers

1. Spending on buying, adding to or improving non-current assets, including
costs of getting them ready for use.
2. (a) Capital; (b) revenue.
3. (a) Capital receipt; (b) revenue receipt.
4. Profit understated by $1,200.
5. Non-current assets understated by $1,200.
6. Profit overstated by $650; non-current assets overstated by $650.
7. Profit understated by $6,000 − $600 = $5,400.
8. Correctly there is a loss on disposal of $600. Revenue includes $2,000
instead. Profit overstated by $2,000 + $600 = $2,600.
9. Dr Machinery $3,500, Cr Hale Ltd $3,500.
10. So that costs are matched against the periods they benefit and assets
are not misstated; otherwise profit and the statement of financial position
are both wrong.
11. Interest received.
12. Capital expenditure treated as revenue expenditure.
