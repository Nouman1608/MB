**These notes cover the first two statements of 3.2.2.4** in OxfordAQA International GCSE Sociology
(9292): "Social construction of crime and deviance, including the differences between societies and
over time" and "Types of crimes and examples of them". They condense the
[social construction and types of crime study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-sociology-9292-social-construction-and-types-of-crime/);
measuring crime is condensed in the [research methods (Paper 2) revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-sociology-9292-research-methods-paper2-revision-notes/).

## Crime and deviance in four lines

- **Deviance** breaks norms; **crime** breaks the criminal law.
- Some deviance is not criminal (queue-jumping).
- Some crime is not widely seen as deviant (minor speeding).
- Both are **socially constructed**: defined by rules and reactions, not fixed in the act.

## Evidence of social construction

| Kind of evidence | Example |
|---|---|
| Between societies | Alcohol legal in most countries, prohibited in some, such as Saudi Arabia |
| Between societies | Recreational cannabis legalised in Uruguay (2013) and Canada (2018), criminal elsewhere |
| Over time (England and Wales) | Partial decriminalisation of sex between men, 1967 |
| Over time (England and Wales) | Breathalyser introduced, 1967; drink-driving now stigmatised |
| Over time (England and Wales) | Rape within marriage confirmed as a crime by the courts, 1991 |
| Over time (England) | Smoking banned in enclosed public places, 2007 |

Always explain the **changing value** behind a change in law.

## Labelling and moral panics

- **Becker:** deviance is the result of a label successfully applied; enforcement is selective.
- **Stanley Cohen,** *Folk Devils and Moral Panics* (1972): mods and rockers at English seaside
  resorts, 1964.
- Media **exaggeration**, **distortion**, **prediction** and **symbolisation**.
- **Folk devils**: groups blamed as a threat. **Moral panic**: an exaggerated public reaction.
- **Deviancy amplification**: the reaction makes deviance seem larger and can increase it.

## The eight types of crime

| Type | Key feature | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Violent | Force or threat against a person | Assault, robbery |
| Property | Taking or damaging property without force against a person | Burglary, theft, vandalism |
| Cyber | Using computers or the internet | Hacking, phishing, ransomware |
| White-collar | High-status person, in the course of work, for personal gain (Sutherland, 1939) | Embezzlement, insider trading |
| Corporate | By or for a company, for the organisation's benefit | Price fixing, safety breaches, illegal pollution |
| State | By governments or their agencies | Genocide, torture, war crimes |
| Green / environmental | Harm to the environment | Illegal logging, wildlife trafficking, illegal dumping |
| "Victimless" | No obvious direct victim (label disputed) | Illegal drug use, illegal gambling |

## Analysis points

- White-collar and corporate crime are **under-recorded**: hard to detect, often handled by
  regulators.
- State crime is hard to **prosecute**: the state controls the justice system. Example: Rwanda, 1994.
- Green crime asks whether crime is defined by **law** or by **harm**.
- "Victimless" crime may have **hidden victims**: families and communities.
- Cybercrime crosses **borders**, making investigation hard.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Social construction | The idea that crime and deviance are defined by society, not fixed in acts |
| Decriminalisation | Removing an act from the criminal law |
| Moral panic | An exaggerated public reaction to a group or behaviour seen as a threat |
| Folk devil | A group blamed as the cause of a moral panic |
| Deviancy amplification | Reaction to deviance that increases it or makes it seem larger |
| Moral entrepreneur | A person or group that campaigns for a rule (Becker) |

## Exam traps

- Treating crime and deviance as the same.
- Examples of change over time with no explanation.
- Confusing white-collar crime (individual gain) with corporate crime (organisational gain).
- Calling "victimless" crimes harmless without discussion.
- Naming Cohen without the stages of a moral panic.
- No named society in an extended answer.

## Self-test

1. Define deviance and crime.
2. Give an example of deviance that is not a crime.
3. What does it mean to say crime is socially constructed?
4. Give one example of a difference between societies in what is criminal.
5. Give one example of change over time in what is criminal.
6. Who coined the term "white-collar crime", and when?
7. What is the difference between white-collar and corporate crime?
8. Give two examples of state crime.
9. Why is "victimless" in quotation marks in the specification?
10. Which study did Stanley Cohen make, and what did he find?
11. What is deviancy amplification?
12. Give one reason why corporate crime is under-recorded.

**Answers:** 1. Deviance is behaviour that breaks norms; crime is behaviour that breaks the criminal
law. 2. For example, queue-jumping. 3. That it is defined by the rules and reactions of a society
rather than being a fixed quality of an act. 4. For example, alcohol is legal to buy in most countries
but prohibited in some, such as Saudi Arabia. 5. For example, smoking in enclosed public places was
banned in England in 2007. 6. Sutherland, in 1939. 7. White-collar crime is committed by a high-status
individual at work for personal gain; corporate crime is committed by or for a company to benefit the
organisation. 8. Any two: genocide; torture; war crimes; corruption by officials. 9. Because the label
is disputed; such crimes may harm families and communities. 10. *Folk Devils and Moral Panics* (1972),
on reactions to mods and rockers in 1964; he found media exaggeration and prediction created a moral
panic. 11. Reaction to deviance that makes it seem larger and can increase it. 12. Any one: it is hard
to detect; victims may not know they are victims; it is often handled by regulators rather than
police; companies have the resources to defend themselves.
