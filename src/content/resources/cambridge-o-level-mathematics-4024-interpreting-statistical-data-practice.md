These questions cover section 9.2, Interpreting statistical data, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. All data is invented. For written answers, give the evidence and the conclusion. The [interpreting statistical data study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-interpreting-statistical-data/) has model answers, and the [interpreting statistical data revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-interpreting-statistical-data-revision-notes/) give the comparison template.

## Section A

**1.** The numbers of visitors to a museum one week were:

| Day | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Visitors | 120 | 95 | 140 | 110 | 185 |

**(a)** Find the total number of visitors.

**(b)** On which day were there most visitors?

**(c)** Suggest one inference the museum manager might draw from the table. **[3]**

**2.** Club A has 75 members, of whom 45 are juniors. Club B has 48 members, of whom 36 are juniors. Which club has the greater proportion of juniors? Show your working. **[2]**

**3.** Delivery times, in days, for two shops have these statistics: Shop S, median 3, range 6; Shop T, median 4, range 2. Compare the delivery times of the two shops. **[2]**

**4.** A bar chart shows a company's profits as 205 thousand dollars one year and 215 thousand dollars the next. The vertical axis starts at 200 thousand. Explain why the chart is misleading. **[2]**

**5.** To find out how people in a city travel to work, a student asks 30 people waiting at a train station. Give two reasons why the results may not represent the city. **[2]**

**6.** A report finds that towns with more libraries also have more shops, and concludes that building libraries causes shops to open. Explain the restriction on this conclusion. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Six plants were grown with fertiliser X and six with fertiliser Y. Their heights, in cm, were:

```
X: 12 15 15 16 18 20
Y: 10 13 17 19 21 22
```

**(a)** Find the mean and range of each set.

**(b)** Compare the heights of the two sets of plants.

**(c)** A gardener wants plants of similar heights. Which fertiliser should she choose? Give a reason. **[6]**

**8.** Pie charts of the same size show the favourite sports of students at two schools. School P has 360 students, and its football sector is 90°. School Q has 720 students, and its football sector is 60°. Lina says more students at School P like football, because its sector is bigger.

**(a)** Find the number of students who chose football at each school.

**(b)** Comment on Lina's statement. **[4]**

**9.** 250 people were asked whether they prefer e-books or printed books.

| | E-books | Printed | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 30 | 72 | 48 | 120 |
| 30 and over | 39 | 91 | 130 |
| Total | 111 | 139 | 250 |

**(a)** Find the percentage of each age group that prefers e-books.

**(b)** Write one inference from your answers.

**(c)** The survey was carried out online. Explain why this might affect the conclusion. **[5]**

**10.** A grouped frequency table shows the salaries of 40 employees. Its top class is 50 000 ≤ s < 60 000 dollars.

**(a)** A manager says the highest salary is 59 999 dollars. Explain why this cannot be known from the table.

**(b)** The mean salary estimated from the table is 34 500 dollars. Why is this only an estimate? **[3]**

**11.** Waiting times, in minutes, at two clinics were summarised from cumulative frequency diagrams: Clinic A, median 42 and interquartile range 18; Clinic B, median 47 and interquartile range 9. Compare the waiting times at the two clinics. **[4]**

**12.** A shop's sales rose from 2400 items to 3600 items. An advert says "Our sales have doubled!" Is the advert's claim correct? Support your answer with a calculation. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 120 + 95 + 140 + 110 + 185 = **650** [1].

**(b)** **Friday** [1].

**(c)** For example: "More staff may be needed on Fridays" or "Tuesday is the quietest day, so it may suit maintenance" [1].

**2.** Club A: 45/75 = 60%; Club B: 36/48 = 75% [1]. **Club B** has the greater proportion [1].

*Examiner insight:* Club A has more juniors in number, which is exactly why proportions are needed.

**3.** Shop S has the lower median (3 days against 4), so it is quicker on average [1]. Shop T has the smaller range (2 against 6), so its delivery times are more consistent [1].

**4.** The axis does not start at zero, so the second bar appears three times as tall as the first (15 against 5 above 200) [1], although profit rose by only 10 thousand, about 5% [1].

**5.** The sample of 30 is small [1]; people at a train station are more likely to travel by train, so other methods are under-represented [1].

**6.** A link (correlation) between the two does not show that one causes the other; both may depend on something else, such as town size [1].

**7. (a)** Mean X = 96 ÷ 6 = **16 cm**, range 20 − 12 = **8 cm** [1]. Mean Y = 102 ÷ 6 = **17 cm**, range 22 − 10 = **12 cm** [2] (one mark for each value).

**(b)** The mean for Y (17 cm) is higher than for X (16 cm), so on average plants with Y grew taller [1]. The range for X (8 cm) is smaller than for Y (12 cm), so the heights with X were more consistent [1].

**(c)** **X**, because it has the smaller range, so its plants are more similar in height [1].

*Examiner insight:* Part (c) asks about similarity, which is spread, not average. Match the measure to the question.

**8. (a)** P: (90/360) × 360 = **90** students [1]. Q: (60/360) × 720 = **120** students [1].

**(b)** Lina is **wrong** [1]: the sectors show proportions of different totals, and School Q has twice as many students, so its smaller sector represents more people [1].

**9. (a)** Under 30: 72 ÷ 120 = **60%** [1]. 30 and over: 39 ÷ 130 = **30%** [1].

**(b)** Younger people in this sample were twice as likely to prefer e-books as older people [1].

**(c)** People who answer online surveys are likely to use the internet more [1], so preference for e-books may be overstated, especially among older people [1].

**10. (a)** The table only shows that the highest salary is in the class 50 000 to 60 000 dollars; the exact value is not recorded [1], and it could be anywhere in that class [1].

**(b)** It uses class midpoints in place of the exact salaries, which are not known [1].

**11.** Clinic A's median (42 minutes) is lower than Clinic B's (47 minutes) [1], so on average patients waited less at Clinic A [1]. Clinic B's interquartile range (9 minutes) is smaller than Clinic A's (18 minutes) [1], so waiting times at Clinic B were more consistent [1].

**12.** Increase = 1200, which is 1200 ÷ 2400 = 50% [1]. **Not correct** [1]: doubling would mean 4800 items, but sales rose by half [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Comparing counts for groups of different sizes instead of proportions.
- A comparison that quotes values but draws no conclusion.
- Commenting on the average only, with nothing on spread.
- Missing the context in "which is better".
- Restrictions given as vague statements rather than reasons tied to the survey.
- Taking a bar chart with a truncated axis at face value.
