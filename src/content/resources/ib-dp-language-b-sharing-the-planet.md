This guide covers **Sharing the Planet**, one of five compulsory
themes for IB Diploma Programme Language B, first assessment 2020. The
[full syllabus guide](/resources/ib-dp-language-b-syllabus-guide/)
confirms all five themes are examined through unseen material on
Paper 2, so this theme cannot be safely under-revised relative to the
others.

## Where this fits in the syllabus

Sharing the Planet explores the challenges and opportunities faced by
individuals and communities in the modern world, asking what
environmental and social challenges the world faces, how they might be
overcome, and what challenges and benefits globalization brings.
Unlike a theme built around personal or cultural experience, this
theme leans toward issues and debate, which changes the kind of
language required: opinion, cause-and-effect, and comparison
structures matter more here than for a theme such as Identities.

## Syllabus coverage

**IB DP LANGUAGE B — SHARING THE PLANET: RECOMMENDED TOPICS**

- **The environment** — climate, natural resources, conservation and
environmental impact, plus opinion language for discussing
environmental policy
- **Human rights** — rights, freedoms, justice and equality, plus the
more formal register this topic often calls for
- **Peace and conflict** — cooperation, negotiation, tension and
resolution
- **Equality** — comparison and contrast (more/less,
similarity/difference), since equality-focused texts frequently
compare groups, regions or outcomes
- **Globalization** — cause and effect (because of, as a result, this
leads to), since globalization texts often trace chains of consequence
across borders
- **Ethics** — expressing and justifying an opinion or moral position
- **Urban and rural environments** — comparing settings and describing
change over time

## How to approach it

Because all five themes are compulsory and examined through unseen
material, build genuine breadth across all seven recommended topics
rather than concentrating only on the one or two your class discussed
most. Because this theme leans toward opinion, cause-and-effect and
comparison language more than some of the other four themes, make sure
your target-language toolkit for expressing and justifying a position,
and for tracing cause and consequence, is genuinely fluent, not just
recognised passively when reading. Paper 2's combined 50% weighting
for listening and reading comprehension equals the writing task and
internal oral combined, yet revision time tends to default toward
speaking and writing output — Sharing the Planet is a theme where
unseen texts (news reports, opinion pieces, documentary-style audio)
are especially likely to appear, since the theme is inherently
issue-driven and current-affairs adjacent, so build comprehension
practice specifically around authentic or course-level materials
discussing environmental, social or global issues in the target
language, not just vocabulary lists.

## Worked example: preparing one topic across all four skills

Take the Globalization topic as an example of preparing a single topic
thoroughly across the whole assessment.

```
Vocabulary:     cause-and-effect connectives (because of, as a
                result, this leads to, consequently)

Reading/listening: practise unseen news-style texts tracing a chain
                    of consequence across borders (e.g. how a change
                    in one country's economy affects another)

Writing (Paper 1): practise a formal article or report arguing a
                    position on a globalization-related issue, using
                    the syllabus's specified text-type conventions

Speaking (oral):    prepare to discuss a globalization-related image
                    or, at HL, connect a studied literary work's
                    engagement with globalization to this theme
```

Preparing one topic across reading, listening, writing and speaking
together, rather than treating vocabulary and skills practice as
separate activities, produces revision that transfers directly to
whichever paper or task actually uses that topic.

## Preparing Paper 1's writing task for this theme

If a Paper 1 writing prompt draws on Sharing the Planet, expect the
syllabus's specified text types (such as a blog post, an article, a
formal letter, or a report) to require the more formal,
argument-structured register this theme often calls for. Practising an
opinion-based article or a formal letter arguing for a specific
environmental or social position, written in the target language's
real conventions for that text type, is more targeted preparation than
only reviewing topic vocabulary in isolation -- examiners assess
whether the register and structure genuinely match the specified text
type, not only whether the content is on-topic.

## Preparing the individual oral around this theme

At SL, the individual oral's visual stimulus could be an image
relevant to any of the five themes, including Sharing the Planet --
practise describing and discussing an environmental, human-rights or
globalization-related image spontaneously in the target language. At
HL, if either of your two studied literary works engages with issues
from this theme, identify and prepare that connection explicitly ahead
of the assessment, since being able to draw a literary work into a
discussion of a compulsory theme demonstrates range that a purely
theme-focused response does not.

## Common mistakes

Concentrating revision on only one or two of the seven recommended
topics, when Paper 2's unseen material could draw on any of them.
Under-preparing listening and reading comprehension relative to
speaking and writing, despite comprehension carrying an equal 50%
weighting on Paper 2. Using descriptive or personal-experience
language where this theme calls for opinion, comparison, or
cause-and-effect structures. Preparing only formal writing register
without also building comprehension fluency for authentic,
issue-driven texts.

## Quick revision checklist

- Build vocabulary and structures across all seven recommended topics,
  not just one or two.
- Dedicate genuine revision time to listening and reading
  comprehension, not only speaking and writing.
- Practise a formal, argument-structured text type (article, report,
  formal letter) for Paper 1 if a prompt draws on this theme.
- Prepare to discuss an environmental, human-rights or
  globalization-related image spontaneously for the individual oral.
- At HL, identify any connection between your studied literary works
  and this theme ahead of the oral assessment.
