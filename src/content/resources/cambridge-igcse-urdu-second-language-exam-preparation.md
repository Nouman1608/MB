Cambridge IGCSE Urdu as a Second Language 0539 is assessed through two graded written components plus
one optional, separately endorsed component. Paper 1 Reading and Writing runs 2 hours for 60 marks and
carries two-thirds of the qualification; Paper 2 Listening runs approximately 35-45 minutes for 30
marks and carries the remaining third; Component 5 Speaking lasts approximately 10-12 minutes, is
marked out of 60, and is **optional** -- it is internally assessed and externally moderated, reported
as a separate endorsement graded 1-5, and does not contribute to the IGCSE grade at all. These notes
turn that structure into a preparation plan alongside the site's
[Paper 1 Reading and Writing guide](/resources/igcse-cambridge-urdu-paper-1-reading-writing/) and
[Paper 2 Listening guide](/resources/igcse-cambridge-urdu-paper-2-listening/).

## Paper 1 is worth twice Paper 2 -- and it is the component you can most improve

The 60-mark Reading and Writing paper carries roughly 67% of the grade against Listening's 33%.
It is also the component most responsive to deliberate practice, because writing accuracy and reading
technique both improve with drilling in a way that listening comprehension improves only slowly.
**Exam-preparation priority**: if revision time is limited, weight it towards Paper 1, and within
Paper 1 towards the writing tasks, where the gap between a hurried answer and a checked one is
largest.

## Listening is a single-hearing skill and must be practised as one

A listening test cannot be paused, re-read or returned to at leisure in the way a reading text can.
The skill being tested is extracting specific required information in real time. **Exam-preparation
priority**: practise with audio at natural speed and resist the temptation to replay immediately.
Read the questions during any pause before an extract begins, so you know what you are listening
*for* rather than trying to understand everything and then locate the answer afterwards. Note-taking
in the margin during the extract, then writing the answer properly afterwards, is far more reliable
than trying to compose a final answer while still listening.

## The optional Speaking component changes what you should and should not revise

Because Component 5 does not contribute to the IGCSE grade, time spent on it is time not spent on the
two components that do. This is not an argument for ignoring it -- the endorsement is a genuine,
separately reported outcome, and oral fluency supports written fluency -- but it is an argument for
being deliberate. **Exam-preparation priority**: confirm early with your centre whether you are
entered for Component 5 at all. If you are, timetable its preparation separately rather than letting
it absorb time budgeted for Paper 1; if you are not, do not let general speaking practice displace
reading and writing work in the final weeks.

## Two hours for sixty marks means the writing tasks need a planning habit

Paper 1's 120 minutes across 60 marks averages two minutes per mark, and the reading sections
typically consume a disproportionate share of that because comprehension takes reading time.
**Exam-preparation priority**: rehearse a short, fixed planning routine for each writing task -- a
minute to note the purpose, the audience and three content points before starting to write. A minute
of planning consistently returns more than a minute of extra writing, because it prevents the
mid-answer restructure that costs both time and coherence.

## Accuracy marks and communication marks are earned differently

In a second-language qualification, an answer can communicate its message successfully and still lose
marks for language accuracy, and it can be grammatically careful yet fail to address what the task
asked for. **Exam-preparation priority**: build a personal error list from marked practice work --
the specific agreement, tense or postposition errors *you* repeat -- and check for exactly those in
the last few minutes of a writing task, rather than re-reading generally for "mistakes". A targeted
check finds errors that a general re-read reliably misses.

## Worked routine: working a listening extract

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
paper or mark scheme.

```
Before the extract plays:
1. Read every question for that extract.
2. Underline the question word (who / where / how many / why) and
   any number, name or date the question is asking for.
3. Predict the FORM of the answer: a number? a place? a reason?

While the extract plays:
4. Write short marginal notes only -- key words, numbers, names.
   Do not attempt a full sentence answer while still listening.

After the extract:
5. Convert notes into the answer, checking the form matches what
   the question asked for (a question asking "how many" needs a
   number, not a description).
6. If a detail was missed, write the most plausible answer anyway
   rather than leaving a blank.
```

Step 3 is the step most often skipped and the one that most improves accuracy: knowing you are
listening for a number changes what you hear.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: weight revision towards Paper 1, and within it towards the writing tasks;
  practise listening at natural speed without replaying; confirm whether you are entered for
  Component 5 and timetable it separately if so; build a personal error list from marked work.
- **During Paper 2**: read the questions before each extract, predict the form of each answer, take
  marginal notes during the extract and write full answers afterwards; never leave a blank.
- **During Paper 1**: budget roughly two minutes per mark; spend one minute planning purpose,
  audience and content points before each writing task; reserve the final minutes for a targeted
  check against your own recurring errors.

## Self-test

1. Why should limited revision time be weighted towards Paper 1?
2. Does Component 5 Speaking affect the IGCSE grade, and what follows from the answer?
3. Why should you read the questions before a listening extract plays rather than after?
4. Why is a targeted final check more effective than a general re-read?

**Answers:** 1. Because Paper 1 carries roughly two-thirds of the qualification (60 marks against
Paper 2's 30), and because reading and writing technique respond to deliberate practice faster than
listening comprehension does. 2. No -- it is optional, internally assessed and externally moderated,
and reported as a separate endorsement graded 1-5; so its preparation should be timetabled separately
rather than drawn from the time budgeted for the two graded papers. 3. Because knowing what
information you need, and what form the answer takes, changes what you are able to pick out of a
single hearing -- you cannot go back and listen again. 4. Because a general re-read tends to confirm
that the answer reads acceptably, whereas checking for the specific errors you personally repeat
finds the ones that are actually costing accuracy marks.
