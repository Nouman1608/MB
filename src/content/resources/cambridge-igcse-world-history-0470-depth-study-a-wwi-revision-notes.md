These notes condense **Depth Study A: The First World War, 1914-18** in Cambridge IGCSE History 0470. It has four key
questions: why there was stalemate on the Western Front, to what extent it was a world war, how important other fronts
were, and why Germany asked for an armistice in 1918. For explanation and worked answers, read the
[Depth Study A study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-depth-study-first-world-war/); to apply the facts,
try the [Depth Study A practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-depth-study-a-wwi-practice/).

## Key question 1: Why was there stalemate on the Western Front?

**The Schlieffen Plan in practice**

- Germany aimed to defeat France in about six weeks by sweeping through Belgium, then turn east against Russia.
- Belgian forts at Liège held up the advance; Britain entered the war on 4 August 1914 because Belgium was invaded.
- The BEF slowed the Germans at **Mons** (23 August 1914) before retreating.
- Russia mobilised faster than expected and invaded East Prussia, so Moltke moved troops east.
- The German right wing turned east of Paris instead of round it; the French and British counter-attacked at the
  **Marne** (September 1914) and the Germans fell back to the Aisne and dug in.

**From movement to trenches**

- **Race to the sea** (autumn 1914): each side tried to outflank the other northwards.
- **First Ypres** (October-November 1914): the BEF held the town at heavy cost; the line now ran from the Channel to
  Switzerland.

**Trench life:** front-line, support and reserve trenches linked by communication trenches; rats, lice, mud, trench
foot, shell shock; units rotated in and out of the line.

**New methods of warfare**

| Weapon | First notable use | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Machine gun | From 1914 | Gave defenders the advantage; cut down infantry in the open |
| Gas | Chlorine, Second Ypres, April 1915 | Terrifying but rarely decisive once masks were issued |
| Tank | Flers-Courcelette (Somme), September 1916 | Unreliable at first; massed use at Cambrai (1917) and in 1918 |
| Aircraft | Reconnaissance from 1914 | Spotting for artillery; later fighters and bombers |
| Artillery | Throughout | Caused most casualties; creeping barrages developed |

**Verdun and the Somme**

- **Verdun** (February-December 1916): Falkenhayn aimed to "bleed France white"; Pétain organised the defence; huge
  losses on both sides.
- **Somme** (July-November 1916): launched partly to relieve Verdun; a week-long bombardment failed to cut the wire;
  about 57,000 British casualties on 1 July; the Allies advanced only a few miles. Debate on **Haig**: "butcher" who
  wasted lives, or a commander who wore down the German army and learned lessons used in 1918.

## Key question 2: To what extent was it a world war?

- **Indian troops** reached the Western Front in autumn 1914, fought at Ypres and Neuve Chapelle (1915) and earned
  Victoria Crosses, including Khudadad Khan's; most infantry were moved to the Middle East by the end of 1915.
- **Canadian troops** faced the first gas attack at Second Ypres (1915) and captured **Vimy Ridge** (April 1917).
- **German East Africa:** the British-Indian landing at Tanga (1914) failed; Lettow-Vorbeck fought a guerrilla
  campaign and surrendered only after the Armistice. Thousands of African carriers died.
- **South West Africa:** South African forces under Botha conquered the colony by July 1915.
- **Japan:** took Tsingtao (November 1914) with British help, seized German Pacific islands and escorted convoys,
  including in the Mediterranean from 1917.
- **Arab Revolt** (from June 1916): led by Sharif Hussein of Mecca and his son Faisal, advised by T. E. Lawrence;
  attacked the Hejaz railway, took Aqaba (1917) and helped capture Damascus (1918).

## Key question 3: How important were other fronts?

- **War at sea:** the British blockade starved Germany of imports. At **Jutland** (May-June 1916) Britain lost more
  ships and men, but the German High Seas Fleet never seriously challenged it again.
- **U-boats:** the sinking of the *Lusitania* (1915) angered the USA; unrestricted submarine warfare (February 1917)
  almost starved Britain until **convoys** cut losses from mid-1917.
- **Gallipoli** (1915-16): aim to knock out Turkey and open a sea route to Russia; naval attack failed in March 1915;
  landings in April at Cape Helles and ANZAC Cove; Turkish defence under Mustafa Kemal; evacuation by January 1916.
- **Eastern Front:** defeat at Tannenberg (1914); heavy losses in 1915; the Brusilov Offensive (1916); revolution in
  1917; **Treaty of Brest-Litovsk** (March 1918) freed German troops for the west.
- **Civilians:** DORA and censorship in Britain; conscription from 1916; women in munitions; Zeppelin and Gotha
  raids; rationing in Britain in 1918; hunger in Germany, including the "turnip winter" of 1916-17.

## Key question 4: Why did Germany ask for an armistice in 1918?

- **American entry** (April 1917): fresh troops and supplies; about two million Americans in France by late 1918.
- **Ludendorff Spring Offensive** (from 21 March 1918): stormtroopers broke through and advanced far, but outran
  supplies, lost elite troops and created a longer line to defend.
- **Allied counter-attack:** Foch as supreme commander; the **Battle of Amiens** (8 August 1918), the "black day"
  of the German army; the Hindenburg Line broken in late September.
- **Allies collapse:** Bulgaria, Turkey and Austria-Hungary left the war in September-November 1918.
- **Home front:** shortages, strikes and war-weariness; Ludendorff advised an armistice at the end of September; a
  new government under Prince Max of Baden asked for terms in October.
- **Revolution:** sailors at **Kiel** mutinied rather than sail on a final mission; councils spread; the Kaiser
  abdicated on 9 November; the **Armistice** was signed at Compiègne on 11 November 1918.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stalemate | Deadlock in which neither side can advance |
| Attrition | Wearing down the enemy through losses |
| No man's land | Ground between opposing trenches |
| Creeping barrage | Artillery fire moving forward just ahead of advancing infantry |
| BEF | British Expeditionary Force, the regular army sent in 1914 |
| ANZAC | Australian and New Zealand Army Corps |
| Convoy | Merchant ships sailing together under naval escort |
| Unrestricted submarine warfare | Sinking any ship in a war zone without warning |
| Stormtroopers | Specialist German assault troops used in 1918 |
| Armistice | Agreement to stop fighting |

## Exam traps

- **The plan versus its failure.** Describe how Schlieffen was meant to work before saying why it failed.
- **Jutland's result.** Do not call it a simple British victory or defeat; explain the strategic outcome.
- **Indian troops' role.** Most Indian infantry left the Western Front in 1915; do not exaggerate their time there.
- **The order of events in 1918.** Ludendorff asked for an armistice before the Kiel Mutiny.
- **Gas and tanks.** Important, but neither ended the stalemate alone; say why.

## Self-test

1. Which battle in September 1914 ended German hopes of a quick victory? *The Marne.*
2. Why did Moltke move troops east in August 1914? *Russia mobilised and invaded East Prussia faster than expected.*
3. Where was chlorine gas first used on a large scale? *Second Ypres, April 1915.*
4. When were tanks first used? *September 1916, at Flers-Courcelette during the Somme.*
5. What was Falkenhayn's aim at Verdun? *To "bleed France white" by attrition.*
6. Which ridge did Canadian troops capture in April 1917? *Vimy Ridge.*
7. Who led German forces in East Africa? *Paul von Lettow-Vorbeck.*
8. Which German base in China did Japan capture in 1914? *Tsingtao.*
9. Why was Jutland important despite heavier British losses? *The German fleet stayed in port for the rest of the war.*
10. Which method cut shipping losses to U-boats from 1917? *The convoy system.*
11. What did the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk allow Germany to do? *Move troops from the Eastern Front to the West for 1918.*
12. Why was 8 August 1918 called the "black day" of the German army? *The Allied attack at Amiens broke through and many Germans surrendered.*
