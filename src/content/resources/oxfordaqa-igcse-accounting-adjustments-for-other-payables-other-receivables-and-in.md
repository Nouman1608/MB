This guide is aligned to the OxfordAQA International GCSE Accounting (9215) specification, version 1.2, for exams from May/June 2026. It covers the adjustments listed in section 3.3 for other payables, other receivables, income due and income received in advance, and the instruction to make entries for them in ledger accounts, in income statements and in statements of financial position. Section 3.3 falls within the content assessed by Paper 1 (content 1 to 3) and Paper 2 (content 3 to 5). All amounts on this page are in dollars.

## What the specification asks for

| Specification wording | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|
| Adjustments: other payables | Record an expense that belongs to this year but has not been paid |
| Adjustments: other receivables | Record an expense paid this year that belongs to the next period |
| Adjustments: income due | Record income earned this year but not yet received |
| Adjustments: income received in advance | Record income received this year that belongs to the next period |
| Entries in ledger accounts | Balance the account, carry down and bring down the balance, transfer the charge |
| Entries in income statements and statements of financial position | Show the adjusted figure in the income statement and the balance in the right section of the statement of financial position |

Section 3.3 names the accruals concept in its list of concepts. Section 3.4 repeats these four items when it describes how concepts affect financial statements. This guide covers only the four items named in your scope; the other adjustments in the section 3.3 list are separate topics.

## The idea behind every adjustment

The accruals concept says that income and expenses belong to the period in which they are earned or used, not the period in which cash moves. A bank statement shows when money moved. The income statement must show what the business earned and used up in the year. The adjustments close the gap between the two.

Some books use the older names accrued expenses and prepayments. This guide uses the specification's wording.

| Adjustment | Is it an asset or a liability? | Effect on this year's profit | Where it appears in the statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Other payables | Liability (the business owes) | Expense goes up, profit goes down | Current liabilities |
| Other receivables | Asset (the business has paid early) | Expense goes down, profit goes up | Current assets |
| Income due | Asset (the business is owed) | Income goes up, profit goes up | Current assets |
| Income received in advance | Liability (the business owes service) | Income goes down, profit goes down | Current liabilities |

Each of these is short term, so each sits in the current section. Other receivables and income due are separate lines because one is an expense paid early and the other is income not yet received.

## Other payables

Other payables arise when the business has used something in the year but the invoice or bill has not been paid by the year end.

Ivybridge Cycle Hire, a sole trader, has a year end of 31 December 2026. The electricity account shows that 410 was owing on 1 January 2026. During the year the business paid 3,640. A bill for 520 for the last quarter arrived in January 2027 and covers use before the year end.

The charge for the year is the cash paid, less the amount owing at the start (which belongs to last year), plus the amount owing at the end: 3,640 - 410 + 520 = 3,750.

| Electricity account | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Debit** | | **Credit** | |
| Bank | 3,640 | Balance b/d (owing at start) | 410 |
| Balance c/d (owing at end) | 520 | Income statement | 3,750 |
| | **4,160** | | **4,160** |
| | | Balance b/d (owing at start of 2027) | 520 |

Follow the steps in this order. Enter the payments. Enter the opening balance on the credit side, because the account starts with a credit balance when money is owed. Insert the closing owing figure on the debit side as balance c/d. The balancing figure on the credit side is the transfer to the income statement. Finally bring the balance down on the credit side of the new period. The 520 is also shown as an other payable under current liabilities.

## Other receivables

Other receivables arise when the business has paid for something that it will use in a later period.

Ivybridge Cycle Hire paid insurance of 1,260 during 2026. On 1 January 2026 there was a prepaid amount of 300 brought forward from 2025. At 31 December 2026, 340 of the amount paid relates to 2027.

Charge for the year: 1,260 + 300 - 340 = 1,220.

| Insurance account | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Debit** | | **Credit** | |
| Balance b/d (paid in advance at start) | 300 | Income statement | 1,220 |
| Bank | 1,260 | Balance c/d (paid in advance at end) | 340 |
| | **1,560** | | **1,560** |
| Balance b/d (start of 2027) | 340 | | |

This time the opening balance is on the debit side, because the business has an asset at the start. The closing balance is carried down on the credit side, and the balancing figure on the credit side is the charge to the income statement.

### Working out the amount from the dates

Sometimes you have to work out the prepaid amount yourself. The business pays an annual operating licence of 1,800 on 1 April 2026. The licence covers twelve months and the year end is 31 December 2026.

- Months used in 2026: 9, so the charge is 1,800 x 9/12 = 1,350.
- Months left: 3, so the other receivable is 1,800 x 3/12 = 450.
- Check: 1,350 + 450 = 1,800.

## Income due

Income due arises when the business has earned income but has not yet received the cash.

Ivybridge Cycle Hire lets a storage unit to a neighbour. On 1 January 2026 the neighbour owed 350 for rent from 2025. During 2026 the business received 4,200. At 31 December 2026 the neighbour owes 700 for the last months of the year.

Income for the year: 4,200 - 350 + 700 = 4,550.

| Rent receivable account | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Debit** | | **Credit** | |
| Balance b/d (due at start) | 350 | Bank | 4,200 |
| Income statement | 4,550 | Balance c/d (due at end) | 700 |
| | **4,900** | | **4,900** |
| Balance b/d (start of 2027) | 700 | | |

The opening 350 is already an asset on the debit side, and its receipt in 2026 belongs to last year. The 700 closing is an asset, so it appears as balance c/d on the credit side and as balance b/d on the debit side. The 4,550 is transferred to the income statement as income.

## Income received in advance

Income received in advance arises when cash has arrived for a service the business has not yet provided.

Rowan Bay Kayaks charges for boat storage. At 1 January 2026 it held 600 received in 2025 for storage in 2026. During 2026 it received 5,100. Of that, 900 is for storage from January 2027.

Income for 2026: 5,100 + 600 - 900 = 4,800.

| Storage fees account | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Debit** | | **Credit** | |
| Income statement | 4,800 | Balance b/d (received in advance at start) | 600 |
| Balance c/d (received in advance at end) | 900 | Bank | 5,100 |
| | **5,700** | | **5,700** |
| | | Balance b/d (start of 2027) | 900 |

The 900 is a liability because the business owes the customer a service. It appears under current liabilities as income received in advance. It is not income for 2026.

## The four ledger patterns side by side

| Adjustment | Opening balance sits on | Closing balance carried down on | Closing balance brought down on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Other payables (expense account) | Credit | Debit | Credit |
| Other receivables (expense account) | Debit | Credit | Debit |
| Income due (income account) | Debit | Credit | Debit |
| Income received in advance (income account) | Credit | Debit | Credit |

Always finish by checking that both sides of the account have the same total.

## Effects on the financial statements

Sandmere Print Works, a sole trader, has a year end of 30 June 2026. The gross profit is 61,500. Ledger work has produced these figures:

| Item | Paid or received | Adjustment | Figure for the year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wages | 24,000 paid | 800 owing | 24,800 |
| Rates | 4,800 paid | 1,200 paid in advance | 3,600 |
| Heat and light | 3,100 paid | 450 owing | 3,550 |
| Insurance | 1,500 paid | 250 paid in advance | 1,250 |
| Rent received | 3,000 received | 250 due | 3,250 |
| Design fees | 5,400 received | 900 received in advance | 4,500 |

Income statement extract for the year ended 30 June 2026:

| | |
|---|---|
| Gross profit | 61,500 |
| Add rent received | 3,250 |
| Add design fees | 4,500 |
| | **69,250** |
| Less wages | 24,800 |
| Less rates | 3,600 |
| Less heat and light | 3,550 |
| Less insurance | 1,250 |
| Total expenses | **33,200** |
| Net profit | **36,050** |

Statement of financial position extract at 30 June 2026:

| Current assets | |
|---|---|
| Other receivables (rates 1,200 and insurance 250) | 1,450 |
| Income due (rent) | 250 |
| **Current liabilities** | |
| Other payables (wages 800 and heat and light 450) | 1,250 |
| Income received in advance (design fees) | 900 |

Each adjustment appears twice: once in the income statement and once as a balance in the statement of financial position. If you have changed the profit but have no balance to show, or the reverse, something is missing.

## Correcting a draft profit

A draft profit is sometimes prepared from cash records before the year-end adjustments. Podmore Laundry is in its first year, so there are no opening balances. The draft net profit is 18,450. The adjustments not yet made are wages owing 640, rent paid in advance 1,100, commission due 210 and rent received in advance 480.

| Adjustment | Effect on profit | Running profit |
|---|---|---|
| Draft profit | | 18,450 |
| Wages owing | - 640 | 17,810 |
| Rent paid in advance | + 1,100 | 18,910 |
| Commission due | + 210 | 19,120 |
| Rent received in advance | - 480 | 18,640 |

Adjusted net profit is 18,640. The net change is +190, from -640 + 1,100 + 210 - 480.

## Common errors

- Adding the opening balance to the payments in an expense account with other payables. An opening amount owing is last year's cost, so it is taken off the cash paid.
- Putting a balance brought down on the wrong side. Check whether the item is an asset (debit) or a liability (credit).
- Treating income received in advance as income for the year. It is a liability until the service is given.
- Listing other payables as non-current liabilities, or other receivables among non-current assets. All four items are short term.
- Applying the fraction to the wrong period. Count the months used in the year, then the months left, and check that they add to the full period.
- Changing the ledger but leaving the statement figure alone, or the other way round.
