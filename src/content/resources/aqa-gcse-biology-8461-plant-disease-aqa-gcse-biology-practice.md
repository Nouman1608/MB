These questions cover section **4.3.3 Plant disease** (4.3.3.1 and 4.3.3.2) of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461)
specification, for first teaching from 2016. The section is Biology only. Questions on the signs of disease and
on identification are labelled **Higher tier only**. All data are invented for practice. A calculator is useful
for question 6.

Learn the content first in the [Plant disease study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-plant-disease-aqa-gcse-biology/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-plant-disease-aqa-gcse-biology-revision-notes/).
The [Infection and response practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-infection-and-response-practice/)
cover the whole topic; the questions below are different from those.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** State whether each plant defence is physical, chemical or mechanical.

**(a)** A waxy cuticle on the leaves **[1]**
**(b)** Thorns on the stem **[1]**
**(c)** Antibacterial chemicals in the leaves **[1]**
**(d)** Mimicry **[1]**

**2.**

**(a)** Name the ion a plant needs to make chlorophyll. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the condition caused by a deficiency of this ion. **[1]**

**3.** (Higher tier only) From this list, choose the **two** that are methods of identifying a plant disease
rather than signs of disease: spots on leaves; using a testing kit containing monoclonal antibodies; stunted
growth; taking an infected plant to a laboratory; presence of pests. **[2]**

**4.** Explain how the bark on a tree helps to protect it from pathogens. **[2]**

**5.** The leaves of some plants droop or curl when they are touched. Suggest how this protects the plant from
insects. **[2]**

### Section B: longer and structured questions

**6.** A student grows bean seedlings in three mineral solutions for three weeks.

| Solution | Mean height after 3 weeks (cm) | Leaf colour |
|---|---|---|
| All ions | 18 | Green |
| All ions except nitrate | 7 | Green |
| All ions except magnesium | 14 | Yellow |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in mean height without nitrate, compared with all ions. Give your
answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the result for the seedlings grown without nitrate. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the seedlings grown without magnesium had yellow leaves and grew less than those with all
ions. **[3]**
**(d)** Give **two** variables the student should keep the same. **[2]**

**7.** A gardener compares three ways of controlling black spot on rose bushes. After eight weeks she records
the mean percentage of leaves with black spot.

| Treatment | Mean percentage of leaves with black spot |
|---|---|
| None | 64 |
| Affected leaves removed and destroyed each week | 30 |
| Fungicide sprayed each week | 18 |

**(a)** Describe the effect of the two treatments. Use the data. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why removing and destroying affected leaves reduces the spread of black spot. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest **one** reason the gardener might choose to remove leaves rather than spray fungicide. **[1]**

**8.** A gardener's bean plants are stunted. She thinks the cause is nitrate deficiency, aphids or a pathogen.

**(a)** (Higher tier only) Describe how she could find out which is the cause. **[3]**
**(b)** Aphids feed on the sap in the phloem. Explain why large numbers of aphids reduce the growth of a plant. **[2]**

**9.** A tomato plant is infected with tobacco mosaic virus and is also growing in soil low in magnesium.
Explain why this plant grows much less than a plant with only one of these problems. **[3]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) Compare using a gardening website with using a testing kit containing monoclonal
antibodies to identify a plant disease. **[4]**

**11.** Read the information.

*Some plants have small yellow marks on their leaves that look like butterfly eggs. Female butterflies tend not
to lay eggs on leaves that already seem to have eggs on them. Caterpillars that hatch from the eggs feed on the
leaves.*

**(a)** Suggest how the yellow marks protect the plant. **[2]**
**(b)** Another plant makes a poison in its leaves. Explain how this defends the plant even though a herbivore
may eat a few leaves before it is affected. **[2]**

**12.** Describe and explain the ways plants defend themselves against microorganisms and against animals. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Physical [1]
**(b)** Mechanical [1]
**(c)** Chemical [1]
**(d)** Mechanical [1]
*Examiner insight:* The specification lists thorns under mechanical adaptations, so "physical" does not earn (b).

**2. (a)** Magnesium (ions) [1]
**(b)** Chlorosis [1]

**3.** Using a testing kit containing monoclonal antibodies [1]; taking an infected plant to a laboratory [1]
*Examiner insight:* Ticking a third option cancels one correct choice.

**4.** Bark is made of layers of dead cells that act as a barrier [1]; the layers fall off, removing pathogens on them [1]

**5.** Insects may be shaken off or fall off [1]; or the plant looks less appealing, so animals are deterred from feeding [1]
*Examiner insight:* Any two linked points about dislodging or deterring earn the marks; "it hides" alone is too vague.

**6. (a)** (18 − 7) ÷ 18 × 100 [1] = **61.1%** [1]
**(b)** Nitrate is needed to make amino acids and so proteins [1]; without proteins, new cells cannot be made, so growth is stunted [1]
**(c)** Magnesium is needed to make chlorophyll, so less chlorophyll was made and the leaves were yellow (chlorosis) [1]; less light was absorbed, so less photosynthesis took place [1]; less glucose was made for growth [1]
**(d)** Any two: temperature; light intensity; volume of solution; type or age of seedlings; time grown [2]
*Examiner insight:* Allow one mark per correct variable in (d). "Amount of water" is accepted only if it refers to the solution volume.

**7. (a)** Both treatments reduced the percentage of leaves with black spot compared with no treatment (64%) [1]; fungicide had the larger effect (18%) than removing leaves (30%) [1]
**(b)** The affected leaves contain the fungus [1]; destroying them means fewer spores are spread by water or wind to healthy leaves [1]
**(c)** Any one: fungicide costs money; fungicide may harm other organisms; removing leaves needs no chemicals [1]

**8. (a)** Look for aphids on the plants (presence of pests) [1]; compare the symptoms with a gardening manual or website [1]; take an infected plant to a laboratory, or use a testing kit, to identify any pathogen [1]
**(b)** The phloem sap contains sugars made in photosynthesis [1]; the aphids remove these sugars, so less is available for the plant's growth [1]

**9.** The virus causes discolouration of the leaves [1]; magnesium deficiency means less chlorophyll is made [1]; both reduce photosynthesis, so the plant makes even less glucose for growth [1]
*Examiner insight:* The third mark needs the idea that both problems act on photosynthesis, so their effects add together.

**10.** One mark for each paired comparison, up to 4:
- a website is free or cheap, whereas a testing kit costs money [1]
- a website relies on matching signs, which can be similar for different causes, whereas the kit is specific to one pathogen [1]
- a kit detects only the pathogen it is made for, whereas a website can suggest many possible causes [1]
- both can be used by the gardener on site without sending the plant to a laboratory [1]

**11. (a)** Butterflies avoid laying eggs on the leaves [1]; so fewer caterpillars hatch to eat the leaves [1]
**(b)** The herbivore is harmed or finds the leaves unpleasant [1]; so it, or others, learn to avoid the plant and most leaves are not eaten [1]

**12.** One mark for each point:
- cellulose cell walls form a barrier that resists microorganisms [1]
- a tough waxy cuticle covers the leaves, stopping microorganisms entering [1]
- bark is made of layers of dead cells that fall off, removing pathogens [1]
- antibacterial chemicals kill bacteria [1]
- poisons deter herbivores [1]
- thorns and hairs, leaves that droop or curl when touched, or mimicry deter or trick animals [1]
*Examiner insight:* The top marks need defences against both microorganisms and animals, each with how it works.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping nitrate (protein, growth) with magnesium (chlorophyll).
- Calling thorns a physical defence.
- Choosing signs of disease when methods of identification are asked for.
- Describing a data trend without quoting values.
- Explaining poor growth without the link through photosynthesis to glucose.
