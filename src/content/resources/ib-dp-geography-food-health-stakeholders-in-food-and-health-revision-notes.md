Condensed recall for **6.3 Stakeholders in food and health** (IB DP Geography, Option F: Food and health). The [Food and health study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-geography-food-health-study-guide/) has the longer account.

## Stakeholders (household to global)

| Stakeholder | Role |
|---|---|
| Governments | Set agricultural, trade, health and nutrition policy; invest in infrastructure |
| International organisations (UN agencies) | Coordinate responses, set standards, provide emergency aid |
| NGOs and charities | Deliver projects, advocacy and relief |
| Transnational corporations | Seeds, fertiliser, food processing, retail, pharmaceuticals |
| Farmers and communities | Produce food; hold local knowledge |
| Consumers | Choices influence demand |

## Strategies

- Agricultural innovation: Green Revolution style high-yield seeds, fertiliser and irrigation
- Genetically modified crops
- Better storage and transport to cut post-harvest loss
- Nutrition programmes
- Vaccination campaigns and primary health care
- Clean water and sanitation

## Trade-offs

| Strategy | Benefit | Drawback |
|---|---|---|
| High-input agriculture | Higher yields | Pollution; costs for small farmers |
| Aid | Saves lives | Dependency if not linked to local capacity |
| GM crops | Potential yield or resilience gains | Debate over access, cost and control |

## Evaluating

Ask: **who gains**, **who is excluded**, and **is it lasting**? Consider effectiveness, cost, equity and sustainability.

## Exam traps

- Assuming one technology, such as GM crops, is simply good or bad.
- Listing stakeholders without saying what each wants or controls.
- Treating aid as always sustainable.
- Naming a programme with invented outcomes.

## Self-test

1. Name four stakeholder groups in food and health.
2. What did Green Revolution style farming rely on?
3. Give one benefit and one drawback of high-input farming.
4. Why can aid create dependency?
5. Which three questions help evaluate a strategy?

**Answers:** 1. For example governments, UN agencies, NGOs, transnational corporations. 2. High-yield seeds, fertiliser and irrigation. 3. Higher yields; pollution or costs for small farmers. 4. If it is not linked to local capacity, communities rely on outside supply. 5. Who gains, who is excluded, and is it lasting?
