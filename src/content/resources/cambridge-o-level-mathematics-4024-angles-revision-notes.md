These notes summarise section 4.6, Angles, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Full explanations are in the [angles study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-angles/), and exam-style questions are in the [angles practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-angles-practice/).

## What is examined

- Calculate unknown angles and give simple explanations using angles at a point, on a straight line, vertically opposite, and in triangles and quadrilaterals.
- Do the same for angles in parallel lines: corresponding, alternate and co-interior.
- Know and use angle properties of regular and irregular polygons, including exterior and interior angles and the angle sum.
- Use three-letter notation and correct geometrical terminology in reasons.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Transversal | a line crossing two or more other lines |
| Corresponding angles | in matching positions at the two crossings (F shape) |
| Alternate angles | on opposite sides of the transversal, between the lines (Z shape) |
| Co-interior angles | on the same side of the transversal, between the lines (C shape) |
| Interior angle | inside a polygon at a vertex |
| Exterior angle | outside, between a side and the extension of the next |
| Supplementary | adding up to 180° |

## The facts and the reasons to write

| Fact | Reason |
|---|---|
| angles at a point sum to 360° | angles at a point |
| angles on a straight line sum to 180° | angles on a straight line |
| vertically opposite angles are equal | vertically opposite angles |
| triangle angles sum to 180° | angle sum of a triangle |
| quadrilateral angles sum to 360° | angle sum of a quadrilateral |
| isosceles base angles are equal | isosceles triangle |
| equilateral angles are 60° | equilateral triangle |
| exterior angle = sum of two opposite interior angles | exterior angle of a triangle |
| corresponding angles are equal | corresponding angles, parallel lines |
| alternate angles are equal | alternate angles, parallel lines |
| co-interior angles sum to 180° | co-interior angles, parallel lines |

## Polygons

```
interior angle sum = (n − 2) × 180°
exterior angles of any polygon sum to 360°
interior + exterior at a vertex = 180°
regular: exterior = 360° ÷ n,  interior = 180° − exterior
```

| n | Name | Angle sum | Regular: interior | Regular: exterior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | pentagon | 540° | 108° | 72° |
| 6 | hexagon | 720° | 120° | 60° |
| 8 | octagon | 1080° | 135° | 45° |
| 10 | decagon | 1440° | 144° | 36° |
| 12 | dodecagon | 1800° | 150° | 30° |

## Method box: an angle chase

1. Write the angles you know on the diagram.
2. Pick a fact that needs only known angles.
3. Find one new angle and write the reason beside it.
4. Repeat until you have the angle asked for.
5. For algebra, form an equation from the fact and solve.

### Example

Angle ABC and angle BCD are co-interior with AB parallel to CD. Angle ABC = (2x + 10)° and angle BCD = (3x − 5)°.

```
(2x + 10) + (3x − 5) = 180
5x + 5 = 180
x = 35, so the angles are 80° and 100°
```

## Working with a parallel line drawn through a vertex

A line through a vertex of a triangle parallel to the opposite side creates two alternate angles. The three angles at the vertex then form a straight line, which proves the angle sum of a triangle is 180°.

## Angle facts for special quadrilaterals

| Shape | Angle facts |
|---|---|
| Parallelogram | opposite angles equal; angles next to each other sum to 180° |
| Rhombus | as a parallelogram; diagonals bisect the vertex angles |
| Rectangle and square | all four angles are 90° |
| Kite | one pair of opposite angles equal (those between unequal sides) |
| Trapezium | the two angles on each non-parallel side sum to 180° |
| Isosceles trapezium | base angles equal in pairs |

### Example

In a kite ABCD with AB = AD and CB = CD, angle BAD = 80° and angle ABC = 110°. Then angle ADC = 110° and angle BCD = 360° − 80° − 110° − 110° = 60°.

## Exterior angle of a triangle

The exterior angle at a vertex equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles. This avoids a step: for a triangle with interior angles 50° and 60° at two vertices, the exterior angle at the third vertex is 110° straight away.

## Must-know distinctions

| Alternate | Co-interior |
|---|---|
| equal | sum to 180° |
| Z shape | C shape |
| opposite sides of the transversal | same side of the transversal |

| Interior | Exterior |
|---|---|
| sum is (n − 2) × 180° | sum is always 360° |

## Exam traps

- Writing "Z angles" or "F angles" as the reason.
- Using parallel-line rules when the lines are not marked parallel.
- Using the wrong total: 180° for a triangle, 360° for a quadrilateral.
- Using (n − 2) × 180 for the exterior angle sum.
- Giving the number of sides as 360 ÷ interior angle. Use the exterior angle.
- Not naming angles with three letters.

## Command words you will meet

| Command word | What to do |
|---|---|
| Calculate | work out, showing each step with a reason |
| Give a reason | quote the geometrical fact |
| Explain | set out why, using the facts |
| Show that | give structured evidence that leads to the stated result |
| Work out | calculate, with or without a calculator |

## Self-test

1. Angles on a straight line are 3x, 2x and 50. Find x.
2. In an isosceles triangle, the apex angle is 38°. Find a base angle.
3. Two co-interior angles are 115° and y. Find y.
4. Find the sum of the interior angles of a nonagon (9 sides).
5. Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 18°. Find the number of sides.
6. Find one interior angle of a regular octagon.
7. An exterior angle of a triangle is 130° and one opposite interior angle is 75°. Find the other.
8. Four angles at a point are x, 2x, 3x and 90°. Find x.
9. Two vertically opposite angles are (5x + 3)° and (7x − 13)°. Find x.
10. Four angles of a pentagon are 90°, 100°, 120° and 130°. Find the fifth.

### Answers

1. 5x + 50 = 180, so **x = 26**.
2. (180 − 38) ÷ 2 = **71°**.
3. 180 − 115 = **65°**.
4. 7 × 180 = **1260°**.
5. 360 ÷ 18 = **20 sides**.
6. 180 − 45 = **135°**.
7. 130 − 75 = **55°**.
8. 6x + 90 = 360, so **x = 45**.
9. 5x + 3 = 7x − 13, so 16 = 2x and **x = 8**.
10. 540 − (90 + 100 + 120 + 130) = 540 − 440 = **100°**.
