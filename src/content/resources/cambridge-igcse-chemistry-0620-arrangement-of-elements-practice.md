These questions cover subtopic 8.1 Arrangement of elements of Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620, 2026–2028: the structure of the Periodic Table, metallic and non-metallic character, group number and ion charge, group similarities and predicting properties. All data given are real values.

## Section A

**1.** State the order in which elements are arranged in the Periodic Table. **[1]**

**2.** An atom has the electronic configuration 2,8,8,1.

**(a)** State the period of the element. **[1]**

**(b)** State the group of the element. **[1]**

**3.** Which element forms an ion with a 2− charge?

A: magnesium

B: sulfur

C: aluminium

D: chlorine **[1]**

**4.** State the charge on the ions formed by Group III elements. **[1]**

**5.** Describe how the character of the elements changes across a period from left to right. **[1]**

**6.** State the name given to a horizontal row in the Periodic Table. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Lithium, sodium and potassium are in Group I.

**(a)** Explain, in terms of electronic configuration, why they have similar chemical properties. **[2]**

**(b)** Give the formula of the compound formed between potassium and bromine. **[1]**

**8.** Element E is in Group II and Period 4.

**(a)** State the number of outer-shell electrons in an atom of E. **[1]**

**(b)** State the number of occupied electron shells in an atom of E. **[1]**

**(c)** Predict whether E is a metal or a non-metal. Give a reason. **[2]**

**(d)** Predict the formula of the chloride of E. **[1]**

**9.** The table shows information about elements in Period 3.

| Element | Sodium | Magnesium | Sulfur | Chlorine |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conducts electricity when solid | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Type of oxide | Basic | Basic | Acidic | Acidic |

Use the information to describe the change in character across Period 3. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why atoms of Group VII elements form ions with a 1− charge. **[2]**

**11.** Nitrogen is in Group V and magnesium is in Group II.

**(a)** Give the formulae of the ions formed. **[2]**

**(b)** Deduce the formula of magnesium nitride. **[1]**

**12.** *(Extended)* The table shows the melting points of some Group I elements.

| Element | Melting point / °C |
|---|---|
| Lithium | 181 |
| Sodium | 98 |
| Potassium | 63 |
| Rubidium | 39 |

**(a)** Describe the trend in melting point down the group. **[1]**

**(b)** Predict a value for the melting point of caesium, which is below rubidium. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest whether caesium would be solid or liquid on a hot day at 35 °C. Explain. **[2]**

**13.** Germanium is in Group IV, below silicon. Silicon has a giant covalent structure and a very high melting point. Suggest **one** property of germanium and explain how its position helps you to predict it. **[2]**

**14.** The proton numbers of four elements are: P = 9, Q = 11, R = 16, T = 18. (These letters are not chemical symbols.)

**(a)** Give the electronic configuration of element R. **[1]**

**(b)** Which **two** elements are in the same period as each other and form ions? Give the charges of their ions. **[3]**

**(c)** Which element forms no ions? Explain why. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Increasing proton number (atomic number) [1].

**2. (a)** Period 4 [1].

**(b)** Group I [1].

**3.** **B** [1].

**4.** 3+ [1].

**5.** Changes from metallic to non-metallic [1].

**6.** A period [1].

**7. (a)** They all have one electron in the outer shell [1]; chemical reactions involve the outer-shell electrons, so they react in the same way / each forms a 1+ ion [1].

**(b)** KBr [1].

**8. (a)** 2 [1].

**(b)** 4 [1].

**(c)** Metal [1]; it is on the left of the period / in Group II (loses two electrons to form a positive ion) [1].

**(d)** ECl₂ [1].

**9.** Sodium and magnesium are metals (they conduct and form basic oxides) [1]; sulfur and chlorine are non-metals (they do not conduct and form acidic oxides), so the elements change from metallic to non-metallic across the period [1].

**10.** They have seven electrons in the outer shell [1]; they gain one electron to obtain a full outer shell (noble gas configuration), giving a 1− charge [1].

**11. (a)** N³⁻ [1]; Mg²⁺ [1].

**(b)** Mg₃N₂ [1].

**12. (a)** Melting point decreases down the group [1].

**(b)** Any value below 39 °C, e.g. 25–35 °C [1].

**(c)** Liquid (or at/near its melting point) [1]; its melting point is predicted to be below 35 °C (continuing the decreasing trend) [1].

**13.** Germanium is likely to have a high melting point / a giant structure [1]; it is in the same group as silicon, so it has the same number of outer electrons (four) and similar bonding [1]. (Accept other properties justified by its group position, e.g. does not form simple ions.)

**14. (a)** 2,8,6 [1].

**(b)** Q and R (both Period 3) [1]; Q forms a 1+ ion [1]; R forms a 2− ion [1].

**(c)** T (configuration 2,8,8) [1]; it has a full outer shell, so it does not need to lose or gain electrons [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the Periodic Table is arranged by relative atomic mass.
- Confusing period (number of shells) with group (number of outer electrons).
- Writing Group VI and Group V ion charges as 6− and 5−.
- Explaining group similarity with "same number of shells".
- Giving a prediction without the reason when the question says "explain".
- Predicting a data value that goes against the trend shown.
