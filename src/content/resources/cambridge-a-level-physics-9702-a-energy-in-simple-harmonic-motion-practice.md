## Section A

**1.** A trolley attached between two springs on a smooth horizontal track is pulled to one side and released. Describe the energy changes during one complete oscillation. **[3]**

**2.** An oscillator has total energy E.

**(a)** State the total energy if the amplitude is halved, with the frequency unchanged.

**(b)** State the total energy if the frequency is tripled, with the amplitude unchanged. **[2]**

**3.** Show that the kinetic and potential energies of an oscillator in SHM are equal when the displacement is x₀/√2. **[2]**

**4.** Determine, as a fraction of the amplitude, the displacement at which the kinetic energy is three quarters of the total energy. **[2]**

**5.** The displacement of an oscillator has a period of 0.80 s. State the period of the variation of its kinetic energy. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A 0.25 kg mass oscillates in SHM with amplitude 0.060 m and angular frequency 8.0 rad s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the total energy. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the maximum speed, and use it to check your answer to (a). **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the potential energy and kinetic energy when the displacement is 0.036 m. **[3]**

**7.** A 0.50 kg mass on a spring oscillates at 2.0 Hz with total energy 0.12 J.

**(a)** Calculate the amplitude. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the speed when the displacement is 0.030 m. **[2]**

**8.** Describe the graphs of kinetic energy, potential energy and total energy against displacement, from x = −x₀ to x = +x₀, for an undamped oscillator. State the key features you would show on a sketch. **[4]**

**9.** A pendulum bob of mass 0.20 kg is released from rest at a height of 0.040 m above its lowest point. The pendulum has a period of 2.0 s and its motion may be treated as simple harmonic.

**(a)** Calculate the total energy of the oscillation. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the speed of the bob at its lowest point. **[2]**

**(c)** Use your answer to (b) to calculate the amplitude of the horizontal displacement of the bob. **[2]**

**10.** Two mass–spring systems, P and Q, have equal masses and equal amplitudes. The frequency of P is twice the frequency of Q.

**(a)** Calculate the ratio (total energy of P) ÷ (total energy of Q).

**(b)** Calculate the ratio (maximum speed of P) ÷ (maximum speed of Q). **[3]**

**11.** A graph of potential energy against displacement for an oscillating 0.40 kg mass is a parabola that reaches 0.090 J at x = ±0.030 m.

**(a)** State the total energy of the oscillation. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the angular frequency. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the kinetic energy when x = 0.015 m. **[2]**

**12.** A student states: "At half the amplitude, the energy of an oscillator is shared equally between kinetic and potential energy." Explain why the student is wrong, giving the correct fractions at half the amplitude. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** At release the trolley is at rest at maximum displacement: kinetic energy zero, elastic potential energy maximum [1]. As it moves to equilibrium, elastic potential energy is converted to kinetic energy, which is maximum at the equilibrium position [1]. It then slows as kinetic energy is converted back to elastic potential energy at the other extreme, and this repeats on the return; the **total energy stays constant** [1].

**2. (a)** E ∝ x₀², so **E/4** [1].

**(b)** E ∝ ω² ∝ f², so **9E** [1].

**3.** E_P = ½mω²x² = ½mω²(x₀²/2) = ¼mω²x₀² [1], which is **E/2**; so E_K = E − E/2 = E/2 as well [1].

**4.** E_K = ½mω²(x₀² − x²) = ¾ × ½mω²x₀², so x₀² − x² = ¾x₀² [1]; x² = ¼x₀², **x = x₀/2** [1].

**5.** **0.40 s** (half the period of the displacement) [1].

**6. (a)** E = ½mω²x₀² = 0.5 × 0.25 × 8.0² × 0.060² [1] = **0.0288 J** (2.9 × 10⁻² J) [1].

**(b)** v₀ = ωx₀ = 8.0 × 0.060 = **0.48 m s⁻¹** [1]; ½mv₀² = 0.5 × 0.25 × 0.48² = 0.0288 J, which agrees [1].

**(c)** E_P = ½mω²x² = 0.5 × 0.25 × 64 × 0.036² [1] = **0.0104 J** [1]; E_K = 0.0288 − 0.0104 = **0.0184 J** [1].

**7. (a)** ω = 2π × 2.0 = 12.6 rad s⁻¹ [1]. x₀ = √(2E ÷ mω²) = √(0.24 ÷ (0.50 × 12.6²)) [1] = **0.055 m** [1].

**(b)** v = ω√(x₀² − x²) = 12.6 × √(0.0551² − 0.030²) [1] = **0.58 m s⁻¹** [1].

**8.** Potential energy: a **parabola**, zero at x = 0, rising to E at x = ±x₀ [1]. Kinetic energy: an **inverted parabola**, maximum E at x = 0, zero at x = ±x₀ [1]. Total energy: a **horizontal line** at E [1]. The two curves cross at x = ±x₀/√2, where each is E/2 [1].

**9. (a)** E = mgh = 0.20 × 9.81 × 0.040 = **0.0785 J** [1].

**(b)** ½mv₀² = 0.0785 J [1]; v₀ = √(2 × 9.81 × 0.040) = **0.886 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(c)** ω = 2π ÷ 2.0 = 3.14 rad s⁻¹ [1]; x₀ = v₀ ÷ ω = 0.886 ÷ 3.14 = **0.28 m** [1].

**10. (a)** E ∝ ω² at the same mass and amplitude [1], so the ratio is 2² = **4** [1].

**(b)** v₀ = ωx₀, so the ratio is **2** [1].

**11. (a)** **0.090 J** (all energy is potential at the extremes) [1].

**(b)** 0.090 = 0.5 × 0.40 × ω² × 0.030², so ω² = 500 [1]; ω = **22 rad s⁻¹** [1].

**(c)** E_P = 0.090 × (0.015 ÷ 0.030)² = 0.0225 J [1]; E_K = 0.090 − 0.0225 = **0.068 J** [1].

**12.** Potential energy is proportional to **x²**, not x [1]. At x = x₀/2, E_P = E × (½)² = **E/4** [1], so E_K = **3E/4**; the energies are equal only at x₀/√2 [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the displacement x instead of the amplitude x₀ for the total energy.
- Using f instead of ω in ½mω²x₀².
- Assuming energy scales linearly with amplitude or frequency.
- Assuming kinetic and potential energies are equal at half the amplitude.
- Omitting the constant total energy in a description of the interchange.
- Forgetting that the energy varies with half the period of the displacement.
