Component 3 is the non-examined (coursework) alternative to Component 2 in Pearson Edexcel International
GCSE English Language A (4EA1). You write **two assignments, each out of 30**: Assignment A, an essay of
about 1200 words on three texts from Part 2 of the Anthology (at least one poem and one prose text), and
Assignment B, about 700 words of imaginative writing. Together they make 60 marks and 40% of the
qualification. For the full explanation, use the
[Component 3 study guide](/resources/igcse-edexcel-english-language-component-3/).

## Format at a glance

| Detail | Assignment A: Poetry and Prose Texts | Assignment B: Imaginative Writing |
|---|---|---|
| Marks | 30 (AO1 12, AO2 18) | 30 (AO4 18, AO5 12) |
| Advisory length | About 1200 words | About 700 words |
| Texts | Three from Anthology Part 2, at least one poetry and one prose | None required; topic may be suggested by an Anthology text or images |
| Purpose | Analyse ideas, perspectives, language and structure | Narrate, describe or entertain |
| Set by | Teacher or student (self-generated) | Teacher-devised |

**Component total:** 60 marks, 40% of the International GCSE, split Reading 20% and Writing 20%.
Internally assessed, externally moderated by Pearson. Available June and November.

The word counts are **advisory**: the specification says there is no penalty for exceeding them.

## Route check

Every student takes Component 1 (Non-fiction Texts and Transactional Writing, 60%). Students then take
**either** Component 2 (the examined version, 1 hour 30 minutes) **or** Component 3. You cannot do both.

## Anthology Part 2 texts

| Poetry | Prose |
|---|---|
| "Disabled", Wilfred Owen | "The Story of an Hour", Kate Chopin |
| "Out, Out–", Robert Frost | "The Necklace", Guy de Maupassant |
| "An Unknown Girl", Moniza Alvi | "Significant Cigarettes" (from *The Road Home*), Rose Tremain |
| "The Bright Lights of Sarajevo", Tony Harrison | "Whistle and I'll Come to You" (from *The Woman in Black*), Susan Hill |
| "Still I Rise", Maya Angelou | "Night", Alice Munro |

## Assignment A: what the task must do

- Choose **three** Part 2 texts with at least one poem and one prose text.
- Choose a **focus**: the specification suggests setting, character(s), event(s) or theme(s).
- The suggested task format: *Discuss how the writers use language and structure to present [focus] in
  [text 1], [text 2] and [text 3].*
- The response should discuss the writers' ideas and perspectives, how language and structure achieve
  effects, and include textual references.

### Assignment A assessment objectives

| AO | Wording | Marks | Top level (key words) |
|---|---|---|---|
| AO1 | Read and understand a variety of texts, selecting and interpreting information, ideas and perspectives | 12 | Level 4 (10–12): "detailed and persuasive selection", "discriminating use" of quotation, "perceptive interpretation" |
| AO2 | Understand and analyse how writers use linguistic and structural devices to achieve their effects | 18 | Level 5 (15–18): "subtle and discriminating selection", "perceptive analysis" |

AO1 has four levels (1–3, 4–6, 7–9, 10–12); AO2 has five (1–3, 4–6, 7–10, 11–14, 15–18). Because AO2 is
worth 18 of the 30 marks, analysis of methods matters more than the number of ideas.

### Choosing a focus that works for three texts

| Focus | Possible trio | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Loss of youth or life | "Disabled", "Out, Out–", "The Necklace" | Each turns on a single irreversible event |
| Freedom and constraint | "The Story of an Hour", "Still I Rise", "An Unknown Girl" | Contrasting perspectives on identity and confinement |
| Fear and the unknown | "Whistle and I'll Come to You", "Night", "The Bright Lights of Sarajevo" | Darkness, fog and uncertainty handled very differently |

## Assignment B: what the task must do

- A teacher-devised task allowing access to AO4 and AO5.
- Purpose: **narrate, describe or entertain**.
- The topic may be suggested by an Anthology piece (for example, continue Lev's story after "Significant
  Cigarettes", or begin with a line taken from a text) or be unrelated, such as "The visitor".
- You may write about real or imagined experiences.

### Assignment B assessment objectives

| AO | Wording | Marks | Levels |
|---|---|---|---|
| AO4 | Communicate effectively and imaginatively, adapting form, tone and register for specific purposes and audiences | 18 | 1–3, 4–7, 8–11, 12–15, 16–18 |
| AO5 | Write clearly, using a range of vocabulary and sentence structures, with appropriate paragraphing and accurate spelling, grammar and punctuation | 12 | 1–2, 3–4, 5–7, 8–10, 11–12 |

The AO4 top level asks for communication that is "perceptive and subtle" and a "sophisticated use of form,
tone and register". The AO5 top level asks you to "manipulate complex ideas" and use "extensive vocabulary
strategically".

## Worked example: lifting an Assignment A sentence

**Level 2 style:** "In 'Disabled' the man is sad because he lost his legs and Owen uses lots of
description."

**Level 4–5 style:** "Owen builds the poem on contrast between past and present tense: the remembered
evening when 'glow-lamps budded in the light blue trees' is placed beside the cold present of the hospital, so the
reader experiences the loss as a sudden drop. Maupassant uses a similar hinge in 'The Necklace', where
one revelation in the final line reframes ten years of labour."

The second version names structural devices, explains their effect (AO2) and connects two texts' ideas
(AO1).

## Rules on authenticity and feedback

- Work independently; both you and your teacher sign the authentication sheet.
- Teachers may suggest **general** improvements at draft stage; you must make any corrections yourself.
- Work must **not** be rewritten after it has been marked.
- Acknowledge sources; teachers must look out for essays derived from the internet or artificial
  intelligence (AI).
- Each piece shows your name, the date completed, the mark and a brief teacher comment.

## Common mistakes / exam traps

- **Only two texts, or three poems.** Assignment A needs three texts including at least one poem and one
  prose text.
- **Retelling stories.** AO1 Level 1 describes understanding "likely through retelling or paraphrasing".
- **Feature-spotting.** Naming a simile without explaining its effect stays at AO2 Level 2.
- **Treating Assignment B as an essay.** Its purpose is to narrate, describe or entertain.
- **Ignoring AO5.** Twelve marks rest on vocabulary, sentence variety, paragraphing and accuracy;
  proofread before submission.
- **Expecting to revise after marking.** Once marked, the work cannot be rewritten.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Component 3 easier than Component 2?**
It carries the same 60 marks and 40% weighting, and covers the same skills. The difference is that you
have time to plan and draft instead of writing under exam conditions.

**Can everyone in my class write about the same three texts?**
Yes. The specification says there is no requirement for students in a cohort to write on different
texts.

**What happens if I go over 1200 words?**
The word counts are advisory and there is no penalty for exceeding them, but focus usually matters more
than length.

**Can my teacher correct my draft?**
No. Teachers may suggest general improvements at the draft stage, but it is your responsibility to make
corrections.
