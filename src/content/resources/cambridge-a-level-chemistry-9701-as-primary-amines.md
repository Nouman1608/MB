This guide covers subtopic **19.1, Primary amines**, from Topic 19, Nitrogen compounds, of Cambridge
International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027 series. It is AS Level content. The
[Nitrogen Compounds: Amines and Nitriles study guide](/resources/as-nitrogen-compounds-amines-and-nitriles/)
states the reaction with bromoethane; this page explains each condition, shows the mechanism step by
step, practises equations and names for other halogenoalkanes, and uses the reaction in calculations and
synthesis routes.

## What the syllabus asks for

19.1 has a single learning outcome. Candidates should be able to recall the reactions by which amines can
be produced: **(a) reaction of a halogenoalkane with NH₃ in ethanol heated under pressure**. The syllabus
adds that **classification of amines will not be tested at AS Level**.

The same reaction appears in 15.1 as one of the nucleophilic substitution reactions of halogenoalkanes, so
AS questions often test it alongside hydrolysis by NaOH(aq) and substitution by KCN.

## What a primary amine is, and how to name it

A primary amine has an –NH₂ group bonded to a carbon chain: R–NH₂. The 9701 functional group table gives
**propylamine**, CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂, as its example, so name straight-chain amines as alkyl + "amine":

| Formula | Name |
|---|---|
| CH₃NH₂ | methylamine |
| CH₃CH₂NH₂ | ethylamine |
| CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂ | propylamine |
| CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂NH₂ | butylamine |

The molecular formula of a straight-chain primary amine is CₙH₂ₙ₊₃N: propylamine is C₃H₉N.

## The reaction and why each condition matters

    R–X + 2NH₃ → R–NH₂ + NH₄X

For example, from 1-chlorobutane:

    CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂Cl + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂NH₂ + NH₄Cl

| Condition | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Ammonia** | its nitrogen has a lone pair, so NH₃ is a nucleophile that attacks the δ+ carbon of the C–X bond |
| **In ethanol** | ethanol dissolves both the halogenoalkane and ammonia; avoiding water also avoids hydrolysis of the halogenoalkane to an alcohol |
| **Heated** | substitution is slow at room temperature; heat increases the rate |
| **Under pressure** (in a sealed vessel) | ammonia is a gas, so heating in an open vessel would drive it out of the mixture; a sealed, pressurised vessel keeps it in solution |
| **Two moles of NH₃** per mole of R–X | the second NH₃ removes H⁺ from the intermediate ion; in practice ammonia is used in excess |

## The mechanism

The reaction is **nucleophilic substitution**, and the C–X bond breaks heterolytically.

**Step 1.** A curly arrow goes from the **lone pair on the nitrogen of NH₃** to the **δ+ carbon** bonded to
the halogen. At the same time a curly arrow goes from the **C–X bond** to the **halogen**, which leaves as a
halide ion. For a primary halogenoalkane this happens in one step through a transition state (S<sub>N</sub>2),
as described in the [Halogenoalkanes study guide](/resources/as-halogenoalkanes/). The product is an
**alkylammonium ion**:

    CH₃CH₂CH₂Br + NH₃ → CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₃⁺ + Br⁻

**Step 2.** A second ammonia molecule uses its lone pair to remove an H⁺ from the –NH₃⁺ group, releasing
the free amine and forming an ammonium ion:

    CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₃⁺ + NH₃ → CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂ + NH₄⁺

Adding the two steps (with Br⁻ and NH₄⁺ written together as NH₄Br) gives the overall equation.

## Reactivity of different halogenoalkanes

The rate depends on how easily the C–X bond breaks. From the data booklet, average bond energies are
C–Cl 340, C–Br 280 and C–I 240 kJ mol⁻¹. The C–I bond is the weakest, so **iodoalkanes react fastest**
and chloroalkanes slowest, the same order seen in the silver nitrate test for halogenoalkanes.

## Worked example 1: equations and names

**Question.** Write equations for the formation of (a) ethylamine from iodoethane and (b) the amine
formed from 2-bromopropane, and name the organic product of (a).

**Answer.**

(a) CH₃CH₂I + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH₂NH₂ + NH₄I. The product is **ethylamine**.

(b) CH₃CHBrCH₃ + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH(NH₂)CH₃ + NH₄Br. The –NH₂ replaces the Br on the middle carbon, so the
amine has NH₂ on C2. Its formula, C₃H₉N, is the same as propylamine's: the two are positional isomers.

## Worked example 2: a yield calculation

**Question.** 12.29 g of 1-bromopropane is heated with excess ammonia in ethanol in a sealed tube. The
yield of propylamine is 80.0%. Calculate the mass of propylamine obtained and the minimum volume of ammonia
gas, at room conditions, needed for complete reaction. (Mr: C₃H₇Br = 122.9, C₃H₉N = 59.0; molar volume
24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹)

**Answer.**

n(C₃H₇Br) = 12.29 / 122.9 = 0.100 mol

Theoretical n(propylamine) = 0.100 mol, so the actual amount = 0.100 × 0.800 = 0.0800 mol

Mass = 0.0800 × 59.0 = **4.72 g**

The equation needs 2 mol NH₃ per mole of halogenoalkane, so n(NH₃) = 0.200 mol and
V = 0.200 × 24.0 = **4.80 dm³**

## Checking that the amine has formed

Two AS analytical techniques confirm the product. In the infrared spectrum, an N–H absorption appears in
the range 3300–3500 cm⁻¹ (from the data section) that the halogenoalkane did not have. In the mass
spectrum, the molecular ion peak moves to the Mr of the amine:
m/e = 59 for propylamine, C₃H₉N, compared with the pair of peaks at m/e = 122 and 124 for 1-bromopropane,
whose bromine isotopes give the [M+2]⁺ pattern. Loss of that 1 : 1 pair is itself evidence that bromine
has been replaced.

## Amines in synthesis routes

Because the only AS route to an amine starts from a halogenoalkane, a route to an amine from another
compound first makes the halogenoalkane:

- **From an alkene:** propene + HBr(g) at room temperature gives 2-bromopropane (major product), then NH₃
  in ethanol, heated under pressure, gives CH₃CH(NH₂)CH₃.
- **From an alcohol:** propan-1-ol + SOCl₂ (or PCl₅) gives 1-chloropropane, then NH₃ in ethanol under
  pressure gives propylamine.

Compare the three nucleophilic substitutions of halogenoalkanes in 15.1:

| Nucleophile | Conditions | Product |
|---|---|---|
| OH⁻ | NaOH(aq), heat | alcohol |
| CN⁻ | KCN in ethanol, heat | nitrile (one extra carbon) |
| NH₃ | in ethanol, heated under pressure | primary amine |

At A Level, 34.1 adds further routes to amines, including reduction of nitriles.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing only one NH₃** in the overall equation. Two are needed: one to substitute, one to remove H⁺.
- **Giving HX as a product.** The HX is taken up by the second ammonia as NH₄X.
- **Forgetting "under pressure"** or "sealed tube" in the conditions.
- **Using aqueous ammonia and writing an alcohol as the product**, or confusing this reaction with
  hydrolysis.
- **Starting the curly arrow from the H or the N atom** rather than from the lone pair on N.
- **Getting the hydrogen count wrong** in the amine's formula: a straight-chain primary amine is
  CₙH₂ₙ₊₃N, so butylamine is C₄H₁₁N.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **State** or **Give** reagents and conditions: "NH₃ in ethanol, heated under pressure (sealed tube)".
- **Describe** the mechanism: lone pair on N to δ+ C, C–X bond to X, then loss of H⁺ to a second NH₃.
- **Calculate:** use the 1 : 1 ratio of halogenoalkane to amine, and 1 : 2 for ammonia.
- **Suggest** a route: work backwards from the amine to the halogenoalkane, then to the starting material.
