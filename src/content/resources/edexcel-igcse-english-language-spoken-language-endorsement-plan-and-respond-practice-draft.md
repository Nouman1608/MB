# Plan and respond: practice

The endorsement has no marks and is reported separately (Not Classified, Pass, Merit or Distinction); it does not change your written grade. Check your centre's exact arrangements with your teacher. These are original tasks, not Pearson materials; marks in brackets are a revision aid, not Pearson marks.

## Questions

**1.** State the maximum length of the presentation and the two forms it can take. **[3]**

**2.** A student plans a talk for a teacher-only audience. State what the specification says about the design. **[2]**

**3.** Plan a talk on the topic "Should phones be allowed in classrooms?" Give a purpose sentence, an opening, three points and a conclusion. **[5]**

**4.** Prepare answers for these audience questions, each with a direct answer and a reason: (a) "Would a ban be fair to students who use phones to study?" (b) "Is there any evidence for your view?" **[4]**

**5.** A student is asked a question she cannot answer. Write a suitable reply. **[2]**

**6.** Read this original extract of a student's reply. *"Yeah, um, it's like, a good thing, basically."* Rewrite it in spoken Standard English. **[3]**

## Model answers

**1.** No longer than 10 minutes [1]; (a) a speech or talk with questions [1]; (b) a formal debate or dialogue with prepared prompts, followed by questions [1].

**2.** Allowed, because the audience must always include the teacher [1], but design it for a potentially wider audience, such as replicating a television interview [1].

**3.** Purpose: "By the end, my audience will understand both sides and my view." [1]. Opening: a question [1]. Three developed points, including a counter-argument [2]. Conclusion returning to the opening [1].

**4.** (a) Direct answer first, then a reason, such as a school device scheme [2]. (b) Honest, specific evidence the student is sure of [2].

**5.** "I am not sure of the exact figure, but I do know that..." and offer related knowledge [2].

**6.** "I think it is beneficial, because it helps students find information quickly." [3: no fillers, precise vocabulary, a reason]
