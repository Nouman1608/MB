## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an internal reason for a change in business objectives? **[1]**

A: A competitor cuts its prices

B: A new law on product safety

C: The founder decides to retire

D: A fall in consumer incomes

**2.** Define the term product range. **[1]**

**3.** Which **two** of the following are ways business aims and objectives can change, as listed in the specification? **[2]**

A: Entering or exiting markets

B: Changing the exchange rate

C: Growing or reducing the workforce

D: Setting the rate of income tax

E: Choosing the interest rate charged by banks

**4.** State **one** way strong performance could change a business's objectives. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **one** way new technology could change a business's objectives. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** reason why a business might decrease its product range. **[3]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Thornfield Shoes had sales revenue of £1,250,000 in a market worth £10 million. A year later its sales revenue was £1,200,000 and the market was worth £12 million.

**(a)** Calculate Thornfield Shoes' market share in each year. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in Thornfield Shoes' sales revenue. **[2]**

**8.** Using your answers to Question 7, outline **one** way Thornfield Shoes might change its objectives. **[2]**

**9.** Outline **one** way a new law could cause a business to exit a market. **[2]**

**10.** Ivy Tutoring began as a sole trader offering face-to-face maths lessons. After four years it is profitable, demand for online lessons is rising, and the owner has taken on a business partner with experience of running websites. Analyse how Ivy Tutoring's objectives might change. **[6]**

**11.** Harrow Engines has 80 employees. It buys new automated machines and plans to reduce its workforce by 15%.

**(a)** Calculate the number of employees after the reduction. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain **one** possible effect on the business of reducing its workforce. **[3]**

**12.** Glenmoor Outdoor sells camping equipment in five shops. Profit has fallen for two years as online retailers take customers. The owner is considering two changes of objective.

Option 1: focus on survival by closing its two least profitable shops, reducing staff from 40 to 28, and cutting the product range.

Option 2: focus on growth by opening an online shop and adding a range of clothing, financed by a loan.

Justify which option Glenmoor Outdoor should choose. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** All the products a business offers for sale [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A [1]. C [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any one from: set growth objectives such as opening new outlets; increase the product range; enter new markets; recruit more staff [1]. **[1]**

**5.** New technology such as online selling allows a business to reach customers outside its local area [1]. This makes growth more achievable [1], so the business may set an objective of entering a new market, for example a target for online sales in its first year [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Some products may sell in small numbers or make little profit [1]. Keeping them in stock ties up money and staff time [1], so dropping them lets the business cut costs and concentrate on its best-selling products, improving profit [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Year 1: £1.25m ÷ £10m × 100 = **12.5%** [1]. Year 2: £1.2m ÷ £12m × 100 = **10%** [1]

**(b)** Change = −£50,000; −£50,000 ÷ £1,250,000 × 100 = **−4%** (a 4% fall) [1] [1] **[4]**

**8.** The market grew by 20% but Thornfield's sales fell, so its market share fell from 12.5% to 10% [1]; it may set an objective of winning back market share, for example by changing its product range to match what customers in the growing market want [1]. **[2]**

**9.** A law could ban or restrict a product, or make it much more costly to produce safely [1], so if the business can no longer sell it profitably it may stop selling in that market [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Ivy Tutoring is profitable after four years, so it no longer needs to focus on survival and could aim for growth [1]. Demand for online lessons is rising, which is a change in market conditions [1], so it could set an objective of entering the online market and teaching students outside its local area [1]. The new partner's website experience is an internal reason that makes this realistic [1]. Growth online may need more tutors, so it might aim to grow its workforce [1], and it could increase its product range by offering other subjects, although the owner must make sure quality of teaching stays high as the business expands [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** 15% × 80 = 12 [1]. 80 − 12 = **68 employees** [1]

**(b)** Wage costs fall, which could raise profit [1]. However, remaining staff may fear for their own jobs [1], so motivation could fall and productivity could suffer while the new machines are introduced [1]. **[5]**

**12.** Option 1 reduces staff by 12, which is 30% of the workforce (12 ÷ 40 × 100) [1], cutting wage costs and rent from two shops [1].

For Option 1: profit has fallen for two years, so cutting costs protects the business's survival [1], and it avoids borrowing when performance is weak [1]. However, it does not deal with the cause of the fall, which is customers moving online, so sales may keep falling in the remaining shops [1].

For Option 2: an online shop responds directly to the change in market conditions [1] and could win back customers who now buy online [1]. However, a loan adds interest costs at a time when profit is falling, and online retailers are already strong competitors [1].

Judgement: Glenmoor should choose Option 2, but in a smaller form, opening the online shop first and delaying the clothing range, because it tackles the real reason for falling profit while limiting borrowing [1]. This depends on whether Glenmoor can compete online on price and delivery; if not, closing the weakest shops may become necessary as well. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* The source tells you why profit is falling. The strongest answers use that reason to judge which change of objective addresses it.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating market share using the business's sales alone, without the market total.
- Giving a reason for change in Question 10 without saying how the objectives change.
- Forgetting the effect on staff when the workforce is reduced.
- Choosing an option in Question 12 without linking it to the cause of falling profit.
