## Section A

**1.** State **two** characteristics of the First In First Out (FIFO) method of inventory valuation. **[2]**

**2.** Define net realisable value and state how it is used in valuing inventory. **[3]**

**3.** Fifty units of a product cost 34 each. They can be sold for 40 each, but each needs 6 of further work and 4 of selling costs. Calculate the value at which each unit should be included in inventory and the total write-down. **[3]**

**4.** Explain why the FIFO method gives the same closing inventory value whether the perpetual or the periodic system is used. **[2]**

**5.** State **one** reason why Last In First Out (LIFO) is not allowed in published financial statements prepared under International Accounting Standards, and **one** advantage claimed for it. **[2]**

## Section B

Tessa Ng buys and sells one product. Her record for June 2026 is:

| Date | Movement | Units | Cost per unit |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1 June | Opening inventory | 50 | 20 |
| 3 June | Purchase | 100 | 22 |
| 8 June | Issue (sale) | 80 | |
| 15 June | Purchase | 60 | 25 |
| 20 June | Issue (sale) | 70 | |
| 28 June | Purchase | 40 | 26 |

All units sold in June were sold at 40 each.

**6.** Using FIFO on a perpetual basis, calculate the cost of each issue, the cost of sales and the closing inventory. **[6]**

**7.** Using LIFO on a perpetual basis, calculate the cost of each issue, the cost of sales and the closing inventory. **[6]**

**8.** Using LIFO on a periodic basis, calculate the closing inventory and the cost of sales. **[3]**

**9.** Calculate the gross profit for June under each of the three methods in Questions 6 to 8. **[3]**

**10.** Explain why the results differ, and discuss which method Tessa should choose if prices continue to rise. Include the effect on the statement of financial position. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: items are assumed to be issued in the order they were received (oldest first) [1]; cost of sales is charged at the oldest costs [1]; closing inventory is valued at the most recent costs [1]; it often matches the physical flow of goods [1].

**2.** NRV is the estimated selling price less the costs needed to complete the item and to sell it [2]; inventory is valued at the lower of cost and NRV [1].

**3.** NRV = 40 − 6 − 4 = **30** each [1]; this is lower than cost of 34, so inventory is valued at 30 [1]; write-down (34 − 30) × 50 = **200** [1].

**4.** In both systems the oldest items are the first to be used, so the closing inventory is always the most recent purchases [1], and the cost found is the same however often the record is updated [1].

**5.** Any one reason: IAS 2 does not permit it [1]. Any one advantage: cost of sales is matched to current prices [1], or profit is not overstated by holding gains in a period of rising prices.

**6.** FIFO perpetual

- 8 June issue of 80: 50 at 20 + 30 at 22 = 1,000 + 660 = **1,660** [2]
- 20 June issue of 70: remaining 70 at 22 = **1,540** [1]
- Cost of sales 1,660 + 1,540 = **3,200** [1]
- Closing inventory: 60 at 25 + 40 at 26 = 1,500 + 1,040 = **2,540** [1]
- Check: 3,200 + 2,540 = 5,740, the cost of goods available (1,000 + 2,200 + 1,500 + 1,040) [1]

**7.** LIFO perpetual

- 8 June issue of 80: from the 3 June purchase, 80 at 22 = **1,760**. Left: 50 at 20 and 20 at 22 [2]
- 20 June issue of 70: 60 at 25 + 10 at 22 = 1,500 + 220 = **1,720**. Left: 50 at 20 and 10 at 22 [2]
- Cost of sales 1,760 + 1,720 = **3,480** [1]
- Closing inventory: 50 at 20 + 10 at 22 + 40 at 26 = 1,000 + 220 + 1,040 = **2,260** [1]
- Check: 3,480 + 2,260 = 5,740.

**8.** LIFO periodic: the closing 100 units are taken from the oldest layers: 50 at 20 + 50 at 22 = 1,000 + 1,100 = **2,100** [2]; cost of sales 5,740 − 2,100 = **3,640** [1].

**9.** Sales 150 × 40 = 6,000 [1]. FIFO gross profit 6,000 − 3,200 = **2,800**; LIFO perpetual 6,000 − 3,480 = **2,520** [1]; LIFO periodic 6,000 − 3,640 = **2,360** [1].

**10.** The purchase prices rose during the month (20, 22, 25, 26) [1]. FIFO charges the older, lower costs to cost of sales, so profit is highest and closing inventory is valued at recent, higher costs [1]. LIFO periodic charges the newest, higher costs to cost of sales, so profit is lowest and closing inventory is lowest [1]. The total cost is the same, so the methods differ only in the timing of the charge [1].
Recommendation: FIFO gives a statement of financial position inventory value (2,540) closer to current replacement cost, a higher working capital and a higher profit, and it is accepted under International Accounting Standards [1]; LIFO would show lower profit and might reduce tax, but it undervalues inventory and is not allowed in published statements [1]. Tessa should choose FIFO and use it consistently.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking the wrong end of the stack for FIFO or LIFO (Questions 6 and 7).
- Not carrying the remaining layers forward in the perpetual record.
- Using the perpetual LIFO figure for the periodic method (Question 8).
- Skipping the check that cost of sales plus closing inventory equals the cost of goods available.
- Stating that FIFO always gives the higher profit, without saying that this depends on rising prices.

## Approaching these questions

Draw a table of layers (units and unit cost) and update it after each transaction. For each issue, say which layer you use first. Finish by adding cost of sales and closing inventory, and compare the total with the cost of goods available. For comparison questions, say what happened to prices first, then the effect on cost of sales, profit and inventory.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does the valuation method change the total cost of the goods?**
No. It changes only how much is charged to cost of sales and how much remains in closing inventory.
