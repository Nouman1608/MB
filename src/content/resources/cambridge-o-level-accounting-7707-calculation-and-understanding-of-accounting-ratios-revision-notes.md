These notes condense section **6.1 Calculation and understanding of accounting ratios** of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. The syllabus lists ten ratios and states that "Candidates must use the formulas given in the Accounting ratios included at the end of this section. These are the only formulas accepted in candidate responses." The worked set of all ten ratios is in the [ratio calculation study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-calculation-and-understanding-of-accounting-ratios/); test yourself with the [ratio calculation practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-calculation-and-understanding-of-accounting-ratios-practice/). For what the results mean in context, move on to the [interpretation of accounting ratios revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-interpretation-of-accounting-ratios-revision-notes/). Figures are invented and in dollars.

## The ten ratios by group

**Profitability**

| Ratio | Formula | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin | Gross profit / Revenue x 100 | % |
| Mark-up | Gross profit / Cost of sales x 100 | % |
| Profit margin | Profit for the year / Revenue x 100 | % |
| Return on capital employed (ROCE) | Profit for the year before interest / Capital employed x 100 | % |

Capital employed = issued shares + reserves + non-current liabilities.

**Liquidity**

| Ratio | Formula | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Current (working capital) ratio | Current assets / Current liabilities | ratio, x : 1 |
| Acid test (liquid) ratio | (Current assets - inventory) / Current liabilities | ratio, x : 1 |

**Efficiency**

| Ratio | Formula | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Rate of inventory turnover | Cost of sales / Average inventory | times |
| Inventory turnover | Average inventory / Cost of sales x 365 | days |
| Trade receivables turnover | Trade receivables / Credit sales x 365 | days |
| Trade payables turnover | Trade payables / Credit purchases x 365 | days |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Profit for the year before interest | Profit for the year with loan or debenture interest added back |
| Capital employed | Long-term finance: issued shares + reserves + non-current liabilities |
| Average inventory | (Opening inventory + closing inventory) / 2 |
| Credit sales | Sales where the customer pays later; excludes cash sales |
| Credit purchases | Purchases paid for later; excludes cash purchases |
| Liquid assets | Current assets other than inventory |
| Working capital | Current assets - current liabilities, a dollar amount (not the ratio) |

## Where each input comes from

| Input | Where to find it |
|---|---|
| Revenue, cost of sales, gross profit | Statement of profit or loss, trading section |
| Profit for the year and interest | Statement of profit or loss, final lines |
| Opening inventory | Cost of sales section (it is not in this year's statement of financial position) |
| Closing inventory, trade receivables, trade payables | Statement of financial position, current items |
| Capital employed | Statement of financial position: equity plus non-current liabilities |
| Credit sales and credit purchases | The question text, or a sales or purchases ledger total |

## Quick links between ratios

- Margin to mark-up: mark-up = margin / (100 - margin) x 100. A 20% margin is a 25% mark-up; a 50% margin is a 100% mark-up.
- Mark-up to margin: margin = mark-up / (100 + mark-up) x 100.
- Inventory days = 365 / rate of inventory turnover.
- Gross profit margin - profit margin = expenses (including interest) as a % of revenue, less any other income.

## One-sentence meanings

- Gross profit margin 30%: $30 gross profit from each $100 of sales.
- Current ratio 2.25 : 1: $2.25 of current assets for each $1 of current liabilities.
- Inventory turnover 8 times: average inventory is sold and replaced 8 times a year.
- Trade receivables 44 days: credit customers take 44 days to pay, on average.

## Exam traps

- Only the syllabus formulas are accepted. Do not use "net profit" or a different capital employed.
- Add interest back for ROCE; do not add it back for the profit margin.
- Use average inventory when opening and closing figures are both given.
- Use credit sales and credit purchases for the days ratios.
- Present liquidity ratios as x : 1, not as percentages.
- Deduct only inventory for the acid test.
- Keep full figures until the last step, then round as the question says.
- Mark-up is always larger than margin for the same gross profit.

## Self-test

1. Gross profit is $45,000 and revenue is $150,000. Calculate the gross profit margin.
2. Gross profit is $45,000 and cost of sales is $105,000. Calculate the mark-up.
3. Profit for the year is $18,000, loan interest is $2,000 and capital employed is $160,000. Calculate ROCE.
4. Current assets are $54,000 and current liabilities are $24,000. Calculate the current ratio.
5. The current assets in question 4 include inventory of $18,000. Calculate the acid test ratio.
6. Cost of sales is $120,000, opening inventory $14,000 and closing inventory $16,000. Calculate the rate of inventory turnover.
7. Using question 6, calculate inventory turnover in days.
8. Trade receivables are $9,600 and credit sales are $80,000. Calculate trade receivables turnover in days.
9. Trade payables are $7,200 and credit purchases are $96,000. Calculate trade payables turnover in days.
10. Convert a gross profit margin of 25% into a mark-up.
11. A company has issued shares $100,000, general reserve $20,000, retained earnings $35,000 and debentures $40,000. Calculate capital employed.
12. Which current asset is deducted in the acid test ratio?

### Answers

1. 45,000 / 150,000 x 100 = **30.00%**.
2. 45,000 / 105,000 x 100 = **42.86%**.
3. (18,000 + 2,000) / 160,000 x 100 = **12.50%**.
4. 54,000 / 24,000 = **2.25 : 1**.
5. (54,000 - 18,000) / 24,000 = **1.50 : 1**.
6. Average inventory = (14,000 + 16,000) / 2 = 15,000; 120,000 / 15,000 = **8 times**.
7. 15,000 / 120,000 x 365 = **45.63 days** (or 365 / 8).
8. 9,600 / 80,000 x 365 = **43.80 days**.
9. 7,200 / 96,000 x 365 = **27.38 days**.
10. 25 / 75 x 100 = **33.33%**.
11. 100,000 + 20,000 + 35,000 + 40,000 = **$195,000**.
12. Inventory only.
