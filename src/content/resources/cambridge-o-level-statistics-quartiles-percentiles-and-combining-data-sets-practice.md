Use n for a total frequency and take the target positions as n/4, n/2, 3n/4 and (P/100) × n. Give estimates to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.

## Questions

**1.** A sample has 200 values in a cumulative frequency table. State the cumulative frequency you would read to estimate the lower quartile, the median, the upper quartile and the 70th percentile. **[2]**

**2.** A cycle courier records the length, d km, of 90 journeys.

| Distance, d km | 0 < d ≤ 5 | 5 < d ≤ 10 | 10 < d ≤ 20 | 20 < d ≤ 40 | 40 < d ≤ 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 14 | 29 | 27 | 14 | 6 |

**(a)** Complete a cumulative frequency table for these data. **[1]**
**(b)** Estimate the median distance by linear interpolation. **[3]**
**(c)** Estimate the interquartile range. **[3]**

**3.** Use the table in question 2.

**(a)** Estimate the 90th percentile of the distances. **[3]**
**(b)** Estimate how many journeys were longer than 30 km. **[2]**

**4.** The times, to the nearest second, taken by 80 people to complete a puzzle are grouped as follows.

| Time (seconds) | 10–19 | 20–29 | 30–39 | 40–49 | 50–59 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 6 | 18 | 30 | 20 | 6 |

**(a)** Write down the boundaries of the first class and the cumulative frequency at each upper boundary. **[2]**
**(b)** Estimate the median time. **[2]**
**(c)** Estimate the interquartile range. **[3]**

**5.** A cumulative frequency polygon for 60 values of a variable x passes through these points.

| x | 0 | 20 | 40 | 60 | 80 | 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative frequency | 0 | 6 | 21 | 45 | 57 | 60 |

**(a)** Estimate the median. **[2]**
**(b)** Estimate the lower quartile and the upper quartile. **[3]**
**(c)** Estimate the 80th percentile. **[2]**

**6.** In a reading test, school A has median 54 and interquartile range 12. School B has median 58 and interquartile range 24. The 90th percentile of school B is 71.
**(a)** Explain what the 90th percentile of 71 means. **[1]**
**(b)** Compare the results of the two schools. **[3]**

**7.** A class of 36 pupils has mean mark 58. A second class of 24 pupils has mean mark 67. Find the mean mark of all 60 pupils. **[3]**

**8.** Group A has 12 values with mean 40 and standard deviation 4. Group B has 8 values with mean 45 and standard deviation 3.
**(a)** Find the mean and standard deviation of the 20 values combined. **[5]**
**(b)** A student says the combined standard deviation should be 3.5, the mean of 4 and 3 weighted by group size. Explain why this is wrong. **[1]**

**9.** Set A is 12, 15, 9, 18, 12, 12. Set B is 20, 14, 17, 13. Find the mean and standard deviation of the ten values together. **[6]**

**10.** Twenty-four values have mean 52. They are combined with a second set of n values with mean 60, and the mean of the combined set is 57. Find n. **[4]**

**11.** Ten readings have mean 36 and standard deviation 4.
**(a)** An eleventh reading, 36, is added. Find the new mean and standard deviation. **[5]**
**(b)** Explain why the standard deviation has fallen. **[2]**
**(c)** The eleven readings are combined with nine more readings of mean 41 and standard deviation 6. Find the mean and standard deviation of all twenty. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Lower quartile: 200/4 = 50. Median: 100. Upper quartile: 150. [1] 70th percentile: 0.7 × 200 = 140. [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the target next to each measure before reading the table, since a wrong target position is the commonest way to lose the whole question.

**2. (a)** Cumulative frequencies: 14, 43, 70, 84, 90 at 5, 10, 20, 40, 60. [1] **[1]**
**(b)** Target 90/2 = 45, in the class 10–20 (43 before it, 27 in it). [1] Median = 10 + (45 − 43)/27 × 10 [1] = **10.7 km**. [1] **[3]**
**(c)** Q1: target 22.5, in the class 5–10: 5 + (22.5 − 14)/29 × 5 = 6.47. [1] Q3: target 67.5, in the class 10–20: 10 + (67.5 − 43)/27 × 10 = 19.07. [1] Interquartile range = 19.07 − 6.47 = **12.6 km**. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the class chosen, the cumulative frequency before it and the class width, because a slip in one of them then still leaves visible, correct method.

**3. (a)** Target 0.9 × 90 = 81, in the class 20–40 (70 before it, 14 in it, width 20). [1] 20 + (81 − 70)/14 × 20 [1] = **35.7 km**. [1] **[3]**
**(b)** At 30 km the cumulative frequency is 70 + (10/20) × 14 = 77. [1] Journeys longer than 30 km: 90 − 77 = **13**. [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Longer than" needs a subtraction from the total, so say that step out loud in your working.

**4. (a)** The first class runs from 9.5 to 19.5. [1] Cumulative frequencies at 19.5, 29.5, 39.5, 49.5, 59.5 are 6, 24, 54, 74, 80. [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Target 40, in the class 29.5 to 39.5: 29.5 + (40 − 24)/30 × 10 [1] = **34.8 seconds**. [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Q1: target 20: 19.5 + (20 − 6)/18 × 10 = 27.3. [1] Q3: target 60: 39.5 + (60 − 54)/20 × 10 = 42.5. [1] Interquartile range = 42.5 − 27.3 = **15.2 seconds**. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Using the limits 10, 20, 30 instead of the boundaries gives answers that are all off by half a second, so state the boundaries first.

**5. (a)** Target 30, between (40, 21) and (60, 45). [1] 40 + (30 − 21)/24 × 20 = **47.5**. [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Lower quartile: target 15, between (20, 6) and (40, 21): 20 + (15 − 6)/15 × 20 [1] = **32**. [1] Upper quartile: target 45 is a plotted point, so **60**. [1] **[3]**
**(c)** Target 0.8 × 60 = 48, between (60, 45) and (80, 57): 60 + (48 − 45)/12 × 20 [1] = **65**. [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* If a target is exactly one of the plotted cumulative frequencies, say so and read the value directly instead of interpolating.

**6. (a)** 90% of pupils in school B scored 71 or less (and 10% scored more). [1] **[1]**
**(b)** School B has the higher median (58 against 54), so its typical pupil scored more. [1] School B has the larger interquartile range (24 against 12), so its middle half is more spread out and the results are less consistent. [1] In context, school A's pupils are more alike in reading ability, while school B's pupils score higher typically but vary more. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison earns credit when each statement uses a figure and names the property (average or spread) and the context, not just "B is bigger".

**7.** Total marks of the first class = 36 × 58 = 2088. [1] Total of the second = 24 × 67 = 1608. [1] Mean = (2088 + 1608)/60 = 3696/60 = **61.6**. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Averaging 58 and 67 to get 62.5 fails to weight by class size, and a bare wrong answer then has no method to credit.

**8. (a)** Σx: 12 × 40 = 480 and 8 × 45 = 360, total 840, so the mean is 840/20 = **42**. [1] Σx² for A = 12 × (16 + 1600) = 19 392. [1] Σx² for B = 8 × (9 + 2025) = 16 272. [1] Total Σx² = 35 664, so variance = 35 664/20 − 42² = 1783.2 − 1764 = 19.2. [1] Standard deviation = **4.38** (3 s.f.). [1] **[5]**
**(b)** Standard deviation depends on squared distances from the combined mean, and the two group means (40 and 45) differ, which adds spread that a simple average of the two standard deviations cannot include. [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the combined mean unrounded and subtract its square last, as an early rounding slip changes the variance noticeably.

**9.** Σx = 78 + 64 = 142, so the mean is **14.2**. [1] Σx² for A = 144 + 225 + 81 + 324 + 144 + 144 = 1062. [1] Σx² for B = 400 + 196 + 289 + 169 = 1054. [1] Total Σx² = 2116. [1] Variance = 2116/10 − 14.2² = 211.6 − 201.64 = 9.96. [1] Standard deviation = **3.16** (3 s.f.). [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Checking each set's total against a quick estimate catches an arithmetic slip early, which matters because every later step depends on the totals.

**10.** Total of the first set = 24 × 52 = 1248. [1] Total of the second = 60n. [1] Combined: 1248 + 60n = 57 × (24 + n) = 1368 + 57n. [1] 3n = 120, so n = **40**. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Form an equation from totals, not from means, and check by substituting n back into the combined mean.

**11. (a)** New mean = (10 × 36 + 36)/11 = **36**. [1] Σx² for the ten = 10 × (16 + 1296) = 13 120. [1] Adding 36² = 1296 gives 14 416. [1] Variance = 14 416/11 − 36² = 14.545. [1] Standard deviation = **3.81** (3 s.f.). [1] **[5]**
**(b)** The new reading equals the mean, so it adds no squared deviation to the total. [1] The number of readings rises, so the average squared deviation falls. [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Σx = 396 + 9 × 41 = 396 + 369 = 765, so the mean is 765/20 = **38.25**. [1] Σx² for the nine = 9 × (36 + 1681) = 15 453, so total Σx² = 14 416 + 15 453 = 29 869. [1] Variance = 29 869/20 − 38.25² = 1493.45 − 1463.0625 = 30.3875, so standard deviation = **5.51** (3 s.f.). [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the exact sum of squares from part (a) in part (c), not the rounded 3.81, to avoid a small inaccuracy that carries through.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading the wrong target position, such as n/2 for a quartile.
- Interpolating with the cumulative frequency of the class itself instead of the one before it.
- Using the class limits instead of the true boundaries for rounded data.
- Forgetting the subtraction from n when a question asks for "more than" or "the highest".
- Combining means without allowing for group sizes, or taking the mean of two standard deviations.
- Writing Σx² = n × σ² and leaving out the mean squared.
- Rounding the combined mean or variance before the final square root.
- Comparing two data sets with no figures, or comparing without naming both an average and a spread.
- Quoting a graph or interpolation estimate to far more figures than the data supports.
