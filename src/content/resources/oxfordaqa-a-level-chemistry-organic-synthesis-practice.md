The questions below are on section **3.3.14 Organic synthesis** in the OxfordAQA International AS and
A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3, for International AS and A-level exams from May/June
2020 onwards. Every question is **International A-level only**, because the whole section is A2 organic
chemistry, examined in Unit 4; Unit 5 covers all content. For atom economy, use the 3.1.2.5 formula.

Before starting, work through the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-organic-synthesis/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-organic-synthesis-revision-notes/). Course
links: the [OxfordAQA A-Level Chemistry hub](/boards/oxfordaqa/a-level/chemistry/), the
[printable 9620 checklist](/checklists/oxfordaqa/a-level/chemistry/) and the
[free 10-minute diagnostics](/diagnostics/).

**Data for this set.** Relative atomic masses: C = 12.0, H = 1.0, Br = 79.9, Cl = 35.5, K = 39.1,
O = 16.0, N = 14.0.

## Questions

**1.** A fruit-flavour ester used to be made in a reactor with methylbenzene as the solvent. The
manufacturer now uses the liquid alcohol reactant in excess and adds no other solvent. Explain two
advantages of the new process. **[2]**

**2.** A pharmaceutical company makes a hydroxynitrile intermediate by reacting an aldehyde with
potassium cyanide, followed by dilute acid.

**(a)** Explain why adding the dilute acid is a particular hazard. **[1]**
**(b)** Give two reasons why process designers try to avoid hazardous starting materials. **[2]**

**3.** But-1-ene can be made by heating 1-bromobutane with potassium hydroxide in ethanol:

C₄H₉Br + KOH → C₄H₈ + KBr + H₂O

Calculate the percentage atom economy for making but-1-ene. **[2]**

**4.** State the reagent or reagents, and any condition or catalyst, for each one-step conversion.

**(a)** Methylbenzene to (chloromethyl)benzene. **[1]**
**(b)** Benzene to ethylbenzene. **[2]**
**(c)** Nitrobenzene to phenylamine. **[1]**

**5.** Chlorocyclopentane is converted into compound C:

chlorocyclopentane → (NaOH(aq), warm) → A → (acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, reflux) → B → (KCN, then dilute acid) → C

**(a)** Identify A, B and C. **[3]**
**(b)** State whether C is optically active. Give a reason. **[1]**

**6.** A four-step synthesis has step yields of 90%, 75%, 68% and 85%. Every step has a 1 : 1 mole ratio.

**(a)** A chemist starts with 0.150 mol of starting material. The final product has Mr = 151.0.
Calculate the mass of final product. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(b)** A two-step route to the same product has step yields of 70% and 60%. State, with a reason, which
route gives more product from the same starting amount. **[1]**

**7.** Samples of chlorobenzene, C₆H₅Cl, and (chloromethyl)benzene, C₆H₅CH₂Cl, are each warmed with
aqueous sodium hydroxide. Only one of them forms an alcohol. Identify which, and explain the difference
in reactivity. **[3]**

**8.** Phenylmethanamine, C₆H₅CH₂NH₂, can be made from methylbenzene in two steps.

**(a)** Give the reagent and conditions for each step, and name the intermediate. **[4]**
**(b)** The second step is C₆H₅CH₂Cl + 2NH₃ → C₆H₅CH₂NH₂ + NH₄Cl. Calculate the percentage atom economy
of this step. **[2]**

**9.** Hexane-1,6-diamine, H₂N(CH₂)₆NH₂, is a monomer for nylon 6,6.

**(a)** Devise a two-step synthesis of hexane-1,6-diamine from 1,4-dibromobutane, Br(CH₂)₄Br. For each step
give the reagent and conditions, and give the structure of the organic product. **[4]**
**(b)** Name the other monomer used to make nylon 6,6 and state the type of polymerisation. **[2]**

**10.** 2-Methylpropan-1-amine, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂NH₂, is to be made from propan-2-ol, (CH₃)₂CHOH.

**(a)** Devise a four-step synthesis. Name each organic product, and give the reagent and conditions for
every step. **[8]**
**(b)** Explain why the route cannot be shortened by converting propan-2-ol directly into
2-bromopropane. **[1]**

**11.** A cationic surfactant is made in two steps.

Step 1: methylbenzene is converted into (chloromethyl)benzene.
Step 2: C₆H₅CH₂Cl + CH₃(CH₂)₁₅N(CH₃)₂ → [C₆H₅CH₂N(CH₃)₂(CH₂)₁₅CH₃]⁺Cl⁻

**(a)** Write an equation for step 1 and state the condition needed. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage atom economy of step 1. **[2]**
**(c)** Name the type of reaction in step 2, and state its percentage atom economy with a reason. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest two features of this process that make it less green, and explain each. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** No energy is needed to separate and recover a solvent from the product, for example by distillation [1].
No flammable, toxic solvent vapour can escape, and there is no solvent waste to dispose of [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Each point should give a consequence; "it is greener" or "it is cheaper" with no reason
attached repeats the question.

**2. (a)** Acid reacts with cyanide ions to release **hydrogen cyanide**, a very toxic gas [1].
**(b)** Hazardous substances put workers and the surroundings at risk if there is a leak or accident [1].
They also need costly controls such as sealed storage, protective equipment and emergency plans [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), naming the gas HCN is what earns credit; "it is dangerous" does not say why.

**3.** Sum of Mr of reactants = 136.9 + 56.1 = 193.0 [1]; % atom economy = 56.0 ÷ 193.0 × 100 = **29.0%** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Only reactants go in the denominator; adding the masses of KBr and water instead is a
common slip that gives a different answer.

**4. (a)** **Chlorine in UV light** [1].
**(b)** **Chloroethane** [1] with an **aluminium chloride (AlCl₃) catalyst** [1].
**(c)** **Tin and concentrated hydrochloric acid** (then NaOH to free the amine) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a) the condition carries the meaning: AlCl₃ instead of UV light would put the
chlorine on the ring, which is a different product.

**5. (a)** A is **cyclopentanol** [1]. B is **cyclopentanone** [1]. C is **1-hydroxycyclopentane-1-carbonitrile**,
the ring carbon carrying both OH and CN [1].
**(b)** **Not optically active**: going round the ring either way from that carbon gives the same sequence of
atoms, so it carries two identical groups and is not a chiral centre [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A clear structure for C is usually as good as a name; check that C has six carbons,
because the CN carbon is new.

**6. (a)** Overall fraction = 0.90 × 0.75 × 0.68 × 0.85 = 0.39015 [1]; amount = 0.150 × 0.39015 =
0.05852 mol [1]; mass = 0.05852 × 151.0 = **8.84 g** [1].
**(b)** The **two-step route**: 0.70 × 0.60 = 0.42, so 42.0% overall against 39.0% [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep unrounded values until the last line, then round to 3 significant figures as asked.

**7.** **(Chloromethyl)benzene** forms the alcohol, phenylmethanol [1]. Its chlorine is on a side-chain sp³
carbon, so the polar C–Cl bond is attacked by OH⁻ as in a halogenoalkane [1]. In chlorobenzene a chlorine
lone pair overlaps with the delocalised ring electrons, strengthening the C–Cl bond, and the electron-rich
ring repels the nucleophile [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The explanation needs both compounds; describing only why the side-chain chlorine
reacts leaves the comparison half done.

**8. (a)** Step 1: **chlorine** [1] in **UV light** gives **(chloromethyl)benzene** [1].
Step 2: **excess ammonia** [1], in ethanol, heated in a sealed container, gives **phenylmethanamine** [1].
**(b)** Sum = 126.5 + 2(17.0) = 160.5 [1]; % atom economy = 107.0 ÷ 160.5 × 100 = **66.7%** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Excess" ammonia belongs in the answer to step 2; without it, the amine product reacts
further to give secondary and tertiary amines.

**9. (a)** Step 1: **potassium cyanide**, refluxed in a mixture of ethanol and water [1], gives **NC(CH₂)₄CN**, hexanedinitrile [1].
Step 2: reduction, for example **H₂ with a nickel catalyst** or LiAlH₄ [1], gives **H₂N(CH₂)₆NH₂** [1].
**(b)** **Hexanedioic acid** [1]; **condensation** polymerisation [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Both bromine atoms are replaced in step 1, so the chain grows from four carbons to six;
a product with only one CN group cannot give the diamine.

**10. (a)** Step 1: **concentrated sulfuric (or phosphoric) acid, heat** [1] gives **propene** [1].
Step 2: **hydrogen bromide** [1] gives **2-bromopropane** as the major product [1].
Step 3: **KCN in aqueous ethanol, heat under reflux** [1] gives **2-methylpropanenitrile** [1].
Step 4: **reduction**, for example H₂ with a nickel catalyst [1], gives **2-methylpropan-1-amine** [1].
**(b)** The specification contains no reaction that converts an alcohol directly into a halogenoalkane,
so the OH group has to be removed by elimination and HBr added to the alkene [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the major product in step 2; 1-bromopropane would lead to butan-1-amine, a
different compound, so the position matters.

**11. (a)** C₆H₅CH₃ + Cl₂ → C₆H₅CH₂Cl + HCl [1]; **UV light** [1].
**(b)** Sum = 92.0 + 71.0 = 163.0 [1]; % atom economy = 126.5 ÷ 163.0 × 100 = **77.6%** [1].
**(c)** **Nucleophilic substitution** (the amine lone pair attacks the CH₂ carbon) [1]; **100%**, because the
quaternary ammonium salt is the only product [1].
**(d)** Chlorine and (chloromethyl)benzene are toxic and irritant, so storage, handling and leaks are
risks [1]. Step 1 makes hydrogen chloride as a by-product, which is corrosive waste and lowers the atom
economy [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In (d), two different features are needed; a hazard and a waste point score more
safely than two versions of the same hazard.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing AlCl₃ instead of UV light for side-chain chlorination of methylbenzene.
- Proposing a nucleophile to replace chlorine bonded directly to a benzene ring.
- Using steps that are not in 9620, such as steam hydration or a direct alcohol-to-halogenoalkane step.
- Leaving out "excess" ammonia when a primary amine is the target.
- Adding or averaging step yields instead of multiplying them as decimals.
- Missing the coefficient in a denominator, such as 2NH₃.
- Naming the minor product of HBr addition, then building the rest of the route on it.
- Giving two answers that are the same idea ("safer" and "less hazardous") where two points are needed.
