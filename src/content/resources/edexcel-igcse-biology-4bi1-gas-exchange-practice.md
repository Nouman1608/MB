The qualification is untiered and a calculator may be used on both papers. Questions on the flowering-plant points (2.40B–2.45B) are labelled **(Paper 2 only)**, because those points carry a B reference; the rest can appear on either paper.

## Questions

**1.** State, in order, the two types of tube that air passes through between the trachea and the alveoli. **[2]**

**2.** Describe the role of the pleural membranes. **[2]**

**3.** Describe how the intercostal muscles and the diaphragm cause air to leave the lungs when a person breathes out at rest. **[4]**

**4.** Explain how two features of the alveoli and one feature of their blood supply increase the rate of gas exchange. **[4]**

**5.** Five students counted their breaths for one minute at rest and again straight after three minutes of running on the spot.

| Student | A | B | C | D | E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Breaths per minute at rest | 12 | 14 | 13 | 15 | 16 |
| Breaths per minute after exercise | 30 | 33 | 28 | 31 | 33 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean breathing rate at rest and after exercise. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in mean breathing rate. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** Another student breathes 16 times per minute with 0.50 dm³ per breath at rest, and 30 times per minute with 1.8 dm³ per breath during exercise. Volume breathed per minute = breaths per minute × volume per breath. Calculate how many times greater the volume breathed per minute is during exercise. **[2]**

**6.** A student breathes in and out through a two-tube apparatus. Inhaled air is drawn through limewater in tube X; exhaled air bubbles through limewater in tube Y.

**(a)** State the result you would expect. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain this result in terms of processes happening in the body. **[2]**
**(c)** State one other difference between inhaled and exhaled air. **[1]**

**7.** (Paper 2 only) Explain how the structure of a leaf is adapted for gas exchange. **[4]**

**8.** (Paper 2 only) A student set up four sealed tubes, each containing the same volume of red hydrogen-carbonate indicator. Tubes A, B and C each held a leaf of the same size on a gauze platform. Tube D had no leaf. All four were placed 20 cm from a lamp for three hours.

- Tube A: wrapped in foil
- Tube B: covered with several layers of tissue paper
- Tube C: uncovered
- Tube D: uncovered, no leaf

**(a)** Predict the colour of the indicator in each tube after three hours, assuming tube B is at the light intensity where photosynthesis and respiration are equal. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain the result in tube C. **[2]**
**(c)** Give two variables, other than leaf size and volume of indicator, that the student should keep the same. **[2]**

**9.** (Paper 2 only) A plant releases carbon dioxide by respiration at a constant 2.0 arbitrary units per hour. At midday, photosynthesis uses carbon dioxide at 9.0 arbitrary units per hour.

**(a)** Calculate the net carbon dioxide uptake at midday. **[1]**
**(b)** At dusk the rate of photosynthesis falls to 2.0 arbitrary units per hour. State and explain the net exchange of carbon dioxide at this time. **[2]**
**(c)** State the net carbon dioxide exchange at midnight. **[1]**

**10.** The table shows data on deaths from coronary heart disease. (These figures are invented for this question.)

| Group | Deaths per 100 000 people per year |
|---|---|
| Non-smokers | 120 |
| Smokers | 290 |

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater the death rate is for smokers. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how carbon monoxide and nicotine in tobacco smoke increase the risk of a heart attack. **[4]**
**(c)** Suggest why these data alone do not prove that smoking causes coronary heart disease. **[1]**

**11.** A pair of human lungs contains about 3.0 × 10⁸ alveoli. Assume each alveolus has a surface area of 2.5 × 10⁻⁷ m².

**(a)** Calculate the total surface area of the alveoli. **[2]**
**(b)** In a person with emphysema, the surface area is 40% smaller. Calculate the new surface area. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why a person with emphysema becomes breathless during exercise. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Bronchi [1], then bronchioles [1]. **Answer: bronchi → bronchioles** **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Both names must be in the right order for full credit; "bronchus tubes" without the bronchioles scores only the first mark.

**2.** The pleural membranes line the outside of the lungs and the inside of the thorax [1]. The fluid between them reduces friction as the lungs move during breathing [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Protects the lungs" is too vague to score; the creditworthy idea is reducing friction.

**3.** External intercostal muscles relax, so the ribs move down and in [1]. The diaphragm relaxes and moves upwards into a dome [1]. The volume of the thorax decreases [1], so the pressure inside rises above atmospheric pressure and air is forced out [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Volume and pressure are separate marking points; "the lungs get smaller so air goes out" usually scores only one.

**4.** Many alveoli give a large surface area [1]. Alveolar walls are one cell thick, giving a short diffusion distance [1]. A dense capillary network with constant blood flow [1] keeps the concentration gradient steep [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each feature needs its effect; listing three features with no explanation scores at most half the marks.

**5. (a)** Rest: (12 + 14 + 13 + 15 + 16) / 5 = **14 breaths per minute** [1]. After exercise: (30 + 33 + 28 + 31 + 33) / 5 = **31 breaths per minute** [1].
**(b)** (31 − 14) / 14 × 100 [1] = **121.4%** [1].
**(c)** Rest: 16 × 0.50 = 8.0 dm³; exercise: 30 × 1.8 = 54 dm³ [1]. 54 / 8.0 = **6.75 times** [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b) dividing by the after-exercise mean is a common slip; errors carried forward from (a) are usually allowed if the method is correct.

**6. (a)** The limewater in tube Y turns cloudy (milky) sooner than in tube X [1].
**(b)** Body cells respire and produce carbon dioxide [1]; it diffuses into the blood and out into the alveoli, so exhaled air has more carbon dioxide [1].
**(c)** Any one: exhaled air has less oxygen, more water vapour, or is warmer [1].
*Examiner insight:* Writing that exhaled air "contains carbon dioxide and inhaled air does not" is wrong; both contain it, in different amounts.

**7.** Thin leaf, so a short diffusion distance to the cells [1]. Air spaces in the spongy mesophyll let gases reach all cells and give a large internal surface area [1]. Stomata, mostly on the lower surface, let gases diffuse in and out [1]. Moist cell walls let gases dissolve before diffusing into cells [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Points about palisade chloroplasts relate to photosynthesis, not gas exchange, and are not credited here.

**8. (a)** A: yellow [1]. B: red (unchanged) [1]. C: purple [1]. D: red (unchanged) [1].
**(b)** In bright light photosynthesis is faster than respiration [1], so carbon dioxide is removed from the air in the tube, lowering its concentration [1].
**(c)** Any two: temperature; time left; distance from the lamp; type or age of leaf; starting colour of indicator [1] [1].
*Examiner insight:* For (b) you must compare the two rates; "the leaf photosynthesises" alone does not explain why carbon dioxide falls, since respiration continues.

**9. (a)** 9.0 − 2.0 = **7.0 arbitrary units per hour** [1].
**(b)** No net exchange, because photosynthesis uses carbon dioxide at the same rate that respiration releases it [1] [1].
**(c)** **2.0 arbitrary units per hour released** (respiration only) [1].
*Examiner insight:* "No gas exchange" at dusk loses the explanation mark; the word "net" and the equal-rates idea are both needed.

**10. (a)** 290 / 120 = 2.42, so **2.4 times** [1].
**(b)** Carbon monoxide combines with haemoglobin, so the blood carries less oxygen [1]. Nicotine raises heart rate and blood pressure [1]. Smoking increases fatty deposits that narrow the coronary arteries, which can be blocked by a clot [1]. Heart muscle then gets too little oxygen, so it cannot respire and may die [1].
**(c)** Other factors, such as diet, exercise or age, may differ between the groups [1].
*Examiner insight:* Answers must name the coronary arteries and oxygen supply to heart muscle; "blocks the heart" does not score.

**11. (a)** 3.0 × 10⁸ × 2.5 × 10⁻⁷ [1] = **75 m²** [1].
**(b)** 75 × 0.60 = **45 m²** [1].
**(c)** Alveolar walls break down, so the surface area for gas exchange is smaller [1]. Less oxygen diffuses into the blood per minute [1]. During exercise muscles need more oxygen for aerobic respiration [1], so the person breathes faster and deeper to try to make up the shortfall [1].
*Examiner insight:* A unit is needed in (a) and (b); in (c), link surface area to rate of diffusion rather than just saying the lungs are "damaged".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the airway order wrongly, or naming the bronchioles before the bronchi.
- Describing breathing out with the muscles contracting, or leaving out the volume and pressure steps.
- Listing alveolus features without the effect on diffusion.
- Dividing by the wrong mean in percentage-change calculations.
- Saying inhaled air contains no carbon dioxide.
- Getting indicator colours the wrong way round, or saying the balanced tube had no gas exchange at all.
- Forgetting that respiration happens in the light as well as in the dark.
- Explaining heart disease without mentioning the coronary arteries.
- Treating a correlation in smoking data as proof of cause.
- Leaving units off surface-area or volume answers.
