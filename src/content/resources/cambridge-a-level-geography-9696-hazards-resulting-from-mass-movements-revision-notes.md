## What the syllabus lists

- Mass movements and resultant hazards: nature and causes.
- Impacts on lives and property.
- Prediction, hazard mapping, preparedness and monitoring; perception of risk.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mass movement | Downslope movement of rock, regolith or soil under gravity |
| Shear stress | Driving force: downslope component of weight |
| Shear strength | Resisting force: friction and cohesion |
| Pore-water pressure | Pressure of water in soil pores; pushes particles apart and lowers friction |
| Factor of safety | Resisting forces ÷ driving forces; failure at 1 or below |
| Preparatory factor | Slowly reduces stability (weathering, undercutting, deforestation) |
| Trigger | Causes failure at a moment (intense rain, earthquake, excavation) |
| Rotational slide (slump) | Movement on a curved surface; back-tilted block, bulging toe |
| Translational slide | Movement along a planar surface such as a bedding plane |
| Debris flow | Rapid flow of rock, soil and water |
| Rainfall threshold | Rain intensity and duration above which landslides are likely |
| Inclinometer | Borehole instrument that detects movement at depth |
| Piezometer | Instrument that measures pore-water pressure |
| Susceptibility map | Map ranking land by likelihood of mass movement |

## Types by speed

Extremely slow: soil creep. Slow: earthflow, some slumps. Rapid: mudflow, debris flow, rapid slides. Very rapid: rockfall, topple, large debris flows. The faster the movement, the greater the threat to life.

## Causes recall

- **Increase shear stress:** steeper slope, undercutting, added weight (water, buildings, tips), shaking.
- **Reduce shear strength:** weathering, lost root cohesion, rising pore-water pressure, saturated clays.
- **Human:** deforestation, road cuttings, loading slopes, leaking pipes, poor drainage, quarrying, reservoir filling.

## Examples at a glance

| Event | Type and cause | Point to remember |
|---|---|---|
| Aberfan, Wales, 21 October 1966 | Flow of saturated colliery spoil tip built over springs | 144 killed, 116 of them children; human-made slope |
| Vajont, Italy, 9 October 1963 | Landslide into reservoir; filling raised pore-water pressure | Water overtopped the dam; about 2,000 killed |
| Vargas, Venezuela, December 1999 | Debris flows after exceptional rain | Towns on alluvial fans; death estimates into tens of thousands |
| Hong Kong (from 1977) | Dense city on steep slopes | Slope registration, design standards, upgrading, warnings |

## Management recall

- **Monitoring:** inclinometers, extensometers, GPS, satellite radar, piezometers.
- **Prediction:** rainfall thresholds; accelerating movement as warning.
- **Mapping:** slope, geology, soil, drainage, vegetation, past landslides.
- **Engineering:** drainage, regrading, retaining walls, soil nails, rock bolts, netting, catch fences, debris basins, afforestation.
- **Preparedness:** land-use zoning, warnings, evacuation plans, education.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Slide** moves as a coherent mass on a surface; **flow** deforms internally like a fluid.
- **Rotational** slides tilt backward on a curved surface; **translational** slides move parallel to a planar surface.
- **Mudflow** is fine-grained; **debris flow** carries coarse rock and boulders.
- **Monitoring** measures what a slope is doing now; **hazard mapping** ranks where failure is likely.

## Mini essay plan

*"Human activity is now the main cause of mass movement hazards." How far do you agree?*

1. **Define** mass movement hazards and separate preparatory factors from triggers.
2. **For:** Aberfan (spoil tip over springs) and Vajont (reservoir filling) were human-caused; deforestation, road cuttings and informal housing raise risk around growing cities.
3. **Against:** intense rain, earthquakes and undercutting trigger most failures (Vargas, 1999).
4. **Interaction:** natural triggers act on slopes weakened by people; people also decide exposure.
5. **Judgement:** nature usually triggers, people usually turn the event into a disaster.

## Exam traps

- "Landslide" used for every movement type.
- No shear stress and shear strength in causal explanations.
- Triggers given without preparatory factors.
- Secondary impacts missed (landslide-dammed lakes, blocked access).
- Management evaluated without cost or who can afford it.
- Perception of risk treated as simple ignorance.

## Self-test

1. Define factor of safety.
2. How does rising pore-water pressure cause failure?
3. Distinguish a rotational from a translational slide.
4. Why are debris flows more dangerous than earthflows?
5. Give two preparatory factors and one trigger.
6. Explain how deforestation reduces slope stability.
7. Why did filling the Vajont reservoir matter?
8. Why was Aberfan described as a human-made hazard?
9. What is a rainfall threshold?
10. What does an inclinometer measure?
11. Give three engineering measures that increase resisting forces.
12. Give two reasons why people underestimate mass movement risk.

### Answers

1. The ratio of resisting forces (shear strength) to driving forces (shear stress); 1 or below means failure.
2. It pushes particles apart and reduces friction, lowering shear strength until shear stress exceeds it.
3. Rotational slides move on a curved surface and tilt backward; translational slides move along a planar surface.
4. They move rapidly with high density and carry boulders, giving little time to escape.
5. Preparatory: weathering, undercutting, deforestation. Trigger: intense rain, earthquake, excavation.
6. Roots no longer bind the soil, interception falls so more water infiltrates, and pore-water pressure rises.
7. Rising water raised pore-water pressure in the weak valley side, helping trigger the landslide.
8. The tip was built by people from mining waste over springs, so both the slope and its saturation resulted from human activity.
9. A rainfall intensity and duration above which landslides have occurred in the past, used to issue warnings.
10. Movement of the ground at depth in a borehole.
11. Any three: retaining walls, soil nails, rock bolts, afforestation.
12. Any two: slow movements seem harmless, little personal experience, only affordable land is at risk, warning fatigue.
