**3.** State why the concentration of water is not included in a Kstab expression for an aqueous complex. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Copper(II) ions form a deep blue complex with ammonia:

[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺(aq) + 4NH₃(aq) ⇌ [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺(aq) + 4H₂O(l)

An equilibrium mixture contains 0.040 mol dm⁻³ [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺, 0.80 mol dm⁻³ NH₃ and 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol dm⁻³ [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺.

**(a)** Write the Kstab expression. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate Kstab, giving its units. **[2]**

**7.** 0.025 mol of nickel(II) sulfate is dissolved in 1.00 dm³ of 1.00 mol dm⁻³ aqueous ammonia. Kstab for [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺, formed from [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺, is 2.5 × 10⁸ mol⁻⁶ dm¹⁸.

**(a)** Assuming that almost all the nickel forms [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺, calculate the concentration of ammonia used up. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the equilibrium concentration of ammonia. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the equilibrium concentration of [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why the assumption in (a) is justified. **[1]**

**8.** Illustrative lg Kstab values for copper(II) complexes, each formed from [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺, are:

[Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺: 13.1  [Cu(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺: 19.6

**(a)** Predict what happens when en is added to a solution of [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺, and write an equation. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain your prediction in terms of Kstab. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the equilibrium constant for the exchange reaction in (a). **[1]**

**9.** Kstab for [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺, formed from Ag⁺(aq) and NH₃(aq), is 1.6 × 10⁷ mol⁻² dm⁶.

**(a)** Calculate the ratio [[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺] : [Ag⁺] in a solution where [NH₃] = 0.010 mol dm⁻³ at equilibrium. **[2]**

**(b)** Use your answer to state what fraction of the silver, approximately, is present as Ag⁺(aq). **[1]**

**10.** EDTA⁴⁻ forms complexes with very large values of Kstab with many metal ions. Suggest an explanation, referring to the number of particles in the equation for forming [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ from [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺. **[3]**

**11.** Kstab for [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ is 1.0 × 10¹⁸ mol⁻³ dm⁹ (illustrative). Calculate the equilibrium concentration of en needed for the ratio [[Ni(en)₃]²⁺] : [[Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺] to be 1.0 × 10⁶. **[3]**

**12.** A student states: "Kstab for [CuCl₄]²⁻ is much smaller than Kstab for [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺, so adding concentrated hydrochloric acid to aqueous copper(II) ions cannot produce [CuCl₄]²⁻." Explain why the student is wrong, and describe how the change could be reversed. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The equilibrium constant for the formation of a complex ion [1] in a solvent from its constituent ions or molecules [1].

**2.** Kstab = [[CoCl₄]²⁻] / ([[Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺][Cl⁻]⁴) [1]; units mol⁻⁴ dm¹² [1].

**3.** Water is the solvent, present in large excess, so its concentration is effectively constant [1].

**4. (a)** Kstab = 10¹⁸·³ = 2.0 × 10¹⁸ [1].

**(b)** mol⁻³ dm⁹ [1].

**5.** The complex is stable: the equilibrium lies far towards the complex, with very little metal left as the aqua ion [1].

**6. (a)** Kstab = [[Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺] / ([[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺][NH₃]⁴) [1].

**(b)** Kstab = 0.040 / (2.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ × 0.80⁴) = 0.040 / (2.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ × 0.4096) = 4.9 × 10¹² [1]; mol⁻⁴ dm¹² [1].

**7. (a)** 6 × 0.025 = 0.15 mol dm⁻³ [1].

**(b)** 1.00 − 0.15 = 0.85 mol dm⁻³ [1].

**(c)** [[Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺] = 0.025 / (2.5 × 10⁸ × 0.85⁶) [1] = 0.025 / (2.5 × 10⁸ × 0.377) = 2.7 × 10⁻¹⁰ mol dm⁻³ [1].

**(d)** Kstab is very large, so the equilibrium lies far to the right; the calculated [[Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺] is negligible compared with 0.025 mol dm⁻³ [1].

**8. (a)** The en ligands replace the ammonia ligands; the solution changes to the en complex [1]: [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 2en → [Cu(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 4NH₃ [1].

**(b)** Kstab for [Cu(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ is larger than for [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ [1], so the en complex is the more stable and the position of equilibrium lies on the side of the en complex [1].

**(c)** K = 10¹⁹·⁶ ÷ 10¹³·¹ = 10⁶·⁵ = 3.2 × 10⁶ [1].

**9. (a)** Ratio = Kstab × [NH₃]² [1] = 1.6 × 10⁷ × (0.010)² = 1.6 × 10³ [1].

**(b)** About 1 part in 1600, i.e. roughly 6 × 10⁻⁴ (0.06%) of the silver is Ag⁺(aq) [1].

**10.** [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + EDTA⁴⁻ → [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ + 6H₂O: one hexadentate ligand replaces six water ligands [1]. The number of particles increases from two to seven [1], so the entropy of the system increases, which favours the formation of the complex and gives a large Kstab [1].

**11.** Ratio = Kstab × [en]³, so [en]³ = 1.0 × 10⁶ / 1.0 × 10¹⁸ [1] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹² [1]; [en] = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ [1].

**12.** Kstab for [CuCl₄]²⁻ is smaller, but it still has a value: the position of equilibrium depends on the concentration of Cl⁻ as well as on Kstab [1]. Concentrated HCl provides a very high [Cl⁻], which shifts the equilibrium [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CuCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O to the right, so the yellow-green [CuCl₄]²⁻ forms [1]. Adding water (diluting) lowers [Cl⁻] and shifts the equilibrium back to the left, re-forming the pale blue aqua ion [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Including [H₂O] in the expression or leaving out the aqua ion.
- Using the number of donor atoms, not the number of ligand particles, as the power.
- Quoting units without working them out from the expression.
- Using the initial ligand concentration when a large amount of ligand has been used up.
- Dividing lg Kstab values instead of subtracting them.
- Explaining ligand exchange without linking the larger Kstab to the more stable complex.
