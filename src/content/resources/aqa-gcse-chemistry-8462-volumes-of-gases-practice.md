These questions cover section **4.3.5 Use of amount of substance in relation to volumes of gases** of the AQA
GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification, for teaching from September 2016 onwards (version 1.2). The whole
subsection is marked (HT only), so every question is labelled **Higher tier only**. Take the volume of one mole
of any gas at room temperature and pressure as 24 dm³. Relative atomic masses: H 1, C 12, N 14, O 16, Mg 24,
K 39, Cl 35.5, Ca 40, Zn 65. Show your working.

Learn the content first in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-volumes-of-gases/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-volumes-of-gases-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (Higher tier only) State the volume occupied by one mole of any gas at room temperature and pressure, and
the conditions this applies to. **[2]**

**2.** (Higher tier only) Calculate the volume of 3.2 g of oxygen, O₂, at room temperature and pressure. **[2]**

**3.** (Higher tier only) Calculate the volume of 11.0 g of carbon dioxide, CO₂, at room temperature and
pressure. **[2]**

**4.** (Higher tier only) Calculate the mass of 6.0 dm³ of nitrogen, N₂, at room temperature and pressure. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** (Higher tier only) Propane burns completely: C₃H₈(g) + 5O₂(g) → 3CO₂(g) + 4H₂O(g)
2.0 dm³ of propane burns at a constant temperature and pressure.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of oxygen needed. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide formed. **[1]**
**(c)** State why the ratio of volumes can be taken from the equation. **[1]**

**6.** (Higher tier only) Calcium carbonate reacts with excess hydrochloric acid:
CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂
10.0 g of calcium carbonate reacts completely.

**(a)** Calculate the number of moles of calcium carbonate. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide made at room temperature and pressure, in dm³. **[2]**
**(c)** Give your answer to part (b) in cm³. **[1]**

**7.** (Higher tier only) Zinc reacts with excess dilute hydrochloric acid: Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂
Calculate the volume of hydrogen formed at room temperature and pressure from 3.25 g of zinc. **[3]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) Potassium chlorate decomposes on heating: 2KClO₃ → 2KCl + 3O₂ (Mr of KClO₃ = 122.5)
Calculate the volume of oxygen, in dm³, produced at room temperature and pressure from 12.25 g of potassium
chlorate. **[4]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) A gas has a volume of 0.60 dm³ at room temperature and pressure and a mass of 1.1 g.
Calculate its relative formula mass, and suggest what the gas could be. **[3]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) A student reacts 0.12 g of magnesium with excess dilute hydrochloric acid and collects
118 cm³ of hydrogen at room temperature and pressure.

**(a)** Calculate the theoretical volume of hydrogen in cm³. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the volume collected as a percentage of the theoretical volume. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **24 dm³** [1] at **room temperature and pressure (20 °C and 1 atmosphere)** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Moles = 3.2 ÷ 32 = 0.10 mol [1]; volume = 0.10 × 24 = **2.4 dm³** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Moles = 11.0 ÷ 44 = 0.25 mol [1]; volume = 0.25 × 24 = **6.0 dm³** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Moles = 6.0 ÷ 24 = 0.25 mol [1]; mass = 0.25 × 28 = **7.0 g** [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** 5 × 2.0 = **10 dm³** [1]
**(b)** 3 × 2.0 = **6.0 dm³** [1]
**(c)** Equal amounts in moles of gases occupy the same volume under the same conditions, so the volume ratio
equals the mole ratio [1]

**6. (a)** 10.0 ÷ 100 = **0.100 mol** [1]
**(b)** Ratio CaCO₃ : CO₂ = 1 : 1, so 0.100 mol of CO₂ [1]; volume = 0.100 × 24 = **2.4 dm³** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 2.4 × 1000 = **2400 cm³** [1]

**7.** Moles Zn = 3.25 ÷ 65 = 0.050 mol [1]; ratio Zn : H₂ = 1 : 1, so 0.050 mol H₂ [1]; volume = 0.050 × 24 = **1.2 dm³** [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Moles KClO₃ = 12.25 ÷ 122.5 = 0.100 mol [1]; ratio KClO₃ : O₂ = 2 : 3, so moles O₂ = 0.100 × 3 ÷ 2 =
0.150 mol [2]; volume = 0.150 × 24 = **3.6 dm³** [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Moles = 0.60 ÷ 24 = 0.025 mol [1]; Mr = 1.1 ÷ 0.025 = **44** [1]; the gas could be **carbon dioxide** (or
propane, C₃H₈) [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** Moles Mg = 0.12 ÷ 24 = 0.0050 mol [1]; moles H₂ = 0.0050 mol [1]; volume = 0.0050 × 24 = 0.12 dm³ = **120 cm³** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 118 ÷ 120 × 100 = **98.3%** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using 24 with cm³ or forgetting to convert.
- Using the wrong Mr for a diatomic gas.
- Using the mass ratio instead of the mole ratio.
- Forgetting the unit or the conditions.
- Applying the volume ratio incorrectly when one of the substances is a solid.
