## Questions

**1.** Define enterprise as a factor of production. **[2]**

**2.** A florist uses a delivery van, the work of a driver, the cash in her till and a plot of ground for growing flowers.

**(a)** Identify the factor of production each of the van, the driver and the plot represents. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the cash is not capital. **[2]**

**3.** A technician takes a course costing 1,800 cu. It raises her pay by 450 cu a year for 8 years.

**(a)** Calculate the payback period. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the net gain over the 8 years. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why her employer may be unwilling to pay for the course. **[3]**

**4.** An orchard has yearly revenue of 84,000 cu. It pays 30,000 in wages, 9,000 in rent, 4,000 in interest, and 21,000 for other costs.

**(a)** Calculate profit. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate profit as a percentage of revenue, to one decimal place. **[1]**
**(c)** A poor harvest cuts revenue by 25%. Other costs and the rewards above stay the same. Calculate the new profit or loss. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the owner, not the workers or lender, bears this result. **[2]**

**5.** A sandal workshop employs 3 workers, each paid 70 cu a day. Each makes 4 sandals a day working alone. After dividing the work into cutting, stitching and finishing, the shop makes 21 sandals a day.

**(a)** Calculate output per worker before and after. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage rise in output per worker. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate labour cost per sandal before and after. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain one risk of the new system for the shop. **[2]**

**6.** Explain two disadvantages of division of labour. **[4]**

**7.** A kiosk has fixed costs of 2,400 cu a month. It sells each item for 5 cu and each costs 2 cu to supply.

**(a)** Calculate the break-even output. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate profit or loss if it sells 650 items. **[2]**
**(c)** Margin of safety is the gap between sales and break-even output, as a percentage of sales. Calculate it for sales of 1,000 items. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain how your answers to (b) and (c) show the risk taken by the owner. **[2]**

**8.** Compare how the question of what to produce is answered in a market economy and in a planned economy. **[6]**

**9.** In Country C the market for a good has these demand and supply schedules.

| Price (cu) | Quantity demanded | Quantity supplied |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 700 | 300 |
| 6 | 600 | 400 |
| 8 | 500 | 500 |
| 10 | 400 | 600 |

**(a)** State the market equilibrium price and quantity. **[1]**
**(b)** Planners fix the price at 6 cu and set output at the quantity producers would supply at that price. Calculate the shortage and express it as a percentage of the quantity demanded. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain two consequences of this shortage. **[4]**

**10.** Evaluate whether a mixed economy is better than a market economy. **[8]**

**11.** A factory in Country D has 10 workers paid 100 cu a day each. Before it divides tasks, it makes 60 units a day. After, it makes 110 units a day, but yearly staff turnover rises from 5% to 20%.

**(a)** Calculate labour cost per unit before and after, to two decimal places. **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether the factory should keep the new system. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Enterprise is taking the risk of starting or running a business [1] and organising land, labour and capital to produce [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition with only one of the two roles is incomplete, so include both risk and organisation.

**2. (a)** The van is capital [1]. The driver's work is labour [1]. The plot is land [1].
**(b)** Cash is money, a claim on resources, and it does not itself produce anything [1]. Only goods bought with it, such as a van or an oven, are capital [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say why the cash does not produce goods, not just that "money is not capital". The explanation earns the credit.

**3. (a)** Payback = cost ÷ yearly gain [1] = 1,800 ÷ 450 = **4 years** [1].
**(b)** Total gain = 450 × 8 = 3,600 cu [1]. Net gain = 3,600 − 1,800 = **1,800 cu** [1].
**(c)** Human capital leaves with the worker [1]. The employer pays but gains only while she stays [1]. If she left after 2 years the gain would be 900 cu against a cost of 1,800 cu [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* In a "calculate" part, show the formula and each step so your method is clear even if a slip occurs.

**4. (a)** Costs = 30,000 + 9,000 + 4,000 + 21,000 = 64,000 [1]. Profit = 84,000 − 64,000 = **20,000 cu** [1].
**(b)** 20,000 ÷ 84,000 × 100 = **23.8%** [1].
**(c)** New revenue = 84,000 × 0.75 = 63,000 [1]. Profit = 63,000 − 64,000 = **−1,000 cu (a loss of 1,000 cu)** [1].
**(d)** Wages, rent and interest are agreed and paid whatever the harvest [1]. Profit is the residual, so the owner absorbs the fall [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A negative profit should be stated as a loss, with the sign. Do not write a positive figure.

**5. (a)** Before: 4 per worker [1]. After: 21 ÷ 3 = **7 per worker** [1].
**(b)** (7 − 4) ÷ 4 × 100 [1] = **75%** [1].
**(c)** Wage bill = 3 × 70 = 210 cu. Before: 210 ÷ 12 = **17.50 cu** [1]. After: 210 ÷ 21 = **10 cu** [1].
**(d)** If one worker is absent, the other stages cannot work at full speed [1], so total output could fall by far more than the share of work lost [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A risk explained as a chain (cause, then effect on output or cost) is stronger than a named risk alone.

**6.** Any two, each with a point and a development.
Boredom and lower motivation [1]: repeating one task leads to errors, absence and workers leaving [1].
Narrow skills [1]: workers are harder to move to other jobs, which may cause structural unemployment if the task is no longer needed [1]. (Also allow: stoppage if one stage fails, or less variety for consumers.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Boring" on its own gains little. Show what follows for the firm or the worker.

**7. (a)** Contribution = 5 − 2 = 3 cu [1]. Break-even = 2,400 ÷ 3 = **800 items** [1].
**(b)** Revenue 3,250, variable costs 1,300, fixed costs 2,400, total costs 3,700 [1]. Profit = 3,250 − 3,700 = **−450 cu (a loss)** [1].
**(c)** (1,000 − 800) ÷ 1,000 × 100 = **20%** [1].
**(d)** Costs are due whatever sales are, so at 650 items the owner takes a loss [1]. Even at 1,000 items the profit (600 cu) is lost if sales fall more than 20% [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the calculations back to risk in the final part. Repeating the figures without that link misses the point of the question.

**8.** Market: consumers signal demand through spending and willingness to pay [1]. Firms respond to expected profit [1]. Resources move to products where profit is high [1]. Planned: the state or planners decide [1] using targets and quotas based on estimates of need [1]. Difference: the market responds to prices and profit, the plan to central decisions, and the plan may miss real preferences [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs a point of contrast (who decides, what signal). Two separate descriptions without a link are less effective.

**9. (a)** Price **8 cu**, quantity **500** [1].
**(b)** At 6 cu demand is 600 and supply is 400 [1]. Shortage = 600 − 400 = **200 units** [1]. 200 ÷ 600 × 100 = **33.3%** [1].
**(c)** Queues or rationing, because the price cannot rise to clear the market [1], so buyers lose time or have to be selected by the state [1]. A black market may develop [1], where goods sell above 6 cu to those willing to pay more [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Each consequence should be explained, not just named. "Black market" earns more when tied to the unmet demand.

**10.** Relevant points, each developed:
- Market: price signals respond quickly to changes in wants [1] and profit rewards efficiency [1].
- Limits: markets can under-provide public goods and ignore those without income [1].
- Mixed: government can correct these through provision, tax and benefits [1].
- Cost: government can also err, creating costs or delays [1].
- Judgement: the answer depends on how well the state intervenes [1] and how large the gaps in the market are [1].
- Conclusion: a reasoned view, for example that a mixed economy is better where market gaps are large and the state is capable of acting [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A judgement that says "it depends" earns more when you state what it depends on and which way you lean.

**11. (a)** Wage bill = 10 × 100 = 1,000 cu [1]. Before: 1,000 ÷ 60 = **16.67 cu** [1]. After: 1,000 ÷ 110 = **9.09 cu** [1].
**(b)** Unit cost falls by 45.5% [1] and output rises 83.3% [1].
Turnover from 5% to 20% suggests lower motivation [1], so recruitment and training costs rise [1].
These costs are not in the wage-based unit costs above [1].
Whether to keep it depends on how large these extra costs are [1] and whether demand can absorb the extra output [1].
Judgement: keep the system, since unit cost has nearly halved, but monitor turnover [1]. **[11]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the data in your argument, here the unit cost and the turnover, and weigh them against each other before you conclude.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining enterprise as risk-taking only.
- Calling cash or a bank loan capital.
- Treating human capital as something the firm owns.
- Leaving out the sign on a loss.
- Giving percentages of the wrong base, such as dividing by the new value instead of the old.
- Listing disadvantages of division of labour without explaining the effect.
- Describing a planned economy as having no markets at all.
- Concluding an evaluation question without saying what the answer depends on.
