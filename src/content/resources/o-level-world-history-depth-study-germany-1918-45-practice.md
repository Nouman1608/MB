In Cambridge O Level History 2147, Depth Study B is examined in **Paper 1, Section B**: two questions per Depth Study, answer one, each worth 20 marks -- (a) 4, (b) 6, (c) 10. The three questions below cover all of key questions 1, 2 and 3.

## Question 1: The Weimar Republic (key question 1)

**(a)** What were the terms of the Treaty of Versailles that affected Germany's armed forces? **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why the occupation of the Ruhr in 1923 caused problems for the Weimar Republic. **[6]**

**(c)** "The Weimar Republic had achieved a great deal by 1929." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

#### Worked answer

**(a)** The army was limited to 100,000 men; conscription was banned; no tanks; no air force; the navy was limited to six battleships and no submarines; the Rhineland was demilitarised.

**(b)** France and Belgium occupied the Ruhr in January 1923 because Germany fell behind with reparations. The government ordered passive resistance, so the Ruhr's industry -- the heart of German coal and steel production -- stopped producing; the government printed money to pay striking workers, causing hyper-inflation that wiped out savings and pensions; middle-class Germans blamed the Republic; and the crisis encouraged extremists, including Hitler's Munich Putsch in November.

**(c)** *Agree:* the Rentenmark ended hyper-inflation; the Dawes Plan (1924) eased reparations and brought loans that funded new housing, roads and factories; Stresemann won back international respect through Locarno (1925) and membership of the League (1926); the Young Plan (1929) cut reparations further; Weimar culture flourished. *Disagree:* recovery depended on short-term American loans; farmers were in difficulty before 1929; unemployment never fell very low; coalition governments remained unstable; extremist parties and the army were still hostile; Hindenburg, a monarchist, became President in 1925. *Judgement:* much had been achieved, but the foundations were fragile, as the Depression showed.

## Question 2: Hitler's rise to power (key question 2)

**(a)** Describe what happened during the Munich Putsch of 1923. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why Hitler was appointed Chancellor in January 1933. **[6]**

**(c)** "The Enabling Act was the most important step in Hitler's consolidation of power in 1933-34." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

#### Worked answer

**(a)** On 8 November 1923 Hitler and the SA burst into a political meeting in a Munich beer hall; he announced a national revolution and forced Bavarian leaders to support him; they later withdrew their support; the next day Hitler and Ludendorff marched through Munich; police opened fire and sixteen Nazis were killed; Hitler was arrested, tried and sentenced to five years, serving about nine months.

**(b)** The Depression created mass unemployment and despair, and the Nazis gained votes by promising work and national revival; they became the largest party in July 1932, so it was hard to govern without them; Chancellors Papen and Schleicher could not form stable majorities; Papen persuaded Hindenburg that Hitler could be controlled in a cabinet with only a few Nazis; business leaders and conservatives feared communism more than Hitler.

**(c)** *Enabling Act:* passed in March 1933 with communist deputies excluded and the Centre Party's support; it allowed Hitler to pass laws without the Reichstag, making all later steps legal. *Other steps:* the Reichstag Fire decree (February) suspended civil rights and allowed communists to be arrested; banning trade unions (May) and all other parties (July) created a one-party state; the Night of the Long Knives (June 1934) removed the SA threat and won the army's loyalty; Hindenburg's death (August 1934) let Hitler become Führer with the army's oath. *Judgement:* the Enabling Act was the legal foundation, but the army's support after June-August 1934 removed the last power that could stop him.

## Question 3: Nazi Germany (key question 3)

**(a)** Describe Nazi policies towards women. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why the Nazis persecuted many groups in German society. **[6]**

**(c)** "Most Germans benefited from Nazi rule before 1939." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

#### Worked answer

**(a)** Women were encouraged to focus on "children, kitchen, church"; marriage loans were given and reduced with each child; the Mother's Cross rewarded large families; women were discouraged from or removed from some professions such as law and medicine; the League of German Maidens prepared girls for motherhood; make-up and smoking were discouraged.

**(b)** Nazi racial ideology saw "Aryans" as superior and Jews as enemies of Germany, blamed for defeat in 1918 and economic problems; persecution provided scapegoats and united "true" Germans; the Nazis wanted a racially "pure" community, so they targeted Roma, disabled people (forced sterilisation from 1933) and homosexuals; political opponents and some religious groups, such as Jehovah's Witnesses, refused loyalty to the state.

**(c)** *Agree:* unemployment fell from around six million in 1933 through public works, rearmament and conscription; Strength Through Joy offered holidays and leisure; many enjoyed renewed national pride and order. *Disagree:* workers lost their trade unions and the right to strike, and hours increased; consumer goods were limited as rearmament came first; women lost career opportunities; Jews, Roma and others suffered persecution, Kristallnacht and loss of rights; everyone lost freedom of speech. *Judgement:* many "Aryan" Germans felt they benefited materially, but the gains were limited and came at the cost of freedom and the suffering of excluded groups.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing the Treaty of Versailles in full when asked only about armed forces.
- Saying Hitler won an election to become Chancellor.
- In (c), writing about only one side or one group of Germans.

## Frequently asked questions

**How precise should dates be?** Month and year for the key events of 1923 and 1933-34 is ideal.

**Can I use statistics?** Yes. Seats won in 1928 (12) and July 1932 (230), and unemployment figures, make arguments stronger.
