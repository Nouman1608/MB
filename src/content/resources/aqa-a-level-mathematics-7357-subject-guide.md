AQA A-Level Mathematics (7357) takes the algebra, geometry and number
skills from GCSE and develops them into calculus, proof and modelling,
with applications in mechanics and statistics. It is a natural choice
for students heading towards science, engineering, economics or
computing, and it is valuable in its own right for anyone who enjoys
problem solving. The course is assessed by three compulsory written
papers, each 2 hours long and worth 100 marks, for 300 marks in total.

## How the course is organised

The subject content is a single lettered list, A to S, plus a set of
overarching themes that apply everywhere. It helps to see it in three
strands.

- **Overarching themes.** Mathematical argument, language and proof;
  mathematical problem solving; and mathematical modelling. These are
  not taught as a separate block. They describe how every topic should
  be used and are assessed across all three papers.
- **Pure mathematics (A to J).** Proof; algebra and functions;
  coordinate geometry in the (x, y) plane; sequences and series;
  trigonometry; exponentials and logarithms; differentiation;
  integration; numerical methods; and vectors. Algebra and functions is
  the broadest section, running from laws of indices for rational
  exponents and surds through quadratics, simultaneous equations,
  inequalities and polynomials to graphs of functions, composite and
  inverse functions, transformations, partial fractions and functions
  in modelling. Vectors (J) sits in this lettered list, but AQA's paper
  allocation assesses it alongside mechanics on Paper 2.
- **Statistics (K to O).** Statistical sampling; data presentation and
  interpretation; probability; statistical distributions; and
  statistical hypothesis testing, together with the use of data in
  statistics, which runs alongside this strand.
- **Mechanics (P to S).** Quantities and units in mechanics;
  kinematics; forces and Newton's laws; and moments.

## How it's assessed

The qualification is linear and not tiered. All three papers are taken
at the end of the course, and each mixes short single-mark questions
with longer multi-step problems.

| Paper | Content | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7357/1 | Pure content: proof, algebra and functions, coordinate geometry, sequences and series, trigonometry, exponentials and logarithms, differentiation, integration, numerical methods | 2 hours | 100 | 33.33% |
| 7357/2 | Paper 1 content plus mechanics: vectors, quantities and units in mechanics, kinematics, forces and Newton's laws, moments | 2 hours | 100 | 33.33% |
| 7357/3 | Paper 1 content plus statistics: sampling, data presentation and interpretation, probability, statistical distributions, hypothesis testing | 2 hours | 100 | 33.34% |

Each paper is worth one third of the A-level; the third paper is
recorded as 33.34% only so that the figures sum to exactly 100. The
specification was first taught in September 2017 and first assessed in
June 2018. Its subject content is common to all exam boards, because it
is set by the Department for Education.

## How the parts fit together

The structure of the papers tells you how the course really works:
pure mathematics is not confined to Paper 1. Papers 2 and 3 both
assume all of the Paper 1 content as well as their applied topics.
A mechanics question may need you to differentiate a displacement
function or resolve vectors using trigonometry; a statistics question
may need algebraic manipulation, logarithms or careful use of
inequalities. Weaknesses in pure content therefore show up on every
paper.

The applied strands also connect to each other through modelling.
Kinematics and forces ask you to build a simplified model of a moving
object and question its assumptions; statistical distributions and
hypothesis testing ask you to model a population and judge how far the
data supports a claim. In both cases the overarching themes are being
tested as much as the content: choosing a method, justifying each step
and interpreting an answer in context.

Proof is the other thread. It appears as its own section, but the
habit of clear mathematical argument and correct notation is expected
in every extended answer, from showing that an identity holds to
explaining why a model is or is not reasonable.

## Planning your study

There is no single required order, but most courses follow a similar
logic.

1. **Secure the algebra early.** Indices, surds, quadratics, simultaneous
   equations and inequalities are used in almost every later topic, so
   time spent here pays back throughout.
2. **Build the core pure sequence.** Coordinate geometry, trigonometry,
   exponentials and logarithms, and then differentiation and
   integration, ideally with sequences and series and proof woven in
   along the way.
3. **Start the applied strands in parallel.** Many centres introduce
   statistical sampling, data presentation and probability, and then
   kinematics and forces, during the first year, so that applied
   questions can draw on growing pure skills.
4. **Extend in the second year.** Functions, partial fractions, further
   calculus, numerical methods and vectors deepen the pure strand, while
   statistical distributions, hypothesis testing and moments complete
   the applied content.
5. **Consolidate.** Mix topics deliberately in practice so that you get
   used to questions that combine pure and applied ideas.

Topic-by-topic practice should start with the first lesson. Full
papers and the paper-by-paper exam preparation page are most useful
once you have met the content of all three papers, usually during the
final months of the course.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are in AQA A-Level Maths?**
There are three compulsory written papers. Each lasts 2 hours, is worth 100 marks and carries one third of the A-level, giving 300 marks in total.

**Is there pure maths on Papers 2 and 3?**
Yes. Paper 2 assesses the Paper 1 pure content plus mechanics, and Paper 3 assesses the Paper 1 pure content plus statistics.

**Can I choose between mechanics and statistics?**
No. Both applied strands are compulsory: mechanics is assessed on Paper 2 and statistics on Paper 3.

**Is the content the same as other boards' A-Level Maths?**
The subject content is common across all exam boards because it is set by the Department for Education, but each board writes its own papers and structure.

**When was AQA A-Level Mathematics 7357 first assessed?**
It was first taught from September 2017 and first assessed in June 2018.
