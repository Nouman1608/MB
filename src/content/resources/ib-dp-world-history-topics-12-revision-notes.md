These notes condense world history topic 12 for Paper 2: the origins, development and end of the Cold War, the role of leaders and nations, and the crises that defined it. The comparative arguments are in the [Cold War study guide](/resources/ib-dp-world-history-topics-12/); test yourself with the [Cold War practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-world-history-topics-12-practice/).

## Chronology

### 1943-1949: breakdown of the grand alliance

- 1943: Tehran Conference.
- February 1945: Yalta; July-August 1945: Potsdam.
- August 1945: atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki.
- 1946: Kennan's Long Telegram; Churchill's "iron curtain" speech.
- March 1947: Truman Doctrine; June 1947: Marshall Plan announced; Cominform founded (September 1947).
- February 1948: communist takeover in Czechoslovakia.
- June 1948-May 1949: Berlin Blockade and airlift.
- 1949: NATO (April); two German states; Soviet atomic bomb (August); People's Republic of China (October).

### 1947-1979: superpowers and China

- 1950: Sino-Soviet Treaty; Korean War begins (25 June); armistice 27 July 1953.
- 1953: Stalin dies.
- 1955: Warsaw Pact.
- 1956: Khrushchev's "Secret Speech"; peaceful co-existence; Hungarian uprising crushed.
- 1957: Sputnik.
- 1960: U-2 incident; Sino-Soviet split becomes open in the early 1960s.
- 13 August 1961: Berlin Wall.
- October 1962: Cuban Missile Crisis.
- 1963: hotline; Partial Test Ban Treaty.
- 1968: Prague Spring crushed; Brezhnev Doctrine.
- 1969: Sino-Soviet border clashes.
- February 1972: Nixon in Beijing; May 1972: SALT I.
- 1975: Helsinki Accords; fall of Saigon (April).
- December 1979: Soviet invasion of Afghanistan.

### 1980-1991: confrontation, reconciliation and the end

- 1980: Solidarity in Poland.
- 1983: Reagan's "evil empire" speech and the Strategic Defense Initiative.
- 1985: Gorbachev becomes Soviet leader.
- 1986: Reykjavik summit.
- 1987: INF Treaty.
- 1988-89: Soviet withdrawal from Afghanistan (completed February 1989).
- 9 November 1989: Berlin Wall opened.
- October 1990: German reunification.
- December 1991: USSR dissolved.

## Leaders: impact in one line

| Leader | Region | Impact on the Cold War |
|---|---|---|
| Truman | Americas | Containment; Truman Doctrine; Marshall Plan; NATO; intervention in Korea |
| Stalin | Europe | Control of Eastern Europe; Berlin Blockade; approval of North Korea's invasion |
| Khrushchev | Europe | Peaceful co-existence alongside brinkmanship over Berlin and Cuba |
| Mao | Asia and Oceania | Intervention in Korea (1950); split with the USSR; opening to the USA |
| Castro | Americas | Brought the Cold War to the Caribbean; Cuban troops in Angola from 1975 |
| Reagan | Americas | Arms build-up and pressure, then arms control with Gorbachev |
| Gorbachev | Europe | Reform, withdrawal from Afghanistan, refusal to use force in Eastern Europe in 1989 |

**Pairing rule:** for "two leaders, each chosen from a different region", Khrushchev and Mao, or Gorbachev and Reagan, qualify; Stalin and Khrushchev do not.

## Nations: Cold War impact (not the USA or USSR)

| Country | Impact of Cold War tensions |
|---|---|
| Germany | Divided in 1949; Berlin a flashpoint until 1989; reunified 1990 |
| Korea | Divided at the 38th parallel; war 1950-53; permanent division |
| Vietnam | Division at Geneva (1954); US war; reunification under the communists (1975-76) |
| Cuba | Revolution, Bay of Pigs, missile crisis, dependence on Soviet aid |
| Afghanistan | Soviet invasion (1979) and war; US aid to the mujahideen |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Containment | US policy of preventing the spread of communism |
| Peaceful co-existence | Khrushchev's policy of competition without war |
| Brinkmanship | Pushing a crisis to the edge of war to force the other side to back down |
| Détente | Easing of tension through negotiation and agreements in the 1970s |
| Proxy war | A conflict in which the superpowers back opposing sides without fighting each other directly |
| Mutually assured destruction | The idea that nuclear war would destroy both sides, deterring its use |
| Brezhnev Doctrine | Soviet claim to intervene to protect socialism in Eastern Europe |
| Sino-Soviet split | Breakdown of relations between China and the USSR from the late 1950s |

## Historiography in brief

| School | Main claim |
|---|---|
| Orthodox | Soviet expansion caused the Cold War; the West responded |
| Revisionist | US economic and political expansion provoked Soviet reactions (William Appleman Williams) |
| Post-revisionist | Mutual misperception and shared responsibility (John Lewis Gaddis, in his earlier work) |

## Exam traps

- **USA or USSR as one of "two countries".** They are excluded.
- **Two European crises** when the question asks for different regions.
- **The Cold War as a two-power story.** China appears explicitly in the guide.
- **A chronological essay on the end of the Cold War.** Weigh the three factors the guide names.

## Self-test

1. When was the Truman Doctrine announced? *March 1947.*
2. When did the Berlin Blockade begin and end? *June 1948 and May 1949.*
3. When was NATO formed? *April 1949.*
4. When did the Korean War begin and the armistice take place? *25 June 1950 and 27 July 1953.*
5. What was peaceful co-existence? *Khrushchev's 1956 policy of competing with the West without war.*
6. When was the Berlin Wall built? *13 August 1961.*
7. What was the Brezhnev Doctrine? *The Soviet claim to intervene to defend socialism in Eastern Europe, used to justify the 1968 invasion of Czechoslovakia.*
8. Name two achievements of détente. *Any two: SALT I (1972), Nixon's visit to China, the Helsinki Accords (1975).*
9. What ended détente? *The Soviet invasion of Afghanistan in December 1979, among other strains.*
10. What did the INF Treaty of 1987 do? *Eliminated US and Soviet intermediate-range nuclear missiles.*
11. Name the three reasons for the end of the Cold War listed in the guide. *Ideological challenges and dissent, economic problems, the arms race.*
12. Why are Gorbachev and Reagan a valid "two leaders" pair? *They come from different regions: Europe and the Americas.*
