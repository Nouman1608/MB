## The principle in one line

Accruals concept: each item of income or expense is placed in the year it relates to, whatever the date of the cash payment or receipt.

## The four adjustments at a glance

| Adjustment | Meaning | Effect on profit | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Other payables | Expense used, not yet paid | Profit down | Current liability |
| Other receivables | Expense paid, not yet used | Profit up | Current asset |
| Income due | Income earned, not yet received | Profit up | Current asset |
| Income received in advance | Cash received, service not yet given | Profit down | Current liability |

## Formulae

Use these as a check on your ledger. The opening figure is the balance brought down at the start of the year and the closing figure is the balance at the year end.

| Account | Figure transferred to the income statement |
|---|---|
| Expense with other payables | Paid - opening owing + closing owing |
| Expense with other receivables | Paid + opening prepaid - closing prepaid |
| Income with income due | Received - opening due + closing due |
| Income with income received in advance | Received + opening in advance - closing in advance |

Pattern: for anything you owe or are owed at the start, the opening amount belongs to last year, so reverse it. For anything outstanding at the end, the closing amount belongs to this year (or to next year), so apply it.

## Method in steps: balancing the ledger account

1. Write the account name and enter the cash paid or received during the year.
2. Enter the opening balance brought down on its natural side (liabilities on the credit side, assets on the debit side).
3. Enter the closing balance carried down on the opposite side from its brought down side.
4. Find the balancing figure, which is the transfer to the income statement.
5. Total both sides. They must be equal.
6. Bring the closing balance down on the first line of the new period.
7. Copy the closing balance to the statement of financial position, under the right heading.

## Which side? A quick table

| Adjustment | Opening balance on | Balance c/d on | Balance b/d on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Other payables | Credit | Debit | Credit |
| Other receivables | Debit | Credit | Debit |
| Income due | Debit | Credit | Debit |
| Income received in advance | Credit | Debit | Credit |

## Small worked reminders

Fraction method: an annual maintenance contract of 2,400 is paid on 1 October and covers twelve months. The year end is 31 December. Months used: 3, so the charge is 2,400 x 3/12 = 600. Months left: 9, so the other receivable is 2,400 x 9/12 = 1,800.

Profit correction: a draft profit of 15,000 omits 300 owing for telephone and 200 of commission due. The adjusted profit is 15,000 - 300 + 200 = 14,900.

Statement layout reminder: the expense or income appears in the income statement at the adjusted figure, while the closing balance appears separately in the statement of financial position.

## Where each figure goes

| Figure | Income statement | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Adjusted expense (other payables or other receivables) | Deducted from gross profit | Not shown |
| Closing other payables | Not shown | Current liabilities |
| Closing other receivables | Not shown | Current assets |
| Adjusted income (income due or in advance) | Added to gross profit | Not shown |
| Closing income due | Not shown | Current assets |
| Closing income received in advance | Not shown | Current liabilities |

## Checklist before you move on

- I can state what the accruals concept means and link it to each adjustment.
- I can name which adjustments are assets and which are liabilities.
- I can balance an expense account and an income account that have opening and closing balances.
- I can work out a prepaid or owing amount from dates and months.
- I can show the adjusted figure in the income statement and the closing balance in the statement of financial position.
- I can correct a draft profit for omitted adjustments.

## Must-know distinctions

- Other payables versus trade payables: other payables are for expenses such as electricity or wages, whereas trade payables are owed to suppliers of goods for resale. Do not mix them in one ledger.
- Other receivables versus income due: other receivables are expenses paid early, while income due is income earned but unpaid. Both are current assets, but they are separate lines.
- Other payables versus income received in advance: each is a current liability, but the first is a cost still to be paid and the second is money taken before the service.
- Cash paid versus the charge for the year: they are equal only when the opening and closing balances are both zero or cancel out.
- A balance carried down versus brought down: carried down closes the old period and brought down opens the new one.
- Reversal at the start of a year: an amount owing or prepaid at the end of one year becomes the opening balance of the next, so it is dealt with again when the next payment or receipt is recorded.

## Quick self-test

Answer all ten from memory, then check below. Businesses are invented.

1. Hartsop Bikes paid 900 for rates. There was 120 owing at the start of the year and 150 owing at the end. What is the charge to the income statement?
2. Pickwell Studio paid 1,500 for insurance. It had 200 prepaid at the start and 260 prepaid at the end. What is the charge?
3. A tenant paid Mayhew Lettings 3,300 during the year. At the start the tenant owed 100 and at the end owed 180. How much rent is income for the year?
4. Radnor Fitness received 2,400 in membership fees. At the start 300 was held in advance and at the end 500 is held in advance. What is the income for the year?
5. A magazine subscription of 3,600 is paid on 1 October for twelve months. The year end is 31 December. State the charge for the year and the other receivable.
6. A business omits 400 of electricity owing from its draft figures. What happens to profit when the adjustment is made?
7. On which side of an insurance account is the prepaid balance brought down?
8. Under which heading in the statement of financial position is income received in advance shown?
9. A draft profit of 12,000 does not include 250 of income due, 180 of other receivables and 90 of other payables. Find the adjusted profit.
10. Which accounting concept requires these adjustments?

### Answers

1. 900 - 120 + 150 = 930.
2. 1,500 + 200 - 260 = 1,440.
3. 3,300 - 100 + 180 = 3,380.
4. 2,400 + 300 - 500 = 2,200.
5. Charge 3,600 x 3/12 = 900. Other receivable 3,600 x 9/12 = 2,700.
6. Profit is reduced by 400.
7. The debit side.
8. Current liabilities.
9. 12,000 + 250 + 180 - 90 = 12,340.
10. The accruals concept.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the wrong sign for the opening balance, so that the charge comes out too high or too low.
- Leaving out the balance brought down or the line for the new period, or placing it on the wrong side.
- Writing "accrual" or "prepayment" on the statement of financial position when the question uses the specification's terms. Match the wording the question uses.
- Showing the cash paid in the income statement instead of the adjusted figure.
- Treating income received in advance as an asset or as income for the year.
- Forgetting to total both sides of the ledger account, which hides arithmetic slips.
- Adding all four adjustments to profit with the same sign instead of checking whether each increases or decreases profit.
- Listing the items under non-current headings.
- Giving a numerical answer with no working, so that an arithmetic slip leaves nothing to reward.
