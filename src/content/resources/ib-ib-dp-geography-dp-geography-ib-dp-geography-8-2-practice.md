## Section A

**1.** State the formula for the net migration rate. **[1]**

**2.** Define demographic momentum. **[2]**

**3.** State two of Ravenstein's patterns of migration. **[2]**

**4.** Outline the main type of movement in the early transitional stage of Zelinsky's mobility transition. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why male-dominated labour migration changes the population structure of source villages. **[2]**

**6.** Outline what is meant by chain migration. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The table shows invented data for a city in one year.

| Indicator | Value |
|---|---|
| Population | 800,000 |
| Crude birth rate | 11 per 1,000 |
| Crude death rate | 9 per 1,000 |
| Immigrants | 14,000 |
| Emigrants | 6,000 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate of natural change. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the net migration rate. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the total growth rate and the population at the end of the year. **[2]**

**(d)** State which component contributed more to growth. **[1]**

**8.** An invented country has 30 million women aged 15-44, of whom 12 million are aged 30-44. There are 25 million girls aged 0-14. Assume all cohorts survive.

**(a)** Calculate the number of women aged 15-44 in 15 years' time. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why births may rise even if fertility per woman falls slightly. **[2]**

**9.** Using a named example, explain the consequences of forced migration for the area that receives the migrants. **[4]**

**10.** Explain how rural depopulation can become self-reinforcing. **[3]**

## Section C

**11.** Examine the view that migration has a greater effect than natural change on the population structure of places. **[10]**

**12.** Evaluate the usefulness of models for explaining migration. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Net migration rate = (immigrants - emigrants) / population x 1,000 [1]. **[1]**

**2.** The tendency of a population to keep growing after fertility has fallen to replacement level [1], because a youthful age structure means the number of people entering child-bearing age keeps rising [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two, for example: most migrants move short distances [1]; migration often proceeds in steps [1]; long-distance migrants go to large centres of commerce and industry [1]; each stream produces a counter-stream [1]; economic motives dominate [1]. Maximum [2]. **[2]**

**4.** Heavy rural-urban migration [1], together with emigration abroad, as falling death rates increase rural populations beyond what the land can support [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Mainly young men leave to work elsewhere [1], so the source village's pyramid has a deficit of working-age males and a higher share of women, children and older people [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Migrants follow earlier migrants from the same place or family [1], who provide information, housing or jobs, so migration concentrates in particular destinations and neighbourhoods [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 11 - 9 = **+2 per 1,000** [1].

**(b)** (14,000 - 6,000) / 800,000 x 1,000 = **+10 per 1,000** [1].

**(c)** Growth = 2 + 10 = **12 per 1,000** (1.2%) [1]. End population = 800,000 x 1.012 = **809,600** [1].

**(d)** Net migration (+10 compared with +2 per 1,000) [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** 30 - 12 + 25 = **43 million** [2]; award [1] if the 30-44 group is not removed.

**(b)** The number of women of child-bearing age rises from 30 million to 43 million, a 43% increase [1], so total births can rise even if each woman has slightly fewer children [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Credit any named example, for example the Rohingya in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh, from 2017:

- Sudden arrival of more than 700,000 people placed great pressure on water, sanitation and health care [1].
- Forested hills were cleared for shelters and fuel, increasing landslide risk in the monsoon [1].
- Competition for jobs and rising prices affected the host population [1].
- Dependence on international aid and a protracted situation, because many refugees cannot return or work legally [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Young adults leave for jobs and education in cities [1], so births fall and the population ages, which means schools, shops and transport lose users and close [1]. Fewer services make the area less attractive, so more young people and families leave, continuing the decline [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Indicative content:

- **Natural change and structure:** birth rates shape the base of the pyramid; life expectancy shapes the top [1]; at national scale, natural change usually dominates over long periods [1].
- **Migration and structure:** migration is age-selective, mostly young adults, so it can change structure quickly [1]; Gulf states such as Qatar have pyramids dominated by male foreign workers [1].
- **Scale:** migration matters more at local scale, such as retirement coasts or rural areas losing young people [1] [1].
- **Forced migration:** sudden large arrivals reshape host areas, as in Cox's Bazar [1].
- **Interaction:** young migrants raise births in destinations and lower them in source areas, so migration also changes natural change [1].
- **Time:** momentum from past fertility shapes structure for decades [1].
- **Judgement:** for example, migration has the greater effect at local scale and in the short term, natural change at national scale and over the long term [1]. **[10]**

**12.** Indicative content:

- **Lee's model:** adds intervening obstacles and personal factors to push and pull, explaining why many do not move [1] [1].
- **Ravenstein:** distance decay and step migration still often hold [1]; but cheap transport and recruitment of international labour break some patterns [1].
- **Zelinsky:** links migration types to stages of development [1]; generalises from a few countries' history [1].
- **Forced migration:** models based on choice fit poorly when people flee conflict or persecution [1].
- **Uses:** models provide frameworks and testable predictions [1].
- **Limits:** they simplify individual decisions and ignore policy such as border controls [1].
- **Judgement:** for example, useful as frameworks for voluntary migration, less so for forced migration, so they should be applied with named evidence [1]. **[10]**

*Examiner insight:* both essays reward clear attention to scale and type of migration, and named places.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Comparing migration numbers between places without converting to rates.
- Forgetting that today's oldest women of child-bearing age leave the group in 15 years.
- Describing a forced-migration case without its effects on places.
- Treating migration models as laws.
