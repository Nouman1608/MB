## Set 1

**1(a)** Explain **two** ways in which the League's response to the Greek–Bulgarian War (1925) was
different from its response to the Italian invasion of Abyssinia (1935–36). **[6]**

**1(b)** Explain **two** causes of the League's failure in Manchuria (1931–33). **[8]**

**1(c)** "The attitude of the great powers was the main reason for the failures of international
organisations in the years 1930–1989." How far do you agree? **[16]**

You may use the following in your answer:
- Abyssinia (1935–36)
- the veto in the Security Council

You **must** also use information of your own.

## Set 2

**2(a)** Explain **two** ways in which UN peacekeeping in the Congo (1960–64) was different from UN
action in Korea (1950–53). **[6]**

**2(b)** Explain **two** causes of the UN's difficulties in Bosnia (1992–95). **[8]**

**2(c)** "The specialised agencies were the most successful part of the League and the UN in the years
1919–2011." How far do you agree? **[16]**

You may use the following in your answer:
- the Refugee Committee
- the WHO

You **must** also use information of your own.

## Answers and mark guidance

### 1(a) [6]

- **Outcome:** in 1925 the League's ceasefire order was obeyed and Greece withdrew and paid compensation;
  in 1935–36 Italy ignored the League and conquered Abyssinia.
- **Great-power stake:** in 1925 the aggressor was a small state and Britain and France backed the League;
  in 1935 the aggressor was a great power, and Britain and France limited sanctions (no oil ban, Suez Canal
  left open) and made the Hoare–Laval Pact.

### 1(b) [8]

- **Japan's power and distance:** Japan was a permanent Council member and a great power; no member was
  willing to send forces to the Far East.
- **Slow procedure and economic crisis:** the Lytton Commission took about a year to report; in the
  Depression, Britain and France would not risk trade or war with sanctions, so Japan left the League in
  1933 and kept Manchukuo.

### 1(c) [16]

**Great powers:** Britain and France's self-interest over Abyssinia; Japan's and Italy's withdrawals; the
USA's absence from the League; in the UN, Cold War vetoes blocked action; P5 interests in the Middle East
(Resolutions 242 and 338 not enforced).
**Other factors:** structural weaknesses — no League army, unanimity, slow procedures; the Depression;
the scale of aggression by dictators; in the UN, lack of troops and funds, unclear mandates (Congo,
Lebanon); local actors unwilling to make peace.
**Judgement:** great-power attitudes were usually decisive, because structure and resources depended on
them. Range across both organisations.

### 2(a) [6]

- **Type of conflict:** Korea was a war between states, with the UN force fighting North Korean and Chinese
  armies; the Congo was a civil war and secession crisis inside one newly independent state.
- **Leadership and role:** in Korea the USA commanded the UN force; in the Congo the Secretary-General
  organised a multinational peacekeeping force (ONUC) that tried to restore order and end Katanga's
  secession.

### 2(b) [8]

- **Weak mandate and resources:** peacekeepers were lightly armed and not authorised to fight, so they could
  not defend the "safe areas", as at Srebrenica (1995).
- **Divisions among the powers:** disagreements on the Security Council and reluctance to send troops meant
  decisive action came only with NATO air strikes in 1995.

### 2(c) [16]

**Agencies:** the Refugee Committee and Nansen passports; the Drugs, Health, Child Welfare and slavery
commissions; the ILO across both eras; UNICEF, UNHCR, FAO, UNESCO; the WHO's eradication of smallpox
(1980).
**Other successes:** League settlements of the 1920s (Aaland, Upper Silesia, Greece–Bulgaria); UN action in
Korea, Namibia and Mozambique; the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.
**Limits of the agencies:** dependent on funding and member cooperation; could not prevent war.
**Judgement:** the agencies achieved more consistent, lasting success, while peacekeeping succeeded only
when the great powers agreed.

## Set 3 — extra practice

**3(a)** Explain **two** ways in which the structure of the UN in 1945 was different from the structure
of the League in 1920. **[6]**

**3(b)** Explain **two** causes of the UN's success in Mozambique (1992–94). **[8]**

### 3(a) [6]

- **Decision-making:** the League Council needed unanimity; the UN Security Council decides by majority
  but each of its five permanent members has a veto.
- **Enforcement:** the League had no means of raising forces; the UN Charter allows the Security Council
  to authorise military action and member states to provide peacekeeping troops.

### 3(b) [8]

- **Willingness of the parties:** after a long civil war, the government and the rebel movement had both
  signed the 1992 peace agreement and wanted it to work.
- **Clear mandate and support:** the UN mission had a clear task — monitoring the ceasefire,
  demobilisation and elections — and enough troops and funding; the 1994 elections were held peacefully.

## Where marks are usually lost

- In (a), describing two events separately without making the difference explicit.
- In (b), naming causes without detail.
- In (c), discussing only the League or only the UN.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is the Paper 2 breadth study question structured?**
Part (a) asks you to explain two ways something was different at two dates (6 marks), part (b) to explain two causes (8 marks) and part (c) is a 16-mark essay on change, continuity or significance across the period.

**How did the League's response differ in 1925 and 1935–36?**
In 1925 its ceasefire order was obeyed and Greece withdrew and paid compensation; in 1935–36 Italy ignored the League and conquered Abyssinia.

**What is a common mistake in part (a)?**
Describing two events separately without making the difference between them explicit.
