This guide covers subtopic **35.2, Predicting the type of polymerisation**, from Topic 35,
Polymerisation, of Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027 series. It is A
Level content. The broader [Condensation Polymerisation and Polymer Degradability study guide](/resources/a-condensation-polymerisation-and-polymer-degradability/)
gives the basic rule; this page turns it into a step-by-step test that handles the awkward cases
examiners like: monomers with both a C=C and another functional group, benzene rings, side-chain
esters, and molecules that cannot polymerise at all.

## What the syllabus asks for

Subtopic 35.2 has two learning outcomes:

1. "predict the type of polymerisation reaction for a given monomer or pair of monomers"
2. "deduce the type of polymerisation reaction which produces a given section of a polymer molecule"

It draws on AS subtopic 20.1 (addition polymerisation, exemplified by poly(ethene) and
poly(chloroethene)) and A Level subtopic 35.1 (condensation polymerisation).

## The two types side by side

| | Addition | Condensation |
|---|---|---|
| Monomer feature | a C=C double bond | two reactive groups (–OH, –COOH, –COCl, –NH₂) |
| Number of monomers | one, or sometimes two alkenes | one (with two different groups) or two (each with two of the same group) |
| Small molecule lost | none | H₂O or HCl at each link |
| Backbone of polymer | carbon atoms only | contains O or N in ester or amide links |
| Repeat unit formula | same as the monomer | monomers minus the small molecules |

## Predicting from monomers: a three-question test

**Question 1: is there a C=C double bond outside a benzene ring?**
If yes, predict **addition** polymerisation. The C=C opens and the monomers join through those two
carbons. Any other groups on the molecule become **side groups** and do not take part.

- CH₂=CHCl (chloroethene) → addition, giving poly(chloroethene).
- CH₂=CHCOOH (propenoic acid) → addition; the –COOH groups hang off the chain.
- C₆H₅CH=CH₂ (phenylethene) → addition; the C=C is in the side chain, not the ring.

**Question 2: if there is no C=C, does each molecule have two reactive groups that can form ester or
amide links?**
If yes, predict **condensation**.

- One molecule with –OH and –COOH (a hydroxycarboxylic acid) → condensation polyester on its own.
- One molecule with –NH₂ and –COOH (an aminocarboxylic acid or amino acid) → condensation polyamide
  on its own.
- A diol with a dicarboxylic acid or dioyl chloride → polyester.
- A diamine with a dicarboxylic acid or dioyl chloride → polyamide.

**Question 3: if neither applies, no polymer forms.**

- Ethanol and ethanoic acid: only one reactive group each → a single ester, not a polymer.
- Ethane-1,2-diol on its own, or a diamine on its own: two groups, but of the same kind, so they
  cannot react with each other to make ester or amide links.
- Two dicarboxylic acids together: no group to link the acid groups.

## The benzene ring trap

A benzene ring contains delocalised π electrons, not isolated C=C bonds, and it does **not** undergo
addition polymerisation. So benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid with a diol gives a **condensation**
polymer (PET), even though the ring is often drawn with alternating double bonds. Ask whether the C=C
is outside the ring; if not, ignore the ring when deciding the type.

## Predicting from a polymer section

**Step 1: look along the backbone.**

- **Only carbon atoms in the main chain** → **addition** polymer.
- **O or N atoms in the main chain**, in –COO– or –CONH– links → **condensation** polymer.

**Step 2: do not be distracted by side groups.** A polymer such as

–CH₂C(CH₃)(COOCH₃)CH₂C(CH₃)(COOCH₃)–

contains ester groups, but they are **side groups** hanging off an all-carbon backbone. It is an
**addition** polymer, made from methyl 2-methylpropenoate, CH₂=C(CH₃)COOCH₃.

**Step 3: rebuild the monomer to check.**

- Addition: take a two-carbon backbone unit, put the C=C back, keep the side groups.
- Condensation: break each link at C–O or C–N and add back H and OH (see the
  [Condensation polymerisation study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-a-condensation-polymerisation/)).

## Using formulae as evidence

The repeat unit of an addition polymer has **the same molecular formula** as its monomer, because
nothing is lost. A condensation repeat unit has **fewer atoms** than its monomers combined. So if you
are told a polymer's repeat unit formula and its monomer's formula:

- identical → addition;
- different by H₂O, or by 2H₂O for two monomers (or HCl, 2HCl) → condensation.

It follows that the empirical formula of an addition polymer is the same as that of its monomer:
poly(ethene) and ethene are both CH₂.

## Worked example 1: classifying a set of monomers

**Question.** For each monomer or pair, predict the type of polymerisation, or state that no polymer
forms.

(a) CH₂=CHCN (b) H₂NCH₂CH₂COOH (c) HOCH₂CH₂OH with CH₃COOH (d) CH₂=CHCH₂OH (e) H₂N(CH₂)₄NH₂ with
ClOC(CH₂)₄COCl

**Answer.**

| | Key feature | Prediction |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | C=C | addition |
| (b) | –NH₂ and –COOH in one molecule, no C=C | condensation (polyamide) |
| (c) | ethanoic acid has only one –COOH | no polymer; a diester at most |
| (d) | C=C; the –OH is a side group | addition |
| (e) | diamine + dioyl chloride | condensation (polyamide), losing HCl |

*Examiner's note:* in (c), "condensation" is wrong even though an ester forms, because a monomer with
one reactive group stops the chain.

## Worked example 2: deducing the type from sections

**Question.** Deduce the type of polymerisation that produced each section, and identify the
monomer(s).

(i) –CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂CH(CH₃)–
(ii) –OCH₂COOCH₂CO–

**Answer.**

(i) The backbone is all carbon, so **addition**. The repeat unit is –CH₂CH(CH₃)–; putting the C=C
back gives **propene, CH₂=CHCH₃**. Check: the repeat unit and propene are both C₃H₆.

(ii) The backbone contains –COO– links, all facing the same way, so **condensation**, from a single
monomer, **hydroxyethanoic acid, HOCH₂COOH**.

## Worked example 3: an annotated model answer

**Question.** Propenamide, CH₂=CHCONH₂, contains an amide group. A student predicts it forms a
polyamide by condensation. Explain whether the student is correct and give the repeat unit of the
polymer formed. **[3]**

**Model answer.** The student is not correct: propenamide has a C=C double bond, so it polymerises by
**addition** **[1]**. Only one amide-forming group is present, and no small molecule is lost; the
–CONH₂ group becomes a side group on an all-carbon backbone **[1]**. The repeat unit is
**–CH₂CH(CONH₂)–** **[1]**.

*Why answers lose marks:* drawing the amide group inside the backbone, or describing the polymer as
a polyamide because it contains amide groups.

## Common mistakes

- Classifying a monomer as condensation because it contains –COOH or –OH, when it also has a C=C:
  the C=C decides it.
- Treating a benzene ring as a C=C for addition.
- Calling a polymer with side-chain esters a polyester.
- Predicting a polymer from monomers with only one reactive group each.
- Forgetting that addition polymers lose no small molecule, so the repeat unit formula equals the
  monomer formula.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Predict:** name the type and give the structural feature that decides it, in one sentence.
- **Deduce** from a section: say what is in the backbone (only C, or ester/amide links), then name the
  type and rebuild the monomer.
- **Explain** why a molecule cannot polymerise: count its reactive groups and say whether they can
  react with each other.
