These questions cover section **4.4.1 Photosynthesis** (4.4.1.1 to 4.4.1.3 and required practical activity 6)
of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, for first teaching from 2016. The content is on Paper 1. Questions
or parts marked **Higher tier only** cover (HT only) content. All data are invented for practice. A calculator is
useful for questions 3, 4, 8 and 9.

Learn the content first in the [Photosynthesis study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-photosynthesis-aqa-gcse-biology/)

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.**

**(a)** Name the **two** reactants in photosynthesis. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the source of energy for photosynthesis, and the substance in the chloroplast that absorbs it. **[2]**

**2.** A student says: "Plants always photosynthesise faster on a hotter day." Explain why this is not always
true. **[2]**

**3.** Pondweed releases 5.4 cm³ of oxygen in 4.5 minutes. Calculate the rate of photosynthesis. Give the unit. **[2]**

**4.** (Higher tier only) Calculate the relative light intensity, 1/d², for a lamp 30 cm away. Give your answer
in standard form to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**5.** A plant has fewer chloroplasts in its leaves than normal. Explain why its rate of photosynthesis in bright
light is lower than normal. **[2]**

### Section B: longer and structured questions

**6.** A student sets up a lamp next to a boiling tube of pondweed. There is no beaker of water between them. At
each distance she counts the bubbles for 30 seconds, once, starting as soon as she has moved the lamp.

**(a)** Identify **two** weaknesses in her method, and suggest an improvement for each. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why the temperature of the water must be kept constant. **[2]**

**7.** A scientist measures the rate of photosynthesis of an alga at two carbon dioxide concentrations, at a
constant temperature.

| Light intensity (arbitrary units) | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate at 0.01% CO₂ (arbitrary units) | 0 | 12 | 18 | 18 | 18 | 18 |
| Rate at 0.05% CO₂ (arbitrary units) | 0 | 12 | 24 | 33 | 38 | 38 |

**(a)** Describe how the rate at 0.01% CO₂ changes as light intensity increases. Use the data. **[2]**
**(b)** (Higher tier only) Identify the limiting factor at light intensity 2 and at light intensity 6 for the
0.01% CO₂ line. Explain each answer. **[2]**
**(c)** (Higher tier only) Suggest why the rate at 0.05% CO₂ does not rise above 38. **[1]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) A light meter reads 1440 units when a lamp is 12 cm away.

**(a)** Calculate the reading at 24 cm and at 36 cm. **[2]**
**(b)** At what distance would the reading be 1/25 of the reading at 12 cm? **[1]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) A cucumber grower considers three levels of carbon dioxide enrichment. Cucumbers sell
for £1.50 per kg.

| Level | Cost per week (£) | Extra yield per week (kg) |
|---|---|---|
| A | 40 | 40 |
| B | 70 | 60 |
| C | 110 | 62 |

**(a)** Calculate the weekly gain or loss at each level. **[3]**
**(b)** Recommend a level. Justify your choice. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the extra yield hardly changes between levels B and C. **[1]**

**10.**

**(a)** Explain why plants store glucose as starch rather than as glucose. **[2]**
**(b)** A plant grows in soil with no nitrate ions. State which use of glucose is affected, and explain why. **[2]**

**11.** Pondweed in a sealed container is kept outdoors for a day. The volume of oxygen it releases each hour is
measured. A negative value means oxygen is taken in.

| Time | 00:00 | 06:00 | 09:00 | 13:00 | 18:00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oxygen released (cm³ per hour) | −0.4 | 0.0 | 1.6 | 3.8 | 0.9 |

**(a)** Explain why the pondweed takes in oxygen at 00:00. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest why the pondweed releases the most oxygen at 13:00. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain the value at 06:00. **[2]**

**12.** Plan an investigation into the effect of light intensity on the rate of photosynthesis of pondweed. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Carbon dioxide and water (both needed) [1]
**(b)** Light [1]; chlorophyll [1]

**2.** Photosynthesis is controlled by enzymes [1]; above the optimum temperature the enzymes are denatured, so the rate falls (or another factor, such as light, may be limiting) [1]

**3.** 5.4 ÷ 4.5 = 1.2 [1]; **cm³ per minute** [1]

**4.** 1 ÷ 30² = 1 ÷ 900 = 0.001111... [1]; **1.11 × 10⁻³ cm⁻²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* 1/30 = 0.0333 uses distance, not distance squared, and scores zero.

**5.** Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll, so there is less chlorophyll [1]; so less light is absorbed and less energy is available for photosynthesis [1]

**6. (a)** Two weaknesses with an improvement each, for example:
- no heat shield, so the lamp may warm the water [1]; put a beaker of water between the lamp and tube and check with a thermometer [1]
- only one count at each distance [1]; repeat at least three times and calculate a mean [1]
Also allow: no time to adjust after moving the lamp → wait a few minutes before counting; counting bubbles is inaccurate → collect the gas in a syringe and measure volume.
**(b)** Temperature also affects the rate of photosynthesis [1]; so if it changes, the change in rate could not be put down to light intensity alone [1]

**7. (a)** The rate increases from 0 to 18 as light intensity rises from 0 to 4 [1]; then stays constant at 18 from 4 to 10 [1]
**(b)** At 2: light intensity, because both lines are the same and raising light raises the rate [1]; at 6: carbon dioxide concentration, because the 0.05% line is higher at the same light intensity [1]
**(c)** Another factor, such as temperature or the amount of chlorophyll, is now limiting [1]
*Examiner insight:* Naming light as limiting where the line is flat is the most common error in (b).

**8. (a)** 24 cm: 1440 × 1/4 = **360 units** [1]; 36 cm: 1440 × 1/9 = **160 units** [1]
**(b)** Intensity × 1/25 means distance × 5, so **60 cm** [1]

**9. (a)** A: 40 × 1.50 = £60, gain **£20** [1]; B: 60 × 1.50 = £90, gain **£20** [1]; C: 62 × 1.50 = £93, **loss of £17** [1]
**(b)** Level A [1]; it gives the same gain as B (£20) for a lower cost, so less money is at risk (C makes a loss) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Allow level B if justified by the larger yield at the same gain; the mark is for a reasoned choice using the calculated values.
**(c)** Carbon dioxide is no longer the limiting factor at level B, so adding more has little effect [1]

**10. (a)** Starch is insoluble [1]; so it does not affect the movement of water by osmosis (and can be stored in large amounts) [1]
**(b)** Making amino acids (and so proteins) [1]; because nitrate ions are needed with glucose to make amino acids [1]

**11. (a)** It is dark, so no photosynthesis takes place [1]; but the pondweed still respires, using oxygen [1]
**(b)** Light intensity is highest around midday, so the rate of photosynthesis is highest [1]
**(c)** At dawn, light is dim [1]; the rate of photosynthesis equals the rate of respiration, so oxygen made equals oxygen used [1]

**12.** One mark for each point:
- place pondweed in a boiling tube of water with sodium hydrogencarbonate (to supply CO₂) [1]
- place a lamp at a measured distance, for example 10 cm, and vary the distance (such as 10, 20, 30, 40 cm) [1]
- use a heat shield and thermometer to keep temperature constant [1]
- allow the pondweed to adjust for a few minutes at each new distance [1]
- collect the oxygen in a gas syringe (or count bubbles) for a fixed time and calculate rate per minute [1]
- repeat at each distance, calculate a mean, and plot rate against light intensity (or 1/d²) [1]
*Examiner insight:* A plan that never controls temperature cannot reach the top level, because the lamp is itself a heat source.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming the x-axis factor as limiting on a flat section.
- Using 1/d instead of 1/d².
- Weaknesses given without an improvement, or improvements given without the weakness.
- Forgetting that plants respire at all times, including at night.
- Making a recommendation without using the calculated gains.
