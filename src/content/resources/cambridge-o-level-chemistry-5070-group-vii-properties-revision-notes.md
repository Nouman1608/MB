Condensed recall for Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 subtopic **8.3 Group VII properties**. The full explanations and the worked predictions for fluorine and astatine are in the [Group VII properties study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-group-vii-properties/).

## What you must know

- Chlorine, bromine and iodine are **diatomic non-metals**: Cl₂, Br₂, I₂.
- Two trends down the group, and only these two are required: **density increases**, **reactivity decreases**.
- Appearance at r.t.p. (learn word for word).
- Displacement reactions with halide ions: describe **and** explain.
- Predict properties of other halogens from given information.

## Appearance at r.t.p.

| Halogen | Appearance | Solution colour (for displacement observations) |
|---|---|---|
| Chlorine | Pale yellow-green gas | Very pale yellow-green |
| Bromine | Red-brown liquid | Orange / yellow-orange |
| Iodine | Grey-black solid | Brown |

Gas → liquid → solid is the density trend in action. Colours darken down the group.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Halogen | A Group VII element, existing as X₂ molecules |
| Halide ion | The 1− ion formed by a halogen: Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻ |
| Diatomic | Molecules made of two atoms |
| Displacement | A more reactive halogen replaces a less reactive one in a compound |
| Spectator ion | An ion unchanged in a reaction, left out of the ionic equation (e.g. K⁺) |
| Oxidising agent | Substance that oxidises another and is itself reduced (the displacing halogen) |
| Reduction | Gain of electrons (halogen → halide ion) |
| Oxidation | Loss of electrons (halide ion → halogen) |

## Displacement grid

| Added ↓ / to → | Cl⁻ | Br⁻ | I⁻ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cl₂ | — | orange (Br₂) | brown (I₂) |
| Br₂ | ✗ | — | brown (I₂) |
| I₂ | ✗ | ✗ | — |

Rule: a halogen displaces any halide **below** it in the group, never one above.

## Ionic equations to learn

```
Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂
Cl₂ + 2I⁻  → 2Cl⁻ + I₂
Br₂ + 2I⁻  → 2Br⁻ + I₂
```

Half-equations for the first: Cl₂ + 2e⁻ → 2Cl⁻ (reduction); 2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻ (oxidation).

## Three-point explanation of a displacement

1. Chlorine is **more reactive** than bromine (higher in Group VII).
2. Chlorine **gains electrons** more readily, taking them from bromide ions.
3. **Bromine** is formed, so the solution turns **orange**.

## Why reactivity decreases

A halogen atom reacts by gaining one electron to fill its outer shell. Down the group, the outer shell is further from the nucleus and more shielded by inner shells, so the attraction for an extra electron is weaker. The atom gains an electron less readily, so reactivity falls. In Group I the same structural change makes the outer electron easier to **lose**, so reactivity rises: one cause, opposite effects, because one group loses electrons and the other gains them.

## Writing a displacement answer: worked mini example

*Bromine water is added to aqueous potassium iodide. Describe and explain what happens.*

- **Observation:** the solution turns from orange to brown.
- **Reason:** bromine is more reactive than iodine, so it displaces iodine.
- **Electrons:** bromine molecules gain electrons from iodide ions: Br₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Br⁻ + I₂.
- **Redox labels (if asked):** bromine is reduced and is the oxidising agent; iodide ions are oxidised.

Four short, distinct statements; each one is a separate marking point.

## Identifying an unknown halide with halogens

Displacement can identify which halide a solution contains:

- Turns brown with **bromine water** → iodide.
- No change with bromine water but orange with **chlorine water** → bromide.
- No change with either → chloride.

Test with bromine water first; chlorine water alone gives a colour with both bromide and iodide.

## Predicting other halogens

| Element | Position | Predicted state | Reactivity | Displaces |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fluorine | Above chlorine | Gas | Most reactive | Cl₂, Br₂, I₂ from their halides |
| Astatine | Below iodine | Solid | Least reactive | None of Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻ |

Keep the shared features: diatomic (F₂, At₂), forms 1− ions, non-metal.

## Exam traps

- **Chlorine** displaces bromine; **chloride** ions do nothing to bromide.
- Always write halogens as X₂; balance with **two** halide ions.
- Bromine solution is orange, not red-brown (that is the liquid element).
- Iodine solution is brown; grey-black is solid iodine.
- Reactivity **decreases** down Group VII (opposite of Group I).
- The halogen added is **reduced**; the halide ion is **oxidised**.
- "No reaction" is a valid observation: say "no colour change" rather than leaving it blank.
- Only increasing density and decreasing reactivity are named trends; other properties appear only as "given information".

## Self-test

1. Give the appearance of bromine at r.t.p.
2. State the trend in reactivity down Group VII.
3. What is meant by "diatomic"?
4. Chlorine water is added to potassium iodide solution. State the colour change.
5. Write the ionic equation for question 4.
6. Explain why iodine does not react with potassium bromide solution.
7. Name the spectator ion when chlorine reacts with potassium bromide.
8. In the reaction Br₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Br⁻ + I₂, which species is oxidised?
9. Predict the state of astatine at r.t.p. and give a reason.
10. Predict whether fluorine would displace bromine from sodium bromide solution.
11. Give the formula of the ion formed by astatine.
12. Which halogen in the grid displaces two others?

**Answers:** 1. Red-brown liquid. 2. Reactivity decreases down the group. 3. Each molecule contains two atoms. 4. Colourless (or very pale) to brown. 5. Cl₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + I₂. 6. Iodine is less reactive than bromine, so it cannot gain electrons from bromide ions. 7. Potassium ion, K⁺. 8. Iodide ions (I⁻): they lose electrons. 9. Solid; density increases (gas → liquid → solid) down the group and iodine above it is already a solid. 10. Yes; fluorine is more reactive than bromine. 11. At⁻. 12. Chlorine.
