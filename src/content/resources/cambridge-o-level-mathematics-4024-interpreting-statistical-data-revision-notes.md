These notes condense section 9.2, Interpreting statistical data, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. For model answers and longer examples, see the [interpreting statistical data study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-interpreting-statistical-data/); for exam-style practice, use the [interpreting statistical data practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-interpreting-statistical-data-practice/).

## The three outcomes

1. Read, interpret and draw inferences from tables and statistical diagrams.
2. Compare sets of data using tables, graphs and statistical measures (averages and spread).
3. Appreciate restrictions on drawing conclusions from given data.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Read | state a value shown in a table or diagram |
| Interpret | say what the values mean together |
| Inference | a cautious conclusion that goes beyond the figures |
| Average | a typical value: mean, median or mode |
| Spread | how varied the data is: range, or interquartile range |
| Consistent | having a small spread |
| Proportion | a fraction or percentage of a group's total |
| Sample | the group of items actually surveyed |
| Representative | a sample that reflects the whole group it is meant to describe |
| Truncated axis | an axis that does not start at zero |

## The comparison template

> "The [measure] for [group 1] ([value]) is [higher/lower] than for [group 2] ([value]), so on average [context statement]. The [spread measure] for [group 1] ([value]) is [smaller/larger] than for [group 2] ([value]), so [group 1] is [more/less] consistent."

Example: "The mean score for Class P (64) is higher than for Class Q (58), so on average Class P scored better. The range for Class P (30) is larger than for Class Q (18), so Class Q's scores were more consistent."

Checklist for every comparison:

- one average, one measure of spread;
- both values quoted each time;
- like compared with like (median with median);
- context words from the question;
- direction of "better" decided by the context (low times good, high scores good).

## Counts versus proportions

When groups have different totals, compare percentages.

| Group | Yes | Total | Proportion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Town X | 120 | 400 | 30% |
| Town Y | 90 | 200 | 45% |

Town X has more "yes" answers, but Town Y has a greater proportion.

## Reading diagrams safely

- Read the title, axes, units, scale and key first.
- Work out the value of one small square.
- Check whether the vertical axis starts at zero.
- In pictograms, check the value of one symbol and of part symbols.
- Pie charts show proportions, not totals: a larger share of a smaller total can be fewer people.

## Restrictions on conclusions

| Reason | Quick wording |
|---|---|
| Small sample | "only n people were asked, so it may not be reliable" |
| Unrepresentative sample | "people asked at [place] are more likely to [behaviour]" |
| Grouped data | "exact values are not known, so the mean is an estimate" |
| Average alone | "equal averages can hide different spreads" |
| Correlation | "a link between two variables does not show that one causes the other" |
| Beyond the data | "the data does not cover values that large or small" |
| Misleading chart | "the axis does not start at zero, so differences look bigger" |

## Exam traps

- Comparing raw counts between groups of different sizes.
- A comparison with values but no conclusion, or the reverse.
- Mean of one group compared with median of the other.
- "Better" used without saying what it means in the context.
- Overconfident words such as "proves".
- Generic reasons, such as "the data could be wrong", instead of specific ones.

## Self-test

1. What two measures should a full comparison of two data sets include?
2. Class P: mean 64, range 30. Class Q: mean 58, range 18. Write a full comparison.
3. Town X: 120 of 400 say yes. Town Y: 90 of 200 say yes. Which town has the greater proportion saying yes?
4. A bar chart's axis starts at 90. Bars show 92 and 96. Why is it misleading?
5. Running times: Team M median 52 s, Team N median 48 s. Which team was faster on average?
6. Give one reason why surveying 15 people outside a bookshop about reading habits may give a misleading result.
7. Why can the exact range not be found from a grouped frequency table?
8. Two pie charts are the same size. One shows 300 people and the other 60. The "yes" sectors look the same. Are the numbers saying yes the same?
9. Ice cream sales and sunburn cases both rise in summer. What restriction applies to claiming one causes the other?
10. What word is better than "proves" in a statistical inference?
11. Data covers adults aged 20 to 50. Why is a prediction for someone aged 80 unreliable?
12. Two groups have the same mean. What else should you compare?

### Answers

1. **An average** and **a measure of spread**.
2. "The mean for Class P (64) is higher than for Class Q (58), so on average P scored better. The range for P (30) is larger than for Q (18), so **Q's scores were more consistent**."
3. X: 30%; Y: 45%. **Town Y**.
4. The axis does not start at zero, so 96 looks **three times** 92 (6 against 2 above 90) when it is only about 4% higher.
5. **Team N** (lower median time).
6. The sample is **small**, and people near a bookshop are **more likely to read**, so it is not representative.
7. The **exact values** of the smallest and largest items are hidden in their classes.
8. **No**: the same angle means the same proportion, but 300 and 60 are different totals; the first chart's sector represents five times as many people.
9. **Correlation does not show cause**; both are linked to hot weather.
10. "**Suggests**" (or "indicates").
11. It is **outside the range** of the data collected.
12. The **spread** (range or interquartile range).
