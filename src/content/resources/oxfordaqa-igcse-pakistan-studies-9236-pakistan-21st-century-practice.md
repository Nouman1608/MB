This set covers topic 3.1.2, **Pakistan into the 21st Century: from independence to 2022**, the
compulsory topic examined in Section B of Paper 1. Questions are spread across all four key
developments in the specification: politics, culture and the political structure since
independence; political developments 1999–2022; international relations; and women's empowerment.
Tariffs follow the 1, 2, 4, 6 and 9-mark pattern of the board's specimen paper.

Study first with the [Pakistan into the 21st Century study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-pakistan-studies-pakistan-into-the-21st-century/)
and its [revision notes](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-pakistan-studies-pakistan-into-the-21st-century-revision-notes/).
A full timed Paper 1 is in the [Paper 1 practice paper](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-pakistan-studies-history-and-culture-practice/).

## Section A — short-answer questions

**1.** In which year was the constitution that is still in force in Pakistan adopted? **[1]**

A: 1956 &nbsp; B: 1962 &nbsp; C: 1973 &nbsp; D: 1985

**2.** Which constitution first declared Pakistan an Islamic Republic? **[1]**

A: 1956 &nbsp; B: 1962 &nbsp; C: 1973 &nbsp; D: the Objectives Resolution of 1949

**3.** Who founded the Pakistan Peoples Party in 1967? **[1]**

A: Ayub Khan &nbsp; B: Zulfikar Ali Bhutto &nbsp; C: Nawaz Sharif &nbsp; D: Imran Khan

**4.** In which year did Benazir Bhutto first become Prime Minister? **[1]**

A: 1977 &nbsp; B: 1985 &nbsp; C: 1988 &nbsp; D: 1999

**5.** State **two** features of the 1962 Constitution. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** ways in which the 1973 Constitution makes Pakistan a federation. **[2]**

**7.** State **two** steps taken by Benazir Bhutto's governments that aimed to support women. **[2]**

## Section B — extended questions

**8.** Identify and explain **two** challenges faced by Pervez Musharraf's government in 2007. **[4]**

**9.** Identify and explain **two** ways in which Pakistan has supported United Nations
missions. **[4]**

**10.** Analyse **two** reasons why relations between Pakistan and India remained tense between
2001 and 2022. **[6]**

**11.** Analyse **two** ways in which Pakistan's relations with Saudi Arabia and the United Arab
Emirates have benefited Pakistan. **[6]**

**12.** "The 2013 general election was the most important political development in Pakistan
between 1999 and 2022."

How far do you agree with this statement? Explain your answer using the statement above and your
contextual knowledge. **[9]**

**13.** "By 2022 women in Pakistan had gained equal opportunities with men."

How far do you agree with this statement? Explain your answer using the statement above and your
contextual knowledge. **[9]**

## Answers

Multiple-choice questions score 1 mark. "State" questions score 1 mark per valid point. "Identify
and explain" questions score 1 for each point and 1 for its explanation. The 6- and 9-mark questions
are marked by levels, set out after the answers. Credit other accurate points.

**1.** **C: 1973** [1]. It has been amended many times but remains the constitution. **[1]**

**2.** **A: 1956** [1]. The Objectives Resolution was a statement of principles, not a constitution.
**[1]**

**3.** **B: Zulfikar Ali Bhutto** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** **C: 1988** [1], after the elections that followed General Zia-ul-Haq's death. **[1]**

**5.** Two features, 1 mark each: it set up a presidential system in which the President held most
executive power [1]; the President and assemblies were elected indirectly through the Basic
Democrats, who formed an electoral college [1]. Also credit: a single-chamber National Assembly; the
President could veto legislation; it was introduced by Ayub Khan. **[2]**

**6.** Two ways, 1 mark each: there is a two-house parliament in which the Senate gives every
province equal representation [1]; each province has its own elected assembly and government with
its own areas of law-making [1]. Also credit: the Council of Common Interests settles disputes
between the federation and the provinces. **[2]**

**7.** Two steps, 1 mark each: the First Women Bank was set up to give women access to banking and
credit [1]; women's police stations were opened so that women could report crimes to female
officers [1]. Also credit: the Lady Health Workers programme, which trained women to provide basic
healthcare in their communities. **[2]**

**8.** Two challenges, each identified (1) and explained (1):
- the suspension of Chief Justice Iftikhar Muhammad Chaudhry in March 2007 [1] started the Lawyers'
  Movement, whose protests damaged Musharraf's authority and support [1];
- the armed stand-off at the Lal Masjid in Islamabad in July 2007 [1] ended in a military operation
  that was followed by a wave of militant attacks [1].

Also credit: the declaration of a state of emergency in November 2007, which was criticised at home
and abroad; the assassination of Benazir Bhutto in December 2007. **[4]**

**9.** Two ways, each identified (1) and explained (1):
- Pakistan has been one of the largest contributors of troops to UN peacekeeping [1], so its soldiers
  have served in missions in African countries such as Sierra Leone and the Democratic Republic of
  the Congo [1];
- Pakistani police officers and medical teams have also served with UN missions [1], helping to keep
  order and treat civilians in conflict zones [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- *Terrorist attacks:* attacks such as those on the Indian parliament in December 2001, in Mumbai in
  2008 and at Pulwama in 2019 were blamed by India on groups based in Pakistan; the 2001 attack led to
  a military stand-off on the border, and after Pulwama the two air forces clashed in February 2019.
- *Kashmir:* in August 2019 India revoked Article 370, ending the special status of the part of
  Kashmir it controls; Pakistan responded by downgrading diplomatic relations and suspending
  bilateral trade.
- *Diplomacy that did not last:* talks such as the Agra summit (2001) and the composite dialogue
  begun in 2004 stalled after each crisis, although the Kartarpur Corridor opened in 2019. **[6]**

**11.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- *Remittances:* millions of Pakistanis work in Saudi Arabia and the UAE, and the money they send
  home supports families and helps Pakistan pay for imports.
- *Financial support:* both countries have given Pakistan deposits, loans and deferred payment for
  oil during balance-of-payments crises, for example after 2018.
- *Religious and diplomatic links:* Saudi Arabia is home to Makkah and Madinah, so the relationship
  matters to Pakistani pilgrims; both states work with Pakistan in the Organisation of Islamic
  Cooperation.
- *Defence co-operation:* Pakistan has trained and advised Saudi forces. **[6]**

**12.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Indicative content:
- *Agree:* the 2013 election was the first time one elected civilian government completed its term
  and handed power to another through the ballot box, under the PML-N's Nawaz Sharif; it suggested
  democracy was becoming established after decades of interruption.
- *Disagree:* the 18th Amendment (2010) under Zardari did more to change how Pakistan is governed,
  restoring parliamentary government and giving the provinces more power; the end of Musharraf's
  rule in 2008 made civilian government possible in the first place; the 2018 election brought a new
  party, PTI, to power for the first time; Covid-19 after 2020 tested every institution.
- *Judgement:* for example, the 18th Amendment had the greater lasting effect, because the 2013
  transfer took place within the system it created. **[9]**

**13.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Indicative content:
- *Progress:* reserved seats for women in the National and provincial assemblies; Benazir Bhutto led
  two governments; laws such as the Protection of Women (Criminal Laws Amendment) Act (2006), the
  Protection against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act (2010) and the 2016 law against honour
  killings; Dr Sania Nishtar's Ehsaas programme gave payments and accounts to women; more girls in
  school than in earlier decades.
- *Limits:* female literacy and paid employment remain well below men's, especially in rural areas;
  laws are not always enforced; honour crimes and domestic violence continue; women are
  under-represented in senior jobs.
- *Judgement:* for example, the law gave women more protection by 2022, but opportunities in
  practice were not equal. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing up the constitutions: 1956 (Islamic Republic, parliamentary), 1962 (presidential, Basic
  Democrats), 1973 (federal parliamentary, still in force).
- Writing about the 18th Amendment when the question asks about the 19th.
- Answering a question on Saudi Arabia and the UAE with general points about "the Muslim world".
- On women's rights, listing laws without saying whether they changed lives in practice.
- Ending a 9-mark answer with "both are important" instead of a judgement.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need exact election results?**
No. Know who won and why the result mattered; avoid quoting seat totals you are unsure of.

**Which governments does this topic cover after 1999?**
Musharraf, the PPP government under President Zardari, the PML-N government from 2013 and the PTI
government from 2018 to 2022.
