(7404/7405), version 1.1, 1 December 2015, for AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards. The whole section is
**A-level only**. At A-level it is assessed on Paper 2 (organic chemistry), and Paper 3 can test any content.

## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by a chiral centre. **[1]**

**2.** State the meaning of the term *enantiomers*. **[2]**

**3.** The four alcohols below are isomers of C₅H₁₂O. Identify the one that has a chiral centre and give
the four groups attached to that carbon.

pentan-3-ol, CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH₃; 2-methylbutan-2-ol, CH₃CH₂C(OH)(CH₃)₂;
3-methylbutan-1-ol, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂OH; pentan-2-ol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₂CH₃ **[2]**

**4.** Draw 3D representations of the two enantiomers of butan-2-amine, CH₃CH(NH₂)CH₂CH₃. **[2]**

**5.** Describe how plane-polarised light could be used to show that a solution of a single enantiomer is
optically active and that a racemic mixture of the same compound is not. **[4]**

**6.** Explain why a racemic mixture of 2-bromobutane is optically inactive. **[2]**

**7.** Butanone, CH₃COCH₂CH₃, reacts with KCN followed by dilute acid.

**(a)** Name the organic product. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the product has optical isomers. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the product is formed as a racemic mixture. **[3]**
**(d)** A student starts with 1.44 g of butanone. Calculate the maximum mass of product that could form, and
the mass of each enantiomer in it. **[3]**

**8.** There are eight structural isomers with the formula C₅H₁₁Cl.

**(a)** Three of them have a chiral centre. Name these three. **[3]**
**(b)** State the total number of isomers of C₅H₁₁Cl, including stereoisomers. **[1]**

**9.** Hydrogen bromide reacts with propene and with but-1-ene by electrophilic addition.

**(a)** Name the major product from propene and explain why it has no optical isomers. **[2]**
**(b)** The major product from but-1-ene is 2-bromobutane. Explain why it forms as a racemic mixture. **[3]**

**10.** (A-level only, 3.3.13.3) A drug is made as a racemic mixture, but only one enantiomer acts on the
target enzyme. Explain why. **[3]**

**11.** Compound P is pent-3-en-2-ol, CH₃CH=CHCH(OH)CH₃.

**(a)** Identify the chiral centre in P and give the four groups attached to it. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why P also shows E–Z isomerism. **[2]**
**(c)** Deduce the number of stereoisomers of P. Explain your answer. **[2]**
**(d)** A student says that 3-methylpent-1-ene, CH₂=CHCH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃, also has four stereoisomers. Evaluate
this statement. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A carbon atom bonded to **four different atoms or groups** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* "Four different groups" with no mention of carbon (or "asymmetric carbon") is vague; name the carbon atom.

**2.** Stereoisomers: same structural formula, different arrangement in space [1]. They are
**non-superimposable mirror images** of each other [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Mirror images" alone is not enough for the second mark; every molecule has a mirror image, so write "non-superimposable".

**3.** **Pentan-2-ol** [1]. C2 carries **H, OH, CH₃ and CH₂CH₂CH₃** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Pentan-3-ol looks similar; its C3 carries two identical ethyl groups, so choosing it scores nothing here.

**4.** C2 is chiral (H, NH₂, CH₃, C₂H₅). A correct 3D shape for one enantiomer, with two bonds in the plane, one
wedge and one hashed bond [1]; the second drawn as its mirror image [1] **[2]**

```
            CH3           mirror           CH3
             |              :               |
             C              :               C
           / ⋮ ▼            :             ▼ ⋮ \
       C2H5  H  NH2         :         H2N  H  C2H5
```

*Examiner insight:* Two flat drawings with all bonds as plain lines show no 3D arrangement and cannot show the two forms are different.

**5.** Pass plane-polarised light (light through a polarising filter) through the solution [1]. Use a second
polarising filter after the sample and compare its angle with the angle for the pure solvent [1]. The single
enantiomer **rotates the plane** of the polarised light, so the angle changes [1]. The racemic mixture gives
**no rotation**, the same as the solvent [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Comparing with the pure solvent is the control; without it, "the light is rotated" has nothing to be measured against.

**6.** Each enantiomer rotates the plane of plane-polarised light by the same angle in **opposite directions** [1].
There are **equal amounts** of each, so the rotations cancel [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Saying the mixture "has no chiral centre" is wrong; each molecule is chiral, and the answer must be about cancelling rotations.

**7. (a)** **2-hydroxy-2-methylbutanenitrile**, CH₃CH₂C(OH)(CH₃)CN [1]
**(b)** C2 carries four different groups, OH, CN, CH₃ and C₂H₅, so it is a chiral centre [1]
**(c)** The C=O group is **planar** (trigonal planar) [1]. CN⁻ can attack the carbonyl carbon from **either
side** of the plane [1]. Attack from each side is **equally likely**, so equal amounts of the two enantiomers
form [1]
**(d)** n(butanone) = 1.44 / 72.0 = 0.0200 mol [1]. Mr of product (C₅H₉NO) = 99.0, so maximum mass =
0.0200 × 99.0 = **1.98 g** [1]. Each enantiomer = 1.98 / 2 = **0.990 g** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In (d), show the Mr and the mole calculation; with clear working, an earlier slip can still earn the later method marks as an error carried forward.

**8. (a)** **2-chloropentane** [1]; **1-chloro-2-methylbutane** [1]; **2-chloro-3-methylbutane** [1]
**(b)** 8 structural isomers + 3 extra enantiomers = **11** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* 3-chloropentane is the common wrong choice in (a): its C3 carries two ethyl groups.

**9. (a)** **2-bromopropane** [1]. Its C2 carries two CH₃ groups, so no carbon has four different groups [1]
**(b)** The secondary carbocation, CH₃CH₂C⁺HCH₃, is **planar** around the positive carbon [1]. Br⁻ can attack
from **either side** of the plane with equal probability [1]. So **equal amounts** of the two enantiomers form [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Both "planar" and "equal probability" must appear in (b); "Br⁻ attacks from both sides" alone does not explain why the amounts are equal.

**10.** The active site of the enzyme is **stereospecific**, with a particular 3D shape [1]. Only one enantiomer
has its groups arranged so that it fits and bonds to the active site [1]. The mirror-image enantiomer cannot
bind in the same way, so only half of the racemic mixture is active [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "The other enantiomer has a different shape" is not enough; link the mirror-image arrangement to the fit with the active site.

**11. (a)** **C2**, the carbon bonded to OH [1]. Its groups are **H, OH, CH₃ and CH=CHCH₃** [1]
**(b)** Rotation about the C=C bond is restricted [1]. Each carbon of the C=C carries two different groups (C3:
H and CH(OH)CH₃; C4: H and CH₃) [1]
**(c)** There are two E–Z forms, and each has the chiral centre, so each exists as two enantiomers [1].
2 × 2 = **4 stereoisomers** [1]
**(d)** 3-methylpent-1-ene has a chiral centre at C3 (H, CH₃, C₂H₅, CH=CH₂), so it has two enantiomers [1]. C1
of the C=C carries two H atoms, so there is no E–Z isomerism; it has **2 stereoisomers** and the student is
wrong [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c) and (d) the reasoning carries its own mark here, so deal with E–Z and optical isomerism separately rather than writing a bare number.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "mirror images" without "non-superimposable".
- Missing "equal amounts" in a racemate definition, or "equal and opposite" in explaining optical inactivity.
- Choosing a carbon with two identical groups (such as C3 in pentan-3-ol or 3-chloropentane).
- Drawing enantiomers flat, or drawing the "mirror image" with a swapped group as well, which gives the same
  molecule twice.
- Leaving out "planar" or "equal probability of attack from either side" when explaining racemate formation.
- Calling the product of propanone and KCN chiral; its central carbon carries two CH₃ groups.
- Not showing Mr and moles in a calculation, so an error cannot carry forward.
- Counting stereoisomers without checking each type separately (E–Z and optical).
