These questions follow the OCR GCSE (9-1) Gateway Science Physics A (J249) specification, Version 5.0 (July 2026), topic P5.3 Wave interactions. Questions 1, 2, 3 and 12 cover statements on both tiers. Questions marked (Higher Tier only) cover P5.3c, P5.3d or P5.3e. Where needed, use a speed of light in air of 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s. ## Questions

**1.** State three things that can happen to an electromagnetic wave when it meets a boundary between two substances. **[3]**

**2.** A red laser emits light of wavelength 633 nm in air. In a clear plastic the wavelength is 422 nm.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency of the light in air. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the same frequency is used to find the speed in the plastic, then calculate that speed. **[3]**

**3.** Explain, in terms of wave speed, why a ray of light bends towards the normal when it passes from air into glass at an angle. **[5]**

**4.** (Higher Tier only) A ray of light hits a plane mirror at an angle of incidence of 35°.

**(a)** State the angle of reflection and the angle between the reflected ray and the mirror surface. **[2]**
**(b)** A ray enters a rectangular glass block from air at an angle to the normal. Describe how to draw its path through the block and out of the other side. **[3]**

**5.** (Higher Tier only) An object stands on the axis of a convex lens, further from the lens than twice the focal length. Describe how to draw the image of the top of the object, and state the nature of the image. **[4]**

**6.** (Higher Tier only) A convex lens is used as a magnifying glass. Explain how it forms an image of a small object and state what kind of image it is. **[3]**

**7.** (Higher Tier only) Compare convex and concave lenses by stating one similarity and three differences in how they affect light. **[4]**

**8.** (Higher Tier only) A short-sighted person sees distant objects as blurred because the rays focus in front of the retina. Explain which type of lens corrects this. **[3]**

**9.** (Higher Tier only) A red book is lit by white light.

**(a)** Explain why it looks red. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what you would see if it were lit by green light only. **[2]**

**10.** (Higher Tier only) White light passes through a magenta filter, then through a green filter. Describe and explain what emerges. **[4]**

**11.** (Higher Tier only) You can read a page of white paper from many positions, but you only see a clear image of a window in a mirror from certain positions. Explain the difference. **[5]**

**12.** A pulse of light crosses a glass slab of thickness 0.60 m. Its speed in the glass is 2.0 × 10⁸ m/s. In the glass its wavelength is 350 nm.

**(a)** Calculate the time taken to cross the slab. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how much longer this is than the time to travel 0.60 m in air. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the frequency of the light. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the wavelength of the light in air. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three from: absorbed [1]; transmitted [1]; reflected [1]; refracted (not counted beyond three). **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Three separate, correct terms earn credit. Repeating one idea in different words, such as "bounced" and "reflected", does not give a second point.

**2. (a)** f = v ÷ λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 633 × 10⁻⁹ [1] = **4.74 × 10¹⁴ Hz** [1]
**(b)** Frequency is fixed by the source and does not change when light enters a new substance [1]. v = f λ = 4.74 × 10¹⁴ × 422 × 10⁻⁹ [1] = **2.00 × 10⁸ m/s** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that convert nanometres to metres at the start are easier to follow and easier to credit. An answer out by a power of ten shows the conversion step was missed.

**3.** Light travels more slowly in glass than in air [1]. The frequency stays the same, so the wavelength is shorter in the glass [1]. When the wave meets the boundary at an angle, one end of each wavefront enters the glass first [1] and slows down while the other end is still moving faster in the air [1]. This turns the wavefront, so the ray bends towards the normal [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong answers link the change of direction to the change of speed. Saying only "glass is denser" gives a reason without the mechanism the question asks for.

**4. (a)** The angle of reflection is **35°** [1]. The angle to the mirror surface is 90° − 35° = **55°** [1]
**(b)** On entering the block the ray bends towards the normal [1]. On leaving the other side it bends away from the normal [1]. The emerging ray is parallel to the original ray but shifted sideways [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Angles in reflection are measured from the normal. A diagram or description that uses the surface as the reference line is likely to lose the angle point.

**5.** Draw a ray from the top of the object parallel to the axis, bending through the principal focus on the far side [1]. Draw a second ray from the top through the centre of the lens, going straight on [1]. The point where the two rays cross is the top of the image [1]. The image is real, inverted and smaller, between F and 2F on the far side [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use a ruler and put arrows on rays. A description of the image needs at least three properties: real or virtual, upright or inverted, and size.

**6.** The object is closer to the lens than the principal focus [1]. The rays leaving the lens spread out, so they do not meet, but trace back to appear to come from a point behind the object [1]. The image is virtual, upright and larger [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the rays only appear to come from the image. A response that says the rays "meet" describes a real image instead.

**7.** Similarity: both refract light at their two surfaces (or both have a principal focus) [1]. Differences: a convex lens brings parallel rays together whereas a concave lens spreads them apart [1]; a convex lens can form a real image on a screen whereas a concave lens cannot [1]; a convex lens is thicker in the middle whereas a concave lens is thinner in the middle [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both lenses in each statement. Writing "convex converges" alone does not state the contrast.

**8.** A concave lens [1]. The eye focuses the rays too soon, in front of the retina, so the rays need to be spread out slightly before they enter the eye [1]. The concave lens diverges the rays, so the focus moves back onto the retina [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the correct lens is not enough. The explanation needs to say where the image forms and how the lens changes that.

**9. (a)** White light contains all the visible colours [1]. The book reflects red light and absorbs the other colours, so only red reaches the eye [1]
**(b)** Green light contains no red to reflect [1]. The book absorbs the green light, so almost no light reaches the eye and it looks black [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say what the object absorbs as well as what it reflects. "It reflects red" alone is incomplete as an explanation of what happens under different light.

**10.** Magenta transmits red and blue and absorbs green [1]. The green filter only transmits green light [1]. No green light reaches the second filter because the first absorbed it [1]. No light emerges, so it looks black [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Treat each filter in turn and list what is left after each one. Answers that say the filters "mix" their colours miss that filters subtract.

**11.** Paper has a rough surface [1] which scatters light in many directions [1]. A mirror has a smooth surface [1] and gives specular reflection, where parallel rays stay parallel [1]. The reflected beam only reaches the eye from certain positions, so the image can only be seen from those positions [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The key term pair is rough versus smooth. Mention what happens to the reflected rays, not only the name of the process.

**12. (a)** t = d ÷ v = 0.60 ÷ 2.0 × 10⁸ [1] = **3.0 × 10⁻⁹ s** [1]
**(b)** Time in air = 0.60 ÷ 3.00 × 10⁸ = 2.0 × 10⁻⁹ s [1]. Difference = 3.0 × 10⁻⁹ − 2.0 × 10⁻⁹ = **1.0 × 10⁻⁹ s** [1]
**(c)** f = v ÷ λ = 2.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 350 × 10⁻⁹ [1] = **5.7 × 10¹⁴ Hz** [1]
**(d)** λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 5.71 × 10¹⁴ [1] = **5.25 × 10⁻⁷ m (525 nm)** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In a chain of calculations, work with unrounded values from earlier parts and round at the end. Show the substitution in each part so a slip can be traced.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking angles from the mirror or block surface rather than from the normal.
- Saying frequency changes at a boundary when only the speed and wavelength change.
- Writing the cause of refraction as "density" with no mention of wave speed.
- Leaving wavelengths in nanometres in v = f λ.
- Describing a virtual image as one where the rays "meet".
- Stating which lens corrects a fault without explaining where the image forms.
- Saying a filter "makes" light a colour rather than absorbing the other colours.
- Explaining an object's colour from reflection only, without saying what it absorbs.
