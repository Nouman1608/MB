## Section A: short answers

**1.** Explain why the 1953 coup against Mosaddeq mattered for the Shah's later reputation. **[3]**

**2.** Identify two foreign powers that intervened militarily in Lebanon between 1975 and 1990. **[2]**

**3.** State two reasons for young Egyptians' discontent before 2011. **[2]**

## Section B: source and argument

*Constructed for practice, not a real source:* "In 1978 the Shah did not lose his throne because the people had a leader. He lost it because the army would not obey, and the people had found out that it would not." (a historian's view, paraphrased)

**4.** Explain the argument made in this extract about the fall of the Shah. **[3]**

**5.** Explain one way in which a historian might challenge the extract. **[3]**

**6.** Examine the impact of the Iran-Iraq War on the economies of the two countries. **[6]**

## Section C: essay questions

**7.** To what extent did Nasser's rule transform Egyptian society? **[15]**

**8.** Evaluate the view that the Lebanese Civil War was caused mainly by external involvement. **[15]**

## Answers

**1.** One mark for the event (August 1953, the Shah restored after the removal of Prime Minister Mosaddeq, with British and American help). One for the effect on the Shah's image: he was seen as dependent on foreign powers. One for the later significance: memory of 1953 fed hostility towards the USA and the monarchy in 1978 to 1979. **[3]**

**2.** One mark each for two of: Syria (from 1976); Israel (1978 and 1982); the United States and France (the multinational force of 1982 to 1984); the UN (UNIFIL from 1978). Accept Iran for helping to form Hezbollah, with an explanation. **[2]**

**3.** One mark each for two of: high unemployment among educated young people; rising food prices and inequality; police brutality and the emergency law; lack of political freedom; corruption among well-connected businessmen. **[2]**

**4.** One mark for the claim that the army's refusal to defend the regime was decisive; one for the contrast with the claim that the opposition's leadership was the key; one for the implication that the revolution succeeded when the regime's coercive power failed. **[3]**

**5.** One mark for a challenge, up to two for development. For example: the extract ignores Khomeini's role and the organisation of the opposition through mosques, the bazaar and strikes, including in the oil industry; the army's behaviour itself resulted from mass protest and from the Shah's loss of will, so the two are linked; evidence from 1978 shows many soldiers did obey until late in the year. **[3]**

**6.** Up to 6 marks. Iran: war costs, damage to the oil industry and cities, sanctions and a long period of reconstruction. Iraq: huge debts to Gulf states and Western lenders, destroyed infrastructure, a military economy, and the pressure that contributed to the invasion of Kuwait in 1990. Both lost large numbers of workers, and both spent on arms. Credit comparison: Iraq's debt was a particular problem, while Iran used the war to rally support. Level guide: 1 to 2 for description, 3 to 4 for partial examination, 5 to 6 for balanced examination with a conclusion. **[6]**

**7.** Marking points, up to 15 in total:
- up to 3 for understanding "transform" and setting criteria (land, education, class structure, women, culture, the economy);
- up to 4 for evidence of change (land reform of 1952, free education, expansion of universities, women's vote in 1956, industrialisation, the High Dam, mass media);
- up to 4 for limits and continuity (authoritarian state, limited effect of land reform on the poorest, population growth outpacing jobs, defeat of 1967, state control of culture);
- up to 4 for evaluation of perspectives (Nasserist, critical, and balanced accounts) and a judgement. **[15]**

**8.** Marking points, up to 15 in total:
- up to 3 for understanding the claim and the alternative causes;
- up to 4 for external factors (PLO relocating to Lebanon, Syrian and Israeli intervention, the Cold War, the Iranian revolution and Hezbollah);
- up to 4 for internal factors (confessional politics under the 1943 National Pact, inequality between communities, militias, weakness of the state, demographic change);
- up to 4 for evaluation and judgement, for example that internal weaknesses made the country vulnerable and external actors turned a crisis into a long war. **[15]**

## Model paragraph and feedback

Here is a model paragraph for Question 7, followed by points on how to improve a draft.

*Model paragraph:* "Nasser's supporters argue that he gave Egypt dignity and social progress, while critics point to authoritarian rule and the defeat of 1967. Among the changes that affected society were the land reform of 1952, the expansion of free education and the new opportunities for women after 1956. These transformed the prospects of many families. However, the redistribution of land was limited, the population grew rapidly, and the state kept tight control of politics. This essay argues that Nasser's rule transformed Egyptian society in education and in the sense of national identity, but changed the structure of the economy and politics less deeply than it claimed."

*Feedback points:*

- The paragraph sets up both sides and then states a judgement.
- Use specific dates and laws for each claim of change.
- Include continuity in any "transform" question.
- Avoid describing Nasser's foreign policy in a social question unless you link it directly to the society.
