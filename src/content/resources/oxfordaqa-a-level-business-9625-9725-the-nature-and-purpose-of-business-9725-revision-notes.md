Condensed notes on section **3.1.1.1 The nature and purpose of business** of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Business (9725), Version 1.1. This is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1 Business and markets. On 9625? Revise from that specification. The full teaching and a worked business plan are in the [nature and purpose study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-the-nature-and-purpose-of-business-9725/); exam-style questions are in the [nature and purpose practice set](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-the-nature-and-purpose-of-business-9725-practice/).

## The three headings

1. **Entrepreneurs**: motivations, characteristics, challenges, changing role.
2. **Business objectives**: survival, profit, growth, cash flow, social; SMART; value of objectives.
3. **Business plans**: key elements; benefits and limitations; competitiveness.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Entrepreneur | Someone who spots an opportunity and takes the risk of starting a business to exploit it |
| Financial motivation | Starting a business for money: income, wealth, a saleable asset |
| Non-financial motivation | Starting a business for other reasons: independence, interest, flexibility, social aims |
| Business objective | A target the business aims to achieve |
| SMART | Specific, Measurable, Accountable, Realistic, Time specific |
| Business plan | Document setting out what a business intends to do, how, and the results expected |
| Target market | The group of customers the business aims to sell to |
| Competitor analysis | Study of rivals' strengths, weaknesses and offers |
| Unique selling point (USP) | A feature that makes the business's product different from rivals' |
| Financial analysis | Start-up costs and forecasts of sales, costs, profit and cash flow |
| Competitiveness | Ability to attract and keep customers against rivals |

## Entrepreneurs

**Motivations:** financial (higher income, building wealth, few local jobs) and non-financial (independence, interest, flexibility, social aims). The motive predicts later decisions.

**The seven 9725 characteristics:** risk taker; resilient; hard working; creative; passionate; innovative; adaptable.

- Creative = having ideas. Innovative = making ideas work in the market.
- Strengths can become weaknesses: too much risk-taking, or refusing to change course.

**Challenges:** raising finance without a record or security; winning customers; cash shortages before sales arrive; skill gaps; uncertainty and stress.

**Changing role:** start-up (does everything) → early growth (recruits, delegates) → established (leads, sets strategy) → mature (professional managers, steps back or sells).

## Objectives

| Objective | Typical when |
|---|---|
| Survival | New business; crisis; recession |
| Profit | Established business; owners expect return |
| Growth | Expanding market; ambitious owners; finance available |
| Cash flow | Tight cash; rapid expansion; seasonal trade |
| Social | Social enterprise; owners' values; customer expectations |

**SMART check:** what exactly (S)? what number (M)? who is responsible (A)? can it be done (R)? by when (T)?

**Value:** focus and coordination; measuring and judging performance; accountability; basis for decisions. **Reduced when:** unrealistic, ignored or out of date.

## Business plans

**Six key elements:** ownership; objectives; target market and market analysis; competitor analysis; USP; financial analysis.

| Benefits | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Forces research before money is committed | Forecasts rest on assumptions |
| Needed to obtain finance | Goes out of date as conditions change |
| Provides targets to compare results with | Time-consuming; may need paid advice |
| Communicates the idea to partners and staff | Can create false confidence |

**Judging a plan:** is the market research large enough? Is the USP really different from rivals? Are the sales forecasts realistic, and is there a cash-flow forecast for lower sales?

## Competitiveness

- **Price** competitiveness: low costs allow low prices.
- **Non-price** competitiveness: quality, design, reliability, service, brand, convenience.
- **Significance:** market share, margins, growth and survival.
- It is **relative**: rivals improving can reduce it.
- The USP is the plan's claim about where competitiveness will come from.

## Exam traps

- 9725 includes **hard working** and **creative** among the characteristics.
- The A in SMART is **Accountable**.
- Name the plan element and judge its quality, not just describe it.
- Competitiveness is not only about price.
- A profitable business can still fail through poor cash flow.

## Self-test

1. Give two financial and two non-financial motivations for starting a business.
2. List the seven characteristics of an entrepreneur in 9725.
3. Distinguish between being creative and being innovative.
4. Give three challenges of starting a business.
5. How does an entrepreneur's role change once a business has 50 staff?
6. Name the five typical business objectives in 9725.
7. Why might survival be the main objective in the first year?
8. Is this SMART? "Increase sales a lot soon." Rewrite it.
9. List the six key elements of a business plan.
10. Give two benefits and two limitations of a business plan.
11. Define unique selling point.
12. Why is competitiveness described as relative?

### Answers

1. Financial: any two of higher income, building wealth, creating a saleable asset, lack of local jobs. Non-financial: any two of independence, interest, flexibility, social aims.
2. Risk taker, resilient, hard working, creative, passionate, innovative, adaptable.
3. Creative means generating ideas; innovative means turning ideas into products or processes that succeed in the market.
4. Any three: raising finance; winning customers; cash shortages; skill gaps; uncertainty and stress.
5. The entrepreneur delegates daily tasks to managers and concentrates on leading, setting objectives and strategy.
6. Survival, profit, growth, cash flow, social.
7. The business has no established customers, cash is tight and failure rates are high, so continuing to trade comes first.
8. No: it is not specific, measurable, accountable or time specific. For example: "The sales manager will increase monthly sales from £20,000 to £24,000 by 30 June."
9. Ownership; objectives; target market and market analysis; competitor analysis; USP; financial analysis.
10. Benefits: any two of forcing research, obtaining finance, providing targets, communicating the idea. Limitations: any two of uncertain forecasts, going out of date, time and cost, false confidence.
11. A feature of a product or business that makes it different from rivals and gives customers a reason to choose it.
12. It depends on how the business compares with rivals, so it can fall if rivals improve even when the business does not change.
