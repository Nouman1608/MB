Cambridge IGCSE Geography (0460) is a course in human and physical geography for students in the middle years of secondary school, built around three broad themes and a strong strand of practical geographical skills. Every candidate sits Paper 1, Geographical Themes (1 hour 45 minutes, 75 raw marks weighted to 100, 45%), and Paper 2, Geographical Skills (1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks, 27.5%). The remaining 27.5% comes from either Component 3, Coursework, or Paper 4, Alternative to Coursework, depending on the route your school enters you for. This page explains how those parts are organised and how to plan a route through them; it is an overview of the whole course rather than a guide to any single topic.

## How the course is organised

The content of 0460 sits in three themes. Each one mixes ideas, processes and case studies, and the themes are deliberately broad so that the same skills can be practised on very different material.

**Theme 1 – Population and Settlement** looks at people: Population Dynamics, Migration, Population Structure, Population Density and Distribution, Settlements (Rural and Urban) and Service Provision, Urban Settlements, and Urbanisation. It moves from how populations change in size and make-up to where people live and how towns and cities grow.

**Theme 2 – The Natural Environment** is the physical geography of the course: Earthquakes and Volcanoes, Rivers, Coasts, Weather, and Climate and Natural Vegetation. Much of this theme is about processes and the landforms or patterns they produce, together with how people respond to hazards.

**Theme 3 – Economic Development** connects people and environment through Development, Food Production, Industry, Tourism, Energy, Water, and Environmental Risks of Economic Development. It is the theme where human choices and physical limits meet most directly, and its final topic draws on ideas from the rest of the theme.

## How it's assessed

The syllabus is component-based and not tiered, so every candidate faces the same papers. Two components are compulsory; for the third, there is a choice between coursework and a written alternative.

| Component | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Assessed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Geographical Themes | 1 hour 45 minutes | 75 (weighted to 100) | 45% | Externally |
| Paper 2 | Geographical Skills | 1 hour 30 minutes | 60 | 27.5% | Externally |
| Component 3 | Coursework | n/a | 60 | 27.5% | Internally, externally moderated |
| Paper 4 | Alternative to Coursework | 1 hour 30 minutes | 60 | 27.5% | Externally |

Candidates take Component 3 **or** Paper 4, never both. Component 3 is a centre-based task of one assignment of up to 2000 words, marked by your teachers and then moderated by Cambridge. Paper 4 is a written examination marked by Cambridge. Because the choice is usually made by the school rather than the individual, it is worth asking early which one applies to you. The syllabus record covers the 2025-2026 examination window, with June and November series, and was first examined in 2025.

## How the parts fit together

Paper 1 is where the three themes are examined most directly, so it rewards secure knowledge of processes, explanations and case studies across all of them. Paper 2 is built around skills: reading maps, interpreting graphs and data, and drawing conclusions from unfamiliar resources. Those skills are not a separate body of content. They grow out of the themes, which is why a river long profile, a population pyramid or a climate graph can turn up just as easily in skills practice as in theme revision.

The third component turns geography into an investigation. Whether you complete the coursework or sit the Alternative to Coursework, you need to understand how geographers plan a study, collect data, present it and judge how reliable their conclusions are. Fieldwork-style thinking therefore links all three parts: the processes from the themes give you something to investigate, and the skills from Paper 2 give you the tools to present and interpret what you find.

Some topics also connect across themes. Urbanisation in Theme 1 leads naturally into Water and Energy in Theme 3; Weather and Climate and Natural Vegetation in Theme 2 provide the physical background to Food Production and Tourism. Keeping a running list of these links helps when a question asks you to explain an issue that has both human and physical causes.

## Planning your study

A sensible order is to start with Theme 1 or Theme 2, whichever your school teaches first, and to bring skills work in from the first weeks rather than saving it for the end. Each time you meet a new type of graph or map, practise reading and describing it while the topic is fresh.

In general terms, the first part of the course can cover Population and Settlement and most of The Natural Environment, with case studies collected as you go. The later part can complete the physical topics and work through Economic Development, finishing with Environmental Risks of Economic Development, which draws the theme together. If you are taking the coursework route, the assignment will fit into your school's own timetable; keep its demands in mind so that it does not squeeze revision of the themes. If you are taking Paper 4, build regular practice on investigation-style questions throughout the year.

Start using exam-style questions on each theme as soon as you finish it, and move to full past papers in the final months. For the paper-by-paper detail of how to approach each examination, use the dedicated exam preparation page rather than this overview.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers do you sit for Cambridge IGCSE Geography 0460?**
Every candidate takes Paper 1 Geographical Themes and Paper 2 Geographical Skills, plus either Component 3 Coursework or Paper 4 Alternative to Coursework. That makes three assessed components in total.

**Is Cambridge IGCSE Geography 0460 tiered?**
No. The syllabus is not tiered, so all candidates sit the same papers.

**What is the difference between Component 3 and Paper 4?**
Component 3 is a centre-based coursework assignment of up to 2000 words, worth 60 marks and 27.5%, marked by your teachers and moderated by Cambridge. Paper 4 is a 1 hour 30 minute written examination, also 60 marks and 27.5%, marked by Cambridge.

**Why is Paper 1 out of 75 marks but worth 45%?**
Cambridge states that Paper 1's 75 raw marks are weighted to 100, and the paper carries 45% of the final result. The two remaining components each carry 27.5%.

**What are the three themes in 0460?**
Theme 1 is Population and Settlement, Theme 2 is The Natural Environment and Theme 3 is Economic Development.
