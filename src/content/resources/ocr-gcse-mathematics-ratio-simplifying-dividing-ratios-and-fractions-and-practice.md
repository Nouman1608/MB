## Questions

**1.** Equivalent ratios.

**(a)** Simplify 36 : 84. **[1]**
**(b)** Simplify 1.5 km : 600 m. **[2]**
**(c)** Write 20 : 8 in the form 1 : n. **[2]**

**2.** Share £720 between three people in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4. **[3]**

**3.** The ratio of tulips to daffodils in a garden bed is 4 : 9.

**(a)** There are 27 daffodils. How many tulips are there? **[2]**
**(b)** In a second bed with the same ratio there are 20 tulips. How many daffodils are there? **[2]**

**4.** Maya and Ravi share some money in the ratio 9 : 4. Maya receives £65 more than Ravi. Work out how much each person receives. **[4]**

**5.** In a hall the ratio of adults to children is 7 : 5.

**(a)** What fraction of the people in the hall are children? **[1]**
**(b)** There are 180 people in the hall. How many are children? **[2]**

**6.** A fruit drink contains 450 ml of orange juice, 0.3 litres of pineapple juice and 750 ml of apple juice. Write the ratio orange : pineapple : apple in its simplest form. **[3]**

**7.** A recipe makes 12 flapjacks using 300 g of oats, 200 g of butter and 150 g of syrup.

**(a)** Work out the amount of each ingredient needed to make 18 flapjacks. **[3]**
**(b)** Only 500 g of butter is available. What is the greatest number of flapjacks that can be made, if the other ingredients are not limited? **[2]**

**8.** Concentrate and water are mixed in the ratio 1 : 7. Let x be the litres of concentrate and y the litres of water.

**(a)** Write y in terms of x. **[1]**
**(b)** Find y when x = 2.5. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the graph of y against x passes through the point (0, 0). **[1]**
**(d)** A hire charge is C = 4t + 10 pounds for t hours. Show that C and t are not always in the same ratio. **[2]**

**9.** Sam, Tia and Uma share £1,260. Sam receives 2/7 of the total. The rest is shared between Tia and Uma in the ratio 3 : 2.

**(a)** Work out how much each person receives. **[4]**
**(b)** Write the ratio of Sam's share to Tia's share to Uma's share in its simplest form. **[2]**
**(c)** The same sharing is used for a total of £1,575. How much does Tia receive? **[2]**

**10.** A bag holds red and blue counters in the ratio 5 : 3. After 12 more blue counters are put in, the ratio of red to blue is 5 : 6.

**(a)** Work out how many red and blue counters were in the bag at the start. **[5]**
**(b)** What fraction of the counters in the bag at the end are blue? **[2]**

**11.** Mix A is blue and white paint in the ratio 2 : 3. Mix B is blue and white paint in the ratio 3 : 7. A decorator combines 5 litres of mix A with 20 litres of mix B.

**(a)** Find the ratio of blue paint to white paint in the combined mixture. Give your answer in its simplest form. **[5]**
**(b)** What fraction of the combined mixture is blue paint? **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** HCF of 36 and 84 is 12, so 36 : 84 = **3 : 7** [1]
**(b)** Same unit: 1.5 km = 1500 m, giving 1500 : 600 [1]. Divide by 300 to get **5 : 2** [1]
**(c)** 8 ÷ 20 = 0.4 [1], so the ratio is **1 : 0.4** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Converting both quantities to the same unit is the step that matters in part (b), so write it down. In part (c), n is the second number divided by the first, and a decimal is an acceptable form for n.

**2.** Total parts 2 + 3 + 4 = 9 [1]. One part is 720 ÷ 9 = 80 [1]. Shares are **£160, £240 and £320** [1] (check 160 + 240 + 320 = 720). **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* State the value of one part clearly. A check that the shares sum to the total is quick and catches most slips.

**3. (a)** 27 daffodils is 9 parts, so one part is 3 [1]. Tulips: 4 × 3 = **12** [1]
**(b)** 20 tulips is 4 parts, so one part is 5 [1]. Daffodils: 9 × 5 = **45** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Match each given number to the correct side of the ratio. Tulips come first in the ratio, so 20 goes with the 4.

**4.** The difference is 9 - 4 = 5 parts [1]. 5 parts = £65, so one part is £13 [1]. Maya receives 9 × 13 = **£117** [1]. Ravi receives 4 × 13 = **£52** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The £65 is a difference, not a total, so it equals 5 parts rather than 13 parts. Checking that 117 - 52 = 65 confirms the answer.

**5. (a)** There are 7 + 5 = 12 parts, so children are **5/12** of the people. [1]
**(b)** One part is 180 ÷ 12 = 15 [1]. Children: 5 × 15 = **75** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The denominator is the total number of parts, 12, not 7 or 5. Reading which group the question asks about decides the numerator.

**6.** 0.3 litres = 300 ml [1]. The ratio is 450 : 300 : 750 and the HCF is 150 [1]. So the ratio is **3 : 2 : 5** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A unit conversion for pineapple juice is needed before any simplifying. Working in millilitres avoids decimals.

**7. (a)** Scale factor 18 ÷ 12 = 1.5 [1]. Oats 300 × 1.5 = **450 g**, butter 200 × 1.5 = **300 g** [1], syrup 150 × 1.5 = **225 g** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 500 ÷ 200 = 2.5 batches of 12 [1]. 2.5 × 12 = **30 flapjacks** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A single scale factor applies to every ingredient. In part (b), comparing the butter available with the butter in one batch is a short, clear way to set up the working.

**8. (a)** For each 1 of concentrate there are 7 of water, so **y = 7x** [1]
**(b)** y = 7 × 2.5 = **17.5 litres** [1]
**(c)** When there is no concentrate (x = 0) there is no water (y = 0), because y is a multiple of x with no added constant. [1]
**(d)** For t = 1, C = 14, and for t = 3, C = 22 [1]. The ratios C : t are 14 : 1 and 22 : 3, which are different, so they are not always in the same ratio [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" is best answered by choosing two specific values and comparing them in full. A statement that the line has a fixed charge supports the numbers but does not replace them.

**9. (a)** Sam: 2/7 × 1260 = **£360** [1]. The rest is 1260 - 360 = £900 [1]. Tia: 3/5 × 900 = **£540** [1]. Uma: 2/5 × 900 = **£360** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** The ratio is 360 : 540 : 360 [1], which simplifies by 180 to **2 : 3 : 2** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The ratio 2 : 3 : 2 has 7 parts, and 1575 ÷ 7 = 225 [1]. Tia receives 3 × 225 = **£675** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (a) the second sharing applies to what is left after Sam's share, not to the whole £1,260. Part (c) rewards using the simplified ratio from part (b).

**10. (a)** Let the starting numbers be 5k red and 3k blue [1]. After adding blue counters there are 3k + 12 blue [1]. Red to blue is now 5k : (3k + 12) = 5 : 6, so 3k + 12 = 6k [1]. Then 3k = 12 and k = 4 [1]. At the start there were **20 red and 12 blue** counters [1]. **[5]**
**(b)** At the end there are 20 red and 24 blue, which is 44 counters [1]. The blue fraction is 24/44 = **6/11** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Set the problem up with a letter for one part and write the changed quantity before forming the new ratio. Checking that 20 : 24 simplifies to 5 : 6 confirms the answer to part (a).

**11. (a)** Mix A has 5 parts for 5 litres, so blue is 2 litres and white is 3 litres [1]. Mix B has 10 parts for 20 litres, so one part is 2 litres [1]. Blue in B is 3 × 2 = 6 litres and white is 7 × 2 = 14 litres [1]. Totals are blue 2 + 6 = 8 litres [1] and white 3 + 14 = 17 litres, so the ratio is **8 : 17** [1]. **[5]**
**(b)** The total volume is 25 litres [1]. The blue fraction is **8/25** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Find the real amount of each colour in each mix before combining. Adding the two ratios directly (2 : 3 and 3 : 7) does not work because the volumes are different.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not converting to the same unit before simplifying, as in questions 1 and 6.
- Dividing by one number from the ratio instead of by the sum of the parts.
- Putting the numbers in the wrong order, so the larger quantity is paired with the smaller ratio number.
- Treating a given difference as a total, as in question 4.
- Using 7 or 5 as the denominator instead of 12 when turning a ratio into a fraction.
- Applying a second sharing to the original total rather than to the remainder.
- Adding a new quantity to a ratio number instead of to the real amount in a changing-ratio problem.
- Adding two ratios together to combine mixtures of different volumes.
- Stopping before the answer is in its simplest form.
- Skipping a quick sum check when shares are found.
