These questions cover quantitative skill QS1, "calculate, use and understand ratios and fractions", from section 3.5 of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Economics (9640) specification. Every country, firm and figure is invented; "cu" means currency units. Read the [QS1 study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs1-quantitative-skills/) first, or refresh the methods with the [QS1 revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs1-quantitative-skills-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Simplify the ratio 84 : 126. **[1]**

**2.** Country A exports goods worth 72 cu billion and imports goods worth 90 cu billion.

**(a)** Write the ratio of exports to imports in its simplest form. **[1]**

**(b)** Find exports as a fraction of total trade (exports plus imports), in its simplest form. **[1]**

**3.** Six firms supply a market. Their annual sales are 120, 95, 80, 45, 35 and 25 cu million. Calculate the three-firm concentration ratio as a percentage. **[2]**

**4.** In a closed economy with no government, the marginal propensity to consume is 0.75. Calculate the value of the multiplier. **[1]**

**5.** A workshop employs 15 workers, who produce 1,200 units a week. Calculate labour productivity per worker. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** The table shows the market shares of the four largest firms in two markets.

| Firm rank | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Market X (%) | 34 | 22 | 18 | 11 |
| Market Y (%) | 23 | 22 | 20 | 20 |

**(a)** Calculate the four-firm concentration ratio for each market. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the two markets may differ in competition even though the ratios are equal. **[2]**

**(c)** State one other limitation of a concentration ratio as a measure of market power. **[1]**

**7.** In one day, a worker in Country P can make 12 phones or 4 tables. A worker in Country Q can make 6 phones or 3 tables.

**(a)** Calculate the opportunity cost of one table, in phones, in each country. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the country with the comparative advantage in tables, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**(c)** State a range of terms of trade, in phones per table, at which both countries gain from trade. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why both countries can gain from trade even though Country P has an absolute advantage in both goods. **[2]**

**8.** In Country B, the marginal propensity to save is 0.12, to tax is 0.20 and to import is 0.28.

**(a)** Calculate the value of the multiplier. **[2]**

**(b)** Investment rises by 54 cu million. Calculate the resulting change in national income. **[1]**

**(c)** The marginal propensity to import rises to 0.38. Calculate the new change in national income from the same rise in investment. **[2]**

**9.** In a closed economy with no government, national income rises from 800 to 1,000 cu billion and consumption rises from 600 to 720 cu billion.

**(a)** Calculate the marginal propensity to consume, as a fraction in its simplest form. **[2]**

**(b)** State the marginal propensity to save. **[1]**

**10.** A firm has 20 workers who each work 40 hours a week, producing 9,600 units. After reorganising, it has 18 workers who each work 45 hours a week, producing 9,450 units.

**(a)** Calculate output per worker before and after. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate output per hour worked before and after. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the two measures give different pictures of the change in productivity. **[1]**

**11.** Country C's national debt was 540 cu billion when its GDP was 900 cu billion. A year later, the debt was 570 cu billion and GDP was 1,000 cu billion.

**(a)** Calculate the debt-to-GDP ratio as a percentage in each year. **[2]**

**(b)** A politician says, "The debt problem has worsened." Using the data, assess this claim. **[3]**

**12.** A government spends 1,350 cu million on health, education and defence in the ratio 5 : 3 : 1.

**(a)** Calculate the spending on each. **[2]**

**(b)** The next year, 50 cu million is moved from health to defence, with the total unchanged. Write the new ratio of health : education : defence in its simplest form. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Dividing both by 42 gives **2 : 3** [1]. Total: 1

**2. (a)** 72 : 90 = **4 : 5** (dividing by 18) [1].

**(b)** 72/(72 + 90) = 72/162 = **4/9** [1]. Total: 2

**3.** Total sales = 400 cu million, and the three largest firms sell 120 + 95 + 80 = 295 [1]. Ratio = 295/400 × 100 = **73.75%** [1]. Total: 2

**4.** Multiplier = 1/(1 − 0.75) = 1/0.25 = **4** [1]. Total: 1

**5.** 1,200/15 = **80 units per worker per week** [1]. Total: 1

**6. (a)** Market X: 34 + 22 + 18 + 11 = **85%** [1]. Market Y: 23 + 22 + 20 + 20 = **85%** [1].

**(b)** In X the largest firm holds 34%, well ahead of its rivals, so it may have the power to lead prices [1]. In Y the four firms are of similar size, so rivalry between them may be stronger [1].

**(c)** Any one: it depends on how the market is defined; it ignores competition from imports or substitutes; it says nothing about barriers to entry or how firms behave [1]. Total: 5

**7. (a)** P: 12/4 = **3 phones** [1]. Q: 6/3 = **2 phones** [1].

**(b)** **Country Q** [1], because it gives up fewer phones (2 rather than 3) to make one table, so it has the lower opportunity cost [1].

**(c)** Any rate **between 2 and 3 phones per table**, such as 2.5 [1].

**(d)** Gains come from differences in opportunity cost, not in absolute productivity [1]. If P specialises in phones and Q in tables, total output of both goods can rise, and trading at a rate between the two opportunity costs leaves each country better off [1]. Total: 7

**8. (a)** MPW = 0.12 + 0.20 + 0.28 = 0.60 [1]. Multiplier = 1/0.60 = **1.67** (5/3) [1].

**(b)** ΔY = 54 × 5/3 = **90 cu million** [1].

**(c)** New MPW = 0.70, so the multiplier is 1/0.70 = 1.43 [1]. ΔY = 54/0.70 = **77.1 cu million** [1]. Total: 5

**9. (a)** MPC = ΔC/ΔY = 120/200 [1] = **3/5** [1].

**(b)** MPS = 1 − 3/5 = **2/5** (0.4) [1]. Total: 3

**10. (a)** Before: 9,600/20 = **480 units**. After: 9,450/18 = **525 units** [1] [1].

**(b)** Before: 9,600/800 hours = **12 units per hour** [1]. After: 9,450/810 hours = **11.7 units per hour** [1].

**(c)** Output per worker rose because each worker works more hours, but output per hour fell, so workers are less productive in each hour they work [1]. Total: 5

**11. (a)** Year 1: 540/900 = **60%** [1]. Year 2: 570/1,000 = **57%** [1].

**(b)** The debt rose by 30 cu billion, which supports the claim in money terms [1]. But GDP grew faster, by about 11%, so the debt became a smaller fraction of the economy and is relatively easier to service [1]. On the ratio the claim is not supported, though one year's data and no information on interest rates limit the judgement [1]. Total: 5

**12. (a)** One part = 1,350/9 = 150 [1]. Health **750**, education **450** and defence **150 cu million** [1].

**(b)** New amounts: 700 : 450 : 200 [1]. Dividing by 50: **14 : 9 : 4** [1]. Total: 4

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving exports as a fraction of imports when the question asks for a fraction of total trade.
- Adding the shares of firms that are not the largest, or forgetting to divide by total sales.
- Calculating opportunity cost the wrong way up, such as tables per phone.
- Writing the multiplier as 1/MPC.
- Calculating a ratio but not interpreting it, especially in "assess" questions.
- Leaving a ratio unsimplified when the question asks for its simplest form.
