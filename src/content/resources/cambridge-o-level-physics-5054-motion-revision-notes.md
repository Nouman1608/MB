## Definitions to learn word for word

- **Speed:** distance travelled per unit time.
- **Velocity:** change in displacement per unit time.
- **Acceleration:** change in velocity per unit time.
- **Deceleration:** a negative acceleration.
- **Uniform acceleration:** velocity changes by equal amounts in equal times.
- **Non-uniform acceleration:** the rate of change of velocity is itself changing.

## Equations

```
speed            v = s / t
average speed    = total distance travelled / total time taken
acceleration     a = Δv / Δt          (unit: m/s²)
```

Free-fall acceleration near the Earth's surface: **g ≈ 9.8 m/s²**, approximately constant.

**Unit conversion:** km/h ÷ 3.6 = m/s. Example: 108 km/h ÷ 3.6 = **30 m/s**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Scalar / vector** | Speed is scalar; velocity is vector (needs direction) |
| **Gradient** | Rise ÷ run of a graph line |
| **Tangent** | Straight line touching a curve at one point; its gradient gives the instantaneous rate |
| **Area under speed–time graph** | Distance travelled |
| **Free fall** | Motion under gravity alone, acceleration g |
| **Average speed** | Total distance ÷ total time, including stops |
| **Instantaneous speed** | Speed at one moment, from the gradient of the tangent |

## Graph shapes

**Distance–time** (gradient = speed)

- Horizontal → at rest
- Straight slope → constant speed
- Steepening curve → accelerating
- Flattening curve → decelerating

**Speed–time** (gradient = acceleration; area = distance)

- On the time axis → at rest
- Horizontal above the axis → constant speed
- Straight slope up / down → constant acceleration / deceleration
- Curve → changing acceleration

## Areas: break the shape up

- Rectangle (constant speed): speed × time.
- Triangle (constant acceleration from or to rest): ½ × base × height.
- Trapezium: rectangle + triangle, or ½ × (u + v) × t.

**Quick example.** A cyclist accelerates uniformly from 4.0 m/s to 10.0 m/s in 6.0 s.

```
a = (10.0 − 4.0) ÷ 6.0 = 1.0 m/s²
distance = rectangle + triangle = (4.0 × 6.0) + (½ × 6.0 × 6.0) = 24 + 18 = 42 m
```

## Average-speed trap

Never average two speeds. A runner covers 400 m at 8.0 m/s (50 s) and 400 m at 5.0 m/s (80 s):

```
average speed = 800 ÷ 130 = 6.2 m/s   (not 6.5 m/s)
```

## Free fall in numbers (no air resistance)

| Time / s | Speed / m/s | Distance fallen / m |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 1.0 | 9.8 | 4.9 |
| 2.0 | 19.6 | 19.6 |
| 3.0 | 29.4 | 44.1 |

Distance comes from the triangle area ½ × t × (9.8t). Speed rises by 9.8 m/s each second, but the
distance fallen each second increases.

## Multi-stage journey in exam layout

A lift starts from rest, accelerates uniformly to 2.0 m/s in 2.5 s, travels at 2.0 m/s for 6.0 s,
then decelerates uniformly to rest in 2.0 s.

```
accelerations:   0.80 m/s²  then  0      then  −1.0 m/s²
distances:       ½ × 2.5 × 2.0 = 2.5 m
                 2.0 × 6.0     = 12.0 m
                 ½ × 2.0 × 2.0 = 2.0 m
total distance = 16.5 m;  total time = 10.5 s
average speed  = 16.5 / 10.5 = 1.6 m/s
```

Method: sketch the speed–time graph, label each stage, find gradients for accelerations and
areas for distances, then add.

## Describing motion from a graph

Write one sentence per stage, giving the type of motion, the values and the time: "From 0 to
2.5 s the lift accelerates uniformly at 0.80 m/s²; from 2.5 s to 8.5 s it moves at a constant
2.0 m/s; from 8.5 s to 10.5 s it decelerates uniformly to rest."

## Exam traps

- A horizontal line on a speed–time graph means constant speed, not rest.
- Distance comes from the area under a **speed**–time graph, never a distance–time graph.
- Convert minutes to seconds and km/h to m/s before calculating.
- Keep the minus sign for deceleration in calculations.
- Use a large triangle when finding a gradient from a graph.
- Velocity questions need a direction in the answer.
- Average speed includes time spent stopped.

## Self-test

1. Define velocity.
2. Convert 72 km/h to m/s.
3. A car travels 2.4 km in 2.0 minutes. Find its average speed in m/s.
4. A bus slows from 15 m/s to 3 m/s in 4.0 s. Find its acceleration.
5. What does a steepening curve on a distance–time graph show?
6. What does a curved line on a speed–time graph show?
7. A speed–time graph is a horizontal line at 12 m/s for 25 s. Find the distance.
8. An object accelerates from rest to 18 m/s in 6.0 s. Find the distance travelled.
9. Give one example of non-uniform acceleration.
10. State the acceleration of free fall near the Earth's surface.
11. A ball is dropped from rest. Find its speed after 2.5 s, ignoring air resistance.
12. How do you find the speed at one instant from a curved distance–time graph?

### Answers

1. Change in displacement per unit time.
2. 72 ÷ 3.6 = **20 m/s**.
3. 2400 m ÷ 120 s = **20 m/s**.
4. (3 − 15) ÷ 4.0 = **−3.0 m/s²** (a deceleration of 3.0 m/s²).
5. Accelerating.
6. Changing acceleration.
7. 12 × 25 = **300 m**.
8. ½ × 6.0 × 18 = **54 m**.
9. A car pulling away and changing gear, or a skydiver as air resistance increases.
10. Approximately 9.8 m/s², and approximately constant.
11. 9.8 × 2.5 = **24.5 m/s**.
12. Draw a tangent at that point and calculate its gradient.
