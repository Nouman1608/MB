## Meaning (4.1.1)

- **National income:** the total value of goods and services produced in an economy over a period (usually a year), equal to the total income generated by that production.
- It is a **flow** over time, not a stock of wealth.
- **Output = income = expenditure** in principle: each sale is someone's spending and someone's income.

## Counting rules

- Count **final** output only; intermediate goods are inside final prices.
- **Value added** at each stage = sales value - inputs bought from other firms. Sum of value added = value of final output.
- **Exclude** transfer payments (no output in return), second-hand sales (except the dealer's margin) and purely financial transactions.

## The three measures (4.1.2)

```
GDP = value of output produced within the country's borders
GNI = GDP + net primary income from abroad
NNI = GNI - depreciation

Net primary income from abroad
   = primary income received from abroad - primary income paid abroad
```

- **Primary income** = income from factors of production: wages, profits, interest, dividends, rent.
- Net primary income **negative** (much foreign ownership at home) -> GNI below GDP.
- Net primary income **positive** (residents own much overseas) -> GNI above GDP.

## Adjustments (4.1.3 and 4.1.4)

```
Basic prices = market prices - taxes on products + subsidies on products
Net value    = gross value - depreciation
```

- **Market prices:** what buyers pay.
- **Basic prices:** what producers receive.
- **Gross:** before allowing for depreciation. **Net:** after.
- The two adjustments are independent: either order gives the same answer.

**Mini example (invented, billion cu).** GDP at market prices 500; net primary income +12; depreciation 60; taxes on products 55; subsidies on products 10.

```
GNI at market prices = 512
NNI at market prices = 452
NNI at basic prices  = 452 - 55 + 10 = 407
```

**Value added mini example (invented).** A miner sells ore for 20 cu, a smelter sells metal for 50 cu and a factory sells tools for 80 cu. Value added = 20 + 30 + 30 = 80 cu, the value of the final tools. Adding all sales (150 cu) double counts.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| National income | Total value of output, equal to total income generated, over a period |
| Final good | A good bought for final use, not to make another good |
| Intermediate good | A good used up in producing another good |
| Value added | Sales value minus the cost of inputs bought from other firms |
| GDP | Value of output produced within a country's borders in a year |
| GNI | GDP plus net primary income from abroad |
| NNI | GNI minus depreciation |
| Depreciation | Fall in value of the capital stock through wear and obsolescence |
| Market prices | Values including taxes on products and net of subsidies on products |
| Basic prices | Market prices minus taxes on products plus subsidies on products |
| Transfer payment | Money paid with no good or service produced in return |

## Which measure for which question?

| Question asks about | Better measure |
|---|---|
| Production inside the country | GDP |
| Income of the country's residents | GNI |
| Income available without running down capital | NNI |
| What producers actually receive | A measure at basic prices |

## Exam traps

- Counting all sales in a production chain (double counting).
- Including pensions or benefits as income.
- Removing all taxes, not just taxes on products, in the market-to-basic step.
- Subtracting subsidies when moving to basic prices.
- Using depreciation to move from GDP to GNI.
- Mishandling a negative net primary income.

## Self-test

1. Why is national income a flow?
2. A farmer sells wheat to a miller for 30 cu, the miller sells flour to a baker for 55 cu and the baker sells bread for 90 cu. What is the value added by the baker, and the total value added?
3. Why are unemployment benefits excluded from national income?
4. Define GNI.
5. GDP is 640 and net primary income from abroad is -40. Calculate GNI.
6. What does a negative net primary income suggest about a country?
7. GNI is 600 and depreciation is 70. Calculate NNI.
8. Market-price GDP is 900, product taxes are 110 and product subsidies are 20. Calculate GDP at basic prices.
9. Why might GDP at market prices rise without any rise in output?
10. Why is a net figure useful?
11. Does the order of the gross-to-net and market-to-basic adjustments matter?
12. Which measure better shows the income of a country's residents?

**Answers**

1. It measures output or income produced over a period of time, not the value of assets held at one moment.
2. Baker: 90 - 55 = 35 cu. Total value added = 30 + 25 + 35 = 90 cu, equal to the value of the bread.
3. They are transfer payments: no good or service is produced in return.
4. GDP plus net primary income from abroad.
5. 640 - 40 = 600.
6. More primary income is paid to foreign owners of factors than residents earn abroad, suggesting substantial foreign ownership of production at home.
7. 600 - 70 = 530.
8. 900 - 110 + 20 = 810.
9. A rise in taxes on products raises market prices, and so market-price GDP, without any change in output.
10. It shows the output or income available after replacing worn-out capital, so it does not overstate what can be consumed or invested in new capital.
11. No; they are independent, so either order gives the same result.
12. GNI.
