## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Quick example |
|---|---|---|
| Integer | A whole number: positive, negative or zero | −12, 0, 47 |
| Directed number | A number with a sign showing its direction from zero | −5 is 5 below zero |
| Sum | Result of adding | Sum of −3 and 10 is 7 |
| Difference | Result of subtracting; the gap between two values | Difference between −2 and 9 is 11 |
| Product | Result of multiplying | Product of −4 and 5 is −20 |
| Quotient | Result of dividing | Quotient of 36 and −9 is −4 |
| Dividend | The number being divided | In 84 ÷ 7, the dividend is 84 |
| Divisor | The number you divide by | In 84 ÷ 7, the divisor is 7 |
| Remainder | What is left when a division is not exact | 50 ÷ 8 = 6 remainder 2 |

## Signs: what to remember

**Adding and subtracting.**

```
+ (+a)  →  + a        move right
+ (−a)  →  − a        move left
− (+a)  →  − a        move left
− (−a)  →  + a        move right
```

Rewrite each pair of signs first, then work left to right along the number line.

**Multiplying and dividing.**

```
same signs       →  positive
different signs  →  negative
several factors  →  count the negatives: even → +, odd → −
```

Settle the sign, then work with the sizes only.

## Written methods: the checklist

**Column addition and subtraction.** Line up units under units. In subtraction, exchange from the next column when the top digit is smaller. Across zeros, exchange from the first non-zero digit: in 5 000 − 1 784, the 5 thousands becomes 4 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens and 10 units.

**Long multiplication (column).**

```
1. Multiply by the units digit.
2. Write a 0 in the units column, then multiply by the tens digit.
3. Add the rows.
4. Estimate to check the size (round each number to 1 significant figure).
```

**Long multiplication (grid).** Split both numbers by place value, multiply each pair, add the row totals.

**Long division.**

```
1. Write out the first nine multiples of the divisor.
2. Divide into the leading digits; write the quotient digit above.
3. Subtract to get the remainder.
4. Bring down the next digit and repeat.
5. Check: quotient × divisor + remainder = dividend.
```

**Short division** is the same process for a one-digit divisor, with the remainder carried as a small digit in front of the next one.

## Remainders in context

| Context | Do this with the remainder |
|---|---|
| "How many are needed?" (coaches, boxes to hold everything, tins of paint) | Round **up** |
| "How many full ones?" or "How many can be made?" | Round **down** |
| "How many are left over?" | The remainder itself |
| "Share equally" with money | Convert to pence and continue past the point, if the question allows |

Always finish with a sentence in the context's words.

## Exam traps

- **Double minus:** −8 − (−3) is −5, not −11.
- **Wrong rule for addition:** "two negatives make a positive" is for × and ÷ only. −6 + (−9) = −15.
- **Difference across zero:** the difference between −13 and 4 is 17, found by 4 − (−13). Adding the sizes is correct only when the values lie on opposite sides of zero.
- **Missing place-value zero** in the tens row of a column multiplication.
- **Remainder lost** in long division when a digit is brought down before subtracting.
- **Rounding the wrong way** in a remainder context.
- **Sign of a mean:** a negative total divided by a positive count gives a negative mean.
- **No method shown** on Paper 2 or Paper 5. Method marks need visible working.

## Five-second checks

- A product of two numbers above 10 should be close to the product of the rounded numbers.
- A quotient multiplied by the divisor must give back the dividend (plus any remainder).
- A sum of a large negative and a small positive is still negative.
- The difference between two numbers is never negative when the question asks for "the difference".
- The units digit of a product depends only on the units digits: 638 × 47 must end in 6, because 8 × 7 = 56.

## Quick self-test

1. Work out −23 + 8 − (−15).
2. Find the difference between −9 and 17.
3. Work out (−4) × (−6) × (−2).
4. Work out (−144) ÷ 12.
5. Work out 638 × 47 without a calculator.
6. Work out 8 652 ÷ 21 without a calculator.
7. Work out 5 000 − 1 784.
8. 410 people travel in minibuses with 16 seats each. How many minibuses are needed?
9. Find the product of −15 and −20, then the quotient of −300 and 25.
10. The overnight temperature is −7 °C. It rises 12 degrees by noon, then falls 9 degrees by evening. Find the evening temperature.
11. Work out 2 568 ÷ 6 by short division.
12. Find the mean of −6, 4, −11, 9 and −16.

### Answers

1. −23 + 8 = −15, then −15 + 15 = **0**.
2. 17 − (−9) = **26**.
3. Three negatives, so negative: 4 × 6 × 2 = 48, giving **−48**.
4. Different signs: **−12**.
5. 638 × 7 = 4 466 and 638 × 40 = 25 520, so **29 986**. Estimate 600 × 50 = 30 000.
6. 86 ÷ 21 = 4 remainder 2; 25 ÷ 21 = 1 remainder 4; 42 ÷ 21 = 2. Answer **412**. Check: 412 × 21 = 8 240 + 412 = 8 652.
7. **3 216**. Check: 3 216 + 1 784 = 5 000.
8. 410 ÷ 16 = 25 remainder 10, so **26 minibuses** (10 people still need seats).
9. Product **300**; quotient **−12**.
10. −7 + 12 = 5, then 5 − 9 = **−4 °C**.
11. 25 ÷ 6 = 4 remainder 1; 16 ÷ 6 = 2 remainder 4; 48 ÷ 6 = 8. Answer **428**.
12. Total −6 + 4 − 11 + 9 − 16 = −20, so the mean is −20 ÷ 5 = **−4**.
