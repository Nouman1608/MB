## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Proton number (atomic number) | Number of protons in the nucleus; the order of the Periodic Table |
| Period | Horizontal row; period number = number of occupied electron shells |
| Group | Vertical column; group number = number of outer-shell electrons (Groups I–VII) |
| Electronic configuration | Arrangement of electrons in shells, e.g. 2,8,3 |
| Metallic character | Tendency to behave as a metal: conduct, form positive ions, basic oxides |
| Trend | A steady change in a property down a group or across a period |

## Reading the table

- Ordered by **increasing proton number**.
- Period = number of occupied shells.
- Group (I–VII) = outer-shell electrons.
- Group VIII = full outer shell (noble gases).

| Configuration | Period | Group |
|---|---|---|
| 2,3 | 2 | III |
| 2,8,1 | 3 | I |
| 2,8,5 | 3 | V |
| 2,8,8,2 | 4 | II |

## Across a period

Metal → non-metal, left to right.

| Property | Left (metals) | Right (non-metals) |
|---|---|---|
| Electrical conductivity | Good | Poor (most) |
| Ions formed | Positive | Negative (or covalent bonding) |
| Oxides | Basic | Acidic |

Period 3: Na, Mg, Al (metals) → Si → P, S, Cl (non-metals) → Ar (noble gas).

## Group number and ion charge

| Group | I | II | III | IV | V | VI | VII | VIII |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outer electrons | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | Full |
| Usual ion | 1+ | 2+ | 3+ | (none) | 3− | 2− | 1− | (none) |

- Metals lose electrons → positive ions with the charge = group number.
- Non-metals gain (8 − group number) electrons → negative ions.
- Formula check: charges must cancel. Mg²⁺ + N³⁻ → Mg₃N₂.

## Group similarities

- Same number of outer-shell electrons → similar chemical reactions.
- Same ion charge → similar formulae (NaCl, KCl; MgO, CaO).
- The *explanation* is always about **outer-shell electrons**, not number of shells.

## Predicting from position

1. Left or right of the period → metal or non-metal.
2. Group → ion charge → formulae of compounds.
3. Compare with known elements in the same group for general behaviour.

Example: barium (Group II) → metal, Ba²⁺, BaCl₂, BaO (basic).

## Trends from data *(Extended)*

1. State the direction (increases / decreases down the group).
2. Quote data.
3. Predict the next value in the same direction.
4. Comment on any value that does not fit.

Example data (real values): Group I melting points, Li 181 °C, Na 98 °C, K 63 °C → decreases down the group → rubidium predicted lower than 63 °C.

## Exam traps

- Arranged by **proton number**, not mass.
- **Groups** = columns; **periods** = rows.
- Group VI gives **2−**, Group V gives **3−**, not 6− and 5−.
- Group IV and Group VIII elements do not usually form simple ions.
- "Same number of outer electrons" explains group similarity; "same number of shells" does not.
- Hydrogen and helium are in Period 1; the first shell holds only two electrons.
- Use the syllabus's Roman-numeral group names: Group I, Group VII, Group VIII.
- Transition elements sit in the central block, not in a numbered group, and can form ions with different charges.
- When predicting from data, keep the prediction in the direction of the trend and give a value or range, not just "higher".

## Self-test

1. In what order are elements arranged in the Periodic Table?
2. An element has configuration 2,8,6. State its period and group.
3. What is the name for a vertical column of the Periodic Table?
4. State how metallic character changes across a period.
5. Give two properties that show an element is a metal.
6. What charge do Group III ions have?
7. What charge do Group VI ions have? Explain.
8. Why do sodium and potassium react in similar ways?
9. Predict the formula of the compound formed between calcium and nitrogen.
10. Rubidium is in Group I. Predict the formula of rubidium oxide.
11. *(Extended)* Given melting points Cl −101 °C, Br −7 °C, I 114 °C, describe the trend.
12. *(Extended)* Using the data in question 11, predict the state of astatine at room temperature.

**Answers**

1. Increasing proton (atomic) number.
2. Period 3; Group VI.
3. A group.
4. Changes from metallic to non-metallic, left to right.
5. Conducts electricity; forms positive ions; forms a basic oxide.
6. 3+.
7. 2−; the atom has six outer electrons and gains two to fill its outer shell.
8. Both have one outer-shell electron.
9. Ca₃N₂.
10. Rb₂O.
11. Melting point increases down the group.
12. Solid.
