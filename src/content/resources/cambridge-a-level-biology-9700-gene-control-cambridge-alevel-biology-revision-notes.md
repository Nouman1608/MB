These notes condense section **16.3 Gene control** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). It is A Level content, examined in Paper 4. For explanations and worked examples, read the [16.3 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-gene-control-cambridge-alevel-biology/); for exam-style questions, use the [16.3 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-gene-control-cambridge-alevel-biology-practice/).

## Core recall

### Gene types

- **Structural gene**: codes for a protein with a structural or metabolic role (an enzyme, a transport protein).
- **Regulatory gene**: codes for a protein that controls the expression of other genes (a repressor or transcription factor).

### Enzyme types

| | Inducible | Repressible |
|---|---|---|
| Default | Off | On |
| Switched by | Substrate present → on | End product present → off |
| Example | β-galactosidase (lac operon) | Tryptophan-synthesising enzymes in *E. coli* |

### lac operon parts

| Part | Role |
|---|---|
| lacI (regulatory gene) | Codes for the repressor protein; always transcribed |
| Promoter | RNA polymerase binds here |
| Operator | Repressor binds here |
| lacZ | β-galactosidase: hydrolyses lactose to glucose and galactose |
| lacY | Lactose permease: takes lactose into the cell |
| lacA | Transacetylase |

### lac operon in two columns

| Lactose absent | Lactose present |
|---|---|
| Repressor binds to operator | Lactose binds to repressor |
| RNA polymerase blocked | Repressor changes shape, leaves operator |
| No transcription of lacZ, lacY, lacA | RNA polymerase transcribes all three as one mRNA |
| No enzymes made | Enzymes made; lactose taken up and hydrolysed |

When lactose runs out, the repressor rebinds and transcription stops.

### Mutation predictions

- Repressor cannot bind operator, or operator altered → enzymes made all the time.
- Repressor cannot bind lactose → enzymes never made.
- Promoter altered so RNA polymerase cannot bind → enzymes never made.

### Transcription factors (eukaryotes)

- Proteins that bind to DNA.
- Increase or decrease the rate of transcription.
- Allow different cells, and cells at different times, to express different genes.

### Gibberellin and DELLA

1. DELLA proteins inhibit a transcription factor → target genes off.
2. Gibberellin binds to a receptor.
3. DELLA proteins are broken down.
4. Transcription factor freed → binds near promoter → target genes transcribed (for example amylase in barley aleurone; growth genes in stems).

### Prokaryote and eukaryote control compared

| | Prokaryote (lac operon) | Eukaryote |
|---|---|---|
| Genes controlled together | Several structural genes in one operon | Usually each gene separately |
| Controlling proteins | One repressor | Several transcription factors |
| Signal | Lactose binding to repressor | Hormones and other signals, for example gibberellin removing DELLA |

### Reading induction data

- Enzyme activity low before the inducer is added: the repressor is bound to the operator.
- Short lag after lactose is added: time for lactose to enter, bind the repressor, and for transcription and translation.
- mRNA rises before enzyme activity, because translation follows transcription.
- mRNA falls once lactose is used up; enzyme activity falls more slowly as existing enzyme is broken down or diluted by cell division.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Operon | Group of structural genes with one promoter and operator, transcribed together |
| Promoter | DNA sequence where RNA polymerase binds to start transcription |
| Operator | DNA sequence where a repressor binds |
| Repressor protein | Protein that binds to the operator and blocks transcription |
| Inducer | Substance (here lactose) that switches on transcription |
| Transcription factor | Protein that binds to DNA and changes the rate of transcription |
| DELLA protein | Repressor that inhibits a transcription factor until gibberellin causes its breakdown |
| Gene expression | Transcription and translation of a gene to make its protein |

## Exam traps

- The repressor binds the **operator**, not the promoter.
- Lactose binds the **repressor**, not the DNA.
- lacI is **regulatory**; lacZ, lacY and lacA are **structural**.
- Inducible = switched **on** by substrate; repressible = switched **off** by product.
- DELLA proteins inhibit a **transcription factor**; they are not themselves transcription factors.
- Gibberellin binds to a **receptor**, not to DNA.
- cAMP is not required for the lac operon; leave it out.
- Always describe **both** conditions (with and without lactose) when explaining the lac operon.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between a structural and a regulatory gene.
2. Is β-galactosidase an inducible or a repressible enzyme? Why?
3. Name the protein coded for by lacI.
4. Where does RNA polymerase bind?
5. What does lactose bind to in the lac operon?
6. What is the function of lactose permease?
7. Predict enzyme production if the operator is altered so the repressor cannot bind.
8. Predict enzyme production if the repressor cannot bind lactose.
9. State what is meant by a transcription factor.
10. What do DELLA proteins inhibit?
11. What happens to DELLA proteins when gibberellin binds to its receptor?
12. Which gene is transcribed in barley aleurone cells as a result?

### Answers

1. A structural gene codes for a protein with a structural or metabolic role; a regulatory gene codes for a protein that controls the expression of other genes.
2. Inducible: it is made only when its substrate, lactose, is present.
3. The lac repressor protein.
4. The promoter.
5. The repressor protein, changing its shape.
6. It transports lactose into the bacterial cell.
7. Enzymes are made all the time, with or without lactose.
8. Enzymes are never made, even with lactose present.
9. A protein that binds to DNA and increases or decreases the rate of transcription.
10. A transcription factor that promotes transcription.
11. They are broken down.
12. The gene for amylase.
