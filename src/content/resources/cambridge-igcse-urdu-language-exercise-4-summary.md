Exercise 4 of **Paper 1 Reading and Writing** in Cambridge IGCSE Urdu as a Second Language (0539) is a
**summary** worth **10 marks**. You write one paragraph of **no more than 100 words** about an aspect
or aspects of the text you have just read in Exercise 3, using **your own words as far as possible**.
It is the bridge between the reading and writing halves of the paper: it is the only exercise that
tests reading and writing objectives together. This guide goes deeper on that one exercise than the
[Paper 1 Reading and Writing guide](/resources/igcse-cambridge-urdu-paper-1-reading-writing/).

## What the syllabus says

The task is fixed by five facts from the 2025-2027 syllabus:

| Requirement | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Paragraph-length | one paragraph, not a list or several paragraphs |
| No more than 100 words | a ceiling, not a target to pad towards |
| About an aspect or aspects of the Exercise 3 text | only the part the question names |
| Own words as far as possible | paraphrase rather than copy |
| Objectives R2, R3, W1, W2, W3, W4, W5 | reading for ideas and connections, plus all five writing objectives |

The writing objectives are worth looking at closely, because each describes something a summary must
show: **W1** clear and accurate communication, **W2** coherent organisation with linking devices,
**W3** a range of structures and vocabulary used accurately, **W4** control of punctuation and
spelling, and **W5** appropriate register and style. A summary is therefore marked as writing as well
as reading; a list of correct points in broken Urdu cannot score fully.

The syllabus does not publish how the ten marks divide. On this site's practice pages we use a working
convention of **up to 5 for content** (relevant points) and **up to 5 for language** (own words,
organisation, accuracy). That split is our practice convention, not Cambridge's scheme.

## Step 1: read the aspect, not the text

The question names the aspect, for example "the problems the art faces and how it survives" or "why
traditional games declined and how they are being revived". Often there are **two aspects**, and both
must appear. Before rereading the text, write the aspect at the top of your planning space in two or
three words per part.

Everything in the text that does not serve the aspect is irrelevant: the introduction, background
history, examples, statistics that only illustrate a point, and quotations. A summary of the whole
text, however well written, will miss marks because it spends words on content that earns none.

## Step 2: collect the points

Your Exercise 3 notes are a head start, but the summary aspect may cut across the note headings. Go
through the text and mark every sentence that answers the aspect. Then:

- **Merge** repeated points: the text may state one idea twice in different words.
- **Generalise** lists and examples: "پودینہ، دھنیا اور ٹماٹر" becomes "جلدی اگنے والی سبزیاں".
- **Drop** quotations, but keep their point if it is relevant.
- **Count**: for a 100-word summary on two aspects, six to eight points is typical.

## Step 3: put the points in your own words

"Own words as far as possible" does not mean replacing every word. Technical terms and names may stay
(ٹرک آرٹ، کبڈی). It means you should not lift whole phrases and sentences. These five techniques cover
most needs:

| Technique | Text | Your version |
|---|---|---|
| Synonym | پانی کی کمی ہے | پانی کی قلت ہے |
| Change the word class | بچے پہلے سے کم کھیلتے ہیں | بچوں کے کھیل میں کمی آ گئی ہے |
| Generalise | سیب، آم اور کیلے | پھل |
| Active to passive (or back) | انتظامیہ نے سڑکیں بند کیں | سڑکیں بند کی گئیں |
| Combine with a connective | بارش بہت ہوئی۔ اسکول بند ہو گئے۔ | شدید بارش کے باعث اسکول بند ہو گئے۔ |

Combining is the most powerful technique, because it saves words and shows W2 linking at the same
time.

## Step 4: link the points into one paragraph

Connectives turn a list into a summary. Group them by function:

| Function | Connectives |
|---|---|
| Addition | اس کے علاوہ، نیز، مزید یہ کہ |
| Contrast | تاہم، لیکن، اس کے باوجود |
| Cause | کیونکہ، کی وجہ سے، کے باعث |
| Result | اس لیے، لہٰذا، چنانچہ |
| Sequence | پہلے، پھر، آخر میں |

For a two-aspect question, a contrast connective such as **تاہم** is a natural hinge between the two
halves.

**Register:** write in neutral, impersonal Urdu, in the third person. Do not address the reader, do not
add your own opinion, and do not start with "اس مضمون میں بتایا گیا ہے کہ", which spends words on
nothing.

## Step 5: check the count

Count your words. A useful habit is to count one line of your handwriting and multiply. In Urdu,
postpositions and helper verbs (کے لیے، ہو گیا ہے) add words quickly, so compound verbs and noun phrases
help you stay within the limit. If you are over, cut in this order: repeated ideas, introductory
phrases, examples, then the least important point.

## Worked example

An original source paragraph written for this guide:

> شہر میں چلنے والی نئی الیکٹرک بسوں نے لوگوں کی زندگی آسان کر دی ہے۔ ان کا کرایہ رکشے سے آدھا ہے اور یہ ہر دس منٹ بعد آتی ہیں۔ بسوں میں ایئر کنڈیشنر لگے ہیں، اس لیے گرمیوں میں سفر آرام دہ ہو گیا ہے۔ خواتین کے لیے الگ نشستیں مخصوص ہیں۔ لیکن کچھ مسائل بھی ہیں۔ بسیں صرف بڑی سڑکوں پر چلتی ہیں، اس لیے اندرونی علاقوں کے لوگوں کو اسٹاپ تک پہنچنے کے لیے پھر بھی رکشہ لینا پڑتا ہے۔ صبح اور شام کے رش کے وقت بسوں میں کھڑے ہونے کی جگہ بھی نہیں ملتی۔

**Task:** summarise the advantages and the problems of the new bus service (a short version of the
task, so a limit of about 70 words).

**Points:**

1. cheap (half the rickshaw fare)
2. frequent (every ten minutes)
3. air-conditioned, so comfortable in summer
4. reserved seats for women
5. main roads only, so a rickshaw is still needed to reach the stop
6. overcrowded at rush hour

**Model summary** (66 words):

> نئی الیکٹرک بسیں سستی ہیں اور تھوڑے تھوڑے وقفے سے ملتی ہیں۔ ٹھنڈی ہونے کی وجہ سے گرمی میں بھی سفر آرام سے کٹتا ہے، اور خواتین کے لیے نشستیں الگ رکھی گئی ہیں۔ تاہم یہ سروس صرف مرکزی شاہراہوں تک محدود ہے، لہٰذا گلی محلوں کے مسافروں کو اسٹاپ تک الگ سواری لینی پڑتی ہے، جبکہ دفتری اوقات میں بسیں کھچا کھچ بھری ہوتی ہیں۔

**What it does:** "کرایہ رکشے سے آدھا ہے" becomes the general "سستی"; "ہر دس منٹ بعد" becomes
"تھوڑے تھوڑے وقفے سے"; "بڑی سڑکوں" becomes "مرکزی شاہراہوں"; "رش کے وقت… جگہ نہیں ملتی" becomes the
idiom "کھچا کھچ بھری". The opening sentence of the source, a general claim, is not summarised, and
**تاہم** marks the turn from advantages to problems. Every point is present in one linked paragraph.

## Common mistakes

1. **Summarising the whole text** instead of the aspect named in the question.
2. **Covering only one of two aspects.**
3. **Lifting whole sentences.** Points copied verbatim show location, not the writing skills the
   exercise also rewards.
4. **Including examples, figures and quotations** that only illustrate a point.
5. **Writing a list or several paragraphs** instead of one linked paragraph.
6. **Adding an opinion** ("میرے خیال میں یہ اچھی بات ہے") that the summary does not ask for.
7. **Exceeding 100 words.** The limit is part of the task; treat it as a ceiling.
8. **Ignoring accuracy.** Gender agreement, the ergative نے, spelling and punctuation still count.

## Exam technique and timing

Exercise 4 is worth 10 marks. Because you have already read the text for Exercise 3, about **15
minutes** is realistic: 4 minutes to collect points, 8 to write, 3 to count and check. Draft in your
head or in brief notes, not as a full rough copy; a full draft and a fair copy rarely fit in the time.
When checking, read the summary once for meaning (does every point answer the aspect?) and once for
accuracy (agreement, نے, spelling).
