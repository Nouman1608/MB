## Section B

**5.** A mixture and four reference compounds are run on the same plate. The solvent front travels
9.0 cm. The mixture gives spots at 1.8 cm, 4.5 cm and 6.3 cm. The references travel: **P** 4.5 cm,
**Q** 6.3 cm, **R** 2.7 cm, **S** 1.8 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the Rf value of each spot in the mixture. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the compounds probably present in the mixture, and the reference that is absent.
**[2]**

**(c)** Explain why this identification is not certain. **[1]**

**6.** Decane, C₁₀H₂₂, butanone, CH₃COCH₂CH₃, and butan-1-ol, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH, are separated using hexane
as the mobile phase.

**(a)** Predict the order of their Rf values, from highest to lowest. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain your order. **[3]**

**7.** Compound **Z** has an Rf value of 0.15 when hexane is the mobile phase, and 0.60 when ethyl
ethanoate is the mobile phase. Both runs use the same type of plate.

**(a)** State which mobile phase is more polar. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the Rf value of **Z** increased. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest whether **Z** is polar or non-polar, giving a reason. **[1]**

**8.** Two compounds have Rf values of 0.42 and 0.46 in a solvent. On a plate where the solvent front
travels 5.0 cm:

**(a)** Calculate the distance between the centres of the two spots. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest one change to the procedure that could improve the separation. **[1]**

**9.** A student checks the product of an organic synthesis by TLC, running the product next to the
pure starting material. The product lane shows two spots, one of which has the same Rf as the
starting material.

**(a)** State what this suggests about the product. **[1]**

**(b)** State what result would suggest that the product was pure and contained no starting
material. **[1]**

**10.** Explain why compounds containing –OH groups are held strongly by an aluminium oxide stationary
phase. **[2]**

**11.** An unknown compound **U** and a known compound **V** are compared in two solvents.

| Solvent | Rf of **U** | Rf of **V** |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.55 | 0.55 |
| 2 | 0.30 | 0.48 |

State and explain what you can conclude about **U** and **V**. **[2]**

**12.** The same compound is run on two identical plates in the same solvent. On one plate the solvent
front travels 6.0 cm; on the other it travels 9.0 cm. Explain why the Rf value is the same on both
plates. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The line near the bottom of the plate on which the samples are spotted (and from which
distances are measured) [1].

**(b)** The furthest point reached by the solvent (mobile phase) as it moves up the plate [1].

**2.** 2.7 / 7.5 = 0.36 [1].

**3.** It is a ratio of two distances measured in the same units, so the units cancel [1].

**4.** Any one: the compound is very strongly adsorbed on the stationary phase; it is insoluble in the
mobile phase [1].

**5. (a)** 1.8 / 9.0 = 0.20; 4.5 / 9.0 = 0.50; 6.3 / 9.0 = 0.70: all three correct [2] (one mark if two are
correct).

**(b)** **S**, **P** and **Q** are probably present [1]; **R** is absent [1].

**(c)** Different compounds can have the same Rf value under one set of conditions, so a match does
not prove identity (the result should be confirmed with another solvent) [1].

**6. (a)** Decane > butanone > butan-1-ol [1].

**(b)** Butan-1-ol can form hydrogen bonds with the polar stationary phase, so it is held most strongly
and has the lowest Rf [1]; butanone has a polar C=O bond, giving permanent dipole attractions to the
surface, but cannot donate a hydrogen bond, so it is held less strongly [1]; decane is non-polar, is
hardly adsorbed and is very soluble in hexane, so it travels furthest [1].

**7. (a)** Ethyl ethanoate [1].

**(b)** **Z** is more soluble in the more polar mobile phase [1], so it spends more time in the moving
solvent and less time adsorbed on the stationary phase, travelling further [1].

**(c)** Polar: it was held strongly with non-polar hexane and moved much further once the solvent was
polar [1].

**8. (a)** 0.42 × 5.0 = 2.1 cm and 0.46 × 5.0 = 2.3 cm [1]; difference = 0.2 cm [1].

**(b)** Any one: use a different mobile phase in which the Rf values differ more; use a longer plate
so the solvent travels further [1].

**9. (a)** The product is impure: it still contains some unreacted starting material (and at least one
other compound) [1].

**(b)** A single spot in the product lane, at a different Rf from the starting material [1].

**10.** The surface of aluminium oxide is polar [1]; –OH groups form hydrogen bonds (strong
attractions) with it, so the compounds are strongly adsorbed [1].

**11.** **U** and **V** are different compounds [1]: their Rf values match in solvent 1 by chance, but
differ in solvent 2; if they were the same compound, their Rf values would match in every solvent
[1].

**12.** The spot travels a fixed fraction of the solvent's distance under the same conditions, so
both distances increase in proportion and their ratio is unchanged [1].

---

## Where marks are usually lost

- Measuring from the bottom edge of the plate instead of the baseline.
- Dividing the solvent distance by the spot distance, giving an Rf above 1.
- Writing "the same Rf, so they are definitely the same compound".
- Explaining Rf with only one factor: give both the stationary-phase interaction and the solubility in
  the mobile phase.
- Assuming polar compounds always have low Rf values, regardless of the solvent.
- Calling a single spot proof of purity: it shows only that no other component separated under those
  conditions.
