## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student titrates 25.0 cm³ of aqueous sodium hydroxide with dilute hydrochloric acid, using methyl orange indicator.

**(a)** Name the apparatus used to measure the 25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide. **[1]**

**(b)** State the colour change at the end-point. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the flask is placed on a white tile. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why the acid is added drop by drop near the end-point. **[1]**

**8.** Describe how to carry out a titration to find the volume of dilute nitric acid needed to neutralise 25.0 cm³ of aqueous potassium hydroxide. Your answer should include the apparatus used and how the end-point is found. **[5]**

**9.** The table shows a student's titration results.

| | Rough | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Final reading / cm³ | 21.30 | 21.10 | 41.85 | 21.40 |
| Initial reading / cm³ | 0.00 | 0.35 | 21.10 | 0.70 |
| Titre / cm³ | 21.30 | ...... | ...... | ...... |

**(a)** Complete the titre row. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the concordant results and calculate the mean titre. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the rough titre is not used to calculate the mean. **[1]**

**10.** A student makes these errors. For each, state the effect on the titre measured (larger, smaller or no change) and explain.

**(a)** The burette is rinsed with water but not with acid before filling with acid. **[2]**

**(b)** The conical flask is rinsed with distilled water before the alkali is pipetted into it. **[2]**

**11.** A student wants to make pure crystals of sodium chloride from hydrochloric acid and aqueous sodium hydroxide.

**(a)** Explain why the student first does a titration with an indicator. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe what the student does next to obtain sodium chloride solution that is not contaminated with indicator. **[1]**

**12.** *(Extended)* 25.0 cm³ of aqueous potassium hydroxide is neutralised by 18.40 cm³ of 0.150 mol/dm³ nitric acid.

HNO₃ + KOH → KNO₃ + H₂O

Calculate the concentration of the potassium hydroxide in mol/dm³. **[3]**

**13.** *(Extended)* 25.0 cm³ of 0.120 mol/dm³ sodium hydroxide is neutralised by 15.00 cm³ of dilute sulfuric acid.

H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O

Calculate the concentration of the sulfuric acid. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Burette [1].

**2.** **C** [1]. Universal indicator changes colour gradually, so there is no sharp end-point.

**3.** **(a)** Blue [1].

**(b)** Colourless [1].

**4.** It can be swirled without the solution spilling [1].

**5.** The bottom of the meniscus [1].

**6.** The point at which the indicator just changes colour (permanently), showing the acid and alkali have exactly reacted [1].

**7.** **(a)** Volumetric pipette (with a pipette filler) [1].

**(b)** From yellow [1] to orange [1]. *Methyl orange turns red only once excess acid has been added, which is past the end-point.*

**(c)** To make the colour change easier to see [1].

**(d)** So that the end-point is not overshot / the exact volume is found [1].

**8.** Any five [5]:

- use a volumetric pipette (and pipette filler) to put 25.0 cm³ of potassium hydroxide into a conical flask;
- add a few drops of a suitable indicator, such as thymolphthalein or methyl orange;
- fill a burette with nitric acid (after rinsing it with the acid) and record the initial reading;
- add acid from the burette while swirling the flask;
- add acid drop by drop near the end-point;
- stop when the indicator changes colour permanently (e.g. thymolphthalein blue to colourless) and record the final reading;
- repeat until concordant titres are obtained and calculate the mean.

**9.** **(a)** Run 1: 20.75; Run 2: 20.75; Run 3: 20.70 [2]. Award one mark if only two titres are correct.
*Run 1: 21.10 − 0.35; Run 2: 41.85 − 21.10; Run 3: 21.40 − 0.70.*

**(b)** All three accurate titres are concordant (within 0.10 cm³) [1]. Mean = (20.75 + 20.75 + 20.70) ÷ 3 = 20.73 cm³ [1].
*20.733... rounds to 20.73 cm³.*

**(c)** The rough titration is done quickly and usually overshoots the end-point, so it is less accurate [1].

**10.** **(a)** Larger [1]: the water dilutes the acid, so more acid solution is needed to neutralise the same amount of alkali [1].

**(b)** No change [1]: the pipette still delivers the same amount (moles) of alkali; extra water does not change the amount of alkali to be neutralised [1].

**11.** **(a)** To find the exact volume of acid needed to neutralise the alkali [1].

**(b)** Repeat the titration with the same volumes but without the indicator (then evaporate to crystallise) [1].

**12.** Moles of HNO₃ = 0.150 × (18.40 ÷ 1000) = 0.00276 mol [1].
Moles of KOH = 0.00276 mol (1 : 1 ratio) [1].
Concentration of KOH = 0.00276 ÷ 0.0250 = **0.110 mol/dm³** [1].

**13.** Moles of NaOH = 0.120 × (25.0 ÷ 1000) = 0.00300 mol [1].
Moles of H₂SO₄ = 0.00300 ÷ 2 = 0.00150 mol [1].
Concentration of H₂SO₄ = 0.00150 ÷ (15.00 ÷ 1000) = **0.100 mol/dm³** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "pipette" or "measuring cylinder" instead of "volumetric pipette".
- Giving a single colour for the end-point, or the colours in the wrong order.
- Choosing universal indicator.
- Including the rough titre or an outlier in the mean.
- Recording burette readings to one decimal place instead of two.
- *(Extended)* Forgetting to divide volumes in cm³ by 1000, or assuming every reaction is 1 : 1.
