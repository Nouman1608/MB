## Section A

**1.** State **two** reasons why a small trader may keep incomplete records. **[2]**

**2.** At 1 January 2026 a trader had a van 9,500, inventory 3,800, trade receivables 2,200, bank 1,450 and cash 120. She owed trade payables of 1,900 and a loan of 4,000. Calculate her capital at that date. **[3]**

**3.** Her capital at 31 December 2026 was 14,900. During the year she took drawings of 9,600 and introduced 2,000 of her own money. Using the capital at 1 January 2026 from Question 2, calculate her profit for the year. **[3]**

**4.** A business adds a mark-up of 40% to cost. Cost of sales for the year was 21,000. Calculate revenue and the gross profit margin (to one decimal place). **[3]**

**5.** A trader has opening inventory of 5,400, purchases of 38,600 and revenue of 52,000, earning a gross profit margin of 25%. A flood destroyed most of the inventory, and goods worth 800 were saved. Calculate the cost of the inventory lost. **[4]**

**6.** Trade receivables were 2,200 at the start of the year and 2,900 at the end. Customers paid 31,400, discount allowed was 300 and irrecoverable debts written off were 150. Calculate credit sales. **[4]**

**7.** Trade payables were 1,900 at the start of the year and 2,100 at the end. Payments to suppliers were 22,700 and discount received was 250. Calculate credit purchases. **[3]**

## Section B

**8.** Bright Bikes is owned by Layla Ahmed. She does not keep full records. At 1 July 2025 the business had workshop equipment 12,000, inventory 6,300, trade receivables 3,500 and bank 2,800. It owed trade payables of 2,400.

The summary of the bank statements for the year ended 30 June 2026 shows:

| Receipts | $ | Payments | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Customers (cheques) | 64,800 | Suppliers | 38,500 |
| Cash sales paid in | 9,600 | Wages | 12,400 |
| | | Rent | 6,000 |
| | | Drawings | 8,000 |

Other information at 30 June 2026:

- trade receivables 4,200; trade payables 3,100; inventory 7,100
- discount allowed 350; discount received 400
- a debt of 250 was written off as irrecoverable
- wages of 500 are owing; rent of 500 was paid in advance
- equipment is depreciated at 10% of its opening value
- Layla took goods costing 450 for her own use

**(a)** Calculate Layla's capital at 1 July 2025. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the bank balance at 30 June 2026. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate credit sales and total revenue. **[4]**

**(d)** Calculate credit purchases. **[3]**

**(e)** Prepare the statement of profit or loss for the year ended 30 June 2026. **[9]**

**(f)** Prepare the statement of financial position at 30 June 2026. **[8]**

**9.** Explain why the net assets method of finding profit cannot show gross profit. **[2]**

**10.** Explain **two** benefits to a business of changing from incomplete records to a full double-entry system. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: the business is small and the owner lacks bookkeeping skills [1]; the owner thinks the cost or time of full records is not worth it [1]; records were lost or destroyed [1]; no legal requirement forces the owner to keep full records [1].

**2.** Assets 9,500 + 3,800 + 2,200 + 1,450 + 120 = 17,070 [1]; liabilities 1,900 + 4,000 = 5,900 [1]; capital 17,070 − 5,900 = **11,170** [1].

**3.** Profit = closing capital − opening capital + drawings − capital introduced [1] = 14,900 − 11,170 + 9,600 − 2,000 [1] = **11,330** [1].

**4.** Gross profit 21,000 × 40% = 8,400 [1]; revenue 21,000 + 8,400 = **29,400** [1]; margin 8,400 ÷ 29,400 × 100 = **28.6%** [1].

**5.** Gross profit 52,000 × 25% = 13,000 and cost of sales 52,000 − 13,000 = 39,000 [1]; goods available 5,400 + 38,600 = 44,000 [1]; expected closing inventory 44,000 − 39,000 = 5,000 [1]; loss 5,000 − 800 = **4,200** [1].

**6.** Receivables account: debit side opening 2,200 plus credit sales; credit side 31,400 + 300 + 150 + 2,900 = 34,750 [2]; credit sales = 34,750 − 2,200 [1] = **32,550** [1].

**7.** Credit side opening 1,900 plus purchases; debit side 22,700 + 250 + 2,100 = 25,050 [1]; purchases 25,050 − 1,900 [1] = **23,150** [1].

**8. (a)** Assets 12,000 + 6,300 + 3,500 + 2,800 = 24,600 [1]; less trade payables 2,400 = **22,200** [1].

**(b)** Receipts 2,800 + 64,800 + 9,600 = 77,200; payments 38,500 + 12,400 + 6,000 + 8,000 = 64,900 [1]; balance **12,300** [1].

**(c)** Receivables account: debits opening 3,500 plus credit sales; credits 64,800 + 350 + 250 + 4,200 = 69,600 [1 for the credits total]; credit sales 69,600 − 3,500 = **66,100** [1]; total revenue 66,100 + 9,600 = **75,700** [2, with 1 for adding cash sales].

**(d)** Payables account: debits 38,500 + 400 + 3,100 = 42,000 [1]; less opening 2,400 [1]; credit purchases **39,600** [1].

**9.** The method uses only the change in capital [1], so it gives a single figure for profit and no information about revenue, cost of sales or expenses [1].

**10.** Any two benefits with a development, 2 marks each: a trial balance checks the accuracy of the records [1], so errors are found and corrected [1]; reliable figures are available for tax, loans or a sale of the business [1], because outsiders trust properly kept records [1]; the owner can monitor performance during the year [1], for example by comparing gross profit with last year [1]; control over receivables and payables improves [1], because every balance is recorded [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating cheques from customers as sales (Question 8).
- Leaving out opening balances in the total accounts (Questions 6, 7 and 8).
- Treating goods taken by the owner as an expense instead of drawings.
- Dividing by cost when asked for the margin (Question 4).
- Failing to check that net assets equal closing capital.

## Approaching these questions

Start with opening capital. Then summarise the bank, build the receivables and payables accounts and write "balancing figure" beside each result. Adjust for accruals, prepayments, depreciation and goods taken. Finish with the statements and check that net assets equal closing capital.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these Edexcel YAC11 questions from past papers?**
No. They are original questions on outcome 1.3.8, written for revision. Use them alongside the official past papers.

**What if the statement of financial position does not balance?**
Check that every bank, cash, receivables and payables figure is in the statement, that drawings include goods taken, and that opening capital was calculated after deducting all liabilities.
