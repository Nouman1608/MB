## Questions

**1.** Factorise fully.

**(a)** 7x − 21 **[1]**
**(b)** 6y² + 15y **[2]**
**(c)** 12ab² − 8a²b **[2]**

**2.** Factorise.

**(a)** x² + 8x + 15 **[2]**
**(b)** x² − 3x − 28 **[2]**
**(c)** x² − 12x + 35 **[2]**

**3.**

**(a)** Factorise x² − 121. **[1]**
**(b)** Factorise 16a² − 9b². **[2]**
**(c)** Factorise fully 5x² − 45. **[2]**
**(d)** Write x² − 11 as a product of two brackets. **[1]**

**4.** A rectangular lawn has area (x² + 9x + 14) m² and width (x + 2) m.

**(a)** Factorise x² + 9x + 14 and write down the length of the lawn in terms of x. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that the perimeter of the lawn is (4x + 18) m. **[2]**

**5.** (Higher tier only) Factorise.

**(a)** 2x² + 9x + 10 **[2]**
**(b)** 5x² + 17x + 6 **[2]**
**(c)** 8x² + 10x − 3 **[2]**

**6.** (Higher tier only) Factorise fully 18x² − 3x − 6. **[3]**

**7.** (Higher tier only) Write each expression in the form (x + p)² + q.

**(a)** x² + 12x + 5 **[2]**
**(b)** x² − 9x + 3 **[2]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) Write each expression in the form a(x + p)² + q.

**(a)** 4x² − 16x + 9 **[3]**
**(b)** 2x² + 3x − 4 **[3]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) Simplify.

**(a)** (x² − 25)/(x² + 2x − 15) **[3]**
**(b)** (3x² + x − 10)/(x² − 4) **[3]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) Write as a single fraction, simplifying your numerator.

**(a)** 3/(x + 1) + 4/(x − 2) **[3]**
**(b)** 5/(2x − 1) − 2/x **[3]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) Simplify fully [(x² + 4x)/(x² − x − 2)] × [(x + 1)/(x² − 16)]. **[4]**

**12.** (Higher tier only)

**(a)** Show that (x² + 3x − 10)/(x² − 4) + 3/(x + 2) simplifies to (x + 8)/(x + 2). **[4]**
**(b)** Write (x + 8)/(x + 2) in the form 1 + k/(x + 2), where k is an integer. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **7(x − 3)** [1]
**(b)** Common factor 3y [1]; **3y(2y + 5)** [1]
**(c)** Common factor 4ab [1]; **4ab(3b − 2a)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In part (c) the number and each letter must be taken out. 2ab(6b − 4a) is only partly factorised, so check for a common factor left in the bracket.

**2. (a)** Numbers with product 15 and sum 8: 3 and 5 [1]; **(x + 3)(x + 5)** [1]
**(b)** Product −28 and sum −3: 4 and −7 [1]; **(x − 7)(x + 4)** [1]
**(c)** Product 35 and sum −12, so both negative: −5 and −7 [1]; **(x − 5)(x − 7)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Finding the right pair is the main step, so write the pair down. Writing the pair down also makes it easy to spot where a slip happened.

**3. (a)** **(x − 11)(x + 11)** [1]
**(b)** 16a² = (4a)² and 9b² = (3b)² [1]; **(4a − 3b)(4a + 3b)** [1]
**(c)** Take out 5: 5(x² − 9) [1]; **5(x − 3)(x + 3)** [1]
**(d)** 11 = (√11)², so **(x − √11)(x + √11)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) is a common trap. Looking for a difference of two squares before taking out the common factor can leave you stuck, so check for a common factor first.

**4. (a)** x² + 9x + 14 = **(x + 2)(x + 7)** [1]; length = **(x + 7) m** [1]
**(b)** Perimeter = 2(x + 2) + 2(x + 7) [1]; = 2x + 4 + 2x + 14 = **4x + 18** [1]
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" question write every line of the algebra, including the unsimplified perimeter, and finish on the given result.

**5. (a)** ac = 20, numbers 4 and 5 [1]; 2x² + 4x + 5x + 10 = 2x(x + 2) + 5(x + 2) = **(2x + 5)(x + 2)** [1]
**(b)** ac = 30, numbers 15 and 2 [1]; 5x² + 15x + 2x + 6 = 5x(x + 3) + 2(x + 3) = **(5x + 2)(x + 3)** [1]
**(c)** ac = −24, numbers 12 and −2 [1]; 8x² + 12x − 2x − 3 = 4x(2x + 3) − (2x + 3) = **(4x − 1)(2x + 3)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* If the two brackets after splitting do not match, a sign or a number pair is wrong. Expanding your answer is a quick way to catch this.

**6.** Common factor 3: 3(6x² − x − 2) [1]; ac = −12, numbers −4 and 3: 6x² − 4x + 3x − 2 = 2x(3x − 2) + (3x − 2) [1]; **3(3x − 2)(2x + 1)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Fully" signals a common factor. Keep the 3 in front of your final answer, since dropping it changes the expression.

**7. (a)** (x + 6)² = x² + 12x + 36 [1]; **(x + 6)² − 31** [1]
**(b)** (x − 9/2)² = x² − 9x + 81/4 [1]; 3 − 81/4 = **(x − 9/2)² − 69/4** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The sign inside the bracket matches the sign of the x coefficient halved, and the bracket's constant is subtracted. Expand to check.

**8. (a)** 4(x² − 4x) + 9 [1]; = 4[(x − 2)² − 4] + 9 [1]; **4(x − 2)² − 7** [1]
**(b)** 2(x² + 3x/2) − 4 [1]; = 2[(x + 3/4)² − 9/16] − 4 [1]; **2(x + 3/4)² − 41/8** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Multiplying the subtracted square by the outside factor is an easy step to miss. Writing the bracket in square brackets before multiplying out helps.

**9. (a)** Numerator (x − 5)(x + 5) [1]; denominator (x + 5)(x − 3) [1]; **(x − 5)/(x − 3)** [1]
**(b)** Numerator (3x − 5)(x + 2) [1]; denominator (x − 2)(x + 2) [1]; **(3x − 5)/(x − 2)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Factorise top and bottom on separate lines before cancelling. Cancelling the x or the 5 directly is not allowed, so factorise first.

**10. (a)** Common denominator (x + 1)(x − 2) [1]; numerator 3(x − 2) + 4(x + 1) [1]; **(7x − 2)/[(x + 1)(x − 2)]** [1]
**(b)** Common denominator x(2x − 1) [1]; numerator 5x − 2(2x − 1) = 5x − 4x + 2 [1]; **(x + 2)/[x(2x − 1)]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In part (b) the minus sign applies to the whole of 2(2x − 1). Writing the bracket stops the sign error.

**11.** (x² + 4x) = x(x + 4); x² − x − 2 = (x − 2)(x + 1); x² − 16 = (x − 4)(x + 4) [2]; product = x(x + 4)(x + 1) / [(x − 2)(x + 1)(x − 4)(x + 4)] [1]; **x/[(x − 2)(x − 4)]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Factorising all three quadratics first is the efficient route. Multiplying out the numerators and denominators makes cancelling much harder.

**12. (a)** Numerator x² + 3x − 10 = (x + 5)(x − 2) [1]; denominator x² − 4 = (x − 2)(x + 2) [1]; fraction simplifies to (x + 5)/(x + 2) [1]; (x + 5)/(x + 2) + 3/(x + 2) = **(x + 8)/(x + 2)** [1]
**(b)** x + 8 = (x + 2) + 6 [1]; **1 + 6/(x + 2)**, so k = **6** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A fraction with the same denominator is added by adding numerators only. Keep the denominator as (x + 2) and do not add it twice.

## How to check your answers

- **Factorising:** expand your brackets. You should get the original expression back, with every term and sign.
- **Completing the square:** expand the answer. For (x + p)² + q the x term is 2p and the constant is p² + q. With a factor a outside, multiply both by a.
- **Algebraic fractions:** substitute a simple value such as x = 1 or x = 2 into the question and your answer. Both should give the same number, as long as that value does not make a denominator zero.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving a common factor outside the final brackets.
- Using a number pair that adds correctly but does not multiply correctly.
- Sign errors when c is negative.
- Not expanding to check a factorisation.
- Cancelling terms in an algebraic fraction instead of factors.
- Losing a sign when subtracting one algebraic fraction from another.
- Forgetting to multiply the subtracted square by a when completing the square.
- Giving q with the wrong sign in (x + p)² + q.
- Not showing the intermediate line in a "show that" question.
- Stopping at a single bracket pair when a numerical factor, such as the 5 in question 3(c) or the 3 in question 6, is still outside.
