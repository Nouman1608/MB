**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **AS Level** subtopic
13.4: structural isomerism (chain, positional, functional group) and stereoisomerism (cis/trans and
optical), including chiral centres in cyclic compounds and deducing isomers from a molecular formula.

## The classification

| Type | Same | Different | Example pair |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chain | molecular formula, functional group | carbon skeleton | butane / methylpropane |
| Positional | formula, skeleton, functional group | position of the group | propan-1-ol / propan-2-ol |
| Functional group | molecular formula | functional group | ethanoic acid / methyl methanoate (C₂H₄O₂) |
| Cis/trans | structural formula | groups on same or opposite side of C=C or ring | cis- / trans-pent-2-ene |
| Optical | structural formula | non-superimposable mirror-image arrangement | the two enantiomers of 2-chlorobutane |

## Formula clues for functional group isomers

| Formula type | Possible classes |
|---|---|
| CₙH₂ₙ | alkene or cycloalkane |
| CₙH₂ₙO (one O) | aldehyde or ketone (also unsaturated alcohols) |
| CₙH₂ₙO₂ | carboxylic acid or ester |
| CₙH₂ₙ₊₂O | alcohol (or ether) |

## Cis/trans checklist

- There must be **restricted rotation**: a C=C (the π bond would break on rotation) or a ring.
- **Each** of the two carbons must carry two **different** groups.
- cis = the two similar groups (for example the two CH₃ groups in but-2-ene) on the **same side**;
  trans = on **opposite sides**.
- E/Z names are acceptable for 9701 but not required.
- Any carbon of the C=C carrying two H atoms (a =CH₂ end) rules cis/trans out.

## Chiral centre checklist

- A **chiral centre** is a carbon bonded to **four different** atoms or groups.
- It gives **two enantiomers**: non-superimposable mirror images.
- Compare whole groups: C₂H₅ and C₃H₇ are different; two CH₃ groups are not.
- No carbon in a C=C, C=O or C≡N can be a chiral centre (it has only three or two neighbours).
- CH₂ and CH₃ carbons are never chiral (two or three identical H atoms).
- In rings, follow the two ring paths in both directions; if they differ, they count as two different
  groups.
- A molecule may have more than one chiral centre; meso compounds and diastereoisomers are not required.

## Isomer counts worth knowing

| Formula | Structural isomers | Extra stereoisomers |
|---|---|---|
| C₄H₁₀ | 2 (butane, methylpropane) | none |
| C₅H₁₂ | 3 (pentane, 2-methylbutane, 2,2-dimethylpropane) | none |
| C₄H₉Br | 4 (1-bromobutane, 2-bromobutane, 1-bromo-2-methylpropane, 2-bromo-2-methylpropane) | 2-bromobutane is chiral, so 5 isomers in total |
| C₄H₁₀O alcohols | 4 (butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-1-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol) | butan-2-ol is chiral, so 5 alcohols in total |
| C₄H₈ | 5 (but-1-ene, but-2-ene, 2-methylpropene, cyclobutane, methylcyclopropane) | but-2-ene is cis/trans, so 6 in total |

## Systematic method for "deduce the isomers"

1. Work out what classes the formula allows (table above).
2. Longest chain first, then shorten by one carbon and add branches.
3. For each skeleton, move the functional group to every distinct position.
4. Check for duplicates by naming each one (lowest locants).
5. Test every structure for cis/trans and for chiral centres.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Isomers | compounds with the same molecular formula but different arrangements of atoms |
| Structural isomers | same molecular formula, different structural formula |
| Stereoisomers | same structural formula, different spatial arrangement |
| Restricted rotation | no rotation about C=C because the π bond would break |
| Chiral centre | carbon atom attached to four different atoms or groups |
| Enantiomers | pair of optical isomers that are non-superimposable mirror images |

## Exam traps

- Explaining cis/trans with "double bonds cannot rotate" without mentioning the **π bond** loses the mark.
- Pent-1-ene, hex-1-ene and every other alk-1-ene have **no** cis/trans isomers.
- Pentan-3-ol, CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH₃, has **no** chiral centre (two ethyl groups).
- Draw enantiomers in 3D (wedge and dash) and as **mirror images**; two flat drawings earn nothing.
- Name isomers carefully: "1-methylbutane" is pentane, and "3-methylbutane" is 2-methylbutane.
- Include **cyclic** isomers for CₙH₂ₙ formulae.

## Self-test

1. Define structural isomerism.
2. Name the type of isomerism shown by pentan-2-one and pentan-3-one.
3. Name the type shown by propanoic acid and methyl ethanoate.
4. Explain why but-2-ene has two geometrical isomers.
5. Explain why but-1-ene does not show cis/trans isomerism.
6. Define the term chiral centre.
7. Does 2-chloropropane have a chiral centre? Explain.
8. Identify the chiral centre in 2-hydroxypropanoic acid, CH₃CH(OH)COOH.
9. How many structural isomers have the formula C₄H₉Cl? Which of them shows optical isomerism?
10. Draw or describe the structures of cis- and trans-1,2-dichloroethene.
11. Why can a ring compound such as 1,2-dimethylcyclohexane show cis/trans isomerism without a C=C?
12. How many stereoisomers does pent-3-en-2-ol, CH₃CH(OH)CH=CHCH₃, have?

**Answers:**

1. Compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural formulae.
2. Positional isomerism.
3. Functional group isomerism.
4. Rotation about C=C is restricted because it would break the π bond, and each C=C carbon carries two
   different groups (H and CH₃), so the CH₃ groups can be on the same side (cis) or opposite sides
   (trans).
5. The first carbon of the double bond carries two identical groups (two H atoms).
6. A carbon atom bonded to four different atoms or groups.
7. No: C2 carries H, Cl and two identical CH₃ groups.
8. C2, the carbon carrying H, OH, CH₃ and COOH.
9. Four: 1-chlorobutane, 2-chlorobutane, 1-chloro-2-methylpropane and 2-chloro-2-methylpropane. Only
   2-chlorobutane is chiral.
10. ClCH=CHCl: cis has both Cl atoms on the same side of the C=C; trans has them on opposite sides.
11. The ring stops rotation about the ring C–C bonds, so the two methyl groups are fixed on the same face
    (cis) or opposite faces (trans).
12. Four: cis and trans forms, each with two enantiomers from the chiral C2.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need E/Z names for Cambridge 9701?**
No. The syllabus accepts E/Z but only requires cis/trans, so cis/trans with a correct diagram earns
full credit.

**Do I need to explain how enantiomers affect plane-polarised light at AS Level?**
No. At AS you identify chiral centres and state that they give two enantiomers. Optical activity and
racemic mixtures belong to the A Level isomerism subtopic.
