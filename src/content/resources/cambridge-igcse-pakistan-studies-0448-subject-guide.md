Cambridge IGCSE Pakistan Studies (0448) brings together two quite
different disciplines under one qualification: the history and culture
of Pakistan, and the physical and human geography of the country. It
suits students who want to understand how Pakistan came into being and
how its land, resources and economy shape life there today. The course
is assessed by two written papers, each lasting 90 minutes, carrying 75
marks and worth 50% of the final grade. This overview covers the 2026
syllabus edition.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus is split cleanly in two, and each half maps directly onto
one exam paper.

**Paper 1, The History and Culture of Pakistan**, follows a broadly
chronological path through three sections:

- *Cultural and Historical Background to the Pakistan Movement* sets out
  the religious, cultural and political background from which the idea
  of a separate homeland grew.
- *The Emergence of Pakistan, 1906–47* traces the political campaign and
  the key events, personalities and negotiations that led to
  independence.
- *Nationhood, 1947–99* looks at how the new state developed in its
  first half-century, from the problems of partition onwards.

**Paper 2, The Environment of Pakistan**, is a geography course built
around eight sections:

- *The land of Pakistan*, covering the physical setting
- *Natural resources – an issue of sustainability*
- *Power*
- *Agricultural development*
- *Industrial development*
- *Trade*
- *Transport and telecommunications*
- *Population and employment*

The environment half starts with the physical land and its resources,
moves through the productive economy (power, farming and industry),
then widens out to links with the outside world (trade, transport and
telecommunications) before ending with the people themselves.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | The history and culture of Pakistan | 90 minutes | 75 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | The environment of Pakistan | 90 minutes | 75 | 50% |

Both papers are written examinations, both are externally assessed, and
the course is not tiered, so every candidate sits the same two papers.
Assessment is linear: both papers are taken at the end of the course.

Paper 1 has two sections. Section A is a compulsory source-based
question worth 25 marks; Section B carries the remaining 50 marks. Paper
2 asks candidates to answer three of five questions, each worth 25
marks. The syllabus is examined in the June series only; there is no
November series for this IGCSE variant, which matters when you are
planning a timetable or thinking about when you could re-sit.

## How the parts fit together

Although the papers are assessed separately and carry equal weight, the
two halves of the course inform each other more than first appears.
Decisions taken during the *Nationhood, 1947–99* period, such as
priorities for farming, industry and power generation, are the
historical background to many of the patterns studied in Paper 2. In
the other direction, knowing where Pakistan's rivers, fertile plains and
mineral resources lie gives real substance to historical discussion of
partition and early development.

The skills differ, though. Paper 1 rewards explanation of causes and
consequences, a secure sense of chronology and, in Section A, the
ability to read and interpret sources. Paper 2 rewards accurate use of
geographical terms, interpretation of maps, graphs and data, and
balanced discussion of issues such as sustainability. Because the
weighting is split evenly, neither half can be treated as a "lighter"
paper.

The Paper 2 sections also connect closely with one another. Power
supply affects industrial development; agricultural output feeds both
industry and trade; transport links underpin all of them; and
population and employment pull the economic sections together. Studying
those links, rather than treating each section as a separate list of
facts, prepares you for questions that ask you to explain or evaluate.

## Planning your study

Many schools teach the two halves in parallel, often with different
teachers. If you are planning your own order, a sensible approach is:

1. Begin Paper 1 with the background to the Pakistan Movement, since
   the later sections depend on it.
2. Start Paper 2 with *The land of Pakistan* and *Natural resources*,
   which give the physical framework for every later section.
3. Work through the Pakistan Movement to 1947 alongside power,
   agriculture and industry.
4. Finish with *Nationhood, 1947–99* and with trade, transport and
   telecommunications, then population and employment, which draw on
   everything before them.

In general terms, aim to complete first coverage of both papers well
before the final term, leaving time for consolidation. Begin short
timed questions, particularly source-based work and 25-mark Paper 2
questions, once each section is taught, rather than saving all exam
practice for the end. Full paper-by-paper exam preparation belongs in
the last stretch before the June series.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge IGCSE Pakistan Studies 0448?**
There are two written papers: Paper 1, The history and culture of Pakistan, and Paper 2, The environment of Pakistan. Each lasts 90 minutes, carries 75 marks and is worth 50% of the qualification.

**Is IGCSE Pakistan Studies tiered?**
No. The course is not tiered, so all candidates take the same two papers.

**What is in Section A of Paper 1?**
Section A is a compulsory source-based question worth 25 marks. Section B makes up the other 50 marks of the paper.

**How many questions do I answer in Paper 2?**
You answer three of the five questions set, and each question is worth 25 marks.

**Can I take 0448 in November?**
No. This syllabus is examined in the June series only; there is no November series for this IGCSE variant.
