These notes condense section 2.4, Ion and ionic bonds, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). The full explanations and worked dot-and-cross examples are in the [ion and ionic bonds study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-ion-and-ionic-bonds/); the [ion and ionic bonds practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-ion-and-ionic-bonds-practice/) give exam-style practice.

## Must-know statements

- **Cation**: positive ion, formed when a metal atom **loses** electrons.
- **Anion**: negative ion, formed when a non-metal atom **gains** electrons.
- **Ionic bond**: a strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.
- **Ionic lattice**: a giant, regular arrangement of alternating positive and negative ions.
- Ionic bonds form between **metals** and **non-metals**, by **electron transfer** from metal to non-metal.

## Ion charges from the Periodic Table

| Group | I | II | III | V | VI | VII |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Electrons lost or gained | lose 1 | lose 2 | lose 3 | gain 3 | gain 2 | gain 1 |
| Charge | 1+ | 2+ | 3+ | 3− | 2− | 1− |
| Examples | Li⁺, Na⁺, K⁺ | Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺ | Al³⁺ | N³⁻ | O²⁻, S²⁻ | F⁻, Cl⁻, Br⁻ |

Groups IV and VIII do not normally form simple ions at this level.

## Balancing charges: ratios to know

| Compound | Ions | Ratio | Formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| Potassium bromide | K⁺, Br⁻ | 1 : 1 | KBr |
| Calcium oxide | Ca²⁺, O²⁻ | 1 : 1 | CaO |
| Magnesium chloride | Mg²⁺, Cl⁻ | 1 : 2 | MgCl₂ |
| Sodium sulfide | Na⁺, S²⁻ | 2 : 1 | Na₂S |
| Aluminium fluoride | Al³⁺, F⁻ | 1 : 3 | AlF₃ |
| Aluminium oxide | Al³⁺, O²⁻ | 2 : 3 | Al₂O₃ |
| Magnesium nitride | Mg²⁺, N³⁻ | 3 : 2 | Mg₃N₂ |

Rule: total positive charge = total negative charge. Quick method: swap the charge numbers to become the other ion's subscript, then cancel to the simplest ratio (Ca²⁺ and O²⁻ gives Ca₂O₂, which cancels to CaO).

## Dot-and-cross checklist

1. Write each atom's configuration and decide electrons lost or gained.
2. Choose the number of each ion so the charges balance.
3. Draw each ion in **square brackets** with its **charge** outside, top right.
4. Show outer shells: metal ion with no outer electrons (or its new full outer shell); non-metal ion with 8 electrons, own electrons as one symbol, gained electrons as the other.
5. Count: electrons lost by all metal atoms = electrons gained by all non-metal atoms.

Example, sodium sulfide: two Na atoms each give one electron to one S atom. Draw [Na]⁺ twice and [S]²⁻ once, with 6 crosses and 2 dots on the sulfide ion.

## Properties explained

| Property | Description | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Melting / boiling point | high; solids at room temperature | giant lattice with **many strong electrostatic attractions** between oppositely charged ions; **much energy** needed to overcome them |
| Conductivity: solid | does not conduct | ions held in **fixed positions**; cannot move |
| Conductivity: molten | conducts | ions **free to move** and carry charge |
| Conductivity: aqueous | conducts | ions **free to move** and carry charge |

Higher ion charges → stronger attractions → higher melting point (e.g. CaO melts far higher than KF).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ion | atom or group of atoms with an overall charge from losing or gaining electrons |
| Cation | positive ion |
| Anion | negative ion |
| Electrostatic attraction | force between opposite charges |
| Giant lattice | huge, regular 3D arrangement of particles with no separate molecules |
| Dot-and-cross diagram | diagram showing electrons from different atoms as dots and crosses |
| Electron transfer | movement of electrons from a metal atom to a non-metal atom |
| -ide | ending for a simple non-metal anion: oxide, chloride, nitride |

## Exam traps

- Conductivity in ionic compounds is by moving **ions**, never electrons.
- The bond is the **attraction**, not the transfer.
- Charges go **outside** the brackets; one bracket per ion.
- Draw the right **number** of each ion (two Na⁺ for Na₂S).
- "Strong bonds" alone is not enough: mention the giant lattice and many attractions.
- No "molecules" of ionic compounds: the formula is a ratio.

## Self-test

1. Define an ionic bond.
2. What is a cation?
3. Describe the formation of a sulfide ion from a sulfur atom.
4. Give the formula of the compound formed between potassium and oxygen.
5. How many electrons are transferred when one formula unit of aluminium oxide, Al₂O₃, forms?
6. Describe the structure of solid potassium bromide.
7. Why does solid potassium bromide not conduct electricity?
8. Why does aqueous potassium bromide conduct?
9. Explain why magnesium chloride has a high melting point.
10. In a dot-and-cross diagram of magnesium chloride, how many dots and crosses are on each chloride ion if magnesium's electrons are dots?
11. Suggest why calcium oxide has a higher melting point than potassium fluoride.
12. What is the configuration of the aluminium ion?

## Answers

1. A strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.
2. A positive ion.
3. A sulfur atom (2,8,6) gains two electrons, forming S²⁻ (2,8,8).
4. K₂O.
5. 6 (each of 2 Al atoms loses 3; each of 3 O atoms gains 2).
6. A giant lattice: a regular arrangement of alternating K⁺ and Br⁻ ions, held by strong electrostatic attractions.
7. The ions are held in fixed positions and cannot move.
8. The ions are free to move and carry charge through the solution.
9. Its giant lattice contains many strong electrostatic attractions between Mg²⁺ and Cl⁻ ions, which need a lot of energy to overcome.
10. 7 crosses and 1 dot on each of the two chloride ions.
11. Its ions have larger charges (2+ and 2−), so the electrostatic attractions are stronger and need more energy to overcome.
12. 2,8.
