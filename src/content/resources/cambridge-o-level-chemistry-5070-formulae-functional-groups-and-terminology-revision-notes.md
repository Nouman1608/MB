These notes condense section 11.1, Formulae, functional groups and terminology, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). All eight outcomes are required for every candidate, and the vocabulary is used throughout the rest of Topic 11. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [formulae, functional groups and terminology study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-formulae-functional-groups-and-terminology/); then try the [formulae, functional groups and terminology practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-formulae-functional-groups-and-terminology-practice/).

## The eight outcomes in one line each

1. **Structural formula**: an unambiguous description of how the atoms are arranged, e.g. CH₂=CH₂, CH₃CH₂OH, CH₃COOCH₃.
2. **Displayed formula**: draw and interpret, showing all atoms and all bonds.
3. **General formulae**: alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂; alkenes CₙH₂ₙ; alcohols CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH; carboxylic acids CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH.
4. **Structural isomers**: same molecular formula, different structural formulae (C₄H₁₀ and C₄H₈ pairs).
5. **Functional group**: atom or group of atoms that determines the chemical properties of a homologous series.
6. **Homologous series**: same functional group, same general formula, differ by –CH₂–, trend in physical properties, similar chemical properties.
7. **Saturated**: all carbon–carbon bonds single.
8. **Unsaturated**: one or more carbon–carbon bonds not single.

## Formula types compared (propan-1-ol)

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Molecular | C₃H₈O |
| Structural | CH₃CH₂CH₂OH |
| General | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH with n = 3 |
| Displayed | every C–H, C–C, C–O and O–H bond drawn |

## Bonding rules for displayed formulae

| Atom | Bonds |
|---|---|
| C | 4 |
| O | 2 |
| H | 1 |

A double bond counts as two. Always draw the O–H bond.

## General formulae in use

| Series | n = 1 | n = 2 | n = 3 | Mr in terms of n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | CH₄ | C₂H₆ | C₃H₈ | 14n + 2 |
| Alkenes CₙH₂ₙ | (none) | C₂H₄ | C₃H₆ | 14n |
| Alcohols CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH | CH₃OH | C₂H₅OH | C₃H₇OH | 14n + 18 |
| Carboxylic acids CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH | CH₃COOH | C₂H₅COOH | C₃H₇COOH | 14n + 46 |

Ar: C = 12, H = 1, O = 16. The smallest alkene has n = 2, because a C=C bond needs two carbons. For carboxylic acids, n excludes the carbon in –COOH; methanoic acid, HCOOH, is n = 0.

## Converting between formula types

- **Structural to molecular**: count each element. CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃ has 4 C and 10 H, so C₄H₁₀.
- **Molecular to series**: compare with each general formula. C₅H₁₂ fits CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (alkane); C₅H₁₀ fits CₙH₂ₙ (alkene).
- **Mr to formula**: set the Mr expression equal to the value. Alkene with Mr 70: 14n = 70, so n = 5, C₅H₁₀.
- **Structural to displayed**: expand every group. CH₃ means one carbon with three separate C–H bonds drawn.

## Isomer pairs named in the syllabus

| Formula | Isomers | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| C₄H₁₀ | CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ and CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃ | straight against branched chain |
| C₄H₈ | CH₃CH₂CH=CH₂ (but-1-ene) and CH₃CH=CHCH₃ (but-2-ene) | position of C=C |

Test: same molecular formula? Then compare longest chain and positions of branches or groups. A bent drawing of the same chain is the same molecule.

## Functional groups

| Series | Group | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Alkene | C=C | CH₂=CH₂ |
| Alcohol | –OH | CH₃CH₂OH |
| Carboxylic acid | –COOH | CH₃COOH |
| Ester | –COO– | CH₃COOCH₃ |
| Alkane | only single bonds | CH₃CH₃ |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hydrocarbon | compound of hydrogen and carbon only |
| Structural formula | unambiguous description of the arrangement of atoms in a molecule |
| Displayed formula | formula showing all atoms and all bonds |
| General formula | algebraic formula that fits every member of a homologous series |
| Homologous series | family of compounds with the same functional group and general formula |
| Functional group | atom or group of atoms that determines the chemical properties of a series |
| Structural isomers | same molecular formula, different structural formulae |
| Saturated | all carbon–carbon bonds are single |
| Unsaturated | one or more carbon–carbon bonds are not single |

## Exam traps

- "Same formula" is not enough for isomers: say **same molecular formula, different structural formulae**.
- Saturation is about **carbon–carbon** bonds; a C=O bond does not make a compound unsaturated.
- Homologous series have similar **chemical** properties but a **trend** in physical properties.
- In CH₃COOCH₃ the two oxygens are not bonded to each other.
- Count bonds on every carbon in a displayed formula: four, no more, no fewer.
- Ethanoic acid has n = 1 in CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH, not n = 2.

## Self-test

1. Define structural isomers.
2. Write the molecular formula of the alkane with 8 carbon atoms.
3. An alkene has 10 hydrogen atoms. Write its molecular formula.
4. Write the formula of the alcohol with 4 carbon atoms using the general formula.
5. Give the value of n for propanoic acid in CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH.
6. State **two** characteristics of a homologous series.
7. Define a functional group.
8. Is CH₃COOH saturated or unsaturated? Explain.
9. An alkane has Mr = 58. Find its formula.
10. Name the two isomers of C₄H₈ given in the syllabus.
11. What is the difference between a structural formula and a molecular formula?
12. Which series has the functional group –COOH?

## Answers

1. Compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural formulae.
2. C₈H₁₈.
3. C₅H₁₀.
4. C₄H₉OH.
5. n = 2 (C₂H₅COOH).
6. Any two: same functional group; same general formula; differ by –CH₂–; trend in physical properties; similar chemical properties.
7. An atom or group of atoms that determines the chemical properties of a homologous series.
8. Saturated: its only carbon–carbon bond is a single bond (the C=O is not a carbon–carbon bond).
9. 14n + 2 = 58, n = 4, so C₄H₁₀.
10. But-1-ene and but-2-ene.
11. A molecular formula gives only the number of each type of atom; a structural formula shows how the atoms are arranged, without ambiguity.
12. Carboxylic acids.
