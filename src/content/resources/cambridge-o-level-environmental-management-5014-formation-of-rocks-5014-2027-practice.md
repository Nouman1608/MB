## Section A

**1.** Name the type of rock that forms when magma or lava cools. **[1]**

**2.** Define permeability. **[1]**

**3.** State the rock from which each metamorphic rock forms.

(a) Marble

(b) Slate **[2]**

**4.** Identify **two** impermeable rocks from this list: basalt, limestone, sandstone, slate. **[2]**

**5.** State what is meant by weathering, and state how it differs from erosion. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Describe how granite forms. **[3]**

**7.** Describe how sandstone forms from a rock exposed on a mountainside. **[5]**

**8.** Explain why basalt has smaller crystals than granite. **[2]**

**9.** This simplified rock cycle is shown as a list of boxes and labelled arrows.

```
Lava ---P---> Basalt ---Q---> Loose fragments ---R---> Fragments carried by a river
River sediment ---S---> Layers on a sea bed ---T---> Sandstone
```

(a) Name the process at **P**.

(b) Name process **Q**.

(c) Name **two** processes that make up **T**.

(d) Name the process at **S**. **[5]**

**10.** *Invented data.* A student pours 200 cm³ of water onto equal-sized samples of three rocks and
records the volume that passes through in 15 minutes.

| Rock | Volume passing through (cm³) |
|---|---|
| Sandstone | 84 |
| Limestone | 30 |
| Granite | 0 |

(a) Calculate the percentage of water that passed through the limestone.

(b) Explain the result for granite.

(c) Suggest **one** way to make the comparison fair. **[5]**

**11.** Explain how limestone forms and how it can become marble. **[5]**

**12.** A town plans to drill a well for drinking water. Below the town is a layer of sandstone resting on
a layer of shale. Explain why ground water can be stored at this site. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Igneous [1].

**2.** The ability of water to pass through the pore spaces of rock and soil [1].

**3.** (a) Limestone [1]. (b) Shale [1].

**4.** Basalt [1]; slate [1].

**5.** Weathering is the breakdown of rock where it is [1]; erosion is the removal of the broken material,
whereas weathering involves no movement [1].

**6.** Any three:

- Magma rises into the crust but stays deep underground [1].
- It cools very slowly because it is surrounded by hot rock [1].
- Minerals crystallise from the magma [1].
- Slow cooling gives time for large, interlocking crystals to grow [1].

**7.** Any five:

- The rock on the mountainside is weathered, breaking it into fragments and sand grains [1].
- The material is eroded, removed from the slope [1].
- It is transported, for example by a river [1].
- It is deposited when the river loses energy, for example where it enters the sea [1].
- Sedimentation builds up layers of sand [1].
- The weight of layers above compacts the lower layers, squeezing out water [1].
- Minerals in the water crystallise between the grains, cementing them into sandstone [1].

**8.** Basalt forms when lava cools quickly at the surface [1], so crystals have little time to grow,
whereas granite cools slowly underground, giving time for large crystals [1].

**9.** (a) Crystallisation [1]. (b) Weathering [1]. (c) Compaction [1] and cementation [1].
(d) Deposition, or sedimentation [1].

*Tip:* R, which moves the fragments into the river, is erosion followed by transportation. Read what each
arrow joins before naming it.

**10.** (a) 30 / 200 x 100 = **15%** [1 for the working, 1 for the answer].
(b) Granite is made of interlocking crystals [1], so there are almost no pore spaces and water cannot pass
through; it is impermeable [1].
(c) Any one: use samples of the same size or thickness; pour the water at the same rate; measure for the
same length of time; start with dry samples [1].

**11.** Any five:

- Shells and skeletons of sea creatures, made mainly of calcium carbonate, collect on the floor of warm,
  shallow seas [1].
- Layers build up (sedimentation) [1].
- The weight of the layers above compacts them [1].
- Minerals cement the fragments together, forming limestone [1].
- The limestone is later buried or lies near magma and is heated and/or put under pressure [1].
- It recrystallises without melting, forming marble, a metamorphic rock [1].

**12.** Sandstone is permeable, so rain can soak down through its pore spaces [1]. Shale is impermeable,
so water cannot drain any further down [1]. Water therefore collects and is stored in the sandstone above
the shale, forming an aquifer that the well can reach [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying granite cools slowly because it is "in the sea" or "on the surface".
- Leaving out compaction or cementation in a sedimentary sequence.
- Naming the rock type instead of the process for a labelled arrow.
- In 10(a), dividing by 84 or by 100 instead of the 200 cm³ poured in.
- Saying marble forms when limestone melts.
