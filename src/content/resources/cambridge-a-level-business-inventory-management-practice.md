## Questions

**1.** Pellsworth Bakery makes loaves for local shops. Identify one example of each of the three types of inventory it holds. **[3]**

**2.** Define *lead time* and state how a longer lead time affects the re-order level. **[2]**

**3.** Rushwick Lighting uses 36 LED drivers a day. The lead time is 9 days and the buffer inventory is 150 drivers. Each order is for 900 drivers.

**(a)** Calculate the re-order level. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the maximum inventory level, assuming each delivery arrives as inventory reaches the buffer. **[1]**

**4.** Veridane Cycles stocks bicycle frames. Its inventory control chart gives these readings.

| Day | 0 | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frames in inventory | 460 | 380 | 300 | 220 | 140 | 60 |

An order is placed on day 12 and the delivery arrives on day 20.

**(a)** Identify the buffer inventory, the re-order level and the lead time. **[3]**
**(b)** The supplier delivers 3 days late. Calculate the inventory on day 23 and state what this shows. **[3]**

**5.** Analyse **two** costs to a business of holding a large amount of inventory. **[4]**

**6.** Define *Supply Chain Management* and explain **one** benefit of it to a manufacturer. **[3]**

**7.** Distinguish between the purpose of JIT and the purpose of JIC inventory management. **[4]**

**8.** Dunsmere Toys is thinking of moving from JIC to JIT. It buys 180 000 of materials a year (all figures in dollars). Under JIC it holds an average of 4 000 units, costing 1.50 per unit a year to hold. Under JIT it would hold an average of 500 units. JIT would lose a 2% discount on its purchases.

**(a)** Calculate the net annual saving from JIT. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain **one** risk of JIT that the calculation does not show. **[2]**

**9.** Analyse **two** effects on a restaurant chain of adopting JIT for its fresh ingredients. **[6]**

**10.** Rushwick Lighting is a small manufacturer. Demand for its lamps is steady. It buys drivers from one overseas supplier whose delivery times vary between 6 and 14 days. Its warehouse is full and its bank balance is low. The owner is considering JIT. Evaluate whether Rushwick should adopt JIT. **[8]**

**11.** Veridane Cycles (question 4) uses 20 frames a day and holds a buffer of 60 frames. Its supplier is sometimes late, by up to 5 days, about twice a year. Holding one frame for a year costs 4. A lost sale costs Veridane 55 in profit per frame. The owner proposes raising the buffer to 120 frames. Recommend whether she should do so, using calculations. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Raw material: flour (or yeast, sugar) [1]. Work in progress: dough being proved or loaves baked but not yet wrapped [1]. Finished product: wrapped loaves awaiting delivery [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* name each type and give a specific example from the business; a bare list of the three terms shows knowledge but little application.

**2.** Lead time is the delay from the moment an order is placed until the goods arrive from the supplier [1]. A longer lead time means more inventory is used while waiting, so the re-order level must be higher [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* say the direction of the effect and the reason; "it changes" does not answer a "how" question.

**3.** **(a)** Usage during the lead time = 36 x 9 = 324 [1]; re-order level = 324 + 150 = **474 drivers** [1]. **(b)** Maximum = 150 + 900 = **1 050 drivers** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* show the working line; a wrong final figure can still earn credit for a correct method, which a bare answer cannot.

**4.** **(a)** Buffer inventory = **60 frames**, the lowest level, reached on day 20 [1]. Re-order level = **220 frames**, the level at which the order was placed [1]. Lead time = 20 - 12 = **8 days** [1]. **(b)** Usage = (460 - 380) / 4 = 20 frames a day [1]. Delivery would have come on day 20, so on day 23 the inventory would be 60 - (3 x 20) = **0 frames** [1]. The buffer has been used up completely, and any further delay would cause a stock-out [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* read values from the table with their units and say what each one means; interpretation of the result is usually where the final mark sits.

**5.** First cost: storage and insurance, because a large store needs more space, security and cover [1]. This raises fixed costs and lowers profit if the extra inventory is not sold quickly [1]. Second cost: cash tied up, because money spent on inventory cannot pay bills or be invested [1]. This can create cash flow problems, especially for a small business with little cash [1]. (Other valid costs: damage, theft, obsolescence.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* an "analyse" answer goes beyond naming a cost to show its consequence for the business, so develop each point with a "because" or "which leads to" step.

**6.** Supply Chain Management is the coordination of the flow of materials, information and payments between suppliers, the business and its customers [1]. One benefit: closer links with reliable suppliers shorten and stabilise lead times [1], so the manufacturer can hold less buffer inventory and cut holding costs [1]. (Other valid benefits: better quality, faster delivery to customers.) **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* define the term in terms of coordination along the chain, not just "buying from suppliers", and follow the benefit through to a result.

**7.** JIT aims to hold as little inventory as possible, with materials arriving just as they are needed [1], so as to cut holding costs and waste [1]. JIC aims to hold enough inventory, including a large buffer, to meet any unexpected demand or supply delay [1], so as to avoid running out and losing sales or output [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* a "distinguish" answer must make the contrast explicit for both approaches; describing JIT well and JIC vaguely limits the marks.

**8.** **(a)** JIC holding cost = 4 000 x 1.50 = 6 000 [1]. JIT holding cost = 500 x 1.50 = 750, so the holding saving = 5 250 [1]. Discount lost = 2% of 180 000 = 3 600 [1]. Net saving = 5 250 - 3 600 = **1 650 a year** [1]. **(b)** A late delivery under JIT stops production because there is no buffer [1], which could cost far more than the 1 650 saved in lost sales and idle workers [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* the lost discount is the step most often forgotten; the final figure needs a clear statement that it is a saving per year.

**9.** First effect: lower waste and storage cost [1]. Fresh ingredients go off, so ordering small amounts close to use means fewer are thrown away and less refrigeration space is needed [1]; this lifts profit margins [1]. Second effect: greater dependence on suppliers [1]. If a supplier is late, a restaurant may be unable to serve dishes, which loses sales and damages its reputation [1], so the chain needs reliable suppliers and perhaps a second source [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* tie each effect to fresh food and a restaurant; points that would fit any business in any industry gain less credit than ones using the case.

**10.** For JIT: a full warehouse and low cash suggest holding costs are a burden, and JIT would free space and money [1]. Steady demand makes forecasting easier, which supports JIT [1]. Against JIT: one overseas supplier whose delivery times vary from 6 to 14 days is a serious risk, since a late delivery would stop production with no buffer [1]. The firm may also lose bulk discounts and pay for more deliveries [1]. A single source gives little protection [1]. Judgement: JIT is not suitable yet, mainly because supplier unreliability is the biggest risk and the firm cannot easily absorb a stoppage [1]. A better course would be to find a nearer or second supplier, or to reduce the buffer gradually, and to move towards JIT as reliability improves [1]. The decision depends on how dependable the supplier proves to be [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* "evaluate" answers earn their top marks for a justified conclusion that weighs the evidence and says what the decision depends on, not for a longer list of points.

**11.** Cover needed: 5 days x 20 = 100 frames, so the current buffer of 60 covers 60 / 20 = 3 days of delay [1]. A 5-day delay leaves 2 days short, which is 40 frames [1]. Lost profit = 40 x 55 = 2 200 per event, so about 4 400 a year at twice a year [1]. Raising the buffer to 120 covers 6 days, which removes the shortfall [1]. Extra holding cost = 60 x 4 = 240 a year [1]. **Recommend raising the buffer**, since 240 is far lower than 4 400, though cash tied up and the chance of lateness changing should be reviewed [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* a "recommend" answer needs a clear decision supported by the figures; stating the decision without comparing the two costs leaves it unjustified.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving the buffer inventory out of the re-order level.
- Giving a numerical answer with no working and no unit.
- Confusing the vertical rise on a chart (a delivery) with usage.
- Naming costs of holding inventory without explaining the effect on the business.
- Forgetting the lost discount when comparing JIT with JIC.
- Writing that JIT means no inventory, when it means the minimum.
- Evaluating with a list of points but no conclusion.
- Using general answers where the question gives a business with specific facts.
