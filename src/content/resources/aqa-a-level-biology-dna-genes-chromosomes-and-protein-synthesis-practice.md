## Questions

**1.** Compare the DNA in a bacterial cell with the DNA in the nucleus of a human liver cell. **[3]**

**2.** (a) State what is meant by a gene. **[2]**
**(b)** State what is meant by the locus of a gene. **[1]**

**3.** Explain what is meant by each of these descriptions of the genetic code: universal, non-overlapping and degenerate. **[3]**

**4.** Mitochondria contain DNA.
**(a)** Describe two features of mitochondrial DNA. **[2]**
**(b)** Name one other organelle that contains DNA. **[1]**

**5.** A short gene has this template strand: TAC GAA CCT AGG TGC TTA ACT. Use this codon table: AUG = Met (start), CUU = Leu, GGA = Gly, UCC = Ser, ACG = Thr, AAU = Asn, UGA = stop.
**(a)** Write the mRNA sequence. **[1]**
**(b)** Give the amino acid sequence of the polypeptide. **[2]**
**(c)** Give the anticodon of the tRNA that brings the fourth amino acid. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the polypeptide has six amino acids although the template has seven triplets. **[1]**

**6.** A eukaryotic pre-mRNA is 2,760 nucleotides long. It contains three introns of 318, 402 and 207 nucleotides. The mature mRNA has 84 nucleotides that are not translated.
**(a)** Calculate the length of the mature mRNA. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of amino acids in the polypeptide. The translated region includes the stop codon. **[2]**

**7.** Describe two similarities and two differences between the structure of mRNA and the structure of tRNA. **[4]**

**8.** A bacterium has a genome of 4.6 million base pairs and 4,300 genes with an average coding length of 950 base pairs. A eukaryote has a genome of 120 million base pairs and 14,000 genes with an average coding length of 1,700 base pairs.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage of each genome that codes for polypeptides, to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the difference. **[2]**
**(c)** A skin cell and a pancreatic cell from the same person have the same genome but different proteomes. Explain. **[2]**

**9.** Describe how pre-mRNA is made from DNA and converted to mRNA in a eukaryotic cell. **[5]**

**10.** A cell-free extract contains ribosomes, tRNA, ATP, enzymes and 14C-labelled leucine. Different additions were made to six tubes and the radioactivity in newly made polypeptide was measured.

| Tube | Addition | Radioactivity (counts per minute) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | mRNA | 4,820 |
| 2 | None | 130 |
| 3 | mRNA, but ATP omitted | 410 |
| 4 | mRNA treated with ribonuclease | 150 |
| 5 | DNA of the same gene | 170 |
| 6 | mRNA, but tRNA removed | 220 |

**(a)** Calculate the radioactivity in tube 3 as a percentage of tube 1, to the nearest whole number. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain the results for tubes 2 and 4. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the result for tube 5. **[2]**
**(d)** What do the results for tube 6 show about tRNA? **[1]**

**11.** Cultured cells were treated with an inhibitor of RNA polymerase. RNA synthesis and protein synthesis were measured as a percentage of the rates in untreated cells.

| Time after adding inhibitor (h) | RNA synthesis (%) | Protein synthesis (%) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 12 | 85 |
| 2 | 9 | 41 |
| 4 | 8 | 14 |

**(a)** Calculate the fall in RNA synthesis at 0.5 h, in percentage points. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the change in protein synthesis. Use figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why protein synthesis fell more slowly than RNA synthesis. **[2]**

**12.** Describe how a polypeptide is made at a ribosome from mRNA. Include the roles of tRNA and ATP. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Bacterial DNA is short, human nuclear DNA is very long [1]; bacterial DNA is circular, human nuclear DNA is linear [1]; bacterial DNA is not associated with proteins, human DNA is associated with histones [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison answer should state both sides in each point; naming only the bacterial feature, such as "circular", leaves the comparison incomplete.

**2. (a)** A base sequence of DNA [1] that codes for the amino acid sequence of a polypeptide (or for a functional RNA) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The fixed position of the gene on a particular DNA molecule [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* "A gene codes for a protein" is a looser wording; tying the gene to the sequence of amino acids in a polypeptide is the safer definition.

**3.** Universal: a given triplet specifies one particular amino acid whichever organism it is in [1]. Non-overlapping: each base is part of only one triplet, so the sequence is read in separate groups of three [1]. Degenerate: most amino acids are coded for by more than one triplet [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Define each term in terms of triplets and amino acids; repeating the word, such as "degenerate means the code is degenerate", gains nothing.

**4. (a)** Any two from: short [1]; circular [1]; not associated with protein (any two features score; a third adds nothing). **[2]**
**(b)** Chloroplast [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* "Circular" and "no histones" are separate features; state each one clearly so that it is easy to see two distinct points.

**5. (a)** AUG CUU GGA UCC ACG AAU UGA [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Reads from the start codon AUG [1]; Met - Leu - Gly - Ser - Thr - Asn [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The fourth codon is UCC, so the anticodon is **AGG** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** The seventh codon, UGA, is a stop codon and does not code for an amino acid [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the mRNA before the amino acids; if the mRNA contains T, or is not complementary, every later step built on it will be wrong.

**6. (a)** Total introns = 318 + 402 + 207 = 927 [1]; mRNA = 2,760 - 927 = **1,833 nucleotides** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Translated region = 1,833 - 84 = 1,749; 1,749 / 3 = 583 codons [1]; minus the stop codon gives **582 amino acids** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Setting the working out line by line makes it easy to see where an error began; forgetting the stop codon is the commonest slip in part (b).

**7.** Similarities: both are single polynucleotide strands [1]; both contain ribose and uracil [1]. Differences: mRNA is linear whereas tRNA is folded into a clover-leaf shape held by hydrogen bonds between bases [1]; mRNA has codons whereas tRNA has one anticodon and an amino acid binding site [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* If asked for similarities and differences, label them; a list of unlabelled facts about each molecule does not show the comparison.

**8. (a)** Bacterium: 4,300 x 950 = 4,085,000; 4,085,000 / 4,600,000 x 100 = **88.8%** [1]. Eukaryote: 14,000 x 1,700 = 23,800,000; 23,800,000 / 120,000,000 x 100 = **19.8%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The eukaryotic genome contains introns within genes [1] and non-coding multiple repeats between genes [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Genome is the complete set of genes and is the same in both cells [1]; different genes are expressed in each cell type, so they make different proteins (different proteomes) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* For a calculation, give the unrounded step before the answer so that a slip in rounding can be seen; part (c) needs both halves, same genes and different proteins made.

**9.** RNA polymerase attaches to the DNA and the strands separate; one strand acts as the template [1]. Free RNA nucleotides pair with the exposed bases by complementary base pairing [1]. RNA polymerase joins adjacent nucleotides by condensation reactions [1]. This forms pre-mRNA [1]. Introns are removed and exons joined (splicing) to form mRNA [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A sequence of numbered stages helps; the most-missed point is that splicing acts on pre-mRNA, not on the DNA.

**10. (a)** 410 / 4,820 x 100 = 8.5, so **9%** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Without mRNA there is no code for the ribosome to follow, so little polypeptide is made [1]; ribonuclease breaks down the mRNA, so the result is similar to having none [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Ribosomes translate mRNA, not DNA [1]; the DNA must first be transcribed to mRNA, which the extract cannot do here [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** tRNA is needed for translation (to bring amino acids to the ribosome) [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Residual counts in tubes 2 to 6 are small but not zero; say "very little" rather than "none", and use a figure from the table when you compare.

**11. (a)** 100 - 12 = **88 percentage points** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Protein synthesis falls steadily [1] from 85% at 0.5 h to 14% at 4 h, a fall of 71 percentage points [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** mRNA already made continues to be translated [1]; protein synthesis falls as the existing mRNA is broken down and no new mRNA is made [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote figures from the table for "describe" questions, and use the idea of existing mRNA to turn a description into an explanation.

**12.** mRNA binds to a ribosome [1]. A tRNA with an anticodon complementary to the first codon binds, carrying its specific amino acid [1]. A second tRNA binds to the next codon [1]. A peptide bond forms between the amino acids by a condensation reaction [1]. ATP provides the energy [1]. The ribosome moves along the mRNA codon by codon until a stop codon, when the polypeptide is released [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* For a six-mark description, a clear ordered account that names codon, anticodon, ribosome and ATP covers the needed points; vague phrases such as "tRNA helps" usually earn little.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting thymine into an mRNA sequence, or giving a DNA complement when mRNA was asked for.
- Giving the codon when the anticodon was asked, or the other way round.
- Including the stop codon when counting amino acids.
- Describing only one type of DNA when asked to compare.
- Saying "no protein" for the control tubes when the data show small non-zero values.
- Describing a trend without figures from the table.
- Saying introns are cut out of DNA.
- Forgetting to say that a tRNA carries one specific amino acid.
