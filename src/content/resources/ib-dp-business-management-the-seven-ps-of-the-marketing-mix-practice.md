## Questions

**1.** State two extension strategies that a business could use for a product in maturity. **[2]**

**2.** Define the term loss leader. **[2]**

**3.** Lumeo Lamps makes a desk lamp with a unit cost of 24 dollars. It uses cost-plus (mark-up) pricing with a mark-up of 50%.

**(a)** Calculate the selling price of one lamp. **[2]**
**(b)** Lumeo sells 1,200 lamps at this price. Calculate the total mark-up earned. **[2]**

**4.** Distinguish between above the line, below the line and through the line promotion. **[3]**

**5.** Explain why brand loyalty and brand value are each important to a business. **[4]**

**6.** (HL only) Strand & Sparrow Tea raises the price of a tin from 8.00 dollars to 9.20 dollars. Monthly sales fall from 3,000 tins to 2,520 tins.

**(a)** Calculate the price elasticity of demand. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the change in monthly sales revenue. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what these results suggest about a further price rise. **[2]**

**7.** (HL only) Birchlow Bakery has spare capacity. Its variable cost is 2.40 dollars per loaf and its usual price is 4.50 dollars. A cafe chain offers to buy 800 loaves at 3.20 dollars each as a one-off order.

**(a)** Calculate the total contribution from the order. **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether Birchlow should accept the order. **[5]**

**8.** Portaro Skate Co sells boards and clothing to customers aged 14 to 24. It is thinking of moving most of its promotion to social media. Evaluate this strategy. **[6]**

**9.** Teslin Fitness is a gym in a city where members expect friendly, informal contact with staff. Explain the importance of people, processes and physical evidence in marketing the gym. **[6]**

**10.** Gorsebrook Games launched a board game. The table shows its first five years (all money in thousand dollars).

| Year | Sales (thousand units) | Investment | Profit | Net cash flow |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | 400 | -120 | -520 |
| 2 | 18 | 250 | 40 | -210 |
| 3 | 40 | 120 | 210 | 90 |
| 4 | 52 | 30 | 380 | 350 |
| 5 | 49 | 20 | 330 | 310 |

**(a)** Identify the likely product life cycle stage in year 1 and in year 5. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the relationship between the life cycle, investment, profit and cash flow shown in the table. **[3]**
**(c)** Recommend one extension strategy for the game, justifying your choice. **[4]**

**11.** A furniture maker sells only through independent shops. Explain one advantage and one disadvantage for the maker of starting to sell directly to customers online. **[4]**

**12.** Kinnear Cooking School plans to open in a mid-sized city, offering small evening classes taught by trained chefs to adults with high incomes. Recommend an appropriate marketing mix for Kinnear, covering all seven Ps. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: enter a new market [1]; add a new feature or variant [1]; reposition the product; change packaging; find a new use; cut price. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming two different strategies earns credit; repeating the same idea in different words usually earns it once.

**2.** A product sold at or below its cost [1] to attract customers who will also buy other, profitable items [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition needs both the below-cost price and the purpose; the first alone is incomplete.

**3. (a)** Mark-up = 24 × 0.50 = 12 dollars [1]. Price = 24 + 12 = **36 dollars** [1].
**(b)** Total mark-up = 1,200 × 12 [1] = **14,400 dollars** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Showing the mark-up as a separate step helps because a slip in the later step still shows correct method.

**4.** Above the line is paid mass media aimed at a wide audience, such as television or press [1]. Below the line is targeted promotion where the business controls the contact, such as samples or discounts [1]. Through the line combines both in one integrated campaign [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A distinction needs each term explained, not just examples listed.

**5.** Brand loyalty: customers repeatedly choose the brand over rivals [1], which lowers the cost of winning sales and protects revenue when rivals cut prices [1]. Brand value: the extra worth the brand carries [1], which supports a higher price and adds to the worth of the business if it is sold [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Explain" calls for a reason each time, so define the aspect and then say why it benefits the business.

**6. (a)** Price change = (9.20 − 8.00) ÷ 8.00 × 100 = +15% [1]. Quantity change = (2,520 − 3,000) ÷ 3,000 × 100 = −16% [1]. PED = −16 ÷ 15 = **−1.07** (3 s.f.) [1].
**(b)** Revenue before = 8.00 × 3,000 = 24,000; after = 9.20 × 2,520 = 23,184 [1]. Change = **−816 dollars** [1].
**(c)** Demand is slightly elastic because the size of PED is above 1 [1], so a further rise would probably reduce revenue again, so the business should avoid it unless costs have risen [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* State the sign and interpret the result, because a number on its own does not show you can apply it.

**7. (a)** Contribution per loaf = 3.20 − 2.40 = 0.80 dollars [1]. Total = 800 × 0.80 [1] = **640 dollars** [1].
**(b)** For: spare capacity, fixed costs are paid anyway, so the order adds 640 dollars to profit [1]; it builds a relationship with a new customer [1]. Against: the price is below the usual 4.50, so regular customers may demand lower prices [1]; the order may use capacity needed by full-price sales [1]. Judgement: accept if capacity is truly spare and the offer is not made public, since it adds profit without extra fixed cost [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation scores best when it reaches a decision and names the condition on which it rests.

**8.** For: low cost to start [1]; precise targeting of 14 to 24 year olds [1]; two-way contact and shareable content build awareness [1]. Against: negative comments spread quickly [1]; staff time is needed to manage accounts, and little reaches customers who do not use these platforms [1]. Judgement: a mostly social strategy fits this young target market, but some below the line offers or events should be kept to reach shops and parents [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Tie each point to the age group in the stem; generic points about social media earn less.

**9.** People: staff match the informal style members expect [1], so training and recruitment should reflect this local expectation of friendly contact [1]. Processes: simple joining, booking of classes and payment reduce frustration, and changes such as an app need staff support [1][1]. Physical evidence: equipment, cleanliness and branded kit signal quality because a gym cannot be inspected like a product [1][1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Cover all three Ps named in the question, and link each to this gym rather than gyms in general.

**10. (a)** Year 1: introduction [1]. Year 5: maturity [1].
**(b)** Investment is highest at the start [1] when profit is negative and cash flow is most negative [1]; as sales grow, investment falls, profit and cash flow turn positive, and cash flow is strongest at maturity when little new spending is needed [1].
**(c)** Recommendation, for example a travel or junior edition [1], justified by the new segment it reaches [1], by the strong positive cash flow in year 4 that can pay for it [1], and by lower risk than a new game launch, though sales in year 5 slipped from 52 to 49 [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Use figures from the table to support the life cycle link; assertions without data are weaker.

**11.** Advantage: the maker keeps the full margin instead of sharing it with shops [1], and gains customer data [1]. Disadvantage: it must pay for delivery, returns and marketing itself [1], and may upset the shops that currently stock its products [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each point needs a reason, such as why the margin or the cost matters, to earn the second mark.

**12.** Award up to 10 for a consistent mix applied to Kinnear.
- Product: small classes, trained chefs, a defined brand and a clear USP [1].
- Price: premium pricing to match the high-income, quality-led target [1], supported by quality in the other Ps [1].
- Promotion: social media aimed at adults plus below the line offers such as taster evenings [1].
- Place: central location with easy transport, and online booking [1].
- People: chefs who teach well and treat customers courteously [1].
- Processes: easy booking, payment, ingredient preparation and class timetable [1].
- Physical evidence: attractive kitchens, branded aprons, a clear website [1].
- Consistency: all Ps point to a premium, personal experience [1].
- Judgement: a premium mix suits this target, but it depends on enough such customers in the city, so research first [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* A mix that is applied to the case and consistent across the Ps is stronger than seven separate definitions.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing about the seven Ps in general rather than for the named business.
- Calculating percentage change from the new figure instead of the old figure.
- Giving a PED value without its sign or without saying elastic or inelastic (HL only).
- Treating contribution as profit: it still has to cover fixed costs (HL only).
- Defining a term and not explaining why it matters when "explain" is the command word.
- Judging a method as good or bad without stating the conditions.
- Ignoring the data in a table when recommending a strategy.
- Missing cultural variation when discussing people in a service.
