## Section A

**1.** Which particle is formed when a bromine atom gains one electron?

A: Br⁺

B: Br⁻

C: Br₂

D: Br²⁻

**[1]**

**2.** State what is meant by an ionic bond. **[2]**

**3.** State the charge on the ion formed by each of these elements: lithium, magnesium, sulfur and iodine. **[4]**

**4.** A lithium atom has the electronic configuration 2,1.

**(a)** Give the electronic configuration of a lithium ion. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the noble gas whose atoms have the same configuration. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why the ions in sodium chloride, Na⁺ and Cl⁻, have different charges even though both have a noble gas configuration. **[2]**

**6.** A white solid melts at a high temperature. It does not conduct electricity when solid but does conduct when dissolved in water. State the type of bonding present and give the evidence from the description. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** Potassium reacts with chlorine to form potassium chloride.

**(a)** Describe what happens to the electrons when a potassium atom reacts with a chlorine atom. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe the dot-and-cross diagram you would draw for potassium chloride, showing outer electrons only. **[3]**

**8.** *(Supplement)* Magnesium reacts with fluorine to form magnesium fluoride.

**(a)** Use electronic configurations to explain why the formula is MgF₂ and not MgF. **[3]**

**(b)** State the number of electrons in the outer shell of each ion shown in the dot-and-cross diagram, and the charge on each ion. **[2]**

**9.** *(Supplement)* Deduce the formula of each compound, showing how the electrons lost and gained balance.

**(a)** Aluminium chloride. **[2]**

**(b)** Lithium oxide. **[2]**

**10.** *(Supplement)* Describe the structure of solid magnesium oxide. Use a term from the syllabus in your answer. **[2]**

**11.** *(Supplement)* Calcium chloride is an ionic compound.

**(a)** Explain why calcium chloride has a high melting point. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why calcium chloride conducts electricity when molten but not when solid. **[3]**

**12.** A student writes: "Molten sodium chloride conducts electricity because its electrons are free to move." Identify the error and correct it. **[2]**

**13.** An ion X²⁻ has the electronic configuration 2,8,8.

**(a)** Deduce the number of protons in an atom of X and identify element X. **[2]**

**(b)** State the group of the Periodic Table that X is in, and explain how the charge on the ion shows this. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. A bromine atom gains one negative electron, so it becomes a 1− ion, the bromide ion.

**2.** A strong **electrostatic attraction** [1] between **oppositely charged ions** [1].

**3.** Lithium **1+** [1]; magnesium **2+** [1]; sulfur **2−** [1]; iodine **1−** [1].

**4.**

**(a)** **2** [1] (one electron lost, leaving the first shell full).

**(b)** **Helium** [1].

**5.** Sodium **loses one** electron, so it has one more proton than electrons and is 1+ [1]; chlorine **gains one** electron, so it has one more electron than protons and is 1− [1].

**6.** **Ionic** bonding [1]. High melting point [1]; conducts only when dissolved, not as a solid, showing charged particles (ions) that are fixed in the solid but free to move in solution [1].

**7.**

**(a)** The potassium atom **loses one electron** (its single outer electron) [1]; the chlorine atom **gains this electron** [1]; forming **K⁺ and Cl⁻ ions**, each with a noble gas configuration [1].

**(b)** Potassium ion in square brackets with **no outer electrons** and a **+ charge** [1]; chloride ion in square brackets with **eight outer electrons, seven of one symbol and one of the other** [1]; **− charge** shown at the top right of the chloride bracket [1].

**8.**

**(a)** Mg is 2,8,2 and **loses two electrons** [1]; each F is 2,7 and **gains only one** electron [1]; so **two fluorine atoms** are needed to accept the two electrons, giving MgF₂ [1].

**(b)** Mg²⁺ has **no outer electrons shown** (or eight, if the full second shell is drawn) and each F⁻ has **eight** [1]; charges **2+** on magnesium and **1−** on each fluoride ion [1].

**9.**

**(a)** Al loses 3 electrons to form Al³⁺; each Cl gains 1 to form Cl⁻ [1]; three Cl⁻ balance one Al³⁺, so **AlCl₃** [1].

**(b)** Each Li loses 1 electron to form Li⁺; O gains 2 to form O²⁻ [1]; two Li⁺ balance one O²⁻, so **Li₂O** [1].

**10.** A **giant (ionic) lattice** [1] that is a **regular arrangement of alternating** Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ ions [1].

**11.**

**(a)** Giant **lattice** of ions [1]; **strong electrostatic attractions between oppositely charged ions** [1]; a **large amount of energy** is needed to overcome these attractions [1].

**(b)** Molten: the **ions are free to move** [1] and carry charge through the liquid [1]. Solid: the ions are **held in fixed positions** in the lattice and cannot move [1].

**12.** Sodium chloride is ionic, so the current is **not carried by electrons** [1]; it conducts when molten because the **ions are free to move** [1].

**13.**

**(a)** The ion has 2 + 8 + 8 = 18 electrons and a 2− charge, so it has two more electrons than protons: **16 protons** [1]; X is **sulfur** [1].

**(b)** **Group VI** [1]; the atom gains **two** electrons to complete its outer shell, so it had six outer electrons [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "transfer of electrons" as the definition of an ionic bond. The bond is the attraction between the ions.
- Forgetting the **ratio** in Supplement formula questions. Show electrons lost = electrons gained, then write the formula.
- Leaving the square brackets or charges off a dot-and-cross diagram, or putting the charge inside the bracket.
- Giving only one condition for conduction. The syllabus wording is **molten or aqueous**.
- Explaining ionic conduction with electrons. Ions move in ionic compounds; electrons move in metals and graphite.
- Using "intermolecular forces" for an ionic compound when explaining its melting point. There are no molecules in a giant lattice.
