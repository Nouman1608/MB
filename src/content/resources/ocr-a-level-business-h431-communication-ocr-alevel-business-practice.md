## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Distinguish between formal and informal communication. **[3]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by a group norm. **[2]**

**3.** Explain **one** reason why feedback is an important part of the communication process. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **one** disadvantage of using email to announce redundancies. **[2]**

**5.** Which **one** of the following is an example of non-verbal communication?

A: A written report.

B: A telephone call.

C: A manager's folded arms during a meeting.

D: An email.

**[1]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**6.** Lowick Parcels delivers 400 parcels a week. Because instructions from the sales team to the drivers are often unclear, 5% of parcels go to the wrong address. Each wrong delivery costs £60 to put right.

**(a)** Calculate the weekly cost of wrong deliveries. **[2]**

**(b)** A new messaging system will cost £18,000 and is expected to cut wrong deliveries to 2%. Calculate the weekly saving and the payback period in months, to one decimal place, assuming 52 weeks a year. **[4]**

**(c)** Evaluate the importance of effective communication to Lowick Parcels and its stakeholders. **[8]**

**7.** Eight managers at Lowick Parcels attend a two-hour meeting every week for 48 weeks a year. Managers' time is valued at £32 an hour.

**(a)** Calculate the annual cost of managers' time in these meetings. **[2]**

**(b)** The managing director proposes to replace most meetings with a weekly email. Evaluate this proposal. **[9]**

**8.** At Lowick Parcels, the drivers have an informal rule that nobody delivers more than 40 parcels a shift, even though the company target is 50. Analyse how this group norm affects communication between drivers and managers. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Formal communication follows the official channels of the business, such as reports, memos and meetings, and is usually recorded [1]. Informal communication takes place outside official channels, such as casual conversation or the grapevine [1], and is faster but less accurate and usually not recorded [1]. **[3]**

**2.** A group norm is an unwritten rule or expectation about how members of a group should behave [1], which is enforced by the approval or disapproval of other members [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Feedback tells the sender whether the message was received and understood as intended [1], so the sender can correct a misunderstanding before it causes a mistake [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Email is impersonal and gives no chance for the sender to see the reaction or to answer questions [1], so affected staff may feel disrespected and trust in management may fall [1]. Accept: the message may be forwarded, leaked or misread. **[2]**

**5.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** 5% × 400 = 20 wrong deliveries [1]. 20 × £60 = **£1,200** a week [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** 2% × 400 = 8 wrong deliveries; cost = 8 × £60 = £480 [1]. Weekly saving = £1,200 − £480 = **£720** [1]. Annual saving = 52 × £720 = £37,440 [1]. Payback = £18,000 ÷ £37,440 × 12 = **5.8 months** [1]. **[4]**

**(c)** Costs: wrong deliveries cost £1,200 a week, or £62,400 a year [1]. The payback of under six months for the system shows that improving communication is worth the cost [1]. Customers receive parcels late or not at all, which damages reputation and could lose business customers [1]. Drivers waste time putting right errors and may be blamed unfairly, which harms morale [1]. Managers spend time on complaints [1].
Limits: the saving relies on the system delivering a fall to 2%, and a system alone does not fix the cause if the problem is how sales staff write instructions [1]. Training and clear procedures may be needed as well [1].
Judgement: effective communication is very important to a delivery business because accuracy is the service customers are paying for; Lowick should adopt the system, and measure the error rate each month [1]. **[8]**

**7. (a)** 8 × 2 = 16 manager-hours a meeting; 16 × £32 = £512 [1]. 48 × £512 = **£24,576** a year [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Benefits: email saves much of the £24,576 of manager time [1], and gives a written record that managers can read at a convenient time [1]. It suits the routine sharing of figures [1].
Drawbacks: email allows no immediate questions or discussion, so problems may be missed [1], and tone may be misread [1]. Managers may not read long emails, so messages could be missed [1]. Face-to-face meetings build teamwork and trust [1], which matter for solving delivery problems.
Evaluation: the value depends on the content; routine updates suit email, but complex problems and conflict need discussion [1]. Judgement: the managing director should replace most weekly meetings with email and keep a shorter meeting every month, or when a problem needs discussion, since this saves most of the cost while keeping the benefits of discussion [1]. **[9]**

**8.** The norm acts as a barrier between drivers and managers [1]. Drivers hear the target of 50 parcels but follow the group's 40, so the manager's message is not acted on [1]. Drivers are unlikely to report the norm to managers, because the group expects loyalty, so managers receive no honest feedback and may think the target is being met [1].
Managers may then issue stricter formal instructions, which drivers see as pressure, increasing resistance and reducing trust [1]. Informal communication within the group is strong and spreads quickly, so a manager's message may be reinterpreted by the group before individual drivers act on it [1].
The group's objective (a comfortable workload) differs from the company's (more deliveries) [1]. Managers could involve the drivers in setting the target, which would bring the norm into the open. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing verbal with oral.
- Naming media without matching them to the situation.
- Forgetting feedback in the communication process.
- Writing about communication in general terms with no case detail.
- Ignoring the cost of poor communication.
