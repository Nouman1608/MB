Subtopic 15.3 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers the kinetic theory assumptions, the derivation of pV = ⅓Nm⟨c²⟩, the root-mean-square speed and the mean translational kinetic energy ³⁄₂kT. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Kinetic theory of gases study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-kinetic-theory-of-gases/); to apply the ideas, try the [Kinetic theory of gases practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-kinetic-theory-of-gases-practice/).

## Assumptions (learn the precise wording)

1. A gas consists of a very large number of identical molecules in **random motion**.
2. Collisions between molecules, and with the walls, are **elastic**.
3. The **volume of the molecules is negligible** compared with the volume of the gas.
4. There are **no intermolecular forces** except during collisions.
5. The **duration of a collision is negligible** compared with the time between collisions.

## Pressure in five steps

1. Molecules collide with the walls.
2. Each collision changes the molecule's momentum.
3. The wall exerts a force on the molecule; the molecule exerts an equal and opposite force on the wall.
4. Many collisions per second give a steady average force.
5. Pressure = force ÷ area.

## Derivation checkpoints

```
momentum change at wall      2mcₓ
time between hits            2L / cₓ
force from one molecule      mcₓ² / L
all molecules                (Nm / L)⟨cₓ²⟩
pressure                     (Nm / V)⟨cₓ²⟩
3-D, random motion           ⟨cₓ²⟩ = ⅓⟨c²⟩
result                       pV = ⅓ Nm⟨c²⟩     or   p = ⅓ ρ⟨c²⟩
```

## Speeds

| Quantity | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Mean speed | Average of the speeds | m s⁻¹ |
| ⟨c²⟩ | Mean of the squares of the speeds | m² s⁻² |
| c_r.m.s. | √⟨c²⟩ | m s⁻¹ |

- c_r.m.s. ≥ mean speed (equal only if all speeds are the same).
- Example: 200, 400, 500, 600, 800 m s⁻¹ → mean 500 m s⁻¹; ⟨c²⟩ = 2.9 × 10⁵ m² s⁻²; c_r.m.s. = 539 m s⁻¹.

## Linking to temperature

```
⅓Nm⟨c²⟩ = NkT    →    ½m⟨c²⟩ = ³⁄₂kT
c_r.m.s. = √(3kT / m) = √(3RT / M)
```

- Mean translational kinetic energy depends **only on T** (in kelvin).
- Same T, different gases: **same** mean kinetic energy; c_r.m.s. ∝ 1/√m.
- Same gas: c_r.m.s. ∝ √T; T × 4 gives c_r.m.s. × 2.
- Per mole: ³⁄₂RT.

## Values worth recognising

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| ³⁄₂kT at 300 K | 6.21 × 10⁻²¹ J |
| c_r.m.s. of helium at 300 K | 1.37 × 10³ m s⁻¹ |
| c_r.m.s. of nitrogen at 300 K | 517 m s⁻¹ |
| c_r.m.s. of air from p = ⅓ρ⟨c²⟩ (1.01 × 10⁵ Pa, 1.2 kg m⁻³) | 500 m s⁻¹ |
| ³⁄₂RT at 300 K | 3.74 × 10³ J mol⁻¹ |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Elastic collision | Collision in which total kinetic energy is conserved |
| Random motion | Motion with no preferred direction; speeds and directions vary |
| Mean-square speed ⟨c²⟩ | Average of the squared speeds of the molecules |
| Root-mean-square speed | Square root of the mean-square speed |
| Translational kinetic energy | Kinetic energy of a molecule's motion from place to place |
| Boltzmann constant k | 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹ |

## Exam traps

- "Molecules are small" instead of "volume of molecules negligible compared with volume of gas".
- Momentum change mcₓ instead of 2mcₓ; time L/cₓ instead of 2L/cₓ.
- Forgetting the three-dimensional step.
- Writing ⟨c²⟩ as (mean speed)².
- Using molar mass M as m in ½m⟨c²⟩, or forgetting to convert g mol⁻¹ to kg mol⁻¹.
- Saying heavier molecules have greater kinetic energy at the same temperature.
- Using Celsius in ³⁄₂kT.

## Self-test

1. State four assumptions of the kinetic theory of gases.
2. Explain how molecular collisions produce gas pressure.
3. In the derivation, what is the momentum change of a molecule hitting a wall?
4. Why is ⟨cₓ²⟩ = ⅓⟨c²⟩?
5. Four molecules have speeds 100, 200, 300 and 400 m s⁻¹. Find ⟨c²⟩ and c_r.m.s.
6. Show that ½m⟨c²⟩ = ³⁄₂kT.
7. Calculate the mean translational kinetic energy of a molecule at 27 °C.
8. Hydrogen (2.0 g mol⁻¹) and oxygen (32 g mol⁻¹) are at the same temperature. Find the ratio of their r.m.s. speeds.
9. To what temperature must a gas at 300 K be heated to double its r.m.s. speed?
10. A gas has density 0.18 kg m⁻³ at a pressure of 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. Find c_r.m.s.
11. Why is the r.m.s. speed used rather than the mean velocity?
12. What does the model predict for the translational kinetic energy at 0 K?

**Answers:** 1. Any four: very large number of identical molecules in random motion; elastic collisions; molecular volume negligible compared with gas volume; no intermolecular forces except during collisions; collision time negligible compared with time between collisions. 2. Molecules hit the walls and change momentum; the wall exerts a force on each, so each exerts an equal and opposite force on the wall; many collisions give a steady force; pressure is force per unit area. 3. 2mcₓ. 4. Motion is random, so the mean squares of the three components are equal and add to ⟨c²⟩. 5. ⟨c²⟩ = (1 + 4 + 9 + 16) × 10⁴ ÷ 4 = 7.5 × 10⁴ m² s⁻²; c_r.m.s. = 274 m s⁻¹. 6. ⅓Nm⟨c²⟩ = NkT, so m⟨c²⟩ = 3kT and ½m⟨c²⟩ = ³⁄₂kT. 7. 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 300 = 6.2 × 10⁻²¹ J. 8. c_H/c_O = √(32/2) = 4. 9. 1200 K. 10. √(3 × 1.0 × 10⁵ ÷ 0.18) = 1.3 × 10³ m s⁻¹. 11. The mean velocity is zero because motion is random in all directions; squaring removes the sign, and ⟨c²⟩ is the quantity that arises in the derivation. 12. Zero.
