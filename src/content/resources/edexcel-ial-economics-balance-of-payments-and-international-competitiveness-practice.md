## Questions

**1.** Identify two items recorded in the current account of the balance of payments. **[2]**

**2.** Country P's transactions (billion currency units, cu) are: goods exports 82, goods imports 95, services exports 30, services imports 21, net primary income -8, net secondary income +4, capital account +1, net errors and omissions -1.

**(a)** Calculate the current account balance. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the financial account balance, counting inflows as positive. **[2]**

**3.** Country Q's clothing industry pays total labour costs of 45,000 cu for 2,500 units in year 1, and 47,700 cu for 2,600 units in year 2.

**(a)** Calculate unit labour cost in each year and its percentage change to one decimal place. **[3]**
**(b)** Rivals' unit labour costs in a common currency are unchanged. Explain the effect on Country Q's competitiveness. **[2]**

**4.** Output per worker is 60 in Country R and 48 in a rival.

**(a)** Calculate R's relative productivity. **[2]**
**(b)** R's export price index rises 3% and the rival's rises 10%, in the same currency. Calculate and interpret R's relative export price index (base 100). **[3]**

**5.** Explain two reasons why a country might run a persistent current account deficit. **[6]**

**6.** Distinguish between expenditure-reducing and expenditure-switching policies, giving one example of each. **[4]**

**7.** Country S imports 80 billion cu and exports 72 billion cu. Real income rises 6% and the income elasticity of demand for imports is 1.5. Exports do not change.

**(a)** Calculate the new value of imports. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the new trade balance. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how (a) improved infrastructure and (b) higher-quality human capital can raise international competitiveness. **[6]**

**9.** Country T has net external liabilities of 120 billion cu. It runs a deficit of 20 billion cu a year for 4 years, financed by borrowing, at a 5% interest rate on liabilities.

**(a)** Calculate its liabilities after 4 years. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the rise in annual interest due. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain two reasons why persistent imbalances of this kind matter for the world economy. **[4]**

**10.** Explain two problems for an economy of being internationally uncompetitive. **[4]**

**11.** Evaluate the view that education and training is the most effective way for a country to improve its international competitiveness. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Trade in goods [1] and any one of trade in services, primary income or secondary income [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name items that actually sit in the account; "trade" or "money" alone is too vague, and capital-account or financial-account items do not answer the question.

**2. (a)** Goods: 82 - 95 = -13 [1]. Services: 30 - 21 = +9 [1]. Current account = -13 + 9 - 8 + 4 = **-8** [1].
**(b)** The accounts sum to zero, so the financial account = -(-8 + 1 - 1) [1] = **+8** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the sum; a wrong sign on the financial account is a common slip, so state your convention (inflows positive) next to the answer.

**3. (a)** Year 1: 45,000 ÷ 2,500 = 18 [1]. Year 2: 47,700 ÷ 2,600 = 18.35 [1]. Change = **+1.9%** [1].
**(b)** Unit labour cost rose while rivals' did not, so Q is less competitive [1]. Q must raise export prices or accept lower profit, so sales abroad may fall [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) needs the link from cost to price to sales, not just "less competitive"; say the comparison is relative to rivals.

**4. (a)** 60 ÷ 48 × 100 [1] = **125** (R's productivity is 25% higher) [1].
**(b)** 103 ÷ 110 × 100 [1] = **93.6** [1]. R's export prices have fallen relative to the rival's by about 6.4%, improving its price competitiveness [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide own by rival, not the reverse, and give an interpretation; a bare number does not say which country gained.

**5.** Reason 1: strong demand with a high income elasticity of demand for imports [1]; as income rises imports grow faster than exports [1]; the goods and services balance worsens [1]. Reason 2: unit labour costs rising faster than productivity [1]; export prices become uncompetitive and imports cheaper than home goods [1]; exports fall and imports rise [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each reason needs a developed chain ending in the balance; two listed causes with no development gain little.

**6.** Expenditure-reducing policies cut total demand, including demand for imports [1]; for example, higher taxes or interest rates [1]. Expenditure-switching policies move spending from imports to domestic goods [1]; for example, tariffs or a lower exchange rate [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition and an example both matter; make clear which effect on spending each policy targets.

**7. (a)** Rise in imports = 1.5 × 6% = 9% [1]. New imports = 80 × 1.09 [1] = **87.2** [1].
**(b)** Balance = 72 - 87.2 [1] = **-15.2** billion cu (a deficit, up from 8) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the elasticity to find the percentage change first; going straight to the value invites an arithmetic slip, and showing steps keeps your method visible.

**8. (a)** Better infrastructure lowers transport, energy or communication costs and delays [1]; this reduces unit costs, allowing lower prices or higher profit [1]; reliable delivery also improves non-price competitiveness [1]. **(b)** Better skills raise output per worker [1]; if pay grows more slowly than output, unit labour costs fall [1]; skilled workers support higher-quality products [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each factor to a measure of competitiveness (unit costs, price or quality) rather than just saying "more competitive".

**9. (a)** Borrowing = 4 × 20 = 80 [1]; liabilities = 120 + 80 = **200** [1].
**(b)** Interest before = 120 × 0.05 = 6 [1]; after = 200 × 0.05 = 10, a rise of **4** [1].
**(c)** Reason 1: deficit countries become dependent on foreign lenders [1], so a loss of confidence can cause capital flight and a currency crash that spreads abroad [1]. Reason 2: surplus countries are exposed to default by borrowers [1] and the imbalances raise pressure for protectionism [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep calculations separate from the explanation, and tie (c) to the world economy rather than only to Country T.

**10.** Problem 1: a persistent current account deficit as exports lose out [1], which must be financed by borrowing or asset sales [1]. Problem 2: firms in traded industries close, causing unemployment and lost output [1], which lowers growth and incomes [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Two distinct problems are needed; do not split one problem into two points.

**11.** Education and training raise human capital and productivity [1]. If pay does not rise as fast as output, unit labour costs fall [1], export prices fall and product quality improves [1]. The gains are lasting and support higher-value industries, unlike a lower exchange rate, which can be offset [1]. However, benefits take years [1], and cost, skills mismatch and emigration of trained workers reduce the return [1]. A lower exchange rate acts faster [1] but raises import costs and inflation [1]. Investment incentives, infrastructure and deregulation complement training [1]. Judgement: training is most effective for a lasting fix to a structural weakness, but the best choice depends on the cause and time horizon, usually a mix of policies [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Reaching a judgement that names the condition on which your answer depends is more convincing than a one-sided list.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "balance of payments deficit" without saying which account is meant.
- Getting the sign wrong when the financial account balances the current account.
- Dividing rival by own when calculating relative measures.
- Comparing unit labour costs without allowing for productivity or the exchange rate.
- Giving causes and policies with no chain of reasoning to the balance.
- Offering two points that are really one in a "two reasons" question.
- Evaluating with unsupported phrases such as "it depends" and no stated condition.
