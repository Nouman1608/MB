This page is a guide to AQA GCSE Economics (8136) past papers for June 2024 and June 2025. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and a short account of what examiners saw, written by us from AQA's reports on the examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [AQA GCSE Economics (8136) specification page](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/economics/gcse/economics-8136)
- [AQA GCSE Economics (8136) assessment resources](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/economics/gcse/economics-8136/assessment-resources), where question papers, mark schemes and reports are listed by series
- [AQA grade boundaries](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries) and the [grade boundaries archive](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries/archive)

Use the three documents together. Sit the paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which shows what each mark needs and how the levelled questions are banded. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable lost marks. The notes below are a quick way into that report.

Check which specification a paper was set on. GCSE Economics (8136) is linear and untiered, for first teaching from September 2017 and first examined in 2019. Both series covered here were set on that specification. Both papers draw on the whole course, not just their own content block, so questions from either topic area can appear on either paper.

## June 2025

Examiners said performance on Paper 1 was similar to 2024 and on Paper 2 slightly higher. Both reports point to the same weak spots: demand and supply, elasticity, and the details of market failure and the balance of payments. Multiple-choice questions got harder through each section, and the Paper 1 report says some students still label basic demand and supply diagrams incorrectly.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (8136/1), short answers: muddling demand and supply factors or the direction of a shift when explaining a lower equilibrium price; elasticity of supply calculations where the percentage change in quantity was not found or the formula was reversed, and then elasticity of demand written instead of supply; mixing up the features of competitive and non-competitive markets; describing a disadvantage of specialisation for the firm instead of the worker; two types of market described as one for goods and one for services.
- Paper 1, levelled questions: pay differences explained with a single undeveloped idea, about half stopping at level 2; the effect of a declining secondary sector on the economy missed by those who did not know it largely means manufacturing; externality answers that began chains of reasoning but gave only partly supported conclusions.
- Paper 2 (8136/2), short answers: answers about macroeconomic policy in general when the question was on correcting market failure; the budget balance confused with the trade balance; rounding and the wrong denominator in calculations, and the principal included in interest answers; the unemployment rate worked out from the wrong figures; low productivity not linked to a persistent trade deficit, and productivity confused with production.
- Paper 2, extended answers: long quotations from the extract instead of analysis; short conclusions; the 15-mark question rewarded those who argued both sides of a focus on lower unemployment and planned before writing.

**What went well:** stating business objectives and a way to raise productivity, percentage-change productivity calculations, drawing and labelling a demand and supply diagram for labour (64% full marks), defining monopoly, and the multiple-choice questions on Paper 2, where three were answered correctly by over 90% of students.

## June 2024

Examiners found Paper 1 performance similar to 2023 and Paper 2 slightly lower. The topics singled out were demand, supply and elasticity on Paper 1, and solutions to market failure, the foreign exchange market and financial institutions on Paper 2.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (8136/1): multiple choice on monopoly features, where the most popular answer was a wrong one, and on demand, supply and elasticity; features of an oligopoly confused with those of a competitive market; the levelled question on price changes for commodities and related markets reached level 3 for only about a fifth of students; elasticity questions answered correctly by under half.
- Paper 2 (8136/2): an indirect-tax question answered with direct taxes; regressive taxes confused with progressive or proportional ones, or described imprecisely; the unemployment question answered with tax changes instead of government spending; more than half of students scored nothing on solutions to market failure, with monetary policy a common wrong answer; building societies taken to be part of construction (and the highest non-attempt rate on the paper); exchange-rate diagrams with no or limited knowledge of how to build the supply and demand curves.

**What went well:** the profit calculation (91% correct), explaining why online retail is a market, ways a business could raise profit, deductions from gross pay, and the calculation parts of the later questions on Paper 2, where over 80% earned full marks on one.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find the June 2025 8136 papers and mark schemes?**
On AQA's assessment-resources page for 8136, linked above. Papers, mark schemes and reports are listed by series.

**Are the grade thresholds the same every year?**
No. AQA sets them after each series. Compare the tables: a grade 9 needed 133 out of 160 in June 2025 and 130 in June 2024. Do not assume a future series will match.

**Is 8136 tiered?**
No. Every student sits the same two papers, and grades 9 to 1 are available to all.

**What are notional component boundaries?**
They are illustrative boundaries for individual papers. The grade is awarded on the total mark out of 160.

**Which topics did the reports flag most often?**
Demand, supply and elasticity on Paper 1, and solutions to market failure and the balance of payments on Paper 2. Revise those with diagrams and short definitions.
