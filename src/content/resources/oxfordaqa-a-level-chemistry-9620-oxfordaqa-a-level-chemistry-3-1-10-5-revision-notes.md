## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Equivalence point | Where the amounts of acid and base added exactly react according to the equation |
| End point | Where the indicator changes colour; a good indicator makes this match the equivalence point |
| Concordant titres | Titres that agree closely, usually within 0.10 cm³ |
| Vertical section | The near-vertical part of a pH curve around equivalence |
| Half-equivalence point | Half the equivalence volume; for a weak acid titrated with a strong base, pH = pKa here |
| Buffer region | The gently sloping part of a weak acid curve where HA and A⁻ are both present |

## Titration calculation chain

1. Mean of concordant titres (leave out the rough titre).
2. Moles of the solution of known concentration = c × V / 1000.
3. Use the mole ratio from the equation (1 : 1 for monoprotic acids with NaOH).
4. Scale up if only a portion of a made-up solution was titrated (for example × 10 for 25.0 cm³ out of 250 cm³).
5. Convert to the quantity asked: concentration, Mr or mass.

## Regions of a pH curve and how each is calculated

| Region | Strong acid in flask | Weak acid in flask |
|---|---|---|
| Start | pH from [H⁺] = [acid] | pH from [H⁺] = √(Ka × [HA]) |
| Before equivalence | excess H⁺ ÷ total volume | buffer: [H⁺] = Ka × n(HA) / n(A⁻) |
| Half-equivalence | still excess H⁺ | pH = pKa |
| Equivalence | pH 7 with a strong base | above 7 with a strong base |
| After equivalence | excess OH⁻ ÷ total volume, then Kw | identical to the strong acid curve |

## The four curves at a glance

| Acid + base | Start | Vertical section | Equivalence pH | Indicator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strong + strong | low (about 1 at 0.1 mol dm⁻³) | long, about 3 to 11 | 7 | methyl orange, methyl red, bromothymol blue or phenolphthalein |
| Weak + strong | higher | about 7 to 11 | above 7 | phenolphthalein |
| Strong + weak | low | about 3 to 7 | below 7 | methyl orange or methyl red |
| Weak + weak | higher | none | unclear | none: use a pH meter |

Why the equivalence pH is not always 7:
- Weak acid + strong base: the salt anion A⁻ accepts protons from water, releasing OH⁻.
- Strong acid + weak base: the cation (such as NH₄⁺) donates protons to water.

## Effects to remember

- **Equal concentrations of a strong and a weak acid** need the same volume of base: equivalence depends on moles, not strength.
- **Diluting both reagents** shortens the vertical section at both ends but leaves the equivalence volume (and pH 7 for strong + strong) unchanged.
- **Reversing the titration** (base in the flask) gives a mirror-image curve that falls instead of rising.
- **After equivalence**, a weak acid curve and a strong acid curve with the same base coincide.

## Indicator rule

An indicator is suitable only if its **entire** colour-change range lies inside the vertical section. If any part lies outside, the colour changes gradually over several cubic centimetres or before the equivalence point.

## Required practical 7 checklist

- Calibrate the pH meter with buffers of known pH.
- Pipette the acid (or base) into a beaker; add the other solution from a burette.
- Stir and wait for a steady reading after each addition.
- Use smaller additions near the equivalence point.
- Plot pH (y) against volume added (x); equivalence is mid-way up the vertical section.
- Weak acid: read pH at half the equivalence volume to get pKa.

## Exam traps

- Averaging non-concordant titres.
- Forgetting the dilution factor when a sample was made up to a larger volume.
- Using the volume from the burette, not the total volume, after mixing.
- Drawing a weak acid curve that reaches equivalence at a different volume from a strong acid of equal concentration.
- Choosing an indicator because its range contains the equivalence pH without checking the whole range.
- Describing pH = pKa at the equivalence point instead of at half-equivalence.

## Quick self-test

1. Define the equivalence point of a titration.
2. Distinguish between the end point and the equivalence point.
3. Titres: 24.35 cm³ (rough), 23.85, 23.95, 23.90 and 24.10 cm³. Calculate the mean titre.
4. 20.0 cm³ of 0.150 mol dm⁻³ NaOH is neutralised by 18.75 cm³ of hydrochloric acid. Calculate the acid concentration.
5. 0.450 g of a solid monoprotic acid reacts with 24.60 cm³ of 0.120 mol dm⁻³ NaOH. Calculate Mr of the acid.
6. 22.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH is added to 20.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ HCl. Calculate the pH at 25 °C.
7. On a pH curve for a weak acid titrated with NaOH, the pH at half the equivalence volume is 3.86. State pKa and calculate Ka.
8. Explain why the equivalence pH for hydrochloric acid titrated with ammonia is below 7.
9. Which indicator from methyl orange and phenolphthalein suits a weak acid–strong base titration?
10. Why is no indicator suitable for a weak acid–weak base titration?
11. 25.0 cm³ samples of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ HCl and 0.100 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid are each titrated with 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH. Compare the equivalence volumes.
12. State the effect on the vertical section of using solutions ten times more dilute in a strong acid–strong base titration.

### Answers

1. The point at which the amount of base added exactly reacts with the amount of acid present, according to the equation.
2. The end point is where the indicator changes colour; the equivalence point is where the reacting amounts match. With a suitable indicator they coincide.
3. Concordant titres are 23.85, 23.95 and 23.90; mean = **23.90 cm³**.
4. n(NaOH) = 3.00 × 10⁻³ mol = n(HCl); c = 3.00 × 10⁻³ / 0.01875 = **0.160 mol dm⁻³**.
5. n = 0.120 × 0.02460 = 2.952 × 10⁻³ mol; Mr = 0.450 / 2.952 × 10⁻³ = **152**.
6. Excess OH⁻ = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol in 42.0 cm³, so [OH⁻] = 4.76 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³; [H⁺] = 2.10 × 10⁻¹²; pH = **11.68**.
7. pKa = **3.86**; Ka = 10^(−3.86) = **1.38 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³**.
8. The solution at equivalence contains NH₄⁺, which donates protons to water, so [H⁺] > [OH⁻].
9. **Phenolphthalein**: its range lies in the alkaline vertical section.
10. The curve has no vertical section, so the colour changes gradually rather than in one drop.
11. **The same (25.0 cm³)**: both contain the same number of moles of monoprotic acid.
12. The vertical section becomes **shorter** (it starts at a higher pH and ends at a lower pH), but the equivalence volume and pH are unchanged.
