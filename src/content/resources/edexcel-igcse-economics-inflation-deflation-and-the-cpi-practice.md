These questions cover section 2.1.1 b), low and stable inflation, of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Economics (4EC1) specification, Issue 3, February 2026, a single-tier course. Country T, its firms and all figures are invented and illustrative. Learn the content from the [study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-economics-inflation-deflation-and-the-cpi/) and [revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-economics-inflation-deflation-and-the-cpi-revision-notes/). The [course hub](/boards/edexcel/igcse/economics/) and [checklist](/checklists/edexcel/igcse/economics/) show the whole course, and the [free diagnostics](/diagnostics/) test you across topics.

Country T's basket for the CPI has four groups. The base year is 2024 (all groups 100).

| Group | Weight | Index in 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Food | 40 | 112 |
| Transport | 25 | 104 |
| Housing | 20 | 108 |
| Other items | 15 | 96 |

## Questions

**1.** What is meant by deflation? **[2]**

**2.** Calculate the CPI for Country T in 2025. Show your working. **[3]**

**3.** The CPI in Country T rose from 106.8 in 2025 to 111.1 in 2026. Calculate the rate of inflation, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**4.** In Country U the CPI fell from 100.0 to 98.2 in one year. Calculate the rate of change and state what it is called. **[3]**

**5.** Explain how a rise in interest rates could reduce inflation. **[3]**

**6.** A teacher in Country T earns KD 3,200 a month. Her pay rises to KD 3,360, while prices rise 6.2%. Calculate the percentage rise in her pay, and her new pay in terms of last year's prices. Is she better off? **[4]**

**7.** Explain what is meant by menu costs and by shoe leather costs. **[4]**

**8.** Furniture makers in Country T face a 15% rise in the cost of timber and energy. Analyse how this could lead to higher unemployment. **[6]**

**9.** A chair costs KD 80 in Country T and in Country V. Over a year, prices rise 7% in Country T and 2% in Country V. The exchange rate does not change. Calculate each chair's new price, then explain the likely effect on Country T's exports. **[5]**

**10.** A building firm in Country T plans to borrow KD 150,000 for a machine expected to return 4.5% a year. Inflation has risen to 6%, and the interest rate on the loan may rise from 3% to 6%.
**(a)** Calculate the yearly interest at 3% and at 6%. **[2]**
**(b)** Assess how high inflation could affect investment and business and consumer confidence. **[8]**

**11.** Country T has demand-pull inflation. Evaluate whether raising interest rates is the best response. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** A sustained fall [1] in the general level of prices [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition has two parts, so name both the idea of a fall and that it is general and lasting. A fall in one price is not deflation, so say the fall is general.

**2.** Weight × index: 40 × 112 = 4,480; 25 × 104 = 2,600; 20 × 108 = 2,160; 15 × 96 = 1,440 [1]. Total = 10,680 [1]. CPI = 10,680 ÷ 100 = **106.8** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each product and the total so that your method is easy to follow; a bare number gives a reader nothing to check.

**3.** Change = 111.1 − 106.8 = 4.3 [1]. 4.3 ÷ 106.8 × 100 = **4.0%** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the old CPI. Using 111.1 as the denominator gives 3.9% and is a common slip.

**4.** (98.2 − 100.0) ÷ 100.0 × 100 [1] = **−1.8%** [1]. A fall in the general price level is **deflation** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the minus sign. Writing 1.8% suggests inflation and risks losing the point, and the term should be named when asked.

**5.** Higher interest rates make borrowing dearer and saving more rewarding [1]. Households and firms cut spending and investment, so aggregate demand falls or grows more slowly [1]. Firms face less pressure to raise prices, so inflation falls [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Build a chain of linked steps. Saying "it reduces spending" without why it reduces spending is only part of a chain.

**6.** Pay rise = (3,360 − 3,200) ÷ 3,200 × 100 = **5.0%** [1]. Real pay = 3,360 ÷ 1.062 [1] = **KD 3,163.8** [1], which is less than 3,200 so she is **worse off** (real pay down about 1.1%) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the conclusion, backed by your figure. A pay rise smaller than the rise in prices is a real fall, whatever the pay packet shows.

**7.** Menu costs are the costs to firms of changing prices [1], such as reprinting menus or relabelling goods [1]. Shoe leather costs are the time and effort spent protecting the value of money [1], such as visiting several shops to compare prices or moving savings between accounts [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give one example for each term. Swapping the two definitions is the usual error.

**8.** Higher timber and energy costs raise the makers' average costs [1]. To keep profit they raise prices, so quantity demanded falls [1]. Firms sell fewer chairs and tables and cut production [1], so they need fewer workers [1]. Some makers cannot cover their costs and close [1]. Workers laid off may lack skills for other jobs, so unemployment rises [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each step to the next with a reason such as "so" or "which means". A list of effects without the reasoning stays on the surface.

**9.** Country T: 80 × 1.07 = **KD 85.60** [1]. Country V: 80 × 1.02 = **KD 81.60** [1]. Country T's chair is KD 4.00 dearer [1], so foreign buyers switch to the cheaper Country V chair [1]. Country T's export sales fall, along with revenue and perhaps jobs [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The exchange-rate condition in the question matters. Refer to the gap between the two prices, not to Country T's price alone.

**10. (a)** 150,000 × 3% = **KD 4,500** [1]. 150,000 × 6% = **KD 9,000** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** High inflation makes future costs and sales hard to predict, so the firm is less sure of the return on the machine [1]. Uncertainty makes the firm delay investment [1]. If interest rises to 6%, the loan costs KD 9,000 but the machine returns only KD 6,750 (4.5%), a loss of KD 2,250 a year [1], so the firm is likely to cancel [1]. Lower investment reduces future capacity [1]. Consumers worried about rising prices may cut spending, so the firm expects fewer orders [1]. This fall in confidence further discourages investment [1]. Judgement: the effect is largest if inflation is unpredictable and interest rates rise; if inflation stays steady and expected, firms may still invest [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the figures given. Comparing the KD 6,750 return with each interest cost is stronger than saying interest rates may affect investment.

**11.** For: higher rates raise borrowing costs, so spending and investment fall [1]. Saving becomes more attractive [1]. Falling demand eases pressure on prices [1]. Rates can be changed quickly and act across the whole economy [1]. Against: the effect takes time to work through [1]. Lower spending can reduce output and raise unemployment [1]. Borrowers with variable-rate debts suffer [1]. Higher rates may raise firms' costs, which does nothing for cost-push pressure [1]. Alternatives such as reducing government spending or raising taxes also cut demand, but are slower to agree and may be unpopular [1]. Judgement: higher interest rates suit demand-pull inflation well, because they target demand, especially when the economy is near capacity; the best response also depends on how high inflation is and how fragile growth is [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* A supported conclusion needs a reason, such as the cause of the inflation. Naming the cause is stronger than saying "it depends".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Definitions missing "general" or "sustained".
- Dividing by the new CPI rather than the old one.
- Dropping the minus sign when prices fall.
- Reading a CPI of 106.8 as 106.8% inflation.
- Comparing nominal pay with prices without converting to real terms.
- Swapping menu costs and shoe leather costs.
- Listing effects of inflation without a chain of reasoning.
- Ignoring the data in the question, such as the loan return or the exchange rate.
- Concluding "it depends" without saying on what.
