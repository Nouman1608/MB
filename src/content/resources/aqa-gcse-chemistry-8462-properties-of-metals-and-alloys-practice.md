These questions cover section **4.2.2.7 Properties of metals and alloys** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462)
specification, for teaching from September 2016 onwards (version 1.2). The content is examined on Paper 1 at
Foundation and Higher Tier, and no question here tests Higher-tier-only content.

Learn the content first in the [properties of metals and alloys study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-properties-of-metals-and-alloys/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-properties-of-metals-and-alloys-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** **(a)** Name the type of bonding in a metal. **[1]**
**(b)** State whether most metals have high or low melting points. **[1]**

**2.** **(a)** State what is meant by an alloy. **[1]**
**(b)** Name **one** alloy and the metals or elements it is made from. **[2]**

**3.** A pure metal is described as "too soft" for use as a bridge. State **one** change that makes the metal
harder. **[1]**

**4.** Copper melts at 1085 °C and sodium melts at 98 °C. Both have metallic bonding.

**(a)** Which of the two is a typical metal in terms of melting point? **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how the melting point of sodium differs from what you would expect for most metals. **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** Explain why iron has a high melting point. **[3]**

**6.** A blacksmith heats a bar of pure iron and hammers it into the shape of a hook.

**(a)** Explain, in terms of layers of atoms, why the metal can be hammered into shape without breaking. **[3]**
**(b)** State what stays the same in the structure while the layers move. **[1]**

**7.** Steel is an alloy of iron with a small amount of carbon. Pure iron is softer than steel.

**(a)** Describe the arrangement of atoms in pure iron. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why steel is harder than pure iron. **[3]**

**8.** Bronze is an alloy of copper and tin. A sculptor casts a 400 g statue from bronze that is 90% copper by mass.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of copper in the statue. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of tin. **[1]**

**9.** The table shows the results of a hardness test on a pure metal and on two alloys made from it.

| Material | Percentage of the second metal | Hardness value (higher means harder) |
|---|---|---|
| Pure metal | 0 | 35 |
| Alloy 1 | 5 | 52 |
| Alloy 2 | 10 | 71 |

**(a)** Describe the trend in the results. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the increase in hardness value from the pure metal to Alloy 2. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest an explanation for the trend in terms of the atoms present. **[3]**

**10.** A student writes: "Alloys are harder than pure metals because the bonds between the atoms are stronger."

**(a)** Identify what is wrong with the student's explanation. **[2]**
**(b)** Write a correct explanation. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Metallic** [1]
**(b)** **High** [1]

**2. (a)** A **mixture** of a metal with one or more other elements [1]
**(b)** Any one named alloy with its components [1] [1]: for example **brass**: copper and zinc; **bronze**: copper and
tin; **steel**: iron and carbon. **[2]**

**3.** Make it into an **alloy** by mixing it with another element [1]

**4. (a)** **Copper** [1]
**(b)** Sodium's melting point is **much lower** than that of most metals [1]

**5.** Iron has a giant metallic structure [1]; there are strong metallic bonds between the positive ions and the
delocalised electrons, and there are many of them [1]; a large amount of energy is needed to overcome them [1].
**[3]**

**6. (a)** In pure iron the atoms are all the **same size** and arranged in **regular layers** [1]; when the metal is
hammered, the layers **slide over each other** [1]; the metal changes shape without breaking [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The **metallic bonding** holds the structure together / the delocalised electrons keep attracting the
ions [1]

**7. (a)** The atoms are **all the same size** [1] and are in **regular layers** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Steel contains carbon atoms of a **different size** from iron atoms [1]; these **distort** the layers [1]; the
layers cannot slide over each other as easily, so the steel is harder [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** 90 ÷ 100 × 400 = **360 g** [2] (1 for the method, 1 for the answer). **[2]**
**(b)** 400 - 360 = **40 g** [1]

**9. (a)** The hardness **increases** as the percentage of the second metal increases [1]
**(b)** 71 - 35 = **36** [1]
**(c)** The alloy contains atoms of a **different size** [1] that **distort the layers** of atoms [1], so the layers
cannot slide over each other so easily, so the alloy is harder [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** The explanation says nothing about the **layers** [1]; the metallic bonds are not stronger, so the idea
of "stronger bonds" is the wrong reason [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Marking points, up to 4:
- In a pure metal the atoms are the same size and in regular layers [1].
- The layers can slide over each other, so the metal is soft [1].
- In an alloy the atoms are of different sizes, which distorts the layers [1].
- The distorted layers cannot slide so easily, so the alloy is harder [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Explaining alloy hardness with "stronger bonds" instead of distorted layers.
- Writing that the atoms in a pure metal are different sizes.
- Forgetting "layers slide" when explaining why metals bend.
- Describing alloys as compounds.
- Not showing the method in a percentage calculation.
