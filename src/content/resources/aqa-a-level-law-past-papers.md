This page is a guide to AQA A Level Law (7162) past papers for the June 2026, June 2025 and June 2024 series. It gives the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and a short account of what examiners reported, written by us from AQA's Reports on the Examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

Use the three documents together. Sit a paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it with the mark scheme, which shows what each answer needs. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it explains why answers that looked sound still lost marks, and it shows how much of the mark depends on stating the rule, applying it to the facts and reaching a conclusion. The notes for each series below are a quick way into those reports. Law changes, so a paper's marking can depend on the law as it stood at the time; the reports flag where a rule has since been replaced.

Check which specification a paper was set on. 7162 is a linear, untiered qualification, first taught from September 2017 and first assessed in June 2019. It has three written papers, each 2 hours and 100 marks, with multiple-choice, short-answer and extended-writing questions. Each paper combines the nature of law and the English legal system (25 marks) with 75 marks of substantive law: criminal law on Paper 1, tort on Paper 2, and either law of contract or human rights on Paper 3. All the series on this page were set on this specification.

## June 2025

The June 2025 reports describe many strong answers across the papers. Weaker answers lost marks in the same places: the rule was not stated accurately, the facts of the scenario were not used, or the instruction in the question was not followed.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (7162/1, criminal law): inaccurate explanation of principles, unclear application and failure to answer the question set. An out-of-date test for dishonesty in theft was still used. The route of appeal was named but not explained, and explanations of gross negligence manslaughter repeated that the negligence was gross without developing why. Some answers gave detailed discussion of an offence the question had not asked about.
- Paper 2 (7162/2, tort): candidates were not secure on the structure of the courts, on the difference between consolidation and codification, or on the roles of law reform bodies. In the occupiers' liability scenario, weaker answers did not use the statutory framework or offered a narrow range of material in a brief response. Scenario facts were sometimes not properly used.
- Paper 3A (7162/3A, contract): knowledge of statutory instruments was thin and many did not attempt that question. Candidates spent time establishing that a contract existed when the facts made it clear. One consumer question was answered by adding rules the question did not ask about. Rescission was again used as a general word for ending a contract after a breach.
- Paper 3B (7162/3B, human rights): the human rights material was handled better than the domestic law. Weaker answers were incomplete or vague on police powers, confused an arrest with a stop and search, and sometimes thought a private individual could be sued directly under the Convention. Some candidates spent too long on the scope of a right and not enough on applying it. Time was unevenly split in one two-part question, and a significant number did not attempt one of the earlier questions.

**What stronger answers did:** combined accurate law with analysis to build a supported conclusion, used the facts selectively, and followed the instruction in the question exactly.

## June 2024

The June 2024 reports record fewer unattempted questions on Paper 1, an improved ability to spot the issues in scenarios, and much better awareness of who is bound by the Convention on Paper 3B.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (7162/1): not following the instruction, such as discussing murder or other defences when a particular defence was asked for, which earned no credit and used up time. A high number of candidates did not attempt the question on criminal law theory, although the report reminds centres that theory can be examined there. Magistrates' roles, causation and the rules on attempts were also weak points.
- Paper 2 (7162/2): weaker scripts did not identify and explain the relevant law, or did not relate it to the scenario. Answers on law and morality tended to assert a link without explaining it, and some gave few or no examples.
- Paper 3A (7162/3A): scenario answers that ran through the whole of contract formation when only one aspect was needed, discussion of formation when the instruction asked for breach, and the same confusion between rescission and ending a contract after a breach.
- Paper 3B (7162/3B): knowledge of the Convention was stronger than knowledge of domestic powers, with PACE and public order rules often named but not explained. Some candidates ran out of time on the non-substantive part of the last question.

**What stronger answers did:** supported principles with authority, picked the facts from the scenario that mattered, and mixed law and application instead of setting out the law first and applying it afterwards.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find AQA 7162 past papers?**
AQA publishes question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the assessment-resources page for 7162, and its past-paper finder lists papers by series.

**Why are there two threshold rows for Law?**
Paper 3 has two options. AQA publishes one subject boundary row for the candidates who took the contract option and one for those who took the human rights option.

**Do examiner reports mention changes in the law?**
Yes. The June 2025 Paper 1 report says an older test for dishonesty in theft was still being used by some candidates, so always check that you are using the current rule.

**What do examiners say most often about 7162?**
Follow the instruction in the question exactly, state the rule accurately, and use the facts of the scenario in the application instead of reciting the law first.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
