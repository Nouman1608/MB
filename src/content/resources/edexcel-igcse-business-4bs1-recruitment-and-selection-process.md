This guide covers section 2.2, Recruitment and selection process, in Topic 2 (People in business) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1) specification, Issue 2, February 2026. The specification lists:

- 2.2.1 types of employment: full-time, part-time, job share, casual/seasonal/temporary
- 2.2.2 recruitment documents: job description, person specification, application form, curriculum vitae (CV)/résumé
- 2.2.3 internal and external recruitment: job advertisement, shortlisting, interviewing
- 2.2.4 legal controls over employment and their effects: equal opportunities (gender, race, disability, religion, sexual preference, age) and minimum wage laws.

Paper 1 is set on a small business, where the owner may recruit personally and a single bad appointment matters a great deal. Paper 2 is set on a large one, which may have a human resources department and recruit in several countries. The first overview, with communication, is in the [communication, recruitment and selection guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-communication-recruitment-and-selection/). Companion pages: the [recruitment and selection revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-recruitment-and-selection-process-revision-notes/) and the [recruitment and selection practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-recruitment-and-selection-process-practice/). Businesses and figures are invented; amounts are in dollars. The specification names these items without describing them, so the descriptions are standard ones.

## 1. The process in order

1. **Identify the vacancy**: someone leaves, the business grows, or a new skill is needed.
2. **Decide the type of employment**: full-time, part-time, job share or casual/seasonal/temporary.
3. **Write the job description** (the job) and the **person specification** (the person wanted).
4. **Choose internal or external recruitment**, and write the **job advertisement**.
5. **Receive applications**: application forms or CVs.
6. **Shortlist**: compare applicants with the person specification.
7. **Interview** (and any tests), then **appoint** and make a job offer.

Equal opportunities law applies at every step, and minimum wage laws apply to the pay offered. Recruitment means attracting applicants; selection means choosing between them.

## 2. Choosing the type of employment (2.2.1)

The choice depends on when and how much work there is.

| Type | Best when | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Full-time | Work is steady all year; the role needs continuity | Paid even in quiet periods |
| Part-time | Work peaks at certain hours or days | More people to manage and train |
| Job share | A full-time role must be covered but staff want fewer hours | Handover between sharers |
| Casual/seasonal/temporary | Demand rises for a period, or for a one-off task | Less commitment; training each time |

### Worked example 1: seasonal or full-time?

Larchfield Garden Centre needs extra help for a 12-week spring peak. Pay is 11.00 an hour.

- **Option A:** two extra full-time staff all year at 37.5 hours a week: 2 × 52 × 37.5 × 11.00 = **42,900**.
- **Option B:** four seasonal staff for 12 weeks at 30 hours a week: 4 × 12 × 30 × 11.00 = **15,840**.

Option B costs 27,060 less and gives more hands at the peak. Option A gives staff who know the business, so the right answer depends on whether there is useful work for them outside the season.

## 3. Writing the documents (2.2.2)

The **job description** describes the job: title, duties, responsibilities, hours, place of work, who the post reports to. The **person specification** describes the ideal person: qualifications, skills, experience and qualities, often split into **essential** and **desirable**.

### Worked example 2: a person specification

Role: delivery driver at Calder Bakery.

| Criterion | Essential | Desirable |
|---|---|---|
| Qualifications | Full driving licence | First-aid certificate |
| Experience | — | Previous delivery work |
| Skills | Reads maps or navigation apps; handles payments accurately | Basic vehicle checks |
| Qualities | Reliable, polite to customers | Flexible about early starts |

Every criterion is linked to the job. Nothing refers to gender, age, race, religion, sexual preference or disability, because those would be unlawful grounds for choosing.

**Application form or CV?** An application form asks every applicant the same questions, so answers are easy to compare against the person specification. A CV lets applicants present themselves in their own way, which can show initiative but makes comparison harder. A large business recruiting many staff usually prefers forms; a small business may accept CVs.

## 4. Internal or external recruitment (2.2.3)

| | Internal | External |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Filling the post from existing staff | Filling the post from outside |
| Benefits | Cheaper and quicker; applicant knows the business; motivates staff by showing promotion is possible | Wider choice; new skills and ideas; no knock-on vacancy |
| Limitations | Small pool; no fresh ideas; creates another vacancy | Costly and slow; more risk with an unknown person; longer induction |

### Worked example 3: cost per appointment

Ravenscroft Hotels plc fills 40 posts a year externally. It spends 18,000 on online job advertisements and 52,000 on agency fees.

- Total: 18,000 + 52,000 = 70,000.
- Cost per appointment: 70,000 ÷ 40 = **1,750**.

If it filled 10 of the posts internally, at about 300 each for internal advertising and interviews, and the external costs fell in proportion, the total would be 30 × 1,750 + 10 × 300 = **55,500**, a saving of 14,500.

A **job advertisement** should state the job title, main duties, pay, location, key requirements, how to apply and the closing date. Where it is placed depends on who the business wants to reach.

## 5. Shortlisting and interviewing (2.2.3)

**Shortlisting** removes applicants who do not meet the essential criteria, then ranks the rest.

### Worked example 4: a simple scoring grid

Each applicant for the driver role is scored from 0 to 5 against four criteria from the person specification.

| Applicant | Licence (essential) | Navigation | Payments | Experience | Total |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | Yes | 4 | 3 | 5 | 12 |
| B | No | — | — | — | Rejected |
| C | Yes | 5 | 4 | 2 | 11 |
| D | Yes | 3 | 5 | 4 | 12 |

B is rejected for missing an essential criterion. A and D are shortlisted first. Scoring against the same criteria makes the choice fairer and gives a record if the decision is questioned.

**Interviewing** tests claims, communication and attitude. Asking every candidate the same core questions keeps it fair. Interviews can mislead: confident candidates may impress more than able ones, so businesses often add practical tests, such as a driving assessment.

## 6. Legal controls and their effects (2.2.4)

**Equal opportunities** laws prevent less favourable treatment because of gender, race, disability, religion, sexual preference or age. Effects at each stage:

- Adverts and person specifications must state real job requirements only: "recent graduate" or "young and energetic" could exclude applicants by age.
- Interview questions must relate to the job.
- Reasonable changes may be needed for a disabled applicant, such as an accessible interview room.
- Benefits: a wider pool of talent and a better reputation. Costs: training interviewers, keeping records, and fines or compensation if the law is broken.

**Minimum wage laws** set the lowest pay a business may legally offer.

### Worked example 5: a rise in the minimum wage

Calder Bakery employs six part-time staff for 20 hours a week each at the current legal minimum of 9.50 an hour (an invented figure). The minimum rises to 10.10.

- Extra cost per week: 6 × 20 × 0.60 = 72.
- Extra cost per year: 72 × 52 = **3,744**.

The bakery must pay it. It can raise prices, accept lower profit, or look for savings elsewhere; it cannot keep the old rate.

## 7. Annotated "justify" answer

**Justify whether Ravenscroft Hotels should fill a head chef vacancy internally or externally.** *Internally: the deputy chef already knows the menus and suppliers, so the kitchen keeps running smoothly, and promotion would motivate other kitchen staff. Externally would bring new ideas and a wider choice, but costs about 1,750 and risks an unknown person. Since the hotel wants to keep its current menu, internal recruitment is better, provided the deputy meets the essential criteria.*

## Common mistakes

- Swapping the job description (the job) and the person specification (the person).
- Treating recruitment and selection as the same stage.
- Listing benefits of internal recruitment with no limitation.
- Describing equal opportunities law with no effect on the business.
- Writing that a business can pay less than the minimum wage to part-time or casual staff.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do businesses use person specifications?**
They list the qualifications, skills, experience and qualities needed, so applicants can be shortlisted fairly against the same criteria.

**When is seasonal employment suitable?**
When demand rises for a predictable period, such as a holiday season, so the business pays for extra staff only when it needs them.
