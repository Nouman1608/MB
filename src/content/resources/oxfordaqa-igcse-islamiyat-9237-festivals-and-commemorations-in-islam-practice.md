## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. The
4-mark questions give one mark for each accurate detail, or one for each point and one for
explaining it. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a
general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the
question. The 12-mark questions are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** 1 Shawwal [1].

**2.** Qurbani [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: wash and wear their best clothes; eat something sweet such as dates; say the takbir; pray the Eid prayer in congregation; listen to the sermon; greet others with "Eid Mubarak"; visit family and friends.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: a part for the family; a part for relatives and friends; a part for the poor; given to charity.

**5.** Any four, [1] each: Sunni Muslims fast on Ashura [1]; they remember that Allah saved Musa (AS) and the Israelites from Pharaoh [1]; they often fast on the 9th or 11th as well [1]; Shia Muslims mourn the death of Hazrat Husayn (RA) at Karbala [1]; they hold gatherings where the story of Karbala is recited [1]; many wear black and some take part in processions [1].

**6.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **So the poor can celebrate** [1]: it gives food to those in need, so nobody is hungry on Eid [1].
- **To share the joy** [1]: Eid is a festival for the whole community, and the gift includes everyone [1].
- **To complete Ramadhan** [1]: it purifies the fast from small faults, and gratitude should be shown by giving [1].
- **It is the Sunnah** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) taught that it should be paid before the people go to pray [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **Obedience of Ibrahim (AS)** [1]: it remembers how Ibrahim and Ismail obeyed Allah, a model of putting Allah first [1].
- **Generosity** [1]: Qurbani meat is shared with the poor, so they can celebrate too [1].
- **Unity** [1]: it falls on the day after Arafat, so Muslims everywhere share the joy of the pilgrims [1].
- **Trust in Allah's mercy** [1]: Allah replaced the sacrifice with a ram, which teaches that He is merciful [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Their meat will not reach Allah, nor will their blood, but what reaches Him is piety from you" (Qur'an 22:37) [2].
- The hadith that "on the Day of Adha you eat the meat of your sacrifices" [2].

**8.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** Fitr marks the end of Ramadhan, so thanks to Allah for the strength to fast is central.
- **For the statement:** Adha remembers the mercy of Allah to Ibrahim and Ismail, which calls for gratitude.
- **For the statement:** "you break your fast" and "you eat the meat of your sacrifices" show that Allah allows enjoyment, as a blessing.
- **Against the statement:** the Eids also stress obedience (Qurbani), care for the poor (Zakah al-Fitr and shared meat) and community unity.
- **Against the statement:** the ban on fasting teaches that Muslims must accept Allah's gifts, not only do extra acts of worship.
- **Balanced view:** thanks is a main theme, but sacrifice, generosity and unity are equally important.

*Use of the passage*

- "forbade fasting on these two days" shows that the days are for celebration and gratitude.
- "the day when you break your fast" links to thanks for completing Ramadhan.
- "you eat the meat of your sacrifices" links to Qurbani and sharing.
- "He started with the prayer before the sermon" shows worship at the heart of the day.

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Lessons (AO1):

- **Gratitude to Allah** [1]: the Sunni fast remembers how Allah saved Musa (AS), so Muslims thank Allah for help [1].
- **Standing up for justice** [1]: Hazrat Husayn (RA) refused to accept what he saw as injustice, even at great cost [1].
- **Patience and sacrifice** [1]: the story of Karbala teaches patience in suffering for the truth [1].
- **Following the Prophet's example** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) fasted, which shows the value of obeying the Sunnah [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- The hadith in which the Prophet (pbuh) said Muslims have more right to Musa (AS) than others and fasted on the day [2].
- "O you who have believed, be persistently standing firm for Allah, witnesses in justice" (Qur'an 5:8) [2].

**10.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** Zakah al-Fitr and the Qurbani meat make sure the poor share the festival; Eid is for the whole community.
- **For the statement:** the passage says "you eat the meat of your sacrifices", so the sacrifice leads to a shared meal.
- **For the statement:** sharing food builds unity, kindness and gratitude, as well as satisfying hunger.
- **Against the statement:** the Eid prayer is the first act of the day, and the passage says that 'Umar began with it; worship comes first.
- **Against the statement:** Eid-ul-Adha is mainly about obedience and sacrifice, and Eid-ul-Fitr about thanks for Ramadhan; food is the means of celebrating.
- **Balanced view:** sharing is a core way of showing faith, but it is the result of worship and gratitude, not the only purpose.

*Use of the passage*

- "He started with the prayer before the sermon" supports the argument that worship is central.
- "on the Day of Adha you eat the meat of your sacrifices" supports the statement.
- "the day when you break your fast" shows the link to the end of Ramadhan.
- "forbade fasting on these two days" shows that the days are for enjoying Allah's gifts.
