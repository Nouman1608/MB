## Section A

**1.** State the total number of outer-shell electrons shown in a dot-and-cross diagram of methane, CH₄. **[1]**

**2.** In a dot-and-cross diagram of O₂, state the number of shared pairs and the number of lone pairs on each oxygen atom. **[2]**

**3.** Which species is drawn with one atom surrounded by exactly six outer electrons?

A: NH₃

B: BF₃

C: PCl₅

D: CH₄ **[1]**

**4.** State how a coordinate bond is distinguished from an ordinary covalent bond in a dot-and-cross diagram. **[1]**

**5.** State the number of electrons around the phosphorus atom in a dot-and-cross diagram of PCl₅. **[1]**

**6.** Give two errors in a student's diagram of calcium fluoride that shows a single F²⁻ ion next to a Ca²⁺ ion with no brackets. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Draw a dot-and-cross diagram for magnesium oxide. Show outer electrons only. **[3]**

**8.** Draw a dot-and-cross diagram for ethene, C₂H₄. **[3]**

**9.** Ammonia reacts with hydrogen chloride to form ammonium chloride.

**(a)** Draw a dot-and-cross diagram of the ammonium ion. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the total number of outer electrons shown in your diagram. **[1]**

**10.** Aluminium chloride exists as Al₂Cl₆ molecules in the vapour at lower temperatures.

**(a)** Calculate the total number of outer electrons in Al₂Cl₆. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the dot-and-cross diagram of Al₂Cl₆, stating the number of bonding pairs, the number of coordinate bonds, and the lone pairs on terminal and bridging chlorine atoms. **[4]**

**11.** Sulfur dioxide, SO₂, has an expanded octet.

**(a)** Draw a dot-and-cross diagram of SO₂. **[3]**

**(b)** State the number of electrons around the sulfur atom. **[1]**

**12.** Nitrogen monoxide, NO, has an odd number of electrons.

**(a)** Calculate the total number of outer electrons. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe a dot-and-cross diagram of NO in which the atoms are joined by a double bond. **[2]**

**(c)** State the number of electrons around the nitrogen atom in your diagram, and explain why nitrogen cannot reach an octet. **[2]**

**13.** Boron trifluoride, BF₃, reacts with ammonia, NH₃, to form a single molecule, H₃NBF₃, in which nitrogen is bonded to boron.

**(a)** Use the electron count around boron in BF₃ to explain why BF₃ can accept a lone pair. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe how the new N–B bond is shown in a dot-and-cross diagram of H₃NBF₃. **[2]**

**(c)** State the number of electrons around boron in H₃NBF₃. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** 8 [1].

**2.** Two shared pairs [1]; two lone pairs on each oxygen [1].

**3.** B [1].

**4.** Both electrons of the shared pair have the same symbol (both from the donor atom) [1].

**5.** 10 [1].

**6.** There should be two separate F⁻ ions (not one F²⁻) [1]; each ion should be in square brackets with its charge shown [1].

**7.** [Mg]²⁺ in brackets with charge 2+, outer shell empty (or the full shell beneath shown) [1]; [O]²⁻ in brackets with charge 2− [1]; the oxide ion shows eight electrons: six of oxygen's own symbol and two of magnesium's symbol [1].

**8.** Two shared pairs between the carbon atoms (each one dot and one cross) [1]; one shared pair between each carbon and each of four hydrogen atoms [1]; each carbon has eight electrons and each hydrogen two, with no lone pairs [1].

**9. (a)** Four shared pairs around nitrogen [1]; three are one N electron and one H electron, and one contains two N electrons (the coordinate bond) [1]; the ion is drawn in square brackets with a + charge [1].

**(b)** 8 (5 + 4 − 1) [1].

**10. (a)** 2 × 3 + 6 × 7 = 48 [1].

**(b)** Eight Al–Cl bonding pairs in total [1], of which two are coordinate bonds, each from a bridging Cl to the aluminium of the other unit [1]. Each of the four terminal Cl atoms has three lone pairs [1]. Each of the two bridging Cl atoms has two lone pairs, and each Al has eight electrons [1].

**11. (a)** Two shared pairs between S and each O (double bonds) [1]; one lone pair on S [1]; two lone pairs on each O [1].

**(b)** 10 [1].

**12. (a)** 5 + 6 = 11 [1].

**(b)** Two shared pairs between N and O, each one dot and one cross, with two lone pairs on O [1]; one lone pair and one single unpaired electron on N [1].

**(c)** Seven [1]. The total of 11 is odd, so one electron cannot be paired; giving N an octet would need an extra electron that the molecule does not have [1].

**13. (a)** Boron has only six outer electrons in BF₃ (three bonding pairs), so it has an empty orbital that can accept a pair [1].

**(b)** The N–B bond is a shared pair made of two electrons with nitrogen's symbol, both from the lone pair on nitrogen [1]; it is a coordinate bond, which can be labelled with an arrow from N to B [1].

**(c)** Eight [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Drawing shared electrons between ions in an ionic compound.
- Missing brackets or charges on ions.
- Showing a coordinate bond as one dot and one cross.
- Forgetting lone pairs on outer atoms.
- Giving boron a lone pair in BF₃ to make an octet.
- Not adjusting the electron total for charge.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to draw circles for electron shells?**
Circles are optional. What matters is that the electrons are clearly paired and the origin of each electron is clear from its symbol.
