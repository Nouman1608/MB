## Key facts

- Pakistan came into being in **1947**, with the partition of British India into two independent states, India and Pakistan.
- **قائدِ اعظم** is the title of Muhammad Ali Jinnah (محمد علی جناح), founder of Pakistan and its first Governor-General.
- **دو قومی نظریہ** (two-nation theory): the idea that Muslims and Hindus of the subcontinent were two separate nations; the basis of the demand for Pakistan.
- **قراردادِ پاکستان**: the Pakistan (Lahore) Resolution, passed in Lahore in March 1940, calling for Muslim-majority areas to form independent states. Check dates and wording against your sources before using them.

## Issues and perspectives

**Causes: the road to 1947.** The All-India Muslim League was founded in 1906 in Dhaka to represent Muslim political interests. Thinkers and leaders argued that Muslims had a distinct religious, cultural and historical identity: Sir Syed Ahmad Khan is often associated with early forms of this idea, and Allama Muhammad Iqbal (**علامہ اقبال**), in his 1930 address at Allahabad, set out the vision of a Muslim homeland in north-western India. Under Jinnah's leadership the League adopted the Lahore Resolution at its session in Lahore in March 1940; the resolution was moved on 23 March, the date now marked each year as **یومِ پاکستان**. Many Muslims feared that in a united, independent India with a Hindu majority their rights and identity would not be protected.

**Events of 1947.** After the Second World War Britain decided to leave India. The plan announced in June 1947 provided for partition, and a boundary commission drew the new border. Pakistan was created on **14 August 1947**, celebrated as **یومِ آزادی**. Karachi became the first capital, Jinnah became Governor-General and Liaquat Ali Khan the first Prime Minister. At that time Pakistan had two wings, West and East Pakistan; the eastern wing became Bangladesh in 1971.

**Consequences.** Partition was followed by one of the largest migrations in history: Muslims moved to Pakistan and Hindus and Sikhs to India, often on foot or in crowded trains. Communal violence (**فسادات**) killed very large numbers of people, and many families were separated. The new state faced the task of settling refugees, building a government almost from nothing, and disputes over the division of assets and over Kashmir. Jinnah died in September 1948, only about a year after independence.

**Different perspectives.**

- **Celebration**: for many Pakistanis 1947 is a story of freedom, sacrifice (**قربانی**) and the achievement of a homeland, remembered every 14 August.
- **Trauma**: for families who lost relatives, homes and land, the memory is also one of grief. Urdu literature, including Khadija Mastur's novel [Aangan](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-aangan-revision-notes/), shows how the politics of the time reached into ordinary homes.
- **Historians' debates**: historians still discuss whether partition could have been avoided, how far British decisions and timing contributed to the violence, and what kind of state the founders intended. You do not need to settle these debates, but showing that they exist adds depth.

## Writing about 1947

Give causes, events and consequences in order, name the terms in Urdu, and keep to facts you have checked in a source. Where you give an opinion, support it with a reason and use a connective such as **نتیجتاً** or **اس کے علاوہ**.

## Useful sentences with grammar notes

1. **پاکستان 14 اگست 1947 کو معرضِ وجود میں آیا۔** (Pakistan came into being on 14 August 1947.) Dates take **کو**; پاکستان takes masculine agreement, so آیا.
2. **تقسیم کے وقت لاکھوں لوگوں کو اپنا گھر بار چھوڑنا پڑا۔** (At the time of partition, vast numbers of people had to leave their homes.) Obligation with **کو + infinitive + پڑنا**; گھر بار is masculine, so چھوڑنا پڑا.
3. **قائدِ اعظم کی قیادت میں مسلم لیگ نے ایک الگ وطن کا مطالبہ کیا۔** (Under the Quaid-e-Azam's leadership, the Muslim League demanded a separate homeland.) With **نے**, the verb agrees with مطالبہ (masculine): کیا.
4. **23 مارچ 1940 کو لاہور میں قراردادِ پاکستان پیش کی گئی۔** (The Pakistan Resolution was presented in Lahore on 23 March 1940.) Passive agreeing with قرارداد (feminine): پیش کی گئی. The same pattern gives منظور کی گئی (was passed).
5. **آزادی کی خوشی کے ساتھ ساتھ ہجرت کا دکھ بھی اس دور کی یادوں کا حصہ ہے۔** (Alongside the joy of independence, the pain of migration is also part of the memories of that era.) **کے ساتھ ساتھ** (along with) is useful for balance.

## Model paragraph

قیامِ پاکستان برصغیر کی تاریخ کا ایک اہم موڑ تھا۔ دو قومی نظریے کے مطابق مسلمان اور ہندو دو الگ قومیں تھے، اس لیے مسلم لیگ نے قائدِ اعظم محمد علی جناح کی قیادت میں مسلم اکثریتی علاقوں کے لیے الگ وطن کا مطالبہ کیا۔ مارچ 1940 میں لاہور میں منظور ہونے والی قراردادِ پاکستان اس جدوجہد کا ایک سنگِ میل تھی۔ تاہم آزادی کی خوشی کے ساتھ تقسیم کا دکھ بھی آیا، کیونکہ فسادات میں بے شمار لوگ مارے گئے اور لاکھوں خاندانوں کو اپنا گھر بار چھوڑ کر ہجرت کرنی پڑی۔ میرے خیال میں 1947 کو صرف ایک سیاسی واقعہ نہیں بلکہ ایک انسانی تجربہ بھی سمجھنا چاہیے۔

**Translation:** The creation of Pakistan was an important turning point in the history of the subcontinent. According to the two-nation theory, Muslims and Hindus were two separate nations, so the Muslim League, under the leadership of Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah, demanded a separate homeland for the Muslim-majority areas. The Pakistan Resolution, passed in Lahore in March 1940, was a milestone in this struggle. However, with the joy of independence came the pain of partition, because countless people were killed in the riots and vast numbers of families had to leave their homes and migrate. In my opinion, 1947 should be understood not only as a political event but also as a human experience.

**Why it works:** cause, key event and consequence appear in order; the facts are ones you can verify; and **تاہم** turns the paragraph from celebration to cost. Note **ہجرت کرنی پڑی**: the infinitive agrees with ہجرت (feminine), so کرنی, and **قرارداد ... سنگِ میل تھی** agrees with the feminine subject.

## Discussion and essay prompts

- «دو قومی نظریہ قیامِ پاکستان کی بنیاد تھا۔» (The two-nation theory was the foundation of the creation of Pakistan.) Explain and evaluate.
- «آزادی کی قیمت بہت بھاری تھی۔» (The price of independence was very heavy.) Discuss with reference to partition and migration.
- Why do 14 August and 23 March matter to Pakistanis today?
- How did the problems of 1947 shape the early years of the new state?

## Exam traps

- Quoting dates, figures or wording from memory without checking.
- Telling a narrative with no analysis.
- Confusing ہجرت (migration) with تقسیم (partition).
- Mixing up 23 March (Lahore Resolution, Pakistan Day) and 14 August (Independence Day).
- Giving precise death or migration totals; estimates vary, so describe the scale in words.
- Writing about the other side of the border with hostility; analyse, do not insult.
