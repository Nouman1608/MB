## Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the term alloy. **[1]**

**2.** Which metals are mixed to make brass?

A: copper and tin

B: copper and zinc

C: iron and zinc

D: iron and copper

**[1]**

**3.** Stainless steel contains iron and other elements. Name **two** of the other elements. **[2]**

**4.** State **two** properties of stainless steel that make it suitable for cutlery. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why an alloy is described as a mixture and not a compound. **[1]**

**6.** Steel contains carbon, which is a non-metal. Explain why steel is still an alloy. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** Brass is harder than pure copper.

**(a)** Describe the arrangement of atoms in pure copper. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why pure copper is relatively soft and malleable. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain, in terms of structure, why brass is harder than copper. **[2]**

**8.** Four particle diagrams are described.

- Diagram 1: circles all the same size, arranged in regular rows with no gaps.
- Diagram 2: mostly large circles in rows, with a few small circles scattered among them.
- Diagram 3: alternating positive and negative ions in a regular grid.
- Diagram 4: pairs of joined circles, each pair separate from the others, with spaces between them.

**(a)** Identify the diagram that represents an alloy. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify the diagram that represents a pure metal. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how you identified the alloy. **[1]**

**(d)** Suggest what type of substance diagram 4 represents. **[1]**

**9.** A student writes: "Alloys are harder because the bigger atoms of the added metal stop the layers moving at all."

**(a)** Identify **two** errors in this statement. **[2]**

**(b)** Rewrite the statement correctly. **[2]**

**10.** Spoons can be made from pure iron, ordinary steel or stainless steel.

**(a)** Explain why pure iron is unsuitable. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why stainless steel is chosen rather than ordinary steel. **[1]**

**11.** The proportions of copper and zinc in brass can be changed.

**(a)** Explain why this shows that brass is not a compound. **[1]**

**(b)** Brass still conducts electricity. Explain why. **[1]**

**12.** Aircraft are made from aluminium alloys rather than pure aluminium.

**(a)** Suggest the property of aluminium that the alloy keeps. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest why the alloy is used instead of the pure metal. Refer to structure in your answer. **[2]**

**13.** A student has a strip of pure copper and a strip of brass of the same size and shape.

**(a)** Suggest a simple, fair comparison the student could carry out to show that brass is harder than copper. State the result expected. **[3]**

**(b)** State **one** variable that must be kept the same in this comparison. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** A mixture of a metal with other elements [1].

**2.** B [1].

**3.** Any two: chromium; nickel; carbon [2].

**4.** Hard [1]; resistant to rusting [1].

**5.** The elements are not chemically combined / the proportions can vary / there is no fixed formula [1].

**6.** An alloy is a mixture of a metal with other elements, which can include non-metals such as carbon [1].

**7. (a)** All the atoms are the same size, arranged in regular layers [1].

**(b)** The layers can slide over each other easily [1].

**(c)** Zinc atoms are a different size from copper atoms and disrupt the layers [1]; so the layers can no longer slide over each other easily [1].

**8. (a)** Diagram 2 [1].

**(b)** Diagram 1 [1].

**(c)** It has particles of a different size mixed within the main metal lattice [1].

**(d)** A simple molecular substance (diatomic molecules) [1].

**9. (a)** "Bigger" should be "different sized" (added atoms can be smaller, as carbon in steel is) [1]; the layers can still move, but cannot slide over each other easily [1].

**(b)** Alloys are harder because atoms of a different size disrupt the regular layers [1], so the layers cannot slide over each other easily [1].

**10. (a)** It rusts when wet / when washed [1]; it is relatively soft, so the spoon would bend [1].

**(b)** Stainless steel resists rusting, whereas ordinary steel rusts [1].
*Marking note:* in (a), "it is weak" is not accepted for the second mark; the expected property is that pure iron is relatively **soft** (its layers slide easily), so it bends.

**11. (a)** A compound has a fixed composition / formula; brass does not [1].

**(b)** It still has metallic bonding / delocalised electrons that carry charge [1].

**12. (a)** Low density [1].

**(b)** The alloy is harder and stronger [1] because atoms of a different size stop the layers sliding over each other easily [1].

**13. (a)** Any sensible method, for example: press / drop the same weight onto each strip from the same height, or scratch each strip with the same object using the same force [1]; measure or compare the size of the dent or scratch [1]; brass shows the smaller dent / scratch [1].

**(b)** Any one: the force or weight used; the height it is dropped from; the size or thickness of the strips; the object used to scratch or dent [1].

*Marking note:* this is a "suggest" question, so any safe, fair method that compares resistance to denting or scratching is creditworthy. The result mark needs a direction: "brass is harder" without the observation that shows it does not score.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing tin instead of zinc in brass.
- "Bigger atoms" instead of "different sized atoms".
- Describing only the alloy, not the pure metal, in a hardness explanation.
- Giving one property for stainless steel cutlery.
- Choosing an ionic lattice diagram as an alloy because it contains two kinds of particle.
