## Section A

**1.** Name the organic product when methylbenzene is heated with alkaline KMnO₄ and the mixture is then acidified. **[1]**

**2.** Write a balanced equation for the reaction of ethanoic acid with PCl₅. **[1]**

**3.** State the inorganic products when a carboxylic acid reacts with SOCl₂. **[1]**

**4.** Name a reagent that gives a positive result with methanoic acid but not with ethanoic acid, and state the observation. **[2]**

**5.** State the type of effect that makes chloroethanoic acid stronger than ethanoic acid. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Propanoic acid can be converted into propanoyl chloride using PCl₃, PCl₅ or SOCl₂.

**(a)** Write a balanced equation for the reaction with PCl₃, and state the condition. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why SOCl₂ is often chosen in preference to PCl₅. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the apparatus must be dry. **[1]**

**7.** Benzoic acid is made from methylbenzene.

**(a)** State the reagents and conditions. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the final acidification step is needed. **[1]**

**(c)** 4.60 g of methylbenzene gives 4.27 g of benzoic acid. Calculate the percentage yield. **[2]**

**8.** Ethanedioic acid reacts with acidified potassium manganate(VII).

**(a)** Write the ionic equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** State the condition needed and the colour change at the end-point of a titration. **[2]**

**(c)** 25.0 cm³ of ethanedioic acid solution reacts exactly with 22.50 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ KMnO₄. Calculate the concentration of the ethanedioic acid. **[3]**

**9.** Methanoic acid is oxidised by acidified potassium dichromate(VI).

**(a)** State the observation. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the carbon-containing product. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain, in terms of structure, why methanoic acid is oxidised but propanoic acid is not. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why ethanoic acid is a stronger acid than phenol, and phenol is a stronger acid than ethanol. **[4]**

**11.** Three acids have the following illustrative Ka values, in mol dm⁻³: A, CH₃CH₂COOH, 1.3 × 10⁻⁵; B, CH₃CHClCOOH, 1.5 × 10⁻³; C, ClCH₂CH₂COOH, 1.0 × 10⁻⁴.

**(a)** Identify the strongest acid. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain the order of acid strength of A, B and C. **[3]**

**12.** Describe two tests, with expected results, that together distinguish methanoic acid, ethanoic acid and ethanol. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Benzoic acid [1].

**2.** CH₃COOH + PCl₅ → CH₃COCl + POCl₃ + HCl [1].

**3.** Sulfur dioxide and hydrogen chloride [1].

**4.** Tollens' reagent, warm [1]: silver mirror [1]. (Or Fehling's reagent, warm: red-brown precipitate.)

**5.** Inductive (electron-withdrawing) effect of the chlorine atom [1].

**6. (a)** 3CH₃CH₂COOH + PCl₃ → 3CH₃CH₂COCl + H₃PO₃ [1]; heat [1].

**(b)** Both by-products, SO₂ and HCl, are gases, so the acyl chloride is easier to separate (no POCl₃ to remove) [1].

**(c)** Water reacts with the chlorinating reagent and hydrolyses the acyl chloride, lowering the yield [1].

**7. (a)** Hot alkaline KMnO₄ [1], then dilute acid [1].

**(b)** In alkaline conditions the product is the benzoate ion; the acid protonates it to give benzoic acid [1].

**(c)** n(C₆H₅CH₃) = 4.60 / 92.0 = 0.0500 mol; theoretical mass of C₆H₅COOH = 0.0500 × 122.0 = 6.10 g [1]; yield = 4.27 / 6.10 × 100 = 70.0% [1].

**8. (a)** 5HOOCCOOH + 2MnO₄⁻ + 6H⁺ → 10CO₂ + 2Mn²⁺ + 8H₂O [1].

**(b)** Warm (about 60 °C) [1]; colourless to the first permanent pale pink [1].

**(c)** n(MnO₄⁻) = 0.0200 × 22.50 / 1000 = 4.50 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; n(acid) = 4.50 × 10⁻⁴ × 5/2 = 1.125 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; concentration = 1.125 × 10⁻³ / 0.0250 = 0.0450 mol dm⁻³ [1].

**9. (a)** Orange to green [1].

**(b)** Carbon dioxide [1].

**(c)** Methanoic acid has a hydrogen atom bonded directly to the carbonyl carbon, like an aldehyde, which can be oxidised [1]; in propanoic acid the carbonyl carbon is bonded to an alkyl group and –OH, with no such H, so it resists further oxidation [1].

**10.** In the ethanoate ion the negative charge is delocalised over two electronegative oxygen atoms [1], so it is the most stable anion. In the phenoxide ion the charge is spread from one oxygen into the ring carbons, which are less electronegative, so it is less stabilised [1]. In the ethoxide ion the charge stays on one oxygen and the electron-donating ethyl group intensifies it, so it is least stable [1]. The more stable the anion, the further the dissociation equilibrium lies to the right, so the acid strength order is ethanoic acid > phenol > ethanol [1].

**11. (a)** B, CH₃CHClCOOH (largest Ka) [1].

**(b)** Chlorine is electronegative and withdraws electron density through σ bonds (inductive effect), stabilising the carboxylate ion, so B and C are stronger than A [1]. In B the Cl is on carbon 2, next to –COOH [1]; in C it is on carbon 3, further away, and the inductive effect weakens with distance, so B > C > A [1].

**12.** Test 1: add Na₂CO₃(aq); both acids effervesce (CO₂), ethanol does not [1], identifying ethanol [1]. Test 2: warm the two acids with Tollens' reagent; methanoic acid gives a silver mirror [1], ethanoic acid gives no change [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Balancing PCl₃ with one acid molecule instead of three.
- Leaving out the dilute acid step in benzoic acid production.
- Forgetting to warm ethanedioic acid before titrating with KMnO₄.
- Using the wrong mole ratio (it is 5 : 2, acid : MnO₄⁻).
- Explaining chlorine-substituted acids with delocalisation instead of the inductive effect.
- Not mentioning the two oxygen atoms when explaining why carboxylic acids are the strongest.
