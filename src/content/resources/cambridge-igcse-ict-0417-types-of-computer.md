Section 1.4 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 compares the main types of computer. The syllabus names the **desktop computer** and four **mobile computers**: **laptop**, **smartphone**, **tablet** and **phablet**. For each you need its characteristics, its uses, and its advantages and disadvantages. The syllabus picks out two criteria by name, **portability** and **expandability**, and these two words unlock most comparison questions: the more portable a computer is, the less expandable it tends to be.

For condensed recall use the [types of computer revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-types-of-computer-revision-notes/), and test yourself with the [types of computer practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-types-of-computer-practice/).

## The two named criteria

- **Portability**: how easily the computer can be carried and used in different places. It depends on size, weight, battery power and built-in input and output devices.
- **Expandability**: how easily the computer can be upgraded or have extra hardware added, such as more RAM, a larger storage drive, a better graphics card or extra ports.

A desktop is the least portable and the most expandable. A smartphone is the most portable and the least expandable. Laptops, tablets and phablets sit in between.

## Desktop computers

### Characteristics

- Designed to stay in **one place**, on or under a desk.
- **Separate components**: a base unit (tower), monitor, keyboard and mouse are usually separate devices connected together. Some all-in-one designs build the base unit into the monitor.
- Run from **mains electricity**; there is no battery to recharge.
- The case has **space inside** for extra or larger components, and good **cooling** from fans.
- A **full-sized keyboard** and a large monitor are normally used.

### Uses named in the syllabus

- **Office and business management**: word processing, spreadsheets, databases and accounts on a worker's desk.
- **Education**: computer rooms in schools, research and coursework.
- **Gaming**: high-performance processors and graphics cards suit demanding games.
- **Entertainment**: watching films, editing video and music.

### Advantages

- **Expandable**: components can be upgraded or replaced, which extends the computer's useful life.
- Usually **better performance for the price**, because more powerful components fit and can be kept cool.
- **Ergonomic**: a full-sized keyboard and large monitor can be positioned to reduce health problems.
- **Less likely to be lost, stolen or dropped**, because it stays in one place.
- **No battery life limit**.

### Disadvantages

- **Not portable**: the user must work where the computer is.
- **Takes up desk space**, and the cables can be untidy.
- Needs a mains power supply, so work stops in a power cut unless a backup supply is used.
- Separate components can make it **harder to set up**.

## Mobile computers

### Laptop computers

A laptop combines the screen, keyboard, pointing device (usually a touchpad), battery and processing hardware in one folding case. It runs from a rechargeable battery or mains power and has a built-in camera, microphone and speakers.

- **Uses**: work and study away from a fixed desk, presentations at meetings, business travel.
- **Advantages**: portable, all components in one unit, can be used without a mains supply, takes little desk space, connects to wi-fi networks wherever available.
- **Disadvantages**: less expandable because the components are packed tightly; more expensive than a desktop of similar performance; smaller keyboard and screen; touchpad less comfortable than a mouse; easier to lose, steal or damage; battery needs recharging and becomes less effective with age.

### Smartphones

A smartphone is a mobile phone with the functions of a small computer: a touch screen, apps, internet access through mobile networks and wi-fi, cameras, GPS and sensors.

- **Uses**: calls and messages, email, web browsing, social networking, navigation, photographs, mobile payments, apps for learning and gaming, and **controlling devices remotely**.
- **Advantages**: the most portable type; almost always with the user; can connect to the internet in most places using the mobile network; many functions in one device.
- **Disadvantages**: very small screen, so documents and websites are hard to read and edit; on-screen keyboard is slow and error-prone for long text; limited processing power and storage compared with a laptop; battery may not last a full day under heavy use; easily lost or stolen, putting personal data at risk.

### Tablets

A tablet is a thin, flat computer controlled mainly by a touch screen, larger than a smartphone. Some can connect to the mobile network; others use wi-fi only.

- **Uses**: reading, watching video, education apps in classrooms, taking orders in restaurants, presentations to small groups, drawing with a stylus.
- **Advantages**: larger screen than a smartphone; lighter than a laptop; long battery life; quick to start; touch interface is easy to learn.
- **Disadvantages**: on-screen keyboard is less efficient than a physical one for long documents; lower processing power and storage than most laptops; limited ports and expandability; screens can crack if dropped.

### Phablets

A phablet is a smartphone with a large screen, between the size of a typical smartphone and a small tablet. It has all the features of a smartphone, including calls over the mobile network.

- **Uses**: as for a smartphone, with more comfortable reading, video watching and note-taking.
- **Advantages**: larger screen than a smartphone while remaining pocket-sized for many users; one device instead of carrying a phone and a tablet.
- **Disadvantages**: harder to use with one hand; may not fit in a pocket; still smaller than a tablet for detailed work; the larger screen uses more battery power.

## Remotely controlled devices

The syllabus lists **remotely controlled devices** as a use of mobile computers. A smartphone or tablet runs an app that sends commands over wi-fi, Bluetooth or the internet to a device such as a drone, a home heating system, a security camera or smart lighting. The user can change settings or check the device's status from another room or from the other side of the world.

## Worked example: recommending a computer

*An estate agent visits houses to photograph rooms and take measurements, then writes detailed descriptions back at the office. Compare the use of a tablet and a desktop computer for this work. [4]*

> At the houses, a tablet is more suitable because it is portable and battery-powered, so the agent can carry it from room to room (1), and its built-in camera and touch screen let photographs and notes be taken in one device (1). Back at the office, a desktop is better for writing long descriptions, because a full-sized keyboard and large monitor make typing faster and more comfortable (1), and it is less likely to be lost or stolen, which matters because it stores client data (1).

The answer scores because each point is tied to part of the agent's job and the two computers are contrasted, not described separately.

## Common mistakes

- Saying a laptop is "more powerful" than a desktop. The usual comparison is the opposite for a given price.
- Confusing expandability with storage capacity. Expandability is about adding or upgrading hardware.
- Describing a tablet as having a physical keyboard. Its main input is the touch screen; a keyboard is an optional extra.
- Giving "portable" as an advantage without saying why it matters in the scenario.
- Forgetting that the smallest devices are the easiest to lose, which is a security risk for stored data.

## Exam technique

- **Describe the characteristics**: physical features such as size, input method, power source and connectivity.
- **Give uses**: use the syllabus categories (office and business management, education, gaming, entertainment, remotely controlled devices) and add a short example.
- **Compare**: use matched pairs with "whereas", and make portability and expandability two of your points where they fit.
- **Recommend and justify**: name one computer type and link two features to the user's needs.
