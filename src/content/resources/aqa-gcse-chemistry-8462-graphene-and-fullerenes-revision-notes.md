These notes condense **4.2.3.3 Graphene and fullerenes** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification (for
teaching from September 2016, version 1.2). It is Paper 1 content for both tiers, with no Higher-only
statements and no required practical. For full explanations, see the
[graphene and fullerenes study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-graphene-and-fullerenes/); then practise
with the [graphene and fullerenes practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-graphene-and-fullerenes-practice/).

## Core facts

- **Graphene** is a **single layer of graphite**. Its properties make it useful in **electronics** and
  **composites**.
- **Fullerenes** are **molecules of carbon atoms with hollow shapes**.
- Their structure is based on **hexagonal rings**, but they may also contain rings of **five or seven** carbon
  atoms.
- The first fullerene discovered was **Buckminsterfullerene (C₆₀)**, which has a **spherical** shape.
- **Carbon nanotubes** are **cylindrical fullerenes** with very high length to diameter ratios, useful for
  **nanotechnology, electronics and materials**.

## Summary table

| Form | Shape | Bonds per C atom | Key feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Graphene | Flat sheet, one atom thick | 3 | Delocalised electrons; strong |
| C₆₀ | Hollow sphere | 3 | 60 carbon atoms; a molecule |
| Carbon nanotube | Hollow cylinder | 3 | Very long compared with its width |
| Graphite | Many layers | 3 | Layers slide |
| Diamond | 3D network | 4 | Very hard |

## Property explanations

| Property of graphene | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Conducts electricity | One delocalised electron per carbon atom, free to move |
| Very strong | Many strong covalent bonds across the sheet |
| Thin and light | A single layer of atoms |

## Uses

| Material | Use | Property used |
|---|---|---|
| Graphene | Electronics | Conducts electricity |
| Graphene | Composites | Strengthens without adding much mass |
| Nanotubes | Strengthening materials | Very strong |
| Nanotubes | Electronics | Can conduct electricity |
| Fullerenes | Drug delivery | Hollow shape can hold other molecules |
| Fullerenes | Catalysts and lubricants | Examples sometimes given; the exam question supplies the detail |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Graphene | A single layer of graphite, one atom thick |
| Fullerene | A hollow molecule made of carbon atoms |
| Buckminsterfullerene | C₆₀, the first fullerene, with a spherical shape |
| Carbon nanotube | A cylindrical fullerene |
| Composite | A material made from two or more different materials |
| Nanotechnology | The use of structures about 1 to 100 nm in size |
| Length to diameter ratio | How long a tube is compared with its width |

## Calculations

| Question | Working |
|---|---|
| Mr of C₆₀ | 60 × 12 = 720 |
| Bonds in C₆₀ (3 per atom, each shared) | 60 × 3 ÷ 2 = 90 |
| Atoms per hexagon in graphene (each shared by 3) | 6 ÷ 3 = 2 |

## Telling the forms apart in a diagram

| Diagram | Structure |
|---|---|
| Flat sheet of hexagons, one layer | Graphene |
| Several flat layers stacked | Graphite |
| Hollow ball with rings of five and six atoms | Fullerene (C₆₀) |
| Long hollow cylinder | Carbon nanotube |
| Atoms in a 3D network with four bonds each | Diamond |

## Evaluating uses

| Material | Advantage | Possible drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Nanotubes in sports equipment | Very strong, light | Costly to make |
| Graphene in electronics | Conducts, very thin | Not easy to make in large pieces |
| Fullerenes for drug delivery | Hollow shape can carry molecules | Possible risks to health not fully known |

Link each advantage to structure and bonding. State that there are possible risks associated with using
nanoparticles.

## Representations

| Representation | Limitation |
|---|---|
| 2D diagram of a sheet | Cannot show curving or stacking |
| Ball and stick model of C₆₀ | Sticks are not real bonds; atoms not to scale |
| Unrolled nanotube diagram | The real tube is curved |

A ring of 5 or 7 carbon atoms means the diagram shows a fullerene.

## Building a full-mark description

| Form | Say |
|---|---|
| Graphene | A single layer of graphite; each carbon bonded to three others in hexagonal rings; one delocalised electron per atom |
| Buckminsterfullerene | A hollow sphere of 60 carbon atoms, C₆₀, based on hexagonal rings with some five-membered rings |
| Carbon nanotube | A cylindrical fullerene with a very high length to diameter ratio |

Then link one property to the structure: strong covalent bonds give strength, and delocalised electrons give
electrical conduction.

## Command words

| Command word | What to write |
|---|---|
| Recognise | Name the structure and point to the feature in the diagram or description |
| Give an example of a use | State the use and the property it depends on |
| Explain | Link the property to bonding or delocalised electrons |
| Evaluate | Advantage, risk, and a conclusion |

## Exam traps

- **"Graphene is a thick layer."** It is one atom thick.
- **"C₆₀ is a giant structure."** It is a molecule.
- **"Fullerenes contain only hexagons."** They may also have rings with five or seven atoms.
- **"Nanotubes are flat."** They are cylinders.
- **Missing the delocalised electron** when explaining conduction.
- **Four bonds per atom for graphene.** It is three.

## Quick self-test

1. What is graphene?
2. How many covalent bonds does each carbon atom form in graphene?
3. Why does graphene conduct electricity?
4. What are fullerenes?
5. What is the formula of Buckminsterfullerene?
6. What shape is a carbon nanotube?
7. Give one use of carbon nanotubes.
8. Why can fullerenes have curved shapes?
9. Calculate the relative formula mass of C₆₀.
10. Give one use of graphene.

### Answers

1. A single layer of graphite, one atom thick.
2. Three.
3. One delocalised electron per atom is free to move and carry charge.
4. Molecules of carbon atoms with hollow shapes.
5. C₆₀.
6. A hollow cylinder with a very high length to diameter ratio.
7. Strengthening materials, or electronics.
8. They contain rings of five or seven carbon atoms as well as hexagons.
9. 60 × 12 = 720.
10. Electronics, or composites.
