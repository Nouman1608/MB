## Questions

**1.** Explain why carbon compounds formed spontaneously could accumulate on early Earth, but would not accumulate in the same way today. **[2]**

**2.** A student argues that viruses are living because they contain genes and evolve. Discuss this claim. **[3]**

**3.** Match each requirement for the origin of cells to a hypothesis that addresses it: catalysis, self-replication, self-assembly, compartmentalisation. Choose from: ribozymes; RNA copied by base pairing; fatty acids coalescing into bilayers; vesicles enclosing molecules. **[4]**

**4.** Explain why hypotheses about the origin of the first cells are difficult to test. **[2]**

**5.** In the Miller–Urey experiment, gases were circulated past electric sparks and then through a condenser.

**(a)** State what the sparks represented. **[1]**

**(b)** State the purpose of the condenser. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest why the apparatus was sealed and sterilised before the experiment. **[1]**

**(d)** State one reason why the results do not show that life formed this way. **[1]**

**6.** Fatty acid solutions of different concentration were examined for vesicles.

| Fatty acid concentration / mM | 0.5 | 2 | 5 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fatty acid in vesicles / % | 0 | 5 | 40 | 85 |

**(a)** Describe the trend in the data. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how spontaneous vesicle formation addresses two of the requirements for the origin of cells. **[2]**

**7.** Explain how the structure and function of the ribosome support the hypothesis that RNA was the first genetic material. **[2]**

**8.** Suggest why DNA, rather than RNA, became the store of genetic information in cells. **[2]**

**9.** Two lineages differ at 42 positions in a conserved gene. Differences between lineages are estimated to accumulate at 6 per 100 million years.

**(a)** Estimate the time since the lineages diverged. **[1]**

**(b)** Outline two reasons why this estimate is uncertain. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why fossil evidence of the earliest cells is scarce and often disputed. **[2]**

**11.** **(a)** Explain how the universal genetic code supports the existence of LUCA. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why LUCA should not be described as the first living cell. **[1]**

**12.** Suggest two features of deep-sea hydrothermal vents that could have favoured the origin of early life. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** There was little or no free oxygen, so organic molecules were not oxidised or broken down [1]. Today oxygen is abundant and living organisms would consume such molecules, so they would not accumulate [1] **[2]**

**2.** For: viruses have genetic material and change by natural selection, features shared with living things [1]. Against: they have no metabolism and cannot reproduce or carry out life functions outside a host cell, so they are not self-sustaining [1]. Conclusion: by the usual definition based on self-sustaining functions, viruses are classed as non-living, though the boundary depends on the definition used [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* "Discuss" needs both sides and a conclusion; a one-sided answer cannot reach full marks.

**3.** Catalysis: ribozymes [1]. Self-replication: RNA copied by base pairing [1]. Self-assembly: fatty acids coalescing into bilayers [1]. Compartmentalisation: vesicles enclosing molecules [1] **[4]**

**4.** The events happened billions of years ago and cannot be observed or repeated [1]; early protocells left no direct fossil record, so experiments can show only that a step is possible, not that it happened [1] **[2]**

**5. (a)** Lightning (an energy source) [1]

**(b)** To cool the vapour so products condensed and collected, as rain might have [1]

**(c)** So that no living organisms or contaminating organic matter could be the source of the products [1]

**(d)** It produced only monomers such as amino acids, not polymers, membranes or self-replicating molecules (or the gas mixture may not match early Earth) [1] **[4]**

**6. (a)** The percentage in vesicles increases with concentration [1]; no vesicles form at 0.5 mM, and the steepest rise is between 2 and 10 mM, reaching 85% [1]

**(b)** Self-assembly: the bilayer forms without enzymes or a template [1]. Compartmentalisation: the vesicle separates an internal space where molecules can be concentrated [1] **[4]**

**7.** The ribosome is made largely of rRNA [1], and the peptide bond is formed by catalytic rRNA, a ribozyme, so RNA catalysis is still central to protein synthesis [1] **[2]**

**8.** DNA is chemically more stable than RNA, as deoxyribose lacks the reactive –OH group on carbon 2′ [1]; being double-stranded, DNA has a complementary copy that can be used to correct damage [1] **[2]**

**9. (a)** 42 ÷ 6 × 100 = **700 million years** [1]

**(b)** The rate of change may not be constant over time or between lineages [1]; repeated changes at the same position hide earlier differences, and the calibration depends on uncertain fossil dates [1] **[3]**

**10.** Single cells are small and soft, so they rarely fossilise [1]; very old rocks have usually been heated or deformed, and simple structures can form without life, so their biological origin is debated [1] **[2]**

**11. (a)** Almost all organisms use the same codons for the same amino acids [1]; an arbitrary code shared by all is best explained by inheritance from one common ancestor [1]

**(b)** Other, earlier forms of life probably existed but left no surviving descendants; LUCA is the most recent ancestor shared by all life now [1] **[3]**

**12.** A supply of chemical energy from gradients between vent fluid and seawater [1]; minerals that could act as catalysts, and protection from ultraviolet radiation in the deep sea (either) [1] **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Claiming that Miller and Urey produced cells or life.
- Giving only one side in "discuss" or "evaluate".
- Describing vesicle formation as enzyme-controlled.
- Saying RNA came first because it is simpler, without storage and catalysis.
- Quoting a molecular clock estimate as exact.
- Confusing LUCA with the first cell.
