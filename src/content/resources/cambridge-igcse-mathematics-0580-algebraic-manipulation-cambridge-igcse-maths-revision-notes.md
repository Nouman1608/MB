## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Term | One part of an expression, with its sign | in 5x² − 3x + 2, the terms are 5x², −3x, +2 |
| Like terms | Same letters to the same powers | 4ab and −ba are like; a²b and ab² are not |
| Coefficient | The number multiplying the letters | coefficient of x in 7 − 4x is −4 |
| Expand | Multiply out the brackets | 3x(2x − 4y) = 6x² − 12xy |
| Factorise (fully) | Write as a product, taking out everything possible | 9x² + 15xy = 3x(3x + 5y) |
| Identity | True for every value of the letters | (x + 1)² ≡ x² + 2x + 1 |
| Completed square form | a(x + p)² + q | 2x² + 8x + 1 = 2(x + 2)² − 7 |

## Condensed recall

### Collecting and expanding

- Each term carries the sign in front of it. Underline or circle like terms before adding them.
- Single bracket: multiply every term inside by the term outside. −4(2x − 3) = −8x + 12.
- Two brackets: four products, then collect. (2x + 1)(x − 4) = 2x² − 8x + x − 4 = 2x² − 7x − 4.
- *(Extended)* Two variables: (3x + y)(x − 4y) = 3x² − 12xy + xy − 4y² = 3x² − 11xy − 4y².
- *(Extended)* Three brackets: expand two, simplify, then multiply by the third. Two brackets of two terms then one more give up to eight products before collecting.
- Squared bracket: (a ± b)² = a² ± 2ab + b². Never forget the middle term.

### Factorising

**Common factor.** Highest common factor of the numbers × lowest power of each shared letter. 12p³q − 18p²q² = 6p²q(2p − 3q).

**Decision route (Extended)**

```
1. Take out any common factor (a negative one if every term is negative).
2. Count the terms in the bracket:
     2 terms, squares, minus sign  -> difference of two squares
     3 terms                       -> quadratic (check for a perfect square)
     4 terms                       -> grouping
3. Check every bracket again.
```

| Form *(Extended)* | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Grouping | ax + bx + kay + kby = (a + b)(x + ky) | 5x + 5y + ax + ay = (x + y)(5 + a) |
| Difference of two squares | a²x² − b²y² = (ax + by)(ax − by) | 49m² − 4n² = (7m + 2n)(7m − 2n) |
| Perfect square | a² + 2ab + b² = (a + b)² | x² − 14x + 49 = (x − 7)² |
| Quadratic, a = 1 | product c, sum b | x² + x − 20 = (x + 5)(x − 4) |
| Quadratic, a ≠ 1 | product ac, sum b, split, group | 3x² − 10x − 8 = (3x + 2)(x − 4) |
| Cubic, no constant | take out x first | x³ − 5x² + 6x = x(x − 2)(x − 3) |

### Completing the square *(Extended)*

```
x^2 + bx + c        = (x + b/2)^2 - (b/2)^2 + c
ax^2 + bx + c       = a[x^2 + (b/a)x] + c, then complete inside and multiply out by a
```

Example: 2x² + 8x + 1 = 2(x² + 4x) + 1 = 2[(x + 2)² − 4] + 1 = 2(x + 2)² − 7.

What the form tells you: if a > 0, the least value of the expression is q, reached at x = −p. If a < 0, q is the greatest value.

## Exam traps

- **Sign on the second bracket.** −3(x − 5) is −3x + 15. In "show that" work, put the whole second expansion in brackets before subtracting.
- **Half-finished factorising.** 2x² − 18 = 2(x² − 9) is not finished: 2(x + 3)(x − 3).
- **Sum of squares.** x² + 25 does not factorise with real numbers. If you think it does, expand your answer to see why not.
- **Wrong pair in the ac method.** For 3x² − 10x − 8, ac = −24; −12 and 2 give the sum −10. The pair 12 and −2 has the right product but sum +10.
- **Unlike terms.** 3x² + 2x is already simplified; it is not 5x³ or 5x².
- **Completed square with a ≠ 1.** The −(b/2a)² term goes inside the bracket and is multiplied by a.
- **Odd coefficient of x.** x² + 3x = (x + 1.5)² − 2.25; do not round 1.5 to 2.

## Self-test

1. Simplify 3x² − 4xy + 2 − x² + 9yx − 7.
2. Expand and simplify 4(3a − 2) − 5(a − 3).
3. Expand and simplify (3x − 4)(2x + 5).
4. Factorise fully 15a²b − 10ab².
5. *(Extended)* Expand and simplify (x + 2)(x − 3)(x + 1).
6. *(Extended)* Factorise 6xy + 9x − 4y − 6.
7. *(Extended)* Factorise fully 50 − 2x².
8. *(Extended)* Factorise 2x² + 7x − 15.
9. *(Extended)* Factorise fully 4x³ − 4x² − 24x.
10. *(Extended)* Write x² − 10x + 18 in the form (x + p)² + q.
11. *(Extended)* Write 2x² − 6x + 1 in the form a(x + p)² + q.
12. Show that (2n + 1)² − (2n − 1)² is a multiple of 8 for every integer n.

### Answers

1. **2x² + 5xy − 5** (−4xy + 9xy = 5xy because yx = xy).
2. 12a − 8 − 5a + 15 = **7a + 7**.
3. 6x² + 15x − 8x − 20 = **6x² + 7x − 20**.
4. **5ab(3a − 2b)**.
5. (x² − x − 6)(x + 1) = x³ + x² − x² − x − 6x − 6 = **x³ − 7x − 6**.
6. 3x(2y + 3) − 2(2y + 3) = **(3x − 2)(2y + 3)**.
7. 2(25 − x²) = **2(5 − x)(5 + x)**.
8. ac = −30; 10 and −3: 2x² + 10x − 3x − 15 = 2x(x + 5) − 3(x + 5) = **(2x − 3)(x + 5)**.
9. 4x(x² − x − 6) = **4x(x − 3)(x + 2)**.
10. (x − 5)² − 25 + 18 = **(x − 5)² − 7**.
11. 2(x² − 3x) + 1 = 2[(x − 1.5)² − 2.25] + 1 = **2(x − 1.5)² − 3.5**.
12. 4n² + 4n + 1 − (4n² − 4n + 1) = **8n**, which is 8 × n, a multiple of 8 for every integer n.
