## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. The
4-mark "describe" question gives one mark for each relevant, accurate detail; the 4-mark "explain"
question gives one mark for each point and one for explaining it, up to two marks for each of the
two. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general,
accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The
12-mark questions are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** Sumayyah [1].

**2.** Zayd ibn Harithah (RA) [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: no trading with them; no marriage with them; no speaking to them; no selling them food; the agreement was written and hung in the Ka'aba.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: hunger and shortage of food; illness and weakness; hardship for children; loss of trade and income; living in a narrow valley away from Makkah.

**5.** Any four, [1] each: he travelled to Ta'if to seek support [1]; the leaders of Thaqif rejected him [1]; they set children and slaves on him [1]; they threw stones at him until his feet bled [1]; he took shelter in a vineyard [1]; he made a prayer to Allah, complaining of his weakness [1]; he refused the offer of the angel to crush the mountains [1].

**6.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Loss of Abu Talib** [1]: he had protected the Prophet (pbuh) from the Quraysh, so the Prophet (pbuh) now had no tribal protector [1].
- **Loss of Khadija (RA)** [1]: she was his wife, comforter and first believer, so he lost his greatest personal support [1].
- **Greater attacks** [1]: after Abu Talib's death, the Quraysh felt free to insult and attack him more [1].
- **Rejection at Ta'if** [1]: his hope for a new home for Islam was refused soon afterwards [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **The message was spreading** [1]: more people, including in Yathrib, were accepting Islam, so they feared they could not stop it [1].
- **Fear of a base in Yathrib** [1]: if he escaped, he could build a strong community outside their control [1].
- **Threat to their power and trade** [1]: they wished to protect their leadership and the income from the idols [1].
- **Failure of other methods** [1]: insults, bargaining and the boycott had not stopped him [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "when those who disbelieved plotted against you to restrain you or kill you or evict you. But they plan, and Allah plans, and Allah is the best of planners" (Qur'an 8:30) [2].
- "And they planned a plan, and We planned a plan, while they perceived not" (Qur'an 27:50) [2].

**8.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** Abu Lahab was the Prophet's (pbuh) uncle and should have protected him, so his insults and hostility hurt more.
- **For the statement:** the Qur'an names him and his wife, which shows how serious the case was.
- **Against the statement:** other trials were also severe: the boycott with its hunger, the death of Khadija (RA) and Abu Talib, the rejection at Ta'if and the plot to kill him.
- **Against the statement:** Abu Talib, also family, protected him, so family opposition was not complete.
- **Balanced view:** opposition from family was painful, but the Prophet (pbuh) faced many kinds of hardship, and each taught patience.

*Use of the passage*

- "May the hands of Abu Lahab be ruined" shows Allah's judgement on a close relative who opposed him.
- "His wealth will not avail him" shows that family status offers no protection.
- "And his wife [as well]" shows that others who helped him were held responsible.
- The passage names an uncle directly, which shows the personal pain behind the history.

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Lessons (AO1):

- **Patience** [1]: he endured stoning and rejection without anger [1].
- **Forgiveness** [1]: he refused to let the angel destroy the people, hoping their descendants would believe [1].
- **Trust in Allah** [1]: he prayed to Allah in his weakness, relying on His help [1].
- **Hope for guidance** [1]: he did not give up on people, even those who harmed him [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "And not equal are the good deed and the bad. Repel [evil] by that [deed] which is better" (Qur'an 41:34) [2].
- "So be patient, [O Muhammad], as were those of determination among the messengers" (Qur'an 46:35) [2].

**10.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** Abu Lahab was wealthy and from the Prophet's (pbuh) own clan, yet the Qur'an says his wealth would not help him.
- **For the statement:** the Quraysh leaders had great status, but their plots failed and Islam spread.
- **For the statement:** Muslims learn that worldly rank has no value before Allah, only faith and good deeds.
- **Against the statement:** in this world, the wealthy and powerful often did harm the believers for years; the benefit is promised mainly in the Hereafter.
- **Against the statement:** some wealthy people accepted Islam, so wealth is not itself the problem; opposing the truth is.
- **Balanced view:** wealth cannot save a person from Allah's judgement, though its effects on this life can be harsh for the believers.

*Use of the passage*

- "His wealth will not avail him or that which he gained" supports the statement directly.
- "ruined is he" shows the result of opposing the truth.
- "burn in a Fire" points to the Hereafter, where status has no value.
