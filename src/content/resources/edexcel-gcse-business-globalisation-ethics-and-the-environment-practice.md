Read the [study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-globalisation-ethics-and-the-environment/) and [revision notes](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-globalisation-ethics-and-the-environment-revision-notes/) first if you need them. Every business here is invented. The specification does not define terms such as multinational, tariff, trade bloc, e-commerce or pressure group, so the answers use their standard meanings. Show your working in calculations.

## Questions

**1.** Define the term multinational. **[2]**

**2.** Give two barriers to international trade. **[2]**

**3.** Vantage Cycles buys 120 bike frames from an overseas supplier at £185 each. A tariff of 9% of the value applies. A UK supplier quotes £24,500 for 120 frames.

**(a)** Calculate the total cost from the overseas supplier, including the tariff. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate how much cheaper the overseas frames are than the UK quote. **[1]**

**4.** Pennine Preserves, a small jam maker, had sales revenue of £350,000, of which £49,000 came from exports. Export sales rose to £61,250 the next year.

**(a)** Calculate the share of sales revenue from exports. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in export sales. **[2]**

**5.** Pennine Preserves wants to sell to customers in other countries. Outline one way it could use the internet to compete internationally. **[2]**

**6.** Ashgrove Bakes is a small independent bakery. A multinational supermarket chain starts selling cheap imported cakes in the same town. Analyse one effect of this on Ashgrove Bakes. **[6]**

**7.** Mercer Toys makes 90,000 toys a year in the UK. Each takes 0.6 hours of labour at £13.20 an hour. An overseas site would pay £4.10 an hour, and extra transport would cost £0.95 a toy.

**(a)** Calculate the net annual saving from moving production overseas. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain one drawback of a business moving production overseas. **[3]**

**8.** Explain one way in which membership of a trade bloc could help a business that exports. **[3]**

**9.** Greenfield Beverages sells 60,000 cases a year at £14.00. Each case costs £9.50 to make. It plans to buy ingredients from suppliers who treat workers better, adding £0.70 to the cost of each case. It expects sales to rise by 10%.

**(a)** Calculate its profit before the change. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate its profit after the change and sales rise. **[3]**
**(c)** Analyse one possible trade-off between ethics and profit for Greenfield. **[4]**

**10.** Dunmore Candles sells 45,000 candles a year at a profit of £2.40 each. Using recycled glass adds £0.30 to the cost of every candle and wins 8,000 extra sales.

**(a)** Calculate the change in annual profit. **[4]**
**(b)** Analyse one benefit of this change, other than profit. **[4]**

**11.** Calder Crisps sells 2,000,000 packs a year at a profit of £0.30 a pack. A pressure group campaigns against its plastic packs. A retailer that buys 25% of the packs says it will stop stocking them unless the packaging changes. Option 1: paper packs, adding £0.04 to the cost of every pack. Option 2: keep plastic and spend £50,000 on advertising to defend the packs. Justify which option Calder Crisps should choose. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** A business that has operations such as factories, shops or offices [1] in more than one country [1].
*Examiner insight:* A definition tends to be stronger when it names the key feature (more than one country) rather than just saying the business is large.

**2.** Tariffs [1]; trade blocs [1].
*Examiner insight:* Two named barriers are enough; adding detail does not add credit to a give question.

**3. (a)** Value 120 x 185 = £22,200 [1]; tariff 9% x 22,200 = £1,998 [1]; total **£24,198** [1].
**(b)** 24,500 - 24,198 = **£302** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Showing each step makes your method clear; the tariff is a percentage of the value, then added to it.

**4. (a)** 49,000 / 350,000 x 100 [1] = **14%** [1].
**(b)** (61,250 - 49,000) / 49,000 x 100 [1] = **25%** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the starting figure for change but by the total for share; mixing them is a common slip.

**5.** It could sell through a website to overseas customers [1], so it reaches buyers without opening shops abroad, which keeps costs low [1].
*Examiner insight:* An outline needs two linked points; a second point that restates the first earns less than one that follows from it.

**6.** Cheap imports could lower the price customers expect [1]. Ashgrove Bakes may lose customers to the chain [1], so its sales revenue falls [1]. With fixed costs such as rent unchanged, profit falls faster [1]. It could respond by stressing freshness and quality [1], which might hold loyal customers at higher prices [1].
*Examiner insight:* Analysis gains credit when each step follows from the last and refers to a small independent bakery, not any business.

**7. (a)** UK labour per toy 0.6 x 13.20 = £7.92 [1]; overseas 0.6 x 4.10 = £2.46 [1]; net saving per toy 7.92 - 2.46 - 0.95 = £4.51 [1]; x 90,000 = **£405,900** [1].
**(b)** For example, redundancy payments to UK workers [1] would cost money in the first year [1], which could cancel part of the saving [1].
*Examiner insight:* Explain questions carry no context, so give a general drawback of the move with two expansion points.

**8.** Tariffs between members are low or absent [1], so the exporter's goods cost less in member countries [1], which can raise sales there [1].
*Examiner insight:* Three-step explanations tend to do best when the last step links to sales, costs or profit.

**9. (a)** (14.00 - 9.50) x 60,000 [1] = **£270,000** [1].
**(b)** Profit per case 14.00 - 9.50 - 0.70 = £3.80 [1]; cases 66,000 [1]; profit 3.80 x 66,000 = **£250,800** [1].
**(c)** Higher cost per case lowers profit per case [1]; even with higher sales profit falls by £19,200 [1]; a better reputation could attract customers who value fair treatment of workers [1]; this may repay the cost over time, so the trade-off is short-run profit against long-run gain [1].
*Examiner insight:* A trade-off is usually shown by setting a cost against a benefit and using the figures given.

**10. (a)** New units 53,000 [1]; new profit per candle 2.40 - 0.30 = £2.10 [1]; 53,000 x 2.10 = £111,300 [1]; before 45,000 x 2.40 = £108,000, so a **£3,300 increase** [1].
**(b)** The candles use recycled glass, so less new glass is needed [1], which cuts waste [1]. A business that shows this can support a claim of sustainability [1], which may attract customers who care about it [1].
*Examiner insight:* A benefit other than profit must be separate from the sums; repeating the profit rise does not meet the question.

**11.** Option 1 costs 2,000,000 x 0.04 = £80,000 a year [1]. If the retailer leaves, Calder loses 500,000 packs (25% of the total) [1], a profit of 500,000 x 0.30 = £150,000 [1]. Option 2 costs £50,000 [1] but does not remove the cause of the complaint [1], so the retailer may still delist and the loss could reach £200,000 [1]. Against this, paper packs also respond to the pressure group, which might protect the brand beyond this retailer [1], though the £80,000 would repeat every year [1]. Judgement: Option 1 is the better choice, because the certain £80,000 is lower than the likely loss, and it protects the brand [1].
*Examiner insight:* A justify answer should pick one option, use figures from both, and say why the other is weaker.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding the tariff rate to the price instead of calculating a percentage of the value.
- Giving a general effect without naming the business in the question.
- Writing "profit" when the answer needs revenue or cost.
- Missing the final comparison step in a calculation question.
- Describing an environmental action but not saying what it costs or saves.
- Listing marketing mix elements with no link to the pressure group.
- Stopping at "it depends" instead of giving a reasoned judgement.
