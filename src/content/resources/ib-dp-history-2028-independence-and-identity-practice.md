## Part 1: a paper 1 style set (24 marks)

**Inquiry question: How, and with what challenges, was a new identity formed?**

**Source A** (constructed for practice). From a speech by a Kenyan politician at independence celebrations, 1963.

> "We have a flag and a government of our own. Now we must build one nation out of many peoples. Let every child go to school and let every family have land of its own. Harambee: let us pull together."

**Source B** (constructed for practice). From a newspaper editorial in Port-au-Prince written in the nineteenth century.

> "The nation is free, but the freedom was bought dearly. The old masters demand payment for the liberty we took with our own hands. Our harbours are watched by foreign ships, and our treasury is in debt to foreign banks."

**Source C** (constructed for practice). From a report by a historian of Kenya, published in 2010.

> "Land reform after 1963 transferred many farms from European owners to Africans, but the schemes were costly and the best plots often went to people with political connections. Landlessness remained a source of grievance."

*Context for source C:* The historian used records from the Kenyan national archives and interviews with farmers, and wrote for a university press.

**1.** Explain how the **content** of both source A and source B can be used to answer the inquiry question. **[6]**

**2.** Analyse how the **context** of source C influences how it can be used to answer the inquiry question. **[6]**

**3.** Examine how the **perspectives** in all the sources can be used to answer the inquiry question. **[12]**

## Part 2: short knowledge questions

**4.** Explain one reason why the Haitian leaders accepted the demand for payment in 1825. **[3]**

**5.** Explain one way in which the Second World War influenced the Kenyan independence movement. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Marking points (6):
- Source A: the aim of building one nation from many peoples shows that a national identity had to be created **[1]**; education and land for every family show the priorities of the new state **[1]**; harambee ("pull together") links identity with self-help and cooperation **[1]**.
- Source B: freedom was achieved but at high cost **[1]**; the demand for payment by former masters shows that independence was challenged by France **[1]**; foreign ships and debt show external pressure, which limited the new state's identity as a fully free nation **[1]**.
Markbands: 1 to 2 for description; 3 to 4 for partial explanation (maximum 3 if only one source is used); 5 to 6 for specific content from both sources with developed links.

**2.** Marking points (6):
- Author and purpose: a historian writing for a university press, so aiming at an analytical, evidence-based account rather than a political message **[1 to 2]**.
- Evidence base: archives and interviews give a range of voices, which adds credibility for the experience of land reform **[1 to 2]**.
- Date: 2010, with hindsight over decades, so it can judge long-term outcomes, but it is also shaped by later debates about land and corruption **[1 to 2]**.
- Link to the question: it is useful for the challenge of inequality in forming a new identity, but not for how people felt in 1963 **[1]**.
Reward context that is linked to how the source can be used.

**3.** Marking points (12):
- Source A: the perspective of an optimistic national leader who wants to unify and mobilise; it emphasises hope and shared effort **[2]**.
- Source B: a Haitian journalist's perspective, which is critical of France and anxious about debt; it emphasises the burden of freedom **[2]**.
- Source C: a historian's retrospective, balanced and critical, which points out unequal outcomes **[2]**.
- Similarities: A and C both concern land and nation building (A promises land, C reports how it worked in practice); B and C both show that independence left material problems **[2]**.
- Differences: A is hopeful, B and C are critical; A is at the moment of independence, B and C look at consequences; B concerns an external obstacle, while C concerns internal inequality **[2]**.
- Combined: the sources show that identity was shaped by ideals of unity and freedom but limited by debt and inequality, and the two examples differ in whether the main obstacle was external or internal **[2]**.
Markbands: 1 to 3 minimal; 4 to 6 partial; 7 to 9 understanding of at least two sources (maximum 9 for two sources); 10 to 12 insightful understanding of all three sources with similarities and differences examined and supported by references.

**4.** For example: France sent a fleet and the threat of renewed war and blockade **[1]**; without recognition Haiti faced isolation and trade difficulties, since other powers had not recognised its independence **[1]**; leaders hoped that recognition and trade would secure the state, so they accepted the indemnity **[1]**.

**5.** For example: thousands of Kenyans served in the army overseas and saw other societies and colonial weaknesses **[1]**; returning soldiers had higher expectations but found few jobs and little land **[1]**; this increased support for political organisations such as the Kenya African Union and for more radical action **[1]**.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are the sources short?** To let you practise the three skills in a short time. Real sources can be longer, or pictorial.

**What if I do not remember the date of the Lancaster House conferences?** The paper asks about sources, not dates, but dates help you place the context.
