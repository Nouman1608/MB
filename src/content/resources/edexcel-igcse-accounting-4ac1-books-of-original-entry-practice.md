## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State the book of original entry used to record each transaction.

**(a)** Goods for resale bought on credit. **[1]**
**(b)** A customer returns goods bought on credit. **[1]**
**(c)** A filing cabinet bought on credit for office use. **[1]**
**(d)** Cleaning materials of 8.40 paid from the float. **[1]**
**(e)** Goods sold for cash. **[1]**
**(f)** Goods returned to a supplier. **[1]**

**2.** Explain two purposes of books of original entry. **[4]**

**3.** The total of the sales day book for May is 3,480. State the account debited and the account credited with this total, or with its individual lines. **[2]**

**4.** Describe the cash book entries for cash of 400 paid into the bank. **[2]**

**5.** A petty cashier has a float of 250.00. Vouchers for the month total 211.35. Calculate the cash held before reimbursement and the amount of the reimbursement. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** Ashgrove Stationery had these transactions in September 2027. Trade discount is deducted on all invoices and credit notes.

- 3 Sep sold goods on credit to Hale Primary, list price 600, trade discount 10%
- 7 Sep sold goods on credit to K. Ndlovu, list price 350, trade discount 20%
- 10 Sep cash sales 125
- 14 Sep Hale Primary returned goods, list price 80
- 21 Sep sold goods on credit to Orchard Dental, list price 920, trade discount 15%
- 26 Sep K. Ndlovu returned goods, list price 50

**(a)** Prepare the sales day book for September. **[4]**
**(b)** Prepare the sales returns book for September. **[2]**
**(c)** State how the totals of the two books are posted to the ledger. **[2]**

**7.** Fenwick Bikes had these balances on 1 October 2027: cash in hand 145.00 and a bank overdraft of 620.00.

- 3 Oct cash sales 830.00
- 5 Oct paid cash of 700.00 into the bank
- 8 Oct R. Iqbal paid by cheque 1,470.00 in settlement of a debt of 1,500.00
- 12 Oct paid Delta Parts by cheque 931.00 in settlement of 950.00 owed
- 15 Oct paid wages in cash 210.00
- 19 Oct cash sales 415.00
- 24 Oct paid electricity by bank 186.00
- 27 Oct withdrew 150.00 from the bank for office cash
- 30 Oct T. Grant paid 512.00 by bank transfer

**(a)** Prepare the three-column cash book for October, balanced at 31 October. **[10]**
**(b)** State how the totals of the discount columns are posted. **[2]**

**8.** Fenwick Bikes keeps a petty cash book on the imprest system with a float of 120.00 and analysis columns for postage, cleaning, travel and stationery. November vouchers:

- 2 Nov stamps 9.60 (voucher 31)
- 6 Nov cleaning cloths 14.25 (32)
- 9 Nov train fare 22.40 (33)
- 13 Nov envelopes 7.85 (34)
- 18 Nov courier postage 16.30 (35)
- 23 Nov bus fares 11.70 (36)
- 27 Nov floor cleaning 18.00 (37)

**(a)** Prepare the petty cash book for November, showing the reimbursement on 30 November and the balance carried down. **[6]**
**(b)** Explain one benefit of the imprest system. **[2]**

**9.** Lorne Bay Café begins trading on 1 April 2027 with equipment 12,400, inventory 3,750 and a bank balance of 2,850. It owes trade payables 1,600 and has a loan of 5,000.

Prepare the opening journal entry, with a narrative. **[4]**

**10.** Prepare journal entries, with narratives, for these transactions of Lorne Bay Café.

**(a)** 10 May: a coffee machine costing 6,300 was bought on credit from Holt Engineering. **[2]**
**(b)** 31 May: the owner took goods costing 240 for private use. **[2]**
**(c)** 30 June: M. Evans, who owes 160, has left the country and the debt is written off. **[2]**

**11.** A junior bookkeeper made these choices. For each, explain the error and state where the item should be recorded.

**(a)** Shop fittings bought on credit were entered in the purchases day book. **[2]**
**(b)** A cash sale was entered in the sales day book. **[2]**
**(c)** Discount allowed of 15 was added to the bank column of the cash book. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why:

**(a)** the cash book is both a book of original entry and part of the ledger **[2]**
**(b)** the day books record amounts after trade discount. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Purchases day book [1]. (b) Sales returns book [1]. (c) Journal [1]. (d) Petty cash book [1]. (e) Cash book [1]. (f) Purchases returns book [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the cabinet is not goods for resale, so it never enters the purchases day book.

**2.** Any two, each explained. Grouping [1]: similar transactions are listed together so only the totals are posted to the nominal ledger, saving time [1]. Chronological record [1]: each item is entered in date order with its document number, so it can be traced and checked later [1]. Also acceptable: fewer errors in the ledger; division of work; narrative for unusual items in the journal. **[4]**

**3.** Each customer's account in the receivables ledger is debited with the individual amounts [1]; the sales account is credited with the total of 3,480 [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Debit the bank column with 400 [1] and credit the cash column with 400, marking both entries as contra (C) [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Cash held = 250.00 − 211.35 = **38.65** [1]. Reimbursement = **211.35** [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)**

| Date | Customer | Net |
|---|---|---|
| 3 Sep | Hale Primary | 540 |
| 7 Sep | K. Ndlovu | 280 |
| 21 Sep | Orchard Dental | 782 |
| | **Total** | **1,602** |

Hale Primary 540 [1]. K. Ndlovu 280 [1]. Orchard Dental 782 [1]. Cash sale excluded and total 1,602 [1].
**(b)**

| Date | Customer | Net |
|---|---|---|
| 14 Sep | Hale Primary | 72 |
| 26 Sep | K. Ndlovu | 40 |
| | **Total** | **112** |

Both returns at the customer's own trade discount (80 less 10%, 50 less 20%) [1]. Total 112 [1].
**(c)** Sales day book total 1,602 credited to the sales account [1]; sales returns book total 112 debited to the sales returns account [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Returns use the same trade discount the customer received on the sale.

Opening cash on the debit side and opening overdraft on the credit side [1]. Cash sales 830.00 and 415.00 [1]. Contra of 700.00 on both sides [1]. Iqbal: discount 30.00, bank 1,470.00 [1]. Balances c/d and b/d: cash 630.00 and bank 795.00 (debit) [1].

**(b)** The petty cashier must account for all spending before the float is restored [1], so the total of the vouchers plus the cash held always equals the float, which makes errors or theft easy to spot [1]. **[8]**

**9.**

```
                                     Debit      Credit
1 Apr   Equipment                 12,400
        Inventory                  3,750
        Bank                       2,850
            Trade payables                       1,600
            Loan                                 5,000
            Capital                             12,400
        Assets, liabilities and capital at start of trading
```

Three assets debited [1]. Payables and loan credited [1]. Capital 19,000 − 6,600 = 12,400 credited [1]. Narrative and equal totals of 19,000 [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** Dr Coffee machine (equipment) 6,300, Cr Holt Engineering 6,300 [1]; narrative: machine bought on credit [1].
**(b)** Dr Drawings 240, Cr Purchases 240 [1]; narrative: goods taken for private use [1].
**(c)** Dr Irrecoverable debts 160, Cr M. Evans 160 [1]; narrative: debt written off as customer cannot be traced [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** Shop fittings are a non-current asset, not goods for resale [1], so they should be recorded in the journal (debit shop fittings, credit the supplier) [1].
**(b)** The day books record credit transactions only [1]; a cash sale is recorded in the cash column of the cash book [1].
**(c)** The discount column is a memorandum column and no money was received for the discount [1]; the 15 should be in the discount column only, and the bank column should show the amount actually received [1]. **[6]**

**12. (a)** Receipts and payments are first entered in it from documents, so it is a book of original entry [1]; its cash and bank columns are also the cash and bank accounts, so its balances go straight to the trial balance [1].
**(b)** Trade discount is deducted on the invoice and is never paid or owed [1], so only the net amount is a sale or purchase and a debt; recording list price would overstate both [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting non-current assets bought on credit in the purchases day book.
- Including cash sales in the sales day book.
- Placing an opening overdraft on the debit side of the cash book.
- Showing contra entries on one side only.
- Balancing or adding the discount columns into cash or bank.
- Missing journal narratives, or a capital figure that ignores liabilities.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where does an opening bank overdraft go in the cash book?**
On the credit side of the bank column, because the business owes the bank. A debit balance means money in the bank.

**Which journal entry records goods taken by the owner?**
Debit drawings and credit purchases with the cost of the goods, with a narrative explaining that they were taken for private use.
