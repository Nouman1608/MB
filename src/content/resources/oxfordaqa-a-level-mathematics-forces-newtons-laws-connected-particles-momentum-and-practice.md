These questions cover sections M1.3 and M1.4 of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Mathematics (9660) specification, Version 5.2. The specification places this content in International AS, Unit PSM1. Take g = 9.8 m/s² and treat strings as light and inextensible and pulleys as smooth unless told otherwise. Notes: [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-forces-newtons-laws-connected-particles-momentum-and/), [revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-forces-newtons-laws-connected-particles-momentum-and-revision-notes/), [course hub](/boards/oxfordaqa/a-level/mathematics/), [printable checklist](/checklists/oxfordaqa/a-level/mathematics/) and [free diagnostics](/diagnostics/).

## Questions

**1.** A parcel has mass 7.5 kg.

**(a)** Find its weight. **[1]**
**(b)** It is lifted vertically by a cable with tension 90 N. Find its acceleration. **[2]**

**2.** A sledge of mass 40 kg is pulled along rough horizontal ground by a horizontal rope with tension 100 N. The coefficient of friction between sledge and ground is 0.15. Find the sledge's acceleration. **[4]**

**3.** A lift of mass 600 kg carries a load of mass 90 kg. The cable tension is 7107 N and the lift accelerates upwards.

**(a)** Find the acceleration. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the normal reaction between the load and the floor of the lift. **[2]**

**4.** A crate of mass 20 kg rests on horizontal ground.

**(a)** State the magnitude and direction of the normal reaction on the crate. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the force that forms the Newton's third law pair with this normal reaction, and say what body it acts on. **[2]**

**5.** A car of mass 1350 kg pulls a caravan of mass 450 kg along a straight horizontal road. A light rigid bar joins them. Constant resistances are 560 N on the car and 240 N on the caravan.

**(a)** The engine force is 2960 N. Find the acceleration and the tension in the bar. **[5]**
**(b)** The engine is switched off and a braking force of 1360 N acts on the car. Find the force in the bar, stating whether it is a tension or a thrust. **[4]**

**6.** A light inextensible string passes over a smooth fixed pulley. A 0.9 kg particle is attached to one end and a 0.6 kg particle to the other. Both hang freely and the system is released from rest, with the 0.9 kg particle 1.2 m above the floor.

**(a)** Find the acceleration and the tension in the string. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the speed of the 0.9 kg particle as it hits the floor. **[2]**
**(c)** The 0.6 kg particle keeps moving upwards and the string goes slack. Find the further height it rises. **[2]**

**7.** A block P of mass 6 kg rests on a rough horizontal table, with μ = 0.5. A light string joins P, over a smooth pulley at the table edge, to a block Q of mass 8 kg that hangs below the pulley. Both are let go together.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the particles and the tension. **[5]**
**(b)** Q hits the floor after falling 1.4 m. P does not reach the pulley. Find how far P slides after Q lands. **[4]**

**8.** A particle of mass 6 kg moving at 3 m/s collides with a particle of mass 4 kg moving in the opposite direction at 2 m/s. They join and move together.

**(a)** Find their common velocity, stating its direction. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the magnitude of the impulse exerted on the 4 kg particle. **[2]**

**9.** A ball of mass 0.15 kg hits a smooth fixed wall at right angles at 20 m/s and rebounds at 14 m/s. The contact lasts 0.015 s.

**(a)** Find the impulse on the ball. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the average force, assuming it is constant. **[2]**

**10.** A sphere of mass 0.4 kg is released from rest in a tank of oil and falls 2.4 m in 2 s with constant acceleration. Find the resistance, assuming it is constant. **[4]**

**11.** A truck of mass 3000 kg moves at 4 m/s along a straight horizontal track and couples to a stationary wagon of mass 1000 kg.

**(a)** Find the speed immediately after coupling. **[2]**
**(b)** A constant total resistance of 600 N then acts on the coupled vehicles. Find the distance they travel before stopping. **[3]**
**(c)** The coupling takes 0.5 s. Find the average force on the wagon in that time. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** W = 7.5 × 9.8 = **73.5 N** [1]
**(b)** 90 − 73.5 = 7.5a [1]; **a = 2.2 m/s²** upwards [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write the equation with the force terms and the direction stated; a bare number gives the reader nothing to credit if it is wrong.

**2.** R = 40 × 9.8 = 392 N [1]; F = 0.15 × 392 = 58.8 N [1]; 100 − 58.8 = 40a [1]; **a = 1.03 m/s²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show R before F. Friction is μR, and many errors come from using the weight without saying why R equals it.

**3. (a)** Total mass 690 kg; weight 690 × 9.8 = 6762 N [1]; 7107 − 6762 = 690a [1]; **a = 0.5 m/s²** [1]
**(b)** R − 90 × 9.8 = 90 × 0.5 [1]; **R = 927 N** [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (b), apply the second law to the load alone. Using the whole lift would find the tension again, not R.

**4. (a)** **196 N upwards** [1]
**(b)** The crate's push on the floor [1], of the same magnitude and downwards, acting on the floor [1]
*Examiner insight:* A third-law pair acts on two different bodies. Naming the weight as the partner is a typical error, so say what body each force acts on.

**5. (a)** Whole system: 2960 − 560 − 240 = 1800a [1]; **a = 1.2 m/s²** [1]. Caravan: T − 240 = 450 × 1.2 [1]; T = 780 [1]; **tension 780 N** [1]
**(b)** Whole system: 1360 + 560 + 240 = 1800a, so a = 1.2 m/s² deceleration [1]. Caravan, forces backwards: 240 + X = 450 × 1.2 [1]; X = 300 [1]; **the bar is in thrust of 300 N** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Say which body each equation is for. A negative tension is a good prompt to state that the bar is in thrust, and the words matter as much as the sign.

**6. (a)** 0.9g − T = 0.9a and T − 0.6g = 0.6a [1]; add: 0.3g = 1.5a [1]; **a = 1.96 m/s²** [1]; **T = 0.6 × (9.8 + 1.96) = 7.056 N** [1]
**(b)** v² = 2 × 1.96 × 1.2 = 4.704 [1]; **v = 2.17 m/s** (3 s.f.) [1]
**(c)** The 0.6 kg particle moves freely with deceleration g: 0 = 4.704 − 2 × 9.8 × h [1]; **h = 0.24 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c), explain that the string is slack so only gravity acts. Using a = 1.96 again would give a different, wrong answer.

**7. (a)** R = 6 × 9.8 = 58.8 N, F = 0.5 × 58.8 = 29.4 N [1]; P: T − 29.4 = 6a [1]; Q: 78.4 − T = 8a [1]; add: 49 = 14a, **a = 3.5 m/s²** [1]; **T = 50.4 N** [1]
**(b)** v² = 2 × 3.5 × 1.4 = 9.8 [1]; after landing the string is slack and P decelerates at μg = 4.9 m/s² [1]; 0 = 9.8 − 2 × 4.9 × s [1]; **s = 1 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write down both particle equations before adding. In part (b), state that friction alone acts on P once the string is slack.

**8. (a)** Taking the direction of the 6 kg particle as positive: 6 × 3 − 4 × 2 = 10v [1]; 10 = 10v [1]; **v = 1 m/s** in the direction the 6 kg particle was moving [1]
**(b)** Impulse on the 4 kg particle = 4 × (1 − (−2)) [1] = **12 N s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The opposite directions need opposite signs. Stating the direction of the answer as well as its size is the safer habit.

**9. (a)** Take towards the wall as positive: impulse = 0.15 × (−14 − 20) [1] = −5.1, so **5.1 N s** away from the wall [1]
**(b)** F = 5.1 / 0.015 [1] = **340 N** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use −14 for the rebound. Subtracting the speeds (20 − 14) instead of adding their sizes is the common slip.

**10.** s = ½at² gives 2.4 = ½ × a × 4 [1]; a = 1.2 m/s² [1]; 0.4 × 9.8 − D = 0.4 × 1.2 [1]; **D = 3.44 N** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Finding the acceleration first, as a separate labelled step, keeps the method visible even if the final subtraction slips.

**11. (a)** 3000 × 4 = 4000v [1]; **v = 3 m/s** [1]
**(b)** Deceleration a = 600 / 4000 = 0.15 m/s² [1]; 0 = 3² − 2 × 0.15 × s [1]; **s = 30 m** [1]
**(c)** Impulse on the wagon = 1000 × 3 = 3000 N s [1]; **force = 3000 / 0.5 = 6000 N** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the combined mass for the deceleration in (b) and the wagon's mass alone in (c). Say which body each calculation is about.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Using R = mg without checking whether another vertical force acts.
* Taking g as 10 or 9.81 when the question says 9.8.
* Using one sign convention for both particles in a pulley problem, so the tension appears in the wrong sign.
* Forgetting that the string goes slack when the hanging particle lands.
* Mixing mass and weight, for example writing 0.9 where 0.9g is needed.
* Dropping the sign of a velocity in a momentum equation.
* Calling a force in a rod a tension without checking its sign.
* Giving an impulse without a direction, or a force without units.
* Rounding intermediate values before the final line.
