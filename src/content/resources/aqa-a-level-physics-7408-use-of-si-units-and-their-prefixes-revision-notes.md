These notes cover section 3.1.1 Use of SI units and their prefixes of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408), first teaching 2015. They focus on the unit work that later topics depend on: expressing A-level quantities in base units, converting compound units, and reading units from graphs. The six base units, the ten prefixes and the J–eV and J–kW h conversions are set out in the [SI units and estimation revision notes](/resources/aqa-alevel-physics-estimation-of-physical-quantities-revision-notes/); full teaching is in the [SI units and their prefixes study guide](/resources/aqa-alevel-physics-use-of-si-units-and-their-prefixes/), and the [SI units practice questions](/resources/aqa-alevel-physics-use-of-si-units-and-their-prefixes-practice/) test them.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Base unit | One of kg, m, s, mol, K, A (the candela is excluded from 3.1.1) |
| Derived unit | A combination of base units, often with its own name (N, J, V) |
| Prefix | A power of ten attached to a unit: T, G, M, k, c, m, μ, n, p, f |
| Compound unit | A unit built from several units, such as m s⁻¹ or kg m⁻³ |

## Derived units in base units

Each row follows from a defining equation. Work it out rather than memorise it: write the equation, substitute units, combine powers.

| Quantity | Defining equation | Named unit | Base units |
|---|---|---|---|
| Momentum | p = mv | N s | kg m s⁻¹ |
| Potential difference | V = W/Q | V | kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹ |
| Resistance | R = V/I | Ω | kg m² s⁻³ A⁻² |
| Resistivity | ρ = RA/L | Ω m | kg m³ s⁻³ A⁻² |
| Specific charge | charge ÷ mass | C kg⁻¹ | A s kg⁻¹ |
| Planck constant | h = E/f | J s | kg m² s⁻¹ |
| Stiffness (spring constant) | k = F/ΔL | N m⁻¹ | kg s⁻² |
| Stress, Young modulus | F/A | Pa | kg m⁻¹ s⁻² |
| Strain | ΔL/L | none | none |
| Intensity | power ÷ area | W m⁻² | kg s⁻³ |
| Mass per unit length | m/L | kg m⁻¹ | kg m⁻¹ |

Worked reminder (the volt):

```
V = W/Q, so 1 V = 1 J ÷ 1 C
J = kg m² s⁻²   and   C = A s
V = kg m² s⁻² ÷ (A s) = kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹
```

Worked reminder (the Planck constant): E = hf, so h = E/f has unit J ÷ s⁻¹ = J s = kg m² s⁻² × s = kg m² s⁻¹.

## Converting compound units

Convert each part of the unit separately, keeping the powers.

| Convert | Working | Factor |
|---|---|---|
| km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹ | 1000 m ÷ 3600 s | ÷ 3.6 |
| g cm⁻³ to kg m⁻³ | 10⁻³ kg ÷ 10⁻⁶ m³ | × 1000 |
| N mm⁻² to Pa | 1 N ÷ 10⁻⁶ m² | × 10⁶ |
| mm² to m² | (10⁻³ m)² | × 10⁻⁶ |
| cm³ to m³ | (10⁻² m)³ | × 10⁻⁶ |
| mA h to C | 10⁻³ A × 3600 s | × 3.6 |
| MeV to J | 10⁶ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J | × 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ |

Worked reminder: a speed of 72 km h⁻¹ is 72 ÷ 3.6 = **20 m s⁻¹**.

Worked reminder: aluminium has density 2.70 g cm⁻³, which is 2.70 × 1000 = **2700 kg m⁻³**.

Worked reminder: a wire of diameter 0.28 mm has cross-sectional area π(0.14 × 10⁻³)² = **6.2 × 10⁻⁸ m²**. Convert the radius to metres *before* squaring.

Worked reminder: a phone battery rated 3000 mA h stores a charge of 3000 × 10⁻³ × 3600 = **1.08 × 10⁴ C**.

## Temperature

The kelvin is the base unit. Add 273 to a Celsius temperature: 27 °C is 300 K. A temperature *change* is the same size in both units: a rise of 15 °C is a rise of 15 K.

## Units from graphs

The unit of a gradient is (unit of y) ÷ (unit of x). The unit of an area under a graph is (unit of y) × (unit of x).

| Graph | Gradient | Area |
|---|---|---|
| velocity against time | m s⁻¹ ÷ s = m s⁻² (acceleration) | m s⁻¹ × s = m (displacement) |
| force against time | N s⁻¹ | N s (impulse, change in momentum) |
| force against extension | N m⁻¹ (stiffness) | N m = J (work done) |
| pd against current | V A⁻¹ = Ω | — |
| stress against strain | Pa (Young modulus) | — |
| power against time | — | W s = J (energy) |

When an axis carries a prefix or power of ten, such as "extension / mm" or "I / 10⁻³ A", include it when you calculate the gradient.

## Writing units correctly

- Symbols for units named after people are capitals (N, J, Pa); written names are not (newton).
- Symbols are never plural: 5 kg, not 5 kgs.
- Leave a space between multiplied units: m s⁻¹ is metres per second, but ms⁻¹ reads as "per millisecond".
- A power applies to the prefix too: cm² means (cm)².

## Exam traps

| Trap | Correct approach |
|---|---|
| Using grams in Ek = ½mv² | Convert to kg first |
| Squaring the diameter in mm, then converting by 10⁻³ | Convert first; area converts by 10⁻⁶ |
| Writing 1 km h⁻¹ = 3.6 m s⁻¹ | 1 m s⁻¹ = 3.6 km h⁻¹; divide km h⁻¹ by 3.6 |
| Giving the Young modulus in N m⁻¹ | Stress ÷ strain has the unit of stress, Pa |
| Treating strain as having a unit | Strain is a ratio of lengths, so it has no unit |
| Giving resistivity in Ω m⁻¹ | ρ = RA/L gives Ω m² ÷ m = Ω m |
| Leaving a graph gradient without its axis prefix | Include factors such as 10⁻³ from axis labels |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Base vs derived**: the ampere is a base unit; the coulomb (A s) is derived.
- **m vs M**: milli is 10⁻³, mega is 10⁶.
- **N s vs N m**: N s is impulse or momentum; N m is a moment or, as a joule, work.

## Quick self-test

1. Express the newton in base units.
2. Express the volt in base units.
3. Show that the unit of the Planck constant is kg m² s⁻¹.
4. Convert 54 km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹.
5. Convert 7.9 g cm⁻³ to kg m⁻³.
6. Convert 0.45 mm² to m².
7. Convert 250 N mm⁻² to Pa.
8. A cell is rated 2000 mA h. Find the charge it can deliver, in C.
9. State the unit of the gradient of a force–extension graph.
10. State the unit of the area under a force–time graph.
11. Convert 37 °C to kelvin.
12. What is wrong with writing a speed as 15 ms⁻¹?

### Answers

1. F = ma, so **kg m s⁻²**.
2. **kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹**.
3. h = E/f: J ÷ s⁻¹ = J s = kg m² s⁻² × s = **kg m² s⁻¹**.
4. 54 ÷ 3.6 = **15 m s⁻¹**.
5. **7900 kg m⁻³**.
6. 0.45 × 10⁻⁶ = **4.5 × 10⁻⁷ m²**.
7. 250 × 10⁶ = **2.5 × 10⁸ Pa**.
8. 2000 × 10⁻³ × 3600 = **7200 C**.
9. **N m⁻¹** (the stiffness).
10. **N s** (the change in momentum).
11. **310 K**.
12. With no space, ms⁻¹ means "per millisecond"; write **15 m s⁻¹**.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to do dimensional analysis for AQA 7408?**
No. You need to know that every SI unit is derived from the base units and be able to write a derived unit in base units, but checking equations by comparing units on both sides is not required.
