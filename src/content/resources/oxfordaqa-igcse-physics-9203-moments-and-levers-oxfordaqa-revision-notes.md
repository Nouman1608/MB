Condensed recall for **3.1.8 Moments and levers** (P content, full Physics award) in OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203), for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Moments and levers study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-moments-and-levers-oxfordaqa/); exam-style questions are in the [Moments and levers practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-moments-and-levers-oxfordaqa-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Moment | The turning effect of a force about a pivot |
| Pivot | The point about which an object turns |
| Perpendicular distance | The shortest distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force, at right angles to the force |
| Line of action | The line along which a force acts |
| Clockwise / anticlockwise moment | The sense in which a moment tends to turn an object |
| Principle of moments | For an object not turning, total clockwise moment = total anticlockwise moment about any pivot |
| Uniform beam | A beam whose weight acts at its midpoint |
| Lever | A rigid bar that turns about a pivot |
| Effort | The force applied to a lever |
| Load | The force the lever acts against |
| Force multiplier | A device that lets a small effort balance or move a larger load |

## Equation

```
M = F x d     moment (N m) = force (N) x perpendicular distance (m)
```

## Method for a balanced object

1. Mark the pivot.
2. For each force, find its perpendicular distance from the pivot.
3. Label each moment clockwise or anticlockwise.
4. Add all clockwise moments; add all anticlockwise moments.
5. Set the totals equal and solve for the unknown.
6. If asked for the pivot force: upward force = total downward force.

## Beams with weight

- A uniform beam's weight acts at its midpoint.
- Pivot at the midpoint → the beam's weight has no moment.
- Pivot elsewhere → include the beam's weight, at its distance from the pivot.

## Toppling (c)

- The pivot is the edge of the base the object tips about.
- Line of action of weight inside the base → weight's moment turns the object back.
- Line of action outside the base → resultant moment → topples.
- Vehicles: a high centre of mass or a narrow wheelbase makes rolling over more likely on slopes and bends.
- Balancing toys: centre of mass below the support point, so the weight's moment always restores the toy.

## Levers (d)

```
effort x effort distance = load x load distance
```

- Effort further from the pivot than the load → effort smaller than load.
- Force multiplication = load ÷ effort = effort distance ÷ load distance.
- The price: the effort moves further than the load.
- Examples: crowbar, wheelbarrow, bottle opener, claw hammer, spanner, scissors.

## Quick-check numbers

| Situation | Moment |
|---|---|
| 40 N at 0.25 m | 10 N m |
| 250 N at 1.2 m | 300 N m |
| 15 N at 30 cm | 4.5 N m |

## Exam traps

- **Perpendicular distance**, not distance along the bar, when the force is at an angle.
- **Measure from the pivot**, not from the end of the beam.
- **Units.** Moments are in N m; convert cm to m when the answer must be a moment.
- **Add every moment** on each side before equating.
- **Pivot force** is a force-balance question, not a moment question.
- **Uniform only.** A beam's weight acts at its midpoint only if it is uniform.
- **Toppling explanation** needs the line of action and the base, not "top-heavy".

## Self-test

**1.** Calculate the moment of a 60 N force acting at right angles 0.45 m from a pivot.

**2.** State the principle of moments.

**3.** A 200 N load is 0.30 m from a pivot. What force at 1.2 m on the other side balances it?

**4.** A 500 N weight is 1.6 m from a pivot. How far from the pivot on the other side must a 640 N weight sit to balance?

**5.** A uniform 2.0 m beam is pivoted at its centre. Why can its weight be ignored?

**6.** A 300 N and a 150 N force push down on a beam 0.40 m and 1.0 m left of a pivot. Find the total anticlockwise moment.

**7.** In question 6, the beam balances with one force at 1.5 m on the right. Find that force.

**8.** A wheelbarrow carries a 600 N load 0.40 m from the wheel axle. The handles are 1.6 m from the axle. Find the lifting force.

**9.** Why does a force at an angle to a spanner produce a smaller moment than the same force at right angles?

**10.** When does a tilted object topple?

**11.** Why is a balancing toy with weights below its support point stable?

**12.** What is given up when a lever is used as a force multiplier?

## Self-test answers

**1.** 60 × 0.45 = 27 N m.

**2.** For an object not turning, the total clockwise moment equals the total anticlockwise moment about any pivot.

**3.** 200 × 0.30 = 60 N m; 60 ÷ 1.2 = 50 N.

**4.** 500 × 1.6 = 800 N m; 800 ÷ 640 = 1.25 m.

**5.** Its weight acts at the pivot, so its perpendicular distance and moment are zero.

**6.** 300 × 0.40 + 150 × 1.0 = 120 + 150 = 270 N m.

**7.** 270 ÷ 1.5 = 180 N.

**8.** 600 × 0.40 = 240 N m; 240 ÷ 1.6 = 150 N.

**9.** The perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action is shorter.

**10.** When the line of action of its weight lies outside its base.

**11.** When it tilts, its weight's moment turns it back because the centre of mass stays below the support.

**12.** Distance: the effort moves through a larger distance than the load.
