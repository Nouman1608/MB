## Questions

**1.** Define the term *empowerment*. **[2]**

**2.** Wyndham Croft Bakery pays a packer £0.42 for every tray of pastries packed. Last week the packer packed 1,150 trays.

**(a)** Calculate the packer's pay for the week. **[2]**
**(b)** State one disadvantage to the bakery of paying by piece rate. **[2]**

**3.** Garrow Dene Opticians offers all staff free private health cover. One assistant, on a short fixed-term contract, says it makes no difference to her effort.

**(a)** Using Maslow's hierarchy, identify the level of need the health cover mainly meets. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one reason why it may not motivate this assistant. **[2]**

**4.** Drivers at Fieldhead Coaches say their pay is fair but they are never thanked, and the rest-room is cold. Using Herzberg's theory, identify one hygiene factor and one motivator from this description and explain how a manager should respond to each. **[4]**

**5.** Dunsley Quay Hotel offers housekeepers a team bonus. A housekeeper rates the chance that effort leads to a cleaned-room target as 0.7, the chance that hitting the target leads to a bonus as 0.5, and the value of the bonus as 6 out of 10. Use motivational force = expectancy × instrumentality × valence.

**(a)** Calculate the motivational force. **[2]**
**(b)** The hotel switches to an individual bonus, raising instrumentality to 0.9. Calculate the new force. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain, using Vroom's theory, why the force changed. **[2]**

**6.** Explain one difference and one similarity between autocratic and paternalistic management. **[4]**

**7.** At Garrow Dene Opticians the practice manager (i) greets a visiting inspector and signs the visit record, (ii) moves money from the marketing budget to buy a new eye-test machine, and (iii) settles a dispute between two staff over holiday cover.

**(a)** Identify the Mintzberg role shown in each of (i), (ii) and (iii). **[3]**
**(b)** State one way Mintzberg's view of management differs from Fayol's. **[1]**

**8.** A Dunsley Quay Hotel events manager earns £2,150 a month basic plus 3% commission on event bookings above £20,000 a month. Bookings last month were £48,000.

**(a)** Calculate the manager's total pay last month. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one risk to the hotel of paying commission. **[2]**

**9.** Fieldhead Coaches wants to involve its drivers in running the business. Analyse two ways in which the drivers could participate in management and control. **[6]**

**10.** Harlow Dene Print Works prints 24,000 sheets a week. Under the old supervisor 8% were spoiled. A new supervisor changed the shift planning, and 5% are now spoiled. Each spoiled sheet wastes paper costing £0.06.

**(a)** Calculate the number of good sheets per week before and after the change. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in good sheets. Give your answer to two decimal places. **[1]**
**(c)** Analyse how the supervisor has contributed to business performance, and state one reason for caution in crediting the supervisor. **[3]**

**11.** Lowther Beck Cycles pays each mechanic £14 an hour for an 8-hour day, completing 14 repairs a day. About 5% of repairs have to be redone after customer complaints. The owner can pay £8.50 per repair, or keep hourly pay and introduce job enrichment (mechanics plan their own jobs and diagnose faults) with team working. Evaluate which the owner should choose. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Empowerment is giving employees the authority to take decisions about their own work [1], with the resources needed to carry them out [1].
*Examiner insight:* answers that only say "giving power" or "making staff happy" tend to lack the idea that authority over their own work is passed down.

**2. (a)** Pay = trays × rate per tray = 1,150 × 0.42 [1] = **£483.00** [1].
**(b)** Quality may fall because the packer rushes to pack more trays [1], which could lead to damaged pastries and customer complaints [1].
*Examiner insight:* a disadvantage needs a consequence for the business named in the question. "It is unfair" is weaker than saying what happens to quality, cost or sales.

**3. (a)** Safety needs [1], because health cover gives security against the cost of illness [1].
**(b)** The assistant's contract is short [1], so her main worry may be job security or income, which a benefit does not fix; lower needs must be met before a higher one motivates [1].
*Examiner insight:* applying the theory to the facts about this person earns more than reciting the five levels.

**4.** Hygiene factor: the cold rest-room (working conditions) [1]. The manager should fix it, because poor conditions cause dissatisfaction, though fixing them will not by itself motivate for long [1]. Motivator: recognition [1]. The manager should thank drivers and recognise good work, which creates satisfaction and so can raise effort [1].
*Examiner insight:* pay is a hygiene factor in the theory, but the case says it is fair, so naming it as the problem would be a misreading. Choose from the evidence given.

**5. (a)** 0.7 × 0.5 × 6 [1] = **2.1** [1].
**(b)** 0.7 × 0.9 × 6 = **3.78** [1].
**(c)** Instrumentality rose [1], so the housekeeper sees a clearer link between her own performance and the reward; the force rises because it is a product of the three scores [1].
*Examiner insight:* show the multiplication line; a bare final number leaves the working unclear. The explanation should refer to the changed link, not to the bonus being bigger.

**6.** Difference: in an autocratic style the manager instructs without explaining or showing concern for views [1], whereas a paternalistic manager explains decisions and acts out of concern for staff welfare [1]. Similarity: in both, the manager makes the decision rather than sharing it [1], so staff have limited influence over the outcome [1].
*Examiner insight:* a similarity needs a stated common feature, and a difference needs both sides. Defining each style separately without comparing them leaves the comparison undone.

**7. (a)** (i) Figurehead [1]. (ii) Resource allocator [1]. (iii) Disturbance handler [1].
**(b)** Fayol set out what managers should do (their elements), whereas Mintzberg described what managers actually do in practice, in a fragmented, reactive working day [1].
*Examiner insight:* give the role name, not just a description of the task. Accept sensible wording if it matches the role.

**8. (a)** Qualifying bookings = 48,000 − 20,000 = 28,000 [1]. Commission = 0.03 × 28,000 = £840 [1]. Total = 2,150 + 840 = **£2,990** [1].
**(b)** The manager may pressure clients into bookings they do not need [1], which could damage the hotel's reputation and lead to cancellations [1].
*Examiner insight:* in a calculation, show the threshold step. Only the bookings above the threshold earn commission, which is a common slip.

**9.** Works council or consultation committee [1]: drivers' representatives meet managers regularly to discuss rotas and routes, which gives managers practical ideas and may reduce resistance to change [1], but managers can still ignore the advice, so drivers may become cynical [1]. Employee share ownership [1]: drivers who own shares share in decisions and profit, which may raise commitment and care of vehicles [1], but it can slow decision making and exposes drivers' savings to business risk [1].
*Examiner insight:* analysis means a consequence is developed in context. Naming a method and describing it gains less than saying what it changes for these drivers.

**10. (a)** Before: 24,000 × 0.92 = **22,080** [1]. After: 24,000 × 0.95 = **22,800** [1].
**(b)** (22,800 − 22,080) / 22,080 = 720 / 22,080 = **3.26%** [1].
**(c)** Better shift planning means 720 more good sheets from the same inputs [1], and 720 fewer spoiled sheets, saving £43.20 a week of paper (720 × 0.06) [1]. Caution: the improvement may be due to new machinery, different job mix or staff training rather than the supervisor, so more evidence is needed [1].
*Examiner insight:* a calculated percentage should be given to the accuracy the question asks, and the final part scores best when it uses the figures just found rather than general statements about managers.

**11.** Piece rate: Taylor's view suggests pay linked to output should raise repairs [1]. At 14 repairs the current cost is £112 / 14 = £8.00 per repair, while piece rate costs £8.50, £0.50 more per repair [1]. At 17 repairs a mechanic would earn £144.50, a strong incentive [1], but the business gains only if the extra repairs earn more than the higher cost [1]. Piece rate may also worsen the existing quality problem as mechanics rush, increasing the 5% redone [1]. Job enrichment and team working draw on Herzberg's motivators and Mayo's social needs [1]; skilled mechanics who plan and diagnose may take more care, so fewer repairs need redoing [1]. However, results are slower and uncertain, planning takes time, and the mechanics' pay stays flat, so some may want more money [1]. Judgement: the owner should choose enrichment and team working [1], because quality is the stated problem and piece rate would reward speed rather than care; the case does not show that extra output is the constraint, so the higher cost is hard to justify [1].
*Examiner insight:* evaluation needs a decision supported by the case evidence. Listing the two methods' good and bad points without a stated choice and a reason leaves the judgement unmade.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining a term loosely, without the key feature such as authority, link or welfare.
- Giving a calculation answer without the working line or the unit.
- Applying commission to the full sales figure instead of only the part above the threshold.
- Naming a theory level or factor without tying it to the facts given.
- Treating pay as a motivator under Herzberg's theory without noting it is a hygiene factor.
- Describing a participation method without any consequence for the business.
- Crediting a manager for a change without allowing for other causes.
- Concluding an evaluation question with no clear recommendation.
