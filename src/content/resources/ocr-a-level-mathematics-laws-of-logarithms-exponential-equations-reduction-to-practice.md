These questions follow sections 1.06f to 1.06i of the OCR A Level Mathematics A specification (Version 3.1, August 2026, for first assessment in 2018): laws of logarithms, exponential equations, reduction to linear form and exponential modelling. All four are Stage 1, so they are common to AS and A Level. Pure Mathematics content is assessed in all three written papers.

## Questions

**1.** Evaluate log₂ 12 + log₂ 6 − 2 log₂ 3, showing your method. **[3]**

**2.** Given that log_a 2 = p and log_a 5 = q, express in terms of p and q:

**(a)** log_a 50 **[2]**
**(b)** log_a (8/√5) **[2]**

**3.** Solve 2 log₂ x = 3 + log₂ (x + 6). **[5]**

**4.** Solve, giving each answer to 3 significant figures:

**(a)** 7^x = 40 **[2]**
**(b)** 6^(x+1) = 4^(2x−3) **[3]**

**5.**

**(a)** Solve e^(2x−1) = 15, giving x in exact form. **[2]**
**(b)** Solve 4^x − 9 × 2^x + 14 = 0, giving each solution exactly or to 3 significant figures. **[4]**

**6.** Find x and y given that log₂ x + log₂ y = 5 and y = x + 4. **[5]**

**7.** Two variables satisfy y = ax^n. The graph of lg y against lg x is a straight line through (0.2, 0.85) and (0.8, 2.05).

**(a)** Show that lg y = n lg x + lg a. **[2]**
**(b)** Find n and a. **[4]**

**8.** A quantity y is thought to follow y = kb^x. Measurements are:

| x | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 6.1 | 9.7 | 15.6 | 24.9 | 39.8 |

**(a)** Calculate ln y for each reading, to 2 decimal places. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that ln y = x ln b + ln k. **[1]**
**(c)** The points lie close to a straight line. Use the first and last points to estimate b and k. **[3]**
**(d)** Estimate y when x = 7. **[1]**

**9.** The concentration of a drug in the blood is C mg/l, t hours after a dose, where C = C₀e^(−kt). It is 12 at t = 1 and 7.5 at t = 4.

**(a)** Find k and C₀. **[4]**
**(b)** Find when the concentration falls to 2 mg/l. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the half-life. **[2]**

**10.** The number of trees in a young woodland is modelled by P = 800e^(0.025t), where t is in years.

**(a)** Find P when t = 40. **[1]**
**(b)** Give one limitation of this model for large t, and suggest one refinement. **[2]**

**11.** The value £V of an investment after t years is modelled by V = Ae^(rt).

**(a)** Show that ln V = rt + ln A. **[2]**
**(b)** Readings of ln V are:

| t | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ln V | 6.92 | 7.16 | 7.40 | 7.64 | 7.88 |

Use the first and last values to find r and A. **[4]**

**(c)** Find the time for the investment to double. **[2]**
**(d)** The investor uses the model for 30 years ahead. Give two reasons for caution. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 2 log₂ 3 = log₂ 9 [1]. Then log₂ (12 × 6 / 9) [1] = log₂ 8 = **3** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show each law you use. A bare answer of 3 gives the reader no evidence of method, so write the single logarithm before evaluating.

**2. (a)** 50 = 2 × 5², so log_a 50 = log_a 2 + 2 log_a 5 [1] = **p + 2q** [1]
**(b)** 8/√5 = 2³ × 5^(−½), so log_a (8/√5) = 3 log_a 2 − ½ log_a 5 [1] = **3p − q/2** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write the number as a product of powers of 2 and 5 first; trying to apply the laws before factorising is where the working usually goes wrong.

**3.** 2 log₂ x = log₂ x², and 3 = log₂ 8 [1]. So x² = 8(x + 6) [1]. Then x² − 8x − 48 = 0 [1] and (x − 12)(x + 4) = 0 [1]. x = −4 makes log₂ x undefined, so reject it. **x = 12** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A quadratic with two roots invites you to give both. State which root you reject and the reason; leaving it in is a common loss.

**4. (a)** x ln 7 = ln 40 [1], so x = 3.6889/1.9459 = **1.90** [1]
**(b)** (x + 1) ln 6 = (2x − 3) ln 4 [1]. x (2 ln 4 − ln 6) = 3 ln 4 + ln 6 [1]. x = (3 ln 4 + ln 6)/(2 ln 4 − ln 6) = **6.07** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep unrounded values until the last line. Rounded logs inside the fraction can shift the third significant figure.

**5. (a)** 2x − 1 = ln 15 [1], so **x = (1 + ln 15)/2** [1]
**(b)** 4^x = (2^x)², so with t = 2^x: t² − 9t + 14 = 0 [1]. (t − 2)(t − 7) = 0 [1]. 2^x = 2 gives **x = 1** [1]. 2^x = 7 gives **x = ln 7 / ln 2 = 2.81** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Introduce your substitution in words ("let t = 2^x"). Then convert both values of t back to x, not just one.

**6.** log₂ (xy) = 5, so xy = 32 [1]. Then x (x + 4) = 32 [1] and x² + 4x − 32 = 0 [1]. (x + 8)(x − 4) = 0 [1]. x = −8 is rejected because log₂ x needs x > 0. **x = 4, y = 8** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Test each root in the original logarithmic equations. A pair like (−8, −4) satisfies the algebra but not the log conditions.

**7. (a)** lg y = lg (ax^n) = lg a + lg x^n [1] = n lg x + lg a [1]
**(b)** Gradient = (2.05 − 0.85)/(0.8 − 0.2) = 2, so **n = 2** [1]. Using the point (0.2, 0.85): lg a = 0.85 − 2 × 0.2 [1] = 0.45 [1]. a = 10^0.45 = **2.82** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The intercept is lg a, not a. Marks are usually lost by stopping at 0.45.

**8. (a)** ln y = 1.81, 2.27, 2.75, 3.21, 3.68 [2]
**(b)** ln (kb^x) = ln k + ln b^x [1] = x ln b + ln k
**(c)** Gradient = (3.68 − 1.81)/4 = 0.4675, so **b = e^0.4675 = 1.60** [1]. ln k = 1.81 − 0.4675 = 1.3425 [1], so **k = 3.83** [1]
**(d)** y = 3.83 × 1.596^7 ≈ **101** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep b and k unrounded when you substitute. Using the rounded 1.60 gives about 103, which shows why you round last.

**9. (a)** 7.5/12 = e^(−3k), so e^(3k) = 1.6 [1]. k = ln 1.6/3 = **0.157** [1]. C₀ = 12e^k [1] = **14.0** [1]
**(b)** 2 = 14.035e^(−kt) gives e^(kt) = 7.0176 [1], so t = ln 7.0176/0.15667 = **12.4 hours** [1]
**(c)** Half-life = ln 2/k [1] = **4.42 hours** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Carry the unrounded value of k from part (a) into later parts; re-using 0.157 introduces rounding errors.

**10. (a)** t = 40: P = 800e = **2170** [1]
**(b)** Limitation: the model grows without bound, but a woodland has limited land and light [1]. Refinement: use a model that levels off at a maximum population [1]
*Examiner insight:* A comment such as "it is not realistic" is too vague. Name the specific feature of the model and tie it to the woodland.

**11. (a)** ln V = ln (Ae^(rt)) = ln A + ln e^(rt) [1] = rt + ln A [1]
**(b)** Gradient = (7.88 − 6.92)/8 = 0.12 [1], so **r = 0.12** [1]. ln A = 6.92 − 2 × 0.12 = 6.68 [1], so **A = e^6.68 = 796** [1]
**(c)** Doubling: e^(0.12t) = 2 [1], so t = ln 2/0.12 = **5.78 years** [1]
**(d)** The data cover only 2 to 10 years, and 30 years is well outside that range [1]. Interest rates or returns are unlikely to stay at a constant 12% for decades [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give two distinct reasons. Repeating the same point in different words is only one reason.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not stating which root you reject when a logarithm would have a negative input.
- Treating log (x + y) as log x + log y.
- Rounding intermediate logs and then losing accuracy in the final answer.
- Giving the intercept of a log graph as the constant, not its logarithm.
- Mixing bases, for example lg for the gradient and ln for the intercept.
- Writing a bare numerical answer to a "show that" or "evaluate" question with no method.
- Giving vague comments about a model with no link to the context.
- Forgetting units, such as hours or years, in modelling answers.
