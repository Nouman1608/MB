## Questions

**1.** State one role of each of the following in the specific immune response: T helper cell, T killer cell and plasma cell. **[3]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by clonal selection and clonal expansion. **[4]**

**3.** A rabbit is injected with an antigen on day 0 and the same antigen again on day 40. The table shows antibody concentration in arbitrary units (AU).

| Event | Result |
|---|---|
| First exposure | Detected on day 6; peak of 18 AU on day 16 |
| Second exposure | 10 AU on day 41; peak of 400 AU on day 45 |

**(a)** Calculate the average speed at which the concentration rose from day 41 to day 45. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times higher the second peak is than the first, to 3 significant figures. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain the differences between the two responses. **[2]**

**4.** Describe the structure of an antibody molecule and explain how its structure allows it to bind to one type of antigen only. **[4]**

**5.** Outline the action of (a) opsonins, (b) agglutinins and (c) anti-toxins. **[3]**

**6.** Name the type of immunity (for example natural passive) in each situation.
**(a)** A person recovers from measles and does not catch it again. **[1]**
**(b)** A baby receives antibodies in breast milk. **[1]**
**(c)** A patient is given antibodies against rabies after a bite. **[1]**
**(d)** A child is given a vaccine containing a harmless form of a toxin. **[1]**

**7.** Explain what is meant by an autoimmune disease, using arthritis as an example. **[3]**

**8.** In a trial of a new vaccine, 12 000 volunteers received the vaccine and 12 000 received a placebo. Over the study period, 15 vaccinated and 135 placebo volunteers developed the disease.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage reduction in the proportion of people developing the disease that is linked to vaccination. **[3]**
**(b)** The disease has R₀ = 3. The simple model for herd immunity gives the proportion that must be immune as 1 - 1/R₀. Calculate the percentage of the population that must be vaccinated if the vaccine protects 80 % of those vaccinated. **[3]**

**9.** Suggest why vaccination programmes change over time, and explain why some changes are needed on a global scale. **[5]**

**10.** Explain why the maintenance of biodiversity is relevant to the discovery of medicines, and outline how personalised medicine could improve treatment. **[4]**

**11.** A bacterial culture contains 5.0 x 10⁸ cells. One cell in every 10⁷ is resistant to an antibiotic.
**(a)** Calculate how many resistant cells are present. **[1]**
**(b)** The antibiotic kills every sensitive cell. The resistant cells divide every 20 minutes and none die. Calculate the number of resistant cells after 6 hours, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how antibiotic resistance arises and spreads in a bacterial population, and give two implications for the treatment of infection. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** T helper: secretes cytokines (interleukins), signalling to B cells and other cells [1]. T killer: destroys body cells that display the antigen, such as virus-infected cells [1]. Plasma cell: secretes a specific antibody [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Each cell needs a role of its own. Answers that say "fights infection" for all three do not show what each cell does, so state a role for each.

**2.** Many lymphocytes exist, each with a receptor of one shape [1]. Clonal selection: only the lymphocyte whose receptor is complementary to the antigen is activated [1]. Clonal expansion: the selected cell divides by mitosis [1] to form a clone of identical cells with the same receptor [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the type of division, and state that the receptor must be complementary. "The cell is chosen" alone is too vague.

**3.** **(a)** Rate = (400 - 10) / (45 - 41) [1] = 390 / 4 = **97.5 AU per day** [1].
**(b)** 400 / 18 = **22.2 times** [1].
**(c)** The first exposure produced B memory cells (and T memory cells) [1], which are many and already selected, so the second response is faster and larger with no delay to find a matching cell [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A rate needs units that include time. Use two points from the steepest part of the curve and show the subtraction, so your method is clear.

**4.** Globulin protein with four polypeptide chains [1]: two heavy and two light, held by disulfide bridges [1]. The variable region at the tip of each arm has a particular amino acid sequence and tertiary shape [1] that is complementary to one antigen, like an enzyme and substrate, so other antigens do not fit [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the specificity to the variable region, not the whole molecule. Naming the hinge or constant region does not explain binding.

**5.** **(a)** Opsonins bind to the pathogen so phagocytes engulf it more readily [1]. **(b)** Agglutinins cause pathogens to clump together, making them easier to engulf [1]. **(c)** Anti-toxins bind to and neutralise toxins [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Outline" needs a clear action and, where possible, its effect. Repeating the term from the question does not describe an action.

**6.** **(a)** Natural active [1]. **(b)** Natural passive [1]. **(c)** Artificial passive [1]. **(d)** Artificial active [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Decide first whether the person made their own antibodies (active) or was given them (passive), then whether it was a medical procedure. Two words are needed.

**7.** An autoimmune disease occurs when the immune system fails to distinguish self from non-self [1] and attacks the body's own tissues [1]. In rheumatoid arthritis the joints are attacked, causing inflammation, pain and damage to cartilage [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Say "own cells or tissues", not "attacks the body", and name the tissue affected in the example.

**8.** **(a)** Vaccinated: 15 / 12 000 = 0.125 % [1]. Placebo: 135 / 12 000 = 1.125 % [1]. Reduction = (1.125 - 0.125) / 1.125 x 100 = **88.9 %** [1].
**(b)** Immune needed = 1 - 1/3 = 0.667 (66.7 %) [1]. Coverage = 66.7 / 0.80 [1] = **83.3 %** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare proportions, not raw case numbers, although here the group sizes are equal. Give the intermediate values so your method is clear.

**9.** Any five from: pathogens change, such as influenza surface antigens varying, so the vaccine must be updated [1]; new diseases appear and need new vaccines [1]; a disease may be eradicated, so vaccination can stop [1]; coverage may fall and outbreaks follow, so programmes are revised [1]; global issues such as cost, storage and transport, conflict or travel spreading disease across borders make coordinated action necessary [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Pair each change with its reason. A list of vaccines without reasons does not answer "why".

**10.** Species not yet studied may contain useful chemicals [1], so loss of biodiversity through extinction may lose potential medicines [1]. Personalised medicine uses a person's genome to choose the drug or dose [1], giving a better effect with fewer side effects [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give an actual link, not "biodiversity is important". Name what is lost and why that matters for medicines.

**11.** **(a)** 5.0 x 10⁸ / 10⁷ = **50** [1].
**(b)** 6 h = 18 doublings [1]. 50 x 2¹⁸ = **1.31 x 10⁷** [1].
**(c)** Mutation occurs by chance in a gene before exposure [1]. The antibiotic acts as a selection pressure and kills sensitive cells [1]. Resistant cells survive, reproduce and pass on the allele, including to other bacteria on plasmids [1]. Implications, any two: infections harder to treat, longer illness, cost or need for new antibiotics [2]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* The key idea is selection, not adaptation. Bacteria do not change because of the antibiotic, so avoid wording that suggests they do.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a rate without units that include time.
- Writing that antibodies are complementary to the pathogen rather than to the antigen.
- Saying T cells produce antibodies.
- Naming only one word for the type of immunity when two are needed.
- Describing herd immunity without saying it protects people who are not immune.
- Treating antibiotic resistance as bacteria "getting used to" the drug.
- Listing risks of antibiotics without describing resistance in terms of mutation and selection.
- Using raw numbers when proportions or percentages are needed.
