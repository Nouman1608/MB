## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which statement describes a sexually transmitted infection? **[1]**

A: an infection that is passed on only from mother to baby

B: an infection that is transmitted through sexual contact

C: an infection of the reproductive organs that cannot be passed on

D: an infection caused only by bacteria

**2.** What type of pathogen is HIV? **[1]**

A: bacterium

B: fungus

C: protoctist

D: virus

**3.** State the condition that HIV infection may lead to. **[1]**

**4.** Name **two** body fluids in which HIV can be transmitted. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why antibiotics cannot be used to cure HIV infection. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** ways in which a mother with HIV may pass the virus to her baby. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A hospital worker accidentally pricks her finger with a needle that has just been used to take blood from a patient with HIV.

**(a)** Explain how she could become infected with HIV. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest **one** precaution that hospitals take to reduce this risk. **[1]**

**8.** A man is found to have an STI. Explain how testing and contact tracing can reduce the spread of the infection. **[3]**

**9.** An STI clinic recorded the number of people tested for HIV, and the number who tested positive, in two years.

| Year | People tested | People testing positive |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2000 | 60 |
| 2 | 5000 | 100 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of people testing positive in each year. **[2]**

**(b)** The number of positive results rose but the percentage fell. Suggest an explanation. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why testing more people may reduce the spread of HIV in later years. **[2]**

**10.** **(a)** A person can be infected with HIV for years without any symptoms. Explain why this makes HIV harder to control. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why an STI spreads faster in a population in which many people have several sexual partners. **[2]**

**11.** A poster states: "HIV can be passed on by mosquito bites, by sharing cups and by unprotected sex."

**(a)** Identify the **one** correct method of transmission on the poster. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why sharing cups does not transmit HIV. **[1]**

**12.** A town starts a programme of free condoms and school lessons about STIs. Explain how **each** measure could reduce the spread of STIs. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]

**2.** **D** [1]

**3.** **AIDS** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Accept "acquired immune deficiency syndrome". Writing "HIV leads to HIV" or "an STI" does not score.

**4.** Any two of: **blood**; **semen**; **vaginal fluids**; **breast milk** [2].

**5.** HIV is a **virus** [1]; antibiotics **kill bacteria but do not affect viruses** [1].

**6.** Any two of: across the **placenta** during pregnancy; during **birth**; in **breast milk** [2].

**7. (a)** The needle may contain traces of the patient's **infected blood** [1], which can **enter her bloodstream** through the wound [1].

**(b)** Any one of: wearing **gloves**; using each needle **once only** and disposing of it safely in a sharps container; not re-capping used needles [1].

**8.** **Testing** shows the man is infected, so he can be **treated** and can take precautions so that he does not pass the infection on [1]. **Contact tracing** identifies his **sexual partners** [1], who can then be **tested and treated** before they infect other people [1].

**9. (a)** Year 1: 60 ÷ 2000 × 100 = **3%** [1]. Year 2: 100 ÷ 5000 × 100 = **2%** [1].

**(b)** **More people were tested** in year 2, so more infected people were found [1], but many of the extra people tested were **not infected** / were at lower risk, so the percentage positive fell [1].

**(c)** More infected people **find out that they have HIV** [1], so they can be **treated / use condoms / inform partners**, and are less likely to pass it on [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (b), "fewer people had HIV" contradicts the data; the number of positive results went up.

**10. (a)** People who have no symptoms **may not know they are infected** [1], so they may **continue to pass HIV on** to others, for example through unprotected sex [1].

**(b)** Each infected person has **more sexual contacts** [1], so the pathogen passes to **more people** in a shorter time [1].

**11. (a)** **Unprotected sex** [1]

**(b)** HIV is passed in blood, semen, vaginal fluids and breast milk; sharing cups **does not pass any of these fluids** into another person's body [1].

**12.** **Condoms** act as a **barrier** [1], so **body fluids** such as semen and vaginal fluids are **not exchanged** during sex [1]. **Lessons** teach people **how STIs are spread and how to protect themselves** [1], so they are more likely to **change their behaviour**, for example by using condoms, having fewer partners or getting tested [1].

*Examiner insight:* Two measures are named, so two marks are available for each; naming a measure without saying how it works earns at most half the marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "catching AIDS" instead of "infected with HIV".
- Saying antibiotics treat HIV.
- Giving everyday contact as a route of transmission.
- Naming a control method without explaining how it reduces spread.
- Misreading data: confusing the number of positive results with the percentage.
