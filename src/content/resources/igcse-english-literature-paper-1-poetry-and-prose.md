This guide covers **Paper 1 – Poetry and Prose**, the compulsory
component of Cambridge IGCSE English Literature 0475, 2026 series
(Version 2, valid for the 2026 series only). Literature syllabuses are
organised around named papers and set texts rather than a numbered
topic taxonomy, and the set-text list changes by examination year — this
guide covers the paper's structure and assessment objectives, which are
stable, rather than any specific text, since candidates should always
check the current set-text grid published for their own exam series.

## Where this fits in 0475

All candidates take Paper 1 plus either Paper 2 (Drama), or Paper 3
(Drama, Open Text) combined with either Paper 4 (Unseen) or Component 5
(Coursework). Paper 1 is therefore universal — every 0475 candidate
sits it regardless of which route they take through the rest of the
qualification — which makes its two sections, Poetry and Prose, the one
part of the syllabus every candidate needs to prepare for in full.

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE IGCSE ENGLISH LITERATURE 0475 — PAPER 1 POETRY AND PROSE**

- Format: a compulsory, externally set and centrally marked written
paper, 1 hour 30 minutes, 50 marks total
- Structure: candidates answer two questions — one from Section A
(Poetry) and one from Section B (Prose) — each worth 25 marks, with a
choice of two questions on each set text; relevant passages or poems
are printed on the question paper itself, and set texts may not be
taken into the exam room
- Assessment objectives tested by every question: AO1 knowledge of the
content of the text, shown through reference to detail and quotation;
AO2 understanding of characters, relationships, situations and themes;
AO3 understanding of the writer's intentions and methods, including
response to their use of language; AO4 personal response, expressed
either directly or through exploring the effect of the writer's choices
- Set texts: drawn from the poetry and prose set-text lists published
for the specific examination series — always confirm the current list
against the syllabus grid for the year being examined, since it rotates

## How to approach it

Because relevant passages are printed on the paper, Paper 1 is not
primarily a memory test — it rewards close, textual analysis over
recall, which changes how revision time should be spent. Rather than
memorising quotations wholesale, practise annotating a printed
extract cold and building an answer that moves through all four
assessment objectives in sequence: establish what happens (AO1), what
it reveals about character or theme (AO2), how the writer achieves that
effect through specific language or structural choices (AO3), and what
your own considered response to it is (AO4). A common way marks are
lost is stopping at AO1–AO2 (accurate but purely descriptive answers)
without pushing into AO3's "how" and AO4's personal engagement — timed
practice answering the exact essay-style prompts used in past papers,
for the texts actually being studied, is the most direct way to build
that habit before the real exam.

## The command word "Explore"

"Explore" is the one command word Cambridge formally defines for this
syllabus, glossed as "write in detail about particular aspects." It
appears in the implied-question style described above ("Explore the
ways in which…"), and understanding what it is asking for changes how
an answer should be built: not a broad tour of the whole extract, but
sustained, detailed engagement with a small number of well-chosen
moments. Two or three closely analysed quotations, each unpacked for
language, structure and effect, will consistently outscore six
quotations that are each mentioned only briefly — breadth of coverage
is not what earns marks on Paper 1, depth of analysis is.

## Building an answer that satisfies all four AOs together

The strongest answers do not treat AO1 knowledge, AO2 understanding,
AO3 analysis of method and AO4 personal response as four separate
boxes to tick in sequence. A well-built paragraph instead folds them
together: it states a point about the text, supports it with a short,
exactly quoted phrase, analyses the specific language or structural
choice within that quotation, and closes by connecting it to the
writer's likely intention or to the effect on a reader. Written this
way, AO4's personal response tends to emerge naturally through the
quality of the analysis itself, rather than needing a bolted-on "I
think…" sentence at the end of a paragraph, which examiners
routinely identify as a weaker, more mechanical way of satisfying
that objective.

Because the relevant passage or poem is printed on the question
paper, quotation should always be short and exact — copied precisely,
including punctuation, since claiming to analyse a specific word
choice while misquoting it undermines the analysis. With two 25-mark
answers to complete inside 90 minutes, timing discipline matters as
much as textual knowledge: a candidate who spends significantly
longer on their more confident first answer routinely runs short on
their second, so planning a roughly even split between the two
responses, with a few minutes of reading and planning built into
each, tends to protect the mark total more reliably than writing
until a first answer "feels finished."
