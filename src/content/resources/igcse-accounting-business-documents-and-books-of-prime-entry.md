## Business documents

A business document is evidence that a transaction happened. It is the **source** of the figures you record.

| Document | Issued by | What it shows | Used as a source for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Invoice | Seller to buyer | Goods, quantity, price, trade discount, total owed, terms | Seller: sales journal. Buyer: purchases journal |
| Credit note | Seller to buyer | Reduction in the amount owed (returns or overcharge) | Seller: sales returns journal. Buyer: purchases returns journal |
| Debit note | Buyer to seller, or seller to buyer | A request for credit, or an extra charge | The credit note or extra invoice that follows |
| Statement of account | Seller to buyer | Invoices, credit notes, payments and balance owing | Buyer checks it against the supplier's account |
| Receipt | Whoever receives payment | Proof of amount, date and method of payment | Cash book (receipts) |
| Cheque counterfoil | Payer's cheque book | Payee, date and amount of a cheque written | Cash book (payments side) |
| Paying-in slip | Business to bank | Cash and cheques paid into the bank | Cash book (bank receipts) |
| Bank statement | Bank to customer | Every deposit, withdrawal and charge | Items missing from the cash book; a check on it |

The debit note has more than one use, so read a question's wording carefully. A buyer may send one to list goods being returned and ask for a credit note. A seller may send one to charge for an undercharge, recorded like a small invoice.

**Worked example: an invoice.** Verrick Paints sells 40 tins of paint at a list price of $18 to Brayfield Decorators. Trade discount is 25%. Payment terms: 3% cash discount if paid within 10 days.

```
Invoice 5101, 3 June
40 tins x $18.00                 720.00
Less trade discount 25%         (180.00)
Invoice total                    540.00
Terms: 3% cash discount if paid within 10 days
```

The invoice total, 540, is the figure recorded. The cash discount is not on the invoice as a deduction because it may never be earned; it is recorded only if Brayfield pays in time (540 x 3% = 16.20).

**A statement of account.** Verrick's June statement to Brayfield lists invoice 5101 (540.00), the credit note (54.00), the transfer received (471.42) and the discount allowed (14.58), leaving a balance of nil. Brayfield checks it against its own purchases ledger; a difference means a missing document or an error.

**Bank statements.** Items on the statement but not yet in the cash book (bank charges, interest, a direct debit) are entered from it. A counterfoil or paying-in slip gives the details before the statement arrives.

### Manual and digital documents

Documents can be produced and recorded either way.

- **Manual:** handwritten invoices with duplicate copies, a cheque book with counterfoils, paper receipt books, a posted statement.
- **Digital:** invoices, credit notes and statements generated by software and emailed, electronic receipts, an online bank statement, records stored in software.

The information is the same in both forms. Speed, storage and error risk differ (see the last section).

## The books of prime entry

A book of prime entry holds the **first** record of a transaction, made from its source document. The ledger comes next, after posting. The seven books:

| Book | Records | Posted to |
|---|---|---|
| Sales journal | Credit sales | Customers' accounts (sales ledger); sales account (nominal ledger) |
| Purchases journal | Credit purchases of goods for resale | Suppliers' accounts (purchases ledger); purchases account |
| Sales returns journal | Goods customers return | Customers' accounts; sales returns account |
| Purchases returns journal | Goods returned to suppliers | Suppliers' accounts; purchases returns account |
| Cash book | Receipts and payments, cash and bank | Other side of each entry |
| Petty cash book | Small cash payments (vouchers) | Expense accounts |
| General journal | Anything fitting no other book | Accounts named in the entry |

### Journals for a month

Verrick Paints, June. Invoices are entered at the amount after trade discount.

**Sales journal**

| Date | Customer | Invoice | Gross | Trade discount | Net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 Jun | Brayfield Decorators | 5101 | 720.00 | 180.00 | 540.00 |
| 9 Jun | Ostlund Hotels | 5102 | 400.00 | 40.00 | 360.00 |
| 17 Jun | Hexley & Sons | 5103 | 250.00 | 50.00 | 200.00 |
| | **Total to sales account** | | | | **1,100.00** |

**Sales returns journal:** 6 Jun Brayfield Decorators, 4 tins, credit note 301, 54.00 (4 x 18.00 less 25%); 22 Jun Ostlund Hotels, credit note 302, 45.00. Total 99.00.

**Purchases journal:** 5 Jun Aldersey Pigments 640.00; 14 Jun Kinloch Brushes 310.00. Total 950.00.

| Date | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|

| | Tregear Fittings | | 1,200.00 |

Opening entries and corrections of errors also suit the general journal.

### Discounts

- **Trade discount** is deducted on the invoice, often for bulk orders or trade customers. It is never entered in the ledger: only the net figure is recorded.
- **Cash discount** rewards a buyer who settles early. Enter it when the payment is made: **discount allowed** (seller) and **discount received** (buyer) go in the cash book's discount columns.

Check the conditions. Ostlund Hotels pays after the discount period, so it earns none.

## The cash book

The cash book has a cash column and a bank column on each side, plus discount columns. Its **dual function**: it is a book of prime entry (transactions are first recorded in it) and the ledger account for cash and bank (its balances are those accounts' balances). The ledger holds no separate cash or bank account.

The method of payment only changes the column. Cash goes in the cash column. Cheques, debit and credit card receipts, online payments and bank transfers go in the bank column, because the money moves through the bank account.

Opening balances 1 June: cash 150, bank 2,840.

| Date | Details | Discount allowed | Cash | Bank | Date | Details | Discount received | Cash | Bank |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Jun | Balances b/d | | 150.00 | 2,840.00 | 12 Jun | Wages | | 95.00 | |
| 12 Jun | Brayfield (transfer) | 14.58 | | 471.42 | 16 Jun | Rent (cheque) | | | 400.00 |
| 12 Jun | Sales | | 85.00 | | 20 Jun | Bank (contra) | | | 100.00 |
| 18 Jun | Sales (debit card) | | | 230.00 | 27 Jun | Aldersey (online) | 12.00 | | 588.00 |
| 20 Jun | Cash (contra) | | 100.00 | | 29 Jun | Kinloch (cheque) | | | 310.00 |
| 25 Jun | Ostlund (cheque) | | | 315.00 | 30 Jun | Balances c/d | | 240.00 | 2,458.42 |
| | | **14.58** | **335.00** | **3,856.42** | | | **12.00** | **335.00** | **3,856.42** |

The **contra** entry moves 100 from the bank to the till: both sides are in the cash book, so nothing is posted. Brayfield owed 540 less the 54 credit note = 486; the 3% discount is 14.58, so 471.42 was paid. Aldersey was owed 640 less 40 = 600; the 2% discount is 12.00, so 588.00 was paid.

## Posting to the ledger

Each posting is the other half of the double entry.

- **Sales journal:** total 1,100 credited to sales; each invoice debited to the customer.
- **Sales returns journal:** total 99 debited to sales returns; each credit note credited to the customer.
- **Purchases journal:** total 950 debited to purchases; each invoice credited to the supplier.
- **Purchases returns journal:** total 40 credited to purchases returns; each credit note debited to the supplier.
- **Cash book:** receipts are credited to the customer or income account; payments are debited to the supplier or expense account. Discount allowed (14.58) is debited and discount received (12.00) is credited, as column totals.

Brayfield's account after posting (sales ledger):

```
Brayfield Decorators account
3 Jun   Sales            540.00 | 6 Jun   Sales returns       54.00
                                | 12 Jun  Bank               471.42
                                | 12 Jun  Discount allowed    14.58
                         540.00 |                            540.00
```

The two sides agree, so the balance is nil, matching the statement.

## Petty cash and the imprest system

The petty cash book records small payments, such as stamps and fares. Under the **imprest system** the petty cashier holds a fixed float, pays out against vouchers, and is given exactly the amount spent, so the float is restored. **Purpose:** control. The chief cashier sees every voucher before topping up, and the main cash book is spared many small entries.

Verrick uses a $120 float. Payments in June:

| Date | Voucher | Details | Total | Postage | Travel | Cleaning | Sundry |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 Jun | 1 | Stamps | 18.00 | 18.00 | | | |
| 6 Jun | 2 | Taxi | 22.00 | | 22.00 | | |
| 11 Jun | 3 | Cleaning materials | 14.50 | | | 14.50 | |
| 14 Jun | 4 | Stamps | 9.00 | 9.00 | | | |
| 19 Jun | 5 | Tea and coffee | 11.25 | | | | 11.25 |
| 23 Jun | 6 | Bus fare | 8.60 | | 8.60 | | |
| 27 Jun | 7 | Cleaning materials | 6.40 | | | 6.40 | |
| | | **Totals** | **89.75** | **27.00** | **30.60** | **20.90** | **11.25** |

Cash left in the tin should be 120 less 89.75 = 30.25. The cashier receives **89.75** from the cash book (a bank payment) and the float is back to 120. The column totals are debited to postage, travel, cleaning and sundry expenses. If the count differs from 30.25, a voucher is missing or there is an error.

## Benefits and limitations

**Keeping cash at the business property**

- Benefits: small payments and change are available at once.
- Limitations: theft, loss or miscounting; secure storage and insurance; no interest earned.

**Manual original entry** (handwritten books)

- Benefits: low cost, no equipment, works through a power cut.
- Limitations: slow; arithmetic and copying errors; hard to search, back up and summarise.

**Digital original entry** (software)

- Benefits: totals and postings calculated automatically; documents produced quickly; records simple to back up, search and report on.
- Limitations: set-up and training cost; system failure or data loss; an input error spreads to every record it feeds.

## Common errors

- Recording the gross invoice, not the figure after trade discount.
- Putting a cheque payment in the cash column, or equipment in the purchases journal.
- Restoring the petty cash float with the cash left in the tin, not the amount spent.
