## Questions

**1.** State whether each disease is caused by a virus, a bacterium or a fungus. (a) Tobacco mosaic disease **[1]** (b) Barley powdery mildew **[1]** (c) Crown gall disease **[1]** (d) Chlamydia **[1]**

**2.** Athlete's foot is a common fungal infection.

**(a)** Describe two signs of athlete's foot. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest how a person could catch it at a swimming pool, and how this could be prevented. **[2]**

**3.** HIV is a sexually transmitted infection.

**(a)** State two ways, other than sexual contact, in which HIV can be passed on. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how HIV infection can lead to AIDS. **[3]**
**(c)** A patient takes antiretroviral drugs. Explain why they can still pass on HIV. **[1]**

**4.** A clinic screened 2,150 people for chlamydia in a year. 172 tested positive.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage who tested positive. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why many people with chlamydia do not know they have it. **[1]**
**(c)** State one possible long-term effect of untreated chlamydia. **[1]**

**5.** A tomato grower finds leaves with pale and dark green patches. The cause is tobacco mosaic virus.

**(a)** Explain why infected plants grow more slowly. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest two actions to stop the virus spreading to other plants. **[2]**

**6.** A farmer grows 9,000 barley plants. She chooses 30 plants at random and 6 show powdery mildew.

**(a)** Calculate an estimate of the number of infected plants. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the plants were chosen at random. **[1]**
**(c)** Give one change to the method that would improve the estimate. **[1]**

**7.** Describe how each of these defends a plant against pathogens. (a) the waxy cuticle **[2]** (b) the cell wall **[1]** (c) antimicrobial substances **[2]**

**8.** Researchers grew two types of a plant. Type X makes a low concentration of an antimicrobial chemical. Type Y makes a high concentration. Plants were exposed to the same fungal spores. The mean percentage of leaf area covered by fungus was 42% for type X and 11% for type Y.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage reduction in leaf area covered by fungus in type Y compared with type X. **[2]**
**(b)** What conclusion can be drawn? **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest one variable that must be kept the same in the investigation. **[1]**

**9.** Young trees were given different numbers of wounds on the stem and then exposed to *Agrobacterium tumefaciens*. The mean number of galls per tree was recorded.

| Wounds per tree | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean galls per tree | 0 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 8 | 9 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship shown. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the relationship using how the bacterium infects a plant. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest two ways a nursery could reduce crown gall in its stock. **[2]**

**10.** (Higher Tier only) A laboratory tests plant samples for the DNA of a fungal pathogen. A positive control gives a band at 300 base pairs and a negative control gives no band. Sample P gives a band at 300 base pairs, sample Q gives no band and sample R gives a band at 180 base pairs.

**(a)** State what the results show for each sample. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe how a test using antibodies could detect the pathogen. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one advantage of field diagnosis by observation and one advantage of laboratory testing. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) virus [1] (b) fungus [1] (c) bacterium [1] (d) bacterium [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each part needs the pathogen type, not the name of a treatment; answers such as "bacteria" for powdery mildew gain nothing.

**2. (a)** Any two from: itchy skin [1]; red, flaky or cracked skin between the toes [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Spores picked up from damp floors or shared towels [1]; wear flip-flops or dry feet well and do not share towels [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Suggest" questions need a link between the cause and the prevention, so name the route of infection as well as the measure.

**3. (a)** Any two from: sharing needles [1]; from mother to baby (in the womb, at birth or breast milk) [1]; infected blood (mark any two). **[2]**
**(b)** HIV infects and destroys white blood cells (T helper lymphocytes) [1]; so the immune system is weakened [1]; the person then cannot fight other infections, which is AIDS [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Drugs reduce but do not remove the virus, so some remains in body fluids [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that HIV is the virus and AIDS is the later stage; using the two words as if they mean the same thing is a common way to lose credit.

**4. (a)** 172 ÷ 2,150 [1] × 100 = **8.0%** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Many infected people have no symptoms [1] **[1]**
**(c)** Infertility, or damage to the reproductive system [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the division as well as the final figure; and an answer to a stated number of significant figures should match the data given.

**5. (a)** Virus destroys chlorophyll in patches [1]; so less light is absorbed and less photosynthesis happens [1]; less glucose is made for growth [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Any two from: remove and destroy infected plants [1]; wash hands and tools between plants [1]; grow resistant varieties (mark any two). **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* An explanation needs a chain: damage, then less photosynthesis, then less glucose; "it makes them weak" is too vague to credit.

**6. (a)** 6 ÷ 30 = 0.2 or 20% [1]; 0.2 × 9,000 [1]; = **1,800 plants** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** To avoid bias (for example choosing only plants at the field edge) [1] **[1]**
**(c)** Use a larger sample, or repeat in several places and take a mean [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the proportion first and then scale it up; a reason for random choice must mention bias, and "to be fair" alone is too vague.

**7. (a)** It is a waxy, waterproof layer [1]; forming a barrier microbes cannot easily cross, and water runs off it [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** A tough cellulose layer that pathogens must break through to reach the cell [1] **[1]**
**(c)** Chemicals that kill or stop the growth of bacteria and fungi [1]; they work if the pathogen gets past the physical barriers [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Describe the defence and how it prevents infection; naming the cuticle without saying it is a barrier does not complete the point.

**8. (a)** 42 − 11 = 31 [1]; 31 ÷ 42 × 100 = **73.8%** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Type Y has much less fungus than type X [1]; so a higher concentration of the antimicrobial chemical gives better protection [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Number of spores, time of exposure, temperature, light or leaf size (any one) [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A conclusion should refer to both variables in the data, and should say "suggests" if the study cannot prove cause.

**9. (a)** As the number of wounds increases, the number of galls increases [1]; a positive correlation [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The bacteria enter through wounds [1]; so more wounds give more entry points and more infections [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Any two from: avoid wounding plants [1]; clean tools [1]; buy healthy stock; destroy infected plants (mark any two). **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Describe" asks only for the pattern, using figures if possible; save the biology for the "explain" part.

**10. (a)** P has a band matching the positive control, so it contains the pathogen DNA [1]; Q has no band, so no pathogen DNA was found [1]; R has a band of a different size, so it is not the same DNA [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Antibodies that bind a specific antigen from the pathogen are added to the sample [1]; a colour change or line shows the antigen is present [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Field: quick and cheap, no equipment needed [1]; lab: more specific, so it can confirm the exact pathogen [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the controls when interpreting a result; a conclusion that ignores them leaves out the reason the sample can be trusted.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming the wrong pathogen type for the named plant diseases.
- Treating HIV and AIDS as the same thing.
- Saying antiretroviral drugs cure HIV.
- Saying the cuticle or cell wall kills pathogens.
- Writing "less growth" without the chain from damage to less photosynthesis.
- Leaving out the working in percentage calculations.
- Explaining a correlation without naming how the bacteria enter the plant.
- Ignoring the controls in a DNA test result.
