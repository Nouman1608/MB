## Section A

**1.** Which organism is the vector of malaria?

A: Aedes mosquito

B: female Anopheles mosquito

C: housefly

D: Plasmodium

**[1]**

**2.** Which toxic component of tobacco smoke combines with haemoglobin?

A: carbon monoxide

B: nicotine

C: tar

D: water vapour

**[1]**

**3.** Define a pathogen. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** barriers that protect the human body against pathogens. **[2]**

**5.** State the name of the pathogen that causes cholera. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Malaria is a transmissible disease.

**(a)** Define a transmissible disease. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe how malaria is transmitted from one person to another. **[3]**

**(c)** Describe **three** ways of controlling the spread of malaria, with reference to the life cycle of the mosquito. **[3]**

**(d)** A village had 150 cases of malaria per 1000 people. After a control programme it had 45 cases per 1000. Calculate the percentage reduction. **[2]**

**7.** HIV is a viral pathogen.

**(a)** State **two** ways in which HIV is transmitted. **[2]**

**(b)** State **two** ways of reducing the spread of HIV. **[2]**

**(c)** State the relationship between HIV and AIDS. **[1]**

**8.** Explain how the cholera toxin causes diarrhoea and dehydration. **[4]**

**9.** Explain why a clean water supply and sewage treatment help to control cholera. **[3]**

**10.** The table shows illustrative death rates from lung cancer per 100 000 men per year.

| Smoking habit | Deaths per 100 000 per year |
|---|---|
| Non-smokers | 20 |
| 10 cigarettes per day | 140 |
| 20 cigarettes per day | 300 |

**(a)** Calculate how many times higher the death rate is in smokers of 20 cigarettes per day than in non-smokers. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the trend in the data. **[2]**

**(c)** Name the component of tobacco smoke that causes lung cancer, and state how it affects the airways. **[2]**

**11.** State **three** effects of excessive alcohol consumption. **[3]**

**12.** Explain why smoking during pregnancy can reduce the birth weight of a baby. **[3]**

**13.** Explain why people who have had a lot of exposure to mosquito bites in a malaria area are not necessarily infected, and state what must happen for infection to occur. **[2]**

**14.** Describe two ways in which the skin and the airways protect the body from pathogens. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A disease-causing organism [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two of: skin; hairs in the nose; mucus; stomach acid [1 each]. **[2]**

**5.** A bacterium (*Vibrio cholerae*) [1]. **[1]**

**6.**

**(a)** A disease in which the pathogen can be passed from one host to another [1].

**(b)** An infected person is bitten by a female *Anopheles* mosquito, which takes in the parasite with the blood [1]. The mosquito bites another person [1] and the parasite enters that person's blood in the mosquito's saliva [1].

**(c)** Any three of: drain stagnant water or cover tanks, so eggs and larvae cannot develop [1]; spread oil on water so that larvae and pupae cannot breathe [1]; introduce fish that eat larvae [1]; spray insecticide to kill adults and larvae [1]; use bed nets, screens or repellent to prevent bites [1].

**(d)** Reduction = 150 - 45 = 105 [1]. 105 divided by 150 x 100 = 70 per cent [1]. **[9]**

**7.**

**(a)** Any two of: unprotected sexual intercourse; sharing needles; from an infected mother to her baby; infected blood [1 each].

**(b)** Any two of: use condoms; do not share needles; screen donated blood; antiretroviral drugs for infected pregnant women; education [1 each].

**(c)** HIV infection may lead to AIDS, which is the stage when the immune system is badly weakened [1]. **[5]**

**8.** The bacteria produce a toxin in the small intestine [1]. The toxin causes the cells lining the intestine to secrete chloride ions into the gut [1]. This lowers the water potential in the gut, so water moves into the gut by osmosis [1]. The result is diarrhoea, with dehydration and loss of ions from the blood [1]. **[4]**

**9.** The cholera bacteria are passed on in water contaminated with faeces [1]. Sewage treatment stops faeces from entering the water [1], and a clean treated supply means people do not drink the bacteria [1]. **[3]**

**10.**

**(a)** 300 divided by 20 = 15 times [1].

**(b)** The death rate increases as the number of cigarettes increases [1], from 20 for non-smokers to 300 for 20 cigarettes per day [1].

**(c)** Tar [1], which coats the airways, paralyses the cilia and contains chemicals that cause cancer [1]. **[5]**

**11.** Any three of: reduced self-control; slowed reactions; depressant effect on the nervous system; damage to the liver; social problems such as accidents, violence and family problems [1 each]. **[3]**

**12.** Carbon monoxide combines with haemoglobin [1], so the mother's blood carries less oxygen to the baby [1]. The baby does not grow as well, so it has a lower birth weight [1]. **[3]**

**13.** Only female *Anopheles* mosquitoes that carry *Plasmodium* can transmit malaria [1]. A person is infected only if such a mosquito bites them and the parasite passes into their blood [1]. **[2]**

**14.** The skin is a tough barrier, and cuts are sealed by blood clots [1]. In the airways, mucus traps pathogens and hairs in the nose trap dust, and cilia move the mucus away [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming the vector as the pathogen, or the reverse.
- Forgetting osmosis when explaining cholera diarrhoea.
- Saying that AIDS is the virus.
- Confusing the effects of nicotine, tar and carbon monoxide.
- Giving "clean water" as a control for malaria.
