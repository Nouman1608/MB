These notes cover subtopic **3.1 Momentum and Newton's laws of motion** of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702, 2025–2027 series.

## Six things the syllabus asks for

1. Mass resists change in motion.
2. F = ma, with acceleration in the direction of the resultant force.
3. Momentum p = mv.
4. Force = rate of change of momentum.
5. Newton's three laws, stated and applied.
6. Weight = mg, the effect of a gravitational field on a mass.

## Core equations

```
p = m v                       (kg m s^-1, equivalently N s)
F = delta p / delta t         (general form of the second law)
F = m a                       (constant mass only)
W = m g                       (g = 9.81 m s^-2 near Earth's surface)
F = (delta m / delta t) v     (steady flow of mass brought to rest, or set moving, at speed v)
```

## Newton's laws: wording to learn

| Law | Precise statement |
|---|---|
| First | A body remains at rest or at constant velocity unless a resultant force acts on it |
| Second | The resultant force is proportional to the rate of change of momentum, in the same direction |
| Third | If A exerts a force on B, B exerts an equal and opposite force on A |

**Third-law pair checklist:** two different bodies; same type of force; equal magnitude; opposite direction; act at the same time.

## Free-body method

1. Isolate one body (or one system).
2. Draw every force **on** it, with arrows from the point of action.
3. Pick the positive direction (along the acceleration).
4. Resultant = forward forces − backward forces = ma.
5. For a system of connected bodies, internal forces (tension in a tow bar, contact between blocks) cancel; find them by isolating one body.

## Recall examples

- **Lift accelerating down at 2.0 m s⁻², 80 kg passenger:** mg − R = ma, so R = 80(9.81 − 2.0) = 625 N (weight is still 785 N).
- **Lift cable, 900 kg lift accelerating up at 0.50 m s⁻²:** T = 900(9.81 + 0.50) = 9280 N.
- **Two blocks pushed together:** 15 N pushes a 3.0 kg block, which pushes a 2.0 kg block on a smooth floor. a = 15/5.0 = 3.0 m s⁻²; contact force on the 2.0 kg block = 2.0 × 3.0 = 6.0 N.
- **Fan blowing air:** 0.60 kg s⁻¹ of air accelerated from rest to 8.0 m s⁻¹ needs 0.60 × 8.0 = 4.8 N; the air pushes back on the fan with 4.8 N.
- **Momentum-time graph:** the gradient is the resultant force. p rising steadily from 0 to 12 kg m s⁻¹ in 4.0 s means F = 3.0 N.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Inertia | Resistance to change in motion, measured by mass |
| Resultant force | Vector sum of all forces on a body |
| Linear momentum | Product of mass and velocity; a vector |
| Weight | Gravitational force on a mass, W = mg |
| Free-body diagram | Diagram showing only the forces acting on one body |
| Normal contact force | Force perpendicular to a surface, exerted by that surface on a body touching it |

## Mass versus weight

| Mass | Weight |
|---|---|
| Scalar, in kg | Vector (a force), in N |
| Measures inertia | Measures gravitational pull |
| Same everywhere | Depends on g |

## Exam traps

- Putting only the driving force into F = ma, ignoring resistive forces.
- Saying a body moving upwards must have an upward resultant force.
- Calling weight and normal contact force a third-law pair.
- "Mass times speed" for momentum: it must be velocity.
- Using F = ma when mass is flowing (hoses, conveyor belts, fans).
- Writing weight in kg, or mass in N.
- Forgetting the sign of momentum when a body reverses direction.

## Self-test

1. What property of an object does mass describe in this syllabus?
2. State Newton's first law.
3. A crate of mass 50 kg is pushed with 180 N against a friction force of 80 N. Find its acceleration.
4. Define linear momentum and give its unit.
5. Find the momentum of a 0.16 kg ball moving at 30 m s⁻¹.
6. State the general form of Newton's second law.
7. When does F = ma apply?
8. Give two conditions for a pair of forces to be a third-law pair.
9. A lift is moving downwards and slowing down. In which direction is the resultant force on a passenger?
10. A 0.20 kg ball moving east at 5.0 m s⁻¹ is deflected to move north at 5.0 m s⁻¹. Find the magnitude of its change in momentum.
11. Water strikes a wall at 2.0 kg s⁻¹ and 9.0 m s⁻¹ and stops. Find the force on the wall.
12. Why does an astronaut's mass not change on the Moon although the weight does?

### Answers

1. Its resistance to change in motion (inertia).
2. A body remains at rest or moves at constant velocity unless acted on by a resultant force.
3. (180 − 80) ÷ 50 = 2.0 m s⁻².
4. The product of mass and velocity; kg m s⁻¹ (or N s).
5. 0.16 × 30 = 4.8 kg m s⁻¹, in the direction of motion.
6. The resultant force is proportional to (equal to, in SI units) the rate of change of momentum.
7. When the mass of the object is constant.
8. Any two: act on different bodies; same type of force; equal in magnitude; opposite in direction.
9. Upwards (the acceleration is opposite to the downward velocity).
10. 0.20 × √(5.0² + 5.0²) = 1.4 kg m s⁻¹.
11. 2.0 × 9.0 = 18 N.
12. Mass is a property of the object (its inertia), while weight depends on the gravitational field strength, which is smaller on the Moon.
