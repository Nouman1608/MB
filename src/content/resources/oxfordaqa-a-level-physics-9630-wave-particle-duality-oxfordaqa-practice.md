These questions cover section **3.5.11 Wave particle duality** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification: the evidence from electron diffraction and the photoelectric effect, the de Broglie equation λ = h/mv, how diffraction changes with momentum, and how ideas about matter have changed. Marks are shown for each part. All data are invented for practice. Use h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, electron mass 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg and proton mass 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.

Revise first with the [wave-particle duality study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-wave-particle-duality-oxfordaqa/) and the [wave-particle duality revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-wave-particle-duality-oxfordaqa-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State the de Broglie equation and define each symbol. **[2]**

**2.** Which observation suggests that particles have wave properties?

A: the photoelectric effect

B: line emission spectra

C: electron diffraction

D: the production of X-rays **[1]**

**3.** Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of an electron moving at 3.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹. **[2]**

**4.** Calculate the speed of an electron whose de Broglie wavelength is 0.20 nm. **[2]**

**5.** The momentum of a particle is doubled. Its de Broglie wavelength

A: is halved

B: is unchanged

C: is doubled

D: is quartered **[1]**

**6.** A tennis ball of mass 0.058 kg is served at 45 m s⁻¹. Calculate its de Broglie wavelength and explain why it is not diffracted by the gap in a net. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Electrons are accelerated from rest through a potential difference of 2500 V.

**(a)** Calculate the kinetic energy of each electron in joules. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the momentum of each electron. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the de Broglie wavelength. **[1]**

**(d)** The electrons strike a thin crystalline sample and produce rings on a screen. The voltage is reduced. Describe and explain the change in the rings. **[3]**

**8.** Explain how and why the amount of diffraction of a beam of particles by a crystal changes when the momentum of the particles is increased. **[4]**

**9.** An electron and a proton both travel at 3.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of each. **[2]**

**(b)** The atomic spacing in a crystal is about 2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m. Explain which particle would be noticeably diffracted by the crystal. **[2]**

**10.** An electron has a de Broglie wavelength of 0.12 nm.

**(a)** Calculate its momentum. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate its kinetic energy in electronvolts. **[2]**

**(c)** State the potential difference through which it must have been accelerated from rest. **[1]**

**11.** Describe how scientific understanding of the nature of light changed from the early 1800s to the early 1900s, and explain what this shows about how scientific models develop. **[4]**

**12.** Explain what is meant by wave-particle duality, using one piece of evidence for the wave nature of matter and one for the particle nature of electromagnetic radiation. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** λ = h/mv [1], where λ is the de Broglie wavelength, h the Planck constant, m the mass of the particle and v its speed (mv is the momentum) [1].

**2.** **C** [1]

**3.** λ = h/mv = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴/(9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 3.0 × 10⁶) [1] = **2.4 × 10⁻¹⁰ m** [1]

**4.** v = h/mλ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴/(9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 0.20 × 10⁻⁹) [1] = **3.6 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹** [1]

**5.** **A** [1]

**6.** λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴/(0.058 × 45) = **2.5 × 10⁻³⁴ m** [1]; this is vastly smaller than the gap, and diffraction is only noticeable when λ is comparable to the gap size [1].

**7.**

**(a)** Ek = eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 2500 = **4.0 × 10⁻¹⁶ J** [1]

**(b)** p = √(2mEk) [1] = √(2 × 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 4.0 × 10⁻¹⁶) = **2.7 × 10⁻²³ kg m s⁻¹** [1]

**(c)** λ = h/p = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴/2.70 × 10⁻²³ = **2.5 × 10⁻¹¹ m** [1]

**(d)** The rings become **larger** (more widely spaced) [1]; a lower voltage gives the electrons less kinetic energy and less momentum, so their de Broglie wavelength is longer [1]; a longer wavelength is diffracted through larger angles by the same atomic spacing [1].

**8.** Increasing the momentum decreases the de Broglie wavelength, since λ = h/p [1]. Diffraction is greatest when the wavelength is comparable to the spacing of the atoms [1]; a shorter wavelength compared with the same spacing is diffracted less [1], so the maxima are at smaller angles and the pattern is less spread out [1].

**9.**

**(a)** Electron: λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴/(9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 3.0 × 10⁶) = **2.4 × 10⁻¹⁰ m** [1]; proton: λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴/(1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ × 3.0 × 10⁶) = **1.3 × 10⁻¹³ m** [1]

**(b)** The **electron** [1]: its wavelength is comparable to the atomic spacing, while the proton's is over a thousand times smaller, so the proton would be diffracted very little [1].

**10.**

**(a)** p = h/λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴/0.12 × 10⁻⁹ = **5.5 × 10⁻²⁴ kg m s⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** Ek = p²/2m = (5.525 × 10⁻²⁴)²/(2 × 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) = 1.68 × 10⁻¹⁷ J [1] = **105 eV** [1]

**(c)** **105 V** (about 100 V) [1]

**11.** In the early 1800s Young's double-slit experiment showed interference, so light was accepted as a wave [1]. Later Maxwell's theory described light as an electromagnetic wave [1]. In 1905 Einstein explained the photoelectric effect using photons, so light was also given a particle nature [1]. This shows that models are kept while they explain the evidence and are modified or replaced when new evidence, confirmed by other scientists, contradicts them [1].

**12.** Wave-particle duality means that both matter and electromagnetic radiation can show wave properties and particle properties, depending on the experiment [1]. Electron diffraction shows that particles such as electrons behave as waves, since diffraction is a wave property [1]; the photoelectric effect shows that light delivers energy in discrete photons, each absorbed by one electron, which is particle behaviour [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping the evidence: the photoelectric effect is about light, electron diffraction is about matter (Questions 2 and 12).
- Using kinetic energy instead of momentum in λ = h/p (Questions 7 and 10).
- Putting the accelerating voltage straight into λ = h/mv (Question 7).
- Saying higher momentum gives more diffraction (Questions 7 and 8).
- Listing dates and names without the evidence behind each change (Question 11).
- Comparing masses rather than wavelengths when judging diffraction (Question 9).
