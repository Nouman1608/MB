## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for developing it, up to two marks for each of two points (question 6: two marks for what the statement means and two for the explanation of responsibility or test). The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** Books (the revealed scriptures) [1].

**2.** Yaum e Akhirah, belief in the Last Day [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: Tawhid; Malaikah; Kutub; Risalah; Yaum e Akhirah; Al-Qadr.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: Allah revealed books to prophets, such as the Tawrat, Zabur, Injil and Suhuf; the Qur'an is the final book; the Qur'an is preserved by Allah; the Qur'an confirms and corrects earlier books; the books contain guidance on worship and conduct.

**5.** Reason identified [1] and developed [1], for each of two reasons.

- **Guidance** [1]: the messengers taught people how to worship Allah and live well [1].
- **Role models** [1]: Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh) lived the Qur'an, so Muslims follow his Sunnah [1].
- **Unity of message** [1]: all messengers taught Tawhid, so Muslims respect every prophet and "make no distinction" between them [1].
- **Finality** [1]: Muhammad (pbuh) is the Seal of the Prophets, so his message is complete for all times [1].

**6.** Up to four marks: nothing happens without Allah's knowledge and permission [1]; this includes pleasant and unpleasant events [1]; it does not mean that Allah commands or likes evil, as humans choose their actions [1]; people are therefore responsible and will be judged, and hardship is treated as a test of patience [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two ways, the way identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Ways (AO1):

- **Patience in hardship** [1]: a Muslim who loses a job or falls ill accepts that it is within Allah's decree and does not despair [1].
- **Gratitude** [1]: success is seen as Allah's gift, so there is thanks, not pride [1].
- **Effort and trust** [1]: a Muslim works and plans, then relies on Allah for the outcome [1].
- **Peace of mind** [1]: accepting what cannot be changed reduces anxiety about the future [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Allah does not require of any soul more than what it can afford" (Qur'an 2:286): every test is within a person's ability [2].
- "No disaster strikes except by permission of Allah" (64:11): hardship is accepted with trust in Allah [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Reasons (AO1):

- **It is commanded in the Qur'an** [1]: belief in angels is listed with belief in Allah, the books and the messengers, so rejecting angels means rejecting part of faith [1].
- **Angels delivered revelation** [1]: Jibril (AS) brought the Qur'an to the Prophet (pbuh), so belief in angels supports trust in the Qur'an [1].
- **Accountability** [1]: the Kiraman Katibeen record deeds, so Muslims behave well even in private [1].
- **Comfort and unseen reality** [1]: angels assist believers, which shows that the unseen world is real and cared for by Allah [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "All of them have believed in Allah and His angels and His books and His messengers" (2:285) [2].
- Hadith of Jibril: faith is "to believe in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day" [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- Belief in the unseen (*ghayb*) is a mark of the God-conscious: Allah, angels, the Last Day and the decree cannot be seen.
- It shapes behaviour: the Muslim acts as if accountable to Allah and recorded by angels.
- It unites Muslims across cultures: all share the same list of beliefs.
- **Against the statement:** belief alone is not enough; the passage says "we hear and we obey", so action matters; faith without Salah, Zakah and good conduct is incomplete. Some key beliefs concern the seen world, such as prophets who lived in history and the Qur'an, which can be read.
- **Judgement:** belief in the unseen is the foundation and test of faith, but it must lead to obedience and good deeds.

*Use of the passage*

- "believed in Allah and His angels and His books and His messengers" lists the unseen and the revealed.
- "We hear and we obey" links belief to action.
- "to You is the [final] destination" refers to the Last Day.
- "Allah does not require of any soul more than what it can afford" shows trust in the decree.
- "All good will be for its own benefit, and all evil will be to its own loss" shows personal responsibility.
