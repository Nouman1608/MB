These notes condense outcomes 1.1.13 to 1.1.20 of the Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Subsidiary/Advanced Level in Accounting (YAC11) specification, Issue 2: causes of and reasons for depreciation, the reducing balance, straight line and revaluation methods, ledger accounts, the effect on profit of the method and of a change of method, disposals and the schedule of non-current assets. Full worked examples are in the [depreciation study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-non-current-asset-depreciation/), and exam-style questions are in the [depreciation practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-non-current-asset-depreciation-practice/). Amounts are in dollars; businesses are invented.

## Core recall

```
Straight line         (cost − residual value) ÷ useful life      or  % × cost
Reducing balance      % × carrying amount at start of year       (no residual deducted)
Revaluation           opening valuation + purchases − closing valuation
Carrying amount       cost − accumulated depreciation
Profit/loss on disposal  proceeds (or part-exchange allowance) − carrying amount at disposal
```

- Depreciation allocates cost; it is not cash and not market value.
- Causes: wear and tear, time, obsolescence, depletion, inadequacy.
- Reasons: accruals (matching), fairer annual profit, realistic carrying amount, prudence.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Residual value | Expected amount from the asset at the end of its useful life |
| Useful life | Period over which the business expects to use the asset |
| Provision for depreciation | Account holding accumulated depreciation (credit balance) |
| Carrying amount | Cost less accumulated depreciation |
| Depreciation policy | Rules for part years: by months, or full year in year of purchase and none in year of disposal |
| Disposal account | Account that collects cost, accumulated depreciation and proceeds to find profit or loss |
| Part-exchange allowance | Value given for an old asset against a new one; acts as proceeds |
| Schedule of non-current assets | Table of cost, depreciation and carrying amount movements by class |

## Ledger entries

| Event | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Buy asset | Asset (cost) | Bank / payable |
| Annual depreciation | Depreciation expense (to profit or loss) | Provision for depreciation |
| Disposal: transfer cost | Disposal | Asset (cost) |
| Disposal: transfer depreciation | Provision for depreciation | Disposal |
| Disposal: cash proceeds | Bank | Disposal |
| Disposal: part exchange | Asset (new asset) | Disposal |
| Profit on disposal | Disposal | Statement of profit or loss |
| Loss on disposal | Statement of profit or loss | Disposal |
| Revaluation method charge | Statement of profit or loss | Asset account (no provision account) |

## Small worked reminders

### Same asset, two methods

Cost $25,000, residual $1,000, life 6 years.

```
Straight line: (25,000 − 1,000) ÷ 6 = 4,000 every year
Reducing balance 30%: Year 1 7,500; Year 2 (25,000 − 7,500) × 30% = 5,250
Year 1 profit is 3,500 lower under reducing balance; Year 2 is 1,250 lower
```

### Revaluation

Crockery valued at $2,400 at the start, $900 bought, valued at $2,600 at the end: depreciation = 2,400 + 900 − 2,600 = $700.

### Disposal for cash

Machine cost $12,000, accumulated depreciation $7,000, sold for $6,000. Carrying amount $5,000, so profit on disposal $1,000.

### Change of method

Apply the new method to the carrying amount at the date of change over the remaining life; do not restate earlier years; disclose the reason and the effect on profit.

## Effect on profit

| Method | Early years | Later years |
|---|---|---|
| Straight line | Moderate | Moderate |
| Reducing balance | Lower profit | Higher profit |
| Revaluation | Varies with valuations | Varies |

Over the whole life total depreciation is broadly the same; the timing differs.

## Schedule layout (per class and in total)

```
Cost: at start + additions − disposals = at end
Accumulated depreciation: at start + charge for year − on disposals = at end
Carrying amount at end = cost at end − accumulated depreciation at end
```

## Exam traps

- Residual value deducted in reducing balance.
- Wrong part-year policy.
- Missing the months of depreciation before a disposal when the policy is "by months".
- Not removing the disposed asset's depreciation from the provision.
- Recording a part-exchanged new asset at the cash paid only.
- A provision account used for revaluation-method assets.
- Restating earlier years after a change of method.
- Saying depreciation provides cash for replacement.

## Self-test

1. Give three causes of depreciation.
2. Give two reasons for charging depreciation.
3. An asset costs $16,000, residual value $1,000, life 5 years. Find the straight line charge.
4. The same asset is depreciated at 40% reducing balance. Find the Year 1 and Year 2 charges.
5. Bought 1 October for $12,000, straight line 25% of cost, year end 31 December, charged by months. Find the Year 1 charge.
6. Tools valued at $1,800 at the start, $650 bought, $1,950 at the end. Find depreciation.
7. A van cost $20,000, accumulated depreciation $14,500, sold for $4,000. Find the profit or loss.
8. Give the double entry for the annual depreciation charge.
9. A new machine costs $25,000; the dealer allows $6,000 for the old one. At what amount is the new machine recorded?
10. A business changes method. Are earlier years' profits restated?
11. Which method gives higher profit in the first year of an asset's life, straight line or reducing balance (with typical rates)?
12. Write the check that links the three parts of a schedule.

### Answers

1. Any three: wear and tear; passage of time; obsolescence; depletion; inadequacy.
2. Any two: to match cost with revenue (accruals); to give a fairer profit each year; to show a realistic carrying amount; prudence.
3. (16,000 − 1,000) ÷ 5 = $3,000.
4. Year 1: 6,400. Year 2: (16,000 − 6,400) × 40% = 3,840.
5. 12,000 × 25% × 3/12 = $750.
6. 1,800 + 650 − 1,950 = $500.
7. Carrying amount 5,500; loss of $1,500.
8. Dr Depreciation expense, Cr Provision for depreciation.
9. $25,000, the full price.
10. No; the new method applies from the date of change.
11. Straight line, because reducing balance charges more in the early years.
12. Carrying amount at end = cost at end − accumulated depreciation at end.
