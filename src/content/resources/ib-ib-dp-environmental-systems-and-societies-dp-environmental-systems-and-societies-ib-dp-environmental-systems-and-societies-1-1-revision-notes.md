## Condensed recall

**The three layers**
- Value → a principle someone thinks important.
- Perspective → how someone sees one issue.
- Worldview → the whole framework of beliefs and values.
- Environmental value system (EVS) → the part of a value system about nature.

**Two kinds of value**
- Instrumental: valued for what it gives people.
- Intrinsic: valued for itself.

**EVS as a system**
- Inputs: upbringing, religion, culture, education, media, peers, income, experience.
- Process: values.
- Outputs: decisions, votes, purchases, lifestyles, arguments.
- New inputs (a disaster, a documentary, a new job) can change the outputs.

**The spectrum**
- Ecocentric → deep ecologists (nature first); self-reliance soft ecologists (small local
  communities).
- Anthropocentric → environmental managers (regulate and manage for long-term human benefit).
- Technocentric → cornucopians (technology and markets overcome limits).
- Most people sit between positions and may shift from issue to issue.

**Worldviews beyond the spectrum**
- Market economies → price signals: carbon pricing, tradable permits, payments for ecosystem
  services.
- Planned economies → direct regulation and state projects.
- Indigenous worldviews → people as part of nature, duties across generations; varied, not one view.
- Rights of nature in law: Ecuador's constitution (2008); Whanganui River as a legal person in New
  Zealand (2017).

**Events that changed perspectives**
- Minamata disease identified (1956): pollution as a health threat.
- *Silent Spring* (1962): pesticides and chemical harm.
- Chipko movement (1970s): local defence of forests.
- Stockholm conference (1972): environment on the international agenda.
- Bhopal (1984): corporate responsibility.
- Rio Earth Summit (1992): sustainable development as a shared aim.
- Fukushima (2011): nuclear power reconsidered.
- Paris Agreement (2015): near-universal climate commitment.

## Method in steps: labelling a stakeholder

```
1. Find the stakeholder's own words or proposed action in the source.
2. Ask what they value most: nature itself, long-term human benefit,
   or growth enabled by technology.
3. Check for intrinsic or instrumental language.
4. Name the position (and sub-position if you can).
5. Quote the evidence and explain the link with "because".
6. If the view is mixed, place it between two positions and say why.
```

Mini example (invented): a fishing co-operative says, "We will set our own catch limits each season
so the fish are still here for our children, and sell only in the local market." The phrase "our own
catch limits" and the local focus suggest a self-reliance soft ecologist; the concern for future
human use also overlaps with an environmental manager. Placing it between the two, with both pieces
of evidence, is a stronger answer than a single label.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Value | A principle or quality a person considers important |
| Perspective | A person's view of a particular issue |
| Worldview | The framework of beliefs and values used to interpret the world |
| Environmental value system | A worldview or paradigm shaping how a person sees environmental issues |
| Instrumental value | Value as a means to human ends |
| Intrinsic value | Value in itself, regardless of usefulness |
| Ecocentric | Nature-centred; limits on human activity accepted |
| Anthropocentric | People-centred; environment managed for human benefit |
| Technocentric | Technology-centred; human ingenuity solves problems |
| Deep ecologist | Gives nature equal or greater value than humans |
| Cornucopian | Sees resources as effectively unlimited through innovation |
| Stakeholder | A person or group with an interest in an issue or decision |

## Exam traps

- **No evidence, no mark.** Always tie the label to words or proposals in the source.
- **Anthropocentric versus technocentric.** Ask: does the speaker accept limits and regulate
  (anthropocentric), or trust technology to remove the limit (technocentric)?
- **Ecocentric is not anti-human.** Soft ecologists put human communities at the centre of their
  plans, just small ones.
- **Events need the change.** "Chernobyl happened" earns nothing; "Chernobyl made many people see
  nuclear power as too risky" earns the point.
- **Mixed positions are allowed.** Saying a stakeholder is "between environmental manager and
  technocentrist because..." can be the best answer.
- **Discuss means weigh.** Two perspectives described separately with no comparison will not reach
  the top marks.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish a perspective from a worldview.
2. Give one example each of instrumental and intrinsic value for a wetland.
3. List four inputs into an EVS.
4. Which sub-position favours small, local, self-sufficient communities?
5. "Desalination will mean we never run short of water." Which EVS?
6. "We should tax water use so supplies last for our grandchildren." Which EVS?
7. Why might a person's EVS change over their lifetime?
8. What did Ecuador's 2008 constitution recognise?
9. State one change in perspective linked to *Silent Spring*.
10. Why is the 1972 Stockholm conference significant?
11. Give one market-based environmental policy.
12. What must you add to an EVS label to earn credit in an exam?

### Answers

1. A perspective is a view on one issue; a worldview is the overall framework of beliefs and values
   behind many perspectives.
2. Instrumental: water purification or flood storage for people. Intrinsic: the wetland's birds have
   a right to exist.
3. Any four: upbringing, religion, culture, education, media, peers, economic circumstances,
   personal experience.
4. Self-reliance soft ecologists.
5. Technocentric (cornucopian).
6. Anthropocentric (environmental manager).
7. New inputs such as education, experience of a disaster or a change in income alter how values are
   applied.
8. Rights of nature.
9. Growing public concern about pesticides and the wider effects of chemicals on wildlife.
10. It placed the environment on the international political agenda.
11. Carbon pricing, tradable permits or payments for ecosystem services.
12. Evidence from the source and an explanation of how it shows that value system.
