These notes condense **3.3.13.3 Action of anticancer drugs** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A-level only content, assessed in Unit 4 and in the synoptic Unit 5. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [anticancer drugs study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-13-3/); for exam-style questions with mark schemes, use the [anticancer drugs practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-13-3-practice/).

## The specification in one box

- Cisplatin is a **Pt(II) complex** used as an anticancer drug.
- It prevents **DNA replication** in cancer cells by a **ligand replacement reaction with DNA**: a bond forms between platinum and a **nitrogen atom**.
- Society must assess the **balance between benefits and adverse effects** of drugs such as cisplatin.
- Skills: explain why cisplatin prevents DNA replication; explain why such drugs can have adverse effects.
- Linked statement in 3.2.5.3: square planar complexes can show cis–trans isomerism; **cisplatin is the cis isomer**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning for this subtopic |
|---|---|
| Cisplatin | cis-[Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂], a neutral square planar Pt(II) complex |
| Ligand | A molecule or ion that donates a lone pair to a central metal ion |
| Co-ordinate bond | A covalent bond in which both electrons come from one atom; here N→Pt |
| Co-ordination number | Number of co-ordinate bonds to the metal ion; 4 in cisplatin |
| Square planar | Four ligands in one plane around the metal, bond angles 90° |
| Cis isomer | Two identical ligands next to each other (90° apart) |
| Trans isomer | Two identical ligands opposite each other (180° apart) |
| Ligand replacement | One ligand leaves and another takes its place; also called ligand substitution |
| DNA replication | Copying DNA before a cell divides |
| Adverse effect | A harmful, unwanted effect of a drug on the patient |

## Structure: facts to recall

- Formula **[Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂]**; Mr = 300.1 (Pt 195.1, N 14.0, H 1.0, Cl 35.5).
- Oxidation state of Pt: x + 0 + 2(−1) = 0, so **+2**.
- Overall charge **0**: the two Cl⁻ ligands cancel the 2+ of the metal.
- Shape **square planar**, all bond angles **90°**.
- The two Cl⁻ ligands are **cis** (adjacent). In transplatin they are trans (opposite).
- A tetrahedral [MA₂B₂] complex has **no** cis–trans isomers: all four positions are adjacent to one another.

## Mechanism chain: learn it in this order

1. A **chloride ligand is replaced** by a nitrogen atom on DNA (usually on a guanine base).
2. The nitrogen donates its **lone pair** to platinum, forming a **co-ordinate bond**.
3. The two chlorides are **cis**, so both are replaced and platinum binds to **two nearby nitrogen atoms**.
4. The DNA is **distorted**, so it **cannot be replicated**, so the cancer cell **cannot divide**.

Summary equation (G–N: = guanine nitrogen):

```
[Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] + 2 G–N:  →  [Pt(NH₃)₂(N–G)₂]²⁺ + 2Cl⁻
```

- NH₃ ligands **stay** on platinum; Cl⁻ ligands **leave**.
- Co-ordination number stays **4**; oxidation state stays **+2**. It is not a redox reaction.
- Wording note: the specification says the nitrogen is "on a protein"; in DNA it is on a base.

## Adverse effects: the one-line reason

Cisplatin cannot tell cancer cells from healthy cells, so it **binds to the DNA of healthy cells** and stops them dividing too.

- Side effects of cisplatin include **kidney damage** and **hearing loss**; patients often feel very sick.
- Harm is reduced by using the **lowest effective dose**, giving it **in cycles** so healthy tissue recovers, **combining** it with other treatments, and **monitoring** the patient.

## Benefit versus harm: building a judgement

| Weigh | Points |
|---|---|
| Benefit | Stops cancer cells dividing; tumours can shrink; can extend life or cure some cancers |
| Harm | Healthy cells damaged; kidney damage, hearing loss, sickness |
| Cost | Platinum is rare and expensive; side effects may need treating too |
| Who decides | Trials and regulators judge overall benefit; the informed patient consents |
| Judgement | Acceptable when the benefit for that patient outweighs the risk, with monitoring |

## Synoptic links

- **Transition metals (3.2.5):** ligands, co-ordinate bonds, co-ordination number, square planar shape, cis–trans isomerism.
- **Ligand substitution:** cisplatin is made by [PtCl₄]²⁻ + 2NH₃ → [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] + 2Cl⁻.
- **Amount of substance:** percentage of Pt by mass = 195.1 ÷ 300.1 × 100 = **65.0%**; moles from mass using Mr 300.1.
- **Amino acids and proteins (3.3.13.1 and 3.3.13.2):** nitrogen lone pairs as electron-pair donors appear again in amines and amino acids.

## Exam traps

- "Bonds through chlorine": wrong. Chlorides leave; nitrogen bonds.
- "Ionic bond" or "hydrogen bond" to DNA: wrong. It is a co-ordinate bond.
- "Tetrahedral" or "octahedral" cisplatin: wrong. Square planar.
- Ending at "binds to DNA": incomplete. Add "DNA cannot replicate, so the cell cannot divide".
- "It is poisonous" as the reason for side effects: too vague. Say it binds to the DNA of healthy cells.
- Saying cis and trans forms are structural isomers: they are stereoisomers.
- Giving cisplatin a 2+ charge: the complex itself is neutral; the platinum unit is 2+ only after both chlorides have gone.

## Quick self-test

1. Give the formula of cisplatin.
2. State the oxidation state of platinum in cisplatin and show how you get it.
3. Name the shape of cisplatin and give its bond angle.
4. Which ligands leave platinum when cisplatin reacts with DNA, and which stay?
5. Name the type of bond formed between platinum and DNA, and say which atom donates the electron pair.
6. Why is it important that the two chloride ligands are cis?
7. Complete the chain: the DNA is distorted, so...
8. Give the one-sentence reason why cisplatin has adverse effects.
9. Name two side effects of cisplatin.
10. Give two ways in which harm to patients can be reduced.
11. Calculate the percentage by mass of platinum in cisplatin.
12. Explain why a tetrahedral complex [MA₂B₂] has no cis–trans isomers.

### Answers

1. **[Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂]**, the cis isomer.
2. **+2**: x + 2(0) + 2(−1) = 0.
3. **Square planar, 90°.**
4. The two **Cl⁻** ligands leave; the two **NH₃** ligands stay.
5. A **co-ordinate (dative covalent) bond**; the **nitrogen** atom on DNA donates its lone pair.
6. Both can be replaced by nitrogen atoms that are **close together** on the DNA, so one platinum binds to two nearby sites.
7. ...it **cannot be replicated**, so the cancer cell **cannot divide**.
8. It also binds to the **DNA of healthy cells**, stopping them dividing.
9. Any two of: **kidney damage**, **hearing loss**, feeling very sick.
10. Any two of: lowest effective dose; treatment in cycles; combination with other treatments; monitoring the patient.
11. 195.1 ÷ 300.1 × 100 = **65.0%**.
12. In a tetrahedron every position is adjacent to every other, so there is **only one way** to arrange two A and two B ligands.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a correct mechanism but no consequence for cell division.
- Naming side effects without the chemical reason for them.
- Giving only benefits, or only harms, in an "evaluate" answer, with no judgement at the end.
- Losing the charge balance in the replacement equation by forgetting the two Cl⁻ ions released.
