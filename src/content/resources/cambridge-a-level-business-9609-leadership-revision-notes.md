These notes condense **section 7.3 Leadership** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Business 9609 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. The [leadership study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-9609-leadership/) explains each idea with a worked case; the [leadership practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-9609-leadership-practice/) test them. Leadership alongside HRM strategy is summarised in the [leadership and HRM strategy notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-leadership-and-human-resource-management-hrm-strategy-revision-notes/).

## Core distinctions

- **Management**: organising resources to meet plans.
- **Leadership**: setting direction and winning willing support, especially for change.
- **Management style**: how decisions are made (autocratic, democratic, laissez-faire, paternalistic).
- **Leadership theory**: an explanation of what makes leaders effective.

## 7.3.1 Purpose, roles, qualities

**Purpose:** direction, motivation, decisions under uncertainty, leading change.

| Role | Scope | Source of influence |
|---|---|---|
| Directors | Whole organisation; strategy | Formal authority; accountable to shareholders |
| Managers | Department or site | Formal authority over resources and staff |
| Supervisors | Front-line team; daily work | Authority over tasks; example set |
| Worker representatives | Colleagues, in dealings with management | Trust of colleagues, not formal authority |

**Qualities:** vision, integrity, decisiveness, communication, empathy, resilience, delegation. Match to need:

| Situation | Qualities that matter most |
|---|---|
| Crisis | Decisiveness, calm |
| Major change | Vision, communication |
| Expert team | Delegation, listening |
| Lost trust | Integrity, openness |

## 7.3.2 The five theory types

| Theory | Key claim | Example to quote | Main limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trait | Leaders share certain characteristics; born, not made | Confidence, drive, integrity | No agreed list; ignores situation and training |
| Behavioural | What leaders do matters; can be learned | Blake and Mouton grid: concern for task and for people, each 1 to 9 | Implies one best behaviour |
| Contingency | Best approach depends on the situation | Fiedler: match leader to situation (leader–member relations, task structure, position power) | Situations change; leaders may not adapt |
| Power and influence | Leaders act through sources of power | French and Raven's five bases | Some power wins compliance, not commitment |
| Transformational | Leader raises followers' commitment to a shared vision | Bass's four elements | Depends on one person; vision may be wrong |

**Grid positions (Blake and Mouton):** 9,1 authority-compliance (task only); 1,9 country club (people only); 1,1 impoverished; 5,5 middle of the road; 9,9 team management.

**Five bases of power:**

- Legitimate: the position.
- Reward: control of pay, promotion or praise.
- Coercive: ability to punish.
- Expert: respected knowledge.
- Referent: admiration and liking.

Compliance comes from reward and coercive power; commitment comes from expert and referent power.

**Transformational (Bass):** idealised influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, individualised consideration.
**Transactional:** rewards and penalties exchanged for meeting targets; suits stable, routine work.

**Spotting the theory in a case:**

- "Natural leader": trait.
- "Trained to support staff better": behavioural.
- "What worked there failed here": contingency.
- "Obeyed because he controls bonuses": power (reward).
- "Inspired staff to believe in a new future": transformational.

## 7.3.3 Emotional intelligence (Goleman)

| Competency | About | Sign of weakness in a case |
|---|---|---|
| Self-awareness | Recognising own emotions and their effect | Leader unaware that their manner upsets staff |
| Self-management | Controlling own emotions and impulses | Loses temper under pressure |
| Social awareness | Recognising others' feelings and needs | Misses signs of stress or resentment |
| Social skills | Managing relationships and conflict | Cannot settle disputes or win people over |

**Benefits:** better handling of conflict and change; lower absence and turnover; stronger cooperation.
**Limits:** hard to measure; no substitute for strategic or technical judgement; can be used manipulatively.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Leadership | Influencing people to work willingly towards objectives |
| Trait theory | Effective leaders share inborn characteristics |
| Behavioural theory | Effective leadership depends on learnable behaviour |
| Contingency theory | The effective approach depends on the situation |
| Legitimate power | Power from a formal position |
| Referent power | Power from being admired |
| Transformational leadership | Inspiring followers to commit to a shared vision |
| Transactional leadership | Exchanging rewards for performance |
| Emotional intelligence | Ability to recognise and manage your own and others' emotions |

## Evaluation prompts

- "It depends on the situation": the urgency, the skills of the staff and how structured the task is.
- "It depends on the source of power": compliance may be enough for safety rules, but change needs commitment.
- "It depends on one person": transformational success may not survive the leader's departure, so ask who will continue the vision.
- "It depends on evidence": does the case show the cause, or could pay, workload or competition explain the problem instead?

## Exam traps

- Confusing styles with theories.
- Calling a leader transformational without evidence of vision and changed commitment.
- Listing every EQ competency instead of applying the one the case shows.
- Ignoring the worker representative's lack of formal authority.
- Writing a judgement that does not say what success depends on.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish leadership from management.
2. Name the four leadership roles in the syllabus.
3. Which theory implies that leaders can be trained?
4. What does a 9,9 position on the Blake and Mouton grid mean?
5. Give the three situational factors in Fiedler's model.
6. Which two bases of power tend to win commitment rather than compliance?
7. Name Bass's four elements of transformational leadership.
8. When might transactional leadership be enough?
9. Which EQ competency is lacking in a manager who never notices staff stress?
10. Give one limitation of emotional intelligence as a guide to choosing leaders.
11. Which qualities matter most for a leader rebuilding trust?
12. Where does a worker representative's influence come from?

### Answers

1. Management organises resources to meet plans; leadership sets direction and wins willing support, especially for change.
2. Directors, managers, supervisors, worker representatives.
3. Behavioural theory.
4. High concern for both task and people ("team management").
5. Leader–member relations, task structure, position power.
6. Expert and referent.
7. Idealised influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, individualised consideration.
8. In stable, routine work where clear targets and rewards are enough to keep performance high.
9. Social awareness.
10. It is hard to measure reliably (or: it does not replace strategic judgement).
11. Integrity and openness.
12. From the trust of colleagues, not from formal authority.
