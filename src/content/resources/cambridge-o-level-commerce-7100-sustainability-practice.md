## Section A

**1.** State the purpose of sustainability in the supply chain. **[2]**

**2.** State **three** actions a business can take to improve sustainability. **[3]**

**3.** State what is meant by **water stewardship**. **[1]**

**4.** State **one** cost to a business of using sustainable materials. **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** A business ships goods in 25 deliveries a week. Combining orders reduces this to 18 deliveries. Each delivery uses 60 litres of fuel.

**(a)** Calculate the fuel used before and after. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage reduction in fuel use. **[2]**

**6.** A bottled-water company cuts the plastic in each bottle from 20 g to 16 g. It makes 2,000,000 bottles a month. Calculate the plastic saved in a month, in tonnes. (1 tonne = 1,000,000 g.) **[3]**

**7.** A factory produces 50 tonnes of waste a month. It recycles 35 tonnes. Calculate the percentage recycled and the amount that goes to landfill. **[3]**

**8.** An online fashion shop sends 20,000 orders a month, and 9% are returned. Better size guides cut returns to 6%. Each return costs $8.

**(a)** Calculate the number of returns before and after. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the monthly saving. **[2]**

**9.** Explain **two** ways in which reducing packaging can help a business and the environment. **[4]**

**10.** A sports shop offers a digital resale marketplace for used trainers. Explain how this improves sustainability. **[3]**

**11.** Explain why a business that sells eco-friendly products might still find it difficult to make a profit. **[3]**

**12.** Explain **two** benefits to a business of using sustainable logistics. **[4]**

**13.** Evaluate whether a clothing manufacturer should switch to sustainable materials. **[5]**

**14.** Name the sustainability action shown in each case. **[3]**

**(a)** A shop repairs broken zips on its bags free of charge.

**(b)** A bakery sends fewer, fuller delivery vans.

**(c)** A website where customers sell their old phones to other customers.

## Answers

**1.** To make commercial processes eco-friendly [1] and environmentally responsible for future generations [1].

**2.** Any three from: using sustainable materials; implementing sustainable logistics; reducing packaging; selling eco-friendly products; recycling and reducing waste; reducing returns and exchanges; offering digital resale marketplaces; offering free repair services; water stewardship [1 each].

**3.** Using water carefully and responsibly in production and sourcing, and protecting local water supplies [1].

**4.** Any one from: the materials may cost more; they may be harder to find; the production process may need new equipment [1].

**5.** **(a)** Before: 25 × 60 = 1,500 litres [1]. After: 18 × 60 = 1,080 litres [1].

**(b)** Reduction = 1,500 − 1,080 = 420 litres [1]. 420 ÷ 1,500 × 100 = 28% [1].

**6.** Saving per bottle = 20 − 16 = 4 g [1]. Total = 4 × 2,000,000 = 8,000,000 g [1] = 8 tonnes [1].

**7.** 35 ÷ 50 × 100 [1] = 70% [1]. Landfill = 50 − 35 = 15 tonnes [1].

**8.** **(a)** Before: 9% × 20,000 = 1,800 [1]. After: 6% × 20,000 = 1,200 [1].

**(b)** Fewer returns = 600 [1]. Saving = 600 × 8 = $4,800 [1].

**9.** Less packaging means less waste going to landfill or needing to be recycled [1], which protects the environment [1]. It lowers the cost of materials and may reduce transport cost because items are lighter [1], which increases profit [1].

**10.** Customers can sell their used trainers to others [1], so the trainers stay in use for longer [1] and fewer new pairs need to be made, which saves resources and reduces waste [1].

**11.** Eco-friendly products may cost more to develop or make [1], so the price may be higher [1]. If customers will not pay the higher price, sales and profit may fall [1].

**12.** Any two developed: lower fuel costs from fuller loads and shorter routes [1], which reduces expenses [1]; lower emissions [1], which helps the environment and the firm's reputation [1]; may meet legal requirements on emissions [1] and avoid fines [1]. Maximum 4.

**13.** Any five from: benefits: protects resources and reduces pollution [1]; attracts customers who prefer sustainable clothing [1]; improves the firm's reputation [1]; may meet legal requirements [1]; costs: sustainable materials may cost more [1]; may need new machines or suppliers [1]; price rises could reduce sales [1]; a justified decision, for example that switching is worthwhile in the long run if customers value it [1]. Maximum 5.

**14.** **(a)** Offering free repair services [1]. **(b)** Implementing sustainable logistics [1]. **(c)** Offering a digital resale marketplace [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 5(b): dividing by the new fuel use instead of the original.
- Question 6: leaving the answer in grams or dividing by the wrong number.
- Question 7: giving the amount to landfill without the percentage.
- Question 8: working out 9% and 6% of the wrong total.
- Question 10: not saying why used goods reduce new production.
- Question 13: no judgement or no mention of cost.

## Quick reference for checking your answers

| Item | Key point |

| Purpose | Eco-friendly, for future generations |
| Actions | Nine in the syllabus |
