These questions are for Cambridge O Level Economics 2281, syllabus for examination in 2026, statements 4.3.7 to 4.3.9 on fiscal policy: the definition, measures including budget deficit and surplus calculations, and effects on macroeconomic aims. Every country and figure is invented. ## Questions

**1.** Define the term fiscal policy. **[2]**

**2.** Country P's government budget for one year is shown (invented figures, million units).

| Revenue | | Spending | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Income tax | 640 | Health | 400 |
| Sales tax | 480 | Education | 380 |
| Company tax | 210 | Welfare | 360 |
| Other | 90 | Transport | 250 |
| | | Defence | 150 |
| | | Other | 110 |

**(a)** Calculate total government spending. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the budget balance and say which of the two it is. **[2]**
**(c)** State one way the government could finance the balance. **[1]**

**3.** State whether each measure is expansionary or contractionary, and explain its effect on the budget balance.

**(a)** A cut in the rate of income tax. **[2]**
**(b)** A cut in spending on public building projects. **[2]**

**4.** Country R raises its sales tax rate from 8% to 11%. Taxable spending is 2,400 million units.

**(a)** Calculate the extra revenue if taxable spending does not change. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the extra revenue might be lower than your answer to (a). **[2]**

**5.** Country S has revenue of 900 and spending of 855 (million units). It then raises spending by 70 with revenue unchanged. GDP is 2,500.

**(a)** Calculate the new budget balance. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the balance as a percentage of GDP. **[2]**

**6.** Explain how higher government spending on training programmes might reduce unemployment. **[4]**

**7.** Explain how contractionary fiscal policy might reduce inflation, and state one cost of using it. **[5]**

**8.** Country T has a deficit on the current account of its balance of payments. Explain how fiscal policy might reduce it, and explain why the policy might not work. **[6]**

**9.** Country U charges no tax on the first 10,000 units of income and 20% on income above 10,000. Three workers earn 8,000, 30,000 and 50,000.

**(a)** Calculate the average tax rate for each worker. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how the government could use its spending to help redistribute income. **[3]**

**10.** Country V has unemployment of 12%, very slow growth and a deficit of 120 million units (3% of GDP of 4,000). The government plans to spend 80 more on rural roads and to cut company tax, reducing revenue by 20.

**(a)** Calculate the new deficit, and as a percentage of GDP. **[2]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether the plan is likely to help Country V. **[6]**

**11.** Country W has revenue of 1,100 and spending of 1,260 (million units) and high inflation. The government raises its sales tax rate from 12% to 15% (taxable spending 2,000) and cuts spending by 50.

**(a)** Calculate the new budget balance, assuming taxable spending is unchanged. **[3]**
**(b)** Discuss whether this package is a good way to meet the government's macroeconomic aims. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** Deliberate changes by the government to its spending [1] and to its taxes [1], made to steer the economy towards its macroeconomic aims. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition naming only spending or only taxes, or bringing in interest rates, may miss what the question is looking for.

**2. (a)** 400 + 380 + 360 + 250 + 150 + 110 [1] = **1,650 million units** [1]
**(b)** Revenue = 640 + 480 + 210 + 90 = 1,420 [1]. Balance = 1,420 − 1,650 = −230, a **deficit of 230 million units** [1]
**(c)** Borrowing (for example selling government bonds), which builds up the national debt [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give the word as well as the number. A bare minus sign may not show that you know what a deficit is.

**3. (a)** Expansionary [1]. Less tax is collected, so revenue falls and the balance moves towards deficit [1]
**(b)** Contractionary [1]. Spending falls, so the balance moves towards surplus [1]
*Examiner insight:* Both the label and the budget effect are asked for, so a one-word answer is unlikely to gain full credit.

**4. (a)** Old tax = 8% × 2,400 = 192 [1]. New tax = 11% × 2,400 = 264 [1]. Extra revenue = **72 million units** [1]
**(b)** Higher prices may lead people to buy less, so taxable spending falls [1]. The tax is then charged on a smaller base. For example, if the base fell 5% to 2,280, the extra revenue would be about 58.8 [1]
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) rewards the idea that the base can change. Saying only that "people may avoid tax" is a weaker explanation.

**5. (a)** New spending = 855 + 70 = 925 [1]. Balance = 900 − 925 = **−25, a deficit of 25 million units** [1]
**(b)** 25 ÷ 2,500 × 100 [1] = **1.0% of GDP** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The starting position was a surplus of 45. Check the sign at the end of the working to see that a surplus has become a deficit.

**6.** Training raises the skills of unemployed workers [1]. Their skills then match the jobs that firms need to fill [1]. Firms can fill vacancies more easily, so more of the unemployed find work [1]. This works best for structural unemployment, but it takes time and costs the government money [1]
**[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A chain with each link stated tends to earn more than a list of effects. Finish by connecting back to unemployment.

**7.** Higher taxes or lower spending reduce households' spending power [1]. Firms sell less, so there is less pressure to raise prices [1]. The rate of inflation slows (prices still rise, but more slowly) [1]. Cost: firms may cut output [1], so unemployment may rise [1]
**[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Prices fall" is a common slip. Inflation slowing means prices rise more slowly.

**8.** Contractionary policy lowers incomes [1], so households buy fewer imports and the deficit narrows [1]. Subsidies or tax relief for exporters cut their costs and help them sell abroad [1]. It might not work because cutting spending raises unemployment and conflicts with another aim [1]; exporters may still struggle if overseas demand is weak [1]; subsidies cost money, widening the budget deficit [1]
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Part of the credit is for the reasons it may fail. Make these specific to the balance of payments rather than saying "it depends".

**9. (a)** Worker 1: tax = 0 so **0%** [1]. Worker 2: 20% × (30,000 − 10,000) = 4,000, so 4,000 ÷ 30,000 = **13.3%** [1]. Worker 3: 20% × 40,000 = 8,000, so 8,000 ÷ 50,000 = **16%** [1]
**(b)** Spend on welfare payments to low-income households [1], on health and education available to all or aimed at poorer areas [1], which raises their living standards relative to richer households [1]
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the working for each average rate. Point out that richer workers pay a higher share, which is what makes the tax progressive.

**10. (a)** New deficit = 120 + 80 + 20 = **220 million units** [1]. 220 ÷ 4,000 × 100 = **5.5% of GDP** [1]
**(b)** For: road building creates jobs directly and those workers spend wages [1]. Company tax cuts may encourage firms to invest and hire [1]. With 12% unemployment and slow growth there is spare capacity, so inflation risk is small [1]. Against: the deficit rises from 3% to 5.5% of GDP, so more borrowing and future debt costs [1]. Effects take time to appear and firms may save the tax cut [1]. Judgement: the plan is likely to help if the roads are well chosen and the borrowing is manageable, because unemployment is the bigger problem [1]
**[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation is helped by using the figures given, such as the rise in the deficit to 5.5% of GDP, and by ending with a reasoned judgement.

**11. (a)** Extra sales tax = 3% × 2,000 = 60 [1]. New revenue = 1,160 and new spending = 1,210 [1]. Balance = **−50, a deficit of 50 million units** [1]
**(b)** Higher sales tax and lower spending reduce spending power [1], which should slow inflation [1]. The deficit falls from 160 to 50, so less borrowing [1]. However, a sales tax raises prices directly, so inflation may rise at first [1]. Spending cuts could increase unemployment, a conflict between aims [1]. Poorer households spend more of their income on goods, so a sales tax can widen inequality [1]. Judgement: it is a reasonable package if the cuts avoid vital services and the government watches jobs [1]
**[10]**
*Examiner insight:* The strongest answers discuss at least two aims (here prices, jobs and the deficit) and show how they conflict, rather than treating each in isolation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a balance with no word "deficit" or "surplus".
- Subtracting spending from revenue the wrong way round.
- Calculating a tax change on the wrong base, or not applying the new rate to the whole base.
- Saying a tax cut always reduces revenue by the full amount.
- Saying inflation "falls" when prices are still rising.
- Using interest rates or the money supply in a fiscal question.
- Listing effects without a chain to the named aim.
- Evaluation that ignores the figures in the question and ends without a judgement.
