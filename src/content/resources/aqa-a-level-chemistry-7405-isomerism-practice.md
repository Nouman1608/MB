These questions cover section **3.3.1.3 Isomerism** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Learn the ideas first in the [Isomerism study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-isomerism/) and the [Introduction to organic chemistry revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-introduction-to-organic-chemistry-revision-notes/). Give structures as structural formulas, and for E–Z questions state which groups are on the same or opposite sides.

## Questions

**1.** Define the terms structural isomer and stereoisomer. **[2]**

**2.** Draw the structural formula and give the name of each of the three isomers of C₅H₁₂. **[3]**

**3.** State the type of structural isomerism shown by each pair.

**(a)** propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol **[1]**

**(b)** butane and 2-methylpropane **[1]**

**(c)** propanal and propanone **[1]**

**4.** Give the structural formulas and names of all four alcohols with molecular formula C₄H₁₀O. **[4]**

**5.** Explain why 2-methylpropane has a lower boiling point than butane. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why but-2-ene shows E–Z isomerism. **[3]**

**7.** Explain why but-1-ene does not show E–Z isomerism. **[1]**

**8.** State which of these alkenes show E–Z isomerism: pent-1-ene, pent-2-ene, 2-methylbut-2-ene, 1,2-dibromoethene. **[2]**

**9.** The molecule BrCH=CHCH₃ is 1-bromopropene.

**(a)** Give the higher-priority group on each carbon of the double bond and explain how you decided. **[2]**

**(b)** Draw the Z isomer, describing the positions of the Br and CH₃ groups. **[1]**

**10.** For 1-bromo-1-chloropropene, BrClC=CHCH₃, state the higher-priority group on each carbon and describe the E isomer. **[3]**

**11.** Name all the alkenes with molecular formula C₄H₈, including E–Z isomers. **[4]**

**12.** For 3-methylpent-2-ene, CH₃CH=C(CH₃)CH₂CH₃, deduce the priority of the two groups on carbon 3, and describe the Z isomer. **[3]**

**13.** Draw the structural formulas of two functional group isomers with molecular formula C₃H₆O and name the functional group in each. **[2]**

**14.** Explain why cis–trans isomerism is described as a special case of E–Z isomerism. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Structural isomers have the same molecular formula but different structural formulas (atoms joined in a different order) [1]; stereoisomers have the same structural formula but a different arrangement of atoms in space [1]. **[2]**

**2.** pentane, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ [1]; 2-methylbutane, CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ [1]; 2,2-dimethylpropane, (CH₃)₄C [1]. **[3]**

**3. (a)** Position isomers [1]

**(b)** Chain isomers [1]

**(c)** Functional group isomers [1]. **[3]**

**4.** butan-1-ol, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH [1]; butan-2-ol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ [1]; 2-methylpropan-1-ol, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂OH [1]; 2-methylpropan-2-ol, (CH₃)₃COH [1]. **[4]**

**5.** 2-methylpropane is branched and more compact, so its molecules have less surface contact [1]; the London forces between molecules are weaker, so less energy is needed to separate them [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The C=C bond contains a π bond that prevents rotation about the double bond [1]; each carbon of the double bond has two different groups (CH₃ and H) [1]; so the CH₃ groups can be on the same side (Z) or on opposite sides (E) and the two forms cannot interconvert [1]. **[3]**

**7.** One carbon of the double bond has two identical groups (two hydrogen atoms) [1]. **[1]**

**8.** pent-2-ene [1] and 1,2-dibromoethene [1]. pent-1-ene (a CH₂ end) and 2-methylbut-2-ene (two CH₃ groups on one carbon) do not. **[2]**

**9. (a)** On the first carbon, Br outranks H because its atomic number is higher; on the second carbon, CH₃ outranks H (carbon has a higher atomic number than hydrogen) [1 for each carbon, 2 in total]

**(b)** Br and CH₃ on the same side of the double bond [1]. **[3]**

**10.** On the first carbon, Br has a higher atomic number than Cl, so Br is higher priority [1]; on the second carbon, CH₃ is higher than H [1]; in the E isomer, Br and CH₃ are on opposite sides of the double bond [1]. **[3]**

**11.** but-1-ene [1]; (Z)-but-2-ene [1]; (E)-but-2-ene [1]; 2-methylpropene [1]. **[4]**

**12.** On carbon 3 the groups are CH₃ and CH₂CH₃ [1]; both start with carbon, so compare what is attached: CH₃ has (H, H, H) and CH₂CH₃ has (C, H, H), so ethyl has the higher priority [1]; in the Z isomer, the CH₃ on carbon 2 (higher priority on that carbon) and the ethyl group are on the same side [1]. **[3]**

**13.** propanal, CH₃CH₂CHO, an aldehyde [1]; propanone, CH₃COCH₃, a ketone [1]. **[2]**

**14.** In cis–trans isomerism, each carbon of the double bond has one hydrogen and one other group; cis means the two non-hydrogen groups are on the same side and trans means opposite [1]; this is the same as Z and E when those groups are the higher-priority groups, but E–Z can also be used when there are three or four different groups [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The CIP priority depends on atomic number at the first point of difference, not on the size of the group.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not saying that each carbon of the C=C needs two different groups.
- Forgetting the restricted rotation of the π bond.
- Using the size of a group instead of atomic number for priority.
- Missing an isomer, or counting the same molecule twice.
- Confusing stereoisomers and structural isomers.
