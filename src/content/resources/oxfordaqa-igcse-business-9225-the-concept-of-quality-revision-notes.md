Condensed recall for section 3.3.3 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-the-concept-of-quality/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- **Customer expectations** of appropriate quality for goods and services.
- How businesses **identify and measure** quality problems, and the **consequences of poor quality**.
- Methods of **maintaining consistent quality**: **total quality management (TQM)** and its advantages.
- **Costs and benefits** of maintaining quality.
- Quality issues as businesses **grow**, especially with **outsourcing and franchising**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Quality | How well a product meets the needs and expectations of the customer |
| Defect rate | Faulty items as a percentage of items made |
| Quality control | Inspecting products and rejecting faulty ones |
| Quality assurance | Setting standards at every stage to prevent faults |
| TQM | Every employee is responsible for quality; get it right first time |
| Rework | Putting right a faulty product |
| Mystery shopper | A researcher who poses as a customer to rate service |

## Customer expectations

- Quality depends on **what the customer expects for the price**, not on luxury.
- **Goods:** materials, build, durability, safety, as described.
- **Services:** reliability, speed, knowledge, friendliness, handling problems.
- Too little quality loses customers; too much may push prices above what the market will pay.

## Measuring quality

| Measure | Example |
|---|---|
| Defect rate | 450 faulty out of 15,000 = 3% |
| Complaint rate | 120 complaints from 2,400 customers = 5% |
| Returns and refunds | Number of items sent back |
| Customer feedback | Surveys, reviews, ratings |
| Mystery shoppers | Rate service in shops or restaurants |
| Repeat purchases | Customers who come back |

Formula: defect rate (%) = faulty items / total items x 100.

## Consequences of poor quality

- Lost sales and customers.
- Damaged reputation.
- Costs of refunds, repairs, replacements and wasted materials.
- Fines or compensation if goods are unsafe.
- Lower staff morale.
- Shops may refuse to stock the product.

Example: 3% of 180,000 units faulty = 5,400; at $18 each, the cost is $97,200 a year.

## TQM

- **Everyone** is responsible for quality, in every department.
- Aim: **get it right first time**, prevent faults rather than find them at the end.
- Includes training, staff involvement, checks at every stage and continuous improvement.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Fewer faults and lower waste | High training and set-up costs |
| Better reputation, more repeat business | Slow to show results |
| Higher motivation | Staff may resist change |
| Less need for inspectors | Hard with high staff turnover |

## Costs and benefits of quality

| Costs | Benefits |
|---|---|
| Better materials | Fewer returns and refunds |
| Training and systems | Better reputation |
| Inspection equipment | Loyal customers |
| Slower production | Can charge higher prices |

Test: do the savings from fewer faults exceed the cost of the scheme? Example: saving $64,800 less scheme cost $40,000 = net benefit $24,800 a year.

## Quality as a business grows

- **Outsourcing:** less control. Fix standards in the contract, inspect samples, choose reliable partners. Customers blame the brand if the supplier fails.
- **Franchising:** one poor outlet harms the whole chain. Needs training, standards and inspections.
- **General growth:** more staff and sites make consistent training and communication harder.

## Exam traps

- Quality does **not** mean expensive.
- TQM is not just inspection at the end. It is everyone's responsibility.
- Explain the consequences, such as how returns cost money and reputation.
- Quality for services depends on staff behaviour.
- Include costs as well as benefits of quality.
- Use a figure or detail from the case.

## Self-test

1. Define quality.
2. State two ways of measuring quality.
3. A shop sells 2,000 items and 80 are returned as faulty. Calculate the return rate.
4. Give two consequences of poor quality.
5. Distinguish between quality control and TQM.
6. State two advantages of TQM.
7. Give one disadvantage of TQM.
8. Give one cost of maintaining high quality.
9. A firm makes 50,000 items a year with a 4% defect rate, and each faulty item costs $10. Calculate the annual cost of defects.
10. Explain one quality problem that might arise when a business uses franchising.
11. Explain one quality problem that might arise when a business outsources production.
12. Why might a restaurant train all staff to deal with complaints?

### Answers

1. How well a good or service meets the needs and expectations of the customer.
2. Any two: defect rate; returns; complaints; customer surveys; mystery shoppers; repeat purchases.
3. 80 / 2,000 x 100 = **4%**.
4. Any two: lost sales; damaged reputation; extra costs of refunds and repairs; legal action; lower staff morale.
5. Quality control checks products and rejects faulty ones; TQM makes every employee responsible for preventing faults.
6. Any two: fewer faults; lower costs; better reputation; higher motivation; less inspection.
7. For example, high training costs, or it takes time to show results.
8. For example, better materials or staff training.
9. 4% x 50,000 = 2,000 faulty items; 2,000 x $10 = **$20,000**.
10. A franchisee may not follow standards, so customers may think the whole chain is poor.
11. The supplier may cut corners, and the business has less control, but customers still blame the brand.
12. Good handling of complaints keeps customers, protects reputation and may reduce lost sales.
