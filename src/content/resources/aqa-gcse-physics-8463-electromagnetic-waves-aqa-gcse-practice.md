These questions cover section **4.6.2 Electromagnetic waves** of the AQA GCSE Physics (8463) specification,
examined on Paper 2 at both tiers. Questions 8, 10 and 11 test content marked (HT only) and are labelled
**Higher tier only**. Take the speed of electromagnetic waves as 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s. All dose figures are invented
for practice. A calculator is allowed throughout.

Learn the content first in the
[electromagnetic waves study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-electromagnetic-waves-aqa-gcse/) and the
[electromagnetic waves revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-electromagnetic-waves-aqa-gcse-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Name the group of electromagnetic waves with the longest wavelength. **[1]**

**2.** Compare the speeds of X-rays and radio waves in a vacuum. **[1]**

**3.** Name the only group of electromagnetic waves that human eyes can detect. **[1]**

**4.** X-rays used in a hospital have a frequency of 1.5 × 10¹⁸ Hz. Calculate their wavelength. **[3]**

**5.** Give one use of infrared and one use of ultraviolet. **[2]**

**6.** State two factors that the harm caused by X-rays to body tissue depends on. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A ray of light passes from air into a rectangular glass block at an angle to the normal, and out of the
opposite side.

**(a)** Describe how the ray's direction changes as it enters the glass and as it leaves it. **[2]**
**(b)** A second ray enters the block along the normal. State what happens to its direction. **[1]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) Explain how an X-ray image of a broken arm is formed, using the idea that different
substances absorb and transmit electromagnetic waves differently. **[3]**

**9.** The table shows invented doses from some sources of radiation.

| Source | Dose (mSv) |
|---|---|
| Return flight between continents (cosmic rays) | 0.080 |
| Chest X-ray | 0.020 |
| X-ray of a hand | 0.0010 |
| Yearly background radiation | 2.4 |

**(a)** Calculate how many chest X-rays give the same dose as one return flight. **[2]**
**(b)** Convert the yearly background dose into sieverts. **[1]**
**(c)** A patient is worried about an X-ray of her hand. Use the data to draw a conclusion about the risk.
**[2]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) Explain why each type of electromagnetic wave is suitable for its use.

**(a)** Microwaves for cooking food. **[2]**
**(b)** Gamma rays for treating cancer. **[2]**
**(c)** Infrared for cameras that show people in the dark. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) A local radio station transmits at a frequency of 90 MHz.

**(a)** State what produces the radio waves in the transmitter. **[1]**
**(b)** The radio waves are absorbed by a receiver's aerial. State the frequency of the current produced in
the aerial. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the wavelength of the radio waves. **[2]**

**12.** An object stands on the principal axis of a convex lens, further from the lens than the principal
focus.

**(a)** Describe how to construct a ray diagram to find the image. **[3]**
**(b)** The object is 12 mm tall and the image is 3.6 cm tall. Calculate the magnification. **[2]**
**(c)** The image can be shown on a screen. State the type of image and explain your answer. **[1]**

**13.** A stage is lit only by white light that has passed through a blue filter. A performer wears a white
shirt and red trousers. State and explain the colour of the shirt and of the trousers. **[4]**

**14.** This question is about the colour of objects.

**(a)** Explain why a leaf looks green in white light. **[2]**
**(b)** A frosted bathroom window lets light through, but you cannot see a clear image through it. State
whether it is transparent or translucent. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Radio waves [1]

**2.** They travel at the same speed [1]

**3.** Visible light [1]

**4.** λ = v / f [1]; = 3.0 × 10⁸ / 1.5 × 10¹⁸ [1]; = **2.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ m** [1]

**5.** Infrared: any one of electrical heaters, cooking food, infrared cameras [1]; ultraviolet: energy
efficient lamps or sun tanning [1]

**6.** The type of radiation [1]; the size of the dose [1]

**7. (a)** Entering the glass, it bends towards the normal [1]; leaving the glass, it bends away from the
normal [1]
**(b)** It does not change direction [1]

**8.** X-rays are transmitted through soft tissue such as skin and muscle [1]; they are absorbed by bone [1];
so the detector behind the arm receives X-rays except where bone blocks them, and a break shows as a line
where X-rays pass through the gap [1]

**9. (a)** 0.080 / 0.020 [1]; = **4** chest X-rays [1]
**(b)** 2.4 / 1000 = **0.0024 Sv** [1]
**(c)** The hand X-ray dose is 0.0010 mSv, much smaller than one return flight (80 times smaller) and a tiny
fraction of yearly background (1/2400) [1]; so the extra risk is very small and the benefit of a diagnosis
outweighs it [1]

**10. (a)** Microwaves are absorbed by water molecules in food [1]; transferring energy to the food's thermal
store, so it heats up [1]
**(b)** Gamma rays penetrate the body to reach a tumour [1]; they are ionising, with enough energy to kill
cancer cells [1]
**(c)** Warm objects such as people emit infrared radiation [1]; the camera detects the infrared, so it can
form an image without visible light [1]

**11. (a)** Oscillations in an electrical circuit (an alternating current) [1]
**(b)** **90 MHz** (the same frequency as the radio wave) [1]
**(c)** λ = 3.0 × 10⁸ / 90 × 10⁶ [1]; = **3.3 m** [1]

**12. (a)** From the top of the object, draw a ray parallel to the principal axis that refracts through the
principal focus on the far side [1]; draw a second ray from the top of the object straight through the
centre of the lens [1]; the image's top is where the two rays cross, and the image is drawn from there to
the axis [1]
**(b)** 3.6 cm = 36 mm; magnification = 36 / 12 [1]; = **3.0** [1]
**(c)** Real, because the light rays actually meet where the image forms [1]
*Examiner insight:* Magnification has no unit; "3.0 cm" loses the answer mark.

**13.** Only blue light reaches the stage [1]; the white shirt reflects all colours, so it reflects the blue
light and looks blue [1]; the red trousers reflect only red light and absorb other colours [1]; no red light
reaches them, so they absorb the blue and reflect nothing, looking black [1]

**14. (a)** The leaf reflects green wavelengths most strongly [1]; it absorbs the other wavelengths of white
light [1]
**(b)** Translucent [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving MHz unconverted, or mishandling powers of ten in standard form.
- Saying refracted light bends away from the normal on entering glass.
- Giving a use without the property that makes it suitable.
- "Red trousers look purple in blue light": they look black.
- Magnification answers with units, or heights in different units.
- Dose comparisons without a conclusion about risk.
