## Section A: short-answer questions

Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority in your answer. **[6]**

**9.**

> **Qur'an 3:18-19**

## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for developing it (question 5: up to four marks for a developed explanation; question 6: two reasons, each identified and developed). The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** Testimony or witness; the declaration of faith [1].

**2.** The Tashahhud (the sitting recitation) [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: when becoming a Muslim; in the Azaan and Iqamah; in each prayer; when a baby is born; when someone is dying; in Friday sermons; in times of hardship.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: Tawhid (the oneness of Allah); Risalah (the messengership of Muhammad (pbuh)); rejection of false gods; acceptance of the Qur'an and Sunnah as guidance.

**5.** The first half is "there is no god but Allah" [1], which states Tawhid, the belief that Allah alone is worshipped [1]. It begins by rejecting all false gods and then affirms Allah [1], so a Muslim should not give worship, trust or obedience to anything equal to Allah [1].

**6.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and developed [1].

- **It accepts the final messenger** [1]: Muslims believe Allah's guidance came to humanity through Muhammad (pbuh) [1].
- **It shows how to worship** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) taught how to pray, fast and perform Hajj, so without accepting him a person would not know how to worship correctly [1].
- **It links to the Qur'an** [1]: the Qur'an was revealed through him, and accepting him means accepting its authority [1].
- **It gives a model** [1]: his Sunnah shows how to live Islam [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **It is the entry to Islam** [1]: a person becomes a Muslim by sincerely declaring it [1].
- **It is the foundation of the other pillars** [1]: prayer, fasting, Zakah and Hajj only make sense when offered to Allah as taught by His messenger [1].
- **It contains the key beliefs** [1]: it expresses Tawhid and Risalah, the heart of Muslim belief [1].
- **It gives identity** [1]: it marks the believer's commitment and joins them with Muslims everywhere [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Islam is built upon five", beginning with the testimony (Hadith of Ibn Umar) [2].
- "Allah witnesses that there is no deity except Him, and [so do] the angels and those of knowledge" (Qur'an 3:18) [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Ways (AO1):

- **In the Azaan and prayer** [1]: it is called and recited several times a day, which keeps Tawhid in mind [1].
- **At the beginning of life** [1]: the call to prayer is whispered into a newborn's ear, so its first words are of faith [1].
- **At the end of life** [1]: those near death are encouraged to say it as their last words [1].
- **At conversion** [1]: a new Muslim says it sincerely, usually before witnesses, and is welcomed into the community [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- The Hadith that whoever's last words are "La ilaha illa Allah" will enter Paradise (Abu Dawud) [2].
- The Hadith that the highest branch of faith is saying "La ilaha illa Allah" (Sahih Muslim) [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- Saying the Shahadah is the entry to Islam, and nothing else is needed to become a Muslim at that moment.
- The Shahadah contains the core of the faith.
- **Against the statement:** the Shahadah should be sincere, with understanding and commitment; it leads to the other pillars and to living by the Qur'an and Sunnah; Muslims are expected to show their faith in action; the passage says "the religion in the sight of Allah is Islam", which means submission and not only words.
- Some Muslims would say that the Shahadah is the start of Islam, but that a Muslim is judged by faith that is shown in practice.
- **Judgement:** the Shahadah is the necessary beginning but is not the whole of being a Muslim.

*Use of the passage*

- "Allah witnesses that there is no deity except Him" shows that the statement is a weighty testimony.
- "the angels and those of knowledge" shows that understanding is part of witness.
- "maintaining [creation] in justice" links the Shahadah to living justly.
- "the religion in the sight of Allah is Islam" links it to submission.
- "swift in [taking] account" shows that responsibility follows the declaration.
