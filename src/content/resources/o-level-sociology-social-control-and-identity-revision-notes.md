These notes cover key questions 2.2 (*How does society control us?*) and 2.3 (*What influences our
social identity?*) of Cambridge O Level Sociology (2251). Social control is the set of formal and
informal pressures that make people conform, backed by positive and negative sanctions; some people
resist through protest groups and subcultures. Social identity is shaped by age, gender, ethnicity
and class, and increasingly by online life, globalisation and consumer choice.

Fuller explanation of identity: [O Level how we learn our identity guide](/resources/o-level-sociology-learning-and-influences-on-identity/).

## Syllabus coverage

2251 syllabus 2025–2027:

- **2.2** — formal and informal control and sanctions (2.2.1); consensus and conflict views:
  functionalism, Marxism, feminism (2.2.2); formal agencies — government, police, courts, penal
  system, armed forces (2.2.3) — and their methods: law-making, coercion, digital surveillance,
  arrest, sentencing, imprisonment (2.2.4); informal agencies — family, education, peer group,
  media, religion, workplace (2.2.5) — and their sanctions (2.2.6); resistance through protest groups
  and subcultures (2.2.7).
- **2.3** — age, gender, ethnicity, social class (2.3.1); the digital self (2.3.2); globalisation
  (2.3.3); postmodernist views of chosen identity (2.3.4).

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Conformity** | Following society's norms and laws |
| **Sanction** | A reward (positive) or punishment (negative) |
| **Formal control** | Official control through laws and agencies of the state |
| **Informal control** | Everyday control through approval and disapproval of others |
| **Legitimacy** | Acceptance that authority is rightful |
| **Coercion** | Force or threat of force |
| **Surveillance** | Watching and monitoring behaviour, increasingly digitally |
| **Ostracism** | Shutting someone out of a group |
| **Protest group** | Group campaigning on an issue, e.g. environmental or rights groups |
| **Subculture** | Group with distinctive norms and values within wider society |
| **Online subculture** | Subculture formed in digital spaces, e.g. gaming or fan communities |
| **Multiculturalism** | Different cultures living together with equal respect |
| **Global culture** | Shared media, brands and styles across the world |

## 2.2 Social control at a glance

**Formal agencies** — government (passes laws), police (arrest, patrol, digital surveillance such as
CCTV and number-plate cameras), courts (sentencing), penal system (imprisonment, probation), armed
forces (coercion in emergencies or civil unrest).

*Effectiveness:* strongest when people see agencies as **legitimate**; weakened by corruption, low
trust, unreported crime and limited resources.

**Informal agencies and sanctions**

| Agency | Positive sanction | Negative sanction |
|---|---|---|
| Family | Parental rewards, praise | Grounding, withdrawal of pocket money |
| Education | Merits, prizes | Detention, suspension |
| Peer group | Acceptance, popularity | Teasing, ostracism |
| Media | Social media "likes", positive coverage | Online shaming, negative comments |
| Religion | Promise of reward, respect in community | Religious sanctions, community disapproval |
| Workplace | Promotion, bonus | Warning, dismissal from a job |

**Theories**

| | Functionalism (consensus) | Marxism (conflict) | Feminism (conflict) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purpose of control | Protects shared values and order | Protects ruling-class power and property | Protects male power |
| Example | Courts reinforce moral boundaries | Laws against theft protect property owners | Women controlled through reputation and family "honour" |

**Resistance** — reasons for joining protest groups and subcultures: shared grievance, belonging,
identity, excitement, rejection of mainstream values, influence of friends. Examples: student and
environmental protest groups; youth subcultures; online communities; religious subcultures.

## 2.3 Influences on identity — quick list

- **Age:** social meaning of childhood, youth, old age differs between societies.
- **Gender:** learned roles; changing with education and employment of women.
- **Ethnicity:** language, religion, customs; minority and migrant communities.
- **Class:** income, education and occupation shape lifestyle and opportunity.
- **Digital self:** chosen online identities; risks of cyberbullying and pressure.
- **Globalisation:** homogenisation, hybridity, cultural defence.
- **Postmodernism:** "pick and mix" identities through consumption; criticised for overstating
  choice.

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

*Explain three ways the media acts as an agency of informal social control.* (practice)

1. **Online approval** — social media "likes" reward behaviour that matches group norms, encouraging
   people to post what is acceptable (2).
2. **Public shaming** — people who break norms may be criticised in comments or news stories, which
   deters others (2).
3. **Representations** — films and advertising show "normal" behaviour and roles, which audiences
   copy (2).

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Mixing up **formal** (official, written rules) and **informal** (unofficial, unwritten) control.
- Giving examples of sanctions without saying whether they are positive or negative.
- Forgetting **digital surveillance**, which the syllabus names.
- Writing about subcultures without explaining **why** people join them.
- Treating identity as fixed — the syllabus asks how globalisation and choice change it.

## Quick self-test

1. Name two methods used by formal agencies.
2. Give one positive and one negative sanction used in the workplace.
3. Explain the feminist view of social control.
4. Give two examples of subcultures.
5. Explain what is meant by cultural defence.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which is more effective, formal or informal control?** Informal control governs most everyday
behaviour; formal control is needed when informal control fails or for serious crime.

**Is joining a protest group deviant?** It can be non-conformist, but many protest groups act
legally; deviance depends on the norms being challenged.

**How does 2.3 link to 2.2?** Identity is shaped by the same agencies that control us; for example
gender identity is reinforced by sanctions from family and peers.
