## Equivalent fractions and simplifying (1.2A)

Equivalent fractions have the same value. You make one by multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same number: 2/5 = 4/10 = 12/30.

A fraction is in its **simplest form** (lowest terms) when the numerator and denominator have no common factor other than 1. Divide both by their highest common factor (HCF). If you cannot see the HCF, cancel in stages by any common factor until no more cancel.

**Worked example 1.** Write 36/84 in its simplest form.

```
HCF of 36 and 84 is 12
36 ÷ 12 = 3,  84 ÷ 12 = 7
36/84 = 3/7
```

Cancelling in stages works too: 36/84 = 18/42 = 9/21 = 3/7.

## Mixed numbers and vulgar fractions (1.2B)

A **vulgar fraction** is a fraction written as one whole number over another, such as 3/8 or 35/8. When the numerator is larger than the denominator it is also called an improper fraction. A **mixed number** has a whole-number part and a fraction part, such as 4 3/8.

**Worked example 2.**

```
Mixed to improper:  4 3/8 = (4 × 8 + 3)/8 = 35/8
Improper to mixed:  47 ÷ 6 = 7 remainder 5,  so 47/6 = 7 5/6
```

## Common denominators and ordering (1.2C, 1.2D)

A **common denominator** is a number that every denominator divides into. The lowest common multiple (LCM) of the denominators gives the smallest one, which keeps the numbers small.

To **order fractions**, rewrite them all over a common denominator and compare numerators. Converting each to a decimal also works and is often quicker with a calculator.

**Worked example 3.** Write 5/8, 7/12, 2/3 and 3/5 in order of size, smallest first.

## A fraction of a quantity and one number as a fraction of another (1.2D, 1.2E)

To find a fraction of a quantity, divide by the denominator, then multiply by the numerator.

**Worked example 4.** Find 7/12 of 156 kg.

```
156 ÷ 12 = 13
13 × 7 = 91 kg
```

To express one number as a fraction of another, write "part over whole", make the units the same, then simplify.

**Worked example 5.** Write 40 minutes as a fraction of 2½ hours.

```
2½ hours = 150 minutes
40/150 = 4/15
```

Writing 40/2.5 mixes minutes with hours and gives a meaningless answer. Convert first.

## Adding and subtracting fractions and mixed numbers (1.2F)

Fractions can only be added or subtracted when they have the same denominator. Find a common denominator, convert each fraction, then add or subtract the numerators. The denominator does not change.

**Worked example 6.** Work out 3/4 + 5/6.

```
Common denominator 12
3/4 = 9/12,  5/6 = 10/12
9/12 + 10/12 = 19/12 = 1 7/12
```

For mixed numbers, the safest method is to convert to improper fractions first. This avoids the problem of "borrowing" when the second fraction part is larger than the first.

**Worked example 7.** Work out 5 1/4 − 2 5/6.

```
5 1/4 = 21/4,   2 5/6 = 17/6
Common denominator 12:  21/4 = 63/12,  17/6 = 34/12
63/12 − 34/12 = 29/12 = 2 5/12
```

Check with an estimate: about 5.25 − 2.83 ≈ 2.4, and 2 5/12 ≈ 2.42.

## Unit fractions as multiplicative inverses (1.2H)

A **unit fraction** has numerator 1, such as 1/5. Multiplying by 1/5 has the same effect as dividing by 5, so 1/5 is the multiplicative inverse (reciprocal) of 5: 5 × 1/5 = 1. That is why

```
3 ÷ 5 = 3 × 1/5 = 3/5
7 ÷ 4 = 7 × 1/4 = 7/4
```

The same idea extends to any fraction. The reciprocal of a/b is b/a, because (a/b) × (b/a) = 1. Dividing by 2/5 is the same as multiplying by 5/2. This is the reason the division method in the next section works.

## Multiplying and dividing fractions and mixed numbers (1.2I)

**Multiplying:** convert mixed numbers to improper fractions, cancel any common factors between a numerator and a denominator, then multiply numerators and multiply denominators.

**Worked example 8.** Work out 2 1/4 × 1 3/5.

```
2 1/4 = 9/4,   1 3/5 = 8/5
9/4 × 8/5      cancel 4 and 8 by 4:  9/1 × 2/5
= 18/5 = 3 3/5
```

**Dividing:** convert to improper fractions, then multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

**Worked example 9.** Work out 3 1/3 ÷ 1 7/9.

```
3 1/3 = 10/3,   1 7/9 = 16/9
10/3 ÷ 16/9 = 10/3 × 9/16
cancel 3 and 9 by 3, and 10 and 16 by 2:  5/1 × 3/8
= 15/8 = 1 7/8
```

Never multiply the whole numbers and fraction parts separately: 2 1/4 × 1 3/5 is **not** 2 × 1 plus 1/4 × 3/5.

## Fractions to decimals and percentages (1.2G)

Divide the numerator by the denominator to get a decimal. Multiply the decimal by 100 to get a percentage.

```
7/8 = 7 ÷ 8 = 0.875 = 87.5%
5/11 = 5 ÷ 11 = 0.454545... = 45.4545...%
```

Some fractions give a **terminating** decimal (it stops, like 0.875). Others give a **recurring** decimal, where a digit or block of digits repeats forever. A dot over the repeating digit shows this: 0.454545... is written 0.4̇5̇, with dots over the first and last digits of the repeating block. If a question asks for a percentage to a given accuracy, round only at the end: 5/11 = 45.5% to 3 significant figures.

## Decimal notation, place value and ordering (1.3A–C)

Each place to the right of the decimal point is worth one tenth of the place to its left: tenths, hundredths, thousandths and so on. In 47.306 the 3 is worth 3 tenths, the 0 holds the hundredths place, and the 6 is worth 6 thousandths (0.006).

To **order decimals**, line up the decimal points and compare digit by digit from the left. Filling empty places with zeros makes the comparison clearer.

**Worked example 10.** Order 0.45, 0.405, 0.5 and 0.054, smallest first.

```
0.450   0.405   0.500   0.054
Order: 0.054, 0.405, 0.45, 0.5
```

A longer decimal is not automatically larger. 0.405 has more digits than 0.45 but is smaller.

## Terminating decimals to fractions and percentages (1.3D, 1.3E)

Every terminating decimal is a fraction with a denominator of 10, 100, 1000 and so on. The specification limits this skill to **terminating decimals only**. Write the digits over the place value of the last digit, then simplify. Multiply by 100 for a percentage.

**Worked example 11.**

```
0.84  = 84/100   = 21/25     = 84%
2.125 = 2125/1000 = 17/8 = 2 1/8 = 212.5%
```

## Recurring decimals to fractions (Higher tier only)

This statement is **Higher tier only**. The method: call the decimal x, multiply by powers of 10 so that two numbers have identical recurring parts after the decimal point, then subtract so the recurring parts cancel.

**Worked example 12.** Write 0.1̇8̇ (0.181818...) as a fraction in its simplest form.

```
x    = 0.181818...
100x = 18.181818...
99x  = 18
x    = 18/99 = 2/11
```

**Worked example 13.** Write 0.58̇ (0.58888...) as a fraction in its simplest form.

Only the 8 recurs, so you need two multiples of x that both end in ...888.

```
10x  = 5.8888...
100x = 58.8888...
90x  = 53
x    = 53/90
```

**Worked example 14.** Write 0.21̇5̇ (0.2151515...) as a fraction in its simplest form.

```
10x   = 2.151515...
1000x = 215.151515...
990x  = 213
x     = 213/990 = 71/330
```

The key decision is which powers of 10 to use. Their indices differ by the length of the repeating block: here 10x and 1000x are 10¹ and 10³, two apart, for the two-digit block 15. When a question says "prove" or "show that", these lines of algebra are the evidence. A calculator conversion on its own does not prove anything.

## Common errors

- Adding denominators: 3/4 + 5/6 written as 8/10.
- Leaving an answer unsimplified when the question asks for its simplest form.
- Multiplying or dividing mixed numbers without converting them to improper fractions first.
- Turning the first fraction upside down instead of the second when dividing.
- Comparing decimals by length, so 0.405 is wrongly placed above 0.45.
- Using different units in "one number as a fraction of another".
- In recurring-decimal proofs, subtracting two multiples whose recurring parts do not line up, such as 100x − x for 0.58̇.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do you order fractions with different denominators?**
Rewrite them all over a common denominator, such as the lowest common multiple of the denominators, and compare the numerators; converting each to a decimal also works. Give the final order using the original fractions, not the converted ones.

**How do you multiply mixed numbers?**
Convert them to improper fractions, cancel any common factors, then multiply the numerators and the denominators: 2 1/4 × 1 3/5 = 9/4 × 8/5 = 18/5 = 3 3/5. Never multiply the whole numbers and fraction parts separately.

**How do you write one quantity as a fraction of another?**
Write "part over whole" with both quantities in the same units, then simplify. For example, 40 minutes as a fraction of 2½ hours is 40/150 = 4/15.

**What is the reciprocal of a fraction?**
The reciprocal of a/b is b/a, because (a/b) × (b/a) = 1. Multiplying by a unit fraction such as 1/5 has the same effect as dividing by 5, and dividing by 2/5 is the same as multiplying by 5/2.
