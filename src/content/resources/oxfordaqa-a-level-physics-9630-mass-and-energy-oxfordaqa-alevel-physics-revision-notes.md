## Equations and conversions

| Relation | Use |
|---|---|
| ΔE = Δm c² | Any energy change, nuclear or not |
| Mass defect = (Z m_p + N m_n) − m_nucleus | Starting point for binding energy |
| Binding energy = mass defect × 931.5 MeV u⁻¹ | When masses are in u |
| BE per nucleon = binding energy / A | Measure of stability |
| Energy released = (mass before − mass after) × 931.5 | Reaction energy from masses |
| Energy released = BE after − BE before | Reaction energy from binding energies |
| Nuclear mass = atomic mass − Z m_e | Removing electrons |

1 u = 931.5 MeV; 1 MeV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ J; 1 u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.

## Condensed recall

- E = mc² applies to every energy change; only in nuclear reactions is the mass change large enough to measure easily.
- A nucleus has less mass than its separate nucleons. The difference is the mass defect.
- Binding energy is the minimum energy needed to separate a nucleus into free nucleons; it is also the energy released when the nucleus forms.
- Higher binding energy per nucleon means a more tightly bound, more stable nucleus.
- Atomic masses include electrons. Remove Z electron masses for binding energy. In a reaction where proton numbers balance, the electrons cancel.
- A free nucleon has zero binding energy.
- In a decay from rest, products share kinetic energy in inverse proportion to their masses; the light particle takes most.
- Curve: steep rise to helium-4 (about 7.1 MeV), peak about 8.8 MeV near A = 56, slow fall to about 7.6 MeV for uranium.
- Left of the peak: fusion releases energy. Right of the peak: fission releases energy.
- Fusion releases about 3.5 MeV per nucleon (D–T); fission about 0.85 MeV per nucleon.

## A layout that avoids slips

Set out every mass calculation in the same four lines, keeping six decimal places in u until the end:

| Line | Example: neutron decay |
|---|---|
| Mass before | 1.008665 u |
| Mass after | 1.007276 + 0.000549 = 1.007825 u |
| Difference | 0.000840 u |
| Energy | 0.000840 × 931.5 = 0.782 MeV |

A negative difference means the change needs energy supplied and cannot happen on its own.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Atomic mass unit, u | One twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom |
| Mass defect | Mass of separate nucleons minus mass of the nucleus |
| Binding energy | Energy needed to separate a nucleus completely into its nucleons |
| Binding energy per nucleon | Binding energy divided by nucleon number |
| Mass difference | Total mass before a reaction minus total mass after |
| Fission | Splitting of a heavy nucleus into two smaller nuclei |
| Fusion | Joining of two light nuclei to form a heavier one |

## Exam traps

- Mass difference subtracted the wrong way round, giving a negative energy release.
- Atomic mass used as nuclear mass in a binding energy calculation.
- Electron masses removed in a balanced reaction where they already cancel.
- Masses rounded to three decimal places, destroying the difference.
- "Binding energy is released when the nucleus is split": wrong; it must be supplied.
- Total binding energy confused with binding energy per nucleon on the graph.
- Energy left in MeV when the question asks for joules.

## Quick self-test

1. Define mass defect.
2. Define binding energy.
3. Convert 0.0100 u to MeV.
4. Calculate the mass equivalent of 1.0 J.
5. A nucleus with A = 16 has binding energy 127.6 MeV. Find its binding energy per nucleon.
6. When can atomic masses be used directly to find the energy released in a reaction?
7. The atomic mass of carbon-12 is exactly 12.000000 u. Find the binding energy of the carbon-12 nucleus.
8. On the binding energy per nucleon curve, which region releases energy by fission?
9. The reactants in a fission have total binding energy 1786 MeV and the products 1980 MeV. Find the energy released.
10. Explain why fusion releases more energy per kilogram of fuel than fission.
11. A power station transfers 1.0 GW of energy. What mass does this correspond to each second?
12. In D–T fusion, the atomic masses before total 5.030151 u and after total 5.011268 u. Find the energy released.

### Answers

1. The difference between the total mass of the separate protons and neutrons and the mass of the nucleus.
2. The minimum energy needed to separate a nucleus into its individual protons and neutrons.
3. 0.0100 × 931.5 = 9.32 MeV.
4. m = E/c² = 1.0/(3.00 × 10⁸)² = 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁷ kg.
5. 127.6/16 = 7.98 MeV.
6. When the proton numbers (and so the electron numbers) are the same on both sides, so the electron masses cancel.
7. Nuclear mass = 12.000000 − 6 × 0.000549 = 11.996706 u; free nucleons = 6 × 1.007276 + 6 × 1.008665 = 12.095646 u; mass defect = 0.098940 u; binding energy = 92.2 MeV.
8. Nucleon numbers above the peak near 56, the heavy nuclei.
9. 1980 − 1786 = 194 MeV.
10. The curve rises much more steeply for light nuclei, so the gain in binding energy per nucleon in fusion (about 3.5 MeV) is larger than in fission (about 0.85 MeV).
11. Δm = 1.0 × 10⁹/(3.00 × 10⁸)² = 1.1 × 10⁻⁸ kg each second.
12. 0.018883 × 931.5 = 17.6 MeV.
