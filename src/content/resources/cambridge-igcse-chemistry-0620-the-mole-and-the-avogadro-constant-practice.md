## Section A

**1.** *(Supplement)* How many atoms are there in 0.50 mol of carbon dioxide, CO₂?

A: 3.01 × 10²³

B: 6.02 × 10²³

C: 9.03 × 10²³

D: 1.81 × 10²⁴

**[1]**

**2.** *(Supplement)* State what is meant by one mole of a substance, and give the value of the Avogadro constant. **[2]**

**3.** State the **two** units in which the concentration of a solution can be measured. **[1]**

**4.** *(Supplement)* Calculate the amount, in moles, in 6.4 g of sulfur dioxide, SO₂. **[2]**

**5.** *(Supplement)* Calculate the mass of 0.20 mol of calcium chloride, CaCl₂. **[2]**

**6.** *(Supplement)* Calculate the volume, in dm³, occupied by 8.8 g of carbon dioxide at r.t.p. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** *(Supplement)* 5.85 g of sodium chloride is dissolved in water to make 500 cm³ of solution.

**(a)** Calculate the concentration in mol/dm³. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the concentration in g/dm³. **[1]**

**8.** *(Supplement)* Aluminium reacts with chlorine: 2Al + 3Cl₂ → 2AlCl₃. Calculate the mass of aluminium chloride formed from 5.4 g of aluminium. **[3]**

**9.** *(Supplement)* 0.65 g of zinc is added to 25.0 cm³ of 0.50 mol/dm³ sulfuric acid. Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂

**(a)** Show which reactant is limiting. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of hydrogen, in cm³, formed at r.t.p. **[1]**

**10.** *(Supplement)* In a titration, 20.0 cm³ of 0.150 mol/dm³ sodium hydroxide is neutralised by 24.00 cm³ of nitric acid. NaOH + HNO₃ → NaNO₃ + H₂O

**(a)** Calculate the concentration of the nitric acid in mol/dm³. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of this nitric acid needed to neutralise 30.0 cm³ of the same sodium hydroxide solution. **[2]**

**11.** *(Supplement)* A hydrocarbon contains 85.7% carbon and 14.3% hydrogen by mass. Its Mr is 56. Calculate its empirical formula and its molecular formula. **[4]**

**12.** *(Supplement)* Calculate the percentage by mass of water in hydrated copper(II) sulfate, CuSO₄·5H₂O. **[2]**

**13.** *(Supplement)* Iron is made from iron(III) oxide: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum mass of iron that can be made from 8.00 g of iron(III) oxide. **[2]**

**(b)** 4.20 g of iron is obtained. Calculate the percentage yield. **[1]**

**(c)** The 8.00 g of iron(III) oxide came from 10.0 g of ore. Calculate the percentage purity of the ore. **[1]**

**14.** *(Supplement)* Calculate the total number of ions in 0.10 mol of calcium chloride, CaCl₂. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. 0.50 × 3 atoms × 6.02 × 10²³ = 9.03 × 10²³.

**2.** The amount of substance containing **6.02 × 10²³ particles** (atoms, ions or molecules) [1]; the Avogadro constant is **6.02 × 10²³** per mol [1].

**3.** **g/dm³** and **mol/dm³** [1].

**4.** Mr of SO₂ = 32 + 32 = 64 [1]; n = 6.4 ÷ 64 = **0.10 mol** [1].

**5.** Mr of CaCl₂ = 40 + 71 = 111 [1]; mass = 0.20 × 111 = **22.2 g** [1].

**6.** n = 8.8 ÷ 44 = 0.20 mol [1]; volume = 0.20 × 24 = **4.8 dm³** [1].

**7.**

**(a)** n = 5.85 ÷ 58.5 = 0.100 mol [1]; c = 0.100 ÷ 0.500 = **0.200 mol/dm³** [1].

**(b)** 5.85 g ÷ 0.500 dm³ = **11.7 g/dm³** [1].

**8.** n(Al) = 5.4 ÷ 27 = 0.20 mol [1]; ratio 2 : 2, so n(AlCl₃) = 0.20 mol [1]; Mr = 27 + 3 × 35.5 = 133.5; mass = 0.20 × 133.5 = **26.7 g** [1].

**9.**

**(a)** n(Zn) = 0.65 ÷ 65 = 0.010 mol [1]; n(H₂SO₄) = 0.50 × 0.0250 = 0.0125 mol [1]; ratio is 1 : 1, so zinc runs out first: **zinc is limiting** [1].

**(b)** n(H₂) = 0.010 mol; volume = 0.010 × 24 000 = **240 cm³** [1].

**10.**

**(a)** n(NaOH) = 0.150 × 0.0200 = 3.00 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; ratio 1 : 1, so n(HNO₃) = 3.00 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; c = 3.00 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0240 = **0.125 mol/dm³** [1].

**(b)** n(NaOH) = 0.150 × 0.0300 = 4.50 × 10⁻³ mol = n(HNO₃) [1]; V = 4.50 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.125 = 0.0360 dm³ = **36.0 cm³** [1].

**11.** C: 85.7 ÷ 12 = 7.14; H: 14.3 ÷ 1 = 14.3 [1]; ratio 1 : 2, empirical formula **CH₂** [1]; empirical mass = 14, and 56 ÷ 14 = 4 [1]; molecular formula **C₄H₈** [1].

**12.** Mr = 64 + 32 + 64 + 5 × 18 = 250 [1]; % water = 90 ÷ 250 × 100 = **36.0%** [1].

**13.**

**(a)** n(Fe₂O₃) = 8.00 ÷ 160 = 0.0500 mol, so n(Fe) = 0.100 mol [1]; mass = 0.100 × 56 = **5.60 g** [1].

**(b)** 4.20 ÷ 5.60 × 100 = **75.0%** [1].

**(c)** 8.00 ÷ 10.0 × 100 = **80.0%** [1].

**14.** Each CaCl₂ contains 3 ions, so n(ions) = 0.30 mol [1]; number = 0.30 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **1.81 × 10²³** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving volumes in cm³ when using n = c × V.
- Skipping the mole ratio, especially where it is not 1 : 1.
- Using 16 instead of 32 for oxygen gas, or 35.5 instead of 71 for chlorine gas.
- Choosing the limiting reactant by comparing masses instead of moles.
- Answering with the number of molecules when the question asks for atoms or ions.
- Rounding intermediate values and drifting from the correct final answer.
- Turning the percentage-yield fraction upside down.
