Approaches to researching behaviour is the research-methods strand of IB DP Psychology: quantitative and qualitative methods, sampling, ethics, and how far findings can be generalised. It runs through every approach and option -- you evaluate studies with it in every essay -- and at HL it is examined directly on Paper 3, where you answer questions about an unseen summary of a study.

## Syllabus coverage (first assessment 2019)

| Area | What to know |
|---|---|
| Quantitative methods | True (laboratory) experiments, field experiments, natural and quasi-experiments; correlational studies |
| Qualitative methods | Interviews (structured, semi-structured, unstructured, focus groups), observations (participant, non-participant, overt, covert, naturalistic, controlled), case studies |
| Sampling | Random, stratified, self-selected (volunteer), opportunity (convenience), purposive, snowball |
| Ethics | Informed consent, deception, debriefing, right to withdraw, protection from harm, anonymity and confidentiality; ethics in data reporting |
| Quality of research | Validity, reliability, credibility, bias, triangulation, reflexivity |
| Generalisation | Sample-to-population generalisation, theoretical generalisation, case-to-case generalisation (transferability) |

## Experiments compared

| Type | IV manipulated? | Random allocation? | Setting | Main strength | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| True (lab) experiment | Yes | Yes | Controlled | Cause and effect; replicable | Low ecological validity; demand characteristics |
| Field experiment | Yes | Usually | Natural | Higher ecological validity | Less control over extraneous variables |
| Natural experiment | No -- occurs naturally | No | Natural | Studies variables that cannot be manipulated ethically | No causal conclusions with confidence |
| Quasi-experiment | No -- pre-existing groups (e.g. gender) | No | Either | Compares groups that could not be assigned | Confounding participant variables |

**Designs:** independent samples (different participants per condition; risk of participant variables), repeated measures (same participants; risk of order effects, controlled by counterbalancing), matched pairs (participants matched on key variables).

## Correlational studies and hypotheses

- A **correlational study** measures two co-variables without manipulating either. A positive correlation means both rise together; a negative correlation means one falls as the other rises.
- **Correlation is not causation:** a third variable may cause both, or the direction may be the reverse of what is assumed (bidirectional ambiguity).
- An **alternative hypothesis** predicts a difference or relationship; the **null hypothesis** predicts none. Both must name operationalised variables, e.g. "hours of sleep" rather than "tiredness".
- A **confounding variable** varies with the IV and offers another explanation of the result.

## Qualitative research quality

| Concept | Meaning | How it is improved |
|---|---|---|
| Credibility | Findings reflect participants' realities (qualitative equivalent of internal validity) | Triangulation, member checking, rapport, reflexivity |
| Researcher bias | The researcher's beliefs affect data collection or interpretation (e.g. confirmation bias) | Reflexivity, researcher triangulation |
| Participant bias | Acquiescence, social desirability, dominant respondent in focus groups, sensitivity | Rapport, neutral questions, anonymity |
| Triangulation | Using several methods, data sources, researchers or theories | Method, data, researcher and theory triangulation |
| Transferability | How far findings apply to other settings | Rich, thick description of the context and sample |

## Sampling quick table

| Method | How | Generalisation |
|---|---|---|
| Random | Every member of the target population has an equal chance | Best for sample-to-population generalisation |
| Stratified | Sample mirrors subgroups' proportions | Representative on chosen variables |
| Opportunity | Whoever is available | Weak; often students |
| Self-selected | Volunteers respond to an advert | Volunteer bias |
| Purposive | Chosen because they have a characteristic of interest | Qualitative; theoretical generalisation |
| Snowball | Participants recruit others | Hard-to-reach populations; not representative |

## Worked example: identifying a method

*Summary:* researchers interviewed 12 nurses about burnout, using open questions, then coded the transcripts into themes.

- **Method:** semi-structured interview (qualitative). Characteristics: flexible, open-ended questions; data are words analysed by thematic coding.
- **Sampling:** likely purposive -- participants had to be nurses with relevant experience.
- **Ethics:** informed consent, confidentiality of identifiable workplace details, right to withdraw, debriefing; protection from distress when recalling stressful events.
- **Generalisation:** not sample-to-population; possible transferability to similar health-care settings if the context is richly described; theoretical generalisation to models of burnout.

## Common mistakes / exam traps

- **Calling every study an experiment.** No manipulated IV means no true experiment.
- **Quantitative language for qualitative studies.** Use credibility, transferability and reflexivity, not "statistical significance".
- **Listing ethics without application.** Say how each consideration was, or should have been, applied in the study described.
- **"Small sample, so not generalisable" as the whole answer.** Distinguish the three types of generalisation.
- **Forgetting the context.** Paper 3 answers must refer to the specific study summary.
- **Causal claims from correlations.** Say "is associated with", not "causes", unless an IV was manipulated.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is research methodology examined at SL?**
SL students are not set Paper 3, but they need research methods to evaluate studies on Papers 1 and 2 and to design the internal assessment experiment.

**What is Paper 3?**
A 1-hour HL paper worth 24 marks and 20% of the HL grade: one unseen study summary followed by three compulsory questions. Question 1 has three 3-mark parts (research method, sampling method, an alternative method); Question 2 (6 marks) is on ethics; Question 3 (9 marks) asks about one of generalisation, credibility or bias.

**Do I need inferential statistics?**
For Paper 3 you need to understand significance and generalisation conceptually; descriptive and inferential statistics are applied in the internal assessment.

## Self-test

1. What distinguishes a quasi-experiment from a true experiment? *Participants are in pre-existing groups, not randomly allocated.*
2. Define reflexivity. *The researcher's reflection on how their own beliefs and role may have affected the research.*
3. Which sampling method best supports sample-to-population generalisation? *Random (or stratified random) sampling.*
4. Name two types of triangulation. *Method and researcher triangulation.*
5. What is counterbalancing? *Varying the order of conditions to control order effects in repeated measures designs.*
