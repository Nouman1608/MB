This guide goes deeper into **3.3.6.1 Identification of functional groups by test tube reactions**, the first subtopic of section 3.3.6 Organic analysis in the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, examined in Unit 2 (Organic 1 and Physical 1) and, because it is the basis of Required practical 5, a likely topic for the practical and synoptic Unit 5 paper. The [organic analysis study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-organic-analysis/) sets out the toolkit; this page explains how each test works, what can go wrong, and how to reason with compounds that contain more than one functional group.

Condense it with the [functional group tests revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-6-1-revision-notes/) and test yourself with the [functional group tests practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-6-1-practice/).

## What the specification asks for

The content statement is short: "The reactions of functional groups listed in the specification." You should be able to "identify the functional groups using reactions in the specification." Required practical 5 is to "carry out tests for alcohols, aldehydes, alkenes and carboxylic acids."

So there is no separate list to learn. The tests come from other sections, and the skill is choosing and interpreting them. Keep strictly to reactions the specification names; an answer that relies on a test from another course may not be credited.

## The four groups and their tests

### Alkenes: bromine water

- **Method:** add a few drops of bromine water to the sample and shake.
- **Positive:** orange to **colourless**. Bromine adds across C=C (electrophilic addition, 3.3.4.2).
- **Negative:** stays orange.
- **Equation (for a dibromo product):** CH₂=CHCH₃ + Br₂ → CH₂BrCHBrCH₃.

### Primary and secondary alcohols: acidified potassium dichromate(VI)

- **Method:** add acidified potassium dichromate(VI) and warm in a hot water bath.
- **Positive:** orange to **green** (Cr₂O₇²⁻ reduced to Cr³⁺).
- **Negative:** stays orange. **Tertiary alcohols** give this negative result, as do ketones and alkenes without other groups.
- **Interference:** aldehydes are also oxidised and turn it green.

### Aldehydes: Fehling's solution or Tollens' reagent

- **Method:** warm with the reagent in a hot water bath.
- **Positive:** Fehling's: blue solution gives a **red precipitate**. Tollens': a **silver mirror** forms on the inside of the tube.
- **Negative:** Fehling's stays blue; Tollens' gives no silver mirror. Ketones and alcohols give these results.

### Carboxylic acids: sodium carbonate (or sodium hydrogencarbonate)

- **Method:** add sodium carbonate solution; pass any gas into limewater.
- **Positive:** **effervescence**; the gas turns limewater **milky** (carbon dioxide).
- **Equation:** 2CH₃CH₂COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃CH₂COONa + CO₂ + H₂O.
- **Stage note:** the acid–carbonate reaction is stated in 3.3.9.1 (International A2), but it is the test that Required practical 5 needs for carboxylic acids.

## Why the order matters

Two tests overlap: acidified dichromate(VI) gives a positive result with **both** aldehydes and primary or secondary alcohols. Ketones and tertiary alcohols give a negative result with every test above. A sensible order:

1. **Sodium carbonate** (carboxylic acids). This does not depend on any other result.
2. **Fehling's or Tollens'** (aldehydes).
3. **Bromine water** (alkenes).
4. **Acidified dichromate(VI)**, used **after** aldehydes have been ruled out, so a green result means a primary or secondary alcohol.

A sample that is negative to all four could be a ketone, a tertiary alcohol, an alkane or a halogenoalkane. Infrared spectroscopy ([3.3.6.3](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-6-3/)) then separates them, for example C=O against O–H.

### Worked example 1: three isomers of C₃H₆O

*Three liquids are propanal, propanone and prop-2-en-1-ol (CH₂=CHCH₂OH). All have the formula C₃H₆O. Describe tests that identify each one.*

| Test | Propanal | Propanone | Prop-2-en-1-ol |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm with Tollens' reagent | silver mirror | no silver mirror | no silver mirror |
| Shake with bromine water (on the other two) | — | stays orange | orange → colourless |
| Warm with acidified dichromate(VI) (confirmation) | — | stays orange | orange → green |

- Tollens' identifies **propanal**, the only aldehyde.
- Bromine water then separates the remaining two: **prop-2-en-1-ol** has C=C.
- Dichromate confirms it, because prop-2-en-1-ol is also a primary alcohol; propanone, a ketone, is not oxidised.

## Molecules with two functional groups

A single molecule can give more than one positive result. Read each result as evidence for one group and do not stop at the first positive.

### Worked example 2: an unknown with two groups

*Compound Y, C₄H₆O₂, fizzes with sodium carbonate solution and turns bromine water from orange to colourless. It does not react with Tollens' reagent. Suggest a structure for Y.*

- Fizzing with carbonate: Y contains **–COOH**.
- Decolourises bromine water: Y contains **C=C**.
- No silver mirror: no aldehyde.
- The –COOH uses one C and two O, leaving C₃H₅ with a C=C. One possibility is **but-2-enoic acid**, CH₃CH=CHCOOH; others include but-3-enoic acid, CH₂=CHCH₂COOH. Tests alone cannot decide between these positional isomers; spectroscopy can.

The answer "suggest a structure" accepts any structure that fits all the evidence, but the reasoning must use every observation.

### Worked example 3: an alcohol that is also an alkene

*Prop-2-en-1-ol and propan-1-ol are tested with bromine water and with acidified dichromate(VI). Predict the results.*

- Bromine water: prop-2-en-1-ol decolourises it; propan-1-ol leaves it orange.
- Acidified dichromate(VI): **both** turn green, since both are primary alcohols.
- Only the bromine test distinguishes them.

### Worked example 4: spotting a flawed plan

*A student plans to identify butanal, butan-2-ol and butanone by warming each with acidified potassium dichromate(VI) and then testing anything that stayed orange with Fehling's solution. Evaluate the plan.*

- Butanal and butan-2-ol both turn the dichromate green, so the first test does not separate them.
- Only butanone stays orange, so the Fehling's test is applied to a liquid that is already identified and gives no new information.
- **Better plan:** warm all three with Fehling's solution first. Butanal gives a red precipitate. Then warm the other two with acidified dichromate(VI): butan-2-ol turns green and butanone stays orange.

## Technique points for Required practical 5

- Use **small volumes** (a few drops of the organic liquid).
- Warm with a **hot water bath**, never a naked flame, because the liquids are flammable.
- Run a **control** (reagent with water) alongside, so that a colour change can be judged fairly.
- Record the **colour before and after**: an observation names both.
- For carbonate tests, an acid catalyst left over from an earlier step will also fizz, so test the pure compound.

## Common mistakes

- Writing "clear" instead of "colourless" for bromine water.
- Writing "no reaction" instead of an observation such as "stays orange".
- Using acidified dichromate(VI) to identify an alcohol before excluding aldehydes.
- Swapping the Fehling's and Tollens' results.
- Assuming a negative dichromate result means "no alcohol": tertiary alcohols give it.
- Claiming carbonate fizzing shows an alcohol; alcohols do not react with carbonates.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Describe a test**: reagent, what you do (shake, warm in a water bath), result for a positive **and** for a negative.
- **Identify**: link each observation to the group it shows.
- **Suggest a structure**: give one that fits all the evidence; state any group you have ruled out.
- **Explain** a result: name what is oxidised, reduced or added.
- **Plan**: give the order of tests and the reason for it.
