These notes condense **Business objectives** (topics 3.1 Business objectives, 3.2 Costs and economies of scale, 3.3 Revenue and profit) from Component 01 of the OCR *A Level Economics (H460)* specification, Version 3.0 (June 2026). The full worked table for an invented firm is in the [business objectives study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-economics-h460-business-objectives-ocr-alevel-economics/), and the [business objectives practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-economics-h460-business-objectives-ocr-alevel-economics-practice/) give exam-style work. All figures are invented.

## Objectives and their output rules

| Objective | Type | Output rule |
|---|---|---|
| Profit | Maximising | MR = MC, with MC rising through MR |
| Sales revenue | Maximising | MR = 0 |
| Sales volume | Maximising | Largest output with profit ≥ 0 (or ≥ normal profit) |
| Growth | Maximising | Expand size over time; accepts lower short-run profit |
| Utility | Maximising | Managers pursue their own pay, status and comfort |
| Profit satisficing | Non-maximising | Any output reaching a satisfactory profit |
| Social welfare | Non-maximising | Benefit to society, often covering costs only |
| CSR | Non-maximising | Voluntary action for stakeholders beyond legal requirements |

**Ordering to remember** (downward-sloping demand): profit-maximising output ≤ revenue-maximising output with a profit floor ≤ sales-volume-maximising output. Unconstrained revenue maximisation (MR = 0) is always above the profit maximum and can be loss-making.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fixed cost | A cost that does not vary with output in the short run |
| Variable cost | A cost that rises with output |
| Average cost | Total cost ÷ output |
| Marginal cost | Change in total cost from one more unit |
| Short run | At least one factor is fixed |
| Long run | All factors are variable |
| Law of diminishing returns | Adding a variable factor to a fixed factor eventually lowers its marginal product |
| Minimum efficient scale | The lowest output at which long-run average cost is minimised |
| Average revenue | Total revenue ÷ output, equal to price |
| Marginal revenue | Change in total revenue from one more unit |
| Accounting profit | Revenue minus explicit costs |
| Normal profit | The minimum return to keep resources in their current use |
| Supernormal profit | Profit above normal profit |
| Principal-agent problem | Agents acting for principals pursue different aims, with more information |

## Quick table drill (Firm Plover)

P = 50 − 5Q; fixed cost 40; Q in hundred units; £ hundred.

| Q | P | TR | MR | TC | MC | Profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 45 | 45 | 45 | 50 | 10 | −5 |
| 2 | 40 | 80 | 35 | 58 | 8 | 22 |
| 3 | 35 | 105 | 25 | 68 | 10 | 37 |
| 4 | 30 | 120 | 15 | 82 | 14 | 38 |
| 5 | 25 | 125 | 5 | 100 | 18 | 25 |
| 6 | 20 | 120 | −5 | 124 | 24 | −4 |

- Profit maximum: **Q = 4**, profit 38 (MR 15 > MC 14 into row 4; MR 5 < MC 18 into row 5).
- Revenue maximum: **Q = 5**, TR 125 (MR turns negative after 5).
- Sales volume maximum: **Q = 5** (profit 25); Q = 6 makes a loss.
- Satisficing at 30: **Q = 3 or 4**.
- Normal profit of 30: supernormal profit at Q = 4 is 38 − 30 = **8**.

## Cost curve rules

- MC falls then rises; diminishing returns cause the rise in the short run.
- MC cuts AC and AVC at their minimum points.
- AFC falls continuously as output rises.
- Long-run AC falls with economies of scale, is flat over a range after minimum efficient scale, and rises with diseconomies.

## Economies and diseconomies of scale

| Internal economies | Diseconomies | External economies |
|---|---|---|
| Purchasing (bulk buying) | Communication problems | Skilled local labour |
| Technical (specialised machinery) | Co-ordination and control | Specialist suppliers nearby |
| Financial (cheaper borrowing) | Lower motivation, alienation | Shared infrastructure |
| Managerial (specialist managers) | | |
| Risk-bearing (diversification) | | |

**Significance:** larger firms may charge lower prices or earn more profit; markets with a high minimum efficient scale relative to demand support few firms; diseconomies limit growth.

## Principal-agent problem

- Cause: divergent aims plus asymmetric information.
- Example: a revenue-linked bonus pushes managers towards higher output than the profit maximum.
- Remedies: pay linked to profit or long-run share value, share options, audits, independent directors, takeover threat.
- Limits: monitoring is costly; targets can be manipulated; short-term bonuses encourage short-termism.

## Factors influencing objectives

Ownership and control; strength of competition; stage of development; legal form and mission; financial position; stakeholder pressure.

## Exam traps

- Reading MR and MC from the wrong row of a table.
- Giving the highest-price or highest-revenue output as profit maximising.
- Ignoring the profit floor in sales volume or constrained revenue questions.
- Forgetting that normal profit is an opportunity cost.
- Confusing diminishing returns (short run) with diseconomies of scale (long run).
- Listing objectives without linking them to the firm in the question.

## Self-test

1. State the profit-maximising rule.
2. State the sales revenue maximising rule.
3. What constraint applies to sales volume maximisation?
4. Fixed cost is 60 and variable cost at 20 units is 140. Find average cost.
5. TR rises from 300 to 320 when output rises from 10 to 11. Find MR.
6. Distinguish between normal and supernormal profit.
7. Accounting profit is 50 thousand and the owner could earn 35 thousand elsewhere. Find supernormal profit.
8. Why does MC eventually rise in the short run?
9. Define minimum efficient scale.
10. Give two internal economies of scale.
11. Give one cause of diseconomies of scale.
12. Explain one way to reduce the principal-agent problem.

### Answers

1. Produce where MR = MC, with MC rising through MR.
2. Produce where MR = 0.
3. Profit must be at least zero (or at least normal profit if stated).
4. (60 + 140) ÷ 20 = **10**.
5. 320 − 300 = **20**.
6. Normal profit is the minimum return to keep resources in the business; supernormal profit is any profit above that.
7. 50 − 35 = **15 thousand**.
8. The law of diminishing returns: adding variable factors to a fixed factor eventually lowers marginal product, raising the cost of each extra unit.
9. The lowest output at which long-run average cost reaches its minimum.
10. Any two of: purchasing, technical, financial, managerial, risk-bearing.
11. Any one of: communication problems; co-ordination and control difficulties; low motivation.
12. Link managers' pay to long-run profit or share value, so their incentive matches shareholders' aims.
