## Questions

**1.** Solve 9x − 4 = 5x + 20. **[2]**

**2.** Solve 5(2x + 3) − 2(x − 4) = 47. **[3]**

**3.** Solve (x − 4)/3 + (3x − 1)/4 = 6. **[4]**

**4.** This question is about solving 6 − 2x = x + 1 using a graph.

**(a)** Complete the table of values for y = 6 − 2x. **[1]**

| x | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y = 6 − 2x | 6 | | | | −2 |
| y = x + 1 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |

**(b)** Draw both lines for 0 ≤ x ≤ 4 and read from your graph an estimate, to 1 decimal place, of the value of x where 6 − 2x = x + 1. **[2]**
**(c)** Solve the equation algebraically and compare with your estimate. **[2]**

**5.** A taxi firm charges £3.20 plus £1.45 for each kilometre travelled.

**(a)** Write a formula for the cost, C pounds, of a journey of n kilometres. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out the cost of a 12 km journey. **[1]**
**(c)** A journey costs £24.95. Work out its length. **[2]**

**6.** The angles of a quadrilateral are x°, (2x + 15)°, (3x − 25)° and (4x + 10)°. Work out the size of the largest angle. **[4]**

**7.** An isosceles triangle has two equal sides of length (3x − 1) cm and a base of length (x + 9) cm. Its perimeter is 49 cm. Work out the length of each side. **[4]**

**8.** A mother is 4 times as old as her son. In 6 years' time she will be 3 times as old as him. Work out the mother's age now. **[4]**

**9.** Printer X charges a £60 set-up fee plus 8p per copy. Printer Y has no set-up fee and charges 14p per copy.

**(a)** Write an equation and solve it to find the number of copies at which the two printers cost the same. **[3]**
**(b)** For 750 copies, which printer is cheaper and what is its cost? **[2]**

**10.** Five maths books and three science books cost £61.25 in total. Two maths books and six science books cost £54.50 in total. Form two equations and solve them to find the price of one maths book and one science book. **[6]**

**11.** A rectangle has length (3x + 2) cm and width (x + 4) cm. A square has side length (x + 7) cm. The rectangle and the square have the same perimeter.

**(a)** Show that 8x + 12 = 4x + 28. **[2]**
**(b)** Solve the equation. **[2]**
**(c)** Which shape has the larger area, and by how much? **[3]**

**12.** A triangle has a side of length 3(x − 4) cm, and a square has a side of length (2x − 15) cm. A student claims the two lengths are equal. Form an equation, solve it, and explain what your solution tells you about the student's claim. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** 9x − 5x = 20 + 4, so 4x = 24 [1], giving **x = 6** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A line of collecting terms is worth showing. A bare answer that happens to be wrong leaves nothing to reward.

**2.** Expand: 10x + 15 − 2x + 8 = 47 [1]. Collect: 8x + 23 = 47, so 8x = 24 [1]. **x = 3** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The usual slip is −2(x − 4) becoming −2x − 8. Write the expansion on its own line so the sign change is visible.

**3.** Multiply every term by 12: 4(x − 4) + 3(3x − 1) = 72 [1]. Expand: 4x − 16 + 9x − 3 = 72 [1]. 13x − 19 = 72, so 13x = 91 [1]. **x = 7** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Multiplying only the fractions and not the 6 is a common loss. Substituting x = 7 gives 1 + 5 = 6, a quick check worth doing.

**4. (a)** y = 4, 2, 0 for x = 1, 2, 3 [1]
**(b)** Both lines drawn correctly, crossing between x = 1 and x = 2 [1]. Estimate **x ≈ 1.7** [1]
**(c)** 6 − 1 = x + 2x, so 3x = 5 [1]. **x = 5/3 ≈ 1.67**, which agrees with the graph [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The solution is the x-coordinate of the crossing. Writing the y-value, about 2.7, answers a different question.

**5. (a)** C = 3.20 + 1.45n, with the fixed charge 3.20 [1] and the rate 1.45 multiplied by n [1]
**(b)** 3.20 + 1.45 × 12 = 3.20 + 17.40 = **£20.60** [1]
**(c)** 3.20 + 1.45n = 24.95, so 1.45n = 21.75 [1]. **n = 15 km** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Define your letters. A formula such as C = 3.20 + 1.45n is easy to follow when it follows "C is the cost in pounds, n is the distance in km".

**6.** The angle sum of a quadrilateral is 360°, so x + (2x + 15) + (3x − 25) + (4x + 10) = 360 [1]. 10x = 360, so x = 36 [1]. Largest angle is 4x + 10 = 4(36) + 10 = **154°** [1]. Check: 36 + 87 + 83 + 154 = 360 [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Stopping at x = 36 answers the equation but not the question. The largest angle is the one the question asks for.

**7.** Perimeter: 2(3x − 1) + (x + 9) = 49 [1]. 6x − 2 + x + 9 = 49, so 7x + 7 = 49 [1]. x = 6 [1]. Sides are 3(6) − 1 = 17 cm, 17 cm and 6 + 9 = 15 cm [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Counting the equal side twice is the key step. Adding the three expressions only once is a common slip.

**8.** Let s be the son's age now, so the mother is 4s. In 6 years: mother 4s + 6 [1], son s + 6 [1]. Equation: 4s + 6 = 3(s + 6), so 4s + 6 = 3s + 18 and s = 12 [1]. Mother is 4 × 12 = **48 years** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Add 6 to both ages before multiplying. Using 4s + 6 = 3s + 6 forgets that the son also gets older.

**9. (a)** Costs in pence for n copies are 6000 + 8n and 14n [1]. Equate: 6000 = 6n [1]. **n = 1000 copies** [1]
**(b)** Printer X: 60 + 0.08 × 750 = £120 [1]. Printer Y: 0.14 × 750 = £105, so **Printer Y is cheaper at £105** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep to one unit. Mixing pounds and pence in the same equation is the main danger here.

**10.** Let m be the price of a maths book and s of a science book, in pounds. 5m + 3s = 61.25 [1] and 2m + 6s = 54.50 [1]. Multiply the first by 2: 10m + 6s = 122.50. Subtract the second: 8m = 68 [1]. m = 8.50 [1]. 3s = 61.25 − 42.50 = 18.75, so s = 6.25 [1]. Check: 2(8.50) + 6(6.25) = 17 + 37.50 = 54.50. A maths book costs **£8.50** and a science book costs **£6.25** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Test your pair of prices in the equation that was not used to find the second price. An arithmetic slip often shows up at that point.

**11. (a)** Rectangle perimeter: 2[(3x + 2) + (x + 4)] = 2(4x + 6) = 8x + 12 [1]. Square perimeter: 4(x + 7) = 4x + 28. Equal perimeters give 8x + 12 = 4x + 28 [1]
**(b)** 4x = 16 [1], so **x = 4** [1]
**(c)** Rectangle: 14 × 8 = 112 cm² [1]. Square: 11² = 121 cm² [1]. The **square is larger by 9 cm²** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" works best when you write each expansion line. Do not start from the answer; derive it.

**12.** The student's claim gives 3(x − 4) = 2x − 15 [1]. 3x − 12 = 2x − 15 [1], so x = −3 [1]. Then each length is 3(−7) = −21 and 2(−3) − 15 = −21. A length cannot be negative, so **no such triangle and square exist** and the claim is false [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Getting x = −3 is not the end. Putting it back in the lengths shows the impossible situation, and a sentence of explanation completes the answer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dropping a minus sign when expanding a bracket that has a negative multiplier.
- Clearing fractions but leaving a whole-number term unmultiplied.
- Giving the y-coordinate of an intersection when the equation's solution is the x-coordinate.
- Giving a graph estimate to more accuracy than the graph supports.
- Leaving x as the final answer when the question asks for an angle, a length, an age or a cost.
- Mixing units, such as pounds and pence, in one equation.
- Forgetting to define the letters used.
- Skipping the check that a solution is sensible (negative lengths, parts of people).
