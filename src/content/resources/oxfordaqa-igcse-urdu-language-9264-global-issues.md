This guide goes deeper on the third topic of **Theme 2 Local, National, International and Global
Areas of Interest** in OxfordAQA International GCSE Urdu (9264): **Global issues**. The
[Theme 2 study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-urdu-local-national-international/) sets out the
problem-and-solution approach for the theme. This page builds the specific vocabulary for the
environment and poverty and the grammar, especially the passive, that lets you write about large
problems in an impersonal, mature style.

## What this topic covers

The specification sets two sub-topics:

- **The environment** — pollution of air, water and land, climate change, waste and recycling,
  saving energy and water, and what individuals and governments can do.
- **Poverty** — its causes and effects, such as hunger, lack of education and clean water, and
  possible solutions.

Global issues differs from Social issues in **scale**. Social issues (volunteering, health) are
about you and your community; global issues are about problems that cross borders. A strong
answer can still connect the two, for example by showing how a world problem appears in your own
area, but it should not shrink a global question into a purely personal one.

The topic is examined through **Paper 1 (Reading)**, which includes translation from English into
Urdu, and **Paper 2 (Writing)**.

## Structures that carry this topic

**1. The passive with جانا.** Global problems are usually described impersonally, so the passive
is the key structure of this topic. Form it with the past participle plus جانا, agreeing with the
thing affected:

- بہت سے درخت کاٹے جاتے ہیں — many trees are cut down.
- پلاسٹک کو دوبارہ استعمال کیا جا سکتا ہے — plastic can be reused.
- اس مسئلے پر توجہ نہیں دی جاتی — this problem is not given attention.

Note دی (feminine) agreeing with توجہ in the last example.

**2. The present continuous for trends.** درجۂ حرارت بڑھ رہا ہے (the temperature is rising);
جنگلات کم ہو رہے ہیں (forests are shrinking).

**3. Real and hypothetical conditions.**

- Real: اگر ہم پانی بچائیں تو آنے والی نسلوں کو فائدہ ہو گا.
- Warning: اگر ہم نے ابھی کچھ نہ کیا تو حالات اور خراب ہو جائیں گے (if we do not act now,
  things will get worse). The past form نے ... نہ کیا is idiomatic here for a future condition.
- Hypothetical: اگر ہر گھر میں شمسی توانائی ہوتی تو بجلی کا مسئلہ حل ہو جاتا.

**4. Responsibility.** Use کو + چاہیے to say who should act: حکومت کو ... پر پابندی لگانی
چاہیے; ہر شخص کو ... چاہیے. Distinguishing what **individuals** and **governments** can do is a
simple way to organise a paragraph.

**5. Cause and effect chains.** ... کی وجہ سے (because of), اس کا نتیجہ یہ ہے کہ (the result is
that), جس سے (which leads to). Chain them: جنگلات کی کٹائی کی وجہ سے بارش کم ہوتی ہے، جس سے
خشک سالی بڑھتی ہے.

**6. Contrast and concession.** اگرچہ (although), اس کے باوجود (despite this): اگرچہ حکومت نے
کچھ اقدامات کیے ہیں، اس کے باوجود آلودگی کم نہیں ہوئی.

## Worked example 1: translation into Urdu

English: *Pollution is a serious problem in many big cities. Plastic bags should be banned. If we
do not act now, the situation will get worse.*

```
"a serious problem"   ایک سنگین مسئلہ (مسئلہ is masculine; سنگین does not change)
"should be banned"    ... پر پابندی ہونی چاہیے (پابندی is feminine, so ہونی)
"If we do not act now"
                      اگر ہم نے ابھی کچھ نہ کیا (idiomatic warning condition)
"will get worse"      حالات اور خراب ہو جائیں گے (حالات is masculine plural)
```

Answer: بہت سے بڑے شہروں میں آلودگی ایک سنگین مسئلہ ہے۔ پلاسٹک کے تھیلوں پر پابندی ہونی چاہیے۔
اگر ہم نے ابھی کچھ نہ کیا تو حالات اور خراب ہو جائیں گے۔

A word-for-word attempt such as پلاسٹک کے تھیلے بین ہونے چاہئیں borrows "ban" unnecessarily
and misses the natural پابندی construction.

## Worked example 2: a paragraph on poverty

Task (original): *Explain why poverty is a global problem and what can be done about it.*

> غربت دنیا کا ایک بڑا مسئلہ ہے۔ بہت سے بچے غربت کی وجہ سے اسکول نہیں جا سکتے کیونکہ انہیں اپنے
> گھر والوں کے لیے کام کرنا پڑتا ہے۔ اس سے یہ مسئلہ اگلی نسل تک چلتا رہتا ہے۔ پچھلے سال ہمارے
> اسکول نے ایک فلاحی ادارے کے لیے چندہ جمع کیا تاکہ غریب بچوں کو کتابیں مل سکیں۔ میرے خیال میں
> اگر ہر بچے کو مفت تعلیم ملے تو غربت آہستہ آہستہ کم ہو جائے گی۔

What it does well:

- **A cause-and-effect chain**: poverty → children work → no school → the cycle continues
  (اس سے ... چلتا رہتا ہے).
- **Obligation** with کو ... پڑنا (کام کرنا پڑتا ہے, they have to work), a useful alternative to
  چاہیے.
- **A link to the writer's own community** in the past tense, without turning the answer into a
  purely local one.
- **A conditional solution** in the final opinion.

To add range, insert a passive: غریب ملکوں میں اکثر صاف پانی مہیا نہیں کیا جاتا (clean water is
often not provided in poor countries).

## Common mistakes

- Using only active sentences with "we" and "they". The passive is what makes writing on this
  topic sound mature.
- Getting passive agreement wrong: توجہ نہیں دیا جاتا instead of دی جاتی.
- Treating آلودگی, غربت, حکومت and توانائی as masculine. All four are feminine.
- Writing only about your own street when the task asks about the world, or only in vague global
  terms when it asks how the problem affects you.
- Inventing figures. Phrases such as بہت سے, اکثر and دنیا کے کئی حصوں میں communicate the idea
  without unverified statistics.
- Ending without a solution, which wastes the chance to use a conditional.

## Exam technique

**Reading.** As the Theme 2 guide notes, decide first whether a text is describing a problem,
proposing a solution or evaluating one. In this topic, also note **who** is said to be
responsible: questions often ask what governments, companies or individuals should do, and the
answer is usually in a sentence with کو ... چاہیے or ذمہ داری (responsibility). Passive
sentences can hide the agent, so read them carefully.

**Translation.** Expect "should be", "can be" and "is being" structures. Check the participle
agrees with the noun affected, and that you have chosen پابندی, آلودگی and similar nouns rather
than borrowing English words.

**Writing.** Name the problem, chain its causes and effects, divide solutions between individuals
and governments, and finish with a justified opinion. One passive, one conditional and one
concession (اگرچہ ...) in the same answer show a wide range of structures.

## Building a developed answer on poverty

Poverty answers are often thin because students know the word غربت but little else. Build a grid
of causes, effects and solutions and you can always develop a point:

| Causes | Effects | Solutions |
|---|---|---|
| بے روزگاری (unemployment) | بھوک (hunger) | روزگار کے مواقع (job opportunities) |
| تعلیم کی کمی (lack of education) | بیماریاں (illnesses) | مفت تعلیم (free education) |
| قدرتی آفات (natural disasters) | بے گھری (homelessness) | عالمی امداد (international aid) |
| مہنگائی (rising prices) | بچوں کی مشقت (child labour) | صاف پانی کی فراہمی (supply of clean water) |

Pick one item from each column and link them: بے روزگاری کی وجہ سے بہت سے خاندان بھوک کا شکار ہیں؛
اگر روزگار کے مزید مواقع پیدا کیے جائیں تو حالات بہتر ہو سکتے ہیں۔ (Because of unemployment many
families suffer hunger; if more job opportunities were created, the situation could improve.)
That one sentence contains a cause, an effect, a passive (پیدا کیے جائیں) and a condition.
