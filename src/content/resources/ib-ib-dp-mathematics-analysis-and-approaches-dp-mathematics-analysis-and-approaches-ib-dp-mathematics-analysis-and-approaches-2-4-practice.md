This practice set covers section 2.4 of IB Diploma Programme Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches: key features of graphs and points of intersection, found with technology where appropriate. It is for SL and HL. Most questions are "(calculator allowed)", matching Paper 2; give answers to 3 significant figures unless they are exact.

## Section A

**1.** (calculator allowed) Let f(x) = 2x³ − 3x² − 12x + 4.

**(a)** Find the coordinates of the local maximum and local minimum points. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the zeros of f. **[2]**

**2.** (calculator allowed) Let f(x) = 10x/(x² + 4). Find the coordinates of the maximum and minimum points, and write down the equation of the horizontal asymptote. **[3]**

**3.** (calculator allowed) Find the coordinates of the points of intersection of y = e^(x/2) and y = x + 2. **[3]**

**4.** (calculator-free) For y = 2(x + 3)² − 7, write down:

**(a)** the coordinates of the vertex **[1]**
**(b)** the equation of the axis of symmetry. **[1]**

**5.** (calculator allowed) Let g(x) = 3/(x² − 4). Write down the equations of all the asymptotes of the graph of g. **[3]**

**6.** (calculator allowed) Let f(x) = x² − 1 and g(x) = 2 cos x, with x in radians.

**(a)** Explain why any solutions of f(x) = g(x) occur in pairs ±a. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of the points of intersection of the two graphs. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (calculator allowed) Let f(x) = 10x/(x² + 4) and g(x) = x − 1.

**(a)** Use technology to find the x-coordinates of the points of intersection of the two graphs. **[3]**
**(b)** Show that these x-coordinates satisfy x³ − x² − 6x − 4 = 0. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that x³ − x² − 6x − 4 = (x + 1)(x² − 2x − 4), and hence find the exact x-coordinates. **[3]**

**8.** (calculator allowed) In an invented model, a company's monthly profit is P(x) = −2x² + 48x − 160 thousand dollars, where x hundred items are sold and 0 ≤ x ≤ 20.

**(a)** Find the zeros of P and interpret them. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the maximum profit and the number of items that gives it. **[2]**
**(c)** Interpret P(0). **[1]**

**9.** (calculator allowed) Let f(x) = 4 − 2ˣ and g(x) = x².

**(a)** Write down the equation of the horizontal asymptote of the graph of f. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of the points of intersection of the graphs of f and g. **[3]**
**(c)** Hence solve f(x) > g(x). **[2]**

**10.** (calculator allowed) Let f(x) = x³ − 12x for −3 ≤ x ≤ 5.

**(a)** Find the coordinates of the local maximum and local minimum points. **[2]**
**(b)** Find f(−3) and f(5). **[2]**
**(c)** Write down the range of f. **[2]**

**11.** (calculator allowed) Let g(x) = 3/(x² − 4).

**(a)** Write down the coordinates of the local maximum point. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the value of k for which g(x) = k has exactly one solution. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the values of k for which g(x) = k has no solution. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the graph of g is symmetric about the y-axis. **[1]**

**12.** (calculator allowed) In an invented model, the populations, in thousands, of two towns t years after 2020 are A(t) = 120 + 6t and B(t) = 80e^(0.06t), for 0 ≤ t ≤ 40.

**(a)** Find the value of t at which the populations are equal, and the population at that time. **[3]**
**(b)** State which town has the larger population in 2030, with a reason. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Maximum **(−1, 11)** [1]; minimum **(2, −16)** [1]
**(b)** **x = −2** (exact) and **x = 0.314** [1]; **x = 3.19** [1]

**2.** Maximum **(2, 2.5)** [1]; minimum **(−2, −2.5)** [1]; asymptote **y = 0** [1]

**3.** Intersections at x = −1.54 and x = 3.36 [1]; points **(−1.54, 0.464)** [1] and **(3.36, 5.36)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The left-hand point is easy to miss in a window that starts at x = 0.

**4. (a)** **(−3, −7)** [1]
**(b)** **x = −3** [1]

**5.** **x = −2** [1], **x = 2** [1], **y = 0** [1]

**6. (a)** Both functions satisfy h(−x) = h(x), so if x = a is a solution then so is x = −a [1]
**(b)** **(1.27, 0.601)** [1] and **(−1.27, 0.601)** [1]

**7. (a)** **x = −1.24** [1], **x = −1** [1], **x = 3.24** [1]
**(b)** 10x/(x² + 4) = x − 1, so 10x = (x − 1)(x² + 4) [1] = x³ − x² + 4x − 4, giving x³ − x² − 6x − 4 = 0 [1]
**(c)** (x + 1)(x² − 2x − 4) = x³ − 2x² − 4x + x² − 2x − 4 = x³ − x² − 6x − 4 [1]. x² − 2x − 4 = 0 gives x = 1 ± √5 [1], so the exact values are **x = −1, 1 − √5, 1 + √5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* 1 ± √5 = 3.24 and −1.24 agree with the GDC values in (a), which is a useful check.

**8. (a)** −2x² + 48x − 160 = 0 gives x² − 24x + 80 = 0, so **x = 4 or x = 20** [1]. P = 0 at 400 and 2000 items [1]: the company breaks even at these sales levels [1]
**(b)** Vertex at x = 12 [1]; maximum profit P(12) = **128 thousand dollars** with 1200 items [1]
**(c)** P(0) = −160: with no sales the company loses 160 thousand dollars per month (its fixed costs) [1]

**9. (a)** **y = 4** [1]
**(b)** Intersections at x = −1.93 and x = 1.26 [1]; points **(−1.93, 3.74)** [1] and **(1.26, 1.60)** [1]
**(c)** f is above g between the intersections [1]: **−1.93 < x < 1.26** [1]

**10. (a)** Maximum **(−2, 16)** [1]; minimum **(2, −16)** [1]
**(b)** f(−3) = −27 + 36 = **9** [1]; f(5) = 125 − 60 = **65** [1]
**(c)** Least value −16 at the minimum [1]; greatest value 65 at the end point, so **−16 ≤ f(x) ≤ 65** [1]
*Examiner insight:* −16 ≤ f(x) ≤ 16 is the common wrong answer; the end point x = 5 is higher than the local maximum.

**11. (a)** **(0, −0.75)** [1]
**(b)** **k = −0.75** [1]
**(c)** Values above the local maximum value but not above the asymptote y = 0 [1]: **−0.75 < k ≤ 0** [1]
**(d)** g(−x) = 3/((−x)² − 4) = 3/(x² − 4) = g(x) [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c), k = 0 is included because g(x) is never 0; one mark is lost for −0.75 < k < 0.

**12. (a)** Intersection of the two graphs [1]: **t = 17.0** [1]; population **222 thousand** [1]
**(b)** At t = 10, A(10) = 180 and B(10) = 146 [1], so **town A** is larger in 2030, which is before the crossing at t = 17.0 [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a value when coordinates are asked for, or the reverse.
- Ignoring end points when finding a range on a restricted domain, as in question 10.
- Missing an intersection outside the default window, as in questions 3 and 9.
- Writing asymptotes as numbers rather than equations.
- Excluding or including a boundary value of k without checking it.
