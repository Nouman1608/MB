## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** They contract, raising the hairs [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two of: fatty tissue; hairs (with trapped air) [1 each]. **[2]**

**5.** Enzymes work best at an optimum temperature [1], so a large change would slow reactions or denature the enzymes [1]. **[2]**

**6.**

**(a)** Temperature receptors [1] in the skin [1].

**(b)** Sensory neurones carry impulses to the hypothalamus [1], which sends impulses along motor neurones [1] to the effectors, such as muscles and arterioles [1].

**(c)(i)** Vasoconstriction: the arterioles narrow [1], so less blood flows through the surface capillaries and less heat is lost [1].

**(c)(ii)** They contract and raise the hairs [1], trapping a thicker layer of air which insulates [1].

**(c)(iii)** They contract and relax rapidly (shivering) [1], and respiration in the muscles releases heat [1]. **[11]**

**7.**

**(a)** Vasodilation has occurred [1]. The arterioles widen so more blood flows through the capillaries near the skin surface [1], which makes the face look red as heat is lost [1].

**(b)** Sweat evaporates from the skin [1] and evaporation takes heat energy from the skin, cooling it [1].

**(c)** Sweat evaporates more slowly in humid air [1]. **[6]**

**8.**

**(a)** (40 - 12) divided by 12 x 100 [1] = 233 per cent (233.3 to one decimal place) [1].

**(b)** 40 divided by 4 = 10 times [1].

**(c)** As the temperature rises, the blood flow in the skin increases [1], because the arterioles undergo vasodilation [1] and this loses more heat to cool the body; at low temperatures they narrow, which conserves heat [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying that blood vessels move towards or away from the skin.
- Writing that capillaries widen and narrow, not arterioles.
- Saying that sweat cools because it is cold, not because it evaporates.
- Forgetting the hypothalamus or the receptors in the path of the information.
- Mixing up vasodilation and vasoconstriction.
