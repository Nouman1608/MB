All questions are suitable for SL and HL. Use G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻² and give numerical answers to three significant figures unless told otherwise.

## Questions

**1.** State Kepler's first law and Kepler's third law of planetary motion. **[2]**

**2.** A pair of uniform spheres, one of 5.0 × 10³ kg and one of 2.0 × 10³ kg, sit with centres 15 m from each other.

**(a)** Find the gravitational attraction between them. **[2]**
**(b)** The larger mass is replaced by one of twice the mass, and the separation is increased to 45 m. Determine the new force. **[1]**

**3.** The table gives data for two of Jupiter's moons, supplied for this question.

| Moon | Orbital radius / m | Period / days |
|---|---|---|
| Europa | 6.71 × 10⁸ | 3.55 |
| Ganymede | 1.07 × 10⁹ | to find |

Use Kepler's third law to determine the orbital period of Ganymede in days. **[4]**

**4.** An asteroid orbits the Sun on an ellipse. At its closest point it is 1.4 × 10¹¹ m from the Sun and moving at 3.6 × 10⁴ m s⁻¹. Use Kepler's second law (state it first) to find the speed at its furthest point, 4.2 × 10¹¹ m from the Sun. At both points the velocity is perpendicular to the line to the Sun. **[4]**

**5.** For Venus, take a mass of 4.87 × 10²⁴ kg and a radius of 6.05 × 10⁶ m.

**(a)** Define gravitational field strength. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the field strength at the surface of Venus. **[2]**
**(c)** Determine the weight of a 450 kg probe at the surface. **[1]**

**6.** A 950 kg spacecraft is 2.4 × 10⁶ m above the surface of Venus (data from question 5). Calculate the gravitational force on it, and state why the distance used is not 2.4 × 10⁶ m. **[4]**

**7.** Two bodies, A and B, have masses 1.8 × 10²³ kg and 2.0 × 10²² kg. Their centres are 6.0 × 10⁸ m apart.

**(a)** Determine the distance from A of the point on the line joining them where the resultant gravitational field strength is zero. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the resultant field strength at the point on the line joining them that is 1.0 × 10⁸ m from B, and state its direction. **[3]**

**8.** Take the Earth's mass as 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg and its radius as 6.37 × 10⁶ m.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in g between the surface and a point 2.0 km above it. **[3]**
**(b)** At a height of 2000 km the decrease is 42 %. Explain why the field can be treated as uniform near the surface but not at 2000 km. **[2]**

**9.** Gravitational field lines are drawn around a planet.

**(a)** Describe the field lines of the uniform field near the surface of the planet. **[2]**
**(b)** In a diagram of the radial field, 6 lines pass through a small area at point P and 2 lines pass through an area of the same size at point Q. State the ratio of field strengths gP : gQ and the ratio rQ : rP of their distances from the centre. **[3]**

**10.** The Earth has surface field strength 9.81 N kg⁻¹ and radius 6.37 × 10⁶ m.

**(a)** Show that the mass of a uniform spherical planet of density ρ and radius R gives g proportional to R. **[3]**
**(b)** A planet has the same average density as the Earth and a radius 1.5 times larger. Calculate its surface field strength. **[2]**
**(c)** Determine the mass of the Earth from the data given. **[2]**

**11.** A moon orbits a planet in a circular orbit of radius 4.2 × 10⁸ m with a period of 1.5 × 10⁵ s. For a circular orbit, T² = 4π²r³/(GM), where M is the mass of the planet (given for this question).

**(a)** Calculate the mass of the planet. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the gravitational field strength of the planet at the moon's orbit. **[2]**
**(c)** A second moon orbits at a radius of 1.05 × 10⁹ m. Use Kepler's third law to find its period. **[2]**
**(d)** The planet has radius 7.0 × 10⁷ m. Calculate the field strength at its surface. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Planets move in elliptical orbits with the Sun at one focus [1]. The square of the orbital period is proportional to the cube of the mean orbital radius (T² ∝ r³) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the Sun at one focus is the point that separates a first-law answer from a vague "orbits are ellipses". For the third law, saying that the period is proportional to the radius cubed, without the squaring, loses the mark.

**2. (a)** F = Gm₁m₂/r² [1] = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.0 × 10³ × 2.0 × 10³ / 15² = **2.96 × 10⁻⁶ N** [1]
**(b)** One mass doubled gives a factor of 2, and r tripled gives a factor of 1/9, so F = 2/9 × 2.96 × 10⁻⁶ = **6.59 × 10⁻⁷ N** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A scaling argument shows full understanding, but state the factor for each change.

**3.** Kepler's third law: T² ∝ r³, so T₂ = T₁(r₂/r₁)^(3/2) [1]. r₂/r₁ = 1.07 × 10⁹ / 6.71 × 10⁸ = 1.595 [1]. (1.595)^(3/2) = 2.014 [1]. T₂ = 3.55 × 2.014 = **7.15 days** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The ratio form means no unit conversion is needed. Converting to seconds and back is allowed but gives more chances for slips.

**4.** The Sun-asteroid line covers equal areas in equal times [1]. At these two points, r₁v₁ = r₂v₂ [1]. v₂ = 1.4 × 10¹¹ × 3.6 × 10⁴ / 4.2 × 10¹¹ [1] = **1.20 × 10⁴ m s⁻¹** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The shortcut r₁v₁ = r₂v₂ only works where the velocity is perpendicular to the line to the Sun. Saying that is part of justifying the method.

**5. (a)** The gravitational force exerted on a small point mass at that point, divided by that mass [1].
**(b)** g = GM/R² [1] = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 4.87 × 10²⁴ / (6.05 × 10⁶)² = **8.87 N kg⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** W = mg = 450 × 8.87 = **3.99 × 10³ N** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition needs the words "per unit mass" and the idea of a small test mass. "The force of gravity at a point" is not enough.

**6.** r = 6.05 × 10⁶ + 2.4 × 10⁶ = 8.45 × 10⁶ m. F = GMm/r² [1] = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 4.87 × 10²⁴ × 950 / (8.45 × 10⁶)² [1] = **4.32 × 10³ N** [1]
The distance must be measured from the centre of Venus, since the planet acts as a point mass at its centre [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Using the height above the surface as r gives a wrong final value. Show the addition of the radius so the working is clear.

**7. (a)** At the null point, M_A/x² = M_B/(d − x)² [1]. So (d − x)/x = √(2.0 × 10²² / 1.8 × 10²³) = 1/3 [1]. x = d/(1 + 1/3) = **4.5 × 10⁸ m from A** [1]
**(b)** The point is 5.0 × 10⁸ m from A. g_A = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 1.8 × 10²³ / (5.0 × 10⁸)² = 4.80 × 10⁻⁵ N kg⁻¹ towards A, and g_B = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 2.0 × 10²² / (1.0 × 10⁸)² = 1.33 × 10⁻⁴ N kg⁻¹ towards B [1]. The fields oppose, so the resultant = 1.334 × 10⁻⁴ − 4.80 × 10⁻⁵ [1] = **8.54 × 10⁻⁵ N kg⁻¹ towards B** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Marks are earned by showing both individual field strengths before combining them. The direction is part of the answer. A resultant with no direction is incomplete for a vector quantity.

**8. (a)** g₀ = GM/R² = 9.813 N kg⁻¹ [1]. g at 2.0 km = GM/(R + 2000)² = 9.807 N kg⁻¹ [1]. Decrease = (9.813 − 9.807)/9.813 = **0.063 %** [1]
**(b)** At 2.0 km, g changes by under 0.1 %, so the lines are effectively parallel and equally spaced [1]. At 2000 km, g changes by a large fraction, so the lines converge and the field is radial, not uniform [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* An explanation that mentions the lines converging towards the centre is stronger than "g changes".

**9. (a)** The lines are parallel [1], equally spaced, and directed towards the planet's surface (downward) [1].
**(b)** The field strength is proportional to line density, so gP : gQ = 6 : 2 = **3 : 1** [1]. Since g ∝ 1/r², rQ²/rP² = 3 [1], so rQ : rP = √3 : 1 = **1.73 : 1** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A "describe" question wants each feature of the pattern named: direction, spacing and straightness.

**10. (a)** M = ρ × (4/3)πR³ [1]. g = GM/R² = G × ρ(4/3)πR³/R² [1] = (4/3)πGρR, so g ∝ R for constant ρ [1]
**(b)** g = 1.5 × 9.81 [1] = **14.7 N kg⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** M = gR²/G [1] = 9.81 × (6.37 × 10⁶)² / 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ = **5.97 × 10²⁴ kg** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* On "show that" questions, every algebraic step must appear. In (a), stating the volume formula and cancelling the powers of R is what earns the marks.

**11. (a)** M = 4π²r³/(GT²) [1] = 4π² × (4.2 × 10⁸)³ / (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × (1.5 × 10⁵)²) [1] = **1.95 × 10²⁷ kg** [1]
**(b)** g = GM/r² = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 1.95 × 10²⁷ / (4.2 × 10⁸)² [1] = **0.737 N kg⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** T₂ = T₁(r₂/r₁)^(3/2) = 1.5 × 10⁵ × (2.5)^(3/2) [1] = **5.93 × 10⁵ s** [1]
**(d)** g = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 1.95 × 10²⁷ / (7.0 × 10⁷)² [1] = **26.5 N kg⁻¹** [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Carry unrounded values forward between parts. If (b) uses a rounded mass, the third significant figure may drift. In (d) the radius is the planet's radius, not the orbital radius.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking r as the height rather than adding the planet's radius to it.
- Leaving out "per unit mass" and "small" from the definition of field strength.
- Giving a resultant field strength with no direction.
- Adding fields between two bodies where they oppose, or subtracting them outside where they add.
- Using r₁v₁ = r₂v₂ without saying it applies where the velocity is perpendicular to the line to the Sun.
- Writing T ∝ r³ instead of T² ∝ r³.
