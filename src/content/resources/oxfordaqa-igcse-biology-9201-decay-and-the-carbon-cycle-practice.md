## Section A

**1.**

**(a)** State why dead plant material decays. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the type of organism that is mainly responsible. **[1]**

**2.** Give the **three** conditions in which decay is fastest. **[3]**

**3.** Explain why decay is important for plants. **[2]**

**4.**

**(a)** Name the process by which green plants remove carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the **three** types of compound that contain the carbon in the bodies of plants and animals. **[1]**
**(c)** Give **two** processes that return carbon dioxide to the atmosphere. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** A student buried bags of 50.0 g of identical dead leaves for 21 days under three conditions.

| Bag | Condition | Dry mass left (g) |
|---|---|---|
| A | Warm, moist, with air | 15.0 |
| B | Warm, moist, in a sealed jar with little air | 38.0 |
| C | Cold, moist, with air | 44.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of the dry mass lost in bag A. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean rate of mass loss in bag B, in g per day. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the difference between bag A and bag B. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain the difference between bag A and bag C. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why the mass of the leaves decreases during decay, even though no leaf material is removed by hand. **[2]**

**7.** The table shows the carbon moving through a woodland in one year, in arbitrary units.

| Process | Carbon (units per year) |
|---|---|
| Removed from the air by photosynthesis | 900 |
| Returned by plant respiration | 330 |
| Returned by animal respiration | 60 |
| Returned by respiration of microorganisms | 480 |
| Returned by combustion | 30 |

**(a)** Calculate the total carbon returned to the air. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain what the answer shows about the woodland. **[1]**
**(c)** A nearby factory begins burning fossil fuel, adding 45 units of carbon per year to the air above the woodland. Calculate the net change in carbon in the air each year, assuming nothing else changes. **[2]**

**8.** Peat bogs are waterlogged and low in oxygen. Dead plants in peat bogs decay very slowly. Explain why. **[3]**

**9.** A plant is sealed in a clear jar with air. Explain why the amount of carbon dioxide in the jar rises at night and falls in bright daylight. **[3]**

**10.** A carbon atom is in carbon dioxide in the air. Describe a route by which it becomes part of a fox and then returns to the air as carbon dioxide after the fox dies. **[4]**

**11.** Explain why a stable community needs microorganisms that cause decay. **[2]**

**12.** Describe the carbon cycle. Include the removal of carbon dioxide from the air and the ways in which it is returned. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** It is broken down (digested) by microorganisms [1]
**(b)** Microorganisms (bacteria and fungi) [1]

**2.** Warm [1]; moist [1]; aerobic (with oxygen) [1]

**3.** Decay releases substances that plants need to grow [1]; so the materials are returned to the soil and can be used again [1]

**4. (a)** Photosynthesis [1]
**(b)** Carbohydrates, fats and proteins (all three needed) [1]
**(c)** Any two of the following, 1 mark each [2]: respiration (of plants, algae, animals or microorganisms); combustion of wood or fossil fuels

**5. (a)** (50.0 − 15.0) ÷ 50.0 × 100 [1] = **70%** [1]
**(b)** mass lost = 50.0 − 38.0 = 12.0 g; 12.0 ÷ 21 [1] = **0.57 g per day** [1]
**(c)** Bag B has little air (little oxygen), so the microorganisms respire aerobically less [1]; they are less active, so they digest the leaves more slowly [1]
**(d)** Bag C is cold, so the microorganisms are less active [1]; enzymes work more slowly at low temperature, so they digest the leaves more slowly [1]

**6.** Microorganisms digest the leaf material [1]; and respire, releasing the carbon as carbon dioxide (and water) into the air, so the dry mass falls [1]

**7. (a)** 330 + 60 + 480 + 30 = **900 units** [1]
**(b)** The woodland is stable: carbon removed equals carbon returned (a constant cycle) [1]
**(c)** Returned = 900 + 45 = 945 units [1]; net change = 945 − 900 = **+45 units** (carbon in the air increases) [1]

**8.** There is little oxygen, so the aerobic conditions that microorganisms need are missing [1]; microorganisms are less active (less aerobic respiration) [1]; so the plant material is digested very slowly and the carbon stays in the dead plants [1]

**9.** At night there is no photosynthesis, but the plant still respires, releasing carbon dioxide, so it rises [1]; in bright light the plant photosynthesises, taking in carbon dioxide [1]; photosynthesis is faster than respiration, so the amount of carbon dioxide falls [1]

**10.** Any four of the following, 1 mark each [4]: photosynthesis in plants removes carbon dioxide and makes carbohydrates; the carbon is passed to animals that eat the plants (as fats and proteins); the fox eats animals that fed on plants, so some carbon becomes part of the fox; when the fox dies, microorganisms and detritus feeders feed on its body; the microorganisms respire, releasing carbon dioxide into the air

**11.** They break down dead organisms and waste [1]; this releases substances that plants need, so the materials are cycled and removal is balanced by return [1]

**12.** Marking points, 1 mark each [6]:
- green plants and algae remove carbon dioxide from the air by photosynthesis
- the carbon is used to make carbohydrates, fats and proteins in the plants and algae
- when plants and algae are eaten, some carbon becomes part of the fats and proteins of animals
- plants, algae and animals respire, releasing carbon dioxide into the atmosphere
- when organisms die, microorganisms and detritus feeders feed on the bodies and waste, and the microorganisms respire, releasing carbon dioxide
- combustion of wood and fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide into the atmosphere

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "it rots" instead of saying microorganisms digest the material.
- Listing only warm and moist and forgetting aerobic.
- Saying plants do not respire, or that animals "breathe out" carbon without naming respiration.
- Not naming the form of the carbon at each stage.
- Giving the percentage as a mass, or dividing by the final mass instead of the starting mass.
- Ignoring combustion in carbon cycle answers.
