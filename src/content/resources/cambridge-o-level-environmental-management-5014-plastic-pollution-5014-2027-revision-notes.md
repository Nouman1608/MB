## Definitions: learn the exact wording

- **Conventional plastics**: made from fossil fuels; generally non-biodegradable.
- **Bioplastics**: fully or partly made from biological raw materials rather than from fossil fuels; can be biodegradable or non-biodegradable.
- **Biodegradable plastics**: designed to decompose in water or soil; decomposed by bacteria and fungi into water, biomass and gases (carbon dioxide and methane); decompose at different rates depending on biotic and abiotic factors.
- **Non-biodegradable plastics**: break down over a long period of time.
- **Microplastics**: less than 5 mm in length; formed when larger plastics break down; used in commercial products.

## Two questions, four labels

| Question | Answer A | Answer B |
|---|---|---|
| What is it made from? | Fossil fuels → conventional | Biological raw materials → bioplastic |
| What happens at the end? | Decomposed by microorganisms → biodegradable | Breaks down slowly into fragments → non-biodegradable |

A single plastic gets one label from each row. "Bioplastic" never tells you on its own whether it is biodegradable.

## Factors affecting decomposition rate

- **Biotic**: number and type of bacteria and fungi present.
- **Abiotic**: temperature, moisture, oxygen, light.
- Fast: warm, moist, aerated soil rich in microorganisms.
- Slow: cold, dark sea water or compacted landfill.
- No oxygen → methane may be released instead of carbon dioxide.

## Impacts on marine ecosystems (3.6.6)

| Impact | What happens | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Visual pollution | Litter on beaches and floating at sea | Fewer tourists; lost income; cleaning costs |
| Entanglement | Nets, ropes, straps and rings trap animals | Drowning, injury, inability to feed or escape |
| Mistaken for food | Bags look like jellyfish; fragments look like food items | Stomach fills → starvation; gut blocked or damaged |
| Bioaccumulation and biomagnification | Microplastics and attached chemicals eaten by small animals and passed up food chains | Highest amounts in top predators and seafood eaters |

## Strategies (3.6.7) with a limitation each

- **Alternative packaging**: paper, glass, metal, reusable containers / may cost more or be heavier to transport.
- **Avoiding single-use plastics**: reusable bags, bottles, cups, refills / depends on habit change.
- **Safe disposal**: collection and managed landfill / many areas lack services; landfill space limited.
- **Recycling**: collected and remade / sorting difficult; contamination; quality falls.
- **Legislation and enforcement**: bans, charges, microbead bans / needs enforcing; plastic crosses borders.

General point: these reduce new plastic entering the sea; none removes microplastics already in it.

## Calculations to recognise

- Percentage change in litter = (before − after) ÷ before × 100. Bottles falling from 80 to 52 on a beach is a 35% fall.
- Time to decompose = mass ÷ rate of mass loss. A 15 g item losing 0.3 g per day lasts 50 days.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Conventional plastic | Plastic made from fossil fuels, generally non-biodegradable |
| Bioplastic | Plastic fully or partly made from biological raw materials |
| Biodegradable | Able to be decomposed by bacteria and fungi |
| Non-biodegradable | Breaks down only over a long period of time |
| Microplastic | A plastic piece less than 5 mm long |
| Biomass | Organic matter made by living organisms, here the decomposers |
| Entanglement | Animals becoming trapped in plastic items |
| Single-use plastic | An item designed to be used once and thrown away |
| Legislation | Laws made by a government |
| Enforcement | Making sure laws are followed, for example through inspections and fines |

## Exam traps

- Not all bioplastics are biodegradable.
- Non-biodegradable does not mean "never breaks down".
- Microplastic limit is **5 mm**, not 5 cm or 1 mm.
- Biodegradable plastics release methane as well as carbon dioxide, a link to climate change.
- Entanglement and ingestion are separate impacts; name the one the question asks for.
- Recycling is one strategy; do not offer it as the answer to every plastic question.

## Self-test

1. What are conventional plastics made from?
2. Can a bioplastic be non-biodegradable?
3. Name the two groups of organisms that decompose biodegradable plastics.
4. Name the three products of decomposition of a biodegradable plastic.
5. Give two abiotic factors that affect how fast a biodegradable plastic decomposes.
6. State the size that defines a microplastic.
7. Give two ways microplastics enter the sea.
8. Explain why a turtle might swallow a plastic bag.
9. How can plastics harm top predators that never eat plastic directly?
10. Give one limitation of recycling.
11. Give one example of legislation used to reduce plastic pollution.
12. A beach had 120 plastic bottles before a deposit scheme and 78 after. Calculate the percentage fall.

## Answers

1. Fossil fuels.
2. Yes; bioplastics can be biodegradable or non-biodegradable.
3. Bacteria and fungi.
4. Water, biomass and gases (carbon dioxide and methane).
5. Any two of temperature, moisture, oxygen, light.
6. Less than 5 mm in length.
7. Larger plastics breaking down; use in commercial products that are washed away.
8. It can mistake the floating bag for a jellyfish, its food.
9. Microplastics and chemicals pass up the food chain and build up through biomagnification.
10. Many plastics are hard to sort or contaminated, and quality falls each time they are recycled.
11. A ban or a charge on plastic bags, or a ban on microbeads in products.
12. 120 − 78 = 42; 42 ÷ 120 × 100 = 35%.
