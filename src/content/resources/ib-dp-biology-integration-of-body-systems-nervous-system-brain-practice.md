## Questions

**1.** (a) List these in order from smallest to largest: organ system, tissue, cell, organism, organ. **[1]**
**(b)** State what is meant by system integration. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain what an emergent property is, using an example from an animal. **[2]**

**2.** State four differences between signalling by the nervous system and signalling by the endocrine system. **[4]**

**3.** Describe two examples of the blood system transporting a material between organs, naming the substance and the organs. **[4]**

**4.** (a) Distinguish between a conscious process and an unconscious process. **[2]**
**(b)** Give one example of each. **[2]**
**(c)** State the region of the spinal cord where integration of an unconscious process takes place. **[1]**

**5.** Describe the pain reflex arc that withdraws the hand from a sharp object. **[6]**

**6.** A pain signal travels 1.0 m from the foot to the spinal cord. In a myelinated fibre the speed is 50 m/s. In an unmyelinated fibre the speed is 1.0 m/s.
**(a)** Calculate the time taken in each fibre, in seconds. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times faster the myelinated fibre is. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why most of the fibres in a reflex pathway for sharp pain are expected to be myelinated. **[2]**

**7.** A micrograph shows a transverse section of a nerve. A scale bar 18 mm long represents 90 µm. The nerve measures 54 mm across on the micrograph. In a sampled area there are 150 myelinated and 90 unmyelinated fibres.
**(a)** Work out the magnification of the micrograph. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out the real diameter of the nerve in µm. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage of fibres that are myelinated. **[1]**
**(d)** State one structure visible in the section that protects the nerve fibres. **[1]**

**8.** In a ruler-drop test, a person catches a falling ruler. The distance it falls before being caught is measured over five trials, before and after a week of daily practice.

| | Distance fallen (cm) |
|---|---|
| Before practice | 21, 19, 18, 20, 17 |
| After practice | 16, 14, 13, 15, 12 |

Reaction time t is given by t = √(2d ÷ g), where d is distance in metres and g = 9.81 m/s².
**(a)** Calculate the reaction time for the mean distance before and after practice, to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in reaction time. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest two reasons why this response is slower than a withdrawal reflex. **[2]**

**9.** Twelve volunteers with cerebellar damage and twelve controls walked heel to toe along a line for 20 steps. The mean number of steps that left the line was 1.5 for the controls and 11.4 for the cerebellar group.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage of steps off the line for each group. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the mean for the cerebellar group is. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how the data relate to the role of the cerebellum. **[3]**
**(d)** State two limitations of drawing a conclusion from this investigation. **[2]**

**10.** A person has a complete injury to the spinal cord in the mid-back. A pinprick on the sole of the foot is not felt, yet the foot withdraws.
**(a)** Explain why the person does not feel the pinprick. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the foot can still withdraw. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the person cannot voluntarily move the leg. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain how this supports the idea that the spinal cord integrates unconscious processes. **[2]**

**11.** A goalkeeper dives to save a penalty kick.
**(a)** Outline how information from the kick leads to muscle contraction, from receptor to effector. **[4]**
**(b)** State two ways in which the cerebellum contributes. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the blood system supports the working muscles. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the save is an emergent property of the body. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism [1]
**(b)** Coordination of the parts of a system so that they work together to do an overall function [1]
**(c)** An emergent property is a feature of the whole that none of its parts has alone [1]; for example, the heart pumps blood although a single cardiac muscle cell cannot, or an animal can hunt only because senses, brain, muscles and circulation work together [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Credit tends to follow when the example states what the whole does that a part cannot; naming a system without that comparison is usually too vague.

**2.** Any four differences, each with both sides stated, for example:
Nervous uses electrical impulses along neurons, endocrine uses hormones carried in the blood [1]
Nervous acts on specific targets joined by neurons, endocrine acts on any cell with receptors for the hormone [1]
Nervous is very fast, endocrine is slower [1]
Nervous effects are short, endocrine effects usually last longer [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A one-sided statement such as "nerves are fast" is a weak comparison; state both systems in each point.

**3.** Any two, each with substance and organs, for example:
Oxygen [1] from lungs to muscles [1]
Glucose [1] from the small intestine or liver to the brain [1]
Urea from the liver to the kidneys; hormones from a gland to target cells; carbon dioxide from muscle to lungs **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Blood transports oxygen" earns little without the two organs; give origin and destination.

**4.** (a) A conscious process involves awareness and a decision by the cerebral hemispheres [1]; an unconscious process occurs without awareness or choice [1]
**(b)** Conscious: choosing to pick up a cup [1]. Unconscious: the pain withdrawal reflex [1]
**(c)** The grey matter [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* An example must be a process, not a body part; "reflex" alone is less secure than naming the withdrawal reflex.

**5.** Free sensory nerve endings in the skin of the hand act as pain receptors [1]; a sensory neuron carries the impulse to the spinal cord [1], entering through the dorsal root [1]; in the grey matter, an interneuron receives it across a synapse [1]; a motor neuron receives it across a second synapse and leaves through the ventral root [1]; the effector, a skeletal muscle, contracts and pulls the hand away [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Sequence matters in this kind of answer; use words such as "then" and name each structure so the route can be followed.

**6.** (a) Myelinated: 1.0 ÷ 50 = **0.020 s** [1]; unmyelinated: 1.0 ÷ 1.0 = **1.0 s** [1]
**(b)** 1.0 ÷ 0.020 = **50 times** [1]
**(c)** Myelinated fibres conduct impulses much faster [1], so the foot is withdrawn before further damage [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the division with units; a time given with no unit, or a ratio inverted, is a common way to lose credit.

**7.** (a) Magnification = 18 ÷ 0.090 [1] = **×200** [1]
**(b)** 54 ÷ 200 = 0.27 mm [1] = **270 µm** [1]
**(c)** 150 ÷ (150 + 90) × 100 = **62.5%** [1]
**(d)** The (connective tissue) protective sheath [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert the scale bar to the same unit as the image before dividing; the units of the final answer should be stated.

**8.** (a) Mean before = 19 cm, mean after = 14 cm [1]; t = √(2 × 0.19 ÷ 9.81) = **0.197 s** [1]; t = √(2 × 0.14 ÷ 9.81) = **0.169 s** [1]
**(b)** (0.169 − 0.197) ÷ 0.197 × 100 [1] = **−14.2%** (a decrease of about 14%) [1]
**(c)** The response is voluntary, so the signal goes to the cerebral hemispheres [1]; more neurons and synapses, and a longer pathway, add delay compared with the short reflex arc [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Calculate the mean before converting to metres, then keep consistent units; percentage change is divided by the original value.

**9.** (a) Controls: 1.5 ÷ 20 × 100 = **7.5%** [1]; cerebellar group: 11.4 ÷ 20 × 100 = **57.0%** [1]
**(b)** 11.4 ÷ 1.5 = **7.6 times** [1]
**(c)** Those with damage left the line much more often [1], showing poorer coordination and balance [1], consistent with the cerebellum coordinating muscle contractions and maintaining balance [1]
**(d)** Any two: small sample of twelve [1]; mean only, with no measure of spread [1]; damage may differ in extent or location; ages and fitness may differ **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A conclusion should refer to the data and to the function; saying only "the cerebellum controls movement" is too general to explain the evidence.

**10.** (a) The signal cannot travel up the cord past the injury [1]; feeling occurs only when the signal reaches the cerebral hemispheres [1]
**(b)** The pain receptor and sensory neuron send impulses into the cord below the injury [1]; an interneuron in the grey matter passes the signal to a motor neuron [1]; the motor neuron stimulates the muscle to contract without any signal from the brain [1]
**(c)** Impulses from the motor area of the cerebral hemisphere cannot pass down the cord past the injury [1]; the motor neurons below receive no voluntary instruction [1]
**(d)** The withdrawal happens without awareness [1]; integration occurs in the grey matter of the cord alone [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention where the pathway breaks (above or below the injury); an answer that says "the brain is damaged" does not match the information given.

**11.** (a) Photoreceptors in the eye detect the ball [1]; sensory neurons carry impulses to the brain [1]; the cerebral hemispheres combine the input with other information and send impulses down motor neurons [1]; motor neurons stimulate skeletal muscle to contract [1]
**(b)** Coordinates the timing and force of muscle contractions [1]; maintains balance during the dive [1]
**(c)** Blood delivers oxygen and glucose to the muscles [1]; it removes carbon dioxide (or heat) [1]
**(d)** The save depends on sensing, brain, muscles and circulation all being coordinated [1]; no single organ or system could produce it alone [1] **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* In extended questions credit is spread over several short points; give each part its own sentence so none is lost inside a long paragraph.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "brain" for the centre of a reflex arc.
- Missing the dorsal and ventral roots or mixing them up.
- Placing the interneuron in white matter.
- Giving a hierarchy in the wrong order, or leaving out organ systems.
- Describing transport without saying from which organ to which.
- Forgetting to convert units in magnification.
- Dividing by the new value in a percentage change.
- Not comparing both sides when asked for differences.
- Describing the cerebellum as starting movement instead of coordinating it.
