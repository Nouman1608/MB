**How is Cambridge O Level English Language 1123 assessed?** Two 2-hour papers, each worth 50 marks
and 50% of the qualification: Paper 1 Reading (comprehension, use of language, summary and a short
response) and Paper 2 Writing (directed writing and composition). These notes condense both papers
from the 1123 syllabus for 2024, 2025 and 2026.

Condensed for the final weeks. For the full explanation, use the
[English Language: Reading and Writing study guide](/resources/o-level-cambridge-english-language-reading-and-writing/).

## The two papers at a glance

| | Paper 1 – Reading | Paper 2 – Writing |
|---|---|---|
| Time | 2 hours | 2 hours |
| Marks | 50 | 50 |
| Weighting | 50% | 50% |
| Reading AO | 80% of the paper | 20% |
| Writing AO | 20% | 80% |

Both papers carry equal weight, and **both** test reading *and* writing — Paper 2 still awards 20% for reading, because the directed writing task requires you to use a source text.

## Paper 1 structure

**Section A — Comprehension and Use of Language (25 marks)**, based on Text A, a narrative text of about 900 words from the twentieth or twenty-first century.

| Question | Marks | Tests |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 Comprehension | 16 | Explicit **and** implicit meanings |
| Q2 Use of Language | 9 | How the writer achieves effects on the reader |

**Section B — Summary and Short response (25 marks)**, based on Text B, about 550–600 words.

| Question | Marks | Tests |
|---|---|---|
| Q3a Summary | 10 reading + 10 writing | Selecting information; organising it |
| Q3b Short response | 5 | Implicit meanings and attitude |

The summary must be **continuous writing of no more than 150 words**. Bullet points and exceeding the limit both cost marks.

**Worked example.** Source: "The scheme reduced congestion by 20%. It also cut air pollution. However, small shopkeepers reported a fall in trade, and some elderly residents found the new routes confusing." Select the points first — congestion fell by a fifth; air quality improved; small businesses lost trade; older residents struggled with the new routes — then join them into continuous prose in your own words: "Traffic levels dropped by a fifth and the air became cleaner, though small retailers saw takings decline and older people found the altered routes difficult to follow." All four points, no lifted phrasing.

## Paper 2 structure

**Section A — Directed Writing (25 marks).** Read one or two texts totalling about 400–450 words; produce a discursive, argumentative or persuasive **speech, email, report, letter or article** of about **250–350 words**.

Marks split 15 writing + 10 reading — the reading marks come from *using, developing and evaluating* the source material, not merely referring to it.

**Section B — Composition (25 marks).** One extended piece of about **350–450 words**, chosen from
four titles (two descriptive, two narrative); all 25 marks are for writing (W1–W5).

## The assessment objectives

**Reading (R1–R5):** explicit meanings · implicit meanings and attitude · analyse, evaluate and develop facts and opinions with support from the text · how writers achieve effects · select and use information for a purpose.

**Writing (W1–W5):** articulate experience and ideas · organise and structure for deliberate effect · range of vocabulary and sentence structures · **register appropriate to context** · accurate spelling, punctuation and grammar.

W5 means accuracy is directly credited — proofreading is not optional.

## Technique that maps to the marks

- **Q2 Use of Language:** quote briefly, then explain the **effect on the reader**. Naming a device alone scores nothing (R4). *Weak:* "The writer uses a metaphor to make it more interesting." *Strong:* "The metaphor 'the crowd was a river' suggests the people moved as one uncontrollable mass, emphasising the narrator's helplessness within it."
- **Q3a Summary:** identify every relevant point first, then write continuously in your own words within 150 words.
- **Directed Writing:** honour form, purpose **and** audience; develop and evaluate the source ideas rather than copying them.
- **Composition:** choose from **two descriptive and two narrative** options based on what you can sustain, not what sounds impressive — a simple plot told well beats an elaborate one told badly. (Argumentative writing belongs to the directed writing task, not composition.) Plan before writing; range and accuracy carry as much weight as content.

## Exam traps

- Exceeding 150 words on the summary, or writing it in bullets.
- Copying phrasing from the passage where own words are required.
- Ignoring register in directed writing — W4 is a stated objective.
- Writing outside 250–350 words on Paper 2 Section A.
- Treating Paper 2 as writing only, and so not using the source text.
- Dictionaries are **not permitted** in either paper.
- Recurring accuracy errors worth drilling: **subject–verb agreement**, **consistent tense**, sentence separation (avoiding comma splices), and **apostrophes for possession** against plurals.
- Read the questions **before** the passage, then read once for overall sense before locating and re-reading the relevant section.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should the composition be?**
About 350–450 words, according to the 1123 syllabus. Writing much more leaves less time to plan and
proofread, and accuracy (W5) is credited directly.

**Does the summary have to be in my own words?**
Yes, as far as possible. The summary is continuous writing of no more than 150 words, and it is also
marked for writing, so lifted phrases and bullet points both cost marks.

**Which paper should I practise first?**
Both carry 50%, so practise them equally. Paper 1 rewards close reading and selection; Paper 2
rewards control of form, audience and register.


## Self-test

1. How long is each paper and what is each worth?
2. How many marks does the Use of Language question carry?
3. What is the word limit for the summary?
4. What proportion of Paper 2 is awarded for reading?
5. Which assessment objective covers register?
6. Name the composition options on Paper 2 Section B.
7. Why does reading the questions before the passage help?
8. Describe the two-step method for writing a good summary.

**Answers:** 1. Both 2 hours, 50 marks, 50% each. 2. 9 marks. 3. No more than 150 words, written as continuous prose. 4. 20% — awarded for using, developing and evaluating the source text in the directed writing task. 5. W4 — use register appropriate to context. 6. A choice of four: two descriptive and two narrative (argumentative writing belongs to directed writing, not composition). 7. It focuses your first read of the passage on what you actually need to find, rather than absorbing every detail equally before knowing which parts matter. 8. List every relevant point first, then join them into continuous prose in your own words, excluding examples, repetition and personal opinion, and staying within the word limit.
