Condensed notes for sub-topic 5.3, Insurance: purposes, types of risk, pooling, premiums, documents and brokers.

## Four facts to start with

1. Insurance pays **compensation** for a loss, in return for a **premium**.
2. Purposes: risk reduction, compensation, financial protection, business confidence, investment.
3. Types of risk: consequential loss, employer's liability, fire, motor, premises, public liability, theft.
4. Documents in order: **proposal form**, **cover note**, **policy**. A **broker** arranges cover.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Insurer | Company that provides insurance |
| Insured | Person or business covered |
| Premium | Payment for insurance |
| Claim | Request for payment after a loss |
| Pooling | Many people paying into a fund that pays the few who suffer loss |
| Excess | Amount the insured pays on each claim |
| No-claims discount | Reduction in premium for years without a claim |
| Proposal form | Form giving details of the risk |
| Cover note | Temporary proof of cover |
| Policy | Formal contract of insurance |
| Broker | Independent agent who arranges insurance |

## Purposes

Risk reduction; compensation; financial protection; business confidence; investment.

## Types of risk

| Insurance | Covers |
|---|---|
| Consequential loss | Lost profit and extra costs after an event |
| Employer's liability | Injury to employees at work |
| Fire | Fire damage |
| Motor | Vehicles and harm caused by them |
| Premises | Buildings and contents |
| Public liability | Harm to the public |
| Theft | Stolen goods or money |

## Pooling and premiums

| Step | Calculation |
|---|---|
| Total premiums | Number insured × premium |
| Expected claims | Number of claims × average claim |
| Left for costs and profit | Premiums − claims |
| Premium | Sum insured × rate |
| Discount | Premium × discount % |

## Worked examples

**Pooling.** 1,000 shops × $300 = $300,000; 12 claims × $20,000 = $240,000; $60,000 left.

**Premium.** 0.5% of $80,000 = $400. With 20% no-claims discount: $400 − $80 = $320. With 10% loading instead: $440.

## Role of a broker

Compares insurers; gives advice; helps with forms and claims; paid by commission.

## Model answers to learn

**Why is the proposal form important?** The insurer uses the answers to decide whether to accept the risk and what premium to charge. False answers can lead to a refused claim.

**How does pooling work?** Many customers pay premiums into a fund. Only a few claim, so the fund pays them, and each customer pays only a small amount.

## Choosing the cover: practice grid

| Event | Insurance |
|---|---|
| Shop floods and goods are ruined | Premises (and stock cover) |
| A visitor trips on a loose carpet in the office | Public liability |
| An employee is hurt by a machine | Employer's liability |
| Business cannot trade for a month after a fire | Consequential loss |
| A lorry is damaged in a crash | Motor |
| Money is stolen from the till | Theft |

## Why businesses insure: model answer

Insurance gives financial protection. If a fire destroys a warehouse, the insurer pays compensation so the business does not have to find the whole cost itself. This gives the owner confidence to keep trading and makes banks more willing to lend.

## Premium steps

1. Premium = sum insured × rate.
2. Work out any discount: premium × discount %.
3. Subtract the discount (or add a loading).
4. Excess: the insurer pays the loss minus the excess.

Example: stock $60,000 at 0.5% = $300; 20% no-claims discount = $60; premium = $240.

## Benefits and limits of insurance

| Benefits | Limits |
|---|---|
| Compensation after a loss | Premiums are a regular cost |
| Business can keep trading | Some risks cannot be insured |
| Lenders and customers feel safer | Claims can be refused if the proposal form was dishonest |
| Spreads risk across many policyholders | Excess means the insured still pays part of each loss |

## Exam traps

- **Insurance prevents loss.** It compensates.
- **Employer's and public liability swapped.**
- **Cover note as policy.**
- **Everything can be insured.**
- **Percent taken from the wrong value.**
- **Broker as insurer's employee.**

## Self-test

1. What is a premium?
2. Name three purposes of insurance.
3. Which insurance covers injury to customers?
4. Which covers workers injured at work?
5. What is consequential loss insurance?
6. What is pooling?
7. Calculate the premium on $50,000 at 0.8%.
8. What is a cover note?
9. Name the three insurance documents in order.
10. What does a broker do?

**Answers**

1. The regular payment made for insurance.
2. Any three: risk reduction, compensation, financial protection, business confidence, investment.
3. Public liability.
4. Employer's liability.
5. Cover for lost profit and extra costs after an event such as a fire.
6. Many people paying into a common fund that pays the few who suffer loss.
7. 0.8% × 50,000 = $400.
8. A temporary document proving cover until the policy is issued.
9. Proposal form, cover note, policy.
10. Arranges insurance for the customer, compares insurers and gives advice.
