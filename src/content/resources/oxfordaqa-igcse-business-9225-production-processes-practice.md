All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Alder Cycles.

> **Case study: Alder Cycles**
>
> Alder Cycles makes bicycles. Its standard commuter bike is made on a
> production line with 25 workers; the line makes 300 bikes a day at a
> total daily cost of $18,000. Alder also makes a small number of
> hand-built racing frames, one at a time, to order for racing clubs.
> Alder holds an average of 3,000 parts in stock. It costs $2 per part
> each month to store them. The managing director is considering JIT,
> which would cut average stock to 500 parts but add $1,500 a month in
> extra delivery costs. Workers have also suggested a Kaizen scheme.
> One idea is to move the paint station, saving 6 minutes on each bike.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an example of job production? **[1]**
- A. A soft-drink bottling line
- B. A tailor making a suit for one customer
- C. A car assembly line
- D. A factory making thousands of identical plastic cups

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a feature of flow production? **[1]**
- A. Each item is different
- B. Products pass continuously along a line
- C. Items are made one at a time to order
- D. Teams make a whole product by themselves

**3.** Define the term "batch production". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** disadvantages of job production. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** advantage of cell production for workers. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate the number of bikes made per worker per day on Alder's production line. **[1]**

**7.** Calculate the cost of making one bike on the production line. **[2]**

**8.** Calculate the total time saved each day if the Kaizen idea saves 6 minutes on each of the 300 bikes. Give your answer in hours. **[2]**

**9.** Calculate the monthly saving for Alder if it adopts JIT, after allowing for the extra delivery costs. **[3]**

**10.** Explain **one** reason why job production is suitable for the racing frames. **[2]**

**11.** Analyse **one** possible benefit and **one** possible risk to Alder of adopting JIT. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate whether Alder should use flow production for all of its bicycles. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A method in which a group of identical products is made together [1] before the machines and workers switch to the next group [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: slow [1]; costly per item [1]; needs skilled, expensive workers [1]; difficult to plan [1]; few cost savings from machines [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Workers are in a small team responsible for a whole part of the product [1], so the work is more varied and workers may feel more involved and motivated [1]. **[2]**

**6.** 300 / 25 = **12** bikes per worker [1]. **[1]**

**7.** $18,000 / 300 [1] = **$60** [1]. **[2]**

**8.** 6 x 300 = 1,800 minutes [1]; 1,800 / 60 = **30 hours** [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Holding cost now = 3,000 x $2 = $6,000 [1]; with JIT = 500 x $2 = $1,000, saving $5,000 [1]; net saving = $5,000 - $1,500 = **$3,500** a month [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Each racing frame is made to a club's own requirements [1], and job production allows each frame to be built exactly to order by skilled workers, so a high price can be charged [1]. **[2]**

**11.** **Benefit:** storage costs fall from $6,000 to $1,000 a month [1], leaving a net saving of $3,500 even after paying $1,500 extra for deliveries [1]. Less stock also means less risk of parts being damaged or becoming out of date [1].
**Risk:** parts arrive just as they are needed, so a late delivery can stop the production line [1]; with 25 workers and a daily cost of $18,000, a stoppage would still need to be paid for [1], so Alder needs reliable suppliers [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **For flow production:** the commuter bike is standard and made at a low cost of $60 per bike [1]; flow production is fast, with 300 bikes a day [1]; consistent quality [1].
**Against:** the racing frames are one-off, made to order, so flow production could not make them [1]; setting up a line for small numbers would be very expensive [1]; a breakdown on the line could stop all production [1]; repetitive work may lower the motivation of workers [1].
**Judgement:** Alder should use flow production only for the commuter bikes, where demand is high and steady, and keep job production for racing frames [1], because the two products have different needs for variety and quantity, and a mix of methods allows each to be made at the right cost and quality [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the answer to Question 8 in minutes only; the question asks for hours.
- Forgetting to subtract the extra delivery cost from the holding-cost saving.
- Treating "JIT" as meaning no stock and no risk.
- Describing a production method without linking it to the bicycles in the case.
- Giving only one side for the evaluation question.
