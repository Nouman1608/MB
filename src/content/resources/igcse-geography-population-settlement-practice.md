## Section A

**1.** A country has a birth rate of 32 per 1000, a death rate of 11 per 1000 and net migration of −4 per 1000. Calculate the population change per 1000. **[2]**

**2.** State the formula for the dependency ratio and explain what a high value indicates. **[3]**

**3.** Distinguish between site and situation. **[2]**

---

## Section B

**4.** A country has a high birth rate and a rapidly falling death rate.

**(a)** Identify the stage of the demographic transition model. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why population grows fastest at this stage. **[4]**

**(c)** Explain three reasons why the birth rate falls in the following stage. **[3]**

**(d)** State two limitations of the demographic transition model. **[2]**

**5.** Many young adults migrate from a rural area of an LIC to the capital city.

**(a)** Give two push and two pull factors. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain two benefits and two problems for the **source** area. **[4]**

**(c)** Explain why the city's population grows faster than migration alone would suggest. **[2]**

**6.** A town has one department store but fourteen newsagents.

**(a)** Explain this difference using the terms *range* and *threshold population*. **[4]**

**(b)** Define sphere of influence. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Natural increase = 32 − 11 = 21 per 1000 [1].
Population change = 21 + (−4) = **+17 per 1000** [1].
*Forgetting migration is the commonest error.*

**2.** (under 15 + over 64) ÷ (15–64) × 100 [1]. A high value means a **large dependent population** [1] supported by a **relatively small working population**, increasing the tax burden on workers [1].

**3. Site** is the actual land a settlement is built on [1]; **situation** is its position relative to surrounding features and other settlements [1].

**4. (a)** **Stage 2** [1].

**(b)** The **death rate falls rapidly** because of improved medicine, sanitation, clean water and food supply [1] [1]. The **birth rate remains high** because attitudes, religion and the economic value of children change **far more slowly** [1]. The **gap between the two rates is therefore at its widest**, giving very rapid natural increase [1].

**(c)** Any three: **contraception** becomes available and accepted [1]; **female education and employment** increase [1]; **infant mortality falls**, so fewer births are needed for children to survive [1]; urbanisation means children are a cost rather than farm labour.

**(d)** Any two: it is **based on European experience** and may not apply everywhere [1]; it **ignores migration** [1]; it gives **no timescale**; it assumes all countries follow the same path.

**5. (a)** Push (any two): unemployment, poverty, poor services, lack of land, natural disaster [1] [1].
Pull (any two): jobs, higher wages, better healthcare and education, family already there [1] [1].

**(b)** Benefits: **remittances** are sent home, raising incomes [1]; **unemployment and pressure on resources fall** [1].
Problems: **brain drain** — the young and skilled leave [1]; **families are separated** and an ageing population is left behind [1].

**(c)** The migrants are **disproportionately of childbearing age** [1], so the city also experiences **high natural increase** on top of the migration itself [1].

**6. (a)** A department store has a **high threshold population** — it needs many customers to be viable [1] — and a **large range**, since people will travel far for it [1]. A newsagent has a **low threshold** and a **small range**, since people will not travel far for a newspaper [1], so many are needed to serve the town [1].

**(b)** The **area served by a settlement or service** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Omitting migration from a population change calculation.
- Saying birth rates fall in stage 2 — they fall in stage 3.
- Describing the DTM instead of explaining the mechanism.
- Evaluating migration from only one country's perspective.
- Confusing range with threshold population.
