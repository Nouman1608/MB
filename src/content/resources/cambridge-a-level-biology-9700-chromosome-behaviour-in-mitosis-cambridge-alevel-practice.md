## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** In which stage of mitosis do sister chromatids separate? **[1]**

A: Prophase

B: Metaphase

C: Anaphase

D: Telophase

**2.** Which statement about mitosis in plant cells is correct? **[1]**

A: Centrioles move to opposite poles in prophase.

B: A spindle forms although there are no centrioles.

C: The cell surface membrane furrows inwards during cytokinesis.

D: The nuclear envelope remains intact throughout.

**3.** State two events that happen to the nuclear envelope and nucleolus during mitosis. **[2]**

**4.** Describe the arrangement of chromosomes at metaphase. **[2]**

**5.** A cell has 10 chromosomes in G1. State the number of chromatids visible in prophase and the number of chromosomes at each pole in telophase. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why the chromatids appear V-shaped as they move in anaphase. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Describe the behaviour of the spindle from prophase to telophase in an animal cell. **[4]**

**8.** Compare cytokinesis in animal cells and plant cells. **[3]**

**9.** The descriptions below are of cells in a stained onion root tip squash. Identify the stage of each cell and give one feature that supports your answer.

**(a)** Short, thick chromosomes form a single line across the centre; no nuclear envelope is visible. **[2]**

**(b)** A round nucleus with a dark nucleolus; no individual chromosomes can be seen. **[2]**

**(c)** Two groups of V-shaped structures part-way between the centre and the ends of the cell. **[2]**

**(d)** Long thin threads are visible inside a nucleus whose outline is still clear. **[2]**

**10.** A student prepares a root tip squash.

**(a)** Explain why the root tip is warmed in dilute hydrochloric acid. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the tissue is stained. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the coverslip is pressed down gently. **[1]**

**11.** Describe what happens to chromosomes and the nuclear envelope during telophase. **[3]**

**12.** A student labels a cell as "anaphase" because "the chromosomes are being pulled apart". Explain how the description should be corrected. **[2]**

**13.** State three rules to follow when drawing a cell in mitosis from a microscope slide. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. Plant cells lack centrioles but still form a spindle; A and C describe animal cells; the envelope breaks down in all cells. **[1]**

**3.** Any two: the nuclear envelope breaks down at the end of prophase [1]; the nucleolus disappears in prophase [1]; the envelope re-forms in telophase; nucleoli reappear in telophase. **[2]**

**4.** The chromosomes line up in a single plane across the equator of the spindle [1], each attached to spindle microtubules by its centromere [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Prophase: **20** chromatids [1]; telophase: **10** chromosomes at each pole [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Spindle microtubules attached at the centromere shorten, so the centromere moves first [1], and the arms of the chromatid trail behind, forming a V [1]. **[2]**

**7.** In prophase the centrioles move to opposite poles and the spindle of microtubules forms between them [1]. In metaphase microtubules attach to the centromere of each chromosome [1]. In anaphase the attached microtubules shorten, pulling sister chromatids to opposite poles [1]. In telophase the spindle breaks down [1]. **[4]**

**8.** In both, the cytoplasm divides to form two cells, usually starting in late anaphase or telophase [1]. In animal cells the cell surface membrane is drawn inwards at the equator until the cell is pinched in two [1]. In plant cells vesicles fuse at the equator to form a cell plate, which becomes new cell surface membranes and a new wall [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* "Compare" needs a similarity as well as differences.

**9. (a)** Metaphase [1]; chromosomes in a single line on the equator (or no nuclear envelope) [1].

**(b)** Interphase [1]; intact nucleus with nucleolus and no visible chromosomes [1].

**(c)** Anaphase [1]; chromatids moving to opposite poles, centromere first [1].

**(d)** Prophase [1]; chromosomes condensing and becoming visible while the nuclear envelope is still present [1]. **[8]**

**10. (a)** It breaks down the material holding the cells together [1], so the cells separate and spread into a single layer when squashed (it also stops further division) [1].

**(b)** Chromosomes are colourless, so a stain that binds to DNA makes them visible [1].

**(c)** To spread the cells into a single layer so that individual cells and chromosomes can be seen [1]. **[4]**

**11.** The chromatids, now chromosomes, arrive at the poles [1]. They uncoil and become less visible [1]. A nuclear envelope re-forms around each set [1]. **[3]**

**12.** In anaphase the centromeres divide and sister chromatids separate [1]; they move to opposite poles, so "sister chromatids are being pulled apart" is correct, not whole chromosomes [1]. **[2]**

**13.** Any three: use a sharp pencil [1]; use single, continuous lines with no shading [1]; draw only what is seen, large enough to show detail [1]; use ruled label lines that do not cross. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a stage without the feature that supports it.
- Writing "chromosomes separate" for anaphase.
- Giving the plant cell centrioles, or saying it cannot form a spindle.
- Describing the envelope breaking down at the start of prophase.
- Forgetting the similarity in a "Compare" question.
