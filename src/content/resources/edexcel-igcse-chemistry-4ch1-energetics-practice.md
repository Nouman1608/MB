These questions cover sub-topic 3(a) Energetics of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024): points 3.1 to 3.8. Points 3.5C, 3.6C and 3.7C are Paper 2 only, and questions on them are marked **(Paper 2 only)**. Use 4.2 J/g/°C for the specific heat capacity of water and solutions, and take 1 cm³ of solution as 1 g. Revise with the [energetics study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-energetics/) and the [physical chemistry revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-physical-chemistry-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** State what is meant by an endothermic reaction. **[1]**

**2.** The temperature of a mixture rises when two solutions are mixed. State whether the reaction is exothermic or endothermic and give the sign of ΔH. **[2]**

**3.** Calculate the heat energy needed to warm 200 g of water by 9.0 °C. **[2]**

**4.** Give two ways of reducing heat loss when measuring the temperature change of a reaction in solution. **[2]**

**5.** (Paper 2 only) State whether bond breaking is exothermic or endothermic. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** A student investigates the reaction between zinc powder and copper(II) sulfate solution. She adds excess zinc to 50 cm³ of 0.50 mol/dm³ copper(II) sulfate solution. The temperature rises by 12.0 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the heat energy released. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of moles of copper(II) sulfate. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the molar enthalpy change in kJ/mol, with its sign. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why excess zinc is used. **[1]**

**7.** A student burns propan-1-ol (Mr = 60) in a spirit burner to heat 150 g of water in a can. The temperature of the water rises by 28 °C. The burner loses 0.60 g in mass.

**(a)** Calculate the heat energy gained by the water. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the moles of propan-1-ol burned. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the molar enthalpy change of combustion in kJ/mol. **[2]**
**(d)** The data book value is about -2,020 kJ/mol. Give two reasons why the experimental value is less negative. **[2]**

**8.** A student dissolves 5.35 g of ammonium chloride (Mr = 53.5) in 100 g of water. The temperature falls by 3.5 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the heat energy absorbed. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the molar enthalpy change, with its sign, and state what the sign shows. **[3]**

**9.** (Paper 2 only) Draw and label an energy level diagram for an endothermic reaction. **[3]**

**10.** (Paper 2 only) Hydrogen reacts with bromine: H₂ + Br₂ → 2HBr. The bond energies are H-H 436, Br-Br 193 and H-Br 366 kJ/mol.

**(a)** Calculate the energy needed to break the bonds in the reactants. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy released when the bonds in the products form. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate ΔH for the reaction and state whether it is exothermic or endothermic. **[2]**

**11.** (Paper 2 only) Methane reacts with chlorine in sunlight: CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl. The bond energies are C-H 413, Cl-Cl 243, C-Cl 346 and H-Cl 432 kJ/mol.

**(a)** Calculate ΔH for this reaction. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain, in terms of bond energies, why the reaction is exothermic. **[1]**

**12.** Describe how you would carry out an experiment to compare the temperature change when 2.0 g of each of three different salts dissolves in 50 cm³ of water. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** A reaction that takes in heat energy from the surroundings [1].

**2.** Exothermic [1]; ΔH is negative [1].

**3.** Q = mcΔT = 200 x 4.2 x 9.0 [1] = 7,560 J (7.56 kJ) [1].

**4.** Any two from: use a polystyrene (insulated) cup [1]; use a lid [1]; stir to even out the temperature [1]; use a draught shield or insulate the container [1].

**5.** Endothermic [1].

**6. (a)** Q = 50 x 4.2 x 12.0 [1] = 2,520 J = 2.52 kJ [1].
**(b)** Moles = 0.50 x 50 / 1,000 = 0.025 mol [1].
**(c)** ΔH = -2.52 / 0.025 [1] = -101 kJ/mol (-100.8) [1].
**(d)** So that all of the copper(II) sulfate reacts (zinc is not the limiting reagent) [1].

**7. (a)** Q = 150 x 4.2 x 28 [1] = 17,640 J (17.64 kJ) [1].
**(b)** Moles = 0.60 / 60 = 0.010 mol [1].
**(c)** ΔH = -17.64 / 0.010 [1] = -1,764 kJ/mol [1].
**(d)** Heat is lost to the surroundings (air) [1]; incomplete combustion, so less energy is released (or the can absorbs heat) [1].

**8. (a)** Q = 100 x 4.2 x 3.5 [1] = 1,470 J (1.47 kJ) [1].
**(b)** Moles = 5.35 / 53.5 = 0.100 mol [1]. ΔH = +1.47 / 0.100 = +14.7 kJ/mol [1]. The positive sign shows the dissolving is endothermic [1].

**9.** Axes labelled energy (vertical) and progress of reaction (horizontal) [1]. Reactants shown as a line at a lower energy than the products [1]. An upward arrow between the two levels labelled ΔH, positive [1].

**10. (a)** 436 + 193 = 629 kJ [1].
**(b)** 2 x 366 = 732 kJ [1].
**(c)** ΔH = 629 - 732 = -103 kJ/mol [1]; exothermic, since more energy is released making bonds than is needed to break them [1].

**11. (a)** Bonds broken: C-H (413) + Cl-Cl (243) = 656 kJ [1]. Bonds made: C-Cl (346) + H-Cl (432) = 778 kJ [1]. ΔH = 656 - 778 = -122 kJ/mol [1].
**(b)** More energy is released when the new bonds form (778 kJ) than is needed to break the old bonds (656 kJ) [1].

**12.** Use a measuring cylinder to put 50 cm³ of water into a polystyrene cup and record the starting temperature [1]. Add 2.0 g of the first salt and stir [1]. Record the highest or lowest temperature reached [1]. Repeat for each salt with a clean cup and fresh water at the same starting temperature, keeping the volume and mass the same (a fair test), and calculate each temperature change [1].
*Marking note:* A fair test needs the same volume of water and the same mass of salt each time.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the mass of the reactant instead of the mass of the water in Q = mcΔT.
- Leaving the answer in joules when the question asks for kJ/mol.
- Giving ΔH the wrong sign for the direction of the temperature change.
- Subtracting bonds made minus bonds broken instead of broken minus made.
- Saying that experimental heat losses make the result "too big".
- Writing "energy is created" for an exothermic reaction.
