## Questions

**1.** A pulse of height +5.0 cm travels towards a pulse of height −3.5 cm on the same rope. State the principle that gives the rope's shape when they overlap, and find the displacement when their centres coincide. **[2]**

**2.** (a) Define coherent sources. **[1]** (b) Explain why light from two separate filament lamps does not form stable fringes on a screen. **[2]**

**3.** Two ultrasound transmitters emit in phase at 40 kHz. The speed of sound is 340 m s⁻¹. A detector at P is 214.0 mm from one transmitter and 231.0 mm from the other. At Q the distances are 120.00 mm and 124.25 mm. Determine the type of interference at P and at Q. **[4]**

**4.** Sodium light of wavelength 589 nm illuminates a double slit of separation 0.45 mm. The screen is 1.35 m away. (a) Calculate the fringe separation. **[2]** (b) Find the distance from the central maximum to the fourth bright fringe. **[1]** (c) The screen is moved to half the distance and the slit separation is doubled. Find the new fringe separation. **[2]**

**5.** In a double-slit experiment d = 0.20 mm and D = 2.50 m. The distance across six adjacent fringe spacings is 4.5 cm. (a) Calculate the wavelength of the light. **[3]** (b) Explain why the measurement is taken across several spacings. **[1]**

**6.** A laser beam lights both slits of a double slit. (a) Explain why a single laser beam gives coherent sources. **[2]** (b) At a bright fringe each slit alone would give intensity I. State the intensity with both slits open. **[2]** (c) One slit is covered. Describe the change on the screen. **[2]**

**7.** (HL only) Light of wavelength 600 nm passes through a single slit of width 0.10 mm. The screen is 2.4 m away. Calculate the angle to the first minimum and the width of the central maximum. **[4]**

**8.** (HL only) A slit of width 0.20 mm gives a single-slit pattern. The slit is narrowed to 0.10 mm. (a) State and explain the change in the width of the central maximum. **[2]** (b) State the change in its intensity. **[1]** (c) State the effect of using longer-wavelength light with the original slit. **[1]**

**9.** (HL only) A double slit has d = 0.45 mm and b = 0.15 mm. The light has wavelength 600 nm and the screen is 1.8 m away. (a) Calculate the fringe separation. **[2]** (b) Find the distance from the centre to the first minimum of the single-slit pattern. **[2]** (c) State which double-slit order is missing. **[1]** (d) State how many bright fringes lie within the central maximum of the single-slit pattern. **[1]** (e) Describe how fringe brightness varies across the central maximum. **[1]**

**10.** (HL only) A grating has 700 lines per mm. A second-order maximum of monochromatic light appears at 40.0° to the normal. (a) Calculate the wavelength. **[3]** (b) State the highest order observed and the total number of maxima. **[2]**

**11.** (HL only) White light (400 nm to 700 nm) is incident normally on a grating with 500 lines per mm. (a) Describe and explain the central maximum. **[2]** (b) Calculate the angles of the first-order violet and red light. **[2]** (c) Show that the second-order and third-order spectra overlap. **[3]** (d) Give one advantage of a grating over a double slit for measuring wavelength. **[1]**

**12.** (HL only) Light containing wavelengths 450 nm and 640 nm falls normally on a grating with 800 lines per mm. Calculate the first-order angles for both, and determine which wavelengths have a second order. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Principle of superposition: resultant displacement is the sum of the individual displacements [1]. +5.0 + (−3.5) = **+1.5 cm [1]**. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* the sign must be carried through the sum; giving 8.5 cm shows the sign was ignored.

**2.** (a) Sources with the same frequency and a constant phase difference [1].
(b) Each lamp emits in random bursts, so the phase difference between them keeps changing [1]. Maxima and minima shift about too quickly to see, so the screen is evenly lit [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "different colours" is not an explanation; the answer must mention the changing phase relationship.

**3.** λ = 340 / 40 000 = 8.5 mm [1]. P: path difference = 17.0 mm = 2λ [1], so **constructive [1]**. Q: path difference = 4.25 mm = ½λ, so **destructive [1]**. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* showing the division of path difference by λ is what supports the conclusion; a bare "constructive" is weak.

**4.** (a) s = λD/d = (589 × 10⁻⁹ × 1.35)/(0.45 × 10⁻³) [1] = **1.77 × 10⁻³ m [1]**.
(b) 4s = **7.07 mm [1]**.
(c) s ∝ D/d, which becomes (1/2)/2 = ¼ of the original [1], so s = **0.442 mm [1]**. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* conversion of mm and nm to metres is the usual place where answers go wrong by powers of ten.

**5.** (a) s = 0.045/6 = 7.5 × 10⁻³ m [1]. λ = sd/D = (7.5 × 10⁻³ × 0.20 × 10⁻³)/2.50 [1] = **6.0 × 10⁻⁷ m (600 nm) [1]**.
(b) A fixed reading uncertainty is shared by six spacings, so the percentage uncertainty in s is smaller [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "to be more accurate" alone is vague; say what is reduced.

**6.** (a) Both slits receive light from the same wavefront of one source [1], so any change in the laser affects both beams alike and their phase difference is constant [1].
(b) Amplitude doubles [1]; intensity depends on amplitude squared, so intensity = **4I [1]**.
(c) The fringes disappear [1] because there is no second beam to interfere with; the old bright positions fall to ¼ of their brightness (and the screen is lit by one beam) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* stating "2I" is a common slip; the answer should link intensity to amplitude squared.

**7.** θ = λ/b = (600 × 10⁻⁹)/(0.10 × 10⁻³) [1] = **6.0 × 10⁻³ rad [1]**. Distance to first minimum = Dθ = 2.4 × 6.0 × 10⁻³ = 14.4 mm. Width = 2 × 14.4 = **28.8 mm [1]**. The factor of two is applied for both sides [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the angle θ = λ/b reaches only the first minimum, so the full width needs the doubling, and units (rad, mm) should be stated.

**8.** (a) The central maximum becomes **twice as wide [1]** because θ = λ/b, so halving b doubles θ [1].
(b) Intensity **decreases [1]** because less light passes through the slit.
(c) The central maximum becomes **wider [1]** since θ increases with λ. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "narrower slit, narrower pattern" is the commonly lost point; link width and spread inversely.

**9.** (a) s = λD/d = (600 × 10⁻⁹ × 1.8)/(0.45 × 10⁻³) [1] = **2.4 mm [1]**.
(b) y = λD/b = (600 × 10⁻⁹ × 1.8)/(0.15 × 10⁻³) [1] = **7.2 mm [1]**.
(c) d/b = 3, so the **third order** is missing [1].
(d) Orders 0, ±1, ±2: **5** bright fringes [1].
(e) The fringes are brightest at the centre and fade towards the edges of the central maximum, following the single-slit envelope [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* the envelope must be linked to the slit width b, and the fringe spacing to d; mixing them loses the explanation.

**10.** (a) d = 10⁻³/700 = 1.43 × 10⁻⁶ m [1]. λ = d sin θ/n = 1.43 × 10⁻⁶ × sin 40.0°/2 [1] = **4.59 × 10⁻⁷ m (459 nm) [1]**.
(b) d/λ = 3.11, so the highest order is **3 [1]**; maxima n = 0, ±1, ±2, ±3 give **7 [1]**. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* the order is a whole number below d/λ; rounding 3.11 up to 4 breaks sin θ ≤ 1.

**11.** (a) The central maximum is **white [1]**: at θ = 0 the path difference is zero for every wavelength, so all colours are in phase [1].
(b) d = 2.00 × 10⁻⁶ m. Violet: sin θ = 400 × 10⁻⁹/2.00 × 10⁻⁶ = 0.200, θ = **11.5° [1]**. Red: sin θ = 0.350, θ = **20.5° [1]**.
(c) Third-order violet: sin θ = 3 × 0.200 = 0.600, θ = 36.9° [1]. Second-order red: sin θ = 2 × 0.350 = 0.700, θ = 44.4° [1]. The third-order violet (36.9°) is at a smaller angle than the second-order red (44.4°) and larger than the second-order violet (23.6°), so the spectra overlap [1].
(d) The maxima are narrower and brighter, so angles can be measured more precisely [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* "show that" asks for both angles to be calculated and compared; one value alone is not a demonstration.

**12.** d = 10⁻³/800 = 1.25 × 10⁻⁶ m [1]. First order, 450 nm: sin θ = 0.360, θ = **21.1° [1]**; 640 nm: sin θ = 0.512, θ = **30.8° [1]**. Second order, 450 nm: sin θ = 0.720, θ = **46.1° [1]**; 640 nm: sin θ = 1.024, which exceeds 1, so **no second order [1]**. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* showing that sin θ exceeds 1 is how a missing order is justified; saying "too big" is not enough.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not converting mm, nm and lines per mm to metres.
- Using nλ for dark fringes.
- Swapping the slit width b and the slit separation d.
- Forgetting to double the angle or distance to get the full width of the central maximum.
- Saying intensity doubles when the amplitude doubles.
- Rounding the maximum order up instead of down.
- Not explaining coherence in terms of constant phase difference.
- Giving angles in the wrong order for violet and red.
