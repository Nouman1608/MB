## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Explain the difference between a direct cost and an indirect cost. **[2]**

**2.** State the section of a manufacturer's statements in which each cost appears.

**(a)** Salary of the factory supervisor. **[1]**

**(b)** Raw materials used. **[1]**

**(c)** Salary of the sales manager. **[1]**

**(d)** Depreciation of factory machinery. **[1]**

**3.** Opening inventory of raw materials was $11,400, purchases of raw materials were $63,800, carriage inwards was $1,450 and closing inventory of raw materials was $12,650. Calculate the cost of raw materials consumed. **[2]**

**4.** Direct material is $64,000. Direct wages paid were $47,300 and $700 of direct wages is owing at the year end. Calculate prime cost. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why closing work in progress is deducted in the manufacturing account. **[2]**

**6.** Which item is included in factory overheads?

A: carriage inwards

B: carriage outwards

C: factory rent

D: direct wages

**[1]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Norland Ceramics provides the following for the year ended 30 June 2029:

| Item | $ |
|---|---|
| Inventory of raw materials 1 July 2028 | 7,800 |
| Inventory of raw materials 30 June 2029 | 9,200 |
| Work in progress 1 July 2028 | 5,400 |
| Work in progress 30 June 2029 | 6,100 |
| Purchases of raw materials | 58,400 |
| Carriage inwards | 1,600 |
| Direct wages | 66,000 |
| Indirect factory wages | 21,500 |
| Factory heat and light paid | 9,300 |
| Rent of premises | 18,000 |

Additional information:

- factory heat and light of $450 is owing at 30 June 2029
- two-thirds of the rent relates to the factory
- factory machinery has a net book value of $45,000 at 1 July 2028 and is depreciated at 20% a year on the reducing balance.

Prepare the manufacturing account for the year ended 30 June 2029. **[8]**

**8.** Norland Ceramics' factory cost of production for the year ended 30 June 2029 was $176,150. Revenue was $268,000. Inventory of finished goods was $18,300 at 1 July 2028 and $15,900 at 30 June 2029. Office expenses were: office share of rent $6,000; office salaries $31,200; selling expenses $8,700.

**(a)** Calculate the gross profit. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the profit for the year. **[2]**

**9.** At 30 June 2029 Norland Ceramics also had: trade receivables $22,400, against which an allowance for irrecoverable debts of 5% is kept; factory insurance prepaid $600; bank $4,750. Inventories are as given in Question 7 and Question 8.

**(a)** Prepare the current assets section of the statement of financial position at 30 June 2029. **[5]**

**(b)** State where the $450 of factory heat and light owing appears in the statement of financial position. **[1]**

**10.** Tavistock Fabrics prepared draft statements showing a factory cost of production of $212,000 and a gross profit of $96,000. The following errors were then found:

- carriage outwards of $2,400 had been included in factory overheads
- closing work in progress had been understated by $1,800
- depreciation of office equipment of $3,000 had been charged in the manufacturing account.

**(a)** Calculate the corrected factory cost of production. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the corrected gross profit, assuming finished goods inventory is unchanged. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why only one of the three errors changes the profit for the year. **[2]**

**11.** Some figures from the manufacturing account of Amberley Metals are missing.

| Item | $ |
|---|---|
| Prime cost | 140,000 |
| Direct labour | 72,500 |
| Opening inventory of raw materials | 9,000 |
| Closing inventory of raw materials | 8,200 |
| Carriage inwards | 1,100 |
| Factory overheads | 38,600 |
| Opening work in progress | 4,900 |
| Factory cost of production | 181,300 |

**(a)** Calculate the cost of raw materials consumed. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the purchases of raw materials. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the closing work in progress. **[2]**

**12.** Hollis Bakery makes bread in a building that is two-thirds bakery and one-third office. The owner suggests charging all of the $27,000 building rent to the manufacturing account "to make the office look cheaper".

**(a)** Explain the effect of the owner's suggestion on the factory cost of production and on the profit for the year. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain **one** reason why the rent should be split correctly. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A direct cost can be traced to each unit produced, such as the raw materials in it [1]. An indirect cost is incurred for the factory as a whole and cannot be traced to a single unit, such as the supervisor's wages [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** Manufacturing account: factory overheads [1].

**(b)** Manufacturing account: direct material (cost of raw materials consumed) [1].

**(c)** Statement of profit or loss: expenses [1].

**(d)** Manufacturing account: factory overheads [1]. **[4]**

**3.** 11,400 + 63,800 + 1,450 - 12,650 [1] = **$64,000** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Direct labour = 47,300 + 700 = $48,000 [1]. Prime cost = 64,000 + 48,000 = **$112,000** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Closing work in progress is goods not yet finished at the year end [1]. Their cost will be completed and charged next year, so it is carried forward as a current asset instead of being included in this year's factory cost of production [1]. **[2]**

**6.** C [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* Carriage inwards is part of direct material, direct wages are part of prime cost and carriage outwards is a selling expense.

**7.**

```
Norland Ceramics
Manufacturing account for the year ended 30 June 2029
                                                USD        USD
Cost of raw materials
  Inventory of raw materials 1 July 2028      7,800
  Purchases of raw materials                 58,400
  Carriage inwards                            1,600
                                             67,800
  Less Inventory of raw materials 30 June     9,200
Cost of raw materials consumed                          58,600
Direct wages                                            66,000
Prime cost                                             124,600
Factory overheads
  Indirect factory wages                     21,500
  Heat and light (9,300 + 450)                9,750
  Rent (18,000 x 2/3)                        12,000
  Depreciation of machinery (45,000 x 20%)    9,000
                                                        52,250
                                                       176,850
Add Work in progress 1 July 2028                         5,400
                                                       182,250
Less Work in progress 30 June 2029                       6,100
Factory cost of production                             176,150
```

Marks: cost of raw materials consumed $58,600 [1]; prime cost $124,600 [1]; heat and light $9,750 [1]; rent $12,000 [1]; depreciation $9,000 [1]; total factory overheads $52,250 [1]; work in progress added and deducted correctly [1]; factory cost of production $176,150 [1]. **[8]**

*Examiner insight:* Reducing balance depreciation uses the net book value, not cost.

**8. (a)** Cost of sales = 18,300 + 176,150 - 15,900 [1] = $178,550 [1]. Gross profit = 268,000 - 178,550 = **$89,450** [1].

**(b)** Expenses = 6,000 + 31,200 + 8,700 = $45,900 [1]. Profit for the year = 89,450 - 45,900 = **$43,550** [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)**

```
Current assets                                  USD        USD
  Inventory: raw materials                    9,200
  Inventory: work in progress                 6,100
  Inventory: finished goods                  15,900
  Trade receivables                22,400
  Less Allowance (5%)               1,120    21,280
  Other receivables: insurance prepaid          600
  Bank                                        4,750
                                                        57,830
```

Marks: three inventories at closing values [1]; allowance $1,120 [1]; net trade receivables $21,280 [1]; prepaid insurance and bank [1]; total $57,830 [1].

**(b)** Under current liabilities, as an other payable [1]. **[6]**

**10. (a)** Remove the misclassified items: 212,000 - 2,400 - 3,000 [1]; correct the work in progress: - 1,800 [1]; factory cost of production = **$204,800** [1].

**(b)** Cost of sales falls by $7,200, so gross profit = 96,000 + 7,200 = **$103,200** [1].

**(c)** The carriage outwards and the office depreciation are still expenses; they only move from the manufacturing account to the statement of profit or loss, so total costs are unchanged [1]. The work in progress correction reduces this year's costs by $1,800, so profit for the year rises by $1,800 [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Gross profit rises by $7,200 but profit for the year by only $1,800; questions often test this difference.

**11. (a)** 140,000 - 72,500 = **$67,500** [1].

**(b)** Purchases = 67,500 - 9,000 - 1,100 + 8,200 [1] = **$65,600** [1].

**(c)** Closing work in progress = 140,000 + 38,600 + 4,900 - 181,300 [1] = **$2,200** [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Work backwards through the layout in order; write the layout first and fill in the known lines.

**12. (a)** The factory cost of production rises by the office share, 27,000 x 1/3 = $9,000 [1], so cost of sales rises and gross profit falls by $9,000 [1]. Office expenses fall by the same $9,000, so the profit for the year is unchanged [1].

**(b)** Any one, explained: the factory cost of production would overstate the cost of making the bread [1], so decisions based on it, such as setting selling prices, would be wrong [1]; the gross profit margin would be understated, misleading users comparing it with other years or bakeries. **[5]**
