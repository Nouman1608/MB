## What the guide states

- Criterion C is about collecting, processing and interpreting qualitative or quantitative data, and explaining conclusions that have been appropriately reached.
- It develops analytical thinking, which is used to evaluate the method and discuss improvements or extensions.
- Maximum 8, in bands 1-2, 3-4, 5-6 and 7-8.
- In the on-screen examination, the Investigation skills task is worth 50 marks: 25 for Criterion B and 25 for Criterion C.

## The five Year 5 strands

| Strand | Top band (7-8) wording | Plain words |
|---|---|---|
| i | correctly collect, organise, transform and present data in numerical and/or visual forms | tidy table, calculation, graph |
| ii | accurately interpret data and explain results using correct scientific reasoning | pattern, numbers, science |
| iii | evaluate the validity of a hypothesis based on the outcome | does the evidence back it? how far? |
| iv | evaluate the validity of the method | did the method give good enough data? |
| v | explain improvements or extensions to the method | what to change, how, why |

## Key terms

- **Transforming data**: processing raw data into a form suitable for visual representation, for example averaging or calculating a quantity.
- **Numerical forms**: calculations such as averaging or finding values from a graph or table.
- **Visual forms**: graphs of a type that fits the data (line graph, bar graph, histogram, pie chart).
- **Validity of the method**: whether the method collects sufficient valid data to answer the question. This includes whether instruments measure what they should, the experimental conditions and fair testing.
- **Extension**: development for further inquiry related to the outcome.
- **Anomaly**: a result that does not fit the pattern of the others.

## Presenting data: checklist

- Title for every table and graph.
- Column headings with units.
- The same number of decimal places in each column.
- The independent variable first or on the horizontal axis.
- Even scales that use most of the grid.
- Axes labelled with quantity and unit.
- Accurate points, and a best-fit line or smooth curve.
- Anomalies marked and explained.

## Calculations

- **Mean** = sum of usable values ÷ number of values. Example: 142, 150, 146 gives (142 + 150 + 146) ÷ 3 = 146.
- **Range** of repeats = largest − smallest.
- **Rate** from time: 1 ÷ time. A time of 40.5 s gives 1 ÷ 40.5 = 0.0247 s⁻¹, or 24.7 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹.
- **Percentage change** = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. From 146 s to 27 s: (27 − 146) ÷ 146 × 100 = −81.5%.
- **Ratio**: 37.0 ÷ 6.85 = 5.40, so the rate is about 5.4 times larger.
- Give final answers to three significant figures unless the question says otherwise, and include the unit.

## Dealing with anomalies

1. Compare the spread of repeats at each value.
2. A value far from the others, while the others agree, is an anomaly.
3. Say which result it is, exclude it from the mean and say so.
4. Suggest a cause (for example a late stopwatch start).
5. If time allows, repeat that measurement.

## Interpreting results: pattern, numbers, science

1. **Pattern**: say what happens to the dependent variable as the independent variable changes.
2. **Numbers**: quote values from your table or graph, with units, and show a difference or a ratio.
3. **Science**: explain with a scientific idea (particle collisions, energy transfer, enzyme shape, forces).

A good sentence: "As the temperature rose from 10 °C to 50 °C, the mean time fell from 146 s to 27 s because the particles had more kinetic energy and collided more often."

## Evaluating the hypothesis

- **Verdict**: supported, partly supported or not supported.
- **Evidence**: two or three data points or a trend.
- **Limit**: the range tested, the quality of the data, and what else could explain the pattern.
- Do not write "prove". Use "support".

## Evaluating the method

For each point, give strength or limitation, then its effect on the data.

- **Repeats and consistency**: how close were the repeats?
- **Control of variables**: which variable drifted?
- **Measuring instruments**: precision against the size of the quantity.
- **Human error**: reaction time, judging an end point. Note the percentage effect on short times.
- **Range and number of values**: enough to show the shape?

## Improvements and extensions

For each improvement: **what** to change, **how**, and **why it helps**.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| use better equipment | use a digital thermometer that reads to 0.1 °C, so that the water temperature can be set within 0.5 °C of the target |
| be more careful | start the stopwatch as the tablet touches the water, and have a second person watch the end point |
| do more repeats | do five trials at each temperature to give a more reliable mean |

An extension asks a new question: for example, does crushing the tablet change the time at each temperature?

## Must-know distinctions

- **Describe vs interpret vs explain a result**: describe says what the graph does. Interpret says what it shows. Explain gives the science.
- **Accuracy vs precision**: accuracy is closeness to the true value. Precision is closeness of repeats to each other.
- **Random vs systematic error**: random errors scatter results either way. Systematic errors push all results the same way.
- **Validity of hypothesis vs method**: one is about the idea. The other is about the quality of the data.

## Where marks are usually lost

- No units, or a different precision in one column.
- Graph with joined dots and no best-fit line.
- A trend described without numbers.
- No scientific reason given for the pattern.
- Vague "human error" with no effect on the result.
- A conclusion that goes beyond the range tested.
- Improvements with no detail.

## Quick self-test

1. Three repeat times are 31 s, 29 s and 30 s. Calculate the mean.
2. Another set is 20 s, 21 s and 38 s. Which value is the anomaly, and what mean do you report?
3. State two features of a good graph axis.
4. Write one sentence that interprets this: mean time was 120 s at 20 °C and 60 s at 40 °C.
5. A stopwatch is started 0.4 s late. Why is this a larger problem for a time of 12 s than for 120 s?

### Answers

1. (31 + 29 + 30) ÷ 3 = 30 s.
2. The 38 s value is the anomaly. The mean of 20 s and 21 s is 20.5 s, and the exclusion should be stated.
3. Any two of: label with quantity and unit; even scale; scale that uses more than half the grid; independent variable on the horizontal axis.
4. When the temperature doubled from 20 °C to 40 °C, the mean time halved from 120 s to 60 s, so the rate doubled.
5. 0.4 s is 0.4 ÷ 12 × 100 = 3.3% of 12 s, but only 0.4 ÷ 120 × 100 = 0.33% of 120 s, so the same error has a ten times larger percentage effect on the short time.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I know when a result is an anomaly?**
Look at the spread of repeats at each value. A repeat that is much further from the others than the repeats at other values differ from each other is an anomaly.

**Should I always draw a line of best fit?**
Draw one when the points show a trend. Choose a straight line or a smooth curve to suit the data and do not force the line through the origin without reason.

**What is the difference between an improvement and an extension?**
An improvement makes the same investigation more valid or reliable. An extension asks a new question that follows from the result.
