**What do these questions practise?** All of Section 5 Industrial development in Cambridge IGCSE
Pakistan Studies 0448 Paper 2: definitions, the ten listed industries and their locations, the
formal and informal sectors, government promotion and EPZs, call centres and tourism. Answers are
written in mark-scheme style so you can see where each mark is earned.

## Section A: recall

**1.** Define *secondary industry* and give **one** example from Pakistan. **[2]**

**2.** What is meant by *value-added*? **[1]**

**3.** Name the town that is the centre of the sports goods and surgical instruments industries. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** differences between large-scale and cottage industry. **[2]**

**5.** Name **one** export processing zone in Pakistan. **[1]**

**6.** Name **one** natural and **one** cultural tourist attraction in Pakistan. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Industry that processes raw materials or manufactures goods [1]; example: sugar refining,
cement, textiles, fertiliser or steel [1].

**2.** The increase in value of a product at each stage of processing [1].

**3.** Sialkot [1].

**4.** Any two: large-scale uses many workers, cottage uses family members [1]; large-scale uses
heavy machinery, cottage uses simple tools [1]; large-scale needs large capital, cottage little [1];
large-scale is in factories, cottage in homes [1].

**5.** Karachi EPZ (accept Sialkot or Gwadar) [1].

**6.** Natural: Hunza, Swat, Naran–Kaghan, K2 or a beach [1]. Cultural: Mohenjo-daro, Taxila,
Lahore Fort or Badshahi Mosque [1].

**7.** Ginning separates the lint from the seed [1]; spinning turns lint into yarn [1]; weaving or
knitting turns yarn into cloth [1]; cloth is dyed or printed and stitched into garments, towels or
bed linen [1].

**8.** Limestone is heavy and bulky [1], and a large weight is lost in processing, so transporting
the raw material is costly [1]. Locating at the deposit cuts transport costs; gypsum, another raw
material, is often found nearby [1].

**9.** Pakistan imports most of its iron ore and coking coal [1], which arrive by ship [1], so a site
beside Port Qasim cuts transport costs; Karachi also offers flat land, labour and a large market
[1].

**10.** Any three: street vending of food or goods [1]; repairing vehicles, shoes or electrical
items [1]; transport such as rickshaws [1]; domestic work [1]; collecting and recycling waste [1].

**11.** Any two developed: building infrastructure such as roads, power and industrial estates [1],
which lowers costs for firms [1]; tax holidays and concessions [1], which attract investment [1];
loans and credit for small industry [1], helping new firms start [1]; training institutes [1],
which raise worker skills [1].

**12.** Characteristics (up to 3): enclosed, secure site [1]; good infrastructure including power,
water and roads [1]; duty-free import of machinery and raw materials [1]; tax concessions [1];
output mainly for export [1]. Reasons (up to 3): attract foreign investment [1]; earn foreign
exchange [1]; create jobs [1]; transfer technology and skills [1]. Maximum 5.

**13.** Natural gas is the main raw material (feedstock) for making urea fertiliser [1] and is
also used as the fuel to power the process [1]. A site on or near a gas field or pipeline gives a
cheap, reliable supply and saves transport costs; the product is then sent by road and rail to
farming areas of Punjab and Sindh [1].

**14.** Positive: provides jobs for many unskilled and migrant workers [1]; needs little capital
[1]; supplies cheap goods and services to the poor [1]; feeds parts and components into formal
exporters such as sports goods [1]. Negative: low and irregular incomes keep families in poverty
[1]; no tax revenue for government [1]; poor safety and use of child labour [1]; poor quality may
harm exports [1]. Judgement needed. Maximum 6.

**15.** For: spectacular scenery and mountaineering (K2, Hunza) attract domestic and foreign visitors
[1]; jobs in hotels, guiding and transport [1]; foreign exchange raises GDP [1]; the Karakoram
Highway gives road access [1]. Against: remote areas with landslides and road closures [1]; very
seasonal, mostly summer [1]; security concerns deter some foreign tourists [1]; risk of litter,
deforestation and pressure on water [1]. A judgement on feasibility, for example with investment
in access and eco-tourism, is needed for full marks. Maximum 6.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many location factors should I give?**
Usually two or three, each explained, unless the mark
allocation suggests more.

**Can I use the same example for several questions?**
Yes. Sialkot, Karachi EPZ and the cotton chain
are strong examples across many parts of this section.
