These questions practise the 3.3 Skills of AQA GCSE English Literature (8702) one strand at a time,
using two short extracts from 19th-century novels on the 8702 list (both out of copyright) and two
original poems. Mark tariffs are for self-checking; the real papers set longer essay questions that
combine all these skills. Apply the same method to whichever texts you study.

## Section A: Comprehension and critical reading

**Extract 1** (Stevenson, *The Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde*, Chapter 1; Enfield describes
Hyde):

> "He is not easy to describe. There is something wrong with his appearance; something displeasing,
> something down-right detestable. I never saw a man I so disliked, and yet I scarce know why."

**1.** State one thing that is explicit and one thing that is implied in Enfield's description. **[2]**

**2.** Explain what this description suggests about Enfield's motivation for telling the story. **[2]**

**3.** Identify a theme of the novella that this extract introduces, and explain how. **[2]**

**4.** "Stevenson makes Hyde frightening by refusing to describe him." Give one reason to agree and one
reason to question this view. **[4]**

## Section B: Evaluating a writer's choices and using context

**Extract 2** (Dickens, *A Christmas Carol*, Stave One):

> "Hard and sharp as flint, from which no steel had ever struck out generous fire; secret, and
> self-contained, and solitary as an oyster."

**5.** Analyse how Dickens uses language in this extract to present Scrooge. Use subject terminology.
**[6]**

**6.** Explain how one piece of contextual knowledge could inform a reader's evaluation of this
description. **[3]**

## Section C: Comparing texts

> **Poem A: Lighthouse**
> All night it speaks one word
> across the water, the same word,
> patient as a parent
> who knows the child will not listen
> and says it anyway.

> **Poem B: The Night Shift**
> She leaves as we come home,
> a coat on a hook, then no coat.
> In the morning, toast already cut,
> a note in her careful hand:
> *Eat something. Sleep.*

**7.** Compare how the poets present care in Poem A and Poem B. **[8]**

## Section D: Writing skills

**8.** Rewrite this paragraph in a critical style with accurate Standard English. **[6]**

> "In the story jekyll is a scientist and he makes a potion and turns into hyde which is really evil, I
> think Stevenson is saying people are good and bad. its quite scary because you dont know whats going
> to happen."

**9.** Explain the difference between "summarise" and "analyse" as purposes for writing about
literature, as listed in the specification. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Explicit [1]: Enfield dislikes Hyde intensely ("I never saw a man I so disliked"). Implied [1]:
something about Hyde is unnatural or inhuman, beyond physical appearance.

**2.** Any reasonable inference [2]: Enfield is disturbed and cannot rationalise his reaction, so he tells
the story to make sense of it; or he wants Utterson to share his unease.

**3.** For example [1 for theme, 1 for how]: the limits of rational explanation or the hidden nature of
evil, introduced through a respectable man's inability to say why Hyde is "detestable".

**4.** Agree [2]: vagueness ("something wrong") lets readers imagine the worst, and the repetition of
"something" makes the unnamed quality more threatening. Question [2]: the novella does describe Hyde's
violence in detail (for example, the trampling of the child and the Carew murder), so fear also comes
from his actions, not only from mystery.

**5.** Level-style guidance (6 marks):
- 5–6: Analyses several choices with precise terminology and effect: the simile "Hard and sharp as
  flint" makes Scrooge cold and unyielding; the extended image of steel striking fire implies no one has
  ever drawn warmth from him; "generous fire" pairs warmth with generosity; the tricolon "secret, and
  self-contained, and solitary", with repeated "s" sounds and polysyndeton, emphasises his isolation; the
  closing simile "solitary as an oyster" is comic as well as critical, and hints that something valuable
  may be hidden inside him.
- 3–4: Explains one or two techniques with some effect.
- 1–2: Identifies a technique or paraphrases.

**6.** Any one, linked to evaluation [3]: Victorian debates about the poor and the 1834 Poor Law make
Scrooge's isolation a social criticism, not just a character flaw; or the novella's Christian framing of
Christmas charity makes his lack of "generous fire" a moral failing; or the oyster image prepares for a
redemption plot that suits a Christmas story's purpose.

**7.** Level-style guidance (8 marks):
- 7–8: Sustained comparison of ideas and methods. Poem A presents care as constant and unrewarded,
  through the extended metaphor of a lighthouse and the simile "patient as a parent"; the repetition of
  "the same word" suggests endurance. Poem B presents care through absence and small actions: the
  contrast "a coat on a hook, then no coat" shows the mother's departure, and the italicised note
  presents love as practical instruction. Both use a single scene and end on a moment of speaking or
  writing, but Poem A generalises while Poem B is domestic and specific.
- 4–6: Clear comparison of ideas with some analysis of methods.
- 1–3: Describes each poem separately, with simple comment.

**8.** Model answer [6]: "Stevenson presents Jekyll as a respected scientist whose experiment releases
Hyde, a figure of pure evil. Through this transformation, Stevenson suggests that human nature is divided
between good and evil impulses. The novella's suspense comes from withholding information: because the
reader, like Utterson, does not know the connection between the two men, the tension builds until the
final revelation." [Credit capital letters for names, correct sentence boundaries and apostrophes (2);
critical style and present tense (2); analysis replacing retelling (2).]

**9.** Summarising gives a brief account of what happens or what a text says [1]; analysing explains how
the writer's choices of language, form and structure create meanings and effects [1].

## Frequently asked questions

**Which skills matter most for the top grades?**
AO2 analysis carries 42.5% of the 8702 marks, so explaining the effects of writers' choices is essential.
It must be combined with a clear, personal argument (AO1).

**How do I practise comparison without my anthology?**
Use any two short poems on a shared theme, as in Section C, and compare ideas, methods and effects
throughout your answer.

**Is technical accuracy marked in every answer?**
AO4 is 5% of the qualification overall, but it is not assessed equally in every question; write accurately
throughout as a habit.
