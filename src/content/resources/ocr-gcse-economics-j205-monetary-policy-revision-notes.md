Recall notes for section 3.6 of OCR GCSE (9-1) Economics (J205, Version 2.0, June 2026), examined in Component 02. The [monetary policy study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-economics-j205-monetary-policy/) gives full explanations; the [monetary policy practice questions](/resources/ocr-gcse-economics-j205-monetary-policy-practice/) let you apply them. Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- Explain **monetary policy** and how it can achieve economic objectives.
- **Analyse** its effects on **growth, employment and price stability**.
- **Evaluate** its effects on **consumer spending, borrowing, saving and investment**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Monetary policy | Using interest rates (and sometimes the money supply) to influence spending |
| Central bank | The bank that runs monetary policy; in the UK, the Bank of England |
| Bank Rate | The Bank of England's key interest rate, which guides other rates |
| Monetary Policy Committee | The Bank of England group that sets Bank Rate |
| Inflation target | The rate the central bank aims for: 2% CPI in the UK |
| Expansionary monetary policy | Cutting rates to raise demand |
| Contractionary monetary policy | Raising rates to reduce demand |
| Time lag | The delay before a policy change has its full effect |
| Real interest rate | Interest rate adjusted for inflation |

## UK facts to know

- The **Bank of England** sets Bank Rate, independently of the government since 1997.
- The **government sets the target**: 2% CPI inflation.
- Independence aims to make the target **believable**, which helps keep expectations, and so inflation, low.

## The route, step by step

Bank Rate changes → lenders change loan, mortgage and savings rates → households and firms change borrowing, saving, spending and investment → total demand changes → output, jobs and prices respond.

## Rate rise in one line per objective

- **Growth** slows, as spending and investment fall.
- **Employment** may fall, as firms need fewer workers.
- **Inflation** eases, as demand-pull pressure falls.

A rate cut does the reverse. The trade-off: lower inflation may cost jobs and growth.

**Full chain to learn (rate cut, employment):** Bank Rate falls → mortgage and loan rates fall → borrowers have more disposable income and new credit is cheaper → consumer spending and investment rise → firms sell more and expand output → firms hire more workers → unemployment falls. Each arrow is a step an examiner looks for; missing one weakens the analysis.

## Methods

| Task | Method |
|---|---|
| Extra yearly interest | Loan × change in rate |
| Per month | Yearly figure ÷ 12 |
| Economy-wide effect | Number of borrowers × extra interest each |
| Investment test | Invest if expected return > interest rate |
| Real return | (1 + interest rate) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1 |

## Worked reminders

**Household.** A £220,000 variable mortgage with a 0.75 point rise costs 220,000 × 0.0075 = **£1,650** more a year, **£137.50** a month.

**Economy.** If 1.5 million households face that rise, spending power falls by 1.5m × £1,650 = **£2.475bn** a year.

**Saver.** Savings at 3% with inflation at 4.5%: 1.03 ÷ 1.045 − 1 = **−1.4%** real return.

## What strengthens or weakens the effect

| Stronger | Weaker |
|---|---|
| Many variable-rate borrowers | Most mortgages fixed |
| High household and business debt | Firms funding investment from profits |
| Large change in rates | Very low confidence (cuts) or very high confidence (rises) |
| Lenders pass on changes in full | Lenders keep part of the change |
| Demand-pull inflation | Cost-push inflation from world prices |

## Other evaluation points

- **Time lags**: often a year or more before the full effect.
- **Winners and losers**: rises help savers, hurt borrowers; cuts do the reverse.
- **One rate for every region**, whether it suits each or not.
- **Strengths**: quick to decide, small steps, reversible, independent.

## Exam traps

- Monetary policy is **interest rates**; fiscal policy is **tax and spending**.
- The Bank of England sets the rate; the government sets the **target**.
- Not everyone is affected at once: fixed-rate deals delay the impact.
- Higher rates cannot lower world energy prices.
- Follow every step of the chain; do not jump straight to the objective.

## Self-test

1. Define monetary policy.
2. Who sets Bank Rate in the UK, and who sets the inflation target?
3. What is the UK's inflation target?
4. Explain how a rise in interest rates affects saving.
5. A £220,000 variable-rate mortgage faces a 0.75 percentage point rise. Calculate the extra interest per month.
6. If 1.5 million households each pay £1,650 more interest a year, what is the total effect on spending power?
7. A project's expected return is 6.2%. Should the firm borrow to fund it at 5.5%? At 7%?
8. Savings earn 3% while inflation is 4.5%. What is the approximate real return?
9. Why may a rate rise do little to reduce cost-push inflation?
10. Give one reason the effects of a rate change are delayed.
11. State one conflict between objectives when rates rise.
12. Explain why a rate cut may not increase consumer spending.

### Answers

1. Using **interest rates** (and sometimes the money supply) to influence total spending and meet economic objectives.
2. The **Bank of England** (its Monetary Policy Committee) sets Bank Rate; the **government** sets the target.
3. **2% CPI inflation.**
4. Saving becomes **more rewarding**, so households save a larger share of income.
5. 220,000 × 0.0075 = £1,650 a year, so **£137.50** a month.
6. 1.5 million × £1,650 = **£2.475bn** a year.
7. At 5.5%: **yes**, the return exceeds the cost. At 7%: **no**.
8. About **−1.4%**.
9. The cause is higher **costs** such as world energy prices, which interest rates cannot change; rates only reduce **demand**.
10. Any one: **fixed-rate** deals; lenders slow to **pass on** changes; firms' investment plans take time to change.
11. Lower inflation may come with **slower growth and higher unemployment**.
12. Households may have low **confidence** or already high **debts**, so they save or repay rather than spend; lenders may not pass on the cut.
