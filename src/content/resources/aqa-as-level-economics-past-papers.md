This page is a guide to AQA AS Level Economics (7135) past papers for June 2024 and June 2025. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and a short account of what examiners saw, written by us from AQA's reports on the examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [AQA AS Economics (7135) specification page](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/economics/as-level/economics-7135)
- [AQA AS Economics (7135) assessment resources](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/economics/as-level/economics-7135/assessment-resources), where question papers, mark schemes and reports are listed by series
- [AQA grade boundaries](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries) and the [grade boundaries archive](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries/archive)

Use the three documents together. Sit the paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which explains the levels of response and the accepted definitions. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it shows why answers that looked reasonable lost marks. The notes below are a quick way into that report.

Check which specification a paper was set on. AS Economics (7135) is linear and untiered, for first teaching from September 2015 and first examined in June 2016. Both series covered here were set on that specification. The AS papers and boundaries are separate from those of the A-level (7136), which has its own page.

### Exam structure

| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7135/1 | The operation of markets and market failure | 1 hour 30 minutes | 70 | 50% |
| 7135/2 | The national economy in a global context | 1 hour 30 minutes | 70 | 50% |

Each paper has a Section A of 20 multiple-choice questions (20 marks) and a Section B of context questions (50 marks), where students choose one of two contexts. Section B builds from a definition and a calculation to a diagram and ends with a 25-mark extended response.

## June 2025

Paper 1 offered contexts on flood defences and coffee, and Paper 2 offered a choice that included population changes and the UK economy. Examiners on both papers repeated three messages: diagrams earn credit only when accurate, labelled and used in the answer; extract data should be used selectively, with units and years; and the last 25-mark question rewards analysis first, then judgements supported by it.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (7135/1), Section A: the hardest questions were on a concentration ratio when the firms in the top three changed, and on what a shift of a production possibility boundary does and does not show.
- Paper 1, Section B: a definition of opportunity cost that left out the next best alternative; a calculation not given to the requested decimal place; "significant comparison" answers that joined the highest and lowest points into one feature; supply and demand diagrams with missing labels or both curves shifted; reasons for rising house prices that were listed but not developed, with explanations of rising demand stronger than those of limited supply. Fewer than 5% reached the top level on the 25-mark question.
- Paper 2 (7135/2), Section A: the multiplier calculation, the accelerator confused with the multiplier, and the effect of different changes on saving and on interest-rate decisions.
- Paper 2, Section B: national debt defined as the deficit instead of the stock of outstanding debt; unit errors between millions and billions when working out debt per head; comparisons made from the peaks and troughs of two data series rather than over the whole period, without units or years; pie chart sectors drawn inaccurately; productivity and production used as if they meant the same; a supply-side shock defined imprecisely.

**What went well:** precise opportunity cost definitions from many students, supply-curve shift diagrams that were fully labelled (around 40% full marks), the chain of reasoning from a labour shortage to higher wages and cost-push inflation, and the 25-mark question on education and training spending, where over 30% reached level 4 or above.

## June 2024

Paper 1 had contexts on travel and tourism and on eye care, and Paper 2 on troubling times for the UK economy and on the levelling up agenda. The multiple-choice sections were done well, and the reports focus on precise definitions, calculation care and the structure of extended answers. Our notes here cover the general sections and the first part of each context that we read in the reports.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (7135/1): an incomplete definition of scarce resources, since the idea of a resource was missed even where "limited" was given; calculation slips such as the wrong currency symbol or the wrong number of nights; significant-feature answers outside the data tolerance or combining two features in one; diagram marks lost to missing labels or an inaccurate movement along a curve; developed demand reasons for travel and tourism that stopped at the middle of level 2.
- Paper 2 (7135/2): definitions that were nearly right but imprecise, with 64% not fully credited on the first and over 94% on the second; calculations that stopped at an annual figure when a monthly one was asked for; comparisons joining the highest and lowest points into one feature; extended answers that quoted the extracts heavily but had little underlying analysis.

**What went well:** the recognition of both market failures in the Paper 1 contexts, the calculation questions on Paper 1 (a mean of 80%), and the set-up of a basic AD/AS diagram (90% earned at least one mark).

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find the June 2025 7135 papers and mark schemes?**
On AQA's assessment-resources page for 7135, linked above. Papers, mark schemes and reports are listed by series.

**Are the grade thresholds the same every year?**
No. AQA sets them after each series. Compare the tables: an A grade needed 102 out of 140 in June 2025 and 97 in June 2024. Do not assume a future series will match.

**Is there a grade A* at AS?**
No. AS grades run from A to E, so the tables above stop at A.

**What are notional component boundaries?**
They are illustrative boundaries for individual papers. The grade is awarded on the total out of 140.

**How should I practise the "significant feature" and comparison questions?**
Both reports say to give one feature in each answer space, to use the whole period shown, and to include units and years.
