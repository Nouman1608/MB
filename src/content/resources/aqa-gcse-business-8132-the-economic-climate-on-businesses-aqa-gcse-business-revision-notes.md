## Core recall

### The three parts of 3.2.3

1. **Interest rates:** effects on businesses using overdrafts and loans; effects on consumer and business spending.
2. **Level of employment:** how and why businesses are affected when it changes.
3. **Consumer spending:** how demand changes as incomes fluctuate.

Not required: economic theory about how or why interest rates change.

### Interest rates: the chains

**Rates rise → borrowing businesses**
- Interest payments rise → profit falls.
- More cash paid out → cash flow tighter.
- Borrowing to expand less attractive → investment delayed.

**Rates rise → consumers**
- Credit dearer → fewer big purchases on credit (cars, furniture, holidays).
- Variable-rate mortgage payments rise → less left to spend.
- Saving pays more → some people save instead of spend.

**Rates rise → businesses as customers**
- Firms delay buying equipment and vehicles → suppliers of those goods lose orders.

**Who gains:** businesses with cash savings; businesses with little or no borrowing selling essentials for cash.

Rates falling: reverse each chain.

### Employment: two sides

| Change | Demand side | Labour side |
|---|---|---|
| Employment rises | More income, spending rises | Harder to recruit, wages pushed up, staff may leave |
| Employment falls | Less income, spending falls (non-essentials most) | Easier to recruit, less pressure on wages, staff stay |

### Incomes and demand

| Type of product | When incomes fall | When incomes rise |
|---|---|---|
| Luxuries and expensive items | Demand falls a lot | Demand rises a lot |
| Essentials | Demand changes little | Demand changes little |
| Budget alternatives | Demand may rise | Demand may fall as people trade up |

### Business responses

- Finance: reduce borrowing, delay investment, build cash reserves.
- Marketing: cheaper ranges, promotions, payment plans, or premium lines when incomes rise.
- Operations: cut output and stock when demand falls; raise them when it grows.
- Human resources: freeze recruitment or cut hours; raise pay to keep staff when employment is high.

### Quick worked reminder

Figures invented. A business has a £150,000 loan. The rate rises from 5% to 7%. Interest rises from £7,500 to £10,500, an extra £3,000 a year or £250 a month.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Economic climate | The state of the economy in which a business operates |
| Interest rate | The cost of borrowing, and reward for saving, as a percentage |
| Overdraft | Arrangement allowing a business to take more out of its bank account than it holds, up to a limit |
| Loan | A sum borrowed and repaid with interest over an agreed period |
| Consumer spending | Spending by households on goods and services |
| Business spending | Spending by businesses, for example on equipment and supplies |
| Level of employment | The number of people in work |
| Unemployment | People able and willing to work who cannot find a job |
| Income | Money received by households, mainly from wages |
| Luxury | A product people can do without, bought more when incomes rise |
| Essential | A product people need, bought in similar amounts whatever their income |

## Exam traps

- **Explaining causes of rate changes.** Not required; focus on effects.
- **"High rates are bad for all businesses."** Savers and cash businesses can gain.
- **Ignoring how much a business borrows.** The effect depends on the size of its debt.
- **One-sided employment answers.** Cover demand and labour.
- **Treating all products alike.** Luxuries, essentials and budget alternatives react differently.
- **Generic answers.** Use the business's product, customers and finance in the case.
- **Percentage points.** A rise from 4% to 6% is 2 percentage points; work interest out on the amount borrowed.

## Self-test

1. What does the specification say is not required about interest rates?
2. Give two ways a rise in interest rates affects a business with a large overdraft.
3. Why might a furniture retailer suffer more than a supermarket when interest rates rise?
4. Name one type of business that might gain from higher interest rates.
5. How can a rise in interest rates reduce orders for a van manufacturer that has no borrowing?
6. Give one benefit and one drawback for a business when unemployment rises.
7. Why might wages rise when employment levels are high?
8. Name one product whose demand changes little when incomes fall.
9. Why might a discount store gain when incomes fall?
10. A business has a £60,000 loan. The rate rises from 6% to 8%. Calculate the extra interest per year.
11. A hotel sells 4,000 room-nights a year at £90. Falling incomes cut sales by 15%. Calculate the lost revenue.
12. Suggest one marketing response for a restaurant when incomes fall.

### Answers

1. Economic theory about how or why interest rates change.
2. Any two: higher interest payments reduce profit; more cash paid out tightens cash flow; borrowing to expand becomes less attractive.
3. Furniture is expensive and often bought on credit, so customers delay buying when credit costs more; food is essential and bought regularly.
4. A business with large cash savings, which earns more interest.
5. Its business customers find borrowing dearer and delay buying new vans.
6. Benefit: more applicants for vacancies, or less pressure on wages. Drawback: lower consumer spending.
7. Fewer people are looking for work, so businesses compete for staff by offering higher pay.
8. For example: basic food, energy or toiletries.
9. Shoppers trade down from dearer stores to save money.
10. 2% of £60,000 = £1,200 a year.
11. 15% of 4,000 = 600 room-nights; 600 × £90 = £54,000.
12. For example: a lower-priced set menu, or promotions to attract budget-conscious diners.
