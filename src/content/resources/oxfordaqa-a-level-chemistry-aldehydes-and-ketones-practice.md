These questions cover section 3.3.8 (subsection 3.3.8.1), Aldehydes and ketones, of the OxfordAQA *International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620)* specification (Version 5.3; International AS and A-level exams May/June 2020 onwards). This content is **International A-level only**. Questions on it belong to Unit 4 (Organic 2 and Physical 2), with Unit 5 (practical and synoptic) also able to test it. Some questions also draw on 3.3.5.1 and 3.3.7.1.

## Questions

**1.** Two isomers, 3-ethylpentanal and 3-ethylpentan-2-one, are in unlabelled bottles. Describe how Tollens' reagent could be used to identify them. Give the result for each compound. **[3]**

**2.** 3,3-dimethylbutanal, (CH₃)₃CCH₂CHO, is readily oxidised.

**(a)** Write an equation, using [O], for its oxidation, and name the organic product. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why ketones are not readily oxidised. **[1]**

**3.** Give an overall equation, using [H], for reducing each compound with aqueous NaBH₄, and name the alcohol formed.

**(a)** 2-methylhexan-3-one, CH₃CH₂CH₂COCH(CH₃)₂ **[2]**
**(b)** 5-methylhexanal, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂CH₂CHO **[2]**

**4.** Aqueous NaBH₄ reduces 5-methylhexan-2-one, CH₃COCH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)₂. Outline the mechanism, representing the nucleophile as H⁻, and name the product. **[5]**

**5.** For each compound, give the overall equation (with HCN) for making its hydroxynitrile, and name the hydroxynitrile.

**(a)** 3,3-dimethylbutanal **[2]**
**(b)** nonan-5-one, CH₃(CH₂)₃CO(CH₂)₃CH₃ **[2]**

**6.** 3-ethylpentan-2-one, CH₃COCH(C₂H₅)₂, is treated with KCN and then dilute acid.

**(a)** Outline the mechanism. **[4]**
**(b)** The product has exactly one chiral centre. Identify it, and explain why carbon 3 of the product is not a chiral centre. **[2]**

**7.** A student writes this plan for making a hydroxynitrile: "Dissolve potassium cyanide in water in a beaker on the open bench, add the ketone, then pour in all the acid at once." Identify two hazards of this reaction and suggest a control measure for each. **[4]**

**8.** 0.600 g of a saturated, non-cyclic aldehyde X (general formula CₙH₂ₙO) is warmed with excess Fehling's solution. 0.858 g of copper(I) oxide, Cu₂O, forms. One mole of X gives one mole of Cu₂O.

**(a)** Calculate the relative molecular mass of X. **[3]**
**(b)** Deduce the molecular formula of X. **[1]**
**(c)** In X, the carbon atom next to the –CHO group carries no hydrogen atoms. Name X. **[1]**
**(d)** State whether X gives a mixture of enantiomers with KCN followed by dilute acid. **[1]**

**9.** A student reacts 5.70 g of 4,4-dimethylpentan-2-one (C₇H₁₄O) with 2.93 g of potassium cyanide, then adds excess dilute acid. The ketone and KCN react in a 1 : 1 ratio. 4.76 g of 2-hydroxy-2,4,4-trimethylpentanenitrile (C₈H₁₅NO) is obtained.

**(a)** Show that KCN is the limiting reagent. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**10.** A pure sample of one enantiomer of heptan-3-ol, CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃, rotates the plane of plane-polarised light. It is oxidised with acidified potassium dichromate(VI) to ketone K. K is then reduced with NaBH₄ in aqueous solution.

**(a)** Name K and write an equation, using [O], for its formation. **[2]**
**(b)** Write an equation, using [H], for the reduction of K. **[1]**
**(c)** The heptan-3-ol recovered from K does not rotate plane-polarised light. Explain this. **[3]**
**(d)** A second portion of K is treated with KCN, then dilute acid. Give the product's name and say whether the sample is optically active. **[2]**

**11.** This question links the silver test, amounts and chirality.

**(a)** Write a half-equation for the reduction of the silver complex in Tollens' reagent. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of silver deposited when 0.500 g of 3,3-dimethylbutanal (C₆H₁₂O) is warmed with excess Tollens' reagent. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why reducing any aldehyde with NaBH₄ never creates a chiral centre at the old carbonyl carbon, whereas KCN followed by dilute acid can. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Warm a sample of each with Tollens' reagent in a hot water bath [1]; 3-ethylpentanal: a silver mirror forms [1]; 3-ethylpentan-2-one: no silver mirror, the mixture stays colourless [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give an observation for both compounds; "no silver mirror" for the ketone is stronger than "nothing happens".

**2. (a)** (CH₃)₃CCH₂CHO + [O] → (CH₃)₃CCH₂COOH [1]; **3,3-dimethylbutanoic acid** [1]
**(b)** The C=O carbon of a ketone has no hydrogen atom, so oxidation would need a C–C bond to break [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep the carbon skeleton unchanged in the acid; drawing the acid with one carbon fewer is a common slip and the name then no longer matches the equation.

**3. (a)** CH₃CH₂CH₂COCH(CH₃)₂ + 2[H] → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(OH)CH(CH₃)₂ [1]; **2-methylhexan-3-ol** [1]
**(b)** (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂CH₂CHO + 2[H] → (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂OH [1]; **5-methylhexan-1-ol** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check that each equation balances; a single [H] leaves the equation two hydrogen atoms short, and an unbalanced equation is unlikely to gain credit even with the right product.

**4.** Cδ+=Oδ− shown, with an arrow starting at the H⁻ lone pair and ending on the carbonyl carbon [1]; arrow from the double bond on to O [1]; alkoxide intermediate CH₃CH(O⁻)CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)₂ with the charge on oxygen [1]; arrow from an O⁻ lone pair to a hydrogen of water (or to H⁺) [1]; product **5-methylhexan-2-ol** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Start each arrow at a lone pair or a bond and end it at an atom; an arrow starting from the minus sign of H⁻, or ending between atoms, is ambiguous.

**5. (a)** (CH₃)₃CCH₂CHO + HCN → (CH₃)₃CCH₂CH(OH)CN [1]; **2-hydroxy-4,4-dimethylpentanenitrile** [1]
**(b)** CH₃(CH₂)₃CO(CH₂)₃CH₃ + HCN → CH₃(CH₂)₃C(OH)(CN)(CH₂)₃CH₃ [1]; **2-butyl-2-hydroxyhexanenitrile** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Number from the nitrile carbon, which is C1; in (a) that puts both methyl groups on C4, and answers that number from the other end give a different, wrong name.

**6. (a)** Cδ+=Oδ− shown, with an arrow from the carbon-end lone pair of :CN⁻ to the δ+ carbon [1]; arrow from the double bond on to O [1]; intermediate CH₃C(O⁻)(CN)CH(C₂H₅)₂ [1]; arrow from an O⁻ lone pair to H⁺ [1]
**(b)** Carbon 2, which carries CH₃, OH, CN and CH(C₂H₅)₂ [1]; carbon 3 carries two identical ethyl groups [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The arrow must leave the carbon end of the cyanide ion; starting it at nitrogen implies a C–N bond to the carbonyl carbon, which is the wrong product.

**7.** Potassium cyanide is highly toxic by contact or if swallowed [1]; wear gloves and eye protection [1]. Adding acid forms hydrogen cyanide, a highly toxic, volatile gas [1]; work in a fume cupboard and add dilute acid slowly (not all at once) [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each control must match the hazard it answers; "wear gloves" does nothing against HCN gas, so pair the fume cupboard with the gas and gloves with the solid.

**8. (a)** Mr(Cu₂O) = 127.0 + 16.0 = 143.0, so n(Cu₂O) = 0.858 ÷ 143.0 = 0.00600 mol [1]; n(X) = 0.00600 mol [1]; Mr(X) = 0.600 ÷ 0.00600 = **100.0** [1]
**(b)** 14n + 16 = 100, so n = 6: **C₆H₁₂O** [1]
**(c)** **2,2-dimethylbutanal**, CH₃CH₂C(CH₃)₂CHO [1]
**(d)** Yes: the new carbon carries H, OH, CN and C(CH₃)₂C₂H₅, four different groups, so a racemate forms [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the 1 : 1 ratio stated for Fehling's here; carrying over the 1 : 2 ratio from the silver test doubles the Mr to 200, which gives no whole-number value of n.

**9. (a)** n(ketone) = 5.70 ÷ 114.0 = 0.0500 mol [1]; n(KCN) = 2.93 ÷ 65.1 = 0.0450 mol, which is less than 0.0500 mol in a 1 : 1 reaction, so KCN is limiting [1]
**(b)** Mr(C₈H₁₅NO) = 96.0 + 15.0 + 14.0 + 16.0 = 141.0 [1]; maximum mass = 0.0450 × 141.0 = 6.35 g [1]; yield = 4.76 ÷ 6.35 × 100 = **75.0 %** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Base the yield on the limiting reagent; using the ketone gives 7.05 g as the maximum and a yield of 67.5 %, which does not answer the question as set.

**10. (a)** **Heptan-3-one** [1]; CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ + [O] → CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ + H₂O [1]
**(b)** CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ + 2[H] → CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ [1]
**(c)** The C=O carbon in K is trigonal planar, so the chirality of the original alcohol is lost [1]; H⁻ attacks either face of the plane with equal probability [1]; equal amounts of the two enantiomers form (a racemate), and their equal and opposite rotations cancel [1]
**(d)** **2-ethyl-2-hydroxyhexanenitrile**, CH₃CH₂C(OH)(CN)CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ [1]; not optically active, because it forms as a racemate [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c), the planar intermediate, attack from either face and equal amounts are three separate points; an answer that says only "a racemic mixture forms" covers the conclusion but not the reasons.

**11. (a)** [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + e⁻ → Ag + 2NH₃ [1]
**(b)** n(aldehyde) = 0.500 ÷ 100.0 = 0.00500 mol, so n(Ag) = 2 × 0.00500 = 0.0100 mol [1]; mass = 0.0100 × 107.9 = **1.08 g** [1]
**(c)** Reduction turns –CHO into –CH₂OH, so the old carbonyl carbon carries two hydrogen atoms [1]; with KCN it carries H, OH, CN and the R group, which can be four different groups [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), write the 1 : 2 ratio in your working; a final answer of 0.54 g shows the ratio was missed, and without working there is nothing to credit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a 1 : 1 aldehyde-to-silver ratio, or 1 : 2 for Fehling's.
- Cyanide arrows that start at N; the attacking lone pair is on C.
- BH₄⁻ drawn as the nucleophile instead of H⁻.
- No arrow from the double bond on to oxygen.
- O⁻ left unprotonated at the end of a mechanism.
- Writing [H] rather than 2[H] in reduction equations.
- Basing a yield on the reagent in excess instead of the limiting reagent.
- Explaining a racemate without the planar carbonyl group and equal attack from either face.
- Pairing a hazard with a control that does not address it.
