## Section A: short answers

**1.** Identify two technological developments that helped European expansion into the African interior. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why Britain occupied Egypt in 1882. **[3]**

**3.** State what the Berlin Conference decided about "effective occupation". **[2]**

## Section B: source and argument

*Constructed for practice, not a real source:* "In the end it was not the rifle that took Africa but the signature. A chief who put his mark on a treaty he could not read gave away more than any army could have captured." (a modern historian's summary)

**4.** Explain the argument made in this extract. **[3]**

**5.** Give one reason why the extract may exaggerate the importance of treaties. **[2]**

**6.** Examine the reasons why some African leaders chose to resist European rule while others collaborated. **[6]**

## Section C: essay questions

**7.** Evaluate the claim that the partition of Africa was caused mainly by rivalry between European powers. **[15]**

**8.** To what extent was African resistance to European imperialism a failure? **[15]**

## Answers

**1.** One mark each for two of: quinine against malaria; steamships and river steamers; breech-loading rifles; the Maxim gun; the telegraph. **[2]**

**2.** One mark for the strategic importance of the Suez Canal (opened 1869) to Britain's route to India. One for the immediate trigger: the Urabi revolt and European debt control, which threatened British and French interests. One for the action: British forces defeated Urabi at Tel el-Kebir and stayed to protect British interests, which unsettled the balance with France. **[3]**

**3.** One mark for the rule that claims to coastal territory had to be supported by real control, for example administration or force, not only by a flag. One for the effect: it encouraged rapid occupation of the interior. **[2]**

**4.** One mark for the claim that treaties were more effective than weapons; one for the explanation (chiefs signed texts they could not read or whose meaning differed); one for the conclusion that political and legal means gave away territory. **[3]**

**5.** One mark for a reason, one for development. For example: treaties were often followed or backed by force, such as Rudd's concession from Lobengula (1888) followed by war against the Ndebele in 1893; many societies were conquered with no treaty, such as the Mahdist state at Omdurman in 1898. **[2]**

**6.** Up to 6 marks. Credit reasons for resistance: strong military leadership and firearms (Samori Toure, Menelik II), religious or political ideology (the Mahdi, Maji Maji), anger at tax and forced labour, and a strong state to defend. Credit reasons for collaboration: hope for protection (Khama, Lewanika), gains for elites (Buganda chiefs), rivalries with neighbouring states, and a realistic view of European weapons. Level guide: 1 to 2 for description; 3 to 4 for partial examination; 5 to 6 for a developed examination of reasons and a conclusion about how they combined. **[6]**

**7.** Marking points, up to 15 in total:
- up to 3 for understanding the claim and identifying rivalry as a cause;
- up to 4 for evidence in favour (French reaction to 1870 and Tunis 1881, German entry in 1884, Berlin rules, Fashoda 1898, British ultimatum to Portugal 1890);
- up to 4 for other factors and perspectives (economic motives and Hobson, strategy and Robinson and Gallagher, missionary ideas, technology, African conditions);
- up to 4 for judgement, for example arguing that rivalry decided the pace and borders while economic and strategic interests created the underlying motive. **[15]**

**8.** Marking points, up to 15 in total:
- up to 3 for defining "failure" and choosing criteria (preventing conquest, delaying conquest, winning concessions, influencing later nationalism);
- up to 4 for evidence of failure (Samori's capture in 1898, Omdurman, Maji Maji, the Herero and Nama in 1904 to 1908);
- up to 4 for evidence of success or partial success (Adwa in 1896, delays, treaties that kept some autonomy, memory of resistance as an inspiration);
- up to 4 for evaluation of perspectives and a reasoned judgement, such as that resistance failed in most military terms but shaped colonial policy and later nationalism. **[15]**

## Model paragraph and feedback

Here is a model opening paragraph for Question 8, followed by points on how to improve a draft.

*Model paragraph:* "African resistance to European rule is sometimes judged by its military results. By that test most of it failed: Samori Toure was captured in 1898, the Mahdist army was destroyed at Omdurman, and the Herero and the Maji Maji fighters were crushed. However, historians who stress African agency point out that resistance delayed conquest, forced Europeans to negotiate, and left a memory that later nationalists used. The success of Ethiopia at Adwa in 1896 shows what was possible with a united state and modern weapons. This essay argues that resistance usually failed in the short term but was not a failure in all its effects, and that the right judgement depends on the criteria chosen."

*Feedback points:*

- The paragraph defines "failure" through criteria, which is the key step in a "to what extent" question.
- Use specific names and dates for each point, and avoid saying "Africans" as if there was one response.
- Include collaboration where it helps the argument, but do not let it take over the answer.
- Aim for a conclusion that gives a clear final position.
