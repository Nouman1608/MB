**6.** Write ionic equations, with state symbols, for each reaction.

**(a)** Aqueous silver nitrate added to aqueous potassium iodide, forming a yellow precipitate. **[2]**

**(b)** Magnesium ribbon added to dilute sulfuric acid, giving hydrogen gas. **[2]**

**7.** Sodium carbonate solution reacts with dilute nitric acid.

**(a)** Write the balanced full equation, with state symbols. **[2]**

**8.** A hydrocarbon contains 85.7 % carbon by mass. Its relative molecular mass is 42.0.

**(a)** Calculate its empirical formula. **[2]**

**(b)** Deduce its molecular formula. **[1]**

**9.** 1.38 g of an organic compound containing only carbon, hydrogen and oxygen is burned completely. It forms 2.64 g of carbon dioxide and 1.62 g of water. Its Mr is 46.0.

**(a)** Calculate the masses of carbon and hydrogen in the sample. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of oxygen in the sample. **[1]**

**(c)** Determine the empirical formula and the molecular formula. **[3]**

**10.** A chloride of aluminium contains 20.2 % aluminium by mass. Its relative molecular mass in the gas phase is 267.

**(a)** Calculate the empirical formula. **[2]**

**(b)** Deduce the molecular formula of the gas-phase molecule. **[1]**

**11.** 5.00 g of hydrated magnesium sulfate, MgSO₄·xH₂O, was heated to constant mass. The residue had a mass of 2.44 g.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of water lost. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of anhydrous MgSO₄. **[1]**

**(c)** Determine x and write the formula of the hydrate. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain how the value of x would be affected if heating had stopped before constant mass was reached. **[2]**

**12.** Balance these equations.

**(a)** C₃H₈(g) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + H₂O(l) **[1]**

**(b)** Fe₂O₃(s) + CO(g) → Fe(s) + CO₂(g) **[1]**

**(c)** Al(s) + H⁺(aq) → Al³⁺(aq) + H₂(g) **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. Two NH₄⁺ ions (total +2) balance one CO₃²⁻. *(Total: 1 mark)*

**2.** (a) Zn(OH)₂ [1]. (b) Ag₂SO₄ [1]. (c) Ca(HCO₃)₂ [1]. *(Total: 3 marks)*

**3.** Cr₂O₃ [1]; PbCl₄ [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**4.** Strontium forms Sr²⁺ and selenium forms Se²⁻ [1], so they combine 1 : 1: SrSe [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**5.** Molecular formula: the actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule [1]. Water of crystallisation: water molecules held within the structure of a hydrated crystal [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**6. (a)** Ag⁺(aq) + I⁻(aq) → AgI(s): correct species [1]; state symbols [1].

**(b)** Mg(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + H₂(g): correct balanced species [1]; state symbols [1].

**(c)** CuO(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Cu²⁺(aq) + H₂O(l): correct balanced species [1]; state symbols [1]. *(Total: 6 marks)*

**7. (a)** Na₂CO₃(aq) + 2HNO₃(aq) → 2NaNO₃(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g): balanced [1]; state symbols [1].

**(b)** CO₃²⁻(aq) + 2H⁺(aq) → H₂O(l) + CO₂(g) [1]; spectator ions Na⁺ and NO₃⁻ [1]. *(Total: 4 marks)*

**8. (a)** C: 85.7 ÷ 12.0 = 7.14; H: 14.3 ÷ 1.0 = 14.3 [1]; ratio 1 : 2, CH₂ [1].

**(b)** Empirical formula mass 14.0; 42.0 ÷ 14.0 = 3; C₃H₆ [1]. *(Total: 3 marks)*

**9. (a)** C: 2.64 × 12.0 ÷ 44.0 = 0.720 g [1]. H: 1.62 × 2.0 ÷ 18.0 = 0.180 g [1].

**(b)** O: 1.38 − 0.720 − 0.180 = 0.480 g [1].

**(c)** Moles: C 0.0600, H 0.180, O 0.0300 [1]; ratio 2 : 6 : 1, empirical formula C₂H₆O [1]. Empirical formula mass 46.0 = Mr, so molecular formula C₂H₆O [1]. *(Total: 6 marks)*

**10. (a)** Al: 20.2 ÷ 27.0 = 0.748; Cl: 79.8 ÷ 35.5 = 2.248 [1]; ratio 1 : 3, AlCl₃ [1].

**(b)** Empirical formula mass 133.5; 267 ÷ 133.5 = 2; Al₂Cl₆ [1]. *(Total: 3 marks)*

**11. (a)** Mass of water = 5.00 − 2.44 = 2.56 g; 2.56 ÷ 18.0 = 0.142 mol [1].

**(b)** MgSO₄ formula mass 120.4; 2.44 ÷ 120.4 = 0.0203 mol [1].

**(c)** x = 0.142 ÷ 0.0203 = 7; MgSO₄·7H₂O [1].

**(d)** Some water would remain in the residue, so the mass of water lost would be too small [1] and the residue would be heavier than pure MgSO₄, so x would be too low [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

**12. (a)** C₃H₈(g) + 5O₂(g) → 3CO₂(g) + 4H₂O(l) [1].

**(b)** Fe₂O₃(s) + 3CO(g) → 2Fe(s) + 3CO₂(g) [1].

**(c)** 2Al(s) + 6H⁺(aq) → 2Al³⁺(aq) + 3H₂(g): atoms balanced [1]; charge balanced, +6 on each side [1]. *(Total: 4 marks)*

## Where marks are usually lost

- Missing brackets around polyatomic ions.
- Splitting solid oxides or carbonates into ions in an ionic equation.
- Leaving out state symbols, or writing (aq) for a precipitate.
- Rounding a mole ratio too early.
- Forgetting to find oxygen by difference in combustion analysis.
- Not checking that charges balance in an ionic equation.

## Frequently asked questions

**When should I keep a compound whole in an ionic equation?**
Keep solids, liquids such as water, gases and covalent molecules whole. Only aqueous ionic compounds and acids in solution are written as separate ions.

**How do I know the molecular formula from the empirical formula?**
Divide the relative molecular mass by the empirical formula mass. The answer is the whole number by which every subscript is multiplied.
