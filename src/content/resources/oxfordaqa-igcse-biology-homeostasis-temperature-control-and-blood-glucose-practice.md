## Questions

**1.** State where in the body you would find receptors that are sensitive to each of the following. **(a)** The concentration of glucose in the blood. **[1]** **(b)** The temperature of blood. **[1]** **(c)** Touch and pressure. **[1]**

**2.** The body has automatic control systems. **(a)** Name two internal conditions that are controlled, other than temperature. **[2]** **(b)** Name two coordination centres. **[2]**

**3.** On a hot day a cyclist's core temperature begins to rise. Explain how the body responds. **[5]**

**4.** A volunteer's temperatures were measured in cold air and in warm air.

| Air temperature | Core temperature (°C) | Finger skin temperature (°C) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 °C | 37.0 | 12.0 |
| 30 °C | 37.1 | 31.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the difference between core and finger temperature at each air temperature. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain, with reference to blood vessels, why the finger was much cooler at 5 °C. **[3]**
**(c)** At 5 °C the volunteer began to shiver. Explain how shivering helps to keep core temperature constant. **[2]**

**5.** A builder works for 6 hours on a hot day and sweats at a mean rate of 1.2 dm³ per hour. She drinks 3.9 dm³ of water during the shift.
**(a)** Calculate how much more sweat she lost than water she drank. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how sweating cools the body. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why she needs to take in more fluid than on a cold day. **[1]**

**6.** A student drinks a large glass of sweetened juice. Explain how the body brings blood glucose back down. **[5]**

**7.** A person goes for many hours without food. Explain how the body stops blood glucose from falling too far. **[4]**

**8.** A volunteer did not eat for 8 hours. Blood glucose and the concentration of hormone P were measured. Hormone P is made in the pancreas.

| Time (h) | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blood glucose (mmol/dm³) | 5.4 | 4.8 | 4.2 | 4.6 | 5.0 |
| Hormone P (arbitrary units) | 6 | 14 | 30 | 22 | 12 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage fall in blood glucose from 0 to 4 hours. **[2]**
**(b)** Hormone P is glucagon. Use the data to support this. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how glucagon makes blood glucose rise. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the mean rate of increase in blood glucose from 4 to 8 hours. **[2]**

**9.** A trial compared two ways of treating adults with Type 2 diabetes for 12 weeks. Mean blood glucose is given.

| Treatment | Before (mmol/dm³) | After (mmol/dm³) |
|---|---|---|
| Diet and exercise only | 9.8 | 9.1 |
| Diet, exercise and a drug that helps cells respond to insulin | 9.9 | 7.4 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage fall in each group. **[3]**
**(b)** What conclusion can be drawn about the drug? **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how this kind of drug helps in Type 2 diabetes. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest one factor that should be the same in both groups. **[1]**

**10.** Compare Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes. Include the cause of each and how each may be controlled. **[6]**

**11.** A marathon runner runs for 3 hours on a hot day. Explain how her body maintains her core temperature and her blood glucose concentration. **[9]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The pancreas. [1]
**(b)** The brain. [1]
**(c)** The skin. [1]
*Examiner insight:* A named organ is needed for each part. "Blood" is not a place that holds receptors, so an answer of that kind is unlikely to gain credit.

**2. (a)** Any two of: water content of the body [1], ion content of the body [1], blood glucose levels. Two different conditions are needed.
**(b)** Any two of: brain [1], spinal cord [1], pancreas.
*Examiner insight:* "Temperature" is excluded by the question, so repeating it cannot gain credit. Read the exclusion in the stem before listing.

**3.** Receptors in the brain detect the temperature of the blood, and skin receptors send impulses to the thermoregulatory centre. [1] Blood vessels supplying the skin capillaries dilate. [1] More blood flows through the skin capillaries, so more energy is transferred from the skin to the environment. [1] Sweat glands release more sweat. [1] The sweat cools the body as it evaporates. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to gain credit when they say what the blood vessels do and what that does to energy transfer. "Vessels move to the skin" gives a wrong picture of vasodilation.

**4. (a)** At 5 °C: 37.0 − 12.0 = **25.0 °C**. [1] At 30 °C: 37.1 − 31.0 = **6.1 °C**. [1]
**(b)** The blood vessels supplying the skin capillaries constrict. [1] Less blood flows through the capillaries in the finger. [1] Less energy is transferred from the blood to the surroundings, so the skin stays cooler while the core stays near 37 °C. [1]
**(c)** Muscle contraction needs respiration. [1] Respiration transfers some energy that warms the body. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the subtraction and include the unit. In part (b), a chain from vessel diameter to blood flow to energy transfer gains credit more reliably than "to keep heat in".

**5. (a)** Sweat lost = 1.2 × 6 = 7.2 dm³. [1] Difference = 7.2 − 3.9 = **3.3 dm³**. [1]
**(b)** Sweat is made by sweat glands and spreads over the skin surface. [1] Sweat cools the body as it evaporates. [1]
**(c)** More water is lost when it is hot, so more fluid has to be taken in through drink or food to balance the loss. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Quoting the intermediate value of 7.2 dm³ makes the method visible. In part (b), the word "evaporates" carries the point.

**6.** The pancreas detects the high glucose concentration. [1] The pancreas produces insulin. [1] Insulin allows glucose to move from the blood into the cells. [1] Much of the glucose is stored as glycogen in the liver and muscles. [1] When these stores are full, excess glucose is stored as lipid. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Say where the glucose goes (into cells) and what it becomes (glycogen). "Insulin gets rid of sugar" is too vague to show the mechanism.

**7.** The pancreas detects that blood glucose is falling. [1] The pancreas produces glucagon. [1] Stored glycogen is turned back into glucose. [1] This glucose enters the blood, so blood glucose rises. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Naming the stored substance (glycogen) and the product (glucose) is what separates a complete answer from "glucagon raises blood sugar".

**8. (a)** Fall = 5.4 − 4.2 = 1.2. [1] 1.2 ÷ 5.4 × 100 = **22%**. [1]
**(b)** Hormone P rises as blood glucose falls. [1] It peaks (30 units) at 4 hours, when glucose is lowest (4.2), and then glucose rises. [1]
**(c)** Glucagon makes stored glycogen change back into glucose. [1] The glucose passes into the blood. [1]
**(d)** Rise = 5.0 − 4.2 = 0.8 mmol/dm³ in 4 hours. [1] Rate = 0.8 ÷ 4 = **0.20 mmol/dm³ per hour**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Quote values from the table when supporting a claim, and divide by the starting value for a percentage. A rate needs its unit, including "per hour".

**9. (a)** Percentage change = fall ÷ starting value × 100. [1] Diet and exercise only: 0.7 ÷ 9.8 × 100 = **7.1%**. [1] With the drug: 2.5 ÷ 9.9 × 100 = **25%**. [1]
**(b)** The group that also took the drug had a larger fall (25% against 7.1%). [1] This suggests the drug lowers blood glucose further than diet and exercise alone. [1]
**(c)** It helps the cells to respond to insulin, so more glucose moves from the blood into cells. [1]
**(d)** Any one of: the diet, the amount of exercise, the age of the patients, body mass, the starting blood glucose. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Support a conclusion with both figures and keep the claim to what the data show. Saying the drug "cures" diabetes goes beyond a 12-week fall in glucose.

**10.** Type 1: the pancreas does not produce enough insulin. [1] Type 2: the body does not respond to its own insulin. [1] Obesity is a significant factor in the development of Type 2 diabetes. [1] Type 1 may be controlled by careful diet, exercise and injecting insulin. [1] Type 2 may be controlled by careful diet, exercise and drugs that help the cells to respond to insulin. [1] Both can lead to blood glucose being too high. [1]
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both types in each point. Writing two separate lists without a link between them tends to leave the comparison implied rather than stated.

**11.** Temperature: Receptors detect the rise in blood temperature and skin temperature, and send information to the thermoregulatory centre. [1] Skin blood vessels dilate, so more energy is transferred to the environment. [1] Sweat glands release more sweat, which cools the body as it evaporates. [1] She must drink to replace the water lost. [1]
Glucose: Her muscles use glucose in respiration, so blood glucose falls. [1] The pancreas detects the fall and produces glucagon. [1] Glucagon makes stored glycogen change back into glucose, which enters the blood. [1] After the run, eating raises blood glucose, so the pancreas produces insulin. [1] Insulin allows glucose to move from the blood into cells, where it can be stored as glycogen. [1]
*Examiner insight:* A longer answer is easier to follow when it is split into temperature and glucose, with each response linked to what it changes. Listing responses without the effect leaves points unproven.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Naming "blood" or "the body" instead of an organ in a question about where receptors are found.
* Repeating a condition the question has already excluded.
* Saying that vessels move, rather than that they dilate or constrict.
* Saying sweat cools you without mentioning evaporation.
* Writing "insulin lowers blood sugar" with no mention of glucose moving into cells.
* Writing "glucagon raises blood sugar" with no mention of stored glycogen changing back into glucose.
* Describing Type 2 diabetes as a lack of insulin.
* Dividing by the final value when calculating a percentage change.
* Leaving out units, or the "per hour", on a rate.
