AQA GCSE Economics (8136) introduces students to the choices people, firms and governments make, and to how the
UK economy fits into a wider world of trade, exchange rates and financial markets. It suits students who are
curious about the news, enjoy argument backed by evidence and are comfortable with straightforward calculations.
The qualification is linear and not tiered, and it is assessed by two written papers, each lasting 1 hour
45 minutes, worth 80 marks and carrying 50% of the GCSE.

## How the course is organised

The content falls into two parts, matching the titles of the two papers.

**How markets work.** This part covers economic foundations, resource allocation, how prices are determined,
production, costs, revenue and profit, competitive and concentrated markets, and market failure. It is the
microeconomic half of the course, concerned with individual markets and the businesses and consumers in them.

**How the economy works.** This is the larger part. It begins with interest rates, saving, borrowing, spending
and investment, and government income and expenditure. It then covers the government's economic objectives:
economic growth, employment and unemployment, inflation and price stability, the balance of payments and the
distribution of income. Policy follows, with fiscal policy, monetary policy, supply-side policies and policies
to correct positive and negative externalities. The international economy is covered through why countries trade
and the importance of trade to the UK, exchange rates, free-trade agreements including the European Union, and
the benefits and drawbacks of globalisation. The part closes with the role of money and the role and importance
of the financial sector.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8136/1 Paper 1: How markets work | 1 hour 45 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| 8136/2 Paper 2: How the economy works | 1 hour 45 minutes | 80 | 50% |

Paper 1 is set on content sections 1 to 6 and Paper 2 on content sections 7 to 11 of the specification. Both
papers are externally assessed written exams with the same question format. Section A contains ten
multiple-choice questions followed by calculation, short-answer and extended-response questions. Section B
contains five questions mixing calculations, short answers and extended responses.

Importantly, both papers explicitly draw on knowledge from the entire course, not only their own content block.
The assessment objectives are weighted AO1 35%, AO2 35% and AO3 30% overall, which works out at roughly 17.5%,
17.5% and 15% on each paper. The specification was first taught in September 2017 and first assessed in 2019.

## How the parts fit together

Because each paper can draw on the whole course, the two halves cannot be revised as separate subjects. Many of
the most natural exam questions sit at the join between them. Market failure and externalities in How markets
work lead straight into the policies to correct positive and negative externalities in How the economy works.
Interest rates affect what consumers and firms in individual markets decide to do. Production costs, studied
with firms and markets, return when inflation and price stability are discussed.

Two skills run through the whole course. The first is calculation: costs, revenue and profit, percentage changes,
and interpreting simple economic data. The second is extended writing that weighs up an argument; with AO3 worth
30% of the marks, analysing and evaluating economic ideas matters as much as knowing them. Practising both from
the start makes the extended-response questions far less daunting.

The closing topics on money and the financial sector are a good example of why the order of teaching is not the
order of importance. They are listed last, but they explain how interest rates reach households and firms, which
is the mechanism behind monetary policy and much of the earlier discussion of saving, borrowing and investment.

## Planning your study

A sensible two-year order is:

1. **Start with economic foundations and resource allocation**, which introduce scarcity, choice and opportunity
   cost.
2. **Work through price determination, then production, costs, revenue and profit**, followed by competitive and
   concentrated markets and market failure.
3. **Begin How the economy works with interest rates, spending and government income and expenditure**, then the
   government's objectives.
4. **Move to policy: fiscal, monetary, supply-side and externality policies.**
5. **Finish with trade, exchange rates, free-trade agreements, globalisation, money and the financial sector.**

Keep a short glossary and a calculations sheet that you add to as each topic is taught, and follow a few economic
news stories through the course, linking each to the relevant content. Multiple-choice and short-answer practice
can begin in the first term. Full papers are most useful once both halves have been taught, because each paper
can draw on the whole course. Paper-by-paper strategy is on the exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in AQA GCSE Economics?**
Two written papers, How markets work and How the economy works. Each lasts 1 hour 45 minutes, is worth 80 marks and counts for 50% of the GCSE.

**Can Paper 1 include questions on the economy as a whole?**
Yes. Both papers explicitly draw on knowledge from the entire course, not just their own content block.

**What types of questions are on the papers?**
Section A has ten multiple-choice questions followed by calculation, short-answer and extended-response questions. Section B has five questions mixing calculations, short answers and extended responses.

**Is AQA GCSE Economics tiered?**
No. Everyone sits the same two papers, and the course is linear, so both are taken at the end.

**How are the assessment objectives weighted?**
AO1 carries 35%, AO2 35% and AO3 30% overall, roughly 17.5%, 17.5% and 15% on each paper.
