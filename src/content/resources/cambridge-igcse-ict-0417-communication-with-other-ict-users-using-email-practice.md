## Section A: short answers

**1.** State what is meant by an *email group*. **[1]**

**2.** Which **one** of these sends a received email, with its attachments, to a new recipient?

A: reply

B: reply all

C: forward

D: cc **[1]**

**3.** Give **two** reasons why a business might choose email rather than a letter sent by post. **[2]**

**4.** State **one** reason why an email system may refuse to send an attachment. **[1]**

**5.** Give **two** rules of netiquette that apply to writing emails. **[2]**

**6.** State what happens to the junk folder when a spam filter wrongly identifies a genuine email. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A travel company employs 200 staff. It has an acceptable use policy for email.

**(a)** Describe **three** guidelines the policy is likely to contain. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the company sets these guidelines. **[2]**

**8.** A charity wants to email a newsletter to its 2 500 supporters and keep its two trustees informed.

**(a)** State which field the supporters' addresses should be placed in, and explain why. **[2]**

**(b)** State which field the trustees' addresses should be placed in, and explain why. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe how an email group would help the charity. **[2]**

**9.** A student receives an email that appears to come from her bank. It asks her to click a link and confirm her password.

**(a)** Describe **two** checks she should make before acting on the email. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **two** other ways she can keep her email account secure. **[2]**

**10.** An office worker receives about 80 spam emails every day.

**(a)** Describe the characteristics of spam email. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **two** effects of this spam on the worker. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe **three** methods the worker could use to reduce the spam. **[3]**

**11.** An architect needs to email a set of building plans totalling 40 MB to a client. The email system has a 20 MB attachment limit. Describe **two** ways the architect could send the plans, and state **one** further issue the architect should consider about the client opening the files. **[3]**

**12.** Discuss the advantages and disadvantages for a school of using email to communicate with parents. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** A single name in the address book that represents a list of many email addresses (1). **[1]**

**2.** C: forward. **[1]**

**3.** Any two: faster, arrives within seconds (1); cheaper, no postage or printing (1); can send to many customers at once (1); attachments can be included (1); a searchable electronic record is kept (1). **[2]**

**4.** Any one: the file is larger than the maximum attachment size (1); the file type, such as an executable, is blocked for security (1). **[1]**

**5.** Any two: use a meaningful subject line (1); do not type in capitals (1); be polite and brief (1); check spelling and grammar (1); do not forward private messages without permission (1); do not send spam or chain messages (1). **[2]**

**6.** The genuine email is moved into the junk folder, so it may be missed unless the folder is checked (1). **[1]**

**7. (a)** Any three: personal use of work email is limited or banned (1); confidential company information must not be sent outside the company (1); emails may be monitored (1); staff must use formal language and an approved signature (1); no offensive or inappropriate content (1); misuse will lead to disciplinary action (1).

**(b)** Staff emails represent the company (1), so misuse could damage its reputation, leak confidential data or lead to legal action against the company (1). **[5]**

**8. (a)** bcc (1), because each supporter's address is hidden from the other supporters, protecting their privacy and stopping addresses being collected for spam (1).

**(b)** cc (1), because the trustees only need to be kept informed, not to act, and it is acceptable for their addresses to be visible (1).

**(c)** All 2 500 addresses are stored under one group name (1), so the newsletter is sent by typing one name, which saves time and ensures no supporter is missed (1). **[6]**

**9. (a)** Any two: check the sender's full email address matches the bank's genuine domain (1); hover over the link to see the real web address before clicking (1); look for spelling errors or a generic greeting (1); contact the bank using its known phone number or website instead of the link (1). Credit: a bank would not ask for a password by email.

**(b)** Any two: use a strong password that is not used elsewhere (1); turn on two-factor authentication (1); log out when using a shared computer (1); do not open unexpected attachments (1). **[4]**

**10. (a)** Unsolicited email (not requested by the recipient) (1), sent in bulk to many addresses at once, often automatically or from false addresses (1).

**(b)** Any two: important emails may be missed among the spam (1); time is wasted deleting it (1); it uses mailbox storage (1); it may contain phishing links or malware that could infect the computer (1).

**(c)** Any three: use a spam filter (1); block the senders or mark messages as spam (1); do not reply or click links, including unsubscribe links in suspicious emails (1); do not publish the email address publicly (1); untick boxes allowing the address to be shared (1); use a separate address for online sign-ups (1). **[7]**

**11.** Any two ways: compress the plans into a .zip file to reduce their size (1); split them across two emails, each under 20 MB (1); upload them to cloud storage and email a link (1). Further issue: the client needs software that can open the file format, so a generic format such as PDF may be better (1); or the client's mailbox may have limited storage (1). **[3]**

**12.** Up to 6 marks for points on both sides.
- Advantages: messages reach parents within seconds (1); cheaper than printing and posting letters (1); one email can go to all parents using a group (1); attachments such as reports or forms can be included (1); a written record is kept (1); parents can read it when convenient (1).
- Disadvantages: some parents may not have internet access or check email regularly (1); messages may be filtered as spam and missed (1); a mistake such as forgetting bcc could reveal all parents' addresses (1); tone can be misunderstood without face-to-face contact (1); the school must keep groups up to date (1).

Answers covering only one side are limited to 4 marks. **[6]**

## Frequently asked questions

**Does bcc stop the recipient seeing the email?**
No. A bcc recipient receives the full message; only their address is hidden from the other recipients.

**Is clicking unsubscribe a good way to stop spam?**
Only for genuine organisations you signed up to. In a suspicious email the link may confirm that your address is active or lead to a malicious site.
