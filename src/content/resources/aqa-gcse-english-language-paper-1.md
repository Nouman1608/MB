This guide covers **Paper 1: Explorations in Creative Reading and
Writing**, the first of two examined components in AQA GCSE English
Language (8700), for first teaching 2015. It focuses on the paper's
official structure, marks and assessment objectives -- for reading
technique and creative-writing craft, use the site's existing
[Paper 1 revision notes](/resources/gcse-english-language-paper-1-revision-notes/)
and [Paper 1 practice questions](/resources/gcse-english-language-paper-1-practice/).
Together with [Paper 2](/resources/aqa-gcse-english-language-paper-2/),
Paper 1 makes up the whole of the assessed GCSE.


## Syllabus coverage

**AQA GCSE ENGLISH LANGUAGE (8700) -- PAPER 1 EXPLORATIONS IN CREATIVE
READING AND WRITING**

- **Section A: Reading (40 marks, 25% of the GCSE)** -- one unseen
  literature fiction text, drawn from **either the 20th or the 21st
  century** (never the 19th -- that source period belongs to Paper 2).
  The extract's genre is prose fiction: novels and short stories,
  focused on openings, endings, narrative perspective and point of
  view, narrative or descriptive passages, character and atmospheric
  description. Four questions assess it in a fixed sequence: one short
  form question (4 marks), two longer form questions (8 marks each),
  and one extended evaluative question (20 marks) -- 4 + 8 + 8 + 20 = 40.
- **Section B: Writing (40 marks, 25% of the GCSE)** -- a single
  extended writing task, chosen from a scenario, written prompt or
  visual image linked to the topic of the Section A reading text.
  Students write narrative or descriptive fiction of their own. The
  task is marked out of 24 for content and organisation, and 16 for
  technical accuracy (spelling, punctuation, grammar) -- 24 + 16 = 40.

The paper lasts 1 hour 45 minutes and is worth 80 raw marks in total,
scaled at x1 alongside Paper 2's 80 to give a 160-mark overall total.
Section A and Section B are equally weighted within the paper, and
Paper 1 and Paper 2 are equally weighted within the GCSE (50% each).


## Assessment objectives tested

AQA's own specification sets out exactly which assessment objectives
(AOs) each section of Paper 1 targets, in this sequence:

- **Section A (Reading)** assesses **AO1** (identify and interpret
  explicit and implicit information and ideas), **AO2** (explain,
  comment on and analyse how writers use language and structure to
  achieve effects, using relevant subject terminology), and **AO4**
  (evaluate texts critically, supporting judgements with textual
  references).
- **Section B (Writing)** assesses **AO5** (communicate clearly,
  effectively and imaginatively; organise ideas using structural and
  grammatical features) and **AO6** (use a range of vocabulary and
  sentence structures accurately, with correct spelling and
  punctuation).

Notably, Paper 1 does **not** assess AO3 (comparing writers' ideas and
perspectives across texts) -- that objective belongs entirely to
Paper 2, since Section A here works with a single text, not two. This
is a genuinely useful distinction for revision: Paper 1's Section A
is about depth of analysis and evaluation on one extract, not
comparison.

Across the whole GCSE, AO5 carries the heaviest overall weighting (30%
of all marks, split evenly across both papers' writing sections), and
AO6's technical-accuracy requirement is fixed by the specification at
20% of all marks for the qualification as a whole -- both figures worth
knowing when deciding where to invest revision time.

## Why fiction, and why no 19th-century source here

Paper 1's reading source is always literature fiction from the 20th or
21st century -- prose fiction only, never poetry, drama or non-fiction,
and never a 19th-century extract. This design choice exists so that
Paper 1 and Paper 2 test genuinely different reading skills: Paper 1
rewards close, sustained engagement with a single narrative voice,
while Paper 2's two-source, cross-period structure rewards comparison.
Each exam series pairs a Paper 1 text from one of the 20th/21st
centuries with a Paper 2 second source from whichever of those two
periods Paper 1 did *not* use that series -- so across the two papers,
students meet material from all three centuries (19th, 20th, 21st),
just never the same period twice within a single series' pairing.


## Section B: writing from a choice of stimulus

Unlike Paper 2, where Section B sets a single fixed task, Paper 1
Section B offers a **choice** of scenario, written prompt or visual
image, all linked to the theme of the Section A reading text but each
setting a different context, audience, purpose and form from anything
that would appear on Paper 2. Students write their own narrative or
descriptive piece in response -- inspired by the reading extract's
topic, never a continuation of the extract itself. Because the mark
split (24 content, 16 technical accuracy) mirrors Paper 2's Section B
exactly, technical accuracy is worth exactly the same amount on both
papers, even though the writing genre (creative fiction here, versus
viewpoint-driven non-fiction on Paper 2) is entirely different.

## The final reading question and stretch

AQA's own scheme of assessment singles out Question 4 -- the extended,
20-mark evaluative question -- as the point in Section A designed to
let students "draw together different areas of knowledge, skills and
understanding" from across the whole course, rather than testing a
single isolated skill. In practice this means Question 4 typically
asks students to evaluate a specific statement about part of the text
("a critic said this opening is particularly engaging -- to what extent
do you agree?"), which rewards a sustained, evidenced argument built
from multiple points rather than a single well-spotted quotation.
Recognising this question as the paper's deliberate stretch-and-
challenge point, and allocating revision time accordingly, is worth
building into a revision plan from early on.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating Section A's Question 4 as another language-analysis
  question** rather than a sustained evaluative argument that draws on
  the whole extract and requires a clear, supported judgement.
- **Writing a continuation of the Section A extract in Section B**
  instead of an original piece merely inspired by its topic --
  examiners mark against the task's own scenario, prompt or image, not
  against the source text's plot.
- **Confusing Paper 1's single-text, evaluative focus (AO4) with Paper
  2's comparative focus (AO3)** -- since Paper 1 has only one reading
  source, there is nothing to compare, and answers that try to bring in
  a second, unset text score no credit for doing so.
- **Under-planning Section B**, given the choice of three stimulus
  types (scenario, prompt, image) can tempt students into writing
  immediately rather than spending two or three minutes fixing a clear
  structure first.
- **Neglecting technical accuracy in Section B**, which is worth 16 of
  the section's 40 marks (40%) -- a strong, imaginative piece with
  frequent spelling and punctuation errors cannot access the top bands
  for that half of the mark scheme.

## How to approach it

Since Section A always draws on 20th- or 21st-century prose fiction,
build reading stamina and confidence with a wide range of published
short-story and novel extracts during revision, rather than a narrow
set of set texts -- there is no fixed reading list to memorise. Read
each extract once for overall sense, then a second time hunting
specifically for language and structural choices worth analysing, so
that Section A's question ladder (short recall, then two language/
structure questions, then the extended evaluation) becomes a familiar
rhythm rather than a surprise. For Section B, practise choosing quickly
between the scenario, prompt and image options under timed conditions --
indecision costs planning time -- and always spend a minute or two
fixing a shape (opening, development, turning point, ending) before
writing a single sentence. Finally, since Section B's technical-
accuracy marks are identical in weight to Section A's most valuable
single question, leave two or three minutes at the end of the paper
specifically to proofread the Section B writing, rather than assuming
accuracy will look after itself.
