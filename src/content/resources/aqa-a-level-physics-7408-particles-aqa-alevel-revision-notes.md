## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Proton number Z | Number of protons in the nucleus |
| Nucleon number A | Number of protons plus neutrons |
| Isotopes | Same Z, different A (different neutron numbers) |
| Specific charge | Charge ÷ mass, C kg⁻¹ |
| Strong nuclear force | Force between nucleons: attractive about 0.5–3 fm, repulsive below about 0.5 fm, negligible beyond about 3 fm |
| Antiparticle | Same mass and rest energy, opposite charge and quantum numbers |
| Annihilation | Particle + antiparticle → two photons |
| Pair production | Photon → particle + antiparticle |
| Exchange particle | Carries a force between particles (virtual photon, W±, pion) |
| Hadron | Feels the strong interaction: baryons and mesons |
| Lepton | Does not feel the strong interaction: e, μ, νₑ, ν_μ and antiparticles |
| Strange particle | Made in pairs by the strong interaction, decays by the weak |

## Numbers to recall

| Particle | Charge / C | Mass / kg | Rest energy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proton | +1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ | 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ | 938.3 MeV |
| Neutron | 0 | 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ | 939.6 MeV |
| Electron | −1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ | 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ | 0.511 MeV |

Specific charges: electron 1.76 × 10¹¹ C kg⁻¹; proton 9.58 × 10⁷ C kg⁻¹. For a nucleus, specific charge ∝ Z/A.

Worked reminder: ⁹₄Be nucleus: (4 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) ÷ (9 × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷) = **4.26 × 10⁷ C kg⁻¹**.

## Decay equations

| Decay | Change | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Alpha | A − 4, Z − 2 | ²¹⁰₈₄Po → ²⁰⁶₈₂Pb + ⁴₂α |
| β⁻ | A same, Z + 1; emits e⁻ and ν̄ₑ | ³H → ³He + e⁻ + ν̄ₑ (Z: 1 → 2) |

Neutrino hypothesis: β particles have a range of energies, so a third particle must carry the remaining energy for energy to be conserved.

## Annihilation and pair production

- Minimum photon energy for pair production = 2 × rest energy of one particle.
- Electron–positron pair: 1.022 MeV minimum.
- Annihilation of slow particles: two photons, opposite directions, each with one particle's rest energy.

Method for a photon from an energy in MeV:
1. Multiply MeV by 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ to get J.
2. f = E/h.
3. λ = c/f, or λ = hc/E directly.

## Interactions

| Interaction | Exchange particle | Typical process |
|---|---|---|
| Electromagnetic | Virtual photon | Repulsion between two electrons |
| Weak | W⁺, W⁻ | β⁻, β⁺, electron capture, electron–proton collision |
| Strong | Pion (between nucleons) | Holding nucleons together |

| Weak process | Equation | W boson |
|---|---|---|
| β⁻ decay | n → p + e⁻ + ν̄ₑ | W⁻ |
| β⁺ decay | p → n + e⁺ + νₑ | W⁺ |
| Electron capture | p + e⁻ → n + νₑ | W⁺ (p to e⁻) or W⁻ (e⁻ to p) |
| Electron–proton collision | e⁻ + p → n + νₑ | W⁻ (e⁻ to p) |

## Quarks and hadrons

| Quark | Q | B | S |
|---|---|---|---|
| u | +2/3 | +1/3 | 0 |
| d | −1/3 | +1/3 | 0 |
| s | −1/3 | +1/3 | −1 |

Antiquarks reverse all three. Recall: p = uud, n = udd, p̄ = ūūd̄, n̄ = ūd̄d̄, π⁺ = ud̄, π⁻ = dū, π⁰ = uū or dd̄, K⁺ = us̄, K⁻ = sū, K⁰ = ds̄, K̄⁰ = sd̄.

β⁻ decay: d → u. β⁺ decay: u → d.

## Conservation checks

| Quantity | Strong | Weak |
|---|---|---|
| Charge | Conserved | Conserved |
| Baryon number | Conserved | Conserved |
| Lₑ and L_μ (separately) | Conserved | Conserved |
| Strangeness | Conserved | Can change by 0 or ±1 |
| Energy and momentum | Conserved | Conserved |

Worked reminder: is π⁻ + p → K⁻ + K⁺ + n possible? Q: 0 → 0. B: 1 → 1. S: 0 → −1 + 1 + 0 = 0. All conserved, so it can occur by the strong interaction (given enough energy).

## Exam traps

- Including the electron mass in a nuclear specific charge.
- Writing νₑ instead of ν̄ₑ in β⁻ decay.
- Forgetting that the strong force becomes repulsive at very short range.
- One photon in annihilation, which breaks momentum conservation.
- Checking total lepton number instead of Lₑ and L_μ separately.
- Treating the muon as a meson or the pion as a lepton.
- Applying strangeness conservation to a decay that involves a neutrino.
- Leaving pair-production energy in MeV when using E = hf.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Baryon vs meson**: three quarks (B = ±1) vs quark–antiquark (B = 0).
- **Hadron vs lepton**: feels the strong interaction vs does not.
- **β⁻ vs β⁺**: d → u with e⁻ and ν̄ₑ vs u → d with e⁺ and νₑ.
- **Strong vs weak for strange particles**: produced by strong (S conserved), decay by weak (S can change).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does the strong force need to be repulsive at very small separations?**
Without a repulsive part, the attraction would pull nucleons ever closer and the nucleus would collapse. The repulsion below about 0.5 fm keeps nucleons apart.

**Which particle is the only stable baryon?**
The proton. All other baryons eventually decay into a proton.
