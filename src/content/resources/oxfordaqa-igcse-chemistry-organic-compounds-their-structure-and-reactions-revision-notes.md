## The three families at a glance

| Family | Functional group | Name pattern | Members you must name | Solution in water |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alcohols | –OH | ends -ol | methanol, ethanol, propanol | neutral, pH 7 |
| Carboxylic acids | –COOH | ends -oic acid | methanoic, ethanoic, propanoic acid | acidic, weak acid |
| Esters | –COO– | two words, second ends -oate | ethyl ethanoate only | -- |

**Homologous series:** same functional group, similar reactions, each member differs from the next by CH₂.

**Recognition rule:** you must recognise any alcohol, carboxylic acid or ester from its name or formula, even ones you are not asked to name. Butanol is still an alcohol; methyl propanoate is still an ester.

## Formulae to know

| Compound | Structural formula | Mr (H = 1, C = 12, O = 16) |
|---|---|---|
| Methanol | CH₃OH | 32 |
| Ethanol | CH₃CH₂OH or C₂H₅OH | 46 |
| Propanol | CH₃CH₂CH₂OH or C₃H₇OH | 60 |
| Methanoic acid | HCOOH | 46 |
| Ethanoic acid | CH₃COOH | 60 |
| Propanoic acid | CH₃CH₂COOH | 74 |
| Ethyl ethanoate | CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ or CH₃COOC₂H₅ | 88 |

Displayed formula of propanoic acid (three carbons, including the –COOH carbon):

```
    H   H   O
    |   |   ||
H - C - C - C - O - H
    |   |
    H   H
```

### Method in steps: which family is it?

1. Find the oxygen atoms.
2. One O as –OH on a carbon with no C=O: **alcohol**.
3. C=O and O–H on the same carbon, at the end of the chain (–COOH): **carboxylic acid**.
4. C=O with the second O joined to another carbon (–COO– in the middle): **ester**.

## Alcohols (3.10.3.1 b)

| Test or condition | What happens |
|---|---|
| Added to water | dissolves; neutral solution; universal indicator green |
| Small piece of sodium | fizzing; sodium shrinks and disappears; hydrogen given off (squeaky pop) |
| Burned in air | complete combustion → carbon dioxide + water; energy released |

**Uses:** fuels, solvents; ethanol is the main alcohol in alcoholic drinks.

**Complete combustion equations:**

| Alcohol | Complete combustion |
|---|---|
| Methanol | 2CH₃OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 4H₂O |
| Ethanol | C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O |
| Propanol | 2C₃H₇OH + 9O₂ → 6CO₂ + 8H₂O |

**With sodium:** 2C₂H₅OH + 2Na → 2C₂H₅ONa + H₂. Two moles of alcohol (or sodium) give one mole of hydrogen.

## Oxidation of ethanol (3.10.3.1 c)

Ethanol → **ethanoic acid**, by either:

- a **chemical oxidising agent** (for example acidified potassium dichromate, orange → green), or
- **microbial action** using oxygen from the air (why opened wine turns sour).

Ethanoic acid is the main acid in **vinegar**. Overall: C₂H₅OH + O₂ → CH₃COOH + H₂O.

## Carboxylic acids (3.10.3.2 b)

| Reacts with | Products | What you see |
|---|---|---|
| Water | acidic solution (partly ionised) | universal indicator orange/red |
| Metal carbonate | salt + water + carbon dioxide | fizzing; gas turns limewater milky |
| Alcohol + acid catalyst | ester + water | sweet or fruity smell |

Salt names end in **-oate**: sodium methanoate, calcium ethanoate, potassium propanoate.

### Method in steps: balancing acid + carbonate

1. Write the acid, carbonate, salt, H₂O and CO₂.
2. A carbonate ion (2–) takes **two** H⁺, so a metal carbonate M₂CO₃ or MCO₃ needs **two** carboxylic acid molecules.
3. For a 1+ metal (Na, K) write two salt molecules, RCOONa; for a 2+ metal (Ca, Mg) write one salt, (RCOO)₂Ca.
4. Count C, H and O on each side.

Examples:

```
2CH₃COOH + K₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COOK + H₂O + CO₂
2HCOOH + CaCO₃ → (HCOO)₂Ca + H₂O + CO₂
```

### Weak acids and pH

- Carboxylic acids **do not ionise completely** in water: CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺. That makes them **weak acids**.
- **Strong** acids (hydrochloric, nitric, sulfuric) ionise completely.
- Same concentration → weak acid gives **fewer H⁺ ions** → **higher pH**.

## Esters (3.10.3.3)

```
ethanol + ethanoic acid → ethyl ethanoate + water
C₂H₅OH + CH₃COOH → CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O      (acid catalyst)
```

Displayed formula of ethyl ethanoate:

```
    H   O       H   H
    |   ||      |   |
H - C - C - O - C - C - H
    |           |   |
    H           H   H
```

- Name: "ethyl" from ethanol, "ethanoate" from ethanoic acid.
- Properties: **volatile** (evaporate easily), **distinctive smells**.
- Uses: **flavourings** and **perfumes**.
- The reaction is reversible, so you never get the full calculated mass of ester.

## How the three families link

```
ethanol --(oxidising agent or microbes)--> ethanoic acid
ethanol + ethanoic acid --(acid catalyst)--> ethyl ethanoate + water
```

One alcohol can therefore supply both halves of an ester: oxidise some of it to the acid, then react the acid with more of the alcohol. If you are given an unknown compound and its reactions, work back to the family. Use these clues:

| Clue in the question | Points to |
|---|---|
| Neutral, but fizzes with sodium | alcohol |
| Acidic, fizzes with a carbonate, gas turns limewater milky | carboxylic acid |
| Made from an acid and an alcohol; fruity smell; evaporates easily | ester |
| Formed when wine is left open to the air | ethanoic acid |

### Method in steps: mole calculations in this unit

1. Write or read the balanced equation.
2. Work out Mr for the substance whose mass you know, using the Ar values given.
3. Moles = mass ÷ Mr (or volume in dm³ ÷ 24 for a gas at room temperature and pressure).
4. Use the equation ratio to get moles of the substance you want.
5. Mass = moles × Mr, or gas volume = moles × 24 dm³.
6. Give units, and check you used the ratio from the equation, not 1 : 1 by habit.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Oxidation vs combustion of ethanol.** Oxidation (oxidising agent or microbes) → ethanoic acid. Combustion (burning) → CO₂ + H₂O.
- **Weak vs dilute.** Weak = partly ionised. Dilute = little acid per dm³. A dilute strong acid still ionises fully.
- **Alcohol –OH vs hydroxide OH⁻.** An alcohol has a covalent O–H; it gives a neutral solution, not an alkaline one.
- **Acid vs ester with the same formula.** CH₃CH₂COOH (acid) and CH₃COOCH₃ (ester) are both C₃H₆O₂. Look at where –COO– sits.

## Small worked reminders

- **Moles of hydrogen from sodium:** 1.15 g Na = 0.050 mol; H₂ = 0.025 mol; volume = 0.025 × 24 = 0.60 dm³.
- **Mass of acid from alcohol:** 1 mol ethanol → 1 mol ethanoic acid, so mass scales by 60/46.
- **Gas from a carbonate:** 1 mol of any metal carbonate gives 1 mol CO₂, so 0.050 mol carbonate gives 1.2 dm³.

## Quick self-test

1. Give the functional group of a carboxylic acid.
2. Is CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH an alcohol, an acid or an ester?
3. State the colour of universal indicator in aqueous propanol.
4. Name the gas made when sodium reacts with methanol, and give its test.
5. Name two ways of oxidising ethanol to ethanoic acid.
6. Calculate the Mr of propanoic acid.
7. Write a balanced equation for ethanoic acid reacting with potassium carbonate.
8. Why does 0.1 mol/dm³ ethanoic acid have a higher pH than 0.1 mol/dm³ nitric acid?
9. Which word in "propyl methanoate" shows it is an ester?
10. 3.2 g of methanol burns completely. Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide formed.
11. What mass of calcium carbonate gives 1.2 dm³ of carbon dioxide at room temperature and pressure with excess acid? (Ca = 40)
12. Give two properties of esters that make them useful in perfumes.

### Answers

1. –COOH.
2. An alcohol (–OH on a carbon chain, no C=O).
3. Green (pH 7, neutral).
4. Hydrogen; a lighted splint gives a squeaky pop.
5. A chemical oxidising agent; microbial action (oxygen from the air).
6. 3 × 12 + 6 × 1 + 2 × 16 = **74**.
7. 2CH₃COOH + K₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COOK + H₂O + CO₂.
8. Ethanoic acid is weak and only partly ionises; nitric acid ionises completely, so ethanoic acid gives a lower H⁺ concentration.
9. "Methanoate" (the -oate ending of a two-word name).
10. 3.2 ÷ 32 = 0.10 mol methanol; 2CH₃OH → 2CO₂ is 1 : 1, so 0.10 mol CO₂ × 44 = **4.4 g**.
11. 1.2 ÷ 24 = 0.050 mol CO₂ = 0.050 mol CaCO₃; × 100 = **5.0 g**.
12. Volatile; distinctive (pleasant) smell.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling an alcohol solution alkaline because the formula contains OH.
- Writing "gas given off" with sodium but not naming hydrogen, or giving the limewater test instead of the squeaky pop.
- Giving CO₂ and water as the product of microbial oxidation of ethanol.
- Forgetting the carbon in –COOH, so propanoic acid is drawn with two carbons.
- Writing a single acid molecule with a carbonate, leaving the hydrogen unbalanced.
- Using → for weak-acid ionisation where ⇌ is expected, or not linking weak to partial ionisation.
- Explaining the higher pH of a weak acid by saying it is "more dilute".
- Missing the acid catalyst when describing how an ester is made.
- Naming ethyl ethanoate as "ethanoyl ethanol", or drawing it with an O–H left on the end, which makes it an acid.

## Frequently asked questions

**What functional groups do alcohols, carboxylic acids and esters have?**
Alcohols contain –OH, carboxylic acids contain –COOH and esters contain –COO– with a carbon atom on both sides. Alcohol names end in -ol and carboxylic acid names end in -oic acid.

**What is the difference between a weak acid and a dilute acid?**
"Weak" describes how much an acid ionises: a weak acid is only partly ionised. "Dilute" means there is only a small amount of acid in each dm³; a dilute strong acid still ionises fully.

**What gas forms when an alcohol reacts with sodium?**
Alcohols react with sodium to produce hydrogen, which gives a squeaky pop with a lighted splint. Two moles of alcohol react with two moles of sodium to give one mole of hydrogen.

**How are the salts of carboxylic acids named?**
The salt names end in -oate, for example sodium methanoate, calcium ethanoate and potassium propanoate.
