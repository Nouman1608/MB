## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *buffer stock*. **[1]**

**2.** State the **three** factors affecting the choice of supplier that are named in the specification. **[3]**

**3.** Explain the difference between procurement and logistics. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of using just in time (JIT) stock management. **[3]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a business might lose purchasing economies of scale by using JIT. **[2]**

**6.** A café needs 3,000 coffee cups a month. Supplier P charges £0.12 a cup. Supplier Q charges £0.10 a cup but has delivered late twice in the last three months.

**(a)** Calculate the monthly saving from using Supplier Q. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain **one** reason why the café might still choose Supplier P. **[2]**

### Section B: longer and case-study questions

**7.** Fairhaven Florist sells fresh flowers that last about a week. Demand is high around Valentine's Day and Mother's Day but steady at other times. Evaluate whether Fairhaven Florist should use JIT stock management. **[9]**

**8.** Explain **one** benefit of holding buffer stock for a car repair garage. **[3]**

**9.** Saddleworth Shoes buys leather from a supplier it has used for ten years. Analyse how working closely with this supplier could benefit Saddleworth Shoes. **[6]**

**10.** Brookfield Hardware is comparing two ways of buying 1,800 boxes of screws over a year.

| | Option 1: one large order | Option 2: monthly orders |
|---|---|---|
| Price per box | £2.40 | £2.65 |
| Number of deliveries | 1 | 12 |
| Charge per delivery | £60 | £25 |
| Yearly holding cost | £350 | £40 |

**(a)** Calculate the total yearly cost of each option. **[4]**
**(b)** State which option is cheaper and by how much. **[1]**

**11.** Explain how effective logistics can lower a business's unit costs. **[3]**

**12.** Ridgeway Electronics assembles phone chargers from components made overseas. Deliveries take three weeks. It is considering moving from JIC to JIT to reduce warehouse costs. Analyse the risks to Ridgeway Electronics of this change. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Extra stock held in reserve to meet unexpected demand or cover late deliveries [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Price [1]; quality [1]; reliability [1]. **[3]**

**3.** Procurement is finding, choosing and buying the goods and services a business needs [1]. Logistics is moving and storing those goods so they arrive in the right place at the right time [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The business holds very little stock [1], so it needs less storage space and less cash is tied up in stock [1], which lowers costs and leaves more cash available for other uses (or reduces waste from out-of-date stock) [1]. **[3]**

**5.** JIT uses small, frequent orders rather than large ones [1], so the business does not qualify for the discounts suppliers give on bulk orders, raising the price paid per unit [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 3,000 × (£0.12 − £0.10) = £60 a month [1].
**(b)** Supplier Q has been unreliable [1]; if cups arrive late the café may be unable to serve takeaway drinks, losing sales and upsetting customers, which could cost more than the £60 saved [1]. **[3]**

**7.** **For JIT:** fresh flowers last only about a week [1], so holding large stocks would mean many flowers wilting before they are sold [1]. JIT would cut this waste and reduce the cash tied up in stock [1]. For most of the year demand is steady, so the florist can predict what to order [1]. **Against JIT:** demand rises sharply around Valentine's Day and Mother's Day [1], and if deliveries are late or too small at these times, the florist could run out on its busiest days, losing sales [1]. JIT also means more frequent deliveries, each with a charge [1]. **Judgement:** Fairhaven should use JIT for most of the year because its product is perishable and demand is predictable [1], but it should order extra stock in advance for the peak days, since those are the times when running out would cost it most [1]. **[9]**

**8.** A garage cannot finish a repair without the right part [1]. Holding buffer stock of common parts, such as brake pads, means it can complete jobs the same day [1], so customers are not left without their cars and are more likely to return and recommend the garage [1]. **[3]**

**9.** A long relationship means the supplier understands Saddleworth's needs and quality standards [1], so the leather is more likely to be right first time, reducing waste and faulty shoes [1]. Saddleworth can share its sales forecasts with the supplier [1], so the supplier can plan production and deliver on time, keeping Saddleworth's production running efficiently [1]. A loyal customer may also be offered better prices or payment terms [1], and priority when leather is in short supply, which protects Saddleworth's ability to meet orders and keep its unit costs down [1]. **[6]**

**10. (a)** Option 1: 1,800 × £2.40 = £4,320 [1]; + £60 + £350 = £4,730 [1]. Option 2: 1,800 × £2.65 = £4,770 [1]; + (12 × £25 = £300) + £40 = £5,110 [1].
**(b)** Option 1 is cheaper by £5,110 − £4,730 = £380 [1]. **[5]**

**11.** Effective logistics plans deliveries so that vehicles travel full and by the shortest routes [1], and goods are stored in a well-organised way so less time is spent finding them [1]. This lowers transport and handling costs, which are spread over each unit, so the unit cost falls [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Components take three weeks to arrive [1], so under JIT Ridgeway must order well in advance and predict demand accurately [1]. If demand rises suddenly, it will have no buffer stock and cannot get more components quickly [1], so it could lose orders to rivals [1]. Overseas deliveries can also be delayed, for example by shipping problems [1], which would stop assembly altogether and could damage its reputation with retailers; these risks may outweigh the saving on warehouse costs [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Assuming JIT is always cheaper than JIC.
- Choosing a supplier on price alone.
- Mixing up procurement and logistics.
- Forgetting delivery charges when comparing stock options.
- Ignoring how far away suppliers are when evaluating JIT.
- Referring to stock control charts, which are not required.
