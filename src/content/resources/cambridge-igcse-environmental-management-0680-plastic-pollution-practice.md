## Section A

**1.** Describe a non-biodegradable plastic. **[1]**

**2.** State the **three** products formed when biodegradable plastics are decomposed. **[3]**

**3.** A shampoo contains plastic beads 0.5 mm across. Explain whether these are microplastics. **[2]**

**4.** State **two** biotic or abiotic factors that affect how quickly a biodegradable plastic decomposes. **[2]**

**5.** Give **one** example of a single-use plastic item and **one** alternative to it. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A company advertises its drinks bottles as "bioplastic, made from plants". Explain why this does not mean the bottles will decompose if they reach the sea. **[3]**

**7.** Explain how large plastic items floating in the sea become microplastics, and why this does not remove the problem. **[3]**

**8.** Describe and explain how discarded fishing nets harm marine animals. **[3]**

**9.** A student counts plastic pieces on a beach in five 1 m by 1 m quadrats: 14, 22, 9, 31 and 19.
(a) Calculate the mean number of pieces per square metre.
(b) The beach has an area of 2,500 square metres. Estimate the total number of plastic pieces on the beach.
(c) Suggest **one** way to make the estimate more reliable. **[4]**

**10.** Biodegradable bags were left in three places for six months. Results are shown as percentage of mass lost.

| Location | Mass lost (%) |
|---|---|
| Garden compost heap | 85 |
| Surface of a warm lagoon | 30 |
| Deep, cold sea floor | 5 |

Explain the differences in the results. **[4]**

**11.** Explain how plastic pollution can affect tourism in a coastal town. **[2]**

**12.** A small island has no recycling plant and limited landfill space. Plastic waste often blows into the sea. Describe and explain **three** strategies the island could use to reduce plastic pollution. **[6]**

**13.** "Recycling is the best way to deal with plastic pollution in the oceans." Discuss this statement. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** A plastic that is not decomposed by organisms and breaks down only over a long period of time [1].

**2.** Water [1]; biomass [1]; gases, carbon dioxide and methane [1].

**3.** Yes: they are less than 5 mm in length [1], and they are used in a commercial product, which is one of the sources of microplastics [1].

**4.** Any two, 1 mark each: temperature [1]; number of bacteria and fungi present [1]. Also accept: moisture, oxygen availability, light, thickness of the item.

**5.** Item, for example a plastic carrier bag, straw, cup or water bottle [1]; matching alternative, for example a cloth bag, paper straw, reusable cup or refillable metal bottle [1].

**6.** "Bioplastic" describes the raw material: the plastic is made fully or partly from biological materials rather than fossil fuels [1]; bioplastics can be biodegradable or non-biodegradable [1]; even if biodegradable, decomposition is slow in cold seawater with few decomposers, so the bottles may persist [1].

**7.** Sunlight, wave action and rubbing against rocks and sand break the plastic into smaller and smaller fragments [1]; pieces under 5 mm are microplastics [1]; the plastic is not decomposed, so it stays in the ecosystem and is now small enough to be eaten by plankton and filter feeders [1].

**8.** Animals such as turtles, seals and seabirds become entangled [1]; they cannot swim to the surface to breathe and drown, or cannot feed and starve [1]; lost nets keep trapping animals for years because they do not decompose [1].

**9. (a)** Total = 14 + 22 + 9 + 31 + 19 = 95; mean = 95 / 5 = 19 pieces per square metre [1].
**(b)** 19 × 2,500 = 47,500 pieces [1] [1].
**(c)** Use more quadrats, placed at random positions, to reduce the effect of uneven spread [1].

**10.** Decomposition is carried out by bacteria and fungi, which are most numerous in compost, so the compost heap has the greatest loss [1]; warmth and moisture in the compost and the warm lagoon speed up decomposition [1]; the deep sea floor is cold [1] and has little oxygen and few decomposers, so only 5% of the mass was lost [1].

**11.** Plastic litter on beaches and in the water is visual pollution, so fewer tourists visit [1]; hotels, restaurants and tour businesses lose income, and the town must pay for clean-ups [1].

**12.** Three strategies, 2 marks each. Avoid single-use plastics, for example a ban or charge on plastic bags and straws [1], which reduces the amount of plastic waste produced in the first place [1]. Safe disposal, with covered bins and regular collections [1], so waste cannot blow into the sea [1]. Legislation and enforcement, such as fines for littering or for dumping waste at sea [1], which deters people and businesses [1]. Also accept alternative packaging, or shipping waste to a recycling plant elsewhere, each with an explanation.

**13.** For: recycling reduces the amount of new plastic made and the waste that could reach the sea [1]; it turns waste into a resource and can create jobs [1]. Against: it does nothing for plastic already in the ocean, including microplastics that cannot be collected [1]; many plastics are hard or costly to recycle and quality falls each time [1]; reducing use, through avoiding single-use plastics and alternative packaging, stops plastic being made at all [1]. Judgement: recycling helps but is not the best single strategy; it works best combined with reducing use, safe disposal and legislation [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating "bioplastic" as meaning "biodegradable".
- Leaving out the 5 mm limit or the two sources of microplastics.
- Explaining slow decomposition without linking it to decomposers and abiotic conditions.
- Forgetting to multiply the mean by the area in an estimate question, or giving no unit.
- Agreeing with a statement in a "discuss" question without any counter-argument.
