## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Functional areas | The main areas, for example operations, marketing, finance, human resources |
| Hierarchy | The ranking of jobs from top to bottom |
| Level of hierarchy | One rank in that ranking |
| Chain of command | The line of authority from the top of the business to the bottom |
| Span of control | The number of employees who report directly to one manager |
| Delegation | Passing authority for a task and its decisions to someone lower down |
| Leadership style | The way a manager directs staff |
| Downsizing | Deliberately reducing the number of employees |
| Redundancy | Losing a job because the job is no longer needed |
| Trade union | An organisation of workers who join together to protect and improve their interests |

## 2.2.1 Structures

### Tall against flat

| | Tall | Flat |
|---|---|---|
| Levels of hierarchy | Many | Few |
| Chain of command | Long | Short |
| Span of control | Narrow | Wide |
| Communication | Slower, passes through many people | Faster |
| Promotion | Many steps | Few steps |
| Manager cost | Higher | Lower |

### Reading an organisational chart: steps

1. Start at the top box and count the rows. That gives the levels.
2. For any manager, count the boxes directly below. That is the span.
3. Trace a line from the bottom box up to the top. The number of links is the chain of command.
4. Add up every box if the question asks for the total employees.

Small reminder: a managing director has 3 managers. Each manager has 5 staff. There are 3 levels, the director's span is 3, a manager's span is 5, and the total is 1 + 3 + 15 = 19 employees.

### Flexible working

| Type | Benefit | Drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Home working | Saves travel time and cost for staff. Less office space needed | Harder to supervise. Staff may feel isolated |
| Flexible hours | Fits work around family life, which may motivate staff | Harder to arrange meetings and cover busy times |

### Part-time against full-time

| | Part-time | Full-time |
|---|---|---|
| For the business | Cover for busy times only. Often easier to recruit | Reliable, committed, knows the business |
| Against the business | More people to train and manage. Handovers | Paid for quiet times too. Less flexible |

## 2.2.2 Functions of management and delegation

| Function | Key idea | Clue words in a case |
|---|---|---|
| Planning | Set objectives and decide how to reach them | targets, forecast, decides what to launch |
| Organising | Arrange people, money, equipment, time | allocates staff, schedules, sets up teams |
| Coordinating | Make departments work together | joint meeting, links production and sales |
| Commanding | Give instructions and direct staff | tells, instructs, orders |
| Controlling | Compare actual with plan and act | checks results, corrects, monitors budgets |

**Delegation**

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Frees the manager for planning | Employee may lack skill |
| Staff feel trusted, so may be more motivated | Manager still responsible, may lose control |
| Staff gain experience | Takes time to explain |
| Faster decisions near the problem | Some staff see it as extra work |

## 2.2.3 Leadership styles

| Style | Who decides | Best when | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Autocratic | Leader alone | Urgent, unskilled or new staff, safety | Low motivation, ideas lost |
| Democratic | Leader with staff input | Staff are skilled and time allows | Slow, views may clash |
| Laissez-faire | Staff themselves | Expert, self-motivated, creative staff | Lack of direction |

### Method: recommend and justify a style

1. Pick out the evidence on time, staff skill, the task and staff mood.
2. State your choice.
3. Link each piece of evidence to the style.
4. Give one cost of your choice and one reason it is still the best option.
5. Say why one other style is less suitable.

## 2.2.4 Downsizing and redundancy

**Reasons for downsizing:** automation, reduced demand for products, need to lower costs.

**Costs of redundancy:** payments to those leaving, lower morale among staff who remain, loss of skills and knowledge, possible legal disputes if unfair.

### Method: who to make redundant

1. List the criteria the case allows: skills, performance, attendance, length of service, cost, flexibility, role.
2. Compare the people using the data given.
3. Choose one and give the main reason.
4. Show why the others are less suitable.
5. Admit the downside and say why you still choose them.

Small reminder: closing a line cuts 5 jobs paid 18,000 each, with 2,500 paid to each person leaving. Saving 5 x 18,000 = 90,000 a year. One-off cost 5 x 2,500 = 12,500. First-year net saving = 77,500, if there are no other costs.

## 2.2.5 Trade unions

A union is formed by workers, who pay a subscription to belong. Benefits of membership for employees:

- Collective voice with the employer, for example over pay and conditions.
- Advice and support in a dispute.
- Pressure for safe conditions and fair treatment.

Answer from the employee's viewpoint when the question asks about employees. Benefits to the employer or the economy are not asked for.

## Exam technique for this topic

- **Define** needs the precise meaning, so include both parts of a term such as span of control.
- **Identify** and **state** need a name or a short statement, with no explanation.
- **Explain** needs a point, then a reason or effect, in the context given.
- **Calculate** needs the working shown, in the units the question uses.
- **Justify** needs evidence from the case, a reason for your choice, and a reason why the alternative is less suitable.
- Use the business's own figures, such as span, hours or pay, in your answer wherever the case gives them.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Span of control against levels of hierarchy.** Span is across, levels are down.
- **Delegation against giving orders.** Delegation passes decision-making authority. Commanding is an instruction about how to do a task.
- **Democratic against laissez-faire.** In democratic leadership the leader still takes the final decision. In laissez-faire the staff decide.
- **Redundancy against dismissal for misconduct.** Redundancy is about the job, not the person's behaviour.
- **Downsizing against redundancy.** Downsizing is the policy of cutting staff. Redundancy is what happens to each employee affected.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the four example functional areas given in the syllabus.
2. Define span of control.
3. A manager has 3 supervisors, and each supervisor has 7 workers. How many supervisors and workers are under the manager in total?
4. Which kind of structure has the shorter chain of command, tall or flat?
5. State one benefit to an employee of home working.
6. A manager compares monthly sales with the target and takes action where sales are low. Which function of management is this?
7. State one disadvantage of delegation.
8. A leader sets a goal and leaves the team to decide how to reach it. Which style is this?
9. Define redundancy.
10. State one benefit to employees of belonging to a trade union.
11. A firm cuts 5 jobs paid 18,000 each, and pays 2,500 to each person leaving. Calculate the net saving in the first year.

### Answers

1. Operations, marketing, finance, human resources.
2. The number of employees who report directly to one manager.
3. 3 + (3 x 7) = 3 + 21 = 24.
4. Flat.
5. Saves travel time and cost (or fits work around home life).
6. Controlling.
7. The employee may make mistakes, or the manager may lose control but still be responsible.
8. Laissez-faire.
9. Losing a job because the job is no longer needed.
10. A group voice with the employer, or support in a dispute, or pressure for safe conditions.
11. 90,000 - 12,500 = 77,500.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a definition of span of control that mentions levels or the whole workforce.
- Describing a feature of a flat structure with no consequence for the business in the question.
- Treating coordinating and commanding as the same thing.
- Writing "delegation" for any task a manager hands out, with no authority passing down.
- Recommending a leadership style without evidence from the case on time, skills or staff mood.
- Choosing who to make redundant using only length of service, with no comparison across the people given.
- Giving only the advantages when asked to justify a recommendation.
- Describing benefits of a union for the employer when the question asks about employees.
