This guide covers subtopic **13.1, Formulas, functional groups and the naming of organic compounds**, from Topic 13, An introduction to AS Level organic chemistry, of Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027 series. It is AS Level content.

The [Organic Mechanisms: An Introduction](/resources/organic-mechanisms-an-introduction/) guide surveys all of Topic 13. This page concentrates on 13.1: the functional groups in the syllabus table, the conventions for each type of formula, a step-by-step naming method with worked examples for every AS homologous series, and deducing molecular and empirical formulas. Condensed notes are in the [13.1 revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-as-formulas-functional-groups-and-the-naming-of-organic-revision-notes/), and the [13.1 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-as-formulas-functional-groups-and-the-naming-of-organic-practice/) test it.

## What 13.1 asks you to do

1. Define a **hydrocarbon** as a compound made up of C and H atoms only.
2. Understand that **alkanes** are simple hydrocarbons with **no functional group**.
3. Understand that the compounds in the syllabus table contain a **functional group** which dictates their physical and chemical properties.
4. Interpret and use the **general, structural, displayed and skeletal** formulas of those classes of compound.
5. Use **systematic nomenclature** of simple aliphatic molecules with those functional groups, **up to six carbon atoms** (six plus six for esters; **straight chains only** for esters and nitriles).
6. Deduce the **molecular and/or empirical formula** of a compound from its structural, displayed or skeletal formula.

## The functional groups

A **functional group** is the atom or group of atoms that gives a homologous series its characteristic reactions. Every member of the series reacts in a similar way because they share it. Alkanes have no functional group; their C–C and C–H bonds are all that is present.

| Homologous series | Functional group | General formula | Name ending or prefix | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alkane | none | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | -ane | butane, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ |
| Alkene | C=C | CₙH₂ₙ | -ene | but-1-ene, CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ |
| Halogenoalkane | –X (halogen) | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁X | chloro-, bromo-, iodo- | 2-bromobutane, CH₃CHBrCH₂CH₃ |
| Alcohol | –OH (hydroxyl) | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH | -ol | butan-2-ol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ |
| Aldehyde | –CHO (carbonyl at chain end) | CₙH₂ₙO | -al | butanal, CH₃CH₂CH₂CHO |
| Ketone | C=O (carbonyl within chain) | CₙH₂ₙO | -one | butanone, CH₃COCH₂CH₃ |
| Carboxylic acid | –COOH (carboxyl) | CₙH₂ₙO₂ | -oic acid | butanoic acid, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH |
| Ester | –COO– (ester) | CₙH₂ₙO₂ | alkyl -oate | ethyl ethanoate, CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ |
| Amine (primary) | –NH₂ | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁NH₂ | -amine | butylamine, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂NH₂ |
| Nitrile | –C≡N | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁CN | -nitrile | butanenitrile, CH₃CH₂CH₂CN |

Halogenoalkanes and alcohols are further classed as **primary, secondary or tertiary** by the number of carbon atoms attached to the carbon carrying the functional group (one, two or three).

## The types of formula

| Type | What it shows | Example: propan-1-ol |
|---|---|---|
| Molecular | The actual number of atoms of each element | C₃H₈O |
| Empirical | The simplest whole-number ratio of atoms | C₃H₈O (already simplest) |
| General | A formula for the whole series, in terms of n | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH |
| Structural | Enough detail to show the arrangement unambiguously, without drawing bonds | CH₃CH₂CH₂OH |
| Displayed | Every atom and every bond drawn | All 12 atoms and 11 bonds shown |
| Skeletal | Carbon chain as a zigzag; C atoms and H atoms on C omitted; functional groups shown | A zigzag of three carbons with OH on one end |

**Conventions in structural formulas** that examiners check:

- Aldehydes are written **–CHO**, never –COH (which would suggest an alcohol).
- Ketones: CH₃COCH₃. Acids: –COOH. Esters: **RCOOR′**, with the acid part first, for example CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ for methyl propanoate.
- Branches go in brackets after the carbon they are attached to: CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃ is methylpropane.

**Reading a skeletal formula.** Every line end and every vertex is a carbon atom. Each carbon has enough hydrogen atoms to make four bonds. Any atom other than C or H, and any H attached to an atom other than carbon, is drawn in. A double line is a double bond. So a zigzag of four carbons with a double line between the first two is but-1-ene, CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃.

## Naming: the step-by-step method

1. **Find the principal functional group.** It decides the suffix.
2. **Find the longest carbon chain that contains it.** The stem: meth (1), eth (2), prop (3), but (4), pent (5), hex (6). For nitriles, the carbon of C≡N **counts** in the chain.
3. **Number the chain** from the end that gives the principal group the **lowest number**. For aldehydes, acids and nitriles the group is always carbon 1, so no number is written.
4. **Name the side groups** as prefixes: methyl, ethyl, chloro, bromo, iodo. List them in **alphabetical order**, using di- and tri- for repeats (the multiplier does not count for alphabetical order).
5. If the principal group gives a tie, number to give the **lowest set of numbers** to the substituents. If there is still a tie, the substituent first in alphabetical order gets the lower number.
6. **Punctuate**: commas between numbers, hyphens between numbers and letters.

### Worked examples

| Structural formula | Reasoning | Name |
|---|---|---|
| HOCH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₃ | Longest chain with C–OH: 4 C; OH on C1; methyl on C3 | **3-methylbutan-1-ol** |
| CH₃CHBrCH(CH₃)CH₃ | 4 C chain; substituents on 2 and 3 from either end; bromo comes first alphabetically, so it takes 2 | **2-bromo-3-methylbutane** |
| CH₃CH₂C(CH₃)₂CHO | CHO is C1; two methyls on C2 | **2,2-dimethylbutanal** |
| CH₃COCH(CH₃)CH₃ | 4 C chain; C=O on C2; methyl on C3 | **3-methylbutan-2-one** |
| CH₂=C(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ | 4 C chain containing C=C; double bond starts at C1; methyl on C2 | **2-methylbut-1-ene** |
| CH₃CHClCH₂COOH | COOH is C1; Cl on C3 | **3-chlorobutanoic acid** |
| CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CN | 5 C including the C of CN | **pentanenitrile** |
| CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ | Alcohol part: ethyl; acid part: butanoate (4 C including C=O) | **ethyl butanoate** |
| HCOOCH₃ | Alcohol part: methyl; acid part: methanoate | **methyl methanoate** |

**Naming esters.** Split the ester at the single-bonded O. The part attached to that O through carbon came from the **alcohol** and is named first as an alkyl group. The part containing C=O came from the **acid** and ends in -oate. So CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ is **propyl ethanoate**, whereas CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ is **ethyl propanoate**. Both are C₅H₁₀O₂, so getting the order right matters.

## Deducing molecular and empirical formulas

Count every atom, including the hydrogens implied in a skeletal formula, then simplify the ratio.

| Compound | Molecular formula | Empirical formula |
|---|---|---|
| Hexane, CH₃(CH₂)₄CH₃ | C₆H₁₄ | C₃H₇ |
| Ethyl ethanoate, CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ | C₄H₈O₂ | C₂H₄O |
| Butanoic acid, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH | C₄H₈O₂ | C₂H₄O |
| Pentan-2-ol (skeletal: five-carbon zigzag, OH on the second carbon) | C₅H₁₂O | C₅H₁₂O |
| Cyclohexene | C₆H₁₀ | C₃H₅ |

**Worked example.** A skeletal formula shows a six-carbon zigzag with =O on the second carbon. Name it and give its molecular and empirical formulas.

The carbonyl is within the chain, so it is a ketone: **hexan-2-one**. Carbons: 6. Hydrogens: C1 has 3, C2 has 0, C3 to C5 have 2 each (6), C6 has 3, total 12. Oxygen: 1. Molecular formula **C₆H₁₂O**. The ratio 6 : 12 : 1 cannot be simplified, so the empirical formula is also **C₆H₁₂O**. It fits CₙH₂ₙO, the general formula of aldehydes and ketones.

## Common mistakes

- **Not finding the longest chain.** "2-ethylbutane" is really 3-methylpentane; "1-methylpropane" is butane.
- **Numbering from the wrong end.** "Pentan-4-ol" should be pentan-2-ol.
- **Numbers on aldehydes and acids.** Write propanal, not propan-1-al.
- **Esters named backwards.** Alcohol part first, acid part second.
- **Forgetting the nitrile carbon.** CH₃CH₂CN is propanenitrile, not ethanenitrile.
- **Writing –COH for an aldehyde.**
- **Missing implied hydrogens in skeletal formulas** when working out a molecular formula.
- **Giving alkanes a functional group.** They have none: C–C and C–H bonds are not functional groups.

## Exam technique: command words in 13.1

- **Name** (or "give the systematic name"): spelling, numbers and punctuation all count.
- **Draw** (displayed formula): every bond, including O–H and C–H.
- **Draw** (skeletal formula): zigzag carbon chain, functional groups shown, no C or H on carbon.
- **Deduce** (formulas): count from the structure given; show the simplification for the empirical formula.
- **Identify** (functional group): name the group as the syllabus does, for example "carbonyl" or "hydroxyl".
