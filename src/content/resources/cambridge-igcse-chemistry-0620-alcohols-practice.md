## Section A: short questions

**1.** State the functional group present in all alcohols. **[1]**

**2.** Which set of conditions is used to make ethanol by fermentation?

A: 300 °C, 6000 kPa, acid catalyst

B: 25–35 °C, yeast, absence of oxygen

C: 25–35 °C, yeast, plentiful supply of oxygen

D: 300 °C, yeast, absence of oxygen

**[1]**

**4.** State **two** uses of ethanol. **[2]**

**5.** Write the balanced symbol equation for the complete combustion of ethanol. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Name the alcohol with the structural formula CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**8.** A student sets up a fermentation experiment using a flask of aqueous glucose and yeast, fitted with a tube that lets gas out but stops air getting in.

**(a)** Explain why air must be kept out of the flask. **[1]**

**(b)** The student keeps the flask in a water bath at 30 °C. Explain why the temperature should not be raised to 60 °C. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the reaction would be very slow if the flask were kept at 10 °C. **[1]**

**(d)** After several days, the mixture contains dilute ethanol and yeast. Name the method used to remove the yeast and the method used to obtain more concentrated ethanol. **[2]**

**9.** Ethanol is manufactured from ethene.

**(a)** Name the other reactant. **[1]**

**(b)** State the temperature and pressure used. **[2]**

**(c)** State the type of catalyst used. **[1]**

**(d)** Write the equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Compare the manufacture of ethanol by fermentation with its manufacture from ethene. Give **two** advantages of fermentation and **two** advantages of the ethene method. **[4]**

**11.** *(Extended)* This question uses the reacting-mass calculations from Topic 3. Glucose is fermented:

C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂

**(a)** Calculate the relative formula mass, Mᵣ, of glucose. (Aᵣ: C = 12, H = 1, O = 16) **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of ethanol that could be made from 45 g of glucose. **[2]**

**12.** Propan-1-ol, C₃H₇OH, can also be used as a fuel.

**(a)** Balance the equation for its complete combustion.

...C₃H₇OH + ...O₂ → ...CO₂ + ...H₂O **[2]**

**(b)** *(Extended)* Draw the structural formula of propan-2-ol, an isomer of propan-1-ol. **[1]**

**13.** A student writes: "All ethanol is made from a renewable resource." Explain why this is not true for ethanol made by the catalytic addition of steam to ethene. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Hydroxyl group / –OH [1].

**2.** **B** [1]. A is the condition set for the ethene method; C includes oxygen, which must be excluded; D mixes the two methods (yeast would be destroyed at 300 °C).

**3.** Carbon dioxide [1].

**4.** Solvent [1]; fuel [1].

**5.** C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O. Correct formulae of all reactants and products [1]; correct balancing [1].

**6.** Butan-1-ol [1].

**7.** It contains oxygen as well as carbon and hydrogen; a hydrocarbon contains hydrogen and carbon only [1].

**8.** **(a)** If oxygen is present, the yeast respires aerobically, producing carbon dioxide and water instead of ethanol [1]. *Accept: fermentation is anaerobic.*

**(b)** The enzymes in the yeast would be denatured, so fermentation would stop [1].

**(c)** At low temperature the enzymes work very slowly / particles have less energy, so fewer successful collisions [1].

**(d)** Filtration [1]; fractional distillation [1].

**9.** **(a)** Steam / water vapour [1].

**(b)** 300 °C [1]; 6000 kPa / 60 atm [1].

**(c)** Acid (catalyst) [1].

**(d)** C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH [1].

**10.** Fermentation, any two [2]:

- uses a renewable raw material (sugar from crops);
- low temperature / normal pressure, so less energy needed.

Ethene method, any two [2]:

- much faster;
- gives pure ethanol, so no distillation needed;
- continuous process.

*Do not credit "cheaper" or "better for the environment" without a reason.*

**11.** **(a)** (6 × 12) + (12 × 1) + (6 × 16) = **180** [1].

**(b)** Moles of glucose = 45 ÷ 180 = 0.25 mol; moles of ethanol = 2 × 0.25 = 0.50 mol [1]. Mᵣ of ethanol = 46, so mass = 0.50 × 46 = **23 g** [1].

**12.** **(a)** 2C₃H₇OH + 9O₂ → 6CO₂ + 8H₂O. Correct CO₂ and H₂O coefficients [1]; correct O₂ coefficient [1].
*Check: O on the left = 2 + 18 = 20; on the right = 12 + 8 = 20. Accept C₃H₇OH + 4½O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O.*

**(b)** CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ [1].

**13.** The ethene used is obtained by cracking alkanes from petroleum [1]; petroleum is a fossil fuel that cannot be replaced once used, so it is non-renewable [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping the conditions for the two methods, especially putting yeast with 300 °C.
- Writing "warm" for the fermentation temperature instead of 25–35 °C.
- Forgetting carbon dioxide as the second product of fermentation.
- Using 3½O₂ for ethanol combustion by forgetting the oxygen atom already in the alcohol.
- Giving vague advantages such as "cheaper" or "more efficient".
- Forgetting that fermentation produces dilute ethanol that still has to be distilled.
