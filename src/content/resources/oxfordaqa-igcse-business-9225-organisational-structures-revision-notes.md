Condensed recall for section 3.4.1 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-organisational-structures/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- **Internal organisational structures**, including **span of control, chain of command, delayering and delegation**.
- **Why** businesses have structures, and the **job roles and responsibilities** in them.
- The impact of a **tall or flat** structure on how a business is managed.
- **Centralisation and decentralisation**.
- The **appropriateness** of structures for a business.
- How structure may affect **communication**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organisational structure | How a business is organised: levels, roles and who reports to whom |
| Organisation chart | A diagram of the structure |
| Chain of command | Line of authority from the top to the bottom |
| Span of control | Number of people who report directly to one manager |
| Delegation | Passing authority for a task or decision to a lower level; the manager stays responsible |
| Delayering | Removing one or more levels from the structure |
| Tall structure | Many levels and narrow spans |
| Flat structure | Few levels and wide spans |
| Centralisation | Decisions made at the top |
| Decentralisation | Decisions passed down to lower levels |

## Levels and roles

| Level | Roles | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Top | Directors | Aims, strategy, major decisions |
| Middle | Managers | Plans, budgets, managing supervisors |
| Lower | Supervisors | Day-to-day organisation of staff |
| Operational | Workers | Carry out tasks |

## Tall against flat

| | Tall | Flat |
|---|---|---|
| Levels | Many | Few |
| Span of control | Narrow | Wide |
| Supervision | Close | Looser |
| Communication | Slow, may change on the way | Faster, more direct |
| Cost | Higher (more managers) | Lower |
| Promotion | Many steps | Few steps |
| Staff responsibility | Less | More |

## Delegation and delayering

**Delegation**
- Benefits: frees managers; develops and motivates staff; faster decisions.
- Drawbacks: task may be done less well; staff need training; manager is still responsible.

**Delayering**
- Benefits: lower costs; faster communication; more responsibility for remaining staff.
- Drawbacks: bigger spans overload managers; redundancy costs; falling morale; fewer promotion steps.

## Centralisation against decentralisation

| | Centralised | Decentralised |
|---|---|---|
| Who decides | Senior managers | Local or department managers |
| Speed | Slower | Faster |
| Consistency | High | May vary |
| Local knowledge | Less | More |
| Motivation of managers | Lower | Higher |
| Risk | Senior managers overloaded | Poor local decisions; loss of control |

## Communication

- More levels means more **delays** and more chance that messages are **changed** or lost.
- Flat structures allow **direct** contact between staff and senior managers.
- Decentralised structures encourage **two-way** communication.
- A message taking one day per level through 5 levels takes 4 days; through 3 levels it takes 2 days.

## Choosing a structure

| Situation | Suited to |
|---|---|
| Small business | Flat and simple |
| Factory with strict safety rules | Taller, closer supervision |
| Creative or fast-changing business | Flat and decentralised |
| Branches in several countries | Decentralised |

The best structure can change as the business grows.

## Calculation practice

- Number of people at each level: span x people at the level above. Span of 4 under 1 director gives 4 managers, then 16 supervisors, then 64 workers.
- Span of control = number of workers / number of supervisors.
- Cost saving from delayering = number of posts removed x salary, less one-off costs such as redundancy.

Example: removing 10 supervisors on $30,000 saves $300,000 a year; redundancy at $5,000 each costs $50,000 once, so the first-year saving is $250,000.

## Exam traps

- Span of control is **not** the number of levels.
- Delegation passes authority, **not** responsibility.
- Tall and flat each have both advantages and drawbacks. Judge them against the business described.
- Explain how a feature affects **communication** or **motivation**, not just that it does.
- Delayering is removing levels, not simply sacking workers.
- Use case details such as the numbers of levels, spans and staff.

## Self-test

1. Define "chain of command".
2. Define "span of control".
3. A supervisor manages 7 workers. State the span of control.
4. Give two features of a flat structure.
5. State one advantage and one disadvantage of a tall structure.
6. Define delegation.
7. Give one reason why a business might delayer.
8. Give one risk of delayering.
9. State one benefit of decentralisation.
10. Explain how a tall structure can slow down communication.
11. A business removes 8 supervisors paid $28,000 each. Calculate the annual saving.
12. A firm has 1 director, with a span of 5, 3 levels in all. How many people are at the bottom level?

### Answers

1. The line of authority from the top of a business to the bottom, showing who reports to whom.
2. The number of people who report directly to one manager.
3. **7**.
4. Any two: few levels; wide spans of control; faster communication; lower costs; more responsibility for staff.
5. Advantage: close supervision (or clear promotion path). Disadvantage: slow communication (or higher costs).
6. Passing authority to a lower level to carry out a task or make a decision, while the manager stays responsible.
7. To cut costs, speed up communication or give staff more responsibility.
8. Any one: remaining managers are overloaded; staff morale falls; redundancy costs; fewer promotion chances.
9. Faster decisions that fit local needs (or motivated local managers).
10. Messages pass through many levels, each adding delay and the chance of error.
11. 8 x $28,000 = **$224,000** a year.
12. Level 1: 1 director; level 2: 5; level 3: 5 x 5 = **25** people.
