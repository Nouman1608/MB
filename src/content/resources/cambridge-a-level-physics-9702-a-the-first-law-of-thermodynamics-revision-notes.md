Subtopic 16.2 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers W = pΔV at constant pressure, the difference between work done by and on a gas, and the first law ΔU = q + W. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read [The first law of thermodynamics study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-the-first-law-of-thermodynamics/); to apply the ideas, try the [First law practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-the-first-law-of-thermodynamics-practice/).

## Work done at constant pressure

```
W = pΔV         (p in Pa, ΔV in m³, W in J)
```

- Derivation: F = pA; work = FΔx = pAΔx = pΔV.
- **Constant pressure only.**
- pΔV is the work done **by** the gas when it expands.
- No volume change → **no work**, whatever the pressure does.
- On a p–V graph: area under a horizontal line = pΔV; area enclosed by a cycle = net work done per cycle (by the gas if the cycle runs clockwise).

| Conversion | Factor |
|---|---|
| cm³ → m³ | × 10⁻⁶ |
| dm³ (litre) → m³ | × 10⁻³ |
| kPa → Pa | × 10³ |

## The first law

```
ΔU = q + W
```

| Symbol | Meaning | + when | − when |
|---|---|---|---|
| ΔU | Increase in internal energy | U rises (ideal gas: T rises) | U falls |
| q | Energy transferred **to** the system by heating | Heated | Loses energy by heating |
| W | Work done **on** the system | Compressed | Expands |

**Translation rule:** "the gas does 200 J of work" means W = −200 J. "200 J of work is done on the gas" means W = +200 J.

## Special cases

| Condition | Consequence | Typical example |
|---|---|---|
| Constant volume | W = 0, ΔU = q | Gas heated in a rigid container |
| No heating (rapid change, insulated) | q = 0, ΔU = W | Pump compressed quickly: air warms; aerosol gas expanding: cools |
| Ideal gas, constant temperature | ΔU = 0, q = −W | Slow compression in a cylinder kept in a water bath |
| Complete cycle | ΔU = 0, net q = net work done by gas | Engine cycle on a p–V graph |
| Constant pressure | W = −pΔV, ΔU = q − pΔV | Gas heated under a free piston |

## Method for any first-law question

1. Identify the system (usually "the gas").
2. Decide the sign of each term from the physics: heated or cooled? expanding or compressed? temperature up or down?
3. Calculate W from pΔV if the pressure is constant.
4. Substitute into ΔU = q + W.
5. Interpret: the sign of ΔU tells you whether the temperature of an ideal gas rises or falls.

## Tables for cycles

- Fill W first (pΔV, sign from the volume change; zero for vertical lines on a p–V graph).
- Then ΔU (from the change of state, or given).
- Then q = ΔU − W.
- Checks: ΔU column sums to zero; net q = −(net W) = area enclosed.
- For a monatomic ideal gas, U = (3/2)pV gives ΔU straight from the state values.

## Boiling and the first law

When a liquid boils at atmospheric pressure, the energy supplied by heating (mL) does two jobs: it raises the potential energy of the molecules (ΔU) and it does work pushing back the atmosphere as the volume increases greatly (pΔV). So ΔU = mL − pΔV, slightly less than mL.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| First law of thermodynamics | ΔU = q + W: the increase in internal energy equals the energy supplied by heating plus the work done on the system |
| Heating (q) | Energy transfer caused by a temperature difference |
| Work done on a gas | Energy transferred to a gas by a force from the surroundings moving its boundary (compression) |
| Work done by a gas | Energy transferred from a gas to its surroundings as it expands, pΔV at constant pressure |
| Function of state | A quantity, such as U, that depends only on the present state, so ΔU = 0 round a cycle |

## Exam traps

- ΔU = q + pΔV for an expansion: the sign of the work is wrong.
- Using W = pΔV when the pressure changes.
- Assuming q = 0 means no temperature change.
- Assuming constant temperature means no energy transfer.
- Forgetting cm³ → m³.
- Stating the first law without defining the symbols or their directions.
- Mixing "heat" with "internal energy".

## Self-test

1. State the first law of thermodynamics, defining each term.
2. Derive W = pΔV.
3. A gas expands at 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa from 1.0 × 10⁻³ m³ to 1.6 × 10⁻³ m³. Calculate the work done by the gas.
4. For Question 3, what is W in the first law?
5. A gas in a rigid container receives 250 J by heating. Find ΔU.
6. A gas is compressed quickly, with no heating, by 90 J of work. Find ΔU and say what happens to its temperature.
7. An ideal gas expands at constant temperature, doing 140 J of work. Find q.
8. A gas absorbs 500 J by heating and does 180 J of work. Find ΔU.
9. A gas loses 300 J by heating while 120 J of work is done on it. Find ΔU.
10. Why is ΔU = 0 for a complete cycle?
11. A gas is compressed at a constant 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa by 30 cm³. Find the work done on it.
12. What does the area enclosed by a cycle on a p–V graph represent?

**Answers:** 1. ΔU = q + W, where ΔU is the increase in internal energy, q the energy transferred to the system by heating and W the work done on the system. 2. F = pA; W = FΔx = pAΔx = pΔV, since AΔx = ΔV. 3. pΔV = 2.0 × 10⁵ × 0.6 × 10⁻³ = 120 J. 4. W = −120 J. 5. W = 0, so ΔU = +250 J. 6. ΔU = +90 J; the temperature rises. 7. ΔU = 0, W = −140 J, so q = +140 J. 8. ΔU = 500 + (−180) = +320 J. 9. ΔU = −300 + 120 = −180 J. 10. The gas returns to its starting state and U is a function of state. 11. 1.0 × 10⁵ × 30 × 10⁻⁶ = 3.0 J. 12. The net work done by the gas in one cycle (equal to the net energy supplied by heating).
