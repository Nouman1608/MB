## Questions

**1.** Here are six formulas: CH₄, SiO₂, NaCl, NH₃, Cu, H₂O. Give the **three** that are substances made of small molecules. **[3]**

**2.** Describe what a covalent bond is, and explain why it is a strong bond. **[3]**

**3.** This question is about a dot and cross diagram of a hydrogen chloride molecule, HCl.

**(a)** State the number of electrons shared between the two atoms. **[1]**
**(b)** State the number of non-shared pairs of electrons on the chlorine atom. **[1]**
**(c)** State the total number of electrons in the outer shell of the chlorine atom in the molecule. **[1]**

**4.** Oxygen gas is made of O₂ molecules.

**(a)** State the number of electrons shared between the two oxygen atoms. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain, in terms of electrons, why the two oxygen atoms are joined by a double bond. **[3]**

**5.** Diagram X is a displayed formula:

```
    H   H   H
    |   |   |
H - C - C - C - H
    |   |   |
    H   H   H
```

**(a)** Give the molecular formula of X. **[1]**
**(b)** State the number of covalent bonds in one molecule of X. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the total number of electrons shared in one molecule of X. **[1]**

A ball and stick model of molecule Y shows two red balls joined to each other by one stick, with one white ball joined to each red ball. Red = oxygen, white = hydrogen.

**(d)** Give the molecular formula of Y and explain why it should not be written as HO. **[2]**

**6.** Methane can be shown as a dot and cross diagram, a 2D displayed formula, a ball and stick model and a 3D space-filling model.

**(a)** Give one limitation of the 2D displayed formula. **[1]**
**(b)** Give one limitation of the ball and stick model. **[1]**
**(c)** Give one thing the dot and cross diagram shows that the displayed formula does not. **[1]**
**(d)** Give one limitation of the 3D space-filling model. **[1]**

**7.** Silicon carbide is a giant covalent structure. Each silicon atom is covalently bonded to four carbon atoms, and each carbon atom is covalently bonded to four silicon atoms.

**(a)** Deduce the formula of silicon carbide. Explain your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** Silicon dioxide and carbon dioxide have formulas of the same pattern, SiO₂ and CO₂. Explain why you cannot tell from the formula alone which one is a giant covalent structure. **[2]**
**(c)** State the number of covalent bonds formed by each carbon atom in diamond. **[1]**

**8.** Poly(ethene) is a polymer made from repeating units of formula C₂H₄.

**(a)** Draw the repeating unit of poly(ethene), using a line to represent each single covalent bond. **[2]**
**(b)** State what *n* represents in the polymer formula. **[1]**
**(c)** One poly(ethene) chain has *n* = 5000. Calculate the number of carbon atoms and the number of hydrogen atoms in this chain. **[2]**

**9.** This question is about metallic bonding.

**(a)** Describe the structure of a solid metal. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why metallic bonds are strong. **[2]**
**(c)** State how many delocalised electrons each atom provides in sodium (Group 1) and in magnesium (Group 2). **[2]**

**10.** Five diagrams are described below.

- **A**: rows of circles marked +, in a regular pattern, with e⁻ symbols scattered between them.
- **B**: many separate units, each one N atom joined to three H atoms, with space between the units.
- **C**: a network of atoms, each joined to four others, with bonds running off every edge of the diagram.
- **D**: a C–C unit with two H atoms on each C, drawn in brackets with *n* after the bracket.
- **E**: alternating + and − ions in a regular lattice.

**(a)** For each of A, B, C and D, state whether it shows a metallic giant structure, small molecules, a giant covalent structure or a polymer. **[4]**
**(b)** Give the feature of diagram C that shows it is a giant structure. **[1]**

**11.** Methane (CH₄) and diamond (C) both contain carbon atoms that form covalent bonds. Compare the bonding and structure of methane and diamond. **[6]**

**12.** Chlorine exists as Cl₂ molecules. Copper is a metal. Compare the bonding in a chlorine molecule with the bonding in solid copper. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** **CH₄** [1]; **NH₃** [1]; **H₂O** [1]. (SiO₂ is giant covalent, NaCl is ionic, Cu is metallic.) **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Extra wrong formulas usually cancel correct ones, so give exactly three.

**2.** A **shared pair of electrons** [1] between two (non-metal) **atoms** [1]. It is strong because there is **strong electrostatic attraction** between the shared pair and the **nuclei of both atoms** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Sharing electrons" alone is not enough for the first mark: the word **pair** is the creditworthy point.

**3. (a)** **2** electrons (one shared pair) [1]
**(b)** **3** non-shared pairs [1]
**(c)** **8** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answering "1" to (a) confuses one pair with one electron and scores zero.

**4. (a)** **4** electrons (two shared pairs) [1]
**(b)** Each oxygen atom has **6 outer electrons** [1]; each needs **2 more** to fill its outer shell [1]; so the atoms share **two pairs**, giving each oxygen 8 outer electrons [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The explanation needs the link from 6 electrons to "needs 2" to "two pairs shared"; stating "it has a double bond" repeats the question and earns nothing.

**5. (a)** **C₃H₈** [1]
**(b)** 2 C–C + 8 C–H = **10** bonds [1]
**(c)** 10 × 2 = **20** electrons [1]
**(d)** **H₂O₂** [1]; a molecular formula gives the **actual number** of each atom in one molecule, which is 2 H and 2 O, not a simplified ratio [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c), allow error carried forward from an incorrect bond count in (b), provided you multiply by 2.

**6. (a)** It does not show the **3D shape** of the molecule / it does not show the electrons [1]
**(b)** Any one: bonds are not physical sticks; atoms are drawn too far apart; atoms not to scale; no electrons shown [1]
**(c)** Which atom each bonding electron came from / that each bond is a **shared pair** of electrons [1]
**(d)** It is hard to see the bonds, or how many bonds there are [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A limitation must fit the named model; "it is not 3D" for the ball and stick model scores zero because that model is 3D.

**7. (a)** **SiC** [1]. Every Si is bonded to 4 C and every C is bonded to 4 Si, so the ratio of Si to C atoms is **1 : 1** [1].
**(b)** Both formulas show 1 atom of one element to 2 of the other [1]; for a giant structure the formula shows only the **ratio** of atoms, not the number in a molecule, so the formula does not show the structure [1].
**(c)** **4** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a) the formula with no explanation earns only the first mark; the 1 : 1 ratio must be justified from the bonding.

**8. (a)** One C–C single bond with two H atoms on each C, shown with single lines [1]; square brackets around the unit with a bond line passing through each bracket and *n* written after it [1].

```
   [  H   H  ]
   [  |   |  ]
 --[--C---C--]--
   [  |   |  ]
   [  H   H  ] n
```

**(b)** A **large number**: the number of repeating units in the chain [1]
**(c)** Carbon: 2 × 5000 = **10 000** [1]; hydrogen: 4 × 5000 = **20 000** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The bracket mark needs the bond lines to go **through** the brackets; brackets drawn around the unit with no extension bonds usually lose that mark.

**9. (a)** A **giant structure** of atoms arranged in a **regular pattern** [1]; the **outer-shell electrons are delocalised** and free to move through the whole structure [1].
**(b)** There is **strong electrostatic attraction** between the **positive metal ions** and the **delocalised electrons** [1]; these electrons are shared throughout the structure, so the attraction acts on every ion, in all directions [1].
**(c)** Sodium: **1** [1]; magnesium: **2** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Free electrons" or "sea of electrons" without the idea that they are outer-shell electrons moving through the whole structure usually does not score the delocalised mark.

**10. (a)** A: **metallic giant structure** [1]; B: **small molecules** (ammonia) [1]; C: **giant covalent structure** [1]; D: **polymer** [1]
**(b)** The bonds run off every edge, showing the network **continues in all directions** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* E is ionic and is not asked about; classifying it earns nothing.

**11.** Indicative content, one mark per point, up to 6:
- In both, each carbon atom has **4 outer electrons** and forms **4 covalent bonds** [1].
- Each covalent bond is a **shared pair of electrons**, so each carbon has **8 outer electrons** [1].
- In methane, each carbon bonds to **4 hydrogen atoms** [1].
- Methane is a **small molecule** with a fixed number of atoms, CH₄ [1].
- In diamond, each carbon bonds to **4 other carbon atoms** [1].
- Diamond is a **giant covalent structure**: the network continues in all directions with a huge number of atoms, so its formula is just C [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An answer describing only one substance is capped, because "compare" needs points about both.

**12.** Indicative content, one mark per point, up to 6:
- Chlorine: each Cl atom has **7 outer electrons** and needs 1 more [1].
- The two atoms **share one pair** of electrons, a **single covalent bond** [1].
- The shared pair is held **between those two atoms only**; each Cl has 3 non-shared pairs and 8 outer electrons [1].
- Copper: a **giant structure** of atoms in a **regular pattern** [1].
- The **outer-shell electrons are delocalised**, free to move through the whole structure [1].
- **Strong electrostatic attraction** between the positive ions and the delocalised electrons forms strong metallic bonds [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The key difference is **localised** (between two atoms) versus **delocalised** (through the whole structure); "covalent shares, metallic does not" is wrong, as metallic bonding also shares electrons.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing electrons with pairs: a single bond is 2 shared electrons.
- Dot and cross diagrams with a shared pair shown as two dots, or placed outside the overlap.
- Leaving off non-shared pairs on N, O or Cl, so the outer shell totals fewer than 8.
- Cancelling a molecular formula (H₂O₂ to HO, N₂H₄ to NH₂).
- Treating SiO₂ as a small molecule because its formula resembles CO₂.
- Limitations that do not match the named model, or vague ones such as "it is just a model".
- Polymer repeating units with no bond lines through the brackets, or no *n*.
- Metallic bonding answers that miss "regular pattern", "delocalised" or "whole structure".
