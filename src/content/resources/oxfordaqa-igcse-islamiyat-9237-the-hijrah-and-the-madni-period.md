**This guide covers content area 3.2.1.7 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), The Hijrah and the Madni Period: "The events leading up to the Hijrah including the pledges of Aqaba", "Hazrat Muhammad's (pbuh) journey from Makkah to Madinah, including the roles of Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA) and Hazrat Ali (RA)", "Early life in Madinah, including Hazrat Muhammad's (pbuh) role as a statesman and the establishment of the Charter of Madinah" and "The establishment of the first mosque in Madinah."** This page follows the whole story as a timeline, which helps with questions that ask you to put events in order or explain how one event led to the next. The other Hijrah guide on this site is organised by topic; use both.

## Timeline at a glance

| Date (CE) | Event |
|---|---|
| About 619 | Year of Sorrow: Abu Talib and Khadija (RA) die |
| About 620 | First contact with a small group of the Khazraj at the pilgrimage |
| 621 | First pledge of Aqaba (twelve men); Mus'ab ibn Umair (RA) sent to Yathrib |
| 622 (early) | Second pledge of Aqaba (more than seventy men and two women) |
| 622 | Muslims migrate to Yathrib in small groups |
| 622 | The Quraysh plot; Hijrah of the Prophet (pbuh) and Abu Bakr (RA) |
| 622 | Arrival at Quba; first mosque; entry to Yathrib, which becomes Madinah |
| 622 to 623 | Building of the Prophet's Mosque; brotherhood; Charter of Madinah |

Some dates are approximate. Examiners expect the order of events, not exact days.

## Stage 1: why Makkah had become impossible

Before the Hijrah, the Prophet (pbuh) had endured insults, the boycott and the rejection at Ta'if. With Abu Talib gone, he had no strong tribal protector. Each pilgrimage season he met visiting tribes and offered them the message. Most refused, and some demanded a price in power. He needed a place where Muslims could worship in safety and build a community.

## Stage 2: the pledges of Aqaba

Yathrib (later Madinah) was an oasis about 250 miles north of Makkah. Its two main Arab tribes, the **Aws** and the **Khazraj**, had fought a long war and were exhausted. Jewish tribes in the city spoke of a coming prophet, which made the people more willing to listen.

1. **First contact.** Six men of the Khazraj met the Prophet (pbuh) at Aqaba, a valley near Mina, listened to the Qur'an and accepted Islam. They returned home and spoke of him.
2. **First pledge.** The following year twelve men met him there. They promised not to worship anyone but Allah, not to steal, commit adultery, kill their children or slander, and to obey him in what was good. The Prophet (pbuh) sent **Mus'ab ibn Umair (RA)** to teach them. By the next season, almost every household in Yathrib had someone who had accepted Islam.
3. **Second pledge.** More than seventy men and two women came in secret at night. This time they promised to defend the Prophet (pbuh) as they defended their own families and invited him to live among them. The Prophet (pbuh) appointed twelve leaders from among them.

**Why the pledges matter:** they gave Islam its first home and its first protectors; they began the idea of a community held together by belief, not by tribe; and they made the Hijrah possible.

## Stage 3: the departure

After the second pledge, the Prophet (pbuh) told the Muslims to go to Yathrib. They left in small groups, secretly, over some weeks. The Quraysh tried to stop some, and some families were separated. The Prophet (pbuh) stayed, waiting for the command of Allah. Abu Bakr (RA) asked to come with him, and was told to wait.

When the Quraysh saw the Muslims leaving, they feared that the Prophet (pbuh) would gather an army. At the Dar al-Nadwa they agreed on the plot of one man from each clan striking together (see 8:30).

## Stage 4: the journey

| Night or day | Event |
|---|---|
| Night of the plot | The Prophet (pbuh) is told by Jibril (AS) not to sleep in his bed. Ali (RA) lies in his place wearing his green cloak. The Prophet (pbuh) leaves through the ring of men, reciting verses of Surah Ya-Sin |
| Same night | He joins Abu Bakr (RA); they leave Makkah heading south toward Yemen, in the opposite direction from Madinah |
| Three nights | They hide in the **Cave of Thawr**. Abu Bakr's son Abdullah brings news at night; his daughter Asma (RA) brings food; his servant Amir ibn Fuhayra grazes his flock over their tracks |
| Searchers arrive | Quraysh trackers reach the cave mouth. Abu Bakr (RA) fears for the Prophet (pbuh), who says, "Do not grieve; indeed Allah is with us" (9:40) |
| Departure | Their guide, Abdullah ibn Urayqit, brings the camels. They go by the coast road |
| The pursuit | The Quraysh offer a reward of one hundred camels. Suraqa ibn Malik catches up, but his horse sinks into the ground. He asks for forgiveness and a written promise of safety, and turns back |
| Arrival | After several more days of travel they reach Quba on the edge of Yathrib |

### The role of Hazrat Ali (RA)

- He **slept in the Prophet's bed**, risking his life and fooling the watchers.
- He **returned the trusts**. The Makkans had left valuables with the Prophet (pbuh) for safekeeping, as he was al-Amin. Ali (RA) stayed to give each one back, then followed on foot.
- **Lesson:** courage, honesty and keeping trust even with enemies.

### The role of Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA)

- He **prepared two camels** some months before and paid for the guide.
- He **travelled with the Prophet (pbuh)** and kept watch in the cave.
- His **family helped**: Abdullah, Asma (RA) and Amir ibn Fuhayra.
- The Qur'an refers to him as "the second of the two" (9:40).
- **Lesson:** loyalty, planning and sacrifice.

## Stage 5: the first mosque at Quba

The Prophet (pbuh) stayed at Quba for several days. Ali (RA) joined him there. He laid the foundation of the **Mosque of Quba**, the first mosque in Islam, and worked on it with his companions. The prescribed passage, Qur'an 9:108, mentions "a mosque founded on righteousness from the first day" and men "who love to purify themselves". Many scholars understand this as the mosque of Quba, and others as the Prophet's Mosque in Madinah. In either case, the verse teaches that a mosque's worth lies in the sincerity of those who use it.

## Stage 6: entering Madinah

On a Friday the Prophet (pbuh) entered Yathrib, which came to be called **Madinat an-Nabi**, the City of the Prophet, or Madinah. Families called him to stay with them, but he said his camel, which Allah guided, should choose. It knelt on land that belonged to two orphans. He paid for it and built the **Prophet's Mosque** there. While it was built he stayed with **Hazrat Abu Ayyub al-Ansari (RA)**.

The mosque was made of mud brick and palm trunks. It was used for prayer, learning, councils, hearing disputes and welcoming delegations. It also had a shaded area, the suffah, where poor Muslims lived.

## Stage 7: building the community

**Brotherhood.** The Prophet (pbuh) paired the Muhajirun (migrants from Makkah) with the Ansar (the helpers of Madinah). They shared homes, food and work. The Ansar offered half of what they owned.

**The Charter of Madinah.** The Prophet (pbuh) agreed a written document with the main groups of the city. Its main terms were:

1. The believers of Quraysh and Yathrib are **one community (Ummah)**.
2. The Jewish tribes who joined are part of the community and keep their **own religion**.
3. All parties **defend Madinah** together against attack.
4. No party may **help the Quraysh**.
5. The **oppressed must be helped**, and wrongdoers must not be protected.
6. Disputes are referred to **Allah and the Prophet (pbuh)**.
7. The city is a **sacred area**.

**Why it matters:** it ended the Aws and Khazraj feud, gave security to different faiths and set up law above tribal loyalty. It showed the Prophet (pbuh) as a statesman, not only a preacher.

**Other steps in the Madni period.** The call to prayer (*adhan*) was introduced. The direction of prayer was changed from Jerusalem to the Ka'aba. The Muslims began to be allowed to defend themselves (22:39). Years later, the Caliph Umar (RA) fixed the Hijrah as the start of the Islamic calendar.

## Worked example: a 12-mark plan

**Question:** 'The Charter of Madinah was the most important achievement of the Prophet (pbuh) in his first year in Madinah.' Examine why this statement is significant for Muslims. Use Qur'an 9:108.

**Plan.**

1. **For:** the Charter united different groups, ended a feud and protected religious freedom, so it set the pattern for a lawful state.
2. **Against:** the mosque was the base for all other work; the brotherhood solved the migrants' poverty; without the mosque and brotherhood, the Charter would not have worked.
3. **Passage:** "founded on righteousness from the first day" shows that the first project was moral and spiritual; "men who love to purify themselves" shows the character of the community.
4. **Judgement:** the Charter gave the community its political shape, but it grew out of the spiritual base set by the mosque.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing up the two mosques.** Quba was first; the Prophet's Mosque came later in Madinah.
- **Placing the Hijrah before the second pledge of Aqaba.** The pledge made it possible.
- **Giving Ali (RA) one role only.** He slept in the bed and returned the trusts.
- **Saying that the Charter was only for Muslims.** Jewish tribes and others were included.
- **Using the cave of Hira for the Hijrah.** The cave was Thawr.

## Exam technique

**Give** questions need a name (Mus'ab, Thawr, Quba). **Explain** questions need an event and its effect. For the 12-mark question, use two or three phrases from 9:108, and give a balanced judgement.
