## 2.5.1 Market equilibrium

**Market equilibrium** is the situation in which, at the current price, the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied, so there is no tendency for the price to change.

- **Equilibrium price:** the price at which the plans of buyers and sellers match. It is sometimes called the market-clearing price because everything offered for sale is bought.
- **Equilibrium sales (quantity):** the amount bought and sold at that price.

Equilibrium is a balance of forces, not a fixed number. It lasts only while the conditions of demand and supply stay the same.

### Finding equilibrium from a schedule

Daily market for fresh fish in the port of Lorin (invented); price per kg, quantities in kg.

| Price | Quantity demanded | Quantity supplied | Demand minus supply |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 900 | 300 | +600 (shortage) |
| 3 | 800 | 450 | +350 (shortage) |
| 4 | 700 | 600 | +100 (shortage) |
| 5 | 600 | 750 | −150 (surplus) |
| 6 | 500 | 900 | −400 (surplus) |

No row has equal quantities. The sign of the gap changes between 4 and 5, so the equilibrium price lies between them.

**Worked example: locating equilibrium between rows.**

1. Each rise of 1 in price lowers quantity demanded by 100 and raises quantity supplied by 150, so the gap closes by 100 + 150 = 250 for every 1 added to the price.
2. At a price of 4 the shortage is 100. The price must rise by 100 ÷ 250 = 0.4.
3. Equilibrium price = 4 + 0.4 = **4.40**.
4. Equilibrium sales: demand = 700 − (0.4 × 100) = 660; supply = 600 + (0.4 × 150) = 660. Sales = **660 kg**.
5. Total spending on fish = 4.40 × 660 = **2,904**, which is also the sellers' total revenue.

This method works when the schedule changes by equal steps, as here. In an exam a diagram drawn to scale gives the same answer: the curves cross at a price of 4.40 and a quantity of 660.

### Finding equilibrium on a diagram

1. Choose a scale that fits the largest price and quantity in the table.
2. Plot quantity demanded against price and join the points: the demand curve D slopes down.
3. Plot quantity supplied against price and join the points: the supply curve S slopes up.
4. Mark the crossing point E. Draw dotted lines from E to each axis and label them Pe (price) and Qe (quantity).

```
Price
 6 | \                     /S
 5 |    \              /
4.4|- - - - - - E
 4 |         /    \
 3 |      /          \
 2 |  /                 \D
   +----------|------------ Quantity
             660
```

The sketch is not to scale. In an answer, label both axes, both curves, E, Pe and Qe.

## 2.5.2 Market disequilibrium

**Market disequilibrium** occurs when, at the current price, quantity demanded and quantity supplied are not equal.

| Situation | Price compared with equilibrium | What the gap is called |
|---|---|---|
| Quantity demanded greater than quantity supplied | Below | Shortage (excess demand) |
| Quantity supplied greater than quantity demanded | Above | Surplus (excess supply) |

### Measuring the gap and what is traded

At a disequilibrium price, the quantity actually bought and sold is the **smaller** of the two quantities, because no one can be made to buy or sell more than they want.

**Worked example: Lorin fish market.**

| Price | Gap | Quantity traded | Total spending |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Shortage of 800 − 450 = 350 | 450 (limited by sellers) | 3 × 450 = 1,350 |
| 4.40 | None (equilibrium) | 660 | 2,904 |
| 6 | Surplus of 900 − 500 = 400 | 500 (limited by buyers) | 6 × 500 = 3,000 |

Notice that more fish is traded at equilibrium than at either disequilibrium price. Away from equilibrium, some buyers who would pay the price, or some sellers who would accept it, are left out.

### Reading disequilibrium from a diagram

Draw a horizontal line at the price you are given.

- If the line meets the supply curve before (to the left of) the demand curve, quantity supplied is smaller: a **shortage**. Its size is the horizontal distance between the curves.
- If the line meets the demand curve first, quantity demanded is smaller: a **surplus**.

### How the market adjusts

**From a shortage (price 3):**

1. 350 kg of fish that buyers want is not available; queues form and stalls sell out early.
2. Sellers realise they can raise the price without losing sales, and some buyers offer more.
3. As the price rises, quantity demanded contracts and quantity supplied extends.
4. The shortage shrinks until the price reaches 4.40.

**From a surplus (price 6):**

1. 400 kg of fish is unsold. Fish is perishable, so sellers risk throwing it away.
2. Sellers cut the price to clear their stock.
3. As the price falls, quantity demanded extends and quantity supplied contracts.
4. The surplus shrinks until the price reaches 4.40.

During adjustment neither curve shifts. The market moves along both curves.

### When disequilibrium lasts

Adjustment is not always quick. A disequilibrium price can persist when:

- a seller fixes its price in advance, such as a concert promoter who prints tickets at a set price and then sells out within minutes;
- prices are written into contracts or printed price lists that are costly to change;
- the government sets a maximum or minimum price (studied in section 2.11).

A lasting shortage brings queues, waiting lists and resale at higher prices. A lasting surplus brings unsold stock, storage costs and waste.

## Common mistakes

- Calling a price below equilibrium a surplus. Low price, too few sellers: shortage.
- Giving the larger quantity as the amount traded at a disequilibrium price.
- Drawing a shift of the curves to show adjustment to equilibrium. Adjustment is a movement along them.
- Reading equilibrium from the row with the smallest gap rather than finding where the gap changes sign.
- Omitting the time period and units ("660 kg a day").

## Exam technique for the 0455 command words

- **Define:** equilibrium, "quantity demanded equals quantity supplied at the current price, so there is no pressure for the price to change".
- **Identify:** read equilibrium price and quantity from a table or diagram, giving units.
- **Calculate:** the size of a shortage or surplus, the quantity traded and total spending at any price.
- **Explain:** describe the adjustment as a chain, using extend and contract.
- **Draw:** both curves from a schedule, labelled, with Pe, Qe and any disequilibrium gap shown by a horizontal line.
