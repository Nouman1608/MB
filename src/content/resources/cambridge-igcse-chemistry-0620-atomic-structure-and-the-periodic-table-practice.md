## Section A

**1.** Which particle has a relative mass of 1 and no charge?

A: Electron

B: Neutron

C: Proton

D: Nucleus

**[1]**

**2.** State where in an atom protons, neutrons and electrons are found. **[2]**

**3.** Define *proton number*. **[1]**

**4.** State the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in each of the following:

**(a)** ³¹₁₅P **[3]**

**(b)** ⁷₃Li⁺ **[3]**

**(c)** ³²₁₆S²⁻ **[3]**

**5.** Write the electronic configuration of **(a)** a calcium atom (proton number 20), **(b)** an aluminium ion, Al³⁺ (proton number 13), **(c)** a nitride ion, N³⁻ (proton number 7). **[3]**

**6.** Explain why an atom has no overall charge. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The electronic configuration of an atom of element X is 2,8,6.

**(a)** State the group and the period of X. **[2]**

**(b)** State the proton number of X and name the element. **[2]**

**(c)** X forms an ion with a 2− charge. Write the electronic configuration of this ion. **[1]**

**(d)** An atom of X has 16 neutrons. Calculate its mass number. **[1]**

**8.** The aluminium ion, Al³⁺, and the oxide ion, O²⁻, both have the electronic configuration 2,8. Explain why they are different ions even though their configurations are the same. **[3]**

**9.** An element is in Group II and Period 4 of the Periodic Table. Write its electronic configuration and name the element. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why almost all of the mass of an atom is in its nucleus. **[2]**

**11.** A particle contains 19 protons, 20 neutrons and 18 electrons.

**(a)** Explain whether the particle is an atom or an ion, and give its charge. **[2]**

**(b)** State its mass number. **[1]**

**(c)** Write its electronic configuration. **[1]**

**12.** A student says: "Helium has two outer-shell electrons, so it should be in Group II." Explain why helium is placed in Group VIII. **[2]**

**13.** An element has the electronic configuration 2,8,1.

**(a)** Name the element. **[1]**

**(b)** The atom loses one electron to form an ion. Write the symbol and the electronic configuration of the ion. **[2]**

**(c)** Name the noble gas with the same electronic configuration as this ion. **[1]**

**14.** Oxygen (proton number 8) and sulfur (proton number 16) are both in Group VI.

**(a)** Write the electronic configuration of each atom. **[2]**

**(b)** Use your answers to explain why they are in the same group but different periods. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1].

**2.** Protons and neutrons are in the nucleus at the centre of the atom [1]; electrons are in shells around the nucleus [1].

**3.** The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom [1].

**4. (a)** Protons 15 [1]; neutrons 31 − 15 = 16 [1]; electrons 15 [1].

**(b)** Protons 3 [1]; neutrons 7 − 3 = 4 [1]; electrons 3 − 1 = 2 [1].

**(c)** Protons 16 [1]; neutrons 32 − 16 = 16 [1]; electrons 16 + 2 = 18 [1]. **[9]**

**5. (a)** 2,8,8,2 [1].

**(b)** 2,8 [1] (13 − 3 = 10 electrons).

**(c)** 2,8 [1] (7 + 3 = 10 electrons). **[3]**

**6.** The number of protons equals the number of electrons [1]. Each proton has a charge of +1 and each electron −1, so the charges cancel; neutrons have no charge [1].

**7. (a)** Group VI [1]; Period 3 [1].

**(b)** 2 + 8 + 6 = 16, so proton number 16 [1]: sulfur [1].

**(c)** 2,8,8 [1].

**(d)** 16 + 16 = **32** [1]. **[6]**

**8.** They contain different numbers of protons: Al³⁺ has 13 and O²⁻ has 8 [1], so they come from different elements [1]. Al³⁺ has lost three electrons and has a positive charge, while O²⁻ has gained two electrons and has a negative charge [1].

**9.** Four occupied shells with two outer electrons: 2,8,8,2 [1]; calcium [1].

**10.** The nucleus contains the protons and neutrons, each with a relative mass of 1 [1]. Electrons, outside the nucleus, have a relative mass of only about 1/1840, which is negligible [1].

**11. (a)** It is an ion, because it has one more proton than electrons [1]; its charge is 1+ (it is K⁺) [1].

**(b)** 19 + 20 = **39** [1].

**(c)** 2,8,8 [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Helium's only shell can hold just two electrons, so with two electrons its outer shell is full [1]. Elements with a full outer shell are the noble gases in Group VIII; the rule linking outer electrons to group number applies only to Groups I to VII [1].

**13. (a)** Sodium (2 + 8 + 1 = 11 electrons, so proton number 11) [1].

**(b)** Na⁺ [1]; 2,8 [1].

**(c)** Neon [1]. **[4]**

**14. (a)** Oxygen 2,6 [1]; sulfur 2,8,6 [1].

**(b)** Both have six outer-shell electrons, so both are in Group VI [1]. Oxygen has two occupied shells and is in Period 2, while sulfur has three occupied shells and is in Period 3 [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* when asked to "use your answers", quote the configurations you wrote. A statement such as "they have the same number of outer electrons" without the numbers 2,6 and 2,8,6 may not gain the explanation mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding the proton number to the mass number to find neutrons.
- Adding electrons for positive ions instead of removing them.
- Writing potassium or calcium with 9 or 10 electrons in the third shell.
- Using the mass number instead of the proton number to identify an element.
- Applying the group rule to helium.
