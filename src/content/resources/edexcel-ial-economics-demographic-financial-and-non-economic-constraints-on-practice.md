## Questions

**1.** Country E has 18 million people aged 0 to 14, 42 million aged 15 to 64 and 6 million aged 65 and over.
**(a)** Calculate the total dependency ratio. Show your working. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one way in which a large youth population may constrain development. **[2]**

**2.** In Country A the birth rate is 26 per thousand, the death rate is 6 per thousand and net migration is plus 1 per thousand. Real GDP grows by 5.5%.
**(a)** Calculate the population growth rate as a percentage. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the growth rate of real GDP per head to two decimal places. **[2]**

**3.** Country G owes 14.4bn dollars. Its GNI is 48bn dollars, export earnings are 12bn dollars and annual debt service is 2.4bn dollars.
**(a)** Calculate the ratio of debt to GNI. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the debt service ratio. **[2]**
**(c)** The debt is in dollars. The local currency falls from 8 to 10 units per dollar. Calculate the percentage rise in the debt measured in local currency. **[2]**

**4.** The average household in Country H has income of 30,000 and debt of 42,000.
**(a)** Calculate the ratio of debt to income as a percentage. **[1]**
**(b)** The interest rate on this debt rises from 6% to 9%. Calculate the extra interest as a percentage of income. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one effect of this rise on economic growth. **[2]**

**5.** A farmer needs a machine costing 900. It will raise revenue by 270 a year. A bank offers a loan at 20% a year. A moneylender charges 8% a month.
**(a)** Calculate the effective annual rate charged by the moneylender, to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the farmer's net annual gain from the machine using the bank loan. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why limited access to banking can constrain development. **[2]**

**6.** Country J has 15,000 skilled engineers. In one year 1,200 emigrate. Training each cost the state 40,000. Remittances are 0.9bn on GDP of 25bn.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage of engineers who emigrate. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the training investment lost, in millions. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate remittances as a percentage of GDP. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the net effect of this migration on development is uncertain. **[3]**

**7.** Explain how poor infrastructure can constrain economic development. **[4]**

**8.** Training costs 2,000 and raises a worker's annual earnings by 320.
**(a)** Calculate the annual rate of return and the payback period. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a low-income household might not buy this training. **[2]**

**9.** A government plans a road programme with a budget of 150m at 3m per kilometre. Corruption causes 24% of the budget to be lost.
**(a)** Calculate the length of road built and the shortfall against plan. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain two ways in which poor governance may reduce investment. **[4]**

**10.** Country K's real GDP index is 100 before a civil war. It falls by 12%, 18% and 10% in three successive years.
**(a)** Calculate the index at the end of the war. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage rise needed to return to 100. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain two reasons why recovery after a civil war may be slow. **[4]**

**11.** Terrorist attacks cut tourist arrivals in Country L from 500,000 to 350,000. Each visitor spends 1,000. The marginal propensity to withdraw is 0.6 and GDP is 20,000m.
**(a)** Calculate the direct loss of tourist spending, in millions. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the total fall in income. **[2]**
**(c)** Express the total fall as a percentage of GDP. **[1]**

**12.** Assess whether non-economic factors are more significant than economic factors in constraining development. **[10]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Dependants = 18 + 6 = 24 million [1]. Ratio = 24 / 42 x 100 = **57.1%** [1]
**(b)** Many children raise spending on schools and health [1], while low saving and a rising number of job seekers leave fewer resources and jobs per head [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the dependants added before dividing, and say which age band you divide by, because a bare answer cannot show the method.

**2. (a)** Natural increase = 26 minus 6 = 20 per thousand = 2.0% [1]. Add 0.1% migration: **2.1%** [1]
**(b)** (1.055 / 1.021) minus 1 [1] = **3.33%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The subtraction 5.5 minus 2.1 gives 3.4, which is only an approximation. The ratio method is the safer route when the question asks for two decimal places.

**3. (a)** 14.4 / 48 x 100 [1] = **30%** [1]
**(b)** 2.4 / 12 x 100 [1] = **20%** [1]
**(c)** Debt rises from 14.4 x 8 = 115.2 to 14.4 x 10 = 144 [1]. Rise = 28.8 / 115.2 = **25%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Debt to GNI and debt service ratio use different denominators. Writing the denominator in your working shows which measure you used.

**4. (a)** 42,000 / 30,000 x 100 = **140%** [1]
**(b)** Interest rises from 2,520 to 3,780, an extra 1,260 [1]. As a share of income: 1,260 / 30,000 = **4.2%** [1]
**(c)** Households have less to spend on goods [1], so firms' sales and investment fall and growth slows [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A development of the effect (spending falls, then sales, then growth) gains credit that a bare assertion of lower growth does not.

**5. (a)** 1.08 to the power 12 is 2.518 [1], so the effective rate is **151.8%** [1]
**(b)** Interest = 900 x 0.20 = 180 [1]. Net gain = 270 minus 180 = **90** [1]
**(c)** Without affordable loans, profitable projects such as this one cannot be funded [1]. Firms stay small, productivity and incomes stay low, and saving is low too [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Compound the monthly rate. Multiplying 8% by 12 gives 96%, which understates the cost.

**6. (a)** 1,200 / 15,000 = **8%** [1]
**(b)** 1,200 x 40,000 = **48m** [1]
**(c)** 0.9 / 25 x 100 = **3.6%** [1]
**(d)** Loss: the training investment and the skills are gone [1]. Gain: remittances raise incomes and returning migrants bring skills [1]. Which effect wins turns on how big the lost training investment is next to remittances, and on what remittances are spent on [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A reasoned conclusion on what the net effect depends on is more useful than a list of benefits and costs.

**7.** Poor roads, ports or power raise firms' costs [1]. Higher costs reduce competitiveness in export markets [1]. Unreliable power idles capital and cuts output [1]. Foreign investors prefer locations with better networks, so FDI and technology transfer are lower, slowing growth [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each point should end in a consequence for output or living standards. Listing types of infrastructure without a link stays at description.

**8. (a)** Return = 320 / 2,000 x 100 = **16%** [1]. Payback = 2,000 / 320 = **6.25 years** [1]
**(b)** The household cannot afford the upfront cost [1], and weak credit markets stop it borrowing against future earnings [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* State the units (a percentage and years) because an unlabelled number does not show which measure you calculated.

**9. (a)** Budget delivered = 150 x 0.76 = 114m [1]. Road built = 114 / 3 = **38 km** against 50 km planned [1]. Shortfall = **12 km** [1]
**(b)** Insecure property rights or weak courts mean investors cannot enforce contracts [1], which lowers the expected return [1]. Unstable or arbitrary policy raises risk [1], so investors demand higher returns or go elsewhere and investment falls [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the mechanism (return or risk) that links governance to investment.

**10. (a)** 100 x 0.88 x 0.82 x 0.90 [1] = **64.9** [1]
**(b)** 100 / 64.944 minus 1 [1] = **54.0%** [1]
**(c)** Capital such as roads and factories must be rebuilt [1], which takes years and needs investment that is hard to attract after a war [1]. Skilled workers have died or emigrated [1], and public finances and institutions are weak [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide the required gain by the lower figure, not the original one; that is why a percentage fall and the percentage rise needed to recover are not equal.

**11. (a)** Loss of 150,000 visitors x 1,000 [1] = **150m** [1]
**(b)** Multiplier = 1 / 0.6 = 1.67 [1]. Total fall = 150 x 1.67 = **250m** [1]
**(c)** 250 / 20,000 x 100 = **1.25%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the multiplier as a separate step. An unexplained final figure gives no route to credit if the arithmetic slips.

**12.** Economic constraints include debt, credit, infrastructure and skills [1]. Non-economic ones include corruption, poor governance, civil war and terrorism [1]. Non-economic factors can be more damaging when severe: a civil war destroys capital and people, as in question 10 where output stays far below its pre-war level [1]. Corruption acts as a tax and diverts public funds, as in question 9, so fewer roads are built [1]. They also undermine the conditions in which economic factors can be tackled, because investors, lenders and donors avoid unstable or corrupt states [1]. Economic constraints can be more persistent: poor infrastructure and low skills hold back productivity for decades even in peaceful, well-governed countries [1]. Debt service can absorb 20% of export earnings, as in question 3, whatever the quality of governance [1]. The constraints interact, so ranking them is hard: poverty makes governance weaker and weak governance deters the investment that would reduce poverty [1]. Context decides: conflict-affected countries are constrained first by non-economic factors, while stable low-income countries are constrained mainly by economic ones [1]. A judgement that picks a view for a type of country and explains why is needed to complete the argument [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Reach a supported judgement about which matters more and under what conditions. A list of both groups of factors with no weighing is a description, not an assessment.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a bare number with no working or no unit.
- Using 5.5 minus 2.1 for GDP per head when two decimal places are requested.
- Multiplying a monthly interest rate by 12 instead of compounding.
- Dividing a fall by the new figure instead of the starting figure, or the reverse for recovery.
- Mixing debt to GNI with debt service ratio.
- Naming a constraint without a chain to lower output, investment or living standards.
- Giving only the costs of migration and ignoring remittances and skills.
- Writing an assessment that lists factors but never judges which matters more.
