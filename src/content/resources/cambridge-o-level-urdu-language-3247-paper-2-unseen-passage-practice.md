## Section A

**1.** Name the technique in *شٹر ... جیسے وہ بھی جھک کر الوداع کہہ رہا ہو* and explain its effect.
**[2]**

**2.** What does the comparison *جیسے کسی بچے کو سلا رہے ہوں* suggest about Rahim Chacha's feelings
for his books? **[2]**

**3.** Rahim Chacha smiles as he speaks. Why is the smile significant, given what the narrator says
next? **[2]**

**4.** What contrast does the writer set up between the bookshop and the shop that will replace it,
and what does it imply? **[2]**

**5.** Explain the metaphor *بازار کا ایک دل دھڑکنا بند کر رہا ہے*. **[2]**

**6.** What is the narrator's attitude to the passers-by in the last sentence? Support your answer
with a quotation. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Personification combined with a simile (*تشبیہ*): the half-closed shutter is compared to a
person bowing in farewell [1]. The effect is that even the shop itself seems to take part in the
goodbye, so the scene opens on a note of sad ceremony [1].

**2.** He handles the books with tenderness and care, as a parent handles a child [1]; it suggests
they are like family to him and that packing them away is painful, done slowly as if to delay the
end [1].

**3.** The smile seems to accept the change calmly [1], but the narrator notices the missing
"کھنک" in his voice, so the smile hides sorrow; the contrast between face and voice reveals the
feeling he will not admit [1].

**4.** The old, modest bookshop "in the corner" for forty years is set against a "چمکتی ہوئی" mobile
phone shop [1]. The contrast implies that something lasting and thoughtful is being replaced by
something new and showy, and the writer's sympathy lies with what is being lost [1].

**5.** The shop is called the market's "heart", so it was the living centre of the place [1]; its
closing is a kind of death, which makes the loss feel serious and final rather than an ordinary
change of business [1].

**6.** Regret and quiet reproach at their indifference [1], shown in *کسی نے مڑ کر بھی نہ دیکھا*:
the word *بھی* stresses that no one gave even a glance [1].

**7.** Why it earns few marks [2]: it only retells what happens (surface meaning), with no comment on
language or effect and no link to the question.

Rewrite, for example [2]:

> دیوان کا گرنا ایک معمولی واقعہ ہے، مگر رحیم چچا کا اسے "جھاڑ" کر "دیر تک سینے سے لگائے" رکھنا
> بتاتا ہے کہ کتاب ان کے لیے محض سامان نہیں بلکہ کوئی پیارا ہے جس سے جدا ہونا مشکل ہے۔ یہ خاموش
> حرکت ان کے مسکراتے لفظوں سے زیادہ سچ بولتی ہے۔

Credit a quotation used inside the sentence [1] and an effect linked to the theme of loss [1].

**8.** For example [2]: *مصنف نے دکان کے بند ہونے کو ایک عزیز کی رخصتی کی طرح پیش کیا ہے۔ تشبیہات،
رحیم چچا کی چھوٹی چھوٹی حرکات اور باہر کی بے حس دنیا کے تضاد سے یہ دکھ بغیر کسی شور کے قاری کے دل
تک پہنچتا ہے۔* One mark for a direct answer, one for previewing the methods.

**9.** Marked on the six-level scheme, best fit [20]. Indicative content (not a checklist):

- **Description of the shop and its contents**: the bowing shutter and the books laid to rest like a
  sleeping child turn objects into mourners and loved ones; "forty years" and "the corner" give the
  shop a settled, almost family place in the market; the bright new shop is described only by its
  glitter.
- **Rahim Chacha's words and actions**: the light question *اب کتاب کون خریدتا ہے؟* sounds resigned
  but the missing ring in his voice betrays grief; slow, careful packing; holding the *دیوان* to his
  chest says what he does not say.
- **The outside world**: traffic noise "continues", showing that life moves on without pause; the
  heart metaphor makes the closure a death; nobody "even" looks back, so the narrator's sadness is
  also a criticism of indifference.

Level 6 (18–20): a perceptive, sustained personal response; deeper implications such as the loss of
a reading culture or the cost of change; sensitive, detailed comment on effects; many short,
integrated quotations. Level 4 (11–14): understanding with some comment on language and some
supporting detail. Level 2 (4–6): mainly retelling, little reference to language.

**10.** The tone is satirical/mocking (*طنزیہ*) [1]. Any two details explained [2]: the ribbon cut
"so many times" that its pieces ran out exaggerates the ceremony, mocking the officials' love of show;
the final contrast, no swing yet but a marble plaque, exposes that display mattered more than the
children's park itself; the precise numbers (three ministers, four speeches) make the fuss sound
absurd.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Questions 1 and 5**: naming the device and stopping there.
- **Question 3**: missing that the smile and the voice contradict each other.
- **Question 9**: one long paragraph that covers only the first prompt in detail.
- **Question 10**: calling the tone "funny" without saying who or what is being mocked.
