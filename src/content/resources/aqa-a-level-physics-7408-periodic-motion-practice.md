These questions cover sub-section 3.6.1 Periodic motion of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408, AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards): circular motion, simple harmonic motion, simple harmonic systems and forced vibrations. It is A-level only and assessed in Paper 1.

Work through the [periodic motion study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-periodic-motion/) and [periodic motion revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-periodic-motion-revision-notes/) first. Questions on the whole of section 3.6 are in the [Further mechanics and thermal physics practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-further-mechanics-and-thermal-physics-practice/).

Data: g = 9.81 m s⁻². Use radians throughout.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** A turntable rotates at 45 revolutions per minute.

**(a)** Calculate its angular speed. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed of a point 0.12 m from the axis. **[1]**

**2.** State the two conditions that define simple harmonic motion. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why the resultant force on an object moving in a circle at constant speed does no work on it. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why a simple pendulum moves with simple harmonic motion only when its amplitude is small. **[2]**

**5.** An undamped oscillator has a frequency of 3.0 Hz. State the frequency at which its kinetic energy varies, and explain your answer. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** A 0.30 kg puck on a frictionless horizontal table is tied to a fixed pivot by a string. It moves in a circle of radius 0.50 m at 2.0 revolutions per second.

**(a)** Calculate the angular speed. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the tension in the string. **[2]**
**(c)** The string breaks when the tension reaches 40 N. Calculate the highest rotation frequency possible. **[2]**
**(d)** Describe the motion of the puck immediately after the string breaks. **[1]**

**7.** A fairground ride carries a 60 kg rider in a horizontal circle of radius 4.0 m with a period of 3.0 s.

**(a)** Calculate the rider's speed. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the rider's centripetal acceleration. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the resultant horizontal force on the rider and express it as a multiple of the rider's weight. **[2]**

**8.** A car of mass 850 kg drives over the top of a hump whose road surface is an arc of radius 25 m.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum speed at which the car stays in contact with the road at the top. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the normal contact force on the car at the top when it travels at 10 m s⁻¹. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the passengers feel lighter at the top of the hump. **[1]**

**9.** An object performs SHM with amplitude 0.030 m and frequency 4.0 Hz. Timing starts when it is at x = +0.030 m.

**(a)** Calculate its maximum speed. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate its maximum acceleration. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate its displacement at t = 0.020 s. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate its speed when x = 0.015 m. **[2]**

**10.** A 0.25 kg mass hung from a spring extends it by 0.049 m.

**(a)** Show that the spring constant is about 50 N m⁻¹. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the period of vertical oscillations. **[1]**
**(c)** The mass is pulled down a further 0.040 m and released. Calculate the total energy of the oscillation. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the maximum speed of the mass. **[2]**

**11.** On a distant planet, 10 oscillations of a pendulum of length 0.90 m take 24.0 s.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational field strength at the planet's surface. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the time for 10 oscillations is measured instead of the time for one. **[1]**
**(c)** On Earth, a student plots T² against l for a pendulum and finds a gradient of 4.02 s² m⁻¹. Calculate the value of g this gives. **[2]**

**12.** A car's suspension behaves as a mass-spring system with shock absorbers.

**(a)** The car drives along a road with regularly spaced bumps. Explain why the car bounces violently at one particular speed. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the shock absorbers are designed to give critical damping. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how the graph of amplitude against driving frequency changes when the damping is increased. **[2]**

**13.** A 0.50 kg mass on a horizontal spring of stiffness 200 N m⁻¹ oscillates with amplitude 0.060 m. Damping is negligible.

**(a)** Calculate the total energy of the oscillator. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the kinetic energy when the displacement is 0.030 m. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the displacement at which the kinetic and potential energies are equal. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** f = 45/60 = 0.75 Hz [1]; ω = 2πf = **4.7 rad s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** v = ωr = 4.71 × 0.12 = **0.57 m s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Convert rpm to Hz before multiplying by 2π; using 45 directly gives an answer 60 times too large.

**2.** Acceleration is proportional to displacement from the equilibrium position [1]; acceleration is directed towards the equilibrium position (opposite to displacement) [1].

**3.** The resultant force acts towards the centre, perpendicular to the velocity [1]; there is no displacement in the direction of the force, so no work is done and the kinetic energy (speed) stays constant [1].

**4.** The restoring force is mg sin θ, which is not proportional to the displacement [1]; only for small angles does sin θ ≈ θ (in radians), making the acceleration proportional to displacement [1].

**5.** **6.0 Hz** [1]; kinetic energy reaches a maximum twice each cycle, once in each direction through equilibrium [1].

**6. (a)** ω = 2π × 2.0 = **12.6 rad s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** T = mω²r = 0.30 × 12.6² × 0.50 [1] = **24 N** [1]
**(c)** ω_max = √(40/(0.30 × 0.50)) = 16.3 rad s⁻¹ [1]; f = ω/2π = **2.6 Hz** [1]
**(d)** It moves in a straight line at a tangent to the circle, at constant speed [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (d) "flies outwards" is wrong; with no resultant force the puck keeps the velocity it had at that instant.

**7. (a)** v = 2πr/T = 2π × 4.0/3.0 = **8.4 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** a = v²/r = 8.38²/4.0 = **17.5 m s⁻²** [1]
**(c)** F = ma = 60 × 17.5 = **1.05 × 10³ N** [1]; F/mg = 17.5/9.81 = **1.8 times the weight** [1]

**8. (a)** Contact is lost when mg = mv²/r [1]; v = √(9.81 × 25) = **16 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** N = m(g − v²/r) = 850 × (9.81 − 100/25) [1] = **4.9 × 10³ N** [1]
**(c)** The normal contact force on them is less than their weight, because part of the weight provides the centripetal force [1].
*Examiner insight:* Weight does not change; what changes is the contact force, which is what a person feels.

**9. (a)** ω = 2π × 4.0 = 25.1 rad s⁻¹ [1]; v_max = ωA = **0.75 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** a_max = ω²A = 25.1² × 0.030 = **19 m s⁻²** [1]
**(c)** x = 0.030 cos(25.1 × 0.020) = 0.030 cos(0.503 rad) [1] = **0.026 m** [1]
**(d)** v = ω√(A² − x²) = 25.1 × √(0.030² − 0.015²) [1] = **0.65 m s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* With the calculator in degrees, (c) gives 0.030 m, a sign that the angle mode is wrong.

**10. (a)** At equilibrium kx = mg [1]; k = 0.25 × 9.81/0.049 = **50 N m⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** T = 2π√(0.25/50) = **0.44 s** [1]
**(c)** E = ½kA² = ½ × 50 × 0.040² = **0.040 J** [1]
**(d)** ω = √(k/m) = √(50/0.25) = 14.1 rad s⁻¹ [1]; v_max = ωA = **0.57 m s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* For a "show that" give more figures than the target: 50.05 N m⁻¹, not just "50".

**11. (a)** T = 24.0/10 = 2.40 s [1]; g = 4π²l/T² [1] = 4π² × 0.90/2.40² = **6.2 m s⁻²** [1]
**(b)** It reduces the percentage uncertainty in T, because the reaction-time error is shared over many oscillations [1].
**(c)** Gradient = 4π²/g [1]; g = 4π²/4.02 = **9.82 m s⁻²** [1]

**12. (a)** The bumps provide a periodic driving force; at one speed the driving frequency equals the natural frequency of the suspension [1]; resonance occurs, energy transfer is maximum and the amplitude is very large [1].
**(b)** Critical damping returns the car body to equilibrium in the shortest time [1]; without oscillating, so the car does not keep bouncing after a bump [1].
**(c)** The peak amplitude is lower [1]; the peak is broader (less sharp) and occurs at a slightly lower frequency [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (a) you must state the frequency condition; "the car resonates" without saying why scores at most one mark.

**13. (a)** E = ½kA² = ½ × 200 × 0.060² = **0.36 J** [1]
**(b)** E_p = ½kx² = ½ × 200 × 0.030² = 0.090 J [1]; E_k = 0.36 − 0.090 = **0.27 J** [1]
**(c)** ½kx² = ½ × ½kA², so x = A/√2 [1] = **0.042 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* At half the amplitude the kinetic energy is three quarters of the total, not half; energy depends on x².

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving rotation rates in rpm.
- Treating centripetal force as an extra force rather than the resultant.
- Evaluating cos ωt with the calculator in degrees.
- Using total extension instead of displacement from equilibrium.
- Assuming energy is shared equally at half amplitude.
- Describing resonance without the frequency condition.
