**1.** Which feature shows discontinuous variation in humans?

A: body mass

B: ABO blood group

C: height

A: bar chart

B: histogram

C: line graph

D: scatter graph

**[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Explain the difference between continuous and discontinuous variation. **[4]**

**7.** A student measured the length of 60 leaves from a hedge. The results are in the table.

| Length class (mm) | Number of leaves |
|---|---|
| 30 to 39 | 4 |
| 40 to 49 | 11 |
| 50 to 59 | 25 |
| 60 to 69 | 15 |
| 70 to 79 | 5 |

**(a)** Check that the table includes all 60 leaves by calculating the total. **[1]**

**(b)** State the modal class. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage of leaves in the modal class. **[1]**

**(d)** State what type of graph should be drawn, and describe its expected shape. **[3]**

**(e)** Explain why the student measured 60 leaves and not 6. **[2]**

**8.** Two groups of genetically identical sunflower seedlings were grown. Group A was given plenty of water. Group B was given little water. After 30 days the mean height of group A was 48 cm and the mean height of group B was 32 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage by which group A is taller than group B. **[2]**

**(b)** State which cause of variation is shown by this investigation, and explain. **[2]**

**(c)** State whether the sunflower height is an example of continuous or discontinuous variation and explain why. **[2]**

**9.** In a class of 30 students, 12 had blood group O, 10 had group A, 6 had group B and 2 had group AB.

**(a)** Check that the total is 30. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of students with blood group O. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why this is an example of discontinuous variation. **[2]**

**(d)** State what causes the variation in blood groups. **[1]**

**10.** Explain why height in humans shows continuous variation but seed shape in peas shows discontinuous variation. **[4]**

**11.** Suggest why variation in a population is important for the survival of a species. **[2]**

**12.** A farmer measured the mass of 40 eggs from one flock of hens and drew a histogram. The histogram showed a bell-shaped curve with a range from 52 g to 68 g.

**(a)** State the type of variation shown. **[1]**

**(b)** Give **one** cause of this type of variation in the flock. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the graph is a histogram and not a bar chart with gaps. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Differences between individuals of the same species [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any one of: height; body mass; foot length [1]. **[1]**

**5.** The observable features of an organism [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Continuous variation shows a range of phenotypes between two extremes with intermediates [1], for example height [1]. Discontinuous variation shows a limited number of distinct phenotypes with no intermediates [1], for example blood groups [1]. **[4]**

**7.**

**(a)** 4 + 11 + 25 + 15 + 5 = 60 [1].

**(b)** 50 to 59 mm [1].

**(c)** 25 divided by 60 x 100 = 41.7 per cent (to one decimal place) [1].

**(d)** A histogram (or a frequency graph with touching bars) [1], because the data are continuous [1]. It would rise to a peak in the middle and fall on either side [1].

**(e)** A larger sample gives a more reliable picture of the variation in the population [1] and reduces the effect of unusual leaves [1]. **[8]**

**8.**

**(a)** (48 - 32) divided by 32 x 100 [1] = 50 per cent [1].

**(b)** The environment [1], because the plants are genetically identical and the only difference was the amount of water [1].

**(c)** Continuous variation [1], because height can take any value in a range, with intermediates [1]. **[6]**

**9.**

**(a)** 12 + 10 + 6 + 2 = 30 [1].

**(b)** 12 divided by 30 x 100 = 40 per cent [1].

**(c)** Each student falls into one of a few clear categories [1], with no intermediates [1].

**(d)** Genes (only) [1]. **[5]**

**10.** Height can take any value between two extremes, so it shows continuous variation [1]. It is caused by genes and the environment (for example diet) [1]. Seed shape is either round or wrinkled, with no intermediates, so it shows discontinuous variation [1]. It is caused by genes only, so the environment does not change the category [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Variation means that some individuals may be better adapted if the environment changes [1], so they survive and reproduce and the species continues [1]. **[2]**

**12.**

**(a)** Continuous variation [1].

**(b)** Either genes or the environment (for example diet of the hens) [1].

**(c)** The mass is a continuous measurement that can take any value in a range [1], so the classes run on from each other and the bars touch [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that variation is between species.
- Using a histogram for categories or a bar chart for continuous measurements.
- Forgetting that the environment affects continuous variation.
- Dividing by the wrong value in a percentage calculation.
- Not giving an example when the question asks for one.
