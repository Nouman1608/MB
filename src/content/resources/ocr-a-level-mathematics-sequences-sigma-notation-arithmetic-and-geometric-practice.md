## Questions

**1.** A sequence has uₙ = n² − 8n + 20.
**(a)** Write down u₁ to u₅. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the sequence is neither increasing nor decreasing. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the values of n for which uₙ = 13. **[2]**

**2.** A sequence is defined by xₙ₊₁ = 0.6xₙ + 8 with x₁ = 5. Find x₂ and x₃, then find the limit L that the terms approach, justifying your method. **[3]**

**3.** A sequence has x₁ = 5 and xₙ₊₁ = k − xₙ, where k is a constant. The sum of the first 30 terms is 135. Find k and state the order of the sequence. **[4]**

**4.**
**(a)** Write 5 + 8 + 11 + ... + 32 using sigma notation. **[2]**
**(b)** Evaluate Σ (4r − 7), from r = 5 to r = 30. **[3]**

**5.** The sum of the first n terms of a sequence is Sₙ = 3n² + 2n.
**(a)** Find u₁. **[1]**
**(b)** Show that uₙ = 6n − 1 and that the sequence is arithmetic. **[3]**
**(c)** Find u₁₀₀. **[1]**

**6.** A geometric sequence has second term 54 and fifth term 16.
**(a)** Find the common ratio and the first term. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the sum to infinity, stating why it exists. **[2]**

**7.** A geometric series has first term 10 and common ratio (3 − x)/5.
**(a)** Find the values of x for which the series converges. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the sum to infinity when x = −1. **[2]**

**8.** A saver pays £300 into an account at the start of each year. The account earns 4.5% compound interest per year. Find the total in the account at the end of the 12th year, to the nearest pound. **[4]**

**9.** A machine costs £24 000 and loses 12% of its value each year.
**(a)** Find its value after 3 years. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the first year in which its value is below £5000. **[4]**

**10.** Show that the recurring decimal 0.1353535... can be written as 67/495. **[4]**

**11.** A loan of £20 000 is charged 5% interest per year. At the end of each year, after interest is added, a payment of £x is made. Let Bₙ be the amount owed after n payments.
**(a)** Show that Bₙ = 20 000 × 1.05ⁿ − x(1.05ⁿ − 1)/0.05. **[3]**
**(b)** Find x, to the nearest penny, so that the loan is cleared by the 10th payment. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the total repaid, to the nearest pound. **[1]**

**12.** Two training plans start in week 1. Plan A runs 5 km in week 1 and adds 0.8 km each week. Plan B runs 4 km in week 1 and increases the distance by 12% each week.
**(a)** Find the distance in week 10 for each plan, to 2 decimal places. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that week 12 is the first week in which Plan B's distance is greater than Plan A's. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the total distance for each plan over the first 12 weeks, to 1 decimal place. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 13, 8, 5, 4, 5 [1]
**(b)** The terms fall from u₁ to u₄ and then rise, so uₙ₊₁ − uₙ is not one sign for all n. [1]
**(c)** n² − 8n + 20 = 13 gives n² − 8n + 7 = 0 [1], so (n − 1)(n − 7) = 0 and **n = 1 or n = 7 [1]**
*Examiner insight:* A list of terms shows a change of direction; naming the term where the direction changes makes the explanation complete.

**2.** x₂ = 0.6(5) + 8 = 11 [1] and x₃ = 0.6(11) + 8 = **14.6 [1]**. At the limit xₙ₊₁ and xₙ both equal L, so L = 0.6L + 8, 0.4L = 8 and **L = 20 [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing the limit equation L = f(L) is the step that shows the method; quoting 20 from a long list of terms is weaker.

**3.** The terms are 5, k − 5, 5, k − 5, ... so each pair sums to k [1]. There are 15 pairs in 30 terms [1], so 15k = 135 [1]. Then **k = 9** and the **order is 2 [1]**
*Examiner insight:* The key move is to group the terms; explaining why the sequence repeats avoids relying on a guess.

**4. (a)** The rth term is 3r + 2 [1]. Since 32 = 3(10) + 2, the series is **Σ (3r + 2), r = 1 to 10 [1]**
**(b)** The number of terms is 30 − 5 + 1 = 26 [1], first term 13, last term 113 [1]. S = 26/2 × (13 + 113) = **1638 [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Checking both end terms of the sigma expression is a good habit, and counting terms as last minus first plus one avoids an off-by-one slip.

**5. (a)** u₁ = S₁ = 3 + 2 = **5 [1]**
**(b)** For n ≥ 2, uₙ = Sₙ − Sₙ₋₁ [1] = 3n² + 2n − 3(n − 1)² − 2(n − 1) = 6n − 1 [1]. This also gives 5 when n = 1, and uₙ₊₁ − uₙ = 6 is constant, so the sequence is arithmetic [1]
**(c)** u₁₀₀ = 600 − 1 = **599 [1]**
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" the final form is given, so every algebraic step in the expansion should be written; also check n = 1 separately.

**6. (a)** r³ = 16/54 = 8/27 [1], so r = 2/3 [1]. Then a × (2/3) = 54 gives **a = 81 [1]**
**(b)** |r| = 2/3 < 1, so the series converges [1]. S∞ = 81/(1 − 2/3) = **243 [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Stating |r| < 1 before using the sum to infinity formula shows you have checked that the series converges.

**7. (a)** |3 − x|/5 < 1, so |3 − x| < 5 [1], giving **−2 < x < 8 [1]**
**(b)** r = 4/5 [1] and S∞ = 10/(1 − 4/5) = **50 [1]**
*Examiner insight:* A modulus condition gives a double inequality; a single inequality such as x < 8 alone usually loses the lower bound.

**8.** The first payment earns 12 years of interest, the last earns 1 year [1]. The total is 300(1.045) + 300(1.045)² + ... + 300(1.045)¹², a geometric series with a = 313.5 and r = 1.045 [1]. S₁₂ = 313.5(1.045¹² − 1)/0.045 [1], so the total is **£4848 [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Listing the payments with their powers first shows the structure; jumping to a formula with the wrong first term is the usual slip.

**9. (a)** 24 000 × 0.88³ = **£16 355.33 [1]**
**(b)** 24 000 × 0.88ⁿ < 5000 [1], so 0.88ⁿ < 5/24 and n ln 0.88 < ln(5/24) [1]. Dividing by ln 0.88, which is negative, reverses the inequality: n > 12.27... [1]. The first whole year is **year 13 [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Dividing by a negative logarithm is a common place to lose a mark; checking the values at 12 and 13 years confirms the direction.

**10.** 0.1353535... = 0.1 + 0.035 + 0.00035 + 0.0000035 + ... [1]. The terms after 0.1 form a geometric series with a = 0.035 and r = 0.01 [1]. Its sum is 0.035/0.99 = 7/198 [1]. Over the common denominator 990, 1/10 = 99/990 and 7/198 = 35/990, so the total is 134/990 = **67/495** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" needs every step; combine the fractions over a common denominator and simplify visibly.

**11. (a)** B₁ = 1.05(20 000) − x [1]. B₂ = 1.05B₁ − x = 20 000 × 1.05² − x(1.05 + 1), and so on, so Bₙ = 20 000 × 1.05ⁿ − x(1 + 1.05 + ... + 1.05ⁿ⁻¹) [1]. The bracket is a geometric series with sum (1.05ⁿ − 1)/0.05, giving the result [1]
**(b)** B₁₀ = 0 [1], so x = 20 000 × 1.05¹⁰ × 0.05/(1.05¹⁰ − 1) [1] = **£2590.09 [1]**
**(c)** 10 × 2590.09 = **£25 901 [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show how the previous balance is multiplied and reduced before generalising; stating the geometric sum separately makes the derivation easy to follow.

**12. (a)** Plan A: 5 + 9(0.8) = **12.20 km** [1]. Plan B: 4 × 1.12⁹ = **11.09 km** [1]
**(b)** Plan A is 5 + 0.8(w − 1) and Plan B is 4 × 1.12^(w−1) [1]. Week 11: A = 13.00, B = 12.42 [1]. Week 12: A = 13.80, B = 13.91, so B is first greater [1]
**(c)** Plan A: 12/2 × (2 × 5 + 11 × 0.8) = **112.8 km [1]**. Plan B: 4(1.12¹² − 1)/0.12 [1] = **96.5 km [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Comparing both values at the two weeks either side of the crossing point is what proves "first"; one value alone does not.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting the number of terms in a sigma sum as last minus first instead of last minus first plus one.
- Using the sum to infinity formula without checking |r| < 1.
- Leaving a modulus condition as a single inequality.
- Forgetting to reverse an inequality when dividing by a negative logarithm.
- Using 0.12 instead of 0.88 as the ratio for a 12% fall.
- Rounding an interest ratio early, so the final pound figure is out.
- Giving n as a decimal when the question asks for a whole number of years or weeks.
- Assuming the first term of a saving plan earns interest for the wrong number of periods.
