These notes condense section **3.7 Vectors** of Pure Mathematics 3 (Paper 3) in the Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics 9709 syllabus for 2026 and 2027. For worked examples see the [3.7 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-vectors-cambridge-alevel-maths/); for marked questions use the [3.7 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-vectors-cambridge-alevel-maths-practice/).

## Core results

```text
AB = b − a                         midpoint of AB: ½(a + b)
|xi + yj + zk| = √(x² + y² + z²)  unit vector: v/|v|
Line: r = a + tb                   through A and B: r = a + t(b − a)
a.b = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + a₃b₃ = |a||b| cos θ
a.b = 0  ⇔  a and b are perpendicular (non-zero vectors)
OABC parallelogram  ⇔  OB = OA + OC
```

## Two lines: the decision

| Step | Test | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Are the direction vectors multiples? | Yes: parallel (check a point: same line or distinct) |
| 2 | Solve two component equations for s and t | Gives candidate values |
| 3 | Do s and t satisfy the third component? | Yes: intersect (find the point). No: skew |

## Scalar product jobs

| Task | Method |
|---|---|
| Angle between lines | Directions b₁ and b₂; cos θ = \|b₁.b₂\|/(\|b₁\|\|b₂\|) for the acute angle |
| Angle at a vertex, such as angle ABC | Use BA and BC, both starting at B |
| Perpendicular check | Scalar product = 0 |
| Foot of perpendicular F from P to r = a + tb | F = a + tb; solve (F − P).b = 0 |
| Distance from P to the line | \|PF\| |
| Reflection of P in the line | P′ = 2F − P |
| Area of triangle ABC | ½\|AB\|\|AC\| sin A, with cos A from a scalar product |

## Solids

- **Cuboid**: label all eight vertices with coordinates before starting. Diagonals of a face and of the solid are common.
- **Tetrahedron or pyramid**: the apex often lies above the midpoint or centre of the base; find that point as a position vector first.
- Edges meeting at right angles give zero scalar products: a quick check on your coordinates.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Position vector | Vector from the origin O to a point |
| Displacement vector | Vector from one point to another, AB = b − a |
| Direction vector | b in r = a + tb; any non-zero multiple is equally valid |
| Unit vector | A vector of magnitude 1 |
| Parallel lines | Direction vectors are scalar multiples |
| Skew lines | Not parallel and do not intersect (only possible in 3D) |
| Scalar product | a.b, a number, equal to \|a\|\|b\| cos θ |
| Foot of the perpendicular | The point on a line closest to a given point |

## Exam traps

- **Order of subtraction.** AB = b − a; reversing gives the opposite direction and a wrong vertex.
- **Skew needs two checks.** Not parallel, and no intersection.
- **The third equation.** Always test the component you did not use.
- **Line angle versus vertex angle.** For lines, take the acute angle; for an angle in a triangle, the obtuse value may be correct.
- **Directions, not points.** Using position vectors of points on the lines gives a meaningless angle.
- **Out of syllabus.** Vector product, shortest distance between skew lines and the ratio theorem are not required; do not waste time on them.
- **Exact forms.** Leave cos θ as a surd fraction if "exact" is asked.

## Self-test

1. Find |2i − 3j + 6k|.
2. A(1, 4, −2), B(3, 0, 2). Find AB and the midpoint of AB.
3. Find the unit vector in the direction of 4i − 3k.
4. Write a vector equation of the line through (2, −1, 0) parallel to i + 2j − k.
5. Does (5, 5, −3) lie on r = (2, −1, 0) + t(1, 2, −1)?
6. Find p if (1, p, 3) is perpendicular to (2, −1, 1).
7. Find the angle between (1, 1, 0) and (0, 1, 1).
8. Are the lines with directions (2, −6, 4) and (−1, 3, −2) parallel?
9. OABC is a parallelogram with a = (3, 1, 0) and c = (1, 2, 4). Find b.
10. What two things must be shown to prove two lines skew?
11. F is the foot of the perpendicular from P to a line with direction d. Write the equation that determines F.
12. P(1, 2, 3) has foot of perpendicular F(2, 2, 1) on a line. Find the reflection of P in the line.

**Answers**

1. √(4 + 9 + 36) = **7**.
2. AB = **2i − 4j + 4k**; midpoint **(2, 2, 0)**.
3. |4i − 3k| = 5, so **(1/5)(4i − 3k)**.
4. **r = (2i − j) + t(i + 2j − k)**.
5. t = 3 from x: y = −1 + 6 = 5 ✓, z = −3 ✓. **Yes**.
6. 2 − p + 3 = 0, so **p = 5**.
7. cos θ = 1/(√2 × √2) = ½, so **θ = 60°**.
8. (2, −6, 4) = −2(−1, 3, −2), so **yes**.
9. b = a + c = **(4, 3, 4)**.
10. The lines are **not parallel** and **do not intersect**.
11. **(F − P).d = 0**, with F written in terms of the line's parameter.
12. P′ = 2F − P = **(3, 2, −1)**.
