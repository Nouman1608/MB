These questions cover section **3.1.12.2 Definition and determination of pH** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 and Paper 3. Learn the methods first in the [Definition and determination of pH study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-definition-and-determination-of-ph/) and the [Acids and bases revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-acids-and-bases-revision-notes/). Give pH values to two decimal places. Use Mᵣ(HNO₃) = 63.0.

## Questions

**1.** Calculate the pH of each solution.

**(a)** [H⁺] = 0.0020 mol dm⁻³ **[1]**

**(b)** [H⁺] = 0.35 mol dm⁻³ **[1]**

**(c)** [H⁺] = 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ **[1]**

**2.** Calculate [H⁺] for each solution.

**(a)** pH = 2.70 **[1]**

**(b)** pH = 5.15 **[1]**

**3.** Calculate the pH of 0.0150 mol dm⁻³ sulfuric acid, assuming that both hydrogen atoms dissociate completely. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why chemists use the pH scale rather than the concentration of hydrogen ions. **[2]**

**5.** 10.0 cm³ of hydrochloric acid of concentration 0.50 mol dm⁻³ is diluted with water to a total volume of 250 cm³. Calculate the pH of the diluted acid. **[3]**

**6.** 50.0 cm³ of 0.20 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid is mixed with 150 cm³ of 0.080 mol dm⁻³ nitric acid. Calculate the pH of the mixture. **[3]**

**7.** 30.0 cm³ of 0.150 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid is mixed with 20.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide solution. Calculate the pH of the mixture. **[4]**

**8.** Calculate the mass of nitric acid needed to make 500 cm³ of a solution with pH 2.30. **[3]**

**9.** Solution A has pH 2.20 and solution B has pH 3.80. Calculate how many times greater [H⁺] is in solution A than in solution B. **[2]**

**10.** A student mixes equal volumes of two strong acids with pH 1.00 and pH 3.00 and claims the pH of the mixture is the average, 2.00.

**(a)** Calculate the actual pH of the mixture. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the student's method is wrong. **[1]**

**11.** What volume of water must be added to 50.0 cm³ of hydrochloric acid of pH 1.30 to give a solution of pH 2.30? **[2]**

**12.** 25.0 cm³ of 0.0400 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid is added to 25.0 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide solution. Calculate the pH of the mixture. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** pH = –log(0.0020) = **2.70** [1]

**(b)** pH = –log(0.35) = **0.46** [1]

**(c)** pH = –log(4.0 × 10⁻⁵) = **4.40** [1]. **[3]**

**2. (a)** [H⁺] = 10^(–2.70) = **2.0 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³** [1]

**(b)** [H⁺] = 10^(–5.15) = **7.1 × 10⁻⁶ mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** [H⁺] = 2 × 0.0150 = 0.0300 mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = –log(0.0300) = **1.52** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The concentration of hydrogen ions in solutions covers a very wide range (from about 1 to 10⁻¹⁴ mol dm⁻³) [1]; a logarithmic scale converts this into small, easy-to-compare numbers [1]. **[2]**

**5.** n(HCl) = 0.50 × 0.0100 = 5.0 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; [H⁺] = 5.0 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.250 = 0.020 mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **1.70** [1]. **[3]**

**6.** n(H⁺) = (0.20 × 0.0500) + (0.080 × 0.150) = 0.010 + 0.012 = 0.022 mol [1]; [H⁺] = 0.022 ÷ 0.200 = 0.11 mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **0.96** [1]. **[3]**

**7.** n(HCl) = 0.150 × 0.0300 = 4.50 × 10⁻³ mol and n(NaOH) = 0.100 × 0.0200 = 2.00 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; excess H⁺ = 2.50 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; [H⁺] = 2.50 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0500 = 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **1.30** [1]. **[4]**

**8.** [H⁺] = 10^(–2.30) = 5.01 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; n(HNO₃) = 5.01 × 10⁻³ × 0.500 = 2.51 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; mass = 2.51 × 10⁻³ × 63.0 = **0.158 g** [1]. **[3]**

**9.** The difference in pH is 3.80 – 2.20 = 1.60 [1]; ratio = 10^1.60 = **40** (39.8) times greater [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** [H⁺] values 0.1 and 0.001 mol dm⁻³ [1]; mixing equal volumes gives (0.1 + 0.001) ÷ 2 = 0.0505 mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **1.30** [1]

**(b)** pH is a logarithmic scale, so pH values cannot be averaged; the concentrations must be averaged [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* The acid with the lower pH dominates a mixture, because its [H⁺] is far larger.

**11.** A rise of one pH unit means [H⁺] falls by a factor of 10, so the final volume is 10 × 50.0 = 500 cm³ [1]; volume of water added = 500 – 50.0 = **450 cm³** [1]. **[2]**

**12.** n(HCl) = 0.0400 × 0.0250 = 1.00 × 10⁻³ mol and n(NaOH) = 0.0200 × 0.0250 = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; excess H⁺ = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol in 0.0500 dm³, so [H⁺] = 0.0100 mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **2.00** [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Missing the minus sign in –log[H⁺].
- Not doubling [H⁺] for a diprotic acid when told to assume complete dissociation.
- Dividing by the volume of acid alone instead of the total volume.
- Averaging pH values.
- Giving pH to one decimal place or to too many places.
