## Section A

**1.** Which is a statement of the economic growth objective?

A: A sustained rise in nominal GDP

B: A sustained rise in real GDP

C: A rise in the price level

D: A rise in the number of firms

**[1]**

**2.** A government's inflation target is 2% with a tolerance of one percentage point either side. In which case is the target being met?

A: Inflation of 0.5%

B: Inflation of 2.8%

C: Inflation of 3.4%

D: Inflation of -1.0%

**[1]**

**3.** Define price stability. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why a government would not aim for zero unemployment. **[2]**

**5.** State the three types of policy a government can use to pursue its macroeconomic objectives. **[3]**

**6.** Explain one reason why economic growth is a government objective. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Country L (invented figures):

| Indicator | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real GDP growth | 3.1% | 1.4% | -0.6% |
| Inflation (CPI) | 2.2% | 1.8% | 0.9% |
| Unemployment rate | 4.8% | 5.9% | 7.7% |

**(a)** State the change in the unemployment rate between Year 1 and Year 3, in percentage points. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify which objectives were being missed in Year 3, using the data. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain what happened to real output and to the price level in Year 2 and Year 3. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why expansionary policy would be an appropriate response to the Year 3 position. **[3]**

**8.** Country M's labour force is 18 million and 1.35 million people are unemployed.

**(a)** Calculate the unemployment rate. **[1]**

**(b)** The government aims for a rate of 5%. Calculate how many fewer people would need to be unemployed, with the labour force unchanged. **[2]**

**9.** In Country N, nominal GDP rose by 7% in a year while the price level rose by 3%.

**(a)** Calculate the real growth rate, to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the government measures progress on its growth objective with real rather than nominal GDP. **[2]**

**10.** Explain how expansionary monetary policy could be used to reduce cyclical unemployment. **[3]**

**11.** Explain why supply-side policy, rather than expansionary fiscal policy, would be the more appropriate way to reduce unemployment caused by a mismatch of skills. **[4]**

**12.** Explain two reasons why a government aims to keep unemployment low. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Growth is measured in real terms.

**2.** **B** [1]. The band is 1% to 3%.

**3.** A **low** [1] and **stable (predictable)** rate of inflation, so the general price level rises slowly and steadily [1].

**4.** Some **frictional** unemployment always exists as people move between jobs [1], and some **seasonal** unemployment arises as work varies through the year, so zero is not achievable [1].

**5.** **Fiscal** policy [1], **monetary** policy [1] and **supply-side** policy [1].

**6.** Growth raises **real output and incomes** [1], so average living standards rise and more goods and services are available (or: tax revenue rises without higher tax rates, funding public services) [1].

**7. (a)** 7.7 - 4.8 = **2.9 percentage points** rise [1].

**(b)** **Economic growth**: real GDP fell by 0.6% [1]. **Low unemployment**: the rate rose to 7.7%, its highest in the period [1].

**(c)** In Year 2 real output **still rose**, by 1.4%, but more slowly; in Year 3 it **fell** [1]. The price level **rose in both years**, but more slowly each year (disinflation), since inflation stayed positive [1].

**(d)** Falling output, rising unemployment and falling inflation together suggest **weak aggregate demand** [1]. Expansionary fiscal or monetary policy **shifts AD to the right** [1], so firms raise output and employ more workers, helping the growth and unemployment objectives [1].

**8. (a)** 1.35 / 18 x 100 = **7.5%** [1].

**(b)** 5% of 18 million = 0.9 million [1]. Reduction needed = 1.35 - 0.9 = **0.45 million** [1].

**9. (a)** 1.07 / 1.03 = 1.0388 [1], so real growth = **3.9%** [1]. (Answers of about 4% using 7 - 3 earn the first mark only.)

**(b)** Nominal GDP can rise because of **higher prices** without any more output [1]. Real GDP **removes the effect of inflation**, so it shows whether the quantity of goods and services produced has actually increased [1].

**10.** The central bank **cuts interest rates** (or increases the money supply) [1]. Borrowing becomes cheaper, so households spend more and firms invest more, and **AD shifts right** [1]. Firms raise output and need more workers, so **cyclical unemployment falls** [1].

**11.** Unemployment caused by a mismatch is **structural**: jobs exist, but workers lack the required skills [1]. Expansionary fiscal policy raises **aggregate demand**, which creates more vacancies but does not change workers' skills [1]. Supply-side policy such as **training and education** gives workers the skills employers need [1], so they can fill existing vacancies and unemployment falls [1].

**12.** **Lower waste of resources** [1]: when more people work, output is closer to potential, raising incomes and living standards [1]. **Better public finances** [1]: the government pays less in benefits and collects more tax, freeing money for services such as health and education [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading the fall in inflation in Question 7(c) as a fall in prices.
- Using subtraction (7 - 3) as the exact answer in Question 9(a).
- Recommending expansionary fiscal policy for a skills mismatch in Question 11.
- Defining price stability as zero inflation in Question 3.
