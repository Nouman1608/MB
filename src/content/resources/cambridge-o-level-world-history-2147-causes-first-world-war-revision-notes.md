**These notes condense key question 1.6 of Cambridge O Level History 2147: what caused the First World War.** Learn
the order of the alliances and crises from 1879 to 1914, and practise sorting causes into long-term, short-term and
trigger. Full explanation is in the
[First World War causes study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-causes-first-world-war/); exam
practice is in the [First World War causes practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-causes-first-world-war-practice/).

## Condensed recall by focus point

**Alliances and diplomacy: more or less likely?**
- Two armed camps by 1907: Triple Alliance (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy) and Triple Entente (Britain, France,
  Russia).
- Germany felt encircled; its Schlieffen Plan assumed a two-front war.
- Alliances were defensive, and Britain's ententes were not binding military alliances.
- Crises were settled by conferences in 1906 and 1912–13.
- The 1902 Anglo-Japanese Alliance ended British isolation, let Britain concentrate its fleet at home, and encouraged
  the Russo-Japanese War, which turned Russia towards the Balkans.

**Colonial problems: how much tension?**
- Fashoda (1898) led to Anglo-French agreement, not war.
- Kruger telegram (1896) and the Boer War angered Britain against Germany.
- Moroccan crises (1905–06, 1911) were meant to split the Entente but strengthened it.
- All colonial disputes were settled peacefully; the war began in Europe.

**The Balkans: why so hard to solve?**
- Ottoman decline left a power vacuum.
- Austria-Hungary feared Serbian nationalism among its own Slavs.
- Russia claimed to protect the Slavs and wanted influence near the Straits.
- Bosnian Crisis (1908–09) humiliated Russia and enraged Serbia.
- Balkan Wars (1912–13) made Serbia larger and more confident.

**How did the assassination lead to war?**
- Sarajevo (28 June) → blank cheque (5–6 July) → ultimatum (23 July) → Austria declares war on Serbia (28 July) →
  Russian mobilisation (30 July) → Germany declares war on Russia (1 August) and France (3 August) → invasion of
  Belgium and British declaration (4 August).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Alliance System | The two blocs of Great Powers formed between 1879 and 1907 |
| Entente | A friendly understanding, less binding than an alliance |
| Splendid isolation | Britain's late nineteenth-century policy of avoiding alliances |
| Weltpolitik | William II's "world policy" of colonies, a navy and global influence |
| Dreadnought | A new class of fast battleship with big guns, first launched in 1906 |
| Schlieffen Plan | German plan to defeat France quickly through Belgium before turning on Russia |
| Mobilisation | Calling up and moving armed forces ready for war |
| Pan-Slavism | Movement for unity of Slav peoples, often looking to Russia |
| Black Hand | Serbian nationalist secret society that armed the Sarajevo conspirators |
| Blank cheque | Germany's promise of unconditional support to Austria-Hungary, July 1914 |
| Ultimatum | A final demand whose rejection leads to action, such as war |

## Linking the causes

| Cause | Type | How it connects |
|---|---|---|
| Rivalry between Austria-Hungary and Russia in the Balkans | Long-term | Made a local Balkan crisis a Great Power crisis |
| Alliances and military plans | Long-term | Turned a local war into a continental war |
| Naval race | Long-term | Made Britain see Germany as its main threat |
| Moroccan crises | Short-term | Tightened the Entente and German fears of encirclement |
| Bosnian Crisis and Balkan Wars | Short-term | Left Russia unwilling to back down again |
| Assassination | Trigger | Gave Austria-Hungary a reason to crush Serbia |

## Exam traps

- **"The alliances made war automatic."** Italy stayed out in 1914 and joined the other side in 1915; Britain decided
  for itself.
- **Mixing Fashoda with Morocco.** Fashoda was Anglo-French (1898); the Moroccan crises were German challenges to France.
- **Forgetting the 1902 alliance.** It is in the specified content: explain its effect on Britain's navy and on Russia.
- **Blaming only Germany** without explaining Austria-Hungary's and Russia's decisions in July 1914.
- **Describing the July crisis** without showing how each step caused the next.

## Self-test

1. Which treaty did Germany fail to renew in 1890, and what was the consequence?
2. Why did Britain sign the Anglo-Japanese Alliance in 1902?
3. How did the Russo-Japanese War affect the Balkans?
4. What was the outcome of the Fashoda crisis?
5. What did Germany hope to achieve in the First Moroccan Crisis, and what actually happened?
6. Why did the launch of HMS *Dreadnought* intensify the naval race?
7. Why did the Bosnian Crisis of 1908–09 make war more likely?
8. How did the Balkan Wars change Serbia?
9. What was the blank cheque?
10. Why did Russia's mobilisation lead to a German attack on France?
11. Why did Britain declare war on 4 August 1914?

**Answers:** 1. The Reinsurance Treaty with Russia; Russia allied with France in 1894. 2. To end isolation, protect its
interests against Russia in East Asia, and free warships to return to home waters. 3. Defeat turned Russian ambitions
back towards the Balkans, increasing rivalry with Austria-Hungary. 4. France backed down, and the two powers later
settled their colonial differences in the Entente Cordiale. 5. To split Britain and France; at Algeciras only
Austria-Hungary supported Germany and the Entente grew stronger. 6. It made existing battleships obsolete, so Germany
could compete from a fresh start and Britain had to build more. 7. Russia was humiliated and resolved not to back down
again; Serbia was enraged against Austria-Hungary. 8. It nearly doubled in size and became more confident and more
threatening to Austria-Hungary. 9. Germany's promise of unconditional support to Austria-Hungary in early July 1914.
10. The Schlieffen Plan required Germany to defeat France first before Russia was ready. 11. Germany invaded Belgium,
whose neutrality Britain had guaranteed in 1839; Britain also feared German domination of Europe.
