These questions cover section 9.6, Cumulative frequency diagrams, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. All data is invented. So that answers can be checked without a drawn graph, **assume the curve is a straight line between plotted points** when you estimate a reading. In the exam you read values from your own smooth curve, and a small tolerance is allowed. The method is in the [cumulative frequency diagrams study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-cumulative-frequency-diagrams/), and the [cumulative frequency diagrams revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-cumulative-frequency-diagrams-revision-notes/) summarise the rules.

## Section A

**1.** The times, t minutes, that 60 customers waited are grouped with classes 0 < t ≤ 10, 10 < t ≤ 20, 20 < t ≤ 30, 30 < t ≤ 40 and 40 < t ≤ 50. The frequencies are 5, 13, 22, 16 and 4. Write down the cumulative frequencies. **[2]**

**2.** For the 60 customers in question 1, state the heights on the cumulative frequency axis at which you would read the lower quartile, the median, the upper quartile and the 80th percentile. **[2]**

**3.** Write down all the points you would plot to draw the cumulative frequency diagram for question 1. **[2]**

**4.** Using your answers to question 1, state between which two times the median lies, and estimate it. **[2]**

**5.** For the customers in question 1, estimate how many waited more than 35 minutes, and what percentage of the customers this is. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** The lengths, l cm, of 100 fish are shown.

| Length l (cm) | 10 < l ≤ 15 | 15 < l ≤ 20 | 20 < l ≤ 25 | 25 < l ≤ 30 | 30 < l ≤ 35 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 8 | 22 | 35 | 25 | 10 |

**(a)** Complete a cumulative frequency table.

**(b)** Write down the coordinates of the points to plot, including the starting point.

**(c)** Estimate the median, the lower quartile, the upper quartile and the interquartile range. **[7]**

**7.** Use the fish data in question 6.

**(a)** Estimate the number of fish longer than 28 cm.

**(b)** Fish of 17 cm or less must be returned to the water. Estimate how many fish are returned.

**(c)** The longest 10% of fish are sold to a restaurant. Estimate the shortest length of fish sold to the restaurant. **[5]**

**8.** A cumulative frequency curve for the masses, m kg, of 90 bags passes through (0, 0), (5, 12), (10, 30), (15, 62), (20, 84) and (25, 90). Find the frequency of each class 0 < m ≤ 5, 5 < m ≤ 10, and so on. **[3]**

**9.** Journey times to work, in minutes, are summarised from cumulative frequency diagrams. Town A: median 34, interquartile range 12. Town B: median 31, interquartile range 18. Compare the journey times in the two towns. **[4]**

**10.** In a test taken by 400 students, Ali's mark is at the 75th percentile.

**(a)** Explain what this means.

**(b)** About how many students scored more than Ali? **[2]**

**11.** A student draws a cumulative frequency diagram for the data in question 6. She plots the cumulative frequencies at the class midpoints (12.5, 17.5, ...), joins the crosses with a ruler, and starts the graph at (0, 0). Describe three errors. **[3]**

**12.** The lifetimes, in hours, of 200 batteries give a cumulative frequency diagram through (20, 0), (30, 18), (40, 62), (50, 128), (60, 172), (70, 192) and (80, 200).

**(a)** Estimate the median lifetime.

**(b)** The maker claims that "most batteries last more than 45 hours". Use the diagram to test this claim. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** **5, 18, 40, 56, 60** [2] (one mark for at least three correct).

**2.** LQ **15**, median **30**, UQ **45** [1]; 80th percentile 0.8 × 60 = **48** [1].

**3.** **(0, 0)** [1], **(10, 5), (20, 18), (30, 40), (40, 56), (50, 60)** [1].

*Examiner insight:* The starting point is at the lower boundary of the first class, here 0, with cumulative frequency 0.

**4.** Height 30 lies between CF 18 (at 20 minutes) and CF 40 (at 30 minutes), so the median is **between 20 and 30 minutes** [1]: about **25 minutes** (25.5) [1].

**5.** At 35 minutes, CF ≈ 40 + 16 × 0.5 = 48 [1], so 60 − 48 = **12 customers**, which is **20%** [1].

**6. (a)** **8, 30, 65, 90, 100** [2] (one mark for at least three correct).

**(b)** **(10, 0), (15, 8), (20, 30), (25, 65), (30, 90), (35, 100)** [1].

**(c)** Median at height 50: about **22.9 cm** [1]. LQ at 25: about **18.9 cm** [1]. UQ at 75: **27 cm** [1]. IQR ≈ 27 − 18.9 = **8.1 cm** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Each reading should be checked against the table. Height 50 lies between 30 (at 20 cm) and 65 (at 25 cm), so a median between 20 and 25 cm is reasonable.

**7. (a)** At 28 cm, CF ≈ 65 + 25 × 3/5 = 80 [1], so 100 − 80 = **20 fish** [1].

**(b)** At 17 cm, CF ≈ 8 + 22 × 2/5 ≈ **17 fish** [1].

**(c)** 90th percentile at height 90 [1] gives **30 cm** [1].

**8.** Frequencies: **12, 18, 32, 22, 6** [3] (one mark off for each error). Check: they add to 90.

**9.** Town B's median (31 minutes) is lower than Town A's (34 minutes) [1], so journeys in Town B are shorter on average [1]. Town A's IQR (12 minutes) is smaller than Town B's (18 minutes) [1], so journey times in Town A are more consistent [1].

**10. (a)** About 75% of the students scored at or below Ali's mark [1].

**(b)** About 25% of 400 = **100 students** [1].

**11.** The points should be plotted at the **upper boundaries** (15, 20, 25, ...) [1]; they should be joined with a **smooth curve**, not ruled lines [1]; the graph should start at **(10, 0)**, the lower boundary of the first class [1].

**12. (a)** Height 100 lies between 62 (at 40 hours) and 128 (at 50 hours) [1]; median ≈ 40 + 10 × 38/66 ≈ **45.8 hours** [1].

**(b)** At 45 hours, CF ≈ 95 [1], so about 200 − 95 = 105 batteries last more than 45 hours [1]. That is about 52.5%, so the claim is **only just true**; with estimated readings, "most" is a weak claim [1].

*Examiner insight:* The median is about 45.8 hours, so roughly half the batteries last longer than 45.8 hours. A claim near the median needs careful wording.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Plotting at midpoints or forgetting the starting point.
- Ruled segments instead of a smooth curve.
- Quoting the reading height, not the value on the horizontal axis.
- Forgetting to subtract from the total in "more than" questions.
- Readings that fall outside the class the table shows they must be in.
- Comparisons with medians only, or with no context.
