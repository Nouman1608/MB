These notes condense section 3.1.2, A simple model of the atom, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202) specification, Version 6.2. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [simple model of the atom study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-chemistry-9202-a-simple-model-of-the-atom/); then try the [simple model of the atom practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-chemistry-9202-a-simple-model-of-the-atom-practice/).

## Statements a to k in one line each

- **a** All substances are made of atoms; an element contains only one sort of atom; about 100 elements.
- **b** Each element has a chemical symbol (O = oxygen).
- **c** Small central nucleus (protons and neutrons) with electrons around it; the model has changed over time.
- **d** Relative charges: proton +1, neutron 0, electron −1.
- **e** In an atom, electrons = protons; no overall charge.
- **f** Atomic number = protons; mass number = protons + neutrons.
- **g** Isotopes: same element, different numbers of neutrons.
- **h** Notation: mass number top left, atomic number bottom left.
- **i** Electrons fill the lowest energy levels first; structures of elements 1–20.
- **j** Relative masses: proton 1, neutron 1, electron very small.
- **k** Ar compares atoms with ¹²C; an average for the isotopes. No Ar calculations from abundances.

## Sub-atomic particles

| Particle | Location | Charge | Mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proton | nucleus | +1 | 1 |
| Neutron | nucleus | 0 | 1 |
| Electron | shells | −1 | very small |

## Counting particles

```
protons   = atomic number (bottom number)
electrons = protons (atom)
neutrons  = mass number (top number) − atomic number
```

| Atom | p | n | e |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⁷₃Li | 3 | 4 | 3 |
| ²⁷₁₃Al | 13 | 14 | 13 |
| ³²₁₆S | 16 | 16 | 16 |
| ³⁹₁₉K | 19 | 20 | 19 |

## Isotopes

- Same protons and electrons → same element, same chemistry.
- Different neutrons → different mass numbers.
- Example: ³⁵Cl (18 neutrons) and ³⁷Cl (20 neutrons).
- The Ar of chlorine, 35.5, is an average of its isotopes.

## Changing models of the atom

| Scientist | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Dalton | atoms as tiny solid spheres |
| Thomson | discovered the electron; "plum pudding" model |
| Rutherford | nuclear model, from alpha-particle scattering |
| Bohr | electrons in fixed energy levels |
| Chadwick | discovered the neutron |

New evidence → model changed.

## Worked recall: from particles to symbol

An atom has 13 protons, 14 neutrons and 13 electrons.

```
atomic number = protons            = 13  → aluminium (Al)
mass number   = protons + neutrons = 27
symbol        = ²⁷₁₃Al
structure     = 2,8,3              (2 + 8 + 3 = 13)
```

Work in this order every time: protons first (they fix the element), then mass number, then electrons and shells.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Element | substance made of only one sort of atom |
| Nucleus | small central part of an atom, containing protons and neutrons |
| Atomic number | number of protons in an atom |
| Mass number | number of protons + neutrons |
| Isotopes | atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons |
| Energy level (shell) | the region an electron occupies; lowest levels fill first |
| Relative atomic mass (Ar) | average mass of an element's atoms compared with ¹²C |

## Exam traps

- Isotopes differ **only** in neutrons.
- The bigger number in the notation is the **mass number**.
- Electron relative mass is **very small**, not zero.
- K is 2,8,8,1, not 2,8,9.
- Atoms are neutral; do not add or remove electrons unless the question is about ions.
- Ar is not the mass number of one atom; it is an average.
- You will not be asked to calculate Ar from isotopic abundances.

- Shell diagrams: put the electrons for each shell on the correct circle, and check that the number on each circle matches the comma-separated structure.

## Self-test

1. State the relative charge and relative mass of a neutron.
2. Where is almost all the mass of an atom found?
3. Why does an atom have no overall charge?
4. How many neutrons are in ¹⁹₉F?
5. An atom has 15 protons and 16 neutrons. Give its mass number and name the element.
6. Define isotopes.
7. ²⁰Ne and ²²Ne are isotopes. How many neutrons does each have?
8. Give the electronic structure of aluminium (atomic number 13).
9. Which element has the electronic structure 2,8,7?
10. Why is the Ar of chlorine not a whole number?
11. Which scientist's model first included a nucleus?
12. Why do isotopes of an element have the same chemical reactions?

## Answers

1. Charge 0; mass 1.
2. In the nucleus.
3. It has equal numbers of protons (+) and electrons (−).
4. 19 − 9 = 10.
5. Mass number 31; phosphorus.
6. Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons.
7. ²⁰Ne: 10 neutrons; ²²Ne: 12 neutrons (neon has 10 protons).
8. 2,8,3.
9. Chlorine.
10. It is an average of the masses of the chlorine isotopes, weighted by how common each is.
11. Rutherford.
12. They have the same number of electrons arranged in the same way.
