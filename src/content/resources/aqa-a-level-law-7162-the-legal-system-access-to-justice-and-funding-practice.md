These questions test the section 3.1 row on access to justice and funding: alternative sources of legal advice, private funding and public funding. Several Section B questions ask you to advise a person, so apply each source or method to their facts.

---

## Section A

**1.** Identify **two** alternative sources of legal advice other than a private solicitor. **[2]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by before-the-event insurance. **[2]**

**3.** Explain how a conditional fee agreement works. **[3]**

**4.** State the three tests applied when deciding whether to grant civil legal aid. **[3]**

**5.** Explain what is meant by the interests of justice test in criminal legal aid. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** effect of the Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012 on conditional fee agreements. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Explain the role of law centres in providing access to justice. **[4]**

**8.** Kofi is injured when a delivery van hits him on a pedestrian crossing. He is a student with no savings. Advise Kofi on how he might fund a claim for compensation. **[6]**

**9.** Mia is arrested on suspicion of theft and later charged with an either-way offence. She is unemployed. Advise Mia on the state funding available to her. **[6]**

**10.** Sam, a tenant, has fallen behind with his rent and his landlord has begun possession proceedings. Advise Sam on where he could obtain advice and whether he could receive civil legal aid. **[6]**

**11.** Explain the advantages and disadvantages of conditional fee agreements for claimants. **[6]**

**12.** "Since 2013, access to justice in civil cases has depended more on charities and private funding than on the state." Discuss. **[12]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Any two of: help lines [1]; Citizens Advice; law centres; trade unions [1].

**2.** Legal expenses insurance taken out before any dispute arises [1], often as an add-on to home or motor insurance, which pays legal costs if the insured person later needs to bring or defend a claim [1].

**3.** The lawyer agrees to take no fee, or a reduced fee, if the case is lost [1]. If the case is won, the lawyer receives the normal fee plus a success fee [1], which can be up to 100% of the normal fee [1].

**4.** Whether the matter is within the scope of legal aid [1]; whether the applicant passes the means test on disposable income and capital [1]; whether the case passes the merits test on prospects of success and benefit [1].

**5.** It decides whether a defendant should be represented in court at public expense [1]. Factors include whether the defendant is likely to lose their liberty or livelihood or suffer serious damage to reputation [1], and whether there is a substantial question of law or the defendant cannot understand the proceedings or present their own case [1].

**6.** The success fee can no longer be recovered from the losing party [1], so it is paid out of the winning claimant's damages [1]. (Credit instead the cap in personal injury cases: the success fee may not exceed 25% of damages, excluding damages for future loss, for the same two marks.)

**7.** Law centres are independent not-for-profit organisations, the first opening in North Kensington in 1970 [1]. They provide free legal advice, and often representation, by solicitors and caseworkers [1], specialising in social welfare law such as housing, employment, immigration and welfare benefits [1]. Their reach is limited because they exist only in some areas and depend on grants and contracts, so many have closed or reduced services [1].

**8.** Civil legal aid is unlikely, because most personal injury claims are outside its scope [1]. Kofi has no savings, so funding the claim from his own resources is not realistic [1]. He should check whether he or his family has before-the-event legal expenses insurance, for example with home or motor insurance [1]. The most likely route is a conditional fee agreement: a solicitor would act on a "no win, no fee" basis because a claim against a driver who hit a pedestrian on a crossing appears to have good prospects [1]. If he wins, the success fee will come out of his damages, capped at 25% of damages excluding future loss [1]. He could take out after-the-event insurance against the risk of paying the other side's costs if the claim fails [1].

**9.** At the police station Mia is entitled to free legal advice, whatever her means [1], under s58 PACE 1984, through the duty solicitor scheme [1]. For representation in court she must pass the interests of justice test [1]; a theft charge could lead to a custodial sentence and damage to her reputation, which supports her application [1]. She must also pass the means test [1]; as she is unemployed she is likely to qualify, and a duty solicitor at the magistrates' court can help her on the day [1].

**10.** Sam could get free advice from Citizens Advice, which advises on housing and debt [1], or from a law centre, which may also represent him [1]. A housing help line run by a charity could give immediate advice [1]. Civil legal aid remains in scope where a person's home is at risk, so possession proceedings fall within scope [1]. He must pass the means test, which he may well do as he is in rent arrears [1], and the merits test, depending on whether he has a defence or can negotiate to keep his home [1].

**11.** **Advantages:** people who could not afford fees and do not qualify for legal aid can bring claims [1]; the client does not pay fees if the case is lost [1]; lawyers have an incentive to work efficiently and win [1]. **Disadvantages:** lawyers may refuse cases with weaker prospects, so some valid claims go unfunded [1]; since 2013 the success fee comes out of the claimant's damages [1]; the claimant may still need after-the-event insurance to cover the other side's costs, which can be expensive [1].

**12.** Levels-based. **Agree:** LASPO removed large areas of civil law from legal aid scope, such as most private family, employment, welfare benefits and debt matters [1]. The number of litigants in person has increased, especially in family courts [1]. People therefore rely on Citizens Advice and law centres [1], but these depend on charitable and local funding and provision is patchy [1]. Trade unions help, but only their members [1]. Personal injury claims are now funded mainly by conditional fee agreements [1] and legal expenses insurance [1]. **Disagree:** the state still funds civil legal aid in key areas, such as care proceedings, domestic abuse and cases where a home is at risk [1]. Exceptional case funding under s10 LASPO provides a safety net where human rights require it [1]. Government help lines such as Civil Legal Advice remain [1]. **Evaluation:** private methods depend on prospects of success and ability to pay, so people with low means and complex non-injury problems may have no route at all [1]. A reasoned conclusion, for example that the statement is largely accurate outside the protected areas [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Recommending civil legal aid without checking scope.
- Forgetting that police station advice is free to everyone.
- Listing all funding methods without applying them to the person's means.
- Ignoring the cost of the other side's costs in civil claims.
