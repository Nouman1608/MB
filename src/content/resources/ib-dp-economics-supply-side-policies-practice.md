## Questions

**1.** (a) Define supply-side policies. **[2]**
**(b)** Outline two goals of supply-side policies. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between market-based and interventionist supply-side policies, giving one example of each. **[4]**

**3.** In the fictional country of Tarvan, a package of competition policies raises potential output from 800 to 860 billion crowns. Aggregate demand is given by P = 300 − 0.1Y, where P is the price level and Y is real output in billions of crowns.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in potential output. **[2]**
**(b)** Draw an AD/AS diagram with an LRAS curve to show the effect, and state the new price level. **[4]**

**4.** In the fictional town of Ashgrove, the market for care workers has labour demand Qd = 1,000 − 40W and labour supply Qs = 200 + 60W, where Q is the number of workers and W is the hourly wage in crowns.
**(a)** Find the market-clearing wage and the employment level. **[2]**
**(b)** A minimum wage of 10 crowns is introduced. Calculate the number of workers unemployed. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage fall in employment compared with equilibrium. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[2]**

**5.** Using a minimum wage diagram, explain how abolishing a minimum wage could reduce unemployment, and state one equity concern. **[4]**

**6.** In the fictional country of Brennar, firms earn total profits of 200 million crowns. The business tax rate falls from 30% to 24%. Assume profits stay the same.
**(a)** Calculate after-tax profits before and after the cut. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in business tax revenue. **[2]**

**7.** Explain two demand-side effects of supply-side policies. **[4]**

**8.** A government in the fictional country of Cordel spends 90 million crowns on a new road and rail link. It estimates that the link saves firms 18 million crowns a year.
**(a)** Calculate how many years it takes for the savings to equal the cost. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the net gain after 8 years. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one reason why the real return might differ from this estimate. **[2]**

**9.** Explain two constraints on market-based supply-side policies. **[4]**

**10.** Explain how an income tax cut could have both supply-side and demand-side effects, and comment on what this means for the inflation objective. **[6]**

**11.** Evaluate the use of market-based policies to reduce unemployment and inflation. **[10]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Supply-side policies are government actions to increase the productive capacity of the economy or the efficiency of markets [1], rather than changing total spending [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Any two of: long-term growth by raising productive capacity; better competition and efficiency; lower labour costs and unemployment through labour market flexibility; lower inflation for competitiveness; stronger incentives to invest in innovation. One mark for each goal stated clearly [1] [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Outline" asks for a brief account, so a named goal with a few words of explanation is enough; a full paragraph per goal wastes time.

**2.** Market-based policies free markets or change incentives so that private decisions raise efficiency or output [1]. Example: privatization, deregulation or a tax cut [1]. Interventionist policies involve the government directly providing or funding something that raises capacity [1]. Example: education and training, infrastructure or research and development [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Distinguish" needs the difference stated clearly, not two separate definitions; use a linking word such as "whereas".

**3. (a)** Increase = (860 − 800) / 800 × 100 [1] = **7.5%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Axes labelled price level and real output [1]. Original LRAS1 at 800, AD downward sloping, equilibrium labelled at 800 [1]. LRAS2 drawn to the right at 860 with the new equilibrium labelled [1]. New price level: P = 300 − 0.1 × 860 = **214** (down from 220) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A diagram earns credit when every axis, curve and equilibrium is labelled; a correct shape with missing labels is easy to lose marks on.

**4. (a)** 1,000 − 40W = 200 + 60W, so 800 = 100W [1]. **W = 8 crowns** and Q = 1,000 − 320 = **680 workers** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** At W = 10, Qd = 1,000 − 400 = 600 and Qs = 200 + 600 = 800 [1]. Unemployment = 800 − 600 = **200 workers** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Jobs lost = 680 − 600 = 80 [1]. 80 / 680 × 100 = **11.8%** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* For "calculate", show the relevant stages in the working; a bare number gives nothing to reward if it is wrong, and the base for a percentage must be the original figure.

**5.** Diagram: wage against quantity of labour with a minimum wage line above the equilibrium wage, Qd below Qs and the gap labelled unemployment [1]. Abolishing the minimum wage lets the wage fall to the equilibrium level [1]. As the wage drops, employers want more workers and fewer people offer labour, until the two match and the surplus vanishes [1]. Equity concern: workers who keep jobs may earn less, so low-paid households could be worse off [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain the movement along both curves; saying "unemployment falls" without the mechanism leaves the analysis incomplete.

**6. (a)** Before: 200 × (1 − 0.30) = **140 million crowns** [1]. After: 200 × (1 − 0.24) = **152 million crowns** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Revenue before = 200 × 0.30 = 60 and after = 200 × 0.24 = 48 [1]. Change = (48 − 60) / 60 × 100 = **−20%** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A fall in tax revenue is a cost to the government budget; link it back to the claim that market-based policies are cheap.

**7.** Any two, each with explanation. Income tax cut: disposable income rises [1], so consumption, a component of AD, increases and AD shifts right [1]. Spending on infrastructure or education: it is government spending [1], so AD rises in the short run before capacity grows [1]. Also acceptable: business tax cuts raising investment, or lower costs improving net exports. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the AD component each effect works through; "AD goes up" alone does not show the chain.

**8. (a)** 90 / 18 [1] = **5 years** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 8 × 18 = 144 [1]. 144 − 90 = **54 million crowns** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** One reason with explanation, for example: construction takes years, so benefits start late [1], which lowers the value of the savings in the early years [1]. A cost overrun making the payback longer is also acceptable. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Relate the reason to the guide's constraints of costs and time lags and to the numbers in the question.

**9.** Any two of: equity issues, time lags, vested interests, environmental impact. For each, one mark for the constraint and one for the explanation. For example, equity: tax cuts mostly help high earners and lower benefits hit the poor [1][1]. Time lags: competition and incentives take time to change behaviour, so effects on growth come late [1][1]. Vested interests (unions or protected firms blocking reform) and environmental impact (more pollution from deregulation and growth) are equally acceptable. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A constraint stated as a single word earns less than one tied to a named policy and a consequence.

**10.** Supply side: lower tax raises the reward for work, so labour supply or hours may rise [1], which increases productive capacity and shifts LRAS right [1]. Demand side: higher take-home pay raises consumption, so AD shifts right [1]. If AD rises more than LRAS, price level pressure increases [1]. If LRAS rises as much or more, prices may stay stable or fall [1]. Comment: inflation turns on how large each shift is and when it arrives [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Explain" needs the chain on both sides, and a comment should reach a judgement, not repeat the explanation.

**11.** Indicative points; a real answer is judged on the quality of the argument, not on counting points.
- Define market-based policies and identify those relevant, for example labour market flexibility and competition policies [1].
- Unemployment: abolishing minimum wages or cutting benefits lowers labour costs and encourages hiring [1], using the minimum wage diagram [1].
- Inflation: deregulation, privatization or trade liberalization raises competition and cuts costs, shifting LRAS right and lowering the price level [1].
- Strength: improved resource allocation [1], and no burden on the government budget [1].
- Constraint: equity issues, as low-paid or unemployed households lose income [1].
- Constraint: time lags and vested interests, so effects may be slow [1].
- Limit: they do little for cyclical (demand deficient) unemployment [1].
- Judgement that depends on the type of unemployment and the time horizon [1].
**[10]**
*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" asks you to weigh strengths against limitations and reach a supported judgement; a one-sided list of benefits stays descriptive.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not labelling axes, curves and equilibrium points on a diagram.
- Setting the minimum wage line at or below equilibrium.
- Shifting AD instead of LRAS for a supply-side policy.
- Using the wrong base for a percentage change.
- Giving a calculated number with no working.
- Describing a policy without explaining how it reaches growth, unemployment or inflation.
- Listing constraints without tying them to the policy named.
- Ending an evaluation with no judgement.
