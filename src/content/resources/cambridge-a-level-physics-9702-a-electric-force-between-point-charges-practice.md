Take 1/(4πε₀) = 8.99 × 10⁹ m F⁻¹, e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻², mass of electron = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg, mass of proton = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg and g = 9.81 m s⁻².

## Section A

**1.** State Coulomb's law. **[2]**

**2.** Two point charges exert a force F on each other. One charge is doubled and the separation is tripled. Express the new force in terms of F. **[2]**

**3.** Two charged metal spheres, each of radius 2.0 cm, have their centres 10 cm apart. State the distance that should be used in Coulomb's law, and explain why. **[2]**

**4.** Calculate the force between charges of +4.0 μC and +6.0 μC that are 0.30 m apart, and state whether it is attractive or repulsive. **[3]**

**5.** Use Coulomb's law to show that the units of ε₀ can be written as C² N⁻¹ m⁻², and show that these are equivalent to F m⁻¹. **[2]**

**6.** Calculate the ratio of the electric force to the gravitational force between two protons, and explain why the ratio does not depend on their separation. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** Two identical small conducting spheres carry charges of +8.0 nC and −4.0 nC. Their centres are 6.0 cm apart.

**(a)** Calculate the force between them and state its direction. **[2]**

**(b)** The spheres are touched together and then returned to their original positions. Calculate the new force and state its direction. **[3]**

**8.** A charge A of +2.0 μC is at x = 0 and a charge B of +8.0 μC is at x = 0.60 m.

**(a)** Determine the position between A and B at which a third charge would experience no resultant force. **[3]**

**(b)** A charge of −1.0 μC is placed at x = 0.30 m. Calculate the magnitude and direction of the resultant force on it. **[3]**

**9.** A charge C of +5.0 nC is at the origin. A charge of +4.0 nC is 0.030 m from C along the positive x-axis, and a charge of −4.0 nC is 0.040 m from C along the positive y-axis. Calculate the magnitude of the resultant force on C and its angle to the negative x-axis. **[4]**

**10.** Two identical small balls, each of mass 0.20 g, hang from a common point on insulating threads 0.50 m long. They are given equal charges and come to rest with their centres 0.060 m apart. Calculate the charge on each ball. **[5]**

**11.** In a simple model of the hydrogen atom, an electron moves in a circle of radius 5.3 × 10⁻¹¹ m around a proton.

**(a)** Calculate the electric force on the electron. **[2]**

**(b)** This force provides the centripetal force. Calculate the speed of the electron. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why the force between two charged conducting spheres calculated using Coulomb's law becomes less accurate when the spheres are very close together. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The force between two point charges is **proportional to the product of the charges** [1] and **inversely proportional to the square of their separation** [1].

**2.** F ∝ Q₁Q₂/r², so new force = F × 2 ÷ 3² [1] = **2F/9** [1].

**3.** **10 cm** (0.10 m) [1]. Outside a charged sphere, its charge acts as if concentrated at its **centre**, so the centre-to-centre distance is used [1].

**4.** F = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 4.0 × 10⁻⁶ × 6.0 × 10⁻⁶ ÷ 0.30² [1] = **2.4 N** [1], **repulsive** (like charges) [1].

**5.** ε₀ = Q₁Q₂/(4πFr²), so units are C² ÷ (N m²) = **C² N⁻¹ m⁻²** [1]. F m⁻¹ = C V⁻¹ m⁻¹ = C ÷ (J C⁻¹) ÷ m = C² J⁻¹ m⁻¹ = C² N⁻¹ m⁻² [1].

**6.** F_E/F_G = (8.99 × 10⁹ × e²) ÷ (G m_p²) = (8.99 × 10⁹ × 2.56 × 10⁻³⁸) ÷ (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × (1.67 × 10⁻²⁷)²) [1] = **1.2 × 10³⁶** [1]. Both forces are proportional to **1/r²**, so r cancels [1].

**7. (a)** F = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 8.0 × 10⁻⁹ × 4.0 × 10⁻⁹ ÷ 0.060² [1] = **8.0 × 10⁻⁵ N**, **attractive** [1].

**(b)** Total charge = +4.0 nC, so each has **+2.0 nC** [1]. F = 8.99 × 10⁹ × (2.0 × 10⁻⁹)² ÷ 0.060² [1] = **1.0 × 10⁻⁵ N**, **repulsive** [1].

**8. (a)** At distance x from A: 2.0/x² = 8.0/(0.60 − x)² [1], so (0.60 − x)/x = 2 [1]; x = **0.20 m** from A [1].

**(b)** Force from A (attractive, towards A, −x direction): 8.99 × 10⁹ × 2.0 × 10⁻⁶ × 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ ÷ 0.30² = 0.20 N [1]. Force from B (attractive, towards B, +x direction): 8.99 × 10⁹ × 8.0 × 10⁻¹² ÷ 0.30² = 0.80 N [1]. Resultant = **0.60 N towards B** (+x direction) [1].

**9.** Force from +4.0 nC (repulsive, along −x): 8.99 × 10⁹ × 20 × 10⁻¹⁸ ÷ 0.030² = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ N [1]. Force from −4.0 nC (attractive, along +y): 8.99 × 10⁹ × 20 × 10⁻¹⁸ ÷ 0.040² = 1.1 × 10⁻⁴ N [1]. Resultant = √(2.0² + 1.12²) × 10⁻⁴ = **2.3 × 10⁻⁴ N** [1], at tan⁻¹(1.12/2.0) = **29°** to the negative x-axis (towards +y) [1].

**10.** Each thread makes angle θ with the vertical where sin θ = 0.030 ÷ 0.50, so θ = 3.4° [1]. Horizontal: F = T sin θ; vertical: T cos θ = mg; so F = mg tan θ [1] = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ × 9.81 × 0.060 = 1.18 × 10⁻⁴ N [1]. Q² = F r² ÷ 8.99 × 10⁹ = 1.18 × 10⁻⁴ × 0.060² ÷ 8.99 × 10⁹ = 4.7 × 10⁻¹⁷ C² [1]; Q = **6.9 × 10⁻⁹ C** [1].

**11. (a)** F = 8.99 × 10⁹ × (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)² ÷ (5.3 × 10⁻¹¹)² [1] = **8.2 × 10⁻⁸ N** [1].

**(b)** mv²/r = F, so v = √(Fr/m) = √(8.2 × 10⁻⁸ × 5.3 × 10⁻¹¹ ÷ 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) [1] = **2.2 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹** [1].

**12.** The charge on each sphere is **attracted or repelled by the charge on the other**, so it **redistributes** over the surface and is no longer uniform [1]. The charge then no longer acts as if it were at the centre, so the centre-to-centre distance no longer gives the correct force [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the gap between sphere surfaces instead of the centre-to-centre distance.
- Not squaring the separation.
- Not converting nC and μC.
- Adding forces from different directions as if they were in a line.
- Sharing charge by magnitude rather than by algebraic sum.
- Giving a force value without saying whether it is attractive or repulsive.
