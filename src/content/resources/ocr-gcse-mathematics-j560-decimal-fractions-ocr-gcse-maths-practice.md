## Section B: structured questions

**7.** You are given that 54 × 23 = 1 242. Write down the value of

**(b)** 0.54 × 230 **[1]**

**(c)** 12.42 ÷ 0.23 **[1]**

**8.** Six concert tickets cost £40.50 in total.

**(a)** Find the cost of one ticket. **[2]**

**(b)** How many tickets can be bought for £94.50? **[2]**

**9.**

**(a)** Work out 0.0378 ÷ 0.0006. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the answer to part (a) is larger than 0.0378. **[1]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) Show that 0.454545... (0.45 recurring) = 5/11. **[3]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) Write 0.1666... (0.16 with the 6 recurring) as a fraction in its simplest form. **[3]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) Write 0.318181... (0.318 with the 18 recurring) as a fraction in its simplest form. **[4]**

**13.** A plank 2.85 m long is cut into pieces that are each 0.15 m long.

**(a)** How many pieces are there? **[2]**

**(b)** The plank cost £4.56. Find the cost of the wood in each piece. **[2]**

**14.** Lina says, "2/9 = 0.2, because the top number is 2."

**(a)** Use division to write 2/9 as a decimal. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why 2/9 cannot be written as a terminating decimal. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 28/100 = **0.28** [1]

**(b)** 275/1 000 = **0.275** [1]

**(c)** 84/1 000 = **21/250** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Scale to a power of 10: × 4 for twenty-fifths, × 25 for fortieths.

**2. (a)** **6 hundredths** (0.06) [1]

**(b)** **427** [1]

**(c)** **0.0521** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* "Value" needs the place: "6" alone does not score.

**3. (a)** **0.625** [1]

**(b)** Division showing remainders 2 and 9 repeating [1]; **0.181818...** (18 recurring) [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Once a remainder repeats, the digits repeat, so you can stop and write the recurring notation.

**4. (a)** Points lined up, 7.040 − 2.689 [1]; **4.351** [1]

**(b)** **−2.35** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* In part (b) the negative number is the larger size, so the answer is negative: 3.80 − 1.45 = 2.35, then the sign.

**5. (a)** **0.042** [1]

**(b)** 43 × 25 = 1 075 [1]; **10.75** [1]

**(c)** **−0.14** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Count decimal places in the question: 0.06 × 0.7 has three, so 42 becomes 0.042.

**6. (a)** **−0.88** [1]

**(b)** 360 ÷ 12 [1]; **30** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Scale both numbers by 100 in part (b). Scaling only 0.12 gives 3.6 ÷ 12 = 0.3, which is 100 times too small.

**7. (a)** **12.42** [1]

**(b)** **124.2** [1]

**(c)** **54** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* In part (c), 1 242 ÷ 23 = 54. The dividend is 100 times smaller and the divisor 100 times smaller, so the quotient is unchanged.

**8. (a)** 40.50 ÷ 6 [1]; **£6.75** [1]

**(b)** 94.50 ÷ 6.75 = 9 450 ÷ 675 [1]; **14 tickets** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* An alternative for part (b): 12 tickets cost 2 × £40.50 = £81, and the remaining £13.50 buys 2 more, giving 14.

**9. (a)** 378 ÷ 6 (both × 10 000) [1]; **63** [1]

**(b)** Dividing by a number less than 1 (0.0006) gives an answer larger than the number being divided [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* 0.0006 fits into 0.0378 many times, so the answer must be large.

**10.** x = 0.4545..., 100x = 45.4545... [1]; 99x = 45 [1]; x = 45/99 = 5/11 [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Show the subtraction line clearly. Writing "0.45 recurring = 45/99" without algebra may not earn the method marks in a "show that".

**11.** 10x = 1.666... and 100x = 16.666... [1]; 90x = 15 [1]; x = 15/90 = **1/6** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* 100x − x leaves a decimal, because only the 6 recurs. Choose two multiples with identical decimal parts.

**12.** 10x = 3.1818... and 1 000x = 318.1818... [1]; 990x = 315 [1]; x = 315/990 [1]; **7/22** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Check by division: 7 ÷ 22 = 0.31818..., which matches.

**13. (a)** 2.85 ÷ 0.15 = 285 ÷ 15 [1]; **19 pieces** [1]

**(b)** 4.56 ÷ 19 [1]; **£0.24** (24p) [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Estimate part (a): 3 ÷ 0.15 = 20, so 19 is sensible.

**14. (a)** **0.222...** (2 recurring) [1]

**(b)** Dividing 2 by 9 leaves remainder 2 at every step, so the digit 2 repeats for ever (or: 9 has a prime factor other than 2 and 5) [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Lina has confused 2/9 with 2/10. Only a denominator of 10 turns the numerator straight into tenths.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Copying the digits of a fraction into a decimal, as in 3/25 = 0.325.
- Giving the digit rather than its value in place-value questions.
- Lining up the right-hand digits rather than the decimal points.
- Counting decimal places from the answer instead of the question.
- Scaling only the divisor when dividing by a decimal.
- (Higher tier only) Using 100x − x when there is a non-repeating digit first.
- Rounding a recurring decimal when the question asks for an exact answer.
