Condensed recall for **7.3 Humid tropical (rainforest) and seasonally humid tropical (savanna) ecosystems**, Topic 7, Paper 3 Advanced Physical Geography Options. The syllabus strands are plant communities (climax, subclimax, plagioclimax), vegetation characteristics, nutrient cycling (Gersmehl diagrams, soil fertility, energy flows, trophic levels) and soil formation (oxisols/latosols, tropical red and brown earths). For full explanations, read the [7.3 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-geography-9696-humid-tropical-rainforest-ecosystems-and-seasonally-humid-tropical-savanna-ecosystems/), then try the [7.3 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-geography-9696-humid-tropical-rainforest-ecosystems-and-seasonally-humid-tropical-savanna-ecosystems-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Succession | Sequence of plant communities replacing one another over time |
| Sere / seral stage | The whole sequence / one stage in it |
| Primary succession | Starts on a surface with no soil, such as new lava |
| Secondary succession | Starts where vegetation is removed but soil remains |
| Pioneer species | First colonisers; fast-growing and light-demanding |
| Climatic climax | Final stable community in balance with climate (Clements's monoclimax) |
| Polyclimax | Idea that soils, drainage and relief can also give stable communities (Tansley) |
| Subclimax | Succession halted by a natural factor, such as waterlogging or lightning fire |
| Plagioclimax | Succession deflected or held by people, such as burning or grazing |
| Pyrophyte | Plant adapted to survive fire |
| Mycorrhizae | Fungi on roots that help plants take up nutrients |
| Cation exchange capacity | Ability of soil particles to hold nutrient ions |
| NPP | Net primary productivity: energy or biomass left after plant respiration |
| Trophic level | Feeding level in a food chain |
| Ferrallitisation | Removal of silica and bases, leaving iron and aluminium oxides |
| Laterite | Hardened iron-rich layer (ferricrete) |
| Catena | Repeated sequence of soils down a slope |

## Plant communities

- **Rainforest:** climatic climax of the humid tropics. Secondary forest after clearance is a plagioclimax.
- **Krakatau** (eruption 1883): classic tropical primary succession, from ferns and grasses to forest over decades.
- **Pioneer trees** such as *Cecropia* in the American tropics form secondary forest on abandoned land.
- **Savanna:** debated status.
  - Climatic climax: wet-dry climate limits trees.
  - Edaphic subclimax: poor or seasonally waterlogged soils.
  - Plagioclimax: burning and grazing keep it open.
  - Fire-exclusion plots often turn to woodland, supporting the fire view in wetter savanna; in drier savanna climate dominates.

## Vegetation features and their purpose

| Rainforest | Purpose | Savanna | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Layers up to emergents over 40 m | Competition for light | Grass with scattered trees | Drought and fire limit trees |
| Buttress roots | Support with shallow roots | Deep tap roots | Reach groundwater |
| Drip tips | Shed water | Small, waxy or thorny leaves | Cut water loss, deter browsers |
| Lianas, epiphytes | Reach light | Deciduous trees | Save water in the dry season |
| Thin bark | No cold, dry or fire stress | Thick, corky bark | Survive fire |
| Very high biodiversity | Stable climate, many niches | Baobab water storage | Survive long drought |

Tree cover in savanna decreases from wetter woodland savanna to drier grass savanna.

## Gersmehl comparison

| Store or flow | Rainforest | Savanna |
|---|---|---|
| Biomass | Largest store | Smaller |
| Litter | Small: decomposition in weeks to months | Small: fire and termites remove it |
| Soil | Small: rapid uptake and leaching | Relatively larger |
| Flows | Large and fast | Smaller, seasonal |
| Special feature | Mycorrhizae speed uptake | Fire returns nutrients as ash; some nitrogen lost |

## Why rainforest soils are infertile

1. Year-round leaching removes calcium, magnesium and potassium.
2. Kaolinite and sesquioxides have low cation exchange capacity.
3. Acidic soils; aluminium can harm crops.
4. Most nutrients are held in the biomass.
5. After burning, ash fertility lasts only a few years.

## Energy flows

- Whittaker and Likens estimates: tropical rainforest NPP about 2 200 g/m²/year; savanna about 900 g/m²/year.
- About 10% transfer between levels; most energy lost as heat in respiration.
- Example with 10%: 20 000 → 2 000 → 200 → 20 kJ per m² per year.
- Savanna supports large grazing herds because productivity is in edible grass; rainforest productivity is largely in wood and the canopy.

## Soils

| | Oxisol (latosol) | Tropical red and brown earths |
|---|---|---|
| Ecosystem | Rainforest | Savanna |
| Depth | Very deep, often many metres | Shallower |
| Leaching | Intense, all year | Seasonal |
| Key horizon | Deep red or yellow B horizon rich in sesquioxides and kaolinite | B horizon with ironstone concretions or laterite crust |
| Water movement | Downward | Downward in wet season, upward (capillary rise) in dry season |
| Colour | Red or yellow | Red where well drained; browner with more organic matter |

**Catena (Milne, 1935, East Africa):** red, well-drained soils on upper slopes; browner on mid-slopes; grey or black waterlogged clays in valley bottoms.

## Exam traps

- Lush vegetation does not mean fertile soil.
- Lightning fire gives a subclimax; human burning gives a plagioclimax.
- Scale Gersmehl circles and arrows.
- Explain vegetation features, do not just list them.
- The oxisol's thick red layer is the B horizon, not the A.
- In energy questions, show each multiplication and state units.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between primary and secondary succession.
2. Name a tropical example used to study primary succession.
3. What is the difference between a subclimax and a plagioclimax?
4. Give one piece of evidence that savanna may be a plagioclimax.
5. Why do many rainforest trees have buttress roots?
6. Give two savanna plant adaptations to fire or drought.
7. Which Gersmehl store is largest in the rainforest?
8. Why is the litter store small in the savanna?
9. Why does kaolinite make soils infertile?
10. Using a 10% transfer, how much energy reaches secondary consumers from NPP of 12 000 kJ per m² per year?
11. Why do savanna soils have ironstone concretions?
12. What is a catena?

### Answers

1. Primary succession starts with no soil; secondary succession starts where soil remains after vegetation is removed.
2. Krakatau, after the 1883 eruption.
3. A subclimax is halted by a natural factor; a plagioclimax by human activity.
4. Plots protected from fire often become woodland.
5. To support tall trees whose roots are shallow.
6. Deep tap roots; thick, corky bark (also small waxy leaves, deciduous habit, water storage).
7. Biomass.
8. Fire burns it and termites remove it.
9. It has a low cation exchange capacity, so it holds few nutrient ions.
10. 12 000 × 0.1 = 1 200 kJ to primary consumers; 1 200 × 0.1 = 120 kJ to secondary consumers.
11. Iron is leached down in the wet season and concentrated as water evaporates and rises in the dry season.
12. A repeated sequence of soils from hilltop to valley floor caused by differences in drainage and movement of material.
