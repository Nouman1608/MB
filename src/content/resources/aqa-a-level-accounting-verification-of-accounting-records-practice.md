## Questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following errors would cause the trial balance totals to disagree? **[1]**

A. A £600 purchase invoice was not entered in the purchases journal.
B. Cash sales of £300 were credited to commission received.
C. Wages of £1 240, correct in the cash book, were posted to the wages account as £1 420.
D. A £820 credit sale was debited to the wrong customer's account.

**2.** Prepare journal entries, with narratives, to correct the following errors.

**(a)** Shop shelving costing £3 600, bought for use in the business, was debited to the purchases account. **[2]**
**(b)** A credit sale of £1 240 to T Marsh was entered in the sales journal as £1 420. **[2]**

**3.** The debit total of a trial balance exceeds the credit total by £540. The bookkeeper thinks this is a transposition error. Explain whether this is possible, and name one other type of error that could cause the difference. **[3]**

**4.** On 31 August 2026 the cash book of Lowther Cycles showed a bank overdraft of £1 380. The bank statement showed an overdraft of £1 079. Comparing the two revealed:

- a direct debit for electricity, £245, bank interest charged, £38, and a credit transfer from a customer, £960, all missing from the cash book
- a cheque received for £615, entered in the cash book as £651
- unpresented cheques totalling £1 870
- an outstanding lodgement of £2 210.

**(a)** Calculate the updated cash book balance. **[4]**
**(b)** Prepare a bank reconciliation statement at 31 August 2026. **[3]**

**5.** Brennan Fabrics provides the following for July 2026.

| Item | £ |
|---|---|
| Sales ledger balances 1 July: debit / credit | 27 340 / 190 |
| Credit sales | 48 760 |
| Cash sales | 6 300 |
| Receipts from credit customers | 44 815 |
| Discount allowed | 920 |
| Sales returns | 1 470 |
| Irrecoverable debts written off | 680 |
| Customer cheque dishonoured | 830 |
| Interest charged on overdue accounts | 145 |
| Contra with purchases ledger | 1 050 |
| Increase in provision for doubtful debts | 400 |
| Sales ledger credit balances 31 July | 260 |

Prepare the sales ledger control account for July 2026. **[6]**

**6.** Brennan Fabrics also provides the following for July 2026.

| Item | £ |
|---|---|
| Purchases ledger balances 1 July: credit / debit | 19 425 / 140 |
| Credit purchases | 31 680 |
| Cash purchases | 2 900 |
| Payments to credit suppliers | 28 960 |
| Discount received | 610 |
| Purchases returns | 845 |
| Contra with sales ledger | 1 050 |
| Interest charged by a supplier on an overdue account | 70 |
| Purchases ledger debit balances 31 July | 115 |

Prepare the purchases ledger control account for July 2026. **[5]**

**7.** The trial balance of Tarn Valley Deli at 30 September 2026 had debit totals of £95 189 and credit totals of £94 245. The difference went to a suspense account. Draft profit for the year was £28 640. These errors were found.

(i) The purchases journal was overcast by £300.
(ii) A receipt of £725 from R Haddad was entered in the cash book but not posted to R Haddad's account.
(iii) Rates of £1 090, correctly entered in the cash book, were posted to the rates account as £1 009.
(iv) The owner's private electricity bill of £360 was paid from the business bank account and debited to the electricity account.

**(a)** Prepare journal entries to correct the errors. Narratives are not required. **[4]**
**(b)** Prepare the suspense account. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the corrected profit for the year. **[3]**

**8.** For each error, state the effect on the profit for the year and on the statement of financial position.

**(a)** Closing inventory costing £14 600 was included in the accounts at £16 400. **[2]**
**(b)** Insurance of £420 paid in advance for next year was charged as an expense of this year. **[2]**
**(c)** A cheque for £950 from A Lund was credited to the account of A Lunt. **[2]**

**9.** The draft statement of financial position of Quarry Lane Florists, a sole trader, at 31 December 2025 showed: non-current assets (carrying amount) £46 500; inventory £8 240; trade receivables £6 900; bank £2 115; trade payables £5 380; accruals £410; net assets £57 965. Capital was £50 000 at 1 January 2025, plus profit £21 965, less drawings £14 000. The trial balance had agreed.

These errors were then found.

1. Goods costing £350 taken by the owner for private use had not been recorded.
2. An irrecoverable debt of £480 had not been written off.
3. Repairs to the delivery van, £1 100, had been debited to the motor vehicles account. Ignore depreciation.
4. A cheque of £640 paid to a supplier had been entered on the debit side of the cash book and the credit side of the supplier's account.
5. Electricity of £260 owing at the year end had not been accrued.

**(a)** Prepare a statement of corrected profit for the year. **[5]**
**(b)** Calculate the corrected figure for each line of the statement of financial position that changes, and show that the corrected statement balances. **[7]**

**10.** The owner of a small business says: "Our trial balance agrees, both control accounts agree with the ledgers and the bank reconciliation agrees, so our accounting records contain no errors." Evaluate this statement. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** -- a transposition (debit £1 420, credit £1 240) [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* B and D (both errors of commission) leave the totals equal; only the exact error type earns the mark.

**2. (a)** Dr Shop fittings £3 600; Cr Purchases £3 600 [1]. Narrative: purchase of shelving (a non-current asset) wrongly debited to purchases [1].
**(b)** Dr Revenue £180; Cr T Marsh £180 [1]. Narrative: credit sale entered in the sales journal as £1 420 instead of £1 240 [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A missing narrative loses its mark even when the double entry is right.

**3.** 540 ÷ 9 = 60, so the difference is divisible by 9 and a transposition in one posting is possible [1], for example a debit of £1 170 posted as £1 710 [1]. It could equally be an unequal posting, an addition error or a partial omission of a £540 credit [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Divisible by 9 is evidence, not proof; a mark is lost for saying the error must be a transposition.

**4. (a)** −1 380 − 245 − 38 (direct debit and interest) [1] + 960 (credit transfer) [1] − 36 (receipt overstated, 651 − 615) [1] = **overdraft £739** [1].
**(b)**

```
Lowther Cycles
Bank reconciliation statement at 31 August 2026
                                         £
Balance per bank statement (overdrawn) (1 079)
Add: outstanding lodgement              2 210
                                        1 131
Less: unpresented cheques              (1 870)
Balance per cash book (overdrawn)        (739)
```

Overdrawn balance shown negative [1]; lodgement added [1]; unpresented cheques deducted to reach **(739)** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Own-figure marks usually apply in (b) if a wrong (a) figure is used consistently.

**5.**

```
                Sales ledger control account
Balance b/d          27 340 | Balance b/d             190
Credit sales         48 760 | Bank                 44 815
Bank (dishonoured)      830 | Discount allowed        920
Interest charged        145 | Sales returns         1 470
Balance c/d             260 | Irrecoverable debts     680
                            | Purchases ledger
                            |   control (contra)    1 050
                            | Balance c/d          28 210
                     ------                        ------
                     77 335                        77 335
Balance b/d          28 210 | Balance b/d             260
```

Opening debit balance and credit sales [1]; dishonoured cheque and interest [1]; credit balance c/d on the debit side [1]; receipts, discount and returns [1]; irrecoverable debts and contra [1]; closing debit balance **£28 210** with cash sales and the provision excluded [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Including the £400 provision or £6 300 cash sales loses the closing balance mark.

**6.** Credit side: 19 425 + 31 680 + 70 interest + 115 debit balances c/d = 51 290 [1]. Debit side: 140 b/d + 28 960 payments [1] + 610 discount + 845 returns [1] + 1 050 contra [1]. Closing credit balance = 51 290 − 31 605 = **£19 685**, cash purchases excluded [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The £115 debit balances are carried down on the credit side; netting them against the credit balances loses the closing balance mark.

**7. (a)** (i) Dr Suspense 300, Cr Purchases 300 [1]. (ii) Dr Suspense 725, Cr R Haddad 725 [1]. (iii) Dr Rates 81, Cr Suspense 81 [1]. (iv) Dr Drawings 360, Cr Electricity 360 [1].
**(b)**

```
                       Suspense account
Purchases          300 | Balance b/d          944
R Haddad           725 | Rates                 81
                 -----                      -----
                 1 025                      1 025
```

Opening credit balance £944 (95 189 − 94 245) [1]; three correct postings [1]; account closes with totals of £1 025 [1].
**(c)** 28 640 + 300 (purchases overstated) [1] + 360 (private bill removed from expenses) [1] − 81 (rates understated) = **£29 219**; error (ii) has no effect on profit [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Putting principle error (iv) through suspense loses the (a) mark and the closing mark in (b).

**8. (a)** Profit overstated by £1 800 [1]; current assets (inventory) overstated by £1 800 [1].
**(b)** Profit understated by £420 [1]; current assets understated, as a £420 prepayment is missing [1].
**(c)** No effect on profit [1]; no effect on total trade receivables, though A Lund's and A Lunt's balances are both wrong [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Profit overstated" with no amount, or with no statement of financial position effect, earns at most one mark.

**9. (a)** Draft profit 21 965 + 350 goods for own use (purchases reduced) [1] − 480 irrecoverable debt [1] − 1 100 van repairs [1] − 260 electricity accrued [1] = **£20 475**; error 4 has no profit effect [1].
**(b)** Non-current assets 46 500 − 1 100 = £45 400 [1]; trade receivables 6 900 − 480 = £6 420 [1]; bank 2 115 − 1 280 = £835, as the reversal doubles the error [1]; trade payables 5 380 − 1 280 = £4 100 [1]; accruals 410 + 260 = £670 [1]; net current assets 15 495 − 4 770 = £10 725, so net assets £56 125 [1]; capital 50 000 + 20 475 − 14 350 drawings = **£56 125**, which agrees [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* The complete reversal changes bank and trade payables by twice £640; using £640 loses both marks but own-figure credit remains for the totals.

**10.** An agreed trial balance makes addition, partial omission, transposition and unequal posting errors unlikely [1]. It misses commission, complete reversal, compensating, omission, original entry and principle errors; capitalised repairs would overstate profit unnoticed [1]. Control accounts locate errors and deter fraud [1], but an invoice omitted from, or misstated in, a book of prime entry agrees in both [1]. The bank reconciliation checks only the bank column [1]. Conclusion: the checks reduce the risk of error but cannot prove there is none, so the claim is too strong [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" needs a judgement on the owner's claim; a list of points without a conclusion cannot reach full marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Posting commission, principle or complete reversal corrections through suspense.
- Correcting a complete reversal with the single amount.
- Including cash sales, cash purchases or the provision for doubtful debts in control accounts.
- Treating an overdraft as a positive balance in a bank reconciliation.
- Giving only the profit effect when the statement of financial position effect is also asked for.
- Not checking that corrected net assets equal corrected capital.
