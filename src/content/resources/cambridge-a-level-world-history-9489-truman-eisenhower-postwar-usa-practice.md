## Section A

**1.** Identify three Fair Deal proposals that Congress rejected. **[3]**

**2.** Explain two external developments in 1949-50 that increased fear of communism in the USA. **[4]**

**3.** Give three reasons for Truman's victory in 1948. **[3]**

**4.** Identify three actions Truman took on civil rights. **[3]**

**5.** Explain two features of Modern Republicanism. **[4]**

**6.** Identify three groups that did not share in 1950s prosperity. **[3]**

**7.** Give three features of the New Look in defence policy. **[3]**

**8.** Explain why the Berlin crisis of 1958-59 began. **[3]**

---

## Section B

**9.** "Truman dealt successfully with the domestic problems facing the USA after 1945." Assess this view. **[30]**

**10.** Evaluate the reasons for the decline of McCarthy's influence by 1954. **[30]**

**11.** "Civil rights campaigners achieved more than the federal government in the years 1945-61." Assess this view. **[30]**

**12.** Assess the significance of the Korean War for US foreign policy, 1950-61. **[30]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: national health insurance (1); federal aid to education (1); the Brannan Plan for farm incomes (1); repeal of the Taft-Hartley Act (1); civil rights legislation such as anti-lynching and anti-poll-tax laws (1). **[3]**

**2.** Two developments, 2 marks each: the Soviet atomic test in August 1949 ended the US monopoly and raised fears that spies had passed secrets, confirmed by the arrest of Klaus Fuchs in 1950 (2); the communist victory in China in October 1949 led Republicans to accuse Truman of having "lost" China (2); North Korea's invasion of the South in June 1950 seemed to prove that communism was expanding by force (2). **[4]**

**3.** Any three, 1 mark each: his energetic whistle-stop campaign (1); attacks on the "do-nothing" Republican 80th Congress, which he recalled in a special session (1); support from labour, farmers and African American voters in northern cities (1); his anti-communist record (Truman Doctrine, Berlin airlift) (1); Dewey's cautious and complacent campaign (1); the Dixiecrat and Progressive splits proved smaller than expected (1). **[3]**

**4.** Any three, 1 mark each: set up the President's Committee on Civil Rights (1946) (1); its report *To Secure These Rights* (1947) (1); addressed the NAACP in 1947 (1); sent a civil rights message to Congress (February 1948) (1); Executive Order 9981 on the armed forces (1948) (1); an executive order on fair employment in the federal service (1948) (1). **[3]**

**5.** Two features, 2 marks each: acceptance of New Deal programmes, shown by the extension of Social Security in 1954 and the creation of the Department of Health, Education and Welfare (2); fiscal restraint -- tax and spending control and balanced budgets in several years (2); support for infrastructure through the 1956 Highway Act rather than large welfare schemes (2). **[4]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: the rural poor in the South and Southwest (1); inner-city residents as jobs and middle-class families moved to suburbs (1); unskilled and non-unionised workers (1); African Americans and Hispanic Americans facing discrimination in pay and housing (1); women paid less than men (1); elderly people on small pensions (1). **[3]**

**7.** Any three, 1 mark each: reliance on nuclear weapons rather than large conventional forces (1); "massive retaliation" as the threat to deter attack (1); expansion of air power and missiles (1); cutting defence costs to protect the economy ("more bang for the buck") (1); greater use of CIA covert operations (1); alliances such as SEATO (1954) (1). **[3]**

**8.** In November 1958 Khrushchev demanded that the Western powers leave West Berlin within six months and that it become a "free city" (1); the flow of East Germans escaping through Berlin was damaging the GDR (1); Khrushchev also wanted Western recognition of East Germany and to stop West German access to nuclear weapons (1). **[3]**

**9.** Indicative content. **Successes:** reconversion without a return to depression; Employment Act (1946); Housing Act (1949), minimum wage increase and Social Security extension (1950); re-election in 1948 against the odds; executive action on civil rights. **Failures and limits:** inflation and strikes in 1946; Taft-Hartley passed over his veto; health insurance, education aid and the Brannan Plan defeated; civil rights legislation blocked; the Red Scare escalated, partly through his own Loyalty Program, and McCarthy undermined his administration. **Criterion:** measure success against Truman's own Fair Deal aims and against what was possible with a conservative coalition in Congress. **Judgement:** he protected and modestly extended the New Deal, but most ambitious reforms failed; partial success is a defensible verdict. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**10.** Indicative content. **McCarthy's own excesses:** attacks on the US Army in 1953-54 alienated moderates; the televised Army-McCarthy hearings (April-June 1954) exposed his bullying, and Joseph Welch's rebuke became famous. **Media:** Edward R. Murrow's *See It Now* broadcast (March 1954). **Politics:** with a Republican in the White House, attacks on the government now hurt Republicans; Eisenhower worked against him behind the scenes; the Senate censured him in December 1954. **Changing context:** the Korean armistice (1953), the death of Stalin and the execution of the Rosenbergs reduced the sense of emergency. **Judgement:** television and his own overreach were the immediate causes, but the change in party advantage after 1952 explains why fellow Republicans abandoned him. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**11.** Indicative content. **Campaigners:** the NAACP's legal strategy won *Brown v. Board* (1954); the Montgomery Bus Boycott (1955-56) and SCLC; the Greensboro sit-ins (1960) and SNCC; individuals such as Rosa Parks and Martin Luther King Jr. **Federal government:** Truman's executive orders on the armed forces and federal employment; the Supreme Court (part of the federal government) in *Brown* and *Browder v. Gayle* (1956); Eisenhower sent troops to Little Rock (1957); the Civil Rights Acts of 1957 and 1960. **Limits:** Southern resistance (the Southern Manifesto, 1956) blocked enforcement; the Acts did little for voting. **Criterion:** decide whether "federal government" includes the Supreme Court -- strong answers say so explicitly. **Judgement:** campaigners created the pressure and the cases; the courts gave the decisive legal rulings; Congress and the presidents did least. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**12.** Indicative content. **Significance:** turned containment into a global and military policy; triggered the rearmament proposed in NSC-68; committed the USA to defend Taiwan and to aid the French in Indochina; strengthened ties with Japan and led to alliances such as ANZUS (1951) and SEATO (1954); deepened the Red Scare at home; the costly stalemate encouraged Eisenhower's New Look and his reluctance to fight another land war in Asia, shown at Dien Bien Phu (1954). **Limits:** the shift towards containment in Europe (Truman Doctrine, Marshall Aid, NATO) came before Korea; later crises (Suez, Hungary, Berlin, Cuba) arose from other causes. **Judgement:** Korea was a turning point in the scale and geography of US commitments, though not in the basic aim of containment. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**
