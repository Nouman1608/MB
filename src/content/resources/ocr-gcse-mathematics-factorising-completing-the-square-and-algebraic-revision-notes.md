## Method boxes

### Factorising: order of attack

```
1. Take out any common factor
2. Two terms, minus sign, both squares?  -> (a − b)(a + b)
3. Three terms, x² coefficient 1?        -> two numbers: product c, sum b
4. Three terms, x² coefficient not 1?    -> split the middle term (Higher tier only)
5. Expand to check
```

### Splitting the middle term (Higher tier only)

```
8x² + 10x − 3
ac = −24. Product −24, sum +10: 12 and −2
8x² + 12x − 2x − 3
= 4x(2x + 3) − 1(2x + 3)
= (4x − 1)(2x + 3)
```

The brackets from the two pairs must be identical. If they are not, check the pair of numbers or the signs.

### Completing the square (Higher tier only)

```
1. Take out a from the x² and x terms only (if a is not 1)
2. Halve the x coefficient: this is p
3. Write a(x + p)² and subtract a × p²
4. Add the original constant and tidy up
```

Worked reminder: x² − 8x + 3.

```
Half of −8 is −4
x² − 8x + 3 = (x − 4)² − 16 + 3 = (x − 4)² − 13
```

Try 5x² + 20x + 1 yourself. The answer is in the self-test.

### Algebraic fractions (Higher tier only)

```
Simplify:     factorise top and bottom, cancel common FACTORS
Multiply:     factorise, multiply tops, multiply bottoms, cancel
Divide:       flip the second fraction, then multiply
Add/subtract: common denominator, combine numerators, simplify
```

Worked reminder: simplify (x² + x − 12)/(x² − 16).

```
Numerator:   (x + 4)(x − 3)
Denominator: (x − 4)(x + 4)
Cancel (x + 4):  (x − 3)/(x − 4)
```

Worked reminder: 1/x + 1/(x + 4).

```
= [(x + 4) + x] / [x(x + 4)] = (2x + 4)/[x(x + 4)]
```

You may leave the denominator factorised. Do not expand it unless asked.

## Must-know distinctions

| Idea | Take care |
|---|---|
| Factor and term | You cancel factors (things multiplied) never terms (things added) |
| x² − 25 and x² + 25 | The first factorises. The second does not |
| Expand and factorise | They undo each other. Use one to check the other |
| 4x² − 9 and 2x² − 18 | Perfect squares 2x and 3 in the first. The second needs a common factor 2 first |
| (x + 3)² − 4 and x² + 6x + 5 | These are equal. Completing the square changes the form, not the value |
| Simplify and write as a single fraction | Simplify means cancel. Single fraction means combine |

## Quick worked checks

**Common factors.** 15xy² + 10x²y has HCF 5xy. Answer: 5xy(3y + 2x).

**Difference of two squares with surds.** x² − 5 = (x − √5)(x + √5).

**Completing the square with a fraction.** x² + 5x + 4 = (x + 5/2)² − 25/4 + 4 = (x + 5/2)² − 9/4.

**Check a completed square.** Expand (x + 5/2)² − 9/4: x² + 5x + 25/4 − 9/4 = x² + 5x + 4. Correct.

## Command words and what they ask for

| Wording | What to give |
|---|---|
| Factorise | Brackets with no common factor left over, even if the question does not say "fully" |
| Factorise fully | The same. Check every bracket for a hidden common factor |
| Simplify (algebraic fraction) | One fraction with no common factor in top and bottom |
| Write as a single fraction | One fraction. Cancelling afterwards is a good habit |
| Write in the form (x + p)² + q | Find the numbers p and q |
| Write in the form a(x + p)² + q | Find the numbers a, p and q. Here a is the original x² coefficient |

## Number pairs worth knowing

| Product | Pairs (positive) |
|---|---|
| 12 | 1 and 12, 2 and 6, 3 and 4 |
| 16 | 1 and 16, 2 and 8, 4 and 4 |
| 18 | 1 and 18, 2 and 9, 3 and 6 |
| 24 | 1 and 24, 2 and 12, 3 and 8, 4 and 6 |
| 36 | 1 and 36, 2 and 18, 3 and 12, 4 and 9, 6 and 6 |

For a negative product, use the same pairs and give one of them a minus sign. Then pick the pair whose sum is b.

Squares to recognise: the squares of 2 up to 12, that is 4 up to 144. In algebra, x², 4x², 9x² and 25x² are squares of x, 2x, 3x and 5x.

## Links between the skills

- **Completing the square gives a check on a quadratic.** When you expand (x + p)² + q, the middle term is 2p and the constant is p² + q. Use this to check your values.
- **Difference of two squares appears inside fractions.** A denominator such as x² − 16 usually signals that a numerator or another denominator will cancel with (x − 4) or (x + 4).
- **Common factors come first everywhere.** In a fraction, take common factors out of the top and bottom before searching for anything else.
- **A quadratic that will not factorise is not a mistake.** For example x² + 2x + 5 has no pair of whole numbers with product 5 and sum 2. Completing the square still works: (x + 1)² + 4.

## Extra worked reminders

**Higher tier only: fraction with a common factor to remove first.** Simplify (4x² − 16)/(8x + 16).

```
Numerator:   4(x² − 4) = 4(x − 2)(x + 2)
Denominator: 8(x + 2)
Cancel 4 and (x + 2):  (x − 2)/2
```

**Higher tier only: subtracting when one denominator is a difference of two squares.** Write 4/(x − 3) − 6/(x² − 9) as a single fraction.

```
Common denominator (x − 3)(x + 3)
4(x + 3) − 6 = 4x + 12 − 6 = 4x + 6
Answer: (4x + 6)/[(x − 3)(x + 3)] = 2(2x + 3)/[(x − 3)(x + 3)]
```

**Both tiers: three steps in one question.** Factorise fully 3x² − 27. Take out 3: 3(x² − 9). Difference of two squares: 3(x − 3)(x + 3).

### Answers

1. 3(3a − 4b)
2. 2x(5x + 2)
3. (x + 6)(x − 5)
4. (x − 8)(x + 8)
5. (2m − 1)(2m + 1)
6. (3x + 4)(x + 2). Using ac = 24, numbers 6 and 4: 3x² + 6x + 4x + 8 = 3x(x + 2) + 4(x + 2).
7. (x − 3)² − 8
8. 2(x + 2)² − 11. Working: 2(x² + 4x) − 3 = 2[(x + 2)² − 4] − 3 = 2(x + 2)² − 11.
9. x/(x − 3). Working: x(x + 3) / [(x − 3)(x + 3)].
10. (7 − x)/[(x − 1)(x + 2)]. Working: numerator 2(x + 2) − 3(x − 1) = 2x + 4 − 3x + 3 = 7 − x.
11. 5(x + 2)² − 19. Working: 5(x² + 4x) + 1 = 5[(x + 2)² − 4] + 1 = 5(x + 2)² − 19.
12. (5x − 4)(x + 1). Using ac = −20, numbers 5 and −4: 5x² + 5x − 4x − 4 = 5x(x + 1) − 4(x + 1).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stopping early: a common factor still sits inside a bracket, so the answer is not fully factorised.
- Choosing a number pair that adds to the right value but multiplies to the wrong one. Always test both.
- Sign slips with a negative c. The pair 3 and −8 gives −5, not +5.
- Treating x² + 16 as a difference of two squares.
- Missing a common factor before using the difference of two squares, as in 2x² − 18.
- Halving the wrong number when completing the square. You halve the coefficient of x, after taking out a.
- Multiplying the subtracted p² by a in the form a(x + p)² + q. Forgetting leaves the wrong constant.
- Cancelling a term, such as the x in (x + 3)/x, rather than a factor.
- Subtracting fractions and changing only the first sign of the numerator, not both terms of the bracket.
- Showing no working. Visible working lets you and the reader follow your reasoning, so write the factorised numerator and denominator before cancelling.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do you factorise x² + bx + c?**
Find two numbers whose product is c and whose sum is b, then write x² + bx + c = (x + m)(x + n). For example, x² + 10x + 21 = (x + 3)(x + 7), because 3 × 7 = 21 and 3 + 7 = 10.

**Does x² + 25 factorise as a difference of two squares?**
No. The difference of two squares, a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b), needs a minus sign between the two squares. A sum of squares such as x² + 25 does not factorise this way.

**How do you split the middle term to factorise ax² + bx + c?**
Multiply a by c, find two numbers that multiply to ac and add to b, and rewrite bx as two x-terms using them. Then factorise in two pairs; the two brackets must match, and this method is Higher tier only.

**What is the formula for completing the square?**
x² + bx + c = (x + b/2)² + c − (b/2)². Halve the coefficient of x to get p, subtract p², then add the original constant.
