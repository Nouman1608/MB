## Section A

**1.** Which statement correctly defines a species?

A: a group of organisms that live in the same place

B: a group of organisms that look the same

C: a group of organisms that can reproduce to produce fertile offspring

D: a group of organisms that can mate with each other

**[1]**

**2.** Which is the correct way to write the scientific name of the domestic cat?

A: felis catus

B: Felis catus

C: Felis Catus

D: FELIS CATUS

**[1]**

**3.** State **two** reasons why biologists classify living organisms. **[2]**

**4.** A horse and a donkey can mate and produce a mule, which is sterile. Explain why the horse and the donkey are classified as different species. **[2]**

**5.** Describe the binomial system of naming species. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** A student has four invertebrate animals: a ladybird (six legs, wings), a spider (eight legs, no wings), a millipede (many legs, no wings, a long body) and a snail (no legs, a shell).

**(a)** Construct a dichotomous key to identify the four animals. Use only features given in the description. **[4]**

**(b)** Use your key to write down the path you follow to identify the spider. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest why body length would be a poor feature to use in a key. **[2]**

**7.** The table shows scientific names of four organisms.

| Organism | Scientific name |
|---|---|
| P | *Panthera leo* |
| Q | *Panthera tigris* |
| R | *Felis catus* |
| S | *Canis lupus* |

**(a)** Identify the genus of organism P. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify which **two** organisms are most closely related, and give a reason. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why scientists use scientific names instead of local names. **[2]**

**8.** Two types of plant, X and Y, were grown in the same field. They look different, but when pollen from X was placed on flowers of Y the plants produced seeds that grew into healthy plants. Those plants later produced seeds of their own.

**(a)** State whether X and Y are the same species. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain your answer to (a). **[2]**

**9.** Explain why a key that uses the statement "tall plant" and "short plant" may give wrong answers. **[2]**

**10.** The dog rose belongs to the genus *Rosa* and the species *canina*. Write its scientific name correctly and state **two** rules for how a scientific name is written. **[3]**

---

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Any two of: it makes the huge variety of organisms easier to study [1]; it makes identification easier [1]; it gives scientists a shared way to refer to organisms [1]; it helps predict the features of new organisms placed in a group [1]. **[2]**

**4.** They can breed to produce offspring [1], but the offspring (the mule) is sterile, so a horse and a donkey do not produce fertile offspring and are not one species [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Each species has a scientific name made up of two parts [1]. The first part is the genus and the second part is the species [1]. The system is agreed internationally, so the same name is used by scientists in every country [1]. **[3]**

**6.**

**(a)** One mark for each correct step that separates the animals, using observable features, for example: 1a legs present, go to 2; 1b legs absent, snail [1]. 2a six legs, ladybird [1]. 2b more than six legs, go to 3 [1]. 3a eight legs, spider; 3b many more than eight legs, millipede [1]. Accept any valid order, provided each pair is a true opposite and each animal ends at one name.

**(b)** Legs present (1a), then more than six legs (2b), then eight legs (3a) [1].

**(c)** Length varies between young and adult individuals of the same species [1], so one animal could fit either statement, or the statement is not clear and fixed [1]. **[7]**

**7.**

**(a)** *Panthera* [1].

**(b)** P and Q (lion and tiger) [1], because they belong to the same genus [1].

**(c)** Local names differ between languages and places, and the same name can refer to different organisms [1]. A scientific name is the same everywhere and so avoids confusion [1]. **[5]**

**8.**

**(a)** Yes, they are the same species [1].

**(b)** They can reproduce [1] and their offspring are fertile, because the new plants produced seeds [1]. Differences in appearance do not matter. **[3]**

**9.** The words "tall" and "short" are relative and not fixed [1]. A plant's height changes as it grows or with its conditions, so one plant could fit both statements or be placed wrongly [1]. **[2]**

**10.** *Rosa canina* [1]. Two rules from: genus begins with a capital letter [1]; species begins with a lower-case letter [1]; printed in italics, or each word underlined if handwritten [1]. **[3]**

---

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out **fertile** in the species definition.
- Writing a genus without a capital letter or a species with one.
- Using size, colour or height in a key.
- Giving a pair of statements that are not opposites.
- Not stating the path through the key when asked.
