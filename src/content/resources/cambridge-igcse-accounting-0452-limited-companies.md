## What 5.3 covers

Candidates should understand: how a limited company can be a trading, service or
manufacturing business, or a combination; the advantages and disadvantages of
operating as a limited company; the terms "limited liability" and "equity"; the
capital structure of preference share capital, ordinary share capital, general
reserve and retained earnings; the features of ordinary share capital and loan
capital (debentures); share capital that is issued, called-up and paid-up; how to
prepare statements of profit or loss, statements of changes in equity and
statements of financial position; and adjustments as in 5.1.

## Types of company business

Like a sole trader, a company may be a **trading** business (a food wholesaler), a
**service** business (an IT support company), a **manufacturing** business (a
furniture maker) or a **combination** (a bakery that makes bread, buys in drinks
to resell and runs a catering service). The type decides the top of the statement
of profit or loss: a trading section for goods bought for resale, a manufacturing
account for goods made, and service income added below gross profit. The type of
business does not change the equity section, which is the same for every company.

## Limited liability and equity

**Limited liability** means a shareholder can lose no more than the amount they
have paid, or agreed to pay, for their shares. If the company cannot pay its
debts, creditors cannot claim the shareholders' personal possessions. The company
is a separate legal person: it owns its assets and owes its debts.

**Equity** is the shareholders' interest in the company: total assets less total
liabilities. It equals share capital plus reserves.

## Operating as a limited company

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Limited liability encourages people to invest | Profits are shared among many shareholders as dividends |
| Large amounts of capital can be raised by issuing shares | More legal formalities and costs to set up and run |
| The company continues when shareholders change | Financial statements must be made available to others |
| Easier to borrow because of its size and continuity | Shareholders who are not directors have little day-to-day control |

## The capital structure

| Part | Features |
|---|---|
| **Preference share capital** | Fixed rate of dividend (for example 6%), paid before any ordinary dividend; holders usually cannot vote |
| **Ordinary share capital** | Dividend varies with profit and the directors' decision and may be nil; holders vote and carry the most risk |
| **General reserve** | Profit transferred out of retained earnings and kept in the business, often for future expansion |
| **Retained earnings** | Accumulated profits not yet paid out as dividends or transferred to reserves |

**Loan capital (debentures)** is not part of equity. Debenture holders are lenders:
they receive fixed interest, which must be paid whether or not there is a profit,
and their loan is repaid on an agreed date. Debenture interest is a finance cost
in the statement of profit or loss; dividends are not, they are shown in the
statement of changes in equity.

## Issued, called-up and paid-up

Gatwood Ltd has issued 400,000 ordinary shares of $0.25 each. It has asked
shareholders for $0.20 a share so far. Holders of 10,000 shares have not yet
paid the amount called.

```
Issued share capital      400,000 × 0.25            = 100,000
Called-up share capital   400,000 × 0.20            =  80,000
Calls unpaid               10,000 × 0.20            =   2,000
Paid-up share capital     80,000 − 2,000            =  78,000
```

- **Issued**: the nominal value of shares actually given to shareholders.
- **Called-up**: the part of the nominal value the company has asked for.
- **Paid-up**: the part of the called-up amount actually received.

## Worked example: Fenwick Foods Ltd

At 1 January 2028 Fenwick Foods Ltd's equity was: ordinary shares of $1 each
200,000; 6% preference shares of $1 each 50,000; general reserve 30,000; retained
earnings 41,500. It also had 60,000 of 8% debentures, repayable in 2035.

During 2028:

1. On 1 July, 50,000 ordinary shares of $1 were issued at par, fully paid in cash.
2. Profit from operations was 78,000. Debenture interest of 2,400 was paid; the
   rest of the year's interest was unpaid at 31 December.
3. The preference dividend for the year was paid.
4. An ordinary dividend of $0.10 per share was paid in December on all ordinary
   shares then in issue.
5. The directors transferred 15,000 to the general reserve.

**Statement of profit or loss extract**

```
Profit from operations                                78,000
Finance costs: debenture interest (60,000 × 8%)       (4,800)
Profit for the year                                   73,200
```

The unpaid 2,400 is an accrual: a current liability in other payables.

**Statement of changes in equity**

```
Fenwick Foods Ltd: Statement of changes in equity for the year ended 31 December 2028
                          Ordinary   Preference   General   Retained      Total
                          shares     shares       reserve   earnings
Balance at 1 Jan 2028     200,000      50,000      30,000     41,500    321,500
Shares issued              50,000                                        50,000
Profit for the year                                           73,200     73,200
Dividends paid:
   preference                                                 (3,000)    (3,000)
   ordinary                                                  (25,000)   (25,000)
Transfer to general reserve                        15,000    (15,000)         –
Balance at 31 Dec 2028    250,000      50,000      45,000     71,700    416,700
```

Workings: preference dividend 50,000 × 6% = 3,000; ordinary dividend 250,000 × 0.10
= 25,000, because the new shares were in issue when the dividend was paid. The
transfer moves profit between two reserves, so total equity is unchanged by it.

**Statement of financial position extract at 31 December 2028**

```
Equity
   Ordinary shares of $1 each              250,000
   6% preference shares of $1 each          50,000
   General reserve                          45,000
   Retained earnings                        71,700
                                           416,700
Non-current liabilities
   8% debentures (2035)                     60,000
Current liabilities include
   Other payables: debenture interest        2,400
```

## The 5.1 adjustments in a company

Accruals, prepayments, depreciation, irrecoverable debts and the allowance are
made exactly as for a sole trader. Two differences: a company has **no drawings**
(shareholders receive dividends instead), and directors' fees and salaries are
expenses because directors are employed by the company.

## Common mistakes

- Showing dividends in the statement of profit or loss.
- Showing debenture interest in the statement of changes in equity.
- Leaving accrued debenture interest out when only part has been paid.
- Calculating the ordinary dividend on the opening number of shares when new
  shares were issued before payment.
- Treating a transfer to general reserve as a reduction in equity.
- Adding debentures to equity.
- Mixing up called-up and paid-up capital.

## Exam technique

- **Define** "limited liability" precisely: shareholders' loss is limited to the
  amount paid or agreed to be paid for their shares.
- **Compare** ordinary shares and debentures point by point: ownership, return,
  risk, repayment, where the return is shown.
- **Prepare** a statement of changes in equity in columns, one per component of
  equity, with a total column that you check against the sum of the others.
