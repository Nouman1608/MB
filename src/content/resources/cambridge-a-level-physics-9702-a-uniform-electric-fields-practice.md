Take e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, mass of electron = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg, mass of proton = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg and g = 9.81 m s⁻². Ignore edge effects.

## Section A

**1.** Two parallel plates are 2.5 cm apart and have a potential difference of 1500 V between them. Calculate the electric field strength between the plates. **[2]**

**2.** Show that the unit V m⁻¹ is equivalent to N C⁻¹. **[2]**

**3.** The p.d. between two parallel plates is doubled and their separation is halved. State and explain the factor by which the field strength changes. **[2]**

**4.** Describe the electric field lines between, and at the edges of, two oppositely charged parallel plates. **[2]**

**5.** An electron is accelerated from rest through a potential difference of 800 V. Calculate its final kinetic energy and its speed. **[3]**

**6.** A proton enters a uniform electric field travelling in the same direction as the field lines. Describe and explain its subsequent motion while it remains in the field. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** An electron travelling at 3.0 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹ enters the region midway between two horizontal parallel plates, moving parallel to them. The plates are 0.050 m long and 0.015 m apart, and the p.d. between them is 240 V.

**(a)** Calculate the electric field strength. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the acceleration of the electron. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the time the electron spends between the plates. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the vertical displacement of the electron as it leaves the plates, and show that it does not strike a plate. **[2]**

**(e)** Calculate the angle between the electron's path and its original direction as it leaves the plates. **[2]**

**8.** A proton moving at 6.0 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹ passes through a small hole in one plate into a uniform field of strength 3.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹. The field opposes the motion of the proton.

**(a)** Calculate the kinetic energy of the proton. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the force on the proton. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the distance the proton travels before it stops. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the minimum p.d. between the plates that would prevent the proton reaching the second plate. **[2]**

**9.** An electron and a proton enter the same uniform field at the same speed, both moving at right angles to the field. Compare their paths, giving the ratio of their sideways accelerations. **[3]**

**10.** A small charged ball of mass 0.50 g hangs from an insulating thread between two vertical parallel plates 6.0 cm apart. The ball carries a charge of +4.0 × 10⁻⁸ C, and a p.d. of 3000 V is applied across the plates. Calculate the angle the thread makes with the vertical. **[4]**

**11.** An electron beam passes between deflecting plates and then travels on to a screen.

**(a)** Explain why the beam follows a straight path after it leaves the plates. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the weight of the electrons can be ignored in calculating their deflection. **[2]**

**12.** The lower of two horizontal parallel plates is at 0 V and the upper plate is at +600 V. The plates are 40 mm apart.

**(a)** Calculate the potential at a point 10 mm above the lower plate. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the electric field strength and state its direction. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the work done in moving a charge of +2.0 μC from the lower plate to the point in (a). **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** E = V ÷ d = 1500 ÷ 0.025 [1] = **6.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹** [1].

**2.** V = J C⁻¹, so V m⁻¹ = J C⁻¹ m⁻¹ [1]; J = N m, so this is N m C⁻¹ m⁻¹ = **N C⁻¹** [1].

**3.** E = V/d, so doubling V doubles E and halving d doubles E again [1]: E increases by a factor of **4** [1].

**4.** Between the plates: straight, parallel, **evenly spaced** lines perpendicular to the plates, pointing from the positive to the negative plate [1]. At the edges: lines **bow outwards** and are further apart (the field is weaker and non-uniform) [1].

**5.** E_k = eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 800 = **1.28 × 10⁻¹⁶ J** [1]. v = √(2E_k ÷ m) = √(2 × 1.28 × 10⁻¹⁶ ÷ 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) [1] = **1.7 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹** [1].

**6.** The force on the proton is constant and in the **direction of its motion** [1], so it **accelerates uniformly** in a straight line, speeding up [1].

**7. (a)** E = 240 ÷ 0.015 = **1.6 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** F = eE = 2.56 × 10⁻¹⁵ N [1]; a = F ÷ m = **2.8 × 10¹⁵ m s⁻²** [1].

**(c)** t = 0.050 ÷ 3.0 × 10⁷ = **1.67 × 10⁻⁹ s** [1].

**(d)** y = ½at² = 0.5 × 2.81 × 10¹⁵ × (1.67 × 10⁻⁹)² = **3.9 × 10⁻³ m** [1]; this is less than the 7.5 mm from the centre line to a plate, so it emerges [1].

**(e)** v_y = at = 2.81 × 10¹⁵ × 1.67 × 10⁻⁹ = 4.7 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹ [1]; tan θ = 4.7 × 10⁶ ÷ 3.0 × 10⁷, so θ = **8.9°** [1].

**8. (a)** E_k = ½ × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ × (6.0 × 10⁵)² = **3.0 × 10⁻¹⁶ J** [1].

**(b)** F = eE = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 3.0 × 10⁴ = **4.8 × 10⁻¹⁵ N** [1].

**(c)** Work done by the field = E_k: F × s = 3.0 × 10⁻¹⁶ [1]; s = 3.0 × 10⁻¹⁶ ÷ 4.8 × 10⁻¹⁵ = **0.063 m** [1].

**(d)** eV = E_k [1]; V = 3.0 × 10⁻¹⁶ ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **1.9 × 10³ V** [1].

**9.** Both follow **parabolic** paths [1], deflected in **opposite directions** (electron towards the positive plate, proton towards the negative plate) [1]. The forces are equal in size, so a_e ÷ a_p = m_p ÷ m_e = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ ÷ 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ = **1.8 × 10³**: the electron's path curves much more sharply [1].

**10.** E = 3000 ÷ 0.060 = 5.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹ [1]. Electric force = qE = 4.0 × 10⁻⁸ × 5.0 × 10⁴ = 2.0 × 10⁻³ N (horizontal) [1]. Weight = 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ × 9.81 = 4.9 × 10⁻³ N [1]. tan θ = 2.0 × 10⁻³ ÷ 4.9 × 10⁻³ = 0.41, so θ = **22°** [1].

**11. (a)** Outside the plates there is **no electric field and so no force**, so the electrons move at constant velocity [1].

**(b)** The weight of an electron is about 9 × 10⁻³⁰ N [1], many orders of magnitude smaller than the electric force (about 10⁻¹⁵ N in typical fields), so its effect on the path is negligible [1].

**12. (a)** Potential changes linearly: 600 × (10 ÷ 40) = **150 V** [1].

**(b)** E = 600 ÷ 0.040 = **1.5 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹** [1], directed **downwards** (from the +600 V plate to the 0 V plate) [1].

**(c)** W = qΔV = 2.0 × 10⁻⁶ × 150 = **3.0 × 10⁻⁴ J** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using cm or mm for the plate separation.
- Using the length of the plates in E = V/d.
- Stating that the horizontal velocity changes between the plates.
- Calling the path a circle, or curving it after the particle leaves the plates.
- Deflecting the electron towards the negative plate.
- Mixing up the electric force and the weight when a charged ball hangs between plates.
