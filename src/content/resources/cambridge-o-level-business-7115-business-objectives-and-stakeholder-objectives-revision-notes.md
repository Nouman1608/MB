Condensed recall for subtopic **1.5 Business objectives and stakeholder objectives**. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [Business objectives and stakeholder objectives study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-business-objectives-and-stakeholder-objectives/).

## Why objectives are needed

Objectives are targets that:

- give direction to everyone in the business
- guide decisions
- let managers measure performance
- motivate employees
- help departments coordinate.

## The four named objectives

| Objective | Typical when… | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| Survival | The business is new, faces a new rival or a downturn | Still trading, enough cash to pay bills |
| Growth | The business is established and wants to expand | Rise in sales revenue, outlets or employees |
| Profit | Owners want a return, or investors must be attracted | Profit compared with previous years |
| Market share | Competition is strong and position matters | Business sales ÷ market sales × 100 |

```
market share (%) = business sales ÷ total market sales × 100
growth in sales (%) = (new sales − old sales) ÷ old sales × 100
```

Sales can grow while market share falls, if the whole market grows faster.

## Why priorities change

New business → survival. Established → growth, profit or market share. New competitor or recession → survival or defending share. Seeking finance → profit. Public criticism → social and environmental aims.

## Social enterprises

- Main purpose: a social or environmental need.
- Objectives: **social** (jobs, services for the community), **environmental** (recycling, less pollution), **financial** (enough profit to survive and reinvest).
- Profit is a means to the purpose, not the main aim.

## Stakeholders

| Internal | Objective | External | Objective |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owners/shareholders | Profit, dividends | Customers | Quality, low price, service |
| Managers | Salary, status, growth | Suppliers | Regular orders, prompt payment |
| Employees | Pay, security, conditions | Lenders | Repayment with interest |
| | | Government | Taxes, jobs, legal compliance |
| | | Local community | Jobs, low pollution |

## Typical conflicts

- **Shareholders v employees**: higher wages reduce profit.
- **Shareholders v customers**: higher prices raise profit but hurt customers.
- **Business growth v local community**: a new factory brings jobs but also traffic and noise.
- **Managers v shareholders**: managers may want growth and high salaries; shareholders want dividends.
- **Suppliers v business**: the business wants low prices and long payment times; suppliers want the opposite.

Some objectives **align**: better quality can satisfy customers and raise profit.

**Four steps for a conflict answer:**

1. State the decision from the case (for example, closing a branch).
2. Name the group that gains and say what it gains.
3. Name the group that loses and say what it loses.
4. Judge whose objective the business is likely to put first, and how it could reduce the conflict (compensation, consultation, retraining).

## Private versus public sector objectives

- **Private**: profit, survival, growth, market share; answerable to owners.
- **Public**: service to the public, affordable or free provision, serving all areas, keeping within budget; answerable to government.
- Differences are of priority: public enterprises may still be asked to break even.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Business objective | A target a business aims to achieve |
| Survival | Continuing to trade |
| Growth | Increasing the size of the business |
| Market share | A business's sales as a percentage of total market sales |
| Social enterprise | A business whose main purpose is social or environmental, using profit to support it |
| Stakeholder | A person or group with an interest in a business's activities |
| Internal stakeholder | A stakeholder within the business: owners, managers, employees |
| External stakeholder | A stakeholder outside the business: customers, suppliers, lenders, government, community |

## Exam traps

- **One business, several objectives**; priorities shift.
- **Market share needs the market total.**
- **Shareholders are internal stakeholders**; customers are external.
- **Conflicts need a reason**: say what the business does and why one group gains and the other loses.
- **Social enterprises still need profit** to survive.
- **"Public sector" does not mean a public limited company.**

## Self-test

1. Give two reasons why a business needs objectives.
2. Why is survival usually the main objective of a new business?
3. A firm's sales are $90,000 in a market worth $600,000. Calculate its market share.
4. Its sales rise to $99,000. Calculate the percentage growth in sales.
5. The market grows to $750,000. What has happened to its market share?
6. State the three types of objective of a social enterprise.
7. Identify two internal and two external stakeholders.
8. State one objective of a bank that lends to a business.
9. Explain one conflict between customers and shareholders.
10. Give one example where stakeholder objectives align.
11. State one difference between private and public sector objectives.
12. Why might a business make profit its main objective before applying for a loan?

## Answers

1. Any two: direction; guide decisions; measure performance; motivate staff; coordinate departments.
2. It has few customers and limited cash; it must stay in business before it can grow or profit.
3. 90,000 ÷ 600,000 × 100 = **15%**.
4. (99,000 − 90,000) ÷ 90,000 × 100 = **10%**.
5. 99,000 ÷ 750,000 × 100 = 13.2%, so its share has **fallen**.
6. Social, environmental and financial.
7. Internal: any two of owners/shareholders, managers, employees. External: any two of customers, suppliers, lenders, government, local community.
8. To have the loan repaid on time with interest.
9. Shareholders want higher profit, which may mean higher prices; customers want lower prices.
10. Any one, for example better quality pleases customers and raises sales and profit for shareholders.
11. Private enterprises usually aim for profit; public enterprises aim mainly to provide a service to the public.
12. Lenders want evidence the business can repay, and profit shows it earns more than it spends.
