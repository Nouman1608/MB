## Questions

**1.** State two of the homeostatic variables in humans named in the guide, other than body temperature. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why negative feedback rather than positive feedback is used to control body temperature. **[3]**

**3.** Describe how the body responds to a rise in blood glucose concentration after a meal. **[5]**

**4.** A glucose drink was given to a person without diabetes (control) and to a patient. Blood glucose (mmol L⁻¹) was measured:

| Time / min | 0 | 30 | 60 | 120 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Control | 4.6 | 7.1 | 5.4 | 4.7 |
| Patient | 7.2 | 10.8 | 11.5 | 9.9 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in the patient's blood glucose from 0 to 60 minutes. **[2]**
**(b)** Compare the responses of the two people. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest one reason why the patient's glucose stays high. **[1]**

**5.** Compare the physiological changes, risk factors and treatment of type 1 and type 2 diabetes. **[5]**

**6.** Explain how the body responds to a fall in environmental temperature, including the role of thyroxin. **[7]**

**7.** Outline the responses that help a person lose heat on a hot day. **[4]**

**8.** (HL only) A kidney forms filtrate at 120 mL min⁻¹ and produces urine at 1.2 mL min⁻¹.
**(a)** Distinguish between excretion and osmoregulation. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the volume of filtrate formed per day in litres, and the percentage of filtrate that is reabsorbed. **[3]**

**9.** (HL only) Concentrations (g L⁻¹) in three fluids were:

| Substance | Plasma | Filtrate | Urine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glucose | 1.0 | 1.0 | 0.0 |
| Protein | 70.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| Urea | 0.30 | 0.30 | 21.0 |

**(a)** Explain why protein is absent from the filtrate. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why glucose is absent from the urine. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the factor by which the urea concentration rises from plasma to urine. **[1]**
**(d)** Urea and glucose have the same concentration in plasma and filtrate. Explain what this shows about ultrafiltration. **[2]**

**10.** (HL only) Explain the role of the loop of Henle in allowing the production of concentrated urine. **[4]**

**11.** (HL only) A person drinks a large volume of water after a long period without drinking. Explain the changes that follow in the control of blood osmotic concentration, and the effect on urine. **[8]**

**12.** (HL only) At rest, cardiac output is 5.5 L min⁻¹ and in vigorous exercise it is 22 L min⁻¹. Illustrative data on the share of cardiac output received by organs:

| Organ | Rest / % | Exercise / % |
|---|---|---|
| Skeletal muscle | 21 | 80 |
| Gut | 24 | 4 |
| Brain | 13 | 3.5 |
| Kidneys | 20 | 4 |

**(a)** Calculate the blood flow to skeletal muscle in exercise, in L min⁻¹. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in blood flow to the gut between rest and exercise. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the change in flow to the muscle is brought about and why it is useful. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Blood pH [1]; blood glucose concentration [1] (blood osmotic concentration is also accepted). **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the variable is enough; adding a value is not needed, and "water" or "sugar" alone is too vague to be safe.

**2.** Negative feedback reverses a change, so a rise in temperature causes cooling responses [1] and a fall causes warming responses, returning the temperature to the set point from either side [1]. Positive feedback would amplify a change and drive the temperature further from the set point [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The strongest answers contrast the two directly. Describing only what negative feedback does leaves the "rather than" part of the question unanswered.

**3.** The beta cells of the pancreas detect the high glucose [1] and secrete insulin into the blood [1]. Insulin is carried in the plasma to target cells in the liver, muscle and fat [1], which take up glucose [1]. The liver and muscle convert glucose to glycogen; blood glucose falls, so insulin secretion falls (negative feedback) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the cells and the target tissues. "The pancreas makes a hormone" is a description of the organ, not of the mechanism.

**4. (a)** Increase = 11.5 − 7.2 = 4.3 [1]. 4.3 / 7.2 × 100 = **59.7%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The patient has a higher starting glucose (7.2 against 4.6) and higher values throughout [1]. The control returns almost to its starting value by 120 min (4.7), but the patient is still 2.7 above start (9.9) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Target cells take up little glucose because they respond poorly to insulin, or too little insulin is secreted [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A percentage change needs the working shown; divide by the starting value, not the final one. A comparison needs figures from both rows.

**5.** Type 1: beta cells are destroyed, so little or no insulin is made [1]. Type 2: target cells respond poorly to insulin [1]. Risk factors: type 1 has a genetic predisposition and is not linked to lifestyle; type 2 is linked to excess body fat, inactivity, diet, age or family history [1]. Treatment: type 1 needs insulin by injection or pump [1]; type 2 may be managed with diet, exercise or medicines, with insulin in some cases [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A "compare" answer must be two-sided in each point. Giving type 1 facts in one paragraph and type 2 facts in another, without stating the contrast, loses the comparison.

**6.** Peripheral thermoreceptors in the skin detect the fall [1]. They send signals to the hypothalamus, which also monitors blood temperature [1]. The hypothalamus signals the pituitary gland, which stimulates the thyroid to release thyroxin [1]. Thyroxin raises metabolic rate, so more heat is released [1]. Skeletal muscle shivers, releasing heat from respiration [1]. Brown adipose tissue releases heat by uncoupled respiration, and skin arterioles constrict to reduce heat loss [1]. As temperature rises towards the set point, the responses weaken: negative feedback [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Cover receptor, control centre and effectors in sequence. Naming thyroxin without saying what it does to metabolic rate leaves the answer incomplete.

**7.** Skin arterioles dilate (vasodilation) [1], so extra blood passes close to the skin surface and heat is radiated to the surroundings [1]. Sweat glands secrete sweat [1], and evaporation of sweat removes heat from the skin [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each response needs its effect stated. "Sweating" alone is a name, not an explanation.

**8. (a)** Excretion is the removal of metabolic waste from the body [1]. Osmoregulation is the regulation of osmotic concentration [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Filtrate = 120 × 1440 = 172 800 mL = **172.8 L** per day [1]. Reabsorbed = (120 − 1.2) / 120 × 100 = **99.0%** [1]. Both answers carry correct units or a percentage sign [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert mL per minute to a daily volume before comparing. State the units with each answer, and take the percentage of the filtrate volume, not of the urine volume.

**9. (a)** Protein molecules are too large to pass through the basement membrane [1], so they stay in the blood [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Glucose passes into the filtrate but is reabsorbed [1] by transport proteins in the cells of the proximal convoluted tubule [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 21.0 / 0.30 = **70** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Both are small molecules [1] that pass freely through the capillary wall, basement membrane and podocyte slits, so filtration removes solutes by size, not by whether they are wanted [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* For "explain absent from", name the process and where it happens. "It is reabsorbed" is incomplete without the location. For part (d), link the equal concentrations to molecular size.

**10.** The ascending limb actively transports sodium ions out into the medulla [1]. This keeps the medulla at a high osmotic concentration [1]. Water leaves the collecting duct by osmosis [1] down this gradient, so the urine becomes concentrated [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The key idea is sodium ions being pumped out of the ascending limb. Writing that the loop "filters" or "reabsorbs glucose" is off target.

**11.** The water lowers blood osmotic concentration [1]. Osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus detect the fall [1]. The pituitary gland secretes less ADH [1]. Less ADH reaches the collecting duct cells [1]. Aquaporins are moved from the cell membrane into vesicles [1]. The collecting duct becomes less permeable to water [1]. Less water is reabsorbed, so a large volume of dilute urine is produced [1]. Blood osmotic concentration returns to the set point: negative feedback [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* The location switch of aquaporins is the guide's key mechanism. Answers that say "ADH is made" or "aquaporins are destroyed" miss it.

**12. (a)** 22 × 0.80 = **17.6 L min⁻¹** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Rest: 5.5 × 0.24 = 1.32. Exercise: 22 × 0.04 = 0.88 [1]. Change = (0.88 − 1.32) / 1.32 × 100 = **−33.3%** (a fall of 33.3%) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Smooth muscle in the muscle arterioles relaxes (vasodilation) [1], while arterioles to organs such as the gut constrict [1]. Muscle receives more oxygen and glucose for respiration and carbon dioxide is removed faster [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Calculate the actual flow from the percentage and the cardiac output. The gut's share fell, but because cardiac output rose, the fall in flow is smaller than the share suggests.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing the pancreas as "producing a hormone" without naming beta cells or alpha cells.
- Stating that negative feedback "keeps the variable constant" without the return to the set point.
- Giving type 1 and type 2 diabetes the same cause.
- Listing responses to heat without explaining how they lose heat.
- Writing "brown fat burns fat" instead of uncoupled respiration.
- Calculating percentage change from the final value.
- Giving no units or the wrong units for osmotic concentration and flow rates.
- Confusing filtration, reabsorption and secretion.
- Saying ADH creates or destroys aquaporins instead of moving them.
