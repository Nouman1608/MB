These notes condense sub-topic 4(d) Alkenes (points 4.23 to 4.28) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024). None of the points has a C reference, so all can appear on Paper 1 and Paper 2. Worked examples and model answers are in the [alkenes study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-alkenes/); then try the [alkenes practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-alkenes-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Alkene | Hydrocarbon with at least one C=C double bond |
| Functional group | The C=C double bond (>C=C<) |
| Unsaturated | Contains a C=C double bond, so fewer hydrogens than the alkane with the same carbon number |
| Addition reaction | Two molecules join to give one product; the double bond opens |
| Dibromoalkane | Alkane with two bromine atoms, one on each carbon of the former double bond |

## Formulae and members

| Alkene | Formula | Structural formula | Mr |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ethene | C₂H₄ | CH₂=CH₂ | 28 |
| Propene | C₃H₆ | CH₂=CHCH₃ | 42 |
| But-1-ene | C₄H₈ | CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ | 56 |
| But-2-ene | C₄H₈ | CH₃CH=CHCH₃ | 56 |

- **General formula:** CₙH₂ₙ (twice as many H as C).
- **Empirical formula:** CH₂ for every alkene.
- **No alkene has n = 1.** The smallest is ethene.
- Cis/trans notation is not required.

## Why unsaturated?

- Alkanes are saturated: all single bonds, the maximum number of hydrogens.
- Alkenes are unsaturated: a C=C double bond, so fewer hydrogens, and the double bond can open to bond to more atoms.
- Model sentence: "Alkenes are unsaturated because they contain a C=C double bond, so they could bond to more atoms."

## Drawing and naming

1. Find the longest chain that contains the double bond.
2. Choose the stem (eth, prop, but) and the ending -ene.
3. Number from the end that gives the lower number to the first carbon of the double bond.
4. Put the number before -ene: but-1-ene, but-2-ene. No number is needed for ethene and propene.
5. Check every carbon has four bonds. Each C in the C=C counts two bonds for the double bond.

Branched isomer of C₄H₈: 2-methylpropene, CH₂=C(CH₃)CH₃.

## Bromine reactions

```
CH2=CH2 + Br2 → CH2Br-CH2Br     (1,2-dibromoethane)
CH3CH=CH2 + Br2 → CH3CHBr-CH2Br (1,2-dibromopropane)
```

- Addition: one product only.
- Room temperature, no UV light needed.
- Compare alkanes: need UV light; substitution; two products.

## The bromine water test

| Sample | Result with bromine water |
|---|---|
| Alkane | No change; stays orange-brown |
| Alkene | Decolourised: orange-brown to colourless |

- Write "colourless", not "clear".
- The colour is lost because bromine adds across the double bond and is used up.
- The test works at room temperature by shaking the sample with the bromine water.

## Calculation pattern

1. Moles = mass / Mr.
2. The ratio alkene : bromine : dibromoalkane is 1 : 1 : 1.
3. Mass = moles x Mr (Br₂ = 160).
4. Check conservation of mass: mass of alkene + mass of bromine = mass of product.
5. Gas volume at room temperature and pressure: moles x 24,000 cm³.

## Answer frames

- **Test:** "Add bromine water and shake. Alkene: turns from orange to colourless. Alkane: stays orange."
- **Explain the test:** "Alkenes contain C=C, so bromine adds across the double bond and the colour is lost."
- **Distinguish addition from substitution:** addition gives one product; substitution gives two.
- **Name a dibromo product:** one bromine on each carbon of the double bond.

## Exam traps

- The general formula has no "+2".
- Alkenes are unsaturated, not "unsaturated with hydrogen".
- The bromine water test needs the words "orange" and "colourless".
- Addition to ethene with bromine gives 1,2-dibromoethane, not 1,1.
- A smoky flame comes from the high carbon content, not from the double bond itself.
- Alkenes come mainly from cracking, not directly from fractional distillation.

## Alkanes and alkenes compared

| | Ethane | Ethene |
|---|---|---|
| Formula | C₂H₆ | C₂H₄ |
| Bonding | C-C single | C=C double |
| General formula | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | CₙH₂ₙ |
| Type | Saturated | Unsaturated |
| Bromine water | No change | Decolourised |
| Reaction with bromine | Substitution (UV); two products | Addition (room temperature); one product |
| Flame | Cleaner | More smoky |
| Percentage of carbon | 80.0% | 85.7% |

## Two more worked examples

**Finding n from Mr.** An alkene has Mr = 84. The general formula gives Mr = 14n, so n = 84 / 14 = 6 and the formula is **C₆H₁₂**.

**Spotting an alkene from the formula.** A chain hydrocarbon with 5 carbons would be saturated if it were C₅H₁₂ (2n + 2). C₅H₁₀ has two fewer hydrogens, so it has one double bond and is an alkene (pentene). Always compare the number of hydrogens with 2n + 2: two fewer means one double bond.

## Last-minute checklist

- Can you give the general formula, and use it to find the formula of an alkene with a given Mr?
- Can you explain "unsaturated" in one sentence that mentions the double bond and the number of hydrogens?
- Can you draw and name ethene, propene, but-1-ene and but-2-ene?
- Can you write the bromine equation and name the product?
- Can you describe the bromine water test with both colours and the result for the alkane?
- Can you state that these points can be examined on Paper 1 and Paper 2?

## Self-test

1. State the general formula of the alkenes.
2. State the functional group in alkenes.
3. Draw the structural formula of propene.
4. Name CH₃CH=CHCH₃.
5. Write the equation for ethene reacting with bromine.
6. 2.8 g of ethene (Mr = 28) reacts completely with bromine. Calculate the mass of bromine used.
7. 5.6 g of but-1-ene (Mr = 56) reacts completely with bromine. Calculate the mass of dibromobutane formed (Mr = 216).
8. State the colour change when an alkene is shaken with bromine water.
9. State what happens when an alkane is shaken with bromine water at room temperature.
10. Explain why alkenes are classed as unsaturated.

### Answers

1. CₙH₂ₙ.
2. The carbon-carbon double bond, C=C.
3. CH₂=CHCH₃.
4. But-2-ene.
5. C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂ (CH₂BrCH₂Br).
6. Moles of ethene = 2.8 / 28 = 0.10 mol; moles of Br₂ = 0.10 mol; mass = 0.10 x 160 = **16 g**.
7. Moles = 5.6 / 56 = 0.10 mol; mass of product = 0.10 x 216 = **21.6 g**.
8. Orange-brown to colourless.
9. No change; it stays orange-brown.
10. They contain a C=C double bond, so they have fewer hydrogen atoms than the corresponding alkane and can bond to more atoms.
