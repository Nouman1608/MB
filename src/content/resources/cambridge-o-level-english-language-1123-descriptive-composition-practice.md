> **These are original questions written for Marlbridge**, for practice on the descriptive option in
> 1123 Paper 2 Section B, where "a descriptive piece of writing describes a person, place or situation
> (including atmosphere) in detail so that the reader can picture it". Question 10 is a full
> composition at the syllabus tariff of 25 marks; the other questions build its separate skills, with
> practice marks.

Related: [Descriptive composition study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-english-language-1123-descriptive-composition/),
[revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-english-language-1123-descriptive-composition-revision-notes/)
and the [Paper 2 Writing study guide](/resources/o-level-english-language-paper-2-writing/).

## Section A

**1.** Rewrite each phrase with specific, picturable detail: (a) "a nice garden" (b) "the room was
messy" (c) "loud music". **[3]**

**2.** Replace the cliché in "The sun was as hot as fire" with a fresh image. **[1]**

**3.** Name the structure used by this plan: *paragraph 1, the beach at sunrise, empty; paragraph 2,
midday crowds; paragraph 3, evening, the tide coming in over footprints.* **[1]**

**4.** From this list, choose the three details that best create an eerie atmosphere for "an empty
school at night", and explain your choice: a flickering corridor light; a cheerful poster about
sports day; a door swinging slightly; a vending machine's steady hum; children laughing; a coat left
on a peg. **[3]**

**5.** Rewrite as two or three varied sentences: "The market was busy. The market was loud. The
market smelled of spices." **[2]**

**6.** This paragraph is for "Describe a park in autumn". Identify the sentence that drifts into
narrative, and rewrite it so that it serves the description.

> Leaves lay in drifts along the path, rust and gold and the brown of old paper. A man swept them into
> heaps that the wind undid at once. Suddenly I saw my friend Ali, and we decided to go and buy ice
> cream, so we ran to the shop.

**[2]**

**7.** Correct the tense inconsistency: "The kitchen is warm and the kettle was whistling. My
grandmother stands at the stove, and she stirred the pot slowly." **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** Plan a composition for the title *Describe a place in the hour before a big celebration*. State
the atmosphere, the structure and two specific details for each of four paragraphs. **[4]**

**9.** Write the opening paragraph (about 80 words) for the title in Question 8. **[4]**

**10.** Write a composition of about 350–450 words on **one** of these titles:

(a) *Describe a busy hospital waiting room.*

(b) *Describe a person you have watched at work, and the place where they work.* **[25]**

## Answers

**1.** One mark each for specific, picturable detail [3], for example: (a) "a narrow garden where
marigolds spill over cracked pots and a lemon tree leans against the wall"; (b) "clothes were heaped
on the chair, and a tower of books had collapsed across the carpet"; (c) "the bass thudded through the
floor until the glasses on the shelf trembled".

**2.** For example: "The sun pressed on the back of my neck like a hot iron" or "The heat lay on the
street like a lid" [1]. Credit any fresh, precise image.

**3.** Time-lapse (the same place at different times of day) [1].

**4.** The flickering light, the swinging door and the coat left on a peg [1 for the three choices];
they suggest something unsettled or recently abandoned, as if someone has just left or is still there
[1]; the poster and laughter belong to the daytime school and would break the eerie mood, while a
steady hum is neutral rather than eerie [1]. (Credit the hum if it is justified, for example as a
sound that makes the silence more noticeable.)

**5.** For example [2]: "Crammed with shoppers and loud with the cries of traders, the market smelled
of cumin and roasting chillies." One mark for removing the repetition, one for varied structure.

**6.** The third sentence drifts into a story (meeting a friend, deciding, running) [1]. Rewrite, for
example: "On a bench by the gate, two boys shared a paper cone of chips, their breath steaming in the
cold." [1]

**7.** Present tense throughout: "The kitchen is warm and the kettle is whistling. My grandmother
stands at the stove and stirs the pot slowly." (Or past tense throughout.) [2] One mark per sentence
corrected.

**8.** One mark each [4]: a stated atmosphere (for example excited, nervous anticipation); a clear
structure (for example a journey through the house, or a zoom from the street to one room); specific
details in each paragraph (for example strings of lights not yet switched on, a pot of biryani
simmering, a child in new shoes refusing to sit down); a final paragraph that turns the structure (the
first guests' car arriving, the lights coming on).

**9.** Model [4]:

> The house is holding its breath. Strings of fairy lights hang unlit along the balcony, waiting for
> dusk, and in the hall the shoes have been lined up in pairs for the first time all year. From the
> kitchen comes the slow bubbling of a pot that has been simmering since noon, and the smell of
> cardamom has found its way into every room. Upstairs, someone is ironing a shirt for the third time.

Marking: precise images [1]; atmosphere established (anticipation) [1]; varied sentences, including a
short opening sentence [1]; consistent tense and accurate expression [1].

**10.** Marked out of 25, all writing (W1, W2, W3, W5) [25]. High-level responses:

- create a convincing, detailed picture and atmosphere, with specific nouns and precise verbs (W1);
- follow a deliberate structure, such as zoom, journey through space or before-and-after, with
  paragraphs that each have a clear focus (W2);
- use a range of vocabulary and varied sentence structures that suit the mood (W3);
- are accurate in spelling, punctuation and grammar (W5).

For (a), strong answers might contrast the stillness of waiting with sudden movement at the doors,
using a few precise human details. For (b), strong answers balance the person and the place, showing
the person through characteristic actions rather than a list of qualities. Lower-level responses tell
a story about an illness or a job, list senses mechanically, or rely on general words.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 4**: choosing details by how dramatic they sound rather than by mood.
- **Question 6**: rewriting the narrative sentence as another event.
- **Question 10**: turning the waiting room into a story about a family emergency.
- **Question 10**: describing the person's appearance only, with no sense of their work.
