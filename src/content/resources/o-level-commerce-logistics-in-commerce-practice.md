2026-2028). They cover the functions and methods of transport, choosing a method for a given situation,
containerisation and transshipment, warehouse functions and types, and warehouse automation.

## Section A - multiple choice

**1.** Which is the main disadvantage of rail transport for a manufacturer? **[1]**
A it cannot carry heavy loads   B goods usually need road transport at each end
C it is faster than air   D it can only carry liquids

**2.** A small grocer drives to a large warehouse, pays for goods and takes them away. The warehouse is a:
**[1]**
A bonded warehouse   B cold store   C cash and carry   D regional distribution centre

**3.** Delivering goods "in the right amount" is which function of transport? **[1]**
A time   B place   C quantity   D security

## Section B - structured questions

**4.** *Hill Dairy makes yoghurt in a rural area and sells it to supermarkets in a city 150 km away.*
(a) Recommend a method of transport for Hill Dairy. Justify your answer. **[4]**
(b) Explain why Hill Dairy might need a cold storage warehouse. **[2]**

**5.** Compare rail and road transport for moving 500 tonnes of coal a day from a mine to a power station
400 km away. **[6]**

**6.** *Pearl Jewellers sends diamond necklaces from Antwerp to customers in Dubai.*
Explain **two** factors that make air transport the most suitable choice. **[4]**

**7.** (a) Describe what is meant by *containerisation*. **[2]**
(b) Explain why containerisation reduces the cost of insurance. **[2]**
(c) Explain **one** disadvantage of containerisation for a country with only small ports. **[2]**

**8.** Explain what is meant by *transshipment* and why it is used. **[3]**

**9.** *A toy importer brings in most of its stock in August for sale in December.*
(a) Explain **two** functions of warehousing for the importer. **[4]**
(b) Explain why the importer might use a bonded warehouse. **[3]**

**10.** Explain what *logistics postponement* means, using an example. **[3]**

**11.** *A supermarket chain plans to replace three small warehouses with one regional distribution centre
that uses robotics and automated sorting.* Evaluate this decision. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** B. **[1]**

**2.** C. **[1]**

**3.** C. **[1]**

**4.** (a) Road, using refrigerated trucks **[1]**: door-to-door delivery to each supermarket **[1]**; suitable for a
medium distance of 150 km **[1]**; flexible delivery times to keep yoghurt fresh **[1]**.
(b) Yoghurt is perishable and must be kept cold **[1]**, so it needs refrigerated storage before delivery
to prevent spoiling **[1]**.

**5.** **[6]** Rail: carries very heavy loads in one train; lower cost per tonne over 400 km; less congestion
and fewer emissions per tonne; a fixed route is no problem between a mine and a power station, which
may both have rail sidings. Road: flexible, but would need many trucks a day, raising fuel and labour
costs, congestion and road damage. Conclusion: rail is more suitable for a regular, bulky, long-distance
flow.

**6.** Any two explained: security - necklaces are very valuable and air cargo is closely controlled **[2]**;
urgency - customers expect quick delivery **[2]**; nature of goods - small and light, so the high air cost
per kilogram is a small share of their value **[2]**; distance - long international journey made quickly
**[2]**. **[max 4]**

**7.** (a) Packing goods into standard-sized metal containers **[1]** that are moved between ships, trains and
trucks without unpacking **[1]**.
(b) Sealed containers reduce theft and damage **[1]**, so the risk to the insurer is lower and premiums fall
**[1]**.
(c) Small ports may lack the cranes and deep water for container ships **[1]**, so goods must be transshipped
or the country is bypassed, raising costs **[1]**.

**8.** Transferring goods or containers from one ship or vehicle to another during the journey **[1]**, usually
at a hub port **[1]**, so that large ships can serve main routes while smaller ships or trucks reach final
destinations **[1]**.

**9.** (a) Any two explained: links with demand - stock is built up before the December peak **[2]**; protecting
supply - goods are available even if later shipments are delayed **[2]**; price stability - goods can be
released steadily **[2]**. **[max 4]**
(b) Imported toys can be stored without paying customs duty at once **[1]**; duty is paid only when goods
are released for sale **[1]**, which helps the importer's cash flow (or avoids duty if goods are re-exported)
**[1]**.

**10.** Delaying final stages such as labelling, packing or assembly until customer orders are known **[1]**,
often at a warehouse near the market **[1]**; e.g. printers stored without the country-specific plug and
manual, which are added when the destination is known **[1]**.

**11.** **[8]** Benefits of an RDC: fewer, fuller deliveries to shops; central stock control; shops need less
back-room space. Benefits of automation: lower labour costs; less human error and better picking
accuracy; improved inventory control and customer service. Drawbacks: very high investment in the
building and robots; job losses and staff resistance; breakdowns could stop supplies to all shops; longer
journeys to some shops. Conclusion: likely to be worthwhile for a large chain with high volumes, provided
there is a back-up plan for system failures.

## A note on exam technique for this topic

When recommending a transport method, name it, then use at least two facts from the scenario linked to
syllabus factors. For an 8-mark evaluation, give developed points on both sides and a conclusion that
says whether the business should go ahead and under what conditions.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which warehouse types does 7100 name?** Bonded, cold storage, cash and carry, and regional distribution
centres (RDCs).

**What are the benefits of warehouse automation in the syllabus?** Lower labour costs, less human error,
improved picking accuracy, improved inventory control and improved customer service.

**Can a question ask me to choose between two methods?** Yes. Compare them on the factors in the scenario
and give a clear recommendation.
