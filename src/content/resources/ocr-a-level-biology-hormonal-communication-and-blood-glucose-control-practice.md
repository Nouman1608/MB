## Questions

**1.** Describe how a hormone released by an endocrine gland affects only its target cells. **[4]**

**2.** The adrenal glands are endocrine glands.

**(a)** State where the adrenal glands are located and name the two regions of each gland. **[2]**
**(b)** For each region, name one hormone and state one of its functions. **[4]**
**(c)** A patient cannot make aldosterone. Predict and explain the effects on the sodium ion and potassium ion concentrations of the blood. **[3]**

**3.** A section of pancreas is viewed with a light microscope.

**(a)** Describe two differences in appearance between an islet of Langerhans and the surrounding acini. **[2]**
**(b)** A photomicrograph of the section is printed at a magnification of ×60. An islet measures 15 mm across on the print. Calculate the actual width of the islet in micrometres. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why an islet has no duct but a dense network of capillaries. **[2]**

**4.** A student's drawing of a pancreas section has shaded cells, overlapping label lines and no title. State four changes that would improve it. **[4]**

**5.** A person eats a meal rich in carbohydrate. Explain how the blood glucose concentration is returned to its normal level. **[5]**

**6.** During a long fast, blood glucose would fall without a hormonal response. Explain how glucagon and the liver prevent this. **[4]**

**7.** Describe how a rise in blood glucose concentration leads to the release of insulin from a beta cell. **[6]**

**8.** Beta cells were loaded with a fluorescent dye whose brightness shows the calcium ion concentration in the cytoplasm. Relative fluorescence was measured at four glucose concentrations.

| Glucose / mmol dm⁻³ | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Relative fluorescence | 1.0 | 1.1 | 2.9 | 4.1 |

At 12 mmol dm⁻³, adding diazoxide, a drug that holds potassium channels open, gave a relative fluorescence of 1.2.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in fluorescence between 3 and 12 mmol dm⁻³. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the result with diazoxide. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest why fluorescence barely changed between 3 and 6 mmol dm⁻³. **[1]**

**9.** A healthy adult and an adult with Type 2 diabetes each drank a glucose solution after fasting. Blood glucose (mmol dm⁻³) was measured over three hours.

| Time / min | 0 | 30 | 60 | 120 | 180 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Healthy adult | 4.8 | 7.4 | 6.2 | 5.0 | 4.7 |
| Type 2 adult | 8.1 | 12.6 | 13.4 | 11.7 | 9.6 |

**(a)** For the healthy adult, calculate the percentage rise in glucose between 0 and 30 minutes. **[2]**
**(b)** Find how quickly glucose fell, on average, in the Type 2 adult from 60 to 180 minutes, in mmol dm⁻³ per hour. **[2]**
**(c)** Use the data to explain how the Type 2 adult's response differs from the healthy adult's. **[3]**

**10.** **(a)** Explain what causes Type 1 diabetes. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what causes Type 2 diabetes. **[2]**
**(c)** A sulfonylurea drug closes potassium channels in beta cells. Explain why it is useful in Type 2 diabetes but not in Type 1. **[2]**

**11.** **(a)** Describe how bacteria can be genetically modified to make human insulin. **[4]**
**(b)** Give two advantages of using this insulin rather than insulin extracted from pigs. **[2]**

**12.** Some people with Type 1 diabetes use a continuous glucose sensor linked to an insulin pump.

**(a)** Explain how this system can act like a negative feedback loop. **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate the potential of stem cell treatment compared with insulin injections for Type 1 diabetes. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** The gland cells secrete the hormone into the blood capillaries around them [1]. The blood plasma carries it to all parts of the body [1]. Target cells have receptors on (or in) them with a shape complementary to the hormone [1]. Other cells lack the receptor, so the hormone does not affect them [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the three stages (secretion, transport, detection) separate and say why non-target cells do not respond.

**2. (a)** The glands are on top of (above) each kidney [1]. The regions are the cortex and the medulla [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Cortex: aldosterone [1] increases sodium ion reabsorption in the kidney tubules (or cortisol raises blood glucose) [1]. Medulla: adrenaline [1] increases heart rate or causes glycogenolysis [1]. **[4]**
**(c)** Less sodium ion reabsorption, so sodium ions are lost in the urine and blood sodium concentration falls [1]. Less potassium ion excretion, so blood potassium concentration rises [1]. Water follows sodium, so blood volume and blood pressure fall [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give each hormone with its own function as a matched pair; "controls stress" on its own is too vague.

**3. (a)** Islets are paler staining than the surrounding tissue [1]. Islets are rounded patches with no ducts, whereas acini are clusters of cells around small ducts [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Actual width = 15 mm ÷ 60 [1] = 0.25 mm = **250 µm** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** There is no duct because hormones are not carried away along a duct [1]. The dense capillaries let hormones enter the blood quickly [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In magnification questions, show the rearrangement and the unit conversion so that a slip in the last step is visible.

**4.** Remove the shading and use clear outlines [1]; use single continuous lines rather than sketchy ones [1]; draw label lines with a ruler so that they do not cross [1]; add a title, and the magnification or a scale bar [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give four different improvements.

**5.** Absorbed glucose raises blood glucose [1]. Beta cells detect the rise [1]. They secrete insulin into the blood [1]. Insulin binds to receptors on liver, muscle and fat cells; they take up more glucose and the liver forms glycogen (glycogenesis) [1]. Blood glucose falls, so the stimulus for insulin decreases and secretion falls: negative feedback [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Finish the loop: saying that the falling level reduces insulin secretion turns a description of insulin into an explanation of negative feedback.

**6.** Low glucose is detected by alpha cells, which release glucagon [1]. At the hepatocyte surface it activates adenylyl cyclase, so cyclic AMP is made [1]. The liver converts glycogen to glucose (glycogenolysis) [1]. The liver also makes glucose from amino acids or glycerol (gluconeogenesis) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the correct term for each process: "glycogenesis" for the breakdown of glycogen would contradict an otherwise correct description.

**7.** Glucose enters the beta cell [1] by facilitated diffusion [1]. It is respired, so ATP concentration rises [1]. ATP closes potassium ion channels [1], so the membrane depolarises [1]. Voltage-gated calcium ion channels open and calcium ions enter, which makes insulin vesicles fuse with the membrane (exocytosis) [1]. (Any six.) **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* This is a sequence question. Link each step to the next (ATP closes channels, which causes depolarisation) and keep the order.

**8. (a)** (4.1 − 1.0) ÷ 1.0 × 100 [1] = **310%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Diazoxide keeps potassium channels open, so potassium ions keep leaving [1]. The membrane does not depolarise even though glucose is high [1]. Calcium channels stay shut, so calcium ions do not enter and fluorescence stays near the baseline [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** At low glucose too little ATP is made to close the potassium channels, so the calcium channels are not opened [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the data: comparing 4.1 with 1.2 at the same glucose concentration shows the drug effect, then link it to the channel sequence.

**9. (a)** (7.4 − 4.8) ÷ 4.8 × 100 [1] = **54%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (13.4 − 9.6) ÷ 2 hours [1] = **1.9 mmol dm⁻³ per hour** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The Type 2 adult starts higher (8.1 compared with 4.8) [1]. The peak is much higher (13.4 compared with 7.4) and occurs later (60 minutes compared with 30) [1]. By 180 minutes the Type 2 adult is still 1.5 above the starting value, whereas the healthy adult has returned to about the starting value [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A rate alone can mislead: here the Type 2 fall per hour (1.9) is faster than the healthy adult's (0.75), but the level has not been restored. Quote values from both people and compare them directly.

**10. (a)** The immune system attacks and destroys the beta cells (an autoimmune response) [1], so little or no insulin is made [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Target cells become less responsive to insulin (insulin resistance) [1]. Beta cells may later be unable to make enough insulin to compensate, and risk factors include obesity and inactivity [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The drug makes surviving beta cells release more insulin, which helps in Type 2 where beta cells are present [1]. In Type 1 few or no beta cells remain, so there are none to stimulate and insulin must be supplied directly [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Type 2 is caused by obesity" is incomplete. State the physiological problem (reduced response to insulin) and treat risk factors as contributors.

**11. (a)** Obtain the human insulin gene, without introns [1]. Cut the gene and a plasmid with the same restriction enzyme to give sticky ends, and join them with DNA ligase [1]. Insert the recombinant plasmid into bacteria and identify those that took it up using a marker gene [1]. Culture the bacteria in a fermenter and extract and purify the insulin [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** The insulin has the human amino acid sequence, so is less likely to cause an immune response [1]. Supply is large and reliable, or no animal tissue is needed, avoiding ethical or religious objections [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the enzymes and say what each does; "the gene is put into the bacteria" gives the aim, not the technique.

**12. (a)** The sensor detects blood glucose, like a receptor [1]. A control unit compares the reading with a target and signals the pump, like a coordinator [1]. The pump gives insulin when glucose is high and reduces or stops it when glucose falls, so the change is reversed (the effector) [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Benefits: it could restore the body's own regulation, giving a possible cure without lifelong injections [1]. Limitations: transplanted cells may be rejected, so immunosuppressant drugs are needed [1]. The autoimmune attack could destroy the new beta cells [1]. Embryonic stem cells raise ethical objections and uncontrolled division could cause tumours [1]. Evidence is limited and it is not yet routine, so injections remain the established treatment: a balanced conclusion [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give points for and against, then a judgement. A list of only advantages, or only problems, is incomplete.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the pancreas converts glucose to glycogen. The liver and muscle do this.
- Mixing up glycogenesis, glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis.
- Reversing or skipping steps in the beta cell sequence.
- Not finishing the negative feedback loop.
- Quoting a rate without comparing end and start values.
- Writing one-sided answers to "evaluate" questions.
