These questions cover section 9.2, Interpreting statistical data, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions marked **(Extended)** use the interquartile range, which only Extended candidates compare; every other question suits Core and Extended candidates. Each question is also labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured ones.

Revise first with the [interpreting statistical data study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-interpreting-statistical-data/) and the [interpreting data revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-interpreting-statistical-data-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) The table shows the number of brothers and sisters of 30 students.

```
Number of brothers and sisters   0    1    2    3    4 or more
Frequency                         5   12    8    3    2
```

Decide whether each statement is true, false, or cannot be told from the table. Give a reason each time.

**(a)** "Most of the students have at least one brother or sister." **[1]**

**(b)** "Two students have exactly 4 brothers and sisters." **[1]**

**(c)** "More than a third of the students have 2 or more brothers and sisters." **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) Two classes took the same test. Class R had a mean of 58 marks and a range of 30 marks. Class S had a mean of 64 marks and a range of 45 marks. Make two comparisons between the marks of the two classes. **[2]**

**3.** (calculator) The prices, in thousands of dollars, of five houses sold on one street are 180, 195, 200, 210 and 950.

**(a)** Find the mean and the median price. **[2]**

**(b)** Which average better represents a typical house price on this street? Give a reason. **[1]**

**4.** (non-calculator) A bar chart shows ticket sales of 60 in week 1 and 75 in week 2. Its vertical axis starts at 50.

**(a)** How many times as tall as the week 1 bar does the week 2 bar appear? **[1]**

**(b)** Find the actual percentage increase in ticket sales. **[2]**

**5.** (calculator) In Town A, 35% of 260 people surveyed cycle to work. In Town B, 20% of 480 people surveyed cycle to work. Mia says "More of the people surveyed in Town A cycle to work." Is Mia correct? Show your working. **[2]**

**6.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Workers in two factories assemble the same part. In Factory A the median time is 42 seconds and the interquartile range is 6 seconds. In Factory B the median time is 39 seconds and the interquartile range is 11 seconds. Make two comparisons between the times in the two factories. **[2]**

### Section B

**7.** (non-calculator) The waiting times, in minutes, of seven patients at each of two clinics are:

```
Clinic 1:  12  15   9  20  14  18  10
Clinic 2:   8  25  11   6  30   9  16
```

**(a)** Find the mean and the range for each clinic. **[3]**

**(b)** Use your answers to compare the waiting times at the two clinics. **[2]**

**8.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Two athletes each made 11 long jumps. The distances, in metres, are:

```
Athlete A:  5.2  5.5  5.6  5.8  5.9  6.0  6.0  6.1  6.2  6.4  6.6
Athlete B:  6.3  4.9  6.7  5.1  6.0  6.8  5.6  6.2  7.0  6.1  6.4
```

**(a)** Find the median, the lower quartile and the upper quartile for each athlete. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the interquartile range for each athlete. **[1]**

**(c)** Compare the distances jumped by the two athletes. **[2]**

**9.** (calculator) 150 people were asked where they get most of their news.

```
              TV    Online   Newspaper   Total
Under 30      15      45         5         65
30 to 59      20      25        10         55
60 and over   18       4         8         30
Total         53      74        23        150
```

**(a)** What percentage of the people under 30 prefer online news? **[2]**

**(b)** What fraction of the people who prefer a newspaper are aged 60 and over? **[1]**

**(c)** Raj says "More people aged 30 to 59 prefer TV than people aged 60 and over, so TV is more popular with the 30 to 59 group." Explain, with calculations, why Raj is wrong. **[3]**

**(d)** The survey was carried out online. Give one reason why this could affect the results. **[1]**

**10.** (non-calculator) A club had 120 members in 2021, 150 in 2022, 180 in 2023 and 210 in 2024.

**(a)** Describe the trend in the number of members. **[1]**

**(b)** Ben says "The pattern shows the club will have 510 members in 2034." Give two reasons why this prediction may not be reliable. **[2]**

**11.** (non-calculator) Two cricketers each played 10 innings. The table gives some statistics for their scores.

```
              Mean   Median   Range
Player X       34      22       95
Player Y       30      29       40
```

**(a)** Player X's mean is much higher than his median. What does this suggest about his scores? **[1]**

**(b)** A coach wants a player who scores steadily in every innings. Which player should she choose? Give two reasons using the statistics. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why comparing Player X's mean with Player Y's median would not be fair. **[1]**

**12.** (non-calculator) A head teacher asks 20 students in the school library at lunchtime how many hours they spend reading each week. Their mean is 6 hours. She concludes "Students at this school read for 6 hours a week on average."

Give three different reasons why her conclusion may not be reliable. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** True: 12 + 8 + 3 + 2 = 25 out of 30 have at least one [1]

**(b)** Cannot tell: the last column is "4 or more", so the 2 students could have more than 4 [1]

**(c)** True: 8 + 3 + 2 = 13, and 13/30 is more than 10/30 [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* "cannot tell" earns the mark only with the reason; here the reason is the open-ended last class.

**2.** Class S had a higher mean (64 against 58), so Class S did better on average [1]; Class R had a smaller range (30 against 45), so Class R's marks were more consistent [1]

**3. (a)** Mean = 1735 ÷ 5 = **347** thousand dollars [1]; median = **200** thousand dollars [1]

**(b)** The **median**, because the mean is pulled up by the one extreme price of 950 thousand dollars; four of the five houses cost less than the mean [1] **[3]**

**4. (a)** Bars are 75 − 50 = 25 and 60 − 50 = 10 units tall, so the week 2 bar appears **2.5 times** as tall [1]

**(b)** Increase = 15 [1]; 15 ÷ 60 × 100 = **25%** [1] **[3]**

**5.** Town A: 0.35 × 260 = 91; Town B: 0.20 × 480 = 96 [1]; **No**, more people in Town B cycle (96 > 91), although a higher percentage cycle in Town A [1]

**6.** Factory B's median (39 s) is lower, so a typical part is assembled faster in Factory B [1]; Factory A's IQR (6 s) is smaller, so Factory A's times are more consistent [1]

*Examiner insight:* each comparison needs the measure, both values and what it means; "39 is less than 42" on its own does not earn the mark.

**7. (a)** Clinic 1: mean = 98 ÷ 7 = **14 minutes** [1]; Clinic 2: mean = 105 ÷ 7 = **15 minutes** [1]; ranges: Clinic 1 = 20 − 9 = **11 minutes**, Clinic 2 = 30 − 6 = **24 minutes** [1]

**(b)** Clinic 1 has the lower mean, so patients there wait slightly less on average [1]; Clinic 1 has the smaller range, so its waiting times are more consistent [1] **[5]**

**8. (a)** Athlete B ordered: 4.9, 5.1, 5.6, 6.0, 6.1, 6.2, 6.3, 6.4, 6.7, 6.8, 7.0

Athlete A: median **6.0 m**, LQ **5.6 m**, UQ **6.2 m** [1]

Athlete B: median **6.2 m** [1]; LQ **5.6 m**, UQ **6.7 m** [1]

**(b)** A: 6.2 − 5.6 = **0.6 m**; B: 6.7 − 5.6 = **1.1 m** [1]

**(c)** Athlete B has the higher median (6.2 m against 6.0 m), so B typically jumps further [1]; Athlete A has the smaller IQR (0.6 m against 1.1 m), so A's jumps are more consistent [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Athlete B's data must be put in order first; the 6th value of the unordered list (6.8) is not the median.

**9. (a)** 45 ÷ 65 × 100 [1] = **69.2%** (3 s.f.) [1]

**(b)** **8/23** [1]

**(c)** The groups are different sizes (55 and 30 people) [1]; 20 ÷ 55 = 36.4% of the 30 to 59 group prefer TV, but 18 ÷ 30 = 60% of the 60 and over group do [1]; so a greater **proportion** of the 60 and over group prefer TV, and TV is more popular with them [1]

**(d)** Everyone surveyed uses the internet, so people who prefer online news are likely to be over-represented (the sample is not representative) [1] **[7]**

**10. (a)** The number of members **increases by 30 each year** [1]

**(b)** Any two different reasons [1] [1], for example: the prediction goes 10 years beyond the data; only four years of data are used; the increase may slow or stop (the club may have a limited capacity or lose members) **[3]**

**11. (a)** He has at least one **very high score** that pulls the mean up; most of his scores are lower than the mean [1]

**(b)** **Player Y**, with two reasons: Y's range (40) is much smaller than X's (95), so Y's scores are more consistent [1]; Y's median (29) is higher than X's (22), so Y's typical innings is better [1]

**(c)** A mean and a median are different measures, so a difference between them may come from the choice of average, not from the players' scores [1] **[4]**

**12.** Any three different reasons [1] [1] [1], for example:

- the sample of 20 is small for a whole school;
- students in the library at lunchtime are likely to read more than average, so the sample is not representative;
- students may overestimate or guess how long they read;
- one week may not be typical (for example, an exam week).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Comparisons with values but no context, or with only one of average and spread.
- "Cannot tell" with no reason.
- Comparing percentages as if they were numbers of people.
- Taking a median from unordered data.
- Reasons such as "it might be wrong" that do not refer to the situation in the question.
