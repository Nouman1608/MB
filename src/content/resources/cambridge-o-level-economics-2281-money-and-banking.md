This guide covers section 3.1 **Money and banking** of the Cambridge O Level Economics 2281 syllabus for examination in 2026. The syllabus names two parts: money, meaning "the forms, functions and characteristics of money", and banking, meaning "the role and importance of central banks and commercial banks for government, producers and consumers".

The combined [money, banking and household spending guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-money-banking-and-household-spending-saving-and/) sets out the lists of functions, characteristics and bank roles. This page builds on it in three ways: it shows how the forms of money developed and why paper and digital money are accepted, it tests real and invented items against the characteristics, and it follows banks through situations (a bank under pressure, a firm needing finance, a government paying its bills) so that you can explain their importance rather than only list their roles. Companion pages: [money and banking revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-money-and-banking-revision-notes/) and [money and banking practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-money-and-banking-practice/). All banks, firms and figures are invented.

## 3.1.1 Money

**Money** is anything that is generally accepted as a means of payment for goods and services and for settling debts. The key word is *accepted*: an item is money because people are willing to take it, not because of what it is made of.

### Forms of money and how they developed

| Form | What it is | Why people accept it |
|---|---|---|
| Commodity money | An item with its own use or value, such as grain, salt, cattle or gold | The item itself is valued |
| Coins | Standard pieces of metal stamped by an authority | The stamp guarantees weight and quality, so coins need not be weighed |
| Banknotes | Paper or plastic notes issued by a central bank | Trust in the issuer and their status as legal tender |
| Bank deposits | Balances in bank accounts, moved by card, transfer or cheque | Trust in the bank; deposits can be turned into cash |
| Electronic and mobile money | Balances moved by phone or online | The balance is backed by money held in a bank account |

The long-run trend is away from items valued for themselves and towards money whose value rests on **trust**. A banknote costs very little to print, yet it buys goods because everyone expects others to accept it. Money of this kind, valuable because the state declares it legal tender and people trust it, is often called **fiat money**. If that trust collapses, the money stops working, however official it looks.

### Functions: what goes wrong without them

The clearest way to explain a function is to show the problem it solves.

| Function | Problem under barter | How money solves it |
|---|---|---|
| Medium of exchange | Needs a double coincidence of wants | Sell to anyone for money, buy from anyone with it |
| Unit of account | No common measure; every good priced in every other good | All prices in one unit, so values can be compared and profits measured |
| Store of value | Goods rot, wear out or cost money to keep | Money keeps its purchasing power if prices are stable |
| Standard of deferred payment | Debts in goods are hard to agree (what size of sack? what quality?) | Debts are set in money and repaid later |

**Worked example 1: when the store of value fails.** A family keeps $2,000 in cash for a year while prices rise by 8%. The cash now buys only 2,000 ÷ 1.08 = $1,852 worth of goods at the old prices, a loss of about $148 of purchasing power. If the family had placed it in a deposit paying 3% interest, it would have $2,060, worth 2,060 ÷ 1.08 = $1,907 in old prices. Interest reduces the loss but does not remove it. When prices rise very fast, people stop holding money, spend it immediately or switch to goods or a foreign currency, and money starts to fail as a medium of exchange too.

### Characteristics: judging an item

Good money is **acceptable, durable, portable, divisible, homogeneous (uniform), limited in supply and stable in value**. Exam questions often ask you to judge a given item. Work through the list and give a reason for each judgement.

| Characteristic | Cattle | Gold coins | Mobile money credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acceptable | Only in some communities | Widely, historically | Only where phone networks and agents exist |
| Durable | No: animals age and die | Yes | Yes, while the system works |
| Portable | No | Fairly, in small amounts | Yes |
| Divisible | No: half a cow is no longer a cow | Partly: coins come in fixed sizes | Yes, to the smallest unit |
| Homogeneous | No: animals differ in size and health | Yes, if standard weight and purity | Yes |
| Limited in supply | Herds can grow | Yes: gold is scarce | Yes, if backed by bank deposits |

The table supports a conclusion: cattle fail on most characteristics, gold coins pass most but are hard to divide and expensive to carry in large amounts, and mobile money passes most as long as people trust the system behind it.

## 3.1.2 Banking

### Commercial banks

A **commercial bank** is a profit-making firm that accepts deposits, lends money and provides payment services to households and firms. Its central job is to act as a go-between: it takes in the savings of people who do not need their money now and lends them to people who want to spend or invest now. It earns a profit by charging borrowers a higher rate of interest than it pays savers.

### Central banks

A **central bank** is the bank of the government and of the banking system. It issues the notes and coins, holds the government's accounts, holds accounts for the commercial banks and settles payments between them, carries out monetary policy, supervises banks and acts as **lender of last resort**.

### Worked example 2: a bank under pressure

Bank Z holds deposits of $800 million. It keeps $60 million as cash and reserves and has lent the rest. A rumour spreads that Bank Z is in trouble, and 12% of depositors try to withdraw their money at once.

1. Withdrawals demanded: 0.12 × 800 = $96 million.
2. Cash available: $60 million. Shortfall: 96 − 60 = $36 million.
3. Bank Z's loans cannot be called back overnight, so without help it must refuse customers. That would spread fear to other banks.
4. The central bank, as lender of last resort, lends Bank Z enough cash (say $40 million) against the security of its loans. Withdrawals are paid, the panic fades and depositors at other banks have no reason to rush.

This is why the central bank matters to **consumers** even though they never hold an account there: it protects their deposits by keeping the banking system trusted.

### Worked example 3: a firm needing finance

A clothing workshop must pay for fabric in March but will not be paid by its customers until June. Its bank lets it run an overdraft of $5,000 for three months at 12% a year. Interest is 5,000 × 0.12 × 3 ÷ 12 = $150. Without the overdraft it could not buy the fabric, so the order, and the jobs it supports, would be lost. This is the importance of commercial banks to **producers**: they bridge gaps between paying costs and receiving revenue, and they finance investment in equipment.

### Importance to each group

| Group | Central bank | Commercial banks |
|---|---|---|
| Government | Holds its accounts, manages its borrowing, carries out monetary policy to control inflation | Buy government debt; carry tax payments and benefit payments |
| Producers | Keeps the payment system and the currency trusted, helping firms plan | Loans, overdrafts, accounts for paying wages and suppliers, currency exchange for traders |
| Consumers | Protects deposits by supporting banks; keeps money stable in value | Safe place for savings, interest, mortgages and loans, cards and transfers |

When answering, link each role to a benefit. "Commercial banks lend to producers" is a role; "this lets firms buy machinery now and repay from future revenue, so output and jobs grow" shows its importance.

## Common mistakes

- Saying money must be made of something valuable. Most money today is notes and bank deposits, accepted because of trust.
- Listing functions with no example or explanation of the problem each solves.
- Judging an item against characteristics without giving reasons.
- Saying the central bank lends to households or firms. It deals with the government and the banks.
- Describing roles without explaining why they matter to the named group.

## Exam technique for 3.1

- **Define** money in terms of acceptability as a means of payment.
- **Identify** a function or characteristic from a short description: "prices of all goods are shown in dollars" is unit of account.
- **Explain** a function by naming the barter problem it solves.
- **Analyse** whether an item would make good money by working through the characteristics with reasons.
- **Discuss** the importance of a bank to a group: give the roles, show the benefit of each, and judge which matters most and when, for example the central bank's role becomes most visible in a crisis.
