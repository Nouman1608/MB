> **These are original questions written for Marlbridge** for revision and practice on Criterion D. They are **not** reproduced IB material and they do not copy the structure or mark tariffs of any on-screen examination. Real MYP work is judged against criterion level descriptors (achievement levels 1-8), so the `[n]` marks below are a revision aid, not IB marks.

Study first: the [Criterion D study guide](/resources/ib-myp-individuals-and-societies-criterion-d-thinking-critically/) and [revision notes](/resources/ib-myp-individuals-and-societies-criterion-d-thinking-critically-revision-notes/). Also: [IB MYP Individuals and Societies hub](/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-individuals-and-societies/) and [printable checklist](/checklists/ib/ib-myp/myp-individuals-and-societies/). The place, people and sources below are invented for practice.

## Sources

The investigation question is: *Should the proposed Karran Dam be built?*

> **Source A.** Extract from a press release issued by the Regional Energy Office, 2024.
> "The Karran Dam will supply clean electricity to 150 000 homes and create 800 jobs during construction. Flood protection will benefit the lower valley. Families who must move will receive new homes and compensation. The project is a milestone for the future of our region."

> **Source B.** Extract from a letter by Elena Marsh, a farmer in the Karran valley, to a local newspaper, 2024.
> "My family has farmed this land for four generations. The dam will drown our fields, and the compensation offered would not buy equivalent land. They talk about jobs, but most of them last two years. When the water rises, our community will be gone."

> **Source C.** Data from an independent environmental assessment, 2023.
>
> | Item | Figure |
> |---|---|
> | Farmland in the valley | 6000 hectares |
> | Farmland to be flooded | 1800 hectares |
> | Households in the valley | 9600 |
> | Households to be relocated | 1200 |
> | Homes supplied with electricity | 150 000 |

## Questions

**1.** (Criterion D) Identify the origin of Source A. **[2]**

**2.** (Criterion D) Identify the purpose of Source B. **[2]**

**3.** (Criterion D) Analyse the value and limitations of Source A for investigating the impact of the dam on local people. **[4]**

**4.** (Criterion D) Analyse the value and limitations of Source B for investigating the impact of the dam on local people. **[4]**

**5.** (Criterion D) Use Source C.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of the valley's farmland that would be flooded, and the percentage of households that would be relocated. **[2]**
**(b)** Interpret what these figures show, and state one limitation of the data. **[3]**

**6.** (Criterion D) Identify two different perspectives on the dam and interpret the implications of each. **[4]**

**7.** (Criterion D) Evaluate the reliability of Source A and Source B in relation to Source C. **[4]**

**8.** (Criterion D) Explain why Source A and Source B may disagree about the effects of the dam. **[3]**

**9.** (Criterion D) Using all three sources and your own knowledge of the issues involved in managing resources, discuss whether the Karran Dam should be built. Make a supported judgment. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Source A is from the Regional Energy Office [1], a press release from 2024, so it is an official statement from the organisation that is proposing the project [1]. **[2]**

**2.** To persuade the readers of the newspaper [1] to oppose the dam, or to protect the writer's land and community from it [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Value: it gives specific figures (150 000 homes, 800 jobs) and states the official benefits, so it shows what the project's supporters claim [1]. It reveals the plan for people who must move (new homes and compensation) [1]. Limitations: it is written by the organisation that wants the dam built, so it presents only benefits and no costs, such as the land lost [1]. It gives no evidence or figures for compensation or the number of people affected, so it is of limited use for local impact [1]. **[4]**

**4.** Value: it is a firsthand account from someone who would be directly affected [1], and it shows the personal and community impact, which official statements leave out [1]. Limitations: it is one person's view and not typical of all residents; some might welcome the dam or the compensation [1]. It is emotional, gives no data to support claims such as the cost of replacement land or the length of jobs, and the writer's interest (her farm) affects her view [1]. **[4]**

**5. (a)** Farmland: 1800 ÷ 6000 × 100 = **30%** [1]. Households: 1200 ÷ 9600 × 100 = **12.5%** [1].
**(b)** Nearly a third of farmland would be flooded and one household in eight would have to move, so the impact on the valley is large [1]. It supports Source B's claim that farming would be badly affected, and 150 000 homes would gain electricity [1]. Limitation (any one): the data does not show the quality of the farmland, how many people live in each household, the compensation offered or the effects on wildlife [1]. **[5]**

**6.** Any two perspectives, each with an implication (2 marks each).
- Regional Energy Office (supporter): wants clean electricity and economic growth [1]. Implication: if the dam is built, many homes gain power and there are construction jobs, but the office may play down the costs to families [1].
- Farmers and valley residents (opponents): value their land, livelihood and community [1]. Implication: they may lose land and community ties, and may resist the project or demand better compensation [1].
- Residents of the lower valley (flood protection): may favour the dam for safety [1]. Implication: they gain security, which may be at the cost of neighbours upstream [1].
**[4]**

**7.** Source A agrees with Source C on the number of homes supplied (150 000), which supports its claim about electricity [1]. But it leaves out the figures that Source C reveals, such as the 30% of farmland and 1200 households affected, which suggests it is selective [1]. Source B is supported by Source C, which confirms that a large amount of farmland would be lost, but Source B's claims about jobs and compensation are not checked by Source C [1]. Source C is independent and gives data, so it is the most reliable of the three, though it does not give reasons or the views of residents [1]. **[4]**

**8.** They have different origins and interests [1]. Source A is written by the organisation that wants the dam, so it stresses the benefits [1]. Source B is written by a farmer who would lose land, so she stresses the harm. Each selects the facts that support their position [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Award up to eight marks.
- A clear judgment that answers the question, for example: the dam should be built only if the compensation and relocation are improved [1].
- Evidence from all three sources used accurately, for example 150 000 homes, 800 jobs, 30% of farmland, 12.5% of households [2].
- Evaluation of source reliability (origin and purpose, value and limitations) linked to the argument [1].
- Discussion of at least two perspectives, with implications [1].
- Use of own knowledge or concepts, for example sustainability, scale, power or competition for resources: the long-term benefit of renewable energy against the loss of land and community; local costs against benefits for a wider region [2].
- A counter-view acknowledged and answered [1].
- Synthesis: a conclusion that combines the sources rather than summarising them separately [1].
Award a maximum of 8. **[8]**
*Marking note:* A response that accepts the official figures without evaluating the source, or that summarises each source in turn, cannot reach the top marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a source without evaluating its value and limitations.
- Saying "it is biased" with no explanation of effect.
- Ignoring the question when judging value (for example the impact on local people).
- Using one source only.
- Writing a one-sided answer to a "discuss" question.
- Summarising each source in turn, and not combining them.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I reach a different judgment in question 9?**
Yes. Any judgment is acceptable if it follows from evidence from the sources and is reasoned, with counter-views considered.

**Why should I use own knowledge as well as the sources?**
The guide says students show knowledge and understanding either from their course or from the source material, and discussion of concepts needs your own knowledge.

**How should I use a table such as Source C?**
Quote figures, calculate where useful, describe what they show and say what they do not show.
