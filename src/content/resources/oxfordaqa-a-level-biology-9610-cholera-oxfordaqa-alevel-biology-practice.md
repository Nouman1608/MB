## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Cholera is caused by a bacterium. Name the interface through which it enters the body and the way in which it causes disease. **[2]**

**2.** State the effect of the cholera toxin on epithelial cells of the small intestine. **[1]**

**3.** Which statement correctly describes water movement during cholera?

A: Water moves from the lumen into the cells because the lumen has a higher water potential.

B: Water moves from the cells into the lumen because the lumen has a lower water potential.

C: Water moves from the cells into the lumen by active transport.

D: Water moves into the lumen because the toxin damages the cell walls. **[1]**

**4.** State three substances found in a typical oral rehydration solution. **[3]**

**5.** Explain why a patient with cholera may have a reduced blood volume. **[2]**

**6.** Give two reasons why an ORS can be used in places with few hospitals. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** In an investigation, the water potential of intestinal epithelial cells was −700 kPa. The water potential of the lumen contents was −660 kPa in a healthy intestine and −790 kPa during cholera infection (illustrative values).

**(a)** State the direction of net water movement in each case. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how the toxin causes the change in the water potential of the lumen. **[3]**

**8.** Explain why an ORS containing glucose and sodium ions rehydrates a cholera patient more effectively than pure water. **[4]**

**9.** A health worker makes up an ORS using too little water, so the solution is much more concentrated than intended. Suggest and explain the effect on the patient's diarrhoea. **[3]**

**10.** Researchers developed a rice-based ORS, in which most of the glucose is replaced by rice starch. Suggest two advantages of this change. Explain each. **[4]**

**11.** A trial compared a standard ORS with a new ORS in children with acute watery diarrhoea.

| Outcome | Standard ORS (n = 80) | New ORS (n = 82) |
|---|---|---|
| Mean duration of diarrhoea (hours) | 61 | 52 |
| Children needing an intravenous drip | 12 | 7 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of children needing a drip in each group. Give your answers to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage reduction in mean duration of diarrhoea with the new ORS. **[1]**

**(c)** Give three pieces of further information you would need before concluding that the new ORS is better. **[3]**

**12.** Discuss the ethical implications of trialling an improved ORS on people with cholera. **[6]**

**13.** Explain why ORS reduces deaths from cholera, but does not on its own prevent cholera outbreaks. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The digestive system (gut), in contaminated water or food [1]. It produces a toxin [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Section 3.2.1 names toxin production and damage to host cells as the two ways pathogens cause disease. For cholera, the toxin is the answer.

**2.** It increases the secretion of chloride ions into the lumen [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* "Makes the cells lose salt" is too vague. Name chloride ions.

**3.** **B** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Osmosis is passive, and animal cells have no cell walls, which rules out C and D.

**4.** Water [1], glucose [1], sodium ions (or another named salt or ion, such as potassium or chloride) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Salts" alone counts once only; name ions for further credit.

**5.** Water moves out of the blood and tissues into the lumen by osmosis [1] and is lost in the diarrhoea faster than it is replaced [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Connect the water loss to the blood, not just to "the body".

**6.** Any two: it is cheap [1]; it is taken by mouth, so no trained staff or sterile equipment are needed for a drip [1]; it can be stored as dry sachets and made up at home. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Each reason should be a practical point, not "it works well".

**7. (a)** Healthy: from the lumen into the epithelial cells [1]. Cholera: from the epithelial cells into the lumen [1].

**(b)** The toxin increases secretion of chloride ions into the lumen [1]. Sodium ions follow, so the solute concentration in the lumen rises [1]. This lowers the water potential of the lumen below that of the cells (from −660 kPa to −790 kPa) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Water moves from higher (less negative) to lower (more negative) water potential. Check the signs before you answer part (a).

**8.** The toxin does not stop the sodium-glucose co-transporter [1]. Glucose and sodium ions are absorbed together into the epithelial cells [1]. This lowers the water potential inside the cells [1], so water moves from the lumen into the cells by osmosis and then into the blood; pure water provides no solutes to create this gradient [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The phrase "water follows the solutes" needs the water potential link to earn credit.

**9.** The diarrhoea would get worse (or would not improve) [1]. The concentrated solution lowers the water potential of the lumen [1], so more water moves from the cells into the lumen by osmosis [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* This is the reason developers worry about the total solute concentration of an ORS.

**10.** Starch is a large, insoluble or less soluble molecule, so it has little effect on the water potential of the lumen; less water is drawn into the gut [1] [1]. Starch is hydrolysed gradually by amylase and maltase, so glucose is released steadily at the epithelium where it can be co-transported with sodium ions [1] [1]. (Allow: rice is cheap and locally available, with the reason that this makes the ORS easier to provide.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each advantage needs its explanation. Two advantages without reasons score two marks at most.

**11. (a)** Standard: 12 ÷ 80 × 100 = **15.0%** [1]. New: 7 ÷ 82 × 100 = **8.5%** [1].

**(b)** (61 − 52) ÷ 61 × 100 = **14.8%** [1].

**(c)** Any three: whether children were allocated to groups at random [1]; whether the trial was blind, so those assessing outcomes did not know which ORS each child had [1]; whether the groups were similar in age and severity of illness [1]; whether a statistical test shows the differences are significant; spread of the data (for example standard deviation); whether the result has been repeated. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The groups are of different sizes, so compare percentages, not raw numbers.

**12.** Any six, with at least one point on each side for full marks:
- Informed consent is needed from the patient, or from a parent or guardian for a child [1].
- Participants must be free to withdraw at any time without losing treatment [1].
- A placebo or no-treatment control would be unethical because dehydration can be fatal, so the control group must receive the standard ORS [1].
- The new ORS might be less effective, so patients must be monitored and given intravenous fluids if they worsen [1].
- The trial should be approved by an independent ethics committee [1].
- Vulnerable people, for example during an outbreak or in poverty, must not be pressured or exploited [1].
- On the other hand, a trial is the only way to show that a new ORS saves more lives, so a well-designed trial is justified. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* "Discuss" needs balance. A list of rules with no judgement on whether the trial should go ahead rarely reaches full marks.

**13.** ORS replaces lost water and ions, so patients survive the dehydration [1]. It does not kill the bacteria or stop them being spread [1]. Outbreaks are prevented by stopping the bacteria reaching people, for example through clean water supplies and sanitation [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Separate treating symptoms from preventing transmission.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "salt" instead of naming chloride ions in the toxin's effect.
- Describing water moving to "less water" instead of lower water potential.
- Mixing up the sign of water potential values.
- Claiming ORS cures the infection.
- Suggesting a placebo group.
- Comparing raw numbers from groups of different sizes.
- One-sided "discuss" answers.
