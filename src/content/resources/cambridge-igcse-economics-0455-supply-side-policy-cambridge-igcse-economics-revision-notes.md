## Definition

**Supply-side policy:** government measures to increase the economy's productive capacity by improving the quantity, quality and efficiency of factors of production and the working of markets. Success shifts the PPC outwards.

| | Demand-side (fiscal, monetary) | Supply-side |
|---|---|---|
| Works on | Total spending | Productive capacity |
| Speed | Months | Often years |
| Main risk | Inflation if spending outruns capacity | Slow results; possible inequality |

## The six measures named in the syllabus

| Measure | What the government does | Chain to capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Education and training | Funds schools, colleges, apprenticeships, retraining | Skills ↑ → productivity ↑ → output per worker ↑ |
| Labour market reforms | Easier hiring; job information; help to move; fewer restrictive practices | Workers matched to jobs faster → unemployment ↓ → output ↑ |
| Lower direct taxes | Cut income tax and corporation tax | More reward for work and investment → more labour and capital |
| Deregulation | Remove rules that restrict entry and competition | More firms → competition ↑ → costs and prices ↓, efficiency ↑ |
| Improving incentives to work and invest | Keep part of benefits when starting work; investment allowances and grants | More people working; more capital bought |
| Privatisation | Sell state-owned firms to private owners | Profit motive → waste ↓, efficiency ↑ (if competition exists) |

## Calculations to practise

```
Gain from working = take-home pay - benefits lost
Tax saved by investment allowance = cost of investment x tax rate
Pay-back time of training = cost / yearly gain (plus any delay before gains start)
```

Worked reminder: benefits 150 a week; job pays 300 gross with 20% tax.

```
Take-home = 240; gain = 240 - 150 = 90 a week
```

Worked reminder: machine costs 50,000; corporation tax 20%; full allowance.

```
Tax saved = 50,000 x 0.20 = 10,000; effective cost 40,000
```

## Effects on the aims

| Aim | Help | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Economic growth | PPC shifts outwards | Needs demand to use new capacity; slow |
| Low unemployment | Skills match jobs; faster job search | Does not cure unemployment caused by weak demand |
| Stable prices | Lower unit costs; more output | Little effect on inflation from excess spending |
| Balance of payments | More competitive exports | Depends on foreign demand and exchange rate |
| Redistribution | Training lifts low earners | Tax cuts and benefit changes may widen inequality |

**Key evaluation point:** supply-side policy can ease conflicts between aims, because extra capacity lets the economy grow with less inflation and more competitive exports.

## Evaluation for each measure

- **Education and training:** slow; costly; skills must match employers' needs; trained workers may emigrate.
- **Labour market reforms:** less job security; may reduce workers' bargaining power and pay.
- **Lower direct taxes:** lost revenue; some may work less; gains often largest for high earners.
- **Deregulation:** removed rules may have protected safety, the environment or consumers.
- **Incentives:** benefit changes can increase poverty; grants may reward investment that would have happened anyway.
- **Privatisation:** a private monopoly may raise prices; unprofitable but useful services may close; sale revenue is one-off.

## Exam traps

- **Route matters.** A tax cut explained only through spending is demand-side.
- **Name the measure precisely.** Use the syllabus terms.
- **Time lags.** Say "in the long run" when discussing training or investment.
- **Privatisation needs competition** to deliver efficiency gains.
- **Lower direct taxes, not indirect.** A cut in sales tax is not on the syllabus list of supply-side measures.
- **Link to an aim.** End each chain with growth, unemployment, inflation, the balance of payments or redistribution.

## Self-test

1. Define supply-side policy.
2. List the six supply-side measures named in the syllabus.
3. How does education and training raise productive capacity?
4. Give one example of a labour market reform.
5. How can lower corporation tax increase investment?
6. Define deregulation.
7. Benefits are 120 a week; a job pays 200 gross with 15% tax. Calculate the gain from working.
8. A firm buys equipment for 30,000; corporation tax is 25%; the full cost is allowed against profit. Calculate the tax saved.
9. Why might privatisation fail to improve efficiency?
10. Why is supply-side policy slow to work?
11. How can supply-side policy help the balance of payments?
12. Give one way supply-side policy may conflict with redistribution of income.

### Answers

1. Government measures to increase productive capacity by improving the quantity, quality and efficiency of factors and markets.
2. Education and training; labour market reforms; lower direct taxes; deregulation; improving incentives to work and invest; privatisation.
3. Workers gain skills, so output per worker rises and the PPC shifts outwards.
4. Any one: easier hiring; job information services; help with moving costs; reducing restrictive practices.
5. Firms keep more profit to fund investment, and investment earns a higher return after tax.
6. Removing rules that restrict entry or competition in a market.
7. Take-home 200 x 0.85 = 170; gain = 170 - 120 = 50 a week.
8. 30,000 x 0.25 = 7,500.
9. If the firm becomes a private monopoly with no competition, it can raise prices instead of cutting costs.
10. Training, education and new investment take years to raise output.
11. Lower costs and better quality make exports more competitive.
12. Lower income tax and cuts in benefits give larger gains to higher earners and can increase poverty.
