These questions cover sub-topic 6.3, Flooding, of the 2027-2029 Cambridge O Level Environmental
Management (5014) syllabus. The contexts are a farming valley, a fast-growing riverside city and a coastal
town exposed to the sea.

## Section A

**1.** Define *compacted soil*. **[1]**

**2.** Name **two** causes of coastal flooding listed in the syllabus. **[2]**

**3.** State what is meant by *land use zoning*. **[1]**

**4.** Name the strategy in which people move to safe buildings on high ground during a flood. **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** The table shows land use in a river catchment of 80 km². *The data are invented for practice.*

| Land use | 1995 / km² | 2025 / km² |
|---|---|---|
| Forest | 48 | 20 |
| Farmland | 28 | 42 |
| Built-up | 4 | 18 |

(a) Calculate the percentage of the catchment that was forest in 1995 and in 2025. **[2]**
(b) Calculate the percentage increase in built-up area from 1995 to 2025. **[1]**
(c) Explain how the changes shown would increase the risk of flooding. **[4]**

**6.** In a farming valley, cattle are kept on the same fields all year and tractors work the land when it is
wet. Explain how these practices can increase flooding. **[3]**

**7.** Explain why a long period of rain followed by a short heavy storm is likely to cause a flood. **[2]**

**8.** A coastal town is protected from river flooding but has never prepared for coastal flooding. Explain
how climate change could increase the town's flood risk. **[3]**

**9.** Describe **three** impacts of a flood on the health of people in a city. **[3]**

**10.** Describe how monitoring and warning systems help reduce the impacts of river floods. **[3]**

**11.** Describe **two** ways the structure of a house can be improved to reduce flood damage. **[2]**

**12.** Explain **two** opportunities flooding provides to people living on a floodplain. **[4]**

## Section C

**13.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of evacuation and rebuilding of damaged areas as strategies for
managing the impacts of flooding. **[6]**

**14.** A government plans to ban all new building on a river's floodplain. Discuss whether this is a good
strategy. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Soil that has been squashed (for example by machinery or animals) so its pore spaces are reduced and
water infiltrates slowly. **[1]**

**2.** Any two: storm surges **[1]**; tsunamis **[1]**; rise in sea level (climate change) **[1]**. **[max 2]**

**3.** Planning rules that control what each area of land can be used for, for example keeping housing off
land at high risk of flooding. **[1]**

**4.** Shelters (accept evacuation to shelters). **[1]**

**5.** (a) 1995: 48 / 80 x 100 = **60%** **[1]**; 2025: 20 / 80 x 100 = **25%** **[1]**.
(b) (18 - 4) / 4 x 100 = 14 / 4 x 100 = **350%**. **[1]**
(c) Forest has fallen from 60% to 25%, so less rain is intercepted and taken up by roots **[1]**, and more
reaches the river quickly as run-off **[1]**. Built-up area has more than quadrupled, adding impermeable
surfaces where rain cannot infiltrate **[1]**; drains carry it straight to the river, which rises higher and
faster and overflows **[1]**. (Accept: more farmland may mean ploughing and compaction, increasing run-off.)

**6.** Cattle trampling and tractors on wet soil compact it **[1]**, closing the pore spaces so rain infiltrates
slowly **[1]**; more water runs off over the surface into the river, raising its level quickly (and overgrazed
bare soil is eroded, silting the channel) **[1]**.

**7.** The long rain saturates the soil, so there is no room for more water **[1]**; the heavy storm's rain
cannot infiltrate and runs straight into the river, which overflows **[1]**.

**8.** Rising sea level brings the sea closer to the height of the land **[1]**; more extreme weather events mean
stronger storms **[1]**, so storm surges are more likely to flood the town, which has no preparation **[1]**.

**9.** Any three: drowning or injuries from fast water and debris **[1]**; water-related disease from
contaminated drinking water **[1]**; illness from mould or cold in damp homes **[1]**; stress and mental
health effects from losing homes **[1]**; injuries during evacuation or clean-up **[1]**. **[max 3]**

**10.** River-level sensors and rain gauges measure rising water, and weather forecasts predict heavy rain
**[1]**; warnings are sent by phone, radio or sirens **[1]**, giving people time to evacuate, move belongings
upstairs and move livestock to higher ground **[1]**.

**11.** Any two: raised floor levels or building on stilts **[1]**; waterproof materials or flood-resistant
doors **[1]**; electrical sockets and wiring placed high on walls **[1]**. **[max 2]**

**12.** Two explained opportunities, two marks each. Floodwater deposits nutrient-rich silt on farmland
**[1]**, renewing soil fertility so crops grow well with less fertiliser **[1]**. Floods recharge surface water
and ground water stores **[1]**, refilling lakes, wetlands and aquifers that supply water for drinking and
irrigation in the dry season **[1]**.

**13.** **[6]** One mark per point, up to six. Evacuation: moves people and livestock away before or during a
flood, saving lives **[1]**; river floods often give warning time **[1]**; but flash floods may give no time,
roads may already be flooded, and some people refuse to leave **[1]**; evacuees need shelters and supplies
**[1]**. Rebuilding: restores homes, roads and services so the economy recovers **[1]**; can include
flood-resistant designs **[1]**; but it is slow and costly, and may put buildings back in the same risky place
**[1]**. Conclusion: evacuation saves lives; rebuilding reduces long-term losses only if it reduces future risk
**[1]**. **[max 6]**

**14.** **[6]** One mark per point, up to six. For: fewer new homes and people exposed to floods **[1]**; cheap
compared with building defences or rebuilding **[1]**; the floodplain can still be farmed, keeping the benefit
of silt **[1]**. Against: land may be scarce, so housing must go elsewhere at higher cost **[1]**; existing
buildings remain at risk, so other strategies are still needed **[1]**; people may build informally anyway if
the ban is not enforced **[1]**. Conclusion: a good strategy for new development if enforced and combined
with warnings and improved building structure for existing homes **[1]**. **[max 6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating percentage change by dividing by the new value.
- Explaining run-off without naming infiltration.
- Forgetting that floods also bring benefits.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are dams and levees part of 6.3?**
The 6.3.3 list does not name them. It lists monitoring and warning, land use zoning, structure of buildings,
disaster preparation, evacuation, shelters, rebuilding and international aid. Dams and reservoirs appear in
6.4 as a way to increase water supply in a drought.

**What are the two flood opportunities?**
Deposition of nutrient-rich silt on farmland, and recharge of surface water and ground water stores.
