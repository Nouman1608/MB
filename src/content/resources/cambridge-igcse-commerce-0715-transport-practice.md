## Section A - short questions

**1.** A supplier delivers goods the day before a shop's sale begins. Which function of transport does this show? **[1]**
A place   B quantity   C time   D security

**2.** Which method of transport is most suitable for moving crude oil over a long distance on land? **[1]**
A freight train   B pipeline   C courier   D freight plane

**3.** A ship that sails a fixed route on a timetable and carries mixed cargo is a: **[1]**
A tanker   B ferry   C cargo liner   D container

**4.** State **two** methods of road transport named in the syllabus. **[2]**

**5.** Define transshipment. **[2]**

**6.** Identify **two** disadvantages of air freight. **[2]**

## Section B - structured questions

**7.** *Fresh Bay Ltd catches and packs prawns. It sells to restaurants 900 km away.*
Explain **two** factors that Fresh Bay Ltd should consider when choosing a method of transport. **[4]**

**8.** Explain **three** advantages of sending goods by container ship. **[6]**

**9.** Describe the three functions of transport in the supply chain. **[6]**

**10.** *Stone Works exports 30 tonnes of floor tiles. Road and ferry costs $70 per tonne. Container ship costs $45 per tonne plus a one-off handling fee of $240.*
(a) Calculate the total cost of each method. **[3]**
(b) Calculate how much Stone Works saves by choosing the cheaper method. **[1]**
(c) Advise Stone Works which method to use, giving **two** reasons. **[3]**

**11.** *Hana Textiles sends silk scarves worth a high price per item to a boutique in another country. The boutique wants them within two days.*
(a) State **one** method of transport suitable for this order. **[1]**
(b) Justify your choice using **two** of the factors that affect the choice of transport. **[4]**
(c) Identify **one** other method that would be unsuitable and explain why. **[2]**

**12.** Discuss whether containerisation is of more benefit to a large exporter than to a small business. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** C. Goods arrive when they are needed, which is the time function. **[1]**

**2.** B. A pipeline carries liquids and gases directly from source to destination. **[1]**

**3.** C. A cargo liner follows a timetable on a fixed route. **[1]**

**4.** Any two: delivery vans [1]; trucks [1]; couriers [1]. **[max 2]**

**5.** Moving a container from one ship, train or truck to another [1] during its journey, with the goods staying inside the container [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two: most expensive method per tonne [1]; limited size and weight of load [1]; airports are often far from the buyer, so road transport is also needed [1]; weather can delay or cancel flights [1]; high fuel use and pollution [1]. **[max 2]**

**7.** Any two factors, 2 marks each (factor plus link to prawns):
Nature of goods: prawns are perishable [1], so they need a fast, refrigerated method such as a refrigerated truck [1].
Urgency: restaurants need fresh stock for each day's menu [1], so a slow method such as sea freight would spoil the goods [1].
Distance: 900 km is a long road journey [1], so the firm should compare the time taken by road and by air [1].
Cost: prawns have a limited price per box [1], so air freight may cost more than the firm can recover in its selling price [1].
Reliability: a late delivery loses restaurant customers [1], so a dependable carrier is worth a higher fee [1]. **[max 4]**

**8.** Any three, 2 marks each (advantage plus explanation):
Large loads at low cost: ships carry thousands of containers, so the cost per tonne is low [2].
Protection: the container is sealed, which protects the goods from weather and theft [2].
Fast handling: cranes move containers quickly, so ships spend less time in port [2].
Lower handling costs: the goods are packed once and are not unpacked until they arrive, so less labour is needed [2].
Easy transfer: standard boxes move between truck, train and ship without being opened [2].
Global reach: container ships serve most major ports, which makes international trade possible [2]. **[max 6]**

**9.** Time: goods are delivered when they are needed, so shops do not run out and perishable goods stay fresh [2]. Place: goods are moved from where they are made to where customers can buy them, for example from a factory to a distant shop [2]. Quantity: goods are moved in the amounts needed, such as a truckload to a wholesaler and a small van load to a retailer [2]. (1 mark for each function named, 1 mark for each description or example.) **[6]**

**10.** (a) Road and ferry: 30 x 70 = **$2,100** [1]. Container ship: 30 x 45 = 1,350 [1], plus 240 = **$1,590** [1]. (3 marks)
(b) Saving = 2,100 - 1,590 = **$510** [1]. (1 mark)
(c) Recommend the container ship [1]. Reasons, any two: $510 cheaper [1]; tiles are heavy and not perishable, so a slow method is acceptable [1]; the container protects the tiles from damage and theft [1]. (3 marks) **[7]**

**11.** (a) Air freight (or a courier using air freight) [1].
(b) Any two factors, 2 marks each: urgency, because two days is too short for sea or rail [2]; value of goods and security, because high-value scarves justify a higher cost and air freight is secure [2]; distance, because the boutique is in another country, so air is the fastest way to cover it [2]; cost, because the high price per item means the freight cost is a small part of the value [2]. (max 4 marks)
(c) Sea freight (or a cargo liner or container ship) [1], because it is too slow to meet a two-day deadline [1]. Accept pipeline, because it cannot carry scarves, for 2 marks. **[7]**

**12.** Up to 2 for benefits to a large exporter, up to 2 for the position of a small business, up to 2 for a justified conclusion.
Large exporter: it can fill whole containers, so cost per unit is low [1]; regular shipments make the investment in handling and tracking worthwhile [1]; it can negotiate lower rates with shipping lines [1].
Small business: it may not fill a container, so it pays for empty space or has to share a container with others [1]; the cost of handling and returning containers is a larger share of its income [1]; it still benefits from protection against theft and damage [1].
Conclusion: for example, large exporters benefit more because of volume, but small businesses can use shared containers to gain similar protection [2]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Explaining a factor in general terms without linking it to the goods in the question.
- Giving the advantages of a method when the question asks for disadvantages.
- Confusing transshipment with containerisation.
- Choosing air freight for every situation, ignoring cost and the nature of the goods.
- In calculations, forgetting the one-off fee.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the three functions of transport?** Time, place and quantity.

**Is transshipment the same as containerisation?** No. Containerisation is the use of standard boxes; transshipment is moving a container from one vehicle or ship to another.
