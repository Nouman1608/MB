These notes condense section **3.1.4.4 Bond enthalpies** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Papers 1 and 2 (and Paper 3). The [Bond enthalpies study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-bond-enthalpies/) explains each calculation with worked examples, and the [Bond enthalpies practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-bond-enthalpies-practice/) give exam-style practice.

## What the specification asks

- Define **mean bond enthalpy**.
- Use mean bond enthalpies to calculate an **approximate** ΔH for reactions in the **gaseous phase**.
- **Explain why** values from mean bond enthalpy calculations differ from those determined using Hess's law.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mean bond enthalpy | The enthalpy change when one mole of a given type of bond is broken in the gas phase, averaged over a range of compounds |
| Bond breaking | Endothermic: energy is absorbed |
| Bond making | Exothermic: energy is released |
| Gaseous phase | All reactants and products are gases |
| Approximate | Not exact, because mean values are used |

## Formula and method

```
ΔH = Σ(bonds broken) − Σ(bonds formed)
```

1. Draw displayed formulae.
2. List bonds broken in reactants; list bonds formed in products.
3. Multiply each value by the number of bonds, using the numbers in the balanced equation.
4. Subtract: broken minus formed.
5. Check sign and unit: kJ mol⁻¹.

If bonds broken exceed bonds formed (in energy), ΔH is positive (endothermic). If formed exceed broken, ΔH is negative (exothermic).

## Typical data (kJ mol⁻¹)

| Bond | C–H | C–C | C=C | C–O | C=O | O–H | O=O | H–H | N–H | N≡N | Cl–Cl | H–Cl | C–Cl |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Value | 413 | 347 | 612 | 358 | 805 | 464 | 498 | 436 | 391 | 945 | 243 | 432 | 346 |

Data are given in the exam, so there is no need to learn them.

## Worked reminders

**Methane burning (to steam).** CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O(g).
Broken: 4(413) + 2(498) = 2648. Formed: 2(805) + 4(464) = 3466.
ΔH = 2648 − 3466 = **−818 kJ mol⁻¹**.

**Hydrogenation.** C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆.
Broken: 612 + 436 = 1048. Formed: 347 + 2(413) = 1173.
ΔH = **−125 kJ mol⁻¹**.

**Hydrogen chloride.** H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl.
Broken: 436 + 243 = 679. Formed: 2(432) = 864.
ΔH = **−185 kJ mol⁻¹**.

**Addition of HCl.** C₂H₄ + HCl → C₂H₅Cl.
Broken: C=C 612 + H–Cl 432 = 1044. Formed: C–C 347 + C–H 413 + C–Cl 346 = 1106.
ΔH = **−62 kJ mol⁻¹**.

**Finding a bond.** N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃, ΔH = −92. Broken: 945 + 3(436) = 2253. Formed: 6x.
2253 − 6x = −92, so x = 2345 ÷ 6 = **391 kJ mol⁻¹** (mean N–H).

**Propane to steam.** C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O(g). Propane has 8 C–H and 2 C–C bonds.
Broken: 8(413) + 2(347) + 5(498) = 6488. Formed: 6(805) + 8(464) = 8542.
ΔH = **−2054 kJ mol⁻¹**.

## Why answers differ from Hess's law values

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Mean values | A bond's enthalpy varies slightly from compound to compound, so the mean is not exact |
| Gas phase | Bond enthalpies take no account of the state of liquids or solids; the water produced is steam, not liquid |
| Data | Hess's law uses measured values for the actual compounds |

Example: burning methane gives −818 by bond enthalpies (steam). The data-book value (liquid water) is −890. For steam, the value would be −890 + 88 = −802 kJ mol⁻¹, so the two are close once the state is the same.

## Definition checklist

- "one mole of a given type of bond"
- "broken"
- "in the gaseous state" (or "gas phase")
- "averaged (mean) over a range of different compounds"

## Common mistakes

- Formed minus broken instead of broken minus formed.
- Forgetting to multiply by the number of bonds, for example 6 N–H bonds in 2NH₃.
- Missing a bond in the displayed formula.
- Treating a C=C double bond as two C–C bonds.
- Using bond enthalpies for a reaction involving liquids without comment.
- Omitting "gaseous" from the definition.
- Leaving out the sign or unit.

## Self-test

1. Define mean bond enthalpy.
2. State whether bond breaking is exothermic or endothermic.
3. Calculate ΔH for 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(g), using H–H 436, O=O 498, O–H 464.
4. Calculate ΔH for CH₂=CH₂ + HCl → CH₃CH₂Cl using C=C 612, H–Cl 432, C–C 347, C–H 413, C–Cl 346.
5. Calculate ΔH for H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl using H–H 436, Cl–Cl 243, H–Cl 432.
6. For N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃, ΔH = −92 kJ mol⁻¹. Calculate the mean N–H bond enthalpy (N≡N 945, H–H 436).
7. Why are the answers from bond enthalpies described as approximate?
8. State one reason why a bond enthalpy calculation for burning ethanol(l) differs from the Hess's law value.
9. Explain why the value for burning methane to steam is less negative than the value to liquid water.
10. How many N–H bonds are formed in the equation N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃?
11. Write the expression for ΔH in terms of bonds broken and bonds formed.
12. A reaction has bonds broken totalling 1800 kJ and bonds formed totalling 2100 kJ. State ΔH and say whether the reaction is exothermic.

### Answers

1. The enthalpy change when one mole of a given type of bond is broken in the gas phase, averaged over a range of compounds.
2. Endothermic.
3. Broken: 2(436) + 498 = 1370. Formed: 4(464) = 1856. ΔH = **−486 kJ mol⁻¹**.
4. Broken: 612 + 432 = 1044. Formed: 347 + 413 + 346 = 1106. ΔH = **−62 kJ mol⁻¹**.
5. 679 − 864 = **−185 kJ mol⁻¹**.
6. 2253 − 6x = −92, so x = **391 kJ mol⁻¹**.
7. They use mean values, which are averages over many compounds and not exact for the molecules in the reaction.
8. Bond enthalpies apply to gaseous species, but ethanol and water are liquids in the data-book value. The mean values also differ from the true values.
9. Condensing steam to liquid water releases extra energy (about 44 kJ per mole of water).
10. Six.
11. ΔH = Σ(bond enthalpies of bonds broken) − Σ(bond enthalpies of bonds formed).
12. ΔH = 1800 − 2100 = **−300 kJ mol⁻¹**; the reaction is exothermic.
