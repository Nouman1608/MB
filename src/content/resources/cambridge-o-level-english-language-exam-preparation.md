Cambridge O Level English Language (1123) is assessed by two externally assessed written papers, with no coursework or speaking component, and it is not tiered. Paper 1 Reading lasts 2 hours, carries 50 marks and is worth 50%. Paper 2 Writing also lasts 2 hours, carries 50 marks and is worth 50%. The two papers are weighted equally, so reading and writing deserve equal preparation. Pair this page with the study guides for [Paper 1 Reading](/resources/o-level-english-language-paper-1-reading/) and [Paper 2 Writing](/resources/o-level-english-language-paper-2-writing/), and the [overall revision notes](/resources/o-level-english-language-revision-notes/).

## Paper 1 Reading

According to the verified record, Paper 1 has two compulsory sections based on two reading texts: Comprehension and Use of Language, and Summary and Short response. The paper is 2 hours for 50 marks.

Because every section is compulsory, you cannot avoid a weaker skill. Practise reading unfamiliar non-fiction and fiction extracts under time conditions. For comprehension, answer in your own words where the question asks for it, and use the text as evidence where the question asks you to select or quote. For use of language, concentrate on how writers choose words for effect, and explain the effect rather than only naming a technique. For the summary and short response work, practise selecting the relevant points, discarding examples and repetition, and writing concisely and fluently in continuous prose.

Read each text actively: note the writer's purpose, attitude and audience on a first read, then return to each question with the relevant lines marked. Keep a small number of minutes aside for rereading your answers for accuracy of expression, since the paper also rewards the quality of your English.

## Paper 2 Writing

Paper 2 has one Directed Writing question and one Composition task chosen from four options, and is again 2 hours for 50 marks. Directed Writing asks you to write in a specified form for a specified audience, drawing on material you are given. Composition offers a choice of four tasks, so the skill is picking the one that best matches your strengths and planning it quickly.

For Directed Writing, identify the form (for example a letter, report, speech or article), the audience and the purpose, then match register accordingly. Use the source material, but adapt and develop it rather than copying. For Composition, choose a task for which you have clear ideas and control of structure, and plan a beginning, development and ending before drafting. Accuracy matters throughout: sentence structure, punctuation, spelling and paragraphing are all part of writing well. The [Paper 2 Writing revision notes](/resources/o-level-english-paper-2-writing-revision-notes/) are useful for practising planning.

## Timing and marks per minute

Both papers share the same arithmetic: 120 minutes for 50 marks is 120 / 50 = 2.4 minutes per mark. For Paper 1, that does not mean 2.4 minutes for each small point, because longer responses such as the summary carry larger mark totals; allocate time in proportion to the marks printed beside each question. For Paper 2, the record does not state how marks divide between the two tasks, so check the printed marks on the paper and split time in proportion, leaving 10 to 15 minutes for planning and 10 minutes for proofreading across the paper.

The weightings are 50% + 50% = 100%. A realistic revision balance is therefore equal time on reading and writing, with regular timed practice of each.

## Command words and question styles

In reading papers, expect verbs such as explain, identify, select, quote, summarise and comment on the effect of. Explain asks for meaning in your own words. Comment on the effect requires you to name the effect on the reader and link it to a specific word or phrase. In writing papers, the key terms are form, audience and purpose. A response that is technically accurate but written in the wrong register for the audience will lose credit, so check the instruction before drafting.

## Worked practice scenario

Task (original): You are the student president. Write a speech to your classmates persuading them to take part in a weekend neighbourhood clean-up. Use the facts given: 40 volunteers are needed, and the work takes three hours on Saturday morning.

**Outline plan.**

1. Opening: a direct address and a vivid image of the neglected park.
2. Reasons to take part: pride in the area, practical benefit, friendship.
3. Answer the likely objection: the time needed is modest, three hours.
4. Call to action: the figure of 40 volunteers and how to sign up.
5. Memorable closing line.

**Model paragraph.**

"Friends, we walk past that park every morning, and every morning it looks a little sadder. Fifteen minutes' work from each of us would barely be noticed, yet three hours on Saturday, shared between forty of us, can bring it back to life. Nobody is asking for a sacrifice, only a morning. So when you leave this hall today, put your name on the list, and let us make our neighbourhood something to be proud of."

The paragraph addresses the audience directly, uses persuasive devices sparingly (a vivid image, a contrast between small effort and large result, a direct request) and keeps the register appropriate for a speech to peers.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm both papers are two hours and that there is no coursework.
- Practise one reading paper and one writing paper under timed conditions each week.
- Build a bank of precise vocabulary for describing effect and tone.
- Learn the conventions of the common directed-writing forms.
- During reading, read both texts before answering anything.
- During writing, plan for several minutes before drafting.
- Keep to the form, audience and purpose throughout.
- Reserve time to proofread spelling, punctuation and sentence boundaries.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge O Level English Language 1123?**
Two: Paper 1 Reading and Paper 2 Writing, both externally assessed, with no coursework or speaking component.

**How long and how many marks is each paper?**
Each is 2 hours and 50 marks, and each is worth 50%.

**What is in Paper 2?**
One Directed Writing question and one Composition task chosen from four options.

**Is it tiered?**
No. It is not tiered, and the grade range is A* to E.
