Related pages: the [practice questions](/resources/edexcel-ial-mathematics-newtons-laws-connected-particles-inclined-planes-and-practice/), the [Edexcel mathematics hub](/boards/edexcel/a-level/mathematics/), the [printable checklist](/checklists/edexcel/a-level/mathematics/) and the [all free 10-minute diagnostics](/diagnostics/). Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² unless a question says otherwise.

## Key results

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| F = ma | Resultant force = mass × acceleration, same direction |
| W = mg | Weight acts vertically downwards |
| F = μR | Friction on a moving particle, opposing sliding |
| a = F/m with F = ai + bj | Add vector forces, then divide by the mass |
| mg sin α | Weight component down a slope at angle α |
| mg cos α | Weight component into the slope, so R = mg cos α when nothing else pushes perpendicular to the plane |

Constant acceleration formulae, which the specification lists as formulae you are expected to know:

```
v = u + at          s = ut + ½at²
s = vt − ½at²       v² = u² + 2as       s = ½(u + v)t
```

## Models and assumptions (4.2)

| Term | What you may assume |
|---|---|
| Particle | Mass but no size, so no turning effects |
| Light string | No mass, so tension is the same along it |
| Inextensible string | Does not stretch, so both particles have the same speed and acceleration size |
| Smooth pulley or peg | No friction, so tension is the same on both sides |
| Smooth surface | Friction is zero |
| Rough surface | Friction F = μR while sliding |

## Method boxes

### Vector dynamics (4.1)

1. Add the forces component by component.
2. Divide by the mass to get a = (ai + bj).
3. Use v = u + at and s = ut + ½at² with vectors, or find a magnitude with √(a² + b²).
4. Give a direction as an angle to i or j, with a diagram if it helps.

### Connected particles (4.2 i)

1. Draw each particle with its own forces. Mark the same tension on both sides of a smooth pulley.
2. Choose the direction of motion as positive for each particle.
3. Write one F = ma equation per particle.
4. Add the equations to remove T and find a, then substitute back for T.
5. Check T with the second particle.

For two masses M and m with M the heavier:

| Set-up | Acceleration | Tension |
|---|---|---|
| Both hang over a smooth pulley | (M − m)g/(M + m) | 2Mmg/(M + m) |
| Smooth table, M on the table, m hanging | mg/(M + m) | Mmg/(M + m) |

Use these only to check a worked answer. Show the equations in the exam.

### Force changes (4.2 ii)

1. Stage 1: find a, then the speed when the particle lands or the string goes slack.
2. Stage 2: remove the tension. Recalculate the acceleration from the forces that are left.
3. Use the speed at the end of stage 1 as u for stage 2.

### Inclined planes (4.2 iii) and friction (4.4)

1. Draw weight, R, friction and any other forces.
2. Resolve perpendicular to the plane: R = mg cos α (add or subtract other perpendicular components).
3. Find F = μR.
4. Resolve along the plane: sliding down, friction acts up; moving up, friction acts down.

| Motion | Equation along the slope |
|---|---|
| Down a smooth plane | ma = mg sin α |
| Down a rough plane | ma = mg sin α − μmg cos α |
| Up a smooth plane (deceleration) | ma = −mg sin α |
| Up a rough plane (deceleration) | ma = −mg sin α − μmg cos α |

## Worked reminders

**Reminder 1.** A 2 kg block is sliding at 5 m s⁻¹ on a rough horizontal surface, μ = 0.2, with no other horizontal force. Find the distance and the time to stop.

```
R = 2g, F = 0.2 × 2g, so 2a = −0.2 × 2g
a = −1.96 m s⁻²
0 = 25 − 2 × 1.96 × s, so s = 6.38 m
0 = 5 − 1.96t, so t = 2.55 s
```

**Reminder 2.** Masses 5 kg and 3 kg hang over a smooth pulley. Equations: 5g − T = 5a and T − 3g = 3a. Adding gives 2g = 8a, so a = 2.45 m s⁻², and T = 3(g + a) = 36.75 N.

Now suppose the 5 kg particle starts 0.8 m above the ground and lands. Its speed is given by v² = 2 × 2.45 × 0.8 = 3.92. The string then goes slack, and the 3 kg particle decelerates at g, so it rises a further 3.92/(2 × 9.8) = 0.2 m before stopping.

**Reminder 3.** A particle is released from rest on a rough plane at 25° to the horizontal, μ = 0.15, and slides 2 m down the line of greatest slope.

```
R = mg cos 25°, so F = 0.15mg cos 25°
a = g(sin 25° − 0.15 cos 25°) = 2.81 m s⁻²
v² = 2 × 2.809 × 2, so v = 3.35 m s⁻¹
2 = ½ × 2.809 × t², so t = 1.19 s
```

Mass cancels on a slope when only gravity, R and friction act. If a question gives the mass, you still do not need it for a.

## Must-know distinctions

- Mass is in kg and does not change. Weight is in N and equals mg.
- Normal reaction is perpendicular to the surface. Friction is along it.
- On a horizontal surface with no vertical forces other than weight, R = mg. On a slope, R = mg cos α.
- Tension pulls on both particles it joins. Thrust in a rod pushes. If an answer for T comes out negative in a rod, it is a thrust.
- Speed is a magnitude. Velocity and acceleration in ai + bj form have direction. Find the speed as √(a² + b²).
- A smooth surface has no friction. A rough surface has F = μR only while the particle is moving. Once it stops, there is no sliding friction to use in this unit.

## Quick self-test

1. A particle of mass 5 kg is acted on by a single force (10i − 15j) N. Find its acceleration as a vector and its magnitude.
2. Find the weight of a 12 kg object.
3. Masses 8 kg and 2 kg hang from the ends of a string over a smooth pulley. Find the acceleration and the tension.
4. Find the acceleration of a particle released on a smooth plane at 40° to the horizontal.
5. A 10 kg box is pushed along rough horizontal ground by a horizontal force of 30 N. The coefficient of friction is 0.15. Find the acceleration.
6. A particle slides down a rough plane at 20° to the horizontal, with μ = 0.3. Find its acceleration.
7. The particle in question 6 is now projected up the same plane. Find its deceleration.
8. A particle slides on rough horizontal ground, μ = 0.25, at 7 m s⁻¹ with no other horizontal force. Find the distance to rest.
9. A particle of mass 3 kg lies on a smooth horizontal table. It is joined by a string over a smooth pulley to a 1 kg particle hanging freely. Find the acceleration and the tension.
10. A particle of mass 3 kg has acceleration (2i + j) m s⁻². One force is (4i − j) N and the only other force is F. Find F.

### Answers

1. a = (10i − 15j)/5 = (2i − 3j) m s⁻²; magnitude √13 = 3.61 m s⁻².
2. 12 × 9.8 = 117.6 N.
3. 8g − T = 8a and T − 2g = 2a, so 6g = 10a, a = 5.88 m s⁻². T = 2(9.8 + 5.88) = 31.4 N.
4. a = g sin 40° = 6.30 m s⁻².
5. R = 98 N, F = 14.7 N, 10a = 30 − 14.7, so a = 1.53 m s⁻².
6. a = g(sin 20° − 0.3 cos 20°) = 0.589 m s⁻².
7. Deceleration = g(sin 20° + 0.3 cos 20°) = 6.11 m s⁻².
8. a = −0.25g = −2.45, so 0 = 49 − 2 × 2.45 × s, s = 10 m.
9. 1g − T = 1a and T = 3a, so a = g/4 = 2.45 m s⁻² and T = 7.35 N.
10. Total force = 3(2i + j) = (6i + 3j) N, so F = (6i + 3j) − (4i − j) = (2i + 4j) N.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing R = mg on an inclined plane.
- Putting friction in the wrong direction. It opposes the motion, not the weight component.
- Using one equation for two particles that have different forces, instead of one equation per particle.
- Writing 2T on the left of a pulley equation. A smooth pulley gives the same tension on each side, not double.
- Keeping the tension in stage 2 after a particle has landed.
- Mixing signs: a particle moving up should have T − mg = ma, and a particle moving down should have mg − T = ma.
- Rounding too early. Carry full values through the working and round the final answer, using the value of g the question gives.
- Stating an answer as a magnitude when the question asks for a vector, or the reverse.
- Forgetting units, or leaving an acceleration negative without saying what the sign means.
