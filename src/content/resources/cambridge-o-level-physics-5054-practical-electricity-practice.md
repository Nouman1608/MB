## Section A

**1.** State **two** common uses of electricity in the home. **[2]**

**2.** Define the *kilowatt-hour* and state its value in joules. **[2]**

**3.** A hair dryer has a power of 1.2 kW and runs on a 230 V supply. Calculate the current it takes. **[2]**

**4.** An appliance takes 4.0 A from a 230 V supply for 2.0 minutes. Calculate the energy transferred. **[2]**

**5.** State the danger of **(a)** damaged insulation on a mains cable, and **(b)** plugging too many appliances into one extension lead. **[2]**

**6.** Name the three wires in a mains circuit, and state which one carries a current only when there is a fault. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The lights in a house are connected in parallel on a 230 V circuit.

**(a)** State **three** advantages of connecting the lamps in parallel rather than in series. **[3]**
**(b)** Eight 60 W lamps are switched on together. Calculate the total power and the current from the supply. **[2]**
**(c)** Trip switches with settings of 3 A and 5 A are available. State which is more suitable for this circuit. **[1]**

**8.** In one day a family uses a 2.2 kW washing machine for 1.5 hours, a 3.0 kW oven for 40 minutes and a 120 W television for 5.0 hours. Electricity costs $0.18 per kW h.

**(a)** Calculate the energy, in kW h, used by each appliance. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the total energy used and the total cost. **[2]**

**9.** Fuses rated at 3 A, 5 A and 13 A are available. The supply is 230 V.

**(a)** Choose a fuse for an 800 W microwave oven, showing your working. **[2]**
**(b)** Choose a fuse for a 2.9 kW kettle, showing your working. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why fitting a 13 A fuse to the microwave oven would be unsafe. **[2]**

**10.** A washing machine has a metal case connected to the earth wire. A fault causes the live wire to touch the case. Explain how the earth wire and the fuse together prevent an electric shock. **[4]**

**11.** A hair dryer has a plastic case and is connected with only live and neutral wires. Explain why it does not need an earth wire. **[2]**

**12.** An electrician wrongly connects a light switch into the neutral wire instead of the live wire. The light still turns on and off. Explain why this wiring is dangerous. **[3]**

**13.** **(a)** Describe how a fuse protects a circuit. **[2]**
**(b)** Give **two** advantages of a trip switch (circuit breaker) over a fuse. **[2]**

**14.** Explain why overloading an extension lead with several high-power appliances is a fire hazard. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, 1 mark each: **heating** (kettle, heater); **lighting**; **charging batteries**; **powering motors** (fan, washing machine); **powering electronic systems** (computer, television).

**2.** The **energy transferred by a 1 kW appliance in 1 hour** [1]; 1 kW h = **3.6 × 10⁶ J** [1].

**3.** I = P ÷ V = 1200 ÷ 230 [1] = **5.2 A** [1].

**4.** E = I V t = 4.0 × 230 × 120 [1] = **110 000 J** (1.1 × 10⁵ J) [1].

**5. (a)** Exposed live wire can give an **electric shock** (or cause a short circuit and fire) [1].
**(b)** Excess current makes the **cables overheat**, which can cause a **fire** [1].

**6.** **Live, neutral and earth** [1]; the **earth** wire carries current only in a fault [1].

**7. (a)** Each lamp has the **full supply p.d. and works at normal brightness** [1]; each lamp can be **switched on and off independently** [1]; if **one lamp fails the others stay on** [1].
**(b)** P = 8 × 60 = **480 W** [1]; I = 480 ÷ 230 = **2.1 A** [1].
**(c)** **3 A** — the smallest setting above the working current [1].

**8. (a)** Washing machine: 2.2 × 1.5 = **3.3 kW h** [1]. Oven: 3.0 × (40 ÷ 60) = **2.0 kW h** [1]. Television: 0.12 × 5.0 = **0.60 kW h** [1].
**(b)** Total = 3.3 + 2.0 + 0.60 = **5.9 kW h** [1]; cost = 5.9 × 0.18 = **$1.06** [1].

**9. (a)** I = 800 ÷ 230 = 3.5 A [1]; choose the **5 A** fuse [1].
**(b)** I = 2900 ÷ 230 = 12.6 A [1]; choose the **13 A** fuse [1].
**(c)** A fault current well above the normal 3.5 A, but below 13 A, **would not melt the fuse** [1], so the **cable or appliance could overheat and catch fire** [1].

**10.** The live wire touching the case gives a **low-resistance path from live through the case to earth** [1], so a **very large current flows** [1]. This **melts the fuse** (in the live wire) [1], **disconnecting the appliance from the live supply**, so the case does not stay live and anyone touching it is safe [1].

**11.** The case is **non-conducting (double-insulated)** [1], so even if a live wire came loose inside, the **outer case could not become live** [1].

**12.** With the switch in the neutral wire, turning it off stops the current but **leaves the lamp connected to the live wire** [1]. The lamp fitting therefore stays at a **high p.d.** even when the light is off [1], so someone changing the bulb or touching the fitting could get an **electric shock** [1].

**13. (a)** The fuse wire **heats up and melts if the current exceeds its rating** [1], **breaking the circuit** and stopping the current before cables overheat [1].
**(b)** It **can be reset** once the fault is cleared, instead of being replaced [1]; it **acts faster** when the current becomes too large [1].

**14.** Each appliance draws current through the lead, so the **total current is very large** [1]. A large current **heats the cable and connections** [1]; the **insulation can melt and the heat can start a fire** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing units: kW with minutes, or W with hours, in energy and cost calculations.
- Choosing a fuse equal to or below the working current, or far above it.
- Explaining earthing without stating that the large current melts the fuse or trips the breaker.
- Saying the earth wire carries current all the time.
- Giving "so the light works" as the reason for a switch in the live wire, instead of disconnecting the appliance from the high p.d.
- Saying parallel lamps share the supply p.d.; each has the full p.d.
