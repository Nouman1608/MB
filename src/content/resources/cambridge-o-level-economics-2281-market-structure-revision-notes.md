Quick recall for section 3.8 **Market structure** of the Cambridge O Level Economics 2281 syllabus for examination in 2026: competitive markets ("the effect of having a high number of firms on price, quality, choice, profit") and monopoly markets ("characteristics, advantages and disadvantages of monopoly"). The syllabus says diagrams and the theory of perfect and imperfect competition are not required. Full explanations are in the [market structure study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-market-structure/); test yourself with the [market structure practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-market-structure-practice/). All figures are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Market structure | The features of a market, especially the number and size of firms and ease of entry |
| Competitive market | Many firms, each with a small share, selling similar products |
| Monopoly | A single supplier, or one firm that dominates the market |
| Market share | A firm's sales as a percentage of total market sales |
| Barrier to entry | Anything that makes it hard for new firms to enter a market |
| Price maker | A firm able to set its own price |
| Natural monopoly | A market where one firm can supply everyone at lower cost than several firms |
| Patent | A legal right giving an inventor sole use of an invention for a period |
| Economies of scale | Falling cost per unit as a firm grows |

## Competitive markets: four effects

| Effect | Result | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Price | Low | Dearer firms lose customers to rivals |
| Quality | High | Firms compete for buyers by improving products |
| Choice | Wide | Many firms, many versions |
| Profit | Low in the long run | Profit attracts entry; entry competes it away |

**Worked reminder.** One bakery: price $3, cost $2, 1,000 loaves, profit $1,000 a day. After two bakeries enter: price $2.40, each sells 400, each profit $160, total $480. Price down 20%, quantity up 20%, profit down.

**Limits**: small firms may miss economies of scale; low profit limits research; some choice is trivial; quality may suffer where buyers cannot check it.

## Monopoly: characteristics

- One seller or a dominant seller
- No close substitutes
- Barriers to entry
- Price maker
- High profit can last in the long run

## Barriers to entry

| Barrier | Example |
|---|---|
| Legal (patents, licences) | Sole right to sell a new medicine |
| High start-up costs | Building a railway or steel works |
| Economies of scale | Established firm's low unit cost undercuts entrants |
| Control of resources or outlets | Owning the only quarry or the shops |
| Brand loyalty and advertising | Long-established brand |

## Advantages and disadvantages of monopoly

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Economies of scale can lower costs and prices | Higher prices and lower output |
| Profits fund research; patents reward invention | Less choice |
| No wasteful duplication in natural monopolies | Complacency: weaker pressure on quality and costs |
| Stable supply | May pay suppliers and workers less |
| | Lower costs may not be passed on |

## A monopoly price rise in numbers

A monopoly sells 10,000 units at $4 with a cost of $3 a unit: profit $10,000. It raises the price to $5 and sales fall to 8,000: revenue is unchanged at $40,000, but profit rises to (5 − 3) × 8,000 = $16,000. Consumers pay more and 2,000 units are no longer bought.

## Judging a monopoly: "it depends on…"

- whether economies of scale are passed on as lower prices
- government regulation of prices and standards
- the threat of new entrants
- the availability of substitutes
- whether it is a natural monopoly

## Exam traps

- Drawing diagrams that are not required.
- Listing "many firms" as an effect rather than explaining price, quality, choice and profit.
- Assuming a monopoly can charge any price without losing sales.
- Treating every large firm as a monopoly without checking market share and substitutes.
- Giving only disadvantages of monopoly in a discussion.

## Quick self-test

1. Define market share.
2. A firm sells $150 million in a market worth $250 million. Calculate its market share.
3. Why do prices tend to be low in competitive markets?
4. Explain why profit in a competitive market tends to fall over time.
5. State three characteristics of monopoly.
6. Give two barriers to entry.
7. What is a natural monopoly? Give an example.
8. Give one advantage of monopoly for consumers.
9. Why might a monopoly offer poor service?
10. A monopoly raises its price from $5 to $6 and sales fall from 2,000 to 1,800 units. Its cost is $4 a unit. Calculate the change in profit.
11. Why are diagrams not needed for this section?
12. Give one reason why a monopoly might keep prices low.

### Answers

1. A firm's sales as a percentage of total market sales.
2. 150 ÷ 250 × 100 = 60%.
3. A firm that charges more than its rivals loses customers, so each firm keeps its price close to its costs.
4. High profit attracts new firms; more sellers push prices down, so each firm's profit falls.
5. Any three: one seller; no close substitutes; barriers to entry; price maker; long-run high profit.
6. Any two: patents or licences; high start-up costs; economies of scale; control of resources or outlets; brand loyalty.
7. A market where one firm can supply everyone more cheaply than several firms, such as a water supply network.
8. Economies of scale may lower prices; profits may fund new products (any one).
9. With no rivals, customers cannot switch, so the firm faces little pressure to improve.
10. Before: (5 − 4) × 2,000 = $2,000. After: (6 − 4) × 1,800 = $3,600. Profit rises by $1,600.
11. The syllabus states that diagrams are not required for competitive or monopoly markets.
12. Fear of new entrants; government regulation; competition from substitutes (any one).
