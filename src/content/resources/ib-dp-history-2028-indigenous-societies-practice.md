**HL only.** These questions practise the Paper 3 regional study on Indigenous societies in the Americas, circa 750 to 1500. Use one society throughout (the answers use the Aztec Triple Alliance and the Inca). Revise first with the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-indigenous-societies/) and the [revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-indigenous-societies-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Explain how rulers of one Indigenous society used religion to maintain political authority. **[6]**

**2.** Explain two ways in which tribute shaped the economy of one Indigenous society. **[4]**

**3.** Read the extract (constructed for practice).

> "The merchants travelled far beyond the lands of the empire. They carried cloth and feathers to distant peoples and brought back goods for the ruler. They also listened carefully and told the ruler what they heard about the strength of other cities." (Constructed for practice: a paraphrase in the style of a post-conquest description.)

**(a)** Identify two ways in which the extract suggests trade served the state. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one limitation of using a post-conquest description of this kind to study the society before 1500. **[3]**

**4.** Analyse the role of women in the social organisation of one Indigenous society. **[6]**

**5.** Examine the view that warfare was the main reason why one Indigenous society expanded. **[8]**

**6.** Discuss the challenges to one Indigenous society that came from other Indigenous societies and from the environment. **[8]**

**7.** To what extent was the Inca state based on reciprocity rather than compulsion? Write a plan with an introduction line, three paragraph topics, a counter-argument and a judgement. **[8]**

**8.** Paper 3 style essay question: "Religion, not military force, was the main way in which political authority was maintained in one Indigenous society." To what extent do you agree? **[15]**

## Answers

**1.** Any one society, for example the Aztec. Rulers claimed that the state kept the cosmos in motion by feeding the gods, especially Huitzilopochtli, with sacrifice, so the tlatoani and priests had a sacred role [1]. Public ceremonies at the Templo Mayor displayed the power of the state to its own people and to visiting rulers [1]. Religion justified war, because victories and captives were offerings to the gods [1]. The Inca equivalent: the Sapa Inca was the child of the sun god Inti [1], dead rulers were preserved as mummies and kept estates [1], and the state cult spread through shrines in conquered regions [1]. Award up to 3 marks for explained reasons and up to 3 for specific evidence. **[6]**

**2.** Two developed points, 2 marks each. Aztec examples: tribute in cloth, food, cacao and feathers fed the capital and elite, and allowed a large non-farming population in Tenochtitlan [2]; the demand for tribute linked conquered cities to the centre but also bred resentment [2]. Inca examples: mit'a labour built roads, terraces and storehouses [2]; labour tribute let the state support its army without a money economy, and was presented as part of reciprocal obligations [2]. **[4]**

**3. (a)** Any two: trade supplied the ruler with goods [1]; merchants gathered intelligence about other cities [1]; trade carried exports (cloth, feathers) that served diplomacy [1]. Maximum 2. **[2]**
**(b)** One limitation, developed: a description written after the conquest reflects the writer's purposes and readers [1]; it may exaggerate or simplify, or be shaped by what the Spanish wanted to learn or by what informants chose to tell [1]; it describes a society already damaged by conquest, so it cannot be taken as direct evidence of conditions before 1500 and needs support from other sources such as codices or archaeology [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Analysis should break down women's roles and link them to society. Weaving and household labour produced cloth used for tribute and exchange [1]. Women in the Aztec world could be midwives, healers, market traders or priestesses [1], but not rulers, and boys and girls were trained for different roles [1]. In Haudenosaunee society, descent was matrilineal, women controlled much of the farming and the longhouse, and clan mothers named and could remove leaders [1]. Overall, roles were complementary but not equal in most societies; the position of women varied by society, which is why one society must be chosen [1]. One mark for a clear overall conclusion about women's influence [1]. **[6]**

**5.** Credit a balanced response. For the view: warfare brought tribute, land, captives and prestige, and rulers were war leaders [2]; the Triple Alliance began with conquest (the defeat of Azcapotzalco in 1428) [1]; the Inca expansion under Pachacuti was military [1]. Against, or in addition: alliances, diplomacy and marriage ties [1]; economic motives such as access to different ecological zones or labour [1]; religious justification [1]. Judgement with a supported ranking [1]. **[8]**

**6.** Other Indigenous societies: Tlaxcala and the Tarascans resisted the Aztec, and the Inca faced hard fighting on frontiers [2]. Environmental: drought and famine in the Valley of Mexico in the 1450s, frost and drought in the Andes, Maya drought [2]. Weighing: environmental stress made states dependent on stored food and tribute, so one challenge could intensify the other [2]. Brief treatment of social or political weakness, for context, is credited only if linked to the question [1]. Supported judgement on which challenge was greater [1]. **[8]**

**7.** A strong plan: introduction line such as "The Inca state used reciprocity as a way to make compulsion acceptable" [1]. Paragraph topics: (1) reciprocity: Murra's view that the state gave food, drink and gifts in return for labour [1]; (2) compulsion: mit'a was owed, mitmaq resettlement was forced, resistance was punished [1]; (3) the way the two worked together, with the state's storehouses [1]. Counter-argument: the older "socialist empire" view (Baudin) overstated care for the poor [1]. Perspectives: note that Spanish chroniclers had reasons to praise or condemn Inca rule [1]. Judgement: both were present, with reciprocity as the language of rule and compulsion as its enforcement [2]. **[8]**

**8.** Use the Paper 3 markbands. A top answer (13 to 15 marks) shows in-depth understanding of the argument, evaluates a range of perspectives, and reaches a substantiated judgement. Possible content for the Aztec: religion legitimised the tlatoani and the Templo Mayor ceremonies (about 3 marks); but military force, garrisons at key points and the threat of attack against rebellious cities were what made tribute reliable (about 3 marks); the economic system and the Triple Alliance gave stakeholders a share (about 2 marks). Perspectives: religion as a driver (the cosmological view of sacrifice) versus political terror (sacrifice as a warning to rivals); the hegemonic empire view, in which force was threatened more than used (about 3 marks). Judgement: for example, that religion and force were bound together, so "main" cannot be given to either alone; with an argued ranking (about 4 marks). **[15]**
