These questions cover sub-section 3.7.5 Magnetic fields of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408, AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards), from magnetic flux density to the operation of a transformer. It is A-level only and assessed in Paper 2.

Revise from the [magnetic fields study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-magnetic-fields/) and [magnetic fields revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-magnetic-fields-revision-notes/) first. Mixed section 3.7 questions are in the [Fields and their consequences practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-fields-and-their-consequences-practice/).

Data: e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; electron mass 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg; g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the tesla. **[2]**

**2.** A 0.25 m length of wire carrying 6.0 A lies at right angles to a uniform field of 0.040 T. Calculate the force on it. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why a charged particle moving at right angles to a uniform magnetic field follows a circular path. **[2]**

**4.** State Lenz's law and explain how it follows from conservation of energy. **[2]**

**5.** The mains supply is 230 V rms. Calculate its peak and peak-to-peak values. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** Electrons move in a circle of radius 0.050 m in a uniform magnetic field of 1.2 mT.

**(a)** Calculate their speed. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the potential difference through which they were accelerated from rest. **[2]**
**(c)** State how the path of protons with the same speed would differ. **[1]**

**7.** In Required practical 10, 0.050 m of a fixed horizontal wire lies in the field of a magnet on a top pan balance. A current of 2.0 A changes the balance reading by 0.86 g.

**(a)** Calculate the force on the wire. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the magnetic flux density. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the balance reading changes even though the wire does not touch the magnet. **[1]**

**8.** A 150-turn coil of area 3.0 × 10⁻³ m² is placed in a uniform field of 0.20 T.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum flux linkage. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the flux linkage when the normal to the coil is at 60° to the field. **[1]**
**(c)** The coil is turned from θ = 0 to θ = 90° in 0.050 s. Calculate the average emf induced. **[2]**

**9.** A bar magnet falls through a 300-turn coil connected to a data logger.

**(a)** As the magnet enters the coil, the flux through each turn rises by 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ Wb in 0.050 s. Calculate the average emf. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the emf has the opposite sign as the magnet leaves the coil. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the second peak of emf is larger than the first. **[2]**
**(d)** The coil is connected to a resistor. Explain why the magnet now falls with an acceleration less than g. **[1]**

**10.** A metal rod of length 0.50 m slides at 6.0 m s⁻¹ along rails, perpendicular to a uniform field of 0.12 T.

**(a)** Use Faraday's law to show that the emf is Blv. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the emf. **[1]**
**(c)** The circuit has a total resistance of 1.8 Ω. Calculate the current and the force needed to keep the rod moving at constant speed. **[2]**

**11.** A generator coil of 80 turns and area 0.020 m² rotates at 50 revolutions per second in a uniform field of 0.15 T.

**(a)** Calculate the peak emf. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the rms emf. **[1]**
**(c)** State the orientation of the coil when the emf is at its peak. **[1]**

**12.** An oscilloscope is set to a Y-gain of 5.0 V div⁻¹ and a time base of 2.0 ms div⁻¹. A sinusoidal trace is 5.6 divisions from top to bottom, and two complete cycles occupy 10 divisions. Calculate the peak voltage, the rms voltage and the frequency. **[4]**

**13.** Power of 2.0 MW is transmitted through cables of total resistance 8.0 Ω.

**(a)** A step-down transformer converts 11 kV to 230 V and has 4000 primary turns. Calculate the number of secondary turns. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the power lost in the cables when transmitting at 132 kV and at 33 kV. **[3]**
**(c)** State two causes of energy loss in a transformer and how each is reduced. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The magnetic flux density that produces a force of 1 N [1] on a 1 m length of wire carrying 1 A at right angles to the field [1].

**2.** F = BIl = 0.040 × 6.0 × 0.25 [1] = **0.060 N** [1]

**3.** The force BQv is always perpendicular to the velocity [1]; so it changes direction but not speed, providing a centripetal force of constant magnitude [1].

**4.** The induced current flows in a direction that opposes the change producing it [1]; if it aided the change, energy would be created without work being done, breaking conservation of energy [1].

**5.** V₀ = 230√2 = **325 V** [1]; V_pp = 2V₀ = **651 V** [1]

**6. (a)** BQv = mv²/r, so v = BQr/m [1] = 1.2 × 10⁻³ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 0.050/9.11 × 10⁻³¹ = **1.1 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** E_k = ½mv² = 5.06 × 10⁻¹⁷ J [1]; V = E_k/e = **320 V** [1]
**(c)** Protons curve the opposite way, in a circle of much larger radius (r ∝ m) [1].

**7. (a)** F = Δm × g = 0.86 × 10⁻³ × 9.81 = **8.4 × 10⁻³ N** [1]
**(b)** B = F/Il = 8.44 × 10⁻³/(2.0 × 0.050) [1] = **0.084 T** [1]
**(c)** The field exerts a force on the wire; by Newton's third law the wire exerts an equal and opposite force on the magnet, changing the reading [1].

**8. (a)** NΦ = BAN = 0.20 × 3.0 × 10⁻³ × 150 = **0.090 Wb turns** [1]
**(b)** 0.090 cos 60° = **0.045 Wb turns** [1]
**(c)** Change = 0.090 Wb turns [1]; ε = 0.090/0.050 = **1.8 V** [1]

**9. (a)** ε = NΔΦ/Δt = 300 × 2.4 × 10⁻⁴/0.050 [1] = **1.4 V** [1]
**(b)** The flux through the coil is now decreasing instead of increasing, so the induced emf reverses [1].
**(c)** The magnet has accelerated and is moving faster as it leaves [1]; the same flux change happens in less time, so the rate of change of flux linkage is greater [1].
**(d)** The induced current produces a field that opposes the magnet's motion (Lenz), so there is an upward force on the magnet [1].

**10. (a)** In time Δt the rod sweeps area lvΔt, so ΔΦ = BlvΔt [1]; ε = ΔΦ/Δt = Blv [1]
**(b)** ε = 0.12 × 0.50 × 6.0 = **0.36 V** [1]
**(c)** I = 0.36/1.8 = **0.20 A** [1]; F = BIl = 0.12 × 0.20 × 0.50 = **0.012 N** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The force on the rod opposes its motion, so a constant applied force is needed; the work it does becomes electrical energy.

**11. (a)** ω = 2π × 50 = 314 rad s⁻¹ [1]; ε₀ = BANω = 0.15 × 0.020 × 80 × 314 = **75 V** [1]
**(b)** 75/√2 = **53 V** [1]
**(c)** The plane of the coil is parallel to the field (normal perpendicular to it) [1].

**12.** V_pp = 5.6 × 5.0 = 28 V, so V₀ = **14 V** [1]; V_rms = 14/√2 = **9.9 V** [1]; T = (10/2) × 2.0 ms = 10 ms [1]; f = **100 Hz** [1]

**13. (a)** Nₛ = 4000 × 230/11 000 = **84 turns** [1]
**(b)** At 132 kV: I = 2.0 × 10⁶/1.32 × 10⁵ = 15.2 A [1]; loss = I²R = **1.8 kW** [1]; at 33 kV: I = 60.6 A, loss = **29 kW** [1]
**(c)** Any two, one mark each: eddy currents, reduced by a laminated core; resistance of the coils, reduced by thick low-resistance wire; magnetising and demagnetising the core, reduced by a soft iron core; flux leakage, reduced by winding the coils together on one core [2].
*Examiner insight:* Four times the voltage cuts the loss by a factor of 16, since loss ∝ 1/V² at fixed power.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to convert mT, g and rpm.
- Taking θ from the coil plane instead of the normal.
- Using full flux linkage instead of its change.
- Using the transmission voltage in V²/R for the loss.
- Giving peak values when rms is asked for.
