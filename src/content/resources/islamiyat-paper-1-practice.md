## Section A

**1.** Describe the main themes of the Qur'an, giving **four** themes. **[8]**

**2.** Explain the difference between Makkan and Madinan surahs, giving two features of each. **[4]**

## Section B

**3.** Describe the events of the Hijrah and explain its significance for the Muslim community. **[10]**

**4.** Describe the main events of the Battle of Badr and explain **two** lessons Muslims draw from it. **[10]**

**5.** Outline the achievements of Abu Bakr as the first Caliph, and explain why the Ridda wars were significant. **[10]**

**6.** Explain the significance of the compilation of the Qur'an under Uthman. **[8]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Any four, 2 marks each: **Tawhid — the absolute oneness of God**, which is the central message of the Qur'an and the basis of all its other teachings [1] [1]. **Prophethood** — the succession of prophets sent to every people with the same essential message, culminating in Muhammad [1] [1]. **The Day of Judgement and the afterlife** — every person is accountable for their deeds and will be rewarded or punished [1] [1]. **Guidance for individual and social conduct** — worship, family law, trade, justice and the treatment of the poor [1] [1]. **Stories of earlier prophets and communities**, presented as lessons and warnings for the present [1] [1].

**2. Makkan surahs** are generally **shorter, with a rhythmic and forceful style**, and focus on **belief — tawhid, prophethood and the Day of Judgement** [1] [1]. **Madinan surahs** are generally **longer and more detailed**, and deal with **legislation and the organisation of the community** — worship, inheritance, marriage, treaties and warfare [1] [1].

**3.** The Prophet and his followers faced **increasing persecution in Makkah**, particularly after the deaths of Abu Talib and Khadijah [1]. Delegations from Yathrib pledged their support at the two **pledges of Aqabah** [1]. Muslims migrated in small groups, and the Quraysh **plotted to kill the Prophet**; Ali slept in his bed while the Prophet left with Abu Bakr [1] [1]. They **sheltered in the cave of Thawr for three days** before completing the journey to Yathrib, which became **Madinah** [1].
**Significance:** it marked the **beginning of the Islamic calendar**, showing its importance [1]. It transformed the Muslims from a **persecuted minority into a community with its own territory and authority** [1]. It allowed the establishment of the **first mosque and the Constitution of Madinah**, which organised relations between the Muhajirun, Ansar and the Jewish tribes [1] [1]. It demonstrated the principle that **Muslims may migrate to preserve their faith**, and the brotherhood established between Muhajirun and Ansar became a model of social solidarity [1].

**4.** The Muslims set out to intercept a **Quraysh caravan returning from Syria led by Abu Sufyan**, who took a different route and sent for help [1]. A Quraysh army of about **1000 met roughly 313 Muslims at Badr** in 2 AH [1]. The Prophet **secured the wells**, denying the enemy water [1]. The battle opened with **single combat**, in which the Muslims prevailed, followed by a general engagement [1]. The Quraysh were defeated, losing around seventy men including **Abu Jahl**, with a similar number taken prisoner; prisoners were treated humanely and some ransomed themselves by teaching Muslims to read and write [1].
**Lessons**, any two: **faith and trust in God matter more than numbers** — a far smaller force prevailed, which the Qur'an itself presents as a sign [1] [1]. **Planning and consultation are still required** — the Prophet took advice on positioning and secured the water supply, showing that reliance on God does not replace preparation [1] [1]. **Mercy towards prisoners** — the humane treatment of captives set a standard for the conduct of war [1] [1].

**5.** Abu Bakr was chosen at **Saqifah** and immediately faced **crises that threatened the survival of the community** [1]. He **despatched the expedition of Usama** as the Prophet had ordered, despite advice to keep the army at home, showing his determination to fulfil the Prophet's instructions [1]. He **suppressed the false prophets**, including Musaylimah at Yamama [1], and **compelled the tribes who refused zakat to return to obedience** [1]. He also **initiated the collection of the Qur'an** after many huffaz were killed at Yamama, entrusting it to Zayd ibn Thabit [1].
**Significance of the Ridda wars:** they established that **allegiance was to Islam and the Muslim state, not personally to the Prophet**, so the community could survive his death [1] [1]. They **preserved the unity of Arabia** at the moment it might have fragmented [1], and confirmed that **zakat is an obligation of faith, not a voluntary tribute** [1]. The heavy losses of Qur'an memorisers at Yamama also directly prompted the compilation of the text [1].

**6.** As Islam spread, Muslims in different regions were **reciting the Qur'an in different dialects**, and disputes arose during the campaigns in Armenia and Azerbaijan, with **Hudhayfah warning Uthman** of the danger [1] [1]. Uthman **had the sheets held by Hafsah copied by a committee under Zayd ibn Thabit**, using the dialect of the Quraysh where there was disagreement [1] [1]. **Standard copies were sent to the main centres of the Muslim world** and other versions were destroyed [1].
Its significance is that it **preserved a single, uniform text**, preventing the division and doctrinal dispute that variant texts would have produced [1] [1]; the text in use today is the one established then, which Muslims regard as evidence of the Qur'an's preservation [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Narrating events without explaining their significance when the question asks for it.
- Confusing the features of Makkan and Madinan surahs.
- Omitting the Ridda wars from Abu Bakr's achievements.
- Not linking the losses at Yamama to the compilation of the Qur'an.
