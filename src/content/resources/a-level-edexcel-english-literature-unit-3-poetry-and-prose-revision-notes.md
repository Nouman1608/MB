Unit 3 (WET03) of Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Level English Literature (YET01) is a two-hour,
open-book exam worth 50 marks and 25% of the full International A Level. **Section A** is one compulsory
20-mark essay on an **unseen post-1900 poem** (AO1, AO2). **Section B** is one 30-mark **comparative
essay** on two prose texts from one theme, chosen from two questions (AO1, AO2, AO3, AO4). For the full
method, use the
[Unit 3 study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-english-literature-unit-3-poetry-and-prose-study-guide/).

## Unit 3 at a glance

| Detail | Section A: Poetry (3.3) | Section B: Prose (3.4) |
|---|---|---|
| Task | One compulsory essay on one unseen post-1900 poem, printed in a Source Booklet | One comparative essay, from a choice of two, on your two theme texts |
| Marks | 20 | 30 |
| AOs | AO1, AO2 | AO1, AO2, AO3, AO4 |
| Preparation | Skills from Unit 1 applied to unseen poems | Two prose fiction texts from one of four themes |

**Exam:** 2 hours, open book, 50 marks, available January and June; first assessed January 2017.
Unit 3 is an IA2 unit, so it counts only towards the full International A Level, not the International
AS.

**Timing tip:** split the time roughly in proportion to the marks: about 45–50 minutes for Section A and
70–75 minutes for Section B.

## Prose themes and texts

You study **two** of the three texts in **one** theme.

| Theme | Texts |
|---|---|
| 1 Growing Up | *The Color Purple* (Walker); *Great Expectations* (Dickens); *What Maisie Knew* (James) |
| 2 Colonisation and After | *Heart of Darkness* (Conrad); *The Lonely Londoners* (Selvon); *A Passage to India* (Forster) |
| 3 Science and Society | *Frankenstein* (Shelley); *The Handmaid's Tale* (Atwood); *Never Let Me Go* (Ishiguro) |
| 4 Women and Society | *Beloved* (Morrison); *Mrs Dalloway* (Woolf); *Wuthering Heights* (Brontë) |

## The assessment objectives in Unit 3

| AO | Wording | Section A | Section B |
|---|---|---|---|
| AO1 | Articulate informed, personal and creative responses, using concepts and terminology, and coherent, accurate written expression | Yes | Yes |
| AO2 | Analyse ways in which meanings are shaped in literary texts | Yes | Yes |
| AO3 | Demonstrate understanding of the significance and influence of the contexts in which texts are written and received | — | Yes |
| AO4 | Explore connections across literary texts | — | Yes |

Across the full International A Level, Unit 3 contributes AO1 7.5%, AO2 7.5%, AO3 5% and AO4 5%. AO5
(different interpretations) is **not** assessed in Unit 3.

## Section A: approaching the unseen poem

The 3.3 learning outcomes ask you to show understanding of the function of key features of poetry, read
for detail of how poets use and adapt language, form and structure, explore attitudes and values, and use
critical terminology with discrimination.

A reliable sequence (about 10 minutes of reading and planning):

1. **First reading:** What is the situation? Who speaks, to whom, and when?
2. **Second reading:** Mark the turn or shift (tense, tone, addressee, stanza break).
3. **Form and structure:** stanza pattern, line length, enjambment, rhyme or its absence, the ending.
4. **Language:** imagery clusters, diction, sound, voice.
5. **Attitudes and values:** What does the poem seem to value or question?
6. **Thesis:** one sentence answering the question.

Context and comparison are not rewarded in Section A, so stay with the poem.

## Section B: writing the comparative prose essay

The questions "require learners to compare the two prose texts and consider relevant contextual
factors". So every paragraph should do three things: analyse a method, compare it across the texts, and
connect it to context.

| Skill | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| AO4 comparison | Text A essay, then Text B essay | Integrated paragraphs organised by idea |
| AO3 context | A paragraph of history | Context explaining a writer's choice, including context of reception |
| AO2 analysis | Plot reference | Narrative voice, structure, symbolism, setting |

### Comparative connectives worth using

*Whereas*, *similarly*, *by contrast*, *both writers*, *where Shelley… Ishiguro instead…*, *this
difference reflects…*

## Worked example: a Theme 3 comparative paragraph

Question focus: the presentation of creators and the created.

"Both novels deny their created beings a stable place in society, but they do so through opposite
narrative methods. Shelley lets the creature speak at length in the central part of the novel, so his
eloquence argues against Victor's view of him as a monster; Ishiguro gives Kathy a calm, restrained
voice that never openly protests her fate, so the horror lies in her acceptance. Shelley wrote in an age
of new experiments with electricity and anatomy, and Ishiguro after debates about cloning in the 1990s,
yet both novels move the question from whether science can create life to whether society will accept
responsibility for the lives it creates."

This combines AO2 (narrative voice and structure), AO4 (comparison within the paragraph), AO3 (contexts of
production) and AO1 (an argued, personal judgement).

## Common mistakes / exam traps

- **Bringing context into Section A.** The unseen-poem essay rewards AO1 and AO2 only.
- **Writing two separate mini-essays in Section B.** AO4 rewards connections across texts.
- **Treating "open book" as "no revision".** You still need to know where key passages are; time spent
  searching is time lost.
- **Context as biography.** AO3 covers contexts in which texts are written **and received**, and must be
  tied to the text.
- **Feature-spotting in the unseen.** Naming enjambment without explaining its effect does not meet AO2.
- **Ignoring the question's key word.** The two Section B questions differ; plan around the one you choose.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the Unit 3 poem one I have studied?**
No. Section A uses an unseen post-1900 poem printed in a Source Booklet. You apply the poetry skills you
developed in Unit 1.

**Do I compare in Section A?**
No. Section A is one essay on one poem, assessing AO1 and AO2. Comparison (AO4) is assessed in Section B.

**Can I take my prose texts into the exam?**
Yes. Unit 3 is an open-book examination; check your centre's guidance on the editions allowed.

**Do I need critics in Unit 3?**
AO5 (different interpretations) is not assessed in Unit 3, so critics are optional. Spend the time on
analysis, comparison and context.

## Self-test

1. How many marks is Section A worth, and which AOs? (20; AO1 and AO2.)
2. How many prose texts do you study, and from how many themes? (Two texts from one theme.)
3. Name the three texts in Theme 2. (*Heart of Darkness*, *The Lonely Londoners*, *A Passage to India*.)
4. Which AO is not assessed in Unit 3? (AO5.)
5. What does AO3 include besides the context of writing? (The context in which texts are received.)
