Condensed recall for **12.3 Chromatography**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 (2026–2028). The full method and worked examples are in the [chromatography study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-chromatography/); exam-style questions are in the [chromatography practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-chromatography-practice/).

> **Tier note:** outcomes 1–2 (coloured substances; interpreting chromatograms) are Core. Outcomes 3–4 (colourless substances with a locating agent; Rf) are Supplement, marked *(Extended)*. Specific locating agents are not required.

## What paper chromatography does

- Separates mixtures of **soluble** substances.
- Substances travel **different distances** up the paper because they dissolve in the solvent and are held by the paper to different extents.
- Needs a **suitable solvent**: one that dissolves the substances.

## Method and the reason for each step

| Step | Reason |
|---|---|
| baseline drawn in **pencil** | graphite does not dissolve and run; ink would |
| small, concentrated spots | gives clear, separate spots |
| solvent level **below** the baseline | otherwise the spots dissolve into the solvent in the beaker |
| **lid** on the beaker | stops the solvent evaporating from the paper |
| remove before solvent reaches the top | so the solvent front can be seen and marked |
| mark the **solvent front** at once | it evaporates and disappears |
| **dry** the paper | before measuring or applying a locating agent |
| *(Extended)* spray with **locating agent** | makes colourless spots visible |

## Interpreting chromatograms

| Observation | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| one spot | pure substance (likely) |
| two or more spots | impure / a mixture |
| unknown's spot at same height as a known's | unknown contains that known substance |
| spot left on the baseline | that substance is insoluble in this solvent |

Comparisons only work on the **same paper with the same solvent** – or by comparing Rf values in the same solvent.

## Rf *(Extended)*

```
Rf = distance travelled by substance ÷ distance travelled by solvent
```

- Measure both distances **from the baseline**.
- Substance: to the **centre** of the spot. Solvent: to the **solvent front**.
- **No units.** Always **between 0 and 1**.
- Rf = 0: spot did not move (insoluble). Rf close to 1: very soluble, moved almost with the solvent front.
- Same substance, same solvent → same Rf.

Rearranged: distance travelled by substance = Rf × distance travelled by solvent.

## Reading a chromatogram in four checks

1. **Count** the spots for each sample, including any on the baseline.
2. **Compare heights** of the unknown's spots with the known substances on the same paper.
3. **Decide purity**: one spot → likely pure; more than one → mixture.
4. *(Extended)* **Measure** from the baseline and calculate Rf if asked; compare with given values.

## Answer frame: describe the method

"Draw a pencil line near the bottom of the paper and put a small spot of the mixture on it. Stand the paper in a beaker of a suitable solvent, with the solvent below the line, and cover it. When the solvent has nearly reached the top, remove the paper, mark the solvent front and let it dry." *(Extended: for colourless substances, then apply a locating agent.)*

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Chromatogram | the dried paper showing the separated spots |
| Baseline (start line) | pencil line where samples are spotted |
| Solvent front | furthest point the solvent reached |
| Suitable solvent | a solvent in which the substances dissolve |
| Locating agent *(Extended)* | substance applied to a dried chromatogram that makes colourless spots visible |
| Rf value *(Extended)* | distance travelled by substance ÷ distance travelled by solvent |

## Worked numbers *(Extended)*

| Solvent distance / cm | Spot distance / cm | Rf |
|---|---|---|
| 10.0 | 4.5 | 0.45 |
| 7.5 | 6.0 | 0.80 |
| 6.0 | 1.5 | 0.25 |
| 8.0 | 0.0 | 0 |

## Exam traps

- Baseline in **pencil**, not pen.
- Solvent **below** the baseline.
- Distances from the **baseline**, not from the bottom of the paper; to the **solvent front**, not to the top of the paper.
- Rf has **no unit** and is **never more than 1**. *(Extended)*
- One spot does not always prove purity: a spot may sit on the baseline, or two substances may travel together.
- The locating agent goes on **after** drying. *(Extended)*

## Self-test

1. What type of substances does paper chromatography separate?
2. Why is the baseline drawn in pencil?
3. Why must the solvent level be below the baseline?
4. Why is a lid placed on the beaker?
5. How many spots does a pure substance give?
6. An unknown gives spots at the same heights as substances P and R. What does the unknown contain?
7. A spot stays on the baseline. What does this tell you?
8. *(Extended)* Why is a locating agent used?
9. *(Extended)* State the equation for Rf.
10. *(Extended)* The solvent travels 9.0 cm and a spot travels 3.6 cm. Calculate Rf.
11. *(Extended)* A substance has Rf 0.70. The solvent travels 5.0 cm. How far does the spot travel?
12. *(Extended)* A student calculates Rf = 1.2. Suggest the error.

**Answers:**

1. Soluble substances (coloured, or colourless with a locating agent).
2. Pencil does not dissolve in the solvent, so it does not run and form extra spots.
3. So the spots do not dissolve into the solvent in the beaker.
4. To stop the solvent evaporating from the paper.
5. One.
6. P and R.
7. That substance is insoluble in the solvent used.
8. To make colourless spots visible (it forms coloured spots).
9. Rf = distance travelled by substance ÷ distance travelled by solvent.
10. 3.6 ÷ 9.0 = 0.40.
11. 0.70 × 5.0 = 3.5 cm.
12. The distances were swapped (solvent distance divided by substance distance), or the spot was measured beyond the solvent front.
