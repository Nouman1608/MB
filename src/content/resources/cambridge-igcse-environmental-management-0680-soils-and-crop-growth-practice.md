## Section A

**1.** State the **two** parts of the organic content of soil. **[2]**

**2.** Define weather. **[1]**

**3.** State the pH of a neutral soil, and describe the pH of an acidic soil. **[2]**

**4.** Name the ion that supplies phosphorus to plants, and state its main role. **[2]**

**5.** State **one** property of a clay soil that benefits crops and **one** property that can harm them. **[2]**

**6.** Explain how earthworms and decomposers improve soil for crop growth. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A student poured 100 cm³ of water through 50 g of each of three soils, A, B and C, and measured the volume collected after 5 minutes. The results (invented for practice) were: Soil A 64 cm³, Soil B 20 cm³, Soil C 41 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of water retained by each soil. **[2]**

**(b)** One soil is loam. Identify it, and give a reason for your choice. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain which soil is most likely to become waterlogged. **[2]**

**(d)** State **two** variables the student kept the same to make this a fair test. **[2]**

**8.** Explain why crops grow poorly in waterlogged soil. **[3]**

**9.** A farmer notices that a crop has yellowing leaves and is stunted. A soil test shows low levels of one ion.

**(a)** Identify the ion most likely to be lacking. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why a lack of this ion causes stunted growth. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why this deficiency is more common in sandy soils after heavy rain. **[2]**

**10.** The table shows the rate of photosynthesis of a crop plant at different light intensities, with temperature kept constant. The data are invented for practice.

| Light intensity (arbitrary units) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate of photosynthesis (arbitrary units) | 0 | 4 | 8 | 11 | 12 | 12 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship between light intensity and the rate of photosynthesis. Use data in your answer. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the rate does not increase above a light intensity of 4 units. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest **one** condition that could be changed to raise the maximum rate of photosynthesis. **[1]**

**11.** Explain how wet and dry seasons affect when crops are planted and harvested, and the risks farmers face. **[3]**

**12.** The information describes growing conditions in two places. It is invented for practice.

| | Place X | Place Y |
|---|---|---|
| Growing season | About 300 days | About 150 days |
| Daylight hours | About 12 hours all year | 16 hours in summer, 8 in winter |
| Frost | Never | Most winter nights |
| Rainfall | Two wet seasons a year | Rain spread through the year |

Suggest which place could produce more harvests of a fast-growing crop each year. Explain your answer using the information. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Living organisms (plants, animals, fungi and bacteria) [1]; organic matter from decomposition (humus) [1].

**2.** The day-to-day conditions of the atmosphere in a location [1].

**3.** Neutral: pH 7 [1]. Acidic: pH below 7 [1].

**4.** Phosphate ion (PO₄³⁻) [1]; healthy root growth [1].

**5.** Benefit, any one: holds water well; holds nutrients well [1]. Harm, any one: drains slowly, so it can become waterlogged; heavy and difficult to cultivate; sets hard when dry [1].

**6.** Earthworms burrow, mixing organic matter into the soil and creating spaces for air and drainage [1]; decomposers break down dead material, releasing nutrients such as nitrate ions for plants [1].

**7. (a)** A: 100 − 64 = 36 cm³; B: 100 − 20 = 80 cm³; C: 100 − 41 = 59 cm³. All three correct [2]; two correct [1].

**(b)** Soil C [1]; it lets an intermediate amount of water drain, so it drains excess water while retaining a good amount for roots [1].

**(c)** Soil B [1]; it retains the most water and drains slowly, suggesting tiny pore spaces like clay, so pores fill with water [1].

**(d)** Any two: mass of soil (50 g) [1]; volume of water (100 cm³) [1]; time allowed (5 minutes) [1]; size of funnel or filter paper [1].

**8.** Any three: water fills the pore spaces, leaving little air [1]; roots cannot get enough oxygen for aerobic respiration [1]; roots release less energy, so they absorb fewer mineral ions and grow less [1]; roots may die, and soil organisms that need oxygen are also harmed [1].

**9. (a)** Nitrate ion (NO₃⁻) [1].

**(b)** Nitrogen from nitrate is needed to make proteins [1], which the plant uses to build new cells for growth, especially in leaves [1].

**(c)** Sandy soils have large pore spaces, so water drains through quickly [1] and dissolved nitrate is washed down beyond the roots (leaching) [1].

**10. (a)** As light intensity increases, the rate of photosynthesis increases, rising from 0 to 12 units [1]; the rate levels off at 12 units at light intensities of 4 and 5 [1].

**(b)** Light is no longer the limiting factor [1]; another factor, such as carbon dioxide concentration or temperature, is now limiting the rate [1].

**(c)** Any one: increase carbon dioxide concentration [1]; raise temperature towards the optimum [1]. Do not accept more light.

**11.** Any three: crops are planted at the start of the wet season so that they have water while growing [1]; they are harvested as the dry season begins, when the crop has ripened [1]; if the wet season starts late or brings little rain, crops may fail [1]; growing crops in the dry season needs irrigation [1].

**12.** Place X [1]. Explanation, any three:
- The growing season is about twice as long, so there is time for more than one crop to mature [1].
- No frost, so crops are not killed in winter [1].
- Two wet seasons give water for two planting periods [1].
- Steady 12 hours of daylight all year allows photosynthesis throughout the year, whereas Place Y has only 8 hours in winter [1].

Accept the point that Place Y's 16-hour summer days allow fast summer growth, but this does not outweigh the shorter season.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 3**: writing "pH less than 7" for neutral, or confusing acidic with alkaline.
- **Question 7(a)**: giving the volume collected rather than the volume retained.
- **Question 8**: saying roots "drown" without referring to oxygen and respiration.
- **Question 10(b)**: saying the plant has "enough light" without naming the idea of another limiting factor.
- **Question 12**: choosing a place without using the information. Each explanation mark needs a feature from the table.
