## Questions

**1.** **(a)** Define the term polymer. **[1]**
**(b)** Name three monomers found in living organisms. **[3]**

**2.** Disaccharides are formed by condensation of two monosaccharides.
**(a)** Name the two monosaccharides joined in maltose, in sucrose and in lactose. **[3]**
**(b)** State which of these three disaccharides gives a negative result when heated directly with Benedict's solution. **[1]**

**3.** **(a)** Describe what happens, in terms of the molecules involved, when a glycosidic bond forms between two α-glucose molecules. **[3]**
**(b)** A 51.3 g sample of lactose is completely hydrolysed. Calculate the mass of water used and the total mass of sugars formed. **[3]**

**4.** **(a)** Compare the structure of β-glucose with that of α-glucose. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the polysaccharide made from β-glucose. **[1]**

**5.** A plant cell is placed in distilled water. Explain how the structure of cellulose prevents the cell wall from breaking as water enters the cell. **[5]**

**6.** Starch and glycogen are both storage polysaccharides.
**(a)** State two ways in which they are similar in structure. **[2]**
**(b)** State two ways in which glycogen differs from starch. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the structure of glycogen suits it to its function in a muscle cell. **[2]**

**7.** Four samples, P to S, are each one of maltose solution, sucrose solution, starch suspension or distilled water. The results were:

| Sample | Heated with Benedict's | Boiled with acid, neutralised, heated with Benedict's | Iodine solution |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | Brick-red | Not done | Yellow-brown |
| Q | Blue | Blue | Blue-black |
| R | Blue | Brick-red | Yellow-brown |
| S | Blue | Blue | Yellow-brown |

**(a)** Identify each sample. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why sample R gave a different result after acid hydrolysis. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why it was necessary to test R with Benedict's solution before hydrolysing it. **[1]**

**8.** A student makes standards from an 8 g dm⁻³ glucose stock. After a Benedict's test the absorbance of the remaining solution was measured.

| Glucose (g dm⁻³) | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Absorbance | 0.90 | 0.72 | 0.55 | 0.35 | 0.18 |

**(a)** Work out how much stock solution and how much distilled water are mixed to give 20 cm³ of the 6 g dm⁻³ standard. **[2]**
**(b)** An unknown gives an absorbance of 0.62. Estimate its concentration. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why absorbance falls as glucose concentration rises. **[2]**

**9.** A mixture produced by hydrolysing a disaccharide was run on paper chromatography with standards. The solvent front moved 9.0 cm. Spots moved: galactose 2.7 cm, glucose 3.6 cm, fructose 5.4 cm. The mixture gave spots at 2.7 cm and 3.6 cm.
**(a)** Calculate the R_f value of the galactose standard. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify the sugars in the mixture and justify your answer. **[2]**
**(c)** Name the disaccharide that was hydrolysed and explain your choice. **[2]**

**10.** A glycogen fragment contains 60 glucose units, including several branch points.
**(a)** State the number of glycosidic bonds in the fragment. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate its relative molecular mass. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass of water needed to hydrolyse 9738 g of the fragment completely. **[1]**

**11.** Starch and cellulose are both polymers of glucose, yet a plant stores energy as one and builds its walls from the other. Explain how differences in structure make each suited to its role. **[6]**

**12.** A student wants to find out whether a plant extract contains sucrose, with no other sugar present. Describe a method, including the results that would show sucrose is present. **[7]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A molecule made from a large number of monomers joined together [1].
**(b)** Any three of: monosaccharide (for example glucose) [1], amino acid [1], nucleotide [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming "sugars" or "proteins" does not answer part (b); give monomers, not polymers.

**2. (a)** Maltose: glucose and glucose [1]. Sucrose: glucose and fructose [1]. Lactose: glucose and galactose [1].
**(b)** **Sucrose** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A common slip is to list all disaccharides as non-reducing; maltose and lactose give a direct positive.

**3. (a)** A condensation reaction [1] in which an OH group on each molecule reacts, and one molecule of water is released [1]. The two glucose molecules are joined through an oxygen atom by a glycosidic bond [1] **[3]**
**(b)** Moles = 51.3 ÷ 342 = 0.150 mol [1]. One water per bond: 0.150 × 18 = **2.7 g** [1]. Sugars formed: 51.3 + 2.7 = **54.0 g** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use conservation of mass for the second figure (sugars formed equal lactose plus the water used), and show each step so your reasoning is visible.

**4. (a)** The OH group on carbon 1 [1] is above the ring in β-glucose but below the ring in α-glucose [1].
**(b)** Cellulose [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming carbon 1 as well as the direction is what makes this answer precise.

**5.** Cellulose is made of β-glucose joined with alternate units rotated 180° [1], giving straight, unbranched chains [1]. Hydrogen bonds form between neighbouring chains [1] so they bundle into microfibrils [1]. Many hydrogen bonds give high tensile strength, so the wall resists the pressure from water entering by osmosis and does not burst [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong answers state that the bonds between chains are hydrogen bonds; writing "glycosidic bonds between chains" is a different, incorrect idea.

**6. (a)** Both are made of α-glucose [1]. Both are branched, or both are compact and insoluble [1] (any two correct similarities).
**(b)** Glycogen is more highly branched [1] and has shorter chains [1].
**(c)** More branches give more free ends [1] so enzymes can hydrolyse glucose off quickly for respiration during activity [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "More branched" scores better when you finish the chain of reasoning: more ends, faster hydrolysis, faster release of glucose.

**7. (a)** P is **maltose** [1]. Q is **starch** [1]. R is **sucrose** [1]. S is **distilled water** [1].
**(b)** Acid hydrolysed the glycosidic bond in sucrose [1], releasing glucose and fructose, which are reducing sugars [1].
**(c)** To show there was no reducing sugar before hydrolysis, so the later result is due to a non-reducing sugar [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Linking the later colour to the products of hydrolysis, not just to "acid", is the reasoning that earns credit in part (b).

**8. (a)** Volume of stock = 6 ÷ 8 × 20 = 15 cm³ [1] and water = 5 cm³ [1].
**(b)** The value lies between the 2 and 4 standards [1]: 2 + (0.72 − 0.62) ÷ (0.72 − 0.55) × 2 = 3.2 g dm⁻³ [1].
**(c)** More glucose reduces more copper(II) ions in Benedict's solution [1], so less blue copper(II) remains and the solution absorbs less [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* State units with your estimate, and show the reading on the curve or the interpolation, not only the number.

**9. (a)** 2.7 ÷ 9.0 = **0.30** [1].
**(b)** The spots match the R_f values of galactose (0.30) and glucose (0.40) [1], and there is no spot at the fructose position (0.60) [1].
**(c)** **Lactose** [1], because it is the disaccharide made from glucose and galactose [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* R_f has no units, and the distance is measured from the origin line, not from the bottom of the paper.

**10. (a)** 60 − 1 = **59** [1].
**(b)** 60 × 180 = 10 800 and 59 × 18 = 1062 [1]; 10 800 − 1062 = **9738** [1].
**(c)** Complete hydrolysis uses 59 × 18 = **1062 g** of water per 9738 g [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A branch point is just another glycosidic bond, so branching does not change the n − 1 rule.

**11.** Starch is made of α-glucose [1] and is helical or branched, so it is compact [1] and insoluble, so it leaves the water potential of the cell unchanged [1]. Its branched ends can be hydrolysed to release glucose [1]. Cellulose is made of β-glucose [1] in straight chains joined by hydrogen bonds into strong microfibrils, giving tensile strength for a cell wall [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Pair each structural feature with its consequence; a list of features alone seldom answers an "explain" question.

**12.** Heat one portion of the extract with Benedict's solution [1]; the mixture stays blue, which rules out a reducing sugar [1]. Heat a second portion with dilute hydrochloric acid [1], then neutralise it, for example with sodium hydrogencarbonate [1]. Now heat it with Benedict's solution again [1]. A brick-red precipitate appears [1], which means hydrolysis released reducing sugars from a non-reducing sugar [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention a negative control of water to show the reagents are not responsible, and give start and end colours for each stage.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving "sugars" or "proteins" where monomers are asked for.
- Saying hydrolysis releases water.
- Writing that sucrose, maltose or lactose are all non-reducing.
- Not naming carbon 1 when comparing α- and β-glucose.
- Writing that glycosidic bonds hold cellulose chains together.
- Leaving out neutralisation in the non-reducing sugar test.
- Forgetting the n − 1 rule when counting bonds or water molecules.
- Giving R_f with units or measuring from the wrong baseline.
- Stating features of glycogen or starch without linking them to function.
