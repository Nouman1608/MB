Condensed recall for Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 subtopic **8.5 Noble gases**. For the full explanation and worked answers, use the [Noble gases study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-noble-gases/).

## The outcome

Describe the Group VIII noble gases as **unreactive, monatomic gases** and **explain** this in terms of **electronic configuration**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Noble gas | A Group VIII element: helium, neon, argon, krypton, xenon, radon |
| Unreactive | Does not take part in chemical reactions under normal conditions |
| Monatomic | Exists as single, separate atoms (not bonded in molecules) |
| Diatomic | Exists as molecules of two atoms (H₂, N₂, O₂, Cl₂), for contrast |
| Full outer shell | Outer shell holding its maximum number of electrons: 2 for helium, 8 for the others at this level |
| Noble gas configuration | The electronic configuration of a noble gas, reached by other atoms when they form ions or covalent bonds |

## Configurations to know

| Gas | Configuration |
|---|---|
| Helium | 2 |
| Neon | 2,8 |
| Argon | 2,8,8 |

## The explanation chain

```
full outer shell
   → no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons
       → unreactive (does not form compounds)
       → monatomic (atoms do not bond to each other)
   → only weak forces between separate atoms
       → very low boiling points → gases at r.t.p.
```

## Other atoms copy the noble gases

| Atom | Change | Ion | Configuration | Noble gas |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Na (2,8,1) | loses 1 e⁻ | Na⁺ | 2,8 | Neon |
| Mg (2,8,2) | loses 2 e⁻ | Mg²⁺ | 2,8 | Neon |
| O (2,6) | gains 2 e⁻ | O²⁻ | 2,8 | Neon |
| Cl (2,8,7) | gains 1 e⁻ | Cl⁻ | 2,8,8 | Argon |
| Ca (2,8,8,2) | loses 2 e⁻ | Ca²⁺ | 2,8,8 | Argon |

Covalent bonds do the same by sharing: each H atom in H₂ ends with two electrons (like helium); each Cl in Cl₂ with eight (like argon).

## Helium's place in the table

Helium (configuration 2) has two outer electrons, like the Group II metals, but it is placed in **Group VIII** because its only shell is full and it behaves like the other noble gases: unreactive, monatomic, a gas. Position in a group reflects chemical behaviour.

## Model answers for the three common question types

**"Describe the noble gases." [2–3]** They are unreactive; they are monatomic (exist as single atoms); they are gases at r.t.p. One mark per syllabus word.

**"Explain why neon is unreactive." [2]** Neon's outer shell is full (2,8). So it has no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons.

**"Explain why neon is monatomic." [2]** Each neon atom already has a full outer shell. So neon atoms do not share electrons with each other / do not form bonds, and stay as single atoms.

The second and third answers share the same first point; the difference is the consequence you draw from it.

## Writing noble gases in equations

- Symbols only: **He, Ne, Ar** (never He₂, Ar₂).
- Relative formula mass of argon gas = 40 (same as its relative atomic mass); compare Cl₂ = 71.

## Group VIII next to Group VII

| Feature | Group VII (e.g. chlorine) | Group VIII (e.g. argon) |
|---|---|---|
| Outer electrons | 7 | 8 (full) |
| Formula of element | Cl₂ (diatomic) | Ar (monatomic) |
| Reactivity | Reactive; gains one electron | Unreactive |
| Ion formed | Cl⁻ | None |
| Trend in reactivity down group | Decreases | No reactivity to compare |

One extra electron separates a very reactive element from an unreactive one, which is why the explanation must focus on whether the outer shell is full.

## Data skills

Boiling point **increases** down Group VIII (helium −269 °C, neon −246 °C, argon −186 °C, krypton −153 °C, xenon −108 °C). Predict radon above −108 °C and still a gas at r.t.p. Reactivity does **not** change: every member has a full outer shell.

## Exam traps

- Helium has **two** outer electrons, not eight; say "full outer shell".
- "Stable" is not an explanation: give the full shell and the electron consequence.
- Monatomic means **single atoms**, not "made of one type of atom" (all elements are).
- Do not write diatomic formulae for noble gases.
- They have electrons; they simply have no tendency to lose, gain or share them.
- Use the syllabus word **unreactive**; do not claim a reactivity trend down the group.
- Group VIII is sometimes printed as Group 0; both refer to the same column.

## Self-test

1. Name the three things the syllabus says the noble gases are.
2. Give the electronic configuration of neon.
3. Explain why argon is unreactive.
4. What does "monatomic" mean?
5. Why are noble gases monatomic when chlorine is diatomic?
6. How many outer-shell electrons does helium have, and why is its shell full?
7. Name the noble gas whose configuration is the same as that of a Ca²⁺ ion.
8. Give the formula of neon gas as it would appear in an equation.
9. Explain why the noble gases have low boiling points.
10. State the trend in boiling point down Group VIII.
11. Predict whether radon is reactive. Give a reason.
12. Name two ions with the same electronic configuration as neon.

**Answers:** 1. Unreactive, monatomic, gases. 2. 2,8. 3. Its outer shell is full (2,8,8), so it has no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons. 4. Existing as single, separate atoms. 5. Each noble gas atom already has a full outer shell, so atoms do not share electrons with each other; chlorine atoms share a pair to complete their outer shells. 6. Two; the first shell holds a maximum of two electrons. 7. Argon. 8. Ne. 9. The separate atoms are held by very weak forces, which need little energy to overcome. 10. Increases down the group. 11. Unreactive; it has a full outer shell like all noble gases. 12. Any two of Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺, O²⁻, F⁻, N³⁻.
