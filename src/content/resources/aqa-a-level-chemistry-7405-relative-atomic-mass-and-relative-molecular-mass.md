## Why carbon-12?

Atoms are far too light to weigh one at a time in grams, so chemists compare their masses with a standard. The agreed standard is the **carbon-12 atom**, given a mass of exactly 12. One twelfth of its mass is therefore exactly 1 on this scale, and every other relative mass is a comparison with that twelfth. Because a relative mass is one mass divided by another, it has **no units**.

## The definitions, built to score

**Relative atomic mass, Aᵣ:** the average (weighted mean) mass of an atom of an element, compared with one twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12.

```
Aᵣ = average mass of one atom of the element
     ÷ (1/12 × mass of one atom of carbon-12)
```

**Relative molecular mass, Mᵣ:** the average mass of a molecule, compared with one twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12.

```
Mᵣ = average mass of one molecule
     ÷ (1/12 × mass of one atom of carbon-12)
```

Each definition has three parts that must all appear: **average** (or weighted mean), **the particle** (an atom of the element, or a molecule), and **one twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom**. The common faults are writing "1/12 of carbon" (which carbon? which mass?), leaving out "average", or writing "compared with carbon-12" without the one twelfth.

**Why "average"?** Most elements are mixtures of isotopes. Chlorine atoms have masses of about 35 or 37, never 35.5. Aᵣ is the mean of all the atoms in a natural sample, weighted by abundance. The same logic makes Mᵣ an average: molecules of HCl contain either chlorine isotope.

**Relative isotopic mass** is different: the mass of one atom of a single isotope compared with one twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom. It is not an average.

## Choosing the term

| Substance | Structure | Term |
|---|---|---|
| H₂O, CO₂, C₆H₁₂O₆, Cl₂ | Simple molecules | Relative molecular mass |
| NaCl, MgO, CaCO₃ | Ionic lattice | Relative formula mass |
| CuSO₄·5H₂O | Ionic, hydrated | Relative formula mass |

The specification's wording is that relative formula mass "will be used for ionic compounds". An ionic compound has no separate molecules: its formula only gives the ratio of ions. The calculation is identical; only the name changes. The symbol Mᵣ is commonly written for both.

## Working out formula masses

Add the Aᵣ of every atom in the formula. Use the Aᵣ values printed on the Periodic Table provided in the exam, which are given to one decimal place.

### Method in steps

1. Write the formula and expand any brackets: a subscript after a bracket multiplies everything inside.
2. For a hydrate, treat "·xH₂O" as x extra water units added on, not multiplied.
3. List each element with its count.
4. Multiply each count by Aᵣ and add.
5. Keep the answer to one decimal place, as the data allow.

### Worked example 1: a bracket

Aluminium sulfate, Al₂(SO₄)₃. (Aᵣ: Al = 27.0, S = 32.1, O = 16.0)

```
Al  2 × 27.0          =  54.0
S   3 × 32.1          =  96.3
O   3 × 4 = 12 × 16.0 = 192.0
relative formula mass = 342.3
```

The trap is counting 4 oxygens instead of 12.

### Worked example 2: a hydrate

Hydrated copper(II) sulfate, CuSO₄·5H₂O. (Aᵣ: Cu = 63.5, S = 32.1, O = 16.0, H = 1.0)

```
CuSO₄  63.5 + 32.1 + (4 × 16.0) = 159.6
5H₂O   5 × (2 × 1.0 + 16.0)    =  90.0
relative formula mass           = 249.6
```

The dot does not mean multiply: 159.6 × 90.0 is not the answer.

### Percentage by mass

Once you have the formula mass, the percentage of an element or of the water is a direct ratio.

```
% water in CuSO₄·5H₂O = 90.0 ÷ 249.6 × 100 = 36.1 %
```

## Using relative masses in reverse

Questions often give an Mᵣ and ask for an unknown part of the formula.

### Worked example 3: identifying a metal

A metal carbonate MCO₃ has a relative formula mass of 197.3. Identify M.

```
CO₃ = 12.0 + 3 × 16.0 = 60.0
Aᵣ(M) = 197.3 − 60.0 = 137.3
```

The Periodic Table gives 137.3 for barium, so M is barium.

### Worked example 4: water of crystallisation

Hydrated magnesium sulfate, MgSO₄·xH₂O, has a relative formula mass of 246.4. Find x. (Aᵣ: Mg = 24.3)

```
MgSO₄ = 24.3 + 32.1 + 64.0 = 120.4
water = 246.4 − 120.4 = 126.0
x = 126.0 ÷ 18.0 = 7
```

## Relative mass and molar mass

Mᵣ has no units. The mass of one mole of a substance, its **molar mass**, has the same number but the unit g mol⁻¹: water has Mᵣ = 18.0 and molar mass 18.0 g mol⁻¹. Writing "Mᵣ = 18.0 g" mixes the two ideas. The link between them is the mole, which is 3.1.2.2.

## Common mistakes

- Definitions missing "average", or missing "one twelfth", or comparing with "carbon" rather than "an atom of carbon-12".
- Giving units for Aᵣ or Mᵣ.
- Calling the formula mass of sodium chloride a relative molecular mass.
- Multiplying only the first atom in a bracket by the subscript.
- Multiplying by the water in a hydrate instead of adding it.
- Using rounded whole-number Aᵣ values (Cl = 35, Cu = 64) when the Periodic Table supplied gives one decimal place.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Define**: give the full definition with all three parts; an equation-style ratio is a good alternative to words.
- **Calculate**: show each element's contribution on its own line so a slip costs only one mark.
- **Deduce** or **identify**: subtract the known part, then name the element and quote the matching Aᵣ.
- **Explain** why Aᵣ is not a whole number: refer to isotopes of different mass and to a weighted mean.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is the relative atomic mass of chlorine 35.5 when no chlorine atom has that mass?**
Aᵣ is a weighted mean of the isotopes in a natural sample. About three quarters of chlorine atoms are chlorine-35 and a quarter are chlorine-37, so the mean is 35.5.

**Can the symbol Mᵣ be used for an ionic compound?**
The symbol Mᵣ is commonly written for all compounds, but the specification says the term relative formula mass will be used for ionic compounds, so use that name when describing one.

**What is the difference between Mᵣ and molar mass?**
They have the same value, but Mᵣ is a ratio with no units, while molar mass is the mass of one mole and has units of g mol⁻¹.
