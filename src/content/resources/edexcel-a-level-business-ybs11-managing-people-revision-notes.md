## Staffing in brief

- **Staff as an asset:** invest in training and retention; suits firms where skills and service create advantage.
- **Staff as a cost:** minimise and flex wage spending; suits firms with volatile demand and simple tasks.
- **Flexible workforce:** multi-skilling; part-time and temporary; zero-hour contracts; flexible hours and home working; outsourcing.
- **Dismissal:** the employee's conduct or capability ends the contract. **Redundancy:** the job disappears.
- **Individual approach:** terms agreed person by person. **Collective bargaining:** terms agreed with representatives for a group.

## Recruitment and training

| | Internal | External |
|---|---|---|
| Cost and speed | Cheaper, faster | Dearer, slower |
| Knowledge of candidate | Known performance | Interview evidence only |
| New ideas | Few | More |
| Side effects | Creates another vacancy; motivates others | May upset internal candidates |

**Costs:** advertising, agency fees, managers' time, induction, training courses, lost output while the newcomer learns.

**Training:** induction (introduction to the business); on-the-job (learning while working, cheap, specific); off-the-job (away from the workplace, dearer, broader).

## Structure

- Fewer levels ↔ wider spans. Number of managers at each level = people below ÷ span.
- **Tall:** narrow spans, close control, more promotion steps, slow communication, higher management cost.
- **Flat:** wide spans, faster communication, more autonomy, overstretched managers, fewer promotion steps.
- **Matrix:** project teams across functions; two bosses; flexible but conflict-prone.
- **Centralised:** consistent, controlled, slow to respond locally. **Decentralised:** responsive and motivating, risk of inconsistency.

## Motivation

| Theorist | One-line idea | Method it supports |
|---|---|---|
| Taylor | Money and efficient task design | Piecework, close supervision |
| Mayo | Social needs and attention | Team working, consultation |
| Maslow | Hierarchy: physiological → safety → social → esteem → self-actualisation | Match rewards to the level reached |
| Herzberg | Hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction; motivators create satisfaction | Job enrichment, empowerment |

**Financial:** piecework (per unit), commission (% of sales), bonus (lump sum for a target), profit share, performance-related pay (linked to appraisal).

**Non-financial:** delegation, consultation, empowerment, team working, flexible working, job enrichment (more responsibility), job rotation (moving between tasks), job enlargement (more tasks at the same level).

**Matching method to problem:**

| Problem in the source | Likely cause | Method that targets it |
|---|---|---|
| Staff leave for rivals paying more | Unmet hygiene factor (Herzberg) | Review pay to the local rate |
| Repetitive work, boredom | Lack of motivators | Job rotation or enrichment |
| Isolated staff, weak team spirit | Unmet social needs (Mayo, Maslow) | Team working, consultation |
| Output too low on simple tasks | Little incentive per unit | Piecework or bonus (Taylor) |
| Skilled staff frustrated by slow approvals | Too little authority | Delegation, empowerment |

## Leadership

- **Management** plans, organises and controls; **leadership** sets direction and inspires.
- **Autocratic:** leader decides alone. **Paternalistic:** leader decides in staff's interests and explains. **Democratic:** staff take part. **Laissez-faire:** staff decide within broad aims.
- **Entrepreneur to leader:** the founder must delegate and trust others; failure to do so creates bottlenecks and loses talent.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Multi-skilling | Training staff to do several different jobs |
| Zero-hour contract | No guaranteed minimum hours; paid only for hours worked |
| Outsourcing | Paying another business to carry out a function |
| Collective bargaining | Negotiation between employers and employee representatives |
| Span of control | Number of subordinates reporting directly to one manager |
| Chain of command | The route through which instructions pass down a hierarchy |
| Delegation | Passing authority for a task to a subordinate |
| Empowerment | Giving staff the power to make decisions about their own work |
| Hygiene factor | A factor whose absence causes dissatisfaction (Herzberg) |
| Paternalistic leadership | Leader decides, but with staff welfare in mind |

## Exam traps

- Enrichment adds **responsibility**; enlargement adds **tasks**; rotation **moves** people.
- Pay is a hygiene factor for Herzberg but the main motivator for Taylor: say which theory you are using.
- Redundancy is about the job, dismissal about the person.
- Flat is not automatically better than tall; it depends on staff skills and the work.
- Commission and piecework reward quantity, which may lower quality.
- Mayo's work points to social needs, not to higher pay.

## Self-test

1. State two forms of flexible working named in the specification.
2. Distinguish dismissal from redundancy.
3. Give one advantage of internal recruitment.
4. A business has 360 staff and a span of control of 12. How many first-line managers does it need?
5. Halve that span to 6. How many first-line managers are now needed?
6. Give one drawback of a matrix structure.
7. State Herzberg's two groups of factors, with one example each.
8. Which theorist is linked to scientific management?
9. Distinguish job enrichment from job enlargement.
10. A salesperson earns 3% commission on sales of $240,000. Calculate the commission.
11. Which leadership style suits a team of highly skilled researchers, and why?
12. Why can moving from entrepreneur to leader be difficult?

### Answers

1. Any two: multi-skilling; part-time and temporary; zero-hour contracts; flexible hours and home working; outsourcing.
2. Dismissal ends a contract for a reason linked to the employee; redundancy happens because the job is no longer needed.
3. Cheaper and quicker, or the candidate's performance is already known, or promotion motivates other staff.
4. 360 ÷ 12 = **30**.
5. 360 ÷ 6 = **60**.
6. Two lines of authority can cause conflict over priorities and confusion about who to report to.
7. Hygiene factors, such as pay or working conditions; motivators, such as recognition or responsibility.
8. Taylor.
9. Enrichment adds responsibility and more challenging work; enlargement adds more tasks at the same level.
10. 3% × $240,000 = **$7,200**.
11. Laissez-faire or democratic, because skilled, self-motivated staff work best with freedom and their expertise improves decisions.
12. The founder is used to making every decision, so must learn to delegate and trust others; failure creates bottlenecks and frustrates skilled staff.
