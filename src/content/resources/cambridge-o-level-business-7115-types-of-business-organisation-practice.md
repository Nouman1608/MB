## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define unlimited liability. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** differences between a sole trader and a private limited company. **[2]**

**3.** Identify **two** features of a franchise. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why a limited company has continuity. **[2]**

**5.** Outline **two** disadvantages of a partnership. **[4]**

**6.** State **two** aims of a public corporation. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** Kestrel Cycles is owned by Sami as a sole trader. He repairs and sells bicycles from a small rented shop. He wants to open a second, larger shop, which will need $50,000 of new capital. His friend Lena, an experienced mechanic, has offered to invest $25,000.

**(a)** Explain **two** disadvantages to Sami of remaining a sole trader.

**(b)** Sami is considering either forming a partnership with Lena or forming a private limited company with Lena as a shareholder. Recommend which form he should choose. Justify your answer. **[10]**

**8.** Dana is buying a franchise to run a sandwich bar. She pays the franchisor an initial fee of $30,000 and a royalty of 5% of her sales revenue each year. In the first year she expects sales revenue of $360,000 and other costs of $250,000.

**(a)** Calculate her expected profit for the first year, after the fee and the royalty.

**(b)** Explain **two** benefits to Dana of buying a franchise rather than opening a sandwich bar under her own name. **[7]**

**9.** The Okafor family owns a private limited company that makes paint. They are thinking of turning it into a public limited company.

**(a)** Explain what is meant by the separation of ownership and control.

**(b)** Explain **one** risk to the Okafor family of becoming a public limited company. **[4]**

**10.** Greenfold Foods is a food manufacturer in Country A. A large supermarket chain from Country B proposes a joint venture to open shops in Country A selling both businesses' products. Consider the benefits and limitations of the joint venture for Greenfold Foods and recommend whether it should agree. Justify your answer. **[6]**

**11.** A sole trader closes her business owing $48,000. Selling the business's equipment and stock raises $20,000.

**(a)** Calculate the amount she must pay from her personal assets.

**(b)** If the business had been a private limited company in which she had invested $15,000 in shares, state the most she could have lost.

**(c)** Explain why a bank may still be cautious about lending to a small private limited company. **[4]**

**12.** Explain **two** advantages and **two** disadvantages of providing postal services through a public corporation. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The owners are personally responsible for all the debts of the business [1], so they may have to use personal assets such as savings or a house to pay them [1].

**2.** Any two [1] [1]: a sole trader has one owner, a company has shareholders; a sole trader has unlimited liability, company shareholders have limited liability; a company is a separate legal entity, a sole trader is not; a company has continuity, a sole trader business does not; a company must publish some financial information.

**3.** Any two [1] [1]: the franchisee uses the franchisor's brand name and methods; the franchisee pays an initial fee and royalties; the franchisor supplies training, products or promotion; the franchisee must follow the franchisor's rules.

**4.** A limited company is a separate legal entity from its owners [1], so it continues to exist when a shareholder dies or sells their shares to someone else [1].

**5.** Two disadvantages, each identified [1] and outlined [1]: partners usually have unlimited liability, so each can lose personal assets; partners may disagree over decisions, slowing the business down; profits must be shared between the partners; the partnership may have to be re-formed if a partner leaves or dies.

**6.** Any two [1] [1]: provide an essential service to everyone; keep prices affordable; serve areas private businesses would not; protect jobs; cover costs or break even.

**7.** **(a)** Two disadvantages, each identified [1] and explained [1]: unlimited liability means Sami could lose personal assets if the new shop fails and he cannot repay debts; he is unlikely to have $50,000 himself, so limited capital restricts the expansion; he bears all the workload and risk alone, which is harder with two shops.

**(b)** Partnership: Lena brings $25,000 and mechanic skills, and it is simple and cheap to set up [1]; but both would have unlimited liability for debts of the larger shop [1]. Private limited company: Sami and Lena would have limited liability, protecting their personal assets on a $50,000 expansion [1]; the business would have continuity if either leaves [1]; set-up costs and publishing some accounts are drawbacks [1]. Recommendation: a private limited company, because the expansion is large and risky, so limited liability matters more than the extra cost [1].

**8.** **(a)** Royalty = 5% × 360,000 = $18,000 [1]. Profit = 360,000 − 250,000 − 18,000 − 30,000 [1] = **$62,000** [1].

**(b)** Two benefits, each identified [1] and explained [1]: the known brand attracts customers from the start, reducing the risk of failure; she receives training and a tested way of running the bar, which helps if she lacks experience; she benefits from the franchisor's national promotion; she can buy supplies through the franchisor, possibly at lower cost.

**9.** **(a)** The shareholders own the company [1] but directors and managers control its day-to-day running, so the owners do not make the decisions themselves [1].

**(b)** One risk identified [1] and explained [1]: shares can be bought by anyone, so the family could lose control or face a takeover if outsiders buy a majority; or more financial information must be published, which competitors can see.

**10.** Benefits: Greenfold gains new shops to sell its products without paying the full cost [1]; costs and risks of opening the shops are shared [1]; it may learn retail skills from the supermarket chain [1]. Limitations: profits must be shared with the partner [1]; the two businesses may disagree over control, prices or which products get shelf space [1]. Judgement: Greenfold should agree if the agreement sets out control and profit shares clearly, because it gains a large new outlet for its products at shared risk [1].

**11.** **(a)** 48,000 − 20,000 = **$28,000** [1].

**(b)** **$15,000** [1].

**(c)** Shareholders cannot be made to pay the company's debts from personal assets [1], so if the company fails the bank may not get all its money back; it may ask the owners for a personal guarantee [1].

**12.** Advantages, any two [1] [1]: a service is provided to all areas, including remote ones that might be unprofitable; prices can be kept affordable; the government can consider social needs, such as jobs, not only profit. Disadvantages, any two [1] [1]: less pressure to be efficient if losses are covered by the government; political interference in decisions; funding competes with other government spending such as schools.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that a limited company "cannot go bankrupt"; it can fail, only the owners' loss is limited.
- Forgetting to subtract the royalty or the initial fee in question 8.
- Recommending a form without using facts from the case, such as the $50,000 in question 7.
- Confusing a public limited company with a public corporation in question 12.
- Giving benefits only in question 10, with no limitations or judgement.
