## Structure of diamond

Diamond is a form of the element **carbon**. A carbon atom has four electrons in its outer shell. In diamond,
each carbon atom uses all four to form **four covalent bonds** with four other carbon atoms. These bonds point
towards the corners of a tetrahedron, so the atoms are arranged in a rigid three-dimensional network.

```
          C
         /|\
        / | \
       C  |  C        Each carbon atom is bonded to four others.
        \ | /         The pattern repeats in all directions.
         \|/
          C
```

The structure is a **giant covalent structure**. There are no separate molecules. The whole crystal is one
giant network of atoms joined by strong covalent bonds. The formula is simply C, because the structure contains
only carbon atoms.

## Properties explained

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Very hard | Each carbon atom is held by four strong covalent bonds in a rigid 3D network, so it is very hard to push atoms out of position |
| Very high melting point | The strong covalent bonds must be broken to melt it, and there are very many of them, so a very large amount of energy is needed |
| Does not conduct electricity | All four outer electrons of every carbon atom are used in covalent bonds, so there are no delocalised electrons and no ions to carry charge |

### Hardness

When a force is applied to diamond, the atoms cannot slide past one another, because each is locked in place by
four bonds. To scratch or cut the crystal, covalent bonds must be broken. That is why diamond is among the
hardest natural materials.

### Melting point

Diamond does not melt at the temperature at which most substances do. To change its state, the covalent bonds
must be broken in a very large number of places. This takes a very large amount of energy, so the melting point
is very high, above 3500 °C.

### Electrical conduction

Conduction needs charged particles that are free to move. In diamond all the outer electrons are held in
localised covalent bonds. No electrons are delocalised, and there are no ions. So diamond does **not** conduct
electricity. Be careful with the contrast: graphite is another form of carbon, but it does conduct, because in
graphite each carbon atom has only three bonds, leaving one electron free.

## Uses that follow from the properties

| Use | Property used |
|---|---|
| Cutting tools and drill bits | Extreme hardness |
| Polished gemstones in jewellery | Hardness; it keeps its shine and does not scratch easily |
| Coatings on tools | Hardness |

The specification asks you to explain properties, so be ready to link any use you meet in an exam question to
the structure.

## Worked examples

### Worked example 1: why diamond is hard

*Explain why diamond is very hard.*

```
1. Diamond has a giant covalent structure.
2. Each carbon atom is joined to four other carbon atoms by strong covalent bonds.
3. The atoms are held rigidly in a 3D network and cannot slide.
4. A lot of energy/force is needed to break the covalent bonds, so it is very hard.
```

### Worked example 2: why diamond has a very high melting point

*Explain why diamond has a very high melting point.*

```
Giant covalent structure; every atom is linked by strong covalent bonds in all directions.
These covalent bonds must be broken to melt it.
There are very many bonds, so a very large amount of energy is needed.
```

Do not write "intermolecular forces". Diamond has no molecules.

### Worked example 3: why diamond does not conduct

*Explain why diamond does not conduct electricity.*

```
Each carbon atom has four outer electrons.
All four are used in covalent bonds.
There are no delocalised electrons and no ions, so no charged particles are free to move.
```

### Worked example 4: counting bonds

*A diamond crystal contains 500 carbon atoms. Assuming every atom is bonded to four others and that each bond
joins two atoms, how many covalent bonds are there?*

```
Each atom has 4 bonds, but each bond is shared by 2 atoms.
Bonds = 500 x 4 / 2 = 1000
```

This idea, counting each bond once, is the same as the idea behind the SiO₂ ratio. (A real crystal has atoms at
the surface that have fewer than four bonds, so the exact number is slightly lower.)

### Worked example 5: identifying diamond from information

*A solid has a melting point above 3500 °C. It does not conduct electricity and it is the hardest known natural
material. It contains only carbon. Name it and say what type of structure it has.*

Diamond, a giant covalent structure.

## Diamond compared with graphite

| Property | Diamond | Graphite |
|---|---|---|
| Bonds per carbon atom | 4 | 3 |
| Structure | 3D network | Layers of hexagonal rings |
| Hardness | Very hard | Soft and slippery |
| Conducts electricity | No | Yes |
| Melting point | Very high | Very high |

Both are forms of carbon with giant covalent structures. Comparing them is common in exam questions, and it is
explored further in the [graphite guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-graphite/).

## Common mistakes

- Writing "diamond has strong intermolecular forces". It has no molecules.
- Saying only "strong bonds" without "covalent" or without "many bonds must be broken".
- Writing "diamond has four electrons in its outer shell, so it conducts". All four are used in bonding.
- Writing "diamond is an element, so it is a metal". Carbon is a non-metal.
- Mixing up diamond and graphite: diamond has four bonds per atom, graphite has three.
- Saying the carbon atoms are "hard". Hardness is a bulk property of the structure.

## Exam technique

- **Describe the structure:** giant covalent; each carbon bonded to four others; strong covalent bonds.
- **Explain hardness:** rigid 3D network; many strong covalent bonds.
- **Explain melting point:** covalent bonds must be broken; large amount of energy.
- **Explain non-conduction:** no delocalised electrons or ions.
- **Compare with graphite:** use the number of bonds per atom.
