## The sequence

**Disguise or concealment** opens a gap between identity and appearance → **escape** moves a
character out of danger or out of ordinary rules → **discovery** closes the gap and allows
resolution. **The supernatural** adds the unexplained, which the text may take seriously, mock or
explain away.

Always ask: who knows, who does not, and what does the audience know?

## Disguise by type

| Type | Example | What it reveals |
|---|---|---|
| Gender | Viola as Cesario (*Twelfth Night*) | She must woo for the man she loves; identity and desire are unstable |
| Rank | Kate taken for a barmaid (*She Stoops to Conquer*) | Marlow's split manners; Kate learns the real man |
| Multiplied | Cambio, false Lucentio, a pedant posing as Vincentio (*The Taming of the Shrew*) | Comedy of collision when the real Vincentio arrives |
| Habit | Jack and Algernon as "Ernest" (*The Importance of Being Earnest*) | Pretence as a way of life for the leisured class |
| Social mask | Frank Churchill's concealed engagement (*Emma*) | Manners can deceive; Emma's misreading is the target |

## Escapes by type

- **Physical**: Chauntecleer from the fox; Tam from the witches, his mare losing her tail at the bridge.
- **Social**: Bunburying in *Earnest*; the planned elopement of Hastings and Constance.
- **From disaster**: Viola's survival of the shipwreck at the start of *Twelfth Night*.

Escapes are comic when narrow, undignified or overtaken by discovery.

## Discovery by type

- **By evidence**: Viola and Sebastian question each other about their father.
- **By object**: the handbag left at a railway station in *Earnest*.
- **As humiliation**: Marlow learns the "barmaid" is Miss Hardcastle.
- **As self-knowledge**: Emma recognises her errors and her love for Mr Knightley.

## Supernatural elements

- **Dream and prophecy**: Chauntecleer's dream and his learned debate with Pertelote.
- **Witchcraft**: the witches' dance and the Devil's music in "Tam o' Shanter".
- **Pretended possession**: Malvolio treated as mad; Feste disguised as the curate Sir Topas.
- **Legend**: the dragon's complaint in "Not My Best Side", spoken from Uccello's painting.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Dramatic irony | The audience knows what characters do not |
| Mistaken identity | Confusion caused by one character being taken for another |
| Recognition (anagnorisis) | Discovery of true identity; in comedy it brings joy |
| Recognition token | An object that proves identity, such as the handbag |
| Breeches role | A female character disguised as a man |
| Mock-heroic | Treating a trivial subject in grand style, as in the cockerel's dream debate |
| Farce | Comedy of improbable action and near-collision |
| Go-between | A character who carries messages between lovers |

## Context to use

- **Boy actors**: on the Elizabethan stage, female roles were played by boys, so Viola's disguise was
  layered.
- **Rank and manners**: eighteenth-century codes of class make Kate's stooping and Marlow's two
  manners funny.
- **Respectability**: Wilde's double lives satirise Victorian earnestness and the need to appear
  respectable.

## Exam traps

- Listing disguises without explaining the gap they open.
- Describing discovery as instant when the writer delays it.
- Treating the supernatural as decoration rather than asking how seriously the text takes it.
- Forgetting the audience's knowledge, which is the engine of disguise comedy.
- Leaving out escapes, which AQA names in the same phrase.
- Ignoring what remains unresolved after discovery, such as Viola still in men's clothes.

## Self-test

1. Quote the AQA aspect this page covers.
2. Explain how the four elements form a sequence.
3. Why is Viola's disguise layered on the Elizabethan stage?
4. What does Kate's disguise reveal about Marlow?
5. Which characters take false identities in the Bianca plot of *The Taming of the Shrew*?
6. What does "Bunburying" mean?
7. How does Chauntecleer escape from the fox?
8. How does Tam give himself away to the witches?
9. How do Viola and Sebastian confirm each other's identity?
10. What is the recognition token in *Earnest*?
11. Which character in *Twelfth Night* combines disguise with a pretence of the supernatural?
12. Why can *Emma* be discussed under disguise even though no one wears a costume?

## Answers

1. "The use of disguise, escapes and discovery, elements of the supernatural".
2. Disguise opens a gap between identity and appearance; escape moves characters away from danger or
   rules; discovery closes the gap and allows resolution; the supernatural adds the unexplained.
3. Female roles were played by boys, so a boy played a woman disguised as a young man.
4. That he is shy with women of his own class but bold with women he thinks are servants.
5. Lucentio as the tutor Cambio, Tranio as Lucentio, Hortensio as a music tutor, and a pedant as
   Lucentio's father Vincentio.
6. Inventing a person or excuse in order to escape social obligations.
7. He persuades the fox to speak, and escapes when it opens its mouth.
8. He shouts out in admiration of one of the dancing witches.
9. They question each other about their father before accepting the truth.
10. The handbag Miss Prism left at a railway station.
11. Feste, disguised as the curate Sir Topas visiting Malvolio, who is treated as possessed.
12. Frank Churchill conceals his engagement to Jane Fairfax behind social manners, so concealment
    works like disguise and misleads Emma.
