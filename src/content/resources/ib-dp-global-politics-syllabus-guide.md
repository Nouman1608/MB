This guide covers the full syllabus structure of **IB Diploma Programme
Global Politics**, first assessment 2026, for both Standard Level (SL)
and Higher Level (HL) students. It complements the
[subject overview](/resources/ib-dp-global-politics-subject-guide/) and
the
[assessment revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-global-politics-assessment-revision-notes/)
already on the site by listing what is actually taught -- the core
thematic studies, the internal assessment, and the HL-only extension.

Total teaching time is 150 hours at SL and 240 hours at HL. Unlike some
DP individuals and societies subjects, the core content hours (125) are
identical for SL and HL -- the difference between the two levels comes
from the internal assessment allocation and, at HL, an entire additional
extension component.

## Core (125 hours, both SL and HL)

### Understanding power and global politics

The foundational strand introducing core concepts -- power, sovereignty,
legitimacy and interdependence -- that are then explored critically
throughout the rest of the course. Rather than being taught and
assessed as a stand-alone block, this strand functions more like a
toolkit: students return to these four concepts again and again as they
work through the three thematic studies, applying them to genuinely
different political contexts each time. Power is examined not just as a
single idea but through multiple lenses -- who holds it, how it is
exercised, and how it is limited or checked -- while sovereignty,
legitimacy and interdependence are each explored as contested rather
than fixed concepts, since real political actors frequently disagree
about what counts as legitimate authority or genuine independence.

### Thematic studies -- three required themes

- **Rights and justice** -- how rights are claimed, contested and
  enforced (or denied) across political systems.
- **Development and sustainability** -- how development is pursued and
  measured, and its tensions with environmental and social
  sustainability.
- **Peace and conflict** -- the causes, dynamics and resolution of
  political conflict.

Unlike the geography course's optional themes, all three thematic
studies here are compulsory for every student, at both SL and HL --
there is no teacher-selected subset to confirm. This means every DP
Global Politics student, regardless of school or teacher, studies the
same three thematic studies alongside the same foundational strand on
power -- a genuinely shared syllabus core across the whole subject, not
just a shared assessment structure.

## Internal assessment -- engagement project (25 hours SL / 35 hours HL)

A written report on a political issue explored through the student's own
engagement and research. The HL allocation is 10 hours longer than SL's,
reflecting the greater depth expected in the written report itself
rather than any difference in the topics available to write about. As
with the fieldwork-style internal assessments in other DP individuals
and societies subjects, the strongest engagement projects tend to come
from students who commit to a specific, well-scoped political issue
early -- one they can genuinely engage with directly, whether through
research, interviews or participation -- rather than a broad topic that
only allows for secondary-source summary.

## HL extension only -- Global political challenges (80 hours, HL only)

This entire component does not exist for SL students. It is examined
separately in Paper 3, which only HL students sit, and is worth 30% of
the HL final grade on its own -- a larger single-component weighting
than either Paper 1 or the engagement project at HL. The extension asks
students to examine political issues that cut across the boundaries of
a single thematic study, requiring genuine synthesis of material from
Rights and justice, Development and sustainability and Peace and
conflict rather than treating global political challenges as a fourth,
separate topic learned in isolation from the three compulsory themes.

## How to approach it

Because the three thematic studies are compulsory (not options, as in
several other DP individuals and societies subjects), build revision
coverage across all three from the start rather than gambling on which
one might come up -- Paper 2's extended-response questions draw on
prescribed content from the thematic studies directly. The recurring
concepts from "Understanding power and global politics" (power,
sovereignty, legitimacy, interdependence) are meant to be applied inside
every thematic-study answer, not recalled as a separate definitions
list, so practise naming which of the four concepts a given real-world
case actually illustrates as you work through Rights and justice,
Development and sustainability, and Peace and conflict in turn. HL
students should treat the global political challenges extension as a
genuinely separate 80-hour block of content with its own paper and
weighting, not a light add-on to the shared core.

Because this course is explicitly built around "contemporary real-world
examples and cases", the strongest revision resource most students
underuse is simply following current political news across the year the
course is taught, rather than relying only on textbook case studies
written before the course started -- a live, ongoing example you can
discuss with genuine up-to-date detail is usually more persuasive in an
extended-response answer than a well-known but dated case everyone else
in the cohort is also using. Keep a running case-study log by theme
(Rights and justice, Development and sustainability, Peace and conflict)
as the course progresses, noting which political concepts each example
illustrates, so that by the time Paper 2 and the engagement project
arrive you have a ready bank of material rather than needing to research
examples from scratch under time pressure.
