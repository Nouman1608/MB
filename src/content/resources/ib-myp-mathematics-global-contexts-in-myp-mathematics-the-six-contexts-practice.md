## Questions

**1.** Name the global context that best fits each unit description: (i) designing a floor from repeating tiles; (ii) comparing the daily water allowance per person in two villages; (iii) planning a journey across time zones. **[3]**

**2.** State two things the guide says about how teachers use global contexts. **[2]**

**3.** Name the three key concepts that mathematics contributes to the MYP. **[3]**

**4.** A unit has the statement of inquiry "Choosing a suitable model helps a community plan its use of limited resources." Identify a key concept, two related concepts and the global context it most suggests. **[4]**

**5.** Three drafts are offered for a unit on proportion. Draft A: "Proportion". Draft B: "Why does proportion matter?". Draft C: "Comparing quantities on a fair basis shapes the decisions a community makes." Choose the best statement of inquiry and give two reasons. **[3]**

**6.** (MYP 1-3 and MYP 4-5) A map has scale 1 : 20 000. A path runs 9 cm east and then 12 cm north on the map. A walker moves at 4.5 km/h.

**(a)** Find the length of the path in kilometres. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the length of the straight line between the start and the end, in kilometres. **[2]**
**(c)** Find how many minutes the walker saves by taking the straight line. **[2]**

**7.** (Fairness and development) A fund of 12 000 dollars is to be shared between town P (2 500 people) and town Q (3 500 people).

**(a)** The fund is split equally. Find the amount per person in each town, to the nearest cent. **[3]**
**(b)** The fund is instead shared in proportion to population. Find each town's share. **[2]**

**8.** (MYP 4-5, personal and cultural expression) A craftsperson plans tiling patterns with regular polygons.

**(a)** Find the interior angle of a regular hexagon. **[1]**
**(b)** Show that regular pentagons alone cannot tile a flat surface without gaps by finding the gap left by three pentagons at a point. **[3]**
**(c)** Show that two regular hexagons and two equilateral triangles can fit round a point. **[2]**

**9.** (MYP 4-5, scientific and technical innovation) A solar panel keeps 99% of its output each year, so after n years the output is 100 × 0.99ⁿ percent of the original.

**(a)** Find the output after 10 years, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the output after 25 years, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Give one reason why the model may not match a real panel. **[2]**

**10.** (MYP 4-5 standard, globalization and sustainability) Data supplied: sea freight produces 0.011 kg of carbon dioxide per tonne-kilometre and air freight 0.6 kg per tonne-kilometre. A shop moves 5 tonnes of goods. Sea freight covers 12 000 km. Air freight covers 8 000 km.

**(a)** Find the emissions for the sea route. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the emissions for the air route, in standard form. **[2]**
**(c)** Find how many times greater the air emissions are, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(d)** State one limitation of comparing the two routes in this way. **[2]**

**11.** (MYP 4-5 standard, linear functions) A bike-share scheme charges a start fee of 1.50 dollars plus 0.40 dollars for each minute, so the cost in dollars is C = 1.50 + 0.40t for a ride of t minutes. A class is planning a unit on this model.

**(a)** Find the cost of a 25 minute ride. **[2]**
**(b)** A rider pays 8.30 dollars. Find the length of the ride. **[2]**
**(c)** Write a statement of inquiry for the unit. Name your key concept, two related concepts and the global context. **[4]**
**(d)** Write one factual question and one conceptual question that follow from your statement. **[2]**

**12.** (Fairness and development) A school's planning rule, given here as data, says a ramp may rise no more than 1 m for every 12 m of horizontal distance. A doorway step is 0.45 m high. The space available along the ground is 5.0 m.

**(a)** Find the least horizontal distance a single straight ramp needs. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the length of the sloping surface of that ramp, to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain what your results mean for the school's plan. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** (i) Personal and cultural expression [1]. (ii) Fairness and development [1]. (iii) Orientation in space and time [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Some units can sit in more than one context. When that happens, name the one that best explains why the inquiry matters, and give a short reason.

**2.** Teachers have to pick a global context for their teaching, and may create extra contexts that show why an inquiry matters [1]. Many mathematics inquiries fit scientific and technical innovation, but teachers are encouraged to offer the other contexts too [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* For a two-part "state" question, give two separate points. A single long sentence that blends them is harder to credit.

**3.** Form [1], logic [1], relationships [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Do not mix these up with the related concepts. Words such as models, patterns or systems are related concepts, not key concepts.

**4.** Key concept: relationships [1]. Related concepts, any two sensible ones such as models [1] and quantity or systems [1]. Context: globalization and sustainability [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Several answers can be sensible. Pick concepts that match the words in the statement, and be ready to say which words point to each.

**5.** Draft C is best [1]. It is a full sentence stating a general idea, not a topic name or a question [1]. It places the mathematics in a context (decisions a community makes) and names a concept (fair comparison, relationships) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A reason such as "it is longer" earns little. Say what makes a statement of inquiry different from a topic or a question.

**6. (a)** Path on the map = 9 + 12 = 21 cm [1]. 21 × 20 000 = 420 000 cm = **4.2 km** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Straight line = the square root of 9² + 12² = 15 cm [1]. 15 × 20 000 = 300 000 cm = **3.0 km** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Times: 4.2 ÷ 4.5 × 60 = 56 minutes and 3.0 ÷ 4.5 × 60 = 40 minutes [1]. Saving = **16 minutes** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the unit conversion from centimetres to kilometres. A common slip is dividing by 1 000 rather than 100 000.

**7. (a)** Equal split: 6 000 dollars each [1]. P: 6000 ÷ 2500 = **2.40 dollars** per person [1]. Q: 6000 ÷ 3500 = **1.71 dollars** per person [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** P: 12 000 × 2500 ÷ 6000 = **5 000 dollars** [1]. Q: 12 000 × 3500 ÷ 6000 = **7 000 dollars** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Round money to the nearest cent only at the end. Give a one-line comment that equal totals are not equal per person.

**8. (a)** (6 - 2) × 180 ÷ 6 = **120°** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Interior angle of a regular pentagon = 3 × 180 ÷ 5 = 108° [1]. Three pentagons give 3 × 108 = 324° [1]. Gap = 360 - 324 = **36°**, so they do not fill the point [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** The triangle's angle is 60° [1]. 2 × 120 + 2 × 60 = **360°**, so they fit with no gap [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" question, state the target value and finish with a clear statement that it has been reached.

**9. (a)** 100 × 0.99¹⁰ [1] = **90.4%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 100 × 0.99²⁵ [1] = **77.8%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The model assumes the same percentage loss every year [1]. Real output also varies with weather, dirt or temperature, so the model is a simplification [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A model comment is stronger when it names a specific assumption and says how reality could differ from it.

**10. (a)** 5 × 12 000 × 0.011 [1] = **660 kg** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 5 × 8 000 × 0.6 = 24 000 [1] = **2.4 × 10⁴ kg** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 24 000 ÷ 660 [1] = **36.4 times** greater [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Any one, such as: only transport is counted, so production and storage are ignored [1]. The comparison therefore only shows part of the environmental picture [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the units in each line of working. Say what the limitation does to the conclusion, not only what is missing.

**11. (a)** C = 1.50 + 0.40 × 25 [1] = **11.50 dollars** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 8.30 = 1.50 + 0.40t, so 0.40t = 6.80 [1]. t = **17 minutes** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Key concept: relationships [1]. Related concepts: models and representation [1]. Context: for example identities and relationships, or scientific and technical innovation [1]. Statement, for example "Modelling a rate and a fixed charge lets people predict and compare the costs of everyday choices" [1]. **[4]**
**(d)** Factual, for example "What does the 0.40 represent in the formula?" [1]. Conceptual, for example "Why can a linear model be useful even when it is only approximate?" [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Make sure the statement, concepts and context agree with each other. A statement mentioning prediction should not name unrelated concepts.

**12. (a)** 0.45 × 12 [1] = **5.4 m** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Length = the square root of 5.4² + 0.45² [1] = the square root of 29.3625 [1] = **5.42 m** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** The space is 5.0 m, which is less than 5.4 m, so one straight ramp does not meet the rule [1]. The school would need a different design, such as a longer or turning ramp with a landing [1]. This links the mathematics to fairness, because the rule exists so that all pupils can use the entrance [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The final part rewards a sentence in context. Say whether the solution makes sense for the real situation, not only what the numbers are.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a topic instead of a general idea as a statement of inquiry.
- Confusing key concepts with related concepts.
- Giving a context with no explanation of why it suits the mathematics.
- Forgetting to convert map centimetres to kilometres.
- Comparing totals when a rate per person is needed.
- Rounding too early, then giving an answer that is slightly out.
- Leaving out the final comment on whether the answer makes sense.
- Presenting a model as an exact description of the real world.
