## Flowering plants

### 2.18–2.19 Photosynthesis

- Photosynthesis converts **light energy into chemical energy** (in glucose).
- Chlorophyll, in chloroplasts, absorbs the light.
- It supplies the energy at the start of nearly every food chain, and releases oxygen.

| Equation | Form |
|---|---|
| Word | carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (light, chlorophyll) |
| Symbol | 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ |

Raw materials: carbon dioxide (from the air, through stomata) and water (from the soil, through xylem). Products: glucose and oxygen.

### 2.20 Limiting factors

| Factor | Why it affects the rate | Shape of graph |
|---|---|---|
| Light intensity | Supplies the energy | Rises, then levels off |
| Carbon dioxide concentration | Raw material | Rises, then levels off |
| Temperature | Enzymes control the reactions | Rises to an optimum, then falls steeply |

**Method: naming the limiting factor from a graph**

1. Find the part of the curve you are asked about.
2. If the line is still rising as the x-axis factor increases, **that factor** is limiting.
3. If the line is flat, the x-axis factor is **not** limiting. Name one of the other two factors.
4. If another curve at a higher level of a second factor sits above it, that second factor was limiting on the lower curve's flat part.

### 2.21 Leaf adaptations

| Structure | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| Large surface area | Absorbs more light |
| Thin | Short diffusion path for carbon dioxide |
| Transparent cuticle and upper epidermis | Let light through to the mesophyll |
| Palisade mesophyll | Most chloroplasts; near the upper surface |
| Spongy mesophyll | Air spaces for gas diffusion |
| Stomata and guard cells | Let carbon dioxide in |
| Xylem / phloem | Bring water in / take sugars away |

### 2.22 Mineral ions

| Ion | Needed for | If short |
|---|---|---|
| Magnesium | Making chlorophyll | Yellow leaves, less photosynthesis |
| Nitrate | Making amino acids (then proteins) | Poor growth |

### 2.23 Practicals in steps

**Oxygen from pondweed**

1. Pondweed in water with sodium hydrogencarbonate.
2. Lamp at a set distance; heat shield to keep temperature steady.
3. Let the plant adjust, then count bubbles per minute or measure gas volume.
4. Repeat at each distance; calculate the mean.
5. Test the gas: it relights a glowing splint.

**Starch test on a leaf**

1. Boiling water: kills the leaf, makes cells permeable.
2. Hot ethanol (flame off): removes chlorophyll.
3. Warm water: softens the leaf.
4. Iodine: blue-black means starch.

**Requirement experiments (destarch first, about 48 hours in the dark)**

| Requirement | Set-up | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Light | Part of leaf covered with foil | Covered part: no starch |
| Carbon dioxide | Soda lime with the plant; control without | Soda lime plant: no starch |
| Chlorophyll | Variegated leaf | White parts: no starch |

## Humans

### 2.24–2.25 Balanced diet

A balanced diet has carbohydrate, protein, lipid, vitamins, minerals, water and dietary fibre **in appropriate proportions**.

| Nutrient | Source | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrate | Rice, bread, potatoes | Energy |
| Protein | Meat, fish, beans | Growth, repair, enzymes |
| Lipid | Oils, butter, nuts | Energy store, insulation, membranes |
| Vitamin A | Liver, carrots | Vision in dim light, healthy skin |
| Vitamin C | Citrus fruit | Healthy skin and gums (prevents scurvy) |
| Vitamin D | Oily fish; skin in sunlight | Calcium absorption, bones (prevents rickets) |
| Calcium | Milk, cheese | Bones, teeth, blood clotting |
| Iron | Red meat, spinach | Haemoglobin (prevents anaemia) |
| Water | Drinks | Solvent, transport, cooling |
| Fibre | Vegetables, wholegrain | Bulk for peristalsis, prevents constipation |

### 2.26 Energy requirements

- Higher with **more activity** (more respiration in muscles).
- Varies with **age**: high for size while growing; often lower in old age.
- Higher in **pregnancy**: growth of fetus and placenta, extra mass to carry.

### 2.27–2.28 Alimentary canal

Order: mouth → oesophagus → stomach → duodenum → ileum → colon → rectum → anus. The **pancreas** releases enzymes into the duodenum; the **liver** makes bile.

| Region | Key job |
|---|---|
| Mouth | Chewing; salivary amylase |
| Oesophagus | Peristalsis to stomach |
| Stomach | Churning; protease; acid kills bacteria |
| Duodenum | Pancreatic enzymes and bile added |
| Ileum | Digestion finished; absorption |
| Colon | Water absorbed |
| Rectum | Faeces stored |

**Peristalsis:** waves of muscle contraction and relaxation. Circular muscle contracts behind the food and relaxes in front of it.

### 2.29 Enzymes

| Enzyme | Substrate | Product | Made by / acts in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amylase | Starch | Maltose | Salivary glands, pancreas |
| Maltase | Maltose | Glucose | Lining of small intestine |
| Protease | Protein | Amino acids | Stomach, pancreas, small intestine |
| Lipase | Lipid | Fatty acids + glycerol | Pancreas; acts in small intestine |

### 2.30–2.31 Bile

- Made in the **liver**, stored in the **gall bladder**, released into the duodenum.
- **Alkaline:** neutralises stomach acid, giving the right pH for enzymes in the small intestine.
- **Emulsifies lipids:** large drops → small droplets → larger surface area for lipase.
- Not an enzyme. It does not break bonds.

### 2.32 Villus

| Feature | Benefit |
|---|---|
| Many villi and microvilli; long ileum | Large surface area |
| Wall one cell thick | Short diffusion distance |
| Capillary network | Removes glucose and amino acids; keeps concentration gradient |
| Lacteal | Absorbs fatty acids and glycerol |

### 2.33B Energy in food (Paper 2 only)

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Energy released (J) | mass of water (g) × 4.2 × temperature rise (°C) |
| Energy per gram (J/g) | energy released ÷ mass of food (g) |

1 cm³ of water has a mass of 1 g. Divide J by 1000 for kJ. The measured value is too low because of heat loss and incomplete burning.

## Small worked reminders

**Linking a structure to its function.** For an "explain" mark, name the feature, then say what it does, then say why that helps. For example: "The villi have a dense capillary network (feature), which carries absorbed glucose away quickly (what it does), so a steep concentration gradient is kept and diffusion stays fast (why it helps)."

**Reading a carbon dioxide curve.** If raising carbon dioxide from 0.02% to 0.06% raises the rate, but raising it from 0.10% to 0.14% does not, then carbon dioxide was limiting at low concentration but not at high concentration. At high concentration, light intensity or temperature is limiting.

**Controlled variables in the pondweed practical.** When light is the independent variable, keep carbon dioxide (same amount of sodium hydrogencarbonate), temperature (heat shield) and the type and length of pondweed the same.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Amylase vs maltase:** amylase makes maltose; maltase makes glucose.
- **Bile vs lipase:** bile emulsifies (physical); lipase digests (chemical).
- **Liver vs gall bladder:** liver makes bile; gall bladder stores it.
- **Magnesium vs nitrate:** chlorophyll vs amino acids.
- **Digestion vs absorption:** breaking down vs taking into the blood.
- **Light vs temperature curves:** light levels off; temperature has an optimum and then falls.

## Quick self-test

1. Write the balanced symbol equation for photosynthesis.
2. What energy change happens in photosynthesis?
3. Which ion does a plant need to make chlorophyll?
4. Why is a plant kept in the dark for 48 hours before a starch experiment?
5. Why is ethanol heated in a water bath and not over a flame?
6. Name the vitamin that helps the body absorb calcium.
7. Why does a pregnant woman need more energy?
8. Which enzyme breaks maltose into glucose?
9. Give two jobs of bile.
10. What does the lacteal absorb?
11. A 0.60 g food sample raises the temperature of 30 cm³ of water by 15 °C. Calculate the energy released per gram, in kJ/g.
12. On a rate against temperature graph, why does the rate fall above the optimum?

### Answers

1. 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
2. Light energy → chemical energy (in glucose).
3. Magnesium.
4. To use up stored starch, so any starch found was made during the experiment.
5. Ethanol is flammable.
6. Vitamin D.
7. For the growth of the fetus and placenta and to carry the extra mass.
8. Maltase.
9. Neutralises stomach acid; emulsifies lipids.
10. Fatty acids and glycerol.
11. 30 × 4.2 × 15 = 1890 J; 1890 ÷ 0.60 = 3150 J/g = **3.15 kJ/g**.
12. The enzymes' active sites change shape, so the reactions slow down.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "sunlight" as a reactant in the word equation; light goes over the arrow, not as a substance.
- Unbalanced symbol equations, such as 6O instead of 6O₂.
- Naming the x-axis factor as limiting on the flat part of a graph.
- Saying high temperature "kills" the enzymes rather than changing the shape of the active site.
- Giving "makes chlorophyll" for nitrate or "makes protein" for magnesium.
- Defining a balanced diet without "in the right proportions".
- Saying bile is made in the gall bladder, or that bile digests fat.
- Saying amylase produces glucose.
- Describing villi as only "having a large surface area" when the question asks for three adaptations.
- In 2.33B, forgetting to divide by the mass of food, or mixing up J and kJ.
