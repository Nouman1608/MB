These questions cover 3.2.1 The measurement of macroeconomic performance in AQA A-level Economics (7136). Every
country and figure is invented. Read the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-economics-7136-the-measurement-of-macroeconomic-performance-aqa-alevel-economics/)
and [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-economics-7136-the-measurement-of-macroeconomic-performance-aqa-alevel-economics-revision-notes/)
first. Show your working and give answers to one decimal place unless told otherwise.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Distinguish between nominal GDP and real GDP. **[2]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by the "weights" in a consumer price index. **[2]**

**3.** A price index rises from 125 in one year to 130 in the next. Calculate the rate of inflation. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why a fall in the rate of inflation from 5% to 2% does not mean that prices have fallen. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the unemployment rate can fall even when the number of people unemployed stays the same.
**[2]**

**6.** State **two** reasons why the importance a government attaches to different macroeconomic objectives may
change over time. **[2]**

### Section B

**7.** Country Q's consumer price index uses three groups.

| Group | Weight | Price index (base year = 100) |
|---|---|---|
| Food | 50 | 102 |
| Energy | 25 | 110 |
| Clothing | 25 | 98 |

**(a)** Calculate the weighted price index. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why energy prices have a smaller effect on the overall index than food prices, even though they
rose more. **[2]**

**8.** A price index with 2019 = 100 reads 125 in 2024 and 130 in 2025. Rebase the index so that 2024 = 100,
giving the values for 2019 and 2025. **[2]**

**9.** Country R's nominal GDP rises from 800 billion to 880 billion over a year, while its price index rises from
100 to 110.

**(a)** Calculate real GDP in the second year at first-year prices. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain what has happened to the volume of output. **[2]**

**10.** Country T's real GDP rises from 1,200 billion to 1,236 billion while its population rises from 60 million to
61.2 million. Calculate real GDP per capita in each year and its percentage growth, and comment on the result.
**[4]**

**11.** Country V's current account items (billions) are: trade in goods +12, trade in services −20, primary income
+6, secondary income −8. GDP is 500 billion.

**(a)** Calculate the current account balance and express it as a percentage of GDP. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain **one** reason why the balance might be expressed as a percentage of GDP. **[1]**

**12.** Income per head in Country W is 90,000 local units. The market exchange rate is 3 local units per US dollar,
and the PPP rate is 4.5 local units per dollar. Calculate income per head in dollars at each rate and explain
which is the better guide to living standards. **[4]**

**13.** Assess the usefulness of real GDP per capita for comparing living standards between two countries. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Nominal GDP is measured at **current prices**, so it rises when prices rise even if output does not [1]. Real
GDP is **adjusted for changes in the price level**, so it measures changes in the volume of output [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Weights reflect each item's **share of household spending** [1], so items that take up more of a typical
household's budget have a proportionally larger effect on the index [1]. **[2]**

**3.** (130 − 125) ÷ 125 × 100 = **4.0%** [1].

**4.** The inflation rate measures how fast the **price level is rising** [1]. A fall from 5% to 2% means prices are
still rising, but more slowly (disinflation); prices fall only if inflation is negative (deflation) [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The rate is unemployed ÷ **labour force** [1]. If the labour force grows, for example through migration or
more people seeking work, the same number unemployed is a smaller percentage of it [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two [2]: changes in which problem is most serious (for example a period of high inflation); changes in
economic theory or views about what policy can achieve; political priorities of different governments; new
international commitments or events.

**7. (a)** (50 × 102) + (25 × 110) + (25 × 98) = 5,100 + 2,750 + 2,450 = 10,300 [1]. 10,300 ÷ 100 = **103.0**
[1].

**(b)** Energy has a **weight of 25** compared with 50 for food [1], so a given rise in energy prices moves the index
only half as much as the same rise in food prices would [1].

**8.** 2019: 100 ÷ 125 × 100 = **80.0** [1]. 2025: 130 ÷ 125 × 100 = **104.0** [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** 880 ÷ 1.10 = **800 billion** [1].

**(b)** Real GDP is **unchanged** at 800 billion [1]: the whole 10% rise in nominal GDP is due to higher prices, so
there was no growth in the volume of output [1].

**10.** Year 1: 1,200 billion ÷ 60 million = **20,000** [1]. Year 2: 1,236 billion ÷ 61.2 million = **20,196**
(to the nearest unit) [1]. Growth = **1.0%** [1]. Real GDP grew 3% but population grew 2%, so most of the extra
output was shared among more people and average living standards rose only slightly [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** 12 − 20 + 6 − 8 = **−10 billion**, a deficit [1]; −10 ÷ 500 × 100 = **−2.0% of GDP** [1].

**(b)** It allows comparison with other years or countries of **different sizes**, since a deficit of 10 billion is
more serious in a small economy than in a large one [1].

**12.** Market rate: 90,000 ÷ 3 = **30,000 dollars** [1]. PPP rate: 90,000 ÷ 4.5 = **20,000 dollars** [1]. The PPP
figure is the better guide [1], because it allows for the higher cost of living in Country W, showing what income
can actually buy there [1]. **[4]**

**13.** *Usefulness:* real GDP per capita adjusts for **inflation and population size** [1]; it is widely available
and measured in a broadly similar way across countries [1]; it is closely linked to the goods and services
people can consume [1]. *Limitations:* it is an **average** and ignores inequality [1]; differences in the size of
the **informal economy** and unpaid work distort comparisons [1]; it excludes leisure, health, pollution and
working hours [1]; comparisons at market exchange rates mislead unless **PPP** rates are used [1]. *Judgement:*
it is a useful starting point, especially at PPP, but should be combined with indicators of health, education and
distribution before concluding that one country has higher living standards [2]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Subtracting index numbers instead of calculating a percentage change.
- Rebasing by subtracting rather than dividing.
- Using nominal growth as if it were real growth.
- Mixing percentage points and per cent.
- Forgetting the sign of a current account balance.
- Using PPP without saying why it gives a better comparison.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I rebase an index number series?**
Divide every value by the value in the new base year and multiply by 100. The new base year then reads 100 and
all percentage changes are preserved.

**Why do I need real GDP per capita rather than real GDP?**
Real GDP measures total output. Dividing by population shows output per person, which is closer to average living
standards when population is changing.
