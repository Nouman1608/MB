## Section A

**1.** Define binding energy. **[1]**

**2.** A large power station transfers 9.0 × 10¹⁶ J of energy from nuclear reactions in a year. Calculate the decrease in mass of its fuel that corresponds to this energy. **[2]**

**3.** Complete the following nuclear equations.

**(a)** ²³⁵₉₂U + ¹₀n → ¹⁴⁴₅₆Ba + ⁸⁹₃₆Kr + x ¹₀n. State the value of x. **[1]**

**(b)** ⁹₄Be + ⁴₂He → ¹²₆C + ? **[1]**

**4.** Explain why the binding energy per nucleon, rather than the total binding energy, is used to compare the stability of different nuclei. **[2]**

**5.** The deuterium nucleus ²₁H has a mass of 2.013553 u.

**(a)** Calculate its binding energy in joules. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the maximum wavelength of a gamma-ray photon that could split the nucleus into a proton and a neutron. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A lithium-7 nucleus (⁷₃Li) has a mass of 7.014358 u.

**(a)** Calculate its mass defect in u. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate its binding energy in joules. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate its binding energy per nucleon in MeV. **[1]**

**(d)** Helium-4 has a binding energy per nucleon of 7.09 MeV. State, with a reason, which nucleus is more stable. **[1]**

**7.** **(a)** Sketch a graph of binding energy per nucleon against nucleon number for nucleon numbers from 1 to 240. Give approximate values for the key points. **[3]**

**(b)** On your graph, indicate the regions in which nuclei can release energy by fusion and by fission. **[2]**

**(c)** Use the graph to explain why both fusion and fission release energy. **[3]**

**8.** A possible fission reaction is ²³⁵₉₂U + ¹₀n → ¹⁴¹₅₆Ba + ⁹²₃₆Kr + 3¹₀n. The atomic masses are: uranium-235, 235.043930 u; barium-141, 140.914411 u; krypton-92, 91.926156 u.

**(a)** Calculate the decrease in mass in the reaction, in u. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy released per fission in joules and in MeV. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the number of uranium-235 nuclei in 1.0 kg of uranium-235. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the energy released if all the nuclei in 1.0 kg undergo this fission. **[1]**

**9.** In the reaction ¹⁴₇N + ⁴₂He → ¹⁷₈O + ¹₁H, the atomic masses are: nitrogen-14, 14.003074 u; helium-4, 4.002603 u; oxygen-17, 16.999132 u; hydrogen-1, 1.007825 u.

**(a)** Show that this reaction does not release energy. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy, in MeV, that must be supplied for the reaction to occur. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest where this energy comes from in practice. **[1]**

**10.** The total binding energy of a helium-4 nucleus is 28.4 MeV and that of an oxygen-16 nucleus is 127.9 MeV.

**(a)** Calculate the binding energy per nucleon of oxygen-16. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy released if four helium-4 nuclei could fuse to form one oxygen-16 nucleus. **[2]**

**11.** A student writes: "In fission, some of the mass of the uranium is destroyed, and energy is created from nothing."

Explain the error in this statement. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why a nucleus near the peak of the binding energy per nucleon curve cannot release energy by either fusion or fission. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The minimum energy required to separate a nucleus into its constituent (individual) protons and neutrons [1].

**2.** Δm = E/c² [1] = 9.0 × 10¹⁶ ÷ (3.00 × 10⁸)² = 1.0 kg [1].

**3. (a)** 235 + 1 = 144 + 89 + x, so x = 3 [1].

**(b)** ¹₀n (a neutron): nucleon number 9 + 4 − 12 = 1, proton number 4 + 2 − 6 = 0 [1].

**4.** A nucleus with more nucleons has a larger total binding energy simply because it has more nucleons [1]; the binding energy per nucleon shows how much energy is needed, on average, to remove each nucleon, so a higher value means a more tightly bound, more stable nucleus [1].

**5. (a)** Δm = 1.007276 + 1.008665 − 2.013553 = 0.002388 u [1]; E = 0.002388 × 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ × (3.00 × 10⁸)² = 3.57 × 10⁻¹³ J [1].

**(b)** λ = hc/E = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 3.57 × 10⁻¹³ = 5.6 × 10⁻¹³ m [1].

**6. (a)** Mass of nucleons = 3(1.007276) + 4(1.008665) = 7.056488 u [1]; Δm = 7.056488 − 7.014358 = 0.042130 u [1].

**(b)** E = 0.042130 × 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ × (3.00 × 10⁸)² = 6.29 × 10⁻¹² J [1].

**(c)** 6.29 × 10⁻¹² ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ = 39.3 MeV; ÷ 7 = 5.62 MeV per nucleon [1].

**(d)** Helium-4, because it has the higher binding energy per nucleon [1].

**7. (a)** Axes labelled (binding energy per nucleon / MeV against nucleon number) [1]; steep rise for small A from about 1 MeV, with helium-4 at about 7 MeV above the trend [1]; maximum of about 8.8 MeV near A = 56 (iron), then a gradual fall to about 7.6 MeV at A ≈ 235 [1].

**(b)** Fusion: light nuclei on the steep left-hand side (A well below 56) [1]. Fission: heavy nuclei on the right-hand side (A well above 56) [1].

**(c)** In both processes the products lie closer to the peak and so have a higher binding energy per nucleon than the reactants [1]. The total binding energy of the products is therefore greater [1], so their total mass is less, and the mass difference is released as energy (ΔE = c²Δm) [1].

**8. (a)** Mass before = 235.043930 + 1.008665 = 236.052595 u; mass after = 140.914411 + 91.926156 + 3(1.008665) = 235.866562 u [1]; decrease = 0.186033 u [1].

**(b)** E = 0.186033 × 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ × (3.00 × 10⁸)² = 2.78 × 10⁻¹¹ J [1]; = 2.78 × 10⁻¹¹ ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ = 174 MeV [1].

**(c)** N = 1.0 ÷ (235.04 × 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷) = 2.56 × 10²⁴ [1].

**(d)** E = 2.56 × 10²⁴ × 2.78 × 10⁻¹¹ = 7.1 × 10¹³ J [1].

**9. (a)** Mass before = 14.003074 + 4.002603 = 18.005677 u; mass after = 16.999132 + 1.007825 = 18.006957 u [1]; the products have more mass than the reactants, so energy must be supplied, not released [1].

**(b)** Δm = 18.006957 − 18.005677 = 0.001280 u [1]; E = 0.001280 × 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ × (3.00 × 10⁸)² = 1.91 × 10⁻¹³ J = 1.2 MeV [1].

**(c)** From the kinetic energy of the incoming alpha particle [1].

**10. (a)** 127.9 ÷ 16 = 8.0 MeV per nucleon [1].

**(b)** Binding energy of four helium-4 nuclei = 4 × 28.4 = 113.6 MeV [1]; energy released = 127.9 − 113.6 = 14.3 MeV [1].

**11.** Mass and energy are equivalent (E = mc²), so mass is not destroyed and energy is not created: total mass-energy is conserved [1]. The rest mass of the products is less than that of the reactants, and that mass difference is transferred as the kinetic energy of the products (and radiation) [1].

**12.** Nuclei at the peak have the highest binding energy per nucleon [1]; both joining them into heavier nuclei and splitting them into lighter nuclei produce products with a lower binding energy per nucleon, so energy would have to be supplied [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining binding energy loosely as "the energy holding a nucleus together".
- Rounding masses before subtracting, so the mass defect is lost or badly wrong.
- Forgetting to include the emitted neutrons when adding up the products' mass.
- Mixing atomic and nuclear masses in one calculation.
- Using 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ instead of 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ to convert between MeV and joules.
- Sketching the curve with its peak at uranium, or without approximate values.
