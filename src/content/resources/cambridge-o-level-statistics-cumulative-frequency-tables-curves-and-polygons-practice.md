These questions cover topic 4 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics 4040 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: section 4.1 (cumulative frequency tables) and section 4.2 (cumulative frequency curves and polygons for continuous data). Learn the method from the [study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-cumulative-frequency-tables-curves-and-polygons/) or the [revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-cumulative-frequency-tables-curves-and-polygons-revision-notes/). The wider course is on the [4040 hub](/boards/cambridge/o-level/statistics/) and the [printable checklist](/checklists/cambridge/o-level/statistics/). For graph readings, use the straight-line polygon through the points.

## Questions

**1.** A taxi firm records the number of passengers in each of 60 taxis.

| Passengers | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 7 | 15 | 22 | 11 | 5 |

**(a)** Write down the cumulative frequency for each number of passengers. **[2]**
**(b)** How many taxis carried more than 3 passengers? **[1]**

**2.** The lengths of leaves are measured to the nearest millimetre and grouped in classes 30–39, 40–49 and 50–59.

**(a)** State the lower and upper class boundaries of the class 40–49. **[1]**
**(b)** At which length is the cumulative frequency for this class plotted? **[1]**

**3.** The waiting times, in minutes, of 24 callers at a help desk are:

2.4, 7.1, 11.8, 4.6, 9.3, 14.2, 6.0, 3.5, 12.7, 8.8, 1.9, 5.5, 10.4, 16.3, 7.7, 4.0, 13.1, 9.9, 6.8, 18.2, 3.1, 12.0, 8.2, 5.0

**(a)** Complete a frequency table using the classes 0 ≤ t < 5, 5 ≤ t < 10, 10 ≤ t < 15 and 15 ≤ t < 20. **[2]**
**(b)** Form the cumulative frequency table, labelled with the upper class boundaries. **[2]**

**4.** The cumulative frequency table shows the time, t minutes, that 100 pupils spent on homework one evening.

| t ≤ | 30 | 60 | 90 | 120 | 150 | 180 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative frequency | 8 | 27 | 61 | 84 | 95 | 100 |

**(a)** Find the frequency of the class 60 < t ≤ 90. **[1]**
**(b)** How many pupils spent more than 120 minutes? **[1]**
**(c)** What percentage of the pupils spent 60 minutes or less? **[1]**

**5.** The masses of 100 apples are recorded.

| Mass, m grams | 0 < m ≤ 50 | 50 < m ≤ 100 | 100 < m ≤ 150 | 150 < m ≤ 250 | 250 < m ≤ 400 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 4 | 16 | 30 | 40 | 10 |

**(a)** Complete the cumulative frequency row. **[2]**
**(b)** List the coordinates you would plot for a cumulative frequency polygon. **[2]**

**6.** A battery test gives the lifetimes, t hours, of 50 batteries. The cumulative frequencies are 3, 12, 29, 43 and 50 for the classes 0 < t ≤ 10, 10 < t ≤ 20, 20 < t ≤ 30, 30 < t ≤ 40 and 40 < t ≤ 50. A pupil plots (5, 3), (15, 12), (25, 29), (35, 43) and (45, 50) and joins them with a smooth curve.

**(a)** Identify two errors. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the correct coordinates. **[2]**

**7.** The table shows the waiting times, w minutes, of 140 patients at a clinic.

| w ≤ | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative frequency | 0 | 11 | 39 | 83 | 119 | 140 |

Use a polygon, or straight-line working, to estimate:

**(a)** the number of patients who waited 25 minutes or less, **[2]**
**(b)** the number who waited more than 35 minutes, **[2]**
**(c)** the time that 70 patients did not exceed. **[1]**

**8.** Class A (40 pupils) and Class B (50 pupils) sat one test marked out of 60.

| Marks, m | 0 < m ≤ 10 | 10 < m ≤ 20 | 20 < m ≤ 30 | 30 < m ≤ 40 | 40 < m ≤ 50 | 50 < m ≤ 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Class A frequency | 2 | 5 | 9 | 12 | 8 | 4 |
| Class B frequency | 3 | 8 | 14 | 13 | 9 | 3 |

**(a)** Form the cumulative frequency table for each class. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why percentages, not counts, should be compared. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the percentage of each class that scored 30 or less. **[2]**
**(d)** Which class did better on this evidence? Give a reason. **[1]**

**9.** A cumulative frequency table for the distances, d km, travelled by 60 shoppers is incomplete.

| d ≤ | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative frequency | 6 | 19 | x | 51 | 60 |

The class 10 < d ≤ 15 has frequency 18. Find x, and find the frequency of the class 20 < d ≤ 25. **[3]**

**10.** Explain, in terms of a cumulative frequency graph: **(a)** how a polygon differs from a curve, **[1]** **(b)** why the cumulative frequency of a class is plotted at its upper boundary, **[1]** **(c)** what a nearly flat section of the graph shows. **[1]**

**11.** The heights of 60 sunflowers are measured to the nearest centimetre.

| Height (cm) | 100–119 | 120–139 | 140–159 | 160–179 | 180–199 | 200–229 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 5 | 11 | 19 | 15 | 7 | 3 |

**(a)** State the boundaries of the first and last classes. **[2]**
**(b)** Form the cumulative frequency table, with the upper boundaries. **[2]**
**(c)** Estimate the number of sunflowers shorter than 150 cm. **[2]**
**(d)** Estimate the height that only 15 sunflowers exceed. **[2]**

**12.** A gym records the length of 140 sessions, t minutes.

| Length, t minutes | 0 < t ≤ 15 | 15 < t ≤ 30 | 30 < t ≤ 45 | 45 < t ≤ 60 | 60 < t ≤ 90 | 90 < t ≤ 120 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 8 | 22 | 35 | 40 | 25 | 10 |

**(a)** Form the cumulative frequency table and list the points to plot, including the starting point. **[3]**
**(b)** Estimate how many sessions lasted 78 minutes or less. **[2]**
**(c)** Estimate the time within which 115 sessions finished. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Adding the running total: 7, then 7 + 15 = 22, then 22 + 22 = 44, 44 + 11 = 55, 55 + 5 = 60 [1]. Cumulative frequencies: **7, 22, 44, 55, 60**, with the last equal to 60 [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 60 − 44 = **16** taxis [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Showing that the last total equals the number of taxis is a quick check on the table.

**2. (a)** Rounded to the nearest mm, 40 covers 39.5 up to 49.5, so the boundaries are **39.5 and 49.5** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** At the upper boundary, **49.5** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Giving 40 and 49 as the boundaries is a common slip, since they are the class limits.

**3. (a)** Sorting the values: 0 ≤ t < 5 holds 2.4, 4.6, 3.5, 1.9, 4.0, 3.1 [1]; the classes are **6, 10, 6, 2**, with a total of 24 [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Running totals: 6, 16, 22, 24 [1]. Table: t < 5: 6, t < 10: 16, t < 15: 22, t < 20: 24 [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Tick values off as you sort them. Here 5.0 belongs in the second class.

**4. (a)** 61 − 27 = **34** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 100 − 84 = **16** pupils [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** 27 out of 100 is **27%** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Subtract the cumulative frequency before it, and not the one after, to get a class frequency.

**5. (a)** Running total: 4, 4 + 16 = 20, 20 + 30 = 50, 50 + 40 = 90, 90 + 10 = 100 [1]. **4, 20, 50, 90, 100** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Upper boundaries 50, 100, 150, 250, 400 with these totals [1], and the start (0, 0). Coordinates: **(0, 0), (50, 4), (100, 20), (150, 50), (250, 90), (400, 100)** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Unequal class widths do not change the cumulative frequencies. The coordinates are still upper boundary against running total.

**6. (a)** The points are plotted at the **class midpoints** and not the upper boundaries [1], and the graph has **no starting point**: the first lower boundary, with cumulative frequency 0, is missing [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Upper boundaries with totals [1]: **(0, 0), (10, 3), (20, 12), (30, 29), (40, 43), (50, 50)** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name each error exactly, such as "plotted at midpoints", and avoid a vague comment like "wrong points".

**7. (a)** 25 is halfway between 20 and 30, so the value is halfway between 39 and 83 [1]: 39 + 22 = **61** patients [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** At 35, halfway between 83 and 119: 83 + 18 = 101 [1]. More than 35: 140 − 101 = **39** patients [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Between (20, 39) and (30, 83): 20 + (31/44) × 10 ≈ **27 minutes** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* For "more than", subtract your reading from the total. Quoting the reading itself answers the opposite question.

**8. (a)** Class A: **2, 7, 16, 28, 36, 40** [1]. Class B: **3, 11, 25, 38, 47, 50** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The classes have different sizes (40 and 50 pupils), so counts are not a fair comparison [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Class A: 16/40 × 100 = **40%** [1]. Class B: 25/50 × 100 = **50%** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Class A, because a smaller percentage of Class A (40% against 50%) scored 30 or less [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Use figures from both groups in a comparison, such as "40% against 50%".

**9.** Add the class frequency: x = 19 + 18 [1] = **37** [1]. The last class frequency is 60 − 51 = **9** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the addition you used, so your method is visible.

**10. (a)** A polygon joins the points with straight lines, and a curve joins them with one smooth line [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Every item in the class has been counted only once the top of the class is reached [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Few items fall in that range, so the cumulative frequency barely rises [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Reason from what the running total counts, and avoid saying only "the graph goes up".

**11. (a)** The first class has lower boundary **99.5** [1] and the last class has upper boundary **229.5** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Running total: 5, 16, 35, 50, 57, 60 [1]. Table with the upper boundaries 119.5, 139.5, 159.5, 179.5, 199.5, 229.5 [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 150 lies between 139.5 (total 16) and 159.5 (total 35), at a fraction 10.5/20 of the way [1]: 16 + (10.5/20) × 19 = 25.975, so about **26** sunflowers [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Read at 60 − 15 = 45 [1]. Between (159.5, 35) and (179.5, 50): 159.5 + (10/15) × 20 = 172.8, so about **173 cm** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Only 15 exceed" means 45 are at or below, so you read at 45. Reading at 15 answers a different question.

**12. (a)** Running total: 8, 30, 65, 105, 130, 140 [1]. Table: t ≤ 15: 8, t ≤ 30: 30, t ≤ 45: 65, t ≤ 60: 105, t ≤ 90: 130, t ≤ 120: 140 [1]. Points: **(0, 0), (15, 8), (30, 30), (45, 65), (60, 105), (90, 130), (120, 140)** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 78 lies between 60 and 90, at a fraction 18/30 of the way [1]: 105 + (18/30) × 25 = **120** sessions [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 115 lies between totals 105 and 130, at a fraction 10/25 of the way [1]: 60 + (10/25) × 30 = **72 minutes** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* With unequal classes, find the class holding your target first, then take the fraction of that class's width.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Plotting at the midpoint of a class and not at its upper boundary.
- Leaving out the starting point, which has cumulative frequency 0 at the first lower boundary.
- Using class limits (24) instead of boundaries (24.5) for rounded data.
- Errors in the running total, with no check against the total frequency.
- Reading at the wrong height for "more than" or "highest" questions.
- Comparing counts for groups of different sizes and not percentages.
- Quoting graph readings as exact, or without units.
- Leaving out working, although the syllabus says essential working must be shown.
