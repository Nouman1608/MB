**What these questions cover.** Topic 3.1.2 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat 9237 is
examined in Paper 1 Section B (25 marks), whose 12-mark question uses a prescribed passage from
Section 6 of the specification. This page gives more practice than one exam section, across the
Five Pillars, commanding good and forbidding evil, Salah and Jummah, Sawm, Zakah and Ushr, Hajj and
Umrah, Jihad, and the festivals and commemorations.

## Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** What is the name of the call to prayer? **[1]**

**2.** Name the night prayer offered in congregation during Ramadhan. **[1]**

**3.** Give **two** groups of people who may receive Zakah. **[2]**

**4.** Give **two** groups of people who are excused from fasting in Ramadhan. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **two** actions that pilgrims perform during Hajj. **[4]**

**6.** Explain **two** of the four types of Jihad named in the specification. **[4]**

**7.** Explain **two** differences between Hajj and Umrah. **[4]**

**8.** Explain **two** reasons why Ashura is important to Muslims. **[4]**

## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** The 1- and 2-mark questions give one mark for each correct answer.
The 4-mark questions give one mark for each point identified and one more for explaining it, up to
two marks for each of the two. The 6-mark questions award those four AO1 marks plus up to two AO2
marks for applying a source of authority: **one** mark for a general, relevant and accurate use,
**two** marks for a specific, accurate use applied directly to the question. The 12-mark questions
are AO2 and are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points of significance; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent use of the passage, applied directly |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point of significance is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not referred to** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

Other correct answers of the same quality receive the same credit.

**1.** The Adhan (Azaan) [1].

**2.** Taraweeh [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each, from Qur'an 9:60: the poor; the needy; those employed to collect Zakah;
those whose hearts are to be brought together; to free captives; those in debt; in the cause of
Allah; the stranded traveller.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: people who are ill; travellers; the elderly or those who cannot fast
without harm; pregnant or breastfeeding women where fasting may harm them or the child; women
during menstruation or bleeding after childbirth; children who have not reached puberty. (Missed
fasts are made up later or compensated by feeding the poor, depending on the reason.)

**5.** For each of two actions: action identified [1] and explained [1].

- Entering **Ihram** at the Meeqat [1]: pilgrims wear simple clothing and follow its restrictions,
  showing equality and humility before Allah [1].
- **Tawaf** [1]: walking seven times around the Ka'aba, showing that Allah is the centre of a
  Muslim's life [1].
- **Sa'i** between Safa and Marwah [1], seven times, remembering Hajar's search for water for
  Isma'il (AS) [1].
- **Standing at Arafat** [1] on 9 Dhul Hijjah, praying for forgiveness; this is the most important
  part of Hajj [1].
- Spending the night at **Muzdalifah** [1] and collecting pebbles for the stoning [1].
- **Stoning the pillars at Mina** [1], recalling Ibraheem's (AS) rejection of Shaytan's temptation
  [1].

**6.** For each of two types: type identified [1] and explained [1].

- **With the heart (bil-qalb)** [1]: struggling inwardly against one's own wrong desires and
  hating what is evil [1].
- **With the tongue (bil-lisan)** [1]: speaking the truth, explaining Islam and speaking out
  against injustice [1].
- **With the hand (bil-yad)** [1]: taking practical action to do good and stop wrong, such as
  helping those in need [1].
- **With the sword (bil-sayf)** [1]: armed struggle only in defence and under strict rules, without
  transgression (Qur'an 2:190) [1].

**7.** For each of two differences: difference identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Obligation** [1]: Hajj is one of the Five Pillars, required once in a lifetime for those able,
  while Umrah is not one of the pillars [1].
- **Timing** [1]: Hajj is performed only in the month of Dhul Hijjah, on set days, while Umrah can
  be performed at any time of year [1].
- **Rituals** [1]: Hajj includes standing at Arafat, the night at Muzdalifah and the stoning at Mina,
  which Umrah does not; Umrah is mainly Ihram, Tawaf, Sa'i and cutting or shaving the hair [1].

**8.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **The deliverance of Musa (AS)** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) fasted on the 10th of Muharram, the day
  Allah saved Musa (AS) and his people from Pharaoh, and encouraged Muslims to fast [1].
- **A day of voluntary fasting** [1]: many Muslims fast on Ashura, often with the day before or
  after, seeking forgiveness [1].
- **The martyrdom of Imam Husayn (RA)** [1]: Muslims remember the killing of the Prophet's (pbuh)
  grandson at Karbala on that day; for Shia Muslims especially it is a major commemoration of
  sacrifice and standing against injustice [1].

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**:
one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- It is the **second pillar** and a duty [1], so performing it five times a day is direct
  obedience to Allah [1].
- It **keeps Allah in mind** throughout the day [1], from Fajr before dawn to Isha at night [1].
- It **purifies and disciplines** [1]: Wudu cleans the body and regular prayer helps a person avoid
  wrongdoing [1].
- It **builds community** [1]: congregational prayer, especially Jummah, brings Muslims together
  in equal rows [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing" (Qur'an 29:45), so Salah shapes behaviour as
  well as worship [2].
- Qur'an 62:9 commands believers to "proceed to the remembrance of Allah and leave trade" at the
  Friday call, showing that prayer takes priority over work [2].

**10.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2
**[2]**: as in question 9.

Reasons (AO1):

- It is a **duty of the Ummah** [1]: Muslims share responsibility for the moral health of their
  community [1].
- It **protects society** from injustice and harm [1], since wrong that no one opposes spreads [1].
- It **reflects faith** [1]: a believer cares about others' wellbeing, not only their own [1].
- It links to **Jihad bil-lisan and bil-yad** [1], since speaking and acting against wrong are
  forms of striving for Allah [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Let there be [arising] from you a nation inviting to [all that is] good, enjoining what is right
  and forbidding what is wrong" (Qur'an 3:104), so the community as a whole must take on this
  duty [2].
- The Prophet (pbuh) taught that whoever sees an evil should change it with his hand, and if he
  cannot, with his tongue, and if he cannot, with his heart, which is the weakest of faith (Sahih
  Muslim), so every Muslim has a part to play in proportion to their ability [2].

**11.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- Jummah is **commanded in the Qur'an** for those it is required of, replacing the midday prayer
  on Fridays.
- It includes a **khutbah** (sermon), so it teaches the community as well as uniting it in
  worship.
- It **unites the Ummah** locally each week, strengthening brotherhood and equality.
- It shows that **worship comes before work**, yet the passage also permits returning to trade, so
  Islam balances worship and daily life.
- Against the statement: the five daily prayers are a pillar for every Muslim every day, and
  Fajr or Isha in congregation are also highly valued, so Jummah may be special rather than "most
  important".

*Use of the passage*

- "When [the Adhan] is called ... proceed to the remembrance of Allah and leave trade" shows the
  urgency of attending.
- "That is better for you, if you only knew" teaches that the benefit of Jummah exceeds worldly
  gain.
- "Disperse within the land and seek from the bounty of Allah" shows that work is permitted again
  after the prayer, so Islam does not separate worship from daily life.
- "They rushed to it and left you standing" warns against putting trade and entertainment before
  prayer.
- "Allah is the best of providers" reassures Muslims that leaving work for prayer does not lose
  them provision.

**12.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- Zakah is the **third pillar**, an "obligation [imposed] by Allah", so paying it is obedience and
  worship.
- It **purifies wealth** and the soul, removing greed and reminding Muslims that wealth belongs to
  Allah.
- It is calculated on wealth above the **Nisaab** held for a year, so it is a regular, ordered
  duty, not occasional charity; Ushr is paid on agricultural produce.
- It **meets social needs**, reducing poverty and debt and supporting travellers.
- It builds **brotherhood and the Ummah**, linking the rich and the poor.
- Voluntary charity (sadaqah) is separate, which shows Zakah is a fixed act of worship as well as
  help for others.

*Use of the passage*

- The list of eight categories shows Zakah is directed by Allah, not by personal choice, so giving
  it is an act of obedience.
- "The poor and ... the needy" and "those in debt" show its role in relieving hardship.
- "For bringing hearts together" and "the cause of Allah" show that it serves the faith and the
  community, not only individuals.
- "An obligation [imposed] by Allah. And Allah is Knowing and Wise" shows that it is a command based
  on Allah's wisdom, so it is worship as much as charity.

An answer that explains a range of points, reasons about their implications for Muslim practice,
and uses the passage directly reaches Level 4.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving one answer where two are asked for, or repeating one reason in different words.
- Describing a practice without explaining why it matters.
- Vague references to sources that limit AO2 credit to one mark.
- In the 12-mark questions, one point only, or no use of the passage.
