## Core facts

- Two groups of lipid in the specification: **triglycerides** and **phospholipids**.
- Triglyceride = **1 glycerol + 3 fatty acids**, joined by condensation.
- Glycerol + fatty acid (RCOOH) forms an **ester bond**; each releases one water molecule.
- R group may be **saturated** (no C=C) or **unsaturated** (one or more C=C).
- Phospholipid: one fatty acid replaced by a **phosphate-containing group**.
- Lipids are **not polymers**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Glycerol | Three-carbon alcohol with three –OH groups, the backbone of both lipid groups |
| Fatty acid | Hydrocarbon chain (R) with a carboxyl group, RCOOH |
| Ester bond | Bond formed by condensation between an –OH of glycerol and the –COOH of a fatty acid |
| Saturated | Only C–C single bonds in the R group |
| Monounsaturated | One C=C in the R group |
| Polyunsaturated | Two or more C=C in the R group |
| Hydrophilic | Attracted to water (the phosphate head) |
| Hydrophobic | Repelled by water (the fatty acid tails) |
| Amphipathic | Having a hydrophilic and a hydrophobic part |
| Micelle | Sphere of phospholipids, heads out and tails in |
| Bilayer | Two layers of phospholipids, tails inward, heads facing water on both sides |

## Saturation rule

Saturated R group = CₙH₂ₙ₊₁. Double bonds = (2n + 1 − H in R) ÷ 2.

Mini reminder with new numbers: C₁₇H₃₃COOH: saturated would be C₁₇H₃₅, missing 2 H, so 1 double bond (monounsaturated). C₁₃H₂₇COOH: 2 × 13 + 1 = 27, so saturated.

## Counting in triglycerides and phospholipids

| Molecule | Ester bonds to fatty acids | Water released in forming them |
|---|---|---|
| Triglyceride | 3 | 3 |
| Phospholipid | 2 | 2 (plus the phosphate group's own link) |

Mr of a triglyceride = 92 + sum of the three fatty acid Mr values − 54.

## Structure to property

| Feature | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Many C–H bonds | Releases much energy when oxidised: good energy store |
| Non-polar, insoluble | No effect on water potential; stored as droplets |
| High hydrogen content | Releases metabolic water when oxidised |
| Low thermal conductivity | Insulation in adipose tissue |
| C=C kinks | Chains pack loosely: lower melting point, oils |
| Phosphate head hydrophilic, tails hydrophobic | Forms bilayers: basis of membranes |
| Hydrophobic bilayer core | Barrier to ions and water-soluble molecules |

## Writing a comparison

Use matched pairs, each tied to a property:

- "A triglyceride has three fatty acids, whereas a phospholipid has two and a phosphate-containing group, so only the phospholipid has a hydrophilic region."
- "A triglyceride is non-polar, so it forms droplets in the cytoplasm, whereas a phospholipid is amphipathic, so it forms bilayers."
- "A triglyceride has more C–H bonds per molecule, so it is the better energy store."

## Phospholipid arrangements

- Air–water surface: monolayer, tails up into the air.
- In water: micelle.
- Water on both sides: bilayer.

## Emulsion test

1. Shake the sample with ethanol in a dry tube (lipid dissolves).
2. Add water and shake.
3. Milky white emulsion = lipid present. Clear = no lipid.

Negative control: ethanol and water only. Positive control: a known oil. Qualitative only. Ethanol is flammable.

## Exam traps

- Saying "lipids are polymers".
- Saying unsaturated means fewer carbons; it means fewer hydrogens.
- Giving phospholipids three fatty acids.
- Swapping head and tail properties.
- Judging saturation from chain length.
- Writing "white precipitate" for the emulsion test.
- Adding the sample to water before ethanol.
- Explaining low melting point without the idea of loose packing.

## Self-test

1. Name the bond formed between glycerol and a fatty acid.
2. How many water molecules are released when one triglyceride forms?
3. What replaces one fatty acid in a phospholipid?
4. C₁₅H₂₉COOH: saturated or unsaturated? How many double bonds?
5. Why are unsaturated fats usually liquid at room temperature?
6. Calculate the Mr of a triglyceride made from three fatty acids of Mr 284 (glycerol 92, water 18).
7. Which part of a phospholipid is hydrophilic?
8. Describe the arrangement of phospholipids when there is water on both sides.
9. Give two reasons triglycerides are good energy stores.
10. Describe the emulsion test and its positive result.
11. What is the purpose of a negative control in the emulsion test?
12. Explain why a phospholipid bilayer is a barrier to ions.

### Answers

1. Ester bond.
2. Three.
3. A phosphate-containing group.
4. Saturated would be C₁₅H₃₁; it is missing 2 H, so unsaturated with 1 double bond.
5. Double bonds kink the chains so they cannot pack closely; weaker forces between molecules lower the melting point.
6. 92 + (3 × 284) − 54 = 92 + 852 − 54 = 890.
7. The phosphate-containing head.
8. A bilayer: two layers with hydrophobic tails pointing inward and hydrophilic heads facing the water on each side.
9. Any two: many C–H bonds release much energy; insoluble, so no osmotic effect; release metabolic water when oxidised.
10. Shake with ethanol, then add water; a milky white emulsion shows lipid.
11. To show that the reagents and glassware alone do not produce cloudiness.
12. The core of the bilayer is made of hydrophobic fatty acid tails, which charged ions cannot pass through easily.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the two groups of lipid in AQA A-level Biology?**
Triglycerides and phospholipids.

**What is the difference between saturated and unsaturated fatty acids?**
Saturated fatty acids have no C=C double bonds in the R group; unsaturated fatty acids have one or more.

**Is the emulsion test quantitative?**
No. It shows whether lipid is present but not how much.
