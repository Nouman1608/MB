These notes condense section **3.3.1.1 Nomenclature** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Nomenclature study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-nomenclature/), and exam-style questions are in the [Nomenclature practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-nomenclature-practice/). The wider topic is in the [Introduction to organic chemistry revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-introduction-to-organic-chemistry-revision-notes/).

## What you must be able to do

- Use **empirical, molecular, general, structural, displayed and skeletal** formulas.
- Describe a **homologous series**.
- Apply **IUPAC rules** to name compounds and to draw structures from names (chains and rings up to six carbons).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Empirical formula | Simplest whole-number ratio of atoms |
| Molecular formula | Actual number of atoms of each element |
| General formula | Formula for a whole homologous series |
| Structural formula | Shows arrangement of atoms in groups |
| Displayed formula | Shows all atoms and all bonds |
| Skeletal formula | Carbon skeleton as lines, with functional groups shown |
| Functional group | Atom or group responsible for characteristic reactions |
| Homologous series | Same functional group, same general formula, differ by CH₂ |

## Stems and series

| Carbons | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stem | meth | eth | prop | but | pent | hex |

| Series | Group | General formula | Ending / prefix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alkane | C–C | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | -ane |
| Alkene | C=C | CₙH₂ₙ | -ene |
| Halogenoalkane | –X | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁X | chloro-, bromo-, iodo- |
| Alcohol | –OH | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH | -ol |
| Aldehyde | –CHO | CₙH₂ₙO | -al |
| Ketone | C=O | CₙH₂ₙO | -one |
| Carboxylic acid | –COOH | CₙH₂ₙO₂ | -oic acid |

Rings: add **cyclo** (cyclohexane, cyclohexene, cyclohexanol).

## Naming rules

1. Longest chain **containing the functional group**.
2. Suffix for the principal group (priority: acid, aldehyde, ketone, alcohol, alkene).
3. Number from the end giving the **lowest number** to the principal group (or to substituents if there is none).
4. Prefixes for substituents (methyl, ethyl, halogens) with positions.
5. di-, tri-, tetra- for repeats, with a number for each.
6. **Alphabetical** order for prefixes (ignore di-, tri-).
7. Commas between numbers; hyphens between numbers and letters.

Special cases:

- –CHO and –COOH are always C1: no numbers.
- Ketone numbers are needed from pentanone: pentan-2-one, pentan-3-one.
- Halogens and alkyl groups are always prefixes.

## Worked examples

| Structure | Name | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| CH₃CH(CH₃)CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ | 2,3-dimethylpentane | Pentane, methyls on C2 and C3 |
| CH₃CH₂CH(Cl)CH₃ | 2-chlorobutane | Number to give Cl the lower number |
| CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₂OH | 3-methylbutan-1-ol | –OH takes priority; C1 |
| CH₃C(CH₃)=CHCH₃ | 2-methylbut-2-ene | Number from the end nearest the C=C |
| CH₃CH(OH)CH(Cl)CH₃ | 3-chlorobutan-2-ol | –OH gets the lower number |
| CH₃CH(CH₃)CHO | 2-methylpropanal | CHO is C1 |
| CH₃CH(Br)CH₂COOH | 3-bromobutanoic acid | COOH is C1 |

From name to structure: 2,2-dimethylbutane is (CH₃)₃CCH₂CH₃, molecular formula C₆H₁₄.

| Hex-1-ene | C₆H₁₂ | CH₂ | CₙH₂ₙ |
| Hexan-1-ol | C₆H₁₄O | C₆H₁₄O | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH |
| Butanoic acid | C₄H₈O₂ | C₂H₄O | CₙH₂ₙO₂ |

## Skeletal formulas

- Each line end and each corner is a carbon.
- Hydrogen on carbon is not drawn, but count it: each carbon has four bonds.
- Draw functional groups in full (OH, Cl, C=O).

## Step-by-step naming routine

1. Circle the functional group that decides the suffix.
2. Count the longest chain through that group; write the stem.
3. Number the chain; check both directions and take the lower number for the principal group.
4. List the side groups with their numbers; use di-, tri- for repeats.
5. Put the prefixes in alphabetical order; add the suffix with its number.
6. Check the name by counting the carbons and the groups again.

Example: CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃. The longest chain is six carbons (hexane). Methyl groups lie on C3 and C4 from either end, so the name is 3,4-dimethylhexane.

## Isomers have different names

Butane (CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃) and 2-methylpropane (CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₃) have the same molecular formula, C₄H₁₀, but different structures. Propanal (CH₃CH₂CHO) and propanone (CH₃COCH₃) have the same molecular formula, C₃H₆O, but different functional groups. The name always describes the structure, not the formula.

## Common prefixes and suffixes at a glance

| Group | As a prefix | As a suffix |
|---|---|---|
| Alkyl | methyl-, ethyl- | none |
| Halogen | fluoro-, chloro-, bromo-, iodo- | none |
| Hydroxyl | hydroxy- (only when a higher priority group is present) | -ol |
| Carbonyl (C=O) | oxo- (not needed at this level) | -al, -one |
| Carboxyl | none | -oic acid |

## Quick checks on a finished name

- The number of carbons in the stem plus the carbons in the side groups must equal the carbons in the formula.
- Every number must have a matching group in the name.
- The suffix must match the functional group in the structure.

## Exam traps

- Longest chain chosen without the functional group.
- Numbering from the wrong end.
- Hydroxy- used as a prefix when the compound is an alcohol.
- Wrong alphabetical order (ethyl before methyl; bromo before chloro).
- Missing punctuation: commas and hyphens.
- A number added to an aldehyde or acid name.
- Wrong carbon count in a skeletal formula.
- Forgetting that "di" does not count when alphabetising.

## Self-test

1. What is a homologous series?
2. What is the general formula of alkenes?
3. Name CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃.
4. Name (CH₃)₂CHCH₂OH.
5. Name CH₃COCH₂CH₂CH₃.
6. Name CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)COOH.
7. Draw pent-2-ene (structural formula).
8. What is the empirical formula of C₆H₁₂O₂?
9. What is the molecular formula of cyclohexane?
10. How many carbons are in a but-2-ene molecule?
11. Why does an aldehyde name need no number?
12. In what order are prefixes listed?

### Answers

1. A series of compounds with the same functional group and general formula, each differing by CH₂.
2. CₙH₂ₙ.
3. 3-methylhexane.
4. 2-methylpropan-1-ol.
5. pentan-2-one.
6. 2-methylbutanoic acid.
7. CH₃CH=CHCH₂CH₃.
8. C₃H₆O.
9. C₆H₁₂.
10. Four.
11. The –CHO group must be at the end of the chain, on C1.
12. Alphabetically (ignoring di-, tri-).
