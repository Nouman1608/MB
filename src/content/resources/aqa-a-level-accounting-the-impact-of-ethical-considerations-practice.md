The qualification is not tiered, and section 18 is listed for both Paper 1 and Paper 2. All businesses, people and figures are invented.

## Questions

**1.** Identify the fundamental principle breached in each case.

**(a)** A payroll clerk tells a neighbour how much the managing director earns. **[1]**
**(b)** An accountant prepares a client's tax computation using last year's rules without checking for changes. **[1]**

**2.** Explain the difference between an accountant in business and an accountant in public practice, giving one ethical threat each is likely to face. **[4]**

**3.** Explain what each of these audit opinions tells a shareholder: qualified, adverse, disclaimer. **[3]**

**4.** Explain the role and composition of a remuneration committee and how it supports ethical behaviour. **[4]**

**5.** Explain the role of each of the following in the regulatory framework for accounting: the Financial Reporting Council, the government, and the European Union. **[6]**

**6.** Explain how CCAB and CIMA help establish and enforce codes of conduct for accountants. **[4]**

**7.** Pellow & Co audits Marston Ltd. The same partner has led the audit for eleven years. Pellow & Co also prepares Marston's financial statements, which it then audits. Fees from Marston are 30% of Pellow & Co's income. Identify **three** threats to the fundamental principles and suggest a safeguard for each. **[6]**

**8.** Dana is the management accountant on a panel choosing a new packaging supplier. One bidder offers her a free weekend at a spa hotel. Explain the ethical issues and recommend what Dana should do. **[4]**

**9.** Ashby Kitchens Ltd has a draft profit for the year of £150,000. Closing inventory includes discontinued units at cost of £24,000; they can now be sold for only £6,000. Draft current assets are £210,000 and current liabilities £120,000. A bank loan requires a current ratio of at least 1.70 : 1. The managing director tells the accountant to keep the units at cost.

**(a)** Calculate the corrected profit for the year. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the current ratio before and after the correction and state whether the loan condition is met. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain the ethical issues and recommend the accountant's course of action. **[4]**

**10.** Brindle Foods plc plans to pay small suppliers within 30 days instead of 75, and to switch to fully recyclable packaging at higher cost. One shareholder says: "Corporate social responsibility conflicts with the directors' duty to shareholders." Evaluate this view. **[10]**

**11.** Corvell Logistics plc reports a profit for the year after tax of £3,400,000 and has 8,000,000 issued ordinary shares. Executive share awards vest only if earnings per share is at least 40p. Tom, the financial controller, finds that £520,000 of vehicle repairs was recorded as non-current assets and depreciated at 10%. The chief financial officer tells Tom "the board is happy with it". Ignore taxation.

**(a)** Calculate the corrected profit for the year and earnings per share, and state whether the target is met. **[4]**
**(b)** Evaluate the courses of action open to Tom. **[8]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Confidentiality** [1].
**(b)** **Professional competence and due care** [1].
*Examiner insight:* One word answers are fine for "identify", but "honesty" is not the name of a principle; use the specification's wording.

**2.** An accountant in business is employed by one organisation and prepares its records [1]; they may face an **intimidation threat** from a manager who wants better results [1]. An accountant in public practice sells services such as audit and tax to clients for fees [1]; they may face a **self-interest threat** if one client provides a large share of fees [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each threat must match the role; a threat given without a reason linked to that role is unlikely to gain the second mark.

**3.** **Qualified:** the statements are true and fair except for a specific material matter [1]. **Adverse:** misstatements are material and pervasive, so the statements are not true and fair [1]. **Disclaimer:** the auditor could not obtain enough evidence to give any opinion [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Adverse and disclaimer are often swapped; disagreement is adverse, lack of evidence is a disclaimer.

**4.** It decides the pay of executive directors [1]. It is made up of independent non-executive directors [1], and no director decides their own pay [1]. This reduces the self-interest threat, so pay is less likely to reward short-term figures that executives can influence [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Saying the committee "includes the managing director" contradicts its purpose and loses the composition mark.

**5.** The **FRC** sets UK accounting standards and the UK Corporate Governance Code [1], and regulates auditors and accountants, investigating misconduct and imposing sanctions [1]. The **government** makes company law, such as the Companies Act 2006, which requires accounts to give a true and fair view [1]; the department named in the specification (BIS) has since been replaced, and the Department for Business and Trade now oversees Companies House, the Insolvency Service and the FRC [1]. The **EU**'s IAS Regulation required publicly traded companies to use international standards for consolidated accounts [1]; since the UK left, those standards were frozen into UK law and UK-adopted standards now apply [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Two developed points per body are needed for full marks; "the government makes laws" alone is too vague.

**6.** CCAB is a forum through which its member bodies agree common positions and speak to the UK government with one voice [1]. Each member body publishes a code of ethics that its members must follow as a condition of membership [1]. CIMA, outside CCAB, publishes its own Code of Ethics built on the five fundamental principles [1]. The bodies enforce codes by investigating complaints and disciplining members, up to exclusion from membership [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Describing CIMA as a CCAB member is a factual error; keep the two bodies separate.

**7.** **Familiarity threat** from eleven years with the same partner [1]; safeguard: rotate the audit partner [1]. **Self-review threat** from auditing statements the firm prepared [1]; safeguard: use a separate team for preparation, or have an independent partner review the audit [1]. **Self-interest threat** from dependence on 30% of fees [1]; safeguard: an independent quality review, or reduce dependence or decline reappointment if it cannot be managed [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each safeguard must fit its threat; rotating the partner does nothing about fee dependence.

**8.** The gift could bias her choice, a threat to **objectivity** [1]. Even if she stays fair, others may see a conflict of interest, which risks **professional behaviour** and integrity [1]. This is a **self-interest threat** [1]. She should decline the offer, declare it to her manager under the organisation's gifts policy, and step back from the decision if necessary [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Recommend a specific action; "she should be careful" gains nothing.

**9. (a)** Write-down to net realisable value = 24,000 − 6,000 = £18,000 [1]. Corrected profit = 150,000 − 18,000 = **£132,000** [1].
**(b)** Before: 210,000 / 120,000 = **1.75 : 1** [1]. After: 192,000 / 120,000 = **1.60 : 1** [1]. The corrected ratio is below 1.70 : 1, so the **condition is not met** [1].
**(c)** Keeping the units at cost knowingly overstates profit and assets, breaching **integrity** [1]. Inventory must be valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value, so it breaches **professional competence and due care** [1]. The bank would be misled about a broken loan condition, and the accountant faces an **intimidation threat** [1]. The accountant should refuse, explain the rule in writing, raise it with the board and auditors, and seek advice from their professional body if it is not corrected [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b), an error carried from (a) can still earn method marks if the method is shown; a bare ratio cannot.

**10.** Section 172 of the Companies Act 2006 requires directors to promote the success of the company for its members [1]. **For the view:** both plans raise costs, reducing profit and possibly dividends [1]. Paying in 30 days instead of 75 means cash leaves earlier, reducing liquidity [1]. Shareholders own the company, and spending on others transfers value away from them [1]. **Against:** s172 also requires regard to suppliers, the community and the environment, so CSR is part of the duty, not a conflict [1]. Faster payment keeps small suppliers in business and secures supply [1]. Recyclable packaging can protect reputation and customer loyalty, supporting long-term revenue [1]. It lowers the risk of future regulation or penalties [1]. Under good governance the board should report its approach openly so shareholders can judge it [1]. **Judgement:** CSR conflicts with short-term profit, but not with long-term shareholder value, provided Brindle can afford the cash cost [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" needs points on both sides and a supported judgement; a one-sided answer cannot reach full marks.

**11. (a)** Add back depreciation charged: 520,000 × 10% = £52,000 [1]. Deduct repairs as an expense: 3,400,000 − 520,000 + 52,000 [1] = **£2,932,000** [1]. EPS = 293,200,000p / 8,000,000 = **36.65p**, below the 40p target, so the awards **would not vest** [1].
**(b)** Recording repairs as non-current assets breaches **integrity** and **professional competence and due care** (revenue expenditure treated as capital) [1]. Executives face a **self-interest threat**: the target is met only because of the misstatement [1]. Tom faces an **intimidation threat** from the CFO [1]. **Option 1, escalate internally:** go above the CFO to the audit committee of independent NEDs [1]; this keeps the matter confidential and lets the company correct the accounts before publication [1]. **Option 2, go outside:** seek advice from his professional body and, if needed, report to the auditors or a regulator [1]; this is protected under the Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998 if he reasonably believes there is wrongdoing, but risks his job and needs firm evidence [1]. **Judgement:** Tom should first put his concern in writing to the audit committee, refuse to sign off the figures, and go outside only if the board will not correct them [1].
*Examiner insight:* Weigh at least two options before the judgement; a list of actions with no comparison limits marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a principle without the fact from the scenario that breaches it.
- Calling every breach "integrity" when objectivity or competence fits better.
- Giving a safeguard that does not match the threat.
- Swapping adverse and disclaimer opinions.
- Putting executives on the remuneration committee.
- Saying the government sets accounting standards; that is the FRC.
- Recommending external reporting with no internal step first.
- Skipping the calculation when figures show the ethical effect.
- Finishing an evaluation without a judgement.
