These revision notes cover sub-topic 4(c), **Cycles within ecosystems** (specification points 4.10 and 4.11B), of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) specification, Issue 3. The qualification is untiered. Point 4.10 can be examined on **either paper**. Point 4.11B is bold with a **B** reference and is **Paper 2 only**. They apply to the June and November series examined under Issue 3.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [cycles within ecosystems study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-biology-4bi1-cycles-within-ecosystems/) first. Then test yourself with the [cycles practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-biology-4bi1-cycles-within-ecosystems-practice/). ## Key definitions

- **Photosynthesis:** the process in which plants and algae use light energy to make glucose from carbon dioxide and water.
- **Respiration:** the release of energy from glucose in all living cells, giving off carbon dioxide.
- **Decomposition:** the breakdown of dead organisms and waste by bacteria and fungi.
- **Combustion:** burning, which releases carbon dioxide from fossil fuels or wood.
- **Nitrogen fixation:** converting nitrogen gas into nitrogen compounds that plants can use.
- **Nitrification:** converting ammonium compounds into nitrates.
- **Denitrification:** converting nitrates into nitrogen gas.
- **Legume:** a plant such as a pea or bean with nitrogen-fixing bacteria in its root nodules.

## 4.10 Carbon cycle at a glance

| Process | Carbon moves from | To | Carbon dioxide in air |
|---|---|---|---|
| Photosynthesis | Air | Plants (glucose, starch, cellulose) | Falls |
| Feeding | Plants, then animals | Animals | No change |
| Respiration | Organisms | Air | Rises |
| Decomposition | Dead material and waste | Air (decomposers respire) | Rises |
| Combustion | Fossil fuels and wood | Air | Rises |

Key points:

- Plants both photosynthesise **and** respire. In the light, photosynthesis is usually faster, so there is a net uptake of carbon dioxide.
- Decomposers return carbon from dead material to the air. They also return mineral nutrients to the soil.
- Fossil fuels are the remains of organisms from millions of years ago. Burning them returns that carbon to the air quickly.

## 4.11B Nitrogen cycle at a glance (Paper 2 only)

| Group | Job | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Nitrogen-fixing bacteria | Fix nitrogen | Nitrogen gas → ammonium compounds |
| Decomposers | Break down dead material and urea | Protein and urea → ammonium compounds |
| Nitrifying bacteria | Nitrify | Ammonium compounds → nitrates |
| Denitrifying bacteria | Denitrify | Nitrates → nitrogen gas |

The cycle in one line: **nitrogen gas → (fixing) → ammonium → (nitrifying) → nitrate → (plant roots) → plant protein → animal protein → (decomposers) → ammonium**, with denitrifying bacteria returning nitrate to nitrogen gas.

Key points:

- Plants absorb **nitrate ions** through their roots and use them to make amino acids and proteins.
- Nitrogen-fixing bacteria in the **root nodules** of legumes give the plant a supply of nitrogen compounds. The plant gives the bacteria carbohydrate.
- Denitrifying bacteria work best in **waterlogged soil** with little oxygen. Nitrifying bacteria need oxygen.
- The specification does **not** require the names of the bacteria.

## Two calculations to know

**Net carbon uptake** = carbon taken in by photosynthesis − carbon released by respiration.
Example: 900 − 380 = 520 units.

**Percentage retained** = net uptake ÷ carbon taken in × 100.
Example: 520 ÷ 900 × 100 = 57.8%.

## Method in steps: describing a carbon atom's journey

1. Start with carbon dioxide in the air and name **photosynthesis**.
2. Say where the carbon is now (glucose, then other plant compounds).
3. Name the **feeding** steps in the food chain.
4. Name the processes that return carbon dioxide: **respiration** and **decomposition**.
5. Add **combustion** if fossil fuels or wood are involved.

## Answer frame: "Explain why nitrate is needed"

Plants need nitrate to make amino acids, which are used to make proteins. Without nitrate, growth is poor and leaves may turn yellow.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Respiration vs combustion:** a controlled release of energy in cells vs burning.
- **Nitrification vs denitrification:** ammonium to nitrate vs nitrate to nitrogen gas.
- **Nitrogen-fixing vs nitrifying bacteria:** nitrogen gas to ammonium vs ammonium to nitrate.
- **Decomposer vs nitrifying bacteria:** release ammonium vs convert ammonium.
- **Photosynthesis vs respiration:** takes in carbon dioxide vs gives out carbon dioxide.

## Quick self-test

1. Name two processes that remove carbon from the air or add carbon to it, and say which does which.
2. Why are decomposers important for the carbon cycle?
3. Name the process that returns carbon from fossil fuels to the air.
4. (Paper 2 only) Name the compound in which plants take up nitrogen.
5. (Paper 2 only) Which bacteria live in the root nodules of legumes?
6. (Paper 2 only) Which bacteria turn ammonium compounds into nitrates?
7. (Paper 2 only) Why do farmers drain waterlogged fields?
8. (Paper 2 only) What do decomposers release from urea?
9. In a year, plants take in 640 units of carbon and release 160 units by respiration. Calculate the net uptake.
10. Use your answer to question 9 to find the percentage of carbon taken in that is kept.

### Answers

1. Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide; respiration (or combustion or decomposition) adds it.
2. They break down dead material and release carbon dioxide to the air, so carbon is recycled.
3. Combustion.
4. Nitrate ions.
5. Nitrogen-fixing bacteria.
6. Nitrifying bacteria.
7. Waterlogging encourages denitrifying bacteria, which remove nitrate from the soil.
8. Ammonium compounds (ammonia).
9. 640 − 160 = **480 units**.
10. 480 ÷ 640 × 100 = **75%**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "plants take in nitrogen from the air".
- Forgetting that plants respire.
- Confusing the roles of nitrifying and denitrifying bacteria.
- Writing "carbon" when the question needs "carbon dioxide".
- Missing out decomposers, or describing them as only breaking down food.
- Describing the cycles without naming the processes.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is the nitrogen cycle needed if the air is 78% nitrogen?**
Plants cannot use nitrogen gas. They need nitrate ions, which are made from nitrogen by bacteria.

**What is the role of lightning in the nitrogen cycle?**
It is not required by the 4BI1 specification, so you do not need it for the exam.

**Does burning wood add to the carbon cycle?**
Yes. Combustion releases the carbon stored in the wood as carbon dioxide.
