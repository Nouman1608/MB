Cambridge O Level Mathematics (4024) is not tiered and has two externally assessed papers of identical size. Paper 1 is Non-calculator: 2 hours, 100 marks, 50%, with the use of a calculator not allowed. Paper 2 is Calculator: 2 hours, 100 marks, 50%, and a scientific calculator is required. The papers cover the same nine content areas, so the preparation task is to build accuracy by hand and efficiency with a calculator. Useful companion pages include [algebraic manipulation](/resources/algebraic-manipulation/), [transformations](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-transformations-reflection-rotation-enlargement-and/) and [histograms and frequency density](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-histograms-and-frequency-density-with-unequal-class/).

## One syllabus, two ways of working

Both papers can examine number, algebra and graphs, coordinate geometry, geometry, mensuration, trigonometry, transformations and vectors, probability and statistics. The record does not assign particular topics to a particular paper, so do not assume a topic will appear only on one. Instead, revise every topic in both modes: can you do it without a calculator, and can you do it quickly with one?

## Paper 1: Non-calculator

Paper 1 lasts 2 hours for 100 marks. Without a calculator, the questions reward fluent arithmetic, algebra, exact forms and sensible estimation. Practise operations with fractions and decimals, percentages, ratio, powers and roots, and keep answers exact (for example in fractions or surd form) when the question invites it. Learn the standard values you will need to recall instead of computing. Write more steps than you think necessary, because neat working catches slips and earns method marks.

Algebra and graphs deserve special attention here: expansion, factorisation, rearranging formulae, solving equations and sketching straight lines and curves. Use the [algebraic manipulation revision notes](/resources/algebraic-manipulation-revision-notes/) to practise those skills without a calculator.

## Paper 2: Calculator

Paper 2 also lasts 2 hours for 100 marks, and the record states that a scientific calculator is required. Check your model before the examination: it should be set to degrees, and you should be confident with brackets, fractions, powers, roots, standard form and trigonometric functions. Keep unrounded values in the calculator memory until the final step, then round to the accuracy asked, commonly three significant figures if none is given.

Use the calculator to check reasonableness, not to replace thinking. An estimate made first (for example, rounding each number to one significant figure) will catch an input slip such as a missing bracket. Statistics work such as [scatter diagrams and cumulative frequency](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-scatter-diagrams-correlation-and-cumulative-frequency/) benefits from careful reading of graph scales as much as from calculation.

## Timing and marks per minute

Each paper allows 120 / 100 = 1.2 minutes per mark. A 2-mark question should take about 2.4 minutes, a 5-mark question about 6 minutes. The weightings 50% + 50% = 100% mean that neither paper can be traded off against the other. Because the allocation is slightly above one minute per mark, a 10-minute stall costs roughly 8 marks of opportunity elsewhere, so set a firm rule: if a part has produced nothing after about two minutes, mark it and move on.

## Command words and question styles

Calculate and work out need method shown. Solve needs a value or values satisfying an equation. Show that needs every step to the given result. Simplify and factorise need a final form with no remaining common factors. Sketch needs a shape and key features, and draw needs accurate plotting. Estimate may mean either a rounded calculation or a reading from a graph or histogram. Give answers to the accuracy requested and include units.

## Worked practice scenario

A histogram shows the times in minutes that 105 students spent on homework. The data are grouped as follows: 0 < t <= 10 has 15 students, 10 < t <= 30 has 50 students, 30 < t <= 40 has 18 students and 40 < t <= 60 has 22 students.

(a) Calculate the frequency density of each class. (b) Estimate how many students spent more than 20 minutes and up to 30 minutes.

**Model answer.**

(a) Frequency density = frequency / class width.
- 0 < t <= 10: 15 / 10 = 1.5
- 10 < t <= 30: 50 / 20 = 2.5
- 30 < t <= 40: 18 / 10 = 1.8
- 40 < t <= 60: 22 / 20 = 1.1

Check the total: 15 + 50 + 18 + 22 = 105, which matches.

(b) The interval 20 < t <= 30 lies inside the class 10 < t <= 30 and has width 10. Assuming students are spread evenly through the class, the estimate is 10 x 2.5 = 25 students.

Note that this question can be completed without a calculator, so it fits Paper 1 style, and a candidate with a calculator should still show the frequency density formula.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Check that you have a scientific calculator, set to degrees, plus geometry equipment.
- Practise a full non-calculator paper and a full calculator paper under timed conditions.
- Learn formulae you must recall and know which are not provided.
- On Paper 1, show every arithmetic step and keep exact forms.
- On Paper 2, store unrounded values and round only at the end.
- Estimate before calculating to catch input errors.
- Label graphs and axes, and plot carefully.
- Use spare time to recheck signs, units and accuracy requirements.

## Self-test

1. Which paper requires a scientific calculator?
2. How many minutes per mark does each paper allow?
3. What fraction of the total does each paper carry?
4. In the worked scenario, what is the frequency density for 10 < t <= 30?
5. Why estimate before using a calculator?

**Answers:** 1. Paper 2. 2. 120 / 100 = 1.2 minutes per mark. 3. 50% each. 4. 50 / 20 = 2.5. 5. An estimate catches input errors and confirms the answer is reasonable.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge O Level Mathematics 4024?**
Two, both externally assessed: Paper 1 Non-calculator and Paper 2 Calculator.

**How long and how many marks is each paper?**
Each is 2 hours and 100 marks, and each is worth 50%.

**Can I use a calculator in Paper 1?**
No. A calculator is not allowed in Paper 1, while a scientific calculator is required in Paper 2.

**Is the syllabus tiered?**
No. All candidates take the same two papers.

**Which series does this syllabus cover?**
Examinations in 2025 to 2027, in the June and November series, with no separate first-teaching date published by Cambridge.
