Pearson Edexcel International A Level Law (YLA1) introduces students to how
law is made, how it is applied and how it affects businesses, offenders and
individuals, using English law as its framework. It rewards students who
enjoy structured argument and applying rules to facts. The course is not
split into separate AS and A2 stages. The
whole qualification is assessed by two compulsory papers taken in the same
series, each lasting 180 minutes, carrying 100 marks and worth 50%. The
specification was first taught in September 2015 and first examined in June
2017.

## How the course is organised

The content is arranged around the two papers.

**Paper 1: Underlying Principles of Law and the English Legal System**

- **The nature, purpose of and liability in law** asks what law is for and
  how legal liability arises.
- **The sources of English law, its enforcement and administration** looks
  at where law comes from and at the institutions and people who enforce and
  administer it.

**Paper 2: The Law in Action**

- **The market** is the law of contract: how agreements become legally
  binding and what happens when they go wrong.
- **The criminal offender** is criminal law.
- **The individual** covers freedom of speech and defamation, human rights,
  privacy, occupiers' liability and trespass to land.

Paper 1 is about the legal system as a whole; Paper 2 is about specific areas
of substantive law and how they apply to real situations.

## How it's assessed

YLA1 is a linear qualification. Both papers are compulsory and are sat
together in a single series, which the specification gives as June only.
There are no tiers and no optional components.

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| YLA1/01 | Paper 1: Underlying Principles of Law and the English Legal System | 180 minutes (3h) | 100 | 50% |
| YLA1/02 | Paper 2: The Law in Action | 180 minutes (3h) | 100 | 50% |

Both papers are written exams and both are externally assessed. As the two
papers have equal weight and are taken in the same series, the
qualification rewards consistent preparation across all of the content
rather than early completion of part of it.

## How the parts fit together

Paper 1 provides the tools that Paper 2 uses. Understanding where English
law comes from, and how it is enforced and administered, helps explain why contract law, criminal law and the law
protecting individuals look the way they do. Ideas about the purpose of law
and the nature of liability in Paper 1 reappear when Paper 2 asks whether a
person is liable in contract, guilty of an offence, or responsible to a
visitor or trespasser.

The links also run the other way. Topics in Paper 2, such as human rights,
freedom of speech and privacy, give concrete examples of the tensions
between individual freedom and wider interests that Paper 1 discusses in
general terms. Using Paper 2 cases as illustrations in Paper 1 answers, and
Paper 1 concepts as a frame for Paper 2 evaluation, strengthens both.

Three skills run through the whole course. The first is accurate knowledge
of legal rules and the authorities behind them. The second is application:
taking a set of facts and working out, step by step, how the law applies.
The third is evaluation: discussing whether the law is fair, clear or in
need of reform. All three are needed on both papers.

## Planning your study

Because both papers are sat at the end of the course, you have freedom to
order the content, but a logical route helps. Many teachers begin with
Paper 1's principles and the sources of law, since these give the
vocabulary and methods used everywhere else. Contract law (the market) and
criminal law (the criminal offender) are large blocks that benefit from
steady coverage over the first and second years. The individual, with its
range of topics from defamation to occupiers' liability, can be taught in
smaller units.

Keep a case and statute log from the first week, organised by topic, with a
one-line summary of what each authority decides. Practise applying the law to
short scenarios regularly, rather than only learning rules, because
application is tested on both papers.

In the final months, revisit Paper 1 as well as Paper 2, since it is easy to
let the principles fade while working on substantive law. Start timed
practice once all the content has been taught. For how to structure your
time across two three-hour papers, see the exam-preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Law YLA1?**
There are two compulsory papers, Paper 1 (Underlying Principles of Law and the English Legal System) and Paper 2 (The Law in Action). Each lasts 180 minutes, carries 100 marks and is worth 50%.

**Does International A Level Law have separate AS and A2 units?**
No. YLA1 is not split into separate IAS and IA2 stages, and both papers are taken in the same series.

**When can YLA1 be sat?**
Both papers are taken together in a single series, which the specification gives as June only.

**What areas of law does Paper 2 cover?**
Paper 2 covers the market (the law of contract), the criminal offender (criminal law) and the individual (freedom of speech and defamation, human rights, privacy, occupiers' liability and trespass to land).

**When was YLA1 first examined?**
It was first taught in September 2015 and first examined in June 2017.
