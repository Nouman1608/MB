## Section A

**1.** Which is the correct formula of nitrogen gas as it is written in a chemical equation?

A: N

B: N₂

C: N₃

D: NH₃

**[1]**

**2.** Define the term *molecular formula*. **[1]**

**3.** A ball-and-stick model of a molecule shows three carbon atoms and eight hydrogen atoms. Write its molecular formula. **[1]**

**4.** State the meaning of each state symbol: (s), (l), (g) and (aq). **[2]**

**5.** *(Supplement)* Which is the empirical formula of butene, C₄H₈?

A: CH

B: CH₂

C: C₂H₄

D: C₄H₈

**[1]**

**6.** *(Supplement)* Deduce the formulae of: (a) potassium oxide; (b) aluminium hydroxide; (c) ammonium nitrate; (d) iron(II) sulfate. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** Calcium reacts with water to form calcium hydroxide and hydrogen.

**(a)** Write a word equation for this reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Write a balanced symbol equation, including state symbols. Calcium hydroxide is slightly soluble and may be shown as aqueous. **[3]**

**8.** Balance each equation.

**(a)** `Na + Cl₂ → NaCl` **[1]**

**(b)** `Fe₂O₃ + CO → Fe + CO₂` **[1]**

**(c)** `C₂H₆ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O` **[1]**

**9.** A student writes the equation for burning hydrogen as `H₂ + O₂ → H₂O₂`. Explain what the student has done wrong and write the correct balanced equation. **[3]**

**10.** *(Supplement)* Lead(II) nitrate solution is added to potassium iodide solution. A yellow precipitate of lead(II) iodide forms.

**(a)** Write the balanced symbol equation with state symbols. **[3]**

**(b)** Write the ionic equation, with state symbols. **[2]**

**(c)** Name the spectator ions. **[1]**

**11.** *(Supplement)* Write the ionic equation, with state symbols, for the reaction of zinc with aqueous copper(II) sulfate, and show that the charges balance. **[3]**

**12.** *(Supplement)* When solid sodium hydrogencarbonate, NaHCO₃, is heated, it forms solid sodium carbonate, water vapour and carbon dioxide gas. Deduce the balanced symbol equation, with state symbols. **[3]**

**13.** *(Supplement)* A diagram of part of a lattice shows aluminium ions and oxide ions. For every two aluminium ions there are three oxide ions.

**(a)** Write the formula of the compound. **[1]**

**(b)** Use the charges on the ions to show that this formula is neutral. **[2]**

**14.** In the Contact process, sulfur dioxide gas reacts with oxygen gas to form sulfur trioxide gas, SO₃.

**(a)** Write a word equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Write a balanced symbol equation with state symbols. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Nitrogen exists as diatomic molecules.

**2.** The **number and type of different atoms in one molecule** [1].

**3.** **C₃H₈** [1].

**4.** (s) solid and (l) liquid [1]; (g) gas and (aq) aqueous, meaning dissolved in water [1].

**5.** **B** [1]. 4 : 8 simplifies to 1 : 2.

**6.**

**(a)** **K₂O** [1].

**(b)** **Al(OH)₃** [1].

**(c)** **NH₄NO₃** [1].

**(d)** **FeSO₄** [1].

**7.**

**(a)** calcium + water → calcium hydroxide + hydrogen [1].

**(b)** Correct formulae: Ca, H₂O, Ca(OH)₂, H₂ [1]; balanced: **Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂** [1]; state symbols: Ca(s), H₂O(l), Ca(OH)₂(aq), H₂(g) [1].

**8.**

**(a)** **2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl** [1].

**(b)** **Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂** [1].

**(c)** **2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O** [1]. (Check: C 4 = 4; H 12 = 12; O 14 = 8 + 6. Balancing C and H first gives 3.5O₂ for one ethane, so double everything to clear the fraction.)

**9.** The student **changed the formula of the product** (H₂O to H₂O₂) [1] instead of balancing with coefficients; H₂O₂ is a different substance [1]. Correct: **2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O** [1].

**10.**

**(a)** Lead(II) is Pb²⁺, so two NO₃⁻ and two I⁻ are needed. Correct formulae Pb(NO₃)₂ and PbI₂ [1]; balanced: Pb(NO₃)₂ + 2KI → PbI₂ + 2KNO₃ [1]; states: Pb(NO₃)₂(aq), KI(aq), PbI₂(s), KNO₃(aq) [1].

**(b)** **Pb²⁺(aq) + 2I⁻(aq)** [1] **→ PbI₂(s)** [1].

**(c)** **Potassium ions and nitrate ions** (K⁺ and NO₃⁻) [1].

**11.** **Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq)** [1] **→ Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s)** [1]. Charge: left 0 + (2+) = 2+; right (2+) + 0 = 2+, so balanced [1].

**12.** Correct formulae: NaHCO₃, Na₂CO₃, H₂O, CO₂ [1]; balanced: **2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂** [1]; states: NaHCO₃(s), Na₂CO₃(s), H₂O(g), CO₂(g) [1]. Water is (g) here because the question says water vapour. (Check: Na 2 = 2; H 2 = 2; C 2 = 1 + 1; O 6 = 3 + 1 + 2.)

**13.**

**(a)** **Al₂O₃** [1].

**(b)** Two Al³⁺ give **6+** and three O²⁻ give **6−** [1]; total charge 6+ + 6− = **0** [1].

**14.**

**(a)** sulfur dioxide + oxygen → sulfur trioxide [1].

**(b)** Balanced: **2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃** [1]; all three species shown as **(g)** [1]. (Check: S 2 = 2; O 4 + 2 = 6 = 2 × 3.)

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing Cl, O, N or H instead of the diatomic molecules.
- Balancing by altering subscripts, which changes the identity of a substance.
- Leaving brackets off compound ions when more than one is needed, as in Al(OH)₃ and Pb(NO₃)₂.
- Using (aq) for water or for a precipitate.
- Including spectator ions in an ionic equation, or splitting a solid metal into ions.
- Giving state symbols for some species but not all, when the question asks for them.
- Simplifying a molecular formula when the question asks for the molecular, not the empirical, formula.
- Forgetting the Roman numeral: iron(II) sulfate is FeSO₄ but iron(III) sulfate is Fe₂(SO₄)₃, so the numeral decides the formula.
- Not checking charge balance in an ionic equation; an equation can have equal atoms on both sides and still be wrong.
