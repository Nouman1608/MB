O Level Accounting 7707. The syllabus asks you to understand:

- "the purposes and limitations of a trial balance"
- how to prepare a trial balance "from a given list of balances and amend a
trial balance which contains errors"
- the types of errors which do not affect the trial balance: "commission,
compensating, complete reversal, omission, original entry, principle".

## What a trial balance is

A **trial balance** is a list of the balances on all the ledger accounts
(including the cash book) at a stated date, set out in a debit column and a
credit column. Because every transaction has a debit entry and an equal
credit entry, the two column totals should agree.

The heading always names the business and the date: "Trial balance at 31
December 2027", not "for the year ended". It is a list at one moment, not a
statement covering a period.

Every balance goes in the column on which it sits in the ledger. A quick way
to decide is to ask what kind of account it is:

| Debit column | Credit column |
|---|---|
| Assets (premises, equipment, vehicles, inventory, trade receivables, bank, cash) | Capital |
| Expenses (wages, rent paid, carriage inwards, carriage outwards, discount allowed) | Liabilities (trade payables, loans, bank overdraft) |
| Purchases and sales returns (returns inwards) | Revenue and purchases returns (returns outwards) |
| Drawings | Incomes (rent received, commission received, discount received) |
| | Provision for depreciation, allowance for irrecoverable debts |

The two "less" accounts in the last row are credit balances because they
reduce an asset. The inventory in a trial balance is the **opening**
inventory. Closing inventory is counted at the year end and is normally
given as a note below the trial balance.

## Purposes and limitations

A trial balance has three main purposes:

1. **To check the arithmetical accuracy of the double entry.** If the totals
agree, each debit has probably been matched by an equal credit.
2. **To help find errors.** A difference tells you something has gone wrong
before the financial statements are prepared.
3. **To provide a summary of balances** from which the statement of profit or
loss and statement of financial position can be drawn up.

Its main **limitation** is that agreement does not prove the records are
correct. Six types of error leave the totals equal, because each of them has
an equal debit and credit. The trial balance also says nothing about whether
a transaction has been recorded in the right period or at a sensible value.

## Worked example 1: preparing a trial balance from a list of balances

Farida's fabric shop has the following balances at 31 December 2027.
Closing inventory was $7,900.

```
Farida
Trial balance at 31 December 2027
                                                    Debit $     Credit $
Capital                                                          48,000
Drawings                                              9,600
Revenue                                                          96,400
Purchases                                            58,300
Sales returns                                         1,200
Purchases returns                                                   900
Inventory at 1 January 2027                           6,800
Premises                                             40,000
Fixtures and fittings                                12,500
Provision for depreciation of fixtures and fittings               5,000
Motor vehicle                                        16,000
Trade receivables                                     7,400
Trade payables                                                    5,900
Bank overdraft                                                    2,300
Cash                                                    350
Wages                                                14,800
General expenses                                      3,300
Carriage inwards                                        520
Discount allowed                                        610
Discount received                                                   480
Rent received                                                     2,400
Bank loan (repayable 2032)                                       10,000
                                                    -------     -------
                                                    171,380     171,380
                                                    =======     =======
```

Points to notice:

- The **bank overdraft** is a credit balance: the business owes the bank.
Cash in hand is a debit, so one business can have both.
- **Discount allowed** is an expense (debit); **discount received** is an
income (credit). Swapping them is the most common single slip.
- The closing inventory of $7,900 is **not** in the list. It goes into the
statement of profit or loss and statement of financial position later.

## Worked example 2: amending a trial balance that contains errors

A trainee at Hollis Repairs drew up this trial balance at 30 June 2027. The
totals do not agree.

| Account | Trainee's debit $ | Trainee's credit $ |
|---|---|---|
| Capital | | 30,000 |
| Equipment | 39,680 | |
| Discount received | 350 | |
| Sales returns | | 640 |
| Trade payables | | 4,200 |
| Trade receivables | 5,100 | |
| Revenue | | 62,000 |
| Purchases | 38,500 | |
| Purchases returns | 420 | |
| Bank overdraft | 1,900 | |
| Allowance for irrecoverable debts | 150 | |
| General expenses | 12,000 | |
| Drawings | | 7,000 |
| Inventory at 1 July 2026 | 3,500 | |
| Loan interest | 600 | |
| Loan from finance company | | 8,000 |
| **Totals** | **102,200** | **111,840** |

**Step 1: test each balance against the column rules.** Four credit balances
have been listed as debits: discount received, purchases returns, bank
overdraft and the allowance for irrecoverable debts ($350 + $420 + $1,900 +
$150 = $2,820). Two debit balances have been listed as credits: sales
returns and drawings ($640 + $7,000 = $7,640).

**Step 2: redraft, do not patch.** Move each item to its correct column and
total again:

```
Hollis Repairs
Trial balance at 30 June 2027
                                          Debit $     Credit $
Capital                                                30,000
Equipment                                  39,680
Discount received                                         350
Sales returns                                 640
Trade payables                                          4,200
Trade receivables                           5,100
Revenue                                                62,000
Purchases                                  38,500
Purchases returns                                         420
Bank overdraft                                          1,900
Allowance for irrecoverable debts                         150
General expenses                           12,000
Drawings                                    7,000
Inventory at 1 July 2026                    3,500
Loan interest                                 600
Loan from finance company                               8,000
                                          -------     -------
                                          107,020     107,020
                                          =======     =======
```

**Step 3: check with the arithmetic.** Moving $2,820 from debit to credit and
$7,640 from credit to debit changes the debit total by −$2,820 + $7,640 =
+$4,820, from $102,200 to $107,020. The credit total changes by +$2,820 −
$7,640 = −$4,820, from $111,840 to $107,020. A useful rule follows: **one
balance in the wrong column creates a difference of twice its amount**. If a
trial balance is out by $1,300, look for a balance of $650 on the wrong side.

## The six errors that do not affect the trial balance

Each error below has an equal debit and credit, so the totals still agree.
Learn the name, a definition and an example of each.

| Error | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Commission | Correct amount posted to the correct side of the wrong account of the **same class** | A payment to J Ali, a supplier, debited to J Allen, another supplier |
| Compensating | Two or more errors that cancel each other out | Wages account overcast by $100 and revenue account overcast by $100 |
| Complete reversal | Correct accounts used but debit and credit entries swapped | Cash sales of $300 debited to revenue and credited to cash |
| Omission | Transaction left out of the books completely | A credit sale invoice of $480 never entered in the sales journal |
| Original entry | Wrong amount in the book of prime entry, so both postings are wrong | A purchase invoice of $1,620 entered as $1,260 in the purchases journal |
| Principle | Amount posted to the wrong **class** of account | Repairs to a van debited to the motor vehicles account |

The difference between commission and principle is the class of account.
Two trade receivables, or two expense accounts, are the same class, so a
mix-up between them is commission. An expense posted to a non-current asset
account crosses classes, so it is principle.

Errors that **do** make the totals disagree include posting only one side of
a transaction, posting both entries to the same side, posting different
amounts to each side, adding up an account wrongly (a casting error on one
side), leaving a balance out of the trial balance, and putting a balance in
the wrong column. These are corrected through a suspense account in 3.2.

## Common mistakes

- Heading the trial balance "for the year ended" instead of "at".
- Placing closing inventory in the trial balance as well as the opening
figure.
- Treating the provision for depreciation or the allowance for irrecoverable
debts as a debit because they relate to assets.
- Calling a mix-up between two supplier accounts an error of principle.
- Claiming that a trial balance that agrees "proves the books are correct".
- Patching a wrong total by adding a figure, instead of redrafting.

## Exam technique

The 7707 command words set the shape of the answer:

- **Prepare** means "present information in a suitable format": a full
heading, account names, two money columns, and double-ruled totals.
- **Explain** means "set out purposes or reasons": for a purpose, say what
the trial balance checks and why that matters.
- **State** and **Identify** need the name only, for example "error of
commission".
- **Give an example** questions need figures and named accounts, not a
restated definition.

Paper 1 multiple-choice items can ask which error leaves the totals
unchanged, or which balance belongs on the credit side. In the structured
Paper 2, a trial balance question can lead into the suspense work of
3.2, so a correct trial balance is the base for later marks.
