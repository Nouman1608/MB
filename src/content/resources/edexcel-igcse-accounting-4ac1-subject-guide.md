Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Accounting (4AC1) introduces the
way businesses record transactions, check their records and produce
financial statements. It is a good fit for students who like working
with numbers carefully and want a practical understanding of how
organisations keep track of money, whether or not they go on to study
accounting further. The course is linear and examined through two
written papers: Paper 1, Introduction to Bookkeeping and Accounting,
lasting 2 hours for 100 marks (66.6%), and Paper 2, Financial
Statements, lasting 1 hour 15 minutes for 50 marks (33.3%). The
specification is Issue 1 (October 2016), first examined in June 2019.

## How the course is organised

The specification divides the content into five areas that move from
context, to recording, to checking, to reporting.

1. **The accounting environment** sets the scene: types of business
   organisation, accounting concepts, the use of technology in
   accounting and professional ethics.
2. **Introduction to bookkeeping** is the practical heart of the
   course. It covers business documentation, books of original entry,
   ledger accounting, capital and revenue expenditure, depreciation,
   irrecoverable debts, and other receivables and other payables.
3. **Introduction to control processes** shows how records are checked,
   through the trial balance, control accounts, the correction of errors
   and bank reconciliation statements.
4. **The preparation of financial statements** brings everything
   together. Students prepare the financial statements of a sole trader,
   a partnership and a manufacturer, work with incomplete records, and
   calculate and interpret accounting ratios.
5. **Accounting for end of period adjustments** returns to accounting
   concepts, depreciation, irrecoverable debts, and other receivables
   and payables, this time as adjustments made when statements are
   prepared.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4AC1/01 | Paper 1: Introduction to Bookkeeping and Accounting | 2 hours | 100 | 66.6% |
| 4AC1/02 | Paper 2: Financial Statements | 1 hour 15 minutes | 50 | 33.3% |

Both papers are externally assessed and calculators may be used in
each. The qualification is linear, so both papers are sat in the same
series, and the specification offers January and June series.

The weighting is uneven by design. Paper 1 carries roughly two-thirds
of the qualification and, as its title suggests, focuses on the
bookkeeping and accounting foundations. Paper 2 is shorter and centres
on financial statements. Pearson also lists a modular route under a
separate code (4XAC1), which this overview does not cover; check with
your school which route you are entered for.

## How the parts fit together

Accounting is cumulative, and 4AC1 makes that very clear. You cannot
prepare a sole trader's financial statements without understanding
ledger accounts, and you cannot correct a trial balance without knowing
how the double entry system is supposed to work.

Some topics deliberately appear twice. Depreciation, irrecoverable
debts, and other receivables and payables are introduced in the
bookkeeping section, where the focus is on recording them in the
ledger. They reappear in the end of period adjustments section, where
the focus shifts to how they change the figures in the financial
statements. Studying the two treatments side by side is one of the most
useful things you can do in this course.

The accounting concepts introduced in the first section also run
through everything else. They explain why adjustments are needed in
the first place, and they give you the
language to justify a treatment in written answers. Ratio analysis,
meanwhile, links the numbers you prepare to judgements about a
business's performance, so it draws on almost the whole course.

## Planning your study

A logical order follows the specification closely:

- **Begin with the accounting environment and documentation,** so you
  know what kinds of businesses and records you are dealing with.
- **Spend plenty of time on ledger accounting.** Practise until
  recording transactions, balancing accounts and extracting a trial
  balance become automatic.
- **Move to control processes,** including bank reconciliations and
  error correction, once ledger work is secure.
- **Prepare financial statements,** starting with the sole trader and
  then moving to partnerships, manufacturers and incomplete records.
- **Finish with end of period adjustments and ratios,** returning to
  earlier topics to see how they feed into the final figures.

Over a two-year course, aim to complete the bookkeeping and control
sections in the first year and the financial statements, adjustments
and ratios in the second. Short, frequent practice is better than
occasional long sessions, because accuracy improves with repetition.
Start full timed papers once all five areas are covered, and use the
exam-preparation page for advice on approaching each paper.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are in Edexcel IGCSE Accounting 4AC1?**
Two. Paper 1, Introduction to Bookkeeping and Accounting, lasts 2 hours and is worth 100 marks (66.6%). Paper 2, Financial Statements, lasts 1 hour 15 minutes and is worth 50 marks (33.3%).

**Can I use a calculator in IGCSE Accounting?**
Yes. Calculators may be used in both papers.

**When are IGCSE Accounting exams available?**
The specification offers January and June series. Because the course is linear, both papers are taken in the same series.

**Why do depreciation and irrecoverable debts appear twice in the specification?**
They are first studied as bookkeeping entries and then again as end of period adjustments, where the focus is how they affect the financial statements.

**Is there coursework in Edexcel IGCSE Accounting?**
No. Both papers are externally assessed written exams.
