## The three statements in 3.7

All three are **internal** statements: prepared for use inside the company, **not for publication**.

| Statement | Purpose | Must show |
|---|---|---|
| Income statement | Profit or loss for the period | Profit from operations; profit for the year before tax; profit for the year after tax |
| Statement of changes in equity | Movement in each part of equity | Opening balances; share issues (including at a premium); dividends paid; profit (or loss) for the year; closing balances |
| Statement of financial position | Assets, equity and liabilities at the year end | Sub-headings: non-current assets, current assets, equity, current liabilities, non-current liabilities |

**Not examined in 3.7:** preference shares and general reserves.
**Section 3.16 instead:** statements of cash flow, schedules of non-current assets, revaluation, rights and bonus issues, published accounts.

## Key terms

- **Ordinary share capital** -- number of shares issued x nominal value.
- **Nominal value** -- the face value of a share (for example £1 or £0.50).
- **Share premium** -- the amount received above nominal value on a share issue. Part of equity, never income.
- **Retained earnings** -- accumulated profits after tax, less dividends paid.
- **Dividend** -- a distribution of profit to shareholders. Not an expense.
- **Debenture** -- long-term borrowing with a fixed rate of interest.
- **Directors' remuneration** -- pay for directors. An operating expense.
- **Cash and cash equivalents** -- the company term for cash in hand and cash at bank.

## Formulas and links between statements

| What | How |
|---|---|
| Share capital on an issue | Number of shares x nominal value |
| Share premium on an issue | Number of shares x (issue price - nominal value) |
| Profit from operations | Gross profit + other operating income - operating expenses |
| Profit for the year before tax | Profit from operations - finance costs |
| Profit for the year after tax | Profit for the year before tax - tax |
| Debenture interest charge | Rate x debentures for the full year (or the months outstanding) |
| Closing retained earnings | Opening + profit after tax (or - loss) - dividends paid |
| Total equity | Share capital + share premium + retained earnings |
| Balance check | Total assets = total equity + total liabilities |

## Method in steps: trial balance to company statements

```
1. Work every adjustment twice (income statement effect
   and statement of financial position effect).
2. Income statement: trading section -> gross profit ->
   operating expenses -> PROFIT FROM OPERATIONS ->
   finance costs -> PROFIT BEFORE TAX -> tax ->
   PROFIT AFTER TAX.
3. Statement of changes in equity: opening balances,
   share issue split into capital and premium,
   profit after tax, dividends paid, closing balances.
4. Statement of financial position: closing equity from
   step 3; add tax payable and accrued interest to
   current liabilities.
5. Check total assets = total equity and liabilities.
```

## Small worked reminders

**Share issue.** 80,000 ordinary shares of £0.25 each are issued at £0.90.

```
Dr Bank            72,000   (80,000 x 0.90)
Cr Share capital   20,000   (80,000 x 0.25)
Cr Share premium   52,000   (80,000 x 0.65)
```

**Profit levels.** Gross profit £180,000; operating expenses £112,500; 8% debentures of £75,000 outstanding all year; tax £14,700.

```
Gross profit                        180,000
Less operating expenses             112,500
Profit from operations               67,500
Less debenture interest (8% x 75,000) 6,000
Profit for the year before tax       61,500
Less tax                             14,700
Profit for the year after tax        46,800
```

**Retained earnings.** Opening £84,300, profit after tax £46,800, dividends paid £20,000: closing = 84,300 + 46,800 - 20,000 = **£111,100**.

## Where each item goes

| Item | Statement and position |
|---|---|
| Directors' remuneration | Income statement, operating expenses |
| Debenture interest (full year) | Income statement, finance costs |
| Accrued debenture interest | SOFP, current liabilities (other payables) |
| Tax for the year | Income statement, below profit before tax |
| Tax unpaid at year end | SOFP, current liabilities |
| Dividends paid | Statement of changes in equity, retained earnings column |
| Share premium | SOCE (on issue) and SOFP, equity |
| Debentures repayable after more than 12 months | SOFP, non-current liabilities |
| Bank overdraft | SOFP, current liabilities |

## Skeleton layouts to memorise

```
STATEMENT OF FINANCIAL POSITION
Non-current assets (carrying amounts)          x
Current assets                                 x
Total assets                                   X
Equity: share capital, share premium,
        retained earnings                      x
Non-current liabilities: debentures, loans     x
Current liabilities: trade payables, other
  payables, tax payable, bank overdraft        x
Total equity and liabilities                   X

STATEMENT OF CHANGES IN EQUITY
Columns: share capital | share premium | retained earnings | total
Rows: start balance, share issue, profit (loss) for the year,
      dividends paid, end balance
```

**Loss year.** If the company makes a loss after tax of £9,000, show (9,000) on the profit line of the statement of changes in equity. Any dividends paid are deducted as well, so retained earnings fall by both amounts.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Sole trader capital vs company equity.** A sole trader has one capital account; a company splits equity into share capital, share premium and retained earnings.
- **Drawings vs dividends.** Drawings reduce a sole trader's capital; dividends paid reduce retained earnings. Neither is an expense.
- **Owner's reward vs directors' remuneration.** A sole trader's own "wage" is drawings; directors' pay is an expense of the company.
- **Profit from operations vs profit before tax.** The gap is finance costs.
- **Profit before tax vs profit after tax.** The gap is tax. Only profit after tax goes to retained earnings.
- **Share capital vs share premium.** Nominal value only vs the excess over nominal value.

## Quick self-test

1. 120,000 ordinary shares of £0.50 are issued at £0.75. Calculate the bank receipt and the share premium.
2. Which profit figure goes into the statement of changes in equity?
3. A company has 5% debentures of £90,000 all year and has paid £2,250 interest. How much interest is accrued?
4. Profit from operations £48,000; finance costs £6,000; tax £9,500. Calculate profit before tax and profit after tax.
5. Retained earnings at the start £25,000; loss for the year £8,000; dividends paid £4,000. Calculate closing retained earnings.
6. Under which sub-heading does tax payable appear?
7. Where does directors' remuneration appear?
8. Retained earnings rose from £60,000 to £71,000. Profit after tax was £23,000. Calculate dividends paid.
9. Name the two items the specification says will not be examined in 3.7.
10. Share capital £200,000; share premium £35,000; retained earnings £64,000. Calculate total equity.
11. Gross profit £150,000; profit from operations £54,000; no other operating income. Calculate operating expenses.
12. Why are these statements described as "internal"?

### Answers

1. Bank 120,000 x £0.75 = **£90,000**; premium 120,000 x £0.25 = **£30,000**.
2. **Profit for the year after tax** (or the loss).
3. Full year 5% x 90,000 = £4,500; accrued 4,500 - 2,250 = **£2,250**.
4. Before tax 48,000 - 6,000 = **£42,000**; after tax 42,000 - 9,500 = **£32,500**.
5. 25,000 - 8,000 - 4,000 = **£13,000**.
6. **Current liabilities.**
7. **Income statement**, as an operating expense (before profit from operations).
8. 60,000 + 23,000 - 71,000 = **£12,000**.
9. **Preference shares** and **general reserves**.
10. 200,000 + 35,000 + 64,000 = **£299,000**.
11. 150,000 - 54,000 = **£96,000**.
12. They are prepared for **use within the company** (directors and managers), not for publication.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividends paid deducted in the income statement as well as, or instead of, in the statement of changes in equity.
- Only the debenture interest paid charged, not the full year's interest.
- Debenture interest placed among operating expenses, so profit from operations is wrong.
- Tax deducted before profit from operations, or tax payable missing from current liabilities.
- Whole proceeds of a share issue credited to share capital, with no share premium.
- Profit before tax carried into retained earnings.
- The trial balance share capital used as the opening balance when shares were issued during the year.
- Profit lines without the exact labels: profit from operations, profit for the year before tax, profit for the year after tax.
- Equity totals in the statement of financial position not agreeing with the closing totals in the statement of changes in equity.
