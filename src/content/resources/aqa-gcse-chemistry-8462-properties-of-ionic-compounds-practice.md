These questions cover section **4.2.2.3 Properties of ionic compounds** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462)
specification, for teaching from September 2016 onwards (version 1.2). The content is examined on Paper 1 at
Foundation and Higher Tier, and no question here tests Higher-tier-only content.

Learn the content first in the [properties of ionic compounds study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-properties-of-ionic-compounds/)

## Section A

**1.** **(a)** Name the type of structure found in sodium chloride. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the force that holds the particles in sodium chloride together. **[1]**

**2.** Sodium chloride is a solid at room temperature.

**(a)** State whether it conducts electricity when solid. **[1]**
**(b)** State **two** conditions under which it conducts electricity. **[2]**

**3.** Which statement about the melting points of ionic compounds is correct? Choose **one**. **[1]**

- A They are low because the forces between ions are weak.
- B They are high because many strong bonds must be broken.
- C They are low because ionic compounds are made of small molecules.
- D They are high because the covalent bonds inside molecules are strong.

**4.** Potassium chloride melts at 770 °C. Describe what happens to the ions when potassium chloride melts. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** The table shows the results of tests on three white solids, A, B and C.

| Solid | Melting point / °C | Conducts as a solid? | Conducts when dissolved in water? |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 801 | No | Yes |
| B | 63 | No | No |
| C | 1535 | No | No |

**(a)** Which solid is ionic? **[1]**
**(b)** Give **two** pieces of evidence from the table for your answer. **[2]**
**(c)** Which solid is most likely to consist of small molecules? Give a reason. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why solid sodium chloride does not conduct electricity but molten sodium chloride does. **[3]**

**7.** A student dissolves sodium chloride in water and connects the solution to a lamp and a cell. The lamp
lights.

**(a)** State what carries the charge through the solution. **[1]**
**(b)** The student replaces the solution with dry crystals of sodium chloride. The lamp does not light. Explain
the difference. **[2]**

**8.** A student says: "Sodium chloride consists of molecules, each containing one sodium atom and one chlorine
atom, held together by weak forces, so it should have a low melting point."

**(a)** Give **two** things that are wrong with this statement. **[2]**
**(b)** Write a correct explanation of why sodium chloride has a high melting point. **[4]**

**9.** A lattice contains many positive ions and many negative ions.

**(a)** Explain why a large amount of energy is needed to melt an ionic compound. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the formula of an ionic compound does not show a molecule. **[1]**

**10.** Describe, in terms of structure and bonding, why magnesium oxide has a high melting point and conducts
electricity only when molten or dissolved. You may assume magnesium oxide has a lattice like sodium chloride.
**[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Giant ionic lattice** [1]
**(b)** **Electrostatic** attraction between oppositely charged ions (ionic bonds) [1]

**2. (a)** **No** [1]
**(b)** Any two [1] [1]: **melted** (molten); **dissolved in water** (aqueous solution). **[2]**

**3.** **B** [1]

**4.** The **regular lattice breaks down** [1]; the ions are **free to move** past each other [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** **A** [1]
**(b)** Any two [1] [1]: **high melting point** (801 °C); does **not conduct as a solid**; **conducts when dissolved**
(the ions are free to move). **[2]**
**(c)** **B** [1]; it has a **low melting point** and does not conduct, so it has only weak forces between
molecules [1]. **[2]**

**6.** In the solid, the ions are held in **fixed positions** in the lattice [1] so they cannot move [1]. When
molten, the ions are **free to move** and carry the charge [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** The **ions** (Na⁺ and Cl⁻) [1]
**(b)** In the dry crystals the ions are held in a lattice and cannot move [1]; in solution the ions are free
to move, so charge can flow and the lamp lights [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** Any two [1] [1]: sodium chloride is a **giant lattice of ions** and does not consist of molecules; it
contains **ions**, not atoms; the forces are **strong electrostatic forces**, not weak forces. **[2]**
**(b)** Marking points, up to 4:
- It has a giant ionic lattice [1].
- There are strong electrostatic forces of attraction between oppositely charged ions [1].
- There are many such bonds acting in all directions [1].
- A large amount of energy is needed to break them, so the melting point is high [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** The forces between oppositely charged ions are **strong electrostatic** forces [1]; there are a **very
large number** of these bonds throughout the lattice, and all must be overcome [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The lattice is continuous, so the formula only gives the **ratio of ions** [1].

**10.** Indicative content, one mark per point, up to 6:
- Magnesium oxide has a giant ionic lattice of oppositely charged ions [1].
- There are strong electrostatic forces of attraction between the ions, in all directions [1].
- Many strong bonds need to be broken, so a large amount of energy is needed [1], and the melting point is
  high [1].
- In the solid the ions are in fixed positions and cannot move, so charge cannot flow [1].
- When molten or dissolved the ions are free to move and carry charge, so it conducts [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* the strongest answers say what moves (ions), why it can (the lattice has broken down) and
what that allows (charge flows).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying "molecules" for an ionic compound.
- Giving "contains ions" as the reason for conduction, without "free to move".
- Forgetting "many" bonds and a "large amount of energy".
- Confusing the ions that carry charge in an ionic compound with the electrons that carry charge in a metal.
- Using only one piece of data when asked for two.
