These questions cover section 5.3 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: "Quartiles and percentiles". Revise with the [quartiles and percentiles study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-quartiles-and-percentiles/) or the [quartiles and percentiles revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-quartiles-and-percentiles-revision-notes/). Lists use the halves method; grouped data use targets n/4, n/2, 3n/4 and P × n/100. All data are invented.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Nine journey times, in minutes, are: 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 26, 30, 33, 41. Find the median, the quartiles and the interquartile range. **[3]**

**2.** The number of siblings of 23 pupils is recorded.

| Siblings | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 4 | 8 | 6 | 3 | 2 |

Find the median and the interquartile range. **[3]**

**3.** A grouped frequency distribution has n = 180. Write down the target cumulative frequencies for the lower quartile and for the 70th percentile. **[2]**

**4.** The 90th percentile of the masses of a group of adults is 62 kg. Explain what this means. **[1]**

**5.** In a cumulative frequency table with n = 120, the cumulative frequency at 30 is 44 and the class 30 ≤ x < 40 has frequency 36. Estimate the median. **[2]**

**6.** A cumulative percentage polygon for race times reads 72% at 48 seconds.

**(a)** What percentile is 48 seconds? **[1]**

**(b)** What percentage of runners took longer than 48 seconds? **[1]**

## Section B: longer questions

**7.** The times, t seconds, of 160 swimmers are recorded.

| Time, t s | 40 ≤ t < 45 | 45 ≤ t < 50 | 50 ≤ t < 55 | 55 ≤ t < 60 | 60 ≤ t < 70 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 12 | 30 | 54 | 40 | 24 |

**(a)** Form the cumulative frequency table. **[1]**

**(b)** Estimate the median time. **[2]**

**(c)** Estimate the interquartile range. **[3]**

**(d)** The fastest 20% of swimmers qualify for a final. Estimate the qualifying time. **[2]**

**8.** Using the data in question 7, a swimmer's time is 57 seconds.

**(a)** Estimate the percentile rank of this time. **[2]**

**(b)** Estimate the percentage of swimmers who were slower. **[1]**

**9.** Two groups of pupils sit the same reading test.

| | Q1 | Median | Q3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Group P | 31 | 44 | 52 |
| Group Q | 38 | 42 | 47 |

Compare the scores of the two groups. **[3]**

**10.** The lengths of 64 leaves, measured to the nearest centimetre, are grouped.

| Length (cm) | 10–14 | 15–19 | 20–24 | 25–29 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 6 | 20 | 28 | 10 |

**(a)** Write down the class boundaries. **[1]**

**(b)** Estimate the median length. **[2]**

**(c)** Estimate the 90th percentile. **[2]**

**11.** A cumulative percentage polygon for the masses, m kg, of parcels passes through (0, 0), (20, 15), (40, 45), (60, 80) and (80, 100).

**(a)** Estimate the median mass. **[1]**

**(b)** Estimate the interquartile range. **[2]**

**(c)** Parcels heavier than the 90th percentile need a special label. Estimate the mass above which labels are needed. **[1]**

**12.** A teacher estimates the median of a grouped distribution from a smooth cumulative frequency curve; a pupil uses linear interpolation in the table. Their answers differ slightly.

**(a)** Explain why the answers differ. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why neither answer is exact. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Median = 5th value = 24 [1]. Q1 = (15 + 18)/2 = 16.5 and Q3 = (30 + 33)/2 = 31.5 [1]. IQR = 15 minutes [1]. **[3]**

**2.** Cumulative frequencies 4, 12, 18, 21, 23. Median = 12th value = 1 [1]. Lower half is the first 11 values, Q1 = 6th value = 1; upper half is the 13th to 23rd, Q3 = 18th value = 2 [1]. IQR = 2 − 1 = 1 [1]. **[3]**

**3.** 180/4 = 45 [1]; 0.7 × 180 = 126 [1]. **[2]**

**4.** 90% of the adults have a mass of 62 kg or less [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Target 60, in the class 30 ≤ x < 40 [1]: 30 + (60 − 44)/36 × 10 = 34.4 [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** The 72nd percentile [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** 100 − 72 = 28% [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** t < 40: 0, t < 45: 12, t < 50: 42, t < 55: 96, t < 60: 136, t < 70: 160 [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Target 80, class 50 ≤ t < 55 [1]: 50 + (80 − 42)/54 × 5 = 53.5 s [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Q1: target 40, 45 + (40 − 12)/30 × 5 = 49.7 [1]. Q3: target 120, 55 + (120 − 96)/40 × 5 = 58.0 [1]. IQR = 58.0 − 49.7 = 8.3 s [1]. **[3]**

**(d)** Fastest 20% means the 20th percentile; target 0.2 × 160 = 32 [1]: 45 + (32 − 12)/30 × 5 = 48.3 s [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Here a low time is good, so the fastest 20% are at the bottom of the cumulative frequency, not the top.

**8. (a)** Cumulative frequency at 57 = 96 + (2/5) × 40 = 112 [1]; 112/160 × 100 = 70%, the 70th percentile [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** 30% [1]. **[1]**

**9.** Group P has the higher median (44 against 42), so it did slightly better on average [1]. Group Q's scores are more consistent: IQR 9 against 21 for Group P [1]. Group P's lower quartile is much lower (31 against 38), so it has more weak scores as well as a higher upper quartile [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** 9.5, 14.5, 19.5, 24.5, 29.5 [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Cumulative frequencies 6, 26, 54, 64; target 32, in 19.5–24.5 [1]: 19.5 + (32 − 26)/28 × 5 = 20.6 cm [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Target 0.9 × 64 = 57.6, in 24.5–29.5 [1]: 24.5 + (57.6 − 54)/10 × 5 = 26.3 cm [1]. **[2]**

**11. (a)** At 50%: 40 + (5/35) × 20 = 42.9 kg [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Q1 at 25%: 20 + (10/30) × 20 = 26.7; Q3 at 75%: 40 + (30/35) × 20 = 57.1 [1]. IQR = 57.1 − 26.7 = 30.5 kg [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** At 90%: 60 + (10/20) × 20 = 70 kg [1]. **[1]**

**12. (a)** Interpolation assumes values are spread evenly within the class (straight lines), but a smooth curve bends between the plotted points [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** The original values are not known, only how many fall in each class, so any value inside a class is an estimate [1]. **[1]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the wrong end for "top" or "fastest" percentages.
- Using class limits instead of boundaries for rounded data.
- Forgetting to subtract the cumulative frequency before the class.
- Giving a comparison without figures, or without a measure of spread.
- Including the median in both halves for an odd-sized list.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many decimal places should estimates have?**
Usually 3 significant figures, unless the question says otherwise. Keep more figures in your working.

**What if the target equals a cumulative frequency exactly?**
Then the estimate is the boundary at which that cumulative frequency is recorded.
