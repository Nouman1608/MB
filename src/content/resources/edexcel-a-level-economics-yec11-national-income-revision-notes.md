## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Circular flow of income | Model of income, spending and output flowing between households and firms, with withdrawals and injections |
| National income | The total income of an economy in a period; equal to the value of output and of spending on it |
| Income | A flow of money received per period |
| Wealth | A stock of assets valued at a point in time |
| Injection | Spending on domestic output that does not come from domestic households' current income: I, G, X |
| Withdrawal (leakage) | Income not passed on as spending on domestic output: S, T, M |
| Net injection | Injections greater than withdrawals: national income tends to rise |
| Net withdrawal | Withdrawals greater than injections: national income tends to fall |
| Equilibrium real national output | Output where AD = AS (equivalently, where W = J) |
| Multiplier | The ratio of the final change in national income to the initial change in an injection |
| MPC, MPS, MPT, MPM | Shares of a change in income that are consumed, saved, taxed and spent on imports |
| MPW | MPS + MPT + MPM |

## Item 1: circular flow and income versus wealth

- Households supply factor services; firms pay factor incomes; households spend on firms' output.
- **Output = income = expenditure**: three ways of measuring the same national income.
- Adding sectors adds pairs: financial (S and I), government (T and G), foreign (M and X).

| Income (flow) | Wealth (stock) |
|---|---|
| Wages, salaries, rent received, interest, dividends, profit | Property, shares, savings accounts, pension funds |
| Measured per period | Measured at a moment |
| Saving turns income into wealth | Wealth earns income, and can be run down to fund spending |

## Item 2: injections and withdrawals

```
J = I + G + X        W = S + T + M
J > W  →  net injection   →  output and income rise
J < W  →  net withdrawal  →  output and income fall
J = W  →  income stable (equilibrium)
```

**Why the flow settles.** Withdrawals rise with income; injections do not depend mainly on current domestic income. After a net injection, income rises until withdrawals have risen to match injections.

**Equilibrium income with a fixed withdrawal share.** If W = MPW × Y, then equilibrium is Y = J ÷ MPW.

## Item 3: equilibrium real output

- Equilibrium: AD = AS, so planned spending equals planned output.
- AD right: output up, price level up (less of a price rise where there is spare capacity).
- SRAS left: output down, price level up.
- LRAS right: output up, price level down.
- Two shifts at once: one effect is certain, the other depends on the sizes of the shifts.

## Item 4: the multiplier

**Formulae in the specification.**

```
multiplier = 1 / (1 − MPC)
multiplier = 1 / MPW,   MPW = MPS + MPT + MPM
change in national income = multiplier × change in injection
```

The two forms agree when MPC is the share of extra income spent on domestic output, because then MPC + MPW = 1.

**Effects of the propensities.**

| Change | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| MPC on domestic output rises | Larger |
| MPS rises (households save more of extra income) | Smaller |
| MPT rises (higher marginal tax rates) | Smaller |
| MPM rises (more spending on imports) | Smaller |

**The process in one line.** Injection → income for some → part withdrawn, the rest spent → income for others → … until total new withdrawals equal the original injection.

**Significance.**

- AD shifts by more than the initial change in G, I or X.
- Works downwards: a fall in an injection reduces income by a multiple.
- Open economies with a high MPM and economies with high tax rates have small multipliers.
- The effect on **real** output depends on spare capacity; near full capacity more of the effect is inflation.
- Time lags and uncertain propensities make the final size hard to predict.

## Exam traps

- **Saving is a withdrawal**, not an injection, even though banks may lend it out for investment.
- **Exports are an injection**; imports are a withdrawal.
- **Income and wealth confused:** a rise in house prices raises wealth, not income.
- **Wrong formula:** the multiplier is 1/(1 − MPC), not 1/MPC.
- **Real versus nominal:** a multiplier on AD does not guarantee the same rise in real output.
- **Equilibrium misunderstood:** J = W does not require S = I, T = G or X = M separately.
- **Missing chain:** "income rises" needs the link through spending and output.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the three injections and the three withdrawals.
2. House prices rise by 10%. Is this a change in income or in wealth?
3. Why does output equal income equal expenditure in the simple circular flow?
4. Injections total 600. Withdrawals are always 25% of national income. Find equilibrium national income.
5. What happens to national income when there is a net withdrawal, and why does it stop changing?
6. The MPC on domestic output is 0.6. Find the multiplier.
7. MPS = 0.15, MPT = 0.2, MPM = 0.25. Find the MPW and the multiplier.
8. Investment rises by 12 billion and the multiplier is 2.5. Find the change in national income.
9. With MPW = 0.3, what injection is needed to raise national income by 45?
10. Explain the effect of a rise in income tax rates on the size of the multiplier.
11. AD shifts right and SRAS shifts right at the same time. Which change is certain?
12. Why might a large multiplier produce only a small rise in real output?

### Answers

1. Injections: **investment, government expenditure, exports**. Withdrawals: **savings, taxation, imports**.
2. **Wealth**: the value of an asset has changed; no extra income has been received.
3. Every unit of spending on output is received by firms and paid out as factor incomes, so the three measure the same flow.
4. 0.25Y = 600, so **Y = 2400**.
5. National income **falls**. As it falls, withdrawals fall too, until they equal injections again.
6. 1 ÷ (1 − 0.6) = 1 ÷ 0.4 = **2.5**.
7. MPW = 0.15 + 0.2 + 0.25 = **0.6**; multiplier = 1 ÷ 0.6 = **1.67** (2 d.p.).
8. 2.5 × 12 = **30 billion**.
9. Multiplier = 1 ÷ 0.3 = 3.33; injection = 45 × 0.3 = **13.5**.
10. A higher tax rate raises the **MPT**, so more of each round of income is withdrawn, the MPW rises and the multiplier becomes **smaller**.
11. **Real output rises**; the change in the price level depends on the relative sizes of the shifts.
12. If the economy is **near full capacity**, the larger AD shift mainly raises the price level rather than real output.
