## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *loss leader*. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** extension strategies a business could use for a product in decline. **[2]**

**3.** Which **one** of the following describes a dog in the Boston Matrix? A: high market share, high market growth. B: high market share, low market growth. C: low market share, high market growth. D: low market share, low market growth. **[1]**

**4.** Lowenna Candles makes a candle for £5.20 and adds a 75% mark-up. Calculate the selling price. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a business might use sponsorship as a method of promotion. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of having a USP. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Tamsin's Tea Room sells 800 cream teas a month at £7.50. The owner plans to cut the price to £6.50 and expects sales to rise to 950 a month.

**(a)** Calculate the monthly revenue before and after the price change. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the change in revenue. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain **one** reason why profit might fall even though more cream teas are sold. **[2]**

**8.** Bryher Electronics has four products.

| Product | Market share | Market growth |
|---|---|---|
| Speaker W | 32% | 1% |
| Earbuds X | 28% | 15% |
| Smartwatch Y | 5% | 22% |
| Radio Z | 3% | −4% |

**(a)** Identify the Boston Matrix category of each product. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain how Bryher Electronics could use Speaker W to support Smartwatch Y. **[3]**

**9.** Haylow Honey sells jars for £4.00 each to a wholesaler. The wholesaler sells them to shops for £5.20. Shops sell them for £7.00. Each jar costs Haylow Honey £2.50 to produce.

**(a)** Calculate the profit Haylow Honey makes on each jar. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the amount the shops add to the price of each jar. **[1]**
**(c)** Analyse **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage to Haylow Honey of selling direct to customers online instead. **[4]**

**10.** Explain how the stage of the product life cycle can influence the price a business charges. **[3]**

**11.** Zenmoor Drinks makes a fizzy drink that has been on sale for 12 years. Sales have fallen for two years. Research shows teenagers now see it as old-fashioned, but many adults aged 30 to 50 remember it fondly and would buy it as an occasional treat. Analyse which extension strategy Zenmoor Drinks should use. **[6]**

**12.** Mylow Paddleboards is a new business that has designed a lightweight inflatable paddleboard. It has £8,000 to spend on promotion. Two large rivals sell similar boards at £450 and advertise on television. Its target market is 20 to 40 year olds living near the coast. Recommend a marketing mix for Mylow Paddleboards, justifying your choices. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** A product sold at a price below its cost [1] to attract customers who will then buy other, profitable products [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two of: updating packaging, adding more or different features, changing the target market, advertising, price reduction [1] [1]. **[2]**

**3.** **D** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** £5.20 × 1.75 [1] = **£9.10** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The brand is linked to a popular event or team [1], so it is seen by many people who follow it [1], improving brand awareness and image, which can increase sales [1]. **[3]**

**6.** It makes the product stand out from rivals [1], so customers are less likely to compare on price alone, allowing a higher price or stronger loyalty [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Before: 800 × £7.50 = **£6,000**; after: 950 × £6.50 = **£6,175** [1] [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** £6,175 − £6,000 = **+£175** a month [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Each cream tea now earns £1 less [1], and the extra 150 cream teas add ingredient and staff costs, which could be greater than the extra £175 of revenue [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** Speaker W: cash cow [1]. Earbuds X: star [1]. Smartwatch Y: question mark (problem child) [1]. Radio Z: dog [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Speaker W has a high share of a stable market, so it earns steady revenue with little promotion spending [1]. Bryher can use that cash [1] to fund promotion or development of Smartwatch Y, helping it gain share in its fast-growing market [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** £4.00 − £2.50 = **£1.50** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** £7.00 − £5.20 = **£1.80** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Advantage: Haylow Honey keeps more of the £7.00 selling price [1], so it earns more profit per jar, even after paying delivery costs [1]. Disadvantage: it must find and attract customers itself and pay for packing and postage [1], so sales may be lower than through the shops the wholesaler reaches [1]. **[4]**

**10.** At introduction a business may skim with a high price because the product is new and early buyers will pay more [1], or use penetration to win share quickly [1]. In maturity or decline, competition and falling sales usually push the price down, for example through competitive pricing or a price reduction [1]. **[3]**

**11.** The original target market has turned away from the drink [1], so advertising to teenagers or cutting the price is unlikely to solve the image problem [1]. Changing the target market to adults aged 30 to 50 suits the research, because they already like the drink [1]. Updating the packaging to a retro style could appeal to their fond memories [1], so the two strategies together could increase sales [1]. A price cut is less suitable, because a treat is not mainly bought on price and profit per can would fall [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Knowledge: the mix is price, product, promotion and place, which must fit the target market [1].
Application: Mylow is new with £8,000 for promotion [1]; rivals charge £450 and use television, which Mylow cannot afford [1].
Analysis: Product: promote the lightweight design as a USP, so customers see a reason to choose Mylow over larger brands [1]. Price: competitive, around £450, or slightly above if the USP justifies it, because customers can easily compare with rivals [1]. Promotion: social media videos and sponsorship of local paddleboard events, which reach 20 to 40 year olds near the coast within the budget [1].
Evaluation: Place: selling online, plus a few coastal water sports shops, reaches the target market without the cost of its own shops, although shops take a margin [1]; a premium price could limit sales against established brands, while a low price may suggest poor quality [1]. Justified overall judgement that the elements fit together, for example a competitive price, USP-led social media promotion and online sales, because together they target coastal 20 to 40 year olds within the budget [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* The strongest answers make every element fit the same target market and use the £8,000 budget and the rivals' £450 price.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping skimming and penetration.
- Classifying Boston Matrix products by sales instead of market share and growth.
- Forgetting that a price cut can raise revenue but lower profit.
- Choosing an extension strategy that does not address why sales are falling.
- Recommending television advertising for a business with a small budget.
- Writing about each P in isolation, with choices that clash.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I justify a marketing mix recommendation?**
Link each element to a fact in the case: the target market, the budget, the rivals or the product's features, and check that the four elements fit together.

**Do I need to calculate in marketing mix questions?**
Often, for example revenue before and after a price change, a cost-plus price, or the margin each business in a channel takes. Show each step.
