This guide covers section **3.4.2 Recruitment and selection of employees** of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225), part of Topic 4, Human resources. The specification asks you to understand **the need for recruitment**; the difference between **internal and external recruitment** and the benefits and drawbacks of each; the **stages in the recruitment and selection process**; and the benefits of an **effective process**, including **high productivity, high-quality output or customer service and staff retention**. All businesses and figures in this guide are invented.

For the whole topic, see the [Human Resources study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-human-resources/). Condensed notes are in the [revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-recruitment-and-selection-of-employees-revision-notes/) and questions are in the [practice set](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-recruitment-and-selection-of-employees-practice/).

## Why do businesses need to recruit?

**Recruitment** is the process of attracting suitable people to apply for a job. **Selection** is the process of choosing the best person from the applicants. Together they are how a business gets the workforce it needs.

Reasons for recruiting:

- **Replacing staff** who leave, retire or are dismissed.
- **Growth:** more customers or new shops need more staff.
- **New skills:** the business may need skills it does not have, for example in online marketing or a new technology.
- **Seasonal demand:** shops hire extra staff for festivals and holiday periods.
- **Reorganisation:** a new department or new products may need new roles.

Employees are a business's most important resource. The wrong choice is costly: an unsuitable employee may make mistakes, upset customers, need extra training or leave soon.

## Internal and external recruitment

**Internal recruitment** means filling a vacancy with someone who already works for the business, by promotion or transfer. **External recruitment** means appointing someone from outside the business.

### Internal recruitment

| Benefits | Drawbacks |
|---|---|
| Cheaper and quicker: no need for expensive advertising | Fewer applicants, so the best person may not be found |
| The business already knows the person's strengths and weaknesses | Creates another vacancy to fill |
| The person already knows the business, so needs less induction | No new ideas or skills from outside |
| Motivates staff: they see a chance of promotion | Rejected candidates may feel resentful and demotivated |

### External recruitment

| Benefits | Drawbacks |
|---|---|
| A wider choice of applicants, so a better chance of finding the best person | Expensive: advertising, interview time and possibly agency fees |
| New ideas, skills and experience come into the business | Slower: time needed to advertise and interview |
| Can fill a vacancy when nobody inside has the right skills | Greater risk, as the business knows less about the person |
| | Needs a longer induction, and existing staff may be upset at being passed over |

**Worked example 1: comparing the cost.** A hotel needs a head chef.

- Internal: a notice on the staff board and a short interview; cost $600.
- External: a newspaper and website advert costing $1,800, and 40 hours of manager time for reading applications and interviewing, with a manager's time valued at $25 an hour.

```
Management time = 40 x $25 = $1,000
External cost   = $1,800 + $1,000 = $2,800
Extra cost of external recruitment = $2,800 - $600 = $2,200
```

External recruitment costs $2,200 more. It could still be better if no internal candidate is suitable, or if the hotel wants a chef with new menu ideas.

Which method is better depends on the business. A small business with few staff may have no suitable internal candidates and must recruit externally. A large business with many levels often promotes from within for senior posts.

## The recruitment and selection process

Businesses usually follow these stages.

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Identify the vacancy | Decide that a post is needed, and whether it is new or a replacement |
| 2. Job description and person specification | The **job description** lists the duties, hours, pay and reporting lines. The **person specification** lists the qualifications, skills and experience needed |
| 3. Advertise | Place an advert, internally or externally, say in newspapers, websites, job centres or agencies |
| 4. Applications | Candidates send a CV and a letter or an application form |
| 5. Shortlist | The business compares applications with the person specification and chooses the best few |
| 6. Selection methods | Interviews, and possibly tests, presentations or work trials |
| 7. References and checks | The business contacts previous employers and checks qualifications |
| 8. Job offer | The successful candidate is offered the job and a contract |
| 9. Induction | The new employee is introduced to the business and the job |

**Selection methods** are the tools used to choose between shortlisted candidates:

- **Interviews:** allow face-to-face questions and show how a person communicates, but interviewers can be biased and candidates may perform well in an interview but not at work.
- **Tests:** such as aptitude tests, or typing, maths or practical tests. They measure particular skills fairly.
- **Presentations or work trials:** show how a person performs a real task.
- **Group exercises:** show how candidates work with others.

Using more than one method gives a better picture of each candidate.

**Worked example 2: funnel of applicants.** A shop advertises for a sales assistant and receives 80 applications. It shortlists 12, interviews them and hires 1.

```
Shortlisted as a percentage of applicants = 12 / 80 x 100 = 15%
Hired as a percentage of interviewed      = 1 / 12 x 100 = 8.3% (to one decimal place)
```

The process takes time at each stage, so a business needs to plan its timing well before the post must be filled.

## Benefits of an effective recruitment and selection process

An effective process finds the right person for the job. The specification names these benefits.

- **High productivity:** well-matched employees work faster and make fewer mistakes, so more output per worker.
- **High-quality output or customer service:** employees with the right skills and attitude produce better goods and look after customers well.
- **Staff retention:** people who fit the job and the business are more likely to stay, so the business saves the cost of recruiting and training replacements.

**Worked example 3: the cost of staff leaving.** A firm has 120 employees on average, and 18 leave in a year.

```
Staff turnover = 18 / 120 x 100 = 15%
```

Each replacement costs $4,500 in recruitment, selection and training.

```
Cost of replacing leavers = 18 x $4,500 = $81,000 a year
```

If better selection cut leavers to 12, turnover would be 12 / 120 x 100 = 10%, and the cost 12 x $4,500 = $54,000, a saving of $27,000 a year.

Poor recruitment has the opposite effect: low productivity, errors, unhappy customers and high turnover.

## Fairness and honesty in recruitment

Businesses are expected to treat applicants fairly. Adverts and interviews should not discriminate against people because of characteristics such as sex, race, religion or disability, and the job description should be honest. Laws about this differ between countries, so you should use the general principle in your answer, not a particular country's law.

## Worked exam-style answer

**Question (6 marks):** Explain why effective recruitment and selection is important for a hotel.

**Answer:** The hotel depends on staff to give guests a good experience. If it chooses receptionists with the right skills and attitude, guests are served quickly and politely, which improves reviews and repeat bookings. A good match between person and job also improves retention. If turnover falls from 18% to 10% of 150 staff, the hotel replaces 15 people instead of 27, and at $3,200 each saves $38,400 a year. A weak process does the opposite: staff who are unsuited leave quickly, guests are unhappy, and money is wasted on recruiting again.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing recruitment (attracting applicants) with selection (choosing among them).
- Saying that internal recruitment is always better, or always cheaper overall. It leaves another vacancy to fill.
- Listing the stages without explaining the purpose of each.
- Mixing up the job description (about the job) and the person specification (about the person).
- Forgetting that an effective process improves productivity, quality and retention.
- Writing about a wide choice of applicants without linking it to a better match for the post.

## Exam technique for this section

For **state** and **identify** questions, name the stage or method. For **explain** questions, give a benefit or drawback with a reason that fits the business: "external recruitment brings in new skills, which helps a business that wants to start selling online". For **calculate** questions, show each step, such as turnover or cost. For **evaluate** questions, such as whether to recruit internally or externally, weigh the cost, speed and quality of candidates, using the facts in the case, and give a reasoned judgement.
