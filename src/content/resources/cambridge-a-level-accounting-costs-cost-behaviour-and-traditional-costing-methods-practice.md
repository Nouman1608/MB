## Questions

**1.** Oakhallow Foods bakes loaves.

**(a)** Classify each cost as direct or indirect, and as fixed, variable, semi-variable or stepped: (i) flour in the loaves; (ii) the production manager's salary; (iii) one quality inspector per 2,000 loaves made weekly; (iv) oven gas. **[4]**
**(b)** The gas bill was 1,900 for 20,000 loaves and 2,650 for 35,000. Calculate the variable cost per loaf and the fixed part. **[2]**

**2.** Lowood Stores holds 80 units at 5.00. It receives 120 at 5.50, issues 100, receives 100 at 6.00, then issues 120. Using perpetual records, calculate closing inventory under:

**(a)** FIFO **[3]**
**(b)** AVCO **[3]**

**3.** Hexbeck Paints (periodic method) holds 200 units at 4.00, buys 300 at 4.50 and 500 at 5.00, and uses 700 units.

**(a)** Calculate closing inventory under FIFO and under AVCO. **[4]**
**(b)** State which method gives the higher profit and by how much. **[1]**

**4.** Kingsmoor Electronics assembles tablets.

**(a)** State two features of a JIT inventory system. **[2]**
**(b)** Its only supplier is overseas and often delivers late. Assess whether JIT suits Kingsmoor, giving two reasons. **[3]**

**5.** Ferndown Metalworks has production departments Press and Finishing and a service department, Maintenance. Rent is 24,000 (floor area) and machine depreciation is 18,000 (machine cost).

| | Press | Finishing | Maintenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Floor area (square metres) | 400 | 300 | 100 |
| Machine cost | 150,000 | 30,000 | 20,000 |
| Indirect wages (allocated) | 9,500 | 6,500 | 5,200 |

Maintenance is reapportioned 60:40 to Press and Finishing. Budgeted activity is 4,100 machine hours (Press) and 3,700 direct labour hours (Finishing).

**(a)** Apportion rent and depreciation and calculate each department's subtotal. **[4]**
**(b)** Reapportion Maintenance. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate an overhead absorption rate for each production department. **[2]**

**6.** Haverbeck Plastics budgeted overhead of 96,000 on 12,000 machine hours. Actual hours were 12,900 and actual overhead 100,500.

**(a)** Calculate the absorption rate, the overhead absorbed and the under or over absorption. **[3]**
**(b)** State the effect on profit. **[1]**
**(c)** Give one cause. **[1]**

**7.** Ullswick Design Studio absorbs overhead at 9.00 per direct labour hour and prices work to earn a margin of 20% of selling price.

**(a)** Job 17 used 36 consultant hours at 40, 20 designer hours at 30 and printing costing 240. Prepare a costing statement and calculate the selling price. **[5]**
**(b)** A run of 800 sketchbooks cost 960 materials, 480 labour and 160 overhead. Calculate the cost per sketchbook and name the costing method. **[2]**

**8.** Cottesley Garments sells at 30 with variable production cost 12. Fixed production overhead of 54,000 a quarter is absorbed on normal output of 9,000 units; fixed non-production overhead is 30,000. In quarter 1 it makes 9,000 units, sells 8,000 and has no opening inventory.

**(a)** Prepare the quarter 1 profit statement using absorption costing. **[3]**
**(b)** Prepare it using marginal costing. **[3]**
**(c)** Reconcile the two profits. **[2]**
**(d)** In quarter 2 it makes 9,000 and sells 9,500. Without recalculating, state which method gives the higher profit and why. **[2]**

**9.** Penbury Gifts sells at 25 with variable cost 15. Fixed costs are 36,000 a year and budgeted sales are 4,500 units.

**(a)** Calculate the contribution per unit and the break-even point in units. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the contribution to sales ratio and break-even revenue. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the margin of safety in units and as a percentage of budget. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the units needed for a profit of 18,000. **[1]**
**(e)** On a break-even chart, state what the gap between the revenue and total cost lines shows to the right of break-even. **[1]**

**10.** Wadsley Kitchens makes 6,000 handles a year: materials 5.00, labour 3.50 and variable overhead 1.50 each. Fixed overhead of 24,000 is charged to handles, but only 9,000 of it would be avoided if production stopped.

**(a)** A supplier quotes 11.00 a handle. Advise whether to buy. **[4]**
**(b)** A customer offers 1,500 cabinets at 21.00. The normal price is 28.00, variable cost 17.00, and capacity is spare. Calculate the extra profit. **[2]**
**(c)** State two non-financial factors to consider before accepting the offer. **[2]**

**11.** Yeldham Engineering makes two products with 7,000 machine hours available and fixed costs of 38,000.

| | Alpha | Beta |
|---|---|---|
| Selling price | 60 | 45 |
| Variable cost | 36 | 29 |
| Machine hours per unit | 4 | 2 |
| Maximum demand (units) | 1,500 | 2,000 |

**(a)** Show that machine hours are a limiting factor. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate contribution per machine hour for each product. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the production plan that maximises profit. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate total contribution and profit. **[2]**
**(e)** Recommend whether to follow the plan, giving two non-financial factors. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** (i) Direct material, variable [1]. (ii) Indirect labour, fixed [1]. (iii) Indirect labour, stepped [1]. (iv) Indirect expense, semi-variable [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Variable cost per loaf 750 / 15,000 = **0.05** [1]. Fixed part 1,900 − 1,000 = **900** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A classification tends to earn credit when both the traceability and the behaviour label are given.

**2. (a)** Issue 1: 80 × 5.00 + 20 × 5.50 = 510 [1]. Issue 2: 100 × 5.50 + 20 × 6.00 = 670 [1]. Closing 80 × 6.00 = **480** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Average after first receipt (400 + 660) / 200 = 5.30 [1]. After second receipt (530 + 600) / 200 = 5.65 [1]. Closing 80 × 5.65 = **452** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A running record showing units, value and average after each movement lets a slip be traced.

**3. (a)** FIFO: closing units are the newest, 300 × 5.00 = **1,500** [1][1]. AVCO: total cost 800 + 1,350 + 2,500 = 4,650 for 1,000 units, average 4.65 [1]; closing 300 × 4.65 = **1,395** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** **FIFO** by 105 (1,500 − 1,395), because it charges less to materials used when prices rise [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the direction of the profit difference as well as its size.

**4. (a)** Materials arrive close to when needed, in small frequent deliveries [1]. Little inventory is held, with reliable suppliers [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** A late delivery would stop production [1]. Late orders could lose customers [1]. JIT suits only if reliability improves or some safety stock is kept [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An assess question tends to reward a reasoned judgement, not a list of general points.

**5. (a)** Rent 24,000 / 800 = 30 per square metre: 12,000, 9,000, 3,000 [1]. Depreciation 9% of cost: 13,500, 2,700, 1,800 [1]. Subtotals: Press 35,000 [1]; Finishing 18,200 and Maintenance 10,000 [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Press 6,000 and Finishing 4,000 [1]. Totals: Press **41,000** and Finishing **22,200** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Press 41,000 / 4,100 = **10.00 per machine hour** [1]. Finishing 22,200 / 3,700 = **6.00 per labour hour** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each apportionment basis and check that the totals still equal 63,200.

**6. (a)** Rate 96,000 / 12,000 = **8.00** [1]. Absorbed 12,900 × 8.00 = **103,200** [1]. Over-absorbed by 103,200 − 100,500 = **2,700** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Profit is increased by 2,700 [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Activity was 900 hours above budget (7,200 more absorbed), outweighing 4,500 extra spending [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The label over- or under-absorbed and the effect on profit are separate points from the figure.

**7. (a)** Labour 36 × 40 + 20 × 30 = 2,040 [1][1]. Overhead 56 hours × 9.00 = 504 [1]. Total cost 2,040 + 240 + 504 = **2,784** [1]. Price 2,784 / 0.80 = **3,480** [1]. **[5]**
**(b)** 1,600 / 800 = **2.00** [1]. Batch costing [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A margin is a percentage of selling price, so dividing cost by 0.80 differs from adding 20% to cost; show the method.

**8. (a)** Sales 240,000 [1]. Cost of sales 8,000 × 18 = 144,000 [1]. Profit after 30,000 overhead **66,000** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Contribution 8,000 × 18 = 144,000 [1]. Fixed costs 84,000 [1]. Profit **60,000** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Marginal profit 60,000 + closing inventory 1,000 × 6 = **66,000** [1][1]. **[2]**
**(d)** **Marginal costing** gives the higher profit [1]: inventory falls by 500 units, so absorption costing charges 500 × 6 = 3,000 of earlier fixed overhead [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Linking the difference to the change in inventory units × fixed overhead per unit shows understanding.

**9. (a)** Contribution 25 − 15 = **10** [1]. Break-even 36,000 / 10 = **3,600 units** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** C/S ratio 10 / 25 = **40%** [1]. Break-even revenue 36,000 / 0.40 = **90,000** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 4,500 − 3,600 = **900 units** [1]; 900 / 4,500 = **20%** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** (36,000 + 18,000) / 10 = **5,400 units** [1]. **[1]**
**(e)** The profit at that level of output [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Label each answer as units, revenue or percentage.

**10. (a)** Variable cost 10.00 a handle [1]. Making costs 60,000 + avoidable fixed 9,000 = 69,000 [1]. Buying costs 66,000 [1]. **Buy**, saving 3,000, if non-financial factors allow [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Contribution 21.00 − 17.00 = 4.00 [1]. Extra profit 1,500 × 4.00 = **6,000** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Any two, for example: regular customers may ask for lower prices [1]; capacity may be needed for regular orders, or reputation for delivery could suffer [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Unavoidable fixed overhead is not relevant, so including the full 24,000 usually goes wrong here.

**11. (a)** Hours needed 6,000 + 4,000 = 10,000, above 7,000 [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Alpha (60 − 36) / 4 = **6** [1]. Beta (45 − 29) / 2 = **8** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Beta first: 2,000 units use 4,000 hours [1]. Alpha 3,000 / 4 = **750** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Contribution 32,000 + 18,000 = **50,000** [1]. Profit **12,000** [1]. **[2]**
**(e)** Follow the plan, as it maximises profit [1]. Half of Alpha demand goes unmet, risking those customers [1]. Subcontracting the shortfall might help, but a third party's quality is a concern [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Ranking by contribution per unit instead of per machine hour reverses the order here, so show the per-hour working.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving one label when a cost needs a traceability and a behaviour label.
- Not recalculating the average after each receipt in perpetual AVCO.
- Using budgeted hours instead of actual hours to find overhead absorbed.
- Stating under or over absorption without the effect on profit.
- Dividing fixed costs by selling price, not contribution per unit.
- Adding unavoidable fixed overhead to a make-or-buy comparison.
- Recommending without any non-financial factor.
