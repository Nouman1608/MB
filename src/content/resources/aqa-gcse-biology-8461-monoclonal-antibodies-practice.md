These questions cover section **4.3.2 Monoclonal antibodies** (4.3.2.1 and 4.3.2.2) of the AQA GCSE Biology
(8461) specification, for first teaching from 2016. The section is **Biology only** and **Higher tier only**,
so every question here is Higher tier only. Tests and treatments described in the questions are simplified
examples, and all data are invented for practice; you are expected to explain them from the information given.

Learn the content first in the [Monoclonal antibodies study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-monoclonal-antibodies/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-monoclonal-antibodies-revision-notes/).
The [Infection and response practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-infection-and-response-practice/)
include one further monoclonal antibody question on pregnancy tests.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** (Higher tier only)

**(a)** State what is meant by the term monoclonal antibodies. **[1]**
**(b)** Complete the sentence: monoclonal antibodies are specific to one binding site on one protein ______. **[1]**

**2.** (Higher tier only) These steps in producing monoclonal antibodies are in the wrong order.

- A -- a single hybridoma cell is cloned
- B -- mouse lymphocytes are stimulated to make a particular antibody
- C -- the antibody is collected and purified
- D -- lymphocytes are combined with tumour cells

Write the letters in the correct order. **[2]**

**3.** (Higher tier only)

**(a)** Explain why lymphocytes are combined with tumour cells. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a single hybridoma cell is cloned. **[2]**

**4.** (Higher tier only) Researchers use monoclonal antibodies to find where a particular protein is located in
a tissue. Name the substance attached to the antibodies, and explain why it is used. **[2]**

**5.** (Higher tier only) Give **two** disadvantages of using monoclonal antibodies. **[2]**

### Section B: longer and structured questions

**6.** (Higher tier only) A hospital laboratory measures the concentration of a hormone in blood. Monoclonal
antibodies specific to the hormone are attached to a marker. The more hormone that binds, the higher the colour
reading. The lab first tests samples of known concentration.

| Hormone concentration (arbitrary units) | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Colour reading | 0 | 12 | 24 | 36 | 48 |

**(a)** Explain why the test measures only this hormone and not other chemicals in the blood. **[2]**
**(b)** A patient's sample gives a colour reading of 30. Use the table to find the hormone concentration. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest why the lab tests each patient's sample three times. **[1]**

**7.** (Higher tier only) A grower uses a testing kit to check whether tomato plants are infected with tobacco
mosaic virus (TMV). The kit contains monoclonal antibodies that bind to an antigen on TMV. A coloured band
appears when the antibodies bind.

**(a)** Explain why a coloured band appears when sap from an infected plant is added. **[2]**
**(b)** A rose leaf with black spot is tested with the same kit. Explain why no band appears. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest **one** advantage of using the kit rather than sending the plant to a laboratory. **[1]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) Read the information.

*Drug P kills cells that are dividing. When given on its own, it travels in the blood to all body cells. It
damages cancer cells, but also healthy cells that divide often, such as cells in hair roots and the lining of
the gut. In a new treatment, drug P is attached to a monoclonal antibody that binds to an antigen found only on
the surface of one type of cancer cell.*

**(a)** Use the information to explain why patients given drug P on its own may lose their hair. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the new treatment is expected to cause fewer of these side effects. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest why the new treatment would not work for a patient whose cancer cells do not have the antigen. **[1]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) A test strip uses mobile antibodies attached to coloured beads, a test line and a
control line, in the same way as a pregnancy test. Three strips are used on three samples.

| Strip | Control line | Test line |
|---|---|---|
| X | Coloured | Coloured |
| Y | Coloured | No colour |
| Z | No colour | Coloured |

**(a)** Give the conclusion for each strip. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the mobile antibodies must be attached to coloured beads. **[1]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) A culture starts with one hybridoma cell. The cells divide once every 12 hours.

**(a)** Calculate the number of cells after 5 days, assuming every cell divides on time. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why all the antibodies made by this culture are identical. **[1]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) A scientist wants to find which cells in a sample of tissue contain a particular
protein. Describe how she could use monoclonal antibodies to do this. **[3]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) A hospital is deciding whether to offer patients a new treatment for a cancer: a
monoclonal antibody bound to a radioactive substance. The current treatment directs radiation at the body from
outside, and it damages some healthy tissue between the skin and the tumour. In early trials (invented
information), the new treatment caused less damage to healthy tissue, but some patients had side effects that
had not been predicted.

Evaluate the use of the new treatment. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Identical antibodies produced from a single clone of cells [1]
**(b)** Antigen [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Antibodies that fight one disease" does not earn (a); the mark needs "single clone of cells".

**2.** B, D [1]; A, C [1]
*Examiner insight:* One mark for the first pair in the right order and one for the second; D must come before A because the hybridoma must exist before it is cloned.

**3. (a)** Lymphocytes make the antibody but do not keep dividing; tumour cells divide repeatedly [1]; the hybridoma cell formed can both divide and make the antibody [1]
**(b)** So that all the cells in the clone are identical [1]; and they all produce the same antibody, specific to the same antigen [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (a), naming only one parent cell's property earns one mark; the second needs the combined property.

**4.** A fluorescent dye [1]; it glows, so it shows where the antibody has bound to the specific protein [1]

**5.** They create more side effects than expected [1]; their production uses mice, which some people object to on ethical grounds [1]
*Examiner insight:* "They are not yet as widely used as hoped" describes a consequence rather than a disadvantage; credit it only if linked to a reason such as side effects.

**6. (a)** The monoclonal antibodies are specific to one binding site on one antigen [1]; only the hormone has a shape that fits, so the antibodies bind only to the hormone and not to other chemicals [1]
**(b)** **25** arbitrary units (the reading rises by 1.2 for each unit: 30 ÷ 1.2 = 25) [1]
**(c)** To check the results are repeatable, or to identify an anomalous reading and calculate a mean [1]

**7. (a)** The antibodies bind to the TMV antigen in the sap [1]; binding produces the coloured band, showing TMV is present [1]
**(b)** Black spot is caused by a fungus, which does not have the TMV antigen [1]; the antibodies are specific to the TMV antigen, so they do not bind and no band forms [1]
**(c)** Any one: faster result; can be used on site by the grower; plants need not be sent away [1]

**8. (a)** Hair root cells divide often [1]; drug P reaches them in the blood and kills them, so hair is not replaced [1]
**(b)** The antibody binds only to the antigen on the cancer cells [1]; so drug P is delivered to the cancer cells [1]; and fewer healthy dividing cells (such as hair roots and gut lining) are exposed to the drug [1]
**(c)** The antibody would have nothing to bind to, so the drug would not be delivered to the cancer cells [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), "it only kills cancer cells" without mentioning binding to an antigen earns one mark at most.

**9. (a)** X: positive, the substance is present (test working) [1]; Y: negative, the substance is not present (test working) [1]; Z: the test has not worked, so the result cannot be trusted [1]
**(b)** So that binding at a line can be seen as a colour [1]
*Examiner insight:* Strip Z is the trap: a test line without a control line is not a positive result.

**10. (a)** 5 days = 120 hours, so 120 ÷ 12 = 10 divisions [1]; 2¹⁰ = **1024 cells** [1]
**(b)** All the cells are clones of one hybridoma cell, so they all make the same antibody [1]
*Examiner insight:* 2 × 10 = 20 cells treats doubling as adding; each division doubles the number.

**11.** Produce monoclonal antibodies specific to the protein [1]; attach a fluorescent dye to the antibodies and add them to the tissue [1]; the antibodies bind only to the protein, so the cells that glow under the microscope contain it [1]

**12.** One mark for each point:
- the antibody binds only to antigens on the cancer cells [1]
- so the radioactive substance is delivered to the cancer cells [1]
- so less healthy tissue is damaged than with radiation from outside, as the trial information shows [1]
- but monoclonal antibodies create more side effects than expected, as some trial patients showed [1]
- the trials are early, so more testing is needed to check safety and the best dose [1]
- a justified conclusion, for example: offer it within further trials, because the reduced tissue damage is promising but the unpredicted side effects must be understood first [1]
*Examiner insight:* The conclusion must follow from the points made; "it is better" with no reason does not earn the last mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing the tumour cell as injected into the mouse.
- Forgetting the word **hybridoma**, or the **single** cell that is cloned.
- Saying the antibody kills cancer cells itself.
- Reading a test line without checking the control line.
- Treating cell doubling as adding rather than multiplying by 2 each time.
- Evaluations that ignore the information given in the question.
