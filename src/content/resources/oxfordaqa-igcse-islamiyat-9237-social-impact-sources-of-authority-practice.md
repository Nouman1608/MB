## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for explaining it. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

**1.** A trust: something placed in a person's care which he must protect and return [1].

**2.** Ghibah [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: safety from harm; respect for privacy; sharing food or gifts; help in time of need; consideration over noise or space; visiting when ill.

**4. (a)** Wishing that another person would lose a blessing [1].

**(b)** One reason identified [1] and explained [2].

- **It harms relationships** [1]: envy leads to hatred and a wish to hurt another person [1], which breaks up friendships and families [1].
- **It questions Allah's wisdom** [1]: the Qur'an says not to wish for what Allah has given others (4:32) [1], because Allah gives as He wills [1].
- **It harms the envier** [1]: a jealous person has no peace of mind [1], and may lie or plot to cause harm [1].
- **It can lead to serious wrong** [1]: the first murder, of one son of Adam by his brother, arose from envy [1], showing how far it can lead [1].

**5.** For each of two ways: way identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Looking after property** [1]: caring for a neighbour's key, parcel or house while they are away and returning it unharmed [1].
- **Keeping secrets** [1]: not repeating private matters learned as a neighbour [1].
- **Honest dealings** [1]: returning borrowed items and being fair in any agreement [1].
- **Not exploiting access** [1]: not misusing the knowledge of when a neighbour is away [1].

**6.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **It denies equality** [1]: it makes a person look down on others, while taqwa is the true measure (49:13) [1].
- **It blocks acceptance of truth** [1]: the Hadith defines arrogance as rejecting the truth and looking down on people [1].
- **It leads to injustice** [1]: the arrogant person may treat the poor with contempt [1].
- **It follows Iblis** [1]: Iblis refused to bow because he thought himself better than Adam [1].

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **It harms the person absent** [1]: he cannot defend himself against what is said [1].
- **It breaks trust** [1]: people learn that private matters are not safe with the speaker [1].
- **It causes quarrels** [1]: gossip spreads suspicion and enmity in a family or community [1].
- **It shows lack of mercy** [1]: it exposes the faults that a believer should hide [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- Qur'an 49:12: "Would one of you like to eat the flesh of his brother when dead? You would detest it" [2].
- The Hadith in Muslim: backbiting is mentioning your brother in a way he would dislike; if what you say is false, it is slander [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Ways (AO1):

- **Safety and security** [1]: people feel safe in their homes and streets [1].
- **Mutual help** [1]: neighbours share food and help the sick and the elderly [1].
- **Trust between families** [1]: people can rely on each other for children, property and privacy [1].
- **A good example** [1]: kindness to neighbours shows the character of Islam to others [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- Qur'an 4:36: do good to the near neighbour and the neighbour farther away [2].
- The Hadith that Jibril kept advising the Prophet (pbuh) about the neighbour until he thought they would be made heirs [2].
- Bukhari 6016: the person whose neighbour is not safe from his evil does not believe [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the Hadith denies belief to the person who harms his neighbour, using strong language; faith is shown in behaviour; prayer and fasting that do not change behaviour are incomplete; Jibril's repeated advice shows the neighbour's high rights.
- **Against the statement:** prayer, fasting, Zakah and Hajj are pillars; they are direct commands of worship; neighbours' rights are an important duty but are not one of the Five Pillars; a person who neglects worship neglects his relationship with Allah.
- **Balanced view:** worship and conduct support each other; prayer is meant to prevent wrongdoing (Qur'an 29:45), and kindness to neighbours shows that worship has had an effect.

*Use of the passage*

- "By Allah, he does not believe!" shows the seriousness of harming a neighbour.
- "Neighbour does not feel safe" shows that safety is the test.
- "From his evil" includes harm by words and neglect as well as by hands.
- Credit candidates who explain that the Hadith speaks of perfect faith.

Level 4 answers use the passage directly, give reasoning on both sides, and reach a reasoned judgement.
