Condensed recall for section 6.1 (Graphical representation of data) of the 4MA1 specification. Pictograms, bar charts, pie charts, two-way tables, tabulation and interpretation are needed on both tiers; histograms with unequal class widths and cumulative frequency diagrams are **Higher tier only**. Worked examples are in the [Graphical representation study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-graphical-representation-of-data-edexcel-igcse-maths/); exam-style questions are in the [Graphical representation practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-graphical-representation-of-data-edexcel-igcse-maths-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Frequency | How many times a value or class occurs |
| Key | States what one pictogram symbol represents |
| Dual bar chart | Two bars per category, one for each group |
| Sector | A "slice" of a pie chart |
| Two-way table | A table showing two categories at once, with totals |
| Class interval | A group of values in a grouped frequency table |
| Continuous data | Measured data that can take any value in a range |
| Frequency density | Frequency ÷ class width (Higher) |
| Cumulative frequency | Running total of frequencies (Higher) |
| Upper class boundary | The top value of a class (Higher, for plotting) |

## Pictograms

- Always include a key.
- Part symbols for part amounts: with 1 symbol = 8, ½ = 4 and ¼ = 2.
- Frequency = number of symbols × key value. Symbols = frequency ÷ key value.

## Bar charts

- Equal widths, equal gaps, frequency on the vertical axis.
- Work out the axis scale before reading a bar.
- Dual bar chart: compare each category between the two groups, with numbers.

## Pie charts

```
degrees per item = 360 ÷ total
angle = frequency × degrees per item
frequency = angle ÷ degrees per item
total = 360 ÷ (angle ÷ frequency)
```

- Angles must add to 360°; frequencies must add to the total.
- Two pie charts with different totals: compare **proportions** from the angles, but compare **numbers** only after converting each sector with its own total.

| Total | Degrees per item |
|---|---|
| 36 | 10° |
| 40 | 9° |
| 60 | 6° |
| 90 | 4° |
| 120 | 3° |
| 180 | 2° |

## Two-way tables

- Each row adds to its row total; each column adds to its column total; the grand total is in the corner.
- Fill any cell that has two known values in its row or column, then repeat.
- Check: the totals row adds to the grand total.

## Tabulation

- Tally, then count. Check the frequencies sum to the number of data values.
- Continuous classes as inequalities: 10 < t ≤ 20 includes 20, excludes 10.
- No overlaps, no gaps.

## Higher tier only: histograms

```
frequency density = frequency ÷ class width
frequency = frequency density × class width
```

- Bars touch (no gaps), width = class width, height = frequency density.
- Area represents frequency.
- Part of a class: frequency ≈ width of the part × frequency density.
- Finding a missing scale: use a bar whose frequency is known. If a bar of width 10 represents 24, its density is 2.4.

## Higher tier only: cumulative frequency diagrams

- Running totals in a table.
- Plot at **upper class boundaries**, plus the starting point (lowest boundary, 0).
- Join with a smooth curve or straight segments.
- Number **below** a value: read the cumulative frequency at that value.
- Number **above** a value: total − cumulative frequency at that value.

Mini example: CF 44 at 30 and 74 at 40 (total 100). With straight segments, at 34 the CF is about 44 + 0.4 × 30 = 56, so about 100 − 56 = 44 values are above 34.

## Exam traps

- Pictogram key ignored or misread.
- Angles of two pie charts compared as numbers.
- 1° assumed to be 1 item when the total is not 360.
- Boundary values put in the wrong class.
- Histogram drawn with frequency instead of frequency density.
- Adding frequency densities to get a total.
- Cumulative frequency plotted at midpoints.
- "More than" answers given as the cumulative frequency itself.

## Self-test

1. One symbol represents 6 items. How many items do 3½ symbols show?
2. A survey of 72 people is shown in a pie chart. What angle represents one person?
3. A sector of 120° represents 40 people. What is the total?
4. In a two-way table, the boys' row reads 12, ?, 9 with total 30. Find the missing value.
5. Which class contains 15 in the classes 10 < x ≤ 15 and 15 < x ≤ 20?
6. Why must classes not overlap?
7. (Higher) Frequency 18 in a class of width 6. Find the frequency density.
8. (Higher) Frequency density 0.8 for a class of width 25. Find the frequency.
9. (Higher) A bar of density 3 covers 20 < x ≤ 30. Estimate how many values lie between 24 and 30.
10. (Higher) Where do you plot cumulative frequencies?
11. (Higher) Total 80; CF at 50 is 62. How many values exceed 50?
12. Which diagram shows proportions of a whole most directly?

### Answers

1. 21.
2. 360 ÷ 72 = 5°.
3. 120 ÷ 40 = 3° per person, so 360 ÷ 3 = 120 people.
4. 30 − 12 − 9 = 9.
5. 10 < x ≤ 15.
6. So that every value belongs to exactly one class.
7. 3.
8. 20.
9. 6 × 3 = 18.
10. At the upper class boundaries.
11. 80 − 62 = 18.
12. A pie chart.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which diagrams are on the Foundation tier for 4MA1 section 6.1?**
Pictograms, bar charts, pie charts and two-way tables, together with tabulating data and interpreting diagrams. Histograms and cumulative frequency diagrams are Higher tier only.

**How do you find a frequency from a histogram?**
Multiply the frequency density (bar height) by the class width (bar width). For part of a class, multiply by the width of that part.
