These notes condense section **3.7.1 Newton's gravitational law** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification: "gravity as a universal attractive force acting between all matter" and the "force between point masses", F = Gm₁m₂/r². Section 3.7 is International A-level only.

For explanations and worked examples, read the [Newton's gravitational law study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-newtons-gravitational-law/). Test yourself with the [Newton's gravitational law practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-newtons-gravitational-law-practice/).

Data: G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²; Earth mass 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg, radius 6.37 × 10⁶ m; Moon mass 7.35 × 10²² kg; Earth-Moon distance 3.84 × 10⁸ m.

## Core recall

- Every mass attracts every other mass. The force is always attractive.
- F = Gm₁m₂/r² for point masses; r is the distance between centres.
- A uniform sphere acts on outside masses as if its mass were at its centre.
- The two forces in the pair are equal in size and opposite in direction (Newton's third law).
- F ∝ m₁m₂ and F ∝ 1/r².
- G is very small, so gravity between everyday objects is negligible.
- Gravitational forces are vectors and add as vectors.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Point mass | An object whose size is negligible compared with the distances involved |
| Gravitational constant G | The constant in Newton's law, 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻², the same everywhere |
| Inverse square law | A quantity proportional to 1/r²; doubling r gives one quarter |
| Separation r | Centre-to-centre distance between two masses |
| Resultant force | The vector sum of all forces acting on an object |
| Weight | The gravitational force on an object due to a planet, W = mg near the surface |

## Equation and unit

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Law | F = Gm₁m₂/r² |
| Rearranged for G | G = Fr²/(m₁m₂) |
| Unit of G | N m² kg⁻² (base units kg⁻¹ m³ s⁻²) |
| Check at the Earth's surface | GMm/R² for 1 kg = 9.81 N |

## Scaling table

| Change | Factor on F |
|---|---|
| One mass doubled | × 2 |
| Both masses doubled | × 4 |
| r doubled | × 1/4 |
| r tripled | × 1/9 |
| r halved | × 4 |
| Both masses doubled and r doubled | × 1 |
| Planet with mass k × Earth and radius n × Earth, surface force | × k/n² |

## Method: resultant force from two bodies

1. Calculate each force separately with F = Gm₁m₂/r², using the correct centre-to-centre distance for each.
2. Mark the direction of each force: towards the body causing it.
3. On a line, subtract opposite forces; otherwise add vectors by components or a scale diagram.
4. State the size and direction of the resultant.

## Testing for an inverse square law

- F × r² constant within uncertainty.
- Tripling r divides F by 9.
- Graph of F against 1/r²: straight line through the origin, gradient Gm₁m₂.
- Graph of log F against log r: straight line, gradient −2.

## Quick reminders with numbers

- Earth-Moon force: 2.0 × 10²⁰ N, on each body.
- Two 70 kg people 1.0 m apart: 3.3 × 10⁻⁷ N, about two billion times smaller than one person's weight.
- A 1500 kg spacecraft 3.00 × 10⁸ m from the Earth (0.84 × 10⁸ m from the Moon): 6.64 N towards the Earth, 1.04 N towards the Moon, resultant 5.6 N towards the Earth.

## Exam traps

- Altitude is not r: add the planet's radius.
- Square the whole of r, including the power of ten.
- The small mass pulls the large one just as hard; it is the accelerations that differ.
- "Inversely proportional to the distance" is wrong: it is the square of the distance.
- Do not mix G and g.
- When two bodies pull in opposite directions, subtract.
- Give the direction as well as the size when a resultant is asked for.

## Self-test

1. State Newton's law of gravitation in words.
2. Show that the unit of G is N m² kg⁻².
3. Find the force between two 5.0 kg spheres whose centres are 0.10 m apart.
4. The separation of two masses is quadrupled. By what factor does the force change?
5. Both masses are tripled and the separation is tripled. What happens to the force?
6. A satellite is 400 km above the Earth's surface. What value of r do you use?
7. The Earth pulls on a 60 kg person with 589 N. What force does the person exert on the Earth?
8. Why do you not notice the gravitational pull of a nearby building?
9. A planet has 3 times the Earth's mass and twice its radius. Compare the surface force on a given mass with that on Earth.
10. Two forces act on a probe along a line: 8.2 N towards a planet and 3.1 N towards its moon, in the opposite direction. Find the resultant.
11. What is the gradient of a graph of log F against log r for gravity?
12. Calculate the force between the Earth and the Moon.

### Answers

1. Two point masses attract each other with a force **proportional to the product of their masses** and **inversely proportional to the square of their separation**.
2. G = Fr²/(m₁m₂), so the unit is **N × m² / kg² = N m² kg⁻²**.
3. F = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.0 × 5.0/0.10² = **1.7 × 10⁻⁷ N**.
4. **1/16**.
5. Factor 9/9: **unchanged**.
6. r = 6.37 × 10⁶ + 4.0 × 10⁵ = **6.77 × 10⁶ m**.
7. **589 N**, directed towards the person.
8. G is so small that the force is **negligible** compared with the Earth's pull on you.
9. 3/2² = **0.75 times** the force on Earth.
10. **5.1 N towards the planet**.
11. **−2**.
12. F = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.97 × 10²⁴ × 7.35 × 10²²/(3.84 × 10⁸)² = **2.0 × 10²⁰ N**.
