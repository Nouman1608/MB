Language in Action is the non-exam assessment (NEA) of AQA A-level English Language (7702), worth **100
marks and 20% of the A-level**. You produce a **language investigation of 2,000 words (excluding data)**
and a **piece of original writing with a commentary (750 words each)**, about 3,500 words in total, marked
by your teacher and moderated by AQA. Methods of language analysis (the language levels) underpin both.
For the full explanation, use the
[Language in Action study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-english-language-nea-language-in-action/).

## NEA at a glance

| Part (7702, 4.3) | Length | Marks | AOs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Language Investigation (3.3.1) | 2,000 words, excluding data | 50 | AO1 15, AO2 15, AO3 20 |
| Original Writing (3.3.2) | 750 words | 25 | AO5 |
| Commentary (3.3.2) | 750 words | 25 | AO1–AO5 |
| **Total** | **about 3,500 words** | **100** | Scaled ×1 to 100 of the 500 A-level marks |

The NEA is A-level only; the AS (7701) has no coursework.

## The assessment objectives

| AO | Wording | Share of A-level from NEA |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 | Apply appropriate methods of language analysis, using associated terminology and coherent written expression | 4% |
| AO2 | Demonstrate critical understanding of concepts and issues relevant to language use | 4% |
| AO3 | Analyse and evaluate how contextual factors and language features are associated with the construction of meaning | 5% |
| AO4 | Explore connections across texts, informed by linguistic concepts and methods | 1% |
| AO5 | Demonstrate expertise and creativity in the use of English to communicate in different ways | 6% |

AO3 carries the most marks in the investigation (20 of 50), and AO5 is the whole of the original-writing
mark.

## 3.3.1 Language Investigation

### Possible areas (the specification's examples)

Representations of individuals, social groups or nationalities; regional dialect; gendered talk; language
of new communication technologies; children's language use; norms and variations; language of the media;
code switching and mixing; language of occupations or pastimes; historical change. You may also choose
other areas your teacher agrees will yield interesting questions.

### Four kinds of question

| Type | Guiding question |
|---|---|
| Genre-based | What are the distinctive features of this type of language use? |
| Function/use-based | What is the language used to do? |
| Attitudes-based | How do people feel about this language? |
| User-based | Who uses this type of language? |

### Data types

Spoken, written, multimodal, word lists, attitudes to language, uses of language, views about language.
You should **collect your own data** and select your own approach, in consultation with your teacher.

### Required sections

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| Introduction | Reasons for the focus; aims |
| Methodology | Evaluative account of how data was collected and organised; approaches to analysis |
| Analysis | Interpretation of findings against the aims; concepts and issues; contextual influences |
| Conclusion | Findings interpreted in relation to the aims |
| References | All paper and web sources |
| Appendices | Clean copies of data; evidence for quantitative approaches |

### What the top level looks like (Level 5)

- **AO1 (13–15):** apply methods and terminology identifying patterns and complexities; guide the reader
  with controlled, accurate expression.
- **AO2 (13–15):** a synthesised, conceptualised and individual overview; use data to challenge
  viewpoints; evaluate methodology and explore alternative approaches; detailed bibliography.
- **AO3 (17–20):** evaluate how language is influenced by different contexts; explore data in light of
  wider social and cultural contexts; reflective and **cautious** conclusions.

## 3.3.2 Original Writing

- Choose one of three areas: **The Power of Persuasion**, **The Power of Storytelling**, **The Power of
  Information**.
- Study a range of style models, then select and **analyse one style model in detail**, chosen with your
  teacher.
- Folder contents: the original writing, an **annotated style model**, a **reflective commentary**, and
  references.

| Area | Example forms from the specification |
|---|---|
| Persuasion | Investigative journalism; a speech on a controversial topic; a letter to an MP |
| Storytelling | A short story; an extract from a biography; a dramatic monologue |
| Information | Travel journalism; a blog on social issues; a piece of local history |

### Original writing top level (AO5, 21–25)

Use form creatively and innovatively; manipulate register skilfully at all levels; position the audience
skilfully; write accurately; guide the reader through a coherent, cohesive text.

### Commentary top level (21–25)

Evaluate connections across your writing and style model (AO4); evaluate language and representations in
the two contexts (AO3); show genre as a "dynamic process" (AO2); apply language levels in an integrated way
(AO1); write accurately with a coherent structure (AO5). A commentary that discusses only one text is
capped at 3 marks for AO4.

## 3.3.3 Methods of language analysis

| Level | Covers |
|---|---|
| Phonetics, phonology and prosodics | Speech sounds and effects |
| Graphology | Visual design and appearance |
| Lexis and semantics | Vocabulary, including social and historical variation |
| Grammar, including morphology | Patterns at sentence, clause, phrase and word level |
| Pragmatics | Contextual aspects of language use |
| Discourse | Extended communication in different genres, modes and contexts |

## Worked example: turning a topic into an investigation

- **Topic (too broad):** "Language and gender."
- **Focus:** "How do male and female presenters on two football podcasts use hedges and boosters?"
- **Data:** 20 minutes transcribed from each podcast, plus a word count of hedges and boosters per 1,000
  words.
- **Methods:** grammar (modal verbs, adverbs), pragmatics (politeness), discourse (turn-taking).
- **Concepts (AO2):** deficit, dominance and difference debates; later critiques of binary gender models.
- **Context (AO3):** the podcast format, audience, expertise roles.
- **Cautious conclusion:** findings from two podcasts cannot be generalised to all speakers.

## Common mistakes / traps

- **Too broad a focus**, leading to thin analysis.
- **Data you did not collect** yourself, without your teacher's agreement.
- **Methodology that only describes** rather than evaluates.
- **Overclaiming**: the top band asks for "reflective and cautious conclusions".
- **Theory dumping**: AO2 rewards connecting theories with data, not listing them.
- **A commentary about one text only**, with no connection to the style model.
- **Missing the folder elements**: no annotated style model or references.
- **Forgetting authentication**: you sign the Candidate record form, and teachers must be able to
  authenticate the work.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long is the AQA English Language NEA?**
About 3,500 words: a 2,000-word investigation (data excluded) and 750 words each for the original writing
and commentary.

**Can I choose any topic for my language investigation?**
The specification gives examples but says the list is "neither definitive nor prescriptive". Any area your
supervising teacher agrees yields interesting questions about language in use can be chosen.

**Does my data count towards the word limit?**
No. The 2,000 words exclude data; clean copies of the data go in the appendices.

**What is a style model?**
A published text in the genre you are writing, which you analyse in detail and use to inform your own
writing; it is submitted annotated in your folder.
