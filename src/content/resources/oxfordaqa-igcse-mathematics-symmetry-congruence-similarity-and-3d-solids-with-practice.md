## Questions

**1.** State how many mirror lines each shape has, and give its order of rotation symmetry.

**(a)** A kite that is not a rhombus **[2]**
**(b)** A regular octagon **[2]**

**2.** Triangle PQR has vertices P(1, 2), Q(7, 2) and R(4, 6).

**(a)** Give the equation of the mirror line of triangle PQR. **[2]**
**(b)** The point S is added below PQ so that PRQS is a rhombus. Find the coordinates of S. **[2]**
**(c)** Give the number of mirror lines of PRQS and its order of rotation symmetry. **[2]**

**3.** Triangles ABC and DEF are congruent, and the letters correspond in alphabetical order. AB = 9.5 cm, BC = 7 cm, angle A = 64° and angle B = 53°. Find DE, EF and angle F. **[3]**

**4.** (Extension only)

**(a)** Triangle 1 has sides of 7 cm and 9 cm with an angle of 40° between them. Triangle 2 has sides of 9 cm and 7 cm with an angle of 40° between them. Are the triangles congruent? Give the condition. **[2]**
**(b)** Triangle 3 and triangle 4 each have angles of 50°, 60° and 70°. Does this prove they are congruent? Give a reason. **[1]**
**(c)** In triangle ABC, angle B = 90°, AC = 13 cm and AB = 5 cm. In triangle DEF, angle E = 90°, DF = 13 cm and DE = 5 cm. Write a congruence statement for the triangles and give the condition. **[2]**

**5.** (Extension only) In triangle ABC, AB = AC. M is the midpoint of BC. Prove that angle AMB = 90°. **[4]**

**6.** Triangle ABC has AB = 8 cm, BC = 12 cm and CA = 10 cm. Triangle PQR is similar to triangle ABC, with PQ = 6 cm matching AB.

**(a)** Find QR. **[2]**
**(b)** Find PR and the perimeter of triangle PQR. **[2]**

**7.** Rectangle A measures 15 cm by 9 cm. Rectangle B measures 25 cm by 15 cm. Rectangle C measures 24 cm by 16 cm.

**(a)** Show that rectangles A and B are similar. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why rectangles A and C are not similar. **[2]**

**8.** Points D and E lie on sides AB and AC of triangle ABC, and the line DE is parallel to BC. AD = 4 cm, DB = 6 cm and DE = 5 cm.

**(a)** Find BC. **[2]**
**(b)** AC = 15 cm. Find EC. **[2]**

**9.** The lines AE and BD cross at C. AB is parallel to DE. AC = 6 cm, CE = 9 cm, DE = 12 cm and CD = 7.5 cm.

**(a)** Show that triangles ABC and EDC are similar. **[2]**
**(b)** Find AB. **[2]**
**(c)** Find BC. **[2]**

**10.**

**(a)** A prism has a regular hexagon as its cross-section. Give its numbers of faces, edges and vertices. **[3]**
**(b)** Another solid is a pyramid with 16 edges. Find the number of sides of its base and the total number of faces. **[3]**
**(c)** A solid has a plan that is a circle with a dot at its centre, and a front elevation that is an isosceles triangle. Name the solid, and explain why it is not a pyramid. **[2]**

**11.** A model is built from cubes. Each number is the count of cubes stacked at that position of the plan, and the back row is at the top.

```
Back:    2  0  3
Middle:  1  1  0
Front:   0  2  2
```

**(a)** Give the heights, in cubes, of the front elevation from left to right. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the heights of the side elevation seen from the right, from left to right. **[2]**
**(c)** How many cubes are in the model? **[1]**

**12.** (Extension only) A solid is made from two cuboids. The lower cuboid is 6 cm wide, 4 cm deep and 2 cm high. The upper cuboid is 3 cm wide, 4 cm deep and 2 cm high, sits on top at the left-hand end, and is flush with the front and back. On squared paper, draw to a scale of 1 cm to 1 cm:

**(a)** the plan **[2]**
**(b)** the front elevation **[2]**
**(c)** the side elevation seen from the right **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **One line of symmetry** [1] and **rotation symmetry of order 1** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** **8 lines of symmetry** [1] and **order 8** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A shape with no rotation symmetry has order 1, not 0, so writing order 0 is a likely way to lose the second mark.

**2. (a)** PQ is horizontal, so the midpoint has x-coordinate (1 + 7)/2 = 4 [1]. R is directly above it, so the line is **x = 4** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** The midpoint of PQ is (4, 2), and it is also the midpoint of RS [1]. S = (2 × 4 − 4, 2 × 2 − 6) = **(4, −2)** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** **2 lines of symmetry** [1] and **order 2** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Giving the line as an equation, x = 4, answers the question as asked; saying "the vertical line through R" is less precise.

**3.** DE matches AB, so **DE = 9.5 cm** [1]. EF matches BC, so **EF = 7 cm** [1]. Angle C = 180° − 64° − 53° = 63°, and F matches C, so **angle F = 63°** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Matching the letters in order (A with D, B with E) rather than by position on the page prevents answers being swapped.

**4. (a)** **Congruent** [1], by **SAS** (two sides and the included angle) [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Equal angles fix only the shape, so one triangle could be an enlargement of the other [1] **[1]**
**(c)** **Triangle ABC is congruent to triangle DEF** [1], by **RHS** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the condition in words or letters is part of the answer; saying "they look the same" does not show a reason.

**5.** AB = AC (given) [1]. BM = CM, since M is the midpoint [1]. AM is common, so the triangles ABM and ACM are congruent by SSS [1]. Then angle AMB = angle AMC. They lie on a straight line and sum to 180°, so each is **90°** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A proof is easiest to credit when each equal pair has its own line with a reason, then the condition, then the conclusion.

**6. (a)** k = 6/8 = 0.75 [1]. QR = 0.75 × 12 = **9 cm** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** PR = 0.75 × 10 = **7.5 cm** [1]. Perimeter = 6 + 9 + 7.5 = **22.5 cm** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing the scale factor first lets a slip in the multiplication be seen separately from a wrong choice of matching sides.

**7. (a)** 25/15 = 5/3 [1] and 15/9 = 5/3, so the ratios are equal and the rectangles are similar [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Long side to short side: 24/16 = 1.5, but 15/9 = 1.667 (to 3 decimal places) [1]. The ratios differ, so C is not similar to A [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Quoting both ratios with their values is stronger than stating that the sides "do not match".

**8. (a)** AB = 4 + 6 = 10 cm [1]. BC = (10/4) × 5 = **12.5 cm** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** AE = (4/10) × 15 = 6 cm [1]. EC = 15 − 6 = **9 cm** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A scale factor built from the part DB instead of the whole side AB is a common wrong route, so writing the matching sides down first helps.

**9. (a)** Angle ACB = angle ECD, vertically opposite [1]. Angle BAC = angle DEC, alternate angles as AB is parallel to DE [1]. The triangles have equal angles, so they are similar **[2]**
**(b)** A matches E, B matches D and C matches C, so AB matches ED. k = AC/EC = 6/9 = 2/3 [1]. AB = 2/3 × 12 = **8 cm** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** BC matches DC, so BC = 2/3 × 7.5 = **5 cm** [1] [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In crossing-line diagrams the pairing of letters decides the answer, so naming A matches E and B matches D before calculating avoids using the wrong side.

**10. (a)** **8 faces** [1], **18 edges** [1], **12 vertices** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** A pyramid with an n-sided base has 2n edges, so 2n = 16 [1] and n = **8** [1]. Faces = 8 + 1 = **9** [1] **[3]**
**(c)** **Cone** [1]. The plan of a pyramid is a polygon, not a circle [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* An explanation that names the actual view, such as "a pyramid's plan is a polygon", gives a clear reason; "it looks like a cone" does not.

**11. (a)** Column maxima: first column 2, 1, 0 gives 2; second column 0, 1, 2 gives 2; third column 3, 0, 2 gives 3 [1]. **2, 2, 3** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Row maxima: back 3, middle 1, front 2 [1]. From the right the front is on the left, so **2, 1, 3** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** 2 + 0 + 3 + 1 + 1 + 0 + 0 + 2 + 2 = **11 cubes** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A side elevation reads in the opposite order from the plan rows, and stating which side you are looking from makes the order clear.

**12. (a)** A rectangle 6 cm by 4 cm [1], with a vertical line 3 cm from the left-hand edge [1] **[2]**
**(b)** An L-shaped outline: the left 3 cm is 4 cm high and the right 3 cm is 2 cm high [1], with a total width of 6 cm and the base along one line [1] **[2]**
**(c)** A rectangle 4 cm wide and 4 cm high [1], with a horizontal line across it at a height of 2 cm [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Aligning the views so that the widths line up, and writing the scale and dimensions on the drawing, makes a construction easier to check.

```
Plan (6 by 4)       Front elevation      Side elevation (from right)
+-----+-----+       +-----+              +-----------+
|     |     |       |     |              |           |
|     |     |       |     |              +-----------+
|     |     |       |     +-----+        |           |
+-----+-----+       +-----+-----+        +-----------+
```

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing order 0 for a shape with no rotation symmetry.
- Giving a rhombus or parallelogram four lines of symmetry.
- Matching triangle vertices by position on the page rather than by the letters in the statement.
- Giving AAA or SSA as the reason for congruence.
- Stating that triangles are similar or congruent without naming the equal angles, sides or condition.
- Using a part of a side, such as DB, in a scale factor.
- Dividing by the scale factor when the answer should be larger.
- Counting a curved surface as a face, or forgetting the edge of a cone.
- Reading a side elevation in the wrong order.
- Leaving a drawn view without a scale or labelled dimensions.
