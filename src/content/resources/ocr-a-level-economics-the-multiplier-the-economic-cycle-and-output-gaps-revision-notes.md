## Definitions to know

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Economic cycle | Repeated fluctuation of real GDP around its long-run trend |
| Actual growth | Percentage change in real GDP |
| Trend rate of growth | Long-run average growth of potential output; the sustainable rate |
| National income multiplier | Eventual change in income divided by the injection that started it |
| Accelerator | Net investment is driven by the change in income, not its level |
| Output gap | Gap between actual and potential real GDP, usually as a percentage of potential |
| Negative gap | Actual below potential: spare capacity |
| Positive gap | Actual above potential: overstretched resources |

## Stages of the cycle

| Stage | Growth | Typical signs |
|---|---|---|
| Boom | High, output above trend | Low unemployment, rising prices and wages |
| Downturn | Positive but falling | Slowing orders, investment cut |
| Recession, trough | Negative, then bottoming | Unemployment up, spare capacity |
| Recovery | Positive and rising | Confidence returns, slack remains |

A common definition of a recession is two consecutive quarters of falling real GDP. Say it is a convention.

## Actual versus trend growth

Method in steps:

1. Use real GDP, not nominal. Real GDP = nominal GDP ÷ deflator × 100. For example, 330 ÷ 110 × 100 = 300.
2. Growth (%) = (new - old) ÷ old × 100. For example, from 250 to 258.5: 8.5 ÷ 250 = 3.4%.
3. Estimate trend as an average over a full cycle, peak to peak or trough to trough.
4. Compare: actual above trend narrows or reverses a negative gap; actual below trend widens it.

Actual growth can be fast while trend is slow (a recovery from a deep recession). Trend growth needs more or better resources: it shifts LRAS and the PPF/C outward.

## Factors determining trend growth

| Factor | How it raises trend growth |
|---|---|
| Labour supply | More workers through population, migration, participation |
| Human capital | Education, training, health raise output per worker |
| Capital | Net investment widens and deepens the capital stock |
| Technology | More output from the same inputs |
| Natural resources | Discoveries and energy supply |
| Institutions and infrastructure | Secure property rights, transport, stable rules |
| Competition and enterprise | Pressure to cut costs and innovate |

Evaluation prompts: which factor matters most for this economy (usually productivity in a mature economy); time lags; whether factors reinforce each other; whether a deep recession causes hysteresis and lowers trend growth; whether the gains are sustainable (resources, environment).

## The multiplier

**Formulae**

| Case | Formula |
|---|---|
| No taxes or imports | k = 1 / (1 - MPC) = 1 / mps |
| With withdrawals | k = 1 ÷ MPW, adding mps, mpt and mpm to get MPW |
| Change in income | Initial change in injection × k |

**Method in steps**

1. Add the marginal propensities to save, to be taxed and to import to get MPW.
2. Work out 1 ÷ MPW.
3. Multiply that by the injection.
4. State the units and say whether it is a rise or a fall.

**Worked reminder.** mps = 0.07, mpt = 0.15 and mpm = 0.10 give MPW = 0.32 and k = 1 ÷ 0.32 = 3.125. An injection of 48 raises income by 48 × 3.125 = 150. If mpm rises to 0.25, MPW = 0.47, k = 2.13 and the same injection raises income by about 102. A higher MPC alone gives, for example, k = 1 ÷ (1 - 0.9) = 10.

**Circular flow link.** Injections (I, G, X) add to income. Withdrawals (S, T, M) take it out. Each round, a share (1 - MPW) of extra income is re-spent, so the rounds shrink and sum to injection × k. Equilibrium is where injections equal withdrawals.

**AD link.** An injection of 48 with k = 3.125 shifts AD right by 150 at each price level. With an upward-sloping SRAS, real GDP rises by less than 150 because the price level also rises.

**Determinants of k**

- Size of mps, mpt, mpm: higher leakages, lower k.
- Spare capacity: with slack, the effect falls on output; near full capacity, on prices.
- Confidence and credit: they change whether extra income is spent.
- Time lags and crowding out: they reduce the effect in practice.
- Who receives the money: groups with a high MPC raise k.

**Role and purpose.** It explains why spending changes have a magnified effect on income and helps estimate the impact of a change in investment, government spending or exports.

## The multiplier-accelerator model

Net induced investment = v × change in income. With v = 1.5:

| Year | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Income | 200 | 210 | 224 | 236 | 242 | 244 | 242 |
| Change in income | | 10 | 14 | 12 | 6 | 2 | -2 |
| Net investment | | 15 | 21 | 18 | 9 | 3 | -3 |

Investment is highest in year 3, when income growth is highest, and falls from year 4 although income keeps rising to year 6.

Chain of reasoning for the cycle:

1. Rising demand raises income.
2. Higher income growth raises investment (accelerator).
3. More investment raises income again (multiplier), so the boom feeds itself.
4. Growth slows, as capacity limits are reached or confidence falters.
5. Investment falls even though income still rises.
6. The multiplier works in reverse, so income falls and investment falls further.
7. Capital wears out and must be replaced, so investment and income recover.

Weaknesses: assumes spare capacity and constant v; ignores expectations, credit and policy; explains turning points but not their timing.

## Output gaps

Output gap (%) = (actual - potential) ÷ potential × 100. Example: actual 541.2 and potential 520 give 21.2 ÷ 520 = +4.08%.

| Diagram | Negative gap | Positive gap |
|---|---|---|
| Cycle diagram (time, real GDP) | Actual wave below trend line | Actual wave above trend line |
| AD/AS | AD meets SRAS left of vertical LRAS | AD meets SRAS right of LRAS |
| PPF/C | Point inside the curve | Output at or beyond normal capacity, stretched |

**Causes**

| Negative gap | Positive gap |
|---|---|
| Falling consumption, investment, government spending or exports | Cheap credit and rapid lending growth |
| Tight credit, financial crisis | Tax cuts or large spending rises |
| Currency rise hurting exports | Rising house or share prices raising spending |
| Sticky wages and prices slow adjustment | Export boom |

Evaluation prompts: size and speed of the shock; flexibility of wages and prices; whether the cause is in the economy or abroad; and that potential output is an estimate.

**Consequences**

| Negative gap | Positive gap |
|---|---|
| Unemployment and lost output | Inflation pressure |
| Falling profits, bankruptcies | Labour and skill shortages |
| Lower tax revenue, wider budget deficit | Imports rise, so the trade balance worsens |
| Weak investment, risk of hysteresis | Asset bubbles that may burst |
| Low inflation or deflation | Short-term gains in jobs and profits |

Evaluation prompts: how big and how long; who is hurt or helped; whether the economy self-corrects; trade-offs with other objectives.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Actual versus trend growth:** actual moves with the cycle and trend is the sustainable rate.
- **Multiplier versus accelerator:** the multiplier links a change in spending to income. The accelerator links a change in income to investment.
- **Shift of AD versus change in real GDP:** the AD shift is injection × k. The change in real GDP is smaller when prices rise.
- **Negative versus positive gap:** spare capacity versus overstretched capacity.
- **Cyclical versus long-run change:** a gap closing is movement back to trend. Trend growth shifts LRAS.

## Quick self-test

1. Define an output gap.
2. Real GDP rises from 250 to 258.5. Calculate growth.
3. mps = 0.07, mpt = 0.15, mpm = 0.10. Calculate k.
4. Calculate the rise in income from an injection of 48 with that k.
5. MPC is 0.9 and there are no taxes or imports. Calculate k.
6. v = 1.5 and income rises by 14. Calculate net induced investment.
7. Actual output is 541.2 and potential is 520. Calculate the gap.
8. Nominal GDP is 330 and the deflator is 110. Calculate real GDP.
9. If mpm rises to 0.25 (others unchanged), calculate k to 2 decimal places.
10. Why can investment fall while income is still rising?
11. Where does AD meet SRAS in a negative gap?

### Answers

1. The difference between actual and potential real GDP, as a percentage of potential.
2. (258.5 - 250) ÷ 250 × 100 = 3.4%.
3. MPW = 0.32, k = 3.125.
4. 48 × 3.125 = 150.
5. 1 ÷ 0.1 = 10.
6. 1.5 × 14 = 21.
7. (541.2 - 520) ÷ 520 × 100 = +4.08%, a positive gap.
8. 330 ÷ 110 × 100 = 300.
9. MPW = 0.47, k = 2.13.
10. Under the accelerator, investment depends on the change in income. When growth slows, the change falls, so investment falls.
11. To the left of the vertical LRAS at potential output.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using nominal figures to measure growth.
- Giving k as 1 ÷ (1 - MPC) when taxes and imports are in the question.
- Forgetting to multiply by the injection after finding k.
- Stating the AD shift as the final rise in real GDP when SRAS slopes upward.
- Saying investment depends on the level of income, not its rate of change.
- Describing a positive gap as costless, or listing consequences without judging which matters most.
- Evaluation that stops at "it depends" without saying on what, or which factor is more important and why.
- Unlabelled diagrams: name the axes, the curves and the equilibrium.
