## Answers

**1.** B. **[1]**

**2.** C. **[1]**

**3.** B. **[1]**

**4.** Any two explained: Sri Lanka can obtain fuel it cannot produce itself, giving a wider choice of goods
**[2]**; tea exports earn foreign currency and create jobs **[2]**; a higher standard of living **[2]**; more
investment from foreign businesses **[2]**. **[max 4]**

**5.** (a) Any three explained: distance - long, costly delivery and freshness issues **[2]**; documentation -
customs and food safety certificates **[2]**; non-payment - difficult to recover money from a Canadian
buyer **[2]**; different legal systems - food labelling rules differ **[2]**; competition from other spice
exporters **[2]**; customs duties raise the price **[2]**. **[max 6]**
(b) Indian spices become cheaper for Canadian buyers **[1]**, so demand may increase **[1]**; revenue in rupees
from each sale rises if the price in dollars stays the same **[1]**.

**6.** (a) 84 - 71 = **+$13 billion (surplus)**. **[2]**
(b) 13 + (22 - 39) + (-3) = 13 - 17 - 3 = **-$7 billion (deficit)**. **[2]**
(c) Country K sells more goods than it buys **[1]**, but imports of services and outflows of income exceed
this, so overall more money leaves the country than enters **[1]**.

**7.** Any two explained: manufacturers winning export orders increase money flowing in **[2]**; tourism
businesses attracting foreign visitors earn foreign currency **[2]**; banks or insurers selling services
abroad **[2]**; buying from domestic suppliers instead of importing reduces outflows **[2]**. **[max 4]**

**8.** (a) Trade between countries without restrictions such as tariffs, quotas or embargoes. **[1]**
(b) Any two: no or reduced tariffs between members **[1]**; a common external tariff on non-members **[1]**;
common trade rules **[1]**; freer movement of goods **[1]**. **[max 2]**
(c) **[6]** For: tariff-free access to a much bigger market for exporters; cheaper imported materials; more
foreign investment. Against: domestic firms face stronger competition and may close; must accept common
rules; trade with non-members may become harder. Conclusion: likely to benefit a small country with
competitive exporters, but it may need support for vulnerable industries.

**9.** Any two explained: the retailer's cost of buying phones rises **[2]**; it may have to raise prices,
reducing sales **[2]**; or keep prices the same and accept lower profit **[2]**; it may switch to locally
made products **[2]**. **[max 4]**

**10.** Any two: international trade fairs **[1]**; cheaper bank loans **[1]**; insurance guarantees against
non-payment **[1]**. **[max 2]**

**11.** (a) Upstream: suppliers providing materials, such as cotton, to the enterprise **[1]**; downstream:
distribution of finished goods to retailers and consumers **[1]**.
(b) Their price, quality and reliability decide whether the brand can produce on time and at the right
cost **[2]**.
(c) Any two explained: lower cost of production and labour by making in lower-cost locations **[2]**; lower
purchasing costs by buying in bulk globally **[2]**; better logistics and communication help track orders
**[2]**. **[max 4]**

**12.** **[6]** Natural disaster risk: relying on one flood-prone region could stop production; risk assessment
would lead the brand to find suppliers in other regions or hold extra stock. Ethical risk: child labour
damages reputation and may lead to boycotts; assessment would include supplier audits. Limits: risk
assessment costs time and money and cannot remove every risk. Conclusion: very important, because both
risks could seriously harm sales and supply.

## A note on exam technique for this topic

Show the formula and the sign for every balance calculation (surplus or deficit). In "discuss" and
"evaluate" questions, apply the points to the country or business in the question and end with a
justified judgement.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I know whether a balance is a surplus or deficit?** Subtract outflows from inflows. A positive answer
is a surplus; a negative answer is a deficit.

**What are the six global supply chain risks in 7100?** Environmental (such as pollution and deforestation),
natural disasters, financial, ethical, technological and political risks.

**What is the difference between an embargo and a quota?** A quota limits the quantity imported; an embargo
bans the trade altogether.
