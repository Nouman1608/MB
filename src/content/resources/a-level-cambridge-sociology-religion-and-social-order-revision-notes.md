These notes condense heading 11 *Religion and social order* from Cambridge International A Level
Sociology (9699) Paper 4. The debate in one line: religion either integrates society
(functionalism), supports the powerful (Marxism) or can transform society (Weber, liberation
theology, religious political movements). Defining and measuring religion shapes every answer, so
start there.

Full explanations: [religion and social order study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-sociology-religion-and-social-order/).

## Syllabus coverage

9699 (2024–2026) Paper 4 Section C, heading 11: **11.1** Religion and society; **11.2** Religion and
social order; **11.3** Religion as a source of social change.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Substantive definition** | Defines religion by its content, e.g. belief in the supernatural (Weber) |
| **Functional definition** | Defines religion by what it does for society or individuals (Durkheim) |
| **Religiosity** | Degree of religious belief, belonging and practice |
| **Sacred / profane** | Set apart and special / ordinary (Durkheim) |
| **Collective conscience** | Shared norms and values binding society |
| **Civil religion** | Sacred reverence for the nation (Bellah, 1967) |
| **Ideology** | Ideas that legitimate the interests of the powerful |
| **Theodicy of disprivilege** | Religious explanation of suffering that appeals to the poor (Weber) |
| **Asceticism** | Self-discipline and denial of pleasure |
| **Calling** | Duty to serve God through one's worldly work |
| **Liberation theology** | Catholic movement committed to the poor and social justice |
| **Relative autonomy** | Religion can act partly independently of the economy (Maduro, 1982) |

## 11.1 Defining, measuring and religiosity

- **Definitions:** substantive (Weber), functional (Durkheim), social constructionist
  (interactionist).
- **Measurement problems:** belief versus belonging versus practice; Glock and Stark's dimensions
  (1968); question wording; social desirability; private belief unobservable.
- **Religiosity by group:**
  - Gender — women often more religious; socialisation, caring roles, life course.
  - Age — older people often more religious; ageing or generation effect.
  - Ethnicity — high among many minorities; cultural defence and cultural transition (Bruce, 1996).
  - Class — theodicy of disprivilege for the poor; world-affirming movements for the affluent.
- **Other belief systems:** science, political ideologies, humanism — rivals or functional
  equivalents?

## 11.2 Functionalism versus Marxism

| | Functionalism | Marxism |
|---|---|---|
| Key names | Durkheim (1912), Malinowski, Parsons, Bellah (1967) | Marx (1844), Maduro (1982) |
| Role of religion | Social solidarity, shared values, coping with crisis | Ideology, legitimation of inequality, compensation |
| View of society | Consensus | Class conflict |
| Strength | Explains ritual, national ceremony, crisis response | Explains links between religion and power |
| Limitation | Ignores conflict and diversity; small-scale evidence | Ignores religion as a source of change; assumes false consciousness |

## 11.3 Religion and social change

- **Weber (1905):** Calvinist predestination → anxiety → ascetic hard work and reinvestment →
  spirit of capitalism.
- **Alternatives:** Tawney (1926) — economic change came first; Marxist economic explanation;
  capitalism in non-Calvinist areas.
- **Liberation theology:** Gutiérrez (1971); Archbishop Romero assassinated (1980); religion
  against oppression.
- **Evangelicals in US politics:** Moral Majority (1979, Jerry Falwell); Bruce (2003) — limited
  policy success.
- **Iranian revolution (1979):** Ayatollah Khomeini; religion as revolutionary force.

## Worked essay plan

*Practice question:* "Religion is a conservative force in society." Evaluate this view. [35]

1. Define "conservative" (preserving existing values and power) and define religion.
2. Support: functionalism — social solidarity and shared values; Marxism — ideology and opium of the
   people.
3. Challenge: Weber's Protestant ethic; liberation theology; Iranian revolution.
4. Nuance: Maduro's relative autonomy; religion can be conservative in values but radical in
   action (e.g. a revolution establishing a traditional moral order).
5. Judgement: religion's effect depends on its relationship to power in a given society.

## Model evaluation paragraph

*On Durkheim's functionalism:* "Durkheim's study of totemism shows how shared ritual can bind a
small, homogeneous society together, and national ceremonies in modern states suggest religion-like
rituals still create solidarity. However, his evidence came from secondary accounts of a small-scale
society, and in large, religiously diverse societies religion often divides rather than unites, as
sectarian conflicts show. Marxists would add that any 'solidarity' produced may simply mask class
inequality. Durkheim's theory is most convincing for societies with one dominant faith and least
convincing where several religions compete."

This shows the examiner methodological criticism, a competing theory and a conditional judgement.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Using only Durkheim and Marx — 11.3 expects change arguments too.
- Treating "conservative" only as "right-wing"; sociologically it means resisting change.
- Describing Weber without the mechanism (predestination, anxiety, asceticism, reinvestment).
- Forgetting **measurement difficulties** and **social groups** in 11.1.
- Not using the syllabus's named case studies (US Evangelicals, Iran).

## Quick self-test

1. Give one problem with functional definitions of religion.
2. What did Malinowski observe among Trobriand Islanders?
3. Explain "opium of the people".
4. Outline Tawney's challenge to Weber.
5. Name one example of religion causing political change.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I bring interactionism into religion essays?** The syllabus asks you to contrast structural
and interactionist theories; use social constructionist definitions and the meaning religion has for
believers as the interactionist angle.

**Is Weber a Marxist?** No. Weber accepted that economic factors matter but argued ideas —
including religious ideas — can drive economic change.

**What is the best evidence that religion can be radical?** Liberation theology and the Iranian
revolution are the clearest cases.
