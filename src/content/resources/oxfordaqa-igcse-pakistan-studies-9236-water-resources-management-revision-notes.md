These notes condense the water content of key development 3.2.1.4 of the 9236 specification: "uses
of water as a natural resource, management of water supplies and implications for the economy and
people of Pakistan". The full explanation is in the
[water resources study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-pakistan-studies-9236-water-resources-management/);
the wider topic is in the
[Landscape revision notes](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-pakistan-studies-landscape-revision-notes/).

## Sources

- **Indus rivers**: summer snow and glacier melt plus monsoon rain; very uneven flow.
- **Groundwater**: tube wells, especially in Punjab; recharged partly by canal seepage.
- **Rainfall**: low over most of the country.
- **Karez**: underground channels in Balochistan carrying groundwater by gravity.

## Uses

- **Agriculture** (by far the largest): wheat, rice, cotton, sugar cane, fodder.
- **Domestic**: drinking, cooking, sanitation.
- **Industry**: textiles, sugar, fertiliser, food processing.
- **Hydel power**: Tarbela, Mangla and other dams.
- **Fisheries and environment**: delta flows and mangroves.

## Agreements and institutions

| Agreement or body | Date | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Indus Waters Treaty | 1960 | With India, World Bank help: eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) to India; western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) to Pakistan |
| Indus Basin Project | 1960s–1970s | Mangla Dam (1967), Tarbela Dam (1976), barrages and link canals |
| Water Apportionment Accord | 1991 | Shares Indus water between the provinces |
| IRSA | Created after the accord | Regulates distribution between provinces |
| National Water Policy | 2018 | National framework for storage, efficiency and conservation |

## Problems

| Problem | Cause | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Limited storage | Most flow in summer; silt fills reservoirs | Shortages in the dry season |
| Waterlogging and salinity | Canal seepage, over-irrigation, evaporation | Lower yields; land abandoned |
| Falling groundwater | Over-pumping by tube wells | Higher costs; dry wells |
| Waste | Flood irrigation, leakage, theft | Less water reaches crops |
| Provincial disputes | Upstream and downstream interests | Delayed projects, such as Kalabagh |
| Pollution | Sewage, industrial waste, arsenic | Disease; unsafe supplies |
| Urban shortages | Population growth in Karachi and Lahore | Rationing; tanker supply |
| Climate change | Changing melt; floods and droughts | Less predictable supply |

## Strategies and their limits

- **New dams** (Diamer-Bhasha, Mohmand): storage and power; costly and slow, with displacement.
- **Canal lining**: less seepage; very expensive across the network.
- **Drip, sprinkler, laser levelling**: efficient; costly for small farmers.
- **Groundwater regulation**: protects aquifers; hard to enforce.
- **Rainwater harvesting and small dams**: local help in dry areas; small volumes.
- **Wastewater treatment**: cleaner water; few plants.

## Implications

| Economy | People |
|---|---|
| Irrigated farming and the cotton-textile chain depend on water | Safe water reduces disease; contamination spreads it |
| Hydel power output falls when reservoirs are low | Women and girls spend time collecting water in rural areas |
| Shortages raise food prices and imports | Unequal access causes disputes |
| Waterlogged land reduces farm income | Shortage and salinity can push migration to cities |

## Water, energy and development

- **Hydel depends on water:** low reservoirs mean less electricity.
- **Water depends on energy:** tube wells, city pumps and treatment plants need power.
- **Development needs both:** factories, hospitals, schools and farms rely on reliable supplies.
- **Population growth:** water per person falls unless storage and efficiency improve.

## Answering command words

- **"Explain how"** questions on waterlogging, salinity or falling water tables need a linked sequence
  of three or four steps.
- **"Analyse"** questions need two developed points with named places, dams or agreements.
- **"How far do you agree"** questions on dams or shortages need both sides, with the costs and limits
  of each strategy, and a clear final judgement.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Barrage | Low dam that raises river level to divert water into canals |
| Link canal | Canal that transfers water from one river to another |
| Perennial canal | Canal that flows all year |
| Aquifer | Underground rock or sediment that stores water |
| Water table | Upper level of saturated ground |
| Waterlogging | Soil saturated because the water table is near the surface |
| Salinity | Build-up of salt in soil |
| Karez | Underground channel carrying groundwater by gravity |

## Exam traps

- Eastern and western rivers reversed.
- Calling the 1991 accord an agreement with India.
- Strategies with no limitations in evaluation answers.
- Forgetting groundwater and focusing only on rivers.
- No named dam, barrage or city.

## Self-test

1. Which sector uses most of Pakistan's water?
2. Name the three rivers allocated to Pakistan by the 1960 treaty.
3. Which organisation helped negotiate the Indus Waters Treaty?
4. On which rivers are the Mangla and Tarbela dams?
5. What does the 1991 Water Apportionment Accord do?
6. What is a barrage?
7. Explain how salinity develops.
8. Why are groundwater levels falling in many areas?
9. Name two dams under construction to increase storage.
10. What is a karez?
11. Give one implication of water shortage for the economy.
12. Give one implication of unsafe water for people.

## Answers

1. Agriculture.
2. The Indus, Jhelum and Chenab.
3. The World Bank.
4. Mangla on the Jhelum; Tarbela on the Indus.
5. It shares Indus water between Pakistan's provinces.
6. A low dam that raises the river level so water can be diverted into canals.
7. Seepage and over-irrigation raise the water table; evaporation leaves salts in the soil.
8. Tube wells pump out more water than is recharged.
9. Diamer-Bhasha and Mohmand.
10. An underground channel that carries groundwater from the foot of hills to fields by gravity.
11. Any one: lower crop yields; higher food prices; less hydel power; more imports.
12. Any one: diarrhoea, typhoid or other water-borne disease; illness from arsenic.
