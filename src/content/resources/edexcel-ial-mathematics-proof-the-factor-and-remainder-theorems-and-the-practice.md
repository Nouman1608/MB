## Questions

**1.** Prove that the sum of the squares of any two consecutive integers is always odd. **[3]**

**2.** Prove that the cube of any integer is either a multiple of 9, or one more than a multiple of 9, or one less than a multiple of 9. **[5]**

**3.** Show by counter example that each statement is false.

**(a)** "4n² + 1 is prime for all positive integers n." **[2]**
**(b)** "For real numbers a and b, if a² > b² then a > b." **[2]**

**4.** The polynomial f(x) = 2x³ + px² + qx − 8 has a factor (2x + 1). When f(x) is divided by (x + 1) the remainder is 5.

**(a)** Determine p and q. **[4]**
**(b)** Hence solve f(x) = 0. **[3]**

**5.** f(x) = 6x³ − 11x² − 12x + 5.

**(a)** Show that (2x − 5) is a factor of f(x). **[2]**
**(b)** Hence factorise f(x) completely. **[3]**

**6.** A circle has equation x² + y² + 6x − 4y − 23 = 0.

**(a)** State the coordinates of the centre and the value of the radius. **[3]**
**(b)** Show that the point (3, 2) lies on the circle. **[2]**

**7.** The line segment PQ, where P is (−2, 3) and Q is (4, −1), is a diameter of a circle.

**(a)** Find an equation of the circle. **[4]**
**(b)** The point R has x-coordinate 3 and positive y-coordinate and lies on the circle. Find the y-coordinate of R and show that angle PRQ = 90°. **[3]**

**8.** The circle C has equation (x − 2)² + (y + 3)² = 34.

**(a)** Show that T(7, −6) lies on C. **[1]**
**(b)** Find an equation of the tangent to C at T, in the form ax + by = c where a, b and c are integers. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the coordinates of the point where this tangent crosses the x-axis. **[2]**

**9.** The line y = x + 2 meets the circle x² + y² − 8x + 2y − 8 = 0 at the points A and B.

**(a)** Work out the coordinates of A and B. **[4]**
**(b)** The centre of the circle is C. Show that the perpendicular from C to the line AB meets AB at the midpoint of AB. **[3]**

**10.** f(x) = x³ − 6x² + 3x + 10.

**(a)** Show that (x + 1) is a factor of f(x). **[2]**
**(b)** Factorise f(x) completely. **[3]**
**(c)** The curve y = f(x) crosses the x-axis at three points. The circle S has as a diameter the line joining the outer two of these points. Find an equation of S. **[2]**
**(d)** Show that P(3, 2√2) lies on S and that the angle at P between the lines joining P to the ends of the diameter is a right angle. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Let the integers be n and n + 1, with n an integer. [1]
Sum of squares = n² + (n + 1)² = 2n² + 2n + 1 [1]
= 2(n² + n) + 1, which is twice a whole number plus 1, so it is odd. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A proof built from a few numerical cases earns little; the general argument with a letter, and a closing sentence that links 2(n² + n) + 1 back to the claim, is what the question asks for.

**2.** Every integer has the form 3m, 3m + 1 or 3m + 2, where m is an integer, so these cases cover all integers. [1]
Case 3m: (3m)³ = 27m³ = 9(3m³), a multiple of 9. [1]
Case 3m + 1: (3m + 1)³ = 27m³ + 27m² + 9m + 1 = 9(3m³ + 3m² + m) + 1, one more than a multiple of 9. [1]
Case 3m + 2: (3m + 2)³ = 27m³ + 54m² + 36m + 8 = 9(3m³ + 6m² + 4m + 1) − 1, one less than a multiple of 9. [1]
Every case gives one of the three forms, so the statement holds for all integers. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In exhaustion, say why the list of cases is complete and write a conclusion; checking only some of the cases leaves the proof unfinished.

**3. (a)** Take n = 4: 4(16) + 1 = 65. [1]
65 = 5 × 13 is not prime, so the statement is false. [1]
**(b)** Take a = −5 and b = 2: a² = 25 and b² = 4, so a² > b². [1]
But a = −5 is less than b = 2, so a > b fails. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A counter example must satisfy the condition of the statement and then break the conclusion; show both calculations rather than quoting the numbers.

**4. (a)** Factor theorem: f(−1/2) = 0, so −1/4 + p/4 − q/2 − 8 = 0, which gives p − 2q = 33. [1]
Remainder theorem: f(−1) = 5, so −2 + p − q − 8 = 5, which gives p − q = 15. [1]
Subtract the second equation from the first: −q = 18, so q = −18. [1]
**p = −3, q = −18** [1]
**(b)** Division by (2x + 1) gives f(x) = (2x + 1)(x² − 2x − 8). [1]
x² − 2x − 8 = (x − 4)(x + 2). [1]
**x = −1/2, x = 4, x = −2** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the substitution value for each condition before you simplify, so the equations are clear even if an arithmetic slip follows; then check your p and q in both conditions.

**5. (a)** f(5/2) = 6(125/8) − 11(25/4) − 12(5/2) + 5 [1]
= 375/4 − 275/4 − 30 + 5 = 25 − 25 = 0, so (2x − 5) is a factor. [1]
**(b)** Dividing gives f(x) = (2x − 5)(3x² + 2x − 1). [1]
3x² + 2x − 1 = (3x − 1)(x + 1). [1]
**f(x) = (2x − 5)(3x − 1)(x + 1)** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* For "show that", write out the substitution and the zero result, then state the conclusion; "completely" means the quadratic factor must also be factorised.

**6. (a)** Complete the square: (x + 3)² − 9 + (y − 2)² − 4 − 23 = 0. [1]
(x + 3)² + (y − 2)² = 36 [1]
**Centre (−3, 2), radius 6** [1]
**(b)** (3 + 3)² + (2 − 2)² = 36 + 0 [1]
= 36, equal to r², so (3, 2) lies on the circle. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The centre has the opposite signs to the brackets, and the radius is the square root of the right-hand side; both slips are common on expanded equations.

**7. (a)** Centre = midpoint of PQ = ((−2 + 4)/2, (3 − 1)/2) = (1, 1). [1]
PQ² = 6² + 4² = 52. [1]
r² = (1/4)(52) = 13. [1]
**(x − 1)² + (y − 1)² = 13** [1]
**(b)** (3 − 1)² + (y − 1)² = 13, so (y − 1)² = 9, giving y = 4 or y = −2. Since y is positive, **y = 4**. [1]
Gradient PR = (4 − 3)/(3 + 2) = 1/5 and gradient RQ = (−1 − 4)/(4 − 3) = −5. [1]
The product is −1, so PR is perpendicular to RQ and angle PRQ = 90°. [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the positive condition to reject the second root explicitly, and show the gradient product rather than quoting the semicircle property alone, since the question says "show".

**8. (a)** (7 − 2)² + (−6 + 3)² = 25 + 9 = 34, so T lies on C. [1]
**(b)** Centre (2, −3). Gradient of radius = (−6 + 3)/(7 − 2) = −3/5. [1]
Gradient of tangent = 5/3. [1]
y + 6 = (5/3)(x − 7), so 3y + 18 = 5x − 35, giving **5x − 3y = 53**. [1]
**(c)** Put y = 0: 5x = 53. [1]
**(53/5, 0)** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Flip and negate the radius gradient to get the tangent gradient; check your final line by substituting T, and give integer coefficients because the form is specified.

**9. (a)** Substitute y = x + 2: x² + (x + 2)² − 8x + 2(x + 2) − 8 = 0. [1]
2x² − 2x = 0, so 2x(x − 1) = 0. [1]
x = 0 or x = 1. [1]
**A(0, 2) and B(1, 3)** (using y = x + 2) [1]
**(b)** Complete the square: (x − 4)² + (y + 1)² = 25, so C is (4, −1). [1]
Midpoint M of AB = (1/2, 5/2). Gradient CM = (5/2 + 1)/(1/2 − 4) = −1. [1]
Gradient AB = 1, and 1 × (−1) = −1, so CM is perpendicular to AB. The perpendicular from C meets AB at M. [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Substitute the line into the circle, not the other way round, and find the y-values from the line; then state the conclusion in words, because the question asks you to show a property.

**10. (a)** f(−1) = −1 − 6 − 3 + 10 = 0. [1]
So (x + 1) is a factor. [1]
**(b)** Division gives f(x) = (x + 1)(x² − 7x + 10). [1]
x² − 7x + 10 = (x − 2)(x − 5). [1]
**f(x) = (x + 1)(x − 2)(x − 5)** [1]
**(c)** The outer crossing points are (−1, 0) and (5, 0), so the centre is (2, 0) and the radius is 3. [1]
**(x − 2)² + y² = 9** [1]
**(d)** (3 − 2)² + (2√2)² = 1 + 8 = 9, so P lies on S. [1]
Gradient from (−1, 0) to P = 2√2/4 = √2/2; gradient from P to (5, 0) = −2√2/2 = −√2. The product is −1, so the angle at P is 90°. [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the three crossing points from your factorisation before building the circle, and use the angle-in-a-semicircle property in your explanation as well as the gradient check.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Checking one or two numbers in a proof instead of using general letters.
- Missing a final sentence that links the algebra to the statement being proved.
- Listing exhaustion cases that do not cover every integer.
- Offering a counter example that does not meet the condition in the statement.
- Substituting x = b/a instead of x = −b/a for the divisor (ax + b).
- Stopping at a quadratic factor when the question says "completely".
- Reading the centre with the wrong signs, or giving r² as the radius.
- Using the radius gradient for the tangent instead of its negative reciprocal.
- Stating a circle property without showing the numerical check when the question says "show that".
