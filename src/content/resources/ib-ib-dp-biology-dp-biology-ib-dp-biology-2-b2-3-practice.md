## Questions

**1.** All the cells of an adult human developed from one zygote and contain the same genes. Explain how they can become specialized in different ways. **[2]**

**2.** In an early embryo, a signalling chemical is released at one end and spreads through the tissue. Suggest how this can cause cells in different positions to develop differently. **[2]**

**3.** **(a)** State the two properties that define a stem cell. **[2]**

**(b)** Outline the role of a stem cell niche. **[1]**

**4.** State the potency of each cell.

**(a)** A cell from an eight-cell embryo that, separated from the others, develops into a complete embryo with its placenta. **[1]**

**(b)** A cell in a hair follicle that produces new follicle and hair cells only. **[1]**

**(c)** A cell from an embryo several days old that can form muscle, nerve and liver cells but not placenta. **[1]**

**5.** A human egg is about 0.1 mm across, and a red blood cell about 7.5 µm. Explain how the size of each cell suits its function. **[2]**

**6.** Two spherical model cells have radii of 3 µm and 9 µm. For a sphere, surface area ÷ volume = 3 ÷ r.

**(a)** Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of each cell. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the larger cell would have difficulty surviving. **[2]**

**7.** A spherical cell of radius 6 µm has a volume of 905 µm³ and a surface area of 452 µm². The same volume is reshaped into a flat disc 2 µm thick.

**(a)** Calculate the radius of the disc. Use volume = πR² × thickness. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the surface area of the disc. Use surface area = 2πR² + 2πR × thickness. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the surface area to volume ratio before and after flattening, and comment. **[1]**

**8.** Proximal convoluted tubule cells in the kidney have microvilli on the side facing the filtrate and infoldings of the membrane on the opposite side. Explain how these features suit the cells' function. **[2]**

**9.** (HL only) Compare and contrast type I and type II pneumocytes. **[3]**

**10.** (HL only) Babies born very early may not yet produce enough surfactant. Suggest why they have difficulty breathing. **[2]**

**11.** (HL only) **(a)** State two structural differences between cardiac muscle cells and striated muscle fibres. **[2]**

**(b)** Discuss whether a striated muscle fibre should be regarded as a single cell. **[2]**

**12.** (HL only) Explain three ways in which a sperm cell is adapted for fertilization. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Cells express different genes, so different proteins are made [1]; these proteins give each cell type its particular structure and function, although all cells keep the full genome [1] **[2]**

**2.** Cells near the source receive a higher concentration of the chemical than cells further away [1]; different concentrations switch on different genes, so cells develop into different types according to position [1] **[2]**

**3. (a)** They can divide repeatedly to produce more stem cells (self-renewal) [1]; they can differentiate into specialized cells [1]

**(b)** It provides signals that keep the stem cells unspecialized and control when they divide and differentiate [1] **[3]**

**4. (a)** Totipotent [1]

**(b)** Multipotent [1]

**(c)** Pluripotent [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The placenta is the test: only totipotent cells can make extra-embryonic tissue.

**5.** The egg is large because its cytoplasm holds nutrient reserves for the early embryo [1]; the red blood cell is small so that it can pass through narrow capillaries and has a short diffusion distance for oxygen [1] **[2]**

**6. (a)** 3 ÷ 3 = **1.0 µm⁻¹**; 3 ÷ 9 = **0.33 µm⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** The larger cell has only a third as much surface per unit volume [1], so it cannot exchange oxygen, nutrients and waste fast enough to meet the needs of its volume, and diffusion to its centre takes longer [1] **[3]**

**7. (a)** R² = 905 ÷ (π × 2) = 144, so **R = 12 µm** [1]

**(b)** 2π × 144 + 2π × 12 × 2 = 905 + 151 = **1056 µm²** [1]

**(c)** Before: 452 ÷ 905 = 0.50 µm⁻¹; after: 1056 ÷ 905 = 1.17 µm⁻¹, so flattening more than doubles the ratio, speeding exchange [1] **[3]**

**8.** Microvilli greatly increase the surface area of membrane in contact with the filtrate, so more useful substances can be reabsorbed [1]; infoldings increase the area for moving substances out of the cell towards the blood [1] **[2]**

**9.** Similarity: both are cells of the alveolar wall [1]. Type I cells are extremely thin and flat, giving a short diffusion distance for gas exchange [1], whereas type II cells are cuboidal and secrete surfactant from lamellar bodies [1] **[3]**

**10.** Without surfactant, surface tension of the fluid lining the alveoli is high [1], so alveoli tend to collapse and stick together when breathing out, making them hard to inflate and reducing the surface for gas exchange [1] **[2]**

**11. (a)** Any two: cardiac cells are branched, striated fibres are not [1]; cardiac cells have one or two nuclei, striated fibres have many [1]; striated fibres are much longer **[2]**

**(b)** For: a fibre is enclosed by a single plasma membrane and functions as one unit [1]. Against: it is far larger than a typical cell and has many nuclei because it formed by fusion of many cells, so it does not fit the usual idea of a cell; the answer depends on the definition used [1] **[4]**

**12.** Any three: the flagellum allows it to swim to the egg [1]; many mitochondria in the midpiece provide ATP for the flagellum [1]; the acrosome contains enzymes that digest a path through the egg's outer layers [1]; a small size with little cytoplasm makes it light and streamlined **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying specialized cells contain different genes.
- Confusing totipotent and pluripotent.
- Writing that a larger cell has a larger ratio.
- Leaving units off surface area to volume ratios.
- Assigning surfactant secretion to type I pneumocytes.
- Giving a sperm feature without the function it serves.
