For the other inquiry topics you choose **one country** outside the USA, and one in Latin America for solutions. These notes use Canada and Brazil. ## Key terms

- **Margin buying**: buying shares with borrowed money.
- **Smoot-Hawley Tariff (1930)**: US tariff raising import duties, which provoked retaliation.
- **Dust Bowl**: drought and soil erosion on the Great Plains in the 1930s.
- **Hooverville**: camp of homeless people named after President Hoover.
- **New Deal**: Roosevelt's programmes of relief, recovery and reform.
- **FDIC**: Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, insuring bank deposits.
- **Wagner Act (1935)**: protected collective bargaining.
- **ISI**: Import Substitution Industrialisation.
- **Estado Novo**: Vargas's authoritarian regime in Brazil from 1937.
- **CCF**: Co-operative Commonwealth Federation in Canada (1932).

## Causes

| Cause | Detail |
|---|---|
| Political | Weak regulation; high tariffs; Federal Reserve failed to stop the fall in the money supply |
| Economic | Bank failures (about 9,000 in the USA, 1930 to 1933); instalment credit; uneven income; gold standard spreading shocks |
| Agricultural | Overproduction, falling prices, farm debt; Dust Bowl; prairie drought in Canada |
| Reaction to the Crash | Hoover trusted in voluntary action and balanced budgets |

Historians: Galbraith (speculation and structure), Friedman and Schwartz (monetary failure), Kindleberger (no leader in the international system).

## Political impact in the USA

- Party politics: Democrats dominant after 1932; New Deal coalition of South, urban workers, immigrants, Black voters and unions.
- Executive power: federal agencies; fireside chats; court-packing plan (1937).
- Labour and social policy: Wagner Act, Social Security, Fair Labor Standards Act. Exclusions of farm and domestic workers.
- Opposition: Liberty League, Supreme Court rulings, Huey Long, Coughlin, Townsend.

## Political impact in one other country

| Country | Points |
|---|---|
| Canada | Unemployment above a quarter at the peak; governments voted out; CCF, Social Credit, Union Nationale; strain on federalism |
| Brazil | Coffee collapse; 1930 revolution; Vargas centralised; 1932 São Paulo revolt; 1934 constitution; Estado Novo 1937 |

## Social impact

- USA: unemployment about a quarter in 1933; falling births; Hoovervilles; Okies.
- Women: pressure on married women's jobs, yet total women's employment held up; Perkins and Eleanor Roosevelt.
- Marginalised groups: Black Americans "last hired, first fired" but some relief; Mexican repatriation; Indian Reorganization Act (1934).
- Culture: Lange, WPA arts projects, Steinbeck, Guthrie, radio, Hollywood.

## Solutions in the USA and Canada

| Leader | Actions | Judgement |
|---|---|---|
| Hoover | RFC, farm board, voluntary action | Too limited and late |
| Roosevelt | Relief, recovery and reform: CCC, WPA, AAA, NRA, FDIC, SEC, TVA, Social Security | Restored confidence and built a safety net; unemployment high; recession 1937; war ended it |
| Bennett | Tariffs, relief camps; "New Deal" broadcasts (1935); Bank of Canada; Wheat Board | Late and partly struck down by courts |
| King | Cautious; Rowell-Sirois Commission (1937) | Limited change |

## Solutions in one Latin American country

- Brazil: coffee support, ISI, labour ministry and regulations, state-controlled unions, authoritarian rule.
- Mexico: land reform and nationalisation under Cárdenas.
- Argentina: trade deal with Britain (1933), growth of industry.
- ISI created industry and jobs but kept dependence on imported machinery and strengthened state control.

## Debates

- Which cause mattered most?
- New Deal: success, failure or limited success? (Schlesinger and Leuchtenburg; critics from left and right.)
- Depression and authoritarianism: Vargas compared with Roosevelt.
- War versus New Deal as the end of the Depression.

## Essay skeleton

1. Define the argument and take a line.
2. Rank two or three causes or measures.
3. Use a named perspective.
4. Compare with a second country where relevant.
5. Judge.

## Worked example: planning an answer

Question: "To what extent was the New Deal successful in dealing with the effects of the Great Depression?"

1. Line: it was successful in relief and reform and less so in recovery.
2. Relief: CCC, WPA, FERA gave work to millions.
3. Reform: FDIC, SEC, Social Security and the Wagner Act created lasting protection.
4. Recovery: GDP grew from 1933, but unemployment stayed high and the 1937 to 1938 recession followed spending cuts.
5. Perspective: Schlesinger and Leuchtenburg (success), critics on the left (too little) and the right (prolonged the slump).
6. Compare: Bennett's late "New Deal" in Canada or Vargas in Brazil.
7. Judgement: success is clearest in reform and in confidence, and war ended the Depression.

## Common mistakes

- Listing New Deal agencies without judging them.
- Ignoring the second country.
- Single-cause explanations.

## Self-test

1. Give two causes of the Depression other than the Crash.
2. What was the Wagner Act?
3. Name one New Deal agency for each of relief and reform.
4. Name two new parties in Canada in the 1930s.
5. What was ISI?
6. Why is the 1937 recession important to debates about the New Deal?

**Answers:** 1. Any two: weak regulation, bank failures, tariffs, uneven income, farm overproduction, gold standard. 2. A 1935 law protecting workers' right to organise and bargain collectively. 3. Relief: CCC, WPA or FERA; reform: FDIC, SEC, TVA or Social Security. 4. Any two: CCF, Social Credit, Union Nationale. 5. Import Substitution Industrialisation: producing at home goods previously imported. 6. It shows that recovery was fragile and depended on government spending, which supports critics who say the New Deal was not enough.
