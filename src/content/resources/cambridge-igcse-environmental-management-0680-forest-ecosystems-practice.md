These questions cover sub-topic 5.2 of Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680, 2027-2029): the causes of deforestation, its impacts, the definition of sustainable forest management and the reasons forests need it. All data in the sources is invented for practice.

## Section A

**1.** Define *sustainable management of forests*. **[2]**

**2.** State **three** causes of deforestation named in the syllabus, other than farming. **[3]**

**3.** Distinguish between subsistence farming and commercial farming as causes of deforestation. **[2]**

**4.** State what is meant by *interception*. **[1]**

**5.** Name **two** raw materials, other than timber, that forests provide. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Explain how trees act as both a carbon sink and a carbon store, and why deforestation contributes to global warming. **[4]**

**7.** Source: *A survey of a forested river basin recorded 8,200 km² of forest. Twelve years later, after a new highway and two open-cast mines were built, the forest covered 6,560 km².*
(a) Calculate the percentage of forest lost. Show your working. **[2]**
(b) Calculate the mean area lost per year. **[1]**
(c) Using the source, explain how the highway could have caused more forest loss than the land it occupies. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how deforestation can lead to flooding in a river valley downstream of the cleared area. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how the loss of a large area of forest can change rainfall patterns in the surrounding region. **[3]**

**10.** A government plans a hydro-electric power station that will flood 300 km² of forest. Suggest **two** impacts on wildlife and **one** impact on people living in the valley. **[3]**

**11.** Describe **four** reasons why forests need sustainable management. **[4]**

**12.** "Climate change is both a cause and an effect of deforestation." Explain this statement. **[4]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Balancing the needs of the environment, wildlife and humans **[1]** while conserving forests for future generations **[1]**.

**2.** Any three, 1 mark each: logging and timber extraction **[1]**; roads and settlements **[1]**; rock, ore and mineral extraction **[1]**; hydro-electric power stations **[1]**; climate change **[1]**. **[max 3]**

**3.** Subsistence farming clears small plots to grow food for the farmer's own family **[1]**; commercial farming clears large areas to grow crops or rear livestock for sale or export, for profit **[1]**.

**4.** Leaves and branches catching rainfall before it reaches the ground **[1]**.

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: rubber; fibres; medicinal plants; fruit or nuts; resins; fuelwood. **[max 2]**

**6.** Growing trees absorb carbon dioxide from the air by photosynthesis, so they are a carbon sink **[1]**; the carbon is held in wood, roots and soil for many years, so forests are a carbon store **[1]**. When trees are burned or left to decay, the stored carbon is released as carbon dioxide **[1]**, and fewer trees remain to absorb it, so carbon dioxide in the atmosphere rises, enhancing the greenhouse effect **[1]**.

**7.** (a) Loss = 8,200 − 6,560 = 1,640 km² **[1]**; 1,640 ÷ 8,200 × 100 = 20% **[1]**.
(b) 1,640 ÷ 12 = 136.7 km² per year (accept 137 km² per year; unit required) **[1]**.
(c) The highway gives access to remote forest **[1]**, so loggers, farmers and settlers move in and clear land beside it, and the mines in the source can also be reached and supplied **[1]**.

**8.** With fewer trees, less rain is intercepted and taken up by roots **[1]**, so more surface run-off reaches the river, and reaches it more quickly **[1]**. Bare soil is eroded and washed into the river **[1]**, where it is deposited (silting), reducing channel capacity so the river overflows more easily **[1]**.

**9.** Trees release water vapour by transpiration **[1]**; this vapour forms clouds that produce rain further inland **[1]**; with the forest removed, less moisture is recycled, so rainfall decreases and dry seasons can lengthen **[1]**.

**10.** Wildlife, any two: habitat loss as forest is drowned under the reservoir **[1]**; loss of biodiversity as species unable to move die or leave **[1]**; fragmentation of the remaining forest, separating populations; genetic depletion. People, any one: villages flooded so families must be resettled **[1]**; loss of farmland or forest products they depended on; loss of livelihoods. **[max 3: 2 wildlife + 1 people]**

**11.** Any four described, 1 mark each: climate regulation, as trees are carbon sinks and stores **[1]**; role in the water cycle through interception, uptake, transpiration and reduced run-off **[1]**; flood control **[1]**; prevention of soil erosion, as roots bind the soil **[1]**; genetic resource for crops and medicines **[1]**; food, medicine and raw materials **[1]**; opportunities for recreation, ecotourism and education **[1]**. **[max 4]**

**12.** Cause: higher temperatures and longer dry seasons stress trees **[1]** and increase drought and wildfire, so forest is lost without felling **[1]**. Effect: deforestation releases stored carbon as carbon dioxide and removes a carbon sink **[1]**, which adds to global warming, so the two reinforce each other **[1]**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving "farming" as one cause without separating subsistence and commercial reasons.
- Explaining global warming through oxygen rather than carbon dioxide release and loss of a carbon sink.
- In calculations, dividing the loss by the new area instead of the original area, or leaving out units.
- Answering a "using the source" question from memory without referring to the highway, mines or figures given.
- Listing impacts on wildlife when the question also asks for an impact on people.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these Forest ecosystems questions from Cambridge past papers?**
No. They are original questions written for Marlbridge and do not copy past papers or their mark tariffs. Use them alongside the past papers for Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680).

**Do I need to know the rainforest layers for sub-topic 5.2?**
The 5.2 statements focus on causes and impacts of deforestation and why forests need sustainable management. Layers and the nutrient store help explain erosion, but the marks here go to the named causes, impacts and reasons.

**Why is percentage loss calculated from the original area?**
Percentage change always compares the change with the starting value, so divide the area lost by the first survey's area, then multiply by 100.
