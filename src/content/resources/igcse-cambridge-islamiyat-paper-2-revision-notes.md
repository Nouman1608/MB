Condensed recall notes for **Paper 2** of Cambridge IGCSE Islamiyat
(0493), 2026–2027 series. Paper 2 is a 1.5-hour exam of five questions,
of which candidates must answer Question 1, Question 2 and two others,
covering the Hadiths of the Prophet, the Rightly Guided Caliphs, and
the Articles of Faith and Pillars of Islam. Use this alongside the full
study guide on Paper 2 for explanation and context — these notes are
for quick recall in the final stretch of revision.

## 1. Major teachings in the Hadiths of the Prophet

- Two groups of passages are set for close study: those relating to
  **individual conduct** and those relating to **life in the
  community**.
- Study each set passage for what it teaches Muslims to **believe**
  and how it teaches them to **act**.
- Passages are studied with reference to both the **original Arabic
  text** and the **English translation** — in the exam, passages
  appear in both.
- The exact passages set for study are listed in the syllabus's
  Appendix 2 and are reviewed periodically, so always revise from the
  current series' appendix, not an old list.
- Exam question: four passages are set, of which candidates choose any
  **two** to write on. [8 marks]

## 2. The history and importance of the Hadiths

- The **history of compilation**: how the Hadiths were gathered after
  the Prophet's death, and the earliest collections.
- The **main musnad and musannaf collections** and their compilers,
  and what each compiler did.
- The method of testing a Hadith's reliability: examining the chain of
  transmitters (**isnad**) and the wording (**matn**).
- The **six major Sunni collections** and the **four major Shi'a
  collections**.
- The **major themes** running through the Hadiths, both in the set
  passages and in similar material.
- How Hadiths are used in **legal thinking**, alongside the Qur'an,
  consensus (**ijma'**) and analogy (**qiyas**).
- Their overall **significance in Muslim thought and action**.
- Exam question: usually set in more than one part, essay-length
  answer required. [14 marks]

## 3. The period of rule of the Rightly Guided Caliphs and their importance as leaders

- The **main events** of the rule of each of the four Caliphs.
- Their **policies** for maintaining and expanding the Islamic state.
- Their individual **approaches to leadership** of the community.
- Their **main achievements** and the **main difficulties** each
  encountered.
- Their **significance as models of leadership**, including as
  examples for government today.
- Their importance as examples for how Muslim communities relate to
  other states.
- Note the boundary with Paper 1: the Caliphs' lives and roles *during
  the Prophet's lifetime* are examined under Paper 1 — this topic in
  Paper 2 covers only their period of **rule after** the Prophet's
  death.
- Exam question: one or two questions, usually multi-part,
  essay-length answers. [14 marks]

## 4. The Articles of Faith and the Pillars of Islam

**The Six Articles of Faith:**

- **God** — what Muslims believe about Him
- **Angels** — their nature and duties
- **Books** — their contents and purpose
- **Prophets** — their character and function
- **God's predestination and decree** — its meaning and significance
- **Resurrection and the Last Day** — the main events and significance

**Jihad** — its range of meanings: physical, mental and spiritual.

**The Five Pillars of Islam:**

- **Shahada** (declaration of faith) — the significance of what it
  contains
- **Salat** (prayer) — preparation, performance, importance,
  congregational Friday and festival prayers, prayer times, place of
  prayer, private and delayed prayer
- **Zakat** (alms-giving) — how it is performed and its significance
  in the community
- **Sawm** (fasting) — how it is observed, its significance, and who
  is exempted
- **Hajj** (pilgrimage) — the main observances and their significance

- Candidates should understand the **significance** of each belief and
  observance, not just describe it.
- Exam question: one or two questions, usually multi-part,
  essay-length answers. [14 marks]

## Quick recall checklist

- Can I name the two categories of Hadith passages set for study,
      and explain the individual-conduct vs community-life split?
- Do I know the difference between isnad and matn, and why each
      matters for testing a Hadith's reliability?
- Can I list the four Rightly Guided Caliphs in order and give one
      achievement and one difficulty for each?
- Can I state all Six Articles of Faith and all Five Pillars of
      Islam without prompting?
- Do I understand jihad's three meanings (physical, mental,
      spiritual), not just the most commonly assumed one?

## How to approach it

Because Question 1 and Question 2 are compulsory every year, prioritise
rock-solid recall of the set Hadith passages (topic 1) and the history
of the Hadiths (topic 2) before spending equal time on your two
optional questions from topics 3 and 4. For the Caliphs (topic 3), build
a simple four-row table — one row per Caliph, columns for main events,
achievements, difficulties — since exam questions often ask candidates
to compare or evaluate rather than just narrate. For the Articles of
Faith and Pillars (topic 4), practise explaining *significance* in one
sentence for each item on the list; "describe" answers that stop short
of significance are the most common way marks are lost on this topic.
