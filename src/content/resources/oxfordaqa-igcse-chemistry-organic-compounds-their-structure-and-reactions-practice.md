## Questions

**1.** Six organic compounds are listed.

| Letter | Formula |
|---|---|
| A | CH₃CH₂CH₂OH |
| B | HCOOH |
| C | CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ |
| D | C₄H₉OH |
| E | CH₃CH₂COOH |
| F | CH₃OH |

**(a)** Give the letters of **all** the alcohols. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the letter of the ester. **[1]**
**(c)** Name compound E. **[1]**

**2.** A student investigates methanol.

**(a)** She adds methanol to water and then adds universal indicator. State the colour she sees and the pH of the solution. **[2]**
**(b)** She adds a small piece of sodium to methanol. Give **two** observations. **[2]**
**(c)** The equation is 2CH₃OH + 2Na → 2CH₃ONa + H₂. Calculate the volume of hydrogen, in cm³, formed when 0.23 g of sodium reacts with excess methanol. **[3]**

**3.** Ethanol is used as a fuel in some camping stoves.

**(a)** Write a balanced equation for the complete combustion of ethanol, C₂H₅OH. **[2]**
**(b)** 9.2 g of ethanol burns completely. Work out the volume of CO₂ released, in dm³, under room conditions. **[3]**
**(c)** State **one** use of alcohols other than as fuels. **[1]**

**4.** An opened bottle of wine is left in a warm kitchen for a week. The wine starts to taste sour.

**(a)** Name the acid that forms, and state the type of reaction that has happened to the ethanol. **[2]**
**(b)** State what causes this change. **[1]**
**(c)** State **one** other way that ethanol can be converted into the same acid. **[1]**
**(d)** A student says, "Burning ethanol also turns it into this acid." Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**5.** A kettle descaler contains methanoic acid. Limescale is calcium carbonate, CaCO₃.

**(a)** Describe what you would see when the descaler is added to limescale, and give a test for the gas formed, with its result. **[2]**
**(b)** Write a balanced equation for the reaction of methanoic acid with calcium carbonate. The salt formed is calcium methanoate, (HCOO)₂Ca. **[2]**
**(c)** 1.25 g of calcium carbonate reacts with excess methanoic acid. Work out how many cm³ of CO₂ are given off under room conditions. **[3]**

**6.** A student measures the pH of two acid solutions. Both have a concentration of 0.10 mol/dm³.

| Solution | pH |
|---|---|
| Nitric acid | 1 |
| Propanoic acid | 3 |

**(a)** Explain why the propanoic acid solution has the higher pH. **[3]**
**(b)** Write an equation to show propanoic acid ionising in water. Use the correct arrow. **[2]**
**(c)** The student dilutes some nitric acid until its pH is 3. He says it is now a weak acid. Evaluate his claim. **[2]**

**7.** A student makes ethyl ethanoate from ethanol and ethanoic acid.

**(a)** State the name of the type of compound formed, and what must be added to the mixture for the reaction to happen. **[2]**
**(b)** Write a balanced equation for the reaction using structural formulae. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the maximum mass of ethyl ethanoate that can be made from 4.6 g of ethanol and excess ethanoic acid. **[3]**
**(d)** The student obtains less than this mass. Suggest **two** reasons. **[2]**

**8.** You do not need to have met these compounds before.

butyl methanoate · pentanoic acid · ethyl propanoate · hexanol

**(a)** Which **two** of the compounds are esters? **[2]**
**(b)** A compound has the structural formula CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃. State the family it belongs to and give its functional group. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why esters are used in perfumes. **[2]**

**9.** A food chemist makes a fruit flavouring from ethanol in two steps.

Step 1: some ethanol is oxidised to compound X.
Step 2: X reacts with more ethanol to make compound Y.

**(a)** Name compound X. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe a test with potassium carbonate that shows X is an acid. Include a balanced equation for the reaction. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass of potassium carbonate, K₂CO₃, that reacts exactly with 6.0 g of X. **[3]**
**(d)** Name Y, state the condition needed for step 2, and give **one** property of Y that makes it suitable as a flavouring. **[3]**

**10.** Two colourless liquids, J and K, both have the molecular formula C₃H₆O₂.

J is CH₃CH₂COOH. K is CH₃COOCH₃.

**(a)** Identify which liquid is the carboxylic acid. Explain your answer using the formulae. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe **one** chemical test to tell J and K apart. Give the result for each liquid. **[3]**
**(c)** J reacts with sodium carbonate: 2CH₃CH₂COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃CH₂COONa + H₂O + CO₂. 1.11 g of J reacts with excess sodium carbonate. Find the volume of gas released, in cm³, under room conditions. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A and F [1]; D [1].
**(b)** **C** [1]
**(c)** **Propanoic acid** [1]
*Examiner insight:* You must recognise D as an alcohol from its –OH, even though you never need to name it.

**2. (a)** Green [1]; pH 7 [1].
**(b)** Fizzing or bubbles [1]; the sodium gets smaller and disappears [1].
**(c)** Moles Na = 0.23 ÷ 23 = 0.010 mol [1]. Moles H₂ = 0.010 ÷ 2 = 0.0050 mol [1]. Volume = 0.0050 × 24 000 = **120 cm³** [1].
*Examiner insight:* The question asks for cm³; converting from dm³ is your job, and an answer of 0.12 with no unit change does not match what was asked.

**3. (a)** Correct formulae of all four substances [1]; C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O balanced [1].
**(b)** Moles ethanol = 9.2 ÷ 46 = 0.20 mol [1]. Moles CO₂ = 2 × 0.20 = 0.40 mol [1]. Volume = 0.40 × 24 = **9.6 dm³** [1].
**(c)** As a solvent (or ethanol in alcoholic drinks) [1].
*Examiner insight:* Write "0.20 mol ethanol gives 0.40 mol CO₂" so the 1 : 2 ratio in your method is visible.

**4. (a)** **Ethanoic acid** [1]; oxidation [1].
**(b)** Microbes (microbial action) using oxygen from the air [1].
**(c)** Warming it with a chemical oxidising agent, such as acidified potassium dichromate [1].
**(d)** Burning (complete combustion) produces carbon dioxide and water [1]; the molecule is broken down completely, whereas oxidation to ethanoic acid keeps the two-carbon chain [1].
*Examiner insight:* In part (a) "it goes off" or "fermentation" does not name a reaction type; the word needed is oxidation.

**5. (a)** Fizzing or bubbles [1]; the gas turns limewater milky (cloudy) [1].
**(b)** Correct formulae [1]; 2HCOOH + CaCO₃ → (HCOO)₂Ca + H₂O + CO₂ balanced [1].
**(c)** Moles CaCO₃ = 1.25 ÷ 100 = 0.0125 mol [1]. Ratio 1 : 1, so 0.0125 mol CO₂ [1]. Volume = 0.0125 × 24 000 = **300 cm³** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Two acid molecules are needed per carbonate; an equation with one HCOOH leaves hydrogen and oxygen unbalanced, so count atoms before moving on.

**6. (a)** Propanoic acid is a weak acid: it only partly ionises in water [1]. Nitric acid is a strong acid and ionises completely [1]. At the same concentration, propanoic acid therefore gives a lower concentration of H⁺ ions, and so a higher pH [1].
**(b)** CH₃CH₂COOH ⇌ CH₃CH₂COO⁻ + H⁺: correct ions [1]; reversible arrow ⇌ [1].
**(c)** The claim is wrong: nitric acid is still a strong acid [1]. It still ionises completely; it is now dilute, with fewer H⁺ ions per dm³, which is why its pH is higher [1].
*Examiner insight:* Part (a) needs the link to H⁺ concentration; stating "it is weak" and stopping there leaves the pH difference unexplained.

**7. (a)** An ester [1]; an acid catalyst [1].
**(b)** C₂H₅OH + CH₃COOH → CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O: correct ester formula [1]; water as the other product, balanced [1].
**(c)** Moles ethanol = 4.6 ÷ 46 = 0.10 mol [1]. Ratio 1 : 1, so 0.10 mol ethyl ethanoate [1]. Mr = 88, so mass = 0.10 × 88 = **8.8 g** [1].
**(d)** The reaction is reversible, so it does not go to completion [1]. Some ester is lost while it is being separated and purified (or some reactants take part in other reactions) [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Human error" is too vague for part (d); name a chemical reason such as reversibility.

**8. (a)** Butyl methanoate [1]; ethyl propanoate [1].
**(b)** Ester [1]; –COO– [1].
**(c)** Esters are volatile, so they evaporate easily and reach the nose [1]. They have distinctive, pleasant smells [1].
*Examiner insight:* "They smell nice" makes only one of the two points; add volatility, because a substance must evaporate before you can smell it.

**9. (a)** **Ethanoic acid** [1]
**(b)** Add potassium carbonate: it fizzes and the gas turns limewater milky [1]. 2CH₃COOH + K₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COOK + H₂O + CO₂: correct formulae [1]; balanced [1].
**(c)** Moles ethanoic acid = 6.0 ÷ 60 = 0.10 mol [1]. Ratio 2 : 1, so moles K₂CO₃ = 0.050 mol [1]. Mr K₂CO₃ = 138, so mass = 0.050 × 138 = **6.9 g** [1].
**(d)** Y is **ethyl ethanoate** [1]; an acid catalyst is needed [1]; it is volatile (or it has a distinctive smell) [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (c) many errors come from using a 1 : 1 ratio out of habit; read the ratio from your own balanced equation in (b).

**10. (a)** J is the carboxylic acid [1]. Its formula ends in –COOH, so the carboxyl carbon carries an O–H; in K the –COO– group has carbon on both sides, which makes K an ester [1].
**(b)** Add sodium carbonate solution (or another metal carbonate) to each [1]. J fizzes and gives carbon dioxide, which turns limewater milky [1]. K gives no fizzing [1].
**(c)** Mr J = 74, moles = 1.11 ÷ 74 = 0.015 mol [1]. Ratio 2 : 1, so moles CO₂ = 0.0075 mol [1]. Volume = 0.0075 × 24 000 = **180 cm³** [1].
*Examiner insight:* A test answer needs a result for both liquids; "J fizzes" alone does not show how you would tell K apart.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out an alcohol or ester you have not met by name.
- Writing CO₂ and H₂O as the products when ethanol is oxidised by microbes or an oxidising agent.
- Using one acid molecule per carbonate, so the equation will not balance.
- Forgetting the ⇌ arrow for weak-acid ionisation, or not linking "weak" to partial ionisation.
- Missing the acid catalyst when describing how an ester forms.
- Using a 1 : 1 mole ratio when the equation shows 2 : 1, or forgetting to convert dm³ to cm³.
- Giving a test result for only one of two substances in a "tell apart" question.
