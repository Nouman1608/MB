Subtopic 25.3 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers redshift of spectral lines, the redshift equation, the expanding Universe, Hubble's law and the Big Bang theory. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Hubble's law and the Big Bang study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-hubbles-law-and-the-big-bang-theory/); to apply the ideas, try the [Hubble's law and the Big Bang practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-hubbles-law-and-the-big-bang-theory-practice/).

## Equations

```
Δλ / λ ≈ Δf / f ≈ v / c          (data sheet; valid for v much less than c)
v ≈ H₀ d                          (recall; SI units only)
H₀ ≈ 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹ (use the value given in the question)
d = v / H₀        v = c Δλ / λ
```

## Redshift essentials

- Spectral lines of known elements appear at **longer wavelengths** than laboratory values.
- Every line shifts by the **same fraction** Δλ/λ, so the pattern is still recognised.
- Longer wavelength = lower frequency.
- **Receding** source → **redshift**; **approaching** source → **blueshift**.
- Divide by the **laboratory** (emitted) wavelength.

## The argument for expansion

| Step | Statement |
|---|---|
| Observation 1 | Light from almost all distant galaxies is redshifted |
| Deduction 1 | So they are moving away from us |
| Observation 2 | Redshift (so recession speed) increases with distance |
| Conclusion | The Universe is expanding |
| Not the centre | Every observer in an expanding Universe sees the same pattern |

## Hubble's law

- **Statement:** the recession speed of a galaxy is approximately proportional to its distance.
- Graph of v against d: straight line through the origin, gradient H₀.
- v from redshift; d from standard candles.
- Units: v in m s⁻¹, d in m, H₀ in s⁻¹.

## Hubble's law to the Big Bang

1. Galaxies moving apart now.
2. Run time backwards: they were closer together.
3. v ∝ d, so all galaxies were together at about the same time.
4. Universe began from an extremely small, hot, dense state and has expanded since: the **Big Bang theory**.

## Quick calculations (H₀ = 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹)

| Data | Result |
|---|---|
| 486.1 nm observed at 502.4 nm | v = 1.0 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹; d = 4.4 × 10²⁴ m |
| Δλ/λ = 0.010 | v = 3.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹; d = 1.3 × 10²⁴ m |
| d = 3.0 × 10²⁴ m, 656.3 nm line | v = 6.9 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹; observed at 671.4 nm |
| f falls from 4.57 to 4.48 × 10¹⁴ Hz | v = 5.9 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹, receding |

## Method for redshift-distance questions

1. Δλ = observed − laboratory wavelength (positive: receding).
2. v = c × Δλ/λ, dividing by the laboratory wavelength.
3. d = v/H₀ with v in m s⁻¹ and H₀ in s⁻¹, giving d in metres.
4. To predict another line, multiply its laboratory wavelength by (1 + Δλ/λ).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Redshift | Increase in wavelength of received radiation from a receding source |
| Blueshift | Decrease in wavelength from an approaching source |
| Recession speed | Speed at which a galaxy moves away from the observer |
| Hubble constant H₀ | Constant of proportionality between recession speed and distance |
| Expanding Universe | Distances between galaxies are increasing with time |
| Big Bang theory | The Universe began in an extremely hot, dense state and has expanded since |

## Exam traps

- Using the observed wavelength in the denominator.
- Getting the sign of Δf wrong and saying a galaxy with lower received frequency is approaching.
- Saying redshift means the galaxy "looks red".
- Concluding that the Milky Way is at the centre.
- Quoting H₀ in non-SI units.
- Stating Hubble's law without "proportional".
- Describing the Big Bang as an explosion of matter into empty space from one point.
