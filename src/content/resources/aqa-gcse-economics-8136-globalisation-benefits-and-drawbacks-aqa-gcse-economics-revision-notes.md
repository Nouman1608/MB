## The four things 3.2.4.4 asks

1. The **main features** of globalisation and the factors behind its growth, including **new technology** and the **operations of multinational companies**.
2. Benefits and drawbacks for **producers, workers and consumers in developed countries** (the UK).
3. Benefits and drawbacks for **producers, workers and consumers in less developed countries**.
4. **Moral, ethical and sustainability** considerations when UK producers trade with other countries.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Globalisation | The growing integration of the world's economies through trade, investment, technology and the movement of people |
| Interdependence | Countries relying on each other, so events in one economy affect others |
| Multinational company (MNC) | A firm with operations in more than one country |
| Foreign direct investment (FDI) | A firm from one country building or buying productive assets, such as factories, in another |
| Supply chain | The stages a product passes through, from raw materials to the final buyer; global supply chains cross several countries |
| Offshoring | Moving part of production from the home country to another country |
| Developed country | A country with high average income, such as the UK |
| Less developed country | A country with lower average income and less industrial development |
| Sustainability | Meeting today's needs without reducing the ability of future generations to meet theirs |
| Fair trade | Trading schemes that guarantee producers in poorer countries a minimum price and decent conditions |

## Features in one line each

- Trade: more exports and imports as a share of output.
- Supply chains: parts made in several countries.
- Investment: MNCs build and buy assets abroad.
- Capital: money moves quickly between countries.
- Labour: more migration for work.
- Culture: global brands and media.

## Causes: the chain to learn

**New technology** → cheaper container shipping and instant communication → lower cost of trading and of running production far away → firms trade and locate abroad more.

**MNCs** → seek lowest-cost locations and new markets → build factories abroad and split production across countries → more trade and investment, more interdependence.

Also: lower trade barriers, emerging economies with low labour costs, freer movement of capital.

## The who-gains grid

### UK (developed country)

| | Gains | Losses |
|---|---|---|
| Producers | Bigger markets, economies of scale, cheaper inputs | Low-cost foreign competition; supply-chain risk |
| Workers | Jobs in exporters and in foreign MNCs based in the UK; high-skill service jobs | Manufacturing jobs offshored; structural unemployment; wage pressure in import-competing jobs |
| Consumers | Lower prices, more choice | Disruption when supply chains break; fewer locally made goods |

### Less developed countries

| | Gains | Losses |
|---|---|---|
| Producers | Export markets; supplying MNCs; new technology | Pushed out by MNCs; reliance on a few products or buyers |
| Workers | New, often better-paid jobs; skills | Low wages, long hours, unsafe conditions; jobs can move on |
| Consumers | More goods; rising incomes | Profits leave the country; local products displaced |

**Linking idea:** one MNC decision can create jobs abroad and remove them in the UK at the same time.

## Ethics and sustainability checklist

- Who checks pay and safety in overseas suppliers' factories?
- Is production moving to escape stricter employment or environmental laws?
- Transport emissions are an external cost not included in prices.
- Cheap goods encourage over-consumption of finite resources and more waste.
- Fair trade and supply-chain checks are responses, but they raise prices.
- These are value judgements: economics shows who gains and loses, not what is "right".

## Calculation patterns

**Unit cost saving from offshoring.** UK cost £24 a unit; cost abroad £15 plus £3 transport = £18. Saving = £6, which is 6 ÷ 24 × 100 = **25%**.

**Share of trade.** Exports of 300 and imports of 340 in an economy with output (GDP) of 1,000: trade = (300 + 340) ÷ 1,000 × 100 = **64%** of GDP. A rising figure over time is one sign of globalisation.

**Percentage change.** A firm's overseas sales rise from 40 to 58: (58 − 40) ÷ 40 × 100 = **45%** increase.

## Exam traps

- Globalisation is not just "more trade": name investment, supply chains or technology as well.
- Always name the **group** (producer, worker, consumer) and the **country type**.
- "Cheaper" needs a reason: lower labour costs, economies of scale or competition.
- An MNC is defined by operating in more than one country, not by size.
- In ethics questions, tie the issue to a UK producer's decision, such as where to buy.
- An evaluation needs a judgement that says what the outcome depends on.

## Evaluation "it depends on" list

- The skills of the workers affected, and whether retraining is available.
- How well workers and the environment are protected by regulation abroad.
- Whether income earned in a less developed country is spent on health, education and infrastructure.
- The time period: job losses come quickly; gains from new industries may take years.
- Whether supply chains are reliable or easily disrupted.

## Quick self-test

1. Define globalisation.
2. Name the two factors behind globalisation that the specification names.
3. What is a multinational company?
4. Give one way new technology has increased globalisation.
5. Give one benefit of globalisation for UK consumers.
6. Give one drawback of globalisation for UK workers.
7. Give one benefit of globalisation for workers in a less developed country.
8. Why might profits from an MNC's factory not benefit the country where it is located?
9. Give one sustainability concern about global trade.
10. A unit costs £50 to make in the UK and £32 abroad, plus £6 transport. Find the percentage saving.
11. What does "interdependence" mean?
12. Why are ethical issues described as value judgements?

### Answers

1. The growing integration of the world's economies through trade, investment, technology and the movement of people.
2. New technology and the operations of multinational companies.
3. A firm with operations in more than one country.
4. Cheaper container shipping, or the internet allowing firms to manage production and sell across the world.
5. Lower prices or wider choice.
6. Jobs lost when production moves to lower-cost countries.
7. New jobs, often better paid than local alternatives, or new skills.
8. They may be sent back to the MNC's home country.
9. Transport emissions, or the use of finite resources and waste.
10. Cost abroad 32 + 6 = £38; saving £12; 12 ÷ 50 × 100 = **24%**.
11. Countries rely on each other, so events in one economy affect others.
12. Economics can show who gains and who loses, but deciding whether that is fair depends on opinion.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is globalisation good or bad for the UK?**
It brings lower prices and larger markets but costs some workers their jobs, so the answer depends on which group you look at.

**What is the difference between globalisation and free trade?**
Free trade is trade without barriers; globalisation is wider and includes investment, supply chains, technology and migration.
