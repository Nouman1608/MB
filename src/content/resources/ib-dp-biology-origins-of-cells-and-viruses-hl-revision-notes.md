## A2.1 Origins of cells: the chain of ideas

Read this as a sequence. Each step needs the one before.

1. **A2.1.1** Early Earth: no free oxygen, so no ozone and more ultraviolet light; more carbon dioxide and methane; higher temperatures. Carbon compounds formed spontaneously by processes that do not now occur.
2. **A2.1.4** Evidence: Miller-Urey made amino acids from water, methane, ammonia and hydrogen with sparks.
3. **A2.1.5** Fatty acids coalesce into spherical bilayers (vesicles), giving a compartment.
4. **A2.1.6** RNA both stores information and catalyses reactions.
5. **A2.1.2 and A2.1.3** Cells are the smallest self-sustaining units; their origin needs catalysis, self-replication, self-assembly and compartmentalisation.
6. **A2.1.7 to A2.1.9** LUCA, its date, and its likely home near hydrothermal vents.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pre-biotic | Before cells existed |
| Protocell | A hypothesised early membrane-bound structure with simple chemistry |
| Vesicle | Spherical bilayer, hydrophobic tails inward |
| Ribozyme | RNA molecule with catalytic activity |
| LUCA | Last universal common ancestor: the most recent organism from which all life descends |
| Molecular clock | Dating by counting sequence differences between species |
| Capsid | Protein coat of a virus |
| Envelope | Host membrane layer around some viruses |
| Prophage | Phage DNA integrated into the host chromosome |
| Obligate parasite | Cannot reproduce without a host |

## Evaluating Miller-Urey

| Supports the idea | Limits the idea |
|---|---|
| Organic molecules formed from simple inorganic ones | The gas mixture is thought by many to be too reducing |
| An energy source drove the reactions | Only small molecules formed: no polymer, membrane or replicator |
| Amino acids also occur in meteorites | Shows what is possible, not what happened |

Always give both columns when the command word is evaluate, then reach a judgement.

## Living versus non-living

A cell shows the characteristics of life using its own machinery: metabolism, response, homeostasis, growth, reproduction, nutrition and excretion. Viruses are non-living because they have no cytoplasm, no ribosomes, few or no enzymes, and no metabolism of their own. They reproduce only inside a host.

## RNA first

- RNA is replicated by complementary base pairing.
- RNA can fold and catalyse, so it could act as gene and enzyme.
- Evidence: ribozymes in the ribosome still catalyse peptide bond formation.
- Later: DNA took over information storage, proteins took over catalysis.

## LUCA: evidence and dating

| Evidence for LUCA | Link to vents | Dating methods |
|---|---|---|
| Universal genetic code | Fossilised life in ancient vent precipitates | Radiometric dating of rocks |
| Genes shared by all organisms | Conserved sequences from genomic analysis | Microbial fossils in old rock |
| Other lineages probably became extinct | Anaerobic, hydrogen-using organism inferred | Molecular clocks |

Remember the nature of science point: the first protocells did not fossilise and pre-biotic conditions cannot be recreated, so hypotheses here are hard to test.

## A2.3 Viruses

### Common features (A2.3.1)

Small fixed size, DNA or RNA genetic material, protein capsid, no cytoplasm, few or no enzymes.

### Diversity (A2.3.2)

| Virus | Genetic material | Enveloped? |
|---|---|---|
| Bacteriophage lambda | double-stranded DNA | no |
| Coronaviruses | single-stranded RNA | yes |
| HIV | single-stranded RNA | yes |

### Lytic versus lysogenic (A2.3.3 and A2.3.4), bacteriophage lambda

| Stage | Lytic cycle | Lysogenic cycle |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Attachment, DNA injected | Attachment, DNA injected |
| Viral DNA | Copied at once | Integrated as a prophage |
| Host cell | Makes new phages, then bursts | Survives and divides |
| Outcome | New phages released by lysis | Prophage copied to daughter cells |
| Switch | None | Stress can trigger induction, then lytic cycle |

Method for a lytic cycle answer: attach, inject, synthesise, assemble, lyse. Name the order every time.

### Origins and evolution (A2.3.5 and A2.3.6)

- Diversity suggests several origins.
- Shared extreme obligate parasitism means common features may be convergent evolution.
- The genetic code is shared with living organisms.
- Fast evolution: short generations, large numbers, high mutation rate, no proofreading in many RNA viruses.
- Influenza: gradual change by point mutations, sudden change by mixing segments; vaccines reformulated.
- HIV: error-prone reverse transcriptase; resistance arises; combination drug treatment.

## Must-know distinctions

- **LUCA is not the first life.** It is the most recent organism that all living things descend from. Other lineages probably existed and went extinct, likely through competition with LUCA and its descendants.
- **Gene versus enzyme in RNA world.** One molecule type did both jobs. Later they separated: DNA stored information and proteins catalysed.
- **Capsid versus envelope.** The capsid is protein and every virus has one. The envelope is host membrane and only some viruses have it.
- **Influenza change.** Small steady change comes from point mutations. Sudden large change comes from mixing of genome segments between strains.
- **Pre-biotic chemistry versus cells.** Chemistry produced molecules. Compartments, replication and catalysis together are needed before something counts as a cell.
- **Evidence versus explanation.** A universal genetic code is evidence for common ancestry. It does not say where the ancestor lived; that comes from the vent fossils and conserved sequences.

## Small worked reminders

**Rate of amino acid formation.** 14 µmol on day 3 and 41 µmol on day 7: (41 − 14) ÷ (7 − 3) = 6.75 µmol per day.

**Time scale.** If life appears 1060 million years after a 4540 million year old Earth formed, that is 1060 ÷ 4540 × 100 = 23% of the way through. Life has existed for the remaining 77%.

**Lambda burst size.** 3.6 × 10⁹ phages from 1.5 × 10⁷ infected cells gives 240 per cell.

**Mutations per genome.** Genome length × error rate. A value below 1 means most copies are identical to the parent, but huge numbers of copies still produce many variants.

## Command words for this unit

- **Describe**: give the stages in order, for example the five phases of the lytic cycle.
- **Explain**: give the reason as well, for example why a compartment lets internal chemistry differ.
- **Discuss**: give more than one view, for example why viruses are classed as non-living even though they have genes.
- **Evaluate**: weigh strengths and limitations, then judge. Use this for Miller-Urey and for evidence on viral origins.
- **Outline**: a brief account, for example the approaches used to date LUCA.

## Method boxes

**Actual size from a micrograph.** Convert the scale bar to the same units. Magnification = bar length ÷ real length. Real size = image size ÷ magnification. Show the units.

**Burst size.** Burst size = phages released ÷ infected cells.

**Percentage change.** (new − old) ÷ old × 100.

## Quick self-test

1. State two features that all viruses share.
2. A micrograph shows a virus 60 mm wide at ×300 000. Calculate its actual diameter in nm.
3. A virus is 150 nm across. How wide is its image at ×200 000, in mm?
4. Name the structure that lets fatty acids create an inside different from the outside.
5. What is a ribozyme, and where is one still used today?
6. A culture has 8.0 × 10⁵ infected cells. After lysis it holds 2.0 × 10⁸ phages. Calculate the burst size.
7. In an experiment, amino acids rise from 20 µmol on day 2 to 62 µmol on day 8. Calculate the mean rate and the percentage increase.
8. A genome of 11 000 nucleotides has an error rate of 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ per nucleotide per copy. Calculate mutations per genome copy.
9. Give two reasons why a lambda prophage is passed on without killing its host.
10. State one reason the Miller-Urey result does not prove how life began.

### Answers

1. Any two: nucleic acid as genetic material, a protein capsid, small fixed size, no cytoplasm, few or no enzymes.
2. 60 mm = 60 000 000 nm; ÷ 300 000 = **200 nm**.
3. 150 nm × 200 000 = 30 000 000 nm = **30 mm**.
4. A vesicle (spherical bilayer).
5. An RNA molecule with catalytic activity; in the ribosome, where it catalyses peptide bond formation.
6. 2.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 8.0 × 10⁵ = **250**.
7. Rate = 42 ÷ 6 = **7.0 µmol per day**; increase = 42 ÷ 20 × 100 = **210%**.
8. 11 000 × 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ = **0.22**.
9. It is integrated into the host chromosome and a repressor keeps lytic genes off, so it is copied with the host DNA at division.
10. It shows what can happen, not what did; the gas mixture may not match early Earth, and no polymers or membranes were made.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling viruses living, or saying they respire. They are non-living.
- Writing that Miller and Urey created life instead of amino acids.
- Giving only strengths or only limitations when asked to evaluate.
- Stating that early Earth had oxygen, or leaving out the effect of no ozone layer.
- Mixing lytic and lysogenic steps, for example saying the host bursts in lysogeny.
- Forgetting that the capsid stays outside after a phage injects its DNA.
- Skipping unit conversion between mm and nm in magnification questions.
- Naming HIV as DNA-based, when it has an RNA genome and uses reverse transcriptase.
