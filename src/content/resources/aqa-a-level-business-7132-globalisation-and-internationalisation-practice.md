Revise first with the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-globalisation-and-internationalisation/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-globalisation-and-internationalisation-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** A UK clothing brand allows a company in Turkey to make and sell its designs under the brand name for a royalty. Which entry method is this? **[1]**

A: Export

B: Licensing

C: Direct investment

D: Off-shoring

**2.** Which **one** of the following is an example of re-shoring? **[1]**

A: A firm moves its call centre from the UK to India

B: A firm builds a new factory in Poland

C: A firm moves production from China back to the UK

D: A firm starts selling online abroad

**3.** Define "multinational". **[2]**

**4.** A licensee sells £10m of a firm's branded goods and pays a 6% royalty. Calculate the annual royalty. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a UK business might target an emerging economy. **[3]**

## Section B: Structured questions

Torvane Appliances is choosing a country for its first overseas sales. The table shows the importance (weight) of each factor and scores from 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent).

| Factor | Weight | Mavora | Delmar | Tessin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Market size | 0.40 | 3 | 4 | 2 |
| Growth | 0.30 | 5 | 3 | 4 |
| Political stability | 0.15 | 2 | 5 | 4 |
| Ease of entry | 0.15 | 4 | 3 | 5 |

**6.** Calculate the weighted score for each country. **[6]**

**7.** Torvane's managers argue that Delmar is the best market. Assess the limitations of using this table to make the decision. **[6]**

**8.** Torvane makes a kettle in the UK at a cost of £40 a unit. Producing it overseas would cost £28 manufacturing, £4 shipping, a tariff of 5% of the £28 manufacturing cost, and £1.60 other costs. Calculate the overseas cost per unit and the annual saving on 100,000 units. **[4]**

**9.** The set-up cost of the overseas factory is £800,000. Calculate the payback period and comment on whether the move is attractive. **[4]**

**10.** Explain **two** reasons why Torvane might later decide to bring production back to the UK. **[4]**

**11.** Torvane is deciding between exporting to Delmar and building a factory there. Analyse **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of each. **[8]**

**12.** Evaluate the view that a multinational should adapt its products to each country rather than sell a standard global product. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. The brand owner grants rights for a fee. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. Re-shoring is bringing production back to the home country. **[1]**

**3.** A business that owns or controls operations [1] in more than one country [1]. **[2]**

**4.** 6% × £10m [1] = **£600,000** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Emerging economies often have fast-growing middle classes [1] with rising incomes, so demand for branded goods and services is increasing [1], giving the business a chance to grow when its home market may be slow [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Mavora: 0.40 × 3 + 0.30 × 5 + 0.15 × 2 + 0.15 × 4 = 1.2 + 1.5 + 0.3 + 0.6 = **3.60** [2]. Delmar: 1.6 + 0.9 + 0.75 + 0.45 = **3.70** [2]. Tessin: 0.8 + 1.2 + 0.6 + 0.75 = **3.35** [2]. **[6]** (Award one mark for correct method and one for the answer in each case.)

**7.** The weights and scores are subjective [1], so different managers could produce a different ranking [1]. Delmar leads Mavora by only 0.10, so a small change in weights could change the result [1]. The table ignores factors not listed, such as exchange rates, culture and competition [1], and it does not show the size of the risks, for example a politically unstable country could be disastrous even with a good score overall [1]. It also depends on the quality of the data used for the scores [1]. **[6]**

**8.** Tariff = 5% × £28 = £1.40 [1]. Cost = £28 + £4 + £1.40 + £1.60 = **£35.00** [1]. Saving per unit = £40 − £35 = £5 [1]; annual saving = £5 × 100,000 = **£500,000** [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Payback = £800,000 ÷ £500,000 [1] = **1.6 years** [1]. This is a quick payback, so the move looks attractive financially [1]. But it ignores redundancy costs, exchange rate risk, quality, and wage rises overseas that could shrink the saving [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Reason 1: rising costs abroad [1]. If wages and shipping costs overseas rise, the saving shrinks, and production at home may become competitive again [1]. Reason 2: quality and responsiveness [1]. Long supply chains cause delays and quality problems, and customers may value fast delivery or a UK-made label [1]. (Credit also: supply chain disruption; government incentives.) **[4]**

**11.** Exporting, advantage: low cost and risk, because Torvane uses its existing factory and does not need heavy investment [1][1]. Exporting, disadvantage: tariffs, transport costs and exchange rate risk, with less control over local marketing [1][1]. Direct investment, advantage: closer to customers, avoiding tariffs and transport, with full control over production and brand [1][1]. Direct investment, disadvantage: large cost and risk, including political risk, and the need to learn local rules and management [1][1]. **[8]**

**12.** For adaptation: tastes, laws and culture differ, so a product suited to one country may fail in another [1]. Adapting menus, languages, sizes and prices can raise sales and customer loyalty [1]. Local rules or standards may require changes [1]. Local managers know the market better [1]. Against: adaptation raises costs because production runs are shorter and marketing differs by country [1]. A standard product gives economies of scale and a consistent global brand [1]. Control of quality and image is easier [1]. Factors: how different the markets are [1], how strong the cost pressures are in the industry (for example electronics against food) [1], and how important the brand is [1]. Judgement: most firms use a mixture, with a global core and local variations [1], and the balance depends on the product, since basic commodities suit standardisation while food and services need more adaptation [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* Strong answers weigh costs against benefits for the business in the case and finish with a judgement that states what it depends on.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating off-shoring as always cheaper.
- Ignoring political, legal and cultural risk.
- Confusing licensing with direct investment.
- Taking a weighted score as certain without questioning weights.
- Leaving out a conclusion on the entry method or market.
