## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *primary research*. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** methods of secondary research. **[2]**

**3.** Which **one** of the following is an example of qualitative data? A: 45% of customers visit weekly. B: 120 people bought the product. C: "The packaging looks cheap." D: Average spend is £6.50. **[1]**

**4.** Kenwell Kitchens sold £210,000 of kitchens last year in a regional market worth £1,400,000. Calculate Kenwell Kitchens' market share. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** drawback of using a focus group. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **one** reason why a business carries out market research before launching a new product. **[3]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Ruan Pizza surveyed 320 people about how they prefer to order takeaway food.

| Method | Number of people |
|---|---|
| Mobile app | 144 |
| Website | 80 |
| Phone call | 64 |
| In person | 32 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of people who prefer to order by mobile app. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage who prefer to order online, by app or website combined. **[2]**
**(c)** Analyse what these results suggest Ruan Pizza should do. **[4]**

**8.** The market for school uniform in a town was worth £900,000 in Year 1 and £990,000 in Year 2. Pinwell Uniforms sold £180,000 in Year 1 and £188,100 in Year 2.

**(a)** Calculate Pinwell Uniforms' market share in each year. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what the figures show about Pinwell's performance. **[3]**

**9.** Gwithow Games has a 12% share of the board game market and sales of £96,000. Calculate the size of the market. **[2]**

**10.** Explain **one** benefit and **one** drawback of a business using internet research. **[4]**

**11.** Treen Gelato is a new ice cream business with £600 to spend on research. It wants to know whether people in the town would buy luxury ice cream and what flavours they prefer. Analyse which **two** market research methods Treen Gelato should use. **[6]**

**12.** Polwyn Paints, a large manufacturer, plans to spend £2 million launching a new range of eco-friendly paints across the UK. The marketing director wants a national survey of 5,000 people. The finance director says secondary research using online reports and trade magazines is enough. Evaluate which approach Polwyn Paints should take. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Information collected first-hand [1] by or for the business, for its own purpose, such as through a survey [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Internet research, printed press such as newspapers or magazines [1] [1]. Also accept government statistics or competitors' websites as examples of internet research. **[2]**

**3.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** £210,000 ÷ £1,400,000 × 100 [1] = **15%** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Only a small number of people take part [1], so their views may not represent the whole market, and decisions based on them could be wrong [1]. Also accept: one strong personality may sway others; costly to run. **[2]**

**6.** It shows whether there is demand for the product [1], so the business can change or drop the idea before spending on production and promotion [1], avoiding the cost of launching a product nobody buys [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** 144 ÷ 320 × 100 [1] = **45%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 144 + 80 = 224 [1]; 224 ÷ 320 × 100 = **70%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The app is the most popular single method at 45% [1], so Ruan Pizza should make sure it offers app ordering, or improve its app [1]. With 70% preferring online, investing in online ordering is likely to reach most customers and increase orders [1]. However, 30% still prefer phone or in-person, so it should keep these options to avoid losing those customers [1]. **[4]**

**8. (a)** Year 1: £180,000 ÷ £900,000 × 100 = **20%**; Year 2: £188,100 ÷ £990,000 × 100 = **19%** [1] [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Pinwell's sales rose by £8,100 [1], but its market share fell from 20% to 19% [1], because the market grew by 10% while Pinwell's sales grew by only 4.5%, so rivals gained more of the growth [1]. **[3]**

**9.** £96,000 ÷ 0.12 [1] = **£800,000** [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Benefit: it is quick and often free [1], so a business with little money can learn about rivals' prices and products without paying for a survey [1]. Drawback: the information may be out of date or inaccurate [1], so decisions based on it could be wrong, wasting money [1]. **[4]**

**11.** With only £600, Treen Gelato needs low-cost methods [1]. A questionnaire handed out in the town centre or online is cheap and can reach many people [1], giving quantitative data on how many would buy luxury ice cream and at what price [1]. A small tasting focus group of local people [1] would show which flavours they prefer and why, which a questionnaire cannot fully explain [1]. Together they answer both questions within the budget; a national survey would be far too costly [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Knowledge: primary research such as a survey is collected first-hand; secondary research already exists [1].
Application: Polwyn is spending £2 million on a national launch [1]; eco-friendly paint is a new range, so specific customer views may not exist in published reports [1].
Analysis: a survey of 5,000 people gives reliable quantitative data on demand, price and which customers care about eco-friendly products [1]; this reduces the risk of losing a large share of the £2 million [1]; secondary research is cheaper and quicker and shows market trends and competitors' products [1].
Evaluation: the survey costs time and money and only shows "how many", not why [1]; secondary data may be out of date and is available to rivals too [1]. Judgement: use both, starting with secondary research on trends and rivals, then the survey to answer Polperro's own questions, because with £2 million at stake the cost of the survey is small compared with the risk [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* The judgement should be weighed against what is at stake; the size of the launch is the key fact in the stem.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining primary research as "research done first" rather than first-hand research.
- Calling a focus group quantitative.
- Using the wrong total in a percentage, such as dividing by the number who chose one option.
- Concluding that a business is failing because its share fell, without noting its sales rose.
- Suggesting expensive methods for a business with a tiny budget.
- Repeating figures from a table without saying what the business should do.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I choose between two research methods in an evaluate question?**
Compare them on cost, time and what the business needs to know, then use the size of the decision in the case to justify your choice.

**What should I write after calculating a percentage from survey data?**
Say what the figure means for the business, such as which option to offer, and note any limitation, such as who was asked.
