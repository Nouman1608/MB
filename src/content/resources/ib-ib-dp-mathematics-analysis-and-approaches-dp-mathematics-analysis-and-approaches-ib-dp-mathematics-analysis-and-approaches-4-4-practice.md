correlation, Pearson's r and the regression line of y on x. Learn the methods first with the

All data are invented. Questions marked (calculator allowed) need a GDC; do the others by hand.

## Section A

**1.** Describe the correlation indicated by each value of r.

**(a)** r = 0.87 **[1]**
**(b)** r = −0.21 **[1]**
**(c)** r = −0.995 **[1]**

**2.** A line of best fit drawn by eye passes through the mean point (8, 55) and the point (2, 31). Find the
equation of the line in the form y = ax + b. **[2]**

**3.** For taxi journeys in a city, the regression line of fare y (dollars) on distance x (km) is
y = 0.85x + 3.2. Interpret, in context,

**(a)** the value 0.85; **[1]**
**(b)** the value 3.2. **[1]**

**4.** Across a year, weekly sales of hot chocolate and weekly sales of gloves at a shopping centre show a
strong positive correlation. A manager says that selling more hot chocolate makes people buy gloves.
Explain why this conclusion is not justified. **[2]**

**5.** For nine adults, the regression line of hours of sleep y on hours of phone use x is y = −0.416x + 9.03.
The values of x ranged from 1 to 7.

**(a)** Estimate the hours of sleep for an adult who uses a phone for 5.5 hours. **[1]**
**(b)** Comment on using the line to estimate the sleep of an adult who uses a phone for 12 hours. **[1]**

**6.** A scatter diagram shows points lying close to a parabola, yet r = 0.03. Explain how this is possible.
**[1]**

## Section B

**7.** (calculator allowed) A shop recorded the midday temperature, x °C, and the number of ice creams sold,
y, on eight days.

| x | 14 | 17 | 19 | 21 | 23 | 25 | 27 | 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 62 | 80 | 85 | 101 | 108 | 122 | 130 | 151 |

**(a)** Write down the value of r. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the correlation in context. **[1]**
**(c)** Write down the equation of the regression line of y on x. **[2]**
**(d)** Estimate the number of ice creams sold on a day when the temperature is 24 °C. **[2]**
**(e)** Comment on the reliability of using the line when the temperature is 40 °C. **[1]**
**(f)** Interpret the gradient of the line in context. **[1]**

**8.** (calculator allowed) Five runners recorded hours of training per week, x, and their 5 km time in
minutes, y.

| x | 2 | 3 | 5 | 6 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 30 | 28 | 26 | 25 | 21 |

**(a)** Find the mean point. **[2]**
**(b)** A student draws a line by eye through the mean point and (1, 29.2). Find its equation. **[2]**
**(c)** Write down r and the equation of the regression line of y on x. **[3]**
**(d)** Use the regression line to estimate the time for a runner who trains 4 hours per week. **[1]**
**(e)** Explain why the regression line is a better model than the student's line. **[1]**

**9.** (calculator allowed) The points (1, 3), (2, 5), (3, 6), (4, 8), (5, 9), (6, 11), (7, 12) and (8, 2) are
recorded.

**(a)** Find r for all eight points. **[1]**
**(b)** Find r for the first seven points only. **[1]**
**(c)** The last point was recorded wrongly. Explain what this shows about relying on r alone. **[2]**

**10.** A researcher records the shoe size and the height of 40 adults and wants to estimate an adult's
height from their shoe size.

**(a)** State which variable should be the independent variable. **[1]**
**(b)** State which regression line should be used, and why. **[1]**

**11.** A regression line for the mass y (kg) of a breed of puppy against its age x (weeks), from data for
ages 8 to 20 weeks, is y = 0.62x + 1.1. Explain whether the value 1.1 is a meaningful prediction of a
puppy's mass at birth. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Strong positive linear correlation [1].
**(b)** Weak negative linear correlation [1].
**(c)** Very strong negative linear correlation [1]. **[3]**

**2.** Gradient = (55 − 31)/(8 − 2) = 4 [1]; y − 55 = 4(x − 8), so y = 4x + 23 [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** Each extra kilometre adds about $0.85 to the fare, on average [1].
**(b)** About $3.20 is the predicted fare for a journey of 0 km: a fixed starting charge [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Correlation does not show causation [1]. Both sales are likely to rise in cold weather, a third
variable that drives both [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** −0.416 × 5.5 + 9.03 ≈ 6.74 hours [1].
**(b)** 12 hours is outside the data range (extrapolation), so the estimate is unreliable [1]. **[2]**

**6.** r measures only linear correlation; a strong curved relationship can have r close to 0 [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** r = 0.997 (0.99677...) [2].
**(b)** Very strong positive linear correlation: hotter days are associated with more ice creams sold [1].
**(c)** y = 5.45x − 15.1 (a = 5.4545..., b = −15.125) [2].
**(d)** y ≈ 5.4545 × 24 − 15.125 [1] ≈ 116 ice creams [1].
**(e)** 40 °C is outside the data range, so this is extrapolation and unreliable [1].
**(f)** Each 1 °C rise is associated with about 5.45 more ice creams sold, on average [1]. **[9]**

**8. (a)** x̄ = 25/5 = 5 [1]; ȳ = 130/5 = 26 [1].
**(b)** Gradient = (26 − 29.2)/(5 − 1) = −0.8 [1]; y = −0.8x + 30 [1].
**(c)** r = −0.996 [1]; y = −1.23x + 32.2 [2].
**(d)** −1.2333 × 4 + 32.167 ≈ 27.2 minutes [1].
**(e)** The regression line is calculated to fit all the points as closely as possible; the student's
gradient (−0.8) is much shallower than the trend in the data [1]. **[9]**

**9. (a)** r ≈ 0.354 [1].
**(b)** r ≈ 0.997 [1].
**(c)** One point changed a very strong positive correlation into a weak one [1]; r should be checked
against the scatter diagram, and errors corrected or removed before it is interpreted [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** Shoe size [1].
**(b)** The regression line of y (height) on x (shoe size), because it is designed to predict y from a given
x [1]. **[2]**

**11.** At birth x = 0, which is well outside the data range of 8 to 20 weeks [1], so 1.1 kg is an
extrapolation and may not be a reliable birth mass; growth is unlikely to be linear from birth [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the word "linear" when describing r.
- Interpreting the gradient without "on average" or without units.
- Using rounded coefficients in question 7(d) and 8(d).
- Treating correlation as proof of causation in question 4.
- Not mentioning the data range when commenting on reliability.
