These notes condense section **3.1.2.3 The ideal gas equation** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 1. The [Ideal gas equation study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-the-ideal-gas-equation/) explains each method with worked examples, and the [Ideal gas equation practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-the-ideal-gas-equation-practice/) test them. ## What the specification asks

- pV = nRT **with the variables in SI units**.
- **Use the equation in calculations**, including rearranging for any unknown.
- **R is not recalled**: it is given (8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹).
- Practical opportunity: the **Mᵣ of a volatile liquid**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pressure, p | Force per unit area; SI unit Pa (1 Pa = 1 N m⁻²) |
| Volume, V | SI unit m³ |
| Amount, n | Moles of gas |
| Gas constant, R | 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, given in questions |
| Absolute temperature, T | Temperature in kelvin; °C + 273 |
| Volatile liquid | A liquid with a low boiling point that vaporises easily |

## Conversions table

| From | To SI | Operation |
|---|---|---|
| kPa | Pa | × 10³ |
| MPa | Pa | × 10⁶ |
| dm³ | m³ | × 10⁻³ |
| cm³ | m³ | × 10⁻⁶ |
| °C | K | + 273 |

And back again at the end: m³ × 10³ = dm³; m³ × 10⁶ = cm³; Pa ÷ 10³ = kPa.

## Rearrangements

```
p = nRT / V        V = nRT / p        n = pV / RT        T = pV / nR
Mᵣ = mRT / pV      (from n = m / Mᵣ)
```

## Method in steps

1. List p, V, n, T with units as given.
2. Convert each to SI on its own line.
3. Write the rearranged equation in symbols.
4. Substitute and calculate, keeping extra figures.
5. Convert the answer to the unit asked for and round.
6. Sanity check: 1 mol at 298 K and 100 kPa is about 0.0248 m³ (24.8 dm³).

## Typical question types

| Type | Key step |
|---|---|
| Find p, V, n or T | Convert, rearrange, substitute |
| Find Mᵣ of a gas | Mᵣ = mRT / pV, then match to a formula |
| Gas made in a reaction | Moles of reactant → mole ratio → moles of gas → pV = nRT |
| Fixed amount, new conditions | p₁V₁/T₁ = p₂V₂/T₂, temperatures in K; other units cancel |
| Number of molecules in a gas | n from pV = nRT, then × Avogadro constant |

## Worked reminders

**Pressure.** 2.20 g of CO₂ (Mᵣ 44.0) in 1.50 dm³ at 50 °C.
n = 0.0500 mol; T = 323 K; V = 1.50 × 10⁻³ m³.
p = 0.0500 × 8.31 × 323 / 1.50 × 10⁻³ = 8.95 × 10⁴ Pa = **89.5 kPa**.

**Gas from a reaction.** 0.654 g of zinc (Aᵣ 65.4) with excess acid: Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂.
n(H₂) = 0.0100 mol; at 298 K and 100 kPa,
V = 0.0100 × 8.31 × 298 / 1.00 × 10⁵ = 2.48 × 10⁻⁴ m³ = **248 cm³**.

**New conditions.** A sealed can holds gas at 300 kPa and 20 °C; it warms to 60 °C at fixed volume.
p₂ = 300 × 333 / 293 = **341 kPa**. (Using 60/20 gives 900 kPa, which is wrong.)

## Volatile-liquid practical: what each error does

Mᵣ = mRT / pV, so:

- V measured too small (liquid not all vaporised, vapour condenses) → Mᵣ **too high**.
- m recorded too large (some liquid lost before it enters the syringe) → Mᵣ **too high**.
- T used too high (vapour cooler than the oven reading) → Mᵣ **too high**.
- Small V reading → large percentage uncertainty; use more liquid.

Mass by difference: weigh the hypodermic syringe before and after injecting.

## Precision

Answers usually to 3 s.f. because R = 8.31. Use fewer if any data value has fewer.

## Exam traps

- Unconverted kPa, cm³ or °C in pV = nRT.
- cm³ ÷ 1000 written as m³.
- Kelvin forgotten in p₁V₁/T₁ = p₂V₂/T₂.
- Final answer left in m³ or Pa.
- Mole ratio from the equation ignored.
- Answer far from the size the sanity check predicts, not questioned.

## Self-test

1. Convert 2.5 MPa to Pa.
2. Convert 750 cm³ to m³.
3. Convert −40 °C to K.
4. Write the expression for Mᵣ in terms of m, R, T, p and V.
5. Calculate the amount of gas in a 5.00 dm³ flask at 200 kPa and 350 K.
6. Calculate the volume, in dm³, of 8.00 g of O₂ (Mᵣ 32.0) at 27 °C and 150 kPa.
7. At what temperature does 0.100 mol of gas occupy 3.00 dm³ at 101 kPa?
8. 50.0 cm³ of gas at 18 °C is heated to 100 °C at constant pressure. Find the new volume.
9. When can you leave pressure in kPa?
10. Calculate the number of molecules in 1.00 cm³ of gas at 293 K and 101 kPa (L = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹).
11. Liquid in a volatile-liquid experiment does not all vaporise. What happens to the calculated Mᵣ?
12. Why is the answer usually quoted to three significant figures?

### Answers

1. 2.5 × 10⁶ Pa.
2. 7.50 × 10⁻⁴ m³.
3. 233 K.
4. Mᵣ = mRT / pV.
5. n = 2.00 × 10⁵ × 5.00 × 10⁻³ / (8.31 × 350) = **0.344 mol**.
6. n = 0.250 mol; V = 0.250 × 8.31 × 300 / 1.50 × 10⁵ = 4.16 × 10⁻³ m³ = **4.16 dm³**.
7. T = 1.01 × 10⁵ × 3.00 × 10⁻³ / (0.100 × 8.31) = **365 K**.
8. 50.0 × 373 / 291 = **64.1 cm³**.
9. In a ratio such as p₁V₁/T₁ = p₂V₂/T₂, where pressure units cancel.
10. n = 4.15 × 10⁻⁵ mol; × L = **2.50 × 10¹⁹** molecules.
11. It is too high, because the measured volume is too small.
12. R is given to three significant figures (8.31), which limits the precision.
