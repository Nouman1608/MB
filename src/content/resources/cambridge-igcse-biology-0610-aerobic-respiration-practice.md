These questions cover sub-topic **12.2 Aerobic respiration** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Questions and parts marked **(Extended)** test the balanced chemical equation; Core candidates can skip them.

Revise first with the [aerobic respiration study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-aerobic-respiration/) and the [aerobic respiration revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-aerobic-respiration-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which word equation shows aerobic respiration?

A: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen

B: glucose → lactic acid

C: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water

D: glucose → alcohol + carbon dioxide

**[1]**

**2.** Describe aerobic respiration. **[3]**

**3.** State where aerobic respiration takes place in a plant. **[1]**

**4.** Name the **two** reactants of aerobic respiration. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* State the balanced chemical equation for aerobic respiration. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Calculate the number of oxygen molecules needed to respire 7 molecules of glucose aerobically. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student writes three statements about aerobic respiration. For each statement, say whether it is correct and, if it is not, correct it.

**(a)** "Aerobic respiration produces energy." **[1]**

**(b)** "Aerobic respiration takes place in the lungs." **[1]**

**(c)** "Aerobic respiration uses oxygen." **[1]**

**8.** During a football match a player's muscles carry out more aerobic respiration.

**(a)** State the use of the extra energy released in the leg muscles. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the player's breathing rate increases. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe how glucose reaches the leg muscle cells from the small intestine. **[2]**

**9.** *(Extended)* Use the balanced chemical equation for aerobic respiration to answer these questions.

**(a)** A cell respires 12 molecules of glucose. Calculate the number of carbon dioxide molecules released. **[1]**

**(b)** A tissue releases 90 molecules of water by aerobic respiration. Calculate the number of glucose molecules respired. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the number of oxygen molecules used is always equal to the number of carbon dioxide molecules released. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A student writes the equation: C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O.

**(a)** Count the number of oxygen atoms on each side of the student's equation. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the correct balanced equation. **[1]**

**(c)** Show that your equation has the same number of hydrogen atoms on each side. **[1]**

**11.** A leaf is kept in bright light. It photosynthesises and also respires aerobically.

**(a)** Compare the reactants and products of aerobic respiration with those of photosynthesis. **[2]**

**(b)** A student says the leaf does not respire in the light. Explain why this is wrong. **[2]**

**(c)** In dim light, the leaf gives out carbon dioxide. Suggest why. **[2]**

**12.** A student sets up two tubes containing hydrogencarbonate indicator, which is red at the start. Tube X has living, soaked pea seeds held above the indicator on gauze. Tube Y has the same mass of soaked pea seeds that have been boiled and cooled. Both tubes are kept at 25 °C for two hours.

**(a)** Predict the colour of the indicator in each tube after two hours. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain your prediction for tube X. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why tube Y is included. **[1]**

**(d)** State **one** variable, other than temperature, that is kept the same in both tubes. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1].

*Examiner insight:* A is photosynthesis; B and D are anaerobic respiration in muscles and in yeast.

**2.** Chemical reactions in cells [1] that use oxygen [1] to break down nutrient molecules / glucose to release energy [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Breathing in oxygen" is not respiration; the definition is about chemistry inside cells.

**3.** In (all of) its living cells [1].

**4.** Glucose [1]; oxygen [1].

**5.** Correct formulae: C₆H₁₂O₆, O₂, CO₂, H₂O [1]; correct balancing: 1, 6, 6, 6 [1]. C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O.

**6.** 7 × 6 = **42** [1].

**7. (a)** Incorrect; it **releases** energy [1].

**(b)** Incorrect; it takes place in **cells** (all living cells) [1].

**(c)** Correct [1].

**8. (a)** Muscle contraction [1].

**(b)** More oxygen is needed for the increased aerobic respiration [1]; more carbon dioxide is produced and must be removed [1].

**(c)** Glucose is absorbed into the blood (in the small intestine) [1] and carried in the plasma to the muscles, where it diffuses into the cells [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (b) one mark is for each side of the equation; giving only "to get more oxygen" scores one.

**9. (a)** 12 × 6 = **72** [1].

**(b)** 90 ÷ 6 = **15** [1].

**(c)** The balanced equation uses 6 oxygen molecules and releases 6 carbon dioxide molecules for every glucose molecule (a 1 : 1 ratio) [1].

**10. (a)** Left: 6 + 2 = **8** [1]. Right: 2 + 1 = **3** [1].

**(b)** C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O [1].

**(c)** Left: 12 (in glucose). Right: 6 × 2 = 12 (in 6H₂O) [1].

**11. (a)** The reactants of respiration (glucose and oxygen) are the products of photosynthesis [1]; the products of respiration (carbon dioxide and water) are the reactants of photosynthesis [1].

**(b)** Respiration takes place in all living cells all the time [1]; cells need a continuous release of energy, so respiration continues in the light alongside photosynthesis [1].

**(c)** In dim light, photosynthesis is slow [1], so the leaf releases carbon dioxide from respiration faster than photosynthesis uses it [1].

*Examiner insight:* Part (c) needs both processes compared; "it is respiring" alone does not explain why carbon dioxide is given out overall.

**12. (a)** Tube X: yellow [1]. Tube Y: red / no change [1].

**(b)** The living seeds respire aerobically [1], releasing carbon dioxide, which raises the carbon dioxide concentration and turns the indicator yellow [1].

**(c)** It is a control, showing that the colour change is caused by living (respiring) seeds and not by the seeds themselves or the indicator alone [1].

**(d)** Any one of: mass of seeds; volume of indicator; time left; type of seed [1].

*Examiner insight:* Boiled seeds are dead, so their enzymes are denatured and they cannot respire. The control is useful only if everything else, including seed mass and temperature, is the same.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "produces energy" instead of "releases energy".
- Placing respiration in the lungs.
- Confusing the respiration and photosynthesis equations.
- (Extended) Writing O or O₆ for oxygen; dropping the 6s.
- (Extended) Multiplying in a backwards calculation instead of dividing.
- Saying plants respire only in the dark.
