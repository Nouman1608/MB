## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State the quantity represented by the gradient of a distance-time graph. **[1]**

**2.** Explain why velocity is a vector but speed is a scalar. **[2]**

**3.** A sprinter runs 200 m in 25 s. Calculate the sprinter's average speed. **[2]**

**4.** A car travels at 108 km/h.

**(a)** Convert this speed to m/s. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the distance the car travels in 12 s at this speed. **[2]**

**5.** A sound signal travels 5100 m at 340 m/s. Calculate the time taken. **[2]**

**6.** Describe the motion shown by each section of a distance-time graph:

**(a)** a horizontal line **[1]**

**(b)** a straight line sloping downwards **[1]**

**(c)** a curve that becomes steeper. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A boat moves along a straight canal. Its distance from a lock gate is recorded.

| Time (s) | 0 | 20 | 40 | 60 | 80 | 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Distance (m) | 0 | 30 | 60 | 60 | 40 | 20 |

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the boat between 0 s and 40 s. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the motion between 40 s and 60 s. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the speed between 60 s and 100 s and state the direction of travel. **[3]**

**(d)** Calculate the average speed for the whole 100 s. **[3]**

**8.** A student plots a distance-time graph for a toy car released at the top of a ramp. The line is a curve that gets steeper. She draws a tangent at t = 3.0 s. The tangent passes through (1.5 s, 0 m) and (4.5 s, 1.8 m).

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the car at t = 3.0 s. **[2]**

**(b)** State what the shape of the curve shows about the car's speed. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the speed at 3.0 s cannot be found by dividing the distance travelled by 3.0 s. **[2]**

**9.** A cyclist rides 4.5 km at 6.0 m/s, stops for 5.0 minutes, then rides a further 3.0 km at 5.0 m/s.

**(a)** Calculate the time for each moving part of the journey. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the average speed for the whole journey. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the answer to (b) is not the mean of 6.0 m/s and 5.0 m/s. **[1]**

**10.** A dog runs 40 m due east in 8.0 s, then 10 m due west in 4.0 s.

**(a)** Calculate the total distance and the displacement at the end. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the average speed. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the average velocity. **[2]**

**11.** A lift moves up a building. It starts at the ground floor, rises 24 m in 12 s at a steady speed, waits 8 s, then rises another 12 m in 10 s at a steady speed.

**(a)** Describe how you would draw the distance-time graph for this journey, including the shape of each section. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the speed in each moving section. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the average speed over the 30 s. **[2]**

**12.** Two runners, A and B, start together on a straight track. A runs at a steady 5.0 m/s. B runs at a steady 4.0 m/s for 60 s and then at 7.0 m/s.

**(a)** Calculate how far each runner has gone after 60 s. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the time after the start when B catches up with A. **[3]**

**(c)** Describe how the two lines on a distance-time graph would show the moment B catches A. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Speed [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Velocity has a direction as well as a magnitude [1]; speed has magnitude only [1]. **[2]**

**3.** v = s ÷ t = 200 ÷ 25 [1] = **8.0 m/s** [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** 108 ÷ 3.6 = **30 m/s** [1].

**(b)** s = v × t = 30 × 12 [1] = **360 m** [1]. **[3]**

**5.** t = s ÷ v = 5100 ÷ 340 [1] = **15 s** [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Stationary [1].

**(b)** Moving at a constant speed back towards the reference point [1].

**(c)** Speeding up (accelerating) [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** speed = 60 ÷ 40 [1] = **1.5 m/s** [1].

**(b)** Stationary (stopped at 60 m from the gate) for 20 s [1].

**(c)** Distance changes from 60 m to 20 m, a change of 40 m [1]; speed = 40 ÷ 40 = **1.0 m/s** [1]; moving back towards the lock gate [1].

**(d)** Total distance = 60 + 40 = 100 m [1]; total time = 100 s [1]; average speed = **1.0 m/s** [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* in (d), the final reading of 20 m is not the distance travelled; the boat went out 60 m and came back 40 m.

**8. (a)** gradient = (1.8 − 0) ÷ (4.5 − 1.5) [1] = **0.60 m/s** [1].

**(b)** The speed is increasing [1].

**(c)** Distance ÷ time gives the average speed over the first 3.0 s [1]; the speed is changing, so the average is less than the speed at 3.0 s (the tangent gives the instantaneous value) [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** 4500 ÷ 6.0 = 750 s [1]; 3000 ÷ 5.0 = 600 s [1].

**(b)** Total distance = 7500 m [1]; total time = 750 + 300 + 600 = 1650 s [1]; average speed = 7500 ÷ 1650 = **4.5 m/s** (4.55 m/s) [1].

**(c)** The average must include the stop, and the two speeds were kept for different times, so a simple mean does not give total distance ÷ total time [1]. **[6]**

**10. (a)** Distance = 40 + 10 = **50 m** [1]; displacement = 40 − 10 = **30 m east** [1].

**(b)** Average speed = 50 ÷ 12 [1] = **4.2 m/s** (4.17 m/s) [1].

**(c)** Average velocity = 30 ÷ 12 [1] = **2.5 m/s east** [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* a velocity answer without "east" is incomplete.

**11. (a)** Straight line rising from (0 s, 0 m) to (12 s, 24 m) [1]; horizontal line from 12 s to 20 s at 24 m [1]; straight line, less steep than the first, from (20 s, 24 m) to (30 s, 36 m) [1].

**(b)** 24 ÷ 12 = **2.0 m/s** [1]; 12 ÷ 10 = **1.2 m/s** [1].

**(c)** 36 ÷ 30 [1] = **1.2 m/s** [1]. **[7]**

**12. (a)** A: 5.0 × 60 = **300 m** [1]; B: 4.0 × 60 = **240 m** [1].

**(b)** After 60 s, B gains 7.0 − 5.0 = 2.0 m every second [1]; the gap of 60 m closes in 60 ÷ 2.0 = 30 s [1]; B catches A at **90 s** after the start [1].

**(c)** The two lines cross at that time (both runners are the same distance from the start) [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* check by substitution: A has run 5.0 × 90 = 450 m; B has run 240 + 7.0 × 30 = 450 m.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating the last reading on a distance-from-a-point graph as the total distance travelled.
- Leaving out stopped time in average speed.
- Giving velocity without a direction.
- Reading tangent coordinates from the curve.
