All businesses are invented. Money is in dollars.

## Quality = meeting customer expectations

- Expectations depend on **price paid** and **target segment**: a budget product can be high quality for its customers.
- Product dimensions: performance, reliability, durability, appearance, after-sales support.
- Service dimensions: reliability, responsiveness, accuracy, courtesy, consistency.
- In a case: find the dimension the customers care about most.

## Importance of quality: cost of quality

| Group | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Prevention | Stopping faults | Training, design, supplier standards |
| Appraisal | Finding faults | Inspection, testing |
| Internal failure | Faults found before the customer | Scrap, rework, downtime |
| External failure | Faults found by the customer | Refunds, guarantee repairs, lost customers |

Key argument: prevention spending usually saves more in failure costs; external failure is the most damaging because lost future sales are hidden.

Other reasons quality matters: supports a higher price; repeat purchase and recommendations; fewer complaints to handle; easier to motivate staff who are not fixing errors; needed to win contracts with business buyers.

## Methods and their impact

| | Quality control | Quality assurance | TQM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Idea | Inspect and test output | Standards and procedures at every stage | Quality is everyone's job in every department |
| Focus | Detect faults | Prevent faults | Prevent faults and improve continuously |
| Who is responsible | Inspectors / quality department | Managers and staff following procedures | Every employee |
| Positive impact | Protects customers; suits safety-critical goods | Fewer faults; trust; certification wins contracts | Big fall in failure costs; fewer inspectors; motivating responsibility |
| Negative impact | Adds cost not value; faults recur; staff ignore quality | Paperwork; rigid; certification ≠ excellence | Training cost; slow culture change; resistance; needs senior commitment |

**QC choices:** 100% inspection (catches more, costly) or sampling (cheaper, some faults pass); end of line (late) or at stages (earlier).

**TQM principles:** customer focus (including internal customers), right first time, zero defects, continuous improvement, employee involvement (quality circles).

**Worked reminder: Ashby Bottling.** 2,000,000 bottles a year; reject rate falls from 1.5% to 0.6% after quality assurance. Rejects fall from 30,000 to 12,000; at $0.40 each, saving = 18,000 × 0.40 = $7,200 a year, before the cost of the new procedures.

## Benchmarking

**Process:** choose what to measure → choose comparison (internal, competitor, or leader in another industry) → collect data → find out how the leader does it → set targets and adapt → monitor and repeat.

**Importance in quality management:**

- Customers compare with rivals, so internal standards alone mislead.
- Shows the size of the gap with evidence.
- Turns vague aims into specific targets.
- Shows how to improve, not just that improvement is needed.
- Supports continuous improvement.

**Limits:** data hard to obtain or compare; methods may not transfer; always following, never leading.

**Gap calculation:** gap = leader's figure − business's figure; a gap per 1,000 × (volume ÷ 1,000) gives the number of extra complaints, defects or late deliveries.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Quality | Meeting the expectations of the target customers |
| Quality control | Checking output to find and remove faults |
| Quality assurance | Setting and checking standards at each stage to prevent faults |
| TQM | Whole-business approach making every employee responsible for quality |
| Internal customer | The next person or department in the production process |
| Zero defects | Aim that nothing faulty is produced |
| Quality circle | A small group of workers meeting to solve quality problems |
| Benchmarking | Comparing performance with the best and using the gap to improve |

## Exam traps

- "Quality means luxury": no, it means meeting expectations.
- Describing a method without its impact on cost, staff or customers.
- Forgetting external failure costs when judging if a method pays.
- Saying TQM removes all inspection: some checks usually remain.
- Calling benchmarking "copying".
- Ignoring implementation time and staff resistance in evaluation.

## Self-test

1. Why can a budget product be described as high quality?
2. Name the four groups of quality costs.
3. Which group of quality costs is usually most damaging, and why?
4. Give one advantage of sampling over 100% inspection.
5. What is the main difference between quality control and quality assurance?
6. What is an internal customer?
7. Give two principles of TQM.
8. Give one reason TQM may fail.
9. List the six steps of benchmarking.
10. Firm A has 6 complaints per 1,000 orders; the leader has 2. On 250,000 orders, how many extra complaints does Firm A have?
11. Give one limitation of benchmarking.
12. Why might a business buyer require a supplier to have a recognised quality certificate?

### Answers

1. Because it meets the expectations of its target customers at the price they pay.
2. Prevention, appraisal, internal failure, external failure.
3. External failure, because it includes refunds and lost future customers that may never be recovered.
4. It is cheaper and quicker.
5. QC detects faults after production; QA prevents them by controlling each stage.
6. The next person or department in the process, who receives your work.
7. Any two: customer focus, right first time, zero defects, continuous improvement, employee involvement.
8. Lack of senior management commitment (or staff resistance, or high training cost).
9. Choose what to measure; choose comparison; collect data; find how the leader does it; set targets and adapt; monitor and repeat.
10. Gap = 4 per 1,000; 250 × 4 = **1,000** extra complaints.
11. Competitor data is hard to obtain (or methods may not suit the business).
12. It gives confidence that the supplier follows documented procedures, reducing the buyer's own risk of faults.
