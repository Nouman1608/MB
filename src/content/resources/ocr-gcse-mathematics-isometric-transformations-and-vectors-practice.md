## Questions

**1.** Triangle PQR has vertices P(1, −2), Q(4, −2) and R(1, −4). Write the coordinates of its image after reflection in the line x = −1. **[3]**

**2.** Triangle T has vertices (3, 1), (5, 1) and (5, 4). Its image has vertices (1, 3), (1, 5) and (4, 5).

**(a)** Give the equation of the mirror line. **[2]**
**(b)** The point (−2, 6) is reflected in the line y = −x. Find its image. **[1]**

**3.** A triangle has vertices (3, 1), (6, 1) and (6, 3). Rotate it through 90° anticlockwise about (2, 0). Write the coordinates of the image. **[3]**

**4.** Shape F has vertices (1, 1), (4, 1) and (4, 2). Its image has vertices (2, −4), (2, −7) and (3, −7). Describe fully the single transformation. **[4]**

**5.** The vertex (2, 5) of a tile maps to (−1, 3) under a translation.

**(a)** Write the translation as a column vector. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the translation that maps the image back onto the tile. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the image of (4, −1) under the original translation. **[1]**

**6.** **a** = (3 over −2) and **b** = (−4 over 1).

**(a)** Work out 2**a** + 3**b**. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out **a** − 2**b**. **[2]**
**(c)** The vector (9 over y) is a multiple of **a**. Find y. **[1]**

**7.** P(−2, 1), Q(3, 4) and R(7, 2) are three vertices of parallelogram PQRS.

**(a)** Write **PQ** and **QR** as column vectors. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of S. **[2]**

**8.** **(Higher tier only)** Shape D is reflected in the horizontal line y = −2. Its image is then reflected in the horizontal line y = 5.

**(a)** Describe the single transformation that maps D onto the final image. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why no point is invariant. **[1]**

**9.** **(Higher tier only)** Shape S is reflected in the x-axis and then in the line y = x.

**(a)** Describe the single transformation that has the same effect. **[3]**
**(b)** The reflections are done in the opposite order. Describe the single transformation. **[2]**

**10.** **(Higher tier only)** A shape is rotated through 90° anticlockwise about the origin, then translated by (2 over 4).

**(a)** Find the final image of the point (3, 1). **[2]**
**(b)** Show that the point (−1, 3) is invariant under the combined transformation. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe the single transformation. **[2]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** In quadrilateral ABCD, **AB** = 2**p** + **q**, **BC** = **p** − 3**q** and **CD** = −6**p** − 3**q**. Prove that ABCD is a trapezium. **[5]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)** ORS is a triangle with **OR** = **r** and **OS** = **s**. The point T is on RS with RT : TS = 2 : 3. The point U is such that **OU** = 3**r** + 2**s**.

**(a)** Show that **OT** = (1/5)(3**r** + 2**s**). **[3]**
**(b)** Prove that O, T and U are collinear. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the ratio OT : TU. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Use x → 2(−1) − x = −2 − x. P(1, −2) goes to (−3, −2) [1]. Q(4, −2) goes to (−6, −2) [1]. R(1, −4) goes to (−3, −4) [1]. **Image: (−3, −2), (−6, −2), (−3, −4)**
*Examiner insight:* Give all three image vertices as coordinates, and count from the mirror line, not from the y-axis. Check each distance to the line is equal on both sides.

**2. (a)** (3, 1) goes to (1, 3): the coordinates swap [1]. The mirror line is **y = x** [1].
**(b)** (−y, −x) gives **(−6, 2)** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Test the line on one more vertex. Writing "reflection" without the equation does not answer a question that asks for the equation of the mirror line.

**3.** Relative to (2, 0): (1, 1), (4, 1), (4, 3). Turn anticlockwise (−dy, dx): (−1, 1), (−1, 4), (−3, 4) [1]. Add the centre back: (1, 1), (1, 4), (−1, 4) [1]. **Image: (1, 1), (1, 4), (−1, 4)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the relative positions so that each step of the turn can be followed and checked.

**4.** Try the centre (−1, −2). Its squared distance from both (1, 1) and (2, −4) is 13 [1]. From the centre, (1, 1) is at (2, 3) and its image is at (3, −2) [1]. Turning (2, 3) a quarter turn clockwise gives (3, −2) [1]. **A rotation of 90° clockwise about (−1, −2)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A rotation answer needs three items: centre, angle and sense. Naming only "rotation 90°" leaves the answer incomplete.

**5. (a)** (−1 − 2 over 3 − 5) = **(−3 over −2)** [1].
**(b)** **(3 over 2)** [1].
**(c)** (4 − 3, −1 − 2) = **(1, −3)** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Subtract object from image, in that order. Give a column vector rather than "3 left and 2 down" when the question says to write one.

**6. (a)** 2**a** = (6 over −4) and 3**b** = (−12 over 3) [1]. Sum: **(−6 over −1)** [1].
**(b)** 2**b** = (−8 over 2) [1]. **a** − 2**b** = (3 + 8 over −2 − 2) = **(11 over −4)** [1].
**(c)** 9 = 3 × 3, so y = 3 × (−2) = **−6** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show each scalar multiple before combining. Subtracting a negative number is a common slip, so write the sign change explicitly.

**7. (a)** **PQ** = (3 + 2 over 4 − 1) = **(5 over 3)** [1]. **QR** = (7 − 3 over 2 − 4) = **(4 over −2)** [1].
**(b)** In a parallelogram **PS** = **QR** [1]. S = (−2 + 4, 1 − 2) = **(2, −1)** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Say which equal vectors you use. Check by confirming **SR** equals **PQ**: both are (5 over 3).

**8. (a)** (3, 4) goes to (3, −8), then to (3, 18) [1]. The shift is 14 upwards, and 14 = 2 × (5 − (−2)) [1]. **A translation by (0 over 14)** [1].
**(b)** A translation moves every point, so none stays fixed [1].
*Examiner insight:* Name the transformation type first, then give its full details. Tracking one point lets you check the shortcut for parallel mirror lines.

**9. (a)** Take (2, 1). The x-axis sends it to (2, −1) [1], then y = x sends it to (−1, 2) [1]. This is a quarter turn anticlockwise about the origin where the mirror lines meet: **rotation 90° anticlockwise about (0, 0)** [1].
**(b)** (2, 1) goes to (1, 2), then to (1, −2) [1]. **Rotation 90° clockwise about (0, 0)** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Use the same test point for both orders. Showing that the results differ is the clearest way to show that order matters.

**10. (a)** Rotation sends (x, y) to (−y, x), so (3, 1) goes to (−1, 3) [1]. Adding (2 over 4) gives **(1, 7)** [1].
**(b)** (−1, 3) goes to (−3, −1) under the rotation [1]. Adding (2 over 4) gives (−1, 3), the starting point, so it is invariant [1].
**(c)** The invariant point is the centre. Check: relative to (−1, 3), the point (3, 1) is at (4, −2) and its image (1, 7) is at (2, 4), which is a 90° anticlockwise turn [1]. **Rotation of 90° anticlockwise about (−1, 3)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" part, write the start point, the point after each step and the final point. An unsupported statement that the point is invariant is hard to credit.

**11.** **DC** = −**CD** = 6**p** + 3**q** [1]. 6**p** + 3**q** = 3(2**p** + **q**) = 3**AB** [1]. So DC is parallel to AB [1]. **AD** = **AB** + **BC** + **CD** = −3**p** − 5**q** [1]. **BC** = **p** − 3**q** is not a multiple of **AD**, so BC and AD are not parallel and ABCD has exactly one pair of parallel sides: a trapezium [1]
*Examiner insight:* Finish with a sentence that links the algebra to the geometry. Showing the second pair is not parallel supports "trapezium" rather than "parallelogram".

**12. (a)** **RS** = **s** − **r** [1]. **RT** = (2/5)**RS**, since RT : TS = 2 : 3 [1]. **OT** = **r** + (2/5)(**s** − **r**) = (3/5)**r** + (2/5)**s** = (1/5)(3**r** + 2**s**) [1].
**(b)** **OU** = 3**r** + 2**s** = 5**OT** [1]. The vectors share the point O and are multiples, so O, T and U are on one line [1].
**(c)** **TU** = **OU** − **OT** = 4**OT**, so OT : TU = **1 : 4** [1].
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" question needs each line of the route written down, ending on the stated result. Give the reason (shared point plus multiple), not just the multiple.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a rotation or reflection without every detail asked for.
- Mixing up clockwise and anticlockwise, and not checking with a point.
- Measuring a reflection from an axis when the mirror line is x = a or y = b.
- Writing a translation as coordinates or words, not a column vector.
- Subtracting in the wrong order when finding a vector from two points.
- Slipping on signs when subtracting a vector with negative entries.
- Applying two transformations in the wrong order, or applying the second to the original shape.
- Stating that vectors are parallel without showing the multiple.
- Concluding a proof without a sentence naming the geometric result.
