These questions cover section 6.1 Calculation and understanding of accounting ratios of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. Use only the syllabus formulas, and give answers to two decimal places unless told otherwise. Questions 7 to 9 use the same business. Revise first with the [ratio calculation study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-calculation-and-understanding-of-accounting-ratios/) and the [ratio calculation revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-calculation-and-understanding-of-accounting-ratios-revision-notes/). All businesses and figures are invented, and amounts are in dollars.

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** State the syllabus formula for return on capital employed, including how capital employed is calculated. **[2]**

**2.** A business has revenue of $240,000 and cost of sales of $168,000. Calculate the gross profit margin. **[2]**

**3.** Using the figures in Question 2, calculate the mark-up. **[1]**

**4.** A business has current assets of $36,000, including inventory of $15,000, and current liabilities of $14,000. What is its acid test (liquid) ratio?

A: 0.67 : 1

B: 1.07 : 1

C: 1.50 : 1

D: 2.57 : 1

**[1]**

**5.** Trade receivables are $13,150 and credit sales for the year are $120,000. Calculate the trade receivables turnover in days. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why ROCE uses profit for the year before interest. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Pellow Garden Supplies is owned by a sole trader. For the year ended 31 March 2029:

| Item | $ |
|---|---|
| Revenue (all on credit) | 180,000 |
| Opening inventory | 15,500 |
| Purchases (all on credit) | 118,000 |
| Closing inventory | 18,500 |
| Expenses, excluding loan interest | 37,600 |
| Loan interest | 2,400 |
| Capital at 31 March 2029 | 160,000 |
| Loan repayable in 2034 | 40,000 |

**(a)** Calculate the gross profit margin. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the profit margin. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the return on capital employed, using the owner's capital in place of shares and reserves. **[3]**

**8.** At 31 March 2029 Pellow Garden Supplies also had trade receivables $19,730, bank $3,270, trade payables $12,610 and other payables $1,890. Closing inventory is as in Question 7.

**(a)** Calculate the current ratio. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the acid test ratio. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the working capital. **[1]**

**9.** Using the information in Questions 7 and 8 for Pellow Garden Supplies:

**(a)** Calculate the rate of inventory turnover. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the inventory turnover in days. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the trade receivables turnover in days. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the trade payables turnover in days. **[2]**

**10.** Fenwick Stores has revenue of $200,000 and a gross profit margin of 35%. Its rate of inventory turnover is 6.5 times and its opening inventory was $18,000.

**(a)** Calculate the gross profit. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the cost of sales. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the average inventory. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the closing inventory. **[1]**

**(e)** Calculate the purchases for the year. **[2]**

**11.** A student calculated four ratios for Garnet Ltd:

| Ratio | Student's working | Student's answer |
|---|---|---|
| Mark-up | 48,000 / 128,000 x 100 | 37.50% |
| ROCE | 21,000 / 150,000 x 100 | 14.00% |
| Trade receivables turnover | 10,520 / 128,000 x 365 | 30.00 days |
| Current ratio | 30,000 / 12,000 x 100 | 250% |

Garnet Ltd's figures are: revenue $128,000 (credit sales $96,000, cash sales $32,000); cost of sales $80,000; gross profit $48,000; profit for the year $21,000 after debenture interest of $3,000; capital employed $150,000; trade receivables $10,520; current assets $30,000; current liabilities $12,000.

For each ratio, identify the student's error and calculate the correct result. **[8]**

**12.** Explain what each result shows.

**(a)** An acid test ratio of 0.85 : 1. **[2]**

**(b)** Trade payables turnover of 62 days. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why a business's mark-up is higher than its gross profit margin. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Profit for the year before interest / capital employed x 100 [1], where capital employed = issued shares + reserves + non-current liabilities [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Gross profit = 240,000 - 168,000 = $72,000 [1]. Margin = 72,000 / 240,000 x 100 = **30.00%** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** 72,000 / 168,000 x 100 = **42.86%** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** C [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* D is the current ratio and B divides inventory by current liabilities; both are common slips.

**5.** 13,150 / 120,000 x 365 [1] = **40.00 days** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Interest is a return paid to lenders, whose loans are part of capital employed [1]. Adding interest back measures the return made on all the long-term finance, not only the owners' part, so businesses financed differently can be compared [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Cost of sales = 15,500 + 118,000 - 18,500 = $115,000; gross profit = $65,000 [1]. Margin = 65,000 / 180,000 x 100 = **36.11%** [1].

**(b)** Profit for the year = 65,000 - 37,600 - 2,400 = $25,000 [1]. Profit margin = 25,000 / 180,000 x 100 = **13.89%** [1].

**(c)** Profit before interest = 25,000 + 2,400 = $27,400 [1]. Capital employed = 160,000 + 40,000 = $200,000 [1]. ROCE = 27,400 / 200,000 x 100 = **13.70%** [1]. **[7]**

**8. (a)** Current assets = 18,500 + 19,730 + 3,270 = $41,500; current liabilities = 12,610 + 1,890 = $14,500 [1]. Current ratio = **2.86 : 1** [1].

**(b)** (41,500 - 18,500) / 14,500 [1] = **1.59 : 1** [1].

**(c)** 41,500 - 14,500 = **$27,000** [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** Average inventory = (15,500 + 18,500) / 2 = $17,000 [1]. Rate = 115,000 / 17,000 = **6.76 times** [1].

**(b)** 17,000 / 115,000 x 365 [1] = **53.96 days** [1].

**(c)** 19,730 / 180,000 x 365 [1] = **40.01 days** [1].

**(d)** 12,610 / 118,000 x 365 [1] = **39.01 days** [1]. **[8]**

**10. (a)** 200,000 x 35% = **$70,000** [1].

**(b)** 200,000 - 70,000 = **$130,000** [1].

**(c)** 130,000 / 6.5 = **$20,000** [1].

**(d)** (20,000 x 2) - 18,000 = **$22,000** [1].

**(e)** Purchases = cost of sales - opening inventory + closing inventory = 130,000 - 18,000 + 22,000 [1] = **$134,000** [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Mark-up: the student divided by revenue, which gives the margin [1]; correct mark-up = 48,000 / 80,000 x 100 = **60.00%** [1]. ROCE: interest was not added back [1]; correct ROCE = 24,000 / 150,000 x 100 = **16.00%** [1]. Trade receivables: total revenue was used instead of credit sales [1]; correct = 10,520 / 96,000 x 365 = **40.00 days** [1]. Current ratio: shown as a percentage [1]; correct = 30,000 / 12,000 = **2.50 : 1** [1]. **[8]**

**12. (a)** The business has only $0.85 of current assets other than inventory for each $1 of current liabilities [1], so it could not pay all its current liabilities from liquid assets without selling inventory [1].

**(b)** On average the business takes 62 days to pay its credit suppliers [1], based on the trade payables at the year end compared with the year's credit purchases [1].

**(c)** Both use the same gross profit [1], but mark-up divides it by cost of sales, which is smaller than revenue whenever there is a gross profit, so the percentage is larger [1]. **[6]**
