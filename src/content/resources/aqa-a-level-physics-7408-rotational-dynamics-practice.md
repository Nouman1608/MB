These questions cover section **3.11.1 Rotational dynamics** of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408), part of the A-level only Engineering physics option. Learn the content first in the [rotational dynamics study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-rotational-dynamics/) and the [rotational dynamics revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-rotational-dynamics-revision-notes/).

Data: g = 9.81 m s⁻².

## Section A

**1.** State two factors that affect the moment of inertia of a rotating object. **[2]**

**2.** A light rod rotates about its centre. A 0.50 kg mass is fixed 0.40 m from the axis on each side, and a 0.10 kg mass is fixed 0.20 m from the axis. Calculate the moment of inertia of the system. **[2]**

**3.** A flywheel with I = 0.90 kg m² rotates at 3000 revolutions per minute. Calculate its rotational kinetic energy. **[3]**

**4.** State the condition for the angular momentum of a system to be conserved. **[1]**

**5.** A torque acting on a wheel (I = 0.25 kg m²), initially at rest, rises steadily from zero to 6.0 N m in 0.50 s, then falls steadily to zero at 1.5 s. Calculate the angular speed of the wheel at 1.5 s. **[3]**

**6.** A motor delivers a torque of 12 N m at 1500 revolutions per minute. Calculate its output power. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A drum with I = 0.90 kg m² starts from rest and accelerates uniformly. It makes 50 revolutions in the first 10 s.

**(a)** Calculate the angular displacement in radians. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the angular acceleration. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the angular speed after 10 s. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the resultant torque on the drum. **[1]**
**(e)** A frictional torque of 0.60 N m acts. Calculate the torque the motor must apply. **[1]**

**8.** A playground roundabout with I = 150 kg m² spins freely at 1.2 rad s⁻¹. A child of mass 40 kg steps onto it from rest, 1.5 m from the axis. Treat the child as a point mass.

**(a)** Calculate the new moment of inertia. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the new angular speed. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the kinetic energy before and after, and explain why kinetic energy is not conserved. **[3]**

**9.** A student finds the moment of inertia of a wheel. A string wound round its axle (radius 0.015 m) supports a 0.20 kg mass, which is released from rest and falls 0.80 m in 3.2 s.

**(a)** Calculate the linear acceleration of the mass. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the tension in the string. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the angular acceleration of the wheel. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the moment of inertia of the wheel, ignoring friction. **[2]**
**(e)** State and explain the effect of friction at the axle on the value found. **[1]**

**10.** A punching press uses a flywheel with I = 1.6 kg m². During each punch, the flywheel slows from 120 rad s⁻¹ to 90 rad s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the energy released by the flywheel in one punch. **[2]**
**(b)** The punch lasts 0.20 s. Calculate the average power delivered. **[1]**
**(c)** A 1.5 kW motor restores the speed. Calculate the minimum time needed. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the flywheel's mass is concentrated near its rim, and why this design suits a press. **[2]**

**11.** A flywheel with I = 0.50 kg m² is disconnected from its motor at 250 rad s⁻¹ and decelerates uniformly to rest in 80 s.

**(a)** Calculate the angular deceleration. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the frictional torque. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the number of revolutions made before it stops. **[2]**
**(d)** Show that the work done against friction equals the initial kinetic energy. **[1]**

**12.** A figure skater spins on ice with her arms outstretched, then pulls her arms close to her body. Explain what happens to her angular speed and to her rotational kinetic energy. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: the total mass; how the mass is distributed relative to the axis (its distance from the axis); the position of the axis of rotation [2]

**2.** I = Σmr² = 2 × 0.50 × 0.40² + 0.10 × 0.20² [1]; = **0.164 kg m²** [1]

**3.** ω = 3000 × 2π/60 = 314 rad s⁻¹ [1]; E_k = ½ × 0.90 × 314² [1]; = **4.4 × 10⁴ J** [1]

**4.** No external resultant torque acts on the system [1]

**5.** Angular impulse = area under the torque–time graph = ½ × 1.5 × 6.0 = 4.5 N m s [1]; Δ(Iω) = 4.5, so ω = 4.5/0.25 [1]; = **18 rad s⁻¹** [1]

**6.** ω = 1500 × 2π/60 = 157 rad s⁻¹ [1]; P = Tω = 12 × 157 = **1.9 kW** [1]

**7. (a)** θ = 50 × 2π = **314 rad** [1]
**(b)** θ = ½αt², so α = 2 × 314/10² [1]; = **6.3 rad s⁻²** [1]
**(c)** ω = αt = **63 rad s⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** T = Iα = 0.90 × 6.28 = **5.7 N m** [1]
**(e)** 5.65 + 0.60 = **6.3 N m** [1]

**8. (a)** I = 150 + 40 × 1.5² = **240 kg m²** [1]
**(b)** 150 × 1.2 = 240ω [1]; ω = **0.75 rad s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** Before: ½ × 150 × 1.2² = **108 J**; after: ½ × 240 × 0.75² = **67.5 J** [1]; the child must be accelerated by frictional forces between her feet and the roundabout [1]; work is done against friction, so energy is transferred to internal energy, like an inelastic collision [1]
*Examiner insight:* Angular momentum is conserved because no external torque acts about the axis; kinetic energy is not, and both statements are needed.

**9. (a)** a = 2s/t² = 2 × 0.80/3.2² = **0.156 m s⁻²** [1]
**(b)** T = m(g − a) = 0.20 × (9.81 − 0.156) = **1.93 N** [1]
**(c)** α = a/r = 0.156/0.015 = **10.4 rad s⁻²** [1]
**(d)** torque = 1.93 × 0.015 = 0.0290 N m [1]; I = 0.0290/10.4 = **2.8 × 10⁻³ kg m²** [1]
**(e)** It is too large, because part of the torque overcomes friction, so α is smaller than it would be for that torque alone [1]

**10. (a)** ΔE = ½ × 1.6 × (120² − 90²) [1]; = **5.0 × 10³ J** [1]
**(b)** P = 5040/0.20 = **25 kW** [1]
**(c)** t = 5040/1500 = **3.4 s** [1]
**(d)** Mass far from the axis gives a large I for a given total mass, so more energy is stored at a given ω [1]; the press needs a large energy in a short burst, which a small motor cannot supply directly but can build up in the flywheel between punches [1]

**11. (a)** α = 250/80 = **3.1 rad s⁻²** [1]
**(b)** T = Iα = 0.50 × 3.125 = **1.6 N m** [1]
**(c)** θ = ½ × 250 × 80 = 1.0 × 10⁴ rad [1]; = **1.6 × 10³ revolutions** [1]
**(d)** W = Tθ = 1.5625 × 1.0 × 10⁴ = 1.56 × 10⁴ J, and ½Iω² = ½ × 0.50 × 250² = 1.56 × 10⁴ J [1]

**12.** There is (almost) no external torque, so her angular momentum Iω stays constant [1]; pulling her arms in reduces her moment of inertia, so her angular speed increases [1]; her kinetic energy ½Iω² increases, because she does work pulling her arms inwards [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving rpm or revolutions unconverted.
- Ignoring frictional torque in T = Iα.
- Claiming kinetic energy is conserved in a coupling problem.
- Taking the string tension as mg.
- Forgetting that the area under a torque–time graph is the angular impulse.
