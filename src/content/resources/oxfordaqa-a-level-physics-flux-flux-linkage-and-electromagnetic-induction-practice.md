These questions cover sections 3.10.3 (Magnetic flux and flux linkage) and 3.10.4 (Electromagnetic induction), of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), Version 4.4, which the specification marks as International A-level only. Read the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-flux-flux-linkage-and-electromagnetic-induction/) first, or use the [revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-flux-flux-linkage-and-electromagnetic-induction-revision-notes/). Give numerical answers to a sensible number of significant figures. 

## Questions

**1.** A rectangular loop measures 0.15 m by 0.060 m. It is in a uniform field of 0.28 T, perpendicular to the plane of the loop.
**(a)** Calculate the magnetic flux through the loop. **[2]**
**(b)** The loop is replaced by a coil of 120 turns of the same size. Calculate the flux linkage. **[1]**

**2.** A rectangular coil of 60 turns measures 0.10 m by 0.20 m. It is in a field of 0.090 T.
**(a)** The normal to the coil makes an angle of 50° with the field. Calculate the flux linkage. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the angle between the normal and the field when the flux linkage is 0.054 Wb. **[2]**

**3.** Write down the two laws of electromagnetic induction named after Faraday and Lenz. **[3]**

**4.** A student connects a coil to a sensitive meter and pushes a bar magnet into the coil.
**(a)** Explain why the meter deflects only while the magnet is moving. **[2]**
**(b)** The magnet is then pulled out faster. Describe and explain two differences in what the meter shows. **[2]**

**5.** The flux linkage of a coil changes with time as follows.

| Time / s | 0 | 0.20 | 0.40 | 0.50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flux linkage / mWb | 0 | 30 | 30 | 0 |

The flux linkage changes uniformly between readings.
**(a)** Calculate the emf from 0 to 0.20 s. **[2]**
**(b)** State and explain the emf from 0.20 s to 0.40 s. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the size of the emf from 0.40 s to 0.50 s and say how its direction compares with part (a). **[2]**

**6.** A metal rod of length 0.30 m slides at a constant 0.80 m s⁻¹ along two horizontal rails in a vertical field of 0.45 T. The rod and rails form a circuit of total resistance 0.15 Ω.
**(a)** Calculate the emf induced in the rod. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the current. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the horizontal force needed to keep the rod moving at constant speed. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain, using Lenz's law, why this force is needed. **[1]**

**7.** A small cylindrical magnet is dropped down a long vertical aluminium tube. It takes much longer to emerge than a non-magnetic object of the same size. Explain why. **[4]**

**8.** A coil rotates uniformly in a uniform field. Explain why the induced emf is zero at the instant the flux linkage is at its maximum. **[3]**

**9.** A rectangular coil of 80 turns measures 0.050 m by 0.040 m. It rotates at 1200 revolutions per minute about an axis perpendicular to a uniform field of 0.25 T. At t = 0 the normal to the coil is along the field.
**(a)** Calculate the angular speed of the coil. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the peak emf. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the emf at t = 3.0 ms. **[2]**
**(d)** The coil is now rotated at twice the speed. State the new peak emf and period. **[2]**

**10.** A coil of 400 turns has area 2.5 × 10⁻³ m². It lies perpendicular to a uniform field of 0.12 T directed into the page. It is pulled completely out of the field in 0.15 s. The resistance of the circuit is 8.0 Ω.
**(a)** Find the flux linkage at the start. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the mean induced emf. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the average current. **[1]**
**(d)** State and explain the direction of the current, clockwise or anticlockwise as seen with the field into the page. **[2]**
**(e)** Calculate the electrical energy transferred in the circuit during the 0.15 s. **[1]**

**11.** A coil of 150 turns and area 5.0 × 10⁻³ m² rotates uniformly with period 0.040 s in a field of 0.080 T. The normal is along the field at t = 0.
**(a)** Calculate the angular speed. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the peak emf. **[2]**
**(c)** State the times in the first period when the emf is zero, and say what is true of the coil then. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the first time when the emf equals half the peak value. **[2]**

**12.** Show that the weber is equivalent to the volt second. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A = 0.15 × 0.060 = 9.0 × 10⁻³ m² [1]; Φ = BA = 0.28 × 9.0 × 10⁻³ = **2.5 × 10⁻³ Wb** [1]
**(b)** NΦ = 120 × 2.52 × 10⁻³ = **0.30 Wb** [1]
**[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the area in m² first; a slip on the area or a missing power of ten affects both parts.

**2. (a)** A = 0.10 × 0.20 = 0.020 m² [1]; BAN = 0.090 × 0.020 × 60 = 0.108 Wb [1]; NΦ = 0.108 × cos 50° = **0.069 Wb** [1]
**(b)** cos θ = 0.054 / 0.108 = 0.50 [1]; θ = **60°** [1]
**[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The angle is from the normal, so cos is right. Using sin 50° (0.083 Wb) is the typical slip; sketch the normal.

**3.** Faraday: the size of the induced emf is directly proportional to how fast the flux linkage changes [1]. Lenz: the induced emf (and any current) has a direction that opposes [1] the change causing it [1].
**[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Opposes the change" is what gains credit; "opposes the flux" or "opposes the magnet" is a looser statement that may not.

**4. (a)** The flux linkage through the coil changes only while the magnet moves [1]; an emf is induced only when flux linkage changes, so with the magnet at rest the rate of change is zero and there is no deflection [1].
**(b)** The deflection is larger because the rate of change of flux linkage is greater [1]; it lasts for a shorter time, as the magnet takes less time to travel the same distance [1].
**[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each observation to the rate of change of flux linkage.

**5. (a)** ε = Δ(NΦ)/Δt = 30 × 10⁻³ / 0.20 [1] = **0.15 V** [1]
**(b)** **Zero**, because the flux linkage is constant (zero gradient) [1].
**(c)** ε = 30 × 10⁻³ / 0.10 = **0.30 V** [1]; the direction is **opposite** to part (a), as the flux linkage is decreasing rather than increasing [1]
**[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert mWb to Wb and give the reason for part (b) in terms of the gradient, not just "nothing is happening".

**6. (a)** ε = BLv [1] = 0.45 × 0.30 × 0.80 = **0.11 V** [1]
**(b)** I = ε/R = 0.108 / 0.15 = **0.72 A** [1]
**(c)** F = BIL [1] = 0.45 × 0.72 × 0.30 = **0.097 N** [1]
**(d)** The induced current opposes the change in flux (the increase in area of the circuit), so the magnetic force on the rod opposes its motion; an applied force is needed to balance it [1]
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the unrounded emf in later steps; using 0.11 V gives 0.73 A and a slightly different force.

**7.** The moving magnet changes the flux through the tube wall [1]; this induces emfs and so eddy currents in the aluminium [1]; by Lenz's law, the currents produce a field that opposes the motion, so the magnet is pushed back upwards [1]; the magnet's energy is transferred to thermal energy in the tube, so it falls at a lower speed than a non-magnetic object [1]
**[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the cause (changing flux), the effect (eddy currents) and the consequence (opposing force) in that order.

**8.** The emf is the rate of change of flux linkage [1]. At the maximum of the NΦ–t graph the gradient is zero, so the emf is zero [1]. Also, the sides of the coil are moving parallel to the field, so no field lines are cut [1]
**[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Saying the emf is large because the flux is large is a common error. The emf depends on the gradient, not the value.

**9. (a)** f = 1200/60 = 20 Hz [1]; ω = 2πf = **126 rad s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** BAN = 0.25 × (0.050 × 0.040) × 80 = 0.040 Wb [1]; ε₀ = BANω = 0.040 × 125.7 = **5.0 V** [1]
**(c)** ωt = 125.7 × 3.0 × 10⁻³ = 0.377 rad [1]; ε = 5.03 × sin 0.377 = **1.9 V** [1]
**(d)** Peak emf = **10 V** (ω doubles) [1]; period = **0.025 s** (half of 0.050 s) [1]
**[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert revolutions per minute to hertz before using 2π, and use radians in sin ωt.

**10. (a)** NΦ = BAN = 0.12 × 2.5 × 10⁻³ × 400 [1] = **0.12 Wb** [1]
**(b)** Final flux linkage is zero, so Δ(NΦ) = 0.12 Wb [1]; ε = 0.12 / 0.15 = **0.80 V** [1]
**(c)** I = 0.80 / 8.0 = **0.10 A** [1]
**(d)** The flux linkage into the page is decreasing [1]; the induced current opposes this by producing a field into the page inside the coil, so the current is **clockwise** [1]
**(e)** E = εIt = 0.80 × 0.10 × 0.15 = **0.012 J** [1]
**[8]**
*Examiner insight:* For the direction, state whether the flux is rising or falling before giving the sense of the current; the sense alone, however correct, shows no reasoning.

**11. (a)** ω = 2π/T = 2π / 0.040 = **157 rad s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** ε₀ = BANω = 0.080 × 5.0 × 10⁻³ × 150 × 157.1 [1] = **9.4 V** [1]
**(c)** At t = **0, 0.020 s and 0.040 s** [1]; at these times the coil plane is perpendicular to the field and the flux linkage is at its maximum (or a maximum in the opposite sense) [1]
**(d)** sin ωt = 0.50, so ωt = π/6 = 0.5236 rad [1]; t = 0.5236 / 157.1 = **3.3 × 10⁻³ s** [1]
**[7]**
*Examiner insight:* For (d), the exact angle π/6 (one twelfth of a period) gives a check on your answer.

**12.** B = F/(IL), so the tesla is N A⁻¹ m⁻¹, and Wb = T m² = N m A⁻¹ [1]; N m = J, so Wb = J A⁻¹ [1]; J = V A s, so Wb = V A s A⁻¹ = **V s** [1]
**[3]**
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" question, write each step in the chain of units; the final result alone does not show the working.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting the angle to the coil's plane into cos θ instead of the angle to the normal.
- Forgetting the number of turns, or including it in the flux through one turn.
- Using a flux value, not a change in flux, to find an emf.
- Not converting mWb, cm² or minutes to base units.
- Quoting a current direction with no statement about whether the flux linkage is rising or falling.
- Using degrees for sin ωt when ωt is in radians.
- Explaining eddy-current damping without mentioning the changing flux and the opposing force.
- Rounding the emf early.
