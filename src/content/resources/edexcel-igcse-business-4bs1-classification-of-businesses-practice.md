These questions cover section 1.3, Classification of businesses, of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1) specification, Issue 2, February 2026. Use the [classification of businesses study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-classification-of-businesses/) and the [classification of businesses revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-classification-of-businesses-revision-notes/) first if you need them. Every business is invented and amounts are in dollars. Give percentages to 1 decimal place where needed.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.**
**(a)** Define the primary sector. **[1]**
**(b)** Define the tertiary sector. **[1]**

**2.** State the sector of each business.
**(a)** A forestry company. **[1]**
**(b)** A car factory. **[1]**
**(c)** A hairdresser. **[1]**
**(d)** A house builder. **[1]**

**3.** Give two examples of tertiary businesses that a furniture manufacturer might use. **[2]**

**4.** Blackthorn Farm earns revenue from three activities: growing fruit 180,000, making jam 270,000 and running a farm shop 150,000. Calculate the percentage of its revenue that comes from the secondary sector. **[2]**

**5.** Complete the table. **[3]**

| Sector | Jobs | Share of total |
|---|---:|---:|
| Primary | 12,000 | ? |
| Secondary | ? | 35% |
| Tertiary | 27,000 | ? |
| Total | 60,000 | 100% |

**6.** Arden Fisheries runs four boats. Outline one reason why its revenue may vary from year to year. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Explain one way a business in the tertiary sector depends on the secondary sector. **[3]**

**8.** Halden Mills grinds wheat into flour and sells it to bakeries. A drought has cut the local wheat harvest by a third.
**(a)** State the sector in which Halden Mills operates. **[1]**
**(b)** Analyse the effect of the drought on Halden Mills and on a bakery that buys its flour. **[6]**

**9.** The table shows jobs in a region.

| Sector | Year 1 | Year 8 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Primary | 30,000 | 21,000 |
| Secondary | 70,000 | 63,000 |
| Tertiary | 100,000 | 126,000 |
| Total | 200,000 | 210,000 |

**(a)** Identify the sector with the largest increase in jobs. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in primary sector jobs. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the tertiary sector's share of jobs in Year 8. **[2]**
**(d)** Rowe Agri sells tractors and farm machinery in the region. Analyse what the data could mean for Rowe Agri. **[4]**

**10.** Tierra Coffee plc owns coffee farms, a roasting plant and 80 cafés. Its revenues last year were: farms 12 million, roasting 30 million, cafés 58 million.
**(a)** Calculate the share of revenue earned by the cafés. **[2]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether Tierra Coffee should sell its farms and buy beans from other farmers instead. **[8]**

**11.** Kofi has 40,000 to start a business. Option A is to rent land and grow vegetables. Option B is to buy a van and deliver vegetables from local farms to homes. Local farms have spare produce, and there is no delivery service in his town. Kofi has no farming experience but has worked as a driver. Justify which option Kofi should choose. **[6]**

**12.** Explain why a construction company is classified in the secondary sector. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The sector that extracts raw materials from the earth [1].
**(b)** The sector that provides a wide variety of services [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** Primary [1]. **(b)** Secondary [1]. **(c)** Tertiary [1]. **(d)** Secondary [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Building is converting materials into a finished structure, so it is secondary.

**3.** Any two of: a transport or delivery company [1], a bank [1], an insurance company, a furniture shop, an advertising agency. **[2]**

**4.** Total 180,000 + 270,000 + 150,000 = 600,000 [1]; 270,000 ÷ 600,000 × 100 = **45%** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Primary 12,000 ÷ 60,000 × 100 = **20%** [1]; secondary 35% × 60,000 = **21,000** [1]; tertiary 27,000 ÷ 60,000 × 100 = **45%** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Check: 12,000 + 21,000 + 27,000 = 60,000 and 20% + 35% + 45% = 100%.

**6.** Fishing is a primary activity that depends on natural conditions [1], so storms that keep the boats in harbour, or smaller fish stocks, can reduce the catch and so the revenue (or: the price of fish varies with supply) [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Tertiary businesses such as shops sell goods made by secondary businesses [1]. If manufacturers cannot make enough, for example because of a shortage of materials [1], shops have less to sell, so their sales and revenue fall [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** Secondary [1].
**(b)** With a third less wheat, Halden Mills may not get all the wheat it needs [1], and the wheat it can buy will cost more because supply has fallen [1]. Its costs rise, so it must raise flour prices or accept lower profit [1]. If it produces less flour, its revenue may fall [1]. The bakery then pays more for flour, or cannot get enough [1], so it may raise bread prices, which could lose customers, or cut its own margin [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Follow the effect along the chain: farm, mill, bakery.

**9. (a)** Tertiary [1].
**(b)** (21,000 − 30,000) ÷ 30,000 × 100 [1] = **−30%** [1].
**(c)** 126,000 ÷ 210,000 × 100 [1] = **60%** [1].
**(d)** Primary jobs have fallen by 30%, which suggests less farming in the region [1], so there may be fewer farms buying tractors and machinery [1]. Rowe Agri's sales could fall [1], so it may need to find customers further away or sell to other sectors, such as construction firms that use similar machines [1]. **[9]**

**10. (a)** Total 12 + 30 + 58 = 100 million [1]; cafés 58 ÷ 100 × 100 = **58%** [1].
**(b)** For selling: the farms earn only 12% of revenue [1], and selling them would raise money to open more cafés, which earn the most [1]. Tierra would also avoid risks such as bad weather and crop disease [1]. Against: owning farms gives Tierra control over the supply and quality of its beans [1], so its cafés can rely on a steady flow of the coffee they are known for [1]. If it buys from others, it may pay more when world prices rise [1], and a poor harvest elsewhere could leave it short [1]. Judgement: for example, keep the farms because quality and supply matter to a coffee brand, unless they make a loss that the cafés cannot cover [1]. **[10]**

**11.** Option B is the better choice [1]. Kofi has worked as a driver, so he has the skills for a delivery service, while he has no farming experience [1]. There is no delivery service in his town, so he faces no direct competition [1], and local farms have spare produce, so supply is available [1]. Option A is riskier: as a primary business it depends on weather, and inexperience could mean a poor first harvest [1]. The 40,000 may also go further on a van than on renting land, equipment and seeds [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use every fact in the case: experience, competition and supply.

**12.** A construction company converts raw materials such as stone, timber and steel [1] into a finished structure, such as a house or a bridge [1]. Converting materials into finished goods is the definition of the secondary sector, so it is not tertiary even though it works to a customer's order [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Classifying by the product sold instead of the activity carried out.
- Putting construction in the tertiary sector.
- Percentage change worked from the new figure instead of the old one.
- Listing effects without following the chain of production to the business named.
- Ending "evaluate" or "justify" answers without a decision.
