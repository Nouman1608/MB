## Section A

**1.** Name the effect that produces an e.m.f. in a coil when a magnet is pushed into it. **[1]**

**2.** State the material used for the core of a transformer. **[1]**

**3.** A wire carrying a current in a magnetic field experiences a force. State what happens to the force when the current is reversed. **[1]**

**4.** Name the coil of a transformer that is connected to (a) the input supply and (b) the output. **[2]**

**5.** State what is meant by a step-down transformer. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A coil is connected to a sensitive centre-zero meter. A student moves a bar magnet relative to the coil.

**(a)** Describe what the meter shows when the magnet is (i) pushed into the coil, (ii) held still inside the coil and (iii) pulled out. **[3]**
**(b)** State **two** changes that would give a larger deflection. **[2]**

**7.** A short copper rod rests across two horizontal rails between the poles of a U-shaped magnet. When the current is switched on, the rod rolls to the left.

**(a)** State the direction the rod rolls when the current is reversed. **[1]**
**(b)** The current is reversed **and** the magnet is turned over. State the direction the rod rolls. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest one change that would make the force on the rod larger. **[1]**

**8.** Explain how a loudspeaker uses the magnetic effect of a current to produce sound. **[3]**

**9.** A model railway needs 11.5 V from the 230 V mains. A transformer with 2000 turns on its primary coil is used.

**(a)** State whether this is a step-up or a step-down transformer. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of turns on the secondary coil. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe the construction of a simple transformer. **[2]**

**10.** Electricity is transmitted from power stations to homes at a very high voltage.

**(a)** Describe how transformers are used in this system. **[2]**
**(b)** State **two** advantages of transmitting electricity at a high voltage. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* This question is about a simple a.c. generator with a rotating coil.

**(a)** Describe how the generator produces an alternating e.m.f., and state the purpose of the slip rings and brushes. **[3]**
**(b)** State the position of the plane of the coil, relative to the magnetic field, when the e.m.f. is (i) at a peak and (ii) zero. **[2]**
**(c)** The coil is rotated twice as fast. State **two** ways in which the graph of e.m.f. against time changes. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)* This question is about the directions of electromagnetic forces and induced currents.

**(a)** The N pole of a magnet is pushed into a coil connected in a circuit. State the pole formed at the end of the coil nearest the magnet, and explain why. **[2]**
**(b)** A beam of electrons travels from left to right across the page into a magnetic field directed into the page. State the direction of the force on the electrons, and explain how you found it. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the purpose of the split-ring commutator in a d.c. motor. **[2]**

**13.** *(Extended)* At a power station, a 100% efficient transformer takes an input of 25 kV and 400 A and steps the voltage up to 400 kV. The transmission cables have a total resistance of 8.0 Ω.

**(a)** Calculate the current in the transmission cables. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the power lost in the cables. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the power that would be lost if the same power were sent at 25 kV. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain the principle of operation of the transformer. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **Electromagnetic induction** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **Soft iron** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The force **reverses** direction [1]. **[1]**

**4. (a)** **Primary** coil [1].
**(b)** **Secondary** coil [1]. **[2]**

**5.** A transformer whose output (secondary) voltage is less than its input (primary) voltage; it has fewer turns on the secondary [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** (i) The needle deflects one way [1]; (ii) it reads zero (no deflection) [1]; (iii) it deflects the opposite way [1].
**(b)** Any two: move the magnet faster; use a stronger magnet; use a coil with more turns [1 each]. **[5]**

*Common mistake:* saying the meter keeps reading while the magnet is still. An e.m.f. is induced only while the field linking the coil is changing.

**7. (a)** To the **right** [1].
**(b)** To the **left** (both reversals cancel) [1].
**(c)** Increase the current (or use a stronger magnet) [1]. **[3]**

**8.** A varying (alternating) current flows in a coil placed in the field of a permanent magnet [1]. The coil experiences a force that changes in size and direction with the current, so it moves back and forth [1]. The coil is attached to a cone, which vibrates and makes the air vibrate, producing sound waves [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** **Step-down** [1].
**(b)** Ns = Np × Vs / Vp = 2000 × 11.5 / 230 [1] = **100 turns** [1].
**(c)** Two coils of insulated wire, the primary and the secondary [1], wound on the same soft-iron core [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** A step-up transformer at the power station raises the voltage for transmission [1]; step-down transformers near homes lower it to the mains voltage [1].
**(b)** For the same power the current is smaller, so less energy is wasted heating the cables [1]; thinner (lighter, cheaper) cables can be used [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** A coil is rotated in a magnetic field, so its sides cut the field lines and an e.m.f. is induced [1]; each side moves up and then down through the field every turn, so the e.m.f. reverses every half turn [1]. Slip rings and brushes connect the rotating coil to the external circuit without the wires twisting [1].
**(b)** (i) Parallel to the field [1]; (ii) perpendicular to the field [1].
**(c)** The peaks are higher (larger maximum e.m.f.) [1]; there are more cycles in the same time (higher frequency, shorter period) [1]. **[7]**

**12. (a)** An **N pole** [1]; the induced current opposes the change causing it, so the coil repels the approaching N pole [1].
**(b)** **Downwards** (towards the bottom of the page) [1]; the conventional current is from right to left, opposite to the electron motion, and Fleming's left-hand rule gives a downward force [1].
**(c)** It reverses the current in the coil every half turn [1], so the forces on the sides of the coil keep turning it in the same direction [1]. **[6]**

*Common mistake:* in (b), applying the left-hand rule with the second finger along the electrons' motion. For electrons, the current is opposite to their direction of travel.

**13. (a)** Is = IpVp / Vs = (400 × 25 × 10³) / 400 × 10³ [1] = **25 A** [1].
**(b)** P = I²R = 25² × 8.0 [1] = **5000 W** (5.0 kW) [1].
**(c)** P = 400² × 8.0 = **1.28 × 10⁶ W** (1.3 MW) [1].
**(d)** An alternating current in the primary coil produces a changing magnetic field [1] in the soft-iron core, which links the secondary coil [1]; the changing field induces an alternating e.m.f. in the secondary coil [1]. **[8]**

*Common mistake:* using P = V²/R with 400 kV. That p.d. is not across the cables; use the cable current in P = I²R.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying an e.m.f. is induced when the magnet is stationary.
- Confusing the left-hand (motor) and right-hand (generator) rules.
- Placing the generator's peak e.m.f. at the perpendicular position.
- Forgetting to reverse the direction for electron beams.
- Explaining a transformer without the word "changing".
- Calculating cable loss from the transmission voltage instead of the current.
