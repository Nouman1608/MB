These notes condense section 3.1, Formulae, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). Worked examples for every skill are in the [formulae study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-formulae/); exam-style questions are in the [formulae practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-formulae-practice/).

## The seven outcomes, as tasks

1. Write formulae of named elements and compounds.
2. Define molecular formula.
3. Define empirical formula.
4. Deduce a formula from a model or diagram.
5. Deduce an ionic formula from ion charges.
6. Construct word, symbol and ionic equations with state symbols.
7. Deduce a symbol equation with state symbols from information.

## Definitions

- **Molecular formula**: the number and type of different atoms in one molecule.
- **Empirical formula**: the simplest whole number ratio of the different atoms or ions in a compound.

## Elements

- Diatomic: **H₂, N₂, O₂, F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂**.
- Everything else at this level: the symbol alone (Mg, Fe, C, Si, He).

## Compound ions

| Name | Formula | Charge |
|---|---|---|
| ammonium | NH₄⁺ | 1+ |
| hydroxide | OH⁻ | 1− |
| nitrate | NO₃⁻ | 1− |
| sulfate | SO₄²⁻ | 2− |
| carbonate | CO₃²⁻ | 2− |

Brackets when more than one compound ion is needed: Mg(OH)₂, (NH₄)₂CO₃, Al₂(SO₄)₃, Cu(NO₃)₂.

## Molecular → empirical

Divide all subscripts by their highest common factor.

C₂H₄ → CH₂ · C₂H₆ → CH₃ · C₆H₁₂O₆ → CH₂O · N₂O₄ → NO₂ · H₂O stays H₂O.

Ionic and giant covalent substances: formula = empirical formula (no molecules).

## From models and diagrams

- **Molecule**: count all atoms; do not simplify.
- **Lattice**: give the simplest ratio of ions or atoms.

## Equations checklist

1. Correct formula for every substance (diatomic elements!).
2. Balance with **coefficients only**.
3. State symbols: (s), (l), (g), (aq).
4. Count each element on both sides.

| Situation | State symbol |
|---|---|
| pure water, molten substance | (l) |
| dissolved acid, alkali or salt | (aq) |
| precipitate, metal, insoluble solid | (s) |
| gas given off, or gaseous reactant | (g) |

## Ionic equations: four steps

1. Full balanced equation with state symbols.
2. Split **(aq) ionic** compounds into ions.
3. Delete spectator ions (unchanged on both sides).
4. Check atoms **and** charges balance.

Must-know ionic equations:

- Neutralisation: H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l)
- Silver chloride precipitate: Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s)
- Barium sulfate precipitate: Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s)
- Copper(II) hydroxide precipitate: Cu²⁺(aq) + 2OH⁻(aq) → Cu(OH)₂(s)
- Zinc displacing copper: Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s)

## Turning words into an equation

Keywords in a description tell you formulae and states:

| Words in the question | What to write |
|---|---|
| "a solution of", "dissolved", "dilute ... acid" | (aq) |
| "a precipitate", "a solid forms" | (s) |
| "fizzes", "bubbles of ... gas", "a gas is given off" | (g) |
| "molten", "pure water" | (l) |
| "heated strongly" (one reactant) | thermal decomposition: one solid reactant |

Then: list every reactant and product, write each formula, balance, add the states. Example: "magnesium burns in oxygen to form a white solid" → 2Mg(s) + O₂(g) → 2MgO(s).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Compound ion | an ion made of more than one atom, e.g. SO₄²⁻ |
| Spectator ion | an ion present in the reaction mixture that is unchanged by the reaction |
| State symbol | label showing the physical state: (s), (l), (g), (aq) |
| Aqueous | dissolved in water |
| Coefficient | number in front of a formula in an equation |
| Subscript | small number within a formula showing how many atoms |

## Exam traps

- H₂O is (l), never (aq).
- Brackets around compound ions when there is more than one.
- Never change subscripts to balance.
- Metals and precipitates stay as (s) in ionic equations; do not split them.
- Charges must balance in an ionic equation as well as atoms.
- Molecular formulae are not simplified; empirical formulae are.

## Self-test

1. Define empirical formula.
2. Give the empirical formula of butene, C₄H₈.
3. Write the formula of aluminium hydroxide.
4. Write the formula of ammonium nitrate.
5. Why does magnesium oxide have only an empirical formula?
6. Balance: Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃.
7. Give the state symbol for silver chloride formed when two solutions are mixed.
8. Write the ionic equation for the reaction between aqueous lead(II) nitrate and aqueous potassium iodide, which forms solid lead(II) iodide.
9. Identify the spectator ions when aqueous sodium hydroxide reacts with hydrochloric acid.
10. Write the equation, with state symbols, for calcium carbonate decomposing on heating to calcium oxide and carbon dioxide.
11. A diagram of a lattice shows calcium and chloride ions in the ratio 1 : 2. Give the formula.
12. What is wrong with "Ca + Cl → CaCl₂"?

## Answers

1. The simplest whole number ratio of the different atoms or ions in a compound.
2. CH₂.
3. Al(OH)₃.
4. NH₄NO₃.
5. It is ionic: a giant lattice with no molecules, so its formula is the simplest ratio of ions.
6. 4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃.
7. (s).
8. Pb²⁺(aq) + 2I⁻(aq) → PbI₂(s).
9. Na⁺ and Cl⁻.
10. CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g).
11. CaCl₂.
12. Chlorine is diatomic: Ca + Cl₂ → CaCl₂.
