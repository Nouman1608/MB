This guide covers **Paper 1 Literary Genres**, one of two exam papers
in AQA A-Level English Literature B (7717), first teaching September
2015. English Literature B is distinctive among A-Level literature
specifications for its genre-led approach: rather than studying set
texts in isolation, candidates explore how texts work within and
against the conventions of a literary genre.

## Where this fits in 7717

Candidates study one genre option in depth for Paper 1, choosing
between Aspects of Tragedy and Aspects of Comedy. This pairs with
Paper 2 (Texts and Genres), which covers a different genre focus, and
a Non-exam Assessment component on Theory and Independence.

## Syllabus coverage

**AQA A-LEVEL ENGLISH LITERATURE B (7717) — PAPER 1 LITERARY GENRES**

- Option 1A: Aspects of Tragedy — a close study of tragedy as a
literary genre across a prescribed set of texts (drama, prose and
poetry), examining how writers use and adapt tragic conventions
- Option 1B: Aspects of Comedy — a close study of comedy as a literary
genre across a prescribed set of texts (drama, prose and poetry),
examining how writers use and adapt comic conventions

## How to approach it

Because this is a genre-led specification, the strongest answers treat
"tragedy" or "comedy" as a set of contested critical ideas rather than
a fixed checklist -- track how each of your set texts both follows and
departs from typical genre conventions, since AQA's assessment
objectives explicitly reward debate about genre rather than simple
identification of features. Comparing across the prescribed texts
within your chosen option (rather than writing about them in
isolation) is central to how Paper 1 is assessed, so practise drawing
connections between texts as a routine part of revision, not just in
the final weeks. Because contextual and critical interpretations are
also credited, build a small bank of critical viewpoints on your
genre and texts that you can draw on and challenge in your response.
