These questions cover section 16.1 (Passage of information from parents to offspring) of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. This is A Level content, examined in Paper 4. All data are invented for the exercise. ## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** In which stage of meiosis do homologous chromosomes move to opposite poles?

A: Anaphase I

B: Metaphase I

C: Anaphase II

D: Telophase II **[1]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by a homologous pair of chromosomes. **[2]**

**3.** A garden pea has a diploid number of 14. Calculate the number of genetically different gametes one plant can produce by random orientation of homologous pairs alone. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why a reduction division is needed in the production of gametes. **[2]**

**5.** Where and when does crossing over take place?

A: Between sister chromatids, in prophase II

B: Between non-sister chromatids, in prophase I

C: Between sister chromatids, in metaphase I

D: Between non-sister chromatids, in anaphase I **[1]**

**6.** A diploid cell with 20 chromosomes contains 30 arbitrary units of DNA immediately after DNA replication. State the number of chromosomes and the mass of DNA in each gamete produced from it. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** Identify the stage of meiosis described in each case, giving one reason.

**(a)** Pairs of chromosomes are lined up across the middle of a single cell. **[2]**

**(b)** In each of two cells, single chromatids are being pulled towards the poles. **[2]**

**(c)** In a single cell, chromosomes each made of two chromatids are moving towards opposite poles. **[2]**

**8.** Describe the behaviour of the chromosomes, the nuclear envelope and the spindle during meiosis I in an animal cell. **[5]**

**9.** Explain how meiosis and fertilisation lead to genetic variation among offspring. **[5]**

**10.** The DNA content of a single cell, and then of the cells produced from it, was measured during meiosis.

| Time / h | 0 | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DNA per cell / arbitrary units | 8 | 12 | 16 | 16 | 8 | 4 |

**(a)** State the time period during which DNA replication takes place. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain the decreases in DNA per cell after 12 hours. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the DNA content of each gamete is half that of the cell at 0 hours. **[1]**

**11.** Describe two differences between meiosis in a plant cell and meiosis in an animal cell. **[2]**

**12.** One homologous pair carries the alleles A and B on the maternal chromosome and a and b on the paternal chromosome. The two loci are far apart on the chromosome.

**(a)** State the allele combinations in gametes if no crossing over occurs between the loci. **[1]**

**(b)** State the new allele combinations produced if crossing over occurs between the loci. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how these new combinations arise. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A [1]

**2.** Two chromosomes, one from each parent, with the same genes at the same loci [1]. They have the same length and centromere position but may carry different alleles [1].

**3.** n = 7, so 2⁷ = **128** [1]

**4.** Gametes must contain half the chromosome number (haploid) so that fertilisation restores the diploid number [1]. Otherwise the chromosome number would double in every generation [1].

**5.** B [1]

**6.** 10 chromosomes [1]; 30 ÷ 4 = 7.5 arbitrary units of DNA [1].

**7.**

**(a)** Metaphase I [1]: homologous chromosomes are paired as bivalents on the equator [1].

**(b)** Anaphase II [1]: there are already two cells, and centromeres have divided so single chromatids are moving [1].

**(c)** Anaphase I [1]: centromeres have not divided, so whole chromosomes of a homologous pair are separating [1].

**8.** In prophase I chromosomes condense and homologous chromosomes pair to form bivalents; crossing over occurs [1]. The nuclear envelope breaks down and a spindle forms from the centrioles [1]. In metaphase I bivalents line up on the equator and spindle fibres attach to the centromeres [1]. In anaphase I spindle fibres shorten and homologous chromosomes are pulled to opposite poles, each still with two chromatids [1]. In telophase I nuclear envelopes may re-form and the cell surface membrane pinches in to give two haploid cells [1].

**9.** Crossing over in prophase I exchanges sections between non-sister chromatids, producing new combinations of alleles [1]. Random orientation of homologous pairs at metaphase I gives different mixtures of maternal and paternal chromosomes in gametes [1]. Random orientation of the now different sister chromatids at metaphase II adds further variety [1]. Together these mean each gamete is genetically different [1]. Random fertilisation means any of these gametes can fuse with any gamete from the other parent, so each zygote is genetically different [1].

**10.**

**(a)** Between 0 and 8 hours [1]

**(b)** The fall from 16 to 8 units is meiosis I, where homologous chromosomes go to two cells; the fall from 8 to 4 units is meiosis II, where sister chromatids go to two cells [1]. Each division shares the DNA equally between two cells, halving the DNA per cell [1].

**(c)** DNA doubles once in replication but is halved twice in the two divisions, so the final content is half the starting value [1].

**11.** Animal cells have centrioles that organise the spindle; plant cells have none [1]. In cytokinesis, animal cell surface membranes pinch in, whereas plant cells form a new cell wall between the daughter cells [1].

**12.**

**(a)** AB and ab [1]

**(b)** Ab and aB [1]

**(c)** During prophase I, a chiasma forms between non-sister chromatids at a point between the two loci [1]. The chromatids break and rejoin, exchanging sections, so one chromatid now carries A with b and another a with B [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "identical" for homologous chromosomes.
- Placing crossing over between sister chromatids or in metaphase.
- Saying centromeres divide in anaphase I.
- Confusing chromosome counts with chromatid counts.
- Naming a stage without giving visible evidence.
- Missing random fertilisation in variation answers.
