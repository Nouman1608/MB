## Fractions

| Operation | Method |
|---|---|
| Add or subtract | Common denominator, then combine numerators |
| Multiply | Cancel across, then numerator × numerator, denominator × denominator |
| Divide | Keep, flip, multiply |
| Mixed numbers | Convert to improper fractions first |
| a/b of N | (a × N) ÷ b |
| Find the whole | Part ÷ fraction |

**Method in steps**

1. Write mixed numbers as improper fractions.
2. For + or −, find a common denominator. For ×, cancel first. For ÷, flip the second fraction.
3. Simplify the result.
4. Convert back to a mixed number only if the question asks for one.

**Reminder:** 2 3/8 − 7/12. Convert: 19/8 − 7/12 = 57/24 − 14/24 = 43/24 = **1 19/24**.

**Reminder:** 4/9 of a quantity is 28, so the whole is 28 × 9/4 = **63**.

Keep fractions rather than rounded decimals, because 1/3 is exact and 0.33 is not.

## Multiples of π

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Circumference | πd or 2πr |
| Area of circle | πr² |
| Sector area | (θ/360) × πr² |
| Arc length | (θ/360) × 2πr |
| Cylinder volume | πr²h |
| Cylinder curved surface | 2πrh |

**Method in steps**

1. Substitute into the formula and keep π as a symbol.
2. Collect like terms: 9π + 4π = 13π.
3. Write the unit and "in terms of π" if asked.
4. Never replace π with a decimal until, and unless, a decimal is requested.

**Reminder:** a semicircle of diameter 8 has perimeter 4π + 8 and area (1/2) × π × 16 = 8π.

**Reminder:** the area of a circle is 100π, so r² = 100, r = 10 and the circumference is 20π.

Ratios of circle areas: radii 3 and 5 give 9π : 25π = 9 : 25. The π cancels.

## Surds (Higher tier only)

A surd is an irrational root such as √2 or √11. A root that is a whole number, such as √49, is not a surd.

**Rules**

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| √a × √b = √(ab) | √6 × √15 = √90 = 3√10 |
| √a ÷ √b = √(a/b) | √98 ÷ √2 = √49 = 7 |
| √a × √a = a | (2√3)² = 12 |
| Roots do not split over addition | √(9 + 16) = 5, whereas √9 + √16 = 7 |

**Simplify a surd**

1. List square numbers: 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100.
2. Find the largest that divides the number.
3. Take its root outside: √75 = √(25 × 3) = 5√3.

**Add and subtract:** simplify each surd first, then collect matching surds.

**Expand:** multiply every term by every term. Surd × same surd gives a whole number.

**Difference of two squares:** (a + √b)(a − √b) = a² − b. For example (√5 + 2)(√5 − 2) = 5 − 4 = 1.

## Rationalising the denominator (Higher tier only)

| Bottom of fraction | Multiplier (top and bottom) | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| √k | √k | rational denominator k |
| a + √k | a − √k | denominator a² − k |
| a − √k | a + √k | denominator a² − k |
| √k − √m | √k + √m | denominator k − m |

**Method in steps**

1. Simplify any surd in the denominator first.
2. Choose the multiplier: the surd itself, or the conjugate.
3. Multiply the numerator carefully, expanding every bracket.
4. Simplify the whole fraction, dividing through if possible.

**Reminder:** 10/√5 = 10√5/5 = **2√5**.

**Reminder:** 3/(√7 + 2) = 3(√7 − 2)/(7 − 4) = 3(√7 − 2)/3 = **√7 − 2**.

## More worked reminders

**Fractions in sequence.** Work out 3/4 × 5/6 and then 3/5 × 10/9.

```
3/4 × 5/6 = 15/24 = 5/8
3/5 × 10/9 = 30/45 = 2/3   (or cancel first: 3/9 = 1/3, then 1/3 × 2 = 2/3)
```

Mixed operations: 7/10 − 1/4 + 1/2 = 14/20 − 5/20 + 10/20 = **19/20**.

**A compound solid in terms of π.** A cylinder has radius 2 and height 7. A hemisphere of radius 2 sits on top. If the volume of a hemisphere is (2/3)πr³, then:

```
Cylinder: π × 2² × 7 = 28π
Hemisphere: 2/3 × π × 8 = 16π/3
Total: 28π + 16π/3 = 84π/3 + 16π/3 = 100π/3
```

Answer: **100π/3** cubic units. A fraction multiple of π is still exact.

**Squaring and expanding surds (Higher tier only).**

```
(√7 + 3)² = 7 + 3√7 + 3√7 + 9 = 16 + 6√7
(5 - √2)(1 + √2) = 5 + 5√2 - √2 - 2 = 3 + 4√2
```

**Rationalising with simplification (Higher tier only).**

```
6/(√10 - 2) × (√10 + 2)/(√10 + 2)
Denominator: 10 - 4 = 6
Numerator: 6(√10 + 2)
6(√10 + 2)/6 = 2 + √10
```

**Exact lengths (Higher tier only).** A rectangle measures 3√2 by 2√6. Its area is 3√2 × 2√6 = 6√12 = 6 × 2√3 = **12√3**. Its diagonal squared is 18 + 24 = 42, so the diagonal is **√42**. Leave √42 as it is, because 42 has no square factor.

## Exact answers in the exam

- Read the instruction. "In terms of π", "exact" and "without a calculator" all point to symbols, not decimals.
- Show each stage on separate lines. A short chain of lines that can be followed is better than one crowded line.
- Check a surd answer against a rough decimal in your head, for example √50 is a little over 7, so 5√2 is about 7.07. This is a sense check only, and the exact form is what you write down.
- If an answer asks for the form a + b√c, finish by writing it in exactly that form.
- When asked to "show that", write every line of the argument, including the expansion and the conjugate, so that nothing is left to be assumed.

## Must-know distinctions

- Exact (5/3, 12π, 4√5) is not the same as approximate (1.67, 37.7, 8.94). Follow the question wording.
- √9 + √16 = 7 but √25 = 5. Roots do not split over addition.
- Simplifying a surd is not the same as rationalising a denominator.
- Rational numbers can be written as a fraction of integers. Surds cannot.
- A fraction of an amount multiplies. Finding the whole divides.

## Quick self-test

Do these without a calculator, then check the answers.

1. Work out 5/8 + 7/12.
2. Work out 2 2/5 ÷ 3/10.
3. A circle has diameter 12 cm. Give its circumference and its area in terms of π.
4. A circle has area 100π. Give its circumference in terms of π.
5. (Higher tier only) Simplify √75.
6. (Higher tier only) Simplify √6 × √15.
7. (Higher tier only) Expand and simplify (√5 + 2)(√5 − 2).
8. (Higher tier only) Rationalise 10/√5.
9. (Higher tier only) Rationalise 3/(√7 + 2).
10. (Higher tier only) Work out (2√3)².
11. (Higher tier only) Simplify 2√12 + √27.
12. A sector has angle 90° and radius 8 cm. Give its area and its arc length in terms of π.

### Answers

1. 15/24 + 14/24 = **29/24** (1 5/24).
2. 12/5 × 10/3 = **8**.
3. Circumference 12π cm. Area π × 6² = 36π cm². **12π cm and 36π cm²**.
4. r = 10, so circumference = 2π × 10 = **20π**.
5. **5√3**.
6. √90 = **3√10**.
7. 5 − 4 = **1**.
8. 10√5/5 = **2√5**.
9. 3(√7 − 2)/3 = **√7 − 2**.
10. 4 × 3 = **12**.
11. 2 × 2√3 + 3√3 = 4√3 + 3√3 = **7√3**.
12. Area = 1/4 × π × 64 = 16π. Arc = 1/4 × 2π × 8 = 4π. **16π cm² and 4π cm**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding fractions by adding the denominators.
- Failing to cancel before multiplying, which produces big numbers and errors.
- Dividing by the wrong fraction. Only the second fraction is flipped.
- Writing 3.14 or a calculator value for π when the question asks for an answer in terms of π.
- Using the diameter in a formula that needs the radius.
- Leaving √48 or √72 without checking for the largest square factor.
- Treating √(a + b) as √a + √b.
- Using only the surd to rationalise a two-term denominator.
- Sign errors in the conjugate or in the middle terms of an expansion.
- Not showing the working in an exact question. A bare decimal does not show an exact method.
