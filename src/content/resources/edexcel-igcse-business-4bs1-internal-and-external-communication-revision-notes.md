Quick recall for section 2.1, Internal and external communication, of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1) specification, Issue 2, February 2026. Worked examples are in the [internal and external communication study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-internal-and-external-communication/); exam-style questions are in the [internal and external communication practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-internal-and-external-communication-practice/). The specification lists no particular methods or barriers, so the examples here are standard ones. Figures are invented and in dollars.

## What the specification lists

- 2.1.1 Importance of good communication and the problems of ineffective communication; the different communication methods used, including information technology (IT), and the benefits and limitations of each method.
- 2.1.2 Barriers to communication: how they arise; problems of ineffective communication and how they can be removed.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Communication | Passing information from a sender to a receiver so that it is understood as intended |
| Internal communication | Within the business |
| External communication | With people outside the business: customers, suppliers, lenders, government |
| Method | The way a message is sent |
| Feedback | The receiver's response, showing whether the message was understood |
| Barrier | Anything that stops a message arriving or being understood |
| Jargon | Technical language the receiver may not know |
| Information overload | Receiving more messages than can be dealt with |

## The process

Sender → message → method → receiver → feedback. A barrier can arise at any point.

## Methods: benefit and limitation

| Method | Benefit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Face-to-face | Immediate feedback; tone helps | No record unless noted; takes time |
| Meeting | Several people discuss at once | Time-consuming; some may not speak |
| Telephone | Quick and personal over distance | No record; no visual cues |
| Letter | Formal record | Slow; costly |
| Notice board | Cheap; reaches many | May not be read; no check |
| Email (IT) | Fast, cheap, record, many receivers | Overload; tone misread |
| Video call (IT) | Face-to-face over distance | Needs equipment and a good connection |
| Intranet or website (IT) | Available any time, one place | Must be updated; set-up cost |
| Social media (IT) | Reaches many customers; two-way | Public, so mistakes spread |

## Choosing a method: seven questions

Urgency, detail, record, two-way, sensitivity, number of receivers, cost and access.

- Sensitive personal news: face to face, then in writing.
- Detailed information to many: email or intranet.
- Urgent safety warning: spoken announcement plus notice.

## Why good communication matters

- Fewer mistakes and less waste.
- Better decisions from accurate, timely information.
- Higher morale; fewer rumours.
- Customers and suppliers get correct information, so sales and supplies run smoothly.

Reminder: 2% of 15,000 orders wrong at 30 each costs 0.02 × 15,000 × 30 = 9,000 a year.

## Barriers and remedies

| Arises from | Barrier | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Sender | Jargon, unclear message | Plain language |
| Method | Wrong method, technology failure | Better choice; back-up |
| Receiver | Overload, not listening, language | Short messages, translation, feedback |
| Organisation | Too many levels, distance | Direct messages; fewer levels |
| Attitude | Distrust | Honest, regular, two-way updates |

Reminder: through three levels each losing 20%, 0.8 × 0.8 × 0.8 = 0.512, so about half the detail arrives.

## Small and large businesses

- Paper 1 (small): mostly face to face; fewer levels; owner can check understanding directly; limited money for IT systems.
- Paper 2 (large): more levels, sites and languages; relies on email, intranet and video calls; greater risk of overload and distortion.

## Exam traps

- A method with a benefit but no limitation, or the reverse.
- A barrier named with no cause or remedy.
- "Communication is important" with no effect on cost, sales or morale.
- Forgetting the receiver's access to technology.
- External communication treated as advertising only.
- "Explain" answers with only one expansion point.

## Self-test

1. Define internal communication.
2. Give two external groups a business communicates with.
3. Name the part of the process that shows whether a message was understood.
4. Give one benefit and one limitation of a notice board.
5. Which method would you choose to tell an employee about a disciplinary problem, and why?
6. Give one benefit of video calls for a business with sites in several countries.
7. What is jargon, and how can it be removed as a barrier?
8. A message passes through two levels, each losing 25% of the detail. What share arrives?
9. Give two problems caused by ineffective communication.
10. Why might barriers be greater in a large business?
11. 4% of 5,000 deliveries go to the wrong address, each costing 25 to fix. Calculate the yearly cost.
12. What does "explain" require, according to the specification?

### Answers

1. Communication within the business.
2. Any two of: customers, suppliers, banks or lenders, the government, the public.
3. Feedback.
4. Benefit: cheap and reaches many staff. Limitation: may not be read, and there is no check of understanding.
5. Face to face, because it is sensitive and needs two-way discussion; then confirm in writing for a record.
6. Managers can meet face to face without the cost and time of travel.
7. Technical language the receiver may not know; use plain language or explain the terms.
8. 0.75 × 0.75 = 0.5625, about 56%.
9. Any two of: mistakes, wasted time, low morale, rumours, poor decisions, lost customers.
10. Messages pass through more levels and sites, and staff may speak different languages.
11. 0.04 × 5,000 × 25 = 5,000.
12. A statement of fact with two further expansion points.
