## 4.1.1 The role of government

| Level | Typical roles | Short example |
|---|---|---|
| Local | Local services, local land use, local licences | A council decides where a new housing estate may be built |
| National | Laws, courts, police, defence; tax and spending; funding or providing schooling and health; regulation; the macroeconomic aims | A national government taxes incomes to fund schools |
| International | Agreements, trade rules, joint action on cross-border problems, aid | Governments agree shared limits on a polluting gas |

Remember the logic: match the level to the scale of the problem. A problem that crosses borders cannot be solved by one country alone, because others can act differently.

Why governments act at all: markets under-supply some goods, ignore some harm to third parties, leave incomes unequal and cannot guarantee stable output, jobs and prices.

## 4.2.1 The five aims

| Aim | Meaning | How success is judged |
|---|---|---|
| Economic growth | Rise in real output over time | Real GDP growth rate against a target range |
| Full employment / low unemployment | Almost all who want work have it | Unemployment rate against a ceiling |
| Stable prices / low inflation | Low, predictable rise in prices | Inflation rate within a band |
| Balance of payments stability | No large, lasting deficit or surplus | Current account as a share of GDP within a limit |
| Redistribution of income | Narrower gap between rich and poor | Income shares, or numbers below a poverty line |

A **criterion** is the measurable standard a government sets for an aim. Numerical criteria let progress be checked and the government held to account.

### Formulas

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Unemployment rate | unemployed / labour force x 100, where labour force = employed + unemployed |
| Real GDP | nominal GDP / price index x 100 |
| Real growth | (real GDP now - real GDP before) / real GDP before x 100 |
| Inflation rate | change in the price index / earlier index x 100 |
| Current account balance | add the four balances: goods, services, primary income, secondary income |
| Balance as a share of GDP | balance / GDP x 100 |
| Average tax rate | tax paid / income x 100 |

### Method in steps: any calculation

1. Write the formula.
2. Substitute with units or labels.
3. Calculate and state the result with a unit (%, percentage points, currency units).
4. Compare with the criterion if the question gives one.

### Worked reminders

```
Unemployment: 6,650 in work, 350 jobless
Labour force = 7,000; rate = 350 / 7,000 x 100 = 5.0%
Criterion 4%: 0.04 x 7,000 = 280, so 70 fewer jobless are needed
```

```
Real growth: nominal GDP 1,350 -> 1,458; price index 100 -> 108
Real GDP = 1,458 / 1.08 = 1,350; real growth = 0%
Nominal growth = 8.0%, which is all price rise
```

```
Inflation: index 250 -> 262.5 = 12.5 / 250 x 100 = 5.0%
Current account -12 on GDP 600 = -2.0% of GDP
```

```
Tax: nothing to 5,000, then 20% above
Income 35,000: (35,000 - 5,000) x 20% = 6,000; average rate 17.14%
Income 12,000: (12,000 - 5,000) x 20% = 1,400; average rate 11.67%
A rising average rate narrows the gap: the tax is progressive.
```

### Reasons behind the choice of aims

- Each aim links to living standards or fairness.
- Current problems set priorities, for example high unemployment or a large deficit.
- Stage of development: poorer countries often stress growth; richer ones stress stability.
- Public pressure: voters notice jobs and prices.
- Measurability: criteria make results checkable.

## 4.2.2 Conflicts between aims

| Conflict | Chain of cause and effect | Weaker when |
|---|---|---|
| Full employment v stable prices | Higher demand, more hiring, scarce labour, higher wages and costs, higher prices | There is spare capacity; unemployment falls through better skills; output can rise |
| Growth v balance of payments | Higher incomes, more imports bought, imports rise faster than exports | Growth is export-led; imports are machinery raising future output; the country starts in surplus |
| Full employment v balance of payments | New jobs, more income, spending on imports; higher prices make exports dearer | New jobs are in export or import-replacing firms |

Worked reminders:

```
Imports are 25% of GDP. GDP rises from 400 to 420 and exports stay at 90.
Imports 100 -> 105; balance 90 - 100 = -10 -> 90 - 105 = -15
```

```
1,000 new workers earn 30 each = 30,000; if 20% goes on imports: 6,000 extra imports
```

### Must-know distinctions

- **Real versus nominal.** Real removes price changes; growth criteria use real output.
- **Percentage versus percentage points.** Inflation rising from 2.0% to 3.5% is up 1.5 percentage points, not 1.5%.
- **Low unemployment versus zero.** Some unemployment always exists as people change jobs.
- **Stable versus fixed.** Stable prices still rise slowly; stability means low and predictable.
- **Aim versus criterion.** The aim is the goal; the criterion is the number that tests it.

### Evaluation toolkit for conflicts

Ask five things: how much spare capacity exists; whether the extra demand is spent on home goods or imports; whether the problem is short run or long run; where the new jobs or growth come from; and which aim matters most for this country now. Then give a judgement.

### Chains to learn

```
Jobs v prices:    demand up -> output and hiring up -> unemployment down -> wages up -> costs up -> prices up
Growth v balance: incomes up -> imports bought up -> imports exceed exports -> deficit widens
Jobs v balance:   new jobs -> new incomes -> share spent on imports -> exports less competitive if prices rise
```

Each chain should end by naming the aim that suffers. A good answer then adds one condition that weakens the conflict and one sentence of judgement.

### Planning a discuss answer

1. Define the two aims in a clause each.
2. Give the chain showing the clash, with a figure if the question supplies one.
3. Give a counter-case in which the clash is weak or absent.
4. State which aim matters more for this country now, and why.

### Redistribution of income in brief

Redistribution moves purchasing power from higher to lower earners. A progressive tax takes a larger share of higher incomes, and the revenue can fund benefits and public services that low earners use more. Judge success by a narrowing gap, for example a falling ratio of after-tax incomes. Evaluation: very high tax rates may weaken incentives to work, and redistribution may cost growth in the short run.

### Why numerical criteria matter

A target such as "annual inflation between 1% and 3%" can be tested. It lets voters judge the government, helps firms plan, and shows when policy must change. A vague aim such as "a strong economy" cannot be tested. Criteria are usually set over a period, such as a year, so that short-lived swings do not look like failure.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the five macroeconomic aims in the syllabus.
2. Give one role of government at the local level.
3. State why a cross-border problem needs international action.
4. In Country X, 7,600 people are employed and 400 are unemployed. Calculate the unemployment rate.
5. Nominal GDP rises from 900 to 945 while the price index rises from 100 to 105. Calculate real growth.
6. A price index rises from 250 to 262.5. Calculate inflation.
7. The inflation rate rises from 2.5% to 6.0%. State the change in percentage points.
8. Tax is 0% to 5,000 and 20% above. Calculate the tax and the average rate on 35,000.
9. Name the three conflicts between aims in the syllabus.
10. Give one situation in which the conflict between growth and the balance of payments is weaker.

### Answers

1. Economic growth; full employment/low unemployment; stable prices/low inflation; balance of payments stability; redistribution of income.
2. For example, a council maintains local roads or decides land use.
3. The problem crosses borders and one country cannot control what others do, so joint action with shared rules is needed.
4. Labour force = 8,000; rate = 400 / 8,000 x 100 = 5.0%.
5. Real GDP = 945 / 1.05 = 900; real growth = 0%.
6. (262.5 - 250) / 250 x 100 = 5.0%.
7. 3.5 percentage points.
8. Tax = (35,000 - 5,000) x 20% = 6,000; average rate = 6,000 / 35,000 x 100 = 17.14%.
9. Full employment versus stable prices; economic growth versus balance of payments stability; full employment versus balance of payments stability.
10. For example, when growth is led by exports, or when the extra imports are machinery that raises future output.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming an aim with no reason for the government choosing it.
- Calculating unemployment as a share of those in work.
- Quoting nominal rather than real growth.
- Writing percent when the question wants percentage points.
- Saying "full employment means no unemployment".
- Describing a conflict as a bare claim with no chain of cause and effect.
- Mixing up which two aims clash, for example saying growth conflicts with employment.
- Giving a discussion with no judgement, or a judgement not tied to the case.
- Giving a role of government at the wrong level, such as naming defence as a local role.
