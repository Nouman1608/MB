Condensed notes for section 5.2 of Cambridge IGCSE Commerce (0715). For fuller explanations, worked examples and an annotated answer, use the [banking study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-commerce-0715-banking/).

## The syllabus in two parts

1. **5.2.1 Banking services:** accounts, customer services, payments, sources of finance, and choosing the appropriate service.
2. **5.2.2 Digital technology in banking:** internet and mobile banking, advantages and disadvantages, and the impact.

## Four groups of banking service

| Group | Services |
|---|---|
| Accounts | Current; deposit/savings |
| Customer services | ATM; night safe; foreign currency; financial advice; telephone banking |
| Payments | Cash; cheque; credit card; debit card; store card; credit transfer; standing order; direct debit; electronic transfer; documentary credit (letter of credit) |
| Sources of finance | Loan; overdraft; mortgage; hire purchase |

## Accounts

| | Current | Deposit/savings |
|---|---|---|
| Use | Everyday payments | Saving |
| Access | Any time | May need notice or have limits |
| Interest | Little or none | Pays interest |
| Extras | Cheque book, debit card | Fewer payment tools |

## Customer services in one line each

- **ATM:** cash and balance at any hour.
- **Night safe:** deposit takings after the bank closes.
- **Foreign currency:** exchange money for travel or trade.
- **Financial advice:** guidance on saving, borrowing and investment.
- **Telephone banking:** manage the account by phone.

## Payment methods compared

| Method | Key point |
|---|---|
| Cash | Immediate; no record; theft risk |
| Cheque | Written order; can bounce; slow |
| Credit card | Borrowing; pay later; interest if not cleared |
| Debit card | Money leaves account at once |
| Store card | Credit card for one shop |
| Credit transfer | Payer moves money to another account |
| Standing order | Fixed amount, set by the payer, regular dates |
| Direct debit | Variable amount, collected by the receiver, with permission |
| Electronic transfer | Computer transfer; fast, cheap |
| Documentary credit | Bank pays against shipping documents; protects importer and exporter |

Memory aid: **S**tanding order = **S**ame amount; **D**irect debit = **D**ifferent amounts.

## Finance from banks

| Source | Key point | Term |
|---|---|---|
| Loan | Fixed sum repaid with interest | Medium |
| Overdraft | Spend more than balance, up to a limit | Short |
| Mortgage | Loan for property, secured on it | Long |
| Hire purchase | Deposit plus instalments; ownership after final payment | Medium |

## Choosing the right service

| Need | Choose |
|---|---|
| Deposit takings after hours | Night safe |
| Pay fixed rent each month | Standing order |
| Pay variable utility bill | Direct debit |
| Buy from overseas supplier safely | Documentary credit |
| Cover a short cash shortage | Overdraft |
| Buy a vehicle over time | Loan or hire purchase |
| Buy property | Mortgage |
| Travel abroad | Foreign currency |

Test every answer by asking: how much, for how long, how quickly, how secure?

## Digital banking

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Internet banking | Accounts managed via a website |
| Mobile banking | Accounts managed via an app on a phone or tablet |

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Open 24 hours a day | Needs a device and internet |
| No travel or queues | Fraud, hacking, phishing |
| Lower costs and fees | Difficult for some customers |
| Instant transfers and alerts | Cannot deposit cash easily |
| Easy tracking of spending | Outages and power cuts |

**Impact (three effects named in the syllabus):**

1. Closure of bank branches.
2. More digital-only banks.
3. Less use of cash (a cashless society).

Add a fair view: gains in speed and cost, but risks for people without technology, rural customers and small traders.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Current account | Account for everyday payments |
| Deposit account | Account that earns interest on savings |
| Overdraft | Permission to spend more than the balance, to a limit |
| Mortgage | Long-term loan secured on property |
| Hire purchase | Buying by deposit and instalments, with ownership at the end |
| Standing order | Fixed regular payment set up by the payer |
| Direct debit | Variable payment collected by the receiver with permission |
| Documentary credit | Bank guarantee of payment on proof of shipment |
| Digital banking | Banking by internet or mobile app |
| Cashless society | Economy where most payments are not made in notes and coins |
| Interest | Charge for borrowing, or reward for saving |

## Exam traps

- A **debit card** is not credit.
- **Standing orders** are fixed; **direct debits** can vary.
- **Overdraft** is for short-term shortages; **loan** and **mortgage** are longer.
- A **night safe** is for depositing, not for withdrawing.
- Digital banking answers need both sides, plus a decision.
- For hire purchase, add the deposit **and** the instalments.

## Self-test

1. Name the two types of account in the syllabus.
2. What is a night safe used for?
3. Give **one** difference between a credit card and a debit card.
4. Which is fixed, a standing order or a direct debit?
5. What is documentary credit?
6. Define overdraft.
7. Which banking service suits buying a home?
8. State **two** advantages of mobile banking.
9. State **two** disadvantages of digital banking.
10. Name **two** impacts of digital banking.
11. What is meant by a cashless society?
12. A machine costs $900 cash or $150 deposit plus 6 payments of $135 on hire purchase. How much extra does hire purchase cost?

**Answers**

1. Current account; deposit (savings) account.
2. Depositing takings safely after the bank has closed.
3. A credit card lends money to be repaid later, perhaps with interest; a debit card takes the money straight from the account.
4. A standing order.
5. A bank's promise to pay an exporter once documents show that goods have been shipped (a letter of credit).
6. Permission from the bank to spend more than the balance, up to a set limit.
7. A mortgage.
8. Any two: available at any time; no travel; instant transfers; easy tracking of spending.
9. Any two: fraud and hacking; needs internet and a device; hard for some customers; system failure.
10. Any two: closure of bank branches; more digital-only banks; less use of cash.
11. An economy in which most payments are made by card, phone or transfer instead of cash.
12. Instalments: 6 x 135 = 810. Total = 150 + 810 = $960. Extra cost = 960 - 900 = $60.
