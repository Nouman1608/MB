## Water cycle recall

**Stores**
- Salt water: oceans.
- Fresh water: ice sheets and glaciers; ground water; atmosphere; lakes and rivers.

**Transfers, grouped by direction**
- Upwards into the air: evaporation (from water surfaces), transpiration (from leaves).
- In the air: condensation (vapour → droplets, forming clouds).
- Downwards: precipitation; interception (caught by vegetation first).
- Into and through the ground: infiltration (into soil), through-flow (sideways in soil), ground water flow (through rock).
- Over the ground: surface run-off.

**Land use effects to reason through**
- Deforestation: less interception and transpiration → more, faster surface run-off → higher flood risk and soil erosion.
- Urban surfaces (concrete, tarmac): almost no infiltration → rapid run-off into drains and rivers.
- Ground water use faster than infiltration refills it → water table falls and wells dry up.

## Sources, treatment and access

| Source | Example | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Atmosphere | Roof rainwater tank; snowmelt | Cheap, local | Seasonal; limited storage |
| Surface water | River intake; reservoir | Large volumes, easy to reach | Pollution; evaporation |
| Ground water | Well into an aquifer | Often cleaner; available in dry season | Slow to refill; pumping cost |
| Oceans | Desalination plant | Unlimited at the coast | Energy cost; brine |

**Treatment stages and what each does**
- Screening: removes large floating objects.
- Sedimentation: suspended particles settle out in still tanks.
- Filtration: sand and gravel trap fine particles.
- Chlorination: kills bacteria and other microorganisms.

**Access comparisons**
- Water-rich vs water-poor: depends on rainfall, rivers and aquifers, but wealth decides whether water is treated and piped.
- Urban vs rural: piped supply is cheaper per person where people live close together; scattered rural households rely more on untreated sources and long walks.
- Global: the lowest access to potable water and to sewage treatment is in low-income countries; missing sewage treatment pollutes the water sources people use.

## Desalination and dams

**Distillation**: boil sea water → salt stays in the liquid → steam (pure water) cooled and condensed.
**Reverse osmosis**: high pressure → membrane lets water molecules through but stops most ions and other molecules.

| Desalination benefits | Desalination limitations |
|---|---|
| Reliable in drought | High energy use and cost |
| Unlimited source at the coast | Carbon dioxide if fossil-fuel powered |
| Eases pressure on aquifers | Brine harms marine life |
| | Costly to pump inland |

**Dam uses (FHISTRTF)**: Flood control, Hydro-electric power, Irrigation, Storage, Transport, Recreation, Tourism, Fish farming.

| Dam benefits | Dam limitations |
|---|---|
| Year-round water supply | Land, homes and habitats flooded |
| Renewable electricity | People resettled |
| Irrigation raises yields | Sediment trapped; less silt downstream |
| Downstream flood protection | Fish migration blocked |
| Jobs in fishing and tourism | High cost; evaporation losses |

Conflict to mention: flood control needs spare space in the reservoir, whereas storage and power need it full.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Store | A place where water is held in the water cycle |
| Transfer | A process that moves water between stores |
| Aquifer | Permeable rock that holds ground water |
| Water table | The upper level of saturated ground |
| Potable water | Water that is safe to drink |
| Sedimentation | Settling of suspended particles in still water |
| Chlorination | Adding chlorine to kill microorganisms |
| Distillation | Boiling sea water and condensing the steam |
| Reverse osmosis | Forcing water through a membrane under high pressure |
| Brine | Very salty waste water from desalination |
| Multipurpose dam | A dam built to serve several uses at once |
| Reservoir | Artificial lake that stores water behind a dam |

## Exam traps

- Interception (on vegetation) is not infiltration (into soil).
- Through-flow is in the soil; ground water flow is in rock.
- Do not reverse the treatment order: chlorination is always last.
- Reverse osmosis uses pressure, not heat.
- "Compare" needs a comparative word ("higher than", "whereas").
- A water-poor country is not automatically a country with poor access.
- For a dam, a benefit for one group can be a limitation for another; name who gains and who loses.

## Self-test

1. Name the only salt water store in the water cycle.
2. Which transfer moves water sideways through the soil?
3. State what happens to the salt during distillation.
4. Define potable water.
5. What is removed at the screening stage?
6. Why is chlorination the final treatment stage?
7. Name the ocean that surrounds Antarctica.
8. Give two reasons why rural areas often have lower access to potable water than cities.
9. State what the membrane does in reverse osmosis.
10. Give two limitations of desalination.
11. Name four uses of a multipurpose dam.
12. Explain why a reservoir used for flood control cannot always be kept full.

## Answers

1. The oceans.
2. Through-flow.
3. It remains in the liquid that is left behind; only water evaporates as steam.
4. Water that is safe to drink.
5. Large floating objects such as branches and litter.
6. It kills microorganisms after the particles have been removed, and the remaining chlorine keeps the water safe in the pipes.
7. The Southern Ocean.
8. Scattered homes make pipes expensive per person; fewer treatment works; reliance on untreated wells or rivers (any two).
9. It lets water molecules pass through but stops most ions and other molecules.
10. High energy use and cost; brine harms marine life; carbon dioxide emissions if powered by fossil fuels; cost of pumping inland (any two).
11. Any four of: flood control, hydro-electric power, irrigation, storage of water, transport, recreation, tourism, fish farming.
12. To absorb a flood the reservoir needs spare capacity, so it must be kept partly empty before the wet season.
