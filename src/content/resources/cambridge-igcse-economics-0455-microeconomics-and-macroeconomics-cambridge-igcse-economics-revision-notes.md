These notes condense section 2.1 of the Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455 syllabus for examination in 2026: 2.1.1 microeconomics and 2.1.2 macroeconomics, the difference between them and the decision makers involved in each. All figures are invented. For full explanations read the [microeconomics and macroeconomics study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-microeconomics-and-macroeconomics-cambridge-igcse-economics/); then try the [microeconomics and macroeconomics practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-microeconomics-and-macroeconomics-cambridge-igcse-economics-practice/).

## Core recall

- **Microeconomics:** the study of individual decision makers (households, workers, firms) and individual markets.
- **Macroeconomics:** the study of the economy as a whole: total output, the general price level, total unemployment and the country's trade with the rest of the world.
- The difference is one of **scale**: one market or agent versus the whole economy.
- The same agents appear in both. A household choosing what to buy is micro; all households' total spending is macro.

## Decision makers

| Branch | Decision maker | Example decision |
|---|---|---|
| Micro | Consumer or household | Buy a bicycle instead of bus passes |
| Micro | Worker | Accept a job with longer hours but higher pay |
| Micro | Trade union | Negotiate a pay rise for bus drivers |
| Micro | Firm | Raise the price of its own product; hire 20 more staff |
| Micro | Government in one market | Tax plastic bags; subsidise solar panels |
| Macro | Government | Change income tax rates; raise total public spending |
| Macro | Central bank | Change the interest rate for the whole economy |
| Macro | All households, firms and foreign buyers together | Total consumer spending, total investment, total exports |

## Side-by-side comparison

| Feature | Micro | Macro |
|---|---|---|
| Prices | Price of one product | General price level and inflation |
| Jobs | Wages and jobs in one firm or occupation | Unemployment rate for the country |
| Output | One firm's or one industry's output | Total output (GDP) and growth |
| Policy | Tax or subsidy on one product | Fiscal, monetary and supply-side policy |
| Syllabus sections | Mainly 2 and 3 | Mainly 4, 5 and 6 |

**Macro aims of government (section 4.2):** economic growth, full employment or low unemployment, stable prices or low inflation, balance of payments stability, redistribution of income.

## Classification method

1. One market, product, firm or group of workers? Micro.
2. A total or average for the whole economy, or a policy aimed at the whole economy? Macro.
3. Check the decision maker and what it is deciding.
4. If both apply, say which part of the event is micro and which is macro.

## Links between the branches

- Macro totals are the sum of micro decisions.
- Macro policy changes micro incentives (a higher interest rate makes each loan dearer).
- A micro shock in a large industry can show up in macro data.

Example: an economy's output is 400 (farming), 600 (manufacturing) and 1,000 (services), all in millions.

```
Total output = 400 + 600 + 1,000 = 2,000 million
Manufacturing falls by 15%: 600 x 0.15 = 90 million lost
Effect on total output = 90 / 2,000 x 100 = 4.5% fall
```

The 15% fall in one industry is micro; the 4.5% fall in total output is macro.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Microeconomics | Study of individual decision makers and individual markets |
| Macroeconomics | Study of the economy as a whole |
| Economic agent | A decision maker: household, worker, firm or government |
| General price level | The average level of prices across the economy |
| Total output | The value of all goods and services produced in the economy (GDP) |
| Central bank | The institution that sets interest rates and manages the money supply |
| Aggregate | A total for the whole economy, built from individual decisions |

## Exam traps

- Treating every government action as macro. A tax on one good is micro.
- Treating a large firm as macro. Its decisions are still micro.
- Leaving out the central bank when listing macro decision makers.
- Giving a definition based on "small" and "big" without naming individual markets and the whole economy.
- Naming decision makers but not the decisions they make.
- Confusing "the price of rice rose" (micro) with "prices rose by 4% on average" (macro).

## Quick self-test

1. Define microeconomics.
2. Define macroeconomics.
3. Is a rise in the national unemployment rate micro or macro?
4. Is a firm's decision to cut the price of its laptops micro or macro?
5. Name two macroeconomic decision makers.
6. Is a government tax on sugary drinks micro or macro? Why?
7. Is a central bank's cut in interest rates micro or macro?
8. Give one microeconomic decision made by a worker.
9. State two macroeconomic aims of government.
10. Total output is 2,000 million and one industry's output rises by 50 million. Calculate the percentage rise in total output.
11. Give one way a macroeconomic change affects a household's micro decisions.
12. Why can a household appear in both micro and macro analysis?

### Answers

1. The study of individual decision makers (households, workers, firms) and individual markets.
2. The study of the economy as a whole.
3. Macro: it is a total for the whole economy.
4. Micro: one firm's price in one market.
5. Any two: government, central bank, households/firms/foreign buyers taken together.
6. Micro, because it acts on the price and quantity in one market.
7. Macro: it affects borrowing and spending across the whole economy.
8. For example which job to accept, or how many hours to work.
9. Any two: economic growth, full employment, stable prices, balance of payments stability, redistribution of income.
10. 50 / 2,000 x 100 = 2.5%.
11. For example a higher interest rate raises the cost of borrowing, so a household may delay buying a car.
12. Its own choices are micro; added to all other households' choices they form totals studied in macro.
