These questions cover section **3.3.2.1 Fractional distillation of crude oil** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Learn the ideas first in the [Fractional distillation of crude oil study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-fractional-distillation-of-crude-oil/) and the [Fractional distillation of crude oil revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-fractional-distillation-of-crude-oil-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values C = 12.0 and H = 1.0.

## Questions

**1.** Define the terms hydrocarbon and saturated. **[2]**

**2.** State the general formula of the alkanes and use it to give the molecular formula of the alkane with 12 carbon atoms. **[2]**

**3.** Crude oil is a mixture. State what this means for the properties of the hydrocarbons it contains and why the mixture can be separated. **[2]**

**4.** Describe how crude oil is separated into fractions in an oil refinery. **[5]**

**5.** Explain why the hydrocarbons with the highest boiling points are collected at the bottom of the fractionating column. **[3]**

**6.** The boiling points of pentane and decane are 36 °C and 174 °C. Explain this difference. **[3]**

**7.** Hexane boils at 69 °C and 2,2-dimethylbutane boils at 50 °C. Explain why. **[3]**

**8.** A fraction contains hydrocarbons with 10 to 16 carbon atoms. State what is meant by a fraction and state one use of this fraction (kerosene). **[2]**

**9.** Calculate the relative molecular mass of hexadecane, C₁₆H₃₄, and state whether the molecules are saturated. **[2]**

**10.** A student states that "when crude oil is fractionally distilled, the covalent bonds in the long-chain alkanes break". Identify the error. **[2]**

**11.** In a laboratory fractional distillation, a thermometer is placed at the top of the fractionating column. Explain what the thermometer reading shows and why the temperature rises during the distillation. **[3]**

**12.** Suggest why a refinery produces more of the long-chain fractions than the market demands. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A hydrocarbon is a compound containing hydrogen and carbon only [1]; saturated means it has only single bonds (no C=C double bonds) [1]. **[2]**

**2.** CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ [1]; C₁₂H₂₆ [1]. **[2]**

**3.** The components of a mixture are not chemically bonded to each other, so each keeps its own properties, including its boiling point [1]; the different boiling points allow them to be separated by a physical method, fractional distillation [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The crude oil is heated until most of it vaporises [1]; the hot vapour enters a fractionating column that is hot at the bottom and cooler at the top [1]; the vapours rise and cool [1]; each hydrocarbon condenses at the height where the temperature is below its boiling point [1]; the liquid fractions are collected from trays at different levels [1]. **[5]**

**5.** The long-chain hydrocarbons have the highest boiling points [1]; they condense first, near the bottom where it is hottest [1]; the short-chain hydrocarbons remain as vapour until they reach cooler parts higher up the column [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Both are non-polar molecules held by London forces [1]; decane has more electrons and a larger surface area, so its London forces are stronger [1]; more energy is needed to overcome them, so it has a higher boiling point [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Both have the same molecular formula and are held by London forces [1]; 2,2-dimethylbutane is branched and more compact, with less surface contact between molecules [1]; so its London forces are weaker and less energy is needed to separate the molecules [1]. **[3]**

**8.** A fraction is a mixture of hydrocarbons with similar boiling points (similar chain lengths) [1]; kerosene is used as jet fuel [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Mᵣ = 16 × 12.0 + 34 × 1.0 = 192.0 + 34.0 = **226.0** [1]; it is saturated because it has the formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ and only single bonds [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Fractional distillation is a physical process that depends on boiling points [1]; the covalent bonds do not break: only the London forces between the molecules are overcome (breaking bonds would be cracking) [1]. **[2]**

**11.** The thermometer shows the temperature of the vapour that reaches the top of the column, which is the boiling point of the fraction distilling [1]; the temperature rises because the more volatile (lower boiling point) components distil first and are followed by components with higher boiling points [1]; a rise in the reading therefore marks the change from one fraction to the next [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Crude oil contains more long-chain hydrocarbons than the market needs, because demand is greater for shorter chains such as petrol [1]; the surplus can be converted into shorter, more useful molecules by cracking [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Answers about boiling points need three links: size of molecule, strength of London forces, energy needed. Missing one link loses a mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that covalent bonds break during fractional distillation.
- Saying the column is hotter at the top.
- Forgetting that a fraction is a mixture.
- Not mentioning London forces (or calling them "bonds").
- Explaining branched isomers by "weaker bonds".
