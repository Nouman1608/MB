## Section A: short answers

**1.** Which **one** of the following is stored in the stock file rather than on the barcode? **[1]**

A: The product's identification number

B: The price of the product

C: The manufacturer's code

D: The check digit

**2.** State what EFTPOS stands for. **[1]**

**3.** Give **two** checks made to confirm that a payment card is valid. **[2]**

**4.** Name **two** devices that can be used for NFC payment. **[2]**

**5.** State what is meant by the *reorder level* of a product. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** A bookshop's stock file has this record for one book: number in stock 18, reorder level 15, reorder quantity 25, new order flag No.

**(a)** Four copies are sold. Describe what the computer does. **[3]**

**(b)** Two more copies are then sold before the delivery arrives. State the number in stock and explain why no new order is sent. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the number in stock after the delivery arrives. **[1]**

**7.** Describe how a POS terminal finds the price of an item when its barcode is scanned. **[3]**

**8.** A café accepts contactless cards and phone payments.

**(a)** Give **two** advantages of contactless payment for the café. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why a phone payment using NFC can be more secure than paying with a contactless card. **[2]**

**9.** A customer pays for shopping using chip and PIN. Describe the communication between the supermarket's computer and the bank's computer. **[4]**

**10.** A clothing shop opens a website so customers can buy online.

**(a)** Give **three** characteristics of the website that help customers to shop. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe **one** disadvantage of buying clothes online for the customer. **[2]**

## Section C: extended questions

**11.** Discuss the advantages and disadvantages to a supermarket of using POS terminals linked to an automatic stock control system. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate the effects of the growth of internet shopping on a town's high street and its residents. **[6]**

---

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. The barcode holds a product number; the price is looked up in the stock file so it can be changed centrally. **[1]**

**2.** Electronic funds transfer at point of sale [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The card has not passed its expiry date [1]; the card has not been reported lost or stolen [1] (also credit: card number is genuine; PIN matches the chip). **[2]**

**4.** Smartphone [1]; smartwatch [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The number in stock at which new stock is ordered [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** The number in stock is reduced from 18 to 14 [1]; the computer compares 14 with the reorder level of 15 and finds it is below [1]; because the flag is No, it orders 25 copies automatically and sets the flag to Yes [1].

**(b)** 12 [1]. The new order flag is Yes, which shows an order has already been placed, so no duplicate order is sent [1].

**(c)** 12 + 25 = 37 [1]. **[6]**

**7.** The scanner reads the barcode number [1]; the number is sent to the store's computer, which searches the stock file for the matching record [1]; the price and description are sent back to the POS terminal and displayed [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** Payments are very quick, so queues are shorter at busy times [1]; less cash is kept on the premises, reducing the risk of theft [1].

**(b)** The phone must be unlocked by its owner using a fingerprint, face scan or PIN before paying [1], whereas a lost contactless card can be used by anyone for small payments [1]. **[4]**

**9.** The supermarket's computer sends the card details and the amount to the bank's computer [1]; the bank's computer checks the card is valid and that there are enough funds in the account [1]; it sends back authorisation (or declines the payment) [1]; the amount is debited from the customer's account and credited to the supermarket's account [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** A search facility to find items by size or type [1]; pictures and descriptions with reviews [1]; a shopping basket and secure checkout [1] (also credit: customer account; delivery tracking; size guides).

**(b)** The customer cannot try clothes on before buying [1], so they may not fit and must be returned, which takes time and may cost money [1]. **[5]**

**11.** Advantages: stock levels are updated with every sale, so records are always accurate [1]; stock is reordered automatically, so shelves rarely run empty [1]; staff do not need to count stock manually, saving wages [1]; sales data shows which products sell best for planning [1]. Disadvantages: the system is expensive to buy and maintain [1]; if theft or damaged goods are not recorded, the stock file becomes inaccurate and orders may be wrong [1]. **[6]**

**12.** For residents: they can shop at any time and have goods delivered, which helps people who find it difficult to travel [1]; easy price comparison may save them money [1]. Against: shops on the high street lose customers and may close [1], so jobs are lost and the town centre becomes less attractive [1]; residents who rely on local shops, such as older people without internet access, have less choice [1]. Judgement: internet shopping benefits individual residents but harms the high street unless shops also sell online or offer services that cannot be bought online [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the price is stored in the barcode.
- Leaving out the new order flag in reordering questions.
- Saying the EFTPOS terminal checks funds instead of the bank's computer.
- Giving customer points when the question asks about the business or the town.
