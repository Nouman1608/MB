## What you must be able to do

- State the **conditions and products** of the reaction of methane with chlorine.
- Explain it as a **free-radical substitution** with **initiation, propagation and termination** steps.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Free-radical substitution | Replacement of an atom by another through a radical chain mechanism |
| Radical | Species with an unpaired electron (dot) |
| Homolytic fission | Bond breaks, one electron to each atom |
| Initiation | Step that produces radicals |
| Propagation | Step in which a radical reacts and another is formed |
| Termination | Step in which two radicals combine and are removed |

## Reaction summary

```
CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl          UV light
```

Alkanes are unreactive because C–C and C–H bonds are strong and non-polar. UV light provides the energy to break the Cl–Cl bond.

## The mechanism

| Stage | Equation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Initiation | Cl₂ → 2Cl• | UV light; homolytic fission |
| Propagation 1 | Cl• + CH₄ → •CH₃ + HCl | One radical in, one out |
| Propagation 2 | •CH₃ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl• | Chlorine radical regenerated |
| Termination | Cl• + Cl• → Cl₂ | No radicals left |
| Termination | •CH₃ + Cl• → CH₃Cl | No radicals left |
| Termination | •CH₃ + •CH₃ → C₂H₆ | Ethane by-product |

Chain reaction: Cl• is regenerated, so one initiation leads to many cycles.

Rules for writing:
1. Dots on radicals.
2. Initiation: one molecule to two radicals.
3. Propagation: one radical on each side.
4. Termination: no radical on the right.
5. Label every step.
6. No curly arrows needed.

## Further substitution

```
CH₃Cl + Cl₂ → CH₂Cl₂ + HCl
CH₂Cl₂ + Cl₂ → CHCl₃ + HCl
CHCl₃ + Cl₂ → CCl₄ + HCl
```

- Gives a mixture of products.
- Use an excess of methane to favour CH₃Cl.
- Overall for complete substitution: CH₄ + 4Cl₂ → CCl₄ + 4HCl.

## Other alkanes

| Alkane | Product | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Ethane | Chloroethane | Propagation: Cl• + C₂H₆ → •C₂H₅ + HCl; •C₂H₅ + Cl₂ → C₂H₅Cl + Cl• |
| Propane | 1-chloropropane and 2-chloropropane | Two types of hydrogen |
| Bromine instead of chlorine | Bromoalkanes | Same mechanism with UV light |

## Calculations

**Atom economy** of CH₃Cl: 50.5 ÷ (16.0 + 71.0) × 100 = 58.0%.

**Percentage yield.** 8.00 g CH₄ gives 17.5 g CH₃Cl.

```
n(CH₄) = 0.500 mol; theoretical mass = 0.500 × 50.5 = 25.25 g
yield = 17.5 ÷ 25.25 × 100 = 69.3 %
```

**Moles of chlorine.** For CH₄ → CCl₄, 4 mol Cl₂ per mol CH₄. 0.500 mol CH₄ needs 2.00 mol Cl₂ and gives 0.500 × 154.0 = 77.0 g CCl₄.

## Why UV light and not heat or a catalyst?

- The Cl–Cl bond is weaker than C–H and C–C, so a photon of ultraviolet light breaks it homolytically.
- The energy of the UV photon is not enough to break the C–H bond directly, so chlorine radicals form first and then attack methane.
- In the dark at room temperature, methane and chlorine do not react.

## Radical or ion? Quick check

| Feature | Radical step | Ionic step |
|---|---|---|
| Fission | Homolytic | Heterolytic |
| Species formed | Neutral radicals (dots) | Positive and negative ions |
| Arrows | Not required | Curly arrows from lone pairs or bonds |
| Typical conditions | UV light | Polar solvent, reagent such as OH⁻ |

## Summary of what each propagation step does

1. Cl• + CH₄ → •CH₃ + HCl: the chlorine radical takes a hydrogen atom and forms HCl.
2. •CH₃ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl•: the methyl radical takes a chlorine atom and releases a new chlorine radical.

Add them together and cancel the radicals to get the overall equation. If the radicals do not cancel, one of your equations is wrong.

## Worked example: write the mechanism for bromine and methane

```
Initiation:   Br₂ → 2Br•
Propagation:  Br• + CH₄ → •CH₃ + HBr
              •CH₃ + Br₂ → CH₃Br + Br•
Termination:  Br• + Br• → Br₂
```

The mechanism is the same, with Br in place of Cl.

## Checking a mechanism answer

- Are there three headed stages: initiation, propagation, termination?
- Does each equation balance?
- Does each propagation step have exactly one radical on each side?
- Is UV light stated?
- Have you said why more than one product forms?

## Exam traps

- No dot on a radical.
- Two radicals in a propagation step.
- A radical remaining in a termination step.
- Initiation written as ion formation.
- UV light missing.
- Curly arrows used.
- Substitution called addition.
- Only one product expected.

## Self-test

1. State the condition for the chlorination of methane.
2. Write the initiation step.
3. Write the first propagation step.
4. Write the second propagation step.
5. Why is it a chain reaction?
6. Write a termination step that forms ethane.
7. Why are dichloromethane and trichloromethane found in the products?
8. How can the yield of chloromethane be increased?
9. What type of bond fission occurs in initiation?
10. Why are alkanes generally unreactive?
11. Which two products form when propane reacts with chlorine to give a monochloro compound?
12. Calculate the atom economy to make chloromethane.

### Answers

1. Ultraviolet light.
2. Cl₂ → 2Cl•.
3. Cl• + CH₄ → •CH₃ + HCl.
4. •CH₃ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl•.
5. The chlorine radical is regenerated and starts a new cycle.
6. •CH₃ + •CH₃ → C₂H₆.
7. Chloromethane still has C–H bonds that chlorine radicals can attack.
8. Use an excess of methane.
9. Homolytic fission.
10. Their bonds are strong and non-polar.
11. 1-chloropropane and 2-chloropropane.
12. 50.5 ÷ 87.0 × 100 = 58.0%.
