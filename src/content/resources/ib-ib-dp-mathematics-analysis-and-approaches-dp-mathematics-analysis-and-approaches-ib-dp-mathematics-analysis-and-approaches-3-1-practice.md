This set covers **section 3.1** (SL and HL) of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches, first assessment 2021: distance and midpoint in 3D, volume and surface area of solids and their combinations, and angles between lines and planes. Questions are calculator-free unless marked "(calculator allowed)"; give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures and angles in degrees. The methods are in the [3D geometry study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-1/) and the [3D geometry revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-1-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** The points A(−1, 4, 2) and B(5, −2, 5) are given.

**(a)** Write down the midpoint of [AB]. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the length AB. **[2]**

**2.** The distance between P(3, k, −2) and Q(1, 5, 4) is 7. Find the possible values of k. **[3]**

**3.** M(0, 2, −3) is the midpoint of [AB], where A is (−4, 7, 1). Find the coordinates of B. **[2]**

**4.** A sphere has volume 288π cm³.

**(a)** Find its radius. **[2]**
**(b)** Find its exact surface area. **[1]**

**5.** A right cone has base radius 8 cm and curved surface area 80π cm².

**(a)** Find the slant height. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the vertical height. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the exact volume. **[2]**

**6.** The points A(1, 2, 3), B(3, 4, 4) and C(3, 1, 1) form a triangle.

**(a)** Show that triangle ABC is isosceles and right-angled. **[3]**
**(b)** Find its area. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A solid is made from a cylinder of radius 4 cm and height 9 cm, with a right cone of radius 4 cm and height 3 cm on top and a hemisphere of radius 4 cm underneath.

**(a)** Find the exact volume of the solid. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the slant height of the cone. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the exact surface area of the solid. **[3]**

**8.** A hemispherical bowl of internal radius 9 cm is full of water. All the water is poured into an empty cylindrical jar of internal radius 6 cm.

**(a)** Find the exact volume of water. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the depth of water in the jar. **[2]**

**9.** (calculator allowed) ABCDEFGH is a cuboid with base ABCD, where AB = 8 cm, BC = 5 cm and the vertical edge AE = 4 cm. E, F, G and H lie above A, B, C and D respectively.

**(a)** Find AC. **[1]**
**(b)** Find AG. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the angle between [AG] and the base ABCD. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the angle between [AG] and the face ABFE. **[3]**

**10.** (calculator allowed) A right pyramid VABCD has a rectangular base ABCD with AB = 12 cm and BC = 8 cm. M is the centre of the base and each slant edge has length 13 cm.

**(a)** Find MA. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the height VM. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the volume of the pyramid. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the angle between [VA] and the base. **[2]**

**11.** (calculator allowed) A right cone has base radius 5 cm and height 12 cm. P and Q are opposite ends of a diameter of the base and V is the apex.

**(a)** Find angle PVQ. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the angle between [VP] and the base. **[1]**

**12.** A sphere of radius r fits exactly inside a cylinder, touching its curved surface and both ends.

**(a)** Show that the volume of the sphere is 2/3 of the volume of the cylinder. **[3]**
**(b)** Show that the surface area of the sphere equals the curved surface area of the cylinder. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** (2, 1, 3.5) [1]
**(b)** AB = √(6² + 6² + 3²) [1] = √81 = 9 [1]

**2.** (3 − 1)² + (k − 5)² + (−2 − 4)² = 49 [1], so (k − 5)² = 9 [1], giving k = 2 or k = 8 [1]

**3.** B = (2(0) + 4, 2(2) − 7, 2(−3) − 1) [1] = (4, −3, −7) [1]

**4. (a)** (4/3)πr³ = 288π, so r³ = 216 [1] and r = 6 cm [1]
**(b)** 4π(36) = 144π cm² [1]

**5. (a)** π(8)l = 80π, so l = 10 cm [1]
**(b)** h = √(10² − 8²) = 6 cm [1]
**(c)** (1/3)π(64)(6) [1] = 128π cm³ [1]

**6. (a)** AB² = 2² + 2² + 1² = 9 [1]; AC² = 2² + 1² + 2² = 9, so AB = AC = 3 [1]; BC² = 0 + 9 + 9 = 18 = AB² + AC², so the right angle is at A [1]
**(b)** ½ × 3 × 3 = 4.5 square units [1]

**7. (a)** Cylinder 144π, cone (1/3)π(16)(3) = 16π, hemisphere (2/3)π(64) = 128π/3 [2]. Total 160π + 128π/3 = 608π/3 cm³ [1]
**(b)** l = √(4² + 3²) = 5 cm [1]
**(c)** Cone curved π(4)(5) = 20π; cylinder curved 2π(4)(9) = 72π [1]; hemisphere curved 2π(16) = 32π [1]. Total 124π cm² [1]
*Examiner insight:* Neither end of the cylinder is on the outside, so no flat circles are counted.

**8. (a)** (2/3)π(9³) = 486π cm³ [1]
**(b)** π(6²)h = 486π [1], so h = 13.5 cm [1]

**9. (a)** AC = √(64 + 25) = √89 = 9.43 cm [1]
**(b)** AG = √(89 + 16) [1] = √105 = 10.2 cm [1]
**(c)** tan θ = 4/√89 [1], θ = 23.0° [1]
**(d)** The projection of G onto face ABFE is F, since GF is perpendicular to that face [1]. AF = √(64 + 16) = √80 and GF = 5 [1]. tan φ = 5/√80, φ = 29.2° [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (d) the projection changes with the plane. Using AC again would answer (c) a second time.

**10. (a)** AC = √(144 + 64) = √208, so MA = √52 = 7.21 cm [1]
**(b)** VM² = 13² − 52 = 117 [1], VM = 10.8 cm [1]
**(c)** (1/3)(12 × 8)(√117) [1] = 346 cm³ [1]
**(d)** cos θ = √52/13 [1], θ = 56.3° [1]

**11. (a)** In triangle VMP, with M the centre of the base, tan(PVM) = 5/12 [1], so PVM = 22.6° [1]. PVQ = 2 × 22.62° = 45.2° [1]
**(b)** 90° − 22.62° = 67.4° [1]

**12. (a)** The cylinder has radius r and height 2r [1]. Its volume is πr²(2r) = 2πr³ [1]. (4/3)πr³ ÷ 2πr³ = 2/3 [1]
**(b)** Curved surface of cylinder = 2πr(2r) = 4πr² [1], which equals the sphere's surface area 4πr² [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Arithmetic with negative coordinates in the distance formula.
- Forgetting the second value when an unknown coordinate is squared.
- Counting hidden circular faces in a composite solid.
- Using the slant height as the height in a volume formula.
- Taking the wrong projection for an angle with a vertical face.
- Rounding intermediate lengths such as √52 or √117.
