## Questions

**1.** State the condition for simple harmonic motion, in words and as an equation, defining each symbol. **[2]**

**2.** The acceleration–displacement graph of an oscillator is a straight line through the origin and through the point x = 0.040 m, a = −1.44 m s⁻². The amplitude is 0.055 m.

**(a)** Show that the motion is simple harmonic and find ω. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the period. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the maximum speed. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the maximum acceleration. **[1]**

**3.** An oscillator has amplitude 0.085 m and angular frequency 14 rad s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate its speed when its displacement is 0.050 m. **[2]**
**(b)** State the magnitude and direction of its acceleration at this displacement, which is on the positive side of equilibrium. **[1]**

**4.** A tip of a vibrating ruler moves with amplitude 3.5 cm at frequency 2.5 Hz. Timing starts when it is at maximum positive displacement.

**(a)** Write down x as a function of t. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate x at t = 0.060 s. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the first time at which x = −1.75 cm. **[2]**

**5.** A displacement–time graph for an oscillating block is a cosine curve with amplitude 6.0 cm and period 0.80 s.

**(a)** Calculate ω. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the maximum speed and state the first time it occurs. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe the acceleration–time graph, giving its maximum value and where it starts. **[2]**

**6.** A mass m is attached to a spring of constant k and oscillates horizontally without friction.

**(a)** Show that the period is T = 2π√(m/k). **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate T for m = 0.45 kg and k = 28 N m⁻¹. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate T if the mass is changed to 1.80 kg. **[1]**

**7.** A simple pendulum has a light string of length l and a bob of mass m.

**(a)** Derive T = 2π√(l/g) for small oscillations. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate l for T = 1.50 s. **[2]**
**(c)** The same pendulum is used where g = 1.62 m s⁻². Calculate its period. **[1]**

**8.** A 0.30 kg oscillator has ω = 8.0 rad s⁻¹ and amplitude 0.045 m.

**(a)** Calculate its total energy. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate its kinetic and potential energy at x = 0.027 m. **[3]**

**9.** An oscillator of amplitude 0.072 m has period 1.2 s.

**(a)** Sketch in words how Ek and Ep vary with displacement, stating where the curves meet. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the displacement at which Ek = Ep. **[1]**
**(c)** Starting at x = A, calculate the time taken to first reach that displacement. **[1]**
**(d)** State the period of the Ek–time graph. **[1]**

**10.** A 0.60 kg mass hangs at rest from a vertical spring and stretches it by 0.12 m. It is then pulled down a further 0.040 m and released.

**(a)** Calculate the spring constant. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the period of the oscillation. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the maximum speed. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the total energy of the oscillation. **[2]**
**(e)** Calculate the displacement from equilibrium at which Ek = Ep. **[2]**

**11.** A pendulum bob of mass 0.15 kg hangs on a string 0.80 m long. It is released from rest with the bob 0.060 m (measured along the arc) from its lowest point.

**(a)** Calculate the angle of the string in radians and degrees, and comment on whether the small-angle model is reasonable. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the period. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the maximum speed of the bob. **[2]**
**(d)** Check this speed using energy conservation, by finding the height gained by the bob. **[3]**
**(e)** Calculate the maximum acceleration along the arc. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The acceleration is directly proportional to the displacement from equilibrium and is directed towards equilibrium [1]. a = −ω²x, where a is acceleration, x is displacement from equilibrium and ω is the (constant) angular frequency [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A statement of only "a is proportional to x" misses the direction, so include "towards equilibrium" or the minus sign.

**2. (a)** The graph is a straight line through the origin with negative gradient, so a is proportional to −x [1]. Gradient = −1.44/0.040 = −36 s⁻², so ω² = 36 and ω = **6.0 rad s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** T = 2π/6.0 = **1.05 s** [1].
**(c)** vmax = ωA = 6.0 × 0.055 = **0.33 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(d)** amax = ω²A = 36 × 0.055 = **1.98 m s⁻²** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Explaining why a straight line through the origin means SHM, rather than just quoting ω, is what a "show that" part rewards.

**3. (a)** v = ω√(A² − x²) = 14 × √(0.085² − 0.050²) [1] = 14 × 0.0687 = **0.96 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** a = −ω²x = −196 × 0.050 = **9.8 m s⁻² towards equilibrium (negative direction)** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the squares inside the root. Subtracting before squaring is a frequent slip.

**4. (a)** ω = 2π × 2.5 = 15.7 rad s⁻¹, so x = **3.5 cos(15.7t) cm** [1].
**(b)** ωt = 15.708 × 0.060 = 0.942 rad [1]; x = 3.5 × cos(0.942) = **2.1 cm** [1].
**(c)** cos ωt = −0.5, so ωt = 2π/3 [1]; t = (2π/3)/15.708 = **0.133 s** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Using degrees in cos ωt gives a wrong x, so check the angle unit and keep answers in cm or convert to metres consistently.

**5. (a)** ω = 2π/0.80 = **7.85 rad s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** vmax = ωA = 7.85 × 0.060 = **0.47 m s⁻¹** [1]; first at equilibrium, t = T/4 = **0.20 s** [1].
**(c)** A negative cosine curve with the same period [1], starting at −ω²A = −61.7 × 0.060 = **−3.7 m s⁻²** and reaching +3.7 m s⁻² at 0.40 s [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Descriptions of graphs gain credit for the starting value, the sign and the period, not just "it is a cosine curve".

**6. (a)** The spring force gives ma = −kx [1], so a = −(k/m)x [1], which is a = −ω²x with ω² = k/m, so T = 2π/ω = 2π√(m/k) [1].
**(b)** T = 2π√(0.45/28) = **0.80 s** [1].
**(c)** The mass is four times larger, so T doubles: **1.59 s** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In a derivation, state the comparison with a = −ω²x explicitly, as that links the algebra to the definition.

**7. (a)** Restoring force along the arc F = −mg sin θ [1]. For small θ, sin θ ≈ θ = x/l, so ma = −mg x/l and a = −(g/l)x [1]. So ω² = g/l and T = 2π/ω = 2π√(l/g) [1].
**(b)** l = gT²/(4π²) = 9.81 × 2.25/39.48 [1] = **0.559 m** [1].
**(c)** T = 2π√(0.559/1.62) = **3.69 s** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention that the small-angle approximation is used and that the mass cancels, since both points show you understand the result.

**8. (a)** E = ½mω²A² = ½ × 0.30 × 64 × 0.045² [1] = **0.0194 J** [1].
**(b)** Ek = ½mω²(A² − x²) = ½ × 0.30 × 64 × (0.002025 − 0.000729) [1] = **0.0124 J** [1]. Ep = E − Ek = **0.0070 J** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use a quick check that Ek + Ep equals the total. It catches slips and shows the energy link.

**9. (a)** Ep against x is an upright parabola, zero at x = 0 [1]. Ek is an inverted parabola, maximum at x = 0 and zero at ±A, and the two curves cross at x = ±A/√2 [1].
**(b)** x = 0.072/√2 = **0.051 m** [1].
**(c)** cos ωt = 1/√2, so ωt = π/4, which is T/8 = **0.15 s** [1].
**(d)** **0.60 s** (T/2) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A common slip is to say the energy graphs have the same period as the motion. Count how many times energy peaks per cycle.

**10. (a)** At rest, kx = mg [1], so k = 0.60 × 9.81/0.12 = **49 N m⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** ω = √(k/m) = √(49.05/0.60) = 9.04 rad s⁻¹ [1]; T = 2π/9.04 = **0.69 s** [1].
**(c)** The amplitude is 0.040 m, so vmax = ωA = 9.04 × 0.040 = **0.36 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(d)** E = ½kA² = ½ × 49.05 × 0.040² [1] = **0.039 J** [1].
**(e)** Ek = Ep = E/2 at x = A/√2 [1] = 0.040/√2 = **0.028 m** from equilibrium [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* The amplitude is 0.040 m, not 0.16 m. Using the total extension as the amplitude is a typical way to lose most of the later marks.

**11. (a)** θ = x/l = 0.060/0.80 = **0.075 rad** [1] = 4.3°, which is small, so sin θ ≈ θ is reasonable [1].
**(b)** ω = √(g/l) = √(9.81/0.80) = 3.50 rad s⁻¹ [1]; T = 2π/3.50 = **1.79 s** [1].
**(c)** vmax = ωA = 3.50 × 0.060 [1] = **0.21 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(d)** h = l(1 − cos θ) = 0.80 × (1 − cos 0.075) = 0.00225 m [1]. Gain in gravitational potential energy mgh = 0.15 × 9.81 × 0.00225 = 3.31 × 10⁻³ J [1]. Kinetic energy ½mv² = ½ × 0.15 × 0.2101² = 3.31 × 10⁻³ J, so the two agree [1].
**(e)** amax = ω²A = 12.26 × 0.060 = **0.74 m s⁻²** [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the unrounded intermediate values for h, because the difference between 1 and cos θ is tiny and early rounding makes the energy check fail.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the minus sign or the words "towards equilibrium" when stating the SHM condition.
- Using degrees when evaluating cos ωt.
- Taking the amplitude as the total extension of a hanging spring instead of the extra displacement.
- Using x instead of A in total energy.
- Giving maximum speed at an extreme or maximum acceleration at equilibrium.
- Forgetting that the pendulum result holds only for small angles in radians.
- Drawing Ek and Ep against time with period T rather than T/2.
- Reading a gradient off a chord instead of a tangent.
