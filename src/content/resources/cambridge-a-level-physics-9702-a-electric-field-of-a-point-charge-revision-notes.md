Subtopic 18.4 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) asks you to recall and use E = Q/(4πε₀r²) for the field of a point charge in free space. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Electric field of a point charge study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-electric-field-of-a-point-charge/); to apply the ideas, try the [Electric field of a point charge practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-electric-field-of-a-point-charge-practice/).

## The equation

```
E = Q / (4πε₀r²)       1/(4πε₀) = 8.99 × 10⁹ m F⁻¹
unit: N C⁻¹ (≡ V m⁻¹)
```

- **Recall** it (not on the data sheet); the constant is given.
- Derivation: Coulomb force on test charge q, divided by q.
- Direction: **away** from +Q, **towards** −Q.
- Rearranged: Q = 4πε₀r²E = Er²/(8.99 × 10⁹); r = √(Q/(4πε₀E)).

## Inverse-square scaling

| r | E |
|---|---|
| r | E |
| 2r | E/4 |
| 3r | E/9 |
| 10r | E/100 |
| r/2 | 4E |

**Test for inverse-square data:** E × r² is constant.

**E–r graph:** falls steeply then flattens; never reaches zero; for a sphere, starts at the surface value at r = R.

## Charged spheres

- Outside: treat as a point charge at the centre; r from the **centre**.
- Surface field: E = Q/(4πε₀R²).
- Maximum charge set by the breakdown field of the surrounding air: Q_max = 4πε₀R²E_max, so a bigger sphere holds more charge.
- Field at r > R with the same charge: E_surface × (R/r)².

## Two charges on a line

| Pair | Between the charges | Zero-field point |
|---|---|---|
| Like (+ +, or − −) | Fields oppose | Between them, closer to the **smaller** charge |
| Unlike (+ −) | Fields add (both point towards −) | Outside, beyond the **smaller** charge |

**Method:** Q₁/x² = Q₂/(d − x)² → √Q₁/x = √Q₂/(d − x).

**Examples:**

- +4 nC and +1 nC, 0.12 m apart → zero 0.080 m from the +4 nC charge.
- +9 nC and +4 nC, 0.25 m apart → zero 0.15 m from the +9 nC charge.
- +8 nC at 0 and −2 nC at 0.30 m → zero at x = 0.60 m (beyond the −2 nC charge).

## Off-axis fields

1. Magnitude of each field from E = Q/(4πε₀r²).
2. Direction: away from +, towards −.
3. Resolve into x and y components.
4. Add components; combine with Pythagoras; angle with tan⁻¹.

## Midpoint of equal and opposite charges

+3.0 nC and −3.0 nC, 0.10 m apart. At the midpoint each is 0.050 m away:

```
each field = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 3.0 × 10⁻⁹ / 0.050² = 1.08 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹
both point towards the negative charge, so they add:
E = 2.2 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹ towards the −3.0 nC charge
```

Compare two equal like charges: at the midpoint the fields cancel and E = 0.

## Electric versus gravitational

| | Point charge | Point mass |
|---|---|---|
| Field | E = Q/(4πε₀r²) | g = GM/r² |
| Direction | Out (+) or in (−) | In |
| Definition | Force per unit positive charge | Force per unit mass |
| Distance law | 1/r² | 1/r² |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Radial field | Field whose lines point directly away from or towards a central point |
| Point charge | Charge of negligible size compared with the distances considered |
| Inverse-square law | Quantity proportional to 1/r² |
| Zero-field (neutral) point | Point where the vector sum of fields is zero |
| Breakdown field | Field strength at which air becomes conducting and sparks occur |

## Exam traps

- r used instead of r² (or V formula used for E).
- r measured from the sphere's surface.
- Field magnitudes added without directions.
- Zero point placed between unlike charges.
- Zero point placed nearer the larger charge.
- Negative charge treated as giving a "smaller" field.

## Self-test

1. Derive E = Q/(4πε₀r²) from Coulomb's law.
2. Find E 0.050 m from a −3.0 nC charge, with direction.
3. E at 0.10 m is 800 N C⁻¹. Find E at 0.40 m.
4. At what distance from +5.0 μC is E = 2.0 × 10⁵ N C⁻¹?
5. A sphere of radius 0.10 m carries 2.0 × 10⁻⁸ C. Find E at its surface.
6. Find E 0.30 m from the centre of the sphere in Question 5.
7. Where is the zero-field point for +16 nC and +1 nC 0.50 m apart?
8. Why is there no zero-field point between +Q and −Q?
9. A data table gives E × r² = constant. What does this show?
10. Find the field 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ m from a proton.
11. Two fields of 3.0 × 10³ N C⁻¹ and 4.0 × 10³ N C⁻¹ act at right angles. Find the resultant.
12. Why does a larger sphere hold more charge before sparking?
