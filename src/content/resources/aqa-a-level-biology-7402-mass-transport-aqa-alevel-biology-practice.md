These questions cover AQA A-level Biology (7402) section 3.3.4, *Mass transport*, in animals and plants. All data are invented. Teaching is in the [mass transport study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-mass-transport-aqa-alevel-biology/) and the [mass transport revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-mass-transport-aqa-alevel-biology-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** **(a)** Name the blood vessel that carries blood from the heart to the lungs. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the blood vessel that carries blood to a kidney. **[1]**

**(c)** Name the vessels that supply the heart muscle with oxygenated blood. **[1]**

**2.** Explain why the oxyhaemoglobin dissociation curve is S-shaped. **[2]**

**3.** Blood leaving the lungs is 98% saturated with oxygen. In resting muscle it is 70% saturated, and in exercising muscle 30% saturated. Fully saturated blood carries 20 cm³ of oxygen per 100 cm³ of blood.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of oxygen released to resting muscle by 100 cm³ of blood. **[2]**

**(b)** How many times more oxygen is released per 100 cm³ of blood in exercising muscle than in resting muscle? Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[1]**

**4.** An animal lives in burrows in which the partial pressure of oxygen is low. Its oxyhaemoglobin dissociation curve lies to the left of that of a similar-sized animal living above ground. Explain the advantage of this. **[2]**

**5.** One cardiac cycle takes 0.6 s. Calculate the heart rate. **[1]**

**6.** Give two differences between the structure of an artery and a vein, and explain how each relates to function. **[2]**

## Section B: longer questions

**7.** Explain how the Bohr effect helps to supply respiring muscle with oxygen during exercise. **[3]**

**8.** The table shows pressures in the left side of the heart during one cardiac cycle.

| Time / s | Left atrium / kPa | Left ventricle / kPa | Aorta / kPa |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 0.8 | 0.5 | 10.0 |
| 0.1 | 1.2 | 1.0 | 9.6 |
| 0.2 | 0.4 | 8.0 | 9.2 |
| 0.3 | 0.5 | 14.0 | 13.0 |
| 0.4 | 0.7 | 15.5 | 15.0 |
| 0.5 | 0.9 | 9.0 | 11.5 |
| 0.6 | 1.0 | 0.6 | 10.8 |

**(a)** Between which two times does the semilunar valve in the aorta open? **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the atrioventricular valve is closed at 0.2 s. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the semilunar valve is closed at 0.5 s. **[1]**

**9.** Describe how tissue fluid is formed and how it is returned to the circulatory system. **[4]**

**10.** A diet very low in protein can reduce the concentration of protein in the blood plasma. Suggest how this could cause tissue fluid to build up. **[3]**

**11.** Explain how water moves up the xylem from the roots to the leaves, according to the cohesion-tension theory. **[4]**

**12.** A ring of bark, including the phloem, was removed from the trunk of a tree. After some weeks the bark just above the ring had swollen. Fluid from the swelling contained a high concentration of sucrose.

**(a)** Explain these results. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the leaves above the ring did not wilt. **[1]**

**13.** Evaluate the mass flow hypothesis as an explanation of translocation in the phloem. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Pulmonary artery [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Renal artery [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** Coronary arteries [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Binding of the first oxygen molecule changes the shape (tertiary or quaternary structure) of haemoglobin [1], making it easier for further oxygen molecules to bind (cooperative binding) [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** 98 − 70 = 28% of capacity [1]; 0.28 × 20 = **5.6 cm³** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Exercising: (98 − 30) = 68% × 20 = 13.6 cm³; 13.6 ÷ 5.6 = **2.4 times** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** The haemoglobin has a higher affinity for oxygen [1], so it can load (become highly saturated with) oxygen at the low partial pressure in the burrow [1]. **[2]**

**5.** 60 ÷ 0.6 = **100 beats per minute** [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Any two, each with function [1 each]: artery has a thicker muscle and elastic layer, to withstand high pressure and recoil; artery has a narrower lumen, maintaining high pressure; veins have valves, preventing backflow at low pressure; veins have a wider lumen, giving less resistance to flow. **[2]**

**7.** Respiring muscle produces more carbon dioxide during exercise, lowering the blood pH [1]. This changes the shape of haemoglobin and lowers its affinity for oxygen (curve shifts right) [1]. So more oxygen is unloaded to the muscle at the same partial pressure [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** Between 0.2 s and 0.3 s [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Pressure in the ventricle (8.0 kPa) is higher than in the atrium (0.4 kPa) [1], so blood is pushed against the valve and closes it, preventing backflow into the atrium [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Pressure in the aorta (11.5 kPa) is higher than in the ventricle (9.0 kPa), so the valve is pushed shut [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* Quote the pressures from the table; valves respond only to pressure differences.

**9.** At the arteriole end of the capillary, hydrostatic pressure is high [1] and forces water and small solutes out through the capillary wall; proteins stay in the blood [1]. At the venule end, hydrostatic pressure is lower and the blood has a lower water potential because of the plasma proteins, so water returns by osmosis [1]. Excess tissue fluid drains into the lymphatic system and returns to the blood [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Less plasma protein raises the water potential of the blood [1]. The water potential gradient from tissue fluid to blood at the venule end is smaller [1], so less water returns to the capillaries by osmosis and tissue fluid accumulates [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Water evaporates from leaf mesophyll cells and diffuses out through stomata (transpiration) [1]. This lowers the water potential of leaf cells, so water moves into them from the xylem [1]. Water molecules stick together by hydrogen bonds (cohesion) [1], so a continuous column of water is pulled up the xylem under tension [1]. **[4]**

**12. (a)** Sucrose is transported down from the leaves in the phloem [1]. Removing the phloem stops this, so sucrose builds up above the ring, lowering water potential; water enters by osmosis and the tissue swells [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** The xylem was not removed, so water was still transported to the leaves [1]. **[1]**

**13.** For: sap is released under pressure when phloem is cut, showing a pressure difference [1]; sucrose concentration is higher at sources than at sinks, and metabolic inhibitors stop translocation, consistent with active loading [1]. Against: solutes move at different speeds, and sucrose moves in different directions to different sinks at the same time, which simple mass flow does not explain [1]. Sieve plates would resist flow; so the hypothesis explains much of the evidence but not all of it [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" needs points on both sides and a conclusion.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Shifting the curve the wrong way for the Bohr effect.
- Saying muscles open and close valves.
- Not quoting pressures when the data are given.
- Forgetting plasma proteins in tissue fluid answers.
- Describing xylem transport without cohesion and tension.
