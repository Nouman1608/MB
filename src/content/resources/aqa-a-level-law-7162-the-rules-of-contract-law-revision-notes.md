Condensed notes for the section 3.4 row **The rules of contract law**: "rules and principles of contract law concerning formation, terms, vitiating factors, discharge of a contract and associated remedies". These notes cover the framework; the detailed rules are in each area's own notes. For explanation and a worked example, use the [rules of contract law study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-law-7162-the-rules-of-contract-law/).

## The five areas in order

```
FORMATION -> TERMS -> VITIATING FACTORS -> DISCHARGE -> REMEDIES
offer        express     misrepresentation   performance   damages
acceptance   implied     economic duress     breach        specific performance
consideration conditions                     frustration   rescission
intention    exclusion                                      termination
```

## Sources at a glance

**Mainly common law:** offer and acceptance; consideration; intention to create legal relations; express and implied-in-fact terms; classification of terms; incorporation and construction of exclusion clauses; economic duress; frustration; damages.

**Equity:** specific performance; rescission; promissory estoppel.

**Statute:**
- Consumer Rights Act 2015: implied terms (ss9–11, 49, 52), remedies (ss20, 23, 24, 55, 56), exclusion controls (ss31, 57, 65).
- Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977: exclusion clauses in business contracts (ss2, 3).
- Misrepresentation Act 1967: s2(1) damages for negligent misrepresentation; s2(2) damages in lieu of rescission.
- Contracts (Rights of Third Parties) Act 1999: third-party enforcement.
- Law Reform (Frustrated Contracts) Act 1943: money after frustration.

**Statute overrides common law:** for example s31 CRA 2015 prevents exclusion of the consumer implied terms.

## Burden and standard

```
Claimant  ---- contract, terms, breach ---- balance of probabilities
Defendant ---- vitiating factor / frustration / exclusion ---- balance of probabilities
```

## The objective approach

- Agreement judged by what a reasonable person would understand from words and conduct.
- *Smith v Hughes* (1871): buyer's private mistake about old and new oats did not prevent a contract.
- Explains rules on offers, communication of acceptance and presumptions about intention.

## Consumer or business?

| Term | Definition (s2 CRA 2015) |
|---|---|
| Trader | Person acting for purposes relating to their trade, business, craft or profession |
| Consumer | Individual acting for purposes wholly or mainly outside their trade, business, craft or profession |

| Contract type | Governing rules |
|---|---|
| Trader to consumer | Consumer Rights Act 2015 |
| Business to business | UCTA 1977 and common law |
| Private individuals | Common law |

## Status of contracts

| Status | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Valid | Binding and enforceable | Ordinary contract |
| Void | No contract in law | No intention to create legal relations |
| Voidable | Valid until set aside by innocent party | Misrepresentation; economic duress |
| Unenforceable | Valid but not enforceable in court | Guarantee not evidenced in writing (Statute of Frauds 1677, s4) |

**Rescission** (for voidable contracts) can be lost through affirmation, delay or third-party rights.

## Bilateral and unilateral

| Bilateral | Unilateral |
|---|---|
| Promise for a promise | Promise for an act |
| Most commercial and consumer sales | Rewards; *Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co* (1893) |
| Accepted by communicating agreement | Accepted by performing the act |

## Problem method

1. Formation (only if in doubt).
2. Terms: express, implied, statutory; classify.
3. Breach or vitiating factor.
4. Exclusion clause.
5. Discharge.
6. Remedies.
7. Conclude for each party.

## Choosing the right authority

| Issue in the facts | Go to | Typical authority |
|---|---|---|
| Was there an offer or only an invitation? | Formation | *Pharmaceutical Society v Boots* (1953) |
| Was a statement a term or a representation? | Terms | *Bannerman v White* (1861) |
| Faulty goods bought from a trader | Consumer statutory terms | s9 CRA 2015 |
| A clause limiting liability | Exclusion clauses | *Olley v Marlborough Court* (1949); UCTA 1977 |
| A false statement before the contract | Misrepresentation | Misrepresentation Act 1967 |
| An event making performance impossible | Frustration | *Taylor v Caldwell* (1863) |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Common law | Law made by judges through precedent |
| Equity | Body of discretionary rules and remedies developed to supplement the common law |
| Objective approach | Judging agreement by the view of a reasonable person |
| Consumer | Individual acting wholly or mainly outside their trade |
| Trader | Person acting for business purposes |
| Void | Of no legal effect |
| Voidable | Valid until rescinded by the innocent party |
| Rescission | Setting a contract aside and restoring the parties to their original positions |
| Unilateral contract | A promise in return for an act |

## Exam traps

- Classify the contract (consumer or business) before choosing a statute.
- Misrepresentation and duress make a contract **voidable**, not void.
- Do not start with remedies: show the contract and the breach first.
- Use authorities: contract is case-based.
- Spend time on the stage the facts actually dispute.

## Self-test

1. List the five areas named in the specification row. *Formation, terms, vitiating factors, discharge and remedies.*
2. Name two areas of contract that are mainly common law. *Any two: offer and acceptance, consideration, intention, frustration, damages, economic duress.*
3. Which Act governs exclusion clauses in business-to-business contracts? *The Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977.*
4. What does the 1999 Act allow? *A third party to enforce a term in certain circumstances.*
5. What standard of proof applies in contract claims? *Balance of probabilities.*
6. What is the objective approach? *Agreement is judged by what a reasonable person would understand.*
7. What did *Smith v Hughes* decide? *A party's private mistake, not caused by the other, does not prevent a binding contract.*
8. Define "consumer" under s2 CRA 2015. *An individual acting for purposes wholly or mainly outside their trade, business, craft or profession.*
9. What is the difference between void and voidable? *Void: no contract; voidable: valid until set aside.*
10. Give an example of a voidable contract. *One induced by misrepresentation or economic duress.*
11. How is a unilateral contract accepted? *By performing the requested act.*
12. Which section prevents exclusion of the CRA implied terms? *s31 Consumer Rights Act 2015.*
