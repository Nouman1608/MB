This guide covers sub-topic **9.2 Heart** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. It is the largest part of topic 9, Transport in animals, with eleven learning outcomes. Outcomes 1 to 6 are Core. Outcomes 7 to 11 are Supplement content, marked **Extended only**. The heart-rate investigation (outcome 4) is the kind of practical that can appear on Paper 5 or Paper 6.

The [transport in animals study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-transport-in-animals/) summarises the heart; this page adds diagram reading, valve positions at each stage, the activity investigation and a full "discuss" answer. ## Reading a heart diagram (9.2.1 and 9.2.7)

Most marks lost on heart diagrams come from three habits. Fix these and identification becomes reliable.

1. **Left and right are swapped on the page.** The diagram shows the heart as if you were facing the person. The left ventricle is on the **right-hand side** of the page.
2. **Find the thickest wall first.** The ventricle with the thickest muscular wall is the left ventricle. Once you have found it, every other chamber follows.
3. **Atria are at the top, ventricles below.** The atria receive blood from veins; the ventricles pump it out into arteries.

The **septum** is the wall of muscle running down the middle between the left and right sides. The **coronary arteries** are drawn on the outside surface, branching over the ventricles. They supply the heart muscle itself.

**Extended only.** There are two kinds of one-way valve:

| Valve | Position | Stops blood flowing back from |
|---|---|---|
| Atrioventricular valves | between each atrium and its ventricle | the ventricles into the atria |
| Semilunar valves | at the base of the aorta and of the pulmonary artery | the arteries into the ventricles |

You do not need the separate names of the left and right atrioventricular valves.

## Arteries, veins and the heart (9.2.2)

The rule is about **direction**, not oxygen: arteries carry blood **away** from the heart; veins **return** blood to the heart. Apply it to the four vessels attached to the heart:

| Vessel | Joined to | Direction | Blood |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vena cava | right atrium | into heart, so a vein | deoxygenated |
| Pulmonary artery | right ventricle | out of heart, so an artery | deoxygenated |
| Pulmonary vein | left atrium | into heart, so a vein | oxygenated |
| Aorta | left ventricle | out of heart, so an artery | oxygenated |

## Monitoring the heart (9.2.3)

| Method | What it detects |
|---|---|
| ECG (electrocardiogram) | electrical activity of the heart, shown as a trace with repeating peaks |
| Pulse rate | the surge of blood in an artery near the skin, counted at the wrist or neck |
| Listening with a stethoscope | the sounds made by valves **closing** |

The two heart sounds are valves shutting. The first sound comes as the atrioventricular valves close when the ventricles start to contract. The second comes as the semilunar valves close when the ventricles relax.

## How the heart works (9.2.10, Extended only)

Learn this as a table of what the muscles and valves are doing at each stage. Exam answers that state the **valve position** and the **reason** score most highly.

| Stage | Atria | Ventricles | Atrioventricular valves | Semilunar valves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Filling | relaxed; blood flows in from vena cava and pulmonary vein | relaxed | open | closed |
| 2. Atria contract | contract | relaxed; fill with blood | open | closed |
| 3. Ventricles contract | relaxed | contract; pressure rises | **close**, preventing backflow into atria | **open**; blood leaves in aorta and pulmonary artery |
| 4. Ventricles relax | relaxed | relax; pressure falls | open again as atria refill | **close**, preventing backflow into ventricles |

Both atria contract together, then both ventricles. Valves open and close because of **pressure differences**; they are not muscles.

## Wall thickness and the septum (9.2.8 and 9.2.9, Extended only)

- **Left ventricle thicker than right:** the left ventricle pumps blood round the whole body, so it must create a higher pressure. The right ventricle pumps only to the lungs, which are close by, and lower pressure protects the lung capillaries.
- **Atria thinner than ventricles:** atria pump blood only a short distance, into the ventricles. Ventricles pump blood out of the heart and through the arteries.
- **Septum:** keeps oxygenated blood (left) separate from deoxygenated blood (right). Blood sent to the body therefore carries the maximum amount of oxygen, so respiring cells receive enough.

Both ventricles pump the **same volume** of blood each beat. The difference is pressure, not volume.

## Physical activity and heart rate (9.2.4 and 9.2.11)

### Planning the investigation

| Variable | In this investigation |
|---|---|
| Independent | intensity or duration of activity, for example step-ups at different rates for two minutes |
| Dependent | heart rate in beats per minute, from the pulse |
| Controlled | same person, same type of activity, same duration, same time to start counting, rested before each trial |

Count the pulse for 15 or 30 seconds and multiply to get beats per minute. Repeat each count and calculate a mean. Rest until the pulse returns to the resting value before the next trial.

### Worked example 1: recovery data

A student measured his heart rate before and after three minutes of skipping. (Example data written for this guide.)

| Reading | Heart rate / beats per minute |
|---|---|
| At rest, before skipping | 70 |
| End of skipping | 150 |
| 1 minute after | 128 |
| 2 minutes after | 104 |
| 3 minutes after | 86 |
| 4 minutes after | 76 |
| 5 minutes after | 70 |

- **Describe:** heart rate rose from 70 to 150 beats per minute during skipping. After skipping it fell, quickly at first and then more slowly, and returned to the resting rate of 70 after 5 minutes.
- **Calculate the change in the first minute:** 150 − 128 = 22 beats per minute.
- **Percentage increase caused by skipping:** (150 − 70) ÷ 70 × 100 = 114.28...%, which is **114%** to three significant figures.
- **Recovery time:** 5 minutes.

### Explaining the effect (Extended only)

During activity muscles contract more, so their cells **respire faster**. They need oxygen and glucose delivered faster and carbon dioxide removed faster. The heart beats faster so blood flows to the muscles at a greater rate. Heart rate stays high for a while after exercise; this links to removing the oxygen debt, which you meet in [anaerobic respiration](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-anaerobic-respiration/).

## Coronary heart disease (9.2.5 and 9.2.6)

Coronary heart disease (CHD) is the **blockage of the coronary arteries**. Less blood reaches part of the heart muscle, so that muscle receives less oxygen and glucose, cannot respire aerobically, and may die.

The syllabus lists the risk factors: **diet, lack of exercise, stress, smoking, genetic predisposition, age and sex**. Sort them in your head: diet, exercise, stress and smoking can be changed; genetic predisposition, age and sex cannot.

### Worked example 2: annotated "discuss" answer

**Question:** Discuss the roles of diet and exercise in reducing the risk of coronary heart disease. [4]

**Model answer:** A diet low in saturated fat and cholesterol reduces the build-up of fatty deposits in the coronary arteries (1). Eating less salt helps to keep blood pressure lower (1). Regular exercise helps to control body mass and lower blood pressure, and strengthens the heart muscle (1). However, diet and exercise cannot remove risk factors such as genetic predisposition, age or sex, so they reduce the risk but do not prevent CHD completely (1).

**Why it scores:** "discuss" needs more than one side. The last sentence gives a limitation, which a list of benefits alone never earns.

## Common mistakes

- Labelling the left ventricle on the left of the page.
- Saying the left ventricle is thicker "to pump more blood".
- Putting semilunar valves between the atria and ventricles.
- Saying valves "contract" or "pump". They open and close because of pressure.
- Calling the pulmonary artery a vein because its blood is deoxygenated.
- Describing CHD as blood "not reaching the heart". Blood fills the chambers; it is the **heart muscle** supplied by the coronary arteries that misses out.

## Exam technique for 9.2 command words

- **Identify**: name the structure exactly: "left atrium", not "atrium".
- **State**: short, factual; for monitoring give all three methods if asked for three.
- **Describe** heart function: muscle contraction, then valve action, in the right order.
- **Explain**: always add the consequence ("so a higher pressure is produced").
- **Investigate and describe**: name variables, repeats and how heart rate is calculated.
- **Discuss**: benefits **and** a limitation.
