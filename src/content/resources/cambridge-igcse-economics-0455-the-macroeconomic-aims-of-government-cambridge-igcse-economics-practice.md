All countries and figures are invented. Learn the content first from the [macroeconomic aims study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-the-macroeconomic-aims-of-government-cambridge-igcse-economics/) and the [macroeconomic aims revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-the-macroeconomic-aims-of-government-cambridge-igcse-economics-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Identify **two** macroeconomic aims of government. **[2]**

**2.** Which of these is the usual measure of economic growth?

A: the percentage change in nominal GDP

B: the percentage change in real GDP

C: the number of people in work

D: the value of exports

**[1]**

**3.** In Country S, 1.35 million people are unemployed and 25.65 million are employed. Calculate the unemployment rate. **[2]**

**4.** Which pair of aims is named in the syllabus as a possible conflict?

A: economic growth and full employment

B: full employment and stable prices

C: stable prices and redistribution of income

D: economic growth and redistribution of income

**[1]**

**5.** State **one** reason why a government aims for balance of payments stability. **[1]**

**6.** A consumer price index rises from 140 to 147. Calculate the rate of inflation. **[1]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** The government of Country T has these targets: growth of at least 3%, unemployment no higher than 6% and inflation between 1% and 3%. The table shows invented data.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Real GDP (billion) | 250 | 260 |
| Unemployment rate | 7.0% | 5.5% |
| Price index | 100 | 104.5 |

**(a)** Calculate the growth rate and the inflation rate in Year 2. **[2]**

**(b)** State which targets were met in Year 2. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how the data may show a conflict between two aims. **[3]**

**8.** Explain **two** reasons why a government aims for low unemployment. **[4]**

**9.** Country V's GDP rises from 800 to 840 billion. Imports are 30% of GDP and exports stay at 245 billion.

**(a)** Calculate the trade balance before and after the rise in GDP. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why economic growth may conflict with balance of payments stability. **[3]**

**10.** Explain why the criteria a government sets for its aims may differ from those of another country. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse how policies to achieve full employment may make balance of payments stability harder to achieve. **[5]**

**12.** Discuss whether a government should give priority to low inflation over low unemployment. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: economic growth [1]; full employment or low unemployment [1]; stable prices or low inflation; balance of payments stability; redistribution of income. **[2]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Nominal GDP can rise just because prices rise.

**3.** Labour force = 1.35 + 25.65 = 27 million [1]; 1.35 / 27 x 100 = **5%** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** B [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Any one: to avoid having to borrow from abroad or sell assets to pay for imports; to avoid pressure on the exchange rate [1]. **[1]**

**6.** (147 - 140) / 140 x 100 = **5%** [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** Growth = (260 - 250) / 250 x 100 = **4%** [1]; inflation = **4.5%** [1].

**(b)** Growth target met (4% is above 3%) [1]; unemployment target met (5.5% is below 6%) [1]; the inflation target was not met.

**(c)** Unemployment fell from 7.0% to 5.5% while inflation rose to 4.5%, above its target [1]. As more people found work, workers became scarcer and wages may have risen [1], increasing firms' costs and prices, so lower unemployment came with higher inflation [1]. **[7]**

**8.** Unemployed workers are an idle resource, so output is lower than the economy could produce [1], which means lower living standards for the country as a whole [1]. Unemployed people lose income and may fall into poverty [1], and the government must pay more in benefits while receiving less tax revenue [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** Before: imports = 0.30 x 800 = 240; balance = 245 - 240 = **+5 billion** [1]. After: imports = 0.30 x 840 = 252; balance = 245 - 252 = **-7 billion** [1].

**(b)** Growth raises incomes, so households spend more, including on imported goods [1]. Firms expanding output import more raw materials and machinery [1]. If exports do not rise as fast, the current account moves towards deficit [1]. **[5]**

**10.** Countries are at different stages of development: a low-income country may set a higher growth target to raise living standards quickly [1], while a high-income country may accept slower growth [1]. A country with a history of very high inflation may set a strict, low inflation target to restore confidence in its money [1]. A country that depends on foreign borrowing may set a tighter limit on its current account deficit [1]. **[4]**

**11.** To cut unemployment, the government may raise spending or cut taxes [1]. More people in work and higher incomes raise spending on all goods, including imports [1]. As the labour market tightens, wages and prices may rise [1], making the country's exports dearer for foreign buyers, so exports may fall [1]. Rising imports and falling exports worsen the current account [1]. **[5]**

**12.** For priority to low inflation: high inflation reduces the purchasing power of incomes and savings [1]; it makes exports less competitive and makes it hard for firms to plan, which can eventually harm jobs too [1].
For priority to low unemployment: unemployment wastes resources and causes hardship for families [1]; lost output can never be recovered [1].
Judgement: the priority should depend on the current situation [1]: if inflation is well above target and unemployment is low, reducing inflation should come first; in a recession with high unemployment and low inflation, creating jobs should come first [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by the population, not the labour force, when calculating unemployment.
- Using nominal GDP to measure growth.
- Stating a conflict without the steps that connect the two aims.
- Saying which targets were met without quoting the figures.
- Giving a "discuss" answer that never states what the priority depends on.
