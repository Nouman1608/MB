Condensed recall for **3.1.1 Forces and their interactions** in OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203), for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Statement f is P content (full Physics award only). For explanations and worked examples read the [Forces and their interactions study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-forces-and-their-interactions-oxfordaqa/); for exam-style questions use the [Forces and their interactions practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-forces-and-their-interactions-oxfordaqa-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Contact force | A force that acts only while the objects touch: friction, air resistance, tension, normal contact force |
| Non-contact (field) force | A force that acts across a gap: gravity, electrostatic force, magnetic force |
| Friction | A force between two surfaces that impedes motion and may cause heating |
| Air resistance | A form of friction on an object moving through air |
| Normal contact force | The push of a surface on an object, at right angles to the surface |
| Tension | The pull in a stretched rope, string, cable or spring |
| Scalar | A quantity with magnitude only (distance, speed, time) |
| Vector | A quantity with magnitude and direction (displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum) |
| Weight | The force on an object due to gravity; W = m × g |
| Gravitational field strength, g | Weight per kilogram, in N/kg; always given in the exam |
| Elastic distortion | The object returns to its original shape when the forces are removed |
| Inelastic distortion | The object does not return to its original shape |
| Extension | New length minus original length |
| Spring constant, k | Force per metre of extension, in N/m; a stiffer spring has a larger k |
| Limit of proportionality | The point beyond which extension is no longer proportional to force |

## Condensed recall

**Interactions (a and c).**
- Every force comes from an interaction between two objects.
- The two objects exert forces on each other. The forces are equal in size, opposite in direction, of the same type, and act on **different** objects.
- Each force is a vector, so draw it as an arrow: length for size, head for direction.

**Friction (b).**
- Opposes motion between surfaces; work done against it heats the surfaces and surroundings.
- Air resistance is friction with the air and increases with speed.
- Useful friction: grip of tyres, brakes, walking. Wasteful friction: in engines and bearings.

**Scalars and vectors (d).**
- Distance (scalar) is the length of the path; displacement (vector) is the straight-line distance from start to finish with a direction.
- A scale drawing must state its scale, for example 1 cm to 5 N.

**Weight (e).**
- W (N) = m (kg) × g (N/kg).
- Mass stays the same everywhere; weight changes with g.
- To go from weight to mass: m = W ÷ g.

**Distortion (f, P).**
- Needs more than one force on the object.
- Three kinds of distortion: stretching, bending, compressing.
- Test for elastic behaviour: remove the forces and check the original length or shape returns.

**Springs (g and h).**
- A stretched spring stores elastic potential energy (the equation is in section 3.2.1).
- F = k × e up to the limit of proportionality.
- Force-extension graph: a straight line through the origin while Hooke's law holds; gradient = k when force is on the y-axis.
- Beyond the limit the line curves and F ÷ e is no longer constant.

## Required practical in brief

Investigate the relationship between force and extension for a spring.

1. Hang the spring from a clamp with a vertical metre rule beside it; record the unloaded length.
2. Add masses one at a time, calculating each force with W = m × g.
3. Read the length at eye level each time, and calculate extension.
4. Plot force against extension; identify the straight section and the limit of proportionality.

Independent variable: force. Dependent variable: extension. Control variables: the same spring, the same reading point, the same rule.

## Unit conversions you will need

| From | To | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| cm | m | divide by 100 |
| mm | m | divide by 1000 |
| g | kg | divide by 1000 |
| mass in kg | force in N | multiply by g |

## Exam traps

- **Force pairs.** The weight of a book and the table's push on it both act on the book, so they are not a pair. The pair of the weight is the book's pull on the Earth.
- **Extension, not length.** Always subtract the original length first.
- **Masses are not forces.** A "200 g mass" must become 0.200 kg and then a weight in newtons.
- **Field forces need no touching.** A falling apple has weight acting on it even though nothing touches it.
- **"Elastic" is about returning to shape**, not about being stretchy.
- **Gradient direction.** If a graph has extension on the y-axis, the gradient is 1/k, not k.

## Self-test

**1.** Classify each as contact or non-contact: tension in a tow rope; the attraction between a charged comb and a scrap of paper.

**2.** A crate is dragged along the floor. State the direction of the friction on the crate and one effect of the friction besides slowing it.

**3.** A bird pushes down on the air with its wings. State the force pair partner of this push.

**4.** Which of these are vectors: speed, momentum, time, acceleration?

**5.** An arrow representing a 35 N force is drawn to a scale of 1 cm to 5 N. How long is it?

**6.** A 64 kg climber stands where g = 9.8 N/kg. Calculate the climber's weight.

**7.** An instrument weighs 18 N where g = 1.6 N/kg. Find its mass.

**8.** Why can a single force not bend a ruler?

**9.** A paper clip is bent open and stays open. Name this kind of distortion.

**10.** A spring with k = 25 N/m is stretched by 0.12 m. Find the force.

**11.** A spring 8.0 cm long stretches to 11.0 cm under a 1.5 N load. Find k.

**12.** In a spring experiment, F ÷ e is 50 N/m for the first five loads and 41 N/m for the sixth. What does this show?

## Self-test answers

**1.** Tension: contact. Charged comb and paper: non-contact (electrostatic).

**2.** Opposite to the direction of motion. It heats the crate and floor (energy is transferred by heating).

**3.** The air pushes up on the bird's wings with a force of equal size.

**4.** Momentum and acceleration.

**5.** 35 ÷ 5 = 7.0 cm.

**6.** W = 64 × 9.8 = 627.2 N, about 630 N.

**7.** m = 18 ÷ 1.6 = 11.25 kg, about 11 kg.

**8.** One force alone accelerates the ruler; at least two forces acting on it in different places or directions are needed to change its shape.

**9.** Inelastic distortion.

**10.** F = 25 × 0.12 = 3.0 N.

**11.** e = 3.0 cm = 0.030 m; k = 1.5 ÷ 0.030 = 50 N/m.

**12.** The limit of proportionality was passed between the fifth and sixth loads: extension is no longer proportional to force.
