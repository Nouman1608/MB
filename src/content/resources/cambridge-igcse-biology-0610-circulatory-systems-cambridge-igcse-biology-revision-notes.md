## Core recall: the definition

> A circulatory system is a system of **blood vessels** with a **pump** and **valves** to ensure **one-way flow** of blood.

Three parts, three jobs:

- **Vessels** carry the blood (arteries, capillaries, veins).
- **Pump** (the heart) creates pressure to move it.
- **Valves** (in the heart and in veins) stop backflow, so flow is one-way.

One-way flow means blood moves steadily from where it collects substances (lungs, gut) to where it delivers them (respiring tissues).

## Extended recall: two kinds of circulation

### Fish: single circulation

```
heart → gills → body → heart
```

- Blood passes through the heart **once** per complete circuit.
- The heart pumps only **deoxygenated** blood (it arrives from the body).
- Blood loses pressure in the gill capillaries, so it reaches the body at **low pressure**.

### Mammal: double circulation

```
pulmonary:  right side of heart → lungs → left side of heart
systemic:   left side of heart → body → right side of heart
```

- Blood passes through the heart **twice** per complete circuit.
- Right side handles deoxygenated blood; left side handles oxygenated blood.

### Full route of one circuit (mammal)

```
body → vena cava → right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs
lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → body
```

## Advantages of double circulation (Extended only)

Learn each advantage **with its consequence**; the consequence earns the mark in an "explain" question.

1. Blood returns to the heart after the lungs and is pumped again → reaches the body at **high pressure** → flows faster → oxygen and glucose reach respiring cells quickly.
2. Lungs receive blood at **lower pressure** → delicate lung capillaries are not damaged → time for gas exchange.
3. Oxygenated and deoxygenated blood are **kept separate** → blood sent to the body carries as much oxygen as possible.

Mammals need these because they are active and keep a constant body temperature, which needs a high rate of respiration.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Circulatory system | Blood vessels with a pump and valves ensuring one-way flow of blood |
| Pump | The heart; its muscle contracts to raise blood pressure |
| Valve | A flap that closes to prevent blood flowing backwards |
| Single circulation | Blood passes through the heart once per complete circuit (fish) |
| Double circulation | Blood passes through the heart twice per complete circuit (mammal) |
| Pulmonary circulation | Loop from the right side of the heart to the lungs and back to the left side |
| Systemic circulation | Loop from the left side of the heart to the body and back to the right side |
| Oxygenated blood | Blood that has collected oxygen at the lungs or gills |
| Deoxygenated blood | Blood that has given up oxygen to respiring tissues |

## Fish vs mammal at a glance

| | Fish | Mammal |
|---|---|---|
| Loops | 1 | 2 |
| Passes through heart per circuit | 1 | 2 |
| Exchange surface | gills | lungs |
| Pressure reaching body | low | high |
| Mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood in heart | heart holds deoxygenated blood only | prevented by separate sides |

## Exam traps

- **"Two hearts."** A mammal has one heart with two sides. Double means two passes through the heart.
- **"Blood goes round twice."** Say "passes through the heart twice in one complete circuit".
- **Lungs in the wrong loop.** Lungs are pulmonary; all other organs are systemic.
- **Fish route reversed.** The gills come directly after the heart, before the body.
- **Valves as pumps.** Valves never push blood; they prevent backflow.
- **Advantage with no consequence.** "Higher pressure" alone rarely scores in an explain question.
- **Wrong reason for low lung pressure.** The capillaries are delicate; the size of the lungs is not the reason.
- **Definition missing a part.** Vessels, pump **and** valves; the word "one-way" must appear.

## Command-word reminders

- **Describe** a circulation: route in order + number of passes through the heart.
- **Explain** an advantage: feature + "so" + effect on oxygen delivery or capillaries.
- **Compare**: one sentence covering both animals, joined by "whereas".

## Self-test

1. State the three parts of a circulatory system named in the syllabus.
2. What is the function of valves in a circulatory system?
3. (Extended) Write the route of blood in a fish, starting at the heart.
4. (Extended) How many times does blood pass through a fish heart in one complete circuit?
5. (Extended) Name the two circulations that make up the double circulation of a mammal.
6. (Extended) Which side of the mammalian heart pumps blood to the lungs?
7. (Extended) Why does blood reach the body organs of a fish at low pressure?
8. (Extended) Give the advantage of blood reaching mammalian body organs at high pressure.
9. (Extended) Why is it an advantage that blood is sent to the lungs at lower pressure?
10. (Extended) Is the blood pumped by a fish heart oxygenated or deoxygenated?
11. (Extended) A blood cell leaves the left ventricle. Name the vessel it enters and the circulation it is in.
12. (Extended) A student writes: "Mammals have a double circulation because they have two hearts." Correct the statement.

### Answers

1. Blood vessels, a pump and valves.
2. To prevent backflow, so blood flows in one direction only.
3. Heart → gills → body organs → heart.
4. Once.
5. Pulmonary circulation and systemic circulation.
6. The right side.
7. It loses pressure passing through the gill capillaries and is not pumped again before reaching the body.
8. Blood flows faster, so oxygen and glucose are delivered to respiring cells more quickly.
9. The delicate lung capillaries are not damaged, and blood moves slowly enough for gas exchange.
10. Deoxygenated.
11. The aorta; the systemic circulation.
12. Mammals have one heart with two sides; blood passes through the heart twice in each complete circuit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a definition with no mention of valves or one-way flow.
- Describing only the route without the number of passes through the heart.
- Giving three advantages as single words ("pressure", "separate", "lungs") with no explanation.
- Mixing up which side of the heart serves the lungs.
- Calling the lungs part of the body circuit.
