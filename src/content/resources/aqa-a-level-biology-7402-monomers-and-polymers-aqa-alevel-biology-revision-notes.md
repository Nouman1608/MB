## The five specification statements

1. Monomers are the smaller units from which larger molecules are made.
2. Polymers are molecules made from a large number of monomers joined together.
3. Monosaccharides, amino acids and nucleotides are examples of monomers.
4. Condensation joins two molecules with the formation of a chemical bond and the elimination of a molecule of water.
5. Hydrolysis breaks a chemical bond between two molecules and uses a water molecule.

Learn statements 4 and 5 word for word. Each has two parts (bond, water) and mark schemes usually want both.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Monomer | Smaller unit from which larger molecules are made |
| Polymer | Molecule made from a large number of monomers joined together |
| Condensation | Joins two molecules, forms a bond, eliminates water |
| Hydrolysis | Breaks a bond between two molecules, uses water |
| Glycosidic bond | Bond between two monosaccharides |
| Peptide bond | Bond between two amino acids |
| Phosphodiester bond | Bond between two nucleotides |
| Ester bond | Bond between glycerol and a fatty acid (in lipids, which are not polymers) |
| Dimer | Two monomers joined: disaccharide, dipeptide, dinucleotide |

## Monomer to polymer

| Monomer | Two joined | Many joined | Bond |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monosaccharide | Disaccharide | Polysaccharide | Glycosidic |
| Amino acid | Dipeptide | Polypeptide | Peptide |
| Nucleotide | Dinucleotide | Polynucleotide | Phosphodiester |

## Where the water comes from

- Condensation: –OH from one molecule + –H from the other leave as H₂O.
- Hydrolysis: H₂O splits; –OH and –H go back on to the two products.
- So condensation lowers total mass; hydrolysis raises it.

## Bond-counting rules

| Molecule | Bonds (and water molecules) for n monomers |
|---|---|
| One unbranched chain | n − 1 |
| One branched chain | n − 1 (each added unit adds one bond) |
| Circular molecule | n |
| Two separate chains, m and k units | m + k − 2 |

Mass of a chain = (n × Mr of monomer) − ((n − 1) × 18).

Mini reminder with new numbers: a chain of 300 identical monomers of Mr 150 has Mr = 45,000 − (299 × 18) = 45,000 − 5,382 = 39,618. Full hydrolysis uses 299 water molecules.

## Recognising bonds in diagrams

| Clue | Bond |
|---|---|
| Sugar–O–sugar | Glycosidic |
| C=O beside N–H in backbone | Peptide |
| Phosphate bridging two sugars | Phosphodiester |
| C=O beside O, joined to glycerol | Ester |

To find the monomers: add –OH to one side of each bond and –H to the other.

## Where hydrolysis and condensation happen in organisms

| Process | Reaction |
|---|---|
| Digestion of starch and proteins | Hydrolysis |
| Lysosome enzymes breaking down old organelles | Hydrolysis |
| ATP to ADP + Pi | Hydrolysis |
| Glycogen to glucose in liver | Hydrolysis |
| Protein synthesis at ribosomes | Condensation |
| DNA replication (DNA polymerase) | Condensation |
| ADP + Pi to ATP (ATP synthase) | Condensation |

## Exam traps

- "Hydrolysis releases water." Wrong: it uses water.
- "Lipids are polymers." Wrong: triglycerides are one glycerol plus three fatty acids.
- "A disaccharide is a polymer." Wrong: it is two units, not many.
- Subtracting one for a circular molecule. A ring of n units has n bonds.
- Forgetting branched molecules still have n − 1 bonds.
- Writing "starch bond" or "protein bond" instead of the bond's name.
- Leaving out the water when defining condensation.
- Saying total mass is unchanged on hydrolysis without counting the water added.

## Self-test

1. Define a monomer.
2. Define a polymer.
3. Name the three examples of monomers in the specification.
4. Define a condensation reaction.
5. Define a hydrolysis reaction.
6. Name the bond formed between two nucleotides.
7. A branched polysaccharide has 85 glucose units. How many water molecules were released in making it?
8. A circular DNA molecule has 4,200 nucleotides. How many phosphodiester bonds does it have?
9. Explain why a triglyceride is not a polymer.
10. Calculate the Mr of a linear chain of 50 glucose units (glucose 180, water 18).
11. Give one example of hydrolysis in the human body.
12. Two separate polypeptides of 120 and 95 amino acids are made. How many peptide bonds are formed in total?

### Answers

1. A smaller unit from which larger molecules are made.
2. A molecule made from a large number of monomers joined together.
3. Monosaccharides, amino acids and nucleotides.
4. A reaction that joins two molecules together with the formation of a chemical bond and the elimination of a molecule of water.
5. A reaction that breaks a chemical bond between two molecules and involves the use of a water molecule.
6. Phosphodiester bond.
7. 85 − 1 = 84.
8. 4,200 (a ring has as many bonds as monomers).
9. It is made of one glycerol and three fatty acids, not a large number of repeating monomers.
10. (50 × 180) − (49 × 18) = 9,000 − 882 = 8,118.
11. Any one: digestion of starch or proteins in the gut; breakdown of glycogen to glucose in the liver; hydrolysis of ATP.
12. 119 + 94 = 213.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between condensation and hydrolysis?**
Condensation joins two molecules, forming a chemical bond and eliminating water. Hydrolysis breaks a bond between two molecules using water.

**How do you count water molecules for a branched polymer?**
Use n − 1, the same as an unbranched chain, because each monomer added at a branch still forms only one new bond.

**Which part of the specification is this?**
