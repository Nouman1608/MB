These questions cover section **3.1.12.4 Weak acids and bases Ka for weak acids** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 and Paper 3. Learn the methods first in the [Weak acids and bases, Ka for weak acids study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-weak-acids-and-bases-ka-for-weak-acids/) and the [Acids and bases revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-acids-and-bases-revision-notes/). Assume 298 K throughout and give pH to two decimal places.

## Questions

**1.** Propanoic acid, CH₃CH₂COOH, is a weak acid.

**(a)** Write an expression for its acid dissociation constant, Ka. **[1]**

**(b)** State the units of Ka. **[1]**

**2.** Convert each value.

**(a)** Ka = 6.3 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ into pKa **[1]**

**(b)** pKa = 5.65 into Ka **[1]**

**3.** State the two assumptions used when calculating the pH of a solution of a weak acid HA from its concentration and Ka. **[2]**

**4.** Calculate the pH of 0.250 mol dm⁻³ benzoic acid solution. Ka = 6.3 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³. **[3]**

**5.** Calculate the pH of 0.0400 mol dm⁻³ propanoic acid solution. Ka = 1.35 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³. **[3]**

**6.** A 0.150 mol dm⁻³ solution of a weak acid HX has pH 2.62. Calculate Ka and pKa of HX. **[4]**

**7.** A solution of ethanoic acid has pH 3.20. Calculate its concentration. Ka = 1.75 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³. **[3]**

**8.** The pH of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid is 1.00 and the pH of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid is 2.88. Explain this difference. **[3]**

**9.** In a titration, a weak acid HA is half-neutralised with sodium hydroxide and the pH is measured as 5.35.

**(a)** Explain why [HA] = [A⁻] at this point. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate Ka for HA. **[2]**

**10.** Hydrogen cyanide, HCN, has Ka = 4.9 × 10⁻¹⁰ mol dm⁻³. Calculate the pH of 0.0100 mol dm⁻³ hydrogen cyanide solution. **[3]**

**11.** Calculate the percentage of propanoic acid molecules that are dissociated in a 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ solution. Ka = 1.35 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³. **[3]**

**12.** A student calculates the pH of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid (Ka = 1.75 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³) as follows: [H⁺] = 1.75 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.100 = 1.75 × 10⁻⁶ mol dm⁻³, so pH = 5.76.

**(a)** Identify the error. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the correct pH. **[2]**

**13.** A 0.200 mol dm⁻³ solution of ethanoic acid (Ka = 1.75 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³) is diluted to 0.0200 mol dm⁻³. Calculate the change in pH, and explain why it is not 1.00 as it would be for a strong acid. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Ka = [H⁺][CH₃CH₂COO⁻] / [CH₃CH₂COOH] [1]

**(b)** mol dm⁻³ [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** pKa = –log(6.3 × 10⁻⁵) = **4.20** [1]

**(b)** Ka = 10^(–5.65) = **2.2 × 10⁻⁶ mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** [H⁺] = [A⁻] (the ionisation of water is negligible) [1]; [HA] at equilibrium equals the initial concentration of HA (because dissociation is very small) [1]. **[2]**

**4.** [H⁺]² = Ka × c = 6.3 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.250 = 1.575 × 10⁻⁵ [1]; [H⁺] = 3.97 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **2.40** [1]. **[3]**

**5.** [H⁺]² = 1.35 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.0400 = 5.40 × 10⁻⁷ [1]; [H⁺] = 7.35 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **3.13** [1]. **[3]**

**6.** [H⁺] = 10^(–2.62) = 2.40 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; [X⁻] = [H⁺], [HX] ≈ 0.150 [1]; Ka = (2.40 × 10⁻³)² ÷ 0.150 = **3.84 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³** [1]; pKa = **4.42** [1]. **[4]**

**7.** [H⁺] = 10^(–3.20) = 6.31 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ [1]; c = [H⁺]² ÷ Ka = (6.31 × 10⁻⁴)² ÷ 1.75 × 10⁻⁵ [1]; c = **0.0227 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Hydrochloric acid is a strong acid and dissociates completely, so [H⁺] = 0.100 mol dm⁻³ [1]; ethanoic acid is a weak acid and dissociates only slightly, so only a small fraction of its molecules release H⁺ [1]; [H⁺] is much lower in ethanoic acid, so its pH is higher [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** Half of the acid has been converted into its salt (A⁻) by the alkali, so the amounts of HA and A⁻ are equal [1]

**(b)** Ka = [H⁺][A⁻] ÷ [HA] = [H⁺] [1]; Ka = 10^(–5.35) = **4.5 × 10⁻⁶ mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[3]**

**10.** [H⁺]² = 4.9 × 10⁻¹⁰ × 0.0100 = 4.9 × 10⁻¹² [1]; [H⁺] = 2.21 × 10⁻⁶ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **5.65** [1]. **[3]**

**11.** [H⁺] = √(1.35 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.0200) = 5.20 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ [1]; percentage = 5.20 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.0200 × 100 [1] = **2.60 %** [1]. **[3]**

**12. (a)** The student did not take the square root; [H⁺]² = Ka × c, so [H⁺] = √(1.75 × 10⁻⁶) [1]

**(b)** [H⁺] = 1.32 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **2.88** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A weak acid should never have a pH as high as 5.76 at a concentration of 0.1 mol dm⁻³. Check that your answer is sensible.

**13.** For 0.200 mol dm⁻³: [H⁺] = √(1.75 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.200) = 1.87 × 10⁻³ and pH = 2.73 [1]; for 0.0200 mol dm⁻³: [H⁺] = √(1.75 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.0200) = 5.92 × 10⁻⁴ and pH = 3.23 [1]; change = **0.50** [1]; [H⁺] depends on the square root of the concentration, so a tenfold dilution reduces [H⁺] by √10 (about 3.16), not by 10 [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating the weak acid as strong: [H⁺] = concentration.
- Forgetting the square root.
- Not stating the two assumptions.
- Giving Ka the wrong units.
- Forgetting that pH = pKa only at half-neutralisation.
