Quick recall for the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1) specification, Issue 2, February 2026, Topic 2 "People in business", sections 2.1 and 2.2. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-communication-recruitment-and-selection/); questions to try are in the [practice set](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-communication-recruitment-and-selection-practice/). Course links: the [4BS1 Business hub](/boards/edexcel/igcse/business/), the [printable checklist](/checklists/edexcel/igcse/business/) and the [free diagnostics](/diagnostics/). The methods, barriers, document contents and legal effects below are standard teaching, not specification wording; the specification states only the numbered points. Papers 1 and 2 both draw on the whole content, so be ready to apply every point to a small business and a large one. Amounts below are invented and in dollars.

## 1. Communication: why it matters (2.1.1)

- Communication is the passing of information from sender to receiver so that it is understood. Internal links staff and managers; external links the business to customers, suppliers and others.
- Good communication: fewer errors, better decisions, clear instructions, higher morale and employee satisfaction.
- Ineffective communication: wrong orders, missed deadlines, rumours, complaints, lost sales, staff who feel ignored.
- Always finish the chain: poor communication leads to a result, and the result costs money, time or reputation.

## 2. Methods, benefits and limitations (2.1.1)

| Method | Key benefit | Key limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Face-to-face / meeting | Instant feedback, tone | Slow, no record without minutes |
| Telephone | Fast, personal | No record, no visual cues |
| Letter | Formal, permanent | Slow, costs postage |
| Memo / notice board | Cheap, reaches many | May not be read |
| Report | Detailed, filed | Long, may not be read in full |
| Email (IT) | Fast, cheap, record | Overload, tone misread |
| Text / instant message (IT) | Very fast | Informal, little detail |
| Video conferencing (IT) | No travel | Equipment and connection needed |
| Intranet / website (IT) | One place, always available | Upkeep cost, can be out of date |
| Social media (IT) | Wide external reach | Public mistakes, monitoring |

**Choosing a method.** Ask five things: how urgent, how detailed, is a record needed, what does it cost, who has access to the technology.

**Method in steps for a "which method" answer:**

1. Name the method suited to the message.
2. Give one benefit linked to the case.
3. Give one limitation or a better alternative.
4. Make a judgement.

## 3. Barriers (2.1.2)

| Barrier | How it arises | Removal |
|---|---|---|
| Jargon | Technical words the receiver does not know | Plain language, explain terms |
| Language difference | Staff or customers use different languages | Translation, simple wording |
| Long chain of command | Message passes through many layers | Fewer layers, direct briefing |
| Overload | Too many messages or too much detail | Short messages, summaries |
| Technology failure | System down, no access | Back-up method, training |
| Poor listening or noise | Distraction, no attention | Quiet setting, note-taking |

**Remedy to remember:** feedback. Two-way communication (conversation, meeting) catches misunderstanding; one-way communication (notice, memo) does not.

## 4. Types of employment (2.2.1)

| Type | Meaning | One benefit | One limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full-time | The standard full working week | Reliable and committed | Highest wage cost |
| Part-time | Fewer hours than full-time | Flexible, pay for hours needed | Less availability |
| Job share | One full-time job split between two people | Cover for absence, keeps skilled staff | Handover and extra admin |
| Casual / seasonal / temporary | Short period, peak season or set task | Cost only while needed | Low loyalty, repeated training |

**Worked reminder.** A part-time worker on 25 hours when the full-time week is 40 hours works 25 ÷ 40 = 62.5% of a full-time week. Pay at 11.00 an hour is 25 × 11.00 = 275 a week.

## 5. Recruitment documents (2.2.2)

| Document | Who writes it | One line to remember |
|---|---|---|
| Job description | Employer | What the job is: title, duties, hours, reporting line |
| Person specification | Employer | Who is wanted: skills, qualifications, experience, qualities |
| Application form | Applicant completes the employer's form | Same questions for all, easy to compare |
| CV/résumé | Applicant | Applicant's own summary of history and skills |

## 6. The recruitment process (2.2.3)

| | Internal recruitment | External recruitment |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Existing staff | Outside the business |
| Benefits | Cheaper, quicker, known person, motivates staff | Wider choice, new ideas and skills |
| Limitations | Few candidates, no fresh ideas, leaves a vacancy | Costly, slower, unknown person |

- **Job advertisement:** title, duties, pay, location, requirements, how to apply, closing date. Choose a place that reaches the right people.
- **Shortlisting:** compare applications with the person specification and keep the best few.
- **Interviewing:** same core questions for everyone, scored against the person specification. It tests claims and personality but takes time.

**Worked reminder.** An advertisement runs for 5 days at 85 a day, and a listing fee is 300. Cost: 5 × 85 = 425, plus 300 = 725.

## 7. Legal controls (2.2.4)

- **Equal opportunities:** no less favourable treatment because of gender, race, disability, religion, sexual preference or age. Applies to adverts, shortlisting, interviews, pay and promotion.
- **Effects of equal opportunities:** fair adverts and criteria, consistent interviews, records, possible adaptations for disabled staff, training; benefits are a wider talent pool and reputation; risks of breaking the law are legal action and bad publicity.
- **Minimum wage laws:** a legal lowest hourly pay. Effects: higher wage costs (especially for part-time and casual roles), possible price rises or lower profit, better pay and motivation for workers.

**Worked reminder.** Minimum hourly pay 8.50 (invented). A worker on 35 hours must earn at least 35 × 8.50 = 297.50 a week. A worker on 28 hours paid 7.40 is short by 1.10 an hour: 28 × 1.10 = 30.80 a week, or 1,601.60 over 52 weeks.

## Command words with this topic

The specification defines the command words. Here is how each looks on people-in-business content.

| Command word | The specification's meaning, shortened | Example on this topic |
|---|---|---|
| Outline | Two linked points about a concept or issue, placed in the question's context | Outline one problem for Tolland Foods of using only notice boards |
| Explain | A statement of fact plus two expansion points, with no context | Explain one benefit of job share |
| Analyse | An extended answer expanding and exploring the issue, in context | Analyse how a language barrier could affect a call centre |
| Justify | Use the information given to recommend one of two options | Justify internal or external recruitment for a supervisor post |
| Evaluate | Use specification knowledge to reach a supported conclusion | Evaluate whether video conferencing suits a firm with three sites |

**Chain for an "explain" answer:** fact, then because, then so. For example: job share keeps skilled staff (fact), because two people can fit work around family time (because), so the business avoids recruiting and training replacements (so).

**Chain for "justify" and "evaluate":** weigh both sides using figures or details in the case, then give a decision and the main reason it wins, with a risk or condition that could change it.

## Must-know distinctions

- Job description (the job) versus person specification (the person).
- Internal recruitment (existing staff) versus external (outside).
- Job share (one job, two people) versus part-time (a separate smaller job).
- Barrier (what blocks a message) versus problem (what happens as a result) versus removal (the fix).
- Equal opportunities (who may be chosen and how) versus minimum wage (what must be paid).

## Quick self-test

1. State two benefits of email as a communication method.
2. Give one limitation of using a notice board.
3. Define a communication barrier.
4. Explain how jargon can be removed as a barrier.
5. State what a job share is.
6. State which recruitment document lists the duties of a post.
7. Give one benefit and one limitation of internal recruitment.
8. State the order of the three stages: interviewing, job advertisement, shortlisting.
9. Calculate the weekly minimum pay for 32 hours at a minimum of 8.50 an hour.
10. Explain one effect of equal opportunities laws on how a firm writes an advertisement.

### Answers

1. Fast and cheap; leaves a written record (also attachments and many recipients).
2. It may not be read, and there is no check that it was understood.
3. Anything that stops a message being received and understood as the sender meant.
4. Use plain language, explain technical terms and check understanding with feedback.
5. One full-time job divided between two (or more) people.
6. The job description.
7. Benefit: cheaper and quicker. Limitation: a smaller choice of candidates and no new ideas.
8. Job advertisement, then shortlisting, then interviewing.
9. 32 × 8.50 = 272 a week.
10. It must list the real requirements of the job and not prefer one gender, race, age or other group, so the firm reaches a wider pool and avoids legal action.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a method with no benefit and limitation that fit the situation.
- Listing barriers without saying how they arise or how to remove them.
- Swapping the job description and the person specification.
- Treating all part-time work as job share.
- Saying internal recruitment is always better, or external always dearer, with no reasoning.
- Describing equal opportunities in general without applying it to recruitment, selection or pay.
- Forgetting that minimum wage applies to part-time and casual workers too.
- Giving an "evaluate" or "justify" answer with no decision.
