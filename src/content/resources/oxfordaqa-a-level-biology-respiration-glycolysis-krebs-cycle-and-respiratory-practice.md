## Questions

**1.** State where in a cell each of these occurs: **(a)** glycolysis, **(b)** the Krebs cycle, **(c)** the electron transfer chain used in oxidative phosphorylation. **[3]**

**2.** A cell extract oxidises 0.35 mol of glucose by glycolysis. Calculate the amount of **(a)** ATP used, **(b)** ATP made, **(c)** net ATP gained and **(d)** reduced NAD formed. **[4]**

**3.** Describe the steps by which glucose is converted to pyruvate in glycolysis. **[4]**

**4.** A sprinter's leg muscle cells are short of oxygen.
**(a)** Explain why the cells convert pyruvate to lactate. **[3]**
**(b)** State the net number of ATP molecules gained from one glucose molecule respired in this way. **[1]**
**(c)** Give one difference between this pathway and the pathway in yeast without oxygen. **[1]**

**5.** A mitochondrial preparation respires 0.25 mol of glucose aerobically. Calculate the amount of **(a)** carbon dioxide released in the link reaction, **(b)** carbon dioxide released in the Krebs cycle, **(c)** reduced NAD made in the link reaction and Krebs cycle together, **(d)** reduced FAD made, and **(e)** ATP made directly in the Krebs cycle. **[5]**

**6.** Explain how the electron transfer chain and ATP synthase together make ATP in a mitochondrion. **[5]**

**7.** A compound that blocks the channel through ATP synthase is added to mitochondria that have been given pyruvate and oxygen.
**(a)** Predict what happens to the concentration of protons in the intermembrane space. **[1]**
**(b)** Predict what happens to the rate of oxygen uptake and explain your answer. **[3]**

**8.** Use these data. Assume 2.5 ATP for each reduced NAD made in the matrix, 1.5 ATP for each reduced NAD made in glycolysis, and 1.5 ATP for each reduced FAD.
**(a)** Work out the ATP yield of one glucose molecule respired aerobically, counting the ATP made directly in glycolysis and in the Krebs cycle. **[4]**
**(b)** Anaerobic respiration gives a net 2 ATP per glucose. Express this as a percentage of your answer to (a), to 2 significant figures. **[2]**

**9.** Cells in a fasting person respire stored triglycerides and some protein.
**(a)** Describe how a triglyceride can be used as a respiratory substrate. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why one gram of lipid gives more ATP than one gram of carbohydrate. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how an amino acid can be used in respiration. **[2]**

**10.** Palmitic acid is respired: C16H32O2 + 23 O2 → 16 CO2 + 16 H2O. Malic acid is respired: C4H6O5 + 3 O2 → 4 CO2 + 3 H2O.
**(a)** Calculate the RQ of palmitic acid, to 2 decimal places. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the RQ of malic acid, to 2 decimal places. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the RQ of malic acid is higher than that of palmitic acid. **[2]**

**11.** Germinating sunflower seeds are placed in a respirometer. The capillary tube has a diameter of 1.0 mm. In one tube, containing potassium hydroxide solution, the coloured drop moves 38 mm in 20 minutes. In a second tube with the same mass of seeds and no absorber, it moves 8 mm in the same time.
**(a)** Explain the role of the potassium hydroxide solution. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the volume of oxygen taken up in 20 minutes. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide released in 20 minutes. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the RQ. **[1]**
**(e)** Suggest what the RQ indicates about the substrate, and comment on how firm this conclusion is. **[2]**

**12.** A student measured the volume of carbon dioxide released by yeast in a glucose solution in 10 minutes at five temperatures, using a gas syringe.

| Temperature / °C | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume of CO2 / cm³ | 3.2 | 7.4 | 12.6 | 6.1 | 0.4 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate of respiration at 40 °C in cm³ per minute. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in volume between 40 °C and 50 °C. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the fall in volume above 40 °C. **[2]**
**(d)** State the purpose of a tube containing boiled yeast. **[1]**
**(e)** Suggest two changes that would let the student locate the optimum temperature more precisely. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Cytoplasm [1]. (b) Mitochondrial matrix [1]. (c) Inner mitochondrial membrane [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the part of the structure. "Mitochondria" alone is less precise than "matrix".

**2.** (a) ATP used = 2 × 0.35 = **0.70 mol** [1]. (b) ATP made = 4 × 0.35 = **1.4 mol** [1]. (c) Net = 1.4 - 0.70 = **0.70 mol** [1]. (d) Reduced NAD = 2 × 0.35 = **0.70 mol** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Start from the per-glucose figures and show the multiplication on each line.

**3.** Glucose is phosphorylated using ATP [1]. The six-carbon sugar phosphate splits into two triose phosphate molecules [1]. Triose phosphate is oxidised to pyruvate, with NAD accepting hydrogen to become reduced NAD [1]. ATP is formed from ADP, giving a net gain of ATP [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Put the stages in order and name the molecule at each. "Glucose is broken down to pyruvate" alone gives little to credit.

**4.** (a) Without oxygen, reduced NAD cannot be oxidised by the electron transfer chain [1]. Reduced NAD hands its hydrogen to pyruvate, forming lactate [1]. This regenerates NAD, so glycolysis can continue and keep making ATP [1]. **(b)** **2** ATP [1]. **(c)** Yeast forms ethanol and releases carbon dioxide, but lactate formation releases no carbon dioxide [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the conversion to NAD. Saying lactate formation "produces energy" is weak, because no extra ATP is made at this step.

**5.** Per glucose, link reaction: 2 CO2, 2 reduced NAD. Krebs cycle: 4 CO2, 6 reduced NAD, 2 reduced FAD, 2 ATP.
(a) 2 × 0.25 = **0.50 mol** [1]. (b) 4 × 0.25 = **1.0 mol** [1]. (c) (2 + 6) × 0.25 = **2.0 mol** [1]. (d) 2 × 0.25 = **0.50 mol** [1]. (e) 2 × 0.25 = **0.50 mol** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Multiply by the two turns per glucose. Per-acetyl CoA figures are easy to leave undoubled.

**6.** Electrons from reduced NAD and reduced FAD enter the electron transfer chain [1]. As electrons move between carriers in the inner membrane they release energy [1]. The energy is used to pump protons out of the matrix into the space between the membranes [1]. Protons flow back down their gradient through ATP synthase [1]. ATP synthase catalyses the formation of ATP from ADP and phosphate [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* State where protons are pumped from and to. "Protons pass through the membrane" gives no direction.

**7.** (a) It increases [1]. (b) Oxygen uptake decreases [1]. Protons cannot flow back through ATP synthase, so the proton gradient steepens and further pumping becomes harder [1]. Electron transfer slows, so less oxygen is accepted at the end of the chain [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For "predict and explain", give the direction of change first, then a chain of cause and effect.

**8.** (a) Glycolysis: 2 ATP + (2 × 1.5) = 5 [1]. Link reaction: 2 × 2.5 = 5 [1]. Krebs cycle: 2 ATP + (6 × 2.5) + (2 × 1.5) = 2 + 15 + 3 = 20 [1]. Total = 5 + 5 + 20 = **30 ATP** [1]. (b) 2 ÷ 30 × 100 [1] = **6.7%** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the yields the question supplies, not remembered figures, and set the sum out stage by stage.

**9.** (a) The triglyceride is hydrolysed to glycerol and fatty acids [1]. Glycerol is converted to triose phosphate, which enters glycolysis [1]. Each fatty acid chain is cut into two-carbon pieces that become acetyl CoA and go into the Krebs cycle [1]. (b) Lipids have more hydrogen atoms per gram [1], so more reduced NAD and reduced FAD are made, more protons are pumped and more ATP is made [1]. (c) The amino group is removed (deamination) [1]. The remaining carbon skeleton enters respiration, for example as pyruvate, acetyl CoA or a Krebs cycle intermediate [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* For (b), refer to hydrogen and reduced coenzymes. "Lipids have more energy" only restates the question.

**10.** (a) RQ = 16 ÷ 23 [1] = **0.70** [1]. (b) RQ = 4 ÷ 3 [1] = **1.33** [1]. (c) Malic acid has more oxygen atoms in the molecule than palmitic acid, so less oxygen from outside is needed to oxidise it [1]. More carbon dioxide is released for each oxygen molecule used, giving a higher ratio [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the fraction before the decimal. The substrate with less oxygen demand gives the larger RQ.

**11.** (a) It absorbs carbon dioxide, so the drop movement shows only the oxygen taken up [1]. (b) Radius = 0.50 mm, so volume = π × 0.50² × 38 [1] = **29.8 mm³** [1]. (c) Net volume without absorber = π × 0.50² × 8 = 6.28 mm³ [1]. Carbon dioxide = 29.845 - 6.283 = **23.6 mm³** [1]. (d) RQ = 23.56 ÷ 29.85 = **0.79** [1]. (e) A value of 0.79 is closer to the value for lipid (about 0.7) than carbohydrate, so the seeds may be respiring mainly lipid, perhaps with some carbohydrate or protein [1]. The conclusion is tentative: a mixture of substrates could give this value, and one run with one tube per condition gives no check on repeatability [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert the diameter to a radius first. In (e), give the value, a substrate and one reason for caution.

**12.** (a) 12.6 ÷ 10 = **1.26 cm³ per min** [1]. (b) (6.1 - 12.6) ÷ 12.6 × 100 [1] = **-52%** [1]. (c) At higher temperatures the enzymes of respiration denature [1], so fewer substrate molecules are respired and less carbon dioxide is released [1]. (d) It shows that the gas comes from living, respiring yeast and not from the solution alone [1]. (e) Measure at more temperatures between 30 °C and 50 °C [1]. Repeat each temperature at least three times and calculate a mean [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a sign with a percentage change. Two suggested changes should be different from each other.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Quoting 2 ATP for glycolysis without showing 4 made and 2 used.
- Confusing NAD with reduced NAD.
- Explaining anaerobic respiration as a way to make ATP, instead of regenerating NAD so glycolysis continues.
- Using per-acetyl CoA figures for a whole glucose without doubling them.
- Placing oxygen in the Krebs cycle instead of at the end of the chain.
- Describing protons as "pumped through ATP synthase" when the chain pumps them and they flow through ATP synthase.
- Dividing oxygen by carbon dioxide when working out RQ, or putting units on it.
- Using the capillary diameter as the radius.
- Naming a substrate from an RQ with no comment on how tentative that is.
