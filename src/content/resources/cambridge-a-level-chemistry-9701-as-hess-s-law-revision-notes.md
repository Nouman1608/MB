## Learning outcomes

2. **Calculate** using cycles, including enthalpy changes that **cannot be found by direct experiment**, and using **bond energy data**.

## Hess's law

The enthalpy change for a reaction is **independent of the route taken**, provided the **initial and final conditions are the same**. (A consequence of conservation of energy.)

## Choosing the cycle

| Data given | Common intermediate | Arrows | Expression |
|---|---|---|---|
| ΔH_f | elements (below) | up to reactants and products | ΔH_r = ΣΔH_f(products) − ΣΔH_f(reactants) |
| ΔH_c | combustion products, CO₂ + H₂O (below) | down from reactants and products | ΔH_r = ΣΔH_c(reactants) − ΣΔH_c(products) |
| bond energies | gaseous atoms (above) | up from reactants, down to products | ΔH_r = Σ(bonds broken) − Σ(bonds formed) |

Memory aid: **formation is products minus reactants; combustion is reactants minus products.**

## Rules for manipulating equations

- Reverse an equation → **change the sign** of ΔH.
- Multiply an equation by a number → **multiply ΔH** by the same number.
- Add equations → **add** their ΔH values; species on both sides cancel.
- ΔH_f of an element in its standard state = **0**.

## Cycle-drawing checklist

- Write the **target equation** across the top with state symbols.
- Put the **intermediate** (elements, combustion products or gaseous atoms) in the right place.
- Label every arrow with its value **multiplied by the moles** in the equation.
- Check the **direction** of each arrow before deciding whether to add or subtract.
- Make sure both routes start and finish with the **same substances in the same states**.
- Finish with the **sign**, the value and **kJ mol⁻¹**.

## Typical "cannot be measured directly" examples

| Target | Why not direct | Route used |
|---|---|---|
| ΔH_f(CO) | burning carbon gives some CO₂ | ΔH_c(C) and ΔH_c(CO) |
| ΔH_f of hydrocarbons, e.g. propane | elements do not combine to give it | combustion data |
| CuSO₄(s) → CuSO₄·5H₂O(s) | hydration is slow and uneven | two enthalpies of solution |
| CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ | needs very high temperatures | reactions of each solid with HCl |

## Quick worked examples

**From combustion data** (ΔH_c: C −394, H₂ −286, C₂H₅OH −1367 kJ mol⁻¹): ΔH_f of ethanol,

    2C + 3H₂ + ½O₂ → C₂H₅OH
    ΔH_f = [2(−394) + 3(−286)] − (−1367) = −788 − 858 + 1367 = −279 kJ mol⁻¹

**From formation data** (ΔH_f: SO₂ −297, SO₃ −396 kJ mol⁻¹):

    2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃
    ΔH_r = 2(−396) − [2(−297) + 0] = −792 + 594 = −198 kJ mol⁻¹

**From experiments** (illustrative values): CaCO₃ + 2HCl, ΔH₁ = −17 kJ mol⁻¹; CaO + 2HCl, ΔH₂ = −195 kJ mol⁻¹.

    ΔH(CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂) = ΔH₁ − ΔH₂ = −17 − (−195) = +178 kJ mol⁻¹

| Term | Meaning |

| Energy cycle | Diagram linking a target reaction to an alternative route through a common intermediate |

## Exam traps

- **Multiply** each value by its coefficient (3CO₂, 4H₂O and so on).
- Use the right expression: formation and combustion are **opposite** subtractions.
- Elements: ΔH_f = 0. Do not include O₂ in a formation sum.
- Reversing a reaction **changes the sign**.
- Bond energies only give good answers for **gas-phase** reactions; liquids need an extra step.
- Use **brackets** in your working; most sign errors happen when negative numbers are subtracted without them.
- Give the sign and units on the answer.

## Self-test

1. State Hess's law.
2. Which expression is used with enthalpies of combustion?
3. What is ΔH_f⦵ of N₂(g)?
4. Reverse the equation C(s) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g), ΔH = −394 kJ mol⁻¹. What is ΔH for the reversed equation?
5. Why can ΔH_f of carbon monoxide not be measured directly?
6. ΔH_c: C₂H₄ −1411, H₂ −286, C₂H₆ −1560 kJ mol⁻¹. Calculate ΔH for C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆.
7. ΔH_f: NaHCO₃ −951, Na₂CO₃ −1131, CO₂ −394, H₂O(l) −286 kJ mol⁻¹. Calculate ΔH for 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + CO₂ + H₂O.
8. Two enthalpies of solution are −66.5 (anhydrous salt) and +11.7 kJ mol⁻¹ (hydrated salt). Calculate the enthalpy change of hydration of the anhydrous salt.
9. Given (1) S + O₂ → SO₂, ΔH = −297 and (2) 2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃, ΔH = −198 kJ mol⁻¹, calculate ΔH for S + 1½O₂ → SO₃.
10. Why does a bond-energy cycle give a different value from ΔH_c⦵ for a fuel?
11. In a cycle using formation data, where are the elements placed and which way do the arrows point?

**Answers:**

1. The enthalpy change for a reaction is independent of the route taken, provided the initial and final conditions are the same.
2. ΔH_r = ΣΔH_c(reactants) − ΣΔH_c(products).
3. Zero.
4. +394 kJ mol⁻¹.
5. Burning carbon in limited oxygen gives a mixture of CO and CO₂.
6. (−1411 − 286) − (−1560) = −137 kJ mol⁻¹.
7. (−1131 − 394 − 286) − 2(−951) = −1811 + 1902 = +91 kJ mol⁻¹.
8. −66.5 − 11.7 = −78.2 kJ mol⁻¹.
9. (1) + ½ × (2): −297 + (−99) = −396 kJ mol⁻¹.
10. Bond energies are averages and refer to gaseous substances; ΔH_c⦵ forms liquid water.
11. Below the reaction; arrows point up from the elements to the reactants and to the products.
