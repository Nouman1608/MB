This page is a guide to AQA A Level English Language (7702) past papers for the June 2026, June 2025 and June 2024 series. It gives the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and a short account of what examiners reported, written by us from AQA's Reports on the Examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

Use the three documents together. Sit a paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it with the mark scheme, which shows what each level of response needs. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it explains why answers that looked sound still sat in a lower level. The notes for each series below are a quick way into those reports. Because this is a text-analysis subject, the reports are most useful once you have your own marked answer beside them.

Check which specification a paper was set on. 7702 is a linear, untiered qualification, first taught from September 2015 and first assessed in June 2017. It has two written papers of 2 hours 30 minutes, each worth 100 marks and 40 per cent of the A level: Paper 1 (Language, the individual and society) and Paper 2 (Language diversity and change). The third component is a non-exam assessment, Language in action, worth 100 marks and 20 per cent, marked by the teacher and moderated by AQA. That is 300 marks in all. All three series on this page were set on this specification.

## June 2025

The reports for June 2025 describe a cohort that was well prepared. On Paper 1 the analysis of texts was handled more securely than the data-based section, and on Paper 2 the later questions were harder than the essay. The common thread across the three components was the gap between knowing language-study ideas and using them on the data in front of you.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (7702/1): quotations offered with little or no linguistic description, features labelled loosely (whole clauses called phrases, a single word called a semantic field, every sentence called simple or complex), and word classes confused. In the data-based section, answers recalled theory well but gave thin description of the data and did not always match the right theory to the given statement. Some candidates ran short of time and left later answers brief. Very few attempted the optional written-language data question.
- Paper 2 (7702/2): the evaluative essay slipped when candidates recounted predictable case studies, or used older research on gender without questioning it. The discourse-analysis question was harder than the year before because the texts did not fit a simple prescriptive-or-descriptive split. In the directed writing task, many knew the attitudes towards accents but offered little linguistic knowledge, and some had little feel for the form and style of an opinion column.
- NEA (7702/C): the stated reasons for choosing the data were weak, ideas from language study were not built into either the method or the analysis, contextual factors were not tied to the language choices, and the commentary on the original writing did not deal properly with genre. Purely quantitative counting without examples in context was also flagged.

**What stronger answers did:** explored what the key words of an essay title might mean, evaluated specific examples, analysed how writers position their audience, and used the views in the source texts as the base for their own linguistically informed article. In the NEA, the best original writing showed a clear control of form, structure and register.

## June 2024

For June 2024, AQA's assessment-resources page lists a report on the examination for the NEA only. We did not find reports for Papers 1 and 2 in this series, so the notes below cover 7702/C and the thresholds cover the whole qualification.

**Where marks were lost**

- NEA (7702/C): tasks with vague or overlarge aims, investigations not rooted in a question from language study, and a method that did not explain why this text, time frame, speaker or variable was chosen. Analysis that stopped at naming individual word classes, without looking at patterns across the whole text or discourse, held marks back.

**What stronger work did:** set a realistic, tightly worded aim, mixed counting with close reading in context where that suited the question, grouped extracts to show patterns, and reached cautious conclusions that took account of how the data was produced and received. The moderators also noted wide-ranging topic choices, from media representation to code-switching and children's literature.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find AQA 7702 past papers?**
AQA publishes question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the assessment-resources page for 7702, and its past-paper finder lists papers by series.

**Why is the subject maximum 500 and not 300?**
AQA reports the subject boundaries on a scaled total. In its component tables the raw marks for Papers 1 and 2 are shown alongside scaled marks, so the total is larger than the 300 raw marks.

**Is there a report for every paper in every series?**
Not on the page we used. For June 2024 we found a report for the NEA but not for Papers 1 and 2, and no June 2026 reports were available to us.

**What do examiners say about weaker 7702 answers?**
Across June 2025 they describe linguistic description that is vague or inaccurate, theory that is listed without being applied, and NEA data choices that are not explained.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
