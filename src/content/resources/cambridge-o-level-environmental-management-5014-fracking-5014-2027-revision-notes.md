## Definition

**Fracking**: the extraction of natural gas or petroleum from shale rock by hydraulic fracturing.

Three parts to include: **what** (natural gas or petroleum), **where** (shale rock), **how** (hydraulic
fracturing).

## Why shale needs it

- Shale is a sedimentary rock made of compacted mud and clay.
- It is **impermeable**: pore spaces too small and poorly connected for gas to flow.
- Gas and oil stay locked in the shale, so cracks must be made for them to reach a well.
- Conventional reserves are different: gas has already moved up through permeable rock and is trapped
  under an impermeable layer, so it flows out of a simple well.

## The process in seven steps

1. Drill a vertical well down to the shale, far below drinking-water aquifers.
2. Turn the drill horizontally along the shale layer.
3. Line the well with steel casing and cement to seal it from surrounding rock.
4. Perforate the casing along the horizontal section.
5. Pump fracking fluid (water, sand, chemicals) down at very high pressure; the shale cracks.
6. Sand holds the cracks open.
7. Waste water flows back; gas or oil flows through the cracks to the well.

## Benefits bank

| Benefit | Because |
|---|---|
| Access to reserves that could not otherwise be used | Cracks give gas a path out of impermeable shale |
| Energy security | Less reliance on imports that can be disrupted |
| Lower energy prices | More supply |
| Jobs | Drilling, transport, services |
| Tax income | Government revenue from production |
| Less carbon dioxide than coal | Gas releases less carbon dioxide than coal when burned for electricity |
| Several wells from one pad | Horizontal drilling reaches a wide area of shale |

## Limitations bank

| Limitation | Because |
|---|---|
| Heavy water use | Large volumes per fracture job; competes with farms and homes |
| Ground water contamination risk | Failed casing can let chemicals or methane into aquifers |
| Waste water disposal | Flowback contains chemicals and dissolved substances |
| Small earthquakes | High-pressure fluid injection |
| Methane leaks | Greenhouse gas escaping from wells and pipes |
| Still a fossil fuel | Burning releases carbon dioxide; finite |
| Local disruption | Lorries, noise, light, land for well pads |
| Slower move to renewables | Cheap gas reduces investment in alternatives |

## Stakeholder views

- **Government**: energy security, tax income → often supportive.
- **Energy companies**: profit → supportive.
- **Job seekers**: employment → supportive.
- **Residents**: traffic, noise, water and earthquake worries → often opposed.
- **Farmers**: payments versus water competition and pollution risk → mixed.
- **Environmental groups**: methane, carbon dioxide, delayed renewables → opposed.

## "Discuss" plan (6 marks)

1. Benefit applied to the place in the question, with a reason.
2. Second benefit, with a reason.
3. Limitation applied to the place, with a reason.
4. Second limitation, with a reason.
5. A third point on either side, or a stakeholder contrast.
6. Conditional judgement: "worth it if... / only when..."

**Useful judgement phrases:** "as a short-term replacement for coal"; "only if strictly regulated and
monitored"; "not in areas short of water"; "alongside, not instead of, renewables".

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fracking | Extraction of natural gas or petroleum from shale rock by hydraulic fracturing |
| Hydraulic fracturing | Cracking rock using fluid pumped at high pressure |
| Shale | Impermeable sedimentary rock formed from compacted mud and clay |
| Fracking fluid | Mostly water, with sand and a small amount of chemicals |
| Casing | Steel pipe and cement lining a well |
| Flowback | Waste water that returns up the well after fracturing |
| Well pad | Surface site from which one or more wells are drilled |
| Energy security | Having a reliable supply of energy that is not easily disrupted |
| Aquifer | Permeable rock storing ground water |

## Exam traps

- A definition without "shale" or "hydraulic fracturing" is incomplete.
- Fracking is not used for coal, and it is needed because shale is **impermeable**.
- Gas releases **less** carbon dioxide than coal, not none.
- Methane is a greenhouse gas: leaks are a climate limitation, not just a safety issue.
- "Pollution" alone scores little; say what pollutes what, and how.
- "Discuss" needs both sides and a judgement.

## Self-test

1. Define fracking.
2. Name the rock from which fracking extracts gas.
3. Why can gas not flow out of shale without fracturing?
4. What are the three main components of fracking fluid?
5. What is the job of the sand?
6. Why is a fracking well lined with steel casing and cement?
7. Give **two** economic benefits of fracking.
8. Why can switching from coal to fracked gas reduce carbon dioxide emissions?
9. How could fracking contaminate ground water?
10. Why are methane leaks a limitation?
11. Suggest **one** group likely to oppose a new fracking site, and why.
12. Give **one** reason why fracking might be less suitable in a dry region.

**Answers:** 1. The extraction of natural gas or petroleum from shale rock by hydraulic fracturing.
2. Shale. 3. Shale is impermeable; its pore spaces are too small and poorly connected. 4. Water, sand and
chemicals. 5. It holds the cracks open so gas can flow. 6. To seal the well off from surrounding rock,
especially aquifers, so fluids and gas cannot escape. 7. Any two: jobs, tax income, lower energy prices,
fewer imports. 8. Gas releases less carbon dioxide than coal when burned to generate electricity.
9. If the casing or cement fails, chemicals or methane can escape into aquifers; waste water can also
be spilled. 10. Methane is a greenhouse gas, so leaks add to climate change and reduce gas's advantage
over coal. 11. Local residents, because of lorries, noise and worries about water pollution and
earthquakes (or environmental groups, because of greenhouse gases). 12. Each fracture job uses large
volumes of water, which would compete with farms and homes.
