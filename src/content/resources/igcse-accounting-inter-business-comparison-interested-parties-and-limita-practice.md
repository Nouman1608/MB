## Questions

**1.** Zephra Stationers is a small shop. A national chain of stationers
publishes its ratios. State two factors, other than management skill, that
may make the chain's gross profit margin differ from Zephra's. **[2]**

**2.** Stanmoor Cycles Limited and Nettlefold Cycles Limited sell similar
bicycles. For the latest year:

```
                                 Stanmoor      Nettlefold
Profit for the year                36,000          45,000
Interest paid                           0          15,000
Issued shares                     120,000         100,000
Reserves                           60,000          50,000
Loan (non-current)                      0         250,000
```

**(a)** Calculate ROCE for each business. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain one reason why the difference in ROCE may not show that
Stanmoor is better managed. **[2]**

**3.** Zephra's year ends on 31 December. A rival's year ends on 30 June
and it publishes only summarised statements. Explain two problems of
comparing Zephra's ratios with the rival's. **[4]**

**4.** Amberley Print Shop has current assets of 52,000, including inventory
of 14,000, and current liabilities of 40,000. It has asked its bank for a
loan.
**(a)** Calculate the current ratio and the acid test ratio. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the bank might use these ratios, and one other item of
accounting information, in deciding whether to lend. **[3]**

**5.** Pennyfold Angling Club has 45 members, each paying a subscription of 80
for the year. Its costs for the year were 4,050.
**(a)** Calculate the deficit and the deficit per member. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest one action the members could take, and justify it. **[3]**

**6.** Zephra Stationers has trade payables of 36,000, credit purchases of 292,000
and a current ratio of 0.9 : 1. Its supplier allows 30 days.
**(a)** Calculate trade payables turnover in days. **[2]**
**(b)** Advise the supplier whether to continue offering 30 days' credit,
using your answer and one other figure. **[3]**

**7.** Harlowe Foods Limited has profit for the year before interest of
63,000, issued shares of 180,000, reserves of 120,000 and a non-current
loan of 100,000. A property bought for 90,000 is now worth 210,000.
**(a)** Calculate ROCE with the property at historic cost. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate ROCE if the property were shown at its current value,
assuming profit is unchanged. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why historic cost makes a comparison with a business that
bought similar property recently misleading. **[2]**

**8.** A business buys a machine for 48,000 with no residual value. Profit
before depreciation is 90,000 and revenue is 600,000. It could depreciate
using straight line over four years or 40% reducing balance.
**(a)** Calculate the first-year depreciation under each method. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the profit margin under each method. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how this is a limitation when comparing two businesses. **[2]**

**9.** Amberley Print Shop's statements show rising profit. Explain how each
of the following could affect its future results although the statements do
not show it: (i) skill of the workforce; (ii) location of the business;
(iii) economic climate. **[6]**

**10.** A private investor has money to invest in Stanmoor Cycles Limited
or Nettlefold Cycles Limited. Ratios from the statements:

```
                              Stanmoor     Nettlefold
ROCE                            20.00%         15.00%
Profit margin                   12.00%          9.00%
Current ratio                  2.4 : 1         0.8 : 1
Trade receivables (days)         20.00           55.00
```

Evaluate which business looks the better investment, referring to the
ratios, the position of the investor and the limits of the information. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** The chain buys in bulk and may get lower prices [1]; pricing policy or the
type of goods sold may differ [1]. (Different accounting policies for
inventory would also be a valid factor.) **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that only say "better management" or "bigger",
with no link to prices or costs, are unlikely to answer the question set.

**2. (a)** Stanmoor capital employed = 120,000 + 60,000 = 180,000 [1];
ROCE = 36,000 ÷ 180,000 × 100 = **20.00%** [1]. Nettlefold profit before
interest = 45,000 + 15,000 = 60,000 and capital employed = 100,000 +
50,000 + 250,000 = 400,000 [1]; ROCE is 60,000 ÷ 400,000 × 100 =
15.00% [1].
**(b)** Nettlefold is financed mainly by a loan [1], which makes its capital
employed larger, so ROCE is lower for the same profit before interest [1].
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Adding interest back before dividing is an easy step to
overlook; writing each line out makes your method easier to credit.

**3.** Different year ends [1]: inventory and receivables would be at different
seasonal levels, so ratios are not like for like [1]. Summarised statements
[1]: credit sales or credit purchases may be missing, so some ratios cannot
be calculated [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the problem and then say what it does to the
ratios; a bare list of two problems leaves the explanation marks unclaimed.

**4. (a)** Current ratio = 52,000 ÷ 40,000 = **1.30 : 1** [1]. Acid test =
(52,000 - 14,000) ÷ 40,000 = **0.95 : 1** [1].
**(b)** Acid test below 1 : 1 means liquid assets are less than current
liabilities [1], so the bank may worry that repayments depend on selling
inventory [1]. Other item: profit for the year shows whether the shop can
meet interest, or non-current assets show security available [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Present both answers as ratios, as the syllabus asks, and
link them to the bank's decision rather than only defining them.

**5. (a)** Deficit = 4,050 - (45 × 80) = **450** [1]; per member = 450 ÷ 45 = **10.00** [1].
**(b)** Raise subscriptions by about 10.00 per member to cover the deficit
[1], because the deficit shows that costs exceed income [1]; but a higher
fee may cause members to leave, so the club could instead cut costs [1].
**[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A suggestion tied to the figure just calculated reads as
justified; one with no figure behind it reads as a guess.

**6. (a)** 36,000 ÷ 292,000 × 365 [1] = **45.00 days** [1].
**(b)** The business pays in 45 days against 30 allowed [1], and a current
ratio of 0.9 : 1 means current liabilities exceed current assets [1], so
the supplier might shorten credit or ask for earlier payment [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Advise" asks for a course of action backed by figures,
not a restatement of them.

**7. (a)** Capital employed = 180,000 + 120,000 + 100,000 = 400,000; ROCE
= 63,000 ÷ 400,000 × 100 = **15.75%** [2].
**(b)** Reserves rise by 210,000 - 90,000 = 120,000 [1]; ROCE = 63,000 ÷
520,000 × 100 = **12.12%** [1].
**(c)** The property is shown at an old, lower price [1], so a business that
bought recently shows higher capital employed and a lower ROCE even if
performance is the same [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Showing capital employed makes your method visible; "historic
cost is out of date" alone is weaker than an answer linked to ROCE.

**8. (a)** Straight line = 48,000 ÷ 4 = **12,000** [1]; reducing balance =
48,000 × 40% = **19,200** [1].
**(b)** Straight line profit = 90,000 - 12,000 = 78,000, so 78,000 ÷ 600,000
× 100 = **13.00%** [1]. Reducing balance profit = 70,800, so **11.80%** [1].
**(c)** Same machine and same year, but profit differs by 7,200 [1], so
two businesses using different methods can look unequal when they are not
[1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A difference in profit alone is not the point; the
answer should say why the comparison with another business is affected.

**9.** (i) A skilled workforce can improve quality and customer service [1]
but skill is not measured in money, so it is not in the statements [1].
(ii) A busy location can bring regular customers, or a move or road
closure can lose them [1], which statements do not show [1]. (iii) A
recession can reduce spending on printing, or high interest rates raise
costs [1], and the statements show past results only [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Give each aspect its own effect on the business; one
sentence repeated three times is unlikely to cover all three.

**10.** Stanmoor has the higher ROCE [1] and profit margin [1], so it earns
more on capital and per dollar of sales. Its current ratio of 2.4 : 1
suggests liquidity, against 0.8 : 1 at Nettlefold [1], and customers take
20 days to pay on average, against 55 [1]. A shareholder in Nettlefold may bear more
risk because the loan has to be repaid and interest paid before profit
reaches owners [1]. But Nettlefold's loan lowers its ROCE, and other
factors may explain the gap, such as size or type of goods [1]. The ratios
come from historic cost figures, which may undervalue assets [1], and
different accounting policies could also affect profit [1]. One year may be
unusual, and workforce skill or location is not shown [1]. Overall,
Stanmoor looks the stronger investment on these figures, but the
investor should check more years and the non-financial aspects first [1].
**[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation marks tend to go to answers that reach a
judgement and give a reason for caution; a list of ratios without a
conclusion usually leaves them unclaimed.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a ratio as a bare number when the syllabus wants a ratio such as
1.30 : 1.
- Using profit for the year instead of profit before interest in ROCE.
- Naming a problem of comparison without saying how it changes the ratios.
- Giving a party's name but no decision.
- Calling historic cost "inaccurate" instead of saying it shows past cost.
- Giving a non-financial aspect with no effect on the business.
- Ending an evaluation without a judgement.
