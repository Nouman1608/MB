These notes condense section 3.4.1, Purity and chromatography, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202) specification, Version 6.2. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [purity and chromatography study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-chemistry-9202-purity-and-chromatography/); then try the [purity and chromatography practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-chemistry-9202-purity-and-chromatography-practice/).

## Statements a to e in one line each

- **a** A pure element or compound contains only one substance; identify and assess purity from melting and boiling points.
- **b** Purity matters in foodstuffs and drugs.
- **c** Mixtures: substances not chemically combined, properties unchanged, separated physically (distillation, filtration, crystallisation).
- **d** Paper chromatography analyses solutions such as food colourings and inks; identify components with Rf values; solvents other than water can be used.
- **e** Stationary phase and mobile phase; separation depends on relative solubility; suggest methods to tell pure from impure.

## Pure: chemistry versus everyday

| Chemistry | Everyday |
|---|---|
| one substance only | nothing added, natural |
| "pure water" = H₂O only | "pure spring water" contains dissolved minerals |

## Melting and boiling point evidence

| Observation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sharp, matches data value | pure; identified |
| Melts over a range | impure |
| Melts below data value | impure |
| Boils over a range or above data value | impure |
| Sharp, but no match in data | a different pure substance |

## Mixture or compound?

| Mixture | Compound |
|---|---|
| substances not chemically combined | elements chemically combined |
| each keeps its own properties | new properties |
| any proportions | fixed proportions |
| separated physically | separated only by reactions |

Physical methods: **filtration** (insoluble solid), **crystallisation** (dissolved solid), **distillation** (liquid from solution, or liquids by boiling point).

## Paper chromatography

```
           solvent front  ─────────────
                          ●  more soluble in solvent: travels further
                          ●
                          ●  less soluble: travels less
start line (pencil)       ──●──●──────
solvent (below the line)  ~~~~~~~~~~~
```

| Phase | What it is | Moves? |
|---|---|---|
| Stationary | the paper | no |
| Mobile | the solvent | yes |

Separation depends on **relative solubility**: how a substance is distributed between the mobile and stationary phases.

## Rf

```
Rf = distance moved by substance ÷ distance moved by solvent
```

- Measure both from the start line.
- No units; less than 1.
- Same substance + same solvent = same Rf.
- Rearranged: distance moved by substance = Rf × distance moved by solvent.

Quick check: solvent 8.0 cm, spot 2.0 cm → Rf 0.25.

## Methods for showing purity

1. One spot = pure; two or more spots = impure.
2. Run alongside a pure reference.
3. Repeat in a second, different solvent.

## Why purity matters

- **Drugs**: correct dose; impurities may be harmful.
- **Foods**: safety, taste, colour, keeping qualities.

## Worked recall: identifying an unknown spot

Solvent front 10.0 cm. Unknown spot at 4.5 cm. References in the same solvent: P 0.25, R 0.45, S 0.60.

```
Rf = 4.5 ÷ 10.0 = 0.45  → matches R
```

Always compare Rf values measured in the **same solvent**; Rf changes when the solvent changes.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pure substance | contains only one substance |
| Mixture | two or more substances not chemically combined |
| Stationary phase | the part of a chromatography system that does not move (the paper) |
| Mobile phase | the part that moves (the solvent) |
| Solvent front | the furthest point the solvent reaches |
| Rf value | distance moved by substance ÷ distance moved by solvent |
| Relative solubility | how soluble a substance is in the mobile phase compared with its attraction to the stationary phase |

## Exam traps

- Use the **chemical** meaning of pure.
- Impurities **lower** melting points and cause a **range**.
- Pencil start line, **above** the solvent.
- Paper = stationary; solvent = mobile.
- Separation depends on **relative solubility**, not particle size.
- One spot in one solvent is strong evidence of purity, not absolute proof.
- Rf is always less than 1; if yours is more, the division is upside down.

- Mark the solvent front as soon as the paper is removed; it fades as the solvent evaporates.
- A substance that stays on the start line is insoluble in that solvent: try another solvent.

## Self-test

1. What does "pure" mean in chemistry?
2. How does the melting point of an impure solid differ from that of the pure substance?
3. Give two reasons why the purity of a drug is important.
4. Name three physical methods for separating mixtures.
5. Name the stationary phase and the mobile phase in paper chromatography.
6. What does separation in chromatography depend on?
7. Why is the start line drawn in pencil?
8. A spot moves 3.3 cm and the solvent front 6.0 cm. Calculate the Rf value.
9. Dye D has an Rf of 0.40. The solvent front moves 7.5 cm. How far does D move?
10. Why might a solvent other than water be used?
11. Suggest two chromatographic ways of checking whether a sample is pure.
12. A liquid boils between 96 °C and 104 °C. What does this show?

## Answers

1. It contains only one substance, with nothing else mixed in.
2. It is lower and the solid melts over a range of temperatures.
3. The dose depends on the amount of active substance; impurities could be harmful.
4. Distillation, filtration, crystallisation.
5. Stationary: the paper. Mobile: the solvent.
6. The relative solubility of the components (how they are distributed between the mobile and stationary phases).
7. Pencil does not dissolve in the solvent, so it does not move or interfere with the spots.
8. 3.3 ÷ 6.0 = 0.55.
9. 0.40 × 7.5 = 3.0 cm.
10. The substances may not dissolve in water, so they would not move.
11. Any two: look for a single spot; compare with a pure reference run alongside; repeat with a different solvent.
12. It is not pure: it boils over a range.
