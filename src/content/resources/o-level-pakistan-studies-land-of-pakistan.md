This guide covers **Section 1: The Land of Pakistan**, the opening
section of Paper 2 The Environment of Pakistan, for Cambridge O
Level Pakistan Studies 2059, 2026 series (Version 1, no significant
changes affecting teaching).

## Where this fits in 2059

Paper 1 (covered separately) is The History and Culture of Pakistan.
Paper 2 is The Environment of Pakistan, and Section 1 lays the
physical foundation for everything that follows in that paper --
location, administrative geography, topography and climate all
recur as context in later Paper 2 sections on natural resources,
agriculture, industry and population.

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE O LEVEL PAKISTAN STUDIES (2059) — PAPER 2, SECTION 1: THE
LAND OF PAKISTAN**

- (a) Location of Pakistan — identifying the Tropic of Cancer,
  latitudes 30°N and 36°N, longitudes 64°E, 70°E and 76°E, the
  Arabian Sea, the countries bordering Pakistan, and Pakistan's
  position within South and Central Asia
- (b) Location of administrative areas and cities — identifying
  Pakistan's administrative areas, and named cities including
  Islamabad, Murree, Rawalpindi, Gujranwala, Lahore, Faisalabad,
  Multan, Sialkot, Peshawar, Chitral, Gilgit, Hyderabad, Karachi,
  Quetta and Gwadar
- (c) The natural topography, including drainage — identifying named
  landforms (Balochistan Plateau, Sulaiman Range, Safed Koh, Potwar
  Plateau, Salt Range, Hindu Kush, Karakoram and Himalaya mountain
  ranges), named rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Sutlej, Kabul,
  Hab and Dasht) and named deserts (Thar, Thal and Kharan); using
  appropriate vocabulary to describe mountains, plateaux, floodplains
  and deserts; and understanding the influence of topography on
  human activities such as land use and transport routes
- (d) Climate — the distribution of temperature and rainfall
  (including monsoon, depressions and convectional rain); seasonal
  and regional variation and its causes; the causes of the monsoon;
  the characteristics of arid, semi-arid, humid and highland climate
  regions; the influence of latitude and longitude on day length and
  climate; and the influence of climate on the economy and on
  people's lives, including the effects of low temperature, flooding
  and drought

## How to approach it

Map-based recall is examined directly here, so practise locating
every named landform, river, desert and city from memory on a blank
outline map of Pakistan -- this section is tested visually as often
as it is tested in writing. Learn topography and climate together
rather than as separate blocks: the syllabus explicitly links the two
(mountains and deserts shaping road and rail networks, climate
shaping agriculture and water supply), and Paper 2 questions reward
candidates who can connect a physical feature to its human
consequence. Note that the formation of Pakistan's landforms is
explicitly *not* required -- only their identification and influence
-- so don't spend revision time on geological processes this section
does not ask for.

## Locating Pakistan

Pakistan sits in South Asia, bordered by the Arabian Sea to the
south, and shares land borders with neighbouring countries across
its northern, western and eastern frontiers. The syllabus specifies
exact reference lines candidates should be able to place on a map --
the Tropic of Cancer and the latitudes 30°N and 36°N, and the
longitudes 64°E, 70°E and 76°E -- which together frame Pakistan's
position and help explain its range of climates from tropical in the
south to temperate and alpine in the northern mountains.

## Topography: mountains, plains, deserts

Pakistan's topography divides broadly into three: the great mountain
ranges of the north and west (Hindu Kush, Karakoram, Himalaya,
Sulaiman Range, Safed Koh), the plateaux (Balochistan Plateau, Potwar
Plateau, Salt Range), and the river plains and deserts of the south
(fed by the Indus and its tributaries -- Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi and
Sutlej -- alongside the Thar, Thal and Kharan deserts).

| Feature type | Named examples |
|---|---|
| Mountain ranges | Hindu Kush, Karakoram, Himalaya, Sulaiman Range, Safed Koh |
| Plateaux | Balochistan Plateau, Potwar Plateau, Salt Range |
| Rivers | Indus, Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Sutlej, Kabul, Hab, Dasht |
| Deserts | Thar, Thal, Kharan |

This topography directly shapes where people live and how they move:
steep mountain terrain limits road and rail construction and
concentrates settlement in valleys, while flatter land in the plains
supports denser transport networks and agriculture.

## Climate zones and their human impact

Pakistan spans several climate zones -- arid, semi-arid, humid and
highland -- each with distinct seasonal characteristics. The monsoon,
driven by seasonal reversal of wind direction and pressure systems,
brings the bulk of annual rainfall to much of the country and is the
single most consequential climatic feature for agriculture and water
supply. Highland regions in the north experience low temperatures,
ice and snow that shape the lives of mountain communities, while
lowland regions face the opposite risks: drought and water shortage
on one hand, and flooding from heavy rain storms on the other. Every
climate answer in this section should end by tying the physical
pattern to its effect on people -- on farming, industry or daily
life -- since that link is what the syllabus tests.

## Worked example

A question asks candidates to explain how the natural topography of
northern Pakistan has influenced its transport networks. A strong
answer identifies the specific mountain ranges involved (for example,
the Karakoram and Himalaya), explains that steep, high-altitude
terrain makes road and rail construction difficult and expensive, and
concludes that this has historically concentrated transport routes
along valleys and passes rather than allowing a dense, evenly spread
network -- moving from named feature, to physical constraint, to
human consequence.

## Common mistakes

Naming landforms, rivers or deserts without being able to locate them
on a map. Describing the geological formation of a landform, which
this section does not require. Treating climate and topography as
unconnected topics rather than explaining how one influences the
other. Giving a vague answer about "harsh climate" instead of
specifying which climate zone, which season, and which precise human
effect (agriculture, water supply, transport) is being described.

## Quick revision checklist

- Locate the Tropic of Cancer, the specified latitudes and
  longitudes, and Pakistan's regional position on a blank map.
- Name and locate Pakistan's administrative areas and the cities
  listed in the syllabus.
- Name and locate Pakistan's major mountain ranges, plateaux, rivers
  and deserts.
- Explain how topography has influenced land use and transport
  networks.
- Describe the causes of the monsoon and Pakistan's main climate
  zones.
- Explain the effects of climate (temperature, rainfall, flooding,
  drought) on Pakistan's economy and people.
