This guide goes deeper on one topic: **Theme 2, Topic 4 Travel and Tourism** in OxfordAQA
International GCSE Urdu (9264), for International GCSE exams May/June 2026 onwards. The
[Theme 2 study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-urdu-local-national-international/) explains how
the four topics of the theme fit together; this page concentrates on the vocabulary, grammar and
task habits that travel and tourism in particular call for.

## What this topic covers

The specification (section 3.1.2) lists Travel and tourism as the fourth topic of Theme 2. In
practice it asks you to understand and produce Urdu about:

- **Holidays and trips** -- where you went or would like to go, who with, and what you did there.
- **Transport** -- how you travel, and the advantages and disadvantages of different ways of
  travelling.
- **Planning and booking** -- tickets, hotels, dates and simple requests.
- **Destinations** -- describing and comparing places, weather and sights.
- **Tourism as an issue** -- what tourism brings to a place and what problems it can cause, which
  links this topic to Topic 3 (Global issues).

## Where this fits in 9264

Like every topic in the specification, Travel and tourism has no paper of its own. It can appear in
Paper 1 (Reading, Grammar and Meaning), which includes translation from English into Urdu, and in
Paper 2 (Writing). Each paper lasts 1 hour 45 minutes and is worth 70 marks (50% of the
qualification), so travel vocabulary has to be ready for both reading and writing.

## Key vocabulary

Learn these actively, so that you can write them from memory, not only recognise them.

| Urdu | English | Urdu | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| سفر | journey, travel | منزل | destination |
| سیاحت | tourism | سیاح | tourist |
| چھٹیاں | holidays | سیر کرنا | to go sightseeing, to tour |
| ہوائی جہاز | aeroplane | ہوائی اڈا | airport |
| ٹرین / ریل گاڑی | train | ٹکٹ | ticket |
| پاسپورٹ | passport | سامان | luggage |
| ہوٹل | hotel | ٹھہرنا | to stay |
| ساحلِ سمندر | seaside, beach | پہاڑ | mountain |
| تاریخی عمارت | historic building | عجائب گھر | museum |
| موسم | weather | یادگار | memorable |

Gender matters for agreement: سفر is masculine (لمبا سفر, a long journey), while چھٹی is
feminine (اچھی چھٹی, a good holiday).

## Structures that carry this topic

### 1. Past tense, with and without نے

A holiday recount lives in the past tense, and the commonest accuracy problem is نے.

- With an **intransitive** verb (go, come, stay), there is no نے and the verb agrees with the
  subject: ہم ٹرین سے لاہور گئے۔ (We went to Lahore by train.)
- With a **transitive** verb in the simple past (see, do, eat), the subject takes نے and the verb
  agrees with the object: ہم نے بادشاہی مسجد دیکھی۔ (We saw the Badshahi Mosque.) Here
  دیکھی is feminine because مسجد is feminine.

### 2. Past habitual for description

To say what you used to do every day of a holiday, use the habitual past, which does not take
نے: ہر روز ہم ساحل پر جاتے تھے۔ (Every day we used to go to the beach.) Description of
conditions uses تھا / تھی / تھے: موسم بہت گرم تھا۔ (The weather was very hot.)

### 3. Comparatives and superlatives

Use سے for "than" and سب سے for "the most":

- ٹرین کا سفر بس کے سفر سے زیادہ آرام دہ ہے۔ (Travelling by train is more comfortable than by bus.)
- یہ میری سب سے یادگار چھٹی تھی۔ (This was my most memorable holiday.)

### 4. Future plans and conditions

- اگلے سال ہم مری جائیں گے۔ (Next year we will go to Murree.)
- اگر موسم اچھا رہا تو ہم پہاڑوں پر جائیں گے۔ (If the weather stays good, we will go to
  the mountains.)
- اگر میرے پاس پیسے ہوتے تو میں ترکی جاتا۔ (If I had the money, I would go to Turkey.) This
  unreal condition uses the same form in both halves.

### 5. Evaluation and the wider issue

A recount becomes a strong answer when you judge it: میرے خیال میں یہ ایک یادگار چھٹی تھی
کیونکہ پورا خاندان ساتھ تھا۔ (In my opinion it was a memorable holiday because the whole
family was together.) For tourism as an issue, a balanced sentence works well:
سیاحت سے مقامی لوگوں کو روزگار ملتا ہے، لیکن اس سے آلودگی بھی بڑھتی ہے۔ (Tourism gives
local people work, but it also increases pollution.)

## Worked example 1: a holiday recount

Task (original): *Write a short blog post about a memorable trip. Say where you went, how you
travelled, what you did, what you thought of it and where you would like to go next.*

Model paragraph:

پچھلی گرمیوں میں ہم اپنے دادا دادی سے ملنے کراچی گئے۔ ہم نے ہوائی جہاز سے سفر کیا اور سفر
بہت آرام دہ تھا۔ ایک دن ہم ساحلِ سمندر پر گئے جہاں ہم نے اونٹ کی سواری کی۔ میرے خیال میں یہ
ایک یادگار چھٹی تھی کیونکہ پورا خاندان ساتھ تھا۔ اگلے سال میں شمالی علاقوں کی سیر کرنا چاہتا
ہوں۔

Gloss: "Last summer we went to Karachi to see my grandparents. We travelled by plane and the journey
was very comfortable. One day we went to the beach, where we rode a camel. In my opinion it was a
memorable holiday because the whole family was together. Next year I would like to tour the northern
areas."

Why it works:

```
past, no نے (intransitive)   ہم ... کراچی گئے / ساحلِ سمندر پر گئے
past with نے (transitive)    ہم نے ... سفر کیا / ہم نے اونٹ کی سواری کی
description                  سفر بہت آرام دہ تھا
opinion + reason             میرے خیال میں ... کیونکہ ...
future wish                  اگلے سال ... سیر کرنا چاہتا ہوں
```

Every part of the task is answered, and the paragraph shows three time frames: past events,
an evaluation, and a plan. A girl would write چاہتی ہوں in the last sentence.

## Worked example 2: translating into Urdu

Task (original): *Translate into Urdu: "Last year we went to Lahore by train. We saw the Badshahi
Mosque. Next year I will go to Murree."*

Answer: پچھلے سال ہم ٹرین سے لاہور گئے۔ ہم نے بادشاہی مسجد دیکھی۔ اگلے سال میں مری جاؤں گا۔

Decisions to make, sentence by sentence:

1. "went" is intransitive, so no نے, and گئے agrees with ہم.
2. "saw" is transitive, so ہم takes نے and دیکھی agrees with the feminine مسجد.
3. "will go" uses the future: جاؤں گا for a boy, جاؤں گی for a girl.

"By train" is ٹرین سے, not a word-for-word "on the train". Translating the sense of the whole
sentence, rather than one English word at a time, is what keeps Urdu word order natural, with the
verb at the end.

## Common mistakes

- **نے with an intransitive verb.** ہم نے لاہور گئے is wrong; write ہم لاہور گئے.
- **Verb agreeing with the subject after نے.** ہم نے مسجد دیکھا is wrong; after نے the
  verb agrees with the object, so ہم نے مسجد دیکھی.
- **Recount with no judgement.** A list of past events, however accurate, leaves out the opinion
  and reason that show a wider range of language.
- **One time frame only.** Travel tasks often invite past, present and future; using only the past
  tense under-shows what you can do.
- **Gender slips on travel nouns.** اچھا چھٹی and لمبی سفر are both wrong; check the noun's
  gender before choosing the adjective ending.
- **Mixing up سیر and سفر.** سفر is the journey itself; سیر is sightseeing or an outing.

## Exam technique

- **Read the instruction first.** Note whether a reading question asks you to answer in English or
  in Urdu, and whether a writing task names an audience (a friend, a blog, a hotel).
- **Answer every bullet point** of a writing task, in the order given, so none is forgotten.
- **Plan the time frames.** Before you write, mark which points need the past, which the present,
  and which the future or a condition.
- **Budget by marks.** Both papers give about 1.5 minutes per mark (105 minutes for 70 marks), so a
  10-mark task deserves about 15 minutes, including checking.
- **Check نے and agreement last.** A two-minute check of every past-tense verb catches the errors
  that are most frequent in this topic.
