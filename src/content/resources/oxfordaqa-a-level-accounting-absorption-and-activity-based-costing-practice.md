## Questions

**1.** A department absorbed overheads of 64,000. Its actual overheads were 61,500. Which **one** statement is correct? **[1]**

A. Under-absorbed by 2,500; profit is reduced
B. Over-absorbed by 2,500; profit is increased
C. Over-absorbed by 2,500; profit is reduced
D. Under-absorbed by 2,500; profit is increased

**2.** Explain the difference between apportionment and attribution. **[2]**

**3.** Define a cost driver and suggest a suitable one for a machine maintenance cost pool. **[2]**

**4.** Yelland Kayaks has two production departments: Shell (machine-led) and Fitting-out (hand work). Budgeted overheads:

| Overhead | Total |
|---|---|
| Indirect wages: Shell 29,300, Fitting-out 41,900 | 71,200 |
| Factory rent | 57,600 |
| Plant depreciation | 32,000 |
| Canteen costs | 13,500 |

| | Shell | Fitting-out |
|---|---|---|
| Floor area (m²) | 1,000 | 1,400 |
| Cost of plant | 360,000 | 40,000 |
| Employees | 7 | 20 |
| Budgeted machine hours | 10,700 | 900 |
| Budgeted direct labour hours | 2,300 | 17,740 |

**(a)** Prepare an overhead analysis showing each department's total budgeted overheads. **[5]**
**(b)** Calculate an appropriate overhead absorption rate for each department. **[2]**
**(c)** One kayak uses 214.00 of materials, 1.5 direct labour hours and 4 machine hours in Shell, and 7 direct labour hours in Fitting-out. Labour is paid 17 an hour. Calculate the total cost of one kayak. **[3]**
**(d)** Calculate the selling price at a 20% mark-up on cost. **[1]**

**5.** Dunholme Signs absorbs overheads at 12.50 per direct labour hour, based on 9,600 budgeted hours. Actual overheads were 118,300 and were under-absorbed by 3,550.

**(a)** Calculate the actual direct labour hours worked. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain the under-absorption using the effects of activity and spending. **[3]**
**(c)** State how the under-absorption is treated in the statement of profit or loss. **[1]**

**6.** Saltash Ropes makes 12,000 coils of Mooring line and 3,000 coils of Climbing rope a year. Production overheads of 216,000 are absorbed at a single rate per direct labour hour.

| Per coil | Mooring | Climbing |
|---|---|---|
| Direct materials | 21.40 | 38.20 |
| Direct labour at 15 an hour | 0.4 hours | 0.8 hours |

The overheads have also been analysed by activity:

| Cost pool | Cost | Cost driver | Mooring | Climbing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Machine set-ups | 63,000 | Set-ups | 10 | 60 |
| Inspection | 45,000 | Inspections | 60 | 90 |
| Materials handling | 36,000 | Movements | 200 | 280 |
| Machine running | 72,000 | Machine hours | 6,000 | 3,000 |

**(a)** Using absorption costing, calculate the total cost of one coil of each product. **[3]**
**(b)** Repeat (a) using activity based costing. **[7]**
**(c)** Using a 40% mark-up on cost, find each product's price under absorption costing and under ABC. **[2]**
**(d)** Comment on Saltash's Climbing rope price if it continues to use absorption costing. **[2]**

**7.** Saltash had no opening inventory and closes with 600 coils of Mooring line and 250 of Climbing rope. Using question 6, calculate how much higher or lower profit is under ABC than under absorption costing. **[3]**

**8.** Kirkhope Planters makes one product. Variable production cost is 23 per unit and fixed production overheads are absorbed at 9 per unit, the same in both years. This year:

- opening inventory 800 units; production 9,000 units; sales 9,400 units at 48
- fixed production overheads 81,000, as budgeted (no under- or over-absorption)
- selling and administration expenses 62,000.

**(a)** Value the opening and closing inventory under marginal costing and under absorption costing. **[2]**
**(b)** Prepare a statement showing profit for the year using marginal costing. **[4]**
**(c)** Calculate profit for the year using absorption costing. **[3]**
**(d)** Reconcile the two profit figures. **[2]**

**9.** A product's ABC total cost is 64.80.

**(a)** Calculate the selling price that gives a 28% margin. **[1]**
**(b)** Express the profit per unit as a mark-up on cost, to one decimal place. **[1]**

**10.** Glaisdale Bakery sells 400,000 loaves and 9,000 celebration cakes a year, pricing at a 30% mark-up on absorption cost. An ABC study gives these unit costs:

| | Loaf | Celebration cake |
|---|---|---|
| Absorption cost | 1.10 | 14.20 |
| ABC cost | 0.95 | 19.80 |

Running ABC would cost about 24,000 a year. Evaluate whether Glaisdale should adopt activity based costing for setting prices. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Work out absorbed minus actual first; a positive figure is over-absorption, which adds to profit.

**2.** Apportionment shares an overhead between departments on a basis such as floor area [1]; attribution shares an ABC cost pool among products in line with how much of its driver each consumes [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Say what is shared and between what: departments for apportionment, products for attribution.

**3.** The factor whose quantity causes the cost of a pool to change [1]; for machine maintenance, the **number of maintenance visits** (or machine hours) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The suggested driver must cause the cost to change; floor area would not.

**4. (a)**

| | Shell | Fitting-out |
|---|---|---|
| Indirect wages (allocated) | 29,300 | 41,900 |
| Rent (1,000 : 1,400) | 24,000 | 33,600 |
| Depreciation (360,000 : 40,000) | 28,800 | 3,200 |
| Canteen (7 : 20) | 3,500 | 10,000 |
| **Total** | **85,600** | **88,700** |

Indirect wages allocated [1]; rent [1]; depreciation [1]; canteen [1]; totals 85,600 and 88,700, adding to 174,300 [1].
**(b)** Shell 85,600 ÷ 10,700 = **8.00 per machine hour** [1]; Fitting-out 88,700 ÷ 17,740 = **5.00 per direct labour hour** [1].
**(c)** Labour 8.5 × 17 = 144.50 [1]; overheads 4 × 8.00 + 7 × 5.00 = 32.00 + 35.00 = 67.00 [1]; total 214.00 + 144.50 + 67.00 = **425.50** [1].
**(d)** 425.50 × 1.2 = **510.60** [1] **[11]**
*Examiner insight:* Basing Shell's rate on its labour hours misstates every later figure; use the activity that drives each department's costs.

**5. (a)** Absorbed = 118,300 − 3,550 = 114,750 [1]; hours = 114,750 ÷ 12.50 [1] = **9,180 hours** [1].
**(b)** Budgeted overheads were 9,600 × 12.50 = 120,000 [1]. Hours were 420 below budget, so 420 × 12.50 = 5,250 less was absorbed [1]; spending was 1,700 below budget, which offsets part of it: 5,250 − 1,700 = 3,550 under-absorbed [1].
**(c)** It is **deducted from profit** (added to cost of sales) [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Under-absorbed means absorbed was below actual, so subtract; adding 3,550 gives a wrong 9,748 hours.

**6. (a)** Labour hours 12,000 × 0.4 + 3,000 × 0.8 = 7,200; OAR 216,000 ÷ 7,200 = 30 per hour [1]. Mooring 21.40 + 6.00 + 12.00 = **39.40** [1]; Climbing 38.20 + 12.00 + 24.00 = **74.20** [1].
**(b)** Driver rates: set-ups 63,000 ÷ 70 = 900 [1]; inspections 45,000 ÷ 150 = 300 [1]; movements 36,000 ÷ 480 = 75; machine hours 72,000 ÷ 9,000 = 8 [1].
Mooring 9,000 + 18,000 + 15,000 + 48,000 = 90,000 [1]; Climbing 54,000 + 27,000 + 21,000 + 24,000 = 126,000 [1].
Mooring 90,000 ÷ 12,000 = 7.50, total **34.90** [1]; Climbing 126,000 ÷ 3,000 = 42.00, total **92.20** [1].
**(c)** Absorption: Mooring **55.16**, Climbing **103.88** [1]. ABC: Mooring **48.86**, Climbing **129.08** [1].
**(d)** At 103.88 Climbing earns only 11.68 a coil over its ABC cost of 92.20, not the intended 36.88 [1]; it is subsidised by Mooring line, which is overpriced and could lose sales [1]. **[14]**
*Examiner insight:* Show every driver rate and attributed total so an examiner can follow your method if a final figure is wrong.

**7.** Overhead in inventory under absorption: 600 × 12.00 + 250 × 24.00 = 13,200 [1]; under ABC: 600 × 7.50 + 250 × 42.00 = 15,000 [1]; ABC profit is **1,800 higher** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Direct costs are identical under both methods, so only overhead in inventory affects the difference.

**8. (a)** Marginal: opening 800 × 23 = 18,400, closing 400 × 23 = 9,200; absorption: opening 800 × 32 = 25,600, closing 400 × 32 = 12,800 [1] [1].
**(b)**

| | |
|---|---|
| Revenue (9,400 × 48) | 451,200 |
| Opening inventory | 18,400 |
| Variable production cost (9,000 × 23) | 207,000 |
| Less closing inventory | (9,200) |
| Variable cost of sales | 216,200 |
| Contribution | 235,000 |
| Fixed production overheads | (81,000) |
| Selling and administration | (62,000) |
| **Profit** | **92,000** |

Revenue [1]; variable cost of sales 216,200 [1]; contribution 235,000 [1]; profit **92,000** [1].
**(c)** Cost of sales 25,600 + 288,000 − 12,800 = 300,800 [1]; gross profit 451,200 − 300,800 = 150,400 [1]; profit 150,400 − 62,000 = **88,400** [1].
**(d)** Inventory fell 400 units, so 400 × 9 = 3,600 of last year's fixed overhead enters this year's absorption cost of sales [1]; 92,000 − 3,600 = 88,400 [1]. **[11]**
*Examiner insight:* With falling inventory absorption profit is lower; adding the 3,600 contradicts your own statements.

**9. (a)** 64.80 ÷ 0.72 = **90.00** [1]
**(b)** 25.20 ÷ 64.80 × 100 = **38.9%** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A margin is a share of price; adding 28% of 64.80 gives a wrong 82.94.

**10.** Cake price 14.20 × 1.3 = 18.46 is below its ABC cost of 19.80, a loss of 1.34 a cake [1]; on 9,000 cakes about 12,060 a year [1]. Loaf price 1.43 against an ABC-based 1.24 (0.95 × 1.3, rounded): about 0.19 overpriced, so rivals may undercut [1]. ABC shows cakes use costly small-batch activities, so managers can reduce drivers or reprice [1]. Against: 24,000 a year exceeds the cake loss alone [1]; pools and drivers are judgements, so ABC costs are estimates too [1]; a higher cake price may lose customers, since demand matters as well as cost [1]; rent still needs arbitrary sharing [1]. Judgement: adopt ABC, as the cake loss plus about 76,000 of loaf revenue open to undercutting (0.19 × 400,000) outweighs 24,000 [1], but review drivers yearly and test cake demand before repricing [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong evaluations use Glaisdale's figures on both sides and end in a supported decision; generic ABC benefits add little.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using budgeted hours instead of actual hours to find overheads absorbed.
- Reversing the treatment of under- and over-absorption in the profit statement.
- Dividing a cost pool by units of output instead of total driver quantity.
- Confusing mark-up with margin in pricing questions.
- Charging fixed production overheads twice in an absorption statement.
- Forgetting opening inventory when reconciling absorption and marginal profit.
- Evaluations that never reach a decision or ignore the figures given.
