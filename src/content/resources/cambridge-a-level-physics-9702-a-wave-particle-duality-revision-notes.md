Subtopic 22.3 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers the evidence that electromagnetic radiation behaves both as waves and as particles, the electron diffraction evidence that particles behave as waves, and the de Broglie wavelength. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Wave-particle duality study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-wave-particle-duality/); to apply the ideas, try the [Wave-particle duality practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-wave-particle-duality-practice/).

## The four syllabus outcomes

1. Photoelectric effect → evidence for the **particulate** nature of electromagnetic radiation; interference and diffraction → evidence for its **wave** nature.
2. Describe and interpret **qualitatively** the evidence from **electron diffraction** for the wave nature of particles.
3. The **de Broglie wavelength** is the wavelength associated with a moving particle.
4. Recall and use **λ = h/p**.

## Evidence sorted by model

| Evidence | Supports | Key reason in one line |
|---|---|---|
| Double-slit interference | Waves | Dark fringes need cancellation by superposition |
| Diffraction by a gap or grating | Waves | Spreading depends on λ compared with gap |
| Threshold frequency in photoelectric effect | Particles | One photon, one electron: hf must reach the work function |
| E_k(max) independent of intensity | Particles | Intensity changes photon rate, not photon energy |
| Electron diffraction rings | Waves (for particles) | Only waves diffract |

Memory hook: radiation **travels** as a wave and **interacts** as a particle.

## The de Broglie equation

```
λ = h / p        p = mv  (for v much less than c)
h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s
```

For a particle of charge q accelerated from rest through p.d. V:

```
E_k = qV        p = √(2mE_k)        λ = h / p
```

Proportionalities to remember:

- λ ∝ 1/p
- at fixed mass, λ ∝ 1/v
- for accelerated electrons, λ ∝ 1/√V: four times the p.d. halves the wavelength
- at the same speed, heavier particle → shorter λ (proton about 1800 times shorter than electron)
- at the same kinetic energy, λ ∝ 1/√m

## Electron diffraction tube

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| Heated filament | Releases electrons |
| Anode at high p.d. (a few kV) | Accelerates electrons to known energy eV |
| Thin polycrystalline graphite | Atom spacing (order 10⁻¹⁰ m) comparable with λ, so diffraction is clear |
| Fluorescent screen | Shows where electrons land |
| Vacuum | Stops electrons colliding with gas molecules |

**Observation:** bright central spot with concentric rings.

**Interpretation checklist:**

- rings = diffraction pattern, so electrons show **wave** behaviour
- λ must be **comparable** with the atomic spacing
- **rings** (not spots) because the graphite crystals have random orientations
- higher p.d. → more momentum → shorter λ → smaller angles → **smaller rings**
- a magnet **shifts** the pattern → it is made by charged particles, not by X-rays or light

## Quick calculations

- 4.0 kV electrons: E_k = 6.40 × 10⁻¹⁶ J; p = 3.41 × 10⁻²³ kg m s⁻¹; λ = 1.94 × 10⁻¹¹ m.
- 1.0 kV electrons: λ = 3.88 × 10⁻¹¹ m (twice the 4.0 kV value, as λ ∝ 1/√V).
- Electron at 2.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹: p = 1.82 × 10⁻²⁴ kg m s⁻¹; λ = 3.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ m.
- 0.16 kg ball at 30 m s⁻¹: λ ≈ 1.4 × 10⁻³⁴ m, far too small to diffract.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Wave-particle duality | Radiation and matter show both wave and particle properties, depending on the experiment |
| Photon | A quantum of electromagnetic energy, energy hf |
| de Broglie wavelength | The wavelength associated with a moving particle, λ = h/p |
| Momentum | p = mv for a particle; for a photon p = E/c |
| Diffraction | Spreading of a wave through a gap or around an obstacle; noticeable when λ is comparable with the gap |
| Polycrystalline | Made of many tiny crystals at random orientations |

## Exam traps

- Citing the photoelectric effect as wave evidence, or diffraction as particle evidence.
- Saying the rings get **bigger** when the p.d. increases.
- Treating λ as inversely proportional to V instead of √V.
- Forgetting to convert kV to V, or to multiply V by e to get joules.
- Using p = E/c for an electron. That is for photons; for an electron use p = √(2mE_k).
- Stating that electron diffraction shows electrons "are" waves; they show a wave nature while still being detected as particles.
- Describing the experiment without saying what the rings **show**.

## Self-test

1. Name one phenomenon that shows the wave nature of light and one that shows its particle nature.
2. Explain why the photoelectric effect cannot be explained by a wave model.
3. State what is meant by the de Broglie wavelength.
4. Write λ = h/p in terms of mass and speed.
5. Why is polycrystalline graphite used in an electron diffraction tube?
6. Why does the pattern consist of rings rather than spots?
7. The accelerating p.d. is increased. What happens to the rings, and why?
8. How could you show the pattern is produced by electrons and not X-rays?
9. Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of an electron moving at 2.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹.
10. By what factor must the accelerating p.d. change to halve the electron wavelength?
11. A proton and an electron move at the same speed. Which has the longer wavelength?
12. Why can the wave nature of a moving car never be observed?

**Answers:** 1. Wave: interference or diffraction; particle: the photoelectric effect. 2. A wave model predicts emission at any frequency if the radiation is intense enough or shines for long enough, but no electrons are emitted below the threshold frequency however intense the light. 3. The wavelength associated with a moving particle, λ = h/p. 4. λ = h/(mv). 5. Its atomic spacing is comparable with the electron wavelength, and a thin layer lets electrons pass through. 6. The tiny crystals have random orientations, so diffracted beams spread around the axis into circles. 7. They get smaller: more momentum, shorter wavelength, smaller diffraction angles. 8. Bring a magnet close; the pattern moves, so it is made by charged particles. 9. λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ / (9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 2.0 × 10⁶) = 3.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ m. 10. Increase it by a factor of 4. 11. The electron, because its smaller mass gives smaller momentum. 12. Its momentum is so large that its wavelength is far smaller than any gap or obstacle.
