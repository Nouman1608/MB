## Questions

**1.** A porter carries a 12 kg bag at constant speed for 20 m along a level corridor.
**(a)** State the work done by the porter's upward force on the bag over the 20 m, and explain your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** The porter then lifts the bag at constant speed from the floor onto a shelf 0.90 m high. Calculate the work done against gravity. **[2]**

**2.** A boy pulls a loaded trolley 15 m along level ground with a rope. The tension is 40 N and the rope makes 28° with the ground.
**(a)** Calculate the work done by the tension. **[2]**
**(b)** The pull takes 20 s. Calculate the average power developed by the tension. **[2]**

**3.** A winch takes in energy at a rate of 2.4 kW and does useful work at a rate of 1.5 kW.
**(a)** Calculate its efficiency as a percentage. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy dissipated in one minute. **[2]**

**4.** A 1.5 kg trolley starts from rest on a smooth horizontal track. A horizontal force acts along the track. It rises uniformly from 0 to 18 N over the first 0.40 m, stays at 18 N from 0.40 m to 0.80 m, then falls uniformly to 6.0 N at 1.00 m.
**(a)** Calculate the work done on the trolley over the 1.00 m. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed of the trolley at 1.00 m. **[2]**

**5.** A car has a maximum power output of 55 kW. The resistive force is a constant 1400 N. Its mass is 1100 kg.
**(a)** Calculate its maximum speed on a level road. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate its acceleration at 20 m s⁻¹ with full power. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the acceleration falls to zero as the car approaches its maximum speed. **[1]**

**6.** A pendulum bob hangs from a light string of length 0.80 m. It is pulled aside until the string makes 60° with the vertical and released from rest. Ignore air resistance.
**(a)** Calculate the speed of the bob at the lowest point. **[3]**
**(b)** State the effect on this speed of doubling the mass of the bob. **[1]**

**7.** A ball is dropped onto a hard floor and rebounds to a lower height than it was released from. Describe the energy transfers until it reaches the top of the rebound, and explain why the rebound is lower. **[4]**

**8.** A catapult band behaves like a spring with spring constant 300 N m⁻¹. It is stretched 0.20 m and used to fire a 0.025 kg stone horizontally.
**(a)** Calculate the elastic energy stored. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the stone's speed if all the energy becomes its kinetic energy. **[2]**
**(c)** In practice 15% of the stored energy is dissipated. Calculate the actual launch speed. **[2]**

**9.** A skater of mass 62 kg passes the top of a slope at 5.0 m s⁻¹. The top is 3.0 m above the bottom, and the slope is 14 m long. She reaches the bottom at 8.0 m s⁻¹.
**(a)** Calculate the work done against resistive forces. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean resistive force along the slope. **[1]**

**10.** A motorised conveyor raises 4.0 kg of gravel every second through a vertical height of 5.5 m. Its electrical power input is 350 W. The gravel gains no kinetic energy.
**(a)** Calculate the useful power output. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the efficiency of the conveyor. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest one reason why the efficiency is less than 100%. **[1]**

**11.** A lift cabin and passengers have a total mass of 900 kg. They are raised at a steady 2.0 m s⁻¹, with friction in the guide rails giving a constant resistive force of 1500 N.
**(a)** Calculate the force the cable must exert. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the useful power delivered to the cabin. **[2]**
**(c)** The motor is 70% efficient. Calculate its input power. **[2]**

**12.** A cyclist and bicycle have a combined mass of 85 kg. They freewheel 120 m down a straight road inclined at 4.0° to the horizontal, without pedalling. Their speed rises from 3.0 m s⁻¹ to 11 m s⁻¹.
**(a)** Calculate the loss in gravitational potential energy. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the gain in kinetic energy. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the work done against resistive forces and hence the mean resistive force. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the resistive force at the bottom is likely to be greater than this mean value. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The work done is **zero** [1] because the upward force is at 90° to the horizontal displacement, so cos 90° = 0 [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** W = mgΔh = 12 × 9.81 × 0.90 [1] = **106 J** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain using the angle between force and displacement. "Nothing moves" is wrong here, because the bag does move.

**2. (a)** W = Fs cos θ = 40 × 15 × cos 28° [1] = **5.3 × 10² J** (530 J) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** P = W ÷ t = 530 ÷ 20 [1] = **26.5 W** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Using F × s without cos θ gives 600 J, which is a different quantity. Carry the unrounded work into part (b).

**3. (a)** Efficiency = 1.5 ÷ 2.4 = 0.625, so **62.5%** (or 63%) [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Dissipated power = 2.4 − 1.5 = 0.9 kW [1]. Energy = 900 × 60 = **5.4 × 10⁴ J** (54 kJ) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert kW to W before multiplying by the time in seconds.

**4. (a)** Triangle: ½ × 0.40 × 18 = 3.6 J [1]. Rectangle: 0.40 × 18 = 7.2 J [1]. Trapezium: ½ × (18 + 6.0) × 0.20 = 2.4 J. Total = **13.2 J** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Smooth track, so the work becomes kinetic energy: ½ × 1.5 × v² = 13.2 [1]. v = √17.6 = **4.2 m s⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each shape's area separately, so a slip in one can be spotted. The speed comes from equating the work to kinetic energy.

**5. (a)** At maximum speed driving force equals resistive force, 1400 N [1]. v = P ÷ F = 55 000 ÷ 1400 = **39 m s⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Driving force = 55 000 ÷ 20 = 2750 N [1]. Resultant = 2750 − 1400 = 1350 N [1]. a = 1350 ÷ 1100 = **1.2 m s⁻²** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** As speed rises at fixed power the driving force P ÷ v falls until it equals the resistive force, so the resultant force is zero [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* At 20 m s⁻¹ the driving force is P ÷ v, not the 1400 N resistive force. Quote F = ma only for the resultant.

**6. (a)** Height: Δh = 0.80 (1 − cos 60°) = 0.40 m [1]. mgΔh = ½mv² [1]. v = √(2 × 9.81 × 0.40) = **2.8 m s⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** **No effect** (the mass cancels) [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Measure the height vertically from the lowest point, not along the arc. A small triangle diagram shows where 0.40 m comes from.

**7.** Gravitational potential energy transfers to kinetic energy as the ball falls [1]. On impact, kinetic energy goes to elastic energy as the ball and floor deform, then back to kinetic energy as it rebounds [1]. Some energy is dissipated as thermal energy and sound in the ball and floor [1]. Air resistance also does work, so less energy remains and the rebound height is lower [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name each store and give the transfers in order. "Energy is lost" does not say where it has gone.

**8. (a)** Energy = ½kΔL² = ½ × 300 × 0.20² [1] = **6.0 J** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** ½mv² = 6.0 so v = √(2 × 6.0 ÷ 0.025) [1] = **22 m s⁻¹** (21.9) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Kinetic energy = 0.85 × 6.0 = 5.1 J [1]. v = √(2 × 5.1 ÷ 0.025) = **20 m s⁻¹** (20.2) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Take the 15% off the energy, not off the speed.

**9. (a)** Initial kinetic energy = ½ × 62 × 5.0² = 775 J [1]. Potential energy lost = 62 × 9.81 × 3.0 = 1825 J [1]. Final kinetic energy = ½ × 62 × 8.0² = 1984 J [1]. Work against resistance = 775 + 1825 − 1984 = **616 J** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Force = 616 ÷ 14 = **44 N** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Use 3.0 m for the potential energy and 14 m for the work against the resistive force.

**10. (a)** Useful power = mgΔh per second = 4.0 × 9.81 × 5.5 [1] = **216 W** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Efficiency = 215.8 ÷ 350 = 0.617, so **62%** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** For example, friction in the roller bearings, or thermal energy in the motor windings [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* State that the useful output is the potential energy gained per second, since the gravel gains no kinetic energy.

**11. (a)** Constant speed, so the cable force equals weight plus resistance: 900 × 9.81 + 1500 [1] = 8829 + 1500 = **1.03 × 10⁴ N** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** P = Fv = 10 329 × 2.0 [1] = **2.1 × 10⁴ W** (20.7 kW) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Input = 20 658 ÷ 0.70 [1] = **3.0 × 10⁴ W** (29.5 kW) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the forces balance at constant speed. To find input from output, divide by the efficiency.

**12. (a)** Δh = 120 sin 4.0° = 8.37 m [1]. ΔEp = 85 × 9.81 × 8.37 = **6.98 × 10³ J** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** ΔEk = ½ × 85 × (11² − 3.0²) [1] = ½ × 85 × 112 = **4.76 × 10³ J** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Work against resistive forces = 6980 − 4760 = **2.2 × 10³ J** [1]. Mean force = 2220 ÷ 120 = **18.5 N** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Air resistance increases with speed, so it is larger at 11 m s⁻¹ than the average over the descent [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The kinetic energy change uses the difference of the squares of the speeds. Write the energy balance in words first.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out cos θ when the force is not along the displacement, or using sin where cos belongs.
- Using slope length in mgΔh, or vertical height in work against friction.
- Using peak force times total distance instead of the area under the graph.
- Using the resistive force in P = Fv below maximum speed.
- Subtracting speeds before squaring in the change in kinetic energy.
- Writing "energy is lost" instead of naming where it goes.
- Mixing up input and output when using efficiency, which gives a value above 100%.
- Missing units, or mixing kJ and J without converting.
