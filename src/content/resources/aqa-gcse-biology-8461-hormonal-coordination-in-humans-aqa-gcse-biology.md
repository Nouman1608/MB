## 4.5.3.1 The endocrine system

The endocrine system is made of **glands** that secrete **hormones directly into the bloodstream**. The blood
carries each hormone to a **target organ**, where it produces an effect. Compared with the nervous system, the
effects are **slower but act for longer**.

The **pituitary gland** in the brain is a **"master gland"**: it secretes several hormones in response to body
conditions, and these act on other glands, stimulating them to release their own hormones.

| Gland | Position | Hormone(s) in this specification |
|---|---|---|
| Pituitary | Base of the brain | FSH, LH, ADH (and others that act on other glands) |
| Thyroid | Neck | Thyroxine (Higher tier only) |
| Adrenal glands | On top of the kidneys | Adrenaline (Higher tier only) |
| Pancreas | Abdomen, below the stomach | Insulin; glucagon (Higher tier only) |
| Ovaries | Lower abdomen (female) | Oestrogen, progesterone |
| Testes | Scrotum (male) | Testosterone |

## 4.5.3.2 Blood glucose concentration

The **pancreas** monitors and controls blood glucose.

- Too high → the pancreas releases **insulin** → glucose moves **from the blood into cells** → in **liver and
  muscle cells**, excess glucose is converted to **glycogen** for storage.
- **Higher tier only:** too low → the pancreas releases **glucagon** → **glycogen is converted into glucose** and
  released into the blood. Insulin and glucagon act in a **negative feedback cycle**: each hormone's effect
  removes the stimulus for its own release.

| | Type 1 diabetes | Type 2 diabetes |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Pancreas fails to produce sufficient insulin | Body cells no longer respond to insulin |
| Effect | Uncontrolled high blood glucose | High blood glucose |
| Treatment | Insulin injections | Carbohydrate-controlled diet and an exercise regime |
| Risk factor named | | Obesity |

**Worked example 1: interpreting a glucose tolerance graph** (invented data). After fasting, two people drink
the same glucose solution.

| Time (minutes) | 0 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 120 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Person P (mmol/dm³) | 4.8 | 7.6 | 6.2 | 5.1 | 4.8 |
| Person Q (mmol/dm³) | 6.0 | 11.0 | 13.5 | 13.0 | 12.4 |

Person P's concentration peaks at 30 minutes and is back to 4.8 by 120 minutes, showing insulin is working.
Person Q starts higher, keeps rising to 13.5 and is still 12.4 at 120 minutes: glucose is not being removed from
the blood, consistent with diabetes. The data alone cannot show which type; you would need to know whether insulin
is being produced.

## 4.5.3.3 Water and nitrogen balance (Biology only)

**Uncontrolled losses:** water leaves via the **lungs** when we breathe out; water, ions and urea are lost from the
**skin** in sweat. There is no control over these.

**Controlled loss:** excess water, ions and urea are removed by the **kidneys** in urine. If body cells lose or gain
too much water by osmosis, they do not function efficiently.

**Higher tier only:** excess amino acids from digested protein are **deaminated** in the **liver** to form
**ammonia**, which is toxic and is immediately converted to **urea** for safe excretion.

The kidneys make urine by **filtration** of the blood and **selective reabsorption** of useful substances such as
**glucose, some ions and water**.

**Worked example 2: translating kidney data** (invented data, per day)

| Substance | Filtered | In urine | Reabsorbed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glucose (g) | 160 | 0 | 160 (all) |
| Urea (g) | 50 | 30 | 20 |
| Water (dm³) | 170 | 1.7 | 168.3 (99%) |

All glucose is reabsorbed, so none appears in healthy urine. Urea is mostly not reabsorbed, so it is excreted.
Percentage of water reabsorbed = 168.3 ÷ 170 × 100 = 99%.

**Higher tier only: ADH.** When the blood is too concentrated, the **pituitary gland** releases **ADH**, which makes
the kidney tubules **more permeable**, so **more water is reabsorbed** into the blood and a smaller volume of more
concentrated urine is produced. This is controlled by **negative feedback**.

**Kidney failure** is treated by **dialysis** or a **transplant**. In dialysis, blood passes over a partially
permeable membrane; the dialysis fluid contains the same concentration of useful substances as normal blood, so
glucose and useful ions are not lost, while urea and excess ions diffuse out of the blood. A transplant frees the
patient from regular dialysis, but donors are in short supply and the new kidney may be rejected, so drugs that
suppress the immune system are needed.

## 4.5.3.4 Hormones in reproduction

At **puberty**, reproductive hormones cause **secondary sex characteristics** to develop. **Oestrogen**, from the
**ovaries**, is the main female reproductive hormone; eggs begin to mature and one is released about every 28 days
(**ovulation**). **Testosterone**, from the **testes**, is the main male reproductive hormone and stimulates sperm
production.

| Hormone | Made in | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FSH | Pituitary | Causes an egg to mature in the ovary |
| LH | Pituitary | Stimulates release of the egg |
| Oestrogen | Ovary | Involved in maintaining the uterus lining |
| Progesterone | Ovary | Involved in maintaining the uterus lining |

**Higher tier only: the interactions.**

1. FSH from the pituitary causes an egg to mature and stimulates the ovaries to produce oestrogen.
2. Rising oestrogen builds the uterus lining, **inhibits FSH** and **stimulates LH** release.
3. A surge of LH triggers ovulation.
4. Progesterone maintains the lining and **inhibits FSH and LH**.
5. If no pregnancy occurs, progesterone falls, the lining breaks down (menstruation), and FSH is no longer
   inhibited, so the cycle restarts.

## 4.5.3.5 Contraception

| Method | Hormonal? | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Oral contraceptive | Yes | Hormones inhibit FSH, so no eggs mature |
| Injection, implant or skin patch | Yes | Slow-release progesterone inhibits maturation and release of eggs for months or years |
| Condom, diaphragm | No | Barrier: stops sperm reaching an egg |
| Intrauterine device | Either | Prevents implantation of an embryo, or releases a hormone |
| Spermicidal agents | No | Kill or disable sperm |
| Abstaining when an egg may be in the oviduct | No | Avoids sperm meeting an egg |
| Male or female sterilisation | No | Surgical |

When evaluating a method, consider how reliable it is, side effects, whether it protects against sexually
transmitted infections (only barrier methods such as condoms do), and personal or religious views. The
specification notes that issues around contraception cannot be answered by science alone.

## 4.5.3.6 Treating infertility (Higher tier only)

- A **fertility drug** containing **FSH and LH** may allow a woman to become pregnant in the normal way.
- **IVF**: the mother is given FSH and LH to mature several eggs; the eggs are collected and fertilised by the
  father's sperm in the laboratory; the fertilised eggs develop into embryos; when they are tiny balls of cells,
  one or two are inserted into the mother's uterus.

IVF gives a woman the chance of a baby of her own, but it is very emotionally and physically stressful, success
rates are not high, and it can lead to multiple births, which are a risk to both babies and mother.

## 4.5.3.7 Feedback systems (Higher tier only)

- **Adrenaline**, from the **adrenal glands** in times of fear or stress, increases heart rate and boosts the
  delivery of oxygen and glucose to the brain and muscles, preparing the body for "flight or fight".
- **Thyroxine**, from the **thyroid gland**, stimulates the **basal metabolic rate** and plays an important role in
  growth and development. Its level is controlled by **negative feedback**: when it is high, its release is
  reduced; when it is low, release increases.

## Common mistakes

- Writing that insulin "turns glucose into glycogen" in the blood. Insulin causes glucose to move into cells,
  where liver and muscle cells convert it.
- Confusing glucagon (hormone) and glycogen (storage carbohydrate).
- Saying Type 2 diabetes means no insulin is made.
- Stating that ADH makes more urine. It makes **less**, more concentrated urine.
- Mixing up FSH (maturation) and LH (release).
- Claiming all contraceptives protect against infection.

## Exam technique for AQA command words

- **Compare** Type 1 and Type 2: paired points on cause and treatment.
- **Explain** a graph: link the trend to what the hormone is doing.
- **Evaluate** a treatment (dialysis, transplant, contraception, IVF): advantages, disadvantages and a judgement.
- **Explain** feedback (Higher tier only): change → hormone released → effect → change reversed → release falls.
