These revision notes are for **higher level (HL) only**. The emergence of Central Asian republics (1917 to 2020) is a regional study examined on Paper 3, which standard level students do not sit. They condense the IB *History guide*, first assessment 2028. Every question on this study requires you to refer to **one** Central Asian republic; these notes use Kazakhstan with comparisons. Read the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-central-asian-republics/) first, then try the [practice set](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-central-asian-republics-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ASSR | Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic, a unit within the USSR |
| Alash Orda | Kazakh nationalist government of 1917 to 1920 |
| Jadids | Muslim reformers who wanted modern education and national revival |
| Basmachi | Armed resistance to Soviet rule in the 1920s |
| Korenizatsiia | "Indigenisation": promoting local people in government and culture |
| National delimitation | Soviet redrawing of Central Asian borders in 1924 to 1925 |
| Hujum | The 1927 campaign for women to unveil |
| Collectivisation | Forcing farmers and herders into collective farms |
| Virgin Lands | Khrushchev's campaign to plough grassland from 1954 |
| Glasnost, perestroika | Openness and restructuring under Gorbachev |
| Multi-vector policy | Balancing relations with several great powers |

## Civil war period: who resisted and why

| Group | Aim | Fate |
|---|---|---|
| Alash Orda | Kazakh autonomy | Dissolved by the Bolsheviks; leaders later purged |
| Jadids | Modern Muslim education and reform | Some joined the Bolsheviks; most purged in the 1930s |
| Basmachi | Armed opposition to Soviet rule and requisitioning | Defeated by the late 1920s |
| Bolsheviks | Control and socialism | Used force, then concessions (NEP, korenizatsiia) |

## Soviet policies in the 1920s and 1930s

- **Political:** one-party control; 1936 constitution; Latin script then Cyrillic; Russification.
- **Repression:** Great Terror, purge of local elites and intellectuals.
- **Economic:** forced settlement of nomads; collectivisation; cotton monoculture; the Turksib railway.
- **Social:** hujum for women; atheism campaigns; rising literacy.
- **Identity:** national delimitation created republics; "national in form, socialist in content".

## Second World War: effects

| Area | Effect |
|---|---|
| Military | Central Asians in the Red Army; Panfilov division at Moscow |
| Political | An official Muslim board set up in 1943 |
| Economic | Evacuated industry; mining and metals; heavy farming quotas |
| Social | Evacuees and deported peoples; mixed ethnic populations |

## Post-war changes

- **Khrushchev:** Virgin Lands; many Slavic settlers; Kazakhs about 30 per cent in 1959; short-term gains, long-term soil damage.
- **Brezhnev:** stable local leaders (Kunaev, Rashidov); corruption; cotton affair.
- **Environment:** Aral Sea shrinking; Semipalatinsk nuclear tests; Baikonur.

## Reasons for independence

1. Glasnost opened debate; Jeltoqsan (1986) and the anti-nuclear movement.
2. Ethnic violence (Ferghana 1989).
3. Economic decline of the USSR.
4. August 1991 coup in Moscow weakened the centre.
5. Leaders (Nazarbayev, Karimov and others) took independence once the union was collapsing.

## Nation-building: strengths and weaknesses (Kazakhstan)

| Successes | Challenges |
|---|---|
| Oil and gas growth; foreign investment | Dependence on energy; inequality |
| New capital, Astana | Corruption |
| Stability and ethnic peace | Limited political opposition |
| Multi-vector foreign policy | Pressure from Russia and China |
| Gave up nuclear weapons | Language and identity debates |

## Perspectives

- Famine: policy of collectivisation; genocide debate.
- Soviet rule: colonial or modernising.
- Independence: popular nationalism or elite decision.
- Post-Soviet states: authoritarian stability or missed democracy.

## Worked example: a short plan

**Question:** "Evaluate the impact of Soviet policies on society and identity in one Central Asian republic, 1920 to 1940."

1. Thesis: Soviet policy transformed society and identity, but with great cost and with local adaptation.
2. Creation of national republics and borders.
3. Collectivisation and famine.
4. Hujum and religion.
5. Language, script and culture.
6. Repression of elites and a judgement.

## Common mistakes

- Using the Russian Empire's conquest as the main topic of a Soviet question.
- Confusing the Virgin Lands Campaign with collectivisation.
- Forgetting that the Soviet system changed after Stalin.
- Writing generally about "Central Asia" in an essay that must name one republic.

## Self-test

1. What was the Alash Orda?
2. What was the National Delimitation?
3. What did collectivisation do to Kazakh herds?
4. What was the Virgin Lands Campaign?
5. Why did the Aral Sea shrink?
6. What happened in Almaty in December 1986?
7. Which republic declared independence last?
8. Name two features of Kazakhstan's foreign policy after 1991.

**Answers:** 1. The Kazakh nationalist government of 1917 to 1920. 2. The 1924 to 1925 redrawing of Central Asian borders into national republics. 3. Herds were seized and slaughtered, causing mass starvation. 4. Khrushchev's plan from 1954 to plough grassland in northern Kazakhstan for grain. 5. Rivers were diverted to irrigate cotton. 6. Protests against the replacement of Kunaev by Kolbin; crushed by force. 7. Kazakhstan (16 December 1991). 8. A multi-vector policy balancing Russia, China and the USA; renouncing the Soviet nuclear weapons.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which republic should I choose?**
The one your teacher has taught, or the one for which you can find enough detail. Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan have the most accessible material.

**How are the 1920 to 1940 and 1991 to 2020 sections linked?**
Many post-Soviet issues, such as language, borders and ethnic balance, come from Soviet policies. Linking them earns marks for analysis.
