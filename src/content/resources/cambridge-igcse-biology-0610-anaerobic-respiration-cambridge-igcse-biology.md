This guide covers sub-topic **12.3 Anaerobic respiration** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 4 are Core: the definition, the energy comparison and the two word equations. Outcomes 5 to 7 are **Extended only**: the balanced yeast equation, lactic acid and the oxygen debt, and how the oxygen debt is removed.

## 12.3.1 and 12.3.2 Definition and energy

Anaerobic respiration is the **chemical reactions in cells** that **break down nutrient molecules** to **release energy** **without using oxygen**. Compare it with the aerobic definition: the only change is "without using oxygen" in place of "use oxygen".

It releases **much less energy per glucose molecule** than aerobic respiration. The syllabus asks you to state this; it does not ask for numbers, so do not invent any. In an answer, "much less energy per glucose molecule" is the phrase to use. "No energy" and "less energy" (without "much" and "per glucose molecule") are both weaker.

## 12.3.3 and 12.3.4 Two organisms, two equations

| | Yeast | Human muscle (vigorous exercise) |
|---|---|---|
| Word equation | glucose → alcohol + carbon dioxide | glucose → lactic acid |
| Oxygen used | no | no |
| Carbon dioxide made | **yes** | **no** |
| Product that matters in everyday life | alcohol (ethanol) and carbon dioxide in brewing and bread-making | lactic acid, which builds up in muscles |

The commonest error in this sub-topic is mixing the two equations. Use this check: **yeast gives off gas; muscles do not.**

### When does each happen?

- **Yeast** respires anaerobically when oxygen is absent, for example in a sealed container of sugar solution.
- **Muscles** respire anaerobically during **vigorous exercise**, when the heart and lungs cannot deliver oxygen fast enough for aerobic respiration alone to release all the energy needed. Aerobic respiration continues; anaerobic respiration is added on top.

### Worked example 1: setting up anaerobic conditions for yeast

A student wants yeast to respire anaerobically. She boils a glucose solution and cools it, adds yeast, and pours a thin layer of oil on top.

- **Boiling** removes dissolved oxygen from the solution.
- **Cooling** before adding the yeast stops the high temperature denaturing the yeast's enzymes.
- The **oil layer** stops oxygen from the air dissolving back into the solution.
- Gas bubbling out through a delivery tube into limewater turns the limewater cloudy (milky), showing **carbon dioxide** is released.

## 12.3.5 The balanced yeast equation (Extended only)

```
C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂
```

C₂H₅OH is ethanol (the alcohol). The ratio is **1 glucose : 2 ethanol : 2 carbon dioxide**. There is no oxygen on the left, and only two products, not six of each as in the aerobic equation.

### Worked example 2: yeast ratio calculations

**(a)** How many carbon dioxide molecules are released when 15 glucose molecules are respired anaerobically by yeast?

```
1 glucose → 2 CO₂
15 glucose → 15 × 2 = 30 CO₂
```

**(b)** A yeast culture releases 64 molecules of ethanol. How many glucose molecules were used?

```
2 ethanol per glucose → 64 ÷ 2 = 32 glucose
```

**(c)** Compare: 15 glucose molecules respired **aerobically** would release 15 × 6 = 90 carbon dioxide molecules. Aerobic respiration releases three times as many carbon dioxide molecules per glucose molecule as anaerobic respiration in yeast.

## 12.3.6 Lactic acid and the oxygen debt (Extended only)

During vigorous exercise, **lactic acid builds up in the muscles and in the blood**. This build-up causes an **oxygen debt**: extra oxygen is needed after exercise to deal with the lactic acid.

## 12.3.7 Removing the oxygen debt (Extended only)

The syllabus limits you to exactly three points. Learn them as a chain:

1. **Heart rate stays fast** after exercise, to **transport lactic acid in the blood from the muscles to the liver**.
2. **Breathing stays deeper and faster**, to **supply oxygen for the aerobic respiration of lactic acid**.
3. **Lactic acid is respired aerobically in the liver.**

Each point has a "what" and a "why". The "why" earns the mark.

### Worked example 3: interpreting lactic acid data

The concentration of lactic acid in an athlete's blood was measured during and after a 400 m race. (Example data written for this guide.)

| Time | Blood lactic acid / arbitrary units |
|---|---|
| Before race | 1 |
| End of race | 12 |
| 10 minutes after | 7 |
| 30 minutes after | 3 |
| 60 minutes after | 1 |

- **Describe:** lactic acid rose from 1 to 12 during the race, then fell after the race, returning to 1 by 60 minutes.
- **Explain the rise:** during the sprint, oxygen could not be supplied fast enough, so muscles also respired anaerobically, producing lactic acid that entered the blood.
- **Explain the fall:** the fast heart rate carried lactic acid to the liver, and deeper, faster breathing supplied oxygen for its aerobic respiration in the liver, so the oxygen debt was repaid.

### Model answer: oxygen debt

**Question:** Outline how the oxygen debt is removed after exercise. [3]

**Answer:** The heart rate stays high to carry lactic acid in the blood from the muscles to the liver (1). Breathing stays deeper and faster to supply oxygen (1). The lactic acid is broken down by aerobic respiration in the liver (1).

## Common mistakes

- Writing carbon dioxide as a product of anaerobic respiration in **muscles**.
- Writing lactic acid as a product of **yeast**.
- Saying anaerobic respiration releases **no** energy.
- Stating that muscles switch off aerobic respiration during vigorous exercise.
- (Extended) Putting oxygen on the left of the balanced yeast equation, or writing 6CO₂.
- (Extended) Saying lactic acid is removed "in the muscles" or "by breathing it out".
- (Extended) Describing the oxygen debt as "being out of breath" with no mention of lactic acid.

## Exam technique for 12.3 command words

- **Describe**: give the definition with "without using oxygen".
- **State** an equation: choose the organism first, then write that equation exactly.
- **State** the energy comparison: "much less energy per glucose molecule than aerobic respiration".
- **Outline** (Extended): the three oxygen-debt points, each with its purpose; no extra detail needed.
- **Explain** data: link rises to anaerobic respiration and falls to the removal of lactic acid.
