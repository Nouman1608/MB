## Section A

**1.** State the unit of pressure and write it in terms of newtons and metres. **[2]**

**2.** Convert each temperature.

**(a)** 18 °C into kelvin **[1]**
**(b)** 400 K into degrees Celsius **[1]**

**3.** Convert a density of 1.2 g/cm³ into kg/m³. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why a temperature rise can be given in either °C or K without changing the number. **[1]**

**5.** Convert each value into the unit shown.

**(a)** 80 cm² into m² **[1]**
**(b)** 500 cm³ into m³ **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A student finds that a stone has a mass of 156 g and a volume, by displacement of water, of 60 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the density of the stone in kg/m³. **[3]**
**(b)** Another student gives the answer as 2.6 kg/m³. Explain the mistake. **[1]**

**7.** A box weighing 480 N rests on a floor. Its base measures 40 cm by 30 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the pressure the box exerts on the floor, in pascals. **[3]**
**(b)** Give your answer in kPa. **[1]**

**8.** A swimming pool is 180 cm deep. The density of water is 1000 kg/m³.

**(a)** Calculate the pressure difference between the surface and the bottom of the pool. **[3]**
**(b)** Atmospheric pressure is 100 kPa. Calculate the total pressure at the bottom of the pool, in kPa. **[1]**

**9.** This question is about specific heat capacity (Physics only).

**(a)** A 750 g copper block is heated from 22 °C to 62 °C. The specific heat capacity of copper is 385 J/kg °C. Calculate the energy transferred to the block. **[3]**
**(b)** A 2.0 kg block of another metal needs 36 000 J to warm by 40 °C. Calculate its specific heat capacity, giving the unit. **[2]**

**10.** A gas in a syringe has a pressure of 120 kPa and a volume of 50 cm³. It expands at constant temperature to 80 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate its new pressure. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the volumes did not need to be converted into m³. **[1]**

**11.** A sealed can of gas at 100 kPa and 27 °C is heated to 177 °C. Its volume does not change.

**(a)** Calculate the new pressure. **[3]**
**(b)** A student calculates 100 × 177 ÷ 27 = 656 kPa. Explain why this is wrong. **[1]**

**12.** A force of 1.5 N pushes a needle into fabric. The area of the needle's tip is 0.010 mm². Calculate the pressure under the tip. Give your answer in standard form. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The pascal (Pa) [1]; 1 Pa = 1 N/m² (newton per square metre) [1].

**2. (a)** 18 + 273 = 291 K [1].
**(b)** 400 − 273 = 127 °C [1].

**3.** 1.2 × 1000 = 1200 kg/m³ [1].

**4.** One kelvin is the same size as one degree Celsius; the scales differ only in where zero is [1].

**5. (a)** 80 ÷ 10 000 = 0.0080 m² [1].
**(b)** 500 ÷ 1 000 000 = 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³ [1].

**6. (a)** m = 0.156 kg and V = 60 × 10⁻⁶ = 6.0 × 10⁻⁵ m³ [1]. ρ = m ÷ V = 0.156 ÷ 6.0 × 10⁻⁵ [1] = 2600 kg/m³ [1]. (Or: 156 ÷ 60 = 2.6 g/cm³, × 1000 = 2600 kg/m³.)
**(b)** 2.6 is the density in g/cm³; the student did not multiply by 1000 to convert to kg/m³ (or used grams with cm³ but wrote the SI unit) [1].

**7. (a)** A = 0.40 × 0.30 = 0.12 m² [1]. p = F ÷ A = 480 ÷ 0.12 [1] = 4000 Pa [1].
**(b)** 4.0 kPa [1].

**8. (a)** h = 1.80 m [1]. p = h × ρ × g = 1.80 × 1000 × 10 [1] = 18 000 Pa [1].
**(b)** 100 + 18 = 118 kPa [1].

**9. (a)** m = 0.750 kg and ΔT = 62 − 22 = 40 °C [1]. ΔQ = m × c × ΔT = 0.750 × 385 × 40 [1] = 11 550 J (1.2 × 10⁴ J to 2 s.f.) [1].
**(b)** c = ΔQ ÷ (m × ΔT) = 36 000 ÷ (2.0 × 40) = 450 [1] J/kg °C [1].

**10. (a)** p₁V₁ = p₂V₂, so p₂ = 120 × 50 ÷ 80 [1] = 75 kPa [1].
**(b)** Volume appears on both sides of the equation, so the conversion factor cancels; any unit can be used as long as both volumes are in the same unit [1].

**11. (a)** T₁ = 27 + 273 = 300 K and T₂ = 177 + 273 = 450 K [1]. p₂ = p₁ × T₂ ÷ T₁ = 100 × 450 ÷ 300 [1] = 150 kPa [1].
**(b)** Temperatures in this relationship must be in kelvin, because pressure is proportional to temperature measured from absolute zero, not from 0 °C [1].

**12.** A = 0.010 mm² = 0.010 × 10⁻⁶ m² = 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ m² [1]. p = F ÷ A = 1.5 ÷ 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ [1] = 1.5 × 10⁸ Pa [1].

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I always have to convert to m² and m³?**
For p = F/A and ρ = m/V, yes, if you want the answer in Pa or kg/m³. In p₁V₁ = p₂V₂ the volume unit cancels, so any
unit works if both sides match.
