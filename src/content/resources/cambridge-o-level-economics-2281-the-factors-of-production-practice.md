These questions cover section 1.2, *The factors of production*, of the Cambridge O Level Economics 2281 syllabus for examination in 2026: definitions and rewards, mobility, and changes in quantity and quality. All businesses, countries and figures are invented. Revise first with the [study guide on the factors of production](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-the-factors-of-production/) and the [revision notes on the factors of production](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-the-factors-of-production-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Which factor of production earns interest? **[1]**

A: Land

B: Labour

C: Capital

D: Enterprise

**2.** Which **one** of the following is an example of capital? **[1]**

A: A forest owned by a timber firm

B: A tractor used on a farm

C: Savings held in a bank account

D: A farmer's skill in growing crops

**3.** A coal mine closes and its miners lack the skills needed for jobs in a new call centre in the same town. What does this show? **[1]**

A: Geographical immobility of labour

B: Occupational immobility of labour

C: Geographical immobility of land

D: Occupational mobility of capital

**4.** Define *enterprise* and state its reward. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why a delivery van used by a bakery is capital, but the bread it delivers is not. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** causes of a fall in the quantity of land available to an economy. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Green Valley is an invented market garden. It rents 4 hectares of land, employs 6 workers, owns a greenhouse and an irrigation pump, and is run by its owner, who borrowed money to start it.

**(a)** Identify the factor of production represented by each of: the 4 hectares; the 6 workers; the greenhouse and pump; the owner. **[4]**

**(b)** State the reward earned by each factor in part (a). **[2]**

**8.** Explain **two** influences on the geographical mobility of labour. **[4]**

**9.** Explain why the occupational mobility of capital depends on the type of capital. Use examples. **[3]**

**10.** Country K (invented) has a working-age population of 12 million. Of these, 58% are in the labour force.

**(a)** Calculate the size of the labour force. **[1]**

**(b)** The government raises the retirement age and the labour force rises by 0.348 million. Calculate the percentage increase in the labour force. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain whether raising the retirement age changes the quantity or the quality of labour. **[2]**

**11.** Explain how each of the following could change the quality of a factor of production:

**(a)** a new irrigation scheme on farmland; **[2]**

**(b)** a government programme of free healthcare for workers. **[2]**

**12.** A government wants to increase the quantity and quality of capital in its economy. Discuss whether encouraging investment by firms is likely to achieve this. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** B [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* the miners stay in the same town, so the barrier is skills, not distance. Check whether a question is about changing place or changing job.

**4.** The organising of the other factors of production and the taking of risks in production [1]; its reward is profit [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The van is man-made and used to produce a service (delivering bread), so it is capital [1]; the bread is a final good consumed by households, not used to produce other goods [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two, one mark each: depletion of non-renewable resources such as minerals [1]; erosion or flooding [1]; over-use of renewable resources such as fish or forests; land lost to rising sea levels. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Hectares: land [1]. Workers: labour [1]. Greenhouse and pump: capital [1]. Owner: enterprise [1].

**(b)** Land: rent; labour: wages [1]. Capital: interest; enterprise: profit [1]. **[6]**

**8.** Two from, two marks each: the cost of housing in the new area [1], because high rents or house prices make it too expensive to move to where the jobs are [1]; family and social ties [1], because people may be unwilling to leave relatives, schools or friends [1]; lack of information about jobs elsewhere, so workers do not know vacancies exist; the cost of moving; language or cultural differences and rules on working in another country. **[4]**

**9.** General-purpose capital, such as a van or a computer, can be switched to many uses, so it is occupationally mobile [1]. Specialised capital, such as a machine built to bottle one drink, cannot easily be used for anything else [1], so it is occupationally immobile and may be scrapped if demand for its product falls [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** 12 × 0.58 = **6.96 million** [1].

**(b)** 0.348 ÷ 6.96 [1] × 100 = **5%** [1].

**(c)** It changes the quantity of labour [1], because there are more workers available, but each worker's skills are unchanged, so quality is not directly affected [1]. **[5]**

**11. (a)** Irrigation provides a reliable water supply, so the land becomes more fertile [1] and can produce more crops per hectare, a higher quality of land [1].

**(b)** Healthier workers take fewer days off and have more energy [1], so each worker produces more, a higher quality of labour [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Investment means firms buying new capital goods, so the quantity of capital rises if investment is greater than depreciation [1]. New machines often contain newer technology, so the quality of capital also rises [1]. However, if firms only replace worn-out machines, the quantity stays the same [1], and investment uses resources that could have produced consumer goods now [1]. Conclusion: encouraging investment is likely to work if firms expect enough demand to make it profitable and invest in up-to-date equipment [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* "discuss" needs points on both sides and a conclusion that depends on something, here firms' expectations and whether investment exceeds depreciation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping rewards, especially for capital and enterprise.
- Calling savings or money "capital".
- Confusing occupational with geographical mobility.
- Naming an influence on mobility without explaining how it stops the factor moving.
- Describing a change in the number of workers as a change in labour quality.
