## Names (6.2.1 a)

Tropical cyclones = **storms, hurricanes and typhoons**. Same system, different regions:
- Hurricane: North Atlantic, north-east Pacific.
- Typhoon: north-west Pacific.
- Cyclone: Indian Ocean, South Pacific.

## The three numbers to learn (6.2.1 b to d)

| Condition | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Latitude | 5° to 30° north or south of the Equator | Below 5°, too little spin from the Earth's rotation; beyond 30°, sea usually too cool |
| Ocean temperature | At least 27 °C | Rapid evaporation supplies heat and moisture |
| Ocean depth | At least 60 m | Enough warm water that the storm does not mix up cold water |

Memory line: **"5 to 30, 27, 60."**

## Life of a cyclone in five steps

1. Warm, moist air rises from a sea at 27 °C or more, leaving low pressure at the surface.
2. Rising air cools; vapour condenses; heat released makes the air rise faster.
3. More air is drawn in and spirals because of the Earth's rotation.
4. An eye forms, surrounded by the eye wall with the strongest winds and rain.
5. Over land or cooler water the energy supply is cut, and friction slows the winds, so it weakens.

## Three physical causes of damage

- **Strong winds**: roofs, power lines, trees, flying debris.
- **Heavy rainfall**: river flooding and landslides inland.
- **Storm surge**: sea level pushed up by wind and low pressure, flooding low coasts with sea water.

## The ten impacts (6.2.2)

Damage to buildings and infrastructure · loss of crops · livestock and habitats · evacuation of people and livestock · flooding · landslides · contamination of drinking-water supplies · water-related disease · financial losses · human health.

Chains worth writing out in full:
- Surge → sea water on farmland → salt in soil → crops fail for several seasons → food shortage, lost income.
- Flooding → sewage and sea water in wells → contaminated drinking water → cholera and diarrhoea.
- Heavy rain on steep, cleared slopes → saturated soil → landslides → blocked roads, delayed rescue.

## The seven strategies (6.2.3) by stage

| Before | During | After |
|---|---|---|
| Monitoring and warning | Evacuation | Rebuilding of damaged areas |
| Structure of buildings | Shelters | International aid |
| Disaster preparation (plans, drills, emergency supplies, rescue teams) | | |

Not on the cyclone list: land use zoning (it is on the earthquake and flood lists).

## Benefit and limitation pairs (6.2.4)

- **Monitoring and warning**: days of notice / path may change; warnings may not reach everyone; false alarms reduce trust.
- **Structure of buildings**: roof straps, shutters, raised floors reduce damage / costly for poor households.
- **Disaster preparation**: organised, quick response / needs funding and repeated drills.
- **Evacuation**: escapes the surge / jams, no transport for the poorest, people stay to guard property.
- **Shelters**: safe raised refuge nearby / cost; overcrowding and sanitation problems.
- **Rebuilding**: chance to build stronger / slow and expensive.
- **International aid**: food, water purification, medical teams / late, uneven, can create dependency.

## Applying the conditions to data

When a question gives a table of ocean locations, check each against all three conditions in turn and name the one it fails. A location at 3° N with a sea at 29 °C still fails, because it is too close to the Equator. A location at 20° S with a sea at 28 °C but only 45 m of warm water also fails, on depth. Only a location that passes all three can form a cyclone.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tropical cyclone | Intense low-pressure storm forming over warm tropical oceans |
| Eye | Calm, clear centre of a cyclone |
| Eye wall | Ring of cloud around the eye with the strongest winds |
| Storm surge | Rise in sea level as wind and low pressure push water onshore |
| Condensation | Water vapour turning to liquid, releasing heat |
| Evacuation | Moving people and livestock out of danger |
| Cyclone shelter | Strong, raised building for refuge during a storm |
| International aid | Help given by other countries or organisations |

## Exam traps

- Writing "26.5 °C" or "warm water": the syllabus value is at least 27 °C.
- Forgetting the depth condition of 60 m.
- Saying cyclones form at the Equator; they need at least 5° of latitude.
- Treating storms, hurricanes and typhoons as different hazards.
- Listing impacts without linking each to wind, rain or surge.
- A "discuss" answer with benefits only.
- Calling international aid a "before" strategy.

## Self-test

1. Give the three regional names for tropical cyclones in the syllabus.
2. State the latitude range in which tropical cyclones form.
3. What minimum ocean temperature is needed?
4. What minimum ocean depth is needed, and why?
5. Why do cyclones not form on the Equator?
6. Why does a cyclone weaken over land?
7. What is a storm surge?
8. Explain how a cyclone can cause water-related disease.
9. Name the two strategies used mainly during a cyclone.
10. Give one limitation of warnings.
11. Give two ways building structure can be improved for cyclones.
12. Give one benefit and one limitation of international aid.

**Answers:** 1. Storms, hurricanes, typhoons. 2. Between 5° and 30° north or south of the Equator. 3. At least 27 °C. 4. At least 60 m, so there is enough warm water and the storm does not mix up cold water from below. 5. The Earth's rotation is too weak there to make the air spin. 6. It loses its supply of warm, moist air, and friction slows the wind. 7. A rise in sea level as wind and low pressure push water onshore. 8. Flooding puts sewage and sea water into wells, so people drink contaminated water. 9. Evacuation; shelters. 10. Any one: path may change; may not reach remote areas; people may ignore them. 11. Any two: roofs strapped to walls; shutters; raised floors; stronger frames. 12. Brings resources beyond local capacity; may arrive late or create dependency.
