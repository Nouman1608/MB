## Section A

**1.** State the splitting pattern expected for a proton signal with three protons on the adjacent carbon
atom. **[1]**

**2.** Explain why the signal for the CH₃ protons in methyl ethanoate, CH₃COOCH₃, attached to the C=O group is
a singlet. **[2]**

**3.** An integration trace for a compound C₅H₁₀O shows steps of 8 mm and 12 mm. Deduce the number of protons
in each environment. **[2]**

**4.** State why the sample for proton NMR is dissolved in CDCl₃ rather than CHCl₃. **[2]**

**5.** Give the chemical shift region, from the data table, for:

**(a)** the CHO proton in an aldehyde **[1]**

**(b)** a CH₂ group bonded to a chlorine atom **[1]**

**(c)** a proton on a benzene ring **[1]**

**6.** Predict the splitting pattern of each signal in 1-chloropropane, CH₃CH₂CH₂Cl, using 9701 terminology.
**[3]**

## Section B

**7.** Ethanal, CH₃CHO, is analysed by proton NMR.

**(a)** Predict the number of signals and their relative areas. **[2]**

**(b)** Predict the splitting pattern and chemical shift region of each signal. **[4]**

**8.** Compound **G**, C₄H₈O₂, gives these signals: δ 1.2 (triplet, 3H), δ 2.2 (singlet, 3H), δ 3.9
(quartet, 2H).

**(a)** Explain what the triplet and quartet together show. **[2]**

**(b)** Use the chemical shifts to place the CH₂ group and the singlet CH₃ group in the molecule. **[2]**

**(c)** Deduce the structure of G and explain why it is not methyl propanoate. **[2]**

**9.** Compound **H**, C₈H₁₀, gives two signals: a singlet in the 2.3–3.0 region and a signal in the 6.0–9.0
region, with relative areas 3 : 2.

**(a)** Explain what the 6.0–9.0 signal and the area ratio show about the benzene ring. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest a structure for H consistent with the data. **[1]**

**(c)** Predict how the spectrum of ethylbenzene, also C₈H₁₀, would differ. **[3]**

**10.** 2-chloropropane, CH₃CHClCH₃, is analysed by proton NMR. Predict the number of signals, and for each its
relative area, splitting pattern and chemical shift region. **[5]**

**11.** Propan-2-ol, (CH₃)₂CHOH, and methoxyethane, CH₃OCH₂CH₃, are isomers.

**(a)** Predict the splitting pattern and relative area of each signal for methoxyethane. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe how shaking each sample with D₂O would help distinguish them, giving the observations. **[3]**

**12.** Compound **J**, C₂H₄O₂, shows two signals: a singlet in the 2.2–3.0 region and a broad singlet in the
9.0–13.0 region, with relative areas 3 : 1. The broad signal disappears after shaking with D₂O.

**(a)** Identify the group responsible for the broad signal and explain the effect of D₂O. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the signal in the 2.2–3.0 region is a singlet. **[1]**

**(c)** Deduce the structure of J. **[1]**

**13.** Explain why the CH₂ protons in the middle of propane, CH₃CH₂CH₃, are described as a multiplet in
9701 terminology, and why the CH₃ protons are a triplet. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **Quartet** (n = 3, so n + 1 = 4 lines) [1].

**2.** The adjacent atom is the **C=O carbon, which has no hydrogen atoms** [1], so n = 0 and n + 1 = 1 line, a
singlet [1].

**3.** 8 : 12 = 2 : 3, a total of 5 [1]; the formula has 10 H, so double: **4H and 6H** [1]. (For example
pentan-3-one, CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₃.)

**4.** CHCl₃ contains ¹H, which would give a **large solvent signal that hides the sample's signals** [1];
**deuterium does not give a signal** in a proton spectrum [1].

**5. (a)** **9.3–10.5 ppm** [1].

**(b)** **3.2–4.0 ppm** (alkyl next to an electronegative atom) [1].

**(c)** **6.0–9.0 ppm** [1].

**6.** CH₃: **triplet** (two neighbouring protons) [1]. Middle CH₂: **multiplet** (five neighbouring protons
on two different carbons) [1]. CH₂Cl: **triplet** (two neighbouring protons) [1].

**7. (a)** **Two signals** [1], relative areas **3 : 1** (CH₃ : CHO) [1].

**(b)** CH₃: **doublet** [1], in the **2.2–3.0** region (alkyl next to C=O) [1]. CHO: **quartet** [1], in the
**9.3–10.5** region [1].

**8. (a)** A **CH₃CH₂– group** [1]: the CH₃ is split into a triplet by the two CH₂ protons and the CH₂ into a
quartet by the three CH₃ protons [1].

**(b)** The quartet at δ 3.9 is in the 3.2–4.0 region, so the **CH₂ is bonded to an oxygen atom** [1]. The
singlet at δ 2.2 is in the 2.2–3.0 region, so the **CH₃ is bonded to the C=O carbon**, with no neighbouring
protons [1].

**(c)** G is **ethyl ethanoate, CH₃COOCH₂CH₃** [1]. In methyl propanoate the quartet would be in the 2.2–3.0
region (CH₂ next to C=O) and the singlet in the 3.2–4.0 region (OCH₃), the reverse of these data [1].

**9. (a)** Ring protons are present, so the compound contains a **benzene ring** [1]. With 10 H in total, a 3 : 2
ratio means 6H : 4H, so there are **four ring protons and two substituents** [1].

**(b)** **1,4-dimethylbenzene** (accept 1,2- or 1,3-dimethylbenzene: each has two methyl groups and four ring
protons) [1].

**(c)** Ethylbenzene would give **three** signals [1]: a **triplet** (CH₃, 3H) and a **quartet** (CH₂ in the
2.3–3.0 region, 2H) [1], and a ring signal for **five** protons, giving a ratio of 3 : 2 : 5 [1].

**10.** **Two signals** [1]. CH₃ protons: area **6**, **doublet**, **0.9–1.7** region [2] (any two of area,
pattern and region for 1 mark; all three for 2). CH proton: area **1**, **multiplet**, **3.2–4.0** region (next
to Cl) [2] (same rule). **[5]**

**11. (a)** OCH₃: **singlet**, area 3 [1]. OCH₂: **quartet**, area 2 [1]. CH₃: **triplet**, area 3 [1].

**(b)** Record each spectrum, shake with D₂O and record again [1]. In **propan-2-ol** the **O–H signal
disappears** because the proton exchanges for deuterium [1]. **Methoxyethane** has no O–H proton, so its
spectrum is **unchanged** [1].

**12. (a)** A **carboxylic acid O–H** (COOH) [1]; the O–H proton exchanges for deuterium, which gives no
signal in a proton spectrum, so the peak disappears [1].

**(b)** The CH₃ group is bonded to the **C=O carbon, which carries no hydrogen**, so there are no neighbouring
protons to split it [1].

**(c)** **Ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH**: a CH₃ next to C=O (3H) and a COOH proton (1H), matching the 3 : 1 ratio and
the four hydrogens in the formula [1].

**13.** The middle CH₂ has **six neighbouring protons**, three on each side [1], which would give seven lines,
and 9701 names any pattern beyond a quartet a **multiplet** [1]. Each CH₃ has only the **two CH₂ protons** as
neighbours, so n + 1 = 3, a triplet [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the number of neighbouring protons when the question asks for the pattern.
- Missing neighbours on one side of a CH₂ group.
- Splitting a CH₃ that sits next to O or C=O.
- Telling ester isomers apart by splitting alone instead of by the shift of the quartet.
- Not scaling an area ratio to the molecular formula.
- Describing the D₂O test without stating which signal disappears and why.
