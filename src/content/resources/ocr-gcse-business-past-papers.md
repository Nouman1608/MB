This page is a guide to OCR GCSE (9-1) Business (J204) past papers from June 2023 to June 2026. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from OCR's grade-boundary documents, and, for June 2023 to June 2025, a short account of what examiners reported, written by us from OCR's own examiners' reports. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on OCR's site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [OCR GCSE Business (J204) qualification page](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/gcse/business-j204-from-2017/)
- [OCR GCSE Business (J204) assessment page, with question papers, mark schemes and examiners' reports](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/gcse/business-j204-from-2017/assessment/)
- [OCR grade boundaries](https://www.ocr.org.uk/administration/grade-boundaries/) and the [grade boundaries archive](https://www.ocr.org.uk/administration/grade-boundaries/grade-boundaries-archive/grade-boundaries-archive.aspx)

Use paper, mark scheme and examiners' report together. Sit the paper under timed conditions, mark it strictly with the mark scheme, then read the report for that paper to see why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. Our notes for each series are a quick way into the report, not a replacement for it.

Check the structure before you rely on an older paper. J204 is linear, with two written papers sat at the end of the course: Business 1: business activity, marketing and people (J204/01) and Business 2: operations, finance and influences on business (J204/02). Each is 80 marks, 1 hour 30 minutes and 50% of the qualification, so 160 marks in total. Paper 2 includes synoptic assessment. The papers use short texts as case-study material, so practise applying your answer to the business in the texts. For topic study, see our [marketing notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-business-marketing/).

## June 2025

The June 2025 reports describe a marked improvement on the two high-mark questions in Paper 1 and a slightly better Paper 2 overall, with more candidates structuring their answers well. The pattern that held back marks was answers that were sound in general but not applied to the case texts.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (J204/01): multiple-choice questions left blank, or answers changed by writing one letter over another so the choice was unclear; generic Section B answers; recommendations with little or no supporting reason; not using the graph data given in the text when answering the data-based question. Stronger scripts gave comparative, contextual reasons.
- Paper 2 (J204/02): answers that lacked the knowledge or understanding that application and analysis rest on; misreading what the question asked; missed questions; fewer than half marks on the multiple-choice section, where the quantitative questions were answered worst. Good answers sometimes ran too long for 3 marks, and some candidates did not finish the last two questions. Answers not applied to the case studies lost application marks.

## June 2024

In June 2024 examiners reported a growing entry, good engagement with the business contexts and performance on Paper 2 slightly down on the previous year. Handwriting that was hard to read was a concern again.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (J204/01): multiple-choice questions left blank; generic answers where Section B needed applied ones; brief points with no explanation or analysis; recommendations without reasons; not using the numerical data given in the text when answering the data-based question. Candidates who did well wrote about the impact on the business rather than on its stakeholders.
- Paper 2 (J204/02): a high omit rate; gaps in knowledge; answers that showed analysis without first showing understanding; not being clear which skill a question wanted; using a context of the candidate's own instead of the one in the case material, which earns no application marks; unreadable handwriting, even from candidates who had a laptop.

## June 2023

Examiners in June 2023 found Paper 2 very accessible but with some challenging topics, and said the paper separated stronger and weaker candidates well.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (J204/01): blank multiple-choice questions and unclear letters; generic answers in Section B; brief points with no explanation; recommendations with limited or no reason; not using the numerical data given in the text when answering the data-based question.
- Paper 2 (J204/02): finance, procurement and break-even, which had high omit rates; the 9-mark final question, where candidates did not always reach all the marks; application of context left out where it was needed; many questions missed by weaker candidates; poor handwriting. Higher scorers showed knowledge first, then application and analysis.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find OCR J204 past papers and mark schemes?**
On the assessment page for OCR GCSE Business, linked above, which lists question papers, mark schemes and examiners' reports by series.

**Are the J204 papers tiered?**
No. Both papers are sat by every candidate, and grades 9 to 1 are awarded from the combined 160 marks.

**How should I use the multiple-choice section?**
Answer all 15 questions, because a blank earns nothing. If you change an answer, cross out the first one fully rather than writing over it, as examiners reported that unclear letters lost marks.

**What mistakes do examiners report most often?**
Generic answers that are not applied to the case texts, recommendations without reasons, knowledge missing before analysis is attempted, and questions left blank.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and are only a guide for the series they belong to.
