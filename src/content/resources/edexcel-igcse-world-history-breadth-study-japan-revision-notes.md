## Worked example: explain two ways different

*Explain two ways in which Japan's relations with the West in 1858 were different from its relations
with the West in 1902.* (6 marks)

1. **Equality:** in 1858 Japan was forced to accept unequal treaties; in 1902 Britain signed an alliance
   with Japan as an equal partner.
2. **Power:** in 1858 Japan could not resist Western warships; by 1902 it had a modern army and navy and
   had defeated China, making it a regional power.

## Essay plan in outline

*"Contact with the West was the main reason for Japan's transformation in the years 1853–1912." How far
do you agree?*

- **For:** Perry and the unequal treaties triggered the Tokugawa's fall; Western models for the army,
  constitution, industry and education; the Iwakura Mission.
- **Against:** Japanese leadership (Satsuma and Choshu oligarchs); the drive for national strength;
  existing literacy and commerce; war with China and Russia as engines of change.
- **Judgement:** the West provided the shock and the models; Japanese leaders chose how to use them.

## Turning points to argue about

- **1853–54:** the end of seclusion — the start of every later change.
- **1868:** the Restoration — new leaders with a programme of modernisation.
- **1905:** victory over Russia — Japan recognised as a great power.
- **1931:** Manchuria — the army acts independently and the road to war begins.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Treating the Restoration as a revolution from below.** It was led by samurai of a few domains.
- **Assuming the Meiji Constitution created democracy.** The emperor was sovereign; the army answered to
  him.
- **Ignoring continuity.** Village life changed slowly even as cities modernised.
- **Confusing the Boxer Rebellion (1900) with the Sino-Japanese War (1894–95).**
- **Covering one topic only in (c).** Use at least two key topics.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why did Japan modernise so quickly?**
Fear of colonisation, determined leaders and a literate population.

**Why did the military gain power in the 1930s?**
Depression, distrust of party politicians, success in
Manchuria and the army's independence under the constitution.

**Why did Japan surrender in 1945?**
The atomic bombs and the Soviet declaration of war.
