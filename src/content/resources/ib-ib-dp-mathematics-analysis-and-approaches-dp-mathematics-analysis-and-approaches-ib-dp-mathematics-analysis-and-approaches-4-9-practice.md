This set practises syllabus section 4.9 of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (SL and HL): the
normal distribution, normal probabilities and inverse normal calculations. Learn the methods first with the
[4.9 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-4-9/)
and the [4.9 revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-4-9-revision-notes/).
All contexts are invented. Section A needs no calculator; Section B needs a GDC. Give answers to 3
significant figures.

## Section A

**1.** X ~ N(40, 25).

**(a)** Write down the standard deviation of X. **[1]**
**(b)** Write down P(X > 40). **[1]**

**2.** X ~ N(μ, σ²) with μ = 120. P(X > 135) = 0.21.

**(a)** Find P(X < 105). **[1]**
**(b)** Find P(105 < X < 135). **[1]**
**(c)** Find P(120 < X < 135). **[1]**

**3.** The lengths of a type of fish are modelled by N(32, 4²) cm. Use the approximate percentages for 1, 2
and 3 standard deviations to estimate the percentage of fish

**(a)** between 28 cm and 36 cm long; **[1]**
**(b)** longer than 40 cm; **[2]**
**(c)** between 24 cm and 36 cm long. **[2]**

**4.** X ~ N(μ, σ²) and P(X < 18) = P(X > 30). Find μ. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why, for a normal variable X, P(X < 10) = P(X ≤ 10). **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** The masses of bags of flour are modelled by X ~ N(500, 6²) grams.

**(a)** Find the probability that a bag has mass less than 490 g. **[2]**
**(b)** Find P(495 < X < 510). **[2]**
**(c)** In a delivery of 1000 bags, find the expected number with mass less than 490 g. **[1]**
**(d)** Find the probability that none of 10 randomly chosen bags has mass less than 490 g. **[2]**

**7.** For the bags in question 6:

**(a)** The lightest 2% of bags are rejected. Find the mass below which a bag is rejected. **[2]**
**(b)** The heaviest 15% of bags are labelled "generous". Find the least mass of a "generous" bag. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the limits of the middle 80% of masses, symmetric about the mean. **[3]**

**8.** Reaction times of drivers are modelled by T ~ N(0.42, 0.05²) seconds.

**(a)** Sketch the distribution, shading the region T > 0.5. **[2]**
**(b)** Find P(T > 0.5). **[2]**
**(c)** Find P(T < 0.35). **[2]**
**(d)** Find the upper quartile of T. **[2]**

**9.** Exam scores are modelled by N(64, 9²). A grade A is awarded to the top 12% of scores. Find the least
score that earns a grade A. **[2]**

**10.** For the scores in question 9, find the probability that a student scores more than 70, given that
they score more than 64. **[3]**

**11.** The seedlings in a tray have heights modelled by N(12.4, 1.8²) cm, independently. Find the probability
that, of 8 seedlings chosen at random, exactly one is taller than 15 cm. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** σ = 5 [1].
**(b)** 0.5 [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** 105 and 135 are each 15 from the mean, so P(X < 105) = 0.21 [1].
**(b)** 1 − 0.21 − 0.21 = 0.58 [1].
**(c)** 0.58/2 = 0.29 [1]. **[3]**

**3. (a)** 28 to 36 is μ ± σ: about 68% [1].
**(b)** 40 is μ + 2σ [1]; about (100 − 95)/2 = 2.5% [1].
**(c)** 24 is μ − 2σ and 36 is μ + σ [1]; about 95/2 + 68/2 = 47.5 + 34 = 81.5% [1]. **[5]**

**4.** The two tails are equal, so 18 and 30 are equidistant from μ [1]; μ = (18 + 30)/2 = 24 [1]. **[2]**

**5.** X is continuous, so P(X = 10) = 0 [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** P(X < 490) [1] = 0.0478 [1].
**(b)** 0.750 [2].
**(c)** 1000 × 0.04779 ≈ 47.8 [1].
**(d)** Y ~ B(10, 0.04779), P(Y = 0) = (1 − 0.04779)¹⁰ [1] = 0.613 [1]. **[7]**

**7. (a)** P(X < k) = 0.02 [1], so k = 488 g (487.68) [1].
**(b)** P(X < k) = 0.85 [1], so k = 506 g (506.22) [1].
**(c)** Areas 0.1 and 0.9 to the left [1]; 492 g (492.31) [1] and 508 g (507.69) [1]. **[7]**

**8. (a)** A symmetric bell curve centred on 0.42 with the axis labelled [1], and the region to the right of
0.5 (to the right of the mean) shaded [1].
**(b)** P(T > 0.5) [1] = 0.0548 [1].
**(c)** P(T < 0.35) [1] = 0.0808 [1].
**(d)** P(T < q) = 0.75 [1], q = 0.454 s [1]. **[8]**

**9.** P(X > k) = 0.12, so P(X < k) = 0.88 [1]; k = 74.6, so a score of about 75 [1]. **[2]**

**10.** P(X > 70 | X > 64) = P(X > 70)/P(X > 64) [1] = 0.25249.../0.5 [1] = 0.505 [1]. **[3]**

**11.** p = P(height > 15) = 0.074307 [1]. Y ~ B(8, 0.074307) [1]; P(Y = 1) = 0.346 [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Entering 25 rather than 5 as the standard deviation in question 1.
- Using the area 0.15 instead of 0.85 in question 7(b), which gives a value below the mean.
- Giving 0.5 as the denominator in question 10 without explaining why P(X > 64) = 0.5.
- Rounding p before the binomial step in questions 6(d) and 11.
- Leaving question 3(c) at 95% or 68% instead of combining half of each.
