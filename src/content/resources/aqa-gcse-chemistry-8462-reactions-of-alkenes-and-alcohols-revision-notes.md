These notes condense **4.7.2 Reactions of alkenes and alcohols (chemistry only)** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry
(8462) specification (for teaching from September 2016). Paper 2 content for the separate chemistry
qualification. Higher tier only: explaining why carboxylic acids are weak acids. For explanations use the
[reactions of alkenes and alcohols study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-reactions-of-alkenes-and-alcohols/);
to test yourself use the
[practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-reactions-of-alkenes-and-alcohols-practice/).

## Homologous series at a glance

| Series | Functional group | General formula or ending | First four members |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alkenes | C=C | CₙH₂ₙ, **-ene** | ethene, propene, butene, pentene |
| Alcohols | –OH | **-ol** | methanol, ethanol, propanol, butanol |
| Carboxylic acids | –COOH | **-oic acid** | methanoic, ethanoic, propanoic, butanoic |

Alkenes are **unsaturated** (two fewer hydrogen atoms than the matching alkane).

## Alkene reactions

| Reaction | Conditions | Product |
|---|---|---|
| Combustion | Air | CO₂ and H₂O; **smoky flame** (incomplete combustion) |
| + Hydrogen | Nickel catalyst, about 60 °C | Alkane (C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆) |
| + Steam | Phosphoric acid catalyst, high temperature and pressure | Alcohol (C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH) |
| + Halogen (Cl₂, Br₂, I₂) | Room temperature | Dihalogenoalkane (C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂) |

- **Addition**: atoms add across C=C, which becomes a **single bond**. **One product only.**
- **Test for alkenes**: bromine water **orange → colourless**.

## Alcohols

| Reaction | Result |
|---|---|
| + sodium | Fizzing, **hydrogen** given off; sodium alkoxide forms |
| Burn in air | **CO₂ + H₂O** |
| In water | Dissolve (first four); **neutral** solution |
| + oxidising agent | **Carboxylic acid**; acidified potassium dichromate goes **orange → green** |

Combustion equations (the only alcohol equations needed):

- **C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O**
- **2CH₃OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 4H₂O**
- **2C₃H₇OH + 9O₂ → 6CO₂ + 8H₂O**

Uses: **solvents**, **fuels**, ethanol in **alcoholic drinks**.

## Fermentation

- Sugar solution + **yeast**.
- **About 30 to 40 °C**, **no oxygen**.
- C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂
- Gives **aqueous ethanol** (a dilute solution).

## Carboxylic acids

| Reaction | Result |
|---|---|
| + carbonate | Salt + water + **carbon dioxide** (fizzing) |
| In water | **Acidic** solution (pH below 7) |
| + alcohol (acid catalyst) | **Ester + water** |

Ester to know: **ethyl ethanoate** (ethanoic acid + ethanol → ethyl ethanoate + water). Esters smell fruity and
are used in perfumes and flavourings. Balanced equations for carboxylic acids are not required.

## Higher tier only: weak acids

Carboxylic acids are **partially ionised** in water, so the solution has a **lower H⁺ concentration** and a
**higher pH** than a strong acid of the same concentration.

## Formulae to recognise

| Name | Formula |
|---|---|
| Ethene | C₂H₄ |
| Propene | C₃H₆ |
| Ethanol | C₂H₅OH |
| Propanol | C₃H₇OH |
| Ethanoic acid | CH₃COOH |
| Ethyl ethanoate | CH₃COOC₂H₅ |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Unsaturated | Contains a C=C double bond |
| Functional group | Atom or group that decides a compound's reactions |
| Addition reaction | Atoms add across a double bond, one product |
| Fermentation | Yeast converts sugar to ethanol and carbon dioxide |
| Ester | Compound formed from a carboxylic acid and an alcohol |
| Oxidising agent | Substance that oxidises another, for example acidified dichromate |

## Exam traps

- **Alkane or alkene?** Check the hydrogen count against 2n and 2n + 2.
- **Two products from addition.** There is one.
- **Alcohol solutions acidic.** They are neutral.
- **Colour changes mixed up.** Bromine water: orange to colourless. Dichromate: orange to green.
- **Fermentation in air.** Oxygen makes ethanoic acid; no oxygen is needed.
- **Missing conditions.** Name nickel for hydrogen and phosphoric acid for steam.
- **Combustion equations unbalanced.** Remember the oxygen already in the alcohol.

## Quick self-test

1. What is the general formula of the alkenes?
2. Why do alkenes burn with smoky flames?
3. Name the product of ethene with hydrogen and give the catalyst.
4. Name the product of ethene with steam.
5. What happens to bromine water when shaken with propene?
6. What gas forms when sodium reacts with ethanol?
7. State the colour change when ethanol is oxidised by acidified potassium dichromate.
8. State the conditions for fermentation.
9. Name the products of the reaction of ethanoic acid with sodium carbonate.
10. Name the ester made from ethanoic acid and ethanol.
11. (Higher tier only) Why is ethanoic acid a weak acid?
12. (Higher tier only) A solution of ethanoic acid and a solution of hydrochloric acid have the same concentration. Which has the higher pH? Explain.

### Answers

1. CₙH₂ₙ.
2. Incomplete combustion occurs, forming carbon (soot), because the molecule has a high proportion of carbon.
3. Ethane; nickel.
4. Ethanol.
5. It changes from orange to colourless.
6. Hydrogen.
7. Orange to green.
8. Yeast, about 30 to 40 °C, no oxygen.
9. A salt (sodium ethanoate), water and carbon dioxide.
10. Ethyl ethanoate.
11. It is only partially ionised in water.
12. Ethanoic acid, because it is only partially ionised, so it has a lower hydrogen ion concentration.
