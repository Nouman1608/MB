Each question uses one of the specification's command words at its published International A Level tariff: **define** (2), **calculate** (4), **explain** (4), **analyse** (6), **discuss** (8) and **assess** (12). All countries, businesses and figures are invented.

## Sources

> **Source 1: The economy of Zephra**
>
> Zephra is an emerging economy. Its GDP rose from $400bn to $432bn in
> one year, while its population rose from 80 million to 82 million. Its
> human development index is 0.62. Exports were $90bn and imports $110bn.
> Zephra's middle class is growing quickly and several foreign
> manufacturers have built factories there.
>
> **Source 2: Tidewater Appliances**
>
> Tidewater makes kettles in its home country and sells 60,000 a year in
> Landia at $40 each, with variable costs of $28 each. A local rival sells
> similar kettles in Landia for $48. Landia has just introduced a 25%
> tariff on imported kettles. If Tidewater passes the tariff to customers,
> it expects to sell 35,000 kettles. If it absorbs the tariff and keeps
> the customer price at $40, it expects to sell 55,000. Tidewater is
> considering building a factory in Landia, which would let it avoid the
> tariff.

## Section A

Questions 1 to 6 refer to Source 1.

**1.** Define the term "foreign direct investment". **[2]**

**2.** Calculate Zephra's GDP per capita before and after the year of growth. **[4]**

**3.** Calculate the percentage growth of GDP per capita. **[4]**

**4.** State Zephra's trade balance and explain what it means. **[4]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why foreign manufacturers might build factories in Zephra. **[4]**

**6.** Analyse why GDP per capita may give a better picture of Zephra's market than GDP alone. **[6]**

## Section B

Questions 7 to 12 refer to Source 2.

**7.** Define the term "tariff". **[2]**

**8.** Calculate the tariff per kettle and the price in Landia if Tidewater passes the tariff to customers. **[4]**

**9.** Calculate Tidewater's total contribution before the tariff, and after the tariff if it passes the tariff on. **[4]**

**10.** Calculate Tidewater's total contribution if it absorbs the tariff, and the tariff revenue Landia's government receives. **[4]**

**11.** Discuss whether Tidewater should absorb the tariff or pass it on to customers. **[8]**

**12.** Assess whether Tidewater should build a factory in Landia. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** Investment by a business in one country [1] in productive assets in another country, such as a factory, offices or the purchase of a local firm [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Before: 400bn / 80m = **$5,000** [2]. After: 432bn / 82m = **$5,268** [2]. (Award 1 mark for each correct method if the arithmetic is wrong.) **[4]**

**3.** (5,268.29 - 5,000) / 5,000 [1] x 100 [1] = **5.4%** [2]. (Alternatively 1.08 / 1.025 - 1 = 5.4%.) **[4]**

**4.** Trade balance = 90 - 110 [1] = **-$20bn**, a deficit [1]. Zephra imports more than it exports [1], so it must finance the gap through borrowing, investment from abroad or using reserves; for businesses it shows that Zephra is a market for imports, such as capital goods and consumer goods [1]. **[4]**

**5.** Zephra's middle class is growing quickly, so there is rising demand for goods [1], and by producing in Zephra, manufacturers can sell to the market without paying import tariffs or transport costs [1]. They may also benefit from lower wages and local knowledge [1], which reduces costs and strengthens their position against local rivals [1]. **[4]**

**6.** GDP rose 8%, but population rose 2.5%, so the average person's income rose by only 5.4% [1]. GDP alone suggests a bigger increase in spending power than customers have [1]. GDP per capita of $5,268 shows that Zephra is a lower-income market than a developed country, so firms must set prices accordingly [1]. Businesses use it to judge demand for products such as cars or phones that depend on income [1]. However, an average hides inequality: a growing middle class may have much more spending power than the average [1], and the HDI of 0.62 shows that health and education are also still developing [1]. **[6]**

**7.** A tax on imported goods [1] that raises their price and protects domestic producers [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Tariff = 25% x $40 [1] = **$10** per kettle [1]. Price to Landian customers = 40 + 10 [1] = **$50** [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Before: contribution per kettle = 40 - 28 = $12, so total = 12 x 60,000 = **$720,000** [2]. After passing it on: 12 x 35,000 = **$420,000** [2]. **[4]**

**10.** Tidewater receives 40 - 10 = $30 per kettle, so contribution per kettle = 30 - 28 = $2 [1]. Total = 2 x 55,000 = **$110,000** [1]. Tariff revenue = 55,000 x $10 [1] = **$550,000** [1]. **[4]**

**11.** **Pass it on:** contribution of $420,000 is far higher than the $110,000 from absorbing it [1]; each sale keeps the same $12 contribution [1]; at $50 the kettle is only $2 above the local rival's $48 price, so it may keep customers who like the brand [1].
**Absorb it:** the kettle stays at $40, which is $8 below the local rival, so it keeps 55,000 sales and its market share [1]; but contribution falls by 85% from $720,000 to $110,000 [1] and may be too low to cover fixed costs [1].
**Judgement:** Tidewater should pass the tariff on, because it earns $310,000 more contribution than absorbing it [1]. It may choose to absorb the tariff for a short time only if it needs to keep its market share while it sets up another response [1]. **[8]**

**12.** **For a factory:** production in Landia avoids the 25% tariff, so Tidewater could return to selling at $40 [1]; it could sell more than 55,000 kettles at the lower price, regaining up to 60,000 [1]; the local presence could cut transport costs and build goodwill, and might qualify for government incentives [1]; it would be a long-term answer if Landia's tariffs stay [1].
**Against:** a factory needs large capital with no certainty that sales would justify it [1]; Tidewater has only 35,000 to 60,000 sales a year, which may be too small to support a factory efficiently [1]; Landia could change its policies, making the investment less valuable [1]; running a business abroad adds management and cultural risks [1].
**Judgement:** a factory is probably not justified now: the volume is small, and passing on the tariff still leaves $420,000 of contribution [1]. Tidewater should review the decision if Landian sales grow, if the tariff rises further or if the cost of the factory is low [1]. An alternative is to contract with a Landian manufacturer or to find other markets [1]. The answer depends on the capital cost, which the source does not give, and on how likely the tariff is to continue [1]. **[12]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using GDP growth as the growth of GDP per capita.
- Forgetting that the tariff reduces Tidewater's revenue when it absorbs it.
- Calculating contribution with the tariff-inclusive price when Tidewater absorbs it.
- Describing FDI as exporting.
- Saying a factory is "better" without referring to volumes or cost.
