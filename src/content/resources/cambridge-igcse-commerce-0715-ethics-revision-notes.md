Condensed notes for section 6.2 of Cambridge IGCSE Commerce (0715). For fuller explanations, two worked examples and an annotated answer, use the [ethics study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-commerce-0715-ethics/).

## The syllabus in three lines

Section 6.2.1 asks for:

1. The **meaning of ethics**.
2. The **role of fair trade** in commerce.
3. **Ethical issues** in commerce: **misleading advertising** and **harmful disposal of products**.

## Meaning of ethics

- **Ethics:** moral principles about what is right and wrong, such as honesty, fairness and responsibility.
- Ethical is **not** the same as legal. A business can be legal but still treat people unfairly.
- Ethical behaviour often costs more in the short term and protects reputation and loyalty in the long term.

| Reasons to act ethically | Possible cost |
|---|---|
| Good reputation | Higher prices paid to suppliers |
| Customer loyalty | Money spent on safe disposal or checks |
| Motivated staff | Lower short-term profit |
| Avoids fines and legal action | Prices to customers may rise |

## Fair trade

**Definition:** an arrangement in which producers, often in poorer countries, are paid a fair price and work in decent conditions.

| Feature | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Guaranteed minimum price | Covers the producer's costs even if market price falls |
| Premium | Extra money for community projects such as schools, clean water |
| Safe conditions | No child or forced labour |
| Long-term relationships | Producers can plan ahead |
| Environmental care | Support for greener farming |

**Role in commerce:** fairer pay; better conditions; consumer choice; brand image for businesses; development of producer communities.

| Advantages for the business | Disadvantages for the business |
|---|---|
| Attracts ethical customers | Higher purchase costs |
| Improves reputation | Customers may not pay more |
| Reliable supply from committed producers | Certification costs |

## Misleading advertising

**Meaning:** advertising that gives a false or unclear impression so customers are deceived.

Examples: untrue health claims; "up to 70% off" when almost nothing is reduced that much; unsupported "100% natural" or "eco-friendly" claims; hidden charges in small print.

| Effects on customers | Effects on the business |
|---|---|
| Waste money; possible harm | Loss of trust |
| Lose confidence | Refunds and complaints |
| Honest rivals disadvantaged | Fines; legal action |

Honest advertising: facts that can be proved, clear prices, no exaggeration.

## Harmful disposal of products

**Meaning:** getting rid of products, packaging or waste in a way that damages people or the environment.

Examples: dumping chemicals in rivers; burning plastic; throwing electronic waste in ordinary rubbish; leaving unsold goods to rot.

| Effects | Detail |
|---|---|
| Environment | Poisoned water and soil; harm to wildlife |
| People | Health problems for nearby communities |
| Business | Fines, clean-up costs, damaged reputation |

Ethical alternatives: safe treatment of chemicals; recycling; take-back schemes; careful waste sorting.

## Sustainability and ethics: the link

Sustainability (6.1) is about protecting the environment for the future. Ethics (6.2) is about honest, fair behaviour towards all people and the environment. Harmful disposal is an ethical issue because it harms others; a false green claim is a misleading advertising issue.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ethics | Moral principles that guide behaviour |
| Ethical issue | A situation in which the business must choose between right and wrong, or profit and fairness |
| Fair trade | Trade that gives producers a fair price and decent conditions |
| Premium | Extra payment used for community projects |
| Misleading advertising | Advertising that gives a false or unclear impression |
| Greenwashing | Making unproven or false environmental claims |
| Harmful disposal | Getting rid of waste or products in a way that causes harm |
| Take-back scheme | A scheme in which sellers collect old products for safe recycling or disposal |

## Exam traps

- **Ethical** does not mean **legal**.
- Fair trade is a **fair price**, not a gift.
- Fair trade also has **costs** for the buyer.
- Name **what** makes an advert misleading (false claim, hidden charge, exaggeration).
- Harmful disposal needs a **harm**: who or what is damaged.
- In an evaluation, weigh cost against reputation and risk of penalties.

## Self-test

1. Define ethics.
2. Give **one** difference between ethical and legal behaviour.
3. What is fair trade?
4. State **two** features of fair trade.
5. State **two** advantages of fair trade to a retailer.
6. Give **one** disadvantage of fair trade to a business.
7. Define misleading advertising.
8. Give **two** examples of misleading advertising.
9. State **two** effects of misleading advertising on a business.
10. What is harmful disposal of products?
11. Give **two** ways a firm could dispose of waste ethically.
12. A farmer sells 2,500 kg at a market price of $1.40 a kg or a fair trade price of $1.80. Calculate the extra income.

**Answers**

1. Moral principles that guide what is right and wrong in behaviour.
2. Legal behaviour follows the law; ethical behaviour also considers fairness and honesty, which the law may not require.
3. Trade in which producers receive a fair price and decent working conditions.
4. Any two: guaranteed minimum price; community premium; safe working conditions; long-term relationships; environmentally friendly methods.
5. Any two: attracts ethical customers; better reputation; reliable supply of quality goods.
6. Any one: higher purchase costs; certification costs; customers may not pay more.
7. Advertising that gives a false or unclear impression so customers are deceived.
8. Any two: untrue health claim; hidden charges; unsupported eco-friendly claim; "up to" discount that applies to almost no items.
9. Any two: loss of trust; refunds and complaints; fines; legal action.
10. Getting rid of products, packaging or waste in a way that damages people or the environment.
11. Any two: safe treatment of chemicals; recycling; take-back schemes; careful sorting of waste.
12. Market: 2,500 x 1.40 = $3,500. Fair trade: 2,500 x 1.80 = $4,500. Extra = $1,000.
