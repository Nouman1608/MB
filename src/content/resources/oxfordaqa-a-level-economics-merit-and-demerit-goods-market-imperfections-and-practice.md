## Questions

**1.** Explain why classifying a good as a merit good involves a value judgement. **[3]**

**2.** In Town C (illustrative), eye-test clinics have market demand Qd = 150 − 3P and supply Qs = 30 + 3P. Price is in dollars and quantity in tests per week. If people knew the full benefit, demand would be Qd* = 210 − 3P.

**(a)** Find the equilibrium price and quantity in the unaided market. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the output that would be desirable and the price at that output. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the net benefit lost because of the market outcome. **[2]**

**3.** Drivers in a city cause congestion, a negative externality in consumption. Explain why this does not by itself make car use a demerit good. **[4]**

**4.** A second-hand e-bike is worth 1,500 to a buyer if it is sound and 500 if it is faulty. Of the e-bikes offered, 30% are sound. Buyers cannot tell them apart. Owners of sound e-bikes will accept no less than 1,100.

**(a)** Work out the most a buyer will pay for an e-bike picked blindly. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what happens in this market and why it is a market failure. **[3]**

**5.** A sole supplier of airport shuttle trips faces demand P = 120 − 2Q, with constant marginal cost of 20 (illustrative units).

**(a)** Find the output and price in a competitive market. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the profit-maximising output and price for the monopolist. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the monopolist's profit and the deadweight loss. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the monopoly outcome is a market failure. **[2]**

**6.** A town's only factory closes. Its workers have skills specific to that plant, while another region has vacancies in care work. Explain how this illustrates market failure. **[4]**

**7.** Demand for a crop has a price elasticity of −0.5.

**(a)** A poor harvest cuts supply by 12%. Estimate the percentage change in price. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the change in growers' total revenue. **[2]**
**(c)** In the next year supply rises by 12%. Calculate the revenue change. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why price instability is a market failure. **[2]**

**8.** A household in Country E (illustrative) receives 34,000 a year. It owns a house worth 210,000 with a mortgage of 120,000, savings of 9,000 and a car worth 7,000, and owes 6,000 on a loan.

**(a)** Calculate the household's net wealth. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the difference between its income and its wealth. **[2]**

**9.** In Country A (illustrative) the shares of income received by each fifth of households, from poorest to richest, are 4%, 8%, 13%, 20% and 55%.

**(a)** Express the richest fifth's share as a multiple of the poorest fifth's. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the combined share of the poorest 40% and of the richest 40%. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what these figures cannot tell you about equity. **[1]**

**10.** Distinguish between equality and equity, using an example. **[4]**

**11.** Evaluate whether a very unequal distribution of income and wealth means the market has failed. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** A merit good is one that society judges people should consume more of than they do [1]. Different people and governments hold different views about what is good for people, even when they agree on the facts [1]. There is no objective test, so the classification rests on opinion and can change over time [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong answers name the judgement ("people undervalue it") and do not just say the good is "good for you".

**2. (a)** 150 − 3P = 30 + 3P, so 6P = 120 [1]. P = 20 and Q = 90 [1].
**(b)** 210 − 3P = 30 + 3P, so P = 30 [1] and Q = 120 [1].
**(c)** At Q = 90 the full-benefit price is (210 − 90)/3 = 40 and the supply price is (90 − 30)/3 = 20, a gap of 20 [1]. Net benefit lost = ½ × 20 × (120 − 90) = 300 [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the price gap at the market quantity before the triangle, because the area needs both the height and the quantity difference.

**3.** Congestion imposes costs on other road users, so it is a negative externality in consumption [1]. A demerit good needs a judgement that consumers overconsume because they misjudge the harm to themselves [1]. Informed drivers understand their own costs, so they are not misjudging their own benefit [1]. The problem is the cost to third parties, so not every good with a consumption externality is a demerit good [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* This tests the specification's point that not all consumption externalities create merit or demerit goods, so state the extra test explicitly.

**4. (a)** 0.3 × 1,500 = 450 [1]. 0.7 × 500 = 350, so the maximum price is 450 + 350 = 800 [1].
**(b)** Owners of sound e-bikes want at least 1,100, above 800, so they withdraw [1]. Only faulty e-bikes are left, so buyers expect 500 and the price falls to about that [1]. Sales of sound e-bikes that would benefit both sides never happen, so resources are misallocated because of asymmetric information [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say who knows more (the seller) and follow the chain through to the missing trades.

**5. (a)** P = MC: 120 − 2Q = 20, so Q = 50 [1] and P = 20 [1].
**(b)** MR = 120 − 4Q [1]. Set MR = MC: 120 − 4Q = 20, so Q = 25 [1]. P = 120 − 2 × 25 = 70 [1].
**(c)** Profit = 25 × (70 − 20) = 1,250 [1]. Deadweight loss = ½ × (70 − 20) × (50 − 25) = 625 [1].
**(d)** Output (25) is below the competitive level (50) [1]. Price exceeds marginal cost, so some units that are worth more than their cost go unmade, a misallocation [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* The marginal revenue curve has twice the slope of demand; using the demand curve as MR is a slip to avoid.

**6.** The workers' skills are specific to the plant and do not transfer to care work, which is occupational immobility [1]. They remain unemployed while the vacancies remain unfilled [1]. Output and incomes are lost [1]. Labour is not allocated to where it is valued, so the market misallocates resources [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the scenario (plant skills, care vacancies) rather than a general definition of immobility.

**7. (a)** Price change ≈ quantity change ÷ elasticity [1] = 12 ÷ 0.5 = +24% [1].
**(b)** Revenue index = 1.24 × 0.88 = 1.0912 [1], so revenue rises about 9.1% [1].
**(c)** Price falls about 24% [1]. Revenue index = 0.76 × 1.12 = 0.8512, a fall of about 14.9% [1].
**(d)** Revenue swings from +9.1% to −14.9%, so growers' incomes are unstable [1]. This uncertainty discourages investment and planning, so resources are misallocated [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the percentage price estimate is a rough guide; it is not an exact result.

**8. (a)** Assets = 210,000 + 9,000 + 7,000 = 226,000 [1]. Debts = 120,000 + 6,000 = 126,000, so net wealth = 100,000 [1].
**(b)** Income is a flow of receipts over a period, here 34,000 a year [1]. Wealth is what the household owns net of what it owes at one moment, here 100,000 [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Flow" and "stock" earn the distinction; give the figures to show you apply it.

**9. (a)** 55 ÷ 4 [1] = 13.75 times [1].
**(b)** Poorest 40% = 4 + 8 = 12% [1]. Richest 40% = 20 + 55 = 75% [1].
**(c)** They measure the degree of inequality but cannot show whether it is fair, because equity is a value judgement [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Interpret the number in a sentence; a bare ratio gives you little to build on.

**10.** Equality means everyone has the same amount [1]. Equity means fairness or justice, which involves a value judgement [1]. Example: equal pay for a nurse who works long hours and a person who works few hours is equal but many would say unfair [1]. So an equal outcome is not automatically equitable, and an unequal one is not automatically inequitable [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A distinction needs both terms defined and one example; a definition of only one term is incomplete.

**11.** Consumption depends on income and wealth rather than need [1]. Low-income households may be unable to afford education or healthcare even where the benefit exceeds the cost [1]. Resources then go to goods that richer households can pay for, which is a misallocation [1]. Economic welfare is affected by the distribution [1]. Against: an unequal distribution is not necessarily inequitable, because fairness is a value judgement and some inequality rewards skill and effort [1]. Redistribution may weaken incentives, though extreme inequality can waste talent [1]. The market failure is strongest where low income blocks access to essentials [1]. Judgement: the case rests on the value judgement about fairness and on how unequal the distribution is [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Weigh the two sides and reach a verdict that refers back to the question; a list of points on one side does not evaluate.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating that a good is a merit good without saying consumers undervalue it.
- Treating every consumption externality as making a good a merit or demerit good.
- Using the demand curve as the marginal revenue curve in monopoly calculations.
- Giving the formula but not the units or the direction of the change.
- Treating the percentage price estimate from elasticity as exact.
- Writing "equal" where the answer needs "equitable".
- Describing a policy fix when the question asks why the market fails.
- Finishing an evaluation by repeating points without reaching a verdict.
