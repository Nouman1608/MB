## Key business terms (9625)

Businesses turn **inputs** into **outputs**. The inputs are land, labour, capital and enterprise. The **transformation process** is whatever the business does to them. Outputs are **goods** (physical) and **services** (not physical). **Adding value** means the output is worth more to customers than the cost of the inputs used.

**Strategy** is the long-term plan to reach objectives. **Tactics** are the short-term actions that carry it out. **Profit** is revenue minus costs. **Opportunity cost** is the value of the best alternative forgone when you choose. **Efficiency** means producing more from unchanged inputs, or the same from less. **Competitiveness** is how well a business does against rivals, for example on price, quality or service. **Risk** is the chance that an outcome is worse than planned.

**Owners** put in capital and take the risk. **Managers** run the business day to day, so in a large firm the two groups are different people. Managers organise the **business functions** (for example marketing, operations, human resources and finance), set targets and monitor results using **key performance indicators (KPIs)**, which are measures such as sales growth or customer complaints. **Customers** buy from the business. **Consumers** use the product and may be someone else, such as a child. **Ethical influences** are beliefs about right and wrong that shape a decision even when a cheaper option exists.

## Entrepreneurs (both)

An **entrepreneur** sets up and takes the risk of a new business.

**Motivation (both).** 9725 names financial and non-financial motivations; 9625 says only motivation to set up in business. Our examples: higher income or wealth; independence, using a talent, flexible hours, a passion, helping others.

**Characteristics (both; 9725 names them).** Risk taker, resilient, hard working, creative, passionate, innovative, adaptable.

**Identifying an opportunity (9625).** Look for an unmet need, a gap in the market, a problem that annoys customers, or a new way to do something.

**Challenges (both).** Raising capital, uncertain demand, cash-flow shortages, long hours, thin experience of every function, established rivals and personal financial risk. **Why many start-ups fail (9625, our wording):** poor cash-flow control, too little market research, too little capital, weak management skills, and underestimating costs.

**Role changes as the business grows (9725).** At first the entrepreneur does everything: selling, buying, banking. As the firm grows, the role moves toward leadership and delegation. Managers are hired for functions, and the entrepreneur spends more time on strategy.

**Environment and government (9625).** The business environment of a country influences how many entrepreneurs succeed: access to finance, taxes, regulation, infrastructure, skills, stability and demand. Governments may support start-ups because new firms create jobs, competition and tax revenue. Support includes grants, cheap loans, tax breaks, advice and training. **Factors influencing the number and type of start-ups** include the state of the economy, how easy it is to borrow, attitudes to risk, and the skills available.

## Business objectives and SMART (both)

Typical objectives are **survival**, **profit**, **growth** and **cash flow** (both specifications), **social** objectives (both), and personal and ethical objectives (9625).

Effective objectives are **SMART**. The 9725 text spells this out as Specific, Measurable, Accountable, Realistic, Time specific. "Raise sales by 8% by 31 December, owned by the sales manager" can be checked; "do better this year" cannot.

**The value of setting objectives:** direction, measurable success, motivation and a basis for choosing. Objectives can conflict: growth can drain cash.

## Business plans (both)

A **business plan** sets out what a business intends to do and how. The 9725 key elements are ownership, objectives, target market and market analysis, competitor analysis, unique selling point (USP) and financial analysis.

**Benefits:** it forces careful thinking, shows weaknesses early, helps win loans or investment. **Limitations:** it takes time, rests on forecasts that can be wrong, can become out of date, and a good plan does not guarantee success. 

**Competitiveness** (both; 9725 adds meaning and significance) is how well a firm can win customers against rivals. A USP, lower costs or better service improve it.

## Why business matters (9625)

Business, including start-ups, creates **jobs and incomes**, produces goods and services people want, pays **taxes**, and can raise **economic growth**. It can also cause pollution, congestion or the closure of local shops. Effects on stakeholders are covered below.

## Decision trees, expected value and net gain (9625)

A **decision tree** maps choices (a square) and uncertain outcomes (a circle, with probabilities adding to 1). **Expected value** = the sum of probability x outcome. **Net gain** = expected value minus the cost of that option. These are the standard forms.

Worked example. Hazelquay Boatyard can build a new workshop for £120,000 or refit the old one for £40,000. Demand is high with probability 0.7 and low with probability 0.3.

```
New workshop: high £260,000, low £90,000
  EV = 0.7 x 260,000 + 0.3 x 90,000 = 182,000 + 27,000 = £209,000
  Net gain = 209,000 - 120,000 = £89,000
Refit: high £110,000, low £60,000
  EV = 0.7 x 110,000 + 0.3 x 60,000 = 77,000 + 18,000 = £95,000
  Net gain = 95,000 - 40,000 = £55,000
```

The new workshop has the higher net gain. But in the low-demand case it returns £90,000 on £120,000, a loss of £30,000, while the refit still gains £20,000. If the yard cannot survive a loss, the refit is safer. **Limitations of trees:** guessed probabilities, no non-financial factors or ethics, and an average that may never happen.

## Types of business (3.1.1.2)

| Type | Owner(s) and liability | Finance and profit | Spec |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sole trader | One owner, unlimited liability | Own savings, loans, retained profit; owner keeps all profit | both |
| Partnership | Two or more owners, usually unlimited liability | Partners' capital; profit shared | 9725 |
| Private limited company (Ltd) | Shareholders with limited liability; shares not sold to the public | Can sell shares privately; dividends | both |
| Public limited company (plc) | Limited liability; shares can be bought by the public | Large sums from share issues; dividends | both |
| Social enterprise, charity or other non-profit | Social aim first; surplus reinvested | Grants, donations, trading income | both |
| Co-operative | Owned by members | Members' capital; profit shared among members | 9625 |

**Unlimited liability** means the owner's own assets can be called on to settle business debts. **Limited liability** means shareholders risk no more than the money they put in.

**Compare forms using:** objectives, finance, distribution of profits and liability (both), and control (9725).

**Reasons for choosing or changing form:** wanting full control, protecting personal assets, needing more capital, planning growth, or seeking social impact. A fast-growing sole trader may become a Ltd to limit liability and attract investors. A Ltd may become a plc to raise large sums, but accepts more regulation, public accounts and takeover risk.

**Advantages and disadvantages.** Sole trader: easy to start, full control and profit, but unlimited liability and limited funds. Partnership: more capital and skills, but disagreements and unlimited liability. Ltd: limited liability, but less control and shared profit. plc: large funds, but loss of control, scrutiny and short-term pressure. Social enterprise: purpose and goodwill, but lower profit and harder fund-raising.

**Private sector** organisations belong to individuals or shareholders. **Public sector** organisations are government-owned and government-run. Objectives differ: profit versus public service. A public limited company is private sector.

**Sectors.** **Primary** extracts natural resources (farming, mining). **Secondary** makes or builds (manufacturing, construction). **Tertiary** provides services (retail, banking, teaching).

**Influences on the business population (9625, our wording):** the economy, ease of raising finance, regulation, attitudes to risk and skills.

**Family business** (both): advantages are loyalty, shared values, long-term thinking and quick decisions. Disadvantages are conflict, limited finance and skills, and difficult succession. **Small business:** flexible, personal service and close customer contact, but limited finance, little buying power and weaker brand. In 9625 you also compare small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) with large businesses, which gain economies of scale, finance and market power but can be slower and less personal.

## Shareholders (both)

Shareholders invest to receive **dividends** (a share of profit paid out), to gain if the **share price** rises, and sometimes for a say in decisions or because they back the business's aims. **Share capital (equity)** is the money raised by selling shares. Ordinary shares usually carry votes and a dividend that varies with profit (standard definition).

**Influences on share price:** company profit and forecasts, dividends, the economy, interest rates, rival performance, news and rumours, and supply and demand for the shares. Rises help shareholders and the firm's ability to raise capital; falls can bring takeover risk.

Standard forms (9725 asks for all three calculations; 9625 names yield calculation and market capitalisation):

```
Dividend per share = total dividend / number of shares
Dividend yield (%) = dividend per share / share price x 100
Market capitalisation = share price x number of shares issued
```

Worked example. Oakreach Foods plc has 8,000,000 ordinary shares, pays a total dividend of £1,200,000, and its share price is £3.00.

```
Dividend per share = 1,200,000 / 8,000,000 = £0.15 (15p)
Dividend yield = 0.15 / 3.00 x 100 = 5%
Market capitalisation = 3.00 x 8,000,000 = £24,000,000
```

If the price rises to £3.60 and the dividend stays at 15p, the yield falls to 0.15 / 3.60 x 100 = 4.2% (1 d.p.) and market capitalisation rises to £28,800,000.

## Stakeholders (both)

**Internal:** owners and shareholders, employees. **External:** customers, suppliers, the community, the government and, in 9725, creditors (those owed money by the firm). Stakeholders have different interests: shareholders want returns, employees want pay and security, suppliers want prompt payment, the community wants jobs without disruption, the government wants tax and legal compliance. To show the **impact of business activity**, name the stakeholder, the change and the effect.

## Common errors

- Writing "limited liability means limited debts". The firm owes the whole debt, but the shareholder's loss is limited.
- Confusing a public limited company with the public sector.
- Stopping a decision-tree answer at expected value, without taking off the cost.
- Using partnerships in a 9625 answer, or co-operatives in a 9725 answer, without checking your specification.
