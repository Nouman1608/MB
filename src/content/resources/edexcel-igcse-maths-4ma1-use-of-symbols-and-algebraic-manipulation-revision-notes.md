## Key words (2.1A, 2.1B)

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Term | A number, letter or product of them | 5x²y |
| Expression | Terms joined by + or −, no equals sign | 3a − 2b + 7 |
| Equation | True only for certain values of the letter | 2x + 5 = 11 |
| Formula | A rule linking variables | P = 2(l + w) |
| Variable | A letter that can take many values | r in A = πr² |
| Coefficient | The number multiplying a letter | −4 in −4x |
| Like terms | Same letters, same powers | 3ab and −ab |

Algebra obeys the rules of arithmetic: a + b = b + a, ab = ba, a(b + c) = ab + ac. Subtraction and division do **not** swap: a − b ≠ b − a in general.

## Indices

### Integer powers (both tiers, 2.1C, 2.1D)

| Rule | Formula |
|---|---|
| Multiply | aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ |
| Divide | aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ |
| Power of a power | (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ |
| Zero | a⁰ = 1 |
| Negative | a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ |

The laws need the **same base**. With numbers in front, multiply the numbers and add the powers: 4x³ × 2x⁻¹ = 8x².

### Fractional powers -- Higher tier only (2.1A)

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a^(1/n) | nth root of a | 27^(1/3) = 3 |
| a^(m/n) | (nth root of a)ᵐ | 27^(2/3) = 3² = 9 |
| a^(−m/n) | 1 ÷ a^(m/n) | 4^(−1/2) = 1/2 |

> **Method: evaluating a^(−m/n)**
> 1. Negative sign: take the reciprocal (flip a fraction).
> 2. Denominator n: take the nth root.
> 3. Numerator m: raise to the power m.

## Substitution (2.2A)

> **Method**
> 1. Write the expression with each letter replaced by its value **in brackets**.
> 2. Powers first, then × and ÷, then + and −.
> 3. Watch signs: (−3)² = 9, but −3² = −9.

Worked reminder: find (a − 2b)/c when a = 3.5, b = −1.25 and c = 4.

```
(3.5 - 2(-1.25)) / 4 = (3.5 + 2.5) / 4 = 6 / 4 = 1.5
```

Subtracting a negative adds. Work out the whole numerator before dividing, because the fraction line acts as a bracket.

## Brackets and factors (2.2B to 2.2F)

| Task | Reminder | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Collect like terms | Only add terms with identical letters and powers | 4ab + 2a − ab = 3ab + 2a |
| Single bracket | Multiply every term inside | −2y(3y − 5) = −6y² + 10y |
| Common factor | Take out the HCF of numbers **and** letters | 14x²y + 21xy = 7xy(2x + 3) |
| Two brackets | Each term × each term (4 products) | (x + 5)(x − 5) = x² − 25 |
| x² + bx + c | Two numbers: product c, sum b | x² − x − 12 = (x − 4)(x + 3) |

### Sign patterns for x² + bx + c

| c | b | Numbers |
|---|---|---|
| positive | positive | both positive |
| positive | negative | both negative |
| negative | either | one of each; the larger has the sign of b |

## Higher tier only (2.2A to 2.2E)

### Three or more brackets

> **Method**
> 1. Expand any two brackets and simplify.
> 2. Multiply the result by the next bracket (6 products for a quadratic × linear).
> 3. Collect like terms, highest power first.
> 4. Check with x = 1 or x = 2 in both forms.

### Factorising ax² + bx + c

> **Method**
> 1. Take out any common factor first.
> 2. Find two numbers with product **ac** and sum **b**.
> 3. Split bx using those numbers.
> 4. Factorise each pair; the brackets must match.
> 5. Expand to check.

Difference of two squares: p² − q² = (p − q)(p + q). It has no middle term.

### Algebraic fractions

| Task | Steps |
|---|---|
| Simplify | Factorise top and bottom fully, cancel whole brackets |
| Add or subtract | Common denominator, put each numerator in brackets, expand, collect |
| Multiply | Factorise everything, cancel, multiply tops and bottoms |
| Divide | Flip the second fraction, then multiply |

Numeric denominators work the same way: use the lowest common multiple (for 4 and 6, use 12).

### Completing the square

x² + bx + c = (x + b/2)² − (b/2)² + c

a(x² + …): take a out of the x² and x terms only, complete the square inside, then multiply the subtracted number by a.

Small reminder: x² + 6x − 2 = (x + 3)² − 9 − 2 = (x + 3)² − 11.

| Object | Write as (n an integer) |
|---|---|
| Even number | 2n |
| Odd number | 2n + 1 |
| Consecutive integers | n, n + 1 |

## Worked reminders (Higher tier only)

These are short, so you can redo them from memory before an exam.

**Numeric denominators.** Write (2x − 1)/5 + (x + 3)/2 as a single fraction.

```
LCM of 5 and 2 is 10
[2(2x - 1) + 5(x + 3)] / 10
= (4x - 2 + 5x + 15) / 10
= (9x + 13) / 10
```

**Dividing.** Simplify (x + 4)/(x² − 1) ÷ (2x + 8)/(x + 1).

```
flip and multiply:  (x + 4)/[(x - 1)(x + 1)] × (x + 1)/[2(x + 4)]
cancel (x + 4) and (x + 1):  1 / [2(x - 1)]
```

**Choosing a method.** Before factorising any quadratic, ask in this order: is there a common factor? Is it a difference of two squares? Is the x² coefficient 1? Each "yes" points to a quicker route than splitting the middle term.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Expand vs factorise.** Expand removes brackets; factorise puts them in. They undo each other.
- **x² + 25 vs x² − 25.** Only the difference factorises: (x − 5)(x + 5). A sum of squares does not.
- **(x + 5)² vs x² + 25.** (x + 5)² = x² + 10x + 25.
- **a⁻² vs −a².** a⁻² = 1/a² is positive when a ≠ 0; −a² is never positive.
- **Factor vs term.** In (x + 2)(x − 1), (x + 2) is a factor and can cancel. In x + 2, the x is a term and cannot.
- **Partly vs fully factorised.** 4(3x² − 6x) is not fully factorised; 12x(x − 2) is.

## Quick self-test

1. Simplify a⁻³ × a⁸.
2. Write down the value of 9⁰ and of 9⁻¹.
3. (Higher) Work out 16^(−3/4).
4. Find the value of 2x² − 5x when x = −3.
5. Factorise fully 12pq − 18q².
6. Expand and simplify (2y − 5)(y + 4).
7. Factorise x² + x − 42.
8. (Higher) Factorise 6x² + 7x − 3.
9. (Higher) Simplify (x² − 6x + 9)/(x² − 9).
10. (Higher) Write x² + 7x + 2 in the form (x + p)² + q.
11. (Higher) Expand and simplify (x − 2)³.
12. (Higher) Prove that (2n + 1)² − 1 is a multiple of 4 for every integer n.

### Answers

1. a⁻³⁺⁸ = **a⁵**
2. **9⁰ = 1** and **9⁻¹ = 1/9**
3. Reciprocal 1/16^(3/4); fourth root of 16 is 2; 2³ = 8; answer **1/8**
4. 2(−3)² − 5(−3) = 18 + 15 = **33**
5. **6q(2p − 3q)**
6. 2y² + 8y − 5y − 20 = **2y² + 3y − 20**
7. Product −42, sum +1: +7 and −6, so **(x + 7)(x − 6)**
8. ac = −18, sum 7: 9 and −2. 6x² + 9x − 2x − 3 = 3x(2x + 3) − (2x + 3) = **(3x − 1)(2x + 3)**
9. (x − 3)(x − 3) / [(x − 3)(x + 3)] = **(x − 3)/(x + 3)**
10. (x + 3.5)² − 12.25 + 2 = **(x + 3.5)² − 10.25**
11. (x − 2)² = x² − 4x + 4; × (x − 2) gives **x³ − 6x² + 12x − 8**
12. 4n² + 4n + 1 − 1 = 4n² + 4n = **4n(n + 1)**, which is 4 × an integer, so it is a multiple of 4.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing x³ × x⁴ = x¹² (multiplying powers instead of adding them).
- Giving 5⁰ = 0 or 5⁻² = −25.
- Substituting a negative number without brackets, so −3² is worked out as −9 instead of 9.
- Stopping at a partial factorisation such as 3(4x²y − 6xy²) when the question says "factorise fully".
- Dropping the middle term when squaring a bracket: (x − 6)² written as x² + 36 or x² − 36.
- Factorising ax² + bx + c with brackets that expand to the right x² and constant terms but the wrong middle term. Always expand to check.
- Cancelling terms instead of factors in algebraic fractions, for example crossing out x² in (x² + 3x)/(x² − 9).
- Forgetting to subtract every term of the second numerator: −(2x − 5) must become −2x + 5.
- Completing the square on 2x² − 8x + 1 as 2(x − 2)² − 4 + 1. The −4 must be multiplied by 2, giving 2(x − 2)² − 7.
- In a proof, checking a few numbers and calling it proved, or showing the algebra without the concluding sentence.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which sections of Edexcel IGCSE Maths 4MA1 cover algebraic manipulation?**
Section 2.1 (Use of symbols) and section 2.2 (Algebraic manipulation) in Topic 2 cover index notation, substitution, expanding and factorising. Fractional powers, three brackets, factorising ax² + bx + c, algebraic fractions, completing the square and proof are Higher tier only.

**What are like terms?**
Like terms have exactly the same letters with the same powers, so they can be collected. x²y and xy² are not like terms, so 5x²y + 2x²y = 7x²y, but x²y and xy² stay separate.

**How do you expand three brackets?**
Expand two of the brackets first and simplify, then multiply every term of the result by each term in the third bracket before collecting like terms. Losing a term at this stage is a common error, so count your products before simplifying; this is Higher tier content.

**What does a fractional power mean?**
A fractional power means a root: a^(1/n) is the nth root of a, and a^(m/n) means take the nth root and then raise it to the power m. For example, 64^(1/3) = 4 and 32^(3/5) = 8.
