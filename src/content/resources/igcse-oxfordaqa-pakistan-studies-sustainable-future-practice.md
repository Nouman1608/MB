**What do these questions practise?** The three key developments of optional topic 3.2.4 in
OxfordAQA International GCSE Pakistan Studies 9236: environmental issues, managing climate change,
and supporting development and equality. Answers are written as mark-scheme points so you can check
where marks are earned.

## Short answer

**1.** State **two** causes of air pollution in Pakistan's cities. **[2]**

**2.** Define *desertification*. **[1]**

**3.** Name **one** tree-planting programme in Pakistan. **[1]**

**4.** State the difference between climate change *mitigation* and *adaptation*. **[2]**

**5.** Name the international agreement that aims to limit global warming to well below 2°C. **[1]**

**6.** Name **one** province or region of Pakistan with low levels of development. **[1]**

## Explain

**7.** Explain **two** impacts of water pollution on people in Pakistan. **[4]**

**8.** Explain why over-grazing can lead to desertification. **[3]**

**9.** Explain why Pakistan is vulnerable to climate change even though it produces a small share of
global emissions. **[4]**

**10.** Explain how early warning systems can reduce the impacts of floods. **[3]**

**11.** Explain **two** strategies used to improve girls' education in Pakistan. **[4]**

**12.** Describe **one** local and **one** national strategy used to protect biodiversity in
Pakistan. **[4]**

**13.** Explain why some regions of Pakistan are less developed than others. **[4]**

## Evaluate

**14.** "Adaptation is more important than mitigation for Pakistan's response to climate change."
Evaluate this view. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: vehicle exhaust [1]; brick kilns [1]; factory emissions [1]; burning crop stubble [1];
burning rubbish [1].

**2.** The spread of desert-like conditions into dry areas, caused by climate and human misuse of
land [1].

**3.** Billion Tree Tsunami or Ten Billion Tree Tsunami [1].

**4.** Mitigation reduces the causes of climate change, for example by cutting greenhouse gas
emissions [1]; adaptation reduces the harm it causes, for example by building flood defences [1].

**5.** The Paris Agreement [1].

**6.** Balochistan, or the former tribal districts now in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa [1].

**7.** Any two developed: drinking contaminated water spreads diseases such as diarrhoea and typhoid
[1], which are especially dangerous for young children [1]; polluted rivers and coastal water harm
fish [1], reducing catches and fishermen's incomes [1]; farmers irrigating with polluted water may
contaminate crops [1], affecting health and sales [1].

**8.** Too many animals eat grass and shrubs faster than they can regrow [1]. The bare soil is
exposed to wind and occasional heavy rain, which remove the fertile topsoil [1]. Without vegetation
and topsoil the land cannot hold moisture and becomes desert-like [1].

**9.** Its rivers depend on glaciers and snowmelt in the Karakoram and Himalaya, which are melting
[1]. Agriculture relies on monsoon rain, which is becoming more extreme [1]. Summers are already very
hot, so heatwaves are dangerous [1]. Many poor people live on floodplains or in the low-lying Indus
delta and have few resources to recover from disasters [1].

**10.** Forecasts and warnings give people time to move to safety with their animals and belongings
[1]. Authorities can prepare shelters, food and rescue teams in advance [1]. Fewer people die and
less property is lost [1].

**11.** Any two developed: stipends or cash payments to families for girls who attend school [1],
offsetting the cost and loss of help at home [1]; building schools close to villages [1], so girls do
not have to travel long distances [1]; recruiting female teachers [1], which reassures parents [1];
non-formal or online classes [1] for girls who have missed school [1].

**12.** Local: community trophy-hunting schemes in the north, where income from licensed hunting
of markhor is shared with villages that protect the animals and their habitat [1–2]. National:
national parks and protected areas such as Khunjerab or Margalla Hills, where hunting, logging and
building are restricted [1–2].

**13.** Physical factors: mountains, deserts and lack of water in Balochistan and the north make
farming and building infrastructure difficult [1]. Economic factors: industry and services are
concentrated in Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad, so jobs and investment are there [1]. Social
factors: fewer schools and hospitals in remote areas mean lower literacy and health [1]. Political
factors: insecurity and historical neglect, for example in the former tribal areas, discouraged
investment [1].

**14.** Level-marked. For adaptation: Pakistan's emissions are a very small share of the global
total, so cutting them alone will not stop climate change [1–2]; impacts such as the 2022 floods and
heatwaves are already happening and kill people now [1–2]; early warnings, flood defences and
heatwave plans save lives quickly [1]. For mitigation: renewable energy reduces costly fuel imports
and air pollution as well as emissions [1–2]; reforestation also reduces floods and erosion [1];
Pakistan's emissions will rise as its population and economy grow [1]. Top-level answers reach a
supported judgement, for example that adaptation is the more urgent priority but mitigation brings
extra economic and health benefits, so both are needed. Maximum 9.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which named examples work best?**
The 2022 floods, the Billion Tree Tsunami, Lahore's smog, the
National Adaptation Plan and the Benazir Income Support Programme cover most of the topic.

**Should I learn the 2022 flood figures?**
Know that the floods killed more than 1,700 people and
that damage and losses were estimated at about US$30 billion; use any figures given in the paper.
