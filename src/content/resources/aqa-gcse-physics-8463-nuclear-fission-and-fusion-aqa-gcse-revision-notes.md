## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nuclear fission | The splitting of a large and unstable nucleus, such as uranium or plutonium |
| Spontaneous fission | Fission without a neutron being absorbed first; rare |
| Fission products | The two smaller nuclei and the neutrons that fly apart; all have kinetic energy |
| Chain reaction | Neutrons released by one fission cause further fissions, which release more neutrons |
| Controlled chain reaction | Kept at a steady rate in a nuclear reactor, to control the energy released |
| Uncontrolled chain reaction | Grows rapidly; the cause of the explosion of a nuclear weapon |
| Nuclear fusion | The joining of two light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus |
| Mass converted | In fusion, some of the mass may be converted into the energy of radiation |

## Fission in four lines

```
1  neutron absorbed by a large unstable nucleus (U or Pu)
2  nucleus splits into two smaller nuclei of roughly equal size
3  two or three neutrons emitted, plus gamma rays
4  energy released; all fission products have kinetic energy
```

Learn these in order. A six-mark description that misses step 1 or step 3 rarely reaches the top level.

## Balancing a fission equation

```
Step 1  write the absorbed neutron on the left: ¹₀n
Step 2  add the mass numbers on each side; the difference is the number of neutrons released
Step 3  check the atomic numbers balance (neutrons have atomic number 0)
```

*Worked reminder.* Plutonium-239 (atomic number 94) absorbs a neutron and splits into barium-144 (56) and a
second nucleus with mass number 93, releasing 3 neutrons. Find the second nucleus's atomic number.

```
²³⁹₉₄Pu + ¹₀n → ¹⁴⁴₅₆Ba + (mass number 93, atomic number x) + 3 ¹₀n
mass check: 239 + 1 = 240;  144 + 93 + 3 = 240 ✓
atomic numbers: 94 = 56 + x   →   x = 38 (strontium)
```

## Chain reaction numbers

| Fissions caused by each fission | Generation 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (controlled) | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 |
| 3 | 1 | 3 | 9 | 27 | 81 |

- Multiply by the same number each generation.
- To keep a reactor steady, all but one of the released neutrons from each fission must be absorbed or
  escape without causing fission.
- The energy released per second follows the number of fissions per second.

## Controlled vs uncontrolled

| | Reactor | Weapon |
|---|---|---|
| Chain reaction | Controlled | Uncontrolled |
| Neutrons going on to cause fission | On average one per fission | More than one per fission |
| Result | Steady release of energy | Very rapid release of energy: an explosion |

## Fusion

- Two **light** nuclei join to make one **heavier** nucleus.
- Some mass may be converted into the energy of radiation, so the products have slightly less mass.
- Example: ²₁H + ³₁H → ⁴₂He + ¹₀n (top: 2 + 3 = 4 + 1; bottom: 1 + 1 = 2 + 0).
- Stars are powered by fusion: a main sequence star fuses hydrogen into helium.

## Must-know contrasts

| | Fission | Fusion |
|---|---|---|
| Change to nuclei | Splits one large nucleus | Joins two light nuclei |
| Starting nuclei | Uranium, plutonium | Hydrogen isotopes, for example |
| Needs a neutron absorbed? | Usually yes | No |
| Products | Two smaller nuclei + 2 or 3 neutrons + gamma | One heavier nucleus |
| Energy released? | Yes | Yes |

## Reading a chain reaction diagram

- Each arrow that ends at a large nucleus which then splits is a neutron that **continues** the chain.
- An arrow that leaves the diagram, or ends at a nucleus that does not split, is a neutron that is **lost**
  or **absorbed**.
- Count the fissions in each row of the diagram to find the generation sizes, then say whether the numbers
  stay steady (controlled) or grow (uncontrolled).

## Exam traps

- "The atom splits." Write **the nucleus splits**.
- "Fission releases a neutron." It releases **two or three**.
- Leaving out the **gamma rays** from the list of products.
- Forgetting that the products have **kinetic energy**.
- Confusing chain reaction "controlled" with "stopped".
- Mixing up which process joins and which splits. Fusion: joins, light. Fission: splits, heavy.
- Treating the products of fission as random sizes; the specification says **roughly equal**.
- Counting neutrons in a fission equation without including the absorbed neutron.

## Quick self-test

1. Name two elements whose nuclei can undergo fission.
2. What must usually happen to a nucleus before it undergoes fission?
3. List everything produced when a uranium-235 nucleus undergoes fission.
4. What type of energy do all the fission products have?
5. Explain what is meant by a chain reaction.
6. Each fission causes two more. How many fissions are there in the fifth generation?
7. In a reactor each fission releases 3 neutrons. How many, on average, must not go on to cause fission to
   keep the reaction steady?
8. Why is the chain reaction in a nuclear reactor controlled?
9. What causes the explosion of a nuclear weapon?
10. ²³⁵₉₂U + ¹₀n → ¹³⁷₅₅Cs + ⁹⁷₃₇Rb + x ¹₀n. Find x.
11. Define nuclear fusion.
12. In fusion, what happens to some of the mass?

### Answers

1. Uranium and plutonium.
2. It must absorb a neutron.
3. Two smaller nuclei of roughly equal size, two or three neutrons, gamma rays (and energy is released).
4. Kinetic energy.
5. Neutrons released by one fission are absorbed by other nuclei, causing further fissions that release more
   neutrons.
6. 1, 2, 4, 8, **16**.
7. 3 − 1 = **2**.
8. To control the energy released, so it is released at a steady rate.
9. An uncontrolled chain reaction.
10. 235 + 1 = 236; 137 + 97 = 234; x = **2**. (Check: 55 + 37 = 92 ✓)
11. The joining of two light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus.
12. It may be converted into the energy of radiation.
