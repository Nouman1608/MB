## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reversible reaction | A reaction that can go in both directions, shown by ⇌ |
| Hydrated | Chemically combined with water |
| Anhydrous | Containing no water |
| Closed system *(Extended)* | Nothing can enter or leave |
| Equilibrium *(Extended)* | Forward rate = reverse rate; concentrations no longer changing |
| Dynamic *(Extended)* | Both reactions continue at equilibrium |
| Position of equilibrium *(Extended)* | The relative amounts of reactants and products at equilibrium |

## Core: hydrated and anhydrous compounds

```
CuSO4·5H2O ⇌ CuSO4 + 5H2O        blue ⇌ white
CoCl2·6H2O ⇌ CoCl2 + 6H2O        pink ⇌ blue
```

- **Heat** the hydrated solid → water driven off → anhydrous form.
- **Add water** to the anhydrous solid → hydrated form; the mixture warms up (heat released).
- Syllabus limits this to copper(II) sulfate and cobalt(II) chloride.
- Test for water: white anhydrous CuSO₄ turns blue; blue anhydrous CoCl₂ turns pink.

## Equilibrium *(Extended)*

Two conditions, both needed in a definition:

1. Rate of forward reaction = rate of reverse reaction.
2. Concentrations of reactants and products no longer change.

Concentrations are **constant**, not equal. Reactions have **not** stopped.

## Predicting shifts *(Extended)*

| Change | Equilibrium shifts towards... |
|---|---|
| Temperature increased | Endothermic direction |
| Temperature decreased | Exothermic direction |
| Pressure increased | Side with fewer moles of **gas** |
| Pressure decreased | Side with more moles of gas |
| Concentration of a reactant increased | Products (right) |
| Product removed | Products (right) |
| Catalyst added | No change in position; equilibrium reached faster |

Equal moles of gas on each side → pressure has no effect on position.

**Four-step method:** identify the change → find the information (ΔH sign, gas moles) → apply the rule → state the effect on yield or colour.

## Haber process *(Extended)*

```
N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ 2NH3(g)
```

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen source | Air |
| Hydrogen source | Methane |
| Temperature | 450 °C |
| Pressure | 20 000 kPa / 200 atm |
| Catalyst | Iron |
| Forward reaction | Exothermic; 4 mol gas → 2 mol gas |

## Contact process step *(Extended)*

```
2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g)
```

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Sulfur dioxide source | Burning sulfur or roasting sulfide ores |
| Oxygen source | Air |
| Temperature | 450 °C |
| Pressure | 200 kPa / 2 atm |
| Catalyst | Vanadium(V) oxide |
| Forward reaction | Exothermic; 3 mol gas → 2 mol gas |

## Why these conditions *(Extended)*

- **Temperature, both processes:** lower temperature → higher yield (forward exothermic) but slower rate. 450 °C is a compromise.
- **Haber pressure:** higher pressure → higher yield (fewer gas moles on right) and faster rate, but high pressure is dangerous and equipment is expensive. 200 atm is a compromise.
- **Contact pressure:** equilibrium already lies well to the right at low pressure, so higher pressure would add cost for little extra yield. 2 atm is used.
- **Catalyst:** increases rate so equilibrium is reached faster at a moderate temperature; does not change the yield.

## Exam traps

- Don't say "the reaction stops" at equilibrium.
- Don't say concentrations become equal.
- Count only **gaseous** moles for pressure arguments.
- A catalyst never changes the equilibrium position or the yield.
- Cobalt(II) chloride: **pink** hydrated, **blue** anhydrous. Copper(II) sulfate: **blue** hydrated, **white** anhydrous.
- 20 000 kPa and 200 kPa: don't swap the Haber and Contact pressures.
- Condition answers need both a **rate** point and a **position** point.

## Self-test

1. What does the symbol ⇌ show?
2. Describe what you see when blue hydrated copper(II) sulfate is heated.
3. State the colour change when water is added to anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride.
4. *(Extended)* State the two conditions for a reaction to be at equilibrium.
5. *(Extended)* For a reaction with an exothermic forward reaction, what is the effect of raising the temperature on the yield of product?
6. *(Extended)* For 2A(g) + B(g) ⇌ 2C(g), what is the effect of raising the pressure on the amount of C?
7. *(Extended)* What effect does a catalyst have on the position of equilibrium?
8. *(Extended)* State the conditions used in the Haber process.
9. *(Extended)* Give the sources of the raw materials for the Haber process.
10. *(Extended)* Name the catalyst in the Contact process and state the pressure used.
11. *(Extended)* Why is 450 °C described as a compromise temperature?
12. *(Extended)* Why is a pressure of only 2 atm used in the Contact process?

**Answers**

1. The reaction is reversible.
2. It turns from blue to white, and water vapour/steam is given off.
3. Blue to pink.
4. Forward and reverse rates are equal; concentrations of reactants and products no longer change (in a closed system).
5. The yield decreases (the equilibrium shifts towards the reactants).
6. The amount of C increases (3 mol gas → 2 mol gas, so the right is favoured).
7. None; it only makes equilibrium reached faster.
8. 450 °C, 20 000 kPa (200 atm), iron catalyst.
9. Nitrogen from air; hydrogen from methane.
10. Vanadium(V) oxide; 200 kPa (2 atm).
11. A lower temperature would give a higher yield but too slow a rate; 450 °C gives a reasonable yield at an acceptable rate.
12. The equilibrium already lies far to the right, so higher pressure would give little extra yield for the extra cost.
