These questions cover sub-topic **7.1 Diet** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. All of 7.1 is Core, so every question suits both Core and Extended candidates. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. Food data in the questions are invented for practice.

Revise first with the [diet study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-diet/) and the [diet revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-diet-revision-notes/). The topic-level [human nutrition practice questions](/resources/igcse-biology-human-nutrition-practice/) cover digestion and absorption as well.

## Section A

**1.** Describe what is meant by a balanced diet. **[2]**

**2.** Which nutrient is needed to make haemoglobin?

A: calcium

B: iron

C: vitamin C

D: vitamin D **[1]**

**3.** State the principal dietary source of **two** of these: vitamin C, calcium, protein. **[2]**

**4.** State the cause of rickets. **[1]**

**5.** Fibre passes through the alimentary canal without being digested. Explain why fibre is still an important part of the diet. **[2]**

**6.** Give **three** reasons why the body needs water. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** A student lists the meals she ate in one day.

| Meal | Food |
|---|---|
| Breakfast | white toast with butter, cup of tea |
| Lunch | chips and fried chicken, fizzy drink |
| Dinner | white rice and fried egg |

**(a)** Name the nutrient group supplied by the toast, chips and rice.

**(b)** Identify **two** nutrient groups that are missing or very low in her day's food, and explain why each is needed.

**(c)** Suggest **one** food she could add to supply each missing nutrient group you named in (b). **[7]**

**8.** In the past, sailors on long voyages often developed scurvy. Their food was mainly dry biscuits and salted meat.

**(a)** Explain why the sailors developed scurvy.

**(b)** Suggest why taking lemons or limes on the voyage prevented the disease.

**(c)** State **two** signs of scurvy. **[5]**

**9.** The table shows nutrients in 100 g of three foods.

| Per 100 g | Milk | Spinach | Brown rice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protein / g | 3.4 | 2.9 | 2.6 |
| Carbohydrate / g | 4.8 | 3.6 | 23.0 |
| Calcium / mg | 120 | 99 | 10 |
| Iron / mg | 0.0 | 2.7 | 0.4 |
| Fibre / g | 0.0 | 2.2 | 1.8 |

**(a)** Using the table, identify the best source of iron and the best source of calcium.

**(b)** Calculate how much calcium a person would take in from 250 g of milk.

**(c)** Explain why a person who eats no dairy food and no green vegetables may develop weak bones.

**(d)** Suggest why brown rice is a better source of energy than spinach. **[7]**

**10.** A young child lives in a city where she spends almost no time outdoors. Her diet contains very little oily fish, egg or milk.

**(a)** Name the deficiency disease she is at risk of developing.

**(b)** Explain how her lifestyle and her diet both increase the risk.

**(c)** Describe **one** sign of this disease. **[5]**

**11.** Compare the roles of carbohydrates, fats and proteins in the diet. Refer to energy, storage and growth. **[5]**

**12.** Two people need different amounts of nutrients. Person P is a 15-year-old who trains for athletics every day. Person Q is a 70-year-old who walks a little each day.

**(a)** Suggest why P needs more carbohydrate than Q.

**(b)** Suggest why P needs a high protein intake.

**(c)** Q's doctor advises a diet rich in calcium and vitamin D. Explain this advice.

**(d)** Explain why both P and Q are said to need a balanced diet, even though the amounts of food they need are different. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** Contains all the nutrients / all the nutrient groups [1]; in the correct proportions or amounts to stay healthy [1]. **Total: 2 marks**

*Examiner insight:* "Eating all types of food" earns the first mark only; the proportions idea is the second mark.

**2.** **B** (iron) [1]. **Total: 1 mark**

**3.** Any two from: vitamin C: oranges, lemons or other citrus fruit / fresh fruit and vegetables; calcium: milk, cheese or yoghurt; protein: meat, fish, eggs, beans or lentils [2]. **Total: 2 marks**

**4.** Lack of vitamin D in the diet (allow lack of calcium) [1]. **Total: 1 mark**

**5.** It adds bulk to the food in the gut [1]; so the muscles of the gut wall can push food along, preventing constipation [1]. **Total: 2 marks**

*Examiner insight:* An answer saying fibre is "digested slowly" or "gives energy" scores zero.

**6.** Solvent in which chemical reactions take place [1]; transport of substances in blood plasma [1]; removal of waste in urine / cooling the body by sweating [1]. **Total: 3 marks**

**7. (a)** Carbohydrate [1].

**(b)** Any two missing groups, each with its reason: vitamin C (fresh fruit and vegetables), needed for healthy skin and gums and to prevent scurvy; fibre, needed to add bulk so food moves along the gut and constipation is prevented; calcium, needed for strong bones and teeth. One mark for each group named and one for each matching reason [4].

**(c)** A suitable food for each group named in (b): for example oranges for vitamin C, wholemeal bread or vegetables for fibre, milk or cheese for calcium [2]. **Total: 7 marks**

*Examiner insight:* In (b) a reason must describe the nutrient's job; "because it is healthy" does not score.

**8. (a)** Their food contained no fresh fruit or vegetables [1]; so their diet lacked vitamin C [1].

**(b)** Lemons and limes contain vitamin C [1].

**(c)** Any two from: bleeding gums; wounds that heal slowly; bleeding under the skin [2]. **Total: 5 marks**

**9. (a)** Iron: spinach [1]. Calcium: milk [1].

**(b)** 120 × 250 ÷ 100 [1] = **300 mg** [1].

**(c)** Dairy foods and green vegetables are the main sources of calcium [1]; calcium is needed for strong bones, so bones may become weak [1].

**(d)** Brown rice contains much more carbohydrate, 23.0 g compared with 3.6 g per 100 g [1]. **Total: 7 marks**

*Examiner insight:* In (d) quote both values; the comparison is only complete when the figures are given.

**10. (a)** Rickets [1].

**(b)** Little time in sunlight, so little vitamin D is made in her skin [1]; little oily fish or egg in her diet, so little vitamin D is taken in [1]; little milk, so little calcium is taken in [1].

**(c)** Soft or weak bones / bowed legs [1]. **Total: 5 marks**

**11.** Carbohydrates are the main source of energy, as glucose for respiration [1]. Fats and oils also provide energy, with about twice as much per gram as carbohydrate [1]. Fats are a store of energy in the body [1] and provide insulation under the skin [1]. Proteins are used mainly for growth and repair of cells and tissues [1]. **Total: 5 marks**

*Examiner insight:* "Compare" needs statements about more than one nutrient on the same point; listing each nutrient separately with no comparison of energy limits the marks.

**12. (a)** P is more active, so uses more energy [1]; carbohydrate supplies glucose for respiration to release that energy [1].

**(b)** P is still growing and builds muscle in training [1]; protein is needed for growth and repair [1].

**(c)** Calcium makes bones strong [1]; vitamin D is needed to absorb the calcium [1].

**(d)** Both need all the nutrients in the correct proportions; only the amounts differ [1]. **Total: 7 marks**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "a bit of everything" for a balanced diet, with no idea of proportions.
- Giving vitamins or mineral ions an energy role.
- Swapping the jobs of calcium and iron.
- Linking rickets to vitamin C or scurvy to calcium.
- Naming a vague source such as "food" or "vegetables" for every nutrient.
- Comparing data without quoting the figures, or quoting them without units.
- Explaining a deficiency without the chain: missing food, missing nutrient, effect.
