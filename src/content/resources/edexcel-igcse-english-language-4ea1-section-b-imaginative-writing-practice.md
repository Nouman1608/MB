## Section A: short craft questions

**1.** Rewrite this sentence so that it shows the character's feeling instead of telling it:
*"Sam was really angry with his sister."* **[2]**

**2.** Punctuate this dialogue correctly, setting it out as it should appear in a story:
*where are you going asked Mum nowhere said Leo* **[3]**

**3.** The paragraph below drifts between tenses. Identify the **two** verbs that break the tense
pattern and correct them.
*"She walked to the window and pulls back the curtain. The street was empty. A cat crosses the road and
disappeared."* **[2]**

**4.** Write a one-sentence opening for a story called "The Visitor" that starts *in medias res*. **[2]**

**5.** Replace the list of adjectives with **one** precise image:
*"The old house was dark, cold, creepy and scary."* **[2]**

**6.** Write two sentences describing a market: the first long and cumulative, the second short, for
contrast. **[2]**

**7.** Explain how a cyclical ending could be used in a story titled "The Last Day of Summer". **[2]**

**8.** Write the final sentence of a story about a child waiting at an airport, in a way that
deliberately leaves the ending open. Then explain its effect in one sentence. **[3]**

## Section B: timed writing tasks

Choose **one** prompt from each set and write for 45 minutes. Each response is marked out of 30
(AO4 18, AO5 12).

**9.** Set 1:
(a) Write a story with the title "The Key".
(b) Write about a time when you, or someone you know, had to be brave.
(c) Write a description of a city street just before dawn. **[30]**

**10.** Set 2:
(a) Write a story that ends with the words: "And that was the last time I saw it."
(b) Write about an unexpected meeting.
(c) Write a description of a crowded place from the point of view of someone who feels alone in it. **[30]**

**11.** Set 3:
(a) Write a story with the title "Lost and Found".
(b) Write a story about a journey that does not go to plan.
(c) Write a description of a storm approaching a village. **[30]**

**12.** Write the opening **two paragraphs only** (about 200 words) of a story called "The Promise",
then write three bullet points explaining the choices you made for structure, voice and vocabulary.
**[10]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** One mark for removing the abstract statement of feeling (1); one mark for a physical action,
gesture or speech that conveys anger (1). Example: *"Sam slammed his sister's door so hard that the
picture beside it slipped sideways."*

**2.** One mark for correct speech marks around both spoken parts (1), one for punctuation inside the
speech marks with the question mark and comma placed correctly (1), one for a new line for the new
speaker (1):
*"Where are you going?" asked Mum.*
*"Nowhere," said Leo.*

**3.** "pulls" should be "pulled" (1); "crosses" should be "crossed" (1).

**4.** One mark for beginning in the middle of action (1); one mark for a clear link to a visitor
(1). Example: *"The knocking had been going on for a full minute before I realised the man on the
doorstep was calling my name."*

**5.** One mark for a single, precise image (1) and one for avoiding the listed adjectives while
keeping the mood (1). Example: *"The old house held its cold like a grudge."*

**6.** One mark for a long, cumulative sentence that builds detail (1); one mark for a short contrasting
sentence (1). Example: *"Under the awnings the traders were already shouting prices over pyramids of
oranges, buckets of silver fish and bolts of cloth that rippled whenever a scooter passed. Then the rain
came."*

**7.** The story could open and close with the same image, such as a swimming pool being covered (1);
at the end the image would carry a changed meaning, for example the end of childhood or of a
friendship, giving a deliberate, satisfying close (1).

**8.** One mark for an appropriate final sentence (1); one mark for leaving the outcome unresolved (1);
one mark for an explanation of its effect (1). Example: *"The doors slid open again, and she stood up,
not yet able to see whose face was coming through."* The reader is left to imagine whether the person
she is waiting for has arrived, prolonging the tension of the whole story.

### Section B: how to mark questions 9 to 11

The bands below are Marlbridge's illustrative guidance, not Pearson's published mark scheme.

| AO4 (18) | Typical features |
|---|---|
| 16–18 | Perceptive, subtle communication; sharply focused on the prompt; sophisticated control of form, tone and register; deliberate structure |
| 12–15 | Successful communication; secure, effective structure and voice |
| 8–11 | Clear communication; appropriate form and tone; some development |
| 4–7 | Broadly appropriate; straightforward and uneven |
| 1–3 | Basic; limited sense of purpose or reader |

| AO5 (12) | Typical features |
|---|---|
| 11–12 | Extensive vocabulary used strategically; wide range of sentence structures for effect; very accurate |
| 8–10 | Wide, selective vocabulary; deliberate sentence variety; occasional errors |
| 5–7 | Varied vocabulary; generally accurate punctuation and paragraphing |
| 3–4 | Some range; noticeable errors |
| 1–2 | Basic vocabulary and control |

**Indicative approaches.**
- *Story prompts* ("The Key", "Lost and Found", the given ending): one central situation, a clear change,
  and an ending planned in advance; the key or lost object can carry symbolic meaning.
- *"Write about a time..."* prompts: first-person reflective voice, a single focused episode rather than a
  life story, and a closing reflection showing what changed.
- *Description prompts* (dawn street, crowded place, storm): little plot; structure by movement through
  space or time (zoom in, or from calm to chaos); sensory detail beyond sight; precise verbs.
- For Set 2(c), the viewpoint matters: contrast the noise and movement of the crowd with the
  character's stillness, so description reveals feeling.

**What separates stronger answers.** A controlled voice from the first line, a structure with a
deliberate ending, precise vocabulary rather than lists, and sentence variety used for effect.

### Question 12

Marked out of 10: up to 6 for the opening (AO4 control of voice and hook, 3; AO5 range and accuracy, 3)
and up to 4 for the explanation (structure 1, voice 1, vocabulary 1, and a clear link between choices
and intended effect on the reader 1).

**Example explanation points:**
- Structure: I began in the moment the promise is broken, then moved back to when it was made, to
  create a time shift and suspense.
- Voice: first person, past tense, so the narrator can reflect with regret.
- Vocabulary: I used precise verbs ("snapped", "lingered") instead of adverbs, and repeated "promise" at
  the end of the second paragraph to emphasise its weight.
- Effect: the reader knows the outcome before the cause, so the story becomes about why, not what.
