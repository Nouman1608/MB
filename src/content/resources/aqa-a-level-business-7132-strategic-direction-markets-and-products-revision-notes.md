Condensed notes on section **3.8.1 Strategic direction: choosing which markets to compete in and what products to offer** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). Section 3.8 is A-level only. The Lowry Outdoor example is in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-strategic-direction-markets-and-products/); questions are in the [practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-strategic-direction-markets-and-products-practice/).

## Specification content

- Factors influencing which markets to compete in and which products to offer.
- Strategic direction, to include the Ansoff matrix and the value of market penetration, market development, new product development and diversification.
- The reasons for choosing and value of different options for strategic direction.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Strategic direction | The choice of which markets to compete in and which products to offer |
| Market penetration | More sales of existing products in existing markets |
| Market development | Existing products sold in new markets |
| New product development (NPD) | New products for existing markets |
| Diversification | New products in new markets |
| Related diversification | New activity linked to the existing business |
| Unrelated (conglomerate) diversification | New activity in an unconnected industry |
| Core competence | A hard-to-copy strength that gives customers value |

## The Ansoff matrix

| | Existing products | New products |
|---|---|---|
| **Existing markets** | Market penetration (lowest risk) | New product development |
| **New markets** | Market development | Diversification (highest risk) |

## The four options

| Option | Value | Limits | Suits when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Penetration | Low risk; quick; uses current skills | Capped by market size; rivals retaliate; price cuts hurt margin | Market growing; spare capacity; weak rivals |
| Market development | Longer product life; spreads risk | Unfamiliar tastes, laws, rivals; adaptation and entry costs | Home market saturated; product has appeal elsewhere |
| NPD | Meets changing needs; builds loyalty; advantage | Costly, uncertain R&D; may cannibalise sales | Needs and technology changing; strong R&D |
| Diversification | Spreads risk; growth where the core market is saturated | Highest risk and cost; lack of expertise; loss of focus | Core market stagnant; funds available; acquisition possible |

## Factors influencing choice

Market growth and size; competition; resources and finance; core competences; attitude to risk; corporate objectives; product life cycle; technology and customer trends; law and regulation; ownership.

## Risk and reward pattern

The further the move from the core business, the greater the potential reward and the lower the chance of success.

## Worked mini-example

A firm has a 10% share of a £80m market. A penetration campaign raises its share to 13%.
Sales rise from £8.0m to £10.4m, an increase of **£2.4m**.

Expected return method: expected extra revenue = extra revenue × probability. A £3.5m gain with a 50% chance of success has an expected value of £1.75m. If it costs £1.0m, expected revenue per £1 invested is 1.75. Revenue is not profit, so use this as a guide only.

## Limitations of the Ansoff matrix

- Ignores finance, resources and how to grow (organic or by takeover).
- Categories overlap; the boundary between "new" and "existing" is blurred.
- No guidance on competitors' reactions or timing.
- Risk rankings are generalisations.

## Exam traps

- Check **both** the product and the market before classifying.
- A new market for an existing product is market development, not diversification.
- Risk depends on the firm's skills and funds.
- Firms often combine options or follow them in sequence.
- Tie the recommendation to objectives, finances and risk.

## Self-test

1. Name the four options in the Ansoff matrix.
2. A firm sells its existing biscuits in a new country. Which option is this?
3. A firm launches a new drink to its current customers. Which option is this?
4. Why is diversification the riskiest option?
5. Give two ways of achieving market penetration.
6. Give one limit of market penetration.
7. A firm has a 15% share of a £60m market and raises it to 18%. Calculate the increase in sales.
8. A project costs £2m, would add £5m of revenue if it works, and has a 40% chance of success. Calculate the expected extra revenue.
9. Give two factors that would favour a cautious firm choosing market penetration.
10. Why can NPD cannibalise existing sales?
11. Name one factor that may stop a firm entering a new overseas market.
12. Give one limitation of the Ansoff matrix.

### Answers

1. Market penetration, market development, new product development, diversification.
2. Market development.
3. New product development.
4. The firm has no experience of the new product or market, and usually needs a large investment.
5. Any two: advertising; price cuts; loyalty schemes; better distribution; taking over a rival.
6. Growth is capped by the size of the market; rivals may retaliate.
7. 3% of £60m = **£1.8m**.
8. £5m × 40% = **£2m**.
9. Any two: limited funds; low risk appetite; a growing market; spare capacity; strong existing brand.
10. A new product may attract customers away from the firm's existing products.
11. Any one: tariffs; laws or standards; cultural differences; strong local competitors; cost of distribution.
12. Any one: ignores finance and resources; categories overlap; ignores competitors' reactions; gives no guidance on timing.
