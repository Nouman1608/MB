## Questions

**1.** (a) Explain the difference between a good and a service. **[3]**
**(b)** Outline two stages a business might go through when developing a new product. **[4]**

**2.** Ottley Bakes makes 4,000 loaves a month. Variable cost is 2.20 per loaf and fixed costs are 12,000 a month. Ottley prices with the cost plus method, using a 35% mark-up.

**(a)** Calculate the total cost of one loaf. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the selling price of one loaf. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the monthly profit if all 4,000 loaves are sold at this price. **[2]**
**(d)** State one limitation of cost plus pricing. **[1]**

**3.** (a) State the pricing strategy used in each case. **[3]**

- (i) A firm launches a new headset with unique technology at a high price, and plans to lower it after a year.
- (ii) A new streaming app is launched at a very low price to win subscribers quickly.
- (iii) A fuel station sets its price one cent below a rival station across the road.

**(b)** Explain one situation in which a business might use promotional pricing. **[2]**

**4.** Sales of a gadget, in thousands of units, were: Year 1: 4; Year 2: 31; Year 3: 68; Year 4: 74; Year 5: 73; Year 6: 52.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in sales from Year 5 to Year 6, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the phase of the product life cycle in Year 3 and in Year 5. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain two extension strategies the maker could use. **[4]**

**5.** Greylake Foods manages four products.

| Product | Own market share | Largest rival's share | Market growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | 36% | 18% | 2% |
| Q | 6% | 30% | 14% |
| R | 28% | 14% | 15% |
| S | 4% | 25% | 1% |

**(a)** Calculate the relative market share of each product. **[2]**
**(b)** Place each product in its Boston matrix category. **[4]**
**(c)** Greylake is reviewing its product portfolio. Evaluate whether it should stop selling product S. **[5]**

**6.** Sandcroft Garden Tools sells a trowel at a retail price of 24. A shop that stocks the trowel keeps 35% of the retail price. Selling on its own website (as an e-tailer), Sandcroft pays 3.50 delivery and a payment fee of 5% of the price.

**(a)** Calculate the amount Sandcroft receives per trowel sold through the shop. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the amount Sandcroft receives per trowel sold on its website. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate how much more Sandcroft would receive if 10,000 trowels were sold online instead of through shops. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain one non-financial reason why Sandcroft might still sell through shops. **[2]**

**7.** (a) State whether each is above the line or below the line promotion: a cinema advert; a free sample handed out in a supermarket; a local radio advert; a discount voucher sent by post. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain the difference between the two. **[2]**

**8.** Zinta Wear is an online clothing store aimed at 16 to 19 year olds who have low incomes.

**(a)** Explain what is meant by public relations. **[2]**
**(b)** Define targeted advertising online and state one benefit of it. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one risk of viral advertising via social media. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain which one promotion method would suit Zinta Wear best. **[3]**

**9.** Orlindo Snacks sells a 250g pack for 4.00. Unit cost is 2.60 and it sells 1,500 packs a month. For one month it runs a special offer of 25% off, and sales rise to 2,800 packs.

**(a)** Calculate the monthly revenue with and without the offer. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the monthly profit with and without the offer, and the change in profit. **[3]**
**(c)** Evaluate whether Orlindo should run the offer. **[6]**

**10.** Kesta Studio is a small business with 12 employees. It makes ceramic mugs and is launching a travel cup. The cost of making each cup is 11. The owner is choosing between two pricing strategies. Skimming: price 28, expected first-year sales 800 cups. Penetration: price 16, expected first-year sales 2,400 cups.

**(a)** Calculate the first-year profit under each strategy. **[4]**
**(b)** Justify which strategy Kesta Studio should use. **[8]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A good is a physical item [1]; a service is an activity done for the customer [1]; one difference, for example goods can be stored in stock but services usually cannot [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Market research, such as asking possible customers whether they would buy the product [1], which reduces the risk of making something nobody wants [1]. Testing a sample or prototype [1], so faults or unpopular features can be changed before launch [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Outline" needs the stage and a short statement of what happens or why.

**2. (a)** Fixed cost per loaf = 12,000 ÷ 4,000 = 3.00 [1]; total cost = 2.20 + 3.00 = **5.20** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Mark-up = 5.20 × 0.35 = 1.82 [1]; price = 5.20 + 1.82 = **7.02** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Profit per loaf 1.82 [1] × 4,000 = **7,280** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** It ignores rivals' prices; or the unit cost is only right if output is 4,000 [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each stage of a calculation, so that a slip in one step can still be followed.

**3. (a)** (i) Skimming [1]. (ii) Penetration [1]. (iii) Competition [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** A business wants to clear old stock before a new model arrives [1], and a temporary price cut raises sales quickly and frees storage space [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "When" asks for a situation, not a definition.

**4. (a)** Change = 52 − 73 = −21 [1]; −21 ÷ 73 × 100 = **−28.8%**, a fall of 28.8% [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Year 3: growth [1]. Year 5: maturity [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Selling in a new market or to a new segment [1], which gives a new group of customers who may buy [1]. Adding a new feature or changing the packaging [1], which gives existing customers a reason to buy again [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain how each strategy delays decline; a name alone is usually not enough.

**5. (a)** P: 36 ÷ 18 = 2.0 and R: 28 ÷ 14 = 2.0 [1]; Q: 6 ÷ 30 = 0.2 and S: 4 ÷ 25 = 0.16 [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** P: cash cow [1]. Q: question mark [1]. R: star [1]. S: dog [1]. **[4]**
**(c)** S has low relative share (0.16) and its market is growing at only 1% [1], so it has little prospect of becoming a leader [1]. Keeping it ties up money and effort that could support R, which is a star [1]. Against stopping: S may still bring some sales and customers who like the range [1]. Judgement: stop selling S unless it covers its costs, and move the resources to R and the question mark Q [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the figures given; a recommendation with no data from the table reads as a general comment.

**6. (a)** Shop keeps 24 × 0.35 = 8.40 [1]; Sandcroft receives 24 − 8.40 = **15.60** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Fee = 24 × 0.05 = 1.20 [1]; receives 24 − 3.50 − 1.20 = **19.30** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** (19.30 − 15.60) × 10,000 = **37,000** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Shops give customers a chance to see and try the product [1], and shop shelves put the product in front of people who would not visit the website [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A point about money does not answer a question asking for a non-financial reason.

**7. (a)** Cinema advert: above the line [1]. Free sample: below the line [1]. Local radio advert: above the line [1]. Discount voucher by post: below the line [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Above the line is paid advertising in media aimed at a wide audience [1]; below the line uses other methods, often more direct and aimed at a particular group, such as offers and trials [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Describe both sides in a "difference" question.

**8. (a)** Managing how the public sees the business [1], for example through news stories or supporting a local cause, to improve its image and brand [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Adverts are shown online to chosen groups, such as by age, location or interests [1]. Benefit: less money is wasted on people unlikely to buy [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Once a post is shared, the business cannot control it [1], so the message may be changed or criticised and could harm the brand [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Method, such as a discount code shared on social media [1]. Reason: suits 16 to 19 year olds, who are heavy users of social media [1], and the discount suits low incomes, so it is more likely to lead to a purchase [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the method to the age and income given, not just to popularity.

**9. (a)** Without offer: 4.00 × 1,500 = **6,000** [1]. With offer: price 3.00 × 2,800 = **8,400** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Without: (4.00 − 2.60) × 1,500 = **2,100** [1]. With: (3.00 − 2.60) × 2,800 = **1,120** [1]. Profit falls by **980** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** For: revenue rises by 2,400 [1]; the offer may bring new customers who try the product and buy again later [1]. Against: profit falls by 980 [1] because profit per pack drops from 1.40 to 0.40 [1], and customers may wait for the next offer [1]. Judgement: it loses money, so run it only to win new customers or clear stock, if they will return at the full price [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Revenue and profit can move in opposite directions; reach a decision supported by both.

**10. (a)** Skimming: (28 − 11) × 800 [1] = **13,600** [1]. Penetration: (16 − 11) × 2,400 [1] = **12,000** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Skimming gives 1,600 more profit in year one [1]. Profit per cup is 17 against 5, and only 800 cups need making, which suits a small business with 12 employees and limited cash [2]. Penetration reaches 2,400 customers, builds awareness for the new product and may bring repeat sales [2]. Both rely on sales forecasts that may be wrong [1]. Recommendation, for example: skimming, because it earns more with less risk, lowering the price when rivals appear; or penetration, if market share matters more than first-year profit. The reasons must fit the figures [2]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Both choices can be argued, so credit follows the reasoning and the data used, not which strategy is picked.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a strategy without saying when it suits the business.
- Not working out relative market share before placing a product in the Boston matrix.
- Judging a promotion by revenue and ignoring profit.
- Giving a financial reason when the question asks for a non-financial one.
- Describing a promotion method without linking it to the segment in the question.
- Reaching no decision on an evaluate or justify question.
- Forgetting to add fixed cost per unit for cost plus.
