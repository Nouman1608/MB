## Section A: short questions

**1.** Identify **two** alternatives to living in a family. **[2]**

**2.** What is meant by a **horizontally extended** family? **[2]**

**3.** Identify **one** difference between a patriarchal family and a matriarchal family. **[2]**

**4.** Describe what Parsons meant by the **instrumental** and **expressive** roles. **[4]**

**5.** Study **Item A** and answer the questions that follow.

> **Item A** — Average number of people per household in four countries, 1980 and 2020
>
> *Illustrative data, invented for this practice question. The countries are
> identified only by letter.*
>
> | Country | 1980 | 2020 |
> |---|---|---|
> | W | 5.8 | 4.1 |
> | X | 3.1 | 2.4 |
> | Y | 6.5 | 5.9 |
> | Z | 2.8 | 2.2 |

**(a)** Using Item A, identify the country with the largest fall in average household size, and state the size of the fall. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify **one** pattern shown in Item A. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain **one** reason why average household size may have fallen. **[3]**

## Section B: longer questions

**6.** Explain **one** way in which Marxists see families as agents of social control. **[4]**

**7.** Explain **two** reasons for the growth of ageing populations in many societies. **[6]**

**8.** Explain how laws and social policies can affect family size or family type, using an example. **[4]**

**9.** Explain **two** ways in which the roles of children differ between societies. **[6]**

**10.** Discuss how far men and women now share domestic labour and decision making equally in families. Use examples from your own society or a society you know about. **[12]**

## Answers: Section A

**A1** [2 marks — 1 per alternative]

Any two: communes; living alone; foster care; children's homes. Also credit shared student or worker housing if explained as an alternative to family living.

**A2** [2 marks]

A family in which relatives of the **same generation** live together or share a household [1], such as two brothers living with their wives and children [1].

**A3** [2 marks]

In a **patriarchal** family authority lies with the father or eldest male [1]; in a **matriarchal** family it lies with the mother or a senior woman [1].

**A4** [4 marks — 2 per role]

The **instrumental** role is the male **breadwinner** role [1], earning money and providing for the family financially [1]. The **expressive** role is the female role of **caring and emotional support** [1], including bringing up children and looking after the home [1]. Credit a note that Parsons saw these as complementary.

**A5 (a)** [2 marks]

**Country W** [1]; the fall is **1.7 people per household** (5.8 − 4.1) [1].

**A5 (b)** [2 marks — 1 for the pattern, 1 for supporting figures]

e.g. **Household size fell in all four countries** [1], from 6.5 to 5.9 in Y and from 2.8 to 2.2 in Z [1]. Or: countries with **larger households in 1980 still had larger households in 2020** [1], with Y the largest in both years (6.5 and 5.9) [1].

**A5 (c)** [3 marks — 1 for the reason, 2 for development]

e.g. **Falling birth rates** [1]: with access to contraception and more women in education and paid work, couples have fewer children [1], so each household contains fewer people [1]. Or: **urbanisation** [1]: people move to cities for work, where housing is smaller and costly [1], so extended families are less likely to live together [1].

## Answers: Section B

**A6** [4 marks]

Families **socialise children to accept authority** [1], for example by teaching them to obey parents without question [1]. Marxists argue this prepares them to **accept the authority of employers** and the inequality of capitalist society [1], so families help keep the system stable and prevent challenge to the ruling class [1]. Credit Engels' link between the family and private property where it is applied to control.

**A7** [6 marks — up to 3 per reason]

Two from: **rising life expectancy** [1], due to better nutrition, sanitation and healthcare [1], so more people live into old age [1]; **falling birth rates** [1], due to contraception and the rising cost of children [1], so older people form a larger share of the population [1]; **falling infant mortality** reducing the need to have many children [1] [1] [1]; **emigration of young adults** leaving a higher proportion of older people behind [1] [1] [1].

**A8** [4 marks]

Laws and policies can **encourage or limit** family size or **permit or restrict** family types [1]. Example: China's **one-child policy** from 1980 [1] reduced family size and contributed to an ageing population and to "4-2-1" family structures [1]; it was replaced by two- and three-child policies in 2016 and 2021 [1]. Also credit divorce law reform, welfare support for single parents, or childcare provision, with explanation.

**A9** [6 marks — up to 3 per way]

Two from: **work** [1] — in some societies children contribute to family income through farming or paid work [1], whereas elsewhere they are financially dependent for many years [1]; **education** [1] — the length of compulsory schooling differs [1], which shapes how long childhood lasts [1]; **status and child-centredness** [1] — in some societies family life is organised around children's needs [1], while in others children are expected to defer to elders [1]; **care roles** [1] — children may care for siblings or sick relatives [1] [1]. Credit reference to Ariès' view that childhood is socially constructed.

**A10** [12 marks — levels]

| Level | Marks | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Detailed knowledge; well applied to a named society; balanced discussion with a clear, justified conclusion |
| 3 | 7–9 | Good knowledge; some application; discussion with a conclusion |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some relevant knowledge; limited application or one-sided |
| 1 | 1–3 | Basic, mainly descriptive points |

Indicative content:

- **Towards equality**: Young and Willmott's symmetrical family; more women in paid work; changing attitudes among younger couples; laws promoting gender equality.
- **Continuing inequality**: Oakley's findings on housework; the triple shift; men often control major financial decisions; domestic violence linked to male power (Dobash and Dobash).
- **Variation**: by class, religion, ethnicity and society; traditional instrumental and expressive roles remain strong in some societies.
- **Application**: a specific society the candidate knows.
- **Judgement**: for example, sharing has increased but full equality is rare, and women still carry more domestic and emotional work.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing horizontally and vertically extended families.
- Giving a reason for demographic change without developing it.
- Reading Item A carelessly, or describing a pattern without figures.
- Describing roles without naming Parsons or explaining the terms.
- Writing a one-sided extended answer with no example from a named society.
