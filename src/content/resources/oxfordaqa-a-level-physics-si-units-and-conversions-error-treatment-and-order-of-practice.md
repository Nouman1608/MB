## Questions

**1.** Convert (i) 2.8 cm² to m², (ii) 13.6 g cm⁻³ to kg m⁻³, (iii) 90 km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹. **[3]**

**2.** **(a)** A particle has kinetic energy 1.4 MeV. Express this in joules. **[2]**
**(b)** An electron in an atom changes energy by 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. Express this in eV. **[1]**

**3.** An electric shower of power 3.0 kW is used for 12 minutes each day.
**(a)** Calculate the energy transferred per day in kW h. **[2]**
**(b)** Express this in joules. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the energy transferred in 30 days, in MJ. **[1]**

**4.** A digital balance shows +0.4 g with nothing on the pan. A coin is then placed on it five times and the readings are 7.52, 7.49, 7.51, 7.50 and 7.53 g.
**(a)** State the type of error shown by the reading with nothing on the pan. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the corrected mean mass. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why taking more readings does not remove this error. **[1]**
**(d)** Estimate the uncertainty in the mean from the spread of the readings. **[1]**

**5.** Match each statement to one of these terms: accuracy, precision, repeatability, reproducibility, resolution.
**(a)** A digital timer displays to 0.01 s. **[1]**
**(b)** Five timings of a period lie between 1.51 s and 1.53 s. **[1]**
**(c)** The same student repeats the experiment on another day with the same kit and gets the same mean. **[1]**
**(d)** A different group using different timers and a different method obtains a mean that agrees. **[1]**
**(e)** The mean differs from the accepted value by only 0.3%. **[1]**

**6.** A length is measured as 0.842 m ± 0.003 m.
**(a)** Calculate the fractional uncertainty. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage uncertainty. **[1]**
**(c)** A second length, 0.640 m, has a percentage uncertainty of 2.5%. Calculate its absolute uncertainty. **[1]**
**(d)** Write the second length with its uncertainty, using a suitable number of significant figures. **[1]**

**7.** The power dissipated in a resistor is P = V²/R. V = 12.0 ± 0.2 V and R = 47 ± 2 Ω. Calculate P and its absolute uncertainty, and state the result with its uncertainty. **[5]**

**8.** The temperature θ of a heated liquid is plotted against time t, with each θ having an uncertainty of ±2 °C. The best-fit line passes through (0, 21) and (10, 60). The steepest line through all the error bars passes through (0, 19) and (10, 62); the shallowest passes through (0, 23) and (10, 58). Temperatures are in °C and times in minutes.
**(a)** Calculate the gradient of the best-fit line. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the gradients of the steepest and shallowest lines. **[2]**
**(c)** State the uncertainty in the gradient. **[1]**
**(d)** State the intercept on the θ axis, with its uncertainty. **[2]**
**(e)** Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the gradient. **[1]**

**9.** **(a)** Three students quote a result as A: 4.56 ± 0.1, B: 4.6 ± 0.1, C: 4.6 ± 0.12. State which is consistent, and give a reason why each of the others is not. **[3]**
**(b)** A calculation gives 0.0472 with an uncertainty of 0.0036. Write the result in a suitable form. **[2]**

**10.** Estimate the number of times a car wheel turns during a 10 km journey. State your assumption and give the answer to the nearest order of magnitude. **[4]**

**11.** A raindrop of radius 2 mm falls at 7 m s⁻¹. The density of water is 1000 kg m⁻³.
**(a)** Calculate the mass of the drop. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate its kinetic energy and state the order of magnitude in J. **[2]**
**(c)** Express the energy in eV and state the order of magnitude. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (i) 1 cm² = 10⁻⁴ m², so **2.8 × 10⁻⁴ m²** [1]. (ii) 13.6 × 10⁻³ kg ÷ 10⁻⁶ m³ = **1.36 × 10⁴ kg m⁻³** [1]. (iii) 90 × 1000 ÷ 3600 = **25 m s⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the conversion factor you used for the squared and cubed units, because a bare answer with the wrong power of ten shows nothing of your method.

**2.** **(a)** 1.4 MeV = 1.4 × 10⁶ eV [1]; × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **2.2 × 10⁻¹³ J** (2.24 × 10⁻¹³) [1].
**(b)** 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁹ ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **4.0 eV** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the prefix as a power of ten before multiplying; skipping that step is a frequent route to an answer a million times too small.

**3.** **(a)** 12 min = 0.20 h [1]; energy = 3.0 × 0.20 = **0.60 kW h** [1].
**(b)** 0.60 × 3.6 × 10⁶ = **2.2 × 10⁶ J** (2.16 × 10⁶) [1].
**(c)** 30 × 2.16 × 10⁶ = 6.48 × 10⁷ J = **65 MJ** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert minutes to hours before using kW h; using 12 directly is a common error that spoils every later part.

**4.** **(a)** **Systematic (zero) error** [1].
**(b)** Mean = 7.51 g; corrected = 7.51 − 0.4 = **7.11 g** [1].
**(c)** The offset is the same size and direction in every reading, so averaging leaves it unchanged [1].
**(d)** Half-range = (7.53 − 7.49) ÷ 2 = **±0.02 g** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the error is the same size every time; stating only that repeats are "not helpful" does not explain why.

**5.** **(a)** **Resolution** [1]. **(b)** **Precision** [1]. **(c)** **Repeatability** [1]. **(d)** **Reproducibility** [1]. **(e)** **Accuracy** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Precision and accuracy are different words: a tight spread about the wrong value is precise but not accurate, so say which one the data support.

**6.** **(a)** 0.003 ÷ 0.842 = **0.0036** [1].
**(b)** **0.36%** [1].
**(c)** 0.025 × 0.640 = **0.016 m** [1].
**(d)** One significant figure for the uncertainty: **0.64 ± 0.02 m** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Match the decimal places of the value to those of the uncertainty; 0.640 ± 0.02 m is inconsistent.

**7.** P = 12.0² ÷ 47 = 3.06 W [1]. Percentage uncertainty in V = 0.2 ÷ 12.0 = 1.67% [1]. Percentage uncertainty in R = 2 ÷ 47 = 4.26% [1]. V is squared: total = 2 × 1.67 + 4.26 = 7.6% [1]. Absolute uncertainty = 0.076 × 3.06 = 0.23 W, so **P = 3.1 ± 0.2 W** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the doubling for the squared term explicitly; the final figure alone is hard to credit if it contains an arithmetic slip.

**8.** **(a)** (60 − 21) ÷ 10 = **3.9 °C per minute** [1].
**(b)** Steepest: (62 − 19) ÷ 10 = **4.3** [1]. Shallowest: (58 − 23) ÷ 10 = **3.5** [1].
**(c)** (4.3 − 3.5) ÷ 2 = **±0.4 °C per minute** [1].
**(d)** Best intercept **21 °C**; the extreme intercepts are 19 and 23, so uncertainty = (23 − 19) ÷ 2 = **±2 °C** [1] [1].
**(e)** 0.4 ÷ 3.9 × 100 = **10%** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Use points far apart when finding a gradient and give units; the half-difference of the extreme lines is worth stating in words as well as giving the number.

**9.** **(a)** **B** is consistent [1]. A gives the value to more decimal places than its uncertainty supports [1]. C gives the uncertainty to more decimal places than the value [1].
**(b)** Uncertainty to 1 significant figure: 0.004 [1]; value to the same decimal place: **0.047 ± 0.004** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a reason that mentions decimal places or significant figures; "looks wrong" does not identify the problem.

**10.** Assume wheel diameter about 0.6 m [1]. Circumference = π × 0.6 = 1.9 m [1]. Turns = 10 000 ÷ 1.9 = 5.3 × 10³ [1]. Since 5.3 is above about 3.2, the order of magnitude is **10⁴** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Always state the assumption you used; an unstated size cannot be checked against a sensible range.

**11.** **(a)** Volume = 4/3 × π × (2 × 10⁻³)³ = 3.4 × 10⁻⁸ m³ [1]; mass = 1000 × 3.35 × 10⁻⁸ = **3.4 × 10⁻⁵ kg** [1].
**(b)** KE = ½ × 3.35 × 10⁻⁵ × 7² = 8.2 × 10⁻⁴ J [1]; order of magnitude **10⁻³ J** [1].
**(c)** 8.2 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 5.1 × 10¹⁵ eV [1]; order of magnitude **10¹⁶ eV** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert the radius from mm to m before cubing; the cube makes any error in that step very large.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Converting mm², cm³ and similar units with the linear factor.
- Leaving a prefix in an equation, such as using 250 for 250 mA.
- Using minutes in a kW h calculation without converting to hours.
- Describing a systematic error as something repeating can fix.
- Using accuracy and precision as if they meant the same.
- Not doubling the percentage uncertainty for a squared quantity.
- Quoting a result with more decimal places than its uncertainty.
- Subtracting uncertainties, or adding absolute uncertainties in a product.
- Drawing extreme lines that miss error bars, or finding gradients from points close together.
- Giving an order of magnitude with no stated assumptions or rounding the wrong way at the boundary.
