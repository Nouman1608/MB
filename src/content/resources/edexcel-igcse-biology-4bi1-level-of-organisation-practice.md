These questions cover sub-topic 2(a) Level of organisation, point 2.1, of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1), Issue 3. The point can be examined on Paper 1 and Paper 2.

## Section A

**1.** Name the level of organisation of each structure: (i) ribosome; (ii) neurone; (iii) phloem; (iv) kidney. **[4]**

**2.** Complete the sentence: "A tissue is a group of ______ cells that work together to carry out a particular ______." **[2]**

**3.** Give **one** difference between a tissue and an organ. **[1]**

**4.** A student says that skin is a tissue because it covers the body. Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**5.** Name the system to which each organ belongs: (i) the trachea; (ii) the pancreas; (iii) the spinal cord. **[3]**

**6.** Explain why a single-celled organism has no tissues or organs. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The structures below are from a flowering plant.

guard cell, leaf, shoot system, chloroplast, lower epidermis

**(a)** Arrange them in order from the smallest level of organisation to the largest. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the level of organisation of the lower epidermis and give a reason. **[2]**

**8.** The wall of the stomach contains muscle tissue, glandular tissue and epithelial tissue.

**(a)** Use this information to explain why the stomach is an organ. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest the function of the muscle tissue and of the glandular tissue in the stomach. **[2]**
**(c)** Name the system the stomach belongs to and **two** other organs in that system. **[3]**

**9.** Describe the levels of organisation in the human circulatory system, from an organelle to the system, giving a named example at each level. **[5]**

**10.** Blood contains red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets in plasma.

**(a)** State the level of organisation of blood. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain your answer to (a). **[2]**
**(c)** Name the level of organisation of a single red blood cell, and explain why it is not a tissue. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why large multicellular organisms need specialised cells organised into tissues, organs and systems. **[4]**

**12.** A root is an organ.

**(a)** Name **two** tissues found in a root. **[2]**
**(b)** Name **one** specialised cell found in a root and an organelle it contains, and state the function of that organelle in the cell. **[3]**
**(c)** Name the level of organisation formed by all the roots of a plant. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** (i) Organelle [1]. (ii) Cell [1]. (iii) Tissue [1]. (iv) Organ [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A neurone is one nerve cell; nervous tissue is the group of them.

**2.** similar [1]; function [1]. **[2]**

**3.** A tissue is made of similar cells, whereas an organ is made of several different tissues [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Skin is an organ [1], because it contains several different tissues, such as epithelium, nervous tissue and blood, working together [1]. **[2]**

**5.** (i) Breathing (gas exchange) system [1]. (ii) Digestive system [1]. (iii) Nervous system [1]. **[3]**

**6.** The organism is made of only one cell [1], so there are no groups of similar cells to form tissues, and so no tissues to form organs; the one cell carries out all life processes [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** chloroplast → guard cell → lower epidermis → leaf → shoot system. All five correct [2]; one pair reversed [1]. **[2]** for (a).
**(b)** Tissue [1], because it is a layer of similar cells working together to protect the leaf and allow gas exchange through its stomata [1]. **[4]**

**8. (a)** It contains several different tissues [1] working together to carry out one function, digestion of food [1].
**(b)** Muscle tissue contracts to churn the food and mix it with digestive juices [1]; glandular tissue secretes digestive juices containing enzymes [1].
**(c)** Digestive system [1]; any two of: mouth, oesophagus, small intestine, large intestine, liver, pancreas [1] [1]. **[7]**

**9.** Organelle: mitochondrion in a cardiac muscle cell, releasing energy for contraction [1]. Cell: cardiac muscle cell, or red blood cell [1]. Tissue: cardiac muscle, or blood [1]. Organ: heart, or an artery [1]. System: the circulatory system, made of the heart, blood vessels and blood working together to transport substances [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep each example within the circulatory system; an example from another system does not answer this question.

**10. (a)** Tissue [1].
**(b)** It is made of similar cells [1] that work together to carry out functions such as transporting oxygen and defending against disease [1].
**(c)** Cell [1]; it is a single cell, and a tissue is a group of many similar cells [1]. **[5]**

**11.** In a large organism most cells are far from the surface or from food and oxygen [1], so one cell type cannot carry out every life process efficiently [1]. Cells become specialised, with structures suited to one function [1], and grouping them into tissues, organs and systems lets each life process, such as transport or gas exchange, be carried out by structures adapted to it [1]. **[4]**

**12. (a)** Any two: epidermis, xylem, phloem [1] [1].
**(b)** Root hair cell [1]; mitochondrion [1], which releases energy by respiration for active transport of mineral ions; or nucleus, which controls the cell's activities [1].
**(c)** The root system [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out "similar" when defining a tissue.
- Saying an organ is "made of cells".
- Calling blood an organ, or xylem and phloem organs.
- Mixing levels from different systems in a "describe the levels" answer.
- Starting a sequence with the cell when an organelle is listed.
- Naming a structure without its level when both are asked for.
