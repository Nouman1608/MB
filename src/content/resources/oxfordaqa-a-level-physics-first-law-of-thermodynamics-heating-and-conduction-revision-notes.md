## Key definitions

- **Internal energy:** the sum of the randomly distributed kinetic energies and potential energies of the particles in a body.
- **Specific heat capacity, c:** the energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 K. Unit J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.
- **Specific latent heat, l:** the energy needed to change the state of 1 kg of a substance at constant temperature. Unit J kg⁻¹.
- **Thermal conductivity, k:** a property of the material that measures how well it conducts energy, as in rate = kAΔθ/L. Unit W m⁻¹ K⁻¹.
- **U-value:** U = k/L. Unit W m⁻² K⁻¹. Lower means better insulation.

## Formulas

| Quantity | Equation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| First law | ΔU = Q + W | Q = energy input by heating. W = work done ON the system |
| Temperature change | Q = mcΔθ | No change of state. Δθ in °C or K |
| Change of state | Q = ml | Temperature stays constant |
| Continuous flow | P = (Δm/Δt) c Δθ | Steady flow. Power in W, flow rate in kg s⁻¹ |
| Conduction | rate = kAΔθ/L | A at right angles to flow, L in metres |
| U-value rate | rate = UAΔθ | Parallel surfaces only. U = k/L |

## First law: signs

| Situation | Sign |
|---|---|
| Energy flows into the system by heating | Q positive |
| Energy flows out of the system by heating | Q negative |
| Surroundings do work on the system (compression) | W positive |
| System does work on surroundings (expansion) | W negative |

If ΔU is positive, the internal energy rises. If ΔU is zero, then Q = −W: the energy taken in by heating exactly equals the work done by the system.

**Reminder:** a gas is compressed. The surroundings do 210 J of work on it and 75 J leaves it by heating.
ΔU = (−75) + 210 = **+135 J**.

## Method: temperature change

1. Find the mass in kilograms.
2. Find Δθ as final minus initial.
3. Use Q = mcΔθ with the c value given.
4. Give the answer in joules, and check the number of significant figures against the data.

**Reminder:** 2.5 kg of water is heated from 20 °C to 100 °C. Take c = 4180 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.
Q = 2.5 × 4180 × 80 = 836 000 J = **8.4 × 10⁵ J**.

## Method: change of state

1. Use Q = ml for each stage at constant temperature.
2. Use Q = mcΔθ for each stage where the temperature changes.
3. Add the stages.

**Reminder:** melting 0.60 kg of ice at 0 °C needs 0.60 × 3.34 × 10⁵ = 2.0 × 10⁵ J. A 2.5 kW heater would take 2.004 × 10⁵ / 2500 = 80 s, if all its energy went into the ice.

### Particle explanation (must-know)

- On a heating curve, a sloping section means the average kinetic energy of the particles is rising.
- A flat section means a change of state. The supplied energy increases the potential energy of the particles as they move apart. The average kinetic energy, and so the temperature, does not change.
- Always name both kinds of energy. State which one changes.

## Method: continuous flow

1. Write the power as energy per second delivered to the fluid.
2. Convert any volume flow rate to mass flow rate using the density.
3. Use P = (Δm/Δt) c Δθ.
4. Rearrange for whichever quantity is missing.

**Reminder:** a 6.0 kW heater warms water flowing at 0.032 kg s⁻¹. Taking all the power to reach the water, Δθ = 6000 / (0.032 × 4180) = **45 K**. If only 85% of the power reaches the water, Δθ = 38 K.

## Required practical 8: electrical method

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Aim | Determine the specific heat capacity of a material by an electrical method |
| Measurements | Mass m, potential difference V, current I, time t, temperature at the start and end (or at regular intervals) |
| Equation | VIt = mcΔθ, so c = VIt / (mΔθ) |
| Graph method | Plot temperature against time. c = VI / (m × gradient) |
| Main systematic error | Energy lost to the surroundings, so the temperature rise is too small and c is too large |
| Improvements | Lag the block, add a lid, use a little oil for thermal contact, stir a liquid, start below room temperature |

**Reminder:** a 0.50 kg block, heater at 10.0 V and 3.0 A, temperature gradient 0.065 K s⁻¹.
P = VI = 30 W. c = 30 / (0.50 × 0.065) = 923, so c = **9.2 × 10² J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹**.

## Conduction

- Rate = kAΔθ/L in watts.
- Rate is proportional to area and temperature difference. It is inversely proportional to thickness.
- A steady state is needed for the equation: the temperatures at each face are constant.
- Metals conduct well because free electrons carry energy as well as lattice vibrations.

**Reminder:** a copper bar has k = 390 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹, length 0.40 m and cross-sectional area 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ m². Its ends are at 100 °C and 30 °C.
Rate = 390 × 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ × 70 / 0.40 = **14 W**.

## U-values

- U = k/L, so a wall with k = 0.025 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ and L = 0.050 m has U = 0.50 W m⁻² K⁻¹.
- Rate = UAΔθ.
- For parallel surfaces, add the separate rates: total = (U₁A₁ + U₂A₂ + …) Δθ.
- The specification limits this to parallel surfaces only.

**Reminder:** a roof of area 12 m² has U = 0.25 W m⁻² K⁻¹. The temperature difference is 14 K. Rate = 0.25 × 12 × 14 = **42 W**. In one hour this is 42 × 3600 = 1.5 × 10⁵ J.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Heating and work:** both transfer energy and both change internal energy. Neither is a store.
- **Temperature and internal energy:** boiling water at 100 °C gains internal energy without any rise in temperature.
- **k and U:** k belongs to a material. U belongs to a particular thickness of that material.
- **c and l:** c is for a temperature change. l is for a change of state.

## Quick self-test

1. Define internal energy.
2. A gas receives 55 J by heating and does 140 J of work on its surroundings. Find ΔU.
3. Calculate the energy needed to warm 0.250 kg of copper from 20 °C to 80 °C. Take c = 385 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.
4. Calculate the energy needed to melt 0.60 kg of ice at 0 °C. Take l = 3.34 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹.
5. Explain why the temperature of boiling water stays constant while a heater is on.
6. Water flows at 0.032 kg s⁻¹ over a 6.0 kW heater. All the power reaches the water. Find the temperature rise. Take c = 4180 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.
7. State one reason why an electrical determination of c gives a value that is too high.
8. A polystyrene slab has k = 0.035 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹, area 0.50 m² and thickness 0.040 m. Its faces differ in temperature by 22 K. Find the rate of energy transfer.
9. Find U for a layer with k = 0.025 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ and thickness 0.050 m.
10. A sheet of area 0.30 m² has faces 20 K apart. Its k is 0.015 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ and it transfers 18 W. Find its thickness.

### Answers

1. The sum of the randomly distributed kinetic energies and potential energies of the particles in the body.
2. Q = +55 J. W = −140 J. ΔU = 55 − 140 = **−85 J**.
3. Q = 0.250 × 385 × 60 = 5775 J = **5.8 × 10³ J**.
4. Q = 0.60 × 3.34 × 10⁵ = 2.004 × 10⁵ J = **2.0 × 10⁵ J**.
5. The supplied energy increases the potential energy of the molecules as they separate. Their average kinetic energy is unchanged, so the temperature is constant.
6. Δθ = 6000 / (0.032 × 4180) = **45 K**.
7. Some energy is lost to the surroundings, so the measured temperature rise is too small and c comes out too large. Lagging reduces this.
8. Rate = 0.035 × 0.50 × 22 / 0.040 = **9.6 W**.
9. U = 0.025 / 0.050 = **0.50 W m⁻² K⁻¹**.
10. L = kAΔθ / rate = 0.015 × 0.30 × 20 / 18 = **0.0050 m** (5.0 mm).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing W with the wrong sign for an expanding gas.
- Defining internal energy without both kinetic and potential energies, or without "random".
- Saying the kinetic energy increases at a boiling point.
- Leaving mass in grams, or thickness in centimetres or millimetres.
- Mixing up the formulas Q = mcΔθ and Q = ml in a question that has two stages.
- Giving a continuous flow answer in joules instead of a rate in watts or kg s⁻¹.
- Stating that lagging "stops" heat loss. Say it reduces the rate of loss.
- Adding thickness-based U-values for layers in series. This specification limits U-value work to parallel surfaces.
- Giving final answers to far more significant figures than the data supports.
