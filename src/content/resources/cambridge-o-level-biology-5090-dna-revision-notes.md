These notes condense subtopic **17.2 DNA** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the structure of DNA, base pairing, the definition of a gene, and how DNA controls cell function through proteins. The full explanations and worked examples are in the [17.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-dna/). Then try the [17.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-dna-practice/).

## Key facts

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| DNA | The molecule that carries genetic information; found in chromosomes |
| Shape | Double helix: two strands coiled together |
| Strand | A chain of nucleotides |
| Nucleotide | Contains a base: A, T, C or G |
| Base pairs | A with T, and C with G; held together by bonds between the strands |
| Gene | A length of DNA that codes for a protein |

## The base-pairing rule

| One strand | A | T | C | G |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Other strand | T | A | G | C |

Totals in a DNA molecule: amount of A = amount of T; amount of C = amount of G.

## Worked examples

**Complementary strand.** Strand 1: T A G G C T A. Strand 2: A T C C G A T. Each base is swapped for its partner.

**Percentages.** If 32 per cent of the bases are A: T = 32 per cent. A + T = 64 per cent. C + G = 36 per cent, so C = 18 per cent and G = 18 per cent.

**Counting.** A length of DNA with 15 base pairs has 15 bases on each strand, so 30 bases in total.

## From gene to function

1. A gene is a length of DNA with a sequence of bases.
2. The base sequence determines the sequence of amino acids in a protein.
3. The amino acid sequence determines the shape of the protein.
4. The shape determines how the protein works (for example the active site of an enzyme).
5. Proteins, including enzymes, control the activity of the cell.

**Therefore DNA controls cell function by controlling the production of proteins.**

## Terms that are easy to confuse

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Chromosome | A long DNA molecule with proteins, in the nucleus; contains many genes |
| Gene | A length of DNA that codes for a protein |
| Base | One of A, T, C, G; part of a nucleotide |
| Amino acid | A building block of a protein |
| Protein | A chain of amino acids, for example an enzyme |

## Links to other subtopics

- **Mutation:** a change in the base sequence of a gene can change the amino acid sequence and the shape of the protein (see inheritance).
- **Genetic modification:** a gene is cut out of one organism and put into another (see genetic modification).
- **Specialised cells:** different cells use different genes, so they make different proteins.
- **Enzymes:** the active site shape depends on the amino acid sequence.

## Short model answers

**Describe the structure of DNA.** Two strands coiled together to form a double helix. Each strand is a chain of nucleotides, each with a base. The strands are held together by bonds between pairs of bases: A with T, and C with G.

**Define a gene.** A length of DNA that codes for a protein.

**Explain how DNA controls the cell.** It controls the production of proteins, including enzymes, which carry out the reactions in the cell.

**Explain why a change in the base sequence can affect a protein.** The sequence of bases determines the sequence of amino acids. A different sequence may give a different shape, so the protein may not work.

**State the base-pairing rule.** A pairs with T, and C pairs with G.

## Exam traps

- A pairs with **T**, C with **G**. Never A with C.
- DNA is a **double** helix.
- A gene codes for a **protein**, not for a "trait".
- Bonds are **between** the strands, linking base pairs.
- Chromosomes contain **many genes**.
- Base sequence leads to amino acid sequence, which leads to **shape**.
- Do not say that the nucleotide is a base. A nucleotide **contains** a base.
- Show your working in base percentage questions.

## Self-test

1. Where is DNA found in a cell?
2. What is the shape of a DNA molecule?
3. What is each strand made of?
4. Name the four bases using their letters.
5. Which base pairs with A?
6. Which base pairs with C?
7. What holds the two strands together?
8. Define a gene.
9. What does a gene code for?
10. What does the sequence of bases determine?
11. Why does the sequence of amino acids matter?
12. If 20 per cent of the bases in a DNA sample are T, what is the percentage of G?

### Answers

1. In the chromosomes in the nucleus.
2. A double helix.
3. A chain of nucleotides.
4. A, T, C and G.
5. T.
6. G.
7. Bonds between pairs of bases.
8. A length of DNA that codes for a protein.
9. A protein.
10. The sequence of amino acids in the protein.
11. It gives the protein its shape, which decides how it works.
12. A = 20 per cent as well, so A + T = 40 per cent. C + G = 60 per cent, so G = 30 per cent.
