## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which substance is the reducing agent that converts iron(III) oxide to iron in the blast furnace?

A: calcium oxide

B: carbon dioxide

C: carbon monoxide

D: limestone **[1]**

**2.** State the name of the main ore of aluminium. **[1]**

**3.** Metal X is between magnesium and aluminium in reactivity. Suggest how X would be extracted from its oxide, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**4.** State the job of the hot air blown into the blast furnace. **[1]**

**5.** Give the chemical name of the slag formed in the blast furnace. **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Write the balanced equation for the thermal decomposition of limestone. **[1]**

**7.** *(Extended)* Write the ionic half-equation for the reaction at the anode during the extraction of aluminium. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**8.** The statements below describe stages in the blast furnace, but they are in the wrong order.

- P: Carbon monoxide reduces iron(III) oxide to iron.
- Q: Coke burns in hot air, releasing heat.
- R: Calcium oxide reacts with sand to form slag.
- S: Carbon dioxide reacts with more coke to form carbon monoxide.
- T: Limestone is decomposed by heat.

**(a)** Put stages P, Q and S in the correct order. **[2]**

**(b)** State which stage, T or R, must happen first, and explain why. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why stage R is described as a neutralisation reaction. **[2]**

**9.** *(Extended)* Consider the equation Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂.

**(a)** Identify the substance that is reduced, giving a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the substance that is oxidised, giving a reason. **[2]**

**(c)** Write the equation for the reaction in which carbon monoxide is formed. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Aluminium is extracted by the electrolysis of aluminium oxide dissolved in molten cryolite, using carbon electrodes.

**(a)** Explain why the aluminium oxide must be molten or dissolved for electrolysis to occur. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why cryolite is used. **[2]**

**(c)** Write the ionic half-equation for the formation of aluminium, and state at which electrode it happens. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why the carbon anodes need to be replaced regularly. Include an equation. **[3]**

**11.** This question is about extracting metals in simple proportions.

**(a)** Calculate the relative formula mass of iron(III) oxide, Fe₂O₃. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of iron that could be obtained from 40 tonnes of pure iron(III) oxide. **[2]**

**(c)** The relative formula mass of aluminium oxide, Al₂O₃, is 102. Calculate the maximum mass of aluminium that could be obtained from 51 tonnes of pure aluminium oxide. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why gold can be found as the uncombined metal, iron is extracted using carbon, and aluminium is extracted by electrolysis. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1].

**2.** Bauxite [1].

**3.** By electrolysis (of the molten compound) [1]: X is more reactive than aluminium, so it is above carbon in the series and carbon cannot remove the oxygen from its oxide [1].

**4.** To supply oxygen so that the coke can burn (providing heat) [1].

**5.** Calcium silicate [1].

**6.** CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ [1].

**7.** 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻: correct species [1]; balanced with electrons on the right [1].

**8. (a)** Q, S, P [2]; one mark if Q is first but S and P are reversed.

**(b)** T must happen first, because the calcium oxide used in stage R is made when limestone decomposes [1].

**(c)** Calcium oxide is a basic oxide [1] and silicon(IV) oxide (sand) is an acidic oxide; they react together to form a salt, calcium silicate [1].

**9. (a)** Iron(III) oxide / Fe₂O₃ [1], because it loses oxygen [1].

**(b)** Carbon monoxide / CO [1], because it gains oxygen to form carbon dioxide [1].

**(c)** C + CO₂ → 2CO [1].

**10. (a)** The ions must be free to move to the electrodes [1].

**(b)** Aluminium oxide dissolves in molten cryolite, so the electrolyte is molten at a much lower temperature than pure aluminium oxide [1]; this reduces the energy needed / cost [1].

**(c)** Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al [1]; at the cathode / negative electrode [1].

**(d)** Oxygen is produced at the anode [1]; it reacts with the hot carbon of the anode [1]: C + O₂ → CO₂ [1], so the anode burns away.

**11. (a)** (2 × 56) + (3 × 16) = **160** [1].

**(b)** 160 t of Fe₂O₃ gives 112 t of iron, so 40 t gives 112 × 40 ÷ 160 [1] = **28 t** [1].

**(c)** 102 t of Al₂O₃ contains 2 × 27 = 54 t of aluminium [1], so 51 t gives 54 × 51 ÷ 102 = **27 t** [1].

**12.** Gold is very unreactive, so it does not readily form compounds and is found uncombined [1]. Iron is below carbon in the reactivity series, so carbon (carbon monoxide) can remove oxygen from its oxide [1]. Aluminium is above carbon, so carbon cannot reduce its oxide and electrolysis is needed [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that carbon, rather than carbon monoxide, reduces iron(III) oxide.
- Saying limestone "removes impurities" without naming sand or slag.
- Explaining aluminium extraction by cost rather than by its position above carbon.
- Calling cryolite a catalyst.
- Writing O²⁻ → O + 2e⁻ at the anode.
- Placing aluminium formation at the anode.
- Forgetting to multiply by two for the two metal atoms in Fe₂O₃ or Al₂O₃ in a calculation.
