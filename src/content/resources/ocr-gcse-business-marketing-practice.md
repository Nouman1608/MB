## Questions

**1.** State two ways in which marketing helps a business. **[2]**

**2.** Tansy Outdoor sells walking boots from one shop and wants to know whether hikers would pay extra for waterproof boots.

**(a)** Identify two secondary sources the business could use. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one reason why Tansy Outdoor might still need primary research. **[2]**

**3.** The market for walking boots in Tansy Outdoor's region was worth £2,400,000 last year and £2,600,000 this year. Tansy Outdoor's sales were £312,000 last year and £364,000 this year.

**(a)** Calculate Tansy Outdoor's market share in each year. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in Tansy Outdoor's sales. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what these results show about Tansy Outdoor's performance. **[2]**

**4.** Zarvel Audio sells headphones. A questionnaire to 240 customers found that 138 would pay more for noise cancelling. A focus group of customers said the case "feels cheap".

**(a)** Calculate the percentage who would pay more for noise cancelling. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one way the focus group result is more useful to Zarvel Audio than the percentage. **[2]**

**5.** Orchardby Juices plans a juice bar near a university and an office block.

**(a)** Identify two bases on which it could segment its market. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how one of these could help Orchardby Juices target customers. **[2]**

**6.** Orchardby Juices makes a cup of juice for £0.95.

**(a)** Calculate the selling price using cost-plus pricing with a 60% mark-up. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one disadvantage of cost-plus pricing. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the price after a promotional reduction of 25% on the price in (a). **[1]**

**7.** Zarvel Audio launches a newly invented earbud at £149, and plans to cut the price to £99 when rivals appear.

**(a)** Identify the pricing method. **[1]**
**(b)** Analyse why this method could suit Zarvel Audio. **[3]**

**8.** Tansy Outdoor's trail socks sold 2, 9, 21, 22 and 14 thousand pairs in years 1 to 5.

**(a)** Identify the stage of the product life cycle in year 4 and give one piece of evidence. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in sales from year 4 to year 5. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how Tansy Outdoor's promotion might differ at introduction and at maturity. **[2]**

**9.** Orchardby Juices wants to attract more shoppers to its stall.

**(a)** Identify two point of sale methods it could use. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how a loss leader could still help the stall's profit. **[2]**

**10.** Tansy Outdoor, targeting hikers aged 18 to 30, has a small budget. It is choosing between social media and local radio. Discuss which it should choose. **[6]**

**11.** Zarvel Audio sells only through its website.

**(a)** Identify one other digital channel and one physical channel. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one benefit to Zarvel Audio of adding a physical channel. **[2]**

**12.** Orchardby Juices plans a bottled cold-pressed range for office workers aged 18 to 35. The bottle will sell at £3.20, with plain packaging, advertised on local radio and sold through a wholesaler to discount stores. The regional juice market grew from £3,500,000 to £4,200,000. Orchardby Juices' sales grew from £280,000 to £357,000.

**(a)** Calculate Orchardby Juices' market share in each year and the percentage change in its sales. **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate the plan and recommend any changes to the marketing mix. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two from: it helps the business identify and understand its customers; it informs customers about the product, price or where to buy; it increases sales. [1] [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "State" asks for two short points; developing them earns no extra, so spend the time elsewhere.

**2. (a)** Any two from: newspapers and magazines; census; websites; internal data such as past boot sales. [1] [1]
**(b)** Secondary data was gathered for other purposes, so it may not say whether hikers would pay extra for waterproof boots [1]. Tansy Outdoor could question hikers directly with a questionnaire or trial [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link your reason to the exact decision in the stem (the waterproof premium), not to primary research in general.

**3. (a)** Last year: 312,000 / 2,400,000 × 100 = **13%** [1]. This year: 364,000 / 2,600,000 × 100 = **14%** [1].
**(b)** (364,000 - 312,000) / 312,000 × 100 [1] = **16.7%** increase [1].
**(c)** Sales grew 16.7% and the market grew 8.3% [1], so Tansy Outdoor grew faster than the market and gained share, from 13% to 14% [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing the formula and the working gives credit a chance even if the final figure slips, and part (c) needs a comparison with the market, not just the sales figure.

**4. (a)** 138 / 240 × 100 [1] = **57.5%** [1].
**(b)** The percentage is quantitative and shows how many would pay more, not why [1]. The focus group is qualitative and shows a specific problem (a cheap-looking case) that Zarvel Audio can fix [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the type of data and say what it does or does not reveal; "it is better" on its own earns little.

**5. (a)** Any two from: age; gender; income; location; lifestyle. [1] [1]
**(b)** For example, segmenting by income: students have less to spend than office workers [1], so Orchardby Juices could offer a lower-priced small cup near the university and premium blends near the offices [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A segment alone earns the identification mark; the explanation mark needs a decision the business could make because of it.

**6. (a)** 60% of £0.95 = £0.57 [1]. £0.95 + £0.57 = **£1.52** [1].
**(b)** Cost-plus ignores what customers will pay and what rivals charge [1], so the price may be too high to sell, or lower than customers would have accepted [1].
**(c)** £1.52 × 0.75 = **£1.14** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A disadvantage should be developed, with a consequence for the business, to reach both marks.

**7. (a)** **Price skimming** [1].
**(b)** The earbud is new and few rivals exist [1], so early buyers may pay a high price to have it first [1], which recovers the cost of invention quickly before rivals force the price down [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* An analysis answer is a chain of linked steps using the stem; a list of unconnected facts does not build one.

**8. (a)** **Maturity** [1]. Sales rose only from 21 to 22 thousand, so growth has almost stopped [1].
**(b)** (14 - 22) / 22 × 100 [1] = **-36.4%** (a fall of 36.4%) [1].
**(c)** At introduction promotion tells people the socks exist [1]. At maturity it defends sales against rivals, for example by promotional offers [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* State the direction of change; a bare 36.4 might lose the meaning of the figure.

**9. (a)** Any two from: price reductions; loss leaders; competitions; free samples. [1] [1]
**(b)** A loss leader sells at a low price, even below cost [1]. Customers drawn in may buy other items at full price, so total profit can rise [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain why the loss on one item is outweighed; stating the loss leader is cheap does not explain it.

**10.** Social media can target ages 18 to 30 cheaply, because adverts can be aimed at that age group and at people interested in walking [1], so little money is wasted [1]. Local radio reaches a wider audience, including people outside the target age, so some of the cost is wasted [1], and it is usually costlier for a small budget [1]. Judgement: Tansy Outdoor should choose social media [1] because its audience and budget match, though it could add radio later if sales justify it [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A "discuss" answer scores best when both options are weighed and the final choice is justified with the stem's audience and budget.

**11. (a)** Digital: an app or an online marketplace [1]. Physical: a shop or market stall [1].
**(b)** Customers can see and try the headphones before buying [1], which may increase sales to people unwilling to buy untested sound quality online [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a benefit for this business, not simply that shops "reach more people"; the stem's product (sound) is the hook.

**12. (a)** Last year: 280,000 / 3,500,000 × 100 = **8.0%** [1]. This year: 357,000 / 4,200,000 × 100 = **8.5%** [1]. Sales change: (357,000 - 280,000) / 280,000 × 100 = **27.5%** [1].
**(b)** Orchardby Juices has grown (27.5%) faster than the market (20%), so its share is up [1], which suggests its offer works with customers. The mix has inconsistencies, though. The £3.20 price and cold-pressed product suit the target market, but plain packaging does not signal a premium product [1]. Discount stores do not match a premium price and may attract customers who will not pay it [1]. Local radio may not reach office workers aged 18 to 35 efficiently, as social media can target them [1]. Recommendation: keep the product and price, change the packaging, sell through offices, delivery or health-food shops (place), and advertise on social media [1]. This is the better choice if the extra cost of packaging is covered by the higher sales a premium image brings [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong answers use the data for part of the argument and then judge whether the four Ps fit the stated segment, ending with a reasoned recommendation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a market share figure but not comparing it with sales growth.
- Dividing by the new figure when working out a percentage change.
- Leaving out the percentage sign or the direction of a change.
- Choosing a method of research without linking it to what the business needs to find out.
- Calling qualitative data "less reliable" without saying what it reveals that numbers cannot.
- Describing skimming or penetration without saying why it suits the product and market.
- Listing the four Ps but not judging whether they fit the target segment.
- Giving a general answer on advertising with no mention of the audience or budget in the stem.
