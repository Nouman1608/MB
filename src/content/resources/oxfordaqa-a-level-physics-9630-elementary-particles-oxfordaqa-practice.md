These questions cover section 3.3.2, Elementary particles, of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), shared International AS and A-level content. Some questions also use E = hf from section 3.5. Learn the content first with the [elementary particles study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-elementary-particles-oxfordaqa/) and the [elementary particles revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-elementary-particles-oxfordaqa-revision-notes/). Data: 1 MeV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ J; h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s; c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹; rest energies: electron 0.511 MeV, proton 938.3 MeV, neutron 939.6 MeV.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Name the antiparticles of the neutron and of the neutrino, and state the charge of each. **[2]**

**2.** Compare the mass, charge and rest energy of a proton and an antiproton. **[3]**

**3.** Convert the threshold energy for proton-antiproton pair production, 1876.6 MeV, into joules. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why an electron and a positron that annihilate at rest cannot produce a single photon. **[2]**

**5.** State why pair production takes place only close to a nucleus. **[1]**

**6.** A photon has frequency 1.50 × 10²⁰ Hz. Determine whether it can produce an electron-positron pair. **[3]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A positron with kinetic energy 0.150 MeV annihilates with an electron that is at rest.

**(a)** Calculate the total energy of the photons produced, in MeV. **[2]**

**(b)** Convert this energy into joules. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the two photons do not have equal energies travelling in exactly opposite directions. **[1]**

**8.** A photon of wavelength 4.00 × 10⁻¹³ m produces an electron-positron pair.

**(a)** Calculate the energy of the photon in joules and in MeV. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the kinetic energy of each particle, assuming they share it equally. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the longest wavelength of photon that could produce this pair. **[2]**

**9.** A proton and an antiproton, each with kinetic energy 50.0 MeV, collide head-on and annihilate into two photons of equal energy.

**(a)** Calculate the energy of each photon in MeV. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the frequency of each photon. **[2]**

**(c)** State, with a reason, whether a single photon of the energy found in (a) could produce a proton-antiproton pair. **[1]**

**10.** A student writes this summary: "The antineutron has charge −1. A positron is more massive than an electron because it is positive. The antineutrino has the same charge as the neutrino." Identify each error, if any, and correct it. **[3]**

**11.**

**(a)** Use the rest energies to calculate the ratio of the mass of a proton to the mass of an electron. **[1]**

**(b)** The electron mass is 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg. Use your answer to (a) to calculate the proton mass. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the difference between the rest energies of the neutron and the proton, in joules. **[1]**

**12.** A pair of detectors on opposite sides of a sample each record a photon of energy 0.511 MeV at the same instant.

**(a)** Identify the process that produced the photons and the particles involved. **[2]**

**(b)** State what the photon energies show about the kinetic energy of the particles before the process. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the photons travel in opposite directions. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Antineutron, charge 0 [1]; antineutrino, charge 0 [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Both are neutral, but they are still distinct from the neutron and neutrino.

**2.** Mass is the same [1]; charge is opposite, +1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C for the proton and −1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C for the antiproton [1]; rest energy is the same, 938.3 MeV [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* "Compare" needs each property stated for both particles, not just a list of differences.

**3.** 1876.6 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ = 3.00 × 10⁻¹⁰ J [1] **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* Multiply, do not divide, when going from MeV to joules: the joule value is the tiny one.

**4.** The total momentum before is zero [1]; a single photon always carries momentum, so momentum would not be conserved; at least two photons moving in opposite directions are needed [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Energy conservation does not rule out one photon; the argument must be about momentum.

**5.** The nucleus takes up some momentum, so that momentum and energy can both be conserved [1] **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* "To provide energy" is wrong: the energy comes from the photon.

**6.** E = hf = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 1.50 × 10²⁰ = 9.95 × 10⁻¹⁴ J [1]; in MeV: 9.95 × 10⁻¹⁴ / 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ = 0.622 MeV [1]; this is less than the 1.022 MeV threshold, so it cannot [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The conclusion must compare with the threshold for the pair, 2 × 0.511 MeV.

**7. (a)** Rest energies = 2 × 0.511 = 1.022 MeV [1]; total = 1.022 + 0.150 = 1.172 MeV [1]

**(b)** 1.172 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ = 1.88 × 10⁻¹³ J [1]

**(c)** The positron was moving, so the total momentum before was not zero; the photons must together carry this momentum, so they cannot be equal and exactly opposite [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Only the positron's kinetic energy is added here: the electron is at rest.

**8. (a)** E = hc/λ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸) / 4.00 × 10⁻¹³ = 4.97 × 10⁻¹³ J [1]; = 3.11 MeV [1]

**(b)** Kinetic energy = 3.11 − 1.022 = 2.09 MeV in total [1]; each particle 1.04 MeV [1]

**(c)** Minimum energy 1.022 MeV = 1.64 × 10⁻¹³ J [1]; λ = hc/E = 1.22 × 10⁻¹² m [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* A minimum energy gives a maximum wavelength; check that your answer to (c) is longer than 4.00 × 10⁻¹³ m.

**9. (a)** Each particle has 938.3 + 50.0 = 988.3 MeV [1]; symmetrical, so each photon has 988.3 MeV [1]

**(b)** E = 988.3 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ = 1.58 × 10⁻¹⁰ J [1]; f = E/h = 2.39 × 10²³ Hz [1]

**(c)** No: 988.3 MeV is less than the 1876.6 MeV needed to create both a proton and an antiproton [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Annihilation needs two photons; reversing it with one photon of half the energy is not possible.

**10.** "Antineutron has charge −1" is wrong: its charge is 0 [1]; "positron more massive" is wrong: it has the same mass as the electron and the opposite charge [1]; the third statement is correct: both have zero charge [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Say explicitly when a statement is correct; leaving it unmentioned does not earn the mark.

**11. (a)** 938.3 / 0.511 = 1836 [1]

**(b)** 1836 × 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg [1]

**(c)** 939.6 − 938.3 = 1.3 MeV = 2.08 × 10⁻¹³ J [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The ratio of rest energies equals the ratio of masses, so no E = mc² calculation is needed.

**12. (a)** Annihilation [1] of an electron and a positron [1]

**(b)** Each photon has exactly the rest energy of one particle, so the particles had (negligible) kinetic energy: they were effectively at rest [1]

**(c)** The total momentum before was zero [1]; the photons must have equal and opposite momenta, so they travel in opposite directions [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Photon energy of 0.511 MeV identifies the electron mass scale; a proton-antiproton pair would give 938.3 MeV photons.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking the pair-production threshold as one rest energy.
- Using MeV in E = hf or λ = hc/E.
- Adding kinetic energy to a particle that is at rest.
- Explaining photon number with energy rather than momentum.
- Describing antiparticles as having different mass.
- Comparing a property for only one of the two particles.
