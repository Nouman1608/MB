This guide covers subtopic **11.6 Alcohols** of Topic 11, Organic chemistry, in the Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 syllabus for 2026–2028. Outcomes 1–3 are **Core**; outcome 4, comparing the two manufacturing methods, is **Supplement** and is labelled *(Extended)*. Use the [alcohols revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-alcohols-revision-notes/) for quick recall and the [alcohols practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-alcohols-practice/) to test yourself. The reactions that turn ethanol into ethanoic acid and esters are in the [carboxylic acids study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-carboxylic-acids/), and a combined overview is in [Alcohols and Carboxylic Acids](/resources/alcohols-and-carboxylic-acids/).

## What 11.6 asks you to do

- **Core 1:** describe the manufacture of ethanol by **(a) fermentation** of aqueous glucose at **25–35 °C** in the presence of **yeast** and in the **absence of oxygen**, and **(b) catalytic addition of steam to ethene** at **300 °C** and **6000 kPa / 60 atm** in the presence of an **acid catalyst**.
- **Core 2:** describe the **combustion** of ethanol.
- **Core 3:** state the uses of ethanol as **a solvent** and **a fuel**.
- **Supplement 4:** describe the **advantages and disadvantages** of each manufacturing method.

From 11.1 and 11.2 you also need the general formula **CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH**, the name and displayed formula of ethanol (Core), and the names propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol, butan-1-ol and butan-2-ol *(Extended)*.

## The alcohol functional group

Alcohols contain the **–OH** functional group bonded to a carbon atom. They are **not hydrocarbons**, because they contain oxygen. Ethanol is C₂H₅OH, structural formula CH₃CH₂OH:

```
    H   H
    |   |
H — C — C — O — H
    |   |
    H   H
```

In a displayed formula, show the O–H bond: writing "–OH" at the end of a displayed formula leaves one bond undrawn.

| Alcohol *(names beyond ethanol: Extended)* | Structural formula |
|---|---|
| methanol | CH₃OH |
| ethanol | CH₃CH₂OH |
| propan-1-ol | CH₃CH₂CH₂OH |
| propan-2-ol | CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ |
| butan-1-ol | CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH |
| butan-2-ol | CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₃ |

The number tells you which carbon carries the –OH group.

## Method 1: fermentation

**Fermentation** converts aqueous glucose into ethanol and carbon dioxide using yeast:

```
C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂
glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide
```

Each condition has a reason, and "explain" questions want the reason, not just the condition:

| Condition | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Yeast** | contains enzymes that catalyse the reaction |
| **25–35 °C** | warm enough for a reasonable rate; hotter temperatures denature the enzymes, colder ones make the reaction very slow |
| **Absence of oxygen** | with oxygen present the yeast respires aerobically, producing carbon dioxide and water instead of ethanol |
| **Aqueous** glucose | the glucose must be dissolved for the yeast to use it |

The mixture produced is **dilute aqueous ethanol** containing yeast and other substances. The yeast is removed by filtration and the ethanol is concentrated by **fractional distillation** (see [separation and purification](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-separation-and-purification/)).

## Method 2: catalytic addition of steam to ethene

Ethene, made by cracking alkanes from petroleum, reacts with **steam**:

```
C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH
```

Conditions: **300 °C**, **6000 kPa (60 atm)**, **acid catalyst**. This is an **addition** reaction, the same type described in the [alkenes study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-alkenes/): the only product is ethanol. Ethanol produced this way is pure, and the reactants flow through the reactor continuously.

Learn the two sets of conditions as two separate lists. Swapping them (yeast at 300 °C, or 6000 kPa for fermentation) is the most common error in this subtopic.

## Combustion of ethanol

Ethanol burns in oxygen to give carbon dioxide and water, releasing heat energy:

```
C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O
```

### Worked example 1: balancing an alcohol combustion equation

*Balance the complete combustion of butan-1-ol, C₄H₉OH.*

1. Carbon: 4 C, so **4CO₂**.
2. Hydrogen: 9 + 1 = **10 H**, so **5H₂O**. (Count the H in –OH.)
3. Oxygen needed on the right: (4 × 2) + 5 = 13 atoms.
4. The alcohol already supplies **1** oxygen atom, so O₂ must supply 12 atoms: **6O₂**.

```
C₄H₉OH + 6O₂ → 4CO₂ + 5H₂O
```

The trap is forgetting the oxygen atom already in the alcohol, which gives 6½ O₂ instead of 6.

## Uses of ethanol

- **Solvent:** it dissolves many substances that do not dissolve in water, for example in perfumes and some medicines.
- **Fuel:** its combustion is highly exothermic and produces carbon dioxide and water.

The syllabus only asks you to *state* these two uses.

## Worked example 2: a reacting-mass calculation *(Extended)*

*This links 11.6 to the Supplement reacting-mass calculations in Topic 3.* What mass of ethanol could be made by fermenting 90 g of glucose? (Aᵣ: C = 12, H = 1, O = 16)

1. Mᵣ of glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆ = (6 × 12) + (12 × 1) + (6 × 16) = 180.
2. Moles of glucose = 90 ÷ 180 = 0.50 mol.
3. From the equation, 1 mol glucose gives 2 mol ethanol, so 1.0 mol ethanol.
4. Mᵣ of ethanol, C₂H₅OH = (2 × 12) + (6 × 1) + 16 = 46.
5. Mass = 1.0 × 46 = **46 g**.

## Comparing the two methods *(Extended)*

Think in terms of a few headings: raw material, rate, purity, energy, and type of process.

| Heading | Fermentation | Steam + ethene |
|---|---|---|
| Raw material | glucose from crops: **renewable** | ethene from petroleum: **non-renewable** |
| Rate | **slow** | **fast** |
| Product | **dilute, impure** ethanol; needs filtering and fractional distillation | **pure** ethanol |
| Energy | **low** temperature and normal pressure: little energy needed | **high** temperature and pressure: a lot of energy needed |
| Process | stops and restarts: the vessel is emptied and refilled | **continuous** |
| Other | needs land to grow crops | needs a supply of petroleum and cracking |

A good comparison names a feature of **each** method, side by side. "Fermentation is renewable" earns a mark; "fermentation is better for the environment" does not, because it gives no reason.

### Annotated model answer *(Extended)*

**Question:** Give one advantage and one disadvantage of making ethanol by fermentation rather than from ethene. [2]

**Answer:** Advantage: fermentation uses glucose from crops, which is a renewable resource, while ethene comes from non-renewable petroleum [1]. Disadvantage: fermentation is much slower and produces dilute, impure ethanol that has to be distilled [1].

*Why it scores:* each point is specific and is compared with the other method.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing "warm" instead of 25–35 °C**, or "high pressure" without a value for the ethene method.
- **Saying fermentation needs oxygen.** It must be anaerobic.
- **Forgetting the acid catalyst** for the steam method, or naming yeast as its catalyst.
- **Calling ethanol a hydrocarbon.**
- **Miscounting oxygen** in combustion equations by ignoring the O in the alcohol.
- **Vague comparisons** such as "cheaper" or "better" without a reason.

## Exam technique

- **Describe** (manufacture): reactants, all conditions, products.
- **Explain** (a condition): link it to the yeast enzymes or to aerobic respiration.
- **State** (uses): solvent; fuel.
- **Describe advantages and disadvantages** *(Extended)*: one point per mark, each tied to a heading such as renewable, rate, purity or energy.
