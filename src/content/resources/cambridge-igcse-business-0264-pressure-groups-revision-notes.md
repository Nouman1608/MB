Condensed notes for section 6.5 (Pressure groups) of the Cambridge IGCSE Business 0264 syllabus for examination in 2027, 2028 and 2029. The content statement is 6.5.1 The role of pressure groups: "how pressure groups can influence business decisions". Two campaigns compared and a model answer are in the [pressure groups study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-business-0264-pressure-groups/). Test yourself with the [pressure groups practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-business-0264-pressure-groups-practice/). The combined [ethical issues and pressure groups notes](/resources/igcse-business-0264-business-and-ethical-issues-and-pressure-groups-revision-notes/) also cover section 6.4.

All examples are invented.

## Definition

**Pressure group:** an organised group of people who share a concern and campaign to change the decisions of businesses or government.

- Concerns include the environment, animal welfare, workers' rights, consumer safety and local issues.
- Members need not be customers, employees or neighbours of the business.
- A pressure group has **no legal power** over a business: it can only influence.

## The three levers

| Lever | Methods | How it affects the business |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | Boycotts; asking customers to switch to rivals | Lower revenue and profit |
| Reputation | News stories, social media, petitions, peaceful protests | Damaged brand; customers, staff and investors put off |
| The law | Lobbying government for new laws or rules | Every firm in the industry must change |

Also: **direct talks** with managers, and **praise** for firms that improve.

## Decisions pressure groups influence

| Decision | Example |
|---|---|
| What to produce | Removing a harmful ingredient |
| How to produce | Cleaner equipment to stop pollution |
| Where to locate | Choosing a site away from homes or a school |
| Which suppliers to use | Dropping a supplier using child labour |
| How to promote | Changing adverts aimed at children |
| Packaging | Switching from single-use plastic |

## When a group has more influence

- Strong public support and an issue that is simple to understand.
- Wide media and social media coverage.
- The business sells to the public under a well-known brand.
- Customers can easily switch to rivals.
- The change asked for is affordable and clear.
- The group is large, organised and well funded.

**Less influence:** business sells to other businesses; few alternatives for customers; costly or vague demands; little public interest.

## How a business can respond

| Response | Suits when... |
|---|---|
| Make the change | Cost is affordable; strong support for the group |
| Negotiate part of the change | Full demand too costly |
| Explain its side publicly | Group's claims are inaccurate |
| Ignore | Support is small and the business is little known (risky) |

## Explain chain for answers

Method, then effect on customers or government, then effect on sales, reputation or the law, then the decision that changes.

Example: "The group shares videos of the polluted river online, so customers learn about it and some stop buying, which cuts sales, so the firm may decide to install filters."

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pressure group | Organised group campaigning to change business or government decisions |
| Boycott | Customers refusing to buy from a business to pressure it to change |
| Petition | A written request signed by many people, showing support for a demand |
| Lobbying | Trying to persuade government to change laws or rules |
| Publicity | Attention from the media and the public |
| Campaign | Organised activity aimed at a particular change |

## Exam traps

- A pressure group *influences*; it cannot *force*.
- Always name the decision that changes, not just the method used.
- Judge strength from the case: Who are the customers? How well known is the brand? What would the change cost?
- A business does not always give in; negotiating or explaining can be sensible.
- Lobbying works through government, not directly on the business.

## Quick self-test

1. Define a pressure group.
2. Name three methods pressure groups use.
3. What is a boycott?
4. What is lobbying?
5. Give two business decisions a pressure group might influence.
6. Why is a well-known consumer brand more vulnerable to pressure groups?
7. Why might a firm that sells only to other businesses be less affected by a boycott?
8. Give two factors that increase a pressure group's influence.
9. State two ways a business could respond to a campaign.
10. Explain how lobbying could affect a whole industry.
11. 1,500 of a gym's 6,000 members sign a petition. What percentage is this?
12. Give one way a pressure group could benefit a business.

### Answers

1. An organised group of people who share a concern and campaign to change the decisions of businesses or government.
2. Any three: boycotts, publicity through the media or social media, petitions, protests, lobbying government, direct talks.
3. Customers refusing to buy from a business to pressure it to change.
4. Trying to persuade government to change laws or rules.
5. Any two: what to produce; how to produce; where to locate; which suppliers to use; how to promote; packaging.
6. More people know its name, its customers can see the campaign, and it has more reputation to lose.
7. Other businesses buy on price and quality and the public may not know its name, so publicity has less effect on its sales.
8. Any two: strong public support; media coverage; well-known brand; customers can switch easily; affordable demand; large, organised group.
9. Any two: make the change; negotiate a partial change; explain its position; ignore the campaign.
10. If government passes a new law, every business in the industry must follow it, not just the one targeted.
11. 1,500 / 6,000 x 100 = 25%.
12. Any one: praise that can be used in promotion; early warning of an issue customers care about.
