**What do these questions practise?** Section 1 The land of Pakistan in Cambridge IGCSE Pakistan
Studies 0448 Paper 2: (a) location, (b) administrative areas and cities, (c) natural topography and
drainage, and (d) climate, including the monsoon and the influence of relief and climate on human
activity. Paper 2 questions on this section usually start from a map, so practise with a blank
outline map of Pakistan beside you.

## Section A: location and map skills

**1.** Name the line of latitude that passes through the south of Pakistan close to Karachi, and
state whether it lies north or south of the city. **[2]**

**2.** Name **four** countries that share a border with Pakistan. **[4]**

**3.** Name the sea that forms Pakistan's southern coastline. **[1]**

**4.** Name the city that is Pakistan's capital and the city that lies next to it. **[2]**

**5.** Name **two** cities in Balochistan and **two** in Sindh named in the syllabus. **[4]**

## Section B: topography and drainage

**6.** Name **two** mountain ranges in the north of Pakistan and **one** plateau in the
south-west. **[3]**

**7.** Name **three** tributaries of the Indus that join it from the east. **[3]**

**8.** Name the river that joins the Indus from the west near Attock. **[1]**

**9.** Name **one** desert in Sindh and **one** in Punjab. **[2]**

**10.** Explain how steep slopes in the northern mountains affect the way land is used. **[3]**

**11.** Explain why mountains and deserts make it difficult to build road and rail networks. **[4]**

## Section C: climate

**12.** Explain the causes of the summer monsoon. **[5]**

**13.** Describe the characteristics of the climate of the arid region of Pakistan. **[3]**

**14.** Explain why winter rainfall is higher in the north-west than in the south of Pakistan. **[3]**

**15.** Explain why day length in summer is longer in Gilgit than in Karachi. **[2]**

**16.** "Heavy monsoon rain brings more problems than benefits to Pakistan." To what extent do you
agree? **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Tropic of Cancer [1], which lies south of Karachi [1].

**2.** Any four: India, China, Afghanistan, Iran [4].

**3.** Arabian Sea [1].

**4.** Islamabad [1]; Rawalpindi [1].

**5.** Balochistan: Quetta and Gwadar [2]. Sindh: Karachi and Hyderabad [2].

**6.** Mountains, any two: Himalaya, Karakoram, Hindu Kush [2]. Plateau: Balochistan Plateau [1].

**7.** Any three: Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Sutlej [3].

**8.** Kabul River [1].

**9.** Sindh: Thar [1]. Punjab: Thal [1].

**10.** Steep slopes have thin soils that are easily eroded, so large-scale farming is difficult [1].
Farmers cut terraces to create flat fields for crops such as maize and fruit [1]. Steeper land is
left for forest or for grazing sheep and goats [1].

**11.** In mountains, steep gradients mean roads and railways must zigzag or be tunnelled, which is
costly [1]; landslides, snow and earthquakes block routes and raise maintenance costs [1]. In
deserts, shifting sand covers tracks and roads [1], and very high temperatures and the lack of water
make construction and maintenance difficult; low population means little demand to justify the
cost [1].

**12.** In summer the land heats up much faster than the sea [1]. Hot air rises over the land,
creating an area of low pressure over the subcontinent [1]. The sea stays cooler, so higher
pressure lies over the Indian Ocean [1]. Moist winds blow from the sea towards the low pressure
[1]. As they rise over land and hills they cool and condense, giving heavy rain, mainly in July and
August [1].

**13.** Very hot summers and mild or cool winters [1]; very low and unreliable rainfall, under about
250 mm a year [1]; high evaporation and a large daily range of temperature [1].

**14.** Western depressions enter Pakistan from the west in winter [1] and bring rain and snow mainly
to the north-west and northern mountains [1]. They have weakened or lost moisture by the time they
reach the south, which stays dry [1].

**15.** Gilgit is at a higher latitude, further north than Karachi [1]. In the Northern Hemisphere
summer, places further from the equator have longer days [1].

**16.** Benefits: monsoon rain supplies most of the country's rainfall [1], fills reservoirs such as
Tarbela and Mangla for irrigation and hydel power [1], and waters kharif crops such as rice [1].
Problems: floods damage crops, homes, roads and railways [1]; disease spreads in standing water [1];
erosion and landslides in hilly areas [1]. A supported judgement, for example that the benefits
outweigh the problems if dams and flood defences store and control the water, is needed for full
marks. Maximum 6.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know how the mountains were formed?**
No. The 0448 syllabus says knowledge of the
formation of Pakistan's natural topography is not required, but you must use the right vocabulary to
describe mountains, plateaux, floodplains and deserts.

**Which rain types should I explain?**
You must understand the causes of the monsoon. You should know
the distribution of convectional rain and depressions, but the syllabus does not require you to
explain their causes.
