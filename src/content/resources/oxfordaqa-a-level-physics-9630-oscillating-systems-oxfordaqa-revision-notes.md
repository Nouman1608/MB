## The two period equations

| System | Equation | Depends on | Independent of | Condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mass-spring | T = 2π√(m/k) | m, k | amplitude, g | Hooke's law obeyed |
| Simple pendulum | T = 2π√(l/g) | l, g | mass, amplitude | Small angles (below about 10°) |

Rearranged: k = 4π²m/T²; l = gT²/(4π²); g = 4π²l/T². Frequency f = 1/T.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Equilibrium position | Where the restoring force is zero |
| Amplitude | Maximum displacement from equilibrium |
| Period | Time for one complete oscillation |
| Damping | Energy transfer to the surroundings by resistive forces, reducing amplitude |
| Light damping | Many oscillations with slowly falling amplitude; period nearly unchanged |
| Critical damping | Return to equilibrium in the shortest time without oscillating |
| Heavy damping | Return to equilibrium without oscillating, more slowly than critical |
| Fiducial marker | Fixed reference pointer used to start and stop timing |

## Scaling

| Change | Effect on T |
|---|---|
| m × 4 or l × 4 | × 2 |
| k × 4 or g × 4 | × 1/2 |
| Amplitude halved (within limits) | No change |
| Bob mass doubled | No change |

## Energy graphs (undamped)

| Graph | Ek | Ep | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Against displacement | Maximum at x = 0, zero at ±A (inverted parabola) | Zero at x = 0, maximum at ±A (parabola) | Horizontal line |
| Against time | Two peaks per period, never negative | Two peaks per period, out of step with Ek | Horizontal line |

With damping, the total energy falls with time and both peaks shrink.

Useful numbers: total energy = ½kA² for a horizontal spring; for a pendulum, total = mgh at the highest point = ½mv² at the lowest.

## Required practical 4

- Time 10 to 20 oscillations; repeat and average.
- Start and stop timing at the equilibrium position using a fiducial marker (fastest point, sharpest timing).
- Small amplitudes: small-angle condition for the pendulum, Hooke's law for the spring.
- Pendulum length from suspension to centre of the bob.
- Plot T² against m (gradient 4π²/k) or T² against l (gradient 4π²/g).
- A constant error in l (for example, measuring to the top of the bob) shifts the line but leaves the gradient unchanged.

## Worked reminder

A pendulum bob of mass 0.080 kg is released from 12 mm above its lowest point.

1. Total energy = mgh = 0.080 × 9.81 × 0.012 = 9.4 × 10⁻³ J.
2. At the lowest point all of it is kinetic: v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.81 × 0.012) = 0.49 m s⁻¹.
3. Halfway down in height, Ep and Ek are equal: 4.7 × 10⁻³ J each. The speed does not depend on the mass.

## Damping on a displacement-time graph

| Damping | What the graph shows | Example |
|---|---|---|
| None (ideal) | Constant amplitude for ever | Idealised pendulum |
| Light | Peaks shrink gradually; peak spacing (period) nearly constant | Pendulum swinging in air |
| Critical | Falls to zero in the shortest time, no overshoot | Car suspension with shock absorbers (designed close to critical) |
| Heavy | Creeps slowly back to zero, no overshoot | Oscillator in thick oil |

## Exam traps

- T used where T² is needed, or total time not divided by the number of oscillations.
- Mass said to affect a pendulum's period, or g a mass-spring period.
- Energy-time graph drawn with the same period as displacement.
- Negative kinetic or potential energy drawn.
- Critical and heavy damping confused.
- Damping said to change the period a lot.

## Quick self-test

1. Find the period of a 0.25 kg mass on a spring of constant 16 N m⁻¹.
2. Find the length of a simple pendulum with period 1.2 s.
3. The mass on a spring is increased by a factor of 4. What happens to the period?
4. Name two quantities that do not affect the period of a simple pendulum.
5. A horizontal mass-spring system has k = 30 N m⁻¹ and amplitude 0.040 m. Find its total energy.
6. An oscillator has period 0.80 s. What is the period of its kinetic energy?
7. What effect does light damping have on the amplitude and on the period?
8. Define critical damping.
9. Why is timing started as the bob passes the equilibrium position?
10. A graph of T² against l has gradient 4.0 s² m⁻¹. Find g.
11. Why does a mass-spring system have the same period on the Moon as on Earth?
12. Why are 20 oscillations timed rather than one?

### Answers

1. T = 2π√(0.25/16) = 0.79 s.
2. l = 9.81 × 1.2² / (4π²) = 0.36 m.
3. It doubles.
4. Mass of the bob; amplitude (for small angles).
5. ½kA² = ½ × 30 × 0.040² = 0.024 J.
6. 0.40 s.
7. The amplitude decreases gradually; the period is almost unchanged.
8. Damping that returns the system to equilibrium in the shortest possible time without oscillating.
9. The bob moves fastest there, so the instant it passes the marker is sharpest and timing uncertainty is smallest.
10. g = 4π² / 4.0 = 9.9 m s⁻².
11. Its period depends only on m and k, and neither changes; g does not appear in the equation.
12. Reaction-time uncertainty becomes a much smaller fraction of the measured time.
