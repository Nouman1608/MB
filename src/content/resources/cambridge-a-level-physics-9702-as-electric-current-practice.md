## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by an electric current, and name the charge carriers in a copper wire and in a solution of copper sulfate. **[3]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by the statement that charge is *quantised*. State which of the following charges could be carried by an oil droplet: 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, 7.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, 2.4 × 10⁻¹⁸ C. **[3]**

**3.** A current of 0.40 A flows in a resistor for 5.0 minutes. Calculate the charge that passes through it. **[2]**

**4.** A current of 8.0 mA is carried by electrons. Calculate the number of electrons passing a point in the circuit each second. **[2]**

**5.** Define the coulomb. **[1]**

**6.** In the equation I = Anvq, state what is represented by n, and give its SI unit. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The current in a circuit falls steadily from 50 mA to zero over a time of 6.0 s.

**(a)** State how the charge that flows can be found from a graph of current against time. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the charge that flows. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the number of electrons that pass a point in the circuit in this time. **[2]**

**8.** A copper wire has a cross-sectional area of 1.0 mm² and carries a current of 2.0 A. The number density of free electrons in copper is 8.5 × 10²⁸ m⁻³.

**(a)** Calculate the mean drift speed of the electrons. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the time taken for an electron to drift 1.0 m along the wire, in hours. **[2]**

**(c)** A lamp 1.0 m from a switch lights almost as soon as the switch is closed. Explain why, despite your answer to (b). **[2]**

**9.** Two wires X and Y, made of the same metal, are connected in series in a circuit. Wire X has half the diameter of wire Y.

**(a)** Compare the currents in X and Y, giving a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** Determine the ratio of the drift speed in X to the drift speed in Y. **[2]**

**10.** A sample of a semiconducting material has a cross-sectional area of 4.0 × 10⁻⁶ m² and contains 2.0 × 10²³ charge carriers per cubic metre, each of charge 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. It carries a current of 5.0 mA.

**(a)** Calculate the drift speed of the charge carriers. **[2]**

**(b)** A copper wire of the same cross-sectional area carries the same current. Copper has 8.5 × 10²⁸ free electrons per cubic metre. Calculate the ratio of the drift speed in the semiconductor to the drift speed in the copper. **[2]**

**11.** In an electrolysis experiment, 2.5 × 10¹⁸ copper ions, each carrying a charge of +2e, reach the negative electrode in 20 s. All the current in the solution near the electrode is carried by these ions.

**(a)** Calculate the charge carried by the ions. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the current. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the charge on each ion is a whole-number multiple of e. **[1]**

**12.** A conductor has cross-sectional area A and contains n charge carriers per unit volume, each of charge q, moving with mean drift speed v. Derive the expression I = Anvq. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Current is the rate of flow of charge [1]. Copper wire: free (delocalised) electrons [1]. Copper sulfate solution: ions (positive copper ions and negative sulfate ions) [1].

**2.** Charge exists only in whole-number multiples of the elementary charge e [1]. 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁹ C = 4e (possible) and 2.4 × 10⁻¹⁸ C = 15e (possible) [1]; 7.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ C = 4.5e, which is not possible [1].

**3.** Q = It = 0.40 × (5.0 × 60) [1] = 120 C [1].

**4.** Charge per second = 8.0 × 10⁻³ C; number = 8.0 × 10⁻³ ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ [1] = 5.0 × 10¹⁶ [1].

**5.** The charge that passes a point when a current of one ampere flows for one second [1].

**6.** The number of charge carriers per unit volume (number density) [1]; unit m⁻³ [1].

**7. (a)** Charge is the area under the current–time graph [1].

**(b)** Area of triangle = ½ × 0.050 × 6.0 [1] = 0.15 C [1].

**(c)** Number = 0.15 ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ [1] = 9.4 × 10¹⁷ [1].

**8. (a)** A = 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ m² [1]; v = I ÷ (Anq) = 2.0 ÷ (1.0 × 10⁻⁶ × 8.5 × 10²⁸ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) [1] = 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ m s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** t = 1.0 ÷ 1.47 × 10⁻⁴ = 6.8 × 10³ s [1] = 1.9 hours [1].

**(c)** The wire is already full of free electrons [1]; closing the switch sets up an electric field round the whole circuit almost instantly, so electrons everywhere, including in the lamp filament, begin to drift at the same time [1].

**9. (a)** The currents are equal [1], because X and Y are in series and charge is conserved, so the same charge passes through each per second [1].

**(b)** v = I/(Anq), with I, n and q the same, so v ∝ 1/A [1]. Half the diameter gives a quarter of the area, so v_X : v_Y = 4 : 1 [1].

**10. (a)** v = I ÷ (Anq) = 5.0 × 10⁻³ ÷ (4.0 × 10⁻⁶ × 2.0 × 10²³ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) [1] = 0.039 m s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** With I, A and q the same, v ∝ 1/n [1]; ratio = 8.5 × 10²⁸ ÷ 2.0 × 10²³ = 4.3 × 10⁵ [1].

**11. (a)** Charge per ion = 2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ C [1]; total = 2.5 × 10¹⁸ × 3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 0.80 C [1].

**(b)** I = Q ÷ t = 0.80 ÷ 20 = 0.040 A [1].

**(c)** An ion's charge comes from gaining or losing whole electrons, and each electron carries charge e, so the ion's charge is a whole-number multiple of e [1].

**12.** In time t, each carrier moves a distance vt [1]. The carriers that pass a cross-section in time t are those in a volume Avt, so their number is nAvt [1]. The charge passing is therefore Q = nAvtq [1]. Current I = Q/t = nAvtq/t = Anvq [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining current as "flow of electrons", which is wrong for electrolytes.
- Multiplying the starting or final current by the time when the current changes.
- Not converting mm² to m² (1 mm² = 1.0 × 10⁻⁶ m²).
- Using the diameter as the radius when calculating a cross-sectional area.
- Stating that the thinner wire in a series circuit has a smaller current.
- Forgetting that an ion of charge +2e carries 3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, not 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.
