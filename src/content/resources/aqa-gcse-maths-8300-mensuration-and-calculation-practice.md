These questions cover section 3.4.2, **Mensuration and calculation** (G14 to G23), of the AQA GCSE Mathematics (8300) specification. Questions 1 to 9 suit both tiers. Questions 10 to 13 test Higher tier only content (areas and volumes of similar figures, the sine and cosine rules, the area formula ½ab sin C and trigonometry in three dimensions) and are labelled. A calculator is needed except where stated. Learn the methods first in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-maths-8300-mensuration-and-calculation/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-maths-8300-mensuration-and-calculation-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.**

**(a)** The bearing of B from A is 072°. Work out the bearing of A from B. **[1]**

**(b)** On a map with scale 1 : 25 000, two villages are 8.4 cm apart. Work out the real distance in kilometres. **[2]**

**2.** A garden is a rectangle 12 m long and 8 m wide with a semicircle added on one of the 8 m sides. Work out the total area of the garden. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**3.** A cylinder has radius 7 cm and height 15 cm.

**(a)** Work out its volume. Give your answer in terms of π. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out its curved surface area. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**4.** A solid hemisphere has radius 3 cm. Give each answer in terms of π.

**(a)** Work out its volume. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out its total surface area, including the flat circular face. **[2]**

**5.** A sector of a circle has radius 6 cm and angle 120°.

**(a)** Work out the arc length. Give your answer in terms of π. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out the area of the sector. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**6.** A right-angled triangle has shorter sides of length 9 cm and 12 cm.

**(a)** Work out the length of the hypotenuse. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out the size of the smaller acute angle, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**7.**

**(a)** A ramp is 12 m long and makes an angle of 32° with the horizontal. Work out the height of the top of the ramp above the ground, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**(b)** A wire is stretched from the top of a post to a point on level ground. The wire is 11 m long, and the point is 8 m from the foot of the post. Work out the angle between the wire and the ground, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**8.** (non-calculator) Use exact values to work out:

**(a)** sin 30° + cos 60° **[1]**

**(b)** tan 60° × cos 30°. Give your answer as a fraction. **[2]**

**9.** Two bottles are similar. The small bottle is 15 cm high and the large bottle is 25 cm high. The base of the small bottle is 6 cm wide. Work out the width of the base of the large bottle. **[2]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) Two solids, A and B, are mathematically similar. The lengths of A and B are in the ratio 3 : 5. The surface area of A is 90 cm² and the volume of A is 54 cm³.

**(a)** Work out the surface area of B. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out the volume of B. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) In triangle ABC, AB = 6 cm, AC = 8 cm and angle BAC = 110°.

**(a)** Work out the length of BC, to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**(b)** Work out the area of triangle ABC, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**12.** (Higher tier only)

**(a)** In triangle PQR, angle P = 48°, angle Q = 70° and QR = 12 cm. Work out the length of PR, to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**(b)** A triangle has sides 7 cm, 9 cm and 12 cm. Work out the size of the largest angle, to 1 decimal place. **[3]**

**13.** (Higher tier only) A cuboid measures 5 cm by 12 cm by 9 cm. The 9 cm edge is vertical.

**(a)** Work out the length of the longest diagonal, passing through the cuboid from one corner to the opposite corner. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**(b)** Work out the angle between this diagonal and the base of the cuboid, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** 072° + 180° = **252°** [1]
**(b)** 8.4 × 25 000 = 210 000 cm [1] = 2100 m = **2.1 km** [1]

**2.** Rectangle: 12 × 8 = 96 m² [1]. Semicircle has diameter 8, so radius 4: ½ × π × 4² = 8π [1]. Total = 96 + 8π = 121.13… = **121 m²** (3 s.f.) [1]

**3.** **(a)** Volume = π × 7² × 15 [1] = **735π cm³** [1]
**(b)** Curved surface area = 2π × 7 × 15 [1] = 210π = **660 cm²** (3 s.f.) [1]

**4.** **(a)** Volume = ½ × 4/3 × π × 3³ [1] = **18π cm³** [1]
**(b)** Curved surface = ½ × 4π × 3² = 18π [1]; add the flat face π × 3² = 9π; total = **27π cm²** [1]

**5.** **(a)** Arc = 120/360 × 2π × 6 [1] = **4π cm** [1]
**(b)** Area = 120/360 × π × 6² [1] = 12π = **37.7 cm²** (3 s.f.) [1]

**6.** **(a)** √(9² + 12²) = √(81 + 144) [1] = √225 = **15 cm** [1]
**(b)** The smaller angle is opposite the 9 cm side: tan θ = 9/12 [1]; θ = **36.9°** [1]

**7.** **(a)** Height = 12 × sin 32° [1] = **6.36 m** [1]
**(b)** cos θ = 8/11 [1]; θ = **43.3°** [1]

**8.** **(a)** 1/2 + 1/2 = **1** [1]
**(b)** tan 60° = √3 and cos 30° = √3/2 [1]; product = 3/2 [1]

**9.** Scale factor = 25 ÷ 15 = 5/3 [1]. Width = 6 × 5/3 = **10 cm** [1]

**10.** (Higher tier only) Length scale factor = 5/3, so area scale factor = 25/9 and volume scale factor = 125/27.
**(a)** 90 × 25/9 [1] = **250 cm²** [1]
**(b)** 54 × 125/27 [1] = **250 cm³** [1]

**11.** (Higher tier only) **(a)** BC² = 6² + 8² − 2 × 6 × 8 × cos 110° [1] = 100 + 32.83… = 132.83… [1]; BC = **11.5 cm** (3 s.f.) [1]
**(b)** Area = ½ × 6 × 8 × sin 110° [1] = **22.6 cm²** (3 s.f.) [1]

**12.** (Higher tier only) **(a)** Side QR is opposite angle P and PR is opposite angle Q. PR/sin 70° = 12/sin 48° [1]; PR = 12 × sin 70° ÷ sin 48° [1] = **15.2 cm** (3 s.f.) [1]
**(b)** The largest angle is opposite the longest side (12 cm). cos A = (7² + 9² − 12²) ÷ (2 × 7 × 9) [1] (numerator 49 + 81 − 144 = −14) = −14/126 = −0.111… [1]; A = **96.4°** [1]

**13.** (Higher tier only) **(a)** Longest diagonal = √(5² + 12² + 9²) [1] = √(25 + 144 + 81) [1] = √250 = **15.8 cm** [1]
**(b)** The diagonal of the base is √(5² + 12²) = 13 cm [1]. tan θ = 9/13, so θ = **34.7°** [1]

## Which questions are Higher tier only?

Questions 10 to 13 are labelled Higher tier only. Question 10 uses the relationships between lengths, areas and volumes in similar figures (G19). Questions 11 and 12 use the sine rule, cosine rule and the area formula (G22, G23). Question 13 uses Pythagoras and trigonometry in three dimensions (G20). Questions 1 to 9 use content that both tiers are assessed on, including bearings, areas and volumes of prisms, cylinders and spheres, sectors, right-angled trigonometry in two dimensions, exact values and lengths in similar figures.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the diameter instead of the radius in circle formulae.
- Giving a bearing with fewer than three figures or the wrong direction.
- Leaving the calculator in radians.
- Using the sine rule for a side that is not opposite the known angle.
- (Higher tier only) Using k for areas or volumes instead of k² and k³.
