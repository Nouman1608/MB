## Questions

**1.** Agar plates inoculated with bacteria are sealed with short strips of tape and incubated upside down at 25 °C. Explain each of these three precautions. **[3]**

**2.** A broth culture of bacteria was diluted three times, each time by adding 1 cm³ to 99 cm³ of sterile diluent. A 0.2 cm³ sample of the final tube was spread on agar and gave a mean of 38 colonies. Calculate the number of colony-forming units per cm³ in the original culture. **[3]**

**3.** A yeast culture was diluted 1 in 20. A haemocytometer counted 28, 33, 30 and 33 cells in four large squares. Each large square has an area of 1 mm² and the chamber is 0.1 mm deep. Calculate the number of cells per cm³ in the original culture. **[4]**

**4.** Compare dilution plating with the use of a colorimeter to follow the growth of a bacterial culture over 12 hours. **[4]**

**5.** A colorimeter was calibrated with samples of known cell concentration.

| Absorbance | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.30 | 0.40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cells cm⁻³ | 1.0 × 10⁷ | 2.1 × 10⁷ | 3.0 × 10⁷ | 3.9 × 10⁷ |

**(a)** A sample reads 0.26. Estimate its cell concentration. **[2]**
**(b)** A very dense sample reads 1.8. Suggest why it should be diluted and read again. **[2]**

**6.** A 20 cm³ sample of culture has a dry mass of 0.031 g at the start and 0.109 g after 6 hours.
**(a)** Calculate the increase in dry mass in g dm⁻³. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean rate of increase in g dm⁻³ h⁻¹. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why dry mass is used rather than wet mass. **[1]**

**7.** In a closed batch culture the number of viable bacterial cells changes in four phases.
**(a)** Explain why the number of viable cells stays almost constant in the stationary phase. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the number falls in the death phase. **[2]**
**(c)** In the death phase the viable count fell from 3.6 × 10⁷ to 9.0 × 10⁶ cells cm⁻³. Calculate the percentage fall. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the number barely changes during the lag phase. **[2]**

**8.** During exponential growth a culture rose from 4.0 × 10⁵ cells cm⁻³ at 3 h to 6.4 × 10⁷ cells cm⁻³ at 7 h. Calculate the growth rate constant, k, in generations per hour, and the generation time in minutes. **[5]**

**9.** A culture in exponential growth has k = 1.5 generations per hour. Starting from 5.0 × 10⁴ cells cm⁻³, calculate how long it takes to reach 1.0 × 10⁷ cells cm⁻³. **[4]**

**10.** Plan an investigation into the effect of glucose concentration on the rate of growth of yeast in a liquid culture. Include how you would measure growth, control variables, and take account of safety and ethical use of the organisms. **[8]**

**11.** The absorbance of a bacterial culture in nutrient broth at 25 °C was measured every hour.

| Time (h) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Absorbance | 0.05 | 0.05 | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.40 | 0.62 |

**(a)** Identify the phase between 1 h and 4 h and justify your answer using the data. **[2]**
**(b)** Assume absorbance is proportional to cell number in this range. Calculate k and the generation time. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest why the increase from 4 h to 5 h is smaller than doubling. **[2]**
**(d)** State one limitation of using absorbance to follow this culture. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Short strips of tape, not fully sealed, let oxygen enter [1], so strict anaerobes that could be harmful are not encouraged. Inverting the plate stops condensation dripping on the agar and spreading or merging the colonies [1]. A modest temperature of 25 °C does not favour human pathogens that may have entered by accident [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Each precaution needs its reason. "To stop contamination" for all three repeats one idea and gains less than a different reason for each.

**2.** Three 1 in 100 dilutions give a dilution factor of 10⁻⁶ [1]. 38 ÷ 0.2 = 190 CFU cm⁻³ in the final tube [1]. 190 × 10⁶ = **1.9 × 10⁸ CFU cm⁻³** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the volume plated and the dilution factor in the working. Showing each step makes a slip in the dilution factor easy to spot and correct.

**3.** Mean = (28 + 33 + 30 + 33) ÷ 4 = 31 cells per square [1]. Volume of one square = 1 × 1 × 0.1 = 0.1 mm³ = 1 × 10⁻⁴ cm³ [1]. 31 ÷ 10⁻⁴ = 3.1 × 10⁵ cells cm⁻³ in the diluted sample [1]. Multiply by 20: **6.2 × 10⁶ cells cm⁻³** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Unit conversion is where most working goes wrong. Show 0.1 mm³ becoming cm³, because 1000 mm³ equals 1 cm³.

**4.** Dilution plating counts only viable cells, whereas absorbance includes dead cells [1]. Plating needs serial dilution and a day or more of incubation, whereas a colorimeter gives an immediate reading [1]. The colorimeter can follow the same culture repeatedly without much sampling [1], but absorbance is not proportional to cell number at high density, whereas plating is valid when plates have a countable number of colonies [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both methods addressed on the same point. Describing each separately without a link such as "whereas" can lose credit.

**5. (a)** 0.26 lies between 0.20 and 0.30 [1]. 2.1 × 10⁷ + 0.6 × (3.0 × 10⁷ − 2.1 × 10⁷) = **2.6 × 10⁷ cells cm⁻³** (2.64 × 10⁷) [1].
**(b)** At high density, absorbance is no longer proportional to the cell number, so it would not be reliable [1]. Diluting the sample brings it into the calibrated range, and the result is then multiplied by the dilution factor [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Read from the calibration data only within the range it covers. Show the interpolation, not just the answer.

**6. (a)** 0.109 − 0.031 = 0.078 g in 20 cm³ [1]. × 50 = **3.9 g dm⁻³** [1].
**(b)** 3.9 ÷ 6 = **0.65 g dm⁻³ h⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** Wet mass includes a variable amount of water, so it is less reliable than dry mass [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the unit in every part. Mentioning that the sample is dried until the mass is constant shows why the method is reliable.

**7. (a)** The rate of cell division equals the rate of cell death [1], because nutrients are running out and toxic waste is building up [1].
**(b)** Cells die faster than they divide [1], because nutrients are exhausted and waste products are at a toxic concentration [1].
**(c)** (3.6 × 10⁷ − 9.0 × 10⁶) ÷ 3.6 × 10⁷ × 100 = **75%** [1].
**(d)** Cells are adjusting to the new medium [1] by making enzymes and taking up nutrients, so few cells divide yet [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* "Stationary" is explained by a balance of two rates. Writing that "growth stops" without that balance is less complete.

**8.** log₁₀ (6.4 × 10⁷) − log₁₀ (4.0 × 10⁵) = 7.806 − 5.602 = 2.204 [1]. n = 2.204 ÷ 0.301 = 7.32 generations [1]. Time = 7 − 3 = 4 h, so k = 7.32 ÷ 4 = **1.83 generations per hour** [1]. g = 1 ÷ 1.83 = 0.546 h [1] = **33 minutes** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the log difference and the division by 0.301. State the unit for k. Converting hours to minutes at the end is an easy step to omit.

**9.** n = (log₁₀ 1.0 × 10⁷ − log₁₀ 5.0 × 10⁴) ÷ 0.301 = (7 − 4.699) ÷ 0.301 [1]. n = 7.64 generations [1]. t = n ÷ k = 7.64 ÷ 1.5 [1] = **5.1 hours** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Rearranging k = n ÷ t is the skill being tested. A check that 2 to the power 7.64 is about 200 confirms the answer is reasonable.

**10.** Choose several glucose concentrations, for example 0, 1, 2, 4 and 8 g dm⁻³ in sterile nutrient broth [1]. Add the same volume and density of yeast suspension to each flask, using aseptic technique [1]. Keep temperature, pH, volume of broth, flask size and aeration constant [1]. Include a flask of sterile broth without yeast as a control [1]. Measure growth by absorbance in a colorimeter at set intervals, such as every 30 minutes, blanking with sterile broth and mixing before each reading; or use a haemocytometer or dilution plating [1]. Plot a suitable measure of growth against time, find the exponential phase and calculate k for each concentration [1]. Repeat each concentration and calculate means [1]. Safety and ethics: complete a risk assessment, use an approved safe strain, incubate at a modest temperature, disinfect spills, sterilise cultures and equipment before disposal, do not release cultures, and wash hands [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A plan scores more when each controlled variable is paired with how it is held constant, and when the range of concentrations is stated with numbers. Safety needs a specific action, not "be careful".

**11. (a)** Exponential phase [1], because absorbance doubles every hour (0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.40) [1].
**(b)** From 1 h to 4 h: 0.40 ÷ 0.05 = 8 = 2³, so n = 3 generations in 3 h [1]. k = **1.0 generation per hour** [1]. g = **1 h (60 minutes)** [1].
**(c)** Nutrients are becoming limiting or waste products are building up [1], so the division rate is slowing as the culture moves towards the stationary phase [1].
**(d)** It includes dead cells and cannot distinguish them from living cells [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote values from the table to justify a phase. Linking the slowing growth to a named cause and the next phase gains more than saying "it levels off".

## Where marks are usually lost

* Omitting the dilution factor or the volume plated in a colony-forming unit calculation.
* Converting 0.1 mm³ to cm³ incorrectly in haemocytometer calculations.
* Treating all the dilution steps as 1 in 10 when a question uses 1 in 100.
* Describing the lag phase as "no growth" with no mention of adaptation.
* Defining the stationary phase without the balance of division and death.
* Using stationary or lag-phase values when calculating k.
* Forgetting to divide the number of generations by the time.
* Writing aseptic steps with no reason, or listing only "keep it sterile".
* Leaving out the control and the way of holding variables constant in a plan.
