These questions cover **3.2.2 Moments** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [moments study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-moments/) or the [moments revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-moments-revision-notes/) first.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Define the moment of a force about a point. **[2]**

**2.** What is the SI unit of the moment of a force?

A: N m⁻¹

B: N m

C: J m

D: N s

**[1]**

**3.** A force of 120 N acts at right angles to a spanner, 0.25 m from the centre of the nut. Calculate the moment about the nut. **[1]**

**4.** The same 120 N force now acts at 50° to the spanner, still 0.25 m from the nut. Calculate the new moment. **[2]**

**5.** A couple of moment 1.8 N m is applied to a tap handle by two equal forces whose lines of action are 0.060 m apart. Calculate the size of each force. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why a couple acting on a free object makes it rotate but does not accelerate its centre of mass. **[2]**

**7.** A uniform metre rule balances at its 50 cm mark. A 2.0 N weight hangs at the 20 cm mark. Where must a 3.0 N weight hang to restore balance?

A: 60 cm mark

B: 70 cm mark

C: 80 cm mark

D: 95 cm mark

**[1]**

### Section B

**8.** A uniform plank of length 4.0 m and weight 300 N rests on supports at each end. A person of weight 700 N stands 1.0 m from the left end.

**(a)** Calculate the force from the right-hand support. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the force from the left-hand support. **[1]**

**(c)** The person walks slowly to the right-hand end. State the force from each support when the person stands at the right-hand end. **[2]**

**9.** A uniform trapdoor of width 1.2 m and weight 150 N is hinged along one edge and lies horizontal. A rope is attached to the opposite edge.

**(a)** Calculate the tension needed to just lift the door when the rope pulls vertically upwards. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the tension needed when the rope pulls at 60° to the door. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the tension in (b) is larger. **[1]**

**10.** A student wants to find the centre of mass of a flat, irregularly shaped sheet of card.

**(a)** Describe how she can locate the centre of mass using a pin, a stand and a plumb line. **[3]**

**(b)** A non-uniform rod 1.5 m long rests horizontally on two force sensors, one at each end. The left sensor reads 24 N and the right sensor reads 36 N. Calculate the distance of the centre of mass from the left end. **[2]**

**11.** A uniform rod of length 0.80 m and weight 30 N is hinged to a wall and held horizontal by a string from its free end to a point higher up the wall. The string makes 40° with the rod. A 50 N load hangs 0.60 m from the wall.

**(a)** Calculate the tension in the string. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the horizontal component of the force from the hinge on the rod. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the vertical component of the force from the hinge and state its direction. **[2]**

**12.** A uniform cabinet is 1.8 m tall and has a base 0.50 m deep. Its weight is 400 N and acts through its centre.

**(a)** A horizontal force F is applied at the top front edge. Calculate the value of F that will just begin to tip the cabinet about its bottom front edge. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain, in terms of the line of action of the weight, why the cabinet falls over once it has been tilted beyond a certain angle. **[2]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** Force × perpendicular distance [1]; from the point to the line of action of the force [1] **[2]**

**2.** **B** [1] **[1]**

**3.** 120 × 0.25 = **30 N m** [1] **[1]**

**4.** Moment = 120 × 0.25 × sin 50° [1] = **23 N m** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Using cos 50° gives 19 N m. The component that turns the nut is the one perpendicular to the spanner.

**5.** F = 1.8 ÷ 0.060 = **30 N** [1] **[1]**

**6.** The two forces are equal and opposite, so the resultant force is zero and there is no linear acceleration [1]; the forces are not along the same line, so there is a resultant moment that causes rotation [1] **[2]**

**7.** **B** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* 2.0 × 0.30 = 3.0 × d, so d = 0.20 m to the right of the pivot, at the 70 cm mark. The rule's own weight acts at the pivot, so it has no moment.

### Section B

**8.**

**(a)** Moments about the left end: R × 4.0 = 300 × 2.0 + 700 × 1.0 [1]; R = 1300 ÷ 4.0 = **325 N** [1]

**(b)** 1000 − 325 = **675 N** [1]

**(c)** Right-hand support: R × 4.0 = 300 × 2.0 + 700 × 4.0, so R = **850 N** [1]; left-hand support: 1000 − 850 = **150 N** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* At the right-hand end the person's weight has no moment about that support, but it still counts in the total upward force.

**9.**

**(a)** Moments about the hinge: T × 1.2 = 150 × 0.60 [1]; T = **75 N** [1]

**(b)** Perpendicular component: T sin 60° × 1.2 = 90 [1]; T = 90 ÷ (1.2 × 0.866) = **87 N** [1]

**(c)** Only the component of the tension perpendicular to the door produces a moment, so a larger tension is needed for the same moment [1] **[5]**

**10.**

**(a)** Hang the card freely from a pin through a hole near its edge and let it come to rest [1]; hang a plumb line from the pin and mark the vertical line on the card [1]; repeat from a second hole; the centre of mass is where the two lines cross [1]

**(b)** Weight = 24 + 36 = 60 N; moments about the left end: 60 × x = 36 × 1.5 [1]; x = **0.90 m** [1] **[5]**

**11.**

**(a)** Moments about the hinge: clockwise = 30 × 0.40 + 50 × 0.60 = 42 N m [1]; anticlockwise = T sin 40° × 0.80 [1]; T = 42 ÷ (0.80 × 0.643) = **82 N** [1]

**(b)** H = T cos 40° = 81.7 × 0.766 = **63 N** (directed away from the wall) [1]

**(c)** V = 80 − T sin 40° = 80 − 52.5 [1] = **27.5 N, upwards** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Taking moments about the hinge removes both hinge components at once, which is why it is the best pivot to choose first.

**12.**

**(a)** Moments about the bottom front edge [1]: F × 1.8 = 400 × 0.25 [1]; F = **56 N** [1]

**(b)** While the line of action of the weight passes inside the base, its moment about the front edge acts to return the cabinet [1]; once tilting moves the centre of mass beyond the front edge, the weight's moment acts in the same direction as the tilt, so the cabinet falls over [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The perpendicular distance for the weight is half the base depth (0.25 m), not the full depth or the height.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Distances measured along the lever, not perpendicular to the line of action.
- The weight of a uniform beam or door left out, or placed at the wrong point.
- Moments taken about a point where two unknown forces still have moments.
- Definitions that say "force × distance" without "perpendicular" and "line of action".
