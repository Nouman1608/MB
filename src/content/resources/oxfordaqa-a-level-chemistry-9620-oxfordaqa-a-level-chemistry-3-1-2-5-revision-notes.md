## Equations

- Balance with coefficients only; never change subscripts.
- Combustion order: C, then H, then O; use halves, then double.
- Ionic equation: split aqueous ionic compounds, cancel spectator ions, check atoms and charge.

| Reaction type | Ionic equation |
|---|---|
| Strong acid + soluble hydroxide | H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l) |
| Carbonate + acid | CO₃²⁻(aq) + 2H⁺(aq) → CO₂(g) + H₂O(l) |
| Silver nitrate + chloride | Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s) |
| Barium ions + sulfate | Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s) |
| Metal + acid (for example Mg) | Mg(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + H₂(g) |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Spectator ion | Ion present but unchanged in a reaction; left out of the ionic equation |
| Limiting reagent | Reactant that is completely used up; it fixes the amount of product |
| Theoretical yield | Mass of product expected from the limiting reagent |
| Percentage yield | (actual mass ÷ theoretical mass) × 100 |
| Percentage atom economy | (molecular mass of desired product ÷ sum of molecular masses of all reactants) × 100 |
| Volumetric (standard) solution | Solution whose concentration is accurately known |
| Titre | Volume delivered from the burette to reach the end point |
| Concordant titres | Titres within 0.10 cm³ of each other |

## The three-step method

1. **Moles of the known**: n = m ÷ Mr, n = c × V, or n = pV ÷ RT.
2. **Mole ratio** from the balanced equation.
3. **Convert** moles of the unknown into mass, volume, concentration or particles.

For two reactant quantities, check which is limiting first: divide each amount by its coefficient; the smaller result is the limiting reagent.

## Gas volumes

- From moles: V = nRT ÷ p (SI units).
- Gases only, at the same T and p: volume ratio = mole ratio. 50 cm³ of propane, C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O, needs 250 cm³ of O₂ and gives 150 cm³ of CO₂.

## Yield and atom economy compared

| | Percentage yield | Percentage atom economy |
|---|---|---|
| Depends on | Experimental result | Balanced equation only |
| Lowered by | Incomplete reaction, side reactions, losses in separation | By-products formed alongside the desired product |
| 100% when | All theoretical product is collected | The desired product is the only product |

Advantages of high atom economy: economic (less raw material bought per unit of product, less waste to treat), environmental (less waste, finite resources used efficiently) and ethical (resources conserved, less harm from waste). A useful by-product reduces the effective waste.

## Required practical 1 summary

1. Weigh the solid accurately (weigh the container before and after transfer).
2. Dissolve in distilled water in a beaker.
3. Transfer, with all washings, to a volumetric flask.
4. Make up to the mark (bottom of meniscus on the line); stopper and invert.
5. Pipette a known volume into a conical flask; add indicator.
6. Titrate from a burette with swirling; add dropwise near the end point.
7. Repeat to concordant titres; average only those.

## Exam traps

- Product amount must come from the limiting reagent.
- Atom economy uses the coefficients in the equation for the desired product and all reactants.
- Ionic equations must balance charge as well as atoms.
- Spectator ions do not appear; precipitates keep (s).
- cm³ to dm³ before n = c × V.
- Exclude the rough titre from the mean.
- "Human error" is not an acceptable reason for low yield; name a specific loss.

## Self-test

1. Balance: C₃H₈ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O
2. Balance: Fe₂O₃ + CO → Fe + CO₂
3. Write the ionic equation, with state symbols, for KOH(aq) reacting with HNO₃(aq).
4. Name the spectator ions when BaCl₂(aq) reacts with Na₂SO₄(aq).
5. Calculate the mass of CaO (Mr 56.1) made from 25.0 g of CaCO₃ (Mr 100.1).
6. 2.70 g of Al reacts with 7.10 g of Cl₂: 2Al + 3Cl₂ → 2AlCl₃. Which is limiting?
7. What volume of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ H₂SO₄ neutralises 30.0 cm³ of 0.150 mol dm⁻³ NaOH?
8. Calculate the atom economy for making Na₂CO₃ from 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂ (Mr NaHCO₃ 84.0, Na₂CO₃ 106.0).
9. A reaction has a theoretical yield of 8.90 g and 7.12 g is collected. Find the percentage yield.
10. Why does an addition reaction have an atom economy of 100%?
11. Titres: 24.60 (rough), 24.20, 24.10, 24.15 cm³. Give the mean titre.
12. Why are the washings from the beaker added to the volumetric flask?

### Answers

1. C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O
2. Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂
3. H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l)
4. Na⁺ and Cl⁻
5. 0.250 mol CaCO₃ gives 0.250 mol CaO; 0.250 × 56.1 = 14.0 g
6. Both are 0.100 mol; Al needs 0.150 mol Cl₂, so Cl₂ is limiting.
7. n(NaOH) = 4.50 × 10⁻³; n(H₂SO₄) = 2.25 × 10⁻³; V = 0.01125 dm³ = 11.3 cm³
8. 106.0 ÷ 168.0 × 100 = 63.1%
9. 7.12 ÷ 8.90 × 100 = 80.0%
10. All the reactant atoms end up in the single product.
11. (24.20 + 24.10 + 24.15) ÷ 3 = 24.15 cm³
12. So that all the weighed solid ends up in the solution and its concentration is accurate.
