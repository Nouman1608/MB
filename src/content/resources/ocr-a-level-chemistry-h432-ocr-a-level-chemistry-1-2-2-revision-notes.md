These notes condense **1.2.2 Use of apparatus and techniques** from OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432), Version 3.1 (May 2026). Statement 1.2.2 lists twelve groups of apparatus and techniques, (a) to (l), used across the minimum of 12 assessed practicals in the Practical Endorsement. The written papers test the same techniques, usually by asking you to choose apparatus, justify a step or predict the effect of a fault.

The explanations and worked examples are in the [1.2.2 study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-h432-ocr-a-level-chemistry-1-2-2/), and the [1.2.2 practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-h432-ocr-a-level-chemistry-1-2-2-practice/) test them. Standard methods for every PAG are in the [practical endorsement revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-practical-skills-practical-endorsement-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Resolution | Smallest change a piece of apparatus can show |
| Uncertainty | Range either side of a reading within which the true value lies |
| Percentage uncertainty | Uncertainty ÷ quantity measured × 100 |
| Mass by difference | Weigh container with solid, transfer solid, reweigh container |
| Titre | Volume delivered from the burette: final reading minus initial reading |
| Concordant titres | Titres that agree closely, usually within 0.10 cm³ |
| Standard solution | Solution of accurately known concentration, made in a volumetric flask |
| Calibration | Setting an instrument against standards of known value, such as pH buffers |
| Continuous monitoring | Following one property through a single run of a reaction |
| Initial rate method | Measuring rate at the start of each run, for example with a clock reaction |

## Statement (a): uncertainties to recall

- Single analogue reading: half the smallest division.
- Any **difference** of two readings (titre, mass by difference, temperature change): **double** the single-reading uncertainty.

| Measurement | Uncertainty |
|---|---|
| Burette reading / titre | ±0.05 cm³ / ±0.10 cm³ |
| Mass by difference, 2 d.p. balance | ±0.01 g |
| Mass by difference, 3 d.p. balance | ±0.001 g |
| Temperature change, 1 °C thermometer | ±1.0 °C |
| Temperature change, 0.1 °C thermometer | ±0.10 °C |
| Measuring cylinder, 1 cm³ divisions | ±0.5 cm³ |
| Pipette, volumetric flask | Tolerance printed on the glass |

**Reducing a percentage uncertainty.** Either measure a larger quantity (more solid, longer titre, bigger temperature rise) or use apparatus with finer resolution. Aim the improvement at the measurement with the largest percentage.

### Worked reminder

A titre of 12.50 cm³ has 0.10 ÷ 12.50 × 100 = **0.80%** uncertainty. Doubling the concentration of the solution in the flask would double the titre to 25.00 cm³ and halve the percentage to **0.40%**.

## Statements (b) and (c): heating and pH

| Heat source | Use it when | Avoid it when |
|---|---|---|
| Water bath | Gentle, even warming below 100 °C | Mixture must exceed 100 °C |
| Electric heater (mantle) | Flammable mixture must boil (reflux, distillation) | Gentle warming of test-tubes |
| Sand bath | Even heating above 100 °C | Rapid temperature changes are needed |

| pH method | Precision | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| pH chart | About 1 unit | Colour judged by eye |
| pH meter | About 0.01 unit | Calibrate with buffers; rinse between samples |
| Probe on data logger | About 0.01 unit | Records pH continuously, e.g. a pH curve |

## Statements (d)(i), (e), (f): faults and their effects

Learn each line as fault → effect on titre → effect on calculated result.

| Fault | Effect on titre | If the titrant concentration is being found |
|---|---|---|
| Burette rinsed with water only | Too large (titrant diluted) | Concentration too low |
| Pipette rinsed with water only | Too small (less analyte delivered) | Concentration too high |
| Conical flask rinsed with analyte | Too large (extra analyte) | Concentration too low |
| Air bubble in burette tip escapes | Too large | Concentration too low |
| Funnel left in burette drips in | Level rises, so recorded titre too small | Concentration too high |
| End point overshot | Too large | Concentration too low |

**Standard solution checks (e).** Rinse beaker, rod and funnel into the flask; make up to the mark with the bottom of the meniscus on the line at eye level; stopper and invert. Lost washings make the solution **too dilute**.

**Indicator (f).** Strong acid–weak base: methyl orange. Weak acid–strong base: phenolphthalein. Weak–weak: no sharp end point.

## Statements (d)(ii)–(iv), (g)–(i): preparation and purification

- **Reflux**: condenser vertical, open top, water in at the bottom; never heat a closed system.
- **Distillation**: thermometer bulb at the side arm; collect the fraction at the expected boiling point.
- **Retort stand and clamps**: clamp the flask neck and the condenser.
- **Qualitative tests (d)(iii)**: warm with a water bath, add reagents dropwise, record colours and precipitates exactly.
- **Fluted filter paper**: fast gravity filtration, keep the filtrate.
- **Reduced pressure (Büchner)**: keep the solid; drier crystals.
- **Separating funnel**: denser layer at the bottom; identify the aqueous layer by adding water; vent pressure often.
- **Recrystallisation**: minimum hot solvent, cool, filter, wash with ice-cold solvent, dry.
- **Melting point**: impure or damp solid melts lower and over a wider range.
- **Chromatography**: pencil baseline above the solvent; mark the front at once; Rf = spot distance ÷ solvent front distance.

## Statements (j)–(l): cells, safety and rates

- **Cell**: two half-cells, salt bridge (ions carry charge), high-resistance voltmeter (no current drawn), clean electrodes.
- **Hazards (k)**: corrosive, irritant, flammable, toxic. Name the hazard, then a matching control.
- **Rates (l)**: at least two methods. Initial rate, e.g. clock reaction (rate ∝ 1/t). Continuous monitoring: gas volume, mass loss or colour against time in one run.

## Exam traps

- A titre uncertainty is ±0.10 cm³, not ±0.05 cm³.
- "Inaccurate" earns nothing: give the direction of the error.
- Mass loss in an open flask: acid spray exaggerates the loss; use cotton wool.
- A water bath cannot reach 120 °C.
- A wire is not a salt bridge.
- Fluted paper is for filtrates; Büchner filtration is for solids.

## Quick self-test

1. What is the uncertainty of a temperature change measured with a thermometer graduated every 1 °C?
2. Give the percentage uncertainty in a titre of 20.00 cm³.
3. 0.150 g is weighed by difference on a three-decimal-place balance. What is the percentage uncertainty?
4. Which heat source would you use to boil a flammable organic mixture under reflux?
5. Why must a pH meter be calibrated before use?
6. A pipette is rinsed with distilled water but not with the solution. What happens to the titre?
7. The burette is not rinsed with the titrant. Is the calculated titrant concentration too high or too low?
8. Why is the stoppered volumetric flask inverted several times?
9. When would you choose fluted filter paper?
10. How can you tell which layer in a separating funnel is aqueous?
11. Why is a high-resistance voltmeter used?
12. Name the two types of method for measuring rates required by 1.2.2(l).

### Answers

1. ±1.0 °C (two readings of ±0.5 °C).
2. 0.10 ÷ 20.00 × 100 = **0.50%**.
3. 0.001 ÷ 0.150 × 100 = **0.67%**.
4. An electric heater, such as a heating mantle.
5. So its readings match buffers of known pH; otherwise every reading carries a systematic error.
6. The aliquot is diluted, fewer moles are delivered, and the titre is too small.
7. Too low: the titrant is diluted, so the titre is too large.
8. To mix the solution so every aliquot has the same concentration.
9. When you need the filtrate quickly, for example removing insoluble impurities from a hot solution.
10. Add a few drops of water and see which layer increases.
11. It draws almost no current, so the reading is the cell potential.
12. An initial rate method (such as a clock reaction) and a continuous monitoring method.
