These questions cover sections 3.2.1.1 and 3.2.1.2 of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Economics (9214) specification, version 1.0, for exams from May/June 2025. Every country, firm and figure is invented, and the currency unit is the cu. ## Questions

**1.** Define the term interest rate. **[2]**

**2.** A saver in Country A deposits 5,000 cu for one year.

**(a)** Calculate the simple interest earned at a rate of 4% and at a rate of 6%. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one reason why a rise in the interest rate may increase saving. **[2]**

**3.** A household has a variable-rate mortgage of 80,000 cu and an after-tax income of 30,000 cu a year. The interest rate rises from 6% to 8%.

**(a)** Calculate the increase in interest paid each year. **[2]**
**(b)** Express the increase as a percentage of the household's income. Give your answer to two decimal places. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how this change may affect the household's spending. **[3]**

**4.** A firm in Country B is considering a machine that costs 120,000 cu and is expected to add 9,000 cu to profit each year.

**(a)** Calculate the expected annual return as a percentage of the cost. **[1]**
**(b)** The firm can borrow the full cost at 6%. Calculate the annual interest and the profit remaining after interest. **[2]**
**(c)** The interest rate rises to 9%. Calculate the effect on the profit remaining and state, with a reason, whether the firm should still buy the machine. **[2]**

**5.** A bank buys a van for 30,000 cu and sells it to a trader for 31,800 cu, paid in instalments. The price is fixed at the start.

**(a)** Calculate the mark-up as a percentage of the bank's cost. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one way in which this arrangement differs from an interest-based loan. **[2]**

**6.** A bank in Country C pays savers 3% a year and charges borrowers 8% a year.

**(a)** Calculate the interest earned in one year on a deposit of 2,000 cu. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the interest paid in one year on a loan of 2,000 cu. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain one reason why the rate charged to borrowers is higher than the rate paid to savers. **[2]**

**7.** Country D charges income tax of 0% on the first 12,000 cu of income, 15% on income from 12,000 cu to 36,000 cu, and 35% on income above 36,000 cu.

**(a)** Find the tax due on 20,000 cu of income and express it as a percentage of that income. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the tax paid on an income of 50,000 cu. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain, using your answers, why this tax is progressive. **[2]**

**8.** Country E's revenue in a year (billions of cu) was: income tax 18, corporation tax 7, sales tax 17, excise duties 6, import duties 3, other income 4.

**(a)** Calculate total revenue. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate direct taxes as a percentage of total revenue. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** Identify two indirect taxes from the table. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why government borrowing is not included in the table. **[1]**

**9.** Country E taxes fuel at 0.5 cu per litre. Household P has an income of 15,000 cu and uses 1,200 litres a year. Household Q has an income of 75,000 cu and uses 3,000 litres a year.

**(a)** Calculate the fuel tax paid by each household. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the tax as a percentage of each household's income. **[2]**
**(c)** State, with a reason, whether the tax is progressive or regressive. **[1]**

**10.** Country E spends 14 billion cu on health, 10 on education, 18 on social protection, 4 on defence, 5 on transport and 8 on other items. Its revenue is given in question 8.

**(a)** Calculate total spending. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the budget deficit. **[1]**
**(c)** What percentage of total spending goes on health? Give one decimal place. **[1]**
**(d)** State one way in which the deficit may be financed. **[1]**

**11.** The government of Country E is thinking of raising extra revenue by increasing the sales tax from 10% to 12%, instead of raising income tax.

**(a)** An item costs 150 cu before tax. Calculate the price paid at each rate. **[2]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether raising the sales tax is a better way to raise revenue than raising income tax. **[7]**

**12.** The central bank of Country A raises interest rates. Analyse and evaluate the likely effects on consumers and on producers. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** The cost of borrowing (or the payment a saver receives) [1], stated as a percentage of the sum each year [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Definitions tend to gain credit when they give both the idea of a price or reward and the percentage per year.

**2. (a)** At 4%: 5,000 × 0.04 = 200 cu [1]. At 6%: 5,000 × 0.06 = 300 cu [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The reward for saving is higher [1]; the opportunity cost of spending now rises, because each unit spent gives up more future interest [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Explanations tend to need a reason as well as a result.

**3. (a)** Interest at 6% = 80,000 × 0.06 = 4,800 cu; at 8% = 6,400 cu [1]. Increase = **1,600 cu a year** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 1,600 ÷ 30,000 × 100 = **5.33%** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Higher repayments leave less income to spend [1]; spending on other goods falls by up to the extra 1,600 cu [1]; the effect is immediate because the mortgage is variable-rate [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to gain more when the chain runs from repayment to disposable income to spending, using the figure from part (a).

**4. (a)** 9,000 ÷ 120,000 × 100 = **7.5%** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Interest = 120,000 × 0.06 = 7,200 cu [1]. Profit remaining = 9,000 − 7,200 = **1,800 cu** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Interest = 120,000 × 0.09 = 10,800 cu, so profit remaining = 9,000 − 10,800 = **−1,800 cu** (a loss) [1]. The firm should not buy, because the return of 7.5% is below the borrowing rate of 9% [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A decision tends to gain credit when tied to a comparison, here the return against the interest rate.

**5. (a)** Mark-up = 31,800 − 30,000 = 1,800 cu [1]. 1,800 ÷ 30,000 × 100 = **6%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The bank's return comes from selling an asset at an agreed price, not from a charge for lending money [1], so it does not change if market rates move later [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong answers say what the return is linked to; calling it "free of cost" tends to lose credit because the bank still earns the mark-up.

**6. (a)** 2,000 × 0.03 = **60 cu** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 2,000 × 0.08 = **160 cu** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The gap covers the bank's costs and the risk that some loans are not repaid [1]; the remainder is the bank's profit [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A reason tends to gain credit when it names what the gap pays for, not only that the bank wants more money.

**7. (a)** Taxable slice = 20,000 − 12,000 = 8,000 cu, so tax = 8,000 × 0.15 = 1,200 cu [1]. Average rate = 1,200 ÷ 20,000 × 100 = **6.0%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Middle band: 24,000 × 0.15 = 3,600 cu [1]. Top slice: 14,000 × 0.35 = 4,900 cu, so total tax = **8,500 cu** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The average rate on 50,000 cu is 8,500 ÷ 50,000 = 17.0% [1], which is higher than 6.0% on 20,000 cu, so the percentage of income taken rises with income, which makes the tax progressive [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Showing the working band by band makes the method clear to a reader; applying 35% to the whole income is a common error.

**8. (a)** 18 + 7 + 17 + 6 + 3 + 4 = **55** billion cu [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Direct taxes = 18 + 7 = 25 [1]. 25 ÷ 55 × 100 = **45.5%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Any two of sales tax, excise duties, import duties [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Borrowing must be repaid, so it is not income [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Pick taxes on spending, not on income or profit; corporation tax is a common wrong choice.

**9. (a)** P: 1,200 × 0.5 = 600 cu [1]. Q: 3,000 × 0.5 = 1,500 cu [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** P: 600 ÷ 15,000 × 100 = 4.0% [1]. Q: 1,500 ÷ 75,000 × 100 = 2.0% [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Regressive: the richer household pays a smaller percentage [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Household Q pays more money, but the percentages decide the type of tax.

**10. (a)** 14 + 10 + 18 + 4 + 5 + 8 = **59** billion cu [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 59 − 55 = **4** billion cu [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** 14 ÷ 59 × 100 = **23.7%** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** By borrowing (for example selling government bonds) [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The deficit is spending minus revenue; reversing the subtraction gives a negative figure.

**11. (a)** At 10%: 150 × 1.10 = **165 cu** [1]. At 12%: 150 × 1.12 = **168 cu** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** For the sales tax: the base is wide and avoidance is hard, so revenue is reliable [1]; sellers collect it, so it is cheap to run [1]. Against it: it is regressive, because the rise from 165 cu to 168 cu falls on every buyer whatever their income [1]; it raises prices and may reduce spending [1]. Income tax: a progressive rise takes more from high earners and reduces inequality [1], but may weaken incentives or encourage avoidance [1]. Judgement, for example that the choice depends on whether revenue certainty or equity matters more, with a reason [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation tends to gain credit when both options are weighed against a stated aim and a supported judgement follows.

**12.** Consumers: saving is more rewarding [1], borrowing costs more, with higher repayments on variable-rate debt [1], so spending tends to fall [1]. Producers: loans cost more [1]; fewer projects have an expected return above the interest rate, so investment tends to fall [1]; lower spending and investment reduce demand [1]. Evaluation: the strength of the effect varies with how large the rise is, fixed-rate loans and confidence [1]; savers gain while borrowers lose, so the net effect depends on which group is larger [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Longer answers tend to gain credit when consumers and producers both get a developed chain of reasoning.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Judging a tax progressive from the money paid, not the share of income.
* Applying the top band rate to the whole income.
* Writing "people borrow less" with no link to spending.
* Treating a rate rise as having the same effect on savers and borrowers.
* Describing sharia-compliant finance as having no return for the bank.
* Counting borrowing as revenue.
* Closing an evaluation with no judgement.
