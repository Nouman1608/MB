These questions cover the OCR A Level Business (H431) content "Stakeholders" (Version 2.1, January 2026; final assessment summer 2027). Every business named is invented. Start with the [study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-stakeholders/) and [revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-stakeholders-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Explain what is meant by the term "stakeholder". **[2]**

**2.** Identify **one** internal and **one** external stakeholder of a supermarket, and state **one** objective of each. **[4]**

**3.** Explain why an employee who also owns shares in the business might have mixed views about a pay freeze. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **one** reason why a lender and the owners of a business may disagree about a risky expansion. **[2]**

**5.** Using a power and interest grid, state how a business should treat a stakeholder group with:

**(a)** high power and low interest **[1]**

**(b)** low power and high interest **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Kestrel Cinemas Ltd plans to replace its staffed ticket desks with self-service machines. Analyse the objectives of **two** stakeholder groups affected by this plan. **[4]**

**7.** Tollgate Dairy Ltd buys 2 million litres of milk a year from local farmers at 38p per litre. It plans to cut the price it pays to 35p per litre.

**(a)** Calculate the annual saving to Tollgate. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage fall in the price farmers receive, to one decimal place. **[1]**

**(c)** Evaluate the impact of this decision on Tollgate's stakeholders. **[8]**

**8.** Holloway Builders plc wants to build 120 homes on a field at the edge of a village. Residents have formed an action group to oppose the plan, while shareholders expect the development to raise profit. Explain **two** reasons why Holloway needs to manage this conflict. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how a pressure group can influence the behaviour of a business even though it has no legal authority over it. **[3]**

**10.** Brightwater Care Ltd runs eight care homes. Evaluate the influence that employees have on Brightwater's performance. **[8]**

**11.** Calder Valley Brewery Ltd wants to build a new brewhouse funded by a £500,000 bank loan. The bank will lend only if the two owners offer their homes as security. Employees want a 4% pay rise on the £800,000 wage bill, saying the expansion will increase their workload. Residents near the new site worry about extra lorry traffic. Recommend and justify how Calder Valley should deal with these conflicting objectives. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** A stakeholder is any individual or group with an interest in a business [1], or who is affected by its activities, whether or not they own part of it [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Internal: employees [1], who want fair pay and secure jobs [1]. External: customers [1], who want good value and quality [1]. Other valid pairs: managers (meeting targets, pay), shareholders (dividends); suppliers (regular orders, prompt payment), the local community (jobs, low disruption). **[4]**

**3.** As an employee, the freeze reduces their real income and may lower morale [1]; as a shareholder, lower wage costs may raise profit, dividends and the share price, so they gain in that role [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The lender's main objective is to be repaid with interest at low risk, so it gains little if the expansion succeeds but may lose if it fails [1]; the owners receive all the extra profit if it succeeds, so they may be more willing to accept the risk [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Keep satisfied [1].

**(b)** Keep informed [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Two groups, 2 marks each (objective plus developed reasoning linked to the plan):
- **Shareholders** want higher profit [1]; machines lower staff costs over time, so they are likely to support the plan, provided customers keep coming [1].
- **Ticket staff** want secure jobs and hours [1]; the machines directly threaten their roles, so they may resist, and morale among remaining staff may fall if they fear further cuts [1].
- **Customers** want quick, easy service [1]; machines may cut queues for confident users but frustrate those who prefer help, such as some older customers, who might go elsewhere [1]. **[4]**

**7. (a)** 3p × 2,000,000 = **£60,000** [1].

**(b)** 3 ÷ 38 × 100 = **7.9%** [1].

**(c)** Points (1 mark each, up to 6), then judgement [2]:
- Shareholders gain from £60,000 a year lower costs and higher profit [1].
- Farmers lose £60,000 of income between them; a 7.9% price cut may make some farms unprofitable [1].
- Farmers may switch to another buyer, threatening Tollgate's local supply [1].
- If supply falls, Tollgate may have to buy from further away at higher transport cost, reducing the saving [1].
- Customers may gain if part of the saving is passed on in lower prices [1].
- Tollgate's local image may suffer if it is seen to squeeze small farmers, especially if it markets its milk as local [1].
- Employees may gain security if lower costs make the business more competitive [1].
Judgement: the saving looks attractive, but its value depends on how much power the farmers have: if they can sell elsewhere, the cut may cost Tollgate its supply and reputation [1]; a smaller cut, or a longer-term contract that offers farmers security in return, would be a safer choice [1]. **[10]**

**8.** Any two reasons, 2 marks each:
- The action group may object to the planning application, delaying or preventing the development [1], which would reduce or delay the profit shareholders expect [1].
- Public opposition can attract media attention [1], damaging Holloway's reputation and making future developments and home sales harder [1].
- Time spent dealing with an unresolved dispute raises costs [1], and the delay increases the risk that market conditions change before the homes are sold [1]. **[4]**

**9.** A pressure group can run campaigns and use the media and social media to publicise a business's actions [1]. This can damage the business's reputation and lead customers to boycott its products or investors to question its practices [1]. To protect sales and its image, the business may change its behaviour, for example by altering its sourcing or reducing waste, even without a legal requirement [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Points (1 mark each, up to 6), then judgement [2]:
- Care quality depends directly on staff, so motivated, skilled employees improve the service residents receive [1].
- Inspection ratings and reputation depend on that quality, influencing how many families choose Brightwater [1].
- High staff turnover means agency staff must be used, raising costs and possibly lowering continuity of care [1].
- Employees with scarce skills, such as qualified nurses, have more power because they are hard to replace [1].
- If staff are organised, for example through a trade union, they can influence pay and working conditions, affecting costs [1].
- However, managers set rotas, training and pay, so employee influence depends on how the business is run [1].
- Other stakeholders, such as regulators and local councils that fund places, may have more influence over performance overall [1].
Judgement: employees have a strong influence on Brightwater's performance because the service is delivered by people and good staff are hard to replace [1]; this influence is greatest where skills are scarce, so retention should be a priority [1]. **[8]**

**11.** Up to 10 marks:
- Calculation: the pay rise costs 4% × £800,000 = **£32,000** a year [1].
- Identifies the main conflicts: owners want growth but face personal risk from the bank's security demand [1]; staff want a share of the reward for extra work [1]; residents want low disruption [1].
- Power: the bank has high power and high interest, since without the loan the plan cannot go ahead [1]; staff have high power because the brewery depends on their skills [1]; residents have lower power unless they can influence the planning decision [1].
- Recommendation: proceed, but negotiate with the bank to reduce the personal guarantee (for example by part-funding from retained profit) and offer staff a smaller rise now with a bonus linked to the new brewhouse's output [1].
- Agree delivery hours and a lorry route with residents before applying for planning permission, to reduce objections [1].
- Justification and judgement: this deals first with the groups that can block the plan, accepts a modest cost to keep staff motivated, and leaves the owners with some risk, which is the price of expansion [1]. **[10]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming stakeholders without their objectives, or objectives without a reason.
- Treating every group as equally important in an evaluation.
- Forgetting to use the calculations in the judgement.
- Recommending "keep everyone happy" instead of choosing an approach.
- Ignoring a group's power to block the decision.
