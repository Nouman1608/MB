## Questions

**1.** A red blood cell is placed in a salt solution that has a lower water potential than its cytoplasm. Describe and explain what happens to the cell. **[3]**

**2.** Cultured animal cells were placed in three salt solutions. The table shows their volume after 30 minutes as a percentage of the starting volume.

| Salt solution (%) | 0.3 | 0.9 | 1.8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cell volume (% of start) | 131 | 100 | 84 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in volume in the 1.8% solution. **[2]**
**(b)** State which solution has the same water potential as the cells. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain the result in the 0.3% solution. **[2]**

**3.** The kidney tubule has four named parts.
**(a)** Put these in the order in which filtrate passes through them: collecting duct, Bowman's capsule, loop of Henlé, proximal convoluted tubule. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe what happens in the Bowman's capsule. **[2]**

**4.** Name the structure described in each case.
**(a)** It carries blood into the kidney. **[1]**
**(b)** The outer region of the kidney, which contains the Bowman's capsules. **[1]**
**(c)** The tube linking the kidney to the bladder. **[1]**
**(d)** The hollow centre of the kidney where urine collects. **[1]**

**5.** On a mild day a person gains 1800 cm³ of water from drinks, 600 cm³ from food and 200 cm³ from respiration. They lose 300 cm³ in breath, 400 cm³ in sweat and 100 cm³ in faeces. The rest is lost in urine and the person stays in water balance.
**(a)** Calculate the volume of urine. **[2]**
**(b)** On a hot day, sweat loss is 1900 cm³ and everything else is unchanged. Calculate the volume of urine. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage decrease in urine volume from the mild day to the hot day. **[1]**
**(d)** Describe how the urine differs in volume and concentration on the hot day. **[1]**

**6.** **(a)** Name two components of blood that cannot enter the Bowman's capsule. **[2]**
**(b)** State what happens to the glucose that enters the filtrate. **[1]**
**(c)** Urea is found in urine. Explain why. **[1]**

**7.** (Higher Tier only) Describe the effect of ADH on the kidney tubules and explain why this is an example of negative feedback. **[4]**

**8.** (Higher Tier only) The table shows the ADH level in the blood and the volume of urine produced in one hour in four situations.

| ADH level (arbitrary units) | 2 | 5 | 9 | 14 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Urine volume (cm³ per hour) | 420 | 240 | 110 | 50 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship shown by the data. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage fall in urine volume between ADH levels 2 and 14. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the relationship. **[2]**

**9.** (Higher Tier only) A hiker has a mass of 72.0 kg at the start of a hot day and 70.4 kg at the end, having drunk very little.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage of body mass lost. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the hiker's body responds to the loss of water, including thirst and the kidneys. **[4]**

**10.** (Higher Tier only) A volunteer drinks 2.0 dm³ of water in 30 minutes. Over the next 4 hours the volunteer produces 1.25 dm³ of urine.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage of the water drunk that was lost as urine. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the urine was a large volume and dilute. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why drinking much more water than this, very quickly, could harm the body. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher Tier only) After a very salty meal a person feels thirsty and passes only a small volume of urine. Explain these responses. **[4]**

**12.** (Higher Tier only) After exercise, a dehydrated volunteer drank 1 dm³ of one of three drinks. The urine collected over the next 3 hours is shown.

| Drink | Water | Low-salt drink | High-salt drink |
|---|---|---|---|
| Urine volume (cm³) | 480 | 330 | 190 |

**(a)** Calculate how much less urine was produced after the high-salt drink than after water, as a percentage of the volume after water. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the high-salt drink produced the least urine. **[2]**
**(c)** A student concludes that the high-salt drink is best for athletes. Give two reasons why this conclusion is not safe. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Water moves out of the cell by osmosis [1] from the higher water potential inside the cell to the lower water potential outside, through the partially permeable membrane [1]. The cell shrinks [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Say which way the water moves and name the process. "The cell shrinks" alone describes the result but does not explain it.

**2. (a)** 84 - 100 = -16 [1]. The volume fell by **16%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** **0.9%** [1] because the volume did not change. **[1]**
**(c)** The outside solution is more dilute than the cells, so water moves in by osmosis [1] and the cells swell to 131% of their starting volume [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a sign or a word such as fall or rise with a percentage. Dividing by the original volume, not the final one, is the usual method.

**3. (a)** Bowman's capsule, proximal convoluted tubule [1], loop of Henlé, collecting duct [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Blood is under high pressure and small molecules such as water, glucose, urea and ions are forced out into the capsule [1]. Blood cells and large proteins stay in the blood [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name what passes through and what does not. "Waste is filtered" is too vague for two marks.

**4. (a)** Renal artery [1]. **(b)** Cortex [1]. **(c)** Ureter [1]. **(d)** Pelvis [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Do not confuse the ureter, which leaves the kidney, with the urethra, which leaves the bladder. Spell each term clearly.

**5. (a)** Total gain = 1800 + 600 + 200 = 2600 cm³ [1]. Urine = 2600 - (300 + 400 + 100) = **1800 cm³** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 2600 - (300 + 1900 + 100) = **300 cm³** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** (1800 - 300) / 1800 x 100 = **83.3%** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** The urine is a smaller volume and more concentrated [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the total gain and the total loss in your working, so that each step of the method is visible and checkable.

**6. (a)** Any two of: red blood cells, white blood cells, platelets, large proteins [1] [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** All of it is reabsorbed into the blood (in the proximal convoluted tubule) [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Most of the urea in the filtrate is not reabsorbed, so it passes out in the urine [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Plasma proteins are not a cell, so list the two items carefully. Glucose "removed" is wrong because it is taken back into the blood.

**7.** ADH raises the permeability of the collecting duct walls to water [1], so more water passes back into the blood [1]. This raises the water potential of the blood back towards normal [1]. The change then reduces the release of ADH, so the response reverses the original change [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Two marks are for the effect, two for the feedback. State that the correction switches the response off.

**8. (a)** As ADH level increases, urine volume decreases [1]. Quote data, for example 420 cm³ per hour at ADH 2 and 50 cm³ per hour at ADH 14 [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 420 - 50 = 370 [1]. 370 / 420 x 100 = **88%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** ADH increases how easily water crosses the collecting duct [1], so more is taken back and less leaves as urine [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A description of a trend is stronger with two quoted values and units. Do not explain in part (a) when the command word is describe.

**9. (a)** 72.0 - 70.4 = 1.6 kg [1]. 1.6 / 72.0 x 100 = **2.2%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Sweating lowered the water potential of the blood [1]. The hypothalamus detects this and causes thirst, so the hiker drinks [1]. More ADH is released [1]. The collecting ducts become more permeable, so more water is reabsorbed and a small volume of concentrated urine is produced [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Thirst is a response too, so a complete answer links the sensation of thirst to the kidney response.

**10. (a)** 1.25 / 2.0 x 100 [1] = **62.5%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The blood became more dilute (higher water potential) [1]. Less ADH was released [1]. The collecting ducts were less permeable, so less water was reabsorbed [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** The body fluids would become too dilute [1], so water would enter cells by osmosis and they would swell [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain the chain in order. Saying only "less ADH" without the change in permeability leaves the mechanism incomplete.

**11.** The salt makes the blood more concentrated (lower water potential) [1]. The hypothalamus triggers thirst, so the person drinks and dilutes the blood [1]. More ADH is released and the collecting ducts become more permeable [1]. More water is reabsorbed, so a small volume of concentrated urine, containing the excess salt, is made [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each response to its cause. A list of "thirst, ADH, small urine" without the chain gains less credit.

**12. (a)** 480 - 190 = 290 [1]. 290 / 480 x 100 = **60%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The salt made the blood more concentrated, so more ADH was released [1] and more water was reabsorbed, leaving less urine [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Two of: only one volunteer, with no repeats [1]; urine volume does not show how hydrated the body is, because blood water level or body mass was not measured [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation answers need a limitation of the evidence, not just a restatement of the result.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that cells "burst" or "shrink" without naming osmosis and the direction of water movement.
- Calculating a percentage change using the new value as the divisor.
- Giving a percentage with no sign, direction or the word fall or rise.
- Describing the urine as "concentrated" without mentioning the small volume, or the reverse.
- Saying ADH makes the kidney "hold on to" water without stating that the permeability of the collecting duct changes.
- Leaving out the final feedback step, where the blood returning to normal reduces ADH release.
- Forgetting thirst in answers about dehydration or high salt intake.
- Mixing up the ureter and urethra, or the cortex and medulla.
- Using vague terms such as "waste" where the question needs a named substance such as urea.
