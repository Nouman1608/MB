## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. The
4-mark "describe" question gives one mark for each relevant, accurate detail; the 4-mark "explain"
question gives one mark for each point and one for explaining it, up to two marks for each of the
two. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general,
accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The
12-mark questions are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** Abdullah ibn Urayqit [1].

**2.** 622 CE [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: where the Prophet (pbuh) would live; where the Prophet's Mosque would be built; the land of the two orphans; which family would host him, avoiding disputes among the families.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: Muslims of Quraysh (the Muhajirun); Muslims of Yathrib (the Ansar); the Jewish tribes; other groups who joined the agreement.

**5.** Any four, [1] each: more than seventy men and two women from Yathrib attended [1]; they met in secret at night at Aqaba [1]; they pledged to protect the Prophet (pbuh) as they protected their own families [1]; they invited him to live in Yathrib [1]; twelve leaders were appointed [1]; it turned the faith into a political and military alliance [1].

**6.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **The Muslims were leaving** [1]: the Quraysh saw them migrating to Yathrib and feared a rival base [1].
- **The protection of Yathrib** [1]: the second pledge promised defence, so a leader there would be safe from their power [1].
- **To end the message** [1]: they believed that killing him would end Islam [1].
- **To avoid revenge** [1]: choosing one man from each clan would spread the blame so Banu Hashim could not take revenge on everyone [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two ways, the way identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Ways (AO1):

- **Sleeping in the Prophet's bed** [1]: the watchers thought he was still inside, which gave the Prophet (pbuh) time to escape [1].
- **Returning the trusts** [1]: he gave back the valuables entrusted to the Prophet (pbuh), including those of his enemies [1].
- **Courage** [1]: he risked his life as the Quraysh planned to strike the bed [1].
- **Following the Prophet (pbuh)** [1]: he travelled to Madinah on foot to join him [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Indeed, Allah commands you to render trusts to whom they are due" (Qur'an 4:58) [2].
- "when those who disbelieved plotted against you to restrain you or kill you or evict you. But they plan, and Allah plans" (Qur'an 8:30) [2].

**8.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the brotherhood met immediate needs of homes, food and work for the migrants who had left everything, and built personal bonds between two groups.
- **For the statement:** it showed the Ansar's generosity and the migrants' dignity, such as asking only to be shown the market.
- **Against the statement:** the Charter gave the whole city law, security and religious freedom; without it, the different groups might have fallen back into feud.
- **Against the statement:** the mosque was the centre that supported both.
- **Balanced view:** the brotherhood met the human need and the Charter met the political one; both depended on the faith and on the leadership of the Prophet (pbuh).

*Use of the passage*

- "founded on righteousness from the first day" shows that the first community was built on faith.
- "men who love to purify themselves" shows the character of the people, as in the brotherhood's generosity.
- "Allah loves those who purify themselves" shows divine approval of a sincere community.

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Reasons (AO1):

- **Protection** [1]: the Ansar promised to defend the Prophet (pbuh) as their families, so he had a safe base [1].
- **An invitation** [1]: it opened the way for the Hijrah and the first Muslim community [1].
- **Spread of Islam** [1]: it showed that the message was accepted beyond Makkah [1].
- **A new kind of community** [1]: the bond was faith, not tribe, which is the idea of the Ummah [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "The believers are but brothers" (Qur'an 49:10) [2].
- "And [also for] those who were settled in the Home [Madinah] and [adopted] the faith before them, they love those who emigrated to them" (Qur'an 59:9) [2].

**10.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the Hijrah ended persecution, created the first Muslim state and began the Islamic calendar.
- **For the statement:** from Madinah the Muslims could build a community, a government and the mosque as a centre.
- **Against the statement:** the first revelation began Islam, and the conquest of Makkah showed its triumph.
- **Against the statement:** the Hijrah depended on earlier steps, such as the pledges of Aqaba.
- **Balanced view:** the Hijrah was the turning point from weakness to strength, but it cannot be separated from the events before and after it.

*Use of the passage*

- "founded on righteousness from the first day" shows that the new community began with faith.
- "men who love to purify themselves" shows the character of the community that formed after the Hijrah.
- The mention of a mosque shows that building a place of worship was the first act of the new community.
