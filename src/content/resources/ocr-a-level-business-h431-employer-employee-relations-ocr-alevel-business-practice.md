## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by employer/employee relations. **[2]**

**2.** Identify **two** costs to a business of poor employer/employee relations. **[2]**

**3.** A firm has 60 staff, each working 230 days a year. Absence falls from 5% to 3%. Calculate the number of working days saved. **[3]**

**4.** Which **one** of the following is an example of a works council?

A: A group of managers who set pay.

B: A standing forum of elected employees and managers.

C: A union meeting held without the employer.

D: A shareholder meeting. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **one** purpose of dismissal and grievance procedures. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**6.** Pelham Plastics has 150 staff. A dispute over pay leads to a 2-day strike. Normal output is 80 units a day and each unit earns a contribution of £300. Strikers lose £100 a day each. Assume wages are not included in the variable costs used for contribution.

**(a)** Calculate the contribution lost during the strike. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the net cost to the business after allowing for wages not paid. **[3]**

**(c)** Evaluate the other costs, to the business and its stakeholders, that this figure leaves out. **[6]**

**7.** Dunmere Care employs 12 care workers for 25 hours a week, 52 weeks a year, at £10.50 an hour. The minimum wage rises so that Dunmere must pay each worker £11.00 an hour.

**(a)** Calculate the increase in annual labour cost for Dunmere. **[3]**

**(b)** Evaluate the impact of legislation on pay and conditions for a small business such as Dunmere. **[8]**

**8.** A tribunal finds that Hollin Garage dismissed a mechanic unfairly. It awards £14,500 in compensation. Hollin also pays £6,000 in legal fees, and managers spend 9 days dealing with the claim, each day costing the garage £210.

**(a)** Calculate the total cost of the claim. **[3]**

**(b)** Analyse how the business could reduce the chance of similar claims. **[5]**

**9.** Explain **two** types of employee participation. **[4]**

**10.** Vantell Rail has seen union membership fall from 5,000 to 4,000 over ten years, and fewer strikes. Evaluate the impact of the changing nature of union membership on Vantell Rail and its stakeholders. **[9]**

**11.** A bakery chain employs 500 people. Staff complain that managers do not listen. Evaluate whether the chain should set up a works council. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Employer/employee relations are the quality of the dealings between management and the workforce [1], including communication, negotiation and how disputes are settled [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two from: strikes or industrial action; high absence; high staff turnover; tribunal or legal costs; damaged reputation as an employer [1] [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Total days = 60 × 230 = 13,800 [1]. At 5% = 690 days; at 3% = 414 days [1]. Days saved = 690 − 414 = **276** [1]. **[3]**

**4.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Dismissal procedures make sure that employees are dismissed for a fair reason and in a fair way [1], and grievance procedures give staff a route to raise complaints, which allows problems to be solved before they turn into disputes or claims [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 2 × 80 × £300 [1] = **£48,000** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Wages not paid = 150 × £100 × 2 = £30,000 [1]. Net cost = £48,000 − £30,000 [1] = **£18,000** [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** Business: late deliveries may lead customers to switch supplier [1]; lower effort and morale after the strike [1]; damage to reputation as an employer or supplier [1].
Stakeholders: employees lose £30,000 in pay and may fear for their jobs [1]; customers face delays [1]; the local community loses spending [1].
Judgement: the lasting cost may exceed the £18,000 net figure if customers are lost permanently, so resolving the pay dispute early would have been cheaper [1]. (Maximum 6 marks.) **[6]**

**7. (a)** Rise per hour = £11.00 − £10.50 = £0.50 [1]. Hours per worker = 25 × 52 = 1,300 [1]. Increase = £0.50 × 1,300 × 12 = **£7,800** [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Impact on costs: Dunmere's wage bill goes from £163,800 to £171,600, a rise of 4.8% (7,800 ÷ 163,800) [1], which is a large cost for a small care provider whose income is often fixed by local authority contracts [1].
Possible responses: raise prices, reduce hours, or accept lower profit [1]; cutting hours could harm the care workers and the quality of care [1].
Benefits: workers have a higher income and may be more motivated, so turnover and the cost of recruiting may fall [1]; all competitors face the same rule, so Dunmere is not at a disadvantage [1].
Other legislation: contracts, health and safety and equality also bring costs and admin for a small firm without a human resources department [1].
Judgement: legislation is important because it protects low-paid workers; the impact on Dunmere is significant but manageable if funding rises with costs, and harmful if it does not [1]. **[8]**

**8. (a)** Manager time = 9 × £210 = £1,890 [1]. Total = £14,500 + £6,000 + £1,890 [1] = **£22,390** [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Use a written dismissal procedure that includes warnings, evidence and a right to appeal [1], so that decisions are fair and recorded [1]. Train managers in the procedure and in equality law [1]. Offer a grievance route so concerns are raised early [1]. These cost time but cost far less than £22,390 per claim [1]. **[5]**

**9.** Industrial democracy [1]: employees share in running the business, for example by having a representative on the board [1]. Participative decision making [1]: managers involve employees in decisions through meetings or suggestion schemes [1]. (Works councils may be given instead: a standing forum of elected employees and managers.) **[4]**

**10.** Vantell: fewer members (a fall of 1,000, or 20%) weakens the unions' bargaining power [1], so management has more freedom to change pay, hours and working practices [1]. Fewer strikes mean fewer disruptions to services [1].
Stakeholders: passengers gain from more reliable services [1]; staff may lose a collective voice and protection, and may feel less secure [1]; owners may gain lower costs [1].
Evaluation: without unions the company loses a single channel to consult staff, and staff may leave or become less committed if they feel unrepresented [1]. Judgement: the change benefits Vantell in the short term through flexibility, but it will need other ways of consulting staff to keep relations good [1], and the fall in strikes might also be due to better pay or a weak labour market [1]. **[9]**

**11.** For: a works council gives staff a formal voice, which answers the complaint [1]; it brings better decisions because managers learn what staff know [1]; it may reduce absence and turnover in a large workforce [1].
Against: it costs time and money, and slows some decisions [1]; if managers ignore the council's views, staff will be more frustrated than before [1]; with 500 staff across many shops, representation may be uneven [1].
Judgement: the chain should set one up, but only if managers commit to responding and to reporting what they do [1], because the main problem is that staff feel unheard, which a council addresses directly [1]. **[8]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a law without saying what it requires or costs.
- Forgetting the wages saved when calculating the cost of a strike.
- Using only the owner's viewpoint.
- Stating that unions "cause strikes" without recognising their other roles.
- Listing costs without judging which is largest.
