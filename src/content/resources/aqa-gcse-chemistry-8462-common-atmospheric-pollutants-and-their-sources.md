This guide covers **4.9.3 Common atmospheric pollutants and their sources** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462)
specification, for teaching from September 2016. It sits in topic 4.9, Chemistry of the atmosphere, and is
assessed on Paper 2 at both Foundation and Higher Tier. It is not chemistry only, and there is no Higher tier
only statement.

Use the [common atmospheric pollutants revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-common-atmospheric-pollutants-and-their-sources-revision-notes/)
for quick recall and the [practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-common-atmospheric-pollutants-and-their-sources-practice/)

## 4.9.3.1 Atmospheric pollutants from fuels

The **combustion of fuels** is a major source of atmospheric pollutants. Most fuels, including **coal**, contain
**carbon and/or hydrogen**, and may also contain some **sulfur**.

When a fuel is burned, the gases released may include:

- **carbon dioxide** and **water vapour**
- **carbon monoxide**
- **sulfur dioxide**
- **oxides of nitrogen**

Solid particles and **unburned hydrocarbons** may also be released, and these form **particulates** in the
atmosphere.

### How each pollutant is produced

| Substance | How it forms | Equation example |
|---|---|---|
| **Carbon dioxide and water** | **Complete combustion**: plenty of oxygen | CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O |
| **Carbon monoxide** | **Incomplete combustion**: not enough oxygen | 2CH₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO + 4H₂O |
| **Soot (carbon particles)** | Incomplete combustion: even less oxygen | CH₄ + O₂ → C + 2H₂O |
| **Sulfur dioxide** | The **sulfur** in the fuel burns | S + O₂ → SO₂ |
| **Oxides of nitrogen** | **Nitrogen and oxygen from the air** react at the **high temperatures** in engines | N₂ + O₂ → 2NO |
| **Unburned hydrocarbons** | Fuel that did not burn at all | - |

Notice that oxides of nitrogen do not come from the fuel itself. They come from the **air**, as the very high
temperature in a car engine gives the nitrogen and oxygen enough energy to react. Nitrogen monoxide can then
react with more oxygen to form nitrogen dioxide, NO₂.

### Predicting products

You must be able to **predict the products of combustion of a fuel**, given information about the fuel's
composition and the conditions.

| Fuel composition | Oxygen supply | Products |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon and hydrogen only (for example natural gas) | Plenty | Carbon dioxide and water |
| Carbon and hydrogen only | Limited | Carbon monoxide and/or carbon (soot), and water |
| Contains sulfur (for example coal) | Plenty | Carbon dioxide, water **and sulfur dioxide** |
| Any fuel in a hot engine using air | Any | Also **oxides of nitrogen** |

### Worked example 1: incomplete combustion of propane

*Write an equation for the incomplete combustion of propane (C₃H₈) to form carbon monoxide and water.*

1. Carbon: 3 carbon atoms give 3CO per propane. For two propane molecules, 6CO.
2. Hydrogen: 8 hydrogens per propane give 4H₂O per propane, 8H₂O for two.
3. Oxygen on the right: 6 + 8 = 14 atoms, which needs 7O₂.

**2C₃H₈ + 7O₂ → 6CO + 8H₂O**

### Worked example 2: coal

*Coal contains carbon, a little hydrogen and some sulfur. It is burned in a power station with a good supply of
air. Predict the products.*

- Carbon burns completely to **carbon dioxide**.
- Hydrogen forms **water vapour**.
- Sulfur forms **sulfur dioxide**.
- High temperature in the furnace may also form **oxides of nitrogen** from the air.
- Some **particulates** from ash and unburned carbon.

### Worked example 3: a faulty gas boiler

*A gas boiler has a blocked air supply. What is the dangerous product?*

The methane burns in too little oxygen, which gives **carbon monoxide** (and soot). This is dangerous because
carbon monoxide is poisonous and has no colour or smell.

## 4.9.3.2 Properties and effects of atmospheric pollutants

| Pollutant | Properties and effects |
|---|---|
| **Carbon monoxide** | **Toxic**. **Colourless and odourless**, so not easily detected. It combines with haemoglobin in the blood, reducing the oxygen carried to the body, which causes headaches, drowsiness and, in high concentrations, death. |
| **Sulfur dioxide** | Causes **respiratory problems** (asthma, breathing difficulties) and **acid rain**. |
| **Oxides of nitrogen** | Cause **respiratory problems** and **acid rain**. |
| **Particulates** (soot) | Cause **global dimming** (less sunlight reaches the surface) and **health problems** (lung damage, breathing problems). |

### Acid rain

Sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen dissolve in rain water to form **acidic solutions**. Acid rain:

- damages **buildings and statues** made of limestone and marble (carbonates react with acid)
- harms **trees** and **plants** by damaging leaves and soil
- makes **lakes and rivers** too acidic for fish and other wildlife.

### Explaining problems

To "describe and explain the problems caused by increased amounts of these pollutants", write a sentence in
the form pollutant → source → effect:

- "Sulfur dioxide is formed by burning fuels that contain sulfur, and it causes acid rain, which damages
  limestone buildings."
- "Carbon monoxide forms in incomplete combustion and is toxic because it reduces the oxygen carried by the
  blood. It has no smell, so people cannot tell it is there."

### Reducing the pollution (extension)

The specification does not require these, but they help in discussion questions: using fuels with the sulfur
removed, removing sulfur dioxide from power station flue gases, fitting catalytic converters to vehicles to
convert carbon monoxide and nitrogen oxides to less harmful gases, and keeping gas appliances serviced.

## Exam technique

- Always link the **conditions** (limited or plenty of oxygen, high temperature, sulfur in the fuel) to the
  **product**.
- Name the pollutant, say how it **forms**, and state its **effect**.
- Use **incomplete combustion** for carbon monoxide and soot; do not use "partial burning".
- State that nitrogen oxides come from **air**, not the fuel.
- Write balanced equations for combustion, checking atoms on both sides.

## Common mistakes

- Saying sulfur dioxide comes from nitrogen.
- Saying oxides of nitrogen are made from the fuel.
- Saying carbon monoxide has a smell.
- Saying carbon dioxide causes acid rain. It is not one of the pollutants listed for acid rain.
- Saying particulates cause the greenhouse effect. They cause global dimming.
- Writing soot as CO.

## Putting it together: a model extended answer

*A town sits beside a busy road and a coal-fired power station. Explain the air pollution problems it may have.*

> The vehicles burn petrol and diesel. In the hot engines, nitrogen and oxygen from the air react to form oxides of
> nitrogen, which cause respiratory problems and acid rain. If the air supply is limited, incomplete combustion
> produces carbon monoxide, which is toxic and cannot be detected by smell, and soot, which causes global dimming
> and lung problems. The power station burns coal that contains sulfur. The sulfur burns to form sulfur dioxide,
> which also causes respiratory problems and acid rain that damages buildings and kills trees. Particulates from
> the coal add to the health problems.

This answer names each pollutant, says where it comes from and gives an effect, which is the structure that
earns marks.
