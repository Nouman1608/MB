**HL only.** These notes cover Regional study 11, The USSR and post-Soviet Russia (1945 to 2020), in the Europe option examined on HL Paper 3. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-ussr-post-soviet-russia/) for fuller explanation.

## What Paper 3 wants

Two 15-mark essays from different regional studies in two hours. Aim for in-depth understanding of the argument, a thorough and balanced evaluation of perspectives and a substantiated judgement. Comparison across leaders is common, so build comparison criteria.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| De-Stalinization | Khrushchev's criticism of Stalin and the release of prisoners |
| Thaw | Period of easier censorship in the late 1950s and early 1960s |
| Nomenklatura | The party's list of appointed officials and the privileged elite |
| Stagnation (zastoi) | Slow growth and ageing leadership under Brezhnev |
| Perestroika | Gorbachev's "restructuring" of the economy and system |
| Glasnost | "Openness" in discussion and the press |
| Shock therapy | Rapid price freeing and privatisation from 1992 |
| Oligarchs | Businessmen who gained great wealth and political influence in the 1990s |
| Vertical of power | Putin's centralised system of control |

## Post-war USSR and Khrushchev

| Theme | Post-war (1945 to 1953) | Khrushchev (1953 to 1964) |
|---|---|---|
| Politics | Stalinist control; Zhdanov; Leningrad Affair; Doctors' Plot | Secret Speech; releases; limits of the Thaw; Hungary 1956 |
| Economy | Heavy industry rebuilt; agriculture neglected | Virgin Lands; housing; regional councils; mixed results |
| Society | Hardship; famine 1946 to 1947 | Better housing; more freedom in culture |
| Technology | Atomic and military programmes | Sputnik; Gagarin |
| End | Death of Stalin | Removed in October 1964 |

## Brezhnev era

- **Kosygin:** 1965 reforms to give enterprises more autonomy and use profit; abandoned by the early 1970s.
- **Brezhnev:** stability of cadres; 1977 constitution; détente but also Czechoslovakia and Afghanistan; slowing growth.
- **Nomenklatura:** privileged and ageing; corruption.
- **Dissidents:** Sakharov, Solzhenitsyn, Helsinki Group; KGB pressure, exile and psychiatric hospitals.

## Gorbachev

| Policy | Aims | Results |
|---|---|---|
| Acceleration and perestroika | Revive the economy | Shortages, deficits, disorganisation |
| Glasnost | Open discussion | Revealed past crimes; strengthened opposition |
| Democratization | Elections 1989; end of party monopoly 1990 | Weakened party control |
| Foreign policy | Withdrawal from Eastern Europe | Fall of communist regimes in 1989 |
| Opposition | Hardliners; Yeltsin; national movements; miners | August 1991 coup; end of the USSR |

## Yeltsin and Putin

| Theme | Yeltsin (1991 to 1999) | Putin (2000 to 2020) |
|---|---|---|
| Political | Strong presidency; clash in 1993; Communist revival | Vertical of power; United Russia; term changes |
| Economic | Shock therapy; privatisation; oligarchs; 1998 default | Oil-led growth; state control of energy; sanctions after 2014 |
| Social | Poverty; falling life expectancy | Rising incomes; pension reform protest; Orthodox emphasis |
| Control | Chaotic; press free but dominated by oligarchs | State control of media; critics silenced; foreign agent laws |
| Conflicts | Chechnya (1994 to 1996; from 1999) | Georgia (2008); Crimea (2014); Ukraine |

## Historians' views

- Why did the USSR collapse: Gorbachev's reforms, structural economic failure, nationalism, or loss of belief?
- Totalitarian model against revisionist views of the Soviet state.
- Putin's Russia: managed democracy or authoritarian state? How popular is it?

## Worked example: planning an essay

**Question:** "To what extent was Gorbachev responsible for the collapse of the USSR?"

1. **Thesis:** Gorbachev's reforms set off the collapse, but deep economic and national problems made it likely.
2. **Paragraph 1, Gorbachev:** perestroika disorganised the economy; glasnost freed criticism; ending party monopoly; refusal to use force in Eastern Europe.
3. **Paragraph 2, structural:** stagnation, oil dependence, military spending, Afghanistan.
4. **Paragraph 3, nationalism and rivals:** Baltic movements; Yeltsin and the Russian republic; hardliners' coup.
5. **Perspective:** historians who stress leadership against those who stress structure.
6. **Judgement:** he was a necessary but not sufficient cause.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the Brezhnev era as only stagnation, without noting stability and détente.
- Mixing up perestroika and glasnost.
- Treating the 1990s as all failure without mentioning why Yeltsin remained in power.
- Over-relying on recent political opinion for Putin.
- Giving death or output figures without "about".

## Self-test

1. What was the Secret Speech?
2. Why was Khrushchev removed?
3. Name one policy of Kosygin.
4. What was the difference between perestroika and glasnost?
5. What were oligarchs?
6. Give one action by Putin to centralize power.

**Answers:** 1. Khrushchev's 1956 denunciation of Stalin at the 20th Party Congress. 2. Erratic leadership, failed agriculture and the Cuban crisis alienated the elite. 3. Giving factories more autonomy and using profit as a measure. 4. Perestroika was economic and political restructuring; glasnost was openness in discussion and media. 5. Businessmen who gained great wealth through 1990s privatisation. 6. For example appointing governors after 2004 or building the dominance of United Russia.
