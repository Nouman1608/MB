Study first with the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-peoples-republic-of-china/) and the [revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-peoples-republic-of-china-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Explain two reasons why Mao launched the Great Leap Forward. **[4]**

**2.** Read the extract, which is *constructed for practice* and is not a real quotation.

> "The county reported a harvest twice the real figure, because no one dared report less. The state took its share by the report, and the villagers ate what was left, which was little." (A historian's summary, constructed for practice)

**(a)** Explain what the extract suggests about the causes of the famine. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one limitation of the extract as an explanation of the famine. **[3]**

**3.** Analyse the importance of the Cultural Revolution in Mao's maintenance of power. **[6]**

**4.** To what extent did the Great Leap Forward and the Cultural Revolution damage China's society and economy? **[15]**

**5.** Evaluate the success of China's foreign policy between 1949 and 1976. **[15]**

**6.** Explain the importance of Deng Xiaoping's economic reforms for China between 1978 and 2000. **[6]**

**7.** Explain two consequences of the one-child policy. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, each developed (1 mark for the reason, 1 for explanation). Mao wished to modernise quickly and overtake Britain in steel production, to prove that China could grow without relying on Soviet methods; he believed mass mobilisation of peasants could substitute for capital; the First Five-Year Plan had favoured heavy industry and left agriculture behind, so he sought a way to raise grain output; he wished to build communism through communes after the Soviet-style plan seemed too bureaucratic. **[4]**

**2.** **(a)** The extract suggests that local officials exaggerated harvest figures out of fear (1), that the state took grain according to the false reports (1), and that this left villagers short of food (1). **[3]**
**(b)** Award up to 3. It focuses on false reporting and leaves out other causes such as the diversion of labour to steel and the disruption of communes (1); the weather, including floods and drought in some provinces, and the withdrawal of Soviet aid also played a part (1); it deals with one county, so it cannot show how far the pattern held across China (1). **[3]**

**3.** Marking points (up to 6): it allowed Mao to remove rivals such as Liu Shaoqi and Deng Xiaoping (up to 2); it created a cult of personality through the Little Red Book and mass rallies (up to 1); Red Guards attacked officials, so the Party bureaucracy was weakened and Mao's authority reasserted (up to 1); the army restored order in 1968 and the Party was rebuilt around Mao's supporters (up to 1); the violence and disorder also damaged Mao's standing and led to a search for stability (up to 1). **[6]**

**4.** Marking points (up to 15):
- A thesis and criteria for "damage" (up to 2).
- The Great Leap Forward: communes, backyard furnaces, falsified reporting, a famine with tens of millions of deaths, and the recovery from 1962 (up to 4).
- The Cultural Revolution: attacks on teachers and officials, closed schools and universities, lost talent, destroyed cultural sites, the sending of millions of urban youth to the countryside (up to 4).
- Limits to the damage and gains: growth in some industry, continuing gains in health, the nuclear programme (up to 2).
- Perspectives: Dikötter and Yang Jisheng against more cautious views, and the Party's own 1981 resolution (up to 1).
- A judgement consistent with the evidence (up to 2). **[15]**

**5.** Marking points (up to 15):
- A thesis and criteria for "success" (up to 2).
- Successes: prestige from the Korean War, Bandung and the Five Principles, the atomic bomb in 1964, the opening to the USA, the UN seat in 1971, the Nixon visit in 1972 (up to 5).
- Failures: dependence on and break with the USSR, border clashes in 1969, the Sino-Indian War of 1962 and lasting hostility with India, isolation, the Taiwan question (up to 4).
- Motives: security, ideology and national pride (up to 1).
- Perspectives: how far policy was planned and how far reactive (up to 1).
- A reasoned judgement (up to 2). **[15]**

**6.** Marking points (up to 6): the household responsibility system raised farm output and ended the communes (up to 2); Special Economic Zones such as Shenzhen attracted foreign investment and technology (up to 2); the reforms produced very high growth and lifted many millions out of poverty, but also inequality and corruption (up to 2). **[6]**

**7.** Any two, each developed (1 mark for the consequence, 1 for explanation). The birth rate fell, which slowed population growth but contributed to an ageing society and a smaller future workforce; a preference for sons, with sex-selective abortion, produced an imbalance of boys and girls; there were forced abortions and sterilisations in some places, which caused hardship and resentment; "only children" were spoiled or burdened with the care of two parents and four grandparents. **[4]**

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I avoid a one-sided essay on Mao?**
Name both gains and harm, give the evidence for each, and say which weighs more and why.

**Do I need to learn every campaign?**
Know the ones named in the guide: the Three- and Five-Antis, Hundred Flowers, Anti-Rightist and the Cultural Revolution. Be clear on the purpose of each.
