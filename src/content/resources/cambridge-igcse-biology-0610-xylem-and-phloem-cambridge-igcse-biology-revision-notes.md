## Functions (Core)

| Tissue | Function(s) |
|---|---|
| Xylem | transport of **water and mineral ions**; **support** |
| Phloem | transport of **sucrose and amino acids** |

- Xylem: upwards only, from roots to leaves.
- Phloem: up or down, to wherever the substances are needed.
- Mineral ions travel dissolved in the water in xylem.

## Key terms

| Term | Recall |
|---|---|
| Xylem | tissue that transports water and mineral ions and supports the plant |
| Phloem | tissue that transports sucrose and amino acids |
| Vascular bundle | a group of xylem and phloem together, as in a stem or leaf vein |
| Non-woody dicotyledonous plant | plant with soft green stems, such as a bean or sunflower; the type used for 0610 sections |
| Midrib | the main central vein of a leaf |
| Lignin (Extended only) | substance in the thick walls of xylem vessels that makes them strong |
| Xylem vessel (Extended only) | long continuous tube of cells joined end to end, with no cross walls and no cell contents |

## Positions in a non-woody dicotyledonous plant (Core)

| Organ | Xylem | Phloem |
|---|---|---|
| Root | central star or cross | patches between the arms of the star |
| Stem | inner side of each bundle | outer side of each bundle |
| Leaf vein or midrib | upper side | lower side |

Memory hooks:

- **Root:** "xylem is the hub".
- **Stem:** "phloem faces the fresh air" (outside), xylem faces the centre.
- **Leaf:** "xylem on top, phloem below".

## Telling the sections apart

| You see | It is a |
|---|---|
| one central core of transport tissue | root |
| a ring of separate bundles around a large central region | stem |
| thin flat blade, thicker midrib, palisade and spongy layers | leaf |

Then confirm xylem by appearance: widest cells, thick walls, empty-looking centres.

## Method in steps: identifying a labelled tissue

1. Name the organ from the overall pattern.
2. Find the labelled tissue's position (centre, inner, outer, upper, lower).
3. Apply the position rule for that organ.
4. Check: wide thick-walled empty cells = xylem; small thin-walled cells = phloem.

## Xylem structure and function (Extended only)

| Feature | So... |
|---|---|
| thick walls with lignin | strong, do not collapse; support the plant |
| no cell contents | nothing obstructs water flow |
| cells joined end to end, no cross walls | one long continuous tube from root to leaf |

Each mark = feature + effect. "Hollow" or "dead" alone does not score.

## Answer patterns

- **"State the functions of xylem"** (2 marks): transport of water and mineral ions; support.
- **"Compare xylem and phloem"**: one point per sentence, both tissues in each, joined by "whereas" or "but".
- **"Identify X on the diagram"**: organ → position → appearance check, then write the tissue name only.
- **"Relate structure to function"** (Extended): feature, then "so", then the effect, for each of the three features.
- **"Suggest the effect of damage"**: name the tissue, its lost function, and what the plant then lacks (water, mineral ions, support, sucrose or amino acids).

## Links

- The stain investigation in water uptake shows where xylem is: dye appears in the xylem only.
- The continuous tube allows the column of water pulled up by transpiration.
- Phloem carries sucrose from leaves, where it is made from glucose in photosynthesis.

## Exam traps

- Phloem carries **sucrose**, not glucose.
- Xylem has **two** functions: transport (water and mineral ions) and support.
- Stem bundle: xylem inside, phloem outside. Do not reverse it.
- Leaf vein: xylem upper, phloem lower.
- A round section is not automatically a stem; check for a ring of bundles.
- (Extended) Lignin's syllabus role is strength and support.

## Self-test

1. State **two** functions of xylem.
2. Name the **two** substances transported in phloem.
3. In which direction does water move in xylem?
4. Where is the xylem in a root section?
5. In a stem bundle, which tissue is nearer the outside?
6. In a leaf vein, which tissue is on the lower side?
7. How would you tell a stem section from a root section?
8. Describe what xylem vessels look like in a section.
9. (Extended) Name the substance that thickens xylem walls.
10. (Extended) Explain why xylem vessels have no cell contents.
11. (Extended) Explain the importance of xylem cells being joined end to end with no cross walls.
12. A dye that moves only in xylem is taken up by a stem. Describe where the dye appears in a stem section.

### Answers

1. Transport of water and mineral ions; support.
2. Sucrose and amino acids.
3. Upwards, from the roots to the leaves.
4. In the centre, in a star or cross shape.
5. Phloem.
6. Phloem.
7. A stem has separate vascular bundles in a ring near the edge; a root has one central core.
8. Wide cells with thick walls and empty-looking centres.
9. Lignin.
10. So nothing obstructs the flow of water and mineral ions.
11. They form a long continuous tube, so water moves from root to leaf without interruption.
12. As coloured spots in a ring, on the inner side of each vascular bundle.
