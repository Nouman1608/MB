Teaching is in the [money and banking study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-money-and-banking/) and the [money and banking revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-money-and-banking-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define money. **[2]**

**2.** A builder agrees today to be paid $3,000 when a wall is finished in two months. Which function of money does this show?

A: Medium of exchange

B: Unit of account

C: Store of value

D: Standard of deferred payment

**[1]**

**3.** Which is a role of a central bank but not of a commercial bank?

A: Accepting deposits from households

B: Acting as lender of last resort

C: Providing overdrafts to firms

D: Exchanging foreign currency for travellers

**[1]**

**4.** Identify the characteristic of money that each item lacks.

**(a)** Bags of rice of different weights and quality. **[1]**

**(b)** Shells that anyone can collect freely from a beach. **[1]**

**(c)** Large stone wheels that weigh hundreds of kilograms. **[1]**

**5.** A household keeps $1,500 in cash for a year while prices rise by 5%.

**(a)** Calculate the value of the cash at the old prices after one year. **[1]**

**(b)** The household could instead have used a deposit account paying 2% interest. Calculate the value of the deposit at the old prices after one year. **[2]**

**6.** A small firm uses an overdraft of $12,000 for 4 months at an interest rate of 9% a year. Calculate the interest it pays. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Explain two problems of barter that money solves. **[4]**

**8.** Bank K holds deposits of $500 million and keeps $40 million in cash and reserves. It has lent $300 million at 8% a year and pays 2% a year on all deposits.

**(a)** Calculate Bank K's interest income and interest paid in a year. **[2]**

**(b)** One day 10% of depositors try to withdraw their money. Calculate the shortfall. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how the central bank could respond, and why this matters for consumers. **[3]**

**9.** Analyse whether mobile phone credit would make good money. **[5]**

**10.** Explain two ways in which commercial banks are important to producers. **[4]**

**11.** Explain why a central bank is important to the government. **[3]**

**12.** Discuss whether commercial banks are more important to consumers than the central bank is. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Anything that is generally accepted [1] as a means of payment for goods and services or for settling debts [1]. **[2]**

**2.** D [1]. The debt is fixed in money now and paid later. **[1]**

**3.** B [1]. Commercial banks do A, C and D for the public. **[1]**

**4. (a)** Homogeneous (uniform). [1]

**(b)** Limited in supply. [1]

**(c)** Portable. [1]

**5. (a)** 1,500 ÷ 1.05 = $1,428.57 [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** With interest: 1,500 × 1.02 = $1,530 [1]. At old prices: 1,530 ÷ 1.05 = $1,457.14 [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Interest reduces the loss of purchasing power but does not remove it while prices rise faster than the interest rate.

**6.** 12,000 × 0.09 = $1,080 a year [1]; for 4 months: 1,080 × 4 ÷ 12 = $360 [1]. **[2]**

**7.** First: barter needs a double coincidence of wants, so a trader must find someone who wants what they offer and has what they need [1]; money is accepted by everyone, so goods can be sold to one person and the money spent with another (medium of exchange) [1]. Second: under barter there is no common measure of value, so every good must be priced in every other good [1]; money gives one unit of account, so prices can be compared quickly [1]. (Credit also: storing value, deferred payment.) **[4]**

**8. (a)** Interest income: 300 × 0.08 = $24 million [1]. Interest paid: 500 × 0.02 = $10 million [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Withdrawals: 0.10 × 500 = $50 million [1]. Shortfall: 50 − 40 = $10 million [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** As lender of last resort, the central bank lends Bank K the cash it needs, against the security of its loans [1]. Depositors are paid, so the panic ends and does not spread to other banks [1]. Consumers matter here because their savings stay safe and they keep trust in bank deposits as money [1]. **[3]**

**9.** In favour: it is portable, since it is stored on a phone [1]; it is divisible into very small amounts and every unit is identical (homogeneous) [1]; it is durable while the system works [1]. Against: it is only acceptable where people have phones and sellers take it, so acceptance may be limited [1]; its supply and value depend on trust in the phone company or the bank behind it, so a failure of the system would stop it working [1]. **[5]**

**10.** First: banks lend for investment [1], so a firm can buy machinery now and repay from future revenue, raising output and productivity [1]. Second: overdrafts cover gaps between paying costs and receiving revenue [1], so a firm can buy materials before customers pay and does not lose orders [1]. (Credit also: payment services for wages and suppliers; currency exchange for traders.) **[4]**

**11.** The central bank holds the government's accounts and handles its payments [1]. It manages government borrowing, so the government can spend more than its tax revenue when needed [1]. It carries out monetary policy, such as changing interest rates, which helps the government reach its aim of stable prices [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Commercial banks: consumers use them directly every day for payments, savings and loans [1]; mortgages and loans let households buy homes and other large items they could not pay for at once [1]. Central bank: consumers rarely deal with it, but it issues the notes they use and keeps the value of money stable [1]; as lender of last resort it protects their deposits in a crisis, without which commercial banks could fail [1]. Evaluation: in normal times commercial banks matter more to consumers' daily lives [1], but their services depend on the trust the central bank maintains, so the central bank matters most in a crisis; the two are complements rather than rivals [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining money as "notes and coins". Bank deposits are money too.
- Naming a function without explaining the problem it solves.
- Confusing characteristics (portable, durable) with functions (medium of exchange).
- Saying the central bank lends to households or ordinary firms.
- Describing a bank's roles without saying why they matter to the named group.
- Forgetting to divide by 12 when an interest rate is annual and the period is in months.
