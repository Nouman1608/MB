These notes condense section **3.3.2.3 Combustion of alkanes** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Combustion of alkanes study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-combustion-of-alkanes/), and exam-style questions are in the [Combustion of alkanes practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-combustion-of-alkanes-practice/). The wider topic is in the [Alkanes revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-alkanes-revision-notes/).

## What you must be able to do

- Write equations for **complete and incomplete combustion**.
- Explain how **engine pollutants** form: NOₓ, CO, carbon, unburned hydrocarbons.
- Describe how **catalytic converters** remove them.
- Explain why **SO₂** is removed from flue gases with CaO or CaCO₃.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Complete combustion | Burning in plentiful oxygen to give CO₂ and H₂O |
| Incomplete combustion | Burning in limited oxygen to give CO and/or C and H₂O |
| NOₓ | Oxides of nitrogen, NO and NO₂ |
| Catalytic converter | Honeycomb with Pt, Pd and Rh catalysts in a car exhaust |
| Flue gas | Waste gas from a power station chimney |
| Acid rain | Rain made acidic by dissolved SO₂ and NOₓ |

## Complete combustion

```
CₓHᵧ + (x + y/4)O₂ → xCO₂ + (y/2)H₂O
```

| Fuel | Equation |
|---|---|
| Methane | CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O |
| Propane | C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O |
| Butane | 2C₄H₁₀ + 13O₂ → 8CO₂ + 10H₂O |
| Octane | 2C₈H₁₈ + 25O₂ → 16CO₂ + 18H₂O |

Balance C, then H, then O.

## Incomplete combustion

```
CH₄ + 1½O₂ → CO + 2H₂O
CH₄ + O₂ → C + 2H₂O
2C₈H₁₈ + 17O₂ → 16CO + 18H₂O
```

- CO is toxic: binds to haemoglobin.
- Carbon: soot, yellow flame, respiratory problems.
- Less energy released.

## Engine pollutants

| Pollutant | Formation | Harm |
|---|---|---|
| NOₓ | N₂ + O₂ → 2NO at high temperature or spark; 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂ | Acid rain, smog |
| CO | Incomplete combustion | Toxic |
| Carbon (soot) | Incomplete combustion | Respiratory problems |
| Unburned hydrocarbons | Fuel not burned | Smog |

NOₓ come from air, not from the fuel.

## Catalytic converters

- Ceramic honeycomb coated with a thin layer of Pt, Pd and Rh.
- Large surface area (honeycomb), less metal needed (thin layer).
- Poisoned by lead.
- Less effective when cold.

```
2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂
2CO + O₂ → 2CO₂
C₈H₁₈ + 25NO → 8CO₂ + 9H₂O + 12½N₂
```

## Sulfur dioxide

```
S + O₂ → SO₂
```

- Source: sulfur compounds in coal and oil fuels.
- Harm: dissolves in rain water to form acids (acid rain).

### Flue gas removal

```
CaO + SO₂ → CaSO₃
CaCO₃ + SO₂ → CaSO₃ + CO₂
2CaSO₃ + O₂ → 2CaSO₄          (gypsum, used for plasterboard)
```

**Why:** SO₂ is an acidic oxide; CaO is a basic oxide and CaCO₃ a base, so they neutralise it and trap it as a solid. Limestone is cheap and abundant.

## Greenhouse effect

Burning alkanes produces CO₂, a greenhouse gas that contributes to global warming. A catalytic converter does not remove it.

## Calculations

**Volume of gas.** 20.0 g of propane (44.0) burns completely.

```
n = 20.0 ÷ 44.0 = 0.4545 mol
V(CO₂) = 3 × 0.4545 × 24.0 = 32.7 dm³
V(O₂) = 5 × 0.4545 × 24.0 = 54.5 dm³
```

**Mass of CO₂.** 1.00 kg of octane.

```
n = 1000 ÷ 114.0 = 8.77 mol; n(CO₂) = 70.2 mol; mass = 3090 g
```

**Limestone for SO₂.** 3.20 kg of SO₂.

```
n(SO₂) = 3200 ÷ 64.1 = 49.9 mol; mass CaCO₃ = 49.9 × 100.1 = 5.00 kg
```

## Why complete and incomplete combustion differ

| Feature | Complete | Incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen supply | Plentiful | Limited |
| Carbon-containing products | CO₂ | CO, C |
| Energy released | Maximum | Less |
| Flame | Blue | Yellow, smoky |
| Danger | Greenhouse gas | Toxic gas, soot |

A Bunsen burner with the air hole open gives a blue flame (complete combustion). With the air hole closed the flame is yellow and sooty (incomplete combustion).

## Matching pollutant to problem

| Problem | Pollutant responsible |
|---|---|
| Acid rain | SO₂ and NOₓ |
| Photochemical smog | NOₓ and unburned hydrocarbons |
| Poisoning | CO |
| Breathing difficulties, dirt | Particulates (carbon) |
| Global warming | CO₂ |

## Controlling each pollutant

| Pollutant | Method of control |
|---|---|
| CO, NOₓ, unburned hydrocarbons | Catalytic converter in the exhaust |
| SO₂ (power stations) | Flue gas desulfurisation with CaO or CaCO₃ |
| SO₂ (vehicles) | Remove sulfur from the fuel before it is sold |
| Particulates | Engine design and filters |
| CO₂ | Use less fuel, or use other energy sources |

## Exam traps

- Water missing from products.
- Oxygen not balanced.
- NOₓ said to come from the fuel.
- Catalytic converter said to remove CO₂ (it produces it).
- Calcium carbonate called a catalyst.
- CaSO₄ given as the first product instead of CaSO₃.
- Acid rain linked to CO₂.
- Leaded petrol allowed with a catalytic converter.

## Self-test

1. Write the equation for the complete combustion of methane.
2. Name two products of incomplete combustion.
3. Why is carbon monoxide toxic?
4. How are nitrogen oxides formed in an engine?
5. Name the metals in a catalytic converter.
6. Write an equation for a reaction in a catalytic converter.
7. Why is the converter a honeycomb?
8. How does sulfur dioxide form when fuels burn?
9. Write an equation for calcium oxide with sulfur dioxide.
10. Why can limestone remove SO₂?
11. What is formed from calcium sulfite and oxygen?
12. Which pollutant causes global warming?

### Answers

1. CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O.
2. Carbon monoxide and carbon (soot), with water.
3. It binds to haemoglobin so oxygen cannot be carried.
4. Nitrogen and oxygen from the air react at high temperature.
5. Platinum, palladium and rhodium.
6. 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂.
7. For a large surface area.
8. Sulfur impurities burn: S + O₂ → SO₂.
9. CaO + SO₂ → CaSO₃.
10. SO₂ is acidic and CaCO₃ reacts with acids, neutralising it.
11. Calcium sulfate (gypsum).
12. Carbon dioxide.
