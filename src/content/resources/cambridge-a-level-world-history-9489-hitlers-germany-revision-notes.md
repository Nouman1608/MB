**These notes condense Hitler's Germany, 1929-41, the third topic of the Paper 4 European option in Cambridge 9489.** For explanation and an annotated plan see the [Hitler's Germany study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-hitlers-germany/); for essays and short questions see the [Hitler's Germany practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-hitlers-germany-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage

- 3.1 How did Hitler gain control of Germany?
- 3.2 How effectively did the Nazis govern Germany?
- 3.3 How successful were Nazi economic policies?
- 3.4 How far did the Nazis transform German society?

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Article 48 | Weimar emergency clause letting the president rule by decree |
| Presidential cabinets | Brüning, Papen and Schleicher governments relying on Hindenburg, not the Reichstag |
| Harzburg Front | 1931 alliance of Nazis, DNVP and nationalist groups against the Republic |
| Enabling Act | March 1933 law letting the cabinet make laws without the Reichstag for four years |
| Gleichschaltung | "Co-ordination": bringing states, unions, parties and organisations under Nazi control |
| SD | Security Service of the SS, under Heydrich |
| Block warden | Party official watching a group of households |
| Mefo bills | Credit notes used from 1934 to fund rearmament off the books |
| New Plan | Schacht's 1934 controls on imports and foreign exchange through bilateral trade |
| Four Year Plan | Göring's 1936 plan to prepare the economy for war and achieve autarky |
| DAF | German Labour Front, replacing trade unions in 1933 |
| KdF | Strength through Joy: subsidised leisure for workers |
| Volksgemeinschaft | The "people's community" the Nazis claimed to build |
| Confessing Church | Protestant church opposing Nazi control of doctrine, from 1934 |
| Edelweiss Pirates | Working-class youth groups who rejected the Hitler Youth |

## 3.1 Gaining control -- three layers

1. **Crisis:** Depression, unemployment, presidential decree government, communist growth.
2. **Mass support:** Hitler's appeal, SA, Goebbels' targeted propaganda, elite backing.
3. **Intrigue:** Papen and Hindenburg's deal after Schleicher's failure -- the decisive step.

Then **consolidation**: Fire decree, March election, Enabling Act, Gleichschaltung, Night of the Long Knives, army oath.

## 3.2 Governing -- key points

- **Terror:** SS, Gestapo, SD, block wardens, People's Court, camps from 1933; denunciations did much of the work.
- **Propaganda:** Goebbels' ministry, radio, film, rallies, the 1936 Olympics; strongest when reinforcing existing views.
- **Opposition:** youth groups, churches (Niemöller, Galen), the underground left, passive non-conformity; none threatened the regime before 1941.
- **Foreign policy:** aims (Versailles, pan-Germanism, Lebensraum, anti-communism) against methods (bluff, treaties, war); debate on planning versus opportunism.

## 3.3 Economy -- two phases

| Phase | Leader | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Recovery, 1933-36 | Schacht | Deficit spending, Mefo bills, public works, Labour Service, New Plan |
| War economy, 1936-39 | Göring | Four Year Plan, synthetic fuel and rubber, state steelworks, autarky |

**Living standards:** jobs restored, but wages frozen, longer hours, no unions; KdF and Beauty of Labour as compensation.

## 3.4 Society -- checklist

- **Women:** marriage loans, Mother's Cross; reversal from 1937 as labour ran short.
- **Youth:** new syllabus, controlled teachers, elite schools; Hitler Youth and BDM compulsory from 1939.
- **Churches:** Concordat; Reich Church and German Christians; German Faith Movement.
- **Minorities:** boycott, Nuremberg Laws, Kristallnacht; sterilisation and T4; Roma, Jehovah's Witnesses, homosexual men.

## Exam traps

- **Appointed, not elected:** Hitler never won a majority in a free election.
- **Legal revolution:** each legal step relied on coercion; say how.
- **Economic success:** distinguish employment from living standards and from war readiness.
- **Opposition:** define it; non-conformity is not resistance aimed at overthrow.
- **Women's policy:** note the change of direction from 1937.

## Self-test

1. Why did Müller's coalition collapse in 1930? *The SPD and DVP could not agree over unemployment insurance contributions.*
2. How did Brüning govern? *Through presidential decrees under Article 48.*
3. How many seats did the Nazis win in July 1932? *230.*
4. Who arranged the deal that made Hitler Chancellor? *Papen, with Hindenburg's agreement, after meeting Hitler in Cologne on 4 January 1933.*
5. How many Nazis were in Hitler's first cabinet? *Three, including Hitler.*
6. What did the decree of 28 February 1933 do? *It suspended civil liberties after the Reichstag Fire.*
7. Which party's votes ensured the Enabling Act's two-thirds majority? *The Centre Party's.*
8. What were Mefo bills? *Credit notes used to fund rearmament without showing it in the budget.*
9. What did the Reich Entailed Farm Law do? *It made medium-sized farms unsaleable and inheritable by a single heir.*
10. Who led the Four Year Plan? *Hermann Göring.*
11. Name two youth groups that rejected the Hitler Youth. *The Swing movement and the Edelweiss Pirates.*
12. What happened on 9-10 November 1938? *Kristallnacht, the pogrom against Jewish homes, shops and synagogues.*
