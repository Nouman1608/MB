Condensed recall for **1.1 The purpose of accounting**, Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707, 2027-2029 syllabus. Three points: "the difference between book-keeping and accounting"; "the purposes of measuring business profit and loss"; "the role of accounting in providing information for monitoring progress and decision-making". Figures below are invented; amounts in dollars.

## Book-keeping and accounting

| | Book-keeping | Accounting |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Detailed, day-to-day recording of financial transactions | Using the records to prepare, analyse, interpret and communicate financial information |
| Typical tasks | Entering documents in books of prime entry; posting to ledgers; balancing accounts | Preparing financial statements; comparing results; calculating ratios; advising on decisions |
| Nature | Routine and mechanical | Analytical and judgement-based |
| Output | Accurate, complete records | Statements and information users can act on |

**Test:** records it = book-keeping; summarises, explains or uses it = accounting. One person may do both; the difference is the activity, not the job title.

## Purposes of measuring profit and loss

- **Judge performance**: has the business achieved its aim of making a profit?
- **Set drawings or dividends**: drawings above profit reduce capital.
- **Compare** with earlier periods and with other businesses.
- **Calculate tax** due.
- **Support loan applications**: lenders want evidence of profit to pay interest.
- **Share profit** between partners.
- **Plan**: expand, continue or cut back.
- **A loss** is a warning: raise prices, cut costs, or consider closure if it continues.

**Quick figures.** Revenue $60,000; expenses (including cost of sales) $47,500; profit **$12,500**. Drawings $14,000, so capital falls by **$1,500**: the owner took more than the business earned.

## Monitoring progress

Compare the figures with:

- **earlier periods** (trend)
- **plans or targets**
- **similar businesses**

**Quick figures.** Revenue rose from $50,000 to $55,000 (10%), but profit fell from $9,000 to $8,800. Profit per $100 of revenue fell from $18.00 to $16.00, so expenses grew faster than sales: investigate costs.

## Decision-making

| Decision | Information needed |
|---|---|
| Change selling prices | Costs compared with revenue; profit trend |
| Buy a non-current asset | Bank balance, profit, existing loans |
| Give a customer credit | Customer's payment record in the sales ledger |
| Employ more staff | Wages against the extra revenue expected |
| Borrow | Profit available for interest; assets for security |

Good information is **accurate**, **up to date** and **understandable**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Book-keeping | The detailed recording of financial transactions |
| Accounting | Preparing, analysing, interpreting and communicating financial information from the records |
| Transaction | An exchange of value, such as a sale or a payment |
| Revenue | Income from the sale of goods or services |
| Expense | A cost of running the business in the period |
| Profit for the year | Revenue minus all expenses |
| Loss | Expenses exceed revenue |
| Drawings | Money or goods taken from the business by the owner for private use |
| Monitoring progress | Regularly checking results against earlier periods, plans or other businesses |

## Short answers that score

**"Explain the difference between book-keeping and accounting." [2]** Book-keeping is the detailed recording of financial transactions in the books of prime entry and ledger [1]; accounting uses those records to prepare, analyse and interpret statements so that users can make decisions [1].

**"Explain one purpose of measuring profit." [2]** To decide how much the owner can withdraw [1], because drawings above profit reduce the owner's capital in the business [1].

**"How does accounting help monitor progress?" [2]** It allows this year's results to be compared with earlier years or targets [1], so that a fall in profit can be spotted and investigated early [1].

## Exam traps

- **Accounting is not just recording.** Recording is book-keeping.
- **Profit is not cash.** A profitable business can be short of cash.
- **Purpose, not definition.** "Why is profit measured?" needs reasons, not a definition of profit.
- **Monitoring needs a comparison.** Say what the figure is compared with.
- **Rising revenue is not always progress.** Check whether profit kept pace.
- **One point per mark.** Two purposes asked for means two different purposes, each explained.
