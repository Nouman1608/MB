These questions cover subtopic 2.9, Graphs in practical situations, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions 1 to 6 use Core content (C2.9) and suit every candidate. Questions 7 to 11 are labelled **(Extended)**: they use E2.9 content (tangents, speed–time graphs, acceleration and area under a graph) that is examined only on Papers 2 and 4. Each question is also labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator).

Graphs are described by the points where their straight sections meet, so you can sketch them. For explanations, read the [graphs in practical situations study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-graphs-in-practical-situations/).

## Questions

**1.** (non-calculator) A conversion graph for kilograms (horizontal axis) and pounds (vertical axis) is a straight line through (0, 0) and (10, 22).

**(a)** Use the graph to convert 5 kg to pounds. **[1]**
**(b)** The graph only goes up to 10 kg. Convert 154 pounds to kilograms. **[2]**

**2.** (non-calculator) The total cost, $C, of gym membership for m months is shown by a straight line through (0, 40) and (12, 160).

**(a)** State the joining fee. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the gradient of the line and explain what it represents. **[2]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Tariq walks from home to a shop and back. His distance–time graph joins these points with straight lines: (0 min, 0 km), (40 min, 3 km), (55 min, 3 km), (85 min, 0 km).

**(a)** Calculate his walking speed to the shop, in km/h. **[2]**
**(b)** How long did he spend at the shop? **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate his speed on the way home, in km/h. **[1]**

**4.** (calculator) A bus leaves a station at 08 00. It travels 30 km at a constant 45 km/h, stops for 10 minutes, then travels a further 20 km at a constant 40 km/h.

**(a)** Find the times at which the bus arrives at the stop, leaves the stop and reaches the end of the route. **[3]**
**(b)** Write down the four points you would plot to draw the distance–time graph. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the average speed for the whole route. **[2]**

**5.** (non-calculator) Towns A and B are 36 km apart. Ben leaves A at 10 00 and cycles towards B at 18 km/h. Cara leaves B at 10 15 and cycles towards A at 24 km/h. Both travel at constant speed. On a distance–time graph showing distance from A, their lines cross. Find the time at which they meet and their distance from A. **[4]**

**6.** (calculator) Water is pumped into a cuboid tank and later drained. The depth of water joins these points with straight lines: (0 min, 20 cm), (5 min, 50 cm), (8 min, 50 cm), (14 min, 20 cm).

**(a)** Find the rate at which the depth rises during the first 5 minutes. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the rate at which the depth falls while the tank is draining. **[1]**
**(c)** The base of the tank measures 40 cm by 25 cm. Find the rate at which water is pumped in during the first 5 minutes, in litres per minute. (1 litre = 1000 cm³.) **[2]**

**7.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A speed–time graph joins these points with straight lines: (0 s, 4 m/s), (10 s, 16 m/s), (40 s, 16 m/s), (48 s, 0 m/s).

**(a)** Find the acceleration during the first 10 seconds. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the deceleration during the last 8 seconds. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the total distance travelled. **[3]**

**8.** (Extended) (calculator) A train starts from rest and accelerates steadily to a speed of V m/s in 25 s. It travels at V m/s for 90 s, then decelerates steadily to rest in 35 s. The total distance is 3 km.

**(a)** Find V. **[3]**
**(b)** Write V in km/h. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the acceleration in the first 25 s. **[1]**

**9.** (Extended) (calculator) A trolley rolls from rest. Its distance d metres after t seconds is shown in the table.

| t (s) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| d (m) | 0 | 0.5 | 2 | 4.5 | 8 | 12.5 | 18 |

**(a)** Draw the distance–time graph for 0 ≤ t ≤ 6. **[2]**
**(b)** By drawing a tangent, estimate the speed of the trolley when t = 4. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain how the graph shows that the trolley is speeding up. **[1]**

**10.** (Extended) (calculator) A cyclist's speed–time graph joins these points with straight lines: (0 s, 0 m/s), (6 s, 9 m/s), (20 s, 9 m/s), (26 s, 3 m/s).

**(a)** Find the distance travelled in the first 4 seconds. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the total distance travelled in the 26 seconds. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the average speed over the 26 seconds. **[1]**
**(d)** Find the deceleration between 20 s and 26 s. **[1]**

**11.** (Extended) (calculator) A tram starts from rest and accelerates steadily to 36 km/h in 20 seconds. It then travels at this constant speed for t seconds. Finally it decelerates steadily at 0.4 m/s² until it stops. The tram travels 1 km in total.

**(a)** Write 36 km/h in m/s. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the acceleration. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the time taken to stop. **[1]**
**(d)** Find t. **[3]**
**(e)** Find the total time for the journey. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Up from 5 to the line and across: **11 pounds** [1]
**(b)** The line goes through the origin, so scale: 22 pounds = 10 kg [1]; 154 ÷ 22 = 7, so 154 pounds = 7 × 10 = **70 kg** [1]
*Examiner insight:* scaling is valid only because the line passes through (0, 0); show the scale factor so the method mark can still be given.

**2. (a)** Value at m = 0: **$40** [1]
**(b)** Gradient = (160 − 40) ÷ 12 = 10 [1]; it represents the **monthly charge of $10 per month** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "explain what it represents" needs the rate in context with units (dollars per month); "the slope of the line" earns nothing for the second mark.

**3. (a)** 40 min = 2/3 h [1]; speed = 3 ÷ (2/3) = **4.5 km/h** [1]
**(b)** 55 − 40 = **15 minutes** [1]
**(c)** 3 km in 30 min = **6 km/h** [1]
*Examiner insight:* dividing 3 by 40 gives a speed in km per minute; the units asked for are km/h, so convert the time before dividing or the accuracy mark is lost.

**4. (a)** 30 ÷ 45 = 2/3 h = 40 min, so arrives at the stop at **08 40** [1]; leaves at **08 50** [1]; 20 ÷ 40 = 0.5 h = 30 min, so ends at **09 20** [1]
**(b)** **(08 00, 0), (08 40, 30), (08 50, 30), (09 20, 50)** with distance in km [1]
**(c)** Total time 08 00 to 09 20 = 80 min = 4/3 h [1]; average speed = 50 ÷ (4/3) = **37.5 km/h** [1]
*Examiner insight:* the 10-minute stop counts in the total time; using 70 minutes loses the accuracy mark but can keep the method mark.

**5.** Ben: distance from A = 18T, where T is hours after 10 00 [1]
Cara starts 0.25 h late: distance from A = 36 − 24(T − 0.25) = 42 − 24T [1]
18T = 42 − 24T, so 42T = 42 and T = 1 [1]
They meet at **11 00**, **18 km from A** [1]
*Examiner insight:* reading the crossing point from an accurate graph is also a valid method; whichever you use, both the time and the distance are needed for the final mark.

**6. (a)** Rise = 50 − 20 = 30 cm in 5 min [1]; rate = **6 cm per minute** [1]
**(b)** 30 cm in 6 min = **5 cm per minute** [1]
**(c)** Volume per minute = 40 × 25 × 6 = 6000 cm³ [1] = **6 litres per minute** [1]
*Examiner insight:* the depth rate and the volume rate are different quantities; give the unit with each answer, since "6" alone is ambiguous between cm/min and litres/min.

**7. (a)** (16 − 4) ÷ 10 = **1.2 m/s²** [1]
**(b)** 16 ÷ 8 = **2 m/s²** [1]
**(c)** Trapezium ½ × (4 + 16) × 10 = 100 m and rectangle 30 × 16 = 480 m [1]; triangle ½ × 8 × 16 = 64 m [1]; total = **644 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* the first section starts at 4 m/s, so it is a trapezium; a triangle (80 m) loses the accuracy mark but the other areas still earn credit.

**8. (a)** Area = V × 90 + ½ × 25 × V + ½ × 35 × V = 120V [1]; 120V = 3000 (using 3 km = 3000 m) [1]; **V = 25** [1]
**(b)** 25 × 3.6 = **90 km/h** [1]
**(c)** 25 ÷ 25 = **1 m/s²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* leaving the distance as 3 instead of 3000 m gives V = 0.025; check that units match before setting area equal to distance.

**9. (a)** Points plotted correctly [1]; joined with a smooth curve through the origin [1]
**(b)** Tangent drawn touching the curve at (4, 8) [1]; for example, it passes through about (2, 0) and (6, 16), so gradient = 16 ÷ 4 [1]; speed ≈ **4 m/s** [1]
**(c)** The curve gets steeper as t increases, so the gradient (speed) increases [1]
*Examiner insight:* the gradient must come from two points on your drawn tangent, read from the grid; a line joining two table values is a chord, not a tangent, so it is not the method the question asks for.

**10. (a)** Speed at 4 s = 9 ÷ 6 × 4 = 6 m/s [1]; distance = ½ × 4 × 6 = **12 m** [1]
**(b)** ½ × 6 × 9 = 27 m and 14 × 9 = 126 m [1]; trapezium ½ × (9 + 3) × 6 = 36 m [1]; total = **189 m** [1]
**(c)** 189 ÷ 26 = **7.27 m/s** (3 s.f.) [1]
**(d)** (9 − 3) ÷ 6 = **1 m/s²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* in (a) the height of the triangle is the speed at 4 s, not 9 m/s; using 9 gives 18 m and scores no accuracy mark.

**11. (a)** 36 ÷ 3.6 = **10 m/s** [1]
**(b)** 10 ÷ 20 = **0.5 m/s²** [1]
**(c)** 10 ÷ 0.4 = **25 s** [1]
**(d)** Areas: ½ × 20 × 10 = 100 m and ½ × 25 × 10 = 125 m [1]; 100 + 10t + 125 = 1000 [1]; **t = 77.5** [1]
**(e)** 20 + 77.5 + 25 = **122.5 s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* convert the speed to m/s before finding areas; mixing km/h with seconds makes every later area wrong.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Misreading a scale where each square is worth 2, 5 or 10 units.
- Using minutes with km/h, or km with m/s, without converting.
- Leaving out stopped time when finding an average speed.
- Scaling from a graph that does not pass through the origin.
- Giving a gradient with no units or no meaning when asked to interpret it.
- Calling the gradient of a distance–time graph an acceleration.
- (Extended) Measuring a tangent gradient from points on the curve.
- (Extended) Treating a trapezium section of a speed–time graph as a triangle.
- (Extended) Writing a deceleration as a negative value.
