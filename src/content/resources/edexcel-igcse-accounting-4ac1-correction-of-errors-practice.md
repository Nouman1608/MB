## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State whether each error would cause the trial balance totals to disagree.

**(a)** A credit sale was not recorded at all. **[1]**
**(b)** Electricity of 230 was debited to electricity but the bank was not credited. **[1]**
**(c)** A new printer was debited to office expenses. **[1]**
**(d)** The sales account was overcast by 500. **[1]**
**(e)** A payment to J. Moss was debited to J. Ross. **[1]**
**(f)** A cheque from a customer was debited to the customer and credited to the bank. **[1]**

**2.** Explain why an error of principle does not affect the agreement of the trial balance. **[2]**

**3.** A credit sale of 520 to F. Lowe was completely omitted from the books. Prepare the journal entry to correct the error. **[2]**

**4.** A trial balance shows debits of 61,380 and credits of 60,940. State the amount and side of the opening entry in the suspense account. **[2]**

**5.** A cash sale of 90 was debited to sales and credited to cash. Explain why the correction uses 180 and not 90. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** Prepare journal entries to correct these errors made by Tarrant Electrical. None of them affects the agreement of the trial balance.

**(a)** A credit purchase of 870 from Rowe Supplies was not recorded. **[2]**
**(b)** A payment of 340 to K. Bell was debited to the account of K. Bello. **[2]**
**(c)** A computer for office use costing 1,150 was debited to office expenses. **[2]**
**(d)** A cash sale of 215 was recorded as 251 in both the cash account and the sales account. **[2]**

**7.** The trial balance of Pemberton Hardware at 31 December 2027 showed debits of 73,210 and credits of 72,640. The difference was entered in a suspense account. These errors were then found.

1. The purchases day book was overcast by 300.
2. Discount received of 85 was posted to the debit side of the discount received account. The supplier's account was correct.
3. Wages of 2,140 were correctly entered in the bank but were debited to the wages account as 2,410.
4. A credit sale of 170 to M. Doyle was credited to sales but not posted to Doyle's account.
5. Repairs to machinery of 600 were debited to the machinery account.

Prepare journal entries to correct errors 1 to 5. **[8]**

**8.** Using question 7, prepare the suspense account, showing that it clears. **[4]**

**9.** Pemberton Hardware's draft profit was 25,300 before the errors in question 7 were found. No depreciation has been charged on the repair cost in error 5.

Prepare a statement showing the corrected profit. **[5]**

**10.** A bookkeeper says: "Our trial balance agreed, so there can be no errors in our sales and wages accounts." Using an example of a compensating error, explain why this is not necessarily true. **[4]**

**11.** After all known errors have been corrected, a suspense account still has a debit balance of 90. Explain what this means and suggest two possible causes. **[3]**

**12.** For each error, state the effect on profit and on the statement of financial position.

**(a)** Rent received of 450 was credited to commission received. **[2]**
**(b)** The owner's private car insurance of 320, paid from the business bank account, was debited to insurance expense. **[2]**
**(c)** Goods for resale costing 1,100 were debited to the equipment account. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) No [1]. (b) Yes [1]. (c) No [1]. (d) Yes [1]. (e) No [1]. (f) No [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (f) both entries are on the wrong sides but are equal, so the totals still agree.

**2.** The debit and credit are both posted with the same amount [1]; only the class of account is wrong, such as an asset debited to an expense, so total debits still equal total credits [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Dr F. Lowe 520, Cr Sales 520 [1]. Narrative: credit sale omitted from the books [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Debits exceed credits by 440 [1], so the suspense account is opened with a **credit** of 440 [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The wrong entries must first be cancelled, needing 90 on each side [1], and then the correct entries (Dr cash, Cr sales) made, needing another 90, so 180 in total [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Dr Purchases 870, Cr Rowe Supplies 870 [1]; narrative: credit purchase omitted [1].
**(b)** Dr K. Bell 340, Cr K. Bello 340 [1]; narrative: payment posted to the wrong supplier's account [1].
**(c)** Dr Office equipment 1,150, Cr Office expenses 1,150 [1]; narrative: computer is capital expenditure [1].
**(d)** Dr Sales 36, Cr Cash 36 [1]; narrative: cash sale of 215 recorded as 251 [1]. **[8]**

**10.** An agreed trial balance shows only that total debits equal total credits [1]. For example, if sales were overcast by 200 and wages were also overcast by 200 [1], the extra credit and the extra debit cancel out, so the totals agree [1]. Both accounts are wrong, and profit is unaffected only by chance; the errors are found only by checking the accounts themselves [1]. **[4]**

**11.** At least one error has not yet been found, because the corrections made so far do not explain the whole difference [1]. Possible causes, any two: a casting error in an account or book; a balance omitted from the trial balance; an amount posted on one side only [1]; a transposition in one account [1]. **[3]**

**12. (a)** No effect on profit: both are income accounts and total income is unchanged [1]. No effect on the statement of financial position [1].
**(b)** Profit is understated by 320, because a private cost was treated as an expense [1]. Drawings are understated by 320, so the owner's capital at the year end is unaffected overall, but the drawings line is wrong [1].
**(c)** Profit is overstated by 1,100, because purchases are understated [1]. Non-current assets (equipment) are overstated by 1,100 [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using suspense for errors that leave the trial balance agreeing.
- Correcting a wrong-side posting by the single amount.
- Opening the suspense account on the wrong side.
- Leaving out narratives.
- Changing profit for corrections that only affect assets or liabilities.
- Ignoring the error of principle in a profit statement because it does not involve suspense.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I decide whether a correction needs suspense?**
If the original error left the debits and credits unequal, one side of the correction goes to suspense. If both sides were posted with equal amounts, suspense is not used.

**Does every correction change profit?**
No. Only corrections to income or expense accounts change profit. A correction between two customers' accounts, or between assets, does not.
