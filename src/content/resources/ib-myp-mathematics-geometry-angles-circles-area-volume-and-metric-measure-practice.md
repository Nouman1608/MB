## Questions

**1.** Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 18°. Find (a) the number of sides, (b) each interior angle, (c) the sum of the interior angles. **[3]**

**2.** Two parallel lines are crossed by a transversal. A pair of alternate angles measure (4x − 15)° and (2x + 39)°. Find x, the size of each alternate angle, and the size of the co-interior angle to one of them. **[4]**

**3.** A garden bed is a rectangle 9 m long and 4 m wide with a semicircle of diameter 4 m attached to one short end. Find (a) its area and (b) the length of its outer boundary. **[5]**

**4.** (a) Convert 3.2 m² to cm². **[1]**
**(b)** Convert 450 000 cm³ to m³. **[1]**
**(c)** (Extended) A tank measures 1.5 m by 0.8 m by 0.6 m. Find its capacity in litres. **[2]**

**5.** A tin is a cylinder of radius 3.5 cm and height 12 cm.

**(a)** Find its volume. **[2]**
**(b)** A paper label covers the curved surface exactly. Find the area of the label. **[2]**
**(c)** The label is drawn as a net. Find the length of the rectangle's edge that wraps around the tin. **[1]**

**6.** A cone has base radius 9 cm and slant height 15 cm.

**(a)** Find its vertical height. **[2]**
**(b)** Find its volume. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the area of its curved surface. **[1]**

**7.** A candle holder is a cube of side 6 cm with a square-based pyramid of height 8 cm fixed on top. The pyramid's base is the top face of the cube.

**(a)** Find the total volume. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the exposed surface area, including the underside of the cube. **[4]**

**8.** A circle has radius 8 cm. A sector has an angle of 60° at the centre. The chord joining the ends of the bounding arc forms a segment.

**(a)** Calculate the length of the arc. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the sector's area. **[2]**
**(c)** The triangle formed by the two radii and the chord is equilateral. Find the area of the segment. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the perimeter of the segment. **[1]**

**9.** (a) A chord of length 30 cm is in a circle of radius 17 cm. Find the distance from the centre to the chord. **[3]**
**(b)** A tangent PT of length 40 cm touches a circle of radius 9 cm at T. The centre is O. Find OP. **[3]**

**10.** A, B, C and D lie in order on a circle with centre O. Angle AOC is 140°, where this angle stands on the minor arc AC and D is on that arc. B is on the major arc.

**(a)** Find angle ABC, giving a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** Find angle ADC and state the rule you used. **[2]**
**(c)** A tangent is drawn at A. Find the angle between this tangent and chord AC on the side of D, using the alternate segment theorem. **[3]**

**11.** (Enrichment) (a) A sector has radius 9 cm and angle π/3 radians. Find the arc length and the sector area. **[2]**
**(b)** A square is inscribed in a circle of radius 5 cm. Find the side of the square and the percentage of the circle it fills. **[3]**

**12.** (Enrichment) A fractal curve starts as an equilateral triangle of perimeter 18 cm. At each stage every side is replaced by four sides, each one third as long.

**(a)** Find the perimeter at stages 1, 2 and 3. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the first stage at which the perimeter exceeds 100 cm. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) 360 ÷ 18 = **20 sides** [1]
(b) Interior = 180 − 18 = **162°** [1]
(c) (20 − 2) × 180 = **3240°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each part is a separate line of working. Writing only the final sum, without showing how you found n, leaves nothing to credit if the value of n is wrong.

**2.** Alternate angles are equal: 4x − 15 = 2x + 39 [1]
2x = 54, so **x = 27** [1]
Angle = 4(27) − 15 = **93°** [1]
Co-interior angle = 180 − 93 = **87°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name the rule (alternate angles, parallel lines) next to the equation. Mixing up alternate and co-interior would give the equation 6x + 24 = 180, a different value of x, so check the answer against the diagram.

**3.** (a) Rectangle 9 × 4 = 36 [1]; semicircle ½ × π × 2² = 2π = 6.28 [1]; area = **42.3 m²** [1]
(b) Boundary = 9 + 9 + 4 + arc π × 2 [1] = 22 + 6.28 = **28.3 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The boundary leaves out the diameter where the semicircle joins the rectangle. Including 4 m for that join is the usual slip on compound perimeters.

**4.** (a) 3.2 × 10 000 = **32 000 cm²** [1]
(b) 450 000 ÷ 1 000 000 = **0.45 m³** [1]
(c) Volume = 1.5 × 0.8 × 0.6 = 0.72 m³ [1]; capacity = 0.72 × 1000 = **720 L** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using the length factor (100) for area, or 100 for volume, is the typical error. Write the factor you apply, for example 100² = 10 000.

**5.** (a) V = π × 3.5² × 12 [1] = **462 cm³** [1]
(b) Curved area = 2π × 3.5 × 12 [1] = **264 cm²** [1]
(c) Circumference = 2π × 3.5 = **22.0 cm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) uses 2πrh, not the total surface area. Reading what the label covers tells you which faces to include.

**6.** (a) h² = 15² − 9² = 144 [1]; **h = 12 cm** [1]
(b) V = ⅓ × π × 81 × 12 [1] = 324π = **1020 cm³** [1]
(c) Curved area = π × 9 × 15 = 135π = **424 cm²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Volume uses vertical height and curved area uses slant height. Using 15 in the volume formula is a common slip, so label which length you are using.

**7.** (a) Cube 6³ = 216 [1]; pyramid ⅓ × 36 × 8 = 96; total **312 cm³** [1]
(b) Pyramid face height = √(8² + 3²) = √73 = 8.544 [1]
Four triangles: 4 × ½ × 6 × 8.544 = 102.5 [1]
Cube exposed: four sides 4 × 36 = 144 plus underside 36 = 180 [1]
Total = 180 + 102.5 = **283 cm²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The top face of the cube is joined to the pyramid, so it is hidden. A good solution says which faces are counted before adding them.

**8.** (a) Arc = (60/360) × 2π × 8 [1] = 8π/3 = **8.38 cm** [1]
(b) Sector = (60/360) × π × 64 [1] = **33.5 cm²** [1]
(c) Triangle = ¼ × √3 × 64 = 16√3 = 27.7 [1]; segment = 33.5 − 27.7 = **5.80 cm²** [1]
(d) Perimeter = arc + chord = 8.38 + 8 = **16.4 cm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A segment is sector minus triangle, not sector minus a rectangle or a single radius. Keep unrounded values until the final subtraction, since the two areas are close in size.

**9.** (a) Half the chord = 15 cm, as the perpendicular from the centre bisects it [1]
d² = 17² − 15² = 64 [1]; **d = 8 cm** [1]
(b) The radius OT is perpendicular to the tangent PT, so triangle OTP is right-angled at T [1]
OP² = 9² + 40² = 1681 [1]; **OP = 41 cm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* State the theorem that justifies the right angle. Without it, the use of Pythagoras is not supported by a geometric reason.

**10.** (a) Angle at circumference = ½ × 140 = **70°**, since the central angle on arc AC is double the angle at B on that arc [2]
(b) Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°, so ADC = 180 − 70 = **110°** [2]
(c) Triangle OAC is isosceles, so angle OAC = (180 − 140) ÷ 2 = 20° [1]
The tangent at A is perpendicular to OA, so the angle is 90 − 20 = **70°** [1]
This matches angle ABC in the alternate segment, which confirms the result [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each answer needs its reason, not only the number. Check which arc your angle stands on before using the centre theorem: B on the major arc gives half of 140°, not half of the reflex angle.

**11.** (a) Arc = 9 × π/3 = 3π = **9.42 cm**; sector = ½ × 81 × π/3 = 13.5π = **42.4 cm²** [2]
(b) The square's diagonal is the diameter, 10 cm. Side = 10 ÷ √2 = **7.07 cm** [1]; area = 50 cm² [1]
Circle area = 25π = 78.5; fraction = 50 ÷ 78.5 = **63.7%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In radians the angle must stay in radians in the formulas rθ and ½r²θ. Mixing it with the degree formula gives a wrong answer.

**12.** (a) Each stage multiplies the perimeter by 4/3: stage 1 = 24 cm, stage 2 = 32 cm, stage 3 = **42.7 cm** [2]
(b) Stage 5 = 75.9, stage 6 = 18 × (4/3)⁶ = 101.1 [1]; so **stage 6** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the multiplying factor and the repeated pattern. A list of stages with no stated rule makes it hard to see your reasoning.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using diameter where a formula needs the radius.
- Including the join between two shapes in a compound perimeter.
- Counting hidden faces in the surface area of a compound solid.
- Converting area or volume with the length factor.
- Using slant height for volume or vertical height for curved surface area.
- Giving an angle with no theorem named.
- Mixing alternate and co-interior angle rules.
- Rounding in the middle of a multi-step solution.
- Omitting units, or writing cm² for a volume.
