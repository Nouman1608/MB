## Questions

**1.** A woodland measures 60 m by 30 m. A student places ten random quadrats, each 1 m², and counts wood sorrel plants: 4, 0, 2, 7, 3, 1, 5, 2, 0, 6. Estimate the number of wood sorrel plants in the whole woodland. Show your working. **[4]**

**2.** A researcher marks 60 field voles with a small ear tag and releases them. A week later 45 voles are trapped, of which 9 carry a tag.

**(a)** Calculate an estimate of the population. **[3]**
**(b)** State one assumption made in this method. **[1]**

**3.** A student suspects that daisies are more abundant on a mown lawn than in an unmown meadow next to it. Describe how the student could investigate this using quadrats. **[6]**

**4.** Name the equipment best suited to each job.

**(a)** Collecting small insects from leaf litter by suction without crushing them. **[1]**
**(b)** Collecting invertebrates from the water of a pond. **[1]**
**(c)** Identifying an unfamiliar plant from its features. **[1]**

**5.** A student lays a transect away from a footpath and records percentage cover of clover in a 0.5 m by 0.5 m quadrat at each point.

| Distance from path (m) | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clover cover (%) | 4 | 11 | 19 | 27 | 31 |

**(a)** Give one reason for using a transect rather than random quadrats here. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the pattern shown, quoting figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the mean percentage cover. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest one factor that changes with distance from the path and could explain the pattern. **[1]**

**6.** The numbers of snails found in 20 quadrats are listed in order: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11, 12, 14, 15, 17, 18, 20, 21, 23, 24, 26, 27, 30.

**(a)** Find the 75th percentile, showing which position you used. **[2]**
**(b)** State what the value in (a) tells you about the quadrats. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the mean number of snails per quadrat. **[2]**

**7.** A hunted species of antelope fell from 1850 animals to 1110 animals over ten years.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how hunting can reduce biodiversity. **[2]**

**8.** A nature reserve receives 1200 visitors in a season. Each visitor pays £85, and 40% of the income is spent on conservation.

**(a)** Calculate the amount spent on conservation. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain two ways in which ecotourism can help to maintain biodiversity. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest one way in which ecotourism could harm the wildlife it relies on. **[1]**

**9.** Describe two positive and two negative human interactions within ecosystems, and for each say how it affects biodiversity. **[4]**

**10.** Many countries share one migration route used by an endangered bird.

**(a)** Explain why it can be hard to gain agreement on a scheme to protect the whole route. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why it is hard to monitor whether such a scheme is working. **[2]**
**(c)** State one benefit of maintaining high biodiversity. **[1]**

**11.** **(Higher Tier only)** A stream is sampled at three sites. Site 1 is upstream of a sewage outflow, Site 2 is 50 m downstream and Site 3 is 500 m downstream.

| Site | Dissolved oxygen (mg/dm³) | Stonefly nymphs | Bloodworms |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 9.0 | 31 | 0 |
| 2 | 3.6 | 2 | 38 |
| 3 | 7.8 | 22 | 4 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage fall in dissolved oxygen from Site 1 to Site 2. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the distribution of stonefly nymphs and bloodworms. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate how well these data support the idea that the outflow caused the change in distribution. **[3]**

**12.** **(Higher Tier only)** A survey counts lichen species on trees along a line leading away from a city centre. Sulfur dioxide was measured at the same points.

| Distance from centre (km) | 1 | 3 | 5 | 7 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sulfur dioxide (arbitrary units) | 52 | 40 | 24 | 14 | 9 |
| Lichen species | 2 | 3 | 6 | 8 | 9 |

**(a)** Describe the pattern in lichen species. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the pattern. **[2]**
**(c)** Give two limitations of the evidence. **[2]**
**(d)** State one way to make the evidence stronger. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Total count = 4 + 0 + 2 + 7 + 3 + 1 + 5 + 2 + 0 + 6 = 30, so mean = 30 ÷ 10 = 3.0 per quadrat [1]. Quadrat is 1 m², so 3.0 per m² [1]. Area = 60 × 30 = 1800 m² [1]. Estimate = 3.0 × 1800 = **5400 plants [1]**. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each stage. A clear route from count to mean to area lets a slip in one step be seen, and a bare answer gives no way to give credit for method.

**2. (a)** Estimate = (60 × 45) ÷ 9 [1]. = 2700 ÷ 9 [1]. = **300 voles [1]**. **(b)** For example: tagging did not harm the voles or change their chance of capture, or no animals entered or left the population [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the relationship in words or symbols first. The answer is an estimate, and an assumption must be something the method depends on, not a general remark about care.

**3.** Mark out an area of each habitat of similar size [1]. Use a measured grid and random numbers as coordinates to place quadrats, avoiding bias [1]. Use the same quadrat size in both habitats [1]. Place a suitable number of quadrats, for example 10 or more in each habitat [1]. Count the daisies in each quadrat, then calculate the mean per quadrat for each habitat [1]. Control or note other factors such as soil, shade and time of year, and compare the means, scaling up if the area is needed [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Plan answers gain credit for specific choices, such as how the quadrat positions are decided and how many are used. "Count daisies" alone is too vague to be a method.

**4. (a)** Pooter [1]. **(b)** Pond net [1]. **(c)** Key [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the piece of equipment named in the specification list.

**5. (a)** Conditions change gradually with distance from the path, so a transect shows how abundance changes along the gradient [1]. **(b)** Clover cover increases as distance from the path increases [1], from 4% at 0 m to 31% at 8 m [1]. **(c)** Mean = (4 + 11 + 19 + 27 + 31) ÷ 5 = 92 ÷ 5 = **18.4% [1]**. **(d)** For example, trampling, soil compaction or light level [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Pattern questions need figures from the table. A description that only says "it goes up" shows the trend but not that you read the data.

**6. (a)** Position = 0.75 × 20 = 15 [1]. The 15th value is **21 [1]**. **(b)** About 75% of the quadrats had 21 snails or fewer [1]. **(c)** Total = 292 [1], mean = 292 ÷ 20 = **14.6 snails per quadrat [1]**. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say clearly how you found the position. A percentile answer is a value from the data, not the position number itself.

**7. (a)** Decrease = 1850 − 1110 = 740 [1]. Percentage decrease = 740 ÷ 1850 × 100 = **40.0% [1]**. **(b)** Fewer animals of the species are left, so the species may become extinct, reducing the variety of living organisms [1]. Loss of an antelope affects the food web, for example its predators and the plants it grazes [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the starting value, not the final value. In explanations, move from the animal to biodiversity or the food web instead of stopping at "numbers fall".

**8. (a)** Income = 1200 × £85 = £102 000 [1]. 40% of this = 0.40 × 102 000 [1]. = **£40 800 [1]**. **(b)** Any two: income pays for rangers and monitoring [1]; jobs for local people give a reason to protect habitats [1]. **(c)** Too many visitors can disturb breeding animals or damage plants and paths [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Money answers need units. An explanation of benefits should say how the benefit helps wildlife, not just that tourism is "good for the area".

**9.** Positive, any two: captive breeding or reintroduction increases numbers of a threatened species [1]; nature reserves or restored habitats protect many species at once [1]. Negative, any two: clearing forest or draining wetlands destroys habitats so species decline [1]; hunting or poaching reduces a species towards extinction, or pollution such as acid rain removes sensitive species [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each point needs an action and an effect on biodiversity. Naming only the activity is not enough.

**10. (a)** Countries have different priorities, such as economic development or farming, so some may not agree to restrictions [1]. Agreements across borders are hard to enforce or pay for fairly [1]. **(b)** Monitoring needs regular surveys over a large area, which are costly and need trained staff [1]. Remote areas make illegal hunting or habitat loss hard to detect [1]. **(c)** For example, more stable ecosystems, or sources of food and medicines [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Questions that ask you to explain difficulty want a reason for each. Repeating "it is difficult" scores nothing.

**11. (a)** Fall = 9.0 − 3.6 = 5.4 [1]. Percentage fall = 5.4 ÷ 9.0 × 100 = **60% [1]**. **(b)** Stonefly nymphs need high oxygen, so they are few where oxygen is low and many where it is high [1]. Bloodworms tolerate low oxygen and are present at Site 2 where there is little competition [1]. **(c)** Oxygen, stonefly numbers and bloodworm numbers change at the outflow and recover downstream, which supports the idea [1]. However, there is only one sample at each site, so the result may not be typical [1]. Other variables, such as temperature or flow speed, were not controlled, and the data show correlation rather than proof of cause [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation scores best when it gives a strength and a limit, each tied to the data.

**12. (a)** Lichen species increase with distance from the centre [1], from 2 at 1 km to 9 at 9 km [1]. **(b)** Sulfur dioxide is highest near the centre (52 units) and falls with distance [1]. Sensitive lichens cannot survive high sulfur dioxide, so fewer species are present near the centre [1]. **(c)** Any two: a single line of trees only, so it may not be typical [1]; other factors such as tree type, tree age or other pollutants may differ [1]. **(d)** Repeat the survey along several lines from the centre, or record species and sulfur dioxide at more points [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the gas, the effect on the organism and the distribution. A trend alone does not show that sulfur dioxide is the cause, so keep the wording measured.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a mean per quadrat when the question asks for an estimate in the whole area.
- Using a quadrat of 0.25 m² as if it were 1 m².
- Calling quadrats random when they were placed by choice.
- Treating a capture-recapture answer as an exact count.
- Calculating a percentage change by dividing by the new value.
- Describing a graph or table without quoting figures.
- Claiming that a pattern proves a cause.
- Writing "do repeats" with no reason in an evaluation.
