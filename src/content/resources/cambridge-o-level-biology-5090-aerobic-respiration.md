This guide covers subtopic **10.2 Aerobic respiration** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series. The syllabus asks you to describe aerobic respiration as the release of a relatively large amount of energy by the breakdown of glucose in the presence of oxygen, and to state the word equation and the balanced chemical equation for aerobic respiration.

The [Respiration guide](/resources/o-level-biology-5090-respiration/) covers the whole topic, and the [10.2 revision notes](/resources/o-level-biology-5090-respiration-revision-notes/) give a condensed version. This page explains each phrase of the definition, shows how to check and write the balanced equation, and works through data from experiments. Test yourself with the [10.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-aerobic-respiration-practice/). Anaerobic respiration is covered in subtopic 10.3.

## The definition

**Aerobic respiration is the release of a relatively large amount of energy by the breakdown of glucose in the presence of oxygen.**

| Phrase | What it means |
|---|---|
| Release of energy | The chemical energy stored in glucose is released. Energy is not made. |
| Relatively large amount | Aerobic respiration releases far more energy from each glucose molecule than anaerobic respiration does |
| Breakdown of glucose | Glucose is broken down completely into simple inorganic molecules |
| In the presence of oxygen | Oxygen is needed. "Aerobic" means "with air" |

Aerobic respiration takes place in the **mitochondria** of cells, as well as in the cytoplasm. This is why cells with a high energy demand, such as muscle cells, have many mitochondria. The oxygen reaches the cells in the blood, after being absorbed by the lungs, and the carbon dioxide is carried back to the lungs to be breathed out.

### Why "relatively large" matters

The glucose molecule is broken down completely, all the way to carbon dioxide and water, so nearly all of the chemical energy stored in it is released. In anaerobic respiration the glucose is only partly broken down and much of the energy stays locked in the products, such as lactic acid or ethanol. This is why aerobic respiration is the main source of energy for most organisms, and why the word "relatively large" appears in the syllabus.

## The equations

**Word equation**

glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water

(Energy is released.)

**Balanced chemical equation**

C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O

### Checking that it balances

Count each kind of atom on both sides.

| Atom | Left side | Right side |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon | 6 (in C6H12O6) | 6 (in 6CO2) |
| Hydrogen | 12 (in C6H12O6) | 12 (in 6H2O) |
| Oxygen | 6 (in C6H12O6) + 12 (in 6O2) = 18 | 12 (in 6CO2) + 6 (in 6H2O) = 18 |

All three match, so the equation balances. Learn it exactly: **one** glucose, **six** oxygen, **six** carbon dioxide, **six** water.

### The link with photosynthesis

The equation for aerobic respiration is the reverse of the equation for photosynthesis (subtopic 6.1).

| | Photosynthesis | Aerobic respiration |
|---|---|---|
| Reactants | 6CO2 + 6H2O | C6H12O6 + 6O2 |
| Products | C6H12O6 + 6O2 | 6CO2 + 6H2O |
| Energy | Taken in (light) | Released |

Photosynthesis stores energy in glucose and respiration releases it. Both go on in plants. In animals only respiration takes place.

### Common errors in the equation

- Writing **oxygen** as O (one atom) rather than O2.
- Putting the 6 in front of the glucose instead of the oxygen, water and carbon dioxide.
- Leaving out water as a product.
- Writing "energy" as a product in the chemical equation without any note; you can add "+ energy" but do not count it among the atoms.
- Writing glucose as C6H12O6 with the numbers on the line rather than as subscripts. In an exam, always write them clearly so that they can be read as subscripts.

## Evidence for aerobic respiration

Because aerobic respiration produces carbon dioxide, water and heat, and uses oxygen, each product can be shown in an experiment.

- **Carbon dioxide**: breathing out through limewater turns it cloudy; the carbon dioxide from germinating seeds can also be shown with limewater or hydrogencarbonate indicator.
- **Heat**: germinating seeds in a vacuum flask (thermos) warm up, compared with a flask of boiled, killed seeds that do not.
- **Oxygen use**: in a respirometer, a chemical such as soda lime absorbs the carbon dioxide produced, so any fall in gas volume shows the oxygen used.

### Worked example: oxygen uptake

*A respirometer contains 12 g of germinating peas. Soda lime absorbs the carbon dioxide. The coloured liquid in the capillary moved 1.8 cm cubed in 20 minutes. Calculate the rate of oxygen uptake per minute and per gram.*

1. Rate per minute = 1.8 divided by 20 = **0.09 cm cubed per minute**.
2. Rate per gram per minute = 0.09 divided by 12 = **0.0075 cm cubed per gram per minute**.
3. **Explanation of the liquid movement**: the peas take in oxygen, while the soda lime removes the carbon dioxide produced, so the total gas volume falls and the liquid moves towards the peas.
4. **Control**: an identical respirometer with boiled peas (or glass beads of the same mass) should show no movement, which proves that the change is due to living respiration.

Dividing by the mass lets you compare samples of different mass fairly.

## Worked example: balancing from scratch

*Balance the equation: C6H12O6 + O2 → CO2 + H2O.*

Work in a fixed order so that you never lose track.

1. **Carbon first.** There are 6 carbon atoms in the glucose, so there must be 6 CO2 on the right. Now: C6H12O6 + O2 → 6CO2 + H2O.
2. **Hydrogen next.** There are 12 hydrogen atoms in the glucose, and each H2O has 2, so 6 H2O are needed on the right. Now: C6H12O6 + O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O.
3. **Oxygen last.** The right side now has 12 + 6 = 18 oxygen atoms. The glucose supplies 6, so 12 more must come from O2. Each O2 has 2 atoms, so 6 O2 are needed. Now: C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O.
4. **Check** every atom on both sides: 6 carbon, 12 hydrogen and 18 oxygen on each side.

This order works because oxygen is in more than one substance, so it is balanced last.

## Reading a question on aerobic respiration

Most questions on this subtopic fall into four types. Recognising the type tells you what the examiner wants.

| Question type | What to write |
|---|---|
| State the equation | Word equation, or balanced symbol equation, exactly as above |
| Define aerobic respiration | Relatively large energy release, breakdown of glucose, in the presence of oxygen |
| Explain why a tissue has many mitochondria | High energy demand, more aerobic respiration, more energy released |
| Interpret experimental data | Rate calculation, the role of the absorbent chemical, the role of the control |

### An exam-style explanation

*Explain why the breathing rate of a person increases when they run.* A full-mark answer links the ideas in order. Running uses muscle contraction, which needs energy. The muscle cells increase their rate of aerobic respiration. They use more oxygen and release more carbon dioxide. The brain detects the rise in carbon dioxide in the blood, and so breathing becomes faster and deeper to bring in more oxygen and remove the extra carbon dioxide.

## Why cells need oxygen

Cells cannot store much oxygen, so a continuous supply is needed. The body supplies it through the breathing system (topic 9) and the blood (topic 11). If the oxygen supply is too low, cells must use anaerobic respiration (subtopic 10.3).

## Linking aerobic respiration with other topics

- **Topic 3.2**: active transport uses energy from respiration.
- **Topic 9**: the lungs bring oxygen in and remove carbon dioxide.
- **Topic 11.4**: red blood cells carry oxygen to the respiring cells.
- **Topic 13**: carbon dioxide from respiration is excreted through the lungs.

## Summary for revision

Learn aerobic respiration as three linked facts. First, the definition: the release of a relatively large amount of energy by the breakdown of glucose in the presence of oxygen. Second, the equation: C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O. Third, the place: mainly the mitochondria, which are numerous in cells that need a lot of energy. If you can write all three without notes, and explain why muscle cells have many mitochondria, you have the core of this subtopic.

## Common mistakes

- Defining aerobic respiration as breathing.
- Forgetting "relatively large amount" when comparing with anaerobic respiration.
- Writing "energy is produced" instead of "released".
- Writing an unbalanced equation, or using the photosynthesis equation instead.
- Saying that respiration needs oxygen "to make" energy.
- Saying that carbon dioxide is the product used in photosynthesis only. It is also the product of aerobic respiration.

## Command words

- **State**: write the word or chemical equation from memory.
- **Describe**: give the definition and the features of aerobic respiration.
- **Explain**: give the reason, such as why muscle cells have many mitochondria.
- **Calculate**: work out a rate from the respirometer data, showing the working.
