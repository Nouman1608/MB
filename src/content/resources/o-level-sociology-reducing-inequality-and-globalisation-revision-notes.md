These notes condense key questions 3.2 and 3.3 of Cambridge O Level Sociology (2251). Laws, welfare
states and NGOs try to reduce inequality; New Right, Marxist and feminist sociologists, and critics
of NGOs, question how well they succeed. Between societies, globalisation affects inequality through
migration, transnational companies, climate change, pollution and urbanisation, explained by
capitalist exploitation, colonialism and patriarchy.

Fuller explanations: [O Level tackling inequality and globalisation guide](/resources/o-level-sociology-reducing-inequality-and-globalisation/).

## Syllabus coverage

2251 syllabus 2025–2027: **3.2** (3.2.1 legislation; 3.2.2 welfare states; 3.2.3 NGOs; 3.2.4 views
of success) and **3.3** (3.3.1 migration; 3.3.2 ecological issues; 3.3.3 explanations).

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Legislation** | Laws passed by government |
| **Progressive tax** | Tax rate rises as income rises |
| **Benefits** | State payments to those in need |
| **Welfare state** | System of state-provided services and benefits |
| **Life chances** | Opportunities for health, education, income and long life |
| **NGO** | Independent non-profit organisation |
| **Interest-free microfinance** | Small loans without interest to help the poor start businesses |
| **Underclass** | Group said to be dependent on welfare and outside mainstream society |
| **Safety valve** | Marxist idea that welfare releases discontent without changing capitalism |
| **Remittances** | Money migrants send home |
| **Brain drain** | Emigration of educated workers |
| **Urbanisation** | Growth of the urban share of population |
| **Colonialism** | Control and exploitation of one country by another |

## Views of success

| View | Claim | Counter-point |
|---|---|---|
| New Right | Welfare creates dependency and an underclass (Murray) | Most claimants want work; poverty has structural causes |
| Culture of poverty | Poor pass on fatalistic values (Lewis) | Values may be a response to poverty, not its cause |
| Marxist | Welfare is a safety valve for capitalism | Welfare has improved health and education for millions |
| NGO critics | Dependency, donor priorities, small scale | NGOs reach groups governments neglect |
| Feminist | Equality laws do not end the pay gap or unpaid labour | Laws have opened education and jobs to women |

## 3.3 Globalisation and inequality between societies

- **Migration:** push and pull; South Asia–Gulf labour migration; remittances versus brain drain;
  cultural diversity; negative perceptions and poor conditions for migrants.
- **TNCs:** jobs and investment, but low wages and unsafe conditions (Rana Plaza, 2013).
- **Climate change:** poorer countries most affected (Pakistan floods, 2022).
- **Pollution:** concentrated in poor communities and industrial zones.
- **Urbanisation:** informal settlements in fast-growing cities.
- **Explanations:** Marxist exploitation; colonialism; feminist patriarchy.

## Worked example: an 8-mark "discuss" answer plan

*Discuss the effects of migration on sending countries.* (practice)

1. **Positive:** remittances raise family incomes and pay for education and health care.
2. **Positive:** less unemployment at home; returning migrants bring skills and savings.
3. **Negative:** loss of skilled doctors, nurses and engineers (brain drain).
4. **Negative:** family separation; children raised by grandparents; dependence on remittances.
5. **Judgement:** effects depend on who migrates and whether remittances are invested.

## Worked example: a 14-mark evaluation plan

*Evaluate the view that globalisation benefits poorer societies.* (practice)

1. **For:** factory jobs and foreign investment; remittances from migrants in the Gulf and elsewhere;
   spread of mobile phones and online banking; access to global markets for exports.
2. **Against:** low wages and unsafe workplaces (Rana Plaza, 2013); brain drain; climate change and
   pollution fall hardest on poorer countries; Marxists see profits flowing to rich countries;
   colonial patterns of trade continue; women's cheap labour is exploited (feminist view).
3. **Conclusion:** benefits exist but are unevenly shared within and between societies, so the view
   is only partly supported.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Listing examples of welfare without linking them to **life chances**.
- Forgetting that the syllabus asks for **criticisms**, not just descriptions, in 3.2.4.
- Confusing **immigration** (coming in) with **emigration** (going out).
- Writing about climate change without explaining how it affects **inequality between societies**.
- Using examples you cannot explain accurately — choose ones you know well.

## Quick self-test

1. Give two examples of legislation to reduce inequality.
2. What is a dependency culture?
3. Give one criticism of NGOs.
4. Explain one way TNCs may increase inequality.
5. Outline the feminist explanation of global inequality.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between 3.2 and 3.3?** 3.2 is about attempts to reduce inequality, mostly
within societies; 3.3 is about how globalisation affects inequality between societies.

**Should I always mention colonialism in 3.3?** It is one of three named explanations; use it when
asked to explain global inequality, alongside Marxist and feminist views.

**Is universal basic income widely used?** It has mainly been trialled rather than fully adopted, so
evaluate it as a proposal.
