These questions cover section 1.4, Density, of the Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Take the density of water as 1.0 g/cm³ (1000 kg/m³).

> **Tier note:** questions 1 to 5 and 7 to 10 use Core content only (1.4 Core 1–3), and every candidate should do them. Questions 6, 11 and 12, marked *(Extended)*, use Supplement content (1.4 Supplement 4), examined on Papers 2 and 4 only. The marks shown are indicative only.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** **(a)** Define density. **[1]**
**(b)** State the SI unit of density. **[1]**

**2.** A metal block measures 4.0 cm × 2.5 cm × 2.0 cm and has a mass of 158 g.

**(a)** Calculate its density in g/cm³. **[2]**
**(b)** Convert your answer to kg/m³. **[1]**

**3.** A fuel tank holds 0.050 m³ of petrol of density 750 kg/m³. Calculate the mass of the petrol. **[2]**

**4.** A piece of aluminium has a mass of 0.54 kg. The density of aluminium is 2700 kg/m³. Calculate its volume in m³. **[2]**

**5.** Three objects are placed in water. Determine which of them float. Show your working.

| Object | Mass / g | Volume / cm³ |
|---|---|---|
| A | 45 | 60 |
| B | 130 | 100 |
| C | 9.5 | 10 |

**[3]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Oil of density 0.91 g/cm³ and salt water of density 1.03 g/cm³ are poured into a beaker. They do not mix. State which liquid forms the top layer and explain why. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student finds the density of a liquid.

**(a)** Describe how the student should do this using a measuring cylinder and a balance. **[4]**
**(b)** The empty cylinder has a mass of 105.2 g. With 50.0 cm³ of liquid it has a mass of 152.7 g. Calculate the density of the liquid. **[2]**

**8.** A student finds the density of a large, irregularly shaped stone that is too wide to fit in a measuring cylinder.

**(a)** Describe how the student can find the volume of the stone. **[3]**
**(b)** The volume of water collected is 18.0 cm³ and the stone has a mass of 48.6 g. Calculate its density. **[2]**
**(c)** An air bubble was trapped under the stone. State the effect on the calculated density. **[1]**

**9.** A solid metal cylinder is 10.0 cm long, with a diameter of 2.0 cm. Its mass is 245 g.

**(a)** Calculate the density of the metal. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe how a ruler and two wooden blocks can be used to measure the diameter. **[1]**
**(c)** A 3.0 cm length is cut from the cylinder. State the density of this piece. **[1]**

**10.** A wooden beam is 2.0 m long, 0.20 m wide and 0.10 m thick. Its mass is 24 kg.

**(a)** Calculate the density of the wood. **[2]**
**(b)** State whether the beam floats in water, and why. **[1]**
**(c)** A liquid has a density of 500 kg/m³. State whether the beam floats in this liquid. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Four liquids that do not mix are poured into a tall jar: A (0.70 g/cm³), B (1.00 g/cm³), C (1.30 g/cm³) and D (0.85 g/cm³).

**(a)** State the order of the layers from top to bottom. **[2]**
**(b)** A plastic bead of density 0.90 g/cm³ is dropped into the jar. State where it comes to rest and explain why. **[2]**
**(c)** Water and alcohol are poured into a second jar. Suggest why they do not form separate layers. **[1]**

**12.** *(Extended)* Ice has a density of 0.92 g/cm³. A cooking oil has a density of 0.80 g/cm³.

**(a)** State whether an ice cube floats or sinks (i) in water and (ii) in the oil. **[2]**
**(b)** A glass contains a layer of the oil floating on water. An ice cube is dropped in. State where it settles and explain why. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Mass per unit volume [1]
**(b)** kg/m³ [1]

**2. (a)** V = 4.0 × 2.5 × 2.0 = 20 cm³ [1]; ρ = 158 ÷ 20 = **7.9 g/cm³** [1]
**(b)** **7900 kg/m³** [1]

**3.** m = ρV = 750 × 0.050 [1] = **37.5 kg** (38 kg) [1]

**4.** V = m/ρ = 0.54 ÷ 2700 [1] = **2.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³** [1]

**5.** A: 45 ÷ 60 = 0.75 g/cm³; B: 130 ÷ 100 = 1.3 g/cm³; C: 9.5 ÷ 10 = 0.95 g/cm³ [1 for all three densities]; A and C float because their densities are less than 1.0 g/cm³ [1]; B sinks because its density is greater [1]. **[3]**

**6.** The oil [1]; it is less dense than the salt water [1].

**7. (a)** Find the mass of the empty measuring cylinder on a balance [1]; pour in liquid and read the volume at the bottom of the meniscus with the eye level [1]; find the mass of the cylinder and liquid [1]; subtract to find the liquid's mass and calculate density = mass ÷ volume [1].
**(b)** mass of liquid = 152.7 − 105.2 = 47.5 g [1]; ρ = 47.5 ÷ 50.0 = **0.95 g/cm³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using 152.7 g as the liquid's mass gives 3.05 g/cm³ and loses both marks.

**8. (a)** Fill a displacement (overflow) can with water until it drips from the spout, then wait until it stops [1]; place a measuring cylinder under the spout and lower the stone gently in on a thread until it is fully covered [1]; the volume of water collected in the cylinder equals the stone's volume [1].
**(b)** ρ = 48.6 ÷ 18.0 [1] = **2.7 g/cm³** [1]
**(c)** The measured volume is too large, so the calculated density is too small [1].

**9. (a)** radius = 1.0 cm [1]; V = π × 1.0² × 10.0 = 31.4 cm³ [1]; ρ = 245 ÷ 31.4 = **7.8 g/cm³** [1]
**(b)** Place the cylinder between two blocks that touch it on either side and are square to the ruler; measure the distance between the blocks [1].
**(c)** **7.8 g/cm³** (unchanged) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using the diameter (2.0 cm) as the radius gives a volume four times too big.

**10. (a)** V = 2.0 × 0.20 × 0.10 = 0.040 m³ [1]; ρ = 24 ÷ 0.040 = **600 kg/m³** [1]
**(b)** Floats, because 600 kg/m³ is less than 1000 kg/m³ [1].
**(c)** Sinks (600 kg/m³ is greater than 500 kg/m³) [1].

**11. (a)** A, D, B, C [2 for the correct order; 1 if only one pair is swapped]
**(b)** At the boundary between D and B [1]; it is denser than D (0.85 g/cm³), so it sinks through D, but less dense than B (1.00 g/cm³), so it floats on B [1].
**(c)** They mix, so no separate layers can form [1].

**12. (a)** (i) floats [1] (ii) sinks [1]
**(b)** It sinks through the oil and floats on the water, at the boundary between the layers [1]; 0.92 g/cm³ is greater than 0.80 g/cm³ but less than 1.0 g/cm³ [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the mass of the container and liquid together.
- Forgetting to subtract the starting water level.
- Using a diameter as a radius in the volume of a cylinder.
- Comparing masses instead of densities to decide whether something floats.
- *(Extended)* Giving the order of layers without a reason based on density.
