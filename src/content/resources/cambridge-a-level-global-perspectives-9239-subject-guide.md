Cambridge International AS & A Level Global Perspectives & Research (9239) is a skills-based course: rather than a list of content topics, it is organised around four pieces of assessed work that train you to research, analyse arguments, collaborate and reflect. The AS Level is made up of three components, and the full A Level adds a fourth, the Cambridge Research Report, which on its own carries 50% of the A Level. Measured as shares of the full A Level, Component 1, the Written Exam (1 hour 30 minutes, 45 marks), is worth 18%, Component 2, the Essay (40 marks), 16%, and Component 3, the Team Project (40 marks), 16%. Students often take it alongside other A Levels because the skills transfer so readily. This overview explains how the components fit together and how to plan the work.

## How the course is organised

Because 9239 is defined by its components rather than by subject topics, the course structure and the assessment structure are essentially the same thing.

**AS stage**

- **Component 1 – Written Exam.** A timed paper, and the only examination in the course, which tests the analytical skills that the other components then put to work.
- **Component 2 – Essay.** A single essay of 1750-2000 words, researched and written during the course rather than in an examination room.
- **Component 3 – Team Project.** A collaborative project in which you work with others but are assessed individually, through a presentation and a reflective paper.

**A Level stage**

- **Component 4 – Cambridge Research Report.** An independent research report of up to 5000 words, submitted with a research log that records how the work developed.

Note that the AS Level here is built from three components, not the two papers found in many other staged Cambridge subjects.

## How it's assessed

All four components are externally assessed by Cambridge. Component 1 is a timed written examination; Components 2, 3 and 4 are non-exam assessments completed over a period of time and then sent to Cambridge for marking.

| Component | Title | Duration | Marks | % of AS Level | % of A Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Component 1 | Written Exam | 1 hour 30 minutes | 45 | 36% | 18% |
| Component 2 | Essay | n/a | 40 | 32% | 16% |
| Component 3 | Team Project | n/a | 40 | 32% | 16% |
| Component 4 | Cambridge Research Report | n/a | 85 | n/a | 50% |

There are two ways to reach a qualification. The Cambridge International AS Level, made up of Components 1, 2 and 3, can be taken as a standalone qualification. Alternatively, those three components form the first stage of the full A Level, which is completed by adding Component 4 in a later series. The syllabus covers the 2026-2028 examination window and was first examined in 2026.

## How the parts fit together

The components are arranged so that each one builds a skill that the next relies on.

The Written Exam develops the analytical core of the course: judging the strength of an argument, spotting assumptions and weighing evidence. Those habits are exactly what the Essay then asks you to apply to your own research, with the added demand of presenting contrasting perspectives fairly and reaching a supported conclusion.

The Team Project adds collaboration. Working with others on a shared issue, you still have to produce your own presentation and your own reflection, which means learning to explain your thinking and to evaluate how a group process shaped the outcome.

The Cambridge Research Report then draws everything together. With half of the full A Level resting on it, it asks you to define a question, plan and carry out sustained independent research, structure a long argument and document your process in a research log. Students who have already practised source evaluation in Component 1, perspective-taking in Component 2 and reflection in Component 3 tend to find the Research Report's demands far more manageable.

Across all four components, three skills recur: evaluating sources and claims, building a reasoned argument, and reflecting honestly on your own process. Treat these as the real content of the course.

## Planning your study

Since three of the four components are produced over time rather than in a single examination, planning matters more here than in most subjects.

A sensible approach for the AS stage is to start with the analytical skills tested in the Written Exam, because they underpin everything else, and then to begin the Essay early enough that research, drafting and redrafting are not compressed into a few weeks. The Team Project usually runs alongside, and keeping notes on how the team works from the start will make the reflective paper much easier to write.

For the A Level stage, choose a Research Report question you can genuinely investigate with the sources available, and begin your research log from the first day. A rough plan in general terms is: refine the question and gather sources first, draft the main argument in the middle period, and keep the final stretch for revision, checking and completing the log.

Practise Component 1-style questions throughout the AS stage and move to complete past papers as the examination approaches. For component-by-component exam and submission advice, use the dedicated exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many components does Cambridge A Level Global Perspectives 9239 have?**
There are four. Components 1, 2 and 3 make up the AS Level, and Component 4, the Cambridge Research Report, completes the full A Level.

**How much is the Cambridge Research Report worth?**
Component 4 is marked out of 85 and is worth 50% of the full A Level. It is not part of the AS Level.

**Can I take AS Level Global Perspectives on its own?**
Yes. Cambridge International AS Level Global Perspectives & Research (Components 1-3) can be a standalone qualification, or the first three components of the A Level, completed by adding Component 4 in a later series.

**Are any 9239 components marked by my school?**
No. All four components are described as externally assessed by Cambridge, including the Essay, the Team Project and the Research Report.

**How long is the Essay?**
Component 2 is a 1750-2000 word essay, marked out of 40 and worth 32% of the AS Level or 16% of the A Level.
