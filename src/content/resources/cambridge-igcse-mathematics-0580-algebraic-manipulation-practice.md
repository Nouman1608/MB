These questions cover subtopic **2.2 Algebraic manipulation** in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions without a label use content common to Core (C2.2) and Extended (E2.2). Questions marked **(Extended)** use E2.2 content only: brackets in two variables, three brackets, the five factorising forms and completing the square. All questions can be done without a calculator.

For teaching and worked examples, read the [algebraic manipulation study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-algebraic-manipulation/); for a condensed summary, use the [algebraic manipulation revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-algebraic-manipulation-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Simplify 5p − 3q + 2p + 7q − p. **[2]**

**2.** Expand 4y(3y − 2x). **[2]**

**3.** Expand and simplify 3(2x − 1) − 2(4 − x). **[2]**

**4.** Expand and simplify (x − 6)(x + 2). **[2]**

**5.** Factorise fully 14m²n − 21mn². **[2]**

**6.** (Extended) Factorise 81x² − 16y². **[1]**

**7.** (Extended) Factorise x² − 3x − 28. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**8.**

**(a)** Expand and simplify (2x − 5)². **[2]**

**(b)** Hence, or otherwise, expand and simplify (2x − 5)² − (x − 5)(x + 5). **[2]**

**9.** (Extended) Expand and simplify (2x + 1)(x − 3)(x + 4). **[3]**

**10.** (Extended) Factorise completely:

**(a)** 3x² + 11x − 4 **[2]**

**(b)** 2x³ − 32x **[2]**

**(c)** pq − 3p + 2q − 6 **[2]**

**11.** (Extended)

**(a)** Write x² + 6x + 14 in the form (x + p)² + q. **[2]**

**(b)** Use your answer to part (a) to explain why x² + 6x + 14 is positive for every value of x. **[1]**

**(c)** Write 2x² − 12x + 23 in the form a(x + p)² + q. **[3]**

**(d)** Write down the least value of 2x² − 12x + 23 and the value of x at which it occurs. **[1]**

**12.** n is an integer.

**(a)** Write down, in terms of n, the next two consecutive integers after n. **[1]**

**(b)** Show that the sum of the squares of n and the next two consecutive integers is 3n² + 6n + 5. **[3]**

**(c)** Hence explain why this sum is never a multiple of 3. **[2]**

**13.** (Extended) A rectangle has length (3x + 2y) cm and width (x − 4y) cm.

**(a)** Find an expression, in its simplest form, for the area of the rectangle. **[2]**

**(b)** A square has side (2x − y) cm. Show that the area of the square minus the area of the rectangle is x² + 6xy + 9y² cm², and factorise this expression. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** p terms: 5p + 2p − p = 6p [1]. q terms: −3q + 7q = 4q [1]. Answer **6p + 4q**.

**2.** 4y × 3y = 12y² [1]; 4y × (−2x) = −8xy [1]. Answer **12y² − 8xy**.

**3.** 6x − 3 − 8 + 2x, with −2 × (−x) = +2x [1]. Answer **8x − 11** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the most common slip is writing −2x from −2(4 − x); the minus times a minus gives a plus.

**4.** Four products x² + 2x − 6x − 12 [1]. Answer **x² − 4x − 12** [1].

**5.** A correct partial factorisation such as 7m(2mn − 3n²) or mn(14m − 21n) [1]. Fully factorised: **7mn(2m − 3n)** [1].

**6.** 81x² = (9x)² and 16y² = (4y)²: **(9x + 4y)(9x − 4y)** [1].

**7.** Two numbers with product −28 and sum −3: −7 and 4 [1]. Answer **(x − 7)(x + 4)** [1].

**8. (a)** (2x − 5)(2x − 5) = 4x² − 10x − 10x + 25 [1] = **4x² − 20x + 25** [1].

**(b)** (x − 5)(x + 5) = x² − 25 [1]. 4x² − 20x + 25 − (x² − 25) = 4x² − 20x + 25 − x² + 25 = **3x² − 20x + 50** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the bracket around x² − 25 is essential; without it, −25 is often left as −25 instead of becoming +25.

**9.** (2x + 1)(x − 3) = 2x² − 6x + x − 3 = 2x² − 5x − 3 [1]. (2x² − 5x − 3)(x + 4) = 2x³ + 8x² − 5x² − 20x − 3x − 12 [1] (six terms, allow one sign error for this mark) = **2x³ + 3x² − 23x − 12** [1].
*Examiner insight:* substitute x = 1 to check: (3)(−2)(5) = −30 and 2 + 3 − 23 − 12 = −30.

**10. (a)** ac = −12; numbers with product −12 and sum 11 are 12 and −1, giving 3x² + 12x − x − 4 = 3x(x + 4) − 1(x + 4) [1]. Answer **(3x − 1)(x + 4)** [1].

**(b)** Common factor: 2x(x² − 16) [1]. Difference of two squares: **2x(x − 4)(x + 4)** [1].

**(c)** p(q − 3) + 2(q − 3) [1] = **(p + 2)(q − 3)** [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (b), stopping at 2x(x² − 16) is a correct factorisation but not a complete one and earns only the first mark.

**11. (a)** Half of 6 is 3: x² + 6x = (x + 3)² − 9 [1]. x² + 6x + 14 = (x + 3)² − 9 + 14 = **(x + 3)² + 5** [1].

**(b)** (x + 3)² ≥ 0 for every x, so (x + 3)² + 5 ≥ 5, which is positive [1].

**(c)** 2(x² − 6x) + 23 [1]. x² − 6x = (x − 3)² − 9 [1]. 2[(x − 3)² − 9] + 23 = 2(x − 3)² − 18 + 23 = **2(x − 3)² + 5** [1].

**(d)** Least value **5**, when **x = 3** [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (c) the −9 must be multiplied by 2. An answer of 2(x − 3)² + 14 shows the −9 was not doubled.

**12. (a)** **n + 1 and n + 2** [1].

**(b)** (n + 1)² = n² + 2n + 1 [1]. (n + 2)² = n² + 4n + 4 [1]. n² + n² + 2n + 1 + n² + 4n + 4 = **3n² + 6n + 5** [1].

**(c)** 3n² + 6n + 5 = 3(n² + 2n + 1) + 2 [1], so it is always 2 more than a multiple of 3 and is never itself a multiple of 3 [1].
*Examiner insight:* in a "show that" part, writing only the final line earns nothing. Each expansion must be shown.

**13. (a)** (3x + 2y)(x − 4y) = 3x² − 12xy + 2xy − 8y² [1] = **3x² − 10xy − 8y²** cm² [1].

**(b)** (2x − y)² = 4x² − 4xy + y² [1]. 4x² − 4xy + y² − (3x² − 10xy − 8y²) = **x² + 6xy + 9y²** [1]. This is a perfect square: **(x + 3y)²** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Changing only the first sign when a bracket is subtracted.
- Writing (2x − 5)² as 4x² + 25 or 4x² − 25, missing the middle term.
- Leaving a factorisation incomplete, such as 2x(x² − 16).
- Choosing an ac pair with the right product but the wrong sum.
- In completing the square with a ≠ 1, not multiplying the subtracted square by a.
- In "show that" questions, jumping to the printed answer without the expansions.
