Quick recall for section 5.1 Economies and diseconomies of scale of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1), Issue 2, February 2026, points 5.1.1 and 5.1.2. A split cost table, worked internal and external economies and an annotated evaluation are in the [economies and diseconomies of scale study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-economies-and-diseconomies-of-scale/); questions are in the [economies and diseconomies of scale practice set](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-economies-and-diseconomies-of-scale-practice/). Figures are invented, in dollars.

## 1. The specification wording

- **5.1.1 Economies of scale:** internal (falling average total costs as output increases); external (falling average total costs due to external factors).
- **5.1.2 Diseconomies of scale:** limits of growth.

## 2. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Scale | The size of a business's operations, such as its output |
| Average total cost (ATC) | Total cost ÷ output; the cost of one unit |
| Economies of scale | Falls in average total cost that come from operating on a larger scale |
| Internal economies of scale | Falls in ATC as the business's own output increases |
| External economies of scale | Falls in ATC due to factors outside the business, often the growth of the industry in an area |
| Diseconomies of scale | Rises in ATC caused by the business becoming too large |
| Limits of growth | Factors that stop a business growing further, including diseconomies, market size, finance and owners' wishes |

## 3. The cost logic

- Total cost usually **rises** as output rises.
- ATC can **fall** as output rises, because fixed costs are spread and unit costs fall.
- Fixed cost per unit = fixed costs ÷ output, so it always falls as output rises.
- Economies are about **average** cost, not total cost.

**Worked reminder.** Output 2,000, total cost 18,000: ATC = **9.00**. Output 5,000, total cost 35,000: ATC = **7.00**. Output 8,000, total cost 60,000: ATC = **7.50**. Economies of scale up to 5,000; average cost rises after that, which suggests diseconomies.

## 4. Internal economies (standard categories)

The specification names no types, so these are illustrations. Always finish with "so average cost falls".

| Type | One-line explanation |
|---|---|
| Purchasing | Bulk orders get a lower price per unit |
| Technical | Specialised machinery lowers cost per unit at high output |
| Financial | Larger firms may borrow more cheaply |
| Marketing | Advertising spread over more units |
| Managerial | Specialist managers work more efficiently |

**Bulk-buying reminder.** 1,200 units at 0.50 = 600; with a bulk price of 0.42, 1,200 units cost 504. Saving per unit 0.08, so ATC falls by 0.08 (other costs unchanged).

## 5. External economies

| Source | Effect |
|---|---|
| Skilled labour pool in the area | Cheaper recruitment and training |
| Specialist suppliers nearby | Lower delivery costs and faster supply |
| Better infrastructure (roads, ports, internet) | Lower transport and communication costs |
| Local training or research | Workers arrive with relevant skills |

Key point: every firm in the area can benefit, so external economies do not give one local firm an edge over its neighbours.

## 6. Diseconomies of scale

| Cause | What goes wrong | Why ATC rises |
|---|---|---|
| Communication | Messages pass through many layers | Slow or wrong decisions, mistakes repeated |
| Co-ordination and control | Many sites and departments | Duplication and waste |
| Motivation | Staff feel distant from managers | More absence, errors and staff turnover |

Possible responses: fewer management layers, delegation to local managers, central purchasing, better internal communication.

## 7. Limits of growth beyond cost

- The market may be too small to buy more output.
- Finance for expansion may be unavailable or too costly.
- Owners may want to keep control or a manageable workload.
- Diseconomies may make further growth unprofitable.

## 8. Exam traps

- Writing "economies of scale reduce total costs".
- Calling a rise in costs a diseconomy when it was caused by something else, such as a rise in raw material prices.
- Calling a bulk discount external: it comes from the firm's own size, so it is internal.
- Describing a type of economy without the effect on cost per unit.
- Forgetting that diseconomies can appear even while fixed cost per unit is still falling.
- In "evaluate" questions, ignoring whether the market can absorb extra output.

## 9. Answer frames

- **Explain** one internal economy: statement (bulk buying) → expansion (lower price per unit) → expansion (ATC falls, so prices can be cut or profit raised).
- **Analyse** a diseconomy in context: the growth in the case → the problem it causes → effect on ATC → effect on competitiveness or profit.
- **Evaluate** growth: economies gained, diseconomies risked, market size, finance, judgement.

## Quick self-test

1. Give the formula for average total cost.
2. Define internal economies of scale.
3. Total cost is 48,000 at an output of 6,000. Calculate ATC.
4. A firm's fixed costs are 24,000. Calculate fixed cost per unit at 4,000 units and at 8,000 units.
5. Give two types of internal economy of scale.
6. Give one example of an external economy of scale.
7. Why do external economies not give a firm an advantage over local rivals?
8. Give two causes of diseconomies of scale.
9. ATC falls from 12 to 10 and then rises to 11 as output increases. Where do diseconomies begin?
10. A supplier raises its prices for all customers. Is the rise in ATC a diseconomy of scale?
11. Give one limit of growth that is not about cost.
12. Suggest one way a large business could reduce communication problems.

### Answers

1. Total cost ÷ output.
2. Falls in average total cost that result from an increase in the business's own output.
3. 48,000 ÷ 6,000 = 8.00.
4. 24,000 ÷ 4,000 = 6.00; 24,000 ÷ 8,000 = 3.00.
5. Any two of: purchasing, technical, financial, marketing, managerial.
6. Any one of: a skilled labour pool; specialist suppliers nearby; better local infrastructure; a local college training workers.
7. All firms in the area benefit equally from the external factor.
8. Any two of: poor communication; difficulty co-ordinating and controlling; lower motivation.
9. After the output at which ATC was 10, the lowest point.
10. No. It is not caused by the scale of the business.
11. Any one of: market too small; lack of finance; owners want to keep control.
12. Remove layers of management, or delegate decisions to local managers.
