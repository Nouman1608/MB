**These notes condense content area 3.2.1.1 of 9237: "The main religious, political, moral, and social features of Pre-Islamic Arabia, including Jahiliya (Age of ignorance)."** The full explanation is in the [Pre-Islamic Arabia study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-pre-islamic-arabia/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Jahiliya | The Age of Ignorance: ignorance of Allah's guidance, not lack of intelligence |
| Shirk | Associating partners with Allah |
| Hanif | A person who rejected idols and followed the pure faith of Ibrahim (AS) |
| Qabilah | Tribe |
| Sheikh | Tribal chief |
| Quraysh | The tribe that ruled Makkah and guarded the Ka'aba |
| Dar al-Nadwa | The council house of the Quraysh leaders |
| Riba | Usury; interest |
| Asabiyyah | Tribal pride and loyalty |
| Bedouin | Desert-dwelling nomads |

## Four headings

### Religious

- Polytheism: about 360 idols in and around the Ka'aba (traditional figure).
- Main deities: **Hubal** (in the Ka'aba), **Al-Lat** (Ta'if), **Al-Uzza** (near Makkah), **Manat** (near Madinah).
- Belief in Allah as creator, but idols as go-betweens (39:3).
- Omens, fortune-telling, offerings to stones and trees.
- Jews and Christians in oases and the south; a few **hanifs** such as Waraqa ibn Nawfal and Zayd ibn Amr.
- Pilgrimage to Makkah existed but was mixed with idol worship.

### Political

- No central state; loyalty to the **tribe**.
- Chiefs led by persuasion, wisdom and generosity.
- Quraysh ruled Makkah by a council of clan leaders; control of the Ka'aba brought honour and trade.
- Blood feuds and revenge; wars over wells and insults.
- Protection by tribe or by alliance (for example, Hilf al-Fudul).
- Byzantine and Persian empires at the edges.

### Moral

| Admired | Harmful |
|---|---|
| Courage, loyalty, generosity, keeping promises, hospitality | Female infanticide, gambling, drinking, usury, cheating, revenge, tribal pride |

### Social

- Classes: free tribe members, slaves, freed slaves, foreigners.
- Women had little protection or inheritance; unlimited marriages for men; a few exceptions such as Khadija (RA).
- Orphans, widows and the poor were often cheated.
- Wealth held by a few merchant families; caravans to Syria and Yemen (106:1-2).
- Poets were honoured as keepers of the tribe's memory.

## What Jahiliya meant

- It is **ignorance of Allah's guidance**, not stupidity.
- The Arabs had skill in poetry, trade and survival, but no revealed book or moral law.
- Many followed ancestors' habits, tribal pride and desire.
- Islam brought revelation, which Muslims believe replaced ignorance with knowledge.

## Why the message of Islam challenged the old order

| Teaching of Islam | What it challenged |
|---|---|
| One God | Idol worship and income from pilgrims |
| Equality before Allah | Tribal pride, slavery and class |
| Justice and honest trade | Usury and cheating |
| Respect for women and the weak | Infanticide and neglect of orphans |
| Life as sacred | Blood feuds and revenge |

## Using the prescribed passage, Qur'an 17:31

| Words | Use for |
|---|---|
| "do not kill your children" | Ban on infanticide; sanctity of life |
| "for fear of poverty" | The social and economic reason for the practice |
| "We provide for them and for you" | Allah is the provider; trust in Allah |
| "a great sin" | Seriousness of the act; accountability |

**Linked verses:** 81:8-9 (the girl buried alive will be asked for what sin she was killed); 16:58-59 (shame at the birth of a girl).

## Features at a glance: effect on the people

| Feature | Effect |
|---|---|
| Idol worship | Moved people away from one God; gave Makkah income from pilgrims |
| Tribal loyalty | Gave protection, but caused feuds and war |
| Blood revenge | Cycles of killing that lasted for years |
| Usury | The poor fell deeper into debt |
| Infanticide | Loss of innocent life; low value placed on girls |
| Slavery | Slaves had almost no rights |
| Poetry and hospitality | Preserved honour and memory; a positive tradition |

## Three people to know

- **Waraqa ibn Nawfal:** a hanif who studied scripture; later told the Prophet (pbuh) that the angel who came to him was the same one sent to Musa (AS).
- **Zayd ibn Amr:** a hanif who refused to eat meat offered to idols and rejected infanticide.
- **Abdul Muttalib:** chief of the Quraysh and grandfather of the Prophet (pbuh), who guarded the well of Zamzam and the Ka'aba.

## How to build a 4-mark "explain" answer

1. Name the feature (for example, blood feuds).
2. Explain what it was or how it worked.
3. Name a second feature.
4. Explain its effect on people.

**Mini example:** "Society was based on tribes [1]. People had no state to protect them, so they depended on the tribe and could be attacked if they had none [1]."

## Exam traps

- Jahiliya = **ignorance of guidance**, not low intelligence.
- Do not say that **all** Arabs were idol worshippers: there were hanifs, Jews and Christians.
- Keep the **four headings** separate: religious, political, moral, social.
- Hubal was the main idol **in the Ka'aba**.
- Always state the **effect** of a feature, not just the feature.
- Mention good qualities (courage, hospitality) in a balanced answer.

## Self-test

1. What does Jahiliya mean?
2. Name two Arabian deities.
3. What is shirk?
4. Who ruled Makkah?
5. Name the council house of the Quraysh.
6. What was a hanif?
7. Give two harmful moral practices in Pre-Islamic Arabia.
8. Give one way in which women were disadvantaged.
9. Which verse is the prescribed passage for this topic?
10. Why did the Quraysh feel threatened by a call to worship one God?

**Answers:** 1. The Age of Ignorance: ignorance of Allah's guidance. 2. Any two: Hubal, Al-Lat, Al-Uzza, Manat. 3. Associating partners with Allah. 4. The Quraysh. 5. The Dar al-Nadwa. 6. A person who rejected idols and followed the faith of Ibrahim (AS). 7. Any two: infanticide, gambling, drinking, usury, cheating, revenge. 8. Any one: little inheritance; treated as property; no limit on a man's marriages; little protection. 9. Qur'an 17:31. 10. It threatened their income from pilgrims to the idols, their status as guardians of the Ka'aba and the tribal order.
