## 2.2.1 and 2.2.2: definitions and formulae

| Measure | Definition | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| PED | Responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in the good's own price | %ΔQd ÷ %ΔP |
| YED | Responsiveness of demand to a change in income | %ΔQd ÷ %Δincome |
| XED | Responsiveness of demand for A to a change in the price of B | %ΔQd of A ÷ %ΔP of B |

Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.

**Method in steps**

1. Work out the percentage change in quantity (or demand).
2. Work out the percentage change in the other variable: own price, income, or the other good's price.
3. Divide the first by the second, keeping the sign.
4. Name the type of good or the elasticity band, then answer the question asked.

**Reminder.** Price 8 to 10 cu (+25%), quantity 200 to 170 (−15%). PED = −15 ÷ 25 = −0.6, inelastic.

**Reminders for YED and XED.** Income 2,000 to 2,500 cu (+25%) and demand 50 to 57.5 units (+15%): YED = +0.6, a necessity. The price of B falls 5 to 4 cu (−20%) and demand for A rises 400 to 460 (+15%): XED = −0.75, complements. Note the denominators: income for YED, the other good's price for XED.

## 2.2.3: sign and size

| Coefficient | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| PED | Normally negative | Law of demand: price and quantity move opposite ways |
| YED | Positive | Normal good |
| YED | Above +1 | Income elastic ("luxury") |
| YED | Between 0 and +1 | Income inelastic ("necessity") |
| YED | Negative | Inferior good |
| XED | Positive | Substitutes: B's price up, demand for A up |
| XED | Negative | Complements: B's price up, demand for A down |
| XED | Zero | Unrelated |

- Larger size of XED means a closer relationship between the goods.
- Elasticity uses relative (percentage) changes, so a 1 cu rise is large for a 2 cu good and small for a 20 cu good.
- Elasticity has no units.

## 2.2.4: descriptions of PED (using size)

| Term | Size of PED | Curve |
|---|---|---|
| Perfectly inelastic | 0 | Vertical |
| (Highly) inelastic | 0 to 1 | Steep |
| Unitary | 1 | Rectangular hyperbola |
| (Highly) elastic | Above 1 | Shallow |
| Perfectly elastic | Infinity | Horizontal |

**Do not say** "0.9 is elastic". Below 1 is inelastic, even when close to 1.

## 2.2.5: PED along a straight-line demand curve

- Slope is constant. PED changes along the line.
- Point PED = (1 ÷ slope) × (P ÷ Q), where slope = change in P ÷ change in Q.
- High price, low quantity: elastic. Midpoint: unitary. Low price, high quantity: inelastic.
- PED is minus infinity where the line meets the price axis and zero where it meets the quantity axis.
- Total revenue is highest at the midpoint, where PED is unitary.

**Worked reminder.** Demand Q = 60 − P.

| Price | Quantity | Point PED | Total revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 45 | 15 | −3.0 | 675 |
| 30 | 30 | −1.0 | 900 |
| 15 | 45 | −0.33 | 675 |

Here slope in terms of P against Q is −1, so point PED = −P ÷ Q. At P = 45, PED = −45 ÷ 15 = −3.

For a large change, the base matters. State the base you use, or use a small change.

## 2.2.6: factors affecting each elasticity

| Elasticity | Higher (more elastic) when | Lower (more inelastic) when |
|---|---|---|
| PED | Many close substitutes; narrow definition; luxury; large share of income; long time period | Few substitutes; broad definition; necessity or habit; small share of income; short time period |
| YED | Good is a luxury; buyers are on lower incomes who aspire to it | Good is a necessity; buyers already consume plenty |
| XED (size) | Goods are close substitutes or closely tied complements; narrow definitions | Goods are loosely linked; broad definitions |

A good can change character: a basic food may have positive YED at low incomes and negative YED once buyers can afford alternatives.

## 2.2.7: PED and total expenditure (total revenue)

Total expenditure = price × quantity.

| PED | Price rise | Price fall |
|---|---|---|
| Elastic (size above 1) | Falls | Rises |
| Unitary | Unchanged | Unchanged |
| Inelastic (size below 1) | Rises | Falls |

**Reminder.** PED −2.5 and a price rise of 4% means quantity falls by 2.5 × 4 = 10%. Revenue falls, because 1.04 × 0.90 = 0.936 of the original.

**Quick rule.** Compare the two percentages. If quantity moves by more than price, the quantity effect wins.

**Two-step chain for a pricing answer.** First state PED and what it implies for revenue (for example, inelastic demand means a price rise increases revenue). Then qualify it: costs, rivals' reactions, the time period and the objective of the firm.

**Taxes.** A government wanting revenue from an indirect tax prefers a good with inelastic demand, because quantity falls by less. A government wanting to reduce consumption finds the same tax less effective on such a good.

## 2.2.8: implications for decision-making

| Elasticity | Who uses it | How |
|---|---|---|
| PED | Firms | Set price to raise revenue; check cost effects too |
| PED | Governments | Judge the revenue and consumption effects of indirect taxes |
| YED | Firms | Plan output and product range for growth or recession |
| YED | Governments | Forecast demand patterns as income changes |
| XED | Firms | Predict the effect of a rival's price change; price complements together |

**Evaluation points**

- Estimates come from past data and may not hold.
- Ceteris paribus rarely holds: tastes, incomes and other prices change together.
- Short-run and long-run elasticities differ.
- Elasticity varies along a demand curve, so a single value only applies to a price range.
- Revenue is not profit: costs change with output.

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Slope and elasticity | Slope uses absolute changes and is constant on a straight line. Elasticity uses percentage changes and varies along it. |
| Elastic and inelastic | Judge by size, not sign: 1.2 is elastic and 0.9 is inelastic. |
| Substitutes and complements | Positive XED and negative XED respectively. |
| Normal and inferior | Positive YED and negative YED respectively. |
| Income elastic and income inelastic | YED above +1 and between 0 and +1 respectively. |
| Revenue and profit | Revenue is price × quantity. Profit also depends on cost. |

## Quick self-test

1. Write the formula for XED.
2. Price rises from 8 to 10 cu and quantity falls from 200 to 170. Calculate PED and describe demand.
3. Income rises from 2,000 to 2,500 cu and demand rises from 50 to 57.5 units. Calculate YED and classify the good.
4. The price of B falls from 5 to 4 cu and demand for A rises from 400 to 460. Calculate XED and state the relationship.
5. State the sign of YED for an inferior good.
6. PED is −2.5. The price rises 4%. State the percentage change in quantity demanded.
7. Demand is Q = 60 − P. Calculate PED at P = 45 and state the price that maximises revenue.
8. Demand is inelastic. State what happens to total expenditure if price is cut.
9. YED is +1.5 and incomes rise 6%. State the change in demand.
10. State the PED value and curve shape for perfectly inelastic demand.
11. Give two reasons why demand for one brand of toothpaste is more elastic than demand for toothpaste.

### Answers

1. XED = %ΔQd of good A ÷ %ΔP of good B.
2. %ΔP = +25%, %ΔQd = −15%, PED = −0.6. Demand is inelastic.
3. %ΔY = +25%, %ΔQd = +15%, YED = +0.6. Normal good with income inelastic demand (a necessity).
4. %ΔP = −20%, %ΔQd = +15%, XED = −0.75. Complements.
5. Negative.
6. Quantity falls by 10%.
7. PED = −45 ÷ 15 = −3. Revenue is highest at P = 30 (revenue 900), where PED is unitary.
8. Total expenditure falls.
9. Demand rises by 9%.
10. PED = 0. The curve is vertical.
11. Other brands are close substitutes, so buyers can switch if one brand's price rises. A narrow definition means there are more alternatives than for toothpaste as a whole.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the sign, or putting it the wrong way, on YED and XED.
- Using the new value as the base without stating it.
- Calling 0.9 elastic, or 1.2 inelastic.
- Dividing the wrong way round (price change over quantity change).
- Using the price of the same good in XED instead of the other good's price.
- Stating "revenue rises" after a price cut without checking whether demand is elastic.
- Giving factors as a list, without saying which way each factor moves elasticity.
- Ending a decision-making answer with no judgement about the reliability of the estimate.
