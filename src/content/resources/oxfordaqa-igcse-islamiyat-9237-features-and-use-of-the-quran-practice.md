## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for explaining it. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

**1.** 114 [1].

**2.** Madni [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: Tawhid (belief in one God); the Prophethood of Muhammad (pbuh); the Day of Judgement and the Hereafter; stories of earlier prophets; rejection of idol worship; moral character.

**4. (a)** I'jaz [1].

**(b)** One reason identified [1] and explained [2].

- **The challenge** [1]: the Qur'an invites people to produce a surah like it (2:23) [1], and in the Muslim view nobody has done so [1].
- **An unlettered Prophet** [1]: Muhammad (pbuh) did not read or write before revelation [1], so a book of such depth must come from Allah [1].
- **Consistency** [1]: it was revealed over twenty-three years in changing situations [1], yet it contains no contradiction (4:82) [1].
- **Eloquence** [1]: it was unlike poetry or ordinary speech to the Arabs [1], who valued language highly, and many were moved by it [1].

**5.** For each of two differences: difference identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Time** [1]: Makki were revealed before the Hijrah and Madni after it [1].
- **Themes** [1]: Makki surahs stress belief, the Hereafter and earlier prophets, while Madni surahs give rulings on worship, family, trade and the community [1].
- **Style** [1]: Makki verses are short and rhythmic, while Madni verses are longer and more detailed [1].
- **Situation of the Muslims** [1]: Makki surahs addressed a persecuted minority, while Madni surahs addressed a community with a state to run [1].

**6.** For each of two ways: way identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Cleanliness** [1]: many Muslims make wudu before touching the printed Qur'an, based on the verse "none touch it except the purified" (56:79) [1].
- **Careful handling** [1]: it is kept in a clean, high place and not on the floor, because it is the word of Allah [1].
- **Correct recitation** [1]: Muslims learn tajwid so that each letter is pronounced properly (73:4) [1].
- **Listening** [1]: they stay silent and attentive when it is recited (7:204) [1].

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two ways, way identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Ways (AO1):

- **In prayer** [1]: al-Fatiha and other verses are recited in each rak'ah, so worship is built from the Qur'an [1].
- **As a source of law** [1]: rulings on matters such as trade and inheritance must agree with it [1].
- **For guidance** [1]: Muslims consult it for decisions about family, money and conduct [1].
- **For comfort** [1]: verses are recited in times of fear, grief and thanks [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- Qur'an 2:185, which describes the Qur'an as guidance for the people [2].
- Qur'an 7:204: listen and pay attention when it is recited [2].
- The Hadith that the best of you are those who learn the Qur'an and teach it [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Reasons (AO1):

- **It is Allah's word** [1]: Allah does not err, so the book cannot contain mistakes [1].
- **It has been preserved** [1]: it was memorised, written and checked from the Prophet's (pbuh) lifetime [1].
- **No contradictions** [1]: over twenty-three years, it contains no contradictions [1].
- **Allah's promise** [1]: Allah has promised to guard it [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- Qur'an 15:9: "We will be its guardian" [2].
- Qur'an 41:42: "Falsehood cannot approach it from before it or from behind it" [2].
- Qur'an 4:82: if from other than Allah, they would have found much contradiction [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the Qur'an exists to guide; reading and handling it without acting on it misses its purpose; the Prophet (pbuh) is described as a living example of the Qur'an; the verse says it is "a revelation from the Lord of the worlds", which calls for obedience.
- **Against the statement:** outward respect shows reverence and is also a duty; "none touch it except the purified" shows that cleanliness matters; the care Muslims take for the book reminds them of its status; respect in handling teaches children to value it.
- **Balanced view:** both are needed, because respect in handling expresses reverence and living by it completes that reverence.

*Use of the passage*

- "A noble Qur'an" supports respect in treatment.
- "In a Register well-protected" supports its protection and honour.
- "None touch it except the purified" supports cleanliness when handling it.
- "A revelation from the Lord of the worlds" supports obedience to it.

Level 4 answers use the passage directly, give reasoning for both views, and reach a reasoned judgement.
