These notes condense the topic **Economic growth** in Component 02, Macroeconomics, of the OCR *AS Level Economics (H060)* specification, for first assessment in 2016. The topic covers the policy objective, the stages of the economic cycle, real and nominal GDP, growth rates and GDP per capita, short run and long run growth, and their causes and consequences. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [economic growth study guide](/resources/ocr-as-level-economics-h060-economic-growth-ocr-aslevel-economics/). Practise with the [economic growth practice questions](/resources/ocr-as-level-economics-h060-economic-growth-ocr-aslevel-economics-practice/). All figures are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Economic growth | Increase in real output over time, measured by the % change in real GDP |
| GDP | Total value of goods and services produced within a country in a period |
| Nominal GDP | GDP at current prices |
| Real GDP | GDP at constant prices, adjusted for inflation |
| GDP per capita | GDP ÷ population |
| Sustained growth | Steady growth close to the long-run trend |
| Economic cycle | Fluctuations of actual output around its trend |
| Recession | Fall in real GDP, commonly two consecutive quarters of negative growth |
| Short run growth | Rise in actual output using spare capacity |
| Long run growth | Rise in productive capacity (potential output) |
| Trend growth | The average rate at which potential output grows |

## Formulas

| Find | Formula |
|---|---|
| Real GDP | Nominal GDP ÷ price index × 100 |
| Growth rate (%) | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 |
| GDP per capita | GDP ÷ population |
| Per capita growth (approx.) | Real GDP growth − population growth |
| Check | (1 + real growth) × (1 + inflation) = 1 + nominal growth |

## Worked reminder

Nominal GDP rises from 1,250 bn to 1,320 bn and the price index from 100 to 103.

- Real GDP in year 2 = 1,320 ÷ 103 × 100 = **1,281.6 bn**.
- Real growth = 31.6 ÷ 1,250 = **2.52%**, while nominal growth is 5.6%.
- Population rises from 25.0 m to 25.5 m (+2%). Real GDP per capita goes from 50,000 to about **50,257**, growth of only **0.51%**.

Strong population growth can absorb almost all real growth.

## The four stages of the cycle

| Stage | Output | Unemployment | Inflation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boom | Growing above trend | Low and falling | Rising |
| Downturn | Growing more slowly | Starts to rise | Peaks |
| Recession | Falling | Rising | Falling |
| Recovery | Growing again | Falls slowly | Low, starting to rise |

Turning points: **peak** (top of the boom) and **trough** (bottom of the recession). Recovery is complete when output regains its previous peak.

## Diagrams

| | PPC | AD/AS |
|---|---|---|
| Short run growth | Point inside the curve moves towards it | AD shifts right along SRAS |
| Long run growth | Curve shifts outwards | LRAS shifts right |

## Causes

- **Short run:** rises in consumption, investment, government spending or net exports; lower interest rates; lower taxes; a lower exchange rate; rising confidence.
- **Long run:** more labour (population, migration, participation); better skills; more capital and infrastructure; new technology; stable institutions and competition.
- Investment appears in both: it adds to AD now and capacity later.

## Consequences

| | Benefits | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| Short run | Lower unemployment, higher incomes, more tax revenue | Inflation near capacity; more imports |
| Long run | Higher living standards, better public services, less poverty | Pollution, resource depletion, inequality, structural unemployment |

## Evaluation points

- Source matters: supply-led growth lasts; credit-fuelled demand growth may end in recession.
- Pace matters: growth beyond capacity creates inflation.
- Distribution matters: averages hide who gains.
- GDP leaves out unpaid work, leisure, quality and environmental damage.

## Exam traps

- A fall in the growth rate is not a fall in GDP.
- A slowdown is not a recession unless real GDP falls.
- Nominal growth includes inflation; use real GDP.
- Show long run growth with LRAS or an outward PPC shift, not with AD.
- Use per capita figures when the question is about living standards.
- Quote figures from the data with their units.

## Self-test

1. Define economic growth.
2. Distinguish real from nominal GDP.
3. Nominal GDP is 660 bn and the price index is 110 (base year = 100). Calculate real GDP.
4. Real GDP rises from 550 bn to 600 bn. Calculate the growth rate.
5. Nominal GDP rises from 400 bn to 450 bn while the price index rises from 100 to 105. Calculate real growth.
6. GDP is 60 bn and population 1.5 million. Calculate GDP per capita.
7. Real GDP grows 2.4% and population 0.9%. Estimate growth of real GDP per capita.
8. Name the four stages of the economic cycle.
9. Real GDP growth by quarter is 0.4%, −0.2%, −0.3%, 0.1%. When is the economy in recession?
10. How is long run growth shown on a PPC?
11. Give two causes of long run growth.
12. Give one short run cost and one long run cost of growth.

### Answers

1. An increase in the real output of an economy over time.
2. Nominal GDP is at current prices; real GDP removes the effect of price changes.
3. 660 ÷ 110 × 100 = **600 bn**.
4. 50 ÷ 550 × 100 = **9.09%**.
5. Real GDP = 450 ÷ 105 × 100 = 428.6 bn; growth = **7.14%**.
6. 60,000 m ÷ 1.5 m = **40,000** per head.
7. About 2.4 − 0.9 = **1.5%**.
8. Boom, downturn, recession, recovery.
9. In the second and third quarters, the two consecutive quarters of negative growth.
10. As an outward shift of the curve.
11. Any two of: more or better-skilled labour, investment in capital or infrastructure, new technology, better institutions.
12. Short run: inflation or a worsening current account. Long run: pollution, resource depletion, inequality or structural unemployment.
