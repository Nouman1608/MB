These revision notes are for **higher level (HL) only**. Indigenous societies and national identity in Australia and New Zealand (circa 1770 to 2020) is a regional study examined on Paper 3, which standard level students do not sit. They condense the IB *History guide*, first assessment 2028. For the two post-1945 inquiry topics the guide lets you choose either Australia or New Zealand, so revise the one you study. Read the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-indigenous-societies-australia-and-new-zealand/) first and then try the [practice set](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-indigenous-societies-australia-and-new-zealand-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Terra nullius | "Land belonging to no one"; the legal idea used to ignore Aboriginal ownership until 1992 |
| Squatter | A settler who occupied pastoral land without legal title, later leasing it |
| Selection Acts | 1860s laws meant to let small farmers select Crown land |
| Treaty of Waitangi | 1840 agreement between the Crown and Māori chiefs, in English and Māori texts |
| Kāwanatanga | Governance; the Māori-text word for the Crown's authority |
| Tino rangatiratanga | Chiefly authority; guaranteed in the Māori text |
| Whenua | Land, tied to ancestry for Māori |
| Federation | The union of the six Australian colonies as a Commonwealth in 1901 |
| ANZAC | Australian and New Zealand Army Corps |
| Stolen Generations | Indigenous children removed from families by government policy |
| Native title | Indigenous rights in land recognised by Australian law from 1992 and 1993 |

## Colonisation to about 1900

- **Land:** terra nullius in Australia; squatters; Selection Acts with mixed success; confiscation and the Native Land Court in New Zealand.
- **Gold:** population growth, cities, new ideas of rights (Eureka), anti-Chinese feeling.
- **Labour:** eight-hour day, unions, strikes in the 1890s, Labor parties; New Zealand's Liberal reforms and women's vote.
- **Indigenous impact:** disease, land loss, violence, reserves and boards.

## World views and conflict

| Topic | Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander | Māori | Settlers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Land | Country; spiritual and legal duty | Whenua; whakapapa, kaitiakitanga | Private property to farm |
| Authority | Law passed on through kin and community | Chiefs (rangatira) and tribal groups | Crown and the market |
| Conflict | Frontier Wars; massacres such as Myall Creek (1838) | New Zealand Wars; confiscations | Seen as defence of settlement |

## Treaty of Waitangi: key points

- The English text: Māori cede sovereignty to the Crown.
- The Māori text: *kāwanatanga* to the Crown, with *tino rangatiratanga* guaranteed.
- Cause of later disputes: the two texts do not say the same thing.
- Revival: Waitangi Tribunal (1975, wider from 1985) and settlements.

## Emergence of the nations

- **Self-government:** the 1850s in Australian colonies; 1852 for New Zealand.
- **Federation:** Parkes's call in 1889; conventions in 1891 and 1897 to 1898; referendums; 1 January 1901.
- **Exclusion:** the Constitution left Aboriginal people out of the census; White Australia policy from 1901.
- **Identity:** bush legend, Heidelberg School, cricket, rugby, the 1905 Originals.
- **Status:** Australia a dominion in 1901; New Zealand in 1907.

## First World War

- **Gallipoli:** failed campaign, founding myth, ANZAC Day.
- **Losses:** about 60,000 Australian and about 18,000 New Zealand dead.
- **Politics:** two failed referendums in Australia; conscription in New Zealand from 1916.
- **Indigenous soldiers:** barred at first, then enlisted; excluded from settlement land after the war.

## After 1945: Australia

- **Society:** "populate or perish"; end of White Australia; multiculturalism; Medicare; equal pay.
- **Indigenous milestones:** 1962 vote; 1967 referendum; Wave Hill; Tent Embassy; Land Rights Act (1976); Mabo; Native Title Act; Bringing Them Home; apology.
- **Foreign policy:** from Britain to the United States; ANZUS; SEATO; Korea; Vietnam (1962 to 1972); China recognised in 1972; trade with Japan.

## After 1945: New Zealand

- **Society:** welfare state; Pacific and Asian immigration; dawn raids; Māori urbanisation; equal pay (1972).
- **Māori milestones:** land march (1975); Bastion Point; Tribunal; Māori language made official (1987); Ngāi Tahu settlement (1998).
- **Foreign policy:** ANZUS; SEATO; Korea; Vietnam; nuclear-free policy (1984 to 1987); Rainbow Warrior (1985); closer economic relations with Australia (1983).

## Perspectives

- The "Great Australian Silence" (Stanner) and the work of Henry Reynolds against the "history wars" challenge of Windschuttle.
- Belich and Orange on the New Zealand Wars and the Treaty.
- The ANZAC legend as unifying story and as a selective one.

## Worked example: a plan

**Question:** "Evaluate the impact of colonisation on the Indigenous peoples of Australia or New Zealand to about 1900."

1. Thesis: the impact was severe and long-lasting but varied by place and by group.
2. Land and conflict, with named evidence.
3. Demography and disease.
4. Policy: reserves, boards, the Native Land Court.
5. Resistance and adaptation, so that Indigenous people are agents and not only victims.
6. Different perspectives, then a judgement.

## Common mistakes

- Writing about one Indigenous group when the question names several.
- Using "Aborigines" as a single group; use specific peoples where possible.
- Confusing the 1967 referendum with the 1962 right to vote.
- Describing the Treaty of Waitangi as a settled agreement.

## Self-test

1. What was terra nullius?
2. When was the Treaty of Waitangi signed?
3. Which 1854 conflict at Ballarat arose from gold licence fees?
4. What happened on 1 January 1901?
5. What was Gallipoli's importance to national identity?
6. What did the Mabo decision decide?
7. Which treaty bound Australia, New Zealand and the United States?
8. Why did the United States suspend its ANZUS obligations towards New Zealand?

**Answers:** 1. A legal doctrine that Australia belonged to no one before settlement. 2. 6 February 1840. 3. The Eureka Stockade at Ballarat. 4. The Commonwealth of Australia came into being. 5. It became the founding story of shared sacrifice, remembered each year on ANZAC Day. 6. That terra nullius did not apply and native title could exist. 7. ANZUS (1951). 8. New Zealand barred nuclear-armed or nuclear-powered ships from 1984 to 1987.

## Frequently asked questions

**Must I know both countries after 1945?**
No. The guide says "either Australia or New Zealand" for the two post-1945 topics. For the earlier topics you need both.

**How should I write about Indigenous peoples?**
Use specific names where you can, such as Māori or the Meriam people, and show agency and resistance as well as loss.
