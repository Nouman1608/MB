This guide covers subtopic **1.8 Standard form** in Topic 1, Number, of Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024, 2025 to 2027 series. Standard form is a compact way to write very large and very small numbers. The [Number revision notes](/resources/o-level-mathematics-number-revision-notes/) give a brief summary. This page explains the form, shows how to convert in both directions, and works through addition, subtraction, multiplication and division.

Companion pages: the [Standard form revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-standard-form-revision-notes/) and the [Standard form practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-standard-form-practice/).

## What the syllabus asks

The syllabus asks you to:

1. use the standard form A × 10ⁿ, where n is a positive or negative integer and 1 ⩽ A < 10
2. convert numbers into and out of standard form
3. calculate with values in standard form.

Standard form appears on both papers. On Paper 1 you must work by hand. On Paper 2 you may use the calculator's standard form key, but you still need to write the answer correctly.

## The form A × 10ⁿ

A number is in standard form when it is written as **A × 10ⁿ** with two conditions:

- A is at least 1 and less than 10, so it has exactly one non-zero digit before the decimal point
- n is an integer, which may be positive, zero or negative.

| Number | Standard form | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 450 000 | 4.5 × 10⁵ | A = 4.5, decimal point moved 5 places left |
| 6 000 000 000 | 6 × 10⁹ | A = 6, moved 9 places |
| 0.00072 | 7.2 × 10⁻⁴ | A = 7.2, moved 4 places right |
| 83 | 8.3 × 10¹ | A = 8.3, moved 1 place |
| 5 | 5 × 10⁰ | the power 0 is allowed |

The sign of n shows the size of the number. A positive n means the number is 10 or more. A negative n means the number is less than 1.

## Converting into standard form

1. Put the decimal point after the first non-zero digit. That gives A.
2. Count how many places the decimal point has moved. That gives |n|.
3. If the original number is 10 or more, n is positive. If it is below 1, n is negative.

**Worked example.** Write 450 000 in standard form.

```
450 000 = 4.5 x 100 000
        = 4.5 x 10^5
```

**Worked example.** Write 0.00072 in standard form.

```
0.00072 = 7.2 ÷ 10 000
        = 7.2 x 10^-4
```

**Worked example.** Write 6 000 000 000 in standard form.

```
nine places moved:  6 x 10^9
```

### Numbers that look close but are not in standard form

If A is not between 1 and 10, adjust it and change n to compensate.

```
45 x 10^3   →   4.5 x 10 x 10^3   =   4.5 x 10^4
0.62 x 10^5 →   6.2 x 10^-1 x 10^5 =  6.2 x 10^4
```

When you make A ten times smaller, you must make the power one larger. When A becomes ten times larger, the power drops by one.

## Converting out of standard form

Move the decimal point by the number of places given by the power: to the right for a positive power, to the left for a negative one. Fill the gaps with zeros.

```
3.8 x 10^6  = 3 800 000
5.1 x 10^-3 = 0.0051
2.04 x 10^2 = 204
9 x 10^-1   = 0.9
```

## Multiplying and dividing

Treat the numbers in front and the powers of 10 separately.

- **Multiply**: multiply the A values, and add the powers of 10.
- **Divide**: divide the A values, and subtract the powers of 10.

After the calculation, check that the front number is still between 1 and 10. If not, adjust.

**Worked example.** Work out (3 × 10⁴) × (2.5 × 10³).

```
3 x 2.5 = 7.5
10^4 x 10^3 = 10^7
answer: 7.5 x 10^7
```

**Worked example.** Work out (6 × 10⁵) × (5 × 10⁴).

```
6 x 5 = 30
10^5 x 10^4 = 10^9
30 x 10^9 is not standard form
30 x 10^9 = 3 x 10^1 x 10^9 = 3 x 10^10
```

**Worked example.** Work out (9.6 × 10⁷) ÷ (1.2 × 10³).

```
9.6 ÷ 1.2 = 8
10^7 ÷ 10^3 = 10^4
answer: 8 x 10^4
```

**Worked example.** Work out (4.2 × 10⁻³) ÷ (6 × 10⁵).

```
4.2 ÷ 6 = 0.7
10^-3 ÷ 10^5 = 10^(-3 - 5) = 10^-8
0.7 x 10^-8 is not standard form
0.7 x 10^-8 = 7 x 10^-1 x 10^-8 = 7 x 10^-9
```

## Adding and subtracting

The powers of 10 must match before you add or subtract. The easiest approach on Paper 1 is to convert both numbers to ordinary form, calculate, then convert back.

**Worked example.** Work out 3.2 × 10⁵ + 4.5 × 10⁴.

```
3.2 x 10^5 = 320 000
4.5 x 10^4 =  45 000
sum        = 365 000 = 3.65 x 10^5
```

Alternatively, rewrite 4.5 × 10⁴ as 0.45 × 10⁵, then add the front numbers: 3.2 + 0.45 = 3.65.

**Worked example.** Work out 6.1 × 10⁻³ - 2.4 × 10⁻⁴.

```
6.1 x 10^-3 = 0.0061
2.4 x 10^-4 = 0.00024
difference  = 0.00586 = 5.86 x 10^-3
```

Never add the powers when adding the numbers. Adding 10⁵ and 10⁴ does not give 10⁹.

## Powers and roots in standard form

For a power, raise A to the power and multiply the indices of 10. For a square root, take the root of A and halve the index of 10, making the index even first if needed.

```
(2.5 x 10^6)^2 = 2.5^2 x 10^12 = 6.25 x 10^12
√(1.6 x 10^7) = √(16 x 10^6) = 4 x 10^3
```

## Standard form in context

**Worked example.** Light travels at 3 × 10⁸ m/s. The Sun is 1.5 × 10¹¹ m from Earth. How long does the light take to arrive?

```
time = distance ÷ speed = 1.5 x 10^11 ÷ 3 x 10^8
     = 0.5 x 10^3
     = 5 x 10^2 seconds
```

The light takes 500 seconds, which is just over 8 minutes.

**Worked example.** A dust particle has mass 2.5 × 10⁻⁶ g. How many particles make 1 g?

```
1 ÷ (2.5 x 10^-6) = 0.4 x 10^6 = 4 x 10^5
```

**Worked example.** Write the number of seconds in 365 days in standard form.

```
365 x 24 x 3600 = 31 536 000
= 3.1536 x 10^7   (3.15 x 10^7 to 3 significant figures)
```

## Using a calculator (Paper 2)

A scientific calculator has a key for entering the power of 10, often labelled EXP or ×10ˣ. Use it to enter 6.7 × 10⁸ ÷ 3.2 × 10⁻⁵ as a single line, using brackets round the denominator.

```
6.7 x 10^8 ÷ (3.2 x 10^-5) = 2.09375 x 10^13
to 3 significant figures: 2.09 x 10^13
```

Write the answer in full standard form. Do not copy the calculator display "2.09E13" or "2.09 13", which is not acceptable notation.

## Exam technique

- State the answer in the form A × 10ⁿ with A between 1 and 10. An answer such as 30 × 10⁹ loses the final mark.
- Show the product or quotient of the front numbers, and the combined power of 10, as separate lines.
- When a question says "give your answer in standard form", do the conversion even if the working was already in standard form.
- If asked for 3 significant figures, round A, not the whole number.

## Common mistakes

- Using the wrong sign of n: 0.0051 is 5.1 × 10⁻³, not 5.1 × 10³.
- Counting places wrongly when there are many zeros.
- Leaving A outside the range 1 to 10, such as 0.7 × 10⁻⁸ or 30 × 10⁹.
- Adding the powers when adding the numbers.
- Subtracting the indices in the wrong order when dividing, or ignoring negative signs.
- Writing a calculator display (2.09E13) instead of 2.09 × 10¹³.
- Rounding too early.

## Where this leads

Standard form is used throughout science, in estimation and limits of accuracy, and in compound change. It also builds on the index rules from Indices I, because multiplying and dividing powers of 10 follows the same laws.
