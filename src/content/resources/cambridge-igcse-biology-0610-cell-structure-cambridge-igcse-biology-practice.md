## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which structure is found in plant cells, animal cells **and** bacterial cells? **[1]**

A: Nucleus

B: Chloroplast

C: Ribosome

D: Large permanent vacuole

**2.** State the function of each structure.

**(a)** Mitochondria **[1]**

**(b)** Chloroplasts **[1]**

**(c)** The cell wall of a plant cell **[1]**

**3.** State how new cells are produced. **[1]**

**4.** State the function of a ciliated cell and of a red blood cell. **[2]**

**5.** State what plant cell walls are made of, and **one** way in which a bacterial cell wall differs. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** A student describes a micrograph of a cell. Structure K is a thick outer layer with a box-like shape. Structure L is a group of small green oval bodies near the edge. Structure M is a large clear area in the centre. Structure N is a dark, round body pressed to one side.

**(a)** Identify structures K, L, M and N. **[4]**

**(b)** State whether the cell came from a leaf or a root, and give a reason. **[2]**

**7.** A student writes: "All plant cells contain chloroplasts." Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**8.** This question is about a bacterial cell.

**(a)** Name the small rings of DNA that carry extra genes. **[1]**

**(b)** State where the main genetic material of the cell is found. **[1]**

**(c)** State **two** structures found in a bacterial cell that are also found in an animal cell. **[2]**

**9.** Explain how the structure of a root hair cell is related to its function. **[3]**

**10.** Explain how a red blood cell is suited to its function. **[2]**

**11.** State whether each is a cell, a tissue, an organ, an organ system or an organism.

**(a)** A sperm **[1]**

**(b)** A leaf **[1]**

**(c)** The layer of ciliated cells lining the trachea **[1]**

**(d)** The nervous system **[1]**

**(e)** An oak tree **[1]**

**12.** Compare the structure of a palisade mesophyll cell with the structure of a bacterial cell. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. Bacteria have no nucleus, animal cells have no chloroplasts, and only plant cells have a large permanent vacuole. **[1]**

**2. (a)** Where aerobic respiration takes place, releasing energy [1].

**(b)** Absorb light energy for photosynthesis [1].

**(c)** Strengthens the cell, keeps its shape or stops it bursting [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* "Mitochondria make energy" does not score; energy is released by respiration.

**3.** By the division of existing cells [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Ciliated cell: movement of mucus in the trachea and bronchi [1]. Red blood cell: transport of oxygen [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Cellulose [1]. The bacterial cell wall is not made of cellulose [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** K: cell wall [1]. L: chloroplasts [1]. M: vacuole [1]. N: nucleus [1].

**(b)** A leaf [1], because the cell has chloroplasts, which need light; root cells are underground and have none [1]. **[6]**

**7.** Some plant cells, such as root cells, have no chloroplasts [1], because they are not exposed to light and do not photosynthesise [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** Plasmids [1].

**(b)** In a loop of circular DNA, free in the cytoplasm [1].

**(c)** Any two: cell membrane [1]; cytoplasm [1]; ribosomes. **[4]**

**9.** The root hair cell's function is absorption of water and mineral ions [1]. It has a long, thin extension (the root hair) [1], which gives a large surface area, so more water and ions can be absorbed [1]. **[3]**

**10.** It contains haemoglobin, which carries oxygen [1]. It has no nucleus, so there is more space for haemoglobin [1]. **[2]**

**11. (a)** Cell [1].

**(b)** Organ [1].

**(c)** Tissue [1].

**(d)** Organ system [1].

**(e)** Organism [1]. **[5]**

**12.** Any five comparison points, for example:

- Both have a cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm and ribosomes [1].
- The palisade cell has a nucleus, whereas the bacterial cell has no nucleus [1].
- The bacterial cell has circular DNA free in the cytoplasm and plasmids, whereas the palisade cell does not [1].
- The palisade cell has many chloroplasts, but the bacterial cell has none [1].
- The palisade cell wall is made of cellulose, but the bacterial cell wall is not [1].
- The palisade cell has a large permanent vacuole and mitochondria; the bacterial cell has neither (alternative point for the same marks). **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Each comparison point must mention both cells. A list of the palisade cell's parts followed by a list of the bacterial cell's parts earns little credit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying energy is "made" in mitochondria.
- Giving bacteria a nucleus, or a cellulose cell wall.
- Loose wording for specialised-cell functions.
- Describing two cells separately when the question says **compare**.
- Confusing a tissue (similar cells) with an organ (several tissues).

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know the features of specialised cells?**
The syllabus requires their functions; knowing a feature that helps each one lets you answer "explain how this cell is suited" questions.

**Are there Extended questions on cell structure?**
No. All of sub-topic 2.1 is Core in Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610.
