These questions cover subtopic **3.2 Market research** of Cambridge O Level Business Studies 7115 (2026 series). All businesses and figures are invented. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question.

Related: the [Market research study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-market-research/) and the [Market research revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-market-research-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define primary research. **[2]**

**2.** Identify **two** methods of secondary research. **[2]**

**3.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of using an online survey. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why businesses use a sample when carrying out market research. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** factors that can affect the accuracy of market research data. **[2]**

**6.** In a survey of 600 people, 255 said they would try a new breakfast cereal. Calculate the percentage who said they would try it. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** Lakeshore Ice Cream is a small business planning to launch a new flavour in its two shops.

**(a)** Explain **two** benefits to Lakeshore of being a market-orientated business.

**(b)** Recommend **one** method of primary research Lakeshore should use. Justify your answer. **[10]**

**8.** A drinks company surveyed 200 customers about the price they would pay for a new smoothie.

| Price customers would pay | Number of customers |
|---|---|
| Under $2.00 | 90 |
| $2.00 to $3.00 | 70 |
| Over $3.00 | 40 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of customers in each price group.

**(b)** The company plans to sell the smoothie at $3.50. Use the data to draw a conclusion about this plan. **[6]**

**9.** Pulse Fitness is planning to open a gym in a town where it has never operated. Consider whether Pulse Fitness should rely only on secondary research before deciding to open. Justify your answer. **[6]**

**10.** **(a)** Explain the difference between an open question and a closed question.

**(b)** A phone company's questionnaire asks: "Wouldn't you agree our new phone is the best on the market?" Identify the problem with this question and suggest a better version. **[4]**

**11.** A clothing shop is planning a new range aimed at teenagers. It asked 20 customers in the shop on a weekday morning whether they would buy the range. Explain **two** reasons why the results may not be accurate. **[4]**

**12.** Explain **one** benefit and **one** limitation of using a focus group for a toy manufacturer testing a new board game. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Collecting new information that has not been collected before [1], by or for the business for its own specific purpose [1].

**2.** Any two [1] [1]: internet or competitors' websites; government statistics; commercial market research reports; the business's own sales records; newspapers or trade magazines.

**3.** One benefit identified [1] and explained [1]: it is cheap to send to many people, so a large sample can be reached at low cost; or results are collected and analysed automatically, saving time.

**4.** Asking everyone in the target market would take too long and cost too much [1], so a smaller group that represents the market is asked instead [1].

**5.** Any two [1] [1]: sample size; whether the sample represents the market; leading or unclear questions; dishonest answers; interviewer influence; low response rate; out-of-date secondary data.

**6.** 255 ÷ 600 × 100 [1] = **42.5%** [1].

**7.** **(a)** Two benefits, each identified [1] and explained [1]: Lakeshore learns which flavours customers want before making them, so it is less likely to waste ingredients on a flavour that does not sell; it can set a price customers are willing to pay; satisfying customers' wishes builds loyalty in its two shops; it can react to what local competitors offer.

**(b)** Taste-testing with a short questionnaire in its two shops [1]: customers can try free samples and rate them [1], giving direct reactions to the actual flavour [1]. It is cheap for a small business because staff can run it during normal opening hours [1]. A limitation is that only existing customers are asked, so the views of potential new customers are missed [1]. An online survey would reach more people but cannot let them taste the ice cream, so in-shop taste tests are more useful for a new flavour [1].

**8.** **(a)** Under $2.00: 90 ÷ 200 × 100 = **45%** [1]. $2.00 to $3.00: 70 ÷ 200 × 100 = **35%** [1]. Over $3.00: 40 ÷ 200 × 100 = **20%** [1].

**(b)** Only 20% would pay over $3.00, so at $3.50 the smoothie is likely to appeal to a small part of the market [1]. 80% want to pay $3.00 or less, so the company should consider a lower price or a smaller bottle [1]. However, the sample is only 200 customers, and people often say they would pay less than they actually would, so further research or a trial at $3.50 would help [1].

**9.** Secondary research such as government population figures and competitors' websites is quick and cheap [1] and shows the size of the town, the age of its population and how many gyms already exist [1]. However, it may be out of date [1] and will not show what local people want from a gym, such as opening times or prices [1]. Primary research, for example a survey of local residents, would give this specific information but costs more [1]. Judgement: Pulse should not rely only on secondary research; it should use it first to check the market size, then use primary research to find out what local customers want before committing to a large investment [1].

**10.** **(a)** An open question lets the respondent answer in their own words, giving reasons [1]; a closed question gives fixed answers to choose from, which are easy to count [1].

**(b)** It is a leading question that pushes the respondent to agree [1]. A better version: "How would you rate our new phone compared with others you have used? Much better / Better / The same / Worse / Much worse" [1].

**11.** Two reasons, each identified [1] and explained [1]: the sample of 20 is very small, so it may not reflect the views of all teenagers; on a weekday morning most teenagers are at school, so the people asked are probably not in the target market; only existing customers in the shop were asked, missing teenagers who shop elsewhere.

**12.** Benefit identified [1] and explained [1]: players can try the game and discuss it in depth, revealing what they enjoy and what confuses them. Limitation identified [1] and explained [1]: a focus group has only a few people, so their views may not represent all families who buy board games; or one confident person may influence the others.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a research method in question 7 without linking it to the new flavour and the small business.
- Repeating the percentages in question 8 without a conclusion about the $3.50 price.
- One-sided answers to question 9 that do not weigh primary research.
- Failing to explain why the sample in question 11 is unrepresentative, rather than just "too small".
- Defining secondary research as "free".
