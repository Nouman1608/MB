This set covers one content point from key development 3.1.4.1: "social and cultural developments up
to 1658, including religion, literature, education, music and architecture". It is part of the
optional topic examined in Paper 1 Section C, which uses short and extended response questions; the
multiple-choice items below are recall drills. The questions do not repeat those in the
[Mughal Empire practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-pakistan-studies-9236-mughal-empire-practice/).
Learn the content from the [Mughal culture to 1658 study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-pakistan-studies-9236-mughal-culture-to-1658/).

## Section A — short-answer questions

**1.** In which year did Akbar build the Ibadat Khana? **[1]**

A: 1556 &nbsp; B: 1564 &nbsp; C: 1575 &nbsp; D: 1605

**2.** Who wrote the *Ain-i-Akbari*? **[1]**

A: Badauni &nbsp; B: Abul Fazl &nbsp; C: Faizi &nbsp; D: Gulbadan Begum

**3.** In which city is the Wazir Khan Mosque? **[1]**

A: Delhi &nbsp; B: Agra &nbsp; C: Lahore &nbsp; D: Thatta

**4.** Which emperor's reign is associated with the painter Ustad Mansur? **[1]**

A: Babur &nbsp; B: Humayun &nbsp; C: Akbar &nbsp; D: Jahangir

**5.** State **two** features of Mughal architecture under Shah Jahan. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** ways in which Akbar encouraged learning and literature. **[2]**

**7.** State **two** ways in which Akbar won the support of his Hindu subjects. **[2]**

## Section B — extended questions

**8.** Identify and explain **two** ways in which Persian culture influenced the Mughal court before

**9.** Identify and explain **two** reasons why Mughal emperors spent large sums on buildings. **[4]**

**10.** Analyse **two** ways in which Akbar's religious policies affected the Mughal Empire. **[6]**

**11.** Analyse **two** ways in which Mughal cultural developments before 1658 shaped the cities of

## Answers

Multiple-choice questions score 1 mark. "State" questions score 1 mark per valid point. "Identify
and explain" questions score 1 mark for each point identified and 1 for its explanation. The 6- and
9-mark questions are marked by levels; the level descriptions follow the answers. Credit other
accurate points.

**1.** **C: 1575** [1], at Fatehpur Sikri. **[1]**

**2.** **B: Abul Fazl** [1], who also wrote the *Akbarnama*. **[1]**

**3.** **C: Lahore** [1]. It was built in Shah Jahan's reign by his governor, Wazir Khan. **[1]**

**4.** **D: Jahangir** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Two features, 1 mark each: wide use of white marble [1]; inlay of coloured stones (*pietra
dura*) [1]. Also credit: symmetry; large gardens; domes and minarets; tile work, as in Lahore and
Thatta. **[2]**

**6.** Two ways, 1 mark each: he set up a workshop to translate Sanskrit works such as the
*Mahabharata* into Persian [1]; he widened the curriculum to include subjects such as arithmetic,
astronomy and medicine [1]. Also credit: patronage of historians such as Abul Fazl and poets such
as Faizi; a large imperial library. **[2]**

**7.** Two ways, 1 mark each: he abolished the jizya (1564) [1]; he married Rajput princesses and gave
Rajput nobles high rank [1]. Also credit: abolition of the pilgrim tax; inviting Hindu scholars to the
Ibadat Khana; translations of Hindu texts. **[2]**

**8.** Two ways, each identified (1) and explained (1):
- Persian was the language of the court and government [1], so official histories, letters and
  poetry were written in Persian and Persian-speaking scholars were valued [1];
- Humayun brought Persian painters to India after his exile [1], and their style became the basis of
  the Mughal painting workshops [1].

Also credit: Persian garden design (the charbagh); Persian architectural forms such as the double
dome. **[4]**

**9.** Two reasons, each identified (1) and explained (1):
- to display power and wealth [1], since great forts, mosques and tombs impressed subjects and
  rivals with the emperor's greatness [1];
- to show piety and commemorate family [1], for example tombs such as the Taj Mahal and mosques such
  as the Jama Masjid in Delhi [1].

Also credit: new capitals and forts for government and defence, such as Fatehpur Sikri and the Red
Fort. **[4]**

**10.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- *Loyalty of Hindus and Rajputs:* abolishing the jizya and marrying into Rajput families gave the
  empire capable Rajput commanders and reduced rebellion.
- *Cultural blending:* debates at the Ibadat Khana and translations encouraged exchange between
  traditions, seen in art and literature.
- *Strengthened imperial authority:* the Mahzar made the emperor the judge of religious disputes.
- *Opposition:* orthodox scholars, and later Ahmad Sirhindi, criticised the policies as a departure
  from the Sharia, creating tension that later rulers responded to. **[6]**

**11.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- *Lahore:* Akbar rebuilt Lahore Fort; Shah Jahan added the Sheesh Mahal and the Shalimar Gardens;
  the Wazir Khan Mosque was built in his reign; these remain landmarks of the city.
- *Tombs and gardens:* Jahangir's tomb at Shahdara shows the garden-tomb style in Pakistan.
- *Sindh:* the Shah Jahan Mosque in Thatta shows Mughal patronage reaching the lower Indus.
- *Wider culture:* Persian vocabulary and literary forms influenced the development of Urdu, and the
  musical traditions of the court shaped later classical music. **[6]**

**12.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Indicative content:
- *Agree:* Humayun's Tomb, Fatehpur Sikri and the Taj Mahal combined Persian and Indian forms on a
  monumental scale; buildings in Lahore and Thatta show the reach of Mughal patronage; architecture
  has had the most lasting visible impact.
- *Disagree:* literature such as the *Akbarnama* and *Baburnama* records the period in detail;
  translations brought Sanskrit works into Persian; painting reached great refinement under Jahangir;
  Tansen's music and the religious debates under Akbar were influential.
- *Judgement:* for example, architecture was the most visible achievement, but literature and
  translation did more to blend traditions of thought. **[9]**

**13.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Indicative content:
- *Strengthened:* Rajput alliances provided loyal commanders; abolishing the jizya won Hindu
  support; toleration reduced rebellion in a mainly non-Muslim empire; the Mahzar strengthened
  imperial authority.
- *Weakened:* orthodox ulema opposed the policies; Din-i-Ilahi attracted few followers and was
  criticised; reaction against Akbar's approach, voiced by Ahmad Sirhindi, influenced later rulers.
- *Judgement:* for example, the policy strengthened the empire in Akbar's lifetime by securing loyalty,
  while its weaknesses showed only in the longer-term reaction. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing buildings without explaining what they show.
- Overstating Din-i-Ilahi as a mass religion.
- Including Aurangzeb's buildings in a question about culture up to 1658.
- In 9-mark answers, considering only architecture or only religion.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need dates for buildings?**
A few help: the Ibadat Khana (1575) and the Taj Mahal (begun 1632). For most buildings, the reign is
enough.

**Is painting part of this content point?**
Painting is not named separately, but it links to literature (illustrated histories) and to court
culture, so it is useful evidence.
