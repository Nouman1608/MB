These notes condense the first content point of section 3.2 of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Accounting (9215) specification: "Verification of the double entry records". The specification lists four verification techniques: trial balance, trade receivables ledger control accounts, trade payables ledger control accounts and bank reconciliation statements. All amounts are in dollars.

For full explanations and worked examples, use the [verification of the double entry records study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-verification-of-the-double-entry-records-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting/). To practise, go to the [verification practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-verification-of-the-double-entry-records-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Verification | Checking records by comparing them with another, independently produced record |
| Trial balance | List of all ledger balances in debit and credit columns at one date |
| Control account | A summary account built from totals in the books of prime entry, compared with the total of a ledger's individual balances |
| Memorandum record | A record kept for checking only, outside the double entry; 9215 control accounts are memorandum records |
| Receivables ledger | The individual accounts of credit customers |
| Payables ledger | The individual accounts of credit suppliers |
| Bank statement | The bank's record of the business's account, sent to the business |
| Bank reconciliation statement | A statement explaining the difference between the cash book balance and the bank statement balance |

## The four techniques: what is compared

```
TRIAL BALANCE
  total debit balances   vs   total credit balances

RECEIVABLES LEDGER CONTROL ACCOUNT
  totals from sales journal, sales returns journal,
  cash book and general journal              vs   list of customer balances

PAYABLES LEDGER CONTROL ACCOUNT
  totals from purchases journal, purchases returns journal,
  cash book and general journal              vs   list of supplier balances

BANK RECONCILIATION STATEMENT
  cash book (bank columns)   vs   bank statement
```

Agreement supports the records. A difference proves there is an error (or, for the bank, a timing difference still to be explained).

## What each one is good at

| Technique | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Trial balance | Quick check on the whole ledger; reveals addition, partial omission, transposition and unequal posting | Says an error exists, not where; misses commission, complete reversal, compensating, omission, original entry and principle |
| Receivables control account | Locates errors to the customer accounts; a total of what customers owe is quickly available | Misses errors that leave the total unchanged, such as a posting to the wrong customer |
| Payables control account | Locates errors to the supplier accounts | Same limitation, for suppliers |
| Bank reconciliation | Compares with an outside record; finds bank items missing from the cash book and wrong amounts entered on both sides | Checks the bank columns only |

## Which technique reveals which error?

Ask: **which two records would now disagree?**

| Error | Revealed by |
|---|---|
| Direct debit on the bank statement not in the cash book | Bank reconciliation |
| Cheque paid recorded at the wrong amount in both the cash book and the supplier's account | Bank reconciliation |
| Credit purchase posted to the supplier's account at the wrong amount (journal correct) | Trial balance, and the payables control account locates it |
| Expense account overcast | Trial balance |
| Receipt from customer A posted to customer B's account | None of the four |
| Transaction never recorded at all | None of the four |
| Asset debited to an expense account | None of the four |

## Small reminders

**Control account difference.** Receivables control account balance 18,350; list of customer balances 18,170. Difference 180: look in the receivables ledger for a posting 180 too small (or a balance missed off the list).

**Bank check.** Cash book 1,640; bank statement shows a direct debit of 120 not in the cash book. The updated cash book balance is 1,520, and that figure is then reconciled with the statement.

## Exam traps

- "The trial balance agrees, so there are no errors." It proves only that the totals match.
- Calling a 9215 control account part of the double entry. It is a memorandum record.
- Saying a control account finds every error in customer accounts. A posting to the wrong customer leaves the total unchanged.
- Saying a bank reconciliation checks the whole ledger.
- Naming the technique without giving the reason, when the question asks you to explain.
- Forgetting that some errors are revealed by none of the four techniques.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the four verification techniques listed in the 9215 specification.
2. What does a trial balance compare?
3. What is a memorandum record?
4. Which books of prime entry supply the totals for a receivables ledger control account?
5. Why can a bank reconciliation find errors that a trial balance cannot?
6. A trial balance does not agree. What extra information can a control account give?
7. Bank charges of 30 were not entered in the cash book. Which technique reveals this?
8. A credit sale of 900 was posted to the wrong customer's account. Which technique reveals this?
9. Sales of 2,450 were posted to a customer's account as 2,540; the sales journal was correct. Which techniques reveal this?
10. Give one error that none of the four techniques reveals.
11. The payables control account balance is 9,800 and the list of supplier balances totals 9,650. What does this show?
12. Why should the four techniques be used together rather than relying on one?

### Answers

1. **Trial balance**, **trade receivables ledger control accounts**, **trade payables ledger control accounts**, **bank reconciliation statements**.
2. The **total of the debit balances** with the **total of the credit balances**.
3. A record kept **outside the double entry**, for checking only.
4. The **sales journal**, **sales returns journal**, **cash book** and **general journal**.
5. The bank statement is produced **independently by the bank**, so it shows items missing from, or wrongly valued in, the cash book.
6. It shows **which ledger** (receivables or payables) contains the error.
7. **Bank reconciliation.**
8. **None** of the four: the totals do not change.
9. The **trial balance** (debits 90 too high) and the **receivables control account**, which locates it.
10. For example, an **error of omission**, **commission** or **principle**.
11. There is an **error of 150** in the payables ledger or in the control account; the supplier accounts should be checked.
12. **Each one checks different records** and misses different errors.
