## Section 3.1.8 at a glance

1. **Calculate and interpret** each financial ratio and measure the specification names.
2. **Appraise performance** under four headings: profitability, liquidity, efficiency, capital structure.
3. **Explain cash versus profit**, and the effect of a transaction on profitability and liquidity.
4. **State limitations** of financial statements and ratio analysis, financial and non-financial.

## Formula table

OxfordAQA's guide *Formulae for accounting ratios* says students will be required to know these formulae.

| Ratio | Answer as | Formula | A rise usually means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gross profit margin | per cent | Gross profit / Revenue × 100 | Better pricing or cheaper buying |
| Markup | per cent | Gross profit / Cost of sales × 100 | Higher price added to cost |
| Profit in relation to revenue | per cent | Profit for year / Revenue × 100 | More of each sale kept as profit |
| Expenses in relation to revenue | per cent | Expenses / Revenue × 100 | Weaker overhead control (a rise is bad) |
| ROCE (sole trader) | per cent | Profit for year / (Capital + non-current liabilities) × 100 | Long-term funds working harder |
| ROCE (limited company) | per cent | Profit from operations / (Equity + non-current liabilities) × 100 | As above |
| Current ratio | x : 1 | Current assets / Current liabilities | More short-term cover, or idle resources |
| Liquid capital ratio | x : 1 | (Current assets − closing inventory) / Current liabilities | Better cover without selling inventory |
| Rate of inventory turnover | times per year | Cost of sales / Average inventory | Goods sold more quickly |
| Rate of inventory turnover (days) | days | Average inventory / Cost of sales × 365 | Inventory held longer (a rise is bad) |
| Trade receivable days | days | Trade receivables / Credit sales × 365 | Customers paying more slowly |
| Trade payable days | days | Trade payables / Credit purchases × 365 | Taking longer to pay suppliers |
| Capital gearing | per cent | Non-current liabilities / total of (Issued share capital + Reserves + Non-current liabilities) × 100 | More reliance on borrowing |

The OxfordAQA guide lists the days version of inventory turnover; the times version above is the same pair of figures inverted. Average inventory = (opening + closing) / 2. For ROCE, either the opening or the closing capital figure may be used. A sole trader has no share capital, so gearing uses the owner's capital as the equity figure.

## Method in steps

**Calculating a ratio**

1. State the formula.
2. Put in the figures, choosing credit sales or credit purchases where the formula needs them.
3. Give the answer in the right form: %, x : 1, times or days.
4. Round sensibly (two decimal places for % and ratios, one for days) and keep the same rounding for both years.

**Writing an appraisal**

1. Group the ratios under profitability, liquidity, efficiency and capital structure.
2. For each group, state the direction of change with the figures.
3. Suggest a reason, using clues given in the scenario.
4. Say what it means for the business or for a named user.
5. Link groups (slow customers explain falling liquidity).
6. Finish with a judgement, qualified by a limitation.

## Which ratios matter to whom

Aim your appraisal at the reader named in the question.

| User | Ratios they watch most | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Owner | Profit in relation to revenue, ROCE | Is the money tied up in the business earning enough? |
| Bank or other lender | Capital gearing, current ratio, liquid capital ratio | Can the business repay, and how much does it already owe? |
| Trade supplier | Liquid capital ratio, trade payable days | Will invoices be paid, and how quickly? |
| Manager | Expenses in relation to revenue, inventory and receivable days | Where can costs or working capital be tightened? |

A good answer picks the two or three ratios that fit the user, rather than discussing every one.

## Small worked reminders

**Margin and markup convert.** Markup = margin / (100 − margin) × 100. A margin of 40% is a markup of 40 / 60 × 100 = 66.67%. A markup of 50% is a margin of 50 / 150 × 100 = 33.33%.

**Times to days.** Days = 365 / times. Turnover of 10 times is 36.5 days.

**Profit ratios link.** Gross profit margin − expenses in relation to revenue = profit in relation to revenue.

**Opening or closing capital.** Either is allowed for ROCE. Say which and use the same choice for both years.

## Cash versus profit

Profit follows the accruals concept; the bank balance follows money in and out. They differ because of:

- credit sales and credit purchases (counted before cash moves);
- changes in inventory (cash spent on unsold goods);
- depreciation and doubtful debt provisions (expenses with no cash);
- buying or selling non-current assets;
- capital introduced, drawings, loans received and repaid.

**Effect of a transaction: quick rules**

- Swapping one current asset for another (a customer settles a debt) changes neither total, so the current ratio stays put.
- Buying inventory with cash keeps total current assets the same but cuts the liquid capital ratio.
- Paying a current liability from cash raises a ratio that was above 1 : 1 and lowers one that was below 1 : 1.
- Selling inventory at a profit raises profit and both liquidity ratios.
- Depreciation lowers profit and leaves both liquidity ratios unchanged.
- A long-term loan received raises both liquidity ratios with no effect on profit.

## Limitations

**Financial:** historic figures; no inflation adjustment; different accounting policies between firms; year-end snapshot may be untypical; estimates such as depreciation; missing data such as the credit sales split.

**Non-financial:** staff skills and morale; management quality; reputation and customer loyalty; market and competitor changes; environmental and social impact.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Margin vs markup:** margin divides by revenue; markup divides by cost of sales.
- **Current vs liquid capital ratio:** the liquid ratio removes closing inventory.
- **Sole trader vs company ROCE:** profit for year with capital, against profit from operations with equity.
- **Profit vs cash:** profit is earned and incurred; cash is received and paid.
- **Receivable vs payable days:** credit sales against credit purchases.
- **Liquidity vs efficiency:** liquidity is the ability to pay short-term debts; efficiency is how quickly inventory, receivables and payables turn over.

## Quick self-test

1. Gross profit is 45,000 and cost of sales is 150,000. Calculate markup and gross profit margin.
2. Convert a markup of 25% to a gross profit margin.
3. Current assets 48,000 (inventory 20,000), current liabilities 32,000. Calculate both liquidity ratios.
4. Average inventory 15,000, cost of sales 135,000. Find inventory turnover as times, then as days.
5. Gross profit margin is 32% and profit in relation to revenue is 7%. Find expenses in relation to revenue.
6. Does a depreciation charge reduce the bank balance?
7. Which profit figure is used for a limited company's ROCE?
8. Non-current liabilities 60,000, issued share capital 150,000, reserves 90,000. Calculate capital gearing.
9. Trade payables 16,200, credit purchases 197,100. Calculate trade payable days.
10. Name two non-financial limitations of ratio analysis.
11. Profit for the year 27,000; owner's capital 165,000; long-term loan 15,000. Find ROCE for this sole trader.

### Answers

1. Markup = 45,000 / 150,000 × 100 = **30.00%**. Revenue = 195,000, so margin = 45,000 / 195,000 × 100 = **23.08%**.
2. 25 / 125 × 100 = **20%**.
3. Current = 48,000 / 32,000 = **1.5 : 1**. Liquid = 28,000 / 32,000 = **0.88 : 1**.
4. 135,000 / 15,000 = **9 times**; 15,000 / 135,000 × 365 = **40.6 days**.
5. 32 − 7 = **25%**.
6. **No.** It reduces profit; no money leaves the business.
7. **Profit from operations.**
8. 60,000 / 300,000 × 100 = **20.00%**.
9. 16,200 / 197,100 × 365 = **30.0 days**.
10. Any two, for example **staff morale** and **reputation with customers**.
11. 27,000 / 180,000 × 100 = **15.00%**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by revenue for markup, or by cost of sales for margin.
- Using total revenue for receivable days when a credit sales figure is given.
- Using closing inventory instead of average inventory for turnover.
- Adding loan interest back to sole trader profit, which the OxfordAQA formula does not do.
- Using profit for the year, rather than profit from operations, in company ROCE.
- Expressing the current ratio as a percentage, or leaving off ": 1".
- Describing a change ("it fell") without a cause or a consequence.
- Treating a higher current ratio as automatically good when inventory or receivables have swollen.
- Listing limitations without linking them to the business in the question.
- Saying depreciation or a provision for doubtful debts uses cash.
