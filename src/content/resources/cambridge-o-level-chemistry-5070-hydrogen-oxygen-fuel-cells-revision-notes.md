Condensed recall for subtopic 4.2 of Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070. For full explanations and a model six-mark answer, use the [fuel cells study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-hydrogen-oxygen-fuel-cells/); to practise, try the [fuel cells practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-hydrogen-oxygen-fuel-cells-practice/).

## The two outcomes

1. **State** that a hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell uses hydrogen and oxygen to produce electricity with **water as the only chemical product**.
2. **Describe** the advantages and disadvantages of fuel cells **in comparison with** gasoline / petrol engines in vehicles.

## Core facts

- Overall equation: **2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l)**
- Hydrogen is fed to one electrode; oxygen (from air) to the other.
- Chemical energy is converted **directly** into electrical energy; there is no combustion and no flame.
- The cell runs for as long as hydrogen and oxygen are supplied; it is not recharged like a battery.
- Hydrogen loses electrons (oxidised); oxygen gains electrons (reduced). The electrons flow through the external circuit, forming the current.
- The overall reaction is exothermic.
- The fuel cell reverses the electrolysis of water: electrolysis makes H₂ and O₂ in a 2 : 1 volume ratio; the fuel cell uses them in the same ratio.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fuel cell | Device converting chemical energy of a fuel and oxygen directly into electricity |
| Fuel | Substance that releases energy when it reacts with oxygen; here hydrogen |
| Combustion | Burning; reaction of a fuel with oxygen releasing thermal energy, usually with a flame |
| Complete combustion | Burning in plenty of oxygen; hydrocarbons give CO₂ and H₂O |
| Incomplete combustion | Burning in limited oxygen; produces CO and/or carbon (soot) |
| Petrol (gasoline) | Fraction of petroleum, a mixture of hydrocarbons, used as fuel in cars |
| Non-renewable | A resource that cannot be replaced once used, such as petroleum |

## What each system releases from the vehicle

| Substance | Petrol engine | Fuel cell | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water | Yes | Yes (only product) | Harmless |
| Carbon dioxide | Yes | No | Greenhouse gas; climate change |
| Carbon monoxide | Yes (incomplete combustion) | No | Toxic |
| Oxides of nitrogen | Yes (N₂ and O₂ react at high temperature) | No | Acid rain, photochemical smog, respiratory problems |
| Unburned hydrocarbons and particulates | Yes | No | Air pollution |

## Advantages of fuel cells (compared with petrol)

- Water is the only product: no CO₂, CO or oxides of nitrogen from the vehicle.
- Hydrogen does not depend on petroleum, which is non-renewable.
- More efficient: direct conversion to electricity wastes less energy as heat.
- Fewer moving parts; quieter.

## Disadvantages of fuel cells (compared with petrol)

- Hydrogen is a gas: hard to store; needs high-pressure, heavy tanks or very low temperatures. Petrol is an easily stored liquid.
- Hydrogen is highly flammable; leaks can form explosive mixtures with air.
- Most hydrogen is made from methane, releasing CO₂; electrolysis of water needs electricity that may come from fossil fuels.
- Few hydrogen refuelling stations.
- Fuel cells are expensive to make.

## Useful numbers from subtopic 3.3

- 1 mol H₂ = 2 g and occupies 24 dm³ at r.t.p.
- 2 mol H₂ + 1 mol O₂ → 2 mol H₂O, so 4 g H₂ gives 36 g H₂O.
- 1.00 kg of hydrogen = 500 mol = 12 m³ at r.t.p., which shows why the gas must be compressed.

## How marks are earned on the six-mark question

- One mark per distinct, specific point; aim for at least three advantages and three disadvantages.
- Name the substance: "no carbon monoxide, which is toxic", not "no harmful gases".
- Make each point a comparison: "hydrogen is harder to store than petrol because it is a gas".
- Balance both sides; answers with only advantages lose marks.

## Exam traps

- "No pollution at all" is wrong: producing hydrogen can release CO₂. Say "no pollutants **from the vehicle**".
- The hydrogen is not burned; there is no flame in a fuel cell.
- A fuel cell cannot produce CO₂: neither reactant contains carbon.
- Water is the only **chemical** product; electricity is not a chemical product.
- "Cheaper" is not an accepted advantage: fuel cells are expensive.
- "Hydrogen is renewable" needs a condition: only if made from water using renewable electricity.
- Petrol engines release oxides of nitrogen because of the high temperature, not because petrol contains nitrogen.

## Self-test

1. State the only chemical product of a hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell.
2. Write the balanced overall equation, with state symbols.
3. Which reactant is oxidised in a fuel cell?
4. Name **two** gases released by a petrol engine that are not released by a fuel cell, and give a problem caused by each.
5. Explain why oxides of nitrogen form in a petrol engine.
6. Give **one** reason why hydrogen is harder to store in a vehicle than petrol.
7. Why might using a fuel-cell car still lead to carbon dioxide emissions?
8. How does a fuel cell differ from a rechargeable battery?
9. What mass of water is produced when 10 g of hydrogen reacts in a fuel cell?
10. What volume of oxygen at r.t.p. reacts with 48 dm³ of hydrogen?
11. Give **one** safety disadvantage of hydrogen as a vehicle fuel.
12. Give **one** advantage of petrol over hydrogen apart from storage.

**Answers**

1. Water.
2. 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l).
3. Hydrogen (it loses electrons).
4. Any two: carbon dioxide (climate change); carbon monoxide (toxic); oxides of nitrogen (acid rain / photochemical smog).
5. Nitrogen and oxygen from the air react at the high temperature inside the engine.
6. It is a gas, so it must be compressed to high pressure in strong, heavy tanks (or liquefied at very low temperature).
7. Most hydrogen is made from methane, which releases CO₂, or by electrolysis using electricity from fossil fuels.
8. It is not recharged; it produces electricity continuously while hydrogen and oxygen are supplied.
9. 10 g H₂ = 5 mol, giving 5 mol H₂O = 90 g.
10. Ratio H₂ : O₂ = 2 : 1, so 24 dm³.
11. Hydrogen is highly flammable / forms explosive mixtures with air if it leaks.
12. There are many refuelling stations already / the technology is cheaper and well established.
