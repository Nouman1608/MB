## Section A

**1.** Give the structure of the species present when alanine is dissolved in a solution of pH 1.
**[1]**

**2.** State the meaning of the term amphoteric, and explain why amino acids are amphoteric. **[2]**

**3.** Give the structural formula of the dipeptide formed when the –COOH group of alanine reacts
with the –NH₂ group of serine. **[1]**

**4.** State the number of peptide bonds in a tripeptide. **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** Serine is an amino acid with an –OH group in its side chain.

**(a)** Give the structure of the zwitterion of serine. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the structure of the main serine species at pH 12. **[1]**

**(c)** Write the equation for serine reacting with excess NaOH(aq). **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why the side-chain –OH group does not react with NaOH(aq). **[1]**

**6.** Lysine has a second amine group in its side chain.

**(a)** Give the structure of lysine at pH 1 and state its overall charge. **[1]**

**(b)** State the overall charge on lysine at pH 7. **[1]**

**(c)** Give the structure of lysine at pH 13. **[1]**

**(d)** Suggest why the isoelectric point of lysine is above 7. **[1]**

**7.** A mixture of alanine (isoelectric point 6.0), aspartic acid (isoelectric point 2.8) and lysine
(isoelectric point 9.7) undergoes electrophoresis in a buffer at pH 2.8. At this pH lysine carries a
larger positive charge than alanine. Predict the behaviour of each amino acid and identify which
travels furthest. **[4]**

**8.** In electrophoresis at pH 6.0, an unknown amino acid **X** moves towards the anode.

**(a)** State the sign of the overall charge on **X** at pH 6.0. **[1]**

**(b)** State what this shows about the isoelectric point of **X**. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest a feature of the R group of **X**. **[1]**

**9.** A mixture of glycine, alanine and the dipeptide Gly–Ala is placed in a buffer at pH 10, and
each species carries an overall charge of −1.

**(a)** State the electrode towards which all three move. **[1]**

**(b)** Predict the order in which they are found, from furthest travelled to least, and explain your
order. **[2]**

**10.** Peptides are formed from mixtures of amino acids.

**(a)** State the number of different dipeptides that could form from a mixture of glycine, alanine
and serine. **[1]**

**(b)** State the number of different tripeptides that could be formed using only glycine and
alanine, if each may be used more than once. **[1]**

**(c)** Give the structural formula of the tripeptide Gly–Ala–Gly. **[1]**

**11.** The dipeptide H₂NCH(CH₃)CONHCH(CH₂OH)COOH is heated under reflux with HCl(aq) until hydrolysis
is complete. Give the structures of the two organic products as they exist in the acidic solution.
**[2]**

**12.** Relative molecular masses of peptides can be found from those of their amino acids.

**(a)** Calculate the Mᵣ of glycine and of alanine. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the Mᵣ of the dipeptide Gly–Ala. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the Mᵣ of the tripeptide Gly–Gly–Gly. **[1]**

**13.** In an electrophoresis experiment at an unknown buffer pH, glycine (isoelectric point 6.0)
stays at the origin and aspartic acid (isoelectric point 2.8) moves towards the anode.

**(a)** Deduce the pH of the buffer. **[1]**

**(b)** Predict the movement of lysine (isoelectric point 9.7) in the same experiment, with a reason.
**[1]**

## Answers

**1.** ⁺H₃NCH(CH₃)COOH [1].

**2.** Amphoteric: able to react as both an acid and a base [1]; amino acids contain a basic –NH₂
group and an acidic –COOH group [1].

**3.** H₂NCH(CH₃)CONHCH(CH₂OH)COOH [1].

**4.** Two [1].

**5. (a)** ⁺H₃NCH(CH₂OH)COO⁻ [1].

**(b)** H₂NCH(CH₂OH)COO⁻ [1].

**(c)** H₂NCH(CH₂OH)COOH + NaOH → H₂NCH(CH₂OH)COO⁻Na⁺ + H₂O [1].

**(d)** It is an alcohol group, which is too weakly acidic to react with aqueous NaOH [1].

**6. (a)** ⁺H₃NCH((CH₂)₄NH₃⁺)COOH, overall charge +2 (both needed) [1].

**(b)** +1 [1].

**(c)** H₂NCH((CH₂)₄NH₂)COO⁻ [1].

**(d)** The extra basic –NH₂ group means alkali must be added to remove H⁺ from the side chain before
the molecule has no overall charge [1].

**7.** Aspartic acid is at its isoelectric point, has no overall charge and stays at the origin [1];
alanine is below its isoelectric point, is positive and moves towards the cathode [1]; lysine is
also positive and moves towards the cathode [1]; lysine travels furthest because it carries the
larger charge [1].

**8. (a)** Negative [1].

**(b)** Its isoelectric point is below 6.0 [1].

**(c)** The R group contains an extra acidic group such as –COOH [1].

**9. (a)** The anode (positive electrode) [1].

**(b)** Glycine > alanine > Gly–Ala [1]; with equal charge, the species with the smaller mass moves
further: glycine (Mᵣ 75) is lighter than alanine (Mᵣ 89), and the dipeptide is the heaviest [1].

**10. (a)** Nine (3 × 3) [1].

**(b)** Eight (2 × 2 × 2) [1].

**(c)** H₂NCH₂CONHCH(CH₃)CONHCH₂COOH [1].

**11.** ⁺H₃NCH(CH₃)COOH [1]; ⁺H₃NCH(CH₂OH)COOH [1] (accept as chloride salts).

**12. (a)** Glycine C₂H₅NO₂ = 75.0 and alanine C₃H₇NO₂ = 89.0 (both needed) [1].

**(b)** 75.0 + 89.0 − 18.0 = 146.0 [1].

**(c)** 3 × 75.0 − 2 × 18.0 = 189.0 [1].

**13. (a)** pH 6.0, since glycine has no overall charge only at its isoelectric point [1].

**(b)** pH 6.0 is below lysine's isoelectric point, so lysine is positive and moves towards the
cathode [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the uncharged H₂NCH(R)COOH when the question asks for the species at a given pH.
- Forgetting that a charged side chain changes the overall charge: lysine is +2 at pH 1 and +1 at
  pH 7.
- Sending positive species to the anode.
- Ignoring mass when two species carry the same charge.
- Counting Gly–Ala and Ala–Gly as one dipeptide, or forgetting Gly–Gly and Ala–Ala.
- Subtracting only one water when calculating the Mᵣ of a tripeptide: two peptide bonds means two
  molecules of water lost.
- Giving the free amino acids as hydrolysis products in acid, when they exist as cations.
