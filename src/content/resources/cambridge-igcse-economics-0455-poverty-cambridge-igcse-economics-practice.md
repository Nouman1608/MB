These questions cover section 5.2 (poverty) of the Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455 syllabus for examination in 2026. All households, countries and figures are invented. Revise first with the [poverty study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-poverty-cambridge-igcse-economics/) and the [poverty revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-poverty-cambridge-igcse-economics-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Which of the following describes relative poverty? **[1]**

A: Not having enough food to stay healthy

B: Having income well below the typical income in one's society

C: Having no income at all

D: Being unemployed for more than a year

**2.** Which policy is most likely to raise the income of a retired person with no pension? **[1]**

A: A national minimum wage

B: A cut in the top rate of income tax

C: A more generous state pension

D: More spending on secondary schools

**3.** Explain the difference between absolute and relative poverty. **[2]**

**4.** A basic basket of necessities costs 1,500 per person a year. A household of 6 has an income of 8,400. Calculate how far its income is above or below the absolute poverty line. **[2]**

**5.** Seven households earn: 2,500; 3,000; 6,000; 9,000; 14,000; 21,000; 40,000. Calculate the relative poverty line at 50% of median income, and the percentage of households below it. **[3]**

**6.** Explain why low wages can cause poverty even for a person in full-time work. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** In Country D (invented) income tax is 0% on income up to 12,000 and 25% on income above 12,000.

**(a)** Calculate the tax paid by a person earning 52,000 and the proportion of income paid in tax. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the tax paid by a person earning 16,000 and the proportion of income paid in tax. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why this tax is described as progressive. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how illness and age can each cause poverty. **[4]**

**9.** A government raises the national minimum wage from 6 to 7.50 an hour.

**(a)** Calculate the weekly increase in pay for a worker who keeps a 40-hour job. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain two reasons why this may not reduce poverty overall. **[4]**

**10.** Analyse how improved education can reduce poverty. **[4]**

**11.** Country E's real GDP grows by 7% a year, yet the percentage of households in relative poverty rises. Explain how this can happen. **[3]**

**12.** Discuss whether more generous state benefits are the best way to reduce poverty. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. A describes absolute poverty; C and D are not definitions of poverty. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. A retired person does not earn wages, so a minimum wage and education do not help; a tax cut helps those who pay the top rate. **[1]**

**3.** Absolute poverty is not having enough income to afford basic necessities such as food and shelter [1]. Relative poverty is having income well below that of most people in the same society, so the line moves as typical incomes change [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Needs = 6 × 1,500 = 9,000 [1]; 8,400 − 9,000 = **900 below the line** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Median = 9,000 (4th of 7 values) [1]; line = 0.5 × 9,000 = 4,500 [1]; 2 of 7 households are below it, so **28.6%** [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Pay per hour may be so low that even full-time earnings fall short of what the household needs [1], especially if the worker supports dependants such as children, so total income is below the poverty line [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 0.25 × (52,000 − 12,000) = **10,000** [1]; 10,000 ÷ 52,000 × 100 = **19.2%** [1].

**(b)** 0.25 × (16,000 − 12,000) = **1,000** [1]; 1,000 ÷ 16,000 × 100 = **6.25%** [1].

**(c)** The higher earner pays a higher proportion of income in tax (19.2% against 6.25%) [1], because the first 12,000 is untaxed, so the average rate rises as income rises [1]. **[6]**

**8.** Illness: a sick person may work fewer hours or lose their job [1], so income falls just as treatment costs raise spending [1]. Age: elderly people no longer earn and may have little pension or savings [1]; children cannot earn, so large families with many dependants need more income from the same earners [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** (7.50 − 6) × 40 = **60** a week [1].

**(b)** Reason 1: many poor people are unemployed, sick or retired [1], so they receive no wage and gain nothing [1]. Reason 2: if the new minimum is well above the market wage, firms may hire fewer workers [1], and those who lose their jobs may fall into poverty [1]. **[5]**

**10.** Education raises skills [1], which raises productivity [1], so workers can get better-paid jobs and are less likely to be unemployed [1]. Higher earnings lift households above the poverty line, and educated parents are more likely to keep their own children in school, reducing poverty in the next generation [1]. **[4]**

**11.** The gains from growth may go mainly to higher-income households [1]. This raises median income and so the relative poverty line [1]. If the incomes of the poorest rise more slowly than the line, more households fall below it [1]. **[3]**

**12.** For: benefits raise the income of poor households immediately [1]; they can be targeted at groups who cannot work, such as the sick and the elderly, whom other policies miss [1]; they reduce absolute poverty directly [1]. Against: they are costly, so taxes or borrowing must rise or other spending be cut [1]; if withdrawn sharply as earnings rise, they may reduce the incentive to work [1]; they treat the symptom, low income, rather than causes such as low skills [1]; education or growth may give longer-lasting gains [1]. Judgement: benefits are the best short-term policy for those unable to work, but for working-age poor people, education and job creation are needed for a lasting reduction, so a mix is best [1]. **[8]**

*Examiner insight:* A top answer names which poor groups each policy reaches. A judgement that says "it depends" without saying on what does not earn the final mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the mean instead of the median for a relative poverty line.
- Forgetting to multiply by household size for an absolute line.
- Saying progressive tax means "the rich pay more" rather than "a higher proportion".
- Assuming a minimum wage reaches people who are not working.
- Ending a "Discuss" answer without a judgement.
