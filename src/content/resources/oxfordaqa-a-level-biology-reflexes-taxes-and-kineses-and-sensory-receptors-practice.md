## Questions

**1.** A person treads on a sharp stone and the foot lifts before they feel pain. Name, in order, the three neurones in this reflex arc, and explain why the response protects the body. **[4]**

**2.** In a reflex the impulse pathway is 1.40 m long and impulses travel at 56 m s⁻¹. There are two synapses, and each adds a delay of 0.9 ms.

**(a)** Calculate the time from receptor to effector in milliseconds. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest why a response needing a decision by the brain would take longer. **[1]**

**3.** Brine shrimp larvae were placed in a horizontal tube with a lamp at one end. The tube was divided into five equal sections, A (nearest the lamp) to E. After 10 minutes the counts were: A 25, B 15, C 10, D 6, E 4.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of larvae in sections A and B together. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the type of response shown, and say whether it is positive or negative. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how this type of response increases the chance of survival. **[1]**

**4.** A small invertebrate was timed for 60 s in an arena of humid air and then in dry air. Data supplied: humid air, 138 mm travelled and 9 turns; dry air, 420 mm travelled and 34 turns.

**(a)** Calculate the mean speed in each condition in mm s⁻¹. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why these data show a kinesis, not a taxis. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how this behaviour keeps the animal in humid air. **[2]**

**5.** Describe how pressure applied to a Pacinian corpuscle leads to an action potential in the sensory neurone. **[5]**

**6.** The resting potential of a Pacinian corpuscle is −70 mV and the threshold is −55 mV. The table gives data supplied.

| Pressure (kPa) | Membrane potential (mV) | Impulses per second |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | −66 | 0 |
| 2.0 | −58 | 0 |
| 3.0 | −52 | 12 |
| 4.0 | −48 | 26 |

**(a)** Calculate the depolarisation at 3.0 kPa. **[1]**
**(b)** Between which two pressures is the threshold reached? **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why there are no impulses at 2.0 kPa. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the percentage increase in impulse frequency from 3.0 to 4.0 kPa. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why a Pacinian corpuscle responds to pressure but not to light. **[2]**

**8.** In dim light each rod produces a generator potential of 0.5 mV. The threshold of the bipolar neurone it connects to is 2.5 mV.

**(a)** Calculate the minimum number of rods needed to reach the threshold. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why a single cone connected to its own bipolar neurone would not trigger an impulse in dim light. **[3]**

**9.** One region of a retina has 210 rods connected to 7 bipolar neurones. In another region each cone connects to one bipolar neurone.

**(a)** Calculate the mean number of rods per bipolar neurone. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how the difference in connections leads to a difference in visual acuity. **[3]**

**10.** The table gives relative absorbance of the three types of cone at two wavelengths (data supplied).

| Wavelength (nm) | Short-wavelength cone | Medium-wavelength cone | Long-wavelength cone |
|---|---|---|---|
| 540 | 0.04 | 0.95 | 0.62 |
| 600 | 0.00 | 0.20 | 0.80 |

**(a)** State which cone type is stimulated most at 540 nm. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the ratio of long-wavelength to medium-wavelength absorbance at each wavelength, to two significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the brain uses these data to tell the two lights apart, and why rods cannot do this. **[2]**

**11.** The centre of a human retina has 150 000 cones per mm² and few rods. A region at the edge has 6 000 cones per mm² and many rods.

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater the cone density is at the centre. **[1]**
**(b)** A faint star is easier to see when you look slightly to one side of it. Explain why. **[4]**
**(c)** Explain why you look directly at a word to read it. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain why the star appears colourless. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Sensory neurone [1], relay neurone [1], motor neurone [1]. The pathway is short with few synapses, so the response is fast and automatic, and the foot is lifted before the damage increases [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "In order" matters, so a list of three neurone names in the wrong order is unlikely to gain full credit. A reason for protection needs to say why the reflex is fast, not just that it is.

**2. (a)** Conduction time = 1.40 ÷ 56 [1] = 0.025 s = 25.0 ms. Synaptic delay = 2 × 0.9 = 1.8 ms [1]. Total = 25.0 + 1.8 = **26.8 ms** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The impulse must pass through the brain, which adds more neurones and synapses (each with a delay) [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each stage and the unit. A final answer in seconds, or with the synaptic delay left out, is the usual slip.

**3. (a)** A + B = 25 + 15 = 40 [1]; 40 ÷ 60 × 100 = **66.7%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Positive phototaxis [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The larvae move to the light, so they are more likely to reach the lit surface water, where their algal food is found [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the word "phototaxis" with the sign (positive or negative). "Moves to light" alone describes the behaviour without naming it.

**4. (a)** Humid: 138 ÷ 60 = **2.3 mm s⁻¹** [1]. Dry: 420 ÷ 60 = **7.0 mm s⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The speed and the number of turns both change with the humidity [1], and there is no movement in a particular direction relative to the stimulus [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** In dry air the animal moves fast and turns often, so it is likely to leave by chance [1]. In humid air it moves slowly and turns less, so it tends to stay [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers about a kinesis gain credit by tying speed and turning to stimulus intensity. Writing "moves towards humidity" describes a taxis.

**5.** Pressure deforms the lamellae around the sensory neurone ending [1]. The membrane of the ending is stretched [1], so stretch-mediated sodium ion channels widen [1]. Sodium ions diffuse in, depolarising the membrane to give a generator potential [1]. If the generator potential reaches the threshold, an action potential is set up [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A clear sequence from deformation to channel to ion movement reads well. Mention the threshold, because it is the step that separates a generator potential from an action potential.

**6. (a)** −52 − (−70) = **18 mV** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Between **2.0 and 3.0 kPa** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** At 2.0 kPa the potential is −58 mV, a depolarisation of 12 mV [1], which is less than the 15 mV needed to reach the threshold at −55 mV, so no action potential is set up [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** (26 − 12) ÷ 12 × 100 [1] = **117%** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote the numbers when you compare a value with the threshold. Percentage change is worked out from the starting value (12), not the final value.

**7.** The corpuscle's membrane contains stretch-mediated sodium ion channels that respond to deformation [1]. It contains nothing that is changed by light, so light produces no generator potential [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Both halves are needed: what the receptor does respond to and why light has no effect.

**8. (a)** 2.5 ÷ 0.5 = **5 rods** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Cones need a higher light intensity to change their pigment, so they give a small or no generator potential in dim light [1]. A single cone connects to its own bipolar neurone, so there is no summation with other cells [1]. One cone alone cannot reach the threshold [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Rod sensitivity answers earn credit when they mention both the pigment and summation. Pigment alone gives half the explanation.

**9. (a)** 210 ÷ 7 = **30 rods** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Many rods share a bipolar neurone, so two points of light on different rods in the same group send a single set of impulses [1], and the brain sees one point [1]. Each cone has its own neurone, so two stimulated cones give two sets of impulses and the points are seen apart [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Acuity answers should compare both photoreceptors. Describing cones only leaves the contrast incomplete.

**10. (a)** The **medium-wavelength cone** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 540 nm: 0.62 ÷ 0.95 = **0.65** [1]. 600 nm: 0.80 ÷ 0.20 = **4.0** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The brain compares the relative stimulation of the three cone types, which differs between the two wavelengths [1]. Rods contain only one pigment, so there is no comparison [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "The cones detect different colours" is too vague. The brain compares the relative response of the types.

**11. (a)** 150 000 ÷ 6 000 = **25 times** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** A faint star gives dim light [1]. Looking to one side puts its image on the edge, where rods are dense [1]. Many rods share a neurone, so their generator potentials add up by summation [1] and reach the threshold, which the few cones at the centre could not do [1]. **[4]**
**(c)** The centre of the retina has a high density of cones [1], each connected to its own bipolar neurone [1], so there is high acuity and the letters are seen separately [1]. **[3]**
**(d)** Only rods respond in dim light [1], and they have one pigment, so the brain cannot compare different types [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In long explanations, link each feature to its result. The strongest answers use the words summation, separate connections and the density of the photoreceptor.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming neurones in the wrong order, or leaving out the relay neurone.
- Saying a kinesis is steered towards or away from the stimulus.
- Giving a reflex-time answer in the wrong unit, or forgetting the synaptic delay.
- Writing that sodium ions are pumped in, rather than diffusing through opened channels.
- Treating a generator potential as an action potential.
- Calculating percentage increase using the final value as the base.
- Explaining sensitivity without summation, or acuity without separate connections.
- Stating that cones "see colour" without the idea that the brain compares the three types.
