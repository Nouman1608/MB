Cambridge O Level English Language (1123) develops two connected abilities: reading texts closely enough to understand, summarise and respond to them, and writing clearly and effectively for a given purpose and reader. It is best thought of as a skills course: what is assessed is how well you read and write, rather than how much content you can recall. The 2024-2026 syllabus is assessed by two equally weighted papers, Paper 1 Reading and Paper 2 Writing, each lasting 120 minutes, each marked out of 50 and each worth 50% of the qualification.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus has two areas of study, and each corresponds to one paper.

**Reading (Paper 1)**

Paper 1 is based on two reading texts and has two compulsory sections:

- **Comprehension and Use of Language**, which tests how well you understand a text and how well you can explain the way language is used in it.
- **Summary and Short response**, which tests whether you can select the relevant points from a text, express them concisely in your own words, and respond briefly to what you have read.

**Writing (Paper 2)**

Paper 2 has two tasks:

- **Directed Writing**, a single question in which you write for a specified purpose, audience and form, using material provided.
- **Composition**, where you choose one task from four options and write a longer piece.

Everything you study is in service of these skills: reading accurately and efficiently, and writing with control of structure, vocabulary, grammar and tone.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Reading | 120 minutes | 50 | 50% | Two compulsory sections based on two reading texts |
| Paper 2 | Writing | 120 minutes | 50 | 50% | One Directed Writing question and one Composition task from four options |

- **No coursework and no speaking component.** The two written papers are the whole qualification, and both are externally assessed.
- **Not tiered.** All candidates take the same papers, and grades run from A* to E.
- **Linear.** Both papers are sat in the same examination series.
- **Series.** The syllabus is available in the June and November series.

The 2024-2026 syllabus was first assessed in 2024 and has its final examination in 2026. The version described here is Version 2. If you are sitting the examination after 2026, check the syllabus Cambridge publishes for your year.

## How the parts fit together

Reading and Writing are tested separately, but they are really two sides of one skill set.

- **Summary and Directed Writing are close relatives.** Both ask you to work from given material, pick out what matters and present it for a particular purpose. Practising one improves the other.
- **Use of Language feeds Composition.** When you analyse how a writer creates an effect in Paper 1, you are collecting techniques you can use yourself in Paper 2.
- **Accuracy counts everywhere.** Clear sentences, sound punctuation and well-chosen vocabulary help you in a summary just as much as in a story or an argument.
- **Reading for gist and for detail.** Both papers reward reading the question and any source material carefully before you start writing.

A habit that serves the whole course is to read regularly and actively, noticing how writers open and close pieces, vary sentence length and address their reader, and then trying those choices in your own work.

## Planning your study

With only two papers, a balanced plan keeps both in motion at once.

**Opening phase.** Build reading stamina and vocabulary. Practise short comprehension questions and begin learning how to identify the main points of a passage. On the writing side, work on paragraphing, sentence variety and accurate punctuation.

**Middle phase.** Introduce full summary tasks and practise expressing ideas in your own words. Begin Directed Writing, paying attention to how the purpose and audience in the question should shape the tone and layout of your answer. Try a range of Composition types so that you know which of the four options you are likely to handle best.

**Final phase.** Work on complete papers under timed conditions, review your marked work for recurring errors, and refine the approach that suits you for each section. The course's exam-preparation page sets out how to approach each paper; use it alongside the past papers page once you reach this phase.

Throughout, keep a personal list of the mistakes you make most often, such as comma splices or lifting phrases straight from the text in a summary, and check it before each practice piece.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge O Level English Language 1123?**
Two: Paper 1 Reading and Paper 2 Writing. Each lasts 120 minutes, is marked out of 50 and is worth 50%.

**What are the sections of Paper 1 Reading?**
Two compulsory sections based on two reading texts: Comprehension and Use of Language, and Summary and Short response.

**Is there a choice of questions in Paper 2 Writing?**
The Directed Writing question is set, but for the Composition you choose one task from four options.

**Does O Level English Language 1123 have coursework or a speaking test?**
No. The qualification is assessed only by the two externally assessed written papers.

**When can I sit O Level English Language 1123?**
The syllabus is available in the June and November series. This page describes the 2024-2026 syllabus, whose final examination year is 2026.
