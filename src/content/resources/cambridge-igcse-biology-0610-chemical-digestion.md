This guide covers sub-topic **7.4 Chemical digestion** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Statements 1 to 5 are Core and can be tested on every paper. Statements 6 to 8 (maltose and maltase, pepsin and trypsin, and bile as a neutraliser) are Supplement content, so they are **Extended only** (Papers 2 and 4). Rates of enzyme-catalysed reactions are a named experimental context for Paper 5 and Paper 6.

The [human nutrition study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-human-nutrition/) lists the statements. This page teaches them through the journey of each nutrient, with a full "secreted where, acts where" table, the reasons behind the pH conditions, and worked examples on enzyme data. It builds on the [enzymes study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-enzymes/). Condense it with the [chemical digestion revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-chemical-digestion-revision-notes/) and test yourself with the [chemical digestion practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-chemical-digestion-practice/).

## Why food must be chemically digested

Starch, proteins and fats are **large, insoluble** molecules. They cannot pass through the wall of the small intestine into the blood. Chemical digestion uses enzymes to break them into **small, soluble** molecules that **can be absorbed**. Physical digestion only prepares the food by increasing its surface area; it cannot make a molecule small enough to absorb.

## The three enzyme groups (Core)

| Enzyme | Substrate | Products |
|---|---|---|
| Amylase | starch | simple reducing sugars |
| Proteases | protein | amino acids |
| Lipase | fats and oils | fatty acids and glycerol |

These reactions are the reverse of the building-up reactions in the [biological molecules study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-biological-molecules/), and you can use the food tests to show them. If amylase digests starch, the iodine test becomes negative and the Benedict's test becomes positive.

## Secreted where, acts where (Core)

The organ that **secretes** an enzyme is not always the place where it **acts**: pancreatic enzymes are made in the pancreas but work in the small intestine.

| Enzyme | Secreted by | Acts in |
|---|---|---|
| Amylase | salivary glands | mouth |
| Amylase | pancreas | small intestine (duodenum) |
| Protease | stomach (in gastric juice) | stomach |
| Protease | pancreas | small intestine (duodenum) |
| Lipase | pancreas | small intestine (duodenum) |

Notice the pancreas: it is the only organ that secretes all three, which is why damage to the pancreas affects the digestion of every food group.

## Hydrochloric acid in gastric juice (Core)

The stomach secretes hydrochloric acid as part of gastric juice. The syllabus limits its functions to two:

1. It **kills harmful microorganisms** in food, reducing the chance of infection.
2. It provides an **acidic pH** that is the **optimum** for the protease that works in the stomach.

A consequence you can use in "suggest" questions: salivary amylase works best in near-neutral conditions, so it stops working once food is mixed with acid in the stomach.

## Starch digestion in detail (Extended only)

At Extended level, "simple reducing sugars" is split into two steps:

```
starch  --amylase-->  maltose  --maltase-->  glucose
```

- **Amylase** (from the salivary glands and pancreas) breaks starch down to **maltose**.
- **Maltase** breaks maltose down to **glucose**. It is located **on the membranes of the epithelium lining the small intestine**, so this final step happens at the surface of the cells that will absorb the glucose.

## Protein digestion in detail (Extended only)

| Protease | Where it works | pH conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Pepsin | stomach | acidic (hydrochloric acid) |
| Trypsin | small intestine | alkaline |

Each protease has an optimum pH that matches its region. This links to topic 5: away from its optimum pH an enzyme's active site changes shape, the substrate no longer fits, and at extreme pH the enzyme is denatured.

## Bile as a neutraliser (Extended only)

Food leaving the stomach is acidic because it is mixed with gastric juice. Enzymes in the small intestine, such as trypsin, work best in alkaline conditions. **Bile is an alkaline mixture**: when it enters the duodenum it **neutralises** the acid, providing a **suitable pH** for the enzymes there.

Bile therefore has two Extended roles. In physical digestion it emulsifies fats (7.3); in chemical digestion it adjusts the pH. Neither role involves an enzyme, because bile contains none.

## Worked example 1: following a protein (Extended)

**Describe what happens to a protein in a piece of fish from the stomach to absorption.** **[5]**

> In the stomach, pepsin starts to digest the protein [1] in the acidic conditions produced by hydrochloric acid [1]. In the duodenum, bile neutralises the acid [1], and trypsin from the pancreas continues digesting the protein in alkaline conditions [1]. The protein is broken down into amino acids, which are small and soluble, so they are absorbed in the small intestine [1].

## Worked example 2: reading enzyme pH data

Two proteases, X and Y, are tested at different pH values. The relative activities below are for practice only.

| pH | 1 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 11 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Protease X | 60 | 100 | 70 | 10 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Protease Y | 0 | 0 | 0 | 15 | 70 | 100 | 85 | 20 |

**(a) Which protease is pepsin? Explain.** X, because its optimum is pH 2, which is acidic like the stomach.

**(b) Suggest why protease Y would not work in the stomach.** The stomach is acidic; Y has zero activity at pH 1 to 3, because the acid changes the shape of its active site so protein no longer fits.

**(c) Describe the activity of Y between pH 8 and pH 11.** It falls from 100 at pH 8 to 85 at pH 9 and 20 at pH 11.

## Worked example 3: using food tests

Starch solution is mixed with saliva at 37 °C. A drop is tested with iodine every minute. The iodine stays blue-black for 3 minutes and is orange-brown from 4 minutes onwards. At the end the mixture gives a positive Benedict's test.

- The iodine result shows all the starch was digested by about 4 minutes.
- The Benedict's result shows that reducing sugar was produced.
- Conclusion: amylase in saliva digested starch to reducing sugar (maltose, at Extended level).

## Common mistakes

- Saying amylase breaks starch into glucose at Core. Core answer: simple reducing sugars; Extended: maltose, then glucose by maltase.
- Forgetting glycerol as a product of lipase.
- Writing that the liver secretes enzymes. The liver makes bile, which has no enzymes.
- Saying pancreatic enzymes act "in the pancreas". They are secreted there but act in the small intestine.
- Giving acid a role in "digesting food". Its two functions are killing microorganisms and providing the acidic pH.
- (Extended) Placing maltase in the stomach or in the pancreas, or trypsin in the stomach.
- (Extended) Saying bile "neutralises the stomach". It neutralises the acidic mixture **entering the duodenum**.

## Exam technique for 7.4

- **Describe** an enzyme's function: substrate → products, with the enzyme named.
- **State** where secreted and where it acts: two separate answers.
- **Explain** (Extended) the pH idea: the region's pH matches the enzyme's optimum, linked to active-site shape.
- In data questions, quote the optimum and the activity values.
