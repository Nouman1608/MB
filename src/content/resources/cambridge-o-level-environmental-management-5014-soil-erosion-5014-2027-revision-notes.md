## How soil moves

- **Water**: raindrops splash bare soil loose → surface run-off carries it → faster on slopes → rills and
  gullies → soil reaches rivers.
- **Wind**: dry, loose, fine soil on open land → wind lifts the lightest, most fertile particles.

## Five causes

| Cause | Key point |
|---|---|
| Unsustainable agricultural practices | Overgrazing, overcultivation, monoculture, ploughing downslope |
| Deforestation | No roots to bind soil; no canopy to break rainfall |
| Farming on steep slopes | Run-off flows faster with more energy |
| Bare soil | Nothing protects the surface from rain or wind |
| Wind and water erosion | The agents that remove the soil |

Three ways causes work: **remove cover**, **weaken soil**, **speed up water or wind**.

## Seven impacts

| Impact | One-line chain |
|---|---|
| Silting of rivers | Soil settles on river beds → less capacity → floods; reservoirs fill; fish harmed |
| Desertification | Topsoil lost in dry areas → no vegetation → desert-like land |
| Mass movement | Unbound, saturated slopes → landslides, rockslides, mudslides |
| Loss of habitats and biodiversity | Soil and plants removed → organisms lose homes |
| Reduction in crop yield | Fertile topsoil lost → thin, infertile soil |
| Malnutrition and famine | Lower yields → not enough food |
| Displacement of people | Land unfarmable or homes destroyed → people move |

**People chain:** erosion → lower yields → malnutrition and famine → displacement.

## Six strategies with syllabus mechanisms

| Strategy | What it is | Syllabus mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Terracing | Slope cut into flat steps | Reduces movement of soil and surface run-off |
| Contour ploughing | Furrows across the slope along contours | Reduces surface run-off |
| Bunds | Low banks of earth or stone along contours | Reduce surface run-off, wind speed and wind erosion |
| Wind breaks | Rows of trees or hedges across the wind | Protect crops, reduce wind speed and wind erosion |
| Maintaining vegetation cover | Stubble, cover crops, controlled grazing | Roots bind the soil; vegetation reduces surface run-off and absorbs water |
| Addition of organic matter | Manure, compost, crop residue | Improves soil structure |

## Choosing a strategy

| Situation | Best-matched strategies |
|---|---|
| Steep, wet hillside | Terracing, contour ploughing, bunds, vegetation cover |
| Flat, dry, windy plain | Wind breaks, bunds, vegetation cover, organic matter |
| Overgrazed pasture | Maintaining vegetation cover (fewer animals or rotation) |
| Crumbly, exhausted soil | Addition of organic matter |

## Percentage reduction pattern

Reduction = (original loss - new loss) / original loss x 100.
*Invented example:* 30 t/ha falls to 12 t/ha → 18 / 30 x 100 = 60% reduction.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Soil erosion | Removal of soil, especially topsoil, faster than it forms |
| Topsoil | Upper, most fertile layer of soil |
| Surface run-off | Water flowing over the ground surface |
| Overgrazing | Too many animals for the vegetation, leaving bare soil |
| Overcultivation | Farming land so intensively the soil cannot recover |
| Silting | Build-up of sediment on a river or reservoir bed |
| Desertification | Degradation of land in dry areas until it is desert-like |
| Mass movement | Downhill movement of soil and rock under gravity |
| Contour | Line joining points of equal height |
| Bund | Low bank of earth or stones along a contour |

## Exam traps

- Contour ploughing reduces **surface run-off**, not wind erosion.
- Bunds and wind breaks are the strategies linked to **wind**.
- Terracing is for **slopes**; do not suggest it for flat land.
- Explain the mechanism: "roots bind the soil", "reduces wind speed".
- Impacts on people need a link: lower yields **so** malnutrition.
- Soil erosion is not weathering.

## Self-test

1. Name **five** causes of soil erosion from the syllabus.
2. How does deforestation increase soil erosion?
3. Why does farming on steep slopes increase erosion?
4. Describe how silting of rivers can lead to flooding.
5. Name the **three** types of mass movement in the syllabus.
6. How can soil erosion lead to displacement of people?
7. How does terracing reduce soil erosion?
8. Which **two** strategies reduce wind speed and wind erosion?
9. Explain how maintaining vegetation cover reduces erosion.
10. What does adding organic matter improve?
11. Suggest **one** strategy for a steep hillside and **one** for a flat windy plain.
12. Soil loss falls from 50 to 20 tonnes per hectare. Calculate the percentage reduction.

**Answers:** 1. Unsustainable agricultural practices, deforestation, farming on steep slopes, bare soil,
wind and water erosion. 2. Roots no longer bind the soil and the canopy no longer breaks the force of rain.
3. Run-off flows faster downhill, with more energy to carry soil. 4. Sediment settles on the river bed, so
the channel holds less water and overflows more easily. 5. Landslides, rockslides, mudslides. 6. Yields
fall until the land cannot support farming, or mass movement destroys homes, so people must move.
7. Flat steps hold water so it soaks in, reducing movement of soil and surface run-off. 8. Bunds and wind
breaks. 9. Roots bind the soil; vegetation reduces surface run-off and absorbs water. 10. Soil structure.
11. Hillside: terracing or contour ploughing. Plain: wind breaks. 12. 30 / 50 x 100 = 60%.
