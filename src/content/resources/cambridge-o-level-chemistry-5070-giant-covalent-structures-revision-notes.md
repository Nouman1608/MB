These notes condense section 2.6, Giant covalent structures, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). The full descriptions and worked answers are in the [giant covalent structures study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-giant-covalent-structures/); exam-style questions are in the [giant covalent structures practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-giant-covalent-structures-practice/).

## The three outcomes

1. **Describe** the giant covalent structures of graphite, diamond and silicon(IV) oxide.
2. **Relate** structure and bonding to uses: graphite as a lubricant and as an electrode; diamond in cutting tools (the syllabus limits uses to these).
3. **Describe** the similarity in properties between diamond and silicon(IV) oxide, related to structure.

## Giant covalent: the big idea

- A 3D network of atoms, all joined by **strong covalent bonds**.
- **No separate molecules**: the formula is a ratio.
- Melting means **breaking many strong covalent bonds** → very high melting points.

## Structures in one table

| | Diamond | Graphite | Silicon(IV) oxide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Element(s) | carbon | carbon | silicon and oxygen |
| Bonds per atom | 4 per C | 3 per C | 4 per Si; 2 per O |
| Arrangement | tetrahedral, 3D | flat hexagonal layers | tetrahedral, 3D |
| Delocalised electrons | none | 1 per C, move along layers | none |
| Weak forces | none | between layers | none |
| Hard or soft | very hard | soft, slippery | very hard |
| Conducts? | no | yes | no |
| Melting point | very high | very high | very high |

## Structure → property → use chains

**Diamond in cutting tools**
Each C has 4 strong covalent bonds in a rigid 3D network → very hard → cuts and drills hard materials.

**Graphite as a lubricant**
Layers held by weak forces → layers slide over each other → slippery, reduces friction (and stays solid at high temperatures).

**Graphite as an electrode**
Each C has 3 bonds, so 1 electron per atom is delocalised → electrons move along the layers → conducts electricity; also high melting point.

## Diamond vs silicon(IV) oxide: the similarity

| Shared property | Shared structural reason |
|---|---|
| very hard | rigid 3D tetrahedral network of strong covalent bonds |
| very high melting point | many strong covalent bonds must be broken |
| do not conduct | no delocalised electrons and no ions |
| insoluble in water | strong covalent network is not broken up by water |

SiO₂ ≠ CO₂: silicon(IV) oxide is giant covalent (solid, very high melting point); carbon dioxide is simple molecular (gas at room temperature).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Giant covalent structure | a huge 3D network of atoms joined by covalent bonds, with no separate molecules |
| Tetrahedral | four bonds pointing to the corners of a tetrahedron around an atom |
| Delocalised electron | an electron not fixed to one atom, free to move through part of the structure |
| Layer | a flat sheet of bonded atoms, as in graphite |
| Lubricant | a substance that reduces friction between surfaces |
| Electrode | a conductor through which current enters or leaves an electrolyte |
| Allotropes | different structural forms of the same element (diamond and graphite are allotropes of carbon) |

## Exam traps

- Graphite conducts by **delocalised electrons**, not ions.
- Graphite is slippery because forces **between layers** are weak; bonds **within** layers are strong.
- Graphite's melting point is **very high**, despite it being soft.
- Silicon in SiO₂ makes **4** bonds; oxygen makes **2**.
- Do not give uses outside the syllabus list when asked to relate structure to use.
- "Strong bonds" alone is not enough for hardness: say **rigid 3D network**.

## Self-test

1. How many covalent bonds does each carbon atom form in graphite?
2. Describe the structure of diamond.
3. Why does graphite conduct electricity?
4. Why does diamond not conduct electricity?
5. Explain why graphite is used as a lubricant.
6. Give **two** reasons why graphite is suitable for electrodes.
7. Explain why diamond is used in cutting tools.
8. Describe the structure of silicon(IV) oxide.
9. Give **two** properties that diamond and silicon(IV) oxide have in common.
10. Why do diamond and silicon(IV) oxide have similar properties?
11. Why does graphite have a high melting point even though it is soft?
12. Explain why the formula SiO₂ does not represent a molecule.

## Answers

1. Three.
2. Each carbon atom forms four covalent bonds to four other carbon atoms in a tetrahedral arrangement, repeated in a rigid 3D giant structure.
3. Each carbon atom has one delocalised electron, free to move along the layers and carry charge.
4. All four outer electrons of each carbon are used in covalent bonds: no delocalised electrons.
5. The layers are held together by weak forces, so they slide over each other easily.
6. It conducts electricity (delocalised electrons) and has a high melting point.
7. It is very hard, because each atom is held by four strong covalent bonds in a rigid 3D network.
8. Each silicon atom is covalently bonded to four oxygen atoms and each oxygen atom to two silicon atoms, in a 3D giant structure.
9. Any two: very hard; very high melting point; do not conduct electricity; insoluble in water.
10. Both are giant covalent structures with a rigid 3D tetrahedral network of strong covalent bonds and no delocalised electrons.
11. Melting requires breaking the strong covalent bonds within the layers; softness comes only from the weak forces between layers.
12. SiO₂ is a giant structure with no separate molecules; the formula shows the ratio of Si to O atoms, 1 : 2.
