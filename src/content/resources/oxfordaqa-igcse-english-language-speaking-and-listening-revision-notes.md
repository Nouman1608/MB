The speaking and listening endorsement in OxfordAQA International GCSE English Language (9270) is
**optional** and carries **0% of the grade**: it appears as a separate statement on your certificate. You
give one **prepared, formal presentation or an interview with extended turns**, on a topic agreed with
your teacher, and the talk plus questions should take **no more than 10 minutes** (typically 5–8). You are
graded Pass, Merit or Distinction against AO3 (SL1–SL3). For the full skills guide, use the
[Speaking and listening study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-english-language-speaking-and-listening-optional-endorsement-study-guide/).

## Format at a glance

| Detail | Specification (9270, 3.4) |
|---|---|
| Status | Optional endorsement; separate statement on the certificate |
| Weighting | 0% of the International GCSE grade |
| Task | One extended task: a presentation, or an interview with extended turns |
| Forms | Talks, debates, speeches, dialogues; must be formal |
| Topic | Identified in advance and agreed with the teacher |
| Preparation | Must be planned and organised; lack of preparation is likely to block higher grades |
| Aids | Pre-prepared notes or slides allowed, not required |
| Length | Presentation plus questions no more than 10 minutes; average usually 5–8 minutes |
| Audience | One person or more |
| Assessment | Teacher assessed, moderated by the board |

## AO3: Speaking and Listening strands

| Strand | Wording |
|---|---|
| SL1 | Communicate clearly and purposefully; structure and sustain talk, and use a variety of techniques as appropriate |
| SL2 | Prepare and develop ideas and concepts; listen and respond to questions about these |
| SL3 | Interact with others, shaping meanings through comments, responses to questions and drawing ideas together |

## The five principles behind the criteria

The specification says the criteria rest on:

1. the need to **prepare** what you are going to say
2. the need to be **heard and understood**
3. the need to have **ideas and concepts** (content)
4. the need for **interactive strategies** to engage and help the audience (structure and rhetoric)
5. the need to use **spoken English effectively** (vocabulary and formality suited to the talk)

As an international qualification, students "will use international varieties of English".

## Pass, Merit and Distinction

Grading is holistic and on a **competency** basis: you must meet every criterion in a grade to move to the
next. Students below Pass are recorded as Not Classified.

| Pass | Merit | Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Prepares for the talk | Expresses challenging ideas/information/feelings | Expresses sophisticated ideas/information/feelings |
| Is audible and intelligible | Organises and structures clearly and appropriately | Organises and structures using an effective range of strategies to engage the audience |
| Expresses straightforward ideas/information/feelings | Interacts with and engages the audience | Listens to questions/feedback, responds perceptively and, if appropriate, elaborates |
| Attempts to organise and structure | Listens and responds in some detail | Assured and flexible spoken English with a sophisticated repertoire of vocabulary |
| Attempts to interact with the audience | Confident spoken English with a range of vocabulary | |
| Responds to questions in a straightforward manner | | |
| Generally uses appropriate spoken English | | |

## The specification's example tasks

| Example | Form |
|---|---|
| 1. Speech to peers about lobbying a local representative on climate change | Speech |
| 2. Presentation on a TV current-affairs programme on whether your country should bid to host a major sporting event, followed by audience questions (can be a panel, allowing more than one student to be assessed) | Presentation and questions |
| 3. Presenting a novel, play, poem, film or TV programme you think deserves a wider audience | Persuasive talk |
| 4. Debate on whether there should be controls on social media | Debate |
| 5. A topic close to your own life: travel, hobbies, sport, music, your community | Talk |
| 6. Any of the above as an interview, normally conducted by the teacher, with extended, prepared answers | Interview |

The topic can link to the written NEA project, with the talk forming part of your preparation for it.

## Planning for Distinction

| Criterion | How to plan for it |
|---|---|
| Sophisticated ideas | Choose a question with more than one side, not a list of facts |
| Effective range of strategies | Plan at least three: anecdote, rhetorical question, visual, data, direct address |
| Responds perceptively | Predict questions; prepare evidence and a further example for each |
| Assured, flexible spoken English | Rehearse aloud; vary register for quotation, explanation and appeal |
| Time | Rehearse to leave room for questions within 10 minutes |

## Worked example: Merit to Distinction (SL2 and SL3)

Question: "Wouldn't hosting the World Cup cost more than it brings in?"

- **Merit:** "It is expensive, but it brings tourists and jobs, so I think it's worth it."
- **Distinction:** "That depends on what we count as 'bringing in'. If we mean ticket money during the
  month, many hosts lose money. But my argument was about long-term infrastructure, such as the new rail
  line I mentioned, and about national confidence, which is harder to measure. So I'd change your
  question slightly: the real test is what a country builds that it would not otherwise have built."

The Distinction answer responds perceptively, links back to the talk and elaborates with new ideas.

## Common mistakes / traps

- **Over-running**: the talk plus questions should take no more than 10 minutes.
- **Unprepared talks**: preparation is a Pass criterion, and its absence blocks higher grades.
- **A list topic**: straightforward information limits you to Pass.
- **Forgetting interaction**: Merit requires interacting with and engaging the audience.
- **Short answers**: questions are part of the assessment at every grade.
- **Thinking it affects the grade**: it does not; but it is reported to future employers and institutions.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does the speaking and listening endorsement count towards my 9270 grade?**
No. It carries 0% weighting and appears as a separate statement on the certificate.

**Can my audience be just my teacher?**
Yes. The specification says the audience can be one person or more, and an interview is normally
conducted by the teacher.

**Do I need a British accent or British English?**
No. The specification notes that this is an international qualification and students will use
international varieties of English.

**How long should my talk be?**
No presentation plus questions should take more than 10 minutes; the average is usually 5–8 minutes.

## Self-test

1. What weighting does the endorsement carry? (0%.)
2. What is the maximum time for presentation plus questions? (10 minutes.)
3. Name the three AO3 strands. (SL1 communicate and structure; SL2 prepare ideas and respond; SL3 interact.)
4. What does "competency basis" mean? (Every criterion in a grade must be met.)
5. Who usually conducts the interview option? (The teacher.)
