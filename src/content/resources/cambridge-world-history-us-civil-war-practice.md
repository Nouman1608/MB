These questions cover key question 1.4 of Cambridge IGCSE 0470 and O
Level 2147: why there was a civil war in the United States and what its
results were. The question is the prescribed Paper 2 topic for the
November 2025 and June 2026 series, so Question 3 practises the source
skills. The short sources in Question 3 are genuine extracts from
documents of the period; the questions on them are original.

## Paper 1 style: Structured Questions

### Question 1: Slavery and the coming of war

**(a)** Describe the Compromise of 1850. **[4]**

**(b)** Why did the Kansas–Nebraska Act of 1854 increase tension
between North and South? **[6]**

**(c)** 'Slavery was the main cause of the American Civil War.' How far
do you agree with this statement? Explain your answer. **[10]**

#### Worked answer

**(a)** Any four points: California was admitted as a free state; the
territories of New Mexico and Utah would decide on slavery by popular
sovereignty; the slave trade (but not slavery) was abolished in the
District of Columbia; a stricter Fugitive Slave Act required Northern
officials and citizens to help return people who had escaped slavery;
it followed the Mexican War, which had raised the question of slavery
in the new lands. **[4]**

**(b)** Explained reasons, for example: the Act let the settlers of
Kansas and Nebraska decide on slavery, which repealed the Missouri
Compromise ban north of 36°30′, so Northerners felt the South was
breaking a settled agreement; pro- and anti-slavery settlers rushed
into Kansas and fought ("Bleeding Kansas"), showing the issue could no
longer be settled peacefully; opponents of the Act founded the
Republican Party, a purely Northern party, which divided national
politics along sectional lines. Two well-explained reasons reach full
marks. **[6]**

**(c)** **Agree:** every major crisis — 1820, 1850, 1854, 1857 (Dred
Scott), 1859 (Harpers Ferry) — was about slavery and its expansion;
Southern states' own declarations of secession named the defence of
slavery; Lincoln's election mattered because he opposed slavery's
spread. **Disagree / other factors:** states' rights and the
Nullification Crisis show a constitutional argument; economic
differences and tariffs; the breakdown of compromise and the split in
the Democratic Party in 1860; the trigger of Lincoln's election and
Fort Sumter. **Judgement:** the best answers argue that states' rights
and economic differences were largely about slavery, so slavery was the
underlying cause and the 1860 election the trigger. Top-level answers
explain both sides and reach a supported conclusion. **[10]**

### Question 2: Victory and Reconstruction

**(a)** What advantages did the North have at the start of the war in
1861? **[4]**

**(b)** Why was the Emancipation Proclamation important to the Union's
war effort? **[6]**

**(c)** 'Reconstruction was a failure.' How far do you agree? Explain
your answer. **[10]**

#### Worked answer

**(a)** Any four: a much larger population (about 22 million against
about 9 million, over a third of whom were enslaved); most of the
country's factories; most of the railway network; a navy that could
blockade Southern ports; greater food production; an established
government and treasury. **[4]**

**(b)** Explained points: it turned the war into a war against slavery
as well as for the Union, which made it very hard for Britain and
France, both opposed to slavery, to recognise the Confederacy; it
encouraged enslaved people to escape to Union lines, weakening the
Southern economy; it allowed the recruitment of Black soldiers, about
180,000 of whom served in the Union army. **[6]**

**(c)** **Agree:** Black Codes and Ku Klux Klan terror; most freed
people became sharecroppers without land; Northern commitment faded
and the Compromise of 1877 withdrew federal troops; Southern states
later used poll taxes, literacy tests and violence to stop Black men
voting. **Disagree:** slavery was abolished by the 13th Amendment;
the 14th and 15th Amendments wrote citizenship, equal protection and
voting rights into the Constitution; Freedmen's Bureau schools; Black
Americans were elected to Congress, such as Hiram Revels in 1870; the
Union was restored. **Judgement:** for example, a failure in securing
equality in the short term, but a lasting success in ending slavery and
creating constitutional rights that later civil rights campaigns
used. **[10]**

## Paper 2 style: Document Question

**Topic: Why did the Southern states secede, 1860–61?**

**Source A** — Abraham Lincoln, speech at Springfield, Illinois, June
1858: "A house divided against itself cannot stand. I believe this
government cannot endure, permanently half slave and half free."

**Source B** — from South Carolina's declaration of the causes of its
secession, December 1860, which complained of "an increasing hostility
on the part of the non-slaveholding States to the institution of
slavery".

**Source C** — Alexander Stephens, Vice-President of the Confederacy,
speech in Savannah, March 1861: "Our new government is founded upon
exactly the opposite idea; its foundations are laid, its cornerstone
rests, upon the great truth that the negro is not equal to the white
man; that slavery, subordination to the superior race, is his natural
and normal condition."

**Source D** — Abraham Lincoln, letter to the newspaper editor Horace
Greeley, August 1862: "My paramount object in this struggle is to save
the Union, and is not either to save or to destroy slavery."

**(a)** What can you learn from Source B about why South Carolina
seceded? **[4]**

**(b)** Why do you think Southern leaders were alarmed by speeches such
as Source A? **[6]**

**(c)** How far does Source C support Source B? **[8]**

**(d)** Does Source D prove that the war was not about slavery? Explain
your answer. **[7]**

**(e)** 'The Southern states seceded to defend slavery.' How far do
these sources support this view? **[15]**

### Worked answer

**(a)** Source B shows that South Carolina blamed the free states'
growing hostility to slavery; it saw secession as a defence of slavery
as an institution; it implies the state no longer trusted the federal
government to protect it, given what had happened by December 1860
(Lincoln's election). Developed inferences score more than copying.
**[4]**

**(b)** Lincoln was the leading Republican; the speech suggested the
nation would eventually become all free, which Southerners read as a
threat to end slavery even where it existed. Once Lincoln won in 1860
without Southern votes, they feared he would act on it. Context
(Republican opposition to slavery's expansion) is needed for the higher
marks. **[6]**

**(c)** Strong support: both put slavery at the centre; Source B
complains of hostility to slavery, and Source C states that the
Confederacy's "cornerstone" is slavery and racial inequality. Source C
goes further, as it is an open statement of the new government's
founding idea rather than a list of grievances. Both are Southern
public statements, written to justify secession, which explains their
agreement. **[8]**

**(d)** No. Source D shows Lincoln's public war aim in August 1862 was
to save the Union, and he was writing to reassure Northern and
border-state opinion. But he was already preparing the Emancipation
Proclamation, issued in preliminary form a month later, and Sources B
and C show the South left the Union to protect slavery. The war's
purpose for the North and the cause of secession are different
questions. **[7]**

**(e)** Support: Sources B and C directly name slavery as the reason
for secession; Source A explains why the South feared the Republicans.
Challenge: Source D suggests the North was fighting for the Union,
not against slavery, in 1862; Source B's wording also reflects a
states'-rights grievance against other states. Evaluation: Sources B
and C are official Southern statements, so they are strong evidence of
Southern motives; Source D is about Northern aims, not Southern ones.
**Judgement:** the sources strongly support the view. **[15]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing about the whole war when the question asks about one
  turning point or one source.
- Treating Source D as Lincoln's private belief rather than a public
  letter written for a political purpose.
- Copying sources instead of making inferences from them.
- Mixing up the dates of emancipation (1863) and abolition (1865).
