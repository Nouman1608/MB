## Questions

**1.** A student uses a light microscope with a ×10 eyepiece lens and a ×40 objective lens. (a) State the total magnification. **[1]** (b) State the job of the stage. **[1]** (c) State the job of the lamp. **[1]**

**2.** A student makes a slide of cheek cells and adds a drop of methylene blue. (a) Explain why a stain is used. **[2]** (b) The cover slip is lowered at an angle with a mounted needle. Give the reason. **[1]**

**3.** A leaf cell is 18 mm long on a light micrograph. Its real length is 45 µm. Calculate the magnification of the micrograph. **[3]**

**4.** A chloroplast is 54 mm long on a transmission electron micrograph (TEM) of magnification ×6000. (a) Calculate the real length of the chloroplast in µm. **[2]** (b) Give your answer to (a) in metres, in standard form. **[2]**

**6.** A sperm cell has far more mitochondria than a skin cell. Explain why. **[3]**

**7.** (a) Describe the position and form of the DNA that makes up the main genome of a bacterium. **[2]** (b) State what a plasmid is and give one gene that it may carry. **[2]**

**8.** A hormone affects liver cells but has no effect on skin cells. (a) Explain, in terms of the cell membrane, why only liver cells respond. **[2]** (b) State one other role of the cell membrane. **[1]** (c) A liver cell makes large amounts of protein. Name the structure where proteins are made and suggest what you would expect to see a lot of in this cell. **[2]**

**9.** Ribosomes are about 25 nm across. They cannot be seen with a light microscope but are visible with a TEM. (a) Explain why. **[3]** (b) On a TEM image of magnification ×200 000, calculate the image size of a ribosome in mm. **[2]**

**10.** A TEM image has a scale bar of 20 mm, labelled 2 µm. A mitochondrion on the same image is 30 mm long. (a) Work out the magnification. **[2]** (b) Work out the actual length of the mitochondrion in µm. **[2]** (c) Give this length in nm. **[1]** (d) Suggest one reason why a TEM image gives more information about the mitochondrion than a light micrograph. **[1]**

**11.** A student examines onion epidermis with iodine solution. The field of view is 1.8 mm across and about 6 cells fit in a row across it. (a) Estimate the length of one cell in µm. **[2]** (b) The student draws one cell 60 mm long. Calculate the magnification of the drawing, assuming the cell is 0.3 mm long. **[2]** (c) The student sees nuclei but no chloroplasts. Suggest why chloroplasts are absent, and why iodine helps. **[2]** (d) Evaluate the use of a TEM rather than a light microscope for studying a living onion cell. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Total magnification = 10 × 40 = **×400** [1] (b) The stage holds the slide in position, with the specimen over the hole [1] (c) The lamp provides light that passes through the specimen [1]
*Examiner insight:* For a total magnification, show the multiplication and include the "×" sign; a bare number gives no evidence of method if you slip.

**2.** (a) Cells are nearly colourless, so there is little contrast with the background [1]; the stain colours structures such as the nucleus so they can be seen [1] (b) To avoid trapping air bubbles under the cover slip [1]
*Examiner insight:* A two-mark "explain why" answer is best written as a reason (colourless) plus a consequence (structures visible); "to see it better" alone is vague.

**3.** magnification = image size ÷ actual size [1]; convert 18 mm to 18 000 µm [1]; 18 000 ÷ 45 = **×400** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Convert units before dividing. Computing 18 ÷ 45 = 0.4 skips the conversion and gives a figure that cannot be right for a micrograph.

**4.** (a) actual size = 54 ÷ 6000 = 0.009 mm [1]; 0.009 × 1000 = **9 µm** [1] (b) 9 µm = 0.000009 m [1], which is **9 × 10⁻⁶ m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Both parts of a standard-form answer count: the number and the power. A length well under 1 m needs a negative power.

**5.** Palisade cell: nucleus ✓, chloroplast ✓, mitochondrion ✓, plasmid ✗. Cheek cell: nucleus ✓, chloroplast ✗, mitochondrion ✓, plasmid ✗. Bacterium: nucleus ✗, chloroplast ✗, mitochondrion ✗, plasmid ✓. One mark for each structure (row) completely correct: nucleus [1], chloroplast [1], mitochondrion [1], plasmid [1].
*Examiner insight:* Fill every box, checking each row against what the cell type contains; a blank box tells the examiner nothing about what you know.

**6.** Mitochondria contain enzymes for cellular respiration [1]; respiration releases energy from glucose [1]; a sperm cell needs a lot of energy to swim, so it needs many mitochondria [1]
*Examiner insight:* Link the structure, its process and the cell's need. "Mitochondria make energy" is a common slip: energy is released in respiration.

**7.** (a) The genetic material is a single circular chromosome [1] lying free in the cytoplasm, because a bacterium has no nucleus [1] (b) A plasmid is a small ring of DNA separate from the main chromosome [1]; it may carry a gene for antibiotic resistance [1]
*Examiner insight:* "DNA in the nucleus" for a bacterium is a very common error. The absence of a nucleus is what the question is testing.

**8.** (a) Liver cell membranes have receptor molecules [1] that bind the hormone, whereas skin cells lack the matching receptor [1] (b) The membrane is a selective barrier, controlling which molecules enter and leave [1] (c) Ribosomes are the site of protein synthesis [1]; you would expect many ribosomes in the cytoplasm [1]
*Examiner insight:* Match your answer to the number of marks in each part. In (b) one clear role earns the mark, and the word "selective" is the key one.

**9.** (a) Resolution is the smallest gap at which two points still look separate [1]; a light microscope can only separate points more than about 200 nm apart, so 25 nm ribosomes are not resolved [1]; a TEM uses electrons, which give much higher resolution, so the ribosomes can be seen [1] (b) image size = 25 nm × 200 000 = 5 000 000 nm [1] = **5 mm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the word resolution, not "clearer" or "magnifies more". Higher magnification alone would not make the ribosomes visible.

**10.** (a) 20 mm = 20 000 µm [1]; 20 000 ÷ 2 = **×10 000** [1] (b) 30 mm = 30 000 µm; 30 000 ÷ 10 000 = **3 µm** [2] (c) 3 × 1000 = **3000 nm** [1] (d) The TEM has a higher resolution, so it shows internal detail such as the folds of the inner membrane [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show each conversion and write units on every line; a clear layout makes slips easy to spot and lets the reader follow your reasoning.

**11.** (a) 1.8 mm ÷ 6 [1] = 0.3 mm = **300 µm** [1] (b) magnification = 60 ÷ 0.3 [1] = **×200** [1] (c) Onion bulb tissue is underground and does not photosynthesise, so its cells do not contain chloroplasts [1]; iodine solution stains the nucleus so it stands out against the cytoplasm [1] (d) A TEM needs dead, very thin and treated specimens, so it cannot show a living cell [1]; a light microscope can be used on living cells, although its resolution is lower [1]
*Examiner insight:* An "evaluate" answer is best when it gives a limit of one method and a benefit of the other, both tied to the living cell in the question.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing the actual size by the image size instead of the other way round.
- Skipping the conversion between mm and µm, or between µm and nm.
- Writing standard form with a positive power for a tiny size.
- Saying a TEM "magnifies more" instead of explaining that it has higher resolution.
- Placing DNA of a bacterium in a nucleus.
- Saying mitochondria "make" energy rather than release it during respiration.
- Calling the cell membrane a barrier that keeps everything in, without the word selective.
- Giving chloroplasts to cheek cells or to cells in non-green plant tissue.
- Leaving out the unit in a final answer, or using the symbol µ for mm.
- Describing a stain only as "to see it better", without saying it adds contrast.
