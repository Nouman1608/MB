This guide covers **Paper 1 Research Methods, Identity and
Inequality**, for Cambridge O Level Sociology 2251, Version 3,
2025–2027 series. Independently confirmed against 2251's own
specification PDF: it shares identical paper titles and structure with
Cambridge IGCSE Sociology 0495. Paper 1 is compulsory across all three
topics — three questions, candidates answer two, with Question 1
compulsory.

## Where this fits in 2251

Research Methods is the foundational topic of the syllabus — the
methods introduced here are assumed knowledge across the rest of the
qualification, since exam questions routinely ask candidates to
suggest or evaluate a research method for studying whatever
substantive topic — identity, inequality, family, education, crime —
the question is actually about.

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE O LEVEL SOCIOLOGY 2251 — PAPER 1 RESEARCH METHODS,
IDENTITY AND INEQUALITY**

- 1.1 Research Methods — the main methods sociologists use to collect
data (including surveys, interviews, observation and secondary
sources), and their strengths and limitations
- 1.2 Identity: Self and Society — how identity is shaped by social
factors including socialisation, culture, and agents such as family,
education, media and religion
- 1.3 Social Stratification and Inequality — how societies are
divided into unequal groups by factors such as class, gender and
ethnicity, and the causes and consequences of that inequality

## How to approach it

As with the equivalent IGCSE syllabus, Research Methods rewards
evaluation over description — for any given research question,
practise stating one strength and one limitation of a questionnaire
versus an interview versus observation for that specific topic, since
"identify a method and justify your choice" is a recurring question
type. For 1.2 and 1.3, use named sociological concepts precisely (e.g.
primary versus secondary socialisation, ascribed versus achieved
status) rather than everyday language that only loosely gestures at the
same idea. Because Paper 1 has a compulsory Question 1 plus a choice of
one more from the remaining two, do not skip revising any of the three
topics entirely, even if one feels weaker — being forced into an
unprepared topic by the compulsory question is a common and avoidable
source of lost marks. Linking 1.2 and 1.3 in revision also pays off, since
identity and inequality are closely connected in the syllabus —
for instance, how social class or gender shapes the identity someone
develops — and questions sometimes reward candidates who can draw that
connection rather than treating the two topics as entirely separate.
