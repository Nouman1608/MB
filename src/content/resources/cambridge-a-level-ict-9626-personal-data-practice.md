## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by personal data and give **two** examples. **[3]**

**2.** Explain why a photograph posted online may reveal where a person lives. **[2]**

**3.** Describe the difference between anonymising and aggregating data. **[2]**

**4.** Name the type of attack described in each case.

**(a)** A text message says a parcel is waiting and asks the user to follow a link and enter card details. **[1]**

**(b)** A caller claiming to be from a bank asks the customer to read out a one-time code. **[1]**

**(c)** A user types the correct address of their bank but is shown a fake copy of the site. **[1]**

**5.** State **one** way a hardware firewall differs from a software firewall. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Rosewood Hospital stores patient records on its network and wants to use the data for research.

**(a)** Explain how anonymising the data helps protect patients. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **two** duty of confidence measures the hospital could use for its staff. **[4]**

**7.** Explain **three** reasons why personal data should be kept confidential. **[3]**

**8.** A bank customer receives an email that appears to come from the bank. It says "Dear customer, your account will close in 24 hours unless you click here".

**(a)** Name this type of attack. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify **three** features of the email that suggest it is not genuine. **[3]**

**(c)** Describe **two** actions the customer should take. **[2]**

**9.** Explain how pharming differs from phishing and why it is harder for a user to detect. **[4]**

**10.** Compare hardware and software firewalls for a small office of eight computers. **[4]**

**11.** Describe **three** network security measures, other than firewalls, that help prevent the misuse of personal data held by an online retailer. **[6]**

**12.** Ashdown College plans to publish exam results by class to show how well students are doing. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of publishing aggregated results instead of individual results. **[6]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Information about a living person [1] who can be identified from it [1]. Any two examples such as name, address, date of birth, bank details, medical records, fingerprints [1]. **[3]**

**2.** Photographs can contain geotag metadata with the location where they were taken [1], so anyone who downloads the file can read it and find the address, for example of a home [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Anonymising removes or changes details that identify individuals so the data cannot be linked to a person [1]; aggregating combines individual records into totals or averages so individuals cannot be seen [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** Smishing [1].

**(b)** Vishing [1].

**(c)** Pharming [1]. **[3]**

**5.** A hardware firewall is a separate device that protects the whole network, whereas a software firewall is a program that protects only the computer it is installed on [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** Names, addresses and other identifying details are removed or replaced by codes [1], so if the research data is lost or shared, the information cannot be traced to individual patients [1].

**(b)** Any two measures, each with a development: staff sign a confidentiality agreement [1], which gives the hospital a legal means of acting if data is shared [1]; access rights limit each worker to the records they need [1], so fewer people can see the data [1]; training in data protection [1], so staff know what they must and must not do [1]; disciplinary action for staff who break the rules [1], which discourages careless sharing [1]. **[6]**

**7.** Any three from: it prevents identity theft and fraud [1]; it protects personal safety, for example from stalking or burglary [1]; it avoids discrimination or embarrassment from health or financial details becoming known [1]; organisations have a legal duty under data protection law and can be fined [1]; it keeps the trust of customers [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** Phishing [1].

**(b)** Any three from: a generic greeting rather than the customer's name [1]; an urgent threat to close the account [1]; a request to click a link [1]; possible spelling mistakes or an unfamiliar sender address [1]; the link address does not match the bank's website [1].

**(c)** Any two from: do not click the link [1]; contact the bank using a known telephone number or website [1]; report or delete the email [1]. **[6]**

**9.** In phishing the user is sent a message and must click a link to reach a fake website [1]. In pharming, malicious code or altered DNS information redirects the user to the fake site even when the correct web address is typed [1]. There is no suspicious message to notice [1], and the address in the browser can look correct, so it is harder to detect [1]. **[4]**

**10.** A hardware firewall protects all eight computers from one device, whereas a software firewall must be installed and updated on every computer [1]. A hardware firewall does not use the computers' memory and processor, whereas a software firewall does [1]. A hardware firewall costs more and needs set-up by someone with skills, whereas software firewalls are cheap or free [1]. Software firewalls protect a laptop used away from the office, whereas a hardware firewall only protects computers behind it [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Any three measures, each with a description and a purpose: strong passwords with two-factor authentication [1], which makes it harder for a criminal to log in as a customer or staff member [1]; access rights [1], so staff see only the data their job needs [1]; encryption of stored and transmitted data [1], so stolen data cannot be read [1]; anti-malware and regular updates [1], to detect spyware and close weaknesses [1]; back-ups [1], so data can be restored after an attack [1]; audit logs [1], which show who accessed records and reveal unusual activity [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Advantages: individual students cannot be identified, protecting their privacy [1]; trends can be seen, such as which classes need support [1]; it avoids pressure or embarrassment for individuals [1]. Disadvantages: detail is lost, so a student's strengths and weaknesses cannot be seen [1]; in a small class the figures could reveal individuals, for example if the class has two students [1]; parents cannot see how their own child is doing [1]. Conclusion: publishing aggregated results is the safer choice for public figures, with individual results sent privately to each student [1]. Any six marks. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing up smishing (text) and vishing (voice), or saying pharming needs a click.
- Describing anonymising and aggregating as the same thing.
- Saying a firewall protects against phishing.
- Giving a security measure without saying how it protects the data.
- Not linking the answer to the scenario in a discuss question.
