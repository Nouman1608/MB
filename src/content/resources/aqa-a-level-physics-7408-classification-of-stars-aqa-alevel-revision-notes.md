## Equations

| Equation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| I₁/I₂ = 2.51^(m₂ − m₁) | intensity ratio from magnitudes |
| m − M = 5 log₁₀(d/10) | distance modulus, d in parsecs |
| λ_max T = 2.9 × 10⁻³ m K | Wien's displacement law |
| P = σAT⁴, A = 4πr² | Stefan's law |
| I = P/4πd² | inverse square law |
| Rs ≈ 2GM/c² | Schwarzschild radius |

## 3.9.2.1–3.9.2.2 Magnitudes and distance: recall

- **Apparent magnitude m**: brightness as seen from Earth (Hipparcos scale).
- Dimmest naked-eye stars: m = 6. Lower m = brighter; negative values allowed.
- Δm = 1 → ratio 2.51; Δm = 5 → ratio 100; ratio 10 → Δm = 2.5.
- Brightness is **subjective**; intensity is measured.
- **Light year**: distance light travels in a year, 9.46 × 10¹⁵ m.
- **Parsec**: distance at which 1 AU subtends 1 arcsecond, 3.08 × 10¹⁶ m = 3.26 ly.
- **Absolute magnitude M**: apparent magnitude at 10 pc.
- m > M → further than 10 pc; m < M → closer than 10 pc.

## 3.9.2.3 Black bodies: recall

- Star treated as a **black body**.
- Hotter → higher curve at all wavelengths, peak at shorter λ.
- Wien: estimate T from the peak wavelength.
- Stefan: P ∝ r²T⁴. Double T → 16 × power; double r → 4 × power.
- Inverse square assumptions: uniform emission in all directions; no absorption on the way.
- Same T, different P → different radius. Same P, cooler → larger.

## 3.9.2.4 Spectral classes: recall

| Class | Colour | T / K | Prominent absorption lines |
|---|---|---|---|
| O | blue | 25 000–50 000 | He⁺, He, H |
| B | blue | 11 000–25 000 | He, H |
| A | blue-white | 7500–11 000 | H (strongest), ionised metals |
| F | white | 6000–7500 | ionised metals |
| G | yellow-white | 5000–6000 | ionised and neutral metals |
| K | orange | 3500–5000 | neutral metals |
| M | red | < 3500 | neutral atoms, TiO |

- **Balmer lines** need H atoms in **n = 2**.
- Cool stars: few atoms excited to n = 2. Hottest stars: hydrogen ionised.
- So Balmer lines are strongest in **A** stars.

## 3.9.2.5 HR diagram: recall

- Vertical: absolute magnitude, −10 (top) to +15 (bottom).
- Horizontal: temperature **decreasing** left to right, 50 000 K to 2500 K, or O to M.
- Main sequence: top left to bottom right.
- Giants/supergiants: top right (cool, luminous, large).
- White dwarfs: bottom left (hot, faint, small).
- **Sun**: main sequence, G class, about 5800 K, M ≈ +5.
- **Sun-like evolution**: main sequence → red giant (up and right) → white dwarf (left and down).

## 3.9.2.6 Supernovae and compact objects: recall

- **Supernova**: rapid increase in absolute magnitude.
- **Neutron star**: almost entirely neutrons; density about that of a nucleus (around 10¹⁷ kg m⁻³).
- **Black hole**: escape velocity > c; event horizon at Rs ≈ 2GM/c².
- **Gamma ray bursts**: collapse of supergiants to neutron stars or black holes; compare energy with Sun's lifetime output (power × lifetime in seconds).
- **Type 1a**: light curve rises quickly to a peak, then declines slowly; peak M about −19.3; used as standard candles.
- Distant type 1a fainter than expected → accelerating expansion → **dark energy**; controversial (unknown nature; relies on identical peaks).
- **Supermassive black holes** at galaxy centres.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Apparent magnitude | Brightness of a star as seen from Earth |
| Absolute magnitude | Apparent magnitude the star would have at 10 pc |
| Parsec | Distance at which 1 AU subtends 1 arcsecond |
| Black body | Perfect emitter and absorber of all wavelengths |
| Standard candle | Object of known absolute magnitude used to find distance |
| Event horizon | Boundary of a black hole, radius Rs |
| Main sequence | Band on the HR diagram where stars fuse hydrogen in their cores |

## Exam traps

- d must be in **parsecs** in the distance modulus.
- 2.51 raised to the magnitude **difference**, not multiplied by it.
- Both T⁴ and r² in Stefan comparisons.
- Wien's λ_max in metres: 500 nm = 5.0 × 10⁻⁷ m.
- HR temperature axis runs backwards and is not linear.
- Lifetime energy needs years converted to seconds.

## Self-test

**1.** What apparent magnitude do the dimmest naked-eye stars have?
**2.** Two stars differ by 3 magnitudes. Find the intensity ratio.
**3.** Define absolute magnitude.
**4.** A star has m = 7.0 and M = 2.0. Find d.
**5.** A spectrum peaks at 725 nm. Estimate T.
**6.** Star X has twice the radius and twice the temperature of star Y. Find P_X/P_Y.
**7.** State two assumptions of the inverse square law for stars.
**8.** Why are Balmer lines weak in O stars?
**9.** Where is a white dwarf on the HR diagram?
**10.** What defines a black hole?
**11.** Calculate Rs for 3.0 solar masses.
**12.** Why are type 1a supernovae useful standard candles?

## Self-test answers

**1.** 6.
**2.** 2.51³ = 15.8.
**3.** The apparent magnitude the star would have if it were 10 pc away.
**4.** 7.0 − 2.0 = 5 log(d/10), so d = 100 pc.
**5.** T = 2.9 × 10⁻³/725 × 10⁻⁹ = 4.0 × 10³ K.
**6.** 2² × 2⁴ = 64.
**7.** The star emits equally in all directions; no radiation is absorbed between star and Earth.
**8.** Most hydrogen is ionised, so few atoms have electrons in n = 2.
**9.** Bottom left: hot and faint.
**10.** Its escape velocity is greater than the speed of light.
**11.** Rs = 2 × 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.97 × 10³⁰/(3.00 × 10⁸)² = 8.8 × 10³ m.
**12.** They all reach about the same peak absolute magnitude, so their peak apparent magnitude gives distance.
