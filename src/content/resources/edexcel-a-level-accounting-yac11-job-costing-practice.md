## Section B

**6.** Orchard Print makes canvas bags in batches. Batch 12 is 800 bags. The costs are:

- materials 1.80 per bag
- direct labour 50 hours at 13 an hour
- set-up cost 220 for the batch
- overhead absorbed at 5 per machine hour; the batch used 40 machine hours

**(a)** Calculate the total cost of the batch. **[5]**

**(b)** Calculate the cost per bag, and the selling price per bag if a mark-up of 35% is applied (both to the nearest cent). **[3]**

**(c)** A batch of 1,600 bags would need 90 labour hours and 70 machine hours, with the same set-up cost. Calculate the cost per bag and explain why it differs. **[5]**

**7.** Job 301 was quoted on these estimates: materials 900, direct labour 40 hours at 14 an hour, and overhead of 8 per labour hour. The job actually used materials of 960 and 44 labour hours at 14 an hour. Overhead is still absorbed at 8 per labour hour.

**(a)** Calculate the estimated cost and the actual cost. **[4]**

**(b)** Calculate the difference and state the percentage by which the actual cost exceeded the estimate (one decimal place). **[2]**

**8.** At the year end two jobs were incomplete. Job 410 had materials of 350 and 12 labour hours at 14 an hour. Job 411 had materials of 210 and 5 labour hours at 14 an hour. Overhead is absorbed at 8 per labour hour. Calculate the value of work in progress and state where it is shown in the financial statements. **[5]**

**9.** Evaluate the suitability of job costing for a business that produces standard products in large quantities. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: each job is a separate order, often made to a customer's specification [1]; costs are collected for each job on a job cost sheet [1]; a job number identifies the costs [1]; overheads are added using an absorption rate [1]; the profit on each job can be found [1].

**2. (a)** Materials requisition note [1]

**(b)** Time sheet or job card [1]

**3.** Direct labour 28 × 15 = 420 [1]; prime cost 640 + 420 + 90 = 1,150 [1]; overheads 28 × 7 = 196 [1]; total cost 1,150 + 196 = **1,346** [1]; selling price 1,346 × 1.40 = **1,884.40** [1].

**4.** Cost is 80% of the selling price [1]; selling price 1,346 ÷ 0.80 [1] = **1,682.50** [1].

**5.** A margin of 20% means profit is 20 for every 80 of cost [1]; mark-up = 20 ÷ 80 = **25%** [1].

**6. (a)** Materials 800 × 1.80 = 1,440 [1]; labour 50 × 13 = 650 [1]; set-up cost 220 [1]; overheads 40 × 5 = 200 [1]; total **2,510** [1].

**(b)** Cost per bag 2,510 ÷ 800 = **3.14** [1]; selling price 3.1375 × 1.35 = 4.24 [1] (**4.24** to the nearest cent) [1].

**(c)** Materials 1,600 × 1.80 = 2,880; labour 90 × 13 = 1,170; set-up 220; overheads 70 × 5 = 350; total 4,620 [3]; cost per bag 4,620 ÷ 1,600 = **2.89** [1]. The set-up cost of 220 is spread over more bags (0.14 per bag instead of 0.28), so the cost per bag falls [1].

**7. (a)** Estimated cost: materials 900 + labour 560 (40 × 14) + overhead 320 (40 × 8) = **1,780** [2]. Actual cost: materials 960 + labour 616 (44 × 14) + overhead 352 (44 × 8) = **1,928** [2].

**(b)** Difference 1,928 − 1,780 = **148** [1]; 148 ÷ 1,780 × 100 = **8.3%** [1].

**8.** Job 410: 350 + 12 × 14 (168) + 12 × 8 (96) = **614** [2]. Job 411: 210 + 5 × 14 (70) + 5 × 8 (40) = **320** [2]. Total work in progress **934**, shown as inventory in current assets in the statement of financial position [1].

**9.** For job costing: it gives a detailed cost for every order and shows which are profitable [1]; it helps price quotations where each order differs [1]. Against: standard products are made in large quantities and do not need a cost sheet for each unit [1]; recording each job is slow and costly [1]; batch or process costing gives the cost per unit more simply [1]. Judgement: job costing is not suitable for standard products; batch costing or process costing would be better, although job costing might be used for special orders [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out overhead from the total cost (Questions 3 and 7).
- Confusing mark-up with margin (Questions 4 and 5).
- Dividing the batch cost by the wrong number of units (Question 6).
- Forgetting overhead when valuing work in progress (Question 8).
- Writing that the larger batch costs less in total, when it is the cost per unit that falls (Question 6).

## Approaching these questions

Use the job cost sheet layout: direct materials, direct labour, direct expenses, prime cost, overhead, total cost, profit, price. Write a working for each line. For batches, find the total, then divide by the number of units. State whether you are using mark-up or margin before you calculate the price.
