**This guide covers content area 3.1.1.5 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), Belief in Predestination (Al-Qadr): "the meaning, importance and influence of beliefs about predestination for Muslims".** The prescribed passage is a Hadith from Sahih al-Bukhari 1362, Book 23, Hadith 115. Predestination is among the hardest ideas in the course, because it seems to clash with free will. A good answer states the belief precisely, shows how Muslims reconcile it with responsibility, and explains how it affects daily life.

## What Al-Qadr means

*Qadr* means measure, decree or power. Belief in Al-Qadr (also called *qada wa qadar*, decree and measure) is the belief that Allah knows everything before it happens, has written it, wills it and creates it. Nothing happens outside His knowledge and permission. The Qur'an says, "Indeed, all things We created with predestination" (54:49) and "No disaster strikes upon the earth or among yourselves except that it is in a register before We bring it into being" (57:22).

It is the sixth of the key beliefs, and it is the belief that the Hadith of Jibril phrases as faith "in the divine decree, its good and its bad".

## The four levels of belief in Al-Qadr

Scholars often explain the belief in four steps, which makes it easier to understand and to write about.

| Level | Arabic | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Knowledge | *Ilm* | Allah knows everything that was, is and will be | He knows what you will choose tomorrow |
| 2. Writing | *Kitabah* | Allah has recorded it in the Preserved Tablet (*Al-Lawh al-Mahfuz*) | The register in 57:22 |
| 3. Will | *Mashee'ah* | Nothing happens unless Allah wills it | "You do not will except that Allah wills" (76:30) |
| 4. Creation | *Khalq* | Allah is the Creator of all things, including the means by which events happen | "Allah created you and what you do" (37:96) |

These levels do not remove human choice. They describe Allah's knowledge and power, not a force that makes people act against their will.

## Free will and responsibility

The main question is: if everything is decreed, how can people be blamed? Muslim teachers give several answers that you can use in an exam.

1. **Knowing is not forcing.** Allah's knowledge of what a person will choose does not remove the choice, just as a teacher who knows a student will fail does not force the failure.
2. **The Qur'an gives humans choice.** "And say, 'The truth is from your Lord, so whoever wills – let him believe; and whoever wills – let him disbelieve'" (18:29). People are told to choose, and are then judged by their choices.
3. **Allah does not burden anyone beyond capacity.** "Allah does not require of any soul more than what it can afford" (2:286). If a person were compelled, judgement would be unjust, and Allah is never unjust.
4. **Muslims act on the means.** The Prophet (pbuh) is reported to have told a man who left his camel untied and said he trusted in Allah, "Tie it and trust in Allah" (Tirmidhi). Trust is not the same as doing nothing.

A Muslim therefore holds two truths together: Allah's decree covers everything, and humans are responsible for their choices.

**Different emphases in history.** In early Islamic theology, some groups stressed decree so strongly that human action seemed forced (Jabriyya), and others stressed free will so strongly that Allah's prior knowledge seemed limited (Qadariyya). Mainstream Sunni teaching holds that Allah creates all things and humans "acquire" their actions by their choices. Shi'a Muslims emphasise Allah's justice ('Adl) as a root of religion, and stress that humans are free agents who will be judged. All traditions accept that Allah is just and that people are accountable.

## The prescribed passage: Sahih al-Bukhari 1362

In the Hadith, the companions are at a funeral at Baqi' al-Gharqad in Madinah. The Prophet (pbuh) sits and scratches the ground with a stick, then says that everyone has a place assigned in Paradise or Hell, and that it is determined whether they will be among the blessed or the wretched. A man asks: should we then rely on what is written and leave our deeds? The Prophet (pbuh) replies that good deeds are made easy for the blessed and bad deeds are made easy for the wretched, and he recites the verses that begin "As for him who gives (in charity) and is Allah-fearing and believes in the Best reward from Allah" (92:5-6).

**How to read the Hadith:**

- **The decree:** Allah's knowledge includes each person's end.
- **The objection:** "Then why act?" is the question that every student asks.
- **The answer:** do not use the decree as an excuse. A person who gives, fears Allah and believes is led by Allah toward ease; their effort is itself part of the decree.
- **The setting:** a funeral. The reminder of death gives urgency to the lesson.
- **The verses:** the Prophet (pbuh) turns attention from what is hidden to what a person can do now: give, be conscious of Allah, believe.

The practical meaning is that nobody knows what is written for them, so everyone should act as someone who hopes for Paradise.

## Why the belief is important

- **It completes faith.** Without it a Muslim's picture of Allah's knowledge and power would be incomplete.
- **It protects Tawhid.** Allah alone controls events; luck, stars and spirits do not.
- **It explains hardship.** Suffering is not a sign of abandonment but a test within Allah's wisdom (2:155).
- **It gives security.** The Prophet (pbuh) taught, "Know that what has missed you was never going to befall you, and what has befallen you was never going to miss you" (Tirmidhi).

## How the belief influences Muslims

| Situation | Response shaped by Al-Qadr |
|---|---|
| Illness or loss | Patience (*sabr*), saying "Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un" (2:156) |
| Success | Gratitude (*shukr*) and humility, not pride |
| Failure | No despair, learn and try again |
| Planning | Make the best effort, then rely on Allah (*tawakkul*) |
| Fear of the future | Calm, because Allah's knowledge covers it |
| Wrongdoing | No excuse "it was written"; repent and take responsibility |

A well-known example from early Islam: Hazrat Umar (RA) was travelling to Syria when he heard of a plague there and turned back. A companion asked, "Are you fleeing from the decree of Allah?" He answered, "Yes, we flee from the decree of Allah to the decree of Allah." This shows that taking precautions is part of belief in the decree and does not oppose it.

## Common misunderstandings

| Statement | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| "If everything is written, there is no point in trying." | The Prophet (pbuh) rejected this in the Hadith: act, since deeds are made easy for what a person was created for. |
| "Allah forces people to sin." | Allah is just and does not burden a soul beyond ability; people choose their actions. |
| "Prayer cannot change anything." | A Hadith in Tirmidhi says that nothing repels the decree except supplication; Muslims therefore pray as part of the means. |
| "Fatalism is the same as trust." | Trust (tawakkul) follows effort; fatalism abandons it. |

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** ways in which belief in Al-Qadr influences the life of a Muslim. **[4]**

> A Muslim who believes in Al-Qadr is patient when something bad happens [1], because they believe it is within Allah's knowledge and a test from Him, so they do not lose hope [1]. It also makes a Muslim grateful and humble when they succeed [1], because they know that success happens by Allah's will and is not the result of their own power alone [1].

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why belief in Al-Qadr does not remove human responsibility. Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority. **[6]**

> Muslims believe that Allah knows what a person will choose, but knowing is not the same as forcing [1]. A person still chooses to do good or wrong and will be judged by what they chose [1]. Also, the Qur'an says that Allah does not burden a soul beyond what it can bear, so humans must have real ability to act [1], and a just God would not judge people for something they were forced to do [1]. In the Hadith from Bukhari, a man asks if people should stop acting because of what is written, and the Prophet (pbuh) says good deeds are made easy for the blessed [2], which shows that the decree does not cancel effort.

## Common mistakes

- **Using "fate" with no explanation.** Say what Allah knows, writes, wills and creates.
- **Making Muslims sound passive.** Show effort followed by trust.
- **Forgetting both sides.** State the decree and human responsibility in the same answer.
- **Not using the passage.** In a 12-mark answer, use the question "should we not depend on what has been written?" and the Prophet's reply.
