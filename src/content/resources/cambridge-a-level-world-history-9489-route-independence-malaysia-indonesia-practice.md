## Section A

**1.** Give three reasons why Britain wanted the Bangkok Treaty of 1909. **[3]**

**2.** Explain two ways in which British rule polarised Malayan society. **[4]**

**3.** Identify three effects of the Japanese occupation on Malaya. **[3]**

**4.** Explain two reasons why the Malayan Union was abandoned. **[4]**

**5.** Identify three provisions of the Merdeka Constitution of 1957. **[3]**

**6.** Give three effects of the Ethical Policy on the growth of Indonesian nationalism. **[3]**

**7.** Identify three ways in which the Japanese encouraged Indonesian nationalism. **[3]**

**8.** Identify three challenges facing Indonesia in the 1950s. **[3]**

---

## Section B

**9.** "Before 1941, Malay nationalism was more concerned with the threat from immigrant communities than with British rule." Assess this view. **[30]**

**10.** Evaluate the reasons for the formation of the Federation of Malaysia in 1963 and for Singapore's separation in 1965. **[30]**

**11.** "Sukarno was the most important figure in the development of Indonesian nationalism up to 1945." Assess this view. **[30]**

**12.** Assess the reasons why the Dutch failed to re-establish control of Indonesia after 1945. **[30]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: to secure the northern frontier of British Malaya against rival European powers (1); to gain the rubber, rice and tin potential of the northern states (1); to bring all the Malay states under British influence (1); to settle relations with Siam, which received the end of British extraterritorial rights and a railway loan, so that it remained a friendly buffer state (1). **[3]**

**2.** Two ways, 2 marks each: different communities were placed in different economic roles -- Malays in farming and administration, Chinese in tin and trade, Indians on rubber estates -- so they rarely mixed (2); separate vernacular school systems in Malay, Chinese and Tamil taught different languages and loyalties (2); recognising Malays as the indigenous people with special privileges while relying on immigrant labour bred resentment on both sides (2). **[4]**

**3.** Any three, 1 mark each: British prestige was destroyed by the speed of defeat (1); Chinese were killed in the Sook Ching purges, while Malays were kept in the administration, deepening racial division (1); the communist-led MPAJA became the main resistance movement and gained arms and support (1); Malay radicals in the KMM, PETA and KRIS gained experience and ambitions for independence (1); economic hardship and food shortages (1). **[3]**

**4.** Two reasons, 2 marks each: Malays protested strongly against the loss of the sultans' sovereignty, uniting behind Dato Onn in the newly formed UMNO (2); the citizenship terms threatened to make Malays a political minority, which Malay leaders and many former British officials opposed (2); Britain needed Malay co-operation, especially as communist unrest grew, so it replaced the Union with the Federation (1948) (2). **[4]**

**5.** Any three, 1 mark each: a federation with an elected king (the Yang di-Pertuan Agong) chosen by the rulers (1); Islam as the religion of the federation, with freedom of worship (1); Malay as the national language (1); a special position for Malays (1); citizenship by birth for those born after independence (1); a parliamentary system (1). **[3]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: Western education created an elite familiar with ideas of nationalism and self-determination (1); educated Indonesians found that the colonial system blocked their careers, creating resentment (1); schools and newspapers spread Malay as a common language (1); the advisory Volksraad gave nationalists a forum but showed the limits of Dutch reform (1). **[3]**

**7.** Any three, 1 mark each: they removed and interned the Dutch and banned the Dutch language (1); they gave Indonesians higher posts in the administration (1); they let Sukarno and Hatta lead Putera (1943), giving them a mass platform (1); they trained Indonesians in Peta (1943), creating a nationalist army (1); they promised independence in September 1944 and set up preparatory committees (1). **[3]**

**8.** Any three, 1 mark each: short-lived coalition cabinets and party rivalry (1); deadlock in the Constituent Assembly over Islam and the constitution (1); regional rebellions in Sumatra and Sulawesi (1957-58) (1); inflation and falling export earnings (1); ethnic tension, especially towards the Chinese (1); the Darul Islam revolt (1). **[3]**

**9.** Indicative content. **For:** the Malay associations and congresses of the 1920s-30s focused on protecting Malay rights against Chinese and Indian economic power and immigration; English-educated Malays and the aristocracy were loyal to the sultans and to Britain, seeing British protection as a guarantee of Malay privilege; Malay demands targeted citizenship and land for immigrants. **Against:** Kaum Muda criticised traditional elites and the colonial order indirectly; the KMM (1938) was openly anti-British and pan-Malay, and its leaders were arrested by the British in 1941. **Criterion:** distinguish mainstream and radical opinion, and measure by support. **Judgement:** the view fits mainstream Malay opinion, which was communal rather than anti-colonial; radical anti-British nationalism existed but was small before the Japanese occupation. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**10.** Indicative content. **Formation:** fear that an independent Singapore would fall to the left (Barisan Sosialis split from the PAP in 1961); the economic logic of a common market and Singapore's need for a hinterland; including Sarawak and Sabah to balance Singapore's Chinese population; British support for decolonising its remaining territories; the Cobbold Commission's findings on Borneo opinion. **Separation:** Lee Kuan Yew's "Malaysian Malaysia" challenged Malay special rights; the PAP contested the 1964 federal election and UMNO contested Singapore's; race riots in Singapore in 1964; disputes over finance, the common market and Singapore's contribution to federal revenue; Konfrontasi added pressure. **Judgement:** the same communal balance that justified merger also destroyed it. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**11.** Indicative content. **For:** founded the PNI (1927) and articulated independence through non-co-operation; his trial and exile made him a national symbol; led Putera under the Japanese; set out Pancasila (June 1945); proclaimed independence with Hatta (August 1945). **Against:** Hatta and Sjahrir built a cadre organisation and led the 1930s nationalist thinking; earlier movements -- Budi Utomo, Sarekat Islam, the PKI -- built mass politics before Sukarno; co-operative nationalists produced the Soetardjo Petition and Gapi; the Japanese occupation transformed the movement's scale; radical youth forced the timing of the proclamation. **Judgement:** Sukarno was the movement's most important unifying voice, but its growth depended on wider social change and on the occupation. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**12.** Indicative content. **Indonesian strength:** the Republic held Java and Sumatra with Peta-trained forces and mass support; fierce resistance at Surabaya (1945); guerrilla warfare after the police actions. **Dutch weakness:** the Netherlands had been occupied and impoverished; it depended initially on British forces. **Diplomacy:** Linggadjati (1946) recognised Republican authority and its collapse damaged Dutch credibility. **International pressure:** the UN Security Council intervened in 1947 and condemned the second police action (1948); the Republic's suppression of the Madiun revolt reassured the USA, which threatened to cut Marshall Aid. **Judgement:** Indonesian resistance made reconquest costly, but US and UN pressure made it impossible; strong answers show how Madiun linked the two. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**
