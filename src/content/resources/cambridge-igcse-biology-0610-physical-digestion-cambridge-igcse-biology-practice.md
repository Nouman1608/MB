These questions cover sub-topic **7.3 Physical digestion** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Statements 1 to 6 are Core. Questions on statement 7, the role of bile in emulsifying fats, are labelled **(Extended)**; a Core candidate can skip them. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. Experimental results are invented for practice.

Revise first with the [physical digestion study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-physical-digestion-cambridge-igcse-biology/) and the [physical digestion revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-physical-digestion-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Which statement describes physical digestion?

A: large insoluble molecules are broken into small soluble molecules

B: food is broken into smaller pieces without chemical change to the molecules

C: nutrients move from the intestines into the blood

D: enzymes break bonds in food molecules **[1]**

**2.** State the type of tooth used for gripping and tearing food. **[1]**

**3.** Name the hard outer layer of the crown of a tooth and the tissue that covers the root. **[2]**

**4.** State the **two** types of structure found in the pulp of a tooth. **[2]**

**5.** State how physical digestion helps chemical digestion. **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Name the liquid that emulsifies fats in the small intestine and state the organ that makes it. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A dentist's chart describes four teeth in one side of a person's lower jaw, labelled from front to back.

- Tooth W: thin, flat crown with a sharp straight edge.
- Tooth X: single pointed cusp.
- Tooth Y: broad crown with two cusps.
- Tooth Z: the largest, with a broad crown, several cusps and two roots.

**(a)** Identify teeth W, X, Y and Z.

**(b)** Describe how teeth Y and Z help digestion. **[6]**

**8.** Describe the structure of a tooth. Refer to the crown and the root. **[5]**

**9.** A student cuts a block of jelly with sides of 3 cm into cubes with sides of 1 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the surface area of the original block.

**(b)** Calculate the total surface area of the small cubes.

**(c)** Use your answers to explain why chewing food speeds up chemical digestion. **[6]**

**10.** Describe the role of the stomach in physical digestion and explain how this role helps the digestion of protein. **[3]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A student sets up three tubes, each containing 5 cm³ of milk and an alkaline pink indicator. The indicator becomes colourless when fatty acids are produced. All tubes are kept at 35 °C.

| Tube | Added | Time for colour to disappear / min |
|---|---|---|
| P | 1 cm³ lipase + 1 cm³ water | 12 |
| Q | 1 cm³ lipase + 1 cm³ bile | 5 |
| R | 1 cm³ bile + 1 cm³ water | no change |

**(a)** Explain why the colour disappeared in tube P.

**(b)** Explain why the colour disappeared faster in tube Q than in tube P.

**(c)** State what tube R shows about bile.

**(d)** Explain why water was added to tubes P and R. **[7]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A student writes: "Bile digests fat into fatty acids and glycerol."

**(a)** Explain what is wrong with this statement.

**(b)** Outline the role of bile in the digestion of fats and oils. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**2.** Canine [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** Enamel [1]; cement [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**4.** Nerves [1]; blood vessels [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** It increases the surface area of food for enzymes to act on [1]. Total: 1 mark.

*Examiner insight:* "Makes food smaller" alone does not score; the mark is for surface area linked to enzymes.

**6.** *(Extended)* Bile [1]; liver [1]. Total: 2 marks.

*Examiner insight:* The gall bladder stores bile but does not make it.

**7. (a)** W incisor [1]; X canine [1]; Y premolar [1]; Z molar [1].

**(b)** Their broad surfaces with cusps chew and grind food [1], breaking it into smaller pieces with a larger surface area for enzymes [1]. Total: 6 marks.

**8.** The crown is covered by enamel [1]. Beneath the enamel is dentine [1]. The pulp in the centre contains nerves and blood vessels [1]. The root is covered by cement [1]. The root is embedded in the jaw bone and surrounded by the gum [1]. Total: 5 marks.

*Examiner insight:* Position words such as "covers", "beneath" and "centre" turn a list of names into a description.

**9. (a)** 6 × 3 × 3 = **54 cm²** [1].

**(b)** 27 cubes [1]; 27 × 6 × 1 × 1 = **162 cm²** [1].

**(c)** Cutting (or chewing) increases the surface area, here three times as much (162 ÷ 54 = 3) [1]; enzymes act on the surface of food, so more food molecules are exposed to enzymes at once [1]; so chemical digestion is faster [1]. Total: 6 marks.

*Examiner insight:* Students often forget that a 3 cm block makes 27 small cubes, not 9; count 3 × 3 × 3.

**10.** The muscular wall of the stomach contracts to churn the food [1], breaking it into smaller pieces and mixing it with gastric juice [1]. This increases the surface area for protease to act on, so protein is digested faster [1]. Total: 3 marks.

**11.** *(Extended)* **(a)** Lipase digested fat in the milk into fatty acids (and glycerol) [1]; the fatty acids lowered the pH, so the indicator lost its pink colour [1].

**(b)** Bile emulsified the fat into smaller droplets [1], giving a larger surface area for lipase [1], so fatty acids were produced faster [1].

**(c)** Bile does not digest fat / contains no enzyme [1].

**(d)** To keep the total volume the same in every tube, so only the substance being tested differs [1]. Total: 7 marks.

**12.** *(Extended)* **(a)** Bile does not digest fat chemically: it contains no enzymes and does not change fat molecules [1]; lipase digests fat into fatty acids and glycerol [1].

**(b)** Bile emulsifies fats and oils, breaking large droplets into small droplets [1], which increases the surface area for lipase, so chemical digestion is faster [1]. Total: 4 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying physical digestion breaks down molecules.
- Linking the surface area to absorption instead of enzymes.
- Swapping canine and molar functions.
- Listing tooth tissues with no positions.
- Leaving blood vessels out of the pulp.
- Counting 9 cubes instead of 27 when a 3 cm cube is cut into 1 cm cubes.
- (Extended) Calling bile an enzyme, or saying the gall bladder makes it.
