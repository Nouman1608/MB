These notes condense section 10.02, Perimeter calculations, of OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026). The section has three statements: 10.02a Perimeter of rectilinear shapes, 10.02b Circumference of a circle (with arc length of a sector), and 10.02c Perimeter of composite shapes. All of it is content for **both tiers**. You must know circumference = 2πr = πd; it is not given in the paper.

Worked examples are in the [10.02 study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-perimeter-calculations/) and exam-style questions in the [10.02 practice questions](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-perimeter-calculations-practice/). For perimeter alongside units, maps and bearings, use the [units, compound measures, maps, bearings and perimeter revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-units-compound-measures-maps-bearings-perimeter-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Quick example |
|---|---|---|
| Perimeter | Total distance round the outside edge of a 2D shape | A 7 cm by 4 cm rectangle: 22 cm |
| Rectilinear shape | A shape whose straight sides all meet at right angles | L-shapes, T-shapes, steps |
| Circumference | The perimeter of a circle | Diameter 10 cm: 10π cm |
| Radius | Distance from the centre to the circle | Half the diameter |
| Diameter | Chord through the centre | Twice the radius |
| Arc | Part of the circumference | The curved edge of a sector |
| Sector | Region bounded by two radii and an arc | A "pizza slice" |
| Semicircle | Half a circle; its arc is half the circumference | Diameter 8 cm: arc 4π cm |
| Composite shape | A shape made from simpler shapes joined together | Rectangle with a semicircle on top |
| In terms of π | An exact answer with π left as a symbol | 6π + 12 |

## Formulae to know

```
circumference      C = πd = 2πr
reverse            d = C ÷ π        r = C ÷ (2π)
arc length         (θ/360) × 2πr
semicircle arc     ½ × πd
quarter-circle arc ¼ × 2πr = ½πr
sector perimeter   arc + 2r
semicircle perimeter  ½πd + d
```

## Rectilinear shapes: the checklist

1. Mark every edge, including unlabelled ones.
2. Find missing horizontal edges from the horizontal totals: edges going right add up to edges going left.
3. Do the same for vertical edges.
4. Add every edge once, going round in order.

**Short cuts.**

- A rectangular notch cut from a **corner** leaves the perimeter the same as the outer rectangle.
- A notch or slot cut from the **middle** of an edge adds twice its depth.
- With algebraic sides, collect like terms: a 3x by (x + 2) rectangle has perimeter 2(4x + 2) = 8x + 4.

## Circles and arcs: the checklist

- Check whether you are given the radius or the diameter, and choose 2πr or πd to match.
- For an arc, write the fraction θ/360 first, then multiply by the full circumference.
- To find an angle or a radius from an arc, set the arc formula equal to the given length and solve.
- Cancel π when both sides contain it; the answer stays exact.
- For a wheel, distance travelled in one turn = circumference. Number of turns = distance ÷ circumference, with both in the same unit.

**Fractions of 360° worth knowing.**

| Angle | Fraction of circle |
|---|---|
| 30° | 1/12 |
| 40° | 1/9 |
| 45° | 1/8 |
| 60° | 1/6 |
| 72° | 1/5 |
| 90° | 1/4 |
| 120° | 1/3 |
| 150° | 5/12 |
| 270° | 3/4 |

## Composite shapes: the checklist

1. Trace the outside edge with your finger and list each piece: straight edge or arc.
2. Leave out any line where two parts join inside the shape.
3. When a part is **cut out**, its edge becomes part of the new outline. Cutting out can make the perimeter longer.
4. For part of a ring, the straight ends are the difference of the two radii.
5. Add the straight edges and the arcs separately, then combine: for example 40 + 6π.

## Exam traps

- **Radius and diameter swapped:** 2π × diameter gives double the circumference.
- **Sector perimeter as arc only:** add the two radii.
- **Semicircle perimeter as arc only:** add the diameter.
- **Internal lines counted:** the join between a rectangle and the semicircle on it is not on the perimeter.
- **Unlabelled sides ignored** in an L-shape or T-shape.
- **Wheel turns rounded up** when the question asks for complete turns.
- **Mixed units:** convert a wheel diameter in cm to m before dividing a distance in m or km.
- **Early rounding:** keep the full calculator value until the last line.
- **Exact form lost:** if the question says "in terms of π", writing 25.1 instead of 8π loses the mark.

## Quick self-test

1. A 14 cm by 10 cm rectangle has a 4 cm by 3 cm rectangle cut from one corner. Find the perimeter of the shape left.
2. An 18 cm by 6 cm rectangle has a slot 4 cm wide and 2.5 cm deep cut from the middle of a long edge. Find the new perimeter.
3. Find the circumference of a circle of radius 8 cm, in terms of π.
4. Find the circumference of a circle of diameter 2.4 m, to 3 significant figures.
5. A circle has circumference 88 cm. Find its diameter to 3 significant figures.
6. Find the perimeter of a semicircle of diameter 16 cm, in terms of π.
7. Find the arc length of a sector with radius 12 cm and angle 75°, in terms of π.
8. Find the perimeter of a sector with radius 5 cm and angle 216°, in terms of π.
9. A sector of a circle of radius 9 cm has arc length 4π cm. Find the angle of the sector.
10. A sector with angle 60° has arc length 7π cm. Find the radius.
11. A wheel of diameter 60 cm rolls 500 m. How many complete turns does it make?
12. Find the perimeter of a quarter circle of radius 10 cm, to 3 significant figures.

### Answers

1. Corner cut, so 2 × (14 + 10) = **48 cm**.
2. 2 × (18 + 6) = 48, plus 2 × 2.5 = 5, so **53 cm**.
3. 2π × 8 = **16π cm**.
4. 2.4π = 7.539… = **7.54 m**.
5. 88 ÷ π = 28.01… = **28.0 cm**.
6. Arc ½ × π × 16 = 8π, plus diameter 16: **8π + 16 cm**.
7. 75/360 = 5/24; 5/24 × 24π = **5π cm**.
8. 216/360 = 3/5; arc = 3/5 × 10π = 6π; perimeter **6π + 10 cm**.
9. θ/360 × 18π = 4π, so θ/360 = 4/18 = 2/9 and θ = **80°**.
10. 60/360 × 2πr = πr/3 = 7π, so **r = 21 cm**.
11. Circumference 0.6π = 1.884… m; 500 ÷ 1.884… = 265.2…, so **265 complete turns**.
12. Arc ¼ × 2π × 10 = 5π = 15.70…; plus two radii 20: 35.70… = **35.7 cm**.
