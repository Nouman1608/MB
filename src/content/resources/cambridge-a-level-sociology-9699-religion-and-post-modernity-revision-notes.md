Condensed recall for Cambridge International A Level Sociology (9699) sub-topic 12.3, *Religion
and post-modernity*, in Paper 4 Section C. Full explanations and a worked essay plan are in the
[religion and post-modernity study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-sociology-9699-religion-and-post-modernity/).

## The five syllabus points

1. Whether there has been a growth in privatised religion.
2. Spiritual shopping and what it shows about religiosity today.
3. Whether resacralisation (printed in the syllabus as "resacrilisation") is a feature of
   post-modernity.
4. The significance of NRMs and New Age ideas.
5. Explanations for the growth of fundamentalism: cultural defence, cultural transition,
   disengagement, marginality, religious revival.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Post-modernity** | Society marked by fragmentation, choice, consumption and media culture |
| **Metanarrative** | A grand story claiming to explain everything; Lyotard's "incredulity towards metanarratives" |
| **Privatised religion** | Religion moved from public institutions to private life (Luckmann, 1967) |
| **Religion online / online religion** | Top-down information from organisations / interactive practice created by users (Helland) |
| **Spiritual shopping** | Selecting beliefs and practices from a wide market to suit the individual |
| **Cultural amnesia** | Loss of religion handed down as a "chain of memory" (Hervieu-Léger) |
| **Resacralisation** | The return of the sacred in new or renewed forms |
| **Self-spirituality** | The sacred located within the self (Heelas) |
| **Detraditionalisation** | Personal experience replaces external religious authority (Heelas) |
| **Resistance identity** | Identity formed by groups who feel excluded by global change (Castells) |
| **Fundamentalism** | Movement claiming a return to the literal truth of texts, with sharp boundaries against outsiders |

## Privatised religion

- **For:** Luckmann's invisible religion; personal prayer and meditation; online religion.
- **Against:** private belief is hard to measure; Bruce argues it is weak because it lacks social
  support; religion remains public and political in many societies.

## Spiritual shopping

- **Hervieu-Léger:** cultural amnesia breaks the chain of memory, so people assemble their own
  religion.
- **Roof:** a "spiritual marketplace" of American baby-boom seekers.
- **Lyon:** consumer culture and media disembed religion from place and community.
- **Critique:** may describe affluent Western groups more than global religion; Bruce sees loosely
  held belief as secularisation, as it has little effect on behaviour and is rarely passed on.

## Resacralisation

- **Evidence:** Pentecostal growth in Latin America (David Martin, *Tongues of Fire*, 1990);
  holistic spirituality (Kendal Project); NRMs, New Age and fundamentalism; religion in public
  life.
- **Debate:** a feature of post-modernity, or proof that secularisation was never universal?
  Growth in the global South may reflect urbanisation and insecurity, which are features of
  modernisation.

## NRMs and New Age

- **Barker (1984):** most who attended Unification Church workshops did not join, challenging the
  brainwashing claim.
- **Why people join:** marginality and relative deprivation; social change and anomie; a search
  for meaning or self-improvement.
- **Niebuhr (1929):** sects tend to die out or become denominations after the first generation.
- **Heelas (1996):** New Age = self-spirituality + detraditionalisation.
- **Significance:** small numbers but revealing: demand for meaning persists; obedience gives way
  to self-development.

## Fundamentalism: five explanations plus theorists

| Explanation | One-line summary |
|---|---|
| Cultural defence | Religion protects identity against an external threat (Bruce) |
| Cultural transition | Religion eases migration or rapid change |
| Disengagement | Withdrawal from a society seen as corrupt |
| Marginality | The deprived seek certainty and hope (theodicy of disprivilege) |
| Religious revival | Renewal against perceived moral decline |

- **Giddens:** "tradition defended in the traditional way".
- **Bauman:** anxiety caused by freedom and choice draws some to certainty.
- **Castells (1997):** resistance identity versus project identity.
- **Bruce:** Western fundamentalism reacts to internal diversity; elsewhere it reacts to change
  imposed from outside.

## Exam traps

- Assuming spiritual shopping disproves secularisation; it can be read either way.
- Wallis's typology described with no link to "significance for understanding religion today".
- The five fundamentalism explanations listed without saying where each fits best.
- "Fundamentalist" used without a definition or a specific case.
- "Post-modern" used loosely to mean recent.
- Forgetting the global picture: most growth in religion is outside the West.

## Self-test

1. What did Lyotard mean by "incredulity towards metanarratives"?
2. What was Luckmann's argument in *The Invisible Religion*?
3. Distinguish religion online from online religion.
4. What is cultural amnesia?
5. Give one criticism of the idea of spiritual shopping.
6. Define resacralisation and give one example.
7. What did Barker's study of the Unification Church suggest?
8. What did Niebuhr predict about sects?
9. Name Heelas's two core features of New Age thought.
10. Explain the cultural transition explanation of fundamentalism.
11. What does Castells mean by resistance identity?
12. Why might the growth of Pentecostalism be linked to modernisation rather than post-modernity?

### Answers

1. Grand stories claiming to explain everything, including religious ones, lose authority.
2. Religion was not disappearing but moving into the private sphere, where individuals build their
   own systems of meaning.
3. Religion online is top-down information from organisations; online religion is interactive
   practice and community created by users.
4. The breakdown of religion handed down through families and communities as a chain of memory.
5. It may describe affluent Western groups only; or loosely held beliefs have little effect and
   indicate secularisation (Bruce).
6. The return of the sacred; for example, Pentecostal growth in Latin America.
7. Most people attending recruitment workshops did not join, so membership is a choice rather than
   the result of brainwashing.
8. Sects tend to be short-lived or become denominations once the founding generation passes.
9. Self-spirituality and detraditionalisation.
10. Religion helps people undergoing migration or rapid change to cope and to keep their identity.
11. Identity formed by groups who feel excluded or threatened by global change, defending
    themselves against it.
12. It often grows among people moving to cities and facing insecurity, which are processes of
    modernisation.
