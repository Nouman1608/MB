All businesses are invented. Money is in dollars.

## Section A

**1.** Explain **two** reasons why a business needs to use investment appraisal. **[4]**

**2.** A gym buys equipment for $120,000. Net cash inflows are $30,000, $40,000, $35,000 and $45,000 in Years 1 to 4. Calculate the payback period in years and months. **[3]**

**3.** A laundry buys machines for $150,000. They last 4 years and will then be sold for $30,000. Net cash inflows are $50,000, $55,000, $60,000 and $55,000, not including the sale. Calculate the ARR. **[4]**

**4.** A printer considers a machine costing $80,000. Net cash inflows are $30,000, $35,000 and $40,000 in Years 1 to 3. Discount factors at 10% are 0.909, 0.826 and 0.751. Calculate the NPV and state whether the machine is worthwhile on this measure. **[4]**

**5.** Explain why the discount rate chosen affects the NPV of a project. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Kenmore Farms has $250,000 to invest. Its targets are a payback within 4 years and an ARR of at least 10%. Discount factors at 10% are 0.909, 0.826, 0.751, 0.683 and 0.621.

Project M (milking robot): net cash inflows of $60,000 a year for 5 years; residual value $50,000 at the end of Year 5.

Project G (greenhouse): net cash inflows of $100,000, $90,000, $60,000, $30,000 and $20,000; no residual value.

**(a)** Calculate the payback period of each project. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the ARR of each project. **[4]**

**(c)** Calculate the NPV of each project at 10%. **[5]**

**(d)** Recommend which project Kenmore should choose. Justify your answer using your results and qualitative factors. **[10]**

**7.** Evaluate the usefulness of net present value as a method of investment appraisal for a business. **[10]**

**8.** A retailer is considering opening a store in a new country. Explain **two** qualitative factors it should consider in addition to the investment appraisal results. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Capital projects involve large sums that are hard to reverse [1], so a poor decision could seriously damage the business's finances [1]. Funds are limited, so choosing one project means giving up another [1]; appraisal gives a consistent way to compare competing projects [1].

**2.** Cumulative: 30,000; 70,000; 105,000 [1]. Still needed after Year 3 = 15,000; 15,000 ÷ 45,000 = 0.333 of a year = 4 months [1]. Payback = **3 years 4 months** [1].

**3.** Total inflows including sale = 220,000 + 30,000 = 250,000; total profit = 250,000 − 150,000 = 100,000 [1]. Average profit = 100,000 ÷ 4 = 25,000 [1]. Average investment = (150,000 + 30,000) ÷ 2 = 90,000 [1]. ARR = 25,000 ÷ 90,000 × 100 = **27.8%** [1].

**4.** Present values: 27,270 + 28,910 + 30,040 = 86,220 [1] [1]. NPV = 86,220 − 80,000 = **$6,220** [1]. Positive, so worthwhile at a 10% discount rate [1].

**5.** A higher discount rate gives smaller discount factors, so future cash flows have lower present values [1]; NPV falls, and a project could move from positive to negative, especially if its cash arrives late [1].

**6. (a)** M: 250,000 ÷ 60,000 = 4.17 years, about **4 years 2 months** [1]. G: cumulative 100,000 then 190,000; 60,000 still needed equals Year 3's inflow [1], so payback is **exactly 3 years** [1].

**(b)** M: total profit = 300,000 + 50,000 − 250,000 = 100,000; average 20,000; average investment (250,000 + 50,000) ÷ 2 = 150,000 [1]; ARR = **13.3%** [1]. G: total profit = 300,000 − 250,000 = 50,000; average 10,000; average investment 125,000 [1]; ARR = **8%** [1].

**(c)** M: present value of inflows = 60,000 × (0.909 + 0.826 + 0.751 + 0.683 + 0.621) = 60,000 × 3.790 = 227,400 [1]; residual 50,000 × 0.621 = 31,050 [1]; NPV = 258,450 − 250,000 = **$8,450** [1]. G: present values 90,900 + 74,340 + 45,060 + 20,490 + 12,420 = 243,210 [1]; NPV = **−$6,790** [1].

**(d)** M meets the ARR target (13.3% against 10%) and has a positive NPV [1], so it adds value at 10% [1]. G fails both: an ARR of 8% and a negative NPV [1]. However, M's payback of 4 years 2 months is just outside the 4-year limit [1], while G pays back in 3 years, which suits Kenmore if cash is tight [1]. M's NPV depends on the $50,000 residual value; without it, NPV would be negative [1], so the forecast of the robot's resale value is critical. Qualitative factors: the robot may reduce reliance on hard-to-recruit farm labour and give consistent milking times [1]; the greenhouse's returns depend on weather and crop prices, which are uncertain [1]. Judgement: choose M [1], because it is the only project that adds value and meets the ARR target, provided Kenmore can accept a payback slightly over 4 years and is confident in the residual value [1]. **[22]**

**7.** NPV allows for the time value of money, so later cash flows count for less [1], making it more realistic than payback or ARR [1]. It uses all cash flows over the project's life [1] and gives a value in dollars that shows how much a project adds [1]. However, the result depends on the discount rate chosen [1], and choosing a rate involves judgement about the cost of finance and risk [1]. It is harder for non-specialist managers to understand [1]. Like all methods, it relies on forecasts, which are less reliable for distant years [1], and it ignores qualitative factors [1]. Judgement: NPV is the most useful single method, but it should be used with payback for liquidity and with qualitative judgement, especially when the NPV is close to zero [1]. **[10]**

**8.** Factor 1: cultural differences and local tastes [1]; if the store's range or style does not suit local customers, sales may fall short of the forecasts used in the appraisal [1]. Factor 2: political and legal stability in the new country [1]; changes in regulations or taxes could raise costs or restrict trading, adding risk not shown in the numbers [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving payback as a decimal when years and months are asked for.
- Forgetting the residual value in ARR or NPV.
- Discounting the initial cost.
- Recommending a project on one method without noting conflicting results.
- Giving qualitative factors that are not linked to the business in the question.
