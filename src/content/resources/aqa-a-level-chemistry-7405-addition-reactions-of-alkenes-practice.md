These questions cover section **3.3.4.2 Addition reactions of alkenes** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Learn the ideas first in the [Addition reactions of alkenes study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-addition-reactions-of-alkenes/) and the [Addition reactions of alkenes revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-addition-reactions-of-alkenes-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, Br = 79.9.

## Questions

**1.** Define the term electrophile. **[1]**

**2.** Outline the mechanism for the reaction of ethene with hydrogen bromide. Include curly arrows, partial charges and the intermediate. **[4]**

**3.** Propene reacts with bromine at room temperature.

**(a)** Explain how a non-polar bromine molecule can act as an electrophile. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the organic product. **[1]**

**(c)** Outline the mechanism. **[3]**

**4.** Describe a test that distinguishes cyclohexene from cyclohexane, and give the result for each. **[2]**

**5.** Propene reacts with hydrogen bromide to give two products.

**(a)** Name the major product and the minor product. **[2]**

**(b)** Draw or describe the carbocation intermediate that leads to the major product and state its type. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the major product forms in greater amount. **[2]**

**6.** 2-methylbut-2-ene, (CH₃)₂C=CHCH₃, reacts with hydrogen bromide.

**(a)** Name the major product. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the minor product. **[1]**

**(c)** State the type of carbocation that leads to the major product. **[1]**

**7.** But-1-ene is shaken with cold concentrated sulfuric acid, and the product is then warmed with water.

**(a)** Name the major organic product of the first stage. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the major alcohol formed. **[1]**

**(c)** State why the alcohol obtained is a mixture of isomers. **[1]**

**8.** Ethene reacts with concentrated sulfuric acid. Outline the mechanism for the formation of ethyl hydrogensulfate. **[4]**

**9.** 8.40 g of propene (Mᵣ = 42.0) reacts with excess hydrogen bromide. The major product, 2-bromopropane (Mᵣ = 122.9), is collected and 19.7 g is obtained.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of propene. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of 2-bromopropane. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage yield. **[1]**

**10.** Two isomers, P and Q, have the molecular formula C₄H₈. P reacts with hydrogen bromide to give a single product, 2-bromobutane. Q reacts with hydrogen bromide to give a major product with a tertiary carbon bonded to bromine.

**(a)** Identify P, and explain why it gives only one product. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify Q, and name its major product. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why the major product of the addition of hydrogen chloride to 2-methylpropene is 2-chloro-2-methylpropane rather than 1-chloro-2-methylpropane. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** A species that accepts a pair of electrons to form a covalent bond [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Dipole shown on H–Br (Hδ⁺, Brδ⁻) [1]; arrow from the C=C bond to the H, and arrow from the H–Br bond to Br [1]; carbocation CH₃CH₂⁺ and Br⁻ shown [1]; arrow from a lone pair on Br⁻ to the C⁺, giving bromoethane [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* The first arrow starts on the double bond, not on the hydrogen bromide.

**3. (a)** The electrons in the C=C bond repel the electrons in the Br–Br bond [1]; this induces a dipole, making the nearer bromine atom δ+ so that it is attacked by the double bond [1]

**(b)** 1,2-dibromopropane [1]

**(c)** Arrow from the C=C bond to the nearer bromine atom [1]; arrow from the Br–Br bond to the farther bromine atom, giving Br⁻ [1]; arrow from a lone pair on Br⁻ to the carbocation, giving the product [1]. **[6]**

**4.** Add bromine water to each and shake [1]; cyclohexene decolourises it from orange to colourless, cyclohexane stays orange [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Major: 2-bromopropane [1]; minor: 1-bromopropane [1]

**(b)** CH₃–CH⁺–CH₃ [1]; a secondary carbocation [1]

**(c)** The secondary carbocation is more stable than the primary one [1], because the two alkyl groups attached to the positive carbon donate electron density and spread the charge, so it forms faster [1]. **[6]**

**6. (a)** 2-bromo-2-methylbutane [1]

**(b)** 2-bromo-3-methylbutane [1]

**(c)** Tertiary [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Butan-2-yl hydrogensulfate, CH₃CH₂CH(OSO₂OH)CH₃ [1]

**(b)** Butan-2-ol [1]

**(c)** The hydrogen can add to either carbon of the double bond, so butan-1-ol also forms as a minor product [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Arrow from the C=C bond to the H of H₂SO₄ [1]; arrow from the O–H bond to O, forming HSO₄⁻ and CH₃CH₂⁺ [1]; arrow from a lone pair on an oxygen of HSO₄⁻ to the C⁺ [1]; product CH₃CH₂OSO₂OH [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** n = 8.40 ÷ 42.0 = **0.200 mol** [1]

**(b)** mass = 0.200 × 122.9 = **24.58 g** [1]

**(c)** percentage yield = 19.7 ÷ 24.58 × 100 = **80.1 %** [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** P is but-2-ene [1]; it is symmetrical, so hydrogen adding to either carbon gives the same carbocation and the same product [1]

**(b)** Q is 2-methylpropene [1]; its major product is 2-bromo-2-methylpropane [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* But-1-ene also gives 2-bromobutane as its major product, but it would give a minor product too, so the single product points to but-2-ene.

**11.** Hydrogen adds to the CH₂ carbon to form the tertiary carbocation (CH₃)₃C⁺ [1]; adding to the other carbon would form the primary carbocation (CH₃)₂CHCH₂⁺ [1]; alkyl groups donate electron density to the positive carbon, and three groups stabilise the charge more than one [1]; so the tertiary carbocation forms faster, and chloride attacking it gives 2-chloro-2-methylpropane as the major product [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Starting the first curly arrow on the reagent instead of on the double bond.
- Putting the positive charge on the wrong carbon.
- Not mentioning an induced dipole for bromine.
- Giving the major product without justifying it by carbocation stability.
- Writing "clear" for the end colour of bromine water.
- Giving only one alcohol from but-1-ene and sulfuric acid, with no mention of the hydrolysis step.
