## 1. The objectives (item 1, a to f)

| Ref | Objective | Measure | Formula or note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1a | Economic growth | Real GDP growth | Real GDP = nominal GDP / deflator x 100 |
| 1b | Low, stable inflation | CPI | (new CPI - old CPI) / old CPI x 100 |
| 1c | Low unemployment | Unemployment rate | Unemployed / labour force x 100 |
| 1d | Current account equilibrium | Balance, % of GDP | Goods + services + primary income + secondary income |
| 1e | Balanced budget | Deficit, % of GDP | Spending - revenue; debt is the stock of past deficits |
| 1f | Greater income equality | Income shares, ratios | Top 20% share / bottom 20% share |

**Labour force** = employed + unemployed. People not looking for work are outside it.
**Low** = near the target. **Stable** = little variation from year to year.
**Equilibrium** = a balance small enough to finance sustainably, not necessarily exact zero.
**Budget balance** = revenue minus spending; a deficit adds to debt, a surplus can reduce it.
**Inflation target** = the rate a government or central bank aims for; "low" means near it and "stable" means close to it year after year.

## 2. The conflicts (item 2, a to d)

Use the chain: cause → mechanism → effect on the second objective → evaluation.

| Ref | Pair | Core chain |
|---|---|---|
| 2a | Inflation and unemployment | AD up → output up, unemployment down → wages and prices rise faster |
| 2b | Growth and environment | Output up → resource use and emissions up → external costs, depletion |
| 2c | Inflation and current account | Prices up relative to partners → exports fall, imports rise; growth also sucks in imports |
| 2d | Growth and income equality | Gains go mainly to higher incomes → gap widens; redistribution may weaken incentives |

### 2a. Short-run Phillips curve

- **Axes:** unemployment on the horizontal axis, inflation on the vertical axis.
- **Shape:** downward sloping, convex.
- **Movement along the curve:** caused by a change in aggregate demand.
- **Shift outwards (worse):** cost-push shock, higher expected inflation. **Shift inwards (better):** faster productivity growth.
- **Stagflation:** rising unemployment (falling output) together with rising inflation.

Worked reminder, using the straight-line model inflation = 9.5 - 1.5 x unemployment (a simplification of the curve):

```
Unemployment 3%: inflation = 9.5 - 4.5 = 5.0%
Unemployment 4%: inflation = 9.5 - 6.0 = 3.5%
Unemployment 6%: inflation = 9.5 - 9.0 = 0.5%
Each point off unemployment costs 1.5 points of inflation.
A cost-push shock adding 2 points at every rate: at 4%, inflation = 3.5 + 2.0 = 5.5%
```

Evaluate: the curve is short run; spare capacity flattens it; the cause of the shock matters; a convex shape means the last jobs are the most expensive in inflation.

### 2b. Growth and the environment

```
Output +4%, emissions per unit of output -2%:
emissions = 1.04 x 0.98 = 1.0192, a rise of 1.9%  (relative decoupling only)
Needed fall in intensity for no rise: 1 - 1/1.04 = 3.8%
```

Evaluate: GDP omits external costs; technology and carbon pricing narrow the conflict; richer countries may afford cleaner growth; some resource losses cannot be reversed.

### 2c. Inflation and the current account

```
Price channel: Country S prices +4.8%, partners +2.4%
Relative price = 1.048 / 1.024 - 1 = 2.3% dearer

Income channel: imports 228, income elasticity of demand for imports 2.0, income +3%
Import growth = 2.0 x 3% = 6%, so imports = 228 x 1.06 = 241.7
Goods balance (exports unchanged at 210) = 210 - 241.7 = -31.7, from -18
```

Evaluate: elasticities; exchange rate adjustment; whether the deficit is financed; whether inflation is domestic or imported.

### 2d. Growth and income equality

```
Bottom 20% mean income 7,000 grows 2% -> 7,140
Top 20% mean income 42,000 grows 4% -> 43,680
Ratio rises from 6.0 to 43,680 / 7,140 = 6.1
Gains: bottom 140 cu, top 1,680 cu
```

Evaluate: absolute against relative gains; who benefits from growth; tax design and incentives; very high inequality may reduce opportunity.

## 3. Method in steps: a conflict question

1. **Name both objectives** and say which way each moves ("unemployment falls but inflation rises").
2. **Start from the cause**: a rise in aggregate demand, a cost shock, faster growth, a change in the price level relative to partners.
3. **Chain the mechanism** in short links: spare labour used up → wages up → prices up.
4. **Add a figure** from the data if the question gives one, and say what it shows.
5. **Evaluate**: size, time, cause, spare capacity, and what else could be done.
6. **Judge**: say which objective matters more in this case and why.

### Evaluation prompts by conflict

| Conflict | Prompts |
|---|---|
| 2a | Is the economy near capacity? Demand-pull or cost-push? Has the curve shifted? |
| 2b | Does growth rely on fossil fuels? Is there a carbon price? Is the damage reversible? |
| 2c | How elastic is trade? Is the exchange rate fixed? Is the deficit financed by investment? |
| 2d | Who gains from growth? Absolute or relative gain? How would redistribution affect incentives? |

### When objectives help each other

Growth tends to lower unemployment and raise tax revenue, so the budget improves. Lower unemployment also cuts benefit spending. A strong answer says whether the objectives move together or apart in the case given, and does not assume a conflict.

## 4. Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Movement along the Phillips curve vs shift | Demand change vs supply shock or expectations |
| Real vs nominal | Real removes price changes |
| Relative vs absolute decoupling | Emissions rising more slowly vs not rising |
| Deficit vs debt | Annual flow vs accumulated stock |
| Absolute vs relative gain | Size of gain vs change in the gap |
| Unemployment rate vs number | Percentage of labour force vs count |

## 5. Quick self-test

1. Nominal GDP rises from 600 to 636 while the deflator rises from 100 to 103. Calculate real growth to 1 decimal place.
2. A CPI goes from 118.0 to 121.5. Calculate inflation to 1 decimal place.
3. There are 0.9m unemployed and 13.1m employed. Calculate the unemployment rate.
4. Goods exports 150, goods imports 140, net services +12, net primary income -9, net secondary income -4. Calculate the current account balance.
5. A budget has revenue 500 and spending 540. GDP is 2,000. Calculate the deficit as a percentage of GDP.
6. Using inflation = 9.5 - 1.5 x unemployment, find inflation at 5% unemployment.
7. Output rises 3% and emissions per unit of output fall 3%. Calculate the change in total emissions to 1 decimal place.
8. Inflation over four years is 1.0%, 6.5%, -2.0% and 4.5%. Calculate the average and say whether inflation is stable.
9. Top 20% hold 45% of income and bottom 20% hold 9%. Calculate the ratio.
10. State why a cost-push shock moves the short-run Phillips curve rather than moving the economy along it.

### Answers

1. 636/600 = 1.060; 1.060/1.03 - 1 = **2.9%**.
2. (121.5 - 118.0)/118.0 = **3.0%**.
3. Labour force 14.0m; 0.9/14.0 = **6.4%**.
4. Goods +10; +10 + 12 - 9 - 4 = **+9** (a surplus).
5. Deficit 40; 40/2,000 = **2.0%**.
6. 9.5 - 7.5 = **2.0%**.
7. 1.03 x 0.97 = 0.9991, so emissions change by **-0.1%** (absolute decoupling, just).
8. Average = 10.0/4 = **2.5%**. It is **not stable**: the rate swings by several points and turns negative (deflation).
9. 45/9 = **5.0**.
10. It changes the inflation rate at every level of unemployment, so the whole relationship moves.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Quoting nominal growth when real growth is asked.
- Dividing unemployment by the population instead of the labour force.
- Labelling the Phillips axes the wrong way round.
- Describing "inflation and unemployment" without saying which way each moves.
- Treating a movement along the curve and a shift as the same thing.
- Giving a conflict as certain, without time, size or cause.
- Mixing up deficit (flow) and debt (stock).
- Ignoring that a gap can widen while everyone's income rises.
