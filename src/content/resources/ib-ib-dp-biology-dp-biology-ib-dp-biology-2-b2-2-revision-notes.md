## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organelle | Discrete subunit of a cell with a specific function |
| Compartmentalization | Division of a cell into separate membrane-bound spaces |
| Cell fractionation | Breaking cells and separating their parts |
| Ultracentrifugation | Spinning at very high speed to pellet small structures |
| Post-transcriptional modification | Changes to RNA after transcription, such as removing introns |
| Lysosome | Vesicle containing hydrolytic enzymes |
| Phagocytic vacuole | Vacuole formed around an engulfed particle |
| Cristae (HL) | Folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane |
| Thylakoid (HL) | Flattened membrane sac in a chloroplast |
| Cisternae (HL) | Flattened membrane sacs of the ER and Golgi apparatus |
| Clathrin (HL) | Protein forming a cage-like coat that helps vesicles form |

## B2.2.1: organelles

- Count: nucleus, mitochondria, chloroplasts, ER, Golgi apparatus, vesicles, lysosomes, vacuoles, ribosomes.
- Do not count: cell wall (outside the cell), cytoskeleton (not discrete), cytoplasm (whole contents).

**Fractionation in steps**

1. Homogenise in **cold** (slows enzymes), **buffered** (stable pH), **isotonic** (no osmosis) solution.
2. Spin at low speed: nuclei pellet.
3. Spin the liquid above at medium speed: mitochondria (and chloroplasts in plants).
4. Spin at high speed: ribosomes and membrane fragments.
5. Test each pellet for marker molecules or enzymes.

## B2.2.2: nucleus and cytoplasm

| | Eukaryote | Prokaryote |
|---|---|---|
| Transcription | In nucleus | In cytoplasm |
| Translation | In cytoplasm | In cytoplasm, can start during transcription |
| Modification of mRNA before translation | Possible | Not possible |

## B2.2.3: compartments in the cytoplasm

Three advantages:

1. Enzymes and substrates concentrated, so faster reactions.
2. Conditions such as pH can differ from the cytoplasm.
3. Harmful enzymes kept away from other structures.

Lysosome + phagocytic vacuole: engulfed particle digested inside a membrane; products released to cytoplasm.

## B2.2.4 and B2.2.5 (HL only)

| | Mitochondrion | Chloroplast |
|---|---|---|
| Large membrane area | Cristae (inner membrane) | Thylakoid membranes |
| Small space for proton gradient | Intermembrane space | Thylakoid space |
| Enzyme-rich fluid | Matrix: link reaction, Krebs cycle | Stroma: Calvin cycle |
| Membranes | Double | Double envelope plus thylakoids |

**Why small spaces help:** concentration = number of protons ÷ volume, so a tiny volume reaches a steep gradient quickly.

## B2.2.6 to B2.2.9 (HL only)

- **Nuclear envelope**: double membrane with pores; mRNA and ribosome subunits out, proteins in; breaks into vesicles during division and re-forms afterwards.
- **Free ribosomes**: proteins for the cytosol.
- **rER-bound ribosomes**: proteins for secretion, lysosomes or membranes, made into the rER lumen.
- **Golgi apparatus**: stack of cisternae; receives vesicles from rER, modifies proteins (for example by adding carbohydrate), sends vesicles onwards.
- **Vesicles**: move materials between compartments; clathrin coats help them bud.

**Secretory route:** ribosome on rER → rER lumen → vesicle → Golgi → vesicle → plasma membrane → exocytosis.

## Worked reminders

**Concentration.** 3.0 × 10⁴ protons in 1.5 × 10⁻³ µm³ gives 2.0 × 10⁷ per µm³; in ten times the volume, 2.0 × 10⁶.

**Fraction identification.** A pellet with chlorophyll that settles at medium speed: chloroplasts. A pellet with high DNA at low speed: nuclei.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Organelle versus structure.** An organelle is discrete and within the cell; the wall and cytoskeleton are not.
- **Nuclear separation versus protection.** The examined advantage is time and place to modify RNA before translation.
- **Surface area versus volume.** Cristae and thylakoids raise membrane area; the small spaces between membranes keep volume low.
- **Matrix versus stroma.** Both are enzyme-rich fluids: the matrix in mitochondria (Krebs cycle), the stroma in chloroplasts (Calvin cycle).
- **Golgi faces.** Vesicles arrive at one face from the rER and leave from the opposite face.

## Exam traps

- Cell wall or cytoplasm listed as organelles.
- "The nucleus protects DNA" given as the main advantage.
- Cristae described as increasing volume.
- Calvin cycle placed in the thylakoid space.
- Free ribosomes said to make secreted proteins.
- Fractionation solution described as "cold water".

## Quick self-test

1. Define an organelle.
2. Give two cell components that are not organelles, with a reason for each.
3. Why is the fractionation solution kept cold?
4. Why must it be isotonic?
5. Which organelles pellet first?
6. Why can eukaryotic mRNA be modified before translation?
7. Give two advantages of compartments in the cytoplasm.
8. How do lysosomes and phagocytic vacuoles work together?
9. (HL) How does a small intermembrane space help ATP production?
10. (HL) Where in the chloroplast is the Calvin cycle?
11. (HL) Distinguish free ribosomes from ribosomes on the rER.
12. (HL) What is the role of clathrin?

### Answers

1. A discrete subunit of a cell with a specific function.
2. Cell wall (outside the plasma membrane); cytoplasm (whole contents); cytoskeleton (not discrete) (any two).
3. To slow enzymes that could damage organelles.
4. So organelles do not gain or lose water by osmosis.
5. Nuclei.
6. Transcription is in the nucleus and translation in the cytoplasm, so the RNA can be processed before it reaches ribosomes.
7. Concentrated enzymes and substrates; different pH; harmful enzymes isolated (any two).
8. Lysosomes fuse with the vacuole and release hydrolytic enzymes, digesting the particle inside a membrane.
9. Protons pumped into it build a high concentration quickly, giving a steep gradient to drive ATP synthase.
10. The stroma.
11. Free ribosomes make proteins for the cytosol; rER ribosomes make proteins for secretion, lysosomes or membranes.
12. It forms a cage-like coat that helps the membrane curve and bud off as a vesicle.
