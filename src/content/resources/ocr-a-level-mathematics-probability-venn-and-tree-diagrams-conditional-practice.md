## Questions

**1.** A and B are two events that cannot occur together. P(A) = 0.45 and P(B) = 0.3.

**(a)** Find P(A ∪ B). **[1]**
**(b)** Find P(A' ∩ B'). **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why A and B are not independent. **[1]**

**2.** A random variable X has P(X = x) = k/x for x = 1, 2, 4, and takes no other values.

**(a)** Show that k = 4/7. **[2]**
**(b)** Find P(X > 1). **[1]**
**(c)** Two independent values of X are observed. Find the probability that their sum is 5. **[3]**

**3.** A fair spinner numbered 1 to 5 and a fair spinner numbered 1 to 3 are each spun once. The two numbers are multiplied to give the score.

**(a)** Find the probability that the score is even. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that the score is greater than 8. **[2]**
**(c)** Given that the score is even, find the probability that it is greater than 8. **[2]**

**4.** Of 78 stallholders at a market, 41 sell food (F), 33 sell crafts (C) and 15 sell neither.

**(a)** Find the number who sell both. **[2]**
**(b)** One stallholder is chosen at random. Find the probability that they sell food only. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the probability that they sell exactly one of the two types of goods. **[2]**

**5.** A phone app crashes on start-up with probability 0.08 and, independently, on logging out with probability 0.05.

**(a)** Find the probability that it does not crash on either occasion. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that it crashes exactly once. **[2]**

**6.** A bag holds 5 chocolate, 4 mint and 3 toffee sweets. Two are drawn one after the other and not put back.

**(a)** Find the probability that both sweets have the same flavour. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the probability that the second is toffee, given that the first is chocolate. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the probability that at least one sweet is mint. **[2]**

**7.** A survey of 180 residents recorded age group and car ownership.

| | Under 30 | 30 to 59 | 60 and over |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owns a car | 20 | 62 | 38 |
| No car | 28 | 18 | 14 |

A resident is chosen at random.

**(a)** Find the probability that the resident owns a car, given that they are 60 or over. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that the resident is aged 30 to 59, given that they do not own a car. **[2]**
**(c)** Determine whether "owns a car" and "under 30" are independent. **[3]**

**8.** P(A) = 0.6, P(B | A) = 0.25 and P(B | A') = 0.5.

**(a)** Find P(A ∩ B). **[1]**
**(b)** Find P(B). **[2]**
**(c)** Find P(A ∪ B). **[2]**

**9.** An integer is chosen at random from 1 to 40 inclusive. Event A is "the number is a multiple of 4" and event B is "the number is a perfect square".

**(a)** Find P(A | B). **[2]**
**(b)** Find P(B | A). **[2]**

**10.** P(A) = 0.52, P(B) = 0.46 and P(A' ∩ B') = 0.18.

**(a)** Find P(A ∩ B). **[2]**
**(b)** Find P(A | B). **[2]**
**(c)** Show that A and B are not independent. **[2]**
**(d)** Find P(B' | A'). **[2]**

**11.** A transport survey uses a model in which each of four students chosen from one form travels by bus with probability 0.35, independently of the others.

**(a)** Find the probability that at least one of the four travels by bus. **[2]**
**(b)** State two assumptions made by the model. **[2]**
**(c)** The four students are close friends who often travel together. Explain how this affects your answer to (a). **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** P(A ∪ B) = 0.45 + 0.3 = **0.75** [1]
**(b)** P(A' ∩ B') = 1 − 0.75 = **0.25** [1]
**(c)** Mutually exclusive means P(A ∩ B) = 0, but P(A) × P(B) = 0.135, which is not 0, so the product rule fails. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* For an explanation, quote the numbers: stating that P(A ∩ B) = 0 differs from the product 0.135 is more convincing than the general claim that "exclusive events are not independent".

**2. (a)** The probabilities sum to 1: k + k/2 + k/4 = 7k/4 [1], so 7k/4 = 1 and k = 4/7 [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** P(X > 1) = 1 − 4/7 = **3/7** [1]
**(c)** P(X = 1) = 4/7, P(X = 2) = 2/7, P(X = 4) = 1/7. The pairs summing to 5 are (1, 4) and (4, 1). [1] Probability = 2 × 4/7 × 1/7 [1] = **8/49**. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" question, write the line where the probabilities are added and set equal to 1 before the result, because the given value cannot be the starting point of your working.

**3. (a)** There are 15 equally likely outcomes. Odd products need both numbers odd: 3 × 2 = 6 outcomes. [1] Even = 9 outcomes, so P = 9/15 = **3/5**. [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Products above 8 are 9, 10, 12, 15, from (3, 3), (5, 2), (4, 3), (5, 3). [1] P = **4/15**. [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Of those four, 10 and 12 are even. [1] P = 2/9, since there are 9 even outcomes in total. **2/9** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A conditional question restricts the sample space. Using 2/15 (a share of all outcomes) instead of 2/9 is the typical slip, so state the new total before dividing.

**4. (a)** Selling at least one type: 78 − 15 = 63. [1] Both = 41 + 33 − 63 = **11**. [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Food only = 41 − 11 = 30, so P = 30/78 = **5/13**. [1]
**(c)** Crafts only = 33 − 11 = 22. Exactly one = 30 + 22 = 52 [1], so P = 52/78 = **2/3**. [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Drawing the Venn diagram with all four regions totalling 78 lets you check your work and shows method even if one subtraction slips.

**5. (a)** P(no crash) = 0.92 × 0.95 [1] = **0.874** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** P(exactly once) = 0.08 × 0.95 + 0.92 × 0.05 [1] = 0.076 + 0.046 = **0.122** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Exactly once" has two routes. Showing both products before adding gives a clear method, and a missing second route is easy to see and costly.

**6. (a)** P(same) = 5/12 × 4/11 + 4/12 × 3/11 + 3/12 × 2/11 [1] = 20/132 + 12/132 + 6/132 [1] = 38/132 = **19/66** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** After one chocolate is removed, 11 sweets remain and 3 are toffee, so P = **3/11**. [1]
**(c)** P(no mint) = 8/12 × 7/11 = 14/33 [1], so P(at least one mint) = 1 − 14/33 = **19/33**. [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The second-stage denominator must be 11. Labelling each branch with its fraction on a quick tree makes that visible and protects the method.

**7. (a)** Restrict to the 60-and-over column, which has 38 + 14 = 52 residents. [1] P = 38/52 = **19/26**. [1] **[2]**
**(b)** The "no car" row totals 28 + 18 + 14 = 60. [1] P = 18/60 = **3/10**. [1] **[2]**
**(c)** P(car) = 120/180 = 2/3 [1]. P(car | under 30) = 20/48 = 5/12 [1]. These differ, so the events are **not independent**. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An independence conclusion needs both probabilities written down and a statement in words. A bare "not independent" has nothing to credit.

**8. (a)** P(A ∩ B) = 0.6 × 0.25 = **0.15** [1]
**(b)** P(A' ∩ B) = 0.4 × 0.5 = 0.2 [1]. P(B) = 0.15 + 0.2 = **0.35**. [1] **[2]**
**(c)** P(A ∪ B) = 0.6 + 0.35 − 0.15 [1] = **0.8** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) needs the A' branch as well. Using only P(B | A) is a common shortcut that answers a different question.

**9. (a)** Squares: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, so 6 outcomes. [1] Those that are multiples of 4: 4, 16, 36, so P(A | B) = 3/6 = **1/2**. [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Multiples of 4: 4, 8, ..., 40, so 10 outcomes. [1] P(B | A) = 3/10. **3/10** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The two answers differ, which is the point. Write the "given" set and its size first so the denominator is not chosen by habit.

**10. (a)** P(A ∪ B) = 1 − 0.18 = 0.82 [1]. P(A ∩ B) = 0.52 + 0.46 − 0.82 = **0.16**. [1] **[2]**
**(b)** P(A | B) = 0.16 / 0.46 [1] = **8/23**. [1] **[2]**
**(c)** If independent, P(A ∩ B) would equal 0.52 × 0.46 = 0.2392. [1] This is not 0.16, so A and B are not independent. [1] **[2]**
**(d)** P(A') = 0.48. P(B' | A') = 0.18 / 0.48 [1] = **3/8**. [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" for non-independence is complete only when the product is evaluated and compared with the intersection. Say what the two values are.

**11. (a)** P(at least one) = 1 − 0.65⁴ [1] = 1 − 0.1785 = **0.821** (3 s.f.) [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Each student has the same probability, 0.35, of travelling by bus. [1] The students' choices are independent of each other. [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Friends travelling together makes the students' choices positively linked, so independence fails. [1] Bus users tend to appear together, so a group with no bus user becomes more likely and P(at least one) would be expected to be smaller than 0.821. [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the comment to the context and give a direction. "Not realistic" alone has no content, while "less likely than 0.821 because choices cluster" does.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "independent" or "not independent" with no product test shown.
- Treating mutually exclusive events as independent, or the reverse.
- Using the full total as the denominator when the question says "given that".
- Using P(A) + P(B) for a union when the two events overlap.
- Forgetting that the second stage of a without-replacement tree has a smaller total.
- Computing only one route for "exactly one" or "different colours".
- Rounding part-way through, so a later equality test fails by a small amount.
- Giving a modelling comment with no named assumption or direction of effect.
