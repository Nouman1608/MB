## Section A - short questions

**1.** Which type of enterprise is in the public sector? **[1]**
A public limited company   B cooperative   C public corporation   D franchise

**2.** In which form of ownership does each owner usually have one vote, however much they have invested? **[1]**
A cooperative   B private limited company   C public limited company   D partnership

**3.** Which is an ecommerce location factor named in the syllabus? **[1]**
A raw materials   B technology specialists   C power   D competition

**4.** Define limited liability. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** features of a sole trader. **[2]**

**6.** Identify **two** reasons for the existence of multinational companies. **[2]**

## Section B - structured questions

**7.** Explain **two** differences between a private limited company and a public limited company. **[4]**

**8.** *The government of Country Y owns the national electricity company, which supplies power to every town and village.*
Explain **three** reasons why the government might choose to run electricity supply as a public corporation. **[6]**

**9.** *Lina makes and sells jewellery as a sole trader. Demand is rising and she wants to open a second shop, but she has little capital. Her friend Omar has savings and business experience.*
Assess whether Lina should form a partnership with Omar or remain a sole trader. **[6]**

**10.** *AutoGlobal, an MNC, plans to open a car factory in Country Z, which has high unemployment.*
(a) State **two** features of a multinational company. (2 marks)
(b) Discuss the impact of AutoGlobal's factory on Country Z. (6 marks) **[8]**

**11.** *Chen is deciding whether to open an independent café or buy a franchise of a well-known coffee chain.*
Explain **two** advantages and **two** disadvantages for Chen of buying the franchise. **[8]**

**12.** *GreenBox sells vegetable boxes online and delivers them to homes across a large city.*
Explain **two** ecommerce location factors and **one** physical location factor that GreenBox should consider when choosing a site. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** C. A public corporation is owned by the government. **[1]**

**2.** A. Cooperatives work on one member, one vote. **[1]**

**3.** B. The other three are physical location factors. **[1]**

**4.** The owners (shareholders) can lose only the amount they invested [1]; their personal possessions cannot be taken to pay the business's debts [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two: one owner [1]; owner provides the capital [1]; owner makes all decisions [1]; owner keeps all profit [1]; unlimited liability [1]; easy and cheap to set up [1]. **[max 2]**

**6.** Any two: to reach new markets [1]; to lower costs such as labour [1]; to be near raw materials [1]; to avoid trade barriers such as tariffs [1]; to spread risk across countries [1]. **[max 2]**

**7.** Any two differences, 2 marks each: a public limited company can sell shares to the general public, usually on a stock exchange, while a private limited company cannot [2]; a public limited company can therefore raise much larger amounts of capital [2]; a public limited company must publish its accounts, so more information is public [2]; a public limited company is at greater risk of takeover because anyone can buy its shares, while a private company's owners can keep control within a family [2]. **[max 4]**

**8.** Any three, 2 marks each (reason plus explanation applied to electricity):
Electricity is an essential service, so the government wants everyone to have it at an affordable price [2].
It is wasteful to build two sets of power lines to every home, so one supplier is sensible and should not exploit that position [2].
Supplying remote villages is unprofitable, so a private firm might not do it [2].
Energy is strategic, so the government wants control for national security [2].
It protects jobs in an important industry [2]. **[max 6]**

**9.** Up to 2 for partnership benefits, up to 2 for drawbacks or for staying a sole trader, up to 2 for a justified judgement.
Partnership benefits: Omar's savings provide capital for the second shop [1]; his business experience adds skills, and he can run one shop while Lina runs the other [1]; workload and risk are shared [1].
Drawbacks: Lina would share profits and decisions with Omar [1]; disagreements could slow decisions [1]; both still have unlimited liability, and Lina is bound by Omar's decisions [1]. Staying a sole trader keeps full control and all profit but may mean she cannot afford to expand [1].
Judgement: for example, a partnership is better because the second shop cannot be financed without Omar's capital, provided a partnership agreement sets out profit shares and roles [2]. **[6]**

**10.** (a) Any two: headquarters in one country [1]; operations in other countries [1]; very large scale [1]; well-known brand [1]; large capital and research spending [1]. (max 2 marks)
(b) Up to 2 for positive impacts, up to 2 for negative impacts, up to 2 for a conclusion about Country Z.
Positive: creates jobs, reducing Country Z's high unemployment [1]; workers gain skills and training [1]; investment and new technology [1]; cars may be exported, earning foreign currency, and the government gains tax [1].
Negative: profits may be sent back to AutoGlobal's home country [1]; local car or parts makers may be unable to compete [1]; wages may be low, or the factory may pollute [1]; AutoGlobal may leave if costs rise elsewhere [1].
Conclusion: for example, overall beneficial because unemployment is high, as long as the government sets rules on pay and pollution [2]. (6 marks) **[8]**

**11.** Advantages, any two, 2 marks each: a known brand means customers already trust the café, so sales start faster and risk is lower [2]; the franchisor provides training and a proven way of running the café [2]; national advertising by the franchisor brings customers without Chen paying for it all [2].
Disadvantages, any two, 2 marks each: Chen must pay an initial fee and continuing royalties, reducing his profit [2]; he must follow the franchisor's rules on menu, prices and suppliers, so he has little independence [2]; the agreement may not be renewed, or poor publicity about the chain could harm his café [2]. **[8]**

**12.** Ecommerce factors, any two, 2 marks each: warehousing, because GreenBox needs cool storage space to pack fresh vegetables before delivery [2]; logistics, because the site must have good access to delivery vans or couriers to reach homes across the city quickly [2]; wi-fi, because orders arrive online and the site must process them reliably [2]; technology specialists, to maintain the website and ordering system [2]. (max 4 marks)
Physical factor, any one, 2 marks: transport, such as good road links into the city so vegetables stay fresh [2]; suppliers, being near farms to collect produce daily [2]; labour, enough workers to pack boxes [2]; markets, being close to the city's customers to cut delivery times [2]. (max 2 marks) **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling a public limited company part of the public sector.
- Giving franchise points that belong to the franchisor in a question about the franchisee.
- Listing all nine physical location factors instead of choosing the ones that fit the business.
- Ending "assess" and "discuss" answers without a judgement.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a public limited company in the public sector?** No. It is owned by shareholders, so it is in the private sector. A public corporation is the public sector enterprise.

**Do partners have limited liability?** Not in an ordinary partnership: partners usually have unlimited liability.

**What are the ecommerce location factors in 0715?** Wi-fi, warehousing, technology specialists and logistics.
