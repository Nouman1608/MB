Subtopic 18.2 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers the uniform field between charged parallel plates, E = ΔV/Δd, and the effect of a uniform field on the motion of charged particles. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Uniform electric fields study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-uniform-electric-fields/); to apply the ideas, try the [Uniform electric fields practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-uniform-electric-fields-practice/).

## Field between plates

```
E = ΔV / Δd       (V m⁻¹ ≡ N C⁻¹)
```

- Δd = separation **across** the gap, in metres.
- Uniform: same strength and direction everywhere between the plates (ignore edge effects).
- Field lines: straight, parallel, evenly spaced, perpendicular to the plates, + to −.
- Derivation: work to move q across = qEΔd = qΔV.
- Unit check: V m⁻¹ = J C⁻¹ m⁻¹ = N m C⁻¹ m⁻¹ = N C⁻¹.

## Potential across the gap

- Changes **linearly** with distance (constant gradient = E).
- Equipotentials: planes parallel to the plates, equally spaced for equal steps of V.
- Example: 0 V and 600 V plates 40 mm apart → 15 V per mm; 150 V at 10 mm from the 0 V plate.

## Force and acceleration

```
F = qE = qV/d          a = qE/m
```

- Constant force → constant acceleration.
- Positive charge: force along E (towards the negative plate). Negative charge: against E (towards the positive plate).
- Weight of electrons, protons and ions is negligible.

## Three motion cases

| Entry direction | Motion between plates | Key equations |
|---|---|---|
| Along the force | Uniform acceleration, speeds up | ½mv² = qV (from rest) |
| Against the force | Uniform deceleration, may stop and return | stopping distance = E_k / (qE) |
| Perpendicular to the field | Parabola (like a projectile) | t = L/v; y = ½at²; v_y = at; tan θ = v_y/v |

After leaving the field: **straight line** at the exit angle.

## Deflection: what increases it

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| Larger p.d. | Larger E, larger a |
| Smaller plate separation | Larger E |
| Longer plates | More time in the field |
| Slower particle | More time in the field |
| Larger charge | Larger force |
| Smaller mass | Larger acceleration |

Electron vs proton at the same speed: opposite directions; electron's acceleration ≈ 1830 times larger (m_p/m_e).

## Electric versus magnetic deflection (for comparison)

| Uniform electric field | Uniform magnetic field (Topic 20) |
|---|---|
| Force in a fixed direction | Force always perpendicular to velocity |
| Path: parabola | Path: circular arc |
| Speed changes | Speed constant |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Uniform field | Field strength the same in magnitude and direction at every point |
| Parallel plates | Two flat conductors with equal and opposite charges, producing a uniform field between them |
| Edge effect | Non-uniform field at the plate edges, where lines bow outwards |
| Equipotential | Surface on which every point is at the same potential |
| Deflection | Sideways displacement of a particle from its original line of motion |

## Exam traps

- Leaving d in mm or cm.
- Using the plate length instead of the separation in E = V/d.
- Claiming the speed parallel to the plates changes.
- Calling the path a circle.
- Continuing the curve after the particle leaves the plates.
- Deflecting the electron the wrong way.
- Forgetting to check whether the particle hits a plate.

## Self-test

1. Calculate E between plates 2.0 cm apart with a p.d. of 500 V.
2. Show that V m⁻¹ is equivalent to N C⁻¹.
3. The p.d. is halved and the separation doubled. What happens to E?
4. Plates at 0 V and 400 V are 20 mm apart. What is the potential 5.0 mm from the 0 V plate?
5. Calculate the force on a proton in a field of 1.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹.
6. An electron is accelerated from rest through 500 V. Find its kinetic energy.
7. Why is the path of an electron between plates parabolic?
8. What is the path after the electron leaves the plates?
9. Name two changes that would increase the deflection of a particle.
10. An electron spends 2.0 ns between plates where its acceleration is 1.0 × 10¹⁵ m s⁻². Find its sideways displacement.
11. In which direction is an electron deflected between horizontal plates with the top plate positive?
12. Why can the weight of an electron be ignored?
