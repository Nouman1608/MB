Teaching is in the [gas exchange study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-gas-exchange/) and the [gas exchange revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-gas-exchange-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** **(a)** Name the pores through which air enters the tracheal system of an insect. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the thin plates on gill filaments across which gas exchange takes place in a fish. **[1]**

**(c)** Name the airways that connect the bronchi to the alveoli. **[1]**

**2.** Insects transport oxygen to their tissues without using their blood. Explain how the structure of the tracheal system makes this possible. **[2]**

**3.** Give two features of the alveolar epithelium and explain how each increases the rate of gas exchange. **[2]**

**4.** During flight, the ends of the tracheoles in insect flight muscle contain less water than at rest. Explain why. **[3]**

**5.** A resting person's spirometer trace shows one breath every 6 seconds and a tidal volume of 0.45 dm³. After exercise the tidal volume is 1.2 dm³ and the breathing rate is 24 breaths per minute.

**(a)** Calculate the pulmonary ventilation at rest. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in pulmonary ventilation after exercise. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why a dicotyledonous leaf does not need a ventilation system or a transport system for gases. **[2]**

## Section B: longer questions

**7.** Explain how the counter-current principle allows a fish to take up a large proportion of the oxygen from the water passing over its gills. **[4]**

**8.** Describe how air is drawn into the lungs during inspiration. **[4]**

**9.** Marram grass grows on sand dunes. Its leaves roll up in dry weather, have stomata in pits on the inner surface, and are covered on the inner surface with hairs. Explain how these features reduce water loss. **[3]**

**10.** Terrestrial insects face a conflict between efficient gas exchange and limiting water loss. Explain how insects balance these two needs. **[3]**

**11.** In pulmonary fibrosis, scar tissue forms in the walls of the alveoli, making them thicker and less elastic. Patients with this condition have a lower tidal volume and a higher breathing rate at rest than healthy people.

**(a)** Explain why fibrosis reduces the rate of oxygen uptake into the blood. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why patients have a lower tidal volume. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest why patients have a higher breathing rate at rest. **[2]**

**12.** A study recorded the number of cigarettes smoked per day by a large group of adults and the number in each group who later developed lung cancer.

| Cigarettes per day | Lung cancer cases per 1000 people over the study |
|---|---|
| 0 | 1 |
| 1–14 | 8 |
| 15–24 | 17 |
| 25 or more | 30 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship shown. **[1]**

**(b)** A newspaper claimed that the data prove smoking causes lung cancer. Evaluate this claim. **[3]**

**13.** Compare gas exchange in a fish with gas exchange in an insect. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Spiracles [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Gill lamellae [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** Bronchioles [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Tracheae branch into many tracheoles that reach into the tissues and end next to (or within) respiring cells [1], so oxygen diffuses directly from the air in the tracheoles to the cells over a short distance [1]. **[2]**

**3.** One cell thick / made of flattened cells, so a short diffusion distance [1]; very many alveoli give a large surface area, so more diffusion per unit time [1]. Accept: close contact with capillaries whose walls are one cell thick, so short distance. **[2]**

**4.** Respiring muscle produces lactate during flight [1], which lowers the water potential of the muscle cells [1]. Water moves out of the tracheole ends into the cells by osmosis, so more of the tracheoles fill with air [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The mark for osmosis needs the direction: from tracheole into the cells.

**5. (a)** Breathing rate = 60 ÷ 6 = 10 breaths per minute [1]; pulmonary ventilation = 0.45 × 10 = **4.5 dm³ per minute** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** After exercise: 1.2 × 24 = 28.8 dm³ per minute [1]; (28.8 − 4.5) ÷ 4.5 × 100 = **540%** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The leaf is thin and has air spaces in the mesophyll, so every cell is close to the air and the diffusion distance is short [1]. Gases diffuse through stomata and air spaces fast enough without ventilation or transport [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Blood in the lamellae flows in the opposite direction to the water flowing over them [1]. So blood always meets water with a higher oxygen concentration than itself [1]. A concentration gradient is maintained along the whole length of the lamella [1], so oxygen diffuses into the blood all along it and the blood leaving has an oxygen concentration close to that of the incoming water [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Mention what would happen with parallel flow only if it supports the explanation; the marks are for the gradient along the whole lamella.

**8.** External intercostal muscles contract and internal intercostal muscles relax, moving the ribcage up and out [1]. The diaphragm contracts and flattens [1]. The volume of the thoracic cavity increases [1], so the pressure in the lungs falls below atmospheric pressure and air moves in [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Rolled leaves and hairs trap moist air next to the stomata [1]. Stomata in pits are also surrounded by trapped humid air [1]. This reduces the water potential gradient between the inside of the leaf and the air, so less water vapour diffuses out [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Insects have a waxy, waterproof cuticle that reduces evaporation [1]. Spiracles can close when demand for oxygen is low, reducing loss of water vapour [1]. Hairs around spiracles trap moist air, reducing the water potential gradient; or a small surface area to volume ratio reduces the surface for water loss [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** Thicker alveolar walls increase the diffusion distance [1], so oxygen diffuses into the blood more slowly [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** The less elastic alveoli cannot expand as much, so less air enters in each breath [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** Less oxygen enters the blood in each breath [1], so more breaths per minute are needed to supply enough oxygen for respiration [1]. **[2]**

**12. (a)** As the number of cigarettes per day increases, the number of lung cancer cases per 1000 increases [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** The data show a positive correlation, with a large increase from 1 to 30 cases per 1000 [1]. A correlation does not prove causation, because other factors, such as air pollution, occupation or age, may differ between the groups [1]. Stronger evidence would need these factors controlled or a known mechanism by which smoke damages lung cells [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* "Correlation not causation" scores only when you give a reason, such as a named confounding variable.

**13.** Both have a large surface area: many gill lamellae in fish, many branched tracheoles in insects [1]. Both maintain a short diffusion distance [1]. In fish, oxygen diffuses into blood, which carries it to the tissues, whereas in insects oxygen is delivered by the tracheoles directly to the cells [1]. Fish ventilate with a one-way flow of water and use counter-current flow, whereas insects take in air through spiracles, with ventilation by abdominal movements in some insects [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing breathing as "lungs inflate" without volume and pressure changes.
- Saying the internal intercostals contract during inspiration.
- Explaining counter-current flow without the gradient along the whole lamella.
- Giving xerophyte features without "reduces the water potential gradient".
- Saying "correlation is not causation" without naming a confounding factor.
