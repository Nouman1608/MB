These notes condense "The effect of errors on profit calculations" from section 3.2 of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Accounting (9215) specification, which includes "the adjustment of profit or loss for an accounting period following the correction of errors". All amounts are in dollars.

Full explanations and a two-column worked example are in the [effect of errors on profit study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-the-effect-of-errors-on-profit-calculations/). For marked questions, use the [effect of errors on profit practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-the-effect-of-errors-on-profit-calculations-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gross profit | Revenue − cost of sales |
| Profit for the year | Gross profit + other income − expenses |
| Draft profit | Profit calculated before the errors were found |
| Corrected profit | Draft profit adjusted for every error that affects income or expenses |
| Overstated / understated | Shown as more / less than the correct figure |
| Statement of corrected profit | A list starting with draft profit, one line per error, ending with corrected profit |

## Which profit does it hit?

```
TRADING ITEMS  (sales, sales returns, purchases, purchases returns,
                carriage inwards, opening and closing inventory)
     -> gross profit AND profit for the year, by the same amount

OTHER INCOME AND EXPENSES  (discounts, rent, commission, wages,
                            carriage outwards, repairs, ...)
     -> profit for the year only

ASSETS, LIABILITIES, CAPITAL, DRAWINGS only
     -> no effect on either profit
```

Moving an amount between a trading item and an expense (such as carriage inwards posted as carriage outwards) changes gross profit but not profit for the year.

## Direction rules

| Error | Profit was | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Income too low / omitted | Too low | + |
| Income too high | Too high | − |
| Expense too low / omitted | Too high | − |
| Expense too high | Too low | + |
| Closing inventory too high | Too high | − |
| Closing inventory too low | Too low | + |
| Capital item treated as expense | Too low | + |
| Expense treated as capital item | Too high | − |
| Drawings treated as expense | Too low | + |

**Journal check:** a correcting **credit** to an income or expense account raises profit; a correcting **debit** lowers it. The suspense side never affects profit.

## Inventory errors

Closing inventory is deducted in cost of sales, so it moves profit in the **same** direction as the error: overvalued closing inventory means overstated profit. Inventory is counted at the year end rather than posted through the ledger, so an inventory error never causes a trial balance difference.

**Small reminder.** Draft gross profit 30,000; closing inventory undervalued by 450. Corrected gross profit 30,450, and profit for the year also rises by 450.

## Writing an "explain" answer

```
1. Name the account and say whether it was overstated or understated.
2. Say what that did to profit (too high or too low).
3. State the correction's effect, with the amount and the profit affected.
```

## Statement of corrected profit: layout

```
                                       Gross profit   Profit for the year
Draft figures                              x               x
Error 1 (description)                    (x) or x        (x) or x
...
Corrected figures                          x               x
```

- One line per error, described in words.
- Show 0 for errors with no effect when the question uses a table.
- A loss is a negative profit; label the final figure "profit" or "loss".

**Small reminder.** Draft profit 21,600. Insurance overcast by 300; equipment of 2,000 debited to repairs; commission received of 450 omitted.

```
Draft profit for the year     21,600
Insurance overcast              +300
Equipment charged as repairs  +2,000
Commission received omitted     +450
Corrected profit              24,350
```

## Exam traps

- Reversing a direction: an overstated expense means profit was **understated**.
- Giving a gross profit effect for wages, rent, carriage outwards or discounts.
- Forgetting that inventory errors change both profits.
- Claiming a profit effect for commission errors between personal accounts.
- Treating the suspense entry as a profit adjustment.
- Corrected figure with no label, or a loss shown as a profit.

## Quick self-test

For questions 1 to 8, state the effect of **correcting** the error on gross profit (GP) and on profit for the year (PFY).

1. The sales account was overcast by 500.
2. Rent received of 300 was not recorded.
3. Closing inventory was undervalued by 700.
4. Equipment costing 1,000 was debited to the purchases account.
5. Carriage outwards of 150 was debited to the carriage inwards account.
6. The owner's private expenses of 400 were debited to the wages account.
7. A payment of 900 to C Roy was debited to the account of C Ray.
8. Discount allowed of 90 was not recorded.
9. Draft profit is 12,000. Corrections are −900 and +350. Find the corrected profit.
10. A draft loss is 600. A correction adds 1,000 to profit. Find the corrected result.
11. Why does the suspense side of a correcting journal not affect profit?
12. Purchases returns of 260 were recorded as 620 in both the purchases returns account and the supplier's account. State the effect of correcting this on GP and PFY.

### Answers

1. GP **−500**; PFY **−500**.
2. GP **no effect**; PFY **+300**.
3. GP **+700**; PFY **+700**.
4. GP **+1,000**; PFY **+1,000** (purchases were overstated).
5. GP **+150**; PFY **no effect**.
6. GP **no effect**; PFY **+400**.
7. **No effect** on either.
8. GP **no effect**; PFY **−90**.
9. 12,000 − 900 + 350 = **11,450 profit**.
10. −600 + 1,000 = **400 profit**.
11. Suspense is **not an income or expense account**; it is a temporary balance in the trial balance.
12. Purchases returns were overstated by 360, so cost of sales was too low: GP **−360**; PFY **−360**.
