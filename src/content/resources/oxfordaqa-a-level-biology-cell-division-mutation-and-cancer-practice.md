## Questions

**1.** State what happens in each of the G1, S and G2 phases of interphase. **[3]**

**2.** A culture of dividing cells has a cell cycle of 12 hours. A sample of 240 cells is classified: 108 in G1, 60 in S, 42 in G2 and 30 in mitosis.

**(a)** Calculate the time spent in S phase and in mitosis. **[2]**
**(b)** State the assumption made in using these counts. **[1]**
**(c)** Give one process that occurs in G2. **[1]**

**3.** The DNA mass in one nucleus was measured through a 10 hour cycle. It was 4.6 pg from 0 to 3 hours, rose steadily to 9.2 pg at 7 hours, stayed at 9.2 pg until 9.5 hours, then fell to 4.6 pg at 10 hours.

**(a)** Name the phase between 3 and 7 hours and explain the evidence. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the fall in DNA mass between 9.5 and 10 hours. **[2]**

**4.** Four cells from a root tip squash were described. Cell A: chromosomes in two groups at opposite ends, nuclear envelopes reforming. Cell B: chromosomes in a line across the middle. Cell C: short, thick chromosomes scattered, each of two chromatids. Cell D: V-shaped chromosomes moving apart.

**(a)** Put the cells in order and name each stage. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the movement seen in cell D happens. **[3]**

**5.** **(a)** Describe binary fission. **[3]**
**(b)** A culture has 25 bacteria. Each divides every 30 minutes. Calculate the number after 4 hours. **[2]**

**6.** Explain how a gene for antibiotic resistance could pass from one species of bacterium to another, and why this is called horizontal gene transmission. **[4]**

**7.** Part of an mRNA is AUG GAA CCA UUU CGC ACU UAA. Codons: AUG Met; GAA Glu; CCA and CCG Pro; UUU and UUC Phe; CGC Arg; ACU Thr; AAC Asn; CAU His; GCA Ala; CUU Leu; UAA stop.

**(a)** Predict the amino acid sequence if base 9 (A) is replaced by G. **[2]**
**(b)** Predict the effect if base 4 (G) is replaced by U. **[2]**
**(c)** Predict the sequence if base 4 is deleted. **[2]**

**8.** Define a mutagen, say when spontaneous mutations arise, and explain why a substitution may not change a protein. **[3]**

**9.** **(a)** Give two differences between benign and malignant tumours. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how a mutated proto-oncogene can lead to a tumour. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how a mutated tumour suppressor gene can lead to a tumour. **[2]**

**10.** The illustrative table shows melanoma cases per 100 000 fair-skinned adults per year by daily sun exposure: under 1 hour 8.2; 1 to 3 hours 11.5; 3 to 5 hours 19.7; over 5 hours 26.9.

**(a)** Describe the relationship. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times higher the incidence is for over 5 hours than under 1 hour. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate the claim that the data show sunlight causes melanoma. **[3]**

**11.** Drug X blocks DNA replication. Drug Y stops spindle formation.

**(a)** State the phase each drug acts in and explain why dividing cells are harmed. **[4]**
**(b)** Suggest why hair loss is a side effect. **[2]**

**12.** A root tip squash was made. An untreated tip had 29 cells in mitosis among 242 counted. A tip treated with chemical Z had 52 in mitosis among 260.

**(a)** Calculate both mitotic indices as percentages. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how the squash was prepared. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest one improvement to make the comparison more reliable. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** G1: cell grows and makes protein and more organelles such as mitochondria and ribosomes [1]. S: DNA is replicated [1]. G2: rapid growth and protein synthesis continue [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Three separate phases each need their own statement; a general comment such as "the cell prepares" does not distinguish them.

**2. (a)** S: 60 / 240 × 12 = **3.0 hours** [1]. Mitosis: 30 / 240 × 12 = **1.5 hours** [1].
**(b)** Cells are spread evenly through the cycle, so the fraction of cells in a phase equals the fraction of time [1].
**(c)** Rapid cell growth or protein synthesis [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the fraction and the multiplication so your working is clear, and give units (hours) with each value.

**3. (a)** S phase [1]. DNA mass doubles from 4.6 pg to 9.2 pg, showing DNA replication [1].
**(b)** The cell divides, so the DNA is shared between the two daughter cells [1], and each receives half the mass, 4.6 pg [1].
*Examiner insight:* Quote the figures from the data when explaining; saying only "DNA goes up" earns less than naming the values and linking them to replication.

**4. (a)** C (prophase), B (metaphase), D (anaphase), A (telophase) [1] for the correct order, [1] for all four names correct.
**(b)** Spindle fibres are attached to centromeres [1]. The centromeres divide and the fibres shorten [1], pulling the chromatids to opposite poles, so each pole gets one copy of every chromosome [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the fibres to the centromere and say they shorten; "the spindle pulls them" with no detail is too vague to separate the points.

**5. (a)** The circular DNA replicates [1]. Plasmids replicate [1]. The cytoplasm divides to give two daughter cells, each with one copy of the circular DNA, and plasmid number may vary [1].
**(b)** 4 hours = 240 minutes, so 240 / 30 = 8 divisions [1]. 25 × 2⁸ = 25 × 256 = **6400** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the number of divisions before the final multiplication; this makes each step of your reasoning easy to follow.

**6.** A plasmid carrying the resistance gene is copied [1]. The copy passes from one bacterium to another by conjugation [1]. The recipient, which may be a different species, now has the gene [1]. It is horizontal because the gene passes between existing cells, not from parent to offspring [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name conjugation and contrast with parent-to-offspring passage; a description of binary fission does not explain the movement between species.

**7. (a)** Codon 3 becomes CCG [1], which also codes for Pro, so the sequence is **Met - Glu - Pro - Phe - Arg - Thr** with no change [1].
**(b)** Codon 2 becomes UAA [1], a stop codon, so the chain is **Met** only [1].
**(c)** Reading in threes after deleting base 4 gives AUG AAC CAU UUC GCA CUU [1]. The sequence is **Met - Asn - His - Phe - Ala - Leu**, so Glu, Pro, Arg and Thr are replaced by Asn, His, Ala and Leu; Phe is unchanged because UUU and UUC both code for it [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the regrouped codons; naming the effect (no change, early stop or all later amino acids altered) is also expected.

**8.** A mutagen is a physical or chemical agent that increases the frequency of mutations [1]. Spontaneous mutations arise during DNA replication [1]. The genetic code is degenerate, so the new codon may code for the same amino acid [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Do not say mutagens cause mutations that would never otherwise occur; they increase how often they occur.

**9. (a)** Any two from: benign grows slower; benign usually encapsulated and malignant invades surrounding tissue; benign does not spread, malignant spreads to form secondary tumours [1] [1].
**(b)** The mutation produces an oncogene [1] that stimulates cells to divide too quickly, forming a mass of cells [1].
**(c)** The mutation inactivates the gene [1], so division is no longer slowed and the rate of division increases [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the gene type and its normal role; "the gene mutates and cancer forms" is too general.

**10. (a)** Incidence rises as sun exposure increases [1], from 8.2 to 26.9 per 100 000, a positive correlation [1].
**(b)** 26.9 / 8.2 [1] = **3.3 times** (3.28) [1].
**(c)** The rise supports a link [1], but correlation alone does not prove cause, and other factors such as skin type, age or sunburn history may differ between groups [1]. A fuller evaluation needs sample sizes, how exposure was measured and a known mechanism, such as ultraviolet light damaging DNA [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation needs at least one limitation and one point in support; saying only "yes, it shows sunlight causes cancer" claims more than the data allow.

**11. (a)** Drug X acts in S phase [1], so DNA cannot replicate and the cell cannot complete the cycle [1]. Drug Y acts in mitosis [1], so chromatids cannot be separated and cells cannot divide [1].
**(b)** Hair follicle cells divide often [1], so they are also affected by the drug and hair growth stops [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Pair each drug with its phase and with a consequence; a list of side effects without the link to dividing cells misses the reasoning.

**12. (a)** Untreated: 29 / 242 × 100 = **12.0%** [1]. Treated: 52 / 260 × 100 = **20.0%** [1].
**(b)** Cut the root tip and warm it in hydrochloric acid [1]. Add a stain that binds chromosomes [1], place under a coverslip and squash into a single layer of cells [1].
**(c)** Count more fields or cells from several root tips and keep the total number similar [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the formula or the working with the percentage; stating the order of steps (acid, stain, squash) earns more than listing equipment.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "DNA replicates in mitosis" instead of S phase.
- Saying chromosomes separate in metaphase when they separate in anaphase.
- Using "chromosome" and "chromatid" interchangeably.
- Omitting the centromere when describing spindle fibres.
- Saying all mutations change the protein.
- Treating a deletion as a substitution and ignoring the shift in reading frame.
- Concluding from correlation alone that a risk factor causes cancer.
- Dividing by dividing cells only when finding the mitotic index.
