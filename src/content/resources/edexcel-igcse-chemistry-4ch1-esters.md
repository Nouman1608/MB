## The ester group (4.38C)

An **ester** contains the functional group **-COO-**, in which a carbon atom is double-bonded to one oxygen and single-bonded to another oxygen, which is in turn bonded to a second carbon chain:

```
      O
     //
 R - C
     \
      O - R'
```

Here R is the part from the acid and R' is the part from the alcohol. The group is written -COO- in structural formulae, for example CH₃COOCH₂CH₃.

## Making ethyl ethanoate (4.39C and 4.40C)

An ester forms when a **carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol** in the presence of an **acid catalyst** (a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid). The reaction is called **esterification**. It is reversible, and a molecule of **water** is also formed:

```
CH3COOH + CH3CH2OH  ⇌  CH3COOCH2CH3 + H2O
ethanoic acid + ethanol ⇌ ethyl ethanoate + water
```

Formulae of ethyl ethanoate:

- **Molecular formula:** C₄H₈O₂ (Mr = 88).
- **Structural formula:** CH₃COOCH₂CH₃.
- **Displayed formula:**

```
    H   O       H   H
    |   ||      |   |
H - C - C - O - C - C - H
    |           |   |
    H           H   H
```

Check the atoms: four carbons, eight hydrogens (three, two and three), two oxygens. Each carbon has four bonds, and each oxygen has two (the carbonyl oxygen has the C=O, and the other has two single bonds).

**Mass balance.** Ethanol (46) + ethanoic acid (60) = 106, and ethyl ethanoate (88) + water (18) = 106. The masses balance, as they must.

## Naming esters and working backwards (4.41C)

An ester's name has two words. The **first word** comes from the **alcohol** (the alkyl group on the oxygen). The **second word** comes from the **acid** (the -oate part).

| Alcohol | Acid | Ester | Formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ethanol | Ethanoic acid | Ethyl ethanoate | CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ |
| Methanol | Ethanoic acid | Methyl ethanoate | CH₃COOCH₃ |
| Propan-1-ol | Ethanoic acid | Propyl ethanoate | CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ |
| Ethanol | Propanoic acid | Ethyl propanoate | CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ |
| Methanol | Methanoic acid | Methyl methanoate | HCOOCH₃ |

**Method: from the name to the formula.**

1. Take the second word (the acid part): ethanoate comes from ethanoic acid, CH₃CO-.
2. Take the first word (the alcohol part): propyl, CH₃CH₂CH₂-.
3. Join them through the ester link: CH₃COO-CH₂CH₂CH₃, which is propyl ethanoate.

**Method: from the ester back to the acid and alcohol.**

1. Find the C(=O)O link.
2. The carbons on the **C=O side** (including the carbonyl carbon) form the **acid**.
3. The carbons on the **O-single-bond side** form the **alcohol**.

**Worked example 1.** The ester CH₃CH₂COOCH₃. The C=O side: CH₃CH₂C(=O)- has three carbons, so propanoic acid. The other side: -CH₃ has one carbon, so methanol. The ester is **methyl propanoate**, and it is made from propanoic acid and methanol.

**Worked example 2.** Which acid and alcohol give butyl methanoate? Methanoate: methanoic acid, HCOOH. Butyl: butan-1-ol, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH. The structural formula is HCOOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃.

The usual error is to swap the two parts: ethyl ethanoate is CH₃COOCH₂CH₃, but CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ is methyl propanoate, even though both have four carbons. They are isomers.

## Properties and uses (4.42C)

Esters are **volatile** (they evaporate easily), because they are small molecules with weak intermolecular forces, and they have **distinctive, pleasant, often fruity smells**. Ethyl ethanoate smells like pear drops or nail varnish remover, and other esters smell of banana, pineapple or apple. They are used as **food flavourings** and in **perfumes** and cosmetics. Some are solvents.

## Practical: preparing ethyl ethanoate (4.43C)

**Method.**

1. Pour about 2 cm³ of ethanol and 2 cm³ of ethanoic acid into a test tube.
2. Add a few drops of **concentrated sulfuric acid** (the catalyst), carefully.
3. Warm the tube in a **hot water bath** (about 60 to 70 °C) for several minutes. Do not heat with a naked flame, because ethanol and the ester are flammable.
4. Pour the mixture into a beaker of **sodium carbonate solution** (or sodium hydrogencarbonate). It fizzes as unreacted acid is neutralised.
5. Waft the smell towards your nose. A sweet, fruity smell, with an oily layer on the surface, shows an ester has formed.

**Why.** The acid catalyst speeds up the reaction. Heating increases the rate. Sodium carbonate removes the acid so that the smell of the ester is not masked by the sharp smell of vinegar. The ester is less dense than water and does not mix with it, so it forms an oily layer on top.

**Worked example 3: yield.** 4.6 g of ethanol (Mr = 46) reacts with excess ethanoic acid.

1. Moles of ethanol = 4.6 / 46 = **0.10 mol**.
2. The ratio is 1 : 1, so the maximum moles of ester = **0.10 mol**.
3. Maximum mass = 0.10 x 88 = **8.8 g**.
4. If the actual yield is 60%, the mass of ester = 0.60 x 8.8 = **5.28 g**.

The yield is less than 100% because the reaction is reversible and does not go to completion, and because some product is lost during separation.

## Common mistakes

- Putting the acid name first. In ethyl ethanoate, "ethyl" comes from the alcohol.
- Forgetting the water that is also formed.
- Writing the ester link as -COC-, or leaving out the C=O.
- Swapping the alcohol and acid parts of the formula, giving the wrong isomer.
- Using the wrong catalyst. It is concentrated sulfuric acid (an acid catalyst), not a base.
- Heating over a naked flame in the practical.
- Saying that esters smell "sour" or "vinegary".
- Forgetting that the whole sub-topic is Paper 2 only.

## Exam technique

When writing an ester, split it at the ester link and name the two halves. For an equation, include the acid catalyst over the arrow, use the reversible symbol and include water. For the practical, explain the reason for each step (catalyst, water bath, carbonate wash). All of these points are Paper 2 only.
