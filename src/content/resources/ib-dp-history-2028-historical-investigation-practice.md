These tasks practise the three sections of the historical investigation (criteria A, B and C). The mark values are for practice. The real investigation is marked out of 24 (6 + 6 + 12).

## Section A: recall

**1.** State the word limit of the historical investigation, and say what is not counted towards it. **[2]**

**2.** State the maximum number of sources, and give the marks for each criterion. **[4]**

**3.** Explain the difference between a historical source and evidence. **[2]**

## Section B: criterion A and B skills

**4.** A student proposes the question: "What happened in the Boer War?"

**(a)** Explain two reasons why this question is unlikely to score well in criterion A. **[4]**

**(b)** Rewrite the question so that it is conceptual and focused, and name the concept. **[3]**

**5.** A student writes in section 2: "Source 1 is a diary. It is useful. Source 2 is a newspaper. It is biased. Source 3 is a book by a historian. It is reliable."

**(a)** Explain two weaknesses of this section. **[4]**

**(b)** Describe how the student could reorganise the section to improve it. **[3]**

## Section C: criterion C skills

**6.** Read this constructed extract from a student's section 3 (constructed for practice). The inquiry question is: "To what extent did the Salt March of 1930 weaken British rule in India?"

> "The Salt March began in March 1930. Gandhi walked about 240 miles to Dandi with his followers. Many people joined. The British arrested many people. The march was very important. Source 2 says that thousands were arrested. In the end India became independent in 1947."

**(a)** Which level of criterion C (1 to 3, 4 to 6, 7 to 9, 10 to 12) does the extract most closely match? Explain your choice. **[3]**

**(b)** Rewrite the extract as two or three sentences that show synthesis and evaluation. You may invent plausible source labels (for example, a British official's report and a newspaper article). **[4]**

**7.** Explain why a contemporary topic, such as an event from the past five years, needs special care in a historical investigation. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The limit is 2,200 words **[1]**. References, headings and the bibliography are not counted **[1]**.

**2.** A maximum of seven sources **[1]**. Criterion A is worth 6 marks **[1]**, criterion B 6 marks **[1]** and criterion C 12 marks **[1]**.

**3.** A historical source is any object that historians can use in an inquiry (written, visual, diagrammatic, multimodal or material) **[1]**. Evidence is the information the historian takes from the source **[1]**.

**4. (a)** Two reasons, two marks each, for example:
- the question is descriptive: it asks "what happened", so it invites a narrative and not an analytical response **[2]**;
- it is too broad, as the war lasted several years and involved many issues, so it cannot be covered within the word limit **[2]**;
- it has no clear concept and does not invite a judgement or diverse perspectives **[2]**.
**(b)** For example: "To what extent did the use of concentration camps change British public opinion about the war between 1900 and 1902?" **[1 for a focused scope; 1 for an analytical stem; 1 for naming a concept such as continuity and change or cause and consequence]**. Accept any reasoned alternative.

**5. (a)** Two weaknesses, two marks each, for example:
- it is a source-by-source description, and not organised around perspectives **[2]**;
- the comments ("useful", "biased", "reliable") are asserted without reasons and without reference to the inquiry question **[2]**;
- it does not explain how the perspectives are diverse or how they will help answer the question **[2]**.
**(b)** The student should group sources by perspective (for example participants, officials, later historians) **[1]**, explain what each perspective offers and what it leaves out **[1]**, and explain how comparing them will help to answer the question, referring to specific sources where needed **[1]**.

**6. (a)** The extract is closest to the 4 to 6 level, and at its lower end **[1]**, because it is mostly narrative and assertion (for example "very important") **[1]**, with only minimal reference to a source and no evaluation or a clear judgement **[1]**. (Accept the 1 to 3 level if the reasons are sound.)
**(b)** For example: "The Salt March weakened British authority more through its symbolic effect than through its direct impact on revenue. A British official's report (Source 2) admits that thousands were arrested, which shows the authorities felt compelled to act, but as it was written to justify the arrests, it probably understates the march's popular support. A newspaper account (Source 4), though sympathetic to the movement, supports the claim that participation spread across regions. Together they suggest that British rule was damaged in legitimacy more than in practical control." **[1 for a clear claim; 1 for synthesis of two sources; 1 for evaluation linked to context; 1 for a judgement consistent with the analysis]**.

**7.** Contemporary events are recent, so historians may have limited access to sources and archives **[1]**; the consequences cannot yet be judged historically, so the student can study causes but not consequences **[1]**; the topic can be emotive or politically sensitive, so the student must treat it with care and consider ethical issues **[1]**.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these extracts typical?** They are short and simple so that you can practise the skills quickly. Real work is longer.

**Which criterion should I practise most?** Criterion C carries half the marks, so it deserves a large share of your time, but a weak question or sources will limit it.
