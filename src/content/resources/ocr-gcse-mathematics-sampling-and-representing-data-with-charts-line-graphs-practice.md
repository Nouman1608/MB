These questions cover sections 12.01 and 12.02 of the OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics (J560) specification, Version 2.1 (August 2026): sampling, charts, time series, cumulative frequency graphs and histograms. Questions 1 to 7 are suitable for both tiers. Questions 8 to 12 are marked "(Higher tier only)" because the specification lists cumulative frequency graphs and histograms for the Higher tier only.

## Questions

**1.** A librarian wants to know whether the 2,400 members of a library would use a new late opening. She asks the first 30 members who walk in on a Saturday morning.

**(a)** State the population. **[1]**
**(b)** Give two reasons why her sample may be biased. **[2]**

**2.** A school has 500 pupils, numbered 001 to 500.

**(a)** Describe how to select a simple random sample of 25 pupils. **[3]**
**(b)** In the sample, 7 pupils wear glasses. Estimate how many of the 500 pupils wear glasses. **[2]**

**3.** 80 pupils each name their favourite sport: football 28, netball 20, swimming 16, tennis 10, other 6.

**(a)** Work out the angle for each sport in a pie chart. **[3]**
**(b)** In a pie chart for a different group, the sector for swimming has angle 90° and represents 36 pupils. How many pupils are in this group? **[2]**

**4.** A vertical line chart shows the number of goals in 30 matches. The line heights (number of matches) for 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 goals are 4, 7, 9, 5, 3 and 2.

**(a)** Show that the chart represents 30 matches. **[1]**
**(b)** What percentage of the matches had at least 3 goals? **[2]**

**5.** The table shows museum visitors (thousands) in each quarter of two years.

| | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | 18 | 34 | 52 | 22 |
| Year 2 | 21 | 37 | 58 | 25 |

**(a)** In which quarter are visitors highest each year? Name the repeating pattern. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the trend. **[1]**
**(c)** Estimate the third-quarter visitors in year 3 and give a reason. **[2]**

**6.** A survey records the year group and travel method of 160 pupils.

| | Walk | Bus | Car | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Year 10 | 34 | 27 | A | 90 |
| Year 11 | B | 31 | 18 | 70 |

**(a)** Find A and B. **[2]**
**(b)** A questionnaire asks "How long do you spend online each day?" with boxes "0 to 1 hour", "1 to 2 hours", "2 to 3 hours". Give two faults and write an improved set of boxes. **[3]**

**7.** A composite bar chart shows café sales. Monday (total 100): hot drinks from 0 to 40, cold drinks from 40 to 65, food above. Tuesday (total 120): hot drinks from 0 to 55, cold drinks from 55 to 85, food above.

**(a)** Find Tuesday's cold drink sales and food sales. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain whether a composite or a multiple bar chart makes it easier to compare cold drink sales on the two days. **[2]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) The heights of 80 plants are grouped: 0<h≤20: 6, 20<h≤40: 14, 40<h≤60: 25, 60<h≤80: 21, 80<h≤100: 10, 100<h≤120: 4 (cm).

**(a)** Complete the cumulative frequencies. **[2]**
**(b)** State where the curve begins and the first two points plotted. **[2]**
**(c)** Estimate the median height. **[1]**
**(d)** Estimate the interquartile range. **[2]**
**(e)** Estimate how many plants are taller than 90 cm. **[1]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) 60 pupils cycle to school by each of two routes. Cumulative frequencies of their journey times (minutes) at 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 are: Route A 6, 21, 38, 52, 60; Route B 3, 11, 35, 55, 60. Both curves start at 0 minutes with 0.

**(a)** Estimate the median for each route. **[2]**
**(b)** Estimate the interquartile range for each route. **[2]**
**(c)** Compare the routes. **[2]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) Complete the table for a histogram.

| Class | Frequency | Frequency density |
|---|---|---|
| 0<x≤5 | 10 | 2 |
| 5<x≤15 | 35 | 3.5 |
| 15<x≤20 | P | 6 |
| 20<x≤40 | Q | 1.2 |
| 40<x≤60 | 6 | R |

**(a)** Find P, Q and R. **[3]**
**(b)** Estimate how many values lie between 17 and 30. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) A histogram has frequency density bars: 0<x≤20 height 0.9; 20<x≤25 height 4.8; 25<x≤40 height 2.0; 40<x≤80 height 0.45.

**(a)** Find the frequency of each class. **[3]**
**(b)** Sam says the 20<x≤25 bar is tallest, so it has the most values. Is he right? **[1]**
**(c)** Estimate how many values are between 30 and 60. **[2]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) A histogram shows parcel masses (kg) of 80 parcels. Frequency densities: 0<m≤2: 6; 2<m≤5: 10; 5<m≤10: 4.4; 10<m≤20: 1.4; 20<m≤40: 0.1.

**(a)** Find the frequency of each class. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the cumulative frequencies. **[2]**
**(c)** Estimate the median mass. **[2]**
**(d)** What percentage of the parcels weigh more than 10 kg? **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) All 2,400 members of the library [1]. (b) Only Saturday-morning visitors are asked, so members who visit at other times (or never) are left out [1]. The first 30 through the door are not chosen randomly and may arrive together with similar habits [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A reason earns credit when it names who is missing or why the choice is not random; "the sample is too small" on its own is weaker than a reason tied to the context.

**2.** (a) Generate 25 different random numbers between 1 and 500 [1]. Ignore repeats and draw again [1]. Select the pupils with those numbers [1]. (b) 7/25 of the sample wear glasses [1], so 7/25 × 500 = **140 pupils** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Pick 25 at random" repeats the question; a method must say how random numbers are used and what happens to repeats.

**3.** (a) 360 ÷ 80 = 4.5° per pupil [1]. Football 126°, netball 90° [1]. Swimming 72°, tennis 45°, other 27° [1]. (b) 360 ÷ 90 = 4, so the whole group is 4 times the swimming sector [1]: 36 × 4 = **144 pupils** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Adding your angles to 360° is a quick check, and writing it down shows the method is complete.

**4.** (a) 4 + 7 + 9 + 5 + 3 + 2 = 30 [1]. (b) At least 3 goals: 5 + 3 + 2 = 10 matches [1]. 10/30 × 100 = **33.3%** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "At least 3" includes 3. Writing which lines you added makes the working easy to follow.

**5.** (a) Third quarter (Q3) in both years [1]. This is **seasonal variation** [1]. (b) Upward trend, as each quarter is higher than the same quarter a year earlier [1]. (c) Q3 rose by 6 from year 1 to year 2, so about 58 + 6 = **64 thousand** [1], assuming the pattern and trend continue [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A prediction needs a reason that uses the data; a different estimate close to 64 with a clear reason is also sensible.

**6.** (a) A = 90 − 34 − 27 = **29** [1]; B = 70 − 31 − 18 = **21** [1]. (b) The boxes overlap at 1 and 2 hours [1]. There is no box for 3 hours or more, or for none [1]. Improved: 0 up to 1, 1 up to 2, 2 up to 3, 3 or more [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* An improvement must fix the faults you named, so check yours has no overlaps and no gaps.

**7.** (a) Cold drinks: 85 − 55 = **30** [1]. Food: 120 − 85 = **35** [1]. (b) In a composite chart the cold section starts at a different height each day, so you must subtract to compare [1]. In a multiple bar chart every bar starts at the axis, so the heights compare directly [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Reading the top of the cold section (85) as the sales is a common slip; write the subtraction.

**8.** (a) 6, 20, 45 [1]; 66, 76, 80 [1]. (b) The curve begins at (0, 0) [1]; first points (20, 6) and (40, 20) [1]. (c) 40th value: about **56 cm** [1]. (d) Lower quartile at 20: **40**; upper quartile at 60: about **74** [1]. IQR is about 74 − 40 = **34 cm** [1]. (e) At 90, cumulative frequency is about 71, so 80 − 71 = **9 plants** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Plot at upper boundaries and draw reading lines; answers read from a curve are estimates, so a small tolerance is natural.

**9.** (a) The 30th value for route A lies between 20 and 30 minutes: 20 + (30 − 21)/(38 − 21) × 10 = **25.3 minutes** [1]. Route B: 20 + (30 − 11)/(35 − 11) × 10 = **27.9 minutes** [1]. (b) A: lower quartile 16, upper quartile 35, IQR **19.0** [1]. B: lower quartile 21.7, upper quartile 35, IQR **13.3** [1]. (c) Route B has the higher median, so typical journeys are longer [1]. Route B has the smaller IQR, so its times are more consistent [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs one statement about average and one about spread, each written in the context of journey times.

**10.** (a) P = 5 × 6 = **30** [1]; Q = 20 × 1.2 = **24** [1]; R = 6 ÷ 20 = **0.3** [1]. (b) 17 to 20: 3 × 6 = 18 [1]; 20 to 30: 10 × 1.2 = 12, so total **30** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the width in each multiplication; the width of the class is the usual place for errors.

**11.** (a) Frequency = class width × frequency density [1]: 20 × 0.9 = **18** and 5 × 4.8 = **24** [1]; 15 × 2.0 = **30** and 40 × 0.45 = **18** [1]. (b) No. Frequency is area, and the 25<x≤40 bar is much wider, so it has 30 against 24 [1]. (c) 30 to 40: 10 × 2.0 = 20 [1]; 40 to 60: 20 × 0.45 = 9, so **29** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Whenever class widths differ, use area. Saying "the bar is wider" and quoting the two frequencies makes the explanation complete.

**12.** (a) 2 × 6 = **12**; 3 × 10 = **30** [1]; 5 × 4.4 = **22**; 10 × 1.4 = **14**; 20 × 0.1 = **2** [1]. (b) **12, 42, 64, 78, 80** [2]. (c) The 40th value lies in 2<m≤5 [1]: 2 + (40 − 12)/30 × 3 = **4.8 kg** [1]. (d) 80 − 64 = 16 parcels [1]; 16/80 × 100 = **20%** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Carrying your own frequencies into the cumulative table keeps later parts consistent, so check that the final total equals 80.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a sampling method without saying how random numbers are used, or giving bias reasons that do not say who is left out.
- Pie angles that do not add to 360°, or a total that was never worked out first.
- Reading the top edge of a composite bar section as its value.
- Answering a time series question with no mention of the repeating pattern or the overall direction.
- Overlapping or incomplete categories in a table or questionnaire.
- Plotting cumulative frequency at the wrong point, or missing the starting point at zero.
- Giving a histogram frequency as bar height when widths differ.
