Pearson Edexcel International GCSE English Language A 4EA1 has a compulsory component plus a choice.
Every candidate takes **Component 1: Non-fiction Texts and Transactional Writing** -- 2 hours 15
minutes, 90 marks, 60% of the qualification, split evenly between Section A Reading (45 marks) and
Section B Transactional Writing (45 marks). For the remaining 40% a centre enters candidates for
**either Component 2** (Poetry and Prose Texts and Imaginative Writing, a 1 hour 30 minute externally
assessed written exam, 60 marks: Section A reading essay 30 marks, Section B imaginative writing 30
marks) **or Component 3**, the internally assessed and externally moderated coursework alternative
covering the same content, also 60 marks. These notes complement the site's guides to
[Component 1](/resources/igcse-edexcel-english-language-component-1/) and
[Component 2](/resources/igcse-edexcel-english-language-component-2/).

## Establish which second component you are entered for -- they are prepared for differently

Components 2 and 3 carry identical marks and identical weight but are entirely different tasks: one is
a timed, closed exam, the other is coursework produced over time and moderated by Pearson. **Exam-
preparation priority**: confirm your route early. Revising exam technique for a component you are not
sitting, or leaving coursework thin because you expected an exam, forfeits 40% of the qualification to
an avoidable misunderstanding.

## Component 1's marks are split exactly evenly between reading and writing

Forty-five marks each, in a single 2 hour 15 minute paper, means roughly 65 minutes per section
allowing for reading time. **Exam-preparation priority**: rehearse to that split. The characteristic
failure on this component is an over-developed reading section followed by a transactional writing
task that is unfinished or unchecked -- and the writing section is worth exactly as much.

## Transactional writing is judged on fitness for purpose, not on literary flourish

The Section B task specifies an audience, a purpose and a form -- a letter, an article, a report, a
speech. It is marked on how well the writing does that job and on technical accuracy, not on how
decorative it is. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise the same content in two different forms (a
formal letter and a lively article, say) to build genuine control of register. Candidates who write
everything in one voice lose marks that better content cannot recover.

## Non-fiction reading rewards analysis of effect, not identification of features

Naming a technique -- "the writer uses a rhetorical question" -- is not yet analysis. The mark comes
from explaining what effect the choice has on a reader and how. **Exam-preparation priority**: build
the habit of a three-part sentence: the choice, the effect, the reason it has that effect. Practise it
on unseen non-fiction texts rather than on the anthology alone, since Section A works from
unseen-style material.

## On the coursework route, redrafting is the advantage -- use it

Component 3's assignments are produced with time and feedback available. **Exam-preparation
priority**: use that time on structure and on the specific demands of the task, not only on
proofreading. The most common weakness in coursework is a piece that is well written but never
explicitly addresses the assignment's actual question.

## Worked routine: planning a transactional writing task

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - extract FORM, AUDIENCE, PURPOSE from the task:
Write the three words down. They govern every later decision.

Step 2 - choose the register deliberately:
Formal or conversational? First or third person? Are direct
address and rhetorical questions appropriate to this form?

Step 3 - plan four content points in order:
Not a list of ideas -- an order, so the piece has a shape a reader
can follow.

Step 4 - write an opening that signals the form immediately:
A letter opens like a letter; an article opens with a hook. The
first line should be unmistakable.

Step 5 - reserve five minutes for a targeted accuracy check:
Check for your own recurring errors specifically -- the punctuation
or agreement mistakes you personally repeat -- rather than
re-reading generally.
```

Step 5 works because a general re-read tends to confirm that the piece reads well; a targeted check
finds the errors that are actually costing technical accuracy marks.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: confirm whether you are entered for Component 2 or Component 3; practise
  Component 1 to a 65-minute-per-section split; write the same content in two different forms to
  build register control; practise effect-based analysis on unseen non-fiction.
- **During Component 1**: hold the reading section to its time and start the writing task on
  schedule, whatever state the reading answers are in.
- **In any writing task**: fix form, audience and purpose first, plan an order rather than a list,
  and reserve five minutes for a targeted accuracy check.
- **On the coursework route**: redraft for structure and for the assignment's actual question, not
  only for surface errors.

## Self-test

1. Why does it matter, early, which second component you are entered for?
2. How are Component 1's 90 marks divided, and what follows for timing?
3. What turns feature-spotting into analysis?
4. Why is a targeted accuracy check better than a general re-read?

**Answers:** 1. Because Components 2 and 3 carry identical weight (40%) but are completely different
tasks -- a timed exam versus moderated coursework -- so preparing for the wrong one forfeits 40% of the
qualification. 2. Evenly: 45 marks for Section A Reading and 45 for Section B Transactional Writing,
which implies roughly 65 minutes per section within the 2 hour 15 minute paper. 3. Explaining the
effect of the writer's choice on a reader and why it has that effect, rather than naming the technique
used. 4. Because a general re-read confirms that the piece reads acceptably, while checking for the
specific errors you personally repeat finds the ones actually costing technical accuracy marks.
