## Questions

**1.** Explain why the proteome of a bacterium can be predicted from its genome sequence more easily than the proteome of a human. **[3]**

**2.** The genome of a pathogenic bacterium has been sequenced. Explain how this information could be used to help produce a vaccine against it. **[4]**

**3.** A forensic laboratory starts a PCR with 6 DNA molecules.
**(a)** Calculate the number of molecules after 7 cycles. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the smallest whole number of cycles needed to produce at least 10 000 molecules. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why Taq polymerase is used. **[2]**

**4.** A human gene is to be expressed in bacteria. Explain why scientists make the gene from mRNA using reverse transcriptase instead of cutting it from human chromosomal DNA. **[4]**

**5.** A linear piece of DNA is 35 base pairs long. The top strand reads ACGGATCCTTAGCAGGATCCAATGCGGATCCTTCA. A restriction endonuclease recognises GGATCC and cuts between the first G and the second G on each strand.
**(a)** Give the lengths of the fragments produced (top strand). **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the fragments from this DNA can join to a plasmid cut with the same enzyme. **[2]**
**(c)** State the role of DNA ligase in this process. **[2]**

**6.** Bacteria were treated with a plasmid carrying a gene for a fluorescent protein. The plasmid also carried the human gene being cloned. Of 268 colonies on nutrient agar, 37 fluoresced under ultraviolet light.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage of colonies that were transformed. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the fluorescent gene is called a marker gene. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a promoter and a terminator are placed on either side of the human gene. **[2]**

**7.** A normal allele has the region 5' GCATTGACCTA 3'. A mutant allele has a single base change, giving 5' GCATTGTCCTA 3'.
**(a)** Write the 5' to 3' sequence of a probe that is complementary to the mutant region. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how this labelled probe is used to find whether a patient's DNA contains the mutant allele. **[3]**

**8.** A couple are both unaffected, but their first child has a recessive genetic condition.
**(a)** State the genotypes of the parents and the probability that their second child is affected. **[2]**
**(b)** A man's mother is a known carrier of a different recessive allele and his father is not. His partner is from a population where 1 in 50 people are carriers. Calculate the probability that their child is affected. **[3]**

**9.** A gene variant makes a patient break down a drug slowly. Evaluate the use of DNA screening for this variant before prescribing the drug. **[4]**

**10.** Fragments of DNA from a crime scene (C) and three suspects (S1 to S3) were separated by gel electrophoresis. Band sizes in kilobases are: C 7.2, 5.1, 3.8, 2.4; S1 7.2, 5.1, 4.4, 2.4; S2 7.2, 5.1, 3.8, 2.4; S3 6.0, 5.1, 3.8, 1.9.
**(a)** Identify which suspect's profile matches C and give the evidence. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the 2.4 kb band is closer to the end of the gel than the 7.2 kb band. **[3]**
**(c)** The chances of a coincidental match at four independent loci are 0.05, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.1. Calculate the chance of a coincidental match across all four. **[2]**

**11.** Explain how genetic fingerprinting can help a breeder of a rare animal species. **[3]**

**12.** A company proposes to sell crop seed carrying a gene transferred from another species, to farmers in a region with food shortages. Evaluate the ethical, financial and social issues. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** A bacterial genome has little non-coding DNA and few regulatory genes [1], so the base sequence can be read with the genetic code to give amino acid sequences [1]. A human genome has much non-coding DNA and many regulatory genes, so the genome cannot easily be translated into the proteome [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both organisms. Describing only the bacterium leaves the contrast unsaid.

**2.** Use the sequence to identify genes and predict the proteins [1]. Select proteins on the pathogen surface [1] that could act as antigens recognised by the immune system [1]. Produce this protein (or part of it) and use it in the vaccine to stimulate an immune response [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the link from gene to protein to antigen is what separates a complete answer from a general statement about vaccines.

**3. (a)** 6 × 2⁷ [1] = **768** molecules [1].
**(b)** 6 × 2¹⁰ = 6144, which is below 10 000 [1]; 6 × 2¹¹ = 12 288, so **11 cycles** [1].
**(c)** It is heat-stable [1], so it keeps working after the mixture is heated to about 95 °C in every cycle [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (b) show the value just below the target as well as the one above it; stating only the final number gives no evidence that 10 cycles falls short.

**4.** Human chromosomal DNA contains introns [1]. Bacteria cannot remove introns from the transcript [1]. mRNA has already had introns removed [1]. Reverse transcriptase makes cDNA with no introns, which bacteria can transcribe and translate [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Bacteria cannot splice introns" is the explanation. Saying only that mRNA is "easier" does not answer the question.

**5. (a)** GGATCC occurs starting at bases 3, 15 and 26 [1]. Cuts fall after bases 3, 15 and 26 [1]. Fragments are **3, 12, 11 and 9 bases** (sum 35) [1].
**(b)** The cut leaves single-stranded sticky ends [1] whose bases are complementary to the sticky ends on the plasmid, so they pair by hydrogen bonds [1].
**(c)** It joins the sugar-phosphate backbones [1] by forming phosphodiester bonds between the fragment and plasmid [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the cut positions and the check that the lengths add up to 35. Writing only a list of lengths gives no evidence of method.

**6. (a)** 37 ÷ 268 [1] × 100 = **13.8%** [1].
**(b)** It lets you detect which cells took up the plasmid [1] because only transformed cells fluoresce [1].
**(c)** The promoter is where RNA polymerase binds to start transcription [1]; the terminator stops transcription at the end of the gene [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the percentage to a sensible number of significant figures and link the marker to its purpose of detection.

**7. (a)** The mutant strand is 5' GCATTGTCCTA 3'. The complement is written in reverse: **5' TAGGACAATGC 3'**, with correct base pairing [1] and the correct 5' to 3' direction [1].
**(b)** Separate the patient's DNA into single strands (denature) [1]. Add the labelled probe, which binds by complementary base pairing to the mutant allele only [1]. Wash off unbound probe and detect the label (radioactivity or fluorescence); a signal shows the mutant allele is present [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing the complement in the wrong direction is a common slip; label 5' and 3' ends so the direction is checkable.

**8. (a)** Both parents are heterozygous carriers [1]; probability that the second child is affected = **1/4** [1].
**(b)** The man is a carrier with probability 1/2 [1]; 1/2 × 1/50 × 1/4 [1] = 1/400 = **0.25%** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Lay out each probability being multiplied. A single final fraction without working earns less than a clear chain of steps.

**9.** For: identifies patients at risk of side effects [1]; allows a different drug or lower dose, which is personalised medicine [1]. Against: cost of screening [1]; privacy and possible discrimination with genetic data [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" needs points on both sides and not only a list of benefits.

**10. (a)** **S2** [1]; all four bands match C (7.2, 5.1, 3.8, 2.4) [1].
**(b)** DNA carries a negative charge, so it moves towards the positive electrode [1]. The gel acts as a mesh, so smaller fragments pass through more easily [1]. A 2.4 kb fragment is shorter than a 7.2 kb fragment, so it moves further in the same time [1].
**(c)** 0.05 × 0.1 × 0.2 × 0.1 [1] = 0.0001, **1 in 10 000** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) needs the link between size and distance, not only the statement that small fragments move further.

**11.** It can show which animals are closely related [1], so a breeder can avoid matings between close relatives [1] and measure genetic variability in the population, to keep diversity [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the biological consequence, such as avoiding inbreeding, rather than saying only that it "checks relatedness".

**12.** Humanitarian and financial benefits: higher yield or nutrition could reduce hunger [1]; farmers might earn more [1]. Concerns: patents and seed prices could make farmers dependent on one company [1]; poor farmers may not afford the seed [1]. Environmental and ethical: gene flow to wild relatives or harm to other organisms, as environmentalists argue [1]. A judgement is given which weighs the two sides, for example that benefits depend on access and monitoring [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A final reasoned judgement is what turns a list of points into an evaluation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not stating that mRNA has had its introns removed when explaining cDNA.
- Writing "bases" or "genes" where the question needs "amino acids" or "proteins".
- Treating the heat-stable polymerase as the only reason PCR works and forgetting the primers.
- Showing a PCR answer with no working, or stopping at the first value above the target.
- Writing probe sequences in the wrong direction or identical to the allele.
- Confusing ligase (joins) with restriction endonuclease (cuts).
- Saying that larger DNA fragments move further through a gel.
- Treating a fingerprint match as proof instead of giving the chance of coincidence.
- Giving a one-sided evaluation with no judgement.
