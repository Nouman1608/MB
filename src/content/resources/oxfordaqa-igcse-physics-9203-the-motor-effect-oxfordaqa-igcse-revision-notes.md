These notes condense section **3.6.4 The motor effect** (statements a to d) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) specification, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered. For worked direction examples read the [motor effect study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-the-motor-effect-oxfordaqa-igcse/), then use the [motor effect practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-the-motor-effect-oxfordaqa-igcse-practice/).

## Condensed recall

**The motor effect (a).** A current-carrying wire has its own magnetic field. Placed in a magnet's field so that it cuts the field lines, the wire and the magnet exert a force on each other. Wire **parallel** to the field → **no force**. Force is largest at 90° to the field.

**Bigger force (b).**

1. Stronger magnetic field.
2. Larger current.
3. Longer length of conductor in the field.

**Direction (c).** Reverse the current **or** the field → force reverses. Reverse **both** → force unchanged. Find the direction with Fleming's left-hand rule.

**Motor (d).** A current-carrying coil in a magnetic field tends to rotate. The two long sides carry current in opposite directions, so the forces on them are opposite, giving a turning effect about the axle.

## Fleming's left-hand rule

| Finger | Stands for | Points |
|---|---|---|
| First finger | Field | North to south |
| Second finger | Current | Positive to negative (conventional current) |
| Thumb | Motion (force) | Direction of the force on the wire |

Diagram symbols: ⊙ = current out of the page; ⊗ = current into the page.

## Direction table (field from left to right)

| Current | Force |
|---|---|
| Into the page | Down |
| Out of the page | Up |
| Up the page | Into the page |
| Down the page | Out of the page |
| Left to right (parallel to field) | None |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Motor effect | Force between a magnet and a current-carrying conductor that cuts its field lines |
| Magnetic field lines (lines of force) | Lines showing the direction of the field, from N to S |
| Conventional current | Current direction from the positive to the negative terminal |
| Fleming's left-hand rule | Rule linking field, current and force directions |
| Coil | Several turns of wire, so a greater length of wire lies in the field |
| Electric motor | Device in which a current-carrying coil rotates in a magnetic field |
| Turning effect | The tendency of the opposite forces on the coil sides to rotate it |

## Coil rotation in four lines

- Coil plane parallel to the field: forces on the long sides give the largest turning effect.
- Short sides parallel to the field: no force on them.
- Coil plane perpendicular to the field: forces act through the axle, so no turning effect.
- To keep turning, the current in the coil is reversed every half turn.

**Faster motor:** more turns, larger current, stronger field.

## Applying the rule in three steps

1. Point your first finger along the field, from N to S.
2. Keeping it there, rotate your hand until your second finger points along the conventional current.
3. Read the force from your thumb and describe it in words: up, down, left, right, into or out of the page.

If the current runs along the field direction, your fingers cannot be at right angles: there is no force. Check every answer by reversing one quantity in your head; the thumb should flip.

## Newton's third law link

The force on the wire has an equal and opposite partner acting on the magnet. If the wire is pushed up, the magnet is pushed down.

## Exam traps

- Left hand for the motor effect, not right.
- Second finger follows conventional current, not electrons.
- Field points N to S.
- Reversing both current and field gives the same direction as before.
- No force on a wire parallel to the field.
- There is no equation for the size of the force in this specification; questions ask about factors only.

## Quick self-test

1. State what is meant by the motor effect.
2. When does a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field feel no force?
3. Give three ways to increase the force on a wire in a magnetic field.
4. In Fleming's left-hand rule, what does the thumb represent?
5. Field left to right, current out of the page. Which way is the force?
6. Field left to right, current into the page. Which way is the force?
7. The current in question 6 and the magnet are both reversed. Which way is the force?
8. Field from the top of the page to the bottom, current into the page. Which way is the force?
9. Why does a coil carrying a current in a magnetic field rotate?
10. In which position does a coil have no turning effect?
11. Give two ways to make a motor spin faster.
12. A wire pushed upwards by the motor effect sits between the poles of a magnet resting on a balance. What happens to the balance reading?

### Answers

1. A force between a magnet and a current-carrying conductor placed in its field so that it cuts the field lines.
2. When it is parallel to the field lines.
3. Stronger magnetic field, larger current, longer length of wire in the field.
4. The direction of the force (motion).
5. **Up**.
6. **Down**.
7. **Down** (unchanged).
8. **To the left**.
9. The current flows in opposite directions in the two sides, so the forces on them are opposite, creating a turning effect.
10. When the plane of the coil is perpendicular to the field (forces act through the axle).
11. Any two: increase the current, use a stronger magnet, use more turns on the coil.
12. It increases, because the magnet is pushed downwards with an equal and opposite force.
