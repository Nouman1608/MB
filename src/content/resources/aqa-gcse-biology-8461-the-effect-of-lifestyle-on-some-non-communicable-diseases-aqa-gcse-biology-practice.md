These questions cover section **4.2.2.6 The effect of lifestyle on some non-communicable diseases** of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, for teaching from September 2016 and exams from 2018 onwards. The content is assessed on Paper 1 at Foundation and Higher Tier, and nothing in 4.2.2.6 is marked (HT only). A calculator is useful for questions 6 and 9. All data are invented.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State whether each risk factor is an aspect of lifestyle or a substance in the body or environment.

**(a)** High blood cholesterol **[1]**
**(b)** Drinking alcohol **[1]**
**(c)** Ionising radiation **[1]**
**(d)** Lack of exercise **[1]**

**2.** State what is meant by saying that a causal mechanism has been proven for a risk factor. **[1]**

**3.** Explain how a diet high in saturated fat increases the risk of cardiovascular disease. **[3]**

**4.** Explain why a heavy smoker is more likely than a non-smoker to develop lung cancer. **[3]**

**5.** Explain why drinking alcohol during pregnancy may harm the baby. **[2]**

### Section B: data and longer questions

**6.** A study followed adults for 15 years and recorded new cases of liver disease.

| Alcohol intake | People | New cases | Rate per 100 000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| None | 10 000 | 8 | 80 |
| Low | 15 000 | 18 | 120 |
| Moderate | 8 000 | 24 | |
| Heavy | 2 000 | 30 | |

**(a)** Calculate the missing rates for the moderate and heavy groups. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times more likely the heavy drinkers were to develop liver disease than the non-drinkers. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the pattern shown by the rates. **[2]**

**7.** A scatter diagram plots the percentage of adults who smoke in 15 regions against the incidence of lung cancer in each region. The points rise from left to right and lie close to a straight line.

**(a)** Describe the correlation fully. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest two other factors that could affect the incidence of lung cancer in the regions. **[2]**

**8.** A team investigated whether eating fried food is a risk factor for heart disease. They used answers from 200 people who chose to fill in an online survey. Evaluate the sampling method. **[3]**

**9.** A country has 60 million people. The incidence of Type 2 diabetes is 250 new cases per 100 000 people per year.

**(a)** Calculate the expected number of new cases each year. **[2]**
**(b)** A campaign reduces the incidence by 20%. Calculate how many fewer new cases there would be each year. **[2]**

**10.** Explain the effect of a rise in obesity on the incidence of Type 2 diabetes at a national level. **[3]**

**11.** Discuss the human and financial costs of lung disease caused by smoking to an individual, a local community and a nation. **[6]**

**12.** Two people eat the same diet, smoke the same amount and take the same exercise. Only one develops cardiovascular disease. Explain how this can happen. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Substance in the body [1]
**(b)** Lifestyle [1]
**(c)** Substance or agent in the environment [1]
**(d)** Lifestyle [1]
*Examiner insight:* Cholesterol is a substance in the body, even though diet affects its level.

**2.** Scientists can explain how the factor actually causes the disease, not only that the two are linked [1]
*Examiner insight:* "It has been proved that they are linked" describes a correlation, not a mechanism.

**3.** Saturated fat raises the level of cholesterol in the blood [1]; fatty material is then deposited in the walls of arteries, including the coronary arteries [1]; the arteries narrow, reducing blood flow and the supply of oxygen to the heart muscle [1]
*Examiner insight:* Finish the chain with the effect on blood flow or oxygen supply.

**4.** Tobacco smoke contains tar [1], which contains carcinogens [1]; these damage the DNA in lung cells, which can lead to uncontrolled cell division and a tumour [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Smoking damages the lungs" is too vague for any mark.

**5.** Alcohol passes from the mother's blood to the developing baby [1]; it can harm the baby's development, including the brain [1]
*Examiner insight:* Smoking and alcohol both appear in this statement; make sure you answer about the one asked.

**6. (a)** Moderate: 24 ÷ 8 000 × 100 000 = **300** [1]; heavy: 30 ÷ 2 000 × 100 000 = **1500** [1]
**(b)** 1500 ÷ 80 = 18.75, so about **19 times** [1]
**(c)** The rate of liver disease increases as alcohol intake increases [1]; the increase is much steeper at higher intakes, from 120 (low) to 300 (moderate) to 1500 (heavy) per 100 000 [1]
*Examiner insight:* The heavy group had more cases than the moderate group, but the key evidence is the rate, because the groups are different sizes.

**7. (a)** Positive correlation [1]; strong, because the points lie close to a line [1]
**(b)** Any two: exposure to other carcinogens, such as ionising radiation or air pollution [1]; differences in the age of the population [1]; genetic risk factors; how many people work with harmful substances
*Examiner insight:* "Suggest" accepts sensible factors that could differ between regions.

**8.** 200 is a fairly small sample [1]; people who chose to fill in an online survey are not a random sample and may not represent the population, for example by age or health [1]; answers about diet are self-reported and may be inaccurate, so the conclusion is less reliable [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each criticism needs its consequence for the reliability of the conclusion.

**9. (a)** 60 000 000 ÷ 100 000 = 600 [1]; 600 × 250 = **150 000 new cases** [1]
**(b)** 20% of 250 = 50 fewer per 100 000 [1]; 600 × 50 = **30 000 fewer cases** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Allow the alternative method 20% of 150 000 = 30 000 for both marks.

**10.** Obesity is a risk factor for Type 2 diabetes [1]; if more people in the country are obese, more people's body cells stop responding properly to insulin [1]; so the number of new cases of Type 2 diabetes in the country rises [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name the factor, the effect in the body and the change in incidence.

**11.** Marking points (levels-marked; a top answer covers human and financial costs at all three levels):
- individual, human: breathlessness, poorer quality of life, shorter life [1]
- individual, financial: lost earnings if unable to work [1]
- local community: family members become carers; local health services treat more patients [1]
- nation, financial: the cost of treatment through the health service [1]
- nation: fewer people able to work, so productivity falls [1]
- a balanced conclusion, such as that the costs are spread far beyond the smoker [1]
*Examiner insight:* An answer that covers only money, or only the individual, stays in the lower levels.

**12.** Many diseases are caused by the interaction of a number of factors [1]; the people may differ in other factors, such as their genes, so the same lifestyle carries a different risk for each [1]
*Examiner insight:* A risk factor raises the chance of disease; it does not decide the outcome on its own.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling high cholesterol a lifestyle factor.
- Saying smoking "damages the lungs" without tar or carcinogens.
- Comparing case numbers in groups of different sizes.
- Describing a correlation without its strength.
- Covering one level only in a "discuss" question.
