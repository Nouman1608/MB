**Format text** is the second skill under Topic 17 Document production in Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417. The syllabus lists four groups: line spacing ("single, 1.5 times, double, multiple, spacing before and after paragraphs"); tabulation ("left, right, centred and decimal tabs, indented paragraphs and hanging paragraphs"); text enhancement ("bold, underline, italic, superscript and subscript, changes in case"); and lists ("bulleted, numbered"). Each is something you will be told to apply in the practical paper, and each has a correct method and a tempting wrong one. This guide goes further than the [Document production study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-document-production-study-guide/) on these settings.

## Line spacing

**Line spacing** is the distance from one line of text to the next *within* a paragraph.

| Setting | Effect |
|---|---|
| Single | Lines as close as the font size allows |
| 1.5 lines | One and a half times single spacing |
| Double | Twice single spacing; leaves room for handwritten comments on a draft |
| Multiple | Any multiple you type, such as 1.15 or 3 |

Line spacing is applied to whole paragraphs. To change the spacing of a whole document, select all the body text, or better, change the body text style.

## Spacing before and after paragraphs

**Paragraph spacing** is the gap *between* paragraphs, set in points (pt) as "before" and "after". An instruction such as "6 pt after each paragraph" means that every paragraph has a 6 pt gap below it. Set the before and after values exactly as instructed, leave the other at 0 pt unless told otherwise, and check the result in print preview.

The wrong method is pressing Enter to make an empty paragraph. Empty paragraphs give a gap of a whole line, cannot be set to an exact size, and become stray blank lines at the top of a page when text moves. Show formatting marks to find them: each one appears as a ¶ on its own.

## Tabulation

A **tab stop** is a position on the ruler where text lines up when the Tab key is pressed. The four types in the syllabus differ in how text lines up at the stop.

| Tab | Text lines up... | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Left | starting at the stop, running to the right | Columns of words |
| Right | ending at the stop | Page numbers, amounts without decimals, codes at the right margin |
| Centred | centred on the stop | A short heading over a column |
| Decimal | with the decimal point on the stop | Prices and measurements |

Set tab stops at exact positions from the paragraph or tabs dialogue (for example "decimal tab at 12 cm"), or by clicking the ruler for approximate positions. Use one Tab press per stop. Pressing Tab several times to reach a position, or using the space bar, gives columns that do not line up, especially with proportional fonts where each character has a different width.

### Worked example: a price list with tabs

*Task (invented):* "Set a left tab at 1 cm, a centred tab at 9 cm and a decimal tab at 14 cm. Enter the item, size and price for each line."

Each line is typed as: Tab, item, Tab, size, Tab, price, Enter.

| Item (left tab at 1 cm) | Size (centred tab at 9 cm) | Price (decimal tab at 14 cm) |
|---|---|---|
| Notebook | A5 | 2.50 |
| Folder | A4 | 12.75 |
| Poster tube | Large | 104.00 |

With the decimal tab, 2.50, 12.75 and 104.00 line up on their decimal points, so units sit under units. A right tab would line up the last digits instead, which also works here only because every price has two decimal places; with prices such as 3.5 and 12.25 the decimal tab is the one that keeps the points in line. The sizes are centred under the 9 cm position whatever their length. Setting the tab stops on the first line and then pressing Enter carries them to each new line.

## Indented and hanging paragraphs

An **indent** moves the edge of a paragraph in from the margin. It is set with the indent markers on the ruler or with exact values in the paragraph dialogue, never with spaces or tabs at the start of every line.

- **Left indent**: the whole paragraph moves in from the left margin, for example a quotation set 1.5 cm in.
- **Right indent**: the right edge moves in from the right margin.
- **First-line indent**: only the first line moves in, as in many novels.
- **Hanging paragraph** (hanging indent): the first line starts at the left and every following line is indented, so the first line "hangs" out. It is used for reference lists, glossaries and numbered points, so that the start of each entry stands out.

For example, a hanging indent of 1 cm on a glossary entry puts the term at the margin and wraps the rest of the definition 1 cm in, keeping each term easy to find.

## Text enhancement

- **Bold**: emphasis for key words or headings.
- **Italic**: titles of works, foreign words, gentle emphasis.
- **Underline**: use sparingly; on screen it can be mistaken for a hyperlink.
- **Superscript** raises small characters above the line: squared units (m²), cubed units (cm³), powers (2⁸), ordinal endings (1st written with a raised *st*) and footnote numbers.
- **Subscript** lowers small characters below the line: chemical formulae such as H₂O and CO₂.

Apply superscript and subscript only to the characters that need it. Selecting "m2" and making both characters superscript is a common error; only the 2 should change.

### Changes in case

| Case option | Result for "the river trail" |
|---|---|
| UPPERCASE | THE RIVER TRAIL |
| lowercase | the river trail |
| Sentence case | The river trail |
| Capitalise each word (title case) | The River Trail |
| Toggle case (applied to "The River Trail") | tHE rIVER tRAIL: every letter swaps case |

Use the software's change case option rather than retyping. Retyping can introduce spelling errors, and the instruction is testing that you can use the tool.

## Lists

- A **bulleted list** is for items in no particular order. The bullet character can be changed, for example to a square or a tick symbol.
- A **numbered list** is for steps in order or ranked items. The style can be 1, 2, 3 or a, b, c or i, ii, iii.

Editing lists includes adding or removing an item (the numbers update automatically), changing a bulleted list to a numbered one and back, **restarting** numbering at 1 for a new list or **continuing** it from a previous list, and increasing the indent of an item to create a sub-level. Typing numbers by hand is the wrong method: if an item is inserted, every number after it must be retyped.

## Worked example: applying several instructions

*Task (invented):* "Apply 1.5 line spacing and 6 pt after to all body text. Make the five steps a numbered list with lower-case letters. Change the heading to title case. In the line 'Area: 25 m2', format the 2 correctly."

1. Select the body text (or modify the body style): line spacing 1.5 lines, spacing after 6 pt, before 0 pt.
2. Select the five steps and apply numbering, then choose the a, b, c style.
3. Select the heading and apply capitalise each word.
4. Select only the 2 in "25 m2" and apply superscript, so it reads 25 m².
5. Show formatting marks: check there are no empty paragraphs between body paragraphs and no typed letters before the list items.

## Common mistakes

- Using Enter to create space between paragraphs instead of spacing after.
- Using spaces or many tab presses to line up columns.
- Choosing a right tab for prices with different numbers of decimal places, where a decimal tab is needed.
- Making a hanging indent by pressing Enter and Tab at the start of each line.
- Superscripting the whole unit instead of only the number.
- Typing list numbers by hand.

## Exam technique

Instructions in the practical paper are precise: "6 pt after" means after, not before, and "double" means the double setting, not 2 pt. Check each setting in the paragraph dialogue box. On the theory paper, *describe* a tab or indent by saying where the text lines up, and *explain* why a setting is better than the manual method by saying what goes wrong when text is edited.

## Check your understanding

1. What is the difference between line spacing and spacing after? *Line spacing is within a paragraph; spacing after is the gap below each paragraph.*
2. Which tab lines up prices on the decimal point? *A decimal tab.*
3. Describe a hanging paragraph. *The first line starts at the margin and the following lines are indented.*
4. Give one use of subscript. *Chemical formulae, for example the 2 in H₂O.*
5. Apply sentence case to "SAFETY RULES FOR THE LAB". *Safety rules for the lab.*
6. Why should list numbers not be typed by hand? *They do not update when items are added, removed or moved.*
