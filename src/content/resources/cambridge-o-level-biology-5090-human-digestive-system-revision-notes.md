These notes condense subtopic **8.2 Human digestive system** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the regions of the digestive system and their functions, physical and chemical digestion, teeth, the digestive enzymes with substrates and end-products, hydrochloric acid, pH and proteases, bile, and peristalsis. The full explanations and the worked emulsification example are in the [8.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-human-digestive-system/). Then try the [8.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-human-digestive-system-practice/).

## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning in exam wording |
|---|---|
| Ingestion | Taking food into the mouth |
| Physical digestion | Breakdown of food into smaller pieces without chemical change to the food molecules |
| Chemical digestion | Breakdown of large molecules into small molecules by enzymes |
| Peristalsis | Waves of contractions of longitudinal and circular muscles that move food through the gut |
| Egestion | Removal of undigested food (faeces) through the anus |
| Emulsification | Breaking large fat droplets into smaller droplets, by bile |

## Why digest?

Large insoluble molecules (starch, protein, lipids) cannot cross the gut wall. Digestion makes small soluble molecules that can be absorbed into the blood.

## Regions and functions

| Region | Function |
|---|---|
| Mouth | Ingestion; chewing (physical digestion); saliva with amylase begins starch digestion |
| Salivary glands | Secrete saliva containing amylase |
| Oesophagus | Carries food to the stomach |
| Stomach | Churning (physical); hydrochloric acid; protease |
| Duodenum | Receives bile and pancreatic juice; chemical digestion |
| Ileum | Chemical digestion; absorption |
| Pancreas | Alkaline secretion with amylase, protease and lipase |
| Liver | Makes bile; stores glycogen |
| Gall bladder | Stores bile |
| Colon | Absorbs water |
| Rectum and anus | Egestion |

## Enzymes

| Enzyme | Substrate | End-product |
|---|---|---|
| Amylase | Starch | Maltose |
| Maltase | Maltose | Glucose |
| Protease (pepsin, trypsin) | Protein | Amino acids |
| Lipase | Lipids | Fatty acids and glycerol |

- **Pepsin**: stomach, works best at low pH (acid, about pH 2).
- **Trypsin**: duodenum, works best at higher pH (alkaline, about pH 8).
- Hydrochloric acid **kills bacteria** in the food.

## Bile

- Made in the liver, stored in the gall bladder, released into the duodenum.
- Contains **no enzymes**.
- **Emulsifies fats**: large droplets become many small droplets.
- Larger surface area, so lipase digests faster.

Worked example: a 1 cm cube (surface area 6 cm squared) split into 1000 cubes of 1 mm has a total surface area of 1000 x 0.06 = 60 cm squared, which is 10 times as much.

## Teeth

| Type | Function |
|---|---|
| Incisors | Cut and bite |
| Canines | Tear |
| Premolars | Crush and grind |
| Molars | Crush and grind |

Structure: **enamel** (hardest, outer), **dentine** (under enamel), **pulp** (centre, nerves and blood vessels), **cement** (holds the root in the jaw). The tooth is embedded in the **gum**.

## Peristalsis

Waves of contraction of the circular and longitudinal muscles squeeze food along the gut. It happens in the oesophagus, stomach, small intestine and large intestine.

## Short model answers

**Explain why chewing helps digestion.** Chewing breaks food into smaller pieces, which increases the surface area, so enzymes can act on it faster.

**State the role of hydrochloric acid in the stomach.** It kills bacteria that were swallowed with the food (and gives the right pH for pepsin).

**Explain the role of bile.** Bile emulsifies fat into small droplets, which increases the surface area for lipase to digest the fat to fatty acids and glycerol.

**Explain why starch digestion goes on in the small intestine as well as the mouth.** Starch that was not digested in the mouth meets amylase from the pancreas in the small intestine.

**Describe peristalsis.** Waves of contraction of the circular and longitudinal muscles in the gut wall push the food along.

## Exam traps

- Bile **emulsifies**; lipase **digests**.
- Amylase makes **maltose**, not glucose.
- Bile has no enzymes.
- Egestion is not excretion.
- The gall bladder **stores** bile; the liver **makes** it.
- Absorption occurs mainly in the **ileum** (and water in the colon).
- Enzymes in the stomach and duodenum work at different pH values.

## Self-test

1. Why must food be digested?
2. Define physical digestion.
3. Name the four types of teeth.
4. Name the hard outer layer of a tooth.
5. Where are nerves found in a tooth?
6. What does amylase break down, and into what?
7. What does lipase produce?
8. State the function of hydrochloric acid.
9. Which organ makes bile?
10. How does bile help the digestion of fat?
11. What is peristalsis?
12. Which region absorbs most water, and which stores faeces?

### Answers

1. Large insoluble molecules must be broken into small soluble molecules that can be absorbed.
2. Breaking food into smaller pieces without chemical change to the food molecules.
3. Incisors, canines, premolars, molars.
4. Enamel.
5. In the pulp.
6. Starch into maltose.
7. Fatty acids and glycerol.
8. Kills bacteria in the food (and provides acid conditions for pepsin).
9. The liver.
10. It emulsifies fat into small droplets, giving a larger surface area for lipase.
11. Waves of contraction of the longitudinal and circular muscles that move food through the gut.
12. The small intestine absorbs most water (the colon also absorbs water); the rectum stores faeces.
