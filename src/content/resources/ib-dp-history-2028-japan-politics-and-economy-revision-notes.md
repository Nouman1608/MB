These revision notes are for **higher level (HL) only**. Politics and economy in Japan (1912 to 2020) is a regional study examined on Paper 3, which standard level students do not sit. They condense the IB *History guide*, first assessment 2028. Read the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-japan-politics-and-economy/) first, then try the [practice set](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-japan-politics-and-economy-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Taishō | The reign of Emperor Yoshihito, 1912 to 1926; a period of liberal politics |
| Shōwa | The reign of Hirohito, 1926 to 1989 |
| Zaibatsu | Large family-controlled business groups such as Mitsui and Mitsubishi |
| Imperial Way Faction | Army group favouring direct action and an imperial restoration |
| Black Dragon Society | Nationalist society founded in 1901 that backed expansion in Asia |
| Co-Prosperity Sphere | Japan's plan for an Asian bloc under its leadership (announced 1940) |
| SCAP | Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers; MacArthur's occupation authority |
| Article 9 | Constitutional clause renouncing war |
| Reverse Course | Change in US occupation policy from 1947, favouring stability and anti-communism |
| MITI | Ministry of International Trade and Industry |
| Keiretsu | Post-war groups of firms linked by banks and shareholdings |
| Karōshi | Death from overwork |

## The 1920s: strengths and weaknesses of liberalism

| Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|
| Party governments from 1918 | Army and navy remained outside civilian control |
| Universal male suffrage (1925) | Peace Preservation Law limited dissent |
| Washington system and cooperation | Naval ratio resented |
| Growing unions and press | Rural poverty and zaibatsu influence |

## Path to militarism: a causal chain

1. Resentment of the Western powers (racial clause, naval ratio).
2. The Depression, rural distress and anger at parties and business.
3. Ultra-nationalist ideas and societies.
4. Assassinations and coups (1930 to 1936), ending party government.
5. The army's initiative in Manchuria (1931) and China (1937).
6. Isolation, the Tripartite Pact and the search for resources.

## Why Japan lost

- **Strategic:** attacked a far larger economy; no plan to end the China war.
- **Military:** Midway; submarines; loss of shipping; island hopping; bombing.
- **Political:** rivalry between army and navy; no strong central authority.
- **Economic:** shortages of oil and raw materials.
- **Home front:** rationing, forced labour, student and women conscription.
- **Final blows:** atomic bombs and Soviet entry in August 1945.

## Occupation: reforms and the Reverse Course

| Reform | Result |
|---|---|
| New constitution (1947) | Diet supreme; Article 9; women's vote |
| Land reform | Tenants became owners; rural conservatism |
| Zaibatsu dissolution | Only partly achieved; keiretsu emerged |
| Demilitarisation, trials | Military abolished; emperor kept as symbol |
| Reverse Course | Purges eased; unions curbed; Dodge Line; Korean War boom |

## Reasons for the economic miracle

1. MITI industrial policy and protection of key industries.
2. High savings and investment; main-bank finance.
3. Education and skilled workforce; company unions.
4. Low defence spending under Article 9 and the US alliance.
5. LDP stability after 1955.
6. Access to US markets; cheap oil; the Korean War boost.

## After 1989: key issues

| Issue | Detail |
|---|---|
| Lost Decade | Bubble burst; bad loans; deflation; low growth |
| Politics | LDP out in 1993 and 2009 to 2012; short-lived governments; Koizumi; Abe |
| Foreign relations | Yasukuni, comfort women, territorial disputes, China's rise, North Korea |
| Work culture | Long hours; karōshi; reform of overtime rules |
| Demography | Fertility about 1.4; population peaked around 2008; ageing society |

## Perspectives

- Johnson: developmental state. Critics: private firms and markets mattered more.
- Dower: occupation as an interaction with Japanese society.
- Fascism debate: fascist or a unique military-bureaucratic system.
- 1941: forced into war or chose it.

## Worked example: a short plan

**Question:** "Discuss the reasons for the decline of party government in Japan in the early 1930s."

1. Thesis: party government fell because of economic crisis, political violence and army power, with its own weaknesses as an underlying cause.
2. The Depression and gold standard.
3. Corruption and links to zaibatsu.
4. Assassinations: Hamaguchi, 1932, February 26 Incident.
5. The army's actions in Manchuria and public support.
6. Judgement.

## Common mistakes

- Dating Japanese militarism from 1931 only; the roots lie in the 1920s.
- Overlooking the army's links to public opinion.
- Treating the miracle as the result of one factor.
- Quoting growth rates as exact when they are averages.

## Self-test

1. What did the Washington Naval Conference limit?
2. What was the Peace Preservation Law?
3. Which event of 1932 ended party-led cabinets?
4. What did the Tripartite Pact link?
5. What was the Reverse Course?
6. What was MITI?
7. What was the Plaza Accord's effect on Japan?
8. Give two causes of Japan's falling population.

**Answers:** 1. Naval tonnage of the powers, with a 5:5:3 ratio for Britain, the USA and Japan in capital ships. 2. A 1925 law allowing suppression of radical groups. 3. The assassination of Prime Minister Inukai on 15 May. 4. Japan, Germany and Italy. 5. The shift in US occupation policy from reform to economic recovery and anti-communism. 6. The ministry that guided industrial and trade policy. 7. It raised the yen's value and helped fuel the bubble economy. 8. Low birth rate, later marriage, costs of raising children and a demanding work culture.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to cover every period?**
The inquiry topics span the whole period, so prepare each, but a question needs only the part it names.

**Is Japan's economic history only about the miracle?**
No. The Depression, wartime economy and the Lost Decade also come up.
