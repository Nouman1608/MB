## Section A

**1.** Name the ester formed when propan-1-ol reacts with ethanoyl chloride. **[1]**

**2.** State the inorganic product of the reaction between an alcohol and an acyl chloride. **[1]**

**3.** State why no catalyst is needed when ethanol reacts with ethanoyl chloride. **[1]**

**4.** Give the structural formula of the ester formed from methanol and propanoyl chloride. **[1]**

**5.** Name the alcohol and the acyl chloride that react to form butyl ethanoate. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Ethyl ethanoate can be prepared from ethanol by two different methods.

**(a)** Write an equation for the preparation from ethanol and ethanoyl chloride. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the reagents and conditions for the other method, and write its equation. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the yield from method (a) is higher. **[2]**

**7.** Describe the mechanism of the reaction between ethanol and ethanoyl chloride. Show curly arrows, lone pairs, the intermediate and all products. **[4]**

**8.** A student adds ethanoyl chloride to ethanol that contains some water.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction of ethanoyl chloride with water. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain the effect this has on the yield of ethyl ethanoate. **[1]**

**9.** 1.15 g of ethanol reacts with excess ethanoyl chloride.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of ethanol. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of ethyl ethanoate. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the volume of hydrogen chloride formed at room temperature and pressure. **[1]**

**(d)** 1.98 g of ethyl ethanoate is obtained. Calculate the percentage yield. **[1]**

**10.** Propane-1,3-diol, HOCH₂CH₂CH₂OH, is added to an excess of ethanoyl chloride.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of ethanoyl chloride needed to react completely with 0.0100 mol of the diol. **[2]**

**11.** Compound H has the molecular formula C₄H₁₀O and is a straight-chain primary alcohol. H reacts with ethanoyl chloride at room temperature to give steamy fumes and an ester J.

**(a)** Identify H. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the name and structural formula of J. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how the steamy fumes show that H contains an –OH group. **[1]**

**12.** Explain why ethanoyl chloride is attacked by the oxygen atom of ethanol, referring to the structure of both molecules. **[3]**

**13.** A student wants to make methyl benzoate, C₆H₅COOCH₃, by the acyl chloride method.

**(a)** Name the two reagents the student should use. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the equation for the reaction. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Propyl ethanoate [1].

**2.** Hydrogen chloride, HCl [1].

**3.** The carbonyl carbon of the acyl chloride is strongly δ+ and Cl⁻ is a good leaving group, so the reaction is fast at room temperature without a catalyst [1].

**4.** CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ [1].

**5.** Butan-1-ol [1] and ethanoyl chloride [1].

**6. (a)** CH₃COCl + CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + HCl [1].

**(b)** Ethanoic acid with a concentrated sulfuric acid catalyst, heated under reflux [1]; CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂OH ⇌ CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O [1].

**(c)** Method (a) is not reversible, so it goes to completion [1]; method (b) is an equilibrium, so some ethanol and ethanoic acid always remain unreacted [1].

**7.** Curly arrow from a lone pair on the ethanol O to the δ+ carbonyl carbon [1]; curly arrow from the C=O π bond to the carbonyl O, giving an intermediate with O⁻ and a positively charged ethanol O on the same carbon [1]; curly arrow from the O⁻ lone pair re-forming C=O, and a curly arrow from the C–Cl bond to Cl so that Cl⁻ leaves [1]; loss of H⁺ from the positively charged O, giving ethyl ethanoate and HCl [1].

**8. (a)** CH₃COCl + H₂O → CH₃COOH + HCl [1].

**(b)** Some ethanoyl chloride is used up by the water, so less is available to react with ethanol and the yield of ester falls [1].

**9. (a)** 1.15 / 46.0 = 0.0250 mol [1].

**(b)** 0.0250 × 88.0 = 2.20 g [1].

**(c)** 0.0250 × 24.0 = 0.600 dm³ [1].

**(d)** 1.98 / 2.20 × 100 = 90.0% [1].

**10. (a)** HOCH₂CH₂CH₂OH + 2CH₃COCl → CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₂OOCCH₃ + 2HCl [1 for the correct diester, 1 for balancing with 2CH₃COCl and 2HCl].

**(b)** 2 × 0.0100 = 0.0200 mol of CH₃COCl [1]; Mr = 78.5, so mass = 0.0200 × 78.5 = 1.57 g [1].

**11. (a)** Butan-1-ol, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH [1].

**(b)** Butyl ethanoate [1]; CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ [1].

**(c)** The fumes are HCl, formed from the H of the –OH group and the Cl of the acyl chloride, so H must have contained an –OH group [1].

**12.** In ethanoyl chloride the carbonyl carbon is bonded to two electronegative atoms, O and Cl, so it carries a large δ+ charge [1]. The oxygen atom of ethanol has lone pairs of electrons [1], so it acts as a nucleophile, donating a lone pair to the δ+ carbonyl carbon (the C=O π bond accepting the pair) [1].

**13. (a)** Benzoyl chloride, C₆H₅COCl [1], and methanol, CH₃OH [1]. The C=O side of the ester comes from the acyl chloride and the side on the single-bonded O comes from the alcohol.

**(b)** C₆H₅COCl + CH₃OH → C₆H₅COOCH₃ + HCl [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding concentrated sulfuric acid or heating in the acyl chloride method.
- Giving water as the by-product, or using an equilibrium arrow.
- Naming the ester with the acid part first.
- Starting the first curly arrow from the H of the –OH instead of a lone pair on O.
- Not doubling the acyl chloride for a diol.
- Using the mass of ester instead of moles in the HCl volume calculation.
