## Section A

**1.** Define the term fertility rate. **[1]**

**2.** Name **two** causes of rapid population growth in LICs. **[2]**

**3.** What is meant by an asylum seeker? **[1]**

**4.** Give **one** advantage of containerisation. **[1]**

**5.** What is meant by the digital divide? **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** The table shows invented data for a country.

| Year | Population (millions) | Birth rate (per 1 000) | Death rate (per 1 000) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1970 | 20 | 42 | 20 |
| 1990 | 32 | 38 | 10 |
| 2020 | 52 | 22 | 6 |

**(a)** Calculate the natural increase in 1990, per 1 000 people. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in population between 1970 and 2020. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the death rate fell more quickly than the birth rate. **[3]**

**7.** The table shows invented internet use in two regions.

| Region | People using the internet (%) |
|---|---|
| Region A | 92 |
| Region B | 38 |

**(a)** Calculate the difference between the two regions, in percentage points. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest **two** reasons for the digital divide shown. **[4]**

**8.** Explain **two** reasons why population is growing rapidly in some LICs. **[4]**

**9.** Explain the advantages and disadvantages of **one** strategy used to reduce the birth rate. **[4]**

**10.** For a named case study of airport expansion, explain the benefits and challenges. **[6]**

**11.** Explain how ICT has contributed to economic growth in an NEE you have studied. **[4]**

**12.** Explain the causes of **two** types of international migration. **[4]**

**13.** "International migration has more benefits than costs for the destination country." To what extent do you agree? **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The average number of children born to each woman in a population [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Any two from: improved medical care; improved sanitation; aid; religion; low status of women; children's work role; lack of education; young age structure [1 each]. **[2]**

**3.** A person who has asked another country for protection and is waiting for a decision on their claim [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any one from: goods move quickly between ship, train and lorry; cheaper shipping; less damage or theft; standard size makes loading efficient [1]. **[1]**

**5.** The gap between people who have access to ICT (such as the internet and phones) and those who do not [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** 38 - 10 = 28 per 1 000 [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** (52 - 20) ÷ 20 × 100 [1] = 160% [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Death rates fall quickly because medical care, vaccination, sanitation and food supplies improve and are available soon [1]. Birth rates depend on attitudes and customs, which change slowly [1]. Families remain large while infant mortality is high, women have little education and children are valued for work, so the birth rate stays high for longer [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** 92 - 38 = 54 percentage points [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Two reasons, each developed (2 marks each). For example: Region B may be poorer, so fewer people can afford devices and connections [2]; there may be limited infrastructure such as mobile masts or fibre cables, especially in rural areas [2]; lower levels of education or electricity access may limit use [2]; governments or companies may have invested less [2]. **[4]**

**8.** Two causes, each with development (2 marks each). For example: improved medical care means fewer children die, so more survive to adulthood, while birth rates remain high [2]; the role of children means that poor families have many children to work on farms and support parents in old age [2]; lack of education for women means early marriage and larger families [2]; a young age structure means many women are of childbearing age, so births continue to rise [2]. **[4]**

**9.** Named strategy, such as China's one-child policy or voluntary programmes in Thailand [1]. An advantage with development, such as it reduced the birth rate quickly, or it was accepted and lasted [1]. A disadvantage with development, such as an ageing population and a gender imbalance, or the programme took a long time and required funding [1]. A comparison or reason, such as why one approach is more acceptable [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Named airport and expansion [1]. Benefits with detail, such as more flights and links to growing markets, more jobs, economic growth, more trade (up to 2 marks) [2]. Challenges with detail, such as noise, air pollution and emissions, homes and land lost, road traffic, cost and opposition (up to 2 marks) [2]. Facts or figures, such as numbers of passengers or flights [1]. A judgement on the balance [1]. Maximum 6. **[6]**

**11.** Named NEE and examples, such as India or the Philippines [1]. Call centres and outsourcing create jobs for educated workers [1]. TNCs invest in offices and software development, bringing money and skills [1]. Growth in exports of IT services, and increased trade and tourism through online booking and promotion [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Two types named, such as refugees and economic migrants [1 each]. Cause of the first, such as war or persecution forcing people to flee [1]. Cause of the second, such as seeking jobs and higher wages [1]. (Accept asylum seekers: seeking protection from persecution.) **[4]**

**13.** Indicative content:
- Agree: migrants fill labour shortages and contribute taxes [1].
- Agree: they bring new skills, ideas and cultural diversity [1].
- Agree: they help support an ageing population by adding younger workers [1].
- Disagree: rapid arrival can put pressure on housing, schools and health services [1].
- Disagree: there may be social tension or competition for low-paid jobs [1].
- Disagree: costs and benefits vary between places and groups; integration matters [1].
- Judgement: the benefits often outweigh the costs over time where migration is managed and migrants are integrated, but this depends on numbers and local circumstances; a justified conclusion is needed [1]. Maximum 6. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing up the definitions of refugee, asylum seeker and economic migrant.
- Not showing the calculation in percentage questions.
- Writing "contraception" without saying how it reduces the birth rate.
- Case-study answers with no named place or facts.
- Describing ICT without linking it to growth, jobs or trade.
- Judgements that do not weigh benefits against costs.
