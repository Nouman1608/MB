**HL only.** These notes condense Regional study 4 of the Africa and the Middle East option (IB DP History, first assessment 2028): the Ottoman Empire and the creation of Türkiye, circa 1790 to 1938. Paper 3 asks for two 15-mark essays from different regional studies, each evaluating perspectives on an argument. The fuller account is in the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-ottoman-empire-and-turkiye/).

## Six inquiry topics at a glance

| Inquiry topic | Key idea |
|---|---|
| Decline of Ottoman power | Losses in North Africa, Napoleon, Muhammad Ali, Greek independence |
| Eastern Question | Great-power rivalry over Ottoman lands: trade, religion, strategy |
| Tanzimat (1839 to 1876) | Reform from above: law, army, equality, constitution |
| CUP | Young Turk revolution, constitutional politics, then authoritarian nationalism |
| Impact of conflict | Wars from 1911 to 1923 destroy the empire and create the republic |
| Atatürk (1923 to 1938) | Secular, centralising one-party reform |

## Decline of power

- 1798: French in Egypt. 1805: Muhammad Ali governor; 1811 Mamluks destroyed; Egypt autonomous after 1840.
- 1821: Greek revolt; Navarino 1827; independence recognised 1830.
- 1826: Mahmud II ends the Janissaries.
- Algeria to France 1830; Tunisia 1881; Tripolitania to Italy 1911.

**Judgement hint:** external pressure and internal weakness reinforced each other. Finance and the difficulty of keeping a multi-faith empire together were underlying causes.

## Eastern Question

- **Economic:** capitulations, European trade, debt, default in 1875, Public Debt Administration 1881.
- **Religious:** Russia as protector of Orthodox Christians; France and Catholics; Jerusalem holy places.
- **Strategic:** Britain and the route to India; Russia and the Straits.
- **Crimean War, 1853 to 1856:** Ottomans backed by Britain and France; Treaty of Paris 1856.
- **Balkan unrest:** Bosnia 1875, Bulgaria 1876; Russo-Turkish War 1877 to 1878; San Stefano (March 1878) then Berlin (July 1878, Bismarck presiding).

## Tanzimat

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Key texts | Gülhane Edict 1839; Reform Edict 1856; constitution 1876 |
| People | Abdulmejid I, Mustafa Reshid Pasha, Abdul Aziz, Midhat Pasha |
| Political | Ministries, councils, modern army, centralisation |
| Religious | Legal equality for non-Muslims; millet system; Muslim resentment |
| Social | Secular schools and courts; press; new educated class |
| Limits | Debt, foreign pressure, uneven application, parliament suspended in 1878 |

## CUP

- **Origins:** opposition to Abdulhamid II's autocracy; Young Turks in exile and officers in Macedonia.
- **1908:** revolution restores the constitution. 1909: counter-coup fails; Abdulhamid deposed.
- **1908 to 1913:** parties, press and elections, but war and rivalry weaken liberal hopes.
- **1913:** coup at the Sublime Porte; Enver, Talaat and Cemal dominate; Turkish nationalism and authoritarianism.
- **Minorities:** Turkification; Hamidian massacres (1890s) and Adana (1909) came earlier; the 1915 deportations and killings of Armenians are widely described as genocide, a term the Turkish state disputes.

## Conflict

- Italo-Turkish War 1911 to 1912; Balkan Wars 1912 to 1913.
- First World War: Gallipoli 1915; Arab Revolt 1916 to 1918; Armistice of Mudros 1918.
- War of Independence: Greek landing at Smyrna 1919; Sèvres 1920; Ankara assembly 1920; victories 1921 and 1922; Sultanate abolished 1922.
- Lausanne 1923; population exchange between Greece and Turkey; Republic proclaimed 29 October 1923.

## Atatürk

| Area | Reform |
|---|---|
| Political | One-party state; caliphate abolished 1924; centralisation |
| Social and cultural | Hat law 1925; Sufi orders banned 1925; Civil Code 1926; Latin alphabet 1928; surnames 1934 |
| Women | Municipal vote 1930; national vote 1934 |
| Economic | Statism; state enterprises; five-year plan 1934 |
| Opposition | Sheikh Said 1925; party closures; Dersim 1937 to 1938 |

## Argument table

| Claim | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| The CUP revolution was a turning point | End of autocracy; new politics | Continuity of state institutions; authoritarian drift |
| Atatürk's reforms were revolutionary | Law, script, dress, secularism | Many roots in the Tanzimat and CUP |
| The Eastern Question explains the end of the empire | Great-power interference | Internal reform failures; nationalism |
| Economic policy succeeded | State industry, railways | Mostly rural society; limits of statism |

## Worked example

*Question:* "To what extent did the First World War cause the end of the Ottoman Empire?"

- **Claim to test:** the war was the main cause.
- **For:** war losses, Arab Revolt, occupation of Istanbul and Sèvres.
- **Against:** the Balkan Wars and earlier losses, the 1908 to 1913 political crisis and long economic dependence.
- **Perspective:** one view sees steady decline; another sees an empire that might have survived without the 1914 decision to join the war.
- **Judgement:** war was the immediate cause and gave Kemal the opening, but it ended a state already weakened.

## Common mistakes

- Reading history backwards from the republic.
- Saying "Turks" when you mean "Ottomans" or "Muslims".
- Giving no evidence for opposition to Atatürk.
- Confusing Sèvres (1920, rejected) with Lausanne (1923, accepted).

## Self-test

1. Who became governor of Egypt in 1805?
2. Name two reasons Britain supported the Ottomans in the Crimean War.
3. Which treaty reduced the size of Bulgaria in 1878?
4. What happened in 1908?
5. What replaced the Arabic script in 1928?
6. Which treaty in 1923 recognised the new Turkish state?

**Answers:** 1. Muhammad Ali. 2. To keep Russia out of the Straits and Mediterranean; to protect the route to India. 3. The Treaty (Congress) of Berlin. 4. The Young Turk revolution restored the constitution. 5. The Latin alphabet. 6. The Treaty of Lausanne.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I write about the Armenian events?** Use precise terms, state what happened, and acknowledge the differing positions of historians, governments and the Turkish state.

**Should I cover the Arab provinces?** Yes, especially for the Arab Revolt and the empire's loss of control.

## Key terms

- **Tanzimat:** "reorganisation", the reform era from 1839 to 1876.
- **Millet:** a religious community with its own legal affairs inside the empire.
- **Capitulations:** privileges given to foreign merchants, including lower duties and separate courts.
- **Young Turks:** the opposition movement from which the CUP grew.
- **Turkification:** policies that promoted Turkish language and identity over other groups.
- **Statism:** state leadership of the economy in the 1930s.
- **Secularism:** separating the state and law from religious authority.
