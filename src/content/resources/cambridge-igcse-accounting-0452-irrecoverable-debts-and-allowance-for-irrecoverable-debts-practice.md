## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** A debt written off last year is received in cash this year. What is the
first entry made?

A: debit Bank, credit Irrecoverable debts recovered

B: debit Customer, credit Irrecoverable debts recovered

C: debit Irrecoverable debts recovered, credit Customer

D: debit Customer, credit Sales

**[1]**

**2.** The allowance for irrecoverable debts is 1,200. Trade receivables at the
year end are 30,000 and the allowance is to be 3% of trade receivables. What is
the effect on profit for the year?

A: decrease of 300

B: increase of 300

C: decrease of 900

D: increase of 900

**[1]**

**3.** State the meaning of an irrecoverable debt recovered and where it is shown
in the statement of profit or loss. **[2]**

**4.** Explain one reason why a business maintains an allowance for irrecoverable
debts. **[2]**

**5.** T Adeyemi owed Calder Electrical 2,400. He was declared bankrupt and paid
25 cents for each dollar owed in full and final settlement. Prepare the journal
entries, without narratives. **[3]**

**6.** Trade receivables are 52,000 before a debt of 2,000 is written off. The
allowance is to be 2% of trade receivables and currently stands at 1,150.
Calculate the change in the allowance and state its effect on profit. **[3]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Fernhill Supplies has a year end of 31 December. During 2028:

- 3 March: J Lowe's debt of 480 was written off.
- 20 July: S Brandt, who owed 1,300, was declared bankrupt and paid 520 in full
  and final settlement.
- 9 October: P Quinn paid 350, a debt that had been written off in 2026.

**(a)** Prepare the journal entries for these events, without narratives. **[5]**

**(b)** Prepare the irrecoverable debts account for 2028, showing the transfer to
the statement of profit or loss. **[3]**

**(c)** Prepare P Quinn's account for 2028. **[2]**

**8.** Tamsin Florals has a 30 June year end. Its trade receivables, after
writing off irrecoverable debts, were 24,000 (2026), 31,000 (2027) and 27,000
(2028). An allowance of 5% was created in 2026 and kept at 5% in 2027. In 2028
the rate was reduced to 4%.

**(a)** Calculate the allowance needed and the change for each year. **[3]**

**(b)** Prepare the allowance for irrecoverable debts account for the three years
in three-column running balance format. **[4]**

**(c)** Prepare the statement of financial position extract for trade
receivables at 30 June 2028. **[2]**

**9.** Ravi Dhillon's draft profit for the year is 46,300. Trade receivables in the
draft are 58,720. The following have not been dealt with:

1. A debt of 720 is to be written off.
2. The allowance for irrecoverable debts, currently 1,400, is to be 2.5% of trade
   receivables.
3. Cash of 300 was received for a debt written off two years ago. It was
   debited to bank and credited to sales.

**(a)** Calculate the corrected profit for the year. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain the effect of item 3 on gross profit and on profit for the year. **[2]**

**(c)** Prepare the statement of financial position extract for trade receivables. **[2]**

**10.** State the effect on profit for the year of each error.

**(a)** An allowance needing an increase of 400 was charged in full at its new
balance of 2,000. **[2]**

**(b)** A decrease in the allowance of 250 was not recorded. **[2]**

**11.** Grace, a sole trader, says: "I don't know which customers won't pay, so
recording an allowance is just guesswork. I'll wait until a customer actually
fails to pay." Advise Grace whether she should maintain an allowance. **[4]**

**12.** Kestrel Wholesale has trade receivables of 45,000 at the end of 2027 and at
the end of 2028. The allowance was 2% at the end of 2027. At the end of 2028 it was
raised to 6% because a large customer is in financial difficulty.

**(a)** Calculate the effect on profit for 2028. **[2]**

**(b)** State and explain the effect on the acid test (liquid) ratio. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. The debt is reinstated first; the cash is recorded second. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. New allowance 900; the allowance falls by 300, which is income. **[1]**

**3.** Cash received from a customer whose debt had been written off in an earlier
period [1]. It is shown as other income, added to gross profit [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Prudence: trade receivables should not be shown at more than the business
expects to collect [1], so an estimate of the likely loss is deducted, giving a
realistic current asset figure [1]. (Or matching: the expected loss is charged in
the same year as the credit sales that caused it.) **[2]**

**5.** Cash received = 2,400 × 0.25 = 600. Debit Bank 600, credit T Adeyemi 600 [1].
Debit Irrecoverable debts 1,800 [1], credit T Adeyemi 1,800 [1]. **[3]**

**6.** New allowance = 2% × 50,000 = 1,000 [1]. Change = 1,000 − 1,150 = 150
decrease [1]. Profit increases by 150 [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)**

```
                                                Dr       Cr
3 Mar    Irrecoverable debts                   480
            J Lowe                                       480
20 Jul   Bank                                  520
         Irrecoverable debts                   780
            S Brandt                                   1,300
9 Oct    P Quinn                               350
            Irrecoverable debts recovered                350
9 Oct    Bank                                  350
            P Quinn                                      350
```

J Lowe entry [1]; bank 520 [1] and irrecoverable debts 780 credited to Brandt
[1]; Quinn reinstated [1]; cash recorded [1].

**(b)**

```
Irrecoverable debts account ($)
Date      Details                Amount  Date      Details                Amount
3 Mar 28  J Lowe                    480  31 Dec 28 Profit or loss          1,260
20 Jul 28 S Brandt                  780
                                 ------                                   ------
                                  1,260                                    1,260
                                 ======                                   ======
```

J Lowe 480 [1]; S Brandt 780 [1]; transfer 1,260 [1].

**(c)**

```
P Quinn account ($)
Date      Details                Amount  Date      Details                Amount
9 Oct 28  Irrecoverable debts       350  9 Oct 28  Bank                      350
          recovered
```

Debit entry for the reinstated debt [1]; credit entry for bank [1]. **[10]**

**8. (a)** 2026: 5% × 24,000 = 1,200, created [1]. 2027: 5% × 31,000 = 1,550,
increase 350 [1]. 2028: 4% × 27,000 = 1,080, decrease 470 [1].

**(b)**

```
Allowance for irrecoverable debts account ($)
Date        Details             Debit    Credit    Balance
30 Jun 26   Profit or loss               1,200    1,200 Cr
30 Jun 27   Profit or loss                 350    1,550 Cr
30 Jun 28   Profit or loss        470             1,080 Cr
```

Creation of 1,200 as a credit [1]; increase of 350 as a credit [1]; decrease of
470 as a debit [1]; balances correct and marked Cr [1].

**(c)** Trade receivables 27,000 [1], less allowance for irrecoverable debts
1,080, giving 25,920 [1]. **[9]**

**9. (a)** Write-off reduces profit by 720 [1]. Remaining receivables 58,000;
new allowance 2.5% × 58,000 = 1,450 [1]; increase of 50 reduces profit [1].
Item 3 does not change profit for the year: 46,300 − 720 − 50 = **45,530** [1].

**(b)** Revenue, and so gross profit, is overstated by 300 [1]. Profit for the
year is correct, because other income (irrecoverable debts recovered) is
understated by the same 300 [1].

**(c)** Trade receivables 58,000, less allowance 1,450 [1], giving 56,550 [1]. **[8]**

**10. (a)** Profit is understated [1] by 1,600 (2,000 − 400) [1].

**(b)** Profit is understated [1] by 250 [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Grace should maintain an allowance [1]. Some of her trade receivables are
likely not to be collected even if she cannot yet name the customers; prudence
requires the expected loss to be recognised now [1]. Matching charges the loss to
the year of the credit sales, not a later year [1]. The estimate can be based on
her past experience of non-payment, so it is not guesswork, and it gives a
realistic figure for trade receivables [1]. **[4]**

**12. (a)** Old allowance 900; new allowance 2,700 [1]. Increase of 1,800, so
profit for 2028 is 1,800 lower [1].

**(b)** The acid test ratio falls [1], because net trade receivables, a liquid
current asset, are 1,800 lower while current liabilities are unchanged [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Charging the full new allowance instead of the change.
- Applying the percentage before removing debts written off.
- Missing the reinstatement entry for a debt recovered.
- Writing off the whole debt when part of it was paid.
- Treating a decrease in the allowance as an expense.
