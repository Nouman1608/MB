Assignment A in Pearson Edexcel International GCSE English Literature (4ET1) Component 3 is **one
coursework essay of about 650–800 words on a modern drama set text, marked out of 30 (AO1 15, AO2
15)**. No context marks are available. For the full explanation, use the
[Assignment A modern drama study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-english-literature-4et1-assignment-a-modern-drama/).

| Detail | Assignment A |
|---|---|

| Context (AO4) | Not assessed |

## What 15 + 15 looks like

- **AO1 (15):** a personal, critical argument that answers the task from the first sentence and is
  sustained to the last, with precise reference to moments across the play.
- **AO2 (15):** analysis of language, form and structure, which for a play includes stagecraft:
  what the audience sees and hears, and when.

With no AO4 marks and an advisory 650–800 words, every sentence should either advance the argument
or analyse a method. Coursework conditions mean quotations and act and scene references should be
exact.

## Turning a task into three moments

| Play | Example task focus | Beginning | Turning point | Ending |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *A View from the Bridge* | Eddie as a tragic figure | Alfieri's opening narration framing the story | Marco lifting the chair at the end of Act One | Eddie's death in the final confrontation and Alfieri's closing speech |
| *An Inspector Calls* | The Inspector as the playwright's voice | His arrival interrupting Birling's speech in Act One | The questioning of Mrs Birling in Act Two | His final speech and exit in Act Three |
| *The Curious Incident of the Dog in the Night-time* | How the audience shares Christopher's view | Wellington found dead at the opening | Ed's confession about Wellington | Christopher's success and his questions at the close |
| *Kindertransport* | Memory and identity | Eva preparing to leave Hamburg with her mother Helga | Eva choosing a new name and identity in England | Faith confronting Evelyn about the past she hid |
| *Death and the King's Horseman* | Duty and failure | Elesin's confident praise-song in the market | Pilkings's men preventing the ritual | Olunde's sacrifice and Elesin's death |

Each row gives an essay its spine: three moments from different parts of the play, each with a
method to analyse.

## Key terms for writing about plays

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stagecraft | The playwright's control of what happens on stage: movement, sound, light, set, timing |
| Stage direction | Instruction in the script, usually in italics, about action, tone or setting |
| Dramatic irony | The audience knows something a character does not |
| Naturalistic | Staged to look like real life, with a realistic set and everyday speech |
| Non-naturalistic | Staging that is openly theatrical: symbolic sets, ensemble movement, direct address |
| Doubling | One actor playing more than one role, which can link characters in the audience's mind |
| Chorus or narrator figure | A character who comments on or frames the action for the audience |
| Climax | The point of highest tension, usually near the end of the play's structure |

## Methods to look for in each text

| Text | Strong areas for AO2 |
|---|---|
| *A View from the Bridge* | A narrator who frames the story; physical contests on stage; tension built through silence and gesture |
| *An Inspector Calls* | A single room as setting; timed entrances that bring each revelation; a twist ending |
| *The Curious Incident of the Dog in the Night-time* | Non-naturalistic staging; ensemble playing objects and crowds; a teacher reading Christopher's own story aloud |
| *Kindertransport* | Past and present on stage together; a figure from a folk tale haunting the action; objects in boxes carrying memory |
| *Death and the King's Horseman* | Song, dance and verse in the market scenes; contrasting settings; a reversal between father and son |

## Annotated model paragraph

Task: *How does Miller use Alfieri to shape the audience's response to Eddie in* A View from the
Bridge*?*

> Miller uses Alfieri to frame Eddie's story as a tragedy whose ending is already known, so the
> audience watches Eddie with dread rather than suspense. **[AO1: argument that answers the task]**
> Alfieri speaks directly to the audience at the opening and returns between key scenes, which means
> the action is always filtered through a lawyer who admits he could not stop what happened.
> **[AO2: narrator figure and structure]** Because he tells us early that he was powerless, each
> later warning he gives Eddie in his office carries dramatic irony: the audience knows the advice
> will be ignored and watches Eddie walk towards the ending Alfieri has predicted. **[AO2: dramatic
> irony and its effect on an audience]** His closing speech, admitting a reluctant admiration for
> Eddie while observing that most people "settle for half", leaves the audience caught between
> judging Eddie and respecting his refusal to compromise, the divided response on which the tragedy
> depends. **[AO2: the play's ending; AO1: personal, critical response]**

## Paragraph checklist

- Does it answer the task wording?
- Does it name a method and explain its **effect on an audience**?
- Does the evidence come from a precise moment (short quotation or stage direction)?
- Does it move the argument on, rather than repeat the last paragraph?

## Word budget (750 words)

Introduction 60, four paragraphs of about 160, conclusion 50. Use the beginning, a turning point and
the ending of the play.

## Process rules

- Work must be your own; you and your teacher sign the authentication sheet.
- At draft stage your teacher may give general advice only, not corrections.
- No rewriting after marking.
- Acknowledge any sources.

## Common mistakes

- Retelling the plot ("largely narrative" sits in the lower levels).
- Writing "the reader" throughout and ignoring performance.
- Spending words on historical background that earns no marks here.
- Naming a technique without explaining its effect.
- Choosing all evidence from one act, so the essay cannot show development.
- Describing a production you have seen instead of the playwright's script.
- Inaccurate act and scene references or quotations, which coursework conditions give no excuse for.

## Self-test

1. How are Assignment A's 30 marks split?
2. Why should you keep historical background brief in this assignment?
3. Define dramatic irony and say why it suits a stage play.
4. What does "non-naturalistic" staging mean? Name a set text that uses it.
5. What may a teacher do when reading your draft?
6. Give a word budget for a 750-word essay with four main paragraphs.
7. Name two methods that a reader of the script might miss but an audience would notice.
8. Choose three moments from *An Inspector Calls* that would give an essay on the Inspector a
   whole-play spine.
9. Why does Alfieri's early admission that he was powerless create dramatic irony?
10. Which moment in *Death and the King's Horseman* reverses the roles of father and son?
11. What makes *Kindertransport*'s staging of past and present useful for an essay on memory?

**Answers:** 1. AO1 15, AO2 15. 2. AO4 is not assessed in Assignment A, so background only helps if it
serves a point about the play. 3. The audience knows more than a character; on stage the audience
watches the character walk into danger in real time, which builds tension. 4. Staging that is openly
theatrical rather than realistic, with symbolic sets or ensemble movement; *The Curious Incident of
the Dog in the Night-time*. 5. Give general guidance on improvement, not detailed corrections or
rewriting. 6. For example: introduction 60, four paragraphs of about 160, conclusion 50. 7. Any two,
for example: a silence or pause, a sound effect, a character who stays on stage without speaking,
the use of the same actor in two roles, a change of lighting. 8. For example: his arrival
interrupting Birling's speech (Act One), the questioning of Mrs Birling (Act Two), and his final
speech and exit (Act Three). 9. The audience already knows the outcome, so each warning Alfieri gives
Eddie is heard as one that will be ignored. 10. Olunde, returned from England, dies in his father's
place, fulfilling the duty Elesin failed. 11. The child Eva and the adult Evelyn can share the stage,
so the audience sees how the past shapes the present in the same moment.
