OxfordAQA International GCSE Psychology (9218) is a recently launched
course that introduces students to psychology as a science of behaviour.
It looks at how we remember and perceive, how the brain and nervous
system shape what we do, how we communicate, how other people influence
us, and how mental health is understood. The qualification is linear and is assessed by two written
papers, each lasting 120 minutes, each marked out of 100 and each worth
50%, with no coursework.

## How the course is organised

The content is split between the two papers, and research methods
appears in both.

**Paper 1, Cognition and Behaviour**

- **Memory**: how information is taken in, stored and retrieved, and
  why we forget.
- **Perception**: how we interpret what our senses detect, and why that
  interpretation can be mistaken.
- **Biopsychology**: the biological side of behaviour, including the
  brain and nervous system.
- **Research Methods**: how psychological studies are designed, carried
  out and analysed.

**Paper 2, Social Context and Behaviour**

- **Communication**: how people communicate, verbally and non-verbally.
- **Social Influences**: how the presence and actions of others affect
  behaviour, including conformity and obedience.
- **Mental Health**: how mental health and specific conditions are
  explained and treated.
- **Research Methods**: the same methods content, now applied to social
  topics.

The two papers broadly divide the individual mind (Paper 1) from the
person in a social setting (Paper 2), which is a useful way to keep the
course straight in your head.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Cognition and Behaviour | 120 minutes | 100 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Social Context and Behaviour | 120 minutes | 100 | 50% |

Each paper is a written exam marked externally. With no tiers, the whole
cohort answers the same two papers. The
qualification is linear, so both papers are taken in the same series at
the end of the course.

One rule shapes the course more than any other: **individual papers
cannot be resat on their own.** If you want to improve your grade, you
retake the whole qualification, both papers, which you may do any number
of times. In practice this means each exam series counts in full, and
both papers need to be ready at the same time.

The specification was first taught in September 2023 and first examined
in May/June 2025, so it has now been through at least one exam series.
This page follows Version 1.1 of the specification.

## How the parts fit together

Although the papers have separate content, the course works as one
connected subject:

- **Research methods is the shared spine.** Because it is assessed in
  both papers, every study you meet in memory, perception, social
  influence or mental health is also a chance to practise methods: what
  the researchers did, how they measured behaviour, and how far the
  findings can be trusted.
- **Biopsychology supports mental health.** Biological explanations from
  Paper 1 help in understanding some of the explanations and treatments
  of mental health in Paper 2.
- **Perception and memory connect to communication.** How we interpret
  information and recall it affects how messages are sent and
  understood between people.
- **Social influence links to mental health.** Ideas about how other
  people shape behaviour are useful when considering social explanations
  of mental health.

Across the whole course you are asked to describe theories and studies
accurately, apply them to everyday situations, and evaluate their
strengths and weaknesses.

Evaluation is the skill students most often leave too late. It is
tempting to learn a study as a story (who took part, what happened, what
was found) and stop there. The course rewards going one step further:
asking whether the sample represents people in general, whether the
task resembles real life, and whether another explanation could account
for the result. Because those questions are the same whichever topic the
study belongs to, practising them on memory research in the first term
pays off when you reach social influence and mental health later on.

## Planning your study

Since research methods runs across both papers, introduce it early and
keep returning to it. A practical route through the course:

- **First year:** begin with research methods basics, then memory and
  perception. Add biopsychology later in the year. Each time you meet a
  study, note its method, sample and main conclusion.
- **Second year, first part:** move to Paper 2: communication, then
  social influences, then mental health, applying the methods knowledge
  you already have to new kinds of study.
- **Second year, final months:** revise both papers together. Because
  you cannot resit a single paper, there is no advantage in preparing one
  paper first and leaving the other.

Start exam-style questions as soon as the first topic is taught, and
move to full timed papers once both papers' content is covered. For
technique on each paper, use the course's exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is OxfordAQA IGCSE Psychology 9218 assessed?**
By two written papers, Cognition and Behaviour and Social Context and Behaviour. Each lasts 120 minutes, is marked out of 100 and is worth 50%.

**Can I resit just one paper?**
No. Individual papers cannot be resat. You can retake the whole qualification, both papers together, any number of times.

**Is research methods in both papers?**
Yes. Research Methods is listed in both Paper 1 and Paper 2, so methods knowledge is tested alongside the content of each paper.

**Is there coursework in OxfordAQA IGCSE Psychology?**
No. The qualification is linear with no coursework, and the grade comes entirely from the two written papers.

**When was this specification first examined?**
It was first taught in September 2023 and first examined in May/June 2025.
