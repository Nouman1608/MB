These questions cover subtopic E6.5, Non-right-angled triangles, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The subtopic is Extended only. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Give lengths to 3 significant figures and angles to 1 decimal place unless a question says otherwise. The sine rule, cosine rule and area formula are in the List of formulas. Learn the methods first with the [non-right-angled triangles study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-non-right-angled-triangles-cambridge-igcse-maths/) and the [non-right-angled triangles revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-non-right-angled-triangles-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** (non-calculator) In triangle ABC, AB = 4 cm, AC = 6 cm and angle BAC = 60°. Find the exact length of BC, giving your answer in its simplest surd form. **[3]**

**2.** (calculator) A triangle has angles of 47° and 61°. The side opposite the 47° angle is 12.5 cm. Find the length of the side opposite the 61° angle. **[2]**

**3.** (calculator) A triangle has sides of 8 cm, 11 cm and 15 cm. Find the size of its largest angle. **[3]**

**4.** (non-calculator) Find the area of a triangle with sides 10 cm and 14 cm and an included angle of 30°. **[2]**

**5.** (calculator) Triangle DEF has DE = 9 cm, DF = 13 cm and area 52 cm². Angle EDF is acute. Find angle EDF. **[3]**

**6.** (non-calculator) A triangle has sides of 6 cm, 8 cm and 13 cm. Without finding any angle, explain why the angle opposite the 13 cm side is obtuse. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** (calculator) In triangle PQR, PQ = 14 cm, QR = 10 cm and angle QPR = 35°.

**(a)** Find the two possible values of angle PRQ. **[3]**

**(b)** For the case where angle PRQ is obtuse, find the length of PR. **[3]**

**8.** (calculator) A ship sails from harbour H for 18 km on a bearing of 070° to point A. It then sails 25 km on a bearing of 150° to point B.

**(a)** Show that angle HAB = 100°. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the distance HB. **[3]**

**(c)** Find the bearing of B from H, to the nearest degree. **[3]**

**9.** (calculator) Quadrilateral ABCD is split into two triangles by the diagonal AC. AB = 7 cm, BC = 9 cm and angle ABC = 108°. CD = 6 cm and angle ACD = 52°.

**(a)** Find the length of AC. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the area of quadrilateral ABCD. **[3]**

**10.** (non-calculator) A triangle has sides of 7 cm, 8 cm and 13 cm.

**(a)** Show that the largest angle is exactly 120°. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the exact area of the triangle. **[2]**

**11.** (calculator) The minute hand of a clock is 12 cm long and the hour hand is 8 cm long. At 4 o'clock the angle between the hands is 120°. Find the distance between the tips of the hands. **[3]**

**12.** (calculator) In triangle ABC, angle BAC = 70°, BC = 12 cm and AC = 9 cm.

**(a)** Find angle ABC. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why there is only one possible value for angle ABC. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** BC² = 4² + 6² − 2 × 4 × 6 × cos 60° [1]; = 16 + 36 − 24 = 28 [1]; BC = √28 = **2√7 cm** [1]

**2.** x ÷ sin 61° = 12.5 ÷ sin 47° [1]; x = 12.5 × sin 61° ÷ sin 47° = **14.9 cm** [1]

**3.** The largest angle is opposite the 15 cm side [1]; cos θ = (8² + 11² − 15²) ÷ (2 × 8 × 11) = −40 ÷ 176 = −0.2272… [1]; θ = **103.1°** [1]

*Examiner insight:* a negative cosine is not an error. It tells you the angle is obtuse, and the inverse key handles it correctly.

**4.** ½ × 10 × 14 × sin 30° [1] = 70 × ½ = **35 cm²** [1]

**5.** ½ × 9 × 13 × sin D = 52 [1]; sin D = 104 ÷ 117 = 0.8888… [1]; angle EDF = **62.7°** [1]

*Examiner insight:* 117.3° gives the same area. The word "acute" in the question tells you which one to give.

**6.** 6² + 8² = 100 and 13² = 169 [1]; since 100 < 169, the cosine rule gives a negative value for the cosine of that angle, so the angle is obtuse [1]

**7. (a)** sin R ÷ 14 = sin 35° ÷ 10 [1]; sin R = 0.8030… [1]; angle PRQ = **53.4° or 126.6°** [1]

**(b)** Angle PQR = 180° − 35° − 126.58…° = 18.41…° [1]; PR ÷ sin 18.41…° = 10 ÷ sin 35° [1]; PR = **5.51 cm** [1]

*Examiner insight:* both triangles exist because 35° + 126.6° is less than 180°. Using 53.4° in part (b) gives PR = 17.4 cm, which belongs to the other triangle.

**8. (a)** The bearing of H from A is 070° + 180° = 250°, so angle HAB = 250° − 150° = **100°** [1]

**(b)** HB² = 18² + 25² − 2 × 18 × 25 × cos 100° [1]; = 949 + 156.28… = 1105.28… [1]; HB = **33.2 km** [1]

**(c)** sin AHB ÷ 25 = sin 100° ÷ 33.245… [1]; angle AHB = 47.78…° [1]; bearing = 070° + 47.78…° = **118°** [1]

*Examiner insight:* sketch the journey with north lines at H and A. B lies clockwise from A as seen from H, so the angle at H is added to 070°.

**9. (a)** AC² = 7² + 9² − 2 × 7 × 9 × cos 108° [1]; = 130 + 38.93… = 168.93… [1]; AC = **13.0 cm** [1]

**(b)** Area ABC = ½ × 7 × 9 × sin 108° = 29.958… cm² [1]; area ACD = ½ × 12.997… × 6 × sin 52° = 30.726… cm² [1]; total = **60.7 cm²** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the 52° angle is between AC and CD, so those are the two sides to use for triangle ACD. Use the unrounded AC from part (a).

**10. (a)** The largest angle is opposite 13 cm [1]; cos θ = (49 + 64 − 169) ÷ (2 × 7 × 8) = −56 ÷ 112 [1]; = −½, so θ = **120°** [1]

**(b)** ½ × 7 × 8 × sin 120° [1] = 28 × √3/2 = **14√3 cm²** [1]

**11.** d² = 12² + 8² − 2 × 12 × 8 × cos 120° [1]; = 208 + 96 = 304 [1]; d = **17.4 cm** [1]

**12. (a)** sin B ÷ 9 = sin 70° ÷ 12 [1]; sin B = 0.7047…, angle ABC = **44.8°** [1]

**(b)** The other value, 135.2°, would make 70° + 135.2° greater than 180°, which is impossible in a triangle (also, AC is shorter than BC, so B must be smaller than A) [1]

*Examiner insight:* compare this with question 7. The ambiguous case only gives two triangles when the obtuse option still fits the angle sum.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a side and an angle that are not opposite each other in the sine rule.
- Multiplying by cos A at the wrong point in the cosine rule.
- Forgetting the square root after the cosine rule for a side.
- Giving one value when the ambiguous case gives two, or keeping an impossible obtuse angle.
- Using a non-included angle in ½ab sin C.
- Rounding intermediate lengths.
