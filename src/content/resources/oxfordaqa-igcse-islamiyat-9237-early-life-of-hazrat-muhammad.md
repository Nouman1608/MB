**This guide covers content area 3.2.1.2 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), Early Life of Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh): "The birth and upbringing of Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh), his parents and the role of Abdul Mutalib, Abu Talib and Bahira", "Hazrat Muhammad's (pbuh) marriage to Hazrat Khadija (RA), and his business dealings" and "The key qualities of Hazrat Muhammad's (pbuh) character including courage, bravery, treatment of the poor, earning an honest living and fair dealings."** The page follows his life from birth to the age of about forty, the time before revelation, and shows how each stage prepared him for his mission.

## Birth and family

Muhammad (pbuh) was born in Makkah in about 570 CE, in the **Year of the Elephant**, so called because Abraha, the governor of Yemen, marched on Makkah with an army including elephants to destroy the Ka'aba and was turned back. Muslims remember the event in Surah al-Fil (105). He belonged to **Banu Hashim**, a respected clan of the Quraysh.

**His parents.** His father, **Abdullah**, son of Abdul Mutalib, died before he was born, or soon after, while returning from a trading journey to Syria. His mother, **Aminah bint Wahb**, was left to bring him up alone. He was an orphan from the start, and Muslims often say this explains why he later showed such care for orphans.

## Upbringing: the people who cared for him

| Person | Role |
|---|---|
| Halimah al-Sa'diyyah | Desert wet-nurse from the tribe of Banu Sa'd; he lived with her for his first years, in clean desert air, where he learned pure Arabic and grew strong |
| Aminah | His mother; she took him to visit his father's grave in Yathrib, and died on the way back at Abwa when he was about six |
| Abdul Mutalib | His grandfather, chief of Makkah and guardian of the Ka'aba and Zamzam; he took him in and loved him deeply; he died when Muhammad was about eight |
| Abu Talib | His uncle, who then became his guardian; a respected but poor man with many children; he protected the Prophet (pbuh) throughout his early life and during the first years of Islam |
| Bahira | A Christian monk of Busra in Syria, who met Abu Talib's caravan |

### Abdul Mutalib

As leader of Makkah, Abdul Mutalib gave Muhammad (pbuh) a place of honour. He seated him on his own cushion beside the Ka'aba, where the children of other families were not allowed. In this way Muhammad learned about leadership, honour and the care of the sacred house. Abdul Mutalib was also the man who had cleared and re-opened the well of Zamzam, so the boy grew up close to the heart of Makkan life.

### Abu Talib

After Abdul Mutalib's death, Abu Talib took care of Muhammad (pbuh) as his own son. Although Abu Talib never accepted Islam, he protected the Prophet (pbuh) for many years against the Quraysh, which is why his death in the tenth year of the mission was so severe a loss. Abu Talib also introduced him to trade by taking him on a caravan journey.

### Bahira

On a caravan journey to Syria at about the age of twelve, Muhammad (pbuh) travelled with his uncle. Near Busra the caravan met **Bahira**, a Christian monk. According to the traditional account, Bahira noticed signs that the boy would be a prophet, such as a cloud shading the caravan, and told Abu Talib to protect him from those who might harm him. Abu Talib then brought him home. The story is not in the Qur'an, so it is told as a tradition. It shows that Christian scholars expected a prophet and that the boy was marked out from an early age.

## Youth and work

As a youth Muhammad (pbuh) worked as a **shepherd** for the people of Makkah. Muslims point out that earlier prophets had also been shepherds. The work taught him patience, responsibility and care for the weak. He also took part in the Hilf al-Fudul, a pact to defend people who were wronged, and later said he would respond to such a call even in Islam.

His honesty became so well known that the people of Makkah gave him the titles **al-Amin** (the trustworthy) and **as-Sadiq** (the truthful). When the Quraysh rebuilt the Ka'aba, about 605 CE, the clans quarrelled over who should lift the Black Stone into place. They agreed to accept the judgement of the first man to enter the gate; it was Muhammad (pbuh). He placed the stone on a cloak, had the leaders of each clan hold a corner and lifted it into place with his own hands. The dispute ended without bloodshed.

## Marriage to Hazrat Khadija (RA)

**Khadija bint Khuwaylid (RA)** was a wealthy and respected widow of the Quraysh who ran a large trading business. She hired honest men to trade for her. Having heard of Muhammad's honesty, she asked him to take her goods to Syria, sending her servant **Maysarah** with him. The trading trip was very successful, and Maysarah reported on the Prophet's honesty, good manners and fair dealing.

Khadija (RA), about forty, then proposed to him through a friend. Muhammad (pbuh) was twenty-five. They married in about 595 CE and the marriage was happy and lasted twenty-five years. They had several children: Qasim and Abdullah, who died young, and four daughters, Zainab, Ruqayyah, Umm Kulthum and Fatimah (RA). Khadija (RA) was his first supporter. She was the first person to believe in his message, and she stood by him in hardship.

### His business dealings

- **Honesty.** He never hid faults in goods and gave full measure.
- **Fairness.** He gave a fair price to buyer and seller and kept every promise.
- **Reliability.** Customers trusted him with money and goods.
- **Good profit by lawful means.** The Syria trip made a great profit for Khadija (RA) without cheating anyone.

His example is the model for Muslims who work in business: honest trade is a form of worship.

## The key qualities of his character

The prescribed passage is Qur'an 68:4: "And indeed, you [O Muhammad] are of a great moral character." It sums up how Allah described him. The specification names several qualities.

| Quality | Example |
|---|---|
| Courage and bravery | He joined the Hilf al-Fudul, to defend the weak against the powerful; later he remained steady in battle and under threat |
| Treatment of the poor | He fed the hungry, helped orphans and widows and lived simply; he gave generously to those in need |
| Earning an honest living | He worked as a shepherd and merchant and never begged; he said the best food is what a person earns by his own work |
| Fair dealings | Known as al-Amin; he gave fair value in trade and kept promises, even to those who later opposed him |
| Truthfulness | As-Sadiq: he never lied; his own people kept their valuables with him |
| Patience and gentleness | He settled the Black Stone dispute peacefully |

### Why his early character matters

When the Prophet (pbuh) later called people to Islam, the Quraysh could not accuse him of lying, because they had known his honesty for forty years. The Qur'an invites people to think about this: "I have remained among you a lifetime before it" (10:16).

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** ways in which Abu Talib helped Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh) in his early life. **[4]**

> Abu Talib became his guardian after Abdul Mutalib died [1], giving the orphan a home and care [1]. He also took him on a trading journey to Syria [1], which gave him experience of trade and led to the meeting with Bahira [1].

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing up the guardians.** The order is mother Aminah, then grandfather Abdul Mutalib, then uncle Abu Talib.
- **Saying that Abu Talib became a Muslim.** He protected the Prophet (pbuh) but did not accept Islam.
- **Treating the Bahira story as Qur'anic.** It is a historical tradition.
- **Listing qualities without examples.** Link each quality to an event.
- **Placing the marriage after the call to prophethood.** It took place about fifteen years before.

## Exam technique

**Give** or **name** questions ask for a person, place or title (Halimah, Busra, al-Amin). **Explain** questions need the person's role plus its effect. In a 12-mark question on 68:4, use "you [O Muhammad] are of a great moral character" and support it with events: the Black Stone, the trading journey and his honesty with Khadija's goods. Evaluate whether his early life shows that he was prepared for his mission, noting that Muslims believe revelation, not character alone, made him a prophet.
