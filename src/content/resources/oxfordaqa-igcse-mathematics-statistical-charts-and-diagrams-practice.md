## Questions

**1.** The number of passengers in 20 cars passing a school gate is: 1, 3, 2, 1, 1, 4, 2, 1, 3, 2, 1, 5, 2, 1, 2, 3, 1, 2, 4, 1.
**(a)** Complete a tally chart and frequency table for 1 to 5 passengers. **[3]**
**(b)** How many cars carried more than 2 passengers? **[1]**

**2.** A pictogram shows tickets sold for a school play. Key: one symbol = 12 tickets, and a half symbol = 6 tickets. Monday has 3 symbols, Tuesday has 2½ symbols and Wednesday has 4 symbols. On Thursday 54 tickets are sold.
**(a)** How many tickets were sold on Tuesday? **[1]**
**(b)** What should be drawn for Thursday? **[2]**
**(c)** How many tickets were sold from Monday to Wednesday? **[2]**

**3.** A kiosk sells hot and cold drinks. A dual bar chart is drawn from this table.

| Season | Spring | Summer | Autumn | Winter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hot drinks | 40 | 15 | 55 | 80 |
| Cold drinks | 25 | 90 | 30 | 10 |

**(a)** Give the two bar heights for autumn and say what the key must show. **[2]**
**(b)** In which season is the difference between hot and cold sales greatest? Show your working. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that more hot than cold drinks were sold over the year. **[2]**

**4.** A library records visitors on three days.

| Day | Monday | Tuesday | Wednesday |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adults | 18 | 14 | 20 |
| Children | 12 | 22 | 7 |

**(a)** A composite bar chart stacks children on top of adults. Give the height where each adult segment and each whole bar ends. **[3]**
**(b)** Give one reason why the children's numbers are harder to compare across days than the adults'. **[1]**

**5.** 90 pupils choose their favourite sport: football 33, swimming 24, tennis 15, cricket 18.
**(a)** Calculate the sector angle for each sport on a pie chart. **[3]**
**(b)** In a second survey of 150 pupils, the tennis sector is 84°. How many chose tennis? **[2]**

**6.** The water level of a reservoir (metres) at the start of each month is: January 12.4, February 13.1, March 14.8, April 15.6, May 14.2, June 11.9. It is shown on a line graph.
**(a)** Between which two consecutive months was the increase greatest? **[2]**
**(b)** Find the change in level from January to June. **[2]**
**(c)** Estimate the level in mid-March and say why it is only an estimate. **[2]**

**7.** Journey times in minutes for 16 trips: 34, 18, 25, 41, 29, 33, 18, 47, 52, 26, 38, 29, 41, 22, 36, 29.
**(a)** Show the times in an ordered stem-and-leaf diagram, including its key. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the range. **[1]**
**(c)** How many trips took more than 40 minutes, and which time occurs most often? **[2]**

**8.** An ice-cream stall records the midday temperature (°C) and the number of ice creams sold: (14, 22), (17, 31), (19, 38), (21, 41), (24, 55), (26, 58), (28, 70), (30, 64). These are plotted on a scatter graph.
**(a)** State suitable scales for both axes. **[2]**
**(b)** On a ninth day, (23, 15) lies well away from the other points. Suggest a reason that fits the context. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the pattern shown by the first eight points. **[2]**

**9.** The masses of 60 parcels are grouped as: 0 < m ≤ 2 (6 parcels), 2 < m ≤ 4 (14), 4 < m ≤ 6 (21), 6 < m ≤ 8 (13), 8 < m ≤ 10 (6).
**(a)** Give the coordinates of the points for a frequency polygon. **[3]**
**(b)** State the width and height of each bar of a histogram for these data. **[2]**
**(c)** How many parcels have a mass greater than 6 kg? **[1]**

**10.** (Extension only) The daily time t minutes that 100 people spend on a phone: 0 < t ≤ 10 (8 people), 10 < t ≤ 30 (26), 30 < t ≤ 60 (45), 60 < t ≤ 120 (21).
**(a)** Work out the frequency density for every class. **[3]**
**(b)** Estimate how many people spent between 40 and 60 minutes. State your assumption. **[2]**
**(c)** In another histogram a bar is 15 wide and 2.4 high. Find its frequency. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the vertical axis is frequency density and not frequency. **[1]**

**11.** (Extension only) The times in seconds taken by 100 pupils to solve a puzzle: 0 < t ≤ 20 (3), 20 < t ≤ 40 (17), 40 < t ≤ 60 (25), 60 < t ≤ 80 (25), 80 < t ≤ 100 (20), 100 < t ≤ 120 (10).
**(a)** Write the cumulative frequencies. **[2]**
**(b)** Say how the points of a cumulative frequency diagram are plotted. **[2]**
**(c)** From the diagram, estimate the median and the spread of the middle half of the times (the interquartile range). **[4]**
**(d)** Estimate how many pupils took more than 90 seconds. **[2]**

**12.** (Extension only) Test marks for two classes. Class X: minimum 12, lower quartile 25, median 34, upper quartile 41, maximum 55. Class Y: minimum 18, lower quartile 30, median 33, upper quartile 36, maximum 49.
**(a)** Find the interquartile range and the range for Class X. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the box plot for Class Y on a scale from 10 to 60. **[2]**
**(c)** Compare the two classes. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Counting gives 1 → 8, 2 → 6, 3 → 3, 4 → 2, 5 → 1 [1]. Tally marks match: ||||/ |||, ||||/ |, |||, ||, | [1]. Frequencies total 20 [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 [1]. **6 cars** **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Total the frequencies and compare with the values given.

**2. (a)** 2.5 × 12 = 30 [1]. **30 tickets** **[1]**
**(b)** 54 ÷ 12 = 4.5 [1]. **4 whole symbols and 1 half symbol** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Monday 3 × 12 = 36 and Wednesday 4 × 12 = 48 [1]. 36 + 30 + 48 = **114** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide or multiply by the key first. Writing the 4.5 shows your method even if the drawing slips.

**3. (a)** Autumn: **hot 55, cold 30** [1]. The key must show which colour means hot and which cold [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Differences: spring 15, summer 75, autumn 25, winter 70 [1]. **Summer, by 75** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Hot: 40 + 15 + 55 + 80 = 190 [1]. Cold: 25 + 90 + 30 + 10 = 155, and 190 > 155, so **more hot drinks** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* For "show that", write both totals and the comparison.

**4. (a)** Adult segments end at **18, 14 and 20** [1]. Adding children gives **30** for Monday and **36** for Tuesday [1]. Wednesday ends at **27** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The children's segments do not start at the same baseline [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Stacked heights are running totals, so add the segments.

**5. (a)** 360° ÷ 90 = 4° per pupil [1]. Football 33 × 4 = **132°**, swimming 24 × 4 = **96°** [1]. Tennis 15 × 4 = **60°**, cricket 18 × 4 = **72°**, and 132 + 96 + 60 + 72 = 360° [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** (84 ÷ 360) × 150 [1] = **35 pupils** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Check the angles total 360° before you draw.

**6. (a)** Increases: 0.7, 1.7, 0.8 (and May and June fall) [1]. **February to March, by 1.7 m** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 11.9 − 12.4 [1] = **−0.5 m**, a fall of 0.5 m [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** (14.8 + 15.6) ÷ 2 = **15.2 m** [1]. It is an estimate because the level was not measured mid-month [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the pair of months and the size of the change.

**7. (a)** Stems 1 to 5 [1]. Ordered leaves: 1 | 8 8; 2 | 2 5 6 9 9 9; 3 | 3 4 6 8; 4 | 1 1 7; 5 | 2 [1]. Key: 2 | 5 means 25 minutes [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 52 − 18 = **34 minutes** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Over 40: 41, 41, 47, 52, so **4 trips** [1]. The most common time is **29 minutes** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Order the leaves and include the key.

**8. (a)** Horizontal: 12 to 32 °C in steps of 2 [1]. Vertical: 0 to 80 in steps of 10 [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** For example, rain or an early closure, so few were sold despite the temperature [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** As temperature rises, sales generally rise [1]. The points lie in a rising band, with a small dip at 30 °C [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Use context: "hotter days sell more" says more than "the points go up".

**9. (a)** Midpoints 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 [1]. Points **(1, 6), (3, 14), (5, 21), (7, 13), (9, 6)** [1]. Joined with straight lines [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Each bar is **2 wide** (touching) [1]. Heights **6, 14, 21, 13, 6**, because the classes are equal [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 13 + 6 = **19 parcels** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the midpoints first.

**10. (a)** Frequency density = frequency ÷ width [1]. Densities **0.8, 1.3** [1], **1.5, 0.35** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 20 × 1.5 [1] = **30 people**, assuming people are spread evenly across 30 to 60 [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 2.4 × 15 [1] = **36** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Class widths differ, so heights alone would mislead; area shows frequency [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Write out each division by class width, so a single error does not hide the rest of your method.

**11. (a)** **3, 20, 45, 70, 90, 100** [2]. **[2]**
**(b)** Plot each cumulative frequency against the upper class boundary, such as (20, 3) and (40, 20) [1], start at (0, 0) and join with a smooth curve [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Median is at n ÷ 2 = 50th value [1], reading about **64 seconds** [1]. Lower quartile at the 25th value is about 44, upper quartile at the 75th is about 85 [1]. Interquartile range about 85 − 44 = **41 seconds** [1]. **[4]**
**(d)** At 90 seconds the cumulative frequency is about 80 [1]. 100 − 80 = **20 pupils** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Draw reading lines on the curve, so a slightly different reading still shows your method.

**12. (a)** Interquartile range 41 − 25 = **16** [1]. Range 55 − 12 = **43** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Mark 18, 30, 33, 36 and 49 on the scale [1]. Draw a box from 30 to 36 with a line at 33, and whiskers to 18 and 49 [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Medians are close: 34 for X, 33 for Y [1]. Interquartile ranges: 16 for X, 6 for Y [1]. So Y is more consistent and X more varied [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison is stronger with one statement about an average and one about spread, each with figures.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the key on a pictogram, dual bar chart or stem-and-leaf diagram.
- Plotting a polygon at class edges, not midpoints.
- Sector angles that fail to total 360°.
- Using frequency as a bar height for unequal classes. Work out each frequency density.
- Plotting cumulative frequency at the lower boundary, or reading the median at the wrong position.
- Comparing box plots with no figures, or using only the median.
- Quoting curve readings as exact. Say "about".
