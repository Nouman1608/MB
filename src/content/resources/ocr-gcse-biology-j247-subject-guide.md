OCR GCSE (9-1) Biology A (J247) is the separate biology course in OCR's Gateway Science
suite. It takes students from the cell to the whole organism and then out to
communities, inheritance and the global challenges biology helps to address. It is
assessed by two written papers at the end of the course, sat at either Foundation or
Higher tier. Every paper lasts 1 hour 45 minutes, carries 90 marks and is worth 50% of
the GCSE. The specification was first assessed in 2018. This overview explains how the
seven topics are arranged and how they map onto the papers.

## How the course is organised

J247 has six content topics and a seventh topic for practical skills.

**Topic 1: Cell level systems.** Cell structures, what happens in cells and what cells
need, respiration, and photosynthesis.

**Topic 2: Scaling up.** Supplying the cell and the challenges of size, which explain why
larger organisms need exchange surfaces and transport systems.

**Topic 3: Organism level systems.** Coordination and control through the nervous
system and the endocrine system, and maintaining internal environments.

**Topic 4: Community level systems.** Ecosystems.

**Topic 5: Genes, inheritance and selection.** Inheritance, and natural selection and
evolution.

**Topic 6: Global challenges.** Monitoring and maintaining the environment, feeding the
human race, and monitoring and maintaining health.

**Topic 7: Practical skills.** Not a separate block of content but the practical and
investigative skills that are assessed on both papers.

The topics move outward in scale: from cells, through organs and whole organisms, to
populations, ecosystems and the human impact on them.

## How it's assessed

| Tier | Paper | Topics tested | Time | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Foundation | J247/01 Paper 1 | Topics 1 to 3, plus Topic 7 | 1 hour 45 minutes | 90 | 50% |
| Foundation | J247/02 Paper 2 | Topics 4 to 6, plus Topic 7 | 1 hour 45 minutes | 90 | 50% |
| Higher | J247/03 Paper 3 | Topics 1 to 3, plus Topic 7 | 1 hour 45 minutes | 90 | 50% |
| Higher | J247/04 Paper 4 | Topics 4 to 6, plus Topic 7 | 1 hour 45 minutes | 90 | 50% |

A candidate sits two papers, both at the same tier: Papers 1 and 2 at Foundation, or
Papers 3 and 4 at Higher. Your teachers decide the tier entry with you, usually in the
final year. The qualification is linear, so both papers are taken at the end of the
course.

The second paper at each tier (Paper 2 or Paper 4) assumes knowledge of Topics 1 to 3
and includes synoptic assessment. In practice this means that, although the first paper
is where Topics 1 to 3 are directly examined, you still need them for the second.

## How the parts fit together

The two halves of the course are linked by design. Topics 1 to 3 build the
understanding of cells and body systems that Topics 4 to 6 then apply. Respiration and
photosynthesis in Topic 1, for example, underpin energy transfer through ecosystems in
Topic 4. What happens inside cells, from Topic 1, is the starting point for inheritance in
Topic 5. The nervous and
endocrine systems in Topic 3 connect with health and disease in Topic 6. This is why
the second paper can set synoptic questions that reach back to the first half.

Topic 7 links the two papers in a different way. Practical skills, such as planning an
investigation, taking measurements, handling data and evaluating results, are assessed
on both papers, so practical work done in lessons is revision for the exams as well as
experience of doing science.

## Planning your study

Most schools teach the topics in numerical order across the course, and that order makes
sense: each topic depends on ideas from the ones before. A workable plan is:

- **Earlier in the course:** Topics 1 to 3, with practical activities built in as each
  topic is taught.
- **Later in the course:** Topics 4 to 6, revisiting Topics 1 to 3 wherever they come up
  again.
- **Final months:** revision across all seven topics, full papers at your tier, and
  focused work on practical and data questions.

Because the second paper is synoptic, do not revise the two halves as separate units. A
useful habit is to finish each topic in Topics 4 to 6 by noting which earlier ideas it
relies on. Start exam-style questions as soon as a topic is complete, move to full
papers once the content is covered, and use the exam preparation page for advice on
tier choice and paper technique.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in OCR GCSE Biology A?**
Two. Foundation tier candidates sit Papers 1 and 2; Higher tier candidates sit Papers 3 and 4. Each paper lasts 1 hour 45 minutes, carries 90 marks and is worth 50%.

**Which topics are on each J247 paper?**
Paper 1 (Foundation) and Paper 3 (Higher) test Topics 1 to 3 plus Topic 7. Paper 2 (Foundation) and Paper 4 (Higher) test Topics 4 to 6 plus Topic 7.

**Do I need Topics 1 to 3 for the second paper?**
Yes. Papers 2 and 4 assume knowledge of Topics 1 to 3 and include synoptic assessment.

**Is practical work examined in J247?**
Yes. Topic 7, practical skills, is assessed on both papers at both tiers.

**What does the Gateway in OCR Biology A mean?**
Gateway is the name of OCR's science suite that J247 belongs to. The suite also includes OCR's other separate science GCSEs.
