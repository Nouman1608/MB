## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Which statement best describes the bonding in solid sodium?

A: Attraction between Na⁺ ions and Na⁻ ions

B: Shared pairs of electrons between sodium atoms

C: Attraction between Na⁺ ions in a lattice and delocalised electrons

D: Weak forces between Na₂ molecules

**[1]**

**2.** State the charge on the ions in a lattice of barium, a Group 2 metal. **[1]**

**3.** State the ratio of delocalised electrons to positive ions in solid aluminium. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why it is wrong to refer to intermolecular forces in a piece of copper. **[1]**

**5.** Predict whether sodium or aluminium has the stronger metallic bonding, and give a reason. **[2]**

**6.** Calcium and strontium are both in Group 2. Predict which has the weaker metallic bonding and explain your prediction. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why the formula of magnesium metal is written simply as Mg, not as a molecule. **[1]**

### Section B

**8.** Approximate melting points of some Group 1 metals: lithium 181 °C, sodium 98 °C, potassium 63 °C, rubidium 39 °C.

**(a)** Describe the trend in melting point. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain the trend in terms of metallic bonding. **[3]**

**(c)** Predict how the melting point of caesium compares with that of rubidium. **[1]**

**9.** A student writes: "Magnesium contains a lattice of magnesium atoms. Each atom gives one electron to the sea of electrons. The electrons are the metallic bond."

**(a)** Identify three errors in the student's description and correct each one. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe the diagram you would draw to represent the bonding in magnesium. **[2]**

**10.** Compare the bonding in magnesium metal with the bonding in magnesium oxide. Give one similarity and two differences. **[3]**

**11.** The table shows information about three Period 3 metals.

| Metal | Ion | Melting point / °C |
|---|---|---|
| Sodium | Na⁺ | 98 |
| Magnesium | Mg²⁺ | 650 |
| Aluminium | Al³⁺ | 660 |

**(a)** State the number of delocalised electrons per atom in each metal. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the metallic bonding becomes stronger from sodium to aluminium. **[3]**

**(c)** Aluminium's melting point is only slightly higher than magnesium's. Suggest why a melting point does not always show the full difference in bond strength. **[1]**

**12.** Describe the model of metallic bonding. Use it to explain why magnesium has a higher melting point than sodium and why both metals conduct electricity when solid. **[6]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** **C** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* There are no negative metal ions and no molecules in a metal.

**2.** **2+** (Ba²⁺) [1] **[1]**

**3.** **3 : 1** [1] **[1]**

**4.** Copper has a giant metallic lattice of positive ions and delocalised electrons; it contains no molecules [1] **[1]**

**5.** **Aluminium** [1]; Al³⁺ has a higher charge, is smaller, and each atom releases three delocalised electrons rather than one [1] **[2]**

**6.** **Strontium** [1]; both form 2+ ions, but Sr²⁺ is larger, so the delocalised electrons are further from the positive charge and the attraction is weaker [1] **[2]**

**7.** It is a giant lattice of ions and delocalised electrons with no separate molecules, so the formula shows only the element [1] **[1]**

### Section B

**8.**

**(a)** Melting point decreases down the group [1]

**(b)** Each metal forms 1+ ions with one delocalised electron per atom [1]; down the group the ions get larger (an extra shell each time) [1]; so the attraction between the ions and the delocalised electrons is weaker and less energy is needed to overcome it [1]

**(c)** Lower than rubidium's (below 39 °C) [1] **[5]**

**9.**

**(a)** "Atoms" should be positive ions, Mg²⁺ [1]; each atom releases two electrons, not one [1]; the bond is the attraction between the positive ions and the delocalised electrons, not the electrons themselves [1]

**(b)** Rows of circles labelled 2+ (or Mg²⁺) in a regular lattice [1]; electrons drawn between the ions, two for every ion, labelled as delocalised [1] **[5]**

**10.** Similarity: both involve strong electrostatic attraction in a giant lattice (or both contain Mg²⁺ ions) [1]; differences: in MgO the negative particles are O²⁻ ions, in Mg they are delocalised electrons [1]; in MgO electrons are transferred to oxygen and held there, while in Mg the electrons are free to move throughout the structure [1] **[3]**

**11.**

**(a)** Na 1, Mg 2, Al 3 (all needed) [1]

**(b)** The charge on the ions increases [1]; the number of delocalised electrons per atom increases [1]; the ions get smaller, so the attraction between ions and delocalised electrons gets stronger [1]

**(c)** Melting point also depends on how the ions are arranged in the solid and how much of the bonding must be overcome to melt, not only on bond strength [1] **[5]**

**12.** Indicative content; one mark per valid point, up to 6 [6] **[6]**

- Metal atoms release their outer electrons, which become delocalised throughout the structure.
- This leaves positive ions arranged in a regular lattice.
- Metallic bonding is the electrostatic attraction between the positive ions and the delocalised electrons.
- Magnesium forms Mg²⁺ ions and releases two delocalised electrons per atom; sodium forms Na⁺ and releases one.
- Mg²⁺ is also smaller, so the attraction is stronger and more energy is needed to melt magnesium.
- Both conduct because the delocalised electrons can move through the lattice and carry charge.

*Examiner insight:* Strong answers name the particles in each metal and compare them directly, rather than saying "magnesium has stronger bonds" without a reason.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling the ions "atoms".
- Using the wrong ion charge or number of delocalised electrons.
- Saying the electrons, rather than the attraction, are the bond.
- Explaining melting points by atomic mass.
