The command words follow the syllabus list, such as define, state, identify, outline, explain, calculate and justify. All businesses are invented and money is in dollars.

## Questions

**1.** Define the term limited liability. **[2]**

**2.** State two features of a partnership. **[2]**

**3.** Lindrick Cycles is a sole trader's business. It closes owing 64,000 to suppliers and a bank. The sale of its assets raises 22,000.

**(a)** Calculate the amount of debt still unpaid. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the owner is at greater risk than if Lindrick Cycles had been a private limited company in which the owner had invested 15,000. **[3]**

**4.** Rudgwick Cleaning is a partnership of three people. Their agreement shares profit in the ratio 3 : 2 : 1. The profit for the year is 96,000.

**(a)** Calculate the amount each partner receives. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one problem of running a business as a partnership. **[2]**

**5.** Elmsett Dairy shop is a franchise outlet. The franchisee paid an initial fee of 30,000 and pays the franchisor 5% of sales revenue each year. Sales revenue this year is 520,000.

**(a)** Calculate the royalty payable this year. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the total paid to the franchisor in the first year. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain one benefit to the franchisee of buying a franchise rather than starting an independent shop. **[2]**

**6.** Outline two differences between a private limited company and a public limited company. **[4]**

**7.** Wolvey Steel and Cressing Turbines agree to work together on a 800,000 project. Wolvey will pay 45% of the costs and Cressing the rest.

**(a)** Define the term joint venture. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the amount each business pays. **[2]**

**8.** Nadia wants to open Pennyroyal Day Nursery. She needs 400,000 for premises and equipment. She has 120,000 in savings, two friends will each invest 100,000, and she owns a house she does not want to risk. She wants to make decisions with her friends and does not want shares sold to the public.

Recommend and justify a suitable form of business organisation. **[6]**

**9.** Dalwood Telecoms had sales revenue of 90,000 in a market worth 750,000. The next year its sales revenue was 108,000 in a market worth 900,000.

**(a)** Calculate its market share in each year. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain whether Dalwood Telecoms met a growth objective and a market share objective. **[3]**

**10.** A bus company is planning a fare increase.

**(a)** Identify one internal and one external stakeholder group of the company. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one possible conflict between two stakeholder groups over the fare increase. **[4]**
**(c)** Identify two other business objectives, apart from profit, that the company might have. **[2]**

**11.** **(a)** Define the term social enterprise. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the objectives of a public corporation may differ from those of a private sector company. **[4]**

**12.** Abbotts Garments Ltd made a profit after tax of 80,000. The directors retained 35% for a new factory and paid the rest as dividends. The company has 400,000 shares. Some shareholders say that Abbotts should now pay out more.

**(a)** Calculate the amount retained, the total dividends, and the dividend per share. **[3]**
**(b)** A shareholder owns 50,000 shares. Calculate her dividend. **[1]**
**(c)** Justify whether Abbotts should keep retaining 35% of profit. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The owner's loss is limited to the amount they have invested [1], so personal possessions cannot be claimed to pay the business debts [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition needs the idea of a limit; saying only "limited" repeats the term and is unlikely to gain credit.

**2.** Any two from: two or more owners [1]; profit shared as agreed; owners usually have unlimited liability; shared decisions or capital [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "State" asks for features only, so a reason or example adds no further credit.

**3. (a)** 64,000 − 22,000 [1] = **42,000** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** As a sole trader the owner has unlimited liability, so the 42,000 can be recovered from personal assets [1]. In a company the loss is limited to the 15,000 invested [1], so the 42,000 shortfall falls on creditors rather than on the owner [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Using the figures from the stem to show the difference is stronger than a general statement about liability.

**4. (a)** Total parts 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 [1]. One part is 96,000 ÷ 6 = 16,000 [1]. Shares are **48,000, 32,000 and 16,000** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Partners may disagree over decisions [1], which can slow the business or damage it; or unlimited liability means one partner's debts can affect the others [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A problem that is developed, with a consequence, usually earns more than a bare label such as "arguments".

**5. (a)** 520,000 × 5% [1] = **26,000** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** 30,000 + 26,000 = **56,000** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** The franchisee uses a known brand [1], which attracts customers more quickly than an unknown independent shop [1]. (Support and training from the franchisor is an alternative benefit.) **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Explain" needs a benefit and a reason; naming "brand" alone is a statement, not an explanation.

**6.** Shares of a public limited company can be sold to the general public through a stock exchange [1], while a private limited company cannot offer shares to the public [1]. A public limited company can raise much larger sums of capital [1], while a private limited company usually has fewer shareholders and so more control by the original owners [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Outline two differences" is best answered with a pair of linked contrasting points, not two separate features of one company.

**7. (a)** Two or more businesses agree to work together [1] on a particular project, sharing costs, risks and profits [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Wolvey: 800,000 × 0.45 = **360,000** [1]. Cressing: 800,000 − 360,000 = **440,000** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the calculation for each partner, because the second figure is checked against the first.

**8.** Private limited company [1]. Limited liability protects Nadia's house if the nursery fails [1]. She and her friends can put in 320,000 (120,000 + 200,000) as shareholders, with the other 80,000 borrowed or raised from more shares [1], and shares are not sold to the public so the three keep control [1]. A partnership would be quicker and cheaper to set up but Nadia and her friends would have unlimited liability, which is a risk to her house [1]. On balance a private limited company is the better fit because protecting her house and keeping control matter most to Nadia [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that pick a form, link it to several facts in the stem and weigh a rejected option tend to be placed higher than answers that list general features.

**9. (a)** Year 1: 90,000 ÷ 750,000 × 100 = **12%** [1]. Year 2: 108,000 ÷ 900,000 × 100 = **12%** [1]. Market share is unchanged [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Sales revenue grew by 20% (18,000 ÷ 90,000) so a growth objective was met [1]. The market also grew by 20% [1], so market share did not rise and a market share objective of gaining share was not met [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Comparing the growth rates of the firm and the market is the point of the question; either figure alone gives only part of the answer.

**10. (a)** Internal, for example employees, managers or owners [1]; external, for example passengers, the government or the local community [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Passengers want low fares [1] while owners want higher revenue and profit [1]. A fare rise helps the owners but costs passengers more, so both objectives cannot be fully met [1], and passengers may use other transport and reduce revenue [1]. **[4]**
**(c)** Any two of survival [1], growth, market share, customer satisfaction, social benefit [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A conflict answer names two groups, states both objectives and says why they clash; one-sided answers gain less.

**11. (a)** A business that sells goods or services [1] mainly to achieve a social or environmental aim, with most profit reinvested [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** A public corporation is owned by government and often aims to provide a service to everyone at an affordable price [1]. A private sector company usually aims at profit, growth or survival [1] because owners expect a return [1]. A public corporation may accept a low profit or a loss that the government covers [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both sectors in the same answer; describing only the public corporation leaves the difference unstated.

**12. (a)** Retained: 80,000 × 0.35 = **28,000** [1]. Dividends: 80,000 − 28,000 = **52,000** [1]. Per share: 52,000 ÷ 400,000 = **0.13** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 50,000 × 0.13 = **6,500** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** Retaining profit pays for the new factory without borrowing [1] and can raise long-term profit and growth [1]. Shareholders, such as the one receiving 6,500, want more of the profit now [1], and unhappy shareholders may sell their shares or press the directors [1]. The result depends on whether the factory will increase future profit [1]. A reasoned judgement is that Abbotts should keep a high retention while the factory is built and then raise dividends [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A justify answer that supports its decision with both sides and the figures in the stem earns more than one that only repeats advantages.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining limited liability as "no liability" or saying the business is protected rather than the owner.
- Describing a feature when the command word asks for an explanation with a reason.
- Calculating without showing the working or the unit.
- Mixing up growth in sales revenue with a rise in market share.
- Listing stakeholder groups with no stated objectives or no conflict.
- Recommending a form of organisation without using the facts in the stem.
- Writing one-sided answers to a justify question.
- Confusing private and public limited companies, or private and public sector.
