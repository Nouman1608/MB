These notes condense subtopic **3.7 Perpendicular lines** from Topic 3 of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The subtopic is **Extended only** (C3.7 is "Extended content only"): "Find the gradient and equation of a straight line perpendicular to a given line", including perpendicular bisectors. For full explanations, read the [perpendicular lines study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-perpendicular-lines-cambridge-igcse-maths/); for exam-style questions, use the [perpendicular lines practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-perpendicular-lines-cambridge-igcse-maths-practice/).

## The rule

```
Perpendicular gradient = -1 / m        (negative reciprocal)
m1 x m2 = -1                           (test for perpendicular lines)
```

Flip the fraction **and** change the sign.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Perpendicular | Meeting at a right angle (90°) |
| Reciprocal | 1 divided by the number: reciprocal of 4 is ¼, of 2/3 is 3/2 |
| Negative reciprocal | Reciprocal with the sign changed: of 4 is –¼ |
| Perpendicular bisector | Line through the midpoint of a segment at 90° to it |
| Tangent | Line touching a circle at one point; perpendicular to the radius there |

## Quick conversions

| m | –1/m |
|---|---|
| 2 | –½ |
| –5 | 1/5 |
| 3/4 | –4/3 |
| –1/6 | 6 |
| 1.5 = 3/2 | –2/3 |
| 0.2 = 1/5 | –5 |

Turn decimals into fractions first: 1.5 = 3/2, so the perpendicular gradient is –2/3, not –1.5.

## Gradient from awkward equations

```
2y = 3x + 1       y = 1.5x + 0.5       m = 3/2    perpendicular -2/3
4x + y = 9        y = -4x + 9          m = -4     perpendicular 1/4
x - 3y = 6        y = (1/3)x - 2       m = 1/3    perpendicular -3
```

## Method: perpendicular line through a point

```
1. m of the given line (y the subject first)
2. m_perp = -1/m
3. Substitute the point to find c
4. Write the equation; give the requested form
```

Example: perpendicular to y = 4x – 7 through (8, 1). m = –¼; 1 = –2 + c, c = 3; **y = –¼x + 3**, or **x + 4y = 12**.

## Method: perpendicular bisector of AB

```
1. Midpoint M of AB
2. Gradient of AB
3. Negative reciprocal
4. Line through M (not A, not B)
```

Example: A(0, 2), B(4, 6). M = (2, 4); gradient AB = 1; perpendicular gradient –1; 4 = –2 + c, c = 6; **y = –x + 6**.

## Uses in longer questions

| Question asks | Use |
|---|---|
| Is angle ABC a right angle? | Gradient AB × gradient BC = –1? |
| Is the parallelogram a rectangle? | Adjacent sides perpendicular |
| Is the parallelogram a rhombus? | Diagonals perpendicular |
| Tangent to a circle at T | Perpendicular to the radius to T |
| Shortest path from a point to a line | Along the perpendicular |

Horizontal (y = k) and vertical (x = k) lines are perpendicular, but the product rule cannot be used, as the vertical line has no gradient.

## Where perpendicular lines meet

Solve the two equations simultaneously. The crossing point is the **foot of the perpendicular**, and the distance from the original point to it is the shortest distance to the line.

```
Line L: y = 3x - 5.  Perpendicular through A(4, 3): y = -(1/3)x + 13/3
3x - 5 = -(1/3)x + 13/3   ->   9x - 15 = -x + 13   ->   x = 2.8, y = 3.4
Shortest distance from A to L = sqrt(1.2^2 + 0.4^2) = sqrt(1.6) = 1.26 (3 s.f.)
```

## Exam traps

- Changing only the sign, or only flipping the fraction.
- Taking m from 2y = 3x + 1 as 3.
- Using an end point instead of the midpoint for a perpendicular bisector.
- Applying –1/m to a question about parallel lines.
- Converting –1/m into a rounded decimal: –1/3 is not –0.33.
- Losing a sign when clearing fractions to reach ax + by = c.

## Self-test

1. Write down the gradient perpendicular to a line with gradient 5.
2. Write down the gradient perpendicular to a line with gradient –3/7.
3. Find the gradient perpendicular to 3y = 6x – 1.
4. Find the gradient perpendicular to 2x + 5y = 4.
5. Are lines with gradients 4/3 and –3/4 perpendicular?
6. Find the equation of the line perpendicular to y = 2x + 3 through (4, 1).
7. Find the equation of the line perpendicular to y = –x + 4 through (–2, 3).
8. Find the perpendicular bisector of (2, 1) and (6, 5).
9. Show that y = 3x – 2 and x + 3y = 9 are perpendicular.
10. A circle has centre (0, 0) and passes through T(3, 4). Find the gradient of the tangent at T.
11. Write down the equation of the line perpendicular to x = 5 through (1, –2).
12. Is triangle with vertices (0, 0), (2, 4) and (6, 2) right-angled at (2, 4)?

### Answers

1. **–1/5**
2. **7/3**
3. y = 2x – 1/3, m = 2: **–½**
4. y = –0.4x + 0.8, m = –2/5: **5/2**
5. (4/3) × (–3/4) = –1: **yes**
6. m = –½; 1 = –2 + c, c = 3: **y = –½x + 3**
7. m = 1; 3 = –2 + c, c = 5: **y = x + 5**
8. M(4, 3), gradient 1, perpendicular –1; 3 = –4 + c: **y = –x + 7**
9. Gradients 3 and –1/3; 3 × (–1/3) = –1, **so perpendicular**
10. Radius gradient 4/3: tangent gradient **–3/4**
11. x = 5 is vertical, so the line is horizontal: **y = –2**
12. Gradients 4/2 = 2 and (2 – 4)/(6 – 2) = –½; product –1: **yes**
