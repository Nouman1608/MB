## Points, lines and planes (8.01a)

A point has position but no size. A line has length and no width. A line segment is the part of a line between two points. A plane is a flat surface that goes on for ever, such as the top of a table extended in every direction. A vertex is a corner. An edge is where two faces, or two sides, meet.

Parallel lines stay the same distance apart and never meet. On a diagram they carry matching arrows. Perpendicular lines meet at a right angle, shown by a small square at the meeting point.

## Angles and labelling (8.01b)

| Type | Size |
|---|---|
| Acute | more than 0° and less than 90° |
| Right | exactly 90° |
| Obtuse | more than 90° and less than 180° |
| Reflex | more than 180° and less than 360° |

Labelling conventions:

- AB means the line segment from A to B, or its length.
- Angle ABC (written with the angle symbol or in words) is the angle at B, between BA and BC. The middle letter is always the vertex.
- In triangle ABC, the side opposite angle A is written a, so a = BC. Likewise b = CA and c = AB.

### Worked example 1

In triangle PQR, angle QPR = 38° and angle PQR = 112°. (a) Which side is r? (b) Name the type of angle PQR. (c) Find the reflex angle at Q on the outside of the triangle.

```
(a) r is opposite angle R, so r = PQ.
(b) 112° is more than 90° and less than 180°: obtuse.
(c) Reflex angle = 360° - 112° = 248°.
```

Check the answer to (c) is more than 180°. If it is not, you have used the wrong angle.

## Polygons (8.01c)

A polygon is a closed 2D shape with straight sides. A regular polygon has all sides equal and all angles equal.

| Sides | Name |
|---|---|
| 3 | triangle |
| 4 | quadrilateral |
| 5 | pentagon |
| 6 | hexagon |
| 8 | octagon |

Triangles: equilateral (three equal sides), isosceles (two equal sides), scalene (no equal sides).

Quadrilaterals, with their key properties:

| Name | Key properties |
|---|---|
| Square | all four sides equal, each corner a right angle |
| Rectangle | each corner a right angle, so opposite sides are equal |
| Parallelogram | opposite sides equal and parallel |
| Rhombus | four equal sides, opposite sides parallel |
| Kite | two pairs of adjacent equal sides |
| Trapezium | one pair of parallel sides |

A square is also a rectangle and a rhombus, but when a question asks for the most specific name, give the square.

## 3D solids (8.01d and 8.06a)

A face is a flat surface of a solid. Solids with curved parts have a surface rather than only faces. A prism keeps one cross-section, a fixed polygon, through its whole length. A pyramid has a polygon base and triangular faces that meet at one vertex, the apex.

| Solid | Faces or surfaces | Edges | Vertices |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cube | 6 square faces | 12 | 8 |
| Cuboid | 6 rectangular faces | 12 | 8 |
| Triangular prism | 5 | 9 | 6 |
| Square-based pyramid | 5 | 8 | 5 |
| Cylinder | 2 flat circular faces, 1 curved surface | 2 circular edges | 0 |
| Cone | 1 flat circular face, 1 curved surface | 1 circular edge | 1 |
| Sphere | 1 curved surface | 0 | 0 |

For a prism whose cross-section has n sides, the counts are n + 2 faces, 3n edges and 2n vertices. For a pyramid whose base has n sides, they are n + 1 faces, 2n edges and n + 1 vertices. For solids with flat faces, faces + vertices − edges = 2, which is a useful check. It is not listed in the specification, so treat it as a checking tool only.

### Worked example 2

Find the faces, edges and vertices of (a) an octagonal prism and (b) a pentagonal pyramid.

```
(a) n = 8:  faces = 8 + 2 = 10
            edges = 3 x 8 = 24
            vertices = 2 x 8 = 16
    Check: 10 + 16 - 24 = 2
(b) n = 5:  faces = 5 + 1 = 6
            edges = 2 x 5 = 10
            vertices = 5 + 1 = 6
    Check: 6 + 6 - 10 = 2
```

## Drawing diagrams from descriptions (8.01e)

Read the description one fact at a time. Draw a rough sketch first, add each fact as you go, and mark equal lengths, right angles and parallel lines with the standard symbols.

### Worked example 3

Sketch kite PQRS, where PQ = PS = 4 cm, QR = SR = 6 cm and the diagonal PR = 8 cm.

```
Step 1: draw the diagonal PR, 8 cm, vertically.
Step 2: Q and S are on opposite sides of PR.
        PQ = PS = 4 and QR = SR = 6 put Q and S
        at equal distances from P and from R.
Step 3: join P-Q-R-S-P and tick the equal pairs.

        R
       / \
   6  /   \  6
     Q--+--S      PR is the axis of symmetry
      \ | /
     4 \|/ 4
        P
```

Check: 4 + 6 is more than 8, so triangle PQR can exist.

## Instruments (8.01f)

- Ruler: measure and draw straight lines. Start from the zero mark, not the end of the ruler.
- Protractor: line the base line up with one arm and the centre mark on the vertex. Read from the scale that starts at 0° on your arm. Check by asking whether the angle is acute or obtuse.
- Compasses: set the radius against a ruler, put the point on the centre and turn the pencil end through a full turn to draw a circle.

For a reflex angle of x°, draw the angle 360° − x° and the reflex angle is the other side.

### Worked example 4

Construct triangle ABC with AB = 7 cm, angle A = 40° and angle B = 75°.

```
1. Draw AB = 7 cm.
2. At A, measure 40° and draw a long arm.
3. At B, measure 75° on the same side and draw a long arm.
4. C is where the two arms cross.
5. Angle C = 180° - 40° - 75° = 65°.
```

A measuring check: AC should be about 7.5 cm and BC about 5.0 cm. If yours differ by a lot, re-check the protractor scale. Leave construction lines on the diagram.

## Coordinates in plane geometry (8.01g)

Write a point as (x, y): across first, then up or down. Many geometry problems are coordinate problems in disguise.

- The midpoint of the points (a, b) and (c, d) is ((a + c)/2, (b + d)/2).
- Horizontal and vertical distances are found by subtracting coordinates.

### Worked example 5

P(1, 2), Q(6, 2) and R(8, 6) are three vertices of parallelogram PQRS. Find S, then reflect the parallelogram in the x-axis and give the image of S.

```
PQ is horizontal, length 6 - 1 = 5.
In a parallelogram, SR is parallel and equal to PQ,
so S is 5 to the left of R: S = (8 - 5, 6) = (3, 6).
Check: the midpoint of PR is (4.5, 4) and the midpoint of
QS is (4.5, 4). The diagonals bisect each other.
Reflection in the x-axis changes the sign of y:
S' = (3, -6).
```

## Plans and elevations (8.06b)

A plan is the view from directly above. An elevation is a view from the side: the front elevation looks from the front, the side elevation from a side. Mark the viewing direction with an arrow when a question asks you to.

| Solid | Plan | Front elevation | Side elevation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cuboid (standing) | rectangle | rectangle | rectangle |
| Triangular prism (on a rectangular face, triangular end facing you) | rectangle | triangle | rectangle |
| Cylinder (standing) | circle | rectangle | rectangle |
| Cone (standing) | circle with centre point | triangle | triangle |
| Square-based pyramid | square with lines to the corners | triangle | triangle |
| Sphere | circle | circle | circle |

Hidden edges are often drawn dashed. Edges where two faces at different heights meet appear as lines on the view.

### Worked example 6

A solid is built from cubes. The numbers show how many cubes are stacked in each position, viewed from above with the front at the bottom.

```
Plan (heights)        back    3 2 1
                      front   1 1 2
```

Draw the front elevation and the side elevation from the right.

```
Front elevation: tallest stack in each column
columns: left 3, middle 2, right 2

   # . .
   # # #
   # # #

Side elevation from the right (front on the left):
rows: front 2, back 3

   . #
   # #
   # #
```

The solid has 1 + 1 + 2 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10 cubes.

## Isometric drawings (8.06b)

On isometric dot paper, vertical edges are drawn vertically, and the other two directions go up and to the right, and up and to the left, at 30° to the horizontal. Lengths are drawn to scale along all three directions.

Method for the solid in worked example 6:

1. Choose a front-left viewing position. You will see the top, the front faces and the left faces.
2. Count the exposed faces: 6 top, 7 front and 5 left, so 18 squares in all. Taller stacks hide part of some tops, so treat 18 as a rough check at the end.
3. Draw the base outline first, then add the heights one stack at a time, starting with the stacks nearest to you.
4. Rub out lines that would be hidden behind taller stacks.

A cuboid 3 units by 2 units by 1 unit high needs three edges of 3 units, 2 units and 1 unit drawn from one corner, then the parallel edges completed.

## Common errors

- Naming the vertex wrongly: angle ABC is at B, not at A or C.
- Reading the wrong protractor scale, giving an obtuse angle when the angle is acute.
- Calling a trapezium a parallelogram, or a kite a rhombus.
- Giving a cylinder vertices or a cone two vertices. A cylinder has none and a cone has one, at its apex.
- Drawing a side elevation back to front.
- Drawing isometric edges at 45° instead of 30° to the horizontal, or tilting the vertical edges.
