These notes condense section 2.1 of the IB *Mathematics: analysis and approaches guide* (first assessment 2021), equations of a straight line, which is common to SL and HL. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [2.1 study guide](/resources/ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-2-1/); to practise under exam conditions, use the [2.1 practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-2-1-practice/).

## Core facts

- Gradient through (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂): **m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁)**.
- **y = mx + c:** gradient m, y-intercept (0, c).
- **y − y₁ = m(x − x₁):** line with gradient m through (x₁, y₁).
- **ax + by + d = 0:** gradient −a/b (b ≠ 0); intercepts from x = 0 and y = 0.
- **Parallel:** m₁ = m₂. **Perpendicular:** m₁ × m₂ = −1, so m₂ = −1/m₁.
- Horizontal line y = k has gradient 0; vertical line x = k has undefined gradient.
- Midpoint of (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂): ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2).
- Gradient of an incline = vertical rise ÷ horizontal distance.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gradient | Change in y per unit increase in x; the rate of change of a linear function |
| y-intercept | Point where the line meets the y-axis, x = 0 |
| x-intercept | Point where the line meets the x-axis, y = 0 |
| Parallel lines | Lines with equal gradients that never meet (unless they coincide) |
| Perpendicular lines | Lines meeting at right angles; gradients multiply to −1 |
| Collinear | Lying on one straight line |
| Perpendicular bisector | Line through the midpoint of a segment at right angles to it |
| Point of intersection | The point satisfying both equations at once |

## Shortcuts that save time

- **Parallel to ax + by + d = 0:** write ax + by = (value at the given point).
- **Perpendicular to ax + by + d = 0:** write bx − ay = (value at the given point).
- **Gradient from general form:** −(coefficient of x)/(coefficient of y).
- **Collinearity:** compare gradients AB and BC; equal gradients and a shared point B mean one line.
- **Intersection:** substitute y from one equation into the other, or eliminate x or y.

**Quick example.** Through (4, −3), perpendicular to 2x − 7y = 1: 7x + 2y = 7(4) + 2(−3) = 22, so 7x + 2y − 22 = 0.

## Methods in steps

**Equation through two points**
1. Find m from the two points.
2. Substitute m and either point into y − y₁ = m(x − x₁).
3. Rearrange into the form requested.

**Perpendicular bisector of AB**
1. Find the midpoint M.
2. Find the gradient of AB and take the negative reciprocal.
3. Use y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) through M.

**Lines with a constant k**
1. Write each gradient in terms of k.
2. Set m₁ = m₂ (parallel) or m₁m₂ = −1 (perpendicular) and solve.
3. Check any value of k that makes a coefficient zero separately.

## Interpreting a linear model

In y = mx + c used as a model:

- m is the rate of change, with units "y-units per x-unit" (for example dollars per minute);
- c is the starting value when x = 0 (for example a fixed charge);
- the point of intersection of two models is where they give equal outputs.

**Quick example.** An invented gym charges C = 30 + 4v dollars for v visits; another charges C = 6.5v. They cost the same when 30 + 4v = 6.5v, so v = 12.

## Exam traps

- Gradient of ax + by + d = 0 written as a, b/a or −b/a instead of −a/b.
- Perpendicular gradient given as −m or 1/m instead of −1/m.
- Fractions left in an answer that must be "in the form ax + by + d = 0 where a, b, d ∈ ℤ".
- Using a slant length instead of the horizontal distance for an incline.
- Writing x = 5 as y = 5 for a vertical line.
- Sign slips when the point has negative coordinates: y − (−3) is y + 3.

## Self-test

1. Find the gradient of the line through (−1, 4) and (3, −4).
2. Find the gradient and both intercepts of 3x + 4y − 12 = 0.
3. A line has gradient −2/5. Write down the gradient of a perpendicular line.
4. Find the equation of the line with gradient 3 through (2, −1), in the form y = mx + c.
5. Write down the equation of the line through (5, 2) parallel to the y-axis.
6. Find the line through (1, 6) parallel to 2x − y = 3.
7. Are y = 4x − 1 and x + 4y = 8 perpendicular? Give a reason.
8. Find the midpoint of (−2, 3) and (6, −5).
9. Find the intersection of y = 2x + 1 and y = 7 − x.
10. Write y − 3 = −(2/3)(x + 6) in the form ax + by + d = 0 with integer coefficients.
11. A road climbs 36 m over a horizontal distance of 900 m. Find its gradient.
12. The point (k, 5) lies on 3x − 2y = 2. Find k.

### Answers

1. (−4 − 4)/(3 − (−1)) = −8/4 = −2
2. Gradient −3/4; intercepts (4, 0) and (0, 3)
3. 5/2
4. y + 1 = 3(x − 2), so y = 3x − 7
5. x = 5
6. 2x − y = 2(1) − 6 = −4, so y = 2x + 4
7. Yes: the gradients are 4 and −1/4, and 4 × (−1/4) = −1
8. (2, −1)
9. 2x + 1 = 7 − x gives x = 2, so the point is (2, 5)
10. 3y − 9 = −2x − 12, so 2x + 3y + 3 = 0
11. 36/900 = 0.04
12. 3k − 10 = 2, so k = 4
