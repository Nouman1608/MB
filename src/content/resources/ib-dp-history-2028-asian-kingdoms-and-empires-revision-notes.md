These revision notes are for **higher level (HL) only**. The regional study of Asian kingdoms and empires (750 CE to 1500 CE) is examined on Paper 3, which standard level students do not sit. They condense the IB *History guide*, first assessment 2028. Every Paper 3 question on this study requires you to refer to **one** kingdom or empire, so decide on your main example early. These notes centre on the Mongol Empire, with Angkor and the Kamakura shogunate as comparisons.

Read the fuller [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-asian-kingdoms-and-empires/) first, then test yourself with the [practice set](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-asian-kingdoms-and-empires-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Quriltai | A gathering of Mongol leaders that chose a khan or agreed major decisions |
| Khan, Great Khan | Mongol ruler; the Great Khan ruled above the regional khans |
| Keshig | The khan's personal guard and household, used to build loyalty and train officials |
| Yam | The relay system of post stations that carried messages and officials |
| Paiza | A tablet giving its bearer the right to use the yam |
| Pax Mongolica | The period of relatively safe Eurasian travel under Mongol rule |
| Yuan dynasty | The Mongol dynasty in China, proclaimed by Kubilai in 1271 |
| Baray | A large reservoir in the Angkor water system |
| Shogun | The military ruler of Japan, appointed in the emperor's name |
| Gokenin | The shogun's direct vassals (warriors) in Kamakura Japan |
| Regent | A ruler who acts for another; the Hōjō ruled as regents for the shoguns |

## Reasons for emergence (quick grid)

| Factor | Mongol Empire | Angkor |
|---|---|---|
| Military | Decimal army, merit promotion, horse archers, siege engineers | Armies raised by the king; wars with the Cham |
| Economic | Herds, raiding, then control of trade routes | Rice surplus from reservoirs and canals |
| Political | Chinggis Khan's unification, keshig, marriage alliances | A god-king ideology from Jayavarman II |
| Environmental | Debated favourable climate on the steppe; mobility | Monsoon rains and a managed water system |

## Leaders: who did what

- **Chinggis Khan:** unified the steppe tribes, set up the army and guard, began the conquest of Khwarazm and Western Xia.
- **Ögedei (Great Khan 1229 to 1241):** ended the Jin dynasty and founded the capital Karakorum.
- **Möngke (1251 to 1259):** extended Mongol control in Iran and China, and died on campaign.
- **Hülegü:** took Baghdad in 1258 and founded the Ilkhanate.
- **Kubilai (1260 to 1294):** proclaimed the Yuan, conquered the Song, and launched failed sea campaigns.
- **Suryavarman II:** built Angkor Wat, early twelfth century.
- **Jayavarman VII:** rebuilt after the Cham sack of 1177; Bayon, hospitals, Buddhism.
- **Minamoto no Yoritomo:** founded the Kamakura shogunate; the **Hōjō regents** held real power after him.

## Domestic developments in brief

- **Social structures:** Mongol hierarchy based on service and descent; Yuan China ranked peoples into legal groups; Angkor ranked under a god-king.
- **Culture and arts:** Persian, Chinese and Mongol court art; Rashid al-Din's history; Angkor Wat and the Bayon.
- **Belief:** the Mongols tolerated many religions; Angkor moved from Hindu devotion to Mahayana Buddhism under Jayavarman VII; Zen Buddhism grew in Kamakura Japan.
- **Science and technology:** gunpowder, paper money, astronomy, medicine and printing moved along Mongol routes.

## Foreign relations

- **Diplomacy:** submission demanded; marriage alliances (for example with Korea's kings).
- **Exchange:** envoys, merchants and missionaries; Marco Polo (use with care).
- **Trade:** overland routes across Central Asia, sea routes through the Indian Ocean, yam stations.
- **Conflict:** victories (Khwarazm, Baghdad, Song) and failures (Ain Jalut, Japan, Java, Đại Việt).

## Maintenance of power and decline

- **Maintenance:** local officials, taxation, tolerance, terror as a deterrent, ideology of heavenly mandate.
- **Decline (Mongols):** four-way division, princely rivalry, over-issue of paper money, ethnic resentment, rebellion, floods, famine and the Black Death.
- **Decline (Angkor):** drought and flood evidence, shift to Phnom Penh, sea-trade routes, Ayutthaya's pressure.
- **Decline (Kamakura):** no new land to reward warriors after the Mongol defence, debts of vassals, rebellion in 1333.

## Perspectives to cite

- The Mongols as destroyers (accounts by those they defeated) and the Mongols as connectors (modern global history). Explain why each view arises.
- Marco Polo as a witness and as a doubtful one.
- Angkor "fell to Siam" and Angkor "changed", with environmental and economic causes.

## Worked example: turning a question into a plan

**Question:** "To what extent were military factors the main reason for the emergence of one Asian kingdom or empire?"

1. Thesis: military factors were essential, but they depended on political unity and on weak enemies.
2. Paragraph one: army organisation, merit, mobility, siege skills.
3. Paragraph two: political factors, including the unity achieved in 1206 and the loyalty of the keshig.
4. Paragraph three: economic and environmental factors, with the climate debate stated honestly as a debate.
5. Counter-argument: the Mongols often used diplomacy and left local rulers in place.
6. Judgement: military power was the immediate tool, but political unity was the precondition.

## Common mistakes

- Telling the story of conquests without answering the question.
- Treating all Mongol rulers as having the same aims.
- Claiming the Mongol empire "never lost".
- Naming a historian with no view attached.
- Ignoring the chosen example in the second half of the essay.

## Self-test

1. In which year was Temüjin proclaimed Chinggis Khan?
2. Name the four Mongol khanates.
3. What was the yam?
4. Which battle stopped Mongol advance into Syria in 1260?
5. Who built Angkor Wat, and who built the Bayon?
6. Give one reason the Kamakura shogunate weakened after 1281.
7. Name one environmental factor in the decline of Angkor.
8. What command term asks for a balanced review of several arguments?

**Answers:** 1. 1206. 2. The Yuan, the Chagatai Khanate, the Ilkhanate and the Golden Horde. 3. The relay system of post stations for messages and officials. 4. Ain Jalut. 5. Suryavarman II built Angkor Wat; Jayavarman VII built the Bayon. 6. Defence brought no new land for rewards, so vassals were in debt and discontent. 7. Drought or heavy monsoon swings that strained the water system. 8. Discuss.

## Frequently asked questions

**Must I study more than one kingdom?**
No. Questions require reference to one kingdom or empire. Know one in depth.

**Can I write about the Mongols in a question about Angkor?**
Only if the question allows comparison. Check that your chosen example answers the exact wording.

**How are Paper 3 answers marked?**
By the markband for evaluation: understanding of the argument, a balanced evaluation of perspectives, and a substantiated judgement.
