## Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Name the Muslim who suggested digging the trench to defend Madinah. **[1]**

**2.** In which year after the Hijrah was the Treaty of Hudaibiyah agreed? **[1]**

**3.** Give **two** terms of the Treaty of Hudaibiyah. **[2]**

**4.** The Prophet (pbuh) entered Makkah in 8 AH.

**(a)** Give **one** thing he did at the Kaaba. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain **one** reason why his treatment of the Makkans was important. **[3]**

**5.** Explain **two** lessons Muslims can learn from the Battle of Uhud. **[4]**

**6.** Explain **two** reasons why the Battle of Hunayn is remembered. **[4]**

## Section B: longer-answer questions

**7.** Explain **two** reasons why the Battle of Badr was important for the Muslims.

**8.** Explain **two** ways in which Aam ul wafood showed that Islam had become established in Arabia.
Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority in your answer. **[6]**

> **Qur'an 3:121-127** (extract)

'Muslim victories in the Prophet's (pbuh) lifetime were won by faith rather than by military strength.'

Examine why this statement is significant for Muslims.

In your answer you should:

- provide supporting evidence and reasoning. **[12]**

## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for explaining it. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

**1.** Salman al-Farsi (RA) [1].

**2.** The sixth year (6 AH, 628 CE) [1]. Accept 628.

**3.** Any two, [1] each: a truce of ten years; the Muslims to return the next year, not that year, and stay three days; a Quraysh man who joined the Muslims without his guardian's permission to be returned; a Muslim who went to the Quraysh not to be returned; other tribes free to ally with either side.

**4. (a)** Any one [1]: he removed the idols around the Kaaba; he recited "Truth has come and falsehood has departed" (Qur'an 17:81); he asked Bilal (RA) to give the call to prayer there.

**(b)** One reason identified [1] and explained [2].

- **Forgiveness** [1]: he gave a general amnesty to the Makkans who had persecuted the Muslims for years [1], which showed mercy from a position of strength and set an example for Muslim leaders [1].
- **Peaceful conquest** [1]: almost no blood was shed [1], so Makkah could become the centre of Islam without lasting bitterness, and many people accepted Islam [1].

**5.** For each of two lessons: lesson identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Obedience** [1]: the archers left the hill against the Prophet's (pbuh) order, and the Muslims lost the advantage [1].
- **Greed for worldly gain** [1]: the wish to collect spoils broke discipline [1].
- **Patience after defeat** [1]: the community recovered and was comforted by the Qur'an (3:139) [1].
- **Consultation** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) accepted the majority view to march out even though he doubted it [1].

**6.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Pride in numbers** [1]: with about 12,000 men some felt sure of winning, and the Qur'an said their number "did not avail" them (9:25) [1].
- **The Prophet's (pbuh) steadiness** [1]: when the Muslims were ambushed and many fled, he stood firm and called them back [1].
- **Victory after the conquest of Makkah** [1]: it ended the last major tribal opposition in the region [1].
- **Mercy afterwards** [1]: captives and property were returned to the Hawazin after they asked [1].

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **First victory** [1]: it showed that a small, poorly equipped force could defeat a larger one [1].
- **Standing in Arabia** [1]: the Muslims were now seen as a power that could not be ignored [1].
- **Morale** [1]: the community gained confidence after years of persecution [1].
- **Justice to captives** [1]: prisoners were treated kindly and literate ones could teach children to read [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- Qur'an 8:41 calls Badr the day of criterion (Furqan), the day that separated truth from falsehood [2].
- Qur'an 3:123: "already had Allah given you victory at [the battle of] Badr while you were weak", so victory depended on Allah [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Ways (AO1):

- **Delegations came to Madinah** [1]: tribes from across Arabia sent representatives, treating Madinah as the capital [1].
- **Tribes accepted Islam or made agreements** [1]: this happened by persuasion, not conquest [1].
- **Security** [1]: after the conquest of Makkah, travel and trade were safer [1].
- **Unity** [1]: tribes that had been rivals shared one faith [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- Qur'an 110:1-2: "when the victory of Allah has come and the conquest, and you see the people entering into the religion of Allah in multitudes" [2].
- Qur'an 49:13: people were made into nations and tribes to know one another, and the most honoured is the most righteous [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** at Badr the Muslims won while few and weak; at Hudaibiyah patience won more than a battle; at Makkah forgiveness changed hearts; the Prophet (pbuh) spent the night before Badr in prayer; the passage says "victory is not except from Allah".
- **Against the statement:** the Prophet (pbuh) planned carefully at Uhud with archers on the hill, and at the Trench with Salman's plan; the Muslims had to organise and fight; Hunayn was nearly lost because the large army relied on numbers; angels were sent to reassure, not to replace effort.
- **Balanced view:** faith and effort work together; the passage joins planning ("posted the believers at their stations"), patience and taqwa with reliance on Allah.

*Use of the passage*

- "Already had Allah given you victory at Badr while you were weak" shows victory did not come from numbers.
- "Then fear Allah; perhaps you will be grateful" links success to taqwa and gratitude.
- "Victory is not except from Allah, the Exalted in Might, the Wise" states where the final result lies.

Level 4 answers use the passage directly, give at least two points on each side with examples from different events, and reach a reasoned judgement.
