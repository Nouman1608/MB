These questions practise overarching theme **OT3 Mathematical modelling** (OT3.1 to OT3.5) of the AQA A-level Mathematics (7357) specification, for first teaching 2017. The models come from exponentials, kinematics, projectiles and the binomial distribution, because OT3 is assessed inside questions on any content. All contexts and data are invented. Use g = 9.8 m s⁻² where needed. A calculator is required.

Learn the methods first in the [OT3 study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-mathematics-7357-ot3-mathematical-modelling/) and the [OT3 revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-mathematics-7357-ot3-mathematical-modelling-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (OT3.5) A parcel slides down a straight ramp. State two modelling assumptions that would allow constant-acceleration equations to be used, and the likely effect of each on the predicted speed at the bottom. **[4]**

**2.** (OT3.2, OT3.3) The value of a car, £V, t years after it is bought new, is modelled by V = 18 000 × 0.85ᵗ.

**(a)** Interpret the number 0.85 in context. **[1]**

**(b)** Use the model to find the value after 4 years. **[1]**

**(c)** Comment on the model's prediction for very large t. **[1]**

**3.** (OT3.2, OT3.3, OT3.5) The height h m of a ball thrown upwards from a balcony is modelled by h = 1.5 + 14t − 4.9t², where t is the time in seconds.

**(a)** Interpret the 1.5 in context. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the time at which the ball reaches the ground. **[2]**

**(c)** State one assumption made in this model. **[1]**

**4.** (OT3.4, OT3.5) A restaurant finds that 30% of diners order dessert. Explain why B(12, 0.3) may not be a good model for the number of people at one table of 12 friends who order dessert. **[2]**

**5.** (OT3.2) The volume of water, V litres, in a tank t minutes after a tap is opened is modelled by V = 450 − 18t. State the values of t for which the model is valid, with a reason. **[2]**

**6.** (OT3.4) A model's predictions are consistently about 15% higher than measured values. Explain what should be done next. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (OT3.1–OT3.4) A bowl of soup is at 85 °C when served in a room at 22 °C. Ten minutes later it is at 50 °C. Its temperature T °C, t minutes after serving, is modelled by T = 22 + Ae^(−kt).

**(a)** Find A and k. **[3]**

**(b)** Use the model to predict the temperature after 25 minutes. **[1]**

**(c)** The measured temperature after 25 minutes is 33 °C. Comment on the model and suggest one refinement. **[2]**

**8.** (OT3.2–OT3.4) The number of fish, P, in a lake t years after stocking is modelled by P = 2000e^(0.05t).

**(a)** Find the time taken for the number of fish to double. **[2]**

**(b)** Use the model to predict P after 50 years. **[1]**

**(c)** Biologists estimate the lake can support at most 10 000 fish. Comment on the model and suggest how it should be refined. **[3]**

**9.** (OT3.1–OT3.5) A football is kicked from horizontal ground with speed 20 m s⁻¹ at 30° above the horizontal. It is modelled as a projectile.

**(a)** Find the time of flight and the horizontal distance travelled before the ball first hits the ground. **[4]**

**(b)** State two assumptions made in this model. **[2]**

**(c)** The ball actually lands 31 m away. Explain why the model overestimates the distance. **[2]**

**10.** (OT3.1, OT3.3, OT3.5) A student guesses every answer in a 10-question multiple-choice quiz with four options per question. X is the number correct.

**(a)** State a suitable model for X and two assumptions needed. **[3]**

**(b)** Find P(X ≥ 5). **[1]**

**(c)** The student can usually rule out one wrong option on each question. Explain how the model should be refined. **[1]**

**11.** (OT3.1, OT3.4) The height of a young tree was measured every two years.

| t (years) | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| h (m) | 1.20 | 1.68 | 2.35 | 3.29 |

**(a)** Show that an exponential model fits better than a linear model. **[2]**

**(b)** The model h = 1.2 × 1.4^(t/2) is proposed. Use it to predict the height at t = 10. **[2]**

**(c)** Use the model to predict the height at t = 40 and comment. **[2]**

**12.** (OT3.1, OT3.5) A tap drips once every 2 seconds, and each drip has volume about 0.05 ml. Build a model to estimate the volume of water wasted in one year, in litres, stating your assumptions. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two assumptions, one mark for each assumption and one for its effect, to a maximum of 4:
- The parcel is a particle; ignores rotation and air flow, a small effect on the speed.
- The ramp is smooth; no friction, so the predicted speed is too high.
- Air resistance is negligible; predicted speed too high.
- The ramp is a straight, rigid plane; the parcel stays in contact throughout.

**2. (a)** The value falls by 15% each year [1].

**(b)** 18 000 × 0.85⁴ = **£9400** (£9396) [1].

**(c)** The value approaches but never reaches zero, which may be reasonable for a car, though eventually it would be worth only its scrap value, so the model is unreliable for very large t [1].

**3. (a)** The ball is thrown from 1.5 m above the ground [1].

**(b)** 4.9t² − 14t − 1.5 = 0 [1]; t = (14 + √225.4)/9.8 = **2.96 s** (rejecting the negative root) [1].

**(c)** Any one: no air resistance; ball is a particle; g constant at 9.8 m s⁻²; ball moves vertically [1].

**4.** The binomial model needs each person's choice to be independent [1], but friends at one table influence each other (for example, sharing desserts or following others), so the choices are not independent / the probability is not the same for each person [1].

**5.** V = 0 when t = 450/18 = 25 [1]; the model is valid for **0 ≤ t ≤ 25**, as the volume cannot be negative [1].

**6.** Identify which assumption or parameter causes the overestimate [1], change it (refine the model), then compare the new predictions with the data again [1].

**7. (a)** At t = 0: 85 = 22 + A, so **A = 63** [1]. At t = 10: 28 = 63e^(−10k) [1], so k = (1/10) ln(63/28) = **0.0811** [1].

**(b)** T = 22 + 63e^(−2.027) = **30.3 °C** [1].

**(c)** The model predicts the soup is cooler than measured, so it cools too quickly later on / k is not constant [1]. Refinement: any one, e.g. use a room temperature that rises near the bowl; refit k using more data; allow for a lid or the bowl's insulation [1].

**8. (a)** e^(0.05t) = 2 [1]; t = ln 2/0.05 = **13.9 years** [1].

**(b)** 2000e^(2.5) = **24 400** fish (3 s.f.) [1].

**(c)** 24 400 exceeds the 10 000 the lake can support, so the model is unrealistic for large t [1]. It assumes unlimited, constant-rate growth, but food and space are limited [1]. Refine so that the growth rate decreases as P approaches 10 000, or use the model only for small t [1].

**9. (a)** Vertical: 0 = (20 sin 30°)t − 4.9t² [1], so t = 10/4.9 = **2.04 s** [1]. Horizontal: x = (20 cos 30°) × 2.04 [1] = 17.32 × 2.041 = **35.3 m** [1].

**(b)** Any two [2]: ball is a particle; no air resistance; no spin; ground is horizontal; g constant.

**(c)** Air resistance acts against the motion [1], reducing the horizontal speed (and the time of flight), so the ball travels less far than the model predicts [1].

**10. (a)** X ~ B(10, 0.25) [1]. Assumptions: each answer is a random guess with probability 0.25 of being correct [1]; answers are independent of each other [1].

**(b)** P(X ≥ 5) = 1 − P(X ≤ 4) = **0.0781** [1].

**(c)** Use p = 1/3 instead of 0.25, i.e. X ~ B(10, 1/3), since the guess is now from three options [1].

**11. (a)** Differences: 0.48, 0.67, 0.94, not constant, so a linear model is poor [1]. Ratios: 1.68/1.20 = 1.40, 2.35/1.68 = 1.40, 3.29/2.35 = 1.40, constant, so an exponential model fits [1].

**(b)** h = 1.2 × 1.4⁵ [1] = **6.45 m** [1].

**(c)** h = 1.2 × 1.4²⁰ = **1000 m** (1004 m) [1]. This is impossible for a tree; growth slows as the tree matures, so the model should only be used for young trees / over a limited range [1].

**12.** Drips per year = (365 × 24 × 3600)/2 = 15 768 000 [1]. Volume = 15 768 000 × 0.05 = 788 400 ml [1] ≈ **790 litres** [1]. Assumptions, any one: the drip rate is constant all year; every drip has the same volume [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Assumptions without context or without their effect on the prediction.
- Interpreting a parameter with no units.
- Using the model far outside the range of the data and accepting the output.
- Refinements that do not say what to change.

## Frequently asked questions

**How are these questions organised?**
Section A has short modelling questions; Section B has full models to build, use, interpret and evaluate. The OT3 statements practised are in brackets at the start of each question.

**What does "comment on the model" require?**
Compare the model's prediction with the evidence given, name the assumption most likely to explain any difference, and say how the model could be refined or where it can safely be used.
