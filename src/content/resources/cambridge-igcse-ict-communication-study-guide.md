Topic 10 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 is about communicating with other people through ICT, using email, and about using the internet well to find information. There is a practical side, because you may be asked to compose and send an email in the practical paper, and a theory side, because you need to explain terms and judge sources. This guide covers both halves, 10.1 on email and 10.2 on effective internet use.

## Where this fits in the specification

Communication links to the audience topic (an email to a manager is written differently from one to a friend), to safety and security (phishing, spam and attachments carry risks) and to networks (how email and web pages travel between computers). The [Network Issues and Communication study guide](/resources/igcse-ict-network-issues-and-communication/) covers the network side of the same ideas.

## 10.1 Communication with other ICT users using email

### The features of an email

An email message has the following parts:

- **To**: the main recipient or recipients.
- **Cc** (carbon copy): other people who should see the message. Everyone can see these addresses.
- **Bcc** (blind carbon copy): recipients whose addresses are hidden from the other recipients. Use Bcc when emailing a large group so that you do not reveal their addresses.
- **Subject**: a short, informative description.
- **Body**: the message itself.
- **Attachment**: a file sent with the email, such as a document or image.
- **Signature**: automatic closing text containing name and contact details.

Other functions you must be able to use or describe include **reply**, **reply all**, **forward**, an **address book** (stored contacts), **groups** (a single name that sends to several addresses), and **folders** for storing messages.

### Netiquette and good practice

**Netiquette** is the set of accepted rules of polite behaviour online. In email:

- Use a meaningful subject line and a suitable greeting and ending.
- Be polite, clear and brief. Do not write in capital letters, which suggests shouting.
- Avoid unexplained abbreviations and slang when writing formally.
- Check spelling and grammar before sending.
- Do not copy people (Cc, reply all) unnecessarily.
- Do not send offensive or private material, or forward chain letters.
- Compress large attachments or share a link instead.
- Do not reveal other people's email addresses.

### Legal and safety points

Employers often have an **acceptable use policy** that limits personal email at work. Email must not be used to harass or threaten, or to send copyrighted material to others without permission. **Spam** is unsolicited junk email, usually sent in bulk. To reduce it, do not publish your email address on websites, do not reply to spam, use a spam filter and give your address only to trusted sites. Do not open attachments or follow links in unexpected emails, because they may contain malware or lead to phishing sites.

### Worked example: choosing To, Cc and Bcc

A head of year, Mr Okoro, must send a trip reminder to 45 parents and wants the deputy head to be informed.

- **To**: himself (or the school office address).
- **Cc**: the deputy head, who needs to see the message and can be seen by everyone.
- **Bcc**: the 45 parents, so that their addresses are not revealed to each other.
- **Subject**: "Reminder: Year 9 museum trip, Thursday 14 May".
- **Attachment**: a permission form saved as a PDF of small size.

Reasoning: using Bcc for parents protects their privacy, so that a reply-all does not spread personal addresses. Using Cc for the deputy head keeps them informed openly. If a question asks for a mistake, sending to all 45 in the To field would show everyone's address.

### Comparing email with other methods

Advantages: fast, cheap, a written record, attachments, can be read at any time. Disadvantages: may be ignored or misunderstood without tone of voice, attachments may carry malware, spam, and the need for equipment and internet access.

## 10.2 Effective use of the internet

The **internet** is the worldwide network of networks; the **World Wide Web** is the collection of web pages accessed through it using a web browser. Web pages have addresses called **URLs** (uniform resource locators). A URL commonly has a protocol (https), a domain name (the website) and a path or file name.

### Searching effectively

A **search engine** finds web pages that match the words you enter. To search well:

- Choose **specific keywords** rather than a whole question.
- Use **quotation marks** to search for an exact phrase.
- Use **AND** to require both terms, **OR** to find either, and **NOT** to exclude a term. For example, `solar AND panels NOT toys` finds pages with both words but not the word toys.
- Use **advanced search** options such as date, file type or language.
- Look beyond the first results and refine the search if the first attempt gives poor results.
- Use **bookmarks** or favourites to save useful pages, and the browser **history** to find pages you have visited.

### Evaluating the reliability of information

Information on the internet is not controlled, so it needs checking. Questions to ask:

- **Who** wrote it, and are they an expert? Is the organisation known and trusted?
- **When** was it published or updated?
- Is the content **biased**, trying to sell something or push an opinion?
- Is it **supported** by evidence and does it agree with other reliable sources?
- Is the website professional, free of obvious errors and with contact details?

Domain endings can give hints (for example, government or educational sites are often trusted) but are not proof. A wiki or a forum can be edited by anyone, and is generally less reliable than a published source with a named expert author.

### Worked example: refine a search

A student wants information about how many hours of sleep teenagers need, but a search for `sleep` returns millions of results about mattresses and songs. A better strategy:

1. Use the keywords `teenagers sleep hours recommended`.
2. Add quotation marks around `"hours of sleep"` for an exact phrase.
3. Add `NOT mattress` to remove irrelevant pages.
4. Check the source of the first result: a national health organisation, last updated this year, with references. This is more reliable than an anonymous blog.

### Advantages and disadvantages of the internet

Advantages: a huge amount of information, quick, up to date and available at any time, with multimedia. Disadvantages: unreliable or biased content, inappropriate material, too much information to sort, and the dangers of malware and fraud. Always answer with both sides if asked to discuss.

## Common mistakes

- Using Cc when the question needs Bcc to hide addresses.
- Writing a subject line that is blank or vague, such as "Hi".
- Saying that Google is the internet. A search engine is one service used on the web.
- Treating the first search result as reliable.
- Saying that .com or .org addresses are always reliable.
- Using AND, OR and NOT incorrectly. AND narrows a search; OR widens it.

## Exam technique

When asked to **describe** a method of improving search results, name the technique and say what it does to the results. For **evaluate the reliability**, give two or three criteria, apply them to the source in the question, and give a judgement. In practical email tasks, check the recipients, subject, attachment and body before sending, and make sure each address is typed exactly as given.

## Check your understanding

1. State the difference between Cc and Bcc. *Cc recipients are visible to all; Bcc recipients are hidden from the others.*
2. Give two rules of netiquette. *Use polite language and a clear subject; do not write in capitals.*
3. What is spam, and give one way to reduce it? *Unsolicited bulk email; use a spam filter or do not publish your address.*
4. What does the search `cats AND dogs` return? *Pages that contain both words.*
5. Write a search to find pages on volcanoes but not about video games. *volcanoes NOT "video games".*
6. Give two checks for the reliability of a website. *Author's expertise and date of publication; or whether it is biased.*
7. Why is a wiki less reliable than a textbook? *Anyone can edit it, so content may not be checked by an expert.*
8. State one disadvantage of email compared with a phone call. *Tone is lost and a reply may be delayed.*
