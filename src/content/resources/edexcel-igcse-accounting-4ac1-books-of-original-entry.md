## What section 2.2 asks you to do

| Point | Specification wording |
|---|---|
| 2.2 (a) | Explain the purpose of books of original entry |
| 2.2 (b) | Prepare: purchases day book, sales day book, purchases returns book, sales returns book, three-column cash book, petty cash book, the journal |

Terms such as contra entry, imprest and narrative are standard bookkeeping practice used to prepare these books; they are not specification wording.

## Purpose: why not post straight to the ledger?

A book of original entry is where a transaction is first recorded, in date order, from its source document. The purposes are:

- **Grouping similar transactions**, so one total can be posted to the ledger instead of hundreds of single entries.
- **A chronological record**, so any transaction can be found by date and document number.
- **Reducing errors**, because the ledger receives fewer, checked totals.
- **Division of work**, since different staff can keep different books.
- **Explaining unusual entries**, through the narrative in the journal.

The cash book is special: it is a book of original entry *and* part of the ledger, because its balances are the cash and bank accounts themselves.

## Choosing the right book

Ask two questions. Is it on credit or for cash? Is it goods for resale, a return, or something else?

### Worked example 1: classifying transactions

Brindle Hardware records these transactions.

| Transaction | Book |
|---|---|
| Sold goods on credit to A. Moreno, 800 | Sales day book |
| Sold goods for cash, 465 | Cash book (cash column) |
| Bought goods for resale on credit from Corran Tools, 1,200 | Purchases day book |
| Returned faulty goods to Corran Tools, 90 | Purchases returns book |
| A. Moreno returned goods, 45 | Sales returns book |
| Bought a delivery van on credit from Kerr Motors, 14,500 | Journal (not goods for resale) |
| Paid rent by bank transfer, 950 | Cash book (bank column) |
| Paid 6.80 bus fare from the float | Petty cash book |
| Owner took goods costing 180 for private use | Journal |
| Wrote off J. Pike's debt of 275 as irrecoverable | Journal |

Two traps appear here. The van is bought on credit, but it is not goods for resale, so it does not belong in the purchases day book. The owner's goods involve no cash and no invoice, so only the journal can record them.

## Posting the day books and returns books

Each line of a day book is posted to a personal account; the total is posted once to a nominal account.

| Book | Each line posted to | Total posted to |
|---|---|---|
| Sales day book | Debit customer's account (receivables ledger) | Credit sales account |
| Sales returns book | Credit customer's account | Debit sales returns account |
| Purchases day book | Credit supplier's account (payables ledger) | Debit purchases account |
| Purchases returns book | Debit supplier's account | Credit purchases returns account |

The day books themselves are not part of the double entry. They are lists from which the double entry is made. Amounts are always net of trade discount.

## The three-column cash book

The columns on each side are discount, cash and bank. The debit side holds receipts; the credit side holds payments. Discount allowed is entered on the debit side beside the customer's receipt; discount received on the credit side beside the supplier's payment. The discount columns are memorandum columns: they are totalled and posted, never balanced.

A **contra entry** appears on both sides of the cash book because money moves between cash and bank. Cash paid into the bank: debit bank column, credit cash column. Cash withdrawn from the bank for office use: debit cash column, credit bank column. Mark both entries C.

### Worked example 2: Brindle Hardware, June 2027

Balances on 1 June: cash 230.00, bank 4,150.00 (debit). Transactions:

- 2 Jun cash sales 465.00
- 4 Jun A. Moreno paid by cheque 784.00 in settlement of 800.00
- 6 Jun paid 500.00 cash into the bank
- 9 Jun paid Corran Tools by cheque 1,176.00 in settlement of 1,200.00
- 11 Jun wages paid in cash 160.00
- 15 Jun cash sales 390.00
- 18 Jun rent paid by bank 950.00
- 22 Jun B. Lister paid by bank transfer 625.00
- 25 Jun withdrew 200.00 from the bank for office cash
- 28 Jun owner's drawings by cheque 400.00
- 29 Jun shelving bought by cheque 1,300.00

```
DEBIT                         Disc       Cash        Bank
1 Jun  Balance b/d                     230.00    4,150.00
2 Jun  Sales                           465.00
4 Jun  A. Moreno             16.00                 784.00
6 Jun  Cash (C)                                    500.00
15 Jun Sales                           390.00
22 Jun B. Lister                                   625.00
25 Jun Bank (C)                        200.00
                             16.00   1,285.00    6,059.00
1 Jul  Balance b/d                     625.00    2,033.00

CREDIT                        Disc       Cash        Bank
6 Jun  Bank (C)                        500.00
9 Jun  Corran Tools          24.00               1,176.00
11 Jun Wages                           160.00
18 Jun Rent                                        950.00
25 Jun Cash (C)                                    200.00
28 Jun Drawings                                    400.00
29 Jun Shelving                                  1,300.00
30 Jun Balance c/d                     625.00    2,033.00
                             24.00   1,285.00    6,059.00
```

Working: Moreno's discount is 800.00 − 784.00 = 16.00, and Corran's is 1,200.00 − 1,176.00 = 24.00. Cash: 1,285.00 − (500.00 + 160.00) = 625.00. Bank: 6,059.00 − (1,176.00 + 950.00 + 200.00 + 400.00 + 1,300.00) = 2,033.00.

Posting: A. Moreno is credited with 784.00 and with 16.00 discount, clearing 800.00. Corran Tools is debited with 1,176.00 and 24.00. The discount allowed total of 16.00 is debited to the discount allowed account; the discount received total of 24.00 is credited to the discount received account.

## The petty cash book

Under the imprest system the petty cashier starts with a fixed float. At the end of the period the main cashier pays in exactly the amount spent, restoring the float. Analysis columns split each payment by type, and each column total is posted to an expense account.

### Worked example 3: float 150.00

```
Rec'd   Date    Details          Vch   Total   Postage Cleaning Travel Stationery
150.00  1 Jun   Bank
        2 Jun   Stamps           61    18.40   18.40
        5 Jun   Cleaning items   62    23.75           23.75
        8 Jun   Bus fares        63     6.80                    6.80
        12 Jun  Printer paper    64    15.90                           15.90
        16 Jun  Taxi             65    21.50                   21.50
        20 Jun  Window cleaner   66    30.00           30.00
        25 Jun  Parcel postage   67    12.35   12.35
                                      128.70   30.75   53.75   28.30   15.90
128.70  30 Jun  Bank
        30 Jun  Balance c/d           150.00
```

Cross-check: 30.75 + 53.75 + 28.30 + 15.90 = 128.70. Before reimbursement the cashier holds 150.00 − 128.70 = 21.30; the 128.70 paid in restores 150.00. The 128.70 also appears on the credit side of the cash book (bank column).

## The journal

The journal records anything that does not fit another book. Each entry has a date, the account debited (first, at the margin), the account credited (indented), the amounts and a narrative.

### Worked example 4: four journal entries

```
                                              Debit      Credit
1 Jan   Premises                           60,000.00
        Equipment                           8,500.00
        Inventory                           4,200.00
        Bank                                3,300.00
            Loan from Northway Bank                    20,000.00
            Capital                                    56,000.00
        Opening assets, liabilities and capital

14 Mar  Delivery van                       14,500.00
            Kerr Motors                                14,500.00
        Van bought on credit, invoice KM-208

20 Apr  Drawings                              180.00
            Purchases                                     180.00
        Goods taken by owner for private use

30 Jun  Irrecoverable debts                   275.00
            J. Pike                                       275.00
        Debt written off, customer untraceable
```

In the opening entry, capital is the balancing figure: assets of 76,000.00 less the loan of 20,000.00 gives 56,000.00. Goods taken by the owner are credited to purchases because they were bought for resale and will not be sold.

## Exam technique

- **Explain the purpose**: give the reason and its effect, such as "totals are posted, so the ledger has fewer entries and fewer errors".
- **Prepare**: rule the correct columns, date every line, use the other account's name as the detail, total and balance where required, and bring balances down.
- **Narratives** in the journal are expected; a missing narrative is an easy mark lost.

## Common errors

- Recording a non-current asset bought on credit in the purchases day book.
- Recording cash sales or cash purchases in a day book.
- Balancing the discount columns or adding them to cash or bank.
- Entering a contra on one side only, or in the wrong direction.
- Reimbursing the float by a different amount from the total spent.
- Crediting capital with the value of assets alone in an opening entry, forgetting liabilities.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the cash book part of the ledger?**
Yes. It is a book of original entry and also holds the cash and bank accounts, so its balances go directly into the trial balance.

**Why are discount columns in the cash book not balanced?**
They are memorandum columns that collect discount allowed and discount received. Their totals are posted to the discount accounts in the ledger instead.

**Which book records goods taken by the owner?**
The journal: debit drawings and credit purchases, because no cash or invoice is involved.
