## The four gas tests

| Gas | Test | Positive result |
|---|---|---|
| **Hydrogen**, H₂ | **Burning splint** held at the open end of a test tube of the gas | Burns rapidly with a **pop** (squeaky pop) |
| **Oxygen**, O₂ | **Glowing splint** inserted into a test tube of the gas | Splint **relights** |
| **Carbon dioxide**, CO₂ | **Limewater** (aqueous calcium hydroxide), bubbled through or shaken with the gas | Limewater turns **milky (cloudy)** |
| **Chlorine**, Cl₂ | **Damp litmus paper** put into the gas | Paper is **bleached** and turns **white** |

## Remember

- Hydrogen: **lit** splint → **pop**.
- Oxygen: **glowing** splint → **relights**.
- Carbon dioxide: **limewater** → **milky**.
- Chlorine: **damp litmus** → **bleached white**.

## Equations behind the tests

| Test | Equation |
|---|---|
| Hydrogen burning | 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O |
| Limewater with carbon dioxide | Ca(OH)₂(aq) + CO₂(g) → CaCO₃(s) + H₂O(l) |
| Oxygen from hydrogen peroxide | 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂ |

The milky colour is **insoluble calcium carbonate**.

## Where each gas is made

| Gas | Common source in the course |
|---|---|
| Hydrogen | Metal + dilute acid; cathode in aqueous electrolysis of reactive metal compounds |
| Oxygen | Decomposition of hydrogen peroxide; anode in electrolysis of aqueous solutions without halides |
| Carbon dioxide | Carbonate + acid; combustion of hydrocarbons; fermentation |
| Chlorine | Anode in electrolysis of molten chlorides or aqueous chloride solutions |

## Identify the gas

| Observation | Gas |
|---|---|
| Lit splint, squeaky pop | Hydrogen |
| Glowing splint relights | Oxygen |
| Limewater turns milky | Carbon dioxide |
| Damp litmus bleached | Chlorine |
| Lit splint goes out | Not one of the tests (carbon dioxide, nitrogen and others put out a flame) |

A splint going out is not a test in the specification: use limewater to identify carbon dioxide.

## Safety notes

- **Chlorine** is toxic: use small quantities, in a fume cupboard or well-ventilated room.
- **Hydrogen** is flammable: keep flames away except during the test.
- Wear **eye protection** with acids, bromine water and limewater.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Limewater | Aqueous solution of calcium hydroxide |
| Glowing splint | Splint lit then blown out so it still glows |
| Positive result | The observation that confirms the gas |
| Bleached | Has lost its colour |
| Precipitate | Insoluble solid formed in a solution (the milkiness in limewater) |

## Exam traps

- **Wrong splint.** Lit for hydrogen, glowing for oxygen.
- **"Lights" for oxygen.** The glowing splint relights.
- **"Goes white" or "clear" for limewater.** It goes milky or cloudy.
- **Dry litmus for chlorine.** The paper must be damp.
- **Result without the test.** Give both.
- **"Explodes".** The expected word for hydrogen is pop.
- **Naming the wrong reagent.** Limewater is calcium hydroxide solution, not calcium carbonate.

## Quick self-test

1. State the test and result for hydrogen.
2. State the test and result for oxygen.
3. What is limewater?
4. State the positive result for carbon dioxide.
5. What causes the milky colour?
6. State the test for chlorine.
7. Why must the litmus paper be damp?
8. A gas relights a glowing splint. Name it.
9. Name the gas made when magnesium reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid, and how you would test it.
10. A solid carbonate is heated and the gas is tested. What would you see if the gas were carbon dioxide?
11. Write the word equation for the reaction of carbon dioxide with limewater.
12. Why is chlorine tested in a fume cupboard?

### Answers

1. Burning splint at the open end of the tube; the gas burns rapidly with a pop.
2. Glowing splint put in the gas; it relights.
3. An aqueous solution of calcium hydroxide.
4. Limewater turns milky (cloudy).
5. Insoluble calcium carbonate.
6. Damp litmus paper; it is bleached white.
7. Chlorine reacts with water on the paper to bleach it (it does not bleach dry paper well).
8. Oxygen.
9. Hydrogen; a lit splint gives a squeaky pop.
10. Limewater turns milky.
11. Calcium hydroxide + carbon dioxide → calcium carbonate + water.
12. Chlorine is toxic.
