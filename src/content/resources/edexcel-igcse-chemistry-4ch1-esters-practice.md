These questions cover sub-topic 4(g) Esters of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024): points 4.38C to 4.43C. Every point has a C reference, so **all of these questions are Paper 2 only**. Use Ar values: H = 1, C = 12, O = 16. Draw structures on paper, showing every bond in displayed formulae. Revise with the [esters study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-esters/) and the [esters revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-esters-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** (Paper 2 only) Name the functional group in an ester. **[1]**

**2.** (Paper 2 only) Name the ester CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃. **[1]**

**3.** (Paper 2 only) Name the ester made from propan-1-ol and methanoic acid. **[1]**

**4.** (Paper 2 only) Write the structural formula of methyl ethanoate. **[1]**

**5.** (Paper 2 only) State what is meant by saying that esters are volatile. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** (Paper 2 only) Draw the displayed formula of ethyl ethanoate. **[2]**

**7.** (Paper 2 only) Ethyl ethanoate is made from ethanol and ethanoic acid.

**(a)** Write the balanced equation, using the symbol for a reversible reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the catalyst. **[1]**
**(c)** State the name of the type of reaction. **[1]**

**8.** (Paper 2 only) An ester has the structural formula CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃.

**(a)** Name the ester. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the carboxylic acid and the alcohol it is made from. **[2]**

**9.** (Paper 2 only) 6.0 g of ethanoic acid (Mr = 60) reacts with excess ethanol to form ethyl ethanoate (Mr = 88). The mass of ester obtained is 6.16 g.

**(a)** Calculate the moles of ethanoic acid. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of ethyl ethanoate. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage yield. **[1]**
**(d)** Give two reasons why the yield is less than 100%. **[2]**

**10.** (Paper 2 only) A student prepares ethyl ethanoate in a test tube.

**(a)** Describe the method, including the reagents. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why the mixture is heated in a water bath rather than over a flame. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the mixture is poured into sodium carbonate solution. **[1]**
**(d)** Describe how the student can tell that an ester has formed. **[1]**

**11.** (Paper 2 only) Give two uses of esters and one property that makes them suitable for these uses. **[3]**

**12.** (Paper 2 only) Methyl propanoate and ethyl ethanoate are isomers.

**(a)** Give the molecular formula of each. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the structural formula of each. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why they are isomers. **[1]**

**13.** (Paper 2 only) Two esters have the molecular formula C₃H₆O₂.

**(a)** Give the structural formula and name of each. **[2]**
**(b)** State the acid and the alcohol that form each. **[1]**

**14.** (Paper 2 only) A chemist needs 8.8 g of ethyl ethanoate (Mr = 88). Calculate the masses of ethanol (Mr = 46) and ethanoic acid (Mr = 60) needed if the reaction were 100% efficient. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** -COO- (the ester link) [1].

**2.** Propyl ethanoate [1].

**3.** Propyl methanoate [1].

**4.** CH₃COOCH₃ [1].

**5.** They evaporate easily at room temperature (they have low boiling points) [1].

**6.** A CH₃ group bonded to a carbon that has a double bond to one oxygen [1], and a single bond to a second oxygen, which is bonded to a CH₂CH₃ group; all bonds drawn and every atom with the right number of bonds [1].

**7. (a)** CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂OH ⇌ CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ [1] + H₂O [1].
**(b)** Concentrated sulfuric acid [1].
**(c)** Esterification [1].

**8. (a)** Ethyl propanoate [1].
**(b)** Propanoic acid [1] and ethanol [1].

**9. (a)** 6.0 / 60 = 0.10 mol [1].
**(b)** Moles of ester = 0.10 mol (1 : 1) [1]; mass = 0.10 x 88 = 8.8 g [1].
**(c)** 6.16 / 8.8 x 100 = 70% [1].
**(d)** The reaction is reversible, so it does not go to completion [1]; some product is lost when separating it (for example on glassware or by evaporation) [1].

**10. (a)** Mix ethanol and ethanoic acid in a test tube [1]. Add a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid [1]. Warm in a hot water bath for several minutes [1]. Pour the mixture into sodium carbonate solution in a beaker [1].
**(b)** Ethanol and the ester are flammable, so a naked flame could ignite the vapour [1].
**(c)** To neutralise (remove) the unreacted acid, shown by fizzing, so the smell of the ester can be detected [1].
**(d)** A sweet, fruity smell and an oily layer on the surface [1].

**11.** Uses: food flavourings [1]; perfumes (or cosmetics, solvents) [1]. Property: they have distinctive, pleasant smells and are volatile, so the smell reaches the nose [1].

**12. (a)** Both are C₄H₈O₂ [1].
**(b)** Methyl propanoate: CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ [1]. Ethyl ethanoate: CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ [1].
**(c)** They have the same molecular formula but different structural formulae [1].

**13. (a)** CH₃COOCH₃, methyl ethanoate [1]; HCOOCH₂CH₃, ethyl methanoate [1].
**(b)** Methyl ethanoate: ethanoic acid and methanol; ethyl methanoate: methanoic acid and ethanol [1].

**14.** Moles of ester = 8.8 / 88 = 0.10 mol [1]. The ratio is 1 : 1 : 1, so 0.10 mol each of ethanol and ethanoic acid are needed [1]. Mass of ethanol = 0.10 x 46 = 4.6 g; mass of ethanoic acid = 0.10 x 60 = 6.0 g [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing the alcohol and acid parts of an ester name.
- Forgetting water in the equation.
- Writing a base catalyst or leaving the catalyst out.
- Heating over a naked flame in the description of the practical.
- Writing the structural formula so that the carbon count is wrong.
- Giving "human error" as a reason for the loss of yield instead of the reversible reaction.
