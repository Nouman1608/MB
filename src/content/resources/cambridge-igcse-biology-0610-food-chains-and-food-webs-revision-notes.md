These notes condense sub-topic **19.2 Food chains and food webs** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 14 are Core. Outcomes 15 to 19 (pyramids of energy and energy efficiency) are **Extended only**.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [food chains and food webs study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-food-chains-and-food-webs/) first, then test yourself with the [food chains and food webs practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-food-chains-and-food-webs-practice/). The [organisms and their environment revision notes](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-organisms-and-their-environment-revision-notes/) cover the rest of topic 19.

## Core recall: the descriptions

- **Food chain:** shows the transfer of energy from one organism to the next, beginning with a producer.
- **Food web:** a network of interconnected food chains.
- **Producer:** makes its own organic nutrients, usually using energy from sunlight, through photosynthesis.
- **Consumer:** gets its energy by feeding on other organisms.
- **Herbivore:** an animal that gets its energy by eating plants.
- **Carnivore:** an animal that gets its energy by eating other animals.
- **Decomposer:** gets its energy from dead or waste organic material.
- **Trophic level:** the position of an organism in a food chain, food web or ecological pyramid.

## Core recall: trophic levels

| Level | Name | Example (rocky shore) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | producer | seaweed |
| 2 | primary consumer | periwinkle |
| 3 | secondary consumer | crab |
| 4 | tertiary consumer | gull |
| 5 | quaternary consumer | bird of prey |

Arrows point **towards the eater**: the direction of energy transfer. A chain never starts with the Sun.

## Core recall: interpreting webs

For a change in one population, check:

- its **predators** (they lose food and may decrease, or switch to other prey);
- its **prey** (eaten less, may increase);
- its **competitors** (more or less food for them).

Always write **direction + reason**.

**Human impact:**

- **Overharvesting** a food species → its predators lose food and may decrease; its prey may increase.
- **Introducing a foreign species** → may eat natives, compete with them, or lack predators, so it spreads rapidly and native populations fall.

## Core recall: pyramids

| Pyramid | Shows | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers | number of organisms at each level | can be inverted (one tree, many insects; one host, many parasites) |
| Biomass | dry mass of living material at each level | almost always a true pyramid |

**Drawing:** horizontal bars, producer at the bottom, width to scale, each bar labelled.

**Advantages of biomass over numbers:** allows for the size of organisms; gives a truer picture of how much living material each level supports; rarely inverted.

**Limitations of biomass:** organisms must be killed and dried; it shows the mass at one moment only.

## Extended recall

- **Pyramid of energy:** energy transferred into each level over time (kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹).
- **Advantages:** never inverted; shows energy over a period, not a snapshot; allows efficiencies to be compared.
- **Efficiency = energy in higher level ÷ energy in lower level × 100.**
- **Why transfer is inefficient:** uneaten parts; undigested food lost in faeces; excretory products such as urea; energy released in respiration and lost as heat (movement, keeping warm).
- **Fewer than five levels:** energy falls rapidly at each transfer, so too little remains to support a population at a fifth level.
- **Crops vs livestock:** eating crops = one transfer; eating livestock fed on crops = two transfers with heat loss by the animals, so more energy reaches humans per area if they eat the crop.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Food chain | Transfer of energy from one organism to the next, beginning with a producer |
| Food web | Network of interconnected food chains |
| Producer | Makes its own organic nutrients by photosynthesis |
| Consumer | Gets energy by feeding on other organisms |
| Decomposer | Gets energy from dead or waste organic material |
| Trophic level | Position of an organism in a food chain, web or ecological pyramid |
| Overharvesting | Removing a food species faster than it can reproduce |
| Foreign (introduced) species | A species brought into a habitat where it did not live before |
| Biomass | Dry mass of living material |
| Pyramid of energy (Extended) | Energy transferred into each trophic level over time |

## Exam traps

- **Arrow direction.** Prey → predator, never the reverse.
- **The Sun in a chain.** Chains start with a producer.
- **No reason.** "The voles decrease" earns nothing without "because foxes eat more of them".
- **Two levels.** Check each chain separately; an omnivore or general predator may occupy two levels.
- **Inverted pyramids.** Only pyramids of numbers are commonly inverted.
- **Fresh mass.** Biomass pyramids use dry mass.
- **(Extended) Efficiency upside down.** Divide by the lower trophic level.
- **(Extended) "Energy used up".** Say energy is transferred to the environment as heat.
- Drawing arrows the wrong way.
- Stating web changes without reasons.
- Drawing pyramid bars not to scale or unlabelled.
- Forgetting a limitation in a "discuss" question.
- (Extended) Dividing by the wrong value in an efficiency calculation.

## Self-test

1. Describe a food chain.
2. Which way do arrows point in a food chain, and what do they show?
3. Describe a decomposer.
4. Name the trophic level of a carnivore that eats a herbivore.
5. In a web, foxes eat rabbits and voles. Rabbits die out. Suggest the effect on voles and explain.
6. Give two ways in which an introduced species can reduce a native population.
7. One rose bush supports 900 aphids, which are eaten by 15 ladybirds. Describe the shape of the pyramid of numbers.
8. Give two advantages of a pyramid of biomass over a pyramid of numbers.
9. (Extended) State why a pyramid of energy is never inverted.
10. (Extended) Producers transfer 25 000 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹; herbivores gain 2 000 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹. Calculate the efficiency.
11. (Extended) Give three reasons why energy transfer between trophic levels is inefficient.
12. (Extended) Explain why eating crops is more energy efficient than eating livestock fed on crops.

### Answers

1. It shows the transfer of energy from one organism to the next, beginning with a producer.
2. From the organism eaten to the organism that eats it; the direction of energy transfer.
3. An organism that gets its energy from dead or waste organic material.
4. Secondary consumer.
5. Voles decrease, because foxes eat more voles when there are no rabbits.
6. Any two: eats the native species; competes with it for food; spreads disease; has no predators so grows in number quickly.
7. Narrow base (one bush), very wide middle (aphids), narrow top (ladybirds): not a true pyramid.
8. Allows for the size of organisms; almost always pyramid-shaped / gives a truer picture of living material at each level.
9. Energy is lost at every transfer, so each level always has less energy than the one below.
10. 2 000 ÷ 25 000 × 100 = 8%.
11. Any three: parts not eaten; food not digested, lost in faeces; energy in excretory products such as urea; energy released by respiration lost as heat.
12. Eating crops involves one transfer; feeding crops to livestock adds a second transfer in which the animals lose energy as heat in respiration and movement, so less energy reaches humans.
