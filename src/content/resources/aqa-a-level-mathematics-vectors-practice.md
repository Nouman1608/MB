These questions cover **Section J: Vectors (J1 to J5)** of the AQA A-level Mathematics (7357) specification, version 1.3 (31 January 2018), for A-level exams from June 2018 onwards. The specification assesses Section J on Paper 2. A calculator is required in every 7357 paper, but "show that" parts need every step written out. Give answers to 3 significant figures unless a question asks for an exact value.

## Questions

**1.** a = 8i − 15j. Find |a| and the unit vector in the direction of a. **[2]**

**2.** v = −6i + 6√3 j. Find the magnitude of v and the angle v makes with the positive x-direction, measured anticlockwise. **[3]**

**3.** A force of magnitude 18 N acts at 210° anticlockwise from i. Write the force in the form (pi + qj) N, giving p and q to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**4.** p = 4i − 4j + 7k and q = i + 2j − 2k.

**(a)** Find |p|. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the unit vector in the direction of q. **[2]**
**(c)** Find |2p − 3q|, giving your answer to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**5.** The vectors a = 3i − 2j and b = (t + 2)i + (t − 8)j are parallel. Find the value of t and express b in terms of a. **[3]**

**6.** The points A and B have coordinates (2, 1, −3) and (8, −2, 3).

**(a)** Find →AB and the distance AB. **[3]**
**(b)** C lies on AB with AC : CB = 2 : 1. Find the position vector of C. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the exact distance of C from the origin O. **[1]**

**7.** OABC is a parallelogram with →OA = a and →OC = c. M is the midpoint of AB. P lies on the diagonal OB with OP : PB = 2 : 1.

**(a)** Show that C, P and M are collinear. **[5]**
**(b)** State the ratio CP : PM. **[1]**

**8.** In triangle OAB, →OA = a and →OB = b, where a and b are not parallel. C is the midpoint of OA. D lies on AB with AD : DB = 1 : 3. The lines OD and BC meet at X.

**(a)** Find →OD in terms of a and b. **[2]**
**(b)** Find →OX in terms of a and b, and find the ratio OX : XD. **[5]**

**9.** Forces F₁ = (6i − j) N and F₂ = (−2i + 4j) N act on a particle.

**(a)** A third force F₃ is added so that the particle is in equilibrium. Find F₃. **[2]**
**(b)** Instead, the third force is (−i + qj) N, and the resultant of the three forces has magnitude 5 N. Find the possible values of q. **[4]**
**(c)** Take q = 1. The particle has mass 0.5 kg. Find its acceleration as a vector, the magnitude of the acceleration, and the angle the acceleration makes with i. **[3]**

**10.** Unit vectors i and j point east and north. Distances are in km and t is the time in hours after 12:00. At 12:00 ship A is at (3i + 10j) and ship B is at (−5i + j). A moves with constant velocity (4i − 2j) km h⁻¹ and B with constant velocity (8i + 2j) km h⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the speed of A. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down r_A and r_B, and show that the vector from A to B at time t is (4t − 8)i + (4t − 9)j. **[3]**
**(c)** Show that at 14:00 ship B is due south of ship A, and state how far apart they are. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the time at which the ships are closest together and the least distance between them. **[3]**

**11.** The points A, B and C have position vectors a = i + 2k, b = 3i + 4j − 2k and c = 7i + 4j.

**(a)** Find →AB, →BC and →AC, and their exact magnitudes. **[4]**
**(b)** Show that angle ABC is a right angle. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the exact area of triangle ABC. **[2]**
**(d)** D is the point such that ABCD is a parallelogram. Find the position vector of D. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** |a| = √(64 + 225) = √289 = **17** [1]
â = **(8i − 15j)/17** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* the unit vector must be divided by the magnitude in both components; dividing only one component loses the accuracy mark.

**2.** |v| = √(36 + 108) = √144 = **12** [1]
Acute angle with the x-axis: tan α = 6√3/6 = √3, so α = 60° [1]
v points left and up (second quadrant), so the angle is 180° − 60° = **120°** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* a calculator's arctan(−√3) gives −60°; without a sketch or quadrant statement, that answer usually earns the method mark only.

**3.** Components 18 cos 210° and 18 sin 210° [1]
= −9√3 and −9, so the force is **(−15.6i − 9j) N** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* both components are negative because 210° is in the third quadrant; a sign error on either one loses the accuracy mark.

**4. (a)** |p| = √(16 + 16 + 49) = √81 = **9** [1]
**(b)** |q| = √(1 + 4 + 4) = 3 [1]
q̂ = **(i + 2j − 2k)/3** [1]
**(c)** 2p − 3q = (8 − 3)i + (−8 − 6)j + (14 + 6)k = 5i − 14j + 20k [1]
|2p − 3q| = √(25 + 196 + 400) = √621 [1]
= **24.9** (3 s.f.) [1]
*Examiner insight:* find the vector 2p − 3q first; 2|p| − 3|q| is a different quantity and earns nothing in (c).

**5.** Parallel means b = λa, so (t + 2)/3 = (t − 8)/(−2) [1]
−2(t + 2) = 3(t − 8), so −2t − 4 = 3t − 24 [1]
**t = 4**, giving b = 6i − 4j = **2a** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* cross-multiplying with the negative denominator is where the sign usually goes wrong; checking b = 2a confirms the answer.

**6. (a)** →AB = b − a [1]
= **6i − 3j + 6k** [1]
AB = √(36 + 9 + 36) = √81 = **9** [1]
**(b)** c = a + (2/3)→AB [1]
= (2i + j − 3k) + (4i − 2j + 4k) = **6i − j + k** [1]
**(c)** OC = √(36 + 1 + 1) = **√38** [1]
*Examiner insight:* an answer of a − b in (a) can still earn follow-through credit for the distance, but the vector itself is marked wrong.

**7. (a)** →OB = a + c [1]
→AB = →OC = c, so →OM = a + (1/2)c [1]
→CM = →OM − →OC = a − (1/2)c [1]
→OP = (2/3)(a + c), so →CP = (2/3)a + (2/3)c − c = (2/3)a − (1/3)c [1]
→CP = (2/3)(a − (1/2)c) = (2/3)→CM. The vectors are parallel and share the point C, so C, P and M are collinear [1]
**(b)** **CP : PM = 2 : 1** [1]
*Examiner insight:* this is a "show that", so the final mark depends on saying both "parallel" and "common point C"; a scalar multiple with no conclusion is incomplete.

**8. (a)** →OD = a + (1/4)(b − a) [1]
= **(3/4)a + (1/4)b** [1]
**(b)** X on OD: →OX = λ((3/4)a + (1/4)b) [1]
X on BC: →OX = b + μ((1/2)a − b) = (μ/2)a + (1 − μ)b [1]
a and b are not parallel, so equate coefficients: (3/4)λ = μ/2 and (1/4)λ = 1 − μ [1]
Solving: λ = 4/7, μ = 6/7 [1]
→OX = **(3/7)a + (1/7)b**, and OX = (4/7)OD, so **OX : XD = 4 : 3** [1]
*Examiner insight:* using the same letter for both parameters makes the equations unsolvable; you need two different scalars, one for each line.

**9. (a)** F₁ + F₂ + F₃ = 0 [1]
F₃ = −(4i + 3j) = **(−4i − 3j) N** [1]
**(b)** R = (6 − 2 − 1)i + (−1 + 4 + q)j = 3i + (3 + q)j [1]
9 + (3 + q)² = 25 [1]
(3 + q)² = 16, so 3 + q = ±4 [1]
**q = 1 or q = −7** [1]
**(c)** R = 3i + 4j, so a = R/0.5 = **(6i + 8j) m s⁻²** [1]
|a| = √(36 + 64) = **10 m s⁻²** [1]
tan θ = 8/6, so θ = **53.1°** above i [1]
*Examiner insight:* in (b) the negative square root gives a second valid answer; leaving out q = −7 loses the final mark.

**10. (a)** speed = |4i − 2j| = √(16 + 4) = √20 [1]
= **4.47 km h⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** r_A = (3 + 4t)i + (10 − 2t)j [1]
r_B = (−5 + 8t)i + (1 + 2t)j [1]
r_B − r_A = (−8 + 4t)i + (−9 + 4t)j = **(4t − 8)i + (4t − 9)j** [1]
**(c)** At t = 2: r_B − r_A = 0i − 1j [1]
The east component is 0 and the north component is negative, so B is **due south of A, 1 km away** [1]
**(d)** d² = (4t − 8)² + (4t − 9)² = 32t² − 136t + 145; d(d²)/dt = 64t − 136 = 0, so t = 17/8 [1]
d² = 32(17/8)² − 136(17/8) + 145 = 0.5, so d = √0.5 = **0.707 km** [1]
t = 2.125 h after noon, which is **14:07:30** [1]
*Examiner insight:* in (b) the answer is given, so writing r_A − r_B and then "fixing" the signs will not earn the final mark; the subtraction must be in the right order.

**11. (a)** →AB = 2i + 4j − 4k, |→AB| = √36 = **6** [1]
→BC = 4i + 0j + 2k, |→BC| = √20 = **2√5** [1]
→AC = 6i + 4j − 2k [1]
|→AC| = √56 = **2√14** [1]
**(b)** AB² + BC² = 36 + 20 = 56 [1]
= AC², so by the converse of Pythagoras **angle ABC = 90°** [1]
**(c)** Area = (1/2)(6)(2√5) [1]
= **6√5** [1]
**(d)** For ABCD, →AD = →BC, so d = a + c − b [1]
= (1 + 7 − 3)i + (0 + 4 − 4)j + (2 + 0 + 2)k = **5i + 4k** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "exact" means leaving √20 as 2√5 and the area as 6√5; a decimal such as 13.4 alone does not earn the accuracy mark in (c).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing →AB as a − b. It is b − a: end minus start.
- Taking an angle straight from arctan without a sketch, so a second- or third-quadrant direction comes out wrong.
- Calculating 2|p| − 3|q| when the question wants |2p − 3q|.
- Showing two vectors are parallel but not naming the shared point before saying "collinear".
- Using one parameter for two different lines when finding where they meet.
- Dropping the negative root when an unknown appears inside a magnitude.
- Writing the speed when the question asks for velocity, or leaving out units (N, m s⁻², km h⁻¹).
- In "show that" parts, skipping the line that turns a scalar multiple into a conclusion.
- Rounding decimals early in multi-step distance or time questions, so the final answer drifts.
