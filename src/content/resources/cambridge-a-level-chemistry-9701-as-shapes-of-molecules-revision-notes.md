**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **AS Level**, subtopic 3.5 Shapes of molecules.

## What you must be able to do

1. **State and explain** the shapes and bond angles of BF₃, CO₂, CH₄, NH₃, H₂O, SF₆ and PF₅ using VSEPR theory.
2. **Predict** the shapes and bond angles of **analogous** molecules and ions.

## VSEPR in brief

- Electron pairs around the central atom **repel** and get **as far apart as possible**.
- Repulsion order: **lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bonding pair > bonding pair–bonding pair**.
- Each lone pair closes the remaining bond angles by about **2 to 3°**.
- A **double or triple bond counts as one region**.
- The **shape name** describes the positions of the **atoms only**; lone pairs affect it but are not part of it.

## The counting rule

For a central atom with single bonds to H or halogens:

    lone pairs = (outer electrons of central atom − bonds ± charge) ÷ 2
                 (+1 electron per negative charge, −1 per positive charge)

For central atoms with double bonds: subtract **2** electrons per double bond, **1** per single bond, then halve what is left.

## The seven named shapes

| Bonded atoms | Lone pairs | Shape | Angle(s) | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0 | linear | 180° | CO₂ |
| 3 | 0 | trigonal planar | 120° | BF₃ |
| 4 | 0 | tetrahedral | 109.5° | CH₄ |
| 3 | 1 | pyramidal | 107° | NH₃ |
| 2 | 2 | non-linear | 104.5° | H₂O |
| 5 | 0 | trigonal bipyramidal | 120° and 90° | PF₅ |
| 6 | 0 | octahedral | 90° | SF₆ |

## Analogous species to know how to predict

| Species | Count | Analogous to | Shape and angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| NH₄⁺ | (5 − 4 − 1)/2 = 0 lp, 4 bonds | CH₄ | tetrahedral, 109.5° |
| PCl₄⁺ | (5 − 4 − 1)/2 = 0 lp | CH₄ | tetrahedral, 109.5° |
| SiCl₄, CCl₄ | 0 lp, 4 bonds | CH₄ | tetrahedral, 109.5° |
| H₃O⁺ | (6 − 3 − 1)/2 = 1 lp | NH₃ | pyramidal, about 107° |
| NH₂⁻ | (5 − 2 + 1)/2 = 2 lp | H₂O | non-linear, about 104.5° |
| BCl₃ | (3 − 3)/2 = 0 lp | BF₃ | trigonal planar, 120° |
| CO₃²⁻, NO₃⁻ | 3 regions, 0 lp on central atom | BF₃ | trigonal planar, 120° |
| BeCl₂ (gas) | (2 − 2)/2 = 0 lp | CO₂ | linear, 180° |
| HCN | C uses 1 + 3 = 4 electrons, 0 lp | CO₂ | linear, 180° |
| PCl₅ | (5 − 5)/2 = 0 lp | PF₅ | trigonal bipyramidal, 120° and 90° |
| PF₆⁻ | (5 − 6 + 1)/2 = 0 lp | SF₆ | octahedral, 90° |
| SO₂ | S uses 4 of 6 electrons, 1 lp, 3 regions | — | non-linear, slightly less than 120° |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| VSEPR | Valence shell electron pair repulsion: pairs around a central atom repel and arrange to minimise repulsion |
| Bonding pair | Shared pair of electrons in a bond to the central atom |
| Lone pair | Pair of outer electrons on the central atom not used in bonding |
| Region of electron density | A bonding position (single, double or triple bond) or a lone pair |
| Equatorial / axial | In PF₅, the three positions in the central triangle / the two positions above and below it |

## Explaining bond angle differences

**CH₄ → NH₃ → H₂O (109.5° → 107° → 104.5°):** all have four electron pairs; replacing bonding pairs with lone pairs adds stronger repulsion, which pushes the remaining bonding pairs closer together.

Write the explanation in three steps: same number of pairs; lone pairs repel more than bonding pairs; bonds pushed closer, so smaller angle.

## Exam traps

- NH₃ is **pyramidal**, not tetrahedral; H₂O is **non-linear**.
- CO₂ has **two regions**, not four.
- Always **adjust for the charge** on an ion.
- PF₅ needs **two** angles: 120° and 90°.
- "Lone pairs are bigger" is weak; write that lone pairs **repel more strongly**.
- In "explain" questions, give the **number of bonding and lone pairs**, not just the shape.
- Drawings need wedges and dashes for 3D shapes, and a labelled angle.

## Self-test

1. State the shape and bond angle of BF₃.
2. Explain why CO₂ is linear.
3. How many lone pairs are on the central atom of H₃O⁺?
4. Predict the shape of SiH₄ and its bond angle.
5. Predict the shape of NH₂⁻ and its approximate bond angle.
6. State the two bond angles in PCl₅.
7. Predict the shape of the AlCl₄⁻ ion.
8. Explain why the bond angle in H₂O is smaller than in NH₃.
9. Predict the shape of the nitrate ion and its bond angle.
10. Predict the shape of SiF₆²⁻.
11. Explain why SO₂ is not linear, although CO₂ is.
12. Name the shape of a molecule with four bonding pairs and no lone pairs, and give its bond angle.

**Answers:**

1. Trigonal planar, 120°.
2. Carbon has two regions (two C=O double bonds) and no lone pairs; they repel to be as far apart as possible, at 180°.
3. One: (6 − 3 − 1) ÷ 2 = 1.
4. Tetrahedral, 109.5°.
5. Non-linear, about 104.5° (two bonding pairs, two lone pairs).
6. 120° and 90°.
7. Tetrahedral, 109.5°: (3 − 4 + 1) ÷ 2 = 0 lone pairs, four bonding pairs.
8. Both have four pairs; H₂O has two lone pairs and NH₃ one; lone pairs repel more strongly, so the bonding pairs in H₂O are pushed closer together.
9. Trigonal planar, 120°.
10. Octahedral, 90°: (4 − 6 + 2) ÷ 2 = 0 lone pairs.
11. Sulfur has a lone pair as well as two double-bond regions (three regions); carbon in CO₂ has no lone pair (two regions).
12. Tetrahedral, 109.5°.
