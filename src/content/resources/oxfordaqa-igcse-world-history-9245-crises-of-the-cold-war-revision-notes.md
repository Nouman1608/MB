**Condensed notes on the Section A option "Crises of the Cold War, 1960-1975".** The specification names two parts: "Threats to peace in the 1960s" (the U-2 Crisis, Berlin, the space and arms races, the Cuban Missile Crisis and Czechoslovakia) and "Conflict in Vietnam" (the US escalation and the ending of the conflict). The full explanation is in the [Crises of the Cold War study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-world-history-9245-crises-of-the-cold-war/); the question types are in the [Paper 1 Section A revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-world-history-9245-paper-1-section-a-revision-notes/).

## The U-2 Crisis and Berlin

- **U-2, May 1960:** American spy plane shot down; Eisenhower first denied, then admitted spying; Khrushchev demanded an apology; Paris Summit collapsed.
- **Berlin:** around three million East Germans fled via West Berlin, 1949-61; border closed 13 August 1961.
- **Kennedy's response:** protested, did not use force; backed West Berlin (visit in 1963). The Wall became a symbol of division.

## The space and arms races

- Sputnik (1957); Gagarin (April 1961); Kennedy's Moon pledge; Apollo 11 (July 1969).
- ICBMs and Polaris submarine missiles.
- **Nuclear deterrent:** peace kept by fear of mutual destruction.
- Risks: accident and miscalculation.

## The Cuban Missile Crisis

| Stage | Detail |
|---|---|
| Background | Castro (1959); American businesses taken over; embargo; Bay of Pigs failure (April 1961) |
| Soviet action | Secret missiles in Cuba, 1962: to protect Cuba and to match US missiles in Turkey |
| Discovery | U-2 photographs, mid-October |
| Kennedy's choice | Naval blockade ("quarantine"), announced 22 October |
| Resolution | 28 October: missiles removed; US promise not to invade Cuba; secret removal of US missiles from Turkey |
| Results | Hotline; Test Ban Treaty; Kennedy's standing up; Khrushchev's down, removed in 1964 |

## Czechoslovakia, 1968

- **Dubček** and the **Prague Spring**: "socialism with a human face"; less censorship.
- USSR feared the spread of reform.
- 20-21 August: Warsaw Pact invasion; Dubček removed.
- **Brezhnev Doctrine:** the USSR could intervene to defend socialism in any communist state.
- West protested but took no action.

## Vietnam: escalation

- **Domino Theory:** if one state fell to communism, its neighbours would follow.
- Kennedy: advisers rose to about 16,000; **Strategic Hamlets** (unpopular).
- North Vietnam: Ho Chi Minh; support from the USSR and China; Vietcong **guerrilla tactics**, tunnels, Ho Chi Minh Trail.
- Johnson: Gulf of Tonkin (August 1964); bombing and troops (1965); over 500,000 troops by 1968.
- US tactics: Search and Destroy; napalm and Agent Orange; My Lai.
- **Tet Offensive:** communist military defeat but a political success; US opinion turned.
- **Protest movement** at home.

## Vietnam: ending

- **Vietnamisation** (1969): South Vietnamese forces take over; US troops withdraw.
- Heavy bombing; war widened to Cambodia and Laos, 1970-72.
- Kent State (May 1970): four students shot dead.
- Media: television brought the war into American homes.
- Nixon's China visit (1972); Paris peace talks and agreement (January 1973); US withdrawal; Saigon falls (April 1975).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nuclear deterrent | The belief that the threat of retaliation prevents attack |
| ICBM | Intercontinental ballistic missile |
| Quarantine | Kennedy's name for the naval blockade of Cuba |
| Prague Spring | The 1968 reform movement in Czechoslovakia |
| Brezhnev Doctrine | The USSR's right to intervene in communist states |
| Domino Theory | The belief that communism would spread state by state |
| Vietcong | Communist guerrillas in South Vietnam |
| Vietnamisation | Handing the fighting to South Vietnamese forces |
| Tet Offensive | The 1968 surprise attacks on South Vietnamese cities |

## Exam traps

- Retelling the crisis instead of linking knowledge to the source.
- In Question 3, writing about what a source does not tell you.
- Saying Kennedy won in Cuba without the secret Turkey deal.
- Dating Tet in 1969 (it was January 1968).
- Mixing up Vietnamisation (Nixon) with the Strategic Hamlets (Kennedy and Diem).
- Forgetting that Question 4 needs a judgement.

## Self-test

1. What happened to the American U-2 plane in May 1960?
2. Why did East Germany build the Berlin Wall?
3. Who was the first human in space?
4. Why did Kennedy order a naval blockade of Cuba?
5. What did Khrushchev receive in return for removing the missiles?
6. Who led the Prague Spring?
7. What was the Brezhnev Doctrine?
8. What was the Domino Theory?
9. What was the Strategic Hamlets programme?
10. Why was the Tet Offensive important?
11. What was Vietnamisation?
12. When did Saigon fall?

## Answers

1. It was shot down over the USSR and its pilot captured.
2. To stop its people, especially skilled workers, leaving through West Berlin.
3. Yuri Gagarin, in April 1961.
4. To stop more missiles arriving and to force their removal without an immediate invasion.
5. An American promise not to invade Cuba, and the secret withdrawal of US missiles from Turkey.
6. Alexander Dubček.
7. The USSR's claim that it could intervene in any communist country where socialism was threatened.
8. The belief that if one state in South-East Asia fell to communism, its neighbours would follow.
9. A programme that moved peasants into fortified villages to separate them from the Vietcong.
10. It showed that the USA was not winning and turned American opinion against the war, although the communists suffered heavy losses.
11. Handing the fighting to South Vietnamese forces while withdrawing American troops.
12. April 1975.
