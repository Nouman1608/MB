These notes condense sub-topic 2(f) Acids, alkalis and titrations (points 2.28 to 2.33C) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024). Points 2.28 to 2.32 can appear on Paper 1 and Paper 2; point 2.33C is **Paper 2 only**. Worked examples are in the [acids, alkalis and titrations study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-acids-alkalis-and-titrations/); test yourself with the [acids, alkalis and titrations practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-acids-alkalis-and-titrations-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Acid | In aqueous solution, a source of hydrogen ions, H⁺ |
| Alkali | In aqueous solution, a source of hydroxide ions, OH⁻ |
| Neutralisation | Reaction of an acid with an alkali (H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O) |
| Indicator | A substance that changes colour depending on whether a solution is acidic or alkaline |
| Universal indicator | A mixture of indicators that shows the approximate pH |
| End point | The point in a titration where the indicator just changes colour permanently |
| Titre | The volume of solution added from the burette to reach the end point |
| Concordant titres | Titres within 0.10 cm³ of each other |

## Indicator colours (2.28)

| Indicator | Acid | Alkali |
|---|---|---|
| Litmus | Red | Blue |
| Phenolphthalein | Colourless | Pink |
| Methyl orange | Red | Yellow |

Memory check: phenolphthalein is the only one with no colour; methyl orange is never "orange" in alkali.

## pH bands (2.29)

```
0-3    strongly acidic
4-6    weakly acidic
7      neutral
8-10   weakly alkaline
11-14  strongly alkaline
```

## Universal indicator (2.30)

- Add a few drops to the solution, or spot the solution on indicator paper.
- Compare the colour with the pH chart; read the nearest pH.
- Gives an approximate pH only: colours are judged by eye.
- Colour run: red (low pH) → orange → yellow → green (7) → blue → purple (high pH).

## Ions and neutralisation (2.31, 2.32)

```
HCl(aq)  -> H+(aq) + Cl-(aq)      acid: source of H+
NaOH(aq) -> Na+(aq) + OH-(aq)     alkali: source of OH-
H+(aq) + OH-(aq) -> H2O(l)        neutralisation
```

pH during neutralisation: adding acid to alkali, pH falls through 7 at exact neutralisation; adding alkali to acid, pH rises.

## Titration method (2.33C) — Paper 2 only

1. Rinse the pipette with alkali and the burette with acid.
2. Pipette 25.0 cm³ of alkali into a conical flask (use a pipette filler).
3. Add a few drops of a single indicator (phenolphthalein or methyl orange).
4. Fill the burette with acid; remove the funnel; check no air bubble in the tip; read at eye level, bottom of meniscus.
5. Add acid while swirling, flask on a white tile; dropwise near the end point.
6. Stop at a permanent colour change; record the final reading.
7. Titre = final − initial. Rough first; repeat until titres are concordant; average the concordant ones.

End-point colour changes (acid from burette into alkali):

- Phenolphthalein: pink → colourless.
- Methyl orange: yellow → orange.

## Sources of error

| Error | Effect |
|---|---|
| Burette rinsed with water, not acid | Acid diluted → titre too large |
| Air bubble in burette tip | Reading changes without acid being delivered → titre wrong |
| Overshooting the end point | Titre too large |
| Reading the top of the meniscus | Every reading wrong |
| Not swirling | Colour changes locally too early |
| Using universal indicator | No sharp end point |

Rinsing the conical flask with distilled water does **not** cause an error.

## Mean titre reminder

Titres: 22.40 (rough), 21.95, 22.30, 22.00 cm³. Concordant: 21.95 and 22.00. Mean = 21.98 cm³ (21.975 rounded to 2 decimal places). 22.30 is excluded.

## Answer frames

- **Using a single indicator (2.28):** "Add a few drops of [indicator] to a sample. [Colour] shows the solution is acidic; [colour] shows it is alkaline."
- **Neutralisation (2.32):** "The hydroxide ions from the alkali react with the hydrogen ions from the acid to form water, so the solution becomes neutral."
- **Reason for a titration step:** link the step to accuracy (pipette, rinsing, reading the meniscus, dropwise addition) or to reliability (repeating until concordant).

## Exam traps

- "Clear" for phenolphthalein in acid: write colourless.
- Methyl orange "orange" in alkali: it is yellow.
- pH 3 is strongly acidic and pH 10 is weakly alkaline on this specification.
- Alkalis "contain H⁺": they are a source of OH⁻.
- Including the rough titre in the mean.
- Recording burette readings to 1 decimal place.
- Using a measuring cylinder instead of a pipette for the 25.0 cm³.

## Self-test

1. State the colour of litmus in sodium hydroxide solution.
2. State the colour of methyl orange in hydrochloric acid.
3. A solution has pH 9. Classify it.
4. A solution has pH 2. Classify it.
5. Describe how to find the approximate pH of a solution using universal indicator.
6. Name the ion that all acids produce in aqueous solution.
7. Write the ionic equation for neutralisation.
8. Explain why universal indicator is not used in a titration.
9. Name the piece of apparatus used to add acid in a titration.
10. Why is the conical flask placed on a white tile?
11. State the colour change of phenolphthalein at the end point when acid is added to alkali.
12. Titres of 25.30 (rough), 24.70, 24.75 and 24.95 cm³ are recorded. Calculate the mean titre.

### Answers

1. Blue.
2. Red.
3. Weakly alkaline.
4. Strongly acidic.
5. Add a few drops of universal indicator to the solution (or use indicator paper) and compare the colour with a pH chart.
6. The hydrogen ion, H⁺.
7. H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l).
8. Its colour changes gradually, so there is no sharp end point.
9. A burette.
10. To see the colour change clearly.
11. Pink to colourless.
12. Concordant: 24.70 and 24.75; mean = 24.73 cm³ (24.725 rounded to 2 decimal places).
