These questions cover key question 1.5 of Cambridge IGCSE 0470 and O
Level 2147: why, and with what effects, nations gained and expanded

## Paper 1 style: Structured Questions

### Question 1: Europeans in Africa

**(a)** Describe the French policy of assimilation in Senegal. **[4]**

**(b)** Why did Lugard use indirect rule in Northern Nigeria? **[6]**

**(c)** 'Leopold II's rule in the Congo was the most harmful form of
European imperialism for Africans.' How far do you agree? Explain your
answer. **[10]**

#### Worked answer

**(a)** Any four: Africans who adopted the French language, education
and culture could in theory become French citizens; the Four Communes
of Saint-Louis, Gorée, Rufisque and Dakar elected a deputy to the
French parliament; rule was direct, through French officials rather
than local rulers; Faidherbe, as governor, extended French control
inland and recruited African troops, the *tirailleurs sénégalais*; in
practice very few Africans gained citizenship. **[4]**

**(b)** Explained reasons: Britain had few officials and little money
to govern a huge area, so ruling through existing emirs was cheap; the
Sokoto Caliphate already had an organised system of courts, taxation
and administration that Lugard could use; keeping traditional rulers
in place reduced the risk of revolt after the conquest of 1903;
Lugard believed it was the best way to govern Africans according to
their own customs, as he later argued in *The Dual Mandate*. **[6]**

**(c)** **Agree:** the Congo Free State was run as Leopold's private
business; forced rubber quotas were enforced by the Force Publique
through hostage-taking, mutilation and killing; millions died; it was
so extreme that international protest (Casement's report of 1904, the
Congo Reform Association) forced Belgium to take it over in 1908.
**Disagree:** French rule also used forced labour and conquest and
destroyed African states; British indirect rule changed the power of
chiefs and caused problems where chiefs had not existed; all systems
took land and political control. **Judgement:** most answers will
agree that the Congo was the most extreme, but should show that the
difference was one of degree. **[10]**

### Question 2: India, 1857

**(a)** What happened at Meerut in May 1857? **[4]**

**(b)** Why did many Indians rebel against British rule in 1857? **[6]**

**(c)** 'The events of 1857 completely changed British rule in India.'
How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

#### Worked answer

**(a)** Sepoys who had refused the new cartridges were punished; on
10 May 1857 soldiers at Meerut rebelled, freed their imprisoned
comrades, killed British officers and civilians, and marched to Delhi,
where they proclaimed Bahadur Shah II as their leader. **[4]**

**(b)** Explained reasons: the rumour that cartridges for the Enfield
rifle were greased with cow and pig fat offended Hindu and Muslim
soldiers; annexations, including under the Doctrine of Lapse and of
Awadh in 1856, angered rulers and many sepoys whose families came from
Awadh; fear that Britain intended to convert Indians to Christianity
and attack customs; grievances over pay and service overseas. **[6]**

**(c)** **Changed:** the Government of India Act 1858 ended East India
Company rule; the Crown ruled through a Secretary of State and a
Viceroy; Queen Victoria's Proclamation promised religious
non-interference and an end to annexations; the army was reorganised
with more British troops; the Mughal dynasty ended. **Continued:**
India stayed under British control and was run for British interests;
British racial attitudes hardened rather than softened; most Indians
still had no say in government. **Judgement:** the system of rule
changed greatly, but the fact of British control did not. **[10]**

### Question 3: The USA and Spain's colonies

**(a)** Describe the terms of the Treaty of Paris (1898). **[4]**

**(b)** Why did the USA go to war with Spain in 1898? **[6]**

**(c)** 'American control brought more benefits than harm to the
Philippines and Cuba.' How far do you agree? **[10]**

#### Worked answer

**(a)** Spain gave up its claim to Cuba; Spain ceded Puerto Rico and
Guam to the USA; Spain ceded the Philippines to the USA; the USA paid
Spain $20 million. **[4]**

**(b)** Explained reasons: sympathy for the Cubans fighting Spain
since 1895, stirred by sensational newspaper reports of Spanish
reconcentration camps; the explosion of the USS *Maine* in Havana
harbour on 15 February 1898, which the press blamed on Spain; American
economic interests in Cuban sugar and trade; strategic interest in
naval bases and Pacific influence. **[6]**

**(c)** **Benefits:** US schools and the spread of English in the
Philippines; public health and infrastructure works; an elected
Philippine Assembly from 1907; Cuba freed from Spanish rule.
**Harm:** Filipino independence was denied and the Philippine–American
War (1899–1902) caused heavy civilian losses; the Platt Amendment
limited Cuban sovereignty, gave the USA Guantánamo Bay and was used to
justify intervention in 1906. **Judgement:** supported either way, but
the best answers separate short-term material change from the loss of
self-government. **[10]**

## Source practice

**Question 4: The debate over American imperialism, 1898–99**

**Source A** — Rudyard Kipling, opening of the poem "The White Man's
Burden", published in February 1899 and addressed to the USA: "Take up
the White Man's burden— / Send forth the best ye breed".

**Source B** — from the platform of the American Anti-Imperialist
League, 1899: "We hold that the policy known as imperialism is hostile
to liberty and tends toward militarism, an evil from which it has been
our glory to be free."

**(a)** What is the message of Source A? Use the source and your
knowledge to explain your answer. **[7]**

**(b)** How far do Sources A and B disagree about American expansion?
**[8]**

### Worked answer

**(a)** Source A urges Americans to take control of the Philippines
and govern them, presenting empire as a duty of supposedly superior
white nations towards colonised peoples. Context: it was published
while the US Senate was debating the Treaty of Paris and as the
Philippine–American War began, so it was intended to persuade
Americans to keep the islands. Good answers explain the "civilising
mission" motive and its racial assumptions. **[7]**

**(b)** They disagree fundamentally: Source A presents empire as a
noble duty, while Source B says imperialism threatens liberty and
leads to militarism, betraying American ideals. They agree only that
empire would be a major change for the USA. Provenance explains the
difference: a British imperialist poet supporting expansion against
an American pressure group opposing it. **[8]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing an empire instead of explaining its impact on the
  colonised.
- Giving the motives without examples from the case studies.
- Assuming a source is unreliable just because it is biased — explain
  what its bias makes it useful for.
