This guide covers **Paper 1 – Drama and Poetry**, an AS Level paper for
Cambridge International A Level English Literature 9695, 2024–2026
series. Literature syllabuses are organised around named papers and set
texts rather than a numbered topic taxonomy, and the set-text list
rotates by examination year — this guide covers the paper's stable
structure rather than any specific text, since candidates should
always check the current set-text grid for their own exam series.

## Where this fits in 9695

AS Level candidates take Paper 1 and Paper 2 (Prose and Unseen); A
Level candidates additionally take Paper 3 (Shakespeare and Drama) and
Paper 4. Paper 1 is therefore one of the two papers that make up the
standalone AS Level award, and its two sections — Drama and Poetry —
mirror the genre-based split used again in Papers 2 and 3. Because
Paper 1 sits within the AS Level award on its own, it is also the
first point at which candidates are assessed on sustained literary
analysis at this level, rather than the more guided approach typical
of IGCSE Literature.

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE INTERNATIONAL A LEVEL ENGLISH LITERATURE 9695 — PAPER 1
DRAMA AND POETRY**

- 1.1 Section A: Drama — answering on a set drama text, testing
knowledge of plot, character and dramatic technique
- 1.2 Section B: Poetry — answering on a set poetry text or selection,
testing close reading of poetic language, form and structure

## How to approach it

Because both sections sit within a single paper, time management
across Drama and Poetry matters as much as knowledge of either
individually — practising full-length timed responses to both
sections in one sitting, not just revising them separately, builds the
pacing candidates need on the actual exam. For Drama, strong answers
engage with the text's theatricality — staging, dramatic irony, an
audience's likely response to a scene — not just plot summary or
character description. For Poetry, close, line-level analysis of
language and form (word choice, imagery, structure, rhythm) is what
separates higher-band answers from ones that only paraphrase what a
poem is "about." As with any Literature paper, building genuine
familiarity with the actual set texts — through re-reading and
practising past-paper-style questions on them specifically — matters
more than generic essay technique on its own — an essay with a
confident structure but thin, generic textual support scores
noticeably lower than one with a simpler structure but precise,
well-chosen evidence from the text itself.
