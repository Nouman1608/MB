## Section A

**1.** Find the value of:

**(a)** 6⁰ **[1]**

**(b)** 4⁻² **[1]**

**(c)** 125^(1/3) **[1]**

**2.** Find the value of (2/3)⁻². **[2]**

**3.** Find the value of 8^(2/3). **[2]**

**4.** Find the value of 27^(-2/3). **[2]**

**5.** Find the value of (1/4)^(-3/2). **[2]**

**6.** Work out 3⁵ × 3⁻² ÷ 3⁴. **[2]**

**7.** Work out 2⁻³ + 2⁰ + 2³. **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** Work out (2⁻³ × 16) ÷ 4. Write your answer as a fraction. **[3]**

**9.** Find the value of n in each case.

**(a)** 2ⁿ = 1/32 **[1]**

**(b)** 4ⁿ = 8 **[2]**

**10.** Write 1/25 and 0.04 as powers of 5. **[2]**

**11.** Work out 16^(3/4) - 9^(-1/2). **[3]**

**12.** Show that 25^(-1/2) × 10² = 20. **[2]**

**13.** *Calculator.* Work out 12^(3/2), giving your answer correct to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**14.** Work out (3²)^(3/2) ÷ 3⁻¹. **[2]**

**15.** Find the value of:

**(a)** 1000^(2/3) **[1]**

**(b)** 81^(-1/2) **[1]**

**(c)** (27/8)^(-1/3) **[2]**

**16.** Work out (2⁻²)⁻³. **[2]**

**17.** Find n if 9ⁿ = 27. **[2]**

**18.** Work out 49^(-1/2) × 7². **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **1** **[1]**

**(b)** **1/16** **[1]**

**(c)** **5** **[1]** **[3]**

**2.** (3/2)² **[1]** = **9/4** **[1]** **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* A negative index means flip the fraction. Squaring 2/3 without flipping gives 4/9, which is wrong.

**3.** ∛8 = 2 **[1]**; 2² = **4** **[1]** **[2]**

**4.** ∛27 = 3 and 3² = 9 **[1]**; reciprocal: **1/9** **[1]** **[2]**

**5.** Flip: 4^(3/2) **[1]**; (√4)³ = 2³ = **8** **[1]** **[2]**

**6.** 3^(5 - 2 - 4) **[1]** = 3⁻¹ = **1/3** **[1]** **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Treat the three terms as one line of indices: 5, then -2, then -4. Add and subtract in order and the result is -1.

**7.** 1/8 + 1 + 8 **[1]** = 9 1/8 or **73/8** **[1]** **[2]**

**8.** 16 = 2⁴ and 4 = 2² **[1]**; 2^(-3 + 4 - 2) **[1]**; 2⁻¹ = **1/2** **[1]** **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Writing 16 and 4 as powers of 2 is the key step. Without it, candidates multiply 1/8 by 16 and divide by 4, which is correct but slower and easier to get wrong.

**9. (a)** 1/32 = 2⁻⁵, so **n = -5** **[1]**

**(b)** 4ⁿ = 2²ⁿ and 8 = 2³ **[1]**; 2n = 3, so **n = 3/2** **[1]** **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Pick the smaller base (2) that both numbers are powers of.

**10.** 1/25 = **5⁻²** **[1]**; 0.04 = 4/100 = 1/25 = **5⁻²** **[1]** **[2]**

**11.** 16^(3/4) = 2³ = 8 **[1]**; 9^(-1/2) = 1/3 **[1]**; 8 - 1/3 = **7 2/3** (or 23/3) **[1]** **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Deal with each term separately and combine only at the end. The first term is a positive whole number and the second is a small fraction.

**12.** 25^(-1/2) = 1/√25 = 1/5 **[1]**; 1/5 × 100 = **20** **[1]** **[2]**

**13.** 41.569... **[1]**; **41.6** **[1]** **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Put the fractional index in brackets on the calculator. Typing 12^3/2 gives 864.

**14.** (3²)^(3/2) = 3³ = 27 **[1]**; 27 ÷ 3⁻¹ = 27 × 3 = **81** **[1]** **[2]**

**15. (a)** ∛1000 = 10, 10² = **100** **[1]**

**(b)** 1/√81 = **1/9** **[1]**

**(c)** flip: (8/27)^(1/3) **[1]** = **2/3** **[1]** **[4]**

**16.** 2^(-2 × -3) **[1]** = 2⁶ = **64** **[1]** **[2]**

**17.** 9 = 3² and 27 = 3³ **[1]**; 2n = 3, so **n = 3/2** **[1]** **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Neither 9 nor 27 is a power of the other with a whole-number index, but both are powers of 3.

**18.** 49^(-1/2) = 1/7 **[1]**; 1/7 × 49 = **7** **[1]** **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving 2⁻³ as -8 or -6, and 4⁻² as -16 or -8.
- Squaring 2/3 without flipping it in Question 2.
- Working out 8² before taking the cube root in Question 3.
- Forgetting that dividing by a negative power adds to the index in Question 14.
- Writing 6⁰ = 0 in Question 1(a).
- Not converting to a common base in Questions 8 and 9.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a negative index allowed in a final answer?**
In numerical work, give a fraction or a whole number unless the question asks for a power. Evaluate 7⁻² as 1/49.

**Why is any number to the power zero equal to 1?**
Because a power divided by itself is 1. For example 5³ ÷ 5³ = 1, and the division rule says 5³ ÷ 5³ = 5⁰.

**Can I use a calculator on the non-calculator questions to check?**
Yes, once you have written your answer. Check the form too: the calculator may show 0.1111 where you need the fraction 1/9.

**Which comes first, the root or the power?**
The root. Taking the cube root of 8 first gives 2, then squaring gives 4. This is much easier than starting with 8².
