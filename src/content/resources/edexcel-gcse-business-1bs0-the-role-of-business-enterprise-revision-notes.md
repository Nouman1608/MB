## The specification in brief

**Purpose of business activity:** to produce goods or services; to meet customer needs; to add value through convenience, branding, quality, design and unique selling points.

**The role of entrepreneurship:** an entrepreneur organises resources, makes business decisions and takes risks.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Goods | Physical products that can be touched and kept |
| Services | Actions done for a customer, such as a repair or a lesson |
| Customer needs | What customers want from a purchase; a business survives only if it meets them |
| Added value | Selling price minus the cost of bought-in materials and services (standard meaning; no formula in the specification) |
| Convenience | Saving customers time or effort |
| Branding | A recognised name, logo and image that customers trust |
| Quality | How well a product is made or a service delivered compared with what customers expect |
| Design | Appearance, function and ease of use |
| Unique selling point (USP) | A feature that makes a product different from all rivals |
| Entrepreneur | A person who sets up and runs a business, organising resources, making decisions and taking risks |

## Condensed recall

### Why add value?
- Charge a higher price for the same inputs, or
- sell more at the same price, or
- both.
Either way, more money is left to pay overheads and make a profit.

### Added value calculation (standard method)
Added value per item = selling price − cost of bought-in inputs per item.
Total added value = added value per item × number sold.

Example: a candle sells for £14 and uses £3.50 of wax, wick and jar. Added value = £14 − £3.50 = £10.50. At 800 candles a year, total = £8,400.

### Matching the method to the customers

| Customers in the case | Strongest method |
|---|---|
| Busy, short of time | Convenience |
| Buying gifts or status items | Branding, design |
| Want something durable or safe | Quality |
| Many similar rivals nearby | USP |

### Limits of each method
- Convenience and quality raise costs.
- Branding takes time and promotion spending.
- Design and USPs can be copied.

### Entrepreneur roles: what to look for in a case

| Role | Clues |
|---|---|
| Organises resources | Renting premises, hiring staff, buying equipment, raising finance |
| Makes business decisions | Choosing products, prices, target customers, location |
| Takes risks | Using savings, borrowing, leaving a job, launching untested products |

### Mini case for recall: Heddle Lane Yarns

Mirela rents a market stall, buys a spinning wheel and dyes, and pays a friend to run the stall on Saturdays. She decides to sell only hand-dyed wool from local sheep at £12 a skein, about double the price of supermarket wool. She has borrowed £3,000 from her family.

- Organises resources: the stall, spinning wheel, dyes and a helper.
- Makes business decisions: hand-dyed local wool only, priced at £12.
- Takes risks: the £3,000 family loan must be repaid even if sales are slow.
- Adds value through: quality (hand-dyed), a USP (local sheep), and branding if she builds a recognised name for her colours.

## Answer frame for adding value

These are suggested sentence starters, not specification wording.

- "The business adds value through ... because its customers ..."
- "This allows it to charge ... / sell more ..., which increases the money left after paying for bought-in inputs."
- "However, this method ... (raises costs / can be copied / takes time)."

## Exam traps

- Calling added value "profit". Overheads and wages still have to come out of it.
- Naming a method without the effect on price or sales.
- Saying a USP cannot be copied.
- Listing the three entrepreneur roles without evidence from the case.
- Using "goods" for a service business.
- Treating "meet customer needs" as the same as "add value": a business can meet a need without adding much value.

## Self-test

1. State the three purposes of business activity in the specification.
2. Give the five ways of adding value listed in the specification.
3. What is the difference between goods and services?
4. Define unique selling point.
5. A jacket sells for £60. The cloth, zip and buttons cost £18. Calculate the added value.
6. If the business in question 5 sells 250 jackets, what is the total added value?
7. Why is added value not the same as profit?
8. Which method of adding value would suit a takeaway serving office workers with a 30-minute lunch break?
9. Give one disadvantage of adding value through higher quality.
10. State the three roles of an entrepreneur.
11. An owner chooses to sell only vegan cakes. Which role is this?
12. Explain why a USP may not give a long-lasting advantage.

### Answers

1. To produce goods or services; to meet customer needs; to add value.
2. Convenience, branding, quality, design, unique selling points.
3. Goods are physical products; services are actions carried out for customers.
4. A feature of a product or business that no competitor offers, giving customers a reason to choose it.
5. £60 − £18 = £42.
6. 250 × £42 = £10,500.
7. Wages, rent and other overheads have not yet been paid from it.
8. Convenience, such as fast service or ordering ahead.
9. Better materials or more skilled staff raise costs, so the gain in added value may be smaller than the price rise suggests.
10. Organises resources, makes business decisions, takes risks.
11. Making business decisions.
12. Once rivals see that customers like the feature, they can copy it, so it stops being unique and the business must find a new way to stand out.
