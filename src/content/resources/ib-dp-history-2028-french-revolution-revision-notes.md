**HL only.** These notes cover Regional study 4, The French Revolution (1774 to 1815), in the Europe option examined on HL Paper 3. The country case for Napoleonic impact is Spain. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-french-revolution/) for fuller explanation.

## What Paper 3 wants

Two 15-mark essays from two different regional studies in two hours. Top-band essays evaluate a range of perspectives on the argument in the question and reach a substantiated judgement.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Estates | The three orders of old regime society: clergy, nobility, Third Estate |
| Taille | Main direct tax, from which privileged groups were largely exempt |
| Tennis Court Oath | 20 June 1789 pledge to give France a constitution |
| Active citizen | Man paying enough tax to vote under the 1791 constitution |
| Terror | Period of emergency repression, 1793 to 1794 |
| Thermidor | The month of Robespierre's fall (July 1794) |
| Plebiscite | Direct vote used by Napoleon to approve his rule |
| Concordat | 1801 agreement between Napoleon and the pope |
| Continental System | Napoleon's economic blockade of Britain (from 1806) |

## Causes

- **Intellectual:** Enlightenment criticism; American example.
- **Social:** privileges of clergy and nobility; resentment in the educated Third Estate; peasant dues.
- **Economic:** bankruptcy from war debts; failed reforms; the poor harvest of 1788.
- **Louis XVI:** indecisive; dismissed reformers.
- **Triggers:** Estates General, voting dispute, National Assembly, Bastille, Great Fear, Declaration.

## Radicalization

| Stage | Key points |
|---|---|
| 1791 constitution | Constitutional monarchy; active citizens; Civil Constitution of the Clergy divides the Church |
| End of monarchy | Varennes; war from April 1792; 10 August; republic |
| Terror | Foreign invasion, Vendée, economic crisis; Committee of Public Safety; Law of Suspects; Revolutionary Tribunal |
| Thermidor | Convention turns on Robespierre; Terror ends |

## Rise of Napoleon

- **Wars:** mass army; Italian campaign (1796 to 1797); Egypt (1798).
- **Directory's fall:** corruption, disputed elections, reliance on the army, royalist and Jacobin threats.
- **Brumaire:** coup with Sieyès; Consulate; Consul for life (1802); Emperor (1804).

## Napoleon in France

- **Policies:** Bank of France, taxation, Legion of Honour, lycées, Civil Code, Concordat.
- **Foreign policy:** glory and territory, then conscription, taxes and loss of life.
- **Women:** revolution gave inheritance and divorce rights; Napoleon's code limited both.
- **Religion:** Concordat repaired the division but kept state control.

## Spain under Napoleon

| Theme | Points |
|---|---|
| Political | Joseph Bonaparte (1808); Cadiz Constitution (1812); Ferdinand VII restores absolutism (1814) |
| Social | Inquisition and feudal dues abolished; resentment of the attack on the Church; guerrilla violence |
| Economic | War damage; loss of colonial control |
| Marginalized groups | Other territories: Jews gained legal equality; Haiti's enslaved people won freedom |

## Decline

- **Coalitions:** British money; Austerlitz (1805) but no lasting peace.
- **Continental System:** hard to enforce; harmed Europe; led to Peninsular War and break with Russia.
- **Russia (1812):** retreat, winter and disease destroyed the army.
- **1813 to 1815:** Leipzig; abdication; Hundred Days; Waterloo.

## Historians' views

- Marxist (Lefebvre): bourgeois revolution. Revisionist (Cobban): class conflict overstated. Furet and Schama: political culture and violence.
- Terror: circumstances (war, civil war) or ideology?
- Napoleon: heir of the revolution or dictator?

## Common mistakes

- Telling the story without answering the question.
- Treating Louis XVI as the only cause.
- Calling the Terror either entirely planned or entirely accidental.
- Treating Napoleon as purely a soldier and ignoring civil reforms.
- Using France as the country example in the empire question.

## Self-test

1. What was the Tennis Court Oath?
2. Why were many men excluded from voting in 1791?
3. Name two features of the Terror.
4. Give two reasons the Directory fell.
5. What were two effects of the Continental System?
6. What happened at Waterloo?

**Answers:** 1. The Third Estate's pledge on 20 June 1789 not to disband until France had a constitution. 2. Only men paying enough tax were active citizens. 3. For example the Law of Suspects and the Revolutionary Tribunal. 4. For example corruption and dependence on the army, or royalist and Jacobin opposition. 5. For example harm to European economies and war in Spain and Russia. 6. Napoleon was defeated by Wellington and Blücher on 18 June 1815.

## Worked example: planning an essay

**Question:** "To what extent was Napoleon the heir of the revolution?"

1. **Thesis:** He kept several lasting gains of the revolution, but he ended political liberty and brought back personal rule, so he was an heir only in part.
2. **Paragraph 1, continuity:** equality before the law, careers open to talent, property rights and the end of feudal dues kept in the Civil Code; the sale of church land accepted.
3. **Paragraph 2, change:** plebiscites without real choice, censorship, police, Empire in 1804, limits on women's rights and divorce.
4. **Paragraph 3, foreign policy:** wars spread revolutionary laws, but also conquest and heavy demands.
5. **Perspective:** one view sees him as consolidating the revolution; another sees a dictator who used it.
6. **Judgement:** he preserved its social settlement but not its political ideals.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I judge whether the revolution was a success?**
State criteria, such as rights, stability and war, and apply them to different groups.

**Should I name historians?**
Yes, when you can explain their argument.
