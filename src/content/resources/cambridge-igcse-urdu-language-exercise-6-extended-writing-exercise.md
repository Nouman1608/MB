This guide covers **Exercise 6, the extended writing exercise**, the final task of Paper 1 Reading and
Writing in Cambridge IGCSE Urdu as a Second Language (0539). It goes deeper than the site's
[Paper 1 Reading and Writing guide](/resources/igcse-cambridge-urdu-paper-1-reading-writing/), which
introduces all six exercises. Here the focus is one task: how to read it, plan it, write it in about 200
words of accurate Urdu, and check it in the time available.

## What the syllabus says about Exercise 6

The syllabus describes Exercise 6 in five points:

- candidates write **a longer piece of continuous prose, of approximately 200 words**;
- **a stimulus of short prompts** is printed in the question paper;
- **a purpose, format and audience** for the writing are specified;
- it is worth **16 marks**, the largest single exercise on the 60-mark paper (just over a quarter of it);
- it assesses the writing objectives **W1–W5**.

Those five objectives are, in the syllabus's words:

| Objective | What it asks for |
|---|---|
| W1 | communicate information/ideas/opinions clearly, accurately and effectively |
| W2 | organise ideas into coherent paragraphs using a range of appropriate linking devices |
| W3 | use a range of grammatical structures and vocabulary accurately and effectively |
| W4 | show control of punctuation and spelling |
| W5 | use appropriate register and style/format for the given purpose and audience |

As on the rest of Paper 1, the question and your answer are in Urdu, and dictionaries are not allowed.

### How it differs from Exercise 5

Exercise 5 asks for about 120 words of *functional* prose, such as an email, for 8 marks, and its
stimulus may include pictures. Exercise 6 doubles the marks for about 200 words of *continuous* prose.
The difference in practice is **development**: ideas explained, supported and linked across paragraphs
(W2), not just stated.

## Which formats to prepare

The syllabus's Writing content lists the kinds of writing candidates should be able to produce "such as a
summary, an informal letter/email, an article, a blog, a report or review". It does not say which of these
will appear in Exercise 6, so prepare the longer formats and check recent papers and the specimen paper
for your series.

| Format | Urdu name | Conventions worth showing | Usual register |
|---|---|---|---|
| Magazine or website article | مضمون | a headline (سرخی), an opening that draws the reader in, a closing thought | neutral to semi-formal |
| Blog post | بلاگ | first person, a personal and lively tone, perhaps a question to readers | informal to neutral |
| Report | رپورٹ | a title, an opening that states the purpose, factual paragraphs, recommendations (سفارشات) | formal, often impersonal |
| Review | تبصرہ | what is being reviewed, description, strengths and weaknesses, a recommendation | neutral, opinion-led |
| Letter | خط | greeting and closing suited to the reader | set by the reader |

Format is part of W5, so make it visible: a report that opens like a chatty blog is weaker on W5 even if
every sentence is accurate.

## Reading the task: purpose, format, audience

Before planning, find three things in the rubric and underline them:

1. **Purpose** (مقصد): what the writing must *do*: inform, describe, give an opinion, persuade or suggest.
2. **Format** (طرزِ تحریر): article, report, blog and so on.
3. **Audience** (قارئین / مخاطب): who will read it: a head teacher, classmates, readers of a website.

The verbs in the rubric tell you the purpose, and each purpose calls for its own language:

| Rubric verb | Meaning | Language that shows you have done it |
|---|---|---|
| بیان کیجیے | describe, give an account | past tense narration, descriptive adjectives |
| وضاحت کیجیے | explain | کیونکہ، اس کی وجہ یہ ہے کہ |
| اپنی رائے دیجیے | give your opinion | میرے خیال میں، میری رائے یہ ہے کہ |
| مشورہ دیجیے / تجویز دیجیے | advise / suggest | چاہیے، بہتر ہوگا کہ |
| قائل کیجیے | persuade | direct questions to the reader, strong conclusions |

The audience decides the register. For a head teacher or a formal report, use **آپ** and formal words
(مدعو کرنا rather than بلانا, تقریب rather than فنکشن). For a blog for other teenagers, a warmer tone is
right, but it is still written Urdu: avoid slang and English words where an everyday Urdu word exists.

## Using the prompts

The prompts are short: often a few comments, as if from different people, or headings. Treat each one
as a starting point, not as a sentence to copy. A useful habit is **point, reason, example**: state the
idea in your own words, say why it matters, then give a detail or consequence.

```
Prompt:        کچھ پروگرام بہت لمبے تھے۔
Undeveloped:   کچھ پروگرام لمبے تھے۔
Developed:     تقریری مقابلہ تقریباً تین گھنٹے جاری رہا، جس کی وجہ سے
               چھوٹی جماعتوں کے بچے بے چین ہو گئے اور بہت سے لوگ
               انعامات کے اعلان سے پہلے ہی چلے گئے۔
```

The developed version names a specific event and adds a consequence with جس کی وجہ سے, showing W1, W2
and W3 in one sentence. Check whether the rubric lets you add your own ideas, and make sure every part
of the task is covered: a missing part loses content that cannot be made up elsewhere.

## Planning about 200 words in about 30 minutes

Paper 1 gives 2 hours for 60 marks, about two minutes a mark, so Exercise 6 deserves roughly **30
minutes**. A workable split is 3 minutes planning, 22 writing and 5 checking.

A four-paragraph shape fits 200 words well:

```
Paragraph 1  (about 30 words)   introduce the subject; show the format and purpose
Paragraph 2  (about 60-70)      first main point, developed
Paragraph 3  (about 60-70)      second main point or the other side, developed
Paragraph 4  (about 30-40)      conclusion: opinion, recommendation or final thought
```

"Approximately 200 words" is guidance. Far shorter usually means undeveloped points; far longer eats
checking time. Learn in practice how much space 200 words of your handwriting takes.

## Language that lifts the mark

**Linking devices (W2).** Use a range, not the same word every time:

| Function | Urdu |
|---|---|
| adding | اس کے علاوہ، مزید یہ کہ، نیز |
| contrasting | لیکن، تاہم، دوسری طرف، اس کے باوجود |
| cause and result | کیونکہ، اس لیے، اس کی وجہ سے، چنانچہ |
| sequencing | سب سے پہلے، پھر، آخر میں |
| concluding | مختصر یہ کہ، ان تمام باتوں کو دیکھتے ہوئے |

**Structures (W3).** Plan to include a few of these deliberately:

- a condition: اگر ہر جماعت مدد کرے تو کام جلدی ہو جائے گا۔
- a relative clause: جو طلبہ رضاکار بنے، انھیں سرٹیفکیٹ دیے گئے۔
- a passive, very useful in reports: تقریب کا اہتمام لائبریری میں کیا گیا۔
- a suggestion: میری تجویز ہے کہ اگلے سال والدین کو بھی مدعو کیا جائے۔
- a comparison: یہ پروگرام پچھلے سال کے مقابلے میں زیادہ کامیاب رہا۔

**Punctuation and spelling (W4).** End sentences with the Urdu full stop **۔**, use **،** for commas and
**؟** for questions. Check words that sound alike but are spelt differently, such as **ضروری** (not
ظروری) and **ذریعے** (not زریعے), and the difference between ہ and ھ, as in **پہلے** and **پھر**.

## Worked example

The task below is original, written for this guide.

> آپ کے اسکول میں پچھلے مہینے ایک "ثقافتی ہفتہ" منایا گیا۔ پرنسپل صاحبہ نے آپ سے اس کے بارے میں
> رپورٹ لکھنے کو کہا ہے۔ تقریباً 200 الفاظ کی رپورٹ لکھیے۔
>
> طلبہ کے کچھ تبصرے:
> - "کھانوں کا میلہ سب سے دلچسپ تھا۔"
> - "کچھ پروگرام بہت لمبے تھے۔"
> - "اگلے سال والدین کو بھی بلانا چاہیے۔"
>
> *(مقصد: معلومات دینا اور تجاویز پیش کرنا۔ طرزِ تحریر: رپورٹ۔ قارئین: پرنسپل صاحبہ۔)*

**Step 1, the three elements.** Purpose: inform and recommend. Format: report, so a title, a purpose
statement and recommendations. Audience: the principal, so formal register and آپ.

**Step 2, the plan.**

```
1  Title + purpose of the report (what, when, aim of the week)
2  What worked: food fair (each class, one province's dish; money to the library)
3  What did not: programmes too long (speech contest, younger pupils restless)
4  Recommendations: invite parents; a time limit for each programme
```

**Step 3, writing.** An opening and a recommendation from a model answer:

> **ثقافتی ہفتے کی رپورٹ**
>
> یہ رپورٹ اسکول میں پچھلے مہینے منائے گئے ثقافتی ہفتے کے بارے میں ہے۔ اس ہفتے کا مقصد طلبہ کو
> پاکستان کے مختلف علاقوں کی ثقافت سے متعارف کرانا تھا۔ اس رپورٹ میں طلبہ کی رائے اور اگلے سال کے
> لیے کچھ تجاویز شامل ہیں۔
>
> ...
>
> **سفارشات:** میری تجویز ہے کہ اگلے سال والدین کو بھی مدعو کیا جائے، اور کسی بھی پروگرام کا
> دورانیہ ایک گھنٹے سے زیادہ نہ ہو۔

**Why it works.** The title and the opening sentence show the report format at once (W5). The prompt
"والدین کو بھی بلانا چاہیے" has been raised in register to "والدین کو بھی مدعو کیا جائے" instead of
being copied. The recommendation uses a subjunctive passive (مدعو کیا جائے) and a negative subjunctive
(زیادہ نہ ہو), which show range (W3).

**A second, shorter example: lifting a weak sentence.**

```
Weak:    ثقافتی ہفتہ اچھا تھا۔ کھانا اچھا تھا۔ پروگرام لمبے تھے۔
Strong:  اگرچہ زیادہ تر پروگرام دلچسپ تھے، لیکن بعض تقریبات ضرورت سے
         زیادہ لمبی تھیں، جس کی وجہ سے چھوٹی جماعتوں کے بچے جلد تھک گئے۔
```

The weak version repeats اچھا and has three unlinked simple sentences. The strong version contrasts
two ideas (اگرچہ ... لیکن), gives a result, and gets the feminine agreement right: تقریبات ... لمبی تھیں.

## Common mistakes

- **Ignoring the format:** an article with no headline, a report with no recommendations.
- **Register drift:** starting with آپ and slipping into تم.
- **Listing or copying the prompts** instead of restating and developing them.
- **Agreement errors with نے:** میں نے فیصلہ کیا (فیصلہ is masculine), طلبہ نے بہت محنت کی (محنت is
  feminine). "محنت کیا" is a very common slip.
- **No paragraphs**, which makes W2 hard to reward however good the linking words are.
- **No time left to check.** Five minutes spent on your own list of usual errors catches more than a
  general reread.
