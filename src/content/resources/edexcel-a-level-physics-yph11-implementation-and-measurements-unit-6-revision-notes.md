Condensed notes for section **6.4 Implementation and Measurements**, Unit 6: Practical Skills in Physics II, Pearson Edexcel IAL Physics (YPH11), Issue 3. A full worked critique is in the [Unit 6 implementation and measurements study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-physics-yph11-implementation-and-measurements-unit-6/); questions are in the [Unit 6 implementation and measurements practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-physics-yph11-implementation-and-measurements-unit-6-practice/).

## What you are given and what you do

You get **partial details** of how an experiment was carried out, sometimes with results. You:

1. Suggest improvements, possibly with additional apparatus, saying which error each reduces.
2. Comment on the number of readings.
3. Comment on the range.
4. Identify and/or round incorrect figures in a table.
5. Identify and/or amend incorrect units.
6. Identify and check an inconsistent reading.

New compared with Unit 3: **amending units** and **rounding figures yourself**.

## Units: the rules

| Rule | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| Column headings are quantity / unit | t (s), Time in seconds | t / s |
| Values match the heading's unit | V / mV with values 6.00, 4.90 | V / V |
| A log has no unit | ln V / V | ln (V / V) |
| Squared quantities square the unit | T² / s | T² / s² |
| Reciprocals invert the unit | 1/V / m³ | V⁻¹ / m⁻³ |
| Derived units correct | ρ / Ω m⁻¹ | ρ / Ω m |
| Time constant is a time | RC = 50 s⁻¹ | RC = 50 s |
| Gradient unit = y unit ÷ x unit | gradient of ln V against t in s | gradient in s⁻¹ |

## Significant figures: rounding a table

- Raw readings: same number of decimal places as the instrument's resolution, throughout the column.
- An entry with **too many** figures (2.696 V from a meter reading to 0.01 V) is rounded: 2.70 V.
- An entry with **too few** figures (4.9 V) should show the instrument's resolution: 4.90 V if that was the reading.
- Processed values: usually 3 significant figures; a mix of 3 and 4 is accepted across a power of ten.
- Averages: no more decimal places than the readings averaged.

## Checking an inconsistent reading

1. Choose the test the physics suggests: constant ratio, constant difference, constant difference of logs, straight line.
2. Calculate it for every row.
3. Name the odd row and calculate the expected value.
4. Suggest a cause (timing delay, misread scale, wrong range, a variable changed).
5. Check it: repeat that reading, or repeat the run; exclude only with a reason.

Worked reminder: an exponential decay falls by the same **fraction** in equal times, so ln y falls by the same **amount**. Counts of 800, 640, 470, 410, 328 every 30 s: ratios 0.80, 0.73, 0.87, 0.80. The 470 is odd; expected 640 × 0.80 = 512.

## Improvements bank (Units 4 and 5 contexts)

| Weakness | Improvement | Error reduced |
|---|---|---|
| Diameter of a large cylinder judged with a rule held across it | Set squares either side against a fixed rule; repeat at different orientations | Parallax; measuring a chord, not the diameter |
| Oscillations timed from the turning point | Fiducial marker at the equilibrium position | Uncertain start and stop instant |
| Few oscillations timed | Time 20 and divide | Reaction time as a fraction of total |
| Stopwatch and meter read by two people | Data logger | Delay between calling time and reading |
| Short time constant timed by hand | Larger R, or data logger | Timing uncertainty |
| No background count | Measure background over at least 10 minutes and subtract the rate | Systematic overestimate of count rate |
| Gas readings taken immediately after compression | Wait for temperature to settle | Temperature change during readings |
| Heating with no lid or insulation | Insulate the beaker or block, use a lid | Energy lost to surroundings |
| Collision speeds from a stopwatch | Light gates or video analysis with a scale | Reaction time; timing short intervals |

## Number and range

- At least 6 values of the independent variable; more for curves.
- For a decay or discharge, cover at least two to three time constants or half-lives.
- For oscillations, cover a wide range of masses or lengths (for example a factor of five).
- "More readings" and "wider range" are different comments: say which is needed.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Partial details | A description of an experiment with steps or data left out |
| Fiducial marker | A fixed point used to start and stop timing |
| Data logger | A device recording readings automatically at set intervals |
| Inconsistent reading | One that does not fit the pattern of the others |
| Time constant | RC, the time for V to fall to 1/e of its starting value |
| Half-life | Time for the activity (or count rate) to halve |
| Background count rate | Count rate with the source removed |
| Resolution | Smallest interval an instrument can show |

## Exam traps

- Writing a unit after a log column heading.
- Quoting RC or T² with the wrong unit.
- Saying a value has the "wrong significant figures" without writing the corrected value.
- Calling a point anomalous because it "looks off" with no calculation.
- Suggesting set squares or a marker without saying how they are used.
- Ignoring a missing background correction in a radioactivity table.

## Self-test

**1.** Correct the heading "ln T / s".
**2.** A meter reads to 0.01 A. Round 0.4672 A for the table.
**3.** A column labelled "1/p / Pa" should read what?
**4.** State the unit of the gradient of a graph of ln V against t.
**5.** Counts every 20 s: 900, 720, 576, 520, 369. Which is inconsistent?
**6.** Using Question 5, what value is expected?
**7.** How do set squares help measure a cylinder's diameter?
**8.** Why is a fiducial marker placed at the equilibrium position?
**9.** Timing 10 oscillations of 1.2 s with 0.2 s reaction uncertainty gives what percentage uncertainty?
**10.** A discharge is followed for one time constant only. Comment.
**11.** Two students read a stopwatch and a meter together. Suggest an improvement.
**12.** A table gives T² in s. Amend it.

### Answers

**1.** ln (T / s).
**2.** 0.47 A.
**3.** p⁻¹ / Pa⁻¹.
**4.** s⁻¹.
**5.** 520: the first two ratios are 0.80, but 520 ÷ 576 = 0.90 and 369 ÷ 520 = 0.71.
**6.** 576 × 0.80 = 461 (to 3 s.f.).
**7.** Their vertical edges touch the widest points and transfer them to the rule at right angles, avoiding parallax.
**8.** The object moves fastest there, so the instant it passes is sharpest.
**9.** 0.2 ÷ 12 × 100% = 1.7%.
**10.** The range is too small to test the exponential; continue to about three time constants.
**11.** Use a data logger to record p.d. against time automatically.
**12.** T² / s².
