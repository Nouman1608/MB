This guide covers sub-topic **7.5 Absorption** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Statements 1 and 2 are Core, so every candidate needs them. Statements 3 to 5 (villi and microvilli, the structure of a villus, and the roles of capillaries and lacteals) are Supplement content, so they are **Extended only** (Papers 2 and 4).

The [human nutrition study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-human-nutrition/) states these points briefly. This page explains them: why absorption happens in the small intestine, how water is handled along the gut, how each feature of a villus speeds up absorption, and what happens to each product after it is absorbed. Condense it with the [absorption revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-absorption-revision-notes/) and test yourself with the [absorption practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-absorption-practice/).

## What 7.5 asks you to do

| Statement | Content | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| 7.5.1 | State that the small intestine is the region where nutrients are absorbed | Core |
| 7.5.2 | State that most water is absorbed from the small intestine but that some is also absorbed from the colon | Core |
| 7.5.3 | Explain the significance of villi and microvilli in increasing the internal surface area of the small intestine | Extended only |
| 7.5.4 | Describe the structure of a villus | Extended only |
| 7.5.5 | Describe the roles of capillaries and lacteals in villi | Extended only |

Absorption is defined in 7.2 as **the movement of nutrients from the intestines into the blood**. Use that wording when a question asks what absorption is.

## Where absorption happens (Core)

The **small intestine** is the region where nutrients are absorbed. This makes sense once you link it to chemical digestion: digestion is completed in the small intestine, so this is where the small soluble molecules (simple sugars, amino acids, fatty acids and glycerol) are finally available. Mineral ions and vitamins from food are absorbed here too.

Nutrients are not absorbed in the stomach in any significant amount, and by the time food reaches the colon almost all the useful nutrients have already gone.

### Water along the gut (Core)

Water enters the gut in drinks, in food, and in the digestive juices themselves: saliva, gastric juice, pancreatic juice and bile are all watery. That water has to be taken back.

| Region | Water absorbed |
|---|---|
| Small intestine | **most** of the water |
| Colon | **some** of the remaining water |

The remains entering the colon are still watery. As they pass along it, some water is absorbed, so the faeces stored in the rectum are semi-solid. If material moves through the colon too quickly, less water is absorbed and the faeces are watery.

## Villi and microvilli (Extended only)

The rate of absorption depends on how much surface is in contact with the digested food. The small intestine increases its **internal surface area** in two stages:

1. Its inner wall is covered in millions of tiny finger-like projections called **villi** (singular: villus).
2. The epithelial cells covering each villus have tiny folds of their cell membrane called **microvilli** on the surface facing the gut.

Both multiply the area through which molecules can pass. A larger surface area means more molecules can be absorbed at the same time, so absorption is faster and more complete before the food moves on.

**"Explain the significance"** needs that chain: villi and microvilli → much larger internal surface area → more molecules absorbed per second → faster, more efficient absorption.

## Structure of a villus (Extended only)

| Feature | How it helps absorption |
|---|---|
| Finger-like shape projecting into the gut | increases surface area in contact with digested food |
| Wall one cell thick (a single layer of epithelial cells) | short distance for molecules to travel to the blood |
| Microvilli on the epithelial cells | increase the surface area even further |
| Dense network of blood capillaries just under the epithelium | absorbs sugars and amino acids; blood flow carries them away, keeping a concentration gradient |
| Central lacteal | absorbs the products of fat digestion |

### Annotated model answer

**Describe the structure of a villus.** **[4]**

> A villus is a finger-like projection of the wall of the small intestine [1]. It is covered by a single layer of epithelial cells, which have microvilli on their surface [1]. Inside, just below the epithelium, is a network of blood capillaries [1]. In the centre of the villus is a lacteal [1].

Why it scores: each sentence names a feature and its position. A list of words ("capillaries, lacteal, microvilli") with no structure scores less.

## Capillaries and lacteals (Extended only)

| Vessel in the villus | What it absorbs | Where the products go |
|---|---|---|
| Blood capillaries | glucose and amino acids (also water and mineral ions) | into the blood, which drains into the hepatic portal vein and goes to the liver |
| Lacteal | fatty acids and glycerol, the products of fat digestion | into the lymph in the lacteal, which later drains into the blood |

The blood in the capillaries is constantly flowing away. This keeps the concentration of glucose and amino acids in the blood lower than in the gut, so they continue to move in. Molecules cross the epithelium by diffusion and also by active transport, which you met in topic 3.

### Worked example: a "suggest" question

**Some people have a disease that damages and flattens the villi. Suggest and explain one effect on the person.** **[3]**

- Flattened villi give a smaller internal surface area [1].
- So fewer digested nutrients are absorbed into the blood [1].
- The person may lose weight, or lack energy, because fewer nutrients reach the body cells [1].

Any sensible consequence of reduced absorption is credited, as long as it follows from the smaller surface area.

### Worked example: linking the stages

**A meal contains starch, protein and fat. Name the products of digestion absorbed by the capillaries and the lacteals.**

- Starch → glucose → absorbed by the **capillaries**.
- Protein → amino acids → absorbed by the **capillaries**.
- Fat → fatty acids and glycerol → absorbed by the **lacteal**.

## Common mistakes

- Saying nutrients are absorbed in the stomach or the large intestine. They are absorbed in the small intestine.
- Saying "all" or "most" water is absorbed in the colon. Most is absorbed in the small intestine; some in the colon.
- (Extended) Describing villi and microvilli as the same thing. Villi are projections of the gut wall; microvilli are on the surface of each epithelial cell.
- (Extended) Writing "villi have thin walls" without "one cell thick" or "short diffusion distance".
- (Extended) Saying lacteals absorb glucose, or capillaries absorb fatty acids.
- (Extended) Calling the lacteal a blood vessel. It is a lymph vessel.
- Writing "villi increase absorption" without the words **surface area**.

## Exam technique for 7.5

- **State** (Core): small intestine for nutrients; small intestine for most water, colon for some.
- **Explain** (Extended): give the full chain from structure to surface area to rate.
- **Describe** (Extended): feature + position for each mark.
- **Describe the roles**: vessel + what it absorbs, for both capillaries and lacteals.
