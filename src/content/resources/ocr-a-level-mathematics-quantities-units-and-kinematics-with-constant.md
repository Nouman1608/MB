This guide covers quantities, units and kinematics with constant acceleration in OCR A Level Mathematics A (H240), content references 3.01a to 3.01c and 3.02a to 3.02e of the OCR specification, Version 3.1 (August 2026). Two items are A Level only: the unit for moment (3.01c) and the extension of the constant acceleration formulae to two dimensions with vectors (3.02e). The specification places Mechanics in Paper 3. ## Quantities and units (3.01a to 3.01c)

The SI system rests on three base quantities that you need: length in metres (m), time in seconds (s) and mass in kilograms (kg). They are mutually independent. You cannot write one as a combination of the other two.

Every other mechanics unit is built from these. The specification lists these derived units.

| Quantity | Unit | Built from |
|---|---|---|
| Velocity | m s⁻¹ (or m/s) | length ÷ time |
| Acceleration | m s⁻² (or m/s²) | velocity ÷ time |
| Force | newton, N | mass × acceleration, so kg m s⁻² |
| Weight | N | it is a force, not a mass |
| Moment (A Level only) | N m | force × perpendicular distance |

If a question gives a quantity without a unit, you should be able to supply one. If it gives a quantity in other units, convert to SI before you substitute into any formula.

| Convert | Method |
|---|---|
| km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹ | multiply by 1000 ÷ 3600, so divide by 3.6 |
| m s⁻¹ to km h⁻¹ | multiply by 3.6 |
| g to kg, cm to m | divide by 1000, divide by 100 |
| minutes to seconds | multiply by 60 |

**Example 1.** A cyclist travels at 21.6 km h⁻¹. A spanner is 35 cm long and a force of 48 N acts at right angles to it at its end. Express the speed in m s⁻¹ and give the moment about the other end.

```
21.6 km/h = 21.6 ÷ 3.6 = 6 m/s
35 cm = 0.35 m
moment = 48 × 0.35 = 16.8 N m
```

The speed is **6 m s⁻¹** and the moment is **16.8 N m**. Notice that the unit of moment is a product, newtons times metres. It is not a newton per metre.

## The language of kinematics (3.02a)

Kinematics describes motion without asking what causes it. You need precise terms.

| Term | Meaning | Vector or scalar |
|---|---|---|
| Position | where the object is, measured from a fixed origin | vector |
| Displacement | change in position, from start to finish in a straight line | vector |
| Distance travelled | total length of the path actually covered | scalar |
| Velocity | rate of change of displacement | vector |
| Speed | rate of change of distance travelled; the magnitude of velocity | scalar |
| Acceleration | rate of change of velocity | vector |

In one dimension a vector is a signed number. Choose a positive direction and keep to it. A velocity of −3 m s⁻¹ means 3 m s⁻¹ in the negative direction. Speed is never negative.

An equation of motion is an equation that links the quantities describing the motion. The constant acceleration formulae later in this guide are equations of motion.

Average speed is total distance travelled divided by total time. Average velocity is displacement divided by time. They can differ in size and in sign.

**Example 2.** A particle starts at O on a straight line. It moves 12 m in the positive direction, then 5 m in the negative direction, then 9 m more in the negative direction. The whole journey takes 20 s. Find its final position, the distance travelled, its average speed and its average velocity.

```
positions: 0 -> 12 -> 7 -> -2
displacement = -2 m
distance = 12 + 5 + 9 = 26 m
average speed = 26 / 20 = 1.3 m/s
average velocity = -2 / 20 = -0.1 m/s
```

The particle ends **2 m on the negative side of O**, having travelled **26 m**. The average speed is **1.3 m s⁻¹** and the average velocity is **−0.1 m s⁻¹**.

## Graphs in kinematics (3.02b and 3.02c)

For motion in a straight line, three facts do most of the work.

| On a graph of | Gradient gives | Area under it gives |
|---|---|---|
| displacement against time | velocity | not used |
| velocity against time | acceleration | displacement |

A horizontal line on a displacement-time graph means the object is at rest. A straight sloping line means constant velocity. A horizontal line on a velocity-time graph means constant velocity, and a straight sloping line means constant acceleration.

Areas below the time axis count as negative displacement. For total distance travelled, add the sizes of the areas and ignore the signs.

**Example 3.** A particle's velocity increases uniformly from 0 to 12 m s⁻¹ in the first 5 s, stays at 12 m s⁻¹ until t = 11, then decreases uniformly to −4 m s⁻¹ at t = 15. Find the acceleration in each stage, the time it changes direction, the displacement at t = 15 and the distance travelled.

```
stage 1: a = 12 / 5 = 2.4 m/s²
stage 2: a = 0
stage 3: a = (-4 - 12) / 4 = -4 m/s²
velocity is zero when 12 - 4(t - 11) = 0, so t = 14
areas: 0.5 × 5 × 12 = 30
       6 × 12 = 72
       0.5 × 3 × 12 = 18
       -0.5 × 1 × 4 = -2
displacement = 30 + 72 + 18 - 2 = 118 m
distance = 30 + 72 + 18 + 2 = 122 m
```

The accelerations are **2.4, 0 and −4 m s⁻²**. It changes direction at **t = 14**. The displacement is **118 m** and the distance travelled is **122 m**.

Sketch the graph first, then split it into triangles, rectangles and trapezia.

## The constant acceleration formulae (3.02d)

When acceleration is constant and motion is in a straight line, five quantities describe it.

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| s | displacement from the start |
| u | initial velocity |
| v | final velocity |
| a | acceleration |
| t | time taken |

The specification lists these formulae.

| Formula | Missing variable |
|---|---|
| v = u + at | s |
| s = ut + ½at² | v |
| s = ½(u + v)t | a |
| v² = u² + 2as | t |
| s = vt − ½at² | u |

Each formula leaves out exactly one variable. To choose one, list the three quantities you know and the one you want, then pick the formula missing the fifth.

You may be asked to derive these formulae, and the specification names three routes.

**By a velocity-time graph.** A straight line from (0, u) to (t, v) has gradient a, so v = u + at. The area under it is a trapezium, which gives s = ½(u + v)t.

**By substitution.** Put v = u + at into s = ½(u + v)t:

```
s = ½(u + u + at)t = ½(2u + at)t = ut + ½at²
```

To get s = vt − ½at², replace u by v − at in s = ut + ½at²:

```
s = (v - at)t + ½at² = vt - at² + ½at² = vt - ½at²
```

For v² = u² + 2as, find t = (v − u)/a from the first formula and substitute it into s = ½(u + v)t. This gives 2as = (u + v)(v − u) = v² − u².

**By integration.** Constant a gives v = ∫a dt = at + c. Since v = u when t = 0, c = u. Integrating again gives s = ut + ½at², taking s = 0 when t = 0.

**Example 4.** A van moving at 8 m s⁻¹ accelerates uniformly at 1.6 m s⁻² over a distance of 60 m. Find its final speed and the time taken.

```
u = 8, a = 1.6, s = 60, want v: use v² = u² + 2as
v² = 64 + 2 × 1.6 × 60 = 256, so v = 16
time: use v = u + at, so 16 = 8 + 1.6t, t = 5
```

The final speed is **16 m s⁻¹** and the time is **5 s**.

**Example 5.** A particle is at O moving along a line with velocity 6 m s⁻¹ and acceleration −2 m s⁻². Find when it is 5 m from O in the positive direction, when it returns to O, and the distance travelled up to the return.

```
s = 6t - t²
s = 5: t² - 6t + 5 = 0, so t = 1 or t = 5
s = 0: t(6 - t) = 0, so t = 6
v = 0 when 6 - 2t = 0, so t = 3, where s = 18 - 9 = 9
distance up to t = 6 is 9 + 9 = 18 m
```

It is 5 m from O at **t = 1 and t = 5**, returns at **t = 6**, and has travelled **18 m**. A quadratic in t often gives two answers. The particle passes the same point going out and coming back.

## Two dimensions with vectors (3.02e, A Level only)

For constant acceleration in a plane, apply the formulae to vectors. The specification gives these.

```
v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
s = ½(u + v)t
s = vt - ½at²
```

Questions may use column vectors or i, j notation. The vector version of v² = u² + 2as, with dot products, is excluded. The i and j components are independent, so each component obeys the one-dimensional formulae with its own u, v and a.

Speed is the magnitude of the velocity vector. If the object does not start at the origin, add its initial position vector to s to get its position.

**Example 6.** A particle has initial velocity (2, −3) m s⁻¹ and constant acceleration (0.5, 1.5) m s⁻². Find its velocity, speed and displacement after 4 s, and the time at which it moves parallel to the x-axis.

```
v = (2, -3) + 4(0.5, 1.5) = (4, 3)
speed = sqrt(4² + 3²) = 5
s = 4(2, -3) + ½ × 16 × (0.5, 1.5) = (8, -12) + (4, 12) = (12, 0)
check: ½(u + v)t = ½(6, 0) × 4 = (12, 0)
parallel to x-axis: -3 + 1.5t = 0, so t = 2
```

After 4 s the velocity is **(4, 3) m s⁻¹**, the speed is **5 m s⁻¹** and the displacement is **(12, 0) m**. It moves parallel to the x-axis at **t = 2**, when its velocity is (3, 0) m s⁻¹.

## Common errors

- Using the formulae when acceleration is not constant. State the assumption before you start.
- Mixing km h⁻¹ and m s⁻¹ in one equation.
- Treating distance as displacement. They differ whenever the motion reverses.
- Taking the area under a velocity-time graph as distance when part of it lies below the axis.
- Dropping the sign of a deceleration. If motion is in the positive direction and the object slows down, a is negative.
- Missing that "starts from rest" means u = 0, and "comes to rest" means v = 0.
- Quoting a final answer without a unit, or giving a speed as a negative number.
