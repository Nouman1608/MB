These questions cover section **4.5.1 Homeostasis** of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, for first
teaching from 2016, with examples drawn from the rest of Topic 5. The content is on Paper 2. One part, labelled
**Higher tier only**, asks you to explain a temperature-control mechanism. All data are invented for practice.

Learn the content first in the [Homeostasis study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-homeostasis-aqa-gcse-biology/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-homeostasis-aqa-gcse-biology-revision-notes/).
For questions on all of Topic 5, use the [Homeostasis and response practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-homeostasis-and-response-practice/);
the questions below are different from those.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Complete each sentence.

**(a)** Receptors are cells that ______ stimuli. **[1]**
**(b)** Coordination centres ______ and process information from receptors. **[1]**
**(c)** Effectors are muscles or ______. **[1]**

**2.** From this list, choose the **two** effectors: sweat gland; temperature receptor in the skin; spinal cord;
skeletal muscle; thermoregulatory centre. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why homeostasis is needed for enzymes to work well. **[2]**

**4.** State whether each change is internal or external.

**(a)** Getting into a cold bath **[1]**
**(b)** Glucose being absorbed from the gut after a meal **[1]**
**(c)** Muscles releasing energy by respiration during a race **[1]**

**5.**

**(a)** Give **one** difference between a nervous response and a chemical response. **[1]**
**(b)** Name what carries hormones around the body. **[1]**

### Section B: longer and structured questions

**6.** A swimmer enters cold water. Her core body temperature is recorded every 10 minutes. She starts
shivering at 10 minutes.

| Time (minutes) | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core temperature (°C) | 37.0 | 36.6 | 36.2 | 36.4 | 36.8 |

**(a)** Describe how her core temperature changes. Use the data. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the receptors, the coordination centre and **one** effector involved in her response to the cold. **[3]**
**(c)** (Higher tier only) Explain why her core temperature rises after 20 minutes. **[2]**

**7.** After a meal, a person's blood glucose concentration rises. The pancreas releases insulin, and liver and
muscle cells take up glucose and store it as glycogen. The concentration falls back to normal.

Identify in this example:

**(a)** the stimulus **[1]**
**(b)** the coordination centre **[1]**
**(c)** the effectors **[1]**
**(d)** the response **[1]**

**8.** Explain why the body must control its water levels. **[3]**

**9.** A heating system keeps a room at 20 °C. A thermometer measures the room temperature. A control box
compares the reading with 20 °C. If the room is too cold, the control box switches on a heater.

**(a)** Match each part of the heating system to the part of a homeostatic control system. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how the heating system shows the idea that a response restores the optimum. **[1]**

**10.** A student writes: "Homeostasis is keeping your body temperature the same." Give **two** ways to improve
this definition. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why a control system that responded to low blood glucose by lowering it further would harm the
body. **[2]**

**12.** Explain how control systems in the human body maintain optimum conditions. Use examples in your
answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Detect [1]
**(b)** Receive [1]
**(c)** Glands [1]

**2.** Sweat gland [1]; skeletal muscle [1]
*Examiner insight:* The spinal cord and thermoregulatory centre are coordination centres; choosing either cancels a correct answer.

**3.** Enzymes work best at an optimum temperature (and conditions) [1]; homeostasis keeps conditions close to the optimum so enzymes are not slowed or denatured [1]

**4. (a)** External [1]
**(b)** Internal [1]
**(c)** Internal [1]

**5. (a)** Any one: nervous responses are faster; nervous responses are shorter-lived; nervous responses use electrical impulses along neurones while chemical responses use hormones [1]
**(b)** The blood [1]

**6. (a)** The temperature falls from 37.0 °C to 36.2 °C over the first 20 minutes [1]; then rises to 36.8 °C by 40 minutes [1]
**(b)** Receptors: temperature receptors in the skin (and in the thermoregulatory centre) [1]; coordination centre: thermoregulatory centre in the brain [1]; effector: skeletal muscles (shivering), or blood vessels in the skin [1]
**(c)** Shivering is contraction of skeletal muscles, which needs respiration [1]; respiration transfers energy that warms the body (and vasoconstriction reduces energy transfer from the skin) [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Skin" alone is not an effector; name the muscles or the blood vessels.

**7. (a)** A rise in blood glucose concentration [1]
**(b)** The pancreas [1]
**(c)** Liver and muscle cells [1]
**(d)** Glucose moves from the blood into the cells and is stored as glycogen [1]

**8.** Body cells gain or lose water by osmosis [1]; if they gain or lose too much water they do not function efficiently [1]; so water levels are kept close to the optimum [1]

**9. (a)** Thermometer = receptor [1]; control box = coordination centre [1]; heater = effector [1]
**(b)** When the room is too cold the heater warms it, moving the temperature back towards 20 °C [1]

**10.** Any two, one mark each: use the word "regulation"; include all internal conditions, such as blood glucose and water levels, not just temperature; say the aim is optimum conditions for function; say it responds to internal and external changes [2]
*Examiner insight:* "Add more detail" is not an improvement; each mark needs a specific change to the wording.

**11.** Blood glucose would move further from the optimum [1]; cells would not have enough glucose for respiration, so they could not function properly [1]

**12.** One mark for each point:
- homeostasis keeps internal conditions at optimum levels for enzymes and cell functions [1]
- receptors detect changes, for example temperature receptors in the skin [1]
- coordination centres, such as the brain, spinal cord or pancreas, receive and process the information [1]
- effectors, muscles or glands, respond to restore optimum levels, for example sweat glands on a hot day [1]
- control may be nervous (fast, short-lived) or chemical, using hormones (slower, longer-lasting) [1]
- a worked example with all parts, for example rising blood glucose detected by the pancreas, insulin released, glucose taken into liver and muscle cells [1]
*Examiner insight:* An answer that names the parts without examples, or gives examples without the parts, cannot reach the top marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying receptors respond, or effectors detect.
- Naming an organ instead of the muscle or gland that acts.
- Describing data without quoting values and times.
- Linking water control to enzymes instead of osmosis.
- Definitions that mention only temperature.
