These questions cover section **3.2.3.2 Uses of chlorine and chlorate(I)** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 1. Learn the ideas first in the [Uses of chlorine and chlorate(I) study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-uses-of-chlorine-and-chlorate-i/) and the [Uses of chlorine and chlorate(I) revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-uses-of-chlorine-and-chlorate-i-revision-notes/). Use Mᵣ values Cl₂ = 71.0 and NaClO = 74.5, and a molar gas volume of 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** Chlorine reacts with water to form chloride ions and chlorate(I) ions.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** Give the oxidation state of chlorine in Cl₂, Cl⁻ and ClO⁻. **[1]**

**2.** State what is meant by disproportionation and show that the reaction in Question 1 is an example. **[3]**

**3.** Chlorine is bubbled through cold dilute aqueous sodium hydroxide.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the compound that acts as a bleach in the solution formed. **[1]**

**(c)** State one use of the solution formed. **[1]**

**4.** A student writes: "Chlorine reacts with water to give only hydrochloric acid." Identify the error and state the other product. **[2]**

**5.** A bottle of chlorine water kept in bright sunlight slowly produces bubbles of gas.

**(a)** Name the gas. **[1]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**6.** Chlorine is added to drinking water.

**(a)** State the purpose of adding chlorine. **[1]**

**(b)** Give one risk of using chlorine in water treatment and explain why the benefit is judged to outweigh it. **[3]**

**7.** Explain how the reaction of chlorine with water produces a species that can kill bacteria. **[2]**

**8.** Calculate the mass of sodium chlorate(I) formed when 7.10 g of chlorine reacts completely with cold dilute sodium hydroxide, and the percentage yield if 6.20 g is obtained. **[4]**

**9.** What volume of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide solution reacts completely with 0.0100 mol of chlorine? Calculate also the volume of chlorine at the stated molar volume. **[4]**

**10.** A water company adds chlorine at 0.50 mg per dm³. Calculate the mass of chlorine, in grams, required to treat 5.00 × 10⁴ dm³. **[2]**

**11.** Some people object to the addition of fluoride to public water supplies. Give two arguments that could be used in a discussion of whether chemicals should be added to water supplies. **[2]**

**12.** The reaction of chlorine with hot, concentrated sodium hydroxide produces a different product from the reaction with cold, dilute sodium hydroxide. State why bleach is made using cold, dilute alkali. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HClO + HCl, or Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ 2H⁺ + Cl⁻ + ClO⁻ [1 for correct species, 1 for balancing and the equilibrium sign]

**(b)** Cl₂ is 0, Cl⁻ is –1, ClO⁻ is +1 [1]. **[3]**

**2.** Disproportionation is a reaction in which the same element is simultaneously oxidised and reduced [1]; chlorine goes from 0 in Cl₂ to +1 in ClO⁻ (oxidised) [1]; and from 0 to –1 in Cl⁻ (reduced) [1]. **[3]**

**3. (a)** Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O (or Cl₂ + 2OH⁻ → Cl⁻ + ClO⁻ + H₂O) [1]

**(b)** Sodium chlorate(I), NaClO [1]

**(c)** Any one: household bleach, disinfectant, treatment of swimming pool water [1]. **[3]**

**4.** The reaction also forms chloric(I) acid, HClO (chlorate(I) ions) [1]; the reaction is an equilibrium and does not give only HCl [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Oxygen [1]

**(b)** 2Cl₂ + 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4Cl⁻ + O₂ [2: 1 for the correct species, 1 for balancing]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** To kill bacteria and other microorganisms [1]

**(b)** Chlorine is toxic, or it forms chlorinated hydrocarbons that may be carcinogenic [1]; the risk is small because only a very low concentration is used [1], and the benefit of preventing waterborne diseases such as cholera and typhoid is much greater [1]. **[4]**

**7.** Chlorine disproportionates in water to form chlorate(I) ions and chloric(I) acid [1]; chlorate(I) is a strong oxidising agent and kills bacteria by oxidising them [1]. **[2]**

**8.** n(Cl₂) = 7.10 ÷ 71.0 = 0.100 mol [1]; n(NaClO) = 0.100 mol, so mass = 0.100 × 74.5 = **7.45 g** [1]; percentage yield = 6.20 ÷ 7.45 × 100 [1] = **83.2%** [1]. **[4]**

**9.** The equation is Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O, so n(NaOH) = 2 × 0.0100 = 0.0200 mol [1]; volume = 0.0200 ÷ 0.200 = 0.100 dm³ = **100 cm³** [1]; volume of chlorine = 0.0100 × 24.0 = **0.240 dm³** (240 cm³) [2]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Use the mole ratio from the balanced equation before you do any volume calculation.

**10.** mass = 5.00 × 10⁴ × 0.50 mg = 2.5 × 10⁴ mg [1] = **25 g** [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Any two from: fluoride helps to protect teeth against decay (benefit); too much fluoride can damage teeth or health (risk); people who do not want it have no choice, so it raises a question of consent; the benefit may be achieved by other means such as toothpaste [2]. **[2]**

**12.** Cold, dilute alkali gives chlorate(I), which is the bleaching agent, whereas hot, concentrated alkali gives a product in which chlorine has a different oxidation state [1]. **[1]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting the chlorate(I) ion in the reaction with water.
- Using → instead of ⇌ for chlorine and water.
- Not giving oxidation states in a disproportionation answer.
- Leaving out balancing of 4H⁺ and 4Cl⁻ in the sunlight equation.
- Giving only the benefit or only the risk in a "discuss" question.
