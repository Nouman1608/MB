## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Name the sugar and the base found in ATP. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why ATP is described as a nucleotide derivative. **[2]**

**3.** Name the enzyme that catalyses the hydrolysis of ATP and name the products. **[2]**

**4.** State the type of reaction by which ATP is resynthesised, and name the enzyme involved. **[2]**

**5.** Give the two processes during which ATP is resynthesised. **[1]**

**6.** A student writes: "ATP stores large amounts of energy in its bonds, which is released when they break." Rewrite the statement so that it would gain credit. **[2]**

**7.** Explain what is meant by the term *phosphorylation* and give its usual effect on the molecule that is phosphorylated. **[2]**

**8.** Give two reasons why ATP, rather than glucose, is used as the immediate source of energy in cells. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**9.** Use the data supplied. Hydrolysis of ATP releases 30.5 kJ per mole. A reaction in a cell requires 68 kJ per mole of product.

**(a)** Calculate the minimum number of moles of ATP that must be hydrolysed per mole of product. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy released as heat per mole of product. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage of the energy from ATP hydrolysis that is transferred to the reaction. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**

**10.** Use the data supplied. A muscle cell contains 4.5 × 10⁹ ATP molecules. At rest it hydrolyses 1.5 × 10⁷ ATP molecules per second; during contraction the rate rises to 9.0 × 10⁷ per second.

**(a)** Calculate the time taken to use the whole ATP pool once at rest and during contraction. **[2]**

**(b)** A poison stops ATP synthase working. Suggest why the muscle stops contracting within a short time. **[2]**

**11.** Root hair cells absorb mineral ions from soil by active transport, against a concentration gradient. A student measured ion uptake by root tissue in oxygenated water and in water with no oxygen.

| Condition | Ion uptake (arbitrary units per hour) |
|---|---|
| Oxygen present | 48 |
| No oxygen | 9 |

**(a)** Explain the difference in uptake. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest why some uptake continued with no oxygen. **[1]**

**12.** Some cells, such as those lining the small intestine, contain very large numbers of mitochondria. Explain how this links to the role of ATP in these cells. **[3]**

**13.** Explain how the properties of ATP make it suited to its role as the immediate energy source in cells. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Ribose [1]; adenine [1]. **[2]**

**2.** It is formed from a nucleotide: ribose, adenine and a phosphate group [1], with two additional phosphate groups attached, giving three in total [1]. **[2]**

**3.** ATP hydrolase [1]; ADP and inorganic phosphate (Pi) [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Condensation [1]; ATP synthase [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Respiration and photosynthesis (both needed) [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Hydrolysis of ATP to ADP and Pi releases energy [1], which can be coupled to energy-requiring reactions; ATP releases energy in small amounts rather than storing large amounts [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Mark schemes usually reject "energy stored in bonds" and "bonds break and release energy". Attach the energy release to the hydrolysis reaction.

**7.** Phosphorylation is the addition of a phosphate group (from ATP) to another molecule [1]; it often makes that molecule more reactive [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Any two: hydrolysis is a single reaction, so energy is released quickly [1]; energy is released in small, manageable amounts, so little is wasted as heat [1]; ATP is rapidly resynthesised from ADP and Pi; Pi can phosphorylate other molecules. **[2]**

**9. (a)** 68 ÷ 30.5 = 2.23, so **3 moles** [1].

**(b)** (3 × 30.5) − 68 = 91.5 − 68 = **23.5 kJ** [1].

**(c)** 68 ÷ 91.5 × 100 [1] = **74%** [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Part (a) must be rounded up to a whole number. Using 2.23 moles in (b) and (c) gives answers that do not match the biology.

**10. (a)** At rest: 4.5 × 10⁹ ÷ 1.5 × 10⁷ = **300 s** [1]. During contraction: 4.5 × 10⁹ ÷ 9.0 × 10⁷ = **50 s** [1].

**(b)** ADP and Pi can no longer be recombined, so ATP is not resynthesised [1]; the small pool is used up within about 50 s of contraction, and without ATP hydrolysis there is no energy for contraction [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** With oxygen, aerobic respiration resynthesises ATP [1]. Active transport needs energy from ATP hydrolysis to move ions against their concentration gradient [1]. Without oxygen, much less ATP is made, so far fewer ions are taken up [1].

**(b)** A small amount of ATP can still be made without oxygen (or some ATP was already present in the cells), so some active transport continued; also accept some uptake by diffusion where a gradient exists [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Most ATP is resynthesised by ATP synthase in mitochondria during aerobic respiration [1]. These cells carry out a lot of active transport (or other energy-requiring processes) [1], which uses ATP hydrolysis, so many mitochondria provide the high rate of ATP resynthesis needed [1]. **[3]**

**13.** Hydrolysis of ATP is a single reaction catalysed by one enzyme, ATP hydrolase, so energy is released quickly where it is needed [1]. Each hydrolysis releases a small, manageable amount of energy, so little is wasted as heat [1]. ADP and Pi are rapidly recombined by ATP synthase, so the same molecules are recycled [1]. The Pi released can phosphorylate other molecules, making them more reactive [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Each property needs its consequence. "Releases small amounts of energy" alone is weaker than linking it to less energy being wasted.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying ATP stores energy in high-energy bonds.
- Swapping ATP hydrolase and ATP synthase.
- Calling the sugar deoxyribose.
- Leaving a fraction of a mole of ATP in a calculation.
- Forgetting to link oxygen to respiration and then to ATP in active transport questions.
- Listing properties of ATP without explaining why each helps.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which section of the AQA specification do these questions cover?**
Section 3.1.6 ATP of AQA AS and A-level Biology (7401/7402).

**Are these AQA past paper questions?**
No. They are original questions written for practice and do not copy the exam's structure, question count or mark tariffs.

**How do you answer questions about ATP in an unfamiliar process?**
Say that ATP is hydrolysed by ATP hydrolase, that the energy released is coupled to the process, and that ATP is resynthesised by ATP synthase during respiration.
