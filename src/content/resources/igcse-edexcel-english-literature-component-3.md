This guide covers **Component 3: Modern Drama and Literary Heritage
Texts (Non-Examined Assessment)**, for Pearson Edexcel International
GCSE English Literature (4ET1), first teaching 2016, current
specification Issue 3, August 2025. Component 3 is the coursework
alternative to
[Component 2](/resources/igcse-edexcel-english-literature-component-2/):
centres choose one route or the other for their candidates, and both
carry the same 40% weighting and cover the same content areas -- one
modern drama text and one literary heritage text.

## Where this fits in 4ET1

Component 1 (Poetry and Modern Prose) is compulsory for every candidate.
Centres then choose between Component 2, a timed written exam on a
modern drama text and a literary heritage text, and Component 3, covered
here: two teacher-devised assignments, completed independently under
supervised conditions, then internally marked and externally moderated
by Pearson. Both routes assess AO1 and AO2 throughout, with Component
3's Assignment B additionally assessing AO4 (context), matching
Component 2's coverage of the same assessment objectives across its two
sections.

## Specification coverage

**PEARSON EDEXCEL INTERNATIONAL GCSE ENGLISH LITERATURE (4ET1) — COMPONENT 3**

- Overall weighting — Component 3 represents 40% of the qualification,
60 raw marks, split evenly between Assignment A (30 marks) and
Assignment B (30 marks); internally set and assessed by the centre,
externally moderated by Pearson
- Assignment A: Modern Drama (30 marks) — one essay response on a
modern drama text chosen by the centre (learners may take part in
choosing the specific task); assessed against AO1 (15 marks, reading and
understanding, selecting and interpreting) and AO2 (15 marks, analysing
language, form and structure and their effects)
- Assignment B: Literary Heritage Texts (30 marks) — one essay response
on a literary heritage text chosen by the centre; assessed against AO1
(10 marks), AO2 (10 marks) and, unlike Assignment A, AO4 (10 marks,
understanding the significance and influence of the context in which
texts were written and received)
- Word count — an advisory word count of between 650 and 800 words
applies to each assignment, with no penalty specified for exceeding it,
though concise, well-controlled responses are expected to score more
efficiently than padded ones
- Conditions of assessment — learners must complete the work
independently; teachers may give general guidance on improvement only
at the draft stage, not detailed correction; learners sign an
authentication statement confirming the work is their own; no rewriting
is permitted once a piece has been marked; submissions may be handwritten
or word-processed; centres must retain candidate work securely for at
least six months after the relevant examination series

## How to approach it

Because Assignment B is the only place AO4 (context) is directly
assessed within Component 3, it is worth treating contextual knowledge
as a distinct, plannable element of that essay rather than background
colour. A strong Assignment B response integrates context precisely --
linking a specific historical, social or literary detail to a specific
textual moment -- rather than opening with a generic paragraph of
background before moving on to unrelated analysis.

Because teachers devise the exact task and learners "may participate in
the choice of tasks," candidates preparing for Component 3 should treat
their centre's specific set title as the actual target to revise for,
not a generic essay on the text as a whole; revision should focus on the
angle the set task asks about, whether that is a character, theme, or
dramatic technique.

The 650–800 word advisory range rewards precision. Since no penalty
applies for exceeding it, the real risk of writing well past 800 words
is losing focus and repeating points, not a formal deduction -- so
treat the range as a target for a tightly argued response rather than a
hard ceiling.

## Worked example: balancing Assignment A's two AOs

A candidate is writing on a modern drama text, focusing on how a
playwright builds tension in a key scene.

```
AO1 material:  what happens in the scene, and what the characters say
               and do -- the basic evidence the essay is built on
AO2 material:  HOW the playwright builds tension through specific
               dramatic techniques -- stage directions, short lines of
               dialogue, dramatic irony, pacing
Balance check: since AO1 and AO2 are weighted equally (15 marks each),
               an essay that only narrates the scene (AO1-heavy) or
               only lists techniques without evidence (AO2-heavy) will
               under-perform against one that pairs each technique
               with the specific textual evidence for it
```

Explicitly checking that every paragraph pairs a technique (AO2) with
the textual evidence for it (AO1) is a reliable way to keep the two
equally-weighted objectives in balance.

## Common mistakes

Writing generically about a text's themes rather than answering the
centre's specific set task. Treating Assignment A the same way as
Assignment B and forgetting to build in contextual analysis (AO4) where
Assignment B specifically requires it. Accepting detailed teacher
correction at draft stage, which conflicts with the specification's
requirement that guidance be general only, and can raise an
authentication concern. Writing a very long response in the belief that
length alone raises the mark, when precision and a clear line of
argument are what the assessment objectives reward.

## Frequently asked questions

**What do I submit for 4ET1 Component 3?**
Two 30-mark assignments: Assignment A on a modern drama text and Assignment B on a literary heritage text, internally assessed and externally moderated.

**Is Component 3 an alternative to the exam?**
Yes. You take either Component 2 (open-book exam) or Component 3; both are worth 60 marks and 40%.

**Which texts can I use?**
The same set-text lists as Component 2, for example An Inspector Calls or Macbeth.


## Quick revision checklist

- Confirm the centre's exact set task for each assignment before
revising, rather than preparing a generic essay on the whole text.
- For Assignment B specifically, plan at least one precise piece of
contextual knowledge linked to a specific textual moment.
- Pair every technique named (AO2) with the textual evidence for it
(AO1) in each paragraph.
- Treat 650–800 words as a target for a focused, well-argued response,
not a ceiling to reach or a floor to clear.
