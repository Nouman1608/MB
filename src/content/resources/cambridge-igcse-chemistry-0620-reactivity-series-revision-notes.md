**Syllabus:** Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620, 2026–2028, subtopic 9.4 Reactivity series.

## The order

**K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > C > Zn > Fe > H > Cu > Ag > Au**

- **Carbon** sits between aluminium and zinc.
- **Hydrogen** sits between iron and copper.
- Above hydrogen: react with dilute acids. Below hydrogen: no reaction with dilute acids.

## Named reactions (Core)

### Cold water: metal + water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen

| Metal | Key observations |
|---|---|
| K | floats; melts; moves very fast; lilac flame |
| Na | floats; melts into a ball; moves around; fizzes |
| Ca | sinks; steady fizzing; cloudy solution |

All three solutions are alkaline afterwards.

### Steam: magnesium + steam → magnesium oxide + hydrogen

- Mg + H₂O → MgO + H₂
- Bright white glow; white solid left.
- Magnesium reacts only very slowly with cold water.

### Dilute hydrochloric acid: metal + acid → salt + hydrogen

| Metal | Observation |
|---|---|
| Mg | vigorous fizzing |
| Zn | steady fizzing |
| Fe | slow fizzing |
| Cu, Ag, Au | no reaction |

Salts: MgCl₂, ZnCl₂, **FeCl₂** (iron(II) chloride).

## Deducing an order (Core)

**Fair comparison:** same mass and particle size of metal; same volume, concentration and starting temperature of acid or solution.

**Evidence of greater reactivity:**

- faster bubbling;
- more gas in a fixed time;
- shorter time for the metal to disappear;
- larger temperature rise;
- more displacement reactions in a grid.

**Answer frame:** "X is the most reactive because it gave the largest [measurement]."

## Displacement *(Extended)*

- A more reactive metal has a **greater tendency to form positive ions** (loses electrons more readily).
- It displaces a less reactive metal from a solution of its ions.

| Example | Ionic equation | Observations |
|---|---|---|
| zinc + copper(II) ions | Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu | pink-brown deposit; blue colour fades |
| magnesium + iron(II) ions | Mg + Fe²⁺ → Mg²⁺ + Fe | grey-black deposit; pale green colour fades |
| copper + silver ions | Cu + 2Ag⁺ → Cu²⁺ + 2Ag | grey crystals of silver; solution turns blue |

**Count the ticks:** in a grid of Mg, Zn, Fe, Cu and Ag with each other's ions, Mg gives 4 reactions, Zn 3, Fe 2, Cu 1, Ag 0.

**No reaction** when the metal added is *less* reactive than the metal in solution, for example copper in zinc sulfate.

## Aluminium *(Extended)*

- Aluminium is above zinc and iron but **appears unreactive**.
- A thin, unreactive **oxide layer** on its surface stops air, water and acid reaching the metal.
- With dilute acid there is often a slow start, then vigorous fizzing once the layer is removed.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reactivity series | metals listed in order of how readily they react |
| Displacement reaction | a more reactive element takes the place of a less reactive one in a compound |
| Effervescence | fizzing caused by a gas being produced |
| Positive ion | an atom that has lost one or more electrons |
| Oxidation | loss of electrons (the more reactive metal is oxidised in displacement) |
| Reduction | gain of electrons (the ions of the less reactive metal are reduced) |
| Oxide layer | the thin coating of aluminium oxide that protects aluminium |

## Exam traps

- **Copper, silver and gold do not react with dilute acid at all.** Do not write "slowly".
- **Steam gives an oxide;** cold water gives a hydroxide.
- **Iron with dilute hydrochloric acid gives FeCl₂.**
- **Silver ions are Ag⁺,** so balance with 2Ag⁺ and 2Ag when copper is the metal added.
- *(Extended)* **Describe the electron transfer:** the metal loses electrons; the ions gain them.
- *(Extended)* **Aluminium is reactive;** its oxide layer only makes it appear unreactive.
- **Unfair tests give unreliable orders.** Powder reacts faster than lumps of the same metal.

## Self-test

1. Write the reactivity series from potassium to gold, including carbon and hydrogen.
2. State **three** observations when sodium is added to cold water.
3. Why does the solution become cloudy when calcium reacts with water?
4. Write a balanced equation for potassium reacting with water.
5. Name the products when magnesium reacts with steam.
6. Which of these react with dilute hydrochloric acid: zinc, silver, iron, gold?
7. Explain, using the reactivity series, why copper does not react with dilute hydrochloric acid.
8. State **two** variables to keep the same when comparing the reactivity of metals with acid.
9. *(Extended)* Write an ionic equation for magnesium reacting with copper(II) ions.
10. *(Extended)* Predict whether iron will react with zinc sulfate solution. Explain.
11. *(Extended)* Explain why aluminium appears less reactive than its position suggests.
12. *(Extended)* State what is meant by "a greater tendency to form positive ions".

**Answers:**

1. K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, C, Zn, Fe, H, Cu, Ag, Au.
2. Any three: floats; melts into a ball; moves around on the surface; fizzes; gets smaller and disappears.
3. Calcium hydroxide is only slightly soluble, so some stays as a solid.
4. 2K + 2H₂O → 2KOH + H₂.
5. Magnesium oxide and hydrogen.
6. Zinc and iron.
7. Copper is below hydrogen in the series, so it cannot displace hydrogen from the acid.
8. Any two: mass of metal; particle size or surface area; volume of acid; concentration of acid; starting temperature.
9. Mg + Cu²⁺ → Mg²⁺ + Cu.
10. No reaction: iron is less reactive than zinc, so it cannot displace zinc.
11. It is covered by a thin layer of aluminium oxide that stops reactants reaching the metal.
12. The metal's atoms lose electrons more readily to become positive ions.
