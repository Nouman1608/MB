OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Biology (9610) is a modular course for students who want to
study living organisms in depth, from their diversity and the systems that keep them alive to
populations, genetics and control. It is organised into four content units, two at AS and two at
A2, and assessed through five written papers. Every paper lasts 90 minutes, carries 75 marks and is
worth 20% of the A-level, and the fifth paper is synoptic. Students also complete ten required
practical activities, which are assessed through the written papers rather than in a separate
practical exam.

## How the course is organised

**AS stage**

- **Unit 1, The diversity of living organisms**, introduces the variety of life and provides the
  biological foundations for the rest of the course.
- **Unit 2, Biological systems and disease**, studies how organisms work and what happens when
  they go wrong. It covers the causes of disease (pathogens, lifestyle and genes); digestion and
  absorption; cholera; HIV as an example of a human disease caused by a virus; the defensive
  functions of mammalian blood; the circulation of blood and the structure of the mammalian heart;
  risk factors associated with heart disease; mass transport systems in plants; the role of aphids
  in spreading plant viruses; cell division by binary fission and mitosis; and mutation and cancer.

**A2 stage**

- **Unit 3, Populations and genes**, moves from individuals to groups, looking at inheritance and
  how the genetic make-up of populations changes.
- **Unit 4, Control**, studies how organisms detect and respond to change and keep internal
  conditions stable.

**Synoptic assessment.** A fifth unit, the Synoptic Paper, does not introduce new content. It draws
the four content units together.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Unit | Time | Marks | Weighting (A-level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AS Paper 1 | Unit 1: The Diversity of Living Organisms | 90 minutes | 75 | 20% |
| AS Paper 2 | Unit 2: Biological Systems and Disease | 90 minutes | 75 | 20% |
| A-level Paper 1 | Unit 3: Populations and Genes | 90 minutes | 75 | 20% |
| A-level Paper 2 | Unit 4: Control | 90 minutes | 75 | 20% |
| A-level Paper 3 | Unit 5: Synoptic Paper | 90 minutes | 75 | 20% |

The qualification is modular and untiered. The AS units can be cashed in as a standalone AS award or
carried forward towards the full A-level. OxfordAQA's qualification page states that resits are
unlimited and the best result counts. The ten required practicals are examined through the written
papers, so there is no hands-on practical exam. First teaching was in September 2016, with first AS
exams in May/June 2017 and first A-level exams in May/June 2018.

## How the parts fit together

Each content unit has its own paper, which makes the course feel neatly divided, but the units are
closely connected and the Synoptic Paper is there to test those connections.

Unit 2 depends on the foundations laid in Unit 1: digestion, the heart, blood and plant transport
are all easier to understand once the basic biology of organisms is secure. Unit 2's work on cell
division, mutation and cancer leads naturally into Unit 3's study of populations and genes, because
both are about how genetic information is passed on and changed. Unit 4's theme of control also
looks back to Unit 2, where the blood and circulation are the systems that carry signals and
defences around the body.

The required practicals are a further thread. Because they are assessed in the written papers,
students need to be able to describe methods, handle data, identify variables and evaluate results
for any of them, on any paper. The Synoptic Paper rewards students who can apply ideas from one unit
in the context of another, for example linking mutation from Unit 2 to population change in Unit 3.

## Planning your study

In the first year, work through Unit 1 and then Unit 2. Unit 2 is broad, so it helps to group its
subtopics: disease and its causes (pathogens, cholera, HIV and aphid-spread plant viruses), body
systems (digestion, blood, the heart and heart disease, plant transport) and cell biology (cell
division, mutation and cancer). Carry out the relevant required practicals as the content is taught
and keep clear notes on method and analysis.

In the second year, study Unit 3 and Unit 4, then leave time before the exams to revisit the AS units
with the synoptic paper in mind. If you are taking AS papers at the end of the first year, prepare
for them properly; if you resit any, remember that the best result counts.

Begin timed practice on each paper once its unit has been taught, and move to mixed and synoptic
practice in the final months. The exam-preparation page covers the five papers and the required
practicals in detail.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in OxfordAQA International A-level Biology?**
Five. Two AS papers cover Units 1 and 2, and three A-level papers cover Unit 3, Unit 4 and the Unit 5 Synoptic Paper. Each is 90 minutes, 75 marks and 20% of the A-level.

**Is there a practical exam in 9610?**
No. Students complete ten required practical activities, and these are assessed through the written papers.

**Can I take the AS on its own?**
Yes. The AS units can be cashed in as a standalone AS award or carried forward towards the full A-level.

**Can I resit OxfordAQA Biology papers?**
OxfordAQA states that resits are unlimited and that the best result counts.

**What is the Synoptic Paper?**
It is A-level Paper 3, Unit 5. It brings together content from across the course rather than introducing new topics.
