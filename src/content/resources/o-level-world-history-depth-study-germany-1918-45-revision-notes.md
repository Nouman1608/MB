**These notes condense Depth Study B of Cambridge O Level History 2147, Germany 1918-45, for quick recall before Paper 1.** They are arranged by the syllabus key questions: Weimar's survival, Hitler's rise by 1934, Nazi control, and life in Nazi Germany. Full explanations are in the [Depth Study B study guide](/resources/o-level-world-history-depth-study-germany-1918-45/); exam-style questions are in [Depth Study B practice questions](/resources/o-level-world-history-depth-study-germany-1918-45-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE O LEVEL HISTORY (2147), 2024-2026 -- DEPTH STUDY B**

1. Was the Weimar Republic doomed from the start?
2. Why was Hitler able to dominate Germany by 1934?
3. (a) How effectively did the Nazis control Germany, 1933-45? (b) What was it like to live in Nazi Germany?

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Proportional representation | Seats in proportion to votes -- many parties, coalitions |
| Article 48 | President's emergency power to rule by decree |
| Diktat | "Dictated peace" -- German name for Versailles |
| Stab in the back | Myth that the army was betrayed by politicians in 1918 |
| Hyper-inflation | Extreme, rapid price rises (1923) |
| Putsch | Attempt to seize power by force |
| Enabling Act | 1933 law letting Hitler's government make laws without the Reichstag |
| Gleichschaltung | "Co-ordination" -- bringing all organisations under Nazi control |
| Totalitarian | State that controls every aspect of life |
| Volksgemeinschaft | Nazi idea of a united "people's community" |

## Key question 1: weakness vs survival

| Weakness | Evidence of survival or strength |
|---|---|
| Born of defeat; Versailles blamed on it | Survived Spartacists, Kapp and Munich |
| PR, coalitions, Article 48 | Stresemann's coalitions governed 1923-29 |
| Army and judges disloyal | Rentenmark ended inflation |
| Hyper-inflation 1923 | Dawes, Locarno, League membership |
| Dependent on US loans | Cultural flowering (Bauhaus, cinema) |

**Verdict line:** "vulnerable rather than doomed -- the Depression, not 1919, destroyed Weimar."

## Key question 2: Hitler's rise -- the five-step chain

1. **Depression** (1929): unemployment about six million by 1932.
2. **Failure of democratic government**: Brüning's decrees; no majority.
3. **Nazi appeal**: propaganda, Hitler's speeches, SA order, anti-communism.
4. **Votes**: 12 seats (1928) to 107 (1930) to 230 (July 1932).
5. **Backstairs deal**: Papen and Hindenburg appoint Hitler (30 January 1933).

**Consolidation:** Reichstag Fire decree -> Enabling Act -> unions and parties banned -> Night of the Long Knives -> Führer and army oath.

## Key question 3(a): methods of control

- **Fear:** SS, Gestapo, camps, informers.
- **Persuasion:** Goebbels, radio, film, rallies, Olympics.
- **Churches:** Concordat; Reich Church; Confessing Church resisted.
- **Persecution:** Jews, Roma, disabled people, homosexuals.
- **Opposition:** youth groups, White Rose, July 1944 plot -- small and crushed.

## Key question 3(b): who gained and who lost?

| Gained (at least partly) | Lost |
|---|---|
| The unemployed -- public works, rearmament | Trade unionists and political opponents |
| Big business -- contracts, no unions | Jews -- rights, property, lives |
| Many young people -- activities, status | Women in professions |
| Families -- loans, holidays via Strength Through Joy | Roma, disabled, homosexuals |

**War:** rationing from 1939; bombing; total war from 1943; forced labour; the Final Solution.

## Worked example: (c) plan in one box

*"The Nazis controlled Germany mainly through terror." How far do you agree?* **[10]**

- Agree: SS, Gestapo, camps; Night of the Long Knives showed ruthlessness.
- Disagree: propaganda and popular measures (jobs, national pride) won consent; many Germans informed willingly.
- Judgement: terror for opponents, persuasion for the majority -- both needed, but consent made terror effective.

## Exam traps

- "Hitler was elected Chancellor" -- wrong; he was appointed.
- Mixing up the Munich Putsch (1923) and the Night of the Long Knives (1934).
- Treating Weimar's recovery as complete -- it relied on American loans.
- Forgetting the war years under key question 3(b).
- Giving statistics without explaining what they show.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I remember the 1932-33 sequence?**
Brüning (to May 1932), Papen (to November 1932), Schleicher (to January 1933), then Hitler.

**What was Kristallnacht?**
The violence of 9-10 November 1938 against Jewish synagogues, shops and homes across Germany.

**What is the Volksgemeinschaft?**
The Nazi ideal of a racially pure, united community replacing class divisions.

## Self-test

1. Which article allowed rule by decree? *Article 48.*
2. Who introduced the Rentenmark? *Stresemann's government, 1923.*
3. How many seats did the Nazis win in 1928? *12.*
4. When did Hindenburg die? *August 1934.*
5. Name a youth opposition group. *Edelweiss Pirates (or Swing Youth).*
