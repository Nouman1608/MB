## Definition

**Active transport is the movement of molecules or ions into or out of a cell through the cell membrane, from a region of their lower concentration to a region of their higher concentration (against a concentration gradient), using energy released during respiration.**

Four marking ideas to include:

1. Movement of molecules or ions through the cell membrane.
2. From **low** to **high** concentration.
3. **Against** the concentration gradient.
4. Uses energy from **respiration**.

## Active transport and diffusion compared

| Feature | Diffusion | Active transport |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Down the gradient | Against the gradient |
| Energy from respiration | No | Yes |
| Stopped by lack of oxygen | No | Yes, uptake falls |
| End result | Equal concentrations | A concentration difference |
| Examples | Oxygen into blood at alveoli | Nitrate into root hair cells |

## Ion uptake by root hair cells

- Soil water has a **low** concentration of ions such as nitrate and magnesium.
- Root hair cells contain a **higher** concentration.
- Diffusion would not bring the ions in, so the cells use **active transport**.
- The cells have **many mitochondria**, which release energy by aerobic respiration to drive the process.
- The long, thin root hair gives a large surface area.

**Why the ions matter:**

| Ion | Used to make | Therefore needed for |
|---|---|---|
| Nitrate | Amino acids, then proteins | Growth and enzymes |
| Magnesium | Chlorophyll | Photosynthesis |

## Evidence for active transport

- Less oxygen means less aerobic respiration, and ion uptake falls.
- A respiratory poison stops the uptake.
- Uptake continues against a concentration gradient.
- Lower temperature slows respiration, so uptake falls.

## Worked data example

Roots in air: ion uptake 8.4 units per hour. Roots in nitrogen gas: 1.2 units per hour.

Percentage fall = (8.4 - 1.2) divided by 8.4 x 100 = 85.7 per cent. The fall happens because without oxygen aerobic respiration releases much less energy, so active transport slows.

Concentration comparison: soil 0.02 mmol per dm cubed, cell 0.50 mmol per dm cubed. The cell concentration is 0.50 divided by 0.02 = 25 times higher, so ions enter against the gradient.

## Linking the topic to others

- **Respiration (topic 10):** respiration releases the energy that active transport uses. Anything that lowers aerobic respiration, such as a lack of oxygen or a low temperature, lowers active transport.
- **Plant nutrition (topic 6):** nitrate ions taken up by active transport are used to make amino acids and proteins, and magnesium ions are used to make chlorophyll.
- **Transport in plants (topic 7):** once the ions are inside the root hair cells, they move on to the xylem with the water and are carried to the leaves.
- **Human nutrition (topic 8):** absorption in the small intestine includes active transport, for example of glucose when its concentration in the gut is lower than in the blood.

## Naming the process

1. Water crossing a partially permeable membrane: **osmosis**.
2. High to low concentration, no energy from the cell: **diffusion**.
3. Low to high concentration, energy from respiration: **active transport**.

## Short model answers

**Define active transport.** The movement of molecules or ions into or out of a cell, through the cell membrane, from a region of lower concentration to a region of higher concentration, using energy released during respiration.

**Explain why root hair cells have many mitochondria.** The mitochondria carry out aerobic respiration, which releases the energy needed for active transport of ions into the cell.

**Explain why waterlogged soil reduces ion uptake.** There is little oxygen in waterlogged soil, so aerobic respiration in the root cells is reduced. Less energy is released, so less active transport occurs.

**Explain why diffusion cannot take nitrate into a root hair cell.** The concentration inside the cell is higher than in the soil water, so diffusion would move nitrate out of the cell, not into it.

## Exam traps

- Say "energy **released by respiration**", not just "energy".
- Say "**against** the concentration gradient", not "up the gradient" only.
- Energy is **released** by respiration and **used** by active transport.
- Active transport is not stopped by a high concentration gradient; it works against one.
- Do not say that active transport happens only in plants.
- Link the many mitochondria to the energy needed.

## Self-test

1. Define active transport.
2. In which direction does active transport move a substance relative to the concentration gradient?
3. Where does the energy for active transport come from?
4. Why can diffusion not take nitrate into root hair cells?
5. Name two ions taken up by root hair cells.
6. State one use in the plant for each of those ions.
7. Why do root hair cells contain many mitochondria?
8. What happens to ion uptake if the roots lose their oxygen supply?
9. State two differences between diffusion and active transport.
10. Soil nitrate is 0.04 mmol per dm cubed and cell nitrate is 0.80 mmol per dm cubed. How many times higher is the cell concentration?
11. Name the process that moves water into root hair cells.
12. Suggest why a plant in waterlogged soil may show poor growth.

### Answers

1. The movement of molecules or ions into or out of a cell through the cell membrane from a lower to a higher concentration, against the gradient, using energy released during respiration.
2. Against the gradient (low to high concentration).
3. Respiration.
4. The ions are at a higher concentration inside the cell, so diffusion would move them out.
5. Nitrate and magnesium.
6. Nitrate: making amino acids and proteins. Magnesium: making chlorophyll.
7. They release energy by aerobic respiration to power active transport.
8. It falls, because less energy is released.
9. Any two: diffusion is down the gradient, active transport is against it; diffusion needs no energy from respiration, active transport does; diffusion continues without oxygen, active transport slows or stops.
10. 0.80 divided by 0.04 = 20 times higher.
11. Osmosis.
12. Little oxygen means less aerobic respiration, less active transport and so less ion uptake, so less protein and chlorophyll can be made.
