## Questions

**1.** Differentiate y = 7e^(−x/2) + 3 ln 2x − 2 sin 3x. **[3]**

**2.** Given y = x⁴ cos 3x, find the exact gradient of the curve at x = π/3. **[4]**

**3.** f(x) = (1 + ln x) / x², x > 0.

**(a)** Show that f′(x) = −(1 + 2 ln x) / x³. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the exact coordinates of the stationary point of the curve y = f(x). **[3]**

**4.** Differentiate with respect to x:

**(a)** y = ln(2x² + 5) **[2]**
**(b)** y = √(1 + cos 2x) **[2]**

**5.** **(a)** By writing sec x as (cos x)⁻¹, show that d/dx(sec x) = sec x tan x. **[3]**
**(b)** Differentiate y = cot 4x. **[2]**

**6.** Find the equation of the tangent to y = x e^(3x) at the point where x = 1/3. Give your answer in the form y = mx + c, with m and c exact. **[5]**

**7.** Given x = cos 2y, for 0 < y < π/2:

**(a)** find dy/dx in terms of y. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that dy/dx = −1 / (2√(1 − x²)). **[2]**

**8.** Find the exact gradient of y = 5^(3x) at x = 1/3. **[3]**

**9.** A cup of tea cools so that its temperature T °C after t minutes is T = 20 + 65e^(−0.04t).

**(a)** State the initial temperature and the temperature the tea approaches. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the rate of change of temperature when t = 10, to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the time taken to reach 40 °C, to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(d)** Show that dT/dt = −0.04(T − 20). **[2]**

**10.** The number of followers of a channel is modelled by N = 250e^(0.12t), where t is the number of days since launch.

**(a)** Show that dN/dt = 0.12N. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the value of t, to 3 significant figures, when N is increasing at 60 followers per day. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the number of followers predicted when t = 60, to 3 significant figures, and comment on whether this is sensible. **[2]**
**(d)** A second model is N = 4000 − 3750e^(−0.12t). Find, to 3 significant figures, the time at which this model gives N = 3000. **[3]**

**11.** f(x) = e^(−x) sin 2x, 0 ≤ x ≤ π.

**(a)** Find f′(x). **[3]**
**(b)** Find the values of x, to 3 significant figures, at which f′(x) = 0. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the y-coordinate of the stationary point with the smaller x-value, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**12.** A curve has equation x = 3y + e^(2y).

**(a)** Find dy/dx at the point where y = 0. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the equation of the normal at that point, in the form y = mx + c. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** d/dx(7e^(−x/2)) = −(7/2)e^(−x/2) [1]
d/dx(3 ln 2x) = 3/x [1]
d/dx(−2 sin 3x) = −6 cos 3x [1]
**dy/dx = −(7/2)e^(−x/2) + 3/x − 6 cos 3x** **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Each term earns credit separately, so write each derivative on its own line. The 2 inside ln 2x does not appear in the derivative.

**2.** Product rule with u = x⁴ and v = cos 3x [1]
dy/dx = 4x³ cos 3x − 3x⁴ sin 3x [1]
At x = π/3: cos π = −1 and sin π = 0 [1]
**Gradient = −4π³/27** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Exact" means the answer stays in terms of π. A decimal answer does not meet the instruction, and showing cos π and sin π makes your method clear.

**3. (a)** u = 1 + ln x, u′ = 1/x, v = x², v′ = 2x [1]
f′(x) = [(1/x)x² − (1 + ln x)(2x)] / x⁴ [1]
= (x − 2x − 2x ln x) / x⁴ = **−(1 + 2 ln x) / x³** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** f′(x) = 0 gives 1 + 2 ln x = 0 [1]
ln x = −½, so x = e^(−1/2) [1]
y = (1 − ½) / e^(−1) = **e/2**, so the point is **(e^(−1/2), e/2)** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" question, include the line where the numerator is expanded and simplified. In (b), substitute the exact x-value into f, not into f′.

**4. (a)** Chain rule: (1/(2x² + 5)) × 4x [1]
**dy/dx = 4x / (2x² + 5)** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** y = (1 + cos 2x)^(1/2): dy/dx = ½(1 + cos 2x)^(−1/2) × (−2 sin 2x) [1]
**dy/dx = −sin 2x / √(1 + cos 2x)** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A chain rule answer is easiest to check when the inner derivative is written separately. A missing factor of 2 from the inner function is a common slip in (b).

**5. (a)** sec x = (cos x)⁻¹ [1]
d/dx = −(cos x)⁻² × (−sin x) [1]
= sin x / cos² x = (1/cos x)(sin x/cos x) = **sec x tan x** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** d/dx(cot kx) = −k cosec² kx, so with k = 4: [1]
**dy/dx = −4 cosec² 4x** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" needs every step, including the final rewriting in terms of sec and tan. Writing the result first and working backwards does not demonstrate the derivation.

**6.** Product rule: dy/dx = e^(3x) + 3x e^(3x) [1]
At x = 1/3: gradient = e + e = 2e [1]
y = (1/3)e^1 = e/3 [1]
Tangent: y − e/3 = 2e(x − 1/3) [1]
**y = 2e x − e/3** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Both the gradient and the y-coordinate must be found before writing the line. Leave e as e, as the question asks for exact values.

**7. (a)** dx/dy = −2 sin 2y [1]
**dy/dx = −1 / (2 sin 2y)** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** sin² 2y = 1 − cos² 2y = 1 − x² [1]
For 0 < y < π/2, sin 2y > 0, so sin 2y = √(1 − x²) and **dy/dx = −1 / (2√(1 − x²))** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Justify the positive square root using the given interval. Without that statement, the sign of sin 2y is unexplained.

**8.** 5^(3x) = e^(3x ln 5) [1]
dy/dx = 3 ln 5 × 5^(3x) [1]
At x = 1/3: 5^1 = 5, so **gradient = 15 ln 5** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The chain rule factor 3 is easy to omit when a^x is differentiated. Substitute x = 1/3 before simplifying 5^(3x).

**9. (a)** At t = 0: T = 20 + 65 = **85 °C** [1]
As t → ∞, e^(−0.04t) → 0, so T approaches **20 °C** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** dT/dt = −2.6e^(−0.04t) [1]
At t = 10: −2.6e^(−0.4) [1]
**= −1.74 °C per minute** [1] **[3]**
**(c)** 40 = 20 + 65e^(−0.04t), so e^(−0.04t) = 20/65 [1]
−0.04t = ln(4/13), so t = ln(13/4) / 0.04 [1]
**t = 29.5 minutes** [1] **[3]**
**(d)** dT/dt = −0.04 × 65e^(−0.04t) [1]
Since T − 20 = 65e^(−0.04t), **dT/dt = −0.04(T − 20)** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A negative rate needs a sentence of interpretation, such as "the temperature is falling". In (d), start from your derivative and replace the exponential term, rather than starting from the result.

**10. (a)** dN/dt = 0.12 × 250e^(0.12t) [1]
= **0.12N** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** 0.12N = 60, so N = 500 [1]
250e^(0.12t) = 500, so e^(0.12t) = 2 [1]
**t = ln 2 / 0.12 = 5.78 days** [1] **[3]**
**(c)** N = 250e^(7.2) = **about 335 000** [1]
Not sensible: the model has no upper limit, and this is far more followers than a single channel is likely to have. [1] **[2]**
**(d)** 3000 = 4000 − 3750e^(−0.12t), so e^(−0.12t) = 1000/3750 = 4/15 [1]
−0.12t = ln(4/15), so t = ln(15/4) / 0.12 [1]
**t = 11.0 days** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A comment on a model should refer to the context, not just say "too big". Keep unrounded values in your working until the final line.

**11. (a)** Product rule, u = e^(−x), v = sin 2x [1]
f′(x) = −e^(−x) sin 2x + 2e^(−x) cos 2x [1]
**f′(x) = e^(−x)(2 cos 2x − sin 2x)** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** e^(−x) ≠ 0, so 2 cos 2x = sin 2x, giving tan 2x = 2 [1]
2x = 1.1071 or 1.1071 + π = 4.2487 (0 ≤ 2x ≤ 2π) [1]
**x = 0.554 or x = 2.12** [1] **[3]**
**(c)** f(0.5536) = e^(−0.5536) sin(1.1071) [1]
**y = 0.514** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* List all the solutions in the interval by extending the range for 2x, not x. For (c), use the unrounded value of x to avoid a rounding error in the third significant figure.

**12. (a)** dx/dy = 3 + 2e^(2y) [1]
At y = 0: dx/dy = 5 [1]
**dy/dx = 1/5** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** At y = 0: x = 0 + e^0 = 1, so the point is (1, 0) [1]
Normal gradient = −5 [1]
**y = −5(x − 1), that is y = 5 − 5x** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The gradient of the normal comes from dy/dx, not dx/dy. Find the point first, then use the reciprocal, then take the negative reciprocal.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the derivative of cos kx as k sin kx.
- Omitting the inner derivative in chain rule questions.
- Putting the terms in the wrong order in the quotient rule numerator.
- Dropping a common factor, such as e^(−x), when simplifying a product rule answer.
- Giving k/x for the derivative of ln kx.
- Treating a^x as a power of x.
- Using dx/dy as the gradient of the curve.
- Giving a decimal where an exact answer is required, or the reverse.
- Reporting only the first solution in a trigonometric equation within an interval.
- Giving a rate or time without units, or commenting on a model without referring to the context.
