Written for OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202), this set follows the OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202) specification, Version 6.2, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. It covers section 3.8.2 Factors affecting equilibrium (statements a to e) and section 3.8.3 Production of ammonia and sulfuric acid (statements a to d). Neither section has a required practical. The qualification is untiered, so every question suits every candidate. Use relative atomic masses H = 1, N = 14, O = 16 and S = 32 where needed.

## Questions

**1.** A reversible reaction is carried out in a sealed flask.

**(a)** State what is meant by equilibrium for this reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** A student writes: "At equilibrium the reaction has finished and there are equal amounts of reactants and products." Identify the two mistakes in this statement. **[2]**

**2.** Carbon monoxide and chlorine combine to form carbonyl chloride, COCl₂:

CO(g) + Cl₂(g) ⇌ COCl₂(g), with energy given out in the forward direction.

**(a)** What happens to the yield of COCl₂ if the temperature is raised? Explain your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** What happens to the yield of COCl₂ if the pressure is increased? Explain your answer. **[2]**

**3.** Nitrosyl chloride decomposes when heated:

2NOCl(g) ⇌ 2NO(g) + Cl₂(g). The forward reaction is endothermic.

**(a)** State whether a high or a low temperature gives the larger yield of chlorine. Explain your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** State whether a high or a low pressure gives the larger yield of chlorine. Explain your answer. **[2]**
**(c)** For the reaction 2HBr(g) ⇌ H₂(g) + Br₂(g), explain why changing the pressure does not change the yield of hydrogen. **[1]**

**4.** A gas reaction is G(g) + 2J(g) ⇌ K(g). The table shows the percentage of K in the equilibrium mixture under four sets of conditions.

| Temperature | % K at 1 atmosphere | % K at 10 atmospheres |
|---|---|---|
| 100 °C | 52 | 71 |
| 300 °C | 30 | 48 |

**(a)** Is the forward reaction exothermic or endothermic? Use the table to decide, and explain how. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the data support the rule about pressure and numbers of molecules. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why a factory making K might not choose 100 °C, even though it gives a higher yield. **[1]**

**5.** Describe how ammonia is manufactured by the Haber process. Include the raw materials and their sources, the conditions, and what happens to the gases after they leave the reactor. **[6]**

**6.** The forward reaction in the Haber process is exothermic.

**(a)** Suggest why the plant runs at about 450 °C and not at a cooler temperature. **[3]**
**(b)** Give reasons for running at about 200 atmospheres rather than at a much higher pressure. **[3]**

**7.** Most ammonia is used to make fertilizers.

**(a)** Explain why there is a global need for fertilizers. **[3]**
**(b)** Ammonium sulfate is made by the reaction 2NH₃ + H₂SO₄ → (NH₄)₂SO₄. Calculate the mass of ammonia needed to react with 49 tonnes of sulfuric acid, and the mass of ammonium sulfate formed. **[3]**

**8.** Sulfuric acid is made by the contact process.

**(a)** Give balanced equations, including state symbols, for Stages 1 and 3. **[2]**
**(b)** State the catalyst, the temperature and the pressure used in Stage 2. **[3]**

**9.** Stage 2 of the contact process is exothermic in the forward direction (equation: 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g)). Explain why a catalyst, a temperature of about 450 °C and atmospheric pressure are used. **[6]**

**10.** In one pass through a Haber reactor, 560 kg of nitrogen enters and 15% of it reacts.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of nitrogen that reacts. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of ammonia formed. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass of hydrogen that reacts. **[1]**
**(d)** State what happens to the nitrogen that does not react, and explain why this matters. **[2]**

**11.** A contact process plant starts with 4.8 tonnes of sulfur.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of sulfur dioxide formed in Stage 1. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of oxygen needed in Stage 2 to convert all of this sulfur dioxide to sulfur trioxide. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the maximum mass of sulfuric acid that can be made. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest one reason why the plant makes less than this maximum mass. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The forward and backward reactions are both happening [1] at exactly the same rate (in a closed system) [1].
**(b)** The reaction has not finished: both reactions are still taking place [1]. The amounts are constant, not necessarily equal [1].
*Examiner insight:* "The rates are equal" alone is often judged incomplete; also say that both reactions are still going on.

**2. (a)** The yield of COCl₂ decreases [1]. The forward reaction is exothermic, so the backward reaction is endothermic, and raising the temperature increases the yield from the endothermic reaction [1].
**(b)** The yield of COCl₂ increases [1]. There are 2 molecules on the left and 1 on the right, and increased pressure favours the reaction producing fewer molecules [1].
*Examiner insight:* Tie each explanation to this equation (the endothermic direction, or the molecule count); "it shifts" alone is unlikely to gain credit.

**3. (a)** High temperature [1]. The forward reaction is endothermic, and raising the temperature increases the yield from the endothermic reaction [1].
**(b)** Low pressure [1]. 2 molecules become 3, and decreased pressure favours the reaction producing the greater number of molecules [1].
**(c)** There are 2 molecules of gas on each side, so neither direction produces fewer molecules [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the molecule counts from the equation, such as "2 → 3"; a bare "low pressure" with no count rarely earns the explanation mark.

**4. (a)** At each pressure, % K falls as the temperature rises [1]. Raising the temperature favours the endothermic reaction, so the backward reaction is endothermic and the forward reaction is **exothermic** [1].
**(b)** At each temperature, % K is higher at 10 atmospheres than at 1 atmosphere [1]. The forward reaction turns 3 molecules into 1, and increased pressure favours the reaction producing fewer molecules [1].
**(c)** At 100 °C the reaction would be too slow [1].
*Examiner insight:* Quote figures from the table, such as 52% falling to 30% at 1 atmosphere.

**5.** Any six points, one mark each:
- Nitrogen comes from the air [1].
- Hydrogen comes from natural gas (or other sources) [1].
- The purified gases pass over an iron catalyst [1].
- at about 450 °C and about 200 atmospheres [1].
- Only some of the gases react because the reaction is reversible; on cooling, the ammonia turns to liquid and is taken off [1].
- The nitrogen and hydrogen that did not react go back into the reactor (recycled) [1].
*Examiner insight:* Follow the order raw materials → reactor → cooling → recycling, and give each condition with its unit.

**6. (a)** The forward reaction is exothermic, so a lower temperature would give a higher yield of ammonia [1]. However, the reaction would then be too slow to be worthwhile [1]. About 450 °C balances a reasonable yield against a fast enough rate (a compromise) [1].
**(b)** 4 molecules of gas become 2, so a high pressure increases the yield (and the rate) [1]. A much higher pressure would need stronger, more expensive equipment and more energy to compress the gases [1], and would be more dangerous [1].
*Examiner insight:* A "compromise" answer is strongest when both sides are stated: what is gained (yield or rate) and what is lost (rate, cost or safety).

**7. (a)** The world population is rising, so more food is needed from limited farmland [1]. Crops need nitrogen to grow (to make proteins), and harvesting removes nutrients from the soil [1]. Fertilizers replace these nutrients and so maximise food yields [1].
**(b)** Mᵣ: H₂SO₄ = 98, NH₃ = 17, (NH₄)₂SO₄ = 132. From the equation, 98 t of acid reacts with 2 × 17 = 34 t of ammonia [1]. 49 t is half of 98 t, so ammonia needed = **17 tonnes** [1]. Ammonium sulfate formed = 132 ÷ 2 = **66 tonnes** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Write the 2 : 1 ratio down; an answer of 8.5 tonnes of ammonia shows it was missed.

**8. (a)** Stage 1: S(s) + O₂(g) → SO₂(g) [1]. Stage 3: H₂O(l) + SO₃(g) → H₂SO₄(aq) [1].
**(b)** Vanadium(V) oxide, V₂O₅ [1]; around 450 °C [1]; atmospheric pressure [1].
*Examiner insight:* Give the full name with oxidation state or the formula V₂O₅; "vanadium" alone names a different substance and may not be accepted.

**9.** Any six points, one mark each:
- The V₂O₅ catalyst increases the rate of reaction [1].
- It does not change the yield, but equilibrium is reached faster [1].
- The forward reaction is exothermic, so a lower temperature would give a higher yield of SO₃ [1].
- But a lower temperature would make the reaction too slow, so about 450 °C is a compromise between rate and yield [1].
- 3 molecules become 2, so higher pressure would favour SO₃ [1].
- The yield is already high at atmospheric pressure, so the extra cost of high-pressure equipment and energy is not worth the small gain [1].
*Examiner insight:* Give each condition its own reason; merging temperature and pressure into one sentence blurs which reason is which.

**10. (a)** 15% of 560 kg = **84 kg** of nitrogen [1].
**(b)** 84 ÷ 28 = 3 kmol N₂; this gives 2 × 3 = 6 kmol NH₃ [1]. Mass = 6 × 17 = **102 kg** [1].
**(c)** 3 × 3 = 9 kmol H₂; mass = 9 × 2 = **18 kg** [1].
**(d)** The remaining 476 kg of nitrogen is recycled back into the reactor (with unreacted hydrogen) [1]. So raw materials are not wasted and far more ammonia is made overall than one pass gives [1].
*Examiner insight:* Check that mass is conserved (84 + 18 = 102); if your figures do not balance, look again at the mole ratio before moving on.

**11. (a)** S + O₂ → SO₂: 32 t of sulfur gives 64 t of sulfur dioxide [1]. So 4.8 t gives 4.8 × 64 ÷ 32 = **9.6 tonnes** [1].
**(b)** 2SO₂ + O₂: 2 × 64 = 128 t of SO₂ needs 32 t of O₂ [1]. So 9.6 t needs 9.6 × 32 ÷ 128 = **2.4 tonnes** [1].
**(c)** 1 S gives 1 H₂SO₄, so 32 t of sulfur gives 98 t of acid [1]. 4.8 × 98 ÷ 32 = **14.7 tonnes** [1].
**(d)** Stage 2 is reversible, so not all the sulfur dioxide is converted to sulfur trioxide (or some product is lost during separation) [1].
*Examiner insight:* Use 32 for O₂, not 16; using the atomic mass of oxygen halves the answer to part (b).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining equilibrium without "closed system" or without "same rate in both directions".
- Saying the amounts are equal at equilibrium.
- Predicting a temperature effect before working out which direction is exothermic.
- Counting atoms instead of molecules, or ignoring the numbers in front of formulae.
- Claiming a catalyst increases yield.
- Giving one side of a compromise only.
- Swapping the catalysts or pressures of the Haber and contact processes.
- Forgetting the 2 : 1 ratios in the ammonium sulfate and Stage 2 calculations.
