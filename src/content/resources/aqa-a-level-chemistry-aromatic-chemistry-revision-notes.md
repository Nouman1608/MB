## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Aromatic compound | Contains a benzene ring |
| Delocalised electrons | Electrons shared over more than two atoms; in benzene, six p electrons spread over all six carbons |
| Cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene | Theoretical C₆H₆ with three fixed C=C and three C–C bonds; not the real structure |
| Enthalpy of hydrogenation | Enthalpy change when H₂ adds across C=C bonds to give a saturated product |
| Electrophile | Electron pair acceptor |
| Electrophilic substitution | An electrophile replaces a hydrogen atom on the ring; the ring stays intact |
| Nitronium ion | NO₂⁺, the electrophile in nitration |
| Acylium ion | RCO⁺, the electrophile in Friedel–Crafts acylation |
| Monosubstitution | Only one hydrogen on the ring is replaced (the limit of the specification) |

## Bonding in benzene (3.3.10.1)

- C₆H₆: six carbons in a regular hexagon, one H on each carbon.
- Each C forms three σ bonds (two C, one H): bond angles **120°**, molecule **planar**.
- Each C has one electron in a p orbital at right angles to the ring.
- The p orbitals overlap sideways all round the ring: the six p electrons are **delocalised** above and
  below the plane.
- All C–C bonds are the **same length**, **intermediate between C–C single and C=C double**.
- Delocalisation makes benzene **more stable** than cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene.

> **Method: use hydrogenation data to show extra stability**
> 1. Take ΔH for one C=C (from cyclohexene).
> 2. Multiply by 3 for the theoretical triene.
> 3. Compare with the measured value for benzene: benzene's is less exothermic.
> 4. Both give cyclohexane, so benzene must start lower in energy.
> 5. The difference is the extra stability; give the reason: **delocalisation of p electrons**.

Worked reminder with common data: 3 × (−120) = −360 kJ mol⁻¹ predicted; benzene −208 kJ mol⁻¹;
benzene is 152 kJ mol⁻¹ more stable. (Use whatever values the question gives.)

**Energy level diagram, in words.** Put cyclohexane on the lowest line. Draw benzene + 3H₂ above it by
the size of benzene's measured value, and cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene + 3H₂ higher still by the predicted
value. Label the gap between the two upper lines as the extra stability from delocalisation. All arrows
for hydrogenation point down, because the reactions are exothermic. If a question gives a value for
cyclohexa-1,3-diene, the same logic applies: predict 2 × one C=C and compare.

### Substitution, not addition

- Addition would **break the delocalised ring** and lose the extra stability.
- Substitution replaces one H and **keeps the delocalised ring**.
- Benzene's electron density is spread over six carbons, so it needs a **strong electrophile** (a
  positive ion) to react.

## Electrophilic substitution (3.3.10.2)

> **Method: outline any electrophilic substitution mechanism**
> 1. Write the equation that makes the electrophile.
> 2. Arrow 1: from the delocalised ring (circle) to the atom of the electrophile that bonds (N in NO₂⁺,
>    C in RCO⁺).
> 3. Intermediate: attacked carbon carries H and the new group; horseshoe over the other five carbons,
>    gap facing the attacked carbon, **+** inside.
> 4. Arrow 2: from the C–H bond into the ring.
> 5. Product with a full circle, plus H⁺.
> 6. Show the catalyst being regenerated.

### Nitration

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Reagents | concentrated HNO₃ and concentrated H₂SO₄ |
| Electrophile | NO₂⁺ |
| Forming the electrophile | HNO₃ + 2H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + H₃O⁺ + 2HSO₄⁻ (or HNO₃ + H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + HSO₄⁻ + H₂O) |
| Overall | C₆H₆ + HNO₃ → C₆H₅NO₂ + H₂O |
| Catalyst regenerated | H⁺ + HSO₄⁻ → H₂SO₄ |
| Product | nitrobenzene |
| Importance | step in synthesis: manufacture of explosives; reduction gives aromatic amines (3.3.11.1) used to make dyes |

### Friedel–Crafts acylation

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Reagents | acyl chloride RCOCl, with AlCl₃ catalyst, dry conditions |
| Electrophile | RCO⁺ (e.g. CH₃CO⁺ from ethanoyl chloride) |
| Forming the electrophile | RCOCl + AlCl₃ → RCO⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ |
| Overall (ethanoyl chloride) | C₆H₆ + CH₃COCl → C₆H₅COCH₃ + HCl |
| Catalyst regenerated | H⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ → AlCl₃ + HCl |
| Product | phenyl ketone (phenylethanone from ethanoyl chloride) |
| Importance | forms a new C–C bond to the ring: an important step in synthesis |

### Naming the products

| Acyl chloride | Electrophile | Product |
|---|---|---|
| CH₃COCl, ethanoyl chloride | CH₃CO⁺ | C₆H₅COCH₃, phenylethanone |
| CH₃CH₂COCl, propanoyl chloride | CH₃CH₂CO⁺ | C₆H₅COCH₂CH₃, 1-phenylpropan-1-one |
| CH₃CH₂CH₂COCl, butanoyl chloride | CH₃CH₂CH₂CO⁺ | C₆H₅COCH₂CH₂CH₃, 1-phenylbutan-1-one |

### Practical link

- Section 3.3.10 lists no required practical.
- The specification suggests preparing **methyl 3-nitrobenzoate** by nitrating methyl benzoate, then
  recrystallising it and measuring its melting point.
- Key steps: keep the mixture cold, add the nitrating mixture slowly, pour onto ice, filter under reduced
  pressure, recrystallise from the minimum volume of hot solvent, dry, then take the melting point.
- Pure: sharp melting point close to the data value. Impure: lower and over a range.
- These are the techniques of Required practical 10, listed under 3.3.9.2.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Alkene vs benzene:** alkenes do electrophilic **addition**; benzene does electrophilic
  **substitution**.
- **Localised vs delocalised:** an alkene's C=C electron density sits between two carbons; benzene's p
  electrons are spread over six.
- **Bond length vs bond type:** benzene's C–C bonds are all equal, between single and double.
- **Theoretical vs measured hydrogenation:** the triene value is predicted (3 × one C=C); benzene's is
  measured and less exothermic.
- **NO₂⁺ vs NO₂:** the electrophile is the nitronium **ion**; the group in the product is a neutral nitro
  group.
- **Acylation catalyst vs reagent:** AlCl₃ is the catalyst; the acyl chloride supplies the RCO group.
- **Ketone vs aldehyde:** acylation of benzene gives a ketone.

## Quick self-test

1. State the bond angle in benzene and the shape of the molecule.
2. Describe how the C–C bond lengths in benzene compare with C–C and C=C bonds.
3. ΔH hydrogenation of cyclohexene is −120 kJ mol⁻¹. Predict ΔH for hydrogenating
   cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene to cyclohexane.
4. Benzene's measured value is −208 kJ mol⁻¹. By how much is benzene more stable than the triene?
5. Give one reason why benzene does substitution, not addition.
6. Write an equation to form the electrophile in nitration.
7. Which atom of NO₂⁺ does arrow 1 point to?
8. Write an equation to show AlCl₃ being regenerated in acylation.
9. Name the product of benzene with ethanoyl chloride and AlCl₃.
10. Calculate the atom economy for making nitrobenzene: C₆H₆ + HNO₃ → C₆H₅NO₂ + H₂O.
    (Ar: H 1.0, C 12.0, N 14.0, O 16.0)
11. Calculate the atom economy for C₆H₆ + CH₃COCl → C₆H₅COCH₃ + HCl. (Ar: Cl 35.5)
12. Give two uses of nitration in synthesis.

### Answers

1. 120°; planar (flat hexagon).
2. All six are the same length, intermediate between a C–C single bond and a C=C double bond.
3. 3 × (−120) = **−360 kJ mol⁻¹**.
4. −208 − (−360) = +152, so benzene is **152 kJ mol⁻¹** more stable.
5. Addition would destroy the delocalised ring and its extra stability; substitution keeps it.
6. HNO₃ + 2H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + H₃O⁺ + 2HSO₄⁻ (or HNO₃ + H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + HSO₄⁻ + H₂O).
7. The nitrogen atom.
8. H⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ → AlCl₃ + HCl.
9. Phenylethanone, C₆H₅COCH₃.
10. Mr: C₆H₅NO₂ 123.0, C₆H₆ 78.0, HNO₃ 63.0. 123.0 / 141.0 × 100 = **87.2%**.
11. Mr: C₆H₅COCH₃ 120.0, CH₃COCl 78.5. 120.0 / 156.5 × 100 = **76.7%**.
12. Manufacture of explosives; making aromatic amines (by reducing the nitro compound), which are used to
    make dyes.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "bonds between single and double" without saying **length**, or drawing Kekulé double bonds
  when the question is about delocalisation.
- Giving the stability difference without saying that benzene is the **more stable** one and why
  (delocalisation).
- Saying the triene value is "measured" — it is a prediction.
- Explaining "no addition" by saying benzene has no double bonds, instead of loss of delocalisation.
- Arrow 1 starting from a carbon atom or a C–H bond rather than the ring.
- The intermediate drawn with a full circle, no **+**, or the horseshoe gap on the wrong side.
- Arrow 1 pointing at an O of NO₂⁺, or at the O of RCO⁺.
- Leaving out the equation that makes the electrophile, or the catalyst regeneration step.
- Forgetting HCl as the by-product of acylation.
- Naming the acylation product as an aldehyde or an acyl chloride.
