**These notes cover the prose question on Paper 4 Unseen**, which the syllabus describes as "based on
a passage of literary prose, such as an extract from a novel or short story", requiring "a critical
commentary" and an "appreciation of the text". They condense the
[unseen prose commentary study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-english-literature-0475-unseen-prose-commentary/);
for the paper as a whole see the [Paper 4 Unseen revision notes](/resources/igcse-english-literature-paper-4-unseen-revision-notes/).

## The task in brief

- One question from two: a prose passage or a poem.
- A critical commentary on the printed passage, focused by the question.
- Around 20 minutes reading and planning; then roughly 55 minutes writing.
- 25 marks; AO1 to AO4 equally weighted.

## Orientation checklist

- Narrative voice: first or third person; close to one character or wide-ranging.
- Characters, setting and time.
- What happens, in two sentences.
- The question's key words, underlined.
- Any introductory note printed with the passage.

## Sectioning the passage

Divide the extract into three or four sections. Give each a heading that describes what is happening
to the question's focus, for example: "ordinary day", "silent reaction", "displaced feeling",
"suppressed emotion". These headings become your paragraph plan.

## Prose methods and their effects

| Method | What to look for | Typical effect to explain |
|---|---|---|
| First-person narration | "I"; a limited view | Intimacy; possible bias or blind spots |
| Close third person | Narration filtered through one character | We share their perceptions and misreadings |
| Free indirect style | A character's thoughts within the narration | The reader hears the character without speech marks |
| Withholding | Information deliberately left out | Suspense; the reader infers, often feeling more |
| Repetition of a detail | A sound or object that recurs | Measures time or emotion; builds a motif |
| Short sentence or one-line paragraph | Sudden brevity | Shock, emphasis, a turning point |
| Long accumulating sentence | Clauses piled up with "and" or commas | Overwhelm, breathlessness, slowed time |
| Dialogue gaps | Questions unanswered, subjects changed | Evasion, tension, unspoken feeling |
| Pathetic fallacy | Weather or setting matching emotion | Mood; sometimes ironic contrast |
| Ironic contrast | Cheerful surroundings against bad news | Sharpens the painful feeling |
| Gesture | Small physical actions | Shows emotion without stating it |
| Ending of the passage | The final image or line | Often the strongest effect; analyse it |

## The commentary paragraph

**Point** answering the question → **short quotation** → **method** → **effect** on the reader →
**link** to the key word. One or two sentences of personal response, where they grow from the
analysis, meet AO4.

## Quick annotation example

Original sentence: "Her mother folded the letter along its creases, then folded it again, smaller,
as if a thing could be made to mean less by taking up less room."

- **Gesture:** the repeated folding shows emotion through action, not statement.
- **Placement of "smaller":** set apart by commas, the word slows the sentence and stresses the act.
- **Narrative comment:** "as if…" gives the narrator's insight into a desperate hope.
- **Withholding:** the letter's contents are never given, so the reader feels the shock through her.
- **Link to a "shock" question:** a controlled, careful action reveals how much she is trying to hold
  back.

Four or five such notes on the key moments of a passage are enough for a full commentary.

## Inferring without inventing

- Say what the passage implies ("suggests bad news") rather than what it does not state ("her
  husband has died").
- Where the passage withholds, comment on the effect of not knowing.
- Use the introductory note, if there is one, and never contradict it.

## Exam traps

- **Retelling** the passage in order.
- **Inventing back-story** for a novel extract.
- **Dialogue only:** description and pace matter as much.
- **Ignoring the narrator's viewpoint.**
- **Missing the final lines.**
- **Long quotations** with little analysis.
- **Choosing by habit:** read both questions before deciding on prose or poem.

## Self-test

1. How does the syllabus describe the prose passage on Paper 4?
2. What is the main danger in an unseen prose commentary, compared with poetry?
3. Name four things to establish in your first reading.
4. Why divide a passage into sections with headings?
5. What is free indirect style?
6. What is the effect of withholding information in a passage?
7. Give two effects of a one-line paragraph.
8. What is ironic contrast, and why is it effective?
9. Why should you not invent what happened before an extract?
10. What should you do with an introductory note printed with the passage?
11. Where in a passage does the strongest effect often come?
12. In the original sentence "The kettle boiled, clicked off, and nobody moved to make the tea", what
    does the writer suggest, and how?

**Answers:** 1. "a passage of literary prose, such as an extract from a novel or short story".
2. Retelling the events instead of selecting and analysing. 3. Any four: narrative voice; characters;
setting and time; what happens; the question's key words; any introductory note. 4. To track how the
focus changes and give the commentary a clear structure. 5. A character's thoughts blended into the
narration without speech marks. 6. It creates suspense and makes the reader infer, often intensifying
feeling. 7. Any two: shock; emphasis; marking a turning point; isolating an important detail.
8. Placing cheerful or ordinary surroundings against painful events; it sharpens the feeling by
contrast. 9. Because it is not in the text and cannot be supported with evidence; comment instead on
what is implied. 10. Use it to orient your reading and never contradict it. 11. In the final lines of
the passage. 12. That the characters are frozen by shock or tension; the writer shows it through an
ordinary process that completes itself while the people fail to act, ending on "nobody moved".
