These notes condense section 12.1, Experimental design, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). Every outcome is required for every candidate. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [experimental design study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-experimental-design/); then try the [experimental design practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-experimental-design-practice/).

## The three outcomes in one line each

1. **Name** the apparatus for time, temperature, mass and volume: stop-watch, thermometer, balance, burette, volumetric pipette, measuring cylinder, gas syringe.
2. **Suggest** advantages and disadvantages of methods and apparatus, with a reason linked to the measurement.
3. **Describe** solvent, solute, solution, saturated solution, residue and filtrate.

## Apparatus at a glance

| Measure | Use | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time | stop-watch | simple, reads to 1 s | human reaction time matters for short intervals |
| Temperature | thermometer | direct reading | 1 °C divisions, so read to 0.5 °C only |
| Mass | balance | quick, reads to at least 0.1 g | very small mass changes may not show |
| Fixed liquid volume | volumetric pipette | very precise for one volume | delivers only that volume |
| Variable liquid volume | burette | adds drops; reads to 0.05 cm³ | slower to set up; must be read at eye level |
| Rough liquid volume | measuring cylinder | quick, many sizes | less precise; choose a size close to the volume |
| Gas volume | gas syringe | gas not in contact with water | limited capacity; plunger may stick |

## Reading scales

- Read to the **nearest half-scale division** where required.
- Thermometer with 1 °C marks: 18.0 °C or 18.5 °C, never 18.3 °C.
- Burette with 0.1 cm³ marks: two decimal places ending in 0 or 5, for example 12.45 cm³.
- Read liquid volumes at the **bottom of the meniscus**, with your eye level with it.
- Always include the unit in a table heading or after the value.

## Judging a method: ready-made reasons

| Choice | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| Collect gas over water | cheap and simple | soluble gases (ammonia; some carbon dioxide) dissolve, so volume is low |
| Gas syringe | no contact with water | limited volume; can stick |
| Mass loss on a balance | follows the reaction continuously | useless for a very light gas such as hydrogen; spray must be stopped with cotton wool |
| Polystyrene cup | less heat loss than glass | can tip over; add a lid and stir |
| Repeat readings and take the mean | reduces random error; reveals anomalies | does not fix a systematic error |
| Smaller measuring cylinder for small volumes | reading is a larger fraction of the scale | must not overfill |

## The six terms

| Term | Definition to learn |
|---|---|
| Solvent | a substance that dissolves a solute |
| Solute | a substance that is dissolved in a solvent |
| Solution | a mixture of one or more solutes dissolved in a solvent |
| Saturated solution | a solution containing the maximum concentration of a solute dissolved in the solvent at a specified temperature |
| Residue | a substance that remains after evaporation, distillation, filtration or any similar process |
| Filtrate | a liquid or solution that has passed through a filter |

Memory hook: **the filtrate goes through; the residue remains.**

## Linking the terms

```
salt + sand + water, stirred
        │
     filter
   ┌────┴─────┐
residue      filtrate
(sand)       (salt solution: solute = salt, solvent = water)
                │
           evaporate
                │
          residue (salt)
```

The same experiment produces two different residues. "Residue" describes what stays behind in a process, not one particular substance.

## Key terms beyond the definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Precise reading | a reading taken from a scale with small divisions, so repeated readings agree closely |
| Anomalous result | a reading that does not fit the pattern of the others; leave it out of a mean |
| Random error | unpredictable scatter in readings; reduced by repeating |
| Systematic error | an error in the same direction every time, such as an unzeroed balance |
| Meniscus | the curved surface of a liquid in a narrow tube |

## Exam traps

- "Pipette" for a variable volume: wrong; that needs a burette.
- "Accurate" on its own scores little. Say **why**: fixed volume, finer divisions, no gas dissolves, less heat loss.
- A saturated solution needs **a stated temperature**.
- The filtrate can still contain dissolved substances.
- A residue can come from evaporation or distillation, not only filtration.
- Do not record more decimal places than the scale allows, and do not record fewer: a burette reading of "20" should be 20.00 cm³.
- Repeats do not remove systematic errors.
- Hydrogen is too light for a mass-loss method to work well with a school balance.

## Self-test

1. Name the apparatus used to measure the volume of gas given off in a reaction.
2. Name the apparatus that delivers exactly 25.0 cm³ of a solution.
3. Give one disadvantage of collecting ammonia over water.
4. A thermometer is marked every 1 °C. Which reading is correctly recorded: 21 °C, 21.5 °C or 21.48 °C?
5. Define a saturated solution.
6. Copper(II) sulfate solution is filtered to remove some dust. Name the dust and the solution in terms of the six terms.
7. Why is a polystyrene cup used instead of a glass beaker when measuring a temperature change?
8. State why a cotton wool plug is placed in the neck of a flask in a mass-loss experiment.
9. Why should a 10 cm³ measuring cylinder, not a 100 cm³ one, be used to measure 6 cm³ of acid?
10. What is left in the flask after a solution has been distilled to dryness called?
11. Suggest why timing a reaction that lasts 3 seconds with a stop-watch is unreliable.
12. Give one advantage of a burette over a measuring cylinder for adding acid to an alkali.

## Answers

1. Gas syringe (or an upturned measuring cylinder filled with water, for gases that do not dissolve).
2. Volumetric pipette.
3. Ammonia is very soluble in water, so it dissolves and little or no gas is collected.
4. 21.5 °C (21 °C should be written 21.0 °C; 21.48 °C claims more precision than the scale gives).
5. A solution containing the maximum concentration of a solute dissolved in the solvent at a specified temperature.
6. Dust: residue. Solution collected: filtrate.
7. Polystyrene is an insulator, so less energy is lost to the surroundings and the temperature change measured is closer to the true value.
8. To let gas escape while stopping liquid spray escaping, which would give a false loss in mass.
9. The 6 cm³ reading is a larger fraction of the smaller cylinder's scale, which has finer divisions, so the reading is more precise.
10. The residue.
11. The reaction time of the person starting and stopping the watch is a large fraction of 3 seconds.
12. It adds the acid drop by drop and the volume added can be read precisely (to 0.05 cm³).
