## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by globalisation. **[2]**

**2.** Country P records the following (billions of currency units). Year 1: exports 22, imports 26, GDP 150. Year 8: exports 45, imports 51, GDP 240.

**(a)** Calculate trade openness, (exports + imports) / GDP × 100 (a teaching device that the syllabus does not require), for each year. **[3]**
**(b)** Comment on what the change suggests about Country P. **[1]**

**3.** Define a multinational company and explain what is meant by its host country. **[3]**

**4.** An MNC employs 500 workers at 8,000 a year each in Country K. Its annual profit there is 18 million. Country K taxes profit at 25%, and 60% of profit after tax is sent to the MNC's home country.

**(a)** Calculate the wage bill and the tax paid. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the profit sent to the home country. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the profit sent home is a cost to Country K. **[2]**

**5.** Explain two benefits that an MNC may bring to a host country. **[4]**

**6.** Explain two costs that an MNC based in a home country may cause that country. **[4]**

**7.** In Country D the demand for shoes is Qd = 900 − 3P and the supply is Qs = −300 + 3P, where P is the price in dollars and Q is pairs of shoes (thousands).

**(a)** Calculate the equilibrium price and quantity with no international trade. **[2]**
**(b)** Free trade opens at a world price of 140. Calculate the quantity demanded, the quantity supplied and the quantity imported. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage fall in price. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain one effect of free trade on shoe producers in Country D. **[2]**

**8.** A furniture firm in a small country has fixed costs of 240,000 and a variable cost of 5 per unit.

**(a)** Calculate average cost at 30,000 units and at 120,000 units. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the fall in average cost as a percentage, to one decimal place. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how free trade could allow the firm to move from 30,000 to 120,000 units. **[2]**

**9.** Country X cuts corporation tax from 30% to 20%. An MNC earns 50 million profit a year.

**(a)** Calculate the tax payable at each rate. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the cut may affect where the MNC locates its head office, and one gain to Country X if it does. **[2]**

**10.** Explain two benefits of free trade for the economy of a country. **[4]**

**11.** An MNC plans a large factory in a developing country. Evaluate whether the host country is likely to gain. **[8]**

**12.** Country E is small and sells mainly farm products. Discuss whether free trade would benefit its consumers, producers and economy. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Economies becoming more tied together and dependent on each other [1], through trade, investment and the spread of technology or information [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "More trade" alone describes only part of the idea. A second element such as integration, investment or technology is what turns a partial answer into a full one.

**2. (a)** Year 1: (22 + 26) / 150 × 100 [1] = **32%** [1]. Year 8: (45 + 51) / 240 × 100 = **40%** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Openness rose by 8 percentage points, so Country P is more integrated with the world economy [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Write out the addition before the division so that your method can be followed. Say "percentage points" for a change between two percentages.

**3.** An MNC is a business with production or operations in two or more countries [1]. A host country is a place where it runs a factory, shop or office [1], as distinct from the home country that holds its head office [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the home country for contrast makes the host definition clear. Do not define an MNC as just a "large firm".

**4. (a)** Wages: 500 × 8,000 = **4,000,000** [1]. Tax: 25% × 18,000,000 = **4,500,000** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Profit after tax: 18,000,000 − 4,500,000 = 13,500,000 [1]. Sent home: 60% × 13,500,000 = **8,100,000** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The profit leaves the host economy [1], so it cannot be spent or invested there and local income is lower than the firm's profit suggests [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Use profit after tax as the base for the 60%. Using 18 million gives a different figure and loses the answer.

**5.** Jobs [1], so workers earn wages and spend them locally [1]; tax revenue from profits [1], which the government can spend on services [1]. Other acceptable benefits: technology and skills, or investment that raises capacity, each developed in the same way. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A benefit named without a consequence earns less than one with a consequence attached. Two separate benefits are required, not one repeated in different words.

**6.** Jobs lost [1] because production moves abroad, raising unemployment at home [1]; less investment at home [1], so capacity and future growth are lower [1]. Other acceptable costs: profits shifted to low-tax countries, or wages held down because the firm can threaten to move work abroad. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Answer from the home country's side. Writing about host-country benefits here is off-target.

**7. (a)** 900 − 3P = −300 + 3P [1]; 1,200 = 6P, so P = **200** and Q = 900 − 600 = **300** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Qd = 900 − 3(140) = **480** [1]; Qs = −300 + 3(140) = **120** [1]; imports = 480 − 120 = **360** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** (200 − 140) / 200 × 100 = **30%** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Output falls from 300 to 120 thousand [1], because foreign shoes at 140 undercut them, so firms lose sales and may cut jobs or close [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote figures from your own working in part (d). Giving a percentage change needs the original price as the base.

**8. (a)** 240,000 / 30,000 + 5 = **13** [1]; 240,000 / 120,000 + 5 = **7** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (13 − 7) / 13 × 100 = **46.2%** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Free trade gives access to buyers abroad [1], so output can rise beyond the small home market, spreading the fixed cost over more units [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show 6 / 13, not 6 / 7. Linking "bigger market" to "fixed cost per unit" is the chain the question wants.

**9. (a)** At 30%: 0.30 × 50,000,000 = **15,000,000** [1]. At 20%: **10,000,000** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The MNC keeps 5 million more profit each year in Country X [1]; if it moves its head office there, X gains jobs or tax revenue from the new activity [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The consequence has to be a gain for the country, not just a gain for the firm.

**10.** Specialisation [1], so more is produced from the same resources [1]; higher output and growth [1], because export demand adds to total demand [1]. Other acceptable benefits: jobs in export industries, or better resource allocation as resources move to efficient firms. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The question asks about the economy, so do not answer with lower prices for consumers alone.

**11.** One way to earn 8: benefits of jobs and wages [1], with development that incomes and spending rise [1]; tax and technology or skills [1], with development that these can raise future output [1]; costs of profit sent abroad [1], so less local income [1]; costs of pollution or exploitation [1]; a conclusion that says which side wins and why, for example "yes, if the MNC buys locally and trains workers, no if most profit leaves and jobs are few" [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A conclusion should hinge on a stated condition, such as local purchases or skills. A balanced list that stops without deciding often leaves marks unclaimed.

**12.** One way to earn 8: consumers gain lower prices [1] and more choice [1]; producers gain a larger market [1], which allows economies of scale [1]; the economy gains export earnings [1] and can import machinery [1]; a limit: the country relies on few farm exports and is exposed to falling world prices [1]; a judgement, such as that gains are likely overall but uneven, with some local producers losing [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Refer to Country E's size and farm exports in each paragraph. An answer that stays general about trade gains less than one built around this country.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining globalisation as "trade" alone.
- Using pre-tax profit as the base when a share of after-tax profit is sent home.
- Describing benefits without a consequence, such as "jobs" with no wages or spending.
- Writing about the host country when the question asks about the home country.
- Using the new value as the base when finding a percentage change.
- Giving only one side in an evaluation question.
- Leaving out a conclusion that judges which factor matters most.
- Answering a free trade question with tariffs and quotas.
