These notes condense sub-topic **10.1 Infectious diseases** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). It is AS content for Paper 1 and Paper 2, assumed on Paper 4. For explanations and worked examples, use the [infectious diseases study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-infectious-diseases-detail-cambridge-alevel-biology/); for exam-style questions, use the [infectious diseases practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-infectious-diseases-detail-cambridge-alevel-biology-practice/).

## The core statement

Infectious diseases are **caused by pathogens** and are **transmissible** (can be passed from one host to another).

## Pathogens to learn exactly

| Disease | Pathogen | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Cholera | *Vibrio cholerae* | Bacterium |
| Malaria | *Plasmodium falciparum*, *P. malariae*, *P. ovale*, *P. vivax* | Protoctist |
| TB | *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, *M. bovis* | Bacteria |
| HIV/AIDS | Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) | Virus |

Write the genus with a capital, species lower case; underline when handwritten.

## Transmission chains

| Disease | Leaves host in | Route | Enters next host by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cholera | Faeces | Water or food contaminated with sewage; unwashed hands | Swallowing |
| Malaria | Blood taken by a mosquito | Female *Anopheles* mosquito (vector); also transfusion, shared needles, placenta | Mosquito saliva into blood |
| TB (*M. tuberculosis*) | Droplets from coughs, sneezes, talking | Air, especially crowded, poorly ventilated places | Inhaling |
| TB (*M. bovis*) | Infected cattle | Unpasteurised milk, undercooked meat | Swallowing |
| HIV | Blood, semen, vaginal fluids, breast milk | Unprotected sex; shared needles; unscreened blood; placenta, birth, breastfeeding | Into blood; infects T-helper cells |

## Control: which link each method breaks

- **Cholera**: sewage treatment, chlorinated or boiled water, food hygiene, hand-washing (route); oral rehydration and treatment (source).
- **Malaria**: insecticide spraying, draining standing water, fish eating larvae (vector); bed nets, repellents (entry); antimalarial drugs (source); blood screening (route).
- **TB**: quick diagnosis and long supervised drug treatment (source); isolation, ventilation, less crowding (route); BCG vaccine (entry); pasteurisation, testing cattle (*M. bovis*).
- **HIV**: condoms, needle exchange, blood screening (route); anti-retroviral therapy (source); treatment in pregnancy (mother to child).

## Factors to discuss

| Factor type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Biological | Carriers; latent TB; HIV mutates and hides in T-helper cells; *Plasmodium* inside liver and red blood cells; drug-resistant bacteria and parasites; insecticide-resistant mosquitoes; standing water for breeding |
| Social | Crowding; poor sanitation after floods or war; refugee camps; stigma delaying testing; stopping treatment early; whether nets are used every night; education |
| Economic | Cost of sewage systems, nets, insecticides, long drug courses, lifelong ART; limited health budgets; lost income when ill |

**Discuss pattern**: method → factor → why it helps or limits control.

## Pathogen type → what works

- Bacterium → antibiotics possible.
- Protoctist → antimalarial drugs; vaccines difficult (inside cells).
- Virus → no antibiotics; antivirals slow replication.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Pathogen vs vector**: *Plasmodium* causes malaria; the mosquito only carries it.
- **Latent vs active TB**: latent has no symptoms and is not infectious; active TB in the lungs spreads in droplets.
- **HIV vs AIDS**: HIV is the virus; AIDS is the condition that develops when T-helper cells are so reduced that opportunistic infections take hold.
- **Infection vs disease**: a carrier is infected but shows no disease, yet can still transmit cholera.

### Worked reminder: rate per 1000 people

```
rate = cases ÷ population × 1000
240 cases in 160 000 people → 240 ÷ 160 000 × 1000 = 1.5 per 1000
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pathogen | Organism or virus that causes disease |
| Transmissible | Can be passed from one host to another |
| Infectious disease | Disease caused by a pathogen that can be transmitted |
| Host | Organism in which a pathogen lives |
| Vector | Organism carrying a pathogen between hosts (e.g. *Anopheles*) |
| Carrier | Infected person with no symptoms who can pass the pathogen on |
| Latent TB | Infection held in check: no symptoms, not infectious, may later become active |
| Faecal–oral route | Pathogen leaves in faeces and enters through the mouth |
| Anti-retroviral therapy | Drugs that reduce HIV replication; not a cure |

## Exam traps

- "Malaria is caused by mosquitoes": the mosquito is the vector.
- "*Plasmodium* is a bacterium": it is a protoctist.
- Cholera described as airborne; TB described as spread by touch.
- HIV "kills white blood cells": name T-helper lymphocytes.
- A list of control methods without factors for a "discuss" question.
- Writing out the malarial life cycle (not expected).
- Comparing raw case numbers between populations of different sizes.

## Self-test

1. State what makes a disease infectious.
2. Name the pathogen that causes cholera and its type.
3. Name the four species of *Plasmodium* in the syllabus.
4. Which two bacteria cause TB?
5. Explain how cholera reaches a new host.
6. What is a vector? Give the vector of malaria.
7. Give three ways HIV can be transmitted.
8. Why is someone with latent TB not a source of infection?
9. Suggest why HIV increases the number of active TB cases.
10. Give one biological factor that makes malaria hard to control.
11. Give one economic factor in controlling cholera.
12. District X: 90 cases in 30 000 people. District Y: 120 cases in 60 000. Which has the higher rate?

### Answers

1. It is caused by a pathogen and is transmissible.
2. *Vibrio cholerae*; bacterium.
3. *P. falciparum*, *P. malariae*, *P. ovale*, *P. vivax*.
4. *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* and *Mycobacterium bovis*.
5. Bacteria leave in faeces, contaminate water or food, and are swallowed.
6. An organism that carries a pathogen from one host to another; the female *Anopheles* mosquito.
7. Any three: unprotected sex; sharing needles; transfusion of unscreened blood; across the placenta; at birth; breast milk.
8. The bacteria are contained and not being released in droplets.
9. HIV destroys T-helper cells, weakening immunity, so latent TB becomes active.
10. Any one: insecticide resistance; drug resistance; parasite hidden in cells; mosquitoes breed in any standing water.
11. Sewage treatment and piped, chlorinated water are expensive to build and maintain.
12. X: 3.0 per 1000; Y: 2.0 per 1000; **X** is higher.
