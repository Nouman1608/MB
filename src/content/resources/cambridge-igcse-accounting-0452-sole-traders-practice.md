## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** The owner of a shop takes goods costing 300 for her own use. Which entry
records this?

A: debit Drawings, credit Sales

B: debit Drawings, credit Purchases

C: debit Purchases, credit Drawings

D: debit Capital, credit Inventory

**[1]**

**2.** Which of the following is an intangible asset?

A: goodwill

B: inventory

C: trade receivables

D: motor vehicle

**[1]**

**3.** State two advantages of operating as a sole trader. **[2]**

**4.** A machine cost 20,000. Accumulated depreciation at the start of the year is
7,200. Depreciation is charged at 20% a year using the reducing balance method.
Calculate the depreciation for the year. **[2]**

**5.** Loose tools were valued at 2,300 at the start of the year. Tools costing 650
were bought during the year and the tools were valued at 2,100 at the end of the
year. Calculate the depreciation charge. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why a sole trader applying for a bank loan should provide a
statement of financial position. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Sunita Rao is an interior design consultant. Her balances at 31 December
2028 include: fees received 68,400; commission received 2,300; salaries 24,600;
rent 7,800; office expenses 3,920; insurance 1,440; office equipment at cost
16,000; accumulated depreciation of office equipment at 1 January 2028 4,000;
trade receivables 5,200; allowance for irrecoverable debts at 1 January 2028 150;
bank 6,180; trade payables 1,260.

Additional information at 31 December 2028:

1. Fees of 1,600 for work completed in December have not been received or recorded.
2. Rent of 650 is prepaid and salaries of 900 are accrued.
3. Office equipment is depreciated at 25% a year on cost.
4. The allowance for irrecoverable debts is to be 5% of trade receivables.
5. Office expenses include 280 of Sunita's private expenses.

**(a)** Prepare Sunita's statement of profit or loss for the year ended
31 December 2028. **[8]**

**(b)** Prepare the current assets and current liabilities sections of her
statement of financial position at 31 December 2028. **[4]**

**8.** At 1 January 2028 Daniel's capital was 42,000. During 2028 he paid in a
further 5,000 of his savings, took 15,600 in cash, took goods costing 750, and the
business paid his home electricity bill of 420. Profit for the year was 19,300.

**(a)** Prepare the capital section of Daniel's statement of financial position at
31 December 2028. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why the home electricity bill is treated as drawings. **[2]**

**9.** State the effect of each error on profit for the year and on the statement
of financial position.

**(a)** Closing inventory was overstated by 900. **[2]**

**(b)** Depreciation of 1,200 on fixtures was not recorded. **[2]**

**(c)** The owner's private insurance of 300, paid by the business, was included in
insurance expense. **[2]**

**(d)** Rent received in advance of 500 was included in rent received. **[2]**

**10.** State the heading in a sole trader's statement of financial position under
which each item appears: goodwill; bank loan repayable in five years; bank
overdraft; insurance prepaid; shop fittings; accrued wages; bank loan repayable in
six months; trade receivables. **[4]**

**11.** Olu runs a phone shop that also repairs phones. For the year, revenue from
sales of phones was 54,000, cost of sales 32,400, repair fees 9,500 and expenses
21,800.

**(a)** Calculate gross profit and profit for the year. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the repair fees are not included in revenue when gross
profit is calculated. **[2]**

**12.** Kwame is a sole trader who needs 80,000 to open a second shop. Discuss two
disadvantages he faces in raising this finance and running a larger business as a
sole trader. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. Goods for own use are removed from purchases at cost. **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. Goodwill has no physical form. **[1]**

**3.** Any two from: the owner takes all decisions and can act quickly; keeps all
the profit; easy and cheap to set up; financial affairs remain private [1] [1]. **[2]**

**4.** (20,000 − 7,200) × 20% [1] = **2,560** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** 2,300 + 650 − 2,100 [1] = **850** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** It shows the assets the business owns, which could be security for the
loan [1], and the liabilities it already owes, so the bank can judge whether the
loan and interest can be repaid [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)**

```
Sunita Rao: Statement of profit or loss for the year ended 31 December 2028
Fees (68,400 + 1,600)                                      70,000
Commission received                                         2,300
                                                           72,300
Less expenses
   Salaries (24,600 + 900)                       25,500
   Rent (7,800 − 650)                             7,150
   Office expenses (3,920 − 280)                  3,640
   Insurance                                      1,440
   Depreciation: office equipment                 4,000
   Increase in allowance for irrecoverable debts    110     41,840
Profit for the year                                        30,460
```

Fees 70,000 [1]; commission added [1]; salaries 25,500 [1]; rent 7,150 [1];
office expenses 3,640 [1]; depreciation 4,000 [1]; increase in allowance 110
(260 − 150) [1]; profit for the year 30,460 [1].

**(b)**

```
Current assets
   Trade receivables                     5,200
   Less allowance                         (260)    4,940
   Other receivables (1,600 + 650)                 2,250
   Bank                                            6,180
                                                  13,370
Current liabilities
   Trade payables                                  1,260
   Other payables                                    900
                                                   2,160
```

Net trade receivables 4,940 [1]; other receivables 2,250 [1]; total current
assets 13,370 [1]; current liabilities 2,160 [1]. **[12]**

**8. (a)**

```
Capital
   Opening balance                         42,000
   Add capital introduced                   5,000
   Add profit for the year                 19,300
                                           66,300
   Less drawings (15,600 + 750 + 420)      16,770
                                           49,530
```

Opening balance and capital introduced [1]; profit added [1]; drawings of
16,770 [1]; closing capital 49,530 [1].

**(b)** It is a private expense of the owner, not an expense of running the
business [1], so it is a withdrawal of business resources: it is removed from
expenses and added to drawings (business entity concept) [1]. **[6]**

**9. (a)** Profit overstated by 900 [1]; current assets (inventory) and capital
overstated by 900 [1].

**(b)** Profit overstated by 1,200 [1]; non-current assets (net book value) and
capital overstated by 1,200 [1].

**(c)** Profit understated by 300 [1]; drawings understated by 300, so closing
capital is correct [1].

**(d)** Profit overstated by 500 [1]; current liabilities (other payables)
understated by 500 [1]. **[8]**

**10.** One mark for each two correct [1] [1] [1] [1]: goodwill: intangible
assets; loan repayable in five years: non-current liabilities; bank overdraft:
current liabilities; insurance prepaid: current assets (other receivables); shop
fittings: non-current assets; accrued wages: current liabilities (other payables);
loan repayable in six months: current liabilities; trade receivables: current
assets. **[4]**

**11. (a)** Gross profit = 54,000 − 32,400 = **21,600** [1]. Profit for the
year = 21,600 + 9,500 − 21,800 = **9,300** [1].

**(b)** Gross profit measures the profit on buying and selling phones, so only
revenue from goods and their cost of sales are compared [1]. Repair fees come from
a service with no cost of sales line, so they are added below gross profit as
other income; including them would distort the gross profit margin [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Finance: Kwame can only use his own savings and loans, and lenders may
refuse or charge high interest because the business is small and depends on one
person [1], so raising 80,000 may be difficult or expensive [1]. Liability: he has
unlimited liability, so if the second shop fails his personal possessions could be
taken to pay the business's debts [1]; running two shops alone also increases his
workload, and there is no one to share decisions or cover absence [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Deducting goods for own use from revenue instead of purchases.
- Charging reducing balance depreciation on cost.
- Leaving private expenses in the statement of profit or loss.
- Missing capital introduced in the capital section.
- Charging the whole allowance instead of the change.
