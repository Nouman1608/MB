These questions cover section 11.6, Alcohols, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). Learn the content first with the [alcohols study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-alcohols/) and the [alcohols revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-alcohols-revision-notes/).

Use Mr values: C₆H₁₂O₆ = 180, C₂H₅OH = 46.

## Section A

**1.** State **two** uses of ethanol. **[2]**

**2.** Which set of conditions is used to make ethanol from ethene?

A: 25–35 °C, yeast, no oxygen

B: 300 °C, 6000 kPa, acid catalyst

C: 450 °C, 20 000 kPa, iron catalyst

D: 300 °C, 6000 kPa, nickel catalyst

**[1]**

**3.** Name the two products of the fermentation of glucose. **[2]**

**4.** State why the raw material for fermentation is described as renewable. **[1]**

**5.** Name the products of the complete combustion of an alcohol. **[1]**

**6.** State the method used to obtain concentrated ethanol from the mixture produced by fermentation. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Ethanol is made by fermentation.

**(a)** State the temperature range used. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the temperature is not raised above this range. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why oxygen must be absent. **[1]**

**(d)** Write the balanced symbol equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**8.** Ethanol is also made from ethene.

**(a)** Write the equation for this reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** State the type of reaction. **[1]**

**(c)** State where the ethene comes from. **[1]**

**9.** Compare the manufacture of ethanol by fermentation with its manufacture from ethene. Give **two** advantages and **two** disadvantages of fermentation. **[4]**

**10.** Write balanced equations for the complete combustion of:

**(a)** ethanol, C₂H₅OH **[2]**

**(b)** butan-1-ol, C₄H₉OH **[2]**

**11.** A company ferments 270 g of glucose.

**(a)** Calculate the number of moles of glucose. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of ethanol that can be formed. **[2]**

**(c)** The company actually obtains 92 g of ethanol. Calculate the percentage yield. **[1]**

**12.** Two countries plan to make ethanol. Country P has large sugar cane plantations and no oil. Country Q has oil fields and refineries that crack hydrocarbons. Suggest which method each country should choose and explain your choices. **[4]**

**13.** A student balances the combustion of methanol as: CH₃OH + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O.

**(a)** Show that this equation is not balanced. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the correct balanced equation. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain the student's error. **[1]**

**14.** Ethanol is used as a fuel.

**(a)** Explain why ethanol made by fermentation is described as a renewable fuel. **[1]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the incomplete combustion of ethanol to form carbon monoxide and water. **[1]**

**(c)** State why incomplete combustion is dangerous. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Solvent [1]; fuel [1]. **[2]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* A is fermentation, C is the Haber process and D uses the catalyst for adding hydrogen, not steam.

**3.** Ethanol [1]; carbon dioxide [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Glucose comes from plants, which can be regrown (replaced) [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Carbon dioxide and water (both needed) [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Fractional distillation [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** 25–35 °C [1]

**(b)** Higher temperatures denature the enzymes in the yeast, so fermentation stops [1]

**(c)** In oxygen the yeast respires aerobically and ethanol is not produced / ethanol would be oxidised to ethanoic acid [1]

**(d)** C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂: correct formulae [1]; balanced [1]

**[5]**

**8. (a)** C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH [1]

**(b)** Addition (hydration) [1]

**(c)** From cracking larger alkanes obtained from petroleum [1]

**[3]**

**9.** Advantages of fermentation, any two [1] each: renewable raw material; low temperature and atmospheric pressure, so low energy cost; simple equipment.

Disadvantages of fermentation, any two [1] each: slow; produces a dilute, impure solution that needs fractional distillation; not continuous; also produces carbon dioxide. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* a disadvantage of fermentation can be written as an advantage of the ethene route ("the ethene route is faster"), but the question asks about fermentation, so phrase it that way: "fermentation is slow".

**10. (a)** C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O: correct products [1]; balanced [1]

**(b)** C₄H₉OH + 6O₂ → 4CO₂ + 5H₂O: correct products [1]; balanced [1]

**[4]**

**11. (a)** 270 ÷ 180 = 1.5 mol [1]

**(b)** 1.5 × 2 = 3.0 mol ethanol [1]; 3.0 × 46 = 138 g [1]

**(c)** 92 ÷ 138 × 100 = 66.7% [1]

**[4]**

*Examiner insight:* the 1 : 2 mole ratio from the equation is the step most often missed, giving 69 g in (b).

**12.** P: fermentation [1], because it has plenty of renewable plant material (sugar cane) to supply glucose and no petroleum for ethene [1]. Q: steam and ethene [1], because cracking at its refineries supplies ethene, and the method is fast, continuous and gives pure ethanol [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* each reason must come from the information about the country. General advantages that ignore the scenario do not earn the explanation marks.

**13. (a)** Oxygen: left 1 + 4 = 5 atoms; right 2 + 2 = 4 atoms [1]

**(b)** 2CH₃OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 4H₂O [1]

**(c)** The student did not count the oxygen atom already in the methanol molecule [1]

**[3]**

**14. (a)** The glucose comes from plants (crops), which can be grown again [1]

**(b)** C₂H₅OH + 2O₂ → 2CO + 3H₂O [1]

**(c)** Carbon monoxide is a toxic gas [1]

**[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving 6000 atm instead of 6000 kPa, or missing the acid catalyst.
- Writing "with oxygen" for fermentation.
- Listing advantages of one method only in a comparison question.
- Forgetting the oxygen atom in the alcohol when balancing combustion equations.
- Missing the 1 : 2 ratio of glucose to ethanol in calculations.
