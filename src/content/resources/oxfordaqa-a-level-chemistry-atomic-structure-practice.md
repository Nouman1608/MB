## Questions

**1.** A particle contains 17 protons, 18 neutrons and 18 electrons. Give its symbol including the mass number, atomic number and charge. **[3]**

**2.** Chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 are isotopes. Explain what the term means and why the two behave identically in reactions. **[3]**

**3.** Describe how the results of firing alpha particles at thin gold foil led scientists to change the plum pudding model of the atom. **[3]**

**4.** (a) For ⁵⁵Mn²⁺ (atomic number 25), give the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons. **[3]**
**(b)** Give the relative charge and relative mass of an electron. **[2]**

**5.** Describe what happens to the ions in the acceleration and ion drift stages of a time of flight (TOF) mass spectrometer, and explain why ions of different mass arrive at the detector at different times. **[4]**

**6.** An element contains three isotopes. Its mass spectrum has these peaks.

| m/z | 64 | 66 | 68 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relative abundance | 48.0 | 28.0 | 24.0 |

Calculate the relative atomic mass to 4 significant figures. **[3]**

**7.** An element R has two isotopes, ⁷⁹R and ⁸¹R. Its relative atomic mass is 80.2. Calculate the percentage abundance of each isotope, and state which peak is taller in the mass spectrum. **[4]**

**8.** The mass spectrum of a compound has its peak of highest m/z at 72. State what this peak represents and what it shows about the compound. Write an equation, with state symbols, for the formation of this ion from a molecule M in the ionisation stage. **[3]**

**9.** Write the full electron configuration of each of the following, using s, p and d notation.

**(a)** Vanadium atom (Z = 23) **[1]**
**(b)** Chromium atom (Z = 24) **[1]**
**(c)** Co²⁺ (Z = 27 for the atom) **[1]**
**(d)** Se²⁻ (Z = 34 for the atom) **[1]**

**10.** (a) Define the first ionisation energy. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the equation, including state symbols, for the removal of the third electron from aluminium. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the first ionisation energy of barium is lower than that of beryllium, and state what this is evidence for. **[4]**

**11.** Account for the drop in first ionisation energy from magnesium to aluminium, and again from phosphorus to sulfur. State what each trend suggests about the structure of the atom. **[6]**

**12.** The successive ionisation energies of a Period 3 element (invented data) are given in kJ mol⁻¹.

| Number removed | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ionisation energy | 760 | 1480 | 3200 | 4300 | 16000 | 19500 | 23300 | 29400 |

**(a)** Deduce the group of the element and give its electron configuration. **[3]**
**(b)** Write the equation for the fifth ionisation energy. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain the large increase between the fourth and fifth values. **[2]**
**(d)** State which successive ionisation energy shows the next very large increase, and explain why. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Protons = 17 so the element is chlorine, atomic number 17 [1]; mass number = 17 + 18 = 35 [1]; electrons exceed protons by one so the charge is 1−: **³⁵Cl⁻** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the mass number as a superscript on the left and the charge on the right; a symbol with the numbers the wrong way round is easy to misread.

**2.** Isotopes have nuclei with an equal number of protons (so they are one element) [1] but different numbers of neutrons [1]. Their electron arrangements are identical, and reactions are governed by the electrons [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Same number of protons" and "different number of neutrons" both need stating; saying "different mass" alone describes the effect and not the cause.

**3.** The great majority of alpha particles went undeviated, so most of the atom is empty [1]. A small fraction rebounded through big angles, so positive charge is concentrated in one place [1]. This showed a small, dense, positive nucleus, not positive charge spread through the whole atom as the plum pudding model said [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each observation to its conclusion; listing observations with no inference usually leaves marks unclaimed.

**4.** (a) Protons 25 [1]; neutrons 55 − 25 = 30 [1]; electrons 25 − 2 = 23 [1]
**(b)** Relative charge −1 [1]; relative mass about 1/1840 (very small compared with 1 for a proton) [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* For ions, subtract a positive charge from the proton number to get electrons. Either "1/1836" or "1/1840" shows the right idea, but "0" is too loose.

**5.** In acceleration an electric field acts on the positive ions [1], giving every ion the same kinetic energy [1]. In the drift region there is no field and ions move at constant speed [1]. With equal kinetic energy, lighter ions are faster so reach the detector sooner and heavier ions take longer [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The key phrase is "the same kinetic energy". Saying "the same speed" contradicts the explanation of why times differ.

**6.** Total abundance = 48.0 + 28.0 + 24.0 = 100.0 [1]; (64 × 48.0 + 66 × 28.0 + 68 × 24.0) / 100.0 = 6552 / 100.0 [1]; **65.52** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the sum divided by the total abundance. Check that the answer lies between 64 and 68 and nearer the largest peak.

**7.** Let x be the percentage of ⁸¹R: 79(100 − x) + 81x = 80.2 × 100 [1]; 2x = 120 so x = 60 [1]; ⁸¹R is **60%** and ⁷⁹R is **40%** [1]; the peak at m/z 81 is taller [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A quick sense check helps: 80.2 is closer to 81 than to 79, so the heavier isotope must be the more abundant one.

**8.** It is the molecular ion [1], so the relative molecular mass is 72 [1]. M(g) + e⁻ → M⁺(g) + 2e⁻ [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the ion with its positive charge and balance the electrons; "M⁺" with a missing second electron on the right is a frequent slip.

**9.** (a) **1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d³ 4s²** [1]
**(b)** **1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁵ 4s¹** [1]
**(c)** 25 electrons, with the 4s electrons lost first: **1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁷** [1]
**(d)** 36 electrons: **1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p⁶** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Count the total electrons in your answer against the atomic number and charge before moving on.

**10.** (a) The energy change when each atom in a mole of gaseous atoms loses one electron [1], forming a mole of gaseous 1+ ions [1]
**(b)** Al²⁺(g) → Al³⁺(g) + e⁻ [1]
**(c)** Barium's outermost electron sits in a shell much more distant from the nucleus [1] and is screened by extra inner electrons [1]; this outweighs the greater nuclear charge, so less energy is needed [1]; this is evidence for electron shells [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* An answer that cites only "more protons" or only "more shells" is incomplete; the argument needs distance, shielding and the comparison with nuclear charge.

**11.** Al: its outermost electron occupies 3p, a sub-shell higher in energy and slightly further from the nucleus than 3s [1], and is shielded by the 3s electrons [1], so it is removed more easily than a 3s electron of Mg [1]. This is evidence for sub-shells [1]. S: two of its 3p electrons share one orbital [1], and mutual repulsion within that pair makes one electron leave more readily than the unpaired outer electron of P [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the sub-shell in each explanation and say what the trend is evidence for; many answers explain the dip but never state the conclusion the question asks for.

**12.** (a) The biggest jump comes after the fourth electron, so there are four outer electrons [1]: Group 4(14) (silicon) [1]; 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p² [1]
**(b)** X⁴⁺(g) → X⁵⁺(g) + e⁻ [1]
**(c)** The fifth electron comes from the second shell, which is closer to the nucleus [1] and less shielded, so it is held much more strongly [1]
**(d)** The thirteenth [1], because it is the first electron removed from the 1s shell closest to the nucleus [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Count electrons removed before the jump, not the position of the largest number. For the next jump, add the sizes of the shells: 4 outer, then 8, then 2.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating electrons as A − Z for an ion, or adding rather than subtracting a positive charge.
- Describing isotopes with differences in "mass" or "atomic number".
- Saying ions are accelerated to the same speed, rather than the same kinetic energy.
- Not dividing by the total abundance when the percentages do not add to exactly 100.
- Giving a relative atomic mass outside the range of the isotopic masses.
- Writing 4s before 3d in an ion, or losing 3d electrons before 4s.
- Omitting state symbols or the electron in ionisation equations.
- Explaining the Group 2 trend without both shielding and distance.
- Describing a dip in ionisation energy without naming the sub-shell or stating what it shows.
