These notes condense statements B3.3f to B3.3j of module B3.3 in OCR GCSE (9-1) Gateway Science Biology A (J247), specification Version 4.0 (August 2026), for first assessment in 2018. B3.3i and B3.3j are in bold in the specification, so they are tested only in the Higher Tier papers; they are labelled below. ## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Osmosis | Water moving through a partially permeable membrane, from a dilute region to a more concentrated one |
| Water potential | Describe as higher (dilute), lower (concentrated) or equal; no calculations needed |
| Lysis | An animal cell bursting after taking in too much water |
| Filtrate | Fluid forced out of the blood into the Bowman's capsule |
| Reabsorption | Substances moving from the tubule back into the blood |
| ADH | Hormone that changes how permeable the collecting duct is to water (Higher Tier only) |
| Negative feedback | A response that undoes the change which set it off |
| Water balance | Water gained equals water lost |

## B3.3f: Cells and osmotic changes

| Fluid around an animal cell | Water moves | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Higher water potential than the cell | In | Swells, may burst (lysis) |
| Equal | No net movement | No change |
| Lower water potential than the cell | Out | Shrinks |

Why animal cells burst: no cell wall to resist swelling. That is why blood and tissue fluid are kept at a steady water potential.

Method for data questions:
1. Find the solution where the cell volume or mass does not change: equal water potential.
2. Solutions with a smaller concentration than that gain water; stronger ones lose water.
3. Percentage change = (new - original) / original x 100.

Small reminder: a cell volume going from 40 to 34 units is (34 - 40) / 40 x 100 = -15%, a 15% fall.

## B3.3h: Structure

Urinary system: renal artery (blood in) and renal vein (blood out) -> kidney -> ureter -> bladder -> urethra.

| Region of kidney | Contains |
|---|---|
| Cortex | Bowman's capsules, convoluted tubules |
| Medulla | Loops of Henlé, collecting ducts |
| Pelvis | Collects urine and passes it to the ureter |

Order of the tubule: Bowman's capsule, proximal convoluted tubule, loop of Henlé, collecting duct.

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| Bowman's capsule | Filtration under pressure: water, glucose, urea and ions leave the blood; cells and proteins do not |
| Proximal convoluted tubule | Selective reabsorption: all glucose, much water and many ions go back to the blood |
| Loop of Henlé | More water reabsorbed; fluid becomes more concentrated |
| Collecting duct | Final adjustment of water reabsorbed; carries fluid to the pelvis |

## B3.3g: Water balance

| Water gained | Water lost |
|---|---|
| Drinks | Urine |
| Food | Sweat |
| Respiration | Breath, faeces |

The kidneys alter the **volume** and **concentration** of urine to match what the body needs.

| Body state | Urine |
|---|---|
| Too much water | Large volume, dilute |
| Too little water | Small volume, concentrated |

Method for water balance sums: add all gains, add all non-urine losses, urine = gains - non-urine losses (when balance is kept).

Small reminder: gains 2400 cm³, non-urine losses 1100 cm³, so urine = 2400 - 1100 = 1300 cm³.

## B3.3i: ADH and negative feedback (Higher Tier only)

- Released into the blood by the pituitary gland; the hypothalamus detects the water potential of the blood.
- Target: the kidney tubules, mainly the collecting duct.
- ADH raises the permeability of the walls to water, so more water is reabsorbed.

| | More ADH | Less ADH |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Blood too concentrated | Blood too dilute |
| Permeability | Up | Down |
| Water reabsorbed | More | Less |
| Urine | Small volume, concentrated | Large volume, dilute |

Feedback chain (write it in this order):
1. Blood water potential falls.
2. Hypothalamus detects it.
3. More ADH is released.
4. Collecting duct more permeable, more water reabsorbed.
5. Blood water potential rises to normal.
6. Less ADH is released.

## B3.3j: Challenges (Higher Tier only)

| Challenge | Blood becomes | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Hot weather, heavy sweating, dehydration | More concentrated | Thirst; more ADH; little concentrated urine |
| Drinking too much | More dilute | Less ADH; much dilute urine |
| Very salty food | More concentrated | Thirst; more ADH; water kept, salt lost in small volume of concentrated urine |

Thirst is a response of behaviour: it makes you take in water, while the kidneys reduce losses. Both are needed.

Risk of failure: dehydration shrinks cells; very large drinks dilute body fluids so cells swell.

Small reminder: a person of mass 55.0 kg falls to 54.2 kg after a hot run. Loss = 0.8 kg, which is 0.8 / 55.0 x 100 = 1.5% of body mass.

## Answer frames to learn

Use these as skeletons and swap in the details of the question.

**Explain why a cell shrinks.** The fluid has a lower water potential than the cytoplasm. Water moves out of the cell by osmosis, through the partially permeable membrane. The cell loses volume and shrinks.

**Describe how the kidney makes concentrated urine (Higher Tier only).** The blood is too concentrated. The hypothalamus detects this. More ADH is released. The collecting duct becomes more permeable to water. More water is reabsorbed into the blood. A small volume of concentrated urine is produced.

**Explain the response to excess water (Higher Tier only).** The blood is more dilute. Less ADH is released. The collecting duct is less permeable. Less water is reabsorbed. A large volume of dilute urine is produced, which removes the extra water.

## Reading data in this module

Typical tables give urine volume against time, cell volume against solution concentration, or urine volume against ADH level. Steps:
1. Read the axis labels and units before you look at the numbers.
2. Describe the trend first (rises, falls, levels off), quoting two values with units.
3. Then explain it using water potential, permeability and water reabsorbed.
4. For percentage questions, show the subtraction, the division by the original value and the multiplication by 100.

Small reminder: urine volume falls from 1300 cm³ to 400 cm³ on a hot day. Change = 400 - 1300 = -900, and -900 / 1300 x 100 = -69.2%, a fall of about 69%.

## Must-know distinctions

- Filtration (in Bowman's capsule, by pressure, not selective for useful substances) versus reabsorption (later, selective).
- Concentrated urine (small volume, little water) versus dilute urine (large volume, much water).
- High ADH is a response to a concentrated blood; low ADH is a response to a dilute blood.
- Water potential: higher means more dilute; water moves from higher to lower.
- Cortex (capsules) versus medulla (loops and collecting ducts).

## Quick self-test

1. What happens to a red blood cell placed in a fluid with a lower water potential than its cytoplasm?
2. Why does an animal cell burst in pure water?
3. Name the four parts of the kidney tubule in order.
4. Which region of the kidney contains the Bowman's capsules?
5. A person gains 2400 cm³ of water and loses 1100 cm³ by routes other than urine. How much urine keeps the balance?
6. State two substances forced into the Bowman's capsule that are not proteins or blood cells.
7. (Higher Tier only) Where is ADH released from, and where does it act?
8. (Higher Tier only) State the effect of ADH on the collecting duct.
9. (Higher Tier only) Describe the urine produced after a very salty meal and give the reason.
10. (Higher Tier only) Explain why drinking a lot of water makes the urine pale and plentiful.
11. A cell volume falls from 40 to 34 units. Calculate the percentage change.

### Answers

1. Water leaves by osmosis and the cell shrinks.
2. Water enters by osmosis, and with no cell wall the membrane tears (lysis).
3. Bowman's capsule, proximal convoluted tubule, loop of Henlé, collecting duct.
4. The cortex.
5. 2400 - 1100 = 1300 cm³.
6. Any two of water, glucose, urea, ions.
7. Released from the pituitary gland into the blood; acts on the kidney tubules, mainly the collecting duct.
8. It increases the permeability of the duct walls to water, so more water is reabsorbed.
9. Small volume and concentrated: the blood is too concentrated, so more ADH is released and more water is reabsorbed; excess salt leaves in the urine.
10. The blood becomes more dilute, less ADH is released, the ducts are less permeable, and less water is reabsorbed.
11. (34 - 40) / 40 x 100 = -15%, a fall of 15%.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying a cell "bursts" without saying that water enters by osmosis.
- Using vague words such as "weaker" or "stronger" water instead of describing a higher or lower water potential.
- Saying the kidney makes ADH, or that ADH "tells the kidney to keep water" without naming permeability.
- Saying that ADH changes the amount of urine without saying the permeability of the collecting duct changes.
- Describing only one direction of the feedback when the question asks for the response to too much water.
- Missing the stage where the blood returns to normal and ADH release falls.
- Writing the percentage change without a sign or direction, or using the final value as the divisor in place of the starting value.
- Confusing the cortex and the medulla, or placing the loop of Henlé before the proximal convoluted tubule.
- Forgetting thirst when asked for the response to dehydration.
