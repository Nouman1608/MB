## Equations and conversions

| What | How |
|---|---|
| Energy transferred | Q = m × c × ΔT (J) |
| c for water and dilute solutions | 4.2 J/g °C |
| Mass of a dilute solution | 1 cm³ ≈ 1 g; use the total volume |
| Joules to kilojoules | ÷ 1000 |
| Energy per gram | kJ ÷ mass of substance used (g) |
| Energy per mole | kJ ÷ moles that reacted |
| ΔH sign | Temperature rose → negative; temperature fell → positive |
| Bond energy calculation | energy to break bonds − energy released forming bonds |
| Activation energy from a diagram | peak − reactant level |
| ΔH from a diagram | product level − reactant level |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Calorimetry | Measuring energy changes from temperature changes |
| Specific heat capacity | Energy needed to raise 1 g of a substance by 1 °C |
| Insulated container | Reduces energy exchange with the surroundings (polystyrene cup with lid) |
| Energy level diagram | Graph of energy against reaction progress |
| Activation energy | Minimum energy needed for particles to react |
| Bond dissociation energy | Energy needed to break one mole of a given bond |
| Catalyst | Provides a different pathway with lower activation energy |

## Two calorimetry set-ups

| | Fuel calorimetry (3.9.2 a) | Solution calorimetry (3.9.2 c) |
|---|---|---|
| Apparatus | Spirit burner under a glass or metal container of water | Polystyrene cup with lid and thermometer |
| m in Q = mcΔT | Mass of water heated | Total mass of solution (or mass of water for a solid) |
| Divide by | Mass or moles of fuel burned | Moles of the limiting reactant (or of water formed) |
| Main energy losses | Heating the container and air; incomplete combustion; evaporation of fuel | Energy exchanged through the cup and lid; slow reaction |
| Improvements | Draught shield, lid, short flame-to-can distance, stirring | Lid, stirring, record highest or lowest temperature |

## Energy level diagram checklist

- Axes: energy (up), progress of reaction (across).
- Reactant and product levels labelled with formulae or names.
- Curved line from reactants rising to a peak, then falling to products.
- ΔH arrow from reactant level to product level: down for exothermic, up for endothermic.
- Activation energy arrow from reactant level up to the peak.
- Catalysed curve: lower peak, same start and end levels.

Example: reactants 300 kJ, peak 380 kJ, products 120 kJ → activation energy 80 kJ, ΔH −180 kJ, exothermic.

## Bond energies

- Breaking bonds **takes in** energy (endothermic step).
- Making bonds **releases** energy (exothermic step).
- Exothermic overall: released by forming > needed for breaking (3.9.2 f).
- Endothermic overall: needed for breaking > released by forming (3.9.2 g).

Method: draw or picture each displayed formula, count every bond, multiply by coefficients, total each side, then subtract.

| Reaction | Broken (kJ) | Formed (kJ) | Change (kJ) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O | 2(436) + 498 = 1370 | 4(464) = 1856 | −486 |
| N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ | 945 + 3(436) = 2253 | 6(391) = 2346 | −93 |
| 2HBr → H₂ + Br₂ | 2(366) = 732 | 436 + 193 = 629 | +103 |

(Bond energies in kJ/mol: H–H 436, O=O 498, O–H 464, N≡N 945, N–H 391, H–Br 366, Br–Br 193.)

## Catalysts (3.9.2 h)

- Different reaction pathway.
- Lower activation energy, so more particles have enough energy to react.
- Same reactant and product levels, so **ΔH unchanged**.

## Exam traps

- Putting the mass of fuel in Q = mcΔT.
- Adding the mass of a solid to the mass of water when the question says to use the water.
- Forgetting to multiply bond energies by the number of each bond.
- Giving kJ/mol when the calculation produced J/mol.
- Missing the minus sign for exothermic values.
- Measuring activation energy from the product level.
- Drawing the catalysed curve finishing at a different level.

## Quick self-test

1. Calculate Q when 100 g of water warms by 15.0 °C.
2. Convert 4500 J into kJ.
3. A fuel releases 12.0 kJ when 0.40 g burns. Calculate the energy per gram.
4. Why is a polystyrene cup used for reactions in solution?
5. 40.0 cm³ of acid is mixed with 40.0 cm³ of alkali. What value of m is used?
6. The temperature of a solution falls during a reaction. Give the sign of ΔH.
7. On a diagram, reactants are at 80 kJ and the peak is at 210 kJ. Calculate the activation energy.
8. On the same diagram, products are at 150 kJ. Calculate ΔH and classify the reaction.
9. What does a catalyst do to the activation energy and to ΔH?
10. Bonds broken total 1200 kJ and bonds formed total 1450 kJ. Calculate the energy change.
11. Why is bond breaking endothermic?
12. Give one reason why fuel calorimetry gives a less exothermic value than a data book.

### Answers

1. 100 × 4.2 × 15.0 = 6300 J
2. 4.5 kJ
3. 12.0 ÷ 0.40 = 30 kJ/g
4. It is a good insulator, reducing energy exchange with the surroundings.
5. 80.0 g
6. Positive (endothermic).
7. 210 − 80 = 130 kJ
8. 150 − 80 = +70 kJ; endothermic.
9. Lowers the activation energy; no change to ΔH.
10. 1200 − 1450 = −250 kJ (exothermic).
11. Energy must be supplied to overcome the attraction holding the atoms together.
12. Energy heats the container and the air (or incomplete combustion, or fuel evaporating).
