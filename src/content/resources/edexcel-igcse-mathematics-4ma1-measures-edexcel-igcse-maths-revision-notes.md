Condensed recall for section 4.4 (Measures) of the 4MA1 specification. It is Foundation Tier content that the Higher Tier also assumes. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [Measures study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-measures-edexcel-igcse-maths/); exam-style questions with answers are in the [Measures practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-measures-edexcel-igcse-maths-practice/).

## The seven statements in one line each

- **A** Read scales: find the value of one division first.
- **B** Time intervals in 12-hour (am/pm) and 24-hour clocks.
- **C** Sensible estimates of length, mass, capacity and other measures.
- **D** Angle measure, including three-figure bearings.
- **E** Measure an angle to the nearest degree.
- **F** Average speed, distance and time.
- **G** Compound measures: speed, density, pressure (pressure formula given).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Division (on a scale) | The gap between two neighbouring marks |
| Acute / obtuse / reflex | Less than 90° / between 90° and 180° / between 180° and 360° |
| Bearing | Angle measured clockwise from north, written with three figures |
| Back bearing | The bearing for the return direction: differs by 180° |
| Average speed | Total distance ÷ total time |
| Compound measure | A measure combining two others, such as km/h or g/cm³ |
| Density | Mass ÷ volume |
| Pressure | Force ÷ area |

## Scales

```
one division = (difference between labels) ÷ (number of gaps)
```

Labels 0 and 1 kg with 4 gaps → 250 g per gap. Labels 20 and 30 with 5 gaps → 2 per gap. Count gaps, not marks. Below zero, values get more negative as you move away from 0.

## Time

- am = midnight to noon; pm = noon to midnight.
- 24-hour clock always has four digits: 07:05, not 7:5.
- 12:xx am → 00:xx. 12:xx pm → 12:xx. Any other pm time → add 12 to the hour.
- Time intervals: count on to the next hour, then whole hours, then minutes.

**Minutes ↔ decimal hours**

| Minutes | Hours |
|---|---|
| 6 | 0.1 |
| 15 | 0.25 |
| 20 | 0.333… |
| 30 | 0.5 |
| 40 | 0.666… |
| 45 | 0.75 |

Minutes → hours: ÷ 60. Hours → minutes: × 60. So 2.6 h = 2 h 36 min, and 1 h 12 min = 1.2 h.

## Estimates

Compare with a known size: door ≈ 2 m, adult ≈ 1.5–1.9 m, bag of sugar ≈ 1 kg, teaspoon ≈ 5 ml. Round the estimate and state the assumption. Choose a sensible unit: mm or cm for small objects, m for rooms and buildings, km for journeys.

## Angles and bearings

- Protractor: centre on the vertex, zero line on one arm, read the scale that starts at 0 on that arm.
- Reflex angle = 360° − the angle on the other side.
- Bearings: from north, clockwise, three figures.

| Direction | Bearing |
|---|---|
| North-east | 045° |
| East | 090° |
| South-east | 135° |
| South | 180° |
| South-west | 225° |
| West | 270° |
| North-west | 315° |

**Back bearing:** add 180° if the bearing is less than 180°; subtract 180° if it is more. 064° ↔ 244°; 290° ↔ 110°.

"The bearing of X **from** Y" → stand at Y.

## Speed, distance, time

```
speed = distance ÷ time
distance = speed × time
time = distance ÷ speed
```

- Average speed for several stages = **total** distance ÷ **total** time (including stops if the question says the whole journey).
- Never average two speeds.
- km/h → m/s: ÷ 3.6. m/s → km/h: × 3.6. Example: 72 km/h = 20 m/s; 12 m/s = 43.2 km/h.

## Density and pressure

```
density = mass ÷ volume      mass = density × volume      volume = mass ÷ density
pressure = force ÷ area      force = pressure × area      area = force ÷ pressure
```

- Learn the density formula; the pressure formula is printed in the question.
- Keep units consistent: g with cm³ gives g/cm³; kg with m³ gives kg/m³; N with m² gives N/m².
- Mixtures: add masses, add volumes, then divide. Never average the densities.
- Same force, smaller area → bigger pressure.

## Exam traps

- Counting marks instead of gaps on a scale.
- Calculator subtraction of clock times (13.10 − 9.45) instead of counting on.
- Writing 1.3 h for 1 h 30 min (it is 1.5 h).
- Two-figure bearings (45° instead of 045°) or measuring anticlockwise.
- Standing at the wrong point for "the bearing of A from B".
- Mixing kg with cm³, or cm² with N/m².

## Self-test

1. A scale has labels 100 and 200 with 4 gaps between. What is one division worth?
2. Write 12:20 am in the 24-hour clock.
3. Find the time interval from 06:47 to 11:05.
4. Write 3.4 hours in hours and minutes.
5. The bearing of B from A is 218°. Find the bearing of A from B.
6. Write down the three-figure bearing for south-east.
7. A car travels 150 km in 2 h 30 min. Find its average speed.
8. Change 25 m/s to km/h.
9. A runner goes 6 km at 12 km/h and then 6 km at 8 km/h. Find her average speed for the 12 km.
10. A block has mass 540 g and volume 60 cm³. Find its density.
11. A force of 250 N acts on 0.05 m². Find the pressure.
12. Estimate the height of a bus that is about twice the height of a door.

### Answers

1. 100 ÷ 4 = 25.
2. 00:20.
3. 13 min + 4 h + 5 min = 4 h 18 min.
4. 0.4 × 60 = 24, so 3 h 24 min.
5. 218° − 180° = 038°.
6. 135°.
7. 150 ÷ 2.5 = 60 km/h.
8. 25 × 3.6 = 90 km/h.
9. Times 0.5 h and 0.75 h, total 1.25 h; 12 ÷ 1.25 = 9.6 km/h (not 10).
10. 540 ÷ 60 = 9 g/cm³.
11. 250 ÷ 0.05 = 5000 N/m².
12. About 2 × 2 = 4 m.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do you find the bearing of A from B if you know the bearing of B from A?**
Add 180° if the bearing is less than 180°, or subtract 180° if it is more. For example, if B is on a bearing of 218° from A, then A is on a bearing of 038° from B.

**Why can't you average two speeds to get the average speed?**
The object usually spends different amounts of time at each speed. Average speed must be total distance divided by total time; 6 km at 12 km/h then 6 km at 8 km/h gives 9.6 km/h, not 10 km/h.
