OCR GCSE (9-1) Economics (J205) is an introduction to how economies work, starting with
the choices individuals and firms make in markets and moving out to government policy
and the global economy. It is a course for students meeting economics for the first
time, and it was first assessed in 2019. There are two compulsory components,
Introduction to Economics and National and International Economics, each examined by a
written paper of 1 hour 30 minutes worth 80 marks and 50% of the GCSE. Both components
include synoptic assessment. This page explains the shape of the course and how to plan
your way through it.

## How the course is organised

The content is set out in four topics, moving from the individual to the global.

**Topic 1: Introduction to economics.** The main economic groups and the factors of
production, and the basic economic problem of meeting unlimited wants with scarce
resources.

**Topic 2: The role of markets and money.** How markets work, through demand, supply and
price; competition and production; the labour market; and the role of money and
financial markets.

**Topic 3: Economic objectives and the role of government.** The objectives of economic
growth, low unemployment, a fair distribution of income and price stability; the
policies governments use to pursue them (fiscal, monetary and supply-side policies);
and the limitations of markets.

**Topic 4: International trade and the global economy.** The importance of international
trade, the balance of payments, exchange rates and globalisation.

Topics 1 and 2 are mainly about individual markets, while Topics 3 and 4 look at the
economy as a whole and its place in the world.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| J205/01 | Introduction to Economics | 1 hour 30 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| J205/02 | National and International Economics | 1 hour 30 minutes | 80 | 50% |

Both components are compulsory, and the GCSE is not tiered, so every student sits the
same two papers. The course is linear, with both papers taken at the end.

OCR states that both components include synoptic assessment. In practice that means
neither paper can be prepared for in isolation: a question in Component 02 on
government policy, for example, may rely on your understanding of supply and demand,
and a question in Component 01 may ask you to link a market change to the wider
economy. The exam preparation page explains what this means for revision in more
detail.

## How the parts fit together

The course is built as a sequence in which each topic depends on those before it. The
basic economic problem in Topic 1 underlies every later choice, from how a firm decides
what to produce to how a government decides where to spend. Demand, supply and price in
Topic 2 are the tools for explaining unemployment, inflation and the effects of policy
in Topic 3, and for explaining how exchange rates are determined in Topic 4. The labour
market and the role of money in Topic 2 connect directly to the objectives of low
unemployment and price stability.

Alongside the content, a consistent set of skills runs through both papers:

- **Recall and understanding** of key terms and concepts.
- **Application** of ideas to the scenario or data in a question.
- **Quantitative skills**, such as calculating percentage changes and interpreting
  tables and graphs.
- **Analysis and evaluation**, which means explaining the likely effects of a change
  and reaching a supported judgement.

## Planning your study

The topic order is the natural teaching order. A sensible plan across the course is:

- **First stage:** Topics 1 and 2, with plenty of practice drawing and explaining demand
  and supply diagrams and applying them to real markets.
- **Second stage:** Topics 3 and 4, linking each new idea back to the market analysis
  from the first stage.
- **Final months:** revision across all four topics, full timed papers for both
  components and focused practice on calculations and longer answers.

Keep a running glossary of key terms, since precise definitions earn marks across both
papers, and follow economic news so that you have up-to-date examples for application
questions. Because both papers are synoptic, mix topics in your revision sessions
rather than revising one paper at a time. Start exam-style questions as soon as each
topic has been taught, and turn to the exam preparation page once you are moving on to
full papers.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in OCR GCSE Economics?**
Two: Introduction to Economics (J205/01) and National and International Economics (J205/02). Each lasts 1 hour 30 minutes, carries 80 marks and is worth 50%.

**Is OCR GCSE Economics tiered?**
No. All students sit the same two papers.

**What does it mean that both J205 components are synoptic?**
Each paper can draw on content from across the course, so topics need to be revised together rather than paper by paper.

**What are the four topics in OCR GCSE Economics?**
Introduction to economics; the role of markets and money; economic objectives and the role of government; and international trade and the global economy.

**When was J205 first assessed?**
The J205 specification was first assessed in 2019.
