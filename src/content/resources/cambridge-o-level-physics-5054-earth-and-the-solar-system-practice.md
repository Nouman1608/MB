Take the speed of light in a vacuum as 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s and 1 day = 86 400 s.

## Section A

**1.** Light from the Sun takes about 500 s to reach the Earth. Calculate the distance from the Sun to the Earth in metres. **[2]**

**2.** List the eight planets of the Solar System in order, starting with the planet nearest the Sun. **[2]**

**3.** State what is meant by the orbital period of a planet. **[1]**

**4.** Classify each of the following as a planet, a minor planet or a moon.

**(a)** A body that orbits Saturn. **[1]**
**(b)** Pluto. **[1]**
**(c)** A rocky body orbiting the Sun in the region between Mars and Jupiter. **[1]**

**5.** State **two** facts about how the strength of a gravitational field depends on mass and on distance. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why the radius of the Earth's orbit is given as an average value. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A satellite orbits the Earth at an average radius of 2.66 × 10⁷ m. Its orbital period is 12 hours.

**(a)** Calculate its average orbital speed. **[3]**
**(b)** A second satellite orbits at 4.2 × 10⁷ m with a speed of 3.1 × 10³ m/s. State how orbital speed changes with orbital radius, using both satellites. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the force that keeps both satellites in orbit. **[1]**

**8.** The table gives data for four planets of a distant star. The data are invented for this question.

| Planet | Orbital distance (10⁶ km) | Orbital period (days) | Density (kg/m³) | Surface temperature (°C) | Surface field strength (N/kg) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alpha | 70 | 110 | 5000 | 250 | 6.2 |
| Beta | 150 | 345 | 5400 | 60 | 4.0 |
| Gamma | 400 | 1503 | 3200 | −90 | 7.5 |
| Delta | 1100 | 6852 | 1400 | −190 | 15 |

**(a)** Describe how the orbital period changes with orbital distance. Quote data in your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the average orbital speed of Gamma in km/s. **[3]**
**(c)** A student says, "The densest planet has the strongest gravitational field at its surface." Use the data to show that this is wrong, and state what surface field strength actually depends on. **[2]**
**(d)** A fifth planet orbits at 2000 × 10⁶ km. Predict whether its surface temperature is above or below −190 °C. **[1]**

**9.** An astronaut has a mass of 75 kg.

**(a)** Calculate the astronaut's weight on the Earth, where g = 9.8 N/kg. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the astronaut's weight on a planet with a surface field strength of 11 N/kg. **[1]**
**(c)** State the astronaut's mass on that planet and explain your answer. **[2]**

**10.** Venus has an average orbital radius of 1.08 × 10¹¹ m and an orbital period of 225 days.

**(a)** Calculate the average orbital speed of Venus. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the time taken for light from the Sun to reach Venus. **[2]**
**(c)** Without further calculation, state whether the Earth's orbital speed is greater or less than that of Venus, and give a reason. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why Neptune takes much longer than the Earth to orbit the Sun. **[3]**

**12.** Explain why the gravitational field strength at the surface of the Sun is greater than at the surface of any planet. **[2]**

**13.** Describe the force acting on a planet in a near-circular orbit and its effect on the planet's motion. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** distance = speed × time = 3.0 × 10⁸ × 500 [1] = **1.5 × 10¹¹ m** [1].

**2.** All eight correctly named [1]; in the correct order: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune [1].

**3.** The time taken for the planet to complete one orbit (of the Sun) [1].

**4. (a)** Moon [1]. **(b)** Minor planet (a dwarf planet) [1]. **(c)** Minor planet (an asteroid in the asteroid belt) [1].

**5.** The field strength at the surface of a planet depends on (increases with) the planet's mass [1]; the field strength around a planet decreases as distance from the planet increases [1].

**6.** The orbit is an ellipse, not a perfect circle, so the Earth–Sun distance varies [1]; because the ellipse is approximately circular, an average radius can represent it in v = 2πr / T [1].

**7. (a)** T = 12 × 3600 = 43 200 s [1]; v = 2πr / T = 2π × 2.66 × 10⁷ ÷ 43 200 [1] = **3.9 × 10³ m/s** [1].
**(b)** The satellite with the larger orbital radius has the lower speed (3.1 × 10³ m/s compared with 3.9 × 10³ m/s), so speed decreases as radius increases [1].
**(c)** The gravitational attraction of the Earth [1].

**8. (a)** The orbital period increases as orbital distance increases [1], from 110 days at 70 × 10⁶ km (Alpha) to 6852 days at 1100 × 10⁶ km (Delta) [1].
**(b)** T = 1503 × 86 400 = 1.30 × 10⁸ s [1]; v = 2π × 400 × 10⁶ ÷ 1.30 × 10⁸ [1] = **19 km/s** [1].
**(c)** Beta is the densest planet (5400 kg/m³) but has the weakest surface field (4.0 N/kg), while Delta has the lowest density but the strongest field (15 N/kg) [1]; surface field strength depends on the mass of the planet [1].
**(d)** Below −190 °C, since surface temperature falls as orbital distance increases [1].

**9. (a)** W = mg = 75 × 9.8 [1] = **735 N** [1].
**(b)** W = 75 × 11 = **825 N** [1].
**(c)** 75 kg [1]; mass is the quantity of matter in the astronaut, which does not depend on the gravitational field, whereas weight does [1].

**10. (a)** T = 225 × 86 400 = 1.94 × 10⁷ s [1]; v = 2π × 1.08 × 10¹¹ ÷ 1.94 × 10⁷ [1] = **3.5 × 10⁴ m/s** [1].
**(b)** t = distance ÷ speed = 1.08 × 10¹¹ ÷ 3.0 × 10⁸ [1] = **360 s** [1].
**(c)** Less than that of Venus [1]; the Earth is further from the Sun, and orbital speeds decrease as distance from the Sun increases [1].

**11.** Neptune is much further from the Sun, so its orbital path (2πr) is much longer [1]; the Sun's gravitational field is weaker at Neptune's distance [1], so Neptune's orbital speed is lower; a longer path travelled at a lower speed gives a much longer orbital period [1].

**12.** Surface field strength depends on the mass of the body [1]; the Sun contains most of the mass of the Solar System, far more than any planet [1].

**13.** The force is the gravitational attraction of the Sun [1], directed towards the Sun, at right angles to the planet's direction of motion [1]; it continuously changes the direction of the planet's velocity, so the planet follows a curved (near-circular) path instead of a straight line [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving the period in hours or days while the radius is in metres.
- Writing the force as "gravity" without saying it is the Sun's (or the Earth's) gravitational attraction.
- Using density, rather than mass, to explain surface field strength.
- Quoting a trend from a table without any figures.
- Stating that mass changes on another planet.
- Using r as the distance for one orbit instead of 2πr.
