## What the specification lists

- Explain a budget and a variance.
- Calculate and interpret budgets and variances.
- Analyse budgets and variances (favourable and adverse).
- Evaluate the impact of budgets and variances on a business.
- Evaluate the usefulness of the budgeting process and variance analysis.

## Core recall

### Budget

- A financial plan for a future period, in money terms.
- Types: income (sales), expenditure (costs) and profit budgets.
- Purposes: planning, control, coordination, motivation, delegation, evaluation.
- Usually based on last year's actuals, adjusted for expected changes; best agreed with the people responsible.

### Variance

- Difference between budget and actual.
- **Variance = actual − budget**, then label it.
- Percentage variance = variance ÷ budget × 100.

| Item | Favourable | Adverse |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | Actual above budget | Actual below budget |
| Costs | Actual below budget | Actual above budget |
| Profit | Actual above budget | Actual below budget |

Test: does it raise or lower profit?

### Profit variance check

Profit variance = sum of the item variances (favourable positive, adverse negative).

### Analysing a variance

1. How big is it (amount and %)?
2. What caused it (internal, external, or a flawed budget)?
3. Is it controllable?
4. What action follows?
5. Does it link to other variances (lower sales should lower materials)?

### Impact

- Adverse: lower profit and cash; may need cuts or funding.
- Favourable: may mean a cautious budget or neglected spending.
- Depends on size, duration and reversibility.
- Stakeholders: managers (accountability), owners, employees (pressure), lenders (risk), suppliers.

### Usefulness

| | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|
| Budgeting | Direction, control, coordination, early warning | Time-consuming; based on forecasts; inflexible; managers build in slack; short-term focus |
| Variance analysis | Shows gaps; allows action; accountability | Backward-looking; does not explain causes alone; can overload managers |

Variances should be investigated by significance, not all equally.

## Quick worked reminder

Hollin Foods (invented): budget revenue £150,000, actual £141,000 (£9,000 adverse, 6%); materials £60,000 against £63,600 (£3,600 adverse); labour £35,000 against £33,800 (£1,200 favourable); overheads £25,000 against £25,500 (£500 adverse). Budgeted profit £30,000, actual £18,100: £11,900 adverse (39.7%). With sales 6% down, materials should have been about £56,400, so the real overspend is £7,200.

## Layout for a variance table

Use five columns: item, budget, actual, variance and label (favourable or adverse). Work down the table in the order revenue, costs, profit.

1. Revenue variance = actual revenue − budgeted revenue. If positive, it is favourable.
2. Cost variance = actual cost − budgeted cost. If positive, it is adverse.
3. Profit: calculate budgeted and actual profit separately, then subtract.
4. Check: add favourable variances, subtract adverse ones, and compare with the profit variance.
5. Add the percentage of budget for any variance you will analyse.

## Writing the analysis and evaluation

- Name the variance, give its size and say whether it is favourable or adverse.
- Give a likely cause from the case, and say if it is controllable.
- State the consequence (profit, cash, a stakeholder).
- Give an action: investigate, change supplier, revise the budget, or do nothing.
- Link variances: for example, lower sales should mean lower materials, so an adverse materials variance with lower sales is worse than it looks.
- Finish an evaluation with a judgement: budgets and variance analysis are useful for control, but only if budgets are realistic and variances are acted on.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Budget | A financial plan for a future period |
| Variance | The difference between budget and actual |
| Favourable variance | One that increases profit compared with budget |
| Adverse variance | One that reduces profit compared with budget |
| Budget holder | A manager responsible for a budget |
| Income budget | A budget for expected revenue |
| Expenditure budget | A budget for allowed spending |
| Profit budget | The planned profit from income and expenditure budgets |
| Slack | Extra allowance built into a budget |

## Exam traps

- A cost above budget is adverse, not favourable.
- Always state the amount and the label.
- Check: item variances add to the profit variance.
- Do not stop at calculation; analyse cause and action.
- Favourable is not always good.

## Self-test

1. Define a budget.
2. Define a variance.
3. Revenue budget £80,000; actual £84,000. State the variance.
4. Wages budget £30,000; actual £32,400. State the variance.
5. Calculate the wage variance as a percentage of the budget.
6. Give one purpose of budgeting.
7. Explain why a favourable variance may not be good news.
8. Give two possible causes of an adverse materials variance.
9. Budgeted profit £22,000; actual profit £24,700. State the variance.
10. Give one weakness of budgeting.
11. Why should managers help to set their own budgets?
12. What does a profit variance of £11,900 adverse mean for annual profit if repeated for 12 months?

### Answers

1. A financial plan for a future period, in money terms.
2. The difference between a budgeted figure and the actual figure.
3. £4,000 **favourable** (actual is higher than budget).
4. £2,400 **adverse** (cost is above budget).
5. 2,400 ÷ 30,000 × 100 = **8%**.
6. Any one: planning, control, coordination, motivation, delegation, evaluation.
7. It may show that the budget was too cautious, or that something such as maintenance or training was left undone.
8. Any two: higher supplier prices, waste, a change in product mix, theft, an unrealistic budget.
9. £2,700 **favourable**.
10. Any one: it is time-consuming, based on forecasts, inflexible, or encourages slack.
11. They know the work, so the budget is more realistic, and they are more committed to meeting it.
12. 12 × £11,900 = **£142,800** lower profit.
