## The nervous system (Core)

- **Electrical impulses** travel along **neurones**.
- **CNS:** brain + spinal cord.
- **PNS:** nerves outside the brain and spinal cord.
- **Role:** coordination and regulation of body functions.

## Three neurones (Core)

| Neurone | From → to | Cell body position | Recognise it by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sensory | receptor → CNS | side branch, part-way along | fibre on both sides of the cell body |
| Relay | sensory → motor, within CNS | middle of a short cell | short; inside spinal cord |
| Motor | CNS → effector | at one end, in the CNS | long fibre ending on muscle or gland |

## Reflex arc (Core)

```
receptor → sensory neurone → relay neurone → motor neurone → effector
```

Add stimulus at the start and response at the end when applying it.

**Reflex action:** a means of **automatically** and **rapidly** integrating and coordinating stimuli with the responses of **effectors (muscles and glands)**.

### Reflex examples to practise

| Stimulus | Receptor | Effector | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hot object touched | temperature / pain receptors in skin | arm muscle | hand withdrawn |
| Dust near the eye | touch receptors near the eye | eyelid muscles | blink |
| Food in the mouth | chemical receptors in the tongue | salivary glands | saliva secreted |
| Bright light (see sense organs) | light receptors in the retina | iris muscles | pupil narrows |

### Reflex vs voluntary

| | Reflex | Voluntary |
|---|---|---|
| Conscious thought | no | yes |
| Speed | rapid | slower |
| Same response each time | yes | can vary |

## Synapses

**Core:** a synapse is a **junction between two neurones**.

**Extended only: structure**

- **Vesicles** containing **neurotransmitter** molecules (in the first neurone).
- **Synaptic gap** (between the neurones).
- **Receptor proteins** (on the next neurone).

**Extended only: events in order**

1. Impulse stimulates release of neurotransmitter from vesicles into the gap.
2. Neurotransmitter **diffuses** across the gap.
3. Neurotransmitter **binds** with receptor proteins on the next neurone.
4. Impulse is stimulated in the next neurone.

**Extended only: one direction.** Synapses ensure impulses travel in one direction only: vesicles are on one side, receptor proteins on the other.

## Worked reminders

**Labelling a reflex-arc diagram.** Find the receptor (skin) and effector (muscle). The neurone joining the receptor to the spinal cord is sensory. The one from the spinal cord to the muscle is motor. The short one inside the spinal cord linking them is relay.

**Answering "suggest why a reflex is rapid".** Few neurones, few synapses, and no conscious decision in the brain.

**Answering "suggest why reflexes are useful".** They protect the body by responding before harm builds up, for example withdrawing a hand before a burn becomes severe.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Neurone vs nerve:** one cell compared with a bundle of many neurones.
- **Receptor vs effector:** detects the stimulus compared with carries out the response.
- **Sensory vs motor neurone:** towards the CNS compared with away from the CNS.
- **Synapse vs neurone (Extended):** an impulse travels electrically along a neurone, but a chemical carries the signal across the synapse.

## Command words

- **Identify** a neurone: name it; if asked how, say where it connects.
- **Describe** a reflex arc: five parts in order, applied to the named example.
- **Explain** why an action is a reflex: automatic and rapid, no conscious decision.
- **Describe** synapse events (Extended): four steps with "release", "diffuse", "bind", "stimulated".

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Neurone | Nerve cell that carries electrical impulses |
| Nerve | Bundle of neurones |
| CNS | Central nervous system: brain and spinal cord |
| PNS | Peripheral nervous system: nerves outside the brain and spinal cord |
| Receptor | Cell or group of cells that detects a stimulus |
| Effector | Muscle or gland that carries out a response |
| Reflex action | Automatic, rapid response coordinating a stimulus with effectors |
| Synapse | Junction between two neurones |
| Neurotransmitter | Chemical released at a synapse that crosses the gap (Extended) |
| Receptor protein | Protein on the next neurone that binds neurotransmitter (Extended) |

## Exam traps

- Spinal cord left out of the CNS.
- Relay neurone missing from a reflex arc.
- Effectors described as muscles only.
- "The brain decides" in a reflex answer.
- "Neurones touch at a synapse."
- (Extended) "The impulse jumps the gap": the neurotransmitter **diffuses** across it.
- (Extended) Vesicles placed on the receiving neurone.

## Self-test

1. What travels along neurones?
2. Name the two parts of the CNS.
3. What does the PNS consist of?
4. Which neurone carries impulses from the CNS to an effector?
5. Where is the relay neurone found in a reflex arc?
6. Write the five parts of a simple reflex arc in order.
7. Name the two types of effector.
8. Give two features of a reflex action from the syllabus description.
9. What is a synapse?
10. (Extended) Name three structures found at a synapse.
11. (Extended) How do neurotransmitter molecules cross the synaptic gap?
12. (Extended) Explain why impulses cross a synapse in one direction only.

### Answers

1. Electrical impulses.
2. Brain and spinal cord.
3. Nerves outside the brain and spinal cord.
4. Motor neurone.
5. In the CNS (spinal cord).
6. Receptor, sensory neurone, relay neurone, motor neurone, effector.
7. Muscles and glands.
8. Any two of: automatic; rapid; integrates and coordinates stimuli with the responses of effectors.
9. A junction between two neurones.
10. Vesicles containing neurotransmitter, synaptic gap, receptor proteins.
11. By diffusion.
12. Neurotransmitter is released only from vesicles in the first neurone, and receptor proteins are only on the next neurone.
