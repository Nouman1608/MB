These notes condense one content point from key development 3.1.4.1 of the 9236 specification:
"social and cultural developments up to 1658, including religion, literature, education, music and
architecture". The full explanation is in the
[Mughal culture to 1658 study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-pakistan-studies-9236-mughal-culture-to-1658/);
the political narrative is in the
[Mughal Empire revision notes](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-pakistan-studies-mughal-empire-revision-notes/).

## Three traditions combined

- **Persian:** court language, literature, painting, garden design.
- **Central Asian Turkic:** Babur's heritage and memoir.
- **Indian:** music, Sanskrit learning, local building styles, Rajput alliances.

## Religion by reign

| Emperor | Key points |
|---|---|
| Babur, Humayun | Sunni; Humayun's exile in Persia brought Persian influence |
| Akbar | Pilgrim tax (1563) and jizya (1564) abolished; Rajput marriages from 1562; Ibadat Khana (1575); Mahzar (1579); Din-i-Ilahi (1582); sulh-i-kul |
| Jahangir | Toleration largely continued; Guru Arjan executed (1606) |
| Shah Jahan | More orthodox public religion; Dara Shikoh's Sufi and Hindu studies |

**Opposition:** Ahmad Sirhindi urged a return to the Sharia.

## Literature

| Work | Author | Note |
|---|---|---|
| *Baburnama* | Babur | Memoir in Chagatai Turkish; translated into Persian under Akbar |
| *Humayun-nama* | Gulbadan Begum | History by Babur's daughter |
| *Akbarnama*, *Ain-i-Akbari* | Abul Fazl | Official history; description of government and customs |
| Critical history | Badauni | Opposed Akbar's religious policy |
| *Razmnama* | Akbar's translators | Persian *Mahabharata* |
| *Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri* | Jahangir | Memoir |
| *Padshahnama* | Abdul Hamid Lahori | Official history of Shah Jahan |
| *Majma-ul-Bahrain* | Dara Shikoh | Comparison of Sufi and Hindu ideas |

## Education, music and painting

- **Education:** maktabs and madrasas; Akbar's wider curriculum (ethics, arithmetic, agriculture,
  geometry, astronomy, medicine, history); imperial libraries; educated royal women (Gulbadan,
  Nur Jahan, Jahanara).
- **Music:** Tansen at Akbar's court from 1562; dhrupad; Persian and Indian traditions blended.
- **Painting:** Persian masters under Humayun; Akbar's mixed workshop and the *Hamzanama*;
  Jahangir's naturalism (Ustad Mansur, Abu'l Hasan); Shah Jahan's *Padshahnama* scenes.

## Buildings to name

| Emperor | Building | Place |
|---|---|---|
| Babur | Charbagh gardens | Agra |
| Akbar (for Humayun) | Humayun's Tomb | Delhi |
| Akbar | Agra Fort; Fatehpur Sikri, Buland Darwaza; Lahore Fort rebuilt | Agra, Fatehpur Sikri, Lahore |
| Jahangir and Nur Jahan | Akbar's tomb completed; Itimad-ud-Daulah's tomb | Sikandra, Agra |
| After Jahangir's death | Jahangir's tomb | Shahdara, Lahore |
| Shah Jahan | Taj Mahal (begun 1632) | Agra |
| Shah Jahan | Red Fort, Jama Masjid | Delhi |
| Shah Jahan | Shalimar Gardens, Sheesh Mahal, Wazir Khan Mosque | Lahore |
| Shah Jahan | Shah Jahan Mosque | Thatta |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sulh-i-kul | "Peace with all": Akbar's principle of toleration |
| Ibadat Khana | House of Worship for religious debate at Fatehpur Sikri |
| Mahzar | 1579 declaration of Akbar's authority over scholars' disputes |
| Din-i-Ilahi | Akbar's small court discipline of loyalty and ethics |
| Charbagh | Four-part garden divided by water channels |
| Pietra dura | Inlay of coloured stones in marble |
| Maktab | Elementary school, often attached to a mosque |
| Madrasa | Institution of higher religious and legal learning |
| Dhrupad | Classical vocal style associated with Tansen |

## Society and court life

- **Jharokha darshan:** the emperor's daily appearance at a palace window, begun by Akbar.
- **Chain of justice:** Jahangir's bell chain for petitioners, described in his memoir.
- **Festivals:** Nauroz at court; Akbar's participation in Hindu festivals.
- **Women:** Nur Jahan's political influence and coins in her name; royal women as patrons.
- **Sarais and roads:** travel, trade and the spread of court culture.

## What each development shows

| Development | What it shows about the empire |
|---|---|
| Akbar's toleration | Stability through the loyalty of a mainly non-Muslim population |
| Persian histories | The emperor's wish to record and justify his rule |
| Translation of Sanskrit works | Cultural exchange encouraged from the top |
| Great buildings | Wealth, piety, power and the blending of traditions |
| Court painting | Imperial patronage and a taste for record and detail |
| Educated royal women | Learning valued within the imperial family |

## Exam traps

- Claiming Din-i-Ilahi was a mass religion.
- Placing the Badshahi Mosque (Aurangzeb) before 1658.
- Calling the Taj Mahal a palace.
- Listing achievements without saying what they show about the empire.
- Forgetting the Lahore and Thatta buildings that link Mughal culture to Pakistan.

## Self-test

1. Name the three traditions that combined in Mughal culture.
2. In which years did Akbar abolish the pilgrim tax and the jizya?
3. What was the Ibadat Khana and where was it?
4. What did the Mahzar (1579) declare?
5. Who wrote the *Akbarnama*?
6. In which language did Babur write his memoir?
7. Name the singer associated with Akbar's court.
8. Name one painter from Jahangir's reign and his speciality.
9. What is *pietra dura*, and on which tomb was it used under Nur Jahan's patronage?
10. When was the Taj Mahal begun, and for whom?
11. Name two Shah Jahan buildings in Lahore.
12. Who wrote *Majma-ul-Bahrain*, and what did it compare?

## Answers

1. Persian, Central Asian Turkic and Indian.
2. 1563 (pilgrim tax) and 1564 (jizya).
3. A House of Worship for religious debate, at Fatehpur Sikri (1575).
4. Akbar's right to decide between the opinions of religious scholars.
5. Abul Fazl.
6. Chagatai Turkish.
7. Tansen.
8. Ustad Mansur (birds and animals) or Abu'l Hasan (portraits and court scenes).
9. Inlay of coloured stones in marble; the tomb of Itimad-ud-Daulah at Agra.
10. 1632, as the tomb of Mumtaz Mahal.
11. Any two: Shalimar Gardens; Sheesh Mahal in Lahore Fort; Wazir Khan Mosque (built by his
    governor).
12. Dara Shikoh; Sufi and Hindu philosophical ideas.
