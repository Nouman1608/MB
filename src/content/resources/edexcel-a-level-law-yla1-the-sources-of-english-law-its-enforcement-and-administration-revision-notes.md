## Legislation (1.2.1 to 1.2.3)

- **Parliament Act 1911**: Lords lose the veto over most public Bills; power to delay only. **Parliament Act 1949**: delay cut to about one year. Money Bills: one month.
- Used rarely: War Crimes Act 1991, Hunting Act 2004. Validity upheld in *Jackson v Attorney General* [2005].
- **Royal Assent Act 1967**: assent can be notified to each House.
- **Reform influences**: parties (manifestos), pressure groups (Snowdrop campaign, Firearms (Amendment) Act 1997), Law Commission (1965; Fraud Act 2006), judges (*R v R* [1991]), media ("Sarah's Law").
- **Interpretation extras**: *ejusdem generis* (*Powell v Kempton Park* [1899]); presumption of *mens rea* (*Sweet v Parsley* [1970]); *Hansard* (*Pepper v Hart* [1993]); Interpretation Act 1978 (masculine includes feminine, singular includes plural); HRA 1998 s 3 compatible reading (*Ghaidan v Godin-Mendoza* [2004]), s 4 declaration of incompatibility.

## Delegated legislation (1.2.4 to 1.2.6)

Orders in council, statutory instruments, bylaws. Controlled by Parliament (resolution procedures, scrutiny committees) and the courts (*ultra vires*). **Legislative and Regulatory Reform Act 2006**: ministers may reduce regulatory burdens by order, with safeguards; criticised as expanding executive power.

## Judicial law making (1.2.7 to 1.2.9)

- Precedent: *stare decisis*, ratio binds, obiter persuades, 1966 Practice Statement.
- **Equity in contract**: discretionary remedies. Specific performance (*Beswick v Beswick* [1968]); rescission, lost by delay (*Leaf v International Galleries* [1950]).
- For: flexible, responds to real cases. Against: unelected judges, retrospective effect, depends on a case reaching court.

## EU law (1.2.10 to 1.2.12)

**Regulations** apply directly; **directives** bind as to the result; the **Court of Justice** interprets EU law; the **Commission** proposes law and enforces treaties. *Marshall* [1986]: directive enforced against a state body. *Francovich* [1991]: state liable for non-implementation. The UK left the EU in 2020.

## Civil justice (1.2.13 to 1.2.19)

- **Courts**: County Court, High Court, Court of Appeal, Supreme Court. **Civil Procedure Rules 1998**: tracks by value (small claims up to £10,000; fast track up to £25,000; multi-track above, with an intermediate track added in 2023); overriding objective of dealing with cases justly and at proportionate cost.
- **Tribunals**: Tribunals, Courts and Enforcement Act 2007; First-tier and Upper Tribunal; judge plus lay members.

| ADR | Third party role | Binding? |
|---|---|---|
| Negotiation | None | If agreed |
| Mediation | Facilitates | If agreed |
| Conciliation | Suggests terms (e.g. ACAS) | If agreed |
| Arbitration | Decides | Yes (Arbitration Act 1996) |
| Ombudsman | Investigates complaints | Depends on scheme |

## People (1.2.20 to 1.2.22)

Judges: Judicial Appointments Commission (Constitutional Reform Act 2005), wider eligibility (Courts and Legal Services Act 1990). Solicitors (SRA) and barristers (Bar Standards Board). Magistrates: lay, usually three with a legal adviser. Jurors: random from the electoral register (Juries Act 1974).

## Costs and funding (1.2.23 to 1.2.24)

Court fees, lawyers' fees, loser pays, reputational cost. Funding: insurance, legal aid (cut by LASPO 2012), conditional fees, trade unions, Citizens Advice, pro bono.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Ejusdem generis* | General words read in line with the specific list before them |
| Declaration of incompatibility | A court statement that an Act conflicts with a Convention right; the Act still stands |
| Specific performance | Order to carry out a contract |
| Rescission | Setting a contract aside |
| Overriding objective | The CPR aim of dealing with cases justly and at proportionate cost |
| Conditional fee agreement | "No win, no fee" funding |

## Self-test

1. What did the Parliament Act 1949 change?
2. Give one example of a pressure group influencing law reform.
3. What does s 3 of the Human Rights Act 1998 require?
4. Name the two equitable remedies in contract the specification lists.
5. Which ADR method produces a decision binding on the parties?
6. Which Act cut civil legal aid in 2012?

**Answers:** 1. It reduced the House of Lords' power to delay most public Bills to about one year. 2. The Snowdrop campaign after Dunblane, followed by the Firearms (Amendment) Act 1997. 3. That legislation be read and given effect compatibly with Convention rights so far as possible. 4. Specific performance and rescission. 5. Arbitration. 6. The Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012.
