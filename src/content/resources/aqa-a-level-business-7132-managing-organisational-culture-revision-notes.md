Condensed notes on section **3.10.2 Managing organisational culture** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). Section 3.10 is A-level only. The Tarlton Retail worked example is in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-managing-organisational-culture/); questions are in the [practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-managing-organisational-culture-practice/).

## Specification content

- The importance of organisational culture; cultural models to include Handy's task culture, role culture, power culture and person culture.
- The influences on organisational culture.
- The reasons for and problems of changing organisational culture.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organisational culture | Shared values, beliefs and ways of behaving: "how things are done here" |
| Power culture | Power concentrated in a central leader or small group |
| Role culture | Work organised by defined roles, rules and procedures |
| Task culture | Work organised in project teams; power from expertise |
| Person culture | The organisation exists to serve its individual members |
| Subculture | A distinct culture within a department or site |
| Labour turnover | Leavers in a year ÷ average staff × 100 |

## Why culture matters

Guides behaviour where there are no rules; motivates and retains staff; improves service; supports (or blocks) strategy; hard for rivals to copy; eases communication.

## Handy's four cultures

| Culture | Image | Features | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Power | Spider's web | Central leader; quick, personal decisions | Fast; decisive | Depends on one person; fear of challenge |
| Role | Greek temple | Roles, rules, procedures; authority from position | Stable; clear; compliant | Slow to change; little initiative |
| Task | Net | Project teams; expertise gives power | Flexible; innovative; motivating | Hard to control; costly duplication |
| Person | Cluster of stars | Individuals' needs come first | Attracts specialists; autonomy | Hard to co-ordinate or change |

Match to the setting: role suits stable, regulated work; task suits changing, project work; power suits small entrepreneurial firms; person suits professional partnerships. Large firms usually contain a mix.

## Influences on culture

Founders and leaders; history; size and structure; ownership; industry and technology; national and local environment; recruitment and rewards; mergers and takeovers.

## Reasons for changing culture

New strategy; merger or takeover; poor performance or morale; scandal or failure; environmental change; growth; stakeholder pressure.

## Problems of changing culture

Slow (years); staff resistance; subcultures; inconsistent leaders; cost and disruption; loss of good traits; staff leave; hard to measure.

## How to manage the change

Leaders model the behaviour; communicate the reasons; change recruitment, promotion and reward; involve staff and champions; change symbols and structures; train; celebrate early wins and stay patient.

## Worked mini-example

200 staff; turnover 20%; replacement cost £5,000 per leaver. Turnover falls to 12%.
Leavers before: 40; after: 24. Saving = 16 × £5,000 = **£80,000** a year. If the programme costs £120,000, payback = **1.5 years**.

## Spotting the culture in a case

| Evidence in the case | Likely culture |
|---|---|
| "The founder approves every purchase" | Power |
| "Staff follow a 200-page procedures manual" | Role |
| "Cross-functional teams are set up for each client" | Task |
| "Each consultant decides which clients to take" | Person |

Quote the evidence, name the culture, then say what it means for performance or for the change being discussed.

## Culture and strategy: fit

- A **low-cost** strategy often fits a role culture: standard procedures and tight control.
- A **differentiation or innovation** strategy often fits a task culture: creativity and teamwork.
- A **fast-growing start-up** may begin with a power culture and need to move to role or task as it grows.
- A strategy that needs behaviour the culture does not reward will struggle. Check fit before judging the plan.

## Building a paragraph of analysis

1. Identify the culture and give evidence.
2. Explain how it affects behaviour (speed, initiative, control).
3. Link to the result for the business (cost, quality, innovation, staff turnover).
4. Say what the effect depends on, and whether the culture should change.

## Exam traps

- No culture is best in all conditions.
- Apply the type to evidence in the case: who decides, how rule-bound, how teams form.
- Role culture is not always bad: safety-critical and regulated work needs it.
- Culture changes slowly, and leaders' behaviour matters most.
- Large organisations often have more than one culture.

## Self-test

1. Define "organisational culture".
2. Name Handy's four cultures.
3. Which culture is typical of a small entrepreneurial firm with a dominant owner?
4. Give one weakness of a role culture.
5. Which culture is suited to a project-based design agency? Explain.
6. Give two influences on culture.
7. Give two reasons why a business might change its culture.
8. Give two problems of changing culture.
9. A firm has 300 staff and turnover falls from 18% to 12%. How many fewer leavers?
10. If each leaver costs £6,000, what is the saving?
11. Why must leaders model the new culture?
12. Why is culture hard to copy for rivals?

### Answers

1. The shared values, beliefs and ways of behaving in an organisation.
2. Power, role, task, person.
3. Power culture.
4. Any one: slow to change; little initiative; bureaucracy.
5. Task culture, because work is done in flexible teams using expertise and creativity.
6. Any two: founders and leaders; history; size and structure; ownership; industry; recruitment and reward.
7. Any two: new strategy; merger; poor performance; scandal; growth; environmental change.
8. Any two: slow; resistance; subcultures; inconsistent leaders; cost; loss of good traits.
9. 300 × 18% = 54; 300 × 12% = 36; **18 fewer** leavers.
10. 18 × £6,000 = **£108,000**.
11. Staff copy what leaders do, not what they say; inconsistent behaviour makes staff doubt the change.
12. It is built over time from people, history and values, so it cannot be bought or quickly imitated.
