> **Syllabus edition note.** These notes follow the Cambridge O Level Environmental Management 5014
> syllabus for exams in 2027, 2028 and 2029, sub-topic 6.4.

Sub-topic 6.4 has five outcomes: define drought, explain its causes and impacts, describe strategies, and
discuss them. Unlike earthquakes or cyclones, drought develops slowly, so before-event strategies and
water storage dominate.

Full explanations: [drought study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-5014-drought-5014-2027/).
Test yourself: [drought practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-5014-drought-5014-2027-practice/).

## Definition

**Drought: a period of dry weather that is longer or more severe than normal.**
- "Normal" = the usual rainfall for that place.
- "Longer" = the dry period lasts beyond its usual length.
- "More severe" = much less rain than usual.

## Two causes

1. **Lack of rain**: rain-bearing weather fails, arrives late or is weak; persistent high pressure brings dry,
   sinking air.
2. **Climate change**: higher global temperatures (more evaporation and transpiration) and more extreme
   weather events (longer, more intense dry spells).

Human over-use of water is not a listed cause, but it worsens the impacts.

## Ten impacts

| Group | Impacts |
|---|---|
| Water | Water sources dry up |
| Land | Soil erosion; desertification |
| Living things | Loss of crops, livestock and habitats; death of organisms |
| Food | Famine |
| Fire and air | Increased risk of wildfires; decrease in air quality |
| People and money | Financial losses; human health |

**Chain 1:** no rain → crops and grazing fail → livestock die → famine → malnutrition and disease.
**Chain 2:** dry vegetation → wildfires → smoke → decrease in air quality → breathing problems.
**Chain 3:** bare dry soil → wind erosion → dust → land degraded → desertification.

## Five strategies

| Strategy | Stage | What it involves |
|---|---|---|
| Monitoring and warning | Before | Rainfall, soil moisture, river and reservoir levels; satellite images of vegetation |
| Emergency water supplies | During | Tankers, bottled water, temporary pipes |
| Water conservation | Before and during | Use limits, leak repair, efficient taps and irrigation, recycling waste water |
| Increasing water supply | Before (mainly) | Dams and reservoirs; aquifers and wells; desalination; rainwater harvesting |
| International aid | During and after | Food, water, money, equipment |

## Benefits and limitations

| Strategy | Benefit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Monitoring and warning | Months of warning | Useless if people cannot act |
| Emergency water supplies | Fast, life-saving | Costly; short-term |
| Water conservation | Cheap; stretches supply | Needs cooperation; cannot create water |
| Dams and reservoirs | Store wet-season water | Cost; flooded habitats; evaporation |
| Aquifers and wells | Less evaporation loss | Over-pumping; water table falls |
| Desalination | Independent of rainfall | Cost; energy; brine waste |
| Rainwater harvesting | Cheap; local | Little rain to collect in drought |
| International aid | Food and water when local supply fails | Slow; dependence |

## Calculations to practise

- **Percentage below normal** = (normal - actual) / normal x 100.
- **Days of supply** = volume stored / daily use.
- *Invented example:* reservoir 4.5 million m³; use 15 000 m³ per day → 300 days. A 20% cut in use →
  12 000 m³ per day → 375 days.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Drought | A period of dry weather that is longer or more severe than normal |
| Evaporation | Liquid water changing to water vapour |
| Transpiration | Loss of water vapour from plant leaves |
| Famine | A severe and widespread shortage of food |
| Desertification | Land in dry areas becoming desert-like and unproductive |
| Wildfire | An uncontrolled fire in vegetation |
| Aquifer | Rock that stores ground water |
| Water table | The upper level of ground water |
| Desalination | Removing salt from sea water to make fresh water |
| Brine | Very salty waste water produced by desalination |
| Rainwater harvesting | Collecting and storing rain from roofs or the ground |
| Water conservation | Reducing the amount of water used or wasted |

## Exam traps

- "Drought = no rain": use the syllabus definition.
- Over-use given as a cause: it worsens impacts but is not a listed cause.
- Rainwater harvesting during a drought: little rain to harvest; it works by storing water beforehand.
- Desertification and drought treated as the same thing.
- Missing the "decrease in air quality" and "wildfires" impacts.
- Percentage below normal calculated using the actual rainfall as the divisor.

## Self-test

1. Define drought.
2. Explain why the same rainfall total could be a drought in one place but not another.
3. How does an increase in global temperature contribute to drought?
4. Describe how drought can lead to famine.
5. Explain how drought decreases air quality.
6. Name the four methods of increasing water supply in the syllabus.
7. Give one benefit and one limitation of using aquifers and wells.
8. Why is monitoring more useful for drought than for earthquakes?
9. Give two water-conservation measures for a city.
10. Why is desalination mainly used in coastal, high-income countries?
11. Rainfall is 210 mm against a normal of 350 mm. Calculate the percentage below normal.
12. A reservoir holds 6 million m³ and the town uses 25 000 m³ a day. How many days will it last?

### Answers

1. A period of dry weather that is longer or more severe than normal.
2. Drought is measured against each place's normal rainfall, so a total normal for a dry region is a
   shortfall in a wet region.
3. Higher temperatures increase evaporation from soil and water and transpiration from plants, so water is
   lost faster.
4. Crops fail and livestock die, so food production falls; if food cannot be bought or brought in, a
   widespread food shortage (famine) follows.
5. Dust blows from dry, bare soil and smoke comes from wildfires.
6. Dams and reservoirs; use of aquifers and wells; desalination; rainwater harvesting.
7. Benefit: ground water loses little to evaporation. Limitation: over-pumping lowers the water table and
   wells can dry up.
8. Drought builds up slowly, so monitoring can give months of warning; earthquakes cannot be timed.
9. Any two: hosepipe or watering limits; repairing leaking pipes; water-efficient taps and toilets; recycling
   waste water.
10. It needs sea water and is very expensive and energy-intensive.
11. 140 / 350 x 100 = 40%.
12. 6 000 000 / 25 000 = 240 days.
