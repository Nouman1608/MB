Unit 4 (WET04) of Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Level English Literature (YET01) is a two-hour,
open-book exam worth 50 marks and 25% of the full International A Level. You write **two 25-mark essays**:
one on a **Shakespeare play** (Section A) and one on a **pre-1900 poetry movement** (Section B). Each
question gives a **statement** to respond to, and both sections assess **AO1, AO2, AO3 and AO5**, so
context and different interpretations matter in every answer. For the full method, use the
[Unit 4 study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-english-literature-unit-4-shakespeare-and-pre-1900-poetry-study-guide/).

## Unit 4 at a glance

| Detail | Section A: Shakespeare (4.3) | Section B: Pre-1900 Poetry (4.4) |
|---|---|---|
| Task | One essay from a choice of two on your play | One essay from a choice of two on your movement |
| Question style | A statement to respond to, considering context and different interpretations | The same |
| Marks | 25 | 25 |
| AOs | AO1, AO2, AO3, AO5 | AO1, AO2, AO3, AO5 |

**Exam:** 2 hours, open book, available January and June; first assessed January 2017. Unit 4 is an IA2
unit for the full International A Level only. Give each section about an hour.

## Set texts

| Section A: one Shakespeare play | Section B: one pre-1900 movement (prescribed poems) |
|---|---|
| *Hamlet* | Metaphysical Poets — *Metaphysical Poetry*, ed. Colin Burrow (Penguin, 2006) |
| *King Lear* | The Victorians — *The New Oxford Book of Victorian Verse*, ed. Christopher Ricks (OUP, 2008) |
| *Measure for Measure* | The Romantics — *English Romantic Verse*, ed. David Wright (Penguin Classics, 1973) |
| *The Taming of the Shrew* | |

Only the prescribed poems listed in Appendix 5 of the specification are set, for example Donne's "The
Flea" and Marvell's "To His Coy Mistress" (Metaphysical); Robert Browning's "My Last Duchess" and Hardy's
"The Darkling Thrush" (Victorian); Blake's "London" and Keats's "Ode to a Nightingale" (Romantic).

## The four assessment objectives in Unit 4

| AO | Wording | Unit 4 weighting in the IAL |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 | Articulate informed, personal and creative responses, using concepts and terminology, with coherent, accurate written expression | 7.5% |
| AO2 | Analyse ways in which meanings are shaped in literary texts | 7.5% |
| AO3 | Demonstrate understanding of the significance and influence of the contexts in which texts are written and received | 5% |
| AO5 | Explore literary texts informed by different interpretations | 5% |

AO4 (connections across texts) is **not** assessed in Unit 4, so you do not need to compare two works,
although strong poetry answers range across several prescribed poems.

## AO3: which contexts count?

The specification's guidance lists:

- the writer's life, **only where it relates to the text**
- historical setting, time and location
- social and cultural contexts
- literary context: movements and genres (central in Section B)
- **contexts of reception**: reviews, editing, staging history, film adaptation, and how readers and
  critics have reached alternative interpretations

## AO5: what "different interpretations" means

The specification says the statement in each question is "an invitation" to show that texts can be read
in different ways. You can also draw on wider critical reading, class debate, or **performances you have
seen**. AO5 is met by weighing views, not by naming critics:

1. Restate the statement's view.
2. Test it with evidence.
3. Offer an alternative reading (a critic, a production, a different audience).
4. Reach your own judgement.

## Movement features worth knowing

| Movement | Typical features to analyse |
|---|---|
| Metaphysical | Conceits ("The Flea"), argument and wit, colloquial openings, sacred and profane love, the sonnet in Donne's Holy Sonnets |
| Romantic | Nature and the imagination, the individual voice, the ode form, social protest (Blake), the sublime |
| Victorian | Dramatic monologue (Browning), elegy and doubt (*In Memoriam*), faith and science, women's voices (Barrett Browning, Rossetti), Hardy's bleak irony |

## Worked example: an AO5 paragraph on *King Lear*

Statement: "*King Lear* offers its audience no consolation."

"The play's ending seems to support the statement: Cordelia's death comes after the battle is lost and
the order to spare her is too late, so the suffering appears arbitrary. Yet some productions stage
Lear's last lines, in which he believes he sees her breathe, as a moment of hope, while others play it as
final delusion. The Restoration audience found the ending so unbearable that Nahum Tate's adaptation, in
which Cordelia survives, held the stage for more than a century. That history of reception suggests the
play's refusal of consolation is precisely what audiences have found hardest to accept, and I would argue
that the consolation the play does allow, Lear's recognition of Cordelia's love, makes her loss more
painful, not less."

AO5 (alternative readings, staging), AO3 (reception history), AO2 (structure of the ending) and AO1
(personal argument) are combined.

## Common mistakes / exam traps

- **Ignoring the statement.** Every question gives a view; agree, disagree or qualify it explicitly.
- **AO5 as a list of critics.** Unevaluated quotations from critics earn little.
- **Context as biography.** Use it only where it explains the text.
- **One poem only in Section B.** You answer on a movement, so range across prescribed poems.
- **Writing about non-prescribed poems.** Only the Appendix 5 poems for your movement are set.
- **Forgetting the play is drama.** Staging and audience response support AO2, AO3 and AO5.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do Unit 4 questions use a printed extract?**
The specification describes essay questions that give a statement to respond to. It does not describe a
printed extract; Unit 4 is open book, so you can refer to your own copy.

**How many poems should I discuss in Section B?**
The specification does not fix a number. Because the question is on a movement, discussing several
prescribed poems in detail usually gives you the range to respond to the statement.

**Is comparison assessed in Unit 4?**
No. AO4 is not assessed in Unit 4. Comparison of poems can still support your argument, but marks come
from AO1, AO2, AO3 and AO5.

**Can theatre productions count for AO5?**
Yes. The specification says learners may use performances they have seen or viewed, as well as critical
reading and class debate.

## Self-test

1. How many marks is each Unit 4 essay worth? (25.)
2. Which four AOs are assessed in both sections? (AO1, AO2, AO3, AO5.)
3. Name the four Shakespeare plays. (*Hamlet*, *King Lear*, *Measure for Measure*, *The Taming of the Shrew*.)
4. Name the three poetry movements. (Metaphysical, Victorian, Romantic.)
5. What three sources does the specification suggest for AO5? (Wider critical reading, class debate, performances.)
