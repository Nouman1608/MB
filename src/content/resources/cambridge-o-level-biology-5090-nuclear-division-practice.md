## Section B

**6.** A student compares mitosis and meiosis.

**(a)** State the number of cells produced by one mitotic division of a cell and by one meiotic division. **[2]**

**(b)** State how the chromosome number changes in each type of division. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the cells produced by meiosis are genetically different from each other. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why it is important that gametes are haploid. **[2]**

**7.** A cell in a culture divides by mitosis every 30 minutes. One cell was present at the start.

**(a)** Calculate the number of cells after 3 hours. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of cells after 4 hours. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why all the cells in the culture are genetically identical. **[2]**

**8.** A species of mosquito has 6 chromosomes in each body cell.

**(a)** State the number of chromosomes in a sperm cell of the mosquito. **[1]**

**(b)** State the number of chromosomes in the zygote. **[1]**

**(c)** State the number of chromosomes in each cell of the larva that grows from the zygote, and name the process that makes more cells. **[2]**

**9.** The bone marrow contains stem cells.

**(a)** State what is meant by a stem cell. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why stem cells are useful in the replacement of red blood cells. **[2]**

**10.** Explain how uncontrolled mitosis can lead to cancer. **[3]**

**11.** The table shows the chromosome number in four types of human cell.

| Cell | Chromosome number |
|---|---|
| Liver cell | A |
| Egg cell | B |
| Zygote | C |
| Cell in an embryo | D |

Complete the table. **[4]**

**12.** Explain why a pair of identical twins that developed from one zygote are genetically identical, but a pair of brothers who developed from two separate fertilisations are not. **[3]**

**13.** A pea plant has 14 chromosomes in each root cell.

**(a)** State the number of chromosomes in a pea pollen nucleus. **[1]**

**(b)** State the number of pairs of chromosomes in a root cell. **[1]**

**(c)** A piece of root tissue contains 400 cells, each formed by mitosis from one original cell. Suggest how many chromosomes each of these cells has, and explain your answer. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A nucleus that contains two sets of chromosomes [1]. **[1]**

**4.** 23 pairs [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Any two of: growth; repair of damaged tissues; replacement of dying cells; asexual reproduction [1 each]. **[2]**

**6.**

**(a)** Mitosis produces 2 cells [1]. Meiosis produces 4 cells [1].

**(b)** In mitosis the chromosome number is maintained (stays the same) [1]. In meiosis it is halved (diploid to haploid) [1].

**(c)** Meiosis produces cells that are genetically different [1] (the chromosomes and alleles are shared out differently).

**(d)** At fertilisation, two gametes fuse [1], so if gametes were diploid the chromosome number would double in every generation; haploid gametes restore the diploid number [1]. **[7]**

**7.**

**(a)** 3 hours = 180 minutes, so 180 divided by 30 = 6 divisions [1]. 2 to the power 6 = 64 cells [1].

**(b)** 4 hours gives 8 divisions, so 2 to the power 8 = 256 cells [1].

**(c)** Each mitosis makes an exact copy of the DNA [1], so the daughter cells are genetically identical to the parent cell [1]. **[5]**

**8.**

**(a)** 3 [1].

**(b)** 6 [1].

**(c)** 6 [1]; mitosis [1]. **[4]**

**9.**

**(a)** An unspecialised cell [1] that divides by mitosis to make daughter cells that can become specialised [1].

**(b)** Red blood cells wear out and need replacing [1], so stem cells divide to supply new cells that can specialise as red blood cells [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Normally cell division is controlled so that cells divide only when new cells are needed [1]. In cancer, the control is lost and a cell divides repeatedly by mitosis [1], forming a mass of cells (a tumour) [1]. **[3]**

**11.** A: 46 [1]. B: 23 [1]. C: 46 [1]. D: 46 [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Identical twins came from one zygote that divided by mitosis, which gives genetically identical cells [1]. Brothers from different fertilisations came from different eggs and sperm [1], which are made by meiosis and are genetically different from each other [1]. **[3]**

**13.**

**(a)** 7 [1].

**(b)** 7 pairs [1].

**(c)** 14 [1], because mitosis maintains the chromosome number, so every daughter cell has the same number as the parent cell [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Halving the chromosome number in mitosis.
- Saying that meiosis makes identical cells.
- Giving 46 pairs instead of 23 pairs.
- Writing that stem cells are specialised.
- Counting divisions wrongly when working out the number of cells (use doubling).
