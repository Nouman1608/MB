from September 2016 onwards (version 1.2). The content is examined on Paper 1 at Foundation and Higher Tier,
and no question here tests Higher-tier-only content. Where a question refers to a diagram, the diagram is

## Section A

**1.** **(a)** State what type of bond joins the atoms inside a polymer molecule. **[1]**
**(b)** State whether the forces between polymer molecules are weak, relatively strong or ionic. **[1]**

**2.** Poly(ethene) is a polymer. State whether it is a solid, a liquid or a gas at room temperature. **[1]**

**3.** A diagram shows a repeat unit with two carbon atoms and four hydrogen atoms inside square brackets. Bond
lines pass through the brackets and the letter *n* follows the second bracket.

**(a)** State what *n* represents. **[1]**
**(b)** State why the bond lines pass through the brackets. **[1]**

**4.** The diagrams below are described in words.

- Diagram 1: a lattice of alternating positive and negative ions.
- Diagram 2: a long chain of repeated units in brackets, with *n*.
- Diagram 3: a network of atoms, each bonded to four others, extending in all directions.
- Diagram 4: three atoms joined in a bent shape, one oxygen and two hydrogens.

**(a)** Which diagram shows a polymer? **[1]**
**(b)** Which diagram shows a giant covalent structure? **[1]**
**(c)** Which diagram shows a small molecule? **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** A polymer chain contains 150 repeat units. Each repeat unit has the formula C₂H₃Cl.

**(a)** Calculate the number of carbon atoms in the chain. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of hydrogen atoms and chlorine atoms in the chain. **[2]**

**6.** Ethene, C₂H₄, is a gas at room temperature. Poly(ethene) is a solid.

**(a)** Explain why ethene is a gas. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why poly(ethene) is a solid. **[3]**

**7.** A student says that a polymer melts because its covalent bonds break.

**(a)** Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the difference between a polymer and a giant covalent structure such as silicon dioxide. **[2]**

**8.** A repeat unit of poly(propene) contains three carbon atoms and six hydrogen atoms. One carbon atom has a
CH₃ group attached.

**(a)** Give the formula of the repeat unit. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of hydrogen atoms in a chain of 200 repeat units. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the type of bond that joins the carbon atoms in the chain. **[1]**

**9.** The table gives data for three substances.

| Substance | Formula or description | State at 20 °C | Conducts electricity? |
|---|---|---|---|
| J | Poly(ethene), very large molecules | Solid | No |
| K | Methane, CH₄ | Gas | No |
| L | Diamond | Solid, very high melting point | No |

**(a)** Explain why J is a solid but K is a gas. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the melting point of L is much higher than the softening temperature of J. **[3]**

**10.** Describe how the repeat unit of poly(ethene) should be drawn, and explain each feature of the drawing. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Covalent** bonds [1]
**(b)** **Relatively strong** [1]

**2.** **Solid** [1]

**3. (a)** A **very large number** of repeat units [1]
**(b)** They show that the repeat unit is **joined to the next unit** on each side / the chain continues [1]

**4. (a)** **Diagram 2** [1]
**(b)** **Diagram 3** [1]
**(c)** **Diagram 4** [1]

**5. (a)** 150 × 2 = **300** carbon atoms [1]
**(b)** Hydrogen: 150 × 3 = **450** [1]; chlorine: 150 × 1 = **150** [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Ethene is made of **small molecules** with **weak intermolecular forces** [1]; little energy is needed to
separate the molecules, so its boiling point is below room temperature [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Poly(ethene) has **very large molecules** [1]; the intermolecular forces between the long chains are
**relatively strong** [1]; more energy is needed to separate them, so it is a solid at room temperature [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** The **covalent bonds** along the polymer chains are strong and are not overcome when it softens or
melts [1]; the **intermolecular forces** between the chains are overcome [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** A polymer is made of **separate very large molecules** [1]; a giant covalent structure is **one
continuous network** of covalent bonds in all directions [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** **C₃H₆** [1]
**(b)** 200 × 6 = **1200** [1]
**(c)** **Covalent** (single) bonds [1]

**9. (a)** Marking points, up to 3: J has very large molecules and K has small molecules [1]; the intermolecular
forces between polymer chains are relatively strong but those between methane molecules are weak [1]; more
energy is needed to separate polymer molecules, so J is a solid [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** L is a giant covalent structure and **all** the atoms are held by strong covalent bonds [1]; to melt it
the covalent bonds must be broken [1]; J melts or softens when only the intermolecular forces are overcome,
which needs less energy [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Marking points, one each, up to 4:
- The two carbon atoms and four hydrogen atoms (each carbon with two hydrogens) drawn with single bonds [1].
- Square brackets round the repeat unit [1].
- Bond lines through the brackets to show the chain continues [1].
- *n* after the closing bracket to show a very large number of repeat units [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling a polymer a giant covalent structure.
- Saying the covalent bonds break when a polymer melts.
- Leaving off *n* or the bond lines through the brackets.
- Saying the forces between polymer molecules are weak.
- Not multiplying by the number of repeat units when counting atoms.
