These questions cover subtopic 5.3 Probability of Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics 9709 (Paper 5). Give exact fractions where they are simple, and otherwise answers to 3 significant figures. Dice are fair and six-sided.

Related: [probability study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-probability-cambridge-alevel-maths/) | [probability revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-probability-cambridge-alevel-maths-revision-notes/)

## Section A

**1.** Two dice are thrown. Find the probability that the total score is at least 9. **[2]**

**2.** Events A and B are independent, with P(A) = 0.5 and P(B) = 0.3. Find P(A ∩ B) and the probability that neither A nor B happens. **[3]**

**3.** P(A) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.25 and P(A ∩ B) = 0.1. Determine whether A and B are independent. **[2]**

**4.** Events A and B are exclusive, with P(A) = 0.35 and P(B) = 0.45.

**(a)** Find the probability that neither A nor B happens.

**(b)** State, with a reason, whether A and B are independent. **[3]**

**5.** Four people are chosen at random from 6 men and 4 women. Find the probability that exactly two women are chosen. **[3]**

**6.** The letters of the word PENCIL are arranged in a random order. Find the probability that the arrangement begins and ends with a vowel. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** 5% of the items made in a factory are faulty. An inspection machine rejects 90% of faulty items. It also rejects 4% of items that are not faulty.

**(a)** Find the probability that a randomly chosen item is rejected.

**(b)** Find the probability that an item that is rejected is faulty.

**(c)** Find the probability that an item that is accepted is faulty. **[7]**

**8.** A bag contains 6 red and 4 blue counters. Two counters are taken at random without replacement.

**(a)** Find the probability that both counters are the same colour.

**(b)** Find the probability that the first counter is red, given that both counters are the same colour. **[5]**

**9.** Two dice are thrown. Event E is "the total is 6", F is "both dice show the same number" and G is "the second die shows an even number".

**(a)** Show that E and F are not independent.

**(b)** Show that F and G are independent.

**(c)** Find the probability that the total is 6, given that at least one die shows a 2. **[7]**

**10.** The table shows the subject choices of 120 students.

| | Science | Arts |
|---|---|---|
| Year 12 | 36 | 24 |
| Year 13 | 30 | 30 |

A student is chosen at random.

**(a)** Find the probability that the student studies science.

**(b)** Find the probability that the student is in Year 13, given that the student studies science.

**(c)** Determine whether the events "the student is in Year 13" and "the student studies science" are independent. **[6]**

**11.** Ali and Bea take turns to throw a die, starting with Ali. The first person to throw a 6 wins.

**(a)** Find the probability that Ali wins on his first throw.

**(b)** Find the probability that Ali wins on his second throw.

**(c)** Find the probability that Ali wins the game. **[5]**

**12.** Five cards are dealt at random from a pack of 14 cards, of which 8 are red and 6 are black.

**(a)** Find the probability that at least one black card is dealt.

**(b)** Find the probability that exactly three red cards are dealt, given that at least one black card is dealt. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Totals 9, 10, 11, 12 occur 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10 times [1]; 10/36 = **5/18** [1] **[2]**

**2.** P(A ∩ B) = 0.5 × 0.3 = **0.15** [1]; P(A′ ∩ B′) = 0.5 × 0.7 [1] = **0.35** [1] **[3]**

**3.** P(A) × P(B) = 0.4 × 0.25 = 0.1 [1]; this equals P(A ∩ B), so **A and B are independent** [1] **[2]**

**4. (a)** P(A or B) = 0.35 + 0.45 = 0.8 [1]; neither = **0.2** [1]

**(b)** **Not independent**: P(A ∩ B) = 0 but P(A) × P(B) = 0.1575 ≠ 0 [1] **[3]**

**5.** (⁴C₂ × ⁶C₂) ÷ ¹⁰C₄ [1] = (6 × 15) ÷ 210 [1] = **3/7** [1] **[3]**

**6.** Vowels E and I at the ends in 2 ways, other letters 4! ways: 48 arrangements out of 6! = 720 [1]; **1/15** [1] **[2]**

**7. (a)** P(faulty and rejected) = 0.05 × 0.9 = 0.045 [1]; P(not faulty and rejected) = 0.95 × 0.04 = 0.038 [1]; P(rejected) = **0.083** [1]

**(b)** 0.045 ÷ 0.083 [1] = **0.542** [1]

**(c)** P(faulty and accepted) = 0.05 × 0.1 = 0.005; P(accepted) = 0.917 [1]; 0.005 ÷ 0.917 = **0.00545** [1] **[7]**

*Examiner insight:* in (c), the denominator is the probability of being accepted, 1 − 0.083, not the probability of being faulty.

**8. (a)** P(RR) = 6/10 × 5/9 = 30/90 [1]; P(BB) = 4/10 × 3/9 = 12/90 [1]; total 42/90 = **7/15** [1]

**(b)** (30/90) ÷ (42/90) [1] = **5/7** [1] **[5]**

**9. (a)** P(E) = 5/36, P(F) = 6/36 [1]; E ∩ F is only (3, 3), so P(E ∩ F) = 1/36 [1]; P(E) × P(F) = 5/216 ≠ 1/36, so **not independent** [1]

**(b)** P(G) = 1/2; F ∩ G = {(2, 2), (4, 4), (6, 6)}, so P(F ∩ G) = 3/36 = 1/12 [1]; P(F) × P(G) = 1/6 × 1/2 = 1/12, so **independent** [1]

**(c)** At least one 2: 11 outcomes [1]; of these, (2, 4) and (4, 2) total 6, so **2/11** [1] **[7]**

**10. (a)** 66/120 = **11/20** [1]

**(b)** 30/66 [1] = **5/11** [1]

**(c)** P(Year 13) = 60/120 = 1/2 and P(Year 13 ∩ science) = 30/120 = 1/4 [1]; P(Year 13) × P(science) = 1/2 × 11/20 = 11/40 [1]; 11/40 ≠ 1/4, so **not independent** [1] **[6]**

**11. (a)** **1/6** [1]

**(b)** Ali misses, Bea misses, Ali throws a 6: (5/6)² × 1/6 [1] = **25/216** [1]

**(c)** Geometric series with first term 1/6 and common ratio 25/36 [1]: (1/6) ÷ (1 − 25/36) = **6/11** [1] **[5]**

**12. (a)** P(no black) = ⁸C₅ ÷ ¹⁴C₅ = 56/2002 [1]; P(at least one black) = 1946/2002 = **0.972** [1]

**(b)** P(exactly 3 red) = (⁸C₃ × ⁶C₂) ÷ 2002 = 840/2002 [1]; this event lies inside "at least one black" [1]; (840/2002) ÷ (1946/2002) = **0.432** [1] **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dice outcomes counted as unordered pairs.
- Independence claimed without comparing P(A ∩ B) with P(A) × P(B).
- The wrong denominator in a conditional probability, especially in reversed tree questions.
- "Without replacement" ignored at the second draw.
- Repeated games summed term by term instead of as a geometric series.
