This guide is for students who choose **the media (میڈیا)**, the research subject of Theme 2, in Pearson Edexcel A Level Urdu (9UR0). The specification sets three aspects, and you must research all of them:

- **آزادیٔ اظہار**: freedom of expression
- **سیاست پر پرنٹ میڈیا کے اثرات**: the effects of print media on politics
- **سوشل میڈیا کے نتیجے میں بڑھتا ہوا سیاسی شعور**: growing political awareness as a result of social media

All three aspects are political: this subject is about the media's relationship with power and citizens, not about entertainment, which belongs to sub-theme 2.2. The [media revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-media-revision-notes/) give the general background and vocabulary; this guide turns the three aspects into a research plan and an exam method. Practise with the [media practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-9ur0-media-research-subject-practice/).

## The one-area rule for Theme 2

Theme 2 is the only research subject where you choose your context: **either Pakistan or one area of India where Urdu is an official language**. Decide early and keep to it.

- Because candidates research different places, the unseen Section C text for Theme 2 is **general in nature**, equally relevant to any of the permitted areas. Do not expect it to name your area; your research supplies the specific evidence.
- If you refer to more than one country or area, examiners credit the content on the area that gains the highest mark, and the rest earns nothing. Mixing areas only wastes time.
- As with every research subject, the work must be independent, use at least two authentic Urdu sources, and not draw on your Paper 2 works. In the exam you have no notes or dictionary and write approximately 320–370 words.

The examples below are drawn mostly from Pakistan. If you research an area of India, build the same three-part file from sources about that area only, for example its Urdu press and the constitutional guarantee of free speech in India.

## Aspect 1: freedom of expression (آزادیٔ اظہار)

Research questions to answer from your sources:

1. **What does the law say?** In Pakistan, Article 19 of the constitution guarantees freedom of speech and of the press, subject to "reasonable restrictions" on stated grounds. Find the grounds in an Urdu source and learn two of them.
2. **Who regulates?** For broadcast media, the Pakistan Electronic Media Regulatory Authority (PEMRA), established in 2002, licenses channels and can suspend them. Online content is covered by the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act of 2016. Note what each body or law is for, and what critics say about how it is used.
3. **How free is the media in practice?** Look for reported cases: a channel taken off air, a newspaper's distribution disrupted, a journalist threatened, or a court ruling that protected a publication. Record the source and date of each.
4. **Where are the limits?** Most people accept limits on hate speech, defamation (**ہتکِ عزت**) and incitement. The debate is over who draws the line and whether limits protect the public or protect the powerful.

Key terms: **آزادیٔ صحافت** (press freedom), **قدغن** (restriction), **سیلف سنسرشپ** or **خود ساختہ سنسرشپ** (self-censorship), **ضابطہ** (regulation), **اظہارِ رائے** (expression of opinion).

## Aspect 2: the effects of print media on politics

Print still matters even in a digital age, because newspapers set agendas that television and social media follow. Research:

- **Editorials and columns.** The **اداریہ** (editorial) states a paper's position; **کالم نگار** (columnists) shape opinion among the educated and among politicians themselves. Find two columns on the same event taking different sides.
- **Investigative reporting** (**تحقیقاتی صحافت**). A report that exposes corruption or wrongdoing can lead to resignations, inquiries or court cases. Find one example in your sources and note its outcome.
- **History.** Newspapers played a part in political movements, and at times they faced censorship and closure under different governments. One well-documented episode is enough; do not attempt a full history.
- **Ownership and bias.** Large media groups own newspapers and channels together. Ask whether this concentrates influence, and whether some papers are seen as close to particular parties.
- **Decline or adaptation.** Circulation pressures have pushed newspapers online; ask whether their political influence is falling or simply moving to new platforms.

## Aspect 3: social media and growing political awareness

**بڑھتا ہوا** (growing) again signals a process. The aspect asks whether, and how, social media has increased **سیاسی شعور** (political awareness). Research both directions:

**Evidence of growing awareness:**

- Young people follow politics on their phones and share opinions instantly.
- Citizens record events and hold officials to account; campaigns spread quickly across the country.
- Politicians and parties communicate directly with supporters, bypassing traditional media.

**Evidence that complicates the picture:**

- **Disinformation** (**غلط معلومات**) and rumours spread faster than corrections.
- **Echo chambers**: people see mainly what confirms their views, which can increase polarisation (**تقسیم**) rather than understanding.
- **Abuse and intimidation**, particularly of women journalists and activists.
- Platforms or services have at times been restricted, which raises the freedom-of-expression questions of Aspect 1.

A sophisticated answer distinguishes **awareness** (knowing about politics) from **understanding** (judging evidence well) and **participation** (voting, campaigning). Social media may increase the first more than the second.

## Worked example: a research file entry

| Heading | Entry (in Urdu, as you would keep it) |
|---|---|
| پہلو | سیاست پر پرنٹ میڈیا کے اثرات |
| مرکزی نکتہ | تحقیقاتی رپورٹیں حکومت کو جواب دہ بناتی ہیں۔ |
| ثبوت | [newspaper name, date]: ایک رپورٹ کے بعد سرکاری محکمے میں انکوائری شروع ہوئی۔ |
| دوسری رائے | کچھ اخبارات پر سیاسی جماعتوں کی حمایت کا الزام ہے۔ |
| میرا نتیجہ | اخبار اب بھی ایجنڈا طے کرتے ہیں، مگر ان کی ساکھ ان کی غیر جانب داری پر منحصر ہے۔ |

Fill the bracket with a real report you have read. A memorised entry like this gives you a precise sentence for the exam.

## Worked example: combining text and research in one paragraph

Suppose the unseen text argues, in general terms, that social media has made young people politically aware but less patient with serious reading. A body paragraph might be:

> متن میں بجا طور پر کہا گیا ہے کہ سوشل میڈیا نے نوجوانوں کو سیاست سے جوڑ دیا ہے۔ میری تحقیق بھی اس کی تائید کرتی ہے: میں نے جن ذرائع کا مطالعہ کیا، ان کے مطابق انتخابات کے دنوں میں نوجوانوں کی بڑی تعداد خبریں اور تبصرے موبائل فون ہی پر دیکھتی ہے۔ تاہم یہی ذرائع یہ بھی بتاتے ہیں کہ غلط خبریں اکثر تصدیق سے پہلے پھیل جاتی ہیں، اس لیے یہ کہنا مشکل ہے کہ شعور کے ساتھ ساتھ سمجھ بوجھ بھی اسی رفتار سے بڑھی ہے۔

(The text rightly says that social media has connected young people with politics. My research supports this: according to the sources I studied, at election time a large number of young people see news and commentary only on their phones. However, the same sources also show that false news often spreads before it is verified, so it is hard to say that understanding has grown at the same pace as awareness.)

**Why it works:** the text is cited and evaluated (**بجا طور پر**, AO2); research is signalled (**میں نے جن ذرائع کا مطالعہ کیا**, AO4); the paragraph turns with **تاہم** to a distinction between awareness and understanding; and the agreement **بڑی تعداد ... دیکھتی ہے** follows تعداد (feminine), showing control (AO3). In your own answer, name the source.

## Common mistakes

- Writing about television dramas or films, which belong to popular culture, not to this research subject.
- Researching Pakistan and an Indian city together, or switching areas mid-answer.
- Treating social media as wholly good or wholly harmful, with no distinction between awareness and understanding.
- Naming laws or bodies without saying what they do or why they are debated.
- Copying the unseen text instead of responding to it.

## Exam technique

- Underline the statement's key claim and decide your position before planning.
- Aim for at least one paragraph per aspect if the statement allows, because any aspect may be what the examiner is looking for.
- Use the text at least twice, and evaluate it (**متن کی یہ بات جزوی طور پر درست ہے**) rather than only repeating it.
- End with a conclusion that answers the statement and follows from your paragraphs.
