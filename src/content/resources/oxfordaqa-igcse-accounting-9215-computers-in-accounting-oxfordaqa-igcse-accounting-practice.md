These questions cover the section 3.5 statement on the use of computers in accounting: the advantages and disadvantages of computerised software compared with manual methods. Revise first with the [computers in accounting study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-computers-in-accounting-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting/) and the [computers in accounting revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-computers-in-accounting-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-revision-notes/). All businesses and figures are invented, and amounts are in dollars.

## Section A

**1.** State **two** advantages of using computerised accounting software compared with manual methods. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** disadvantages of using computerised accounting software compared with manual methods. **[2]**

**3.** Which error would accounting software prevent?

A: A purchase invoice is never entered

B: A sale is posted to the wrong customer's account

C: The total of a ledger account is added up incorrectly

D: The cost of a new van is entered as motor expenses

**[1]**

**4.** Explain why a trial balance produced by accounting software that agrees does not prove the records are correct. **[2]**

**5.** Explain what an audit trail is and why it is useful. **[2]**

**6.** Name the type of error in each case:

**(a)** a clerk selects the account of the wrong customer **[1]**
**(b)** a sale of 450 is keyed in as 540 **[1]**
**(c)** a new printer is coded to office expenses. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Pellar Electrical is considering accounting software with an annual subscription of 2,400 and one-off training costing 900. The owner expects to save 6 hours of bookkeeping a week for 48 weeks a year. Bookkeeping time costs 15 an hour.

**(a)** Calculate the annual value of the time saved. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the net saving in the first year and in each later year. **[2]**
**(c)** Give **two** non-financial factors the owner should consider before deciding. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **two** controls a business can use to reduce the security risks of computerised accounting records. **[4]**

**9.** Bradwell Wholesale has 300 credit customers. Explain **two** advantages of computerised accounting software for managing its trade receivables. **[4]**

**10.** Tolu runs a market stall. She makes about 40 sales a week, all for cash, and buys her goods for cash from two suppliers. Evaluate whether she should change from manual records to accounting software. **[6]**

**11.** A business has recently started using accounting software. Its trial balance agrees, but the owner believes the profit shown is too low. Explain **two** errors that could have caused this. **[4]**

**12.** Explain why staff training is described as both a disadvantage and a necessity when a business introduces accounting software. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: faster recording; automatic and accurate arithmetic; up-to-date balances at any time; reports produced quickly; easy storage and back-up; several users at once [1] [1].

**2.** Any two: cost of hardware, software, updates and training; security risks such as hacking or viruses; loss of data or work if the system fails; input errors still posted; staff resistance or need for training [1] [1].

**3.** C [1]. The software calculates all totals.

**4.** The software posts every entry to both a debit and a credit automatically, so the totals always agree [1]. Errors such as omission, commission, principle and original entry do not affect the totals, so they remain hidden [1].

**5.** A record of who entered or changed each transaction and when [1]. It allows errors to be traced to their source and discourages fraud, because changes can be identified [1].

**6. (a)** Error of commission [1].

**(b)** Error of original entry [1].

**(c)** Error of principle [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** 6 × 48 = 288 hours [1]; 288 × 15 = **4,320** [1].

**(b)** Year 1: 4,320 − (2,400 + 900) = **1,020** [1]. Later years: 4,320 − 2,400 = **1,920** a year [1].

**(c)** Any two: whether staff are willing and able to learn the system; the risk of data loss or hacking; the value of having up-to-date information for decisions; reliability of the supplier's support [1] [1]. **[6]**

**8.** Any two controls, 1 mark for naming each and 1 mark for explaining it: passwords and access levels, so each user can only see or change the records needed for their job, reducing fraud; regular back-ups stored elsewhere, so data can be restored after failure or theft; up-to-date security software, which reduces the risk of viruses and hacking; an audit trail, which records who made each change, so unauthorised changes can be traced.

First control named [1] and explained [1]; second control named [1] and explained [1].

**9.** Any two advantages, 1 mark for each advantage and 1 mark for applying it to Bradwell: each sale is posted automatically to the customer's account, so 300 balances are always up to date without copying; an aged list of receivables can be produced instantly, so overdue customers can be chased quickly, improving cash flow; statements of account for all 300 customers can be printed or emailed at once, saving clerical time.

First advantage [1] applied [1]; second advantage [1] applied [1].

**10.** For: software would give her up-to-date figures for sales and profit and remove arithmetic errors [1]. Against: with only about 40 cash sales a week and two suppliers, recording by hand takes little time, so the time saved is small [1]; she has no credit customers, so the receivables features are of no use [1]; the subscription and training are a cost that may be larger than the benefit [1]; errors of omission or principle would still depend on her care [1]. Judgement: she should keep manual records for now, and reconsider if she begins to sell on credit or her trade grows [1].

**11.** Any two errors, 1 mark for each error and 1 mark for its effect on profit: a sale was not entered (omission), so revenue is understated; a purchase of a non-current asset was coded to an expense account (principle), so expenses are overstated; a sale or an expense was keyed in with the wrong amount (original entry), understating revenue or overstating an expense. Each keeps the trial balance in agreement.

First error [1] and effect [1]; second error [1] and effect [1].

**12.** Training is a cost in money and in staff time away from normal work, so it is a disadvantage [1]. Without it, staff may enter data wrongly or use the system inefficiently, so the benefits of the software are lost [1]; well-trained staff also reduce the risk of depending on one person [1].

---

## Where marks are usually lost

- Claiming software removes all errors.
- Points that do not compare with manual methods.
- Ignoring the size and type of business in evaluate questions.
- Naming a control without saying which risk it reduces.
