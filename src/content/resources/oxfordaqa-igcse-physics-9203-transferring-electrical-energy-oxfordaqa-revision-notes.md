These notes condense section **3.6.5 Transferring electrical energy** (statements a to f) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) specification, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered. Read the [electrical energy study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-transferring-electrical-energy-oxfordaqa/) for full worked examples, then test yourself with the [electrical energy practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-transferring-electrical-energy-oxfordaqa-practice/).

## Condensed recall

**Appliances (a).** Designed to transfer energy from the mains to useful stores, for example a kettle to the thermal store of water, a fan to kinetic energy of the blades. Some energy is always dissipated, usually by heating.

**Power (b).** The rate at which energy is transferred.

> P = E ÷ t (W = J ÷ s)

**Power, current and pd (c).**

> P = I × V, so I = P ÷ V

Find the current, then choose a fuse rated just above it.

**Energy, pd and charge (d).**

> E = V × Q (J = V × C)

A pd of 1 V means 1 J is transferred for each coulomb of charge.

**Energy depends on time and power (e).** Mains energy is usually measured in kilowatt-hours because the joule is very small.

**Mains energy (f).**

> E = P × t (kWh = kW × h)

You will not be asked to convert kWh to J.

**Cost.** Cost = energy in kWh × price per kWh.

**Meter readings.** Energy used = later reading − earlier reading.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Power | Rate of energy transfer, in watts (1 W = 1 J/s) |
| Power rating | The power an appliance transfers in normal use |
| Kilowatt (kW) | 1000 watts |
| Kilowatt-hour (kWh) | Energy transferred by a 1 kW appliance in 1 hour |
| Charge, Q | Measured in coulombs (C) |
| Potential difference, V | Energy transferred per unit charge, in volts |
| Fuse rating | The current above which the fuse melts |
| Electricity meter | Records the total energy supplied, in kWh |

## Unit pairs to keep together

| If you use | Then use | Energy comes out in |
|---|---|---|
| watts | seconds | joules |
| kilowatts | hours | kilowatt-hours |

Conversions you will need: kW → W (× 1000); minutes → s (× 60); minutes → h (÷ 60).

## Fuse choice method

1. I = P ÷ V (P in watts).
2. List the available ratings.
3. Choose the smallest rating **greater than** the current.

Examples at 230 V with 3 A, 5 A and 13 A fuses:

| Appliance | Current | Fuse |
|---|---|---|
| 120 W television | 0.52 A | 3 A |
| 1.2 kW iron | 5.2 A | 13 A |
| 1.8 kW hairdryer | 7.8 A | 13 A |

Note the iron: 5.2 A is just above 5 A, so the 5 A fuse would melt in normal use.

## Choosing the right equation

| You are given | You want | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Power and pd | Current | I = P ÷ V |
| Energy and time (J, s) | Power | P = E ÷ t |
| pd and charge | Energy | E = V Q |
| Current, time and pd | Energy | Q = I t, then E = V Q |
| Power (kW) and time (h) | Energy in kWh | E = P t |
| Two meter readings and a price | Cost | (later − earlier) × price |

## Worked reminder: a monthly bill

A meter reads 30 118.4 kWh, and a month later 30 402.9 kWh. Price: 25 cents per kWh.

1. Energy = 30 402.9 − 30 118.4 = 284.5 kWh.
2. Cost = 284.5 × 25 = 7112.5 cents.
3. In the main unit, 71.13 (rounded to the nearest cent).

## Exam traps

- Watts with hours, or kilowatts with seconds.
- Not converting minutes.
- A fuse rating equal to or below the working current.
- Adding meter readings instead of subtracting.
- Forgetting to change cents into the main currency unit when asked.
- Saying a higher power appliance always uses more energy: time matters too.

## Quick self-test

1. Define power.
2. A motor transfers 180 000 J in 4 minutes. Find its power.
3. Calculate the current in a 1.8 kW hairdryer on a 230 V supply.
4. Fuses of 3 A, 5 A and 13 A are available. Which fuse suits the hairdryer?
5. A 9.0 V battery transfers 540 J. How much charge flows?
6. What is a kilowatt-hour?
7. How many kWh does a 3.0 kW heater transfer in 20 minutes?
8. A 400 W appliance transfers 1.2 kWh. How long was it on?
9. Meter readings are 12 405.6 kWh and 12 618.1 kWh. Find the energy used.
10. At 20 cents per kWh, what does the energy in question 9 cost?
11. Why is the kWh used for household energy rather than the joule?
12. A current of 2.0 A flows for 3.0 minutes through a 6.0 V lamp. Calculate the energy transferred.

### Answers

1. The rate at which energy is transferred.
2. P = 180 000 ÷ 240 = **750 W**.
3. I = 1800 ÷ 230 = **7.8 A**.
4. **13 A**.
5. Q = E ÷ V = 540 ÷ 9.0 = **60 C**.
6. The energy transferred by a 1 kW appliance used for 1 hour.
7. 3.0 × (20 ÷ 60) = **1.0 kWh**.
8. t = E ÷ P = 1.2 ÷ 0.40 = **3.0 hours**.
9. 12 618.1 − 12 405.6 = **212.5 kWh**.
10. 212.5 × 20 = **4250 cents** (42.50 in the main unit).
11. The joule is very small, so household energies in joules are inconveniently large numbers.
12. Q = 2.0 × 180 = 360 C; E = 6.0 × 360 = **2160 J**.
