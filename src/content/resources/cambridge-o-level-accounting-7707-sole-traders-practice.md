## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** State **two** advantages of operating as a sole trader. **[2]**

**2.** Define the term "intangible asset" and give **one** example. **[2]**

**3.** State the heading in a statement of financial position under which each item appears.

**(a)** Bank overdraft **[1]**

**(b)** Goodwill **[1]**

**(c)** Bank loan repayable in five years **[1]**

**(d)** Rent paid in advance **[1]**

**4.** The owner of a shop took goods costing $650 for her own use. Prepare the journal entry to record this. A narrative is not required. **[2]**

**5.** Closing inventory was understated by $800. State the effect on gross profit, current assets and capital. **[3]**

**6.** Explain why the statement of profit or loss of a service business has no gross profit. **[2]**

| Bank | 7,820 | |
| Loan from bank (repayable 2030) | | 5,000 |
| Loan interest | 300 | |
| **Totals** | **80,750** | **80,750** |

At 30 June 2028:

1. Fees of $1,100 for work done in June had not been received or recorded.

2. Electricity of $410 was owing.

3. Rent includes $750 paid for July 2028.

4. Electricity includes $180 for Hana's private flat.

5. Unused salon supplies were valued at $520.

6. Equipment is depreciated at 20% per year using the reducing balance method.

**(a)** Prepare Hana's statement of profit or loss for the year ended 30 June 2028. **[8]**

**(b)** Prepare Hana's statement of financial position at 30 June 2028. **[10]**

**8.** State the effect of each error on the profit for the year and on the named item in the statement of financial position.

**(a)** Depreciation of $1,500 was not charged. Item: non-current assets. **[2]**

**(b)** Goods costing $300 taken by the owner were not recorded. Item: capital at the year end. **[2]**

**(c)** Rent owing of $200 was not recorded. Item: current liabilities. **[2]**

**(d)** An increase of $90 in the allowance for irrecoverable debts was not recorded. Item: current assets. **[2]**

**9.** Musa is a sole trader who owns one bakery. He wants to open a second bakery in another town.

**(a)** Explain **two** disadvantages of being a sole trader that Musa may face in carrying out his plan. **[4]**

**(b)** Suggest **one** way Musa could raise the capital he needs and state **one** drawback of it. **[2]**

**10.** Wei's capital at 1 January 2027 was $42,000. During 2027 he brought his own car, valued at $5,000, into the business. His profit for the year was $19,400. He withdrew $15,000 in cash and took goods costing $600.

Calculate Wei's capital at 31 December 2027. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Quick decisions because the owner alone decides [1]. The owner keeps all the profit [1]. Also accept: simple and cheap to set up; financial affairs kept private; close contact with customers. **[2]**

**2.** A non-current asset with no physical form [1]; for example, goodwill [1]. **[2]**

**3.** **(a)** Current liabilities [1]

**(b)** Non-current assets (intangible) [1]

**(c)** Non-current liabilities [1]

**(d)** Current assets (other receivables) [1] **[4]**

**4.** Dr Drawings $650 [1], Cr Purchases $650 [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Gross profit understated by $800 [1]; current assets understated by $800 [1]; capital understated by $800 [1]. **[3]**

**6.** A service business does not buy and resell goods [1], so there is no cost of sales to deduct from its income; expenses are deducted directly from fees [1]. **[2]**

**7.** **(a)**

```

                                                    $          $
Fees (62,400 + 1,100)                                     63,500
Less Expenses

  Rent (9,000 − 750)                            8,250
  Electricity (2,350 + 410 − 180)               2,580

  Depreciation of equipment                     1,920

                                                          ------

                                                          ======
```

Heading [1]; fees $63,500 [1]; rent $8,250 [1]; electricity $2,580 [1]; supplies $3,660 [1]; depreciation 20% × ($16,000 − $6,400) = $1,920 [1]; wages, insurance and loan interest [1]; profit $23,990 [1].

**8.** **(a)** Profit overstated by $1,500 [1]; non-current assets overstated by $1,500 [1]

**(b)** Profit understated by $300, because purchases are too high [1]; capital unchanged, because drawings are also understated by $300 [1]

**(c)** Profit overstated by $200 [1]; current liabilities understated by $200 [1]

**(d)** Profit overstated by $90 [1]; current assets overstated by $90 [1] **[8]**

**9.** **(a)** Limited capital [1]: Musa can only use his own savings and what a lender will provide, which may not be enough for a second bakery [1]. Unlimited liability [1]: if the new bakery fails, Musa's house and private savings could be used to pay its debts [1]. Also accept: workload, because he cannot be in two bakeries at once; lack of skills in managing a larger business.

**(b)** A bank loan, which must be repaid with interest even if the new bakery makes little profit [1] [1]. Also accept: taking a partner, who would share the profits and the decisions. **[6]**

**10.** $42,000 + $5,000 + $19,400 [1] − ($15,000 + $600) [1] = **$50,800** [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Deducting goods taken by the owner from revenue.
- Leaving private expenses in business expenses.
- Forgetting to show the inventory of unused supplies as a current asset.
- Placing a long-term loan under current liabilities.
- Using "for the year ended" on the statement of financial position.
