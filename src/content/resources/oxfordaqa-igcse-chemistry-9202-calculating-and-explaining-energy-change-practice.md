This set practises section 3.9.2 Calculating and explaining energy change (statements a to h) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202) specification, Version 6.2, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Section A is short calculations and recall; Section B covers fuel and solution calorimetry, energy level diagrams and bond energies. The course is untiered.

Use c = 4.2 J/g °C for water and dilute solutions, and take 1 cm³ of solution as 1 g. Bond energies (kJ/mol): C–H 413, C–C 347, C=C 612, C–Br 290, C–Cl 338, O=O 498, C=O 805, O–H 464, Br–Br 193, Cl–Cl 243, H–Cl 432.

## Section A

**1.** Calculate the energy transferred when 200 g of water is heated by 12.5 °C. Give your answer in kJ. **[2]**

**2.** State two units in which the energy released by a fuel can be compared fairly. **[2]**

**3.** On an energy level diagram, the reactants are at 300 kJ, the peak is at 380 kJ and the products are at 120 kJ.

**(a)** Calculate the activation energy. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the overall energy change and state whether the reaction is exothermic or endothermic. **[2]**

**4.** State the effect of a catalyst on the activation energy and on the overall energy change of a reaction. **[2]**

**5.** Complete the sentence: in an endothermic reaction, the energy needed to break existing bonds is ________ than the energy released from forming new bonds. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why reactions in solution are carried out in a polystyrene cup with a lid. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A student compares two fuels, X and Y. Each heats 150 g of water in the same copper can.

| Fuel | Mass burned / g | Temperature rise / °C |
|---|---|---|
| X | 0.60 | 19.0 |
| Y | 0.50 | 21.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the energy released per gram by each fuel. **[4]**

**(b)** State which fuel releases more energy per gram. **[1]**

**(c)** Give two variables the student kept the same to make the comparison fair. **[2]**

**8.** 40.0 cm³ of 0.500 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid is mixed with 40.0 cm³ of 0.500 mol/dm³ potassium hydroxide solution in a polystyrene cup. The temperature rises by 3.3 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the energy transferred to the solution, in J. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of moles of water formed. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate ΔH in kJ per mole of water formed. Include the sign. **[2]**

**9.** 5.35 g of ammonium chloride, NH₄Cl (Mᵣ = 53.5), is added to 50.0 g of water. The temperature falls by 7.0 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the energy taken in, using the mass of water. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy change per mole of ammonium chloride, with the correct sign. **[3]**

**10.** For the reaction in Question 3, sketch and describe the energy level diagram. Your description should include the labels and arrows you would draw and how the diagram changes with a catalyst. **[4]**

**11.** Ethene reacts with bromine: C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂ (the product contains one C–C bond, four C–H bonds and two C–Br bonds).

**(a)** Calculate the energy needed to break the bonds that change in this reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy released forming the new bonds. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the energy change for the reaction and state whether it is exothermic or endothermic. **[2]**

**12.** Propane burns completely: C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O. Propane contains two C–C bonds and eight C–H bonds.

**(a)** Calculate the total energy needed to break all the bonds in the reactants. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the total energy released when the bonds in the products form. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the energy change per mole of propane. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain, in terms of bonds, why the reaction is exothermic. **[1]**

**13.** Methane reacts with chlorine: CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl. One C–H bond and one Cl–Cl bond are broken; one C–Cl bond and one H–Cl bond are formed.

**(a)** Calculate the energy change for the reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** A different data book gives the C–Cl bond energy as 346 kJ/mol. Explain how this would change your answer to (a). **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Q = 200 × 4.2 × 12.5 = 10 500 J [1] = **10.5 kJ** [1].

**2.** kJ per gram (kJ/g) [1]; kJ per mole (kJ/mol) [1].

**3. (a)** 380 − 300 = **80 kJ** [1].

**(b)** 120 − 300 = **−180 kJ** [1]; exothermic [1].

**4.** The activation energy is lowered [1]; the overall energy change is unchanged [1].

**5.** Greater [1].

**6.** Polystyrene is an insulator, so less energy is transferred to or from the surroundings [1].

**7. (a)** X: Q = 150 × 4.2 × 19.0 = 11 970 J = 11.97 kJ [1]; 11.97 ÷ 0.60 = **20.0 kJ/g** [1]. Y: Q = 150 × 4.2 × 21.0 = 13 230 J = 13.23 kJ [1]; 13.23 ÷ 0.50 = **26.5 kJ/g** [1].

**(b)** Y [1].

**(c)** Any two: mass of water; the same container (copper can); distance from flame to can; starting temperature; draught shield used [2].

*Examiner insight:* Comparing the temperature rises alone (21.0 vs 19.0) is not enough; the masses of fuel burned were different.

**8. (a)** m = 80.0 g [1]; Q = 80.0 × 4.2 × 3.3 = **1109 J** (1108.8 J) [1].

**(b)** 0.500 × 0.0400 = **0.0200 mol** [1].

**(c)** 1.109 kJ ÷ 0.0200 = 55.4 kJ/mol [1]; ΔH = **−55.4 kJ/mol** [1].

**9. (a)** 50.0 × 4.2 × 7.0 = **1470 J** [1].

**(b)** Moles NH₄Cl = 5.35 ÷ 53.5 = 0.100 mol [1]; 1.470 ÷ 0.100 = 14.7 kJ/mol [1]; temperature fell, so **+14.7 kJ/mol** [1].

**10.**

- Energy axis (vertical) and progress of reaction axis (horizontal) [1].
- Reactant level at 300 kJ above the product level at 120 kJ, joined by a curve with a peak at 380 kJ [1].
- Activation energy arrow from reactant level to peak; ΔH arrow pointing down from reactants to products [1].
- With a catalyst, a lower peak between the same reactant and product levels [1].

**11. (a)** C=C + Br–Br = 612 + 193 = **805 kJ** [1].

**(b)** C–C + 2 C–Br = 347 + 2 × 290 = **927 kJ** [1].

**(c)** 805 − 927 = **−122 kJ/mol** [1]; exothermic [1].

**12. (a)** 2(347) + 8(413) + 5(498) [1] = 694 + 3304 + 2490 = **6488 kJ** [1].

**(b)** 6(805) + 8(464) [1] = 4830 + 3712 = **8542 kJ** [1].

**(c)** 6488 − 8542 = **−2054 kJ/mol** [1].

**(d)** More energy is released forming the bonds in the products than is needed to break the bonds in the reactants [1].

**13. (a)** Broken: 413 + 243 = 656 kJ; formed: 338 + 432 = 770 kJ [1]; 656 − 770 = **−114 kJ/mol** [1].

**(b)** More energy would be released forming bonds, so the energy change would be more negative, −122 kJ/mol [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using fuel mass or a single solution volume for m.
- Forgetting the sign of ΔH, or giving the wrong sign.
- Not converting J to kJ before dividing.
- Counting bonds in one molecule but not multiplying by coefficients (5O₂, 3CO₂, 4H₂O).
- Calculating formed − broken.
- Diagrams with no labelled arrows.
