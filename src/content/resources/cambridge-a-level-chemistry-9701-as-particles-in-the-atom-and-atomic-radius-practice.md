## Section B

**7.** Beams of four particles, all moving at the same velocity, enter a uniform electric field between two charged plates: a proton, a neutron, an electron and a ⁴He²⁺ ion.

**(a)** State which particle or particles are deflected towards the positive plate. **[1]**

**(b)** Compare the deflections of the proton and the ⁴He²⁺ ion, explaining your answer. **[2]**

**(c)** A ⁷Li⁺ ion is added to the experiment. Predict whether it is deflected more or less than the ⁴He²⁺ ion, with a calculation. **[2]**

**8.** The table gives atomic radii for some Period 3 elements.

| Element | Na | Mg | Al | Si | P | S | Cl |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Atomic radius / nm | 0.186 | 0.160 | 0.143 | 0.117 | 0.110 | 0.104 | 0.099 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in atomic radius from Na to Cl. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain the trend shown. **[3]**

**(c)** Predict, with a reason, whether a potassium atom is larger or smaller than a sodium atom. **[2]**

**9.** The ions N³⁻, O²⁻, F⁻, Na⁺, Mg²⁺ and Al³⁺ all have the same electronic arrangement.

**(a)** State the term used for species with the same number of electrons and give their common arrangement. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the ionic radius decreases from N³⁻ to Al³⁺. **[3]**

**10.** Typical ionic radii across Period 3 are Na⁺ 0.095, Mg²⁺ 0.065, Al³⁺ 0.050, P³⁻ 0.212, S²⁻ 0.184 and Cl⁻ 0.181 nm.

**(a)** Explain why there is a large increase in radius from Al³⁺ to P³⁻. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why S²⁻ is larger than Cl⁻. **[2]**

**11.** A student states: "A magnesium atom is smaller than a sodium atom because magnesium has more shielding."

**(a)** Explain why this statement is incorrect. **[2]**

**(b)** Give the correct explanation. **[2]**

**12.** The radius of an atom is about 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m and the radius of its nucleus about 1 × 10⁻¹⁵ m.

## Answers

**1.** D [1]. ¹⁶O²⁻ has 10 electrons and 8 neutrons. (²⁴Mg²⁺: 10 e, 12 n; ¹⁹F⁻: 10 e, 10 n; ²³Na⁺: 10 e, 12 n.) *(Total: 1 mark)*

**2.** Almost all the mass is in the nucleus, because protons and neutrons are far more massive than electrons [1]. All the positive charge is in the nucleus, carried by protons [1]. The negative charge, carried by electrons, is spread through the large volume around the nucleus [1]. *(Total: 3 marks)*

**3.** ⁵⁹Co²⁺: 27 protons, 32 neutrons, 25 electrons [1]. ³¹P³⁻: 15 protons, 16 neutrons, 18 electrons [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**4.** 26 protons is iron and mass number 26 + 30 = 56 [1]; two fewer electrons than protons, so ⁵⁶Fe²⁺ [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**5.** Protons: 6 + 3 × 8 = 30 [1]. Neutrons: 6 + 3 × 8 = 30 [1]. Electrons: 30 + 2 = 32 [1]. *(Total: 3 marks)*

**6.** The electron has the same size of charge but a much smaller mass (about 1/1836), so its charge-to-mass ratio is far larger [1]. *(Total: 1 mark)*

**7. (a)** The electron only [1].

**(b)** Both are deflected in the same direction, towards the negative plate [1]. Charge-to-mass ratios: proton 1 ÷ 1 = 1; ⁴He²⁺ 2 ÷ 4 = 0.5, so the proton is deflected twice as much [1].

**(c)** ⁷Li⁺: 1 ÷ 7 = 0.14, less than 0.5 [1], so it is deflected less than ⁴He²⁺ [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

**8. (a)** (0.186 − 0.099) ÷ 0.186 × 100 = 46.8 % [1].

**(b)** Nuclear charge increases from Na to Cl [1]. Electrons are added to the same (third) shell, and the inner shells are the same, so shielding is roughly constant [1]. The outer electrons are attracted more strongly and pulled closer to the nucleus [1].

**(c)** Larger [1]: potassium has an extra occupied shell, so its outer electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded, which outweighs its greater nuclear charge [1]. *(Total: 6 marks)*

**9. (a)** Isoelectronic [1]; 2,8 (1s²2s²2p⁶) [1].

**(b)** The number of electrons and shells is the same in every ion [1]. Nuclear charge increases from 7 (N) to 13 (Al) [1], so the same electrons are attracted more strongly and pulled closer to the nucleus [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

**10. (a)** Al³⁺ has lost its third shell and has the arrangement 2,8 [1], while P³⁻ has gained electrons into the third shell, 2,8,8, so it has an extra occupied shell [1].

**(b)** Both have the arrangement 2,8,8 [1], but S has 16 protons and Cl has 17, so the electrons in S²⁻ are attracted less strongly [1].

**(c)** K⁺ is larger [1], because it has one more occupied shell than Na⁺ (2,8,8 compared with 2,8) [1]. *(Total: 6 marks)*

**11. (a)** Shielding is roughly the same for Na and Mg [1], because both have the same inner shells (2,8) and their outer electrons are in the same shell [1].

**(b)** Magnesium has a greater nuclear charge (12 compared with 11) [1], so its outer electrons are attracted more strongly and are closer to the nucleus [1]. *(Total: 4 marks)*

**12. (a)** 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ ÷ 1 × 10⁻¹⁵ = 1 × 10⁵ (100 000 times) [1].

**(b)** The nucleus, which holds almost all the mass, occupies only a tiny fraction of the atom's volume [1]; the rest is occupied only by electrons, which have negligible mass, so most of the atom's volume contains almost no matter [1]. *(Total: 3 marks)*

## Where marks are usually lost

- Getting the electron count wrong for anions, especially polyatomic ones.
- Comparing deflection by mass or by charge alone instead of by the ratio.
- Explaining the Period 3 trend with "more shielding".
- Forgetting the extra occupied shell when comparing Al³⁺ with P³⁻.
- Giving isoelectronic trends without stating that the number of electrons is fixed.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I work out the electrons in a polyatomic ion?**
Add the protons of every atom, then subtract the overall charge. A 2− ion such as carbonate therefore has two more electrons than protons.

**Why is a proton deflected more than a ⁴He²⁺ ion?**
Deflection depends on charge ÷ mass. The proton's ratio is 1 and the ⁴He²⁺ ion's is 2/4 = 0.5, so the proton is deflected twice as much, in the same direction.
