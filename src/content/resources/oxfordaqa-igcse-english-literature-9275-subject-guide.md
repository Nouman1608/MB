OxfordAQA International GCSE English Literature (9275) introduces
students to close reading and essay writing on prose fiction, drama and
poetry, together with texts they have not seen before. It suits anyone
who enjoys stories, plays and poems and wants to learn to write about
them with confidence. The qualification is linear and has two routes.
Every student sits Paper 1, Prose and Drama, worth 40%. Route A then adds
Paper 2a, Poetry and Unseen Texts, worth 60%; Route B instead adds Paper
2b, Poetry, worth 40%, together with a non-exam assessment worth 20%.

## How the course is organised

The content is organised by route.

**Prose and drama (both routes).** All students study a prose fiction
text and a drama text, and this element is assessed by Paper 1.

**Route A: poetry and unseen texts.** Route A students study poetry
alongside unseen poetry and unseen prose. The unseen work tests reading
skills directly, on texts you meet for the first time in the exam.

**Route B: poetry and non-exam assessment.** Route B students study
poetry and unseen poetry, and complete a non-exam assessment: one
extended response to a prose fiction text of their choice.

The specification is being revised for first teaching from September
2026 with a refreshed set-text, poetry and short-story list. The board's
page states that the paper structure, timings, marks and weightings are
unchanged; only the prescribed texts differ. Students on the current text
list have final May/June exams in 2027, with final resits in November
2027. Check with your teacher which list your class is following.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Route | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1: Prose and Drama | Both routes | 90 minutes | 60 | 40% |
| Paper 2a: Poetry and Unseen Texts | Route A | 135 minutes | 90 | 60% |
| Paper 2b: Poetry | Route B | 90 minutes | 60 | 40% |
| NEA: one extended response to a prose fiction text | Route B | Not timed | 30 | 20% |

So a Route A student sits two exams, Paper 1 and Paper 2a, while a Route
B student sits Paper 1 and the shorter Paper 2b, and completes the NEA. Paper 2a is an open-book exam. The exams are externally assessed;
the NEA is marked by your teacher and moderated by the board. There are
no tiers. This assessment structure was first taught in September 2016
and first examined in May/June 2018.

## How the parts fit together

Paper 1 is common ground. The skills it builds, tracking how characters,
themes and ideas develop across a whole text and supporting an argument
with well-chosen evidence, are needed in every other component.

The routes then differ in where they place the weight. Route A puts more
emphasis on unseen reading: Paper 2a includes unseen prose as well as
poetry, so the ability to analyse an unfamiliar passage quickly is central
to more than half of the grade. Route B spreads assessment more evenly,
replacing unseen prose with a piece of extended, redrafted writing on a
prose text, which rewards planning and independent thinking.

Poetry appears on both routes, as do unseen poems, so comparison of poems
and close analysis of poetic methods are worth developing whichever route
your school takes. Across everything, the reward goes to clear
arguments, precise references to the text and attention to how a
writer's choices shape meaning.

## Planning your study

Begin by reading your prose and drama texts carefully, more than once,
and build notes on character, theme, context and key quotations as you
go. This prepares you for Paper 1 and builds habits you will reuse.

Next comes poetry. Study each set poem closely and compare poems in
pairs, since comparison is a skill that takes practice. Read a short
unseen poem regularly and annotate it in a set time.

Find out your route early. On Route A, build unseen prose reading into
your weekly routine. On Route B, agree a timeline for the NEA with your
teacher, leaving time for planning, drafting and final checks.

Move to timed questions after each text is taught, and to full papers in
the final months. The course's exam-preparation page covers the
differences between the two routes in more detail, so use it for
paper-level planning.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is OxfordAQA International GCSE English Literature 9275 assessed?**
All students sit Paper 1, Prose and Drama, which is 90 minutes long, worth 60 marks and 40% of the grade. Route A adds Paper 2a (60%), while Route B adds Paper 2b (40%) and a non-exam assessment (20%).

**What is in the Route B non-exam assessment?**
One extended response to a prose fiction text of the student's choice, worth 30 marks and 20% of the grade. It is teacher assessed and moderated by the board.

**Is Paper 2a open-book?**
Yes. Paper 2a, Poetry and Unseen Texts, is an open-book exam of 135 minutes, worth 90 marks.

**Is the 9275 specification changing?**
It is being revised for first teaching from September 2026 with a refreshed set-text, poetry and short-story list. The board states that the paper structure, timings, marks and weightings are unchanged.

**When are the final exams on the current text list?**
The final May/June exams for the current text list are in 2027, with final resits in November 2027.
