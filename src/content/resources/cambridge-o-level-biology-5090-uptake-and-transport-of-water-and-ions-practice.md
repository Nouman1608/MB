## Section A

**1.** Which is the correct order for the pathway of water through a plant?

A: root hair cell, xylem, root cortex cell, mesophyll cell

B: root hair cell, root cortex cell, xylem, mesophyll cell

C: root cortex cell, root hair cell, mesophyll cell, xylem

D: xylem, root hair cell, root cortex cell, mesophyll cell

**[1]**

**2.** By which process does water enter a root hair cell?

A: active transport

B: diffusion of ions

C: osmosis

D: translocation

**[1]**

**3.** State **two** features of a root hair cell that increase the uptake of water and ions. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why root hair cells contain many mitochondria. **[2]**

**5.** Outline the pathway taken by water from the soil to the cells in a leaf. **[4]**

## Section B

**6.** The sap in the cells along a root, from the soil inwards, has the following concentrations.

| Position | Concentration of sap (mol per dm cubed) |
|---|---|
| Soil water | 0.01 |
| Root hair cell | 0.18 |
| Outer cortex cell | 0.24 |
| Inner cortex cell | 0.30 |

**(a)** State the direction in which water will move across the cells. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain your answer using the term water potential. **[3]**

**(c)** Predict what would happen to the movement of water if the soil water concentration increased to 0.40 mol per dm cubed, and explain your answer. **[3]**

**7.** A student placed the cut end of a leafy celery stem in water containing red ink. After 40 minutes, the ink had travelled 50 mm up the stem.

**(a)** Calculate the rate of movement of the ink in mm per minute. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how the student could show which tissue contained the ink. **[3]**

**(c)** State where the ink would be seen in the section. **[1]**

**(d)** State **two** conditions that would increase the rate at which the ink moves up the stem. **[2]**

**(e)** Suggest why the student cut the stem under water. **[2]**

**8.** A gardener moves a young plant from a pot to a flower bed and notices that it wilts even though the soil is wet. The plant had some roots torn off.

Suggest an explanation. **[3]**

**9.** Compare the ways in which water and mineral ions enter a root hair cell. **[4]**

**10.** Root hair cells do not contain chloroplasts. Suggest why. **[2]**

---

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Any two of: long, thin extension giving a large surface area [1]; thin cell wall and membrane giving a short distance [1]; concentrated cell sap giving a low water potential [1]; many mitochondria [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Mitochondria carry out aerobic respiration, which releases energy [1]. The energy is used for the active transport of mineral ions into the cell [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Water enters a root hair cell [1], passes through the root cortex cells [1], enters the xylem and travels up the root and stem to the leaf [1], and then leaves the xylem to enter the mesophyll cells [1]. **[4]**

**6.**

**(a)** Inwards, from the soil to the inner cortex cell [1].

**(b)** Water moves by osmosis from a higher water potential to a lower water potential [1]. The soil water is the most dilute, so it has the highest water potential [1]. The sap becomes more concentrated towards the inside of the root, so the water potential is lowest in the inner cortex cell [1].

**(c)** Water would move out of the root hair cell, or stop moving into it [1]. The soil water (0.40) would be more concentrated than the root hair cell (0.18), so it would have a lower water potential [1]. Water would move from the cell into the soil, and the plant would wilt [1]. **[7]**

**7.**

**(a)** Rate = 50 divided by 40 [1] = 1.25 mm per minute [1].

**(b)** Cut a thin transverse section of the stem [1], put it on a slide and examine it with a hand lens or microscope [1], and look for the red ink in the tissue [1].

**(c)** In the xylem [1].

**(d)** Any two of: higher temperature; a draught or wind; lower humidity; brighter light [1 each].

**(e)** To stop air entering the xylem [1], which would block the vessels and stop the water rising [1]. **[10]**

**8.** Torn roots mean that fewer root hair cells are left, so the surface area for absorbing water is reduced [1]. The plant takes up less water than it loses from the leaves [1], so the cells lose turgor and the plant wilts [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Water enters by osmosis, but ions enter by active transport or diffusion [1]. Water moves from higher to lower water potential [1]. Active transport moves ions against the concentration gradient using energy from respiration [1], but osmosis and diffusion need no energy from respiration [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Root hair cells are underground, so no light reaches them [1]. They cannot photosynthesise, so chloroplasts would be of no use [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Missing out the root cortex cells in the pathway.
- Writing "diffusion" for the uptake of water instead of "osmosis".
- Naming a feature without its function.
- Not linking the mitochondria to energy for active transport.
- Forgetting the unit in the rate calculation.
