## Section A

**1.** Decode this brief by identifying audience, purpose, genre, mode and persona: *"Write the script of a two-minute talk, to be given by a student representative at a parents' evening, explaining the school's new reading scheme."* **[5]**

**2.** Rewrite this sentence for a blog aimed at teenagers: *"Adequate hydration is essential for optimal cognitive performance during examinations."* Then name **two** changes you made. **[3]**

**3.** Rewrite this extract from a spoken transcript as one sentence of a formal written report: *"so basically (.) loads of people (.) like more than half of us (.) said the canteen's too noisy"* **[3]**

**4.** Give **four** conventions you would include in a leaflet, and explain why each suits the genre. **[8]**

**5.** Explain why adding a reflective commentary to a Unit 1 Section B response would not earn credit. **[2]**

## Section B

Read the two source texts below (original, written for this page). Both concern **a community garden**.

> **Text A** (newsletter article from a housing association)
>
> *Green Corner Community Garden opened in April on a former car park. Residents now grow vegetables on 24 raised
> beds, and the association has installed a water tank and a tool shed. Volunteers meet on Saturday mornings. The
> association is seeking new volunteers, particularly residents with experience of carpentry, to build a covered
> seating area.*

> **Text B** (transcript of a resident speaking in a local news video)
>
> *I'd never grown anything (.) not even a cactus (.) and now look (.) tomatoes (.) beans (.) and I know my
> neighbours' names (.) all of them (.) that's the real harvest if you ask me*

**6.** Task option 1: *Write the opening of a speech, to be given by the resident in Text B, at the garden's first anniversary event.* Write **80-120 words**. **[8]**

**7.** Task option 2: *Write the opening of a recruitment leaflet for new volunteers.* Write **80-120 words**. **[8]**

**8.** For **either** task, explain which source material you selected, which you left out, and why. **[4]**

**9.** A student's response to Task option 1 begins: *"Green Corner Community Garden opened in April on a former car park. There are 24 raised beds."* Identify **two** weaknesses and rewrite the opening. **[5]**

**10.** Explain how you would decide between the two task options in the exam. **[3]**

**11.** Explain **three** ways a writer can sustain a consistent style throughout a directed writing response. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** **Audience**: parents (adults, non-specialist, invested in their children's progress) [1]. **Purpose**: to inform and reassure, and possibly persuade parents to support the scheme [1]. **Genre**: a talk at a school event [1]. **Mode**: spoken, planned, delivered aloud in about two minutes [1]. **Persona**: a student representative speaking on behalf of the school, so polite and confident but not adult-official [1].

**2.** Example: *"Drink plenty of water before and during your exams: your brain works better when it isn't thirsty."* [1] Changes, any two: **imperative and direct address** replace the impersonal statement [1]; **plain, everyday lexis** ("brain works better") replaces Latinate terms ("optimal cognitive performance") [1]. Alternatively, a colon or a friendlier tone.

**3.** Example: *"More than half of respondents reported that the canteen was too noisy."* Credit **removal of fillers and repairs** ("so basically", "like") [1], **precise, formal quantification** replacing "loads of people" [1], and **reporting verb and third person** suited to a report [1].

**4.** Any four, two marks each: **heading** identifies the subject at a glance [1] [1]; **subheadings** let readers find relevant sections [1] [1]; **bullet points** make information scannable [1] [1]; **imperatives** prompt the reader to act [1] [1]. Alternatively, contact details or a next step, because a leaflet's purpose is usually practical.

**5.** The specification credits the ability to **sustain an appropriate style in the writing itself** [1]; Section B is a writing task only, so a commentary does not show the assessed skill and wastes time [1].

**6.** Credit, as a response to the task: **speech conventions** such as greeting the audience and direct address [1] [1]; a **first-person voice consistent with the resident** in Text B (warm, humorous, unpretentious) [1] [1]; **material from both texts transformed**, e.g. the beds and tank woven into the story rather than listed [1] [1]; **spoken-mode features** such as rhetorical questions or repetition, but without the non-fluency of the transcript [1]; **an opening that engages and sets up the rest of the speech** [1]. Example opening: *"Good afternoon, everyone. A year ago, I couldn't keep a cactus alive. Today I'm standing in front of twenty-four beds of vegetables that we grew together, on a patch of ground where people used to park their cars..."*

**7.** Credit: **leaflet conventions**, a heading and subheadings or bullets [1] [1]; **persuasive purpose**, e.g. imperatives and benefits for the reader [1] [1]; **the request for carpentry skills transformed** into a clear call to action [1]; **the resident's testimony reused** as a short quotation or paraphrase that personalises the appeal [1]; **practical information** such as when volunteers meet [1]; **register suited to residents**, friendly and direct [1].

**8.** Credit **identifying selected material relevant to the purpose** (e.g. for the speech: the transformation of the car park, neighbours getting to know each other) [1] [1] and **identifying material left out with a reason** (e.g. the water tank and tool shed are less moving for an anniversary audience) [1], with a **clear link to audience and purpose** [1].

**9.** Weaknesses: the sentences are **copied almost word for word from Text A** [1], and the register is **written and informational, not the resident's spoken voice** [1]. Rewrite: credit **a spoken greeting or direct address** [1], **the resident's personal, informal voice** [1], and **the facts transformed** rather than lifted [1].

**10.** Choose the option whose **audience you can picture most clearly** [1], where the **source material supplies enough content** [1], and whose **genre conventions you can sustain confidently** throughout [1].

**11.** Any three, two marks each: **fix style markers before writing** and check each paragraph uses them [1] [1]; **keep the persona, tense and person stable** unless a change is deliberate [1] [1]; **check the ending**, where writers often drift into essay register [1] [1]. Alternatively, keep cohesive devices suited to the mode, such as spoken signposting.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Copying source sentences instead of transforming them.
- A speech written as a page of prose with no spoken features.
- Reproducing the non-fluency of a transcript in a planned speech.
- Losing the persona half-way through.
- Selecting every detail rather than those that suit the new purpose.
