These questions cover subtopic E4.8, Circle theorems II, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The whole subtopic is **Extended only**, so every question is Extended. Each is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). O is always the centre of the circle. Sketch each described diagram before you start.

For explanations, read the [Circle theorems II study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-circle-theorems-ii/); for a summary, use the [Circle theorems II revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-circle-theorems-ii-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) A circle has radius 25 cm. A chord AB is 48 cm long. Find the distance from O to AB. **[2]**

**2.** (non-calculator) A chord is 5 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 13 cm. Find the length of the chord. **[2]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Tangents from a point P touch a circle at A and B. PA = 9 cm and angle APB = 70°.

**(a)** Write down the length of PB, giving a reason. **[1]**

**(b)** Find angle PAB. **[2]**

**4.** (non-calculator) A circle has radius 10 cm. AB and CD are parallel chords with AB = 16 cm and CD = 12 cm.

**(a)** The chords are on the same side of O. Find the distance between them. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the distance between them if they are on opposite sides of O. **[1]**

**5.** (non-calculator) A circle passes through the points (0, 0), (8, 0) and (0, 6). Find the coordinates of its centre and its radius. **[3]**

**6.** (non-calculator) Tangents PA and PB are drawn from P to a circle, touching it at A and B. Angle APB = 2x°. Show that angle OAB = x°. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** (calculator) A circle has radius 9 cm. AB is a chord of length 14 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the distance from O to AB. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate angle AOB. **[2]**

**8.** (non-calculator) A circle touches all three sides of triangle ABC. AB = 9 cm, BC = 11 cm and CA = 8 cm. Find the length of the tangent from each vertex to the circle. **[3]**

**9.** (calculator) A circle has radius 6 cm. A point P is 10 cm from O. Tangents from P touch the circle at A and B.

**(a)** Find PA. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the area of quadrilateral OAPB. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate angle AOB. **[2]**

**10.** (non-calculator) A circle touches all four sides of quadrilateral ABCD. AB = 7 cm, BC = 9 cm and CD = 8 cm. Find the length of DA, explaining your method. **[3]**

**11.** (non-calculator) A horizontal pipe has a circular cross-section of radius 20 cm. It is **more than half full** of water, and the water surface is 32 cm wide. Find the greatest depth of the water. **[3]**

**12.** (non-calculator) Two circles have the same centre O, with radii 13 cm and 5 cm. A chord AB of the larger circle touches the smaller circle at T.

**(a)** Give a reason why OT is perpendicular to AB. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the length of AB, giving a reason for any length you halve or double. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The perpendicular from O bisects AB, so the half-chord is 24 cm [1]; distance = √(25² − 24²) = √49 = **7 cm** [1]

*Examiner insight:* (25 − 24)(25 + 24) = 49 is a quick difference-of-two-squares route on a non-calculator paper.

**2.** Half-chord = √(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12 cm [1]; chord = **24 cm** [1]

**3. (a)** **9 cm**; tangents from an external point are equal in length [1]

**(b)** Triangle PAB is isosceles [1]; angle PAB = (180° − 70°) ÷ 2 = **55°** [1]

**4. (a)** Distance from O to AB = √(100 − 64) = 6 cm [1]; distance from O to CD = √(100 − 36) = 8 cm [1]; same side, so distance between = 8 − 6 = **2 cm** [1]

**(b)** 6 + 8 = **14 cm** [1]

**5.** The perpendicular bisector of the chord from (0, 0) to (8, 0) is x = 4; that of the chord from (0, 0) to (0, 6) is y = 3 [1]; the perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre, so the centre is **(4, 3)** [1]; radius = √(4² + 3²) = **5** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the chord from (8, 0) to (0, 6) has midpoint (4, 3), so it is a diameter. That fits: the angle at (0, 0) is 90°, an angle in a semicircle.

**6.** PA = PB (tangents from an external point are equal in length), so angle PAB = (180 − 2x) ÷ 2 = 90 − x [1]. Angle OAP = 90° (angle between tangent and radius = 90°) [1]. Angle OAB = 90 − (90 − x) = x, as required [1]

**7. (a)** Half-chord = 7 cm [1]; distance = √(81 − 49) = √32 = **5.66 cm** (3 s.f.) [1]

**(b)** sin(angle AOM) = 7/9, so angle AOM = 51.05…° [1]; angle AOB = 2 × 51.05…° = **102.1°** [1]

**8.** Let the tangent lengths from A, B and C be a, b and c: a + b = 9, b + c = 11, c + a = 8 [1]; a + b + c = 14 [1]; **a = 3 cm, b = 6 cm, c = 5 cm** [1]

*Examiner insight:* check each pair against the side: 3 + 6 = 9, 6 + 5 = 11 and 5 + 3 = 8.

**9. (a)** PA = √(10² − 6²) = **8 cm** [1]

**(b)** OAPB is two right-angled triangles, each ½ × 6 × 8 = 24 cm² [1]; area = **48 cm²** [1]

**(c)** cos(angle AOP) = 6/10, so angle AOP = 53.13…° [1]; angle AOB = **106.3°** [1]

**10.** Each vertex is an external point, so its two tangent lengths are equal [1]; each side is made of one tangent length from each end, so AB + CD = BC + DA [1]; 7 + 8 = 9 + DA, so **DA = 6 cm** [1]

**11.** Half the surface width = 16 cm [1]; the distance from O to the surface = √(20² − 16²) = 12 cm [1]; the pipe is more than half full, so O is below the surface and the depth = 20 + 12 = **32 cm** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the subtraction 20 − 12 = 8 cm is the depth for a pipe that is less than half full; read the wording before choosing.

**12. (a)** OT is a radius of the smaller circle and AB is a tangent to it at T; the angle between tangent and radius = 90° [1]

**(b)** OT is perpendicular to the chord AB of the larger circle, so T is the midpoint of AB (the perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre) [1]; AT = √(13² − 5²) = 12 cm [1]; AB = **24 cm** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the whole chord as a side of the right-angled triangle.
- Measuring the distance to a chord along a line that is not perpendicular.
- Taking OP as the tangent length.
- Choosing the wrong case for parallel chords or for a partly filled pipe.
- Rounding the half-angle before doubling on a calculator paper.
- Giving a short reason such as "tangent property" instead of the syllabus wording.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does the perpendicular from the centre cut a chord in half?**
The two radii to the ends of the chord make an isosceles triangle, whose line of symmetry is perpendicular to the chord and meets it at its midpoint. That line is the perpendicular bisector of the chord, and it passes through the centre.

**When do I add and when do I subtract distances for two chords?**
Add when the chords are on opposite sides of the centre and subtract when they are on the same side. Draw both cases if the question does not say which.
