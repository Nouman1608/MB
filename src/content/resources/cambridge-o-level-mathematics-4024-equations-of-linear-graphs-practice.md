## Section A

**1.** Write down the gradient and the y-intercept of the line y = 5 - 2x. **[2]**

**2.** A line has gradient 2 and crosses the y-axis at (0, 3). Write down its equation. **[1]**

**3.** Find the equation of the line with gradient -3 that passes through (2, -1). **[3]**

**4.** Find the gradient and the y-intercept of the line 3x - 4y = 24. **[3]**

**5.** Find the gradient and the y-intercept of the line 4x + 5y = 20. **[3]**

**6.** Write 2y = 6x - 4 in the form y = mx + c. **[2]**

**7.** Write down the equation of:

**(a)** the horizontal line through (4, -2) **[1]**

**(b)** the vertical line through (-3, 5) **[1]**

## Section B

**8.** Find the equation of the line through (-1, 5) and (3, -3). **[4]**

**9.** A line crosses the y-axis at (0, 3) and passes through (2, 7). Find its equation. **[3]**

**10.** Write y = (3/2)x - 3 in the form ax + by = c, where a, b and c are integers. **[2]**

**11.** Which of the points (6, -3) and (8, 0) lie on the line 3x - 4y = 24? Show your working. **[2]**

**12.** A line passes through (0, -2) and (4, 6).

**(a)** Find its equation. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the coordinates of the point where it crosses the x-axis. **[2]**

**13.** A taxi company charges a fixed $3 plus $2.50 for each kilometre.

**(a)** Write down a formula for the cost C dollars of a journey of d kilometres. **[1]**

**(b)** State the meaning of the gradient and of the y-intercept in this formula. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the cost of an 8 km journey. **[1]**

**14.** The line L has equation 2x + y = 7.

**(a)** Write down the gradient of L. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the coordinates of the point where L crosses the x-axis. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the equation of the line parallel to L that passes through (0, 1). **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** y = -2x + 5 **[1]**; gradient **-2**, y-intercept **5** **[1]** **[2]**

**2.** **y = 2x + 3** **[1]**

**3.** y = -3x + c **[1]**; -1 = -6 + c **[1]**; c = 5, so **y = -3x + 5** **[1]** **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Substitute the point into y = mx + c and solve for c. Check by putting (2, -1) back into the final equation.

**4.** -4y = -3x + 24 **[1]**; y = (3/4)x - 6 **[1]**; gradient **3/4**, y-intercept **-6** **[1]** **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Divide every term by -4. The gradient is 3/4, not 3 and not -4.

**5.** 5y = -4x + 20 **[1]**; y = -(4/5)x + 4 **[1]**; gradient **-4/5**, y-intercept **4** **[1]** **[3]**

**6.** divide every term by 2 **[1]**; **y = 3x - 2** **[1]** **[2]**

**7. (a)** **y = -2** **[1]**

**(b)** **x = -3** **[1]** **[2]**

**8.** gradient = (-3 - 5) ÷ (3 - (-1)) = -8 ÷ 4 = -2 **[1]**; y = -2x + c **[1]**; 5 = 2 + c, so c = 3 **[1]**; **y = -2x + 3** **[1]** **[4]**

*Check:* at x = 3, -2(3) + 3 = -3. ✓

**9.** c = 3 **[1]**; gradient = (7 - 3) ÷ (2 - 0) = 2 **[1]**; **y = 2x + 3** **[1]** **[3]**

**10.** multiply by 2: 2y = 3x - 6 **[1]**; **3x - 2y = 6** **[1]** **[2]**

**11.** (6, -3): 3(6) - 4(-3) = 18 + 12 = 30, not 24, so it is **not** on the line **[1]**; (8, 0): 24 - 0 = 24, so it **is** on the line **[1]** **[2]**

**12. (a)** gradient = (6 - (-2)) ÷ (4 - 0) = 2 **[1]**; c = -2 **[1]**; **y = 2x - 2** **[1]**

**(b)** y = 0 gives 2x = 2 **[1]**; **(1, 0)** **[1]** **[5]**

**13. (a)** **C = 2.5d + 3** **[1]**

**(b)** gradient 2.5 is the cost per kilometre **[1]**; the intercept 3 is the fixed charge **[1]**

**(c)** 2.5 × 8 + 3 = **$23** **[1]** **[4]**

**14. (a)** y = -2x + 7, so the gradient is **-2** **[1]**

**(b)** y = 0 gives 2x = 7 **[1]**; **(3.5, 0)** **[1]**

**(c)** parallel lines have the same gradient, so y = -2x + c **[1]**; passes through (0, 1), so **y = -2x + 1** **[1]** **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping m and c in Question 1.
- Dividing only the first term in Question 4 or 6.
- Giving the gradient of 3x - 4y = 24 as 3 in Question 4.
- Losing the sign for the gradient in Question 5.
- Writing the vertical line as y = -3 in Question 7(b).
- Reversing the order of subtraction on only one part of the gradient in Question 8.
- Not checking a second point.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which form should I give my answer in?**
Use the form the question asks for. If it does not say, y = mx + c is the usual choice, with any fraction in its simplest form.

**How can I check my equation?**
Substitute the coordinates of a second point on the line. Both sides of the equation must be equal.
