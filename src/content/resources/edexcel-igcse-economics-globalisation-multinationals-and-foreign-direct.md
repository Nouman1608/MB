This guide follows the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Economics (4EC1) specification, Issue 3, and covers section 2.2.1 (globalisation, multinational corporations and foreign direct investment), parts a) to d). The specification sets a single tier of entry, so there is no Higher or Foundation content to separate. The topic sits in Paper 2: Macroeconomics and the Global Economy, which the specification describes as four compulsory questions of 20 marks in 1 hour 30 minutes.

| Spec point | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|
| 2.2.1 a) | Define globalisation: increased integration and interdependence of economies |
| 2.2.1 b) | Give reasons: fewer tariffs and quotas, cheaper transport, cheaper communication, more important MNCs |

| 2.2.1 d) | Define MNC and FDI; explain why they emerge; weigh advantages and disadvantages |

## 2.2.1 a) What globalisation is

**Globalisation is the increased integration and interdependence of economies.** Integration means economies are more closely linked through trade, investment and finance. Interdependence means each economy relies on others for goods, inputs, customers or investment, so a shock in one place can reach another.

Keep the definition to those two ideas. If a question says "what is meant by", the specification's command-word table asks for two separate parts to the definition, so name both integration and interdependence.

## 2.2.1 b) Reasons for globalisation

| Reason | How it speeds up globalisation |
|---|---|
| Fewer tariffs and quotas | Imports cost less or are less limited, so firms find exporting more profitable |
| Lower transport costs | Goods can be made far from where they are sold |
| Lower communication costs | Firms can manage, design and sell across borders cheaply |
| More important MNCs | Firms that run production in several countries tie economies together |

### Worked example: tariff and transport together

Country A sells shirts to Country B (illustrative). The factory price is 12.00 a shirt. Shipping is 1.50. Country B charges a tariff of 20% of the factory price.

- Landed cost = 12.00 + 1.50 + (0.20 × 12.00) = 12.00 + 1.50 + 2.40 = **15.90**.

Now shipping falls by 40% and the tariff is cut to 5%.

- Shipping = 1.50 × 0.60 = 0.90. Tariff = 0.05 × 12.00 = 0.60.
- New landed cost = 12.00 + 0.90 + 0.60 = **13.50**.
- Fall = 15.90 − 13.50 = 2.40, which is 2.40 ÷ 15.90 × 100 = **15.1%**.

Chain of reasoning: lower tariffs and shipping cost → lower landed price → more imports are bought → exporters sell more → trade grows as a share of output.

### Worked example: communication costs

A firm manages an overseas plant with 10 visits a year at 1,100 each. It switches to video links with a software cost of 400 a year and keeps 2 visits.

- Before: 10 × 1,100 = 11,000. After: (2 × 1,100) + 400 = 2,600.
- Saving = 8,400, which is 8,400 ÷ 11,000 × 100 = **76.4%**.

Cheaper contact makes it easier to run operations abroad, which is one reason MNCs become more common.

## 2.2.1 c) Impacts of globalisation and global companies

The specification lists six effects. For each one, say who gains, who loses, and what the result depends on.

| Effect | Chain of reasoning | Who may lose or what it depends on |
|---|---|---|
| Rising living standards | Trade and investment raise output and incomes; cheaper goods stretch incomes | Gains may go mainly to richer groups or to cities |
| Greater choice | Imports and foreign firms widen the range of goods | Local varieties may disappear |
| Lower prices | More competition and cheaper production cut prices | Local producers who cannot match them lose sales |
| Reduced communication costs | Firms and households contact others cheaply | Gains go to those with access to technology |
| Closing of traditional industries | Cheaper imports undercut old industries, which shed workers | Workers may lack the skills for new jobs |
| Environmental impact | More production and transport raise emissions and resource use | A country with weak rules may suffer more |

### Who is affected

| Group | Likely gain | Likely cost |
|---|---|---|
| Countries | Higher output, exports and investment | More exposed to shocks abroad |
| Governments | More tax revenue if output and incomes grow | Less control over firms that can move; tax may be avoided |
| Producers | Wider markets and cheaper inputs | Rivals from abroad; some firms close |
| Consumers | Lower prices and more choice | Some local goods disappear |
| Workers | New jobs, training, higher pay | Jobs lost in industries that close |
| The environment | Cleaner methods can spread | More emissions, resource use and waste |

### Worked example: lower prices and living standards

A household in Country C has a fixed monthly income of 2,300. A basket of goods costs 500. Imports push the average price of the basket down by 8%.

- New basket cost = 500 × 0.92 = 460.
- Baskets affordable: before 2,300 ÷ 500 = 4.6; after 2,300 ÷ 460 = 5.0.
- Increase = (5.0 − 4.6) ÷ 4.6 × 100 = **8.7%**.

The household buys more with the same income. That is a rise in living standards, but only for as long as the household keeps its income.

### Worked example: a traditional industry closes

A town in Country D has a labour force of 20,000 and 5% unemployment, so 1,000 are out of work. Cheaper imports close a textile mill employing 1,200. A new export firm opens and employs 700.

- Net change in jobs = 700 − 1,200 = **−500**.
- If the laid-off workers and the labour force stay the same, unemployed = 1,000 + 500 = 1,500.
- New unemployment rate = 1,500 ÷ 20,000 × 100 = **7.5%**.

Evaluation: the town loses jobs and skills in the short run, because textile workers may not be able to fill the new jobs. Over time, consumers pay less and resources can move to uses where the country is stronger. Whether the move happens quickly depends on retraining and on how many new jobs appear nearby.

## 2.2.1 d) Multinational corporations and FDI

**A multinational corporation (MNC) is a firm that produces or sells through its own branches in two or more countries.** It is usually based in a home country and operates in one or more host countries.

**Foreign direct investment (FDI) is investment by a firm from one country in business assets in another country**, for example constructing a plant or acquiring a majority stake in a company there. It differs from buying a few shares in a foreign company, which gives no control.

### Why MNCs and FDI emerge

- **Economies of scale.** Serving many markets from larger plants spreads fixed costs.
- **Access to natural resources and cheap materials.** Locate near the raw material, or where inputs are cheaper.
- **Lower transport and communication costs.** The links needed to run distant plants are cheaper.
- **Access to customers in different regions.** Producing near customers can avoid import taxes and delivery costs.

### Worked example: economies of scale

A phone assembler has fixed costs of 90 million and a variable cost of 40 a phone.

- Selling 1.5 million phones: total cost = 90m + (40 × 1.5m) = 150m. Average cost = 150m ÷ 1.5m = **100**.
- Selling 6 million phones across several regions: total cost = 90m + (40 × 6m) = 330m. Average cost = 330m ÷ 6m = **55**.
- Average cost falls by 45 ÷ 100 × 100 = **45%**.

### Worked example: choosing a location

A firm compares three sites. Costs are per unit (illustrative).

| Site | Materials | Labour | Delivery to main customers | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P | 16 | 9 | 3 | 28 |
| Q | 12 | 8 | 5 | 25 |
| R | 9 | 7 | 11 | 27 |

Site R has the cheapest materials and labour, but delivery is expensive, so Site Q has the lowest total. A good answer weighs all the costs and also mentions non-cost factors, such as stable rules, workers' skills and reliable power.

### Advantages and disadvantages of MNCs and FDI

| Advantage | Reasoning |
|---|---|
| Creating jobs | Direct jobs in the plant and indirect jobs in suppliers; wages are spent locally |
| Investing in infrastructure | Roads, ports or power built for the plant can serve others |
| Developing skills | Training raises workers' productivity and later pay |
| Developing capital | Machinery and technology are brought in or paid for |
| Contributing to taxes | Corporation tax and payroll taxes fund public services |

| Disadvantage | Reasoning |
|---|---|
| Avoiding paying taxes | Firms may shift profit to low-tax places, so the host collects less |
| Environmental damage | Pollution or resource depletion, worse where rules are weak |
| Moving profits abroad | Profit goes to owners overseas, so less income stays at home |
| Closing local firms | Local firms may lose out to a stronger rival (see closing of traditional industries) |

### Worked example: tax avoidance

An MNC's subsidiary in Host H earns 40 million profit. Host H taxes profit at 28%. The subsidiary pays a fee of 25 million to a group company in a low-tax country that taxes at 5%. The fee is a legal group charge (figures illustrative).

- Without the fee: tax in H = 0.28 × 40 = **11.2 million**.
- With the fee: taxable profit in H = 40 − 25 = 15, so tax = 0.28 × 15 = **4.2 million**.
- Tax on the fee = 0.05 × 25 = 1.25 million. Total group tax = 4.2 + 1.25 = **5.45 million**.
- Group saving = 11.2 − 5.45 = **5.75 million**. Host H loses 11.2 − 4.2 = **7.0 million**.

**Tax avoidance** uses legal rules to reduce a tax bill. Tax evasion is illegal. The specification names avoiding paying taxes as a disadvantage.

### Evaluating whether FDI helps the host

1. Count the gains: jobs, skills, capital, infrastructure and tax.
2. Count the costs: profits sent out, avoided tax, damage to the environment, the effect on local firms.
3. Say what decides the balance: the share of inputs sourced locally, the length of time the firm remains, the strength of local regulation, and whether workers move into skilled posts.
4. Reach a supported conclusion.

## Common errors

- Defining globalisation as "trade between countries" without integration and interdependence.
- Calling any big firm an MNC. It must be active in at least two countries.
- Listing "jobs" without a chain: jobs → wages → spending → local demand.
- Treating FDI and MNC as the same thing. FDI is the investment. The MNC is the firm that makes it.
- Writing that all effects are good or all bad, with no judgement.
