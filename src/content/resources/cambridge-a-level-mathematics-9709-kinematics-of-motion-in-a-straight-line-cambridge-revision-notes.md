These notes condense section **4.2 Kinematics of motion in a straight line** of Mechanics (Paper 4) in the Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics 9709 syllabus for 2026 and 2027. Use g = 10 m s⁻² for motion under gravity. For worked examples see the [4.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-kinematics-of-motion-in-a-straight-line-cambridge/); for marked questions use the [4.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-kinematics-of-motion-in-a-straight-line-cambridge-practice/).

## Scalars and vectors

| Scalar (size only) | Vector (size and direction, shown by sign in 1D) |
|---|---|
| Distance | Displacement |
| Speed | Velocity |
| | Acceleration |

- Average speed = total distance ÷ total time.
- Average velocity = total displacement ÷ total time.
- "Deceleration of 3 m s⁻²" means an acceleration of −3 m s⁻² in the direction of motion. Use one or the other, never both.

## Graphs

| | Gradient | Area |
|---|---|---|
| s–t graph | velocity | — |
| v–t graph | acceleration | displacement |

- Horizontal s–t line: at rest. Horizontal v–t line: constant velocity.
- Area below the t-axis on a v–t graph: negative displacement, but positive distance.
- A curved s–t graph means the velocity is changing.

## Calculus in time

```text
s  --differentiate-->  v  --differentiate-->  a
a  --integrate + c-->  v  --integrate + c-->  s
```

| Want | Do |
|---|---|
| At rest | Solve v = 0 |
| Maximum or minimum velocity | Solve a = 0 |
| Greatest displacement | Solve v = 0 (and check it is a maximum) |
| Distance over an interval | Split at each time v = 0; add the sizes of the displacements |

Only Paper 1 calculus: powers of t, including t^(½) and t^(−1).

## Constant acceleration

```text
v = u + at           s = ½(u + v)t
s = ut + ½at²        v² = u² + 2as
s = vt − ½at²        (derived; useful when u is unknown)
```

Five quantities s, u, v, a, t: know three, find a fourth with the formula that leaves out the fifth.

**Vertical motion**: a = −10 if up is positive. At the highest point v = 0. A particle thrown up and caught at the same level takes equal times up and down.

## Multi-stage and two-particle problems

- For stages, the final velocity of one stage is the initial velocity of the next.
- For two particles, use the same clock: one has time t, a later starter has time t − k.
- "Meet" or "catch up": equal displacements from the same origin (same direction) or displacements adding to the gap (towards each other).
- Quadratics in t often have two positive roots; interpret both (for example, overtaking and being overtaken again).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Displacement | Position relative to a fixed origin, with sign |
| Velocity | Rate of change of displacement |
| Acceleration | Rate of change of velocity |
| Deceleration | Acceleration that reduces speed |
| Instantaneous rest | v = 0 at a moment, even if a ≠ 0 |
| Uniform acceleration | Constant acceleration |
| Retardation | Another word for deceleration |

## Exam traps

- ∫₀ᵀ v dt gives displacement; if v changes sign you need distance by intervals.
- suvat with a variable acceleration is wrong.
- The constant of integration: use the condition given, such as "starts from rest at O".
- Deceleration double negative: a = −2 or "decelerates at 2", not "a = −2 deceleration".
- Units: km h⁻¹ must be converted to m s⁻¹ (divide by 3.6).
- Two particles: different start times need t and t − k.
- In vertical motion keep the sign convention fixed: a displacement below the start is negative.

## Self-test

1. A particle moves 8 m forward then 3 m back in 5 s. Find the distance, displacement and average speed.
2. A car accelerates uniformly from 4 m s⁻¹ to 16 m s⁻¹ in 6 s. Find the acceleration and distance.
3. A ball is dropped from 20 m. Find the time to reach the ground.
4. v = 12 − 3t. When is the particle at rest, and what is a?
5. s = 2t³ − 3t². Find v and a when t = 2.
6. a = 6t, and v = 1 when t = 0. Find v.
7. A v–t graph is a triangle with base 10 s and height 6 m s⁻¹. Find the displacement.
8. A train decelerates from 30 m s⁻¹ at 0.5 m s⁻². Find the stopping distance.
9. Convert 72 km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹.
10. v = 4√t. Find s when t = 9, given s = 0 when t = 0.
11. A stone is thrown up at 14 m s⁻¹. Find the greatest height.
12. P starts at O at 5 m s⁻¹ constant. Q starts from rest at O 2 s later with a = 2 m s⁻². Write their displacements at time t after P starts.

**Answers**

1. Distance **11 m**, displacement **+5 m**, average speed **2.2 m s⁻¹**.
2. a = 12/6 = **2 m s⁻²**; s = ½(4 + 16)(6) = **60 m**.
3. 20 = 5t², so **t = 2 s**.
4. **t = 4 s**; a = **−3 m s⁻²**.
5. v = 6t² − 6t = **12 m s⁻¹**; a = 12t − 6 = **18 m s⁻²**.
6. v = 3t² + c, c = 1: **v = 3t² + 1**.
7. ½ × 10 × 6 = **30 m**.
8. 0 = 900 − 2(0.5)s, so **s = 900 m**.
9. 72 ÷ 3.6 = **20 m s⁻¹**.
10. s = (8/3)t^(3/2) = (8/3)(27) = **72 m**.
11. 0 = 196 − 20s, so **s = 9.8 m**.
12. For P, s = 5t; for Q, s = (t − 2)² for t ≥ 2.
