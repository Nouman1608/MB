**These notes condense Section 7 Transport and telecommunications of Cambridge O Level Pakistan
Studies 2059 Paper 2.** The section is map-heavy: you must describe network patterns, locate border
crossings, passes, seaports and international airports, and then judge new schemes and the role of
telecommunications. Full explanations are in the
[Transport and Telecommunications study guide](/resources/o-level-pakistan-studies-transport-and-telecommunications/).

## Syllabus checklist (2059, 2026)

- [ ] 7(a) Describe regional variations in density and pattern of road, rail and air networks from maps
- [ ] 7(a) Factors helping and hindering location, maintenance and development of routes and airports
- [ ] 7(a) Recent improvements; feasibility of new developments
- [ ] 7(a) Compare road, rail and air for goods and people; evaluate motorways and airports
- [ ] 7(b) Map: cross-border roads (with passes) and railways; Keamari, Qasim, Gwadar; international airports
- [ ] 7(b) Factors affecting cross-border routes, seaports, dry ports and airports
- [ ] 7(b) Define a dry port, name one, explain why they were developed
- [ ] 7(c) Importance of radio, TV, phones, fax, email and internet for education, industry, services, trade
- [ ] 7(c) Problems of provision in some areas; evaluate the role in development

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Network density | Length of route per unit area, or number of links in an area |
| Linear pattern | Routes following a line, e.g. along a river valley |
| Motorway | A dual carriageway with controlled access and no junctions at level |
| Seaport | A coastal port handling ocean shipping |
| Dry port | Inland customs and container terminal linked to a seaport by rail or road |
| Pass | A low route through a mountain range |
| Teledensity | Telephone connections per 100 people |
| Digital divide | The gap in internet access between groups, e.g. urban and rural |

## Map list to learn

**Border roads and passes**
- China: Karakoram Highway, **Khunjerab Pass** (seasonal, closed in deep winter)
- Afghanistan: **Torkham** via the **Khyber Pass**; **Chaman** via the Khojak area on the
  Quetta–Kandahar road
- Iran: **Taftan** on the Quetta–Zahedan road
- India: **Wagah**, east of Lahore

**Border railways**
- Quetta–Taftan–Zahedan (Iran)
- Wagah–Attari (India), services suspended since 2019
- The Chaman line, through the Khojak Tunnel, ends at the Afghan border

**Seaports:** Keamari (Karachi Port), Port Qasim, Gwadar.

**International airports (examples):** Karachi, Lahore, Islamabad, Peshawar, Quetta, Multan,
Faisalabad, Sialkot, Gwadar.

**Dry ports (examples):** Lahore, Faisalabad, Sialkot, Multan, Peshawar, Quetta, Sost.

## Network pattern in three sentences

Networks are **densest and grid-like** in northern and central Punjab. They run **linearly along
the Indus** through Sindh and along valleys in the north. They are **sparse** across Balochistan,
the Thar desert and the high mountains, where air links matter most.

## Mode comparison: one line each

- **Road:** flexible, door-to-door, most freight; but accidents, congestion, overloaded trucks.
- **Rail:** cheapest for bulk over distance; but slow, ageing, loss-making.
- **Air:** fastest; vital for Gilgit, Skardu, Chitral; but costly, small loads, weather delays.

## Evaluating a motorway: model paragraph

> The M-5 Multan–Sukkur motorway (2019) cut journey times between Punjab and Sindh, lowered
> fuel use per trip and gave farmers faster access to markets. However, it cost a great deal of
> borrowed money, tolls are too high for many small transporters, and villages bypassed by the
> route may lose roadside trade. On balance it strengthens the north–south trade corridor, but its
> benefits go mostly to long-distance freight rather than local people.

The shape: benefit, benefit, cost, cost, judgement.

## Why build dry ports? Four reasons

1. Industry (textiles, sports goods, surgical instruments) is inland, far from Karachi.
2. Customs clearance close to the factory saves time and reduces theft and damage.
3. Less congestion and storage pressure at Keamari and Port Qasim.
4. Sealed containers can travel by rail, which is cheaper for bulk.

## Telecommunications: uses and problems

| Field | Use |
|---|---|
| Education | Open and online universities; TV lessons; online resources |
| Industry | Email with foreign buyers; design files; ordering parts |
| Services | Mobile banking, e-commerce, freelancing, call centres |
| Trade | Tracking shipments; online customs; price information for farmers |

**Problems:** terrain and cost in mountains and deserts; scattered low-income users; power cuts;
low literacy; disaster damage; occasional shutdowns. **Approximate scale (2024):** around 190
million mobile subscriptions (PTA).

## Exam traps

- **Naming a pass without the road, or a road without the pass.** The syllabus asks for both.
- **Calling a dry port a "port on dry land" without explaining customs clearance.**
- **Describing a network as "good in Punjab, bad in Balochistan".** Use density and pattern words
  and name places.
- **Forgetting seasonality.** The Khunjerab route is only open for part of the year.
- **Treating fax as current.** It is in the syllabus, but say it has largely been replaced by email.

## Self-test

1. Name the pass used by the road from Peshawar to Afghanistan.
2. Which seaport was built east of Karachi to take bulk cargo?
3. Name a dry port in Gilgit-Baltistan.
4. Give one advantage of rail over road for moving coal.
5. Why are airports important in northern Pakistan?
6. Give two problems of providing telecommunications in Balochistan.

**Answers:** 1. Khyber Pass (Torkham). 2. Port Qasim. 3. Sost. 4. Cheaper per tonne over long
distance, or less road damage and congestion. 5. Roads are long, steep and often blocked by
landslides or snow, so flights are the only quick link. 6. Any two of mountainous terrain, long
distances, scattered population, low revenue, power shortages, security problems.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I describe a transport network from a map?**
Say where it is dense and where it is sparse, name the pattern (grid, linear, radial), and relate
it to provinces, rivers and cities shown on the map.

**Do I need to know motorway numbers?**
Naming one or two motorways with their end cities, such as the M-2 Lahore–Islamabad, is enough to
support an evaluation.

**Which telecommunications are listed in the 2059 syllabus?**
Radio, television, phones, fax machines, email and the internet.
