Data where needed: c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹; speed of sound in air = 340 m s⁻¹. Wavelength ranges: radio ≥ 10⁻¹ m; microwave 10⁻³–10⁻¹ m; infrared 10⁻⁶–10⁻³ m; visible 4–7 × 10⁻⁷ m; ultraviolet 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁷ m; X-ray 10⁻¹¹–10⁻⁸ m; gamma ≤ 10⁻¹² m.

## Section A

**(b)** Give one example of each. **[1]**

| x / cm | 0 | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 | 30 | 35 | 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**(a)** State the wavelength. **[1]**

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength in soft tissue. **[1]**

**(b)** State the frequency in bone and calculate the wavelength there. **[2]**

**(c)** A boundary lies 4.0 cm below the probe in soft tissue. Calculate the time between sending a pulse and receiving its echo. **[2]**

**9.** A loudspeaker produces a note of 850 Hz in air.

**10.** **(a)** Identify the region of the electromagnetic spectrum for radiation of each frequency. Show your working.

**(b)** State what all three have in common when travelling in a vacuum. **[1]**

**11.** A wave on a rope has frequency 3.0 Hz, wavelength 1.2 m and amplitude 0.10 m. Each point oscillates with simple harmonic motion.

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** Transverse: oscillations perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer [1]. Longitudinal: oscillations parallel to the direction of energy transfer [1].

**(b)** Transverse: light / any EM wave / wave on a string; longitudinal: sound [1]. **[3]**

**2.** λ = c/f = 3.00 × 10⁸ / 2.4 × 10⁹ [1] = **0.13 m** (0.125 m) [1]. **[2]**

**3.** f = 15/3.0 = **5.0 Hz** [1]. λ = 0.40 m, so v = fλ = 5.0 × 0.40 [1] = **2.0 m s⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* 15 crests in 3.0 s means 15 waves pass; do not subtract one unless counting starts from a crest at t = 0.

**4.** Any two: mechanical waves need a medium, EM waves do not [1]; mechanical waves can be longitudinal or transverse, EM waves are only transverse [1]; mechanical speed depends on the medium, EM waves all travel at c in vacuum; particles oscillate in mechanical waves, fields in EM waves. **[2]**

**5.** Sound is a mechanical wave transmitted by particles of a medium pushing on neighbouring particles [1]. A vacuum has no particles, so there is nothing to oscillate and pass on the energy [1]. **[2]**

**6.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**7.** **(a)** λ = **20 cm** (0.20 m) [1].

**(b)** A = **3.0 mm** [1].

**(c)** f = 1/0.050 = **20 Hz** [1]; v = 20 × 0.20 = **4.0 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(d)** **Upwards** (positive y): the crest at x = 5 cm is moving towards it [1].

**(e)** Half a period later the displacement has reversed: **−3.0 mm** [1]. **[6]**

**8.** **(a)** λ = 1540 / 2.0 × 10⁶ = **7.7 × 10⁻⁴ m** [1].

**(b)** f = **2.0 MHz** (unchanged) [1]; λ = 4080 / 2.0 × 10⁶ = **2.0 × 10⁻³ m** [1].

**(c)** Total path = 2 × 0.040 = 0.080 m [1]; t = 0.080/1540 = **5.2 × 10⁻⁵ s** [1]. **[5]**

**9.** **(a)** Particles oscillate back and forth about fixed equilibrium positions [1], parallel to the direction in which the sound travels, with no net movement [1].

**(b)** λ = 340/850 = 0.40 m [1]; compression to next rarefaction = λ/2 = **0.20 m** [1].

**(c)** **Zero** (it is a point of zero displacement; neighbours are displaced towards it) [1]. **[5]**

**10.** **(a)** (i) λ = 3.0 m: **radio** [1]. (ii) λ = 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ m: **infrared** [1]. (iii) λ = 5.0 × 10⁻⁸ m: **ultraviolet** [1].

**(b)** They all travel at the same speed, c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ [1]. **[4]**

**11.** **(a)** v = 3.0 × 1.2 = **3.6 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** v_max = 2πfA = 2π × 3.0 × 0.10 [1] = **1.9 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(c)** Wave speed is the speed of the pattern along the rope; the point's speed is its speed of oscillation at right angles to the rope, which varies through each cycle [1]. **[4]**

**12.** **(a)** d = vt = 340 × 4.5 [1] = **1.5 × 10³ m** (1530 m) [1].

**(b)** Light travels about a million times faster, so it arrives almost instantly (about 5 µs) [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading λ from a time graph or T from a position graph.
- Forgetting the factor 2 in echo calculations.
- Using MHz or GHz without converting to Hz.
- Saying frequency changes in a new medium.
- Mixing up particle speed and wave speed.

## Frequently asked questions

**How are these questions organised?**
Section A has short questions and one multiple-choice item; Section B has data-based and structured questions. All apply to SL and HL.

**Is the maximum particle speed part of C.2?**
The formula comes from simple harmonic motion in C.1, but distinguishing particle motion from wave motion is part of describing waves in C.2.
