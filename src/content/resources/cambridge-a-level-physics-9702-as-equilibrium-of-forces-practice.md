## Section A

**1.** An object is acted on by a couple and by no other forces. Which statement is correct?

A: It is in equilibrium because the resultant force is zero.

B: It is not in equilibrium because there is a resultant torque.

C: It is not in equilibrium because there is a resultant force.

D: It is in equilibrium because the resultant torque is zero. **[1]**

**2.** Three forces of 5.0 N, 12 N and 13 N act on an object in equilibrium. What is the angle between the 5.0 N and 12 N forces?

A: 45°

B: 60°

C: 90°

D: 180° **[1]**

**3.** State the principle of moments. **[2]**

**4.** State the two conditions for a system to be in equilibrium. **[2]**

**5.** A 450 N person sits 1.2 m from the pivot of a see-saw pivoted at its centre. Calculate where a 360 N person must sit to balance it. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A diving board is 3.0 m long and has a weight of 250 N acting at its midpoint. It is held horizontal by a bolt at its left end and a support 0.80 m from the left end. A diver of weight 650 N stands at the right-hand end.

**(a)** By taking moments about the bolt, calculate the upward force from the support. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the magnitude and direction of the force from the bolt. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why taking moments about the bolt was a sensible choice. **[1]**

**7.** A uniform ladder 6.0 m long and of weight 250 N rests against a smooth vertical wall at 65° to the horizontal ground.

**(a)** List the forces acting on the ladder, with their directions. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the force exerted by the wall. **[3]**

**(c)** State the friction force at the foot of the ladder. **[1]**

**8.** A ball of weight 20 N hangs on a string against a smooth vertical wall. The string makes an angle of 30° with the wall.

**(a)** Sketch the vector triangle for the three forces on the ball. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the tension in the string and the force exerted by the wall. **[2]**

**9.** A picture of weight 30 N hangs from a single string passing over a smooth nail. Each half of the string makes 40° with the horizontal.

**(a)** Calculate the tension in the string. **[2]**

**(b)** The string is replaced by a longer one, so each half makes 60° with the horizontal. State and explain the effect on the tension. **[2]**

**10.** A uniform shelf of length 0.90 m and weight 40 N is hinged to a wall at one end and held horizontal by a light chain attached to its other end. The chain makes 40° with the shelf. A 60 N box sits on the shelf 0.60 m from the wall.

**(a)** Calculate the tension in the chain. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the horizontal component of the force exerted by the hinge on the shelf. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why a closed vector triangle of three forces shows that the forces are in equilibrium, and why this alone does not guarantee that the object will not rotate. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** 5.0² + 12² = 13², so the triangle is right-angled between the 5.0 N and 12 N sides; the forces themselves are at **90°** to each other: **C** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** For a body in equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about any point equals the sum of the anticlockwise moments [1] about the same point [1]. **[2]**

**4.** No resultant force [1]; no resultant torque (moment) [1]. **[2]**

**5.** 450 × 1.2 = 360 × d [1]; d = **1.5 m** from the pivot, on the other side [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Clockwise about the bolt: 250 × 1.5 + 650 × 3.0 = 375 + 1950 = 2325 N m [1]. Anticlockwise: S × 0.80 [1]. S = 2325 ÷ 0.80 = **2900 N** (2906 N) [1].

**(b)** Upward forces must equal downward forces: S = 250 + 650 + F_bolt, so F_bolt = 2906 − 900 = **2000 N** (2006 N) [1], acting **downwards** [1].

**(c)** The bolt force is unknown; its moment about the bolt is zero, so it does not appear in the equation [1]. **[6]**

**7. (a)** Weight 250 N vertically down at the midpoint; wall force horizontal, away from the wall [1]; normal force from the ground vertically up and friction at the foot horizontal, towards the wall [1].

**(b)** Moments about the foot: 250 × 3.0 cos 65° = R × 6.0 sin 65° [1]; 317 = R × 5.44 [1]; R = **58 N** [1].

**(c)** **58 N**, towards the wall (it balances the wall force) [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** Weight 20 N drawn vertically down; wall force horizontal; tension closing the triangle, at 30° to the vertical [1]; arrows following each other round the triangle [1].

**(b)** T = 20 ÷ cos 30° = **23.1 N** [1]; wall force = 20 tan 30° = **11.5 N** [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** 2T sin 40° = 30 [1]; T = 30 ÷ (2 × 0.643) = **23.3 N** [1].

**(b)** The tension decreases (to 30 ÷ (2 sin 60°) = 17.3 N) [1], because the strings are steeper, so a larger fraction of each tension acts vertically to support the weight [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** Moments about the hinge: T sin 40° × 0.90 = 40 × 0.45 + 60 × 0.60 [1] = 18 + 36 = 54 N m [1]; T = 54 ÷ (0.90 × 0.643) = **93 N** [1].

**(b)** The chain's horizontal component is T cos 40° = 93.4 × 0.766 = 71.5 N, towards the wall [1]; the hinge must provide **72 N** horizontally, away from the wall [1]. **[5]**

**11.** Drawn tip to tail, the three forces return to the starting point, so their vector sum (resultant force) is zero [1]. This shows only that there is no linear acceleration [1]. Equilibrium also needs zero resultant torque, which requires the lines of action to pass through one point; a closed triangle alone does not check this [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating the principle of moments without "in equilibrium" or "about the same point".
- Forgetting the weight of the beam, shelf or ladder.
- Using the full length of a slanting ladder or chain as the perpendicular distance.
- Assuming a support force acts upwards when the moments show it must act downwards, as for the bolt on the diving board.
- Arrows in a vector triangle that do not follow each other round.
