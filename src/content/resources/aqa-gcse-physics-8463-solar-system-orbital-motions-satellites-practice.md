These questions cover section **4.8.1 Solar system; stability of orbital motions; satellites** of the AQA GCSE
Physics (8463) specification, for first teaching 2016, examined on Paper 2. Parts marked **Higher tier only** test
the two Higher tier statements on circular orbits. A calculator is allowed. Satellite data are invented.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Put these in order of size, smallest first: galaxy, moon, solar system, planet, Sun. **[1]**

**2.** Name the galaxy that contains our solar system. **[1]**

**3.** State what is meant by a natural satellite, and give an example. **[2]**

**4.** State where elements heavier than iron are produced. **[1]**

**5.** Give one use of artificial satellites. **[1]**

**6.** Name the force that keeps the planets in orbit around the Sun. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** The Sun formed from a nebula.

**(a)** Describe what a nebula is. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how the nebula became a star that gives out light. **[4]**

**8.** The Sun is a main sequence star.

**(a)** Explain why the Sun stays the same size for a long time. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest what would happen to the size of a star if the energy released by fusion in its core decreased.
Give a reason. **[2]**

**9.** Compare the life cycle of a star about the size of the Sun with that of a star much more massive than the Sun,
from the end of the main sequence stage. **[4]**

**10.** Explain how fusion in stars, and supernovae, account for the elements found on Earth today. **[4]**

**11.** Describe two similarities and two distinctions between the Moon and an artificial satellite such as a weather
satellite. **[4]**

**12.** A satellite moves in a circular orbit of radius 7.0 × 10⁶ m. One orbit takes 5800 s.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the satellite. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[3]**
**(b)** (Higher tier only) Explain why the satellite is accelerating even though its speed is constant. **[3]**
**(c)** (Higher tier only) A second satellite orbits at a radius of 4.2 × 10⁷ m. State and explain how its speed
compares with the first satellite. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Moon, planet, Sun, solar system, galaxy (all in order) [1]

**2.** The Milky Way [1]

**3.** A natural object that orbits a planet (a moon) [1]; for example, the Moon orbiting the Earth [1]

**4.** In a supernova [1]

**5.** Any one from: communications; weather monitoring; observing the Earth; observing space [1]

**6.** Gravity (the Sun's gravitational attraction) [1]

**7. (a)** A cloud of dust and gas [1]
**(b)** Gravity pulled the dust and gas together [1]; the material became denser and hotter at the centre (forming a protostar) [1]; when it was hot and dense enough, hydrogen nuclei fused to form helium [1]; fusion released energy, so the star shone [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Starting with "the nebula exploded" contradicts the physics and loses the first mark.

**8. (a)** Gravity pulls the material inwards (gravitational collapse) [1]; energy released by fusion pushes the material outwards (expansion) [1]; the two are balanced, so the star is in equilibrium [1]
**(b)** The star would contract (get smaller) [1]; because gravitational collapse would be greater than the expansion due to fusion energy [1] **[5]**

**9.** Both expand at the end of the main sequence: the Sun-sized star becomes a red giant, the massive star a red super giant [1]; the Sun-sized star then becomes a white dwarf, while the massive star explodes as a supernova [1]; the white dwarf cools to a black dwarf [1]; the supernova leaves a neutron star or, for the most massive stars, a black hole [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Compare" needs both stars in each point; two separate lists cap the answer.

**10.** Fusion in main sequence stars turns hydrogen into helium [1]; later fusion in stars produces heavier elements up to iron [1]; elements heavier than iron are produced in a supernova [1]; the supernova explosion distributes the elements through the universe, and the Earth formed from this material [1]

**11.** Similarities, any two from: both orbit the Earth [1]; both are kept in orbit by the Earth's gravity [1]; neither produces its own light [1]
Distinctions, any two from: the Moon is natural, the weather satellite is made by people [1]; the Moon is much larger [1]; the weather satellite was launched for a purpose such as monitoring weather [1] **[4]**

**12. (a)** Distance = 2 π r = 2 × π × 7.0 × 10⁶ = 4.40 × 10⁷ m [1]; v = 4.40 × 10⁷ / 5800 [1]; = 7583 m/s = **7600 m/s** (2 s.f.) [1]
**(b)** The direction of motion changes continuously [1]; so the velocity (a vector) changes [1]; a changing velocity is an acceleration, caused by gravity acting towards the centre of the orbit [1]
**(c)** It moves more slowly [1]; it is in a larger orbit, where gravity is weaker, so a lower speed keeps it in a stable orbit [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), using the radius rather than the circumference as the distance gives about 1200 m/s and only one mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Placing the solar system above the galaxy in size order.
- Leaving gravity out of the formation chain, or saying the nebula exploded.
- Equilibrium answers that do not use "balance" or name only one effect.
- Comparisons written as two separate descriptions.
- Saying supernovae make every element.
- Using r instead of 2πr for the distance in one orbit.
- (Higher tier only) Writing "the speed changes" for a circular orbit.
