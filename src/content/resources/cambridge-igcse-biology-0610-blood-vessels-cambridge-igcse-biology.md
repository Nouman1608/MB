This guide covers sub-topic **9.3 Blood vessels** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028, part of topic 9, Transport in animals. Outcomes 1 to 3 are Core. Outcomes 4 to 6, which link structure to pressure and function and add the vessels of the liver, are **Extended only**.

The [transport in animals study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-transport-in-animals/) gives the comparison table and vessel list. This page adds identification from cross-sections, a measurement example, route-tracing through named vessels and model explanations. Afterwards, use the [blood vessels revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-blood-vessels-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/) and the [blood vessels practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-blood-vessels-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/).

## What sub-topic 9.3 asks you to do

| Outcome | What you must be able to do | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| 9.3.1 | Describe arteries, veins and capillaries, limited to relative wall thickness, lumen diameter and valves in veins | Core |
| 9.3.2 | State the functions of capillaries | Core |
| 9.3.3 | Identify the main vessels to and from the heart, lungs and kidney | Core |
| 9.3.4 | Explain how artery and vein structure relates to the pressure of the blood they carry | Extended only |
| 9.3.5 | Explain how capillary structure relates to their functions | Extended only |
| 9.3.6 | Identify the hepatic artery, hepatic veins and hepatic portal vein | Extended only |

The word "limited" matters. For the Core description you need three features only: **wall thickness**, **lumen diameter** and **valves**. Extra detail is not required.

## 9.3.1 Describing the three vessels

| Feature | Artery | Vein | Capillary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relative wall thickness | thick | thin (compared with an artery) | very thin: one cell thick |
| Lumen diameter | narrow (compared with the vein) | wide | very narrow, just wide enough for red blood cells |
| Valves | none | present | none |

The **lumen** is the space inside the vessel through which blood flows. When you describe a vessel, always compare: "the artery has a **thicker** wall **than** the vein" earns a mark that "the artery has a thick wall" may not.

### Identifying vessels in a cross-section

Photomicrographs and diagrams of cut vessels appear often. Use these clues:

- **Artery:** thick wall relative to the lumen; small, round lumen; usually keeps a round shape when cut.
- **Vein:** thin wall relative to a large lumen; often looks flattened or irregular, because the thin wall collapses; a valve may be visible in a lengthwise section.
- **Capillary:** tiny; wall a single layer of cells; may show a single red blood cell inside.

### Worked example 1: using measurements

A student measures two vessels in a cross-section diagram. (Example data written for this guide.)

| Vessel | Wall thickness / mm | Lumen diameter / mm |
|---|---|---|
| X | 1.2 | 3.0 |
| Y | 0.4 | 6.0 |

**Identify X and Y, using the data.**

- X has a wall three times as thick as Y's (1.2 ÷ 0.4 = 3).
- X has a lumen half the diameter of Y's (3.0 ÷ 6.0 = 0.5).
- Thick wall and narrow lumen: **X is an artery**. Thin wall and wide lumen: **Y is a vein**.

Use both features in your answer and quote the numbers. One feature alone is weaker evidence.

## 9.3.2 Functions of capillaries

Capillaries carry blood through the tissues, close to every cell. Their function is the **exchange of substances** between blood and cells:

| Into the tissue cells | Out of the cells into the blood |
|---|---|
| oxygen | carbon dioxide |
| glucose and other nutrients | urea and other wastes |

Capillaries also link the smallest arteries to the smallest veins, so they are where blood passes from the arterial side to the venous side of the circulation.

## 9.3.3 and 9.3.6 Naming the main vessels

| Organ | Blood in | Blood out | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heart | vena cava (to right atrium); pulmonary vein (to left atrium) | pulmonary artery (from right ventricle); aorta (from left ventricle) | Core |
| Lungs | pulmonary artery | pulmonary vein | Core |
| Kidney | renal artery | renal vein | Core |
| Liver | hepatic artery; hepatic portal vein (from the gut) | hepatic veins | Extended only |

Notice that the pulmonary artery and pulmonary vein appear twice: once for the heart and once for the lungs. They link the two organs.

The **hepatic portal vein** is the odd one out: it is a vein that carries blood from one organ (the gut) to another organ (the liver), not back to the heart. Its blood is rich in nutrients absorbed in the small intestine.

### Worked example 2: tracing a glucose molecule (Extended)

Glucose is absorbed into a capillary in the small intestine. Name, in order, the vessels and heart chambers it passes through to reach a muscle in the arm.

1. Capillary in the small intestine → **hepatic portal vein** → liver.
2. Liver → **hepatic vein** → **vena cava**.
3. Right atrium → right ventricle → **pulmonary artery** → lungs.
4. Lungs → **pulmonary vein** → left atrium → left ventricle.
5. **Aorta** → artery to the arm → capillary in the arm muscle.

A Core version of this question starts at the vena cava and asks only for the heart and lung vessels.

## 9.3.4 Structure and pressure (Extended only)

The pressure of blood falls as it travels from the heart: **highest in arteries**, lower in capillaries, **lowest in veins**. Each vessel's structure suits the pressure inside it.

| Vessel | Pressure | Structural feature | How it relates to pressure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Artery | high, and surges with each heartbeat | thick wall with muscle and elastic tissue | withstands high pressure without bursting; stretches as each surge arrives and recoils between beats, keeping blood flowing |
| Artery | high | narrow lumen | helps maintain high pressure |
| Vein | low | thin wall | no need to withstand high pressure |
| Vein | low | wide lumen | little resistance to flow, so slow, low-pressure blood still returns to the heart |
| Vein | low | valves | prevent backflow, which could happen at such low pressure |

### Model answer: arteries

**Question:** Explain how the structure of an artery is related to the pressure of the blood it carries. [2]

**Answer:** Blood in arteries is at high pressure because it has just left the heart (1). The artery has a thick, muscular, elastic wall, which withstands this pressure without bursting and stretches and recoils to keep blood moving (1).

## 9.3.5 Capillary structure and function (Extended only)

| Structural feature | How it helps exchange |
|---|---|
| Wall one cell thick | short diffusion distance between blood and cells |
| Narrow lumen | red blood cells pass close to the wall, so oxygen has a short distance to travel |
| Very many capillaries, highly branched | large total surface area; every cell is close to a capillary |

The best answers name the feature, the effect (short distance or large surface area) and the process (diffusion of oxygen, glucose and carbon dioxide).

## Common mistakes

- Describing vessels without comparing: "thick wall" instead of "thicker wall than a vein".
- Saying arteries have valves. In this syllabus only veins have valves.
- Saying capillary walls are "thin" instead of "one cell thick".
- Writing that veins have a thin wall "so blood can diffuse out". Exchange happens in capillaries, not veins.
- Saying the hepatic portal vein carries blood to the heart. It carries blood from the gut to the liver.
- Mixing up the renal artery and renal vein: the artery takes blood **to** the kidney.
- Saying arteries always carry oxygenated blood; the pulmonary artery does not.

## Exam technique for 9.3 command words

- **Describe** structure: use the three features named in the syllabus and comparative words.
- **State** functions: "exchange of substances between blood and tissue cells", then name two substances.
- **Identify**: give the full vessel name, such as "renal vein", not "vein".
- **Explain**: link each feature to pressure (arteries and veins) or to diffusion (capillaries), using "so".
