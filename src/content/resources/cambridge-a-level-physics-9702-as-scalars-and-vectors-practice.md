## Section A

**1.** Which list contains only vector quantities?

A: acceleration, momentum, weight

B: displacement, energy, force

C: velocity, pressure, acceleration

D: speed, momentum, electric field strength **[1]**

**2.** Two forces of 6.0 N and 8.0 N act at a point. Which value could **not** be the magnitude of their resultant?

A: 2.0 N

B: 7.0 N

C: 14 N

D: 15 N **[1]**

**3.** Explain why pressure is a scalar quantity even though it produces forces on surfaces. **[2]**

**4.** A ball travelling at 7.5 m s⁻¹ hits a wall at right angles and rebounds along the same line at 5.0 m s⁻¹. Calculate the magnitude of the change in velocity, stating the direction you took as positive. **[2]**

**5.** A sledge is pulled by a rope with a tension of 120 N at 25° above the horizontal. Calculate the horizontal and vertical components of the tension. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A boat heads straight across a river at 3.0 m s⁻¹ relative to the water. The river flows at 1.6 m s⁻¹ and is 45 m wide.

**(a)** Calculate the magnitude and direction of the boat's resultant velocity. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the time taken to cross the river. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate how far downstream the boat lands. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why the current does not change the crossing time. **[1]**

**7.** Three coplanar forces act on a point: 10 N along the positive x-direction, 6.0 N along the positive y-direction, and 8.0 N at 210° measured anticlockwise from the positive x-direction.

**(a)** Calculate the x- and y-components of the 8.0 N force. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the magnitude of the resultant force. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the angle the resultant makes with the positive x-direction. **[1]**

**8.** A car moving east at 12 m s⁻¹ turns a corner and moves south at 9.0 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Draw, or describe in words, the vector diagram used to find the change in velocity. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the magnitude and direction of the change in velocity. **[3]**

**9.** A 3.0 kg box rests on a ramp inclined at 20° to the horizontal.

**(a)** Calculate the weight of the box. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the components of the weight parallel to and perpendicular to the ramp. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the angle between the weight and the perpendicular to the ramp equals 20°. **[1]**

**10.** Two forces of 6.0 N and 10.0 N act on an object. The angle between their directions is 70°.

**(a)** Use the cosine rule to calculate the magnitude of the resultant. **[2]**

**(b)** Use components to find the angle between the resultant and the 10.0 N force. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why calculating the result by two methods is useful in an exam. **[1]**

**11.** A football is kicked with a velocity of 22 m s⁻¹ at 28° above the horizontal.

**(a)** Calculate the horizontal and vertical components of the velocity. **[2]**

**(b)** A student writes the horizontal component as 22 sin 28°. Explain the error. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **A** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** The resultant lies between 8.0 − 6.0 = 2.0 N and 8.0 + 6.0 = 14 N, so **D** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** At a point in a fluid, pressure acts equally in all directions, so it has no single direction [1]; it has magnitude only, and the force it produces on a surface depends on the orientation of that surface [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Taking the initial direction as positive: Δv = −5.0 − (+7.5) [1] = −12.5 m s⁻¹, magnitude **12.5 m s⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Horizontal = 120 cos 25° = **109 N** [1]; vertical = 120 sin 25° = **51 N** [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** v = √(3.0² + 1.6²) [1] = **3.4 m s⁻¹** [1]; at tan⁻¹(1.6/3.0) = **28°** downstream from the line straight across [1].

**(b)** t = 45 ÷ 3.0 = **15 s** [1].

**(c)** 1.6 × 15 = **24 m** [1].

**(d)** The current is perpendicular to the boat's velocity across the river, so it has no component across the river; only the 3.0 m s⁻¹ component determines the crossing time [1]. **[6]**

**7. (a)** x = 8.0 cos 210° = **−6.93 N**; y = 8.0 sin 210° = **−4.0 N** [2].

**(b)** Rx = 10 − 6.93 = 3.07 N; Ry = 6.0 − 4.0 = 2.0 N [1]; R = √(3.07² + 2.0²) = **3.7 N** [1].

**(c)** tan⁻¹(2.0 ÷ 3.07) = **33°** above the positive x-direction [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** Δv = v − u: draw v (9.0 m s⁻¹ south) and then −u (12 m s⁻¹ west) tip to tail [1]; Δv joins the tail of v to the tip of −u [1].

**(b)** |Δv| = √(9.0² + 12²) [1] = **15 m s⁻¹** [1]; direction tan⁻¹(9.0/12) = **37° south of west** [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** W = 3.0 × 9.81 = **29.4 N** [1].

**(b)** Parallel = 29.4 sin 20° = **10.1 N**; perpendicular = 29.4 cos 20° = **27.7 N** [2].

**(c)** The weight is perpendicular to the horizontal and the normal is perpendicular to the ramp; turning both lines by the same angle as the ramp leaves the angle between them equal to the ramp's angle, 20° [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** R² = 6.0² + 10.0² + 2(6.0)(10.0) cos 70° = 36 + 100 + 41.0 = 177 [1]; R = **13.3 N** [1].

**(b)** x = 10.0 + 6.0 cos 70° = 12.05 N; y = 6.0 sin 70° = 5.64 N [1]; angle = tan⁻¹(5.64 ÷ 12.05) = **25°** [1].

**(c)** Agreement between the two methods checks the answer and catches an error such as using the wrong angle [1]. *(Check: √(12.05² + 5.64²) = 13.3 N.)* **[5]**

**11. (a)** Horizontal = 22 cos 28° = **19.4 m s⁻¹** [1]; vertical = 22 sin 28° = **10.3 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** The angle is measured from the horizontal, so the horizontal component is adjacent to it and needs cosine, not sine [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding or subtracting magnitudes of vectors that are not parallel.
- Using the wrong angle in the cosine rule.
- Giving a magnitude without a direction, or a direction without saying what it is measured from.
- Swapping sine and cosine when the angle is measured from the vertical or along a slope.
- Treating a change in velocity as a change in speed.
