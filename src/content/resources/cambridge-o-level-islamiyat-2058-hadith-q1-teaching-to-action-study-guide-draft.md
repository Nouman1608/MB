# From teaching to belief to action

**Paper 2 Question 1 is compulsory and worth 8 marks.** Four passages are set from the twenty Hadiths in Appendix 2 and you choose any two. The syllabus says the Hadiths are all recorded by al-Bukhari or by Muslim in their Sahih collections, many by both. It sets the passages under two headings, individual conduct and life in the community, and asks candidates to study what each passage teaches Muslims about **what to believe** and **how to act**. In the exam each passage is printed in Arabic and English, and you should know the original as well as the translation.

Related pages: [Major Teachings in the Hadiths study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-islamiyat-major-teachings-in-the-hadiths/) and [revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-islamiyat-major-teachings-in-the-hadiths-revision-notes/).

## The three-link chain

Strong answers join three links:

1. **Teaching:** what the Hadith says, in one sentence using a phrase from it.
2. **Belief behind it:** what attitude or belief the Hadith assumes (for example, that faith is shown in conduct).
3. **Action:** what a Muslim does today because of it, and why this follows from the Hadith.

The syllabus's own wording for AO1 describes knowledge quoted from Qur'an verses and Hadiths, and AO2 asks for understanding and reasoning. A chain with all three links meets both.

## Worked example 1: Appendix 2, Hadith 2

*The Hadith says that none of you believes until he wants for his brother what he wants for himself.*

- **Teaching:** complete belief includes wanting for others what one wants for oneself.
- **Belief behind it:** faith is not private feeling only; it shapes how one treats other people.
- **Action:** a Muslim shares notes with a classmate, congratulates a rival honestly, or helps a neighbour in need without expecting return, because wanting good for another is part of belief.

## Worked example 2: Appendix 2, Hadith 5

*The Hadith lists acts of charity that every joint performs each day: acting justly between two people, helping a man with his mount or belongings, a good word, a step to prayer, and removing something harmful from the road.*

- **Teaching:** charity (sadaqa) is wider than money.
- **Belief behind it:** every day brings opportunities for good, and small acts count.
- **Action:** clearing litter from a path, speaking kindly, helping someone carry a bag, being fair in a dispute.

## Worked example 3: Appendix 2, Hadith 16

*The Hadith compares believers to a single man: if his eye is affected he is affected, and if his head is affected all of him is.*

- **Teaching:** the believers form one body and share each other's pain.
- **Belief behind it:** the community of Muslims is united by faith.
- **Action:** supporting people affected by hardship, whether a sick neighbour or people far away, because their pain is shared.

## Two common errors

- **Summary only.** Retelling the Hadith without saying what it teaches.
- **A vague action.** "Be good" earns little; a concrete action tied to the Hadith's own words earns credit.

## What a teacher must confirm

- The three worked examples, and in particular that the actions listed are the type credited at part (b).
- Which of the two headings (individual conduct, life in the community) each Hadith belongs to, which this draft does not state because the syllabus appendix text does not mark the split.
