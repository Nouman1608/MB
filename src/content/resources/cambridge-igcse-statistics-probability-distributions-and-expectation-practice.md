## Questions

**1.** A spinner gives a score X. The distribution is shown below.

| x | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P(X = x) | 0.35 | 0.30 | 0.25 | 0.10 |

**(a)** Show that this is a valid probability distribution. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate E(X). **[2]**
**(c)** Find P(X > 4). **[1]**

**2.** A bag holds 2 gold and 4 silver beads. Two beads are taken at random without replacement. Let X be the number of gold beads taken.

**(a)** Find the probability distribution of X and present it in a table. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the expectation of X. **[2]**

**3.** A fair spinner has three equal sectors numbered 1, 2 and 3. It is spun twice. Let X be the difference between the two scores, found by subtracting the smaller from the larger (so X is 0 if the scores are equal).

**(a)** Complete the probability distribution of X, using a table of outcomes. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate E(X). **[2]**
**(c)** Find P(X ≥ 1). **[1]**

**4.** A factory makes bolts. Each bolt is faulty with probability 0.03, independently of the others. A batch contains 600 bolts.

**(a)** Calculate the expected number of faulty bolts in a batch. **[2]**
**(b)** Each faulty bolt costs £4.50 to replace. Calculate the expected replacement cost for a batch. **[1]**
**(c)** In one batch there are 23 faulty bolts. Does this mean the probability of 0.03 is wrong? Explain. **[1]**

**5.** At a fair you pay £3 to roll a fair six-sided die. If you roll a 6 you receive £10. If you roll a 4 or a 5 you receive £3. Otherwise you receive nothing. Let X be the amount you receive.

**(a)** Write down the probability distribution of X. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate E(X). **[2]**
**(c)** Find the expected gain per game, and state whether the game is fair. Find the expected total gain after 120 games. **[2]**

**6.** A variable X takes the values 1, 2, 3 and 4. P(X = 1) = 0.2 and P(X = 4) = 0.1. Let a = P(X = 2) and b = P(X = 3). It is given that E(X) = 2.5.

**(a)** Find the values of a and b. **[4]**
**(b)** Find P(X ≥ 3). **[1]**
**(c)** X only takes whole-number values. Explain why E(X) = 2.5 is still possible. **[1]**

**7.** The random variable X takes the values 0, 1, 2 and 3, and P(X = x) = k(x² + 1).

**(a)** Show that k = 1/18. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate E(X). **[2]**
**(c)** Find P(X < 2). **[2]**

**8.** An insurance company sells a policy for £40. In a year, the probability of a claim of £100 is 0.07 and the probability of a claim of £500 is 0.03. Otherwise there is no claim. Let X be the amount the company pays out on one policy.

**(a)** Find the probability that there is no claim. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the expected payout on one policy. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the company's expected profit on one policy. **[1]**
**(d)** The company sells 5000 policies. Calculate its expected total profit. **[1]**
**(e)** What premium would make the expected profit on each policy zero? **[1]**

**9.** Ali and Bea each take one shot at a target. Ali hits with probability 0.7 and Bea hits with probability 0.5. Their shots are independent. Let X be the total number of hits.

**(a)** Find the probability distribution of X. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate E(X). **[2]**
**(c)** The pair win £20 if both hit, £5 if exactly one hits and nothing otherwise. Calculate the expected prize. **[2]**

**10.** A bag contains 3 red, 2 blue and 1 green counter. You pay £4 to play. You take two counters at random without replacement. If both counters are the same colour you receive £9. If exactly one counter is green you receive £3. Otherwise you receive nothing.

**(a)** Show that 4/15 is the probability of taking two counters of one colour. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the probability that exactly one counter is green. **[2]**
**(c)** Complete the probability distribution of the amount received. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the expected amount received. **[2]**
**(e)** Find the expected gain per game. Find the expected total gain for a player who plays 150 times. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 0.35 + 0.30 + 0.25 + 0.10 = 1, and every probability is between 0 and 1, so the distribution is valid. [1]
**(b)** E(X) = 2 × 0.35 + 4 × 0.30 + 6 × 0.25 + 8 × 0.10 = 0.7 + 1.2 + 1.5 + 0.8 [1]
**E(X) = 4.2** [1]
**(c)** P(X > 4) = 0.25 + 0.10 = **0.35** [1]
*Examiner insight:* For "show that", write out the addition. Saying the total is 1 without it gives the reader nothing to check.

**2. (a)** P(X = 0) = 4/6 × 3/5 = 12/30 = 2/5 [1]
P(X = 2) = 2/6 × 1/5 = 2/30 = 1/15 [1]
P(X = 1) = 1 − 2/5 − 1/15 = 8/15 (or two routes: 2/6 × 4/5 + 4/6 × 2/5 = 16/30) [1]

Distribution: 6/15, 8/15, 1/15 for x = 0, 1, 2.

**(b)** E(X) = 1 × 8/15 + 2 × 1/15 = 10/15 [1]
**E(X) = 2/3** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The two routes for X = 1 are easy to miss. If you find it by subtraction, check it against the two-route sum.

**3. (a)** The nine equally likely outcomes give these differences:

| First \ Second | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| 2 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |

Counts: 3 zeros, 4 ones, 2 twos [1]

Distribution: 3/9, 4/9, 2/9 for x = 0, 1, 2.

Total 9/9 = 1 [1]
Correct outcomes grid [1]
**(b)** E(X) = (0 × 3 + 1 × 4 + 2 × 2)/9 = 8/9 [1]
**E(X) = 8/9 (about 0.889)** [1]
**(c)** P(X ≥ 1) = 4/9 + 2/9 = **2/3** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A full grid of all nine outcomes lets you count rather than guess.

**4. (a)** Expected number = n × p = 600 × 0.03 [1]
**= 18 faulty bolts** [1]
**(b)** 18 × £4.50 = **£81** [1]
**(c)** No. Expectation is an average over many batches. One batch varies by chance, so 23 does not show 0.03 is wrong. [1]
*Examiner insight:* A comment needs a reason linked to the context. A bare "no" is unlikely to earn credit.

**5. (a)** P(10) = 1/6, P(3) = 2/6, P(0) = 3/6 [1]


Table with total 1 [1]
**(b)** E(X) = 10 × 1/6 + 3 × 2/6 = 10/6 + 6/6 [1]
**E(X) = 16/6 = 8/3, about £2.67** [1]
**(c)** Expected gain = 8/3 − 3 = −1/3, so **−£0.33 per game (to the nearest penny), and the game is not fair** as the expected gain is not zero. [1]
Over 120 games: 120 × (−1/3) = **−£40** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Subtract the stake: £2.67 answers the payout question, not the one about gain. Using the exact fraction 1/3 for 120 games avoids a rounding slip.

**6. (a)** a + b = 1 − 0.2 − 0.1 = 0.7 [1]
E(X) = 1 × 0.2 + 2a + 3b + 4 × 0.1 = 2.5, so 2a + 3b = 1.9 [1]
Substitute a = 0.7 − b: 1.4 − 2b + 3b = 1.9, so b = 0.5 [1]
**b = 0.5 and a = 0.2** [1]
**(b)** P(X ≥ 3) = 0.5 + 0.1 = **0.6** [1]
**(c)** E(X) is a weighted average, so it need not be a value X can take. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Two unknowns need two equations, so state both. The check 0.2 + 0.4 + 1.5 + 0.4 = 2.5 catches slips.

**7. (a)** The values of x² + 1 are 1, 2, 5, 10 [1]
k(1 + 2 + 5 + 10) = 18k = 1 [1]
So **k = 1/18** [1]
**(b)** E(X) = (0 × 1 + 1 × 2 + 2 × 5 + 3 × 10)/18 = 42/18 [1]
**E(X) = 7/3** [1]
**(c)** P(X < 2) = P(0) + P(1) = 1/18 + 2/18 [1]
**= 3/18 = 1/6** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" question the answer is given, so display every step. Note that P(X = 0) = k, not zero.

**8. (a)** 1 − 0.07 − 0.03 = **0.9** [1]
**(b)** E(X) = 100 × 0.07 + 500 × 0.03 = 7 + 15 [1]
**= £22** [1]
**(c)** Expected profit = 40 − 22 = **£18** [1]
**(d)** 5000 × 18 = **£90 000** [1]
**(e)** The premium would equal the expected payout: **£22** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The no-claim outcome adds nothing to E(X) but belongs in the distribution. Label clearly which quantity is payout and which is profit.

**9. (a)** P(X = 0) = 0.3 × 0.5 = 0.15 [1]
P(X = 2) = 0.7 × 0.5 = 0.35 [1]
P(X = 1) = 0.7 × 0.5 + 0.3 × 0.5 = 0.35 + 0.15 = 0.5 [1]

Distribution: 0.15, 0.50, 0.35 for x = 0, 1, 2.

Table, with total 1 shown [1]
**(b)** E(X) = 1 × 0.5 + 2 × 0.35 = 0.5 + 0.7 [1]
**E(X) = 1.2** [1]
**(c)** Expected prize = 20 × 0.35 + 5 × 0.5 = 7 + 2.5 [1]
**= £9.50** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Multiplying along branches uses independence, so say so. Also 1.2 = 0.7 + 0.5, a quick check on part (b).

**10. (a)** P(RR) = 3/6 × 2/5 = 6/30 [1]
P(BB) = 2/6 × 1/5 = 2/30 [1]
P(same colour) = 8/30 = **4/15** [1]
**(b)** P(green then not green) = 1/6 × 5/5 = 1/6, and P(not green then green) = 5/6 × 1/5 = 1/6 [1]
Total = **1/3** [1]
**(c)** P(nothing) = 1 − 4/15 − 5/15 = 6/15 = 2/5

Distribution: £9 with 4/15, £3 with 1/3, £0 with 2/5.

[1]
**(d)** E = 9 × 4/15 + 3 × 1/3 = 2.4 + 1 [1]
**= £3.40** [1]
**(e)** Expected gain = 3.40 − 4 = **−£0.60 per game** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Skipping the ΣP(x) = 1 check, so a wrong entry goes unnoticed.
- Missing one route when a value can occur in two ways, such as one red and one blue in either order.
- Writing E(X) as the simple average of the x values, ignoring the probabilities.
- Giving the expected payout when the question asks for the expected gain, or forgetting to subtract the stake.
- Forgetting to multiply by the number of plays for a total.
- Using n × p when the probability changes between trials, as it does without replacement.
