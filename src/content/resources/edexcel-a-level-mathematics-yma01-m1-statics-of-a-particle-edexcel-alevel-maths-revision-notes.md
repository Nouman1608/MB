These notes cover section 5 of Unit M1 in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics (YMA01): forces as vectors, resolution of forces, equilibrium of a particle under coplanar forces (weight, normal reaction, tension, thrust and friction) and F ≤ μR in equilibrium. For worked examples, use the [M1 statics study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m1-statics-of-a-particle-edexcel-alevel-maths/).

## The method

1. Draw a force diagram. Mark every force and every angle.
2. Choose two perpendicular directions. On a slope: parallel and perpendicular to it.
3. Resolve every force in each direction.
4. Set each total to zero.
5. If the equilibrium is limiting, add F = μR. Otherwise use F ≤ μR.
6. Solve, and give answers that use g to 2 or 3 significant figures.

## The forces

| Force | Direction | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Weight | Vertically down | mg |
| Normal reaction | Perpendicular to the surface, away from it | Found from equilibrium |
| Tension | Along the string or rod, away from the particle | Found from equilibrium |
| Thrust | Along a rod, towards the particle | Found from equilibrium |
| Friction | Along the surface, opposing the likely motion | F ≤ μR |

## Components on a slope at angle α

| Force | Along the slope | Perpendicular to the slope |
|---|---|---|
| Weight mg | mg sin α (down) | mg cos α (into the plane) |
| Horizontal force P, pushing towards the slope | P cos α (up) | P sin α (into the plane) |
| Force P at angle θ above the slope, up the slope | P cos θ (up) | P sin θ (away from the plane) |

So R changes with P. Never assume R = mg cos α if another force has a perpendicular component.

## Friction

| Wording | Meaning |
|---|---|
| "Rests in equilibrium" | F ≤ μR; find F from the equations |
| "On the point of slipping" or "about to move" | Limiting: F = μR |
| "Limiting equilibrium" | F = μR |
| "About to slide up the slope" | Friction acts down the slope |
| "About to slide down the slope" | Friction acts up the slope |

**Particle alone on a rough slope:** it stays at rest if μ ≥ tan α. On the point of slipping, μ = tan α.

## Ranges of a holding force

For a particle held on a rough slope by a force P:

- **Least P:** about to slide down, friction up the slope.
- **Greatest P:** about to slide up, friction down the slope.
- Answer: least P ≤ P ≤ greatest P.

Example: 5 kg on a slope with tan α = 3/4 and μ = 0.5, P parallel to the slope. R = 39.2 N, μR = 19.6 N, 5g sin α = 29.4 N. Least P = 29.4 − 19.6 = 9.8 N; greatest P = 29.4 + 19.6 = 49 N.

## Exact values for slopes

| tan α | sin α | cos α |
|---|---|---|
| 3/4 | 3/5 | 4/5 |
| 4/3 | 4/5 | 3/5 |
| 5/12 | 5/13 | 12/13 |
| 7/24 | 7/25 | 24/25 |

## Triangle of forces

Three forces in equilibrium form a closed triangle when drawn nose to tail. Use right-angled trigonometry or the sine rule. Two strings at right angles to each other carrying a weight W: resolve along each string, so each tension is W times the cosine of the angle that string makes with the vertical.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Equilibrium | The resultant force is zero |
| Coplanar | All forces act in one plane |
| Resolve | Find the component of a force in a chosen direction |
| Limiting equilibrium | At rest, but friction has reached its maximum, μR |
| Coefficient of friction μ | The ratio of maximum friction to normal reaction for two surfaces |
| Thrust | A pushing force along a rod |

## Exam traps

- R = mg on a slope or with an angled applied force.
- F = μR used when the particle is simply "at rest".
- Friction drawn in the wrong direction for the limiting case.
- sin and cos swapped in the weight's components.
- A single value given for a range.
- "Show it stays at rest" without stating the comparison of friction needed with μR.

## Self-test

1. What is the condition for a particle to be in equilibrium?
2. Give the components of the weight of a particle on a plane inclined at α.
3. A 2 kg block rests on rough horizontal ground, μ = 0.4, with a horizontal force of 5 N acting. What is the friction force?
4. A particle on a rough plane at 20° is on the point of slipping down. Find μ.
5. If a particle on a rough slope is about to move up, which way does friction act?
6. Forces (2i − 3j) N and (−5i + 4j) N act with a third force F, in equilibrium. Find F.
7. A 3 kg particle rests on a smooth plane at 25°, held by a string parallel to the plane. Find the tension.
8. Find the normal reaction for question 7.
9. Why does a horizontal pushing force on a slope increase the normal reaction?
10. When is a rod in thrust rather than tension?
11. A particle of weight W hangs on a string held at 30° to the vertical by a horizontal 6 N force. Find W.
12. In question 11, find the tension.

**Answers:**

1. The resultant force is zero: the components in any two perpendicular directions each sum to zero.
2. mg sin α down the slope and mg cos α perpendicular to it, into the plane.
3. 5 N, since the maximum is 0.4 × 2 × 9.8 = 7.84 N, which is more than 5 N.
4. μ = tan 20° = 0.364.
5. Down the slope.
6. F = (3i − j) N, of magnitude √10 = 3.16 N.
7. T = 3g sin 25° = 12.4 N.
8. R = 3g cos 25° = 26.6 N.
9. It has a component P sin α into the plane, which adds to mg cos α.
10. When it pushes on the particle, so the force along it points towards the particle.
11. tan 30° = 6/W, so W = 6/tan 30° = 10.4 N.
12. T = 6/sin 30° = 12 N.
