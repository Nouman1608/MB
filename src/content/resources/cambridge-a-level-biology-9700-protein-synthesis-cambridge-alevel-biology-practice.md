## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Which mRNA codon is a stop codon? **[1]**

A: AUG

B: UGA

C: UGG

D: GAU

**2.** An mRNA codon is GCA. What is the anticodon on the tRNA that pairs with it? **[1]**

A: CGT

B: GCA

C: CGU

D: UGC

**3.** State where transcription and translation take place in a eukaryotic cell. **[2]**

**4.** Explain what is meant by the statement that the genetic code is universal. **[2]**

**5.** Distinguish between the template strand and the non-transcribed strand of a gene. **[2]**

**6.** Define the term gene mutation. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** The template strand of a short gene is 3′-TAC GGT CTT AAA CCG ATT-5′.

**(a)** Write the mRNA sequence produced by transcription, 5′ to 3′. **[2]**

**(b)** Use the codon table to give the amino acid sequence of the polypeptide. **[2]**

**(c)** State the anticodon of the tRNA that pairs with the fourth codon. **[1]**

**8.** A primary transcript of a eukaryotic gene contains 3 300 nucleotides, including four introns of 400, 520, 610 and 270 nucleotides. The exons contain only the coding sequence, from start codon to stop codon.

**(a)** Calculate the number of amino acids in the polypeptide. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the length of the gene in DNA cannot be used directly to calculate the length of the polypeptide. **[1]**

**9.** The normal mRNA for a short polypeptide is AUG UCU AAA GGG CAU UAG. Use the codon table to give the effect of each mutation on the polypeptide.

**(a)** A substitution changes the first base of the third codon from A to U. **[2]**

**(b)** A substitution changes the last base of the fourth codon from G to A. **[2]**

**(c)** The first base of the second codon is deleted. **[2]**

**10.** Describe the roles of tRNA and ribosomes in translation. **[4]**

**11.** Describe how mRNA is produced by transcription. **[4]**

**12.** Explain how a substitution mutation may result in an enzyme that does not function. **[3]**

**13.** Explain why the deletion of three adjacent base pairs usually has a smaller effect on the polypeptide than the deletion of one base pair. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. AUG is the start codon; UGG codes for Trp; GAU for Asp. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. The anticodon is complementary and contains U, not T. **[1]**

**3.** Transcription takes place in the nucleus [1]; translation takes place on ribosomes in the cytoplasm [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The same triplet of bases codes for the same amino acid [1] in almost all organisms [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The template (transcribed) strand is the one RNA polymerase uses to make mRNA by complementary base pairing [1]; the non-transcribed strand is the other strand, and has the same sequence as the mRNA except T in place of U [1]. **[2]**

**6.** A change in the sequence of base pairs in a DNA molecule [1] that may result in an altered polypeptide [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 5′-AUG CCA GAA UUU GGC UAA-3′ (1 mark for correct bases, 1 mark for U throughout with no T) [2].

**(b)** Met–Pro–Glu–Phe–Gly [1]; the final codon is a stop codon and adds no amino acid [1].

**(c)** **AAA** [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** Introns: 400 + 520 + 610 + 270 = 1 800; mRNA = 3 300 − 1 800 = 1 500 nucleotides [1]; 1 500 ÷ 3 = 500 codons, minus the stop codon = **499 amino acids** [1].

**(b)** The gene includes introns, which are removed before translation, so not all of its nucleotides code for amino acids [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** AAA becomes UAA, a stop codon [1], so the polypeptide is shortened to Met–Ser [1].

**(b)** GGG becomes GGA, which still codes for Gly [1], so the polypeptide is unchanged: Met–Ser–Lys–Gly–His [1].

**(c)** The reading frame shifts: AUG CUA AAG GGC AUU AG [1], giving Met–Leu–Lys–Gly–Ile, with every amino acid after Met potentially changed and the original stop codon lost [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* For 9(c), rewrite the whole sequence in new triplets before translating. Changing only one codon is the most common error.

**10.** Each tRNA carries a specific amino acid [1] and has an anticodon that pairs with a complementary codon on the mRNA [1]. The ribosome holds the mRNA and two tRNAs so that a peptide bond forms between adjacent amino acids [1], then moves along the mRNA one codon at a time until a stop codon is reached [1]. **[4]**

**11.** The DNA of the gene unwinds and hydrogen bonds between the strands break [1]. RNA polymerase moves along the template strand [1]. Free RNA nucleotides pair with complementary DNA bases, with uracil pairing with adenine [1]. RNA polymerase joins the nucleotides by phosphodiester bonds, and in eukaryotes the primary transcript has introns removed and exons joined to form mRNA [1]. **[4]**

**12.** The substitution changes one triplet, so a different amino acid is coded for [1]. The new amino acid has a different R group, so bonds holding the tertiary structure may change [1]. The shape of the active site changes, so the substrate no longer fits and the enzyme does not function [1]. **[3]**

**13.** Deleting three base pairs removes one whole triplet, so one amino acid is lost but the reading frame is unchanged [1]; deleting one base pair causes a frame shift, changing every codon after it [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing T in mRNA or anticodon sequences.
- Translating the stop codon as an amino acid.
- Forgetting to subtract introns before calculating polypeptide length.
- Changing only one codon after a deletion instead of re-reading the whole frame.
- Describing enzyme failure without linking the amino acid change to the tertiary structure and active site.
