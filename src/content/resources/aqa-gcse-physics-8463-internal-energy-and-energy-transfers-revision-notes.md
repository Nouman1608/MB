Recall notes for **4.3.2 Internal energy and energy transfers** (4.3.2.1 to 4.3.2.3) in AQA GCSE Physics
(8463). Paper 1, both tiers.

The worked examples and the latent heat experiment are explained in the [internal energy and energy transfers study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-internal-energy-and-energy-transfers/).
Practise with the [internal energy and energy transfers practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-internal-energy-and-energy-transfers-practice/).

## Equations (both on the equation sheet)

| Equation | Use it when | Units |
|---|---|---|
| ΔE = m c Δθ | Temperature changes, no change of state | J, kg, J/kg °C, °C |
| E = m L | State changes, no temperature change | J, kg, J/kg |

Rearranged: c = ΔE ÷ (m Δθ); Δθ = ΔE ÷ (m c); m = E ÷ L; L = E ÷ m.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Internal energy | Total kinetic energy and potential energy of all the particles in a system |
| Specific heat capacity | Energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 °C |
| Latent heat | Energy needed for a substance to change state |
| Specific latent heat | Energy required to change the state of 1 kg of a substance with no change in temperature |
| Specific latent heat of fusion | For the change solid ↔ liquid |
| Specific latent heat of vaporisation | For the change liquid ↔ vapour |

## What heating does

- Heating raises the energy of the particles.
- Result: **either** a temperature rise (more kinetic energy) **or** a change of state (more potential energy).
- Temperature rise depends on **mass**, **type of material** and **energy input**.

## Heating graph decoder

| Feature | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Slope | One state warming; use ΔE = m c Δθ |
| Flat part | Change of state; use E = m L |
| First flat part | Melting point |
| Second flat part | Boiling point |
| Longer flat part | More energy needed for that change |
| Steeper slope (same mass and heating rate) | Smaller specific heat capacity |

Cooling graph: the same in reverse; flat parts show condensing and freezing, with energy transferred to the
surroundings at constant temperature.

## Latent heat of fusion experiment

- Two funnels of crushed ice: one with an immersion heater, one control without.
- Record energy supplied (joulemeter, or P × t).
- Mass of extra meltwater = heated funnel's mass − control's mass.
- L = E ÷ m.
- Energy lost to the surroundings makes the calculated L too high.

## Worked reminders

- 0.50 kg of oil, c = 2000 J/kg °C, warmed by 30 °C: ΔE = **30 000 J**.
- 0.40 kg of ice melted, L = 334 000 J/kg: E = **133 600 J**.
- 5000 J raises 0.50 kg of a metal by 25 °C: c = 5000 ÷ 12.5 = **400 J/kg °C**.
- 690 000 J boils away 0.30 kg of a liquid: L = **2 300 000 J/kg**.

## Two-stage problems

1. Warming to the melting or boiling point: ΔE = m c Δθ.
2. Changing state at that temperature: E = m L.
3. Add the two energies. Use the same mass in both stages.

Example: 0.20 kg of water at 60 °C heated to 100 °C and then boiled away (c = 4200 J/kg °C,
L = 2 260 000 J/kg): 0.20 × 4200 × 40 = 33 600 J; 0.20 × 2 260 000 = 452 000 J; total = **485 600 J**.

## Exam traps

- **Definition trap:** internal energy is kinetic **and** potential energy of **all** the particles.
- **Δθ trap:** use the change in temperature, not the final temperature.
- **Graph trap:** using c on a flat part, or L on a slope.
- **Unit trap:** c is in J/kg °C; L is in J/kg.
- **Temperature trap:** temperature stays constant during a change of state even though energy is supplied.
- **Control trap:** without a control funnel, ice melted by the room is counted as melted by the heater.

## Self-test

1. Define internal energy.
2. State two possible effects of heating a system.
3. Name three factors that affect the temperature rise when a substance is heated.
4. Define specific heat capacity.
5. Calculate the energy to heat 2.0 kg of water by 15 °C (c = 4200 J/kg °C).
6. 7200 J is supplied to 0.60 kg of a metal, raising it by 30 °C. Calculate c.
7. Define specific latent heat.
8. What is specific latent heat of vaporisation?
9. Calculate the energy to melt 0.25 kg of ice (L = 334 000 J/kg).
10. Why is the temperature constant during boiling?
11. On a heating graph for water, which flat part is longer, and why?
12. Calculate the mass of water boiled away by 565 000 J (L = 2 260 000 J/kg).

### Answers

1. The total kinetic energy and potential energy of all the particles that make up a system.
2. A temperature rise, or a change of state.
3. Mass, type of material (its specific heat capacity) and energy input.
4. The energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 °C.
5. ΔE = 2.0 × 4200 × 15 = **126 000 J**.
6. c = 7200 ÷ (0.60 × 30) = **400 J/kg °C**.
7. The energy required to change the state of 1 kg of a substance with no change in temperature.
8. The specific latent heat for the change of state from liquid to vapour.
9. E = 0.25 × 334 000 = **83 500 J**.
10. The energy supplied increases the potential energy of the particles (internal energy), not their kinetic
    energy, as the substance changes state.
11. The boiling part, because the specific latent heat of vaporisation is larger than that of fusion.
12. m = 565 000 ÷ 2 260 000 = **0.25 kg**.
