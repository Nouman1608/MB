Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics (4PH1) is the established linear route and has two externally assessed, untiered written papers, available in the November and June series. Paper 1 (4PH1/1P) is 2 hours, 110 marks and 61.1% of the grade; Paper 2 (4PH1/2P) is 1 hour 15 minutes, 70 marks and 38.9%. Calculators are permitted in both. Paper 1 assesses only core content, whereas Paper 2 assesses all content, including the bold, "P"-referenced material. The eight topics are Forces and motion, Electricity, Waves, Energy resources and energy transfers, Solids, liquids and gases, Magnetism and electromagnetism, Radioactivity and particles, and Astrophysics. Pages that support this preparation include [Forces and Motion](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-forces-and-motion/), [Electricity](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-electricity/) and [Energy Resources and Energy Transfers](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-energy-resources-and-energy-transfers/).

## Choose the right route

Pearson also offers a separate modular route to the same subject, with codes 4WPH1 and 4WPH2, first assessed in June 2025. This page covers only the linear 4PH1 route described above, so check with your teacher which entry you have before using it to plan your timetable.

## Paper 1: core content, 2 hours

Paper 1 gives 120 minutes for 110 marks, or 120 / 110 = about 1.09 minutes per mark. It is the heavier paper, at 61.1% of the grade, and tests only the core content. **Preparation priority**: secure the core across all eight topics before anything else, as a gap in a single topic can cost many marks. Practise multi-part questions in which an early calculation feeds a later part, and always carry forward your own earlier answer if you made a slip, since method marks can still be available.

## Paper 2: all content, 1 hour 15 minutes

Paper 2 gives 75 minutes for 70 marks, or 75 / 70 = about 1.07 minutes per mark. Because it assesses all the content, including the bold "P"-referenced material, questions here can be more demanding in the range of ideas they require. **Preparation priority**: after the core is secure, work through the "P"-referenced content topic by topic, and practise recognising which relationship from your equation list a problem needs. Do not wait until the last week to start the extra content.

## Weightings and time

Together the papers give 110 + 70 = 180 marks over 120 + 75 = 195 minutes. The weightings 110 / 180 = 61.1% and 70 / 180 = 38.9% follow directly from the marks, so each mark is worth the same fraction of the grade on either paper. One mark is roughly 0.56% of the total, which is a useful reminder that a single slipped unit conversion is rarely trivial.

## Calculations, equations and units

Calculators are allowed, so arithmetic speed is not the issue; accuracy and presentation are. **Preparation priority**: practise rearranging equations, converting units (kilowatts to watts, centimetres to metres, minutes to seconds) and giving answers to a suitable number of significant figures with the correct unit. Learn which equations you must recall and which are provided, by checking the specification's equation information for your examination series, since the available support materials can change.

## Command words and question styles

Expect state, describe, explain, calculate, determine, suggest and compare. "Explain" asks for the physics reason, usually in two linked steps. "Suggest" asks you to apply principles to an unfamiliar situation. Graph questions may ask for gradients, areas or readings. Practise drawing clear, labelled circuit and ray diagrams with a ruler.

## Worked practice scenario: electrical energy and temperature rise

Question (original): "An electric kettle has a power of 2.4 kW and is switched on for 150 s. 85% of the electrical energy transferred heats 1.5 kg of water. The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J/kg degC. Calculate the temperature rise of the water."

```
Step 1  Power = 2.4 kW = 2400 W
Step 2  Energy supplied = power x time = 2400 x 150 = 360 000 J
Step 3  Useful energy = 0.85 x 360 000 = 306 000 J
Step 4  Energy = mass x specific heat capacity x temperature change
        306 000 = 1.5 x 4200 x temperature change
        temperature change = 306 000 / 6300 = 48.6 degC
        = 49 degC (2 significant figures)
```

Converting kilowatts to watts in Step 1 is where most errors begin. Stating the efficiency step separately also makes your reasoning easy to credit.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm you are entered for the linear 4PH1 route.
- Secure the core content across all eight topics before extension material.
- Practise full timed papers, including the two-hour Paper 1.
- Check your calculator mode and bring a spare.
- Learn and rearrange the key equations, with units.
- Show each step in a calculation and carry forward answers.
- Read command words and mark allocations before writing.
- Use spare minutes to recheck units and significant figures.

## Self-test

1. How many marks and what weighting does Paper 2 carry?
2. Which paper assesses only core content?
3. How many minutes per mark does Paper 1 allow?
4. Are calculators permitted?
5. In the worked scenario, how much useful energy heats the water?

**Answers:** 1. 70 marks and 38.9%. 2. Paper 1. 3. 120 / 110, about 1.09 minutes per mark. 4. Yes, in both papers. 5. 306 000 J.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Edexcel IGCSE Physics 4PH1?**
Two: Paper 1 (2 hours, 110 marks, 61.1%) and Paper 2 (1 hour 15 minutes, 70 marks, 38.9%).

**Are there Foundation and Higher tiers?**
No. Both papers are untiered.

**Which series are available?**
Both papers are available in the November and June series.

**What is the difference between the papers?**
Paper 1 assesses only core content, and Paper 2 assesses all content, including the bold "P"-referenced material.

**Is there a modular route?**
Yes, with codes 4WPH1 and 4WPH2, but this page is written for the linear 4PH1 route.
