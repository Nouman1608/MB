## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** What causes the depolarisation phase of an action potential?

A: Sodium–potassium pumps moving Na⁺ into the axon

B: Na⁺ diffusing into the axon through voltage-gated channels

C: K⁺ diffusing out of the axon through voltage-gated channels

D: Ca²⁺ diffusing into the axon through voltage-gated channels **[1]**

**2.** Explain the role of calcium ions in the presynaptic knob of a cholinergic synapse. **[2]**

**3.** The refractory period of a neurone lasts 4.0 ms. Calculate the maximum frequency of action potentials in this neurone. **[2]**

**4.** Which band of a sarcomere stays the same length when a muscle contracts?

A: A band

B: I band

C: H zone

D: The distance between Z lines **[1]**

**5.** State two structural differences between a sensory neurone and a motor neurone. **[2]**

**6.** Outline the role of sensory receptor cells. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** Describe the sequence of events by which salt in food leads to an action potential in a sensory neurone from a taste bud. **[5]**

**8.** The membrane potential at one point on an axon was recorded as an action potential passed.

| Time / ms | 0 | 0.5 | 1.0 | 1.5 | 2.0 | 3.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Membrane potential / mV | −70 | −55 | +30 | −20 | −80 | −70 |

**(a)** Explain the change in membrane potential between 0.5 ms and 1.0 ms. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain the change between 1.0 ms and 2.0 ms. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how the potential returns to −70 mV by 3.0 ms. **[1]**

**9.** An action potential took 1.2 ms to travel 60 mm along a myelinated axon.

**(a)** Calculate the conduction velocity in m s⁻¹. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why conduction is faster in a myelinated axon than in a non-myelinated axon of the same diameter. **[3]**

**10.**

**(a)** A toxin prevents voltage-gated calcium ion channels in presynaptic membranes from opening. Predict and explain the effect on transmission at a cholinergic synapse. **[2]**

**(b)** An insecticide inhibits acetylcholinesterase. Predict and explain its effect on the postsynaptic neurone. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why impulses cross a cholinergic synapse in one direction only. **[2]**

**11.**

**(a)** Describe how an action potential in a motor neurone leads to the release of calcium ions inside a muscle fibre. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain the roles of calcium ions, troponin and tropomyosin in starting muscle contraction. **[3]**

**(c)** After death, ATP in muscle fibres runs out and the muscles become rigid. Explain why. **[2]**

**12.** In a model sarcomere, each myosin filament is 1.6 µm long and each actin filament extends 1.0 µm from its Z line.

**(a)** Calculate the length of the I band and the H zone when the sarcomere is 2.6 µm long. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the same two lengths when the sarcomere contracts to 2.2 µm. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the A band does not change length. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]

**2.** Depolarisation opens voltage-gated calcium ion channels, so Ca²⁺ diffuses into the knob [1]. Ca²⁺ causes vesicles of acetylcholine to fuse with the presynaptic membrane and release ACh by exocytosis [1].

**3.** 4.0 ms = 0.0040 s [1]; 1 ÷ 0.0040 = **250 action potentials per second (250 Hz)** [1].

**4.** A [1]

**5.** Any two: a sensory neurone's cell body lies part-way along the fibre, outside the CNS, while a motor neurone's cell body is at one end, inside the CNS [1]; a motor neurone has many dendrites on its cell body, a sensory neurone has a long dendron from the receptor [1]; a motor neurone has a long axon to the effector, a sensory neurone a short axon into the CNS (accept).

**6.** They detect a specific type of stimulus and convert its energy into a receptor potential [1]. If threshold is reached, they cause action potentials in a sensory neurone [1].

**7.** Na⁺ ions from the salt diffuse into the chemoreceptor cell through sodium ion channels [1]. The cell membrane is depolarised (receptor potential) [1]. Voltage-gated calcium ion channels open and Ca²⁺ diffuses in [1]. Vesicles release neurotransmitter, which binds to receptors on the sensory neurone [1]. The sensory neurone is depolarised; if threshold is reached, an action potential is generated [1].

**8.**

**(a)** Threshold has been reached, so many voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open [1]. Na⁺ diffuses in rapidly, making the inside positive (depolarisation to +30 mV) [1].

**(b)** Na⁺ channels close and voltage-gated K⁺ channels open, so K⁺ diffuses out (repolarisation) [1]. So many K⁺ ions leave that the potential overshoots to −80 mV (hyperpolarisation) [1].

**(c)** K⁺ channels close and the sodium–potassium pumps (with leak channels) restore the resting distribution of ions [1].

**9.**

**(a)** 0.060 m ÷ 0.0012 s [1] = **50 m s⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** Myelin from Schwann cells insulates the axon, so ions cannot cross the membrane where it is myelinated [1]. Depolarisation happens only at the nodes of Ranvier [1]. Local circuits form between nodes, so the action potential jumps from node to node (saltatory conduction), and fewer regions of membrane must depolarise [1].

**10.**

**(a)** No Ca²⁺ enters the presynaptic knob, so vesicles do not fuse with the membrane and no ACh is released [1]. The postsynaptic membrane is not depolarised, so no action potential passes on [1].

**(b)** ACh is not broken down and stays bound to the receptors [1]. The postsynaptic membrane stays depolarised, so action potentials continue to be generated or the neurone is continuously stimulated [1].

**(c)** Only the presynaptic knob contains vesicles of ACh [1]. Only the postsynaptic membrane has ACh receptors [1].

**11.**

**(a)** ACh released at the neuromuscular junction depolarises the sarcolemma [1]. The depolarisation spreads down the T-tubules into the fibre [1]. This causes the sarcoplasmic reticulum to release Ca²⁺ into the sarcoplasm [1].

**(b)** Ca²⁺ binds to troponin [1]. Troponin changes shape and moves tropomyosin away from the myosin-binding sites on actin [1]. Myosin heads can now bind to actin, forming cross-bridges [1].

**(c)** ATP is needed for myosin heads to detach from actin [1]. Without ATP, the cross-bridges cannot break, so the filaments are locked in place [1].

**12.**

**(a)** I band = 2.6 − 1.6 = **1.0 µm** [1]; H zone = 2.6 − (2 × 1.0) = **0.6 µm** [1].

**(b)** I band = 2.2 − 1.6 = **0.6 µm** [1]; H zone = 2.2 − 2.0 = **0.2 µm** [1].

**(c)** The A band is the length of the myosin filaments, and filaments do not shorten; they slide past each other [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the pump causes depolarisation.
- Forgetting to convert ms to s or mm to m.
- Putting Ca²⁺ entry on the postsynaptic side.
- Writing that ATP is needed to form cross-bridges.
- Saying actin or myosin filaments shorten.
- Missing the T-tubules in the link from neurone to sarcoplasmic reticulum.
