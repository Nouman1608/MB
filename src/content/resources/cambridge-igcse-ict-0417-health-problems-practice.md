These questions cover subtopic 5.2 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417: repetitive strain injury, back problems, eye problems and headaches caused by prolonged use of IT equipment, and strategies for preventing them. All scenarios are invented.

## Section A: short answers

**1.** Which **one** of the following is most likely to cause RSI? **[1]**

A: Sitting too close to a window

B: Typing for long periods with the wrists bent

C: Using a screen that is too bright

D: Sitting on a chair without back support

**2.** Name **two** body parts commonly affected by RSI. **[2]**

**3.** State **one** piece of equipment that helps to prevent back problems. **[1]**

**4.** Give **two** causes of eye strain when using a computer. **[2]**

**5.** Which **one** of the following would best reduce glare on a screen? **[1]**

A: A wrist rest

B: A footrest

C: An anti-glare filter

D: An ergonomic mouse

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** A call centre worker types customer details for eight hours a day.

**(a)** Identify the health problem she is most likely to develop in her wrists. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe **two** ways her employer could help prevent this problem. **[4]**

**7.** A graphic designer's desk faces away from a large window, so the window is behind him and the sun shines on his screen.

**(a)** Explain why this arrangement may cause headaches. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **two** changes that would reduce the problem. **[2]**

**8.** A student does homework on a laptop placed on a low coffee table while she sits on the sofa.

**(a)** Explain **one** health problem this is likely to cause. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how she should set up her equipment to prevent it. **[3]**

**9.** Explain why taking regular breaks helps prevent eye problems. **[2]**

**10.** A teenager sends hundreds of text messages a day using his thumbs.

**(a)** Identify the health problem this could lead to. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest **one** way he could reduce the risk while still sending messages. **[1]**

## Section C: extended questions

**11.** A company is setting up a new office where staff will use computers all day. Describe the measures it should take to reduce the risk of back problems and eye problems for its staff. **[6]**

**12.** A student writes: "To prevent all health problems from computers, just take breaks." Evaluate this statement. **[5]**

---

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Repeated typing with bent wrists strains the tendons; the other options relate to eyes or the back. **[1]**

**2.** Wrists [1]; fingers [1] (also credit: hands, arms, shoulders). **[2]**

**3.** An adjustable chair with back support [1] (also credit: footrest, monitor stand, laptop stand). **[1]**

**4.** Looking at the screen for long periods without a break [1]; glare or reflections on the screen [1] (also credit: poor lighting, small text, flickering screen, badly set brightness). **[2]**

**5.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** Repetitive strain injury (RSI) [1].

**(b)** Provide ergonomic keyboards [1] that keep her hands and wrists in a natural position, reducing strain [1]. Schedule regular breaks [1] so that the muscles and tendons in her wrists can recover between periods of typing [1] (also credit: wrist rests so her wrists stay straight; voice recognition software to reduce typing). **[5]**

**7. (a)** Sunlight from the window reflects off the screen, causing glare [1], so he strains his eyes to see the screen, which leads to headaches [1].

**(b)** Fit blinds to the window [1]; fit an anti-glare filter to the screen [1] (also credit: turn the desk so the window is to the side). **[4]**

**8. (a)** Back or neck pain [1], because she must lean forward and look down at the low screen for long periods [1].

**(b)** Place the laptop on a desk of suitable height [1]; raise the screen to eye level using a laptop stand, with a separate keyboard [1]; sit on an adjustable chair with back support and keep a straight posture [1]. **[5]**

**9.** The eyes tire when they focus on a screen at one distance for a long time [1]; a break, especially looking at distant objects, lets the focusing muscles relax and the user blink normally, reducing strain and dryness [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** RSI in the thumbs [1].

**(b)** Use voice input to dictate messages instead of typing [1] (also credit: take breaks between messages; use predictive text to reduce key presses). **[2]**

**11.** Back problems: provide adjustable chairs with lower-back support [1]; provide footrests for staff whose feet do not reach the floor [1]; provide monitors that can be raised and tilted so screens are at eye level [1]. Eye problems: fit anti-glare screens or filters and blinds on the windows [1]; ensure lighting is suitable, neither too dim nor too bright [1]; give staff regular breaks and pay for eye tests [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Breaks do help with every problem: they rest the wrists, let the back move, and rest the eyes [1]. However, breaks do not remove the cause [1]: a badly positioned screen will still cause neck pain when the user returns, so it must be raised to eye level [1]. Glare will still cause eye strain and headaches unless an anti-glare screen or blinds are used [1]. Conclusion: the statement is only partly correct; breaks are useful, but each problem also needs a prevention matched to its specific cause [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Repeating "take breaks" as both answers when two different preventions are asked for.
- Giving a cause that is not in the scenario when the question supplies details.
- Saying "bad posture" without saying what is wrong, such as the screen being too low.
- Describing safety hazards such as trailing cables, which are not health problems.
