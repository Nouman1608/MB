These notes condense **1.1.3 Analysis** in OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432), Version 3.1 (May 2026). The four learning outcomes are (a) processing, analysing and interpreting qualitative and quantitative results, including valid conclusions (HSW5); (b) appropriate mathematical skills for quantitative data (HSW3); (c) appropriate use of significant figures (M1.1); and (d) plotting and interpreting graphs, with (i) axes, scales, quantities and units and (ii) gradients and intercepts (M3.2 to M3.5). Section 1.1 is assessed in the written papers.

Worked examples are in the [1.1.3 study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-h432-ocr-a-level-chemistry-1-1-3/). ## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Processed data | Values calculated from raw readings, such as a mean titre or 1/time |
| Concordant | Results that agree closely; for titres, typically within 0.10 cm³ |
| Outlier (anomaly) | A result that does not fit the pattern; leave it out of means and best-fit lines |
| Valid conclusion | A conclusion that answers the question and is supported by the data |
| Correlation | Two variables change together |
| Causal relationship | A change in one variable produces the change in the other |
| Significant figures | The digits that carry meaning about precision, from the first non-zero digit |
| Line of best fit | A straight line or smooth curve with points evenly scattered either side |
| Tangent | A straight line touching a curve at one point; its gradient is the rate there |
| Intercept | Where a line crosses an axis |
| Half-life | Time for a reactant's concentration to fall to half its value |
| Calibration curve | A graph from standards of known value, used to find an unknown |

## 1.1.3(a): processing and conclusions

- **Titres:** leave out the rough titre and any non-concordant titre; average the concordant ones (M1.2).
- **Rate:** 1/time is proportional to rate when the same amount of change is timed.
- **Conclusions:** answer the question, quote numbers as evidence, and stay within the range of the data.
- **Correlation is not proof of cause.** A third variable may drive both.
- **Qualitative tests:** say only what the tests show. A negative result rules ions or groups out; it does not identify what is present.

## 1.1.3(b): maths skills used most

```
mean            sum of chosen values ÷ number of values
percentage      part ÷ whole × 100
standard form   2.245 × 10⁻³ rather than 0.002245
logarithms      pH = −log[H⁺];  ln k for Arrhenius plots
rearranging     c = n ÷ V;  V = n ÷ c
gradient        Δy ÷ Δx;  intercept c = y − mx
```

## 1.1.3(c): significant figures (M1.1)

1. Find the number of significant figures in each measurement used.
2. Give the answer to the **smallest** of these.
3. Never round intermediate values; carry the calculator value.
4. Use standard form to make the figures clear: 3.0 × 10² is 2 s.f.; 300 is ambiguous.

| Value | s.f. | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 0.00450 | 3 | Leading zeros do not count; the final zero does |
| 25.0 | 3 | The trailing zero records precision |
| 22.45 | 4 | All non-zero digits |
| 4.0 × 10⁻² | 2 | Standard form shows two figures |
| 1200 | 2, 3 or 4 | Ambiguous; write in standard form |

## 1.1.3(d)(i): axes and scales

- Independent variable on the x-axis.
- Labels: quantity / unit.
- Scale: points cover more than half the grid; steps of 1, 2 or 5 (times a power of ten).
- Zero is not needed on an axis unless you must read or extrapolate to it.
- Small crosses; one best-fit line or smooth curve; ignore anomalies.

## 1.1.3(d)(ii): reading graphs

| Task | Method |
|---|---|
| Gradient of a line | Large triangle on the line; points read from the line, not data |
| Rate at a time | Draw a tangent at that time; gradient = rate (quote as positive) |
| Initial rate | Tangent at t = 0 |
| Half-life | Time for concentration to halve; constant half-life shows first order |
| First-order k from rate–concentration graph | Gradient of the straight line through the origin (M3.4) |
| Arrhenius plot | Gradient = −Ea/R; intercept = ln A (M3.3) |
| Intercept, axis not from zero | c = y − mx using a point on the line |
| Calibration curve | Read across from the unknown's reading to the line, then down |

## Exam traps

- **Rough titre in the mean.**
- **Too many significant figures**, or rounding early so the last figure is wrong.
- **Ambiguous trailing zeros**: write 2.40 × 10³, not 2400, when 3 s.f. are meant.
- **Awkward scales** (3 or 7 per division) or points squeezed into a corner.
- **Gradient from data points** not on the best-fit line.
- **Short tangent**, so the gradient reading is imprecise.
- **Intercept read off a false origin.**
- **Extrapolating** a conclusion outside the range of the data.

## Quick self-test

1. How many significant figures are in 0.00450?
2. Calculate 2.5 × 3.142 to an appropriate number of significant figures.
3. Titres (cm³): rough 24.20, then 23.60, 23.95, 23.65, 23.70. Find the mean titre.
4. Why is the rough titre not included in the mean?
5. Data for the x-axis run from 0.012 to 0.058 mol dm⁻³. The grid has 10 large squares across. Suggest a scale.
6. A tangent to a concentration–time curve passes through (0 s, 0.12 mol dm⁻³) and (80 s, 0.02 mol dm⁻³). Find the rate.
7. A straight line has gradient 2.0 and passes through (5.0, 14.0). The x-axis starts at 4.0. Find the y-intercept.
8. Two successive half-lives on a concentration–time graph are both 30 s. What does this show?
9. A calibration line through the origin has gradient 15 dm³ mol⁻¹. An unknown gives an absorbance of 0.27. Find its concentration.
10. Rates were measured from 20 °C to 40 °C. A student uses the trend to state the rate at 80 °C. Comment.
11. Explain the difference between a correlation and a causal relationship.
12. A concentration is calculated from a mass of 1.2 g and a volume of 25.0 cm³. To how many significant figures should it be given?

### Answers

1. **3**.
2. 7.855 rounds to **7.9** (2.5 has only 2 s.f.).
3. Concordant: 23.60, 23.65, 23.70; mean = **23.65 cm³**.
4. It is a quick trial to find the approximate end point, so it usually overshoots and is less precise.
5. Start at 0.010 and use **0.005 mol dm⁻³ per large square**, reaching 0.060 at the tenth square.
6. Gradient = (0.02 − 0.12) ÷ 80 = −1.25 × 10⁻³, so the rate is **1.25 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹**.
7. c = 14.0 − 2.0 × 5.0 = **4.0**. It must be calculated, because the y-axis at x = 4.0 is not the intercept.
8. A constant half-life shows the reaction is **first order** with respect to that reactant.
9. 0.27 ÷ 15 = **0.018 mol dm⁻³**.
10. It is **extrapolation beyond the data**, so it is not a valid conclusion; the trend may not continue.
11. In a correlation two variables change together; in a **causal** relationship a change in one brings about the change in the other. Correlation alone does not show cause.
12. **2 significant figures**, matching 1.2 g.
