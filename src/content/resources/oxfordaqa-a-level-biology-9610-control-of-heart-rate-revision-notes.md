These notes condense section 3.4.8 (Control of heart rate) of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Biology (9610) specification, Version 5.1, in Unit 4. For explanations and worked examples, use the [control of heart rate study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-biology-9610-control-of-heart-rate/); then try the [practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-biology-9610-control-of-heart-rate-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Myogenic | Contraction starts within the muscle itself |
| Sinoatrial node (SAN) | Pacemaker in the right atrium wall that starts each wave of excitation |
| Atrioventricular node (AVN) | Node that receives the wave from the atria and delays it before passing it on |
| Bundle of His | Conducting fibres in the septum between the ventricles |
| Purkinje tissue | Fibres that spread the wave through the ventricle walls |
| Medulla oblongata | Brain region containing the cardiac control centre |
| Chemoreceptor | Receptor sensitive to chemical change, such as pH |
| Pressure receptor | Receptor sensitive to blood pressure |
| Sympathetic nerve | Autonomic nerve that speeds the heart up |
| Parasympathetic (vagus) nerve | Autonomic nerve that slows the heart down |
| Cardiac output | Volume of blood pumped per minute |

## The beat in order

1. **SAN** produces a wave of excitation.
2. The wave spreads across the **atria**, which contract.
3. A **non-conducting layer** stops the wave passing straight to the ventricles.
4. The wave reaches the **AVN**, which **delays** it.
5. The wave passes down the **bundle of His**.
6. **Purkinje tissue** spreads the wave through the ventricle walls from the base (apex).
7. The **ventricles contract from the bottom upwards**.

**Why the delay?** The atria finish contracting and the ventricles fill with blood before they contract.
**Why from the bottom?** Blood is forced upwards towards the arteries.

## Control: the loop

| Step | Exercise example |
|---|---|
| Stimulus | More respiration, more carbon dioxide, lower pH |
| Receptor | Chemoreceptors in the carotid arteries and aorta |
| Coordinator | Cardiac centre in the medulla oblongata |
| Nerve | Sympathetic nerve to the SAN |
| Effector | SAN |
| Response | Faster heart rate, more cardiac output |
| Feedback | Carbon dioxide falls, so fewer impulses from the chemoreceptors (negative feedback) |

### Pressure receptors

- **High blood pressure:** pressure receptors send more impulses to the cardiac centre, the parasympathetic (vagus) nerve is stimulated and the heart slows.
- **Low blood pressure:** fewer impulses from the pressure receptors, the sympathetic nerve is stimulated and the heart speeds up.

### Which nerve does what

| Nerve | Effect on heart rate | Transmitter |
|---|---|---|
| Sympathetic | Increases | Noradrenaline |
| Parasympathetic (vagus) | Decreases | Acetylcholine |

## Calculations

- **Heart rate from the interval between beats:** rate = 60 / interval in seconds. 0.8 s gives 75 beats per minute; 0.5 s gives 120.
- **Heart rate from a count:** peaks counted divided by the time in seconds, multiplied by 60. Five peaks in 4.0 s is 75 per minute.
- **Cardiac output = stroke volume x heart rate.** Example: 70 cm3 x 72 = 5,040 cm3 per minute at rest; 110 cm3 x 150 = 16,500 cm3 per minute in exercise.
- **Percentage change = (new - old) / old x 100.** The increase in output is 227 %.

## Exam traps

- The heart is myogenic. The SAN starts the beat and the nerves change the rate.
- The AVN delays the wave. It does not start the beat in the ventricles.
- Purkinje tissue is in the ventricle walls.
- Sympathetic speeds up and parasympathetic slows down.
- The SAN is the effector in the control loop.
- Write "more frequent waves of excitation from the SAN", not "the heart is told to beat faster".
- Give the receptor and the stimulus together: chemoreceptors and pH change.

## Command words and what they need

| Command word | What the answer must contain |
|---|---|
| Describe | The sequence of structures or events, in order, with names |
| Explain | A reason for each step, such as why the AVN delays the wave |
| Calculate | The formula, the numbers substituted, the answer and its unit |
| Suggest | A reasoned idea based on the data or principles given |

## Model sentences

- "The SAN is the pacemaker because it initiates the wave of excitation, which spreads across both atria so that they contract together."
- "The AVN delays the wave, so the atria have finished contracting and the ventricles are full before they contract."
- "Purkinje fibres carry the wave through the ventricle walls from the base upwards, so blood is forced towards the arteries."
- "Chemoreceptors detect the fall in pH caused by carbon dioxide and send more impulses to the cardiac centre, which increases impulses along the sympathetic nerve to the SAN."

## Common graph and data tasks

- **ECG peaks.** Find the interval between two matching peaks, divide 60 by the interval in seconds, and give beats per minute.
- **Nerve stimulation data.** Calculate the percentage change from the control, then say which nerve raised or lowered the rate and why.
- **Cardiac output.** Multiply stroke volume by heart rate, convert units if needed, and compare with the resting value as a percentage change.

## Quick self-test

1. What does myogenic mean?
2. Where is the SAN?
3. Why does the wave not pass directly from the atria to the ventricles?
4. State the function of the delay at the AVN.
5. Name the fibres that spread the wave through the ventricle walls.
6. Name the part of the brain that controls heart rate.
7. What do chemoreceptors detect that increases heart rate in exercise?
8. Name the nerve that decreases heart rate.
9. What do pressure receptors do when blood pressure is high?
10. Calculate the heart rate if the interval between beats is 0.6 s.
11. A person has a stroke volume of 80 cm3 and a heart rate of 70 beats per minute. Calculate the cardiac output.
12. Why is control of heart rate an example of negative feedback?

### Answers

1. Contraction starts in the muscle without a nerve signal.
2. In the wall of the right atrium.
3. A layer of non-conducting tissue separates them; the wave can only pass through the AVN.
4. It allows the atria to finish contracting and the ventricles to fill before the ventricles contract.
5. Purkinje fibres (Purkinje tissue).
6. The medulla oblongata.
7. A fall in pH caused by more carbon dioxide.
8. The parasympathetic (vagus) nerve.
9. They send more impulses to the cardiac centre, which sends impulses along the parasympathetic nerve to slow the heart.
10. 60 / 0.6 = 100 beats per minute.
11. 80 x 70 = 5,600 cm3 per minute.
12. The response (a faster heart rate) reduces carbon dioxide and so reverses the original stimulus.
