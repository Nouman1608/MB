**These notes condense Section 3 of Cambridge O Level Pakistan Studies 2059 Paper 1, Nationhood
1947–99, into a timeline of leaders and a set of quick-reference lists.** Use them to fix which
leader introduced which policy, the order of constitutions and wars, and the main features of
Pakistan's foreign relations, so that your 7- and 14-mark answers rest on accurate detail.

## Syllabus coverage

**2059 Paper 1, Section 3 (2026 syllabus):** Key Question 11 (1947–48), Key Question 12 (stability
1948–71 and constitutions), Key Question 13 (Bangladesh), Key Question 14 (Bhutto and Zia), Key
Question 15 (1988–99) and Key Question 16 (world affairs and nuclear status).

## Key Question 11: problems of 1947–48

1. Radcliffe Award (Gurdaspur) and Indian access to Kashmir.
2. Canal water dispute, later settled by the Indus Waters Treaty (1960, World Bank).
3. Division of assets: cash and military stores delayed.
4. Princely states: Junagadh (occupied by India, 1947), Hyderabad (annexed after Indian military action, 1948), Kashmir (war; UN ceasefire January 1949).
5. Refugee crisis and violence.
6. Administration: new capital, staff and offices at Karachi.
7. National language controversy.

## Key Question 12: constitutions

| Document | Features to remember |
|---|---|
| Objectives Resolution, 1949 | Sovereignty belongs to Allah; democracy and Islamic social justice; minority rights |
| 1956 Constitution | Islamic Republic; parliamentary; Muslim head of state; Urdu and Bengali national languages |
| 1962 Constitution | Presidential; Basic Democracies as electoral college; "Islamic" restored in 1963 |
| 1973 Constitution | Parliamentary federal system; Islam the state religion; Muslim President and PM |

## Key Question 13: road to Bangladesh

- 1948–52: language dispute; deaths in Dhaka protests, February 1952.
- Economic and political disparity between the two wings.
- 1966: Sheikh Mujib's Six Points.
- 1970: cyclone; Awami League wins the general election.
- March 1971: military action in East Pakistan.
- December 1971: Indian military intervention; surrender in Dhaka on 16 December.

## Key Question 14: Bhutto and Zia

**Bhutto:** Simla Agreement 1972; 1973 Constitution; nationalisation of industry, banks and
education; land and labour reforms; Federal Security Force; Lahore Islamic Summit 1974; nuclear
programme; disputed 1977 election; removed July 1977.

**Zia:** Hudood Ordinances (1979); zakat and ushr; Federal Shariat Court; Shariat Benches; referendum
(1984); non-party elections (1985); Eighth Amendment (1985); Soviet invasion of Afghanistan (1979)
and refugees; US aid; died August 1988.

## Key Question 15: the 1990s

- **Recurring problems:** inflation, unemployment, corruption charges, Kashmir tension, dismissals under the Eighth Amendment.
- **Benazir:** clashes with Punjab under Sharif; no-confidence motion 1989; IMF pressure and devaluation in the second term.
- **Sharif:** privatisation; BCCI collapse; loss of US aid; Thirteenth Amendment 1997; nuclear tests 1998; Lahore Declaration and Kargil 1999.

## Worked example

**Question:** Why was Iskander Mirza's government unpopular by 1958? **[7]**

**Model answer outline:** Frequent changes of prime minister made government look unstable
[point 1]. One Unit was resented in Sindh, Balochistan and the Frontier [point 2]. Economic
problems, including food shortages and rising prices, hurt ordinary people [point 3]. Mirza was
seen to manipulate politicians to keep power, and elections were repeatedly postponed [point 4].
Each point should be explained with evidence.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Muddling Governor-General and Prime Minister.** Nazimuddin held both posts at different times.
- **Mixing the constitutions.** Learn which was parliamentary (1956, 1973) and which presidential (1962).
- **Describing Hyderabad's accession as peaceful.** It followed Indian military action in September 1948.
- **One-sided "how successful" answers.** Always give both successes and failures.

## How long should each Section B answer be?

The 2059 syllabus sets the length expected for each part: the **4-mark** part needs a factual answer of no more than one short paragraph; the **7-mark** part needs at least two paragraphs explaining reasons; and the **14-mark** part is an essay of about two pages in continuous prose, weighing factors and reaching a judgement. Use the timeline above to supply the dates that lift a 7- or 14-mark answer.

## Frequently asked questions

**What was One Unit?** The 1955 merger of the provinces of West Pakistan into a single province, ended
by Yahya Khan in 1970.

**What did the Eighth Amendment do?** Introduced under Zia in 1985, it gave the president power to
dismiss the national assembly and the prime minister.
